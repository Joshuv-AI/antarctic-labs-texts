Generalized uncertainty principle and neutrino phenomenology
Ioannis D. Gialamas, Timo J. Kärkkäinen, Luca Marzola

Abstract
Abstract Generalized uncertainty principles are effective changes to the Heisenberg uncertainty principle that emerge in several quantum gravity models. In the present letter, we study the consequences that two classes of these modifications yield on the physics of neutrinos. Besides analyzing the change in the oscillation probabilities that the generalized uncertainty principles entail, we assess their impact on the neutrino coherence length and their possible interpretation as nonstandard neutrino interactions. Constraints cast by present and planned neutrino experiments on the generalized uncertainty principles parameters are also derived.

II
The generalized uncertainty principle

Depending on the specific parametrization, modifications of the Heisenberg uncertainty principle introduce in the theory a minimum measurable length which can be accompanied by a maximum measurable momentum. In the following, we briefly review each of these prototypical cases which we later use in our analysis.

GUP-I: minimum measurable length

The first GUP parametrization we consider is of the form
[
9
]
:

\displaystyle\Delta x_{i}\Delta p_{i}\geq\frac{\hbar}{2}\big[

\displaystyle 1+\beta\left((\Delta p)^{2}+\langle p\rangle^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+2\beta\left((\Delta p_{i})^{2}+\langle p_{i}\rangle^{2}\right)\big]\,,

(1)

with
i=1,2,3
and
p=\sqrt{\sum_{i}p_{i}p_{i}}
. The parameter
\beta
is dubbed
GUP parameter
, and it is defined as

\beta=\beta_{0}l_{\rm Pl}^{2}/\hbar^{2}=\beta_{0}/(cM_{\rm Pl})^{2}
.
In this context,
\beta_{0}
is a dimensionless parameter that regulates the value of the minimum length while
l_{\rm Pl}
and
M_{\rm Pl}
are the Planck length and mass, respectively. In natural units, the GUP parameter is
\beta\simeq 7\beta_{0}\times 10^{-39}\;\mathrm{GeV}^{-2}
.

Upon minimizing (
1
) with respect to
\Delta p_{i}
, it becomes evident that the GUP implies a minimum length
\Delta x_{i}^{\rm min}\sim\sqrt{\beta_{0}}l_{\rm Pl}
for the measurement of any spatial Cartesian coordinate.

The GUP form under consideration arises from modifications of the Heisenberg algebra, as elaborated in
[
9
]
. This altered algebra, which serves as the foundation for the GUP, is expressed by the equation:

[x_{i},p_{j}]=i\hbar\left(\delta_{ij}+\beta\delta_{ij}p^{2}+2\beta p_{i}p_{j}\right)\,.

(2)

It is worth noting that this particular form of the commutator ensures that
[x_{i},x_{j}]=[p_{i},p_{j}]=0
. We remark that this property also applies to the GUP formulation which we will discuss subsequently.

We use the transformations

x_{i}=\tilde{x}_{i}\,,\qquad p_{i}=\tilde{p}_{i}(1+\beta\tilde{p}^{2})\,,\qquad E/c=p_{0}=\tilde{p}_{0}\,,

(3)

to define tilted variables that satisfy the canonical commutation relations
[\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{p}_{j}]=i\hbar\delta_{ij}
. The squared
4-
momentum of the physical variables
p
and
p_{0}
is expanded as
3
3

3

Note that we have used the mostly minus sign convention and from now on we set
c=1
.

p_{0}^{2}-p^{2}=\tilde{p}_{0}^{2}-\tilde{p}^{2}\left(1+2\beta\tilde{p}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{2})\right)\,,

(4)

where the higher order terms in the
\beta-
expansion are Planck suppressed. The tilded variables satisfy the canonical dispersion relation, namely
\tilde{p}_{0}^{2}-\tilde{p}^{2}=m^{2}
, so using this along with the inverse transformation
\tilde{p}^{2}=p^{2}\left(1-2\beta p^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{2})\right)
, Eq. (
4
) can be rewritten as

E^{2}=m^{2}+p^{2}(1-2\beta p^{2})\,,

(5)

where for
\beta\rightarrow 0
the standard energy-momentum dispersion relation is restored. At the leading order in the GUP parameter we have

\Delta p=\Delta E+3\beta E^{2}\Delta E+\mathcal{O}(\beta^{2})\,.

(6)

GUP-II: minimum measurable length and maximum measurable momentum

Next, we examine the generalization suggested in
[
57
,
58
]
, wherein the commutation relation satisfied by the modified position and momentum is given by

[x_{i},p_{j}]=i\hbar\left(\delta_{ij}-\alpha\left(p\delta_{ij}+\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{p}+\alpha^{2}\left(p^{2}\delta_{ij}+3p_{i}p_{j}\right)\right)\right)\,,

(7)

where
\alpha=\alpha_{0}l_{\rm Pl}/\hbar
is the “GUP-II parameter”. This formulation of GUP implies a minimum measurable length
\Delta x_{i}^{\rm min}\sim\alpha_{0}l_{\rm Pl}
and a maximum measurable momentum
\Delta p_{i}^{\rm max}\sim M_{\rm Pl}/\alpha_{0}
. The corresponding dispersion relation is
4
4

4

The momentum transformation (
3
) in this case takes the form
p_{i}=\tilde{p}_{i}(1-\alpha\tilde{p}_{i}+2\alpha^{2}\tilde{p}_{i}^{2})
. This yields a modified dispersion relation of the form
E^{2}=m^{2}+p^{2}(1\pm\alpha p)^{2}
. In our analysis, we retain only the minus sign, as the alternative leads to superluminal speed of gravitational waves
[
34
]
.

E^{2}=m^{2}+p^{2}(1-\alpha p)^{2}\,.

(8)

Expanding again at the leading order in the relevant GUP parameter we find

\Delta p=\Delta E+2\alpha E\Delta E+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})\,.

(9)

Although QC models predict specific value of
\alpha
or
\beta
, in the following we consider arbitrary values for the parameters in order to assess the power of neutrino experiments to constrain GUP scenarios.

III
Experimental constraints and prospects from neutrino oscillations

Prior to quantifying the impact of the modified dispersion relations in Eqs. (
5
) and (
8
) on neutrino oscillations, we briefly summarize the workings of this effect.

III.1
Standard neutrino oscillations

The misalignment between neutrino mass and flavour (interaction) eigenstates is described by the Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata (PMNS) neutrino mixing matrix
U
:

\begin{pmatrix}\nu_{e}\\
\nu_{\mu}\\
\nu_{\tau}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}U_{e1}&U_{e2}&U_{e3}\\
U_{\mu 1}&U_{\mu 2}&U_{\mu 3}\\
U_{\tau 1}&U_{\tau 2}&U_{\tau 3}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\nu_{1}\\
\nu_{2}\\
\nu_{3}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(10)

The matrix elements depend on three mixing angles
\theta_{12}
,
\theta_{13}
,
\theta_{23}
and a CP violating phase
\delta
, which later we set to the experimental values provided by the
NuFit
collaboration
[
59
]
in our numerical analysis.

Let a neutrino be produced in a pure state at
t=0
by a Standard Model (SM) gauge interaction. The time evolution of its flavour state is given by

\nu_{\ell}(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell^{\prime}=e,\mu,\tau\\
i=1,2,3\end{subarray}}U_{\ell i}U_{\ell^{\prime}i}^{*}e^{-iE_{i}t/\hbar}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}}(0)\equiv\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=e,\mu,\tau}A_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\nu_{\ell^{\prime}}(0)\,,

(11)

where
A_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
is the
\ell\rightarrow\ell^{\prime}
flavour transition probability amplitude at time
t>0
. The corresponding transition probability is

P_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=|A_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}|^{2}=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{3}U_{\ell i}^{*}U_{\ell^{\prime}i}U_{\ell j}U_{\ell^{\prime}j}^{*}e^{-i(E_{i}-E_{j})t/\hbar}\,.

(12)

As customary we assume neutrinos are ultrarelativistic, so the baseline length for neutrino oscillation is
L\approx t
. In the limit
m_{i}\ll p_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
, and utilizing the same-momentum approximation,
p_{1}\approx p_{2}\approx p_{3}\approx p\approx E
, the oscillation phase is

\Delta_{ij}\equiv\frac{E_{i}-E_{j}}{2}=\frac{L\Delta m_{ij}^{2}}{4E}\,,

(13)

where
\Delta m_{ij}^{2}\equiv m_{i}^{2}-m_{j}^{2}
are the neutrino mass squared differences. It is straightforward to show that in the two neutrino approximation with mixing angle
\theta
and oscillation frequency
\Delta=L\Delta m_{ij}^{2}/4E
the oscillation probability reduces to

P_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=\sin^{2}(2\theta)\sin^{2}\Delta,\quad\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}\,.

(14)

Neutrino coherence length

The standard derivation of the neutrino oscillation transition probability follows from solving a purely quantum mechanical Schrödinger equation with a plane wave ansatz. At the leading order this produces the correct expression, but sub-leading effects should also be taken into account. Contrary to the plane wave approximation, neutrino production and detection occur in a finite, localized, spacetime region and consequently are more properly modelled in terms of wave packets which posses uncertain energy and momentum. As the neutrino mass eigenstates are non-degenerate, the wave packets will separate over time and the oscillation of the transition probability will be exponentially suppressed by a characteristic
coherence length

L_{\text{coh}}
at which the wave packet separation starts to be significant. After a baseline of a few coherence lengths, the transition probabilities can be regarded as constant, signalling that decoherence took place. The expression for the transition probability computed in the wave packet formalism accounting for the effect is given by
[
60
]

P_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{3}U_{\ell i}^{*}U_{\ell^{\prime}i}U_{\ell j}U_{\ell^{\prime}j}^{*}\exp\left(-2i\Delta_{ij}-\left(\frac{L}{L_{ij}^{\text{coh}}}\right)^{2}\right)\,,

(15)

where the coherence length is

L^{\text{coh}}_{ij}=\frac{4\sqrt{2}E_{\nu}^{2}\Delta x}{|\Delta m_{ij}^{2}|}\,,

(16)

and
\Delta x
is the neutrino position uncertainty.

Nonstandard neutrino interactions

In a medium with electron number density
N_{e}
, the Hamiltonian regulating neutrino oscillations is given by

H=\frac{1}{2E}U^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\Delta m_{21}^{2}&0\\
0&0&\Delta m_{31}^{2}\end{pmatrix}U+\sqrt{2}G_{F}N_{e}\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

(17)

where
G_{F}
is the Fermi constant. The neutrino mixing matrices appearing in the above equation rotate the matrix hosting the squared mass differences to the flavour basis where interactions are diagonal. Theories beyond the SM, including Type II seesaw
[
61
,
62
,
63
,
64
,
65
]
, the Zee-Babu model
[
66
,
67
]
and
U(1)
extensions coupling to neutrino sector
[
68
,
69
,
70
,
71
,
72
,
73
]
, can modify neutrino oscillations upon the introduction of
nonstandard neutrino interactions
(NSI). These are described with dimension-6 effective operators in the Lagrangian:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{NSI}}=-2\sqrt{2}G_{F}\varepsilon^{ff^{\prime},C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}(\overline{\nu_{L,\alpha}}\gamma^{\mu}\nu_{L,\beta})(\overline{f}\gamma^{\mu}P_{C}f^{\prime})\,,

(18)

where a sum over chiralities (
C=L,R
), fermions
(f,f^{\prime})
and lepton flavours
(\ell,\ell^{\prime}=e,\mu,\tau)
is implied. The NSI parameters
\varepsilon^{ff^{\prime},C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
are dimensionless and in general complex numbers. Possible non-vanishing imaginary parts induce CP violation (CPV) in neutrino sector even if the standard CP phase
\delta
vanishes. The absolute values of the NSI parameters can be interpreted as the strength of the interaction with respect to weak interaction.

Integrating out the background fermion fields, the contribution of NSI is
generally modeled in a Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{\text{NSI}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{f}\sqrt{2}G_{F}N_{f}\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon^{f}_{ee}&\varepsilon^{f}_{e\mu}&\varepsilon^{f}_{e\tau}\\
\varepsilon^{f}_{\mu e}&\varepsilon^{f}_{\mu\mu}&\varepsilon^{f}_{\mu\tau}\\
\varepsilon^{f}_{\tau e}&\varepsilon^{f}_{\tau\mu}&\varepsilon^{f}_{\tau\tau}\\
\end{pmatrix}\,,

(19)

where
N_{f}
is the number density of the fermion
f
and
\varepsilon_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{f}=\varepsilon^{f,L}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}+\varepsilon^{f,R}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
. Note that hermiticity requires
\varepsilon_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{f,*}=\varepsilon_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}^{f}
. The inclusion of this operator induces subleading effects in neutrino flavour transitions that modify the standard oscillation framework by changing the oscillation probability
P_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
determined by the composition of the medium. In the following we will derive relations between NSI and GUP parameters and use the current experimental bounds on the former to constrain the latter.

III.2
GUP-I

Having summarized the standard results for neutrino oscillation probabilities, coherence length and NSI, we proceed to detail the how considered GUPs scenario modify these quantities starting with the GUP-I model in Eq. (
1

Neutrino oscillations

Assuming that
m_{i}\ll p
,
i=1,2,3
, we can expand the dispersion relation in Eq. (
5
) as

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\simeq p\sqrt{1-2\beta p^{2}}+\frac{m^{2}}{2p\sqrt{1-2\beta p^{2}}}+\mathcal{O}(m^{4})\,,

(20)

recovering the usual expression in the limit
\beta\rightarrow 0
. Denoting the oscillation frequency in the standard case (
\beta=0

\Delta^{(0)}
, the GUP-I scenario gives

\Delta^{(I)}_{ij}=\frac{\Delta^{(0)}_{ij}}{\sqrt{1-2\beta E^{2}}}\,,

(21)

and the oscillation frequency is consequently increased as the square root is necessarily smaller than unity.

Consider a case where one oscillation channel is dominant. The related transition probability is given by
(\ell\neq\ell^{\prime})

P_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=\sin^{2}(2\theta)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta^{(0)}}{\sqrt{1-2\beta E^{2}}}\right)\,,

(22)

and assuming
2\beta E^{2}\ll 1
it gives

\displaystyle P_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sin^{2}(2\theta)(\sin^{2}\Delta^{(0)}+2\Delta^{(0)}\beta E^{2}\cos\Delta^{(0)})\,.

(23)

Therefore, the difference in oscillation probability induced by the GUP is

\Delta P=2\Delta^{(0)}\beta E^{2}\sin^{2}(2\theta)\cos\Delta^{(0)}\sim\beta E^{2}\,.

(24)

The effect is illustrated in Fig.
1
, where we plot the change in the
P_{e\mu}
and
P_{e\tau}
transitions as a function of the experiment baseline for different values of the GUP parameter and considering a typical neutrino beam energy of 2 GeV.

The current error resolution on transition probabilities,
\Delta P\sim 10^{-2}
, can be used to estimate the maximum GUP effect allowed by current experiments, thereby yielding the exclusion limit indicated by the gray area. The corresponding bound is

\beta E^{2}=\frac{\beta}{\text{GeV}^{-2}}\times\left(\frac{E}{\text{GeV}}\right)^{2}\lesssim 10^{-2}\,,

(25)

and implies the following constraint on the dimensionless GUP-I parameter:

\beta_{0}\lesssim 10^{36}\times\left(\frac{\text{GeV}}{E}\right)^{2}\,.

(26)

A similar result holds true for the experimental constraints cast by measurements of disappearance oscillation probabilities, as shown from the plot in Fig.

\beta=0.01

Nonstandard interactions

Retaining the first nontrivial order of approximation in the dispersion relation (

E\simeq p\sqrt{1-2\beta p^{2}}+\frac{m^{2}}{2p}(1-2\beta p^{2})^{-1/2},

(27)

H=\frac{1}{2E}U^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\

(28)

(30)

or, in terms of the dimensionless quantity
\beta_{0}

\beta_{0}\lesssim 10^{37}\times\frac{\text{GeV}}{E}\,.

(31)

Although experiments utilizing lower energy neutrinos seem more suitable for constraining

Coherence length

GUPs also modify the neutrino coherence length as it is directly sensitive to the uncertainty in the neutrino position. For the GUP-I scenario, considering terms beyond the plane wave approximation, we have

L^{\text{coh,(I)}}_{ij}=\frac{L_{ij}^{\text{coh,(0)}}}{1+3\beta E_{\nu}^{2}}\,,

where
\Delta x

The IceCube neutrino observatory

\beta_{0}\lesssim 10^{30}.

\Delta P=2\Delta^{(0)}\alpha\sin^{2}(2\theta)E\cos\Delta^{(0)}\sim\alpha E\,.

The current experimental resolution proper of transition probability measurements,
\Delta P\sim 10^{-2}

\alpha E=\frac{\alpha}{\text{GeV}^{-1}}\times\frac{E}{\text{GeV}}\lesssim 10^{-2}\,.

\alpha

\alpha\simeq 8\alpha_{0}\times 10^{-20}\;\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}

\alpha_{0}\lesssim 10^{17}\times\frac{\text{GeV}}{E}\,.

IV
Results

In this letter we have investigated the experimental feasibility of using the low energy neutrino data to probe the phenomenological effects of two GUP scenarios. Whereas experiments are not sensible enough to distinguish between the two cases analyzed, we find that the data can indeed constrain the involved GUP parameters. In Tab.

For oscillation experiments, the quoted bounds are mainly driven by the modifications that the considered GUP scenario yield on the neutrino oscillation pattern computed in the plane wave approximation. These constraints are comparable to the—marginally milder—exclusions obtained by modeling the GUP effects as nonstandard neutrino interactions and strongly outclass the limit obtained from the changes in the oscillation pattern induced by the modified neutrino coherence length. The latter is directly probed by the IceCube result, which yields the tightest constraint on the GUP parameters owning also to the higher peak energy of the observed neutrino spectrum. The energy dependence of the mentioned bounds reflects the fact that the modifications to the uncertainty principle in the considered GUP scenarios are necessarily more prominent at higher energies or, equivalently, at larger momenta for set particle masses. Consequently, the upper bounds on GUP parameters scale inversely with the energy even if the parameters themselves are constants.

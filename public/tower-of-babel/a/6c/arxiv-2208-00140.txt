Line bundles on the moduli space of Lie algebroid connections over a curve
Indranil Biswas, Anoop Singh

Abstract
. We explore algebro-geometric properties of the moduli space of holomorphic Lie algebroid ( ℒ \mathcal{L} ) connections on a compact Riemann surface X X of genus g ≥ 3 g\,\geq\,3 . A smooth compactification of the moduli space of ℒ \mathcal{L} -connections, such that underlying vector bundle is stable, is constructed; the complement of the moduli space inside the compactification is a divisor. A criterion for the numerical effectiveness of the boundary divisor is given. We compute the Picard group of the moduli space, and analyze Lie algebroid Atiyah bundles associated with an ample line bundle. This enables us to conclude that regular functions on the space of certain Lie algebroid connections are constants. Moreover, under some condition, it is shown that the moduli space of ℒ \mathcal{L} -connections does not admit non-constant algebraic functions. Rationally connectedness of the moduli spaces is explored.

The notion of Lie algebroid was introduced by J. Pradines

naturally arises from the properties of space of vector fields on a smooth manifold. The Lie algebroids have been studied in different

The moduli space of Lie algebroid connections has received great attention over the years because this is a natural generalization of the
moduli space of holomorphic, logarithmic and meromorphic connections as well as of decorated vector bundles (see Remark

The Picard group of the moduli space of stable vector bundles over a smooth projective curve has been studied in

Additionally, the Picard group and regular functions have been investigated for the moduli space of logarithmic connections singular over

Algebro-geometric invariants play a crucial role when studying the properties of any moduli space.
In the present article, our aim is to compute algebro-geometric invariants, such as the Picard group, regular function, rational connectedness, of the moduli space of Lie algebroid connections over a compact Riemann surface. We also provide a natural
compactification of the moduli space of Lie algebroid connections whose underlying vector bundle is stable.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
X
be a compact connected Riemann surface of genus
g\,\geq\,2
. Let
T_{X}
and
\Omega^{1}_{X}
respectively denote the holomorphic tangent and cotangent bundle on
X
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
(holomorphic) Lie algebroid
over
X
is a triple

\mathcal{L}\,=\,(L,\,[\cdot,\,\cdot],\,\sharp)
consisting of

(1)

a holomorphic vector bundle
L
on
X
,

(2)

a
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear and skew-symmetric map

[\cdot,\,\cdot]\,:\,L\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}L\,\longrightarrow\,L,

called the Lie bracket,

(3)

a vector bundle homomorphism

\sharp\,:\,L\,\longrightarrow\,T_{X},

called the
anchor map
that
induces a homomorphism of Lie algebras from
the sheaf of sections of
L
to the sheaf of sections of
T_{X}
satisfying the following conditions:

a.

[u,\,[v,\,w]]+[v,\,[w,\,u]]+[w,\,[u,\,v]]\,=\,0
(Jacobi identity),

b.

[u,\,fv]\,=\,f[u,\,v]+\sharp(u)(f)v
(Leibniz identity),

for every local holomorphic sections
u,v,w
of
L
and every local holomorphic function
f
on
X
.

From Definition
2.1
it is clear that the space of sections of
L
has a Lie algebra structure. The rank
\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{L})
and degree

\deg{\mathcal{L}}
of a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{L}
are by definition the rank and the degree of the underlying vector bundle
L
respectively.

By a Lie algebroid we will always mean holomorphic Lie algebroid.

Example 2.2
.

Followings are the well-known examples of Lie algebroids.

(1)

Tangent Lie algebroid:
The holomorphic tangent bundle

T_{X}
is a holomorphic Lie algebroid if we take the
Lie bracket to be the usual Lie bracket operation defined for holomorphic vector fields and take the
anchor map
\sharp
to be the identity map
\mathbf{1}_{T_{X}}
. We denote it by
\mathcal{T}_{X}\,=\,(T_{X},\,[\cdot,\,\cdot],\,\mathbf{1}_{T_{X}})
.

(2)

Log Lie algebroid:
Let
S\,=\,\{x_{1},\,\cdots,\,x_{m}\}
be a finite subset of
X
;
the corresponding reduced effective divisor
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m}
is also denoted
by
S
. Let
T_{X}(-\log S)\,:=\,T_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-S)\subset T_{X}
be the line bundle over
X
consisting of those vector
fields which vanish on
S
. Note
T_{X}(-\log S)
is closed under the Lie bracket operation of vector fields.
Take the anchor map to be the inclusion map

\sharp\,\,:\,\,T_{X}(-\log S)\,\,\hookrightarrow\,\,T_{X}.

Then
T_{X}(-\log S)
is a Lie algebroid which is called the Logarithmic or Log Lie algebroid, and it is denoted by

\mathcal{T}_{X}(-\log S)\,:=\,(T_{X}(-\log S),\,[\cdot,\,\cdot],\,\sharp)
.

(3)

Trivial Lie algebroid:
A holomorphic vector
bundle can be equipped with a Lie algebroid structure
by considering the bracket and anchor maps to be the zero homomorphisms.
Such a Lie algebroid is called the trivial Lie algebroid.
It is denoted by
\mathcal{L}_{0}\,=\,(L,\,0,\,0)
, where
L
is a holomorphic vector bundle over
X
.

We can define a morphism between Lie algebroids that are not over the same compact Riemann surface. There are actually two different
definitions (see, e.g.,
[
20
]
). We will mostly be dealing with the Lie algebroid over a single compact Riemann surface
X
. In that
case, the two definitions coincide.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\,:=\,(L,\,[\cdot,\,\cdot]_{L},\,\sharp_{L})
, and

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\,:=\,(L^{\prime},\,[\cdot,\,\cdot]_{L^{\prime}},\,\sharp_{L^{\prime}})
be two Lie algebroids over
X
. A
Lie algebroid morphism

\Phi\,:\,\mathcal{L}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is a vector bundle morphism

\Phi\,:\,L\,\longrightarrow\,L^{\prime}
which is a
\mathbb{C}
-Lie algebra homomorphism
satisfying the condition that
\sharp_{L^{\prime}}\circ\Phi\,=\,\sharp_{L}
.

We say that
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
are isomorphic if the vector bundle morphism
\Phi
is an isomorphism.

For a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{L}\,=\,(L,\,[\cdot,\,\cdot],\,\sharp)
, the morphism
\sharp\,:\,L\,\longrightarrow\,T_{X}
is a
Lie algebroid morphism between
\mathcal{L}
and the tangent Lie algebroid
\mathcal{T}_{X}
.

Remark 2.4
.

In
[
1
, Theorem 3.18]
, authors have classified the Lie algebroid structures on line bundles over

.
Also, in
[
1
, Corollary 3.19]

admits only two non-isomorphic

(T_{X},\,0,\,0)
and the canonical one given by the Lie bracket of vector fields

\mathcal{T}_{X}\,=\,(T_{X},\,[\cdot,\,\cdot]_{\text{Lie}},\,\sharp\,=\,\mathbf{1}_{T_{X}})
.

Let
\sharp^{*}\,:\,\Omega^{1}_{X}\,\longrightarrow\,L^{*}
be the dual of the anchor map, and let

be the

-derivation defined by

(2.1)

where

\text{d}\,:\,\mathcal{O}_{X}\,\longrightarrow\,\Omega^{1}_{X}

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\,=\,(L,\,[\cdot,\,\cdot],\,\sharp)
be a Lie algebroid over
X
, and let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over

X
. An
\mathcal{L}
-connection on

is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map between the sheaves of holomorphic sections

\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}\,:\,E\,\longrightarrow\,E\otimes L^{*}

\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}(fs)\,=\,f\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}(s)+s\otimes d_{\mathcal{L}}(f),

for all locally defined sections

E
and

\mathcal{O}_{X}
, where

is defined in (
2.1
). The rank and degree of an

-connection

(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})
are respectively the rank and degree of the underlying vector bundle

Using the anchor map
\sharp
, the exterior derivation

can be extended to higher order
exterior powers of

such that the composition of
successive derivations vanishes (for more details see
[

Extend

-derivation

d^{p}_{\mathcal{L}}(\alpha)(a_{1},\,\cdots,\,a_{p+1})\,=\,\sum_{i=1}^{p+1}(-i)^{i+1}\sharp a_{i}\alpha(a_{1},\,\cdots,\,\widehat{a_{i}},\,\cdots,\,a_{p+1})

where

,

Thus, we get a complex

Let

-connection on

Now define the

As in the classical case,

gives a global holomorphic section of

\mathcal{O}_{X}

An

The

Under the conditions on

From Serre duality,

\mathrm{at}_{\mathcal{L}}(E)\,\in\,\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\,\,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes L^{*})\,=\,\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\,\,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes L\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X})^{*}\,=\,0.

3.
Moduli space of
\mathcal{L}
-connections

In this section, we describe the moduli space of Lie
algebroid connections over
X
. Henceforth, we assume that the rank of Lie algebroid

\mathcal{L}\,=\,(L,\,[\cdot,\,\cdot],\,\sharp)
is one, that is,
L
is a line bundle.

Definition 3.1
.

An
\mathcal{L}
-connection
(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})
is said to semistable (respectively, stable) if for every non-zero proper
subbundle
F
of
E
, which is invariant under

\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}
, that is,
\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}(F)\,\subset\,F\otimes L^{*}
, the inequality

\mu(F)\,\leq\,\mu(E)\ \ \,\,(\text{respectively, }\ \,\mu(F)\,<\,\mu(E))

holds, where
\mu(E)
denotes the slope of
E
defined above.

A morphism between
\mathcal{L}
-connections
(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})
and
(E^{\prime},\,\nabla^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}})
is a morphism of vector bundles

\phi\,:\,E\,\longrightarrow\,E^{\prime}

such that the following diagram is commutative:

(3.1)

\textstyle{E\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{E\otimes L^{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{E^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{E^{\prime}\otimes L^{*}.}

We say that

and

are isomorphic if

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lie algebroid over
X
. Let

(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})
and
(E^{\prime},\,\nabla^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}})
be semistable
\mathcal{L}
–connections.
Then the following statements hold:

(1)

Suppose
(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})
and
(E^{\prime},\,\nabla^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}})
are stable and
\mu(E)\,=\,\mu(E^{\prime}).

If

\phi\,:\,(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})\,\longrightarrow\,(E^{\prime},\,\nabla^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}})

is a non-zero morphism of
\mathcal{L}
–connections, then
\phi
is an isomorphism.

(2)

If
(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})
is stable, then the only endomorphism of
(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})
are constant scalar
multiplications.

Proof.

(1)

Note that
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)\,\subset\,E
is a
\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}
–invariant coherent
analytic subsheaf of
E
. Since
(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})
is stable, we
have
\mu(\mathrm{Ker}(\phi))\,<\,\mu(E)
. Since
\phi\,\neq\,0
, we have
\mathrm{Im}(\phi)\,\neq\,0
.

Consider the Kernel-Image short exact sequence

0\,\longrightarrow\,\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)\,\longrightarrow\,E\,\longrightarrow\,\mathrm{Im}(\phi)\,\longrightarrow\,0.

Then,
\mu(E)\,<\,\mu(\mathrm{Im}(\phi))
.
Next, consider the Image-Coimage short exact sequence

0\,\longrightarrow\,\mathrm{Im}(\phi)\,\longrightarrow\,E^{\prime}\,\longrightarrow\,E^{\prime}/\mathrm{Im}(\phi)\,\longrightarrow\,0.

Note that
\mathrm{Im}(\Phi)
is a
\nabla^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}
–invariant coherent analytic subsheaf of
E^{\prime}
. Since
(E^{\prime},\,\nabla^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}})
is stable, we have
\mu(\mathrm{Im}(\phi))\,<\,\mu(E^{\prime})
. Thus, we get that

\mu(E)\,<\,\mu(E^{\prime})
, which contradicts the assumption that

\mu(E)=\mu(E^{\prime})
. Therefore,
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi)=0
and

\mathrm{Im}(\phi)\,=\,E^{\prime}
.

(2)

Let
\phi\,:\,(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})\,\longrightarrow\,(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})
be an endomorphism.
Let
x\,\in\,X
, and let
\alpha\,\in\,\mathbb{C}
be an eigenvalue of the linear map
\phi(x)\,:\,E_{x}\,\longrightarrow\,E_{x}
. Note that since
X
is a compact connected Riemann surface, it does not admit any non-constant holomorphic
function. Therefore the eigenvalues of
\phi
and also their multiplicities are independent of
x\,\in\,X
.
Since
\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}
is
\mathbb{C}
-linear,

\phi-\alpha\mathbf{1}_{E}
is an endomorphism of
(E,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})
, that is,

\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}\circ(\phi-\alpha\mathbf{1}_{E})\,\,=\,\,(\phi-\alpha\mathbf{1}_{E})\otimes\mathbf{1}_{L^{*}}\circ\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}.

As
(E,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})
is stable, from Part (
1
) it follows that
\phi-\alpha\mathbf{1}_{E}
is either a zero morphism or
it is an isomorphism. Given that
\alpha
is an eigenvalue of
\phi(x)
, the kernel of

\phi-\alpha\mathbf{1}_{E}
is non-trivial. Thus,
\phi\,=\,\alpha\mathbf{1}_{E}
, where
\alpha\,\in\,\mathbb{C}
.

This completes the proof.
∎

Fix integers

and

(3)

Thus, the formalism of Lie algebroid connections can be effectively applied to uniformly address a wide array of moduli spaces

Let

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)\,\,\subset\,\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)

denote the moduli space of
\mathcal{L}

(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})

We recall the definition of a torsor, and show that

Definition 4.1
.

Let
M
be a connected complex algebraic variety. Let
\pi\,:\,\mathcal{V}\,\longrightarrow\,M
, be an algebraic vector bundle.
A
\mathcal{V}
–
torsor
on
M
is an algebraic fiber bundle
p\,:\,Z\,\longrightarrow\,M
,
and an algebraic map from the fiber product

\varphi\,:\,Z\times_{M}\mathcal{V}\,\longrightarrow\,Z

such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

p\circ\varphi\,=\,p\circ p_{Z}
, where
p_{Z}
is the
natural projection of
Z\times_{M}\mathcal{V}
to
Z
;

(2)

the map
Z\times_{M}\mathcal{V}\,\longrightarrow\,Z\times_{M}Z

defined by
p_{Z}\times\varphi
is an isomorphism;

(3)

\varphi(\varphi(z,\,v),\,w)\,=\,\varphi(z,\,v+w)
for all
z\,\in\,Z
and
v,\,w\,\in\,\mathcal{V}
such that
\pi(v)\,=\,\pi(w)\,=\,p(z)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The isomorphic classes of
\mathcal{V}
–torsors over
M
are
parametrized by
\mathrm{H}^{1}(M,\,\mathcal{V})
.

Proof.

Let
p\,:\,Z\,\longrightarrow\,M
be a
\mathcal{V}
–torsor. Let
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a covering of
M
by open sets such
that there are sections

\sigma_{i}\,:\,U_{i}\,\longrightarrow\,Z{|_{U_{i}}}

for all
i\,\in\,I
. Since
\mathcal{V}
acts on
Z
freely and transitively on the fibers, it follows that

\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j}
is a section of
\mathcal{V}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}
. Then
\{\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j}\}_{i,j\in I}

forms a
1
-cocycle with values in
\mathcal{V}
, and hence defines a class in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(M,\,\,\mathcal{V})
. This class
is trivial if and only if
Z
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{V}
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\pi\,:\,\mathcal{P}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{U}(r,d)
be the algebraic vector bundle defined in (
3.6
). Then, the fiber bundle

p\,:\,\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{U}(r,d)
defined in (
1.1
) is a
\mathcal{P}
–torsor over

\mathcal{U}(r,d)
.

Proof.

Let
(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})
and
(E,\,\nabla^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}})
be two
\mathcal{L}
–connections on
E
.
Then

\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}-\nabla^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}\,\in\,\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\,\,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes L^{*}).

Conversely, given any
\omega\,\in\,\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes L^{*})
, it is evident that
\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}+\omega
is again an

\mathcal{L}
–connection on
E
. Thus,
p^{-1}(E)\,\subset\,\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)
is an affine space modelled over the vector space
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\,\,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes L^{*})

Note that the fiber of the bundle
\pi\,:\,\mathcal{P}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{U}(r,d)
over
E
is

\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\,\,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes L^{*})
. Therefore, we get a natural
action of
\pi^{-1}(E)
on

,
that is, a map

\pi^{-1}(E)\times p^{-1}(E)\,\longrightarrow\,p^{-1}(E)

defined by
(\omega,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})\,\longmapsto\,\omega+\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}
.
This action on the fiber is free and transitive.
It induces a morphism on the fiber products

\varphi\,:\,\mathcal{P}\times_{\mathcal{U}(r,d)}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}{(r,d)}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}{(r,d)},

which satisfies the above conditions in the definition of

∎

Let
\mathcal{C}onn_{\mathcal{L}}(E)
denote the space of all
\mathcal{L}
–connections
\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}
on
E
such that
(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})

is stable. Note that
\mathcal{C}onn_{\mathcal{L}}(E)
is an affine space modelled over the vector space
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes L^{*})

Given an automorphism
\Phi

E
and an
\mathcal{L}
–connection
\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}
on

\mathbb{C}
–linear morphism

-connection on

In fact,

(\nabla_{\mathcal{L}},\,\Phi)\,\,\longmapsto\,\,\Phi\otimes\mathbf{1}_{L^{*}}\circ\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}\circ\Phi^{-1}

defines a natural action of
\text{Aut}(E)
on
\mathcal{C}onn_{\mathcal{L}}(E)

We would like to compute the dimension of the quotient
space
\mathcal{C}onn_{\mathcal{L}}(E)/\text{Aut}(E)
, that
parametrizes all isomorphic
\mathcal{L}
-connections on

The Lie algebra of the holomorphic automorphism group

is

\text{dim}~\text{Aut}(E)=\text{dim}~\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\,\mathrm{End}(E)).

Choose any
\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}\,\in\,\mathcal{C}onn_{\mathcal{L}}(E)

(
2
), the isotropy subgroup

\text{Aut}(E)_{\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}}\,=\,\{\Phi\,\in\,\text{Aut}(E)\,\,\big|\,\,\Phi\otimes\mathbf{1}_{L^{*}}\circ\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}\circ\Phi^{-1}\,=\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}\}

of

is the group of scalar

.
Then, the dimension of the space
\mathcal{C}onn_{\mathcal{L}}(E)/\text{Aut}(E)
is

(4.2)

\dim\,\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\,\,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes L^{*})-\dim\,\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\,\,\mathrm{End}(E))+1.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
E
be a stable vector bundle on
X
of rank
r
and degree
d
. Then

is an affine space modelled on the vector space
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\,\,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes L^{*})

Proof.

Note that when

\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\,\mathrm{End}(E))\,=\,\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{E}
, that is, automorphisms of
E
are just non-zero

\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\,\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes L^{*})

This is the case with the fibers of the morphism

In view of (

), it is enough to compute the dimension of

Given that

\mathrm{End}(E)\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}\otimes L

\,=\,r^{2}(\deg{L^{*}}-g+1).

This completes the proof.

constructed in (
4.6
) and
(
4.7
) respectively, are isomorphisms.

Proof.

Let
Z\,:=\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)\setminus\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)
, that is,
Z
is the locus of those
\mathcal{L}
–connections
such that the underlying vector bundle is not stable.
Then, from
[
1
, Lemma 7.1]
, we have

\text{codim}(Z,\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d))\,\,\geq\,\,(g-1)(r-1).

Thus, for
g\,\geq\,3
and
r\,\geq\,2
, we have

\text{codim}(Z,\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d))\,\geq\,2,

and hence the homomorphism
\iota^{*}
in (
4.6
) is an isomorphism.

Next, to show that
p^{*}
in (
4.7
) is an isomorphism, first we show that
p^{*}
is injective.
Let
\eta\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{U}(r,d)
be an algebraic line bundle such that
p^{*}\eta
is a trivial line bundle
over
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)
.
Since
p^{*}\eta
is a trivial line bundle, we get a
nowhere vanishing section of
p^{*}\eta
over
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)
. Now, fix a nowhere vanishing section

t\,\in\,\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d),\,\,p^{*}\eta),

and choose a
point
E\,\in\,\mathcal{U}(r,d)
. Then, from the following commutative diagram

(4.8)

\textstyle{p^{*}\eta\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\widetilde{p}}
\textstyle{\eta\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{\mathcal{U}(r,d)}

it follows that

t|_{p^{-1}(E)}\,\,:p^{-1}(E)\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,\eta(E)

is nowhere vanishing map. Observe that
p^{-1}(E)\,\cong\,\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
\eta(E)\,\cong\,\mathbb{C}
, where
N\,=\,r^{2}(\deg L^{*}-g+1)
(see Lemma
4.4
). Further, any nowhere vanishing algebraic function on an affine space

\mathbb{C}^{N}
is a constant function, that is,
t|_{p^{-1}(E)}
is a constant
function, and therefore, it corresponds to a non-zero vector
\alpha_{E}\,\in\,\eta(E)
.
Since
t
is constant on each fiber of
p
, the trivialization
t
of
p^{*}\eta
descends to a trivialization of the
line bundle
\eta
over
\mathcal{U}(r,d)
, and hence a nowhere vanishing
section of
\eta
over
\mathcal{U}(r,d)
is produced. Thus,
\eta
is a trivial line bundle
over
\mathcal{U}(r,d)
.

It remains to show that
p^{*}
is a surjective morphism. Let

\vartheta\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)
be an algebraic line
bundle. Since
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F})
is a smooth compactification of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}(r,d)
, we can extend
\vartheta
to a line bundle

\vartheta^{\prime}
over
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F})
.
Now, from the morphism

\widetilde{\Phi}\,:\,\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F})\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{U}(r,d)

induced by the morphism in (
4.3
), we have

(4.9)

\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}))\,\cong\,\widetilde{\Phi}^{*}\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{U}(r,d))\oplus\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F})}(1).

Consequently,

(4.10)

\vartheta^{\prime}\,=\,\widetilde{\Phi}^{*}\Lambda\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F})}(m),

where
\Lambda
is a line bundle over
\mathcal{U}(r,d)
and

m\,\in\,\mathbb{Z}
. Since
{\bf H}\,=\,\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F})\setminus\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)
in (
4.4
) is the
hyperplane at infinity, using (
4.9
), the line bundle

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F})}({\bf H})
associated to the divisor
{\bf H}
can be expressed as

(4.11)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F})}({\bf H})\,=\,\widetilde{\Phi}^{*}\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F})}(1),

where
\Gamma
is a line bundle over
\mathcal{U}(r,d)
.
Now, from (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we get that

\vartheta^{\prime}\,=\,\widetilde{\Phi}^{*}(\Lambda\otimes(\Gamma^{*})^{\otimes m})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F})}(m{\bf H}),

where
\Gamma^{*}
denotes the dual of
\Gamma
.
Since the restriction of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F})}({\bf H})
to the compliment

\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F})\setminus{\bf H}\,=\,\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)

is trivial, and the restriction of
\widetilde{\Phi}
to
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)

is the map

defined in (

), we conclude that

\vartheta\,\,=\,\,p^{*}(\Lambda\otimes(\Gamma^{*})^{\otimes m}).

This completes the proof of the theorem.

5.
the moduli space with fixed determinant

In this section we explore the moduli space of Lie algebroid connections with a fixed determinant. Let
\xi
be a holomorphic line bundle
over
X
of degree
d
such that

is coprime to
r
. Fix a
\mathcal{L}
–connection

\nabla^{\xi}_{\mathcal{L}}\,\,:\,\,\xi\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,\xi\otimes\mathcal{L}^{*}

on
\xi
. Given an
\mathcal{L}
–connection
\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}
on
E
of rank

\mathcal{L}
–connection
\mathrm{tr}(\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})

Consider the moduli space

Let

Let

\mathcal{U}(r,\xi)

(r^{2}-1)(g-1)
. We have a natural projection

(5.3)

q\,:\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}(r,\xi)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{U}(r,\xi)

(E,\,\nabla_{\mathcal{L}})\,\longmapsto\,E
.

Next, consider the moduli space
\mathcal{N}_{L^{*}}(r,d)
of
rank

, degree

, semi-stable

–twisted Higgs bundles as described above.

Let

\mathcal{P}_{\xi}

For any general

of

Let

6.
Rationally connectedness and Divisor at infinity

In
[
16
]
, rationality of moduli spaces of vector bundles over a smooth projective curve has been studied. Also, the rationality and
the rationally connectedness of the moduli space of logarithmic connections has been discussed in
[
31
]
.

Motivated by this, there is a natural question whether the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,\xi)
are rational.
For the theory of
rational varieties, we refer
[
17
]
.
In this section, we show that the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)
is not rational and the moduli space

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,\xi)
is rationally connected.

Recall that a smooth complex variety
V

is said to be rationally connected if any two general points on
V

can be connected by a rational curve in
V
. A Rational variety is always rationally connected. But the converse is not true. The following lemma is an easy consequence of the definition.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
f\,:\,\mathcal{Y}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{X}
be a dominant rational map of complex
algebraic varieties with
\mathcal{Y}
rationally connected. Then,
\mathcal{X}
is rationally connected.

Theorem 6.2

(
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

The moduli space

\mathcal{U}(r,d)
is birational to
J(X)\times\mathbb{A}^{(n^{2}-1)(g-1)}
, where
J(X)
is the Jacobian of
X
.

Note that
J(X)
is not rationally connected,
because it does not contain any rational curve.
Therefore,
\mathcal{U}(r,d)
is not rationally connected.

Proposition 6.3
.

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)
is not rational.

Proof.

We show that the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)
is not rationally connected. Let

p\,:\,\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}(r,d)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{U}(r,d)

be the morphism of varieties defined in (
4
).
Suppose that
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)
is rationally connected. Then, from Lemma
6.1
,

\mathcal{U}(r,d)
is rationally connected, which is not true.
Since
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{lc}(n,d)
is an open dense subset of

\mathcal{M}_{lc}(n,d)
, it follows that
\mathcal{M}_{lc}(n,d)
is not rational.
∎

Lemma 6.4

(
[
15
, Corollary 1.3]
)
.

Let
f\,:\,\mathcal{X}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{Y}
be any dominant morphism
of complex varieties. If
\mathcal{Y}
and the general
fiber of
f
are rationally connected, then
\mathcal{X}

is rationally connected.

Proposition 6.5
.

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,\xi)
is rationally connected.

Proof.

Consider the dominant morphism

q\,:\,\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,\xi)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{U}(r,\xi)

defined in (
5.3
). As observed earlier, every
fiber of
q
is an affine space and hence it is rationally
connected. Since
\mathcal{U}(r,\xi)
is rationally connected,
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,\xi)
is
rationally connected, which follows from Lemma
6.4
.
Now rationally connectedness is a birational invariant, and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,\xi)
is a dense open subset of

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,\xi)
.
∎

Therefore, we have a natural question.

Question 6.6
.

Is the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,\xi)
rational?

Next, we discuss the numerical effectiveness of the divisor at infinity. In view of Proposition
5.2
, we have a compactification

\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi})
of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,\xi)
such that the complement
{\bf H_{0}}\,:=\,\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi})\setminus\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,\xi)
is the hyperplane at infinity, and we call
{\bf H_{0}}
the divisor at infinity.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
{\bf H_{0}}\,:=\,\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi})\setminus\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,\xi)
be the smooth divisor.
Then,
the divisor
{\bf H_{0}}
is numerically effective if and only if the vector bundle bundle
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\xi}
over the moduli
space
\mathcal{U}(r,\xi)
is numerically effective.

Proof.

Recall that the vector bundle
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\xi}
is
numerically effective if and only if the tautological
line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\xi})}(1)
is
numerically effective.
Let

\Psi\,:\,\mathcal{F}_{\xi}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{U}(r,\xi)

be the vector bundle in Proposition
5.2
. Then,
the divisor
{\bf H_{0}}
is numerically effective if and only if the restriction of the line bundle

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi})}({\bf H_{0}})
to
{\bf H_{0}}
is numerically effective.

First we show that the divisor
\bf{H}_{0}
is canonically
isomorphic to projective bundle
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\xi})
.
Let

\widetilde{\Psi}\,:\,\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi})\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{U}(r,\xi)

be the projective bundle. Let
E\,\in\,\mathcal{U}(r,\xi)
, and

(6.1)

\theta\,\in\,\widetilde{\Psi}^{-1}(E)\cap\bf{H}_{0}\,\subset\,\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi}).

Then,
\theta
represents a hyperplane in the fiber

\mathcal{F}_{\xi}(E)\,=\,q^{-1}(E)^{\vee}
of the vector bundle
\mathcal{F}_{\xi}
, where
q
is defined in
(
5.3
). Let
H_{\theta}
denote this hyperplane represented by
\theta
.
Note that
H_{\theta}\,\subset\,q^{-1}(E)^{\vee}
and
q^{-1}(E)
is the affine space modelled
over the vector space
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\,\mathrm{ad}(E)\otimes L^{*})

which is a fiber of the vector bundle
\mathcal{P}_{\xi}
over
E
.
Therefore
for any
f\,\in\,H_{\theta}
, that is, for an affine linear map
f\,:\,q^{-1}(E)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{C}
, we have

\text{d}f\in(\mathcal{P}_{\xi}(E))^{*}\,=\,\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\xi}(E).

Since
\theta\,\in\,\bf{H}_{0}
, and
H_{\theta}
is a hyperplane, the subspace of
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\xi}(E)

generated by
\{\text{d}f\}_{f\in H_{\theta}}
is a
hyperplane in
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\xi}(E)
. Let
\widetilde{\theta}\,\in\,\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\xi})
denote this hyperplane.
Thus, we get a canonical isomorphism

(6.2)

{\bf H}_{0}\,\cong\,\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\xi})

by sending
\theta
to
\widetilde{\theta}
.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi})/{\bf H_{0}}}
denote the normal
bundle of the divisor
{\bf H_{0}}\,\subset\,\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi})
.
Note that from Poincaré adjunction formula we have the following:

(6.3)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi})}({\bf H_{0}})|_{\bf H_{0}}\,\cong\,\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi})/{\bf H_{0}}}.

So,
{\bf H_{0}}
is numerically effective if and only if the normal bundle
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi})/{\bf H_{0}}}

is numerically effective.

Thus, to prove the proposition it is enough to show that
the normal bundle
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi})/\bf{H}_{0}}
is canonically isomorphic to the tautological
line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\xi})}(1)
.

Next, note that the fiber
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi})/{\bf H_{0}}}(\theta)
of the normal bundle
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi})/{\bf H_{0}}}

is canonically isomorphic to the quotient
\mathcal{F}_{\xi}(E)/H_{\theta}
.
Consider the morphism

\mathcal{F}_{\xi}(E)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\xi}(E)

between vector spaces defined by
f\,\longmapsto\,\text{d}f
.
Since the image of the hyperplane
H_{\theta}
is contained in
\widetilde{\theta}
, we have a well defined morphism on quotients

\mathcal{F}_{\xi}(E)/H_{\theta}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\xi}(E)/\widetilde{\theta},

which is an isomorphism. Recall that the fiber of the tautological line bundle

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\xi})}(1)
at
E
is canonically
identified with the quotient
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\xi}(E)/\widetilde{\theta}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

A similar result can be proved for the smooth divisor on the compactification of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}(r,d)

{\bf H}\,:=\,\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F})\setminus\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(r,d)
be the smooth divisor at infinity, where
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{F})
is the compactification of
the moduli space

as in Theorem

Proposition 6.8

The smooth divisor
\bf{H}
is numerically effective
if and only if the vector bundle
\mathcal{P}^{*}
is numerically effective, where
\mathcal{P}^{*}
is the dual of the vector bundle defined in (

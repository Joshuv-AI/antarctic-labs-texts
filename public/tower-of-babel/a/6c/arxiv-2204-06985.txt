The average Euler-genus of the vertex-amalgamation of signed graphs
Yichao Chen

Abstract
. In this paper, we first generalize a theorem for counting the number of faces of an oriented embedding of a graph that passing through a given cut-edge set [S. Stahl, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 259 (1980), 129–145] to all surfaces. Then we extend Stahl’s bounds for the average genus of the vertex-amalgamation of graphs [S. Stahl, Discrete Math. 142 (1995), 235–245] to signed graphs.

1.
Introduction

A graph
G
is often permitted to have both loops and multiple edges in graph embeddings. Since the genus is a homeomorphic invariant, we suppose the graphs in the paper are simple. It is well known that all closed surfaces are classified into the
orientable surfaces

O_{i}
, with
i
handles
(i\geq 0)
, and the
non-orientable surfaces

N_{j}
, with
j
crosscaps
(j>0)
. We will use
S
to denote a surface without regard to orientability. An
embedding
of
G
into a closed surface
S
is a
cellular embedding
. If a graph
G
has an embedding in a surface
S
with

n
vertices,
m
edges and
r
faces, the following classical
Euler’s formula
tell us that

n-m+r=2-\gamma^{E},

where
\gamma^{E}
is the
Euler-genus
of the surface
S
and

\gamma^{E}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2k,&\hbox{If $S=O_{k}$;}\\
h,&\hbox{If $S=N_{h}$.}\end{array}\right.

A
rotation at a vertex

v
of a graph
G
is a cyclic ordering of the edge-ends incident at
v
. A
rotation system

R
of a graph
G
is an assignment of a rotation at every vertex of
G
. There is an one-to-one correspondence between the orientable embeddings and rotation systems.

An
embedding
of a graph
G
on an arbitrary surface
S
can be described combinatorially by a
signed rotation system
(or
a general rotation system)
(R,\sigma),
where
R
is a rotation system of
G
and
\sigma
is the
twist-indicator
, i.e., if
\sigma(e)=-1
, then the edge
e
is twisted; otherwise
\sigma(e)=1
and
e
is untwisted. A
switch
of a signed rotation system at a vertex
v
means reversing the rotation about
v
and changing the sign on each of its incident edges. Two signed rotation system are
equivalent
if one can be transformed into another by a sequence of switches. For terms not defined in this paper, we refer to
[
16
]
.

A
permutation-partition pair

(P,\Pi)
consists of an arbitrary permutation
P
and an arbitrary partition
\Pi,
both defined over some common underlying set
S.
It is a combinatorial generalization of graphs and graph embeddings. The notion of a permutation-partition pair was introduced by Stahl in
[
28
]
. It was also independently rediscovered in
[
2
,
3
]
, called IDS by Archdeacon. For undefined
terminologies on
(P,\Pi)
, see
[
30
]
.

A
signed graph

\Sigma=(G,\sigma)
is a graph
G
together with a mapping
\sigma
which assigns
+1
or
-1
to each edge of the graph
G.
If
\sigma(e)=1,
we call the edge
positive
, otherwise
\sigma(e)=-1
and the edge is
negative
. An embedding of a signed graph
\Sigma=(G,\sigma)
can be described combinatorially by a
signed rotation system

(R,\sigma),
where
R
is the rotation system of
G,
and
\sigma
can be thought of as the twist-indicator. There are many researches on signed graph embeddings, see
[
9
,
21
,
22
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
]
, etc.

Two embeddings
(R_{1},\sigma),
and
(R_{2},\sigma)
of
\Sigma=(G,\sigma)
are equivalent if their rotation system
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are the same. Let the
sample space

\Omega
consist of all inequivalent embeddings
\Sigma
. The
Euler-genus random variable

\gamma^{E}
is define as

\gamma^{E}:\Omega\longrightarrow\{0,1,\ldots,\}.

If
(R,\sigma)
embeds
\Sigma
on
S_{i}
, then
\gamma^{E}(R,\sigma)=i.
The
average Euler-genus

\gamma_{avg}^{E}(\Sigma)
of a signed graph

is the
expected value of the Euler-genus random variable, over all labeled
embeddings of
\Sigma,
using the uniform distribution

P(\Sigma)=\frac{1}{\prod_{v\in V(G)}(d_{v}-1)!},

i.e.,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\gamma_{avg}^{E}(\Sigma)=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\Omega}\gamma^{E}(R,\sigma)}{\prod_{v\in V(G)}(d_{v}-1)!}.

Note that the average genus of a single graph was introduced in

by

.
We may refer to

Given an oriented embedding of a graph
G
, Stahl
[
27
]
proved a local counting theorem that provided an expression for the number of distinct faces which contain a specific vertex on their boundary in terms of the orbits of the product of two permutations. The two permutations were derived from Edmonds’ rotation systems. He then used this expression to give new proofs of several classical theorems in topological graph theory, such as the Duke’s interpolation theorem and the additivity theorem for the genus of Battle, Harary, Kodama, and Youngs. The local counting theorem was also independently rediscovered, in a more geometrical form, in
[
17
,
18
]
, etc. For other works related to the local counting theorem, see
[
28
,
29
,
32
,
11
]
, etc.

Let
U
be a subset of the vertex set
V(G)
of a graph
G
. Then the
boundary
of
U
, denoted by
\partial(U)
, is the set of oriented edges of
G
that emanate from a vertex of
U
and terminate at a vertex of
V(G)-U
. In
[
28
]
, Stahl generalized the local counting theorem to the case of containing edges of
\partial U
for an oriented embedding. Given an embedding of
G
on an arbitrary surface
S
, here we obtain an expression for the number of distinct faces which contain the edges of
\partial U.
Our result can be thought of as a generalization of Stahl’s theorem to nonorientable embeddings.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we prove the counting theorem for graph embeddings. Upper and lower bounds for the average Euler-genus of the vertex-amalgamation of signed graphs is given in Section 3.

2.
The counting theorem

2.1.
Combinatorial embeddings of graphs

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with vertex set
V
and edge set
E.
Suppose that
K=\{1,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}
is the
Klein four-group
. Given an edge
a\in E,
we introduce its
two sides
and
two ends
. Assume
a
itself at one end and on one side. Let
\alpha
be the permutation that interchanges symbols at the same end but different sides of an edge, for each edge. Let
\beta
be the permutation that interchanges symbols at the same side but different ends of an edge, for each edge. We call the set
Ka=\{a,\alpha a,\beta a,\gamma a\}
a
quadricell
.

An embedding
(R,\sigma)
of
G
on some surfaces induces a
bi-rotation system

P
on
V
such that at each vertex, all its incident semi-edges are with a cyclic order, called a
bi-rotation
. Since each edge is considered as a quadricell, the bi-rotation at a vertex
v
is

\left\{(a,Pa,\ldots,P^{(m-1)}a),(\alpha a,\alpha P^{-1}a,\ldots,\alpha P^{-(m-1)}a)\right\}.

We denote by
\langle P,\gamma\rangle
the group of permutations
P,\gamma
generated by them. Tutte provided an axiomatization for any embedding of
G
, as stated in the following result of
Tutte.

An
embedding
M
(or a map)
is an ordered triple
(\alpha,\beta,P)
of permutations acting on a set
S
of
4m
elements, such that

(1)

for any
a\in S,
then
a,\alpha a,\beta a,\gamma a
are distinct.

(2)

\alpha P=P\alpha^{-1}
.

(3)

for each
a\in S
, the orbits of
P
through
a
and
\alpha a
are distinct.

(4)

\langle P,\gamma\rangle
acts transitively on
S.

It’s known that if the action of
\langle P,\gamma\rangle
on
S
is transitive, then
M
is non-orientable, otherwise there are precisely two orbits, and
M
is orientable. For more on Tutte’s axiomatization of graph embeddings, see
[
20
,
35
]
, etc.

whose boundary contains

Again, here’s an intuitive explanation for

Here, we give a proof that

is indeed a permutation of

We only need to give a proof for the case

into a vertex

By
definition,
Ext_{R,U}(e)=f^{r}
for some

Thus
Ext_{R,U}(e)\in\partial(U).

We now go to show that
Ext_{R,U}
is injective. If there exit two different edges
e,f\in\partial(U)
such that
Ext_{R,U}(e)=Ext_{R,U}(f)
. By symmetry, we can assume that
x(e)
is greater than or equal to
x(f).
Thus

\gamma[P\gamma]^{x(e)-x(f)-1}(e^{r_{\delta}})=P^{-1}(f^{r_{\delta}}).

Note that
x(e)-x(f)-1
is less than
x(e).

If
x(e)=x(f)
, then

P^{-1}(f^{r_{\delta}})=P^{-1}(e^{r_{\delta}}),

thus
f=e
which contradicts
e\neq f
.

If
x(e)=x(f)+1
, then

P\gamma(e^{r_{\delta}})=(f^{r_{\delta}}).

This means
e,f
lie on the boundary of the same face and
e,f
are adjacent in the face, this contradicts that
G
is a simple graph, again it’s impossible.

If
x(e)-x(f)\geq 2
, then
\gamma[P\gamma]^{x(e)-x(f)-1}(e^{r_{\delta}})=P^{-1}(f^{r_{\delta}})
. If
f^{r_{\delta}}=f^{r_{+}},
then the initial point of

is the same as that of

and is therefore in
\partial(U),
which contradicts the minimality of

Otherwise
\gamma[P\gamma]^{x(e)-x(f)+1}(e^{r_{-}})=P\gamma(f^{r_{-}})=Pf^{l_{+}}
, again the initial point of
P(f^{l_{+}})
is the same as that of

\partial(U).
This also implies that
x(f)\geq 2
. Thus
x(e)-x(f)+1<x(e)
, it’s impossible.

In summary,
Ext_{R,U}
is a permutation of
\partial(U).
Similarly, we can also prove that
Int_{R,U}
is a permutation of
\partial(U).

Given a permutation
P
, we denote by
||P||
the number of orbits in the permutation
P
. We have the following counting theorem for a graph embedding.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
U
is a set of vertices of a graph
G,
and let
(R,\sigma)
be a signed rotation system of
G
with
\sigma(e)=1
for any
e\in\partial(U).
Then the number of faces of the embedding
\iota_{(R,\sigma)}
whose boundaries contain arcs of
\partial(U)
is
||Ext_{R,U}\circ Int_{R,U}||.

Proof.

Note that
||Int_{R,U}\circ Ext_{R,U}||=||Ext_{R,U}\circ Int_{R,U}||,
we prove that the number of faces of the embedding
\iota_{(R,\sigma)}
whose oriented boundaries contain arcs of
\partial(U)
is
||Int_{R,U}\circ Ext_{R,U}||.
Let
e=Ext_{R,U}(d),
and let
f=Int_{R,U}(e)
. Assume
Int_{R,U}(e)=\gamma[\gamma P]^{x(e)}(e^{l_{\delta}}).
Then

Int_{R,U}\circ Ext_{R,U}(d)=\gamma[\gamma P]^{x(e)}\gamma(P\gamma)^{y(d)}(d^{r_{\delta}})=[P\gamma]^{x(e)+y(d)}(d^{r_{\delta}}).

Since
d
and
[P\gamma]^{x(e)+y(d)}(d^{r_{\delta}})
belong to
\partial(U).
We have the following claim.

Claim:

x(e)+y(d)
is the least such integer that
[P\gamma]^{x(e)+y(d)}(d^{r_{\delta}})=f^{l}
for
f\in\partial(U).

Suppose that
[P\gamma]^{m}(d^{r_{\delta}})=g^{l}
for some
g\in\partial(U).
We have
\gamma[P\gamma]^{m}(d^{r_{\delta}})=g^{r},
namely,

[\gamma P]^{m-y(d)}\gamma[P\gamma]^{y(d)}(d^{r_{\delta}})=g^{r},

for some
g\in\partial(U).

Since
e=Ext_{R,U}(d)=\gamma[P\gamma]^{y(d)}(d^{r_{\delta}})
, hence we have
[\gamma P]^{m-y(d)}(e)=g^{r},
for some
g\in\partial(U).
This implies that
m-y(d)\geq x(e),
i.e.,
m\geq x(e)+y(d).

By the claim, the orbits of

Ext_{R,U}\circ Int_{R,U}
are obtained from those of the faces
P\gamma
of the embedding
\iota_{(R,\sigma)}
by
deleting all the edges not in
\partial(U).
The theorem follows.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Sigma^{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,k,
be connected signed graphs, and let

U=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{l}\}
be an independent set of vertices of
\Sigma^{i}
and
\Sigma=\bigcup_{U}\Sigma_{i}.
Suppose that

d_{\Sigma}(u_{j})=d_{j}

and

d_{\Sigma^{i}}(u_{j})=d_{i,j},\ i=1,\ldots,k,j=1,\ldots,l.

Then

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}h_{d_{i}}\leq\gamma_{avg}^{E}(\Sigma)-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{avg}^{E}(\Sigma^{i})-(k-1)(l-2)\leq\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{l}h_{d_{i,j}}.

Proof.

We denote
\rho_{avg}(\Sigma)
the average number of faces in the random embedding of
\Sigma
into
S_{i}
with Euler-genus
i.
From the Euler formula,

|V(\Sigma)|-|E(\Sigma)|+\rho_{avg}(\Sigma)=2-\gamma_{avg}^{E}(\Sigma),

thus we only need to prove the following formula

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{l}h_{d_{i,j}}\leq\rho_{avg}(\Sigma)-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{avg}(\Sigma^{i})\leq\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{d_{i}}.

If
R
is a rotation system of
G,
then
(R,\sigma)
is a signed rotation system of
\Sigma=(G,\sigma).
For
1\leq i\leq k
, let
R^{i}
be the induced rotation system of
G
on
G^{i}
, i.e.,
\Sigma^{i}=(G^{i},R^{i}).

By Theorem
2.1
,

\displaystyle\rho(\Sigma,R)

\displaystyle=||Ext_{(R,U)}\circ Int_{(R,U)}||+|F|,

\displaystyle\rho(\Sigma^{i},R^{i})

\displaystyle=||Ext_{(R^{i},U)}\circ Int_{(R^{i},U)}||+|F^{i}|,\ \ (1\leq i\leq k).

where
F
(
F^{i}
) consists of all the faces of
(R,\sigma)
(
(R^{i},\sigma)
)that contain no arcs of
\partial(U).
Here
Ext_{(R^{i},U)}
and
Int_{(R^{i},U)}
are permutations of
\partial(U)\cap G^{i}.

It’s clear that

\displaystyle|F|=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}|F^{i}|.

From the discussion above, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\rho(\Sigma,R)-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\rho(\Sigma^{i},R^{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||Ext_{(R,U)}\circ Int_{(R,U)}||-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}||Ext_{(R^{i},U)}\circ Int_{(R^{i},U)}||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||Ext_{(R,U)}\circ Int_{(R,U)}||.

Let
R
take all elements in
\Omega
of
G
with uniform probability
\frac{1}{|\Omega|}
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\Omega}(\rho(\Sigma,R)-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\rho(\Sigma^{i},R^{i}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\Omega}||Ext_{(R,U)}\circ Int_{(R,U)}||

which is equivalent to

(3.2)

\displaystyle\rho_{avg}(\Sigma)-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{avg}(\Sigma^{i})\leq||Ext_{(R,U)}\circ Int_{(R,U)}||.

By Lemma
2.4
,

Int_{R,U}=R^{1}R^{2}\cdots R^{k},

Then we obtain a permutation-partition pair
(P,\Pi)
, where

P=Ext_{(R,U)}

and

\Pi=\{{\partial(U)\cap G^{i}|i=1,2,\ldots,k}\}.

Note that
|\partial(U)\cap G^{i}|=d_{j}
, and by Theorem
3.1
, the expected value of
||Ext_{(R,U)}\circ Int_{(R,U)}||
is less than or equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{d_{i}}.

Since this bound is independent of
Ext_{(R,U)}
for any
R
of
G
, thus,

\displaystyle\rho_{avg}(\Sigma)-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{avg}(\Sigma^{i})\leq\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{d_{i}}.

We next prove the other bound, again by Equation (
3.1
), it follows that

\displaystyle\rho_{avg}(\Sigma)-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{avg}(\Sigma^{i})\geq-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}||Ext_{(R^{i},U)}\circ Int_{(R^{i},U)}||.

Similarly, we have

||Ext_{(R^{i},U)}\circ Int_{(R^{i},U)}||\leq\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{l}h_{d_{i,j}}.

Thus

(3.3)

\displaystyle\rho_{avg}(\Sigma)-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{avg}(\Sigma^{i})\geq-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{l}h_{d_{i,j}}.

The result follows.

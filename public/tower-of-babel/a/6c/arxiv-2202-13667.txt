General Indefinite Backward Stochastic Linear-Quadratic Optimal Control Problems
Jingrui Sun, Jiaqiang Wen, Jie Xiong

Abstract




Abstract.
A general backward stochastic linear-quadratic optimal control problem is studied, in which both the state equation and the cost functional contain the nonhomogeneous terms. The main feature of the problem is that the weighting matrices in the cost functional are allowed to be indefinite and cross-product terms in the control and the state processes are present. Necessary and sufficient conditions for the solvability of the problem are obtained, and a characterization of the optimal control in terms of forward-backward stochastic differential equations is derived. By a Riccati equation approach, a general procedure for constructing optimal controls is developed and the value function is obtained explicitly.

Key words:
Backward stochastic differential equation, linear-quadratic, optimal control, Riccati equation, nonhomogeneous term.

AMS subject classifications.
93E20, 49N10, 49N35.

Introduction

Due to its wide range of applications, the theory of stochastic optimal control developed
rapidly in the past few decades.
The stochastic control is a natural and effective method that can solve the uncertainty,
interference, and ambiguity emerging in real-world control problems.
As an important class of optimal control problems, the forward stochastic linear-quadratic problem
has been studied by a lot of researchers (see Wonham
[
20
]
and Davis
[
4
]
,
and the references cited therein).

In the historical development of stochastic optimal control, the backward stochastic differential equation
(BSDE, for short) plays a central role, which was introduced by Bismut
[
1
]
for the linear case
and by Pardoux–Peng
[
10
]
for the nonlinear situation.
Linear BSDEs serve as the adjoint equation of the state equation in the study of the maximum principle
of stochastic optimal control problems (see Bismut
[
1
]
and Yong–Zhou
[
21
]
).
Control problems of BSDEs are also attractive and important, not only due to the theoretical level,
but also their applications in finance; see, for example, Ma–Yong
[
9
]
, Pham
[
11
]
,
Peng
[
12
]
, Zhang
[
22
]
, and the references cited therein.

In this paper, we study a class of quadratic control problems for linear BSDEs with nonhomogeneous terms,
in which the weighting matrices in the cost functional are allowed to be indefinite and cross-product terms
in the control and the state processes are present.
To precisely state our problem, let
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete filtered probability space on which
a one-dimensional standard Brownian motion
W=\{W(t);t\geqslant 0\}
is defined, where
\mathbb{F}=\{{\cal F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}

is usual augmentation of the natural filtration generated by
W
.
For a random variable
\xi
, we write
\xi\in{\cal F}_{t}
if
\xi
is
{\cal F}_{t}
-measurable;
and for a stochastic process
\varphi
, we write
\varphi\in\mathbb{F}
if it is progressively measurable with respect to
\mathbb{F}
.
Consider the following controlled linear BSDE on a finite horizon
[0,T]
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle dY(t)&=[A(t)Y(t)+B(t)u(t)+C(t)Z(t)+f(t)]dt+Z(t)dW(t),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle Y(T)&=\xi,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where the coefficients
A,C:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
B:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
of the
state equation

(
1.1
) are given bounded deterministic functions; the nonhomogeneous term
f:[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-progressively
measurable process; and the terminal value
\xi
belongs to the space

L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\triangleq\Big\{\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigm|\xi\in{\cal F}_{T}\text{ and }\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{2}<\infty\Big\}.

The control process
u
, valued in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, is taken from

\displaystyle{\cal U}\triangleq\bigg\{u:[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}~\big|

\displaystyle~u\in\mathbb{F}\text{ and }\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|u(s)|^{2}ds<\infty\bigg\}.

The criterion for the performance of
u
is given by the following quadratic functional

\displaystyle\displaystyle J\big(\xi;u\big)

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\bigg\{\langle GY(0),Y(0)\rangle+2\langle g,Y(0)\rangle

(1.2)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\bigg[\Big\langle\!\begin{pmatrix}Q(t)&S_{1}^{\top}(t)&S_{2}^{\top}(t)\\
S_{1}(t)&R_{11}(t)&R_{12}(t)\\
S_{2}(t)&R_{21}(t)&R_{22}(t)\end{pmatrix}\!\begin{pmatrix}Y(t)\\
Z(t)\\
u(t)\end{pmatrix}\!,\begin{pmatrix}Y(t)\\
Z(t)\\
u(t)\end{pmatrix}\!\Big\rangle

\displaystyle+2\Big\langle\!\begin{pmatrix}q(t)\\
\rho_{1}(t)\\
\rho_{2}(t)\end{pmatrix}\!,\begin{pmatrix}Y(t)\\
Z(t)\\
u(t)\end{pmatrix}\!\Big\rangle\bigg]dt\bigg\},

where the superscript
\top
denotes the transpose of a matrix;
G
is a symmetric
n\times n
constant
matrix;
g
is a constant,
q
,
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable processes; and

\displaystyle Q,\quad S=\begin{pmatrix}S_{1}\\
S_{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad R=\begin{pmatrix}R_{11}&R_{12}\\
R_{21}&R_{22}\end{pmatrix}

are bounded deterministic matrix-valued functions of proper dimensions over
[0,T]
such
that the blocked matrix in the cost functional is symmetric.
The backward stochastic optimal control problem of interest is as follows.

Problem (BSLQ).
For a given terminal state
\xi\in L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, find a control
u^{*}\in{\cal U}
such that

\displaystyle J(\xi;u^{*})=\inf_{u\in{\cal U}}J(\xi;u)\equiv V(\xi).

(1.3)

Due to the linearity of the backward state equation (
1.1
) and the quadratic form of the cost (
1.2
),
we call the above problem a
backward stochastic linear-quadratic (LQ) optimal control problem
(BSLQ problem).
A process
u^{*}\in{\cal U}
satisfying (
1.3
) is called an
optimal control
of Problem (BSLQ) for the terminal state
\xi
,
and the corresponding adapted solution
(Y^{*},Z^{*})
of the state equation (
1.1
) is called an
optimal state process
.
The function
V
is called the
value function
of Problem (BSLQ).
When the coefficients
f,g,q,\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
vanish, we denote the corresponding Problem (BSLQ) by Problem (BSLQ)
0
.
The corresponding cost functional and value function are denoted by
J^{0}(\xi;u)
and
V^{0}(\xi)
, respectively.

The BSLQ optimal control problem without nonhomogeneous terms was first studied by Lim–Zhou
[
7
]
,
where all the weighting matrices are assumed to be positive semidefinite and the quadratic cost functional is
independent of the cross terms of
(Y,Z,u)
.
Applying a forward formulation and a limiting procedure, together with the completion-of-squares technique,
a complete solution for such a BSLQ optimal control problem was obtained in
[
7
]
.
A couple of follow-up works have appeared afterward; see, for instance, Huang–Wang–Xiong
[
5
]

and Wang–Wu–Xiong
[
18
]
considered BSLQ optimal control problem with partial information;
Huang–Wang–Wu
[
6
]
investigated a backward mean-field linear-quadratic-Gaussian game with
full and partial information;
Wang–Xiao–Xiong
[
19
]
studied BSLQ optimal control problem with asymmetric information;
a dynamic game of linear BSDE systems with mean-field interactions was studied in Du–Huang–Wu
[
3
]
;
a thorough investigation on BSLQ optimal control problem with random coefficients was further carried out
in Sun–Wang
[
14
]
;
a general mean-field BSLQ optimal control problem was investigated in Li–Sun–Xiong
[
8
]
;
and based on
[
8
,
7
]
, a theory of optimal control for controllable stochastic linear
systems was developed in Bi–Sun–Xiong
[
2
]
.
It is worth pointing out that, the key point of the above-mentioned works is that they assume the positive/nonnegative
definiteness condition imposed on the weighting matrices, and most of their cost functionals are independent
of the cross terms in
(Y,Z,u)
and nonhomogeneous terms are not present.

We say that the stochastic LQ optimal control is
indefinite
, if the weighting matrices in the cost functional
J(\xi,u)
,
are not necessarily positive semi-definite.
Not assuming the positive definiteness/ semi-definiteness on the weighting matrices will bring great challenge
for solving Problem (BSLQ).
Recently, Sun–Wu–Xiong
[
15
]
considered a homogeneous backward stochastic LQ optimal control problem
and obtained the optimal control for the indefinite case.
However, their model is not general enough due to the lack of homogeneous terms.
For this reason, their results cannot directly apply to solving some related problems, especially the two-person
zero-sum Stackelberg game.
In this paper, we study a general indefinite BSLQ optimal control problem, in which both the state equation and
the cost functional contain nonhomogeneous terms.
As we shall see in Section 4, the nonhomogeneous terms bring lots of difficulties when constructing the optimal
control of Problem (BSLQ).
For example, we need to reconstruct the representation of the solution
Z
and the optimal control
u
in terms
of
X
, the solution of the corresponding forward dual process.
We shall first derive necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of optimal controls,
and then characterize the optimal control by means of forward-backward stochastic differential equations
(FBSDEs, for short).
Finally, with this characterization, we develop a general procedure for constructing the optimal control
and the value function of Problem (BSLQ).

The rest of the paper is structured as follows.
We give the preliminaries and collect some recently developed results on general forward stochastic LQ optimal
control problems in Section 2. Characterization of the optimal control is presented in Section 3,
by means of FBSDEs.
In Section 4, we first simplify Problem (BSLQ) and construct the optimal control in the case that the cost functional
is uniformly convex, and then present the general results.
Section 5 concludes the paper.

Preliminaries

First, we introduce some notation. Let
\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}

n\times m

\langle M,N\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(M^{\top}N),\quad M,N\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m},

M^{\top}N
. The norm induced by the Frobenius inner product is denoted by

The identity matrix of size

I_{n}.
When no confusion arises, we often suppress the index

and write
I

Let
\mathbb{S}^{n}

\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

\mathbb{S}^{n}

M
and
N

M\geqslant N
(respectively,

is positive semidefinite (respectively, positive definite) almost everywhere (with respect to the Lebesgue measure), and write

if there exists a constant
\delta>0

M\geqslant\delta I_{n}
. For a subset
\mathbb{H}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
, we denote by

the space of continuous functions from
[0,T]
into
\mathbb{H}
, and by

the space of Lebesgue measurable, essentially bounded functions from
[0,T]
into
\mathbb{H}
. Besides the space
L_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}^{2}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
introduced previously, the following spaces of stochastic processes will also be frequently used in the sequel:

\displaystyle L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H})=

\displaystyle\ \bigg\{\varphi:[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{H}\mid\varphi\text{ is $\mathbb{F}$-progressively measurable and }

\displaystyle\qquad\ \mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\varphi(t)|^{2}dt<\infty\bigg\},

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\right]<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega;L^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{H}))=

\displaystyle\ \bigg\{\varphi:[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{H}\mid\varphi\text{ is $\mathbb{F}$-progressively measurable and }

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\varphi(t)|^{2}\right]<\infty\right\}.

For the coefficients of the state equation (
1.1
) and the weighting matrices of the cost functional (
1.2
), we impose the following conditions.

(A1)

The coefficients of the state equation (
1.1
) satisfy

A\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}),\ B\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}),\ C\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}),\ f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(A2)

The coefficients among the nonhomogeneous term and the weighting matrices in the cost functional (

) satisfy

\displaystyle G\in\mathbb{S}^{n},\ Q\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{S}^{n}),\ S\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{(n+m)\times n}),\ R\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{S}^{n+m}),

\displaystyle g\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ q\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})),\ \rho_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})),\ \rho_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m}).

We present the following lemma concerning the well-posedness of the state equation (
1.1
),
which is a direct consequence of the theory of linear BSDEs (see Chapter 7 of Yong–Zhou
[
21
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Under the assumption (A1), for any
(\xi,u)\in L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times{\cal U}
, the state equation (
1.1
) admits a unique adapted solution

\displaystyle(Y,Z)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Furthermore, there exists a constant
K>0
, independent of
\xi
and
u
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|Y(t)|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|Z(t)|^{2}dt\bigg]\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\bigg[|\xi|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|u(t)|^{2}dt+\int_{0}^{T}|f(t)|^{2}dt\bigg].

We next collect some results from forward stochastic LQ optimal control theory, which will be used to constructing the optimal control of Problem (BSLQ). Consider the forward linear stochastic differential equation on a finite time horizon
[0,T]
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle d{\cal X}(t)&=\big[{\cal A}(t){\cal X}(t)+{\cal B}(t)v(t)+b(t)\big]dt+\big[{\cal C}(t)X(t)+{\cal D}(t)v(t)+\sigma(t)\big]dW(t),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\cal X}(0)&=x,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

and the following cost functional

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal J}\big(x;v\big)\triangleq

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}\Bigg\{\big\langle{\cal G}{\cal X}(T),{\cal X}(T)\big\rangle+2\big\langle\tilde{g},{\cal X}(T)\big\rangle

\displaystyle~+\int_{0}^{T}\Bigg[\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}{\cal Q}(t)&{\cal S}^{\top}(t)\\
{\cal S}(t)&{\cal R}(t)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\cal X}(t)\\
v(t)\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}{\cal X}(t)\\
v(t)\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle

(2.2)

\displaystyle~+2\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{q}(t)\\
\tilde{\rho}(t)\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}{\cal X}(t)\\
v(t)\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\Bigg]ds\Bigg\},

where in the equations (
2.1
) and (
2
),

\displaystyle{\cal A},{\cal C}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}),\ {\cal B},{\cal D}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}),\ b,\sigma\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\displaystyle{\cal G}\in\mathbb{S}^{n},\ {\cal Q}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{S}^{n}),\ {\cal S}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}),\ {\cal R}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{S}^{m}),

\displaystyle\tilde{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ \tilde{q}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})),\ \tilde{\rho}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})).

The stochastic linear-quadratic optimal control problem of the forward type is stated as follows.

Problem (FSLQ).
For a given initial state
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, find a control
v^{*}\in{\cal U}
such that

\displaystyle{\cal J}(x;v^{*})=\inf_{u\in{\cal U}}{\cal J}(x;v)\equiv{\cal V}(x).

(2.3)

The control
v^{*}
(if it exists) in (
2.3
) is called an open-loop optimal control for the initial state
x
, and
\mathcal{V}(x)
is called the value of Problem (FSLQ) at
x
. Note that Problem (FSLQ) is an indefinite LQ optimal control problem, since we do not require the weighting matrices to be positive semidefinite.
The following propositions establish the solvability of Problem (FSLQ) under a condition that is nearly necessary for the existence of open-loop optimal controls, and establish a regularity of the solution to the Riccati equation. We refer the reader to Sun–Yong
[
16
]
, Sun–Li–Yong
[
13
]
and the recent book
[
17
]

for proofs and further information.

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume that there exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that

{\cal J}(0;u)\geqslant\alpha\|u\|^{2},\qquad\forall u\in{\cal U}.

(2.4)

Then the following Riccati differential equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle&\dot{{\cal P}}+{\cal P}{\cal A}+{\cal A}^{\top}{\cal P}+{\cal C}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal C}+{\cal Q}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle&\hphantom{\dot{{\cal P}}}-({\cal P}{\cal B}+{\cal C}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D}+{\cal S}^{\top})({\cal R}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D})^{-1}({\cal B}^{\top}{\cal P}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal C}+{\cal S})=0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle&{\cal P}(T)={\cal G},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

admits a unique solution
{\cal P}\in C([0,T];\mathbb{S}^{n})
such that

{\cal R}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D}\gg 0.

In addition, for each initial state
x
, a unique open-loop optimal control exists and is given
by the following closed-loop form:

\displaystyle\displaystyle v^{*}(t)=

\displaystyle-({\cal R}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D})^{-1}({\cal B}^{\top}{\cal P}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal C}+{\cal S})X^{*}

\displaystyle-({\cal R}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D})^{-1}({\cal B}^{\top}\eta+{\cal D}^{\top}\zeta+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}\tilde{\sigma}+\tilde{\rho}),\qquad t\in[0,T],

where
X^{*}
is the solution of the following closed-loop system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle dX^{*}(t)&=\Big\{\big[{\cal A}-{\cal B}({\cal R}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D})^{-1}({\cal B}^{\top}{\cal P}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal C}+{\cal S})\big]X^{*}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle&\hphantom{=\ }-{\cal B}({\cal R}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D})^{-1}({\cal B}^{\top}\eta+{\cal D}^{\top}\zeta+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}\sigma+\tilde{\rho})+b\Big\}dt\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle&\hphantom{=\ }+\Big\{\big[{\cal C}-{\cal D}({\cal R}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D})^{-1}({\cal B}^{\top}{\cal P}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal C}+{\cal S})\big]X^{*}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle&\hphantom{=\ }-{\cal D}({\cal R}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D})^{-1}({\cal B}^{\top}\eta+{\cal D}^{\top}\zeta+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}\sigma+\tilde{\rho})+\sigma\Big\}dW(t),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X^{*}(0)&=x,\end{aligned}\right.

and
(\eta,\zeta)
is the adapted solution of the following backward stochastic differential equation,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle d\eta(t)&=-\Big\{\big[{\cal A}^{\top}-({\cal P}{\cal B}+{\cal C}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D}+{\cal S}^{\top})({\cal R}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D})^{-1}{\cal B}^{\top}\big]\eta\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle&\hphantom{=\ }+\big[{\cal C}^{\top}-({\cal P}{\cal B}+{\cal C}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D}+{\cal S}^{\top})({\cal R}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D})^{-1}{\cal D}^{\top}\big]\zeta\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle&\hphantom{=\ }+\big[{\cal C}^{\top}-({\cal P}{\cal B}+{\cal C}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D}+{\cal S}^{\top})({\cal R}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D})^{-1}{\cal D}^{\top}\big]{\cal P}\sigma\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle&\hphantom{=\ }-({\cal P}{\cal B}+{\cal C}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D}+{\cal S}^{\top})({\cal R}+{\cal D}^{\top}{\cal P}{\cal D})^{-1}\tilde{\rho}+{\cal P}b+\tilde{q}\Big\}dt\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle&\hphantom{=\ }+\zeta dW(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
\eta(T)&=\tilde{g}.\end{aligned}\right.

Furthermore, the value function at

is given by

{\cal V}(x)=\langle{\cal P}(0)x,x\rangle,\quad~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume that

{\cal G}\gg 0,\quad{\cal R}\gg 0,\quad{\cal Q}-{\cal S}^{\top}{\cal R}^{-1}{\cal S}\gg 0.

(2.6)

Then the uniformly convex condition (

3
A characterization of optimal controls in terms of FBSDEs

We now present a characterization of the optimal control in terms of forward-backward stochastic differential equations, which will be used to prove the control constructed later to be optimal.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let (A1) and (A2) hold and let the terminal state
\xi\in L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
be given.
A control
u^{*}\in{\cal U}
is optimal for
\xi
if and only if the following two conditions hold:

(i)

J^{0}(0;u)\geqslant 0
for all
u\in{\cal U}
.

(ii)

The adapted solution
(X^{*},Y^{*},Z^{*})
to the following decoupled FBSDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle dX^{*}(t)&=\big[-A^{\top}(t)X^{*}(t)+Q(t)Y^{*}(t)+S^{\top}_{1}(t)Z^{*}(t)+S^{\top}_{2}(t)u^{*}(t)+q(t)\big]dt\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle&\hphantom{=\ }+\big[-C(t)X^{*}(t)+S_{1}(t)Y^{*}(t)+R_{11}(t)Z^{*}(t)+R_{12}(t)u^{*}(t)+\rho_{1}(t)\big]dW(t),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle dY^{*}(t)&=\big[A(t)Y^{*}(t)+B(t)u^{*}(t)+C(t)Z^{*}(t)+f(t)\big]dt+Z^{*}(t)dW(t),\ \ t\in[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X^{*}(0)&=GY^{*}(0)+g,\quad Y^{*}(T)=\xi,\end{aligned}\right.

satisfies

\displaystyle S_{2}(t)Y^{*}(t)+R_{21}(t)Z^{*}(t)-B^{\top}(t)X^{*}(t)+R_{22}u^{*}(t)+\rho_{2}(t)=0,\quad~t\in[0,T].

(3.1)

Proof.

Note that
u^{*}\in{\cal U}
is optimal for
\xi
if and only if

\displaystyle J(\xi;u^{*}+\varepsilon u)-J(\xi;u^{*})\geqslant 0,\quad~\forall u\in{\cal U},\ \forall\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.2)

Let
u\in{\cal U}
and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed but arbitrary. Denote by
(Y,Z)
the adapted solution of the following BSDE,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle dY(t)&=\big[A(t)Y(t)+B(t)u(t)+C(t)Z(t)\big]dt+Z(t)dW(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle Y(T)&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

and denote by
(Y^{\varepsilon},Z^{\varepsilon})
the adapted solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle dY^{\varepsilon}(t)&=\big[A(t)Y^{\varepsilon}(t)+B(t)[u^{*}(t)+\varepsilon u(t)]+C(t)Z^{\varepsilon}(t)+f(t)\big]dt+Z^{\varepsilon}(t)dW(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle Y(T)&=\xi.\end{aligned}\right.

By the uniqueness of the adapted solution of BSDEs, it is clearly that

(Y^{\varepsilon},Z^{\varepsilon})=(Y^{*}+\varepsilon Y,Y^{*}+\varepsilon Z)
.
Therefore,

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have investigated an indefinite backward stochastic linear-quadratic optimal
control problem with deterministic nonhomogeneous coefficients and have developed a general procedure
for constructing optimal controls.
The necessary and sufficient conditions of Problem (BSLQ) are derived for the solvability of the problem, and a characterization of the optimal control in terms of forward-backward stochastic differential equations has presented. The optimal control and the value function of Problem (BLSQ) are given out clearly.
The results obtained in this paper provide insight into some related topics, especially into
the study of zero-sum backward stochastic differential games.
In our future publication, we hope to report some relevant results along this line.

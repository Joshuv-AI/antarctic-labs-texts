Abstract
. We present a method to calculate the action of the Mordell-Weil group of an elliptic K ​ 3 K3 surface on the numerical Néron-Severi lattice of the K ​ 3 K3 surface. As an application, we compute a finite generating set of the automorphism group of a K ​ 3 K3 surface birational to the double plane branched along a 6 6 -cuspidal sextic curve of torus type.

1.
Introduction

We work over an algebraically closed field
k
.

Let
X
be a projective
K3
surface.
We denote by
S_{X}
the
numerical Néron-Severi lattice
of
X
,
that is, the group of numerical equivalence classes of divisors of
X
with the intersection pairing

\langle\phantom{a}\phantom{a}\rangle\colon S_{X}\times S_{X}\to\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}.

Let
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(S_{X})
denote the group of isometries of the lattice
S_{X}
.
We investigate the automorphism group
\operatorname{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X)
of
X

by means of the action

\operatorname{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X)\to\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(S_{X})

of
\operatorname{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X)
on the lattice
S_{X}
.

Let
\phi\colon X\to\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
be an elliptic fibration
with a distinguished section
\zeta\colon\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to X
.
In this case, we say that
(\phi,\zeta)

is a
Jacobian fibration
.
We denote by
\mathrm{MW}(X,\phi,\zeta)
the Mordell-Weil group
of sections of
\phi
with
\zeta
being the zero element.
An element
\sigma\in\mathrm{MW}(X,\phi,\zeta)
acts on
the generic fiber of
\phi
by translation.
Since
X
is minimal,
this birational automorphism of
X
is an automorphism of
X
,
and hence we have an embedding of
\mathrm{MW}(X,\phi,\zeta)
into
\operatorname{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X)
.
In this paper,
we investigate the composite homomorphism

(1.1)

\mathrm{MW}(X,\phi,\zeta)\;\;\to\;\;\operatorname{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X)\;\;\to\;\;\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(S_{X}).

This homomorphism has been used in many situations in the study
of automorphisms of
K3
surfaces (see, for example,
[
25
]
).
The purpose of this paper is to present a general algorithm to calculate (
1.1
) explicitly
and to give applications.

Borcherds’ method (
[
5
,
6
]
) is
a method to calculate a finite generating set of the image of

\operatorname{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X)\to\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(S_{X})
by means of
a certain decomposition of the nef-and-big cone of
X
into a union of polyhedral cones.
The first application of this method to the study of the automorphism group of a
K3
surface
was given by Kondo
[
15
]
.
See also
[
29
]
.
Since this method is based on lattice-theoretic computation,
the geometric meaning of elements in the generating set obtained by this method
is not clear in general.
The homomorphism (
1.1
)
helps us to
express the generating set geometrically.
See Remark
5.16
.

As an application,
we calculate the automorphism group of the complex
K3
surface
X_{f,g}

obtained as the minimal resolution of the double cover

\overline{X}_{f,g}
of
\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
defined by

(1.2)

w^{2}=f(x,y,z)^{2}+g(x,y,z)^{3},

where
f
and
g
are very general homogeneous polynomials on
\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
of degree
3
and
2
, respectively.
Here being
very general
means that
there exist at most countably many analytic subsets of
H^{0}(\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{2},\mathord{\mathcal{O}}(3))\times H^{0}(\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{2},\mathord{\mathcal{O}}(2))

with codimension
\geq 1
such that the pair
(f,g)
does not belong to any of them.
We prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

The automorphism group
\operatorname{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X_{f,g})
of
X_{f,g}
is generated by

463

involutions associated with double coverings
X_{f,g}\to\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

and
360
elements of infinite order
in Mordell-Weil groups of Jacobian fibrations of
X_{f,g}
.

Here, by a
double covering
,
we mean a generically finite morphism of degree
2
.

Theorem 1.2
.

The automorphism group
\operatorname{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X_{f,g})

acts on the set of smooth rational curves on
X_{f,g}
transitively.

The branch curve of the finite double cover
\overline{X}_{f,g}\to\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is
defined by the equation
f^{2}+g^{3}=0
.
This plane curve is called a
6
-cuspidal plane sextic of torus type
, and
was studied intensively from various points of view.
See, for example,
[
22
]
.
In fact, Zariski
[
37
]
observed that there exists a
6
-cuspidal plane sextic of
non
-torus type,
and the seminal notion of
Zariski pairs
emerged
from this observation.
See
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
.
In
[
9
]
and
[
27
]
, this classical example of Zariski pairs was studied
in relation to the theory of
K3
surfaces.
It would be an interesting problem to calculate
the automorphism group of the
K3
surface
obtained from the
6
-cuspidal plane sextic of non-torus type.

The generating set in Theorem
1.1
is constructed in such a way that
we can clearly see the geometric meaning of each element.
See Section
6
for more precise descriptions of these automorphisms.
Remark that this generating set
is not minimal at all.

In fact, we give divisors of
X_{f,g}
whose classes generate
S_{X}
.
Hence we can calculate, in principle, the equations of the double coverings and the Jacobian fibrations
by the method given in
[
28
]
.
The actual computation of the equations, however, would be very hard.

Theorem
1.1
is proved in the following three steps.

(a)

We find many automorphisms of
X_{f,g}

geometrically by the methods explained in Section
3

(especially Section
3.7
) and Section
4
.

(b)

We find a finite generating set of
\operatorname{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X_{f,g})
by
Borcherds’ method,
which will be explained in Section
5
.

(c)

We then show that the group generated by the automorphisms obtained in Step (
a
)
contains the generating set obtained in Step (
b
).

See
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
and
[
33
]

for general finiteness results of the automorphism group of a
K3
surface and its action on the nef-and-big cone.

This paper is organized as follows.
After fixing some notions and notation about lattices in Section
2
,
we summarize in Section
3
various computational tools that are useful in the study of the geometry of
K3
surfaces.
These tools are based on an algorithm
given in
[
28
]

to calculate

\mathord{\mathrm{Sep}}(v_{1},v_{2})
of
separating
(-2)
-vectors
in a hyperbolic lattice.
In Section

).
In Section

We employ a graph-theoretic formulation of Borcherds’ method given in
[

, Section 4.1]
.
Sections
3
–

are intended to be summaries of computational methods in the study of
K3
surfaces
for future reference.
In Section

, we calculate
\operatorname{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(X_{f,g})
by means of all these algorithms,
and prove Theorems
1.1

1.2
.
We used
GAP

[

]
for the actual computation.
In the author’s webpage
[

]
,

.

2.
Notation and terminologies

By a
lattice
, we mean a free
\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
-module
L
of finite rank with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form

\langle\phantom{a}\phantom{a}\rangle\colon L\times L\to\mathord{\mathbb{Z}},

which we call the
intersection form
(or the
intersection pairing
) of
L
.
The group of isometries of a lattice
L
is denoted by
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(L)
,
which we let act on
L
from the
right
.

Let
L
be a lattice.
Then the
dual lattice

L^{\vee}
of
L
is defined to be

\{\,{x\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}}\mid{\langle x,v\rangle\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}\;\;\textrm{for all}\;\;v\in L}\,\}.

The finite abelian group
A(L):=L^{\vee}/L
is called the
discriminant group
of
L
.
We say that
L
is
unimodular
if
L=L^{\vee}
.

A lattice
L
is said to be
even
if
\langle v,v\rangle\in 2\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
holds for all
v\in L
.
A
root
of an even lattice
L
is a vector
r\in L
such that
\langle r,r\rangle
is either
2
or
-2
.
A
(-2)
-vector
of
L
is a root
r\in L

such that
\langle r,r\rangle=-2
.
Suppose that
L
is even and negative-definite.
Then the set

\mathord{\mathrm{Roots}}(L):=\{\,{r\in L}\mid{\langle r,r\rangle=-2}\,\}

is finite.
An even negative-definite lattice
L
is called a
root lattice

if
L
is generated by
\mathord{\mathrm{Roots}}(L)
.
A root lattice has a basis consisting of roots
whose dual graph is a Dynkin diagram of type
\mathrm{ADE}
.
See, for example,
[
10
, Section 1]

for the definition of dual graphs, Dynkin diagrams, and their
\mathrm{ADE}
-types.

A lattice
L
of rank
n>1
is said to be
hyperbolic
if the signature of
the real quadratic space
L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
is
(1,n-1)
.
Let
L
be an even hyperbolic lattice.
A
positive cone
of
L

is one of the two connected components of the space

\{\,{x\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}\mid{\langle x,x\rangle>0}\,\}.

Let
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}
be a positive cone of
L
.
We put

\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(L,\mathord{\mathcal{P}}):=\{\,{g\in\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(L)}\mid{\mathord{\mathcal{P}}^{g}=\mathord{\mathcal{P}}}\,\}.

We have
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(L)=\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(L,\mathord{\mathcal{P}})\times\{\pm 1\}
.
For
v\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
,
we put

v^{\perp}:=\{\,{x\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}\mid{\langle x,v\rangle=0}\,\}.

When
v\in\mathord{\mathcal{P}}\cap L
,
the intersection
v^{\perp}\cap L
is an even negative-definite sublattice of
L
,
and hence we can effectively calculate the finite set

\mathord{\mathrm{Roots}}(v^{\perp}\cap L)=\{\,{r\in L}\mid{\langle r,v\rangle=0,\;\;\langle r,r\rangle=-2}\,\}

of
(-2)
-vectors in
L
perpendicular to
v
.

For
v\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
with
\langle v,v\rangle<0
,
we put

(v)^{\perp}:=v^{\perp}\cap\mathord{\mathcal{P}}=\{\,{x\in\mathord{\mathcal{P}}}\mid{\langle x,v\rangle=0}\,\},

which is a real hyperplane of
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}
.
Let
v_{1},v_{2}\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}
be rational vectors in
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}
.
Then we can calculate the finite set

\mathord{\mathrm{Sep}}(v_{1},v_{2}):=\{\,{r\in L}\mid{\;\langle r,v_{1}\rangle>0,\;\langle r,v_{2}\rangle<0,\;\langle r,r\rangle=-2\;}\,\}

of
(-2)
-vectors
separating
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
.
See
[
28
]
for the algorithm.
As will be explained in Section
3
,
this algorithm is very useful in the study of
K3
surfaces.

Definition 2.1
.

By a
chamber
,
we mean a closed subset
D
of
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}
such that

•

D
contains a non-empty open subset of
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}
, and

•

D
is defined by linear inequalities
\langle x,v_{i}\rangle\geq 0
(
i\in I
),
where
v_{i}
(
i\in I
) are vectors of
L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
with
\langle v_{i},v_{i}\rangle<0

such that the family
\{\,{(v_{i})^{\perp}}\mid{i\in I}\,\}
of hyperplanes is locally finite in
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
D
be a chamber.
A
wall
of
D
is a closed subset of
D
of the form
D\cap(v)^{\perp}

such that the hyperplane
(v)^{\perp}
is disjoint from the interior of
D
and that

D\cap(v)^{\perp}
contains a non-empty open subset of
(v)^{\perp}
.
We say that a vector
v\in L\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{R}}

defines
a wall
w
of
D
if

w=D\cap(v)^{\perp}
and
\langle x,v\rangle>0
for an interior point
x
of
D

(and hence
\langle x,v\rangle\geq 0
for all
x\in D
).
A defining vector of a wall of a chamber is unique up to positive multiplicative constant.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathord{\mathcal{F}}:=\{\,{(v_{\alpha})^{\perp}}\mid{\alpha\in F}\,\}
be a locally finite family of hyperplanes in
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}
.
Then the closure in
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}
of each connected component of

\mathord{\mathcal{P}}\;\setminus\;\bigcup_{\alpha\in F}\;(v_{\alpha})^{\perp}

is a chamber.
Let
\mathord{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathord{\mathcal{F}}}
be the set of these chambers.
In this situation, we say that
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}
is
tessellated by the chambers in
\mathord{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathord{\mathcal{F}}}
.
If a subset
N
of
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}
is the union of chambers in a subset of
\mathord{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathord{\mathcal{F}}}
,
we say that
N
is
tessellated by chambers in
\mathord{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathord{\mathcal{F}}}
.

Let
w
be a wall of a chamber
D\in\mathord{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathord{\mathcal{F}}}
.
Then there exists a unique chamber
D^{\prime}\in\mathord{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathord{\mathcal{F}}}

such that
D\neq D^{\prime}
and
w\subset D^{\prime}
.
This chamber
D^{\prime}
is called the chamber
adjacent to
D
across the wall
w
.

A
(-2)
-vector
r\in L
defines a reflection

s_{r}\colon x\mapsto x+\langle x,r\rangle r

into the mirror
(r)^{\perp}
.
We have
s_{r}\in\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(L,\mathord{\mathcal{P}})
.
Let
W(L)
denote the subgroup of
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(L,\mathord{\mathcal{P}})
generated by all the reflections
s_{r}
with respect
to
(-2)
-vectors
r
.
We call
W(L)
the
Weyl group
of
L
.
Note that the family of hyperplanes
(r)^{\perp}
defined by
(-2)
-vectors
r

is locally finite in
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
standard fundamental domain of
W(L)
is
the closure of a connected component of

\mathord{\mathcal{P}}\;\setminus\;\bigcup\;(r)^{\perp},

where

r
runs through the set of
(-2)
-vectors.

Let
D
be a standard fundamental domain of
W(L)
.
We put

\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(L,D):=\{\,{g\in\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(L)}\mid{D^{g}=D}\,\}.

Then we have
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(L,\mathord{\mathcal{P}})=W(L)\rtimes\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(L,D)
.
The action of
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(L,\mathord{\mathcal{P}})
on
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}

preserves the tessellation of
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}
by
the standard fundamental domains of
W(L)
.

3.
The numerical Néron-Severi lattice of a
K3
surface

Let
X
be a
K3
surface, and

S_{X}
the lattice of numerical equivalence classes of
divisors of
X
,
which we call the
numerical Néron-Severi lattice
of
X
.
For a divisor
D
of
X
, we denote by
[D]\in S_{X}
the class of
D
.
Suppose that
S_{X}
is of rank
n>1
.
Then
S_{X}
is an even hyperbolic lattice.
Let
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{X}
be the positive cone
of
S_{X}
containing an ample class of
X
,
and
\overline{\mathord{\mathcal{P}}}_{X}
the closure of
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{X}
in
S_{X}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
.
We put

\displaystyle N_{X}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{\,{x\in\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{X}}\mid{\langle x,[C]\rangle\geq 0\;\;\textrm{for all curves $C$ on $X$}}\,\},

\displaystyle N_{X}^{\circ}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\textrm{the interior of $N_{X}$},

\displaystyle\overline{N}_{X}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\textrm{the closure of $N_{X}$ in $\overline{\mathord{\mathcal{P}}}_{X}$}.

The cone
N_{X}
is called the
nef-and-big cone
of
X
.
If
C
is a smooth rational curve on
X
,
then its class
[C]
is a
(-2)
-vector of
S_{X}
.
We put

\mathord{\mathrm{Rats}}(X):=\{\,{[C]\in S_{X}}\mid{\textrm{$C$ is a smooth rational curve on $X$}}\,\}.

We have the following:

Theorem 3.1
.

The nef-and-big cone
N_{X}
is a standard fundamental domain of
the Weyl group
W(S_{X})
of
S_{X}
.
A
(-2)
-vector
r\in S_{X}

belongs to
\mathord{\mathrm{Rats}}(X)
if and only if
r
defines a wall of the chamber
N_{X}
.

\Box

Suppose that we have an ample class
\boldsymbol{a}\in N_{X}^{\mathord{\circ}}\cap S_{X}
.
Then Vinberg’s algorithm
[
35
]

enables us to enumerate, for a given positive integer
m
,
all the walls
N_{X}\cap(r)^{\perp}
of
N_{X}
defined by
r\in\mathord{\mathrm{Rats}}(X)
with
\langle r,\boldsymbol{a}\rangle\leq m
.
(See (
3.2
) below.)
Our algorithm
[
28
]
of
calculating the set
\mathord{\mathrm{Sep}}(v_{1},v_{2})
of separating
(-2)
-vectors
provides us with an alternative method to investigate the nef-and-big cone
N_{X}
.
Below are some examples.

3.1.
Finding an ample class

It is well-known that
a class
v\in S_{X}
is ample if and only if
v\in N_{X}^{\circ}
.
Let
\overline{X}
be a normal surface birational to
X
,
and
h\in S_{X}
the pull-back of an ample class of
\overline{X}

by the minimal resolution
X\to\overline{X}
.
Then we have
h\in N_{X}
.
It is known
[
3
]
that
\overline{X}
has only rational double points
as its singularities,
and hence the exceptional locus of the desingularization
X\to\overline{X}

is a union of smooth rational curves whose dual graph is
a Dynkin diagram of type
\mathrm{ADE}
.
Let
r_{1},\dots,r_{\mu}
be the classes of smooth rational curves contracted by

X\to\overline{X}
.
Then,
locally around
h
, the chamber
N_{X}
is defined by
\langle x,r_{i}\rangle\geq 0
for
i=1,\dots,\mu
.
Therefore a vector
v\in\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{X}\cap S_{X}
is ample
if and only if

\mathord{\mathrm{Sep}}(h,v)=\emptyset,\quad\mathord{\mathrm{Roots}}(\,v^{\perp}\cap S_{X}\,)=\emptyset,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\langle v,r_{i}\rangle>0\;\;\textrm{for}\;\;i=1,\dots,\mu.

If
a^{\prime}\in S_{X}
satisfies
\langle a^{\prime},r_{i}\rangle>0
for
i=1,\dots,\mu
,
then
\boldsymbol{a}:=mh+a^{\prime}
is ample for sufficiently large integers
m
.

3.2.
Nefness and ampleness

Suppose that we have an ample class
\boldsymbol{a}\in S_{X}
.
Then
we can characterize
N_{X}
as the unique standard fundamental domain of
W(S_{X})

containing
\boldsymbol{a}
.
Let
v\in S_{X}
be a vector with
\langle v,v\rangle>0
.
Then we have

v\in\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{X}\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;\langle\boldsymbol{a},v\rangle>0.

When these are the case, we have

v\in N_{X}\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;\mathord{\mathrm{Sep}}(\boldsymbol{a},v)=\emptyset.

When these are the case, we have

v\in N_{X}^{\circ}\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;\mathord{\mathrm{Roots}}(\,v^{\perp}\cap S_{X}\,)=\emptyset.

3.3.
The group
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(S_{X},N_{X})

Recall that
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(S_{X},N_{X})
is the subgroup of
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(S_{X},\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{X})

consisting of all isometries
g
such that
N_{X}^{g}=N_{X}
.
Suppose again that we have an ample class
\boldsymbol{a}\in S_{X}
.
Let
g
be an element of
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(S_{X})
.
Then we have

g\in\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(S_{X},\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{X})\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;\langle\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{a}^{g}\rangle>0.

When these are the case, we have

(3.1)

g\in\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(S_{X},N_{X})\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;\mathord{\mathrm{Sep}}(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{a}^{g})=\emptyset,

because, for
g\in\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(S_{X},\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{X})
,
the chamber
N_{X}^{g}
is also a standard fundamental domain of
W(S_{X})
.

3.4.
The set
\mathord{\mathrm{Rats}}(X)

Again we assume that we have an ample class
\boldsymbol{a}\in S_{X}
.
Let
r\in S_{X}
be a
(-2)
-vector such that
\langle\boldsymbol{a},r\rangle>0
.
Then there exists an effective divisor
D
of
X
such that
r=[D]
.
We have
r\in\mathord{\mathrm{Rats}}(X)
if and only if
D
is irreducible.

Since
D
contains a smooth rational curve
C
such that
\langle[C],[D]\rangle<0

as an irreducible component, we have the following criterion,
which is a geometric interpretation of Vinberg’s algorithm
[
35
]

applied to
(-2)
-vectors:

(3.2)

r\in\mathord{\mathrm{Rats}}(X)\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;\textrm{$\langle r,r^{\prime}\rangle\geq 0$ for all $r^{\prime}\in\mathord{\mathrm{Rats}}(X)$ with $\langle r^{\prime},\boldsymbol{a}\rangle<\langle r,\boldsymbol{a}\rangle$}

Thanks to the algorithm to calculate
\mathord{\mathrm{Sep}}(v_{1},v_{2})
,
we obtain another criterion.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
r\in S_{X}
be a
(-2)
-vector with
\langle\boldsymbol{a},r\rangle>0
.
We put

a_{r}^{\prime}:=\boldsymbol{a}+\frac{\langle\boldsymbol{a},r\rangle}{2}r.

Then
r\in\mathord{\mathrm{Rats}}(X)
if and only if

(3.3)

\mathord{\mathrm{Roots}}(\,a_{r}^{\prime\perp}\cap S_{X}\,)=\{r,-r\}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathord{\mathrm{Sep}}(a_{r}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{a})=\emptyset.

Proof.

Since
\langle a_{r}^{\prime},r\rangle=0
and
\langle a_{r}^{\prime},a_{r}^{\prime}\rangle>0
,
we have
a_{r}^{\prime}\in(r)^{\perp}\subset\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{X}
, and
hence the set

3.5.
Nefness of a vector of norm
0

Suppose again that we have
\boldsymbol{a}\in N_{X}^{\circ}\cap S_{X}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

Then

if and only if
\mathord{\mathrm{Sep}}(a^{\prime}_{f},\boldsymbol{a})=\emptyset

where

Proof.

First note that, since

,

\langle\boldsymbol{a},f\rangle>0

, and hence

\mathord{\mathrm{Sep}}(a^{\prime}_{f},\boldsymbol{a})

Suppose that

.
Since
\boldsymbol{a}\in N_{X}^{\circ}
, we have

and hence
\mathord{\mathrm{Sep}}(a^{\prime}_{f},\boldsymbol{a})=\emptyset
.
Suppose that
f\notin\overline{N}_{X}
.
Then there exists a smooth rational curve
C
such that
\langle f,[C]\rangle<0
.
We put

.
Then we have
\langle f,r\rangle\leq-1
.
Since

and

,
there exists an effective divisor

on

such that

.
Then

is an irreducible component of

such that

,
and hence
\langle\boldsymbol{a},r\rangle<\langle\boldsymbol{a},f\rangle
.
The intersection point of

and the open line segment

is equal to

,
where

Therefore

is a
(-2)
-vector separating

and

.

\Box

3.7.
Finding automorphisms from nef vectors of norm
2

Let

be an ample class of

Let

By a

we mean a generically finite morphism of degree

By abuse of notation,

•

We put

Since the quadratic part of the intersection form

•

If

polarization of degree

denote by

.
Let

be the Stein factorization of

,
and let

be the branch curve of the finite double covering

We can calculate the set

.
Hence we obtain the

-type of

and the invariant part

of the action of

on

.
Indeed, applying to

of rational double points due to Horikawa

]

•

Suppose that

5.3.

-chambers

Let

denote an even unimodular hyperbolic lattice

We choose a positive cone

of

A standard fundamental domain of

Definition 5.7
.

A vector

Definition 5.8

Let

\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{w}):=\{\,{x\in\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{26}}\mid{\langle x,r\rangle\geq 0\;\;\textrm{for all

(Conway
[
8
]
)

(1)

The mapping

a bijection from the set of Weyl vectors

.

(2)

Let
\mathbf{w}
be a Weyl vector.
Then the mapping

the set
of Leech roots with respect to
\mathbf{w}
to the set of
walls of the chamber

.

\Box

Definition 5.10
.

We call

.
Hence

Suppose that we have a primitive embedding

Replacing

Let

Suppose that

Definition 5.14

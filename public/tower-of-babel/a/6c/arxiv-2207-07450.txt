ℤ \mathbb{Z} -polyregular functions
Thomas Colcombet 1 , Gaëtan Douéneau-Tabot 1 2 and Aliaume Lopez 1 3

Abstract
This paper studies a robust class of functions from finite words to integers that we call ℤ \mathbb{Z} -polyregular functions. We show that it admits natural characterizations in terms of logics, ℤ \mathbb{Z} -rational expressions, ℤ \mathbb{Z} -rational series and transducers. We then study two subclass membership problems. First, we show that the asymptotic growth rate of a function is computable, and corresponds to the minimal number of variables required to represent it using logical formulas. Second, we show that first-order definability of ℤ \mathbb{Z} -polyregular functions is decidable. To show the latter, we introduce an original notion of residual transducer, and provide a semantic characterization based on aperiodicity.

II-A

Counting valuations on finite words

Let
{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}_{k}
be the set of
{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}
-formulas over the signature
(A,<)
which have
exactly
k
free first-order variables.
We then let
{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}{}\coloneqq\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}_{k}
.
If
\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}_{k}
,

w\in A^{*}
and
1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\leq|w|
, we
write
w\models\varphi(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})
whenever
the valuation
x_{1}\mapsto i_{1},\dots,x_{k}\mapsto i_{k}
makes the formula
\varphi

true in the model
w
.

Definition II.1

(Counting)
.

Given
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}_{k}
, we let
{\#}{\varphi}\colon A^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}

be the function defined by
{\#}{\varphi}(w)\coloneqq|\{{(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})}~\colon{w\models\varphi(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})}\}|
.

The value
{\#}{\varphi}(w)
is the number of tuples
that make the formula
\varphi
true in the model
w
.

Example II.2
.

If
\varphi\in{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}_{0}
, then

{\#}{\varphi}
is the indicator function of the (regular)
language
\{{w}~\colon{w\models\varphi}\}\subseteq A^{*}
.

Example II.3
.

Let
A\coloneqq\{a,b\}
. Let
\varphi(x,y)\coloneqq a(x)\land b(y)
,
then
{\#}{\varphi}(w)=|w|_{a}\times|w|_{b}
for all
w\in A^{*}
.
Let
\psi(x,y)\coloneqq\varphi(x,y)\land x>y
,
then
{\#}{\psi}(a^{n_{0}}ba^{n_{1}}\cdots a^{n_{p}})=\sum_{i=0}^{p}i\times n_{i}
.

Example II.4
.

Let
\varphi\in{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}_{k}
,
and
x
be a fresh variable.
Then,
x=x\land\varphi\in{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}_{k+1}
, and

{\#}{(x=x\land\varphi)}(w)=|w|\times{\#}{\varphi}(w)

for every

w\in A^{*}
.
Similarly, for all
w\in A^{*}
and
a\in A
,

{\#}{(}a(x)\land\varphi)(w)=|w|_{a}\times{\#}{\varphi}(w)
.

If
F
is a subset of the set of functions

A^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
and if
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, we let

\operatorname{{\mathsf{Span}}}_{S}{{{}}}(F)\coloneqq\{{\sum_{i}{a_{i}}f_{i}}~\colon{a_{i}\in S,f_{i}\in F}\}

The set

The goal of this paper is to study the

-linear combinations of the basic

-polyregular functions

(

For

Following the construction of

Example II.10

Let

II-B

Regular and polyregular functions

We recall that the class of (word-to-word) functions computed by

two-way transducers
(or equivalently
by
{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}
-transductions, see e.g.
[
20
]
)
is called
regular functions
.
As an easy consequence of its definition,

\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k}
is preserved under pre-composition with a regular function.

Proposition II.12
.

For all
k\geq 0
, the class
\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k}
is (effectively) closed under
pre-composition by regular functions.

Now, we intend to justify the name
“
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular functions” by showing
that this class is deeply connected to
the well-studied class of
polyregular functions

from finite words to finite words.
Informally, this class of functions can be defined using
the formalism of multidimensional
{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}
-interpretations.
The reader is invited
to consult
[
21
]
for its formal
definition, which we skip here.
Let
\operatorname{\mathsf{sum}}:\{\pm 1\}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be the sum operation
mapping
w\in\{\pm 1\}^{*}
to
\sum_{i=1}^{|w|}w[i]
.

Proposition II.13
.

The class
\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}
is (effectively) the class
of functions
\operatorname{\mathsf{sum}}\circ f
where
f:A^{*}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}^{*}

is polyregular.

II-C

Rational series and rational expressions

The class of rational series over the semiring

(\mathbb{Z},+,\times)
, also known as
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series
,
is a robust class of functions from finite words to
\mathbb{Z}

that has been largely studied since the 1960
(see e.g.
[
13
]
for a survey).
It can be defined using the indicator functions

\mathbf{1}_{L}
of regular languages

L\subseteq A^{*}
, and the following combinators
given
f,g:A^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
and
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}
:

•

the external
\mathbb{Z}
-product
\delta f\colon w\mapsto\delta\times f(w)
;

•

the sum
f+g:w\mapsto f(w)+g(w)
;

•

the Cauchy product
f\mathop{{\otimes}}g\colon w\mapsto\sum_{w=uv}f(u)\times g(v)
;

•

if and only if
f(\varepsilon)=0
, the Kleene star

f^{*}\coloneqq\sum_{n\geq 0}f^{n}
where

f^{0}:\varepsilon\mapsto 1,w\neq\varepsilon\mapsto 0

is neutral for Cauchy product
and
f^{n+1}\coloneqq f\mathop{{\otimes}}f^{n}
.

Definition II.14

(
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series)
.

The class of
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series
is the smallest
class of functions from finite words to
\mathbb{Z}

that contains the indicator functions of all regular languages,
and is closed under taking external
\mathbb{Z}
-products, sums,
Cauchy products and Kleene stars.

We intend to connect
\mathbb{Z}
-rational
series and
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular functions. Let us first
observe that not all
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series
are
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular. We say that a function
f:A^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
has

polynomial growth
whenever there exists

k\geq 0
such that
|f(w)|=\mathcal{O}(|w|^{k})
.
It is an easy check that
every
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular function has polynomial growth.

Claim II.15
.

If
k\geq 0
and
f\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k}
then
|f(w)|=\mathcal{O}(|w|^{k})
.

Example II.16
.

The map

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series
because
f=((-3)\mathbf{1}_{A^{+}})^{*}
.
However

since
it does not have polynomial growth.

It is easy to see from the logical definition
that the class
\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}
is closed under
taking Cauchy products.

Claim II.17
.

Let
k,\ell\geq 0
. Let
f\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k}

and
g\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{\ell}
, then

f\mathop{{\otimes}}g\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k+\ell+1}
.
The construction is effective.

As a consequence, if

is regular
and

, then
\mathbf{1}_{L}\mathop{{\otimes}}f\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k+1}
.
The following result states that such functions
actually generate the whole space
\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k+1}
.

Proposition II.18
.

Let
k\geq 0
, the following (effectively)
holds:

\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k+1}=\operatorname{{\mathsf{Span}}}_{\mathbb{Z}}{{}}(\{{\mathbf{1}_{L}\mathop{{\otimes}}f}~\colon{L\text{~regular},f\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k}}\}).

Example II.19
.

The map
w\mapsto(-1)^{|w|}|w|

is in
\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{1}
as it equals

\mathbf{1}_{\textnormal{odd}}\mathop{{\otimes}}\mathbf{1}_{\textnormal{odd}}+\mathbf{1}_{\textnormal{even}}\mathop{{\otimes}}\mathbf{1}_{\textnormal{even}}-\mathbf{1}_{\textnormal{even}}\mathop{{\otimes}}\mathbf{1}_{\textnormal{odd}}-\mathbf{1}_{\textnormal{odd}}\mathop{{\otimes}}\mathbf{1}_{\textnormal{even}}-\mathbf{1}_{\textnormal{odd}}+\mathbf{1}_{\textnormal{even}}
.

Now, let us show that
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular functions
can be characterized both syntactically and semantically
as a subclass of
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series. We
prove that the membership problem is decidable and provide
an effective conversion algorithm.

Theorem II.20

(Rational series of polynomial growth)
.

Let
f:A^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, the following are equivalent:

1.

f
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular function;

2.

f
belongs to the smallest class of functions that
contains the indicator functions of all regular languages
and is closed under taking external
\mathbb{Z}
-products, sums and
Cauchy products;

3.

f
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series having polynomial growth.

Furthermore, one can decide whether a
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular function and the translations
are effective.

Proof.

For
Item

2

\Rightarrow

Item

1
,
observe that
\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}
contains the indicator functions of regular languages, is closed
under external
\mathbb{Z}
-products, sums,
and Cauchy products (thanks to

II.17
). For
Item

1

\Rightarrow

Item

2
, we obtain for all
k\geq 0
as an
immediate consequence of
Proposition

II.18
:

\begin{array}[]{rcll}&\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k}&=\operatorname{{\mathsf{Span}}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\{\mathbf{1}_{L_{0}}\mathop{{\otimes}}\cdots\mathop{{\otimes}}\mathbf{1}_{L_{k}}&\\
&&\colon L_{0},\dots,L_{k}\text{ regular languages}\})\end{array}

(1)

and the result follows.

The equivalence between
Item

2

and
Item

3
follows (in a non effective way)
from
[
13
, Corollary 2.6 p 159]
.
Furthermore polynomial growth is decidable by

[
13
, Corollary 2.4 p 159]
.
To provide an effective translation, one can start from a

\mathbb{Z}
-rational series
f
of polynomial growth,
enumerate all the
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular functions
g
,
rewrite them as rational series (using
Item

1

\Rightarrow

Item

2
)
and check whether
f=g
since this property
can be decided for
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series

[
13
, Corollary 3.6 p 38]
.
∎

Remark II.21
.

It follows from
Remark

II.6
,

[
11
, Proposition 6.1]
,
and

,
that
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series
of polynomial growth are exactly

of polynomial ambiguity.
We are not aware of a direct

Remark II.22
.

[

, Theorem 3.3]

gives a similar result when comparing
\mathbb{N}
-polyregular functions
and
\mathbb{N}
-rational series.

Remark II.23
.

The class of
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular functions
is also closed under Hadamard product
(
f\times g(w)\coloneqq f(w)\times g(w)
).
This can be obtained by generalising

Example

II.4
.
Moreover,
f\times g\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k+\ell}

whenever

and

.

Since the equivalence is decidable
for
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series

(Equivalence problem)
.

One can decide if two

II-D

Rational series and representations

In this section, we intend to provide another
description of
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular functions among
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series.
To that end, we first recall that rational series
can also be described using matrices (or, equivalently,
weighted automata). Let
\mathop{{\mathcal{M}^{n,m}}}(\mathbb{Z})
be the set of all

n\times m
matrices with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
. We equip

\mathop{{\mathcal{M}^{n,m}}}(\mathbb{Z})
with the usual matrix multiplication.

Definition II.25

(Linear representation)
.

We say that a triple
(I,\mu,F)
where

\mu\colon A^{*}\to\mathop{{\mathcal{M}^{n,n}}}(\mathbb{Z})
is a monoid morphism,

I\in\mathop{{\mathcal{M}^{1,n}}}(\mathbb{Z})
and
F\in\mathop{{\mathcal{M}^{n,1}}}(\mathbb{Z})
,
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear representation
of a function
f:A^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}

if
f(w)=I\mu(w)F
for all
w\in A^{*}
.

It is well-known since Schützenberger (see e.g.

[
13
, Theorem 7.1 p 17]
)
that the class of
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series is (effectively) the class
of functions that have a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear representation.

Example II.26
.

The map
w\mapsto(-1)^{|w|}|w|

from
Example

II.19

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular function, hence
it is a
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series. It has the following

\mathbb{Z}
-linear representation:

\left(\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\end{pmatrix},w\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}^{|w|},\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}\right).

Note that the eigenvalues of any matrix in
\mu(A^{*})

are
1
or
-1
.

Example II.27
.

The function
w\mapsto(-2)^{|w|}

from
Example

II.16

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series that is not a
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular function.
It can be represented via
((1),\mu,(1))
where

\mu(w)=((-2)^{|w|})
for all
w\in A^{*}
.
Observe that for all
n\geq 1
, there exists
a matrix in
\mu(A^{*})
whose eigenvalue has modulus

2^{n}>1
.

A
\mathbb{Z}
-linear representation
(I,\mu,F)
of a function
f

is said to be

minimal
, when it has minimal dimension
n
among
all the possible representations of
f
.
Given a matrix
M\in\mathop{{\mathcal{M}^{n,n}}}(\mathbb{Z})
, we let
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Spec}}}(M)\subseteq\mathbb{C}

be its
spectrum
, which is the set of
all its (complex)
eigenvalues
.
If
S\subseteq\mathop{{\mathcal{M}^{n,n}}}(\mathbb{Z})
,
we let
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Spec}}}(S)\coloneqq\bigcup_{M\in S}\operatorname{{\mathsf{Spec}}}(M)

be the union of the spectrums.
Finally, let
B(0,1)\coloneqq\{{x\in\mathbb{C}}~\colon{|x|\leq 1}\}

be the unit disc and

\mathbb{U}\coloneqq\{{x\in\mathbb{C}}~\colon{\exists n\geq 1,x^{n}=1}\}

be the roots of unity.

Now, we show that
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular functions can
be characterized through the eigenvalues of
\mathbb{Z}
-linear representations.
More precisely,
Theorem

II.31

will relate the asymptotic growth of a series to the
spectrum of the set of matrices
\mu(A^{*})
. As a first step,
let us observe that the eigenvalues occurring in a minimal representation
can be revealed by iterating words.

Lemma II.28
.

Let
f\colon A^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series and

(I,\mu,F)
be a minimal
\mathbb{Z}
-linear representation of
f
.
Let
w\in A^{*}
and
\lambda\in\operatorname{{\mathsf{Spec}}}(\mu(w))
.
There exist coefficients

\alpha_{i,j}\in\mathbb{C}

for
1 \leq i,j\leq n
, and words
u_{1},v_{1},\dots,u_{n},v_{n}\in A^{*}
such that

\lambda^{X}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\alpha_{i,j}f(v_{i}w^{X}u_{j})

for all
X\geq 0
.

Now, we refine the notion of polynomial
growth to explicit the behaviour of
a function when iterating factors.

Definition II.29
.

Let
N>0
. A function
f\colon A^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is

ultimately
N
-polynomial

whenever there exists

M\geq 0
such that
for all
\ell\geq 0
, for all

\alpha_{0},w_{1},\alpha_{1},\dots,w_{\ell},\alpha_{\ell}\in A^{*}
,
there exists
P\in\mathbb{Q}[X_{1},\dots,X_{\ell}]
,
such that

f(\alpha_{0}w_{1}^{NX_{1}}\alpha_{1}\cdots w_{\ell}^{NX_{\ell}}\alpha_{\ell})=P(X_{1},\dots,X_{\ell})
,
whenever
X_{1},\dots,X_{\ell}\geq M
.

In this section we only need to have

\ell=1
, but
Definition

II.29
has been made generic
so that it can be reused in
Section

V
when dealing
with aperiodicity. Now, we observe that ultimate polynomiality
is preserved under taking sums, external
\mathbb{Z}
-products
and Cauchy products.
Lemma

II.30
also provides
a fine-grained control over the value
N
of ultimate
N
-polynomiality,
that will mostly be useful in
Section

V
.

Lemma II.30
.

Let
f,g\colon A^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}
be (respectively) ultimately

N_{1}
-polynomial
and ultimately
N_{2}
-polynomial, then:

•

f+g
and
f\mathop{{\otimes}}g

are ultimately
(N_{1}\times N_{2})
-polynomial;

•

\delta f
is ultimately
N_{1}
-polynomial
for
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Furthermore, for every
regular language
L
, there exists
N>0

such that
\mathbf{1}_{L}
is ultimately
N
-polynomial.

Now, we have all the elements to prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem II.31

(Polynomial growth and eigenvalues)
.

Let
f:A^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, the following are equivalent:

1.

f
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular function;

2.

f
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series
that is ultimately
N
-polynomial
for some
N>0
;

3.

f
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series
and for all minimal
\mathbb{Z}
-linear representations
(I,\mu,F)
of
f
,

\operatorname{{\mathsf{Spec}}}(\mu(A^{*}))\subseteq\mathbb{U}\cup\{0\}
.

4.

f
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series
and for some minimal
\mathbb{Z}
-linear representation
(I,\mu,F)
of
f
,

\operatorname{{\mathsf{Spec}}}(\mu(A^{*}))\subseteq B(0,1)
;

Proof.

Item

4

\Rightarrow

Item

1

is a direct consequence of
[
22
, Theorem 2.6]

and
Theorem

II.20
.

Item

1

\Rightarrow

Item

2

follows from
Lemma

II.30
and
Theorem

II.20
.

For
Item

2

\Rightarrow

Item

3
,
let
(I,\mu,F)
be a minimal representation of
f
in
\mathbb{Z}
,
of dimension
n\geq 0
.
Let
w\in A^{*}
and
\lambda\in\operatorname{{\mathsf{Spec}}}(\mu(w))
.
Thanks to
Lemma

II.28
,
there exists
\alpha_{i,j},u_{i},v_{j}
for
1 \leq i,j\leq n
, such that

\lambda^{X}=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\alpha_{i,j}f(v_{i}w^{X}u_{j})

for
X
large enough. By assumption, for all

, there exists

N_{i,j}>0
such that

is a polynomial for

(i.e. the product of the

is a polynomial for

large enough,

Hence

, which implies
that
\lambda\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{U}
.

Item

3

\Rightarrow

Item

4
is obvious.
∎

Remark II.32
.

3
of

.

Leveraging the proof scheme used

,

,

,

.

Beware that
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Spec}}}(\mu(A))\subseteq\{{0}\}\cup\mathbb{U}

III
Free Variable Minimization and Growth Rate

In this section, we study the membership problem
from
\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}
to
\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k}
for a given
k\geq 0
.
As observed in
II.15
,
if
f\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k}
then

|f(w)|=\mathcal{O}(|w|^{k})
. We show
that this asymptotic behavior completely
characterizes
\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k}
inside
\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}
.
This statement is formalized in
Theorem

III.3
,
which also provides both a decision procedure
and an effective conversion algorithm.
It turns out that
Theorem

III.3
is
also a stepping stone towards computing
the residual automaton of a function
f\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}
,
which is done in
Section

IV
.

This can be understood as a result
that “minimizes” the number of free variables needed
to describe a
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular function.
As such, it
is tightly connected with the “pebble minimization”
results that exists
for (word-to-word) polyregular functions

[
17
]

and
\mathbb{N}
-polyregular functions

[
14
]
.
However, these results cannot be used as black box theorems
to minimize the number of free variables of
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular
functions because the negative coefficients of the latter
induce non-trivial behaviors.

To capture the growth rate of
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular functions,
we shall introduce a
quantitative variant of the traditional
pumping lemmas. Before that, let us
extend the
\mathcal{O}
notation to multivariate functions

f,g:\mathbb{N}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
as follows:
we say that
f=\mathcal{O}(g)
whenever
there exist
N,C\geq 0
such that

|f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|\leq C|g(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|
for every

x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\geq N
.
We similarly extend the notation
f(x)=\Omega(g(x))
to multivariate
functions.

Definition III.1
.

A function
f\colon A^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}
is

k
-pumpable
whenever there exist

\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{k}\in A^{*}
,

w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\in A^{*}
,
such that

|f(\alpha_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}^{X_{i}}\alpha_{i})|=\Omega(|X_{1}+\cdots+X_{k}|^{k})
.

Example III.2
.

For all
k\geq 0
, for all
f\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k}
,
f
is not
(k+1)
-pumpable
because
|f(w)|=\mathcal{O}(|w|^{k})
.

The equivalence between
Item

1

and
Item

2
in
Theorem

III.3

is known since
[
12
]
. However,
the equivalence with
Item

3

and the effectivity of the result are novel.

Theorem III.3

(Free Variable Minimization)
.

Let
f\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}
and
k\geq 0
. The following conditions are equivalent:

1.

f\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k}
;

2.

|f(w)|=\mathcal{O}(|w|^{k})
;

3.

f
is not
(k+1)
-pumpable.

Furthermore, the minimal
k

such that
f\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k}
is computable,
and the construction is effective.

The proof of
Theorem

III.3
is done via induction on
k
,
and follows directly from the following induction step, to which
we devote the rest of
Section

III
.

Induction Step III.4
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
f\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k}
. The following conditions are equivalent:

1.

f\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k-1}
;

2.

|f(w)|=\mathcal{O}(|w|^{k-1})
;

3.

f
is not
k
-pumpable.

Moreover this property can be decided and
the construction is effective.

Beware that one must be able to pump several factors at once
to detect the growth rate, as illustrated in the following example.
This has to be contrasted with
Remark

II.33
.

Example III.5
.

Let
f:a^{k}b^{\ell}\mapsto k\times\ell
and
w\mapsto 0
otherwise.
The function
f
is
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular and
2
-pumpable,
however,
f(\alpha_{0}w^{X}\alpha_{1})=\mathcal{O}(X)
for every triple
\alpha_{0},w,\alpha_{1}\in A^{*}
.

Our proof of
III.4
is built upon
factorization forests.
Given a morphism
\mu\colon A^{*}\to M
into a finite monoid and

, a
\mu
-forest of

is a forest that can be represented
as a word over
\hat{A}\coloneqq A\uplus\{\langle,\rangle\}

as follows.

Definition III.6

(Factorization forest
[
23
]
)
.

Given a monoid morphism

Thanks to

VI
Outlook

This paper describes a robust class of functions,
which admits several characterizations in terms
of logics, rational expressions, rational series and transducers.
Furthermore, two natural class membership problems
(free variable minimization and first-order definability) are shown decidable.
We believe that these results together with the technical tools
introduced to prove them open the range towards
a vast study of
\mathbb{Z}
- and
\mathbb{N}
-polyregular functions.
Now, let us discuss a few tracks which
seem to be promising for future work.

Weaker logics

Boolean combinations of existential
first-order formulas define a well-known subclass of first-order logic,
often denoted
\mathcal{B}(\exists{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}})
. Over finite words,
\mathcal{B}(\exists{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}})
-sentences
describe the celebrated class of
piecewise testable languages
(see e.g.
[
6
]
).
In our quantitative setting, one could define for all
k\geq 0

the class of linear combinations of the counting formulas from
\mathcal{B}(\exists{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}})_{k}
,
as we did for
\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}_{k}
(resp.
\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}SF}_{k}
) with
{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}_{k}
(resp.
{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}_{k}
).
While this class seems to be a good candidate for defining
“piecewise testable
\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular functions”, it
does not admit a free variable minimization theorem depending on the
growth rate of the functions. Indeed, let
A\coloneqq\{a,b\}

and consider the indicator function
\mathbf{1}_{aA^{*}}={\#}{\varphi}

for
\varphi(x)\coloneqq a(x)\land\forall y.y\geq x\in\mathcal{B}(\exists{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}})_{1}
.
Even if
|\mathbf{1}_{aA^{*}}(w)|=\mathcal{O}(1)
, this function cannot be written
as a linear combination of counting formulas from
\mathcal{B}(\exists{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}})_{0}
.
Indeed, if we assume the converse, then

\mathbf{1}_{aA^{*}}
could be written
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{i}\mathbf{1}_{L_{i}}

for some piecewise testable languages
L_{i}
,
which implies that
aA^{*}
would be piecewise testable, which is not the case.

Star-free
\mathbb{N}
-polyregular functions

A very natural question is, given an

\mathbb{N}
-polyregular function (recall that it is an element of

\mathsf{\mathbb{N}Poly}\coloneqq\operatorname{{\mathsf{Span}}}_{\mathbb{N}}({\#}{\varphi}:\varphi\in{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}})
)
to decide whether it is in fact
a
star-free
\mathbb{N}
-polyregular
function
(i.e. an element of
\mathsf{\mathbb{N}SF}\coloneqq\operatorname{{\mathsf{Span}}}_{\mathbb{N}}({\#}{\varphi}:\varphi\in{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}})
).
In this setting, we conjecture that

\mathsf{\mathbb{N}SF}=\mathsf{\mathbb{N}Poly}\cap\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}SF}
.
This question seems to be challenging. Indeed, the techniques introduced
in the current paper cannot directly be applied
to solve it, since the residual automaton (see
Section

V
)
of an
\mathbb{N}
-polyregular function may need
labels which are not
\mathbb{N}
-polyregular, or even not nonnegative.
In other words, replacing the output group by an output monoid
seems to prevent from representing
the functions with canonical objects
based on residuals.

Star-free
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series

In
Figure

1
, there is no generalization of the class
\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}SF}

among the whole class of
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series.
We
are not
aware of a way to define a class of
“star free
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series”, neither with logics nor
with
\mathbb{Z}
-rational expressions. Indeed, allowing the use of Kleene star
for series automatically builds the whole class of

\mathbb{Z}
-rational series (including the indicator
functions of all regular languages).

From a logical standpoint,
it is tempting to go from polynomial behaviors to
exponential ones by shifting from
first-order free variables to second-order free variables.
While this approach actually
captures the whole class of
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series,
it fails to circumscribe star-freeness. To make the above
statement precise, let us write

{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}^{X}
(resp.
{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}^{X}
) as the set
of
{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}
(resp.
{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}
) formulas with free
second-order variables, i.e. of the shape

\varphi(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
. Given

\varphi\in{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}^{X}
, we let
{\#}{\varphi}(w)\colon A^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}

be the function that counts second-order valuations.
As an example of the expressiveness of this model,
let us illustrate how to compute
w\mapsto(-2)^{|w|}\not\in\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}
.

Example VI.1
.

Let
\varphi(X)\coloneqq\top
, then
{\#}{\varphi}(w)=2^{|w|}
.
Let
\psi(X)
be the first-order formula
stating that
X
contains the first position of the word,

X
contains the last position of the word,
and if
x\in X
, then
x+1\not\in X
and
x+2\in X
.
It is an easy check that
{\#}{\psi}=\mathbf{1}_{\textnormal{odd}}
,
even though
\psi\in{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}^{X}
(but recall that

\mathbf{1}_{\textnormal{odd}}
is the indicator function
of a non star-free regular language).
Now,
w\mapsto(-2)^{|w|}

equals

{\#}{\varphi}\times(2{\#}{\psi}-1)
.

We are now ready to explain formally how
both
{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}^{X}
and
{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}^{X}
capture
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series.

Proposition VI.2
.

For every function
f\colon A^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}
,
the following are equivalent:

1.

f
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series;

2.

f\in\operatorname{{\mathsf{Span}}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\{{{\#}{\varphi}}~\colon{\varphi\in{\operatorname{\mathsf{MSO}}}^{X}}\})
;

3.

f\in\operatorname{{\mathsf{Span}}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\{{{\#}{\varphi}}~\colon{\varphi\in{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}^{X}}\})
.

In our setting, it seems natural to say that

w\mapsto 2^{|w|}
should be a star-free
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series,
contrary to
w\mapsto(-2)^{|w|}

(as observed in
Example

V.21
, this approach contrasts with
the weighted logics of Droste and Gastin
[
27
]
,
for which
(-2)^{|w|}
is considered as “star free”).
Recall that in
Theorem

V.18
, we have characterized

\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}SF}
as the class of series whose
spectrum falls in
\{0,1\}
.
Following this result, we conjecture that a “good” notion
of star-free
\mathbb{Z}
-rational series could be those
whose spectrum falls in the set
\mathbb{R}_{+}
of
nonnegative real numbers. This way, exponential
growth is allowed (e.g. for
w\mapsto 2^{|w|}
)
but no periodic behaviors (e.g. for
w\mapsto(-2)^{|w|}
).

be a type, it is obtained
as the type of some tuple
\vec{x}
of independent leaves
in some

is composed of independent leaves, hence

.

.

that

,

.
To a tuple

Remark D.6

If

is a binary tree.

Given a map

we let

Claim D.7

For every

is ultimately a
\mathbb{Z}
-polynomial
in

Assume that

is non-empty. Then

is done by
fixing the image of

to

among

.
∎

.

Proof of

Let

which is
ultimately

-polynomial
and

be a

Since ultimate

are
ultimately
1
-polynomial
(by definition of a

-residual

Let

for some

We want to show that
\delta(q_{0},\alpha w)=\delta(q_{0},\alpha)

Since

and
\delta(q_{0},\alpha w)=\delta(q_{0},\alpha\alpha w^{nX+1})

for all

\delta(q_{0},\alpha w^{nX+1})=\delta(q_{0},\alpha w^{nX})

Let

We want to show that
({\alpha w^{nM+1}}\mathop{{\triangleright}}{f})\mathrel{{\sim_{k-1}}}({\alpha w^{nM}}\mathop{{\triangleright}}{f})
, i.e.
|({\alpha w^{nM+1}}\mathop{{\triangleright}}{f})(w)-({\alpha w^{nM}}\mathop{{\triangleright}}{f})(w)|=\mathcal{O}(|w|^{k-1})

\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}Poly}
. For this, let us pick any
\alpha_{0},w_{1},\alpha_{1},\cdots,w_{k},\alpha_{k}\in A^{*}
.
By

III.3
, it is sufficient to show that:

\begin{array}[]{rl}&|({\alpha w^{nM}}\mathop{{\triangleright}}{f}-{\alpha w^{nM+1}}\mathop{{\triangleright}}{f})(\alpha_{0}w_{1}^{X_{1}}\cdots w_{k}^{X_{k}}\alpha_{k}))|\\

F-D

Proof of
Proposition

V.17

The proof of the proposition is essentially the same as
Proposition

II.13

by noticing that everything remains
{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}
-definable. We will
underline

the parts where the two proofs differ, and in particular
when using stability properties of star-free polyregular functions.

We first show that any
star free

\mathbb{Z}
-polyregular
function can be written under the form
\operatorname{\mathsf{sum}}\circ g

where
g:A^{*}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}^{*}
is
star-free
polyregular.
This is a consequence of the following claims.

Claim F.1
.

For all
\varphi\in\underline{{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}}
, there exists a
star-free
polyregular function

f\colon A^{*}\to\{{\pm 1}\}^{*}
such that
{\#}{\varphi}=\operatorname{\mathsf{sum}}\circ f
.

Proof.

Star-free
polyregular functions are characterized
in
[
21
, Theorem 7]

as the functions computed by
(multidimensional)
{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}
-interpretations.
Recall that an
{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}
-interpretation
of dimension
k\in\mathbb{N}

is given by a
\underline{{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}}
formula
\varphi_{\leq}(\vec{x},\vec{y})

defining a total ordering
over
k
-tuples of positions,
a
\underline{{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}}
formula
\varphi^{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dom}}}(\vec{x})
that selects
valid positions, and
\underline{{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}}
formulas
\varphi^{a}(\vec{x})
that
place the letters over the output word

[
21
, Definition 1 and 2]
.
In our specific situation, letting

\varphi_{\leq}
be the usual lexicographic ordering of positions
(which is
\underline{{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}}
-definable)
and placing the letter
1
over every element of the output
is enough: the only thing left to do is select
enough positions of the output word.
For that, we let
\varphi^{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dom}}}
be defined as
\varphi
itself.
It is an easy check that this
\underline{{\operatorname{\mathsf{FO}}}}
-interpretation
precisely computes
1^{f(w)}
over
w
,
hence computes
f
when post-composed with
\operatorname{\mathsf{sum}}
.
∎

Claim F.2
.

The set
\{{\operatorname{\mathsf{sum}}\circ f}~\colon{f\colon A^{*}\to\{{\pm 1}\}^{*}\text{ \lx@text@underline{star-free} polyregular}}\}

is closed under sums and external
\mathbb{Z}
-products.

Proof.

Notice that
\operatorname{\mathsf{sum}}\circ f+\operatorname{\mathsf{sum}}\circ g=\operatorname{\mathsf{sum}}\circ(f\cdot g)
where
f\cdot g(w)\coloneqq f(w)\cdot g(w)
.
As
star-free
polyregular functions are closed under concatenation

[
7
]
,
the set of interest is closed under sums.
To prove that it is closed under external
\mathbb{Z}
-products,
it suffices to show that it is closed under negation.
This follows because one can permute the

1
and
-1
in the output of a
star-free
polyregular function
(
star-free
polyregular functions are closed under post-composition
by a morphism
[
7
, Theorem 2.6]
).
∎

Let us consider a
star-free
polyregular function

g\colon A^{*}\to\{{\pm 1}\}^{*}
.
The maps
g_{+}\colon w\mapsto|g(w)|_{1}

and
g_{-}\colon w\mapsto|g(w)|_{-1}

are
star-free
polyregular functions with unary output
(since they correspond to a post-composition by
the
star-free
polyregular function which removes some letter, and polyregular functions are
closed under post-composition by a regular function
[
7
]
).
Hence
g_{-}
and
g_{+}
are star-free polyregular functions with unary output,
a.k.a.
star-free

\mathbb{N}
-polyregular functions.
As a consequence,

{\operatorname{\mathsf{sum}}}\circ g=g_{+}-g_{-}
lies in
\mathsf{\mathbb{Z}SF}
.

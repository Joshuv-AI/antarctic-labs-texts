Hélène Esnault and Claude Sabbah

Abstract
. We construct a logarithmic model of connections on smooth quasi-projective n n -dimensional geometrically irreducible varieties defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 0 . It consists of a good compactification of the variety together with ( n + 1 ) (n+1) lattices on it which are stabilized by log differential operators, and compute algebraically de Rham cohomology. The construction is derived from the existence of good Deligne-Malgrange lattices, a theorem of Kedlaya and Mochizuki which consists first in eliminating the turning points. Moreover, we show that a logarithmic model obtained in this way, called a good model, yields a formula predicted by Michael Groechenig, computing the class of the characteristic variety of the underlying 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} -module in the K K -theory group of the variety.

), the lemma applies to the graded complex
{\rm gr}^{F}({\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{X},\delta_{\mathrm{triv}})
and therefore each
{\rm gr}^{F}_{p}({\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{X},\delta_{\mathrm{triv}})
has cohomology in degree zero at most and, if the supplementary strictness assumption on the quotients of
{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}
is fulfilled, the cohomology in degree zero is strict. It follows that each
F_{p}({\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{X},\delta_{\mathrm{triv}})
satisfies the same property since
F_{-1}({\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{X},\delta_{\mathrm{triv}})=0
. Passing to the inductive limit, we conclude that so does the complex
({\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{X},\delta_{\mathrm{triv}})
.
∎

B.3.
A criterion for having
{\mathcal{N}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(\log D)}{\mathcal{D}}_{X}\simeq{\mathcal{N}}(*D)

Assumption
B.4
and the corresponding simplifying notation remain in order in this section.

For
I\subset\{1,\dots,\ell\}
we set
I^{\mathrm{c}}=\{1,\dots,\ell\}\setminus I
,
D_{I}=\bigcap_{i\in I}D_{i}
and, for each
j\in I^{\mathrm{c}}
,
D_{I,j}=D_{I}\cap D_{j}
and
D_{I}^{(I^{\mathrm{c}})}=\bigcup_{j\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}\setminus I}D_{I,j}
, so that
D_{I}^{(I^{\mathrm{c}})}
is a divisor with normal crossings in
D_{I}
. If
I=\varnothing
, we have
D_{I}=X
and
D_{I}^{(I^{\mathrm{c}})}=D
.

We identify
i_{D_{I}}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
with
{\mathcal{O}}_{D_{I}}
and
i_{D_{I}}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(\log D)
with
{\mathcal{D}}_{D_{I}}(\log D_{I}^{(I^{\mathrm{c}})})[(\mathrm{Eu}_{i})_{i\in I}]
, where
\mathrm{Eu}_{i}
is the class of
x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}
. It is a central element in this ring.

The following result is inspired by
[

Proposition B.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{N}}
be a right
{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(\log D)
-module. Together with the assumption above, assume the following property: for any (possibly empty) subset

(2)

for all

{\mathcal{N}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{D}}_{X}(\log D)}{\mathcal{D}}_{X}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\mathcal{N}}(*D)

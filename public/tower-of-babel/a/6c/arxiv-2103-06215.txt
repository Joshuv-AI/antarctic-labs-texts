1.
Introduction

1.1.
Intersection cohomology

Intersection cohomology
\mathrm{IH}^{\cdot}(X,\mathbb{Q})
and the BBDG Decomposition Theorem
[
5
]
are important tools in the study of the topology of algebraic varieties. They have many applications in representation theory, see
[
32
]
, or more recently in construction of representation of
\mathcal{W}
-algebras due to Braverman–Finkelberg–Nakajima
[
8
]
and in the study of (Kontsevich–Soibelman) Cohomological Hall algebras
[
28
]
,
[
15
]
.
It is an important problem to define a K-theoretic version of intersection cohomology and it is expected that such a theory will have applications in representation theory.
More generally, one can try to construct categorifications of intersection cohomology.

1.2.
Noncommutative resolutions

A natural place to look for such categorifications is inside noncommutative resolutions (NCRs) of
X
. There are more NCRs than usual resolution of singularities. A conjecture of Bondal–Orlov
[
7
, Section 5]
says that there exists a
minimal
NCR, i.e. a category admissible inside any other NCR of
X
.

We look for natural candidates of minimal NCRs
\mathbb{D}
of good moduli spaces
X
of Artin stacks
\mathcal{X}
. Inspired by the Decomposition Theorem, we expect the periodic cyclic homology
\text{HP}_{\cdot}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
to have
\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{IH}^{\cdot+2i}(X,\mathbb{C})
as a direct summand. We then try to construct noncommutative motives
\mathbb{D}^{\text{nc}}
inside
\mathbb{D}
whose periodic cyclic homology is
\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{IH}^{\cdot+2i}(X,\mathbb{C})
.

1.6.
Intersection K-theory

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a symmetric stack satisfying Assumption C and let
X
be its good moduli space. Then
\textbf{P}^{\leqslant 0}\mathrm{H}^{\cdot}(\mathcal{X})\cong\mathrm{IH}^{\cdot}(X)
. Given Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, we propose to call
\mathrm{K}_{\cdot}(\mathbb{D}^{\text{nc}}(\mathcal{X}))=:\mathrm{IK}_{\cdot}(X)

the intersection K-theory of

X
.
There is a Chern character map

\text{ch}:\mathrm{IK}_{\cdot}(X)\to\mathrm{IH}^{\cdot}(X).

Further, from the construction of
\mathrm{IK}_{\cdot}(X)
, we obtain a natural surjection

\mathrm{K}_{\cdot}(\mathcal{X})\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{IK}_{\cdot}(X).

The analogous statement in cohomology was proved by Kirwan
[
27
]
. Further, in Subsection
5.6.2
, we show that if the Kirwan resolution is a scheme, then

\mathrm{HP}_{i}\left(\mathbb{D}^{\text{nc}}(\mathcal{X})\right)\cong\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{IH}^{i+2j}(X,\mathbb{C}).

We use the construction of K-theory of quiver varieties to prove a version of a Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt Theorem for K-theoretic Hall algebras of quivers with potential
[
35
, Theorem 1.2]
. For example, for a quiver
Q
, the K-theoretic Hall algebra
\text{KHA}(Q,0)
for zero potential is generated by spaces which are twisted versions of
\mathrm{IK}_{\cdot}(X(d))
, where
X(d)
is the coarse space of representation of
Q
of dimension
d
.

1.7.
Previous work on intersection K-theory.

There are other approaches of defining intersection K-theory in particular cases. Cautis
[
11
]
, Cautis–Kamnitzer
[
12
]
have an approach for categorification of intersection sheaves for certain subvarieties of the affine Grassmannian.For varieties with a cellular stratification, Eberhardt proposed a definition in
[
16
]
.

A related problem is defining intersection Chow groups. Corti–Hanamura
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
proposed two approaches towards intersection Chow groups for general varieties
X
, one which proves a version of the decomposition theorem under some conjectures.
In
[
35
]
, we propose a definition of intersection (graded)
\text{gr}^{\cdot}\mathrm{K}
-theory of any singular variety
X
which is a summand of
\text{gr}^{\cdot}\mathrm{K}_{\cdot}(Y)
for any resolution of singularities
Y\to X
; the associated graded is taken with respect to the codimension filtration, so its zeroth level is a version of intersection Chow groups.
De Cataldo–Migliorini
[
9
]
proposed a definition of intersection Chow motive for singularities with a semismall resolution.

1.8.
Future directions

The definitions of
\mathbb{D}^{\text{nc}}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathrm{IK}_{\cdot}(X)
are for symmetric stacks
\mathcal{X}
. It is worth trying to find analogues of these constructions beyond symmetric stacks. One idea is to look for symmetric substacks
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{X}
whose complement is a union of
\Theta
-strata and thus have good moduli spaces
X^{\prime}
. It is true that
\mathrm{IH}^{\cdot}(X)
is a direct summand of
\mathrm{IH}^{\cdot}(X^{\prime})
, but we do not know how to characterize the difference between them.

The discussion in Subsection
1.2
serves as motivation for our work, but we do not make progress towards the Bondal–Orlov conjecture. In general,
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathbb{D}^{\text{nc}}(\mathcal{X})
are different. However, in the cases in which they are equal, for example for quotients
V/T
where
T
is a torus and
V
is a
T
-representation,
it is natural to guess that
\mathbb{D}(V/T)
is minimal in the sense of Bondal–Orlov. The category
\mathbb{D}(V/T)
is indecomposable
[
39
, Appendix A]
, so it should be minimal if the Bondal–Orlov conjecture is true for this particular class of singularities.

We plan to return to these questions in future work.

2.2.1.

Let

be a triangulated category, and let

be triangulated subcategories. We say that there is a

(i)

(ii) the smallest triangulated subcategory of

containing all
\mathcal{A}_{i}

\mathcal{T}
.

2.2.2.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category, and let
\mathcal{A}
be a subcategory.
\mathcal{A}
is called

in
\mathcal{T}
if there exists a semi-orthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{T}=\langle-,\mathcal{A}\rangle
. Equivalently, the inclusion functor
\mathcal{A}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{T}
has a right adjoint.

2.2.3.

In this paper, we say that a triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
is

if it is admissible inside

for a smooth DM stack

. We say that
\mathcal{D}
is

over a given variety

if it is admissible in

for

2.2.4.

Let
X
be a variety. We say that a smooth and proper over
X
triangulated category

is a

if there exists an adjoint pair of functors

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle:\mathcal{D}\to D^{b}(X),

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle:\text{Perf}\,(X)\to\mathcal{D}

such that

.
The definition is slightly more general than the definition in
[

Example.
Let

be a variety with rational singularities and let

be a resolution of singularities. Then

and

.

2.5.
Noncommutative motives

We briefly explain the definition of noncommutative motives. A general reference is
[
41
]
.
Denote by
dgcat
the category of (small) dg categories over
\mathbb{C}
. It has a Quillen model structure whose weak equivalences are derived Morita equivalences. Denote by
Hmo
the corresponding homotopy category.
The universal category through which all additive invariants factor (examples include cyclic homology, K-theory, and related constructions) is a smaller (additive) category
\textbf{Hmo}_{0}
with objects dg categories and morphisms

\text{Hom}_{\textbf{Hmo}_{0}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=\mathrm{K}_{0}\left(\text{rep}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\right)

where
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are dg categories and
\text{rep}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\subset D^{b}(\mathcal{A}^{\text{op}}\otimes\mathcal{B})
is the full subcategory of bimodules
X
such that
X(a,-)\in\text{Perf}\,(\mathcal{B})
for any object
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Consider the functor
\mathcal{U}:\textbf{dgcat}\to\textbf{Hmo}_{0}
.

We consider the category
\textbf{Hmo}_{0;\mathbb{Q}}^{\natural}
, the idempotent completion of the
\mathbb{Q}
-linearization of

\textbf{Hmo}_{0}
. We call its elements (by a slight abuse) noncommutative motives; the original definition considers the subcategory of
\textbf{Hmo}_{0;\mathbb{Q}}^{\natural}
generated by proper and smooth dg categories, but in our case we need to allow proper and smooth categories over (not necessarily proper)
X
.

2.6.
A preliminary result

The following type of result in used by Špenko–Van den Bergh in their construction of NCRs.

Let
A
be a smooth affine variety with an action of a reductive group
G
and let
\mathcal{X}=A/G
. For a locally free sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathcal{X}
, its stalk at the origin is a representation
\Gamma
of
G
. We call
\Gamma

the associated representation of

\mathcal{F}
.

We state the following result for future reference, the proof is same as
[
21
, Section 3.2]
and it uses an explicit Köszul resolution for pushforward along the map
A^{\lambda\geqslant 0}/G^{\lambda\geqslant 0}\hookrightarrow A/G^{\lambda\geqslant 0}
and the Borel-Bott-Weil Theorem for the map
A/G^{\lambda\geqslant 0}\to A/G
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\lambda
be a cocharacter of
G
and consider the diagram of attracting loci

A^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}\xleftarrow{q}A^{\lambda\geqslant 0}/G^{\lambda\geqslant 0}\xrightarrow{p}A/G.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a locally free sheaf on
A^{\lambda}/G^{\lambda}
with associated representation
\Gamma(\chi)
where
\chi
is a dominant weight of
G
.
Then there is a complex

\left(\bigoplus_{I}\mathcal{F}_{I}[|I|-\ell(w)],d\right)\to p_{*}q^{*}\mathcal{F},

where the terms of the complex correspond to subsets
I\subset\{\beta|\,\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle<0\}
,
\mathcal{F}_{I}
is a locally free sheaf with associated representation

\Gamma\left((\chi-\sigma_{I})^{+}\right),

where

\sigma_{I}=\sum_{\beta\in I}\beta
,

(\chi-\sigma_{I})^{+}
is the dominant Weyl-shifted conjugate of
\chi-\sigma_{I}
if it exists, and zero otherwise,
and

w
is the element of the Weyl group such that
w*(\chi-\sigma_{I})
is dominant or zero of length
\ell(w)
.

\pi_{*}\iota\Phi\pi^{*}(\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{F}.

The complexes

have weight zero for any cocharacter

The last equality follows from

Finally, the category

is smooth and proper over

, and thus it is an NCR of

.
∎

m_{\lambda\nu}(x):=\frac{1}{|W^{\nu}|}\sum_{J^{\prime}\subset\textbf{A}^{\mu}_{w_{s}^{-1}\lambda}}\sum_{v\in W}v\left(\frac{(-1)^{c^{\nu}_{\nu^{\prime}}+|J^{\prime}|}w_{s}^{-1}\left(yq^{-\mathcal{N}^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\nu}}\right)q^{-\sigma_{J^{\prime}}}}{\prod_{\beta\in\textbf{n}}(1-q^{-\beta})}\right),

and thus

\Delta_{\mu}m_{\lambda\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{|W^{\nu}|}\sum_{J^{\prime}\subset\textbf{A}^{\mu}_{w_{s}^{-1}\lambda}}\sum_{v\in W^{\mu}}v\left(\frac{(-1)^{c^{\nu}_{\nu^{\prime}}+|J^{\prime}|}w_{s}^{-1}\left(yq^{-\mathcal{N}^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\nu}}\right)q^{-\sigma_{J^{\prime}}}}{\prod_{\beta\in\textbf{n}^{\mu}}(1-q^{-\beta})}\right).

This is the same as the composition of left-bottom maps:

\displaystyle\widetilde{m^{\mu}_{\nu}}\Delta^{\lambda}_{\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{m^{\mu}_{\nu}}\Delta^{\lambda}_{\nu}\left(\sum_{v\in W^{\lambda}}v\left(\frac{y}{\prod_{\beta\in\textbf{n}^{\lambda}}(1-q^{-\beta})}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{m^{\mu}_{\nu}}\sum_{v\in W^{\nu}}v\left(\frac{y}{\prod_{\beta\in\textbf{n}^{\nu}}(1-q^{-\beta})}\right)

\displaystyle=m^{\mu}_{\nu^{\prime}}\sum_{v\in W^{\nu}}v\left(\frac{(-1)^{c^{\nu}_{\nu^{\prime}}}w_{s}^{-1}\left(yq^{-\mathcal{N}^{\nu}_{\nu^{\prime}}}\right)}{\prod_{\beta\in\textbf{n}^{\nu}}(1-q^{-\beta})}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|W^{\nu}|}\sum_{J^{\prime}\subset\textbf{A}^{\mu}_{w_{s}^{-1}\lambda}}\sum_{v\in W^{\mu}}v\left(\frac{(-1)^{c^{\nu}_{\nu^{\prime}}+|J^{\prime}|}w_{s}^{-1}\left(yq^{-\mathcal{N}^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\nu}}\right)q^{-\sigma_{J^{\prime}}}}{\prod_{\beta\in\textbf{n}^{\mu}}(1-q^{-\beta})}\right)

where the second equality follows from Proposition
5.7
and the last one by Proposition
5.4
.
∎

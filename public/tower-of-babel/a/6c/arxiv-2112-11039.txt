A finite sum involving generalized falling factorial polynomials and degenerate Eulerian polynomials
Taekyun Kim, DAE SAN KIM

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is twofold. Firstly, we investigate a finite sum involving the generalized falling factorial polynomials, in some special cases of which we express it in terms of the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials and the degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials. Secondly, we consider the degenerate Eulerian polynomials and deduce the generating function and a recurrence relation for them.

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

The Stirling number of the second
S_{2}(n,k)
is the number of ways to partition a set of
n
objects into
k
nonempty subsets (see (
4
)). The (signed) Stirling number of the first kind
S_{1}(n,k)
is defined such that the number of permutations of
n
elements having exactly
k
cycles is the nonnegative integer
(-1)^{n-k}S_{1}(n,k)=|S_{1}(n,k)|
(see (
3
)). The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
(see (
13
)) and of the first kind
S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
appear most naturally when we replace the powers of
x
by the generalized falling factorial polynomials
(x)_{k,\lambda}
(see (
2
)) in the defining equations (see (
3
)-(
5
), (
13
)).

Eulerian numbers and polynomials have long been studied due to their number-theoretic and combinatorial importance. There are many ways of defining the Eulerian polynomials and numbers, incluing the ones given in (
9
)-(
11
). Let
[n]=\left\{1,2,\dots,n\right\}
. Then a combinatorial interpretation of the Eulerian polynomial
A_{n}(t)
is as follows. For each permutation
\sigma
in the symmetric group
S_{n}
, the descent and the excedance of
\sigma
are respectively given by

\displaystyle D(\sigma)=\left\{i\in[n-1]:\sigma(i)>\sigma(i+1)\right\},

\displaystyle E(\sigma)=\left\{i\in[n-1]:\sigma(i)>i\right\}.

Then
A_{n}(t)
is given by

\displaystyle A_{n}(t)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}t^{d(\sigma)}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}t^{e(\sigma)},

where
d(\sigma)=|D(\sigma)|,e(\sigma)=|E(\sigma)|
. Further, the Eulerian numbers are defined as the coefficients of
A_{n}(t)
.

The aim of this paper is twofold. Firstly, we investigate a finite sum involving the generalized falling factorial polynomials, in some special cases of which we express it in terms of the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second, the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials and the degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials. Secondly, we consider the degenerate Eulerian polynomials and deduce the generating function and a recurrence relation for them.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section 1, we recall the facts that are needed throughout this paper.
In Section 2, we derive the main Theorem 1 which expresses the finite sum over
\alpha\leq k\leq m
of the product
(k)_{\alpha,\lambda}a_{k}
, in terms of the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second. In the case of
a_{k}=1
, we find alternative expressions for the sum, two in terms of the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind and one in terms of the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials. On the other hand, in the case of
a_{k}=x^{k}
, the sum is represented in terms of the degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials. In Section 3, we consider a degenerate version of the Eulerian polynomials, namely the degenerate Eulerian polynomials introduced by Carlitz, find the exponential generating function and the recurrence relation of them and express the sum
\sum_{k=1}^{m}(k)_{n,\lambda}x^{k}
in terms of the degenerate Eulerian polynomials.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponentials are defined by

(1)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11]),

where the generalized falling factorial polynomials
(x)_{n,\lambda}
are given by

(2)

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots\big(x-(n-1)\lambda\big),\quad(n\geq 1).

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=e^{xt},\ \ \lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}(x)_{n,\lambda}=x^{n}\displaystyle
.

In particular, for
\displaystyle x=1,\ e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n,\lambda}}{n!}t^{n}\displaystyle
.

It is well known that the Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(3)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)x^{l},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10]),

where
(x)_{0}=1,\quad(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots\big(x-(n-1)\big),\ (n\geq 1)
.

As the inversion formula of (
3
), the Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(4)

x^{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,l)(x)_{l},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12,13,16-20]).

Recently, Kim-Kim introduced the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind given by

(5)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)(x)_{l,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10,15]).

In addition, the unsigned degenerate Stirling numbers of first kind are defined by

(6)

\langle x\rangle_{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}{n\brack l}_{\lambda}\langle x\rangle_{l,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13]),

where

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle_{0}=1,\quad\langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)(x+n-1),\quad(n\geq 1),

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}=x(x+\lambda)\cdots(x+(n-1)\lambda),\quad(n\geq 1).

From (
5
) and (
6
), we note that

(7)

{n\brack k}_{\lambda}=(-1)^{n-k}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k),\quad(n,k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13]).

The Eulerian numbers are given explicitly by the sum

(8)

\genfrac{<}{>}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}=\sum_{j=0}^{m+1}(-1)^{j}\binom{n+1}{j}(m+1-j)^{n},\,\,(n\geq 1),\,\,\genfrac{<}{>}{0.0pt}{}{0}{0}=1,\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4-8]),

and the Eulerian polynomials are defined by

(9)

A_{n}(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\genfrac{<}{>}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}t^{m},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5,6,8,9]).

Actually,
\genfrac{<}{>}{0.0pt}{}{n}{n}=0
, for
n\geq 1
, and hence
A_{n}(t)
has degree
n-1
, for
n\geq 1
.

From (
9
), we note that

(10)

\frac{A_{n}(t)}{(1-t)^{n+1}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t^{j}(j+1)^{n},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5-9],

and

(11)

\frac{t-1}{t-e^{(t-1)x}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8,9]).

In [5], Carlitz considered the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials given by

(12)

\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=0
,
\beta_{n,\lambda}=\beta_{n,\lambda}(0)
are called the degenerate Bernoulli numbers.

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)=B_{n}(x),\ (n\geq 0)\displaystyle
, where
B_{n}(x)
are the ordinary Bernoulli polynomials given by

\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-21]).

Note that

\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\beta_{l,\lambda}(x)_{n-l,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

As the inversion formula of (
5
), the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(13)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10]).

From (
13
), we note that

(14)

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10]).

By (
14
), we easily get

(15)

S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}(l)_{n,\lambda},\quad(n\geq k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10,15]).

In [14], the degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials are defined by

(16)

\frac{1-u}{e_{\lambda}(t)-u}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n,\lambda}(x|u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
u(\neq 1)\in\mathbb{C}
.

Note that
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}H_{n,\lambda}(x|u)=H_{n}(x|u)
are the ordinary Frobenius-Euler polynomials given by

\frac{1-u}{e^{t}-u}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}(x|u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,2,14,21]).

When
x=0
,
H_{n,\lambda}(0|u)=H_{n,\lambda}(u)
are called the degenerate Frobenius-Euler numbers.

2.
A finite sum involving generalized falling factorial polynomials

Here we derive the main Theorem 1 which represents the finite sum over
\alpha\leq k\leq m
of the product
(k)_{\alpha,\lambda}a_{k}
, in terms of the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second. In the case of
a_{k}=1
, we express the sum in terms of the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind and the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials. Also, in case of
a_{k}=x^{k}
, we represent it in terms of the degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials.

Then we have

(17)

\displaystyle(n)_{\alpha,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)(n)_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)k!\binom{n}{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)k!\binom{n}{k},

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq\alpha
.

By (
17
), for
m\geq\alpha
, we get

(18)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}(k)_{\alpha,\lambda}a_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}a_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,j)j!\binom{k}{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,j)j!\sum_{k=j}^{m}\binom{k}{j}a_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha-1}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,j)j!\sum_{k=j}^{\alpha-1}\binom{k}{j}a_{k}.

Here one should note that
S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,0)=0
, since
\alpha
is a positive integer.
Therefore, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{m}
be any sequence. Then we have

\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}(k)_{\alpha,\lambda}a_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)k!\sum_{l=k}^{m}\binom{l}{k}a_{l}-\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha-1}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)k!\sum_{l=k}^{\alpha-1}\binom{l}{k}a_{l},

where
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq\alpha
.

Let us take
a_{k}=1
in Theorem 1. Then we have

(19)

\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}(k)_{\alpha,\lambda}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)k!\sum_{l=k}^{m}\binom{l}{k}-\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha-1}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)k!\sum_{l=k}^{\alpha-1}\binom{l}{k}.

Note that

(20)

\sum_{l=k}^{m}\binom{l}{k}=\sum_{l=k}^{m}\bigg(\binom{l+1}{k+1}-\binom{l}{k+1}\bigg)=\binom{m+1}{k+1}.

Thus, by (
19
) and (
20
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq\alpha
, we have

\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}(k)_{\alpha,\lambda}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)k!\binom{m+1}{k+1}-\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha-1}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)k!\binom{\alpha}{k+1}.

In [14], the degenerate
\lambda
-binomial coefficients are defined by

(21)

\binom{k}{n}_{\lambda}=\frac{(k)_{n,\lambda}}{n!},\quad(n,k\geq 0).

From Theorem 2 and (
21
), we note that

(22)

\sum_{k=n}^{m}\binom{k}{n}_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=1}^{m}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)k!\binom{m+1}{k+1}-\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)k!\binom{n}{k+1}.

From (
5
), we note that

(23)

\binom{n}{m}=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{k=0}^{m}S_{1,\lambda}(m,k)(n)_{k,\lambda}.

By (
13
), we easily get

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha+1}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha+1,k)(x)_{k}=(x)_{\alpha+1,\lambda}=(x)_{\alpha,\lambda}(x-\alpha\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)(x)_{k}(x-\alpha\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)(x)_{k}(x-k+k-\alpha\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha+1}\Big\{S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k-1)+(k-\alpha\lambda)S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)\Big\}(x)_{k},

because
S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)=0
if
k<0,and\ S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)=0
if
k>\alpha
.

By comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
24
), we get

(25)

S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha+1,k)=S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k-1)+(k-\alpha\lambda)S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k),\quad(0\leq k\leq\alpha+1).

From (
25
), we note that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\binom{m}{j}(j-1)!\Big\{S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha+1,j)+\alpha\lambda S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,j)\Big\}

(26)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\binom{m}{j}(j-1)!\Big\{jS_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,j)+S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,j-1)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\binom{m}{j}j!S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,j)+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\binom{m}{j+1}j!S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bigg(\binom{m}{j}+\binom{m}{j+1}\bigg)j!S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,j)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\binom{m+1}{j+1}j!S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,j).

Therefore, by Theorem 2 and (
26
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq\alpha
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}(k)_{\alpha,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(k-1)!\Big\{S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha+1,k)+\alpha\lambda S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)\Big\}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha-1}\binom{\alpha-1}{k}(k-1)!\Big\{S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha+1,k)+\alpha\lambda S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)\Big\}.

From (
12
), we note that

(27)

\displaystyle\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}^{1-x}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{-t}{e_{\lambda}^{-1}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}^{-x}(t)=\frac{-t}{e_{-\lambda}(-t)-1}e_{-\lambda}^{x}(-t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\beta_{n,-\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
12
) and (
27
) we get

\beta_{n,\lambda}(1-x)=(-1)^{n}\beta_{n,-\lambda}(x),\quad(n\geq 0).

From (
12
), we have

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}e_{\lambda}^{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\big(e_{\lambda}^{m+1}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{\alpha}(t)\big)=\frac{1}{t}\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\big(e_{\lambda}^{m+1}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{\alpha}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j+1}\big(\beta_{j+1,\lambda}(m+1)-\beta_{j+1,\lambda}(\alpha)\big)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}.

On the other hand,

(29)

\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}e_{\lambda}^{k}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}(k)_{j,\lambda}\bigg)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}.

Thus, by (
28
) and (
29
), we get

(30)

\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}(k)_{\alpha,\lambda}=\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\big(\beta_{\alpha+1,\lambda}(m+1)-\beta_{\alpha+1,\lambda}(\alpha)\big).

Therefore, by Theorem 3 and (
30
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq\alpha
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\big(\beta_{\alpha+1,\lambda}(m+1)-\beta_{\alpha+1,\lambda}(\alpha)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(k-1)!\Big\{S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha+1,k)+\alpha\lambda S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)\Big\}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha-1}\binom{\alpha-1}{k}(k-1)!\Big\{S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha+1,k)+\alpha\lambda S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)\Big\}.

Let us take
a_{k}=x^{k}
in Theorem 2. Then we have

(31)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}(k)_{\alpha,\lambda}x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)k!\sum_{l=k}^{m}\binom{l}{k}x^{l}-\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha-1}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)k!\sum_{l=k}^{\alpha-1}\binom{l}{k}x^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)k!x^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{m-k}\binom{l+k}{k}x^{l}-\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha-1}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)k!x^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{\alpha-k-1}\binom{l+k}{k}x^{l},

where
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq\alpha
.

From (
16
), we note that

(32)

H_{n,\lambda}(x|u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}H_{k,\lambda}(u)(x)_{n-k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

Now, we observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}e_{\lambda}^{k}(t)x^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{xe_{\lambda}(t)-1}\big(e_{\lambda}^{m+1}(t)x^{m+1}-e_{\lambda}^{\alpha}(t)x^{\alpha}\big)

(33)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x-1}\bigg(x^{m+1}\frac{1-x^{-1}}{e_{\lambda}(t)-x^{-1}}e_{\lambda}^{m+1}(t)-x^{\alpha}\frac{1-x^{-1}}{e_{\lambda}(t)-x^{-1}}e_{\lambda}^{\alpha}(t)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x-1}\bigg\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(x^{m+1}H_{n,\lambda}(m+1|x^{-1})-x^{\alpha}H_{n,\lambda}(\alpha|x^{-1})\Big)\bigg\}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand,

(34)

\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}e_{\lambda}^{k}(t)x^{k}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}(k)_{n,\lambda}x^{k}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
31
), (
33
) and (
34
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq\alpha
. Then we have

\sum_{k=\alpha}^{m}(k)_{\alpha,\lambda}x^{k}=\frac{1}{x-1}\Big\{x^{m+1}H_{\alpha,\lambda}(m+1|x^{-1})-x^{\alpha}H_{\alpha,\lambda}(\alpha|x^{-1})\Big\}.

In addition, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x-1}\Big\{x^{m+1}H_{\alpha,\lambda}(m+1|x^{-1})-x^{\alpha}H_{\alpha,\lambda}(\alpha|x^{-1})\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)k!x^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{m-k}\binom{l+k}{k}x^{l}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha-1}S_{2,\lambda}(\alpha,k)k!x^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{\alpha-k-1}\binom{l+k}{k}x^{l}.

3.
Degenerate Eulerian polynomials and numbers

In this section, we consider the degenerate Eulerian polynomials, find the exponential generating function and the recurrence relation of them and express the sum
\sum_{k=1}^{m}(k)_{n,\lambda}x^{k}
in terms of the degenerate Eulerian polynomials.

We recall that the degenerate Eulerian polynomials are given by

(35)

\frac{A_{n,\lambda}(x)}{(1-x)^{n+1}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)_{n,\lambda}x^{j},\quad(n\geq 0).

Then
A_{0,\lambda}(x)=1
. Let
n\geq 1
. From (
35
), we observe that

(36)

\displaystyle A_{n,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)_{n,\lambda}x^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n+1}{k}x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{m+1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n+1}{k}(m-k+1)_{n,\lambda}\bigg)x^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{m+1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n+1}{k}(m-k+1)_{n,\lambda}\bigg)x^{m}

Now, we define the degenerate Eulerian numbers as

(37)

\displaystyle\genfrac{<}{>}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}_{\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{m+1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n+1}{k}(m-k+1)_{n,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 1),\quad\genfrac{<}{>}{0.0pt}{}{0}{0}_{\lambda}=1.

Therefore, by (
36
) and (
37
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

For any nonnegative integer
n
, we have

A_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\genfrac{<}{>}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}_{\lambda}x^{m}.

Remark 7
.

(a) Carlitz introduced the degenerate Eulerian polynomials
E_{n,\lambda}(x)
which are slightly different from
A_{n,\lambda}(x)
in (
35
) (see [5]). Indeed, they are defined as

\displaystyle\frac{E_{n,\lambda}(x)}{(1-x)^{n+1}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j)_{n,\lambda}x^{j},\quad(n\geq 0).

(b) As we see, for example, from Theorem 10,
A_{n,\lambda}(t)
has degree
n-1
, for
n\geq 1
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\genfrac{<}{>}{0.0pt}{}{n}{n}_{\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n+1}{k}(n-k+1)_{n,\lambda}=0,\quad\mathrm{for\,\,any}\,\,\lambda\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,n\geq 1.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Then we observe that

(38)

x^{n\lambda-1}\bigg(x^{1-\lambda}\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{n}\frac{1}{1-x}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)_{n,\lambda}x^{j}\Longleftrightarrow x^{n\lambda}\bigg(x^{1-\lambda}\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{n}\frac{1}{1-x}=x\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)_{n,\lambda}x^{j},

and

(39)

\sum_{k=0}^{m}x^{k}=\frac{1}{1-x}-\frac{x^{m+1}}{1-x}.

From (
38
) and (
39
). we note that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}(k)_{n,\lambda}x^{k}

\displaystyle=x^{n\lambda}\bigg(x^{1-\lambda}\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}x^{k}

(40)

\displaystyle=x^{n\lambda}\bigg(x^{1-\lambda}\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{n}\frac{1}{1-x}-x^{n\lambda}\bigg(x^{1-\lambda}\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{n}\frac{x^{m+1}}{1-x}

\displaystyle=\frac{xA_{n,\lambda}(x)}{(1-x)^{n+1}}-x^{m+2}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}(m+1)_{n-l,\lambda}\frac{A_{l,\lambda}(x)}{(1-x)^{l+1}}.

Therefore, by (
40
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 8
.

For
m,n\geq 1
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{m}(k)_{n,\lambda}x^{k}=\frac{xA_{n,\lambda}(x)}{(1-x)^{n+1}}-x^{m+2}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}(m+1)_{n-l,\lambda}\frac{A_{l,\lambda}(x)}{(1-x)^{l+1}}.

From (
35
), we note that

(41)

\displaystyle\frac{t-1}{t-e_{-\lambda}\big((t-1)x\big)}

\displaystyle=\frac{t-1}{e_{-\lambda}((t-1)x)\big(te_{\lambda}((1-t)x)-1)}

\displaystyle=(1-t)e_{\lambda}((1-t)x)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t^{j}e_{\lambda}^{j}((1-t)x)

\displaystyle=(1-t)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t^{j}e_{\lambda}^{j+1}((1-t)x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-t)^{n+1}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t^{j}(j+1)_{n,\lambda}\bigg)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-t)^{n+1}\frac{A_{n,\lambda}(t)}{(1-t)^{n+1}}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n,\lambda}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 9
.

The exponential generating function of the degenerate Eulerian polynomials is given by

\frac{t-1}{t-e_{-\lambda}((t-1)x)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n,\lambda}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

From Theorem 9, we note that

(42)

\displaystyle t-1

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}A_{l,\lambda}(t)\frac{x^{l}}{l!}\bigg)\big(t-e_{-\lambda}((t-1)x)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}tA_{n,\lambda}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}A_{k,\lambda}(t)(t-1)^{n-k}\langle 1\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}\bigg)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg\{tA_{n,\lambda}(t)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}A_{k,\lambda}(t)(t-1)^{n-k}\langle 1\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}\bigg\}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

By comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
42
), we get

(43)

tA_{n,\lambda}(t)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}A_{k,\lambda}(t)(t-1)^{n-k}\langle 1\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}=(t-1)\delta_{0,n},

where
\delta_{n,k}
is the Kronecker’s symbol.

Therefore, by (
43
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 10
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

A_{n,\lambda}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}A_{k,\lambda}(t)(t-1)^{n-k-1}\langle 1\rangle_{n-k,\lambda}.

From (
6

(44)

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle_{m+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m+1}{m+1\brack k}_{\lambda}\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}=\sum_{k=1}^{m+1}{m+1\brack k}_{\lambda}\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m+1\brack k+1}_{\lambda}\langle x\rangle_{k+1,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m+1\brack k+1}_{\lambda}\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}(x+k\lambda)

(45)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m+1\brack k+1}_{\lambda}\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}=\frac{1}{x}\langle x\rangle_{m+1}-\frac{\lambda}{x}\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m+1\brack k+1}_{\lambda}k\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\langle x+1\rangle_{m}-\frac{\lambda}{x}\sum_{k=1}^{m}{m+1\brack k+1}_{\lambda}k\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda}

Theorem 11

{m+1\brack j+1}_{\lambda}=\sum_{k=j}^{m}\binom{k}{j}\bigg\{{m\brack k}_{\lambda}\langle 1\rangle_{k-j,\lambda}-\lambda(k+1){m+1\brack k+2}_{\lambda}\langle 1\rangle_{k-j,\lambda}\bigg\}.

4.
Conclusion

Eulerian numbers and polynomials have long been studied due to their number-theoretic and combinatorial importance.
In this paper, we considered a finite sum involving the generalized falling factorial polynomials, in some special cases of which we represented it in terms of the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, the degenerate Bernoulli polynomials and the degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials. Also, we considered the degenerate Eulerian polynomials and deduced the generating function and a recurrence relation for them.

There are various ways of studying special numbers and polynomials, to mention a few, generating functions,
p
-adic analysis, umbral calculus, combinatorial methods, differential equations, special functions, probability theory and analytic number theory. It is one of our future projects to continue to explore various degenerate versions of many special polynomials and numbers.

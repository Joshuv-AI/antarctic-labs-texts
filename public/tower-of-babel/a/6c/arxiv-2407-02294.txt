Determination of the stably free cancellation property for orders
Werner Bley, Tommy Hofmann, Henri Johnston

Abstract
. Let K K be a number field, let A A be a finite-dimensional semisimple K K -algebra, and let Λ \Lambda be an 𝒪 K \mathcal{O}_{K} -order in A A . We give practical algorithms that determine whether Λ \Lambda has stably free cancellation (SFC). As an application, we determine all finite groups G G of order at most 383 383 such that the integral group ring ℤ ⁡ [ G ] \mathbb{Z}[G] has SFC.

1.
Introduction

A ring
R
is said to have
stably free cancellation
(SFC)
if every finitely generated stably free (left)
R
-module is in fact free, that is,
if
M
is a finitely generated (left)
R
-module such that
M\oplus R^{\oplus n}\cong R^{\oplus(n+m)}
for some
m,n\geq 0
, then
M\cong R^{\oplus m}
.
In this article, we develop practical algorithms
to determine whether a given order in a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra
over a number field has SFC.

Let
K
be a number field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
Let
A
be a finite-dimensional semisimple
K
-algebra and let

\Lambda
be an
\mathcal{O}
-order in
A
.
For example, if
G
is a finite group then the group ring
\mathcal{O}[G]

is an
\mathcal{O}
-order in the group algebra
K[G]
.
In addition to stably free cancellation for
\Lambda
,
one can also consider
locally free cancellation
(LFC),
whose definition we defer to §
3.1
.
If
\Lambda
has LFC then it also has SFC.
Note, however, that there exist orders with SFC but without LFC
(see
[
Sme15
]
or
[
SV19
]
).

We say that
A
satisfies the
Eichler condition
if no simple component of
A

is a totally definite quaternion algebra (see §
3.2
).
Jacobinski
[
Jac68
]
showed that if
A
satisfies the Eichler condition
then
\Lambda
has LFC and thus SFC.
Smertnig–Voight
[
SV19
]
showed that when

A
is a totally definite quaternion algebra, the questions of whether

\Lambda
has SFC or LFC can be decided algorithmically.
They then used this to classify all orders in totally definite quaternion algebras that
have SFC, and of these, all those that have LFC.
This classification builds on work of Vignéras
[
Vig76
]
, Hallouin–Maire
[
HM06
]

and Smertnig
[
Sme15
]
.

We can always decompose
A
into a direct product of
K
-algebras

A=\prod_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}
, where
A_{1}
satisfies the Eichler
condition and
A_{2},\ldots,A_{k}
are all totally definite quaternion algebras.
If
\Lambda
decomposes as a product of
\mathcal{O}
-orders

\Lambda=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Lambda_{i}

such that each
\Lambda_{i}
is contained in
A_{i}
, then the questions of whether

\Lambda
has SFC or LFC can be decided using the results discussed in the previous
paragraph, since both properties respect direct products.
However, in many cases of interest,
\Lambda
does not exhibit such a decomposition.
The desire to consider such cases motivates the following result,
which is the main result of this article.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists an algorithm that, given an arbitrary

\mathcal{O}
-order
\Lambda
in a finite-dimensional semisimple
K
-algebra,
decides whether
\Lambda
has SFC.

In fact, we present three algorithms, all of which are practical in certain situations.
The first of these, Algorithm
8.9
, is only practical when
A
is of ‘small’ dimension.
This relies on Theorem
8.7
and
works by computing a maximal order
\mathcal{M}
containing
\Lambda
, a suitable two-sided ideal
\mathfrak{f}
of
\mathcal{M}
contained in
\Lambda
, and the image of

(\Lambda/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}
in a certain ray class group,
and then using this data to determine whether a finite number of explicit
stably free ‘test lattices’ are in fact free over
\Lambda
.
The main ideas employed utilize and build upon the results of
[
BB06
]
and

[
BHJ22
]
.
The second, Algorithm
9.1
, cannot prove that
\Lambda
has SFC, but
can be used to show that it fails SFC, and in this situation is often much faster than Algorithm
8.9
. This works by sampling ‘random’ test lattices
and checking if they fail to be free over
\Lambda
.
The third, Algorithm
10.3
, uses fiber products to reduce the problem
for the original order to that for an order in an algebra of smaller dimension as well as the
computation of a certain subgroup of the unit group of a finite ring.
It uses Corollary
5.12
, which itself
crucially relies on results of Reiner–Ullom
[
RU74
]
(see Proposition
5.2
) and Swan
[
Swa80
]
(see Theorem
5.10
).
These algorithms require several other algorithms of independent interest,
including those that can be used to compute certain primary decompositions and
unit groups of finite rings (see §
11
), and
new freeness tests for certain modules over orders (see §
12
).

Orders of particular interest are integral group rings
\mathbb{Z}[G]
for
G
a finite group.
For instance, the property of whether
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has SFC or LFC has applications
in topology (see
[
Joh03
]
,
[
Nic21b
]
,
[
Nic24a
]
, for example)
and in the determination of whether a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
has a normal
integral basis (see
[
Cou94
]
,
[
Cou98
]
).

Let
G
be a finite group and let

\mathbb{H}
denote the quaternion division algebra over
\mathbb{R}
.
If no simple component of
\mathbb{R}[G]
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{H}
,
then
\mathbb{Q}[G]
satisfies the Eichler condition and so
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has LFC (and thus SFC)
by the aforementioned result of Jacobinski.
This condition on
G
is equivalent to the requirement that
G
has no quotient that
is isomorphic to a
binary polyhedral group
, that is, a generalized quaternion
group
Q_{4n}
for
n\geq 2
,
or one of
\tilde{T}
,
\tilde{O}
or
\tilde{I}
, the binary
tetrahedral, octahedral and icosahedral groups.
In §
4
,
we review important work of Swan
[
Swa83
]
, Chen
[
Che86
]
, and Nicholson
[
Nic21a
]
on SFC and LFC for
\mathbb{Z}[G]
in the case that
G
does have
a quotient isomorphic to a binary polyhedral group.

Before stating our own results on SFC for integral group rings, we first establish some further notation.
Let
C_{n}
denote the cyclic group of order
n
.
Let
G_{(n,m)}
denote the
m
th group of order
n
in the
GAP
[
GAP24
]
small groups library
[
BEO02
]
.

A special case of a result due to Swan and Fröhlich says that
if
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has SFC then
\mathbb{Z}[G/N]
has SFC for every quotient
G/N
,
or equivalently, if
\mathbb{Z}[G/N]
fails SFC for some choice of
N
, then
\mathbb{Z}[G]
also fails SFC
(see Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
4.1
).
The following result is Theorem
9.4
,
in which the claim that each
\mathbb{Z}[G]
fails SFC was proven using Algorithm
9.1
;
the claim that
\mathbb{Z}[G/N]
has SFC for each proper quotient
G/N

can be interpreted as saying that each
G
is minimal with respect to quotients.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be one of the following finite groups:

(i)

Q_{4n}\times C_{2}
for
2\leq n\leq 5
,

(ii)

\tilde{T}\times C_{2}^{2}
,
\tilde{O}\times C_{2}
,
\tilde{I}\times C_{2}^{2}
, or

(iii)

G_{(32,14)}
,
G_{(36,7)}
,
G_{(64,14)}
,
G_{(100,7)}
.

Then
\mathbb{Z}[G]
fails SFC but
\mathbb{Z}[G/N]
has SFC for every proper quotient
G/N
.

Note that Swan
[
Swa83
]
already proved that
\mathbb{Z}[Q_{8}\times C_{2}]
fails SFC and Chen
[
Che86
]
already proved that
\mathbb{Z}[G]
fails SFC for

G=Q_{12}\times C_{2}
,
Q_{16}\times C_{2}
,
Q_{20}\times C_{2}
,
G_{(36,7)}
,
G_{(100,7)}
. Since Swan
[
Swa83
]
proved that
\mathbb{Z}[Q_{4n}]
fails SFC
for
n\geq 6
, we obtain the following, which is the
most general result to date on integral group rings that fail SFC.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group with a quotient isomorphic to a group listed in
Theorem
1.2

or to
Q_{4n}
for some
n\geq 6
.
Then
\mathbb{Z}[G]
fails SFC.

Using Algorithm
10.3
with judicious choices of fiber products,
we prove the following result on new cases of integral group rings with SFC (this
is Theorem
13.1
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be one of the following finite groups:

\tilde{T}\times C_{2},\,\tilde{T}\times Q_{12},\,\tilde{T}\times Q_{20},\,Q_{8}\rtimes\tilde{T},\,Q_{8}\rtimes Q_{12},\,\tilde{I}\times C_{2},\,G_{(192,183)},\,G_{(384,580)},\,G_{(480,962)}.

Then
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has SFC.

By combining Corollary
1.3
and
Theorem
1.4
, and using the
GAP
[
GAP24
]
small groups library
[
BEO02
]
, we obtain the following result
(see Corollary
13.5
).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group with
|G|\leq 383
.
Then
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has SFC if and only if
G
has no quotient of the form

(i)

Q_{4n}\times C_{2}
for
2\leq n\leq 5
,

(ii)

\tilde{T}\times C_{2}^{2}
,
\tilde{O}\times C_{2}
,
\tilde{I}\times C_{2}^{2}
,

(iii)

G_{(32,14)}
,
G_{(36,7)}
,
G_{(64,14)}
,
G_{(100,7)}
, or

(iv)

Q_{4n}
for
6\leq n\leq 95
.

This represents a significant advance in the state-of-the-art, since before the results of this article,
such a classification was only known for groups
G
with
|G|\leq 31

(for example, it was not known whether
\mathbb{Z}[G_{(32,14)}]
had SFC).
In fact, Theorem
1.5
is a special case of
Theorem
13.4
, which gives a classification for
|G|\leq 1023
, but
with
49
explicitly listed gaps. In other words, of the
11759892
groups
G
with

|G|\leq 1023
, there are now only
49
for which it is not known whether
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has SFC.

Nicholson
[
Nic24b
, Theorem 1.7]

has now shown that
\mathbb{Z}[\tilde{T}\times C_{2}]
in fact has LFC,
using our result that it has SFC
(i.e. part of Theorem
1.4
) as an input.
By combining this with further new results on integral group rings with LFC, and
using Theorem
1.2
as a crucial input, he has given
classifications of all integral group rings
\mathbb{Z}[G]
with LFC and SFC, provided that
either (i)
G
has no quotient isomorphic to
\tilde{T}
,
\tilde{O}
or
\tilde{I}
, or
(ii)
G
does have a quotient isomorphic to
C_{2}\times C_{2}

(see
[
Nic24b
, Theorems A and B]
).
We remark that in the case of integral group rings,
the LFC property discussed here is equivalent to the projective cancellation (PC) property
discussed in loc. cit.

to denote direct products of orders and algebras.
Denote the center of a ring
R
by
Z(R)
.
For a division algebra
D
and a positive integer
k
, let
\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(D)

denote the ring of all
k\times k
matrices with entries in
D
.
We call
\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(D)
a
full matrix algebra
over

.

2.
Preliminaries on lattices and orders

For further background on lattices and orders, we refer the reader to
[
CR81
,
Rei03
]
.

2.1.
Lattices and orders

Let
R
be a Dedekind domain with field of fractions
K
.
An
R
-lattice
is a finitely generated torsion-free
R
-module, or equivalently,
a finitely generated projective
R
-module.
For any finite-dimensional
K
-vector space
V
, an
R
-lattice in
V
is a finitely generated
R
-submodule
M
of
V
.
We say that
M
is a
full

R
-lattice in
V
if
KM=V
.
We may identify
K\otimes_{R}M
with
KM
via the map
k\otimes m\mapsto km
.

Now let
A
be a finite-dimensional semisimple
K
-algebra and let

\Lambda
be an
R
-order
in
A
, that is, a subring of
A
that is an
R
-lattice such that
K\Lambda=A
. A
\Lambda
-lattice
is a left
\Lambda
-module that is also an
R
-lattice.

2.2.
Localizations and completions

Henceforth assume that
K
is a number field.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
range over all maximal ideals of
R
.
Let
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
denote the localization of
R
at
\mathfrak{p}
,
that is, the ring of
\mathfrak{p}
-integral elements in
K
.
For an
R
-lattice
M
, define the localization
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
to be the
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-lattice

R_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{R}M
.
Localising a
\Lambda
-lattice
X
at
\mathfrak{p}
yields a

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}
-lattice
X_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Two
\Lambda
-lattices
X
and
Y
are said to be
locally isomorphic
, or

in the same genus
,
if there is an isomorphism
of
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}
-lattices
X_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong Y_{\mathfrak{p}}
for every
\mathfrak{p}
,
in which case we write
X\vee Y
.

Let
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}

denote the
\mathfrak{p}
-adic completion of
R
.
For an
R
-lattice
M
, define the completion
\widehat{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}
to be the

\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-lattice
\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{R}M
.
Completing a
\Lambda
-lattice
X
at
\mathfrak{p}
yields a

\widehat{\Lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-lattice
\widehat{X}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Given
\Lambda
-lattices
X
and
Y
,
we have that
X_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong Y_{\mathfrak{p}}
as
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}
-lattices
if and only if
\widehat{X}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\widehat{Y}_{\mathfrak{p}}
as

\widehat{\Lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-lattices
(see
[
CR81
, (30.17)]
or
[
Rei03
, (18.2)]
).
Therefore
X\vee Y
if and only if
there is an isomorphism
of
\widehat{\Lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-lattices
\widehat{X}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\widehat{Y}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for every
\mathfrak{p}
.

2.3.
Locally free lattices and the principal genus

A
\Lambda
-lattice
X
is said to be
locally free of rank
n
if

X\vee\Lambda^{\oplus n}
.
Every locally free
\Lambda
-lattice is projective over
\Lambda
by

[
CR81
, (8.19)]
.

Define the
principal genus

g(\Lambda)
to be the class
of locally free
\Lambda
-lattices of rank
1
.
By
[
CR81
, (31.7)]
, given
Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{3}\in g(\Lambda)
, there
exists
Y_{4}\in g(\Lambda)
such that

Y_{1}\oplus Y_{2}\cong Y_{3}\oplus Y_{4}.

(2.1)

Moreover, by
[
CR81
, (31.14)]
, given
Y\vee\Lambda^{\oplus n}
,
there exists
X\in g(\Lambda)
such that

Y\cong\Lambda^{\oplus(n-1)}\oplus X.

(2.2)

For
X\in g(\Lambda)
we write
[X]_{\mathrm{iso}}
for the isomorphism class of
X

and define

\mathrm{LF}_{1}(\Lambda)=\{[X]_{\mathrm{iso}}:X\in g(\Lambda)\}.

Note that
\mathrm{LF}_{1}(\Lambda)
is finite by virtue of the
Jordan–Zassenhaus theorem
[
Rei03
, (26.4)]
.

2.4.
Stable isomorphism

Let
P(\Lambda)
denote the class of finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-modules.
For
X,Y\in P(\Lambda)
, we say that
X
and
Y
are
stably isomorphic
if

X\oplus\Lambda^{\oplus m}\cong Y\oplus\Lambda^{\oplus m}\textrm{ for some }m\geq 0.

A module
X\in P(\Lambda)
is said to be
stably free of rank
n
if
it is stably isomorphic to
\Lambda^{\oplus n}
.
For
X\in P(\Lambda)
, let
[X]_{\mathrm{st}}
denote the stable isomorphism class of
X
.
Note that by
[
CR81
, (6.15)]
and the discussion in §
2.2
, stably isomorphic
\Lambda
-lattices are locally isomorphic.
In particular, stably free lattices are locally free.

2.5.
Locally free class groups

We give an overview of the
locally free class group of
\Lambda
,
for which there are several equivalent definitions (see
[
CR87
, §49A]
).
Here we follow
[
CR87
, (49.10)]
, to which we refer the reader for further details.
Let

\mathrm{Cl}(\Lambda)=\{[X]_{\mathrm{st}}:X\in g(\Lambda)\}.

By (
2.1
), given
X_{1},X_{2}\in g(\Lambda)

there exists
X_{3}\in g(\Lambda)
such that
X_{1}\oplus X_{2}\cong\Lambda\oplus X_{3}
.
It is straightforward to check that the stable isomorphism class

[X_{3}]_{\mathrm{st}}
is uniquely determined by the classes
[X_{1}]_{\mathrm{st}}
and
[X_{2}]_{\mathrm{st}}
.
We define a binary operation on
\mathrm{Cl}(\Lambda)
by

[X_{1}]_{\mathrm{st}}+[X_{2}]_{\mathrm{st}}=[X_{3}]_{\mathrm{st}}\textrm{ whenever }X_{1}\oplus X_{2}\cong\Lambda\oplus X_{3},

where
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}\in g(\Lambda)
.
This is a well-defined commutative and associative operation, with identity element

[\Lambda]_{\mathrm{st}}
.
Moreover, inverses exist since by (
2.1
),
given
X\in g(\Lambda)
there exists
X^{\prime}\in g(\Lambda)
such that
X\oplus X^{\prime}\cong\Lambda^{\oplus 2}
.
Therefore
\mathrm{Cl}(\Lambda)
is an abelian group.
Moreover,
\mathrm{Cl}(\Lambda)
is finite since

\mathrm{LF}_{1}(\Lambda)
is finite and the canonical map
\mathrm{LF}_{1}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\mathrm{Cl}(\Lambda)

defined by
[X]_{\mathrm{iso}}\mapsto[X]_{\mathrm{st}}
is surjective.

3.
Review of cancellation properties for orders

Let
R
be a Dedekind domain whose quotient field
K
is a number field.
Henceforth every
R
-order is assumed to be in a finite-dimensional semisimple
K
-algebra.

3.1.
Stably free and locally free cancellation

Let
\Lambda
be an
R
-order.
Then
\Lambda
is said to have
stably free cancellation
(SFC)
if every finitely generated stably free
\Lambda
-module is in fact free, that is,
if for every finitely generated
\Lambda
-module
X
we have

X\oplus\Lambda^{\oplus m}\cong\Lambda^{\oplus(m+n)}\textrm{ for some }m,n\geq 0\implies X\cong\Lambda^{\oplus n}.

(3.1)

Similarly,
\Lambda
is said to have
locally free cancellation
(LFC) if for every pair of
locally free
\Lambda
-lattices
X
and
Y
we have

X\oplus\Lambda^{\oplus m}\cong Y\oplus\Lambda^{\oplus m}\textrm{ for some }m\geq 0\implies X\cong Y.

(3.2)

Since locally free
\Lambda
-lattices are projective (see §
2.3
),
one can easily deduce that
\Lambda
has LFC if and only if it has the apparently stronger property
that justifies the name: for every triple of locally free
\Lambda
-lattices
X,Y,Z
we have

X\oplus Z\cong Y\oplus Z\implies X\cong Y.

If
X
is a finitely generated
\Lambda
-module such that

X\oplus\Lambda^{\oplus m}\cong\Lambda^{\oplus(m+n)}\textrm{ for some }m,n\geq 0

then it is straightforward to see that
X
is a
\Lambda
-lattice;
since
X
is stably free it is also locally free (see §
2.4
).
Therefore if
\Lambda
has LFC then it also has SFC.
Note, however, that there exist orders with SFC but without LFC
(see
[
Sme15
]
or
[
SV19
]
).

By (
2.2
),
\Lambda
has SFC if and only if (
3.1
) holds
for all
X\in g(\Lambda)
.
Similarly,
\Lambda
has LFC if and only if (
3.2
) holds for all
X,Y\in g(\Lambda)
.
Hence the canonical surjective map
\mathrm{LF}_{1}(\Lambda)\rightarrow\mathrm{Cl}(\Lambda)

defined by
[X]_{\mathrm{iso}}\mapsto[X]_{\mathrm{st}}
is bijective precisely when
\Lambda
has LFC.
Moreover,
\Lambda
has SFC precisely when the fiber above
0
has exactly one element,
that is, when every stably free
\Lambda
-lattice of rank
1

is in fact free (of rank
1
).

Theorem 3.1

(Fröhlich, Swan)
.

Let
\Lambda\rightarrow\Gamma
be a map of
R
-orders
inducing a surjection
K\Lambda\rightarrow K\Gamma
of
K
-algebras.
If
\Lambda
has SFC (resp. LFC) then
\Gamma
has SFC (resp. LFC).

Proof.

The claim for LFC was proven by Fröhlich
[
Frö75
, VIII]
, whose approach was subsequently refined by Swan. Here we adapt the proof of
[
Nic21a
, Theorem 1.1]
.

By
[
Swa83
, Theorem A10]
, the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{LF}_{1}(\Lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{LF}_{1}(\Gamma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Cl}(\Lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Cl}(\Gamma)}

is a weak pull back in the sense that a compatible pair of elements in
\mathrm{Cl}(\Lambda)
and

\mathrm{LF}_{1}(\Gamma)
has a not-necessarily-unique lift to
\mathrm{LF}_{1}(\Lambda)
.
It follows that the fiber in
\mathrm{LF}_{1}(\Lambda)
over an element
x\in\mathrm{Cl}(\Lambda)
maps onto
the fiber in
\mathrm{LF}_{1}(\Gamma)
over the image of
x
in
\mathrm{Cl}(\Gamma)
.
Thus if
\Lambda
has LFC then
\Gamma
has LFC.
Taking
x=0
shows that if
\Lambda
has SFC then
\Gamma
has SFC.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma
be
R
-orders such that
K\Lambda=K\Gamma
.
If
\Lambda
has SFC (resp. LFC) then
\Gamma
has SFC (resp. LFC).

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
be
R
-orders
and let
\Lambda=\Lambda_{1}\oplus\Lambda_{2}
.
Then
\Lambda
has SFC (resp. LFC) if and only if both

and
\Lambda_{2}
have

Proof.

One direction follows from Theorem

;
the other follows from the definitions.
∎

3.2.
Totally definite quaternion algebras and the Eichler condition

Let
\mathbb{H}
denote the quaternion division algebra over
\mathbb{R}
.
A
totally definite quaternion algebra
is a simple finite-dimensional
K
-algebra
B

whose completion at every archimedean prime of the center of
B
is isomorphic to

\mathbb{H}
(in particular, this forces
K
to be totally real).

We say that a finite-dimensional semisimple
K
-algebra

A
satisfies the
Eichler condition
if no simple component of
A

is a totally definite quaternion algebra.
A proof of the following result can be found in
[
CR87
, (51.24)]
or
[
Jac68
, §4]
.

Theorem 3.4

(Jacobinski)
.

If
A
is a finite-dimensional semisimple
K
-algebra satisfying the Eichler condition then
every
R
-order in
A
has LFC.

For the rest of this section, we specialize to the case of orders over the ring
of integers
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{K}
of a number field
K
.
In
[
SV19
]
, it was shown that the questions of whether a given order in a
totally definite quaternion algebra has SFC or LFC can be tackled algorithmically.

Theorem 3.5

(Smertnig–Voight)
.

There exists an algorithm that given an
\mathcal{O}
-order
\Lambda
in a totally definite quaternion algebra over
K
, determines whether
\Lambda
has SFC (resp. LFC).

This result was used to classify all orders inside totally definite quaternion algebras that have SFC,
and of these, all that have LFC (see
[
SV19
, Theorem 1.3]
).
This classification restricted to SFC and the case of maximal or hereditary orders had
previously been given by Vignéras
[
Vig76
]
, Hallouin–Maire
[
HM06
]

and Smertnig
[
Sme15
]
.

Corollary 3.6
.

There exists an algorithm that given a maximal
\mathcal{O}
-order

\mathcal{M}

in a finite-dimensional
semisimple
K
-algebra, determines whether
\mathcal{M}
has SFC (resp. LFC).

Proof.

We can write
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{M}_{r}
,
where each
\mathcal{M}_{i}
is a maximal
\mathcal{O}
-order contained in a simple
K
-algebra
A_{i}
,
and each
A_{i}
either satisfies the Eichler condition or is a totally definite quaternion algebra.
Therefore the desired result now follows from Corollary
3.3
,
Theorem
3.4
and Theorem
3.5
.
∎

4.
Review of cancellation properties for integral group rings

We recall results concerning SFC and LFC for integral group rings
\mathbb{Z}[G]
, where

G
is a finite group.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
H
be a quotient of
G
.
If
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has SFC (resp. LFC) then
\mathbb{Z}[H]
has SFC (resp. LFC).

Proof.

The canonical map
\mathbb{Z}[G]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[H]
of
\mathbb{Z}
-orders
induces a surjection
\mathbb{Q}[G]\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}[H]
of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebras,
and so the desired result is a consequence of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

The finite non-cyclic subgroups of
\mathbb{H}^{\times}
are called the
binary polyhedral groups
.
These are the generalized quaternion groups

for

or one of
\tilde{T}
,
\tilde{O}
or
\tilde{I}

[

, §51A]
and the references therein).
It is well known that a finite group

has a binary polyhedral quotient precisely when
the group algebra

does not satisfy the Eichler condition
(see
[
Nic21a
, Proposition 1.5]
or
[

, (51.3)]

We now recall two important theorems of Swan
[
Swa83
, Theorems I and II]
.

Theorem 4.2

(Swan)
.

Let

be a binary polyhedral group.
Then
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has SFC if and only if
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has LFC if and only if

is one of the following

7

Q_{8},\,Q_{12},\,Q_{16},\,Q_{20},\,\tilde{T},\,\tilde{O},\,\tilde{I}.

Corollary 4.3

(Swan)
.

Let

be a finite group with a quotient isomorphic to

.
Then
\mathbb{Z}[G]
fails SFC.

Proof.

This follows from one direction of Theorem
4.2
combined with

4.1
.
∎

Theorem 4.4

(Swan)
.

Let

be a binary polyhedral group and let
\mathcal{M}
be a maximal
\mathbb{Z}
-order
in

Then

has LFC if and only if

(4.1)

In order, the groups listed in (

\displaystyle G_{(8,4)},\,G_{(12,1)},\,G_{(16,9)},\,G_{(20,1)},\,G_{(24,4)},\,G_{(28,1)},\,G_{(36,1)},\,G_{(60,3)},

.

Let
G
be a finite group and let

be a maximal
\mathbb{Z}
-order in

.

\mathcal{M}
has LFC if and only if

has no binary polyhedral quotient other than those

Proof.

Suppose that
G
has a binary polyhedral quotient

different to those listed in (

).
Then the image of
\mathcal{M}
under the canonical projection map

is
a maximal
\mathbb{Z}
-order, and thus does not have LFC by Theorem
4.4
.
Hence
\mathcal{M}
does not have LFC by Theorem
3.1
.

Suppose that
G
has no binary polyhedral quotient other than those listed in (
4.1
).
We can write
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{M}_{r}
,
where each
\mathcal{M}_{i}
is a maximal
R
-order contained in a simple
K
-algebra
A_{i}
,
and each
A_{i}
either satisfies the Eichler condition or is a totally definite quaternion algebra.
By Corollary
3.3

has LFC.
Fix

with

.
If

satisfies the Eichler condition then

has LFC by Theorem

.
So suppose that

is a totally definite quaternion algebra.
Let
\psi_{i}|_{G}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{\times}
denote the group homomorphism obtained by
restricting the composition
\psi_{i}:\mathbb{Q}[G]\twoheadrightarrow A_{i}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}

Let

and

Then the elements of

\mathbb{H}
over

Then

is a direct summand of

\mathcal{M}_{i}

is a direct summand of a maximal

-order

Then

Define the

.
Recall that for a ring

(Nicholson)

has SFC.

Theorem 4.12

(Nicholson)
.

Let
G
be a finite group with binary polyhedral quotient

such that

.
Then
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has SFC if and only if
\mathbb{Z}[H]
has SFC.

Theorem 4.13

(Nicholson)
.

If
G
is a finite group with

then
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has SFC.

For a finite group

and a field
\mathbb{F}
, let

-representations of

be an

5.1.

Suppose that there is a fiber product

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{1}}

in which
\Lambda_{1}
and

are
R

A_{1}

\Lambda\cong\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Lambda_{1}\oplus\Lambda_{2}\mid g_{1}(x_{1})=g_{2}(x_{2})\}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad A\cong A_{1}\oplus A_{2},

Example 5.1
.

Let

and

be two-sided ideals of
\Lambda

5.2.
Review of results of Reiner–Ullom

We now recall

, which is crucial to all of the results in this section.

Proposition 5.2

(Reiner–Ullom)
.

Given a fiber product as in (
5.1
), assume that at least one of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}

is surjective. For
u\in\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}
, define

M(u):=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Lambda_{1}\oplus\Lambda_{2}\mid g_{1}(x_{1})u=g_{2}(x_{2})\}.

Then the following statements hold:

(i)

Each
M(u)
is a locally free
\Lambda
-lattice of rank

such that

\Lambda_{i}\otimes_{\Lambda}M(u)\cong\Lambda_{i}\cdot M(u)=\Lambda_{i}\textrm{ for }i=1,2.

(ii)

For

, we have

M(u)\oplus M(u^{\prime})\cong\Lambda\oplus M(uu^{\prime})\cong\Lambda\oplus M(u^{\prime}u).

(iii)

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a maximal
R
-order in

containing
\Lambda
.
Then

\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\Lambda}M(u)\cong\mathcal{M}\cdot M(u)=\mathcal{M}.

(iv)

Let
X
be any locally free
\Lambda
-lattice such that
\Lambda_{i}\otimes_{\Lambda}X\cong\Lambda_{i}
for

. Then

for some

.

(v)

For

, we have

M(u)\cong M(u^{\prime})
if and only if

u^{\prime}\in g_{1}(\Lambda_{1}^{\times})\cdot u\cdot g_{2}(\Lambda_{2}^{\times})

Corollary 5.3
.

In the setting of Proposition

, the following statements hold:

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

.

(iv)

Part (iii) follows from the definition of

Part (iv) follows from parts (ii) and (iii).

5.3.
A reduction criterion for SFC

We henceforth assume the setting and notation of Proposition
5.2
and Corollary
5.3
.
The following underpins many of our results.

Proposition 5.4
.

\Lambda
has SFC if and only if both

\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
have SFC and
W=\ker(\partial)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Lambda
has SFC.
The maps
K\otimes_{R}\pi_{i}:A\rightarrow A_{i}
are surjective for
i=1,2

by (

).
Thus both
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
have SFC by Theorem
3.1
.
Let
u\in\ker(\partial)
.
Then
\partial(u)=[M(u)]_{\mathrm{st}}=0
in
\mathrm{Cl}(\Lambda)
and so
M(u)\cong\Lambda
since
\Lambda
has SFC.
Thus
u\in W
by Corollary
5.3
(ii).
Hence
\ker(\partial)\subseteq W
.
But the reverse containment also holds by Corollary
5.3
(iv) and
so
W=\ker(\partial)
.

Suppose conversely that both
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
have SFC and that

W=\ker(\partial)
.
Recall from §
3.1
that in order to show that
\Lambda
has SFC,
it suffices to show that every stably free
\Lambda
-lattice of rank
1

is in fact free of rank
1
.
So suppose that
X
is a stably free
\Lambda
-lattice of rank
1
, that is,
X\in g(\Lambda)

and
[X]_{\mathrm{st}}=0
in
\mathrm{Cl}(\Lambda)
. Then for
i=1,2
we have

[\Lambda_{i}\otimes_{\Lambda}X]_{\mathrm{st}}=0
in
\mathrm{Cl}(\Lambda_{i})

and so
\Lambda_{i}\otimes_{\Lambda}X\cong\Lambda_{i}
since
\Lambda_{i}
has SFC.
Hence
X\cong M(u)
for some
u\in\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}

by Proposition
5.2
(iv).
Since
X
is stably free, Corollary
5.3
(iii)
implies that
u\in\ker(\partial)
.
Thus since
W=\ker(\partial)
, Corollary
5.3
(ii) implies that
M(u)\cong\Lambda
.
Therefore
X\cong M(u)\cong\Lambda
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Suppose that both
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
have SFC and that
W
is a group.
Let
N
be a normal subgroup of
\ker(\partial)
contained in
W
and let
S
be a generating subset
of
\ker(\partial)/N
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\Lambda
has SFC.

(ii)

For every
\overline{u}\in\ker(\partial)/N
and every lift

we have

.

(iii)

For every
\overline{u}\in S
there exists a lift

such that

M(u)\cong\Lambda
.

Proof.

We shall show that (i)
\implies
(ii)
\implies
(iii)
\implies
(i).
Suppose (i) holds.
Then
W=\ker(\partial)
by Proposition
5.4
.
Thus
M(u)\cong\Lambda
for every

by Corollary
5.3
(ii)
and so, in particular, assertion (ii) holds.
Clearly (ii) implies (iii).
Suppose (iii) holds.
By Proposition
5.4
, to show that (i) holds, it suffices to show that
\ker(\partial)=W
.
Let
v\in\ker(\partial)
and let
\overline{v}
denote its image in
\ker(\partial)/N
.
Then there exist (not necessarily distinct) elements
\overline{s}_{1},\ldots,\overline{s}_{r}\in S

such that
\overline{v}=\overline{s}_{1}\cdots\overline{s}_{r}
.
By hypothesis, there exist lifts

such that

, and so

(ii).
Moreover, there exists

such that

.
Since

is a group and

, we conclude that

.
Therefore
\ker(\partial)\subseteq W
.
Since the reverse inclusion holds by Corollary

.

As shown in the proof of loc. cit.

Remark 5.9

In this section, we consider applications of Corollary

6.1.
A fiber product for integral group rings

We first record the following well-known instance of a fiber product.
Let
G
be a finite group, let
N
be a normal subgroup and let
H=G/N
.
Let
n=|N|
and let
\mathrm{Tr}_{N}=\sum_{\tau\in N}\tau\in\mathbb{Z}[G]

be the trace element associated to
N
.
Let
\pi_{1}:\mathbb{Z}[G]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[H]
be the canonical projection and let
I=\ker(\pi_{1})
.
Let
J=\mathrm{Tr}_{N}\mathbb{Z}[G]
, let
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}[G]/J
and let
\pi_{2}:\mathbb{Z}[G]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[G]/J

be the canonical projection. Then
I\cap J=0
and
I+J=n\mathbb{Z}[G]
.
Thus, by Example
5.1
we have a fiber product

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}[G]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}[H]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})[H],}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{2}}

where each of
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},g_{1},g_{2}
is the relevant canonical surjection.
Let

W_{G,N}:=\{w_{1}w_{2}\mid w_{1}\in g_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[H]^{\times}),w_{2}\in g_{2}(\Gamma^{\times})\}.

Then Corollary
5.7
immediately implies the following result.

Proposition 6.1
.

Assume the above setting and notation. Then

|W_{G,N}|\leq\frac{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[H])||\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])||(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})[H]^{\times}|}{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[G])|}.

(6.1)

Moreover,
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has SFC if and only if both
\mathbb{Z}[H]
and
\Gamma
have SFC and
(
6.1
) is an equality.

Remark 6.2
.

The locally free class groups in Proposition
6.1

(and in Propositions
6.4
and
6.10
below)
can be replaced by the corresponding kernel groups (see Remark
5.8
).

Remark 6.3
.

Let
p
be a prime number and suppose that
n=p
in Proposition
6.1
.
Then
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})[H]=\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]
.
Thus left multiplication by an element of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]^{\times}
induces
an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]
.
Together with the choice of
H
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-basis of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]
,
this determines an explicit embedding of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]^{\times}
into
\mathrm{GL}_{h}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
,
where

. This allows computations in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]^{\times}
to be performed
efficiently on a computer algebra system.
By contrast, in the more general setting of Corollary
5.7
, it
can be difficult to perform computations in
\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}

(see §
11.3
).

6.2.
Integral group rings of the form
\mathbb{Z}[H\times C_{2}]

We will need the following result.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
H
be a finite group such that
\mathbb{Z}[H]
has SFC.
Let
V_{H}
be the image of the canonical projection
\mathbb{Z}[H]^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}[H]^{\times}

Then

|V_{H}|\leq\frac{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[H])|^{2}|\mathbb{F}_{2}[H]^{\times}|}{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[H\times C_{2}])|},

and this inequality is an equality if and only if
\mathbb{Z}[H\times C_{2}]
has SFC.

Proof.

Let
G=H\times C_{2}
and let
N=\{1\}\times C_{2}\leq G
.
Then in the notation of §

, we have
n=2
,

\Gamma\simeq\mathbb{Z}[H]
and

.
Thus the desired result now follows immediately from Proposition

.
∎

Example 6.5
.

Let
Q_{8}
denote the quaternion group of order
8
.
Swan
[
Swa83
, Theorem 15.1]
showed that
\mathbb{Z}[Q_{8}\times C_{2}]
does not have SFC.
We now give an alternative proof of this result using Proposition
6.4

and the following facts:

(i)

\mathbb{Z}[Q_{8}]
has SFC (
[
Swa83
, Theorem I]
),

(ii)

\mathbb{Z}[Q_{8}]^{\times}=\pm Q_{8}
(
[
Swa83
, Lemma 15.3]
),

(iii)

|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[Q_{8}])|=2
(
[
Swa83
, Theorem III]

(iv)

|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[Q_{8}\times C_{2}])|=2^{4}
(
[
Swa83
, Theorem 16.5]

(v)

|\mathbb{F}_{2}[Q_{8}]^{\times}|=2^{7}
(since

is a local ring with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{2}
).

Fact (ii) implies that

V_{Q_{8}}=Q_{8}\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}[Q_{8}]^{\times}
.
Hence

2^{3}=|Q_{8}|=|V_{Q_{8}}|<|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[Q_{8}])|^{2}|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[Q_{8}\times C_{2}])|^{-1}|\mathbb{F}_{2}[Q_{8}]^{\times}|=2^{2}\cdot 2^{-4}\cdot 2^{7}=2^{5},

and so
\mathbb{Z}[Q_{8}\times C_{2}]
does not have SFC.
Note that the quantities in (iii) and (iv) can instead be calculated on a computer
by using the algorithm of
[
BB06
, §3]
.

Let
\tilde{T}\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})\cong G_{(24,3)}
denote the binary tetrahedral group.
Then
\tilde{T}\times C_{2}\cong G_{(48,32)}
.

Theorem 6.6
.

The group ring
\mathbb{Z}[\tilde{T}\times C_{2}]
has SFC.

Proof.

Further details on the computations used in this proof can be found in
Appendix
A
.
The algorithm of
[
BB06
, §3]
shows that

|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[\tilde{T}])|=2
and
|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[\tilde{T}\times C_{2}])|=2^{6}
.
By Remark

, we have

|\mathbb{F}_{2}[\tilde{T}]^{\times}|=|J(\mathbb{F}_{2}[\tilde{T}])||(\mathbb{F}_{2}[\tilde{T}]/J(\mathbb{F}_{2}[\tilde{T}]))^{\times}|
,
where
J(\mathbb{F}_{2}[\tilde{T}])
denotes the Jacobson radical of
\mathbb{F}_{2}[\tilde{T}]
.
By
[
MS22
, Theorem 2.5]
we have

(this can also be verified using the algorithm of
[

]

Hence

Moreover,

Therefore

.

The integral group ring
\mathbb{Z}[\tilde{T}]
has SFC by
[
Swa83
, Theorem I]
.
Let

be the image of the canonical projection

.
Then by Proposition

and the above calculations we have

To prove equality, we consider the following units of integral group rings as introduced
by Ritter–Segal
[
RS91
]
.
For

, let

and define

and

.
By Remark

, we obtain an explicit embedding

Since a calculation on a computer algebra system such as

BCP97
]
or

FHHJ17

shows that the image of

of order

and

.
Therefore we conclude that

∎

6.3.
A useful reduction criterion for certain integral group rings

The following reduction criterion only uses the orders of certain locally free class groups, and thus can be applied relatively easily.
It will be used in the proof of Theorem
13.1

(i.e. Theorem
1.4
).

Proposition 6.10
.

Let
G
be a finite group with a normal subgroup
N
of order
2
and let
H=G/N
.
Let
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}[G]/\mathrm{Tr}_{N}\mathbb{Z}[G]
.
Suppose that
\mathbb{Z}[H\times C_{2}]
has SFC and that

\frac{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[H\times C_{2}])|}{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[H])|}=\frac{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[G])|}{|\mathrm{Cl}(\Gamma)|}.

(6.2)

Then
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has SFC if and only if
\Gamma
has SFC.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{Z}[H\times C_{2}]
has SFC by hypothesis, so does
\mathbb{Z}[H]
by Lemma
4.1
.
By Proposition
6.4
and (
6.2
) we have

|V_{H}|=\frac{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[H])|^{2}|\mathbb{F}_{2}[H]^{\times}|}{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[H\times C_{2}])|}=\frac{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[H])||\mathbb{F}_{2}[H]^{\times}||\mathrm{Cl}(\Gamma)|}{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[G])|}.

(6.3)

By Proposition
6.1
and (
6.3
) we have

|W_{G,N}|\leq\frac{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[H])||\mathrm{Cl}(\Gamma)||\mathbb{F}_{2}[H]^{\times}|}{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[G])|}=|V_{H}|,

and that
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has SFC if and only if
\Gamma
has SFC and this inequality is in fact an equality.
But
V_{H}\subseteq W_{G,N}
by the definitions.
Therefore
|W_{G,N}|=|V_{H}|
and so
\mathbb{Z}[G]
has SFC if and only if
\Gamma
has SFC.
∎

7.1.
Setup

Let
K
be a number field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
and let

be a finite-dimensional semisimple
K
-algebra.
Let
\Lambda
be an
\mathcal{O}
-order in

.
By
[
Rei03
, (10.4)]
there exists a (not necessarily unique) maximal
\mathcal{O}
-order

in

containing
\Lambda
.
Let
\mathfrak{f}
be any full two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{M}
that is contained in

.

7.2.
A criterion for freeness

We recall some results from
[

, §4]
.
Define

\pi:(\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}\longrightarrow{(\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}}/{(\Lambda/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}}

(7.1)

to be the map induced by the canonical projection, where the codomain is the
collection of left cosets of

(\Lambda/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}
in
(\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}
.
Note that
(\Lambda/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}
is a subgroup of
(\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}
, but is not necessarily a normal subgroup, and so
\pi
is only a map of sets in general.

For
\eta\in\mathcal{M}
we write
\overline{\eta}
for its image in
\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f}
.

Proposition 7.1

(
[
BHJ22
, Proposition 4.3]
)
.

Let
X
be a left ideal of
\Lambda
.
Suppose that
X+\mathfrak{f}=\Lambda
and that there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{M}\cap A^{\times}
such that

\mathcal{M}X=\mathcal{M}\beta
. Let
u\in\mathcal{M}^{\times}
and let
\alpha=u\beta
.
Then
\overline{\alpha},\overline{\beta},\overline{u}\in(\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}
and
the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

X=\Lambda\alpha
,

(ii)

\Lambda\alpha+\mathfrak{f}=\Lambda
,

(iii)

\overline{\alpha}\in(\Lambda/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}
,

(iv)

\pi(\overline{\beta})=\pi(\overline{u^{-1}})
,

(v)

\alpha\in X
and
X
is locally free over
\Lambda
.

Remark 7.2
.

[
BHJ22
, Proposition 4.3 and Theorem 4.5]
only assumed that
\beta\in\mathcal{M}

but should have assumed that
\beta\in\mathcal{M}\cap A^{\times}
, or equivalently,
that
\beta\in\mathcal{M}
and that

is a
full
left ideal of
\Lambda
, that is,

.
Note that any locally free left ideal of
\Lambda
is necessarily full.
The strengthened assumption is needed to ensure that the condition

implies that

is in fact free over

(see
[

without this, the implication (i)

may not hold.
This omission does not affect any of the other results of
[
BHJ22
]
.
Also note that in loc. cit. it was assumed that

is strictly contained in
\mathcal{M}
, but in fact this assumption is unnecessary.

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
X
be a left ideal of
\Lambda
.
Suppose that

and that there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{M}\cap A^{\times}
such that
\mathcal{M}X=\mathcal{M}\beta
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

is free over
\Lambda
.

(ii)

and

(iii)

This follows from Proposition

after substituting

for their inverses.
∎

7.3.
An exact sequence

Most of the rest of this section will be concerned with the development of
a criterion for stable freeness. We shall first need a certain exact sequence.

Let

\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}(\Lambda,\mathfrak{f}):=\{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in\Lambda\oplus\Lambda\mid\lambda_{1}\equiv\lambda_{2}\bmod{\mathfrak{f}}\}.

, Corollary 2.4]

implies that for
\mathfrak{P}\mid\mathfrak{g}
, we have
\mathrm{nr}_{A_{\mathfrak{P}}/L_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{P}})\equiv 1\bmod{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{P}}}
,
so we can and do choose
\xi=1
.

It suffices to show that

{v_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathrm{nr}_{A_{\mathfrak{P}}/L_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{P}}))}=v_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathrm{nr}_{A_{\mathfrak{P}}/L_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\beta))

(7.8)

for all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{P}
of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. To this end, fix a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}
.

If
\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, then
\alpha_{\mathfrak{P}}=\beta
for all
\mathfrak{P}\mid\mathfrak{p}
and (
7.8
) is clear.
Now suppose
\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. First note that we have

\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}X_{\mathfrak{p}}+\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta+\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Hence there exists an element
\gamma\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}
such that

\gamma\beta\equiv 1\bmod{\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.
It follows that
\gamma\beta\equiv 1\bmod{\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{P}}}
for all
\mathfrak{P}\mid\mathfrak{p}
.
From
[
BB06
, Corollary 2.4]
we obtain
\mathrm{nr}_{A_{\mathfrak{P}}/L_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\gamma\beta)\equiv 1\bmod{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{P}}}

for all
\mathfrak{P}\mid\mathfrak{p}
.
Since
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\mathcal{O}_{C,\mathfrak{p}}
by Lemma
7.10
,
the assumption that
\mathfrak{g}
is locally radical implies that

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{P}}\subseteq\mathfrak{P}\mathcal{O}_{C,\mathfrak{P}}
.
Hence
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathrm{nr}_{A_{\mathfrak{P}}/L_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\beta))=0
.
Since
\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}\equiv 1\bmod{\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
the same argument shows that

v_{\mathfrak{P}}(\mathrm{nr}_{A_{\mathfrak{P}}/L_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{P}}))=0
for all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{P}\mid\mathfrak{p}
.

Hence (
7.8
) holds for all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{P}
of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
.
∎

7.10.
Choosing
\mathfrak{g}
first

Recall that in §
7.1
and §
7.4
, respectively,
we first chose
\mathfrak{f}
to be a full two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{M}
that is contained
in
\Lambda
and then set
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{f}\cap\mathcal{O}_{C}
.
In some applications, it is convenient to work the other way round:
first choose a full ideal
\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
such that
\mathfrak{g}\mathcal{M}\subseteq\Lambda
and then set
\mathfrak{f}:=\mathfrak{g}\mathcal{M}
.
The following lemma shows that the latter approach is consistent with the former.

Lemma 7.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a full ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
such that

\mathfrak{f}:=\mathfrak{g}\mathcal{M}\subseteq\Lambda
.
Then
\mathfrak{f}
is a full two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{M}
and

\mathfrak{f}\cap\mathcal{O}_{C}=\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

The first claim is clear. For the second claim, it suffices to show
that
\mathfrak{f}_{i}\cap\mathcal{O}_{K_{i}}=\mathfrak{g}_{i}

for each
i=1,\ldots,r
. So we fix
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, let
L:=K_{i}
and
replace
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
by
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{f}_{i}
by
\mathfrak{f}
,
\mathcal{M}_{i}
by
\mathcal{M}
and

\mathcal{O}_{C}
by
\mathcal{O}_{L}
.
By
[
Rei03
, Theorem 4.21]
, it suffices to show that

(\mathfrak{f}\cap\mathcal{O}_{L})_{\mathfrak{P}}=\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{P}}
for each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{P}
of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. Fix such an ideal
\mathfrak{P}
.
Then
\mathcal{O}_{L,\mathfrak{P}}
is a discrete valuation ring, and so
we have
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{P}}=g_{\mathfrak{P}}\mathcal{O}_{L,\mathfrak{P}}

for some
g_{\mathfrak{P}}\in\mathcal{O}_{L,\mathfrak{P}}
. Then we have

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{f}\cap\mathcal{O}_{L})_{\mathfrak{P}}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{P}}\cap\mathcal{O}_{L,\mathfrak{P}}=\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{P}}\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{P}}\cap\mathcal{O}_{L,\mathfrak{P}}=g_{\mathfrak{P}}\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{P}}\cap\mathcal{O}_{L,\mathfrak{P}}

\displaystyle=g_{\mathfrak{P}}\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{P}}\cap Z(\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{P}})=g_{\mathfrak{P}}Z(\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{P}})=g_{\mathfrak{P}}\mathcal{O}_{L,\mathfrak{P}}=\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{P}}.

The authors are grateful to an anonymous referee for suggesting this proof.

8.1.
Test lattices

We assume the setup and notation of §
7
.

Definition 8.1
.

For
\beta\in\mathcal{M}\cap A^{\times}

we define
X_{\beta}=X_{{\beta},\Lambda,\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{M}\beta\cap\Lambda
,
and call this
the
test lattice
attached to
\beta
(and
\Lambda,\mathcal{M}
).
Note that
X_{\beta}
is a left ideal of
\Lambda
.

The following lemma determines the localizations of certain test lattices.

Lemma 8.2
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is locally radical.
Let
\beta\in\mathcal{M}\cap A^{\times}
such that
\mathcal{M}\beta+\mathfrak{f}=\mathcal{M}
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}
.
Then

(X_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{p}}=\begin{cases}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta&\text{ if }\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}},\\
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}&\text{ if }\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}.\end{cases}

Moreover, if
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}
then

\beta\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
.

Proof.

If
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}

then

(X_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta\cap\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta\cap\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta
.

Now suppose that
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Then
\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and so by
the assumption that
\mathfrak{g}
is locally radical
and Lemma
7.10
we have that

\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{P}}\subseteq\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{P}})
for all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{P}
of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
with
\mathfrak{P}\mid\mathfrak{p}
.
Thus

\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{P}}\beta+\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{P}}=(\mathcal{M}+\mathfrak{f})_{\mathfrak{P}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{P}}

and so Nakayama’s lemma implies that

\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{P}}\beta=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{P}}
for all
\mathfrak{P}\mid\mathfrak{p}
, or equivalently,
\beta\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
.
Hence
\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and so

(X_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta\cap\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}.\qed

The following result establishes further properties of certain test lattices.
We recall that the maps
\pi,\mu
and
\tilde{\nu}
were defined in (
7.1
),
(
7.5
) and (
7.6
), respectively.

Proposition 8.3
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is locally radical.
Let
\overline{\beta}\in\left(\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f}\right)^{\times}
.
Then for every lift
\beta\in\mathcal{M}\cap A^{\times}
we have that

(i)

\mathcal{M}\beta+\mathfrak{f}=\mathcal{M}
,

(ii)

X_{\beta}
is locally free over
\Lambda
,

(iii)

\mathcal{M}X_{\beta}=\mathcal{M}\beta
,

(iv)

X_{\beta}+\mathfrak{f}=\Lambda
,

(v)

\tilde{\nu}(\overline{\beta})=(\mathrm{nr}(\beta)\mathcal{O}_{C}\bmod P_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+})
in
I_{\mathfrak{g}}/P_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}
,

(vi)

\mu(\tilde{\nu}(\overline{\beta}))=[X_{\beta}]_{\mathrm{st}}
in
\mathrm{Cl}(\Lambda)
,

(vii)

X_{\beta}
is stably free over
\Lambda
if and only if

\tilde{\nu}(\overline{\beta})\in\tilde{\nu}((\Lambda/\mathfrak{f})^{\times})=\ker(\mu)
, and

(viii)

X_{\beta}
is free over
\Lambda
if and only if
there exists
u\in\mathcal{M}^{\times}
such that
\pi(\overline{u})=\pi(\overline{\beta})
.

Proof.

Assertion (i) is equivalent to the hypothesis that
\overline{\beta}\in\left(\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f}\right)^{\times}
.
Assertion (ii) follows trivially from Lemma
8.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}
.
By
[
Rei03
, Theorem 4.21]
, it will suffice to prove assertions (iii) and (iv) after localising at

\mathfrak{p}
.
If
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}

then by Lemma
8.2
we have that

(X_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta
and thus

\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}X_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle(X_{\beta}+\mathfrak{f})_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle=(X_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{p}}+\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta+\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{p}}=(\mathcal{M}\beta+\mathfrak{f})_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}},

where the final equality holds by part (i).
If
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}

then by Lemma
8.2
we have that

(X_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\beta\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
,
and thus

\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}X_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}(X_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle(X_{\beta}+\mathfrak{f})_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle=(X_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{p}}+\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}+\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Assertion (v) is just the definition of
\tilde{\nu}
(
7.6
).
Assertion (vi) follows from (ii), (iii) and (iv) together with Theorem
7.11
.
We have that
X_{\beta}
is stably free over
\Lambda
if and only if
[X_{\beta}]_{\mathrm{st}}=0

and by (
7.7
) we have
\ker(\mu)=\tilde{\nu}((\Lambda/\mathfrak{f})^{\times})
.
Therefore assertion (vii) now follows from (vi).
Finally, assertion (viii) follows from (iii) and (iv) together with Corollary
7.3
.
∎

Corollary 8.4
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is locally radical.
Let
X
be a locally free left ideal of
\Lambda
.
Suppose that

Corollary 8.5
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is locally radical.
Let

\overline{\beta_{1}},\overline{\beta_{2}}\in(\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}

Suppose that

\mathcal{M}^{\times}\backslash(\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}{}{/}{}(\Lambda/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}

(i)

is free over

if and only if
X_{\beta_{2}}
is free over
\Lambda
.

(ii)

X_{\beta_{1}}

is stably

\Lambda

By hypothesis there exist

in

Lemma 8.6
.

Let

.

Theorem 8.7
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
has SFC and that
\mathfrak{g}
is locally radical.
Let

\Lambda
has SFC if and only if
X_{\beta}
is free over
\Lambda
for every
\beta\in T

Proof.

Suppose that
\Lambda
has SFC.
If
\beta\in T
then

is stably free over
\Lambda

and so

is in fact free over
\Lambda

So suppose that
X
is a stably free

and

in

.
By Lemma

is a locally free left ideal of

such that

Moreover,
\mathcal{M}X\cong\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\Lambda}X

over

\mathcal{M}

8.2.
An algorithm for determining SFC

The following algorithm determines whether an order has SFC.
However, it is only practical for orders in algebras of ‘small’ dimension and
in many situations, it is often much faster to use Algorithm
9.1
or

10.3
.

Algorithm 8.9
.

Let
K
be a number field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
and let
A
be a finite-dimensional semisimple
K
-algebra.
Let
\Lambda
be an
\mathcal{O}
-order in
A
.
If the following algorithm returns
\mathsf{true}
, then
\Lambda
has SFC.
If it returns
\mathsf{false}
, then
\Lambda
fails SFC.

(1)

Compute the center
C
of
A
and the decomposition of
A
into simple
K
-algebras.

(2)

Compute a maximal
\mathcal{O}
-order
\mathcal{M}
in
A
containing
\Lambda
.

(3)

Check whether
\mathcal{M}
has SFC.
If not, return
\mathsf{false}
.
If so and
\Lambda=\mathcal{M}
return
\mathsf{true}
.
Otherwise, continue with the steps below.

(4)

Compute

\mathfrak{f}:=\{x\in A\mid x\mathcal{M}\subseteq\Lambda\}\cap\{x\in A\mid\mathcal{M}x\subseteq\Lambda\}
.

(5)

Compute
\mathcal{O}_{C}:=\mathcal{M}\cap C
and

\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{f}\cap\mathcal{O}_{C}=\mathfrak{f}\cap C
.

(6)

Adjust
\mathfrak{f}
(and thus
\mathfrak{g}
) to ensure
that
\mathfrak{g}
is locally radical.

(7)

Compute a set of generators of
(\Lambda/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}
.

(8)

Compute
I_{\mathfrak{g}}/P_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}
and

\tilde{\nu}((\Lambda/\mathfrak{f})^{\times})\leq I_{\mathfrak{g}}/P_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}
as finite abelian groups.

(9)

Compute the set
(\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}
and a set of representatives

S\subseteq\mathcal{M}\cap A^{\times}
of
(\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}
.

(10)

Compute the set

T=\{\beta\in S\colon(\mathrm{nr}(\beta)\mathcal{O}_{C}\bmod P_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+})\in\tilde{\nu}((\Lambda/\mathfrak{f})^{\times})\}
.

(11)

For each
\beta\in T
check whether
X_{\beta}
is free over
\Lambda
.
If this is true for all
\beta\in T
, return
\mathsf{true}
.
Otherwise return
\mathsf{false}
.

Proof of correctness.

In step (
3
), the correctness of an output
\mathsf{false}
follows from Corollary
3.2
and the correctness of an output

\mathsf{true}
is clear.
By Proposition
8.3
(v),(vii) we have

T=\{\beta\in S\colon X_{\beta}\text{ is stably free over }\Lambda\}
.
Thus in step (
11
), the correctness of the output follows from
Theorem
8.7
.
∎

Further details on each step.

Steps (1)–(5) can be performed using
[
FR85
, 1.5 B]
,
[
Fri00
, Kapitel 3 and 4]
,
Corollary
3.6
,
[
Fri00
, Algorithmus (2.16)]
and
[
Coh00
, Algorithm 1.5.1]
, respectively.

Step (6): First note that for a given ideal
\mathfrak{g}

there are only finitely many maximal ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}
such that

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\mathcal{O}_{C,\mathfrak{p}}
.
If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{f}\cap\mathcal{O}_{C}
is not locally radical then
there exists such a
\mathfrak{p}
and a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{P}
of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
with
\mathfrak{P}\mid\mathfrak{p}
such that

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{P}}\not\subseteq\mathfrak{P}\mathcal{O}_{C,\mathfrak{P}}
.
For each such
\mathfrak{P}
,
we replace
\mathfrak{f}
by
\mathfrak{P}\mathfrak{f}
and observe that
(\mathfrak{P}\mathfrak{f}\cap\mathcal{O}_{C})_{\mathfrak{P}}=\mathfrak{P}\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{P}}\cap\mathcal{O}_{C,\mathfrak{P}}\subseteq\mathfrak{P}\left(\mathfrak{f}_{\mathfrak{P}}\cap\mathcal{O}_{C,\mathfrak{P}}\right)\subseteq\mathfrak{P}\mathcal{O}_{C,\mathfrak{P}}
.

Step (7) can be performed using the algorithm described in
[
BHJ22
, §6.6]

(this is a minor modification of
[
BB06
, §3.4–3.7]
).

Step (8): In the notation of §
7.7
, compute
\infty_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r

using
[
NS09
, Theorem 3.5]
.
The structure of the finitely generated abelian group
I_{\mathfrak{g}}/P_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+}
can
then be computed componentwise using
[
Coh00
, Algorithm 4.3.1]
.
Using the output of Step (7) and the definition of
\tilde{\nu}
,
then determine generators of
\tilde{\nu}((\Lambda/\mathfrak{f})^{\times})
.

Steps (9) and (10):
Since both rings
\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f}
and
\Lambda/\mathfrak{f}
are finite, their units can be computed as sets
in finite time. (For a more practical approach, see §
11
).
Given
\beta\in S
, algorithms for finitely generated abelian groups, as presented for example in
[
Coh00
, §4.1]
, can be used
to determine whether
\tilde{\nu}(\overline{\beta})=(\mathrm{nr}(\beta)\mathcal{O}_{C}\bmod P_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+})
belongs to
\tilde{\nu}((\Lambda/\mathfrak{f})^{\times})
.

Step (11): Given
\beta\in T
,
[
BJ11
, Algorithm 3.1]

(see also
[
HJ20
, Algorithm 4.1]
)
can be used to determine
whether
X_{\beta}
is free over
\Lambda
(though stated for orders in group algebras,
this algorithm can also be applied to orders in arbitrary finite-dimensional semisimple algebras
as explained in
[
BJ11
, Remark 4.9]
).
Note that this algorithm requires certain hypotheses, which ensure that one can find the preimage of the image of the canonical map

\mathcal{M}^{\times}\longrightarrow(\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}
,
as well as a generator of
\mathcal{M}X_{\beta}
as an
\mathcal{M}
-module.
Since generators of
\mathcal{M}^{\times}
can in principle be found using the algorithms of
[
BCNS15
]
,
and since
\mathcal{M}X_{\beta}=\mathcal{M}\beta
by Proposition

9.
Orders without SFC

9.1.
An algorithm to show that an order fails SFC

The following algorithm can be viewed as a modified version of Algorithm
8.9

that cannot prove that an order has SFC, but can be used to show that it fails SFC.

Algorithm 9.1
.

Let
K
be a number field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
and let
A
be a finite-dimensional semisimple
K
-algebra.
Let
\Lambda
be an
\mathcal{O}
-order in
A
.
If the following algorithm returns
\mathsf{false}
, then
\Lambda
fails SFC.
If it returns
\mathsf{true}
, then
\Lambda
is maximal and has SFC.
If it returns
\mathsf{inconclusive}
, then nothing can be said about whether
\Lambda
has SFC.

(1)

Perform steps (1) and (2) of Algorithm
8.9
.

(2)

Check whether
\mathcal{M}
has SFC.
If not, return
\mathsf{false}
.
If so and
\Lambda=\mathcal{M}
return
\mathsf{true}
.
Otherwise, continue with the steps below.

(3)

Perform steps (4)–(8) of Algorithm
8.9
.

(4)

Choose
r\geq 1
and compute ‘random’ elements

\beta_{1},\dotsc,\beta_{r}\in\mathcal{M}\cap A^{\times}

such that
\overline{\beta_{i}}\in(\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}

and
(\mathrm{nr}(\beta_{i})\mathcal{O}_{C}\bmod P_{\mathfrak{g}}^{+})\in\tilde{\nu}((\Lambda/\mathfrak{f})^{\times})
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
.

(5)

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
check whether
X_{\beta_{i}}
is free over
\Lambda
.
If this is true for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, return
\mathsf{inconclusive}
.
Otherwise, return
\mathsf{false}
.

Proof of correctness.

In step (
2
), the correctness of an output
\mathsf{false}
follows from Corollary
3.2
and the correctness of an output

\mathsf{true}
is clear.
Step (
4
)
ensures that
X_{\beta_{i}}

is stably free over
\Lambda
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}

by Proposition
8.3
(v),(vii).
Thus the output of step (
5
) is correct because if there exists

i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
such that
X_{\beta_{i}}
is not free over
\Lambda
,
then
\Lambda
clearly fails SFC.
Note that step (
2
) is in fact optional,
in that the algorithm is still correct even if it is omitted.
∎

Further details on each step.

Steps (1)–(3) and step (
5
) of Algorithm
9.1

can be performed in the same way
as the corresponding steps of Algorithm
8.9
.
To find the ‘random’ elements in step (
4
),
one can determine elements of
\mathcal{M}\cap A^{\times}

with random coordinates in a fixed basis of bounded height and then check whether they are invertible modulo
\mathfrak{f}
.
Alternatively, one could also determine elements of
\mathcal{M}\cap A^{\times}

generating
(\mathcal{M}/\mathfrak{f})^{\times}
using the algorithm described
in
[
BHJ22
, §6.6]
(which is a minor modification of

[
BB06
, §3.4–3.7]
)
and then take random words of bounded length in the generators.
∎

Remark 9.2
.

Thanks to the fact that Algorithm
9.1
does not need to perform steps (9) or (10)
of Algorithm
8.9
, in practice the former is often much faster than the latter
if one wants to prove that a given order
\Lambda
fails SFC.

Remark 9.3
.

Due to the invocation of the (expensive) freeness test of
[
BJ11
, Algorithm 3.1]
,
Algorithm
9.1
is only practical when
\Lambda
is an order in an algebra

A
of ‘moderate’ dimension.
However,
it is also possible to obtain results when
A
is of larger dimension as follows.
A projection of
K
-algebras
A\to B
induces a projection of
\Lambda
onto an

\mathcal{O}
-order
\Gamma
in
B
.
If we can apply Algorithm
9.1
to show that
\Gamma
fails SFC,
then Theorem
3.1
shows that
\Lambda
also fails SFC.
Since we are free to choose
B
, we can take
B
to be a direct sum of simple components of
A

of small dimension, chosen such that all simple components of
A

that do not satisfy the Eichler condition
are also components of
B
and Algorithm
9.1
finishes quickly.

9.2.
Integral group rings without SFC

We now use Algorithm
9.1
and Remark
9.3
to
show that certain integral group rings fail SFC.
Parts of the following result, which is Theorem

from the introduction,
have already been proven by Swan
[

]
and Chen

Theorem 9.4
.

Let

be one of the following finite groups:

(ii)

\tilde{T}\times C_{2}^{2}

.

Remark 9.5
.

Theorem

agrees with previously known results.
Recall from §
4

that Swan
[
Swa83
, Theorem 15.1]
already proved that
\mathbb{Z}[Q_{8}\times C_{2}]

Che86

,

,
Q_{20}\times C_{2}

,

.

Remark 9.6
.

In order, the groups listed in Theorem

(i)
are isomorphic to

,

,

,

and those in (ii) to

,

Proof of Theorem

.

We first show that

fails SFC.
Further details on the computations used in this part of the proof can be found in
Appendix

.
Let
e
be the central idempotent of
\mathbb{Q}[G]
such that
e\mathbb{Q}[G]
is the direct sum
of all simple components of
\mathbb{Q}[G]
that are
either commutative or totally definite quaternion algebras.
For each group
G
listed apart from

,
verification that

fails SFC was performed using Algorithm

then implies that

also fails SFC.
For

there exists a normal subgroup

of order

such that

and

3.1
then implies that

also fails SFC.

We now show that

, it suffices to consider maximal proper quotients

If

The only groups

10.
Determining SFC using fiber products

The main result of this section is Algorithm
10.3
, which
may be viewed as a constructive version of Corollary
5.12
.

10.1.
Setup

Let
K
be a number field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
and let
A
be a finite-dimensional semisimple
K
-algebra.
Let
\Lambda
be an
\mathcal{O}
-order in
A
.
Let
I
and
J
be two-sided ideals of
\Lambda
such that
I\cap J=0

and
K(I+J)=A
. Let
\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda/I
, let
\Lambda_{2}=\Lambda/J

and let
\overline{\Lambda}=\Lambda/(I+J)
.
Then
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
are
\mathcal{O}
-orders
and
\overline{\Lambda}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-torsion
\mathcal{O}
-algebra.
Then as in Example
5.1
, we have a fiber product

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\Lambda},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{2}}

(10.1)

where each of
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},g_{1},g_{2}
is the relevant canonical surjection.

Let
A_{1}:=K\Lambda_{1}\cong KJ
and let
A_{2}:=K\Lambda_{2}\cong KI
.
Then by making the appropriate identifications, we can and do assume that

\Lambda=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Lambda_{1}\oplus\Lambda_{2}\mid g_{1}(x_{1})=g_{2}(x_{2})\}\subseteq\Lambda_{1}\oplus\Lambda_{2}\subseteq A_{1}\oplus A_{2}=A.

(10.2)

In particular,
\pi_{i}
is the map induced by the canonical projection
A\to A_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

Recall from Proposition
5.2
that for
u\in\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}
,
we define

M(u):=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Lambda_{1}\oplus\Lambda_{2}\mid g_{1}(x_{1})u=g_{2}(x_{2})\}.

In particular, this is a locally free
\Lambda
-lattice of rank
1
.
We refer to such a lattice
M(u)
as a
pullback lattice
.
Recall from Corollary
5.3
that we have a group homomorphism

\partial:\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cl}(\Lambda),\quad u\mapsto[M(u)]_{\mathrm{st}}.

We shall relate pullback lattices to test lattices and
use this to give an alternative description of
\ker(\partial)
.
Under the further assumption that
A_{1}
satisfies the Eichler condition,
this will lead to an algorithm to test whether
\Lambda
has SFC.

10.2.
Test lattices and pullback lattices

For
i=1,2
, let
\mathcal{M}_{i}
be a choice of maximal

\mathcal{O}
-order in
A_{i}
containing

\Lambda_{i}
(such choices exist by
[
Rei03
, (10.4)]
) and
let
\mathfrak{f}_{i}
be any full two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{M}_{i}
that
is contained in
\Lambda\cap A_{i}
.
Let
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{2}
and let

\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{f}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{f}_{2}
.
For
i=1,2
, let
C_{i}
be the center of
A_{i}
and let
\mathfrak{g}_{i}:=\mathfrak{f}_{i}\cap\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}}=\mathfrak{f}_{i}\cap C_{i}
.
Let
C=C_{1}\oplus C_{2}
and let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
.
We set

\mathfrak{h}_{1}:=\ker(g_{1}),\quad\mathfrak{h}_{2}:=\ker(g_{2}),\quad\mathfrak{h}:=\pi_{1}^{-1}(\mathfrak{h}_{1})=\pi_{2}^{-1}(\mathfrak{h}_{2})=I+J,

and observe that

\overline{\Lambda}=\Lambda/\mathfrak{h}\cong\Lambda_{1}/\mathfrak{h}_{1}\cong\Lambda_{2}/\mathfrak{h}_{2}.

Using the identification in (
10.2
) we have
\mathfrak{h}_{i}=\Lambda\cap A_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

Since for any
x_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}
with
(x_{1},0)\in\Lambda
we have
x_{1}\in\ker(g_{1})
,
it follows that
\mathfrak{f}_{1}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{1}
.
Similarly,
\mathfrak{f}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{2}
and therefore
\mathfrak{f}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
.
Recall from Definition
8.1
that for
\beta\in\mathcal{M}\cap A^{\times}
, the test lattice
X_{\beta}=X_{\beta,\Lambda,\mathcal{M}}
is defined to be

\mathcal{M}\beta\cap\Lambda
.

The following result shows that under mild assumptions, pullback lattices are in fact
(isomorphic to) test lattices.

Proposition 10.1
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is locally radical.
Let
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\Lambda_{1}\oplus\Lambda_{2}
with

\beta\in A^{\times}
and
\overline{\beta_{i}}\in\left(\Lambda_{i}/\mathfrak{f}_{i}\right)^{\times}
for
i=1,2
.
Let
u=g_{1}(\beta_{1})g_{2}(\beta_{2})^{-1}\in\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}
.
Then
X_{\beta}=M(u)\beta
, so in particular,

X_{\beta}\cong M(u)
as
\Lambda
-lattices.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}
.
By
[
Rei03
, Theorem 4.21]
, it suffices to prove that

(X_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{p}}=(M(u)\beta)_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Note that
(M(u)\beta)_{\mathfrak{p}}=M(u)_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta
.

Suppose that
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Since

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\Lambda_{1,\mathfrak{p}}\oplus\Lambda_{2,\mathfrak{p}}

and
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}
is maximal, we have that

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Lambda_{1,\mathfrak{p}}\oplus\Lambda_{2,\mathfrak{p}}
.
Thus localising the fiber product (
5.1
) at
\mathfrak{p}

implies that
\mathfrak{h}_{i,\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{i,\mathfrak{p}}=\Lambda_{i,\mathfrak{p}}
for

and

\overline{\Lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
.
It is then easy to see that

M(u)_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Lambda_{1,\mathfrak{p}}\oplus\Lambda_{2,\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and hence

M(u)_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta=\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta=(X_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
where the last equality follows from Lemma
8.2
.

Now suppose that
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Then
\beta\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
by
Lemma

.
Thus
\beta_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{i,\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}

and so in fact

\beta_{i}\in\Lambda_{i,\mathfrak{p}}\cap\mathcal{M}_{i,\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}=\Lambda_{i,\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
(see
[
Kar92
, Chapter I, Theorem 7.9(iii)]
).
Then we have

\displaystyle M(u)_{\mathfrak{p}}\beta

\displaystyle=\{(a_{1}\beta_{1},a_{2}\beta_{2})\mid(a_{1},a_{2})\in\Lambda_{1,\mathfrak{p}}\oplus\Lambda_{2,\mathfrak{p}}\text{ and }g_{1,\mathfrak{p}}(a_{1})u=g_{2,\mathfrak{p}}(a_{2})\}

\displaystyle=\{(a_{1}\beta_{1},a_{2}\beta_{2})\mid(a_{1},a_{2})\in\Lambda_{1,\mathfrak{p}}\oplus\Lambda_{2,\mathfrak{p}}\text{ and }g_{1,\mathfrak{p}}(a_{1}\beta_{1})=g_{2,\mathfrak{p}}(a_{2}\beta_{2})\}

\displaystyle=\{(b_{1},b_{2})\mid(b_{1},b_{2})\in\Lambda_{1,\mathfrak{p}}\oplus\Lambda_{2,\mathfrak{p}}\text{ and }g_{1,\mathfrak{p}}(b_{1})=g_{2,\mathfrak{p}}(b_{2})\}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}=(X_{\beta})_{\mathfrak{p}},

where the last equality follows from Lemma
8.2
.

10.3.

Recall from (

) and (

) respectively
that we have an exact sequence

and that the reduced norm map induces a group homomorphism

Therefore

Let

Define

where

.

The map

is locally radical

,

.

K

Let
\Lambda
be an
\mathcal{O}
-order in
A
and let

\mathfrak{h}
be any full two-sided ideal of

.
In §
11.2
, we describe a practical algorithm to compute
a set of generators of the unit group
(\Lambda/\mathfrak{h})^{\times}
.
In §

, we then describe a practical algorithm

11.2.
Primary decomposition and generators of unit groups of quotient rings

Let
C
be an étale
K
-algebra and let
\Gamma
be an
\mathcal{O}
-order in
C
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a proper ideal of
\Gamma
of full rank.
We now describe the computation of a primary decomposition of

\mathfrak{g}
, that is, the computation of coprime primary ideals

\mathfrak{q}_{1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{q}_{r}
of
\Gamma
, such that

\mathfrak{g}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{q}_{i}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{q}_{i}.

Proposition 11.1
.

Let
C
be an étale
K
-algebra and let
\Gamma
be an
\mathcal{O}
-order in
C
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a proper ideal of
\Gamma
of full rank.
Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{p}_{r}
be the maximal ideals of
\Gamma

containing
\mathfrak{g}
. Then the following hold.

(i)

There exist exponents
e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that

\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r}^{e_{r}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r}.

(ii)

For each
i\in\{1,\dotsc,r\}
, the ideal

\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{e_{i}}+\mathfrak{g}
is
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
-primary.

(iii)

A primary decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
is given by

\mathfrak{g}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}(\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{e_{i}}+\mathfrak{g})=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{e_{i}}+\mathfrak{g}).

Proof.

(i)
As in the proof of
[
Neu99
, (3.4) Lemma]
one can show that there is a product of maximal ideals

\mathfrak{q}_{i}
of
\Gamma
such that
\mathfrak{q}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{q}_{m}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
.
We take
m
to be minimal.
Suppose that one of the
\mathfrak{q}_{i}
, say
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
, is not contained in the set
\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{r}\}
.
Then we claim that
\mathfrak{q}_{2}\cdots\mathfrak{q}_{m}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
contradicting the minimality of
m
.
Indeed, we then have
\mathfrak{q}_{1}+\mathfrak{g}=\Gamma
, so that we can write
1=x+y
with

x\in\mathfrak{q}_{1}
and
y\in\mathfrak{g}
.
For any
z\in\mathfrak{q}_{2}\cdots\mathfrak{q}_{m}
we obtain
z=zx+zy\in\mathfrak{g}
.
We conclude that there exist
e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that

\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r}^{e_{r}}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
.

(ii) Since
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{e_{i}}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{e_{i}}+\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, the radical of
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{e_{i}}+\mathfrak{g}
is the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. This implies that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{e_{i}}+\mathfrak{g}
is
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
-primary by
[
AM69
, Proposition (4.2)]
.

(iii) By part (ii), it suffices to show that
\mathfrak{g}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}(\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{e_{i}}+\mathfrak{g})=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{e_{i}}+\mathfrak{g})
.
The second equality is clear because the ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{e_{i}}+\mathfrak{g}
are pairwise coprime.
For the first equality, observe that

\prod_{i=1}^{r}(\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{e_{i}}+\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r}^{e_{r}}+\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}
;
the reverse inclusion is obvious.
∎

.

Let

be the (unique) maximal

-order in

.
This is equal to the integral closure of

in

By

11.3.
Group structure of unit groups of finite rings

Let
\overline{\Lambda}=\Lambda/\mathfrak{h}
.
Then
\overline{\Lambda}
is a finite ring and an important challenge is to
compute its units
\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}
as an abstract group.
The point is that, although we can compute generators of
\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}

by Corollary
11.4
, we require a representation of
\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}

that will allow us to efficiently determine the group-theoretic properties required for applications,
such as the computation of the abelianisation
\overline{\Lambda}^{\times\mathrm{ab}}
.
Specifically, representations which are useful for actual computations include
realisations of
\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}
as a subgroup of either

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
for some finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
or of symmetric groups
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
, both of course with reasonable sizes for
n
and

q
, or
d
, respectively (see
[
HEO05
]
).

Let
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\overline{\Lambda})
denote the group of
\mathbb{Z}
-linear automorphisms of the
(additive)
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\overline{\Lambda}
.
Consider the injective homomorphism

\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\overline{\Lambda}),\quad x\longmapsto(y\mapsto xy).

Since we assume that we can compute generators of
\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}
,
the problem is reduced to finding a suitable representation for
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\overline{\Lambda})
.
We now describe two ways in which this can be achieved in practice.

(i)

If
\overline{\Lambda}
is a free
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-algebra of rank
k
for some
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
then after choosing a
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-basis we have an isomorphism

\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\overline{\Lambda})\cong\mathrm{GL}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
.
If we further assume that
n
is equal to a prime
p
, then this in turn yields a realisation
of
\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}
as a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

(ii)

Generators of
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\overline{\Lambda})

can be computed using results of Shoda
[
Sho28
]
.
These can be used to determine a faithful permutation representation (see
[
CH03
]
)

\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\overline{\Lambda})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{d}
for some
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
which in turn yields a representation of
\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}
as a permutation group.

In general, the method of (i) allows computations to be performed more efficiently than the
method of (ii), but of course the method of (i) is not always applicable.
We also note that if
\overline{\Lambda}
can be written as a direct product of rings

R_{1}\times\cdots\times R_{s}
, then

\overline{\Lambda}^{\times}=(R_{1}\times\cdots\times R_{s})^{\times}=R_{1}^{\times}\times\cdots\times R_{s}^{\times}

and so either of the above methods can be applied to each component
R_{i}
individually,
which is more efficient than applying either method to
\overline{\Lambda}
directly.

satisfies

Proof.

Now assume that (ii) holds.
Then there exists a unit

and
an element

exists

Lemma 12.8
.

Consider
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{f}\cap C

-ideals with
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{p}_{r}

\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dotsc,\mathfrak{p}_{r+s}

Let

Step (2) can be performed as follows.

.
Thus in order to find a subgroup

of

can be found by sampling preimages of

Remark 13.3
.

The computations involved in the proof of Theorem

rely on the algorithm of
[
BB06
, §3]
to compute locally free class groups and
on the new freeness testing methods of §

.
These in turn rely on the computation of ray class groups and of unit groups of rings of integers of number fields.
Due to reasons of efficiency, state-of-the-art algorithms to perform these computations yield solutions that are provably correct under the assumption that the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) holds.

As these fields are of small degree and discriminant, the necessary computations can be performed unconditionally (or can be found in the literature) and hence Theorem

does not depend on GRH.

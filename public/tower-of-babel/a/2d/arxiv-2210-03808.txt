Elementary derivations of centripetal acceleration and works of Huygens and Newton: a revisit with a simple physical derivation
Siu A. Chin

Abstract
This work shows how two simple derivations of the centripetal acceleration are fundamentally related to seminal works of Huygens and Newton. A different, more physically motivated derivation is then given.

After reviewing the above two historical derivations, this work proposes the following
more physical picture. From projectile motion, one learns that a baseball

v

d

t=d/v

During this time, the ball would free fall a height of

h=\frac{1}{2}gt^{2}=\frac{1}{2}g\frac{d^{2}}{v^{2}}.

(9)

If

equals to the the initial height of the thrown ball, then the ball hits the ground
at a distance
d
from its original location. This is elementary to all students.
However, the earth is not flat. As shown in Fig.
3

d

earth’s “ground” drops below the horizon by a distance

This

\displaystyle\frac{AB}{BC}=\frac{BC}{BD}\quad\rightarrow\quad H=\frac{d^{2}}{2R}.

v=\sqrt{gR}.

(11)

At this velocity, the baseball becomes airborne and orbits the earth in a circular

In this derivation, the orbit is circular because the object is constantly falling

Additional close links between balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials


Abstract
Using generating functions, we derive many identities involving balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials. By relating these polynomials to Chebyshev polynomials of the first and second kind, and Fibonacci and Lucas numbers, we offer some presumably new combinatorial identities involving these famous sequences.

Introduction and preliminaries

For any integer
n\geq 0
, the balancing polynomials
\big(B_{n}(x)\big)_{n\geq 0}
and Lucas-balancing polynomials
\big(C_{n}(x)\bigr)_{n\geq 0}
are defined by the same second-order homogeneous linear recurrence

u_{n}(x)=6xu_{n-1}(x)-u_{n-2}(x),

(1)

but with different initial terms
B_{0}(x)=0
,
B_{1}(x)=1
and

C_{0}(x)=1
,
C_{1}(x)=3x
. These polynomials have been introduced as a natural extension of the popular balancing and Lucas-balancing numbers
B_{n}
and
C_{n}
, respectively, which were firstly studied in
[
1
]
. Obviously,
B_{n}=B_{n}(1)
and
C_{n}=C_{n}(1)
. Sequences
\big(B_{n}\big)_{n\geq 0}
and
\big(C_{n}\big)_{n\geq 0}
are indexed in the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences
[
16
]
(see entries
A001109
and
A001541
, respectively).

The closed forms which are also called Binet’s formulas for balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials are given by

B_{n}(x)=\frac{\lambda^{n}(x)-\lambda^{-n}(x)}{2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}},\qquad C_{n}(x)=\frac{\lambda^{n}(x)+\lambda^{-n}(x)}{2},

(2)

where
\lambda(x)=3x+\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}
.

For
n\geq 1
, the balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials are given explicitly
[
13
,
14
]
by

\displaystyle B_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor{\frac{n-1}{2}}\right\rfloor}(-1)^{k}\binom{n-1-k}{k}(6x)^{n-1-2k},\quad C_{n}(x)=\frac{n}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor{\frac{n}{2}}\right\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{n-k}\binom{n-k}{k}(6x)^{n-2k}.

The first polynomials are

\displaystyle B_{0}(x)=0,\quad\,\,B_{1}(x)=1,\quad\,\,B_{2}(x)=6x,\quad\,\,B_{3}(x)=36x^{2}-1,

\displaystyle B_{4}(x)=216x^{3}-12x,\,\,\quad B_{5}(x)=1296x^{4}-108x^{2}+1,

and

\displaystyle C_{0}(x)=1,\,\,\quad C_{1}(x)=3x,\,\,\quad C_{2}(x)=18x^{2}-1,\,\,\quad C_{3}(x)=108x^{3}-9x,

\displaystyle C_{4}(x)=648x^{4}-72x^{2}+1,\,\,\quad C_{5}(x)=3888x^{5}-540x^{3}+15x.

These polynomials have been studied extensively in different contexts and a variety of interesting results about them have been uncovered
[
2
,
3
,
6
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
14
]
. For example, in
[
2
]
, the first author established direct connections of the polynomials
B_{n}(x)
and
C_{n}(x)
with Fibonacci numbers, Lucas numbers and Chebyshev and Legendre polynomials. By using combinatorial methods, Meng derived some symmetry identities of the structural properties of balancing numbers and balancing polynomials
[
11
]
. In
[
8
,
9
]
, the authors study sums of finite products of balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials and represent them in terms of nine orthogonal polynomials. In
[
14
]
, Ray studied the sequences obtained by differentiating the balancing polynomials and presented some relations between the balancing polynomials and their derivatives.

In the present study, we derive new identities for polynomials
B_{n}(x)
and
C_{n}(x)
. Evaluating these identities at specific points, we can also establish some interesting combinatorial identities as special cases, especially those with Fibonacci and Lucas numbers. Our approach is in the spirit of
[
4
,
7
]
.

2
Balancing polynomial relations using ordinary

generating functions

To establish our main results, we will find the ordinary (non-exponential) generating functions for the sequences in question. We will make use of the following result
[
12
]
to compute the ordinary generating functions for balancing, Lucas-balancing polynomials and their odd (even) indexed companions.

Lemma 1
.

The second-order polynomial recurrence
u_{n}(x)=pu_{n-1}(x)+qu_{n-2}(x)
,
n\geq 2
,
p^{2}+4q\neq 0
, with initial terms
u_{0}(x)
and
u_{1}(x)
has generating function

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}u_{n}(x)z^{n}=\frac{u_{0}(x)+\bigl(u_{1}(x)-pu_{0}(x)\bigr)z}{1-pz-qz^{2}},

while for odd (even) indexed sequences

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}u_{2n+1}(x)z^{n}=\frac{u_{1}(x)+\bigl(pqu_{0}(x)-qu_{1}(x)\bigr)z}{1-(p^{2}+2q)z+q^{2}z^{2}},

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}u_{2n}(x)z^{n}=\frac{u_{0}(x)+\bigl(u_{2}(x)-(p^{2}+2q)u_{0}(x)\bigr)z}{1-(p^{2}+2q)z+q^{2}z^{2}}.

From the above lemma we obtain ordinary generating functions of the sequences
B_{n}(x)
,
B_{2n+1}(x)
, and
B_{2n}(x)
as follows

\displaystyle b(x,z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}(x)z^{n}=\frac{z}{1-6xz+z^{2}},

(3)

\displaystyle b_{1}(x,z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{2n+1}(x)z^{n}=\frac{1+z}{1-(36x^{2}-2)z+z^{2}},

\displaystyle b_{2}(x,z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{2n}(x)z^{n}=\frac{6xz}{1-(36x^{2}-2)z+z^{2}}.

(4)

In the similar manner, we conclude that ordinary generating functions of the sequences
C_{n}(x)
,
C_{2n+1}(x)
and
C_{2n}(x)
can be derived as

\displaystyle c(x,z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}C_{n}(x)z^{n}=\frac{1-3xz}{1-6xz+z^{2}},

\displaystyle c_{1}(x,z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}C_{2n+1}(x)z^{n}=\frac{3x-3xz}{1-(36x^{2}-2)z+z^{2}},

(5)

\displaystyle c_{2}(x,z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}C_{2n}(x)z^{n}=\frac{1+(1-18x^{2})z}{1-(36x^{2}-2)z+z^{2}}.

(6)

We present our first findings in two theorems, which provide some relations between balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials using their respective ordinary generating functions.

Theorem 2
.

For
n\geq 1
, the following formulas hold

\displaystyle B_{n}(x)-3xB_{n-1}(x)=C_{n-1}(x),

\displaystyle 3x\bigl(B_{2n+1}(x)-B_{2n-1}(x)\bigr)=C_{2n+1}(x)+C_{2n-1}(x),

\displaystyle B_{2n}(x)-(18x^{2}-1)B_{2(n-1)}(x)=6xC_{2(n-1)}(x),

(7)

\displaystyle B_{2n+1}(x)-(18x^{2}-1)B_{2n-1}(x)=C_{2n}(x)+C_{2(n-1)}(x),

\displaystyle 3x\bigl(B_{2n}(x)-B_{2(n-1)}(x)\bigr)=6xC_{2n-1}(x).

Proof.

All stated identities can be proved directly using Binet’s formulas (
2
). We present a proof based on generating functions. We will prove formula (
7
); the others may also be shown in a similar manner.

By (
4
) and (
6
), we obtain

\bigl(1-(18x^{2}-1)z\bigr)b_{2}(x,z)=6xzc_{2}(x,z).

Expanding both sides of the last equation as a power series in
z
, we then have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{2n}(x)z^{n}-(18x^{2}-1)\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{2n}(x)z^{n+1}=6x\sum_{n\geq 0}C_{2n}(x)z^{n+1}

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}B_{2n}(x)z^{n}-(18x^{2}-1)\sum_{n\geq 1}B_{2(n-1)}(x)z^{n}=6x\sum_{n\geq 1}C_{2(n-1)}(x)z^{n}.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides, we get (
7
).
∎

For convention, throughout this paper the empty sums are evaluated to
0
.

Theorem 3
.

For
n\geq 1
, the following formulas hold:

\displaystyle 3x\bigl(B_{n}(x)-B_{n-1}(x)\bigr)=C_{2n-1}(x)-(36x^{2}-6x-2)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}B_{k}(x)C_{2(n-k)-1}(x),

(8)

\displaystyle B_{n}(x)-(18x^{2}-1)B_{n-1}(x)=C_{2(n-1)}(x)-(36x^{2}-6x-2)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}B_{k}(x)C_{2(n-k-1)}(x),

\displaystyle B_{2n+1}(x)-3xB_{2n-1}(x)=C_{n}(x)+C_{n-1}(x)-(36x^{2}-6x-2)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}B_{2k+1}(x)C_{n-k-1}(x),

\displaystyle B_{2n}(x)-3xB_{2(n-1)}(x)=6xC_{n-1}(x)-(36x^{2}-6x-2)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}B_{2k}(x)C_{n-k-1}(x).

Proof.

We prove only (
8
). Proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
2
above, using (
3
) and (
5
), we deduce that

zc_{1}(x,z)-3x(1-z)b(x,z)=(36x^{2}-6x-2)zb(x,z)c_{1}(x,z).

Expanding both sides of the last equation as a power series in
z
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}C_{2n+1}(x)z^{n+1}-3x\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}(x)z^{n}+3x\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}(x)z^{n+1}

\displaystyle=(36x^{2}-6x-2)\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{k}(x)C_{2(n-k)+1}z^{n+1},

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}C_{2n-1}(x)z^{n}-3x\sum_{n\geq 1}B_{n}(x)z^{n}+3x\sum_{n\geq 1}B_{n-1}(x)z^{n}

\displaystyle=(36x^{2}-6x-2)\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}B_{k}(x)C_{2(n-k)-1}z^{n}.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides implies the stated formula.
∎

3
Balancing relations using exponential generating

functions

In this section, we will use the structure of the exponential generating functions to prove our results. In this case, we will use significantly the following lemma from
[
12
]
.

Lemma 4
.

The recurrence of type

u_{n}(x)=pu_{n-1}(x)+qu_{n-2}(x)
,
n\geq 2
,
where
p^{2}+4q\neq 0
and
u_{0}(x)
and
u_{1}(x)
are the initial terms, has the exponential generating function

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}u_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{e^{\frac{p}{2}z}}{\Delta}\Bigl((u_{1}(x)-\beta u_{0}(x))e^{\frac{\Delta}{2}z}-(u_{1}(x)-\alpha u_{0}(x))e^{-\frac{\Delta}{2}z}\Bigr),

while for odd and even indexed sequences

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}u_{2n+1}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{e^{\frac{p^{2}+2q}{2}z}}{p\Delta}\Bigl((u_{3}(x)-\sigma u_{1}(x))e^{\frac{p\Delta}{2}z}-(u_{3}(x)-\rho u_{0}(x))e^{-\frac{p\Delta}{2}z}\Bigr),

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}u_{2n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{e^{\frac{p^{2}+2q}{2}z}}{p\Delta}\Bigl((u_{2}(x)-\sigma u_{0}(x))e^{\frac{p\Delta}{2}z}-(u_{2}(x)-\rho u_{0}(x))e^{-\frac{p\Delta}{2}z}\Bigr),

where
\Delta=\sqrt{p^{2}+4q}
,
\alpha=\frac{p+\Delta}{2}
,
\beta=\frac{p-\Delta}{2}
,
\rho=\frac{p^{2}+2q+p\Delta}{2}
, and
\sigma=\frac{p^{2}+2q-p\Delta}{2}
.

From lemma above it can be shown fairly easily that the exponential generating functions of the balancing polynomials and their odd (even) indexed companions are given by

\displaystyle b^{*}(x,z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{e^{3xz}}{\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\sinh\!\big(\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\,z\big),

(9)

\displaystyle b^{*}_{1}(x,z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{2n+1}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{(18x^{2}-1)z}}{\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\left(3x\sinh\!\big(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\,z\big)+\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\cosh\!\big(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\,z\big)\right),

(10)

\displaystyle b^{*}_{2}(x,z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{2n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{e^{(18x^{2}-1)z}}{\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\sinh\!\big(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\,z\big).

(11)

In the similar manner, we obtain the exponential generating functions of the Lucas-balancing polynomial sequences:

\displaystyle c^{*}(x,z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}C_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=e^{3xz}\cosh\!\big(\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\,z\big),

(12)

\displaystyle c^{*}_{1}(x,z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}C_{2n+1}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e^{(18x^{2}-1)z}\left(3x\cosh\!\big(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\,z\big)+\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\sinh\!\big(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\,z\big)\right),

(13)

\displaystyle c^{*}_{2}(x,z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}C_{2n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=e^{(18x^{2}-1)z}\cosh\!\big(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\,z\big).

(14)

Next, we present some new relations between balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials involving binomial coefficients.

Theorem 5
.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{1+(-1)^{n-k}}{\bigl(\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\bigr)^{k-1}}B_{k}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{1-(-1)^{n-k}}{\bigl(\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\bigr)^{k}}C_{k}(x),

(15)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\!{n\choose k}\!\frac{\lambda(x)+(-1)^{n-k}\lambda^{-1}(x)}{\bigl(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\bigr)^{k-1}}B_{2k+1}(x)=6x\sum_{k=0}^{n}\!{n\choose k}\!\frac{\lambda(x)-(-1)^{n-k}\lambda^{-1}(x)}{\bigl(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\bigr)^{k}}C_{2k+1}(x),

(16)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{1+(-1)^{n-k}}{\bigl(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\bigr)^{k-1}}B_{2k}(x)=6x\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{1-(-1)^{n-k}}{\bigl(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\bigr)^{k}}C_{2k}(x),

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{1+(-1)^{n-k}}{\bigl(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\bigr)^{k-1}}B_{2k+1}(x)=6x\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{\lambda(x)-(-1)^{n-k}\lambda^{-1}(x)}{\bigl(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\bigr)^{k}}C_{2k}(x),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{\lambda(x)+(-1)^{n-k}\lambda^{-1}(x)}{\bigl(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\bigr)^{k-1}}B_{2k}(x)=6x\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{1-(-1)^{n-k}}{\bigl(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\bigr)^{k}}C_{2k+1}(x),

where
\lambda(x)=3x+\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}
.

Proof.

We will prove (
15
). In view of (
9
) and (
12
), we have

\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\cosh\!\big(\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z\big)\,b^{*}(x,z)=\sinh\!\big(\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z\big)\,c^{*}(x,z)

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\sum_{n\geq 0}\big(\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\big)^{n}\big(1+(-1)^{n}\big)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}C_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\sum_{n\geq 0}\big(\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\big)^{n}\big(1-(-1)^{n}\big)\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}B_{k}(x)\big(\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\big)^{n-k}\big(1+(-1)^{n-k}\big)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}C_{k}(x)\big(\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\big)^{n-k}\big(1-(-1)^{n-k}\big)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides yields (
15
).

The formulas (
16
) and (
17
) follow from the relations

\displaystyle\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\left(3x\cosh\!\big(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\big)+\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\sinh\!\big(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\big)\right)b_{1}^{*}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\left(3x\cosh\!\big(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\big)-\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\sinh\!\big(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\big)\right)c_{1}^{*}(x,z)

and

\displaystyle\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\cosh\!\big(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\big)\,b_{2}^{*}(x,z)=\sinh\!\big(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\big)\,c_{2}^{*}(x,z),

that one can obtain from (
10
), (
13
) and (
11
), (
14
), respectively. The proof of the other formulas is similar.
∎

In the next theorem we list a range of more advanced further relations for balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials that can be derived in a similar manner.

Theorem 6
.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\bigl(\lambda(x)+(-1)^{n-k}\lambda^{-1}(x)\bigr)\left(\frac{18x^{2}-1}{18x^{2}\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k-1}B_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{18x^{2}-1}{18x^{2}}\right)^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)\left(\frac{3x}{(18x^{2}-1)\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k}C_{2k+1}(x),

(18)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)\left(\frac{18x^{2}-1}{18x^{2}\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k-1}B_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{18x^{2}-1}{18x^{2}}\right)^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)\left(\frac{3x}{(18x^{2}-1)\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k}C_{2k}(x),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)\left(\frac{3x}{(18x^{2}-1)\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k-1}B_{2k+1}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{18x^{2}}{18x^{2}-1}\right)^{n-1}6x\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\bigl(\lambda(x)-(-1)^{n-k}\lambda^{-1}(x)\bigr)\left(\frac{18x^{2}-1}{18x^{2}\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k}C_{k}(x),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)\left(\frac{3x}{(18x^{2}-1)\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k-1}B_{2k}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{18x^{2}}{18x^{2}-1}\right)^{n-1}6x\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)\left(\frac{18x^{2}-1}{18x^{2}\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k}C_{k}(x),

where
\lambda(x)=3x+\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}
.

Proof.

Formula (
18
) follows from the functional relation

\displaystyle\Big(3x\cosh\!\big(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z\big)+\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\sinh\!\big(6x\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z\big)\Big)b^{*}\!\left(x,\frac{18x^{2}-1}{3x}z\right)

\displaystyle=\sinh\!\left(\frac{(18x^{2}-1)(\sqrt{9x^{2}-1})}{3x}z\right)c_{1}^{*}(x,z),

writing in terms of power series, and collecting terms.

The proof of the other formulas is similar. We omit the corresponding details.
∎

4
Chebyshev polynomial relations via balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials

As usual, Chebyshev polynomials
\big(T_{n}(x)\big)_{n\geq 0}
of the first kind and the Chebyshev polynomials
\big(U_{n}(x)\big)_{n\geq 0}
of the second kind can be defined by the recurrence
[
10
]

W_{n}(x)=2xW_{n-1}(x)-W_{n-2}(x),\quad n\geq 2,

(19)

but with different initial terms
T_{0}(x)=1
,
T_{1}(x)=x
and
U_{0}(x)=1
,
U_{1}(x)=2x
.

Many properties of Chebyshev polynomials can be obtained immediately from the following relations initially observed by the first author in
[
2
]
.

Lemma 7
.

For
n\geq 1
, the following relations hold:

B_{n}\!\left(\frac{x}{3}\right)=U_{n-1}(x),\quad C_{n}\!\left(\frac{x}{3}\right)=T_{n}(x).

(20)

Applying (
20
) to Theorems
2
and
3
yields the following Chebyshev polynomial relations.

Corollary 8
.

For
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle T_{2n-1}(x)-2(2x^{2}-x-1)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}U_{k-1}(x)T_{2n-2k-1}(x)=x(U_{n-1}(x)-U_{n-2}(x)),

\displaystyle T_{2n-2}(x)-2(2x^{2}-x-1)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}U_{k-1}(x)T_{2(n-k-1)}(x)=U_{n-1}(x)-(2x^{2}-1)U_{n-2}(x),

\displaystyle T_{n}(x)+T_{n-1}(x)+2(2x^{2}-x-1)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}U_{2k}(x)T_{n-k-1}(x)=U_{2n}(x)-xU_{2(n-1)}(x),

\displaystyle 2xT_{n-1}(x)+2(2x^{2}-x-1)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}U_{2k-1}(x)T_{n-k-1}(x)=U_{2n-1}(x)-xU_{2n-3}(x).

By virtue of (
20
), from Theorems
5
and
6
we have the following summation formulas involving Chebyshev polynomials and binomial coefficients.

Corollary 9
.

Let
\omega(x)=x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}
. Then for
n\geq 1
the following relations hold:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{U_{k-1}(x)}{\big(\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\big)^{k-1}}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{T_{k}(x)}{\big(\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\big)^{k}}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{U_{2k}(x)}{\big(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\big)^{k-1}}\bigl(\omega(x)+(-1)^{n-k}\omega^{-1}(x)\bigr)

\displaystyle=2x\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{T_{2k+1}(x)}{\big(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\big)^{k}}\bigl(\omega(x)-(-1)^{n-k}\omega^{-1}(x)\bigr),

\displaystyle\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{U_{2k-1}(x)}{\big(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\big)^{k}}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{T_{2k}(x)}{\big(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\big)^{k}}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}U_{k-1}(x)\left(\frac{2x^{2}-1}{2x^{2}\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k-1}\bigl(\omega(x)+(-1)^{n-k}\omega^{-1}(x)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2x^{2}-1}{2x^{2}}\right)^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}T_{2k+1}(x)\left(\frac{x}{(2x^{2}-1)\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}U_{k-1}(x)\left(\frac{2x^{2}-1}{2x^{2}\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k-1}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2x^{2}-1}{2x^{2}}\right)^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}T_{2k}(x)\left(\frac{x}{(2x^{2}-1)\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr),

\displaystyle\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}U_{2k}(x)\left(\frac{x}{(2x^{2}-1)\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2x^{2}}{2x^{2}-1}\right)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}T_{k}(x)\left(\frac{2x^{2}-1}{2x^{2}\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k}\bigl(\omega(x)-(-1)^{n-k}\omega^{-1}(x)\bigr),

\displaystyle\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{U_{2k}(x)}{\big(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\big)^{k}}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{T_{2k}(x)}{\big(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\big)^{k}}\bigl(\omega(x)-(-1)^{n-k}\omega^{-1}(x)\bigr),

\displaystyle(x^{2}-1)\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}U_{2k-1}(x)\left(\frac{x}{(2x^{2}-1)\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2x^{2}}{2x^{2}-1}\right)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}T_{k}(x)\left(\frac{2x^{2}-1}{2x^{2}\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{U_{2k-1}(x)}{\big(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\big)^{k-1}}\bigl(\omega(x)+(-1)^{n-k}\omega^{-1}(x)\bigr)

\displaystyle=2x\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{T_{k}(x)}{\big(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\big)^{k}}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr).

5
Fibonacci-Lucas identities via balancing polynomial relations

The balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials are closely related to the Fibonacci and Lucas numbers. Using this connection, in this section we obtain many Fibonacci-Lucas identities.

Let
F_{n}
denote the
n
-th Fibonacci number and
L_{n}
the
n
-th Lucas number, both satisfying the recurrence

u_{n}=u_{n-1}+u_{n-2},n\geq 2
, but with the respective initial terms
F_{0}=0
,
F_{1}=1
and
L_{0}=2
,
L_{1}=1
.

Lemma 11
.

For
n\geq 0
, the following relations hold:

\displaystyle B_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=F_{2n},\qquad C_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{L_{2n}}{2}.

(21)

Proof.

The first formula follows from (
1
) and the fact that sequence
(F_{2n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the recurrence
u_{n}=3u_{n-1}-u_{n-2}
,
n\geq 2
. The proof of the second formula is omitted.
∎

Using (
21
), from Theorems
2
and
3
we immediately can obtain the following summation Fibonacci-Lucas identities.

Corollary 12
.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle 3F_{2n-1}=L_{4n-2}-4\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}F_{2k}L_{4n-4k-2},

\displaystyle 2F_{2n-1}-5F_{2n-2}=L_{4n-4}-4\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}F_{2k}L_{4n-4k-4},

\displaystyle 2F_{4n+2}-3F_{4n-2}=L_{2n}+L_{2n-2}+4\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}F_{4k+2}L_{2n-2k-2},

\displaystyle 2F_{4n}-3F_{4n-4}=3L_{2n-2}+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}F_{4k}L_{2n-2k-2}.

Next, by (
21
), from Theorem
5
and

we obtain Fibonacci-Lucas identities involving binomial coefficients.

Corollary 13
.

Let
\alpha
be the golden ratio,
\alpha=(1+\sqrt{5})/2

\beta=(1-\sqrt{5})/2=-1/\alpha

\displaystyle\sqrt{5}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)F_{2k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)L_{2k},

\displaystyle\sqrt{5}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(\alpha^{2}+(-1)^{n-k}\beta^{2}\bigr)F_{4k+2}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(\alpha^{2}-(-1)^{n-k}\beta^{2}\bigr)L_{4k+2},

\displaystyle\sqrt{5}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)F_{4k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)L_{4k},

\displaystyle\sqrt{5}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{14}{9\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(\alpha^{2}+(-1)^{n-k}\beta^{2}\bigr)F_{2k}=\left(\frac{7}{9}\right)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{6}{7\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)L_{4k+2},

\displaystyle\sqrt{5}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{14}{9\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)F_{2k}=\left(\frac{7}{9}\right)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{6}{7\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)L_{4k},

\displaystyle\sqrt{5}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{6}{7\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)F_{4k+2}=\left(\frac{9}{7}\right)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{14}{9\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(\alpha^{2}-(-1)^{n-k}\beta^{2}\bigr)L_{2k},

\displaystyle\sqrt{5}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)F_{4k+2}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(\alpha^{2}-(-1)^{n-k}\beta^{2}\bigr)L_{4k},

\displaystyle\sqrt{5}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{6}{7\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)F_{4k}=\left(\frac{9}{7}\right)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{14}{9\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)L_{2k},

\displaystyle\sqrt{5}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(\alpha^{2}+(-1)^{n-k}\beta^{2}\bigr)F_{4k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\!\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt{5}}\right)^{k}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)L_{4k+2}.

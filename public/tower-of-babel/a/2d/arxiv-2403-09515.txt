Free groups are L 2 L^{2} -subgroup rigid
Andrei Jaikin-Zapirain

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the notion of L 2 L^{2} -subgroup rigid groups and demonstrate that free groups are L 2 L^{2} -subgroup rigid. As a consequence, we establish the equivalence between compressibility, inertness, strong inertness, and L 2 L^{2} -independence for a finitely generated subgroup of a free group, confirming a conjecture by Dicks and Ventura as well as the one by Antolin and Jaikin-Zapirain.



1.
Introduction

The study of finitely generated subgroups of a free group
is a classical topic in combinatorial group theory. The standard approach employs Stallings’ graph-theory techniques introduced in
[
Sta83
]
(see also
[
KM02
]
). However, it has been realized that these techniques have limitations in addressing problems related to the intersections of subgroups, as exemplified by the case of the Hanna Neumann Conjecture. The proofs of the conjecture by Friedman
[
Fri15
]
and Mineyev
[
Min12
]
, as well as a more recent proof by Jaikin-Zapirain
[
JZ17
]
use different algebraic tools. These ideas were further developed by Antolin and Jaikin-Zapirain
[
AJZ22
]
, where the notion of
L^{2}
-independence was introduced. This led to the proof of a generalized version of the Hanna Neumann conjecture for various families of groups
[
BK23
,
FM23
]
. In this paper, we introduce the notion of
L^{2}
-subgroup rigid group, providing another powerful tool for proving new results on subgroups of groups possessing this property.

Let
G
be group and let
\mathcal{U}(G)
be the ring of affiliated operators of
G

[
Lüc02
,
Kam19
]
. For a left
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-module
M
we denote

\tor_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}(M)=\bigcap\{\ker\phi\colon\phi:M\to\mathcal{U}(G)\textrm{\ is a $\mathbb{Q}[G]$-homomorphism}\}.

In the case where
M
is finitely generated,
\tor_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}(M)
can be defined as

\tor_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}(M)=\bigcap\{\ker\phi\colon\phi:M\to l^{2}(G)\textrm{\ is a $\mathbb{Q}[G]$-homomorphism}\}.

If
G
is torsion-free and
G
satisfies the strong Atiyah conjecture over
\mathbb{Q}
(which says that the division closure
\D(G)
of
\mathbb{Q}[G]
in
\mathcal{U}(G)
is a division ring) then

\tor_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}(M)=\bigcap\{\ker\phi\colon\phi:M\to\mathcal{D}(G)\textrm{\ is a $\mathbb{Q}[G]$-homomorphism}\},

and so,
\tor_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}(M)
is the kernel of the canonical map
M\to\D(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}M
.

We denote by
I_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}
the augmentation ideal of
\mathbb{Q}[G]
and if
H
is a subgroup of
G
we denote by
{}^{G}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}

the left ideal of
\mathbb{Q}[G]
generated by
I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}
. We say that
G
is
L^{2}
-subgroup rigid
if for any finitely generated subgroup
H
of
G
, there exists a subgroup
\widetilde{H}
of
G
containing
H
such that

\tor_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}/{}^{G}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})={}^{G}I_{\mathbb{Q}[\widetilde{H}]}/{}^{G}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}.

In this paper we will show that free groups are
L^{2}
-subgroup rigid.

Theorem 1.1
.

Free groups are
L^{2}
-subgroup rigid.

It is very plausible that our proof can be extended to show that not only free groups, but also other groups are
L^{2}
-subgroup rigid. We have chosen to explore this aspect in a forthcoming paper and, for simplicity, focus here only on the case of free groups. As we will see later, even in the case of free groups, we already can derive several important consequences.

A finitely generated subgroup
H
of a free group
F
is called
compressed
if for any subgroup
L
of
F
containing
H
,
\rk(H)\leq\rk(L)
, and it is called
inert
if for any finitely generated subgroup
L
of
F
the intersection
H\cap L
is compressed in
L
.

The concept of a subgroup being compressed is quite intuitive; it indicates that the subgroup is not contained in another subgroup of smaller rank. The concept of an inert subgroup was introduced in the work of Dicks and Ventura while they were studying equalizers in free groups
[
DV96
]
.

We also say that
H
is
L^{2}
-independent
in
F
if
the canonical map

\D(F)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}\to\D(F)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}

is injective.
It is clear that inert subgroups are compressed and Antolin and Jaikin-Zapirain
[
AJZ22
]
proved that
L^{2}
-independent subgroups are inert. On the other hand, Dicks and Ventura
[
DV96
, Problem 1]
(see also
[
KM
, Problem 19.102]
) asked whether compressed subgroups are also inert and Antolin and Jaikin-Zapirain
[
AJZ22
, Question 2]
suggested that compressed subgroups may always be
L^{2}
-independent. We confirm these two conjectures.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
F
be a free group and
H
a finitely generated subgroup. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

H
is compressed in
F
;

(2)

H
is inert in
F
;

(3)

H
is
L^{2}
-independent in
F
.

In Corollary
3.16
we will also show that these three properties of
H
are also equivalent of
H
being
strongly inert
in
F
(see Paragraph
2.6
for the definition).

It is clear that the intersection of two inert subgroups is inert and by
[
MVW07
, Theorem 3.9]
, it is algorithmically decidable, for a finitely generated subgroup of a free group, whether it is compressed. Thus, we obtain the following immediate consequences.

Corollary 1.3
.

The following holds.

(1)

[
KM
, Problem 19.103]
The intersection of two compressed subgroups of a free group is again compressed.

(2)

[
KM
, Problem 19.104]
It is algorithmically decidable, for a finitely generated subgroup of a free group, whether it is inert.

If
G
is a group and
H
is a finitely generated subgroup such that for some subgroup
\widetilde{H}
containing
H
,

\tor_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[G]}/{}^{G}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})={}^{G}I_{\mathbb{Q}[\widetilde{H}]}/{}^{G}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]},

then
\widetilde{H}
is unique and we call this subgroup the
L^{2}
-closure
of
H
in
G
.
If
\widetilde{H}=H
, we say that
H
is
L^{2}
-closed
in
G
.

A finitely generated subgroup
H
of a free group

is called
strictly compressed
in
F
if for any subgroup
L
of
F
, properly containing
H
,
\rk(H)<\rk(L)
and
strictly inert
if for any subgroup
L
of
F
,

is strictly compressed in

. Another consequence of Theorem
1.1
is the following equivalence.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
F
a free group and
H
a finitely generated subgroup of
F
. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

H
is strictly compressed in
F
;

(2)

H
is strictly inert in
F
;

(3)

H
is
L^{2}
-closed in
F
.

We also obtain the following surprising property of the lattice of finitely generated subgroups of free groups.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
F
be a free group and
H
a finitely generated subgroup. Define

\overline{\pi}(H\leq F)=\min\{\rk(L)\colon H\leq L\leq F\}\textrm{\ and\ }\\
\Crit(H\leq F)=\{H\leq L\leq F:\rk(L)=\overline{\pi}(H\leq F)\}.

If
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
belong to
\Crit(H\leq F)
, then
L_{1}\cap L_{2}
and
\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle
also belong to
\Crit(H\leq F)
.

We will see that the maximal subgroup in

is the

-closure of

.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we describe preliminary results. The main step of our proof is Section

, where we prove an analogue of Theorem

in characteristic

2.2.
Left ideals associated with subgroups

We have defined in the Introduction the left ideal
{}^{G}I_{k[H]}
of
k[G]
.
It determines uniquely the subgroup
H
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H
and
T
be subgroups of a group
G
and
k
a commutative ring. If
{}^{G}I_{k[H]}\leq{}^{G}I_{k[T]}
, then
H\leq T
. In particular, if
{}^{G}I_{k[H]}={}^{G}I_{k[T]}
, then
H=T
.

Proof.

For any
g\in G
, we put
\overline{g}=gH\in G/H
. It is clear that
\Stab_{G}(\overline{1})=H
.
The left action of
G
on
G/H
induces a structure of left
k[G]
-module on
k[G/H]
.

Since the trivial
k[H]
-module

is isomorphic to
k[H]/I_{k[H]}
,

k[G]/{}^{G}I_{k[H]}\cong k[G]\otimes_{K[H]}k\cong k[G/H].

Moreover, the isomorphism is realized by the map
k[G]/{}^{G}I_{k[H]}\to k[G/H]
, that sends
1+{}^{G}I_{k[H]}
to
\overline{1}
. In particular,

\Stab_{G}(1+{}^{G}I_{k[H]})=\Stab_{G}(\overline{1})=H.

Thus, if
{}^{G}I_{k[H]}\leq{}^{G}I_{k[T]}
, then
H=\Stab_{G}(1+{}^{G}I_{k[H]})\leq\Stab_{G}(1+{}^{G}I_{k[T]})=T.

∎

The following lemma gives an alternative description of the the left

.

2.4.
Universal division ring of fractions of group rings

Let

be a commutative domain and

of
{k[G]}

(P1)

For every division

\dim_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{D}\otimes_{k[G]}M\leq\dim_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{E}\otimes_{k[G]}M.

-ring of fractions
. The definition implies that it is uniquely defined up to

]

.
We define.

\beta_{i}^{k[G]}(M)=\dim_{\mathcal{D}_{k[G]}}\Tor_{i}(\mathcal{D}_{k[G]},M).

(P2)

\D_{k[G]}

{k[G]}
-module

.

(P3)

For any left
{k[G]}

.

(P4)

The division

for any subgroup

of

\D_{H}\otimes_{k[H]}k[G]\to\D_{k[G]},\ d\otimes a\mapsto da\ (d\in\D_{H},a\in k[H]),

is injective.

If

is a field or

and

We will need the following consequence of Property (P1) for

-module, then for every

(1)

.

\beta_{i}^{K_{2}[F]}(K_{2}\otimes_{K_{1}}M)=\beta_{i}^{K_{1}[F]}(M).

2.5.
The Euler characteristic

A

-module

\mathtt{FL}
(resp.

For a

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R^{n_{k}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}

If

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

\mathtt{FL}

Proof.

The

is defined as an abelian group generated by
\{[P]\colon P

with the relations
[P_{1}]+[P_{2}]=[P_{3}]

\widetilde{K}_{0}(R)=K_{0}(R)/\langle[R]\rangle

By

For any

We note that, by Schanuel’s lemma

Secondly, we observe that

For the induction step, suppose that

Thus, we can apply the inductive hypothesis to the short exact sequence

\chi_{u}^{R}(M_{1})=\chi_{u}^{R}(Q_{3})-\chi_{u}^{R}(Q_{2})=\chi_{u}^{R}(M_{2})-\chi_{u}^{R}(M_{3}).\qed

2.6.
\D_{K[F]}
-independent modules

Let
F
be a free group and
K
a field.
By analogy with
L^{2}
-independence, we also say that a subgroup
H
is
\D_{K[F]}
-independent
in
F
if the canonical map

\D_{K[F]}\otimes_{K[H]}I_{K[H]}\to\D_{K[F]}\otimes_{K[F]}I_{K[F]}

is injective. This is equivalent to the condition
\beta_{1}^{K[F]}(I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]})=0
. Observe that, since
\mathcal{D}(F)
is isomorphic as a
K[F]
-ring to
\D_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}
,

\D_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}
-independence is the same as
L^{2}
-independence defined before.

If
H
is a finitely generated subgroup of a free group
F
, we say that
H
is
strongly inert
in
F
if for any finitely generated subgroup
U
of
F
we have that

\sum_{x\in U\backslash F/H}\overline{\rk}(U\cap xHx^{-1})\leq\overline{\rk}(U).

This notion has been itroduced by Ivanov
[
Iva18
]
. It is clear that a strongly inert subgroup of a free group is also inert.
The proof of the following proposition is a small modification of the proof of
[
AJZ22
, Proposition 5.2]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
H
be a finitely generated subgroup of a finitely generated free group
F
. Assume that
H
is
\D_{K[F]}
-independent in
F
. Then
H
is strongly inert in
F
.

Proof.

Let
U
be a finitely generated subgroup of
F
. Since
H
is
\D_{K[F]}
-independent in
F

\Tor_{1}^{K[F]}(\D_{K[F]},I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]})=0.

By Property (P4), the right
K[F]
-module
\D_{K[U]}\otimes_{K[U]}K[F]
naturally embeds into
\D_{K[F]}
. Hence, by Lemma
2.3
, we also have

\Tor_{1}^{K[F]}(\D_{K[U]}\otimes_{K[U]}K[F],I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]})=0.

Thus, by Shapiro’s Lemma

(3)

\Tor_{1}^{K[U]}(\D_{K[U]},I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]})=\\
\Tor_{1}^{K[F]}(\D_{K[U]}\otimes_{K[U]}K[F],I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]})=0.

As we have shown in the proof of Lemma
2.1
, the left
K[F]
-modules
K[F]/{}^{F}I_{K[H]}
and
K[F/H]
are isomorphic.
Thus, we have the following isomorphism of left
K[U]
-modules.

K[F]/{}^{F}I_{K[H]}\cong K[F/H]\cong\bigoplus_{x\in U\backslash F/H}K[U/(U\cap xHx^{-1})].

Since,
\overline{\rk}(U\cap xHx^{-1})=\dim_{\D_{K[U]}}\Tor_{1}^{K[U]}(\D_{K[U]},K[U/(U\cap xHx^{-1})])
, we obtain that

\sum_{x\in U\backslash F/H}\overline{\rk}(U\cap xHx^{-1})=\dim_{\D_{K[U]}}\Tor_{1}^{K[U]}(\D_{K[U]},K[F]/{}^{F}I_{K[H]}).

From the exact sequence of the left
K[U]
-modules

0\to I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]}\to K[F]/{}^{F}I_{K[H]}\to K\to 0,

we obtain that

\dim_{\D_{K[U]}}\Tor_{1}^{K[U]}(\D_{K[U]},K[F]/{}^{F}I_{K[H]})\leq\\
\dim_{\D_{K[U]}}\Tor_{1}^{K[U]}(\D_{K[U]},I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]})+\dim_{\D_{K[U]}}\Tor_{1}^{K[U]}(\D_{K[U]},K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by (\ref{zerotor1})}}}{{=}}\\
\dim_{\D_{K[U]}}\Tor_{1}^{K[U]}(\D_{K[U]},K)=\overline{\rk}(U).

This gives the desired inequality:

\sum_{x\in U\backslash F/H}\overline{\rk}(U\cap xHx^{-1})\leq\overline{\rk}(U).\qed

Now, assume that

then there exists an exact sequence

We want to show that

\tor_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})={}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[\widetilde{H}]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}.

This will follows from the following two claims.

Claim 3.13
.

We have that
\beta_{0}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}({}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[\widetilde{H}]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})=0
. In particular,

{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[\widetilde{H}]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}\leq\tor_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}).

Proof.

Consider the left
\mathbb{Z}[F]
-module
N={}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Z}[\widetilde{H}]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Z}[H]}
.
Then by (
1
), we have

\beta_{0}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N)\leqslant\beta_{0}^{\mathbb{F}_{2}[F]}(\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by (\ref{Euler=tor1})}}}{{=}}0.\qed

Claim 3.14
.

The left
\mathbb{Q}[F]
-module
I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[\widetilde{H}]}
is
\D_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}
-torsion-free. In particular,

\tor_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})\leq{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[\widetilde{H}]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}.

Proof.

Put
M=I_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Z}[\widetilde{H}]}
.
We have the following exact sequence of left
\mathbb{Z}[F]
-modules

0\to\tor_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}(M)\to M\to M/\tor_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}(M)\to 0.

Since all three module are
\mathbb{Z}
-torsion-free, the sequence induces the following exact sequence of left
\mathbb{F}_{2}[F]
-modules.

0\to\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\tor_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}(M)\to\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M\to\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(M/\tor_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}(M))\to 0.

By Corollary
3.4
, and since,
\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\tor_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}(M)\cong\tor_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M)
, we have that

\chi^{\mathbb{F}_{2}[F]}(\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\tor_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}(M))=\chi^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\tor_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}(M))=\chi^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(\tor_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M)).

Observe that by Proposition
3.5
,

\chi^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(\tor_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M))\leqslant 0.

Hence
\chi^{\mathbb{F}_{2}[F]}(\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\tor_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}(M))\leqslant 0
. Since
\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\tor_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}(M)
is a submodule of the left
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{2}[F]}
-torsion-free module
\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M
,
\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\tor_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}(M)
is also
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{2}[F]}
-torsion-free. By Proposition
2.7
,
\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\tor_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}(M)=0
. However,
\tor_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}(M)
is a subgroup of a free abelian group
M
. Hence
\tor_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}(M)
is also free abelian, and so
\tor_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}(M)=0
. Hence
\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}M
is
\D_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}
-torsion-free.
∎

3.5.
Proof of Corollary
1.2

Let
F
be a free group and
H
a finitely generated subgroup. In the Introduction we have defined

\overline{\pi}(H\leq F)=\min\{\rk(L)\colon H\leq L\leq F\}.

We obtain
Corollary
1.2
as a consequence of the following result.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic 0 or 2,
F
a free group and
H
a finitely generated subgroup of
F
. Then

(8)

\overline{\pi}(H\leq F)=\rk(H)-\beta_{1}^{K[F]}(I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]}).

Moreover, if
\widetilde{H}
is the
\D_{K[F]}
-closure of
H
, then
\rk(\widetilde{H})=\overline{\pi}(H\leq F)
.

Proof.

First observe that if
P
is the prime subfield of a field
K
, then, by Proposition
2.5
,

\beta_{1}^{K[F]}(I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]})=\beta_{1}^{P[F]}(I_{P[F]}/{}^{F}I_{P[H]}).

Thus, it is enough to show the theorem in the case where
K
is a prime field.

Notice that for a finitely generated subgroup
L
of
F
containing
H
, we have that

\rk(L)=\rk(F)-\chi^{K[F]}(I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[L]})=\\
\rk(F)-\beta_{0}^{K[F]}(I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[L]})+\beta_{1}^{K[F]}(I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[L]})\geqslant\\
\rk(F)-\beta_{0}^{K[F]}(I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[L]})\geqslant\rk(F)-\beta_{0}^{K[F]}(I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]}),

and so,

(9)

\overline{\pi}(H\leq F)\geqslant\rk(F)-\beta_{0}^{K[F]}(I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]})=\\
\chi^{K[F]}(I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]})+\rk(H)-\beta_{0}^{K[F]}(I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]})=\\
\rk(H)-\beta_{1}^{K[F]}(I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]}).

This implies one direction in the equality (
8
).

Now we prove the result for
K=\mathbb{Q}
, and so, when
K
is a field of characteristic 0. A similar argument gives the theorem for fields of characteristic 2.

Let
\widetilde{H}
be the
L^{2}
-closure of
H
in
F
. Then

(10)

\overline{\pi}(H\leq F)\leq\rk(\widetilde{H})=\rk(F)-\chi^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[\widetilde{H}]})=\\
\rk(F)-\beta_{0}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[\widetilde{H}]})=\rk(F)-\beta_{0}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})=\\
\rk(H)-\beta_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}).\qed

In the case of fields
K
of characteristic
p\neq 0,2
we do not know that free groups are
\D_{K[F]}
-subgroup rigid, and, therefore, the previous proof cannot applied in these cases.

The following corollary is a strong version of Corollary
1.2
.

Corollary 3.16
.

Let
F
be a free group and
H
a finitely generated subgroup. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

H
is compressed in
F
;

(2)

H
is inert in
F
;

(3)

H
is strongly inert in
F
;

(4)

H
is
L^{2}
-independent in
F
;

(5)

for any field
K
of characteristic
0
or
2
,
H
is
\D_{K[F]}
-independent in
F
.

Proof.

The implications (5)
\Rightarrow
(4)
\Rightarrow
(3)
\Rightarrow
(2)
\Rightarrow
(1) have been discussed before.

We will prove now (1)
\Rightarrow
(5). Let
K
be a field of characteristic 0 or 2. If
H
is compressed, then
\overline{\pi}(H\leq F)=\rk(H)
. By Theorem
3.15
,
\beta_{1}^{K[F]}(I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]})=0
. Hence
H
is
\D_{K[F]}
-independent in
F
.
∎

3.6.
Proof of Corollary
1.4

Let
K
be a field and
H
a subgroup of a free group
F
. We say that
H
is
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{2}[F]}
-closed
if

\tor_{K[F]}(I_{K[F]}/{}^{F}I_{K[H]}=\{0\}
.
In this section we will show the following strong version of Corollary
1.4
.

Corollary 3.17
.

Let
F
a free group and
H
a finitely generated subgroup of
F
. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

H
is strictly compressed in
F
;

(2)

H
is strictly inert in
F
;

(3)

H
is
L^{2}
-closed in
F
;

(4)

for any field
K
of characteristic 0 or 2,
H
is
\D_{K[F]}
-closed.

Proof.

The implications (2)
\Rightarrow
(1) and (4)
\Rightarrow
(3) are clear.

Let us show first (3)
\Rightarrow
(2).
Let
H
be a
\D_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}
-closed subgroup if
F
and
U
an arbitrary subgroup of
F
.
Observe that since the left
\mathbb{Q}[F]
-module
I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}
is
\D_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}
-torsion-free, it is also
\D_{\mathbb{Q}[U]}
-torsion-free as a left
\mathbb{Q}[U]
-module.
Thus, since the left
\mathbb{Q}[U]
-module
I_{\mathbb{Q}[U]}/{}^{U}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H\cap U]}
is a submodule of
I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}
,
I_{\mathbb{Q}[U]}/{}^{U}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H\cap U]}
is also
\D_{\mathbb{Q}[U]}
-torsion-free.

Thus, in order to show (3)
\Rightarrow
(2), it is enough to prove (3)
\Rightarrow
(1). Let
L
be a finitely generated subgroup of
F
properly containing
H
. Then

\rk(L)=\rk(F)-\chi^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[L]})=\\
\rk(F)-\chi^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})+\chi^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}({}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[L]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})=\\
\rk(H)+\chi^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}({}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[L]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by Proposition \ref{positiveEuler}}}}{{>}}\rk(H).

Now let us show (1)
\Rightarrow
(4). Let
H
be a strictly compressed subgroup of
F
. Let
\widetilde{H}
be its
\D_{K[F]}
-closure (which does not depend on the field
K
). If
H\neq\widetilde{H}
, then we should have
\rk(\widetilde{H})>\rk(H)
. But this contradicts Theorem
3.15
. Hence
H=\widetilde{H}
.
∎

3.7.
Proof of Corollary
1.5

By Corollary
3.16
,
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are
L^{2}
-independent in
F
. In particular they are inert. Hence
\rk(L_{1}\cap L_{2})\leq\rk(L_{1})
, and so,
L_{1}\cap L_{2}\in\Crit(H\leq F)
.

On the other hand, if
L\in\Crit(H\leq F)
, then

\beta_{0}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}({}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[L]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})=\chi^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}({}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[L]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})+\beta_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}({}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[L]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lemma \ref{0tor1}}}}{{\leqslant}}\\
\rk(L)-\rk(H)+\beta_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by Theorem \ref{pibar}}}}{{=}}0.

Thus, if
L=\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle
, then

\beta_{0}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}({}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[L]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})\leq\beta_{0}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}({}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[L_{1}]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})+\beta_{0}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}({}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[L_{2}]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})=0.

and

(11)

\beta_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}({}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[L]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lemma \ref{0tor1}}}}{{\geqslant}}\beta_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}({}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[L_{1}]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})=\beta_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]}).

Therefore,

\rk(L)=\rk(H)+\chi^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}({}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[L]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})=\\
\rk(H)-\beta_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}({}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[L]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by Theorem \ref{pibar}}}}{{=}}\\
\overline{\pi}(H\leq F)+\beta_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})-\beta_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}({}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[L]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by (\ref{LH})}}}{{\leqslant}}\overline{\pi}(H\leq F).

Hence
L\in\Crit(H\leq F)
.
∎

4.
Final comments

The paper raises several questions, which we will gather in this section.
Our proof of
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{2}[F]}
-subgroup rigidity of a free group
F
depends on a peculiarity of the left
\mathbb{F}_{2}[F]
-module
I_{\mathbb{F}_{2}[F]}
(Lemma
3.9
). However, we strongly believe that there are other methods that work for all primes.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
F
be a free group and
p
an odd prime. Then
F
is
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[F]}
-subgroup rigid.

The previous problem can be divided in two subproblems.

Conjecture 2
.

Let
F
be a free group and
p
an odd prime. Let
H
be finitely generated subgroup of
F
and
\widetilde{H}
its
L^{2}
-closure. Then

(1)

\beta_{0}^{\mathbb{F}_{p}[F]}(I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]})=\beta_{0}^{\mathbb{Q}[F]}(I_{\mathbb{Q}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{Q}[H]})
and

(2)

the left
\mathbb{F}_{p}[F]
-module
I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[F]}/{}^{F}I_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\widetilde{H}]}
is
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[F]}
-torsion-free.

The invariant
\overline{\pi}(H\leq F)
is related with the
primitivity rank of
H
in
F
introduced by Puder and Parzanchevski
[
PP15
]
:

\pi(H\leq F)=\min\{\rk(L):H\lneq L\leq F,\ H\textrm{\ is not a free factor of\ }L\}.

It is clear that

\overline{\pi}(H\leq F)=\min\{\pi(H\leq F),\rk(H)\}.

By analogy, if
K
is a field, we can define

\pi_{K}(H\leq F)=\min\{\rk(N):{}^{F}I_{K[H]}\lneq N\leq I_{K[F]},\ {}^{F}I_{K[H]}\textrm{\ is not a free summand of\ }N\}.

The following conjecture is a variation of
[
EWPS24
, Conjecture 1.9]
.

Conjecture 3
.

Let
F
be a free group and
H
a finitely generated subgroup. Then for any field
K
,
\pi_{K}(H\leq F)=\pi(H\leq F)
.

As mentioned in the Introduction, we anticipate that our methods can be extended to establish
L^{2}
-subgroup rigidity not only for free groups. To provide a specific focus, we put forth the following conjecture.

Conjecture 4
.

Locally indicable groups are
L^{2}
-subgroup rigid.

On the other hand we expect that there are non
L^{2}
-subgroup rigid groups.

Problem 5
.

Construct non
L^{2}
-subgroup rigid (torsion-free) groups.

A short memory condition for infinitely divisible random fields
Vitalii Makogin, Evgeny Spodarev

Abstract
This small note yields a sufficient condition for the short range dependence of measurable stationary infinitely divisible moving average random fields with d d –dimensional index space. Here, the short/long range dependence concept in borrowed from the paper [ 2 ] . In the special case of symmetric stable moving averages, our new condition coincides with the one from paper [ 3 ] .



Keywords
: Stationary random field, moving average, infinitely divisible, integral spectral representation,
\alpha
–stable, time series, heavy tails.

AMS subject classification 2020
: Primary 60G60; Secondary 60E07, 60E10

1
Memory of random fields and their level sets

A random field
X=\{X(t),\;t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
is called
infinitely divisible
(ID, for short) if all its finite dimensional distributions are
infinitely divisible, see e.g.
[
11
,
9
]
for an introduction into infinite divisibility.
X
is said to be
stationary
if all its finite dimensional distributions are translation invariant (with respect to space variable
t
). In this note, we give a sufficient condition for stationary ID
X
to have a short memory. Many definitions of the memory of random functions are available in research literature, confer e.g.
[
1
,
7
]
. However, the only short/long range dependence concept which is invariant with respect to monotone transformations on the marginals of the field
X
has been recently introduced in
[
2
]
. It depends on the level sets
\{t:X(t)>u\}
of
X
in the following way:

Definition 1
.

A measurable stationary random field
X
is called short range dependent (SRD) if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbf{Cov}(\mathbbm{1}\{X(t)>u\},\mathbbm{1}\{X(0)>v\})\right|\,\mu(du)\mu(dv)\,dt<\infty

for all probability measures
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
. If the above integral is infinite for some choice of
\mu
the field
X
is named long range dependent (LRD).

In addition, the above definition can be applied to heavy–tailed fields
X
since it does not require the integrability of
X(0)
.
The target statistic from Definition
1
is the volume of level sets of
X
. In multivariate extreme value theory, it appears naturally e.g. in the study of the asymptotic behaviour of estimates of the tail dependence coefficient, cf. the recent paper
[
4
]
. A statistical procedure for the detection of this kind of SRD/LRD is discussed in
[
5
]
.

In paper
[
3
]
, implications of Definition
1
for
\alpha
– and max–stable processes are shown.
There, necessary and/or sufficient conditions of SRD/LRD are given in terms of the kernel function
f
in the spectral decomposition of
X
.

Here, we prove a similar sufficient condition for stationary ID random fields
X
which possess a moving average integral representation.
The paper is organized as follows: preliminaries on ID fields are given in the next section. The main SRD condition is formulated in Theorem
1
of Section
3
. An example of symmetric stable moving averages is considered there as well. Finally, the proof of Theorem
1
(subdivided into a number of auxiliary results) is given in Section
4
.

2
Infinitely divisible random fields

Let
\Lambda
be a stationary ID independently scattered random measure with Lebesgue control measure and Lévy characteristics
(a_{0},b_{0},\nu_{0}),
where
a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
b_{0}\geq 0
and
\nu_{0}
is a Lévy measure. In the sequel, we follow the exposition of ID random functions in
[
6
]
:

Definition 2
.

A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
\Lambda
-integrable if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\left|a_{0}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{[-1,1]}(yf(x))-\mathbbm{1}_{[-1,1]}(y)\right)y\nu_{0}(dy)\right|dx<\infty,

b_{0}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{2}(x)dx<\infty,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\min\left(1,y^{2}f^{2}(x)\right)\nu_{0}(dy)dx<\infty.

For
b_{0}>0
,
\Lambda
possesses a non–trivial Gaussian part (in addition to the jump part controlled by the Lévy measure
\nu_{0}
).

Let
X=\{X(t),t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
be a real–valued measurable stationary ID moving average random field with spectral representation

X(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t-x)\Lambda(dx),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1)

where
f
is
\Lambda
-integrable.

The characteristic function
\varphi_{X(0)}
of
X(0)
is given by

\varphi_{X(0)}(u)=\exp\left(-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(uf(s))ds\right),

where

K(s)=-isa_{0}+\frac{1}{2}s^{2}b_{0}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{isy}-1-isy\mathbb{I}_{[-1,1]}(y)\right)\nu_{0}(dy),\quad s\in\mathbb{R}.

Functions
K
and
\mathrm{Re}\{K\}
are known to be continuous negative definite functions. Obviously,
\mathrm{Re}\{K(s)\}=\mathrm{Re}\{K(|s|)\}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathrm{Re}\{K(s)\}=\psi(s^{2}),s\geq 0,
where
\psi
is a
Bernstein function
, see
[
10
]
for its definition and properties.

Due to the linearity of ID stochastic integrals, random variable

s_{1}X(t)+s_{2}X(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(s_{1}f(t-x)+s_{2}f(-x)\right)\Lambda(dx)

is ID with characteristic function

\varphi_{t}(s_{1},s_{2})=\exp\left(-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(s_{1}f(t-x)+s_{2}f(-x))\,dx\right),\quad(s_{1},s_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

3
Main result

Let
|\cdot|
be the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Denote by
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the space of all functions

f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
|f|^{p}
is Lebesgue–integrable,
p>0
. Let
a\wedge b
denote the minimum of
a
and
b
.

Introduce

\sigma_{f}^{2}(s):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{Re}\{K(sf(-x))\}\,dx,\quad s\in\mathbb{R},

\rho_{t}(s_{1},s_{2}):=\frac{1}{\sigma_{f}(s_{1})\sigma_{f}(s_{2})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x))\}\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{2}f(-x))\}}\,dx

for
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Note that
0\leq\rho_{t}(s_{1},s_{2})\leq 1
for all
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In addition, set

\tilde{\rho}_{t}:=\sup_{(s_{1},s_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho_{t}(s_{1},s_{2})

and
A_{\tilde{\rho}}=\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\tilde{\rho}_{t}\leq\tilde{\rho}\}
for a fixed
\tilde{\rho}\in(0,1)
.
Let
A_{\tilde{\rho}}^{c}=\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\tilde{\rho}_{t}>\tilde{\rho}\}
be the compliment of
A_{\tilde{\rho}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X
be a measurable stationary ID moving average random field (
1
).
Assume that there exists
\tilde{\rho}<1
such that
|A^{c}_{\tilde{\rho}}|<\infty
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{\sigma_{f}(s)}{s}e^{-(1-\tilde{\rho})\sigma_{f}^{2}(s)}ds<\infty.

(2)

Then,
X
is SRD if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}_{t}dt<\infty.

The proof of this result including several auxiliary lemmata will be given in the next section. Notice that
\sigma_{f}^{2}(s)=-\log|\varphi_{X(0)}(s)|.

Then the integral within (
2
) rewrites as

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{|\log|\varphi_{X(0)}(s)||^{1/2}}{s}|\varphi_{X(0)}(s)|^{1-\tilde{\rho}}ds.

Now let us explain how to check the above condition (
2
) in the special case of an
\alpha
–stable symmetric integrator measure
\Lambda
, cf. the book
[
8
]
:

Example 1

(Stable laws)
.

Let
X=\{X(t),t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
be a measurable stationary symmetric
\alpha-
stable moving average random field (
1
) with
\alpha\in(0,2)
and a continuous bounded kernel
f\in L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Then

\sigma_{f}^{2}(s)=|s|^{\alpha}\|f\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha},\quad s\in\mathbb{R},

where
\|f\|_{\alpha}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{\alpha}\,dx\right)^{1/\alpha}
,

\displaystyle\rho_{t}(s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|s_{1}|^{\alpha/2}|s_{2}|^{\alpha/2}\|f\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sqrt{|s_{1}|^{\alpha}|s_{2}|^{\alpha}|f(t-x)f(-x)|^{\alpha}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|f\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(t-x)f(-x)|^{\alpha/2}\,dx=\tilde{\rho}_{t},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Moreover, if
f
is a continuous bounded function then
\tilde{\rho}_{t}
is a continuous function at the origin as well (by Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem). Hence, there exists
\tilde{\rho}<1
such that
|A_{\tilde{\rho}}^{c}|<\infty
(the set
A_{\tilde{\rho}}^{c}
is a neighbourhood of the origin).
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{\sigma_{f}(s)}{s}e^{-(1-\tilde{\rho})\sigma_{f}^{2}(s)}ds

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}s^{\alpha/2-1}e^{-(1-\tilde{\rho})s^{\alpha}\Sigma_{f}^{2}}ds<\infty,\quad\alpha\in(0,2).

Hence, the assumptions of Theorem
1
hold true. The SRD condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}_{t}dt<\infty

reads (by Fubini theorem) as
f\in L^{\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which coincides with
[
3
, Theorem 3.4]
.

4
Proof of Theorem
1

Notice that by
[
10
, Theorem 4.12]

the function

1-e^{itx}-itx\mathbb{I}_{[-1,1]}(x),\;t\in\mathbb{R}

is negative definite for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
. We formulate the following

Lemma 1

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
be a continuous negative definite function. Then for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
it holds

•

\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x)\}\geq 0,

\psi(x)=\overline{\psi(-x)}
,

•

\left|\psi(x)+\psi(y)-\psi(x\pm y)\right|\leq 2\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x)\}}\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(y)\}},

•

\left|\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x)\}+\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x)\}-\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x\pm y)\}\right|\leq 2\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x)\}}\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(y)\}}.

The next inequality is a corollary of the above statements:

Lemma 2
.

For any continuous negative definite function
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
it holds

\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x+y)\}\wedge(\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x)+\psi(y)\})\geq\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x)\}}-\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(y)\}}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x+y)\}\wedge(\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x)+\psi(y)\})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x)+\psi(y)\}-(\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x)\}+\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(y)\}-\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x+y)\})_{+}

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x)+\psi(y)\}-2\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(x)\}}\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{\psi(y)\}}.

∎

The following evident bound will be used in the sequel: for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}

\left|e^{-z_{1}}-e^{-z_{2}}\right|\leq e^{-(\mathrm{Re}\{z_{1}\}\wedge\mathrm{Re}\{z_{2}\})}|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

(3)

Lemma 3
.

For all
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
it holds

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{E}e^{i\left(s_{1}X(t)+s_{2}X(0)\right)}-\mathbf{E}e^{is_{1}X(t)}\mathbf{E}e^{is_{2}X(0)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left[-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x))\}}-\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{2}f(-x))\}}\right)^{2}dx\right]

\displaystyle\times 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x))\}}\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{2}f(-x))\}}dx.

Proof.

Employing inequality (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{E}e^{i\left(s_{1}X(t)+s_{2}X(0)\right)}-\mathbf{E}e^{is_{1}X(t)}\mathbf{E}e^{is_{2}X(0)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\exp\left[-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K\left(s_{1}f(t-x)+s_{2}f(-x)\right)dx\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\exp\left[-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(K(s_{1}f(t-x))+K(s_{2}f(-x))\right)dx\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left[-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x)+s_{2}f(-x))\}dx\right.

\displaystyle\left.\wedge\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x))\}+\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{2}f(-x))\}\right)dx\right]

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|K(s_{1}f(t-x)+s_{2}f(-x))-K(s_{1}f(t-x))-K(s_{2}f(-x))\right|dx.

(4)

Recall that
\mathrm{Re}\{K\}\geq 0.
Apply Lemma
2
to

\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x)+s_{2}f(-x))\}\wedge\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x))+K(s_{2}f(-x))\}

to get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x)+s_{2}f(-x))\}\wedge\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x))+K(s_{2}f(-x))\}\right]dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x))\}}-\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{2}f(-x))\}}\right)^{2}dx,

Then the right–hand side of (
4
) is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x))\}}-\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{2}f(-x))\}}\right)^{2}dx\right]

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|K(s_{1}f(t-x)+s_{2}f(-x))-K(s_{1}f(t-x))-K(s_{2}f(-x))\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left[-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x))\}}-\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{2}f(-x))\}}\right)^{2}dx\right]

\displaystyle\times 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x))\}}\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{2}f(-x))\}}dx,

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
1
.

∎

Finally, Theorem
1
follows immediately from the lemma below:

Lemma 4
.

Let
\tilde{\rho}<1
such that
|A_{\tilde{\rho}}|>0.
Then for any
t\in A_{\tilde{\rho}}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbf{Cov}(\mathbbm{1}\{X(t)>u\},\mathbbm{1}\{X(0)>v\})\right|\mu(du)\mu(dv)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{\sigma_{f}(s)}{s}e^{-(1-\tilde{\rho})\sigma_{f}^{2}(s)}ds\right)^{2}\tilde{\rho_{t}}.

(5)

Proof.

By
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{Cov}(\mathbbm{1}\{X(t)>u\},\mathbbm{1}\{X(0)>v\})\mu(du)\mu(dv)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\varphi_{(X(0),X(t))}(s_{1},s_{2})-\varphi_{X(0)}(s_{1})\varphi_{X(0)}(s_{2})}{-s_{1}s_{2}}\hat{\mu}(s_{1})\hat{\mu}(s_{2})ds_{1}ds_{2},

where
\hat{\mu}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{isx}\mu(dx)
is the characteristic function of a probability measure
\mu
satisfying the inequality
|\hat{\mu}(s)|\leq 1
for all real
s
.
Applying Lemma
3
to the right–hand side of

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbf{Cov}(\mathbbm{1}\{X(t)>u\},\mathbbm{1}\{X(0)>v\})\right|\mu(du)\mu(dv)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\left|\varphi_{(X(0),X(t))}(s_{1},s_{2})-\varphi_{X(0)}(s_{1})\varphi_{X(0)}(s_{2})\right|}{|s_{1}s_{2}|}ds_{1}ds_{2},

we get that

4\pi^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbf{Cov}(\mathbbm{1}\{X(t)>u\},\mathbbm{1}\{X(0)>v\})\right|\mu(du)\mu(dv)

is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\exp\left\{-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x))\}}-\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{2}f(-x))\}}\right)^{2}dx\right\}}{|s_{1}||s_{2}|}

\displaystyle\times 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{1}f(t-x))\}}\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\{K(s_{2}f(-x))\}}\,dxds_{1}ds_{2}

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\exp\left\{-\sigma_{f}^{2}(s_{1})-\sigma_{f}^{2}(s_{2})+2\sigma_{f}(s_{1})\sigma_{f}(s_{2})\rho_{t}(s_{1},s_{2})\right\}\frac{\sigma_{f}(s_{1})\sigma_{f}(s_{2})\rho_{t}(s_{1},s_{2})}{|s_{1}||s_{2}|}ds_{1}ds_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\tilde{\rho_{t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\exp\left\{-\sigma_{f}^{2}(s_{1})-\sigma_{f}^{2}(s_{2})+2\sigma_{f}(s_{1})\sigma_{f}(s_{2})\tilde{\rho}\right\}\frac{\sigma_{f}(s_{1})\sigma_{f}(s_{2})}{|s_{1}||s_{2}|}ds_{1}ds_{2}

\displaystyle=2\tilde{\rho_{t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\exp\left\{-(1-\tilde{\rho})\sigma_{f}^{2}(s_{1})-(1-\tilde{\rho})\sigma_{f}^{2}(s_{2})-(\sigma_{f}(s_{1})-\sigma_{f}(s_{2}))^{2}\tilde{\rho}\right\}\frac{\sigma_{f}(s_{1})\sigma_{f}(s_{2})}{|s_{1}||s_{2}|}ds_{1}ds_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\tilde{\rho_{t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\exp\left\{-(1-\tilde{\rho})\sigma_{f}^{2}(s_{1})-(1-\tilde{\rho})\sigma_{f}^{2}(s_{2})\right\}\frac{\sigma_{f}(s_{1})\sigma_{f}(s_{2})}{|s_{1}||s_{2}|}ds_{1}ds_{2}

\displaystyle=8\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{\sigma_{f}(s)}{s}e^{-(1-\tilde{\rho})\sigma_{f}^{2}(s)}ds\right)^{2}\tilde{\rho_{t}}.

∎

The statement of Theorem
1
follows now easily by integrating the upper bound in Lemma
4
with respect to
t\in A_{\tilde{\rho}}
. Here, one writes

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbf{Cov}(\mathbbm{1}\{X(t)>u\},\mathbbm{1}\{X(0)>v\})\right|\mu(du)\mu(dv)\,dt

\displaystyle=\left(\int\limits_{A_{\tilde{\rho}}}+\int\limits_{A^{c}_{\tilde{\rho}}}\right)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbf{Cov}(\mathbbm{1}\{X(t)>u\},\mathbbm{1}\{X(0)>v\})\right|\mu(du)\mu(dv)\,dt,

where the integral over
A_{\tilde{\rho}}
is finite by Lemma
4
, and
the integral over
A^{c}_{\tilde{\rho}}
is finite due to the finiteness of the volume of
A^{c}_{\tilde{\rho}}
, since the integrand

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbf{Cov}(\mathbbm{1}\{X(t)>u\},\mathbbm{1}\{X(0)>v\})\right|\mu(du)\mu(dv)\leq 1.

Uniqueness Theorems for Fourier Quasicrystals and Temperate Distributions with Discrete Support
S.Yu.Favorov

Abstract




Abstract.

It is proved that if some points of the supports of two Fourier quasicrystals approach each other while tending to infinity and the same is true for the masses at these points, then these quasicrystals coincide. A similar statement is obtained for a certain class of discrete temperate distributions.

AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 52C23, 42B10, 42A75

Keywords: temperate distribution, Fourier transform, Fourier quasicrystal, measure with discrete support, distribution with discrete support, almost periodic distribution

1.
introduction

P.Kurasov and R.Suhr
[
6
]
noted that if zeros of two holomorphic almost periodic functions in a strip get closer at infinity, then the zero sets of these functions coincide. This result can be interpreted as the coincidence of two almost periodic discrete sets if they get closer at infinity. It is natural to expect the same effect for other almost periodic objects, in particular, for
Fourier quasicrystals or, in general, for distributions with discrete support and spectrum.

Denote by
S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
the Schwartz space of test functions
\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with finite norms

(1)

N_{m}(\varphi)=\sup_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(\max\{1,|x|\})^{m}\max_{\|k\|\leq m}|(D^{k}\varphi)(x))|,\quad m=0,1,2,\dots,

k=(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})\in({\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\})^{d},\ \|k\|=\|k\|_{\infty}=\max\{k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\},\ D^{k}=\partial^{k_{1}}_{x_{1}}\dots\partial^{k_{d}}_{x_{d}}
. These norms generate a topology on
S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and elements of the space
S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

of continuous linear functionals on
S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
are called temperate distributions.

The Fourier transform of a temperate distribution
f
is defined by the equality

(2)

\hat{f}(\varphi)=f(\hat{\varphi})\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\varphi\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

where

\hat{\varphi}(y)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(x)\exp\{-2\pi i\langle x,y\rangle\}dx

is the Fourier transform of the function
\varphi
.

We will say that a distribution (or a measure)
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is
discrete
, if for each
\lambda\in\supp f
there is
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\lambda)>0
such that
B(\lambda,\varepsilon)\cap\supp f=\{\lambda\}
,
and
uniformly discrete
, if there is
\varepsilon>0
such that
B(\lambda,\varepsilon)\cap B(\lambda^{\prime},\varepsilon)=\emptyset
for all
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\supp f,\,\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
;
a measure
\mu
is
atomic
if
\mu=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda}
with
a_{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and countable
\Lambda
, in this case we will write
a_{\lambda}=\mu(\lambda)
.

Here
B(x,r)=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\,|y-x|<r\}
, and
\delta_{\lambda}
means the unit mass at the point
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

A complex measure
\mu\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a
Fourier quasicrystal
if
\mu
and its Fourier transform
\hat{\mu}
are discrete measures, and the measures
|\mu|
and
|\hat{\mu}|
belong to
S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Note that the condition
\mu\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
do not imply
|\mu|\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(see
[
8
]
or Remark 3 of the present article).

Such measures are the main object in the theory of Fourier quasicrystals (see
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
-
[
14
]
,
[
4
]
). The corresponding notion was inspired by experimental discovery
of non-periodic atomic structures with diffraction patterns consisting of spots, which was made in the mid ’80s.

We will say that a complex measure
\mu
is a
sparse
Fourier quasicrystal, when
\mu
is discrete,
\mu\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
\hat{\mu}
is atomic,
|\hat{\mu}|\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
and numbers of elements
\#\{\supp\mu\cap B(x,1)\}
are uniformly bounded in
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Note that, compared with the classical definition of Fourier quasicrystal, we have weakened the conditions on the measure
\hat{\mu}
and removed the requirement
|\mu|\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Clearly, a Fourier quasicrystal with a uniformly discrete support is a sparse Fourier quasicrystal.

Theorem 1
.

If two sparse Fourier quasicrystals
\mu=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mu(\lambda)\delta_{\lambda},\ \nu=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\nu(\gamma)\delta_{\gamma}
under appropriate numbering
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\,\Gamma=\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
have
the properties

(3)

\lambda_{n}-\gamma_{n}\to 0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mu(\lambda_{n})-\nu(\gamma_{n})\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\ n\to\infty,

then the measures
\mu,\,\nu
coincide.

The conditions of the theorem can be significantly weakened. First, the sparseness of measures and conditions (
3
) can only be checked on a set
E
with the property

(4)

\exists\ \{B(x_{k},r_{k})\}_{k=1}^{\infty},\quad r_{k}\to\infty,\quad r_{k}/|x_{k}|\to 0\quad\mbox{such that}\quad\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}B(x_{k},r_{k})\subset E.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mu=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mu(\lambda)\delta_{\lambda},\ \nu=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\nu(\gamma)\delta_{\gamma}
be discrete measures from
S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that

(5)

\hat{\mu},\ \hat{\nu}\quad\mbox{ are atomic measures, and}\quad|\hat{\mu}|,\ |\hat{\nu}|\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

which ”sparse” on a set
E
with property (
4
), i.e., there is
N<\infty
such that for all
x\in E

\#\{n:\,\lambda_{n}\in B(x,1)\}\leq N,\quad\quad\#\{n:\,\gamma_{n}\in B(x,1)\}\leq N.

If under appropriate numbering

(6)

\lambda_{n}-\gamma_{n}\to 0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mu(\lambda_{n})-\nu(\gamma_{n})\to 0

as
n\to\infty
and
\lambda_{n}
or
\gamma_{n}
belong to
E
, then the measures
\mu,\,\nu
coincide.

Second, points of the support of measures can be combined into groups that are located in a certain sense sparse and behave like points in (
6
). Moreover, the measures
\mu,\ \nu
may be atomic:

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mu,\,\nu
be atomic measures from
S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, whose Fourier transforms
\hat{\mu},\ \hat{\nu}
satisfy (
5
). Suppose that there exist a set
E
with property (
4
), disjoint
Borel sets
\Lambda_{n}\subset E
, and disjoint Borel sets
\Gamma_{n}\subset E
such that the restrictions of measures to
E,\ \Lambda_{n},\ \Gamma_{n}
satisfy the conditions

\mu\mid_{E}=\sum_{n}\mu\mid_{\Lambda_{n}},\qquad\nu\mid_{E}=\sum_{n}\nu\mid_{\Gamma_{n}},

(we do not require
\Lambda_{n}\neq\emptyset
or
\Gamma_{n}\neq\emptyset
). Also, for some
N<\infty

(7)

\quad\#\{n:\,(\Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n})\cap B(x,1)\neq\emptyset\}\leq N,\quad\forall\ x\in E.

If

(8)

\diam\{\Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n}\}\to 0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mu(\Lambda_{n})-\nu(\Gamma_{n})\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\to\infty,

then the measures
\mu,\,\nu
coincide.

Remark 1
. In the case of discrete measures
\mu,\ \nu
and of one-point sets
\Lambda_{n},\,\Gamma_{n}
Theorem
3
coincides with Theorem
2
.

Remark 2
. Conditions
\mu,\,\nu\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and (
5
) can be replaced by the following: the functions

\int\varphi(x-t)\mu(dx)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\int\varphi(x-t)\nu(dx)

are almost periodic for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}
with compact support.

Under some additional conditions, the uniqueness theorem also holds for distributions with discrete supports.
Note that by
[

]
, Proposition 3.1, for every temperate distribution
F
with discrete support there is
m<\infty
such that

F=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\sum_{\|j\|\leq m}p_{\lambda,j}D^{j}\delta_{\lambda},\quad j\in({\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\})^{d},\quad p_{\lambda,j}\in{\mathbb{C}}.

Theorem 4
.

Let

f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\sum_{\|j\|\leq m}p_{\lambda,j}D^{j}\delta_{\lambda},\quad g=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{\|j\|\leq m}q_{\gamma,j}D^{j}\delta_{\gamma}

be temperate distributions with discrete supports
\Lambda,\,\Gamma
such that

(9)

\hat{f},\ \hat{g}\quad\mbox{ are atomic measures, and}\quad|\hat{f}|,\ |\hat{g}|\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

and there be a set
E
with property (
4
), disjoint sets
\Lambda_{n}
, and disjoint sets
\Gamma_{n}
with properties (
7
) such that

\Lambda\cap E=\bigcup_{n}\Lambda_{n},\qquad\Gamma\cap E=\bigcup_{n}\Gamma_{n},

(we do not require
\Lambda_{n}\neq\emptyset
or
\Gamma_{n}\neq\emptyset
). If

(10)

\diam\{\Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n}\}\to 0,\quad\quad\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}p_{\lambda,j}-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}}q_{\gamma,j}\to 0\quad\forall j\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\to\infty,

and

(11)

\max_{j}\sup_{n}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}|p_{\lambda,j}|<\infty,\quad\max_{j}\sup_{n}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}}|q_{\gamma,j}|<\infty,

then the distributions
f,\,g
coincide.

Measures and their Fourier transforms can be interchanged in Theorems
3
and
4
. Recall that the support of the Fourier transform of a measure
\mu
is called a
spectrum
of
\mu
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mu,\,\nu
be atomic measures with discrete spectra
\tilde{\Lambda},\,\tilde{\Gamma}
respectively,
|\mu|,\,|\nu|\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and there be a set
E
with property (
4
), disjoint sets
\tilde{\Lambda}_{n}
,
and disjoint sets
\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}
such that

\tilde{\Lambda}\cap E=\bigcup_{n}\tilde{\Lambda}_{n},\qquad\tilde{\Gamma}\cap E=\bigcup_{n}\tilde{\Gamma}_{n},\qquad\diam\{\tilde{\Lambda}_{n}\cup\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}\}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\to\infty,

and conditions (
7
) are met.

If either
\hat{\mu},\,\hat{\nu}
are measures, and

\hat{\mu}(\tilde{\Lambda}_{n})-\hat{\nu}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{n})\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\to\infty,

or

\hat{\mu}=\sum_{\lambda\in\tilde{\Lambda}}\sum_{\|j\|\leq m}\tilde{p}_{\lambda,j}D^{j}\delta_{\lambda},\quad\hat{\nu}=\sum_{\gamma\in\tilde{\Gamma}}\sum_{\|j\|\leq m}\tilde{q}_{\gamma,j}D^{j}\delta_{\gamma},

and

\sum_{\lambda\in\tilde{\Lambda}_{n}}\tilde{p}_{\lambda,j}-\sum_{\gamma\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}}\tilde{q}_{\gamma,j}\to 0\quad\forall j\quad\mbox{ as}\quad n\to\infty,

\max_{j}\sup_{n}\sum_{\lambda\in\tilde{\Lambda}_{n}}|\tilde{p}_{\lambda,j}|<\infty,\quad\max_{j}\sup_{n}\sum_{\gamma\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{n}}|\tilde{q}_{\gamma,j}|<\infty,

then the measures
\mu,\,\nu
coincide.

We also give an analogue of Theorem
1
for the Fourier quasicrystals:

Theorem 6
.

If two Fourier quasicrystals
\mu,\,\nu
with discrete ”sparse” spectra
\tilde{\Lambda},\,\tilde{\Gamma}
(this means that

\sup_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\#\{\tilde{\Lambda}\cap B(y,1)\}<\infty,\quad\sup_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\#\{\tilde{\Gamma}\cap B(y,1)\}<\infty)

under appropriate numbering
\tilde{\Lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\,\tilde{\Gamma}=\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
have
the properties

\lambda_{n}-\gamma_{n}\to 0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\hat{\mu}(\lambda_{n})-\hat{\nu}(\gamma_{n})\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\ n\to\infty,

then the measures
\mu,\,\nu
coincide.

2.
auxiliary results

Lemma 1
.

Let a positive measure
\mu
belong to
S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Then there is
N<\infty
such that

(12)

\mu(B(0,R))=O(R^{N}),

and for any Borel
function
H(x)
such that
\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|H(x)|(1+|x|^{T})<\infty
for all
T<\infty
we get

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|H(x)|\mu(dx)<\infty.

Proof of the Lemma
. Assume the converse. Then there is a sequence
R_{n}\to\infty
such that
\mu(B(0,R_{n}))>R_{n}^{n}
. We may suppose that
R_{n+1}>2R_{n}
for all
n
.
Take
\varphi(t)\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}),\ 0\leq\varphi(t)\leq 1
such that
\varphi(t)=1
for
t\leq 1
and
\varphi(t)=0
for
t\geq 2
. Set

\Psi(x)=\sum_{n}R_{n}^{-n}\varphi(|x|/R_{n}).

Clearly,
\Psi\in C^{\infty}
and

(13)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Psi(x)\mu(dx)\geq\sum_{n}R_{n}^{-n}\mu(B(0,R_{n}))=\infty.

On there other hand, take any
K<\infty
and
x
such that
2R_{p-1}<|x|\leq 2R_{p}
with
p>K
. We have

|x|^{K}\Psi(x)=\sum_{n}|x|^{K}R_{n}^{-n}\varphi(|x|/R_{n})<2^{K}R_{p}^{K-p}\sum_{n\geq p}R_{p}^{p}/R_{n}^{n}.

Taking into account that
R_{n}>2^{n-p}R_{p}
and
p\to\infty
as
|x|\to\infty
, we obtain

|x|^{K}\Psi(x)<2^{K+1}R_{p}^{K-p}\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty.

Similarly, one can check that
|x|^{K}\Psi^{(k)}(x)\to 0
for all
K
and
k\in({\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\})^{d}
, therefore,
\Psi\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Since
\mu\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we get the contradiction with (
13
).
Hence there exists
N
such that
M(R):=\mu(B(0,R))\leq C\max(1,R^{N})
.

Furthermore, let
|H(x)|\leq C_{1}|x|^{-N-1}
for
|x|\geq 1
. Passing to polar coordinates and integrating in parts, we obtain

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|H(x)|\mu(dx)\leq C_{0}+C_{1}\int_{|x|>1}|x|^{-N-1}\mu(dx)=C_{0}+C_{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-N-1}M(dr)

=C_{0}+C_{2}\left(\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{M(R)}{R^{N+1}}-M(1)+(N+1)\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{M(r)}{r^{N+2}}dr\right)<\infty.

Lemma is proved.

Remark 3
. Let

\mu=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}2^{n-1}[\delta_{n+2^{-n}}-\delta_{n-2^{-n}}]

be a measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
. For any
\varphi\in S({\mathbb{R}})
we have for some
t_{n}\in[n-2^{-n},n+2^{-n}]

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\mu(dt)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}2^{n-1}[\varphi(n+2^{-n})-\varphi(n-2^{-n})]=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\varphi^{\prime}(t_{n}),

and

|(\mu,\varphi)|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\mu(dt)\right|\leq\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}t_{n}^{-2}N_{2}(\varphi)\leq CN_{2}(\varphi),

where
N_{2}(\varphi)
is defined in (
1
). Therefore,
\mu\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}})
. On the other hand,

|\mu|(-n-2^{-n},n+2^{-n})=\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}2^{j+1}=2^{n+2},

hence by Lemma
1
, the condition
|\mu|\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}})
contradicts to (
12
).

The proofs of our theorems are also based on the properties of almost periodic functions and distributions. Recall some definitions
related to the notion of almost periodicity

(a detailed exposition of the theory of almost periodic functions on
{\mathbb{R}}
see, for example, in
[
1
]
and
[
9
]
, most of the results can
easily be generalized to functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
; almost periodic measures and distributions were introduced in
[
8
]
and
[
15
]
, see also
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
5
]
).

A set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is relatively dense, if there is
R<\infty
such that every ball of radius
R
intersects with
A
.

A continuous function
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is
almost periodic
, if
for every
\varepsilon>0
the set of
\varepsilon
-almost periods of
f

\{\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\,\sup_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f(t+\tau)-f(t)|<\varepsilon\}

is relatively dense in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

For example, for arbitrary
s_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the function

f(t)=\sum_{n}a_{n}e^{2\pi i\langle t,s_{n}\rangle}

is almost periodic under the condition
\sum_{n}|a_{n}|<\infty
.

It was proved in
[
1
]
that a finite family
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}
of almost periodic functions on
{\mathbb{R}}
has a common relatively dense set
of
\varepsilon
-almost periods for every
\varepsilon
. The same result for almost periodic functions on Euclidean spaces follows immediately from Bochner’s criterion: a function
f(x),\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}
is almost periodic if and only if
for every sequence
x_{n}
there is a subsequence
x_{n^{\prime}}
such that the functions
f(x+x_{n^{\prime}})
converges uniformly in
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Its proof in
[
9
]
practically without changes is
transferred to functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and even to mappings from
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
.

Now, if each
f_{j}
satisfies Bochner’s criterion, then the mapping
F=(f_{1}(x),\dots,f_{M}(x))
satisfies this criterion too.
It remains to notice that every
\varepsilon
-almost period of
F
is an
\varepsilon
-almost period of every
f_{j}
.

Next, put
\varphi_{t}=\varphi(x-t)
for any function
\varphi
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

A measure
\mu
is
almost periodic
, if the function
F(t)=\int\psi_{t}(x)\mu(dx)

is almost periodic for any continuous function
\psi(x)
with compact support.

A temperate distribution
f
is
almost periodic
, if for every
\varphi\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
the function
F(t)=f(\varphi_{t})

is almost periodic.

It is easy to prove that every almost periodic measure is an almost periodic temperate distribution. Also, if
\mu
is a positive measure, or satisfies the condition

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\mu(B(x,1))|<\infty
, then almost periodicity of
\mu
in the sense of distributions implies almost periodicity in the sense of measures.
But there are discrete measures that almost periodic temperate distributions and
not almost periodic measures (
[
8
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
3
]
).

Lemma 2
.

If
f\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
\hat{f}
is an atomic measure, and
|\hat{f}|\in S^{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then
f
is an almost periodic distribution.

In particular, every Fourier quasicrystal is an almost periodic distribution.

Proof of the Lemma
.
Let
\hat{f}=\sum_{n}b_{n}\delta_{s_{n}}
, then
|\hat{f}|=\sum_{n}|b_{n}|\delta_{s_{n}}
. By (
2
), we have for each
\varphi\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

(14)

f(\varphi_{t})=\int\check{\varphi}(y)e^{2\pi i\langle t,y\rangle}\hat{f}(dy)=\sum_{n}b_{n}\check{\varphi}(s_{n})e^{2\pi i\langle t,s_{n}\rangle},

where
\check{\varphi}(y)e^{2\pi i\langle t,y\rangle}
is the inverse Fourier transform of the function
\varphi(x-t)
. Since
\check{\varphi}(y)\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we apply Lemma
1
and get

\sum_{n}|b_{n}||\check{\varphi}(s_{n})|=\int|\check{\varphi}(y)||\hat{f}|(dy)<\infty.

Therefore the function
f(\varphi_{t})
is almost periodic.

3.
proofs of the theorems

Proof of Theorem
3
. Assume the contrary
\mu\not\equiv\nu
. Since
\mu,\,\nu
are atomic measures, we can find
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\nu(a)\neq\mu(a)
.
Set

\varepsilon=\frac{|\mu(a)-\nu(a)|}{3}.

Since

(|\mu|+|\nu|)(\{x:\,0<|x-a|<\rho\})\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\rho\to 0,

we see that for some
\rho\leq 1/2

(15)

|\mu|(\{x:\,0<|x-a|<\rho\})+|\nu|(\{x:\,0<|x-a|<\rho\})<\varepsilon.

Take
\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
,
\supp\varphi\subset B(2)
, and
\varphi(x)=1
for
|x|<1
.
Let
N
be a number from (
7
). Set for
j=1,\dots,2N+1

f_{j}(t)=\int\varphi\left(\frac{x-t}{2^{-j}\rho}\right)\mu(dx),\quad g_{j}(t)=\int\varphi\left(\frac{x-t}{2^{-j}\rho}\right)\nu(dx),\quad H_{j}(t)=f_{j}(t)-g_{j}(t).

Using (
5
) and applying Lemma
2
, we obtain that all the functions
f_{j}(t)
and
g_{j}(t)

are almost periodic, and the functions
H_{j}(t)
too. Also, it follows from (
15
) that

|H_{j}(a)|>2\varepsilon\quad\forall\ j=1,\dots,2N+1.

Denote by
\mathfrak{T}
the set of all common
\varepsilon
-almost periods of the functions
H_{j}(t)
. We get

(16)

|H_{j}(a+\tau)|>\varepsilon\quad\forall\tau\in\mathfrak{T},\quad j=1,\dots,2N+1.

Since
\mathfrak{T}
is relatively dense, it follows from (
4
) that for every
k>k_{1}
there is
\tau_{k}\in\mathfrak{T}
such that
B(x_{k},r_{k})\supset B(a+\tau_{k},\rho)
.
By (
7
), the set

\{n:\,(\Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n})\subset B(x_{k},r_{k})\}

is discrete, hence, we have

(17)

\min\{n:\,(\Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n})\subset B(x_{k},r_{k})\}\to\infty\quad\mbox{as}\quad k\to\infty,

therefore, by the first part of (
8
),

\diam(\Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n})\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\ k\to\infty\quad\mbox{ for }\ \Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n}\subset B(x_{k},r_{k}).

In particular, there is
k_{2}
such that for
k>k_{2}
and
\Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n}\subset B(x_{k},r_{k})
we get

\diam(\Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n})<2^{-2N-1}\rho,

and the set
\Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n}
can intersect with only one of the spherical shells

B(a+\tau_{k},2^{-j+1}\rho)\setminus B(a+\tau_{k},2^{-j}\rho),\ j=1,\dots,2N+1.

On the other hand, by (
7
),

\#\{n:\,(\Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n})\subset B(a+\tau_{k},2\rho)\}\leq 2N,

hence there is
m=m(k),\,1\leq m\leq 2N+1,
such that

(\Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n})\cap[B(a+\tau_{k},2^{-m+1}\rho)\setminus B(a+\tau_{k},2^{-m}\rho)]=\emptyset\quad\mbox{ if }\ \Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n}\subset B(a+\tau_{k},2\rho).

Consequently, the sets
\Lambda_{n},\,\Gamma_{n}
are either both simultaneously subsets of
B(a+\tau_{k},2^{-m}\rho)
and

\varphi\left(\frac{\lambda-a-\tau_{k}}{2^{-m}\rho}\right)=\varphi\left(\frac{\gamma-a-\tau_{k}}{2^{-m}\rho}\right)=1,\quad\forall\,\lambda\in\Lambda_{n},\ \forall\,\gamma\in\Gamma_{n},

or are not subsets of
B(a+\tau_{k},2^{-m+1}\rho)
, and

\varphi\left(\frac{\lambda-a-\tau_{k}}{2^{-m}\rho}\right)=\varphi\left(\frac{\gamma-a-\tau_{k}}{2^{-m}\rho}\right)=0\quad\forall\,\lambda\in\Lambda_{n},\ \forall\,\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}.

Hence,

H_{m}(a+\tau_{k})=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda\cap B(a+\tau_{k},2^{-m}\rho)}\mu(\lambda)-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma\cap B(a+\tau_{k},2^{-m}\rho)}\nu(\gamma)=\sum_{n:\Lambda_{n}\subset B(a+\tau_{k},2^{-m}\rho)}[\mu(\Lambda_{n})-\nu(\Gamma_{n})].

By (
17
) and the second part of (
8
), we obtain
\mu(\Lambda_{n})-\nu(\Gamma_{n})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Since a number of members in the last sum is an most
N
, we get that the sequence
H_{m(k)}(a+\tau_{k})
tends to zero as
k\to\infty
, which contradicts to (
16
).

Proof of Theorem
4
.
Assume the contrary
f\not\equiv g
. Then there is
a\in\supp f
such that either
a\in\Gamma
and
p_{a,j^{0}}\neq q_{a,j^{0}}
for some
j^{0}\in({\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\})^{d}
, or
a\not\in\Gamma
and
p_{a,j^{0}}\neq 0
for some
j^{0}\in({\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\})^{d}
.
In the latter case put
q_{a,j^{0}}=0
. Take
\varphi\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1,\ \supp\varphi\subset B(0,2)
, and
\varphi(x)=1
for
x\in B(0,1)
. Since
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
are discrete, it follows that there are no points of
\Lambda\cup\Gamma
other than
a
in the ball
B(a,2\rho)
for some
\rho\leq 1/2
. Put

\psi(x)=\varphi(x/\rho)x_{1}^{j_{1}^{0}}x_{2}^{j_{2}^{0}}\dots x_{d}^{j_{d}^{0}}/j_{1}^{0}!j_{2}^{0}!\dots j^{0}_{d}!,\qquad x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Clearly,
(D^{j^{0}}\psi)(0)=1
and
(D^{j}\psi)(0)=0
for
j\neq j^{0}
. Therefore,
f(\psi_{a})=p_{a,j^{0}}\neq g(\psi_{a})
.
Using (
9
) and applying Lemma
2
, we obtain that the functions
f(\psi_{t}),\ g(\psi_{t})
are almost periodic, and the function
H(t)=f(\psi_{t})-g(\psi_{t})
too.
Moreover,
H(a)=p_{a,j^{0}}-q_{a,j^{0}}\neq 0
. Set
\varepsilon=|H(a)|/2
. Denote by
\mathfrak{T}
the set of all
\varepsilon
-almost period of the functions
H(t)
. We get

(18)

|H(a+\tau)|>\varepsilon\quad\forall\tau\in\mathfrak{T}.

Since
\mathfrak{T}
is relatively dense, it follows from (
4
) that for every
k>k_{1}
there is
\tau_{k}\in\mathfrak{T}
such that
B(x_{k},r_{k})\supset B(a+\tau_{k},\rho)
.
Set

M_{k}=\{n:\,(\Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n})\cap B(a+\tau_{k},2\rho)\neq\emptyset\}.

By (
7
),
M_{k}
is finite, hence,

(19)

\min M_{k}\to\infty\quad\mbox{ as }k\to\infty.

Furthermore, for sufficiently large
k

(20)

f(\psi_{a+\tau_{k}})-g(\psi_{a+\tau_{k}})=\sum_{n\in M_{k}}\sum_{j}\left[\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}p_{\lambda,j}(D^{j}\psi)(\lambda-a-\tau_{k})-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}}q_{\gamma,j}(D^{j}\psi)(\gamma-a-\tau_{k})\right].

All derivatives of
\psi
are uniformly continuous, hence it follows from the first part of (
10
) and (
11
) that for some fixed points
b_{n}\in\Lambda_{n}\cup\Gamma_{n}
and each
j

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}p_{\lambda,j}\left[(D^{j}\psi)(\lambda-a-\tau_{k})-(D^{j}\psi)(b_{n}-a-\tau_{k})\right]\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\ n\to\infty,

and

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}}q_{\gamma,j}\left[(D^{j}\psi)(\gamma-a-\tau_{k})-(D^{j}\psi)(b_{n}-a-\tau_{k})\right]\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\ n\to\infty.

All derivatives of
\psi
are uniformly bounded, hence it follows (
10
) that for each
j

\left[\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}p_{\lambda,j}-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}}q_{\gamma,j}\right](D^{j}\psi)(b_{n}-a-\tau_{k})\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\ n\to\infty.

Hence,

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}p_{\lambda,j}(D^{j}\psi)(\lambda-a-\tau_{k})-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}}q_{\gamma,j}(D^{j}\psi)(\gamma-a-\tau_{k})\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\ n\to\infty.

Note that the number of values of
j
does not exceed
(m+1)^{d}
and
\#M_{k}\leq 2N
. Thus we obtain from (
19
) and (
20
)

|H(a+\tau_{k})|=|f(\psi_{a+\tau_{k}})-g(\psi_{a+\tau_{k}})|\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\ k\to\infty,

which contradicts to (
18
).

Amir Akbary, Alia Hamieh

Abstract
. Let d ∈ ℕ d\in\mathbb{N} , and let π \pi be a fixed cuspidal automorphic representation of GL d ​ ( 𝔸 ℚ ) \mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}) with unitary central character. We determine the limiting distribution of the family of values − L ′ L ​ ( 1 + i ​ t , π ⊗ χ D ) -\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D}) as D D varies over fundamental discriminants. Here, t t is a fixed real number and χ D \chi_{D} is the real character associated with D D . We establish an upper bound on the discrepancy in the convergence of this family to its limiting distribution. As an application of this result, we obtain an upper bound on the small values of | L ′ L ​ ( 1 , π ⊗ χ D ) | \left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right| when π \pi is self-dual.

Introduction

Bohr and Jessen showed in
[
BJ
]
that the values
\log{\zeta(\sigma+it)}
for a fixed
\sigma>\frac{1}{2}
as
t
varies in
\mathbb{R}
have a limiting distribution with a continuous density in the complex plane. In
[
JW
]
, Jessen and Wintner revisited this problem from a more general perspective using ideas from probability theory and Fourier analysis machinery, which allowed them to reveal detailed information on the distribution function in Bohr-Jessen’s theorem (see
[
JW
, Theorem 19]
). In recent years, this line of research was pursued further by many authors studying the distribution of
\log\zeta(\sigma+it)
in the critical strip (e.g.
[
Harman-Mat
]
,
[
HM
]
,
[
lamzouri2
]
and
[
Lf
]
). We focus here on the work of Lamzouri, Lester, and Radziwiłł
[
Lf
]
in which the authors investigate the discrepancy between the distributions of
\log\zeta(\sigma+it)
and that of an adequately chosen random series
\log\zeta(\sigma,\mathbb{X})
. More precisely, let
\{\mathbb{X}_{p}\}_{p\;\text{prime}}
be a sequence of independent random variables uniformly distributed on the unit circle. Consider the random Euler product
\zeta(\sigma,\mathbb{X})=\prod_{p}\left(1-\mathbb{X}_{p}p^{-\sigma}\right)^{-1}
which converges almost surely for
\sigma>\frac{1}{2}
. We can ask whether
\zeta(\sigma,\mathbb{X})
is a good model for the Riemann zeta function. The authors of
[
Lf
]
answer this question affirmatively.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
Lf
, Theorem 1.1]
Let
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<1
be fixed. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{\sigma}(\log{\zeta};T):=\sup_{\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{C}}\left|\mathbb{P}_{T}\left(\log\zeta(\sigma+it)\in\mathcal{R}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\log\zeta(\sigma,\mathbb{X})\in\mathcal{R}\right)\right|\ll\frac{1}{(\log T)^{\sigma}}.

For
\sigma=1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{1}(\log{\zeta};T):=\sup_{\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{C}}\left|\mathbb{P}_{T}\left(\log\zeta(1+it)\in\mathcal{R}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\log\zeta(1,\mathbb{X})\in\mathcal{R}\right)\right|\ll\frac{\log\log T}{\log T}.

Here
\mathcal{R}
varies over all rectangles in
\mathbb{C}
with sides parallel to the axes, and

\mathbb{P}_{T}\left(f(t)\in\mathcal{R}\right)=\frac{1}{T}\mathrm{meas}\{T\leq t\leq 2T:f(t)\in\mathcal{R}\}.

This improves on the result in
[
Harman-Mat
]
where the authors prove that for any
\epsilon>0
, we have

\mathbb{D}_{\sigma}(\log{\zeta};T)\ll\frac{1}{(\log T)^{\frac{4\sigma-2}{21+8\sigma}-\epsilon}},

provided that
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma\leq 1
.
Recently Xiao and Zhai extended the result of
[
Lf
]
, for
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<1
to the case of
L
-functions attached to Hecke eigenforms of level 1.

In another direction, Mine
[
M20
]
studied the discrepancy
D_{s}(\log{L_{f}};q)
in the value distribution at a fixed point
s=\sigma+it
of averages and harmonic averages of the values of logarithm of the
L
-functions
L_{f}(s)
attached to the primitive cusp forms
f
of weight 2 and prime level
q
, as
q\rightarrow\infty
. In
[
M20

it is proved that

\mathbb{D}_{s}(\log{L_{f}};q)\ll\begin{cases}(\log\log{q})/\log{q}&{if~\sigma>1,}\\
(\log\log{q})(\log\log\log{q})/\log{q}&{if~\sigma=1,}\\
1/(\log{q})^{\sigma}&{if~\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<1,}\end{cases}

generalizing a special case of a 1-dimensional result of Cogdell and Michel
[
CM04

and a 1-dimensional discrepancy estimate of Golubeva
[

. The reader is referred to
[
M20

for the exact definition of

\mathbb{D}_{s}(\log{L_{f}};q)

Inspired by studying the small values of the Euler-Kronecker constants of the cyclotomic fields
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})

[

proved a discrepancy estimate for the distribution of
\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1,\chi)

\chi

q
(prime). By defining a suitable random series
\ld(1,\mathbb{X})
associated to a certain random sequence

\mathbb{X}
and setting

\mathbb{D}_{1}\left({L_{\chi}^{\prime}}/{L_{\chi}};q\right)=\sup_{\mathcal{R}\subset{\mathbb{C}}}\left|\frac{1}{q-1}\left|\left\{\chi\neq\chi_{0}~{\rm mod}~q:~\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1,\chi)\in\mathcal{R}\right\}\right|-\mathbb{P}(\ld(1,\mathbb{X})\in\mathcal{R}\right|,

where the supremum is taken over all rectangles of the
complex plane with sides parallel to the coordinate axes, they proved in
[
LL21
, Theorem 1.5]
that

\mathbb{D}_{1}\left({L_{\chi}^{\prime}}/{L_{\chi}};q\right)\ll\frac{(\log\log{q})^{2}}{\log{q}},\quad\text{as}\;q\to\infty;~q\;\text{prime}.

In
[
Hamieh-Mcclenagan
]
, Hamieh and Mcclenagan, determined an asymptotic distribution function for the values
\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma,\chi_{D})
as
D
varies over all fundamental discriminants
D
, with
|D|\leq N
, for a fixed real number
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<1
, removing the dependence on GRH in a result of Mourtada and Murty
[
M-M
]
.
Here
\chi_{D}=\left(\frac{D}{\cdot}\right)
is the Kronecker symbol for
D
, and
L(s,\chi_{D})
is the associated Dirichlet
L
-function.
In addition, in
[
Hamieh-Mcclenagan
, Theorem 1.3]
they proved

\mathbb{D}_{\sigma}\left({L_{\chi_{D}}^{\prime}}/{L_{\chi_{D}}};N\right)\ll\left(\frac{\log\log{N}}{\log{N}}\right)^{\sigma},

where

\mathbb{D}_{\sigma}\left({L_{\chi_{D}}^{\prime}}/{L_{\chi_{D}}};N\right)=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma,\chi_{D})\leq z\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\ld(\sigma,\mathbb{X})\leq z\right)\right|

is the discrepancy between the value-distribution of quadratic twists and that of a random series
\ld(\sigma,\mathbb{X})
attached to a random sequence
\mathbb{X}
described in the introduction of
[
Hamieh-Mcclenagan
]
.
Here

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma,\chi_{D})\leq z\right)=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\left|\left\{D\in\mathcal{F}(N):~\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma,\chi_{D})\leq z\right\}\right|,

where
\mathcal{F}(N)
denotes the collection of the fundamental discriminants
D
with
|D|\leq N
. Note that we have (see
[
GS
, page 1017]
)

|\mathcal{F}(N)|=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}N+O\left(N^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

In this paper, we consider the analogous problem for logarithmic derivatives of quadratic twists of automorphic
L
-functions. Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
with unitary central character. Let
L(s,\pi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{\pi}(n)n^{-s}
be the associated Dirichlet series. In particular, we have

(1.1)

L(s,\pi)=\prod_{p}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1},

where
\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)
’s are the
Satake parameters
of
\pi
.
Thus,
a_{\pi}(p)={\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}
. By a result of Rudnick and Sarnak
[
R-S
]
, we know that

(1.2)

|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)|\leq p^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{d^{2}+1}}

for all
j\in\{1,2,\dots,d\}
. We set

(1.3)

\lambda_{\pi}(n)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}^{m}}&\;\text{if}\;n=p^{m},\\
0&\;\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Corresponding to a representation
\pi
, there is a dual representation
\tilde{\pi}
. The collection of the Satake parameters for
\tilde{\pi}
coincides with the collection of the complex conjugates of the Satake parameters for
\pi
, and thus
a_{\tilde{\pi}}(n)=\overline{a_{\pi}(n)}
. We call a cuspidal representation
\pi

self-dual
if
{\pi}\simeq\tilde{\pi}
.

For
\Re(s)>1
and a fundamental discriminant
D\in\mathcal{F}(N)
, we set

(1.4)

L(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{s}}

and

(1.5)

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{s}}.

These functions have meromorphic continuations to the entire complex plane.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a rectangle in
\mathbb{C}
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes. We denote by
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{R}}\left(\cdot\right)
the characteristic function of
\mathcal{R}
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
we define

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\in\mathcal{R}\right)=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{R}}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right).

Thus,

\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(-\tfrac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\in\mathcal{R}\right)
is the proportion of the fundamental discriminants
D
for which
-\tfrac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\in\mathcal{R}
.

Let us now introduce the probabilistic random model which we use to approximate the distribution of the arithmetic values
-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})
described above. Consider the sequence of independent random variables
\{\mathbb{X}_{p}\}_{p\;\text{prime}}
given by

\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbb{X}_{p}=a\big)=\begin{cases}\frac{p}{2(p+1)}&\text{if $a=\pm 1$},\\
\frac{1}{p+1}&\text{if $a=0$}.\end{cases}

We set
\mathbb{X}_{n}=\prod_{p\mid n}\mathbb{X}_{p}^{\nu_{p}(n)}
, where
\nu_{p}(n)
is the
p
-adic valuation of
n
. The sequence
\mathbb{X}=\{\mathbb{X}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
was first introduced in
[
GS
]
for the purpose of studying the distribution of the extreme values of
L(1,\chi_{D})
as
D
varies over all fundamental discriminants. We denote the underlying probability measure on the sample space associated to
\mathbb{X}
by
\mathbb{P}
.
For a representation
\pi
as above and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we associate the random series

-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}.

Our main theorem gives an upper bound on the discrepancy between the distribution of the random series
-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})
and that of
-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})
as
D
varies in
\mathcal{F}(N)
. Notice that if
\pi\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{2it}
, then the distribution under consideration is 1-dimensional since the values involved are necessarily real. To see this, observe that for all primes
p
and all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\lambda_{\pi}(p^{m})p^{-mit}=p^{-mit}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)^{m}=p^{-mit}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\overline{\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}p^{2it}\right)^{m}=\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(p^{m})}p^{mit},

which implies that
\lambda_{\pi}(p^{m})p^{-mit}\in\mathbb{R}
. Otherwise, our distributions are 2-dimensional. We denote the discrepancy by
\mathbb{D}_{1+it}\left(L_{\pi\otimes\chi_{D}}^{\prime}/L_{\pi\otimes\chi_{D}};N\right)
and define it as

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\leq x\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\leq x\right)\right|

in the 1-dimensional case, and

\sup_{\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{C}}\left|\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\in\mathcal{R}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\in\mathcal{R}\right)\right|,

where
\mathcal{R}
varies over all rectangles in
\mathbb{C}
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes, in the 2-dimensional case.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\pi
be a fixed cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
with unitary central character. Suppose that the Satake parameters of
\pi
satisfy
\left|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)\right|\leq p^{\theta}
with
0\leq\theta<\frac{1}{4}
for all
j=1,2,\cdots,d
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{1+it}\left(L_{\pi\otimes\chi_{D}}^{\prime}/L_{\pi\otimes\chi_{D}};N\right)\ll\frac{(\log\log N)^{2}}{\log N}.

It is clear from (
1.2
) that the above theorem holds if
d=1
. Moreover, it holds if
d=2
since
\theta<\frac{7}{64}
by
[
K-S
]
. Indeed, we get the following automorphic analogue of
[
Lf
, Theorem 1.1]
for logarithmic derivatives.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\pi
be a fixed cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{1}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
or
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
with unitary central character. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{1+it}\left(L_{\pi\otimes\chi_{D}}^{\prime}/L_{\pi\otimes\chi_{D}};N\right)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{(\log\log N)^{2}}{\log N}.

Remarks 1.4
.

(i) We give the proof of Theorem
1.2
only in the 2-dimensional case, i.e.,
\pi\not\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{2it}
. The proof in the 1-dimensional case follows analogous arguments.

(ii) The condition on the bound for the Satake parameters in Theorem
1.2
is needed in the proof of the exponential decay for the characteristic function of our random series (Proposition
6.1
) which is a crucial ingredient in our argument. In some other parts of the argument, we only require Hypothesis H (see Section
2.1
) which follows readily from the assumed bound on the Satake parameters in our main Theorem.

(iii) Similar results can be obtained for the values of
\log{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})
by following the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

(iv) By examining the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we can see that its assertion also holds when
1+it
is replaced by
\sigma+it
with
1-c_{\pi}<\sigma<1
for some constant
c_{\pi}>0
. The expected discrepancy bound in this case would be of size
O\left(\left(\frac{\log\log N}{\log N}\right)^{\sigma}\right)
. The lower bound on
\sigma
is imposed by the zero density estimate used in the proof (see Lemma
2.9
and Proposition
2.11
) and the domain of convergence of
-\ld(s,\pi,\mathbb{X})
.

(v) In some special cases, Corollary
1.3
holds with a discrepancy bound of size
O\left(\left(\frac{\log\log N}{\log N}\right)^{\sigma}\right)
for a range of
\sigma
that is wider than the one indicated in the previous remark when
1+it
is replaced by
\sigma+it
. In fact, one can fix
\sigma
to be much closer to
\frac{1}{2}
if a suitable zero density result is available for the family
L(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})
. For instance,
[
heath-brown
, Theorem 3]
was used in
[
Hamieh-Mcclenagan
]
to prove a discrepancy result for
-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma,\chi_{D})
(which can be considered as a special case of Corollary
1.3
) that is valid for any
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<1
. Using the zero density theorem in
[
Perelli-Pomykala
]
, one could achieve a similar result for
-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma,f\otimes\chi_{D})
when
f
is primitive cusp form of weight 2.

We also use Theorem
1.2
to derive an asymptotic bound for the small values of
\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right|
when
\pi\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{2it}
. The following result is an analogue of
[
Hamieh-Mcclenagan
, Corollary 1.4 ]
, and

[
LL21
, Theorem 1.1]
where the authors investigate the small values of
\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1,\chi)\right|
for non-principal Dirichlet characters
\chi
modulo
q
, as
q\to\infty
over the primes.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed, and let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
with unitary central character such that
\pi\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{2it}
. Suppose that
\left|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)\right|\leq p^{\theta}
with
0\leq\theta<\frac{1}{4}
for all
j=1,2\cdots,d
. Let
\displaystyle{m_{N}=\min_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left(\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right|\right)}
. As
N\to\infty
, we have

m_{N}\ll\frac{(\log\log N)^{2}}{\log N}.

More precisely, there are at least
N(\log\log{N})^{2}/\log{N}
for which

\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right|\ll\frac{(\log\log{N})^{2}}{\log{N}}.

Remark 1.6
.

We derive our upper bound for the small values of
\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right|
by an application of Theorem
1.2
and using the positivity at the origin of the density function of the associated distribution. The assumption of these two facts together will result in

m_{N}(1+it):=\min_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left(\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right|\right)\ll\mathbb{D}_{1+it}\left(L_{\pi\otimes\chi_{D}}^{\prime}/L_{\pi\otimes\chi_{D}};N\right)

for 1-dimensional distributions and

m_{N}(1+it)\ll\sqrt{\mathbb{D}_{1+it}\left(L_{\pi\otimes\chi_{D}}^{\prime}/L_{\pi\otimes\chi_{D}};N\right)}

for 2-dimensional distributions. The restriction to
\pi\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{2it}
in Theorem
1.5
is due to the fact that, in Lemma
8.2
, we are able to prove the positivity of the density function for
1
-dimensional distributions only.

The proof of Theorem
1.5
is given in Section
8
as an application of Theorem
1.2
. While our proof of Theorem
1.2
follows the approach devised in
[
Lf
]
, we deviate from their method in the last step of the proof. In order to avoid the need for a large deviation result for our family, we employ a 2-dimensional version of the classical Berry-Esseen inequality instead of the Beurling-Selberg functions used in
[
Lf
, Section 6]
to relate the distribution functions under consideration to their characteristic functions. In Section
7
, we show how this 2-dimensional Berry-Esseen inequality yields an upper bound for the discrepancy between
\mathbb{P}_{N}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\in\mathcal{R}\right)
and
\mathbb{P}\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\right)
in terms of the difference between their associated characteristic functions. In doing so, we adapt some of the ideas outlined in
[
M20
, Section 4.3]
. In Section
6
we prove a rapid decay estimate for the characteristic function of the random series
-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})
which is crucial for applying the Berry-Esseen inequality. In Section
5
, we prove Theorem
5.1
which shows that the characteristic function of the joint distribution of
\Re\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right)
and
\Im\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right)
can be very well approximated by the corresponding characteristic function of the random series
-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})
.
The point of departure in the proof of Theorem
5.1
is a result asserting that
-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})
can be approximated by a short Dirichlet polynomial outside a set of fundamental discriminants
D
of size
o(N)
. The proof of this approximation, although mostly standard, entails few complications arising from the fact that we do not assume the Generalized Ramanujan Conjecture in our work. We deal with these subtleties by
employing a truncated Perron’s formula for automorphic
L
-functions
[
L-Y
, Theorem 2.1]
, a Brun-Titchmarsh type inequality
[
S-T
, Theorem 2.4]
, and a recent zero density estimate
[
H-T1
, Theorm 1.1]
. In view of this result, Theorem
5.1
can be extracted from a key result, Proposition
4.2
, that compares the characteristic functions of short Dirichlet polynomials of the form

(1.6)

\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}.

In Section
4
, we prove Proposition
4.2
. More precisely, we show that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\exp\left(z_{1}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}+z_{2}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1-it}}\right)

can be well approximated by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(z_{1}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}+z_{2}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1-it}}\right)\right]

for all complex numbers
z_{1},z_{2}
satisfying
|z_{1}|,|z_{2}|\ll\frac{\log N}{(\log\log N)^{2}}
.
We mention here that widening the range of
|z_{1}|
and
|z_{2}|
in Proposition
4.2
would lead to an improvement in the discrepancy bound in Theorem
1.2
.
In view of the Taylor expansion of the exponential function, the proof of Proposition
4.2
requires upper bounds of integral moments of the partial sums (
1.6
) which we establish in Section
2
and Section
3
.

Conventions and Notation

Given two functions
f(x)
and
g(x)
, we shall interchangeably use the notation
f(x)=O(g(x))
and
f(x)\ll g(x)
to mean that there exists
M>0
such that
|f(x)|\leq M|g(x)|
for all sufficiently large
x
. We write
f(x)\asymp g(x)
to mean that the estimates
f(x)\ll g(x)
and
g(x)\ll f(x)
hold simultaneously. Sometimes we will use the notation
f(x)\ll_{t}g(x)
, or alternatively
f(x)=O_{t}(g(x))
to emphasize the dependence of the
O
-constant on the parameter
t
. Most of our
O
-constants depend on
\pi
and
t
, although we sometimes drop the subscript to simplify the exposition. We use the notation
f(x)=o(g(x))
if
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}f(x)/g(x)=0
.
Finally, the letter
p
will always be used to denote a prime number.

2.
Preliminary Results

In this section, we introduce some notation and preliminary results pertaining to the arithmetic setting of automorphic
L
-functions, quadratic twists and logarithmic derivatives.

2.1.
Prime Number Sums

The Generalized Ramanujan Conjecture (GRC) for a cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
asserts that
|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)|\leq 1
for all
j=1,\dots,d
and all primes
p
. The following condition which follows from GRC is known as Hypothesis H (see
[
R-S
, page 281]
).

Hypothesis H:
For any fixed
k\geq 2
, we have

\sum_{p}\frac{(\log{p})^{2}\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p^{k})\right|^{2}}{p^{k}}<\infty.

Observe that Hypothesis H holds if
\left|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)\right|\leq p^{\theta}
with
0\leq\theta<\frac{1}{4}
for all
j=1,2,\cdots,d
, which is the assumption we make in Theorem
1.2
.
In this work we make frequent use of the following prime number theorem for automorphic representations.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be cuspidal automorphic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
and
\mathrm{GL}_{d^{\prime}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
respectively, and assume that they satisfy Hypothesis
H
. Set

\theta_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}}(u)=\sum_{p\leq u}(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\overline{\lambda_{\pi^{\prime}}(p)}.

Then for any
0<\epsilon<1
, we have

(2.1)

\theta_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}}(u)=\begin{cases}\frac{u^{1+i\tau_{0}}}{1+i\tau_{0}}+O\left(\frac{u}{\log u}\right)&\;\text{if}\;\pi^{\prime}\cong\pi\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{i\tau_{0}}\;\text{for some}\;\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\\
O_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{u}{(\log u)^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{dd^{\prime}}}}\right)&\;\text{if}\;\pi^{\prime}\not\cong\pi\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{i\tau}\;\text{for any}\;\tau\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

If
d,d^{\prime}\leq 4
, then (
2.1
) is true without assuming Hypothesis
H
.

Remark 2.2
.

The first case of Theorem
2.1
can be found in
[
Wu-Ye
, Theorem 3]
. It follows from
[
L-Y
, Theorem 2.3]
and uses Hypothesis H to bound the contributions of composite prime powers in the sum
\sum_{n\leq u}\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi^{\prime}}(n)}
by
O\left(\frac{u}{\log u}\right)
. The condition that at least one of
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
is self dual in
[
L-Y
]
and
[
Wu-Ye
]
can be removed by applying the recent zero-free region result of Humphries and Thorner
[
H-T
, Theorem 2.1]
. The second case of Theorem
2.1
can be derived from
[
K-T
, Theorem 2.6]
by using Hypothesis H to bound the contribution of composite prime powers. We note that the exponent
\frac{1-\epsilon}{dd^{\prime}}
can be replaced by 1 if
\pi
or
\pi^{\prime}
is self-dual
[
Wu-Ye
, Theorem 3]
.

Using this theorem, we derive the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume Hypothesis H. As
X\to\infty
, we have

\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2}}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}=\frac{\log X}{X}+O\left(\frac{1}{X}\right).

Proof.

The proof follows from Theorem
2.1
and an application of Abel’s summation formula.∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume Hypothesis H. The following assertions hold.

(i)

If
\pi\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{i\tau_{0}}
for some
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2+2it}}\lambda_{\pi}(p)^{2}=\frac{\log X}{(1-2it+i\tau_{0})X^{1-2it+i\tau_{0}}}+O\left(\frac{1}{X}\right),

as
X\to\infty
.

(ii)

If
\pi\not\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{i\tau}
for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, then there exists
0<\alpha<1
such that

\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2+2it}}\lambda_{\pi}(p)^{2}\ll\frac{(\log X)^{\alpha}}{X},

as
X\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{t}=\pi\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{-it}
and
\tilde{\pi_{t}}
be its dual representation. Then
\lambda_{\tilde{\pi_{t}}}(p)=\overline{\lambda_{\pi_{t}}(p)}=\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(p)}p^{it}
. We have

\theta_{\pi_{t},\tilde{\pi_{t}}}(u)=\sum_{p\leq u}(\log p)\lambda_{\pi_{t}}(p)\overline{\lambda_{\tilde{\pi_{t}}}(p)}=\sum_{p\leq u}(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)^{2}p^{-2it}.

By Theorem
2.1
, for any
0<\epsilon<1
, we have

\theta_{\pi_{t},\tilde{\pi_{t}}}(u)=\sum_{p\leq u}(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)^{2}p^{-2it}=\begin{cases}\frac{u^{1+i\tau_{0}-2it}}{1+i\tau_{0}-2it}+O\left(\frac{u}{\log u}\right)&\;\text{if}\;\pi\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{i\tau_{0}}\;\text{for some}\;\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\\
O_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{u}{(\log u)^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{dd^{\prime}}}}\right)&\;\text{if}\;\pi\not\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{i\tau}\;\text{for any}\;\tau\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

If
\pi\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{i\tau_{0}}
for some
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2+2it}}\lambda_{\pi}(p)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{\log u}{u^{2}}\theta_{\pi_{t},\tilde{\pi_{t}}}(u)\right]_{X}^{\infty}+\int_{X}^{\infty}\theta_{\pi_{t},\tilde{\pi_{t}}}(u)\frac{2\log u-1}{u^{3}}\;du

\displaystyle=-\frac{\log X}{X^{2}}\left(\frac{X^{1+i\tau_{0}-2it}}{1+i\tau_{0}-2it}+O\left(\frac{X}{\log X}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{X}^{\infty}\left(\frac{u^{1+i\tau_{0}-2it}}{1+i\tau_{0}-2it}+O\left(\frac{u}{\log u}\right)\right)\frac{2\log u-1}{u^{3}}\;du

\displaystyle=\frac{\log X}{(1+i\tau_{0}-2it)X^{1-i\tau_{0}+2it}}+O\left(\frac{1}{X}\right).

If
\pi\not\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{i\tau}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2+2it}}\lambda_{\pi}(p)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{\log u}{u^{2}}\theta_{\pi_{t},\tilde{\pi_{t}}}(u)\right]_{X}^{\infty}+\int_{X}^{\infty}\theta_{\pi_{t},\tilde{\pi_{t}}}(u)\frac{2\log u-1}{u^{3}}\;du

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{(\log X)^{\alpha}}{X}\right),

where
\alpha
can be taken to be
1-\frac{1-\epsilon}{dd^{\prime}}
for any
0<\epsilon<1
.
∎

2.2.
Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions

For a pair of automorphic representations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
and
\mathrm{GL}_{d^{\prime}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
respectively, the associated Rankin-Selberg
L
-function is

L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=\prod_{p}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{d^{\prime}}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(n)}{n^{s}},

where
\Re(s)>1
and the parameters
\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p)
are indexed so that
\alpha_{j,j^{\prime},\pi\times\pi^{\prime}}(p)=\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)\alpha_{j^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}}(p)
for all but finitely many primes
p
.

We continue with the following two results from
[
S-T
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
S-T
, Lemma 2.2]

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. Then

|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{\pi\times\tilde{\pi}}(n)}\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+\lambda_{\pi\times\tilde{\pi}}(n)).

Lemma 2.6
.

[
S-T
, Theorem 2.4]

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. If
Y\gg_{d}C(\pi\times\tilde{\pi})^{36d^{2}}
and
1\leq H\leq Y^{\frac{1}{9d^{2}}}
, then

\sum_{Y<n\leq Ye^{\frac{1}{H}}}\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi\times\tilde{\pi}}(n)\ll_{d}\frac{Y}{H}.

Since
\lambda_{\pi\times\tilde{\pi}}(n)\geq 0
(see
[
R-S
, page 318]
), then as a direct corollary of the above theorem we have

\sum_{Y<n\leq Y+\frac{Y}{H}}\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi\times\tilde{\pi}}(n)\leq\sum_{Y<n\leq Ye^{\frac{1}{H}}}\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi\times\tilde{\pi}}(n)\ll_{d}\frac{Y}{H}

under the conditions of Lemma
2.6
on
Y
and
H
.

2.3.
Short Dirichlet polynomials

In order to prove that the values
\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})
can be approximated by short Dirichlet polynomials outside a set of fundamental discriminants
D
of size
o(N)
, we require the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
T>1
,
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma_{0}<1
, and
s=\sigma+it
. Suppose that
L(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})
has no zeros in the region
\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}
and
|t|\leq T
. Then for all
\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}
we have

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\ll\frac{\log(D(|t|+2))}{\sigma-\sigma_{0}}.

Proof.

The result follows by adapting the proof of
[
lamzouri3
, Lemma 2.2]
to the setting of quadratic twists of automorphic
L
-functions.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
Y\gg_{\pi}1
,
D\in\mathcal{F}(N)
, and
0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{3d^{2}}
.
Assume that

L(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})
has no zeros on
\Re(s)>1-\delta
and
|\Im(s)|\leq Y^{\frac{1}{3d^{2}}}
. Then, we have

(2.2)

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})=\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}+O\left(Y^{-\frac{\delta}{3}}(\log{N})\right).

Proof.

By
[
L-Y
, Theorem 2.1]
and for
c=1/\log{Y}
,
Y\geq 2
,
T\geq 2
, and
H\geq 2
, we have

(2.3)

\begin{split}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-iT}^{c+iT}-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(w+1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\frac{Y^{w}}{w}dw\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt+O\left(\sum_{Y-\frac{Y}{H}<n\leq Y+\frac{Y}{H}}\frac{\Lambda(n)|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{HB(c)}{T}\right),\end{split}

where
B(c)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)|\lambda(n)|}{n^{c+1}}.

By
[
L-Y
, Formula (6.3)]
we have
B(c)\ll\log{Y}
and thus, the last error term in (
2.3
) is

(2.4)

O\left(\frac{H\log{Y}}{T}\right).

To handle the first error term in (
2.3
) observe that by Lemma
2.5
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{Y-\frac{Y}{H}<n\leq Y+\frac{Y}{H}}\frac{\Lambda(n)|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|}{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{Y-\frac{Y}{H}<n\leq Y+\frac{Y}{H}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n}+\sum_{Y-\frac{Y}{H}<n\leq Y+\frac{Y}{H}}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi\times\tilde{\pi}}(n)}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Y}\left(\sum_{Y-\frac{Y}{H}<n\leq Y+\frac{Y}{H}}{\Lambda(n)}+\sum_{Y-\frac{Y}{H}<n\leq Y+\frac{Y}{H}}{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi\times\tilde{\pi}}(n)}\right).

Now if
1\leq H\leq Y^{\frac{1}{9d^{2}}}
, then by Lemma
2.6
and the Brun-Titchmarsh inequality (e.g. see
[
IK
, Theorem 6.6]
) applied to the sums involving
\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi\times\tilde{\pi}}
and
\Lambda(n)
respectively, we have

(2.5)

\sum_{Y-\frac{Y}{H}<n\leq Y+\frac{Y}{H}}\frac{\Lambda(n)|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|}{n}\ll\frac{1}{H}.

Next we deal with the integral in (
2.3
). For
\delta>0
assume that
L(w,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})
does not have any zeros in the box

R_{\delta}:=\{(u,v);~1-\delta\leq u\leq 1,~|v|\leq T\}.

Letting
c_{0}=-\frac{\delta}{2}
and applying the residue theorem yields

(2.6)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{c-iT}^{c+iT}-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(w+1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\frac{Y^{w}}{w}dw&=-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\int_{c-iT}^{c_{0}+iT}(\cdot)-\int_{c_{0}-iT}^{c+iT}(\cdot)-\int_{c_{0}+it}^{c_{0}-iT}(\cdot)\right).\end{split}

We now estimate the integrals in (
2.6
). By employing Lemma
2.7
we have

(2.7)

\begin{split}\left|\int_{c_{0}+iT}^{c_{0}-iT}-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(w+1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\frac{Y^{w}}{w}dw\right|&\ll\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{\log(|D|(|v|+2))}{(1-\frac{\delta}{2})-(1-\delta)}\frac{Y^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}}{\sqrt{v^{2}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}}}dv\\
&\ll_{\delta}Y^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\log(|D|(T+2))\log(T+2).\end{split}

For the two remaining integrals on the right-hand side of (
2.6
), by using Lemma
2.7
, we have

(2.8)

\begin{split}\left|\int_{c+iT}^{c_{0}+iT}-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(w+1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\frac{Y^{w}}{w}dw\right|&\ll\int_{-\frac{\delta}{2}}^{1/\log{Y}}\frac{\log(|D|(|T|+2))}{(1+u)-(1-\delta)}\frac{Y^{u}}{\sqrt{u^{2}+T^{2}}}du\\
&\ll_{\delta}\frac{\log(|D|(T+2))}{T\log{Y}}.\end{split}

Thus, from (
2.3
), (
2.4
), (
2.5
), (
2.6
), (
2.7
), and (
2.8
) and under the assumption that
L(w,\pi\times\chi_{D})
does not have any zero in
R_{\delta}
, we get

(2.9)

\begin{split}-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})&=\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}+O\left(\frac{H\log{Y}}{T}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{H}\right)\\
&+O\left(Y^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\log(|D|(T+2))\log(T+2)\right)+O\left(\frac{\log(|D|(T+2))}{T\log{Y}}\right),\end{split}

for
1\leq H\leq Y^{\frac{1}{9d^{2}}}
. The result follows by setting
H=Y^{\frac{1}{9d^{2}}}
and
T=Y^{\frac{1}{3d^{2}}}
in (
2.9
). ∎

We also require the following zero-density estimate which is a direct application of
[
H-T1
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
with unitary central character, and let
C(\pi)
be the analytic conductor of
\pi
(as defined in
[
IK
, page 95]
). Let
T,N\geq 1
, and set

N_{\pi\otimes\chi_{D}}(\sigma,T)=\left|\left\{\rho=\beta+i\gamma;~L(\rho,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})=0,~\beta\geq\sigma,~|\gamma|\leq T\right\}\right|.

For
\epsilon>0
, we have

(2.10)

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}N_{\pi\otimes\chi_{D}}(\sigma,T)\ll_{\epsilon,d}(C(\pi)NT)^{18d(1-\sigma)+\epsilon},

provided that
\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma\leq 1
.

Remark 2.10
.

An application of
[
H-T1
, Theorem 1.1]
will give a result similar to (
2.10
) for
N_{\pi\times\chi_{D}}(\sigma,T)
attached to the zeros of the Rankin-Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times\chi_{D})
.
We know that the local
L
-functions
L_{p}(s,\pi\times\chi_{D})
and
L_{p}(s,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})
coincide for primes
p\nmid(q_{\pi},D)
, where
q_{\pi}
is the conductor of
\pi
. Moreover, the local parameters
\alpha_{j,\pi\times\chi_{D}}
(for
1\leq j\leq d
) satisfy the bound (
1.2
). Therefore,
N_{\pi\times\chi_{D}}(\sigma,T)=N_{\pi\otimes\chi_{D}}(\sigma,T)
for
\sigma>\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{d^{2}+1}
and thus
(
2.10
)
holds.

We finally arrive at the desired approximation.

Proposition 2.11
.

There are positive constants
\delta_{\pi}
and
\eta_{\pi}
(depending only on
\pi
) such that for all but
O(N^{\frac{3}{4}})
fundamental discriminants
D
in
\mathcal{F}(N)
, we have

(2.11)

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})=\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}+O(Y^{-\delta_{\pi}}),

whenever
(\log{N})^{\eta_{\pi}}\ll Y\ll N^{3d^{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
T=Y^{\frac{1}{3d^{2}}}
where
Y\ll N^{3d^{2}}
,
0<\delta<\min\{\frac{1}{144d},\frac{1}{3d^{2}}\}
, and
\epsilon=\frac{1}{4}
. Then as a direct corollary of Lemmas
2.8
and
2.9
we conclude that (
2.2
) holds for all but
O(N^{\frac{3}{4}})
fundamental discriminants in
\mathcal{F}(N)
.
∎

In the rest of the paper, we shall denote by
\mathcal{A}(N)
the subset of
\mathcal{F}(N)
for which (
2.11
) holds for some
\delta_{\pi}
and
\eta_{\pi}
. We also define
\mathcal{E}(N)
by writing
\mathcal{A}(N)=\mathcal{F}(N)\setminus\mathcal{E}(N)
.

2.4.
More Lemmas

In what follows, we compute upper bounds for
2k
-th moments of sums associated with the short Dirichlet polynomials appearing in Proposition
2.11
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
2\leq y\leq z
. Then, uniformly for
k\leq\frac{{\log{N}}}{{{6}\log{z}}}
, we have

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left|\sum_{y\leq p\leq z}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right|^{2k}\ll k!\left(\sum_{y\leq p\leq z}\frac{(\log{p})^{2}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2}}\right)^{k}.

Proof.

The proof closely follows
[
S
, Lemma 3]
. We have

\left(\sum_{y\leq p\leq x}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right)^{k}=\sum_{y^{k}\leq n\leq z^{k}}\frac{a_{k,y,z}(n)}{n^{1+it}},

where

a_{k,y,z}(n)=\begin{cases}{{k}\choose{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r}}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}((\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p))^{\alpha_{i}}&\text{if}~n=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{\alpha_{r}},~p_{i}^{\prime}s~\text{distinct},~y\leq p_{i}\leq z,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus, we get

(2.12)

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left|\sum_{y\leq p\leq z}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right|^{2k}=\sum_{y^{k}\leq m,n\leq z^{k}}\frac{a_{k,y,z}(m)\overline{a_{k,y,z}(n)}}{(mn)^{1+it}}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\chi_{D}(mn)\right).

From
[
GS
, Lemma 4.1]
we know that if
mn
is not a perfect square, then

(2.13)

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\chi_{D}(mn)\ll N^{\frac{1}{2}}(mn)^{\frac{1}{4}}(\log{(mn}))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Applying this upper bound in (
2.12
) yields

(2.14)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left|\sum_{y\leq p\leq z}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right|^{2k}&\ll\sum_{y^{k}\leq n\leq z^{k}}\frac{|a_{k,y,z}(n)|^{2}}{n^{2}}\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{k}\leq m,n\leq z^{k}\\
mn\neq\square\end{subarray}}\frac{|a_{k,y,z}(m)||a_{k,y,z}(n)|}{(mn)^{\frac{3}{4}}}(\log(mn))^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

Observe that

2\frac{|a_{k,y,z}(m)||a_{k,y,z}(n)|}{(mn)^{\frac{3}{4}}}\leq\frac{|a_{k,y,z}(m)|^{2}}{m^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{|a_{k,y,z}(n)|^{2}}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

By application of this inequality in the last term of (
2.14
), we have

(2.15)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left|\sum_{y\leq p\leq z}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right|^{2k}&\ll\sum_{y^{k}\leq n\leq z^{k}}\frac{|a_{k,y,z}(n)|^{2}}{n^{2}}\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{y^{k}\leq n\leq z^{k}}\frac{|a_{k,y,z}(n)|^{2}}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{k}\leq m\leq z^{k}\\
mn\neq\square\end{subarray}}(\log(mn))^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

We deduce from (
2.15
) that

(2.16)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left|\sum_{y\leq p\leq z}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right|^{2k}&\ll\sum_{y^{k}\leq n\leq z^{k}}\frac{|a_{k,y,z}(n)|^{2}}{n^{2}}\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}z^{\frac{3k}{2}}(\log(z^{2k}))^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{y^{k}\leq n\leq z^{k}}\frac{|a_{k,y,z}(n)|^{2}}{n^{{2}}}\\
&\ll\sum_{y^{k}\leq n\leq z^{k}}\frac{|a_{k,y,z}(n)|^{2}}{n^{2}}\end{split}

since
k\leq(\log{N})/(6\log{z})
. The desired result follows from (
2.16
) since by an argument similar to the one given in the proof of
[
S
, Lemma 3]
we have

\sum_{y^{k}\leq n\leq z^{k}}\frac{|a_{k,y,z}(n)|^{2}}{n^{2}}\leq k!\left(\sum_{y\leq p\leq z}\frac{(\log{p})^{2}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2}}\right)^{k}.

∎

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
A\geq 1
be fixed and set
Y=(\log N)^{A}
. Let
k
be an integer satisfying
2\leq k\leq\frac{\log N}{6A\log\log N}
. Under the assumption of Hypothesis H, we have

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left|\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}\right|^{2k}\ll\left(C\log k\right)^{2k}

for some positive constant
C
that depends only on
\pi
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left|\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}\right|^{2k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\Bigg|\sum_{p\leq\tfrac{k}{\log k}}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt+\sum_{\tfrac{k}{\log k}<p\leq Y}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 2\\
p^{n}\leq Y\end{subarray}}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p^{n})\chi_{D}(p^{n})}{p^{n+int}}\Bigg|^{2k}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left|\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}\right|^{2k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9^{k}}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left|\sum_{p\leq\tfrac{k}{\log k}}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right|^{2k}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{9^{k}}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left|\sum_{\tfrac{k}{\log k}<p\leq Y}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right|^{2k}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{9^{k}}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 2\\
p^{n}\leq Y\end{subarray}}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p^{n})\chi_{D}(p^{n})}{p^{n+int}}\right|^{2k}.

By Lemma
2.12
, we know that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left|\sum_{\frac{k}{\log k}\leq p\leq Y}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right|^{2k}\ll k!\left(\sum_{\frac{k}{\log k}\leq p\leq Y}\frac{(\log{p})^{2}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2}}\right)^{k}.

Hence,

(2.17)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left|\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n}\right|^{2k}&\ll 9^{k}\left(\sum_{p\leq\frac{k}{\log k}}\frac{(\log p)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p)\right|}{p}\right)^{2k}\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt+9^{k}k!\left(\sum_{\frac{k}{\log k}\leq p\leq Y}\frac{(\log{p})^{2}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2}}\right)^{k}\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt+9^{k}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 2\\
p^{n}\leq Y\end{subarray}}\frac{(\log p)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p^{n})\right|}{p^{n}}\right)^{2k}.\end{split}

We have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{p\leq\frac{k}{\log k}}\frac{(\log p)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p)\right|}{p}\right)^{2k}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{p\leq\frac{k}{\log k}}\frac{\log p}{p}\right)^{k}\left(\sum_{p\leq\frac{k}{\log k}}\frac{(\log p)|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}}{p}\right)^{k}\leq(C_{1}\log(k/\log k))^{2k},

where
C_{1}
is a positive constant that depends only on
\pi
, and for the last inequality we use
[
A-S
, page 150]
to bound
\sum_{p\leq\frac{k}{\log k}}\frac{(\log p)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p)\right|^{2}}{p}
.

Now Abel’s summation formula yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\frac{k}{\log k}\leq p\leq Y}\frac{(\log{p})^{2}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2}}

\displaystyle=A(Y)\frac{\log Y}{Y^{2}}-A(k/\log k)\frac{\log(k/\log k)}{(k/\log k)^{2}}-\int_{\frac{k}{\log k}}^{Y}A(t)\left(\frac{1-2\log t}{t^{3}}\right)\;dt,

where
A(t)=\sum_{p\leq t}\log p\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p)\right|^{2}
. Since
A(t)\ll t
(see
[
A-S
, page 150]
), with our choices for
k
and
Y
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\frac{k}{\log k}\leq p\leq Y}\frac{(\log{p})^{2}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2}}\leq C_{2}\frac{\log(k/\log k)}{k/\log k},

for some positive constant
C_{2}
that depends only on
\pi
. Hence, the second sum on the RHS of (
2.17
) is bounded by

\frac{k!}{k^{k}}\left(9C_{2}\log k\log(k/\log k)\right)^{k}\leq\left(9C_{2}\log^{2}k\right)^{k}.

For the last sum in (
2.17
), we use the inequality

(2.18)

|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|^{2})

and
[
A-S
, Equation (2)]
to get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 2\\
p^{n}\leq Y\end{subarray}}\frac{(\log p)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p^{n})\right|}{p^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 2\\
p^{n}\leq Y\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p^{n}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 2\\
p^{n}\leq Y\end{subarray}}\frac{(\log p)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p^{n})\right|^{2}}{p^{n}}

\displaystyle=O(1).

In this argument, Hypothesis H is required for the application of
[
A-S
, Equation (2)]
.
Combining all these estimates gives the desired result.
∎

We end this section by providing an upper bound for the second moment of the values
\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})
as
D
varies in the set
\mathcal{A}(N)
which was introduced after Proposition
2.11
. This result is used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
in Section
7
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Assume Hypothesis
H
. As
N\to\infty
, we have

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}(N)|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{A}(N)}\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right|^{2}\ll_{\pi}1.

Proof.

Let
D\in\mathcal{A}(N)
. By Proposition
2.11
, there exist
\delta_{\pi},\eta_{\pi}>0
such that

(2.19)

\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right|^{2}=\left|\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}}{n^{1+it}}\right|^{2}+O\left(Y^{-\delta_{\pi}}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(n)\right|}{n}\right)+O(Y^{-2\delta_{\pi}}),

provided that
(\log N)^{\eta_{\pi}}\ll Y\ll N^{3d^{2}}
.
It follows from (
2.18
) and (
2.19
) that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{n}\leq Y\\
n\geq 2\end{subarray}}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p^{n})\chi_{D}(p^{n})}{p^{n+nit}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+O\left(Y^{-\delta_{\pi}}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n}\right)+O\left(Y^{-\delta_{\pi}}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(n)\right|^{2}}{n}\right)+O(Y^{-2\delta_{\pi}}).

Since
\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n}
and
\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(n)\right|^{2}}{n}
are both
O(\log Y)
by Mertens’ bound and
[
A-S
, Equation (3)]
respectively, we get

\displaystyle\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\left|\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right|^{2}+\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{n}\leq Y\\
n\geq 2\end{subarray}}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p^{n})\chi_{D}(p^{n})}{p^{n+nit}}\right|^{2}+O(Y^{-\nu_{\pi}}),

for some
\nu_{\pi}>0
depending on
\pi
. Assuming Hypothesis H, by (
2.18
) we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{n}\leq Y\\
n\geq 2\end{subarray}}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p^{n})\chi_{D}(p^{n})}{p^{n+nit}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{n}\leq Y\\
n\geq 2\end{subarray}}\frac{(\log p)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p^{n})\right|}{p^{n}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{n}\leq Y\\
n\geq 2\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p^{n}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{n}\leq Y\\
n\geq 2\end{subarray}}\frac{(\log p)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p^{n})\right|^{2}}{p^{n}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll_{\pi}1.

Hence, under Hypothesis H we have

\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right|^{2}\ll\left|\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right|^{2}+O_{\pi}(1).

Taking the average over
\mathcal{A}(N)
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}(N)|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{A}(N)}\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}(N)|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{A}(N)}\left|\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\chi_{D}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right|^{2}+O_{\pi}(1)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{(\log p)^{2}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2}}+O_{\pi}(1).

Since, by (
1.2
),
|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|\ll p^{\theta}
with
0\leq\theta<\frac{1}{2}
, from the above we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}(N)|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{A}(N)}\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right|^{2}\ll_{\pi}1

as desired.
∎

3.
The Random Model

Recall the definition of the random model
\mathbb{X}=\{\mathbb{X}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
given by

\mathbb{X}_{n}=\prod_{p\mid n}\mathbb{X}_{p}^{\nu_{p}(n)},

where
\nu_{p}(n)
is the
p
-adic valuation of
n
, and
\{\mathbb{X}_{p}\}_{p\;\text{prime}}
is the sequence of independent random variables given by

(3.1)

\mathbb{P}\big(\mathbb{X}_{p}=a\big)=\begin{cases}\frac{p}{2(p+1)}&\text{if $a=\pm 1$},\\
\frac{1}{p+1}&\text{if $a=0$}.\end{cases}

The random variables
\mathbb{X}_{n}
satisfy

(3.2)

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{X}_{n}\right]=\begin{cases}\prod_{p\mid n}\Big(\frac{p}{p+1}\Big)&\text{if $n$ is a square,}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The random sum
-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})
given by

(3.3)

-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}

can be written as
\sum_{p}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}}{p^{1+it}}+O(1)
, and the latter sum is almost surely convergent by the Menshov-Rademacher Theorem (see for example
[
kowalski
, Proposition B.10.5]
).
Moreover, the random sum

(3.4)

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{p}\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}\log p}{p^{1+it}-\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}}

is almost surely convergent by Kolmogorov’s Theorem (see for example
[
kowalski
, Proposition B.10.1]
). More generally, let
\tau>1-\frac{1}{d^{2}+1}
, and let
U_{\tau}=\{s\in\mathbb{C};\Re(s)>\tau\}
. It follows from the Menshov-Rademacher theorem that the random series

(3.5)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{s}}

is almost surely convergent on
U_{\tau}
, and so it defines an almost surely holomorphic function there. We also consider the random series

(3.6)

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{p}\frac{(\log p)\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}}{p^{s}-\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}},

which, by Kolmogorov’s theorem,
is almost surely convergent on
U_{\tau}
, and so it defines a holomorphic function there. One could easily verify that the series (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) are equal for all
s
with
\Re(s)>1
. By analytic continuation, we see that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{s}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{p}\frac{(\log p)\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}}{p^{s}-\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}}

almost surely in
U_{\tau}
.
In particular, we have

-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{p}\frac{(\log p)\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}}{p^{1+it}-\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}}.

In what follows we will be considering the partial random sums

\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}.

We need the following lemmas involving sums of the above form.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
2\leq y\leq z
. Then, uniformly for any positive integer
k
we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{y\leq p\leq z}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}}{p^{1+it}}\right|^{2k}\right]\ll k!\left(\sum_{y\leq p\leq z}\frac{(\log{p})^{2}|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2}}\right)^{k}.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is similar to the proof of Lemma
2.12
where we use (
3.2
) in lieu of the Polya-Vinogradov type inequality (
2.13
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A\geq 1
be fixed and set
Y=(\log N)^{A}
. Let
k\geq 2
be any integer. Under Hypothesis H, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right|^{2k}\right]\ll\left(C\log k\right)^{2k},

for some positive constant
C
that depends only on
\pi
.

Proof.

This lemma follows an argument similar to the one used in the proof of Lemma
2.13
. We use Lemma
3.1
in lieu of Lemma
2.12
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
|\lambda_{\pi}(p)|\ll p^{\theta}
for some
0\leq\theta<\frac{1}{2}
. Let
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}-\theta
be given. Then if
u
and
v
are real numbers such that
|u|+|v|\leq Y^{\frac{1}{2}-(\theta+\epsilon)}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\right)+iv\Im\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right)+iv\Im\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right)\right)\right]+O\left(\frac{|u|+|v|}{Y^{\frac{1}{2}-(\theta+\epsilon)}}\right).

Proof.

To simplify the exposition, we demonstrate the argument for
\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\right)\right)\right]
rather than
\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\right)+iv\Im\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\right)\right)\right]
, for otherwise the expressions would become quite lengthy.
We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(\sum_{p^{m}\leq Y}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p^{m})\mathbb{X}_{p^{m}}}{p^{m(1+it)}}+\sum_{p>Y}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}}{p^{1+it}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 2\\
p^{m}>Y\end{subarray}}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p^{m})\mathbb{X}_{p^{m}}}{p^{m(1+it)}}\right)\right)\right].

It follows that, for
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 2\\
p^{m}>Y\end{subarray}}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p^{m})\mathbb{X}_{p^{m}}}{p^{m(1+it)}}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{m\geq 2}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{p>Y^{\frac{1}{m}}}\frac{1}{p^{m(1-\theta-\epsilon)}}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{m\geq 2}\frac{1}{m}\left(\frac{Y^{\frac{1}{m}-(1-\theta-\epsilon)}}{m(1-\theta-\epsilon)-1}\right)

\displaystyle\ll Y^{\theta+\epsilon-1}Y^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{m\geq 2}\frac{1}{m^{2}}\ll\frac{Y^{\theta+\epsilon}}{Y^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Hence,

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\right)\right)\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(\sum_{p^{m}\leq Y}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p^{m})\mathbb{X}_{p^{m}}}{p^{m(1+it)}}+\sum_{p>Y}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}}{p^{1+it}}\right)+O\left(\frac{|u|}{Y^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta-\epsilon}}\right)\right)\right].\end{split}

Now if
|u|\leq Y^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta-\epsilon}
and
p>Y
, then

(3.8)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}}{p^{1+it}}\right)\right)\right]&=\mathbb{E}\left[1+iu\Re\left(\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}}{p^{1+it}}\right)+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{(iu)^{m}\Re^{m}\left(\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p)\mathbb{X}_{p}}{p^{1+it}}\right)}{m!}\right]\\
&=1+O\left(\frac{(\log p)|u|^{2}}{p^{2-2\theta}}\right).\end{split}

It follows from (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) that

(3.9)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\right)\right)\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(\sum_{p^{m}\leq Y}\frac{(\log p)\lambda_{\pi}(p^{m})\mathbb{X}_{p^{m}}}{p^{m(1+it)}}\right)+\sum_{p>Y}\log\left(1+O\left(\frac{(\log p)|u|^{2}}{p^{2-2\theta}}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{|u|}{Y^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta-\epsilon}}\right)\right)\right].\end{split}

Now since

\sum_{p>Y}\log\left(1+O\left(\frac{(\log p)|u|^{2}}{p^{2-2\theta}}\right)\right)\ll|u|^{2}Y^{-1+2\theta+\epsilon}\ll|u|Y^{-\frac{1}{2}+\theta},

by (
3.9
), we get

(3.10)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\right)\right)\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right)+O\left(\frac{|u|}{Y^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta-\epsilon}}\right)\right)\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right)\right)\right]+O\left(\frac{|u|}{Y^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta-\epsilon}}\right).\end{split}

One can easily check that the above argument applied to

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\right)+iv\Im\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\right)\right)\right]

yields the desired result.
∎

4.
Bridging Lemmas

In this section, we prove two results which serve a crucial role as a bridge between the arithmetic setting and the probabilistic random setting developed in the previous sections.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A\geq 1
be fixed and set
Y=(\log N)^{A}
. Then for any positive integers
j,\ell
such that
j+\ell\leq\frac{\log N}{6A\log\log N}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}\right)^{j}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1-it}}\right)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right)^{j}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1-it}}\right)^{\ell}\right]+O\left(\frac{(Y^{\frac{1}{4}}\log Y)^{j+\ell}(\log N)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right).

Proof.

We have

(4.1)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}\right)^{j}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1-it}}\right)^{\ell}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}^{m_{1}},\cdots,p_{j}^{m_{j}}\leq Y\\
q_{1}^{n_{1}},\cdots,q_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}}\leq Y\end{subarray}}\frac{\left(\log p_{1}\right)\cdots\left(\log p_{j}\right)\left(\log q_{1}\right)\cdots\left(\log q_{\ell}\right)\lambda_{\pi}(p_{1}^{m_{1}})\cdots\lambda_{\pi}(p_{j}^{m_{j}})\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(q_{1}^{n_{1}})}\cdots\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(q_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}})}}{p_{1}^{m_{1}(1+it)}\cdots p_{j}^{m_{j}(1+it)}q_{1}^{n_{1}(1-it)}\cdots q_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}(1-it)}}\\
&\hskip 50.00008pt\times\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\chi_{D}\left(p_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots p_{j}^{m_{j}}q_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots q_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}}\right).\end{split}

Note that

(4.2)

\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\chi_{D}(m^{2})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\in\mathcal{F}(N)\\
(D,m)=1\end{subarray}}1=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}N\prod_{p|m}\bigg(\frac{p}{p+1}\bigg)+O\big(N^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau(m)\big),

where
\tau(m)
is the divisor function (see for example
[
GS
, page 1017]
). In view of (
3.2
) and (
4.2
), we see that (
4.1
) equals

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right)^{j}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1-it}}\right)^{\ell}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}^{m_{1}},\cdots,p_{j}^{m_{j}}\leq Y\\
q_{1}^{n_{1}},\cdots,q_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}}\leq Y\\
p_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots p_{j}^{m_{j}}q_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots q_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}}\neq\square\end{subarray}}\frac{\prod_{s=1}^{j}\left(\log p_{s}\right)\lambda_{\pi}(p_{s}^{m_{s}})\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}\left(\log q_{s}\right)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(q_{s}^{n_{s}})}}{p_{1}^{m_{1}(1+it)}\cdots p_{j}^{m_{j}(1+it)}q_{1}^{n_{1}(1-it)}\cdots q_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}(1-it)}}

\displaystyle\hskip 80.00012pt\times\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\chi_{D}\left(p_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots p_{j}^{m_{j}}q_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots q_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}}\right)+O\left(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Let us now analyze the contribution of non-squares. By (
2.13
), we get

(4.3)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}^{m_{1}},\cdots,p_{j}^{m_{j}}\leq Y\\
q_{1}^{n_{1}},\cdots,q_{k}^{n_{\ell}}\leq Y\\
p_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots p_{j}^{m_{j}}q_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots q_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}}\neq\square\end{subarray}}\frac{\prod_{s=1}^{j}\left(\log p_{s}\right)\lambda_{\pi}(p_{s}^{m_{s}})\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}\left(\log q_{s}\right)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(q_{s}^{n_{s}})}}{p_{1}^{m_{1}(1+it)}\cdots p_{j}^{m_{j}(1+it)}q_{1}^{n_{1}(1-it)}\cdots q_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}(1-it)}}\\
&\hskip 50.00008pt\times\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\chi_{D}\left(p_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots p_{j}^{m_{j}}q_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots q_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}}\right)\\
&\ll\frac{((j+\ell)\log Y)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots p_{j}^{m_{j}}\leq Y\\
q_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots q_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}}\leq Y\\
p_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots p_{j}^{m_{j}}q_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots q_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}}\neq\square\end{subarray}}\frac{\prod_{s=1}^{j}\left(\log p_{s}\right)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p_{s}^{m_{s}})\right|\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}\left(\log q_{s}\right)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(q_{s}^{n_{s}})\right|}{p_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}m_{1}}\cdots p_{j}^{\frac{3}{4}m_{j}}q_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}n_{1}}\cdots q_{\ell}^{\frac{3}{4}n_{\ell}}}.\end{split}

Observe that (
4.3
) is

\displaystyle\ll\frac{((j+\ell)\log Y)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\sum_{p^{m}\leq Y}\frac{\log p\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p^{m})\right|}{p^{\frac{3}{4}m}}\right)^{j+\ell}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{((j+\ell)\log Y)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\sum_{p^{m}\leq Y}\frac{\log p\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p^{m})\right|^{2}}{p^{m}}\right)^{\frac{j+\ell}{2}}\left(\sum_{p^{m}\leq Y}\frac{\log p}{p^{\frac{m}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{j+\ell}{2}}.

Since
\sum_{p^{m}\leq Y}\frac{\log p\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p^{m})\right|^{2}}{p^{m}}=O(\log Y)
(see
[
A-S
, Equation (3)]
) and
\sum_{p^{m}\leq Y}\frac{\log p}{p^{\frac{m}{2}}}=O(Y^{\frac{1}{2}}\log Y)
, we conclude that (
4.3
) is

\displaystyle\ll(Y^{\frac{1}{4}}\log Y)^{j+\ell}\frac{((j+\ell)\log Y)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{(Y^{\frac{1}{4}}\log Y)^{j+\ell}(\log N)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
A\geq 1
and
Y=(\log N)^{A}
. Assume Hypothesis H. Then there exist positive constants
b_{0}=b_{0}(A)
and
c_{0}=c_{0}(A)
such that for all complex numbers
z_{1},z_{2}
with
|z_{1}|,|z_{2}|\leq c_{0}\frac{\log N}{(\log\log N)^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\exp\left(z_{1}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}+z_{2}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1-it}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(z_{1}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}+z_{2}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1-it}}\right)\right]+O\left(\exp\left(-b_{0}\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}\right)\right).

Proof.

Let
k=\max(|z_{1}|,|z_{2}|)
and
M=\left[\frac{\log N}{c\log\log N}\right]
, where
c
is a suitably large positive constant. We have

(4.4)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\exp\left(z_{1}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}+z_{2}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1-it}}\right)\\
&=\sum_{j+\ell\leq M}\frac{z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{\ell}}{j!\ell!}\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}\right)^{j}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1-it}}\right)^{\ell}+E_{1},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j+\ell>M}\frac{z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{\ell}}{j!\ell!}\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}\right)^{j}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1-it}}\right)^{\ell}.

Observe that for
Y=(\log N)^{A}
we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(n)\right|}{n}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\left|\lambda_{\pi}(n)\right|^{2}}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\log\log N,

where
C_{0}
is a positive constant that depends only on A and
\pi
.
Hence,

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{j+\ell>M}\frac{k^{j+\ell}}{j!\ell!}\left(C_{0}\log\log N\right)^{j+\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n>M}\frac{(2C_{0}k\log\log N)^{n}}{n!},

where we used the identity
\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}=2^{n}
. By Stirling’s inequality
\frac{1}{n!}\leq\left(\frac{e}{n}\right)^{n}
, we get

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n>M}\left(\frac{2C_{0}ek\log\log N}{n}\right)^{n}.

Assuming
k\leq c_{0}\frac{\log N}{(\log\log N)^{2}}
, where we choose
c_{0}\leq\frac{e^{-1}}{6cC_{0}}
, we deduce that

(4.5)

\displaystyle E_{1}\ll\sum_{n>M}(6cc_{0}C_{0})^{n}\ll e^{-M}.

To analyze the main term of (
4.4
), note that, by Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}\right)^{j}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1-it}}\right)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right)^{j}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1-it}}\right)^{\ell}\right]+O\left(\frac{(Y^{\frac{1}{4}}\log Y)^{j+\ell}(\log N)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

for
j+\ell\leq M
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\exp\left(z_{1}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}+z_{2}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1-it}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j+\ell\leq M}\frac{z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{\ell}}{j!\ell!}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right)^{j}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1-it}}\right)^{\ell}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+O\left(\sum_{j+\ell\leq M}\frac{z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{\ell}}{j!\ell!}\frac{(Y^{\frac{1}{4}}\log Y)^{j+\ell}(\log N)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right).

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{j+\ell\leq M}\frac{z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{\ell}}{j!\ell!}\frac{(Y^{\frac{1}{4}}\log Y)^{j+\ell}(\log N)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(Y^{\frac{1}{4}}\log Y)^{M}(\log N)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{n\leq M}\frac{(2k)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle\ll Y^{-\epsilon M}\exp(2k)

\displaystyle\ll\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon A}{c}\log N+2c_{0}\frac{\log N}{(\log\log N)^{2}}\right),

provided that
N^{\frac{1}{2}}\gg Y^{(1+\epsilon)M}
which is possible by choosing
c
suitably large. It follows that

(4.6)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\exp\left(z_{1}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}+z_{2}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1-it}}\right)\\
&=\sum_{j+\ell\leq M}\frac{z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{\ell}}{j!\ell!}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right)^{j}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1-it}}\right)^{\ell}\right]\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt+O\left(\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon A}{c}\log N+2c_{0}\frac{\log N}{(\log\log N)^{2}}\right)\right).\end{split}

Now the main term in (
4.6
) can be written as

(4.7)

\begin{split}&\sum_{j+\ell\leq M}\frac{z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{\ell}}{j!\ell!}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right)^{j}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1-it}}\right)^{\ell}\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(z_{1}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right)+z_{2}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1-it}}\right)\right)\right]+E_{2},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j+\ell>M}\frac{z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{\ell}}{j!\ell!}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right)^{j}\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1-it}}\right)^{\ell}\right].

Observe that

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{j+\ell>M}\frac{k^{j+\ell}}{j!\ell!}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right|^{j+\ell}\right]

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{j+\ell>M}\frac{k^{j+\ell}}{j!\ell!}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}\right|^{2(j+\ell)}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using Lemma
3.2
we get

E_{2}\ll\sum_{m>M}\frac{(2k)^{m}}{m!}\left(C\log m\right)^{m}.

By Stirling’s formula and our choices for
k
and
M
, we deduce that

(4.8)

E_{2}\ll\sum_{m>M}\left(\frac{6Ck\log m}{m}\right)^{m}\ll\sum_{m>M}\left(\frac{6Cc_{0}\log N\log M}{M(\log\log N)^{2}}\right)^{m}\ll\sum_{m>M}(6Ccc_{0})^{m}\ll e^{-M},

where the last inequality follows from choosing

.

From (
4.4
), (
4.5
), (
4.6
), (
4.7
), and (
4.8
) we obtain the desired result.
∎

5.
The Characteristic Function

We set

\Phi_{\mathcal{F}(N)}(u,v):=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{F}(N)}\exp\left(iu\Re\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right)+iv\Im\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right)\right)

and

\Phi_{\mathcal{A}(N)}(u,v):=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{A}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{A}(N)}\exp\left(iu\Re\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right)+iv\Im\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right)\right),

where
\mathcal{A}(N)
is the set of fundamental discriminants introduced after Proposition
2.11
.
We also define

\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u,v):=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(iu\Re\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\right)+iv\Im\left(-\ld(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})\right)\right)\right].

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
A\geq 1
be fixed. Under the assumption of the Hypothesis H, there exists a positive constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(A)
such that for
|u|,|v|\leq c_{0}\frac{\log N}{(\log\log N)^{2}}
, we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathcal{F}(N)}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u,v)+O\left(\frac{1}{(\log\log{N})^{2}(\log N)^{A}}\right).

Moreover, the asymptotic formula (
5.1
) holds if we replace
\Phi_{\mathcal{F}(N)}(u,v)
by
\Phi_{\mathcal{A}(N)}(u,v)
.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.11
we know that for all but
O(N^{\frac{3}{4}})
elements
D\in\mathcal{F}(N)
, there are positive constants
\delta_{\pi}
and
\eta_{\pi}
such that for

, we have

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})=\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{1+it}}+O(Y^{-\delta_{\pi}}).

Recall that
\mathcal{A}(N)
is the set of elements for which (

) holds. Let

be such that

(such

\begin{split}\Phi_{\mathcal{F}(N)}(u,v)&=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{F}(N)\right|}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{A}(N)}\exp\left(iu\Re\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right)+iv\Im\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right)\right)\\

with

\begin{split}\Phi_{\mathcal{F}(N)}(u,v)&=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(z_{1}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1+it}}+z_{2}\sum_{n\leq Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\overline{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}\mathbb{X}_{n}}{n^{1-it}}\right)\right]\\

∎

6.
The Exponential Decay

In this section we prove a decay bound for
\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u,v)
. Recall that we are working under the assumption that the distributions are 2-dimensional, hence,
\pi\not\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{2it}
. The following proposition implies among other things that the distribution function associated with our random series admits a smooth density function.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
z=u+iv
and assume that
\left|\lambda_{\pi}(p)\right|\ll p^{\theta}
for some
0\leq\theta<\frac{1}{4}
. Let
0<\epsilon<2-8\theta
be given. Then there exist positive constants
C_{\epsilon}
and
a_{\epsilon}
depending only on
\epsilon
such that for
|z|
large enough we have

\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u,v)\ll\exp\left(-C_{\epsilon}|z|^{a_{\epsilon}}\right).

Proof.

By employing (
3.1
), we get that

\Phi_{\mathrm{rand}}(u,v)=\prod_{p}M_{p}(u,v),

where

\displaystyle M_{p}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{p}{2(p+1)}\exp\left(i\left(\log p\right)\Re\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\overline{z}\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}{p^{1+it}-\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\frac{p}{2(p+1)}\exp\left(-i\left(\log p\right)\Re\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\overline{z}\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}{p^{1+it}+\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}\right)\right).

We observe that
\left|\prod_{p}M_{p}(u,v)\right|\leq\prod_{p>X}\left|M_{p}(u,v)\right|
since
|M_{p}(u,v)|\leq 1
for all
p
. Moreover,

\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}{p^{1+it}-\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}-\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}{p^{1+it}}=\frac{\alpha^{2}_{j,\pi}(p)}{p^{1+it}(p^{1+it}-\alpha_{j,\pi}(p))}=O\left(\frac{|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2}}\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle M_{p}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{p}{2(p+1)}\exp\left(i\left(\log p\right)\Re\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\overline{z}\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\frac{p}{2(p+1)}\exp\left(-i\left(\log p\right)\Re\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\overline{z}\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{(\log p)|z|}{p^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)|^{2}\right).

Next by using the Taylor expansion of the exponential function and simplifying the resulting expressions, from the above we get

\displaystyle M_{p}(u,v)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{p}{2(p+1)}(\log p)^{2}\Re^{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\overline{z}\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+O\left(\frac{|z|^{4}(\log p)^{4}}{p^{4}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)|^{4}+\frac{(\log p)|z|}{p^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)|^{2}\right),

provided that
\left(\log p\right)\left|\Re\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\overline{z}\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right)\right|<1
. Hence, for sufficiently large
X
, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{p>X}M_{p}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p>X}\exp\Bigg(\log\Bigg(1-\frac{p}{2(p+1)}(\log p)^{2}\Re^{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\overline{z}\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+O\left(\frac{|z|^{4}(\log p)^{4}}{p^{4}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)|^{4}+\frac{(\log p)|z|}{p^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)|^{2}\right)\Bigg)\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Bigg(-\sum_{p>X}\frac{p}{2(p+1)}(\log p)^{2}\Re^{2}\left(\frac{\overline{z}a_{\pi}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+O\left(|z|^{4}\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{4}}{p^{4}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)|^{4}+|z|\sum_{p>X}\frac{\log p}{p^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)|^{2}\right)\Bigg),

where in the above estimations we used
\log(1-x)\leq-x
for all
0<x<1
. In order to estimate this expression, we need to first consider the sum
\displaystyle{\sum_{p>X}\frac{p}{2(p+1)}(\log p)^{2}\Re^{2}\left(\frac{\overline{z}a_{\pi}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\right)}
. To simplify exposition, we will consider instead the sum
\displaystyle{\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2}}\Re^{2}\left(\frac{\overline{z}a_{\pi}(p)}{p^{it}}\right)}
. Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2}}\Re^{2}\left(\frac{\overline{z}a_{\pi}(p)}{p^{it}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2}}|a_{\pi}(p)|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\frac{1}{4}\overline{z}^{2}\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2+2it}}a_{\pi}(p)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}z^{2}\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2-2it}}\overline{a_{\pi}(p)}^{2}.

Note that by Lemma
2.3
we have

\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2}}|a_{\pi}(p)|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}\left(\frac{\log X}{X}\right)+O\left(\frac{|z|^{2}}{X}\right).

If
\pi\not\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{i\tau}
for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, then Lemma
2.4
gives

\frac{1}{4}\overline{z}^{2}\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2+2it}}a_{\pi}(p)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}z^{2}\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2-2it}}\overline{a_{\pi}(p)}^{2}\ll|z|^{2}\frac{(\log X)^{\alpha}}{X},

for some
0<\alpha<1
, and so

\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2}}\Re^{2}\left(\frac{\overline{z}a_{\pi}(p)}{p^{it}}\right)\asymp|z|^{2}\frac{\log X}{X}.

If
\pi\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{i\tau_{0}}
for some
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, then Lemma
2.4
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\overline{z}^{2}\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2+2it}}a_{\pi}(p)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}z^{2}\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2-2it}}\overline{a_{\pi}(p)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\overline{z}^{2}\left(\frac{\log X}{(1-2it+i\tau_{0})X^{1-2it+i\tau_{0}}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\frac{1}{4}z^{2}\left(\frac{\log X}{(1+2it-i\tau_{0})X^{1+2it-i\tau_{0}}}\right)+O\left(\frac{|z|^{2}}{X}\right).

In this case, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2}}\Re^{2}\left(\frac{\overline{z}a_{\pi}(p)}{p^{it}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}\frac{\log X}{X}\left(1+\Re\left(\frac{\overline{z}}{z}\cdot\frac{X^{2it-i\tau_{0}}}{1-2it+i\tau_{0}}\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{|z|^{2}}{X}\right).

Note that
1+\Re\left(\frac{\overline{z}}{z}\cdot\frac{X^{2it-i\tau_{0}}}{1-2it+i\tau_{0}}\right)\asymp 1
since
\tau_{0}\neq 2t
(notice that if
\tau_{0}=2t
, then the distribution is 1-dimensional). Hence, in all cases, we have

(6.1)

\begin{split}\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{2}}{p^{2}}\Re^{2}\left(\frac{\overline{z}a_{\pi}(p)}{p^{it}}\right)&\asymp|z|^{2}\frac{\log X}{X}.\end{split}

Next, we note that

(6.2)

\begin{split}|z|^{4}\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{4}}{p^{4}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)|^{4}&\ll|z|^{4}\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{4}}{p^{4-4\theta}}\ll|z|^{4}\frac{(\log X)^{3}}{X^{3-4\theta}}\end{split}

and

(6.3)

\begin{split}|z|\sum_{p>X}\frac{\log p}{p^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)|^{2}\ll|z|\sum_{p>X}\frac{(\log p)^{4}}{p^{2-2\theta}}\ll\frac{|z|}{X^{1-2\theta}}.\end{split}

Thus, choosing
X=|z|^{\frac{2+2\epsilon}{2-4\theta}}
in (
6.1
), (
6.2
) an (
6.3
) guarantees that

\prod_{p}M_{p}(u,v)\leq\prod_{p>X}M_{p}(u,v)\ll\exp(-C_{\epsilon}|z|^{\frac{2-8\theta-2\epsilon}{2-4\theta}}),

for some positive constant
C_{\epsilon}
. Since
0\leq\theta<\frac{1}{4}
, we have the desired result.
∎

In this section, we prove Theorem

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\sum x_{n}
be a conditionally convergent series of real numbers. Let
\alpha
and
\epsilon>0
be fixed. Then there exists a series
\sum y_{n}
, where
y_{n}\in\{0,x_{n},-x_{n}\}
such that

|\sum y_{n}-\alpha|<\epsilon.

Proof.

Let
\alpha>0
and
0<\epsilon<2\alpha
be given. By our assumptions, there exist positive integers
k
and
N
large enough such that

\alpha-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\leq\sum_{n=N}^{k}|x_{n}|\leq\alpha+\frac{\epsilon}{2}

and

-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\leq\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}x_{n}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

We get the desired result by choosing

y_{n}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\;1\leq n\leq N-1,\\
|x_{n}|&\text{if}\;N\leq n\leq k,\\
x_{n}&\text{if}\;n\geq k+1.\end{cases}

If
\alpha<0
, we get the result by applying the previous argument to
-\alpha
. Finally, if
\alpha=0
we set

y_{n}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\;1\leq n\leq N-1,\\
x_{n}&\text{if}\;n\geq N,\end{cases}

where
N
is chosen so that
-\epsilon\leq\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}x_{n}\leq\epsilon.

∎

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
\mathbb{X}_{p}
be a sequence of discrete random variables such that

\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{X}_{p}=a)=\begin{cases}a_{p}&\text{if}\;a=\pm 1,\\

be an analytic function in a disk

\sum_{p}\lambda_{p}f(r_{p}\mathbb{X}_{p}),

where the sequences
\{\lambda_{p}\}
and
\{r_{p}\}

and
\lambda_{p}f(r_{p}\mathbb{X}_{p})\in\mathbb{R}

,

Remark 8.3
.

Note that a sufficient condition for the divergence of

.

Since
\sum_{p,k\geq 1}|l_{k}\lambda_{p}r_{p}^{k}|

\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore,

with
a<b
. Hence,

is not identically zero on any interval
(a,b)
. Now since

is not identically zero on any interval

. By our assumption, we have

(c,d)

(c,d)

Thus,
h(x)>0
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Applying this lemma to the random series

-\mathrm{Ld}(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})=\sum_{p}\log p\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\mathbb{X}_{p}}{1-\frac{\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)}{p^{1+it}}\mathbb{X}_{p}}

yields:

Corollary 8.4
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed, and let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
with unitary central character such that
\pi\cong\tilde{\pi}\otimes\left|\mathrm{det}\right|^{2it}
. Then
-\mathrm{Ld}(1+it,\pi,\mathbb{X})
has an absolutely continuous distribution with a positive density function.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
\eta=\eta(N)
be a positive parameter which will be chosen so that
\eta(N)\to 0
as
N\to\infty
. Let

\Psi_{N}(\eta)=\left|\left\{D\in\mathcal{F}(N):\left|\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+it,\pi\otimes\chi_{D})\right|\leq\eta\right\}\right|.

By Theorem
1.2
, we have

\frac{\Psi_{N}(\eta)}{|\mathcal{F}(N)|}=\mu_{\mathrm{rand}}\left(\left(-\eta,\eta\right)\right)+O\left(\frac{(\log\log N)^{2}}{\log N}\right).

Let
M_{\mathrm{rand}}
be the smooth density function associated with
\mu_{\mathrm{rand}}
. By Corollary
8.4
, we know that
M_{\mathrm{rand}}(x)>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. It follows that

\mu_{\mathrm{rand}}\left(\left(-\eta,\eta\right)\right)=\int_{-\eta}^{\eta}M_{\mathrm{rand}}(x)\;dx\gg\eta.

Choosing
\eta=C\frac{(\log\log N)^{2}}{\log N}
for some large enough positive constant
C
yields

\frac{\Psi_{N}(\eta)}{|\mathcal{F}(N)|}\gg\frac{(\log\log N)^{2}}{\log N}.

Hence, we get
m_{N}\ll\frac{(\log\log N)^{2}}{\log N}
as desired.
∎

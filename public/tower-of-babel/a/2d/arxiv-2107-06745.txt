N. Jara

Abstract
. We prove that smoothness of nonautonomous linearization is of class C 2 . C^{2}. Our approach admits the existence of stable and unstable manifolds determined by a family of nonautonomous hyperbolicities. Moreover, our goal is reached without spectral conditions.

1.
Introduction

The linearization problem was initially regarded locally by P. Hartman
[
9
, Theorem I]
in the context of autonomous equations, later a specific type of those equations, namely a linear and a quasi-linear system, were studied by C. Pugh
[
13
]
in order to achieve a global homeomorphism between the flows of the systems. Next this result was adapted to the nonautonomous case by K. J. Palmer
[
11
]
, who implemented the concepts of exponential dichotomy in order to obtain its linearization problem.

The homoeomorphisms with its properties that K.J. Palmer obtained are known as the concept of topological equivalence which is useful to describe the asymptotic behaviour of solutions, even when they may be unknown, for example, to find locally or global attractors, or more generally, stable and unstable manifolds. Nevertheless, there are more dynamical properties that cannot be described with purely topological tools and require a study of the the differentiability of such homeomorphisms.

The study of the smoothness of homomorphisms has been a problem with a vast analysis in both the autonomous and nonautonomous contexts: In the autonomous framework, S. Sternberg
[
16
,
17
]
proved that dynamical system given by
C^{r}-
diffeomorphism can be
C^{k}-
linearized locally around hyperbolic fixed points that satisfy a nonresonant condition on its spectrum. This results were later improved by G. R. Belickiĭ
[
1
,
2
]
, still locally but giving more explicit conditions for the derivatives of the conjugation. S. van Strein
[
18
]
was the first to obtain a similar result without imposing resonance conditions, but his proof turned out to be wrong, as stated by V. Rayskin
[
14
]
.

As far as the author has been able to ascertain of studying the differentiability of the topological conjugacy between a linear and a quasilinear nonautonomous system was first regarded in the work
[
6
]
by Á. Castañeda and G. Robledo, who ensured the homeomorphisms are
C^{2}
preserving orientation diffeomorphisms if the linear system are exponentially asymptotically stable on
\mathbb{R}
and verified some conditions. This result was later improved in
[
3
]
to include more general decay ratios other than exponential but in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. In these results authors were able to construct a global diffeomorphism in the same fashion as K.J. Palmer.

L. V. Cuong
et. al.

[
7
]
obtained a smooth linearization in a similar way as S. Sternberg when the linear part admits an exponential dichotomy on whole real line. Recently, D. Dragičević
et. al.

[
8
]
have proved Strein’s statement to be true, and further extended it to the nonautonomous case while also improving Cuong’s result, under the assumption that the linear system satisfy a nonuniform hyperbolicity and other technical conditions, which do not include nonresonance conditions but impose spectral bounds.

In this paper we develop ideas hold many similarities with Á. Castañeda, P. Monzón and G. Robledo
[
5
]
, since both study the topological equivalence of a linear and a quasi-linear systems on the positive half real line. We improve their result in two senses: first we present a wider family of dichotomies accepted for the linear system, including both the exponential and the nonuniform exponential under some technical conditions that will be expressed later. Secondly, and we allow the existence of nonempty unstable manifolds for our linear system. We do not rely on of spectral bounds or nonresonant conditions, but only in the interlacing of the properties of nonautonomous hyperbolicity of the linear part, and boundedness and lipschitzness of the nonlinearities.

However, our work does not regard the continuity of the homeomorphism of topological equivalence as a function of two variables, a property that Á. Castañeda
et al.
called continuous topological equivalence
[
5
, Definition 1.4]
. We neither obtain a strong topological equivalence, a characterization introduced by J. Shi
[
15
, Definition 2.4]
, which corresponds to the uniform continuity of the homeomorphism and was also achieved on
[
5
, Theorem 2.1]
. Nevertheless, as a byproduct of the existence of an uniform bound for the derivative of the homeomorphism at any fixed time, we obtain a weaker version of this.

In the third section of this work we present and prove the topological equivalence between our systems, admitting a wide family of dichotomies, similar to those described by P. Gonzalez
et. al.

[
4
]
for the discrete framework, work from which we borrow important tools, adapted for the continuous framework.

In the fourth section we prove under technical conditions that the homeomorphism that we previously constructed is a
C^{1}-
diffeomorphism and give an explicit form for its derivative. Furthermore, we give examples of concrete dichotomies that satisfy our conditions. Finally, in the fifth section we give enough conditions to ensure the second class of differentiability for the topological equivalence, as well as an explicit way to achieve those conditions with an specific dichotomy.

Preliminaries

We study the systems

(2.1)

\dot{x}=A(t)x,

and

(2.2)

\dot{y}=A(t)y+f(t,y).

Denote
t\mapsto x(t,\tau,\xi)
y
t\to y(t,\tau,\eta)
to the solutions of (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) that pass through
\xi
and
\eta
respectively on
t=\tau
. We also denote
X(t,s)
the transition matrix of (
2.1
) such that for
t=s
is the identity. Moreover,
A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathcal{M}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
is continuous, non singular (
i.e.
has invertible images) and uniformly bounded, that is to say, there exists
M>1
such that

\max\left\{\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A(s)\right\rVert},\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A^{-1}(s)\right\rVert}\right\}=M.

The function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is such that there exist sequences
\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
that satisfy that for every
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and every pair
(y,\tilde{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

|f(s,y)-f(s,\tilde{y})|\leq\mathfrak{v}(s)|y-\tilde{y}|\text{ ; }|f(s,y)|\leq\mathfrak{u}(s).

Furthermore, allow us to consider the following hypothesi:

(c1)

(
2.1
) admits a non uniform dichotomy,
i.e.
there are two invariant complementary projectors
P(\cdot)
and
Q(\cdot)
such that
P(t)+Q(t)=I
for every
t\geq 0
, a continuous function
K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,+\infty[\to[0,\infty[
and a decreasing
C^{1}
function
h\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,\infty[\to]0,1]
, such that
h(0)=1
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}h(t)=0
and they satisfy

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X(t,s)P(s)\right\rVert}\leq K(s)\left({\frac{h(t)}{h(s)}}\right),&\forall t\geq s\geq 0\\
\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X(t,s)Q(s)\right\rVert}\leq K(s)\left({\frac{h(s)}{h(t)}}\right),&\forall 0\leq t\leq s.\end{array}\right.

(c2)

\int_{0}^{t}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X(t,s)P(s)\right\rVert}\mathfrak{u}(s)ds+\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X(t,s)Q(s)\right\rVert}\mathfrak{u}(s)ds\leq\mathfrak{p}<\infty\text{ , for every }t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(c3)

\int_{0}^{t}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X(t,s)P(s)\right\rVert}\mathfrak{v}(s)ds+\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X(t,s)Q(s)\right\rVert}\mathfrak{v}(s)ds\leq\mathfrak{q}<1\text{ , for every }t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(c4)

The map
u\mapsto f(t,u)
and its derivatives respect to
u
up to the order
r
(
r\geq 1
) are continuous functions of
(t,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(t,u)\right\rVert}<+\infty
is bounded.

(c5)

For every fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the functions
K,h
and
\mathfrak{v}
satisfy

\int_{\tau}^{\infty}K(s)h(s)\mathfrak{v}(s)\exp\left(\int_{\tau}^{s}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A(r)\right\rVert}+\mathfrak{v}(r)dr\right)<+\infty.

Remark 2.1
.

The projectors
P
and
Q
have been called invariant for (
2.1
), which means that for every
t,s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
they verify:

P(t)X(t,s)=X(t,s)P(s)\text{ and }Q(t)X(t,s)=X(t,s)Q(s).

Definition 2.2
.

Green’s operator associated to (
2.1
) and the dichotomy
(c1)
is the matrix function
\mathcal{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathcal{M}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
given by:

\mathcal{G}(t,s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}X(t,s)P(s)&\forall t\geq s\geq 0,\\
\\
-X(t,s)Q(s)&\forall 0\leq t<s,\end{array}\right.

and it is easily deduced that

\frac{\partial\mathcal{G}}{\partial t}(t,s)=A(t)\mathcal{G}(t,s).

Remark 2.3
.

With this notation, conditions
(c2)
and
(c3)
are rewritten as:

(c2)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(t,s)\right\rVert}\mathfrak{u}(s)ds\leq\mathfrak{p}<\infty\text{ , for every }t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(c3)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(t,s)\right\rVert}\mathfrak{v}(s)ds\leq\mathfrak{q}<1\text{ , for every }t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

3.
Topological equivalence

In this section we prove that the linear system (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are topologically equivalents on the positive half line. In order to state this result, we first recall the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
J\subset\mathbb{R}
an interval. Systems (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are
J
-topologically equivalent if there is a function
H\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}J\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
that satisfies

i)

If
x(t)
is solution of (
2.1
), then
H[t,x(t)]
is solution of (
2.2
).

ii)

H(t,u)-u
is bounded on
J\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

iii)

For every fixed
\tau\in J
, the map
u\mapsto H(\tau,u)
is an homeomophism of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Moreover, the function
u\mapsto G(\tau,u)=H^{-1}(\tau,u)
verifies conditions ii) and iii) and maps solutions of (
2.2
) on solutions of (
2.1
).

Theorem 3.2
.

If conditions
(c1)
,
(c2)
and
(c3)
hold, then (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are topologically equivalent on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Proof.

We develop the proof in several steps.

Step 1: Auxiliary functions.
We define
w^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
(\tau,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

\displaystyle w^{*}(t;(\tau,\eta))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}X(t,s)P(s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds+\int_{t}^{\infty}X(t,s)Q(s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds.

We also define
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}BC(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{R}^{d})\to BC(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

T(\phi)(t;(\tau,\xi))=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,x(s,\tau,\xi)+\phi(s))ds.

T
is well defined by
(c2)
. Using condition
(c3)
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|T(\phi)(t;(\tau,\xi))-T(\psi)(t;(\tau,\xi))\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(t,s)\right\rVert}\left|\phi(s)-\psi(s)\right|\mathfrak{v}(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\phi-\psi\right\rVert}_{\infty},

hence, using Banach’s fixed point Theorem, we obtain the existence of an unique fixed point

z^{*}(t;(\tau,\xi))=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,x(s,\tau,\xi)+z^{*}(s;(\tau,\xi)))ds.

It is easy to verify
t\mapsto w^{*}(t;(\tau,\eta))
is solution to the initial value problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\dot{w}(t)&=&A(t)w(t)-f(t,y(t,\tau,\eta))\\
\\
w(0)&=&-{\int}_{0}^{\infty}X(0,s)Q(s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds,\end{array}\right.

while
t\mapsto z^{*}(t;(\tau,\xi))
is respectively solution to the initial value problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\dot{z}(t)&=&A(t)z(t)-f(t,x(t,\tau,\xi)+z(t))\\
\\
z(0)&=&-{\int}_{0}^{\infty}X(0,s)Q(s)f(s,x(s,\tau,\xi)+z^{*}(s;(\tau,\xi)))ds,\end{array}\right.

furthermore, by using
(c2)
, the maps
t\mapsto w^{*}(t;(t,\eta))
and
t\mapsto z^{*}(t;(t,\xi))
are uniformly bounded.

Step 2: Construct maps
H
and
G
.
By uniqueness of solutions

(3.2)

x(t,\tau,\xi)=x(t,s,x(s,\tau,\xi))\text{ , for every }t,s,\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

and

(3.3)

z^{*}(t;(\tau,\xi))=z^{*}(t;(s,x(s,\tau,\xi)))\text{ , for every }t,s,\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

For every fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
we define
H(t,\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
G(t,\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}H(t,\xi)&=&\xi+{\int}_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,x(s,t,\xi)+z^{*}(s;(t,\xi)))ds\\
\\
&=&\xi+z^{*}(t;(t,\xi))\end{array}\right.

and

(3.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}G(t,\eta)&=&\eta-{\int}_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,y(s,t,\eta))ds\\
\\
&=&\eta+w^{*}(t;(t,\eta)).\end{array}\right.

Using
(c2)
, it follows immediately that
H(t,\xi)-\xi
and
G(t,\eta)-\eta
are bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, hence
H
and
G
satisfy condition ii) from Definition
3.1
. In order to study additional properties of the map
G
, note that for
\tau\geq t

y(t,\tau,\eta)=X(t,\tau)\eta-\int_{t}^{\tau}X(t,s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds,

or equivalently

\displaystyle X(\tau,t)y(t,\tau,\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta-\int_{t}^{\tau}X(\tau,s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta-\int_{t}^{\tau}X(\tau,s)P(s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds-\int_{t}^{\tau}X(\tau,s)Q(s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds,

in particular, for
t=0
we obtain

\displaystyle X(\tau,0)y(0,\tau,\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta-\int_{0}^{\tau}X(\tau,s)P(s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds-\int_{0}^{\tau}X(\tau,s)Q(s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta-\int_{0}^{\tau}X(\tau,s)P(s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds

\displaystyle+\int_{\tau}^{\infty}X(\tau,s)Q(s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds-\int_{0}^{\infty}X(\tau,s)Q(s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(\tau,s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds

\displaystyle-X(\tau,0)\int_{0}^{\infty}X(0,s)Q(s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(\tau,\eta)-X(\tau,0)w^{*}(0;(\tau,\eta)),

thus

(3.6)

G(\tau,\eta)=X(\tau,0)\left\{y(0,\tau,\eta)+w^{*}(0;(\tau,\eta))\right\}.

Step 3:
H
maps solutions of (
2.1
) on solutions of (
2.2
) and
G
maps solutions of (
2.2
) on solutions of (
2.1
)
. By uniqueness of solutions, by simple differentiation we obtain

(3.7)

H[t,x(t,\tau,\xi)]=y(t,\tau,H(\tau,\xi))

and

(3.8)

G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)]=x(t,\tau,G(\tau,\eta))=X(t,\tau)G(\tau,\eta),

hence both maps satisfy condition i) of Definition
3.1
respectively.

Step 4:
u\mapsto G(t,u)
and
u\mapsto H(t,u)
are bijective for every fixed
t\geq 0
.

First we show
H(t,G(t,\eta))=\eta
for every
t\geq 0
. Using (
3.4
) and (
3.5

\displaystyle H(t,G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)]

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,x(s,t,G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)])+z^{*}(s;(t,G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)])))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y(t,\tau,\eta)-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,x(s,t,G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)])+z^{*}(s;(t,G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)]))).

Define
\omega(t)=\left|H[t,G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)]]-y(t,\tau,\eta)\right|
. Note that

\displaystyle\omega(t)\leq\left|H[t,G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)]]-G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)]\right|+\left|G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)]-y(t,\tau,\eta)\right|<\infty,

since both
H
and
G

. Thus, using
(c3)
, along with the previous expression and the identities (

), (
3.4
) and (

), for an arbitrary

we have

\displaystyle\omega(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)\left\{f(s,x(s,t,G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)])+z^{*}(s;(t,G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)])))-f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta))\right\}ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(t,s)\right\rVert}\mathfrak{v}(s)\left|x(s,t,G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)])+z^{*}(s;(t,G[t,y(t,\tau,\eta)]))-y(s,\tau,\eta)\right|ds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(t,s)\right\rVert}\mathfrak{v}(s)\left|x(s,t,x(t,\tau,G(\tau,\eta)))+z^{*}(s;(t,x(t,\tau,G(\tau,\eta))))-y(s,\tau,\eta)\right|ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(t,s)\right\rVert}\mathfrak{v}(s)\left|x(s,\tau,G(\tau,\eta))+z^{*}(s;(s,x(s,\tau,G(\tau,\eta))))-y(s,\tau,\eta)\right|ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(t,s)\right\rVert}\mathfrak{v}(s)\left|H[s,G[s,y(s,\tau,\eta)])]-y(s,\tau,\eta)\right|ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(t,s)\right\rVert}\mathfrak{v}(s)\omega(s)ds\leq\mathfrak{q}\cdot\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}\{\omega(s)\}.

Thus
\omega(t)=0
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, otherwise we get a contradiction. In particular, taking

we obtain
H(\tau,G(\tau,\eta))=\eta
. Now we show
G(t,H(t,\xi))=\xi
. Indeed, using (
3.2
), (
3.3
), (
3.4
), (
3.5

\displaystyle G[t,H[t,x(t,\tau,\xi)]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H[t,x(t,\tau,\xi)]-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,y(s,t,H[t,x(t,\tau,\xi)]))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H[t,x(t,\tau,\xi)]-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,y(s,t,y(t,\tau,H(\tau,\xi))))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H[t,x(t,\tau,\xi)]-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,y(s,\tau,H(\tau,\xi)))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(t,\tau,\xi)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,x(s,t,x(t,\tau,\xi))+z^{*}(s;(t,x(t,\tau,\xi))))ds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,y(s,\tau,H(\tau,\xi)))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(t,\tau,\xi)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,x(s,\tau,x(\tau,\tau,\xi))+z^{*}(s;(s,x(s,\tau,\xi))))ds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,y(s,\tau,H(\tau,\xi)))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(t,\tau,\xi)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)\left\{f(s,x(s,\tau,\xi)+z^{*}(s;(s,x(s,\tau,\xi))))-f(s,y(s,\tau,H(\tau,\xi)))\right\}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(t,\tau,\xi)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)\left\{f(s,H(s,x(s,\tau,\xi)))-f(s,y(s,\tau,H(\tau,\xi)))\right\}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(t,\tau,\xi)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)\left\{f(s,y(s,\tau,H(\tau,\xi)))-f(s,y(s,\tau,H(\tau,\xi)))\right\}ds

\displaystyle x(t,\tau,\xi),

evaluating on

Step 5:
u\mapsto G(t,u)
is continuous.

It is enough to show
\eta\mapsto w^{*}(t;(t,\eta))
is continuous for every
t\geq 0
, since
G(t,\eta)=\eta+w^{*}(t;(t,\eta))
. Let
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\{\eta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a sequence such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta_{n}=\eta
. Fix
t,\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and define
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
the sequence of functions over
\mathbb{R}^{+}
given by

a_{n}(s)=\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta_{n})),

notice that

|a_{n}(s)|\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(t,s)\right\rVert}\mathfrak{u}(s)\text{ , for every }s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\text{ and }n\in\mathbb{N}.

On the other hand, as
u\mapsto f(s,u)
and
\xi\mapsto y(s,\tau,\xi)
are continuous, it is clear
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges pointwise to
a\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by

a(s)=\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta)).

Thus, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence Theorem we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}w^{*}(t;(\tau,\eta_{n}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(t,s)f(s,y(s,\tau,\eta_{n}))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}a_{n}(s)ds=-\int_{0}^{\infty}a(s)ds=w^{*}(t;(\tau,\eta)).

Hence
\eta\mapsto w^{*}(t;(\tau,\eta))
is continuous and in particular
\eta\mapsto w^{*}(t;(t,\eta))
is also continuous.

Step 6:
u\mapsto H(t,u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathcal{G}(0,s)\right\rVert}\left(\mathfrak{v}(s)\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\frac{\partial^{2}y}{\partial\eta^{2}}(s,\tau,\eta)\right\rVert}+1\right)+\mathfrak{V}(s)\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\frac{\partial y}{\partial\eta}(s,\tau,\eta)\right\rVert}^{2}\right)ds

∎

Theorem 5.8

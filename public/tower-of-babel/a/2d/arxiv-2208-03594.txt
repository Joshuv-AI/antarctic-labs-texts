On the L 2 L^{2} well-posedness and decay estimate of third order Benjamin-Ono equation
Lizhe Wan

Abstract
. We consider the L 2 L^{2} well-posedness of third order Benjamin-Ono equation. We show that by means of a normal form and a gauge transformation, the equation can be changed into an Airy-type equation. A second goal of this work is to establish that the solutions to the nonlinear third order Benjamin-Ono equation problem exhibit a dispersive decay estimate analogue to the corresponding linear associated problem. The key ingredient is the use of a nonlinear vector field akin to the work in [ 8 , 9 ] .

35B65, 35G20

1.
Introduction

In this article we consider third order Benjamin-Ono equation

(1.1)

\phi_{t}-\phi_{xxx}+\frac{3}{4}\phi^{2}\phi_{x}+\frac{3}{4}[\phi H\phi_{x}+H(\phi\phi_{x})]_{x}=0,\qquad\phi(0)=\phi_{0}.

Here
\phi
is a real valued function:
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and
H
is the Hilbert transform on the real line, which is given via a Fourier multiplier with symbol
-i
sgn
\xi
.

As its name suggests, third order Benjamin-Ono equation derives from the Benjamin-Ono equation, and it is part of the so called
Benjamin-Ono hierarchy

(1.2)

\phi_{t}+H\phi_{xx}=\phi\phi_{x},\qquad\phi(0)=\phi_{0}.

It was introduced by Benjamin
[
2
]
and Ono
[
16
]
in order to describe long internal waves in a two-layer fluid of great depth.
The Benjamin-Ono equation is completely integrable, and, hence possesses many conserved quantities associated to smooth solutions. Here we list first three conserved quantities:

\displaystyle E_{0}

\displaystyle=\int\phi^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\int\phi H\phi_{x}-\frac{1}{3}\phi^{3}\,dx,

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=\int\phi_{x}^{2}-\frac{3}{4}\phi^{2}H\phi_{x}+\frac{1}{8}\phi^{4}\,dx.

More generally, at each nonnegative integer
k
, there exists a conserved energy
E_{k}
corresponding at leading order to
\dot{H}^{\frac{k}{2}}
norm of
\phi
(see
[
11
]
and
[
15
]
).

The Benjamin-Ono equation itself is generated by the Hamiltonian
E_{1}
,
and the symplectic form

\omega(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=\int\psi_{1}\partial_{x}\psi_{2}\,dx,

with the associated map
J=\partial_{x}
.
In the same fashion, the Hamiltonian
E_{2}
and the symplectic form
\omega
generate the new equation (
1.1
), which is called
third order Benjamin-Ono equation.

See
[
13
]
for a detailed survey of the Benjamin-Ono equation and its hierarchy.

Equation (
1.1
) is also a completely integrable dispersive equation. The group velocity of waves depends on the frequency, which is similar to KdV equation.
Above conserved quantities such as
E_{0}
and
E_{2}
of Benjamin-Ono equation are also the conserved for (
1.1
).
Tools for dispersive partial differential equations like Strichartz estimates can be used for (
1.1
).
It also shares a lot of similarities with the Benjamin-Ono equation (
1.2
).
If
\phi(t,x)
is a solution, so is
\lambda\phi(\lambda^{3}t,\lambda x)
, which suggests that the scale invariant Sobolev space associated to this scaling is
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
.
This scale invariant space is the same as the Benjamin-Ono equation but different from KdV equation.

Our first goal of this article is to prove the following result of (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

The third order Benjamin-Ono equation (
1.1
) is locally well-posed in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Here by locally well-posedness we mean the unique existence of solution and continuous dependence of initial data; this is nothing more than the Hadamard local well-posedness theory.
Since the
L^{2}
norm of the solution is conserved, this also implies global well-posedness of the equation (
1.1
).

The second goal here is to obtain a decay estimate for the positive time
t
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume the initial data
\phi_{0}
for the equation
(
1.1
)
is small in the sense that for any small constant
\delta>0
.

(1.3)

\|\phi_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}_{2,\infty}}+\|x\phi_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\epsilon\ll 1,

then for the lifespan
t\ll\epsilon^{-12}
, we have the dispersive bound for
\phi
:

t^{\frac{1}{4}}\left\langle x\right\rangle_{t}^{\frac{1}{4}-\delta}|\phi(t,x)|+t^{\frac{3}{4}}\left\langle x\right\rangle_{t}^{-\frac{1}{4}-\delta}|\phi_{x}(t,x)|\lesssim\epsilon.

Furthermore, in the elliptic region
E=\{x\lesssim-t^{\frac{1}{3}}\}
we have a better bound

\left\langle x\right\rangle_{t}|\phi(t,x)|+t^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle x\right\rangle_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\phi_{x}(t,x)|\lesssim\epsilon\log(t^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left\langle x\right\rangle_{t}).

Here,
\left\langle x\right\rangle_{t}
denotes the time-depended Japanese bracket given by
\left\langle x\right\rangle_{t}:=(x^{2}+t^{\frac{2}{3}})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, which is consistent with Airy scaling.

This result is similar with the one in
[
8
]
but in our case the time for which the nonlinear solution disperses as the linear solution is longer, i.e..
t\ll\epsilon^{-12}
.

Historically, in
[
4
]
, Feng and Han proved that (
1.1
) is locally well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),s\geq 4
.
Feng further established the well-posedness of (
1.1
) in weighted Sobolev space
H^{s}_{\gamma},s\geq 3,\gamma\in[0,1]
, see
[
3
]
.
Linares, Pilod and Ponce in their paper
[
12
]
considered local well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),s\geq 2
for more general third order type
Benjamin-Ono equations.
Molinet and Pilod in

[
14
]
proved global well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),s\geq 2
.
There are also results for (
1.1
) on the torus. Tanaka
[
21
]
showed that a more general third order type Benjamin-Ono type of equations is well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T}),s\geq\frac{5}{2}
.
Gassot
[
5
]
proved that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the third order Benjamin-Ono flow map continuously extends to
H^{s}_{r,0}(\mathbb{T})
if
s\geq 0
, but does not admit a continuous extension to
H^{-s}_{r,0}(\mathbb{T})
if
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
.
More generally, people even consider higher order Benjamin-Ono equation, such as the fourth order Benjamin-Ono equations, see for instance the work of Tanaka in
[
22
]
for both torus and the real line.

Our goal in this present work is to provide a clean
L^{2}
local well-posedness result for the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) on the real line, improving considerably the previous work accomplished by the above listed authors in the analysis of the same problem. We use novel ideas introduced by Ifrim-Tataru, and Ifrim-Koch-Tataru in
[
9
]
and
[
8
]
in order to achieve both our results. The difficulties we encountered are however different in nature than in the work cited above, and hence require an adapted methodology.

The article is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we first give the definitions and notations that will be needed in the later proofs.
Then we establish the Strichartz and bilinear Strichartz estimates for the linear Airy flow.

In Section
3
, we perform a partial normal form method transformation first introduced by Simon in
[
18
]
followed by Shatah
[
17
]
.
The main principle in the normal form method is to apply a quadratic correction to the unknown in order to replace a nonresonant quadratic nonlinearity by a milder cubic nonlinearity.
Unfortunately this method does not apply directly here, because some terms
in the quadratic correction are unbounded, and so are some of the cubic terms generated by
the correction.
To bypass this issue, we apply the techniques developed in
[
8
]
, which is carried out in two steps:

(1)

a partial normal form transformation which is bounded and removes some of the
quadratic nonlinearity,

(2)

a gauge transform which removes the remaining part of the quadratic nonlinearity in a bounded way.

This will transform (
1.1
) into an equation where the quadratic terms have been removed and replaced by cubic perturbative terms.
We then finalize the proof of apriori
L^{2}
bound for (
1.1
) using a bootstrap argument.

In Section
4
we show that the linearized third order Benjamin-Ono equation is
well-posed in
L^{2}
.
In the proof we also use a normal form transformation to eliminate quadratic terms on the right, and replace them by cubic terms.
The difference with respect to the computation in Section
3
is that here we leave certain quadratic terms on the right, because their corresponding normal form correction would be too singular.
We proceed to establish the estimates using a bootstrap argument .

As a consequence of well-posedness of the linearized equation, we get the weak Lipschitz dependence on the initial data for (
1.1
).
In Section
5
we use this important property to establish Theorem
1.1
.

Section
6
is devoted to the sketch of Theorem
1.2
, where we outline three main steps in the same spirit as in the work of
[
8
]
.
Here the estimate differs from the corresponding result of
[
8
]
by a
\delta
exponent.
This is because in the result of Proposition
6.1
,
\phi
cannot reach
B^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{2,\infty}
regularity.
In Section
7
, we prove that the
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
norm of the linearized solution
v
is almost conserved, in which the lifespan of the solution
\epsilon^{-12}
is much more better than the corresponding lifespan in
[
8
]
.
In Section
8
we consider the inequality in three regions, namely the hyperbolic, self-similar and elliptic regions.
We establish the nonlinear vector field bound in all three regions, which concludes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Notations and the linear flow

The big
O
notation.
We use the notation
A\lesssim B
or
A=O(B)
to denote the estimate that
|A|\leq CB
, where
C
is a universal constant.
If X is a Banach space, we use
O_{X}(B)
to denote any element in X with norm
O(B)
; explicitly we say
u=O_{X}(B)
if
\|u\|_{X}\leq CB
.

Hilbert transform.

The Hilbert transform of a function
f
on the real line is defined by

Hf(x)=PV\frac{1}{\pi}\int\frac{f(y)}{x-y}dy,

where
PV
denotes the principle value.
On the Fourier side, an alternative definition of the Hilbert transform is given by

\widehat{Hf}(\xi)=-i\mbox{sgn}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi).

Here we list some properties of Hilbert transform.

1.

Skew-adjointness:

(2.1)

\int uH(v)\,dx=-\int vH(u)\,dx,\mbox{ if }u,v\in L^{2}.

2.

Skew identity property:

(2.2)

\int H(u)H(v)\,dx=\int uv\,dx,\mbox{ if }u,v\in L^{2}.

3.

Convolution Identity:

(2.3)

H\left((uH(v)+vH(u)\right)=H(u)H(v)-uv,\mbox{ if }u,v\in L^{2}.

An immediate consequence of above properties is that the solution of (
1.1
) satisfies the
L^{2}
conservation law enumerated earlier.

Littlewood-Paley decomposition.

We begin with the Riesz decomposition

\mathbf{1}=P_{-}+P_{+},

where

\widehat{P_{-}f(\xi)}:=\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,0)}\hat{f}(\xi),\quad\widehat{P_{+}f(\xi)}:=\mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}\hat{f}(\xi).

Consider a bump function which is supported on
[-2,2]
and equal to 1 on
[-1,1]
, we define the Littlewood-Paley operator
P_{\leq k}=P_{<k+1}
by

\widehat{P_{\leq k}f(\xi)}:=\psi(\frac{\xi}{2^{k}})\hat{f}(\xi)

for
k\geq 0
,
and
P_{>k}:=P_{\geq k-1}:=1-P_{\leq k}
.
We also define
P_{k}:=P_{\geq k}-P_{\leq k-1}
.
Note that due to the Minkowski inequality, all the operators
P_{k}
,
P_{\leq k}
are bounded on all translation-invariant Banach spaces.

An important feature of the Littlewood-Paley operator is that
P_{\pm}
commutes with
P_{k},P_{<k}
.
Therefore, for simplicity, we will let
P_{k}^{\pm}=P_{k}P_{\pm}
, respective
P_{<k}^{\pm}:=P_{\pm}P_{<k}
.
We also introduce the notations
\phi_{k}^{+}:=P_{k}^{+}\phi
, and
\phi_{k}^{-}=P_{k}^{-}\phi
, respectively.

Given the Littlewood-Paley operator
P_{k}
, we further introduce the enlarged projection operator
\tilde{P}_{k}
.
On the frequency side, it equals
1
in the support of
P_{k}
, and is supported on a sightly larger set than the support of
P_{k}
(say
2^{k-2}
).

From Plancherel theorem we have the bound
for inhomogeneous Sobolev and Besov spaces

\|f\|_{H^{s}_{x}}\approx\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|P_{k}f\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{x}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\approx\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{ks}\|P_{k}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\|f\|_{B^{s}_{2,\infty}}=\sup_{k}2^{sk}\|P_{k}f\|_{L^{2}},

for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Similar Littlewood-Paley characterizations also hold for homogeneous Sobolev and Besov spaces.

A very useful result of Littlewood-Paley decomposition is the Bernstein’s inequality, whose proof can be found on textbook for example
[
1
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(Bernstein’s inequality)
.

For
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
and a function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{k}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim_{p,q,d}2^{k\left(\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{q}\right)}\|P_{k}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

\displaystyle\|P_{\leq k}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim_{p,q,d}2^{k\left(\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{q}\right)}\|P_{\leq k}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Frequency envelopes.

We say that a sequence
\{c_{k}\}_{0}^{\infty}\in l^{2}
is an
L^{2}
frequency envelope for
\phi\in L^{2}
if

(1)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}^{2}\lesssim 1
;

(2)

it is slow varying,
c_{j}/c_{k}\leq 2^{\delta|j-k|}
, with
\delta
a very small universal constant;

(3)

it bounds the dyadic norms of
\phi
, namely
\|P_{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{k}
.

The idea of frequency envelopes dates back from T. Tao
[
24
]
, and is later improved and implemented by D. Tataru
[
25
]
.
Given a frequency envelope
c_{k}
we define

c_{\leq k}=\left(\sum_{j\leq k}c^{2}_{j}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad c_{\geq k}=\left(\sum_{j\geq k}c^{2}_{j}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We harmlessly assume that
c_{0}\approx 1
.
Similarly, for
H^{s}
functions, we replace the last condition by
\|P_{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2^{-ks}c_{k}
.

Later for example in (
3.11
) or (
5.1
), we consider the small initial data satisfying
\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\epsilon
.
Using the language of frequency envelopes, we can replace this condition by
\|P_{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\leq\epsilon c_{k}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Multi-linear expressions.
By
L(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n})
we denote a translation-invariant expression of the form

L(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n})(x)=\int K(y)\phi_{1}(x+y_{1})\dots\phi_{n}(x+y_{n})\,dy,

where
K\in L^{1}
.
By
L_{k}
we denote such multilinear expressions where output is localized at frequency
2^{k}
.
An obvious observation is that the multilinear expressions behave well with respect to reiteration, e.g.

L(L(u,v),w)=L(u,v,w).

Multilinear
L
type expressions can easily be estimated in terms of linear bounds for their entries.
For bilinear
L
type expressions, we have the Hölder’s type of inequality

\|L(u_{1},u_{2})\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{p_{2}}},\qquad\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{r}.

For trilinear
L
type expressions, the effect of uncorrelated translations may be taken into considerations.
We define the translation group
\{T_{y}\}_{y\in\mathbb{R}}
,

(T_{y}u)(x)=u(x+y).

Then we can also estimate the trilinear form by

\|L(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\|u_{2}T_{y}u_{3}\|_{L^{p_{2}}},\qquad\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{r}.

Or without ambiguity in a simpler form

\|L(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|L(u_{2},u_{3})\|_{L^{p_{2}}}.

When classifying cubic and higher order terms obtained after implementing a normal form
transformation, we observe that having a commutator structure can simplify our computation
We will repeatedly use the following lemma in normal form analysis for the Littlewood-Paley projection
P_{k}
:

Lemma 2.2

(Leibniz rule for
P_{k}
,
[
23
]
)
.

(2.4)

[P_{k},f]g=L(f_{x},2^{-k}g).

This lemma can be understood in the following sense. Suppose one function
g
has high frequency
\sim 2^{k}
and the other function
f
has low frequency
\leq 2^{k}
.
Then
P_{k}(fg)-fP_{k}g
shifts a derivative from the high-frequency function
g
to the low-frequency function
f
.
This shift generally ensures that all such commutator terms can be easily estimated.

The Linear flow.

The linear part of (
1.1
) is an Airy-type equation,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\phi_{t}-\phi_{xxx}=0&\\
\phi(0)=\phi_{0}.\end{array}\right.

To better understand the positive long time behavior of the solution, it is useful to separate the domain of evolution
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
into three regions

(1)

The hyperbolic region

H:=\{x\gtrsim t^{\frac{1}{3}}\},

where the solution is oscillatory with dispersive decay.

(2)

The self-similar region

S:=\{|x|\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{3}}\},

where the solution essentially looks like a bump function with
t^{\frac{1}{3}}
decay.

(3)

The elliptic region

E:=\{x\lesssim-t^{\frac{1}{3}}\},

where the solution no longer has oscillatory asymptotic behavior, and
consequently has better decay.

By the stationary phase type argument, one can show

Lemma 2.3

(Stationary phase,
[
19
]
)
.

\|e^{t\partial_{x}^{3}}\|_{L^{1}\rightarrow L^{\infty}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{3}}.

Consider the operator

L=x+3t\partial_{x}^{2},

it satisfies the following properties:

[\partial_{x},L]=1,\qquad[L,\partial_{t}-\partial_{x}^{3}]=0,

which further indicates that

\|L\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|x\phi(0)\|_{L^{2}}

is a conserved quantity.
For the nonlinear equation (
1.1
), we define the the nonlinear counterpart
L^{NL}
of
L
:

(2.5)

L^{NL}\phi=x\phi+3t\phi_{xx}-\frac{3t}{4}\phi^{3}-\frac{3t}{4}[\phi H\phi_{x}+H(\phi\phi_{x})].

The Strichartz estimates.

For the inhomogeneous equation

(2.6)

\phi_{t}-\phi_{xxx}=f,\quad\phi(0)=\phi_{0},

we define Strichartz space
\mathcal{S}
associated to the
L^{2}
flow by

\mathcal{S}=L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{t}^{4}\dot{W}^{\frac{1}{4},\infty},

together with its dual space

\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}=L^{1}_{t}L_{x}^{2}+L_{t}^{\frac{4}{3}}\dot{W}^{-\frac{1}{4},1}.

We also define

\mathcal{S}^{s}=(1+D^{2})^{-\frac{s}{2}}\mathcal{S}

to denote the similar spaces associated to the flow in
H^{s}
.
The Strichartz estimates of (
2.6
) in the
L^{2}
setting are summarized in the following:

Lemma 2.4

(Strichartz estimate,
[
6
]
)
.

\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}}.

The Besov type of above estimates is

\|\phi\|_{l^{2}\mathcal{S}}\lesssim\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{l^{2}\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}},

where

\|\phi\|^{2}_{l^{2}\mathcal{S}}=\sum_{k}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{S}},\qquad\|\phi\|_{l^{2}\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}}^{2}=\sum_{k}\|\phi_{k}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}}.

There are also bilinear Strichartz estimate.

Lemma 2.5

(Bilinear Strichartz estimate)
.

\|e^{t\partial_{x}^{3}}P_{j}f\cdot e^{t\partial_{x}^{3}}P_{k}g\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-max\{j,k\}}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|g\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

for
|j-k|>2
, and for the case
|j-k|\leq 1
as long as
P_{j}f
and
P_{k}g
have
O(2^{k})
frequency separation between their supports.

Proof.

e^{t\partial_{x}^{3}}P_{j}f\cdot e^{t\partial_{x}^{3}}P_{k}g=\int\hat{f}_{j}(\xi_{1})\hat{g_{k}}(\xi_{2})e^{i[(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})x-(\xi_{1}^{3}+\xi_{2}^{3})t]}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}.

Then by duality, we can choose
\|F(t,x)\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}=1
, and a change of variable
\omega=\xi_{1}^{3}+\xi^{3}_{2},\eta=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}
, then the Jacobian
\frac{\partial(\omega,\eta)}{\partial(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}=3(\xi_{1}^{2}-\xi_{2}^{2})
.

\displaystyle\|e^{t\partial_{x}^{3}}P_{j}f\cdot e^{t\partial_{x}^{3}}P_{k}g\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}=\sup\int F(\xi_{1}^{3}+\xi_{2}^{3},\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\hat{f}_{j}(\xi_{1})\hat{g}_{k}(\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\left(\int\hat{f}^{2}_{j}(\xi_{1})\hat{g}^{2}_{k}(\xi_{2})\left|\frac{\partial(\omega,\eta)}{\partial(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}\right|^{-2}d\omega d\eta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\approx 2^{-max\{j,k\}}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|g\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

∎

A natural corollary for inhomogeneous equations then follows.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
f
and
g
be the solution of inhomogeneous airy equation with initial data
P_{j}f_{0}
,
P_{k}g_{0}
, and source terms F,G, such that either
|j-k|>2
or
|j-k|\leq 1
but
P_{j}f_{0}
and
P_{k}g_{0}
have
O(2^{k})
frequency separation between their supports. Then we have

\|fg\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim 2^{-max\{j,k\}}(\|P_{j}f_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|F\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}})(\|P_{k}g_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|G\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}).

3.
Normal form analysis and estimates

In this section we establish apriori
L^{2}
bounds for solutions for the Cauchy problem (
1.1
).
The Sobolev index of
L^{2}
space is greater than the critical Sobolev index
-\frac{1}{2}
. By the scale invariance, we can rescale the solution and work with solution for which the
L^{2}
norm is small. In this case the lifespan is also rescaled, it is natural to consider the solution on the time interval
[-1,1]
.
Resonant analysis suggests that we can use the normal form method to replace a nonresonant quadratic nonlinearity by a higher order cubic nonlinearity.

3.1.
The modified quadratic normal form analysis

We rewrite (
1.1
) as

(3.1)

\phi_{t}-\phi_{xxx}=-\frac{3}{4}\phi^{2}\phi_{x}-\frac{3}{4}\phi_{x}H\phi_{x}-\frac{3}{4}\phi H\phi_{xx}-\frac{3}{4}H(\phi_{xx}\phi+\phi_{x}\phi_{x}).

Then we hope to eliminate the quadratic terms, and to transform (
3.1
) further to
(\partial_{t}-\partial_{x}^{3})\tilde{\phi}=Q^{3}(\phi,\phi,\phi)+Q^{4}(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi)
type, where
Q^{3}
and
Q^{4}
are translation invariant multi-linear forms.
Let

\tilde{\phi}=\phi+B(\phi,\phi)

to be the normal form transformation that eliminate the quadratic terms, where
B(\phi,\phi)
is a bilinear translation invariant symmetric function of
\phi
.
A direct computation shows the explicit expression of
B(\phi,\phi)
,

B(\phi,\phi)=-\frac{1}{4}[H(\phi\cdot\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi)+H\phi\cdot\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi],

which is not invertible at low frequencies.
Therefore, we need more refined estimates at different frequencies.
If we apply the operator
P_{k}^{+}
(
P_{k}^{-}
can be treated a in similar way), where
k\geq 1
, to (
3.1
), we have,

(3.2)

P_{k}^{+}\phi_{t}-P_{k}^{+}\phi_{xxx}=-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(\phi^{2}\phi_{x})-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(\phi_{x}H\phi_{x})-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(\phi H\phi_{xx})+\frac{3}{4}iP_{k}^{+}(\phi_{xx}\phi)+\frac{3}{4}iP_{k}^{+}(\phi_{x}\phi_{x}).

For each of the four quadratic terms on the right hand side of (
3.2
), we can add an additional term, and form the commutator structure.

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(\phi_{x}H\phi_{x})-\frac{3}{4}i\partial_{x}\phi_{<k}\partial_{x}\phi_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(\partial_{x}\phi_{\geq k}H\phi_{x})-\frac{3}{4}[P_{k}^{+},\partial_{x}\phi_{<k}]H\phi_{x},

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(\phi H\phi_{xx})-\frac{3}{4}i\phi_{<k}\partial_{xx}\phi_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(\phi_{\geq k}H\phi_{xx})-\frac{3}{4}[P_{k}^{+},\phi_{<k}]H\phi_{xx},

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}iP_{k}^{+}(\phi_{x}^{2})-\frac{3}{4}i\partial_{x}\phi_{<k}\partial_{x}\phi_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}iP_{k}^{+}(\partial_{x}\phi_{\geq k}\phi_{x})+\frac{3}{4}i[P_{k}^{+},\partial_{x}\phi_{<k}]\phi_{x},

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}iP_{k}^{+}(\phi_{xx}\phi)-\frac{3}{4}i\phi_{<k}\partial_{xx}\phi_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}iP_{k}^{+}(\phi_{\geq k}\phi_{xx})+\frac{3}{4}i[P_{k}^{+},\phi_{<k}]\phi_{xx}.

In this way, define an operator

(3.3)

A_{TBO}^{k,+}=\partial_{t}-\partial_{x}^{3}-\frac{3}{2}i\partial_{x}\phi_{<k}\partial_{x}-\frac{3}{2}i\phi_{<k}\partial_{xx},

we can the rewrite the equation (
3.2
) as

\displaystyle A_{TBO}^{k,+}\phi_{k}^{+}=-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(\phi^{2}\phi_{x})-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(\partial_{x}\phi_{\geq k}H\phi_{x})-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(\phi_{\geq k}H\phi_{xx})+\frac{3}{4}iP_{k}^{+}(\partial_{x}\phi_{\geq k}\phi_{x})+\frac{3}{4}iP_{k}^{+}(\phi_{\geq k}\phi_{xx})

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{4}[P_{k}^{+},\partial_{x}\phi_{<k}]H\phi_{x}-\frac{3}{4}[P_{k}^{+},\phi_{<k}]H\phi_{xx}+\frac{3}{4}i[P_{k}^{+},\partial_{x}\phi_{<k}]\phi_{x}+\frac{3}{4}i[P_{k}^{+},\phi_{<k}]\phi_{xx}.

We further define

(3.4)

\tilde{\phi}_{k}^{+}=\phi_{k}^{+}+B_{k}(\phi,\phi),

and use this normal form transformation to eliminate the quadratic term on the right.
Such a transformation is similarly computed and is given by the expression

(3.5)

B_{k}(\phi,\phi)=\frac{1}{4}[iP_{k}^{+}(\phi\cdot\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi)-P_{k}^{+}(H\phi\cdot\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi)+2i\partial_{x}^{-1}(P_{<k}\phi)P_{k}^{+}\phi].

To show that the bilinear form (
3.5
) is well-defined and the low frequency components are cancelled, we rewrite (
3.5
) using commutator structure.

B_{k}(\phi,\phi)=\frac{1}{4}\{-[HP_{k}^{+},\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi_{<k}]\phi-[P_{k}^{+},\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi_{<k}]H\phi+iP_{k}^{+}(\phi\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi_{\geq k})-P_{k}^{+}(H\phi\cdot\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi_{\geq k})\}.

By (
2.4
), we are able to shift a derivative from the high-frequency function
\phi
or
H\phi
to the low-frequency function
\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi_{<k}
.
This shift ensures that all such commutator terms are easily estimated.
Then we have

(3.6)

A_{TBO}^{k,+}\tilde{\phi}_{k}^{+}=Q_{k}^{3}(\phi,\phi,\phi)+Q_{k}^{4}(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi),

where
Q_{k}^{3}(\phi,\phi,\phi)
contains only cubic terms in
\phi
, and
Q_{k}^{4}(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi)
contains only quartic terms.
For low frequency case
k=0
, we need no paradifferential component, and also we want to avoid the operator
P_{0}^{+}
which does not have a smooth symbol.
We simply write

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\partial_{x}^{3})\phi_{0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{4}P_{0}(\phi^{2}\phi_{x})-\frac{3}{4}P_{0}(\partial_{x}\phi_{0}H\phi_{x})-\frac{3}{4}P_{0}(\phi_{0}H\phi_{xx})-\frac{3}{4}P_{0}H(\phi_{0}\phi_{xx})-\frac{3}{4}P_{0}H(\partial_{x}\phi_{0}\phi_{x})

(3.7)

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{4}P_{0}(\phi_{>0}H\phi_{xx})-\frac{3}{4}P_{0}(\partial_{x}\phi_{>0}H\phi_{x})-\frac{3}{4}P_{0}H(\phi_{>0}\phi_{xx})-\frac{3}{4}P_{0}H(\partial_{x}\phi_{>0}\phi_{x}).

We only need to eliminate the last four terms on the right hand side of (
3.7
) using normal form transformation.
The other quadratic terms on the right is purely at low frequency and will play a perturbative role.
An easy computation shows that

B_{0}(\phi,\phi)=-\frac{1}{4}[P_{0}(\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi_{>0}\cdot H\phi)+P_{0}H(\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi_{>0}\cdot H\phi)].

Next, we perform gauge transformation to simplify the terms on the left hand side of the equality.
Note that many terms on the left have low frequency parts, so we choose the gauge transform

(3.8)

\psi_{k}^{+}:=\tilde{\phi}_{k}^{+}\cdot e^{-i\Phi_{<k}}.

Here
\Phi(t,x)
is a real auxiliary function to be determined later.
The gauge transform (
3.8
) is similar to the Cole-Hopf transformation.
One observes that the expression works as a normal form transformation, in the sense that it removes the paradifferential quadratic terms.
The difference is that the exponential of an imaginary function is a bounded transformation, whereas the corresponding quadratic normal form is not.
One also sees the difference reflected at the level of cubic or higher order terms
obtained after implementing these transformations.

By applying this gauge transform (
3.8
), we rewrite the equation (
3.6
) as a
nonlinear Airy type equation for our final normal form variable
\psi_{k}^{+}
, with essentially cubic, quartic and quintic nonlinear terms:

(3.9)

(\partial_{t}-\partial_{x}^{3})\psi_{k}^{+}=[\tilde{Q}_{k}^{3}(\phi,\phi,\phi)+\tilde{Q}_{k}^{4}(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi)+\tilde{Q}_{k}^{5}(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi)]e^{i\Phi_{<k}},

where
\tilde{Q}_{k}^{3}
,
\tilde{Q}_{k}^{4}
and
\tilde{Q}_{k}^{5}
contain only cubic, quartic, respectively quintic terms.
Obviously the exponent factor
e^{i\Phi_{<k}}
contributes nothing in
L^{2}
norm.

Direct computation shows

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{\phi}_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}(\psi_{k}^{+}e^{i\Phi_{<k}})=(\partial_{t}\psi_{k}^{+}+i\partial_{t}\Phi_{<k}\psi_{k}^{+})e^{i\Phi_{<k}},

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\tilde{\phi}_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}(\psi_{k}^{+}e^{i\Phi_{<k}})=(\partial_{x}\psi_{k}^{+}+i\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k}\psi_{k}^{+})e^{i\Phi_{<k}},

\displaystyle\partial_{xx}\tilde{\phi}_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle=[\partial_{xx}\psi_{k}^{+}+2i\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k}\partial_{x}\psi_{k}^{+}+i\partial_{xx}\Phi_{<k}\psi_{k}^{+}-(\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k})^{2}\psi_{k}^{+}]e^{i\Phi_{<k}},

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{3}\tilde{\phi}_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle=[\partial_{x}^{3}\psi_{k}^{+}+2i\partial_{xx}\Phi_{<k}\partial_{x}\psi_{k}^{+}+2i\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k}\partial_{xx}\psi_{k}^{+}+i\partial_{x}^{3}\Phi_{<k}\psi_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle+i\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi_{<k}\partial_{x}\psi_{k}^{+}-2\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k}\partial_{xx}\Phi_{<k}\psi_{k}^{+}-(\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k})^{2}\partial_{x}\psi_{k}^{+}+i\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k}\partial_{x}^{2}\psi_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle-2(\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k})^{2}\partial_{x}\psi_{k}^{+}-\partial_{xx}\Phi_{<k}\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k}\psi_{k}^{+}-i(\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k})^{3}\psi_{k}^{+}]e^{i\Phi_{<k}}.

By plugging in above terms into the definition of
A_{TBO}^{k,+}
(
3.3
), we have by using (
3.10
),

\displaystyle e^{-i\Phi_{<k}}A_{TBO}^{k,+}\tilde{\phi}_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{t}-\partial_{x}^{3})\psi_{k}^{+}+(-\frac{3}{2}i\phi_{<k}-3i\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k})\partial_{x}^{2}\psi_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle+[-3i\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi_{<k}+3(\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k})^{2}+3\phi_{<k}\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k}-\frac{3}{2}i\partial_{x}\phi_{<k}]\partial_{x}\psi_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle+[i\partial_{t}\Phi_{<k}-i\partial_{x}^{3}\Phi_{<k}-2i(\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k})^{3}+\frac{3}{2}\partial_{x}\phi_{<k}\partial_{x}\Phi_{<k}]\psi_{k}^{+}.

To eliminate
\partial_{x}^{2}\psi_{k}^{+}
the term, we let its coefficient to be 0, so that

(3.10)

\phi=-2\partial_{x}\Phi,

and by (
3.1
),
\Phi
satisfies the equation

\partial_{t}\Phi=\partial_{x}^{3}\Phi-(\partial_{x}\Phi)^{3}+\frac{3}{2}[\partial_{x}\Phi H\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi+H(\partial_{x}\Phi\partial_{x}^{2}\Phi)].

The coefficient for
\partial_{x}\psi_{k}^{+}
terms becomes

-\frac{3}{4}\phi_{<k}^{2},

and the coefficient for
\psi_{k}^{+}
terms becomes

-\frac{3}{4}(\partial_{x}\phi_{<k})^{2}+\frac{3}{8}iP_{<k}[\phi^{3}+\phi H\phi_{x}+H(\phi\phi_{x})].

Next, we compute each source term
Q^{3}_{k}
and
\tilde{Q}^{i}_{k}
,
i=3,4,5
.
They are given by

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{3}(\phi,\phi,\phi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}P_{k}^{+}\partial_{x}(\phi^{3})+\frac{3}{4}B_{k}\big(\partial_{x}(\phi H\phi_{x}),\phi\big)+\frac{3}{4}B_{k}\big(\phi,\partial_{x}(\phi H\phi_{x})\big)+\frac{3}{4}B_{k}\big(\partial_{x}H(\phi_{x}\phi),\phi\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{3}{4}B_{k}\big(\phi,\partial_{x}H(\phi_{x}\phi)\big)-\frac{3}{2}i\partial_{x}\phi_{<k}\partial_{x}B_{k}(\phi,\phi)-\frac{3}{2}i\phi_{<k}\partial_{x}^{2}B_{k}(\phi,\phi),

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{4}(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}B_{k}\big(\partial_{x}(\phi^{3}),\phi\big)+\frac{1}{4}B_{k}\big(\phi,\partial_{x}(\phi^{3})\big),

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{k}^{3}(\phi,\phi,\phi)

\displaystyle=Q_{k}^{3}(\phi,\phi,\phi)+\frac{3}{4}\phi_{<k}^{2}\partial_{x}\phi_{k}^{+}+\frac{3}{4}(\partial_{x}\phi_{<k})^{2}\phi_{k}^{+}-\frac{3}{8}iP_{<k}\big(\phi H\phi_{x}+H(\phi\phi_{x})\big)\phi_{k}^{+},

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{k}^{4}(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi)

\displaystyle=Q_{k}^{4}(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi)+\frac{3}{8}(i+2)\phi_{<k}^{3}\phi_{k}^{+}-\frac{3}{8}iP_{<k}(\phi^{3})\phi_{k}^{+}+\frac{3}{4}\phi_{<k}^{2}\partial_{x}B_{k}(\phi,\phi)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{3}{4}(\partial_{x}\phi_{<k})^{2}B_{k}(\phi,\phi)-\frac{3}{8}iP_{<k}\big(\phi H\phi_{x}+H(\phi\phi_{x})\big)B_{k}(\phi,\phi),

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{k}^{5}(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{8}(i+2)\phi_{<k}^{3}B_{k}(\phi,\phi)-\frac{3}{8}iP_{<k}(\phi^{3})B_{k}(\phi,\phi).

The case
k=0
is special here as well, we simple set
\psi_{0}=\tilde{\phi}_{0}
, and use the original equation.
Owing to the commutator structure, we can easily obtain a similar estimate.

This concludes the algebraic part of the analysis. Our next goal is study the analytic
properties of those multilinear forms:

Lemma 3.1
.

The quadratic form
B_{k}
can be expressed as

B_{k}(\phi,\phi)=2^{-k}L_{k}(H\phi_{<k},\phi_{k})+2^{-k}L_{k}(\phi_{<k},H\phi_{k})+\sum_{j\geq k}2^{-j}L_{k}(\phi_{j},\phi_{j})=2^{-k}L_{k}(\phi,\phi).

For multilinear expressions
Q_{k}^{i}
and
\tilde{Q}_{k}^{i}
, if we define
\bar{\phi}
to be either
\phi
or
H\phi
, then they can be written in translation invariant forms of the type

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{3}(\phi,\phi,\phi)=2^{k}L_{k}(\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}),

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{4}(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi)=L_{k}(\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}),

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{k}^{3}(\phi,\phi,\phi)=2^{k}L_{k}(\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}),

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{k}^{4}(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi)=L_{k}(\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}),

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{k}^{5}(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi)=2^{-k}L_{k}(\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}),

all with output frequency
2^{k}
.

Proof.

For first 2 terms of
B_{k}(\phi,\phi)
we use the properties of commutator structure to shift a derivative from high-frequency to low-frequency function.
For the two remaining terms we split the unlocalized
\phi
factor into

\phi_{<k}+\phi_{\geq k}
. The terms involving
\phi_{<k}
become a part of
2^{-k}L_{k}(H\phi_{<k},\phi_{k})+2^{-k}L_{k}(\phi_{<k},H\phi_{k})
since we have

\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi_{\geq k}=2^{-k}L(\phi).

For the remaining bilinear terms in
\phi_{\geq k}
the frequencies of two inputs must be balanced at some frequency
2^{j}
with
j\geq k
.
The rest expressions follow from direct computations and that of
B_{k}(\phi,\phi)
.

Next, we consider each term of
Q_{k}^{3}(\phi,\phi,\phi)
separately.
The first term is direct, since the spacial derivative
\partial_{x}
is equivalent to the factor
2^{k}
under the frequency projection operator
P_{k}
.
For next four terms, we can use the expression for
B_{k}(\phi,\phi)
, for some terms we may use the properties of commutator structure.
For example,

\displaystyle B_{k}(\partial_{x}(\phi H\phi_{x}),\phi)=-\frac{1}{4}\{[HP_{k}^{+},P_{<k}(\phi H\phi_{x})]\phi+[P_{k}^{+},P_{<k}(\phi H\phi_{x})]\phi

\displaystyle-iP_{k}[\partial_{x}(\phi H\phi_{x})\cdot\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi_{\geq k}]+P_{k}^{+}[H\partial_{x}(\phi H\phi_{x})\cdot\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi_{\geq k}]\}.

The first two equals
L_{k}(\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}_{x})
.
For the third term

P_{k}[\partial_{x}(\phi H\phi_{x})\cdot\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi_{\geq k}]=P_{k}\partial_{x}[\phi H\phi_{x}\cdot\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi_{\geq k}]-P_{k}[\phi H\phi_{x}\phi_{\geq k}],

it equals
L_{k}(\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}_{x})
due to the fact
\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi_{\geq k}=2^{-k}\phi
.
The fourth term is similar to the third term.
As for the last four terms of
Q_{k}^{3}(\phi,\phi,\phi)
, it follows directly from the above expression of
B_{k}(\phi,\phi)
.
Note that

L_{k}(\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}_{x})=L_{k}(\partial_{x}(\bar{\phi})^{3})=2^{k}L_{k}(\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi}),

we can obtain the final expression for
Q_{k}^{3}(\phi,\phi,\phi)
.
The proof for other
Q_{k}^{i}
or
\tilde{Q}^{i}_{k}
is similar.
∎

3.2.
Bounds using the bootstrap argument

We already know from
[
14
]
that the equation (
1.1
) is
H^{2}
globally well-posed.
We prove further an apriori estimate using a standard continuity argument.
We first assume that the initial data is small in
L^{2}
norm,

(3.11)

\|\phi(0)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\epsilon,

for a small positive constant
\epsilon
.
Then our main apriori estimate is as follows:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{c_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}\in l^{2}
, and
\epsilon c_{k}
be a frequency envelope of
\phi
in
L^{2}
, so that the initial condition (
3.11
) is strengthen to

(3.12)

\|\phi_{k}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\epsilon c_{k},

as explained in Section
2
.
Let
0<t\leq 1
and
j
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define

(3.13)

M(t):=\sup_{j}c_{j}^{-2}\|P_{j}\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{0}[0,t]}+\sup_{j\neq k}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}c_{j}^{-1}c_{k}^{-1}2^{\text{max}\{j,k\}}\|\phi_{j}T_{y}\phi_{k}\|_{L^{2}[0,t]}.

For some fixed
t_{0}
,
M(t_{0})\lesssim\epsilon^{2}.

To prove this, for a large constant
C
which is independent of
\epsilon
,
we use a bootstrap argument by assuming that

M(t_{0})\leq C^{2}\epsilon^{2},

and then to show the following improved result

M(t_{0})\lesssim\epsilon^{2}+C^{6}\epsilon^{6}.

Given the bootstrap assumption, we have the starting estimate

(3.14)

\|\phi_{k}\|_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim Cc_{k}\epsilon,

and

(3.15)

\|\phi_{j}T_{y}\phi_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-max\{j,k\}}C^{2}\epsilon^{2}c_{j}c_{k},\qquad j\neq k,\quad y\in\mathbb{R},

where in the bilinear case, we also allow
j=k
provided the two localization multipliers are at least
2^{k-4}
separated.
In the following step, we will consider first the bound for
\psi_{k}^{+}
,
and obtain the bound for
\phi_{k}^{+}
according to the normal form analysis.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume (
3.12
), then we have

\|\psi_{k}^{+}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\epsilon c_{k}.

Proof.

Obviously, the
L^{2}
norms of
\psi_{k}^{+}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{k}^{+}
are the same, it only suffices to prove the estimate for
\tilde{\phi}_{k}^{+}
.
Thus, we need to prove that
L^{2}
norm of
\tilde{\phi}_{k}^{+}
is comparable with the
L^{2}
norm of

\phi_{k}^{+}
.
The two variables are related via the relation (
3.4
), and we reduce our problem to the study of the
L^{2}
bound for the bilinear form
B_{k}(\phi,\phi)
.
From lemma
3.1
, we know that

B_{k}\left(\phi(0),\phi(0)\right)=2^{-k}L_{k}\left(\bar{\phi}_{<k}(0),\bar{\phi}_{k}(0)\right)+\sum_{j\geq k}2^{-j}L_{k}\left(\phi_{j}(0),\phi_{j}(0)\right).

If we apply the Bernstein’s inequality,

\|\phi_{<k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq 2^{\frac{k}{2}}\|\phi_{<k}\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

and the Hölder’s inequality, then we have

\|2^{-k}L_{k}\left(\bar{\phi}_{<k}(0),\bar{\phi}_{k}(0)\right)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{k}{2}}\|\phi_{<k}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\|\phi_{k}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2^{-\frac{k}{2}}c_{k}\epsilon^{2},

and

\|\sum_{j\geq k}2^{-j}L_{k}\left(\phi_{j}(0),\phi_{j}(0)\right)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq k}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\cdot\epsilon\|\phi_{j}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq k}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\epsilon^{2}\cdot c_{j}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{k}{2}}\cdot c_{k}\epsilon^{2}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

In a similar fashion, we can obtain the bound

(3.16)

\|B_{k}(\phi,\phi)\|_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{k}{2}}\|\phi_{<k}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\|\phi_{k}\|_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{k}{2}}C^{2}c_{k}\epsilon^{2}.

For next part, we consider the right hand side of the equation (
3.9
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume the bootstrap assumptions (
3.14
) and (
3.15
), then we have

\|\tilde{Q}_{k}^{3}\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}+\|\tilde{Q}_{k}^{4}\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}+\|\tilde{Q}_{k}^{5}\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim C^{3}\epsilon^{3}(1+C\epsilon+C^{2}\epsilon^{2}).

Proof.

First for
\tilde{Q}_{k}^{3}
terms, let us assume that the first entry of
L_{k}
is localized at frequency
2^{k_{1}}
, the second at frequency
2^{k_{2}}

•

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\frac{k_{2}}{4}}\|L(\phi_{k_{1}},\phi_{k_{3}})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|\phi_{k_{2}}\|_{L_{t}^{4}\dot{W}_{x}^{\frac{1}{4},\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k_{3}-\frac{k_{2}}{4}}C^{3}\epsilon^{3}c_{k_{1}}c_{k_{2}}c_{k_{3}}\lesssim 2^{-k}C^{3}c_{k}^{3}\epsilon^{3}.

•

If

If

Together with the bound for

(
3.16
), we obtain the Strichartz component of the bootstrap argument.

We then consider the bilinear estimate in our bootstrap argument.
According to the bilinear Strichartz estimate for Airy-type equation,
if we define truncation operators
\tilde{P}_{j}
and
\tilde{P}_{k}
which
still have
2^{max\{j,k\}}
separated supports but whose symbols are identically
1
in the support of
P_{j}
, respectively
P_{k}
, then by Corollary
2.6
,

\|\tilde{P}_{j}\psi_{j}^{+}\cdot\tilde{P}_{k}\psi_{k}^{+}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim\epsilon^{2}2^{-max\{j,k\}}c_{j}c_{k}(\epsilon^{2}+C^{6}\epsilon^{6}).

At last, we need to transfer the bound to
\phi_{j}^{+}\phi_{k}^{+}
.

\tilde{P}_{j}\psi_{j}^{+}\cdot\tilde{P}_{k}\psi_{k}^{+}-\phi_{j}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}\phi_{k}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<k}}=\tilde{P}_{j}\psi_{j}^{+}(\tilde{P}_{k}\psi_{k}^{+}-\phi_{k}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<k}})+(\tilde{P}_{j}\psi_{j}^{+}-\phi_{j}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<j}})\phi_{k}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<k}}.

For the first component, note that

(3.17)

\|\psi_{k}^{+}-\phi_{k}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<k}}\|_{L_{t}^{4}\dot{W}_{x}^{\frac{1}{4},\infty}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{k}{2}}\|\phi_{k}\|_{S}\|\phi_{<k}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\sum_{j\geq k}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\|\phi_{j}\|_{S}\|\phi_{<j}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{k}{2}}C^{2}\epsilon^{2}c_{k},

\|\tilde{P}_{j}\psi_{j}^{+}(\tilde{P}_{k}\psi_{k}^{+}-\phi_{k}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<k}})\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{k}{4}}\|\psi_{j}^{+}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\|\psi_{k}^{+}-\phi_{k}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<k}}\|_{L_{t}^{4}\dot{W}_{x}^{\frac{1}{4},\infty}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{3k}{4}}C^{3}\epsilon^{3}c_{j}c_{k}.

For the second term, we rewrite it in a commutator structure, and use the property of commutator (
2.4
).

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{j}\psi_{j}^{+}-\phi_{j}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}

\displaystyle=(\tilde{P}_{j}-1)(\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<j}})+B_{j}(\phi,\phi)e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}

\displaystyle=[\tilde{P}_{j}-1,e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}]\phi_{j}^{+}+(\tilde{P}_{j}-1)\left(B_{j}(\phi,\phi)e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}\right)-e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}\tilde{P}_{j}B_{j}(\phi,\phi)

\displaystyle=2^{-j}L(\partial_{x}e^{-i\Phi_{<j}},\phi_{j}^{+})+L\left(B_{j}(\phi,\phi),e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{-j}L(\phi_{<j},\phi_{j},e^{-i\Phi_{<j}})+\sum_{l>j}2^{-l}L(\phi_{l},\phi_{l},e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}).

Thus we use the bilinear estimate and Bernstein’s inequality to get

\displaystyle\|(\tilde{P}_{j}\psi_{j}^{+}-\phi_{j}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<j}})\phi_{k}^{+}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j}\|L(\phi_{k},\phi_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}\|\phi_{<j}^{+}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\infty}}+\sum_{l>j}2^{-l}\|L(\phi_{l},\phi_{k})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}\|\phi_{l}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-j-max\{j,k\}}C^{2}\epsilon^{2}c_{j}c_{k}2^{\frac{j}{2}}C\epsilon+\sum_{l>j}2^{-l-max\{l,k\}}C^{3}\epsilon^{3}c_{l}^{2}2^{\frac{l}{2}}c_{k}\lesssim 2^{-max\{j,k\}}c_{j}^{2}c_{k}C^{3}\epsilon^{3},

which is a small error term.
In this way, we complete the bootstrap argument.

4.
Linearized third order Benjamin Ono equation

The linearized equation of (
1.1
) is

(4.1)

v_{t}-v_{xxx}+\frac{3}{2}\phi\phi_{x}v+\frac{3}{4}\phi^{2}v_{x}+\frac{3}{4}v_{x}H\phi_{x}+\frac{3}{4}\phi_{x}Hv_{x}+\frac{3}{4}vH\phi_{xx}+\frac{3}{4}\phi Hv_{xx}+\frac{3}{4}H(v_{xx}\phi+\phi_{xx}v+2v_{x}\phi_{x})=0.

Understanding the properties of the linearized flow is critical for any local well-posedness
result.
In this section, we show the following result:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\phi
be a
H^{3}
solution to the third order Benjamin-Ono equation in
[0,1]
with small
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
norm. Then the linearized equation (
4.1
) is well-posed in
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, and satisfies the inequality

(4.2)

\|v\|_{C(0,1;H^{-\frac{1}{2}})}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}}.

with a universal implicit constant that does not depend on the
H^{3}
norm of
\phi
.

The rest of the section is devoted to the proof of this proposition.

First, we show that the
L^{2}
norm of this linearized equation can be controlled by the initial data given that
\phi
is a
H^{3}
solution of (
1.1
).
Multiplying by
v
, and using the integration by part tricks and the properties of Hilbert transform (
2.1
) (
2.2
), we see that
term like
vv_{xxx}
vanishes.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{4}\int[\partial_{x}(\phi^{2})v^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}\partial_{x}(v^{2})-vv_{x}H\phi_{x}-\phi_{x}vHv_{x}-v^{2}H\phi_{xx}]dx

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|\phi_{xx}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}(\phi^{2})\|_{L^{\infty}})\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

\|\phi_{xx}\|_{L^{\infty}}
can be bounded by
\|\phi\|_{H^{3}}
by the Sobolev embedding.
Here, the most difficult term is
\phi_{x}Hv\cdot v_{x}
.
Note that according to the convolution identity of Hilbert transform (
2.3
),

Hv\cdot v_{x}=-vHv_{x}+H[v_{x}\cdot v]-H[Hv_{x}\cdot Hv].

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\int\phi_{x}\cdot Hv_{x}\cdot vdx=-\int\phi_{xx}Hv\cdot vdx-\int\phi_{x}Hv\cdot v_{x}dx

\displaystyle=-\int\phi_{xx}Hv\cdot vdx-\frac{1}{2}\int\phi_{x}(H[v_{x}\cdot v]-H[Hv_{x}\cdot Hv])dx.

These last three terms is easy to handle. By Gronwall’s inequality,

\|v\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}},

which shows the uniqueness of
v
in
L^{2}
.
For the backward adjoint linearized equation

\partial_{t}w-w_{xxx}-\frac{3}{2}\phi\phi_{x}w+\frac{3}{4}(\phi^{2}w)_{x}+\frac{3}{4}w_{x}H\phi_{x}+\frac{3}{4}H(w_{x}\phi)_{x}+\frac{3}{4}Hw_{xx}\phi=0,

with initial data

w(1)=w_{1},

we similarly have

\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\|w_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{2}},

which proves the existence for the direct problem.
Following the method in
[
9
]
by consider a equation of
v_{1}=v_{x}
, we have that the linearized equation (
4.1
) is well-posed in
H^{1}
.

Next, in order to prove Proposition
4.1
, it suffices to show that the
H^{1}

solutions
v
constructed above satisfy the bound (
4.2
).
Suppose for initial condition,
\|v(0)\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\leq 1
, and consider a frequency envelop
\{d_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
for
v(0)
in
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
with the property
c_{k}\lesssim d_{k}
, where
c_{k}
represents an
L^{2}
frequency envelope for
\phi(0)
as in the previous section.
Then the initial condition is strengthen to

\|v_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2^{\frac{k}{2}}d_{k}.

We aim to prove that the dyadic pieces
v_{k}
of
v
satisfy the estimates

\|v_{k}\|_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim 2^{\frac{k}{2}}d_{k},

as well as the bilinear
L^{2}
estimates

\|L(v_{j},\phi_{k})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim\epsilon d_{j}c_{k}2^{\frac{j}{2}}\cdot 2^{-max\{j,k\}}.

To start with, we will use the bootstrap argument, assuming

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|v_{k}\|_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim C2^{\frac{k}{2}}d_{k},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|L(v_{j},\phi_{k})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim\epsilon Cd_{j}c_{k}2^{\frac{j}{2}}\cdot 2^{-max\{j,k\}}.

and prove that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|v_{k}\|_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim(1+\epsilon C)2^{\frac{k}{2}}d_{k},

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|L(v_{j},\phi_{k})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim\epsilon(1+\epsilon C)d_{j}c_{k}2^{\frac{j}{2}}\cdot 2^{-max\{j,k\}}.

We consider each frequency component, and proceed in the same manner as for the nonlinear equation, rewriting the linearized equation in paradifferential form as

\displaystyle A_{HBO}^{k,+}v_{k}^{+}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}P_{k}^{+}(\phi\phi_{x}v)-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(\phi^{2}v_{x})-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(v_{x}H\phi_{x})-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(\phi_{x}Hv_{x})

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(vH\phi_{xx})-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(\phi Hv_{xx})+\frac{3}{4}iP_{k}^{+}(v_{xx}\phi+\phi_{xx}v+2v_{x}\phi_{x})

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{2}i\partial_{x}\phi_{<k}v_{x}-\frac{3}{2}i\phi_{<k}v_{xx}.

We then apply the normal form transformation to eliminate quadratic terms on the right.
The first 5 quadratic terms are eliminated by

(4.7)

\frac{1}{4}[iP_{k}^{+}(\phi\cdot\partial_{x}^{-1}v)+iP_{k}^{+}(v\cdot\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi)-P_{k}^{+}(H\phi\cdot\partial_{x}^{-1}v)-P_{k}^{+}(Hv\cdot\partial_{x}^{-1}\phi)].

Then the last 2 terms are eliminated by

(4.8)

\frac{1}{2}i\partial_{x}^{-1}(P_{<k}\phi)P_{k}^{+}v.

Note that if we add

\frac{1}{2}i\partial_{x}^{-1}(P_{<k}v)P_{k}^{+}\phi

to (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), then it becomes exactly
2B_{k}(v,\phi)

B_{k}
stands for the symmetric bilinear form associated to the quadratic form
B_{k}
defined

3.5
).
Since
\partial_{x}^{-1}v

<1
.
Hence, our quadratic normal form is chosen to be

B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,v)=2B_{k}(v,\phi)-\frac{1}{2}i\partial_{x}^{-1}v_{(0,k)}P_{k}^{+}\phi.

In this way, define

\tilde{v}_{k}^{+}=v_{k}^{+}+B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,v).

Then equation for
\tilde{v}_{k}^{+}

A_{HBO}^{k,+}\tilde{v}_{k}^{+}=Q_{k}^{2,lin}(v_{0},\phi_{k})+Q_{k}^{3,lin}(\phi,\phi,v)+Q_{k}^{4,lin}(\phi,\phi,\phi,v).

Calculations show that

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{2,lin}(v_{0},\phi_{k})

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}i(\partial_{x}v_{0}\partial_{x}\phi_{k}^{+}+v_{0}\partial_{xx}\phi_{k}^{+}),

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{3,lin}(\phi,\phi,v)

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{4}P_{k}^{+}(\phi^{2}v)_{x}+\frac{3}{4}B_{k}^{lin}\left((\phi H\phi_{x})_{x},v\right)+\frac{3}{4}B_{k}^{lin}(H(\phi\phi_{x})_{x},v)

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{4}B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,(vH\phi_{x})_{x})+\frac{3}{4}B_{k}^{lin}\left(\phi,(\phi Hv_{x})_{x}\right)+\frac{3}{4}B_{k}^{lin}\left(\phi,H(v\phi)_{xx}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{2}i\partial_{x}\phi_{<k}\partial_{x}B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,v)-\frac{3}{2}i\phi_{<k}\partial_{x}^{2}B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,v),

\displaystyle Q_{k}^{4,lin}(\phi,\phi,\phi,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}B_{k}^{lin}\left((\phi^{3})_{x},v\right)+\frac{3}{4}B_{k}^{lin}\left(\phi,(\phi^{2}v)_{x}\right).

Again there is a straightforward adjustment in this analysis for the case
k=0
, following the model in Section
3
.
This adds a trivial low frequency quadratic term on the right.

Finally, for
k>0
, we perform renomalization of
\tilde{v}_{k}^{+}
by

w_{k}^{+}:=e^{-i\Phi_{<k}}\tilde{v}_{k}^{+},

which in turn solves the inhomogeneous Airy-type equation

(4.9)

(\partial_{t}-\partial_{x}^{3})w_{k}^{+}=[Q_{k}^{2,lin}(v_{0},\phi_{k}^{+})+\tilde{Q}_{k}^{3,lin}(\phi,\phi,v)+\tilde{Q}_{k}^{4,lin}(\phi,\phi,\phi,v)+\tilde{Q}_{k}^{5,lin}(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi,v)]e^{-i\Phi_{<k}},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{k}^{3,lin}(\phi,\phi,v)

\displaystyle=Q_{k}^{3,lin}(\phi,\phi,v)+\frac{3}{4}(2i-1)\phi_{<k}^{2}\partial_{x}v_{k}^{+}+\frac{3}{4}(\partial_{x}\phi_{<k})^{2}v_{k}^{+}-\frac{3}{8}iP_{<k}\big(\phi H\phi_{x}+H(\phi\phi_{x})\big)v_{k}^{+},

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{k}^{4,lin}(\phi,\phi,\phi,v)

\displaystyle=Q_{k}^{4,lin}(\phi,\phi,\phi,v)+\frac{3}{8}(i+2)\phi_{<k}^{3}v_{k}^{+}-\frac{3}{8}iP_{<k}(\phi^{3})v_{k}^{+}+\frac{3}{4}(2i-1)\phi_{<k}^{2}\partial_{x}B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,v)

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{4}(\partial_{x}\phi_{<k})^{2}B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,v)-\frac{3}{8}iP_{<k}\big(\phi H\phi_{x}+H(\phi\phi_{x})\big)B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,v),

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{k}^{5,lin}(\phi,\phi,\phi,\phi,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{8}(i+2)\phi_{<k}^{3}B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,v)-\frac{3}{8}iP_{<k}(\phi^{3})B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,v).

Clearly,
B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,v)
can be written as

(4.10)

B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,v)=2^{-k}L(\phi_{<k},v_{k})+\sum_{j\in(0,k)}2^{-j}L(\phi_{k},v_{j})+\sum_{j\geq k}2^{-j}L(\phi_{j},v_{j}).

Our goal is now to estimate the initial data for
w_{k}
in
L^{2}
and the inhomogeneous term in
L^{1}_{t}L_{x}^{2}
.
We begin with the initial data, for which we have

Lemma 4.2
.

The initial data
w_{k}(0)
satisfies

(4.11)

\|w_{k}^{+}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{\frac{k}{2}}d_{k}.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the same estimate for
\tilde{v}_{k}^{+}
, which in turn reduced to establish the bound for
B_{k}^{lin}\left(\phi(0),v(0)\right)
.
For three components in (
4.10
),

\displaystyle\|2^{-k}L_{k}\left(\phi_{<k}(0),v_{k}(0)\right)\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2^{-\frac{k}{2}}\|\phi(0)\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{k}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\epsilon d_{k},

\displaystyle\|\sum_{j\geq k}2^{-j}L_{k}\left(\phi_{j}(0),v_{j}(0)\right)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq k}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\cdot\epsilon\|v_{j}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq k}\epsilon\cdot d_{j}\lesssim\epsilon d_{k},

\displaystyle\|\sum_{j\in(0,k)}2^{-j}L(\phi_{k},v_{j})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\sum_{j\in(0,k)}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\|\phi_{k}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|v_{j}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\epsilon d_{k}.

Here we use the slow varying property of frequency envelope.
∎

As is discussed in Section
3
, we consider the inhomogeneous term:

Lemma 4.3
.

The inhomogeneous terms in the
w_{k}^{+}
equation (
4.9
) satisfy

\|Q^{2,lin}_{k}\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}+\|\tilde{Q}^{3,lin}_{k}\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}+\|\tilde{Q}^{4,lin}_{k}\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}+\|\tilde{Q}^{5,lin}_{k}\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim 2^{\frac{k}{2}}C\epsilon d_{k}.

Proof.

The
Q_{k}^{2,lin}
term is easily estimated in

using the bilinear estimates in the bootstrap assumption (

).

\|Q^{2,lin}_{k}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\lesssim\epsilon Cc_{k}\cdot 2^{-k+2k-\frac{k}{2}}\lesssim 2^{\frac{k}{2}}C\epsilon d_{k}.

Then for the term
B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,v)
, we only need to change a bit from the proof for initial data:

\|B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,v)\|_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim C\epsilon d_{k}.

For
\tilde{Q}_{k}^{3,lin}
terms, we have similar estimate for
L_{k}(\bar{\phi},\bar{\phi},v)
, where
\bar{\phi}
is either
\phi
or
H\phi
.
The only exception is the terms like
\partial_{x}^{-1}v_{(0,k)}P_{k}^{+}\partial_{x}(\phi H\phi_{x})
, which admits the expansion

\partial_{x}^{-1}v_{(0,k)}P_{k}^{+}\partial_{x}(\phi H\phi_{x})=\sum_{j\in\{0,k\}}2^{-j+2k}L_{k}(\bar{\phi}_{k},\bar{\phi}_{<k},v_{j})+\sum_{j\in\{0,k\}}\sum_{l\geq k}2^{-j+2k}L_{k}(\bar{\phi_{l}},\bar{\phi_{l}},v_{j}).

Here we necessarily have two unbalanced frequencies, therefore this expression can be estimated by a bilinear

estimate plus a Strichartz estimate.

The rest of proof is identical to the similar argument as lemma
3.4
.
∎

On account of the estimate for
w_{k}(0)
(
4.11
), the inhomogeneous terms and
B_{k}^{lin}(\phi,v)
, we prove the bound for
v_{k}^{+}
.

To recover the bilinear

bounds we again use the bilinear Strichartz estimate,

\|\tilde{P}_{j}w_{j}^{+}\cdot\tilde{P}_{k}\psi_{k}^{+}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim\epsilon d_{j}c_{k}2^{\frac{j}{2}}2^{-max\{j,k\}}.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
j<k
.
To transform this bound to
v_{j}^{+}\phi_{k}^{+}
we write

\tilde{P}_{j}w_{j}^{+}\tilde{P}_{j}\psi_{k}^{+}-v_{j}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}\phi_{k}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<k}}=\tilde{P}_{j}w_{j}^{+}(\tilde{P}_{k}\psi_{k}^{+}-\phi_{k}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<k}})+(\tilde{P}_{j}w_{j}^{+}-v_{j}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<j}})\phi_{k}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<k}}.

For the first term, we use (

For the second term, we rewrite the first factor in a commutator structure as

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{j}w_{j}^{+}-v_{j}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}

\displaystyle=(\tilde{P}_{j}-1)(v_{j}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<j}})+B_{j}^{lin}(\phi,v)e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}

\displaystyle=[\tilde{P}_{j}-1,e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}]v_{j}^{+}+e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}(\tilde{P}_{j}-1)B_{j}(\phi,v)+B_{j}^{lin}(\phi,v)e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}

\displaystyle=2^{-j}L(\partial_{x}e^{-i\Phi_{<j}},\phi_{j}^{+})+L\left(B_{j}^{lin}(\phi,v),e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{-j}L(\phi_{<j},v_{j},e^{-i\Phi_{<j}})+2^{-j}L(v_{<j},\phi_{j},e^{-i\Phi_{<j}})+2^{-j}L(\partial_{x}^{-1}v_{(0,j)},\phi_{j},e^{-i\Phi_{<j}})

\displaystyle+\sum_{l>j}2^{-l}L(v_{l},\phi_{l},e^{-i\Phi_{<j}}).

Now we multiply this by
\phi_{k}^{+}
, discard the exponential factor and estimate in

using our bootstrap hypothesis (

).
For

we can use a bilinear

estimate combined with an

bound obtained via Bernstein’s inequality.
For

We obtain

\|(\tilde{P}_{j}w_{j}^{+}-v_{j}^{+}e^{-i\Phi_{<j}})\phi_{k}^{+}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim C\epsilon^{2}2^{-\frac{k}{2}}d_{j}d_{k}.

Till now, we manage to prove (

) and (
4.6
), and this leads to (

).

5.
L^{2}
well-posedness for third order Benjamin-Ono equation

In this section, we show that the equation
(
1.1
)
is locally well-posed in
L^{2}
, so that it becomes globally well-posed because of the conservation of mass.
By scaling we can assume that our initial data satisfies

(5.1)

\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\epsilon\ll 1.

and prove well-posedness up to time
T=1
.
If the initial data is in
H^{2}
then the solution exists(see
[
12
]
).
And according to the bounds for linearized equation (
4.2
), we can compare two solutions in
\mathcal{S}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,

(5.2)

\|\phi^{(1)}-\phi^{(2)}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\|\phi^{(1)}(0)-\phi^{(2)}(0)\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}}.

We call this property
weak Lipschitz dependence on the initial data
(see
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
).

Next we use this property to construct solution from smooth solutions as a limit for
L^{2}
data.
Given any initial data
\phi_{0}\in L^{2}
satisfying (
5.1
), we consider the corresponding regularized data

\phi^{(n)}(0)=P_{<n}\phi_{0}.

Then these satisfy

\|\phi^{(n)}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\epsilon,\qquad\|P_{k}\phi^{(n)}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\epsilon c_{k}.

By virtue of Theorem
3.2
, the corresponding solutions
\phi^{(n)}
exist in time
[0,1]
, and satisfy the uniform bounds

(5.3)

\|P_{k}\phi^{(n)}\|_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim\epsilon c_{k}.

On the other hand, for their difference,

\|\phi^{(n)}-\phi^{(m)}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\|\phi^{(n)}(0)-\phi^{(m)}(0)\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim(2^{-n}+2^{-m})\epsilon.

Thus the sequence
\phi^{(n)}
converges to some function
\phi
in space
S^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, and

\|\phi^{(n)}-\phi\|_{\mathcal{S}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim 2^{-n}\epsilon.

In particular, for each dyadic component, we have the convergence in
S
.
Therefore the function
\phi
inherits the dyadic bounds (
5.3
)

(5.4)

\|P_{k}\phi\|_{\mathcal{S}}\lesssim\epsilon c_{k}.

This further allows us to prove convergence in
l^{2}S
.
For fixed
k
, we write

\lim\sup\|\phi^{(n)}-\phi\|_{l^{2}\mathcal{S}}\leq\lim\sup\|P_{<k}(\phi^{(n)}-\phi)\|_{l^{2}\mathcal{S}}+\|P_{\geq k}\phi\|_{l^{2}\mathcal{S}}+\lim\sup\|P_{\geq k}\phi^{(n)}\|_{l^{2}\mathcal{S}}\lesssim c_{\geq k}.

Letting
k\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

\lim\|\phi^{(n)}-\phi\|_{l^{2}\mathcal{S}}=0.

This property also implies uniform convergence in
C((0,1),L^{2})
;
it in turn allows us to pass to the limit in the third order Benjamin-Ono equation, and prove that the limit
\phi
solves the equation in the sense of distributions.

Thus, for each initial data
\phi_{0}\in L^{2}
we have obtained a weak solution
\phi\in l^{2}\mathcal{S}
, as the limit of the solutions with regularized data.
Furthermore, this solution satisfies the frequency envelope bound (
5.4
).
Now we consider the dependence of these weak solutions on the initial data.
Suppose that a sequence of initial data
\phi^{(n)}(0)
satisfying (
5.1
) converges uniformly to
\phi_{0}
in
L^{2}
, then by the weak Lipschitz dependence

\phi^{(n)}\rightarrow\phi\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{S}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence for the corresponding solutions we estimate

\phi^{(n)}-\phi=P_{<k}(\phi^{(n)}-\phi)+P_{\geq k}\phi^{(n)}-P_{\geq k}\phi.

Here the first term on the right converges to zero in
l^{2}\mathcal{S}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
by the weak Lipschitz dependence (
5.2
), and the last term converges to zero by the frequency envelope bound (
5.4
).
Hence,

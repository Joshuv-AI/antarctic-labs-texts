Optimization Methods Rooting in Optimal Control
Huanshui Zhang, Hongxia Wang

Abstract
In the paper, we propose solving optimization problems (OPs) and understanding the Newton method from the optimal control view. We propose a new optimization algorithm based on the optimal control problem (OCP). The algorithm features converging more rapidly than gradient descent, meanwhile, it is superior to Newton’s method because it is not divergent in general and can be applied in the case of a singular Hessian matrix. These merits are supported by the convergence analysis for the algorithm in the paper. We also point out that the convergence rate of the proposed algorithm is inversely proportional to the magnitude of the control weight matrix and proportional to the control terminal time inherited from OCP.

Introduction

The research history of optimization problems is long and rich, spanning several centuries and evolving with the development of mathematics, engineering, operations research, and computer science. This originates from that optimization problems are widespread in the modeling of real-world systems for a very broad range of applications. Such applications include economies of scale, fixed charges, finance, allocation and location problems, structural optimization, engineering design, network and transportation problems, chip design and database problems, mechanical design, chemical engineering design and control, molecular biology, and several other combinatorial optimization problems such as integer programming and related graph problems. II
Resolve optimization problem by using optimal control method

Assume that
f(x)
is twice continuously
differentiable. In most practical settings, one would almost surely need to resort to numerical
techniques to address the optimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{x}f(x).

(1)

The traditional numerical method, for instance, gradient descent or Newton’s method, directly finds zeros of
f^{\prime}(x)
by an update recursion

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=\Phi(x_{k})

(2)

with
\Phi(\cdot)
being a designed function.

Instead, we will propose an updated relation with the aid of the following OCP:

\displaystyle\min_{u}\sum_{k=0}^{N}[f(x_{k})+u_{k}^{\prime}Ru_{k}]+f(x_{N+1}),

(3)

\displaystyle\mbox{subject to~}x_{k+1}=x_{k}+u_{k},

(4)

where
x_{k}
and
u_{k}
are the state and control of system (
4
), respectively, integer
N>0
is the control terminal time, positive definite matrix
R
is the control weight matrix, and (
3
) is the cost functional of OCP, closely related to OP (
1
).

Remark 1
.

It can be seen from (
3
) that the optimization algorithm based on OCP will be fast because
u_{k}
in (
4
) is chosen to minimize
f(x_{k+1})
.

Remark 2
.

According
to OCP (
3
)-(
4
), the smaller the weight matrix
R
is, the more control energy is allowed to be
consumed for minimization, i.e., the larger the update step size
u_{k}
in (
4
) is, the more rapidly the
iteration scheme (
4
) may converge to the extremal point of OP (
1
).

Remark 3
.

OCP (
3
)-(
4
) degenerates into
\min_{u_{0}}f(x_{1})
subject
to (
4
) without restriction of consumed control energy.

Lemma 1
.

The optimal control strategy of OCP (
3
)-(
4
) admits

\displaystyle u_{k}=-R^{-1}\sum_{i=k+1}^{N+1}f^{\prime}(x_{i}).

(5)

Proof.

The conclusion is direct by using the maximum principle. We thus omit its proof.
∎

Take (
5
) as an update direction. Then it can be obtained from (
4
) an update relation

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}-R^{-1}\sum_{i=k+1}^{N+1}f^{\prime}(x_{i}).

(6)

Although the relation (
6
) is implicit, some explicit approximation implementations are possible. We will give an explicit approximation recursion later.

It should be stressed that the proposal of a new update scheme is not our ultimate goal. We are also interested in revealing the relationship between existing methods and our methods.

III
The proposed algorithm and convergence analysis

To explore the relationship between the existing method and our method, we reformulate the update relation (
6
) now.
Make the first-order Taylor expansion of
f^{\prime}(x_{i})
at point
x_{i-1}
in (
6
). It can be derived that

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}-g_{k},k=0,\cdots,N,

(7)

\displaystyle g_{k}=\frac{1}{R+f^{(2)}_{k}}(f^{\prime}_{k}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{N}(f^{\prime}_{i}-f^{(2)}_{i}g_{i})),g_{N}=\frac{f^{\prime}_{N}}{R+f^{(2)}_{N}},

(8)

where
f^{\prime}_{k}\dot{=}f^{\prime}(x_{k})
and
f^{(2)}_{k}\dot{=}f^{(2)}(x_{k})
.

To facilitate calculation, we replace all
x_{i},i\geq k+1
, with
x_{k}
in the right-hand side of (
8

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\bar{g}_{k}(x_{k}),k=0,\dots,N,

(9)

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{l}=\frac{1}{R+f^{(2)}_{k}}(f^{\prime}(x)+R\bar{g}_{l+1}(x)),\bar{g}_{N}=\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{R+f^{(2)}(x)},l=N-1,N-2,\dots,k.

(10)

According to (
9
)-(
10
), the following algorithm is proposed.

Algorithm 1
:

1). Fix integer
N>0
and prescribe any
x_{0}
;

2). Carry out the update relation (
9
)-(
10
) and obtain
x_{N+1}
;

Remark 4
.

In the case of
R=0

)-(
10

In the sequel, we will make some convergence analysis pertinent to Algorithm 1. To compare with Newton’s method, we assume that
f(x)

Theorem 1
.

Assume
f^{\prime}(x_{*})=0
and
f^{(2)}(x_{*})>0
. Consider the iteration scheme

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=

\displaystyle x_{k}-\bar{g}_{0}(x_{k}),

(11)

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{l}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R+f^{(2)}(x)}[f^{\prime}(x)+R\bar{g}_{l+1}(x)],\bar{g}_{N}=\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{R+f^{(2)}(x)},l=N-1,\dots,0.

(12)

Then (
11
)-(
12
) is linearly convergent to
x_{*}
and has convergence constant
(\frac{R}{R+f^{(2)}(x_{*})})^{N+1}
.

Proof.

The iteration error evolves as follows

\displaystyle x_{k+1}-x_{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{k}-\bar{g}_{0}(x_{k})-x_{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(x_{k})-\phi(x_{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(x_{*})(x_{k}-x_{*})+\phi^{(2)}(x_{*})(x_{k}-x_{*})^{2}+o(|x_{k}-x_{*}|^{2}).

(13)

In the above,

\displaystyle\phi(x)=

\displaystyle x-\bar{g}_{0}(x),

(14)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(x_{*})=

\displaystyle(\frac{R}{R+f^{(2)}(x_{*})})^{N+1}.

(15)

\phi^{\prime}(x_{*})
in (
15
) can be derived by induction over
\bar{g}_{k}^{\prime}(x_{*})
. In detail,
according to (
10
),

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{k}^{\prime}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R+f^{(2)}(x)}(f^{(2)}(x)+R\bar{g}^{\prime}_{k+1}(x))-\frac{f^{(2)}(x)}{(R+f^{(2)}(x))^{2}}(f^{\prime}(x)+R\bar{g}_{k+1}(x)),

\displaystyle\bar{g}^{\prime}_{N}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{f^{(2)}(x)}{R+f^{(2)}(x)}-\frac{f^{\prime}(x)f^{(3)}(x)}{(R+f^{(2)}(x))^{2}}.

(17)

(

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{i}(x_{*})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R+f^{(2)}(x_{*})}(f^{(2)}(x_{*})+R\bar{g}^{\prime}_{k+1}(x_{*})),

\displaystyle\bar{g}^{\prime}_{N}(x_{*})=

\displaystyle\frac{f^{(2)}(x_{*})}{R+f^{(2)}(x_{*})}.

(20)

Based on (

IV
Conclusion

In the paper, a novel algorithm has been developed based on OCP. The algorithm converges more rapidly than gradient descent, meanwhile, unlike Newton’s method, it still works even if the singular Hessian matrix appears in iterate. We also pointed out that Newton’s method is recovered when the control weight matrix disappears in the developed algorithm. The convergence rate of the algorithm depends on two adjustable parameters. Because of their clear physical meanings inherited from OCP, it is very convenient to tune them to speed up the convergence rate.

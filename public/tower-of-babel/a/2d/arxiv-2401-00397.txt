Degenerate preconditioned backward-backward splitting for inclusion problem
Pankaj Gautam a and V. Vetrivel b

Abstract
In this work, we introduce the notion of warped Yosida regularization and study the asymptotic behaviour of the orbit of dynamical systems generated by warped Yosida regularization, which includes Douglas-Rachford dynamical system. We analyze an algorithm where the inclusion problem is first approximated by a regularized one and then the preconditioned regularization parameter is reduced to converge to a solution of original problem. We propose and investigate backward-backward splitting using degenerate preconditioning for monotone inclusion problems. The applications provide a tool for finding a minima of a preconditioned regularization of the sum of two convex functions.

Monotone operator; Yosida regularization; Preconditioning; Backward-backward splitting.

\displaystyle\text{find}~(x^{*},y^{*})\in\mathcal{H}^{2}~\text{such that}~(0,0)\in((I-R)^{-1}+(M^{-1}A\times M^{-1}B)^{-1}\circ(I/\lambda))(x^{*},y^{*}).

The solution of problems (

Warped Yosida regularization

In this section, we define warped Yosida regularization and provide its properties and some characterizations.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a reflexive Banach space with dual space
X^{*}
. Assume that
(\emptyset\neq)C\subseteq X
,
M:C\to{X^{*}}
and
T:X\to 2^{X^{*}}

\operatorname{ran}(M)\subset\operatorname{ran}(T+\gamma M)
and

is injective. For any
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
, warped Yosida regularization of
T
with kernel
M
is defined by
T_{\gamma}^{M}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\left(M-M\circ J_{\gamma T}^{M}\right)
, where
J_{\gamma T}^{M}
is the warped resolvent of
T

[
5
]
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
C
be a non-empty subset of
X
and
\phi:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper convex lower semicontinuous map. Let
\gamma>0
. Assume that

\operatorname{ran}(M)\subset\operatorname{ran}(M+\gamma\partial\phi)

is
(\partial\phi)_{\gamma}^{M}=\frac{1}{\gamma}(M-M\circ\prox_{\gamma\partial\phi}^{M})

Let

\displaystyle z=(\partial\phi)_{\gamma}^{M}\Leftrightarrow(\forall y\in X)~\langle y-x+\gamma M^{-1}z,\gamma z\rangle+\phi(x-\gamma M^{-1}z)\leq\phi(z)~\forall(x,z)\in X\times X.

4
Motivation to define warped Yosida regularization

Consider the monotone inclusion problem
\colon

\displaystyle\text{find}~u\in\mathcal{H}~\text{such that}~0\in Tu,

(16)

where
T:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is a maximal monotone operator defined on Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. The differential inclusion to solve problem (
16
) is

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{u}(t)\in-Tu(t)\\
u(t_{0})=u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}.\end{cases}

(17)

In general, the differential inclusion (
17
) is not well-posed. For example, if we consider an operator
T:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined by

\displaystyle T(x,y)=(-y,x),

then the orbit of (
17
) does not converge to zero of
T
. But we have
Z(T)=Z(T_{\gamma}^{M})
. In order to overcome this difficulty, we shall consider the following dynamical system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{u}(t)+T_{\gamma}^{M}u(t)=0\\
u(t_{0})=u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}.\end{cases}

(18)

In the next result, we study the properties of the orbit
u(t)
generated by the dynamical system (
18
).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
T:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
be a maximal monotone operator with
T^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
and
M
be an admissible preconditioner. Let
\gamma>0
and
u_{0}\in D(T)
. Then we have the following identities for the unique solution

of dynamical systems (
18
)
\colon

(i)

and
u(t)\in D(T)
,
\forall t>0
.

(ii)

The orbit

is bounded and
\dot{u}\in L^{2}([t_{0},\infty);\mathcal{H})
.

(iii)

, for some

Proof.

(i)

It follows from the fact that

is Lipschitz continuous.

(ii)

. Define an anchor

\displaystyle h(t)=\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)-u^{*}\|^{2}_{M}.

From (

\displaystyle\dot{h}(t)+\langle u(t)-u^{*},T_{\gamma}^{M}x(t)\rangle_{M}=0.

Since

and by (

\displaystyle\dot{h}(t)+\gamma\|T_{\gamma}^{M}u(t)\|^{2}\leq 0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow~

\displaystyle\dot{h}(t)+\gamma\|\dot{u}(t)\|^{2}\leq 0.

(19)

Hence

is a monotonically decreasing function. Since

is a locally absolutely continuous function, there exists

Interestingly, from

one can obtain different splitting based dynamical systems by different choices of operators

and

Let
u=(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}:=H^{2}
, where

is a real Hilbert space. Consider
T:H^{2}\to 2^{H^{2}}
and
M:H^{2}\to H^{2}
be the operators defined by

\displaystyle T=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha A&I\\
-I&(\alpha B)^{-1}\end{bmatrix},~~~M=\begin{bmatrix}I&-I\\
-I&I\end{bmatrix},

(21)

where
A,B:H\to 2^{H}
are maximal monotone operators. With this choice of operator

, problem (

) is converted into

\displaystyle\text{find}~u\in H~\text{such that}~0\in(A+B)u,

(22)

equivalently,

if and only if there exists
y\in H
such that
0\in\alpha Ax+y
and
0\in\alpha Bx
.
Now, for
T
and

defined by (

) we have

\displaystyle J_{T}^{M}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha A+I&0\\
-2I&(\alpha B)^{-1}+I\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\circ\begin{bmatrix}I&-I\\
-I&I\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}(\alpha A+I)^{-1}&-(\alpha A+I)^{-1}\\
2((\alpha B)^{-1}+I)^{-1}(\alpha A+I)^{-1}-((\alpha B)^{-1}+I)^{-1}&-2((\alpha B)^{-1}+I)^{-1}(\alpha A+I)^{-1}+((\alpha B)^{-1}+I)^{-1}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}J_{\alpha A}&-J_{\alpha A}\\
2J_{(\alpha B)^{-1}}J_{\alpha A}-J_{(\alpha B)^{-1}}&-2J_{(\alpha B)^{-1}}J_{\alpha A}+J_{(\alpha B)^{-1}}\end{bmatrix}.

So,

\displaystyle T^{M}=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}I&-I\\
-I&I\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}J_{\alpha A}-2J_{(\alpha B)^{-1}}J_{\alpha A}+J_{(\alpha B)^{-1}}&-J_{\alpha A}+2J_{(\alpha B)^{-1}}J_{\alpha A}-J_{(\alpha B)^{-1}}\\
-J_{\alpha A}+2J_{(\alpha B)^{-1}}J_{\alpha A}-J_{(\alpha B)^{-1}}&J_{\alpha A}-2J_{(\alpha B)^{-1}}J_{\alpha A}+J_{(\alpha B)^{-1}}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\frac{I-R_{\alpha A}R_{\alpha B}}{2}&\frac{R_{\alpha A}R_{\alpha B}-I}{2}\\
\frac{R_{\alpha A}R_{\alpha B}-I}{2}&\frac{I-R_{\alpha A}R_{\alpha B}}{2}\end{bmatrix},

where

and

are reflected resolvents of the operators

and

, respectively.
Hence from dynamical system (

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{x}(t)=-\frac{I-R_{\alpha A}R_{\alpha B}}{2}(x(t)-y(t))\\
\dot{y}(t)=-\frac{I-R_{\alpha A}R_{\alpha B}}{2}(y(t)-x(t))\\

By substituting

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{z}(t)+z(t)=R_{\alpha A}R_{\alpha B}z(t)\\

which is a Douglas-Rachford (without preconditioing) dynamical system investigated in

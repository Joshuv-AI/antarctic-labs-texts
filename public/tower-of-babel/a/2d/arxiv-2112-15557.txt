The conditional measures for the determinantal point process with the Bergman kernel
Alexander I. Bufetov

Abstract
. This note gives an explicit description of conditional measures for the determinantal point process with the Bergman kernel.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Formulation of the main result

The aim of this note is to give an explicit formula for the conditional measures of the zero set of the Gaussian Analytic Function under the condition that the configuration be fixed in the complement of a compact set. Recall that, by the Peres-Viràg theorem
[
18
]
, the zero set of the Gaussian Analytic Function is a determinantal point process with the Bergman kernel, cf. (
2
) below. The main tool is the explicit representation obtained in
[
5
]
of the Radon—Nikodym derivative of the reduced Palm measure of our determinantal point process with respect to the process itself; the Radon—Nikodym derivative is found as a generalized multiplicative functional corresponding to the divergent Blaschke product over the particles of our configuration.

We proceed to the precise formulations. Let
a_{n}(\omega)
,
n\geq 0
, be independent standard complex Gaussian random variables, with expectation
0
and variance
1
. The power series

(1)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(\omega)z^{n}

almost surely has radius of convergence
1
; the zero set of the series (
1
) is a configuration on the unit disc whose law
\mathbb{P}_{K}
, by the Peres—Viràg theorem
[
18
]
, is the determinantal measure on the space
\mathrm{Conf}(\mathbb{D})
of configurations on
\mathbb{D}
corresponding to the Bergman kernel

(2)

K(z,w)=\frac{1}{\pi(1-z\bar{w})^{2}},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D}

of orthogonal projection in the space
L_{2}(\mathbb{D})
of square-integrable functions with respect to the usual Lebesgue measure
onto the closed subspace of square-integrable holomorphic functions.

Consider a decomposition
\mathbb{D}=B\sqcup C
of the unit disc into two disjoint Borel sets with
B
open and having compact closure in
\mathbb{D}
. The natural restriction map
\pi_{C}\colon X\mapsto X\cap C
sends the measure
\mathbb{P}_{K}
forward to its projection
\overline{\mathbb{P}_{K}^{C}}
; the
\overline{\mathbb{P}_{K}^{C}}
-almost surely defined conditional measures of
\mathbb{P}_{K}
for a configuration
Y
on
\mathrm{Conf}(\mathbb{D})
satisfying
Y=Y\cap C
on the preimage
\pi_{C}^{-1}(Y)
is denoted
\mathbb{P}(\,\cdot\,|C;Y)
. Lemma 1.11 in
[
7
]
states that if
{\mathbb{P}}
is a determinantal point process induced by a positive Hermitian contraction, then so is its conditional measure; Lemma 1.11 also gives a limit procedure for finding the kernel governing the conditional measure. Our aim in this note is to give an explicit
formula, see (
6
) below, for the conditional measure
\mathbb{P}(\,\cdot\,|C;Y)
.
The starting point for the argument is Theorem 1.4 in
[
5
]
that gives an explicit expression for the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the Palm measure
{\mathbb{P}}_{K}^{q}
of
{\mathbb{P}}_{K}
with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}_{K}
; the Radon-Nikodym derivative, cf. (
10
) below, is expressed in terms of a regularized multiplicative functional
\overline{\Psi}_{q}
, cf. (
5
) below, that we now write in a slightly different way.
The next step is an expression of the conditional measure
\mathbb{P}(\,\cdot\,|C;Y)
in terms of the multiplicative functional
\overline{\Psi}_{q}
.

We consider the unit disc as the Poincaré model for the Lobachevsky plane, and for
p\in\mathbb{D}
,
R>0
we let
D(p,R)
stand for the Lobachevskian ball of radius
R
centred at
p
.

Proposition 1.1
.

For
\mathbb{P}_{K}
-almost every
X\in\mathrm{Conf}(\mathbb{D})
and any
q\in\mathbb{D}
, the limit

(3)

\tilde{\Psi}_{q}(X)=\\
=\lim_{R\to\infty}\prod_{x\in X\cap D(q,R)}\biggl|\frac{x-q}{1-\bar{x}q}\biggr|^{2}\cdot\exp\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{D(q,R)}\biggl(1-\biggl|\frac{z-q}{1-\bar{z}q}\biggr|^{2}\biggr)\frac{dz\wedge d\bar{z}}{(1-|z|^{2})}\biggr)

exists in
L_{1}(\mathrm{Conf}(\mathbb{D}),\mathbb{P}_{K})
as well as
\mathbb{P}_{K}
-almost surely along a subsequence.

We have

\int\tilde{\Psi}_{q}(X)\,d\mathbb{P}_{K}(X)=\frac{e^{\gamma-1}}{2},

where

(4)

\gamma=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{2}+\dots+\frac{1}{n}-\log n\Bigr)

is the Euler—Mascheroni constant.

Denote

(5)

\overline{\Psi}_{q}(X)=2e^{1-\gamma}\tilde{\Psi}_{q}(X).

We are now ready to proceed to the formulation of the main result of this note, an explicit description of the conditional measures of the determinantal point process with the Bergman kernel.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
\overline{\mathbb{P}_{K}^{C}}
-almost every configuration
Y
, the conditional measure
\mathbb{P}_{K}(\,\cdot\,|C;Y)
has the form

(6)

\eta_{Y,0}\biggl(1+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathop{det}\nolimits L_{Y}(q_{j},q_{k})_{j,k=1,\dots,m}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\frac{dq_{j}\wedge d\bar{q}_{j}}{(1-|q_{j}|^{2})^{2}}\biggr)\biggr),

where

(7)

L_{Y}(q_{1},q_{2})=\frac{\overline{\Psi}_{q_{1}}(Y)\overline{\Psi}_{q_{2}}(Y)}{1-q_{1}\bar{q}_{2}}

and

\eta_{Y,0}=\frac{1}{\mathop{det}\nolimits(1+L_{Y})}=\mathbb{P}(\#_{B}=0\mid C;Y)

is the conditional probability that there are no particles in
B
.

Equivalently, the conditional measure is an L-ensemble in the sense of Borodin
[
1
]
, and the L-kernel is given by the formula (
7
).

1.2.
Palm Measures of the Point Process
{\mathbb{P}}_{K}
.

1.2.1.
Correlation functions of point processes

Let
E
be a Polish space.
A
configuration
on
E
is a countable or finite collection of points in
E
, called
particles
, considered without regard to order and subject to the additional requirement that every compact set contain only finitely many particles of a configuration.
Let
{\mathrm{Conf}}(E)
be the space of configurations on
E
.
For a bounded Borel set
B\subset E
, let

\#_{B}\colon{\mathrm{Conf}}(E)\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}

be
the function that to a configuration
assigns the number of its particles
belonging to
B
. The random variables
\#_{B}
over all bounded Borel sets
B\subset E

determine the Borel sigma-algebra on
{\mathrm{Conf}}(E)
.
A Borel probability measure
{\mathbb{P}}
on
{\mathrm{Conf}}(E)
is called
a point process
with phase space
E
.
Recall that the point process
{\mathbb{P}}
is said to admit correlation measures
of order
l
if for any continuous compactly supported function
\varphi
on
E^{l}

the functional

\sum\limits_{x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\in X}\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})

is
{\mathbb{P}}
-integrable; the sum is taken over all ordered
l
-tuples of distinct particles in
X
. The
l
-th correlation measure
\rho_{l}
of the point process
{\mathbb{P}}
is then
defined by the formula

\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbb{P}}}\left(\sum\limits_{x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\in X}\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})\right)=\displaystyle\int\limits_{E^{l}}\varphi(q_{1},\dots,q_{l})d\rho_{l}(q_{1},\dots,q_{l}).

If all correlation measures of a point process are well-defined and for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
the
m
-th correlation measure is absolutely continuous with respect to
m
-th tensor power of the first one, then say that our point process admits correlation functions of all orders.

1.2.2.
Campbell and Palm Measures.

Following Kallenberg
[
13
]
, Daley–Vere-Jones
[
8
]
, we recall the definition of Campbell
measures of point processes; the notation follows
[
4
]
.
Let
{\mathbb{P}}
be a point process on
E
admitting the first correlation measure

\rho_{1}^{{\mathbb{P}}}
.
The
Campbell measure

{\EuScript C}_{{\mathbb{P}}}
of
{\mathbb{P}}

is a sigma-finite measure on
E\times{\mathrm{Conf}}(E)
such that for any Borel subsets

B\subset E
,
{\mathscr{Z}}\subset{\mathrm{Conf}}(E)
we have

{\EuScript C}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(B\times{\mathscr{Z}})=\displaystyle\int\limits_{{\mathscr{Z}}}\#_{B}(X)d{\mathbb{P}}(X).

The Palm measure
{\hat{\mathbb{P}}}^{q}
is the canonical conditional measure, in the sense of Rohlin
[
19
]
,
of the Campbell measure
{\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}

with respect to the measurable partition of the space
E\times{\mathrm{Conf}}(E)

into subsets
\{q\}\times{\mathrm{Conf}}(E)
,
q\in E
, cf.
[
4
]
.
By definition, the Palm measure
{\hat{\mathbb{P}}}^{q}
is supported on the subset of configurations containing a particle at position
q
.
Removing these particles, one defines the
reduced

Palm measure
{\mathbb{P}}^{q}
as the push-forward of the Palm measure

{\hat{\mathbb{P}}}^{q}
under the erasing map

X\to X\setminus\{q\}
.

Iterating the definition, one arrives at iterated Campbell, Palm and reduced Palm measures:
the
r
-th
Campbell measure

{\EuScript C}^{r}_{{\mathbb{P}}}
of
{\mathbb{P}}

is a sigma-finite measure on the product
E\times\dots\times\mathbb{E}\times{\mathrm{Conf}}(E)
of
r
copies of
E
and
{\mathrm{Conf}}(E)
such that for any disjoint Borel subsets

B_{1},\dots,B_{r}\subset E
,
{\mathscr{Z}}\subset{\mathrm{Conf}}(E)
we have

{\EuScript C}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(B_{1}\times\dots\times B_{r}\times{\mathscr{Z}})=\displaystyle\int\limits_{{\mathscr{Z}}}\#_{B_{1}}(X)\dots\#_{B_{r}}(X)d{\mathbb{P}}(X).

Given distinct
q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\in E
, the Palm measure
{\hat{\mathbb{P}}}^{q_{1},\dots,q_{r}}
is the canonical conditional measure, in the sense of Rohlin
[
19
]
,
of the Campbell measure
{\mathcal{C}}^{r}_{{\mathbb{P}}}

with respect to the measurable partition of the space
E\times\dots\times E\times{\mathrm{Conf}}(E)

into subsets
\{q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\}\times{\mathrm{Conf}}(E)
,
q\in E
, cf.
[
4
]
.
By definition, the Palm measure
{\hat{\mathbb{P}}}^{q_{1},\dots,q_{r}}
is supported on the subset of
configurations containing a particle at each position
q_{1},\dots,q_{r}
.
Removing these particles, one defines the
reduced

Palm measure
{\mathbb{P}}^{q_{1},\dots,q_{r}}
as the push-forward of the Palm measure

{\hat{\mathbb{P}}}^{q}
under the erasing map

X\to X\setminus\{q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\}
;
see Kallenberg
[
13
]
, whose formalism is also adopted in
[
4
]
, for a more detailed exposition.
As all conditional measures, reduced Palm measures
{\mathbb{P}}^{q}
are
a priori
only defined for
\rho_{1}
-almost every

q
. In our context of determinantal point processes, for any distinct
q_{1},\dots,q_{m}\in E
,
the Shirai-Takahashi Theorem allows us to fix a convenient explicit Borel realization
{\mathbb{P}}^{q_{1},\dots,q_{m}}
of reduced Palm measures.

1.2.3.
Determinantal Point Processes

As before, let
E
be a Polish space, and let
\mu
be a sigma-finite Borel measure on
E
. Recall that a Borel probability measure
\mathbb{P}
on

{\mathrm{Conf}}(E)
is called

determinantal
if there exists a locally trace class operator
K
acting in
L_{2}(E,\mu)
such that for any bounded measurable
function
g
, for which
g-1
is supported in a bounded set
B
,
we have

(8)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\prod\limits_{x\in X}g(x)=\mathop{det}\nolimits\biggl(1+(g-1)K\chi_{B}\biggr).

Here and elsewhere in similar formulas,
1
stands for the identity operator.
The Fredholm determinant in (
8
) is well-defined since

K
is locally of trace class.
The equation (
8
) determines the measure
{\mathbb{P}}
uniquely. We use the notation
{\mathbb{P}}_{K}
for the determinantal measure
induced by the operator
K
.
By a theorem due to Macchì and Soshnikov
[
15
]
,
[
24
]
and Shirai-Takahashi
[
20
]
, any
Hermitian positive contraction that belongs
to the local trace class defines a determinantal point process.

1.2.4.
Generalized multiplicative functionals

Let
g
be a Borel function on
E
and let
\Psi
be a Borel function defined on a Borel subset
\mathscr{Z}\subset\mathrm{Conf}(E)
and satisfying the following: if
X,Y\in\mathscr{Z}
and there exist distinct particles

p_{1},\dots,p_{r},q_{1},\dots,q_{s}\in E
such that

X\setminus\{p_{1},\dots,p_{r}\}=Y\setminus\{q_{1},\dots,q_{s}\}
, then

(9)

\Psi(X)=\frac{g(p_{1})\cdots g(p_{r})}{g(q_{1})\cdots g(q_{s})}\Psi(Y).

In this case we say that
\Psi
is a generalized multiplicative functional corresponding to the function
g
.
The regularized multiplicative functional (
3
) is a particular case of a generalized multiplicative functional.
If the point process
\mathbb{P}
has trivial tail
\sigma
-algebra, then a generalized multiplicative functional corresponding to a function
g
, provided it exists, is
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely unique up to multiplication by a constant.

1.2.5.
The Characterization of Palm Measures for
\mathbb{P}_{K}

The starting point for the argument is Theorem 1.4 in
[
5
]
that, in view of Proposition
1.1
, can be formulated as follows: for any
q\in\mathbb{D}
,
the reduced Palm measure
\mathbb{P}_{K}^{q}
of our determinantal point process
{\mathbb{P}}_{K}
with the Bergman kernel is given by the formula

(10)

\mathbb{P}_{K}^{q}=\overline{\Psi}_{q}\mathbb{P}_{K}.

In other words, the Radon-Nikodym derivative is given by a regularized multiplicative functional. The argument is completed by a general proposition describing the conditional measures for a point process whose Palm measures are expressed as a product of the original measure and a multiplicative functional.

1.3.
Palm Measures, Multiplicative Functionals and Conditional Measures.

Let
{\mathbb{P}}
be a point process with phase space
E
admitting correlation functions of all orders. We fix a Borel realization
{\mathbb{P}}^{q}
of its reduced Palm measures and assume that there exists a symmetric positive Borel function

Proposition

). Let
g\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a nonnegative bounded Borel radial function, in other words, a function depending only on the absolute value of its argument. The eigenfunctions of the operator
\sqrt{g}K\sqrt{g}
are precisely the functions
\sqrt{g}z^{k}
,
k\geq 0
, and the corresponding eigenvalue is

\frac{k+1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}g(z)|z|^{2k}\,dz=(k+1)\int_{0}^{1}\tilde{g}(\rho)\rho^{k}\,d\rho,

where
\tilde{g}(\rho)=g(\sqrt{\rho}e^{i\theta})
for any
\theta
.

It directly follows that the operator

(18)

K_{1}(z,w)=\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}K(z,w)\sqrt{1-|w|^{2}}

is Hilbert—Schmidt, as its eigenvalues are

(k+1)\int_{0}^{1}(1-\rho)\rho^{k}\,d\rho=\frac{1}{k+2},\quad k=0,1,\dots.

By definition (
3
), we have

(19)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{K}}\tilde{\Psi}=\mathop{det}\nolimits\nolimits_{2}(1+K_{1}).

Note here that

\frac{1}{\pi(1-|z|^{2})}=(1-|z|^{2})K(z,z).

For
r\in(0,1)
, set

\tilde{\Psi}_{r}(X)=\prod_{x\in X,|x|<r}|x|^{2}\cdot\exp\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\int_{\{z:|z|<r\}}\frac{dz\wedge d\bar{z}}{(1-|z|^{2})}\biggr).

By definition, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{K}}\tilde{\Psi}_{r}=\mathop{det}\nolimits\nolimits_{2}(1+\chi_{\{z:|z|<r\}}K_{1}\chi_{\{z:|z|<r\}}).

Since

\chi_{\{z:|z|<r\}}K_{1}\chi_{\{z:|z|<r\}}\to K_{1}

in the Hilbert—Schmidt norm as
r\to 1
,
writing the Cauchy-Bunyakovsky-Schwartz inequality

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{K}}\left|\tilde{\Psi}-\tilde{\Psi}_{r}\right|\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{K}}\left|\tilde{\Psi}_{r}\right|^{2}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{K}}\left|\tilde{\Psi}\tilde{\Psi}_{r}^{-1}-1\right|^{2}}

one directly checks the relation

\lim\limits_{r\to 1}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}_{K}}\left|\tilde{\Psi}-\tilde{\Psi}_{r}\right|=0,

which is to say that the limit

\tilde{\Psi}=\lim_{r\to 1}\tilde{\Psi}_{r}

exists in
L_{1}(\mathrm{Conf}(\mathbb{D}),\mathbb{P}_{K})
as well as almost surely along a subsequence.

We now compute the right-hand side of (
19
). In order to do so, we let
K^{(n)}
be the orthogonal projection onto
\{1,z,\dots,z^{n}\}
in
L_{2}(\mathbb{D})
and write

K_{1}^{(n)}=\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}K^{(n)}\sqrt{1-|w|^{2}}.

We have
K_{1}^{(n)}\to K_{1}
in the Hilbert—Schmidt norm as
n\to\infty
, whence

\mathop{det}\nolimits\nolimits_{2}(1+K_{1})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathop{det}\nolimits(1+K_{1}^{(n)})\times\exp\biggl(-{\mathrm{tr}}K_{1}^{(n)}\biggr).

By definition, we have

\mathop{det}\nolimits(1+K_{1}^{(n)})=\prod_{k=0}^{n}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{k+2}\Bigr)=\frac{n+3}{2}.

and

{\mathrm{tr}}(K_{1}^{(n)})=\frac{1}{2}+\dots+\frac{1}{n+1}.

Summing up, cf. (
4
), we obtain

\mathop{det}\nolimits\nolimits_{2}(1+K_{1})=\frac{e^{\gamma-1}}{2}.

We therefore obtain an alternative representation of the Palm measure
\mathbb{P}_{K}^{0}
with respect to the original measure:

\frac{d\mathbb{P}_{K}^{0}}{d\mathbb{P}}(X)=\frac{e^{\gamma-1}}{2}\lim\limits_{r\to 1}\prod\limits_{x\in X:|x|<r}|x|^{2}\exp\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\{z:|z|<r\}}\frac{dz\wedge d\bar{z}}{1-|z|^{2}}\biggr).

Setting
D(z,R)
to be the Lobachevskian ball centred at
z
and of Lobachevskian radius
R
, for any
q\in\mathbb{D}
we rewrite

\frac{d\mathbb{P}_{K}^{q}}{d\mathbb{P}}(X)=\frac{e^{\gamma-1}}{2}\lim\limits_{R\to\infty}\prod\limits_{x\in D(q,R)\cap X}\biggl|\frac{x-q}{1-\bar{q}x}\biggr|^{2}\times\\
\times\exp\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\int\limits_{D(q,R)}\biggl(1-\biggl|\frac{z-q}{1-\bar{q}z}\biggr|^{2}\biggr)\biggr)K(z,z)\,dz\wedge d\bar{z}.

Proposition
1.1
is proved. Theorem
1.2
is proved completely. ∎

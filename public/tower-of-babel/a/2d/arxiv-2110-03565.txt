Solutions to nonlocal evolution equations governed by non-autonomous forms and demicontinuous nonlinearities
Vittorio Colao, Luigi Muglia

Abstract
We deal with the existence of solutions having L 2 − L^{2}- regularity for a class of non-autonomous evolution equations. Associated with the equation, a general non-local condition is studied. The technique we used combines a finite dimensional reduction together with the Leray-Schauder continuation principle. This approach permits to consider a wide class of nonlinear terms by allowing demicontinuity assumptions on the nonlinearity.

1
Introduction and preliminaries.

In this paper we investigate the existence and regularity properties of the solutions to the evolution problem

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u^{\prime}(t)+A(t)u(t)=f(t,u(t)),\qquad a.e.\ \ t\in[0,T]\ \\
u(0)=g(u)\end{cases}

where
A(t)
is a non-autonomous operator having
L^{2}-
maximal regularity,
f
is nonlinear and demicontinuous in the second variable and
g
is a function representing a non-local condition.

Maximal regularity results have been the subject of several recent studies. On one side, this is due to the fact that the maximal regularity property of the solutions of differential equations gains a central role in the theory of parabolic problems, by permitting weaker requests on the regularity of the coefficients of differential operators. On the other hand, from an abstract point of view, maximal regularity characterizes a wide class of evolution equations and facilitates the application of linearization techniques.

This property plays an essential part indeed, together with a finite dimensional reduction technique and the Leray-Schauder continuation principle, permit us to prove the existence and regularity properties of the solutions to a wide class of non-autonomous semilinear evolution equations, including demicontinuous nonlinearities.

Turning our attention to the trace space, we should note that while in the autonomous case, the theory is well-developed and it is well known in the literature which spaces must be chosen in order to reach the desired regularity of the solutions, the non-autonomous setting had been the subject of several recent research papers.

The evolution problem is then completed by an initial condition of non-local type; it is remarkable that non-local conditions can be more useful than standard initial conditions
u(0)=u_{0}
to model some physical phenomena (see
KoPr
;
Nto
and references therein).

To be more precise, let
(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V})
and
(H,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H})
be two separable Hilbert spaces such that
V
is continuously and densely embedded into
H
, i.e.
V
is a dense subspace of
H
such that

\|v\|_{H}\leq c\|v\|_{V}

for some constant
c>0
and any
v\in V.

In many practical examples, an operator
\mathcal{A}(t)
is associated to a bounded sesquilinear form
a(t,\cdot,\cdot)
with domain
V
.

More precisely, assume that
a:[0,T]\times V\times V\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies

(H1)

a(\cdot,u,v)
is strongly measurable for any
u,v\in V,

(H2)

there exists
M>0
such that
|a(t,u,v)|\leq M\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V}
for any
t\in[0,T]
and
u,v\in V,

(H3)

there exists
\alpha>0
such that
\mathfrak{Re}(a(t,u,u))\geq\alpha\|u\|^{2}_{V}
for any
t\in[0,T]
and
u\in V;

then, for any
t\in[0,T],

\mathcal{A}(t)\in\mathcal{L}(V,V^{\prime})
is well-defined by
\langle\mathcal{A}(t)u,v\rangle_{V^{\prime}\times V}:=a(t,u,v)
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}(t))=V,
where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V^{\prime}\times V}
is the standard duality pairing.

Turning our attention to evolution problems governed by forms,
we mention the following result proved by J.L. Lions in 1961 (see
Li
):

Theorem 1.1

For any fixed
x\in H
and
f\in L^{2}([0,T],V^{\prime}),
the problem

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u^{\prime}(t)+\mathcal{A}(t)u(t)=f(t),\qquad a.e.\ \ t\in[0,T]\ \\
u(0)=x,\end{cases}

has a unique solution
u\in L^{2}([0,T],V)\cap H^{1}([0,T],V^{\prime}).

We point out that the outstanding result by Lions only requires the measurability of
a(\cdot,u,v)
. On the other side, the above result is not fully satisfactory when applied to boundary value problems. Indeed, it is the part of
\mathcal{A}(t)
which lies in
H
that realizes the boundary conditions.

Therefore, let
A(t),\ t\in[0,T]
be defined by
A(t)u:=\mathcal{A}(t)u,
on the non-empty set
\mathcal{D}(A(t)):=\{u\in V:\mathcal{A}(t)u\in H\}
and focus on

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u^{\prime}(t)+{A}(t)u(t)=f(t),\qquad a.e.\ \ t\in[0,T]\ \\
u(0)=x.\end{cases}

Definition 1.2

For fixed
x
in a suitable trace space,
(
1.3
)
is said to have
L^{2}-
maximal regularity in
H
if for any
f\in L^{2}([0,T],H)
there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{1}([0,T],H)\cap L^{2}([0,T],V)
and such that
u(t)\in\mathcal{D}(A(t)).

Several authors dealt with the problem of establishing which conditions on the trace space and which regularity assumptions on
a(t,u,v)
are sufficient to achieve maximal regularity in
H.
Among others, we cite the papers
ACFP
;
A
and
Pru
.

In 2015, Haak and Ouhabaz

gave a complete treatment of the evolution problem with non-zero initial values. We will mention their general result which we restate to our scope.

To this end, note that condition
(H3)
readily implies that
A(t)
is accretive for any
t\in[0,T],
i.e.

\mathfrak{Re}\langle A(t)u,u\rangle_{H}\geq 0\text{ for any }u\in\mathcal{D}(A(t)).

Secondly, assume that
a(t,u,v)
also satisfies

(H4)

|a(t,u,v)-a(s,u,v)|\leq\omega(|t-s|)\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V},
for some nondecreasing

which satisfies

\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\omega(t)}{t^{3/2}}dt<\infty\text{ and the Dini condition }\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{\omega(t)}{t}\right)^{2}dt<\infty.

Theorem 1.3

(
HaOu
, Corollary 3)

Suppose that (H1)-(H4) and
\mathcal{D}(A(0)^{1/2})=V
are satisfied, then for any

and any

(
1.3

Moreover there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that

(1.4)

\|u\|_{H^{1}([0,T],H)}+\|A(\cdot)u(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}([0,T],H)}\leq C_{0}\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T],H)}+\|x\|_{V}\right).

To our scope we need to introduce a stronger condition on the domain, that is we assume that

(S)

for any

the square root property
\mathcal{D}(A(t)^{1/2})=V
holds.

Three remarkable facts are detailed below. The first of them shows an immediate consequence of the above estimate, while the others two illustrate settings in which conditions (H3) and (S) can be lowered.

Remark 1.4

We stress that condition (H3) guarantees that the solution

lies in
L^{2}([0,T],V).
More precisely, there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1}([0,T],H)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T],V)}

(1.5)

\displaystyle+\|A(\cdot)u(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}([0,T],H)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T],H)}+\|x\|_{V}\right).

Indeed, observe that

\displaystyle\alpha\|u(t)\|^{2}_{V}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{Re}\left(a(t,u(t),u(t))\right)=\mathfrak{Re}\langle A(t)u(t),u(t)\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|u(t)\|^{2}_{H}+\|A(t)u(t)\|^{2}_{H}\right),

which implies, by means of (

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T],V)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\alpha}\left(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T],H)}+\|A(t)u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T],H)}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{C_{0}^{2}}{2\alpha}\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T],H)}+\|x\|_{V}\right)^{2}.

Hence (

Remark 1.5

Let
\mu>0
and set

Then, if

satisfies problem (

), then

is the unique solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v^{\prime}(t)+{A}(t)v(t)+\mu v(t)=g(t),\qquad a.e.\ \ t\in[0,T]\ \\
v(0)=u(0),\end{cases}

where

This shows that condition (H3) can be lowered by assuming that

(H3
∗
)

there exists

such that

holds for any

and

Remark 1.6

Assume that

(H4
∗
)

|a(t,u,v)-a(s,u,v)|\leq\omega(|t-s|)\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V_{\gamma}}

holds for some bounded

and where

is the complex interpolation space

for a fixed

Then, it follows from
(

that property (S) can be relaxed by assuming

(S

\mathcal{D}(A(t_{0})^{1/2})=V
for some

Based on Theorem
1.3
and by closely following the lines provided by

, in Section

In several recent papers (see e.g.
BeLoMaTa
;
LuLiOb
;
ZhLiXi
and references therein), a final dimensional reduction strategy had been proved to be a key point when associated to the study of autonomous evolution problems. In particular a new method has been introduced in
BLT
which combines Yosida approximation with the approximation solvability method. The aforementioned method is a generalization of the well known strong approximation technique (
Bro
;
Pe
) and has been used in
Loi
for studying periodic oscillations, while in
BLMO
it has been applied to nonlocal differential equation problems in Banach spaces.
In this article, we show that the technique also fits well in the non-autonomous case and, as a novelty element, we combine the approximation solvability method with the approximation properties of evolution systems governed by forms. Section
3
is devoted to the scope.

Indeed, we prove that Theorem
1.3
also applies to evolution equations governed by operator of type
\mathbb{P}_{m}A(t),
where
\mathbb{P}_{m}
is the projection onto a finite dimensional space (Lemma
3.3
). An approximation result is also introduced to show the uniform convergence of the evolution systems generated by
\mathbb{P}_{m}A(t)
(Lemma
3.4
).

The above-mentioned approach permits us to lower the hypothesis on the nonlinear term, as explained below.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces and let
f:[0,T]\times X\to Y
be a map. An important concept in Functional Analysis is the one of superposition operator
N_{f}:L^{p}([0,T],X)\to L^{q}([0,T],Y)
, defined by
N_{f}(u)(t):=f(t,u(t)).

We recall the following classical theorem:

Theorem 1.7
(
LuPa
)

If
X
and
Y
are separable and
f
is measurable in
[0,T]\times X,
then
N_{f}:L^{p}([0,T],X)\to L^{q}([0,T],Y)
is well-defined if and only if
there exists a constant
a>0
and a function
b\in L^{q}([0,T],\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that

(1.6)

\|f(t,x)\|_{Y}\leq a\|x\|_{X}^{p/q}+b(t).

Moreover
N_{f}
maps bounded subsets into bounded subsets.

In classical examples on
L^{p}
spaces, nonlinear superposition operators are often constructed on Caratheodory maps
f:[0,T]\times X\to Y
, i.e. by assuming that
f
is continuous w.r.t. the second variable. The continuity of the superposition operator
N_{f}
is then guaranteed as a consequence of this fact.

We stress that the continuity of superposition operator is almost a necessary property in the applications to differential systems. Nevertheless, the use of weak topologies can often be more convenient in several practical problems. Unfortunately,
(
MoTe
, Example 2.3)
shows that weak continuity of superposition operator
N_{f}
acting on
L^{p}
spaces does not derive from the same assumption on the map
f(t,\cdot).

Therefore a stronger form of continuity on
f(t,\cdot)
appears to be necessary in order for the superposition operator to continuously act on spaces endowed with the weak topology (see
(
MoTe
, Theorem 2.6)
).

In Section
4
, we show that the operator
N_{f}
is demicontinuous or, in other words, that for any sequence
x_{n}\to x
it holds
N_{f}(x_{n})\rightharpoonup N_{f}(x),
whenever
f(t,\cdot)
satisfies the same property (Lemma
4.2
).

Demicontinuity fits well with the finite dimensional reduction approach and permit us, under the further assumption that the map
f
satisfies a transversality condition, to apply the Leray-Schauder continuation principle (see also
BeCi
):

Theorem 1.8
(
LeSc
)

Let
D
be a closed and convex set in a Banach space
X.
Let
S:[0,1]\times D\to X
be a completely continuous operator (i.e.
S
maps bounded subsets into compact subsets and it is continuous) and assume that

(i)

S(0,x)\in\operatorname{int}(D)
for any
x\in D;

(ii)

the set

\{x\in D:x=S(\lambda,x)\text{ for some }\lambda\in[0,1]\}

is bounded and does not meet the boundary
\partial D
of
D.

Then there exists
x\in D
such that
x=S(1,x).

Finally, a solution to problem (
1.1
) is proved to exist as a limit. For this last step, the following result is crucial.

Theorem 1.9
(Aubin-Lions Lemma, see e.g.
BoFa
)

Let
X_{0},X
and
X_{1}
be three Banach spaces. Suppose that
X_{0}
is compactly embedded in
X
and
X
is continuously embedded in
X_{1}
. Suppose also that
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
are reflexive. Then for
0<T<+\infty
and
1<r,s<\infty,
we have that
L^{r}([0,T],X_{0})\cap W^{1,s}([0,T],X_{1})
is compactly embedded in
L^{r}([0,T],X).

The results detailed above are contained in Section
5
.

The paper is then concluded by Section
6
, where application examples are discussed. In particular, weexploit the fact the demicontinuity assumption is naturally satisfied by any maximally monotone operator with sublinear growth.

We stress that the novelty elements that we introduce in this manuscript lie in the approximation results (Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
), in Lemma
4.2
where the demicontinuity of the superposition operator is shown and, leastly, in Theorem
5.1
we show the existence of solutions in
H^{1}([0,T],H)\cap L^{2}([0,T],V)
to the problem

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u^{\prime}(t)+A(t)u(t)=f(t,u(t))\qquad t\in[0,T]\ \\
u(0)=g(u).\end{cases}

It is worth mentioning that another novelty element of our approach lies in the regularity properties of the solutions to the nonlocal semilinear problem. Indeed, it is importanto to note that the majority of the papers on the topic mainly deal with the existence of mild solutions, that is continuous solutions of an associated fixed-point problem. Here we prove that the aforementioned approximation solvability method can be a key-strategy also for proving the existence of strong solutions.

Evolution families for operators governed by forms.

In this section we study the properties of the evolution family generated by a non-autonomous form which satisfies properties (H1)-(H4) and (S).

Here and through the rest of the paper,
(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V})
and
(H,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H})
will be two separable Hilbert spaces, with

densely embedded into

and

will denote the set
\{(t,s):0\leq s<t\leq T\}.

As usual,

3
Finite dimensional reduction.

Let
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a Schauder basis for
V
and hence for
H
since
V
densely embeds onto
H
. We assume
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to be orthogonal w.r.t. the inner product of
H,

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}.
For a fixed
m\in\mathbb{N},
let
\mathbb{P}_{m}:H\to\operatorname{span_{\mathbb{C}}}\{\varphi_{j}:1\leq j\leq m\}
be the projection
\mathbb{P}_{m}(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}v_{j}\varphi_{j}):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}\varphi_{j}.
To avoid confusion, by
H_{m}
we will denote the space
\mathbb{P}_{m}H
endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H},
while
V_{m}
indicates that the equivalent norm
\|\cdot\|_{V}
is considered on the same finite dimensional vector space.

Note that
\mathbb{P}_{m}
is self-adjoint with respect the
H
-inner product (that is w.r.t.
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
), indeed whenever
u,v\in H,
we have

\langle u,\mathbb{P}_{m}v\rangle_{H}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_{i}\varphi_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}\varphi_{j}\rangle_{H}=\langle\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_{i}\varphi_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}\varphi_{j}\rangle_{H}=\langle\mathbb{P}_{m}u,v\rangle_{H}.

Of course, since
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Schauder basis for both
V
and
H,
it holds that
\|v-\mathbb{P}_{m}v\|_{V}\to 0
and
\|x-\mathbb{P}_{m}x\|_{H}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
for any fixed
v\in V
and
x\in H.

Let
a:[0,T]\times V\times V\to\mathbb{C}
be a sesquilinear form satisfying (H1)-(H4). For fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
we denote with
a_{m}:[0,T]\times V\times V\to\mathbb{C}
the sesquilinear form given by

a_{m}(t,u.v):=a(t,\mathbb{P}_{m}u,\mathbb{P}_{m}v)+\alpha\langle(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})u,(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})v\rangle_{V}.

Remark 3.1

Note that
a_{m}
also satisfies properties (H1)-(H4). Indeed, (H1) and (H4) are trivially satisfied while (H2) is easily derived from

\displaystyle|a_{m}(t,u,v)|\leq M\|\mathbb{P}_{m}u\|_{V}\|\mathbb{P}_{m}v\|_{V}+\alpha\|(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})u\|_{V}\|(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})v\|_{V}

\displaystyle\leq k_{m}(M+\alpha)\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V},

since both
\|\mathbb{P}_{m}\|_{\mathcal{L}(V)}
and
\|I-\mathbb{P}_{m}\|_{\mathcal{L}(V)}
are bounded by some
k_{m}>0
for any fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}.

To prove (H3) observe that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Re}(a_{m}(t,u,u))=\mathfrak{Re}(a(t,\mathbb{P}_{m}u,\mathbb{P}_{m}u))+\alpha\|I-\mathbb{P}_{m}u\|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\|\mathbb{P}_{m}u\|_{V}^{2}+\alpha\|I-\mathbb{P}_{m}u\|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\alpha(\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbb{P}_{m}u\|_{V}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|I-\mathbb{P}_{m}u\|_{V}^{2})

\displaystyle\geq 2\alpha\left\|\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}_{m}u+\frac{1}{2}u-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}_{m}u\right\|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u\|_{V}^{2}

since
\|\cdot\|_{V}^{2}
is convex.

Let
A_{m}(t)
be the operator defined by

a_{m}(t,u,v):=\langle A_{m}(t)u,v\rangle_{H}\text{ for all }v\in V

and with domain
\mathcal{D}(A_{m}(t)):=\{u\in V:A_{m}(t)u\in H\}.

Remark 3.2

Let
B:V\to H
be the operator associated to the sesquilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V}.
Then for any fixed
t\in[0,T]
and for any
u,v\in V
one has

\displaystyle\langle A_{m}(t)u,v\rangle_{H}=a_{m}(t,u,v)=a(t,\mathbb{P}_{m}u,\mathbb{P}_{m}v)+\alpha\langle(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})u,(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})v\rangle_{V}

\displaystyle=\langle A(t)\mathbb{P}_{m}u,\mathbb{P}_{m}v\rangle_{H}+\alpha\langle B(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})u,(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})v\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\langle(\mathbb{P}_{m}A(t)\mathbb{P}_{m}+\alpha\langle(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})B(I-\mathbb{P}_{m}))u,v\rangle_{H}.

Therefore, the identity

(3.1)

A_{m}(t)=\mathbb{P}_{m}A(t)\mathbb{P}_{m}+(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})B(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})

follows by density. It is then straightforward to see that for any
u\in V_{m},

A_{m}(t)u=\mathbb{P}_{m}A(t)u

holds.

Lemma 3.3

For any fixed
m\in\mathbb{N},

A_{m}(t)
defined above generates a contractive evolution system
\{E_{m}(t,s)\}\subset\mathcal{L}(V)
such that for any
x\in V,

u(t):=E_{m}(t,s)x
is the unique solution in
H^{1}(s,T,H)\cap L^{2}(s,T,V)
of the homogeneous problem

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u^{\prime}(t)+{A_{m}}(t)u(t)=0,\qquad a.e.\ \ t\in[s,T]\ \\
u(s)=x\in V,\end{cases}

which satisfies inequality (
2.2
). Moreover, if
x\in V_{m}
then
u(t)\in V_{m}
for any
t\in[0,T].

Proof

The existence of the evolution system
\{E_{m}(t,s)\}
will directly follow from Lemma
2.1
once we prove that
A_{m}(t)
satisfies property (S).

Fix
t\in[0,T].
It follows from
(
arendtvectorvalued
, Corollary 7.1.4)
that the operator
A_{m}(t)=\mathbb{P}_{m}A(t)\mathbb{P}_{m}+(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})B(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})
generates a cosine function on
H,
since
\mathbb{P}_{m}A(t)\mathbb{P}_{m}
is bounded and
(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})B(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})
is symmetric.

By
(
haase2003
, Corollary 5.18)
, the numerical range
W(A_{m}(t)):=\{\langle A_{m}(t)u,u\rangle_{H}|u\in V,\|u\|_{H}=1\}
is then contained in a parabola.
Lastly, by
(
mcintosh82
, Theorems A and C)
it follows that the square root condition
\mathcal{D}(A_{m}(t)^{1/2})=V
holds (see also
arendt2004
). This proves the first part.

Let
x\in V_{m}
and let
u(t)
be the solution of (
3.2
). Note that the function
t\to u(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)
has a.e. derivative
u^{\prime}(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u^{\prime}(t)
since
\mathbb{P}_{m}
is linear; moreover it holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)\|_{H}^{2}=\mathfrak{Re}\langle u^{\prime}(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u^{\prime}(t),u(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{Re}\langle A_{m}(t)u(t),u(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)\rangle_{H}+\mathfrak{Re}\langle\mathbb{P}_{m}A_{m}(t)u(t),u(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{Re}\langle A_{m}(t)u(t),u(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)\rangle_{H}+\mathfrak{Re}\langle A_{m}(t)u(t),\mathbb{P}_{m}(u(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t))\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{Re}\langle A_{m}(t)u(t),u(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=-a_{m}(t,u(t),u(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t))

(3.3)

\displaystyle=-a(t,\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t),\mathbb{P}_{m}(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})u(t))-\alpha\|(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})u(t)\|_{V}^{2},

where we have used the fact that
\mathbb{P}_{m}
is self-adjoint w.r.t
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
and
I-\mathbb{P}_{m}
is idempotent. Note that
\mathbb{P}_{m}(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})=0,
which implies that
a(t,\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t),\mathbb{P}_{m}(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})u(t))=0
and (
Proof
) brings to

\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)\|_{H}^{2}\leq 0-\alpha\|(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})u(t)\|_{V}^{2}\leq 0.

The direct consequence of this last is that for a.e.
t\in[0,T],

\|u(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)\|_{H}\leq\|x-\mathbb{P}_{m}x\|_{H}=0
since

thus
u(t)=\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)
and

follows.

Lemma 3.4

Let
\{E(t,s)\}_{(t,s)\in\Delta}
and
\{E_{m}(t,s)\}_{(t,s)\in\Delta}
be
the evolution systems generated by
A(t)
and
A_{m}(t)
respectively.
Then for any fixed
x\in H,

\{E_{m}(t,s)\mathbb{P}_{m}x\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in
H
to
E(t,s)x
uniformly on
t>s
in
[0,T].

Proof

We start by fixing
x\in V
and, for fixed
(t,s)\in\Delta,
set
u(t):=E(t,s)x,

u_{m}(t):=E_{m}(t,s)\mathbb{P}_{m}x
and
z_{m}(t):=u_{m}(t)-u(t).
Note that by definition,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|z_{m}(t)\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{Re}\langle\ \mathbb{P}_{m}A(t)u_{m}(t),u_{m}(t)-u(t)\rangle_{H}+\mathfrak{Re}\langle A(t)u(t),u_{m}(t)-u(t)\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{Re}\langle A(t)u_{m}(t),\mathbb{P}_{m}u_{m}(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)\rangle_{H}+\mathfrak{Re}\langle A(t)u(t),u_{m}(t)-u(t)\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{Re}\langle A(t)u_{m}(t),u_{m}(t)-u(t)\rangle_{H}+\mathfrak{Re}\langle A(t)u_{m}(t),\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)-u(t)\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\mathfrak{Re}\langle A(t)u(t),u_{m}(t)-u(t)\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{Re}\langle A(t)u_{m}(t),\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)-u(t)\rangle_{H}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=a(t,u_{m}(t),\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)-u(t))\leq M\|u_{m}(t)\|_{V}\|u(t)-\mathbb{P}_{m}u(t)\|_{V},

since
A
is accretive.
By integrating, from the last and by Hölder inequality, one gets

\displaystyle\|z_{m}(t)\|_{H}^{2}-\|z_{m}(s)\|_{H}^{2}\leq 2M\|u_{m}\|_{L^{2}([s,T],V)}\|u-\mathbb{P}_{m}u\|_{L^{2}([s,T],V)}

\displaystyle\leq 2M\|\mathbb{P}_{m}x\|_{H}\|u-\mathbb{P}_{m}u\|_{L^{2}([s,T],V)}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and let
u_{\varepsilon}
a continuous
\varepsilon/4-
approximation of
u
in
{L^{2}([s,T],V)}.
Let
\tau_{\varepsilon}
be such that

T\|(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})u_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\varepsilon})\|_{V}^{2}=\int_{s}^{T}\|(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{V}^{2}dt=\|(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([s,T],V)}

and let
m
be big enough so that
T\|(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})u_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\varepsilon})\|_{V}^{2}<\varepsilon^{2}/4.
We immediately derive

\displaystyle\|u-\mathbb{P}_{m}u\|_{L^{2}([s,T],V)}\leq 2\|u-u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}([s,T],V)}+\|(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}([s,T],V)}\leq\varepsilon.

Note that
\|z_{m}(s)\|_{H}=\|(I-\mathbb{P}_{m})x\|_{H}\to 0
as

since the embedding of
V
into
H
is continuous, hence by (
Proof
) one gets

\|z_{m}(t)\|_{H}<2\varepsilon

for

Remark 3.5

The thesis of previous lemma remains true if

\displaystyle(a_{m})^{r}(t,u,v)=\overline{a_{m}(T-t,v,u)}

4
Nonlinear superposition operators.

We will make use of the next lemma, which takes inspiration from
(
BaTe
, Lemma 7.11)
(see also
MoTe
). Statement and proof are given in the particular setting of the
L^{2}
space of
H-
valued functions, though further extensions to a more general setting might be possible. We recall the following

Definition 4.1

Given two Banach spaces
X
and
Y,
a function
F:X\to Y
is said to be demicontinuos if for any sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X
strongly converging to
x\in X,
one has that
\{F(x_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
weakly converges to
F(x)
or, in other words, that

w-\lim_{n\to\infty}F(x_{n})=F(x)\text{ whenever }x_{n}\to x.

Lemma 4.2

Suppose that
f:[0,T]\times H\to H
satisfies

(F1)

f(\cdot,x)
is measurable for any
x\in H;

(F2)

f(t,\cdot)
is demicontinuous in
H
for any fixed
t\in[0,T].

(F3)

there exist
a>0
and
b\in L^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that

(4.1)

\|f(t,x)\|_{H}\leq a\|x\|_{H}+b(t).

Then the superposition operator
N_{f}:L^{2}([0,T],H)\to L^{2}([0,T],H)
given by
N_{f}(u)(t):=f(t,u(t))
is well-defined and maps bounded sets into bounded sets; moreover it is demicontinuous.

Proof

The fact that
N_{f}
is well-defined and maps bounded sets into bounded sets is given by Theorem
1.7
. It remains to prove that the superposition operator is demicontinuous.

Hence, let
\{u_{m}\}\subset L^{2}([0,T],H)
be a sequence which converges strongly to
u,
then for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
it holds
u_{m}(t)\to u(t)
in
H
and
f(t,u_{m}(t))\rightharpoonup f(t,u(t))
follows by demicontinuity assumption on
f.

On the hand, we observe that
\{N_{f}(u_{m})\}
is bounded in
L^{2}([0,T],H)
and, by reflexivity, we can assume it converges weakly to
v\in L^{2}([0,T],H)
up to subsequences; thus it remains to prove that
v(t)=f(t,u(t)).

Fix a countable and dense sequence
\{e_{j}\}
in
H;
then for any
j\in\mathbb{N}

\langle f(t,u_{m}(t)),e_{j}\rangle_{H}\to\langle f(t,u(t)),e_{j}\rangle_{H}\text{ as }m\to\infty

for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
and by Egorov Theorem there exists a null set
A_{j}
such that the convergence is uniform on
[0,T]\setminus A_{j}.
Since
\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}A_{j}
is also null, it holds for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
that

(4.2)

\int_{0}^{T}|\langle f(t,u_{m}(t))-f(t,u(t)),e_{j}\rangle_{H}|dt\to 0\text{ as }m\to\infty.

Recalling the fact that
\{N_{f}(u_{m})\}
weakly converges to
v
in
L^{2}([0,T],H),
one in particular gets

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}|\langle N_{f}(u_{m})(t)-v(t),e_{j}\rangle_{H}|dt=0.

The latter together with (
4.2
) bring to
\langle f(t,u(t))-v(t),e_{j}\rangle_{H}=0
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and by density
v=N_{f}(u)
follows.

It is worth noting that the class of demicontinuous function is wide and also includes maximally monotone operators, as we point out in Section
6
.

5
Existence and regularity of solutions

This section is devoted to the study on the existence of solution in
H^{1}([0,T],H)\cap L^{2}([0,T],V)
of the problem

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u^{\prime}(t)+A(t)u(t)=f(t,u(t))\qquad t\in[0,T]\ \\
u(0)=g(u),\end{cases}

where,

(i)

V
and
H
are Hilbert spaces, with
V
densely embedded into
H
and the embedding
V\hookrightarrow H
is compact.

(ii)

\{A(t):t\in[0,T]\}
is generated by a sesquilinear form
a
which satisfies (H1)-(H4) and (S).

(iii)

f:[0,T]\times H\to H
satisfies conditions (F1)-(F3) in Lemma
4.2
and it holds the following

(T)

There exists two real numbers
R_{0}>r_{0}>0
such that for any

x\in H
with
\|x\|_{H}\in(r_{0},R_{0})

(5.2)

\mathfrak{Re}(\langle f(t,x),x\rangle_{H})\leq 0\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

(iv)

g:L^{2}([0,T],H)\to V
is demicontinuous, maps bounded sets into bounded sets and the condition

(5.3)

\|g(u)\|_{H}<r\text{ whenever }\frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T],H)}}{\sqrt{T}}=:r\in(r_{0},R_{0})

holds, where
r_{0}
and
R_{0}
are the constants from condition (T).

Theorem 5.1

Suppose that the above conditions hold, then Problem (
5.1
) has a solution
u_{*}\in H^{1}([0,T],H)\cap L^{2}([0,T],V);
moreover the estimate

\displaystyle\|u_{*}\|_{H^{1}([0,T],H)}+\|u_{*}\|_{L^{2}([0,T],V)}+\|A(\cdot)u_{*}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}([0,T],H)}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(2\max\{ar_{0}\sqrt{T},\|b\|_{L^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}_{+})}\}+g^{*}\right)

holds, where
g^{*}:=\sup\{\|g(u)\|_{V}:\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,T],H)}\leq r_{0}\sqrt{T}\}
and
C_{1}>0
is the constant from (
1.4
).

Proof

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
R\in(r_{0}\sqrt{T},R_{0}\sqrt{T});
we will denote by
\bar{B}_{R,m}\subset L^{2}([0,T],H_{m})
the set

\bar{B}_{R,m}:=\left\{u\in L^{2}([0,T],H_{m}):\|u(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}([0,T],H)}\leq R\right\}

and let
A_{m}(t)
be the operator given in (
3.1
).

For a fixed
w\in\bar{B}_{R,m},
we will at first consider the following problem

(5.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u^{\prime}(t)+A_{m}(t)u(t)=\mathbb{P}_{m}f(t,w(t)),\qquad a.e.\ \ t\in[0,T]\ \\
u(0)=\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w).\end{cases}

From Lemmas
2.1
-(iv),
3.3
and
4.2
that (
5.5
) has a unique solution in
H^{1}([0,T],H_{m})\cap L^{2}([0,T],V)\cap C([0,T],H_{m})
which can be represented by

u_{w}(t):=E_{m}(t,0)\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w)+\int_{0}^{t}E_{m}(t,s)\mathbb{P}_{m}N_{f}(w(s))ds\qquad t\in[0,T],

where
\{E_{m}(t,s)\}_{(t,s)\in\Delta}
is the evolution system associated to
A_{m}(t)
and
N_{f}
is the superposition operator
N_{f}(w)(t):=f(t,w(t)).
Also note that the estimate

\|u_{w}(t)\|_{H^{1}([0,T],H)}\leq C(\|\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w)\|_{V}+\|N_{f}w\|_{L^{2}([0,T],H)})

holds true for some constant
C>0
(see Theorem
1.3
).

The next step is to define the map
S:[0,1]\times\bar{B}_{R,m}\to L^{2}([0,T],H_{m})
by

(5.6)

S(\lambda,w(t))=\lambda E_{m}(t,0)\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w)+\int_{0}^{t}\lambda E_{m}(t,s)\mathbb{P}_{m}N_{f}(w(s))\qquad t\in[0,T].

and to prove that
S
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
1.8
.

Firstly observe that
S(0,x)=0\in\operatorname{int}(\bar{B}_{R,m});
about the complete continuity of
S
, firstly let
\{(\lambda_{k},w_{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset[0,1]\times\bar{B}_{R,m}
be a sequence such that
\lambda_{k}\to\lambda_{0}
and
w_{k}\to w_{0}.

We show that
S(\lambda_{k},w_{k})\to S(\lambda_{0},w_{0})
in
L^{2}([0,T],H)
, as
k\to\infty
.

It is not difficult to note that, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{k}E_{m}(t,0)\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w_{k})-\lambda_{0}E_{m}(t,0)\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w_{0})\|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{0}|\|E_{m}(t,0)\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w_{0})\|_{H}+\lambda_{k}\|E_{m}(t,0)\mathbb{P}_{m}(g(w_{k})-g(w_{0}))\|_{H}.

The latter implies that

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{k}E_{m}(\cdot,0)\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w_{k})-\lambda_{0}E_{m}(\cdot,0)\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w_{0})\|_{L^{2}([0,T],H_{m})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{0}|\|g(w_{0})\|_{H}+\|\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w_{k})-\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w_{0})\|_{H}).

Since
\mathbb{P}_{m}
is weak-to-strong continuous, while
g
is demicontinuous, we derive that
\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w_{k})\to\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w_{0})
, as
k\to\infty.

We have then proved that

(5.7)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}E_{m}(\cdot,0)\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w_{k})=\lambda_{0}E_{m}(\cdot,0)\mathbb{P}_{m}g(w_{0})\text{ in }L^{2}([0,T],H_{m}).

On the other hand, since
\mathbb{P}_{m}
is weak-to-strong continuous, while
N_{f}
is demicontinuous by Lemma
4.2
, we derive that
\mathbb{P}_{m}N_{f}(w_{k})(s)\to\mathbb{P}_{m}N_{f}(w_{0})(s)
, as
k\to\infty.

Note that
w_{k}(s)\to w_{0}(s)
a.e. uniformly on
[0,T]
by Egorov theorem, so that
\|w_{k}(s)\|_{H}\leq\|w_{0}(s)\|_{H}+\varepsilon_{0}
holds uniformly on
[0,T]
for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0.
From condition (F3) and the contractivity of the evolution system, it is derived that

(5.8)

\displaystyle\|E_{m}(t,s)\mathbb{P}_{m}N_{f}(w_{k})(s)\|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle a\|w_{0}(s)\|_{H}+\varepsilon_{0}+b(s)\text{ for a.e. }t\in[0,T],

with

\Big\|a\|w_{0}(\cdot)\|_{H}+\varepsilon_{0}+b(\cdot)\Big\|_{L^{1}([0,T],\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq C(R+\varepsilon_{0}+\|b\|_{L^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}_{+})}).

Then, by Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem,

\int_{0}^{t}E_{m}(t,s)\mathbb{P}_{m}N_{f}(w_{k})(s)ds\to\int_{0}^{t}E_{m}(t,s)\mathbb{P}_{m}N_{f}(w_{0})(s)ds

a.e. on
t\in[0,T]
as
k\to\infty;
since
\lambda_{k}\to\lambda_{0}
too, then

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}\int_{0}^{t}E_{m}(t,s)\mathbb{P}_{m}N_{f}(w_{k})(s)ds=\lambda_{0}\int_{0}^{t}E_{m}(t,s)\mathbb{P}_{m}N_{f}(w_{0})(s)ds

for a.e.
t\in[0,T].
Similarly as in (
5.8
) it can be proved that the above convergence is dominated by the same function in
L^{2}([0,T],H_{m}).
Hence,

\int_{0}^{(\cdot)}E_{m}(\cdot,s)\mathbb{P}_{m}N_{f}(w_{k})(s)ds\to\int_{0}^{(\cdot)}E_{m}(\cdot,s)\mathbb{P}_{m}N_{f}(w_{0})(s)ds

in
L^{2}([0,T],H)
as
k\to\infty
and so

\lim_{k\to\infty}S(\lambda_{k},w_{k})=S(\lambda_{0},w_{0})\text{ in }L^{2}([0,T],H_{m})

and the continuity of the operator
S
is proved.

We next show that
S
maps bounded sets into compact ones. To this end, it is enough to show that
S([0,1],\bar{B}_{R,m})
is relatively compact in

u(0)=\int_{[0,T]}\mathcal{V}(u(t))\operatorname{d}t

has been introduced. In order to better expose the range of applications provided by Theorem
5.1
, we will deal with an initial condition of this type.

As highlighted before, existing literature on the topic mainly deals with existence of mild solutions to nonlocal initial value problems. Here, we are interested in proving the existence of strong solutions and further regularities; this result can be achieved by introducing a smoothing term, below represented by the convolution with a sufficiently smooth function.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
A(t)
be associated to the sesquilinear form given (
6.1
); then assumptions (H1)-(H4) and (S) are still with
V=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see
HaOu
and
Pas
).
We are interested in the following nonlocal evolution problem:

(6.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u^{\prime}(t)+A(t)u(t)=f(t),\ t\in(0,1]\\
\displaystyle u(0)=\int_{I}\varphi*u(t)\operatorname{d}t,\end{cases}

where
f\in L^{\infty}([0,1],\mathbb{R}),\ I=\bigcup[s_{i},t_{i}]\subset[0,1],

\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a mollifier with
\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<1
and where
\varphi*u(t)
represents the convolution product among the two terms.

Note that by choosing
\varepsilon>0
small enough,
A_{\varepsilon}(t):=A(t)-\varepsilon I
still satisfies the required properties, while
f_{\varepsilon}(t,x):=f(t)-\varepsilon x
fullfills requirements
(F1)-(F3)
and the transversality condition (
5.2
) for any
R_{0}>r_{0}>\|f\|_{\infty}.

By a standard argument, it is promptly derived that
\displaystyle g(u)=\int_{I}\varphi*u(t)\operatorname{d}t
sastisfies

\|g(u){\|^{2}}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{[0,1]}\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\operatorname{d}t.

The latter proves that
g:L^{2}([0,T],{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is well-defined, continuous and satisfies (
5.3
). At this point, an immediate application of Theorem
5.1
brings the following

Proposition 6.4

A solution
u_{*}\in H^{1}([0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{2}([0,T],H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
to problem (
6.5
) exists for which the a priori estimate (
5.1
) holds.

The free energy of dilute Bose gases at low temperatures interacting via strong potentials
S. Fournais, L. Junge, T. Girardot, L. Morin, M. Olivieri, A. Triay

Abstract
We consider a dilute Bose gas in the thermodynamic limit and prove a lower bound on the free energy for low temperatures which is in agreement with the conjecture of Lee-Huang-Yang on the excitation spectrum of the system. Combining techniques of [ 15 ] and [ 17 ] , we give a simpler and shorter proof resolving the case of strong interactions, including the hard-core potential.

Introduction

The mathematical analysis of the interacting Bose gas has been subject of intense scrutiny and important breakthroughs in the last decades. In particular, much effort has been dedicated to rigorously justifying the seminal work of Lee, Huang and Yang
[
20
]
where they compute the spectral properties of the dilute Bose gas. A corner stone of this program has been the proof of the leading term of the ground state energy density in
[
24
]
, wherein they complement Dyson’s upper bound
[
10
]
from 40 years before with a matching lower bound. The successes of the approaches initiated around that time are well described in the lecture notes
[
11
]
.

According to
[
20
]
a dilute gas of Bosons behave similarly as a system of independent (quantum) harmonic oscillators following Bogoliubov’s dispersion relation. Interestingly, the authors of
[
20
]
gave their names to the correction to the leading order of the ground state energy density - the famous Lee-Huang-Yang term - which corresponds to the zero point energy of such a system, and was rigorously derived in
[
29
, upper bound]
and
[
14
,
15
, lower bound]
.

To see the effect of the rest of the spectrum, it is necessary to consider the system at positive temperature and, for example, compute its free energy per unit volume. This was recently investigated in the work
[
17
]
(see also
[
18
]
for the matching upper bound) where a two-term asymptotic expansion for the free energy per unit volume was proved for temperatures that are low but for which the thermal correction is of the order of the Lee-Huang-Yang (LHY) term.

Since the foundational work
[
20
]
, a special role has been played by the hard core (or hard sphere) potential. This potential is often chosen for its simplicity as it depends on one parameter only (its scattering length, which equals its radius) and because the system in the dilute regime is not expected to depend on the microscopic details of the potential, at least to some order. However, its rigorous mathematical analysis is very challenging since it strongly penalises approximation schemes by having infinite integral. It is therefore an important testing problem for techniques in the area and for the idea of
universality
, i.e. that the scattering length should be the main parameter governing the dilute regime.

The purpose of the current paper is two-fold. First of all we extend the result of
[
17
]
to the case of interaction potentials with large (possibly infinite) integral, thereby in particular for the first time rigorously obtaining the lower bound on the free energy of the Bose gas in the case of hard core interactions.
The second purpose is expository. By combining the Neumann localization approach of
[
17
]
(see also
[
6
]
) with the techniques of
[
14
,
15
]
, we provide a fairly simple and short proof of lower bounds in the thermodynamic limit in a generality allowing for very singular potentials.
Our proof is clearly shorter than
[
15
]
which is restricted to
T=0
(although we here have slightly more restrictive assumptions on the potential) and is also arguably shorter and simpler than
[
17
]
.

We will now define the model considered and state the result of the paper.
We consider a system of
N
interacting, non-relativistic bosons in a large box of sidelength
L
in
3
dimensions governed by the Hamiltonian

H_{N,L}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}-\Delta_{i}+\sum_{i<j}V(x_{i}-x_{j}).

(1.1)

The operator
H_{N,L}
acts on the bosonic Hilbert space
\otimes_{\mathrm{s}}^{N}L^{2}(\Lambda_{L})=L^{2}_{\rm{sym}}(\Lambda^{N})
, with
\Lambda_{L}:=[0,L]^{3}
.
For concreteness, we will take Neumann boundary conditions in order to realize
H_{N,L}
as a self-adjoint operator.
We define the ground state energy
E(L,N)
and the free energy
F(L,N)
at temperature
T>0
by

E(L,N)=\inf\mathrm{Spec}\,H_{N,L},\qquad F(L,N)=-T\log\mathrm{Tr}\big(e^{-\frac{H_{N,L}}{T}}\big).

(1.2)

We recall that the free energy is the minimum of the variational problem

F(L,N)=\inf_{\Gamma}\Big\{{\rm{Tr}}\big(H_{N}\Gamma\big)-T\,S(\Gamma)\Big\},

(1.3)

where the infimum is taken over trace-class operators
\Gamma
on
L^{2}_{\rm{sym}}(\Lambda^{N})
such that
0\leq\Gamma\leq 1
and
{\rm{Tr}}\,\Gamma=1
and
S(\Gamma)=-\rm{Tr}(\Gamma\ln\Gamma)
is the entropy of
\Gamma
.
The particle density for finite systems is
\rho=N/L^{3}
, and we define the ground state energy density
e(\rho)
and the free energy density
f(\rho,T)
by

\displaystyle e(\rho)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
N/L^{3}\to\rho\end{subarray}}\frac{E(L,N)}{L^{3}},\qquad f(\rho,T)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
N/L^{3}\to\rho\end{subarray}}\frac{F(L,N)}{L^{3}}.

(1.4)

It is standard
[
27
]
that the limits exist and are independent of both the boundary conditions and the sequence of
(N,L)
as long as
N/L^{3}\to\rho
.

The main result of the paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Free energy in the thermodynamic limit)
For all
C_{0}>0
there exists
C>0
such that, for
\eta>0
small enough the following holds. Let
V
be a non-negative, radially symmetric and non-increasing potential with scattering length
a
and compact support of radius
R\leq C_{0}a
. Then for
0\leq\rho a^{3}\leq C^{-1}
,
\nu\in(0,\frac{\eta}{3})
and
0\leq T\leq\rho a(\rho a^{3})^{-\nu}
,

\displaystyle f(\rho,T)\geq 4\pi a\rho^{2}\left(1+\frac{128}{15\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{\rho a^{3}}\right)+\frac{T^{5/2}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\log\left(1-e^{-\sqrt{p^{4}+\tfrac{16\pi\rho a}{T}p^{2}}}\right)\mathrm{d}p-C(\rho a)^{5/2}(\rho a^{3})^{\eta/4}.

(1.5)

Remark 1.2
.

•

The result on the free energy in Theorem
1.1
confirms the picture of
[
20
]

\displaystyle E(L,N)\approx 4\pi N\rho a\left(1+\frac{128}{15\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{\rho a^{3}}\right).

(1.6)

Furthermore, the excitation spectrum for low-lying eigenvalues should be given by the Bogoliubov dispersion relation

\sum n_{p}\sqrt{p^{4}+16\pi\rho ap^{2}},

where

denotes the momentum of an excitation, and

denotes the number of bosonic excitations with that momentum. Combining these two pieces of information leads to the expression given in Theorem

Note that the expansion (

•

2.1
Replacement by an integrable potential

We begin by replacing the initial interaction potential
V
by a suitable integrable potential
v\leq V
, which obviously lowers the free energy. We need however to control the difference between the scattering lengths to ensure that the error coming from the substitution in the leading term does not affect the correction term.

Let us first recall some facts about the scattering problem. We denote by
\varphi_{V}
the radial solution in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
to the zero-energy scattering equation

-\Delta\varphi_{V}+\frac{1}{2}V\varphi_{V}=0,

(2.1)

normalized such that
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\varphi_{V}(x)=1
. Of particular interest throughout the paper are also the functions

\omega_{V}:=1-\varphi_{V},\qquad g_{V}:=V\varphi_{V}=V(1-\omega_{V}).

(2.2)

By these functions, it is possible to rewrite equation (
2.1
) as

-\Delta\omega_{V}=\frac{1}{2}g_{V},

(2.3)

and from this, by the divergence theorem, recover the scattering length by

\widehat{g}_{V}(0)=\int g_{V}(x)\mathrm{d}x=8\pi a.

(2.4)

We refer to Section
3
for more details.

Proposition 2.1

(Replacement by an integrable potential)
.

There exists
C>0
such that the following holds. Let
V
be a positive, radially symmetric and decreasing potential with scattering length
a
, and compact support of radius
0\leq R\leq C_{0}a
. Then we can construct another positive and radially symmetric potential
v\leq V
with scattering length
a(v)
satisfying

0\leq a-a(v)\leq a\sqrt{\rho a^{3}}K_{\ell}^{-1},\qquad\int v(x)\mathrm{d}x\leq C(\rho a)^{-\frac{1}{2}}K_{\ell},\qquad v\leq\ell^{2}a^{-4},

(2.5)

and

g_{v}(y)\leq Cv(x)\quad\text{ for }\quad|x|\leq|y|,

(2.6)

where
g_{v}=v\varphi_{v}
and
\varphi_{v}
is the scattering solution for
v
.

The proof is given in Section
3
. We denote here by
F(\ell,n)[V]
and
F_{\mathrm{Bog}}(\ell,n)[a]
the free energy (
1.3
) associated to the potential
V
in the box
\Lambda
and the expression (
1.8
) with scattering length
a
, respectively. Assuming that the desired estimate (
1.7
) holds for the potential
v
and using that the free energy
F(\ell,n)[V]
is a non-decreasing function of
V
, we obtain

\displaystyle F(\ell,n)[V]\geq F(\ell,n)[v]

\displaystyle\geq F_{\mathrm{Bog}}(\ell,n)[a(v)]-C\ell^{3}(\rho a(v))^{5/2}(\rho a(v)^{3})^{\eta/4}

\displaystyle\geq F_{\mathrm{Bog}}(\ell,n)[a]-C\ell^{3}(\rho a)^{5/2}(\rho a^{3})^{\eta/4}-C\left(\ell^{3}\rho^{2}+T^{3/2}\rho\ell^{3}\right)|a(v)-a|

\displaystyle\geq F_{\mathrm{Bog}}(\ell,n)[a]-C\ell^{3}(\rho a)^{5/2}(\rho a^{3})^{\eta/4},

where we used (
2.5
) to obtain the second line and
[
17
, Lemma 9.1]
for the replacement of

by

in the sum appearing in

Therefore, it is enough to prove Theorem

2.2
Splitting of the potential energy

Let us introduce the projections on and outside the condensate,

P=\frac{1}{\ell^{3}}|1\rangle\langle 1|,\qquad Q=\mathbf{1}-P,

(2.7)

acting on
L^{2}(\Lambda)
. If
\Phi\in L^{2}(\Lambda^{N})
and
1\leq i\leq N
, we denote by
P_{i}\Phi
and
Q_{i}\Phi
the action of
P
and
Q
on the variable
x_{i}
. We also denote the number of particles in the condensate and the number of excited particles respectively by

n_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}P_{i},\qquad n_{+}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}Q_{i}=N-n_{0}.

(2.8)

The strategy carried out in
[
21
]
and used in many other works, is to expand
\mathbf{1}_{(L^{2})^{\otimes_{\textrm{s}}N}}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(P_{i}+Q_{i})
which leads to identifying
(L^{2})^{\otimes_{\textrm{s}}N}
with a truncated Fock space over the excitation space
\mathrm{Ran}\,Q
where the vacuum vector is the pure condensate
\ket{\frac{1}{\ell^{3/2}}}^{\otimes_{\textrm{s}}N}
. Having factored out the condensate, it remains to extract the correlation energy. This strategy does not work for hard core potentials because the vacuum (the pure condensate) does not even belong to the domain of the Hamiltonian: one needs to factor out parts of the correlations first. In this spirit, the strategy initiated in
[
14
]
and then
[
15
,
13
]
is to
renormalize
the potential energy from the beginning by using the identity

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}=(Q_{i}Q_{j}+\omega(x_{i}-x_{j})(\mathbf{1}-Q_{i}Q_{j}))+\varphi(x_{i}-x_{j})(\mathbf{1}-Q_{i}Q_{j}).

(2.9)

Lemma 2.2
.

For all positive and radially symmetric potentials
v
, we have the identity

\sum_{i<j}v(x_{i}-x_{j})={Q}_{0}^{\rm ren}+Q_{1}^{\rm ren}+Q_{2}^{\rm ren}+Q_{3}^{\rm ren}+Q_{4}^{\rm ren}

where

\displaystyle{Q}_{0}^{\rm ren}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}P_{i}P_{j}(g+g\omega)(x_{i}-x_{j})P_{j}P_{i}.

(2.10)

\displaystyle{Q}_{1}^{\rm ren}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i\neq j}\big(Q_{i}P_{j}(g+g\omega)(x_{i}-x_{j})P_{j}P_{i}+\mathrm{h.c.}\big),

(2.11)

\displaystyle{Q}_{2}^{\rm ren}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i\neq j}P_{i}Q_{j}(g+g\omega)(x_{i}-x_{j})P_{j}Q_{i}+\sum_{i\neq j}P_{i}Q_{j}(g+g\omega)(x_{i}-x_{j})Q_{j}P_{i}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}P_{i}P_{j}g(x_{i}-x_{j})Q_{j}Q_{i}+\mathrm{h.c.},

(2.12)

\displaystyle{Q}_{3}^{\rm ren}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i\neq j}P_{i}Q_{j}g(x_{i}-x_{j})Q_{j}Q_{i}+\mathrm{h.c.},

(2.13)

\displaystyle{Q}_{4}^{\rm ren}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\Pi_{ij}^{*}v(x_{i}-x_{j})\Pi_{ij}\,,

(2.14)

with

\displaystyle\Pi_{ij}:=Q_{j}Q_{i}+\omega(x_{i}-x_{j})\left(P_{j}P_{i}+P_{j}Q_{i}+Q_{j}P_{i}\right).

Proof.

This is proved by inserting (
2.9
) in each term of the potential
\sum_{i<j}v(x_{i}-x_{j})
and expanding using that
1-Q_{i}Q_{j}=P_{i}P_{j}+P_{i}Q_{j}+Q_{i}P_{j}
. We recall that
g=v(1-\omega)
.

2.3
Spectral gaps

Many of our estimates will rely on analysis in momentum space. In this article, the momenta are elements of
\frac{\pi}{\ell}\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3}
, and we define low and high momenta by

\mathcal{P}_{L}:=\Big\{p\in\frac{\pi}{\ell}\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3},\;\;0<|p|\leq K_{H}\ell^{-1}\Big\},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{H}:=\Big\{p\in\frac{\pi}{\ell}\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3},\;\;|p|>K_{H}\ell^{-1}\Big\},

(2.15)

where
K_{H}
is a large
\rho
-dependent parameter.
In the theorems below we always write our assumptions on
K_{H}
. The final choice of
K_{H}
, and a few other parameters, will be specified in (
2.42
) below.
We define the corresponding localized projectors by

\displaystyle Q^{L}:=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{P}_{L}}(\sqrt{-\Delta}),\qquad Q^{H}:=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{P}_{H}}(\sqrt{-\Delta}),

(2.16)

where
\Delta
is the Neumann Laplacian on
\Lambda
. The number of high and low excitations are respectively given by

n_{+}^{H}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}Q_{j}^{H},\qquad n_{+}^{L}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}Q^{L}_{j},

(2.17)

both acting on
L^{2}_{\rm{sym}}(\Lambda^{N})
. These definitions imply for instance that
P+Q_{L}+Q_{H}=1
and that
n_{+}^{L}+n_{+}^{H}=n_{+}
.

Many of our error terms will be bounded by
n_{+}
,
n_{+}^{H}
or
n_{+}^{L}
. To control these errors, we will extract some positive quantities from the kinetic energy, refered to as
spectral gaps
. They are gathered into one operator

G:=\frac{\pi n_{+}}{4\ell^{2}}+\frac{K_{H}n^{H}_{+}}{2\ell^{2}}+\frac{\pi n_{+}^{L}n_{+}}{4\mathcal{M}\ell^{2}}+\frac{K_{H}n_{+}^{L}n^{H}_{+}}{2\mathcal{M}\ell^{2}},

(2.18)

where
\mathcal{M}
is some large
\rho
-dependent parameter, which will need to satisfy specific conditions in later theorems. These spectral gaps are extracted from the kinetic energy, from which will only remain

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{T}_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}-\Delta_{j}-\frac{\pi n_{+}}{2\ell^{2}}-\frac{K_{H}n^{H}_{+}}{\ell^{2}},

with

\mathcal{T}=-\Delta-\frac{\pi}{2\ell^{2}}Q-\frac{K_{H}}{\ell^{2}}Q^{H}\geq 0.

(2.19)

More precisely, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 2.3
.

There exist
C>0
such that the following holds. Let
v
be as in Proposition
2.1
,
\rho\ell^{3}(\rho a^{3})^{\alpha}\leq N\leq 20\rho\ell^{3}
,
T\leq\rho a(\rho a^{3})^{-\nu}
, with
\alpha+5\nu/2<6/17
and
\mathcal{M}\geq\rho\ell^{3}(\rho a^{3})^{\gamma}
, for some
\gamma>0
, then for
\rho a^{3}\leq C^{-1}
we have

\displaystyle F(\ell,N)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\Gamma}\Big\{\mathrm{Tr}\big(H_{N}^{\rm{mod}}\Gamma+T\,\Gamma\ln\Gamma+G\Gamma\big)\Big\}-C\ell^{3}(\rho a)^{5/2}(\rho a^{3})^{1/18-2\gamma-\nu}K_{H}^{3},

where the infimum is taken over trace-one operators
\Gamma\geq 0
. Here the modified Hamiltonian is

H_{N}^{\rm{mod}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{T}_{i}+\sum_{i<j}v(x_{i}-x_{j}),

(2.20)

and
G
was defined in (
2.18
).

The proof of Theorem
2.3
is given in Section
4
and relies on rough a priori bounds on the numbers of excitations in
\Lambda_{\ell}
.

2.5
Symmetrization

To deal with Neumann boundary conditions, an important idea of
[
17
]
is to symmetrize the potential using a mirroring technique to make it commute with the Neumann momentum. The mirror transformations
p_{z}
, for
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, are defined by

(p_{z}(x))_{i}=(-1)^{z_{i}}\Big(x_{i}-\frac{\ell}{2}\Big)+\frac{\ell}{2}+\ell z_{i},\qquad i=1,2,3.

(2.22)

Note that in particular
p_{z}(\Lambda)=\{x+\ell z\;:\;x\in\Lambda\}
.
For
p\in\frac{\pi}{\ell}\mathbb{N}^{3}_{0}
, we denote by

u_{p}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Lambda|}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}c_{p_{i}}\cos(p_{i}x_{i}),\qquad\text{where }\quad c_{p_{i}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\quad p_{i}=0,\\
\sqrt{2}&\text{if}\quad p_{i}\neq 0,\end{cases}

(2.23)

the normalized eigenbasis of the Neumann Laplacian on
\Lambda
. For any
f\in L^{1}(\Lambda)
, the symmetrization of
f
is defined by

f^{\rm{s}}(x,y):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}f(p_{z}(x)-y)

(2.24)

for a.e.
x,y\in\Lambda
. If
f
is radial, as shown in
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
, the operator with kernel
f^{\rm{s}}(x,y)
commutes with the Neumann basis.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f:\;\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
be radial and integrable with
\mathrm{supp}(f)\subset B(0,R)
for some
R\leq\ell/2
. Then for
p,q\in\frac{\pi}{\ell}\mathbb{N}^{3}
we have

\int_{\Lambda^{2}}f^{\mathrm{s}}(x,y)u_{p}(x)u_{q}(y)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=\delta_{p,q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x)\prod_{i=1}^{3}\cos(p_{i}x_{i})\mathrm{d}x=\delta_{p,q}\widehat{f}(p).

(2.25)

Here and through all the paper
\widehat{f}
is the Fourier transform of
f
defined by

\widehat{f}(p)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x)e^{-ipx}\mathrm{d}x.

Note that since
\mathrm{supp}\,g\subset\mathrm{supp}\,v\subset B(0,R)
, the sum defining

From now on, we work with the symmetrized Hamiltonian

(Second quantization in the Neumann basis)

6

Definition 3.1

Let

(3.1)

\displaystyle V_{\rm hc}(x):=\begin{cases}+\infty,&|x|\leq R,\\
0,&|x|>R.\end{cases}

(3.2)

For this special potential it is not difficult to see that

. For general potentials,
inserting the test function
\max\{0,1-\frac{R}{|x|}\}
, we find
a\leq R
. It is also easy to verify that
a
is an increasing function of
V
. The minimizer
\varphi_{V}
solves the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation

-\Delta\varphi_{V}+\frac{1}{2}V\varphi_{V}=0,

(3.3)

in a weak sense. It is easy to check with Newton’s theorem that

\varphi_{V}(x)=1-\frac{a}{|x|},\quad\text{for $|x|\geq R$},

(3.4)

and furthermore
\varphi_{V}
is non-decreasing, non-negative and radial.
We will also use the following standard monotonicity result from
[
11
, Lemma C.2]
: if
V_{1}\geq V_{2}\geq 0
, then

\varphi_{V_{1}}(x)\leq\varphi_{V_{2}}(x)
for all
x
.
We will omit the
v
from the notation of the scattering length and write
\varphi:=\varphi_{V}
, if the potential is clear from the context.
We then recall the notation

\displaystyle\omega=1-\varphi,\qquad g=V\varphi=V(1-\omega).

(3.5)

Clearly,

\displaystyle-\Delta\omega=\frac{1}{2}g,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widehat{g}(0)=\int g\,\mathrm{d}x=8\pi a.

(3.6)

Having introduced the necessary theory and notation we may provide the proof of Proposition
2.1
. The proof will rely on the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

For all
v_{1}\geq v_{2}\geq 0
and
v^{\prime}\geq 0
we have

a(v_{1})-a(v_{2})\geq a(v_{1}+v^{\prime})-a(v_{2}+v^{\prime}).

Proof.

For all
t\in[0,1]
, we introduce
\varphi_{1}^{t}
and
\varphi_{2}^{t}
as the scattering solutions for
v_{1}+tv^{\prime}
and
v_{2}+tv^{\prime}
respectively. By definition we have for
j=1,2
,

4\pi a(v_{j}+tv^{\prime})=\int\Big(|\nabla\varphi_{j}^{t}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(v_{j}+tv^{\prime})|\varphi_{j}^{t}|^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}x.

In particular, rearranging the terms we find

\displaystyle 4\pi a(v_{1}+tv^{\prime})-4\pi a(v_{2}+tv^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int\Big(\nabla\varphi^{t}_{1}\cdot\nabla(\varphi_{1}^{t}-\varphi_{2}^{t})+\frac{1}{2}(v_{1}+tv^{\prime})\varphi_{1}^{t}(\varphi_{1}^{t}-\varphi_{2}^{t})\Big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad-\int\Big(\nabla\varphi^{t}_{2}\cdot\nabla(\varphi_{2}^{t}-\varphi_{1}^{t})+\frac{1}{2}(v_{2}+tv^{\prime})\varphi_{2}^{t}(\varphi_{2}^{t}-\varphi_{1}^{t})\Big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\int(v_{1}-v_{2})\varphi^{t}_{1}\varphi^{t}_{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

When we integrate by parts, we find that the two first lines vanish by (
3.3
). For instance,

\int\Big(\nabla\varphi^{t}_{1}\cdot\nabla(\varphi_{1}^{t}-\varphi_{2}^{t})+\frac{1}{2}(v_{1}+tv^{\prime})\varphi_{1}^{t}(\varphi_{1}^{t}-\varphi_{2}^{t})\Big)\mathrm{d}x=\int(-\Delta\varphi_{1}^{t}+\frac{1}{2}(v_{1}+tv^{\prime})\varphi_{1}^{t})(\varphi_{1}^{t}-\varphi_{2}^{t})\mathrm{d}x=0.

Therefore,

a(v_{1}+tv^{\prime})-a(v_{2}+tv^{\prime})=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int(v_{1}-v_{2})\varphi^{t}_{1}\varphi^{t}_{2}\mathrm{d}x,

for all
t\in[0,1]
. Comparing with
t=0
we deduce

a(v_{1}+tv^{\prime})-a(v_{2}+tv^{\prime})=a(v_{1})-a(v_{2})+\frac{1}{8\pi}\int(v_{1}-v_{2})(\varphi^{t}_{1}\varphi^{t}_{2}-\varphi_{1}^{0}\varphi_{2}^{0})\mathrm{d}x.

Since
v_{j}+tv^{\prime}\geq v_{j}
, we have
0\leq\varphi_{j}^{t}\leq\varphi_{j}^{0}
pointwise, and thus the last integral is negative. The result follows with
t=1
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Given
K>0
and a non-increasing potential
v
, the potential
\min(v,K)
satisfies

a(V)\geq a(\min(V,K))\geq a(V)-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{K}}

Proof.

Using that
V
is decreasing, we get
\{V>K\}=B(0,R_{K})
for some
R_{K}\geq 0
.
Note that the hard core potential with radius
R_{K}
has scattering length
R_{K}
.
Then comparing to the hard core potential, we have

a(V)-a(\min(V,K))\leq a(V\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R_{K})})-a(K\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R_{K})})\leq R_{K}-a(K\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R_{K})}),

(3.7)

where in the first inequality we used Lemma
3.2
with
V^{\prime}=V\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R_{K})^{c}}
, and in the second inequality that
V\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R_{K})}
is smaller than the hardcore potential
V_{hc}
of radius
R_{K}
.
Now one can compute
a_{K}:=a(K\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R_{K})})
by solving

-\Delta\varphi+\frac{1}{2}K\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R_{K})}\varphi=0,

to find that there is a
c>0
such that

\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}c\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{\frac{K}{2}}|x|)}{|x|},\qquad&|x|\leq R_{K},\\
1-\frac{a_{K}}{|x|},\qquad&|x|\geq R_{K}.\end{cases}

Knowing that
\varphi
is continuous and differentiable yields we find that

R_{K}-a_{K}=\frac{1-e^{-2\gamma}}{\gamma-1+e^{-2\gamma}(\gamma+1)}a_{K}\qquad\text{where}\quad\gamma=\sqrt{\frac{K}{2}}R_{K}.

(3.8)

Lastly we observe that for
\gamma\geq 2
the right-hand side of (
3.8
) is bounded by
\frac{2}{\gamma}a_{K}
, and if
\gamma\leq 2
we have
R_{K}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{K}}
. Thus combining (
3.7
) with (
3.8
), using
\frac{a_{K}}{R_{K}}\leq 1
, we get

a(V)-a(\min(V,K))\leq R_{K}-a_{K}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{K}}.

∎

Before giving the proof of Proposition
2.1
, we explain the construction of
v
in the case where
V=V_{\rm hc}
is the hard core potential of radius
a
. In
[
15
]
it was explained that to get (
2.5
) the (almost optimal) approximation of
V_{\rm hc}
is by a ‘thin shell’ potential supported on the annulus
A:=\{a-a^{2}\ell^{-1}\leq|x|\leq a\}
and of height
\ell^{2}a^{-4}
.
However, this potential clearly doesn’t satisfy (
2.6
), since it vanishes inside the shell. The remedy is to fill the inside of the shell, without changing too much the
L^{1}
norm. Therefore, the final choice of

is

v=\ell a^{-3}\mathbf{1}_{B(0,a-a^{2}\ell^{-1})}+\ell^{2}a^{-4}\mathbf{1}_{A},\qquad\text{when $V=V_{hc}$ is the hard core.}

Let us now give the details starting from an arbitrary
V
. For convenience we include the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists a universal constant
C_{0}>0
such that the following is true.
Suppose that
V:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is radial and of class
L^{1}
with compact support of radius
R
and that
S\geq 0
.
If
S\geq\frac{\int V}{8\pi a(V)}
define the potential
V_{S}:=V
.
If
S<\frac{\int V}{8\pi a(V)}
define

V_{S}:=V\mathbf{1}_{[R_{S},\infty)},

with
R_{S}
chosen so that
\int V_{S}=8\pi Sa(V)
. Then
V_{S}
satisfies

a(V)\geq a(V_{S})\geq a(V)(1-C_{0}S^{-1}).

This is a reformulation of
[
15
, Lemma 3.3]
using the fact that the proof gives the explicit construction of
v_{S}
. Using this Lemma we can prove Proposition
2.1
.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

By Lemma
3.3
, we may assume that
V\leq K
, with
K=\ell^{2}a^{-4}
. This choice guarantees that the change to the scattering length is of order
a^{2}\ell^{-1}
.
By Lemma
3.4
, we can find
0<R_{S}<R
such that the potential

V_{S}(x)=\begin{cases}V(x)\qquad&|x|\geq R_{S}\\
0\qquad&|x|<R_{S}\end{cases}

has integral
\int V_{S}\leq 8\pi Sa(V)
and satisfies

a(V)\geq a(V_{S})\geq a(V)(1-C_{0}S^{-1}).

(3.9)

Note that this error is small enough with the choice
S=\ell/a(V)
. However note that
V_{S}
does not satisfy (
2.6
), so it requires further modifications. First, we extend slightly
V_{S}
and define

w_{S}=V_{S}(R_{S})\mathbf{1}_{[R_{S}-\varepsilon,R_{S}]}+V_{S},

(3.10)

where we used the convention
\mathbf{1}_{I}(x)=\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\in I\}}
for a subset
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, and where
\varepsilon>0
will be chosen later.
Let
g_{S}=w_{S}\varphi_{w_{S}}
, and let
x_{0}
be the maximal point of
g_{S}
,

g_{S}(x_{0})=\sup g_{S}.

Note that
x_{0}\geq R_{S}
by construction.
Then we define our potential
v
by

v:=\min\big(g_{S}(x_{0}),M\big)\mathbf{1}_{[0,R_{S}-\varepsilon]}+w_{S},

(3.11)

where the parameter
M
will be chosen to bound the
L^{1}
norm of
v
. Indeed, choosing
\varepsilon=a(V)^{2}\ell^{-1}
and
M=\ell R_{S}^{-3}
,

\displaystyle\int v\leq CR_{S}^{3}M+Ca(V)^{2}\ell^{-1}R_{S}^{2}V_{S}(R_{S})+\int V_{S}\leq C\ell,

where we used that
V_{S}(R_{S})\leq K=\ell^{2}a(V)^{-4}
,
R_{S}\leq R\leq Ca(V)
and
\int V_{S}\leq 8\pi\ell
. We can compare the scattering lengths of
v
and
v
using that
V\geq v\geq V_{S}
and therefore, we get from (
3.9
) that

a(V)\geq a(v)\geq a(V)(1-Ca(V)/\ell).

Let us now show that the potential
v
satisfies (
2.6
). We consider
|x|<|y|
.

•

If
|x|\geq R_{S}-\varepsilon
then
v
is non-increasing on this region, and therefore since
\varphi_{v}\leq 1
we have

v(x)\geq v(y)\geq v(y)\varphi_{v}(y)=g_{v}(y).

•

When
|x|\leq R_{S}-\varepsilon
.
Notice that
v\varphi_{v}
is increasing on
[0,R_{S}]
. Therefore,

\displaystyle g_{v}(y)\leq\sup_{|x|\geq R_{S}}v\varphi_{v}\leq\sup_{|x|\geq R_{S}}w_{S}\varphi_{w_{S}}=g_{S}(x_{0}),

(3.12)

where the last inequality follows since
\varphi_{w_{S}}\geq\varphi_{v}
. If
g_{S}(x_{0})<M
then
g_{S}(x_{0})=v(x)
and we are done. In the other case, when
M\leq g_{S}(x_{0})
we introduce an auxiliary potential,

v_{0}=v(x_{0})\mathbf{1}_{[R_{S}-\varepsilon,|x_{0}|]}.

(3.13)

We observe that
v_{0}\leq w_{S}
and this implies that
\varphi_{w_{S}}\leq\varphi_{v_{0}}
. Therefore

g_{S}(x_{0})\leq V(x_{0})\varphi_{v_{0}}(x_{0})=V(x_{0})\Big(1-\frac{a(v_{0})}{|x_{0}|}\Big).

(3.14)

We estimate the difference between
a(v_{0})
and
|x_{0}|
identified with the scattering length of the hard-core of radius
|x_{0}|
. We use Lemma
3.3
with
K=v(x_{0})
to cut the hard-core potential, and then compare the resulting potential to

using Lemma
3.4
(with

) to get

|x_{0}|-a(v_{0})\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{v(x_{0})}}+C\frac{|x_{0}|^{2}}{\int v_{0}}.

Inserting this bound in (

g_{S}(x_{0})\leq C\frac{\sqrt{v(x_{0})}}{|x_{0}|}+Ca(V)\frac{V(x_{0})}{\int v_{0}}\leq C\frac{\ell}{a(V)^{2}R_{S}}+C\frac{a(V)}{\min(\varepsilon,R_{S})R_{S}^{2}}\leq CM,

where we used
V(x_{0})\leq\ell^{2}a(V)^{-4}
,
\varepsilon=a(V)^{2}/\ell
, and
R_{S}\leq|x_{0}|\leq R\leq Ca(V)
.
Therefore,

g_{S}(x_{0})\leq CM=Cv(x)

which combined with (
3.12
) gives the desired bound.

∎

4
Localization of large matrices: Proof of Theorem
2.3

One of the main ingredients to prove Theorem
2.3
is a rough condensation estimate for low energy states. This is by now a well known result and its proof can be found in
[
11
]
, and the technique was also crucial in
[
17
]
. At positive temperature, we need to extend this property to mixed states.

Lemma 4.1

(Condensation of low energy states)
.

There exists a
C>0
such that the following holds. Assume that
\rho\ell^{3}(\rho a^{3})^{\alpha}\leq N\leq 20\rho\ell^{3}
,
T\leq\rho a(\rho a^{3})^{-\nu}
, with
\alpha+5\nu/2<6/17
and
(\rho a^{3})\leq C^{-1}
. Let
\Gamma
be a trace-class operator on
L^{2}(\Lambda^{N})
such that
\Gamma\geq 0
,
\rm{Tr}\,\Gamma=1
, and

\mathrm{Tr}(H_{N}\Gamma)\leq 4\pi N^{2}\ell^{-3}a(1+(\rho a^{3})^{\frac{1}{17}}).

(4.1)

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(n_{+}\Gamma)

\displaystyle\leq CNK_{\ell}^{2}(\rho a^{3})^{\frac{1}{17}}.

(4.2)

In particular, this holds for the Gibbs state

\Gamma_{0}=\frac{e^{-\frac{H_{N}}{T}}}{\mathrm{Tr}(e^{-\frac{H_{N}}{T}})}.

(4.3)

Proof.

The result follows from the following lower bound, which holds as long as
N(\rho a^{3})^{\frac{1}{17}}\geq 1
,

H_{N}\geq 4\pi a\frac{N^{2}}{\ell^{3}}(1-C(\rho a^{3})^{\frac{1}{17}})+C^{\prime}\frac{n_{+}}{\ell^{2}},

(4.4)

and which can be found in
[
11
, Lemma 4.1 and Lemma 5.2]
. The constraint on
\alpha
ensures in particular the condition on
N
. Together with the upper bound (
4.1
) we deduce

{\rm{Tr}}\Big(\frac{n_{+}}{\ell^{2}}\Gamma\Big)\leq CN\rho a(\rho a^{3})^{\frac{1}{17}},

(4.5)

which is the expected condensation estimate. It remains to show that
\Gamma_{0}
satisfies the upper bound (
4.1
), which is not obvious due to the entropy term. To prove this, we use the upper bound on the free energy from
[
11
]
,

\mathrm{Tr}(H_{N}\Gamma_{0}+T\,\Gamma_{0}\ln\Gamma_{0})\leq\inf\sigma(H_{N})\leq 4\pi\rho Na(1+C(\rho a^{3})^{1/3}).

(4.6)

The upper bound on the ground state energy was proven by Dyson
[
10
]
(see also
[
3
]
for an improvement to the order of the LHY correction, but here we do not need such precise estimates).
Therefore, using (
4.6
) and the Gibbs variational principle we can bound the energy for any
0<\varepsilon<1
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(H_{N}\Gamma_{0})

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{Tr}(H_{N}\Gamma_{0}+T\,\Gamma_{0}\ln\Gamma_{0})-\big(\varepsilon\mathrm{Tr}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3N}}\Gamma_{0})+(1+\varepsilon)T\,{\rm{Tr}}(\,\Gamma_{0}\ln\Gamma_{0})\big)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)4\pi\rho Na(1+C(\rho a^{3})^{1/3})+(1+\varepsilon)T\log\mathrm{Tr}\big(e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{(1+\varepsilon)T}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3N}})}\big),

(4.7)

In the second term, the free energy of the ideal gas is bounded by

T\log\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathscr{F}^{\leq N}_{+}}\big(e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{(1+\varepsilon)T}\sum_{p\neq 0}p^{2}a^{*}_{p}a_{p}}\big)\leq T\sum_{p\in\ell^{-1}\mathbb{N}^{3}\setminus\{0\}}\log\big(1-e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{(1+\varepsilon)T}p^{2}}\big)\leq C\varepsilon^{-3/2}T^{5/2}\ell^{3},

where
\mathscr{F}^{\leq N}_{+}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{N}(\mathrm{Ran}\,Q)^{\otimes n}\simeq L^{2}(\Lambda^{N})
is the truncated bosonic Fock space of excitations. Using that
T\leq C\rho a(\rho a^{3})^{-\nu}
and
N\geq\rho\ell^{3}(\rho a^{3})^{\alpha}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(H_{N}\Gamma_{0})

\displaystyle\leq 4\pi\rho Na(1+C(\rho a^{3})^{1/3}+C(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{-3/2}\frac{T^{5/2}\ell^{3}}{N\rho a}))

\displaystyle\leq 4\pi\rho Na(1+C(\rho a^{3})^{1/3}+C(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{-3/2}(\rho a^{3})^{1/2-\alpha-5\nu/2}))

\displaystyle\leq 4\pi\rho Na(1+C(\rho a^{3})^{1/3}+C(\rho a^{3})^{(1-2\alpha-5\nu)/5}),

where we optimized in
\varepsilon
to obtain the last inequality. The condition that
\alpha+5\nu/2<6/17
leads to the estimate (
4.1
).
∎

Theorem
2.3
follows from Proposition
4.2
below, together with the a priori bounds on
n_{+}
from Lemma
4.1
. The proof of Proposition
4.2
is inspired by the localization of large matrices as in
[
15
]
, and simplified in
[
13
]
. It is also similar to the bounds in
[
19
, Proposition 21]
. It can be interpreted as an analogue of the standard IMS localization formula. It roughly says that, for a lower bound, we can restrict the estimates states
\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}
which have bounded number of low excitations.

Proposition 4.2

(Localization to
\{n_{+}^{L}\leq\mathcal{M}\}
)
.

Let
\Gamma_{0}
be the Gibbs state associated to
H_{N}
. Let
\mathcal{M}\geq N(\rho a^{3})^{\gamma}
, for some
\gamma>0
, then there exists a trace class operator
\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}
on
L^{2}(\Lambda^{N})
with
\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\geq 0
and trace
1
such that

\mathbf{1}_{\{n_{+}^{L}\leq\mathcal{M}\}}\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{1}_{\{n_{+}^{L}\leq\mathcal{M}\}}=\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}},

(4.8)

and

\displaystyle F(\ell,N)

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{Tr}\big(H_{N}\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}+T\,\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\ln\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}})-C\frac{1}{\mathcal{M}^{2}}\left(\mathrm{Tr}(H_{N}\Gamma_{0})+\|v\|_{1}K_{H}^{3}\ell^{-3}N\mathrm{Tr}(n_{+}\Gamma_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-CT\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(n_{+}\Gamma_{0})}{\mathcal{M}}\left(1+\left|\log\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(n_{+}\Gamma_{0})}{\mathcal{M}}\right|\right).

Theorem
2.3
follows from this proposition.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Using the a priori bounds on
n_{+}
from Lemma
4.1
, we have for
\mathcal{M}\geq N(\rho a^{3})^{\gamma}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(n_{+}\Gamma_{0})}{\mathcal{M}}\leq(\rho a^{3})^{1/17-\gamma}K_{\ell}^{2}.

(4.9)

We use Proposition
4.2
, and bound the error terms using (
4.9
) and the upper bound (
4.1
) on the energy of
\Gamma_{0}
,

\displaystyle F(\ell,N)-\mathrm{Tr}\big(H_{N}\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}+T\,\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\ln\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\big)

\displaystyle\geq-C\ell^{-3}\left(a(\rho a^{3})^{-2\gamma}+\|v\|_{1}K_{H}^{3}(\rho a^{3})^{1/17-2\gamma}K_{\ell}^{2}\right)-CT(\rho a^{3})^{1/17-\gamma}K_{\ell}^{2}|\log(\rho a^{3})|

\displaystyle\geq-C\ell^{3}(\rho a)^{5/2}\left((\rho a^{3})^{1/2-2\gamma}K_{\ell}^{-6}+K_{H}^{3}K_{\ell}^{-3}(\rho a^{3})^{1/17-2\gamma}+(\rho a^{3})^{1/17-\gamma-\nu}K_{\ell}^{-1}|\log(\rho a^{3})|\right)

where we used that
\ell=K_{\ell}(\rho a)^{-1/2}
,
T\leq C(\rho a)(\rho a^{3})^{-\nu}
and that
\|v\|_{1}\leq\ell
. For
\rho a^{3}
small enough and using that
K_{\ell}\geq 1
, this is smaller than the claimed error term of Theorem
2.3
.
It remains to extract the spectral gaps. By definition of
H_{N}^{\rm{mod}}
in (
2.20
) we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\big(H_{N}\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\Big(H_{N}^{\rm{mod}}+\frac{\pi}{2\ell^{2}}n_{+}+\frac{K_{H}}{\ell^{2}}n_{+}^{H}\Big)\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\Big(H_{N}^{\rm{mod}}+\frac{\pi}{4\ell^{2}}n_{+}+\frac{K_{H}}{2\ell^{2}}n_{+}^{H}+\frac{\pi n_{+}^{L}n_{+}}{4\mathcal{M}\ell^{2}}+\frac{K_{H}n_{+}^{L}n_{+}^{H}}{2\mathcal{M}\ell^{2}}\Big)\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\big(H_{N}^{\rm{mod}}+G\big)\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\Big).

This concludes the proof of Theorem
2.3
.
∎

4.1
Proof of Proposition
4.2

The rest of this section is dedicated to the proof of Proposition
4.2
. It will follow from the Lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
below, both of which are adapted from
[
13
]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\theta:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
be any compactly supported smooth function such that
\theta(s)=1
for
|s|<\frac{1}{8}
and
\theta(s)=0
for
|s|>\frac{1}{4}
. For any
\mathcal{M}\geq 1
, define
c_{\mathcal{M}}>0
and
\theta_{\mathcal{M}}
such that

\theta_{\mathcal{M}}(s)=c_{\mathcal{M}}\theta\Big(\frac{s}{\mathcal{M}}\Big),\qquad\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\theta_{\mathcal{M}}(s)^{2}=1.

Then there exists a
C>0
depending only on
\theta
such that, for any normalized state
\Gamma
,

\mathrm{Tr}(H_{N}\Gamma)\geq\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Tr}(H_{N}\Gamma_{m})-\frac{C}{\mathcal{M}^{2}}(|\mathrm{Tr}(d_{1}\Gamma)|+|\mathrm{Tr}(d_{2}\Gamma)|)

(4.10)

where
\Gamma_{m}=\theta_{\mathcal{M}}(n_{+}^{L}-m)\Gamma\theta_{\mathcal{M}}(n_{+}^{L}-m)
and

\displaystyle d_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}Q^{L}_{i}(1-Q^{L}_{j})v(x_{i}-x_{j})[Q^{L}_{i}Q^{L}_{j}+(1-Q^{L}_{j})(1-Q^{L}_{j})]+\mathrm{h.c.},

(4.11)

\displaystyle d_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}Q^{L}_{i}Q^{L}_{j}v(x_{i}-x_{j})(1-Q^{L}_{i})(1-Q^{L}_{j})+\mathrm{h.c.}

(4.12)

Proof.

Notice that
H_{N}
only contains terms that change
n_{+}^{L}
by
0,\pm 1
or
\pm 2
. Therefore, we write our operator as
H_{N}=\sum_{|k|\leq 2}\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
,
with
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}n_{+}^{L}=(n_{+}^{L}+k)\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
. Moreover, one easily checks that for
k=1,2
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(k)}+\mathcal{H}^{(-k)}=d_{k}.

For
|k|\leq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{H}^{(k)}\Gamma_{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Tr}(\theta_{\mathcal{M}}(n_{+}^{L}-m)\mathcal{H}^{(k)}\theta_{\mathcal{M}}(n_{+}^{L}-m)\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Tr}(\theta_{\mathcal{M}}(n_{+}^{L}-m)\theta_{\mathcal{M}}(n_{+}^{L}-m+k)\mathcal{H}^{(k)}\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\theta_{\mathcal{M}}(m)\theta_{\mathcal{M}}(m+k)\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{H}^{(k)}\Gamma),

where we used the commutation property of
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
and that the function
\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\theta_{\mathcal{M}}(X-m)\theta_{\mathcal{M}}(X-m+k)
is constant on
\mathbb{Z}
. Using that
\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\theta_{\mathcal{M}}(s)^{2}=1
, we obtain that

We start by noting the following bound. For

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<|p|,|q|<K_{H}}\Big(\int_{\Lambda}v_{y}u_{p}u_{q}\Big)\ket{u_{p}}\bra{u_{q}}\leq C\frac{K_{H}^{3}}{\ell^{3}}\lVert v\rVert_{1}n_{+},

Now we can combine Lemmas

and
4.4
to prove Proposition

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Let
\Gamma_{0}=e^{-H_{N}/T}/\mathrm{Tr}\big(e^{-H_{N}/T}\big)
be the Gibbs state. Lemma
4.3
gives

\mathrm{Tr}({H}_{N}\Gamma_{0})\geq\mathrm{Tr}({H}_{N}\Gamma_{\leq})+\mathrm{Tr}({H}_{N}\Gamma_{>})-\frac{C}{\mathcal{M}^{2}}(|\mathrm{Tr}(d_{1}\Gamma_{0})|+|\mathrm{Tr}(d_{2}\Gamma_{0})|),

(4.15)

where

Denoting by

we have

and from the sub-additivity of the entropy

S(\Gamma_{0})\leq S(\Gamma_{\leq})+S(\Gamma_{>})=\alpha_{\Gamma}S\Big(\frac{\Gamma_{>}}{\alpha_{\Gamma}}\Big)+(1-\alpha_{\Gamma})S\Big(\frac{\Gamma_{\leq}}{1-\alpha_{\Gamma}}\Big)-\alpha_{\Gamma}\log(\alpha_{\Gamma})-(1-\alpha_{\Gamma})\log(1-\alpha_{\Gamma}).

Combining (

\displaystyle F(\ell,N)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}(H_{N}\Gamma_{0})-TS(\Gamma_{0})

\displaystyle\geq(1-\alpha_{\Gamma})\left[\mathrm{Tr}\Big(H_{N}\frac{\Gamma_{\leq}}{1-\alpha_{\Gamma}}\Big)-TS\Big(\frac{\Gamma_{\leq}}{1-\alpha_{\Gamma}}\Big)\right]+\alpha_{\Gamma}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\Big(H_{N}\frac{\Gamma_{>}}{\alpha_{\Gamma}}\Big)-TS\Big(\frac{\Gamma_{>}}{\alpha_{\Gamma}}\Big)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{C}{\mathcal{M}^{2}}(|\mathrm{Tr}(d_{1}\Gamma_{0})|+|\mathrm{Tr}(d_{2}\Gamma_{0})|)+T\alpha_{\Gamma}\log(\alpha_{\Gamma})+T(1-\alpha_{\Gamma})\log(1-\alpha_{\Gamma}).

By the variational principle, the second term above is bigger than

, we obtain

\displaystyle F(\ell,N)

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{Tr}\Big({H}_{N}\frac{\Gamma_{\leq}}{1-\alpha_{\Gamma}}\Big)-TS\Big(\frac{\Gamma_{\leq}}{1-\alpha_{\Gamma}}\Big)-\frac{C}{(1-\alpha_{\Gamma})\mathcal{M}^{2}}(|\mathrm{Tr}(d_{1}\Gamma_{0})|+|\mathrm{Tr}(d_{2}\Gamma_{0})|)

\displaystyle\quad+T\frac{\alpha_{\Gamma}}{1-\alpha_{\Gamma}}\log(\alpha_{\Gamma})+T\log(1-\alpha_{\Gamma}).

Lastly we see that

Defining

and using the bounds in Lemma
4.4

1
on the
N
particle sector) at the expanse of an error of order
\rho a
. This will ensure that we never have too many particles after the c-number substitution via (
6.12
) and will be used to prove Lemma
2.11
.

We follow
[
23
]
and perform the c-number substitution. The decomposition
L^{2}(\Lambda)=\mathrm{Ran}P\oplus\mathrm{Ran}Q
leads to the splitting of the bosonic Fock space
\mathscr{F}(L^{2}(\Lambda))=\mathscr{F}(\mathrm{Ran}P)\otimes\mathscr{F}(\mathrm{Ran}Q)
.
Denoting by
\Omega
the vacuum vector, we introduce the class of coherent states in
\mathscr{F}(\mathrm{Ran}P)
, labeled by
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

|z\rangle=e^{-\big(\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}+za^{\dagger}_{0}\big)}\,\Omega,

(6.7)

which are eigenvectors for the annihilation operator of the condensate. One easily checks that

a_{0}|z\rangle=z\,|z\rangle\;\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\;1=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z\rangle\langle z|\,\mathrm{d}z

(6.8)

where
\mathrm{d}z=\pi^{-1}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y
,
z=x+iy
. For any
\Psi\in\mathscr{F}(L^{2}(\Lambda))
, we denote by
\Phi(z)=\langle z|\Psi\rangle
the partial inner product, which is in
\mathscr{F}(\mathrm{Ran}Q)
.
We write any monomial in
a_{0}
and
a^{*}_{0}
as a polynomial sum of terms in anti-normal order thanks to the commutation rules. Using theses definitions and the inequality
[
23
, Eq.(7)]
we are allowed then to replace
a_{0}
with a complex number (called upper symbol) according to the following substitution rules

\displaystyle a_{0}\mapsto z,\qquad

\displaystyle a_{0}a_{0}\mapsto z^{2},

\displaystyle a_{0}a^{*}_{0}\mapsto|z|^{2},

(6.9)

\displaystyle a_{0}^{*}\mapsto\overline{z},\qquad

\displaystyle a_{0}^{*}a_{0}^{*}\mapsto\overline{z}^{2},

\displaystyle a_{0}a_{0}a^{*}_{0}a^{*}_{0}\mapsto|z|^{4}

which also give the substitutions

a_{0}^{*}a_{0}\mapsto|z|^{2}-1,\qquad a_{0}^{*}a_{0}^{*}a_{0}a_{0}\mapsto|z|^{4}-4|z|^{2}+2

(6.10)

before integrating over
z
in front of the trace in (
6.6
).
For the specific case of a power of the
n_{0}
, we use again the commutation rules to write it as polynomial of anti-normal ordered monomials in
a_{0},a_{0}^{*}
and apply the rules (
6.9
) to define the polynomial in
|z|^{2}

n_{0}^{m}=(a^{*}_{0}a_{0})^{m}\mapsto p_{m}(z)=|z|^{2m}+\text{smaller order terms},

(6.11)

where the smaller order terms have explicit, constant coefficients.

We first bound below the new term using that

\rho a\frac{\mathcal{N}^{m}}{N^{m}}\geq\rho a\frac{n_{0}^{m}+n_{0}^{m-1}n_{+}+n_{0}^{m-2}n_{+}^{2}}{N^{m}}.

(6.12)

The upper symbol of the right hand side is

\frac{\rho a}{N^{m}}\big(p_{m}(z)+p_{m-1}(z)n_{+}+p_{m-2}(z)n_{+}^{2}\big).

(6.13)

We observe that, for
|z|^{2}>N,
(and
N
sufficiently large) the polynomials satisfy the bound
p_{m}(z)\geq\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2m}
and therefore, in this region, (
6.13
) can be bounded from below by

\ \frac{\rho a}{2N^{m}}\big(|z|^{2m}+|z|^{2m-2}n_{+}+|z|^{2m-4}n_{+}^{2}\big).

(6.14)

When
|z|^{2}\leq N
, the error made replacing (
6.13
) by (
6.14
) is of order
N^{-1}\rho a
.
The last term in (
6.4
) becomes

\frac{8\pi a(\rho a^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}}{|\Lambda|}(n_{0}^{2}-N^{2})\mapsto\frac{8\pi a(\rho a^{3})^{\frac{1}{4}}}{|\Lambda|}(|z|^{4}-3|z|^{2}+1-N^{2}).

(6.15)

Using that, for
m>3
,

\displaystyle 3\frac{a}{|\Lambda|}|z|^{2}-2\leq C\frac{a}{|\Lambda|}\left(K_{\ell}N\mathbf{1}_{|z|^{2}\leq K_{\ell}N}+\frac{|z|^{2m}}{N^{m}}NK_{\ell}^{1-m}\mathbf{1}_{|z|^{2}>K_{\ell}N}\right)\leq C\rho a\left(K_{\ell}+\frac{|z|^{2m}}{N^{m}}\right)

(6.16)

we recover the last term of (
2.9
) up to an error
C\rho a
(because from the assumptions
K_{\ell}\leq(\rho a^{3})^{-1/26}
) and a fraction of the gap
\mathcal{G}(z)
.

When proceeding to the c-number substitution in the other terms of the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{sym}}_{\mu}
we obtain errors from the terms proportional to
a^{*}_{0}a_{0}
and
a^{*}_{0}a^{*}_{0}a_{0}a_{0}
, which are

\mathcal{E}=\widehat{g\omega}(0)\frac{2-4|z|^{2}}{2|\Lambda|}-\sum_{p\in{\Lambda}^{*}_{+}}\frac{\widehat{g}(p)+\widehat{g\omega}(0)+\widehat{g\omega}(p)}{|\Lambda|}a^{*}_{p}a_{p}-\mu(n_{+}-1).

(6.17)

Note that
|\widehat{g\omega}(p)|+|\widehat{g}(p)|\leq Ca
. Therefore, we can estimate the first term above as in (
6.16
). The second and third terms are estimated by by
C(a|\Lambda|^{-1}+\mu)n_{+}\leq\ell^{-2}n_{+}
which is absorbed by a fraction of the gap (recall that
0\leq 10\mu\leq\ell^{-2}
by assumption). In the end, we recover
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}(z)
as well as
\mathcal{G}(z)
and the inequality stated in Theorem
2.9
.
∎

where we used (

Then the first term of (

∎

In this section we prove the bounds on the 3Q terms stated in Lemma

.
We start with Lemma
2.4
, which estimates the error made by replacing

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

From the definitions we have

Q_{3}^{\text{ren}}-Q_{3,L}=\sum_{i\neq j}(P_{i}Q^{H}_{j}g(x_{i}-x_{j})Q_{j}Q_{i}+\mathrm{h.c.}).

(7.1)

In the right-hand side we aim at reconstructing
Q_{4}^{\rm{ren}}
as

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j}(P_{i}Q^{H}_{j}gQ_{j}Q_{i}+\mathrm{h.c.})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}\big(P_{i}Q^{H}_{j}g\,\Pi_{ij}+\mathrm{h.c.}\big)-\sum_{i\neq j}P_{i}Q^{H}_{j}g\omega(P_{j}P_{i}+P_{j}Q_{i}+Q_{j}P_{i})+\mathrm{h.c.}

(7.2)

We use a weighted Cauchy-Schwarz inequality on both terms. Using that
g\leq v
, the first term in (
7.2
) is controlled by

\displaystyle C\delta^{-1}\sum_{i\neq j}P_{i}Q^{H}_{j}gQ^{H}_{j}P_{i}+\delta Q_{4}^{\text{ren}}

\displaystyle=C\delta^{-1}\widehat{g}(0)\frac{n_{0}n_{+}^{H}}{|\Lambda|}+\delta Q_{4}^{\text{ren}}

for all
\delta>0
. The other terms can be estimated similarly to above. For instance,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j}(P_{i}Q^{H}_{j}g\omega Q_{j}P_{i}+\mathrm{h.c.})

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{-1}\sum_{i\neq j}P_{i}Q^{H}_{j}g\omega Q^{H}_{j}P_{i}+\delta\sum_{i\neq j}P_{i}Q_{j}g\omega Q_{j}P_{i}

\displaystyle\leq C\widehat{g}(0)\frac{n_{0}}{|\Lambda|}\big(\delta^{-1}n_{+}^{H}+\delta n_{+}\big),

(7.3)

where we used
\widehat{g\omega}(0)\leq\widehat{g}(0)
.
We collect the previous inequalities to obtain

|\langle Q_{3}^{\rm{ren}}\rangle_{\Psi}-\langle Q_{3,L}\rangle_{\Psi}|\leq\delta\langle Q_{4}^{\rm{ren}}\rangle_{\Psi}+C\rho\widehat{g}(0)\big(\delta\langle n_{+}\rangle_{\Psi}+\delta^{-1}\langle n_{+}^{H}\rangle_{\Psi}\big),

(7.4)

where we bounded
n_{0}\leq N
. Choosing

, the two last terms are bounded by spectral gaps if
K_{H}\geq CK_{\ell}^{4}
.
∎

Then
Q_{3,L}
was symmetrized in Theorem

, defined in (
2.37
). The following Lemma shows that in

Lemma 7.1
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
C>0
such that the following holds. Let
v
be a positive, radially symmetric potential with scattering length

and assume
\rho a^{3}\leq C^{-1}
. Then for all

\mathcal{M}\leq C^{-1}\rho\ell^{3}K_{H}^{-3}K_{\ell}^{-17/4}

Q_{3,L}^{\rm{sym}}(z)-Q_{3}^{\rm{soft}}(z)\geq-\varepsilon\mathcal{G}(z).

(7.5)

where

Q_{3}^{\rm{soft}}(z)=\frac{1}{\lvert\Lambda\rvert}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{L}^{\mathbb{Z}},k\in\mathcal{P}_{H}}c(p,k)\widehat{g}(k)\overline{z}a^{*}_{p}a_{p-k}a_{k}+\mathrm{h.c.},

(7.6)

and the spectral gaps
\mathcal{G}(z)
are defined in (

Proof.

Recalling the definition of
Q_{3,L}^{\rm{sym}}(z)
in (
2.37

\mathcal{P}_{L}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\{p\in\frac{\pi}{\ell}\mathbb{Z}^{3},\;\;0<|p|\leq\frac{K_{H}}{\ell}\}

Q_{3,L}^{\rm{sym}}(z)-Q_{3}^{\rm{soft}}(z)=\frac{1}{|\Lambda|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}_{L}^{\mathbb{Z}},k\in\mathcal{P}_{L}\\
p\neq k\end{subarray}}c(p,k)\widehat{g}(k)\big(\overline{z}a_{p}^{*}a_{p-k}a_{k}+\mathrm{h.c.}\big).

(7.7)

We use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with weight
\delta>0
and deduce

\displaystyle Q_{3,L}^{\rm{sym}}(z)-Q_{3}^{\rm{soft}}(z)\geq-C\frac{\widehat{g}(0)}{|\Lambda|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}_{L}^{\mathbb{Z}},k\in\mathcal{P}_{L}\\
p\neq k\end{subarray}}\big(\delta|z|^{2}a_{p}^{*}a_{p}+\delta^{-1}a_{k}^{*}a_{p-k}^{*}a_{p-k}a_{k}\big),

(7.8)

where we bounded the

Recall that the commutation relation

only holds when
p
and

\pi\ell^{-1}\mathbb{N}^{3}_{0}
. To deal with the negative components that can appear due to the set where

belongs, we use that
a_{p}=a_{(|p_{1}|,|p_{2}|,|p_{3}|)}
. In particular, note that

\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{L}^{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{p}^{*}a_{p}\leq 8\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{L}}a_{p}^{*}a_{p},

(7.9)

and therefore the first term of (

and a cardinal of

. Similarly in the second term, we bound the

and the

. Choosing

\displaystyle Q_{3,L}^{\rm{sym}}(z)-Q_{3}^{\rm{soft}}(z)

\displaystyle\geq-C\delta\rho\widehat{g}(0)K_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{4}}K_{H}^{3}n_{+}-C\delta^{-1}\rho\widehat{g}(0)\frac{n_{+}n_{+}^{L}}{\rho\ell^{3}}

(7.10)

\displaystyle\geq-\varepsilon\frac{n_{+}}{\ell^{2}}-C\frac{K_{H}^{3}K_{\ell}^{5}}{\varepsilon\rho\ell^{3}}\frac{n_{+}n_{+}^{L}}{\ell^{2}}.

(7.11)

These errors can be absorbed in spectral gaps if
\mathcal{M}\leq C^{-1}\rho\ell^{3}K_{H}^{-3}K_{\ell}^{-17/4}
.
∎

In order to prove Theorem
2.12
, we need the following approximations of
\widehat{g\omega}(0)
.

Lemma 7.2
.

The following estimates hold.

1.

For all
\Psi\in\mathscr{F}^{\perp}
,

\Big|\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}\widehat{g\omega}(p)\langle a_{p}^{*}a_{p}\rangle_{\Psi}-\widehat{g\omega}(0)\langle n_{+}\rangle_{\Psi}\Big|\leq C\widehat{g}(0)\langle n_{+}^{H}\rangle_{\Psi}+C\widehat{g}(0)K_{H}^{2}R^{2}\ell^{-2}\langle n_{+}\rangle_{\Psi}.

2.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\Big|\widehat{g\omega}(0)-\frac{1}{8|\Lambda|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\frac{\pi}{\ell}\mathbb{Z}^{3}\setminus\{0\}\end{subarray}}\frac{\widehat{g}(k)^{2}}{2k^{2}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq Ca^{2}\ell^{-1}

(7.12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Lambda|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{P}_{L}^{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{\widehat{g}(k)^{2}}{2k^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq CK_{H}a^{2}\ell^{-1},

(7.13)

Proof.

1. For the first estimate we split the sum into high and low momenta,

\sum_{p\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}\widehat{g\omega}(p)a_{p}^{*}a_{p}=:\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{P}_{L})+\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{P}_{H}).

(7.14)

The high momenta part is controlled by
n_{+}^{H}
,

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{P}_{H})=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{H}}\widehat{g\omega}(p)a_{p}^{*}a_{p}\leq\widehat{g}(0)n_{+}^{H},

(7.15)

where we used
\widehat{g\omega}(0)\leq\widehat{g}(0)
. For low momenta, we use a Taylor approximation
\widehat{g\omega}(p)\simeq\widehat{g\omega}(0)+\mathcal{O}(p^{2}\widehat{g}(0)R^{2})
, which follows using
\widehat{g\omega}^{\prime}(0)=0
(by radiality) and
|\widehat{g\omega}^{\prime\prime}(p)|\leq\widehat{g}(0)R^{2}
(since
\mathrm{supp}(g)\subset B(0,R)

\big|\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{P}_{L})-\widehat{g\omega}(0)n_{+}^{L}\big|\leq C\widehat{g}(0)R^{2}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{L}}|p|^{2}a_{p}^{*}a_{p}\leq C\widehat{g}(0)K_{H}^{2}R^{2}\ell^{-2}n_{+}.

(7.16)

Finally, the remaining
\widehat{g\omega}(0)n_{+}^{H}
is controlled by
\widehat{g}(0)n_{+}^{H}
.

2. To prove the second estimate, we introduce a cutoff version of the scattering function. Let
\chi
be a smooth and radial function so that
0\leq\chi\leq 1
and
\chi(x)=1
for

\chi(x)=0
for

\displaystyle-\Delta\omega_{c}=\frac{1}{2}g-\frac{a}{\ell^{3}}\left(\frac{\chi^{\prime\prime}}{|\cdot|}\right)(x\ell^{-1}).

Denoting

\displaystyle p^{2}\widehat{\omega}_{c}(p)=\frac{1}{2}\widehat{g}(p)-a\widehat{U}(\ell p).

Using that

\displaystyle\left|\int g\omega_{c}-\frac{1}{8\ell^{3}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\frac{\pi}{\ell}\mathbb{Z}^{3}\setminus\{0\}\end{subarray}}\frac{\widehat{g}(k)^{2}}{2|k|^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{8\ell^{3}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\frac{\pi}{\ell}\mathbb{Z}^{3}\setminus\{0\}\end{subarray}}a\left|\frac{\widehat{g}(k)\widehat{U}(k\ell)}{|k|^{2}}\right|\leq Ca^{2}\ell^{-1},

where we used that
\|\widehat{g}\|_{\infty}\leq Ca
.
This proves (

and the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{L}^{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Now we can prove Theorem
2.12
, combining

with the remaining quadratic part of the Hamiltonian after the Bogoliubov diagonalization. This process also uses a fraction of the diagonalized Hamiltonian.

Proof of Theorem
2.12
.

First of all, we can use Lemma

by

defined in (

\displaystyle Q_{3}^{\mathrm{soft}}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Lambda|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathcal{P}_{H}\\

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{T}_{1}-\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Lambda|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathcal{P}_{H}\\

\mathcal{M}
and

\mathcal{T}_{1}
. Using the remaining fraction of the diagonalized Hamiltonian, we complete a square to find

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}+(1-2K_{H}^{-1})\sum_{k\in\mathcal{P}_{H}}D_{k}b_{k}^{*}b_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{P}_{H}}(1-2K_{H}^{-1})D_{k}(b_{k}+A_{k})^{*}(b_{k}+A_{k})-\sum_{k\in\mathcal{P}_{H}}(1-2K_{H}^{-1})D_{k}A_{k}^{*}A_{k},

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}G(w_{p}(\rho))=\frac{8\pi aT^{-1}p^{2}}{w_{p}(\rho)^{2}}\frac{w_{p}(\rho)}{e^{w_{p}(\rho)}-1}\leq Ce^{-p^{2}}

and that

\displaystyle 0\geq\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\rho^{2}}G(w_{p}(\rho))

\displaystyle=-\frac{\left(8\pi aT^{-1}p^{2}\right)^{2}}{w_{p}(\rho)^{4}}\frac{w_{p}^{2}(\rho)}{(e^{w_{p}(\rho)}-1)^{2}}\left(e^{w_{p}(\rho)}+\frac{e^{w_{p}(\rho)}-1}{w_{p}(\rho)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq-Ce^{-w_{p}(\rho)}(w_{p}(\rho)+1)^{2}\geq-Ce^{-p^{2}/2}(p^{2}+1)^{2},

where we used that
w_{p}(\rho)^{2}\geq 16\pi aT^{-1}p^{2}
, that
(e^{x}-1)/x\leq e^{x}
and that
x/(e^{x}-1))\leq Ce^{-x}(x+1)
. The result follows by estimating the associated Riemann sum.
∎

Slopes and Moreau-Rockafellar Theorem
M. Ivanov, N. Zlateva

Abstract
Properties of local and global slope of a function and its approximate critical points sets are studied in relation to determination of the function. 2020 MSC : 49J52, 47J22 Key words : slope, determination of a function



Introduction

The slopes have been studied in relation to gradient flows, e.g.
[
1
,
10
]
, but they are well defined where the differential inclusions are problematic,
as for example in general metric spaces lacking any linear-like structure. The evolution of the concept is excellently explained in the monograph
[
14
]
,
see also
[
12
]
. For a state-of-the-art account see
[
4
]
.
Many striking and surprising results have been obtained to show that in many cases slope alone determines the function, see e.g.
[
22
,
28
,
4
]
. One drawback of this very active and recent development is the use of transfinite induction. Here we remove any such reference and in the process develop the topic a bit further.

Why it is important to avoid the use of transfinite induction we explain briefly in the Appendix.

In this article we consider results like: let
f
and
g
be continuous functions on a complete metric space and let the local slope of
f
be everywhere bigger than that of
g
.
The case when
f
and
g
are differentiable on a Banach space with
\|f^{\prime}(x)\|>\|g^{\prime}(x)\|
whenever
f^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
, is interesting enough.
Then under certain conditions the infimum of
f-g
is attained on the set of approximately critical points of
f
.

Our approach, based on an idea from
[
3
]
, is to consider for an arbitrary fixed
x_{0}
the minimisation problem

\begin{cases}f(x)\to\min\\
f(x)-g(x)\leq f(x_{0})-g(x_{0}),\end{cases}

and observe that – because of the slopes condition – any (approximately) critical point cannot be on the boundary of the feasible set, so it is actually a critical point of
f
.

Even more can be said in the case of global slopes, where we can estimate the infimum of
f-g
through the infima of
f
and
g
. This fully primal result almost immediately yields the celebrated Moreau-Rockafellar Theorem
[
20
,
24
]
.

The first version of this work has been completed before the appearance of the recent preprint
[
4
]
.
There a new general result is obtained via transfinite induction. Here we cover a variant of this result, see Theorem
2
.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
1
we give the necessary definitions. Section
2
is devoted to the properties of the slopes.
Section
3
contains the main results. In Section
4
we give a proof to Moreau-Rockafellar Theorem using slopes.

1
Definitions

Here we define some concepts and notations we will be using throughout the paper.

We consider functions defined on a metric space
(M,\rho)
with values in
\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
.
Although it is somewhat standard in such setting to adopt the convention
\infty-\infty=\infty
, we find this approach prone to errors, thus we avoid it. So, ”
\infty-\infty
” is undefined.
Therefore, if nothing more is specified, formula like for example
\{x:\ f(x)-g(x)\leq\lambda\}
means those
x
for which at least one of
f(x)
and
g(x)
is finite, and

f(x)-g(x)\leq\lambda
. This said, we usually take care to write more explicitly, so for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
we would rather write the equivalent formulae

\{x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f:\ f(x)-g(x)\leq\lambda\}
where, as usual, for
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, the domain of
f
is

\mathrm{dom}\,f:=\{x:\ f(x)<\infty\}.

We denote the sub-level set of
f
below the level
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
by

L_{\lambda}(f):=\{x:\ f(x)\leq\lambda\}.

Following the convention above,

L_{\lambda}(f-g):=\{x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f:\ f(x)-g(x)\leq\lambda\}.

For
f
which is proper, i.e. such that
\mathrm{dom}\,f\neq\varnothing
, and bounded below; and
\varepsilon\geq 0
, we denote

\varepsilon\argmin f:=\{x:f(x)\leq\inf f+\varepsilon\},

hence,
\varepsilon\argmin f=L_{(\inf f+\varepsilon)}(f)
.

If
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
then the
local slope
of
f
at
x
is defined as

|\nabla f|(x):=\limsup_{y\to x\atop y\neq x}\frac{[f(x)-f(y)]^{+}}{\rho(x,y)}.

Here
[t]^{+}:=\max\{0,t\}=(t+|t|)/2
, so
|\nabla f|(x)=0
if
x
is a local minimum to
f
, and if not

|\nabla f|(x)=\limsup_{y\to x\atop y\neq x}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{\rho(x,y)}.

In the latter statement we explicit that
|\nabla f|(x)=0
at the isolated points of
\mathrm{dom}\,f
.
This notion was introduced by De Giorgi, Marino and Tosques
[
10
]
and used thereafter for the study of concepts as descent curves (e.g.
[
12
]
), error bounds (e.g.
[
2
]
), subdifferential calculus and metric regularity (e.g. the monograph
[
14
]
and references therein).

We may consider
|\nabla f|
as a function from
\mathrm{dom}\,f
to
[0,\infty]
. Moreover,

|\nabla(rf)|(x)=r|\nabla f|(x),\quad\forall r\geq 0,\ \forall x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f.

(1)

Because
[a+b]^{+}\leq[a]^{+}+[b]^{+}
we have

|\nabla(f+g)|\leq|\nabla f|+|\nabla g|,

(2)

which for
g:M\to\mathbb{R}
implies

|\nabla(f-g)|\geq|\nabla f|-|\nabla g|,\quad\forall x\in\mathrm{dom}\,|\nabla g|.

(3)

Let us denote

\varepsilon\crit f:=\{x:\ |\nabla f|(x)\leq\varepsilon\}.

In other words,
\varepsilon\crit f=L_{\varepsilon}(|\nabla f|)
. If the space
M
is complete and the function
f
is lower semicontinuous and bounded below, then Ekeland Variational Principle gives that
\inf|\nabla f|=0
, thus
\varepsilon\crit f=\varepsilon\argmin|\nabla f|
(see Proposition
1
below).

The
global slope
of
f
at
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
is defined as

|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x):=\sup_{y\neq x}\frac{[f(x)-f(y)]^{+}}{\rho(x,y)},

and we can consider
|\widetilde{\nabla}f|
as a function from
\mathrm{dom}\,f
to
[0,\infty]
. This notion is introduced by Thibault and Zagrodny in
[
28
]
.

Obviously,
|\widetilde{\nabla}f|\geq|\nabla f|
on
\mathrm{dom}\,f
. It is also clear that the subadditivity (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
),
holds for the global slope
|\widetilde{\nabla}\cdot|
as well.

By analogy we define

\varepsilon\Crit f:=L_{\varepsilon}(|\widetilde{\nabla}f|).

It is immediate that

\varepsilon\Crit f=\{x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f:\ f(y)\geq f(x)-\varepsilon\rho(y,x),\ \forall y\}.

2
Properties of the slopes

In this section we consider some properties of the local and the global slope of a given function.

Lemma 1
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a metric space and let
a\in M
be fixed. Set

\varphi(x):=-\log\rho(x,a).

Then

|\widetilde{\nabla}\varphi|(x)\leq\frac{1}{\rho(x,a)},\quad\forall x\neq a.

(4)

Proof.

Fix
x\neq a
and let
y\neq a
be such that
\varphi(y)<\varphi(x)
, that is,
\rho(y,a)>\rho(x,a)
. Using the inequality

\log(t+s)\leq\log t+\frac{s}{t},\quad\forall t,s>0,

due to the concavity of the logarithm, we get

\log\rho(y,a)\leq\log(\rho(x,a)+\rho(x,y))\leq\log\rho(x,a)+\rho(x,y)/\rho(x,a).

Therefore,
\varphi(y)\geq\varphi(x)-\rho(x,y)/\rho(x,a)
, that is,

\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{\rho(x,y)}\leq\frac{1}{\rho(x,a)}.

As
y
with
\varphi(y)<\varphi(x)
was arbitrary, we get (
4
).
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a metric space. Let
g:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper function. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Consider the function

g_{1}(x):=\min\{g(x),\lambda\},\quad\forall x\in M.

Then

|\nabla g_{1}|(x)\leq|\nabla g|(x)\text{ and }|\widetilde{\nabla}g_{1}|(x)\leq|\widetilde{\nabla}g|(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathrm{dom}\,g.

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,g
.

It is enough to show that

[g_{1}(x)-g_{1}(y)]^{+}\leq[g(x)-g(y)]^{+},\quad\forall y\in M,

(5)

because then we can just take
\limsup
or
\sup
.

Case 1:

g(x)\leq\lambda
.

Then
g_{1}(x)=g(x)
and so
[g_{1}(x)-g_{1}(y)]^{+}=[g(x)-g_{1}(y)]^{+}
for any
y\in M
.

If
y
is such that
g_{1}(y)\geq g(x)
then also
g(y)\geq g(x)
and
[g(x)-g_{1}(y)]^{+}=[g(x)-g(y)]^{+}=0
.

If, on the other hand,
y
is such that
g_{1}(y)<g(x)
then, of course,
g_{1}(y)=g(y)
. So,

[g(x)-g_{1}(y)]^{+}=[g(x)-g(y)]^{+}
.

We see that in this case (
5
) holds even with equality.

Case 2:

g(x)>\lambda
.

Then
g_{1}(x)=\lambda
and so
[g_{1}(x)-g_{1}(y)]^{+}=[\lambda-g_{1}(y)]^{+}
.

If
y
is such that
g(y)\geq\lambda
then by definition
g_{1}(y)=\lambda
and
[g_{1}(x)-g_{1}(y)]^{+}=0\leq[g(x)-g(y)]^{+}
.

If
y
is such that
g(y)<\lambda
then
g_{1}(y)=g(y)
and
[g_{1}(x)-g_{1}(y)]^{+}=[\lambda-g(y)]^{+}
. Since
t\to[t]^{+}
is a non-decreasing function,
[\lambda-g(y)]^{+}\leq[g(x)-g(y)]^{+}
.

So, (
5
) holds.
∎

Note that
\varepsilon\Crit f
is the set of those points where the Hausdorff regularisation of
f
coincides with
f
:

f(x)=f_{1/\varepsilon}(x):=\inf\{f(y)+\varepsilon\rho(y,x)\}.

As the Hausdorff regularisation is Lipschitz, clearly a lower semicontinuous
f
is Lipschitz on the closed set
\varepsilon\Crit f
, but this can also be proved directly:

Lemma 3
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper and lower semicontinuous function on a metric space
(M,\rho)
. Then
\varepsilon\Crit f
is a closed set on which
f
is
\varepsilon
-Lipschitz.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and a convergent sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\varepsilon\Crit f
. Let

\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=\bar{x}.

By definition

f(\bar{x})\geq f(x_{n})-\varepsilon\rho(\bar{x},x_{n}),

which, after taking a limit, gives

f(\bar{x})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}f(x_{n}).

But
f
is given to be lower semicontinuous at
\bar{x}
, so

\lim_{n\to\infty}f(x_{n})=f(\bar{x}).

Let
y\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
be arbitrary. By definition,

f(y)\geq f(x_{n})-\varepsilon\rho(y,x_{n}),

so

f(y)\geq f(\bar{x})-\varepsilon\rho(y,\bar{x}).

In particular
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
and, therefore,
\bar{x}\in\varepsilon\Crit f
.

We have proved that
\varepsilon\Crit f
is closed.

Let
x,y\in\varepsilon\Crit f
. By definition,
f(y)-f(x)\geq-\varepsilon\rho(y,x)
and
f(x)-f(y)\geq-\varepsilon\rho(x,y)
, so
|f(y)-f(x)|\leq\varepsilon\rho(y,x)
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
f,g:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and let for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,

L_{\lambda}(f-g)\cap\mathrm{dom}\,f\cap\mathrm{dom}\,g\neq\varnothing.

(6)

Let
M_{1}:=L_{\lambda}(f-g)
, and

f_{1}:=f\restriction_{M_{1}}.

If
x\in M_{1}
and

|\nabla f|(x)>|\nabla g|(x),

(7)

then

|\nabla f_{1}|(x)=|\nabla f|(x).

Proof.

Take
x\in M_{1}
satisfying (
7
). Since
|\nabla f|(x)>0
, by definition there is a sequence
y_{n}\to x
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f(x)-f(y_{n})}{\rho(x,y_{n})}=|\nabla f|(x).

(8)

Let
r\in\mathbb{R}
be such that

|\nabla f|(x)>r>|\nabla g|(x).

By definition,

\frac{g(x)-g(y_{n})}{\rho(x,y_{n})}<r

for all
n
large enough. By (
8
)

\frac{f(x)-f(y_{n})}{\rho(x,y_{n})}>r

for all
n
large enough. So,

\frac{g(x)-g(y_{n})}{\rho(x,y_{n})}<r<\frac{f(x)-f(y_{n})}{\rho(x,y_{n})},\quad\forall n>K.

This implies

f(y_{n})-g(y_{n})<f(x)-g(x)\leq\lambda,\quad\forall n>K.

That is,
y_{n}\in M_{1}
for all
n>K
. Then (
8
) becomes

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f_{1}(x)-f_{1}(y_{n})}{\rho(x,y_{n})}=|\nabla f|(x),

meaning that
|\nabla f_{1}|(x)\geq|\nabla f|(x)
, while the inverse inequality is trivial.
∎

Of course, Lemma
4
has a variant for global slopes with a shorter proof. We give the details for completeness.

Lemma 5
.

Let
f,g:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and let (
6
) be satisfied for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
M_{1}:=L_{\lambda}(f-g)
, and let

f_{1}:=f\restriction_{M_{1}}.

If
x\in M_{1}
and

|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x)>|\widetilde{\nabla}g|(x),

(9)

then

|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|(x)=|\widetilde{\nabla}f(x)|.

Proof.

Fix
x\in M_{1}
such that (
9
) holds. Take any
r>0
such that

|\widetilde{\nabla}g|(x)<r<|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x).

By definition, there is
y\in M
such that
f(x)-f(y)>r\rho(x,y)
. On the other hand,
g(x)-g(\cdot)\leq r\rho(x,\cdot)
, so, in particular,
g(x)-g(y)\leq r\rho(x,y)
. Thus,
f(x)-f(y)>g(x)-g(y)
and
f(y)-g(y)<f(x)-g(x)\leq\lambda
meaning that
y\in M_{1}
and so
f_{1}(x)-f_{1}(y)>r\rho(x,y)\Rightarrow|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|(x)>r
.

Therefore,
|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|(x)\geq|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x)
.
∎

Let us recall the famous Ekeland Variational Principle, see e.g.
[
23
, p. 45]
. Let
(M,\rho)
be a complete metric space and let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below function. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
x_{0}\in\varepsilon\argmin f
. Then for every
\lambda>0
there exists
x_{\lambda}
such that

(i)

\lambda\rho(x_{0},x_{\lambda})\leq f(x_{0})-f(x_{\lambda})
;

(ii)

\lambda\rho(x,x_{\lambda})+f(x)>f(x_{\lambda}),\quad\forall x\neq x_{\lambda}
;

(iii)

\rho(x_{0},x_{\lambda})\leq\varepsilon/\lambda
.

Here we will use a variant of the Ekeland Variational Principle, namely

Proposition 1
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a complete metric space.
Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below function and let
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
. Then for every
\lambda>0
there exists
x_{\lambda}\in\lambda\Crit f
such that

f(x_{\lambda})\leq f(x_{0})-\lambda\rho(x_{0},x_{\lambda}).

Proof.

If
f
attains its minimum at
x_{0}
, then take
x_{\lambda}=x_{0}
. Otherwise, set
\varepsilon:=f(x_{0})-\inf f
and apply the Ekeland Variational Principle at
x_{0}
for
\lambda>0
to get
x_{\lambda}
. Then (ii) means that
x_{\lambda}\in\lambda\Crit f
, while (i) is the claimed inequality.
∎

The graphical density of
\mathrm{dom}\,|\widetilde{\nabla}f|
in
\mathrm{dom}\,f
is pretty standard. We sketch a proof for reader’s convenience.

Lemma 6
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a complete metric space and let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below. Then for each
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
there is a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathrm{dom}\,|\widetilde{\nabla}f|
such that

(x,f(x)):=\lim_{n\to\infty}(x_{n},f(x_{n})).

(10)

Proof.

By subtracting
\inf f
from
f
we may assume that
\inf f=0
.

Let
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
. Let
\lambda_{n}\to\infty
. By Proposition
1
there are
x_{n}\in\lambda_{n}\Crit f\subset\mathrm{dom}\,|\widetilde{\nabla}f|
such that
f(x_{n})\leq f(\bar{x})-\lambda_{n}\rho(x_{n},\bar{x})
. Since
f(x_{n})\geq 0
, we have
\rho(x_{n},\bar{x})\leq f(\bar{x})/\lambda_{n}\to 0
, as
n\to\infty
.

Also,
f(x_{n})\leq f(\bar{x})
, so
\limsup_{n\to\infty}f(x_{n})\leq f(\bar{x})
, but
\liminf_{n\to\infty}f(x_{n})\geq f(\bar{x})
by the lower semicontinuity of
\bar{x}
.
∎

In fact, we will be rather using the following consequence of the graphical density.

Lemma 7
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a complete metric space. Let
f,g:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be lower semicontinuous and let
f
be also proper and bounded below. Then

\inf_{\mathrm{dom}\,f}(f-g)=\inf_{\mathrm{dom}\,|\widetilde{\nabla}f|}(f-g).

(11)

Proof.

Let
r\in\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary such that

r>\inf_{\mathrm{dom}\,f}(f-g).

Let
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
be such that
r>f(\bar{x})-g(\bar{x})
. By Lemma
6
there is a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathrm{dom}\,|\widetilde{\nabla}f|
such that (
10
) is fulfilled for
x=\bar{x}
.

By the lower semicontinuity of
g

g(\bar{x})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}g(x_{n})\Rightarrow-g(\bar{x})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}(-g(x_{n})).

Therefore,
f(\bar{x})-g(\bar{x})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}(f(x_{n})-g(x_{n}))
, that is,
r>f(x_{n})-g(x_{n})
for all large enough
n
.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a complete metric space.
Let
f,g:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and let for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
s>0

L_{\lambda}(f-g)\cap s\Crit f\cap\mathrm{dom}\,g\neq\varnothing.

(12)

Let
M_{1}:=L_{\lambda}(f-g)\cap s\Crit f
, and

f_{1}:=f\restriction_{M_{1}}.

If
x\in M_{1}
and (
9
) holds at
x

then

|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|(x)=|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x).

Proof.

Fix
x\in M_{1}
such that (
9
) holds. Take any
r>0
such that

|\widetilde{\nabla}g|(x)<r<|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x).

In particular, because
x\in s\Crit f
,

r<s.

(13)

By definition, there is
y\in M
such that
f(x)-f(y)>r\rho(x,y)
. By Proposition
1
applied at
y
there is
z\in r\Crit f
such that
f(z)\leq f(y)-r\rho(y,z)
. The triangle inequality gives

f(x)-f(z)>r\rho(x,z).

(14)

Since by definition
g(x)-g(z)<r\rho(x,z)
, as in the proof of Lemma
5
we derive that
z\in L_{\lambda}(f-g)
.

But also
z\in r\Crit f
and (
13
) gives that
z\in s\Crit f
and so, (
14
) becomes

f_{1}(x)-f_{1}(z)>r\rho(x,z),

meaning that
|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|(x)\geq|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x)
.
∎

3
Results

We start with a result of Daniilidis and Salas
[
5
]
. Recently it has been independently proved without transfinite induction in
[
4
]
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a metric space and let
\tau
be some topology on
M
. Assume that
f,g:M\to\mathbb{R}
are
\tau
-continuous and such that

|\nabla f|(x)>|\nabla g|(x),\quad\forall x\not\in 0\crit f.

(15)

If
f
has
\tau
-compact level sets then

\inf(f-g)=\inf_{0\crit f}(f-g).

Proof.

Let
x_{0}
be arbitrary and let
\lambda:=f(x_{0})-g(x_{0})
. Let

M_{1}:=L_{\lambda}(f-g),\quad f_{1}:=f\restriction_{M_{1}}.

It is clear that
M_{1}
is
\tau
-closed and
f_{1}
has
\tau
-compact level sets. Let

\bar{x}\in\mathrm{argmin}\,f_{1},

the latter being non-empty by Weierstrass Theorem.

If
\bar{x}\not\in 0\crit f
, then (
15
) and Lemma
4
give
|\nabla f_{1}|(\bar{x})>0
and, therefore,
\bar{x}
cannot be a minimum of
f_{1}
, contradiction.

Thus
\bar{x}\in 0\crit f
and
(f-g)(\bar{x})\leq\lambda=(f-g)(x_{0})
.
∎

We will develop the above result in cases where completeness replaces compactness. The easiest case to work with is that of Lipschitz function because then the slopes are properly defined everywhere and one does not have to meddle with domains.

Proposition 3
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a complete metric space and let
f,g:M\to\mathbb{R}
be Lipschitz and such that
f
is bounded below and

|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x)\geq|\widetilde{\nabla}g|(x),\quad\forall x\in M.

(16)

Then

\inf(f-g)=\inf_{\varepsilon\Crit f}(f-g),\quad\forall\varepsilon>0.

(17)

Proof.

By adding a constant to both
f
and
g
we may assume that

f\geq 0.

Let
x_{0}\in M
and
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Set

g_{1}(x):=\min\{g(x_{0}),g(x)\}.

Consider

h(x):=(1+\varepsilon)f(x)-g_{1}(x).

From (
1
) and (
2
) it follows that
|\widetilde{\nabla}h|(x)\geq(1+\varepsilon)|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x)-|\widetilde{\nabla}g_{1}|(x)=\varepsilon|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x)+(|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x)-|\widetilde{\nabla}g_{1}|(x))
. From Lemma
2
it follows that
|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x)-|\widetilde{\nabla}g_{1}|(x)\geq 0
and so

|\widetilde{\nabla}h|(x)\geq\varepsilon|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x),\quad\forall x\in M.

Now
h\geq-g(x_{0})
, so from Proposition
1
there is
\bar{x}\in\varepsilon^{2}\Crit h
such that
h(x_{0})\geq h(\bar{x})
. So,

\varepsilon^{2}\geq|\widetilde{\nabla}h|(\bar{x})\geq\varepsilon|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(\bar{x})\Rightarrow\bar{x}\in\varepsilon\Crit f,

and

(1+\varepsilon)f(x_{0})-g(x_{0})=h(x_{0})\geq h(\bar{x})=(1+\varepsilon)f(\bar{x})-g_{1}(\bar{x})\geq f(\bar{x})-g(\bar{x}),

because
f\geq 0
and
g_{1}\leq g
. Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we obtain

f(x_{0})-g(x_{0})\geq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf_{\varepsilon\Crit f}(f-g).

Since
x_{0}
was arbitrary, we get

\inf(f-g)\geq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf_{\varepsilon\Crit f}(f-g),

which is clearly equivalent to (
17
), because
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf_{\varepsilon\Crit f}(f-g)\geq\inf_{\varepsilon\Crit f}(f-g)
for any
\varepsilon>0
and
\inf(f-g)\leq\inf_{\varepsilon\Crit f}(f-g)
, as the infimum over the whole space is less or equal to the infimum over any non-empty subset.
∎

Of course, Proposition
3
has a version – and that with the same proof – for the local slope
|\nabla|
and
\varepsilon\crit
set. The following result in the style of Thibault and Zagrodny
[
28
]
, however, is
|\widetilde{\nabla}|
-specific.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a complete metric space and let
f,g:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper and lower semicontinuous. Let
f
be bounded below and such that

|\widetilde{\nabla}g|(x)\leq|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathrm{dom}\,|\widetilde{\nabla}f|.

(18)

Then

\inf_{\mathrm{dom}\,f}(f-g)\geq\inf f-\inf g.

(19)

Note that, since
\displaystyle\inf_{\mathrm{dom}\,f}(f-g)<\infty
, the inequality (
19
) implies that
\inf g\neq-\infty
. Therefore, (
19
) can be rewritten as in
[
28
]
:

f(x)-\inf f\geq g(x)-\inf g,\quad\forall x\in X.

(20)

Indeed, if
f(x)=\infty
then (
20
) is trivial; otherwise it follows from (
19
).

Proof.

By subtracting
\inf f
from both functions we may assume that

\inf f=0.

(21)

Because of (
11
) we have to show only that

\inf_{\mathrm{dom}\,|\widetilde{\nabla}f|}(f-g)\geq-\inf g.

Assume the contrary, that is, there is
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,|\widetilde{\nabla}f|
such that
f(x_{0})-g(x_{0})<-\inf g
. Note that
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,g
because of (
18
). Let
c>1
be such that

\lambda:=cf(x_{0})-g(x_{0})<-\inf g.

Let
s:=c|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x_{0})
. Set

M_{1}:=L_{\lambda}(cf-g)\cap s\Crit cf,\quad f_{1}:=cf\restriction_{M_{1}},\quad g_{1}:=g\restriction_{M_{1}}.

Obviously,
x_{0}\in M_{1}
. From Lemma
3
we know that
s\Crit cf
is closed and
cf
is Lipschitz on
s\Crit cf
.
Also, (
18
) implies that
s\Crit cf\subset s\Crit f\subset s\Crit g
and thus
g
is also Lipschitz on
s\Crit cf
.
So,
(cf-g)\restriction_{s\Crit cf}
is Lipschitz and thus
L_{\lambda}(cf-g)\cap s\Crit cf
is closed.
That is,
M_{1}
is closed and
f_{1}
and
g_{1}
are Lipschitz.

Since
g(x)\geq cf(x)-\lambda
for
x\in M_{1}
, we have
\displaystyle\inf g_{1}\geq c\inf_{M}f-\lambda=-\lambda>\inf g
. That is, there are
a\in M\setminus M_{1}
and
r>0
such that

g_{1}(x)=g(x)>g(a)+r,\quad\forall x\in M_{1}.

By definition,

|\widetilde{\nabla}g|(x)\geq\frac{r}{\rho(x,a)},\quad\forall x\in M_{1}.

By (
16
) we have

|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x)\geq\frac{r}{\rho(x,a)},\quad\forall x\in M_{1}.

Now, (
18
) implies
|\widetilde{\nabla}(cf)|>|\widetilde{\nabla}g|
on
M_{1}
and Lemma
8
applied to
cf
and
g
gives

|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|(x)=c|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x)
, for all
x\in{M_{1}}
.
Since
c>1
and
|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x)>0
, we get
|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|(x)>|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x)
, for all
x\in{M_{1}}
.
In particular,

|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|(x)>\frac{r}{\rho(x,a)},\quad\forall x\in M_{1}.

(22)

Let
\varphi:M_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as
\varphi(x):=-r\log\rho(x,a)
. Lemma
1
and (
22
) show that

|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|(x)>|\widetilde{\nabla}\varphi|(x),\quad\forall x\in M_{1}.

Consider on
M_{1}

h(x):=2f(x)-\varphi(x).

Since
h(x)\geq r\log\rho(x,a)\geq r\log\mathrm{dist}(a,M_{1})>-\infty
, the function
h
is proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below.

Let
\varepsilon_{n}\searrow 0
. By Proposition
1
applied to
h
at
x_{0}
, there are
x_{n}\in\varepsilon_{n}\Crit h
such that

h(x_{n})\leq h(x_{0}),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
h(x_{n})\geq r\log\rho(x_{n},a)
this means that the sequence
\{\rho(x_{n},a)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is bounded.

On the other hand, however, (
3
) and
|\widetilde{\nabla}f|>|\widetilde{\nabla}\varphi|
show that
|\widetilde{\nabla}h|>|\widetilde{\nabla}f|
, so (
22
) gives
\rho(x,a)>r/\varepsilon_{n}\to\infty
, as
n\to\infty
, contradiction.
∎

Our next aim is to give more precise localisation.

Proposition 4
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a complete metric space and let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below function. Let
g:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function such that

|\nabla f|(x)>|\nabla g|(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f\setminus 0\crit f.

(23)

Then for each
\varepsilon>0

f-g\geq\inf_{\varepsilon\crit f}(f-g),

or, more precisely, for each
x_{0}\in M
there exists
x\in\varepsilon\crit f
such that

f(x)\leq f(x_{0})-\varepsilon\rho(x,x_{0}),

(24)

and

f(x_{0})-g(x_{0})\geq f(x)-g(x).

(25)

Proof.

The claim is clear if
f(x_{0})=\infty
, so let
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
. Let
\lambda:=f(x_{0})-g(x_{0})
and

M_{1}:=L_{\lambda}(f-g),\quad f_{1}:=f\restriction_{M_{1}}.

By Proposition
1
applied to
f_{1}
on
M_{1}
there exists
x\in M_{1}
, that is, satisfying (
25
); such that (
24
) is satisfied and
x\in\varepsilon\Crit f_{1}
.

If
|\nabla f|(x)=0
we are done, because
x\in\varepsilon\crit f
.

If
|\nabla f|(x)>0
we apply Lemma
4
to get
|\nabla f|(x)=|\nabla f_{1}|(x)
. Since
x\in\varepsilon\Crit f_{1}
it follows that
|\nabla f_{1}|(x)\leq\varepsilon
, so
|\nabla f|(x)\leq\varepsilon
and we are done.
∎

The statement below is in the style of
[
4
]
, but there an abstract approach is used, so it would be interesting to check how far our approach here goes.
The latter, however, is outside the scope of this article.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a complete metric space and let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below function. Let
g:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function such that (
23
) holds.

For any
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
at least one of the following two assertions holds:

(a) There exists
\overline{x}\in 0\crit f
such that

f(x_{0})-g(x_{0})\geq f(\overline{x})-g(\overline{x});

(b) For every sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\searrow 0
there exists a sequence
x_{n}\in\varepsilon_{n}\crit f
which has no convergent subsequences, and satisfies

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{n}\rho(x_{n},x_{n-1})<\infty,

(26)

and

f(x_{n})-g(x_{n})\geq f(x_{n+1})-g(x_{n+1}),\quad\forall n\in\{0,\mathbb{N}\}.

(27)

Note that (
26
) implies

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\nabla f|(x_{n})\rho(x_{n},x_{n-1})<\infty,

which is the form used in
[
4
]
.

Proof.

Assume first that (a) is false.

Fix a sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\searrow 0
and proceed by induction.

Basic step:
Set
\lambda_{0}:=f(x_{0})-g(x_{0})
,
M_{0}:=L_{\lambda_{0}}(f-g)
,
f_{0}:=f\restriction_{M_{0}}
and take
x_{1}\in\varepsilon_{1}\Crit f_{0}
such that

f(x_{1})\leq f(x_{0})-\varepsilon_{1}\rho(x_{1},x_{0})

and

f(x_{0})-g(x_{0})\geq f(x_{1})-g(x_{1}).

The existence of such
x_{1}
follows from Proposition
4
.

Induction step:
Given
x_{n}\in\varepsilon_{n}\Crit f_{n-1}
, set
\lambda_{n}:=f(x_{n})-g(x_{n})
,
M_{n}:=L_{\lambda_{n}}(f-g)
,
f_{n}:=f\restriction_{M_{n}}
and take
x_{n}\in\varepsilon_{n+1}\Crit f_{n}
such that

f(x_{n+1})\leq f(x_{n})-\varepsilon_{n+1}\rho(x_{n+1},x_{n}),

(28)

and

f(x_{n})-g(x_{n})\geq f(x_{n+1})-g(x_{n+1}).

The existence of
x_{n+1}
follows again from Proposition
4
.

We claim that the sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
has no convergent subsequence. To check this, assume the contrary.
Because
M_{n+1}\subset M_{n}
, any subsequence of the sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
has the same structure, we may assume without loss of generality that

\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
itself converges. Let
x_{n}\to\overline{x}
.

Since

f(x_{0})-g(x_{0})\geq f(x_{n})-g(x_{n}),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

by continuity of
g
at
\overline{x}
and by lower semicontinuity of
f
at
\overline{x}
, after passing to limit, we obtain that

f(x_{0})-g(x_{0})\geq f(\overline{x})-g(\overline{x}).

Since (a) is assumed false,
\overline{x}\not\in 0\crit f
, and therefore

|\nabla f|(\overline{x})>|\nabla g|(\overline{x}).

Now set
\overline{\lambda}:=f(\overline{x})-g(\overline{x})
,
\overline{M}:=L_{\overline{\lambda}}(f-g)
,
\overline{f}:=f\restriction_{\overline{M}}
.

On the one hand, from Lemma
4
it follows that
|\nabla\overline{f}|(\overline{x})=|\nabla f|(\overline{x})
and the above inequality yields
|\nabla\overline{f}|(\overline{x})>0
.

On the other hand,
\overline{M}\subset M_{n}
and
\overline{f}=f_{n}\restriction_{\overline{M}}
for all
n
. Fix
y\in\overline{M}
. Since
x_{n}\in\varepsilon_{n}\Crit f_{n-1}
we have that

f(x_{n})-f(y)\leq\varepsilon_{n}\rho(x_{n},y),

and passing to limit yields
f(\overline{x})-f(y)\leq 0
. Hence,
\overline{x}\in 0\Crit\overline{f}
, and in particular
|\nabla\overline{f}|(\overline{x})=0
, which is a contradiction.

Therefore,
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
has no convergent subsequences, (
27
) is fulfilled by construction, while for (
26
) note that by (
28
) we have

\varepsilon_{n+1}\rho(x_{n+1},x_{n})\leq f(x_{n})-f(x_{n+1}),\quad\forall n\in\{0,\mathbb{N}\},

so

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{n+1}\rho(x_{n+1},x_{n})\leq f(x_{0})-\inf f.

The proof is then complete.
∎

Not surprisingly, Proposition
4
has a compete analogue for the global slope.

Proposition 5
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a complete metric space and let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below function. Let
g:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function such that

|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x)>|\widetilde{\nabla}g|(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f\setminus 0\Crit f.

(29)

Then for each
\varepsilon>0

f-g\geq\inf_{\varepsilon\Crit f}(f-g),

or, more precisely, for each
x_{0}\in M
there exists
x\in\varepsilon\Crit f
such that (
24
) and (
25
) hold.

Proof.

One can repeat the proof of Proposition
4
using Lemma
5
.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a complete metric space and let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below function. Let
g:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function such that (
18
) is fulfilled.
Then for each
\varepsilon>0
,
x_{0}\in M
and
r\in(0,1)
there exists
x\in\varepsilon\Crit f
such that (
24
) holds and

f(x_{0})-rg(x_{0})\geq f(x)-rg(x).

(30)

Proof.

We apply Proposition
5
to
f
and
rg
. It is clear that (
18
) implies (
29
) for
rg
, because
|\widetilde{\nabla}(rg)|=r|\widetilde{\nabla}g|
and
0<r<1
.

Now we can remove the continuity assumption, but once again, only for the global slope
|\widetilde{\nabla}|
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a complete metric space and let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper lower semicontinuous and bounded below function. Let
g:M\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a lower semicontinuous function such that (
18
) holds.

Then for each
\varepsilon>0
,
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,|\widetilde{\nabla}f|
and
r\in(0,1)
there exists
x\in\varepsilon\Crit f
such that (
24
) and (
30
) hold. More precisely,

\inf_{\mathrm{dom}\,f}(f-rg)=\inf_{\varepsilon\Crit f}(f-rg).

(31)

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,|\widetilde{\nabla}f|
and let
s:=|\widetilde{\nabla}f|(x_{0})<\infty
. We can also assume that
s>0
, because otherwise there is nothing to prove.

Let
\lambda:=f(x_{0})-rg(x_{0})
and

M_{1}:=s\Crit f\cap L_{\lambda}(f-rg),\quad f_{1}:=f\restriction_{M_{1}},\quad g_{1}:=rg\restriction_{M_{1}}.

Obviously,
x_{0}\in M_{1}
. From Lemma
3
we know that
s\Crit f
is closed and
f
is Lipschitz on
s\Crit f
. Also, (
18
) implies that
s\Crit f\subset s\Crit g
and thus
g
is also Lipschitz on
s\Crit f
. So,
(f-rg)\restriction_{s\Crit f}
is Lipschitz and thus
L_{\lambda}(f-rg)\cap s\Crit f
is closed.
That is,
M_{1}
is closed and
g_{1}
is Lipschitz.

It is clear that
|\widetilde{\nabla}g_{1}|\leq r|\widetilde{\nabla}g|
on
M_{1}
. On the other hand, Lemma
8
gives that
|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|=|\widetilde{\nabla}f|
on
M_{1}
. Therefore, (
18
) translates to

|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|(x)>|\widetilde{\nabla}g_{1}|(x),\quad\forall x\in M_{1}:\ |\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|(x)>0,

which is (
29
) for
f_{1}
and
g_{1}
. We can apply Proposition
5
to
f_{1}
and
g_{1}
and use once again that
|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|=|\widetilde{\nabla}f|\restriction_{M_{1}}

Now (
31

4
Moreau-Rockafellar Theorem

We finally show that slopes provide quite different and modern approach to Moreau-Rockafellar Theorem. For a discussion on the known proofs see
[
15
]
. In relation to this, note that the recent proof
[
17
]
is also quite classical in flavour.

If
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is a Banach space and
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a convex function, its
subdifferential
at
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
is the subset of the dual space
X^{*}
given by

\partial f(x_{0}):=\{p\in X^{*}:p(x-x_{0})\leq f(x)-f(x_{0}),\ \forall x\in X\},

and
\partial f(x_{0})=\varnothing
whenever
x_{0}\not\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
. As usual,
\mathrm{dom}\,\partial f:=\{x:\partial f(x)\neq\varnothing\}
, see e.g.
[
26
]
.

For any convex function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
it is easy to see that the local slope and global slope coincide at any point.

Theorem 4

(Moreau-Rockafellar)
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space and let
f,g:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper, convex and lower semicontinuous functions. If

\partial g(x)\subset\partial f(x),\quad\forall x\in X,

(32)

then for some constant
c\in\mathbb{R}

f(x)=g(x)+c,\quad\forall x\in X.

Proof.

From the maximal monotonicity of
\partial f
, see
[
25
]
or
[
23
, Theorem 3.24]
, and (
32
)
it follows that

\partial g(x)\equiv\partial f(x),\quad\forall x\in X.

(33)

Take
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,\partial g
and take
p\in\partial g(x_{0})
. So,
p
satisfies

g(x)\geq g(x_{0})+p(x-x_{0}),\quad\forall x\in X.

From (
32
) it follows that
p\in\partial f(x_{0})
, so

f(x)\geq f(x_{0})+p(x-x_{0}),\quad\forall x\in X.

Set

f_{1}(x):=f(x)-f(x_{0})-p(x-x_{0}),\quad g_{1}(x):=g(x)-g(x_{0})-p(x-x_{0}),\quad\forall x\in X.

It is clear that
\min f_{1}=\min g_{1}=0
. From the Sum Theorem, see e.g.
[
23
, Theorem 3.16]
, and (
33
) we have that

\partial g_{1}(x)\equiv\partial f_{1}(x),\quad\forall x\in X.

(34)

Because for the proper convex lower semicontinuous function
f_{1}
it holds that
\mathrm{dom}\,|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|\equiv\mathrm{dom}\,|\nabla f_{1}|\equiv\mathrm{dom}\,\partial f_{1}
and

|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|(x)=|\nabla f_{1}|(x)=\min\{\|p\|:p\in\partial f_{1}(x)\},\quad\forall x\in\mathrm{dom}\,\partial f_{1},

see
[
28
, Lemma 3.2]
, and the same is true for
g_{1}
, from (
34
) it holds that

|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|(x)=|\widetilde{\nabla}g_{1}|(x)\quad\forall x\in\mathrm{dom}\,|\widetilde{\nabla}f_{1}|\equiv\mathrm{dom}\,|\widetilde{\nabla}g_{1}|.

Since
\min f_{1}=\min g_{1}=0
, Theorem
1
implies both
f_{1}(x)\geq g_{1}(x)
and
g_{1}(x)\geq f_{1}(x)
for all
x\in X
. That is,
f_{1}(x)=g_{1}(x)
for all
x\in X
, which means that

f(x)=g(x)+(f(x_{0})-g(x_{0})).

∎

Appendix.

Even in trying to solve simple problems one is tempted to use transfinite induction. Let us take for example the problem of finding the middle point of a segment using only ruler and compass.

Let the segment
[a,b]
is given. Denote the distance between the points
a
and
b
by
d(a,b)
. For finding the middle point
m
consider the following transfinite procedure,
[
16
]
:

l\leftarrow a
;
r\leftarrow b

till
l\neq r
:

take arbitrary point
x
in
[l,r]
different from
l
and
r

if
d(l,x)<d(x,r)
then:

l\leftarrow x
;

r\leftarrow
the point on
[l,r]
at distance
d(l,x)
to
r
;

if
d(l,x)>d(x,r)
then:

l\leftarrow
the point on
[l,r]
at distance
d(x,r)
to
l
;

r\leftarrow x
;

if
d(l,x)=d(x,r)
then stop (
x\equiv m
).

The segments
[l,r]
are nested and their lengths monotonically decrease. Transferring the construction on the real line, the latter implies that we are bypassing at least one new rational number each time, meaning that at some countable ordinal we will get the middle point.

Although the above transfinite procedures works, in practice one uses the well known finite procedure of construction of the bisector. Admittedly, the latter uses the plane, but it is anyway good to know.

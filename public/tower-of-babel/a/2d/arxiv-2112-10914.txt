Cohomologies and deformations of pre-Lie-morphism triples Thanks: ∗ the corresponding author
Yibo Wang, Shilong Zhang, Jiefeng Liu ∗

Abstract
. A (pre-)Lie-morphism triple consists of two (pre-)Lie algebras and a (pre-)Lie algebra homomorphism between them. We give chomologies of pre-Lie-morphism triples. As an application, we study the infinitesimal deformations of pre-Lie-morphism triples. Finally, we show that the cohomology of the pre-Lie-morphism triple can be deduced from a new cohomology of the Lie-morphism triple.

1.
Introduction

Pre-Lie algebras are a class of nonassociative algebras coming from the
study of convex homogeneous cones, affine manifolds and affine
structures on Lie groups, and the aforementioned cohomologies of
associative algebras. They also appeared in many fields of
mathematics and mathematical physics, such as
symplectic structures on Lie groups and Lie algebras, integrable
systems, Poisson brackets and infinite dimensional Lie algebras,
vertex algebras, quantum field theory, operads and numerical analysis. See the survey

[
4
,
17
]
and the references therein for
more details.

The cohomology theory is a classical approach associating
invariants to a mathematical structure. Cohomology controls
deformations and extension problems of the corresponding algebraic
structures. Cohomology theories of various kinds of algebras have
been developed and studied in
[
5
,
12
,
14
,
15
]
. See the
review paper
[
13
]
for more details. The cohomologies of Lie algebra homomorphisms were introduced by Nijenhuis and Richardson in
[
19
]
. Then the cohomology of two Lie algebras with a Lie algebra homomorphism between them were studied by Y. Frégier in
[
10
]
, which can control simultaneous deformations of these two Lie algebras with the Lie algebra homomorphism. We call two Lie algebras with a Lie algebra homomorphism between them a Lie-morphism triple. See
[
7
]
for more details on the cohomology of a Lie-morphism triple with coefficients in a representation and its applications.

In this paper, following the idea given by Y. Frégier in
[
10
]
, we study the cohomologies of pre-Lie-morphism triples. Here a pre-Lie-morphism triple consists of two pre-Lie algebras and a pre-Lie algebra homomorphism between them. Pre-Lie-morphism triples arise from Rota-Baxter operators, Nijenhuis operators, symplectic Lie algebra homomorphisms,
\mathfrak{s}
-matrices, Cayley maps and
B
-series. As an application of the cohomology of pre-Lie-morphism triples, we study infinitesimal deformations of pre-Lie-morphism triples and show that equivalent pre-Lie-morphism triples are in the same cohomology class. We also introduce the notion of a Nijenhuis pair on a pre-Lie-morphism triple and show that it can generate a trivial infinitesimal deformation. We show that compatible
{\mathcal{O}}
-operators on pre-Lie algebras can give rise to Nijenhuis pairs naturally. See
[
8
,
20
]
for more details about Nijenhuis operators on pre-Lie algebras and Lie algebras. Based on the close relationship between the cohomologies of pre-Lie algebras and the cohomologies of Lie algebras, we find that the cohomology of a pre-Lie-morphism triple can be deduced from a new cohomology of the Lie-morphism triple.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall representations, cohomologies and homomorphisms of pre-Lie algebras. Then some examples of pre-Lie algebra homomorphisms are given. In Section
3
, we give the cohomology of a pre-Lie-morphism triple and use it to study infinitesimal deformations of the pre-Lie-morphism triple. Furthermore, we introduce the notion of a Nijenhuis pair on the pre-Lie-morphism triple , which generates trivial infinitesimal deformations. At last, we construct Nijenhuis pairs from compatible
{\mathcal{O}}
-operators on pre-Lie algebras. In Section
4
, we study the relationship between the cohomologies of pre-Lie-morphism triples and the cohomologies of Lie-morphism triples.

In this paper, we work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic 0 and all the vector spaces are taken over
\mathbb{K}
. Unless
otherwise noted, all vector spaces we consider will be finite dimensional .

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the notion of pre-Lie algebra homomorphisms and give some examples.

Definition 2.1
.

A
pre-Lie algebra
is a pair
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space and
\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a bilinear multiplication
satisfying that for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, the associator

(x,y,z)=(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z-x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z)
is symmetric in
x,y
,
i.e.

(x,y,z)=(y,x,z),\;\;{\rm or}\;\;{\rm equivalently,}\;\;(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z-x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z)=(y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z-y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z).

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie algebra. The commutator
[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y-y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x
defines a Lie algebra structure
on
\mathfrak{g}
, which is called the
sub-adjacent Lie algebra
of

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and denoted by
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
. Furthermore,

L:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
with
x\rightarrow L_{x}
, where

L_{x}y=x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y
, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, gives a representation of
the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
on
\mathfrak{g}
. See
[
4
]
for more details.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie algebra and
V
a vector
space. A
representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V
consists of a pair

(\rho,\mu)
, where
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a representation
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
on
V
and
\mu:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a linear
map satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle\rho(x)\mu(y)u-\mu(y)\rho(x)u=\mu(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)u-\mu(y)\mu(x)u,\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~u\in V.

Usually, we denote a representation by
(V;\rho,\mu)
. It is
obvious that
(\mathbb{K};\rho=0,\mu=0)
is a representation, which is
called the
trivial representation
. Let
R:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
be a linear map with
x\longrightarrow R_{x}
, where the
linear map
R_{x}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is defined by

R_{x}(y)=y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x,
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then

(\mathfrak{g};\rho=L,\mu=R)
is also a representation, which is called the

regular representation
. Define two linear maps
L^{*},R^{*}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
with
x\longrightarrow L^{*}_{x}
and

x\longrightarrow R^{*}_{x}
respectively (for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
)
by

(2)

\langle L_{x}^{*}(\xi),y\rangle=-\langle\xi,x\cdot y\rangle,\;\;\langle R_{x}^{*}(\xi),y\rangle=-\langle\xi,y\cdot x\rangle,\;\;\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Then
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};\rho={\rm ad}^{*}=L^{*}-R^{*},\mu=-R^{*})
is a
representation of
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
, which is called the

coregular representation
. In fact, it is the dual
representation of the regular representation
(\mathfrak{g};L,R)
.

The cohomology complex for a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
with coefficients in a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
is given as follows (
[
4
]
). The cohomology of the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in
V
is the cohomology of the cochain complex
(\otimes_{n\geq 1}C_{\rm preLie}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V),\mathrm{d})
, where
C^{n}_{\rm preLie}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g},V)
and the coboundary operator
\mathrm{d}:C_{\rm preLie}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)\longrightarrow C_{\rm preLie}^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g},V)
is given by

(3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}\rho(x_{i})f(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}\mu(x_{n+1})f(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n},x_{i})

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}f(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n},x_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(-1)^{i+j}f([x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,\hat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n+1}),

for all
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g},~i=1,\cdots,n+1
. We denote the corresponding
n
-th cohomology group by
H_{\rm preLie}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},V)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two pre-Lie algebras. A
homomorphism
between
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
is a linear map
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
satisfying

(4)

\phi(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)=\phi(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi(y),\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Moreover, two pre-Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
with a pre-Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi
is call a
pre-Lie-morphism triple
. We denote it by
((\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}),(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}),\phi)
, or simply
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\phi)
.

It is obvious that if
\phi
is a homomorphism from a pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
to a pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
, then
\phi
is a homomorphism from the sub-adjacent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
to the sub-adjacent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{c}
. We call the Lie-morphism triple
(\mathfrak{g}^{c},\mathfrak{h}^{c},\phi)
a
sub-adjacent Lie-morphism triple
of the pre-Lie-morphism triple
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\phi)
.

Let
D
be a derivation on a commutative associative algebra
(A,\cdot_{A})
. Then the new product

(5)

x\ast_{D}y=x\cdot_{A}D(y),\quad\forall~x,y\in A

makes
(A,\ast_{D})
being a pre-Lie algebra by Gel’fand

[
11
]
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
D_{1}
be a derivation on a commutative associative algebra
(A,\cdot_{A})
and
D_{2}
a derivation on a commutative associative algebra
(B,\cdot_{B})
. Assume that
f:A\to B
is an associative algebra homomorphism satisfying
f\circ D_{1}=D_{2}\circ f
. Then
f
is a pre-Lie algebra homomorphism from the pre-Lie algebra
(A,\ast_{D_{1}})
to the pre-Lie algebra
(B,\ast_{D_{2}})
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie algebra. Recall that
\omega\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is a 2-cocycle on
\mathfrak{g}
if

(6)

\omega([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},z)+\omega([z,x]_{\mathfrak{g}},y)+\omega([y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},x)=0,\quad\forall~x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

A
symplectic Lie algebra
, denoted by
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
, is a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
together with a nondegenerate

2
-cocycle
\omega\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

(
[
6
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
be a symplectic Lie algebra.
Then there exists a pre-Lie algebra structure “
\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}
” on

\mathfrak{g}
given by

(7)

\omega(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y,z)=-\omega(y,[x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad\forall~x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},

such that the sub-adjacent Lie algebra is exactly

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
itself.

Example 2.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\omega_{1})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\omega_{2})
be two symplectic Lie algebras. Assume that
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
is a symplectic Lie algebra homomorphism, i.e.

\phi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=[\phi(x),\phi(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\omega_{2}(\phi(x),\phi(y))=\omega_{1}(x,y),\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
\phi
is a pre-Lie algebra homomorphism from the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
to the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}})
.

Definition 2.7
.

(
[
1
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie algebra and
\mathcal{R}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
a linear operator. If
\mathcal{R}
satisfies

(8)

\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{R}(y)=\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

then
\mathcal{R}
is called a

Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Example 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
on a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Define a new operation
\cdot^{\mathcal{R}}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
by

x\cdot^{\mathcal{R}}y=\mathcal{R}(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y,\;\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot^{\mathcal{R}})
is a pre-Lie algebra and
\mathcal{R}
is a pre-Lie algebra homomorphism from the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot^{\mathcal{R}})
to the pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

Definition 2.9
.

(
[
20
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie algebra. A linear operator
N:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is called a
Nijenhuis operator
if

(9)

N(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}N(y)=N\big(N(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}N(y)-N(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)\big),\;\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Example 2.10
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie algebra and
N
a Nijenhuis operator on
\mathfrak{g}
. Define a new operation
\cdot_{N}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by

(10)

\displaystyle x\cdot_{N}y=N(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y+x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}N(y)-N(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y),\;\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{N})
is a pre-Lie algebra and
N
is a pre-Lie algebra homomorphism from
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{N})
to
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

Definition 2.11
.

(
[
3
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie algebra and
(V;\rho,\mu)
a representation. A linear map
T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is called an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator
on a pre-Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
associated to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
if it satisfies

(11)

T(u)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}T(v)=T\Big(\rho(T(u))(v)+\mu(T(v))(u)\Big),\quad\forall u,v\in V.

Note that a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on a
pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is exactly an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator
associated to the regular representation
(\mathfrak{g};L,R)
.

Example 2.12
.

Let
T
be an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator on a pre-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
associated to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
. Define a new operation
\cdot^{T}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by

(12)

\displaystyle x\cdot^{T}y=\rho(T(u))(v)+\mu(T(v))(u),\;\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot^{T})
is a pre-Lie algebra and
T
is a pre-Lie algebra homomorphism from
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot^{T})
to
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie algebra and
r\in\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
. We introduce
\llbracket r,r\rrbracket\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
as follows

(13)

\llbracket r,r\rrbracket(\xi,\eta,\zeta)=-\langle\xi,r^{\sharp}(\eta)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}r^{\sharp}(\zeta)\rangle+\langle\eta,r^{\sharp}(\xi)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}r^{\sharp}(\zeta)\rangle+\langle\zeta,[r^{\sharp}(\xi),r^{\sharp}(\eta)]^{c}\rangle,\quad\forall~\xi,\eta,\zeta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},

where
r^{\sharp}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathfrak{g}
is given by

\langle r^{\sharp}(\xi),\eta\rangle=r(\xi,\eta),
for all
~\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Definition 2.13
.

(
[
2
]
)
Let

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie algebra. If
r\in\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
and
satisfies
\llbracket r,r\rrbracket=0
, then
r
is called an
\mathfrak{s}
-matrix
in
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

Remark 2.14
.

An
\mathfrak{s}
-matrix in a pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a
solution of the
S
-equation in
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
which is an
analogue of the classical Yang-Baxter equation in a Lie algebra.
It plays an important role in the theory of pre-Lie bialgebras,
see
[
2
]
for more details.

It was shown in
[
2
]
that
r\in\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathfrak{g})
is an
\mathfrak{s}
-matrix in the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
r^{\sharp}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathfrak{g}
is an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator
associated to the coregular representation
(\mathfrak{g}^{*};{\rm ad}^{*},-R^{*})
.

Example 2.15
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie algebra and
r
an
\mathfrak{s}
-matrix. Then
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot_{r})
is a pre-Lie algebra, where the multiplication
\cdot^{r}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is given by

(14)

\xi\cdot^{r}\eta=\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{r^{\sharp}(\xi)}\eta-R^{*}_{r^{\sharp}(\eta)}\xi,\quad\forall~\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Furthermore,
r^{\sharp}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a pre-Lie algebra homomorphism from
(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot^{r})
to
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

3.
Cohomologies and deformations of pre-Lie-morphism triples

In this section, we give the cohomlogy of the pre-Lie-morphism triple and then use it to study the deformations of the pre-Lie-morphism triple. In particular, we show that a Nijenhuis pair on the pre-Lie-morphism triple generates a trivial deformation. The relations between Nijenhuis pairs and compatible
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator are discussed.

3.1.
Cohomologies of pre-Lie-morphism triples

Let
\phi
be a homomorphism between pre-Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Define
\rho_{\phi}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
and
\mu_{\phi}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
by

(15)

\rho_{\phi}(x)u=\phi(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}u,\quad\mu_{\phi}(x)u=u\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi(x),\quad\forall~x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in\mathfrak{h}.

Furthermore, we have

Proposition 3.1
.

The pair
(\rho_{\phi},\mu_{\phi})
is a representation of the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

Since
\phi
is a pre-Lie algebra homomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{h}
,
\phi
is a Lie algebra homomorphism from sub-adjacent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
to
\mathfrak{h}^{c}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi(y))\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}u-(\phi(y)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi(x))\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho_{\phi}(y)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}u)-\phi(y)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho_{\phi}(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}(x)\rho_{\phi}(y)u-\rho_{\phi}(y)\rho_{\phi}(x)u,

which implies that
\rho_{\phi}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\rho_{\phi}(x)\rho_{\phi}(y)-\rho_{\phi}(y)\rho_{\phi}(x)
.

Furthermore, since
\mathfrak{h}
is a pre-Lie algebra, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}(x)\mu_{\phi}(y)u-\mu_{\phi}(y)\rho_{\phi}(x)u-\mu_{\phi}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)u+\mu_{\phi}(y)\mu_{\phi}(x)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}(u\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi(y))-(\phi(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}u)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi(y)-u\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi(y))+(u\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi(x))\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

which implies that
\rho_{\phi}(x)\mu_{\phi}(y)z-\mu_{\phi}(y)\rho_{\phi}(x)z=\mu_{\phi}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)z-\mu_{\phi}(y)\mu_{\phi}(x)z
.

Thus
(\rho_{\phi},\mu_{\phi})
is a representation of the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}

∎

Now we give the cohomology complex for pre-Lie-morphism triples. Let
\phi
be pre-Lie algebra homomorphism between pre-Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Define the set of
k
-cochains by

{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm preLie}^{k}(\phi,\phi)=C_{\rm preLie}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\oplus C_{\rm preLie}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h})\oplus C_{\rm preLie}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}),k\geq 0,

in which we set
C_{\rm preLie}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})=0
.

The coboundary operator
\delta:{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm preLie}^{k}(\phi,\phi)\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\rm preLie}^{k+1}(\phi,\phi)
is defined by

\delta_{\rm preLie}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})=(\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}f_{1},\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{h}}f_{2},\mathrm{d}_{\phi}f_{3}+(-1)^{k}(\phi\circ f_{1}-\phi^{*}\circ f_{2})),

where
\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{h}}
are the coboundary operators of the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
associated to their regular representations respectively,
\mathrm{d}_{\phi}
is the coboundary operator of the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
associated to the representation
(\mathfrak{h};\rho_{\phi},\mu_{\phi})
, and
f_{1}\in C_{\rm preLie}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),f_{2}\in C^{k+1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}),f_{3}\in C^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
. Here
\phi\circ f_{1}\in C_{\rm preLie}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
and
\phi^{*}\circ f_{2}\in C^{k+1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h})
are defined by

\displaystyle(\phi\circ f_{1})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi\big(f_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1})\big),

\displaystyle(\phi^{*}\circ f_{2})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{2}(\phi(x_{1}),\cdots,\phi(x_{k+1})),

where
x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1}\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

We have
\delta_{\rm preLie}\circ\delta_{\rm preLie}=0
. Hence
({\mathcal{C}}^{*}(\phi,\phi)=\otimes_{k\geq 1}C^{n}(\phi,\phi),\delta_{\rm preLie})
is a cochain complex.

Proof.

For
f_{1}\in C_{\rm preLie}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, we have

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\phi(f_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+1},x_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{k+2}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq{k+1}}(-1)^{i+j}\phi(f_{1}([x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,\hat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{k+2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}x_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}f_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+2}))

\displaystyle+\phi(\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}f_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+1},x_{i}))\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{k+2})

\displaystyle-\phi(\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}f_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+1},x_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{k+2}))

\displaystyle+\phi(\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq{k+1}}(-1)^{i+j}f_{1}([x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,\hat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{k+2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}(f_{1}))(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+2}),

which implies that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\phi}(\phi(f_{1}))=\phi(\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}(f_{1})).

For

, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\phi}(\phi^{*}f_{2})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\phi(x_{i})\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}f_{2}(\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+2}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}f_{2}(\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+1},x_{i}))\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi(x_{k+2})

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}f_{2}(\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+1},x_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x_{k+2}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq{k+1}}(-1)^{i+j}f_{2}(\phi([x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,\hat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{k+2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\phi(x_{i})\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}f_{2}(\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+2}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}f_{2}(\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+1},x_{i}))\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi(x_{k+2})

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}f_{2}(\phi(x_{1}),\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,\phi(x_{k+1}),\phi(x_{i})\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi(x_{k+2}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq{k+1}}(-1)^{i+j}f_{2}([\phi(x_{i}),\phi(x_{j})]_{\mathfrak{h}},\phi(x_{1}),\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,\hat{x_{j}},\cdots,\phi(x_{k+2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{*}\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{h}}(f_{2})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+2}),

which implies that

(17)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\phi}(\phi^{*}f_{2})=\phi^{*}\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{h}}(f_{2}).

Furthermore, by (
16
), (
17
) and the fact that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}=0
,
\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{h}}=0
and
\mathrm{d}_{\phi}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\phi}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm preLie}\circ\delta_{\rm preLie}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm preLie}(\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}f_{1},\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{h}}f_{2},\mathrm{d}_{\phi}f_{3}+(-1)^{k}(\phi\circ f_{1}-\phi^{*}\circ f_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}f_{1},\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{h}}f_{2},\mathrm{d}_{\phi}(\mathrm{d}_{\phi}f_{3}+(-1)^{k}(\phi\circ f_{1}-\phi^{*}\circ f_{2}))+(-1)^{k+1}(\phi\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}f_{1}-\phi^{*}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{h}}f_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(0,0,(-1)^{k}\big(\mathrm{d}_{\phi}(\phi(f_{1}))-\phi(\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}(f_{1})+\phi^{*}\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{h}}(f_{2})-\mathrm{d}_{\phi}(\phi^{*}f_{2})\big)\Big)=(0,0,0),

\mathfrak{s}
-matrix, then
(N=r_{1}^{\sharp}\circ(r_{2}^{\sharp})^{-1},S=(r_{2}^{\sharp})^{-1}\circ r_{1}^{\sharp})
is both a Nijenhuis pair on the pre-Lie-morphism triples
((\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot^{r_{1}}),(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}),r_{1}^{\sharp})
and
((\mathfrak{g}^{*},\cdot^{r_{2}}),(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}),r_{2}^{\sharp})
, where the operation
\cdot^{r_{i}}
for
i=1,2
is given by (
14
).

4.
Cohomologies of pre-Lie-morphism triples and Lie-morphism triples

In this section, we show that the cohomology for a pre-Lie-morphism triple can be deduced from a new cohomology of the sub-adjacent Lie-morphism triple.

The Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology theory for a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
associated to a representation
(V;\rho)
is given as follows. Denote by
C^{n}_{\mathrm{CE}}(\mathfrak{g},V):=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},V)
, the space of
n
-cochains. The corresponding Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operator

\partial:C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)\longrightarrow C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g},V)
is given by

(45)

\displaystyle\partial f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho({x_{i}})f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+j}f([x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,\hat{x_{j}},\ldots,x_{n+1})

for all
f\in C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)
and
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{g}
. We denote the corresponding
n
-th cohomology group by
H_{\rm CE}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie algebra and
(V;\rho,\mu)
a representation. Define
\varrho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},V))
by

(46)

\varrho(x)(f)(y)=\rho(x)f(y)-\mu(y)f(x)-f(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y),\quad\forall~f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},V),x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then it was shown in
[
9
]
that
\varrho
is a representation of the sub-adjacent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
on the vector space
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},V)
. Furthermore, we define
\Phi:C^{k}_{\mathrm{CE}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},V))\to C_{\rm preLie}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g},V)
by

(47)

\Phi(f)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y)=f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})(y),\quad\forall~x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Theorem 4.1
.

(
[
9
,
18
]
)

The map
\Phi
is a cochain map from the cochain complex
(C^{*}_{\mathrm{CE}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},V)),\partial_{\varrho})
to the cochain complex
(C_{\rm preLie}^{*}(\mathfrak{g},V),\mathrm{d})
, where
\partial_{\varrho}
is the coboundary operator of the sub-adjacent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
associated to representation
\varrho
and
\mathrm{d}
is the coboundary operator of the pre-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
associated to representation
(\rho,\mu)
.

Moreover,
\Phi
induces an isomorphism between the corresponding cohomology groups:

(48)

H^{n}_{\rm preLie}(\mathfrak{g},V)\cong H^{n-1}_{\rm CE}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},V)),\quad n=1,2\cdots.

Let
\phi
be a homomorphism between pre-Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Define
\varrho_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}))
,
\varrho_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}))
and
\varrho_{\phi}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}))
by

(49)

\displaystyle\varrho_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)(f_{1})(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}f_{1}(y)-f_{1}(x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y-f_{1}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y),

(50)

\displaystyle\varrho_{\mathfrak{h}}(u)(f_{2})(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}f_{2}(v)-f_{2}(u)\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}v-f_{2}(u\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}v),

(51)

\displaystyle\varrho_{\phi}(x)(f_{3})(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}(x)f_{3}(y)-\mu_{\phi}(y)f_{3}(x)-f_{3}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y),

where
f_{1}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
,
f_{2}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h})
,
f_{3}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
and
(\rho_{\phi},\mu_{\phi})
is given by (
15
). Then
\varrho_{\mathfrak{g}}
,
\varrho_{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\varrho_{\phi}
are presentations of the corresponding sub-adjacent Lie algebras.

In the following, we generalize Theorem
4.1
to the case of pre-Lie-morphism triples. Define the set of
k
-cochains by

{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k}(\phi,\phi)=C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}))\oplus C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}))\oplus C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})),k\geq 0,

in which we set
C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}))=0
.

The coboundary operator
\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}:{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k}(\phi,\phi)\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k+1}(\phi,\phi)
is defined by

\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})=(\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}f_{1},\partial_{\mathfrak{h}}f_{2},\partial_{\phi}f_{3}+(-1)^{k}(\phi\circ f_{1}-\phi^{*}\circ f_{2})),

for all
f_{1}\in C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})),f_{2}\in C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h})),f_{3}\in C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}))
, where
\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}
,
\partial_{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\partial_{\phi}
are Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operators of the sub-adjacent Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
,
\mathfrak{h}^{c}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
associated to representations
\varrho_{\mathfrak{g}}
,
\varrho_{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\varrho_{\phi}
, respectively. Here
\phi\circ f_{1}\in C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}))
and
\phi^{*}\circ f_{2}\in C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}))
are defined by

\displaystyle(\phi\circ f_{1})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1})(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi\big(f_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1})(y)\big),

\displaystyle(\phi^{*}\circ f_{2})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1})(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{2}(\phi(x_{1}),\cdots,\phi(x_{k+1}))(\phi(y)),

where
x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1},y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

With the above notations, we have
\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}\circ\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}=0
. Hence
({\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{*}(\phi,\phi)=\otimes_{k\geq 1}C^{n}(\phi,\phi),\delta_{\mathrm{CE}})
is a cochain complex. We denote the corresponding
k
-th cohomology group by
H_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k}(\phi,\phi)
.

Proof.

For
f_{1}\in C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}))
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\phi}(\phi\circ f_{1})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1})(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\phi(x_{i})\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi(f_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+1})(y))

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\phi(f_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+1})(x_{i}))\cdot_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi(y)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\phi(f_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+1})(x_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y))

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq{k+1}}(-1)^{i+j}\phi(f_{1}([x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,\hat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{k+1})(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}x_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}f_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+1})(y))

\displaystyle-\phi(\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}f_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+1})(x_{i})\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)

\displaystyle-\phi(f_{1}([x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,\hat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{k+1})(x_{i}\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y))

\displaystyle+\phi(\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq{k+1}}(-1)^{i+j}f_{1}([x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,\hat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{k+1})(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}(f_{1})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1})(y)),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi\circ\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}(f_{1}))(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1})(y)

which implies that

(52)

\displaystyle\partial_{\phi}(\phi\circ f_{1})=\phi\circ\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}(f_{1}).

Similarly, for
f_{2}\in C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}))
, we have

(53)

\displaystyle\partial_{\phi}(\phi^{*}\circ f_{2})=\phi^{*}\circ\partial_{\mathfrak{h}}(f_{2}).

Furthermore, by (
52
), (
53
) and the fact that
\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}=0
,
\partial_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ\partial_{\mathfrak{h}}=0
and
\partial_{\phi}\circ\partial_{\phi}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}\circ\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}(\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}f_{1},\partial_{\mathfrak{h}}f_{2},\partial_{\phi}f_{3}+(-1)^{k}(\phi\circ f_{1}-\phi^{*}\circ f_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}f_{1},\partial_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ\partial_{\mathfrak{h}}f_{2},\partial_{\phi}\circ(\partial_{\phi}f_{3}+(-1)^{k}(\phi\circ f_{1}-\phi^{*}\circ f_{2}))+(-1)^{k+1}(\phi\circ\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}f_{1}-\phi^{*}\circ\partial_{\mathfrak{h}}f_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(0,0,(-1)^{k}\big(\partial_{\phi}(\phi\circ f_{1})-\phi\circ\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}(f_{1})+\phi^{*}\circ\partial_{\mathfrak{h}}(f_{2})-\partial_{\phi}(\phi^{*}\circ f_{2})\big)\Big)=(0,0,0),

which implies that
\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}\circ\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}=0
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

In
[
7
]
, the author gives a cohomology of a Lie-morphism triple with coefficients in a representation. Our cohomology for the sub-adjacent Lie-morphism triple in this paper is not fit in the mentioned above cohomology theory.

Furthermore, we define
\Phi:C^{k}_{\mathrm{CE}}(\phi,\phi)\to C_{\rm preLie}^{k+1}(\phi,\phi)
by

(54)

\Phi(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})=(\Phi_{1}(f_{1}),\Phi_{2}(f_{2}),\Phi_{3}(f_{3})),

where
\Phi_{1}:C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}))\to C_{\rm preLie}^{k+2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
,
\Phi_{2}:C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}))\to C_{\rm preLie}^{k+2}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h})
and
\Phi_{3}:C_{\mathrm{CE}}^{k}(\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}))\to C_{\rm preLie}^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
are given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(f_{1})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1},y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1})(y),

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}(f_{2})(u_{1},\cdots,u_{k+1},v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{2}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{k+1})(v),

\displaystyle\Phi_{3}(f_{3})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k},y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{3}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})(y)

for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1},y\in\mathfrak{g}
,
u_{1},\cdots,u_{k+1},v\in\mathfrak{h}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

The map
\Phi
is an isomorphic cochain map from the cochain complex
(C^{*}_{\mathrm{CE}}(\phi,\phi),\delta_{\mathrm{CE}})
to the cochain complex
(C_{\rm preLie}^{*}(\phi,\phi),\delta_{\rm preLie})
.

Moreover,
\Phi
induces an isomorphism between the corresponding cohomology groups:

(55)

H^{k}_{\rm preLie}(\phi,\phi)\cong H^{k-1}_{\rm CE}(\phi,\phi),\quad k=1,2,\cdots.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\Phi
is an isomorphic map. In the following, we need to show that
\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ\delta_{\rm preLie}
. By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}\circ\Phi(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}\Phi_{1}(f_{1}),\partial_{\mathfrak{h}}\Phi_{2}(f_{2}),\partial_{\phi}\Phi_{3}(f_{3})+(-1)^{k}(\phi\circ\Phi_{1}(f_{1})-\phi^{*}\circ\Phi_{2}(f_{2})));

\displaystyle\Phi\circ\delta_{\rm preLie}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Phi_{1}(\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}f_{1}),\Phi_{2}(\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{h}}f_{2}),\Phi_{3}(\mathrm{d}_{\phi}f_{3})+(-1)^{k}\Phi_{3}(\phi\circ f_{1}-\phi^{*}\circ f_{2})).

By Theorem
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathfrak{g}}\Phi_{1}(f_{1})=\Phi_{1}(\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{g}}f_{1}),\quad\partial_{\mathfrak{h}}\Phi_{2}(f_{2})=\Phi_{2}(\mathrm{d}_{\mathfrak{h}}f_{2}),\quad\partial_{\phi}\Phi_{3}(f_{3})=\Phi_{3}(\mathrm{d}_{\phi}f_{3}).

Thus
\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ\delta_{\rm preLie}
holds if and only if

\phi\circ\Phi_{1}(f_{1})-\phi^{*}\circ\Phi_{2}(f_{2})=\Phi_{3}(\phi\circ f_{1}-\phi^{*}\circ f_{2}).

By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\phi\circ\Phi_{1}(f_{1})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1},y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(f_{1}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1})(y)),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi\circ f_{1})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1})(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{3}(\phi\circ f_{1})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1},y),

which implies that

\phi\circ\Phi_{1}(f_{1})=\Phi_{3}(\phi\circ f_{1}).

Similarly, we also have

\phi^{*}\circ\Phi_{2}(f_{2})=\Phi_{3}(\phi^{*}\circ f_{2}).

Thus
\delta_{\mathrm{CE}}\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ\delta_{\rm preLie}
holds, i.e. the map
\Phi
is an isomorphic cochain map from the cochain complex
(C^{*}_{\mathrm{CE}}(\phi,\phi),\delta_{\mathrm{CE}})
to the cochain complex
(C_{\rm preLie}^{*}(\phi,\phi),\delta_{\rm preLie})
.

The second conclusion follows directly.
∎

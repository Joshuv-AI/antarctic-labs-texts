On biquandles for the groups G n k G_{n}^{k} and surface singular braid monoid


Abstract
The groups G n k G_{n}^{k} were defined by V. O. Manturov [ 16 ] in order to describe dynamical systems in configuration systems. In the paper we consider two applications of this theory: we define a biquandle structure on the groups G n k G_{n}^{k} , and construct a homomorphism from the surface singular braid monoid to the group G n 2 G_{n}^{2} .

Introduction

In 2015, V. O. Manturov
[
16
]
defined a series of groups
G_{n}^{k}
for natural numbers
n>k\geq 1
and formulated the following principle:

If dynamical systems describing the motion of
n
particles possess a nice codimension one property depending exactly on
k
particles, then these dynamical systems admit a topological invariant valued in
G_{n}^{k}
.

These turn out to be good in studying fundamental groups of configuration and moduli spaces when the initial space admits some geometry (for example, in the Euclidean space one can study codimension 1 properties “three points are collinear” or “four points belong to the same circle/line”). One of our goals is to study when we have just topological spaces.

Definition 1.1
.

The groups of
free
k
-braids

G_{n}^{k}
(

which are indexed by all
k

(1)

(a_{m})^{2}=1
for any unordered sets
m\subset\{1,\ldots,n\},Card(m)=k
;

(2)

(far commutativity)
a_{m}a_{m^{\prime}}=a_{m^{\prime}}a_{m},
if
Card(m\cap m^{\prime})<k-1
;

(3)

(tetrahedron relation) for every set
U=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k+1}\}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
of cardinality
(k+1)
, let us denote them by
m^{p}=U\setminus\{i_{p}\},p=1,\dots,k+1

a_{m^{1}}\cdots a_{m^{k+1}}=a_{m^{k+1}}\cdots a_{m^{1}}.

We shall call the words in generators

diagrams of free
k
-braids
. These groups have become a sophisticated tool for researches in knot theory and low dimensional topology
[
4

Biquandles were introduced in
[
5
]
. They can be described as rules for colouring edges of link diagrams by elements of some set
[
3
]
. These rules ensure that the sets of proper colourings of two equivalent link diagrams are isomorphic. We can take this approach to give a definition of biquandles on free
k
-braids.

Definition 2.1
.

A
biquandle for free
k
-braids
(or a
k
-biquandle
) is a pair
(X,B)
where
X
is some set and
B\colon X^{\times k}\to X^{\times k}
is a map that satisfies the relations:

1.

\pi\circ B=B\circ\pi
for any permutation
\pi\in\Sigma_{k}
. Here we consider the natural action of
\Sigma_{k}
on
X^{\times k}
by permutation of the factors;

2.

B^{2}=\mathop{id}
;

3.

B_{\{1,\dots,k\}}B_{\{p+1,\dots,k+p\}}=B_{\{p+1,\dots,k+p\}}B_{\{1,\dots,k\}}
for any
p=2,\dots,k-1
. Here

B_{\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}}\colon X^{\times 2k-1}\to X^{\times 2k-1}

denotes the map which act as
B
on the factors with indices from the set
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
and as the identity on the others;

4.

B_{\hat{1}}B_{\hat{2}}\cdots B_{\widehat{k+1}}=B_{\widehat{k+1}}B_{\hat{k}}\cdots B_{\hat{1}}
where
B_{\hat{i}}=B_{\{1,\dots,k+1\}\setminus\{i\}}\colon X^{\times k+1}\to X^{\times k+1}
.

The definition means that
B
induces an action
\rho
of the group
G_{n}^{k}
on the set
X^{\times n}
with
\rho(a_{m})=B_{m}
.

.

Let
(X,B)
be a biquandle on free

-braids and

(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})=B(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\equiv(b_{1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}),\dots,b_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})).

Definition 5.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer. The
virtual surface singular braid monoid

VSSB_{n}
is the monoid with the generators
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},c_{i}^{-1}
and
v_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n-1
, and the relations (A1)-(A11) from Definition
4.2
and the virtual relations:

(V1)

v_{i}x_{j}=x_{j}v_{i}
for
|i-j|>1
and
x_{j}\in\{a_{j},b_{j},c_{j},c_{j}^{-1},v_{j}\}

(V3)

x_{i}v_{i\pm 1}v_{i}=v_{i\pm 1}v_{i}x_{i\pm 1}
,
x_{j}\in\{a_{j},b_{j},c_{j},c_{j}^{-1},v_{j}\},j=i,i\pm 1

(V4)

x_{i}v_{i}=v_{i}x_{i},

x_{i}\in\{a_{i},b_{i}\}
.

Remark 5.2
.

Yoshikawa moves for virtual surface-links were found by L. Kauffman
[
12
]
. Here we adapt those moves to the braid case. The relations (V2),(V3) correspond to the detour move of Kauffman, and the relation (V4) is the commutation relation between virtual crossings and marks.

Let
\Sigma_{n}
be the group of permutations of the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
. There is a homomorphism
\rho_{n}
from the monoid
VSSB_{n}
to the group
\Sigma_{n}
:

\rho_{n}(a_{i})=\rho_{n}(b_{i})=1,\quad\rho_{n}(c_{i})=\rho_{n}(c^{-1}_{i})=\rho_{n}(v_{i})=(i\ i+1),\quad i=1,\dots,n-1.

Here
(i\ i+1)
is the transposition of elements
i
and
i+1
.

The kernel of the homomorphism
\rho_{n}
is a submonoid
VPSSB_{n}
in
VSSB_{n}
. We call it the
virtual pure surface singular braid monoid
.

Now, let us define an action of the monoid
VSSB_{n}
on the product
G_{n}^{2}\times\Sigma_{n}
. Given an element
(g,\sigma)\in G_{n}^{2}\times\Sigma_{n}
, we define

\displaystyle c_{i}\cdot(g,\sigma)=c_{i}^{-1}\cdot(g,\sigma)=(a_{\sigma(i),\sigma(i+1)}g,\sigma\cdot(i\ i+1)),

\displaystyle a_{i}\cdot(g,\sigma)=b_{i}\cdot(g,\sigma)=(g,\sigma),\quad v_{i}\cdot(g,\sigma)=(g,\sigma\cdot(i\ i+1)).

Then for any
\beta\in VSSB_{n}
we have

\beta\cdot(1,1)=(\phi_{n}(\beta),\rho_{n}(\beta)).

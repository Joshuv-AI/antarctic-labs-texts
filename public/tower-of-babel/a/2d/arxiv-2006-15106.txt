Congruences of Eisenstein series of level Γ 1 ​ ( N ) \Gamma_{1}(N) via Dieudonné theory of formal groups
Ningchuan Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we give a new explanation of congruences of Eisenstein series of level Γ 1 ​ ( N ) \Gamma_{1}(N) and character χ \chi . Our approach is based on Katz’s algebro-geometric explanation of p p -adic congruences of normalized Eisenstein series E 2 ​ k E_{2k} of level 1 1 . One crucial step in our argument is to reformulate a Riemann-Hilbert correspondence in Katz’s explanation in terms of Dieudonné theory of height 1 1 formal A A -modules and their finite subgroup schemes. We give a generalization of this Riemann-Hilbert correspondence in terms of formal groups of height greater than 1 1 .

3.1.
Congruences of
p

Definition 3.1
.

Let
R
be a
p
-complete local ring and
M
be a torsion-free
R
-module with a continuous
R
-module action by a profinite group
G
.
M
is said to be a trivial
G
-representation modulo an ideal
\mathcal{I}\trianglelefteq R
if
G
acts on
M/\mathcal{I}M
trivially, or equivalently
(M/\mathcal{I}M)^{G}=M/\mathcal{I}M
. The maximal congruence of
M
as a
G
-representation is the smallest ideal
\mathcal{I}
such that
M/\mathcal{I}M
is a trivial
G
-representation.

Remark 3.2
.

The
G
-action on the quotient
M/\mathcal{I}M
is well defined since
G
acts by
R
-linear maps. Otherwise, we need to assume
\mathcal{I}\trianglelefteq R
is a
G
-invariant ideal, i.e.
g\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}
for all
g\in G
.

Lemma 3.3
.

When the underlying
R
-module of the
G
-representation
M
is
R
, the
G
-action of
M
is then associated to a character
\chi\colon G\to R^{\times}
. Let
\{g_{i}\mid i\in I\}
be a set of generators of
G
. The maximal congruence of
M
is the ideal
(1-\chi(g_{i})\mid i\in I)
.

Proof.

The maximal congruence of
M
is by definition the ideal
(1-\chi(g)\mid g\in G)
. Notice that

(1-\chi(gg^{\prime}))=(1-\chi(g)+\chi(g)-\chi(gg^{\prime}))\subseteq(1-\chi(g))+(\chi(g)-\chi(gg^{\prime}))=(1-\chi(g))+(1-\chi(g^{\prime})).

and that
(1-\chi(g^{-1}))=(\chi(g)-1)
, we have
(1-\chi(g)\mid g\in G)=(1-\chi(g_{i})\mid i\in I)
.
∎

When

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
is topologically cyclic. When

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\times}=\{\pm 1\}\times(1+4\mathbb{Z}_{2})
and

is topologically cyclic. Let

be a topological generator of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
when

and a topological generator of

K(1)
-local spheres, whose homotopy groups are computed by the group cohomologies
H_{c}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\times\left(\mathbb{Z}/{N^{\prime}}\right)^{\times};\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\otimes t}[\chi])
in the same paper.

Remark 3.19
.

Generalized Bernoulli numbers are also related to the
Dirichlet
L
-functions
attached to the Dirichlet character
\chi

[
Iwa72
]
:

L(1-k,\chi)=-\frac{B_{k,\chi}}{k}.

When
\chi
is the trivial character, the Dirichlet
L
-function is the same as the Riemann
\zeta
-function (up to Euler factors at primes dividing
N
). A theorem of Soulé
[
Sou79
]
implies that the group cohomology

is isomorphic to the étale cohomology group
H^{1}_{{\acute{e}t}}(\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}],\mathbb{Z}_{p}(2k))

p

\zeta(1-2k)
of the Riemann
\zeta
-function by the Lichtenbaum Conjecture. This is a special case of the (confirmed) Bloch-Kato Conjecture for the Riemann zeta function. A similar connection between étale cohomology and Dirichlet

-functions is proved in
[

.

Remark A.3
.

M=PH^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\widehat{G}/R)
does not depend on the lift
\widehat{G}_{0}
to
R
. This is because the ideal
(p)\trianglelefteq R
has a divided power structure. Moreover, the assignment
\widehat{G}_{0}\mapsto PH^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\widehat{G}/R)
is functorial in
\widehat{G}_{0}
.

To compute the Dieudonné module, we need to simplify (
A.2
).

Theorem A.4

(
[
Car67
]
,
[
Rav04
, (A2.2.4)]
)
.

Let
\widehat{G}
be a formal group over a
p
-local algebra
R
. Then
\widehat{G}
has a coordinate
t
such that its logarithm has the form

\log_{\widehat{G}}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{m_{i}}{p^{i}}t^{p^{i}},\qquad m_{0}=1,m_{i}\in R.

This is called the
p
-typical
coordinate of
\widehat{G}
. The
p
-series of a
p
-typical formal group satisfies:

[p]_{\widehat{G}}(t)=pt+_{\widehat{G}}\sum_{i\geq 1}{}^{\widehat{G}}v_{i}t^{p^{i}}.

Remark A.5
.

There are several different, but equivalent, defintions of Dieudonné modules in the literature. The one in
[
Car67
]
switches the
F
and the
V
maps in
A.1
. See
[
Kat81
, §5.5]
for a comparison of different definitions.

Definition A.6
.

Let
\kappa
be a perfect field of characteristic
p
, containing
\mathbb{F}_{p^{h}}
. The Honda formal group
\Gamma_{h}
of height
h
is a one-dimensional commutative formal group scheme over
\kappa
with a coordinate
t
, such that
[p]_{\Gamma_{h}}(t)=t^{p^{h}}
.

Example A.7
.

When
h=1
,
\Gamma_{1}\cong\widehat{G}_{m}
, since the latter has
p
-series
[p](t)=t^{p}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Choose a lift of
\Gamma_{h}
to
\mathbb{W}\kappa
with a
p
-typical coordinate such that
[p]_{\Gamma_{h}}=pt+_{\Gamma_{h}}t^{p^{h}}
. We can find a basis for
\mathbb{D}(\Gamma_{h})
:

f_{0}(t)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{t^{p^{ih}}}{p^{i}},\quad f_{1}(t)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{t^{p^{ih+1}}}{p^{i}},\quad\cdots,\quad f_{h-1}(t)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{t^{p^{ih+h-1}}}{p^{i}},

such that the matrix representations of
F
and
V
with respect to this basis
\{f_{0},\cdots,f_{h-1}\}
are:

\displaystyle F=\begin{pmatrix}&pI_{h-1}\\
1&\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle V=\begin{pmatrix}&p\\
I_{h-1}&\end{pmatrix}.

In particular, we have
V^{h}(f_{0})=f_{0}\left(t^{p^{h}}\right)=pf_{0}(t)
. When
h=1
, we have
F(f_{0})=f_{0}
and
V(f_{0})=pf_{0}
.

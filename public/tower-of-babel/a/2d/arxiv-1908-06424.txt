1.
Introduction

Suppose

X
be a smooth projective surface defined over a number field
K
and assume that it can be spread out to a smooth projective scheme
{\mathscr{X}}
over an affine open subset of the spectrum of the number ring
{\mathscr{O}}_{K}
. Let
A^{2}(X)
denote the group of algebraically trivial cycles of codimension
2
modulo rational equivalence on
X
.

We recall the definition of algebraic equivalence over
{\mathscr{O}}_{K}
, which will be used in the sequel.
Let us consider the free abelian group of codimension two cycles on
{\mathscr{X}}
. Two cycles
z_{1},z_{2}
are said to be algebraically equivalent if there exists a smooth projective curve
C
defined over
{\mathscr{O}}_{K}
, two scheme theoretic points
x_{0},x_{1}
on
C
and a relative correspondence
\Gamma
on
C\times_{{\mathscr{O}}_{K}}{\mathscr{X}}
, such that the intersection

\Gamma.(x_{0}\times_{{\mathscr{O}}_{K}}{\mathscr{X}})-\Gamma.(x_{1}\times_{{\mathscr{O}}_{K}}{\mathscr{X}})=z_{1}-z_{2}.

Here
.
denotes the relative intersection product in the sense of
[
Fu
]
.

Let
\bar{K}
denote the algebraic closure of
K
and
G=
Gal
({\bar{K}}/K)
be the absolute Galois group.
Also,
X_{\bar{K}}
denotes the surface

X\times_{K}\bar{K}

and

{\mathscr{X}}_{\bar{K}}:={\mathscr{X}}\times_{{\mathscr{O}}_{K}}\times\overline{{\mathscr{O}}_{K}}.

Here
\overline{{\mathscr{O}}_{K}}
denotes the integral closure of
{\mathscr{O}}_{K}
in
\bar{K}
.

Let
A^{2}({\mathscr{X}}_{\bar{K}})(K),A^{2}(X_{\bar{K}})(K)
be the
G
-fixed part of the action of
G
on
A^{2}({\mathscr{X}}_{\bar{K}}),A^{2}(X_{\bar{K}})
respectively,
and
j
be the embedding of
X_{\bar{K}}
into
{\mathscr{X}}_{\bar{K}}
.
If one considers the flat pullback

j^{*}:A^{2}({\mathscr{X}}_{\bar{K}})\to A^{2}(X_{\bar{K}})

of codimension
2
-cycles, it gives a map

j^{*}:A^{2}({\mathscr{X}}_{\bar{K}})(K)\to A^{2}(X_{\bar{K}})(K).

Then a general question is: what is the cokernel of this homomorphism?

Mildenhall
[
Mil
]
studied this flat pull-back for
X
to be the self-product of an elliptic curve admitting a complex multiplication. He has shown that the kernel of this flat-pullback over any number field
K
is finite. We use Mildenhall’s result to derive the information about the quotient
{\rm im}(j^{*})(K)[n]/A^{2}({\mathscr{X}})(K)[n]
at the level of
n
-torsions of this homomorphism for the case where
X
is the self-product of a CM elliptic curve.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by the ring of integers of a number field
K
and
N
be it’s discriminant (for this we fix an Weirstrass equation for
E
once and for all). We denote
E\times E
by
X

and suppose that there exists a smooth spread of
X
, say
{\mathscr{X}}
defined over
{\mathscr{O}}_{K}[1/6N]
.
Let

j^{*}
be the flat pull-back at the level of
A^{2}
induced by the embedding

j:X_{\bar{K}}\to{\mathscr{X}}_{\bar{K}}.

Then the main result is:

Theorem
2.2
: The group
S^{6N}(\Sigma_{\bar{K}})
is a colimit of the unramified cohomology groups

H^{1}(G,\Sigma_{\bar{K}}[6N])\to H^{1}(I_{v},\Sigma_{v}[6N])

here
G
is the Galois group
Gal(\bar{K}/L)
for a finite extension
L
of
K
and
I_{v}
is the inertia subgroup of the Galois group
G
corresponding to a finite place
v
and hence

{\rm im}(j^{*}[6N])(K)/A^{2}({\mathscr{X}}_{\bar{K}})[6N](K)

is a colimit of unramified cohomology groups.

The main tools used here are the Galois module structure of the Chow group of
X_{\bar{K}}
and that of
{\mathscr{X}}_{\bar{K}}
and
the Galois cohomology of the groups
A^{2}({\mathscr{X}}_{\bar{K}})
and that of
A^{2}(X_{\bar{K}})
. The proof involves similar techniques as to show that the Selmer group of an abelian variety defined over a number field is finite.

Coombes
[
Co
]

proved that for a surface
X
with geometric genus and irregularity zero,
A^{2}(X)

is finite under the assumption that
A^{2}(X_{\bar{K}})=0
.

Towards proving our main result Theorem
2.2
, we start with a surface
X
of geometric genus and irregularity zero defined over
K
which satisfies the condition that the map:

{\rm{Pic}}(X)\to{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\bar{K}})\to{\rm{NS}}(X_{\bar{K}})

is surjective,

H^{2}({\mathscr{X}}_{{\mathscr{O}}_{K}[1/f],\mathfrak{p}},{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathscr{X}}_{{\mathscr{O}}_{K}[1/f]},\mathfrak{p}})=0

for all
\mathfrak{p}
in
{\rm{Spec}}({\mathscr{O}}_{K}[1/f])
, here
f
is some element in
{\mathscr{O}}_{K}
and

X(K)\neq\emptyset\;.

Then we prove by using the result of
[
CTR
]
[lemma 3.3], that:

Theorem
2.6
: The group

{\rm im}(j^{*})(K)/A^{2}({\mathscr{X}}_{\overline{{\mathscr{O}}_{K}[1/f]}})(K)

is a torsion group.

Here we do not assume the vanishing of
A^{2}(X_{\bar{K}})
as in
[
Co
]
. The above theorem is important to prove the finiteness or triviality of
A^{2}(X_{\bar{K}})(K)
. It says that, atleast to prove that
{\rm im}(j^{*})(K)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}=\{0\}
it is enough to prove that

A^{2}({\mathscr{X}}_{\overline{{\mathscr{O}}_{K}[1/f]}})(K)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}=\{0\}\;.

This gives some information on how to prove
A^{2}(X)
is finite.

Remark 2.7
.

For a surface with geometric genus and irregularity zero, such that the above conditions as in Theorem
2.2
are satisfied, one has that

{\rm im}(j^{*})(K)/A^{2}({\mathscr{X}}_{\bar{K}})(K)

is torsion. Therefore tensoring with

gives

is surjective. Therefore to prove the triviality for

, it is enough to prove that

A^{2}({\mathscr{X}}_{\bar{K}})(K)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}

is trivial. This gives some information about the structure of of

.

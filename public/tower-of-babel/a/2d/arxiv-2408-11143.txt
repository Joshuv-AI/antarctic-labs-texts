Duality of Geometric Tests for Forward-Flatness
Johannes Schrotshamer *cor, Bernd Kolar, Markus Schöberl

Abstract
Recently it has been shown that the property of forward-flatness for discrete-time systems, which is a generalization of static feedback linearizability and a special case of a more general concept of flatness, can be checked by two different geometric tests. One is based on unique sequences of involutive distributions, while the other is based on a unique sequence of integrable codistributions. In this paper, the relation between these sequences is discussed and it is shown that the tests are in fact dual. The presented results are illustrated by an academic example.

Introduction

The concept of flatness has been introduced by Fliess, Lévine,
Martin and Rouchon in the 1990s for nonlinear continuous-time systems,
see e.g.
[
2
]
or
[
3
]
.
Flat continuous-time systems possess the characteristic property that
all system variables can be expressed by a flat output and its time
derivatives. This property facilitates elegant solutions for trajectory
planning and tracking problems, making the concept of flatness highly
relevant in practical applications. However, currently no verifiable
necessary and sufficient conditions to check the flatness of nonlinear
multi-input systems do exist (see e.g.
[
12
]
,

[
13
]
, or
[
5
]
).

For discrete-time systems, replacing time derivatives of the continuous-time
definition by forward-shifts leads to the concept of forward-flatness
as discussed in
[
7
]
or
[
9
]
.
This is a special case of the more general approaches described in

[
1
]
or
[
6
]
.
In
[
8
]
it has been shown that forward-flatness
can be checked by computing a unique sequence of involutive distributions.
Thus, the forward-flatness of discrete-time systems can already be
checked systematically, in contrast to the continuous-time case. Furthermore,
in the recent paper
[
11
]
, another test for forward-flatness
has been derived, which is based on a unique sequence of integrable
codistributions. In the present contribution, we show that the sequences
annihilate each other, proving that the tests are dual.

It has also been shown through different approaches in
[
8
]

and
[
11
]
that if the system is forward-flat, repeated
system decompositions into a subsystem and an endogenous dynamic feedback
are possible by using suitable state- and input transformations. The
method proposed in
[
8
]
requires straightening
out distributions to derive the necessary input transformation. In
contrast, the method from
[
11
]
only requires normalizing
a certain number of equations. We will show that the input transformation
derived from the method in
[
11
]
also straightens
out the corresponding distribution, making it a special case of the
possible input transformations derived from the method in
[
8
]
.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
,
we first recapitulate the concept of forward-flatness for discrete-time
systems, followed by an overview of the existing geometric tests for
forward-flatness from
[
8
]
and
[
11
]

in Section
3
. Then, in Section
4
,
we prove that the two proposed tests are dual. In Section
5

we show that the method proposed in
[
11
]
for a repeated
system decomposition is a special case of the method proposed in
[
8
]
.
Finally, we illustrate our main results based on an academic example
in Section
6
.

To summarize the concept of forward-flatness, we introduce a space

\delta(g(x,u,u_{[1]},u_{[2]},\ldots))=g(f(x,u),u_{[1]},u_{[2]},u_{[3]},\ldots)\,.

In this contribution, we will only need backward-shifts of functions

\delta^{-1}(g(f(x,u)))=g(x)

\omega_{i}(f(x,u))\mathrm{d}f^{i}

As the shift operators are applied to 1-forms by shifting both their

\delta^{-1}(\omega_{i}(f(x,u))\mathrm{d}f^{i})=\omega_{i}(x)\mathrm{d}x^{i}

and the backward-shift of codistributions with base elements of this
form is defined accordingly. Now the concept of forward-flatness can
be defined as follows.

Distribution-based Test for Forward-flatness

As shown in

For a better comparison of the two tests, Algorithm 1 from

The system (

\mathrm{dim}(E_{k-1})+\mathrm{dim}(P_{k})=n+m
.
So, we have shown that the relation
P_{k}=E_{k-1}^{\perp}
holds,
which is consistent with Theorem
10
. Furthermore,
(
36
) and (
38
) also satisfy the
conditions
D_{k-1}\rfloor(P_{k+1}^{+}+P_{k})=0
and
\mathrm{dim}(D_{k-1})+\mathrm{dim}(P_{k+1}^{+}+P_{k})=n+m
,
which shows the validity of Proposition (
11
).

7
Conclusion

We have shown that for the Algorithms
3
and

5
, starting with the distribution
E_{0}=\mathrm{span}\{\partial_{u}\}

and the codistribution
P_{1}=\mathrm{span}\{\mathrm{d}x\}
, the
relation
P_{k}=E_{k-1}^{\perp}
holds for every step
k=1,\ldots,\bar{k}
.
That is, the sequence of integrable codistributions (
14
)
annihilates the sequence of involutive distributions (
13
)
on the manifold
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{U}
. Consequently, the
distribution-based geometric test from Subsection
3.1

and the codistribution-based geometric test from Subsection
3.2

are indeed dual. Additionally, in Proposition
7
, we
established an efficient method for computing the largest projectable
subdistribution
\bar{D}
of a distribution
D
, which is an essential
step in Algorithm
3
. It was also shown that
while the state transformations (
9a
)
required for repeated system decompositions of the form (
10
)
as proposed in the papers
[
8
]
and
[
11
]

do align, the same is not true for the input transformations (
9b
).
We have shown that the input transformation derived from the method
in
[
11
]
, which only requires normalizing a certain
number of equations, automatically straightens out the distributions

D_{k}
. It is therefore a special case of the possible input transformations
derived from the method in
[
8
]
.

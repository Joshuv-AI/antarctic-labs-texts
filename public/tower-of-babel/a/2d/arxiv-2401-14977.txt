Spectral estimate for the Laplace–Beltrami operator on the hyperbolic half-plane
Marc Rouveyrol

Abstract
. The purpose of this note is to investigate the concentration properties of spectral projectors on manifolds. This question has been intensively studied (by Logvinenko–Sereda, Nazarov, Jerison–Lebeau, Kovrizhkin, Egidi–Seelmann–Veselić, Burq–Moyano, among others) in connection with the uncertainty principle. We provide the first high-frequency results in a geometric setting which is neither Euclidean nor a perturbation of Euclidean. Namely, we prove the natural (and optimal) uncertainty principle for the spectral projector on the hyperbolic half-plane.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Assumption and main result

In the present note, we prove spectral estimates from thick sets on the hyperbolic half-plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},y>0\}
, endowed with its usual metric
g=\frac{1}{y^{2}}(dx^{2}+dy^{2})
. The Laplace–Beltrami operator on this manifold is
\Delta_{g}=y^{2}\left(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}\right)
, and the Riemannian measure corresponding to the metric
g
is
\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}
. Recall that the operator
-\Delta_{g}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{2},\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}})
with domain
H^{2}_{g}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is self-adjoint with spectrum
[\frac{1}{4},+\infty)
(we refer to
[
Heb96
,
Gri09
]
for definitions and properties of Sobolev spaces on Riemannian manifolds and to
[
Ber11
]
,
[
Bor16
, Chapter 4]
for spectral theory of the hyperbolic Laplacian).
Thus, by the spectral theorem, it is possible to write

u=\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+\infty}dm_{\lambda}u,\quad\Pi_{\Lambda}u=\mathbbm{1}_{\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}\leq\Lambda}u=\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\Lambda}dm_{\lambda}u,

where
dm_{\lambda}
denotes the spectral measure of the operator
\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
defined by functional calculus. For
\phi,\varphi
two bounded, real-valued functions over
\mathbb{R}
, one has

\phi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})u=\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+\infty}\phi(\lambda)dm_{\lambda}u,\\
\langle\phi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})u,\varphi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})u\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{H}^{2};\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}\right)}=\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+\infty}\phi(\lambda)\varphi(\lambda)(dm_{\lambda}u,u)

so that

(1.1)

\|\phi(\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{H}^{2};\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}\right)}\leq\sup_{\lambda\in[\frac{1}{4},\Lambda]}|\phi(\lambda)|\|\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{H}^{2};\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}\right)}.

Geodesic balls for the metric
g
are given by

(1.2)

\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{H}^{2},r>0,\quad B^{g}_{r}(x,y)=B_{y\sinh(r)}(x,y\cosh(r))

where
B_{r}^{g}
denotes a geodesic ball of center
(x,y)
and radius
r
and
B_{y\sinh(r)}
a Euclidean ball. Throughout the paper, we will consider a subset
\omega
of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
which satisfies the following assumption:

Assumption 1
. –

There exist
\delta,R>0
such that for any
(x,y)\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
, there holds

\vol_{g}(\omega\cap B^{g}_{R}(x,y))=\int_{\omega\cap B^{g}_{R}(x,y)}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}\geq\delta.

It essentially states that the measure of the set
\omega
is large enough and sufficiently spread out so as to be uniformly bounded from below in every geodesic ball,
i.e.
that
\omega
is a
thick set
for the Riemannian metric and density (see
[
LS74
,
Kov01
]
). Note that in the literature, this lower usually concerns the ratio
\frac{\vol_{g}(\omega\cap B^{g}_{R}(x,y))}{\vol_{g}(B^{g}_{R}(x,y))}
. Since all balls of radius
R
have equal volume in our setting, we omit this divider by including it in
\delta
.

Our main result is that this assumption is equivalent to a spectral estimate from set
\omega
, as follows.

Theorem 1
. –

Consider some Borel set
\omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}
and define the spectral projector
\Pi_{\Lambda}
as above, then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\omega
satisfies Assumption
1
for some
\delta,R>0
;

(2)

there exists some constant
C(\delta,R)>0
such that for any
\Lambda\geq\frac{1}{4},u\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{H}^{2},\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}\right)
,

(1.3)

\|\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{H}^{2};\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}\right)}\leq Ce^{C\Lambda}\|\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega;\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}\right)}.

1.2.
Some bibliography on spectral estimates

The first equivalence between a spectral estimate (formulated as an uncertainty principle for the Fourier transform) and a thickness condition on the observability set was obtained by Logvinenko and Sereda using complex analysis methods
[
LS74
]
(see also Zygmund
[
Zyg02
, pp.202-208]
, Nazarov
[
Naz93
]
and Kovrizhkin
[
Kov01
,
Kov03
]
). The idea of using propagation of smallness inequalities for harmonic functions to prove these estimates can be traced back to Jerison and Lebeau
[
JL99
]
, with applications to bounds on the Hausdorff measure of nodal sets.

These spectral estimates have had fruitful applications to controllability (or equivalently observability) of the heat equation and of parabolic systems since the works of Lebeau, Robbiano and Zuazua
[
LR95
,
LZ98
]
. In
[
Mil10
]
, Miller provided an abstract framework for proving observability inequalities at optimal cost for parabolic systems. Phung and G. Wang generalized these observability estimates to any positive-measure time set using a telescoping series method
[
PW13
]
, and Apraiz, Escauriaza, G. Wang and C. Zhang
[
AE13
,
AEWZ14
]
proved observability from any positive measure time-space set for the heat equation on bounded, star-shaped domains.

On unbounded domains, Egidi and Veselić
[
EV18
]
and C. Wang, M. Wang, C. Zhang and Y. Zhang
[
WWZZ19
]
simultaneously proved the equivalence between observability of the (constant-coefficient) heat equation and thickness of the observation set on the whole space. The former used Kovrizhkin’s formulation of the Logvinenko–Sereda theorem
[
Kov00
,
Kov01
]
, while the latter’s proof is based on the equivalence with Jerison–Lebeau type estimates for spectral projectors and the telescopic series method of
[
PW13
]
. The result of
[
WWZZ19
]
can be seen as the flat-Laplacian equivalent of Theorem
1
. We refer to the works of Beauchard, Jaming, Martin and Pravda-Starov
[
BJPS21
,
MPS23
]
, Egidi, Seelmann and their collaborators
[
ES21
,
ENS+20
]
and the references therein for further spectral estimate and parabolic controllability results from thick sets.

Studies about the interaction between spectral estimates for the Laplacian and the ambient geometry are more scarce in the literature. In
[
LM19
]
, Lebeau and Moyano proved spectral estimates for Schrödinger operators with analytic potentials, when the metric is an analytic perturbation of the identity. More recently, Burq and Moyano proved spectral estimates for Laplace operators on compact,
W^{2,\infty}
manifolds with Lipschitz metric and density
[
BM23
]
, combining ideas of Jerison and Lebeau
[
JL99
]
with propagation of smallness results of Logunov and Malinnikova
[
LM18
]
. They then showed that the thickness condition for subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
implies spectral projector estimates for uniformly elliptic Laplacians with Lipschitz metric and density
[
BM21
]
.

The aforementioned propagation of smallness result
[
LM18
, Theorem 5.1]
is a three-sphere type estimate for the gradients of solutions of homogeneous elliptic equations in divergence form. It plays a central role in the present article. A remarkable feat of this result is that the smaller set in the estimate is of Hausdorff dimension smaller than
d-1
, where
d
denotes the dimension of the full space. We refer to
[
Log18a
,
Log18b
,
LM18
]
for details concerning the spirit and proofs of this result. For the convenience of the reader, a statement of the estimate is given in Appendix
A
.

To the best of our knowledge, the first spectral estimate results on non-compact manifolds with a geometry that is not a perturbation of the Euclidean one are those of Rose and Tautenhahn
[
RT24
]
on manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded from below. The main difference with Theorem
1
is that their estimate holds for values of
\Lambda
below a certain threshold given by the geometry.

The main contribution of the present work is to give the first (again to the best of our knowledge) example of a high-frequency spectral estimate from thick sets on a non-compact manifold with a geometry that is not a perturbation of the Euclidean one. The proof is inspired by the works of Burq–Moyano
[
BM23
,
BM21
]
, in which the link between the Logunov–Malinnikova inequality and spectral estimates was established, and the article
[
WWZZ19
]
, where the authors use an observability inequality to recover a thickness condition on the observation set.

1.3.
Scheme of the proof

The rest of the paper is dedicated to proving Theorem
1
. In the proof that Assumption
1
implies the spectral estimate, it is more natural to consider Euclidean rectangles than geodesic balls, hence the following remark:

Remark

Consider
R^{\prime}
given by the one-to-one identity

\tanh(R)=\min\left(1-2^{-R^{\prime}},\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{R^{\prime}}-1\right),

where
R
is given by Assumption
1
. Then, every rectangle

\mathcal{R}_{j,k}(R^{\prime}):=(2^{R^{\prime}j}k-2^{R^{\prime}j},2^{R^{\prime}j}k+2^{R^{\prime}j})_{x}\times(2^{R^{\prime}(j-1)},3^{R^{\prime}}\times 2^{R^{\prime}(j-1)})_{y},

contains some geodesic ball of diameter
R
. Precisely, (
1.2
) gives

(1.4)

B^{g}_{R}\left(2^{R^{\prime}j}k,\frac{2^{R^{\prime}j}}{\cosh(R)}\right)\subset\mathcal{R}_{j,k}(R^{\prime}).

By (
1.4
), Assumption
1
implies the following assumption, which is the one used in the proof:

Assumption 2
. –

There exists some
\delta>0
such that for any
(j,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, there holds

\int_{\omega\cap\mathcal{R}_{j,k}(R^{\prime})}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}\geq\delta.

It is easily checked that

(1.5)

\mathbb{H}^{2}\subset\bigcup_{(j,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathcal{R}_{j,k}

and that for any
x\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
, there holds

\sharp\{(j,k),x\in\mathcal{R}_{j,k}\}\leq N

for some
N(R^{\prime})
independent of
x
. Thus,

(1.6)

\|.\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{2};\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}})}^{2}\leq\sum_{j,k}\|.\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{R}_{j,k};\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}})}^{2}\leq N\|.\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{2};\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}})}^{2}.

The strategy of proof of the sufficient condition (namely that Assumption
1
implies the spectral estimate) adapts Burq and Moyano’s
[
BM23
]
on the whole space: we apply Logunov and Malinnikova’s propagation of smallness inequality to a well-chosen harmonic function
v_{\Lambda}(x,y,t)
, with
t
an auxiliary variable and
(x,y)
localized inside each
\mathcal{R}_{j,k}
. We then use a Sobolev injection-type lemma to recover the spectral projector inequality (
1.3
). The main novelty arises from the fact that the operator
-\Delta_{g}=y^{2}(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2})
is not uniformly elliptic over
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, hence the covering
(\mathcal{R}_{j,k})
of the half-plane is chosen so that the metric
g
does not vary too much over each subset. Besides, the multiplicative constant in Logunov and Malinnikova’s estimate depends on the ellipticity and Lipschitz constants of the operator considered. To avoid dealing with this dependence, we perform a change of coordinates over every subset
\mathcal{R}_{j,k}
to obtain constants uniform with respect to
(j,k)
.

The proof of the necessary condition is inspired by similar work by G. Wang, M. Wang, C. Zhang and Y. Zhang for the flat Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In
[
WWZZ19
]
, they deduce thickness of the set
\omega
from a final-time observability estimate that has been known to derive from spectral projector estimates since the works of Miller
[
Mil10
]
and Phung and G. Wang
[
PW13
]
. We adapt this strategy to our framework using upper and lower Gaussian (in terms of the geodesic distance) bounds for the hyperbolic heat kernel.

The next section is split into three parts. In the first two, we prove that Assumption
2
implies the spectral inequality (
1.3
). In Subsection
2.1
, we apply the Logunov–Malinnikova and Sobolev-type estimates to prove (
1.3
). Subsection
2.2
is dedicated to proving the Sobolev injection lemma. In subsection
2.3
, we show that the spectral inequality (
1.3
) (or rather its corollary, observability inequality (
2.17
)) implies Assumption
1
. The appendices do not contain any new mathematical content and are given for the sake of self-containedness. In Appendix
A
, we state the Logunov–Malinnikova inequality which is used in subsection
2.1
. In Appendix
B
, we reproduce a proof that the spectral estimate (
1.3
) implies observability inequality (
2.17
), following
[
WWZZ19
]
.

We conclude this introduction with a few notations. Throughout the rest of the article, we denote
z=(x,y)
a generic element in the hyperbolic half-plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
,
d_{g}(z,z^{\prime})
the geodesic distance between two points
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
and
d\vol_{g}:=d\vol_{g}(z)=\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}
the Riemannian measure corresponding to the metric
g
. We denote by
\|.\|_{L^{2}_{g}}
norms involving the measure
d\vol_{g}(z)
(or the measure
dtd\vol_{g}(z)
when also integrating over the auxiliary variable
t
). Norms denoted
\|.\|_{L^{2}}
without the index
g
are integrated using Lebesgue measure. The symbol
\lesssim
means that the left-hand-side is bounded by the right-hand-side up to an implicit multiplicative constant which is independent of relevant quantities. The dependence of implicit constants is specified when deemed necessary.
A\simeq B
means that both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
hold.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

2.1.
Proof of the spectral estimate

We prove inequality (
1.3
) under Assumption
2
. For the sake of simplicity, the proof is written for
R^{\prime}=1
. It can be adapted easily to the case
R^{\prime}>0
by adding appropriate
R^{\prime}
exponents in the bounds of the rectangles considered.

Take
u\in L^{2}_{g}(\mathbb{H}^{2})
and define

v_{\Lambda}(t,x,y)=\int_{\lambda=0}^{\Lambda}\frac{\sinh(\lambda t)}{\lambda}dm_{\lambda}u.

Then
-\Delta_{g}v_{\Lambda}=\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\frac{\sinh(\lambda t)}{\lambda}\lambda^{2}dm_{\lambda}u
so that

(\partial_{t}^{2}+\Delta_{g})v_{\Lambda}=0.

Recalling that
\mathcal{R}_{j,k}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{H}^{2},|x-2^{j}k|<2^{j},2^{j-1}<y<3\times 2^{j-1}\}
for any
(j,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
\mathbb{H}^{2}\subset\cup_{j,k}\mathcal{R}_{j,k}
, let
1=\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\chi_{j,k}
be a partition of unity associated to this covering. Over each rectangle
\mathcal{R}_{j,k}
, we perform the change of variables

(X,Y)=\phi_{j,k}(x,y)=(2^{-j}x-k,2^{-j}y)

so that
(X,Y)
spans the rectangle
\mathcal{R}=(-1,1)_{X}\times(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2})_{Y}
when
(x,y)
spans
\mathcal{R}_{j,k}
. This change of coordinates preserves the hyperbolic Laplacian:

\Delta_{g}=y^{2}(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2})=2^{2j}Y^{2}(2^{-2j}\partial_{X}^{2}+2^{-2j}\partial_{Y}^{2})=Y^{2}(\partial_{X}^{2}+\partial_{Y}^{2}).

We then define
V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}(t,X,Y)=v_{\Lambda}(t,\phi_{j,k}^{-1}(X,Y))
, which satisfies

(\partial_{t}^{2}+Y^{2}\Delta_{X,Y})V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}(t,X,Y)=(\partial_{t}^{2}+\Delta_{g})v_{\Lambda}(t,x,y)=0,\quad(X,Y)\in\mathcal{R}.

Multiplying by
\frac{1}{Y^{2}}
, we get

(\partial_{t}\frac{1}{Y^{2}}\partial_{t}+\Delta_{X,Y})V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}=0\text{ over }\mathcal{R},

which in turn can be written
\Div(A\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k})=0
for

A(t,X,Y)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{Y^{2}}&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

It is clear that for any
T>0
,
A
is uniformly elliptic with Lipschitz coefficients over the box
(-T,T)_{t}\penalty\ \times\penalty\ \mathcal{R}\penalty\ =\penalty\ (-T,T)_{t}\penalty\ \times\penalty\ (-1,1)_{X}\penalty\ \times\penalty\ (\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2})_{Y}
, and its ellipticity and Lipschitz constants are independent of
T,j,k
.

Fix
0<T_{1}<T_{2}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}=(-2,2)_{X}\times(\frac{1}{4},\frac{7}{4})_{Y}
. We denote

(2.1)

\mathcal{K}=(-T_{1},T_{1})\times\mathcal{R},\Omega=(-T_{2},T_{2})\times\mathcal{R}_{2},F_{j,k}=\phi_{j,k}(\omega)\cap\mathcal{R},E_{j,k}=\{0\}\times F_{j,k}.

Then by Assumption
2

\int_{\omega\cap\mathcal{R}_{j,k}}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}=\int_{\phi_{j,k}(\omega)\cap\mathcal{R}}\frac{dXdY}{Y^{2}}\geq\delta.

Thus,

(2.2)

\vol_{euc}(\phi_{j,k}(\omega)\cap\mathcal{R})\geq\frac{\delta}{4}

uniformly with respect to
j,k
. By Logunov and Malinnikova’s theorem
[
LM18
, Theorem 5.1]
, the statement of which is recalled in Appendix
A
, we get

(2.3)

\sup_{\mathcal{K}}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|\leq C\sup_{E_{j,k}}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|^{\alpha}\sup_{\Omega}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|^{1-\alpha}.

The constants
C>0,0<\alpha<1
depend on
\delta
but do not depend on
u
,
\Lambda
,
j
or
k
.

We change the
L^{\infty}
norms to
L^{2}
norms using a trick taken from
[
BM21
, Section 2]
. Assume that
\nabla V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|_{\mathcal{K}}
is not identically zero and let

a=\left(\epsilon\frac{\sup_{\mathcal{K}}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|}{\sup_{\Omega}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|^{1-\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\quad F^{\prime}_{j,k}=\{(X,Y)\in F_{j,k},|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}(t=0,X,Y)|\leq a\}.

Let
E^{\prime}_{j,k}=\{0\}\times F^{\prime}_{j,k}
. One necessarily has
\vol_{euc}(F^{\prime}_{j,k})\leq\frac{\delta}{8}
. Indeed, otherwise, the Logunov–Malinnikova estimate (
2.3
) holds with identical constants but replacing
E_{j,k}
with
E^{\prime}_{j,k}
. This gives

(2.4)

\sup_{\mathcal{K}}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|\leq Ca^{\alpha}\sup_{\Omega}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|^{1-\alpha}\leq C\epsilon\sup_{\mathcal{K}}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|.

For
C\epsilon<1
, we obtain that
\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|_{\mathcal{K}}
is identically zero, hence a contradiction.

As a consequence,

(2.5)

\int_{F_{j,k}}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}(t=0,X,Y)|^{2}dXdY\geq\int_{F_{j,k}\setminus F^{\prime}_{j,k}}|\nabla V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}(t=0,X,Y)|^{2}dXdY\\
\geq a^{2}\frac{\delta}{8}\geq\frac{\delta}{8}\left(\epsilon\frac{\sup_{\mathcal{K}}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|}{\sup_{\Omega}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|^{1-\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}.

Since
\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}(t=0)=\begin{pmatrix}\Pi_{\Lambda}u\circ\phi_{j,k}^{-1}\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}
,
denoting
U_{\Lambda}^{j,k}=\Pi_{\Lambda}u\circ\phi_{j,k}^{-1}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{R}}|U_{\Lambda}^{j,k}(X,Y)|^{2}dXdY

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in(-T_{1},T_{1})}\int_{\mathcal{R}}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|^{2}dXdY

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\mathcal{K}}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{F_{j,k}}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}(t=0,X,Y)|^{2}dXdY\right)^{\alpha}\sup_{\Omega}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|^{2-2\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{F_{j,k}}|U_{\Lambda}^{j,k}(X,Y)|^{2}dXdY\right)^{\alpha}\sup_{\Omega}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|^{2-2\alpha}.

The implicit multiplicative constant in the last inequality depends only on
\delta,\epsilon,T_{1},T_{2}
and numerical constants, and does not depend on
\Lambda,u,j,k
. Note that in the case where
R^{\prime}
(see Assumption
2
and the preliminary remark of this section) is fixed and different from 1, this constant also depends on
R^{\prime}
through the lower bound in (
2.2
), the definitions of
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
, and the constants
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\rho
of Theorem
2
.

Recall that since
y
is roughly of size
2^{j}
over
\mathcal{R}_{j,k}
, we have for any function
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{R})
,

\int_{\mathcal{R}}|f(X,Y)|^{2}dXdY=\int_{\mathcal{R}_{j,k}}2^{-2j}|f\circ\phi_{j,k}(x,y)|^{2}dxdy\simeq\int_{\mathcal{R}_{j,k}}|f\circ\phi_{j,k}(x,y)|^{2}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}.

Applying this to
f=U_{\Lambda}^{j,k}
and
f=U_{\Lambda}^{j,k}\mathbbm{1}_{\phi_{j,k}(\omega)}
, we get

(2.6)

\|U_{\Lambda}^{j,k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{R})}\simeq\|\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{g}(\mathcal{R}_{j,k})},\quad\|U_{\Lambda}^{j,k}\|_{L^{2}(F_{j,k})}\simeq\|\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{g}(\mathcal{R}_{j,k}\cap\omega)}.

Hence,

(2.7)

\|\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{g}(\mathcal{R}_{j,k})}^{2}\leq C\|\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{R}_{j,k}\cap\omega)}^{2\alpha}\sup_{\Omega}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|^{2-2\alpha}.

We now state the following Sobolev injection lemma, the proof of which is postponed to the next subsection.

Lemma 2.1
. –

There exist constants
C,K>0
independent of
u
and
\Lambda
such that for any
u\in L^{2}_{g}(\mathbb{H}^{2})
,

\sum_{j,k}\sup_{\Omega}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|^{2}\leq Ce^{K\Lambda}\|\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{g}(\mathbb{H}^{2})}^{2}.

By Young’s inequality,

a^{2-2\alpha}b^{2\alpha}\leq C(a^{2}+b^{2}).

Thus, by (
2.7
), for any
D>0
,

(2.8)

\|\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{g}(\mathcal{R}_{j,k})}^{2}\leq Ce^{-D\Lambda}\sup_{\Omega}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|^{2}+Ce^{\frac{2-2\alpha}{2\alpha}D\Lambda}\|\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{g}(\omega\cap\mathcal{R}_{j,k})}^{2}.

Summing over
(j,k)
and applying Lemma
2.1
then gives

(2.9)

\|\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{g}(\mathbb{H}^{2})}^{2}\leq Ce^{-D\Lambda}\sum_{j,k}\sup_{\Omega}|\nabla_{t,X,Y}V_{\Lambda}^{j,k}|^{2}+Ce^{\frac{2-2\alpha}{2\alpha}D\Lambda}\|\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{g}(\omega\cap\mathcal{R}_{j,k})}^{2}\\
\leq Ce^{-D\Lambda}e^{K\Lambda}\|\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{g}(\mathbb{H}^{2})}^{2}+Ce^{\frac{2-2\alpha}{2\alpha}D\Lambda}\|\Pi_{\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{g}(\omega)}^{2}.

Fixing
D>K
gives the result for
\Lambda
greater than some
\Lambda_{0}
satisfying
Ce^{(K-D)\Lambda_{0}}<1
. For
\Lambda<\Lambda_{0}
, the result is proved by applying Theorem
1
to
\Pi_{\Lambda}u
.

2.2.
Proof of Lemma
2.1

We now prove Lemma
2.1
. Recall that
\Omega
denotes the set

Proof of Lemma

\|y\nabla_{x,y}h\|_{L^{2}_{g}((-T_{3},T_{3})\times\mathbb{H}^{2})}^{2}\leq\sum_{j,k}\|\nabla_{X,Y}(h\circ\phi_{j,k}^{-1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(2)})}^{2}.

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{X,Y}H_{j,k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(2)})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{\chi}H_{j,k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(3)})}\|\Delta_{X,Y}(\tilde{\chi}H_{j,k})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(3)})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|H_{j,k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(3)})}

\displaystyle\times\left(\|H_{j,k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(3)})}+\|\nabla_{X,Y}H_{j,k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(3)})}+\|\Delta_{X,Y}H_{j,k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{(3)})}\right)

\left.+\|y\nabla_{x,y}h\|_{L^{2}_{g}((-T_{3},T_{3})\times\mathbb{H}^{2})}\|h\|_{L^{2}_{g}((-T_{3},T_{3})\times\mathbb{H}^{2})}+\|\Delta_{g}h\|_{L^{2}_{g}((-T_{3},T_{3})\times\mathbb{H}^{2})}^{2}\right).

Necessary condition

We now prove that Assumption

]
for the flat Laplacian. The first step is the following observability estimate:

Lemma 2.3

(Spectral estimate implies observability)
. –

Consider
\omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}
a measurable set such that the spectral estimate (
1.3
) holds for
\omega
. For any solution
u
of the heat equation on
\mathbb{H}^{2}

(2.16)

\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{g}u=0\text{ in }(0,+\infty)_{t}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}_{z},\quad u(0,.)=u_{0}\in L^{2}_{g}(\mathbb{H}^{2}),

for any
T>0
, there exists a positive constant
C_{obs}(T,\omega)
such that

(2.17)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(T,z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}(z)\leq C_{obs}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}|u(s,z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}(z)ds.

We provide a proof and references concerning this classical result in Appendix
B
for the sake of completeness.

We now show that the observability estimate (
2.17
) implies Assumption
1
. Consider the heat kernel on the hyperbolic half-plane:

(2.18)

H(t,z,z^{\prime})=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-\frac{t}{4}}\int_{d_{g}(z,z^{\prime})}^{\infty}\frac{se^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}ds}{\left(\cosh(s)-\cosh(d_{g}(z,z^{\prime}))\right)^{1/2}},\quad\forall z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}^{2},t>0.

Any solution
u
of the heat equation (
2.16
) then satisfies

(2.19)

u(t,z)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}H(t,z,z^{\prime})u_{0}(z^{\prime})d\vol_{g}(z^{\prime}),

and by the semi-group property for
e^{t\Delta_{g}}
, one has

(2.20)

H(t,z_{1},z_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}H(s,z_{1},z)H(t-s,z,z_{2})d\vol_{g}(z),\quad\forall z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{H}^{2},0<s<t.

We refer to
[
McK70
,
GN98
]
for a proof of the expression of the heat kernel on the hyperbolic plane and to
[
CLY19
,
Gri09
, Chapters 4, 7]
and the references therein for a more detailed study of the heat kernel on manifolds.

We now fix some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
and show that there exist some
R,\delta>0
independent of
z_{0}
such that
\vol_{g}\left(B^{g}_{R}(z_{0})\cap\omega\right)\geq\delta
. Let us apply Lemma
2.3
with the initial condition

u_{0}(z)=H(1,z,z_{0}).

By (
2.20
), the corresponding solution
u(t,z)
of the heat equation is

(2.21)

u(t,z)=H(t+1,z,z_{0}),\quad t>0,z\in\mathbb{H}^{2}.

Inserting (
2.21
) in the observability inequality (
2.17
) for time
T=1
yields

(2.22)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|H(2,z,z_{0})|^{2}d\vol_{g}(z)\leq C_{obs}(1)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\omega}|H(s+1,z,z_{0})|^{2}d\vol_{g}(z)ds.

The next lemma allows to conclude:

Lemma 2.4

(Gaussian bounds)
. –

The heat kernel
H
given by (
2.18
) admits Gaussian (as functions of
d_{g}(z,z^{\prime})
) lower and upper bounds. More precisely, there exist some constants
\alpha,\beta>0
such that the lower bound

(2.23)

H(2,z,z_{0})\gtrsim e^{-\beta d_{g}(z,z_{0})^{2}}

and the upper bound

(2.24)

H(t,z,z_{0})\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{\gamma t}}{f(\gamma t)}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{t}d_{g}(z,z_{0})^{2}},\quad t>0,

hold for some
\gamma>0
and
f(t)=e^{\frac{t}{4}}t^{\frac{3}{2}}
.

We deduce the necessary condition of Theorem
1
from the lemma, which will be proved afterwards. The l.h.s. of (
2.22
) can be bounded from below as follows, with
C
a positive constant

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|H(2,z,z_{0})|^{2}d\vol_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\geq C\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}e^{-2\beta d_{g}(z,z_{0})^{2}}d\vol_{g}(z).

The integral in the right-hand-side is independent of
z_{0}
, as shown by the change of variables

z^{\prime}=y_{0}^{-1}(z-(x_{0},0)).

Indeed, the hyperbolic distance and the measure
d\vol_{g}
are invariant by scaling and horizontal translation (see for example
[
Bor16
, (2.6) and (2.8)]
), so that
d_{g}(z,z_{0})=d_{g}(z^{\prime},(0,1))
and
d\vol_{g}(z)=d\vol_{g}(z^{\prime})
.

We now bound the r.h.s. of (
2.22
) from above using the upper bound (
2.24
). We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\omega}|H(s+1,z,z_{0})|^{2}d\vol_{g}(z)ds

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\omega}\frac{(s+1)\gamma}{f((s+1)\gamma)^{2}}e^{-2\frac{\alpha}{s+1}d_{g}(z,z_{0})^{2}}d\vol_{g}(z)ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}\gamma}{f(\gamma)^{2}}\int_{\omega}e^{-\alpha d_{g}(z,z_{0})^{2}}d\vol_{g}(z).

We introduce a parameter
L>0
and split the domain of integration into
B^{g}_{L}(z_{0})
and
B^{g}_{L}(z_{0})^{c}
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\omega}|H(s+1,z,z_{0})|^{2}d\vol_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}\gamma}{f(\gamma)^{2}}\left(\int_{\omega\cap B^{g}_{L}(z_{0})}e^{-\alpha d_{g}(z,z_{0})^{2}}d\vol_{g}(z)+\int_{\omega\cap{B^{g}_{L}(z_{0})}^{c}}e^{-\alpha d_{g}(z,z_{0})^{2}}d\vol_{g}(z)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}\gamma}{f(\gamma)^{2}}\left(\vol_{g}(\omega\cap B^{g}_{L}(z_{0}))+e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}L^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}d_{g}(z,z_{0})^{2}}d\vol_{g}(z)\right).

The first term is precisely the volume we wish to bound from below to prove Assumption
1
. The integral in the second term is independent from
z_{0}
for the same reason as before.

Plugging the lower and upper bounds together gives

(2.25)

C^{\prime\prime}\leq\vol_{g}(\omega\cap B^{g}_{L}(z_{0}))+e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}L^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}d_{g}(z^{\prime},(0,1))^{2}}d\vol_{g}(z^{\prime})

where
C^{\prime\prime}=\frac{Cf(\gamma)^{2}}{C^{\prime}\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}e^{-2\beta d_{g}(z^{\prime},(0,1))^{2}}d\vol_{g}(z^{\prime})
does not depend on
z_{0}
. We fix
L
large enough for the second term of the r.h.s. to be smaller than
\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{2}
. Assumption
1
then holds for
R=L
and
\delta=\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{2}
.

Proof of lemma
2.4
.

There remains to prove Gaussian lower and upper bounds for the heat kernel
H(t,z,z^{\prime})
. We start with the lower bound. The explicit expression (
2.18
) gives

\displaystyle H(2,z,z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{d_{g}(z,z^{\prime})}^{\infty}\frac{se^{-\frac{s^{2}}{8}}ds}{\left(\cosh(s)-\cosh(d_{g}(z,z^{\prime}))\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}ds

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{d_{g}(z,z^{\prime})}^{\infty}\frac{se^{-\frac{s^{2}}{8}}ds}{\left(\cosh(s)-1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}ds

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{d_{g}(z,z^{\prime})}^{\infty}\frac{se^{-\frac{s^{2}}{8}}ds}{\sinh(s/2)}.

Then since
\sinh(\frac{s}{2})\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{s}{2}}
and
\frac{s}{2}+\frac{s^{2}}{8}\leq\frac{1+s^{2}}{2}
, we get the lower bound

H(2,z,z^{\prime})\gtrsim\int_{d_{g}(z,z^{\prime})}^{\infty}se^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}ds\gtrsim e^{-\frac{1}{2}d_{g}(z,z^{\prime})^{2}}.

To prove the upper bound, we employ the strategy developed by Grigor’yan in
[
Gri97
,
Gri99
, Section 5.1]
, which consists in deducing off-diagonal estimates from diagonal estimates.
H
satisfies the following diagonal estimate:

(2.26)

\displaystyle H(t,z,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}f(t)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{se^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}}{(\cosh(s)-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{f(t)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{se^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}}{\sinh(\frac{s}{2})}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{f(t)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{4t}}ds\text{ as }\sinh\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\geq\frac{s}{2}\text{ for positive }s

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t^{\frac{1}{2}}}{f(t)}.

where
f(t)
is the function introduced in Lemma
2.4
. The estimate is independent of
z
.

The upper bound can then be derived from
[
Gri97
, Theorem 1.1]
, the assumption of which is satisfied by
\frac{f(t)}{t^{\frac{1}{2}}}=te^{\frac{t}{4}}
(see Section 2, Example 2.). Thus, there exist some positive constants
\alpha,K,\gamma
such that for any
(z,z^{\prime})\in\left(\mathbb{H}^{2}\right)^{2}
, one has

H(t,z,z^{\prime})\leq\frac{K(\gamma t)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{f(\gamma t)}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{t}d_{g}(z,z^{\prime})^{2}}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Appendix B
Spectral estimate implies observability

In this Appendix, we prove that on any set where the spectral estimate (

[

The proof is divided into two parts. In the first one, we use the spectral inequality to prove the interpolation estimate (

[

]
in the simpler case where

equals

rather than any positive-measure subset.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Assume that (
1.3
) holds for the set
\omega
and consider a solution
u
of the heat equation (
2.16
) on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Fix
T>0
and denote
u_{0}=u(0,\cdot)
. Then,

u(T,z)=e^{T\Delta_{g}}u_{0}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{H}^{2}.

We first prove that
u
satisfies the following Hölder-type inequality for some constant
\tilde{C}
:

(B.1)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(T,z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}\leq\exp\left(\tilde{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{T}\right)\right)\left(\int_{\omega}|u(T,z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u_{0}(z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Denote
\Pi_{>\Lambda}
the spectral projector defined by

\Pi_{>\Lambda}u=\int_{\Lambda}^{+\infty}dm_{\lambda}u,\quad u\in L^{2}_{g}(\mathbb{H}^{2}),

then
u_{0}=\Pi_{\Lambda}u_{0}+\Pi_{>\Lambda}u_{0}
. By the spectral estimate (
1.3
), there exists some
K>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(T,z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|e^{T\Delta_{g}}\Pi_{\Lambda}u_{0}(z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}+2\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|e^{T\Delta_{g}}\Pi_{>\Lambda}u_{0}(z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}

\displaystyle\leq 2Ke^{K\Lambda}\int_{\omega}|e^{T\Delta_{g}}\Pi_{\Lambda}u_{0}(z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}+2\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|e^{T\Delta_{g}}\Pi_{>\Lambda}u_{0}(z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}

\displaystyle\leq 4Ke^{K\Lambda}\int_{\omega}|e^{T\Delta_{g}}u_{0}(z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}+(2+4Ke^{K\Lambda})\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|e^{T\Delta_{g}}\Pi_{>\Lambda}u_{0}(z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}.

Since
\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|e^{T\Delta_{g}}\Pi_{>\Lambda}u_{0}(z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}\leq e^{-T\Lambda^{2}}\|\Pi_{>\Lambda}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{g}(\mathbb{H}^{2})}^{2}\leq e^{-T\Lambda^{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{g}(\mathbb{H}^{2})}^{2}
, it follows that

(B.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(T,z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}

\displaystyle\leq 4Ke^{K\Lambda}\int_{\omega}|e^{T\Delta_{g}}u_{0}(z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}+(2+4Ke^{K\Lambda})e^{-T\Lambda^{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{g}(\mathbb{H}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 6K\left(e^{K\Lambda}\int_{\omega}|e^{T\Delta_{g}}u_{0}(z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}+e^{K\Lambda-T\Lambda^{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{g}(\mathbb{H}^{2})}^{2}\right)

Fix
\eta\in(0,1)
and
\Lambda
such that
e^{K\Lambda-T\Lambda^{2}}=\eta
, then one has

\Lambda=\frac{K+\sqrt{K^{2}+4T\log\frac{1}{\eta}}}{2T}\leq\frac{1}{T}\left(K+\sqrt{T\log\frac{1}{\eta}}\right).

Thus,

\displaystyle e^{K\Lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{K^{2}}{T}\right)\exp\left(\frac{K}{\sqrt{T}}\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{\eta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{K^{2}}{T}\right)\exp\left(\frac{K^{2}}{2T}+\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1}{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{3K^{2}}{2T}\right)\eta^{-1}.

Setting

\eta=\left(\frac{\int_{\omega}|u(T,z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}}{\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u_{0}(z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<1

yields estimate (
B.1
).

Then, by (
B.1
) and translation in time, one gets that for
0<t_{1}<t_{2}
and
\varepsilon>0
,

(B.3)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(t_{2},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}e^{2\tilde{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\right)}\int_{\omega}|u(t_{2},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(t_{1},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}.

Now set
l_{1}=T,\lambda\in(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},1)
and
l_{m+1}=\lambda^{m}l_{1}
for
m\geq 0
. Take an integer
m
and some
s
such that

0<l_{m+2}<l_{m+1}\leq s<l_{m}\leq T,

then by (
B.3
),

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(l_{m},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}e^{2\tilde{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{l_{m+1}-l_{m+2}}\right)}\int_{\omega}|u(s,z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(l_{m+2},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}.

Integrating over
s\in(l_{m+1},l_{m}),
we get

(B.4)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(l_{m},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}\\
\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(l_{m+2},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{l_{m}-l_{m+1}}e^{2\tilde{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{l_{m+1}-l_{m+2}}\right)}\int_{l_{m+1}}^{l_{m}}\int_{\omega}|u(s,z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}ds.

Using the inequality
l_{m}-l_{m+1}\geq e^{-\frac{1}{l_{m}-l_{m+1}}}
, and the identities

l_{m}-l_{m+1}=\frac{1}{1+\lambda}(l_{m}-l_{m+2}),\quad l_{m+1}-l_{m+2}=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}(l_{m}-l_{m+2}),

we obtain

(B.5)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(l_{m},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}\\
\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(l_{m+2},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}e^{2\tilde{C}}e^{\frac{C^{\prime}}{l_{m}-l_{m+2}}}\int_{l_{m+1}}^{l_{m}}\int_{\omega}|u(s,z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}ds

with
C^{\prime}=1+\lambda+\frac{2\tilde{C}(1+\lambda)}{\lambda}
. (
B.5
) can be rewritten as

(B.6)

\varepsilon e^{-\frac{C^{\prime}}{l_{m}-l_{m+2}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(l_{m},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}-\varepsilon^{2}e^{-\frac{C^{\prime}}{l_{m}-l_{m+2}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(l_{m+2},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}\\
\leq e^{2\tilde{C}}\int_{l_{m+1}}^{l_{m}}\int_{\omega}|u(s,z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}ds.

Setting
\mu:=\frac{1}{2-\lambda^{-2}}>1
and
\varepsilon=\exp\left[-\frac{(\mu-1)C^{\prime}}{l_{m}-l_{m+2}}\right]
yields

(B.7)

e^{-\frac{\mu C^{\prime}}{l_{m}-l_{m+2}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(l_{m},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}-e^{-\frac{(2\mu-1)C^{\prime}}{l_{m}-l_{m+2}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(l_{m+2},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}\\
\leq e^{2\tilde{C}}\int_{l_{m+1}}^{l_{m}}\int_{\omega}|u(s,z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}ds.

Now

\frac{2\mu-1}{l_{m}-l_{m+2}}=\frac{\mu}{\lambda^{2}(l_{m}-l_{m+2})}=\frac{\mu}{l_{m+2}-l_{m+4}},

hence

(B.8)

e^{-\frac{\mu C^{\prime}}{l_{m}-l_{m+2}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(l_{m},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}-e^{-\frac{\mu C^{\prime}}{l_{m+2}-l_{m+4}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(l_{m+2},z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}\\
\leq e^{2\tilde{C}}\int_{l_{m+1}}^{l_{m}}\int_{\omega}|u(s,z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}ds.

Summing this inequality over all odd integers
m
, we obtain

\ e^{\frac{\mu C^{\prime}}{l_{1}-l_{3}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|u(l_{1},x)|^{2}dx\leq e^{2\tilde{C}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\omega}|u(s,z)|^{2}d\vol_{g}ds,

which in turn gives the observability estimate (
2.17
).
∎

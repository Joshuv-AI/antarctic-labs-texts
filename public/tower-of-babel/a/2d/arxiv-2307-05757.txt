Introduction

Two of the most fundamental results in graph theory are the necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of perfect matchings in bipartite graphs due to Hall’s “marriage theorem”
[
11
]
and in general graphs due to Tutte’s “odd component count”
[
27
]
.
However, the situation is less understood and also more complicated in hypergraphs.
For example, while we know how to detect perfect matchings in graphs in polynomial time
[
9
,
21
]
and in bipartite graphs even in near-linear time
[
5
]
, the problem is NP-complete already in
3
-uniform
3
-partite hypergraphs
[
15
]
, indicating that there is (in all likelihood) no nice necessary and sufficient criterium for perfect matchings in hypergraphs.

A simple application of Hall’s marriage theorem shows that every regular (all vertices have the same degree) bipartite graph admits a perfect matching.
However, this does not hold for hypergraphs, as for example illustrated by the
2
-regular hypergraph in Figure
1
which admits no perfect matching.
For some integer
r\geq 2
, a hypergraph
H=(V,E)
is
r
-uniform if every edge
e\in E
consists of exactly
r
vertices.
That is,
2
-uniform hypergraphs are just graphs, while the hypergraph in Figure
1
is
3
-uniform.
Moreover,
H=(V,E)
is
r
-partite if its vertices can be partitioned into
r
parts such that each edge contains exactly one vertex of each part.
That is,
2
-uniform
2
-partite hypergraphs are exactly the bipartite graphs, while the hypergraph in Figure
1
is
3
-partite with the a
3
-partition indicated by the shapes of the vertices.
Evidently, as soon as
r\geq 3
, not all regular
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraphs admit perfect matchings.

Figure 1:
A
2
-regular
3
-uniform
3
-partite hypergraph with no perfect matching.

We are concerned with a relaxation of perfect matchings, called shallow hitting edge sets.
While
M
is a perfect matching if every vertex has exactly one incident edge in
M
, an edge subset
M
is a
t
-shallow hitting edge set
for a positive integer

if every vertex has at least one incident edge in
M
(this is the “hitting” part) and at most
t
incident edges in
M
(this is the “

For example, any set of three or four edges in the hypergraph
H
in Figure
1
is a
2
-shallow hitting edge set of
H
.
In this paper, we shall present sufficient conditions for
t
and
r
for which every regular
r
-uniform hypergraph admits a
t
-shallow hitting edge set.

Of course, if a graph or hypergraph admits no perfect matching, it is interesting to prove lower bounds on the size of its largest matchings, for example for regular graphs
[
13
]
.
In this paper, we shall present for regular
n
-vertex
r
-uniform hypergraphs
H
lower bounds for the size of their maximum
t
-shallow edge sets in terms of
n
,
r
and
t
.

Another simple application of Hall’s marriage theorem shows that every bipartite graph with
n
vertices in each part and minimum degree at least

admits a perfect matching.
Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
25
]
, see also Kühn and Osthus
[

]
, extend this by showing that every

-vertex

-uniform hypergraph

with large enough

in terms of

and

(see Section

Similarly, every
r
-uniform

-partite hypergraph with parts of size
n
and large enough

in terms of

and

admits a perfect matching
[
19
,
1
]
.
In this paper, we extend these results to

-shallow hitting edge sets.

1.2
Related work

Let us just briefly mention some further related work on matchings in hypergraphs.
Until now, there is no generalization of Hall’s Theorem or Tutte’s Theorem to hypergraphs.
There is an equivalence for the existence of perfect matchings in so-called balanced hypergraphs, see
[
7
]
and
[
14
]
.
On the other hand, there are two ways to extend the sufficient condition of Hall’s Theorem to uniform bipartite hypergraphs (here, a hypergraph
H=(V,E)
is bipartite if there exists a partition
V=A\dot{\cup}B
such that every edge has exactly one vertex in
A
).
Haxell
[
12
]
gave a sufficient condition in terms of minimum vertex covers while Aharoni and Haxell
[
2
]
gave a sufficient condition in terms of maximum matchings.
Both theorems are equivalent to Hall’s Theorem in the case of bipartite graphs, but do not give a necessary condition in general.

The concept of
t
-shallow hitting edge sets has been considered for graphs implicitly under several names.
Specifically, there is a lot of research on subgraphs
G^{\prime}
of a given graph
G
such that for each vertex
v
its number of incident edges in
G^{\prime}
lies in some predefined set
S_{v}
, as introduced by Lovász
[
20
]
.
(It would be
S_{v}=\{1,\ldots,t\}
for
t
-shallow hitting edge sets.)
Let us just mention that Berge and Las Vergnas
[
4
]
give a Hall-type characterization for the case of bipartite graphs, as well as a Tutte-type characterization for general graphs.

Finally, hitting edge sets of a hypergraph
H
are equivalent to “hitting vertex sets” of the dual hypergraph
H^{*}
of
H
.
Such hitting vertex sets are also known as vertex covers, transversals, or hitting sets in the literature.
Shallow hitting (vertex) sets are for example a standard tool for coloring geometric hypergraphs
[
17
]
.

Preliminaries

A hypergraph is a tuple

1

For a vertex

2
Shallow Hitting Edge Sets in Uniform Near-Regular Hypergraphs

2.1
Upper bound for the general case

Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
be events.
A graph
G=(V,E)
is a
dependency graph
of these events if
V=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\}
and the following holds for all
i
.
For each event
A_{i}
and each set
S
of non-adjacent events with
A_{i}\notin S
it holds that
\Prob\left[A_{i}\mid\bigwedge_{A_{j}\in S}\overline{A}_{j}\right]\leq\Prob[A_{i}]
.
The
degree of dependence
of the events
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
is the smallest possible maximum degree of a dependency graph.
If each “bad” event
A_{i}
only occurs with small probability and each bad event is dependent of a small amount of other bad events, the Lovász Local Lemma is a tool to prove that it is possible that no bad event occurs.

Lemma 2.1

(Lovász Local Lemma
[
26
]
)
.

Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
be events with
\Prob[A_{i}]\leq p
and let
d
be their degree of dependence.
If
\mathrm{e}p(d+1)\leq 1
, then
\Prob[\overline{A}_{1}\overline{A}_{2}\cdots\overline{A}_{n}]>0
.

Remark 2.2
.

In the original Lovász Local Lemma, the notion of a dependency graph is replaced by a slightly stronger definition in which each event
A_{i}
must be mutually independent of all non-adjacent events
A_{j}
with
i\neq j
.
It can be seen by the proof in
[
3
]
that the Lovász Local Lemma remains true if we use the weaker notion of a dependency graph as defined above.
In Lemma
2.1
, we will use the original version of the Lovász Local Lemma (which is included as a special case in Lemma
2.1
).
In Lemma
2.2
, we need the weaker notion of a dependency graph and therefore the slightly stronger Lovász Local Lemma as in Lemma
2.1
.

In the next lemma, we want to find shallow hitting edge sets in uniform near-regular hypergraphs.
For this, we use a random experiment that generates a hitting edge set
M
.
Then, we only need to ensure the condition that
M
is also shallow which will be done using the Lovász Local Lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
H=(V,E)
be an
r
-uniform
\mu
-near-regular hypergraph and let
t
be a positive integer satisfying

\frac{t!}{t+1}\geq\mathrm{e}\mu^{t+1}r^{t+3}~.

(1)

Then,
H
has a
t
-shallow hitting edge set.

Proof.

Let
\Delta
be the maximum degree and
\delta
be the minimum degree of
H
.
We build an edge set
M\subseteq E
with the following random experiment.
For each vertex
v\in V
, we pick an incident edge
e\in\incident(v)
uniformly at random and add it to the edge set
M
.
Clearly,
M
is a hitting edge set.
Observe that for
t\geq\Delta
,
M
is clearly
t
-shallow.
Thereby, assume that
t<\Delta
.
We use the Lovász Local Lemma to prove that there exists an edge set
M
that also satisfies
\degree_{M}(v)\leq t
for all vertices
v\in V
.
For a set
F
of edges, denote by
V(F)
the set of vertices
\bigcup_{e\in F}\,e
.

Define the set
\mathcal{F}
to be the set of all edge sets
F\subseteq E
of size
t+1
such that there exists a vertex
v\in V
with
F\subseteq\incident(v)
, i.e.,

\mathcal{F}=\{F\subseteq E\colon|F|=t+1,F\subseteq\incident(v)\text{ for some }v\in V\}~.

For a set
F\in\mathcal{F}
, we denote the event that
F\subseteq M
by
A_{F}
.
These are the bad events used in the Lovász Local Lemma.

To apply the Lovász Local Lemma, we have to find a bound
p
such that
\Prob[A_{F}]\leq p
holds for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
.
For a vertex
v\in V
, we denote by
P_{v}
the random variable with range
\incident(v)
that describes which edge is picked at the vertex
v
.
Moreover, for
e\in E
and
v\in e
, we define
B_{e,v}
to be the event that
P_{v}=e
and
B_{e}
to be the event that
e\in M
.
Since every vertex has degree at least
\delta
, it holds that
\Prob[B_{e,v}]=\Prob[P_{v}=e]\leq 1/\delta
for all
e\in E
and
v\in e
.
For an edge
e\in E
, we can now bound the probability for the event
B_{e}
by

\Prob[B_{e}]=\Prob\left[\bigvee_{v\in e}B_{e,v}\right]\leq\sum_{v\in e}\Prob[B_{e,v}]\leq\frac{r}{\delta}~.

For
e\in E
and
F\subseteq E
with
e\notin F
, it holds that
\Prob[B_{e}\mid\cap_{e^{\prime}\in F}B_{e^{\prime}}]\leq\Prob[B_{e}]
and hence
\Prob[B_{e}\cap\left(\cap_{e^{\prime}\in F}B_{e^{\prime}}\right)]\leq\Prob[B_{e}]\cdot\Prob[\cap_{e^{\prime}\in F}B_{e^{\prime}}]
.
Thus, for each
F\in\mathcal{F}
, the probability that the event
A_{F}
occurs can be bounded by

\Prob[A_{F}]=\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{e\in F}B_{e}\right]\leq\prod_{e\in F}\Prob[B_{e}]\leq\left(\frac{r}{\delta}\right)^{t+1}~.

We construct a dependency graph
G_{\text{D}}=(V_{\text{D}},E_{\text{D}})
with
V_{\text{D}}=\{A_{F}\mid F\in\mathcal{F}\}
.
Two events
A_{F}
and
A_{F^{\prime}}
are adjacent in
G_{\text{D}}
if and only if
V(F)\cap V(F^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
.
Note that the event
B_{e}
is mutually independent of all events
B_{e^{\prime}}
with
e\cap e^{\prime}=\emptyset
.
Thus, each event
A_{F}
for
F\in\mathcal{F}
is mutually independent of all non-adjacent events
A_{F^{\prime}}
.

In the next step, we have to bound the degree of dependence of these events.
For an edge
e\in E
and a vertex
v\in e
, denote by
\mathcal{F}_{e,v}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
the set of edge sets
F\in\mathcal{F}
with
e\in F
and
F\subseteq\incident(v)
.
We bound the size of the sets
\mathcal{F}_{e,v}
.
The edge
e
is contained in all sets
F\in\mathcal{F}_{e,v}
and the vertex
v
has degree at most
\Delta
.
Since every edge set
F\in\mathcal{F}
has size exactly
t+1
, there are at most
\binom{\Delta-1}{t}
ways to choose the remaining
t
edges (which must be incident with
v
).
Thus,

|\mathcal{F}_{e,v}|\leq\binom{\Delta-1}{t}\quad\text{for all }e\in E\text{ and }v\in e~.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}
be an arbitrary but fixed set of edges.
In order to bound the degree of dependence, we count the number of edge sets
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
with
V(F)\cap V(F^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
.
Let
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
be an arbitrary edge set with
A_{F^{\prime}}
adjacent to
A_{F}
in the dependency graph
G_{\text{D}}
.
Since
V(F)\cap V(F^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
, there must exist a vertex
\tilde{v}
in
V(F)\cap V(F^{\prime})
with an edge
e^{\prime}
in
F^{\prime}
which is incident to
\tilde{v}
.
By definition of
\mathcal{F}
, there is a vertex
v^{\prime}\in V
with
F\subseteq\incident(v^{\prime})
.
As
e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}
, this vertex must be incident to
e^{\prime}
, i.e.,
v^{\prime}\in e^{\prime}
.
Thus,
F^{\prime}
is in the set
\mathcal{F}_{e^{\prime},v^{\prime}}
.
Since the set
F
itself fulfills all the described properties, it is also in a set
\mathcal{F}_{e,v}
and we count it too.
We can now bound the degree of dependence
d
by

d+1\leq\max_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\left|\bigcup\limits_{\tilde{v}\in V(F)}\bigcup\limits_{e^{\prime}\in\incident(\tilde{v})}\bigcup\limits_{v^{\prime}\in e^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}_{e^{\prime},v^{\prime}}\right|\leq(t+1)r\cdot\Delta\cdot r\cdot\binom{\Delta-1}{t}~.

Since we assumed
\Delta-1\geq t
, it holds that

\binom{\Delta-1}{t}\leq\frac{(\Delta-1)^{t}}{t!}\leq\frac{\Delta^{t}}{t!}

and thus

d+1\leq\frac{t+1}{t!}r^{2}\Delta^{t+1}~.

(2)

To apply the Lovász Local Lemma, we calculate
\mathrm{e}p(d+1)
.
With Equation (
1
), it follows that

\mathrm{e}p(d+1)\leq\mathrm{e}\cdot\left(\frac{r}{\delta}\right)^{t+1}\cdot\frac{t+1}{t!}r^{2}\Delta^{t+1}\leq\mathrm{e}\frac{t+1}{t!}\mu^{t+1}r^{t+3}\leq 1~.

By the Lovász Local Lemma, the probability that no event
A_{F}
with
F\in\mathcal{F}
occurs is greater than zero.
Thus, there exists an edge set
M\subseteq E
such that every vertex has degree
1\leq\degree_{M}(v)\leq t
in
M
.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Every
r
-uniform
\mu
-near-regular hypergraph has a
t(\mu,r)
-shallow hitting edge set, where
t(\mu,r)=\mathrm{e}\mu r(1+o_{\mu r}(1))
.

Proof.

We show that

t=1+\left\lceil\mathrm{e}\mu r\left(\mathrm{e}^{2}\mu^{2}r^{4}\right)^{1/(\mathrm{e}\mu r)}\right\rceil=\mathrm{e}\mu r(1+o_{\mu r}(1))

satisfies Equation (
1
).
Indeed, with Stirling’s Formula
t!\geq\sqrt{2\pi t}(t/\mathrm{e})^{t}
and with
(t+1)/\sqrt{2\pi t}\leq t
for
t\geq 1
it follows that

\begin{split}\mathrm{e}\frac{t+1}{t!}\mu^{t+1}r^{t+3}&\leq\mathrm{e}\frac{t+1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\mu r^{3}\cdot\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}\mu r}{t}\right)^{t}\leq\mathrm{e}^{2}\mu^{2}r^{4}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}\mu r}{t-1}\right)^{t-1}\\
&\leq\mathrm{e}^{2}\mu^{2}r^{4}\left(\mathrm{e}^{2}\mu^{2}r^{4}\right)^{-(\mathrm{e}^{2}\mu^{2}r^{4})^{1/(\mathrm{e}\mu r)}}\leq 1~.\end{split}

∎

2.2
Upper bound for girth at least four

In the next theorem, we show a better bound for uniform near-regular hypergraphs without small cycles.
Let
H=(V,E)
be a hypergraph.
A
cycle of length
k
is a sequence
v_{1}e_{1}v_{2}e_{2}\dots v_{k}e_{k}v_{k+1}
of distinct vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k}
and distinct edges
e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{k}
such that
v_{1}=v_{k+1}
and
\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}\subseteq e_{i}
for
i=1,2,\dots,k
.
The
girth
of a hypergraph
H
is the smallest possible length of a cycle in
H
.
In Lemma
2.2
and Theorem
2.11
, we prove a better bound for uniform regular hypergraphs of girth at least
4
.

For that, we need the following lemma.
It says that if we have two events
A
and
B
that are independent by conditioning on the set
\{\tilde{C}_{1},\dots,\tilde{C}_{n}\}
of events for all
\tilde{C}_{i}\in\{C_{i},\overline{C}_{i}\}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, and each event
C_{i}
decreases the probability that
A
occurs but raises the probability that
B
occurs, then
\Prob[A\mid B]\leq\Prob[A]
.
Moreover, we use the abbreviation

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{C}_{i}\in\{C_{i},\overline{C}_{i}\}\\
i=1,\dots,k\end{subarray}}f(\tilde{C}_{1},\dots,\tilde{C}_{k})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{C}_{i}\in\{C_{i},\overline{C}_{i}\}\\
i=1,\dots,k-1\end{subarray}}\;\sum_{\tilde{C}_{k}\in\{C_{k},\overline{C}_{k}\}}f(\tilde{C}_{1},\dots,\tilde{C}_{k})

for
k\geq 1
and with

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{C}_{i}\in\{C_{i},\overline{C}_{i}\}\\
i\in\emptyset\end{subarray}}a=a~.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A
,
B
and
C_{1},\dots,C_{n}
be events such that the following holds for all events
\tilde{C}_{i}\in\{C_{i},\overline{C}_{i}\}
,
i=1,\dots,n
,

1.

\Prob\left[A\mid B,\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]=\Prob\left[A\mid\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]
,

2.

\Prob\left[A\mid C_{k+1},\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]\leq\Prob\left[A\mid\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]
for all
0\leq k<n
,

3.

\Prob\left[B\mid C_{k+1},\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]\geq\Prob\left[B\mid\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]
for all
0\leq k<n
.

Then,
\Prob[A\mid B]\leq\Prob[A]
.

Proof.

We show that the inequality

\Prob\left[A\mid B\right]\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{C}_{i}\in\{C_{i},\overline{C}_{i}\}\\
i=1,\dots,k\end{subarray}}\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\mid B\right]\Prob\left[A\mid\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]

(3)

holds for all
k=0,1,\dots,n
by induction on
n-k
.
Clearly, for
k=0
, it follows that
\Prob[A\mid B]\leq\Prob[A]
.
For the base case
k=n
it holds that

\Prob\left[A\mid B\right]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{C}_{i}\in\{C_{i},\overline{C}_{i}\}\\
i=1,\dots,n\end{subarray}}\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{C}_{i}\mid B\right]\Prob\left[A\mid B,\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]~.

With Condition
1
of Lemma
2.2
, the base case
k=n
of Equation (
3
) follows.
Now, suppose that
0\leq k<n
and that the inequality holds for
k+1
.
Then, we use the induction hypothesis to get

\begin{split}\Prob\left[A\mid B\right]&\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{C}_{i}\in\{C_{i},\overline{C}_{i}\}\\
i=1,\dots,k+1\end{subarray}}\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k+1}\tilde{C}_{i}\mid B\right]\Prob\left[A\mid\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k+1}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{C}_{i}\in\{C_{i},\overline{C}_{i}\}\\
i=1,\dots,k\end{subarray}}\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\mid B\right]\cdot X\end{split}

with

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Prob\left[C_{k+1}\mid B,\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]\Prob\left[A\mid C_{k+1},\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]+\Prob\left[\overline{C}_{k+1}\mid B,\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]\Prob\left[A\mid\overline{C}_{k+1},\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Prob\left[C_{k+1}\mid B,\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]\underbrace{\left(\Prob\left[A\mid C_{k+1},\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]-\Prob\left[A\mid\overline{C}_{k+1},\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]\right)}_{\leq 0}

\displaystyle+\Prob\left[A\mid\overline{C}_{k+1},\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]~.

It follows from Condition
2
that
\Prob\left[A\mid C_{k+1},\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]\leq\Prob\left[A\mid\overline{C}_{k+1},\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]
and it follows from Condition
3
that
\Prob\left[C_{k+1}\mid B,\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]\geq\Prob\left[C_{k+1}\mid\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]
.
We use the second inequality and get

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Prob\left[C_{k+1}\mid\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]\Prob\left[A\mid C_{k+1},\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]+\Prob\left[\overline{C}_{k+1}\mid\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]\Prob\left[A\mid\overline{C}_{k+1},\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Prob\left[A\mid\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{C}_{i}\right]

and the induction step is proven.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
H=(V,E)
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph of girth at least
4
with minimum degree
\delta\geq\ln r+2
and maximum degree
\Delta
.
Let
\eta=\Delta/(\delta-1)
and let
t
be a positive integer satisfying

\frac{t!}{t+1}\geq\mathrm{e}r^{2}\eta^{t+1}(\ln r+1+\mathrm{e}^{-1})^{t+1}~.

(4)

Then
H
has a
t
-shallow hitting edge set.

Proof.

We build an edge set
M
with the following two-step random experiment.
In the first step, we pick each edge in
E
independently at random with probability
(\ln r+1)/(\delta-1)
.
In the second step, we pick for every vertex
v
that is not covered after the first step an incident edge independently uniformly at random.
Clearly,
M
is a hitting edge set.
For an edge set
F
, we denote by
V(F)
the set of vertices
\bigcup_{e\in F}e
.
For a set
\mathcal{F}
of edge sets, we denote by
V(\mathcal{F})
the set of vertices
\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}V(F)
.

For each edge
e\in E
, we define
X_{e}
to be the event that
e
is picked in the first step.
For
each
vertex
v
(independently of the results of the first step), we pick an incident edge independently uniformly at random and denote by
Y_{v}\in\incident(v)
the random variable that indicates which edge is picked, and by
Y_{v,e}
the event that
Y_{v}=e
.
Then,

W_{e}=\bigvee_{v\in e}\left(Y_{v,e}\wedge\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in\incident(v)}\overline{X}_{e^{\prime}}\right)

denotes the event that
e
is picked in the second step and
Z_{e}=X_{e}\vee W_{e}
is the event that
e
is picked in the first or second step.
Define the set
\mathcal{F}
to be the set of all edge sets
F\subseteq E
of size
t+1
such that there exists a vertex
v\in V
with
F\subseteq\incident(v)
, i.e.,

\mathcal{F}=\{F\subseteq E\colon|F|=t+1,F\subseteq\incident(v)\text{ for some }v\in V\}~.

For a set
F\in\mathcal{F}
, we denote the event that
F\subseteq M
by
A_{F}
, i.e.,
A_{F}=\bigwedge_{e\in F}Z_{e}
.
These shall be the bad events in the Lovász Local Lemma below.

We need the following observation, which easily follows from
H
having girth at least
4
.

Observation 2.7
.

For any two distinct edges
e,e^{\prime}\in E
,

1.

|e\cap e^{\prime}|\leq 1
and

2.

if
e\cap e^{\prime}=\{u\}
for some
u\in V
then there exists no edge
f\in E\setminus\{e,e^{\prime}\}
with
e\cap f=\{v\}
and
e^{\prime}\cap f=\{v^{\prime}\}
for
v,v^{\prime}\in E\setminus\{u\}
.

Now, we need to bound the probability that a bad event
A_{F}
occurs.
Therefore, we show that
\Prob\left[Z_{e}\mid\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}Z_{e^{\prime}}\right]\leq\Prob[Z_{e}]
, which will be done in the following two claims and using Observation
2.2
.

Claim 2.8
.

Let
F^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
be an edge set with
\bigcap_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}e^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
.
Then,
\Prob[\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}Z_{e^{\prime}}\mid\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}\overline{X}_{e^{\prime}}]\leq\Prob[\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}Z_{e^{\prime}}]

Let
v
be a vertex in
\bigcap_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}e^{\prime}
, which is unique for

(Observation
2.2

First, we bound the right-hand side:

\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}Z_{e^{\prime}}\right]\geq\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}X_{e^{\prime}}\right]=\prod_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}\Prob[X_{e^{\prime}}]=\left(\frac{\ln r+1}{\delta-1}\right)^{|F^{\prime}|}~,

where we used that the events
X_{e^{\prime}}
are independent.
Now, we bound the left-hand side as follows:

\displaystyle\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}Z_{e^{\prime}}\mid\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}\overline{X}_{e^{\prime}}\right]=\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}W_{e^{\prime}}\mid\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}\overline{X}_{e^{\prime}}\right]

(5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Prob\left[Y_{v}\in F^{\prime}\wedge\bigwedge_{g\in\incident(v)\setminus F^{\prime}}\overline{X}_{g}\right]\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}W_{e^{\prime}}\mid Y_{v}\in F^{\prime},\bigwedge_{g\in\incident(v)}\overline{X}_{g}\right]

\displaystyle+\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}W_{e^{\prime}}\mid\left(Y_{v}\notin F^{\prime}\vee\bigvee_{g\in\incident(v)\setminus F^{\prime}}X_{g}\right),\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}\overline{X}_{e^{\prime}}\right]

Then, we explicitly calculate the probabilities using Observation
2.2
.
First, it holds that

For the calculation of the other probabilities, we need the following bounds.
Let
e,e^{\prime}\in\incident(v)
be two distinct edges and
v^{\prime}\in e^{\prime}\setminus\{v\}
.
Then,

\begin{split}&\Prob\left[Y_{v^{\prime},e^{\prime}}\wedge\bigwedge_{e^{\prime\prime}\in\incident(v^{\prime})}\overline{X}_{e^{\prime\prime}}\mid\overline{X}_{e^{\prime}}\right]=\Prob\left[Y_{v^{\prime},e^{\prime}}\wedge\bigwedge_{e^{\prime\prime}\in\incident(v^{\prime})\setminus\{e^{\prime}\}}\overline{X}_{e^{\prime\prime}}\right]\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\left(1-\frac{\ln r-1}{\delta-1}\right)^{\delta-1}\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}\delta r}\end{split}

and

\Prob\left[W_{e^{\prime}}\mid Y_{v}=e,\overline{X}_{e^{\prime}}\right]\leq\sum_{v^{\prime}\in e^{\prime}\setminus\{v\}}\Prob\left[Y_{v^{\prime},e^{\prime}}\wedge\bigwedge_{e^{\prime\prime}\in\incident{v^{\prime}}}X_{e^{\prime\prime}}\mid\overline{X}_{e^{\prime}}\right]\leq\frac{r-1}{\mathrm{e}\delta r}\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}\delta}~.

Here we used

for

Finally, we can bound the other probabilities occurring in Equation
5

Let
e\in F^{\prime}
be fixed. Then,

\begin{split}&\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}W_{e^{\prime}}\mid Y_{v}\in F^{\prime},\bigwedge_{g\in\incident(v)}\overline{X}_{g}\right]\leq\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}\setminus\{e\}}W_{e^{\prime}}\mid Y_{v}=e,\bigwedge_{g\in\incident(v)}\overline{X}_{g}\right]\\
&=\prod_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}\setminus\{e\}}\Prob\left[W_{e^{\prime}}\mid Y_{v}=e,\overline{X}_{e^{\prime}}\right]\leq\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}\delta}\right)^{|F^{\prime}|-1}~.\end{split}

Analogously, we bound using some fixed

\begin{split}&\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}W_{e^{\prime}}\mid\left(Y_{v}\notin F^{\prime}\vee\bigvee_{g\in\incident(v)\setminus F^{\prime}}X_{g}\right),\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}\overline{X}_{e^{\prime}}\right]\\

In total, we get

\Prob\left[\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}Z_{e^{\prime}}\mid\bigwedge_{e^{\prime}\in F^{\prime}}\overline{X}_{e^{\prime}}\right]\leq(\mathrm{e}|F^{\prime}|+1)\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}\delta}\right)^{|F^{\prime}|}\leq(\mathrm{e}|F^{\prime}|+1)\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}(\delta-1)}\right)^{|F^{\prime}|}~.

Hence, in order to show the claim, we only need to show that

.
Since

This finishes the proof of this claim.

To apply the Lovász Local Lemma, we calculate

With Equation (

\mathrm{e}p(d+1)\leq\mathrm{e}\cdot\left(\frac{\ln r+1+\mathrm{e}^{-1}}{\delta-1}\right)^{t+1}\cdot\frac{t+1}{t!}r^{2}\Delta^{t+1}\leq\mathrm{e}r^{2}\eta^{t+1}\left(\ln r+1+\mathrm{e}^{-1}\right)^{t+1}\frac{t+1}{t!}\leq 1~.

By the Lovász Local Lemma, the probability that no event

Thus, there exists an edge set

in

∎

Using Lemma

Theorem 2.11

We show that

\begin{split}t&=1+\left\lceil\mathrm{e}^{1+h(\eta,r)}\eta(\ln r+1+\mathrm{e}^{-1})\right\rceil=\mathrm{e}^{1+W(2/(\mathrm{e}\eta))}\eta\ln(r)(1+o_{\eta r}(1))\end{split}

2.3

For the lower bounds, we seek to find for infinitely many values of

By using the dualization, we can employ constructions of

[

The currently best bound is due to Kostochka and Rödl, where we cite here only the case of two colors and girth at least four.

]
)
.

For any

-uniform

-regular non-

Remark 2.14
.

Kostochka and Rödl do not claim that

In fact, they start with any

Then, each hyperedge of

Now we can choose

to be the union of

perfect matchings (a random choice on a large enough vertex set will do), which implies that also

is the union of

Corollary 2.15
.

For any

-uniform

-partite

-regular hypergraph of girth at least four that has no

-shallow hitting edge set, where

Let

be the dual of the hypergraph

Then

is

is

-regular since

Finally, a

Theorem 2.16
.

Let

There exists an

-uniform

-partite

-regular hypergraph with two vertices per part that has no

Proof.

This construction is based on the construction of projective spaces of order

Let

be the finite field of two elements and let

be the set of vertices.
For a vector

, we define

to be the set of vertices

with

.
Then, we define the edge set to be

.
The vertices of

and the edges correspond to the

-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t+1}

Let

be an edge.
Since

, there are exactly

-uniform with

.

Let

Analogous, there are
2^{t}-1

(2^{t}-1)
-regular.

Observe that each
t

Indeed, let
L(a_{1}),\dots,L(a_{t})
be

Consider the matrix

.
Then,

.
Thus, there is at least one nonzero solution

.
Then,

.

From

, we construct an

-uniform

-partite

.

.

-regular and

Since every

-shallow hitting edge set.

We define that two vertices

.
Clearly, each edge

of

.
Thus, no edge of

-uniform

-partite with parts of size

Moreover, for every vertex

which are incident to both

and

Since

Then, there are

Thus, we removed

3
Maximum Shallow Edge Sets

In this section, we consider the maximum possible size of shallow edge sets in
r
-uniform
\mu
-near-regular hypergraphs.
In comparison to
t
-shallow hitting edge sets, we relax the condition
1\leq\degree(v)\leq t
to
\degree(v)\leq t
for all vertices
v\in V
.
Additionally, we are not interested in minimizing
t
for a given
r
and
\mu
(as for shallow hitting edge sets) but in maximizing the size of a
t
-shallow edge set for given
t
,
r
and
\mu
in
r
-uniform
\mu
-near-regular hypergraphs.
We show that every
r
-uniform
\mu
-near-regular hypergraph
H
with
n
vertices has a
t
-shallow edge set of size

\Omega\left(\frac{nt}{\mu r^{1+1/t}}\right)~,

whenever
\Delta\geq t
for the maximum degree
\Delta
of
H
.
Moreover, we show in Section
3.2
, that the lower bound above is tight for regular
r
-uniform hypergraphs.

For this, we use the following framework.
For a hypergraph
H
, let
\nu_{t}(H)
be the size of a maximum
t
-shallow edge set in
H
.
Note that
\nu_{1}(H)
is the size of a maximum matching in
H
.
Let
H
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph with
n
vertices.
Observe that
\nu_{t}(H)\leq nt/r
is an upper bound on the size of a
t
-shallow edge set in
H
since every vertex can be covered at most
t
times and every edge has size
r
.
If
H
is an
r
-uniform hypergraph with
n
vertices, we define by
\eta_{t}(H)=\nu_{t}(H)/(nt/r)
.
We are particularly interested in bounding
\eta_{t}(H)
for such hypergraphs
H
.
For this, let
\mathcal{H}_{t}(r,\mu)
be the set of all
r
-uniform
\mu
-near-regular hypergraphs with
\Delta(H)\geq t
.
We only consider such hypergraphs with
\Delta(H)\geq t
since for
\Delta(H)<t
, the whole edge set is trivially a maximum
t
-shallow edge set.
We define

\eta_{t}(r,\mu)=\inf_{H\in\mathcal{H}_{t}(r,\mu)}\eta_{t}(H)

Moreover, we define
\eta_{t}(r)=\eta_{t}(r,1)
.
Observe that
0\leq\eta_{t}(H)\leq 1
for every hypergraph
H\in\mathcal{H}_{t}(r,\mu)
and thus
0\leq\eta_{t}(r,\mu)\leq 1
for every positive integer
r
and every real number
\mu\geq 1
.

We show in Section
3.1
that

\eta_{t}(r,\mu)\geq\frac{1-o_{t}(1)}{\mathrm{e}}\cdot\mu^{-1}r^{-1/t}~.

This result is tight for
\mu=1
up to a constant factor.
In fact, we show that for every real number
\epsilon>0
there exist infinitely many positive integers
r
such that

\eta_{t}(r)\leq(1+\epsilon)\cdot r^{-1/t}

by providing a construction through combinatorial designs.

3.1
A lower bound for near-regular hypergraphs

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
r\geq 2
,
t
and
k
be positive integers.
Let
H=(V,E)
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph of maximum degree
\Delta
.
If

\frac{t!}{t+1}\left(\frac{k}{\Delta}\right)^{t}\geq\mathrm{e}r

(7)

then there exists a partition of
E
into
k
disjoint
t
-shallow edge sets
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}
.

Proof.

We consider the following random experiment.
For each edge
e\in E
, we pick a color
\chi(e)\in\{1,\dots,k\}
uniformly at random.
The colors correspond to the sets
M_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,k
.
We use the Lovász Local Lemma to prove that there exists an edge coloring such that no vertex has
t+1
incident edges of the same color.
For this, we define
\mathcal{F}
to be the set of all edge sets
F\subseteq E
of size
t+1
such that there exists a vertex
v\in V
with
F\subseteq\incident(v)
, i.e.

\mathcal{F}=\{F\subseteq E\colon|F|=t+1,F\subseteq\incident(v)\text{ for some }v\in V\}~.

For a set
F\in\mathcal{F}
, denote by
A_{F}
the event that all edges in
F
received the same color.
Clearly,

\Prob[A_{F}]=\frac{1}{k^{t}}=:p~.

We construct a dependency graph
G_{\text{D}}=(V_{\text{D}},E_{\text{D}})
with
V_{\text{D}}=\{A_{F}\mid F\in\mathcal{F}\}
and two events
A_{F}
and
A_{F^{\prime}}
adjacent if and only if
F\cap F^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
.
Then, each event
A_{F}
is mutually independent of all non-adjacent events
A_{F^{\prime}}
.

In the next step, we have to bound the degree of dependence
d
of the events
A_{F}
for
F\in\mathcal{F}
.
For an edge
e\in E
and a vertex
v\in V
, denote by
\mathcal{F}_{e,v}
the set of all edge sets
F\in\mathcal{F}
with
e\in F
and
F\subseteq\incident(v)
.
Note that the edge
e
is contained in all sets
F\in\mathcal{F}_{e,v}
.
Since
F\subseteq\incident(v)
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{e,v}
, there are at most
\binom{\Delta-1}{t}
ways to choose the remaining edges.
Hence, the size of each
\mathcal{F}_{e,v}
can be bounded by

|\mathcal{F}_{e,v}|\leq\binom{\Delta-1}{t}~.

Let
A_{F}
be an arbitrary but fixed event with
F\in\mathcal{F}
.
For every adjacent event
A_{F^{\prime}}
, it holds that
F\cap F^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
.
Thus, there must be an edge
e
in
F\cap F^{\prime}
.
Moreover, by the definition of
\mathcal{F}
, there exists a vertex
v
such that
F\subseteq\incident(v)
.
Then, the degree of dependence
d
can be bounded by

d+1\leq\max_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\left|\bigcup\limits_{e\in F}\bigcup\limits_{v\in e}\mathcal{F}_{e,v}\right|\leq(t+1)\cdot r\cdot\binom{\Delta-1}{t}\leq\frac{t+1}{t!}r\Delta^{t}~.

To apply the Lovász Local Lemma, we calculate
\mathrm{e}p(d+1)
and use Equation
7
:

\mathrm{e}p(d+1)\leq\frac{\mathrm{e}}{k^{t}}\cdot\frac{t+1}{t!}r\Delta^{t}\leq 1~.

By the Lovász Local Lemma, the probability that no event
A_{F}
with
F\in\mathcal{F}
occurs is greater than zero.
Thus, there exists an edge coloring
\chi\colon E\to\{1,\dots,k\}
such that no vertex has
t+1
incident edges of the same color.
For
i=1,\dots,k
, we define
M_{i}
to be the set of all edges of color
i
.
Then, each set
M_{i}
is
t
-shallow and it holds that
M_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}M_{k}=E
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
t
be positive integers.
Let
H=(V,E)
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph of maximum degree
\Delta\geq t
.
Then, there exists a partition of
E
into
k
disjoint
t
-shallow edge sets
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}
for

k=\frac{\mathrm{e}\Delta r^{1/t}}{t}\cdot(1+o_{t}(1))~.

Proof.

We prove the lemma by showing that

k=\left\lceil\frac{\mathrm{e}\Delta}{t}\left(\frac{t+1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\mathrm{e}r\right)^{1/t}\right\rceil

satisfies Equation
7
.
For that, we use Stirling’s Formula
t!\geq\sqrt{2\pi t}(t/\mathrm{e})^{t}
and the definition of
k
:

\left(\frac{k}{\Delta}\right)^{t}\frac{t!}{t+1}\geq\left(\frac{tk}{\Delta\mathrm{e}}\right)^{t}\frac{\sqrt{2\pi t}}{t+1}\geq\frac{t+1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\mathrm{e}r\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2\pi t}}{t+1}\geq\mathrm{e}r~.

By Lemma
3.1
, there exist
k
disjoint
t
-shallow edge sets
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}
with
M_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}M_{k}=E
.
With

\left(\frac{t+1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\mathrm{e}\right)^{1/t}=1+o_{t}(1)

it holds that

k=\frac{\mathrm{e}\Delta r^{1/t}}{t}\cdot(1+o_{t}(1))~.

∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
n
,
t
and
r\geq 2
be positive integers and
\mu\geq 1
be a real number.
Let
H=(V,E)
be an
r
-uniform
\mu
-near-regular hypergraph with
n

\Delta\geq t
.
Then there exists a
t
-shallow edge set of size at least

\frac{nt}{\mathrm{e}\mu r^{1+1/t}}\cdot(1-o_{t}(1))~.

Proof.

Let
\Delta
be the maximum degree and
\delta
be the minimum degree of

.
By Lemma

, for

k=\frac{\mathrm{e}\Delta r^{1/t}}{t}\cdot(1+o_{t}(1))

there exist

-shallow edge sets

with
M_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}M_{k}=E

It holds that

By the pigeonhole principle, there exists an edge set

of size

\max_{i\in[k]}|M_{i}|\geq\frac{n\delta/r}{k}=\frac{n\delta t}{\mathrm{e}\Delta r^{1+1/t}}\frac{1}{1+o_{t}(1)}=\frac{nt}{\mathrm{e}\mu r^{1+1/t}}\cdot(1-o_{t}(1))~.

∎

With Theorem

, we obtain the following lower bound for

.

.

and

3.2
Upper bound via combinatorial designs

In this section, we show that there exist
r
-uniform (
r
-partite) regular hypergraphs that only have

-shallow edge sets of small size.
This result follows from the existence of combinatorial designs, proved by Keevash

16
]
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
t,v,k
and
\lambda
be positive integers.
A set of
points

V
with a multiset
\mathcal{B}
of subsets of
V
(called
blocks
) is called a
t
-
(v,k,\lambda)
-design
if

1.

|V|=v
and

2.

|B|=k
for each block
B\in\mathcal{B}
and

3.

for each set
U\subseteq V
of size
t
there exist exactly
\lambda
blocks
B\in\mathcal{B}
with
U\subseteq B
.

First, we state a well-known property of combinatorial designs.
This theorem gives a necessary condition for the existence of combinatorial designs.

Theorem 3.6

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
(V,\mathcal{B})
be a
t
-
(v,k,\lambda)
-design.
If
I\subseteq V
is a set of size
0\leq|I|=i\leq t
, then the number of blocks containing
I
is

r_{i}=\lambda\binom{v-i}{t-i}\left/\binom{k-i}{t-i}\right.~.

By Theorem
3.6
, we obtain a necessary condition for the existence of a
t
-
(v,k,\lambda)
-design: for all integers
i
with
0\leq i\leq t-1
it must hold that
\binom{k-i}{t-i}
divides
\lambda\binom{v-i}{t-i}
.
Keevash
[
16
]
recently showed that this condition is also sufficient for large enough
v
.
Here, a
t
-
(v,k,\lambda)
-design
(V,\mathcal{B})
is called
resolvable
if the set
\mathcal{B}
of blocks can be partitioned into perfect matchings.

Theorem 3.7

(Keevash
[
16
]
)
.

Suppose
k\geq t\geq 1
and
\lambda
are fixed and
v>v_{0}(k,t,\lambda)
is sufficiently large such that
k\mid v
and
\binom{k-i}{t-i}\mid\lambda\binom{v-i}{t-i}
for all integers
i
with
0\leq i\leq t-1
.
Then there exists a resolvable
t
-
(v,k,\lambda)
-design.

It immediately follows from Theorem
3.7
that for any positive integers
t
and
k
with
k\geq t\geq 1
there exist
t
-
(nk,k,1)
-designs for infinitely many
n>n_{0}(k,t)
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Suppose
k\geq t\geq 1
are fixed.
Then there exist infinitely many positive integers
n
such that there exists a resolvable
t
-
(nk,k,1)
-design.

Proof.

We use Theorem
3.7
to prove this corollary.
Clearly,
k\mid nk
and thus, the first condition is satisfied.
Therefore, it suffices to show that there exist infinitely many positive integers
n
which satisfy
\binom{k-i}{t-i}\mid\binom{nk-i}{t-i}
for all integers
i
with
0\leq i\leq t-1
.

We claim that for all positive integers
\mu
,

n=1+\mu\cdot k(k-1)(k-2)\cdots(k-t+1)

satisfies the divisibility conditions.
Indeed, for all integers
i
with
0\leq i\leq t-1
,

\frac{nk-i}{k-i}=n+\frac{n-1}{k-i}i

is a positive integer and therefore,

\frac{\binom{nk-i}{t-i}}{\binom{k-i}{t-i}}=\frac{(nk-i)(nk-i-1)\cdots(nk-t+1)}{(k-i)(k-i-1)\cdots(k-t+1)}

is a positive integer for all integers
i=0,1,\dots,t-1
.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
t
and
k
be positive integers with
k>t\geq 1
.
Then there exist infinitely many positive integers
n
such that there exists an
r
-uniform
r
-partite
k
-regular hypergraph
H
with parts of size
n
and maximum
t
-shallow edge set of size
t
, where
r=\binom{nk-1}{t}/\binom{k-1}{t}
.
This shows

\eta_{t}(r)\leq\eta_{t}(H)\leq\frac{1}{1-t/k}\cdot r^{-1/t}

for the specified values of
r
.

Proof.

Let
H=(V,\mathcal{B})
be a resolvable
(t+1)
-
(nk,k,1)
-design.
This exists due to Corollary
3.2
for infinitely many positive integers
n
.
That is,
|V|=nk
, each block
B\in\mathcal{B}
has size
|B|=k
and each set of
t+1
elements of
V
is contained in exactly one block in
\mathcal{B}
.
Then, the hypergraph
H
is
k
-uniform,
r
-regular (because of Theorem
3.6
) and has
nk
vertices and
nr
edges.
Define
H^{*}=(V^{*},E^{*})
to be the dual hypergraph of
H
.
Then,
H^{*}
has
nr
vertices and
nk
edges.
Moreover,
H^{*}
is
r
-uniform and
k
-regular.
Since the design
H=(V,\mathcal{B})
is resolvable, the hypergraph
H^{*}
is
r
-partite with parts of size
n
.
Note that every set of
t+1
edges in
H^{*}
has a vertex that is incident to all
t+1
edges.
Thus, the maximum
t
-shallow edge set has size
t
, i.e.
\nu_{t}(H)=t
.
With

r=\frac{\binom{nk-1}{t}}{\binom{k-1}{t}}=\frac{(nk-1)(nk-2)\cdots(nk-t)}{(k-1)(k-2)\cdots(k-t)}\leq\left(\frac{nk}{k-t}\right)^{t}

we obtain
n\geq r^{1/t}(1-t/k)
and therefore

\eta_{t}(r)\leq\eta_{t}(H)=\frac{t}{tn}=\frac{1}{n}\leq\frac{1}{1-t/k}\cdot r^{-1/t}~.

∎

For a fixed positive integer
t
,
k
can be chosen arbitrarily large.
Therefore, the following corollary follows immediate.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
t
be a fixed positive integer.
For every real number
\epsilon>0
there exist infinitely many positive integers
r
such that

\eta_{t}(r)\leq(1+\epsilon)r^{-1/t}~.

Proof.

We choose
k
such that
t/k\leq\epsilon/(1+\epsilon)
and apply Theorem
3.9
.
∎

Thus, this upper bound asymptotically matches the lower bound obtained in Section
3.1
up to a constant factor.

4
Minimum Degree Conditions

4.1
Uniform hypergraphs

In this section, we study sufficient conditions on the minimum degree of uniform hypergraphs for the existence of
t
-shallow hitting edge sets.
Let
H=(V,E)
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph.
If
\hat{e}
is an
(r-1)
-set of vertices of
H
, the
neighborhood

N_{r-1}(\hat{e})
of
\hat{e}
is the set of all vertices
v
such that
\hat{e}\cup\{v\}
is an edge in
H
.
We define the
minimum degree

\delta_{r-1}(H)
of an
r
-uniform hypergraph to be the minimum of
|N_{r-1}(\hat{e})|
over all
(r-1)
-sets
\hat{e}
of vertices in
H
.
This is also called the
minimum co-degree
of
H
.

The problem of finding minimum degree thresholds for the existence of perfect matchings in
r
-uniform hypergraphs is a classical topic in extremal graph theory.
These questions are referred to as Dirac-type problems as Dirac
[
8
]
showed that every graph on
n
vertices with minimum degree at least
n/2
contains a Hamiltonian cycle (and therefore a perfect matching if
n
is even).
Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
23
]
started the study of Dirac-type problems for
3
-uniform hypergraphs.
Kühn and Osthus
[
19
]
and Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
24
]
improved the minimum degree conditions for
r
-uniform hypergraphs until Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
25
]
settled the problem by proving a tight minimum degree condition for the existence of a perfect matching in
r
-uniform hypergraphs, stated in Theorem
4.1
.
See also
[
28
]
for a survey on Dirac-type problems in hypergraphs.

Theorem 4.1

(Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
25
]
)
.

Let
r\geq 3
be an integer and let
n
be sufficiently large and divisible by
r
.
If
H
is an
r
-uniform hypergraph with
n
vertices of minimum degree

\delta_{r-1}(H)\geq\begin{cases}n/2+3-r,&\text{if $r/2$ is even and $n/r$ is odd}\\
n/2+5/2-r,&\text{if $r$ is odd and $(n-1)/2$ is odd}\\
n/2+3/2-r,&\text{if $r$ is odd and $(n-1)/2$ is even}\\
n/2+2-r,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

then
H
has a perfect matching.
Moreover, the minimum degree condition is tight.

Recall that perfect matchings are exactly
1
-shallow hitting edge sets.
Generalizing this, we seek to find as tight as possible minimum degree conditions for the existence of
t
-shallow hitting edge sets in
r
-uniform hypergraphs for fixed
t
and
r
.
Let us start on the negative side by constructing an
r
-uniform hypergraph
H
with large minimum degree
\delta_{r-1}(H)
that has no
t
-shallow hitting edge set.

Theorem 4.2
.

For all positive integers
r\geq 2
,
t\geq 2
and
n
there exists an
r
-uniform hypergraph
H=(V,E)
with
|V|=n
vertices and

\delta_{r-1}(H)\geq\frac{n}{(r-1)t+1}-1

that has no
t
-shallow hitting edge set.

Proof.

Let
k=(r-1)t+1
and
\delta=n/k-1
.
We define an
r
-uniform hypergraph
H=(V,E)
with
V=A\dot{\cup}B
where

|A|=\lceil\delta\rceil\quad\text{and}\quad|B|=n-\lceil\delta\rceil~.

Note that
|V|=|A|+|B|=n
and
|A|<n/k
and
|B|>(k-1)n/k
.
We define that
e\subseteq V
is an edge in
H
if and only if
|e|=r
and
e
has a vertex in
A
, i.e.,
e\cap A\neq\emptyset
.
Clearly,
H
has minimum degree
\delta_{r-1}(H)\geq\delta
.
Assume, for contradiction, that there exists a
t
-shallow hitting edge set
M
of
H
.
Since every vertex in
A
is covered at most
t
times, we have

|M|\leq t|A|<\frac{tn}{k}~.

On the other hand,

|M|\geq\frac{|B|}{r-1}>\frac{(k-1)n}{(r-1)k}=\frac{tn}{k}~,

since every vertex in
B
is covered at least once and at most
r-1
vertices of
B
are covered by the same edge in
M
.
Thus,
tn/k<|M|<tn/k
is the desired contradiction.
∎

On the positive side, if the minimum degree of
H
is just one larger than the bound in Theorem
4.2
, then
t
-shallow hitting edge sets always exist.
In other words, the following theorem is best-possible.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
t\geq 2
be an integer.
If
H=(V,E)
is an
r
-uniform hypergraph with
|V|=n
vertices which satisfies

\delta_{r-1}(H)\geq\frac{n}{(r-1)t+1}~,

then
H
has a
t
-shallow hitting edge set.

Proof.

Let
k=(r-1)t+1
.
First of all, observe that
n\geq r
as
\delta_{r-1}(H)=0
for
1\leq n<r
, contradicting the assumption
\delta_{r-1}(H)\geq n/k>0
.
Let
M\subseteq E
be a
t
-shallow edge set of
H
.
For
i=0,1,\dots,t
, let
n_{i}(M)
be the number of vertices
v\in V
with
\degree_{M}(v)=i
.
We define
M\subseteq E
to be a
t
-shallow edge set that minimizes
n_{0}(M)
on condition that each edge
e\in M
contains at most one vertex
v\in e
with
\degree_{M}(v)\geq 2
, i.e.,
|\{v\in e\mid\degree_{M}(v)\geq 2\}|\leq 1
for all
e\in M
.
Moreover, we set
n_{i}=n_{i}(M)
for all
i=0,1,\dots,t
and
M
as defined.

Claim 4.4
.

If
n_{0}>0
then
n_{t}<\left\lceil n/k\right\rceil
.

Assume that
n_{t}\geq\lceil n/k\rceil
.
Each vertex of degree
t
in
M
has
(r-1)t
neighbors of degree
1
in
M
, all of them distinct.
Thus,
n_{1}\geq(r-1)tn_{t}
and

n\geq n_{0}+n_{1}+n_{t}>((r-1)t+1)n_{t}\geq k\lceil n/k\rceil\geq n~,

a contradiction.

∎

Claim 4.5
.

n_{0}\leq r-2
.

Assume that
n_{0}\geq r-1
.
Let
\hat{e}
be an
(r-1)
-set of vertices that is not covered by
M
.
By Claim
4.1
it holds that
n_{t}<\lceil n/k\rceil
.
Since
\delta_{r-1}(H)\geq\lceil n/k\rceil
, there exists a vertex
v\in N_{r-1}(\hat{e})
with
\degree_{M}(v)<t
.
We define
e=\hat{e}\cup\{v\}
and distinguish four cases.

First, assume that
v
is not covered by
M
.
Then,
M^{\prime}=M\cup\{e\}
is a
t
-shallow edge set with
n_{0}(M^{\prime})<n_{0}(M)
and each edge
e^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
still has at most one vertex
v^{\prime}\in e^{\prime}
of degree at least two, a contradiction.

Secondly, assume that
\degree_{M}(v)=1
and
the
edge
e^{\prime}\in M
incident to
v
has no vertex of degree at least two in
M
.
As in the first case,
M^{\prime}=M\cup\{e\}
leads to the desired contradiction.

Thirdly, assume that
\degree_{M}(v)=1
and
the
edge
e^{\prime}\in M
incident to
v
has a vertex
v^{\prime}
of degree at least two in
M
.
Then, define
M^{\prime}=(M\cup\{e\})-\{e^{\prime}\}
.
Then,
V(M^{\prime})=(V(M)-V(e^{\prime}))\cup V(e)\cup\{v^{\prime}\}
since
v^{\prime}
has degree at least one in
M^{\prime}
.
Thus,
|V(M^{\prime})|=|V(M)|+1
and
n_{0}(M^{\prime})<n_{0}(M)
.
As we deleted the edge
e^{\prime}
, every edge in
M^{\prime}
still has at most one vertex of degree at least two in
M^{\prime}
.
This is the desired contradiction.

It remains the case that
\degree_{M}(v)\geq 2
.
We define
M^{\prime}=M\cup\{e\}
.
It clearly holds that
n_{0}(M^{\prime})<n_{0}(M)
and
M^{\prime}
is
t
-shallow.
The edge
e
has exactly one vertex of degree at least two in
M^{\prime}
.
Moreover, for each edge
e^{\prime}\in M
that is incident to
e
it holds that
e\cap e^{\prime}=\{v\}
.
Therefore, each edge
e^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
has at most one vertex of degree at least two.

∎

We are now able to prove the theorem.
If
n_{0}=0
, we are done.
Hence, assume that
n_{0}>0
.
By Claim
4.1
we have
n_{t}<\lceil n/k\rceil
and by Claim
4.1
we have
n_{0}\leq r-2
.
We build an
(r-1)
-set
\hat{e}
of vertices of degree less than
t
that contains all vertices that are not covered by
M
.
This set
\hat{e}
exists since
n\geq r
and therefore
n-n_{t}>(k-1)n/k=(r-1)tn/k\geq r-1
for
t\geq 1
.
Since
n_{t}<\lceil n/k\rceil
and
\delta_{r-1}(H)\geq\lceil n/k\rceil
, there is a vertex
v\in N_{r-1}(\hat{e})
with
\degree_{M}(v)<t
.
We define
e=\hat{e}\cup\{v\}
and
M^{\prime}=M\cup\{e\}
.
Then,
M^{\prime}
is a
t
-shallow hitting edge set.
∎

4.2
Uniform partite hypergraphs

Now, let us consider
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraphs.
Recall that for
r=2
these are exactly the bipartite graphs.
It is well-known that every bipartite graph with parts of size
n
has a perfect matching if
\degree(v)\geq n/2
for all vertices
v
.
The definition of the minimum degree of a bipartite graph is extendable to
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraphs in the following way, see for example
[
19
]
and
[
1
]
.
Let
H=(V_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}V_{r},E)
be an
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraph.
We say that a set
\hat{e}\subseteq V
of vertices of
H
is
legal
if
|\hat{e}\cap V_{i}|\leq 1
for all parts
V_{i}
of
H
.
If
\hat{e}\subseteq V
is a set of vertices of
H
with
|\hat{e}|=r-1
, the
neighborhood

N_{r-1}(\hat{e})
of
\hat{e}
is the set of all vertices
v
such that
\hat{e}\cup\{v\}
is an edge in
H
.
Given an
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraph
H
, we define the
minimum degree

\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)
to be the minimum of
|N_{r-1}(\hat{e})|
over all legal sets
\hat{e}
of vertices in
H
with
|\hat{e}|=r-1
.
The minimum degree
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)
is also called
minimum co-degree
, but observe the important difference between
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)
for
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraphs
H
considered here and
\delta_{r-1}(H)
for
r
-uniform hypergraphs
H
from Section
4.1
.

Kühn and Osthus
[
19
]
proved a sufficient bound on
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)
for the existence of perfect matchings in
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraphs.
This bound was improved in
[
1
]
as stated below.

Theorem 4.6

(Aharoni, Georgakopoulos and Sprüssel
[
1
]
)
.

Let
r\geq 2
and let
H=(V=V_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}V_{r},E)
be an
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraph with parts of size
n
such that for every legal
(r-1)
-set
\hat{e}
contained in
V\setminus V_{1}
we have
|N_{r-1}(\hat{e})|>n/2
and for every legal
(r-1)
-set
\hat{f}
contained in
V\setminus V_{r}
we have
|N_{r-1}(\hat{f})|\geq n/2
.
Then there exists a perfect matching in
H
.

The upper bound in Theorem
4.6
is tight up to the condition
|N_{r-1}(\hat{e})|>n/2
for every legal
(r-1)
-set
\hat{e}
contained in
V\setminus V_{1}
.
It is not known whether this condition can be replaced by
|N_{r-1}(\hat{e})|\geq n/2
for every legal
(r-1)
-set
\hat{e}
.
On the other hand, there exist
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraphs
H
with
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)\geq n/2-1
that have no perfect matching, due to a construction in
[
19
]
, which shows that the minimum degree condition in Theorem
4.6
is essentially tight.

In this section, we seek to extend these results to
t
-shallow hitting edge sets in
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraphs
H
with parts of equal sizes and large minimum degree
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)
.
For a start, we show a sufficient condition for the existence of
t
-shallow hitting edge sets in bipartite graphs (that is,
2
-uniform
2
-partite hypergraphs) of large minimum degree and show that our required lower bound on
\delta_{1}(H)
is tight.
Afterwards, we extend this to
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraphs.

Theorem 4.7

(Berge and Las Vergnas
[
4
]
)
.

Let
G=(A\dot{\cup}B,E)
be a bipartite graph and
t
be a positive integer.
Then,
G
has a
t
-shallow hitting edge set if and only if
|X|\leq t|N(X)|
for all sets
X\subseteq A
and
X\subseteq B
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
G=(A\dot{\cup}B,E)
be a bipartite graph with
|A|\leq|B|\leq t|A|
.
Let
\delta_{A}
respectively
\delta_{B}
be the minimum degree of the vertices in
A
respectively
B
.
If
t\delta_{A}+\delta_{B}\geq|A|
and
\delta_{A}+t\delta_{B}\geq|B|
then there exists a
t
-shallow hitting edge set.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.7
, we have to show that
|X|\leq t|N(X)|
holds for all sets
X\subseteq A
and all sets
X\subseteq B
.

We show that every set
X\subseteq A
satisfies
|X|\leq t|N(X)|
.
First, assume that
1\leq|X|\leq t\delta_{A}
.
Since every vertex in
X
has at least
\delta_{A}
neighbors, we have
|N(X)|\geq\delta_{A}
and thus
|X|\leq t|N(X)|
.
Now assume that
|X|>t\delta_{A}
.
It follows that
|A\setminus X|<|A|-t\delta_{A}\leq\delta_{B}
.
Since every vertex in
B
has at least
\delta_{B}
neighbors in
A
, it must have a neighbor in
X
.
Thus,
N(X)=B
and
t|N(X)|=t|B|\geq t|A|\geq|X|
.

Now, we show that every set
X\subseteq B
satisfies
|X|\leq t|N(X)|
.
First, assume that
1\leq|X|\leq t\delta_{B}
.
Since every vertex in
X
has at least
\delta_{B}
neighbors, we have
|N(X)|\geq\delta_{B}
and thus
|X|\leq t|N(X)|
.
Now assume that
|X|>t\delta_{B}
.
It follows that
|B\setminus X|<|B|-t\delta_{B}\leq\delta_{A}
.
Since every vertex in
A
has at least
\delta_{A}
neighbors in
A
, it must have a neighbor in
X
.
Thus,
N(X)=A
and
t|N(X)|=t|A|\geq|B|\geq|X|
.
∎

As a special case of Theorem
4.8
, assume that
|A|=|B|=n
.
It follows that if
G
has minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq n/(t+1)
then
G
has a
t
-shallow hitting edge set.
Indeed,

t\delta_{A}+\delta_{B}\geq\frac{tn}{t+1}+\frac{n}{t+1}=n=|A|

and

\delta_{A}+t\delta_{B}\geq\frac{n}{t+1}+\frac{tn}{t+1}=n=|B|~.

To see that the condition
\delta(G)\geq n/(t+1)
is tight, let
G^{\prime}=(A\dot{\cup}B,E)
be a bipartite graph with parts of size
|A|=|B|=n
such that
t+1
divides
n-1
.
Let
A=A_{1}\dot{\cup}A_{2}
and
B=B_{1}\dot{\cup}B_{2}
where
|A_{1}|=|B_{2}|=(n-1)/(t+1)
and
|A_{2}|=|B_{1}|=(nt+1)/(t+1)
and note that that
|A|=|B|=n
.
The edges of
G^{\prime}
are exactly the pairs
\{a_{1},b_{1}\}
and
\{a_{2},b_{2}\}
with
a_{1}\in A_{1},a_{2}\in A_{2},b_{1}\in B_{1},b_{2}\in B_{2}
.
The graph
G^{\prime}
has minimum degree
\delta(G^{\prime})=(n-1)/(t+1)\geq n/(t+1)-1
but does not contain a
t
-shallow hitting edge set as

t|N(B_{1})|=t|A_{1}|=t\cdot\frac{n-1}{t+1}=\frac{tn+1}{t+1}-1=|B_{1}|-1<|B_{1}|~.

Now, let us turn to
t
-shallow hitting edge sets in
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraphs for
r>2
.
By extending a construction from
[

]
, we obtain the following construction of a hypergraph
H
with large minimum degree
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)
but without a
t
-shallow hitting edge set.

Theorem 4.9
.

For all positive integers
r\geq 2
,
t\geq 2
and
n
there exists an
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraph
H
with parts of size
n
and

\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)\geq\frac{n}{(r-1)t+1}-1

that has no
t
-shallow hitting edge set.

Proof.

Let
\delta^{\prime}=n/((r-1)t+1)-1
.
We define the
r
-partite
r
-uniform hypergraph
H=(V_{1}\dot{\cup}V_{2}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}V_{r},E)
with
|V_{i}|=n
for all
i=1,2,\dots,r
.
Let
V^{\prime}_{i}
be a subset of
V_{i}
of size
|V^{\prime}_{i}|=\lceil\delta^{\prime}\rceil
, for
i=1,2,\dots,r
.
Note that
|V^{\prime}_{1}\cup V^{\prime}_{2}\cup\cdots\cup V^{\prime}_{r}|<r(\delta^{\prime}+1)
.
We define that
e=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{r}\}
is an edge in
H
if and only if
v_{1}\in V_{1},v_{2}\in V_{2},\dots,v_{r}\in V_{r}
and there is at least one
v_{i}
with
v_{i}\in V^{\prime}_{i}
.
Clearly,
H
has minimum degree
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)\geq\delta^{\prime}
.
Assume, for contradiction, that there exists a
t
-shallow hitting edge set
M
of
H
.
Then,

|M|\leq t|V^{\prime}_{1}\cup V^{\prime}_{2}\cup\cdots\cup V^{\prime}_{r}|<tr(\delta^{\prime}+1)

since every vertex in
V^{\prime}_{1}\cup V^{\prime}_{2}\cup\cdots\cup V^{\prime}_{r}
is covered at most
t
times.
On the other hand,

\begin{split}|M|&\geq\frac{1}{r-1}\left|(V_{1}\setminus V^{\prime}_{1})\cup(V_{2}\setminus V^{\prime}_{2})\cup\cdots\cup(V_{r}\setminus V^{\prime}_{r})\right|>\frac{rn-r(\delta^{\prime}+1)}{r-1}\\
&=\frac{rnt}{(r-1)t+1}=tr(\delta^{\prime}+1)\end{split}

since every vertex in
V_{i}\setminus V^{\prime}_{i}
is covered at least once and at most
r-1
vertices of
(V_{1}\setminus V^{\prime}_{1})\cup\cdots\cup(V_{r}\setminus V^{\prime}_{r})
are covered by the same edge in
M
.
Thus,
tr(\delta^{\prime}+1)<|M|<tr(\delta^{\prime}+1)
is the desired contradiction.
∎

To obtain a condition on
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)
that is sufficient for the existence of
t
-shallow hitting edge sets, we first state a theorem from
[
19
]
.
It says that if we relax the condition
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)>n/2
to
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)\geq n/r
, then there exists an almost perfect matching, i.e., a matching that covers all but at most
r-2
vertices from each part of
H
.
In
[
19
]
it is shown that this result is tight as the minimum degree condition
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)\geq n/r
cannot be reduced.

Theorem 4.10

(Kühn and Osthus
[
19
]
)
.

Let
H
be an
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraph with parts of size
n
and
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)\geq n/r
.
Then
H
has a matching that covers all but at most
r-2
vertices in each part of
H
.

As mentioned before, we seek to generalize Theorem
4.6
to
t
-shallow hitting edge sets (cf. Theorem
4.14
below).
To this end, we first show in Lemma
4.2
below a generalization of Theorem
4.10
to
t
-shallow edge sets that are not necessarily hitting but cover almost all vertices of each part.
To show Lemma
4.2
, we consider any
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraph
H
with large minimum degree
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)
and a
t
-shallow edge set

satisfying certain properties, leaving as few vertices as possible uncovered.
We then deduce properties of such a hypergraph

(cf. Lemma

below) and show that these lead to a contradiction in Lemma
4.2
.
Using Lemma
4.2
and raising the minimum degree condition by

, we can show that there exists a

-shallow hitting edge set in hypergraphs with large minimum degree
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)
.
In the first step, we show some properties of
t
-shallow hitting edge sets in
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraphs.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
t\geq 2
be positive integers and
H=(V_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}V_{r},E)
be an
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraph with parts of size
n
.
Let
M
be a
t
-shallow edge set that satisfies the following properties.
Here, let
n_{s,i}
be the number of vertices
v\in V_{i}
with
\degree_{M}(v)=s
.

(1)

There exists a non-negative integer
n_{0}
with
n_{0,1}=n_{0,2}=\cdots=n_{0,r}=n_{0}
.

(2)

|M|\leq\left\lceil n/k\right\rceil(t-1)+n-n_{0}
.

Then, with
k=(r-1)t+1
, all of the following holds.

(A)

n_{t,i}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil
for all
i=1,2,\dots,r
.

(B)

If there exists a vertex
v\in V_{i}
with
2\leq\degree_{M}(v)<t
, then
n_{t,i}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil-1
.

(C)

If
|M|<\lceil n/k\rceil(t-1)+n-n_{0}
, then
n_{t,i}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil-1
for all
i=1,2,\dots,r
.

(D)

If
|M|=\lceil n/k\rceil(t-1)+n-n_{0}
and
|\{v\in e\mid\degree_{M}(v)\geq 2\}|\leq 1
for all
e\in M
, then
n_{t,i}=\lceil n/k\rceil
for all
i=1,2,\dots,r
.

Proof.

(A)

For all
i=1,2,\dots,r
, there exist
n-n_{0}-n_{t,i}
vertices in
V_{i}
that are covered at least once by
M
but less than
t
times.
Thus,
|M|\geq tn_{t,i}+(n-n_{0}-n_{t,i})=(t-1)n_{t,i}+n-n_{0}
.
By comparing this inequality with Property
(2)
, we get
n_{t,i}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil
.

(B)

Using the same argument, we have
|M|\geq tn_{t,i}+(n-n_{0}-n_{t,i})+1=(t-1)n_{t,i}+n-n_{0}+1
.
By comparing this inequality with Property
(2)
, we get
n_{t,i}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil-1/(t-1)<\lceil n/k\rceil
and thus
n_{t,i}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil-1
.

(C)

Using the same argument, we have
|M|\geq(t-1)n_{t,i}+n-n_{0}
.
Since
|M|<\lceil n/k\rceil(t-1)+n-n_{0}
it holds that
n_{t,i}<\lceil n/k\rceil
and thus
n_{t,i}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil-1
.

(D)

Let
n_{s}:=n_{s,i}
for all
s=1,2,\dots,t
and some arbitrary
i\in\{1,2,\dots,r\}
, and let
n_{\geq 2}=n_{2}+n_{3}+\cdots+n_{t}
.
Our first goal is to upper-bound
n_{1}
in the general case
n_{t}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil
and in the case
n_{t}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil-1
.
For the general case, we upper-bound the size of
M
by
|M|\leq n_{1}+tn_{\geq 2}=(t-1)n_{\geq 2}+n-n_{0}
and with
|M|=\lceil n/k\rceil(t-1)+n-n_{0}
it follows that
n_{\geq 2}\geq\lceil n/k\rceil
.
Thus, we have
n_{1}=n-n_{0}-n_{\geq 2}\leq n-n_{0}-\lceil n/k\rceil
.

In the next step, we show that if
n_{t}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil-1
then
n_{\geq 2}\geq\lceil n/k\rceil+1
.
First note that
t\geq 3
since for
t=2
it holds that
\lceil n/k\rceil+n-n_{0}=|M|=n_{1}+2n_{2}=n-n_{0}+n_{2}<\lceil n/k\rceil+n-n_{0}
, a contradiction.
For
t\geq 3
, we have

\begin{split}\left\lceil\frac{n}{k}\right\rceil(t-1)+n-n_{0}&=|M|=n-n_{0}+n_{2}+2n_{3}+\cdots+(t-1)n_{t}\\
&\leq n-n_{0}+(t-2)(n_{2}+n_{3}+\cdots+n_{t-1})+(t-1)n_{t}\end{split}

and thus

(t-1)\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{k}\right\rceil-n_{t}\right)\leq(t-2)(n_{2}+n_{3}+\cdots+n_{t-1})~.

It follows that

n_{\geq 2}\geq n_{t}+\frac{t-1}{t-2}\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{k}\right\rceil-n_{t}\right)=\frac{1}{t-2}\left((t-1)\left\lceil\frac{n}{k}\right\rceil-n_{t}\right)>\left\lceil\frac{n}{k}\right\rceil

and therefore
n_{1}\leq n-n_{0}-\lceil n/k\rceil-1
if
n_{t}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil-1
.

Now, we prove the claim.
Assume, for contradiction, that there exists a part
V_{j}
with
n_{t,j}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil-1
.
Then,
n_{1,i}\leq n-n_{0}-\lceil n/k\rceil
for all
i=1,2,\dots,r
and
n_{1,j}=n-n_{0}-(n_{2,j}+n_{3,j}+\cdots+n_{t,j})\leq n-n_{0}-\lceil n/k\rceil-1
since
n_{t,j}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil-1
and thus
n_{2,j}+n_{3,j}+\cdots+n_{t,j}\geq\lceil n/k\rceil+1
.
In the next step, observe that

(r-1)|M|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{1,i}~,

since each edge in
M
covers at least
(r-1)
vertices that are covered once by
M
.
This follows since each edge
e\in M
has at most one vertex
v\in e
with
\degree_{M}(v)\geq 2
.
We bound the
n_{1,i}
’s in the sum and use
|M|=\lceil n/k\rceil(t-1)+n-n_{0}
and obtain

(r-1)\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{k}\right\rceil(t-1)+n-n_{0}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{1,i}\leq r\left(n-n_{0}-\left\lceil\frac{n}{k}\right\rceil\right)-1~.

Simplifying this inequality and using
k=(r-1)t+1
and we get

\left\lceil\frac{n}{k}\right\rceil k+1\leq n-n_{0}~,

a contradiction since
\lceil n/k\rceil\cdot k\geq n
and
n_{0}\geq 0
.∎

As the next step, we show some properties of
t
-shallow edge sets with specific other properties that maximize the number of covered vertices.
The intuition for the next lemma is, if the hypergraph
H
has large minimum degree but no

-shallow edge set (with specific properties) that covers almost all vertices of each part, then

is not far from the hypergraph constructed in Theorem
4.9
.
In the following, given a hypergraph
H=(V,E)
and a subset of edges

, we define
V(M)\subseteq V
to be the union of all edges in

.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
t\geq 2
be positive integers and
H=(V_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}V_{r},E)

r

be a

-shallow edge set that satisfies the following properties and maximizes the number of covered vertices in

n_{s,i}
be the number of vertices

\degree_{M}(v)=s
.

(1)

There exists a non-negative integer
n_{0}
such that
n_{0,1}=n_{0,2}=\cdots=n_{0,r}=n_{0}
.

(2)

|M|\leq\left\lceil n/k\right\rceil(t-1)+n-n_{0}
.

If
n_{0}\geq r-1
then each of the following holds.

(E)

N_{r-1}(\hat{e})\subseteq V(M)
for each legal
(r-1)
-set
\hat{e}
of uncovered vertices of

.

(F)

|M|=\lceil n/k\rceil(t-1)+n-n_{0}
.

(G)

For all edges

it holds that the number of vertices

that are covered at least twice by

|\{v\in e\mid\degree_{M}(v)\geq 2\}|\leq 1

.

Proof.

We define

.
Moreover, we define

(E)

Let
\hat{e}
be a legal
(r-1)

.

.

satisfies Property

Since

covers more vertices than

and satisfies all properties, we have the desired contradiction.

(F)

In the next step, we show that
|M|=\lceil n/k\rceil(t-1)+n-n_{0}
.
Assume, for contradiction, that
|M|<\lceil n/k\rceil(t-1)+n-n_{0}
.
By Lemma

(C)
, we have
n_{t,i}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil-1
for all parts
V_{i}
.
Since
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)\geq\lceil n/k\rceil

of this lemma, each legal

For

, we build an
(r-1)
-set

.
Define

(2)
.

.
Thus,

n\geq n_{0}+n_{1,1}+n_{t,1}\geq n_{0}+t(r-1)\left\lceil\frac{n}{k}\right\rceil+\left\lceil\frac{n}{k}\right\rceil\geq n_{0}+k\left\lceil\frac{n}{k}\right\rceil\geq n_{0}+n~,

a contradiction since we assumed
n_{0}\geq r-1>0
.
∎

We are now able to prove that every
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraph with parts of size
n
and minimum degree
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)\geq 1+n/((r-1)t+1)
has a
t
-shallow hitting edge set.
This condition is tight up to the additive constant
1
, as shown in Theorem
4.9
.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
t\geq 2
be positive integers and
H=(V_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}V_{r},E)
be an
r
-uniform
r
-partite hypergraph with parts of size
n
and minimum degree

\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)\geq\left\lceil\frac{n}{(r-1)t+1}\right\rceil+1~.

Then, there exists a
t
-shallow hitting edge set in
H
.

Proof.

Let
k=(r-1)t+1
.
By Lemma
4.2
, there exists a
t
-shallow edge set
M
that satisfies Property
(1)
, Property
(2)
and Property
(3)
of Lemma
4.2
.
Let
M
be such a
t
-shallow edge set that maximizes the number of covered vertices with respect to all three properties.
Let
U_{i}=V_{i}\setminus V(M)
be the set of uncovered vertices in
V_{i}
.
Then,
|U_{1}|=\cdots=|U_{r}|=n_{0}
for some non-negative integer
n_{0}\leq r-2
, by Property
(1)
and Property
(3)
.
Moreover,
|M|\leq\lceil n/k\rceil(t-1)+n-n_{0}
by Property
(2)
.
Let
n_{s,i}
be the number of vertices
v\in V_{i}
with
\degree_{M}(v)=s
.
By Lemma
4.2
Result
(A)
, we have
n_{t,i}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil
for all
i=1,2,\dots,r
.
For
n_{0}=0
, there is nothing to show.
Therefore, assume
n_{0}>0
.
We construct a
t
-shallow hitting edge set
M^{\prime}
by adding edges to
M
.

For each
i=1,2,\dots,n_{0}+1
, we build a legal
(r-1)
-set
\hat{e}_{i}
of vertices of
U\setminus U_{i}
, such that each vertex in
U_{1}\cup U_{2}\cup\cdots\cup U_{n_{0}+1}
is contained in exactly one
\hat{e}_{i}
and each vertex in
\hat{U}_{n_{0}+2}\cup\cdots\cup U_{r}
is contained in at least one and at most two
\hat{e}_{i}
’s.
Note that
N_{r-1}(\hat{e}_{i})\subseteq V_{i}\setminus U_{i}
, since otherwise we can build a
t
-shallow edge set
M^{\prime}
that covers more vertices and satisfies all three properties of Lemma
4.2
, a contradiction.
Since
\delta^{\prime}_{r-1}(H)\geq\lceil n/k\rceil+1
and
n_{t,i}\leq\lceil n/k\rceil
for all
i=1,2,\dots,r
, there exists a vertex
v_{i}\in N_{r-1}(\hat{e}_{i})
that is covered at least once but less than
t
times by
M
.
We define
e_{i}=\hat{e}_{i}\cup\{v_{i}\}
.
Let
M^{\prime}=M\cup\{e_{i}\mid i=1,2,\dots,n_{0}+1\}
.
Then,
M^{\prime}
is
t
-shallow and covers all vertices of
H
.
∎

5
Conclusions

Open problems include all cases in which our bounds are not tight yet.
The most intriguing is to determine the least integer
t=t(r)
for which every regular
r
-uniform hypergraph admits a
t
-shallow hitting edge set.
Our results give that
t(r)\geq\lfloor\log_{2}(r+1)\rfloor
and
t(r)\leq\mathrm{e}r(1+o(1))
, where we can improve the upper bound to
c\ln r(1+o(1))
for
c\approx 4.31
(i.e., nearly matching the lower bound) for the special case of regular
r
-uniform hypergraphs of girth at least four.

Let us also mention that in the first author’s Master’s thesis
[
22
]
it is shown that it is NP-complete to determine whether an
r
-uniform
r
-partite regular hypergraph has a
t
-shallow hitting edge set, provided
t\leq\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}(r)-O(\log\log r)
, i.e., only slightly below the smallest value for
t
for which such
t
-shallow hitting edge sets
always
exist.

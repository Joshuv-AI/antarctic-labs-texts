MultiDLO: Simultaneous Shape Tracking of Multiple Deformable Linear Objects with Global-Local Topology Preservation
Jingyi Xiang, Holly Dinkel

Abstract
MultiDLO is a real-time algorithm for estimating the shapes of multiple, intertwining deformable linear objects (DLOs) from RGB-D image sequences. Unlike prior methods that track only a single DLO, MultiDLO simultaneously handles several objects. It uses the geodesic distance in the Global-Local Topology Preservation algorithm to define both inter-object identity and intra-object topology, ensuring entangled DLOs remain distinct with accurate local geometry. The MultiDLO algorithm is demonstrated on two challenging scenarios involving three entangling ropes, and the implementation is open-source and available for the community.

Introduction

Consider an automated robotic system which monitors in real-time the shape of a deformable linear object (DLO), for example a rope, a wire, or a string. This system could perceive the DLO in RGB-D imagery and estimate its configuration to perform a closed-loop manipulation task such as shape control or wire routing, or it could monitor the DLO for collision prevention

III
Methods

For
N

points
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
received at time
t
from a depth sensor,
\bm{\mathrm{X}}^{t}_{N\times 3}=(\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{1}^{t},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{N}^{t})^{T}
, the DLO shape can be represented by a collection of
M
ordered
nodes
,
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{t}_{M\times 3}=(\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{1}^{t},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{M}^{t})^{T}
. The shape of each of
K
DLOs in a scene is represented by a different set of nodes,
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{k\in[1,K]}^{t}
, each consisting of
M_{k}
nodes. Stacking all the
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{k}^{t}

\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{t}
with shape
M\times 3
, where
M=\sum_{k=1}^{K}M_{k}
. All
K

\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{t}

\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{t}

\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{t}
. By treating all DLOs as one deformable object, only semantic segmentation is required in subsequent frames. This approach bypasses the limitation of DLO instance segmentation in 2D RGB images, which can be slow and perform poorly
[

III-A

Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) Node Registration

Fig. 2:
Using Euclidean distance, the distance between nodes is the Euclidean norm of their difference. Using geodesic distance, the distance between two nodes on the same DLO is set to the sum of the segment lengths between them. If the two nodes are not on the same DLO, the distance between them is set to infinity.

Tracking begins with Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) clustering as performed in
[
17
]
. The GMM clustering step computes
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{t}
as the centroids of Gaussian distributions from which
\bm{\mathrm{X}}^{t}
points are randomly sampled with isotropic variance
\sigma^{2}
. Assuming equal membership probability
p(m)=\frac{1}{M}
and
\mu
percent of the points are outliers, the GMM cost function takes the form

\begin{split}&E_{\mathrm{GMM}}(\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{m},\sigma^{2})\\
&\ =\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{m=1}^{M}p(m{\mid}\bm{\mathrm{x}}^{t}_{n})\frac{\|\bm{\mathrm{x}}^{t}_{n}-\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{m}\|^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}+\frac{3N_{p}}{2}\log(\sigma^{2}),\end{split}

(1)

where

\displaystyle N_{p}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{m=1}^{M}p(m{\mid}\bm{\mathrm{x}}^{t}_{n})

(2)

\displaystyle p(m|\bm{\mathrm{x}}^{t}_{n})=\frac{\exp\left(\frac{-D_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{m},\bm{\mathrm{x}}^{t}_{n}}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)}{\sum_{m=1}^{M}\exp\left(\frac{-D_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{m},\bm{\mathrm{x}}^{t}_{n}}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)+\frac{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{3/2}\mu M}{(1-\mu)N}}

(3)

and
D
is a distance metric (discussed in Section
III-E
).

III-B

The Motion Coherence Theory (MCT)

Given node positions
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{t-1}
and
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{t}
from consecutive time steps, the MCT defines a spatial velocity field
v(\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{t-1})=\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{t}-\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{t-1}

[
27
]
. Nodes close to each other should move coherently through the smoothest possible spatial velocity field. For a spatial domain variable
\bm{\mathrm{z}}
and a frequency domain variable
\bm{\mathrm{s}}
, the smoothness of the velocity field
v(\bm{\mathrm{z}})
can be measured by passing it through a high pass filter
1/\tilde{G}(\|\bm{\mathrm{s}}\|)
in the frequency domain as

E_{\mathrm{MCT}}(v(\bm{\mathrm{z}}))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\tilde{v}(\bm{\mathrm{s}})|^{2}/\tilde{G}(\|\bm{\mathrm{s}}\|)d\bm{\mathrm{s}},

(4)

where
\tilde{v}(\bm{\mathrm{s}})
and
\tilde{G}(\|\bm{\mathrm{s}}\|)
are the Fourier Transforms of
v(\bm{\mathrm{z}})
and
G(\|\bm{\mathrm{z}}\|)
. By choosing
G(\|\bm{\mathrm{z}}\|)=\exp(-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\beta^{2}})
, this cost term is equivalent to the cost term of the MCT. The parameter
\beta
controls the width of high pass filter
1/\tilde{G}(\|\bm{\mathrm{s}}\|)
. Therefore, a larger
\beta
leads to a smoother velocity field.

III-C

Locally Linear Embedding (LLE)

Locally Linear Embedding (LLE) represents a node
\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t-1}_{m}
with its closest
2Q
neighbors and a set of weights
\bm{\mathrm{L}}
. At the next time step
t
, LLE preserves local topology by reconstructing
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}^{t}
with its new neighbors and
\bm{\mathrm{L}}
:

\begin{split}E_{LLE}(\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{m})=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{m}-\sum_{i=m-Q}^{m+Q}\bm{\mathrm{L}}(m,i)\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{i}\|^{2}.\end{split}

(5)

The weights
\bm{\mathrm{L}}
are computed by minimizing the cost
[
28
]
,

\begin{split}E_{LLE}(\bm{\mathrm{W}})=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{m}-\sum_{i=m-Q}^{m+Q}\bm{\mathrm{L}}(m,i)\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{i}\|^{2}\end{split},

(6)

with
\bm{\mathrm{W}}
encoded in
\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{m}
and
\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{i}
as

\begin{matrix}\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{m}=\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t-1}_{m}+\bm{\mathrm{G}}(m,\cdot)\bm{\mathrm{W}}\\
\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{i}=\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t-1}_{i}+\bm{\mathrm{G}}(i,\cdot)\bm{\mathrm{W}}\end{matrix}.

(7)

III-D

Expectation-Maximization Update for GLTP

The total cost of GLTP is updated iteratively using the EM algorithm. With
v(\bm{\mathrm{z}})
encoded in
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}^{t}
as
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}^{t}=\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}^{t-1}+v(\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}^{t-1})
, the total cost is

\begin{split}&E(v(\bm{\mathrm{z}}),\sigma^{2})=E_{\mathrm{GMM}}+E_{\mathrm{MCT}}+E_{\mathrm{LLE}}\\
&=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}p(m{\mid}\bm{\mathrm{x}}^{t}_{n})\|\bm{\mathrm{x}}^{t}_{n}-\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{3N_{p}}{2}\log(\sigma^{2})\\
&\ \ \ +\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\tilde{v}(\bm{\mathrm{s}})|^{2}/\tilde{G}(\|\bm{\mathrm{s}}\|)d\bm{\mathrm{s}}\\
&\ \ \ +\sum_{m=1}^{M}\|\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{m}-\sum_{i=m-Q}^{m+Q}\bm{\mathrm{L}}(m,i)\bm{\mathrm{y}}^{t}_{i}\|^{2}\end{split}.

(8)

The solution to the above cost function takes the form
[
15
]

v(\bm{\mathrm{z}})=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bm{\mathrm{w}}_{m}G(\|\bm{\mathrm{z}}-\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}\|).

(9)

For readability, denote
\bm{\mathrm{X}}^{t}
as
\bm{\mathrm{X}}
,
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{t-1}
as
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{0}
, and use the following notations to solve for
\bm{\mathrm{w}}_{m}
and
\sigma^{2}
analytically through EM:

•

\bm{\mathrm{W}}_{M\times 3}=(\bm{\mathrm{w}}_{1}^{t},\ldots,\bm{\mathrm{w}}_{M}^{t})^{T}
, kernel weights,

•

\bm{\mathrm{P}}_{M\times N}
, posterior matrix with
\bm{\mathrm{P}}(m,n)=p(m|\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{n}^{t})
,

•

\bm{\mathrm{G}}_{M\times M}
, kernel matrix with
\bm{\mathrm{G}}(i,m)=\exp(-\frac{D_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{i}^{t},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}^{t}}}{2\beta^{2}})

•

\bm{\mathrm{H}}_{M\times M}=(\bm{\mathrm{I}}-\bm{\mathrm{L}})^{T}(\bm{\mathrm{I}}-\bm{\mathrm{L}})
, LLE weights,

•

\mathrm{d}(\bm{\mathrm{a}})
, the diagonal matrix constructed from vector
\bm{\mathrm{a}}
,

•

\mathrm{tr}(\bm{\mathrm{m}})
, the trace of matrix
\bm{\mathrm{m}}
, and

•

\bm{\mathrm{1}}
, a column vector of ones.

The solutions
\bm{\mathrm{W}}
and
\sigma^{2}
are computed from taking the partial derivatives
\frac{\partial E}{\partial\bm{\mathrm{W}}}
and
\frac{\partial E}{\partial\sigma^{2}}
and setting them to zero as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\mathrm{W}}=\ &(\mathrm{d}(\bm{\mathrm{P1}})\bm{\mathrm{G}}+\lambda\sigma^{2}\bm{\mathrm{I}}+\alpha\sigma^{2}\bm{\mathrm{HG}})^{-1}\\
&\cdot(\bm{\mathrm{PX}}-(\mathrm{d}(\bm{\mathrm{P1}})+\alpha\sigma^{2}\bm{\mathrm{H}})\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{0})\end{split}

(10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma^{2}=&\ \frac{1}{\bm{\mathrm{1}}^{T}\bm{\mathrm{P1}}D}(\mathrm{tr}(\bm{\mathrm{X}}^{T}\mathrm{d}(\bm{\mathrm{P}}^{T}\bm{\mathrm{1}})\bm{\mathrm{X}})-2\mathrm{tr}((\bm{\mathrm{PX}})^{T}\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{0})\\
&+\mathrm{tr}(\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}(\bm{\mathrm{P1}})\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{0})+\mathrm{tr}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}^{T}\bm{\mathrm{G}}^{T}\mathrm{d}(\bm{\mathrm{P1}})\bm{\mathrm{GW}})\\
&+2\mathrm{tr}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}^{T}\bm{\mathrm{G}}^{T}\mathrm{d}(\bm{\mathrm{P1}})\bm{\mathrm{Y}}_{0})-\mathrm{tr}(\bm{\mathrm{W}}^{T}\bm{\mathrm{G}}^{T}\bm{\mathrm{PX}}))\end{split}.

(11)

The new node positions are
\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{t}=\bm{\mathrm{Y}}^{t-1}+\bm{\mathrm{GW}}
.

III-E

Geodesic Proximity

The MCT requires the node velocity field to be smooth, where nodes spatially close to each other move in similar directions with similar speeds. In the node velocity given by

\begin{split}&v(\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m})\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\bm{\mathrm{G}}(m,i)\bm{\mathrm{W}}(i,\cdot)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}G(D_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{i},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}})\bm{\mathrm{W}}(i,\cdot),\end{split}

(12)

D_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{i},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}}
is the distance between nodes
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{i}
and
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}
given some distance metric
D
. Given
G
is Gaussian,
G(D_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{i},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}})
is small if
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{i}
is far from
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}
. This produces a small
G(D_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{i},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}})\bm{\mathrm{W}}(i,\cdot)
, indicating the movement of
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{i}
has insignificant influence on that of
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}
.

The Euclidean distance
d_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{i},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}}=\|\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}-\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{i}\|
is a common choice for
D_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{i},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}}
, however it is not the best choice for representing the geometry of DLOs. If one DLO is resting on top of another DLO, the movement of the top one should have little influence on the movement of the bottom one. However, the small Euclidean distance between nodes near the intersection couples their motion together, causing tracking failure as shown in Fig.
3
.

To resolve this, MultiDLO uses geodesic distance for
D
and defines the node-to-node geodesic distance
\rho_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{i},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}}
to be

\rho_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{i},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}}=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=m}^{i-1}\|\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{j+1}-\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{j}\|&\text{if }m\leq i\\
\sum_{j=i}^{m-1}\|\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{j+1}-\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{j}\|&\text{if }m>i\end{cases}.

(13)

If
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{i}
and
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}
are from two different DLOs, the distance between them is set to infinity.

Similarly, two nodes close in Euclidean distance to an intersection but from different DLOs should have different proximities to points near the intersection. Where
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{c_{1}}
and
\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{c_{2}}
are the two nodes closest to point
\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{n}
, the node-to-point geodesic distance
\rho_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m},\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{n}}
is

\rho_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m},\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{n}}=\begin{cases}d_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{c_{1}},\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{n}}+\rho_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{c_{1}},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}}&\text{if }\rho_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{c_{1}}}\leq\rho_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{c_{2}}}\\
d_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{c_{2}},\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{n}}+\rho_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{c_{1}},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m}}&\text{if }\rho_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{c_{1}}}>\rho_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m},\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{c_{2}}}\\
d_{\bm{\mathrm{y}}_{m},\bm{\mathrm{x}}_{n}}&\text{if }m\in\{c_{1},c_{2}\}\end{cases}.

(14)

IV
Results

Simultaneous MultiDLO shape tracking with intersection among independent objects is demonstrated. For these demonstrations,
Q=3,\beta=0.8,\lambda=1
,
\alpha=3
, and the optimization function tolerance
\epsilon=10^{-5}
. Performance is shown in two scenarios:

1.

Entangling
–Three ropes lie parallel on a table. The ropes are crossed over each other and all three become twisted. This scenario demonstrates tracking when DLOs are wound, knotted, or tied.

2.

Disentangling
–Three ropes lie in a twisted configuration on a table. The ropes are uncrossed until all three ropes reach a separated, parallel configuration. This scenario demonstrates tracking when DLOs are unwound, unknotted, or untied.

The raw data comprise point cloud and RGB image data collected as the ropes are first wound and then unwound. The data are saved in one Robot Operating System (ROS) bag file. For initialization in both scenarios, instance segmentation was performed manually on the first frame due to the limitations of existing DLO instance segmentation algorithms
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
. The subsequent frames used color thresholding as the semantic segmentation input. The tracking results for these scenarios shown in Figure
3
highlight the accuracy of geodesic GLTP for multi-DLO shape tracking and the failure of Euclidean GLTP for this problem.

V
Conclusions and Limitations

The MultiDLO algorithm is a real-time, accurate algorithm for tracking multiple DLOs as they are entangled. One limitation of the method is that a DLO could penetrate itself or other DLOs in the tracking result, which would not happen in real world collision situations. Potential solutions to this include incorporating physics simulators as described in Structure Preserved Registration or adapting the self-intersection constraint introduced in CDCPD2
[
19
,
21
]
. This work could additionally be incorporated into a closed-loop multi-DLO shape controller for robotic manipulation.

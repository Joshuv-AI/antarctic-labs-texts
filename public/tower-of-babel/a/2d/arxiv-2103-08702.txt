Multiplicative finite embeddability vs divisibility of ultrafilters Boris Šobot Department of Mathematics and Informatics, Faculty of Sciences, University of Novi Sad, Trg Dositeja Obradovića 4, 21000 Novi Sad, Serbia e-mail: sobot@dmi.uns.ac.rs, ORCID: 0000-0002-4848-0678


Abstract
We continue the exploration of various aspects of divisibility of ultrafilters, adding one more relation to the picture: multiplicative finite embeddability. We show that it lies between divisibility relations ∣ M \mid_{M} and ∣ ~ \hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt . The set of its minimal elements proves to be very rich, and the ∣ ~ \hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt -hierarchy is used to get a better intuition of this richness. We find the place of the set of ∣ ~ \hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt -maximal ultrafilters among some known families of ultrafilters. Finally, we introduce new notions of largeness of subsets of ℕ {\mathbb{N}} , and compare it to other such notions, important for infinite combinatorics and topological dynamics. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : 54D35, 54D80, 11U99, 03E05, 03H15 Key words and phrases : Stone-Čech compactification, ultrafilter, divisibility, finite embeddability

Introduction

As usual, by
\beta{\mathbb{N}}
we denote the set of ultrafilters on the set
{\mathbb{N}}
of natural numbers. For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the principal ultrafilter
\{A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}:n\in A\}
is identified with
n
, so
\beta{\mathbb{N}}
is an extension of
{\mathbb{N}}
. The topology on
\beta{\mathbb{N}}
is generated by base sets
\overline{A}=\{{\cal F}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}:A\in{\cal F}\}
for all
A\subseteq N
. All such sets are clopen. Because of nonstandard methods used in previous papers on the subject, we will avoid to denote
\overline{A}\setminus A
by
A^{*}
(so it will not get confused with a nonstandard extension
{{}^{*}\hskip-1.42262ptA}
).

Whenever
\star
is an associative operation on
{\mathbb{N}}
, it can be extended to an operation on
\beta{\mathbb{N}}
(which we also denote by
\star
):

{\cal F}\star{\cal G}=\{A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}:\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:n^{-1}A\in{\cal G}\}\in{\cal F}\},

where
n^{-1}A=\{m\in{\mathbb{N}}:n\star m\in A\}
. Then
(\beta{\mathbb{N}},\star)
becomes a compact Hausdorff right-topological semigroup. Various properties of this semigroup are described in
[
4
]
. We will be interested in extensions of operations
+
and
\cdot
(the usual addition and multiplication on
{\mathbb{N}}
). Instead of
n^{-1}A
in the case of multiplication we write
A/n=\{\frac{a}{n}:a\in A\land n\mid a\}
and in the additive case
A-n=\{a-n:a\in A\land a>n\}
. Both in
(\beta{\mathbb{N}},+)
and
(\beta{\mathbb{N}},\cdot)
the elements of
{\mathbb{N}}
are cancellative and commute with all other elements.

Let us fix some more notation. Caligraphic letters
{\cal F},{\cal G},\dots
will mostly be reserved for ultrafilters, and small letters
x,y,\dots
for elements of
{\mathbb{N}}
. By
P
we denote the set of prime numbers, and ultrafilters
{\cal P}\in\overline{P}
are called prime. Let
A
be a subset of
{\mathbb{N}}
. We denote
A^{c}={\mathbb{N}}\setminus A
,
A\uparrow=\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:(\exists a\in A)a\mid n\}
,
A\hskip-2.84544pt\downarrow=\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:(\exists a\in A)n\mid a\}
,
{\cal U}=\{A\in P({\mathbb{N}})\setminus\{\emptyset\}:A=A\uparrow\}
and
{\cal V}=\{A\in P({\mathbb{N}})\setminus\{{\mathbb{N}}\}:A=A\hskip-2.84544pt\downarrow\}=\{A^{c}:A\in{\cal U}\}
. If
\kappa
is a cardinal and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then
[A]^{\kappa}=\{X\subseteq A:|X|=\kappa\}
,
[A]^{<\kappa}=\{X\subseteq A:|X|<\kappa\}
and
A^{n}=\{a^{n}:a\in A\}
. For
{\cal F}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\cal F}^{n}
is the ultrafilter generated by
\{A^{n}:A\in{\cal F}\}
. For subsets
X
and
Y
of
\beta{\mathbb{N}}
,
X\cdot Y=\{{\cal F}\cdot{\cal G}:{\cal F}\in X,{\cal G}\in Y\}
. If one of the sets
X
or
Y
, say
X
, has only one element
{\cal F}
, we will write
{\cal F}\cdot Y
instead of
\{{\cal F}\}Y
; for example if
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then
n{\mathbb{N}}
is the set of natural numbers divisible by
n
. We remind the reader that, for any semigroup
({\mathbb{N}},*)
,
(\beta{\mathbb{N}},*)
has the smallest ideal, usually denoted by
K(\beta{\mathbb{N}},*)
.

There are several ways to extend the divisibility relation
\mid
from
{\mathbb{N}}
to
\beta{\mathbb{N}}
. Some of them were considered in
[
9
]
:

-
{\cal F}\mid_{L}{\cal G}
if
{\cal G}={\cal H}\cdot{\cal F}
for some
{\cal H}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}
;

-
{\cal F}\mid_{R}{\cal G}
if
{\cal G}={\cal F}\cdot{\cal H}
for some
{\cal H}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}
;

-
{\cal F}\mid_{M}{\cal G}
if
{\cal G}={\cal H}_{1}\cdot{\cal F}\cdot{\cal H}_{2}
for some
{\cal H}_{1},{\cal H}_{2}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}
;

-
{\cal F}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal G}
if, for every
A\in{\cal F}
holds
A\uparrow\in{\cal G}
.

The definition of
\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
was inspired by extension of functions
f:{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
to
\beta{\mathbb{N}}
, given by:
\mathaccent 869{f}({\cal F})
is the ultrafilter generated by
\{f[A]:A\in{\cal F}\}
. For example, for the function
pow_{n}(x)=x^{n}
and
{\cal F}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}
, we have
\mathaccent 1373{pow_{n}}({\cal F})={\cal F}^{n}
. The equivalent conditions for
{\cal F}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal G}
, given by

{\cal F}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal G}\Leftrightarrow{\cal F}\cap{\cal U}\subseteq{\cal G}\Leftrightarrow{\cal G}\cap{\cal V}\subseteq{\cal F},

proved to be very useful. In this paper we will include another relation
\leq_{fe}
into consideration. Strictly speaking, it was not originally defined as a divisibility relation but, as we will see in Theorem
2.8
, it is closely connected to the relations above.

Each of the mentioned relations is a quasiorder (reflexive and transitive), so by cutting with a respective equivalence relation we get orders on sets of equivalence classes. For example, we define
{\cal F}=_{\sim}{\cal G}
if
{\cal F}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal G}
and
{\cal G}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal F}
, so we observe
\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
as an order on equivalence classes
[{\cal F}]_{\sim}
of
\beta{\mathbb{N}}/=_{\sim}
. The notation for other relations is analogous.

An ultrafilter
{\cal F}
is divisible by
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
(in any of the considered relations) if and only if
n{\mathbb{N}}\in{\cal F}
(
[
9
]
, Lemma 5.1). Hence we can write just

In
[
11
]
it was shown that the ultrafilters in the “lower” part of the

-hierarchy can be organized into

is the set of numbers with exactly

Proposition 1.1

Let
{\cal A}_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{m}}^{{\cal P}_{1}^{k_{1}},{\cal P}_{2}^{k_{2}},\dots,{\cal P}_{m}^{k_{m}}}
be the family of all ultrafilters containing

F_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{m}}^{{\cal P}_{1}^{k_{1}},{\cal P}_{2}^{k_{2}},\dots,{\cal P}_{m}^{k_{m}}}=\{(A_{1}^{k_{1}})^{(n_{1})}(A_{2}^{k_{2}})^{(n_{2})}\dots(A_{m}^{k_{m}})^{(n_{m})}:

A_{i}\in{\cal P}_{i}\land A_{i}\subseteq P\land(A_{i}=A_{j}\mbox{ if }{\cal P}_{i}={\cal P}_{j}\mbox{ and }A_{i}\cap A_{j}=\emptyset\mbox{ otherwise})\},

where

are prime ultrafilters,
n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{m},k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

\displaystyle(A_{1}^{k_{1}})^{(n_{1})}(A_{2}^{k_{2}})^{(n_{2})}\dots(A_{m}^{k_{m}})^{(n_{m})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i}}a_{i,j}^{k_{i}}:a_{i,j}\in A_{i}\mbox{ for all }i,j\;\land\mbox{ all }a_{i,j}\mbox{ are distinct.}\right\}

Then the level

is the disjoint union of all families

For example,

{\cal P}^{2}

Ultrafilters not divisible by any

are called
{\mathbb{N}}
-free. Sets

belonging to some

We recall several important notions of largeness for subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
. With some of them we also include equivalent conditions; proofs of these equivalences can be found in
[
1
]
and
[
4
]
. Since we want to work with two versions (multiplicative and additive) for some of the definitions, we will add the prefix A- when dealing with the
+
-version. If
\langle x_{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\rangle
is a sequence of elements of
{\mathbb{N}}
, we denote
FS(\langle x_{n}\rangle)=\{\sum_{n\in F}x_{n}:F\in[{\mathbb{N}}]^{<\aleph_{0}}\}
.

A set

is:

-A-IP if there is a sequence
\langle x_{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\rangle
in
{\mathbb{N}}
such that
FS(\langle x_{n}\rangle)\subseteq A
, if and only if it is a member of some
+
-idempotent;

-A-central if it is a member of some minimal
+
-idempotent (idempotent belonging to
K(\beta{\mathbb{N}},+)
);

-A-IP* if it intersects every A-IP set, if and only if it is a member of every
+
-idempotent;

-A-central* if it intersects every A-central set, if and only if it is a member of every minimal
+
-idempotent;

-A-thick if it contains, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, a set
m+\{1,2,\dots,n\}=\{m+k:1\leq k\leq n\}
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
;

-A-piecewise syndetic (short: A-pcws) if there is a finite

such that the family
\{(\bigcup_{t\in F}(A-t))-n:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
has the finite intersection property, if and only if it is a member of some ultrafilter in
K(\beta{\mathbb{N}},+)
;

-A-J set if for any finite set
F
of functions from
{\mathbb{N}}
to
{\mathbb{N}}
there are
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
H\in[{\mathbb{N}}]^{<\aleph_{0}}
such that
a+\sum_{i\in H}f(i)\in A
for all
f\in F
.

The corresponding multiplicative versions of these properties will be denoted with the prefix M-. For example,
A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
is M-IP if there is a sequence
\langle x_{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\rangle
in
{\mathbb{N}}
such that
FP(\langle x_{n}\rangle):=\{\prod_{n\in F}x_{n}:F\in[{\mathbb{N}}]^{<\aleph_{0}}\}\subseteq A
, if and only if it is a member of a
\cdot
-idempotent. We note also that sometimes only sets of the form

are called A-IP sets (not their supersets), but we find the definition used here more convenient.

Remark 1.2

In general it is common to define, for a property P, that
A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
satisfies P* if it intersects all sets with property P. Note that, if P is upwards closed (that is,
A
has P implies that every superset of
A
has P), then
A
has P* if and only if
{\mathbb{N}}\setminus A
does not have P. It follows that, in this case, P and P* are dual:
A
has P if and only if it intersects all sets with property P*. If we have another property Q, clearly P
\Rightarrow
Q implies Q*
\Rightarrow
P*. Hence, for upwards closed properties the reverse implication also holds.

The paper has six sections. Section 2 introduces multiplicative finite embeddability
\leq_{fe}
and proves some of its basic properties. In Section 3 we explore
\leq_{fe}
-minimal ultrafilters. Using the connection between
\leq_{fe}
and
\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
, we show that they are spread all over the lower half, but also present in the upper half of the
\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
-hierarchy. Section 4 deals with families of ultrafilters maximal for some of the relations considered. Using these families, in Section 5 we get new classes of large subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
and find their place among some old notions of this kind. The closing section contains several problems we were not able to resolve.

Throughout the paper we use many results from other sources. This is natural since quite a few times we are able to draw conclusions simply by combining these known results. Some of them we single out as separate propositions, but doing so for all of them would make the paper undesirably long.

2
Multiplicative finite embeddability

The concept of finite embeddability (with respect to the addition operation) was introduced (implicitly) by Ruzsa in
[
8
]
and made precise by Di Nasso in
[
3
]
. It was futher investigated in
[
2
]
and
[
6
]
. The paper
[
7
]
generalized the notion to
F
-finite embeddability with respect to an arbitrary set
F
of operations. We are interested in multiplicative finite embeddability, defined with respect to the multiplication on
{\mathbb{N}}
. Many results on
F
-finite embeddability apply to multiplicative finite embeddability, and some more results on the additive version translate directly to the multiplication version. It turns out that this relation is related to our divisibility relations. For the sake of simplicity we keep the notation
\leq_{fe}
.

Definition 2.1

For
A,B\in P({\mathbb{N}})
,
A
is finitely embeddable in
B
(
A\leq_{fe}B
) if for every finite
F\subseteq A
there is
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
kF\subseteq B
.

For
{\cal F},{\cal G}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\cal F}
is finitely embeddable in
{\cal G}
(
{\cal F}\leq_{fe}{\cal G}
) if for every
B\in{\cal G}
there is
A\in{\cal F}
such that
A\leq_{fe}B
.

It is clear that the relations
\leq_{fe}
(both on
P({\mathbb{N}})
and
\beta{\mathbb{N}}
) are reflexive and transitive. It is a special case of
[
7
]
, Proposition 2.6 that
A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
is in the
\leq_{fe}
-greatest class of sets if and only if it is M-thick; therefore
\leq_{fe}
for subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
is not antisymmetric. Neither is
\leq_{fe}
for ultrafilters, which will follow from Proposition
4.1
.

Naturally we define
{\cal F}=_{fe}{\cal G}
if
{\cal F}\leq_{fe}{\cal G}
and
{\cal G}\leq_{fe}{\cal F}
and think of
\leq_{fe}
as an order on equivalence classes
[{\cal F}]_{fe}
of
=_{fe}
. Let us also denote
{\cal F}<_{fe}{\cal G}
if
{\cal F}\leq_{fe}{\cal G}
but
{\cal F}\neq_{fe}{\cal G}
.

The additive version of the following result is included in
[
2
]
, Theorem 4 with several more equivalent conditions. We include a direct proof here.

Proposition 2.2

For
A,B\in P({\mathbb{N}})
,
A\leq_{fe}B
if and only if there is
{\cal H}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\overline{A}\cdot{\cal H}\subseteq\overline{B}
.

Proof.
Firstly,
A\leq_{fe}B
actually means that the family
\{B/a:a\in A\}
has the finite intersection property. Let
{\cal H}
be an ultrafilter containing this family. Then, for every
{\cal F}\in\overline{A}
,
\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:B/n\in{\cal H}\}\supseteq A\in{\cal F}
, so
B\in{\cal F}\cdot{\cal H}
.

In the other direction, assume that
\overline{A}\cdot{\cal H}\subseteq\overline{B}
, but
\{B/a:a\in A\}
does not have the finite intersection property. Then there is
a\in A
such that
B/a\notin{\cal H}
, so
a{\cal H}\notin\overline{B}
, a contradiction.
\Box

Lemma 2.3

\leq_{fe}
coincides with each of the relations
\mid_{R},\mid_{L},\mid_{M},\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
on
{\mathbb{N}}\times\beta{\mathbb{N}}
. Also
{\cal G}\not\leq_{fe}m
for all
{\cal G}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}\setminus{\mathbb{N}}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.
As we already noted, for
\rho\in\{\mid_{R},\mid_{L},\mid_{M},\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt\}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
{\cal F}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}
,
m\rho{\cal F}
is equivalent to
m{\mathbb{N}}\in{\cal F}
. We show that the same holds for
\leq_{fe}
.

If
{\cal F}=n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then
\{n\}\in{\cal F}
so if there are a set
A
containing
\{m\}
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
kA\subseteq\{n\}
, it follows that
m\mid n
. On the other hand, if
m\mid n
(say
n=mk
) then every
B\in{\cal F}
contains
n
, so if we take
A=\{m\}
then
kA=\{n\}\subseteq B
.

If
{\cal F}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}\setminus{\mathbb{N}}
, assume first that
m{\mathbb{N}}\in{\cal F}
. Then every
B\in{\cal F}
contains an element
mk
divisible by
m
, so there is
A=\{m\}
such that
kA\subseteq B
. If we assume that
m{\mathbb{N}}\notin{\cal F}
, then
(m{\mathbb{N}})^{c}\in{\cal F}
. Now there can be no set
A
containing
\{m\}
such that
A\leq_{fe}(m{\mathbb{N}})^{c}
: if
F=\{m\}
then
kF\subseteq(m{\mathbb{N}})^{c}
does not hold for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

To prove the last statement, assume the opposite: that
{\cal G}\leq_{fe}m
. For
B=\{m\}
and any
A\in{\cal F}
, if
F\subseteq A
is of cardinality at least 2, then clearly
kF\subseteq B
does not hold for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
\Box

Having in mind the lemma above, in the next few proofs it suffices to consider only nonprincipal ultrafilters. The first one is a special case of
[
7
]
, Proposition 3.11, but we include a simple proof here.

Lemma 2.4

If
{\cal F},{\cal G}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}
, then
{\cal F}\leq_{fe}{\cal F}\cdot{\cal G}
and
{\cal G}\leq_{fe}{\cal F}\cdot{\cal G}
.

Proof.
Let
B\in{\cal F}\cdot{\cal G}
be arbitrary. Then
A:=\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:B/n\in{\cal G}\}\in{\cal F}
. For every finite
F\subseteq A
the set
\bigcap_{n\in F}B/n
is in
{\cal G}
, so it is nonempty. For any
k\in\bigcap_{n\in F}B/n
we have
kF\subseteq B
. Thus
{\cal F}\leq_{fe}{\cal F}\cdot{\cal G}
.

On the other hand, for any
n\in A
we have
B/n\in{\cal G}
and
n\cdot B/n\subseteq B
, so in particular for every finite
F\subseteq B/n
we have
nF\subseteq B
. Hence
B/n\leq_{fe}B
and
{\cal G}\leq_{fe}{\cal F}\cdot{\cal G}
.
\Box

Lemma 2.5

\mid_{M}\subset\leq_{fe}
.

Proof.
From Lemma
2.4
it follows directly that
{\cal F}\mid_{R}{\cal H}
implies
{\cal F}\leq_{fe}{\cal H}
and
{\cal G}\mid_{L}{\cal H}
implies
{\cal G}\leq_{fe}{\cal H}
. But
\mid_{M}
is the transitive closure of
\mid_{L}\cup\mid_{R}
and
\leq_{fe}
is transitive, so
\mid_{M}\subseteq\leq_{fe}
as well.

We will see in Proposition
4.1
that the
\mid_{M}
-maximal class is
K(\beta{\mathbb{N}},\cdot)
and the
\leq_{fe}
-maximal class is
\overline{K(\beta{\mathbb{N}},\cdot)}
, which strictly contains
K(\beta{\mathbb{N}},\cdot)
. Therefore the inclusion is strict.
\Box

Let us denote, for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
A,B\in P({\mathbb{N}})

F\in[A]^{m}
there is

such that

{\cal F},{\cal G}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}

A\in{\cal F}
such that
A\leq_{me}B

Lemma 2.6

For
{\cal F},{\cal G}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\cal F}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal G}
if and only if
{\cal F}\leq_{1e}{\cal G}
.

Proof.
If
{\cal F}\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt{\cal G}
then for any
B\in{\cal G}
we can take
A=B\hskip-2.84544pt\downarrow\in{\cal F}
and get

. On the other hand, if
B\in{\cal G}\cap{\cal V}
,
A\leq_{1e}B
implies
A\subseteq B
, so
A\in{\cal F}
implies
B\in{\cal F}
.
\Box

Lemma 2.7

\leq_{fe}\subset\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
.

Proof.
\leq_{fe}\subseteq\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
follows directly from the definition of
\leq_{fe}
and the lemma above. That the inclusion is strict will follow from the fact that there are (many)
\leq_{fe}
-minimal ultrafilters that are not
\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
-minimal (prime), see Theorems

3
Minimal ultrafilters

When speaking about minimal elements for divisibility relations, we exclude
1
, since it would trivially be the smallest for each such order. Thus,
\hskip 2.84526pt\mathaccent 869{\mid}\hskip 2.84526pt
-minimal elements are exactly the prime ultrafilters. The set of
\mid_{M}
-minimal elements is the set of all ultrafilters in
\beta{\mathbb{N}}\setminus((\beta{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\})\cdot(\beta{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\}))
. The following result says that there are many more such ultrafilters beside primes.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
4
]
, Theorem 6.35)
(\beta{\mathbb{N}}\setminus{\mathbb{N}})\cdot(\beta{\mathbb{N}}\setminus{\mathbb{N}})
is nowhere dense in
\beta{\mathbb{N}}\setminus{\mathbb{N}}
, i.e. for every
A\in[{\mathbb{N}}]^{\aleph_{0}}
there is
X\in[A]^{\aleph_{0}}
such that none of the elements of
\overline{X}
can be represented as a product of two elements from
\beta{\mathbb{N}}\setminus{\mathbb{N}}
.

Of course, for
{\cal F}\in\overline{X}
the proposition above only rules out
\mid_{M}
-divisors from
\beta{\mathbb{N}}\setminus{\mathbb{N}}
. We will see in the proof of Theorem
3.6
how to find ultrafilters not divisible by elements of
{\mathbb{N}}
either.

It was left unresolved in
[
6
]
whether there are any minimal elements for additive finite embeddability in
\beta{\mathbb{N}}\setminus{\mathbb{N}}
. For multiplicative
\leq_{fe}
we will show that there are many minimal elements in
(\beta{\mathbb{N}}\setminus{\mathbb{N}},\leq_{fe})
.

Lemma 3.2

Every
\leq_{fe}
-minimal element is either a prime number
p\in P
or an
{\mathbb{N}}
-free ultrafilter.

Proof.
Assume the opposite, that a
\leq_{fe}
-minimal ultrafilter
{\cal F}
is divisible by some
p\in P
, but not equal to
p
. Then
p\leq_{fe}{\cal F}
. However,
{\cal F}\not\neq_{fe}p
follows from Lemma
2.3
: if
{\cal F}\in{\mathbb{N}}
it is a consequence of the fact that the restriction of
\leq_{fe}
to
{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
is the ordinary divisibility, and otherwise it follows from the fact that there are no
{\cal F}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}\setminus{\mathbb{N}}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
{\cal F}\leq_{fe}n
.
\Box

However, not all
{\mathbb{N}}
-free ultrafilters are
\leq_{fe}
-minimal. For example, let
{\cal P},{\cal Q}\in\overline{P}\setminus P
. Then
{\cal P}\cdot{\cal Q}
is
{\mathbb{N}}
-free, but it is not minimal: by Lemma
2.4

{\cal P}\leq_{fe}{\cal P}\cdot{\cal Q}
, and
{\cal P}\cdot{\cal Q}\not\leq_{fe}{\cal P}
(because
{\cal P}\cdot{\cal Q}\;\mathaccent 869{\mathchar 13613\relax}\;{\cal P}
).

Lemma 3.3

For every ultrafilter
{\cal F}
,
{\cal F}^{n}={\cal G}^{\prime}\cdot{\cal H}^{\prime}
holds if and only if
{\cal G}^{\prime}=m_{1}{\cal G}^{n}
,
{\cal H}^{\prime}=m_{2}{\cal H}^{n}
and
m_{1}m_{2}=m^{n}
for some
m_{1},m_{2},m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
{\cal G},{\cal H}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}
such that
{\cal F}=m\cdot{\cal G}\cdot{\cal H}
.

Proof.
First, let
{\cal F}_{1}={\cal G}\cdot{\cal H}
,
{\cal F}=m{\cal F}_{1}
and
m^{n}=m_{1}m_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
. For any
A\in{\cal F}_{1}
we have
B:=\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}:A/k\in{\cal H}\}\in{\cal G}
. If
s=k^{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
, then
A^{n}/s=(A/k)^{n}
. Thus
\{s\in{\mathbb{N}}:A^{n}/s\in{\cal H}^{n}\}\supseteq B^{n}\in{\cal G}^{n}
, so
A^{n}\in{\cal G}^{n}\cdot{\cal H}^{n}
. This proves that
{\cal F}_{1}^{n}={\cal G}^{n}\cdot{\cal H}^{n}
, so
{\cal F}^{n}=m^{n}{\cal F}_{1}^{n}=m_{1}{\cal G}^{n}\cdot m_{2}{\cal H}^{n}
.

In the other direction, let
{\cal F}^{n}={\cal G}^{\prime}\cdot{\cal H}^{\prime}
for some
{\cal G}^{\prime},{\cal H}^{\prime}\in\beta{\mathbb{N}}
. Let

D:=\{p_{1}^{s_{1}}p_{2}^{s_{2}}\dots p_{r}^{s_{r}}:p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}\in P\mbox{ distinct }\land 0<s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{r}<n\}\cup\{1\}.

For any
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, by
l(d)
we denote the unique element od
D
such that
d\cdot l(d)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
. Then
{\mathbb{N}}^{n}/d=l(d){\mathbb{N}}^{n}
. Hence

C:=\{d\in{\mathbb{N}}:{\mathbb{N}}^{n}/d\in{\cal H}^{\prime}\}=\{d\in{\mathbb{N}}:l(d){\mathbb{N}}^{n}\in{\cal H}^{\prime}\}

is nonempty only if
m_{2}{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\in{\cal H}^{\prime}
for some
m_{2}\in D
. But for distinct
d_{1},d_{2}\in D
,
d_{1}{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\cap d_{2}{\mathbb{N}}^{n}=\emptyset
, so such an
m_{2}
is unique. If
m_{1}=l(m_{2})
then

. Hence
{\cal G}^{\prime}=m_{1}{\cal G}^{n}
and
{\cal H}^{\prime}=m_{2}{\cal H}^{n}
for some
{\cal G}
and
{\cal H}

m_{1}m_{2}=m^{n}
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
{\cal F}^{n}=m^{n}\cdot{\cal G}^{n}\cdot{\cal H}^{n}=(m\cdot{\cal G}\cdot{\cal H})^{n}

Corollary 5.4

(a)
\overline{K(\beta{\mathbb{N}},\cdot)}\subset MAX
.

(b)
K(\beta{\mathbb{N}},+)\not\subseteq MAX
.

Proof.
(a) By Lemma
4.5
we only need to show that the inclusion is strict. By Corollary
5.3
(c) there is a set
A
belonging to an ultrafilter
{\cal F}\in MAX
which is not M-piecewise syndetic. This means that
A
is not an element of any ultrafilter in
K(\beta{\mathbb{N}},\cdot)
. Since
\overline{A}
is an open set, it is disjoint from
\overline{K(\beta{\mathbb{N}},\cdot)}
, so
{\cal F}\notin\overline{K(\beta{\mathbb{N}},\cdot)}
.

(b) By Theorem
5.1
(d) there is
A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
which is A-piecewise syndetic set but not a MAX-set. Then there is an ultrafilter
{\cal F}\in\overline{A}\cap K(\beta{\mathbb{N}},+)
, but there is no ultrafilter from
MAX
in
\overline{A}
.
\Box

The

Problem 6.1

Find a nice nonstandard characterization of
\leq_{fe}
for ultrafilters.

{\cal F}\leq_{fe}{\cal G}
but

-minimal ultrafilters.

(b) Is every
\mid_{M}

\leq_{fe}
-minimal?

\mid_{M}
-minimal ultrafilters.

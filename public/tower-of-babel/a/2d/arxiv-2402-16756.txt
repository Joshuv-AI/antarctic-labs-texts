Exact Jacobi elliptic solutions of the a ​ b ​ c ​ d abcd -system
JAKE DANIELS 1 and NGHIEM V. NGUYEN 1

Abstract
In this manuscript, consideration is given to the existence of periodic traveling-wave solutions to the a ​ b ​ c ​ d abcd -system. This system was derived by Bona, Saut, and Chen to describe small amplitude, long wavelength gravity waves on the surface of water. These exact solutions are formulated in terms of the Jacobi elliptic function cnoidal. The existence of explicit traveling-wave solutions is very useful in theoretical investigations such as stability of solutions, as well as other numerical analysis of the system.

Introduction

Let
\Omega_{t}
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
whose boundary consists of two parts: the
fixed surface located at
z=-h(x,y)
, and the free surface
z=\eta(x,y,t)
. It is well-known
that the three-dimensional capillary-gravity waves on an inviscid, irrotational, and
incompressible fluid layer of uniform depth
h
that is impermeable on the fixed surface are
governed by the Euler equations

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\Delta\phi=0,\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega_{t};

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\eta_{t}+\phi_{x}\eta_{x}+\phi_{y}\eta_{y}-\phi_{z}=0,\quad\text{at}\ z=\eta(x,y,t);

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\phi_{t}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+gz=0,\quad\text{at}\ z=\eta(x,y,t);

(1.1d)

\displaystyle\phi_{x}h_{x}+\phi_{y}h_{y}+\phi_{z}=0,\quad\text{at}\ z=-h(x,y).

Here,
g
denotes the acceleration of gravity;
\phi(x,y,z,t)
is the velocity potential
function. The full Euler equations (
1.1a
)-(
1.1d
) are often far
more complex than necessary for many applications, so several approximation
models have been derived in its place under certain restricted physical regimes.
One such model is the
abcd
-system, introduced by Bona
et

al
. in
[
4
,
5
]
,
to describe small amplitude, long wavelength gravity waves on the surface of water

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\eta_{t}+w_{x}+(w\eta)_{x}+aw_{xxx}-b\eta_{xxt}=0\,,\\
w_{t}+\eta_{x}+ww_{x}+c\eta_{xxx}-dw_{xxt}=0\,,\end{cases}

where
a,b,c,
and
d
are real constants and
\theta\in[0,1]
that satisfy

(1.3)

\begin{split}a+b&=\frac{1}{2}(\theta^{2}-\frac{1}{3}),\\
c+d&=\frac{1}{2}(1-\theta^{2})\geq 0,\\
a+b+c+d&=\frac{1}{3}.\end{split}

Precisely, the regime for which the above system is derived to approximate the Euler equations
is when the maximal deviation
\alpha
of the free surface is small and a typical wavelength

\lambda
is large as compared to the undisturbed water depth
h
, such that the Stokes number

S=\frac{\alpha\lambda^{2}}{h^{3}}
is of order one. The functions
\eta(x,t)
and
w(x,t)
are
real valued and
x,t\in\mathbb{R}
. By choosing specific values for the parameters
a,b,c,
and

d
, the system (
1.2
) includes a wide range of other systems that have been
derived over the last few decades such as the classical Boussinesq system
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
,
the Kaup system
[
21
]
, the coupled Benjamin-Bona-Mahony system (BBM-system)
[
3
]
,
the coupled Korteweg-de Vries system (KdV-system)
[
4
,
5
]
,
the Bona-Smith system
[
6
]
, and the integrable version of Boussinesq system
[
22
]
.
In particular, these specializations are:

•

Classical Boussinesq system

\left\{\begin{matrix}\begin{split}&\eta_{t}+w_{x}+(w\eta)_{x}=0,\\
&w_{t}+\eta_{x}+ww_{x}-\frac{1}{3}w_{xxt}=0;\end{split}\end{matrix}\right.

•

Kaup system

\left\{\begin{matrix}\begin{split}&\eta_{t}+w_{x}+(w\eta)_{x}+\frac{1}{3}w_{xxx}=0,\\
&w_{t}+\eta_{x}+ww_{x}=0;\end{split}\end{matrix}\right.

•

Bona-Smith system

\left\{\begin{matrix}\begin{split}&\eta_{t}+w_{x}+(w\eta)_{x}-b\eta_{xxt}=0,\\
&w_{t}+\eta_{x}+ww_{x}+c\eta_{xxx}-bw_{xxt}=0;\end{split}\end{matrix}\right.

•

Coupled BBM-system

\left\{\begin{matrix}\begin{split}&\eta_{t}+w_{x}+(w\eta)_{x}-\frac{1}{6}\eta_{xxt}=0,\\
&w_{t}+\eta_{x}+ww_{x}-\frac{1}{6}w_{xxt}=0;\end{split}\end{matrix}\right.

•

Coupled KdV-system

\left\{\begin{matrix}\begin{split}&\eta_{t}+w_{x}+(w\eta)_{x}+\frac{1}{6}w_{xxx}=0,\\
&w_{t}+\eta_{x}+ww_{x}+\frac{1}{6}\eta_{xxx}=0;\end{split}\end{matrix}\right.

•

Coupled KdV-BBM system

\left\{\begin{matrix}\begin{split}&\eta_{t}+w_{x}+(w\eta)_{x}+\frac{1}{6}w_{xxx}=0,\\
&w_{t}+\eta_{x}+ww_{x}-\frac{1}{6}w_{xxt}=0;\end{split}\end{matrix}\right.

•

Coupled BBM-KdV system

\left\{\begin{matrix}\begin{split}&\eta_{t}+w_{x}+(w\eta)_{x}-\frac{1}{6}\eta_{xxt}=0,\\
&w_{t}+\eta_{x}+ww_{x}+\frac{1}{6}\eta_{xxx}=0.\end{split}\end{matrix}\right.

For a more detailed discussion on this, we refer our readers to the papers

[
4
,
5
]
.

In this manuscript, attention is given to the existence of periodic traveling-wave
solutions to the
abcd
-system. A traveling-wave solution to the system
(
1.2
) is a vector solution

\big(\eta(x,t),w(x,t)\big)
of the form

(1.4)

\eta(x,t)=\eta(x-\sigma t)\quad\text{and}\quad w(x,t)=w(x-\sigma t),

where
\sigma
denotes the speed of the waves. Notice that when
\sigma>0
, one obtains
a right-propagating solution, while for
\sigma<0
, one has a left-propagating solution.
This feature of bi-direction propagation of
(\eta,w)
is a hallmark of the
abcd
-system
in its derivation and is the main difference between system (
1.2
) and the one-way
approximation models such as

u_{t}+u_{x}+\frac{1}{p}(u^{p})_{x}\pm Lu_{x}=0,\ \ \ p>0,

(which is commonly referred to as the generalized KdV equation when
L=\partial_{xx}
,
the generalized BBM equation when
L=-\partial_{xt}
, and simply the KdV and BBM equations,
respectively, when
p=1
), and the cubic, nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS)

iu_{t}+u_{xx}+|u|^{2}u=0.

It would also be interesting to analyze the existence of periodic solutions when the two components
propagate at different speeds, and/or when the two components travel in opposite directions.
Such scenarios are not considered in the scope of this paper.

The most studied types of traveling-wave solutions are periodic traveling-wave solutions and solitary-wave solutions.
Periodic traveling-wave solutions are self-explanatory, while solitary waves are smooth traveling-wave
solutions that are symmetric around a
single
maximum and rapidly decay to zero away from the
maximum. Though less common, the term solitary wave is
sometimes used to describe traveling-wave solutions that are symmetric around a single maximum but
approach nonzero constants as
\xi=x-\sigma t\rightarrow\pm\infty
.
In this manuscript, we consider non-trivial solutions to be solutions where both components are non-constant, and
semi-trivial solutions to be solutions where one component is a constant.
The constant solution,
(f,g)
for any
f,\,g\in\mathbb{R}
, is always a solution and will be referred to as the trivial solution.

The topic of existence of explicit periodic traveling-wave solutions for many single equations are readily
available such as for the KdV equation
[
18
]
, generalized KdV equation
[
13
]
, BBM equation
[
1
]
,
NLS equation
[
20
]
, and Whitham equation
[
19
]
. In contrast, there are far less of these results for
systems due to the complexity of the coupled equations involved. Leveling up the scalar results to systems
is no minor task, requiring new insights and new approaches. For the classical Boussinesq system, Krishnan
showed that the periodic traveling-wave solutions exist and are given by Jacobi elliptic functions

[
22
]
. Chen and Li
[
12
]
(see also
[
25
]
) have recently established the periodic traveling-wave
solutions for the beta derivative of the Kaup system where the first temporal derivative
\partial_{t}
is replaced
by the fractional derivative
\partial^{\beta}_{t}
, with
\beta\in(0,1]
, given in terms of normal trigonometic
functions as well as the Jacobi cnoidal functions. Four systems have been put forth recently by Nguyen

et al
. to describe the interaction of long and short waves in dispersive media
[
17
,
24
,
23
]
, and
periodic traveling-wave solutions given by the Jacobi elliptic function cnoidal have been explicitly calculated
for all four systems
[
11
]
.

Another important subject regarding traveling-wave solutions is the stability of these solutions. The topic of stability of
periodic traveling-wave solutions such as cnoidal waves has attracted far less consideration
than that of solitary wave solutions. Within this limited attention, explicitly known formulae for periodic traveling-wave
solutions often play an instrumental role. Angula, Bona and Scialom
[
2
]
proved that the periodic
traveling-waves for the KdV equation are nonlinearly orbitally stable with respect to perturbations of the same period.
Central to the argument in their proof is the fact that the cnoidal waves lie in the set of minimizers, a conclusion
that would be hard to establish without having explicit formula for the cnoidal waves at hand.
Deconinck
et

al
. established that the KdV cnoidal waves are spectrally stable with respect
to perturbations of arbitrary period
[
7
]
, and orbitally stable with respect to sub-harmonic perturbations
[
16
]
.
Again, the explicit formulae for cnoidal waves play pivital roles in their numerical computations.

In this paper, periodic traveling-wave solutions to the system (
1.2
) of the form

(1.5)

\eta(\xi)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}j_{r}\operatorname{cn}^{r}(\lambda\xi,m)\quad\text{and}\quad w(\xi)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}k_{r}\operatorname{cn}^{r}(\lambda\xi,m)\,,

are analyzed, where
j_{r},\,k_{r}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda>0
,
m\in(0,1]
,
\xi=x-\sigma t
, and
\operatorname{cn}
denotes the Jacobi
elliptic function cnoidal. For the readers’ convenience, a brief introduction to Jacobi elliptic functions can be found in Section 2.
If
m=0
, then (
1.5
) reduces to a cosine series which
is not the aim of this paper and is excluded. The analysis of (
1.5
) requires a case split for when
c\neq 0
and
c=0
.
For the first case,
c\neq 0
, it is shown that non-trivial periodic traveling-wave solutions only exist when
a^{2}+b^{2}\neq 0
,
and in particular,
j_{r}=k_{r}=0
for
r\geq 3
.
When
a^{2}+b^{2}=0
, it is established that only semi-trivial periodic traveling-wave solutions exist, and that

j_{r}=0
for all
r\geq 1
and
k_{r}=0
for
r\geq 3
.
For the second case,
c=0
, it is shown that non-trivial periodic traveling-wave solutions only exist when
b^{2}+d^{2}\neq 0
,
and that
j_{r}=0
for
r=1,\,3
and
r\geq 5
and
k_{r}=0
for
r=1

and
r\geq 3
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, some facts and identities of Jacobi elliptic functions are
reviewed. In Section 3, the existence of periodic traveling-wave solutions to the system (
1.2

is established. The periodic traveling-wave solutions are then explicitly computed in Section 4.
Finally, a discussion about the obtained results is given in Section 5, including remarks about how these

[

15
]
.

Preliminaries

For the readers’ convinience, some notions of the Jacobi elliptic
functions are briefly recalled here. Let

v=\int_{0}^{\phi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-m^{2}\sin^{2}t}}dt,\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{for $0\leq m\leq 1$}.

Then
v=F(\phi,m)
, or equivalently,
\phi=F^{-1}(v,m)=\operatorname{am}(v,m)

which is the Jacobi amplitude and
m
is referred to as the Jacobi elliptic modulus. The two basic Jacobi elliptic
functions
\operatorname{cn}(v,m)
and
\operatorname{sn}(v,m)
are defined as

\operatorname{sn}(v,m)=\sin(\phi)=\sin\big(F^{-1}(v,m)\big)\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{cn}(v,m)=\cos(\phi)=\cos\big(F^{-1}(v,m)\big).

These
functions are generalizations of the trigonometric and hyperbolic
functions which satisfy

\begin{matrix}\operatorname{cn}(v,0)=\cos(v),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{sn}(v,0)=\sin(v),\\
\operatorname{cn}(v,1)=\operatorname{sech}(v),\ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{sn}(v,1)=\tanh(v).\end{matrix}

We recall the following identities:

\left\{\begin{matrix}\begin{split}&\operatorname{sn}^{2}(\lambda\xi,m)=1-\operatorname{cn}^{2}(\lambda\xi,m),\\
&\operatorname{dn}^{2}(\lambda\xi,m)=1-m^{2}+m^{2}\operatorname{cn}^{2}(\lambda\xi,m),\\
&\frac{d}{d\xi}\operatorname{cn}(\lambda\xi,m)=-\lambda\operatorname{sn}(\lambda\xi,m)\operatorname{dn}(\lambda\xi,m),\\
&\frac{d}{d\xi}\operatorname{sn}(\lambda\xi,m)=\lambda\operatorname{cn}(\lambda\xi,m)\operatorname{dn}(\lambda\xi,m),\\
&\frac{d}{d\xi}\operatorname{dn}(\lambda\xi,m)=-m^{2}\lambda\operatorname{cn}(\lambda\xi,m)\operatorname{sn}(\lambda\xi,m).\end{split}\end{matrix}\right.

The following relations will be useful

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{matrix}\begin{split}&\frac{d}{d\xi}\operatorname{cn}^{r}=-r\lambda\operatorname{cn}^{r-1}\operatorname{sn}\operatorname{dn},\\
&\frac{d^{2}}{d\xi^{2}}\operatorname{cn}^{r}=-r\lambda^{2}\big[(r+1)m^{2}\operatorname{cn}^{r+2}+r(1-2m^{2})\operatorname{cn}^{r}+(r-1)(m^{2}-1)\operatorname{cn}^{r-2}\big],\\
&\frac{d^{3}}{d\xi^{3}}\operatorname{cn}^{r}=r\lambda^{3}\operatorname{sn}\operatorname{dn}\big[(r+1)(r+2)m^{2}\operatorname{cn}^{r+1}+r^{2}(1-2m^{2})\operatorname{cn}^{r-1}+(r-1)(r-2)(m^{2}-1)\operatorname{cn}^{r-3}\big],\\
\end{split}\end{matrix}\right.

where the argument
(\lambda\xi,m)
has been dropped for clarity
reason.

3
Existence of Periodic Traveling-Wave Solutions

Substituting the traveling-wave ansatz
(
1.4
) into the abcd-system (
1.2
)
yields the following system of ordinary differential equations (ODE)

(3.1)

\begin{cases}-\sigma\eta^{\prime}+w^{\prime}+(\eta w)^{\prime}+aw^{\prime\prime\prime}+b\sigma\eta^{\prime\prime\prime}=0\,,\\
-\sigma w^{\prime}+\eta^{\prime}+ww^{\prime}+c\eta^{\prime\prime\prime}+d\sigma w^{\prime\prime\prime}=0\,,\end{cases}

where the primes denote the derivatives with respect to the moving
frame
\xi=x-\sigma t
. Replacing
\eta
and
w
in (
3.1
) with the
periodic traveling-wave ansatz (
1.5
) and using cnoidal derivative identities
(
2.1
), the following generic form is obtained

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{matrix}\begin{split}&-\lambda\operatorname{sn}(\lambda\xi,m)\operatorname{dn}(\lambda\xi,m)\sum_{q=0}^{2n-1}h_{1,q}\operatorname{cn}^{q}(\lambda\xi,m)=0,\\
&-\lambda\operatorname{sn}(\lambda\xi,m)\operatorname{dn}(\lambda\xi,m)\sum_{q=0}^{2n-1}h_{2,q}\operatorname{cn}^{q}(\lambda\xi,m)=0,\end{split}\end{matrix}\right.

where the subscript
q
in
h_{1,q}
and
h_{2,q}
corresponds with the
power of
\operatorname{cn}
in their respective equations.

The analysis is now split into two distinct cases. We will show that the
the series (
1.5
) will terminate after
a certain number of terms. When exactly this termination will occur is
found to be dependent on the values of the parameters
a,\,b,\,c,
and
d
.

3.1
The case when
c\neq 0

Notice that as
(
3.2
) must hold true for all
(\lambda\xi,m)
, it must be
the case that
h_{1,q}=0
and
h_{2,q}=0
for all
q
.
Using this, the following theorem is established.
(Recall that
j_{r}
and
k_{r}
are the coefficients of
\operatorname{cn}^{r}
as given
in (
1.5
) for
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.)

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
c\neq 0
, then the periodic traveling-wave ansatz (
1.5
)
will take on one of the following forms depending on the values of
a
and
b
:

(i)

If
a^{2}+b^{2}\neq 0
, then
j_{r}=k_{r}=0

for all integers
r\geq 3
and (
1.5
) reduces to

(3.3)

\eta(\xi)=j_{0}+j_{1}\operatorname{cn}(\lambda\xi,m)+j_{2}\operatorname{cn}^{2}(\lambda\xi,m)\quad\text{and}\quad w(\xi)=k_{0}+k_{1}\operatorname{cn}(\lambda\xi,m)+k_{2}\operatorname{cn}^{2}(\lambda\xi,m)\,.

(ii)

If
a^{2}+b^{2}=0
, then
j_{r}=0
for all integers

r\geq 1
and
k_{r}=0
for all integers
r\geq 3
. Thus,
(
1.5
) reduces to

(3.4)

\eta(\xi)=j_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad w(\xi)=k_{0}+k_{1}\operatorname{cn}(\lambda\xi,m)+k_{2}\operatorname{cn}^{2}(\lambda\xi,m)\,.

Consequently, only semi-trivial periodic traveling-wave solutions exist
in this case.

Proof.

(i)
Let
\rho(\eta^{\prime})
denote the largest power of
\operatorname{cn}

in
\eta^{\prime}
. Then,

\rho(\eta^{\prime})=\rho(w^{\prime})=n-1
,
\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})=\rho(w^{\prime\prime\prime})=n+1
,  and
\rho((\eta w)^{\prime})=\rho(ww^{\prime})=2n-1
.

From this, if
n>2
then
2n-1>n+1
, which implies that the coefficient of

\operatorname{cn}^{2n-1}
in the second equation of (
3.2
) will solely
come from the
ww^{\prime}
-term. Thus,
h_{2,2n-1}=nk_{n}^{2}
. Setting this coefficient equal to zero
gives
k_{n}=0
. Recalculating the above orders yields

\rho(\eta^{\prime})=n-1
,
\rho(w^{\prime})=n-2
,
\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})=n+1
,
\rho(w^{\prime\prime\prime})=n
,
\rho((\eta w)^{\prime})=2n-2
,  and
\rho(ww^{\prime})=2n-3
.

After
i
iterations, the orders become

\rho(\eta^{\prime})=n-1
,
\rho(w^{\prime})=n-2-i
,
\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})=n+1
,
\rho(w^{\prime\prime\prime})=n-i
,
\rho((\eta w)^{\prime})=2n-2-i
,  and
\rho(ww^{\prime})=2n-3-2i
.

This argument can be repeated until
\rho(ww^{\prime})\leq\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})
.
Now, consider the two possibilities for
n
.

•

If n is even: let
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
such that

n=2z
. Then
\rho(ww^{\prime})=2n-3-2i=4z-3-2i
and
\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})=n+1=2z+1
.
Since
\rho(ww^{\prime})
and
\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})
are both odd, there exists an
integer
i
such that
\rho(ww^{\prime})=\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})
. Solving for

gives

\displaystyle 4z-3-2i

\displaystyle=2z+1.

Thus,
\rho(ww^{\prime})=\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})
when

. Recalculating the

\rho(\eta^{\prime})=2z-1
,
\rho(w^{\prime})=z
,
\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})=2z+1
,
\rho(w^{\prime\prime\prime})=z+2
,
\rho((\eta w)^{\prime})=3z
,  and
\rho(ww^{\prime})=2z+1
.

•

If n is odd: let
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
such that

n=2z+1
. Then
\rho(ww^{\prime})=2n-3-2i=4z-1-2i
and
\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})=n+1=2z+2
.
Since
\rho(ww^{\prime})
is odd and
\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})

such that
\rho(ww^{\prime})=\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})
. So this

\rho(ww^{\prime})<\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})
, which will happen when

\displaystyle 4z-1-2i

\displaystyle<2z+2,

which gives

. Thus,

will be the first iteration

\rho(\eta^{\prime})=2z
,
\rho(w^{\prime})=z
,
\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})=2z+2
,
\rho(w^{\prime\prime\prime})=z+2
,
\rho((\eta w)^{\prime})=3z+1
,  and
\rho(ww^{\prime})=2z+1
.

However,
\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})>\rho(ww^{\prime})
, so from the second

will solely come from

\eta^{\prime\prime\prime}
-term, which implies

are not zero, it must be the case that

\rho(\eta^{\prime})=2z-1

\rho(w^{\prime})=z

,
\rho(w^{\prime\prime\prime})=z+2
,
\rho((\eta w)^{\prime})=3z
,  and
\rho(ww^{\prime})=2z+1

•

If

: recalculating the orders

\rho(\eta^{\prime})=2z-1
,
\rho(w^{\prime})=z-1
,
\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})=2z+1
,
\rho(w^{\prime\prime\prime})=z+1
,
\rho((\eta w)^{\prime})=3z-1
,  and

.

. Thus,

\rho(\eta^{\prime})=2z-3
,
\rho(w^{\prime})=z-1
,
\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})=2z-1
,
\rho(w^{\prime\prime\prime})=z+1
,
\rho((\eta w)^{\prime})=3z-3
,  and

•

If
j_{2z}=0
: recalculating the orders

\rho(\eta^{\prime})=2z-2
,
\rho(w^{\prime})=z
,
\rho(\eta^{\prime\prime\prime})=2z
,
\rho(w^{\prime\prime\prime})=z+2
,
\rho((\eta w)^{\prime})=3z-1
,  and

The semi-trivial solution (

When we evaluate system (

and

collapses to a single equation. This equation is

which is exactly the BBM equation after a transformation. This equation has been shown
to have cnoidal wave solutions
[

, so it is especially important
that we recovered a cnoidal solution for this case.

Another fundamental property of the Jacobi cnoidal function is that as
m
approaches
one, the cnoidal function limits to the hyperbolic secant function. This is important
because solitary-wave solutions are often given in terms of hyperbolic secant functions.
Thus, in principle, taking the limit of our solutions as
m

retrieve solitary-wave solutions. Indeed, for (

4.4

m=1

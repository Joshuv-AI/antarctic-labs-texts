Hermite–Einstein metrics in singular settings
Junyan Cao, Patrick Graf, Philipp Naumann, Mihai Paun, Thomas Peternell, Xiaojun Wu

Abstract




1.
Preliminaries and main Results

1.1.
Abstract

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a normal Kähler space in the sense of Grauert, i.e. the Kähler metric
\omega_{X}
has local potentials which are induced by smooth, strictly plurisubharmonic functions defined on the (local) ambient space.
In this article we pursue the following main goals.

\bullet
We establish the existence of Hermite–Einstein metrics for
\omega_{X}
-stable reflexive coherent sheaves
\mathcal{F}
on
X
.
Our method relies on a recent, very important work of Guo, Phong and Sturm
[
GPS22
]
.
It is explained in Sections 1–5.

\bullet
Assume that the space
X
has at most klt singularities.
Then it has quotient singularities in the complement of a set
Z\subset X
of codimension at least three, by the work of
[
GKKP11
]
.
If moreover
\mathcal{F}|_{X\setminus Z}
admits a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle structure, then we obtain a more precise result.
Namely, we show that given any open subset
U\subset X
containing
Z
, the said Hermite–Einstein metric on
\mathcal{F}
is compatible with the quotient structure when restricted to
X\setminus U
.
These results are established in Section 6 and 7.

\bullet
The main motivation for the questions treated in this article was a conjecture formulated by Campana, Höring and Peternell in
[
CHP21
, Conj. 0.1]
.
We discuss a particular case of it in Section 8.

1.3.
Hermitian metrics on reflexive sheaves

In order to state our main results, we start by recalling the notion of metric for a coherent, torsion free sheaf in the spirit of
Grauert and Riemenschneider in
[
GR70
]
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact normal Kähler space. We fix a finite covering
(A_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of
X
such that for each index
\alpha
the corresponding analytic set
A_{\alpha}
admits an embedding in the unit ball
U_{\alpha}
in
\mathbb{C}^{N_{\alpha}}
. Also, here we adopt the definition according to which the metric
\displaystyle\omega_{X}|_{A_{\alpha}}
is equal to the
dd^{c}
of a smooth, strictly psh function defined on
U_{\alpha}
restricted to
X
.

We denote by
\mathcal{F}
a reflexive sheaf on
X
; we can assume that we have the exact sequence

(1.0.1)

{\mathcal{O}}_{A_{\alpha}}^{p_{\alpha}}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{A_{\alpha}}^{q_{\alpha}}\to\mathcal{F}|_{A_{\alpha}}\to 0

for each index
\alpha
, where
p_{\alpha}
and
q_{\alpha}
are positive integers. We are using these maps in order to construct a Hermitian metric
h_{\mathcal{F},0}
on
\mathcal{F}
as follows.

Let
(\rho_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a partition of unit subordinate to

(A_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
and let
s_{1},s_{2}
be two local sections of
\mathcal{F}
; we define their scalar product
h_{\mathcal{F},0}
by the formula

(1.0.2)

\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle:=\sum\rho_{\alpha}\langle\sigma_{\alpha 1},\sigma_{\alpha 2}\rangle_{\alpha}

where
\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha}
is the -trivial- scalar product on

{\mathcal{O}}_{A_{\alpha}}^{q_{\alpha}}
and
\sigma_{i}
are sections of
{\mathcal{O}}_{A_{\alpha}}^{q_{\alpha}}
such that

\pi_{\alpha}(\sigma_{\alpha i})=s_{i},\qquad\langle\sigma_{\alpha i},\tau\rangle_{\alpha}=0

for any section
\tau
in the image of the first map of the sequence (
1.0.1
).
We denote by
\pi_{\alpha}
the second application in (
1.0.1
).

For example, consider the maximum ideal sheaf

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:=(z_{1},z_{2})\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}
, together with the maps

\alpha(f):=(z_{2}f,-z_{1}f),\qquad\beta(f_{1},f_{2}):=z_{1}f_{1}+z_{2}f_{2}.

They induce an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}^{2}\to\mathcal{F}\to 0
, from which one can compute the metric. If
s:=z_{1}
, then we get

|s|^{2}_{h_{\mathcal{F}}}=\frac{|z_{1}|^{2}}{|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}}.

Another indication showing that the metric
h_{\mathcal{F},0}
is natural is given by its connection with the desingularisation of the sheaf

\mathcal{F}
, as follows. An important result due to H. Rossi, cf.
[
Ros68
]
, shows the existence of a birational map
\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
such that

\widetilde{X}
is non-singular and such that the inverse image
E:=\pi^{\star}(\mathcal{F})/\mathrm{Tor}
of our sheaf
\mathcal{F}
modulo its torsion is
locally free.

Let
Z
be the union of the singular set of
X
and
\mathcal{F}
, and set
X_{0}:=X\setminus Z
.
In sub-section
2.1.1
we prove the following result (see Lemma
2.3
as well as the remarks immediately afterwards).

Theorem 1.1
.

The bundle
E
admits a Hermitian metric
h_{E}
such that there exists a constant
C>0
for which the inequality

\sqrt{-1}\Theta(E,h_{E})\geqslant-C\pi^{\star}(\omega_{X})\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E}

is satisfied. Moreover, the restriction of the metric
h_{E}
to the
\pi
-inverse image of
X_{0}
is induced by a metric
h_{\mathcal{F}}
on
\mathcal{F}
constructed by a similar procedure as above.

More precisely, the metric
h_{\mathcal{F}}
will be the dual of the metric
h_{\mathcal{F}^{\star},0}
defined on
\mathcal{F}^{\star}
as in (
1.0.2
).

On the vector bundle
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}|_{X_{0}}
the metric
h_{\mathcal{F},0}
is obtained by piecing together the quotient metrics induced by (
1.0.1
).
We denote by
\phi_{Z}\leqslant 0
a function with log-poles along the generators of the ideal of
Z
and then we have the following statement.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a reflexive coherent sheaf on a normal Kähler space
(X,\omega_{X})
. We assume that
\mathcal{F}
is stable with respect to the Kähler metric
\omega_{X}
. Then we can construct a metric
h_{\mathcal{F}}=h_{\mathcal{F},0}\exp(s)
on
\mathcal{F}|_{X_{0}}
with the following properties:

(i)

It is smooth and it satisfies the Hermite-Einstein equation on
X_{0}
.

(ii)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that the inequalities

\mathrm{Tr}\exp(s)\leqslant C,\qquad\exp(s)\geqslant Ce^{N\phi_{Z}}\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}

hold pointwise on
X_{0}
, where
N>0
is a positive, large enough constant.

(iii)

We have

\int_{X_{0}}\frac{1}{-\phi_{Z}}|D^{\prime}s|^{2}dV\leqslant C,\quad\int_{X_{0}}|\Theta(\mathcal{F},h_{\mathcal{F}})|^{2}dV<\infty

where the volume element
dV
is induced by the metric
\omega_{X}
and the norm
|\cdot|
is with respect to
h_{\mathcal{F},0}
.

Thus, our main contributions to the HE theme can be summarised as follows.

(1)

We allow both
X
and
\mathcal{F}
to be singular.
Modulo the work of
[
GPS22
]
, our proof of Theorem

1.2
is self-contained, in the sense that it only relies on the basic arguments of S. Donaldson
[
Don85
]
, Uhlenbeck-Yau
[
UY86
]
and C. Simpson
[
Sim88
]
. The literature on this subject is abundant, and we only refer to the most recent preprints
[
CW
]
,
[
Chen23
]
,
[
CT23
]
and the references therein for the construction of admissible Hermite-Einstein metrics, as well as for new and very promising points of view in this circle of ideas in
[
JCS22
]
.

(2)

We obtain estimates.
Indeed, the estimates in (ii), (iii) above are more precise than producing an admissible HE metric, cf.
[
BS94
]
and the references quoted above.

In some sense, the proof of Theorem
1.2
is quite standard, and it will be done along the following lines. By using the aforementioned result of Rossi combined with
a theorem due to M. Toma, cf.
[
Tom19
]
, the bundle
E
is stable with respect to any positive, small enough perturbation
\omega_{t}
of the inverse image metric
\pi^{\star}\omega_{X}
. We can therefore construct a Hermitian metric on
E
, which differs from
h_{E}
obtained in
Theorem
1.1
above by a positive endomorphism of
E
and which is Hermite-Einstein with respect to
\omega_{t}
. The tricky part is to extract a limit as
t\to 0
. It is at this point that the mean-value inequality in
[
GPS22
]
is playing a crucial role: thanks to it we obtain uniform estimates for our family of endomorphisms, which combine nicely with the approach of Simpson in
[
Sim88
]
, and ultimately allow us to conclude.

Assume next that locally on some open subset

\mathcal{A}\subset X
, the singularities of
X
are quotient and the restriction
\mathcal{F}|_{A}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-sheaf.
As consequence of Theorem
1.2

we show that the endomorphism
s
defining the HE metric
h_{\mathcal{F}}|_{A}
is bounded when restricted to
\mathcal{A}
, cf. Theorem
5.2
. Thus, the metric
h_{\mathcal{F}}|_{A}
that we construct is
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-close to the orbifold metric of
\mathcal{F}
. Since we are unable to obtain higher order estimates,
this is not precise enough in order to allow us to evaluate e.g. the orbifold Chern classes of
\mathcal{F}
.

This issue is partly addressed in our next result. Let
X
be a compact Kähler space with at most klt singularities. Then by the results in
[
GKKP11
]

there exists an analytic subset
W\subset X
such that
\mathrm{codim}_{X}W\geqslant 3
and such that
X\setminus W
has at most quotient singularities.

It would be ideal to have at our disposal a Kähler metric on
X
whose restriction to

X\setminus W
accounts for the quotient singularities, i.e. its pull-back via the local uniformisations is
quasi-isometric to the flat metric.
Unfortunately, we see no natural candidate for such a metric.
Our substitute for it is contained in the following statement.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
U\subset X
be any open subset of
X
such that
W\subset U
. Let
U^{\prime}
be some open set such that
W\subset U^{\prime}\Subset U
.
Then there exists a Kähler metric
\omega_{\rm orb}

whose restriction to
U^{\prime}
is smooth, and whose restriction to
X\setminus U
has conic singularities, in the sense that its pull-back via the uniformising morphisms is quasi-isometric with the flat metric.

If
X
is projective, the existence of
\omega_{\rm orb}
is a consequence of the following “orbifold partial resolution” theorem, a very important result due to C. Li and G. Tian, cf.
[
LT19
, Thm. 3]
:

there exists a projective variety
\widehat{X}
with at most quotient singularities, together with a birational map
p\colon\widehat{X}\to X
such that
p
is biholomorphic when restricted to
X\setminus W
.

Then we simply consider a Kähler metric with conic singularities on
\widehat{X}
.
Its direct image, modified on
U
by the usual maximum procedure, will be our desired
\omega_{\rm orb}
.

Then we obtain the following more precise version of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a klt Kähler space and
let
\mathcal{F}
be a stable
\mathbb{Q}
-sheaf on
(X,\omega_{\rm orb})
. There exists a metric
h_{\mathcal{F}}=h_{\mathcal{F},0}\exp(s)
on
\mathcal{F}|_{X_{0}}
with the following properties:

(i)

It is smooth and it satisfies the Hermite-Einstein equation on
\displaystyle(X_{0},\omega_{\rm orb}|_{X_{0}})
. Moreover, it has quotient singularities when restricted to

X\setminus U
.

(ii)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that the inequalities

\mathrm{Tr}\exp(s)\leqslant C,\qquad\exp(s)\geqslant Ce^{N\phi_{Z}}\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}

hold pointwise on
X_{0}
, where
N>0
is a positive, large enough constant.

(iii)

We have

\int_{X_{0}}\frac{1}{-\phi_{Z}}|D^{\prime}s|^{2}dV\leqslant C,\quad\int_{X_{0}}|\Theta(\mathcal{F},h_{\mathcal{F}})|^{2}dV<\infty

where the volume element
dV
is induced by the metric
\omega_{\rm orb}
and the norm
|\cdot|
is with respect to
h_{\mathcal{F},0}
.

In particular, the Hermite–Einstein metric
h_{\mathcal{F}}
becomes non-singular when pulled back via the local uniformization maps providing the orbifold structure of
X\setminus U
.
This is the main reason for which we are using the metric
\omega_{\rm orb}
:
we show that the metric
h_{\mathcal{F}}
satisfying the estimates in Theorem
1.4
and the Hermite–Einstein equation with respect to
\omega_{\rm orb}
is automatically smooth in orbifold sense, cf. Theorem
7.2
.

Theorem
1.4
is proved along the same lines as Theorem
1.2
. However, we have to deal with an additional layer of
difficulties: we are obliged to work with the metric
\omega_{\rm orb}
(whose inverse image
on a desingularization of
X
and
\mathcal{F}

has both poles and zeroes), for the reasons just mentioned. Then, prior to applying the result
[
GPS22
]
we have to construct
a family of smooth metrics approximating (the pull-back of)
\omega_{\rm orb}
, such that the hypothesis in
[
GPS22
]
are satisfied.
This is done by using an appropriate family of Monge-Ampère equations, cf. Sections 6 and 7.

Remark 1.5
.

Assume that
X
is projective, and let
H_{1},\dots H_{n-2}
be a set of hyperplane sections, such that
S:=\cap H_{i}
has only quotient singularities
and

Then we can choose our set

restricted to

The conclusion is that the metric

we construct in Theorem
1.4

[
GK20
]
.

2.
Mean-value inequality

In this section we first establish the connection between the metric
h_{\mathcal{F},0}
constructed in Section
1

and the desingularisation result in
[
Ros68
]
.
Then we
recall a fundamental theorem due to
[

, which is the main

2.1.
Desingularisation of sheaves

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
.
Our first results here are dealing with positivity properties of the metric
h_{\mathcal{F},0}
in (
1.0.2
).

Lemma 2.1
.

We denote by
\langle,\rangle_{i}
and
\langle,\rangle_{j}
the scalar products defined by (
1.0.2
), corresponding to

\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
\mathcal{A}_{j}
, respectively. There exists a positive constant
C_{ij}>0
such that
for any section
s
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}|_{\mathcal{A}_{i}\cap\mathcal{A}_{j}}
the following relation

(2.1.1)

C_{ij}^{-1}<\frac{|s|_{i}}{|s|_{j}}<C_{ij}

holds (modulo shrinking a little the trivialising sets
\mathcal{A}_{i}
).

The main point here is that we can compare the metrics above despite of the non-compactness of
X_{0}
.

Proof.

We recall that for each index
i
metric
\displaystyle h_{\mathcal{F},i}
is defined
via the formula

(2.1.2)

\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{i}:=\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\rangle

where
s_{\alpha}
are sections of
\mathcal{F}|_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}
and
\sigma_{\alpha}
are sections of
{\mathcal{O}}^{q}_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}
, for
\alpha=1,2
such that

(2.1.3)

\beta(\sigma_{\alpha})=s_{\alpha},\qquad\langle\sigma_{\alpha},\tau\rangle=0

for any
\tau\in\mathrm{Ker}\,(\beta)
. The scalar product on the RHS of (
2.1.2
) is the usual one.

When restricted to
\mathcal{A}_{i,0}=\mathcal{A}_{i}\cap X_{0}
, the expression (
2.1.2
) is point-wise
defined, and it equals the quotient metric on
\displaystyle V_{0}|_{\mathcal{A}_{i,0}}
.
In order to verify the claim of our lemma, let

(2.1.4)

\beta_{k}:{\mathcal{O}}^{q_{k}}_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}\to\mathcal{F}|_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}

be the projections (
1.0.1
) corresponding to
k=i,j
. We can assume that there exists a morphism

(2.1.5)

\gamma:{\mathcal{O}}^{q_{j}}\to{\mathcal{O}}^{q_{i}},\qquad\beta_{i}\circ\gamma=\beta_{j}

on the intersection
\mathcal{A}_{i}\cap\mathcal{A}_{j}
. Let
\sigma_{j}
be a section of
{\mathcal{O}}^{q_{j}}
such that
\beta_{j}(\sigma_{j})=\xi
,
and such that moreover
\sigma_{j}
is orthogonal to the kernel of
\beta_{j}
(at the point we want to establish our inequality).
According to the definition, we have

(2.1.6)

|\xi|_{j}=|\sigma_{j}|.

On the other hand, there exists a positive constant
C>0
such that we have

(2.1.7)

|\gamma(\tau)|\leqslant C|\tau|

(it is at this point that we might have to shrink the sets
\mathcal{A}_{i}
) so all in all we get

(2.1.8)

|\xi|_{j}=|\sigma_{j}|\geqslant C^{-1}|\gamma(\sigma_{j})|\geqslant C^{-1}|\xi|_{i},

where the last equality holds by the second part of (
2.1.5
).
∎

As consequence, we obtain the following result

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a reflexive sheaf on
X
. Then it admits a metric
h
which is smooth on
X_{0}
and such that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta(\mathcal{F},h)\geqslant-C\omega_{X}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{F}}

holds true at each point of
X_{0}
, where
C>0
is a positive constant.

Proof.

The dual sheaf
\mathcal{F}^{\star}
is equally coherent, so we can
consider the metric
h_{\alpha}^{\star}
induced on
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\star}|_{\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}
by the dual of
\langle,\rangle_{\alpha}
. Griffiths’s formula implies that we have

(2.2.1)

\sqrt{-1}\Theta(\mathcal{F}^{\star},h^{\star}_{\alpha})\leqslant 0

at each point of the intersection
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\cap X_{0}
.

By Lemma
2.1
, given any pair of overlapping sets
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\cap\mathcal{A}_{\beta}
there exists a positive constant
C_{\alpha\beta}>1
such that

(2.2.2)

C_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}<\frac{|\xi|_{h_{\alpha}^{\star}}}{|\xi|_{h_{\beta}^{\star}}}<C_{\alpha\beta}

for any local holomorphic section
\xi
.

This happens to be precisely the condition we need in order to show the existence of a positive constant
C>0
such
that the inequality

(2.2.3)

\sqrt{-1}dd^{c}\log\left(\sum_{i}\theta_{i}^{2}|\xi|_{h_{i}^{\star}}^{2}\right)\geqslant-C\omega_{X},

cf.
[
Dem92
]
, Lemma 3.5.

We denote by
h
the metric induced on
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}^{\star\star}
by the dual of the metric obtained by glueing the
(h_{\alpha}^{\star})
by the partition of unit as in (
2.2.3
). The proposition is proved, since (
2.2.3
) is equivalent to the inequality we want to establish.
∎

2.1.1.
Connection with a theorem of Rossi

We discuss next the link between the results above and the "desingularisation" of
\mathcal{F}
, i.e. Theorem 3.5 in
[
Ros68
]
. To this end, we first will briefly recall the general context in
loc. cit.

We consider an open cover
(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of
X
, such that we have the exact sequence

(2.2.4)

{\mathcal{O}}^{p}_{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{O}}^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{F}|_{\mathcal{A}}\to 0

for each restriction of
\mathcal{F}
to
\mathcal{A}
, where
\mathcal{A}
is one of the
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
above (of course, the integers
p
and
q
depend on
\alpha
).

Then we can construct a rational map

(2.2.5)

f:\mathcal{A}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}(q-r,q)

into the Grassmannian of
q-r
planes in
\mathbb{C}^{q}
, simply given by the kernel of the projection

\beta:{\mathcal{O}}^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{F}|_{\mathcal{A}}

of
(
2.2.4
) on the open subset of
\mathcal{A}
where the dimension of the said kernel is
q-r
.

On the manifold
\mathcal{G}(q-r,q)

3.
Approximate stability and uniformity properties

We now assume that the sheaf
\mathcal{F}
is stable with respect to the data
(X,\{\omega_{X}\})

Thanks to the results in
[

]

Actually, the result in
[

]
is very general; we discuss next a particular case in which it can be easily established directly.

Hypothesis
: Each class
\displaystyle\{\alpha\}\in H^{1,1}(\widehat{X},\mathbb{R})\cap H^{2}(\widehat{X},\mathbb{Z})
is the inverse image of the Chern class of a line bundle on

Remark:
If

[

This equivalence should continue to hold without the algebraicity assumption, as long as

Assuming the

from now on, we argue as follows.
In the first place it is clear that

is stable with respect to

- this follows from the stability of

. We show next that there exists a positive

If such a constant

is impossible to find, then there exists a

(3.0.2)

0\to\mathcal{G}_{k}\to E

such that
\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{G}_{k})=s<r:=\mathrm{rk}(E)
and such that

(3.0.3)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\widehat{X}}c_{1}(L_{k})\wedge\pi^{\star}\omega_{X}^{n-1}=\frac{s}{r}\int_{\widehat{X}}c_{1}(E)\wedge\pi^{\star}\omega_{X}^{n-1}

where

is the (double dual of the) determinant of
\mathcal{G}_{k}
.

Let

\sigma_{k}
be the section of

induced by the map (
3.0.2
).
We have

(3.0.4)

\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log|\sigma_{k}|^{2}_{h_{E},h_{k}}\geqslant\theta_{L_{k}}-C\omega_{\widehat{X}}

where
\theta_{L_{k}}
is the curvature of

with respect a fixed smooth metric

and

is a positive constant bounding the curvature of
\displaystyle(E,h_{E,0})
. The current

is positive, and its mass is bounded by a constant which is independent of

for some constant

is convergent (up to the choice of some subsequence).

Now, by our hypothesis we have

modulo a

-class whose support is included in

.
Then we infer that

is convergent –therefore constant, as soon as

.

In the absence of the

]

We assume that
W=(f_{1}=0,\dots,f_{r}=0)
where
f_{i}
are
holomorphic. Let
p:Y\to B
be a composition of blow-up maps with smooth centre, such that the inverse image of the ideal
(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})
is principal.

Let
(D_{i})_{i}
be the set of exceptional divisors of the map
p
; they are assumed to have transverse intersections at each point. Let

(w^{1}=0)
be the local equation of some of the
D_{i}
. Then we claim that the

(n,n)
–form

(5.1.4)

\sqrt{-1}dw^{1}\wedge d\overline{w}^{1}\wedge p^{\star}\omega^{n-1}_{B}

equals
\mathcal{O}(|w^{1}|)
.

If
p
is very simple, e.g consists in one single blow-up, then the local equation of this map reads as

(5.1.5)

w\to(w^{1},w^{1}w^{2},\dots,w^{1}w^{k},w^{k+1},\dots,w^{n})

for a certain
2\leqslant k\leqslant n
. Then we see that the form in (
5.1.4
) is divisible by
|w^{1}|^{2k-2}
.

The general case follows from a less precise –but more direct– argument: we observe that we have

(5.1.6)

\int_{D_{i}}p^{\star}\omega^{n-1}_{B}=0

and on the other hand the wedge product in (
5.1.4
) is smooth, hence it must be equal to
\mathcal{O}(|w^{1}|)
.

Let
\phi
be a positive test function on
B
and
\Xi_{\varepsilon}
be the
usual truncation function, that is to say
\displaystyle\rho_{\varepsilon}\Big(\log\log\frac{1}{\sum|f_{i}|^{2}}\Big)
. We have to show that

(5.1.7)

\int_{B}|\tau|^{2}_{h_{\mathcal{E}}}\phi\Delta^{\prime\prime}(\Xi_{\varepsilon})\omega_{B}^{n}\to 0,\qquad\int_{B}|\tau|^{2}_{h_{\mathcal{E}}}\langle d\phi,d\Xi_{\varepsilon}\rangle\omega_{B}^{n}\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

To this end, we recall that the estimate

(5.1.8)

\sup_{B\setminus W}|\tau|^{2}_{h_{\mathcal{E}}}\leqslant C(\tau)

holds by (
5.0.2
). The terms involving derivatives of the truncation function
\Xi_{\varepsilon}
can be bounded by integrals of the following type

(5.1.9)

\int_{B}\phi\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\Big(\log\log\frac{1}{\sum|f_{i}|^{2}}\Big)\frac{\sqrt{-1}\partial f_{i}\wedge\overline{\partial f_{i}}}{\sum|f_{i}|^{2}}\wedge{\omega_{U}^{n-1}}

as consequence of the inequality (
5.1.8
). But (
5.1.9
) converges to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
, as we see by blowing up and using the claim (
5.1.4
).
∎

The same method of proof gives the following result.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mu
be any holomorphic section of
\mathcal{E}^{\star}
. Then we have

(5.2.1)

\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{\omega_{B}}\log|\mu|_{h_{\mathcal{E}}^{\star}}^{2}\geqslant-C

holds pointwise on
B\setminus W
and in the sense of distributions on
B
, where
C>0
is a constant independent of
\mu
.

Proof.

Indeed, given the inequality (
5.0.2
), we have

(5.2.2)

\sup_{B\setminus W}\big|\log|\mu|^{2}_{h_{\mathcal{E}}^{\star}}\big|\leqslant C(\mu)\log\frac{1}{\sum|f_{i}|^{2}}

and this is mild enough in order to have the integrals (
5.1.7
) converging to zero.
∎

As consequence of the previous Theorem
5.1
and Theorem
5.2
we have the following statement.

Corollary 5.3
.

There exists a positive constant
C>0
such that the inequalities

(5.3.1)

C^{-1}h_{0}\leqslant h_{\mathcal{E}}\leqslant Ch_{0}

hold on
B\setminus W
.

Proof.

The Laplace inequality established in Theorem
5.2
combines with Moser iteration procedure as in
[
CP22
]
and shows that we have

(5.3.2)

\sup_{B\setminus W}|\mu|^{2}_{h_{\mathcal{E}}^{\star}}\leqslant C(\mu),

and the statement follows.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Corollary

applies for any metric

\omega
on

,

Moreover, we can analyse the metric

a bit further, as follows.

Corollary 5.5
.

We assume that the hypothesis in Theorem

are fulfilled. Let

be a positive constant, and consider the class of smooth positive functions
\psi
such that the inequality

\int_{B}(\psi+|\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{\omega_{B}}\psi|)\omega_{B}^{n}\leqslant C

holds. Then we have
\displaystyle\int_{B}\psi|D^{\prime}\tau|_{h_{\mathcal{E}}}^{2}\omega_{B}^{n}\leqslant C_{0}C\sup_{B\setminus W}|\tau|^{2}
, where

.

Indeed, we use the equality

.

Remark 5.6
.

For example, assume that

Consider the function

5.1.
Chern classes inequality

In this subsection we prove Theorem

.

\bullet

Proof of
(1)
It is sufficient to show that there exists a positive constant
C>0

(5.6.4)

|\Delta(\mathcal{F},h_{\mathcal{F}})|_{\omega_{X}}\leqslant C|\Theta(\mathcal{F},h_{\mathcal{F}})|_{\omega_{X},h_{\mathcal{F}}}^{2}

Let

(z_{i})

\omega_{X}

\Theta(\mathcal{F},h)_{x_{0}}=\sum\theta^{\alpha}_{\beta p\overline{q}}dz^{p}\wedge dz^{\overline{q}}\otimes e_{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}^{\star}

\bullet

Proof of
(2)
From this point on we assume that
\dim(X)=3
.
Let
\alpha
be a smooth
(1,0)
-form with support in

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{V}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h_{\mathcal{F}})\wedge\bar{\partial}\chi_{\varepsilon}\wedge\alpha=0.

By pulling back to
B
via the map

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B}\mathrm{Tr}\big(\Theta(\mathcal{E},h_{\mathcal{E}})\wedge\Theta(\mathcal{E},h_{\mathcal{E}})\big)\wedge p^{\star}(\bar{\partial}\chi_{\varepsilon}\wedge\alpha)=0,

Locally on

we have the equality
\displaystyle\Theta(\mathcal{E},h_{\mathcal{E}})=\bar{\partial}A
, where
A=(A^{p}_{q})
is the matrix of
(1,0)
-forms above,
so (
5.6.8
) is equivalent to

(5.6.9)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B}\mathrm{Tr}\big(A\wedge\Theta(\mathcal{E},h_{\mathcal{E}})\big)\wedge p^{\star}(\bar{\partial}\chi_{\varepsilon}\wedge\bar{\partial}\alpha)=0.

where we use the notation
h_{s}
for the inverse image of the metric

.

By using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it would be sufficient to prove that

(5.6.10)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B}|A|^{2}_{\omega_{B}}\cdot|p^{\star}(\bar{\partial}\chi_{\varepsilon})|^{2}_{\omega_{B}}\omega_{B}^{3}<\infty.

since the

norm of the curvature tensor
\Theta(\mathcal{E},h_{\mathcal{E}})
with respect to the inverse image metric

Let
x_{0}\in V\subset X_{\rm sing}
be a point belonging to the singular set of
X
, such that
the composition of the ramified cover
p:B\to V
composed with the local embedding of
(X,x_{0})
in

can be written as follows

(5.6.11)

(z,u,v)\to\big(z,f_{1}(u,v),f_{2}(u,v),f_{3}(u,v)\big)

where the

’s are local holomorphic functions. For example, in the case of a singularity of type

we have

f_{1}(u,v)=u^{n},\qquad f_{2}(u,v)=v^{n},\qquad f_{3}(u,v)=uv.

Consider next the truncation function

\chi_{\varepsilon}:=\Xi_{\varepsilon}\big(\log\log(1/\sum_{i=2}^{4}|Z_{i}|^{2})\big),\qquad\omega_{X}\simeq\sqrt{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{4}dZ_{i}\wedge d\overline{Z}_{i}

A rough estimate gives

|\bar{\partial}\chi_{\varepsilon}|_{\omega_{X}}\leqslant\frac{-\Xi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{Z})}{(|\widetilde{Z}|^{2})^{1/2}\log(1/|\widetilde{Z}|^{2}}=:\psi_{\varepsilon}^{1/2}(\widetilde{Z})

(5.6.14)

\int_{B}|\Delta_{\omega_{B}}(\psi_{\varepsilon}\circ p)|\omega_{B}^{3}

and since the following formula holds

(5.6.15)

\Delta_{\omega_{B}}(\psi_{\varepsilon}\circ p)=(\Delta_{\omega_{X}}\psi_{\varepsilon})\circ p

it would be sufficient to show that the family of integrals

(5.6.16)

I_{\varepsilon}:=\int_{V}\theta(Z)|\Delta_{\omega_{X}}\psi_{\varepsilon}|dV_{\omega_{X}}

converges to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0

In the expression (
5.6.16

J_{\varepsilon}:=-\int_{V}\frac{\theta(Z)\Xi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{Z})}{|\widetilde{Z}|^{4}\log^{2}(1/|\widetilde{Z}|^{2})}\omega_{\rm euc}^{3}

J_{\varepsilon}=-\int_{(\mathbb{C}^{4},0)}\frac{\theta(Z)\Xi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{Z})}{|\widetilde{Z}|^{4}\log^{2}(1/|\widetilde{Z}|^{2})}\omega_{\rm euc}^{3}\wedge dd^{c}\log|f|^{2}

(5.6.19)

J_{\varepsilon}=\int_{(\mathbb{C}^{4},0)}\log 1/|f|^{2}dd^{c}\Big(\frac{\theta(Z)\Xi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{Z})}{|\widetilde{Z}|^{4}\log^{2}(1/|\widetilde{Z}|^{2})}\Big)\wedge\omega_{\rm euc}^{3}.

\int_{(\mathbb{C}^{4},0)}\frac{\log 1/|f|^{2}}{\log^{2}(1/|\widetilde{Z}|^{2})}\theta(Z)\Xi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{Z})dd^{c}\Big(\frac{1}{|\widetilde{Z}|^{4}}\Big)\wedge\omega_{\rm euc}^{3}

.

The other terms are bounded by integrals of the following type

(5.6.21)

\int_{(\mathrm{Supp}\Xi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime},0)}\frac{\log 1/|f|^{2}}{|\widetilde{Z}|^{6}\log^{3}(1/|\widetilde{Z}|^{2})}\omega_{\rm euc}^{4}

and the integral in (

.

Proof of
(3)
Since the dimension of

is equal to 3, the set
W
consists in a
finite number of points. We denote by
\Xi_{\varepsilon,W}
the truncation function corresponding to

defined on the

.

The equality

(5.6.22)

\int_{X\setminus Z}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h_{\mathcal{F}})\wedge(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)=\int_{X\setminus Z}\Xi_{\varepsilon,W}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h_{\mathcal{F}})\wedge(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)

holds as soon as

is equal to zero locally near

.

By the HE condition we have

(5.6.23)

\int_{X\setminus Z}\Xi_{\varepsilon,W}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h_{\mathcal{F}})\wedge\omega_{X}\geqslant 0

and moreover, the point (2) above shows that the integration by parts

\int_{X\setminus Z}\Xi_{\varepsilon,W}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h_{\mathcal{F}})\wedge dd^{c}\varphi=-\sqrt{-1}\int_{X\setminus Z}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h_{\mathcal{F}})\wedge\bar{\partial}\Xi_{\varepsilon,W}\wedge\partial\varphi

because

All in all, it follows that the RHS of (

6.
Orbifold-type Kähler metrics on klt spaces

Let
X
be a normal Kähler space with at most klt singularities, endowed with a Kähler metric
\omega_{X}
. It is known (by the work of
[
GKKP11
]
) that there exists
Z\subset X
such that
\mathrm{codim}_{X}(Z)\geqslant 3
and such that the complement
X\setminus Z
only has quotient singularities. For example, if
X
has dimension 3, it follows that
Z
consists in at most a finite number of points
(p_{i})_{i\in I}
.

The main results of the current section are as follows. Given any open subset
U\subset X
containing
Z
above
we start by
constructing a
Kähler metric
\omega_{\rm orb}
on
X
which accounts for the quotient singularities when restricted to the complement of
U
. By using Monge-Ampère
theory we show that there exists a Kähler approximation of the form
\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}+t\omega_{\widehat{X}}
whose
L^{p}(\widehat{X},\omega_{\widehat{X}})
–norm of the determinant is uniformly bounded (with respect to
t
and the approximation parameter) for some
p>1
.
This result, combined with
[
GPS22
]
will show that a uniform mean-value inequality holds true for the family of the approximating metrics.
Finally, this is used to establish Theorem
1.4
.

6.1.
Construction of
\omega_{\rm orb}

Let
Z\subset V_{0}^{\prime}\Subset V_{0}^{\prime\prime}\Subset U
be two open subsets of
X
containing
Z
and
contained in a pre-assigned open subset
U\subset X
. We consider
a finite cover

(V_{i}^{\prime}\Subset V_{i}^{\prime\prime})_{i=1,...,M}
of
X\setminus\overline{V_{0}^{\prime\prime}}
such that the following hold.

(1)

We have
\displaystyle X\setminus\overline{V_{0}^{\prime\prime}}\subset\cup_{i\geqslant 1}V_{i}^{\prime}
and none of the sets
V_{i}^{\prime\prime}
are intersecting
V_{0}^{\prime}
, for
i\geqslant 1
.

(2)

For each index
i\geqslant 1
there exists a ball
U_{i}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
together with a finite group
\Gamma_{i}
acting
on
U_{i}
such that
V_{i}^{\prime\prime}\simeq U_{i}/\Gamma_{i}
.

(3)

Let
\pi_{i}:U_{i}\rightarrow U_{i}/\Gamma_{i}\simeq V_{i}^{\prime\prime}
be the cover. There exists a positive continuous function
w_{i}
on
V_{i}^{\prime\prime}
such that

w_{i}\leqslant 1,\qquad\pi_{i}^{\star}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}w_{i})\simeq\omega_{\rm euc}.

Indeed such function is obtained by averaging the usual
\|z_{i}\|^{2}
and dividing by an appropriate
constant.

By a gluing argument –i.e. by using cutoff functions– we can construct a continuous function

\varphi:X\to[-\infty,0[
such that

(6.0.1)

\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi\geqslant-\frac{C}{2}\omega_{X},

where
C
is a constant.

Actually (
6.0.1
) follows directly from the gluing lemma, cf.
[
Dem92
, Lemma 3.5]
, as we briefly recall next. Let
\theta_{i}
be a smooth function
\equiv 1
on
V_{i}^{\prime}
and whose support is contained in
V_{i}^{\prime\prime}
. We then consider

\varphi:=\log\big(\theta_{0}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\theta_{i}^{2}(1+w_{i})\big),

where
w_{i}
is the function in the item (
3
) above.

Given that on overlapping sets
V_{i}^{\prime\prime}\cap V_{j}^{\prime\prime}
we have

(6.0.2)

C_{ij}^{-1}<\frac{1+w_{i}}{1+w_{j}}<C_{ij}

for some positive constants
C_{ij}>0
(and with the convention that
w_{0}=0
if some of the
i,j
above are equal to zero). Moreover, we can find a constant
C
such that

\sqrt{-1}(\theta_{j}\partial\bar{\partial}\theta_{j}-\partial\theta_{j}\wedge\bar{\partial}\theta_{j})\geqslant-C\omega_{X}\qquad\text{on }V_{j}^{\prime\prime}.

Thanks to
[
Dem92
, Lemma 3.5]
, it follows that
(
6.0.1
) holds true.

We introduce then the metric

(6.0.3)

\omega_{\rm orb}:=C\omega_{X}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi

and summarise its properties as follows.

(1)

For each uniformization map
\pi:\widetilde{V}\to V=\widetilde{V}/\Gamma
such that
V\subset(X\setminus U)
the pull-back
\displaystyle\pi^{\star}(\omega_{\rm orb})
is quasi-isometric with the Euclidean metric.

(2)

We have
\displaystyle\omega_{\rm orb}=C\omega_{X}
when restricted to an open subset
\Omega
containing
Z
. Here
\Omega\Subset U
. Moreover, the inequality
\displaystyle\omega_{\rm orb}\geqslant C\omega_{X}
holds globally on
X
, where
C>0
is some positive constant (not necessarily equal to the previous one).

We next discuss an important property of the metric
\omega_{\rm orb}
. When restricted to
X_{\rm reg}
, we can write its determinant as follows

(6.0.4)

\omega_{\rm orb}^{n}=\Psi\omega_{X}^{n}

for some function
\Psi>0
. Our next statement shows that
\Psi
is
L^{p}
-integrable on
(X_{\rm reg},\omega_{X})
for some
p>1
.

Lemma 6.1
.

There exists
p>1
such that the integral

(6.1.1)

\int_{X_{\rm reg}}\Psi^{p}\omega_{X}^{n}<\infty

is convergent.

Proof.

Let
(V_{\alpha})_{\alpha=1,\dots,M}
be a finite cover of

X
such that

(6.1.2)

\frac{1}{2}\Omega\subset\cup_{\alpha=1}^{M^{\prime}}V_{\alpha}\subset\Omega

and such that

(6.1.3)

\cup_{\alpha=1+M^{\prime}}^{M}V_{\alpha}\subset X\setminus\frac{1}{4}\Omega

together with
V_{\alpha}\simeq U_{\alpha}/\Gamma
for
\alpha=1+M^{\prime},\dots,M
.

We analyze the convergence of the integral (
6.1.1
) by restricting to each of the open sets
(V_{\alpha})
above.
In the first place, if
\alpha=1,\dots,M^{\prime}
we have

(6.1.4)

\Psi|_{V_{\alpha}}\leqslant C

by the property (2) above and there is nothing to prove (since the volume of
X_{\rm reg}
is finite). If
\alpha\geqslant 1+M^{\prime}
, then we use the
local uniformisations
p_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\rightarrow V_{\alpha}\simeq U_{\alpha}/\Gamma
. Since
p_{\alpha}^{*}\varphi
is smooth, so we see
that

(6.1.5)

\Psi|_{V_{\alpha}}\circ p_{\alpha}\leqslant\frac{C}{|\mathrm{Jac}(p_{\alpha})|^{2}}

for some constant
C
, and therefore

(6.1.6)

\int_{V_{\alpha}}\Psi^{1+\varepsilon_{0}}\omega_{X}^{n}|_{V_{\alpha}}\leqslant C\int_{U_{\alpha}}\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Jac}(p_{\alpha})|^{2\varepsilon_{0}}}d\lambda

and this last quantity is clearly convergent, as soon as
0<\varepsilon_{0}\ll 1
.
∎

Similar arguments allow us to deduce the following. Assume that
t\in[0,1]
is a positive real number and define the function
\Psi_{t}
by the following equality

(6.1.7)

(\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}+t\omega_{\widehat{X}})^{n}=\Psi_{t}\omega_{\widehat{X}}^{n}.

Then we claim that there exists a positive
\varepsilon_{0}
such that we have

(6.1.8)

\int_{\widehat{X}}\Psi_{t}^{1+\varepsilon_{0}}\omega_{\widehat{X}}^{n}\leqslant C<\infty

for some constant
C
which is
independent of t
.

Indeed, this can be verified as in the previous lemma: for each index
\alpha\geqslant M^{\prime}+1
, let
\pi_{\alpha}:\widehat{U}_{\alpha}\to U_{\alpha}
be a modification of
U_{\alpha}
such that there exists a generically finite morphism
\widehat{p}_{\alpha}:\widehat{U}_{\alpha}\to\pi^{-1}(V_{\alpha})
with the property that

\pi\circ\widehat{p}_{\alpha}=p_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\alpha}

holds true (we take any desingularisation of the fibered product
\displaystyle U_{\alpha}\times_{V_{\alpha}}\pi^{-1}(V_{\alpha})
.)

Let
\Psi_{k}
be the function defined by the equality

\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}^{k}\wedge\omega_{\widehat{X}}^{n-k}=\Psi_{k}\omega_{\widehat{X}}^{n}

and then we remark that we have

\Psi|_{k}\circ\widehat{p}_{\alpha}\leqslant\frac{C}{|\mathrm{Jac}(\widehat{p}_{\alpha})|^{2}},

that is to say, the analogue of (
6.1.5
) holds true. Then we argue as in (
6.1.6
) and (
6.1.8
) follows. ∎

6.2.
Few results from Monge-Ampère theory

Next we recall an important estimate from MA theory. Let
\pi:\widehat{X}\rightarrow X
be a desingularization and we fix a smooth Kähler metric
\omega_{\widehat{X}}
on
\widehat{X}
. We consider a real number
p>1
, a positive constant
C_{0}>0
and a
positive function
\Psi\geqslant 0
such that

(6.1.9)

\int_{\widehat{X}}\Psi^{p}\omega_{\widehat{X}}^{n}<C_{0}.

Associated with such function
\Psi
we consider the Monge-Ampère equation

(6.1.10)

(\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}+t\omega_{\widehat{X}}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi)^{n}=\Psi\omega_{\widehat{X}}^{n},\qquad\sup_{X}\phi=0.

which by the work of
[
Kol98
,
EGZ09
,
TZ06
]
admits a unique solution.
Moreover, the following fundamental estimate
holds

(6.1.11)

\sup_{X}|\phi|\leqslant C=C(\omega_{X},\omega_{\widehat{X}},C_{0},p)

In order to apply the arguments in the smooth case (sections 3-4)

Corollary 6.2
.

For each couple
\eta=(t,\varepsilon)

Proof.

Lemma 7.3

There exist a subsequence of

(1)

(2)

The first thing to remark is that the formula (

one can still computing the degree with respect to the singular metric

by the usual Chern-Weil representative with respect to the induced metric on

restricted to the set
\widehat{X}\setminus\Sigma

in our specific case this is not happening as we next show.

Let

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\gamma}
together with an open coordinate subset

Let

be one of the frames above; the metric induced on

is given by the coefficients

(7.3.4)

so if we write
\displaystyle\iota(s_{\alpha})=\sum_{k=1}^{m}f^{k}_{\alpha}e_{k}

Indeed this can be seen as follows. According to the formula for the Gramm determinant, we have

\mathcal{F}

In general, let

Coming back to our case, integrals of type

(7.3.9)

\int_{\widehat{X}}\theta_{L}\wedge\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}^{n-1}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\widehat{X}}\theta_{L}\wedge\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}

where
\omega_{\varepsilon}\in\{\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}+\varepsilon\omega_{\widehat{X}}\}
is smooth and it converges towards
\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}
in such a way that the potentials
are monotonic, cf.
[
Dem09
, Thm 13.12]
(Here we used the fact that the potentials of
\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}
are continuous. Then we can ask the approximating currents are smooth).
Moreover, we can assume that on the pre-image of the set
\Omega\subset X
(cf. Section 6) the sequence
(\omega_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
is stationary, equal to the inverse image of
\omega_{X}
modulo a bounded form
converging to zero.

In (
7.3.9
) we denote by
\theta_{L}
the curvature form of
L=\det(\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{\gamma})
induced by the
metric (
7.3.4
). Therefore, the total mass of the
(n,n)
-current
\theta_{L}\wedge\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}^{n-1}

equals the degree of
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
with respect to
\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}
.

Next we recall the formula

(7.3.10)

\Theta_{h_{\widehat{\mathcal{F}}}}=\pi_{\gamma}\Theta(E,h_{E})|_{\widehat{\mathcal{F}}}+\beta^{\star}\wedge\beta

which holds pointwise in the complement of the singularities of
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
.
Here we denote by
\beta
the
(1,0)
–form with values in
\mathrm{Hom}(\widehat{\mathcal{F}},E)

whose adjoint is the second fundamental form
\bar{\partial}\pi_{\gamma}
.

By taking the trace we get a
(1,1)
positive current

(7.3.11)

-\mathrm{Tr}(\beta^{\star}\wedge\beta)

which is in
L^{1}(\widehat{X},\widehat{\omega})
and we claim that the relation

(7.3.12)

\int_{\widehat{X}}\mathrm{Tr}(\beta^{\star}\wedge\beta)\wedge\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}^{n-1}<\infty

holds. Indeed, the fact that the (improper) integral on the LHS of (
7.3.12
) is finite can be seen as consequence of the
formula (
7.3.10
), since the projection
\pi_{\gamma}
is a bounded map.

It then follows that we have

(7.3.13)

\int_{\widehat{X}}\theta_{L}\wedge\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}^{n-1}=\int_{\widehat{X}}\mathrm{Tr}\big(\pi_{\gamma}\Theta(E,h_{E})\big)\wedge\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}^{n}-\int_{\widehat{X}}\mathrm{Tr}(\beta^{\star}\wedge\beta)\wedge\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}^{n-1},

and in conclusion
(
7.3.2
) is proved, since we have

\int_{\widehat{X}}\mathrm{Tr}(\beta^{\star}\wedge\beta)\wedge\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}^{n-1}=\int_{\widehat{X}}|D^{\prime\prime}\pi_{\gamma}|^{2}\pi^{*}\omega_{\rm orb}^{n},

and moreover, the LHS of (
7.3.13
) equals precisely the degree of
L
with respect to
\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}
, cf. (
7.3.9
).

The rest of the arguments in
[
Sim88
]
apply
mutatis mutandis
and the proof of Theorem
7.1
is finished.

7.2.
Proof of Theorem
7.2

The basic idea goes back at least to the
work of Li-Tian,
[
LT19
]
.
Let
\Sigma\subset U
be the inverse image of the singular loci (of
\mathcal{F}
and
X
, it has in any case codimension at least two). We have already seen in Remark
5.4
that if we write
\displaystyle\pi^{\star}H=H_{0}\exp(\eta)
, then

(7.3.14)

\sup_{U\setminus\Sigma}|\eta|\leqslant C<\infty,

where
H_{0}
is a fixed, smooth metric on
F
.

Let
\tau
be any holomorphic section of
F
. We have the formula

(7.3.15)

\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}|\tau|^{2}_{H}=\sqrt{-1}\langle D^{\prime}_{H}\tau,D^{\prime}_{H}\tau\rangle-\sqrt{-1}\langle\Theta(F,H)\cdot\tau,\tau\rangle

that we wedge with the
g:=\pi^{\star}\omega_{\rm orb}
raised to the power
n-1
.
It follows that we have the equality

(7.3.16)

\Delta_{g}|\tau|^{2}_{H}=\Lambda_{g}\sqrt{-1}\langle D^{\prime}_{H}\tau,D^{\prime}_{H}\tau\rangle+\gamma|\tau|^{2}_{H}

at each point of
U\setminus\Sigma
, where
\gamma
is a constant.

It follows that
D^{\prime}_{H}\tau
is in
L^{2}
(by multiplying with a truncation function, precisely as we did pages 14-15 in section 6) and since
F|_{U}
is trivial, we can construct sufficiently many sections
\tau
so as to infer that
D^{\prime}H
is in
L^{2}
, and so is
D^{\prime}_{H}\tau
. Then the point-wise equation which
H
satisfies in
U\setminus\Sigma
extends to
U
in weak sense, and from this point we are using the elliptic theory. Indeed, the Hermite-Einstein equation is

(7.3.17)

\Lambda_{g}\bar{\partial}\big((D^{\prime}H)\circ H^{-1}\big)=f

on
U\setminus\Sigma
. Since we already know that
D^{\prime}_{H}\tau
is
L^{2}
-integrable, it is sufficient to show that

(7.3.18)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{U}\sqrt{-1}\partial\Xi_{\varepsilon}\wedge\bar{\partial}\Xi_{\varepsilon}\wedge\omega_{\rm euc}^{n-1}=0

This follows from (
5.1.9
).

8.
Partial results towards Campana-Höring-Peternell conjecture

Let
X
be a
3
-dimensional compact Kähler normal space with klt singularities. Throughout this section we denote by
\Sigma\subset X
the union of the singular loci of
X
and
\mathcal{F}
, and let
Z\subset X
be the locus such that
X\setminus Z
has locally quotient singularities. Then
Z
is either empty, or a finite number of points
(p_{i})
in
X
. We suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is stable with respect to a Kähler metric

\omega_{X}
.

In this section we denote by
h=h_{\mathcal{F}}
the metric obtained in Theorem
1.4
, which verifies the Hermite-Einstein
equation on
X_{0}
and such that it has quotient singularities in the complement of a fixed open nbd
U
of
Z
.
Our first goal in the current section is to show that following holds.

Theorem 8.1
.

We assume that the singular set
\Sigma
of
X
is the disjoint union of
Z\cup\Sigma_{0}
, where
\Sigma_{0}
is closed and
X
has quotient singularities at each point of
\Sigma_{0}
. Then we have

\int_{X\setminus\Sigma}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)\wedge(\omega_{\rm orb}+dd^{c}\phi)\geqslant 0,

where the function
\phi
is such that the support of the form
\omega_{\rm orb}+dd^{c}\phi
is contained in the complement of
U
.

Remark 8.2
.

As a consequence of the results in
[
GK20
]
, the inequality in Theorem
8.1
states that the orbifold Chern
classes corresponding to
\mathcal{F}
are satisfying the Bogomolov inequality.

Remark 8.3
.

Actually, it is conjectured in
[
CHP21
]
that such a result should hold for any klt space, regardless to its dimension and the structure of its singular points. The arguments proving Theorem
8.1

equally show that in order to establish the 3-dimensional version of this conjecture it would suffice to
establish a version of the usual regularisation result for
W^{1,1}
-Sobolev spaces.

Proof.

We note that
h
is smooth in the complement of
\Sigma
, and it is induced by
an orbifold metric in the complement of an open set
U
containing the set of points
(p_{i})
(e.g. the union of "coordinate" sets centred at these points). Moreover, according to our hypothesis,
we can assume that

(8.3.1)

Z\subset U\subset X\setminus\Sigma_{0}.

Moreover, we can assume that

(8.3.2)

\omega_{\rm orb}|_{U}=\omega_{X},

this is actually the only part in our arguments where (
8.3.1
) is needed.

Since
h
is HE with respect to
\omega_{\rm orb}
, the inequality

(8.3.3)

\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)\wedge\omega_{\rm orb}\geqslant 0

holds pointwise (as top forms) on
X\setminus\Sigma
. Moreover, since
\omega_{\rm orb}
is of conic singularity near
\Sigma_{0}
, thanks to Theorem
7.2
, in the neighbourhood of
\Sigma_{0}
,
\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)
is the push-forward of a smooth
\bar{\partial}
-closed
(2,2)
-form from its local uniformisation.
In particular,
\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)
is well defined and
\bar{\partial}
-closed when restricted to the support of the form
\omega_{\rm orb}+dd^{c}\phi
(which contains
\Sigma_{0}
), and moreover satisfies

(8.3.4)

\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)\wedge\omega_{\rm orb}\geqslant 0

locally near
\Sigma_{0}
. Here we use the assumption that
Z
and
\Sigma_{0}
are disjoint.

We also have

(8.3.5)

\int_{X\setminus\Sigma}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)\wedge\big(\omega_{\rm orb}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi\big)=\int_{X}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)\wedge\big(\omega_{\rm orb}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi\big)

because
\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)
is in
L^{1}(X,\omega_{\rm orb})
.

Without loss of generality we can assumed that the condition
\phi=\mathcal{O}(\|z\|^{2})
holds at each of the
p_{i}
and that

(8.3.6)

\sup_{X\setminus Z}|dd^{c}\phi|_{\omega_{\rm orb}}\leqslant C<\infty

since basically the function
\phi
is a well-chosen potential for
\omega_{\rm orb}
multiplied with a truncation function.

Let
(\chi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
be the usual family of truncation functions, converging to the characteristic function of

X\setminus Z
. A first remark is that we have the equality

(8.3.7)

\int_{X}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)\wedge\big(\omega_{\rm orb}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi\big)=\int_{X}\chi_{\varepsilon}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)\wedge\big(\omega_{\rm orb}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi\big),

for all
\varepsilon\ll 1
. Moreover, the first term of the RHS of (
8.3.7
)

(8.3.8)

\int_{X}\chi_{\varepsilon}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)\wedge\omega_{\rm orb}\geqslant 0

is positive, as consequence of (
8.3.3
) and (
8.3.4
).

The next identity we have to establish is

(8.3.9)

\int_{X}\chi_{\varepsilon}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)\wedge\partial\bar{\partial}\phi=-\int_{X}\partial\phi\wedge\bar{\partial}\chi_{\varepsilon}\wedge\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)

for every positive
\varepsilon
. This of course looks like an obvious consequence of Stokes theorem, but it is not so immediate since we are integrating over a non-compact domain singular forms.
In our specific situation, it holds thanks to (
8.3.2
) combined to the point (2) of Theorem
1.6
.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.6
, it is easy to see that

(8.3.10)

\sup_{X_{\rm reg}}|\partial\phi\wedge\bar{\partial}\chi_{\varepsilon}|_{\omega_{\rm orb}}\leqslant C

by a direct calculation, and on the other hand we claim that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(8.3.11)

\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)\wedge\omega_{\rm orb}\leqslant C|\Theta(\mathcal{F},h)|^{2}dV_{\omega_{\rm orb}}

at each point of
X_{\rm reg}
– this has already been verified in Section 5.

In conclusion, we have proved that

(8.3.12)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{X}\chi_{\varepsilon}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h)\wedge\partial\bar{\partial}\phi=0

so Theorem
8.1
is established.
∎

Remark 8.4
.

We assume that, instead of choosing
\phi
such that
\omega_{\rm orb}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi
has compact support we impose the
following condition:
for any point
x\in X_{\rm sing}
contained in the singular loci of
X
we have

\frac{\phi}{\sum|f_{j}|^{2}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\infty}(X,x)

where
(f_{j})
is the ideal corresponding to
X_{\rm sing}
. Then the arguments used for Theorem
8.1
show that

\int_{X\setminus\Sigma}\Delta(\mathcal{F},h_{\mathcal{F}})\wedge(\omega_{\rm orb}+dd^{c}\phi)\geqslant 0

holds true.

We end this section with the proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Proof.

It may be that Theorem
1.9
is an important step towards the general case. Indeed, it is expected that any klt Kähler space
X
admits a so-called
partial resolution of singularities
,
i.e. there exists a birational map

(8.4.1)

\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X

such that
\widetilde{X}
has only quotient singularities and
\pi
is an isomorphism over the complement of the non-orbifold loci of
X

(i.e. over the complement of a finite number of points).

In the context of Theorem
1.9
this is a hypothesis, and consider the inverse image sheaf

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}:=\big(\pi^{\star}(\mathcal{F})/{\rm Tor}\big)^{\star\star}.

It has two important properties that we next discuss, namely:

—

It is isomorphic with the
\pi
-inverse image of
\mathcal{F}
over the orbifold subset of
X
.

—

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
admits a
\mathbb{Q}
-sheaf structure in the complement of a finite number of points
(p_{i})_{i=1,\dots,N}\subset\widehat{X}
of
\widehat{X}
.

To see this, we first consider
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}:=\pi^{\star}(\mathcal{F})/\mathrm{Tor}
, and let
0
be a point in the support of the exceptional divisor of
\pi
, together with the
local uniformization

\tau:U\to V=U/\Gamma.

The double dual of the inverse image
\tau^{\star}\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
is a reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{U}
on a open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
, hence it is a vector bundle
in the complement of a finite set of points. We then take the
\Gamma
-invariant direct image of
\mathcal{F}_{U}
,
which coincides with
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
outside a set of codimension at least two. Therefore, the bi-dual of
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
is isomorphic with
the
\Gamma
-invariant direct image of
\mathcal{F}_{U}
, which shows that the bullets above hold true.

Let
\omega_{\widehat{X}}
be a metric with orbifold singularities on
\widehat{X}
. Then for each positive
\varepsilon>0
the form

(8.4.2)

\omega_{\varepsilon}:=\pi^{\star}\omega_{X}+\varepsilon\omega_{\widehat{X}}

is a metric with quotient singularities.

Next we see that
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
is stable with respect to
\omega_{\varepsilon}
, as soon as the parameter
\varepsilon\ll 1
is small enough.
Let
h_{\varepsilon}
be the corresponding HE metric constructed in Theorem
1.4
. Then we have the inequality

(8.4.3)

\int_{\widehat{X}_{0}}\Delta(\widehat{\mathcal{F}},h_{\varepsilon})\wedge\omega_{\varepsilon}\geqslant 0

which follows by the usual differential geometry calculation, combined with the estimates in Theorem
1.4

in order to justify the convergence of the integral in (
8.4.3
). The main point is to show that the equality

(8.4.4)

\int_{\widehat{X}}\Delta(\widehat{\mathcal{F}},h_{\varepsilon})\wedge(\omega_{\varepsilon}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi)=\int_{\widehat{X}}\Delta(\widehat{\mathcal{F}},h_{\varepsilon})\wedge\omega_{\varepsilon}

holds for any function
\phi
which is smooth in orbifold sense. Modulo the use of local uniformizations, the argument to establish
(
8.4.4
) is the same as the one we used in Theorem
1.6
, so we will not reproduce it here.

In particular, (
8.4.4
) holds for a function
\phi
such that
\omega_{\varepsilon}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi
is equal to zero
locally near the points
p_{i}
. Then we have the equality

(8.4.5)

\int_{\widehat{X}}\Delta(\widehat{\mathcal{F}},h_{\varepsilon})\wedge\big(\omega_{\varepsilon}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi\big)=\int_{\widehat{X}}\Delta(\widehat{\mathcal{F}},h_{1})\wedge\big(\omega_{\varepsilon}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi\big)

justified by the fact that the form
\omega_{\varepsilon}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi
has compact support contained in the
open subset of
\widehat{X}
where
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
–sheaf.

Next we write

\omega_{\varepsilon}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi=\pi^{\star}(\omega_{X}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi)+\varepsilon\eta,

where
\psi
is chosen in such a way that
\omega_{X}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi
has support in a
compact set
K\subset X
contained in the orbifold loci of
X
. Moreover, we choose
\eta
with compact support in
a set
\widehat{K}
contained in the
complement of
(p_{i})_{i}
’s. All in all, we obtain the inequality

(8.4.6)

\int_{\widehat{X}}\Delta(\widehat{\mathcal{F}},h_{1})\wedge\pi^{\star}\big(\omega_{X}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi\big)\geqslant-\varepsilon\int_{\widehat{X}}\Delta(\widehat{\mathcal{F}},h_{1})\wedge\eta

and as a last step we are taking the limit in (
8.4.6
) as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

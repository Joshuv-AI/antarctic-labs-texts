Implication of island for inflation and primordial perturbations
Yun-Song Piao 1,2,3,4

Abstract
It is usually thought that the efolds number of inflation must be bounded by its de Sitter entropy, otherwise we will have an information paradox. However, in light of the island rule for computing the entanglement entropy, we show that such a bound might be nonexistent, while the information flux of primordial perturbation modes the observer after inflation is able to detect follows a Page curve. In corresponding eternally inflating spacetime, it seems that our slow-roll inflation patch is accompanied with a neighbourly collapsed patch (eventually developing into a black hole) so that its Hawking radiation might be just our primordial perturbations. Accordingly, the perturbation spectrum we observed will present a “Page-like” suppression at large scale.

Appendix B
On perturbation modes inside AdS collapsed patch

Inside the AdS bubble the collapsing evolution can hardly be
described by a single state equation
w=const.
, since different
stage of collapse might correspond to different
w
.

As an estimate, we consider that the collapsing evolution consists
of different phases
w_{j}=const.
. The perturbation equation of

\phi
inside the collapsing patch is

u_{k}^{\prime\prime}+\left(k^{2}-\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}\right)u_{k}=0\,,

(35)

where
u_{k}=a\delta\phi_{k}
.
The different phases of collapse are signed with

\epsilon_{j}=-{\dot{H}}_{j}/H^{2}_{j}={3\over 2}(1+w_{j})
. It has been
showed in Ref.
Cai:2015nya
that for the
j
-th phase, we
have

a_{j}\sim\left({T}_{*,j}-T\right)^{\frac{1}{\epsilon_{j}-1}},

(36)

where
{T}_{*,j}=T_{j}-{1\over(\epsilon_{j}-1)a(T_{j})H(T_{j})}
is set
by requiring the continuity of
a
at the end of phase
j
(i.e.,

T=T_{j}
). Thus we have

\frac{a_{j}^{\prime\prime}}{a_{j}}={\nu_{j}^{2}-{1/4}\over{(T-T_{*,j})^{2}}}\,,

(37)

where

\nu_{j}={3\over 2}\left|{1-w_{j}\over 1+3w_{j}}\right|
.
Regarding the phases
j
and
j+1
as adjacent phases, we have the
solutions to Eq.(
35
) as

\displaystyle u_{k,{j+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\sqrt{\pi(T_{*,{j+1}}-T)}\over 2}\Big\{\alpha_{j+1}H_{\nu_{j+1}}^{(1)}[k(T_{*,j+1}-T)]

(38)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\beta_{j+1}H_{\nu_{j+1}}^{(2)}[k(T_{*,j+1}-T)]\Big\},\qquad T_{j}<T<T_{j+1}\,,

where
\alpha_{j+1}
and

\beta_{j+1}
are
k
-dependent coefficients. Thus with the
matching conditions
u_{k,j}(T_{j+1})=u_{k,j+1}(T_{j+1})
and

u_{k,j}^{\prime}(T_{j+1})=u_{k,j+1}^{\prime}(T_{j+1})
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\alpha_{j+1}\\
\beta_{j+1}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal M}^{(j)}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\alpha_{j}\\
\beta_{j}\end{array}\right)\,,\quad{\rm where}\quad{\cal M}^{(j)}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\cal M}^{(j)}_{11}&{\cal M}^{(j)}_{12}\\
{\cal M}^{(j)}_{21}&{\cal M}^{(j)}_{22}\end{array}\right)\,,

see Refs.
Cai:2015nya
,
Cai:2019hge
for
{\cal M}^{(j)}
.
The information of
j=1,2\cdots j
phases has been encoded fully
in the Bogoliubov coefficients
\alpha_{j+1}
and
\beta_{j+1}
.

As an example, we consider a model in which the collapse consists
of the three (
j=1,2,3
) phases. Thus we have

\displaystyle u_{k,3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\sqrt{\pi(T_{*,3}-T)}\over 2}\Big\{\alpha_{3}H_{\nu_{3}}^{(1)}[k(T_{*,3}-T)]

(46)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\beta_{3}H_{\nu_{3}}^{(2)}[k(T_{*,3}-T)]\Big\}.

The resulting spectrum of perturbation will be

P_{\delta\phi}=\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}\left|\delta\phi_{k}\right|^{2}=2^{2\nu_{3}-3}{\Gamma^{2}(\nu_{3})}{\left(T_{*}-T\right)^{1-2v_{3}}\over\pi^{3}a^{2}k^{2\nu_{3}-3}}\left|\alpha_{3}-\beta_{3}\right|^{2}\,,

(47)

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\alpha_{3}\\
\beta_{3}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal M}^{(2)}{\cal M}^{(1)}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\alpha_{1}\\
\beta_{1}\end{array}\right)\,.

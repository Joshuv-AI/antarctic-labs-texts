DERIVED CATEGORIES OF hyper-Kähler MANIFOLDS: Extended Mukai vector and integral structure
Thorsten Beckmann

Abstract
. We introduce a linearised form of the square root of the Todd class inside the Verbitsky component of a hyper-Kähler manifold using the extended Mukai lattice. This enables us to define a Mukai vector for certain objects in the derived category taking values inside the extended Mukai lattice which is functorial for derived equivalences. As applications, we obtain a structure theorem for derived equivalences between hyper-Kähler manifolds as well as an integral lattice associated to the derived category of hyper-Kähler manifolds deformation equivalent to the Hilbert scheme of a K3 surface mimicking the surface case.

1.1.
Background: Derived categories of K3 surfaces

The study of derived categories of smooth projective varieties goes back to the works of Mukai
[
46
,
45
]
. Over the years derived categories and equivalences between them have attracted great attention and culminated in many results, see for example
[
18
,
17
,
15
,
49
,
13
,
24
]
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth projective varieties. We denote by
\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\coloneqq\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(\mathrm{Coh}(X))
the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on
X
. Orlov
[
49
]
showed that any derived equivalence
\Phi\colon\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)
is isomorphic to a Fourier–Mukai functor
\mathsf{FM}_{\mathcal{E}}
with Fourier–Mukai kernel
{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X\times Y)
. In particular, using the Mukai vector

v={\mathrm{ch}}(\_)\mathsf{td}^{1/2}\colon\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\to\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

with
\mathsf{td}^{1/2}
the formal square root of the Todd class
{\mathsf{td}}\in\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

the Fourier–Mukai functor
\mathsf{FM}_{\mathcal{E}}
induces an isomorphism

\Phi^{\mathrm{H}}=\mathsf{FM}_{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{H}}\colon\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}}).

Let us specialize the above to the case of K3 surfaces
S
. Any smooth variety
Y
which is derived equivalent to
S
is again a K3 surface
[
16
]
. The integral cohomology groups
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
are equipped with the
Mukai pairing

\tilde{b}
which is equal to the intersection pairing up to a sign
\tilde{b}(\mathsf{1},{\mathsf{p}})=-1
for
\mathsf{1}\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
the fundamental class and
{\mathsf{p}}\in\mathrm{H}^{4}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
the point class. Moreover, the lattice
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
carries a weight-two Hodge structure inherited from
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
.
As alluded to above for a derived equivalence
\Phi\colon\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(S)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(S^{\prime})
between two K3 surfaces the following diagram commutes

(1.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(S^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(S^{\prime},{\mathbb{Z}}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v}

Mukai has shown that the morphism
\Phi^{\mathrm{H}}
associated to

is a Hodge isometry
[
46
]
. Furthermore, the lattice
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
together with its Hodge structure determines the derived category completely. That is, two
K3 surfaces
S
and
S^{\prime}
are derived equivalent if and only if
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
and
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(S^{\prime},{\mathbb{Z}})
are Hodge isometric
[
49
]
.

In particular, many properties of the derived category of a K3 surface
S
are encoded by the lattice
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
of rank
b_{2}(S)+2
together with its Hodge structure. For example, this can be used to show that the number of Fourier–Mukai partners of
S
, that is the number of non-isomorphic K3 surfaces
S^{\prime}
which are derived equivalent to
S
, is finite.

In addition, the group of auto-equivalences
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(S))
of K3 surfaces admits a representation

\rho^{\mathrm{H}}\colon\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(S))\to\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(S,{\mathbb{Z}}).

The group
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(S))
contains elements such as spherical twists
\mathsf{ST}_{\mathcal{E}}
along spherical objects
{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(S)
. These are symmetries which become only visible in the derived category. The image of
\rho^{\mathrm{H}}
has been computed to be
\operatorname{Aut}^{+}(\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}))
, the group of Hodge isometries with real spinor norm one
[
27
,
28
,
46
,
49
]
. A conjecture describing the kernel of
\rho^{\mathrm{H}}
has been put forward by Bridgeland
[
18
]
and has been proven for K3 surfaces with Picard rank one
[
7
, Thm. 1.3]
.

The main goal of this paper is to find suitable analogues for the above results for the higher-dimensional analogues of K3 surfaces, that is hyper-Kähler manifolds
X
. For example, we want to study their derived categories and equivalences between them by means of an integral lattice of rank
b_{2}(X)+2
.

1.2.
Hyper-Kähler manifolds

Let
X
be a compact irreducible hyper-Kähler manifold of dimension
2n
, that is a compact simply connected Kähler manifold whose space of holomorphic two-forms is spanned by a non-degenerate symplectic form. We briefly recall properties of
X
needed to state our results, see Section
2
for a more thorough recollection.

The second cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
of
X
is endowed with a quadratic form called the Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki (BBF) form
b
. The Verbitsky component

\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\subset\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

of
X
is the
subalgebra generated by all cohomology classes of degree two. It inherits a bilinear form

b_{\textup{SH}}(\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{m},\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{2n-m})=(-1)^{m}\int_{X}\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{m}\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{2n-m}

called
Mukai pairing
from the intersection pairing, where
\omega_{i},\mu_{j}\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. The extended rational Mukai lattice of
X
is the graded vector space

\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\coloneqq{\mathbb{Q}}\alpha\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\oplus{\mathbb{Q}}\beta.

This space is endowed with a bilinear form
\tilde{b}
as well as a Hodge structure which both restrict to the BBF form and the Hodge structure on
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
, respectively. The classes
\alpha
and
\beta
are of Hodge type, orthogonal to
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
and satisfy
\tilde{b}(\alpha,\alpha)=\tilde{b}(\beta,\beta)=0
as well as
\tilde{b}(\alpha,\beta)=-1
such that
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
resembles
\mathrm{H}(S,{\mathbb{Q}})
for
S
a K3 surface, see Section
2.2
for more details. There exists an embedding

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi\colon\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}

of quadratic spaces and
T
denotes the the orthogonal projection onto the subspace
\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. The morphism
\psi
realizes the Verbitsky component as an irreducible representation of the Looijenga–Lunts–Verbitsky (LLV) algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}(X)
, see
[
55
,
37
,
56
]
and Section
2.2
.

Let us turn now to derived categories of hyper-Kähler manifolds
X
. Until recently not much has been known about
\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)
. Huybrechts–Nieper-Wißkirchen have shown that any Fourier–Mukai partner of
X
is again a hyper-Kähler manifold
[
29
, Thm. 0.4]
.
Taelman in
[
55
]
has refined the study of the derived category of
X
. He showed that a derived equivalence
\Phi\colon\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)

between hyper-Kähler manifolds restricts to a Hodge isometry

\Phi^{\mathrm{SH}}\colon\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\mathrm{SH}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})

which is functorially induced by a Hodge isometry

(1.2)

\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}\colon\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}}),

see
[
55
, Sec. 4]
or Section
2.3
. This can be used to show that for derived equivalent hyper-Kähler manifolds
X
and
Y
there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{i}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})

of
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Hodge structures for all
i

[
55
, Thm. D]
.

1.3.
Extended Mukai vector

The starting point of this paper is the following observation.

Proposition
3.4
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler manifold of dimension
2n
. Then

\overline{\mathsf{td}^{1/2}}=T\left(\frac{(\alpha+r_{X}\beta)^{n}}{n!}\right)\in\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

Here, we decompose

\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\oplus\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})^{\perp}

orthogonally with respect to the intersection product on cohomology and
\overline{(\_)}
denotes the projection onto the subspace
\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. The number
r_{X}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
is an explicit constant depending on
n
, the second Chern class

\mathrm{c}_{2}(X)
, and the Fujiki constant
c_{X}
of
X
, see (
3.1
). In particular, it only depends on the deformation type of
X
. For K3 surfaces the proposition reads

\mathsf{td}^{1/2}=T(\alpha+\beta)=\mathsf{1}+{\mathsf{p}}\in\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})

and for
X
of
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type we have

\overline{\mathsf{td}^{1/2}}=T\left(\frac{(\alpha+\frac{n+3}{4}\beta)^{n}}{n!}\right)\in\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

This enables us to define in Section
4
an
extended Mukai vector

\tilde{v}({\mathcal{E}})\in\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

for certain objects
{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)
. It relates to the classical Mukai vector
v({\mathcal{E}})\in\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
via

T(\tilde{v}({\mathcal{E}})^{n})=c\overline{v({\mathcal{E}})}\in\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

for
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}

\tilde{v}({\mathcal{E}})

. For example, to
a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}\in\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(X)
with first Chern class
\mathrm{c}_{1}({\mathcal{L}})=\lambda
we assign

\tilde{v}({\mathcal{L}})=\alpha+\lambda+\left(r_{X}+\frac{b(\lambda,\lambda)}{2}\right)\beta\in\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

1.4.
Derived equivalences of hyper-Kähler manifolds

Consider a derived equivalence
\Phi\colon\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)
between projective hyper-Kähler manifolds
X
and
Y
.
Associated to it we have the induced Hodge isometry

\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}\colon\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}}).

It is a priori very hard to calculate this isometry for a given derived equivalence. However, the above defined extended Mukai vector allows us now to easily compute
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}
for most known examples of derived equivalences between hyper-Kähler manifolds.
We demonstrate this in Sections
7
and
10
.

Moreover, its properties lead to the following structural result for derived equivalences between hyper-Kähler manifolds.

Theorem
4.15
.

Let
X
and
Y
be deformation-equivalent projective hyper-Kähler manifolds and
\Phi\colon\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)
an equivalence with Fourier–Mukai kernel
{\mathcal{E}}
. The rank
r
of
{\mathcal{E}}
is of the form
\frac{a^{n}n!}{c_{X}}
for
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. If
r=0
, then
{\mathcal{E}}
induces coverings of
X
and
Y
with Lagrangian cycles, or there exists a Hodge isometry
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

If, for example,
{\mathcal{E}}
is an
X
-flat sheaf on
X\times Y
, then the second statement of the theorem means that the codimension
n
component of
\textup{supp}({\mathcal{E}})
is a flat family of Lagrangian subvarieties of
Y
which dominates
Y
.

Note that the number in the above theorem

\frac{a^{n}n!}{c_{X}}

must in particular be an integer. For all known examples of hyper-Kähler manifolds
c_{X}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and therefore we must already have
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}

using Legendre’s or de Polignac’s formula.

The theorem splits derived equivalences
\Phi=\mathsf{FM}_{\mathcal{E}}\colon\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)
between hyper-Kähler manifolds into three cases. If the rank of the Fourier–Mukai kernel
{\mathcal{E}}
is non-zero, we are in the first case and the theorem asserts that the possible ranks of
{\mathcal{E}}
are severly restricted. In the second case, the geometries of
X
and
Y
are related by a correspondence in the
n
-th Chow group
\mathsf{A}^{n}(X\times Y)
which induces coverings of both manifolds by Lagrangian cycles. In the last case the derived equivalence implies the existence of a Hodge isometry
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
. To obtain a geometric interpretation of this conclusion, recall that up to finite index any Hodge isometry is induced from a parallel transport operator
[
41
, Lem. 6.23]
. The Global Torelli Theorem
[
57
]
states that the existence of a Hodge isometry
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
induced from a parallel transport operator is equivalent to
X
and
Y
being birational.

1.5.
Integral structure

We now specialize for the rest of the introduction to the case of
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type hyper-Kähler manifolds
X
, that is hyper-Kähler manifolds which are deformation-equivalent to Hilbert scheme of length
n
subschemes on a K3 surface. In this case we are able to obtain an integral lattice of rank
b_{2}(X)+2
invariant under derived equivalences mimicking the situation for K3 surfaces.

More explicitly, we define in Section
5
an integral lattice

\Lambda\subset\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

called
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
lattice
which inherits a Hodge structure
\Lambda_{X}
from
X
through the embedding. As an abstract lattice it is isometric to
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus U
with

U
the hyperbolic plane, but its weight-two Hodge structure differs from
the one induced from
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
by a B-field twist, see Remark
5.8
. The main difference in the higher-dimensional situation compared to the case of K3 surfaces is that
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
is not unimodular and the B-field twist compensates for the non-trivial discriminant.

The
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
lattice is a sublattice

\Lambda\subset\Lambda_{g}
of index two of the lattice
\Lambda_{g}

generated by all extended Mukai vectors of objects in
\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)
. We refer to Section
5
for a discussion of all the lattices that appear and their relations.

Our main result now is the following yielding a complete analogue of Mukai’s results
[
46
]
for derived equivalences of K3 surfaces.

Theorem
9.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be projective
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type hyper-Kähler manifolds
and
\Phi\colon\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)
a derived equivalence. Then
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}
restricts to a Hodge isometry

\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}\colon\Lambda_{X}\cong\Lambda_{Y}.

Even stronger, the
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
lattice is invariant by the action of all (compositions of) parallel transport operators and derived equivalences acting on the extended Mukai lattice
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. The precise statement is Theorem
8.1
.

As in the surface case, the existence of a lattice together with a Hodge structure governing properties of the derived category has strong implications. Here is one example.

Theorem
9.4
.

For a fixed projective
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type hyper-Kähler manifold
X
the
number of projective
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type manifolds
Y
up to isomorphism with
\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)
is finite.

For all currently known deformation types of hyper-Kähler manifolds a derived equivalence
\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)
implies that
X
and
Y
must, in fact, be deformation-equivalent. In general, it is not known whether this conclusion remains true for arbitrary hyper-Kähler manifolds.

In
[
8
, Thm. 1.2]
it is shown that any hyper-Kähler manifold which is birational to a moduli space of stable objects on a K3 surface
S
is itself a moduli space of stable objects on
S
. Using the
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
lattice we are able to upgrade this result to derived categories.

Corollary
9.6
.

Let
M^{S}_{\sigma}(v)
be a smooth moduli space of stable objects on a projective K3 surface
S
and
X
a projective
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type hyper-Kähler manifold such that
\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(M^{S}_{\sigma}(v))
. Then
X
is itself a moduli space of stable objects on
S
.

The corollary also yields the following.

Corollary
9.7
.

For two smooth moduli spaces
M^{S}_{\sigma}(v)
and
M^{S^{\prime}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(v^{\prime})
of stable objects on projective K3 surfaces
S
and
S^{\prime}
with
\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(M^{S}_{\sigma}(v))\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(M^{S^{\prime}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(v^{\prime}))
we have
\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(S)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(S^{\prime})
. Furthermore,
S
and
S^{\prime}
are derived equivalent if and only if their Hilbert schemes
S^{[n]}
and
S^{\prime[n]}
are derived equivalent.

Finally, considering a single hyper-Kähler manifold
X
of
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type Theorem
9.2
implies that the representation

\rho^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}\colon\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X))\to\mathrm{O}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))

induced from (
1.2
) factors via a representation

\rho^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}\colon\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X))\to\operatorname{Aut}(\Lambda_{X}).

Here,
\operatorname{Aut}(\Lambda_{X})
denotes the group of all Hodge isometries of the
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
lattice
\Lambda_{X}
. Specializing to Hilbert schemes
X=S^{[n]}
of elliptic K3 surfaces
S
with a section, we are able to give a lower bound on the image of
\rho^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}
.

Theorem
9.8
.

For the Hilbert scheme
S^{[n]}
of a K3 surface with
U\subset\textup{NS}(S)
the image
\textup{Im}(\rho^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}})
of the representation
\rho^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}
satisfies

\hat{\operatorname{Aut}}^{+}(\Lambda_{S^{[n]}})\subset\textup{Im}(\rho^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}})\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\Lambda_{S^{[n]}}).

The group
\hat{\operatorname{Aut}}^{+}(\Lambda_{S^{[n]}})
is the group of all Hodge isometries with real spinor norm one which act via
\pm\mathrm{id}
on the discriminant group. When
2n-2=4p^{r}
or
2n-2=2p^{r}q^{s}
for odd prime numbers
p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and natural numbers
r,s
the inclusion

\hat{\operatorname{Aut}}^{+}(\Lambda_{S^{[n]}})\subset\operatorname{Aut}^{+}(\Lambda_{S^{[n]}})

is an equality. In these cases Theorem
9.8
determines
\mathrm{Im}(\rho^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}})
up to index two.

Theorem
9.2
as well as the existence of the extended Mukai vectors yield several further strong consequences
for the derived category and derived equivalences of hyper-Kähler manifolds. Instead of reciting all of them here, we invite the reader to directly go to Sections
4
and
9
.

Related work

While finishing writing this paper, Eyal Markman informed us that he has also constructed in

a Mukai vector with image in the extended Mukai lattice for certain objects in the derived category. His approach uses Hochschild

While working on this paper we also realised that a broader

.

1.7.
Structure of the paper

In Section
2
we recall results for hyper-Kähler manifolds and their derived categories.

In the first part of this paper we study arbitrary hyper-Kähler manifolds. In Section
3
we prove Proposition
3.4
using results from Rozansky–Witten theory. In Section
4
we define the extended Mukai vector. There are two different classes of objects for which this can be done and we discuss their properties and give examples.

In the second part we specialize to hyper-Kähler manifolds deformation-equivalent to the Hilbert scheme of a K3 surface. In Sections
5
and
6
we introduce the lattices that will play a role as well as the derived monodromy group. In Section
7
we study derived equivalences of the Hilbert scheme on the
extended Mukai lattice using the extended Mukai vector. With these preparations we prove in the subsequent section the invariance under derived equivalences of the lattice
\Lambda
. Consequences of the previous results
will be drawn in Section
9
. We conclude by demonstrating how known derived equivalences of hyper-Kähler manifolds
fit into the new set-up.

We will always work over the complex numbers. The derived category

of a smooth projective variety
X

X

A lattice is a free

[

and

Recollections

2.1.
Hyper-Kähler manifolds and their cohomology

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler manifold of complex dimension
2n
, i.e. a simply connected compact Kähler manifold such that
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{2})
is generated by an everywhere non-degenerate holomorphic two-form. The second cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
possesses an integral primitive quadratic form
b
called the
Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki (BBF)
form. It is characterized up to sign by the property that there exists a constant
c_{X}
, called the
Fujiki constant
, that only depends on the deformation type of
X
such that

\int_{X}\omega^{2n}=c_{X}\frac{(2n)!}{2^{n}n!}b(\omega,\omega)^{n}

for all
\omega\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
. For the known examples of hyper-Kähler manifolds we have

c_{X}=\begin{cases}1&\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}\textup{ or }\mathrm{OG}^{10}\textup{-type,}\\
n+1&\mathrm{Kum}^{n}\textup{ or }\mathrm{OG}^{6}\textup{-type.}\end{cases}

For the following, see
[
23
, Cor. 23.17]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler manifold of dimension
2n
and consider a
class
\mu\in\mathrm{H}^{4p}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
which is of type
(2p,2p)
on all small deformations of
X
. Then there exists a constant
C(\mu)\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\int_{X}\mu\omega^{2n-2p}=C(\mu)b(\omega,\omega)^{n-p}

for all
\omega\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
.

Using Rozansky–Witten theory Nieper-Wißkirchen
[
47
]
established results on characteristic classes and Riemann–Roch formulae for hyper-Kähler manifolds.

Definition 2.2
.

For
\omega\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
define its
characteristic value
as

\lambda(\omega)\coloneqq\frac{(2n)!12c_{X}}{2^{n}n!(2n-1)C(\mathrm{c}_{2}(X))}b(\omega,\omega).

Using the Fujiki relations, one can check that the above definition agrees with
[
47
, Def. 17]
. For the formula of the square root of the Todd class we will need the following result, cf.
[
47
, p. 738]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

For
X
a hyper-Kähler manifold of dimension
2n
and arbitrary
\omega\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
it holds

\int_{X}\mathsf{td}^{1/2}\exp(\omega)=(1+\lambda(\omega))^{n}\int_{X}\mathsf{td}^{1/2}.

2.2.
Verbitsky component

Denote by
(\tilde{\textup{H}}(X,\mathbb{Q}),\tilde{b})
the rational quadratic vector
space defined by

{\mathbb{Q}}\alpha\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})\oplus{\mathbb{Q}}\beta.

The quadratic form
\tilde{b}
on
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
restricts to the BBF form
b
on
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
and the two classes
\alpha
and
\beta
are orthogonal to
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
and satisfy
\tilde{b}(\alpha,\beta)=-1
as well as
\tilde{b}(\alpha,\alpha)=\tilde{b}(\beta,\beta)=0
. Although it is not integral, we call
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
the
extended Mukai lattice
of
X
.

Furthermore, we define on
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
a grading by declaring
\alpha
to
be of degree zero,
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
remains in degree two and
\beta
is of degree four. Finally, the extended Mukai lattice is equipped with a weight-two Hodge structure

\displaystyle(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}})^{2,0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathrm{H}^{2,0}(X)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}})^{0,2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathrm{H}^{0,2}(X)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}})^{1,1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(X)\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\alpha\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\beta.

Let
\textup{SH}(X,\mathbb{Q})
be the
Verbitsky component
, i.e. the graded subalgebra of
\textup{H}^{*}(X,\mathbb{Q})
generated by
\textup{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
. Verbitsky
[
12
,
56
]
proved the existence of a graded morphism
\psi\colon\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\to\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))
sitting in a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\textup{SH}(X,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\psi}\textup{Sym}^{n}(\tilde{\textup{H}}(X,\mathbb{Q}))\xrightarrow{\Delta}\textup{Sym}^{n-2}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,\mathbb{Q}))\rightarrow 0.

Here, the map
\Delta
is the Laplacian operator defined on pure tensors via

v_{1}\cdots v_{n}\mapsto\sum_{i<j}\tilde{b}(v_{i},v_{j})v_{1}\cdots\hat{v_{i}}\cdots\hat{v_{j}}\cdots v_{n}.

The map
\psi
is uniquely determined (up to scaling) by the condition that it is a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}(X)
-modules, where
\mathfrak{g}(X)

denotes the
Looijenga–Lunts–Verbitsky (LLV)
algebra, see
[
37
]
or
[
21
]
. Recall that
{\mathfrak{g}}(X)
is the Lie algebra generated by all
{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{2}
-triples
(e_{\omega},h,f_{\omega})
with
e_{\omega}
the Lefschetz operator for
\omega\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
satisfying the Hard Lefschetz property,
h
the grading operator and
f_{\omega}
the dual Lefschetz operator.

The
{\mathfrak{g}}(X)
-structure of
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
is defined by the conditions
e_{\omega}(\alpha)=\omega
,
e_{\omega}(\mu)=b(\omega,\mu)\beta
and
e_{\omega}(\beta)=0
for all classes
\omega,\mu\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. The
n
-th symmetric power
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))
then inherits the structure of a
{\mathfrak{g}}(X)
-module by letting
{\mathfrak{g}}(X)
act by derivations. The inclusion realizes
\textup{SH}(X,\mathbb{Q})
as an irreducible Lefschetz module
[
56
]
. We fix once and for all a choice of
\psi
by setting
\psi(1)=\alpha^{n}/n!
.

Taelman
[
55
, Sec. 3]
showed that the map
\psi
is an isometry with respect to the
Mukai pairing

b_{\textup{SH}}(\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{m},\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{2n-m})=(-1)^{m}\int_{X}\omega_{1}\cdots\omega_{m}\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{2n-m}

on
\textup{SH}(X,\mathbb{Q})
and the pairing

b_{[n]}(x_{1}\cdots x_{n},y_{1}\cdots y_{n})=(-1)^{n}c_{X}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{b}(x_{i},y_{\sigma(i)})

on
\textup{Sym}^{n}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))
. Note that our definition of
b_{[n]}
differs from Taelman’s definition by the Fujiki constant. Ours has the advantage that
\psi
is always an isometry. The orthogonal projection onto the subspace
\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
will be denoted by

T\colon\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))\to\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

Remark 2.4
.

Observe that
\psi
is surjective in cohomological degrees
0,2,4n-2
and
4n
. Equivalently, the projection
T
is injective restricted to these
degrees.

Bogomolov and Verbitsky
[
12
,
56
]
showed that the Verbitsky component can also be described via

(2.1)

\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{C}})\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet}\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}})/\langle\mu^{n+1}\mid b(\mu,\mu)=0\rangle.

2.3.
Derived equivalences

The following is
[

, Thm. A]
.

(Taelman)
.

Let
X
and

be projective hyper-Kähler manifolds together with an equivalence
\Phi\colon\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)
. Then
\Phi
induces a canonical Lie algebra isomorphism

\Phi^{{\mathfrak{g}}}\colon{\mathfrak{g}}(X)\cong{\mathfrak{g}}(Y)

which is equivariant for the induced isometry
\Phi^{\mathrm{H}}\colon\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

We reproduce some consequences of this result from

, Sec. 4]
needed below.
The above theorem implies that given an auto-equivalence

which is equivariant with respect to

Moreover, the above represention

factors over a representation

under the assumption that

is odd, or having

even and

odd. Note that all known examples of hyper-Kähler manifolds satisfy one of the two conditions.

every Hodge isometry

of

is induced by an isometry of

which comes from a unique Hodge isometry

of

, Prop. 4.1]
, i.e. the following diagram

commutes. For even

3.
Square root of the Todd class

Denote by
\overline{\mathsf{td}^{1/2}}
the projection of the square root of the Todd
class to the Verbitsky component
\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. The main goal of this section is to express this class in terms of the extended Mukai lattice. Throughout this section
X
will be a fixed hyper-Kähler manifold of dimension
2n
of arbitrary deformation type.

Let us define a number

(3.1)

r_{X}\coloneqq\frac{C(\mathrm{c}_{2}(X))2^{n}n!(2n-1)}{(2n)!24c_{X}}

where
C(\mathrm{c}_{2}(X))
is the constant from Proposition
2.1
associated to the second Chern class
\mathrm{c}_{2}(X)
. The number relates the BBF form and the characteristic value via

(3.2)

b(\omega,\omega)=2r_{X}\lambda(\omega)

for all
\omega\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following equality holds

\int_{X}\mathsf{td}^{1/2}=c_{X}\frac{r_{X}^{n}}{n!}.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
be a Kähler class and
t
a formal variable. Proposition
2.3
gives

(3.3)

\int_{X}\mathsf{td}^{1/2}\exp(t\omega)=(1+\lambda(t\omega))^{n}\int_{X}\mathsf{td}^{1/2}.

Both sides are even polynomials in
t
of degree
2n
.
Comparing the coefficient in front of
t^{2n}
in (
3.3
) and using

\mathsf{td}^{1/2}=\mathsf{1}+\textup{terms of higher degree}

we obtain

c_{X}\frac{(2n)!}{2^{n}n!}\frac{b(\omega,\omega)^{n}}{(2n)!}=\lambda(\omega)^{n}\int_{X}\mathsf{td}^{1/2}.

Solving for
\int_{X}\mathsf{td}^{1/2}
and employing (
3.2
) yields the assertion.
∎

For the known examples of hyper-Kähler manifolds of dimension
2n
we have

r_{X}=\begin{cases}\frac{n+3}{4}&\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}\textup{ or }\mathrm{OG}^{10}\textup{-type,}\\
\frac{n+1}{4}&\mathrm{Kum}^{n}\textup{ or }\mathrm{OG}^{6}\textup{-type.}\end{cases}

Lemma
3.1
for
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
- and
\mathrm{Kum}^{n}
-type hyper-Kähler manifolds was also obtained by Sawon
[
53
]
.

Denote for
0\leq i\leq n
by
\mathsf{q}_{2i}\in\mathrm{SH}^{4i}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
the class defined by the property

\int_{X}\mathsf{q}_{2i}\omega^{2n-2i}=c_{X}\frac{(2n-2i)!}{2^{n-i}(n-i)!}b(\omega,\omega)^{n-i}

for all
\omega\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler manifold. Then the subspace of
\textup{SH}^{2i}(X,\mathbb{Q})
being of type
(i,i)
on all small deformations is one-dimensional if
i
is even and zero
otherwise. These subspaces are generated by
\mathsf{q}_{2i}
.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Using Lemma
3.2
let us write

\overline{\mathsf{td}^{1/2}}=\mathsf{q}_{0}+a_{2}\mathsf{q}_{2}+\dots+a_{2n-2}\mathsf{q}_{2n-2}+\frac{r_{X}^{n}}{n!}\mathsf{q}_{2n}\in\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

for
a_{2i}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. We will now determine the remaining coefficients.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
1\leq i\leq n
we have

a_{2i}=\frac{r_{X}^{i}}{i!}.

Proof.

We use again (
3.3
) and this time compare the coefficients in front of
t^{2n-2i}
. This reads

a_{2i}c_{X}\frac{b(\omega,\omega)^{n-i}}{2^{n-i}(n-i)!}={n\choose n-i}\frac{r_{X}^{i}c_{X}}{2^{n-i}n!}b(\omega,\omega)^{n-i}.\qed

Recall the isometric embedding
\psi\colon\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))
and the orthogonal projection
T\colon\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))\to\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. The class
\overline{\mathsf{td}^{1/2}}
has the following expression.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler manifold of dimension
2n
. Then

\overline{\mathsf{td}^{1/2}}=T\left(\frac{(\alpha+r_{X}\beta)^{n}}{n!}\right)\in\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

If one ignores the orthogonal projection
T
for the moment, then the proposition says that (the projection of) the square root of the Todd class can be expressed as the
n
-th power of a linear polynomial. Note that the orthogonal projection
T
really is necessary since
\alpha^{n-i}\beta^{i}
is not in the kernel of the Laplacian operator
\Delta
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

The key step to prove Proposition
3.4
is to relate
\alpha^{n-i}\beta^{i}
with the classes
\mathsf{q}_{2i}
. By definition we have

T(\alpha^{n})=n!\mathsf{1}.

In general the connection is given by the following.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
1\leq i\leq n
we have

T(\alpha^{n-i}\beta^{i})=(n-i)!\mathsf{q}_{2i}.

Proof.

By definition of the Mukai pairing on
\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
and the defining property of
\mathsf{q}_{2i}
the assertion of the lemma is equivalent to

b_{\mathrm{SH}}(\omega^{2n-2i},T(\alpha^{n-i}\beta^{i}))=\int_{X}\omega^{2n-2i}T(\alpha^{n-i}\beta^{i})=c_{X}\frac{(2n-2i)!}{2^{n-i}}b(\omega,\omega)^{n-i}

for all
\omega\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. The embedding
\psi\colon\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))
is an isometry and
T
is its orthogonal split. The above is therefore equivalent to

b_{[n]}(\psi(\omega^{2n-2i}),\alpha^{n-i}\beta^{i})=c_{X}\frac{(2n-2i)!}{2^{n-i}}b(\omega,\omega)^{n-i}

for all
\omega\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. Since
\psi
is a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}(X)
-modules, we have

\psi(\omega^{2n-2i})=\psi(e_{\omega}^{2n-2i}(\mathsf{1}))=e_{\omega}^{2n-2i}(\psi(\mathsf{1}))\in\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))

which is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear combination of tensors of the form
\alpha^{r}\omega^{s}\beta^{t}
. Only the tensor
\alpha^{i}\beta^{n-i}
pairs non-trivially with
\alpha^{n-i}\beta^{i}
. We claim that

e_{\omega}^{2n-2i}(\psi(\mathsf{1}))=\frac{(2n-2i)!}{2^{n-i}(n-i)!i!}b(\omega,\omega)^{n-i}\alpha^{i}\beta^{n-i}+\dots.

Indeed, let us choose
N\gg n
and consider the terms
\alpha^{r}\omega^{s}\beta^{t}
for
r+s+t=N
. We define the order of such a term as
r
, i.e. the exponent of
\alpha
. For general
j\geq 0
the element
e_{\omega}^{j}(\psi(\mathsf{1}))
decomposes

e_{\omega}^{j}(\psi(\mathsf{1}))=a^{j}_{1}\left(\frac{\alpha^{N-j}\omega^{j}}{(n-j)!}\right)+a^{j}_{2}\left(\frac{\alpha^{N-j+1}\omega^{j-2}\beta}{(n-j+21)!}\right)+\dots+a^{j}_{\lfloor{\frac{j}{2}}\rfloor}\left(\frac{\alpha^{N-\lfloor{\frac{j}{2}}\rfloor}\omega^{j-2\lfloor{\frac{j}{2}}\rfloor}\beta^{\lfloor{\frac{j}{2}}\rfloor}}{(N-\lfloor{\frac{j}{2}}\rfloor)!}\right)

according to the order of the terms that appear. A straight forward proof by induction shows that

a^{j}_{k+1}=\frac{(\lfloor{\frac{j}{2}}\rfloor+k)!}{(\lfloor{\frac{j}{2}}\rfloor-k)!k!2^{k}}

which are the coefficients of the Besse polynomials. This then yields the claim.

Observing that

b_{[n]}(\alpha^{i}\beta^{n-i},\alpha^{n-i}\beta^{i})=c_{X}(n-i)!i!

finishes the proof.
∎

The above lemma helps to understand the two extra classes
\alpha
and
\beta
in the extended Mukai lattice
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. Rather than trying to identify the classes
\alpha
and
\beta
in
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
with a single element in
\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
as in the case of K3 surfaces one should think simultaneously of all the powers
\alpha^{n-i}\beta^{i}
as (multiples of) the powers of the BBF-form
\mathsf{q}_{2}^{i}\in\mathrm{SH}^{4i}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Proof of Proposition
3.4
.

This follows immediately from Lemma
3.1
, Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.5
.
∎

.

is of

4.
Extended Mukai vector

The previous section enables us to define a Mukai vector for interesting objects with image in the extended Mukai lattice. We will distinguish two
cases using the self-intersection of the vector under consideration.

4.1.
Square
-2r_{X}

Let
X

.
A line bundle

via the diagram

where

for all equivalences
\Phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X))
. Since
\mathsf{M}_{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})={\mathcal{L}}
and by the compatibility of the cohomological Fourier–Mukai transform
\mathsf{M}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{H}}(v({\mathcal{O}}_{X}))=v({\mathcal{L}})

(4.3)

\overline{v({\mathcal{L}})}=T\left(\frac{(\alpha+\lambda+(r_{X}+\frac{b(\lambda,\lambda)}{2})\beta)^{n}}{n!}\right)\in\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

for all line bundles
{\mathcal{L}}\in\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(X)
.

Definition 4.1
.

For
{\mathcal{L}}\in\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(X)
we define the
extended Mukai vector
of
{\mathcal{L}}
with
\mathrm{c}_{1}({\mathcal{L}})=\lambda
as

\tilde{v}({\mathcal{L}})=\alpha+\lambda+\left(r_{X}+\frac{b(\lambda,\lambda)}{2}\right)\beta\in\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

With this definition, we have

(4.4)

\overline{v({\mathcal{L}})}=T\left(\frac{\tilde{v}({\mathcal{L}})^{n}}{n!}\right)\in\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

Formula (

) is a helpful tool to deduce properties of
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}
and compute its action on
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
for an auto-equivalence
\Phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X))
. Here is one example.

If

is even, then the functoriality of the representation
\rho^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}\colon\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X))\to\mathrm{O}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))
depends on the determinant of the isometry of
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. A useful criterion to calculate
\epsilon(\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}})
for an auto-equivalence
\Phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X))
is the following.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
X
be a projective hyper-Kähler manifold with
n
even and
b_{2}(X)
odd. Assume that a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
is sent under an auto-equivalence
\Phi
to an object
{\mathcal{F}}
with positive rank. Then
\epsilon(\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}})=\det(\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}})=1
.

Proof.

The class
\tilde{v}({\mathcal{L}})^{n}/n!\in\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))
maps under
T
to
\overline{v({\mathcal{L}})}
. We know that
\Phi^{\mathrm{H}}
sends
v({\mathcal{L}})
to
v({\mathcal{F}})
.
Therefore
\overline{v({\mathcal{L}})}
is sent to
\overline{v({\mathcal{F}})}
under
\Phi^{\mathrm{SH}}
. Since
n
is even, the coefficient in front of
\alpha^{n}
in the expression
(\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}(\tilde{v}({\mathcal{L}})))^{n}
must be positive. The commutativity of (
4.2
) forces
\epsilon(\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}})=1
.
∎

Instead of twists with line bundles, we can also use other auto-equivalences to define an extended Mukai vector for a larger set of objects.

Definition 4.3

Let

Ideally one would like to give an analogous definition in the case that

4.3.
Structural result

The discussion of the two previous subsections enables us to prove the following general structural result for derived equivalences between hyper-Kähler manifolds.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
X
and
Y
be deformation-equivalent projective hyper-Kähler manifolds and
\Phi\colon\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)
an equivalence with Fourier–Mukai kernel
{\mathcal{E}}
. The rank
r
of
{\mathcal{E}}
is of the form
\frac{a^{n}n!}{c_{X}}
for
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. If
r=0
, then
{\mathcal{E}}
induces a covering of
X
and
Y
with Lagrangian cycles, or there exists a Hodge isometry
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Proof.

Let us first assume that either
n
is odd or that
n
is even and
b_{2}(X)
odd.

We distinguish three cases depending on the image vector

v=a\alpha+\lambda+s\beta\in\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

of
\beta
under
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}
. For
a\neq 0
the assertion on the rank follows from Lemma
4.13
.

In the case that
a=0
, but
\lambda\neq 0
, we consider the the
n
-th Chern class
\mathrm{c}_{n}({\mathcal{E}})\in\mathsf{A}^{n}(X\times Y)
in the Chow ring with rational coefficients. The compatibility of derived equivalences with the induced maps between Chow and cohomology groups shows that for all
x\in X
and all
y\in Y
the cycles
\mathrm{c}_{n}({\mathcal{E}})|_{x\times Y}\in\mathsf{A}^{n}(Y)
respectively
\mathrm{c}_{n}({\mathcal{E}})|_{X\times y}\in\mathsf{A}^{n}(X)
are non-zero. Indeed the cohomological degree
2n
component of
v({\mathcal{E}}_{x})
is equal to
\mathrm{c}_{n}({\mathcal{E}}_{x})
considered in cohomology which by assumption equals
\lambda^{n}\in\mathrm{SH}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
and similarly for
X
. This shows that viewing the cycle
\mathrm{c}_{n}({\mathcal{E}})
as a family of cycles on
Y
parametrized by points
x\in X
these cycles cover
Y
in the sense that for each
y\in Y
the family of cycles
\mathrm{c}_{n}({\mathcal{E}})
restricts non-trivially to the subvariety
X\times y
. Since being isotropic is a cohomological property, the assertion follows now from
Lemma
4.13
(v).

Lastly, we assume that
v=s\beta
for
s\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. This assumption implies that the element
{\mathsf{p}}\in\mathrm{H}^{4n}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
gets sent to
\pm s^{n}{\mathsf{p}}\in\mathrm{H}^{4n}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
under the induced cohomological Fourier–Mukai transform
\Phi^{\mathrm{H}}
. We can view the image of topological
K
-theory under the Mukai vector map
v(K_{\textup{top}}(X))
as a lattice inside the full cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
equipped with the generalized Mukai pairing. We refer to
[
2
]
for a recollection of topological
K
-theory and its relationship to derived categories and Fourier–Mukai transforms. The isomorphism
\Phi^{\mathrm{H}}
then induces an isometry between the lattices

v(K_{\textup{top}}(X))
and
v(K_{\textup{top}}(Y))
. Since
{\mathsf{p}}\in v(K_{\textup{top}}(X))
is a primitive element, the same must be true for
\Phi^{\mathrm{H}}({\mathsf{p}})=\pm s^{n}{\mathsf{p}}
. Therefore
s\in\{\pm 1\}
.

We may assume without loss of generality that
s=1
. Since
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}
is an isometry, we infer that

\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}(\alpha)=\alpha+\lambda+t\beta\in\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}}).

We claim that already
\lambda\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
. To see this, consider
\tilde{v}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})=\alpha+r_{X}\beta
and its image

\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}(\tilde{v}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}))=\alpha+\lambda+(t+r_{X})\beta\in\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}}).

As above the element
v({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
belongs to
v(K_{\textup{top}}(X))
and therefore
\Phi^{\mathrm{H}}(v({\mathcal{O}}_{X}))
must be contained in
v(K_{\textup{top}}(Y))
. Using that the projection of
v(K_{\textup{top}}(Y))
to its degree
two component lands inside
\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
and the compatibility (
4.2
) we infer that also
\lambda
belongs to
\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Employing that
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}
is a Hodge isometry we furthermore conclude that
\lambda\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
. Hence, there exists a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}\in\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(Y)
with first Chern class
-\lambda
. Changing
\Phi
by postcomposing it with
\mathsf{M}_{\mathcal{L}}
we obtain a derived equivalence
\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)
still denoted by
\Phi
which satisfies
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}(\alpha)=\alpha
and
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}(\beta)=\beta
. Thus,
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}
restricts to a Hodge isometry

\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}}).

It remains to show that this isometry sends
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
to
\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
. We employ the same strategy as above. For
\lambda\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
the vector

\tilde{v}({\mathcal{L}})=\alpha+\lambda+\left(r_{X}+\frac{b(\lambda,\lambda)}{2}\right)\beta

can be viewed as the extended Mukai vector of the (topological) line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
with first Chern class
\lambda
. The Mukai vector
v({\mathcal{L}})
lies inside
v(K_{\textup{top}}(X))
and
\tilde{v}({\mathcal{L}})
is mapped under
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}
to

\alpha+\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}(\lambda)+\left(r_{X}+\frac{b(\lambda,\lambda)}{2}\right)\beta.

As before, the compatibility with topological
K
-theory forces
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}(\lambda)
to lie inside
\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
. This finishes the proof in the case
n
odd or
n
even and
b_{2}(X)
odd.

If we now assume that
n
as well as
b_{2}(X)
are odd, then we cannot apply the results from
[
55
]
as explained in Section
2.3
directly. That is, given a derived equivalence
\Phi\colon\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)
the induced cohomological Fourier–Mukai transform
\Phi^{\mathrm{H}}\colon\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
still restricts to a Hodge isometry

\Phi^{\mathrm{SH}}\colon\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\mathrm{SH}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}}),

but there may not exist a Hodge isometry
\varphi\in\mathrm{O}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))
such that (
2.2
) commutes. However, inspecting
[
55
, Prop. 4.1]
and its proof we see that there exists a Hodge isometry
\varphi\in\mathrm{O}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))
unique up to sign such that via (
2.2
) either
\Phi^{\mathrm{SH}}
or
-\Phi^{\mathrm{SH}}
agrees with
\varphi^{n}
. Reinspecting the above proof we see that this sign discrepancy does not affect the arguments and the proof remains valid also in the case
n
even and
b_{2}(X)
even.
∎

4.4.
Concluding remarks and further examples

For general sheaves and objects
{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)
we make the following definition.

Definition 4.16
.

An object
{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)
admits an
extended Mukai vector

\tilde{v}({\mathcal{E}})\in\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
if there exists
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}
such that

\overline{v({\mathcal{E}})}=cT(\tilde{v}({\mathcal{E}})^{n})\in\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

With this definition, the vector
\tilde{v}({\mathcal{E}})
is not uniquely defined. One rather considers a one-dimensional subspace
V\subset\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
and demands that
\overline{v({\mathcal{E}})}
lies in the one-dimensional subspace

T(V^{n})
. In the above two series of examples we considered a certain natural choice of
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}
which then enabled us to define the extended Mukai vector as a uniquely determined vector in
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Here are two observations how one can generate new examples of objects admitting an extended Mukai vector from known ones:

•

If
\Phi\colon\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)
is an equivalence, then
{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)
admits an extended Mukai vector if and only if
\Phi({\mathcal{E}})\in\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)
admits an extended Mukai vector.

•

Let
\pi\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to B
be a smooth and projective morphism with hyper-Kähler manifolds as fibres and
{\mathcal{E}}
on
{\mathcal{X}}
a
B
-flat sheaf or a
B
-perfect complex. For two points
b,b^{\prime}\in B
we have that
{\mathcal{E}}|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{b}}
admits an extended Mukai vector if and only if
{\mathcal{E}}|_{{\mathcal{X}}_{b^{\prime}}}
admits an extended Mukai vector.

One can prove similar results as in Lemmas
4.8
and
4.13
for objects
{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)
satisfying Definition
4.16
for a fixed
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. We just mention that if
{\mathcal{E}}
has zero rank, then the projections of all Chern classes of
{\mathcal{E}}
to
\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
are isotropic. To see this one writes
\tilde{v}({\mathcal{E}})=\lambda+s\beta
for
\lambda\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
and
s\in{\mathbb{Q}}
and uses that
e_{\sigma}(\tilde{v}({\mathcal{E}}))=e_{\bar{\sigma}}(\tilde{v}({\mathcal{E}}))=0
for
\sigma
and
\bar{\sigma}
the (anti-)holomorphic two-form.

An important class of cohomologically linearisable objects are line bundles and skyscraper sheaves. We want to give further examples.

Example 4.17

Remark 4.20
.

Ideally, one would like to define a vector
\tilde{w}
for all elements
{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)
in a coherent way. By this we mean for example that its formation should factor through the
K
-group
K(X)
and is compatible with derived equivalences, i.e. the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{w}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{w}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}}

should commute. However, this is too much to ask for.

Indeed, consider for example the case

of the Hilbert scheme of two points on a projective K3 surface

.

.
Therefore all previously defined objects in the

therefore also has to vanish on all divisors.

The same argument as above also shows that any vector

5.
Integral lattices for
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type hyper-Kähler manifolds

We want to apply the results and definitions from the previous sections. From now on,
X
will denote a hyper-Kähler manifold of
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type with
n>1
.

5.1.
Lattices

In this section, we want to discuss the (potential) integral lattices inside the extended Mukai lattice that appear and set up notation which will
be used throughout the rest of the paper. We will fix for
X
once and for all an isometry

(5.1)

\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}(S^{[n]},{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\delta,

where
S
is a K3 surface,
S^{[n]}
is the
n
-th Hilbert scheme with
2\delta
the class of the exceptional divisor of the Hilbert–Chow morphism and the second isometry is given by (
6.1
) (for
X=S^{[n]}
we choose the first isometry to be the identity).

Let us first quickly review the case of a K3 surface
S
. There, the full
integral cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{\ast}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})=\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
with the Mukai pairing is governing the derived category
[
49
,
46
]
. That is, an equivalence of K3 surfaces yields a Hodge isometry between the full
integral cohomologies and two K3 surfaces are derived equivalent if and only if their integral cohomologies are Hodge isometric.
Moreover, the lattice spanned by the Mukai vectors of topological line bundles equals the full integral cohomology.

In higher dimensions, the situation changes. There are several relevant lattices.

Definition 5.1
.

We define the
integral extended Mukai lattice
as the lattice

\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\coloneqq{\mathbb{Z}}\alpha\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\beta\subset\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

Several facts suggest that in higher dimensions this is the wrong lattice
to look at. Firstly, Definition
4.1
and Examples
4.17
and
4.19
suggest that one should allow certain denominators. Secondly, we will see in Proposition
7.2
that derived equivalences do not send integral elements to integral elements.

Definition 5.2
.

For
\delta\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
as above we define the
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
lattice
as

\Lambda\coloneqq B_{-\delta/2}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}))\subset\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

This is independent of our fixed choice of
\delta
, i.e. for any class
\gamma\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
of square
2-2n
and divisibility
2n-2
one has

\Lambda=B_{-\gamma/2}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}))\subset\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

In dimension
4
this lattice was also considered by Taelman
[
55
, Thm. E]
.

We introduce the notation

\tilde{\alpha}\coloneqq B_{-\delta/2}(\alpha)=\alpha-\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{1-n}{4}\beta,\quad\tilde{\delta}\coloneqq B_{-\delta/2}(\delta)=\delta+(n-1)\beta.

With it one can define equivalently the
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
lattice as the lattice

(5.2)

\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{\alpha}\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{\delta}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\beta=\Lambda_{S}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{\delta}

where

\Lambda_{S}={\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{\alpha}\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\beta.

Note that
\tilde{\alpha}
and
\beta
still generate an integral hyperbolic plane and that the decomposition
\Lambda_{S}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{\delta}
is orthogonal.
The integral extended Mukai lattice and the
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
lattice are isometric as abstract lattices and neither is included in the other when seen inside
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Definition 5.3
.

The
geometric lattice

\Lambda_{g}
is defined as

\Lambda_{g}\coloneqq\Lambda_{S}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\tilde{\delta}}{2}\subset\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

Be aware that the quadratic form of
\Lambda_{g}
inherited from
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
may not be integral.

We want to motivate this definition. Recall that
r_{X}=\frac{n+3}{4}
and let us look at the lattice generated by all extended Mukai vectors of topological line bundles, i.e.

\Lambda_{LB}\coloneqq\left\langle\left\{\tilde{v}(\lambda)\coloneqq\alpha+\lambda+\left(\frac{n+3}{4}+\frac{b(\lambda,\lambda)}{2}\right)\beta\;|\;\lambda\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\right\}\right\rangle.

Note that one can write the generators equivalently as

\tilde{v}(\lambda)=\tilde{\alpha}+\frac{\tilde{\delta}}{2}+\lambda+\left(1+\frac{b(\lambda,\lambda)}{2}\right)\beta.

If one ignores the term
\frac{\tilde{\delta}}{2}
for a moment, then the expression resembles the Mukai vector on a K3 surface (where
\mathsf{td}^{1/2}=\mathsf{1}+{\mathsf{p}}
).
One can check that as an abstract lattice
\Lambda_{LB}
is isometric to
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

In Section
4.4
we saw that there are more objects than line bundles and skyscraper sheaves of points for which we can define an extended Mukai vector. We have

\tilde{v}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}})=l+\frac{\tilde{\delta}}{2}+\beta,\quad\tilde{v}({\mathcal{E}}^{1}_{p\times S^{[}n]})=\tilde{\alpha}.

Lemma 5.4
.

The geometric lattice
\Lambda_{g}
equals the lattice spanned by
\Lambda_{LB}
as well as all extended Mukai vectors from Section
4.4
.

Proof.

This follows from a straightforward calculation.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

We expect that for all elements
{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)
for which a (meaningful)
extended Mukai vector
\tilde{v}({\mathcal{E}})
can be defined, one has
\tilde{v}({\mathcal{E}})\in\Lambda_{g}
. We will prove in Corollary
8.7
that
\Lambda_{g}
is invariant under all parallel transport isometries as well as derived equivalences.

5.2.
Hodge structures

All the above defined lattices carry a weight-two Hodge structure from their inclusion into
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Definition 5.6
.

For a lattice
\Gamma\subset\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
we define its
algebraic part
as

\Gamma_{\textup{alg}}\coloneqq\Gamma\cap\tilde{\mathrm{H}}^{1,1}(X,{\mathbb{C}})\subset\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

and its
transcendental part
as

\Gamma_{\textup{tr}}\coloneqq\Gamma_{\textup{alg}}^{\perp}\cap\Gamma\subset\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

With this definition the transcendental part of the integral extended Mukai lattice equals the
transcendental lattice
of the hyper-Kähler manifold
X
, i.e.

\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})_{\textup{tr}}\coloneqq\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\cap\textup{NS(X)}^{\perp}=\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})_{\textup{tr}}\subset\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}).

Lemma 5.7
.

The transcendental part
\Lambda_{\textup{tr}}
of the
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
lattice

\Lambda
equals the transcendental lattice of
X
.

Proof.

Both inclusions follow from (
5.2
).
∎

Remark 5.8
.

The isometry
B_{-\delta/2}
yields an isometry between the integral extended Mukai lattice
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
and the
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
lattice
\Lambda
, which in general does not respect the Hodge structures. However, if we endow

\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
with the twisted Hodge structure associated to the B-field
\delta/2\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
as defined in
[
30
, Def. 2.3]
, then
B_{-\delta/2}
induces a Hodge isometry between
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
endowed with the twisted Hodge structure and
\Lambda
equipped with
the Hodge structure coming from the embedding
\Lambda\subset\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
.

To see this consider a symplectic form
\sigma\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
. The twisted Hodge structure is determined by the element
\sigma+\frac{1}{2}b(\sigma,\delta)\beta
and this is sent under
B_{-\delta/2}
to the symplectic form
\sigma
. The untwisted and the twisted Hodge structure on
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
have the same transcendental lattice, whereas in the case of K3
surfaces the transcendental lattice of a twisted Hodge structure associated to a non-trivial Brauer class is always a proper sublattice of the transcendental lattice of the untwisted Hodge structure
[
30
, Sec. 2]
.

6.
Derived Monodromy group

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler manifold of
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type and let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be deformations of
X
. By this we mean smooth and proper morphisms
\pi_{i}\colon{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\to B_{i}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
with
B_{i}
connected such that there is one point
0_{i}\in B_{i}
with
\pi_{i}^{-1}(0_{i})\cong X
and another point
b_{i}\in B_{i}
such that
\pi_{i}^{-1}(b_{i})\cong X_{i}
. Let
\gamma_{i}\colon\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\mathrm{SH}(X_{i},{\mathbb{Q}})
be parallel transport isometries obtained from choosing a path between
0
and
b_{i}
in
B_{i}
. Moreover, consider a Fourier–Mukai equivalence
f\colon\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X_{1})\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X_{2})
and denote by
F=f^{\mathrm{SH}}
the induced isometry.

Definition 6.1

(Taelman)
.

The
derived monodromy group

\mathrm{DMon}(X)
is the subgroup of
\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))
generated by all isometries of the form

\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{\gamma_{1}}\mathrm{SH}(X_{1},{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{F}\mathrm{SH}(X_{2},{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{\gamma_{2}^{-1}}\mathrm{SH}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

We know from Section
2.3
that the isometry
F
is induced from an isometry
f^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}
. Similarly, the isometries
\gamma_{i}
are by
[
55
, Prop. 4.1]
induced by unique Hodge isometries
\gamma_{i}^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}\in\mathrm{O}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))
. This implies that the derived monodromy group has an inclusion
\mathrm{DMon}(X)\subset\mathrm{O}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))
.
Throughout this paper we will consider the elements of
\mathrm{DMon}(X)
always as
isometries of the extended Mukai lattice.

Let now
S^{[n]}
be the Hilbert scheme of
n
points on a projective K3 surface
S
. Bridgeland, King, and Reid
[
15
]
proved the existence of a derived equivalence

\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(S^{[n]})\cong\textrm{D}^{\textup{b}}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(S^{n})

where the latter is the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-equivariant derived category of the product variety
S^{n}
. For an introduction and notation regarding equivariant categories we refer to
[
10
]
. For our purposes, we will not take the equivalence from
[
15
]
, but the one considered by Krug in
[
36
]

\Psi\colon\textrm{D}^{\textup{b}}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(S^{n})\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(S^{[n]}),

since it has nice properties for the computations we want to perform.
Consider a line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
on
S
. There is a natural line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
on
S^{[n]}
associated to
{\mathcal{L}}
which satisfies
\Psi(({\mathcal{L}}^{\boxtimes n},1))={\mathcal{L}}_{n}

[
36
, Thm. 1.1]
. This yields the well-known
isomorphisms

(6.1)

\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(S^{[n]})\cong\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(S)\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\delta,\quad\mathrm{H}^{2}(S^{[n]},{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\delta

where
2\delta=[E]
is the class of the exceptional divisor of the Hilbert–Chow morphism. Since
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathfrak{S}_{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast})={\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
, the simple
object
{\mathcal{L}}^{\boxtimes n}\in\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(S^{n})
possesses another linearisation given by tensoring with the sign-representation. It holds

(6.2)

\Psi({\mathcal{L}}^{\boxtimes n},-1)={\mathcal{L}}_{n}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S^{[n]}}(-\delta),

where
{\mathcal{O}}_{S^{[n]}}(-\delta)\in\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(S^{[n]})
is the line bundle with first Chern class
-\delta

[
36
, Rem. 3.10]
.

Ploog
[
51
]
, later generalized by Ploog–Sosna
[

Let

7.1.
Sign equivalence

Denote by

the Hodge isometry given by reflection along

Proposition 7.1
.

The action of

on
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(S^{[n]},{\mathbb{Q}})
is given by
(-1)^{n+1}s_{\tilde{\delta}}

Proof.

For all topological line bundles

the involution

exchanges the equivariant objects
({\mathcal{L}}^{\boxtimes n},1)
and

({\mathcal{L}}^{\boxtimes n},-1)
viewed as elements in equivariant topological

) the induced isometry

on the extended Mukai lattice exchanges

and
\tilde{v}({\mathcal{L}}_{n}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S^{[n]}}(-\delta))
.

If

is odd, then we conclude from the above that for all

7.2.

Remark 7.3
.

Here is one observation from the proof which might help to understand the extended Mukai lattice
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(S^{[n]},{\mathbb{Q}})

inside a K3 surface and the corresponding line bundle

8.

Let

\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}

\Gamma\hookrightarrow\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})

\gamma(\Gamma)=\Gamma

The main goal of this section is to prove the following result.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let

be a
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}

.

8.1.
Realizing orthogonal transformations as derived equivalences

The first inclusion follows easily from the results of the last sections.

Proposition 8.2
.

There is an inclusion

Proof.

The shift
[1]
acts on the extended Mukai lattice by

. Proposition

Let us orthogonally decompose

where the hyperbolic plane

\tilde{\alpha}
and

Lemma 8.4

Remark 8.5

Ideally, one would like to conclude in the above situation directly that

Lemma 8.6

9.

\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type hyper-Kähler manifolds

We will draw some consequences from the results of the previous sections.

9.1.
General results

Let
X
be a projective hyper-Kähler manifold of
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type. We denote by
\Lambda_{X}
the Hodge structure obtained from the inclusion
\Lambda\subset\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
and by
\operatorname{Aut}(\Lambda_{X})
the group of all Hodge isometries of
\Lambda_{X}
. Recall the representation

\rho^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}\colon\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X))\to\mathrm{O}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}))

from Section
2.3
.

Corollary 9.1
.

The representation
\rho^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}
of the group of auto-equivalences
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X))
factors via a representation

\rho^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}\colon\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X))\to\operatorname{Aut}(\Lambda_{X})\subset\mathrm{O}(\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})).

One can also formulate the following more general version of the above statement.

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be projective
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type hyper-Kähler manifolds
and
\Phi\colon\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D}^{\textup{b}}(Y)
a derived equivalence. Then
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}
restricts to a Hodge isometry

\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}\colon\Lambda_{X}\cong\Lambda_{Y}.

Proof.

Since
X
and
Y
are deformation-equivalent, there exists a parallel transport isometry
\gamma\colon\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. The composition
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}\circ\gamma
lies in
\mathrm{DMon}(Y)
and, therefore, satisfies
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}\circ\gamma(\Lambda)=\Lambda
by Theorem
8.1
. Now
\gamma(\Lambda)\subset\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
is a lattice invariant under
\mathrm{DMon}(X)
.

Indeed, let
\gamma_{2}\circ F\circ\gamma_{1}
be one of the generators of
\mathrm{DMon}(X)
as in Definition
6.1
. Then
\gamma^{-1}\circ\gamma_{2}\circ F\circ\gamma_{1}\circ\gamma\in\mathrm{DMon}(Y)
, so Theorem
8.1
gives

\gamma^{-1}\circ\gamma_{2}\circ F\circ\gamma_{1}\circ\gamma(\Lambda)=\Lambda

which yields

\gamma_{2}\circ F\circ\gamma_{1}(\gamma(\Lambda))=\gamma(\Lambda).

Using Lemma
8.4
and that
\gamma
is an isometry we see that the subset
\gamma(\Lambda)\subset\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
is equal to
\Lambda\subset\tilde{\mathrm{H}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. Combining everything yields the assertion.
∎

We can use Lemma
5.7
to obtain the following form of Theorem
9.2
.

Corollary 9.3
.

Let
X,Y
and
\Phi
be as above. Then
\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}
restricts to a Hodge
isometry

\Phi^{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}}\colon\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})_{\textup{tr}}\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})_{\textup{tr}}

between the transcendental lattices of
X
and
Y
.∎

An immediate consequence is the following.

Theorem 9.4
.

For a fixed projective
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type hyper-Kähler manifold

the
number of projective
\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}
-type manifolds

up to isomorphism with

Proof.

The proof is similar in flavour to
[
16
, Prop. 5.3]
.

Corollary
9.3
implies that
for any

as in the assertion its transcendental lattice

-type hyper-Kähler manifolds

A tail estimate for empirical processes of multivariate Gaussian under general dependence
Wen Huo, Yasutaka Shimizu

Abstract
In this paper, we discuss the convergence rate of empirical processes of Gaussian processes for a large class of function families. Our main goal is to show the tail of the random quantity sup f ∈ F | P n ^ ​ ( f ) − E ⁡ [ P n ^ ​ ( f ) ] | \sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)-\mathrm{E}[\widehat{P_{n}}(f)]| can be dominated by polynomials. We put forward the properties of Hermite polynomials which play a crucial role in the proof of main theorems. At the end of the paper, we show the expectation of the random quantity converges to zero at the rate of o ⁡ ( n − 1 2 + ε ) o(n^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}) , which is proven to be o ⁡ ( n − 1 3 ) o(n^{-\frac{1}{3}}) in [ 4 ] . Keywords : Gaussian Process, Empirical Process, Concentration Inequality. Mathematics Subject Classification 2020 : 60E15



1
Introduction

In the field of probability theory, the tail estimate of a given stochastic process is one of the most basic and important problems. Specifically, if given a sequence of i.i.d random variables, for any bounded function family
F
such that
0\in F
, we have

\mathrm{E}\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)-\mathrm{E}[\widehat{P_{n}}(f)]|\leq C_{2}(F)n^{-\frac{1}{2}}+C_{1}(F)n^{-1},

where
C_{2}
and
C_{1}
are two constants only relying on the selection of
F
; see
[
1
]
].

We define the size of a given empirical process as
\mathrm{E}\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)-\mathrm{E}[\widehat{P_{n}}(f)]|
. Since it is easy to show the upper bound of the convergence rate of
\mathrm{E}\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)-\mathrm{E}[\widehat{P_{n}}(f)]|
cannot be better than
o(n^{-\frac{1}{2}})
, the result above tells us that under the condition of i.i.d., the structure of the size is extremely simple. To prove the theorem above, it is necessary to employ a famous result in tail estimate demonstrated as follows, which is the so-called Bernstein’s inequality,

\mathrm{P}(|\sum_{i}Y_{i}|\geq v)\leq 2\exp(-\min(\frac{v^{2}}{4\sum\mathrm{E}Y_{i}^{2}},\frac{v}{2U}))

holds for any number
v\geq 0
and any sequence of independent variables
Y_{i}(1\leq i\leq n)
.

Recalling that Bernstein’s inequality is essentially necessary for the proof, one can observe intuitively that by modifying the independent condition of the given Gaussian process slightly, a similar inequality might still be provable. However, this problem is extremely difficult. A well-known consequence shows that an exponential bound still holds for all
L
-Lipschitz functions (see
[
5
]
). In 2017, a result established by Paouris and Valettas in
[
6
]
extended the previous result to all convex functions and improved the exponential bound by adopting a variance-sensitive form. However, these results are not even applicable to the most common case – the indicators. Additionally, in this paper, we are going to prove the best convergence rate for indicators. Through some further assumptions, some papers show the Bernstein-like inequality holds for all measurable functions. For example, in
[
7
]
, the authors prove the inequality by employing the method of bounded martingale differences, and in
[
8
]
, a similar inequality for Markov chains is proven.

A good convergence rate still holds in some special cases without the establishment of Bernstein’s inequality; see
[
2
]
, Chapter 1. Let
[0,1)
be the unit interval equipped with Lebesgue measure and
\{x_{k},k\geq 1\}
be a sequence of random variables defined as
x_{k}:=n_{k}x
, where
1=n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq n_{3}\leq\cdots
, then exploiting the dynamical property of this sequence and some powerful tools from Fourier analysis, Roger Baker
[
9
]
proved

D_{n}=o(n^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon})

holds for any
\varepsilon>0.

In our paper, we do not prove the Bernstein-like inequality but bring in a basic tool in the probability theory, which is called chaining argument or chaining method; see
[
1
]
or
[
3
]
, to dominate the tail of empirical processes by polynomials. And this still implies the size of empirical processes converges at a rate close to the best one
O(n^{-\frac{1}{2}})
.

2
Notations

Throughout this paper, we adopt the notations from
[
1
]
and
[
3
]
.

Definition 2.1
(Empirical Process)

For a sequence of random variables
\{X_{k}\}
, the empirical process of it is defined as

\widehat{P_{n}}(f)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(X_{k}),

where
f\in F
and
F
is a fixed set of measurable function.

Definition 2.2
(Hermite Polynomials)

For
k\geq 0
, we define the
k^{th}
Hermite polynomial
H_{k}
by

H_{k}=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\sqrt{k!}}\exp(\frac{t^{2}}{2})\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\exp(-\frac{t^{2}}{2}).

Definition 2.3
(Entropy Numbers)

Given a compact set
F
, the
n^{th}
entropy number
e_{n}(F)
is defined as follows,

e_{n}(F)=\inf_{T_{n}\in F}\sup_{x\in F}d(x,T_{n}),

where
T_{n}
represents any subset of
F
with n elements.

Definition 2.4
(Size)

We define the size of a given empirical process
\widehat{P_{n}}(f)
as
\mathrm{E}\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)-\mathrm{E}(\widehat{P_{n}}(f))|
.

3
Properties of Hermite Polynomials

We list three useful properties of Hermite polynomials without proof here. See the appendix for complete proof.

Theorem 3.1

For real
\lambda
,
t
and
k^{th}
Hermite polynomials
H_{k}
,

\exp(\lambda t-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k!}}H_{k}(t)\lambda^{k}.

Theorem 3.2

Let
\gamma
be the Gaussian measure with density function
p(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\exp(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}})
. Then

\{H_{k}(\frac{t}{\sigma}):k\geq 0\}

is an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\gamma).

Theorem 3.3

Suppose that a Gaussian vector
(U,V)
satisfies

(U,V)\sim N\left(0,\left(\begin{matrix}\sigma_{11}&\sigma_{12}\\
\sigma_{12}&\sigma_{22}\end{matrix}\right)\right).

Then, for any positive integers
k,l\geq 0
,

\mathrm{E}(H_{k}(\frac{U}{\sqrt{\sigma_{11}}})H_{l}(\frac{V}{\sqrt{\sigma_{22}}}))=\delta_{k,l}(\frac{\sigma_{12}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{11}\sigma_{22}}})^{k}.

4
Main Theorem

Suppose that
\gamma
is a Gaussian measure with a fixed variance
\sigma
and
\{X_{n},n>0\}
is a centered Gaussian process such that each
X_{n}
obeys
X_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma)
in the rest of the article.

Lemma 4.1

Let
f,g
be two arbitrary functions in
L^{2}(\gamma)
with expectation 0. Then

||\widehat{P_{n}}(f)-\widehat{P_{n}}(g)||_{2}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}||f-g||_{2},

where
\Delta_{n}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}d_{ij}
and
d_{ij}=\frac{\sigma_{ij}}{\sigma}.

Proof.

Combining the definition of
\widehat{P_{n}}(f)
and Theorem
3.2
, we obtain

f(X_{i})-g(X_{i})=\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}H_{k}(\frac{X_{i}}{\sigma}),

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[\widehat{P_{n}}(f)-\widehat{P_{n}}(g)]^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathrm{E}[\sum_{k,l,i,j}c_{k}c_{l}H_{k}(\frac{X_{i}}{\sigma})H_{l}(\frac{X_{j}}{\sigma})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k,i,j}c_{k}^{2}d_{ij}^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Delta_{n}}{n^{2}}\sum_{k}c_{k}^{2}=\frac{\Delta_{n}}{n^{2}}||f-g||_{2}^{2}.

Now we are able to prove the following tail inequality.

Theorem 4.2

Suppose that
F
is a compact subset in
L^{2}(\gamma)
with entropy numbers
\{e_{n}\}
and
\{0\}=\pi_{0}\subset\pi_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\pi_{n}\subset\cdots
is a sequence of nets in
F
with
\rm{card}(\pi_{n})=J_{n}
. Assume that
\lim_{n\to\infty}d(f,\pi_{n})=0
holds for any
f\in F
. Then, we have

\mathrm{P}(\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)-E[\widehat{P_{n}}(f)]|\geq\lambda)\leq C_{1}^{2}(e,q)C_{2}(\pi,q)\frac{\Delta_{n}}{\lambda^{2}n^{2}},

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are two constants depending only on nets
\pi
, entropy numbers
e_{n}
and a non-decreasing sequence of positive numbers
q_{n}
taken arbitrarily.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we suppose
\mathrm{E}f=0
for any
f\in F
. Let
\pi_{n}(f)
denote the closest element to
f
in
\pi_{n}
. Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}d(f,\pi_{n})=0
, it is obvious that
\forall f\in F
, we have

f=\sum_{k\geq 0}(\pi_{k+1}(f)-\pi_{k}(f)).

Then,

|f|\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}|\pi_{k+1}(f)-\pi_{k}(f)|,

and

|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)|\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}|\widehat{P_{n}}(\pi_{k+1}(f))-\widehat{P_{n}}(\pi_{k}(f))|.

(1)

Lemma
4.1
implies

\mathrm{P}(|\widehat{P_{n}}(\pi_{k+1}(f))-\widehat{P_{n}}(\pi_{k}(f))|>\lambda)\leq\frac{\Delta_{n}||\pi_{k+1}(f)-\pi_{k}(f)||_{2}^{2}}{n^{2}\lambda^{2}}.

Applying variable substitution, we obtain

\mathrm{P}(|\widehat{P_{n}}(\pi_{k+1}(f))-\widehat{P_{n}}(\pi_{k}(f))|>\lambda q_{k}||\pi_{k+1}(f)-\pi_{k}(f)||_{2}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n})\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}q_{k}^{2}},

where
q_{k}
is a non-decreasing sequence of positive numbers taken arbitrarily. Then, we denote by
\Omega_{\lambda}
the set such that

\forall k\geq 0,\forall f\in F,|\widehat{P_{n}}(\pi_{k+1}(f))-\widehat{P_{n}}(\pi_{k}(f))|\leq\lambda q_{k}||\pi_{k+1}(f)-\pi_{k}(f)||_{2}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}

and observe

P(\Omega_{\lambda}^{c})\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{N_{k}}{\lambda^{2}q_{k}^{2}},

where
N_{k}
is the number of different pairs
(\pi_{k+1}(f),\pi_{k}(f))
while taking different
f
.

Here we define
C_{1},C_{2}
by

C_{1}(e,q):=2\sum_{k\geq 0}e_{j_{k}}(F)q_{k}

and

C_{2}(\pi,q):=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{N_{k}}{q_{k}^{2}}.

When
\Omega_{\lambda}
occurs, formula (
1
) implies

\forall f\in F,|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)|\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\lambda q_{k}||\pi_{k+1}(f)-\pi_{k}(f)||_{2}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}.

Thus,

\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)|\leq\sup_{f\in F}\sum_{k\geq 0}\lambda q_{k}||\pi_{k+1}(f)-\pi_{k}(f)||_{2}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n},

which implies

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}(\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)|\geq\sum_{k\geq 0}\lambda q_{k}\sup_{f\in F}||\pi_{k+1}(f)-\pi_{k}(f)||_{2}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{P}(\Omega_{\lambda}^{c})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}(\pi,q)}{\lambda^{2}}.

Further, since

\sum_{k\geq 0}\lambda q_{k}\sup_{f\in F}||\pi_{k+1}(f)-\pi_{k}(f)||_{2}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}\leq 2\sum_{k\geq 0}\lambda q_{k}\sup_{f\in F}||f-\pi_{k}(f)||_{2}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n},

by choosing
\pi_{n}
properly, we obtain

\mathrm{P}(\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)|\geq\lambda C_{1}(e,q)\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n})\leq\frac{C_{2}(\pi,q)}{\lambda^{2}}.

This implies the theorem.

Corollary 4.3

The size of the empirical process above satisfies

\mathrm{E}(\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)-\mathrm{E}[\widehat{P_{n}}(f)]|)\leq 2C_{1}\sqrt{C_{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}.

Proof.

Suppose
\forall f\in F,\mathrm{E}(f)=0
. Since
\mathrm{E}X=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{P}(X>u)du
holds for any variable
X\geq 0
, applying main theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)|)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{u}\mathrm{P}(\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)|>x)dx+\int_{u}^{\infty}C_{1}^{2}C_{2}\frac{\Delta_{n}}{x^{2}n^{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq u+C_{1}^{2}C_{2}\frac{\Delta_{n}}{un^{2}}

By taking
u
properly, we get

\mathrm{E}(\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)|)\leq 2C_{1}\sqrt{C_{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}.

Note that if
C_{1},C_{2}
are two finite numbers, we can dominate the size of
\widehat{P_{n}}(f)
by
O(\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n})
. However, even in the case of
F=\{1_{\{t\leq x\}}-\Phi(x):x\in\mathbb{R}\}
, we cannot take a sequence of
q_{k}
directly such that
C_{1},C_{2}
are convergent.

5
Upper Bound for Empirical Processes under General Dependence

In this section, we are going to prove that for the set of indicators, the size of the empirical process can still be dominated by
O(\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n})
, and we suppose
F
is the set of indicators in the rest of article.

Lemma 5.1

For any sufficiently small
\delta>0
, there exists a finite subset
F_{\delta}\in F
such that

\mathrm{E}(\sup_{f\in F_{\delta}}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)|)\leq C(\log\frac{1}{\delta})^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n},

and

\sup_{f\in F}d(f,F_{\delta})\leq 2\delta.

Proof.

Choose an integer
m
such that
\frac{1}{2^{m+1}}<\delta\leq\frac{1}{2^{m}}
and

F_{\delta}=\{1_{\{x\leq\alpha_{i}\}}-\frac{i}{2^{m}}:0\leq i\leq 2^{m}\}

where
\alpha_{i}
is a real number such that
\Phi(\alpha_{i})=\frac{i}{2^{m}}.
Choose a sequence of nets by

\{0\}=\pi_{0}\subset\pi_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\pi_{m}=\pi_{m+1}=\cdots

where

\pi_{k}(k\leq m)=\{1_{\{x\leq\alpha_{i2^{m-k}}\}}-\frac{i}{2^{k}}:0\leq i\leq 2^{k}\}.

It is obvious that
J_{k}(0<k\leq m)=1+2^{k}
and
J_{k}(k>m)=J_{m}
. As a result, we have

C_{2}=C(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{2^{k}}{q_{k}^{2}}+\sum_{k>m}\frac{2^{m}}{q_{k}^{2}})

since there is a constant such that
N_{k}\leq C2^{k}
for
k\leq m
. Similarly, we have

C_{1}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{q_{k}}{\sqrt{2^{k}}}.

By main theorem, since we can assign an arbitrarily huge value to
q_{k}
for
k>m
, we obtain

\mathrm{E}\sup_{f\in F_{\delta}}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)|\leq C\sqrt{(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{2^{k}}{q_{k}^{2}})}(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{q_{k}}{\sqrt{2^{k}}})\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}.

By choosing
q_{k}
properly, the inequality above implies

\mathrm{E}\sup_{f\in F_{\delta}}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)|\leq Cm^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}.

As
\frac{1}{2^{m}}\geq\delta
, we have

m\leq\log(\frac{1}{\delta}).

Lemma 5.2

For any sufficiently small
\delta>0
, the following inequality holds,

\mathrm{E}(\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)|)\leq C((\log\frac{1}{\delta})^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}+\delta).

Proof.

For convenience, we represent
\widehat{P_{n}}(f)
by
\widehat{P_{n}}(\alpha)
when
f=1_{\{t\leq\alpha\}}-\Phi(\alpha)
. Suppose

F_{\delta}=\{1_{\{x\leq\alpha_{i}\}}-\Phi(\alpha_{i}):0=\alpha_{0}<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\cdots\}

which is the subset of
F
defined in Lemma
5.1
. Define a new process by

\widehat{Q_{n}}(\alpha)=\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{\{t\leq\alpha_{i}\}}),\alpha_{i-1}\leq\alpha<\alpha_{i}.

Since

\widehat{P_{n}}(\alpha)=\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{\{t\leq\alpha\}})-\Phi(\alpha),

by the definition of
\alpha_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(\sup_{\alpha}|\widehat{Q_{n}}(\alpha)-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})|)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{E}(\sup_{\alpha}|\widehat{Q_{n}}(\alpha)-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha_{i}})|)+2\delta

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{E}(\sup_{\alpha_{i}}|\widehat{P_{n}}(\alpha_{i})|)+2\delta

\displaystyle\leq C((\log\frac{1}{\delta})^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}+\delta).

Further, we have

\mathrm{E}(\sup_{\alpha}|\widehat{Q_{n}}(\alpha)-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})|^{+})\leq C((\log\frac{1}{\delta})^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}+\delta)

and

\mathrm{E}(\sup_{\alpha}|\widehat{Q_{n}}(\alpha)-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})|^{-})\leq C((\log\frac{1}{\delta})^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}+\delta)

A useful observation is that

\widehat{Q_{n}}(\alpha-2\delta)\leq\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})\leq\widehat{Q_{n}}(\alpha+2\delta)

which implies

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(\sup_{\alpha}|\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha+2\delta})|^{+})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{E}(\sup_{\alpha}|\widehat{Q_{n}}(\alpha+2\delta)-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha+2\delta})|^{+})

\displaystyle\leq C((\log\frac{1}{\delta})^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}+\delta)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(\sup_{\alpha}|\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha-2\delta})|^{-})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{E}(\sup_{\alpha}|\widehat{Q_{n}}(\alpha-2\delta)-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha-2\delta})|^{-})

\displaystyle\leq C((\log\frac{1}{\delta})^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}+\delta)

By the definition of
\widehat{P_{n}}
, we have

\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha+2\delta})\leq\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})+2\delta

and

\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha-2\delta})\geq\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})-2\delta.

So,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(\sup_{\alpha}|\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})|^{+})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{E}\sup_{\alpha}(|\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha+2\delta})|+2\delta)^{+}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{E}\sup_{\alpha}(|\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha+2\delta})|^{+}+2\delta

\displaystyle\leq C((\log\frac{1}{\delta})^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}+\delta)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(\sup_{\alpha}|\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})|^{-})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{E}\sup_{\alpha}(|\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha-2\delta})|+2\delta)^{-}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{E}\sup_{\alpha}(|\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha-2\delta})|^{-}+2\delta

\displaystyle\leq C((\log\frac{1}{\delta})^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}+\delta).

Combining the two inequalities above, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(\sup_{\alpha}|\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})|)\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(\sup_{\alpha}|\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})|^{+})

\displaystyle+\mathrm{E}(\sup_{\alpha}|\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})-\mathrm{E}\widehat{P_{n}}(1_{t\leq\alpha})|^{-})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C((\log\frac{1}{\delta})^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}+\delta).

Theorem 5.3

For any
0<\varepsilon<1
, the size of the empirical process satisfies

\mathrm{E}(\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)|)\sim o((\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n})^{\varepsilon}).

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
, we have

\forall\delta>0,\mathrm{E}(\sup_{f\in F}|\widehat{P_{n}}(f)|)\leq C((\log\frac{1}{\delta})^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}+\delta).

(2)

By transforming the right-hand side of (
2
) to a better form, we need to estimate the order of

x^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}+e^{-x}.

Since there exists a constant
c(p)
for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
such that
e^{-x}\leq c(p)x^{-p}
, we obtain

x^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n}+e^{-x}\leq C(\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{n}}}{n})^{\frac{2p}{2p+3}}

by assigning a proper value to
x
. The proof is completed as
p
can be taken arbitrarily.

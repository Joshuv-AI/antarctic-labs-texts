The radius of comparison of the tensor product of a C*-algebra with C ⁡ ( X ) C(X)
Mohammad B. Asadi, M. Ali Asadi-Vasfi

Abstract
. Let X X be a compact metric space, let A A be a unital AH algebra with large matrix sizes, and let B B be a stably finite unital C*-algebra. Then we give a lower bound for the radius of comparison of C ⁡ ( X ) ⊗ B C(X)\otimes B and prove that the dimension-rank ratio satisfies drr ⁡ ( A ) = drr ⁡ ( C ⁡ ( X ) ⊗ A ) {\operatorname{drr}}(A)={\operatorname{drr}}\left(C(X)\otimes A\right) . We also give a class of unital AH algebras A A with rc ⁡ ( C ⁡ ( X ) ⊗ A ) = rc ⁡ ( A ) {\operatorname{rc}}\left(C(X)\otimes A\right)={\operatorname{rc}}(A) . We further give a class of stably finite exact 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} -stable unital C*-algebras with nonzero radius of comparison.

1.
Introduction

The radius of comparison of a C*-algebra, based on the Cuntz semigroup, and the dimension-rank ratio of an AH algebra
are numerical invariants
which were introduced
in
[
29
]
to study exotic examples of simple amenable
C*-algebras that are not
\mathcal{Z}
-stable. Sometimes there is a tight relationship between them. For example,
it was shown in Corollary 4.3 of
[
29
]
that

{\operatorname{rc}}(A)=0
if and only if
{\operatorname{drr}}(A)=0
whenever
A
is a simple infinite-dimensional real rank zero unital AH algebra.

The Cuntz semigroup plays a crucial role in the Elliott program for the classification of C*-algebras
[
7
,
12
,
30
]
.
See
[
1
,
8
,
28
]
for many aspects of the Cuntz semigroup.
It is generally complicated and large.
For simple nuclear C*-algebras,
the classifiable ones are those whose Cuntz semigroups
are easily understood (Section 5 of
[
1
]
).
With the near completion of the Elliott program,
nonclassifiable C*-algebras receive more attention (see
[
2
,
15
,
21
]
)
and the Cuntz semigroup is the main additional available invariant.

Let
X
be a compact metric space.
The covering
dimension of
X
is denoted by
\dim(X)
and
the cohomological dimension with
rational coefficients is denoted by

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
.
For many results about the Cuntz semigroup of
C(X)
when
\dim(X)\leq 3
,
we refer to the work of Robert and Tikusis
[
22
]
.
In the commutative setting, it is well known that the radius of comparison of
C(X)
is dominated by

\frac{1}{2}\dim(X)

[
5
,
11
]
.
For this reason, comparison theory can be viewed as a non-commutative dimension theory.
This fact and the main result of
[
27
]
are part of our motivation to wonder about an upper bound for
the radius of comparison of C*-algebras of the form
C(X,A)
,
where
A
is a unital C*-algebra.
The following conjecture is proposed by N. Christopher Phillips.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
A
be a stably finite unital C*-algebra and let
X
be a compact metric space. Then

(1.1)

{\operatorname{rc}}(A)\leq{\operatorname{rc}}\left(C(X)\otimes A\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\dim(X)+{\operatorname{rc}}(A)+1.

The purpose
of this paper is to give some preliminary results related to
Conjecture
1.1
.

In Section
3
, we prove that the left-hand side of (
1.1
)
is true for any stably finite unital C*-algebra
A
and any compact metric space
X
.
(See Proposition
3.4
.)
The right-hand side of (
1.1
) is also true for the case
A=M_{n}(C(Y))
for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}

with technical hypotheses on

X
and
Y
. (See Proposition
3.5
.)
But there are some difficulties to prove it
for a general C*-algebra
A
and a metric space
X
.
So we decide to show that
Conjecture
1.1
is valid at least for some choices of
A
and
X
.

As an important special case, if
A
is a residually stably finite
\mathcal{Z}
-stable unital C*-algebra,
then it is shown in Proposition
3.9
that
{\operatorname{rc}}(C(X)\otimes A)=0
.
To prove this, we apply Proposition 3.2.4(ii) of
[
5
]
.
Therefore,
finiteness and residual stable finiteness of
C(X,A)
is one of the
starting points.

Another special case comes when
\dim(X)=0
.
(See Corollary
3.8
and the discussion after it.)
To deal with this, we apply Proposition
3.6
which states a
connection between Cuntz comparison over
C(X,A)
and
\dim(X)
.

It is definitely known that
{\operatorname{rc}}(A)=0
for a stably finite exact simple
\mathcal{Z}
-stable unital C*-algebra
A
.
(See Corollary 4.6 of
[
24
]
.)
One might naively expect that
{\operatorname{rc}}(A)=0
for all stably finite exact (not necessarily simple)

\mathcal{Z}
-stable unital C*-algebras.
To show that simplicity of
A
is necessary,
we exhibit a class of stably finite exact
\mathcal{Z}
-stable unital C*-algebras with nonzero radius of comparison.
(See Remark
4.5
.)

In Section
5
, we prove that if

X
is a compact metric space and
A
is
a unital AH algebra with large matrix sizes, then
the dimension-rank ratios of
A
and
C(X,A)

are related by

{\operatorname{drr}}(A)={\operatorname{drr}}\left(C(X)\otimes A)\right).

(See Proposition
5.3
.)
There is an example in which

A
doesn’t have large matrix sizes and the equality fails. We further give a class of AH algebras
A
with

{\operatorname{rc}}\left(C(X)\otimes A)\right)={\operatorname{rc}}(A)
.
So, (
1.1
) is also true if
A
is chosen from this class of AH algebras.

Along the way, we show the radius of comparison of a unital AH
algebra is dominated by one half of its dimension-rank ratio.
(See Lemma
5.1
.) This result is known, but we have
not found it in the literature.

At the end, we give many examples of
infinite-dimensional stably finite simple unital C*-algebras
A
for which
the radii of comparison of
A
and
C(X)\otimes A
are the same.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section,
we gather for easy reference
some information on the Cuntz semigroup, the dimension-rank ratio of AH algebras,
and the radius of comparison.

Notation 2.1
.

We use the following standard notation.
If
A
is a C*-algebra, or if
A=M_{\infty}(B)

for a C*-algebra
B
, we write
A_{+}
for the
set of positive elements of
A
.
The unitization of a C*-algebra
A
is denoted by
A^{+}
.
(We add a new unit even if
A
is already unital.)
We let
{\mathcal{K}}
denote the algebra of compact operators on a separable
and infinite-dimensional Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
.

2.1.
The Cuntz semigroup

Let
A
be a C*-algebra.
For
a,b\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
,
we say that
a
is
Cuntz subequivalent to
b
in
A
,
written
a\precsim_{A}b
,
if there is a sequence
(c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
M_{\infty}(A)

such that

\lim_{{n}\to\infty}c_{n}bc_{n}^{*}=a.

We say that
a
and
b
are
Cuntz equivalent in
A
,
written
a\sim_{A}b
,
if
a\precsim_{A}b
and
b\precsim_{A}a
.
This relation is an equivalence relation,
and we write
\langle a\rangle_{A}
for the equivalence class of
a
.
We define
{\operatorname{W}}(A)=M_{\infty}(A)_{+}/\sim_{A}
,
together with the commutative semigroup operation

\langle a\rangle_{A}+\langle b\rangle_{A}=\langle a\oplus b\rangle_{A}

and the partial order

\langle a\rangle_{A}\leq\langle b\rangle_{A}

if
a\precsim_{A}b
.
We write
0
for
\langle 0\rangle_{A}
.
We also define
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)={\operatorname{W}}(A\otimes{\mathcal{K}})
.

The common notation for Cuntz subequivalence is
a\precsim b
and it is originally from
[
9
]
.
We include
A
in the notation
because we want notation for the Cuntz subequivalence
with respect to C*-algebras.

Part (
1
) of the following
is taken from Proposition 2.4 of
[
23
]
.
Part (
2
) is Lemma 2.5(ii) of
[
16
]
.
Part (
3
) is Lemma 1.5 of
[
20
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra.

(1)

Let
a,b\in A_{+}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

a\precsim_{A}b
.

(b)

(a-\varepsilon)_{+}\precsim_{A}b
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

(c)

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that

(a-\varepsilon)_{+}\precsim_{A}(b-\delta)_{+}
.

(2)

Let
a,b\in A_{+}
and let
\varepsilon>0
.
If
\|a-b\|<\varepsilon
, then

(a-\varepsilon)_{+}\precsim_{A}b
.

(3)

Let
a,b\in A_{+}
and let
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0
. Then

((a+b)-(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}))_{+}\sim_{A}(a-\varepsilon_{1})_{+}+(b-\varepsilon_{2})_{+}\precsim_{A}(a-\varepsilon_{1})_{+}\oplus(b-\varepsilon_{2})_{+}.

2.2.
The dimension-rank ratio of an AH algebra

Part (
1
) and Part (
2
)
of the following definition are taken from Definition 1.1 of
[
29
]
.
Part (
3
) is defined by us to simplify notation.
What we call strictly homogeneous here was called homogeneous in

[
10
,
29
]
.
We want to avoid conflict with Definition IV.1.4.1 of
[
3
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

A C*-algebra is said to be
strictly homogeneous
if it is isomorphic to

p\big(C(X)\otimes{\mathcal{K}}\big)p
for a compact Hausdorff space
X
and
a projection of constant rank
p\in C(X)\otimes{\mathcal{K}}
.
A
strictly semihomogeneous
C*-algebra is
a finite direct sum of strictly homogeneous C*-algebras.

(1)

An
approximately homogeneous
(AH) C*-algebra
is a direct limit

A=\varinjlim(A_{j},\psi_{j})

where
A_{j}
is strictly semihomogeneous for each
j
.

(2)

Let
A=\varinjlim(A_{j},\psi_{j})

be a unital (i.e., both
A_{j}
and

\psi_{j}\colon A_{j}\to A_{j+1}

are unital for every
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
)
AH algebra, where

A_{j}=\bigoplus_{l=1}^{m_{j}}p_{j,l}\big(C(X_{j,l})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}\big)p_{j,l}

for compact Hausdorff spaces
X_{j,l}
, projections

p_{j,l}\in C(X_{j,l})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}
,
and natural numbers
m_{j}
. Define

\psi_{jk}=\psi_{j-1}\circ\psi_{j-2}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{k}
,
and write
\psi_{j\infty}\colon A_{j}\to A

for the canonical map. We consider
this collection of objects and maps as
a decomposition for
A
. If
\psi_{j}
is injective for every
j
,
then we describe this collection as an injective
decomposition.

(3)

If a unital AH algebra
A
admits a decomposition as in (
2
) for which

\lim_{j\to\infty}\min_{1\leq l\leq m_{j}}\big({\operatorname{rank}}(p_{j,l})\big)=\infty,

then we say that
A

has
large matrix sizes
.

Definition 2.4

(Definition 2.1 of
[
29
]
)
.

Let
A
be a unital AH algebra.
The
dimension-rank ratio
of
A
, denoted
{\operatorname{drr}}(A)
,
is the infimum of the set of strictly positive reals

r
such that
A
has a decomposition which satisfies

\limsup\limits_{j\to\infty}\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{j}}\left(\frac{\dim(X_{j,l})}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{j,l})}\right)=r,

whenever this set is not empty, and
\infty
otherwise.

Part (
1
), Part (
2
), and Part (
3
) of
the following lemma are parts of Proposition 2.2 of
[
29
]
and Part (
4
) is
Corollary 2.4 of
[
29
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A
and
B
be unital AH algebras and let
I
a closed ideal of
A
. Then:

(1)

{\operatorname{drr}}(A/I)\leq{\operatorname{drr}}(A)
.

(2)

{\operatorname{drr}}(A\oplus B)=\max\big({\operatorname{drr}}(A),{\operatorname{drr}}(B)\big)
.

(3)

{\operatorname{drr}}(A\otimes M_{k})\leq\frac{1}{k}{\operatorname{drr}}(A)
for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.

(4)

Let
A=\varinjlim(A_{j},\varphi_{j})
be a unital AH algebra where each
A_{j}
is semihomogeneous.
Then

{\operatorname{drr}}(A)\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}{\operatorname{drr}}(A_{j})
.

2.3.
Radius of comparison

The set of normalized 2-quasitraces on a unital C*-algebra
A
is denoted by
{\operatorname{QT}}(A)
.
By the discussion after Proposition II.4.6 of
[
4
]
,
we have
{\operatorname{QT}}(A)\neq\varnothing

for every stably finite unital C*-algebra
A
.
See
[
4
]
for more details about 2-quasitraces.

Definition 2.6

(Definition 12.1.7 of
[
13
]
)
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra,
and let
\tau\in{\operatorname{QT}}(A)
.
Define
d_{\tau}\colon M_{\infty}(A)_{+}\to[0,\infty)

by

d_{\tau}(a)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau(a^{1/n})
.
We also use the same notation for the corresponding functions
on
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
and
{\operatorname{W}}(A)
.

The following is Definition 6.1 of
[
29
]
,
except that we allow
r=0
in (
1
).
This change makes no difference.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
A
be a stably finite unital C*-algebra.

(1)

Let
r\in[0,\infty)
.
We say that
A
has
r
-comparison
if whenever

a,b\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}
satisfy

d_{\tau}(a)+r<d_{\tau}(b)

for all
\tau\in{\operatorname{QT}}(A)
,
then
a\precsim_{A}b
.

(2)

The
radius of comparison
of
A
, denoted

{\operatorname{rc}}(A)
, is

{\operatorname{rc}}(A)=\inf\big(\big\{r\in[0,\infty)\colon{\mbox{$A$ has $r$-comparison}}\big\}\big)

if it exists, and
\infty
otherwise.

If
A
is simple,
then the infimum in Definition
2.7
(
2
)
is attained,
that is,
A
has
{\operatorname{rc}}(A)
-comparison.
(See Proposition 6.3 of
[
29
]
.)

Theorem 2.8

(Corollary 1.2 of
[
11
]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact metrizable space with
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)=\dim(X)
. Then:

(1)

If
\dim(X)
is even, then

\frac{\dim(X)}{2}-2\leq{\operatorname{rc}}\big(C(X)\big)\leq\max\Big(0,\frac{\dim(X)}{2}-1\Big)
.

(2)

If
\dim(X)
is odd, then

{\operatorname{rc}}\big(C(X)\big)=\max\Big(0,\frac{\dim(X)-1}{2}-1\Big)
.

2.4.
Functionals

Let
\Omega
be a semigroup in the category
\mathbf{Cu}

which is given in Denfinition 4.1 of
[
1
]
. A functional
on
\Omega
is a map
\lambda\colon\Omega\to[0,\infty]
which is order preserving, additive, preserves suprema of
increasing sequences and satisfies
\lambda(0)=0
. We denote the set of functionals on
\Omega
by
{\operatorname{F}}(\Omega)
.
Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra. The radius of comparison of
({\operatorname{Cu}}(A)\,,\langle 1_{A}\rangle)
, denoted
r_{A}
, is
the infimum of the set of real numbers
r
in
[0,\infty)
such that, if whenever
a,b\in M_{\infty}(A)_{+}

satisfy
\lambda(\langle a\rangle_{A})+r\lambda(\langle 1_{A}\rangle_{A})\leq\lambda(\langle b\rangle_{A})
for all
\lambda\in{\operatorname{F}}({\operatorname{Cu}}(A))
,
then
a\precsim_{A}b
.

Since we allow
functionals on
{\operatorname{Cu}}(A)
which take the value
\infty
at the unit, it follows that

r_{A}\leq{\operatorname{rc}}(A)
. For sufficiently finite C*-algebras, we have
r_{A}={\operatorname{rc}}(A)
.

Let

3.
An approach to the radius of comparison of
C(X)\otimes A

In this section, we obtain a lower bound for the radius of comparison of
C(X)\otimes A

when
A
is any unital stably finite C*-algebra and
X
is any compact metric space.

We further discuss the radius of comparison of
C(X)\otimes A
when we have stronger assumptions on
A
or
X
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra. We denote by
{\operatorname{Prim}}(A)
the set of primitive ideals of
A
.
See Section II.6.5 of
[
3
]
, Section 5.4 of
[
18
]
, and
[
19
]
for more details about primitive ideals of a C*-algebra.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space, let
A
be a C*-algebra,
and let
J
be a proper closed ideal of
C(X,A)
.
Then there is a closed subset
F\subseteq X\times{\operatorname{Prim}}(A)
such that

J=\big\{f\in C(X,A)\colon f(x)\in I\text{ for all }(x,I)\in F\big\}.

Proof.

The result is essentially immediate from
Theorem 5.4.3 of
[
18
]
, Proposition 2.16 of
[
6
]
and Theorem II.2.2.4 of
[
3
]
.
∎

The first part of the following lemma is needed to apply Definition
2.7

and the second part is needed to prepare for Proposition
3.9
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and let
A
be a unital C*-algebra.
Then:

(1)

If
A
is stably finite, then
C(X,A)
is also stably finite.

(2)

If
A
is residually stably finite, then
C(X,A)
is also residually stably finite.

Proof.

We prove (
1
).
Since
C(X,A)
is unital, we apply Lemma 5.1.2 of
[
25
]
.
Let
f\in C(X,A)
satisfy
f^{*}f=1_{C(X,A)}
.
So,
f(x)^{*}f(x)=1_{A}
for all
x\in A
.
Since
A
is finite, it follows that
ff^{*}=1_{C(X,A)}
.
A similar argument works if
f\in M_{n}(C(X,A))
for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.

To prove (
2
), we must show that all quotients of
C(X,A)
are stably finite.
Let
J
be a proper closed ideal of
C(X,A)
.
Then, by Lemma
3.1
, there is a closed subset
F\subseteq X\times{\operatorname{Prim}}(A)
such that

J=\big\{f\in C(X,A)\colon f(x)\in I\text{ for all }(x,I)\in F\big\}.

Since
M_{n}\big(C(X,A)/J\big)\cong C(X,M_{n}(A))/M_{n}(J)
for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
,
it suffices to show that
C(X,A)/J
is finite.
Let
f\in C(X,A)
satisfy
f^{*}f+J=1_{C(X,A)}+J
. Then
f^{*}f-1_{C(X,A)}\in J
.
Thus
f(x)^{*}f(x)-1_{A}\in I
for all
(x,I)\in F
.
Since
A/I
is finite for all
(x,I)\in F
, it follows that

f(x)f(x)^{*}-1_{A}\in I
for all
(x,I)\in F
.
This relation implies that
ff^{*}-1_{C(X,A)}\in J
and therefore
ff^{*}+J=1_{C(X,A)}+J
.
∎

By Lemma
3.2
(
1
) and the discussion after Proposition II.4.6 of

,
we have
{\operatorname{QT}}(C(X)\otimes A)\neq\varnothing

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and
A
be a unital C*-algebra. Let
also
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
and let
a,b\in M_{l}(A)_{+}
. Then
1_{C(X)}\otimes a\precsim_{C(X)\otimes A}1_{C(X)}\otimes b
if and only if

a\precsim_{A}b
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
l=1
. Set
B=C(X)\otimes A
.

To show the backward implication,
assume
a\precsim_{A}b
.
Define a homomorphism
\varphi\colon A\to B
by
\varphi(c)=1_{C(X)}\otimes c
.
Using this and
a\precsim_{A}b
, we get
1_{C(X)}\otimes a\precsim_{B}1_{C(X)}\otimes b
.

To show the forward implication, assume
a\otimes 1_{C(X)}\precsim_{B}b\otimes 1_{C(X)}
.
It suffices to find a sequence
(v_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}
in
A
with

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}bv^{*}_{n}-a\|=0
.

Identifying
C(X)\otimes A
with
C(X,A)
in the standard way, we get

a\precsim_{C(X,A)}b
.
This relation implies that there exists a sequence
(w_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in

C(X,A)
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|w_{n}bw^{*}_{n}-a\|=0.

Now fix some
x_{0}\in X
and define
v_{n}=w_{n}(x_{0})
for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
∎

The following proposition is essentially immediate from Proposition

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and
let

\displaystyle v\widetilde{b}v^{*}

4.1.
Cones over C*-algebras

Let
A
be a C*-algebra. The

cone
over
A
, denoted
{\operatorname{C}}A
, is the set of
continuous functions
f\colon[0,1]\to A
with
f(0)=0
.
Clearly
{\operatorname{C}}A\cong C_{0}((0,1])\otimes A
.
If
A
is unital,
({\operatorname{C}}A)^{+}
is isomorphic to the
set of continuous functions
f\colon[0,1]\to A
such that
f(0)\in\mathbb{C}1_{A}
.
For a nonunital C*-algebra
A
we say that
A
is stably finite
if
A^{+}
is stably finite.
Then the cone over any C*-algebra is stably finite but often fails to be
residually stably finite.
In general, we do not know whether
the tensor product of two (non-exact) stably finite simple C*-algebras is stably finite or not.
But we have the following nice lemma
for the case that one tensor factor is the unitization of the cone over a unital C*-algebra.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra and let
B
be a stably finite unital C*-algebra.
Then
({\operatorname{C}}A)^{+}\otimes B
is stably finite for any choice of tensor product.

Proof.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
and let
f\in({\operatorname{C}}A)^{+}\otimes M_{k}(B)
.
Suppose
f^{*}f=1_{({\operatorname{C}}A)^{+}}\otimes 1_{M_{k}(B)}

ff^{*}=1_{({\operatorname{C}}A)^{+}}\otimes 1_{M_{k}(B)}
.
We may identify
f
as a continuous function
f\colon[0,1]\to A\otimes M_{k}(B)

with
f(0)=1_{A}\otimes b
for some
b\in M_{k}(B)
.
So, it suffices to show that
f(x)f(x)^{*}=1_{A}\otimes 1_{M_{k}(B)}
for all
x\in[0,1]
.

We note that

1_{A}\otimes(b^{*}b)=f(0)^{*}f(0)=1_{A}\otimes 1_{M_{k}(B)}.

This relation implies that
b^{*}b=1_{M_{k}(B)}
. Since
B
is stably finite, it follows that

bb^{*}=1_{M_{k}(B)}
. Therefore

f(0)f(0)^{*}=1_{A}\otimes(bb^{*})=1_{A}\otimes 1_{M_{k}(B)}.

Now define a uniformly continuous map
\Delta\colon[0,1]\to A\otimes M_{k}(B)
by
\Delta(x)=f(x)f(x)^{*}
.
Clearly

and

Choose

.
Choose

and

),
we get, for all
\theta\in{\operatorname{T}}\big(({\operatorname{C}}A)^{+}\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}B\big)
,

d_{\theta}(\widetilde{f})=d_{\rho}(f)=1\quad\text{and}\quad d_{\theta}(\widetilde{g})=d_{\rho}(g)=n.

Then a similar calculation gives us a similar contradiction as in the first part.
∎

We refer to Section 4 of
[
16
]
for the definition of a purely infinite C*-algebra and its properties.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
A
be a purely infinite exact simple unital C*-algebra and

B
be a stably finite exact unital C*-algebra. Then

{\operatorname{rc}}\big(({\operatorname{C}}A)^{+}\big)={\operatorname{rc}}\big(({\operatorname{C}}A)^{+}\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}B\big)=\infty.

Remark 4.5
.

Applying Corollary
4.4
with
\mathcal{Z}
in place of
B
, we get

{\operatorname{rc}}(({\operatorname{C}}A)^{+}\otimes\mathcal{Z})=\infty
. More generally,
if the C*-algebra
B
in Corollary
4.4
is also
\mathcal{Z}
-stable,
then we can get a class of stably finite exact
\mathcal{Z}
-stable unital C*-algebras with nonzero radius of comparison.

5.
A class of AH algebras with particular radius of comparison

In this section, we give a class of AH algebras satisfying Conjecture
1.1
.
As preparation, we prove that
{\operatorname{drr}}(A)={\operatorname{drr}}\big(C(X)\otimes A\big)

for any unital AH algebra
A
with large matrix sizes.

The dimension-rank ratio of an AH algebra is related to its radius of comparison by the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
A

be a unital AH algebra.
Then

{\operatorname{rc}}(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(A)
.

Proof.

We may assume
{\operatorname{drr}}(A)<\infty
.
Suppose
\varinjlim A_{k}
is an arbitrary decomposition for
A
, where

A_{k}=\bigoplus_{l=1}^{m_{k}}p_{k,l}\big(C(X_{k,l})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}\big)p_{k,l}

for compact Hausdorff spaces
X_{k,l}
, projections

p_{k,l}\in C(X_{k,l})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}
,
and
m_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
. Set

(5.1)

r=\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{k}}\left(\frac{\dim(X_{k,l})}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{k,l})}\right)

which is a nonnegative real number or
\infty
.
Using Proposition 6.2(i) of
[
29
]
at the first step and
Remark
2.12
at the second step, we get, for every
k
,

(5.2)

{\operatorname{rc}}(A_{k})=\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{k}}{\operatorname{rc}}\left(p_{k,l}\big(C(X_{k,l})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}\big)p_{k,l}\right)\leq\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{k}}\left(\frac{\dim(X_{k,l})}{2\,{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{k,l})}\right).

It follows easily from the discussion after Definition V.2.1.9 of
[
3
]
that
AH algebras are residually stably finite.
Therefore, using Proposition
2.11
(
2
) at the first step,
using (
5.2
) at the second step,
and using (
5.1
) at the last step,

\displaystyle{\operatorname{rc}}(A)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}{\operatorname{rc}}(A_{j})\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{j}}\left(\frac{\dim(X_{j,l})}{2\,{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{j,l})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\limsup_{j\to\infty}\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{j}}\left(\frac{\dim(X_{j,l})}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{j,l})}\right)=\frac{r}{2}.

Using this and taking infimum over all
r
with

r=\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{k}}\left(\frac{\dim(X_{k,l})}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{k,l})}\right)
, we get

{\operatorname{rc}}(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(A).

∎

There is an example in which

A
doesn’t have large matrix sizes and the equality

{\operatorname{rc}}(A)=\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(A)

fails. For example, it is enough to take

A=C([0,1]^{6})
.

We now give a more precise statement of Proposition 6.8 of
[
29
]
.

Corollary 5.2
.

For every
r\in[0,\infty)
, there exists a simple unital AH algebra such that
{\operatorname{rc}}(A)=\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(A)=r
.

Proof.

Combine Proposition 6.8 of
[
29
]
and Lemma
5.1
.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
A

be a unital AH algebra with large matrix sizes and let

X
be a compact metric space.
Then

{\operatorname{drr}}(A)={\operatorname{drr}}\big(C(X)\otimes A\big)
.

Proof.

First we show

{\operatorname{drr}}\big(C(X)\otimes A\big)\leq{\operatorname{drr}}(A)
.
We may assume
{\operatorname{drr}}(A)<\infty
and
X=\varprojlim X_{j}
with

\dim(X_{j})<\infty
for all
j
(by Corollary 5.2.6 of
[
26
]
).
Suppose
\varinjlim A_{k}
is an arbitrary decomposition for
A
, where

A_{k}=\bigoplus_{l=1}^{m_{k}}p_{k,l}\big(C(Y_{k,l})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}\big)p_{k,l}

for compact Hausdorff spaces
Y_{k,l}
, projections

p_{k,l}\in C(Y_{k,l})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}
,
and
m_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
. Set

(5.3)

r=\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{k}}\left(\frac{\dim(Y_{k,l})}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{k,l})}\right)

which is a nonnegative real number or
\infty
.
Choose a strictly increasing sequence
r(j)
such that, for all
j
,

(5.4)

\min_{1\leq l\leq m_{r(j)}}\left({\operatorname{rank}}(p_{r(j),l})\right)\geq j\dim(X_{j}).

Clearly,
C(X)\otimes A\cong\varinjlim C(X_{j})\otimes A_{r(j)}
.
Using Lemma
2.5
(
2
) at the second step,
using Remark
2.12
at the third step,
and using

\dim\big(X_{j}\times Y_{r(j),l}\big)\leq\dim(Y_{r(j),l})+\dim(X_{j}),

the fourth step,
we get, for every
j
,

\displaystyle{\operatorname{drr}}\left(C(X_{j})\otimes A_{r(j)}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt{\mbox{}}={\operatorname{drr}}\left(\bigoplus_{l=1}^{m_{r(j)}}(1_{C(X_{j})}\otimes p_{r(j),l})\big(C(X_{j}\times Y_{r(j),l})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}\big)(1_{C(X_{j})}\otimes p_{r(j),l})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt{\mbox{}}\leq\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{r(j)}}\left({\operatorname{drr}}\Big((1_{C(X_{j})}\otimes p_{r(j),l})\left(C(X_{j}\times Y_{r(j),l})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}\right)(1_{C(X_{j})}\otimes p_{r(j),l})\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt{\mbox{}}\leq\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{r(j)}}\left(\frac{\dim\left(X_{j}\times Y_{r(j),l}\right)}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{r(j),l})}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt{\mbox{}}\leq\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{r(j)}}\left(\frac{\dim(X_{j})+\dim(Y_{r(j),l})}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{r(j),l})}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt{\mbox{}}\leq\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{r(j)}}\left(\frac{\dim(X_{j})}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{r(j),l})}\right)+\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{r(j)}}\left(\frac{\dim(Y_{r(j),l})}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{r(j),l})}\right).

Therefore, using this relation at the second step,
using Lemma
2.5
(
4
) at the first step,
and (
5.4
) at the fourth step,

\displaystyle{\operatorname{drr}}\big(C(X)\otimes A\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt{\mbox{}}\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}{\operatorname{drr}}\left(C(X_{j})\otimes A_{r(j)}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt{\mbox{}}\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\left(\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{r(j)}}\left(\frac{\dim(X_{j})}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{r(j),l})}\right)+\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{r(j)}}\left(\frac{\dim(Y_{r(j),l})}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{r(j),l})}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt{\mbox{}}\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\left(\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{r(j)}}\left(\frac{\dim(X_{j})}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{r(j),l})}\right)\right)+\limsup_{j\to\infty}\left(\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{r(j)}}\left(\frac{\dim(Y_{r(j),l})}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{r(j),l})}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt{\mbox{}}\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{j}+\limsup_{j\to\infty}\left(\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{r(j)}}\left(\frac{\dim(Y_{r(j),l})}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{r(j),l})}\right)\right)=r.

Using this and taking the infimum over all
r
with

r=\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\max_{1\leq l\leq m_{k}}\left(\frac{\dim(Y_{k,l})}{{\operatorname{rank}}(p_{k,l})}\right)
, we get

{\operatorname{drr}}\big(C(X)\otimes A\big)\leq{\operatorname{drr}}(A).

To show

{\operatorname{drr}}(A)\leq{\operatorname{drr}}\big(C(X)\otimes A\big)
,
let
x_{0}\in X
. Define a surjective homomorphism

\varphi\colon C(X,A)\to A

by
\varphi(f)=f(x_{0})
.
Using this at the first step and using Lemma
2.5
(
1
) at the second step,
we get

{\operatorname{drr}}(A)={\operatorname{drr}}\left(C(X,A)/\textrm{ker}\varphi\right)\leq{\operatorname{drr}}\left(C(X)\otimes A\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

There is an example in which

A
doesn’t have large matrix sizes and the equality

{\operatorname{drr}}(A)={\operatorname{drr}}\big(C(X)\otimes A\big)
fails. For example, it is enough to take
A=C([0,1])
and
X=[0,1]
.

As we promised, we introduce a class of AH algebras
A

with
{\operatorname{rc}}(C(X)\otimes A)={\operatorname{rc}}(A)
in the following theorem.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
A

be a unital AH algebra with large matrix sizes and let
X
be a compact metric space.
Suppose
{\operatorname{rc}}(A)=\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(A)
.
Then:

(1)

{\operatorname{rc}}(C(X)\otimes A)=\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(C(X)\otimes A)
.

(2)

{\operatorname{rc}}(C(X)\otimes A)={\operatorname{rc}}(A)
.

Proof.

We prove (
1
). Using Proposition
5.3
at the first step,
using Proposition
3.4
at the third step, and using Lemma
5.1
at the last step,
we get

\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(C(X)\otimes A)=\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(A)={\operatorname{rc}}(A)\leq{\operatorname{rc}}(C(X)\otimes A)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(C(X)\otimes A).

This relation implies
{\operatorname{rc}}(C(X)\otimes A)=\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(C(X)\otimes A)
.

We prove (
2
). Using Part (
1
) at the first step and
using Proposition
5.3
at the second step, we get

{\operatorname{rc}}(C(X)\otimes A)=\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(C(X)\otimes A)=\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(A)={\operatorname{rc}}(A).

This completes the proof.
∎

One should ask whether there exists an AH algebra satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
5.4
.
To see that there is such an AH algebra,
we give a simple unital AH algebra
A

with large matrix sizes, stable rank one, nonzero radius of comparison, and

{\operatorname{rc}}(A)=\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(A)
.
The example was recently constructed in Section 6 of
[
2
]
.

Example 5.5
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and
let
A=\varinjlim A_{n}
be the AH algebra which was constructed in Section 6 of
[
2
]
.
Adopt the assumptions and notation of Section 6 of
[
2
]
.
Then:

{\operatorname{rc}}(A)=\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(A),\quad{\operatorname{rc}}(C(X)\otimes A)=\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{drr}}(C(X)\otimes A),\quad\text{and}\quad{\operatorname{rc}}(C(X)\otimes A)={\operatorname{rc}}(A).

By Lemma
5.1
and Theorem
5.4
,
it suffices to show
{\operatorname{drr}}(A)\leq 2\,{\operatorname{rc}}(A)
.
Using Lemma
2.5
(
4
) at the first step,
using Lemma
2.5
(
2
) and
Remark
2.12
at the third step, and using Theorem 6.15 of
[
2
]
at the last step,
we get

\displaystyle{\operatorname{drr}}(A)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\operatorname{drr}}(A_{n})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\operatorname{drr}}\left(M_{r(n)}\left(C(X_{n})\oplus C(X_{n})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim(X_{n})}{r(n)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2s(n)}{r(n)}=2\,{\operatorname{rc}}(A),

as desired.

Corollary
5.2
provides more examples
to feed into Theorem
5.4
.

Corollary 5.6
.

For every
r\in[0,\infty)
and for every compact metric space
X
, there exists a simple unital AH algebra such that

{\operatorname{rc}}(C(X)\otimes A)={\operatorname{rc}}(A)=r
.

M. H. Cunningham, A. Saxena, R. S. Ellis, L. Pentericci

Abstract
The velocity offset of Ly α \alpha emission from a galaxy’s systemic redshift is an excellent tracer of conditions that enable the escape of Ly α \alpha photons from the galaxy, and potentially the all-important hydrogen ionizing Lyman continuum photons. However at z ≥ 6 z\geq 6 , Ly α \alpha is often heavily attenuated by the neutral intergalactic medium. Here we investigate the utility of C iii ] λ ​ λ ​ 1907 , 1909 \lambda\lambda 1907,1909 emission, usually the brightest UV line after Ly α \alpha , as a proxy estimating the Ly α \alpha velocity offset ( Δ ​ v Ly ​ α \Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha} ). To do so, we use analogues of reionization era galaxies based upon 52 star-forming galaxies with robust C iii ] detections drawn from the VANDELS survey. Our sample spans a broad UV magnitude range of − 18.5 < M UV < − 22.0 -18.5<M_{\rm{UV}}<-22.0 , with a sample average value of EW(C iii ]) = 5.3 =5.3 Å. We find a slight increase of EW(C iii ]) with increasing EW(Ly α \alpha ), but find a large range of EW(C iii ]) ∼ \sim 1-13 Å particularly at EW(Ly α \alpha ) < 10 <10 Å. Using the C iii ] line peak as the systemic redshift, we calculate Δ ​ v Ly ​ α \Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha} and recover the previously reported trend of decreasing Δ ​ v Ly ​ α \Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha} with increasing EW(Ly α \alpha ). Interestingly, we find an anti-correlation between Δ ​ v Ly ​ α \Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha} and EW(C iii ]), which also displays a dependence on the UV absolute magnitude. We derive a multi-variate fit to obtain Δ ​ v Ly ​ α \Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha} using both EW(C iii ]) and M UV M_{\rm{UV}} , finding that Δ ​ v Ly ​ α \Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha} is more strongly dependent on EW(C iii ]), with a weaker but non-negligible dependence on M UV M_{\rm{UV}} . We find that for a fixed EW(C iii ]), UV-bright Ly α \alpha emitting galaxies show smaller values of Δ ​ v Ly ​ α \Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha} ,which suggests that such galaxies may be undergoing more bursty star-formation compared to the UV-fainter ones, akin to a population of extremely UV-bright galaxies identified at z > 10 z>10 .

Introduction

A largely uncharted period in the history of the Universe is the Epoch of Reionization (EoR), which began with the emergence of the first stars, black holes and galaxies after the Big Bang. It is widely believed that ultraviolet radiation from early sources governed the reionization of the intergalactic medium (IGM), which ended at a redshift
z\sim 5.5-6
,
(
Fan et al. 2006
;
Becker & Bolton 2013
;
McGreer et al. 2015
;
Salvador-Solé et al. 2017
;
Bosman et al. 2022
)
.

The duration of the EoR can be constrained by, amongst other probes, measures of the scattering and polarisation of the cosmic microwave background (CMB)
(
Haiman & Knox 1999
;
Zahn et al. 2012
;
Bennett et al. 2013
;
Planck Collaboration et al. 2016
;
Choudhury et al. 2021
)
, the opacity of hydrogen absorption in the spectra of distant quasars
(
Erb et al. 2010
;
Stark et al. 2014
;
Rigby et al. 2015
;
Weisz & Boylan-Kolchin 2017
;
Garaldi et al. 2019
;
Bosman et al. 2022
)
, and the visibility or otherwise of Lyman-
\alpha
(Ly
\alpha
) emission in star-forming galaxies (SFGs) since this is resonantly scattered by neutral gas
(
Pentericci et al. 2011
;
Pritchard & Loeb 2012
;
Jones et al. 2023
;
Trapp et al. 2023
)
. Constraining the sources responsible for reionization requires knowing their abundances and ionising capabilities.

With
JWST
, at least constraining the average production rate of ionising photons in galaxies at
z>6
is now within reach
(
Cameron et al. 2023
;
Tang et al. 2023
;
Katz et al. 2023
;
Curti et al. 2023
;
Saxena et al. 2023b
)
. However, as the neutral fraction of the IGM increases, it becomes impractical to directly observe the escaping ionising photons from galaxies beyond
z\simeq 4

(
Inoue et al. 2014
, e.g.)
. Direct measures of the leakage of Lyman continuum (LyC) photons is only possible in lower redshift analogues, where such observations can be linked to other observables
(
Fletcher et al. 2019
;
Izotov et al. 2021
;
Saxena et al. 2022
;
Flury et al. 2022
;
Pahl et al. 2023
, e.g.)
. A further tool in understanding the physics of how LyC photons may escape is via high-resolution cosmological hydrodynamical simulations
(
Maji et al. 2022
;
Choustikov et al. 2023
, e.g.)
.

Ly
\alpha
emission has historically been used to explore how ionising radiation can escape, since both Ly
\alpha
and LyC photons rely on relatively dust-free environments to escape
(
Verhamme et al. 2017
;
Izotov et al. 2021
;
Naidu et al. 2022
;
Maji et al. 2022
;
Hayes & Scarlata 2023
)
. The velocity offset of Ly
\alpha
with respect to the systemic velocity determined from other nebular lines in particular can trace the geometry of the distribution of neutral gas in an H
ii
region, with Ly
\alpha
peaks close to the systemic velocity probing relatively low column densities that facilitate both the escape of Ly
\alpha
as well as LyC photons from galaxies
(
Dijkstra et al. 2006
;
Dijkstra 2014
;
Verhamme et al. 2015
;
Verhamme et al. 2017
)
.

Fundamentally, however, the increasing neutrality of the IGM renders Ly
\alpha
emission an unreliable tool in the EoR
(
Santos 2004
;
Dijkstra et al. 2006
;
Hayes et al. 2010
;
Behrens & Niemeyer 2013
;
Inoue et al. 2014
, e.g.)
and we must rely on other emission lines to infer the leakage of LyC photons. Rest-frame ultraviolet spectroscopy has revealed strong emission lines of the semi-forbidden C
iii
]
\lambda\lambda 1907,1909
doublet (referred to as C
iii
] hereafter) over
z\sim 2-7

(
Shapley et al. 2003
;
Erb et al. 2010
;
Stark et al. 2014
;
Stark et al. 2015
)
and various authors have discussed whether studies of C
iii
] emission may be a valuable probe as a substitute for Ly
\alpha

(
Stark et al. 2014
;
Stark et al. 2015
;
Stark et al. 2017
;
Ding et al. 2017
)
. These observations however pre-date
JWST
. With
JWST
results delving deeper into the reionization era with spectroscopy, we are already seeing how
JWST
is transforming identification of these emission lines
(
Bunker et al. 2023
;
Tang et al. 2023
;
Saxena et al. 2023a
, e.g)

Several studies have previously explored correlations between the equivalent widths (EW) of Ly
\alpha
and C
iii
]
(
Shapley et al. 2003
;
Erb et al. 2010
;
Stark et al. 2014
;
Rigby et al. 2015
;
Stark et al. 2015
;
Ding et al. 2017
;
Schaerer et al. 2018
;
Hutchison et al. 2019
;
Le Fèvre et al. 2019
;
Marchi et al. 2019
;
Cullen et al. 2020
;
Ravindranath et al. 2020
;
Llerena et al. 2021
)
in low redshift SFGs where Ly
\alpha
is un-attenuated by the neutral IGM.
Marchi et al. 2019
found a strong correlation between the two lines, but noted that their sample may be biased as the sample was selected to have both Ly
\alpha
and C
iii
] emission, questioning whether this relation could change if the entire population of galaxies with C
iii
] emission were considered. The basic idea behind any possible correlation is that the conditions which support the efficient production and escape of Ly
\alpha
radiation (i.e. low dust content, low metallicity, partial coverage of neutral hydrogen) may be similar to those required for the production of collisionally-excited emission lines such as C
iii
]. However,
Rigby et al. 2015
pointed out that the correlation is driven primarily by the strongest emitters of Ly
\alpha
and C
iii
] (EW(Ly
\alpha
)
\sim 50
Å & EW(C
iii
])
\sim 5
Å ), whereas for weaker emitters there is no convincing correlation. Moreover, at low metallicities (
Z=0.1-0.2\,Z_{\odot}
), the correlation may weaken further
(
Nakajima et al. 2018b
)
.

Regardless, any correlation between Ly
\alpha
and C
iii
] may offer the prospect of using C
iii
] emission from galaxies at
z>6
, now feasible with
JWST
, to constrain the Ly
\alpha
properties and therefore the nature of the ionising sources and their environment. Using spectroscopic data from the VANDELS survey for SFGs in the redshift range
z\sim 3-4
where both Ly
\alpha
and C
iii
] are visible, we examine in further detail the utility of C
iii
] as a proxy for Ly
\alpha
.

The work explored in this paper complements earlier studies by
Marchi et al. 2019
and
Llerena et al. 2021
that have used VANDELS data to investigate these emission lines.
Llerena et al. 2021
explored the global properties of C
iii
] derived from stacked spectra within the VANDELS dataset. The unique aspect of this work is the emphasis on a larger sample of individual emitters compared to
Llerena et al. 2021
and, crucially, the offset between C
iii
] and Ly
\alpha
and its connection with Lyman continuum leakage.
Marchi et al. 2019
studied C
iii
] and Ly
\alpha
including offsets; however, a key difference in this work is that we do not pre-select for Ly
\alpha
.

The layout of this paper is as follows: In Section
2
we describe the VANDELS data used in this work and the various line measurement methods employed. We present the main results based on this spectroscopic data in Section
3
. Finally, we summarise the findings of this paper in Section
4
and place the results into context with those in the literature and discuss the C
iii
] and Ly
\alpha
relationship along with the implications of the Ly
\alpha
offsets in the reionization era.

Throughout the paper, we assume the following cosmology:
\Omega_{m}
= 0.3,
\Omega_{\Lambda}
= 0.7, H
{}_{0}=70

Mpc

2.1
VANDELS survey

The spectra used in this study all come from the VANDELS survey, which is a VIMOS survey of the CANDELS fields using the VIMOS spectrograph on ESO’s Very Large Telescope (VLT)
(
Pentericci et al. 2018
;
McLure et al. 2018
)
. The survey covers a total area of
~0.2
deg
2
centred on the CANDELS UKIDSS Ultra Deep Survey (UDS) and Chandra Deep Field South (CFDS) fields. It secured high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) spectra at optical wavelengths
(0.48<\lambda<1.0\mu
m) for
~2100
galaxies in the redshift interval
1.0\leq z\leq 7.0

(
Garilli et al. 2021
)
. Around 85% of the galaxies targeted by VANDELS are at
z\sim 3
. The unique aspect of the VANDELS survey is that each source has a significant exposure time, ranging from 20hrs to a maximum of 80hrs
(
Pentericci et al. 2018
)
. Full details and description of the survey and target selection can be found in
McLure et al. 2018
.

2.2
C
iii
] and Ly
\alpha
emitter selection and line measurement

In this work, we focus on spectroscopically confirmed star-forming galaxies from VANDELS data release 4 (DR4) across both UDS and CDFS fields
(
Garilli et al. 2021
)
in the redshift range of 3
\leq z\leq 4
, only selecting those galaxies that have a redshift probability of being 95% correct (a redshift reliability flag of 3 or 4, see
Pentericci et al. 2018
for more details). This redshift range ensures that both the C
iii
] and Ly
\alpha
emission lines lie within the spectral range. These initial selection criteria resulted in a sample of 773 objects.

To identify C
iii
] and Ly
\alpha
emission from this parent sample, both the 1D and 2D spectra were visually inspected using the
pandora
software
1
1

1

https://www.iasf-milano.inaf.it/software/
. Based on the robustness of the C
iii
] emission from visual inspection, the sample was split into three different groups based on the ‘confidence level’ (CL hereafter). CL 3 was assigned to those sources with clear C
iii
] emission in both their 1D and 2D spectra, unaffected by sky features or residual noise. Spectra that showed clear C
iii
] emission in either 1D or 2D spectra (but not both) were assigned a CL of 2, whereas possible line detections likely contaminated by residual noise/sky features were assigned a CL of 1. Out of an initial parent sample of 773 galaxies, C
iii
] emission (CL 1-3) was identified in 280 galaxies, with 139 galaxies assigned the highest confidence level of CL3.

As the main aim of this paper is to study the dependence of C
iii
] emission on Ly
\alpha
, we then proceeded to identify Ly
\alpha
emission from all those galaxies that had possible C
iii
] emission. We did not introduce a confidence level criterion for Ly
\alpha
emission, as the reliability and significance of any Ly
\alpha
emission was determined using emission line fitting (as discussed later). Across our parent sample, 62 galaxies were found to exhibit both Ly
\alpha
and C
iii
] (CL3) via visual inspection.

We then measured the strength of the emission lines in these 62 objects. The first step involved determining a ‘systemic’ redshift using the C
iii
] line, for which we used a single Gaussian fit and the systemic redshift was determined from the peak of this Gaussian
(see
Saxena et al. 2020
, for example)
. Using the systemic redshift, each spectrum was de-redshifted and a single Gaussian was fitted to Ly
\alpha
, C
iii
] and all other rest-UV lines (such as He
ii
and C
iv
) visible in the spectra, with the local continuum value for each line estimated by polynomial fitting to nearby regions free from other emission or absorption features. EWs and integrated fluxes were determined from these Gaussian fits. If the S/N ratio of the continuum was below
2\sigma
, the
2\sigma
noise level was used to determine a lower limit for the line EW.

Thanks to accurate systemic redshifts measured from the C
iii
] line, we could also accurately measure the Ly
\alpha
velocity offset from the systemic velocity. By comparing the centroid of the observed Ly
\alpha
emission with that expected for a rest-frame wavelength of 1215.67Å using the galaxy systemic redshift, the velocity offset of the Ly
\alpha
peak was determined for all galaxies in our sample. The emission line fluxes, widths, EWs and the velocity offset of Ly
\alpha
compared to systemic redshift are listed in Table
3
.

2.3
Identification of possible AGN

Prior to further analysis, we considered it important to identify possible AGN in our sample to remove any biases. As C
iii
] has a high ionisation potential (
24.4
eV), it is possible that some of the stronger C
iii
] emitters are AGN. To explore this, we searched for another high ionisation line, He
ii

\lambda 1640
(
54.4
eV), which is also commonly seen in AGN.

We then attempted to identify possible AGN using rest-UV line ratio diagnostics. Specifically, we used EW(C
iii
]) versus C
iii
]
\lambda 1909
/He
ii

\lambda 1640
proposed by
Nakajima et al. 2018b
, which is shown in Figure
1
. This resulted in the identification of 9 possible AGN out of the 62 CL3 C
iii
] and Ly
\alpha
emitters, which were removed from our sample. Further, cross-matching with the deep X-ray deep catalogue that exists in CDFS
(
Luo et al. 2017
)
revealed one more source identified to be an AGN, which was also removed from our sample. We note that using the C
iii
] and He
ii
based AGN selection, we have reported 7 new possible AGN from VANDELS, which were not identified in previous studies
(
Saxena et al. 2020
, e.g)
.

For those galaxies with no clear He
ii
detection, we estimated an upper flux limit by integrating the 1
\sigma
continuum-subtracted flux at the expected position (red stars with arrows in Figure
1
). Since these values were consistent for star-forming galaxies, they were retained in the sample.

Ly
\alpha

In this section we briefly explore the Ly
\alpha
properties of our galaxies. Across our sample, we measure Ly
\alpha
velocity offsets ranging from
166-1051

-1
, with an average value of
\Delta v_{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha}

\simeq 533

. In Figure

we show the velocity offset from systematic redshift as a function of EW(Ly

), colour-coded by
M_{\rm{UV}}
, finding qualitatively an anti-correlation between the Ly
\alpha
velocity offset and strength. This anti-correlation has also been previously reported in the literature for star-forming galaxies across redshifts
(
Erb et al. 2010
;
Nakajima et al. 2018a
;
Tang et al. 2023
;
Prieto-Lyon et al. 2023
;

;
Saxena et al. 2023b
, e.g.)
.

Overall, the

) and
\Delta v_{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha}
in our sample, and the scatter we find in our sample is highly consistent with other high redshift LAE observations
(
Erb et al. 2010
;
Stark et al. 2014
;
Willott et al. 2015
;
Nakajima et al. 2018a
;
Choudhury et al. 2021
;
Tang et al. 2023
;
Prieto-Lyon et al. 2023

. This demonstrates that the
z>6

\alpha
attenuation and profile is largely being determined by scattering through the ISM.

As has been noted by other studies, the anti-correlation between Ly
\alpha
velocity offset and EW is likely driven by the density and geometry of neutral gas in the systems, where a higher density leads to increased absorption/scattering of Ly
\alpha
photons along a line of sight, leading to a reduction in the observed EW and an increase in the velocity offset from systemic, which can be explained using the ‘expanding shell model’ described in
Verhamme et al. 2006
;
Dijkstra et al. 2016
. This explanation is supported by
Marchi et al. 2019
, who used a similar VANDELS dataset and found a correlation between the Ly
\alpha
velocity offset and the shift in the interstellar absorption lines, where galaxies with high ISM outflow velocities also showed small Ly
\alpha
velocity offsets.

Prieto-Lyon et al. 2023
identified a notably reduced mean value of
\Delta v_{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha}
at 205km s
-1
for some of the faintest galaxies (avg.
M_{\rm{UV}}

\sim-17.8
) within a similar redshift range (
3<z<5
). This observation is expected since their sample primarily encompasses galaxies with limited diversity in terms of their stellar masses and consequently lower neutral gas densities, where a higher
\Delta v_{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha}
may be less frequently observed.

Assuming that the observed Ly
\alpha
properties in our sample of galaxies are regulated by attenuation/scattering by neutral gas in the ISM at redshifts where the neutral IGM is not expected to attenuate Ly
\alpha
significantly, we can now attempt to understand the physical conditions that regulate both the strength and velocity profiles of Ly
\alpha
emission as well as the strength of C
iii
] emission. This can then be used to understand how C
iii
] emission may potentially be used as a diagnostic for Ly
\alpha
properties in reionization era galaxies that do not reside in ionized bubbles, and therefore do not show Ly
\alpha
emission.

3.4
C
iii
] as a tracer of Ly
\alpha
velocity offset

Having confirmed that the Ly
\alpha
properties are indeed sensitive to the neutral gas density that controls the escape of Ly
\alpha
photons along a line of sight, we now compare the velocity offset of Ly
\alpha
with the strength of C
iii
] emission to investigate whether the observed EW(C
iii
]) can be used as a diagnostic of neutral gas content, and consequently Ly
\alpha
and LyC escape from the ISM of high redshift galaxies.

In Figure
7
we show the distribution of Ly
\alpha
velocity offset as a function of EW(C
iii
]), once again colour-coded by
M_{\rm{UV}}
for galaxies in our sample. Qualitatively speaking, there appears to be a tentative anti-correlation between the observed Ly
\alpha
offset and EW(C
iii
]), with UV-faint galaxies showing less scatter and UV-bright galaxies showing a larger scatter. Therefore, any possible correlation between Ly
\alpha
velocity offset and EW(C
iii
]) must take into account the UV absolute magnitudes which seem to affect the relation.

M_{\rm{UV}}

iii

\Delta v_{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha}
, taking into account
M_{\rm{UV}}
. To do this, we employ a multi-variate fit to quantify
\Delta v_{\mathrm{Ly}\alpha}
as a function of both EW(C
iii
]) and
M_{\rm{UV}}
, standardising the independent variables (
x_{i}
), which takes the form:

\frac{\Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha}-\bar{{\Delta v}}_{\rm{Ly}\alpha}}{\sigma_{\Delta v}}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}A_{i}\frac{x_{i}-\bar{x_{i}}}{\sigma_{i}}

and

are the mean and standard deviation, respectively, of the independent variables. Performing a linear regression on this equation gives us the coefficients -0.388 for EW(C

M_{\rm{UV}}
. The values of the coefficients as well as the mean and standard deviations of the independent variables are given in Table

1\sigma
band. This may be consistent with the very low Ly
\alpha
escape fraction reported in
Bunker et al. 2023
, which in combination with a relatively large velocity offset of
\sim 500
km s
-1
suggests that GN-z11 is likely not situated within an ionized bubble
(
Hayes & Scarlata 2023
, see also)
, and the emergent Ly
\alpha
has been heavily attenuated by the neutral IGM surrounding it. Based on the relations that we have derived, the Ly
\alpha
velocity offset emerging from the ISM will be predicted to be much lower (up to 300 km s
-1
lower) based on our multivariate regression.

We find that for a fixed
M_{\rm{UV}}
, an increase in EW(C
iii
]) results in a decreasing
\Delta v_{Ly\alpha}
. However, interestingly, at a fixed EW(C
iii
]) emission, UV-bright galaxies tend to exhibit a lower Ly
\alpha
velocity offset compared to UV-faint galaxies. Notably, as mentioned previously, the UV-bright galaxies tend to lie below the best-fit line we show in Figure
7
. This behaviour suggests that for the same EW(C
iii
]), UV-bright galaxies may be in a more ‘bursty’ phase of star-formation, potentially facilitating a more efficient clearing of channels through which Ly
\alpha
can escape. It has indeed been shown that galaxies in the early Universe may be expected to follow a more bursty star-formation that boosts their observed UV magnitudes
(
Endsley et al. 2023
;
Simmonds et al. 2024
, e.g.)
. Stronger outflows driven by a stronger starburst aligns with expectations from Ly
\alpha
escape in an expanding shell model
(
Verhamme et al. 2006
;
Dijkstra et al. 2016
, e.g.)
, where neutral gas is expelled, allowing the Ly
\alpha
emission to escape close its systemic velocity.

Overall, our results indicate that the C
iii
] emission is likely originating from an ionising radiation field likely driven by young stars. In such a scenario, the UV magnitude serves as a measure of the number of stars undergoing formation. To validate this observation comprehensively, additional UV-bright galaxies with strong C
iii
] and Ly
\alpha
signals are required.
Erb et al. 2014
also noted velocity offset to be correlated with UV luminosity, however found that their faint LAE selected sub-sample of galaxies had smaller Ly
\alpha
velocity offsets and did not see the correlations present in their UV-brighter comparison sample possibly due to lack of dynamical range in mass and luminosity.

In summary, our findings indicate that for a fixed
M_{\rm{UV}}
, a higher EW(C
iii
]) is associated with a lower velocity offset. Conversely, for a fixed EW(C
iii
]), a brighter
M_{\rm{UV}}
results in a lower velocity offset. We propose that this effect may be contributing to the observed patterns. The observed inverse correlation between EW(C
iii
]), Ly
\alpha
velocity offset and its dependence on galaxy UV magnitude, therefore, promotes the utility of C
iii
] as a proxy for Ly
\alpha
from galaxies in the epoch of reionization. With increased C
iii
] line detections across samples of
z>6
galaxies with NIRSpec, our derived correlations may lead to an additional albeit indirect estimate of the leakage of Ly
\alpha
and from that potentially the LyC photons from star-forming galaxies in the early Universe.

4
Discussion and Summary

By employing alternative indicators in the absence of direct Ly
\alpha
observations, we can still make significant strides in studying the processes that regulate the escape of Ly
\alpha
as well as LyC photons, essential to understand the key drivers of reionization. While Ly
\alpha
remains a crucial component for understanding reionization, its unavailability limits our capacity to obtain velocity offsets, which are crucial in estimating parameters such as Ly
\alpha
/Lyman continuum escape fractions and bubble sizes
(
Prieto-Lyon et al. 2023
;
Saxena et al. 2023b
;
Jones et al. 2023
;
Witstok et al. 2023
)
.

Latest results constraining the Ly
\alpha
fractions and EW distributions at
z>6
from
JWST
have found the Ly
\alpha
fractions to be low, which is expected given the increasing neutral fraction of the IGM
(
Jones et al. 2023
, e.g.)
. This means that at the highest redshifts, unless star-forming galaxies are surrounded by ionized regions, observing Ly
\alpha
emission will become increasingly challenging. Particularly beyond
z>9.5
as the prominent O and H lines shift beyond the detectable range of the
JWST
NIRSpec, C
iii
] may emerge as a next best feature to both confirm redshifts and deduce additional properties. Therefore, we propose a potential solution to use the relatively bright C
iii
] emission line in rest-UV, coupled with
M_{\rm{UV}}
, as a tracer for Ly
\alpha
velocity offsets from systemic through the equation provided in Eq.
1
.

With an increase in the number of detections of rest-UV lines at
z>8

(
Tang et al. 2023
;
Bunker et al. 2023
;
Hsiao et al. 2023
;
Fujimoto et al. 2023
;
Topping et al. 2024
)
, including the detection of C
iii
] emission at
z=12.5
reported by
D’Eugenio et al. 2023
, it is becoming essential to maximize the utility of these emission lines in inferring all possible properties of galaxies and their surroundings. Our proposal to employ C
iii
] as an indirect tracer of Ly
\alpha
velocity offsets (Figure
7
) represents an approach to utilise insights from this strong rest-UV line to better understand the escape of Ly
\alpha
photons from the ISM of galaxies in the reionization era, possibly evaluating their contribution towards reionizing their local bubble.

In this study, we have used the rest-UV spectra for a sample of star-forming galaxies in the redshift range
\approx 3-4
from the VANDELS survey with confirmed detections of Ly
\alpha
and C
iii
] lines, to explore the utility of using the C
iii
]
\lambda 1909
line, often the second brightest emission line after Ly
\alpha
in the rest-UV, to infer the intrinsic Ly
\alpha
properties of galaxies in the reionization epoch, where Ly
\alpha
is significantly attenuated by the neutral IGM. Starting with the detection of C
iii
] emission from 391 objects in the UDS and CDFS fields from the VANDELS survey, we find that 36% of objects show C
iii
] emission, with an average EW
\sim
3Å across the total C
iii
] population. Using the C
iii
] line as a prerequisite, we have then searched for Ly
\alpha
emission in the sample, recovering a final sample of 52 star-forming galaxies with robust detections of both Ly
\alpha
and C
iii
] in their spectra.

We find that the equivalent widths of Ly
\alpha
and C
iii
] are not significantly correlated, finding an increased scatter in EW(C
iii
]) for galaxies with particularly low EW(Ly
\alpha
)
<10
Å, in agreement with previous studies. We show that galaxies with strong Ly
\alpha
emission have a higher EW(C
iii
]) on average (
\sim
5Å) compared to those without Ly
\alpha
, highlighting that Ly
\alpha
emitting galaxies that also show C
iii
] emission are likely tracing galaxies undergoing rapid star-formation. In this work we report some of the highest EW(C
iii
]) values (ranging from
1.6-12.8
Å) when compared with those published in the literature at comparable redshifts with a range of
z\sim 2-4
.

Using the peak of the C
iii
] emission as an indicator of the systemic redshift of galaxies in our sample, we calculate Ly
\alpha
velocity offsets,
\Delta v_{Ly\alpha}
, in the range
166-1051
km s
-1
, with an average of
533
km s
-1
. We find a weak anti-correlation between EW(Ly
\alpha
) and
\Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha}
, as has been previously reported in the literature, attributed to the role of scattering of Ly
\alpha
photons by the neutral gas in the ISM.

Interestingly, we report an anti-correlation between EW(C
iii
]) and
\Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha}
, which appears to also depend on the absolute UV magnitude of the galaxy. This dependence of EW(C
iii
]) on both
\Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha}
and
M_{\rm{UV}}
is captured using a multi-variate equation, as shown in Equation
1
and Table
2
, where we find that
\Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha}
anti-correlates more strongly with EW(C
iii
]) (coefficient of -0.388), with a comparatively weaker but non-negligible correlation with
M_{\rm{UV}}
(coefficient of +0.16).

From our multivariate equation, we find that for a fixed
M_{\rm{UV}}
, an increase in EW(C
iii
]) leads to a decrease in
\Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha}
. At a fixed EW(C
iii
]), on the other hand, UV-bright galaxies show lower Ly
\alpha
velocity offsets compared to UV-faint galaxies. In our sample, we find that UV-bright galaxies tend to lie below the best-fitting relationship between only EW(C
iii
]) and
\Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha}
, suggesting that UV-bright galaxies with strong Ly
\alpha
and C
iii
] emission may be undergoing a more ‘bursty’ phase of star-formation, aiding the efficient clearing of channels through which Ly
\alpha
can escape, consistent with the ‘expanding shell model’. Although this is promising within the context of a population of extremely UV-bright galaxies that are now being discovered
z\gtrsim 10
, to validate this trend, additional spectroscopy for galaxies at
z<6
with comparably bright absolute UV magnitudes is essential.

Thanks to
JWST
spectroscopy, it is now possible to detect C
iii
] emission out to
z\gtrsim 10
, and therefore the relationship between
\Delta v_{\rm{Ly}\alpha}
on EW(C
iii
]) and M
UV
that we have presented in this work may provide insights into the nature of the intrinsic Ly
\alpha
emission that may be escaping from the ISM of these galaxies, before it encounters a relatively neutral IGM and gets attenuated along the line of sight. Obtaining a better handle on the intrinsic production and escape of Ly
\alpha
photons from galaxies in the reionization era can provide unprecedented insights into the dominant contributors of ionizing photon towards cosmic reionization.

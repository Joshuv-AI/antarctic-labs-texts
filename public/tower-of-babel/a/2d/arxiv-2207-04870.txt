Partial regularity of solutions to the 3D chemotaxis-Navier-Stokes equations at the first blow-up time
Xiaomeng Chen, Shuai Li, Wendong Wang

Abstract
. As Dombrowski et al. showed in [ 11 ] (see also [ 37 ] ), suspensions of aerobic bacteria often develop flows from the interplay of chemotaxis and buoyancy, which is described as the chemotaxis-Navier-Stokes flow, and they observed self-concentration occurs as a turbulence by exhibiting transient, reconstituting, high-speed jets. Moreover, local concentration leads to a jet descending faster than its surroundings, which entrains nearby fluid to produce paired, oppositely signed vortices. In this note, we investigate the Hausdorff dimension of these vortices (singular points) by considering partial regularity of weak solutions of the three dimensional chemotaxis-Navier-Stokes equations, and obtain the 5 3 \frac{5}{3} -dimensional Hausdorff measure of the possible singular set is vanishing at the first blow-up time, which generalizes the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg’s partial regularity theory to the chemotaxis-fluid model. The new ingredients are to establish certain type of local energy inequality and deal with the non-scaling invariant quantity of n ​ ln ⁡ n n\ln n , where n n represents the cell concentration, which seems to be the first description for the singular set of weak solutions of the model.

chemotaxis-Navier-Stokes, partial regularity, local energy inequality

\displaystyle M_{1}\leq\sup_{-r^{2}<t<0}r^{-1}\int_{B_{r}\cap\{n<r^{-\frac{3}{2}}\}}|n\ln n|+2n|\ln r|dx\leq\frac{1}{8}\varepsilon_{0}+2r^{\frac{1}{2}}|B_{1}||\ln r|\leq\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon_{0}

where we choose
r<r_{1}
for a small
r_{1}<r_{0}
.
Besides,

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{-r^{2}<t<0}r^{-1}\int_{B_{r}\cap\{r^{-\frac{3}{2}}\leq n\leq r^{-2}\}}|n\ln(r^{2}n)|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{-r^{2}<t<0}r^{-1}\int_{B_{r}\cap\{r^{-\frac{3}{2}}\leq n\leq r^{-2}\}}|n\ln(r^{-\frac{1}{2}})|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{-r^{2}<t<0}r^{-1}\int_{B_{r}}|n\ln n|dxdt\leq\frac{1}{8}\varepsilon_{0}

and

\displaystyle M_{3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{-r^{2}<t<0}r^{-1}\int_{B_{r}\cap\{n\geq r^{-2}\}}|n\ln(r^{2}n)|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{-r^{2}<t<0}r^{-1}\int_{B_{r}\cap\{n\geq r^{-2}\}}n\ln ndx\leq\frac{1}{8}\varepsilon_{0}

where we used
r<_{1}<1.

Hence there holds
\sup_{t}\int_{B_{1}}|n_{r}\ln n_{r}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{0}
. Combining this with (
5
) we get

\displaystyle\sup_{t}\int_{B_{1}}n_{r}+|n_{r}\ln n_{r}|+|\nabla\sqrt{\tilde{c_{r}}}|^{2}+|u_{r}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{Q_{1}}|\nabla n_{r}^{\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{r}|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{\tilde{c_{r}}}|^{2}+|p_{r}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

(5.7)

Using Theorem (
1.4
), it follows that
(n_{r},u_{r},\nabla c_{r})
is regular at the point
0
. The proof is complete.

Derived equivalences for mirror-reflective algebras


Abstract
We show that the construction of mirror-reflective algebras inherits derived equivalences of gendo-symmetric algebras. More precisely, suppose A A and B B are gendo-symmetric algebras with both A ​ e Ae and B ​ f Bf faithful projective-injective left ideals generated by idempotents e e in A A and f f in B B , respectively. If A A and B B are derived equivalent, then the mirror-reflective algebras of ( A , e ) (A,e) and ( B , f ) (B,f) are derived equivalent.

1
Introduction

Given an (associative) algebra
A
over a commutative ring
k
, an idempotent
e
of
A
and an element
\lambda
in the center of
\Lambda:=eAe
, we introduced the mirror-reflective algebra
R(A,e,\lambda)
of
A
at level
(e,\lambda)
in
[
4
]
. Roughly speaking, this algebra has the underlying
k
-module structure
A\oplus Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}eA
such that
Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}eA
is an ideal in
R(A,e,\lambda)
. The specialization of
R(A,e,\lambda)
at
\lambda=e
is called the mirror-reflective algebra of
A
at
e
, denoted by
R(A,e)
.
In case that
A
is a finite-dimensional gendo-symmetric algebra over a field
k
and
e
is an idempotent of
A
such that
Ae
is a faithful and projective-injective
A
-module, the algebra
R(A,e)
is called simply the mirror-reflective algebra of
A
. Such a construction can be iterated and thus supplies a series of both higher Auslander algebras and recollements of derived module categories. It turns out that a new characterisation of Tachikawa’s second conjecture for symmetric algebras can be formulated in terms of stratifying ideals and recollements of derived categories (see
[
4
]
).

Our purpose of this note is to show that the construction of mirror-reflective algebras preserves derived equivalences. More precisely, we have the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
A
and
B
are finite-dimensional gendo-symmetric algebras over a field
k
and that
{}_{A}Ae
and
{}_{B}Bf
are faithful projective-injective modules generated by idempotents
e\in A
and
f\in B
, respectively. If
A
and
B
are derived equivalent, then there is an isomorphism
\sigma:Z(eAe)\to Z(fBf)
of algebras from the center of
eAe
to the one of
fBf
such that, for any
\lambda\in Z(eAe)
, the mirror-reflective algebras
R(A,e,\lambda)
and
R(B,f,(\lambda)\sigma)
are derived equivalent.

During the course of the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we will give a general construction of derived equivalences of mirror-reflective algebras of arbitrary algebras at any levels in Theorem
3.1
. So Theorem
1.1
is just its consequence.

This note is sketched as follows. In Section
2
we provide preliminaries for the proof of the main result. This includes recalling basic definitions and proving facts on derived equivalences and on mirror-reflective algebras . In Section
3
we prove Theorem
3.1
.

Derived equivalences of algebras with idempotents

Definition 2.1

Let

(1)
A complex

is the smallest full triangulated subcateory of

(2)
A complex

if there is

in

(3)

are equivalent as triangulated categories, or equivalently,

Let

is a triangle equivalence with the quasi-inverse

This implies that

are isomorphic to tilting complexes in

and

lies in the center
Z(\Lambda)
of
\Lambda
. Recall that the mirror-reflective algebra
R(A,e,\lambda)
of
A
at level
(e,\lambda)
, defined in
[

]
, has the underlying

-module
A\oplus Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}eA
as its abelian group. Its multiplication

is given explicitly by

(a+be\otimes ec)\ast(a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}e\otimes ec^{\prime}):=aa^{\prime}+(ab^{\prime}e\otimes ec^{\prime}+be\otimes eca^{\prime}+becb^{\prime}e\otimes\lambda ec^{\prime})

for
a,b,c,a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime}\in A
.
This can be reformulated as follows: Let
\omega_{\lambda}
be the composite of the natural maps:

(Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}eA)\otimes_{A}(Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}eA)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}(eA\otimes_{A}Ae)\otimes_{\Lambda}eA\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda\otimes_{\Lambda}eA\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm Id}\otimes(\cdot\lambda)\otimes{\rm Id}}}{{\longrightarrow}}Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda\otimes_{\Lambda}eA\rightarrow Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}eA,

where
(\cdot\lambda):\Lambda\to\Lambda
is the multiplication map by
\lambda
.
Then

\big((be\otimes ec)\otimes(b^{\prime}e\otimes ec^{\prime})\big)\omega_{\lambda}=(be\otimes ec)\ast(b^{\prime}e\otimes ec^{\prime}).

Clearly,
R(A,e,0)
is exactly the trivial extension of
A
by
Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}eA
. To understand
R(A,e,\lambda)
, we will employ idealized extensions of algebras.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
X
be an
A
-
A
-bimodule. An
idealized extension
of
A
by
X
is defined to be an algebra

such that
A
is a subalgebra (with the same identity) of
R
,
X
is an ideal of
R
, and

as
A
-
A
-bimodules. Two idealized extensions

and
R_{2}
of
A
by
X
are said to be

if there exists an algebra isomorphism
\phi:R_{1}\to R_{2}
such that
the restriction of
\phi
to
A
is the identity map of
A
and the one of
\phi
to
X
is an bijection from
X
to
X
.

Clearly, an algebra
R
is an idealized extension of
A
by
X
if and only if
R
contains
A
as a subalgebra and
there is an algebra homomorphism
\pi:R\to A
with
X={\rm Ker}(\pi)
such that the composite of the inclusion
A\to R
with
\pi
is the identity map of
A
. Hence a mirror-reflective algebra
R(A,e,\lambda)
is an idealized extension of
A
by
Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}eA
.

Let

F:=Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}-\otimes_{\Lambda}eA:\;\;\Lambda^{\rm{e}}\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}\longrightarrow A^{\rm{e}}\mbox{{\rm-Mod}},\;\;M\mapsto Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}M\otimes_{\Lambda}eA,

G:=e(-)e:\;\;A^{\rm{e}}\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{\rm{e}}\mbox{{\rm-Mod}},\;\;M\mapsto eMe

for

. Since

is an idempotent element of

and there are natural isomorphisms

(F,G)
is an adjoint pair and
F
is fully faithful. This implies the following.

Lemma 2.8
.

The functor
F
induces an algebra isomorphism

\rho:\;\;Z(\Lambda)\longrightarrow{\rm End}_{A^{\rm e}}(Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}eA),\;\;\lambda\mapsto\rho_{\lambda}:=[ae\otimes eb\mapsto ae\lambda\otimes eb]

for
\lambda\in Z(\Lambda)
and

. Moreover,
\omega_{\lambda}=\omega_{e}\rho_{\lambda}
.

The following result parameterizes the idealized extensions of
A
by
Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}eA
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
Z(\Lambda)^{\times}
be the group of units of
Z(\Lambda)
, that is,
Z(\Lambda)^{\times}
is the group of all invertible elements in
Z(\Lambda)
. Then there exists a bijection from the quotient of the multiplicative semigroup

modulo

to the set

of the isomorphism classes of idealized extensions of

by
Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}eA

Z(\Lambda)/Z(\Lambda)^{\times}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{S}(A,e),\quad\lambda\,Z(\Lambda)^{\times}\mapsto R(A,e,\lambda)\;\mbox{ for }\lambda\in Z(\Lambda).

Proof.
Let
Z_{0}(\Lambda):=Z(\Lambda)/Z(\Lambda)^{\times}=\{\lambda\,Z(\Lambda)^{\times}\mid\lambda\in Z(\Lambda)\}
and
[\lambda]:=\lambda\,Z(\Lambda)^{\times}\in Z_{0}(\Lambda)
for
\lambda\in Z(\Lambda)
.
By
[
4
, Lemma 3.2(2)]
, if
\mu\in Z(\Lambda)^{\times}
, then
R(A,e,\lambda)\simeq R(A,e,\lambda\mu)
as algebras. This means that
the map

\varphi:Z_{0}(\Lambda)\longrightarrow\mathscr{S}(A,e),[\lambda]\mapsto R(A,e,\lambda)

is well defined. Let
R
be an idealized extension of
A
by

. Then the multiplication of

induces a homomorphism

of

-modules. Recall that
\omega_{e}:X\otimes_{A}X\to X
is an isomorphism of
A^{\rm e}
-modules. Let

\phi^{\prime}\in{\rm End}_{A^{\rm e}}(X)
and

. By Lemma
2.8
,
\phi^{\prime}=\rho_{z}
for some

and

is surjective.

Now, we show that
\varphi
is injective. Suppose
\lambda_{i}\in Z(\Lambda)
for

and

as algebras. Set

. By Definition

, there is an algebra isomorphism

such that

and

is a homomorphism of

-modules and

(\alpha\otimes_{A}\alpha)\omega_{\lambda_{2}}=\omega_{\lambda_{1}}\alpha:X\otimes_{A}X\to X

A sufficient condition for the isomorphism in Theorem
3.1
to hold true is the vanishing of positive Tor-groups over corner algebras.

Proposition 3.7
.

Suppose that there is a derived equivalence between
(A,e)
and
(B,f)
of algebras with idempotents, which is induced by a two-sided tilting complex
{{}_{A}}T_{B}
. If
{\rm Tor}_{n}^{\Lambda}(Ae,eA)=0={\rm Tor}_{n}^{\Gamma}(Bf,fB)
for all
n\geq 1
, then the derived equivalence
\Phi:{\mathscr{D}}(A^{\rm e})\to{\mathscr{D}}(B^{\rm e})
associated with
T
between the enveloping algebras
A^{\rm e}
and
B^{\rm e}
satisfies

\Phi(Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}eA)\simeq Bf\otimes_{\Gamma}fB
in
{\mathscr{D}}(B^{\rm e})
.

Proof.
Since
{\rm Tor}_{n}^{\Lambda}(Ae,eA)=0
for all
n\geq 1
, we have
Ae\otimes^{\rm\mathbb{L}}_{\Lambda}eA\simeq Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}eA
in
{\mathscr{D}}(A^{\rm e})
.
Similarly,
Bf\otimes^{\rm\mathbb{L}}_{\Gamma}fB\simeq Bf\otimes_{\Gamma}fB
in
{\mathscr{D}}(B^{\rm e})
. Moreover, since
(A,e)
and
(B,f)
are derived equivalent,
it follows from Lemma
2.4
(4) that
\Phi(Ae\otimes^{\rm\mathbb{L}}_{\Lambda}eA)\simeq Bf\otimes^{\rm\mathbb{L}}_{\Gamma}fB
in
{\mathscr{D}}(B^{\rm e})
.
Thus
\Phi(Ae\otimes_{\Lambda}eA)\simeq Bf\otimes_{\Gamma}fB
.

\square

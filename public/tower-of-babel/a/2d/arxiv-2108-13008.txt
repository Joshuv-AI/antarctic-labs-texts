Semiorthogonal decomposition via categorical action
You-Hung Hsu

Abstract
. We show that the categorical action of the shifted q = 0 q=0 affine algebra can be used to construct semiorthogonal decomposition on the weight categories. In particular, this construction recovers Kapranov’s exceptional collection when the weight categories are the derived categories of coherent sheaves on Grassmannians and n n -step partial flag varieties. Finally, as an application, we use this result to construct a semiorthogonal decomposition on the derived categories of coherent sheaves on Grassmannians of a coherent sheaf with homological dimension ≤ 1 \leq 1 over a smooth projective variety X X .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview of semiorthogonal decompostions

Let
X
be a smooth complex projective variety. The bounded derived category of coherent sheaves, denoted by
\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
, is an essential invariant of
X
. One fundamental method to study the structure of
\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
is via semiorthogonal decomposition, which divides a triangulated category into simpler pieces.

One way to produce a semiorthogonal decomposition is from an exceptional collection. The simplest example is Beilinson’s collection for complex projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}

[
3
]
, which is later generalized to Grassmannians and type A partial flag varieties by Kapranov
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
and Kuznetsov-Polishchuk
[
27
]
for isotropic partial flag varieties. For other constructions and examples, we refer to Kuznetsov’s ICM address
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
.

1.2.
Categorical actions

During the past decade, categorification has been an active research topic in representation theory and related areas. One of the important questions is lifting the representations of Lie algebras/quantum groups from vector spaces to categories. Such a notion is called the
categorical actions
.

Initiating from the work of Chuang-Rouquier
[
12
]
for the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-categorification, people have extensively studied the categorical actions of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
or
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
in several flavours. In particular, Cautis-Kamnitzer-Licata
[
10
]
introduced the notion of geometric categorical
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
action in the setting of bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves. Later they
[
11
]
used the geometric categorical
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
action to construct the derived equivalence between derived categories of coherent sheaves on cotangent bundles to complementary Grassmannians.

The lifting of representations to the categorical level also rises other questions. For the categorical action of semisimple or Kac-Moody Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, usually one assigns to each weight space
V(\lambda)
an additive category
\mathcal{C}(\lambda)
and to
generators
e_{i}
,
f_{i}
of
\mathfrak{g}
one assigns functors
\sf{E}_{i}:\mathcal{C}(\lambda)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(\lambda+\alpha_{i})
,
\sf{F}_{i}:\mathcal{C}(\lambda+\alpha_{i})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(\lambda)
respectively. These functors are then required to satisfy certain relations analogous to those in
\mathfrak{g}
. For example, when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
the relation
(ef-fe)|_{V(\lambda)}=\lambda\text{Id}_{V(\lambda)}
becomes

{\sf{E}\sf{F}}|_{\mathcal{C}(\lambda)}\cong{\sf{F}\sf{E}}|_{\mathcal{C}(\lambda)}\bigoplus\text{Id}_{\mathcal{C}(\lambda)}^{\oplus\lambda}\ \text{if}\ \lambda\geq 0,

(1.1)

similarly for
\lambda\leq 0
.

The equation (
1.1
) is an isomorphism between functors. Understanding the natural transformations between the functors in (
1.1
) is a central problem in the categorification of Lie algebras/quantum groups or higher representation theory. Moreover, the natural transformations should ideally induce the isomorphism in (
1.1
). One answer to such a problem is given by Chuang-Rouquier
[
12
]
for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and later generalized to (simply-laced) Kac-Moody algebras
\mathfrak{g}
by Khovanov-Lauda
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
and Rouquier
[
34
]
.

1.3.
Main results

In
[
17
]
, the author defines an algebra called the shifted
q=0
affine algebra, denoted by
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
, and gives a definition of its categorical action. Then he proves that there is a categorical action of the shifted
q=0
affine algebra on the bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves on
n
-step partial flag varieties.

In this article, we try to relate the notion of semiorthogonal decomposition to the categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
. The idea comes from the observation that the Kapranov exceptional collection can be thought of as convolutions of Fourier-Mukai kernels via the categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
.

1.3.1.
The motivating example: Grassmannians.

Let
N\geq 2
be a positive integer and fix the
N
-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Considering the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, denoted by
\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N})
. More precisely,
\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N})=\{0\subset V\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}\ |\ \dim V=k\}
. Let us recall the action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
on
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))
in
[
17
]
. The generators
e_{r}1_{(k,N-k)}
,
f_{s}1_{(k,N-k)}
act on
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))
by the following correspondence diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{Fl}(k-1,k)=\{0\overset{k-1}{\subset}V^{\prime}\overset{1}{\subset}V\overset{N-k}{\subset}\mathbb{C}^{N}\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{p_{2}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N})}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Gr}(k-1,\mathbb{C}^{N})}

(1.2)

where
\mathrm{Fl}(k-1,k)
is the 3-step partial flag variety and
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
are the natural projections. Denoting
\mathcal{V},\ \mathcal{V}^{\prime}
to be the tautological sub-bundles on
\mathrm{Fl}(k-1,k)
of rank
k
,
k-1
respectively. Then the quotient
\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
is a natural line bundle on
\mathrm{Fl}(k-1,k)
. The generators
e_{r}1_{(k,N-k)}
act on
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))
by lifting to the following functors

{\sf{E}}_{r}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N-k,k)}:=p_{2*}(p_{1}^{*}\otimes(\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime})^{r}):\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k-1,\mathbb{C}^{N}))

and similarly for the lift of
f_{s}1_{(k,N-k)}
to
{\sf{F}}_{s}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}
. We refer to Section
3
for more details.

Due to the work by Kapranov
[
22
]
, there are some exceptional collections on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))
. One of them is given by
\{\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}\}
, where
\mathcal{V}
is the universal sub-bundle of rank
k
and
\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is the Schur functor associated to Young diagrams
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{k})
with
0\leq\lambda_{k}\leq...\leq\lambda_{1}\leq N-k
. Moreover, we denote the set of such Young diagrams to be
P(N-k,k)
.

Since
\mathrm{Gr}(0,\mathbb{C}^{N})
is a point,
\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}
can be viewed as an object in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(0,\mathbb{C}^{N})\times\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))
. Also, the functors
{\sf{E}}_{r}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}
and
{\sf{F}}_{s}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}
are Fourier-Mukai transformations with kernels denoted by
\mathcal{E}_{r}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}
and
\mathcal{F}_{s}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}
, respectively. Then by the Borel-Weil-Bott theorem, we have

\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}\cong\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{1}}\ast...\ast\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(0,\mathbb{C}^{N})\times\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))

(1.3)

where we denote
\ast
to be the convolution product of Fourier-Mukai kernels.

From (
1.3
) we know that
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{1}}\ast...\ast\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}
is a
Fourier-Mukai kernel of the functor

{\sf{F}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}:={\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{1}}...{\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(0,\mathbb{C}^{N})),\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))),

and since the categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
can be defined abstractly; it is natural to ask the following question

\boldsymbol{Question:}
Given a categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
action
\mathcal{K}
. Do the functors

\{{\sf{F}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}:={\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{1}}...{\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in P(N-k,k)}

behave like an exceptional collection in the triangulated category
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{K}(0,N),\mathcal{K}(k,N-k))
?

1.3.2.
Statement of the main results

It turns out that the answer is affirmative except that the functors
\{{\sf{F}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\}_{\lambda\in P(N-k,k)}
are not exceptional. Since the above discussion of motivating example can be generalized from Grassmannians to
n
-step partial flag varieties
\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N})
where
\underline{k}=(k_{1},...,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}=N
. We state our main result in full generality.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem
4.1
)
Given a categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
action
\mathcal{K}
. Considering the functors
{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{K}(\eta),\mathcal{K}(\underline{k}))
where

{\sf{F}}_{i,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)}:={\sf{F}}_{i,\lambda(i)_{1}}{\sf{F}}_{i,\lambda(i)_{2}}...{\sf{F}}_{i,\lambda(i)_{\Bbbk_{i}}}

with
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)=(\lambda(i)_{1},...,\lambda(i)_{\Bbbk_{i}})\in P(k_{i+1},\Bbbk_{i})
,
\Bbbk_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}k_{j}
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
, and
\eta=(0,0,...,0,N)
is the highest weight. Then they satisfy the following properties

(1)

\mathrm{Hom}({\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta},{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta})\cong\mathrm{Hom}(\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta},\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta})

(2)

\mathrm{Hom}({\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta},{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)^{\prime}}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)^{\prime}}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta})\cong 0
if
(\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1),...,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1))<_{pl}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)^{\prime},...,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)^{\prime})
where
<_{pl}
denotes the product lexicographic order; i.e., there exist
1\leq i\leq n-1
such that
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(j)=\boldsymbol{\lambda}(j)^{\prime}
for all
0\leq j\leq i-1
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)<_{l}\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)^{\prime}
and
<_{l}
denotes the lexicographic order.

We state two corollaries for the above theorem. The following is the first one, which is easily seen from property (1).

Corollary 1.2
.

(Corollary
4.10
)
Given a categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
action
\mathcal{K}
. The functors

{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{K}(\eta),\mathcal{K}(\underline{k}))

where
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)=(\lambda(i)_{1},...,\lambda(i)_{\Bbbk_{i}})\in P(k_{i+1},\Bbbk_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
, are all fully faithful.

Next, property (2) from Theorem
1.1
tells us that the functors
{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta}
satisfy the semiorthogonal property. Combining with Corollary
1.2
, we obtain the second corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

(Corollary
4.11
)
Given a categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
action
\mathcal{K}
. We denote

\textnormal{Im}{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta}

to be the minimal full triangulated subcategories of
\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})
generated by the class of objects which are the essential images of
{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta}
where
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)=(\lambda(i)_{1},...,\lambda(i)_{\Bbbk_{i}})\in P(k_{i+1},\Bbbk_{i})

for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Then we have the following semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})=\langle\mathcal{A}(\underline{k}),\textnormal{Im}{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta}\rangle_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)\in P(k_{i+1},\Bbbk_{i})}

where
\mathcal{A}(\underline{k})=\langle\textnormal{Im}{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta}\rangle_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)\in P(k_{i+1},\Bbbk_{i})}^{\perp}
is the orthogonal complement.

Remark 1.4
.

When
n=2
and the weight categories are
\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)=\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))
, we have
\mathrm{Hom}(\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)},\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)})\cong\mathbb{C}
. Then
\{{\sf{F}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\}
recovers the Kapranov exceptional collection which mentioned in the Subsection
1.3.1
.

1.4.
Application to Grassmannian of coherent sheaves

In the final part of this paper, we give an application of the main theorem to construct semiorthogonal decomposition on the derived category of coherent sheaves on Grassmannians (more precisely, relative Quot scheme) of coherent sheaf with homological dimension
\leq 1
.

Let
X
be a connected smooth projective variety and
\mathscr{G}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
of homological dimension
\leq 1
, i.e. there is a resolution
\mathcal{E}^{-1}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}^{0}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{G}\rightarrow 0
where
\mathcal{E}^{-1}
,
\mathcal{E}^{0}
are locally free. Let
N=\text{rank}\mathscr{G}=\text{rank}\mathcal{E}^{0}-\text{rank}\mathcal{E}^{-1}\geq 2
be the rank of
\mathscr{G}
. We consider the Grassmannians
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k)
of rank
k
locally free quotients of
\mathscr{G}
and its derived category
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k))
.

This geometric space
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k)
appears in several literatures, and many interesting moduli spaces (related to enumerative geometry) arise via this construction. For example, Addington-Takahashi
[
1
]
use such spaces as correspondences to construct categorical
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
action on derived categories of coherent sheaves on moduli space of sheaves on K3 surface (see Negut
[
32
]
for related work).

Like the usual Grassmannians, constructing semiorthogonal decomposition for
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k))
becomes a natural question. It was already done by Jiang-Leung
[
21
]
for
k=1
(which is called the
projectivization formula
) and Toda
[
35
]
for general
k
(which is called the
Quot formula
).

Here we provide a different construction by using the categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
. We define functors
{\sf{E}}_{r}
,
{\sf{F}}_{s}
as Fourier-Mukai transforms via correspondence that is similar to the diagram (
1.2
). Then we verify those functors satisfy the conditions in Definition
3.2
and thus we can apply Theorem
1.1
to obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

The derived category
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k))
admits the following two semiorthogonal decompositions

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k))

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{A}(N-k,k),\ \textnormal{Im}{\sf{F}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\rangle_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in P(k,N-k)}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{B}(N-k,k),\ \textnormal{Im}{\sf{E}}_{-\boldsymbol{\mu}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N,0)}\rangle_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in P(N-k,k)}

where
\mathcal{A}(N-k,k):=\langle\textnormal{Im}{\sf{F}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\rangle_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in P(k,N-k)}^{\perp}
,
\mathcal{B}(N-k,k):=\langle\textnormal{Im}{\sf{E}}_{-\boldsymbol{\mu}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N,0)}\rangle_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in P(N-k,k)}^{\perp}
are the orthogonal complement.

Unlike the results by Jiang-Leung
[
21
]
and Toda
[
35
]
, the orthogonal complements
\mathcal{A}(N-k,k)
,
\mathcal{B}(N-k,k)
in our Theorem are not clear to see. In future work, we would like to understand the representation-theoretic meaning of those orthogonal complements and see the relation between our construction and Jiang-Leung, Toda.

1.5.
Some further remarks

Finally, we give some remarks about the results and to other related works.

1.5.1.
The dual exceptional collection

We also have the notion of dual exceptional collection (see Definition
2.8
for the definition), which is similar to the dual basis. From Theorem
2.15
the right dual exceptional collection for
\{\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}\}
is given by
\{\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V}[-|\boldsymbol{\mu}|]\}
where
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu_{1},...,\mu_{N-k})
with
0\leq\mu_{N-k}\leq...\leq\mu_{1}\leq k
.

Similarly, we can show that the functors

{\sf{E}}_{-\boldsymbol{\mu}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N,0)}:={\sf{E}}_{-\mu_{1}}...{\sf{E}}_{-\mu_{N-k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N,0)}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{K}(N,0),\mathcal{K}(k,N-k))

also give the same results like Proposition
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
. Thus
\{{\sf{E}}_{-\boldsymbol{\mu}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N,0)}\}
can also be used to construct a semiorthogonal decomposition of the weight category
\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)
(see Theorem
4.6
), and when the weight categories are
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))
we recover the (dual of the) above dual exceptional collection

\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{E}_{-\mu_{1}}\ast...\ast\mathcal{E}_{-\mu_{N-k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N,0)}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(N,N)\times\mathrm{Gr}(k,N)).

1.5.2.
Characteristic free

The classical Kapranov exceptional collection had been generalized in another direction to the characteristic-free setting by Buchweitz-Leuschke-Van den Bergh
[
5
]
and Efimov
[
13
]
. It would be interesting to see whether our construction can also be applied in a characteristic-free setting.

1.5.3.
Comapre to other categorical actions

Since the notion of categorical actions of Lie algebras/quantum groups (e.g.,
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
or
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
) has been defined before, people may wonder whether those categorical actions can be used to produce semiorthogonal decompositions like the one in this article.

To our best knowledge, it seems like there is only little to know about such a question. Also, some definitions of categorical action only require the weight categories to be abelian, for example,
[
12
]
, where the notion of semiorthogonal decomposition does not exist.

We can address a bit about this question in the case of geometric categorical
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
/
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
(or
\mathfrak{g}
) actions developed by Cautis-Kamnitzer-Liciata in the series of articles
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
9
]
. Note that the main examples of weight categories for such action are the derived categories of coherent sheaves on the cotangent bundle of Grassmannians/partial flag varieties (or Nakajima quiver varieties), and the canonical bundle for those varieties are trivial. This implies that the varieties are Calabi-Yau. Thus their derived categories of coherent sheaves do not have non-trivial semi-orthogonal decompositions.

1.6.
Notations

For a
n
-tuple of positive integer
\underline{k}=(k_{1},...,k_{n})
, we denote
\Bbbk_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}k_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. A Young diagram will always be denoted by a bold symbol
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{k})
. We denote
P(a,b)
to be the set of Young diagrams
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
such that
\lambda_{1}\leq a
and
\lambda_{b+1}=0
.

In this paper, we work over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers. For a vector space
V
, we denote
V^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V,\mathbb{C})
to be the dual space. For a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{G}
on an algebraic variety
X
, we denote
\mathcal{G}^{\vee}=\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{O}_{X})
to be the dual coherent sheaf. Let
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V=N
and
k\leq N
. Then we denote
\mathrm{Gr}(k,V)
to be the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional subspace of
V
and
\mathrm{Gr}(V,k)
to be the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional quotient spaces of
V
. They are related by
\mathrm{Gr}(k,V)=\mathrm{Gr}(V,N-k)
, and
\mathrm{Gr}(k,V)=\mathrm{Gr}(N-k,V^{*})=\mathrm{Gr}(V^{*},k)
. In particular, when
k=1
, we have the two projective space
\mathrm{Gr}(1,V)
,
\mathrm{Gr}(V,1)
parametrizing
1
-dimensional subspace, quotient space, respectively. We use the notations
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sub}}(V)=\mathrm{Gr}(1,V)
and
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(V)=\mathrm{Gr}(V,1)
to distinguish them. Moreover, the above construction can be generalized to vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
of rank
N
on
X
, and similarly, we have the Grassmannian bundles
\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathcal{V})
,
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{V},k)
,…etc on
X
.

Finally, for a smooth projective variety
X
, we denote
\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
to be its bounded derived category of coherent sheaves. All functors between derived categories will be assumed to be derived. For example, we will write
f_{*}
and
\otimes
instead of
Rf_{*}
and
\otimes^{L}
, respectively.

Preliminaries

We briefly recall the definitions of semiorthogonal decompositions, Fourier-Mukai transformations, Beilinson-Kapranov exceptional collections, and the projective bundle formula that will be used in the later sections. We follow the book
[

2.1.
Semiorthogonal decompositions

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-linear triangulated category.

For a full triangulated subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{D}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\perp}=\{X\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D})\ |\ \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(A,X)=0\ \forall\ A\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{A})\},

{}^{\perp}\mathcal{A}=\{X\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D})\ |\ \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,A)=0\ \forall\ A\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{A})\},

to be the right and left orthogonals to
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathcal{D}
, respectively. Those are triangulated subcategories of
\mathcal{D}
.

Given a class
\mathcal{E}
of objects in
\mathcal{D}
, we denoted
\langle\mathcal{E}\rangle
to be the minimal full triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
containing all objects in
\mathcal{E}
and closed under taking direct summands. Similarly for any sequence of full triangulated subcategories
\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}
in
\mathcal{D}
we denote by
\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle
the minimal full triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
which contains all of
\mathcal{A}_{i},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
semiorthogonal decomposition
(
\sf{SOD}
for short) of
\mathcal{D}
is a sequence of full triangulated subcategories
\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}
such that

(1)

there is no non-zero Homs from right to left, i.e.

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(A_{i},A_{j})=0
for all
A_{i}\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
,
A_{j}\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{A}_{j})
where
1\leq j<i\leq n
.

(2)

\mathcal{D}
is generated by
\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}
, i.e. the smallest full triangulated subcategory containing
\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}
is equal to
\mathcal{D}
.

We will use the notation
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle
for a semiorthogonal decomposition of
\mathcal{D}
with components
\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that in some literatures, e.g.
[
8
]
, the authors may require the subcategories
\mathcal{A}_{i}\subset\mathcal{D}
in Definition
2.1
to be strictly full. However, in this article, we do not need this requirement.

Definition 2.3
.

A full triangulated subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{D}
is called
right admissible
if, for the inclusion functor
i:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
, there is a right adjoint
i^{!}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
, and
left admissible
if there is a left adjoint
i^{*}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
. It is called
admissible
if it is both left and right admissible.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
6
]
)
.

If
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\rangle
is a semiorthogonal decomposition, then

\mathcal{A}
is left admissible and B is right admissible. Conversely, if
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{D}
is left admissible, then
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A},^{\perp}\mathcal{A}\rangle
is a semiorthogonal decomposition, and if
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{D}
is right admissible,
then
\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{B}^{\perp},\mathcal{B}\rangle
is a semiorthogonal decomposition.

This lemma has the following generalization.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}
be a semiorthogonal sequence of admissible (full triangulated) subcategories in
\mathcal{D}
; i.e.,
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}_{i},\mathcal{A}_{j})=0
for all
1\leq j<i\leq n
. Then for each
0\leq i\leq n
there is a
\sf{SOD}

\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{i},^{\perp}\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},...,\mathcal{A}_{i}\rangle\cap\langle\mathcal{A}_{i+1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle^{\perp},\mathcal{A}_{i+1},...,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle.

The simplest example of an admissible subcategory is the one generated by an exceptional object.

Definition 2.6
.

An object
E\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D})
is called
exceptional
if

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(E,E[l])=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}&\ \text{if}\ l=0\\
0&\ \text{if}\ l\neq 0.\end{cases}

Then we define the notion of exceptional collections.

Definition 2.7
.

An ordered collection
\{E_{1},...,E_{n}\}
, where
E_{i}\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D})
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, is called an
exceptional collection
if each
E_{i}
is exceptional and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(E_{i},E_{j}[l])=0
for all
i>j
and
\l\in\mathbb{Z}
.

An exceptional collection
\{E_{1},...,E_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{D}
naturally give rise to a
\sf{SOD}
of
\mathcal{D}

\mathcal{D}=\langle\mathcal{A},E_{1},...,E_{n}\rangle

where
\mathcal{A}=\langle E_{1},...,E_{n}\rangle^{\perp}
and
E_{i}
denote the full triangulated subcategory generated by the object
E_{i}
. An exceptional collection is called
full
if the subcategory
\mathcal{A}
is zero.

Finally, we define the notion of a (right) dual exceptional collection.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\{E_{1},...,E_{n}\}
be an exceptional collection on the triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
. An exceptional collection
\{F_{1},...,F_{n}\}
of objects in
\mathcal{D}
is called
right dual
to
\{E_{1},...,E_{n}\}
if

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F_{i},E_{i}[l])=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}&\ \text{if}\ l=0\\
0&\ \text{if}\ l\neq 0\end{cases}

and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F_{i},E_{j}[l])=0
for
i\neq j
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We will see more examples of semiorthogonal decompositions given by exceptional collections in Subsection
2.3
.

2.2.
Fourier-Mukai transformations

Next, we recall the tool of Fourier-Mukai transformations which will be used in the proof of Theorem
5.1
for constructing categorical action.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two smooth projective varieties over
\mathbb{C}
. A
Fourier-Mukai kernel
is an object
\mathcal{P}
of the
bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on
X\times Y
. Given
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(X\times Y)
, we may define
the associated
Fourier-Mukai transform
, which is the functor

\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)

\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mathcal{F}\mapsto\pi_{2*}(\pi_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{P})

where
\pi_{1}
,
\pi_{2}
are natural projections from
X\times Y
to
X
,
Y
respectively.

We call
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}
the Fourier-Mukai transform with (Fourier-Mukai) kernel
\mathcal{P}
. For convenience, we will just write FM for Fourier-Mukai. The first property of FM transforms is that they have left and right adjoints that are themselves FM transforms.

Proposition 2.10

(
[
18
]
Proposition 5.9)
.

For
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)
is the FM transform with kernel
\mathcal{P}
, define

\mathcal{P}_{L}=\mathcal{P}^{\vee}\otimes\pi^{*}_{2}\omega_{Y}[\mathrm{dim}Y],\ \mathcal{P}_{R}=\mathcal{P}^{\vee}\otimes\pi^{*}_{1}\omega_{X}[\mathrm{dim}X].

Then

\Phi_{\mathcal{P}_{L}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(X),\ \Phi_{\mathcal{P}_{R}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)

are the left and right adjoints of
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}
, respectively.

The second property is that the composition of FM transforms is also an FM transform.

Proposition 2.11

(
[
18
]
Proposition 5.10)
.

Let
X,Y,Z
be smooth projective varieties over
\mathbb{C}
. Consider objects
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(X\times Y)
and
\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y\times Z)
. They define FM transforms
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)
,
\Phi_{\mathcal{Q}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(Z)
. We use
\ast
to denote the operation for convolution, i.e.

\mathcal{Q}\ast\mathcal{P}:=\pi_{13*}(\pi_{12}^{*}(\mathcal{P})\otimes\pi_{23}^{*}(\mathcal{Q}))

Then for
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{Q}\ast\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(X\times Z)
, we have
\Phi_{\mathcal{Q}}\circ\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}\cong\Phi_{\mathcal{R}}
.

Remark 2.12
.

Moreover by
[
18
]
remark 5.11, we have
(\mathcal{Q}\ast\mathcal{P})_{L}\cong(\mathcal{P})_{L}\ast(\mathcal{Q})_{L}
and
(\mathcal{Q}\ast\mathcal{P})_{R}\cong(\mathcal{P})_{R}\ast(\mathcal{Q})_{R}
.

2.3.
Beilinson-Kapranov exceptional collections

In this section, we recall the exceptional collections constructed by Beilinson for projective spaces and by Kapranov for partial flag varieties.

The first one is the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{N}=\mathbb{P}_{sub}(\mathbb{C}^{N+1})=\mathrm{Gr}(1,\mathbb{C}^{N+1})
which is due to Beilinson.

Theorem 2.13

(
[
3
]
)
.

There is a full exceptional collection

\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{N})=\langle\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(-N),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(-N+1),...,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}\rangle.

Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})
be a non-increasing sequence of positive integers. We can represent
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
as a Young diagram with
n
rows, aligned on the left, such that the
i
th row
has exactly
\lambda_{i}
cells. The size of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
, denoted by
|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|
, is the number
|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}
. The transpose diagram
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{*}
is obtained by exchanging rows and columns of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
.

Next, for such Young diagrams
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
we define the notion of Schur functors. For more details about Schur functors, we refer the readers to Chapter 4 and chapter 6 in
[
14
]
.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
n\geq 1
be a positive integer and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})
be a sequence of non-increasing positive integers. The
Schur functor

\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
associated to
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is defined as a functor

\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}:\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}

such that for any vector space
V
,
\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}V
coincides with the image of the Young symmetrizer
c_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
in the space of tensors of
V
of rank
n
: i.e.,
\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}V=\text{Im}(c_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}|_{V^{\otimes n}})
.

Let
\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N})=\{0\subset V\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}\ |\ \mathrm{dim}V=k\}
be the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Denote
\mathcal{V}
to be the tautological rank
k
bundle on
\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N})
and
\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V}
to be the tautological rank
N-k
quotient bundle. For non-negative integers
a,\ b\geq 0
, we denote by
P(a,b)
the set of Young diagrams
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
such that
\lambda_{1}\leq a
and
\lambda_{b+1}=0
. Then we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.15

(
[
22
]
)
.

There is a full exceptional collection

\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))=\langle\ \mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}\ \rangle_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in P(N-k,k)}.

Its dual exceptional collection, which is also full, is given by

\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))=\langle\ \mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V}[-|\boldsymbol{\mu}|]\ \rangle_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in P(k,N-k)}.

Here we have to mention the order in the set
P(N-k,k)
used for the semiorthogonal property in the definition of exceptional collections. We denote
<_{\l}
for the lexicographical order. Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{k}),\ \boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}=(\lambda^{\prime}_{1},...,\lambda^{\prime}_{k})\in P(N-k,k)
be two different elements. We say that
\boldsymbol{\lambda}<_{l}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}
if counting from the beginning, the order depends on the first
1\leq j\leq k
such that
\lambda_{j}<\lambda^{\prime}_{j}
. More precisely,
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda^{\prime}_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq k
and there exist
j
such that
\lambda_{j}<\lambda^{\prime}_{j}
. For example, considering the set
P(2,2)
, we have the lexicographical order

(0,0)<_{l}(1,0)<_{l}(1,1)<_{l}(2,0)<_{l}(2,1)<_{l}(2,2).

Since
\mathcal{V}
is the tautological bundle on
\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N})
, the semiorthogonal property for
\{\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}\}
should have the following statement; i.e.,

\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathcal{V},\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}}\mathcal{V})=0

if
\boldsymbol{\lambda}<_{l}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}
. For the right dual exceptional collection
\{\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V}[-|\boldsymbol{\mu}|]\}
the order is opposite to the order on
\{\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}\}
. More precisely, we have

\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V},\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\prime}}\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})=0

if
\boldsymbol{\mu}>_{\l}\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\prime}
where
\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\prime}\in P(k,N-k)
.

Moving to the partial flag varieties, for
\underline{k}=(k_{1},...,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}=N
, the partial flag variety is defined as follows

\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}):=\{V_{\bullet}=(0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset...\subset V_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{N})\ |\ \mathrm{dim}V_{i}/V_{i-1}=k_{i}\ \text{for}\ \text{all}\ i\}.

Then we have the following generalization.

Theorem 2.16

(
[
23
]
)
.

There is a full exceptional collection

\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}))=\langle\ \bigotimes_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)}\mathcal{V}_{i}\ \rangle_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)\in P(k_{i+1},\Bbbk_{i}),\ 1\leq i\leq n-1}.

Remark 2.17
.

The order in the partial flag variety case is the product lexicographical order on

P(k_{2},k_{1})\times...\times P(k_{i+1},k_{1}+..+k_{i})\times...\times P(k_{n},k_{1}+..+k_{n-1}),

which is denoted by
<_{pl}
, where ”pl” stands for product lexicographical. More precisely, we have
(\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1),...,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1))<_{pl}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)^{\prime},...,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)^{\prime})
if there exists
1\leq i\leq n-1
such that
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(j)=\boldsymbol{\lambda}(j)^{\prime}
for all
0\leq j\leq i-1
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)<_{l}\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)^{\prime}
, where
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i),\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)^{\prime}\in P(k_{i+1},\Bbbk_{i})
.

Remark 2.18
.

Note that for the partial flag varieties, we do not have the right dual exceptional collection in Theorem
2.16
. It was pointed out in
[
23
]
that there are objects that satisfy the Definition
2.8
for dual exceptional collection; however, they do have higher
\mathrm{Ext}
’s between themself.

2.4.
Projective bundle formula

The final tool we will need in later sections is the projective bundle formula. Let
\mathcal{V}
be a vector bundle of rank
n
on a (smooth) algebraic variety
X
, where
n\geq 2
. Then we can form the projectivization
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(\mathcal{V})
which parametrizes 1-dimensional quotient bundle of
\mathcal{V}
. Note that from our notation we have
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(\mathcal{V})=\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{V},1)=\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{V}^{\vee},1)=\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sub}}(\mathcal{V}^{\vee})
. We denote
\pi:\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(\mathcal{V})\rightarrow X
to be the projection which is a
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
-fibration.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(\mathcal{V})}(-1)
be the tautological bundle and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(\mathcal{V})}(1)
be the dual bundle, and we define
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(\mathcal{V})}(i):=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(\mathcal{V})}(1)^{\otimes i}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then we have the following result.

Proposition 2.19

(Exercise 8.4 in Chapter 3 from
[
16
]
)
.

\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(\mathcal{V})}(i)\cong\begin{cases}\mathrm{Sym}^{i}(\mathcal{V})&\text{if}\ i\geq 0\\
0&\text{if}\ 1-n\leq i\leq-1\\
\mathrm{Sym}^{-i-n}(\mathcal{V}^{\vee})\otimes\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{V})^{-1}[1-n]&\text{if}\ i\leq-n\end{cases}.

The above result is generalized by Yu Zhao
[
36
]
to a two-term complex of locally free sheaves. More precisely, let
\mathcal{U}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
which admits a two-term locally free resolution, i.e.
\mathcal{W}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{U}\rightarrow 0
where
\mathcal{W}
,
\mathcal{V}
are locally free sheaves on
X
. We denote the rank of
\mathcal{U}
to be
u=\text{rank}\mathcal{U}=\text{rank}\mathcal{V}-\text{rank}\mathcal{W}\geq 1
.

Similarly we have the projectivization
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(\mathcal{U})
which is a closed subscheme of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(\mathcal{V})
, and we denote
\pi_{\mathcal{U}}:\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(\mathcal{U})\rightarrow X
to be the projection. We also have the tautological bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(\mathcal{U})}(-1)
and its tensor power
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(\mathcal{U})}(i)
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then the above projective bundle formula can be generalized to the following.

Proposition 2.20

(Lemma 3.9 in
[
36
]
)
.

\pi_{\mathcal{U}*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{quo}}(\mathcal{U})}(i)\cong\begin{cases}\mathrm{Sym}^{i}(\mathcal{U})&\text{if}\ i\geq 0\\
0&\text{if}\ 1-u\leq i\leq-1\\
\wedge^{-i-u}(\mathcal{U}^{\vee})\otimes\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{U})^{-1}[1-u]&\text{if}\ i\leq-u\end{cases}.

Remark 2.21
.

More recently, the above proposition has been generalized by Qingyuan Jiang
[
20
]
to the derived algebraic geometry setting, i.e.
X
is a derived scheme or prestack, which is called the generalized Serre theorem. Since we will not need such a general version in our proof, we refer the reader to Theorem 5.6 in
loc. cit.
.

3.
Shifted
q=0
affine algebras and its categorical action

In this section, we recall the definitions of the shifted
q=0
affine algebra
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0.N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
and its categorical action. We also mention the result where there is a categorical action on the bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves on partial flag varieties.

Before we go to the detailed setup and the definition of the shifted
q=0
affine algebra, we should give the readers some background for it. The main motivation comes from the study of categorical action and categorification of the quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
and its idempotent version
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, where
\mathfrak{g}
a semisimple or Kac-Moody Lie algebra.

We restrict to the case
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and keep in mind that all the results can be naturally generalized to the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
case. Based on the work
[
4
]
, Beilinson-Lusztig-MacPherson give a geometric model for
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
. This can be used to construct categorical
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-action.

The weight space
V_{\lambda}
is replaced by the weight category
\mathcal{C}(\lambda)=\mathcal{D}^{b}Con(\mathrm{Gr}(k,N))
, which is the bounded derived category of constructible sheaves on
\mathrm{Gr}(k,N)
with
\lambda=N-2k
. The generators
e
,
f
act on them by using the following correspondence diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{Fl}(k-1,k)=\{0\overset{k-1}{\subset}V^{\prime}\overset{1}{\subset}V\overset{N-k}{\subset}\mathbb{C}^{N}\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{p_{2}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N})}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Gr}(k-1,\mathbb{C}^{N})}

(3.1)

where
\mathrm{Fl}(k-1,k)
is the 3-step partial flag variety and
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
are the natural projections. Then we define

{\sf{E}}:=p_{2*}p^{*}_{1}
and a similar functor
{\sf{F}}
in the opposite direction that can be viewed as lift of
e
and
f
, respectively. The functors
{\sf{E}}
,
{\sf{F}}
satisfy the defining relations of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
, i.e., we have

{\sf{E}\sf{F}}|_{\mathcal{C}(\lambda)}\cong{\sf{F}\sf{E}}|_{\mathcal{C}(\lambda)}\bigoplus\bigoplus_{[\lambda]}\text{Id}_{\mathcal{C}(\lambda)}\ \text{if}\ \lambda\geq 0,

similarly for
\lambda\leq 0
. Here
[n]:=q^{-n+1}+q^{-n+3}+....+q^{n-3}+q^{n-1}
is the quantum integer and
\bigoplus_{[\lambda]}(.)
denotes a graded direct sum. For example,
\bigoplus_{[2]}f=f[1]\bigoplus f[-1]
, where
[1]
is the homological shift in the derived category that uses to keep track of the power of the variable
q
.

Motivated by the above construction, we now consider the weight categories to be
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))
, i.e., we replace the constructible sheaves by coherent sheaves. Let
\mathcal{V},\ \mathcal{V}^{\prime}
to be the tautological bundles on
Fl(k-1,k)
of rank
k
,
k-1
respectively. Then there is a natural line bundle
\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
on
Fl(k-1,k)
. Using the same correspondence (
3.1
), instead of just pulling back and pushing forward directly, we have an extra twist by the line bundles
(\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime})^{r}
where
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. So we have the functors

{\sf{E}}_{r}:=p_{2*}(p_{1}^{*}\otimes(\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime})^{r}):\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k-1,\mathbb{C}^{N}))

and similarly
{\sf{F}}_{r}
where
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The shifted
q=0
affine algebra arises naturally from the studies of the
L\mathfrak{sl}_{2}:=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]
-like algebra acting on
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))
, where
e\otimes t^{r}
and
f\otimes t^{s}
acting via the functors
{\sf{E}}_{r}
,
{\sf{F}}_{s}
respectively for
r,\ s\in\mathbb{Z}
. In
[
17
]
, the author is the first one to study this action in detail.

After decategorifying (pass to the K-theory), we obtain an algebra with loop generators
e_{r}
,
f_{s}
and relations that look similar to the shifted quantum affine algebra defined in
[
15
]
. There is a variable
q
in their definition that stands for the
q
-deformation of quantum affine algebra. From the geometric aspect, it comes from the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action. In our case, we do not have a natural
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on Grassmannians, so we do not have a variable like
q
.

We call the resulting algebra shifted
q=0
affine algebra. First, the ”
q=0
” part in the name comes from the reason that some of the relations can be obtained from the relations in shifted quantum affine algebra by taking
q=0
directly. Second, the ”shifted” comes from the fact that the commutator relations between
e_{r}
and
f_{s}
vanish within a certain range of
r+s
. Finally, even though the algebra is given by the loop generators
e_{r}
,
f_{s}
, the representation
\bigoplus_{k}K(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))
we study is finite-dimensional. Since affine algebras are central extensions of loop algebras, and central extension acts trivially on the finite-dimensional representations, we still use ”affine algebra” in the name.

3.1.
Shifted
q=0
affine algebras

In this section, we define the shifted
q=0
affine algebras. By imitating the presentation in
[
15
]
(the so-called Levedorskii type presentation), the presentation we use here is by finite numbers of generators and defining relations. In
[
17
]
, we also give a conjectural presentation defined by generating series and conjecture that the two presentations are equivalent; see conjecture A.2. in
loc. cit.

3.2.
Categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})

In this section, we recall the definition of the categorical action for shifted
q=0
affine algebra that is defined in
[
17
]
. However, since most of the relations in the categorical action will not be used for the proof of our results, we only list those that will be used and call such action a
partial categorical action
.
We refer the readers to Definition 3.1. in
loc. cit.
for a full definition of the categorical action.

Definition 3.2
.

A
partial categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
action
consists of a target 2-category
\mathcal{K}
, which is triangulated,
\mathbb{C}
-linear and idempotent complete. The objects in
\mathcal{K}
are

\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{K})=\{\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})\ |\ \underline{k}\in C(n,N)\}

where each
\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})
is a triangulated category, and
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{K}(\underline{k}),\mathcal{K}(\underline{l}))
is also a triangulated category for all
\underline{k},\underline{l}\in C(n,N)
. On those objects we equip with the following 1-morphisms
\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}
,
{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}=\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}+\alpha_{i}}{\sf{E}}_{i,r}
,
{\sf{F}}_{i,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}=\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}-\alpha_{i}}{\sf{F}}_{i,s}
,
({\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm}_{i})^{\pm 1}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}=\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}({\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm}_{i})^{\pm 1}
,
{\sf{H}}_{i,\pm 1}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}=\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}{\sf{H}}_{i,\pm 1}
where
1\leq i\leq n-1
,
-k_{i}-1\leq r\leq 0
,
0\leq s\leq k_{i+1}+1
. Here
\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}
is the identity 1-morphism of
\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})
.

On this data, we impose the following conditions

(1)

The space of maps between any two 1-morphisms is finite-dimensional.

(2)

If
\alpha=\alpha_{i}
or
\alpha=\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}
for some
i,j
with
\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=-1
, then
\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}+r\alpha}=0
for
r\gg 0
or
r\ll 0
.

(3)

Suppose
i\neq j
. If
\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}+\alpha_{i}}
and
\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}+\alpha_{j}}
are nonzero, then
\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}
and
\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}+\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}}
are also nonzero.

(4)

The right adjoint of
{\sf{E}}_{i,r}
and
{\sf{F}}_{i,s}
are given by conjugation of
\sf{\Psi}^{\pm}_{i}
up to homological shifts. More precisely,

(a)

({\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}})^{R}\cong\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}{({\sf{\Psi}}^{+}_{i})^{r+1}}{\sf{F}}_{i,k_{i+1}+2}({\sf{\Psi}}^{+}_{i})^{-r-2}[-r-1]
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
,

(b)

({\sf{F}}_{i,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}})^{R}\cong\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-}_{i})^{-s+1}{\sf{E}}_{i,-k_{i}-2}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-}_{i})^{s-2}[s-1]
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

(5)

The relations between
{\sf{E}}_{i,r}
,
{\sf{F}}_{i,s}
,
\Psi^{\pm}_{i}
are given by the following

(a)

{\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm}_{i}{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\cong{\sf{E}}_{i,r+1}{\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}[\mp 1]
.

(b)

{\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm}_{i}{\sf{F}}_{i,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\cong{\sf{F}}_{i,s-1}{\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}[\pm 1]
.

(6)

For
{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{F}}_{i,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}},{\sf{F}}_{i,s}{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{K}(\underline{k}),\mathcal{K}(\underline{k}))
, they are related by exact triangles, more precisely,

(a)

{\sf{F}}_{i,s}{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\rightarrow{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{F}}_{i,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\rightarrow{\sf{\Psi}}^{+}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\ \text{if}\ r+s=k_{i+1},

(b)

{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{F}}_{i,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\rightarrow{\sf{F}}_{i,s}{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\rightarrow{\sf{\Psi}}^{-}_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\ \text{if}\ r+s=-k_{i},

(c)

{\sf{F}}_{i,s}{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\cong{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{F}}_{i,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\ \text{if}\ -k_{i}+1\leq r+s\leq k_{i+1}-1.

for all
r
,
s
that make the above conditions make sense, and the isomorphisms between functors that appear in every condition are abstractly defined, i.e., we do not specify any 2-morphisms that induce those isomorphisms. Also, we do not require any conditions on
{\sf{H}}_{i,\pm 1}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}
.

Geometric example

In this section, we mention the partial main result, i.e., Theorem 5.2 in

1

)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}

\underline{k}\in C(n,N)

4.
Main results

In this section, we prove the main result of this article and state the consequence. Roughly speaking, it says that certain functors in the categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
satisfy the semiorthogonal property, and thus give rise to
\sf{SOD}
of each weight category.

4.1.
Main theorem

The following is the main result of this article.

Theorem 4.1
.

Given a partial categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
action
\mathcal{K}
. Considering the functors

{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{K}(\eta),\mathcal{K}(\underline{k}))

where
{\sf{F}}_{i,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)}:={\sf{F}}_{i,\lambda(i)_{1}}{\sf{F}}_{i,\lambda(i)_{2}}...{\sf{F}}_{i,\lambda(i)_{\Bbbk_{i}}}
with
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)=(\lambda(i)_{1},...,\lambda(i)_{\Bbbk_{i}})\in P(k_{i+1},\Bbbk_{i})
,
\Bbbk_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}k_{j}
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
, and
\eta=(0,0,...,0,N)
be the highest weight. Then they satisfy the following properties

(1)

\mathrm{Hom}({\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta},{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta})\cong\mathrm{Hom}(\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta},\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta})
,

(2)

\mathrm{Hom}({\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta},{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)^{\prime}}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)^{\prime}}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta})\cong 0
if
(\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1),...,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1))<_{pl}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)^{\prime},...,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)^{\prime})
where
<_{pl}
denotes the product lexicographic order; i.e., there exists
1\leq i\leq n-1
such that
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(j)=\boldsymbol{\lambda}(j)^{\prime}
for all
0\leq j\leq i-1
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)<_{l}\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)^{\prime}
.

Note that the first property is not the same as the definition of
relative exceptional objects
defined in
[
2
]
(see Remark
5.6
for an explanation), and the second property implies that the collection of such functors forms a semiorthogonal collection.

Moreover, there are two direct consequences that are easy to see. The first one is each functor
{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta}
is fully-faithful. The second one is the subcategories that are generated by the essential image of the collection of such functors giving rise to a
\sf{SOD}
of the weight category
\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})
.

Instead of stating these (abstract) corollaries first, we decide to let the readers know some concrete examples. By Theorem
3.3
there is a partial categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})

n
-step partial flag varieties.

So now the weight categories are
\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})=\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}))
. We also know that each functor
{\sf{F}}_{i,s}
is given by an FM kernel
\mathcal{F}_{i,s}
. By Proposition
2.11
, the composition of functors is given by the convolution of FM kernels. Considering
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)=(\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)_{1},...,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)_{\Bbbk_{i}})\in P(k_{i+1},\Bbbk_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
, then we obtain

\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)}\mathcal{V}_{i}\cong\mathcal{F}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}\ast\mathcal{F}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}\ast...\ast\mathcal{F}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Fl}_{\eta}(\mathbb{C}^{N})\times\mathrm{Fl}_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N})),

(4.1)

where
\mathcal{F}_{i,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)}:=\mathcal{F}_{i,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)_{1}}\ast\mathcal{F}_{i,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)_{2}}...\ast\mathcal{F}_{i,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)_{\Bbbk_{i}}}
and
\eta:=(0,0,...,0,N)
is the highest weight.

Note that when
n=2
and
(k_{1},k_{2})=(k,N-k)

4.1
) becomes (

which is the Kapranov exceptional collection for Grassmannians

\mathbb{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathcal{V}\cong\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{1}}\ast...\ast\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(0,\mathbb{C}^{N})\times\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N})).

From Theorem

4.2.
Proof of the main theorem

We prove Theorem
4.1
in this section. Since the result is about
\mathrm{Hom}
space, the idea of the proof is taking the right adjoint of
{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta}
and calculate compositions with other functors. More explicitly, we calculate

\displaystyle\begin{split}&({\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)})^{R}{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)^{\prime}}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)^{\prime}}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta}\\
&\cong({\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)})^{R}...({\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)})^{R}({\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)})^{R}{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)^{\prime}}{\sf{F}}_{2,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(2)^{\prime}}...{\sf{F}}_{n-1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta}\end{split}

(4.2)

for all
\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i),\ \boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)^{\prime}\in P(k_{i+1},\Bbbk_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq n-1
. We show that
(\ref{adcom})\cong\boldsymbol{1}_{\eta}
if
(\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1),...,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1))=(\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)^{\prime},...,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)^{\prime})

(\ref{adcom})\cong 0
if
(\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1),...,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1))<_{pl}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)^{\prime},...,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(n-1)^{\prime})
. Then the result follows directly.

The first step we have to calculate is the composition
({\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)})^{R}{\sf{F}}_{1,\boldsymbol{\lambda}(1)^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,k_{1}+k_{2},k_{3},...,k_{n})}

(0,k_{1}+k_{2},k_{3},...,k_{n})

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

Theorem 4.2
.

Given a partial categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})

5.
Application to Grassmannian of coherent sheaves

Since the Kapranov exceptional collection is well-known, we would like to see examples (besides the usual Grassmanninas and partial flag varieties) where we can apply the above results to get
\sf{SOD}
s. Also, we believe that our result about the categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
on
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{C}^{N}))
can be easily extended to a categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
on Grassmannian bundles, i.e.
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathcal{E}))
where
\mathcal{E}
is a locally free sheaf of rank
N
on a smooth projective variety.

Thus the next possible example we consider is a generalization to Grassmannians (more precisely, relative Quot schemes) of coherent sheaves of homological dimension
\leq 1
. Let
X
be a connected smooth projective variety and
\mathscr{G}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
of homological dimension
\leq 1
, i.e. such that there is a locally free resolution
0\rightarrow\mathcal{E}^{-1}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}^{0}\rightarrow\mathscr{G}\rightarrow 0
. In order to reduce the notations, we still denote the rank of
\mathscr{G}
to be
N\coloneqq\text{rk}\mathcal{E}^{0}-\text{rk}\mathcal{E}^{-1}\geq 2
. Then we consider the Grassmannians/relative Quot scheme
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k)
of rank
k
locally free quotients of
\mathscr{G}
. By Proposition A.1 in
[
1
]
,
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k)
is smooth, thus we can consider its bounded derived category of coherent sheaves
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k))
.

In this section, we apply the main results (in particular Theorem
4.2
and
4.6
) to obtain a
\sf{SOD}
on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k))
. We prove it by defining certain functors
{\sf{E}}_{r}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N-k,k)}
,
{\sf{F}}_{s}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N-k,k)}
via using a correspondence like diagram (
3.1
) and show that they satisfy the conditions in Definition
3.2
.

Consider the following correspondence

\textstyle{\mathrm{Fl}(\mathscr{G},k+1,k)=\{\mathscr{G}\overset{N-k-1}{\twoheadrightarrow}\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\overset{1}{\twoheadrightarrow}\mathcal{W}\overset{k}{\rightarrow}0\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{p_{2}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k)}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k+1)}

(5.1)

where
\mathrm{Fl}(\mathscr{G},k+1,k)
is the 3-step partial flag variety which parametrizing successive locally free quotients of rank
k+1
,
k
of the coherent sheaf
\mathscr{G}
. The numbers above the arrow indicate the decreasing of ranks, and
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
are the natural projections.

Let
\pi:\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k)\rightarrow X
be the natural projection. Then
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k)
carries the following tautological short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\pi}\rightarrow\pi^{*}\mathscr{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{\pi}\rightarrow 0

(5.2)

where
\mathcal{Q}_{\pi}
is the universal quotient bundle whereas the universal subsheaf
\mathcal{S}_{\pi}
may not be locally free. Similarly for
\pi^{\prime}:\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k+1)\rightarrow X
. Pulling back the universal quotient bundles
\mathcal{Q}_{\pi}
,
\mathcal{Q}_{\pi^{\prime}}
in the correspondence (
5.1
), we obtain the surjective morphism
\rho:p^{*}_{2}\mathcal{Q}_{\pi^{\prime}}\twoheadrightarrow p^{*}_{1}\mathcal{Q}_{\pi}
of tautological quotient bundles on
\mathrm{Fl}(\mathscr{G},k+1,k)
. Taking the kernel we get the line bundle, denoted by
\ker(\rho)
, on
\mathrm{Fl}(\mathscr{G},k+1,k)
. Then we define the following functors

{\sf{E}}_{r}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N-k,k)}\coloneqq p_{2*}(p^{*}_{1}\otimes(\ker(\rho))^{r}):\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k+1))

with
-N+k\leq r\leq 0
and similarly for
{\sf{F}}_{s}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N-k,k)}
with
0\leq s\leq k
in the opposite direction.

To check that the functors defined above satisfy the conditions in Definition
3.2
, it remains to define the functors
{\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N-k,k)}
on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k))
. There are two natural determinant line bundle on
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k)
, one is
\det(\mathcal{Q}_{\pi})
, the other is
\det(\mathcal{S}_{\pi})
. Here we use the tautological short exact sequence (
5.2
) and
\mathscr{G}
admits a two-term locally free resolution to get

\det(\mathcal{S}_{\pi})\cong\pi^{*}\det(\mathscr{G})\otimes\det(\mathcal{Q}_{\pi})^{-1}\cong\pi^{*}(\det(\mathcal{E}^{0})\otimes\det(\mathcal{E}^{-1})^{-1})\otimes\det(\mathcal{Q}_{\pi})^{-1}.

Then we define

\displaystyle{\sf{\Psi}}^{+}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N-k,k)}\coloneqq\otimes\det(\mathcal{Q}_{\pi})[1-k]:\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k)),

\displaystyle{\sf{\Psi}}^{-}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N-k,k)}\coloneqq\otimes\det(\mathcal{S}_{\pi})^{-1}[1+k-N]:\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathscr{G},k)).

It is easy to see that both the functors
{\sf{\Psi}}^{+}
,
{\sf{\Psi}}^{-}
are invertible.

Then we state the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.1
.

The functors
{\sf{E}}_{r}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N-k,k)}

{\sf{F}}_{s}\boldsymbol{1}_{(N-k,k)}

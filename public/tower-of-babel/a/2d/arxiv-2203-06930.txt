Parametrix construction and numerical approximation of resonances of azimuthal harmonics of the charged Klein-Gordon operator on general cosmological slowly accelerating and rotating charged black hole type spacetimes Thanks: Université Paris Saclay, Département de Mathématiques d’Orsay, F-91405 Orsay Cedex, France. E–mail: nicolas.besset@universite-paris-saclay.fr
Nicolas Besset

Abstract
. We show the index 0 Fredholm property of the spectral family of the azimuthal harmonics of the charged Klein-Gordon operator on general cosmological slowly accelerating and rotating charged black hole type spacetimes, including the De Sitter-Kerr-Newman family, using a parametrix construction for abstract totally characteristic operators. We then present a numerical scheme to compute the meromorphic poles of the inverse of the spectral family and provide an explicit estimate of the numerical error.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Resonances and decay of the local energy

Long time behaviour of waves in curved spacetimes is an important first step in the analysis of stability of the underlying metric (
cf.

[
HV
]
for the non-linear stability of De Sitter-Kerr metric and
[
HHV
]
for the linear stability of Kerr spacetime). It is also a prerequisite in proving asymptotic completeness for wave type equations (
cf.

[
HN
]
for massless Dirac fields on Kerr metric,
[
GGH
]
for scalar waves on De Sitter-Kerr type metrics and
[
B2
]
for charged Klein-Gordon (KG) fields on the De Sitter-Reissner-Nordström metric when the charged product is sufficiently small).

Similarly to solutions of the wave equation on a compact spatial domain expend as a sum over the (discrete set of) eigenvalues of the spatial Laplacian, we know since the work of Bony-Häfner
[
BoHa
]
that scalar waves on cosmological uncharged spherical black hole metric with compactly supported inital data also have a local expansion of the form

(1)

\displaystyle\chi u(t,\bullet)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\sigma_{j}\in\mathrm{Res}}\\
{\mathrm{Im}(\sigma_{j})>-C}\end{subarray}}\sum_{k=0}^{m(\sigma_{j})}t^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\sigma_{j}t}u_{j,k}+\widetilde{u},\qquad\|\widetilde{u}(t,\bullet)\|\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-Ct}.

Above
t>0
is the time variable,
C>0
and
\chi
is a smooth spatial cut-off; the equation holds in some energy space which the norm
\|\bullet\|
is associated to. The special frequencies appearing in the expansion are called
quasinormal modes
: they are complex frequencies, proper to the black hole and the ambient spacetime, that describe late time oscillation and damping of waves. They are not the eigenvalues of the spatial part of the wave operator as the associated eigenvectors fail in general to be in the appropriate
L^{2}
space (whence the appellation
quasi
normal modes).

Theory of resonances provides a spectral definition of these quasinormal modes (and then gives a precise description of the long time behaviour of wave type fields) when the resolvent of the operator describing the underlying dynamics has sufficiently nice decay at high frequencies.
Resonances
are defined as those frequencies
\sigma\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that a related operator
P_{\sigma}
(the spectral family, see Sect.
3.3
below) is invertible between appropriate functional spaces. Thus resonances and quasinormal modes coincide provided
\|P_{\sigma}^{-1}\|
(when it exists) decays sufficiently fast as
\mathrm{Re}(\sigma)\to\pm\infty
, as we can then shift contour integration and use the residue theorem to obtain the expansion (
1
) with resonances (for a very nice and complete presentation of the mathematical theory of resonances, we refer the reader to
[
DZ
]
). Non trivial elements in the kernel of
P_{\sigma}
are called
resonant states
and those of the dual operator
P_{\sigma}^{*}
are called
dual states
; the term

(k+1)
dimensional vector space spanned by the resonant states associated to the resonance
\sigma_{j}
with
k\leq m(\sigma_{j})
,

being the multiplicity of the resonance
\sigma_{j}
(see
[
BoHa
, eq. (1.10) & eq. (1.11)]
for details for the wave equation on the De Sitter-Schwarzschild metric). Resonance expansions have first been obtained for (massive) waves on De Sitter-Kerr spacetimes by Dyatlov
[
D
]
; we refer to
[
H3
, Sect. 1.1]
for more references in this context; see also
[

for charged KG fields on De Sitter-Reissner-Nordström spacetimes. Notice from (

[
BeHa
]
).

Existence of resonances for (massive) scalar waves in perturbations of De Sitter-Kerr spacetimes has been proved by the seminal work of Vasy
[
V
]
who cast the theory of resonances in the robust framework of analytic Fredholm theory for non elliptic operators. Notice however that the operator
P_{\sigma}
in
[
V
]
is defined on an enlarged spatial domain and a complex potential supported beyond the horizons is added, so that resonances are created beyond the horizons; however, in a cut-off sense, that is in the physical outer domain of the spacetime, they do not depend on the choice of the extended domain nor on the absorbing potential (as this potential is microlocally supported above sets lying beyond the horizons of the spacetime). By the index 0 analytic Fredholm theory, they are thus characterized as the poles of the meromorphic extension from
\{\sigma\in{\mathbb{C}}\ |\ \mathrm{Im}(\sigma)\gg 0\}
to some larger domain in
{\mathbb{C}}
of the cut-off resolvent

(here

[

(used by Sá Barreto-Zworski
[
SZ

then Bony-Häfner
[
BoHa
]
to define resonances).

In this paper, we propose to define resonances of the azimuthal harmonics of charged KG fields on general cosmological spacetimes, including the De Sitter-Kerr-Newman family, using a parametrix construction. We will not deal with semiclassical asymptotics in very high energy regimes. As in
[
V
]
, resonances are the poles of the meromorphic extension of the inverse of the spectral family associated to the charged KG operator, the meromorphic extension being constructed by analytic Fredholm theory. We do not only extend
[
V

(without the trapping statement) to more general settings, but we also make numerical computations of resonances possible; on the other hand, we only consider harmonics of the charged KG operators and work on Sobolev spaces with integer orders (in particular, we do not discuss how and why the order

is critical for the Fredholm theory, we refer the reader to
[
V
]
). The parametrix construction and the underlying analytic Fredholm theory are presented in an abstract setting in Sect.
2

e.g.

[
CCDHJ
]
on De Sitter-Reissner-Nordström spacetimes and, more recently,
[
HX1
]
on De Sitter and
[
HX2
]
on De Sitter-Schwarzschild spacetimes); to our knowledge, the approach in the present paper provides for the very first time a scheme with an explicit estimate of the numerical error for a very large class of cosmological spacetimes. We will only present this scheme and derive the numerical error in a purely theoretical point of view; we plan to explicitly apply it in a future work.

1.3.
Outline of the method

While some parts in this paper use tedious computations, the overall method is simple.

In the quite general setting of Sect.
3.1
, the restriction to azimuthal harmonics of the charged KG operator is
totally characteristic
. An operator is said to be totally characteristic in some domain if its Fourier principal symbol vanishes at the boundary of that domain – in other words, the operator is elliptic inside the domain but its characteristic set is not empty on the boundary. The lack of ellipticity prevents us from using the standard construction of parametrices; however, in the Mellin quantization sense, the operator becomes uniformly elliptic and an explicit (thus workable for numerics) construction is now possible. This construction amounts to first produce a decay in the fiber direction, then in the basis direction of the cotangent bundle in order to obtain compact remainder.

We then deduce the Fredholm property of the spectral family
\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}
,
\sigma\in{\mathbb{C}}
, associated to the (harmonic) charged KG operator outside critical strips of frequencies in
{\mathbb{C}}
; this restriction comes from the existence of real roots of indicial polynomials associated to
\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}
. This amounts to working separately near each radial boundary of the spacetime in a Mellin setting, construct local parametrices there, then glue them together. As a rough summary:

\displaystyle\text{Fourier ellipticity}\qquad\implies\qquad\text{Fredholm property},

\displaystyle\text{Mellin ellipticity }+\text{ restriction outside critical strips}\qquad\implies\qquad\text{Fredholm property}.

In the last case, we will see that we need to lose one derivative with respect to the standard Fourier ellipticity; another noticeable difference is the role of the
subprincipal part
of the spectral family in the frequency restriction (
cf.
Rmk.
12
below where
b(0)
belongs to the subprincipal part of
\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}
).

Having established the equation
\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}\boldsymbol{Q}_{\sigma}=\mathds{1}+\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma}
for some suitable
\boldsymbol{Q}_{\sigma}
and
\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma}
, we can push the spectral analysis of the latter further and show its trace class property. As a result, the
Fredholm determinant

\det(\mathds{1}+\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma})
is well-defined and can be approximated by
\det(\Pi_{R}(\mathds{1}+\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma}))
where
\Pi_{R}
projects onto a
R
dimensional linear space; furthermore, the determinant cancels if and only if
\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma}
has a non trivial kernel
3
3

3

The power of the Fredholm theory is to allow one to apply the standard linear algebra results in finite dimension to problems in infinite dimension.
. If we manage to show that
\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}
has index 0 (that is both the dimensions of its kernel and cokernel coincide), then

\displaystyle\sigma\text{ is a resonance}\ \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\det(\mathds{1}+\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma})=0.

As
\sigma\mapsto\det(\mathds{1}+\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma})
is analytic for
\sigma
outside the critical strips, we can finally use complex integrals of
\det(\Pi_{R}(\mathds{1}+\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma}))
(which is the determinant of a matrix) to approximate or count the number of resonances inside a fixed domain in
{\mathbb{C}}
.

1.4.
Quantizations

We introduce in this section the two types of quantization that we will use in this paper.

The Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},\mathrm{d}x))
and its inverse are defined by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}x\xi}u(x)\mathrm{d}x,\qquad\qquad\mathcal{F}^{-1}[v](x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}x\xi}v(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi.

The Mellin transform
\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{B}\left(L^{2}\left((0,+\infty),\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}\right),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},\mathrm{d}\xi)\right)
and its inverse are defined by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}[u](\xi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\rho^{-\mathrm{i}\xi}u(\rho)\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho},\qquad\qquad\mathcal{M}^{-1}[v](\rho)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{\mathrm{i}\xi}v(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi.

The change of variable
\rho:=\mathrm{e}^{x}
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, allows us to transpose properties of Fourier type quantization to Mellin type quantization
4
4

4

In particular, that
\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{B}\left(L^{2}\left((0,+\infty),\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}\right),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},\mathrm{d}\xi)\right)
is simply a consequence of Plancherel formula. We should by the way talk respectively about the Fourier-Plancherel and Mellin-Plancherel extensions of the standard Fourier and Mellin transforms.
. Setting
v=u\circ\exp
, we have
\mathcal{M}[u]=\mathcal{F}[v]
and
(\rho\partial_{\rho}u)(\rho)=(\partial_{x}v)(x)
5
5

5

From the spectral perspective, Mellin type quantization associates
\rho D_{\rho}
to
\xi
,
i.e.
totally characteristic operators near a boundary are in fact Mellin elliptic.
; in particular,

\displaystyle u\in L^{2}\left((0,+\infty),\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}\right)\qquad

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\qquad v\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},\mathrm{d}x).

Note however that this equivalence fails to hold true between Sobolev spaces, as the above change of variable modifies the differential structures (in short,
\partial_{\rho}
is not equivalent to
\partial_{x}
). Thus, when dealing with Mellin type pseudo-differential operators
H^{k^{\prime}}\left((0,+\infty),\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}\right)\to H^{k}\left((0,+\infty),\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}\right)
with
k,k^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
k^{\prime}\geq k
, we will first take
(k^{\prime}-k)
derivatives (with respect to the variable
\rho\in(0,+\infty)
) then we will change the variable to work with a Fourier type pseudo-differential operator acting on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},\mathrm{d}x)
(this will be used in the proof of Lem.
18
below).

Let
a\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}((0,+\infty)_{\rho}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\omega},{\mathbb{C}})
. The mixed Mellin-Fourier quantization of
a
is by definition the following operator:

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Op}_{\mathcal{M},\mathcal{F}}[a]u)(\rho,\omega)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\xi}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\eta}}\rho^{\mathrm{i}\xi}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega\cdot\eta}a(\rho,\omega,\xi,\eta)\left(\int_{(0,+\infty)_{y}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\zeta}}y^{-\mathrm{i}\xi}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\zeta\cdot\eta}u(y,\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}\xi.

As explained above, we can see this operator as a full Fourier type quantization on
{\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\omega}
. We refer the reader to
e.g.

[
Hö
, Chap. XVIII]
for the basic properties (depending on

) of these pseudo-differential operators acting on Sobolev spaces.

2.
General boundary parametrix construction

We construct in this section local (
i.e.
near a boundary) parametrices for abstract totally characteristic operators in the case where the domain has a radial symmetry, using Mellin type quantization in the radial direction.

2.1.
Definition of the functional framework and main results of the section

Let
(k,l,\sigma)\in\mathbb{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{C}}
and let
\mathcal{M}
be a
n
dimensional compact smooth manifold. Let
(\phi_{\bullet})_{\bullet}
be a smooth partition of unity of
\mathcal{M}
then choose
\widetilde{\phi}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M},[0,1])
such that
\widetilde{\phi}_{\bullet}\phi_{\bullet}=\phi_{\bullet}
on
\mathrm{Supp}(\phi_{\bullet})
; in the sequel, we will identify
\mathcal{M}\cap\mathrm{Supp}(\widetilde{\phi}_{\bullet})
with an open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Let also
\chi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,+\infty),[0,1])
such that

(2)

\displaystyle\chi(\rho)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\rho\leq\rho_{0}$},\\
0&\text{if $\rho\geq\rho_{0}^{\prime}$}\end{cases}

for some
0<\rho_{0}<\rho_{0}^{\prime}
. In applications,
\sigma
is a spectral parameter and
\mathcal{M}
is the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Let
X:=(0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{M}
. On
\mathrm{Supp}(\chi)\times\mathrm{Supp}(\phi_{\bullet})
, we consider

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle:=\rho^{-1}P_{\sigma}+\cancel{P}_{\!\sigma},

\displaystyle P_{\sigma}

\displaystyle:=a(\rho,\omega,\sigma)(\rho D_{\rho})^{2}+b(\rho,\omega,\sigma)\rho D_{\rho}+c(\rho,\omega,\sigma),\qquad\cancel{P}_{\!\sigma}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\cancel{a}_{j}(\rho,\omega,\sigma)D_{\omega_{j}}^{2}+\cancel{b}_{j}(\rho,\omega,\sigma)D_{\omega_{j}}\right)

where

– these functions as well as the coordinate
\omega
of course depend on the cut-off
\phi_{\bullet}
and we should write
a_{\bullet}(\rho,\omega_{\bullet},\sigma)
,
\cancel{a}_{j,\bullet}(\rho,\omega_{\bullet},\sigma)
and so on; we will however not emphasize this dependence in notations below in order to not overload them.

We define the following weighted boundary Sobolev spaces:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{H}^{0,0}:=L^{2}\Big(X,\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}\mathrm{d}\omega\Big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{k,l}

\displaystyle:=\big\{u:X\to{\mathbb{C}}\ \big|\ \rho^{-l}u\in\mathcal{H},\ \rho^{-l}(\partial_{\rho}^{k_{1}}\partial_{\omega}^{k_{2}}u)\in\mathcal{H}\;\text{for all }k_{1}+|k_{2}|=k\big\}.

\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k,l}}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\|\rho^{-l}u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\|\rho^{-l}(\partial_{\rho}^{k}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\rho^{-l}(\partial_{\omega_{j}}^{k}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

We will use the realization
(\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma},\mathcal{X}^{k+1,l})
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{k+1,l}

\displaystyle:=\{u\in\mathcal{H}^{k+1,l}\ |\ \boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}u\in\mathcal{H}^{k,l}\},\qquad\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}^{k+1,l}}^{2}:=\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1,l}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k,l}}^{2}.

We now make some assumptions. Given
(\rho,\omega,\xi,\eta,\sigma)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{M}\times{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{C}}
, we let

\displaystyle\xi_{k,l}

\displaystyle:=\xi-\mathrm{i}(k+l),\qquad|\eta|^{2}_{\cancel{a}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\cancel{a}_{j}(\rho,\omega,\sigma)\eta_{j}^{2}.

Assumptions 9

We assume that
k+l>0
. We also assume that for all

\displaystyle a(\rho,\omega,\sigma)\neq 0\ \mathrm{on}\ \overline{\mathrm{Supp}(\chi)},\qquad\cancel{a}_{j}(\rho,\omega,\sigma)\neq 0\ \mathrm{on}\ \overline{\mathrm{Supp}(\phi_{\bullet})}

\displaystyle|\cancel{a}_{j}(\rho,\omega)\eta_{j}|\lesssim\big||\eta|_{\cancel{a}}\big|,\qquad a(\rho,\omega,\sigma)\xi_{k,l}^{2}+\rho|\eta|_{\cancel{a}}^{2}\neq 0,

\displaystyle\lim_{\|(\xi,\eta)\|\to+\infty}|a(\rho,\omega,\sigma)\xi_{k,l}^{2}+\rho|\eta|_{\cancel{a}}^{2}|=+\infty.

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Op}_{\mathcal{M},\mathcal{F}}[F_{k,l}]v)(\rho,\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{\mathrm{i}\xi+k+l}\frac{1}{(\mathrm{i}\xi+k+l)_{(k)}}\left(\int_{(0,+\infty)}y^{-\mathrm{i}\xi}(y^{-l}\partial_{y}^{k}u)(y,\omega)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{\mathrm{i}\xi+l}\frac{1}{(\mathrm{i}\xi+l)_{(k)}}\left(\int_{(0,+\infty)}y^{-\mathrm{i}\xi+k}(y^{-l}\partial_{y}^{k}u)(y,\omega)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}\right)\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\rho^{l}\mathrm{Op}_{\mathcal{M}}[1]((\rho,\omega)\mapsto\rho^{-l}u(\rho,\omega))

\displaystyle=u(\rho,\omega).

The contour shift uses Cauchy-Riemann equations: to check them, we first need to consider

[

\rho^{l}
to get convergence in
\mathcal{H}^{0,l}
.

\displaystyle\lim_{t^{\prime}\to+\infty}\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}u_{t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=u_{\infty}\in\mathcal{H}^{0,l}.

The proof of Lem.
15
also shows that
\partial_{\rho}^{k_{1}}\partial_{\omega}^{k_{2}}\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}u=\mathrm{Op}_{\mathcal{M},\mathcal{F}}[\breve{\boldsymbol{k}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}^{N,N^{\prime}}]u
with
k_{1}+|k_{2}|=k
, where
{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathcal{M}\times{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\ni(x:=\ln\rho,\omega,\xi,\eta)\mapsto\breve{\boldsymbol{k}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}^{N,N^{\prime}}(\mathrm{e}^{x},\omega,\xi,\eta)
is also a decaying Fourier symbol; as a consequence,
[
Z
, Thm. 4.28]
entails that we can extract a subsequence
(u_{t^{\prime\prime}})_{t^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}}
from
(u_{t^{\prime}})_{t^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t^{\prime\prime}\to+\infty}\partial_{\rho}^{k_{1}}\partial_{\omega}^{k_{2}}\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}u_{t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=w_{\infty}\in\mathcal{H}^{0,l}.

But then,
\rho^{-l}\partial_{\rho}^{k_{1}}\partial_{\omega}^{k_{2}}v_{\infty}=\rho^{-l}w_{\infty}
in the distribution sense; as
\rho^{-l}w_{\infty}\in L^{2}(X,\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}\mathrm{d}\omega)
, we discover that
\rho^{-l}\partial_{\rho}^{k_{1}}\partial_{\omega}^{k_{2}}v_{\infty}\in L^{2}(X,\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}\mathrm{d}\omega)
as well, that is
v_{\infty}\in\mathcal{H}^{k,l}
. This completes the proof.
∎

We now construct a global boundary left parametrix for
\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}
. The construction is very similar to the one for the right parametrix above; as we will not explicitly use the left parametrix in the sequel, we will only give the main lines of its construction. Let again
N,N^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}
such that
N>2(N^{\prime}-1)
and assume that
0\notin\bigcup_{j=1}^{N^{\prime}}\mathfrak{p}_{\sigma,k+j-1,l}({\mathbb{R}})
. Then define

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{\mathrm{f},N}+\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{\mathrm{b},N,N^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{k,l},\mathcal{X}^{k+1,l})

so that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\chi\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{X}^{k+1,l}}+\widetilde{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}.

Above
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{\mathrm{f},N}
first produces decay in the fiber,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{\mathrm{b},N,N^{\prime}}
then creates decay in the basis and
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}
is compact.

To construct
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{\mathrm{f},N}
, we first let

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}^{\mathrm{f},1}u)(\rho)

\displaystyle\quad:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\xi}}\int_{(0,+\infty)_{y}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\eta}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\zeta}}\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\right)^{\mathrm{i}\xi}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\omega-\zeta)\cdot\eta}F_{k,l}(\rho,\xi)\frac{\chi(\rho)\phi_{\bullet}(\omega)y}{a(y,\zeta,\sigma)\xi_{k,l}^{2}+y|\eta|_{\cancel{a}}^{2}}(y^{-l}\partial_{y}^{k}v_{k,l,\bullet})(y,\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}\xi

where
v_{k,l,\bullet}(\rho,\omega):=\widetilde{\phi}_{\bullet}(\omega)(\partial_{\rho}^{k}u)(\rho,\omega)
(once again,
\mathcal{M}\cap\mathrm{Supp}(\widetilde{\phi}_{\bullet})
is identified as an open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
so that the Fourier transform with respect to
\zeta\in\mathcal{M}\cap\mathrm{Supp}(\widetilde{\phi}_{\bullet})
is well defined). Then:

\displaystyle\quad\int_{(0,+\infty)_{y}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\zeta}}\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\right)^{\mathrm{i}\xi}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\omega-\zeta)\cdot\eta}\frac{y^{-l}F_{k,l}(\rho,\xi)y}{a(y)\xi_{k,l}^{2}+y|\eta|_{\cancel{a}}^{2}}\left(\left(-\frac{a(y\partial_{y})^{2}}{y}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\cancel{a}_{j}\partial_{\zeta_{j}}^{2}\right)\partial_{y}^{k}v_{k,l,\bullet}\right)\!(y,\eta)\mathrm{d}\zeta\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,+\infty)_{y}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\zeta}}\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\right)^{\mathrm{i}\xi}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\omega-\zeta)\cdot\eta}\frac{y^{-l}F_{k,l}(\rho,\xi)}{a(y)\xi_{k,l}^{2}+y|\eta|_{\cancel{a}}^{2}}\left(\left(-a\partial_{y}y\partial_{y}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\cancel{a}_{j}\partial_{\zeta_{j}}^{2}\right)\partial_{y}^{k}v_{k,l,\bullet}\right)\!(y,\eta)\mathrm{d}\zeta\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,+\infty)_{y}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\zeta}}\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\right)^{\mathrm{i}\xi}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\omega-\zeta)\cdot\eta}\frac{y^{-l}F_{k,l}(\rho,\xi)}{a(y)\xi_{k,l}^{2}+y|\eta|_{\cancel{a}}^{2}}\left(\left(\frac{a\xi_{k,l}^{2}}{y}+|\eta|_{\cancel{a}}^{2}+\mathrm{subprincipal\ terms}\right)\partial_{y}^{k}v_{k,l,\bullet}\right)\!(y,\eta)\mathrm{d}\zeta\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,+\infty)_{y}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\zeta}}\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\right)^{\mathrm{i}\xi}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\omega-\zeta)\cdot\eta}y^{-l}F_{k,l}(\rho,\xi)(\partial_{y}^{k}v_{k,l,\bullet})(y,\eta)\mathrm{d}\zeta\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}+\mathrm{remainder}.

Above, the subprincipal terms come from commutators (we write
-a\partial_{y}y\partial_{y}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\cancel{a}_{j}\partial_{\zeta_{j}}^{2}=-\partial_{y}y\partial_{y}a-[a,\partial_{y}]y\partial_{y}-\partial_{y}y[a,\partial_{y}]-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\cancel{a}_{j}\partial_{\zeta_{j}}^{2}+[\cancel{a}_{j},\partial_{\zeta_{j}}^{2}])
and so on) and the remainder term is the integral of terms of order
1
in
y
and
-1
in
\xi
and
|\eta|_{\cancel{a}}
. Using Lem.
14
, we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}^{\mathrm{f},1}\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\chi\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{X}^{k+1,l}}+\widetilde{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}^{\mathrm{f},1}

where
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{\mathrm{f},1}
has the same properties as the remainder term in the last line above. That
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{\mathrm{f},1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{k,l},\mathcal{X}^{k+1,l})
then follows using the same arguments as Lem.
15
. We then inductively construct
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}^{\mathrm{f},2},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}^{\mathrm{f},3},\ldots
to decrease the total order in
\xi
and
|\eta|_{\cancel{a}}
of the remainder, then we define

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{\mathrm{f},N}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\bullet}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}^{\mathrm{f},j}.

To construct
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{\mathrm{b},N}
, we first let

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}^{\mathrm{b},1}u)(\rho)

\displaystyle\quad:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\xi}}\int_{(0,+\infty)_{y}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\eta}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\zeta}}\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\right)^{\mathrm{i}\xi}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\omega-\zeta)\cdot\eta}F_{k,l}(\rho,\xi)\frac{\chi(\rho)\phi_{\bullet}(\omega)y\widetilde{\boldsymbol{k}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}(y,\zeta)}{\mathfrak{p}_{\sigma,k,l}^{1}(\xi)}(y^{-l}\partial_{y}^{k}v_{k,l,\bullet})(y,\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}\xi

where is defined in (
7
) and
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\mathrm{f},N,1}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}(y,\zeta)
is such that

\displaystyle\sum_{\bullet}\chi\phi_{\bullet}\mathrm{Op}_{\mathcal{M},\mathcal{F}}[F_{k,l}](\widetilde{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\mathrm{f},N,1}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}v_{k,l,\bullet})

\displaystyle=-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{K}}^{\mathrm{f},N}_{\sigma,k,l}u

As we can write

(11)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{-a_{0}(\rho\partial_{\rho})^{2}+(b_{0}-2\mathrm{i}a_{0})(\rho\partial_{\rho})-\mathrm{i}b_{0}-a_{0}+c_{0}}{\rho}+\mathcal{O}(\partial_{\rho},\partial_{\rho}^{2},\rho\partial_{\omega_{1}},\rho\partial_{\omega_{1}}^{2},\ldots,\rho\partial_{\omega_{n}},\rho\partial_{\omega_{n}}^{2}),

integrations by parts yield

\displaystyle\sum_{\bullet}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}^{\mathrm{b},1}\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}u

\displaystyle=-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{K}}^{\mathrm{f},N}_{\sigma,k,l}u+\mathrm{remainder}

with a remainder having as decay in
\xi
and
|\eta|_{\cancel{a}}
as the
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{k}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}^{\mathrm{f},N,1}
but one more power of
y
. We then inductively construct
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}^{\mathrm{b},N,2},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}^{\mathrm{b},N,3},\ldots
to increase the total order in
y
of the remainder using the polynomials
\mathfrak{p}_{\sigma,k,l,j}(\xi)
defined in (
9
); note that each iteration now increases the total order of
|\eta|_{\cancel{a}}
by 2 since we have to cancel the remainder in (
11
) that does not simplify with the terms
\mathfrak{p}_{\sigma,k,l,j}(\xi)
. Then we define

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{\mathrm{b},N,N^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=1}^{N^{\prime}}\sum_{\bullet}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}^{\mathrm{b},N,j}.

We have then:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\chi\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{X}^{k+1,l}}+\widetilde{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}.

Compactness of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^{k+1,l})
then follows again from
[
Z
, Thm. 4.28]
(using the quantization
\mathrm{Op}_{1}
therein) with the arguments of Lem.
18
above. Furthermore, using the well known property of the Mellin transform
\mathcal{M}[y\mapsto y^{N^{\prime}}U(y)](\xi)=\mathcal{M}[U](\xi+\mathrm{i}N^{\prime})
, the extra term
y^{N^{\prime}}
from the basis correction can be transformed into
\rho^{N^{\prime}}
upon shifting the contour intgral in

from
{\mathbb{R}}
to
{\mathbb{R}}-\mathrm{i}N^{\prime}
, ensuring the Sobolev regularity
\mathcal{H}^{k+1+N-2(N^{\prime}-1),l}
; this amounts to changing
\xi_{k,l}
to
\xi_{k+N^{\prime},l}
, whence the extra assumption
0\notin\bigcup_{0\leq\alpha\leq N^{\prime}}\bigcup_{j=1}^{N^{\prime}}\mathfrak{p}_{\sigma,k+j-1,l}({\mathbb{R}}-\mathrm{i}\alpha)
. As a result,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{K}}^{N,N^{\prime}}_{\sigma,k,l}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^{k+1+N-2(N^{\prime}-1),l})
.

Finally, the regularity in
\sigma
statement for both the right and left parametrices is a mere consequence of the parametrix construction which uses rational functions of the coefficients of
\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}
(the denominators never cancel far away from the critical strips).

2.5.
Local index 0 Fredholm property

We finally prove Prop.
13
.

For the sake of definiteness, we assume that
a(\rho,\omega,\sigma)>0
and
\cancel{a}_{j}(\rho,\omega,\sigma)>0
on
\overline{X^{\prime}}\times\mathcal{M}
. Let
C>0
and
N,N^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}
such that
N>2(N^{\prime}-1)
as in Prop
11
. We define
\boldsymbol{Q}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}
and
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}
with
\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma,k,l}^{\mathrm{f},1}
replaced by
\frac{\rho}{a\xi_{k,l}^{2}+\rho|\eta|_{\cancel{a}}^{2}+\rho C}
. We claim that
\boldsymbol{Q}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime k,l},\mathcal{X}^{\prime k+1,l})
and
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime k,l},\mathcal{X}^{\prime k+1,l})
are invertible for
C
sufficiently large. To see this, we use the above computations to obtain

(12)

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}+\rho C)\boldsymbol{Q}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime k,l}}+\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}},

(13)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{Q}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}+\rho C)

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime k+1,l}}+\widetilde{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}

for some
\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime k,l})
and
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime k+1,l})
with operator norms of order
o_{C\to+\infty}(1)
. Indeed, let us we write
\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}u=\sum_{\bullet}\mathrm{Op}_{\mathcal{M},\mathcal{F}}[\boldsymbol{k}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}]v_{k,l,\bullet}
with
v_{k,l,\bullet}(\rho,\omega):=\chi(y)\widetilde{\phi}_{\bullet}(\omega)(\rho^{-l}\partial_{\rho}^{k}u)(\rho,\omega)
(recall that
\chi_{|X^{\prime}}=\mathds{1}_{X^{\prime}}
; here we implicitly use a Sobolev extension of
u
to
\mathrm{Supp}(\chi)
so that we can consider
u
as a function defined on
(0,+\infty)
vanishing on a neighborhood of
+\infty
).

On the one hand,

\displaystyle\quad\bigg|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\xi}}\int_{(0,+\infty)_{y}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\eta}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\zeta}}\mathds{1}_{\rho/y\leq 1}\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\right)^{\mathrm{i}\xi}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\omega-\zeta)\cdot\eta}\boldsymbol{k}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}(\rho,y,\omega,\zeta,\xi,\eta)v_{k,l,\bullet}(y,\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}\xi\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\xi}}\int_{(0,+\infty)_{y}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\eta}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\zeta}}\mathds{1}_{\rho/y\leq 1}\left|\frac{\boldsymbol{k}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}(\rho,y,\omega,\zeta,\xi,\eta)}{\rho}\right||v_{k,l,\bullet}(y,\zeta)|\mathrm{d}\zeta\frac{\rho}{y}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\xi}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\eta}}\sup_{\rho/y\leq 1/2}\left|\frac{\boldsymbol{k}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}(\rho,y,\omega,\zeta,\xi,\eta)}{\rho}\right|\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}\xi\sqrt{\int_{(0,+\infty)_{y}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\zeta}}|v_{k,l,\bullet}(y,\zeta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\zeta\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}}\sqrt{\int_{\mathrm{Supp}(\chi)}y\mathrm{d}y}

by Cauchy-Schwartz inequality; since
\boldsymbol{k}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}/\rho
is uniformly bounded in
(\rho,\omega)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathcal{M}
and analytic in
\xi
and in
\eta
, we can use the residue theorem (choosing
N
large enough for integrability) to see that the integral over
X^{\prime}\times\mathcal{M}
of the square of the left hand side above is bounded multiple of
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime k,l}}
of order
o_{C\to+\infty}(1)
(integrals with respect to
\rho
and
\omega
are harmless for the estimate as we integrate over bounded domains).

On the other hand, when
\rho/y>1
, we can use the residue theorem for the integral over
{\mathbb{R}}_{\xi}
and sum up over all the residues of
\xi\mapsto\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\right)^{\mathrm{i}\xi}\boldsymbol{k}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}(\rho,y,\omega,\zeta,\xi,\eta)
in the upper half plane
{\mathbb{C}}^{+}
(since
\left|\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\right)^{\mathrm{i}\xi}\right|
decays exponentially fast as a function of
\xi
with
\mathrm{Im}(\xi)>0
when
\rho/y>1
); the residues comes from the falling factorial term
(\mathrm{i}\xi+k+l)_{(k)}
in the denominator of
F_{k,l}
(whose the nearest pole to
{\mathbb{R}}
is
\xi=\mathrm{i}/2
), from the denominator
\frac{\rho}{a\xi_{k,l}^{2}+\rho|\eta|_{\cancel{a}}^{2}+\rho C}
and from the indicial polynomials (both yielding poles with imaginary part greater or equal to
\mathrm{i}(k+l)
). Since
k+l>0
by assumption, we can factor out of the sum of the residues a term
y^{\epsilon}
with
\epsilon>0
:

\displaystyle\quad\bigg|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\xi}}\int_{(0,+\infty)_{y}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\eta}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\zeta}}\mathds{1}_{\rho/y>1}\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\right)^{\mathrm{i}\xi}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\omega-\zeta)\cdot\eta}\boldsymbol{k}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}(\rho,y,\omega,\zeta,\xi,\eta)v_{k,l,\bullet}(y,\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}\xi\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(0,+\infty)_{y}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\eta}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\zeta}}\mathds{1}_{\rho/y>1}\sum\left|\underset{\,{\mathbb{C}}^{+}}{\mathrm{Res}}\left(\xi\mapsto\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\right)^{\mathrm{i}\xi}\frac{\boldsymbol{k}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}(\rho,y,\omega,\zeta,\xi,\eta)}{y^{\epsilon}}\right)\right||v_{k,l,\bullet}(y,\zeta)|\mathrm{d}\zeta\frac{y^{\epsilon}}{y}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\eta}}\sup_{\rho/y>1}\sum\left|\underset{\,{\mathbb{C}}^{+}}{\mathrm{Res}}\left(\xi\mapsto\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\right)^{\mathrm{i}\xi}\frac{\boldsymbol{k}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}(\rho,y,\omega,\zeta,\xi,\eta)}{y^{\epsilon}}\right)\right|\mathrm{d}\eta\sqrt{\int_{(0,+\infty)_{y}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\zeta}}|v_{k,l,\bullet}(y,\zeta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\zeta\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\sqrt{\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{y^{2\epsilon}}{y}\mathrm{d}y}.

It remains to integrate over
X^{\prime}\times\mathcal{M}
(putting
y^{\epsilon}
into the denominator of
\boldsymbol{k}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}
takes away the term
\rho^{-\epsilon}
from
\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\right)^{\mathrm{i}\xi_{*}}=\rho^{-\epsilon}\left(\frac{\rho}{y}\right)^{\mathrm{i}\xi_{*}-\epsilon}y^{\epsilon}
where
\xi_{*}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{+}
is a pole; since the integral over
(0,\rho)
above is equal to
\rho^{2\epsilon}/(2\epsilon)
, integrability near 0 is ensured). As above, the denominator of
\boldsymbol{k}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}
makes that we get a multiple of
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime k,l}}
of order
o_{C\to+\infty}(1)
.

In the same spirit, we can establish the same estimate for the
\mathcal{H}
norm of derivatives of
\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma,k,l}u
, concluding that
\|\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma,k,l}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime k,l})}=o_{C\to+\infty}(1)
. Moreover, if
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}u=\sum_{\bullet}\mathrm{Op}_{\mathcal{M},\mathcal{F}}[\widetilde{\boldsymbol{k}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}]v_{k,l,\bullet}
then
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{k}}_{\sigma,k,l,\bullet}/y
is uniformly bounded so that we can multiply by
y
in the integral with respect to
y
, removing once again the troublesome
\frac{1}{y}
term before using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality (this time we do not need to integrate by parts using
\partial_{\xi}
). The conclusion is
\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{K}}_{\sigma,k,l}\|_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime k+1,l})}=o_{C\to+\infty}(1)
as well.

Now we choose
C
lare enough (depending on
|\sigma|
,
k
and
|l|
) so that the right hand sides in (
12
) and (
13
) are invertible: this proves that
\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}+C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime k+1,l},\mathcal{H}^{\prime k,l})
is invertible. Back into (
12
), we get the invertibility of
\boldsymbol{Q}_{\sigma,k,l}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime k,l},\mathcal{X}^{\prime k+1,l})
.

Finally, Sect.
2.4
tells us that

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}\boldsymbol{Q}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime k,l}}+\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}

with
\boldsymbol{Q}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}\equiv\boldsymbol{Q}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}(C)
still defined with the aforementioned modification of
\mathfrak{q}_{\sigma,k,l}^{\mathrm{f},1}
and
\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}\equiv\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}(C)
compact on
\mathcal{H}^{\prime k,l}
(but not necessarily invertible as
C\to+\infty
). Since both
\boldsymbol{Q}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}
and
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime k,l}}+\boldsymbol{K}_{\sigma,k,l}^{N,N^{\prime}}
are index 0 Fredholm operators, so is
\boldsymbol{P}_{\sigma}
. The proof is complete.

The charged KG operator near cosmological accelerating and rotating charged black holes

We apply in this section the results obtained in Sect.

to charged KG scalar fields near cosmological accelerating and rotating charged black holes.

3.1.
The metric in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates

We follow
cf.

[
PG
]
and
[

, Sect. 3.2 & 3.3]
. Let
M,\Lambda>0
,

and define the horizon function:

\displaystyle\mu(r)

On
\mathbb{R}_{t}\times(r_{-},r_{+})_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\theta,\varphi}

(14)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Omega^{2}}\left(\frac{\mu}{(1+\lambda)^{2}\rho^{2}}\big(\mathrm{d}t-a\sin^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\varphi\big)^{2}-\frac{\kappa\sin^{2}\theta}{(1+\lambda)^{2}\rho^{2}}\big(a\mathrm{d}t-(r^{2}+a^{2})\mathrm{d}\varphi\big)^{2}-\rho^{2}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}{\mu}+\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}}{\kappa}\right)\right)

where

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=1-\alpha r\cos\theta,\qquad\quad\lambda=\frac{\Lambda a^{2}}{3},\qquad\quad\rho^{2}=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta,

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle=1-2\alpha M\cos\theta+\left(\alpha^{2}(a^{2}+Q^{2}(1+\lambda)^{2})+\lambda\right)\cos^{2}\theta.

(Notice that we use the coordinates
\bar{t}
and
\bar{\varphi}
in
[
PG
, eq. (4)]
while
e=Q(1+\lambda)
and
g=0
.) If

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=-\frac{Qr}{\rho^{2}}(\mathrm{d}t-a\sin^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\varphi),

then
(g,A)
solves the coupled Einstein-Maxwell equation with electromagnetic potential
\mathrm{d}A

\mathrm{dVol}_{g}

\displaystyle\mathrm{dVol}_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Omega^{4}}\frac{\rho^{2}\sin\theta}{(1+\lambda)^{2}}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\varphi.

The coefficients of the inverse metric

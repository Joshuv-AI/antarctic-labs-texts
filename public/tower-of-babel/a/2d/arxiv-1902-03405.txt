A new class of special functions arising from the solution of differential equations involving multiple proportional delays


Abstract
Proportional delay is a particular case of time dependent delay. In this article, we consider differential equations involving multiple delays. The series solution of this equation leads to a class of special functions. This class of special functions is independent from all the existing special functions obtained as a solution of differential equations. We analyze the basic properties of this class and discuss various identities and relations.

Introduction

Special functions are plying a vital role in the solutions of differential equations. Exponential, sine, cosine, hypergeometric and Mittag-Leffler are important classes of special functions arising as solutions of various classical and fractional differential equations. Though there is a huge literature devoted to the special functions arising from ordinary differential equations (ODEs), there is a lack of corresponding literature in delay differential equations (DDE).
In
[
1
,
2
]
we discussed the solutions of a class of DDE viz. proportional delay differential equations and provided the solutions in terms of new special functions. In this article, we generalize the DDEs considered in
[
2
]
to involve multiple delays. Since the differential equations without delay are inequivalent to the differential equations involving delay, the special functions proposed in this article are independent from the existing special functions.

3
Existence, uniqueness and convergence:Nonlinear Case

First, we consider the nonlinear equation

y^{\prime}(x)=f\left(x,y(q_{0}x),y(q_{1}x),\cdots,y(q_{n}x)\right),q_{0}=1\,\textrm{and}\,0<q_{i}<1,i=1,2,\cdots,n.

(3.1)

The Eq. (
3.1
) is a particular case of time dependent delay differential equation (DDE)

y^{\prime}(x)=f\left(x,y\left(x-\tau_{0}(x)\right),y\left(x-\tau_{1}(x)\right),y\left(x-\tau_{2}(x)\right)\cdots y\left(x-\tau_{n}(x)\right)\right)

with
\tau_{i}(x)=(1-q_{i})x,\quad i=1,2,3,\cdots,n
. The DJM series solution of Eq. (
3.1
) is of the form

y=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}y_{i}.

(3.2)

We present convergence result of this series solution motivated from
[
6
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let f be a continuous function defined on a
(n+2)
dimensional rectangle

R=\{(x,y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})|0\leq x\leq b,-\delta_{i}\leq y_{i}\leq\delta_{i},i=0,1,2,\cdots,n\}
and
\mid f\mid\leq M
on
R
. Suppose that f satisfies Lipschitz type condition

\mid f\left(x,y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\right)-f\left(x,u_{0},u_{1},\cdots,u_{n}\right)\mid\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}L_{i}\mid y_{i}-u_{i}\mid.

(3.3)

Then the DJM series solution (
3.2
) of DDE (
3.1
)
converges uniformly in the interval [0,b].

Proof.

Without loss of gerenality, assume that
y(0)=1
. The equivalent integral equation of (
3.1
) is

y(x)=1+\int_{0}^{x}f\left(x,y(q_{0}t),y(q_{1}t),y(q_{2}t),\cdots,y(q_{n}t)\right)dt.

Using DJM, we get

\displaystyle y_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,

\displaystyle y_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}f\left(t,y_{0}(q_{0}t),y_{0}(q_{1}t),y_{0}(q_{2}t),\cdots,y_{0}(q_{n}t)\right)dt.

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mid y_{1}(x)\mid

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Mx.

\textrm{Since}\;q_{0}=1,0<q_{i}<1,i=1,2,3,\cdots,n.,\;q_{i}b\leq b.

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mid y_{1}(q_{i}x)\mid

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Mq_{i}x,\quad\forall x\in[0,b].

\displaystyle y_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\left(f\left(t,y_{1}(q_{0}t)+y_{0}(q_{0}t),y_{1}(q_{1}t)+y_{0}(q_{1}t),y_{1}(q_{2}t)+y_{0}(q_{2}t),\cdots,y_{1}(q_{n}t)+y_{0}(q_{n}t)\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-f\left(t,y_{0}(q_{0}t),y_{0}(q_{1}t),y_{0}(q_{2}t),\cdots,y_{0}(q_{n}t)\right)\right)dt.

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mid y_{2}(x)\mid

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}L_{i}\mid y_{1}(q_{i}t)\mid\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}L_{i}q_{i}\right)\frac{x^{2}}{2!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}L_{i}\right)\frac{x^{2}}{2!}.

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mid y_{2}(q_{i}x)\mid

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Mq_{i}^{2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}L_{i}q_{i}\right)\frac{x^{2}}{2!},\quad x\in[0,b]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}L_{i}\right)\frac{x^{2}}{2!}.

Using induction, we get

\displaystyle\mid y_{m}(x)\mid

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}L_{i}q^{j}_{i}\right)\frac{x^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}L_{i}\right)^{m-1}\frac{x^{m}}{m!},\quad m=1,2,3\cdots.

Taking summation over
m
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}y_{m}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{M}{\sum_{i=0}^{n}L_{i}}\left(e^{\sum_{i=0}^{n}L_{i}x}-1\right)+1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{Me^{\sum_{i=0}^{n}L_{i}b}}{\sum_{i=0}^{n}L_{i}}+1,\quad x\in[0,b].

Thus, by
[
7
]
, we can conclude that the series solution of (
3.1
) converges uniformly in the interval [0,b]. Hence existence of (
3.1
) is proved. The uniqueness is of solution is obvious by condition (
3.3
).
∎

Stability analysis

The following definitions and theorems are generalization of corresponding definition and theeorems given in

Definition 4.1
.

Consider the autonomus time-dependent delay differential equation (DDE),

y^{\prime}(x)=g(y\left(x\right),y\left(x-\tau_{1}(x)\right),y\left(x-\tau_{2}(x)\right)\cdots y\left(x-\tau_{n}(x)\right)),

(4.1)

where
g:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
The flow
\phi_{x}(x_{0})
is the solution
y(x)
of (
4.1
) with initial condition

y(x)=x_{0},\,x\leq 0
. The point
y^{*}
is called equilibrium solution of (
4.1
) if
g(y^{*},y^{*},\cdots,y^{*})=0
.

(a)
If, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
\delta>0
such that
|x_{0}-y^{*}|<\delta\Rightarrow|\phi_{x}(x_{0})-y^{*}|<\epsilon,
then the system (
4.1
) is stable (in the Lyapunov sense) at the equilibrium
y^{*}
.

(b)
If the system (
4.1
) is stable at
y^{*}
and moreover,
\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}|\phi_{x}(x_{0})-y^{*}|=0
then the system (
4.1
) is said to be asymptotically stable at
y^{*}
.

(c)
If the system (
4.1
) is not stable then it is called unstable.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that the equilibrium solution
y^{*}
of the equation

y^{\prime}=g(y(x),y(x-\tau_{1}^{*}),y(x-\tau_{2}^{*}),\cdots,y(x-\tau_{n}^{*})),\quad\tau_{1}^{*}=\tau_{1}(x_{0}),\tau_{2}^{*}=\tau_{2}(x_{0}),\cdots,\tau_{n}^{*}=\tau_{n}(x_{0})

is stable at equilibrium
y^{*}
and

\|g(y(x),y(x-\tau_{1}(x)),\cdots,y(x-\tau_{n}(x)))-g(y(x),y(x-\tau_{1}(x_{1})),\cdots,y(x-\tau_{n}(x_{n})))\|<\sum_{i=0}^{n}\epsilon_{i}|x-x_{i}|,

for some
\epsilon_{i}>0
and
x,x_{i}\in[x_{0},x_{0}+c)
(i=1,2,\cdots,n)
,
c is a positive constant, then there exists
\bar{x}>0
such that
the equilibrium solution
y^{*}
of Eq. (
4.1
) is stable at equilibrium
y^{*}
on finite time interval
[x_{0},\bar{x})
.

Corollary 1
.

If the real parts of all roots of
\lambda-\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}e^{-\lambda\tau_{i}^{*}}
are negative, where
a_{i}=\partial_{i}f
,
i=1,2,\cdots,n
evaluated at equilibrium
y^{*}
, then Eq. (
4.1
) is stable at
y^{*}
on finite time
interval
[x_{0},\bar{x})

Linear equation: Exact solution

Consider the differential equation involving multiple proportional delays,

y^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}y(q_{i}x),\quad y(0)=1,

(5.1)

where
q_{0}=1
,
0<q_{i}<1
and
a_{0},a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,2,3,\cdots,n
.
The equation (
5.1
) has applications in Science and Engineering
[
9
,
10
]
.

Integrating (
5.1
), we get

y(x)=1+\int_{0}^{x}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}y(q_{i}t)\right)dt

Using successive approximation, we obtain

\displaystyle y_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,

\displaystyle y_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}y_{0}(q_{i}t)\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\frac{x}{1!},

\displaystyle y_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}y_{1}(q_{i}t)\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}q_{i}t\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}q_{i}\right)\frac{x^{2}}{2!},

\displaystyle y_{3}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}y_{2}(q_{i}t)\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}q_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}q^{2}_{i}\right)\frac{x^{3}}{3!},

\displaystyle y_{m}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x^{m}}{m!}\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}q^{j}_{i}\right),\quad m=1,2,3\cdots.

\therefore
The exact solution of (
5.1
) is

\displaystyle y(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y_{0}(x)+y_{1}(x)+y_{2}(x)+\cdots

(5.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{m}}{m!}\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}q^{j}_{i}\right).

If we define

\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}q^{j}_{i}\right)=1\quad\textrm{for}\quad m=0,

then

y(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{m}}{m!}\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}q^{j}_{i}\right)

(5.3)

This solution of (
5.1
) provides a novel special function

\mathcal{R}(\bar{a};\bar{q};x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{m}}{m!}(\bar{a};\bar{q})_{m}.

(5.4)

Note:
We use a brief notations
\mathcal{R}(\bar{a};\bar{q};x)
for the special function
\mathcal{R}(a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{n};q_{0},q_{1},\cdots,q_{n},x)
and
(\bar{a};\bar{q})_{m}
for
\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}q^{j}_{i}\right)
.

Theorem 6.1
.

The power series

\mathcal{R}(\bar{a};\bar{q};x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{m}}{m!}(\bar{a};\bar{q})_{m},

Proof.

Suppose

A_{m}=\frac{1}{m!}(\bar{a};\bar{q})_{m},\quad m=0,1,2,\cdots.

If
R
is radius of convergence of (
6.1
) then by using ratio test
[
11
]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\left|\frac{A_{m+1}}{A_{m}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\left|\frac{\frac{1}{{(m+1)!}}\prod_{j=0}^{m}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}q^{j}_{i}\right)}{\frac{1}{m!}\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}q^{j}_{i}\right)}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\left|\frac{\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}q^{m}\right)}{(m+1)}\right|

\displaystyle\Rightarrow R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\infty.

Thus the series has infinite radius of convergence.

8
Conclusions

In this paper, we have obtained a new special function arising from differential equation involving multiple proportional delays. The solution is obtained by applying the successive approximation method. The existence, uniqueness, stability and convergence results for the time dependent delay differential equations are presented in this paper. The new special function exhibit different properties and relations. The generalization to fractional order case is also presented.

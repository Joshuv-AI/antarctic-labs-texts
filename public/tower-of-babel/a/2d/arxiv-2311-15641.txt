A generalized nonstandard finite difference method for a class of autonomous dynamical systems and its applications
Manh Tuan Hoang

Abstract
. In this work, a class of continuous-time autonomous dynamical systems describing many important phenomena and processes arising in real-world applications is considered. We apply the nonstandard finite difference (NSFD) methodology proposed by Mickens to design a generalized NSFD method for the dynamical system models under consideration. This method is constructed based on a novel non-local approximation for the right-side functions of the dynamical systems. It is proved by rigorous mathematical analyses that the NSFD method is dynamically consistent with respect to positivity, asymptotic stability and three classes of conservation laws, including direct conservation, generalized conservation and sub-conservation laws. Furthermore, the NSFD method is easy to be implemented and can be applied to solve a broad range of mathematical models arising in real-life. Finally, a set of numerical experiments is performed to illustrate the theoretical findings and to show advantages of the proposed NSFD method.

2.1.
Stability of equilibria of continuous-time dynamical systems

Consider the continuous-time dynamical system (
1.1
). We recall that a point
y^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called an equilibrium point of System (
1.1
) if

(see

,

Definition 2.1

(
[

,
85
]
)
.

The equilibrium point
y^{*}=0
of (

•

stable, if, for each

\|y(0)\|<\delta\quad\mbox{implies that}\quad\|y(t)\|<\epsilon,\quad\forall t\geq 0;

•

unstable if it is not stable;

•

(locally) asymptotically stable if it is stable and

\|y(0)\|<\delta\quad\mbox{implies that}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}y(t)=0.

The following theorem is known as the Lyapunov’s indirect method
[

,
52
,
53
,
85
]

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
y^{*}=0
be an equilibrium point for the system (
1.1
), where

is continuously differentiable and

is a neighborhood of the origin. Let

A=\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y}(y)\bigg|_{y=0}.

Then,

•

y^{*}
is LAS if
Re\lambda_{i}<0
for all eigenvalues of
A
;

•

y^{*}
is unstable if
Re\lambda_{i}>0
for one or more the eigenvalues of
A
.

Remark 2.3
.

An equilibrium point

of (
1.1
) is said to be hyperbolic if none of the eigenvalues of the matrix

lies on the imaginary axis and non-hyperbolic otherwise (see
[
85
]
). Hence, Theorem
2.2
is only applicable for hyperbolic equilibrium points.

Theorem 2.4

(
[

,
84
]
)
.

Suppose that

in (
1.2
) has the property that solutions of initial value problems

. Then

Remark 2.5

We infer from Theorem

2.2.
Conservation laws

We consider three classes of conservation laws for the model (
1.1
), that are direct conservation, generalized conservation and sub-conservation laws
[
65
]
.

For the system (
1.1
), let us denote by
P_{T}(t)=y_{1}(t)+y_{2}(t)+\ldots+y_{n}(t)
. We say that the system (
1.1
) satisfies

•

a direct conservation law (DCL) if

\dfrac{dP_{T}}{dt}=0,

which implies that

is constant;

•

a generalized conservation law (GCL) if

\dfrac{dP_{T}}{dt}=a_{1}-b_{1}P_{T},\quad a_{1},b_{1}>0,

which implies that

monotonically converges to
a_{1}/b_{1}
as

;

•

a sub-conservation law (SCL) if a sub-set of the populations,

, where

P_{S}(t)=P_{1S}(t)+P_{2S}(t)+\ldots+P_{mS}(t),\quad 2\leq m\leq n,

has the property

\dfrac{dP_{T}}{dt}=0,

or

\dfrac{dP_{T}}{dt}=a_{1}-b_{1}P_{T}.

In
[
65
]
, Mickens and Washington introduced NSFD discretizations of some mathematical models satisfying the above conservation laws.

2.3.
Stability of equilibria of discrete-time dynamical systems

Consider a general discrete-time dynamical system governed by first-order difference equations of the form

A point

(

(

3.
Construction of the NSFD scheme

In this section, we construct a generalized NSFD scheme for the model (
1.1
) and investigate its qualitative dynamical properties.

3.1.
Derivation of the NSFD scheme and its positivity

First, suppose that the function
f
satisfies the condition
(C1)
with a real number
\alpha
. We use the following non-local approximation for the function
f

(3.1)

f(y(t_{k}))=f(y(t_{k}))+\big(my(t_{k})-my(t_{k})\big)\approx f(y_{k})+my_{k}-my_{k+1},

where
m
is a real number. By combining (
3.1
) with the nonstandard approximation for the first-order derivative
dy/dt
, we obtain the following NSFD model for the system (
1.1
):

(3.2)

\dfrac{y_{k+1}-y_{k}}{\phi(\Delta t)}=f(y_{k})+my_{k}-my_{k+1},

where
\phi(\Delta t)=\Delta t+\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2})
as
\Delta t\to 0
. The explicit form of the scheme (
3.2
) is given by

(3.3)

y_{k+1}=\dfrac{y_{k}+\phi f(y_{k})+\phi my_{k}}{1+m\phi},

or equivalently,

(3.4)

y_{k+1}=y_{k}+\dfrac{\phi}{1+m\phi}f(y_{k}).

Remark 3.1
.

Although the rule of nonlocal approximation of the NSFD methodology states that nonlinear terms in the right-hand side
f(y)
should be approximated in a nonlocal way, in many cases linear terms with negative coefficients, namely,
-cy
(
c>0
) can be also nonlocally approximated by
-cy_{k+1}
to guarantee the positivity of the corresponding NSFD schemes (see, for instance,
[
1
,
8
,
12
,
22
,
43
,
44
,
55
,
56
,
65
]
). Hence, the approximation (
3.1
) can be considered as a nonlocal approximation, which follows the NSFD methodology.

Lemma 3.2
.

The NSFD sheme (
3.2
) preserves the positivity of the model (
1.1
) for all the values of the step size
\Delta t
whenever

(3.5)

m\geq m_{P}:=\max\{\alpha,\,0\}.

Proof.

We use mathematical induction to prove the positivity of the NSFD scheme (
3.2
). Indeed, assume that
y_{k}\geq 0
. Then,

f(y_{k})+my_{k}\geq f(y_{k})+\alpha y_{k}\geq 0.

Combining this estimate with (
3.3
), we obtain

y_{k+1}=\dfrac{y_{k}+\phi(f(y_{k})+my_{k})}{1+m\phi}\geq 0.

This is the desired conclusion. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The discretization (
3.1
) guarantees that the NSFD scheme (
3.2
) is consistent. Similarly to convergence analysis presented in
[
19
]
, we can conclude that the NSFD scheme (
3.2
) is convergent of order
1
.

Remark 3.4
.

It is easy to see from (
3.4
) that the NSFD scheme (
3.2
) can be rewritten in the form

\dfrac{y_{k+1}-y_{k}}{\Phi(\Delta t)}=f(y_{k}),

where

\Phi(\Delta t)=\dfrac{\phi(\Delta t)}{1+m\phi(\Delta t)}=\phi(\Delta t)+\mathcal{O}(\phi^{2}(\Delta t))=\Delta t+\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2}).

Hence, the NSFD scheme (
3.2
) can be equivalently derived by using renormalization of the denominator. Here, it is important to note that conditions for the NSFD scheme (
3.2
) to be dynamically consistent with the model (
1.1
) will be set to
m
.

Let us denote by
\mathcal{E}_{C}
and

the sets of equilibrium points of the continuous-time dynamical system (

), respectively. It should be emphasized that

for most standard finite difference schemes such as the Taylor’s and Runge-Kutta schemes since they often generate spurious equilibrium points, which depend on the step size

.

\mathcal{E}_{C}=\mathcal{E}_{D}
for all the finite values of the step size

Let us denote by

the set of all asymptotically stable equilibrium points of the system (

). To analyze the stability of the NSFD scheme (

), we introduce the following notation

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{F}=\bigcup_{y^{*}\in\mathcal{E}_{C}^{S}}\sigma(J_{C}(y^{*})),\\
&m_{S}=\max_{\lambda\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg\{-\dfrac{|\lambda|^{2}}{2Re\lambda}\bigg\},\end{split}

where
J_{C}(y^{*})
is the Jacobian matrix of the system (
1.1
) evaluating at
y^{*}
and
\sigma(J_{C}(y^{*}))
is the set of eigenvalues of

. Note that

for all

(see Theorem
2.2
).

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
m
be a real number with the property that

(3.7)

m\geq m_{S},

where
m_{S}
is given in (
3.6
). Then, the NSFD scheme (
3.2
) preserves the LAS of the system (
1.1
) for all the values of the step size
\Delta t
.

Proof.

Let
y^{*}
be any equilibrium point of the continuous system (
1.1
). Let us denote by
{J}_{C}(y^{*})
and
{J}_{D}(y^{*})
the Jacobian matrices of the systems (
1.1
) and (
3.2
) evaluating at

, respectively. Then, it follows from the equation (
3.4
) that

(3.8)

{J}_{D}=I+\dfrac{\phi}{1+m\phi}{J}_{C},

where

is the identity matrix. The relation (
3.8
) implies that

\begin{split}\det(xI-{J}_{C})&=\det\bigg[\bigg(x+\dfrac{1+m\phi}{\phi}\bigg)I-\frac{1+m\phi}{\phi}J_{D}\bigg]\\
&=\bigg(\dfrac{1+m\phi}{\phi}\bigg)^{n}\det\bigg[\bigg(1+\dfrac{\phi x}{1+m\phi}\bigg)I-{J}_{D}\bigg].\end{split}

Hence,
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
{J}_{C}
if and only if
\mu=1+\dfrac{\phi}{1+m\phi}\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
{J}_{D}
. This implies that

(3.9)

\begin{split}|\mu_{i}|^{2}&=\bigg|1+\dfrac{\phi}{1+m\phi}\lambda_{i}\bigg|^{2}=\bigg(1+\dfrac{\phi}{1+m\phi}Re\lambda_{i}\bigg)^{2}+\bigg(\dfrac{\phi}{1+m\phi}Im\lambda_{i}\bigg)^{2}\\
&=1+\dfrac{2\phi}{1+m\phi}Re\lambda_{i}+\bigg(\dfrac{\phi}{1+m\phi}\bigg)^{2}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}.\end{split}

We now consider the following two cases of the stability of
y^{*}
.

Case 1.

y^{*}
is an unstable equilibrium point of the system (
1.1
). In this case, it follows from Theorem
2.2
that there exists
\lambda_{i}\in\sigma({J}_{C}(y^{*}))
such that
Re\lambda_{i}>0
. Then, we infer from (
3.9
) that the corresponding eigenvalue
\mu_{i}
of
{J}_{D}(y^{*})
satisfies

|\mu_{i}|^{2}=1+\dfrac{2\phi}{1+m\phi}Re\lambda_{i}+\bigg(\dfrac{\phi}{1+m\phi}\bigg)^{2}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}>1,

which implies that
y^{*}
is an unstable equilibrium point of the system (
3.2
) (see Theorem
2.7
).

Case 2.

y^{*}
is an asymptotically stable equilibrium point of the system (
1.1
). In this case,
Re\lambda<0
for all
\lambda\in\sigma({J}_{C}(y^{*}))
. By using (
3.9
), we obtain
|\mu|^{2}<1
if and only if

\big(2mRe\lambda+|\lambda|^{2}\big)\phi<-2Re\lambda.

This condition is always satisfied if
m\geq-{|\lambda|^{2}}/{(2Re\lambda)}
. Hence, we obtain from the condition

and Theorem

that

is an asymptotically stable equilibrium point of the NSFD scheme (

).

Finally, the proof is completed by combining Cases
1
and
2
.
∎

of the NSFD scheme (

3.3.
Conservation laws

Assume that the system (
1.1
) satisfies conservation laws. We show that the NSFD scheme (
3.2
) has the ability to preserve this property.

Lemma 3.8
.

The NSFD scheme (
3.2
) preserves the DCL for all finite step sizes.

Proof.

Assume that the system (
1.1
) satisfies a DCL, that is the total population satisfies

\dfrac{dP_{T}}{dt}=\dfrac{dy_{1}}{dt}+\dfrac{dy_{2}}{dt}+\ldots+\dfrac{dy_{n}}{dt}=0,

which implies that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}(t)
is constant, and hence,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(y(t))=0
for all
t\geq 0
. By using (
3.4
) we obtain

P_{T,k+1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i,k+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i,k}+\dfrac{\phi}{1+m\phi}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(y_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i,k+1}:=P_{T,k}.

This implies that the DCL is preserved automatically by the NSFD scheme (
3.2
). The proof is complete.
∎

Next, assume that a GCL exists for the system (
1.1
), that is

(3.10)

\dfrac{dP_{T}}{dt}=a_{1}-b_{1}P_{T},\quad a_{1},b_{1}>0.

Note that (
3.10
) has an exact finite difference scheme of the form (see
[
65
]
)

(3.11)

\dfrac{P_{T,k+1}-P_{T,k}}{\phi^{*}(\Delta t)}=a_{1}-b_{1}P_{T,k},\quad\phi^{*}(\Delta t)=\dfrac{1-e^{-b_{1}\Delta t}}{b_{1}}.

Applying the NSFD scheme (
3.2
) we obtain

(3.12)

P_{T,k+1}=P_{T,k}+\dfrac{\phi}{1+m\phi}(a_{1}-b_{1}P_{T,k}).

Lemma 3.9
.

The following assertions hold for the difference equation (
3.12
):

(1)

the sequence
\{P_{T,k}\}_{k}
defined in (
3.12
) monotonically converges to
{a_{1}}/{b_{1}}
as
k\to\infty
, provided that

(3.13)

m\geq m_{GCL}:=b_{1};

(2)

The scheme (
3.12
) is an exact finite difference scheme for (
3.10
) if

(3.14)

\phi(\Delta t)=\phi_{E}(\Delta t):=\dfrac{\phi^{*}(\Delta t)}{1-m\phi^{*}(\Delta t)},

where
\phi^{*}
is given in (
3.11
).

Proof.

Proof of Part (1).
It follows from (
3.12
) that

\begin{split}P_{T,k+1}&=P_{T,k}+\dfrac{\phi}{1+m\phi}(a_{1}-b_{1}P_{T,k})=\dfrac{a_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}+\bigg(1-\dfrac{b_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}\bigg)P_{T,k}\\
&=\dfrac{a_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}+\bigg(1-\dfrac{b_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}\bigg)\Bigg[\dfrac{a_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}+\bigg(1-\dfrac{b_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}\bigg)P_{T,k-1}\Bigg]\\
&=\dfrac{a_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}+\dfrac{a_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}\bigg(1-\dfrac{b_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}\bigg)+\bigg(1-\dfrac{b_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}\bigg)^{2}P_{T,k-1}.\end{split}

By continuing the above process, we have

P_{T,k+1}=\dfrac{a_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}{\sum_{i=0}^{k}}\bigg(1-\dfrac{b_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}\bigg)^{i}+\bigg(1-\dfrac{b_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}\bigg)^{k}P_{T,0}.

Since
m\geq b_{1}
,
1-\dfrac{b_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}\in(-1,1)
. This implies that

(3.15)

\lim_{t\to\infty}P_{T,k}=\dfrac{a_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}\bigg(\dfrac{b_{1}\phi}{1+m\phi}\bigg)^{-1}=\dfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}.

On the other hand, we infer from
m\geq b_{1}
that

\dfrac{\partial P_{T,k+1}}{\partial P_{T,k}}=1+\dfrac{\phi}{1+m\phi}(-b_{1})\geq 0,

which implies the monotonocity of
\{P_{T,k}\}_{k}
. The proof is completed.

Proof of Part (2).
The exact scheme (
3.11
) can be written in the form

(3.16)

P_{T,k+1}=P_{T,k}+\phi^{*}(a_{1}-b_{1}P_{T,k}).

We infer from (
3.16
) and (
3.12
) that (
3.12
) is an exact scheme for (
3.10
) if

\phi^{*}=\dfrac{\phi}{1+m\phi}.

This condition is equivalent to (
3.14
). The proof is complete.
∎

From Lemma
3.9
we have:

Theorem 3.10
.

The NSFD scheme (
3.2
) satisfies the GCL if one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

m\geq m_{GCL}:=b_{1}
.

(2)

\phi(\Delta t)=\phi_{E}(\Delta t)
, where
\phi_{E}
is given in (
3.14
).

Remark 3.11
.

The function
\phi_{E}
given in (

\phi(\Delta t)=\Delta t+\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{2})
and is defined for all

if

Remark 3.12

The sub-conservation law will be satisfied if the DCL and GCL are satisfied.

3.5.
A note on the nonstandard Euler scheme

As emphasized in Remark
3.4
, the NSFD scheme (
3.2
) can be considered as a nonstandard Euler method. For the sake of convenience, we now consider a special case of the parameter
m
, namely,
m=0
. In this case, the NSFD scheme (
3.2
) becomes

(3.19)

y_{k+1}=y_{k}+\phi(\Delta t)f(y_{k}).

This can be considered as a ”weakly” NSFD scheme
[
28
]
.

Because of the absence of parameter
m
, conditions for the dynamic consistency of the scheme (
3.19
) will be set to the denominator function
\phi
. Below, we will give conditions for the scheme (
3.19
) to be dynamically consistent with the model (
1.1
).

We infer from (
3.19
) that
y_{k+1}\geq 0
for all
\phi>0
if
\alpha\leq 0
. In this case, the nonstandard Euler scheme is unconditionally positive. Otherwise, if
\alpha>0
, then it follows from (
1.2
) that

y_{k+1}=y_{k}+\phi f(y_{k})\geq y_{k}+(-\alpha y_{k})=(1-\phi\alpha)y_{k}.

Consequently,
y_{k+1}\geq 0
if
\phi<(\alpha)^{-1}
. So, we have the following result on the positivity of the nonstandard Euler scheme.

Lemma 3.15
.

The nonstandard Euler scheme (
3.19
) preserves the positivity of the model (
1.1
) for all the values of the step size
\Delta t
whenever the denominator function satisfies the following condition for all
\Delta t>0

(3.20)

\phi(\Delta t)<\phi_{P}:=\begin{cases}&\infty,\,\,\,\quad\qquad\alpha\leq 0,\\
&(\alpha)^{-1},\qquad\alpha>0.\end{cases}

The following result is a direct consequence of the results presented in
[
28
,
29
]
.

Corollary 3.16
.

The nonstandard Euler scheme (
3.19
) preserves the LAS of the model (
1.1
) for all the values of the step size
\Delta t
if the denominator function satisfies the following condition for all
\Delta t>0

(3.21)

\phi(\Delta t)<\phi_{S}:=\min_{\lambda\in\mathcal{F}}\bigg\{-\dfrac{2Re\lambda}{|\lambda|^{2}}\bigg\},

where the set
\mathcal{F}
is defined in (
3.6
).

It is easily seen that the nonstandard Euler scheme always preserves the DCL. On the other hand, when applying the nonstandard Euler scheme for systems satisfying the GLC, we obtain

(3.22)

\dfrac{P_{T,k+1}-P_{T,k}}{\varphi(\Delta t)}=a_{1}-b_{1}P_{T,k}.

Hence, the GLC is preserved if

(3.23)

\phi<\phi_{GCL}=(b_{1})^{-1}.

Furthermore, the scheme (
3.22
) becomes an exact scheme for (
3.10
) if

(3.24)

\phi(\Delta t)=\dfrac{1-e^{-b_{1}\Delta t}}{b_{1}}.

Hence, we obtain the following assertion.

Corollary 3.17
.

The nonstandard Euler scheme (
3.19
) is always dynamically consistent with respect to the DCL and is dynamically consistent with respect to the GCL if the condition (
3.23
) or the condition (
3.24
) holds.

Remark 3.18
.

•

By choosing suitable denominator functions, the nonstandard Euler scheme (
3.19
) can preserve the positivity, LAS and conservation laws as the NSFD scheme (
3.2
). Although the schemes (
3.2
) and (
3.19
) are all convergent of order
1
, in the next section we will provide a numerical example in which errors of Scheme (
3.2
) are better than errors of Scheme (
3.19
).

•

The condition (
3.24
) can conflict with the conditions (
3.20
) and (
3.21
).

\dfrac{d(S+V+I)}{dt}=\Pi-\mu(S+V+I).

Consequently, the condition for the preservation of the GCL is

m\geq m_{GCL}={\mu}=0.03.

Therefore, the condition for the parameter
m
is

m\geq\max\{m_{P},m_{S},m_{GCL}\}=0.98.

In a similar way, we can determine the condition for the nonstandard Euler scheme as

\phi(\Delta t)=\dfrac{1-e^{-\tau\Delta t}}{\tau},\quad\tau>(0.98)^{-1}\approx 1.02.

5.
Conclusions, discussions and some open problems

In this work, we have applied the NSFD methodology proposed by Mickens to construct a generalized NSFD method for a class of autonomous dynamical systems that satisfy positivity, stability and conservation laws. This NSFD method is constructed based on a novel non-local approximation for the right-side functions of the dynamical systems. By rigorous mathematical analyses, we have shown that the NSFD method is dynamically consistent with respect to the positivity, asymptotic stability and three classes of conservation laws, including direct conservation, generalized conservation and sub-conservation laws.
Additionally, a set of numerical experiments has been also performed to illustrate the theoretical findings and to show advantages of the proposed NSFD method over some well-known standard numerical schemes. The numerical results are not only consistent with the theoretical assertions but also indicate that the NSFD method is easy to be implemented and can be used to solve a broad range of mathematical models arising in real-life.

It worth noting that the threshold parameters
(m_{S},m_{P},m_{GCL})
for the NSFD method (
3.2
) and
(\phi_{P},\phi_{S},\phi_{GCL})
for the nonstandard Euler method (
3.19
) can be computed easily. More clearly, these threshold parameters can be obtained by simple calculations or simple numerical algorithms.

Similarly to the NSFD methods formulated in
[
7
,
28
,
29
]
, the NSFD method (
3.2
) also provides a stability-preserving numerical scheme for autonomous dynamical systems in general contexts. However, the positivity of the NSFD schemes in
[
7
,
28
,
29
]
is not guaranteed automatically while the NSFD method (
3.2
) has the ability to preserve not only the positivity but also the conservation laws of the system (
1.1
).

In
[
88
]
, Wood and Kojouharov introduced a nonstandard numerical method preserving the positivity and LAS of autonomous dynamical systems. This method is computationally efficient, easy
to be implemented and can be used to solve a broad range of problems in science and engineering. However, it requires the determination of the sign of the right-hand side functions at each iteration, and hence, the computational time is increased. For the NSFD method (
3.2
), we only need to compute the parameter
m
exactly once and then use it for all iterations. Furthermore, the computation of
m
is easy and can be done by a simple algorithm.

Before ending this section, we mention some open problems and future directions that research in NSFD methods can take.

(1)

The NSFD method (
3.2
) requires the determination of the number
\alpha
in the condition (
1.2
). Although
\alpha
can be determined easily for most of the models in literature, it is necessary to construct an algorithm for computing
\alpha
in general cases. Even though many important mathematical models in real-word applications satisfy the condition (
1.2
), this condition leads to a big restriction of the application of the proposed NSFD method. Hence, it is important to study extensions of the NSFD method (
3.2
) for mathematical models having the positivity and asymptotic stability but not satisfying Condition (
1.2
).

(2)

NSFD methods preserving the asymptotic stability of non-hyperbolic equilibrium points: Most stability-preserving NSFD methods including the NSFD method (
3.2
) require equilibrium points of dynamical systems to be hyperbolic. The construction of generalized NSFD methods preserving the LAS of dynamical systems even when they have non-hyperbolic equilibrium points has been studied in some previous work (see, for instance,
[
11
]
) but is still an important open problem.

(3)

Global asymptotic stability (GAS) of NSFD methods: It is well-known that analyzing the GAS of NSFD schemes for mathematical models having the GAS property is very important but not a simple task in general. This problem has been mentioned and considered in many previous works (see, for instance
[
3
,
8
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
42
,
81
,
82
]
). We observe from the numerical examples in Section
4
that the NSFD method can preserve not only the LAS but also the GAS of the continuous models under consideration. Therefore, it is suitable to predict that there exists a positive number
m_{GAS}
that plays as a GAS threshold of the NSFD method (
3.2
), i.e., the NSFD method (
3.2
) preserves the GAS of the system (
1.1
) whenever
m\geq m_{GAS}
. This prediction will be useful in studying the construction of NSFD methods preserving the GAS of dynamical systems.

(4)

High-order NSFD methods: High-order NSFD methods for differential equations have attracted the attention of many researchers with various approaches (see, for example
[
17
,
21
,
37
,
40
,
50
,
55
,
56
]
); hence, the improvement accuracy of the NSFD method (
3.2
) is essential with many practice applications. A simple approach for improving accuracy of the NSFD method (
3.2
) is to combine it with Richardson’s extrapolation method or variable step size strategies. This is easy to do because (
3.2
) is simple and fully explicit.

(5)

New NSFD methods for differential equation models arising in real-world applications: Although NSFD methods have been strongly developed and achieved many important results, fields of theory and practice always pose new and complex problems; hence, constructing effective NSFD methods for these problems is very essential. Recently, NSFD methods for fractional differential equations have been widely studied (see, for instance
[
12
,
20
]
). It was proved that NSFD methods for fractional-order systems are efficient and powerful; however, except for the positivity, other properties of fractional-order NSFD methods have not been widely discussed. It is easy to extend the NSFD method (
3.2
) for systems in the context of fractional derivative operators. Then, the asymptotic stability in particular and qualitative study in general for the fractional-order NSFD schemes will be posed. It is appropriate to predict that the parameter
m
under suitable conditions will guarantee the dynamic consistency of the proposed NSFD schemes.

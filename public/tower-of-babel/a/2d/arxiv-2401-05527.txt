Extremes of vector-valued locally additive Gaussian fields with application to double crossing probabilities
Pavel Ievlev, Nikolai Kriukov

Abstract
. The asymptotic analysis of high exceedance probabilities for Gaussian processes and fields has been a blooming research area since J. Pickands introduced the now-standard techniques in the late 60’s. The vector-valued processes, however, have long remained out of reach due to the lack of some key tools including Slepian’s lemma, Borell-TIS and Piterbarg inequalities. In a 2020 paper by K. Dȩbicki, E. Hashorva and L. Wang, the authors extended the double-sum method to a large class of vector-valued processes, both stationary and non-stationary. In this contribution we make one step forward, extending these results to a simple yet rich class of non-homogenous vector-valued Gaussian fields . As an application of our findings, we present an exact asymptotic result for the probability that a real-valued process first hits a high positive barrier and then a low negative barrier within a finite time horizon.

We now recapitulate the main ingredients of our approach and emphasize a few
things which, in our opinion, are worth mentioning.

First, it is known that the high exceedance event of a vector-valued Gaussian
process

\sigma_{\bm{b}}^{-2}(t)=\min_{\bm{x}\,\geq\,\bm{b}}\bm{x}^{\top}\,\Sigma^{-1}(t)\,\bm{x},

The asymptotic analysis of the high exceedance probability usually begins with
showing that the probability that the overshoot happens outside some small
vicinity of this maximizer is negligible.

3.
Double crossing probabilities: general facts

Let
X(t)
,
t\in[0,T]\subset\mathbb{R}
be a continuous centered Gaussian process,
with covariance function
r(t,s)=\mathbb{E}\left\{X(t)X(s)\right\}
.

We want to study the probability that in a given finite time interval the process

X
hits two distant barriers: one above and one below its initial point.
Formally, we study the asymptotics of

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,t,\,s\in[0,T]\colon X(t)>au,\ X(s)<-bu\right\},\qquad a,b>0,

which we shall refer to as the double crossing probability. For our purposes, it
may be conveniently rewritten as

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[0,T]^{2}\colon\bm{X}(\bm{t})>u\bm{b}\right\},\quad\bm{X}(\bm{t})=\left(X(t_{1}),\ -X(t_{2})\right)^{\top},\quad\bm{b}=(a,b)^{\top}.

The problem is thus reduced to the study of a simple (not double) crossing of a
two-dimensional vector-field
\bm{X}(\bm{t})
over
[0,T]^{2}
,
which is exactly the setup of our Main Theorem
1
.

Let us briefly show how Main Theorem
1
may be applied in this
case. First, we should find the maximizer of the generalized variance

\sigma_{a,b}^{2}(\bm{t})
defined by

(13)

\sigma_{a,b}^{-2}(\bm{t})=\min_{\bm{x}\geq(a,b)^{\top}}\bm{x}^{\top}\,\Sigma^{-1}(\bm{t})\,\bm{x},

where the matrix
\Sigma(\bm{t})
is given by

\Sigma(\bm{t})=R(\bm{t},\bm{t})
and

R(\bm{t},\bm{s})=\mathbb{E}\left\{\bm{X}(\bm{t})\,\bm{X}(\bm{s})^{\top}\right\}=\begin{pmatrix}r(t_{1},s_{1})&-r(t_{1},s_{2})\\
-r(t_{2},s_{1})&r(t_{2},s_{2})\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bm{t}=(t_{1},t_{2})^{\top},\quad\bm{s}=(s_{1},s_{2})^{\top}.

The inverse of
\Sigma(\bm{t})
, necessary to compute the generalized variance, exists
for all
\bm{t}\not\in\{\bm{t}=(t,t)^{\top}\colon t\in[0,T]\}
and is given by

\Sigma^{-1}(\bm{t})=\frac{1}{r(t_{1},t_{1})\,r(t_{2},t_{2})-r^{2}(t_{1},t_{2})}\begin{pmatrix}r(t_{2},t_{2})&r(t_{1},t_{2})\\
r(t_{1},t_{2})&r(t_{1},t_{1})\end{pmatrix}.

Near the diagonal
\{\bm{t}=(t,t)^{\top}\colon t\in[0,T]\}
the matrix
\Sigma(\bm{t})

is degenerate, so we need some additional lemma to deal with the probability that
the extreme event happens there. Intuitively, such event means that a process
has managed to hit both boundaries during a very short time interval, which seems
unlikely. The precise meaning to this is given by the next lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
X(t)
be a separable centered Gaussian process with a.s. continuous
sample paths. For
\varepsilon>0
, define
D_{\varepsilon}=\{\bm{t}\in[0,T]^{2}\colon|t_{1}-t_{2}|<\varepsilon\}
.
If there exists a function
f
such that

\mathbb{E}\left\{\Big[X(t+l)-X(t)\Big]^{2}\right\}<f(l)

for all
t\in[0,T]
,
l>0
and
f(l)\to 0
as
l\to 0
, then
for any
\delta>0
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in D_{\varepsilon}\colon X(t_{1})>au,\ X(t_{2})<-bu\right\}=o\left(e^{-\delta u^{2}}\right).

Unfortunately, even the problem of minimizing
\sigma_{\bm{b}}^{-2}(\bm{t})

over
\{\bm{t}=(t,s)^{\top}\colon|t-s|>\varepsilon\}
is too
hard in its full generality. In the next two sections we study the double crossing
probabilities for two classes of processes: stationary with positive correlation
and fractional Brownian motion.

4.
Double crossing probabilities: stationary case

Let
X(t)
be a stationary process with unit variance and positive
correlation function
r(t,s)=\rho(|t-s|)>0
satisfying

(14)

\rho(t)=1-\vartheta\,t^{\alpha}+o\left(t^{\alpha}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad t\to 0

with some
\vartheta>0
and
\alpha\in(0,2]
. We additionally assume
that
\rho
is strictly decreasing and differentiable in
t>0
.

We need to minimize

\frac{x_{1}^{2}+2x_{1}x_{2}\,\rho(|t_{1}-t_{2}|)+x_{2}^{2}}{1-\rho^{2}(t_{1},t_{2})}

with respect to
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})^{\top}
subject to
\bm{x}\geq(a,b)^{\top}
,
and then minimize it again, but with respect to
\bm{t}
sufficiently far
away from the diagonal. The unique solution of the first minimization problem in
this case can easily be shown to be
\bm{x}=(a,b)^{\top}
. Therefore, we have

\sigma_{a,b}^{-2}(\bm{t})=\min_{\bm{x}\geq(a,b)^{\top}}\bm{x}^{\top}\,\Sigma^{-1}(\bm{t})\,\bm{x}=\frac{a^{2}+2ab\,\rho(|t_{1}-t_{2}|)+b^{2}}{1-\rho^{2}(|t_{1}-t_{2}|)}.

To solve the second, we note by rewriting
\sigma_{a,b}^{-2}(\bm{t})
as

\sigma_{a,b}^{-2}(\bm{t})=\frac{(a+b)^{2}}{1-\rho^{2}(|t_{1}-t_{2}|)}-\frac{2ab}{1+\rho(|t_{1}-t_{2}|)}

that
\sigma_{a,b}^{-2}(\bm{t})
attains its minimum at the same point
as
\rho(|t_{1}-t_{2}|)
. Since
\rho
is decreasing, we have
two minimizing points
\bm{t}_{*,1}=(0,T)^{\top}
and

\bm{t}_{*,2}=(T,0)^{\top}
. We can easily find the values of the
generalized variance at these points to be equal:

\sigma_{a,b}^{-2}(\bm{t}_{*,1})=\sigma_{a,b}^{-2}(\bm{t}_{*,2})=\frac{a^{2}+2ab\rho(T)+b^{2}}{1-\rho^{2}(T)}.

By Lemma
1
and Piterbarg inequality, we can
easily show that for any
\varepsilon>0
we have

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[\bm{0},\bm{T}]\colon\bm{X}(\bm{t})>u\,\bm{b}\right\}\\[7.0pt]
\sim\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[0,\varepsilon]\times[T-\varepsilon,T]\colon\bm{X}(\bm{t})>u\,\bm{b}\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[T-\varepsilon,T]\times[0,\varepsilon]\colon\bm{X}(\bm{t})>u\,\bm{b}\right\}\\[7.0pt]
=\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[0,\varepsilon]^{2}\colon\bm{X}_{1}(\bm{t})>u\,\bm{b}\right\}+\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[0,\varepsilon]^{2}\colon\bm{X}_{2}(\bm{t})>u\,\bm{b}\right\},

where in the last line we performed appropriate time changes in both
probabilities by introducing

\bm{X}_{1}(\bm{t})=(X(t_{1}),-X(T-t_{2}))^{\top}

and

\bm{X}_{2}(\bm{t})=(X(T-t_{1}),-X(t_{2}))^{\top}
.

In order to apply Main Theorem
1
to

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[0,\varepsilon]^{2}\colon\bm{X}_{1}(\bm{t})>u\,\bm{b}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[0,\varepsilon]^{2}\colon\bm{X}_{2}(\bm{t})>u\,\bm{b}\right\},

we need to derive asymptotic expansions of the corresponding covariances.
This is done in the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

The random field
\bm{X}_{1}(\bm{t})
satisfies the
assumptions
A1:
to
A3:
of Theorem
1

with

\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha,\quad\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=1,\quad\mathcal{F}=\varnothing

and

A_{2,1}=A_{2,2}^{\top}=-\rho^{\prime}(T)\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad A_{5,1}=\vartheta\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad A_{5,2}=\vartheta\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Moreover,

\bm{w}=\Sigma^{-1}(\bm{0})\,(a,b)^{\top}=\frac{1}{1-\rho^{2}(T)}\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho(T)\\
\rho(T)&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a\\
b\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{1-\rho^{2}(T)}\begin{pmatrix}a+b\rho(T)\\
b+a\rho(T)\end{pmatrix}

Assumption
A2.4
is satisfied with
\xi_{i}
given by

\xi_{1}=\bm{w}^{\top}\,A_{2,1}\,\bm{w}=\xi_{2}=\bm{w}^{\top}\,A_{2,2}\,\bm{w}=\frac{-\rho^{\prime}(T)(a+b\rho(T))(b+a\rho(T))}{(1-\rho^{2}(T))^{2}}>0,

and
A2.5
with
\varkappa_{i}
given by

\varkappa_{1}=\bm{w}^{\top}\,A_{5,1}\,\bm{w}=\varkappa_{2}(\bm{w})=\bm{w}^{\top}\,A_{5,2}\,\bm{w}=\frac{C(b+a\rho(T))^{2}}{(1-\rho^{2}(T))^{2}}>0.

Using Lemma
1
, and noting that

\mathcal{F}=\varnothing
, we may apply the first assertion of
Corollary
1
instead of Theorem
1
,
and obtain the following theorem on the asymptotics of double crossing
probabilities.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X(t)
,
t\in[0,T]
be a centered a.s. continuous stationary Gaussian process with
unit variance and positive strictly decreasing and differentiable correlation
function
\rho(t)>0
which satisfies (
14
)
with some
\vartheta>0
and
\alpha\in(0,2]
. Define

Then

Pickands case:

If

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,t,\,s\in[0,T]\colon X(t)>au,\ X(s)<-bu\right\}\sim C\,\mathcal{H}^{2}\,u^{4/\alpha-2}\,p(u),

\mathcal{H}=\lim_{S\to\infty}\frac{1}{S}\,\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,S]}e^{B_{H}(t)-t^{2H}/2}\right\},\quad C=\frac{2^{1+2/\alpha}\,\theta^{2/\alpha}}{(\rho^{\prime}(T))^{2}}\left(\frac{(1-\rho^{2}(T))^{2}}{(a+b\rho(T))(b+a\rho(T))}\right)^{2-2/\alpha}.

If
\alpha=1

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,t,s\in[0,T]\colon X(t)>au,\ X(s)<-bu\right\}\sim 2\ \widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\,\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{2}\,p(u),

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k}=\lim_{S\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,S]}e^{B(t)-(1+\lambda_{k})t/2}\right\},\qquad\lambda_{1}=\frac{-\rho^{\prime}(T)\,w_{2}}{2\,w_{1}\,\vartheta},\quad\lambda_{2}=\frac{-\rho^{\prime}(T)\,w_{1}}{2\,w_{2}\,\vartheta}>0.

Talagrand case:

If
\alpha>1
,

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,t,\,s\in[0,T]\colon X(t)>au,\ X(s)<-bu\right\}\sim 2\,p(u).

5.
Double crossing probabilities: fBm case

In this section we study the double crossing
probability (
1
) in case when

X(t)
is a fractional Brownian motion
B_{H}(t)
, that is, a Gaussian
process associated to the following covariance function:

r(t,s)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\Big).

As explained in Section
3
, we should first
minimize
\sigma_{\bm{b}}^{-2}(\bm{t})
, defined
in (
13
), in
\bm{x}

subject to
\bm{x}\geq(a,b)^{\top}
, and then minimize it again, but with
respect to
\bm{t}
sufficiently far away from the diagonal. Minimization in

\bm{x}
yields
\bm{x}=(a,b)^{\top}
, and therefore we have

\sigma_{a,b}^{-2}(\bm{t})=\min_{\bm{x}\geq(a,b)^{\top}}\bm{x}^{\top}\,\Sigma^{-1}(\bm{t})\,\bm{x}=\frac{a^{2}\,t_{1}^{2H}+2ab\,r(t_{1},t_{2})+b^{2}\,t_{2}^{2H}}{(t_{1}t_{2})^{2H}-r^{2}(t_{1},t_{2})}.

For the second minimization, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3
.

There exists
t_{*}\in(0,T)
, such that the function

\sigma_{a,b}^{-2}(\bm{t})
defined on

\{\bm{t}\in[0,T]^{2}\colon|t_{1}-t_{2}|>\varepsilon\}
,
\varepsilon>0

attains,

if
a<b
:

its unique minimum at point

\bm{t}_{*,1}=(T,t_{*})^{\top}

if
a>b
:

its unique minimum at point

\bm{t}_{*,2}=(t_{*},T)^{\top}

if
a=b
:

its minimum at exactly two points

\bm{t}_{*,1}=(T,t_{*})^{\top}
and
\bm{t}_{*,2}=(t_{*},T)^{\top}

Moreover, we have

\sigma_{\bm{b}}^{-2}(\bm{t}_{*,1})-\sigma_{\bm{b}}^{-2}(\bm{t}_{*,1}-\bm{\tau})\sim-\kappa_{1}\,\tau_{1}-\kappa_{2}\,\tau_{2}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{\bm{b}}^{-2}(\bm{t}_{*,2})-\sigma_{\bm{b}}^{-2}(\bm{t}_{*,2}-\bm{\tau})\sim-\kappa_{2}\,\tau_{1}^{2}-\kappa_{1}\,\tau_{2}

with

\kappa_{1}\coloneqq-\frac{\partial\sigma_{\bm{b}}^{-2}}{\partial t_{1}}(\bm{t}_{*,1})=-\frac{\partial\sigma_{\bm{b}}^{-2}}{\partial t_{2}}(\bm{t}_{*,2})>0,\qquad\kappa_{2}\coloneqq\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma_{\bm{b}}^{-2}}{\partial t_{2}^{2}}(\bm{t}_{*,1})=\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma_{\bm{b}}^{-2}}{\partial t_{1}^{2}}(\bm{t}_{*,2})>0.

By Lemma
1
and Piterbarg
inequality (
25
), we can easily show that for any

\varepsilon>0
we have

(15)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[\bm{0},\bm{T}]\colon\bm{X}(\bm{t})>u\,\bm{b}\right\}

\displaystyle\sim\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[T-\varepsilon,T]\times[t_{*}-\varepsilon,t_{*}+\varepsilon]\colon\bm{X}(\bm{t})>u\,\bm{b}\right\}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[t_{*}-\varepsilon,t_{*}+\varepsilon]\times[T-\varepsilon,T]\colon\bm{X}(\bm{t})>u\,\bm{b}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[0,\varepsilon]\times[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\colon\bm{X}_{1}(\bm{t})>u\,\bm{b}\right\}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\times[0,\varepsilon]\colon\bm{X}_{2}(\bm{t})>u\,\bm{b}\right\},

where in the last line we performed appropriate time changes in both
probabilities by introducing

\bm{X}_{1}(\bm{t})=(X(T-t_{1}),-X(t_{2}-t_{*}))^{\top}

and

\bm{X}_{2}(\bm{t})=(X(t_{1}-t_{*}),-X(T-t_{2}))^{\top}
.

Next, assume that
a>b
. Then we can use the Piterbarg
inequality (
25
) again to show that

(16)

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\times[0,\varepsilon]\colon\bm{X}_{2}(\bm{t})>u\,\bm{b}\right\}=o\big(\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[0,\varepsilon]\times[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\colon\bm{X}_{1}(\bm{t})>u\,\bm{b}\right\}\big).

Intuitively this means that the process is less likely to first hit a higher
barrier and then hit the lower than the other way around. If
a=b
, the
two probabilities are equal, which is clear from the symmetry.
Combining (
15
) and (
16
),
we obtain that if
a\geq b
, then

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[\bm{0},\bm{T}]\colon\bm{X}(\bm{t})>u\bm{b}\right\}\sim\left(1+\mathbb{1}_{a=b}\right)\,\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in[0,\varepsilon]\times[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\colon\bm{X}_{1}(\bm{t})>u\bm{b}\right\}.

In order to apply Theorem
1
to

+u^{2-2\alpha_{i}/\nu_{i}}\,\left[-\frac{\bm{w}^{\top}A_{5,i}\,\bm{w}}{32}\,(\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}+c_{1}\Big[S_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}+S_{i}\,\left((\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i})\vee S_{i}\right)^{\alpha_{i}-1}\Big]\right].

Case
\tau_{i}=\lambda_{i}=0
.

By assumptions of the lemma, this happens if and only if
i\in\mathcal{K}\cup\mathcal{J}
. The
right-hand side of (
54
) reads:

c_{2}\,S_{i}^{\beta_{i}}+c_{3}\,u^{2-2\alpha_{i}/\nu_{i}}\,S_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}

with some new
c_{2},\,c_{3}>0
. This bound can be further simplified if we
note that for
i\in\mathcal{K}
the second term tends to zero as
u\to\infty
for a
fixed
S_{i}
. Hence, this contribution is at most

c_{2}\,S_{i}^{\beta_{i}}+c_{4}.

Case
\lambda_{i}=\tau_{i}\neq 0
.

By assumptions of the lemma, this happens if and only if
i\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\subsetneq\mathcal{I}
. Note
that this set may be empty. The right-hand side of (
54
)
reads:

u^{2-2\beta_{i}/\nu_{i}}\left[-\frac{\bm{w}^{\top}A_{2,i}\,\bm{w}}{4}\,\lambda_{i}^{\beta_{i}}+c_{1}\Big[S_{i}\,(\lambda_{i}\vee S_{i})^{\beta_{i}-1}+\varepsilon^{-1}\,S_{j}^{2}\,(\lambda_{j}\vee S_{j})^{\beta_{j}-2}\Big]\right]+c_{4}\,S_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}.

There exists
N_{i}
such that for all
\lambda_{i}\geq N_{i}\,S_{i}
this
contribution may be bounded from above by

-\frac{\bm{w}^{\top}A_{2,i}\,\bm{w}}{8}\,u^{2-2\beta_{i}/\nu_{i}}\,\lambda_{i}^{\beta_{i}}+c_{4}\,S_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}.

Case
\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i}>S_{i}
.

By assumptions of the lemma, this condition holds for all
i\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
, and
this set is non-empty. The right-hand side of (
54
)
reads:

u^{2-2\beta_{i}/\nu_{i}}\left[-\frac{\bm{w}^{\top}A_{2,i}\,\bm{w}}{4}\,\lambda_{i}^{\beta_{i}}+c_{1}\Big[S_{i}\,\lambda_{i}^{\beta_{i}-1}+\varepsilon^{-1}\,S_{j}^{2}\,\lambda_{j}^{\beta_{j}-2}\Big]\right]+\left[-\frac{\bm{w}^{\top}A_{5,i}\,\bm{w}}{32}\,(\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}+c_{5}S_{i}\,(\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i})^{\alpha_{i}-1}\right].

There exists
N_{i}
such that for all
\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i}\geq N_{i}S_{i}
this
contribution is at most

-\frac{\bm{w}^{\top}A_{2,i}\,\bm{w}}{8}\,u^{2-2\beta_{i}/\nu_{i}}\,\lambda_{i}^{\beta_{i}}-\frac{\bm{w}^{\top}A_{5,i}\,\bm{w}}{64}\,(\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}.

Combined bound.

We have obtained the following inequality: if

(55)

\lambda_{i}\geq N_{i}\,S_{i}\quad\text{for}\quad i\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime},\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i}\geq N_{i}\,S_{i}\quad\text{for}\quad i\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}^{\prime},

then

(56)

\ln\frac{P_{\bm{b}}(\bm{\tau},\bm{\lambda},\bm{S})}{c_{7}\,H_{u}(\bm{\tau})\,\mathbb{P}\{\bm{X}(\bm{0})>u\bm{b}\}}\leq-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}u^{2-2\beta_{i}/\nu_{i}}\,\bm{w}^{\top}A_{2,i}\,\bm{w}\,\lambda_{i}^{\beta_{i}}\\[7.0pt]
-\frac{1}{64}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}\bm{w}^{\top}A_{5,i}\,\bm{w}\,(\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}+c_{6}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}S_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}+c_{6}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}\cup\mathcal{K}}S_{i}^{\beta_{i}}.

Next, we want to lift conditions (
55
).

Lifting the condition
\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i}\geq N_{i}\,S_{i}
.

Assume that there is exactly one
i\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
such that

\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i}<N_{i}\,S_{i}
. The general case can be addressed in the same way.
Define
\Delta\coloneqq(\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i}-S_{i})/N_{i}
, and slice

P_{\bm{b}}
in the
i
-th direction using this new scale:

(57)

P_{\bm{b}}(\bm{\tau},\bm{\lambda},\bm{S})\leq\sum_{j=\tau_{i}/\Delta}^{(\tau_{i}+S_{i})/\Delta}\sum_{k=\lambda_{i}/\Delta}^{(\lambda_{i}+S_{i})/\Delta}P_{\bm{b}}(k,j,\Delta)\leq S_{i}^{2}\,\Delta^{-2}\,P_{\bm{b}}\left(j^{*}\Delta,\,k_{*}\,\Delta,\,\Delta\right),

where
j^{*}=(\tau_{i}+S_{i})/\Delta
,
k_{*}=\lambda_{i}/\Delta
, and

P_{\bm{b}}(\tau,\lambda,\Delta)=\mathbb{P}\left\{\begin{aligned} &\exists\,t_{i}\in u^{-2/\nu_{i}}[\tau,\tau+\Delta],&\\[3.0pt]
&\exists\,\bm{t}^{\prime}\in u^{-2/\bm{\nu}^{\prime}}[\bm{\tau}^{\prime},\bm{\tau}^{\prime}+\bm{S}^{\prime}]\colon&\bm{X}(\bm{t})>u\bm{b},\\[3.0pt]
&\exists\,s_{i}\in u^{-2/\nu_{i}}[\lambda,\lambda+\Delta],&\\[3.0pt]
&\exists\,\bm{s}^{\prime}\in u^{-2/\bm{\nu}^{\prime}}[\bm{\lambda}^{\prime},\bm{\lambda}^{\prime}+\bm{S}^{\prime}]\colon&\bm{X}(\bm{s})>u\bm{b}\end{aligned}\right\},\qquad\bm{x}^{\prime}=(x_{j})_{j\neq i}.

Note that

k_{*}\,\Delta-j^{*}\Delta=\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i}-S_{i}=N_{i}\Delta,

which means that we can apply (
56
). Therefore,

(58)

\ln\frac{P_{\bm{b}}(\bm{\tau},\bm{\lambda},\bm{S})}{c_{7}\,H_{u}(\bm{\tau})\,\mathbb{P}\{\bm{X}(\bm{0})>u\bm{b}\}\,\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}S_{i}^{2}\,\Delta^{-2}}\leq-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}u^{2-2\beta_{i}/\nu_{i}}\,\bm{w}^{\top}A_{2,i}\,\bm{w}\,\lambda_{i}^{\beta_{i}}\\[7.0pt]
-\frac{1}{64}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}\bm{w}^{\top}A_{5,i}\,\bm{w}\,(\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i}-S_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}+c_{6}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}S_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}+c_{6}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}\cup\mathcal{K}}S_{i}^{\beta_{i}}.

which is now valid without the second condition of (
55
).

Lifting the condition
\lambda_{i}\geq N_{i}\,S_{i}
.

Assume now that there is exactly one
i\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
such that

\lambda_{i}<N_{i}\,S_{i}
. Recall that for
i\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
holds
\lambda_{i}=\tau_{i}
.
Using the same approach, take
\Delta\coloneqq(\lambda_{i}\wedge 1)/N_{i}
. Then,

(59)

P_{\bm{b}}(\bm{\tau},\bm{\lambda},\bm{S})\leq\sum_{k\neq j}P_{\bm{b}}(k\Delta,j\Delta,\Delta)+\sum_{k=j}P_{\bm{b}}(j\Delta,j\Delta,\Delta).

where the sums are taken over

\left\{(k,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+}\colon\lambda_{i}/\Delta\leq k,\,j\leq(\lambda_{i}+S_{i})/\Delta\right\}.

Note that all the results up to this point were valid without any assumptions on

\bm{S}>\bm{0}
. Now, assuming that
S_{i}
is large enough, we can use
the bounds proven above to show that

\sum_{|k-j|\geq 2}P_{\bm{b}}(k\Delta,j\Delta,\Delta)\leq c_{8}\sum_{k=j}P_{\bm{b}}(j\Delta,j\Delta,\Delta).

It can also be shown that

\sum_{|k-j|=1}P_{\bm{b}}(k\Delta,j\Delta,\Delta)\leq c_{9}\sum_{k=j}P_{\bm{b}}(j\Delta,j\Delta,\Delta).

It is therefore enough to bound the second sum of (
59
).
We have

(60)

P_{\bm{b}}(\bm{\tau},\bm{\lambda},\bm{S})\leq c_{9}\,S_{i}\,\Delta^{-1}\,P_{\bm{b}}(j_{*}\Delta,j_{*}\Delta,\Delta).

Since

j_{*}\Delta=\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{i}\wedge 1=N_{i}\,\Delta
,
the bound (
58
) is applicable. The
i
-th term of the
right-hand side is

-\frac{\bm{w}^{\top}A_{2,i}\,\bm{w}}{8}\,u^{2-2\beta_{i}/\nu_{i}}\,(j_{*}\,\Delta)^{\beta_{i}}+c_{6}\,\Delta_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}.

Note that
j_{*}=\lambda_{i}/\Delta
, so the first term did not change, whereas the
second is now bounded by a constant:
c_{6}(\lambda_{i}\wedge 1)^{\alpha_{i}}\leq c_{6}
. We have
thus shown that the following bound

(61)

\ln\frac{P_{\bm{b}}(\bm{\tau},\bm{\lambda},\bm{S})}{c_{10}\,H_{u}(\bm{\tau})\,\mathbb{P}\{\bm{X}(\bm{0})>u\bm{b}\}\,\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}S_{i}^{2}\,\Delta^{-2}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}}S_{i}\,\Delta^{-1}}\\[7.0pt]
\leq-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}u^{2-2\beta_{i}/\nu_{i}}\,\bm{w}^{\top}A_{2,i}\,\bm{w}\,\lambda_{i}^{\beta_{i}}-\frac{1}{64}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}\bm{w}^{\top}A_{5,i}\,\bm{w}\,(\lambda_{i}-\tau_{i}-S_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}+c_{6}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}\cup\mathcal{K}}S_{i}^{\beta_{i}}

is valid without conditions (
55
). This concludes the
proof.
∎

C/\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}}S_{i}
, we obtain

\frac{\mathbb{\Sigma}_{2,1}^{\prime}(u,\bm{\Lambda},\bm{S})}{\mathbb{\Sigma}_{1}^{\prime}(u,\bm{\Lambda},\bm{S})}\leq C\prod_{i_{c}\in\mathcal{I}^{c}}\exp\left(c\Lambda_{i_{c}}^{\alpha_{i_{c}}}\right)\times\\[7.0pt]
\times\sum_{\mathcal{I}_{0}\subsetneq\mathcal{I}}\ \sum_{\varnothing\,\neq\,\mathcal{I}_{1}\subset\mathcal{I}}\prod_{i_{0}\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}\frac{\Lambda_{i_{0}}}{S_{i}}\prod_{i_{1}\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}\exp\left(-\frac{\xi_{i_{1}}(\bm{w})}{32}S_{i}^{\alpha_{i_{1}}/2}\right)\prod_{i_{2}\in\mathcal{I}_{2}}\frac{1}{S_{i}}\exp\left(-\frac{\xi_{i_{2}}(\bm{w})}{32}S_{i_{2}}^{\alpha_{i_{2}}}\right).

and

\lim_{\bm{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{I}^{c}}\to\infty}\lim_{\bm{S}_{\mathcal{I}}\to\infty}\limsup_{u\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{\Sigma}_{2,1}^{\prime}(u,\bm{\Lambda},\bm{S})}{\mathbb{\Sigma}_{1}^{\prime}(u,\bm{\Lambda},\bm{S})}=0.

Finally, let us find a bound for the second term of (
72
).
By local Pickands lemma

\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in C_{j}\colon\bm{X}(\bm{t})>u\bm{b}\right\}\sim H_{\bm{\nu},\mathbb{V}_{\bm{w}},\mathbb{W}_{\bm{w}}}\left(\left[\bm{0},\bm{1}_{\mathcal{I}_{1}}\bm{S}^{1/2}+\bm{1}_{\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{1}}\bm{S}\right]\right)\ \mathbb{P}\left\{\bm{X}(\bm{0})>u\bm{b}\right\}

and therefore, using
N_{u,i}=\left\lceil\Lambda_{i}u^{\zeta_{i}}/S_{i}\right\rceil
, obtain

\mathbb{\Sigma}_{2,1}^{\prime\prime}(u,\bm{\Lambda},\bm{S})\coloneqq\sum_{\bm{k}\leq\bm{N}_{u}}\ \sum_{\varnothing\,\neq\,\mathcal{I}_{1}\subset\mathcal{I}}\quad\sum_{j=0}^{2^{|\mathcal{I}_{1}|}-1}\mathbb{P}\left\{\exists\,\bm{t}\in C_{j}\colon\bm{X}(\bm{t})>u\bm{b}\right\}\sim\\[7.0pt]
\sim\mathbb{P}\left\{\bm{X}(\bm{0})>u\bm{b}\right\}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\frac{\Lambda_{i}u^{\zeta_{i}}}{S_{i}}\sum_{\varnothing\,\neq\,\mathcal{I}_{1}\subset\mathcal{I}}2^{|\mathcal{I}_{1}|}H_{\bm{\nu},\mathbb{V}_{\bm{w}},\mathbb{W}_{\bm{w}}}\left(\left[\bm{0},\bm{1}_{\mathcal{I}_{1}}\bm{S}^{1/2}+\bm{1}_{\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{1}}\bm{S}\right]\right)

and

\frac{\mathbb{\Sigma}_{2,1}^{\prime\prime}(u,\bm{\Lambda},\bm{S})}{\mathbb{\Sigma}_{1}^{\prime}(u,\bm{\Lambda},\bm{S})}\leq\Bigg[\prod_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Lambda_{i}\Bigg]\sum_{\varnothing\,\neq\,\mathcal{I}_{1}\subset\mathcal{I}}2^{|\mathcal{I}_{1}|}H_{\bm{\nu},\mathbb{V}_{\bm{w}},\mathbb{W}_{\bm{w}}}\left(\left[\bm{0},\bm{1}_{\mathcal{I}_{1}}\bm{S}^{1/2}+\bm{1}_{\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{1}}\bm{S}\right]\right)\prod_{j\in\mathcal{I}}\frac{1}{S_{i}}.

By subadditivity of the generalized Pickands-Piterbarg constant,

\lim_{\bm{S}\to\infty}H_{\bm{\nu},\mathbb{V}_{\bm{w}},\mathbb{W}_{\bm{w}}}\left([\bm{0},\bm{1}_{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}\bm{S}^{1/2}+\bm{1}_{\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}\bm{S}]\right)\prod_{j\in\mathcal{I}}\frac{1}{S_{i}}\leq\lim_{\bm{S}\to\bm{\infty}}H_{\bm{\nu},\mathbb{V}_{\bm{w}},\mathbb{W}_{\bm{w}}}\left([\bm{0},\bm{1}]\right)\prod_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}S_{j}^{-1/2}=0,

and therefore

\lim_{\bm{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{I}^{c}}\to\infty}\lim_{\bm{S}_{\mathcal{I}}\to\infty}\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{\Sigma}_{2,1}^{\prime\prime}(u,\bm{\Lambda},\bm{S})}{\mathbb{\Sigma}_{1}^{\prime}(u,\bm{\Lambda},\bm{S})}=0.

∎

8.1.
Covariance lemma

Lemma 16
.

Let
D=(D_{i,j})_{i,j\in\mathcal{F}}
be a block matrix, each block of which is
a
d\times d
matrix. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

D\succcurlyeq 0
.

(2)

There exists a family
(C_{i,k})_{i,k\in\mathcal{F}}
of
d\times d
matrices such that

D_{i,j}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{F}}C_{i,k}\,C_{k,j}^{\top}.

(3)

A matrix-valued function
R
defined by

R(\bm{t},\bm{s})=\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{F}}D_{i,j}\,t_{i}\,s_{j},\qquad\bm{t},\bm{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

is a covariance function of some
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-Gaussian random field.

If either of statements hold, then
R
is the covariance function of

\bm{Z}(\bm{t})\coloneqq\sum_{k\in\mathcal{F}}C_{k}(\bm{t})\,\bm{\mathcal{N}}_{k},\quad C_{k}(\bm{t})\coloneqq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{F}}C_{i,k}\,t_{i},

where
\bm{\mathcal{N}}_{k}\sim N(\bm{0},I)
are independent standard Gaussian
vectors.

Proof of Lemma
16
.

Let
n=|\mathcal{F}|
. If
D\succcurlyeq 0
, then there exists a
(nd)\times(nd)

matrix
C
such that
D=CC^{\top}
. By viewing
C
as a block matrix
with the same block structure as
D
, we find that

D_{i,j}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{F}}C_{i,k}\,C_{k,j}^{\top}.

Hence,

R(\bm{t},\bm{s})=\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{F}}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{F}}C_{i,k}\,C_{k,j}^{\top}\,t_{i}\,s_{j}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{F}}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{F}}C_{i,k}\,t_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{F}}C_{j,k}\,s_{j}\right)^{\top},

which clearly is positive definite.

If, on the other hand,
R
is positive definite, then for all collections

\{(\bm{t}_{i},\bm{z}_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{F}},\ i=1,\ldots,p\}
holds

0\leq\sum_{k,l=1}^{p}\bm{z}_{k}^{\top}R(\bm{t}_{k},\bm{t}_{l})\,\bm{z}_{l}=\sum_{k,l=1}^{p}\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{F}}\bm{z}_{k}^{\top}D_{i,j}\,\bm{z}_{l}\,t_{k,i}\,s_{l,j}=\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{F}}\bm{y}_{i}^{\top}D_{i,j}\,\bm{y}_{j}=\bm{y}^{\top}D\,\bm{y},

where

\bm{y}_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\bm{z}_{k}\,t_{k,i},\quad\bm{y}=(\bm{y}_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{F}}.

Since
\bm{z}_{k}
and
\bm{t}_{k}
are arbitrary,
\bm{y}
is also
arbitrary. Hence,
D\succcurlyeq 0
, and the chain of implications has come full
circle.
∎

8.2.
Supplementary lemma on the quadratic optimization problem

Proof of Lemma
7
.

For
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
define

f_{j}(\bm{t})=\widetilde{\bm{b}}(\bm{t})\,\Sigma^{-1}(\bm{t})\,\bm{e}_{j},\quad g_{j}(\bm{t})=\widetilde{b}_{j}(\bm{t})-b_{j}.

By continuity of
\Sigma^{-1}(\bm{t})
and the fact that
\Sigma\mapsto\widetilde{\bm{b}}

is Lipschitz continuous, these functions are continuous and we have

\displaystyle I(\bm{t})

\displaystyle=\max\left\{V\,\middle|\,V=\left\{i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\colon\ g_{i}(\bm{t})=0,\ f_{i}(\bm{t})>0\right\}\right\},

\displaystyle K(\bm{t})

\displaystyle=\max\left\{V\,\middle|\,V=\left\{j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\colon\ g_{j}(\bm{t})=0,\ f_{j}(\bm{t})=0\right\}\right\},

\displaystyle L(\bm{t})

\displaystyle=\max\left\{V\,\middle|\,V=\left\{l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\colon\ g_{l}(\bm{t})>0,\ f_{l}(\bm{t})=0\right\}\right\},

where the maximum is in the power set of
\{1,\ldots,d\}
ordered by
inclusion. It follows from the quadratic optimization lemma that
these three sets are disjoint and the equality

I(\bm{t})\cup K(\bm{t})\cup L(\bm{t})=\{1,\ldots,d\}

holds for each
\bm{t}\in E
.

For
U,\,V\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}
define

A_{U}\coloneqq\bigcap_{j\in V}f_{j}^{-1}(0)\cap\bigcap_{j\in V^{c}}\left(f_{j}^{-1}(0)\right)^{c},\quad B_{V}\coloneqq\bigcap_{j\in V}g_{j}^{-1}(0)\cap\bigcap_{j\in V^{c}}\left(g_{j}^{-1}(0)\right)^{c},\quad C_{U,V}=A_{U}\cap B_{V}.

One can easily check the following properties of these sets:

(1)

V\neq U\iff A_{V}\cap A_{U}=\varnothing\iff\ B_{V}\cap B_{U}=\varnothing

and hence
(U,V)\neq(U^{\prime},V^{\prime})\iff C_{U,V}\neq C_{U^{\prime},V^{\prime}}
.

(2)

(A_{V})_{V\in 2^{d}},\ (B_{V})_{V\in 2^{d}}

and
(C_{U,V})_{U,\,V\in 2^{d}}
are finite covers of
E
.

(3)

A_{V}
,
B_{V}
and
C_{U,V}
are locally closed sets
(that is, intersections of an open and a closed set).

(4)

I(\bm{t})=U^{c}
on
A_{U}
.

(5)

L(\bm{t})=V^{c}
on
B_{V}
.

(6)

K(\bm{t})=U\cap V
on
C_{U,V}
.

In other words, we have

I(\bm{t})=\sum_{U\in 2^{d}}U^{c}\,\mathbb{1}_{A_{U}}(\bm{t}),\quad L(\bm{t})=\sum_{V\in 2^{d}}V^{c}\,\mathbb{1}_{B_{V}}(\bm{t}),\quad K(\bm{t})=\sum_{U,\,V\in 2^{d}}U\cap V\,\mathbb{1}_{C_{U,V}}(\bm{t}).

Note that some of
A_{V}
’s,
B_{V}
’s or
C_{U,V}
’s may be empty.

Let us split
2^{d}
in two parts

2^{d}=\mathbb{V}_{1}(\bm{t})\cup\mathbb{V}_{2}(\bm{t})

in the following way:

(1)

if
V\in\mathbb{V}_{1}(\bm{t})
, then for all
\varepsilon>0
holds

A_{V}\cap B_{\varepsilon}(\bm{t})\neq\varnothing
,

(2)

if
V\in\mathbb{V}_{2}(\bm{t})
, then there exists

\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(V,\bm{t})>0
such that

A_{V}\cap B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(\bm{t})=\varnothing
.

Define

\varepsilon(\bm{t})\coloneqq\min_{V\in\mathbb{V}_{2}(\bm{t})}\varepsilon_{0}(V,\bm{t}).

If
\bm{s}\in B_{\varepsilon}(\bm{t})
with
\varepsilon<\varepsilon(\bm{t})
there
exists
V\in\mathbb{V}_{1}(\bm{t})

such that
\bm{s}\in A_{V}
and therefore
I(\bm{s})=V^{c}
.
Since
j\in I^{c}(\bm{t})=K(\bm{t})\cup L(\bm{t})=V
if and only
if
f_{j}(\bm{s})=0
, using continuity of
f_{j}
and letting

\varepsilon\to 0
we obtain that
f_{j}(\bm{t})=0
and therefore

K(\bm{s})\cup L(\bm{s})=V\subset K(\bm{t})\cup L(\bm{t})
.
The latter is equivalent to
I(\bm{t})\subset I(\bm{s})
.

Now we proceed to the last claim of the lemma. Similarly to what we did above,
split again
2^{d}
in two parts

2^{d}=\mathbb{V}_{1}^{\prime}(\bm{t})\cup\mathbb{V}_{2}^{\prime}(\bm{t})
,
where

(1)

V\in\mathbb{V}_{1}^{\prime}(\bm{t})
if for all
\varepsilon>0

holds
B_{V}\cap B_{\varepsilon}(\bm{t})\neq\varnothing
,

(2)

V\in\mathbb{V}_{2}^{\prime}(\bm{t})
if there exists

\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}=\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}(V,\bm{t})>0
such that

B_{V}\cap B_{\varepsilon_{0}}(\bm{t})=\varnothing
.

Define

\varepsilon^{\prime}(\bm{t})=\min_{V\in\mathbb{V}_{2}^{\prime}(\bm{t})}\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}(V,\bm{t}).

Then, if
\bm{s}\in B_{\varepsilon}(\bm{t})
with
\varepsilon<\varepsilon^{\prime}(\bm{t})
,
there exists
V\in\mathbb{V}_{1}^{\prime}(\bm{t})
such that
\bm{s}\in B_{V}
and therefore

L(\bm{s})=V^{c}
. Since
j\in L^{c}(\bm{s})=I(\bm{s})\cup K(\bm{s})=V

is equivalent to
g_{j}(\bm{s})=0
, letting
\varepsilon\to 0
and using
continuity of
g_{j}
we obtain that
g_{j}(\bm{t})=0
. Hence,

I(\bm{s})\cup K(\bm{s})=V\subset I(\bm{t})\cup K(\bm{t})
.
The latter is equivalent to
L(\bm{t})\subset L(\bm{s})
.

From these two properties follows that if
\bm{s}\in B_{\varepsilon}(\bm{t})

with
\varepsilon<\min\{\varepsilon(\bm{t}),\,\varepsilon^{\prime}(\bm{t})\}
,
then
K(\bm{s})\subset K(\bm{t})
.
∎

17
.

Plugging
\bm{t}=\bm{s}
into (
8
), we obtain

(75)

\Sigma-\Sigma(\bm{t})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[B_{1,i}\,t_{i}^{\beta_{i}^{\prime}}+B_{2,i}\,t_{i}^{\beta_{i}}\Big]+\sum_{i,\,j\,\in\mathcal{F}}A_{6,i,j}\,t_{i}^{\beta_{i}/2}\,t_{j}^{\beta_{j}/2}+o\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{\beta_{i}}\right)

with

).

8.3.2.
Exponential prefactor

On several occasions we shall need a formula for the following quantity, which
we shall refer to as
the exponential prefactor
:

\displaystyle\ln\frac{\varphi_{\Sigma(\bm{\tau})}\left(u\bm{b}-u^{-1}\bm{x}\right)}{\varphi_{\Sigma}(u\,\bm{b})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\,\left(u\bm{b}-u^{-1}\bm{x}\right)^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(\bm{\tau})\left(u\bm{b}-u^{-1}\bm{x}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\,u^{2}\,\bm{b}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\bm{b}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\,u^{2}\,\bm{b}^{\top}\Big[\Sigma^{-1}(\bm{\tau})-\Sigma^{-1}\Big]\bm{b}+\bm{b}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(\bm{\tau})\,\bm{x}-\frac{1}{2u^{2}}\,\bm{x}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(\bm{\tau})\,\bm{x}

(77)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\,u^{2}\,\bm{b}^{\top}\Big[\Sigma^{-1}(\bm{\tau})-\Sigma^{-1}\Big]\bm{b}+\bm{b}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(\bm{\tau})\,\bm{x}.

Using (
73
)
and observing that

(78)

\bm{b}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}B_{1,i}\Sigma^{-1}\bm{b}=\bm{w}^{\top}B_{1,i}\bm{w}=\bm{w}^{\top}(A_{1,i}+A_{1,i}^{\top})\,\bm{w}=\bm{w}^{\top}\,\underbracket{A_{1,i}\,\bm{w}}_{=\,\bm{0}}+\underbracket{\bm{w}^{\top}A_{1,i}}_{=\,\bm{0}}\bm{w}=0,

we obtain

\bm{b}^{\top}\Big[\Sigma^{-1}(\bm{\tau})-\Sigma^{-1}\Big]\bm{b}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\Xi_{i,j}\,\tau_{i}^{\beta_{i}/2}\,\tau_{j}^{\beta_{j}/2}+o\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}^{\beta_{i}}\right),

where

(80)

Note that

(81)

\Xi_{i,j}=\bm{w}^{\top}\Big[2\,A_{2,i}\,\mathbb{1}_{i=j}+D_{i,j}\,\mathbb{1}_{i,j\in\mathcal{F}}\Big]\,\bm{w},\qquad D_{i,j}=A_{6,i,j}+A_{1,i}\Sigma^{-1}A_{1,j}^{\top},

because

and

\bm{w}^{\top}B_{2,i}\,\bm{w}=\bm{w}^{\top}(A_{2,i}+A_{2,i}^{\top})\,\bm{w}=2\,\bm{w}^{\top}A_{2,i}\,\bm{w}

\bm{b}(\bm{\tau})

Conditional mean

.

\bm{d}_{\,\bm{\tau}}(\bm{t})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Bigg[A_{2,i}\Big[(\tau_{i}+t_{i})^{\beta_{i}}-\tau_{i}^{\beta_{i}}\Big]+S_{\alpha_{i},A_{5,i}}(t_{i})\Bigg]\bm{w}\\[7.0pt]

Proof of Lemma
19
.

Throughout this calculation, the equivalence relation

is defined as

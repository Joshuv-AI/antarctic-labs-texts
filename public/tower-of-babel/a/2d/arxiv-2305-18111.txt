The Minimax Risk in Testing Uniformity of Poisson Data under Missing Ball Alternatives This paper was presented in part at the 2023 59th Annual Allerton Conference on Communication, Control, and Computing [ 1 ] .
Alon Kipnis

Abstract
We study the problem of testing the goodness of fit of occurrences of items from many categories to a Poisson distribution uniform over the categories, against a class of alternative hypotheses obtained by the removal of an ℓ p \ell_{p} ball, p ≤ 2 p\leq 2 , of radius ϵ \epsilon around the sequence of uniform Poisson rates. We characterize the minimax risk for this problem as the expected number of samples n n and the number of categories N N go to infinity. Our result enables the comparison at the constant level of the many estimators previously proposed for this problem, rather than at the rate of convergence of the risk or the scaling order of the sample complexity. The minimax test relies exclusively on collisions in the small sample limit but behaves like the chisquared test otherwise. Empirical studies over a range of problem parameters show that the asymptotic risk estimate is accurate in finite samples and that the minimax test is significantly better than the chisquared test or a test that only uses collisions. Our analysis involves the reduciion to a structured subset of alternatives, establishing asymptotic normality for linear statistics, and solving an optimization problem over N N -dimensional sequences that parallels classical results from Gaussian white noise models.

Introduction

We observe data consisting of occurrence counts

o_{1},\ldots,o_{N}
, where each
o_{i}
corresponds to one of
N
distinct categories. We model these counts as realizations of independent Poisson random variables and are interested in testing whether all categories share the same Poisson rate, or whether the rates vary across categories according to some alternative structure. Specifically, suppose

\displaystyle H(Q)\,:\,O_{i}\sim\mathrm{Pois}(nQ_{i}),

(1)

independently for

\displaystyle H_{0}:Q_{i}=1/N\quad\text{for all}\quad i=1,\ldots,N,

deviate from uniformity. Our results are asymptotic and valid across all scaling relationships between

), which is of particular interest, as the case where

This situation might arise when we are interested in testing the spatial uniformity of a light source using a large array of photon counters operating over a prescribed time interval, or when verifying whether the symbols in a random string are distributed uniformly over a large alphabet when the string’s length has a Poisson distribution with mean
n

N

Testing whether these counts follow the same Poisson law would help assess if the species is uniformly distributed across the habitat or if there are preferred sub-regions due to variations in environmental conditions, leading to inhomogeneous Poisson rates

Another justification for testing the uniformity of Poisson data is its connection to the important multinomial setting, arises from (

Though motivated by the multinomial case in many previous studies, our focus is on the Poisson model as a problem of intrinsic interest. We leave to future work a detailed investigation of the conditions under which the upper bound on the multinomial minimax risk is asymptotically tight.

Testing for uniformity includes, as a special case, testing the goodness-of-fit to any continuous distribution with cumulative distribution function (CDF)
F_{0}

\displaystyle o_{i}=\#\left\{j\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,\frac{i-1}{N}\leq F_{0}(y_{j})<\frac{i}{N}\right\},\qquad i=1,\ldots,N;

I-C

Contributions

Consider an asymptotic setting where

and

go to infinity. If
\lim_{n,N\to\infty}R^{*}(V_{\epsilon})\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n,N\to\infty}\frac{R^{*}(V_{\epsilon})}{2\Phi\left(-u_{\epsilon,n,N,p}/2\right)}=1.

(5)

\displaystyle(u_{\epsilon,n,N,p})^{2}:=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{4}n^{2}N^{3-4/p}.

(6)

Furthermore, sufficient conditions for
\lim_{N,n\to\infty}R^{*}(V_{\epsilon})\in(0,1)
are

and

or
\epsilon N^{1-1/p}=o(1)

\displaystyle X_{m}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}\{O_{i}=m\},\qquad m=0,1,2,\ldots.

is asymptotically minimax, and least favorable prior in a Bayesian version of the minimax problem is an

\left\{1/N-\epsilon N^{-1/p},1/N+\epsilon N^{-1/p}\right\}
; see Figure
1
for a conceptual sketch.

Additionally, we deliver the asymptotic risk of the chi-squared test under the least favorable prior, which under similar conditions asymptotes to

\displaystyle 2\Phi\left(-\sqrt{\frac{n}{N+n}}u_{\epsilon,n,N,p}/2\right).

(8)

In particular, the chi-squared test is not asymptotically minimax unless

.

Numerical analysis in the case

shows that the approximation (

) provides to

is accurate in finite

Significance of the contributions

Previous results in the literature that conisdered showed that the condition

,

The minimax properties of the chi-squared test serve as a particularly illustrative case. Its suboptimality in regimes where the alphabet size

Nonetheless, the chi-squared test attains vanishing minimax risk as

. Furthermore, the chi-squared test is asymptotically minimax under smooth alternatives

I-E

Proof Technique

We derive both an upper bound (Theorem
5
) and a lower bound (Theorem
6
) for
R^{*}(V_{\epsilon})
, and show that these bounds are asymptotically tight whenever
R^{*}(V_{\epsilon})
does not vanish asymptotically (Theorem
7
). Both bounds are established via a related Bayesian framework in which Poisson rate sequences are drawn from a class of priors associated with the most informative subset of
V_{\epsilon}
. This technique is standard in minimax analysis, where direct characterization of the least favorable prior is often intractable
[
38
,
7
,
39
,
15
,
40
]
. However, our setting does not reduce to any of these classical frameworks, as the counts, or their histogram ordinates, are generally not asymptotically normal
[
41
]
. The main technical difficulty lies in the derivation of the upper bound. This involves reducing the analysis to a carefully constructed subset of alternative rate vectors, defined via simple separation conditions; establishing an asymptotic normality result for a class of tests linear in the counts histogram; identifying suitable priors over the reduced alternative set; and ultimately solving an optimization problem over
N
-dimensional sequences. The structure of this optimization problem resembles those arising in hypothesis testing within the Gaussian white noise model
[
7
,
39
]
.

An additional complication arises from alternatives that do not give rise to asymptotically normal test statistics. These are handled using standard tests whose behavior can be analyzed via their first two moments.

II-A

Elementary Separation Conditions

We now examine several test statistics used to identify subsets of
V_{\epsilon}
where the minimax risk vanishes. These subsets correspond to alternatives that are easily distinguishable from the null and are subsequently excluded from the main analysis, allowing us to focus on the more challenging regions of
V_{\epsilon}
.

For a test statistic
T
and an alternative rate sequence
Q
, define

A_{n,N}(T;Q):=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[T\mid H(Q)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[T\mid H(U)\right]}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}\left[T\mid H(Q)\right]+\mathrm{Var}\left[T\mid H(U)\right]}}.

When
A_{n,N}(T;Q)\to\infty
, one can construct a test based on
T
with vanishing risk using a standard argument via Chebyshev’s inequality. For example, reject the null when

T\geq\mathbb{E}\left[T\mid H(U)\right]+\frac{A_{n,N}(T;Q)}{2}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}\left[T\mid H(U)\right]}.

In this case, the null
H(U)
and the alternative
H(Q)
are said to be completely separated.

Sum Test

Consider the statistic

T_{\mathrm{sum}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}O_{i}.

Direct calculation shows

\displaystyle A_{n,N}(T_{\mathrm{sum}};Q)

\displaystyle=\frac{n\sum_{i=1}^{N}Q_{i}-n}{\sqrt{n+n\sum_{i=1}^{N}Q_{i}}}.

It follows that a test based on
T_{\mathrm{sum}}
can completely separate an alternative
Q\in V_{\epsilon}
unless

\sqrt{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}Q_{i}-1\right)=O(1).

(9)

Chi-squared Test

The chi-squared test is based on the statistic

\displaystyle T_{\chi^{2}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(O_{i}-n/N)^{2}}{n/N}.

(10)

This test is known to yield an asymptotically minimax test when
N=o(n)
or when the set of alternative sequences is restricted to a binned smooth probability density
[
13
,
9
,
10
]
.

If
Q
satisfies (
9
), then the variance of
T_{\chi^{2}}
under
H(Q)
is at most
3N(1+O(1))
. In this case,

\displaystyle A_{n,N}(T_{\chi^{2}};Q)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{-1+N\sum_{i=1}^{N}Q_{i}+\frac{N}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(nQ_{i}-n/N\right)^{2}}{2\sqrt{3N(1+O(1))}}

\displaystyle\geq Cn\sqrt{N}\left\|Q-U\right\|_{2}^{2}+o(1).

for some constant
C>0
. Therefore, the chi-squared test completely separates alternatives outside the ball
\left\|Q-U\right\|_{2}\leq r_{n,N}
, provided

\displaystyle r_{n,N}^{2}n\sqrt{N}\to\infty.

(11)

Furthermore, using the inequality
\|a\|_{p}\leq N^{1/p-1/2}\|a\|_{2}
valid for all
a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
p\leq 2
, we get complete separation whenever

\displaystyle nN^{3/2-2/p}\left(\left\|Q-U\right\|_{p}\right)^{2}\to\infty.

(12)

For
Q\in V_{\epsilon}
, condition (
12
) is equivalent to
u_{\epsilon,n,N,p}\to\infty
. This condition was recognized as sufficient for complete separation in
[
30
]
and in
[
19
,
29
,
32
]
under the related multinomial setups. These works also showed a converse statement:
u_{\epsilon,n,N,p}\to 0
leads to inseparability, i.e. minimax risk converging to one.

Max Test

Consider the statistic

T_{\max}:=\max_{i=1,\ldots,N}O_{i}.

Under the null
Q=U
, the distribution of
T_{\max}
is degenerated and concentrates on two consecutive integers approaching
\log(N)/\log(\log(N))
to a first order in

[
42
,
41
]
. Therefore, under any
Q\neq U
, if

faster than
\log(N)/\log(\log(N))
for some

, a test that rejects the null if

exceeds
2+\log(N)/\log(\log(N))
detects with probability of error appoaching zero and thus has vanishing risk. Consequently, a test based on

completely separates alternatives outside the hypercube

\displaystyle B_{\xi}^{\infty}(U):=\{Q\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\,:\,\max_{i=1,\ldots,N}|Q_{i}-U_{i}|\leq\xi_{n,N}\},

(13)

provided

\displaystyle\frac{n\log(\log(N))}{\log(N)}\xi_{n,N}\to\infty.

(14)

The following corollary summarizes the separation of the chi-squared and max tests in our setting.

Corollary 1
.

Consider a sequence of multivariate Poisson models (
1
) indexed by

and

, where

and

go to infinity.
Let

satisfy (

R(V_{\epsilon}\cap B_{\xi}^{\infty}(U))=R(V_{\epsilon})+o(1).

Additionally, let

satisfy (

II-B

Minimax Bayesian setup

Assume that the sequence
Q
is sampled from some prior
\pi
over
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
. The Bayes risk of a test
\psi
is defined as

\displaystyle\rho(\psi;\pi)=\mathbb{E}_{Q\sim\pi}\left[R(\psi;Q)\right],

where we used the notation

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{Q\sim\pi}\left[F(Q)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(q_{1},...,q_{N})\prod_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}(dq_{i})

for a measurable function
F:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, assuming all per-coordinate integrals exist.

Consider the restricted set of Poisson rate sequences

\displaystyle V_{\epsilon,\xi,r}:=V_{\epsilon}\cap B^{2}_{r}(U)\cap B^{\infty}_{\xi}(U).

(15)

By the inequality
\|a\|_{p}\leq N^{1/p-1/2}\|a\|_{2}
mentioned earlier,
V_{\epsilon,\xi,r}
is non-empty provided

\displaystyle\epsilon N^{1-1/p}\leq\xi\quad\text{and}\quad\epsilon N^{1/2-1/p}\leq r.

(16)

We consider a companion set to
V_{\epsilon,\xi,r}
of product priors
\pi
over
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\epsilon,\xi,r}:=\Biggl\{\pi=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{i}\,:

\displaystyle\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}_{Q_{i}\sim\pi_{i}}\left[\left|Q_{i}-1/N\right|^{p}\right]\geq\epsilon^{p},

(17)

\displaystyle\left.\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}_{Q_{i}\sim\pi_{i}}\left[\left|Q_{i}-1/N\right|^{2}\right]\leq r^{2}\right.,

\displaystyle\pi([1/N-\xi,1/N+\xi])=1\Biggr\}.

The minimax Bayes risk over
\Pi_{\epsilon,\xi,r}
is defined as

\rho^{*}(\Pi_{\epsilon,\xi,r}):=\inf_{\psi}\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_{\epsilon,\xi,r}}\rho(\psi;\pi).

Whenever it exists, a prior
\pi^{*}\in\Pi_{\epsilon,\xi,r}
attaining the supremum above is said to be
least favorable
.

Lemma 2
.

We have

\displaystyle R^{*}(V_{\epsilon,\xi,r})=\rho^{*}(\Pi_{\epsilon,\xi,r})+o(1).

(18)

The proof of Lemma
2
is in Section
VI-A
. In (
18
),
o(1)
represents a term that goes to zero as
N
goes to infinity independently of the other parameters. The situation of most interest to us is when the risk on either side of (
18
)
converges to a positive constant, in which case
(
18
) implies
R^{*}(V_{\epsilon,\xi,r})/\rho^{*}(\Pi_{\epsilon,\xi,r})\to 1
.

Example: Three-point prior

As an example of an interesting set of priors, consider a prior in which each coordinate
\pi_{i}
is a three-point (or two-point if
\eta=1
) prior symmetric around
U_{i}=1/N
:

\displaystyle\pi_{i}(\eta,\mu)=(1-\eta)\delta_{U_{i}}+\frac{\eta}{2}\delta_{U_{i}+\mu}+\frac{\eta}{2}\delta_{U_{i}-\mu},

(19)

for
i=1,\ldots,N
. One of our key results says that the asymptotically unique least favorable prior
\pi^{*}
within
\Pi_{\epsilon,\xi,r}
is of the form (
19
).

II-C

Properties of the counts’ histogram

Recall that
X_{m}
of (
7
) denotes the number of categories with exactly
m
items. For example,
X_{0}
is the number of categories not represented in the data,
X_{1}
is the number of singletons, and
X_{2}
is the number of pairwise collisions or coincidences. We call the set

\{X_{0},X_{1},\ldots\}
the data’s histogram
(aka the data’s
pattern

[
43
]
or
fingerprint

[
44
]
).

For
\lambda>0
, let
\mathrm{P}_{\lambda}(m)
be the Poisson probability mass function:

\mathrm{P}_{\lambda}(m):=e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{m}}{m!},\quad m=0,1,...

Set

\lambda_{0}:=\lambda_{n,N}:=nU_{i}=n/N.

Under the null, the histogram ordinate
X_{m}
is a binomial random variable with mean

\displaystyle\mu^{0}_{m}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\left[X_{m}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{P}_{\lambda_{0}}(m)=N\cdot\mathrm{P}_{\lambda_{0}}(m).

The covariance between the
\{X_{m}\}
s is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}[X_{m},X_{k}]=N\begin{cases}\mathrm{P}_{\lambda_{0}}(m)(1-\mathrm{P}_{\lambda_{0}}(m))&m=k\\
-\mathrm{P}_{\lambda_{0}}(m)\mathrm{P}_{\lambda_{0}}(k)&m\neq k.\end{cases}

(20)

Henceforth, we write the covariance function using the infinite matrix
\Sigma
such that
\Sigma_{m,k}=\mathrm{Cov}[X_{m},X_{k}]
, where we agree that the first row and column of
\Sigma
has the index
0
. It is convenient to write

\displaystyle\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\mu^{0})-\mu^{0}\mu^{0\top}/N,

(21)

where for two sequences
u=\{u_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
and
v=\{v_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
the notation
uv^{\top}
denotes the infinite matrix whose
(m,k)
entry is
u_{m}v_{k}
, in accordance with standard matrix notation. We note that
\Sigma
is singular because
\Sigma\mathbf{1}=0
, where
\mathbf{1}=(1,1,\ldots)
.

II-D

Linear tests of the histogram

Consider tests that reject the null for large values of

\displaystyle T_{w}=\left\langle w,X\right\rangle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}X_{m}w_{m}

(22)

for some weights sequence
\{w_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
. Note that

\displaystyle T_{w}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i},\qquad A_{i}:=w_{O_{i}},

(23)

and that
A_{i}
,

are independently and identically distributed under the null. Under additional assumptions on the sequence

provided in Proposition

is asymptotically normal with mean and variance

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[T_{w}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}w_{m}\mu^{0}_{m}=\langle w,\mu^{0}\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left[T_{w}\right]

\displaystyle=\langle w,\Sigma w\rangle.

In this situation, a test asymptotically of size
\alpha

is obtained by rejecting when

\displaystyle\frac{T_{w}-\langle w,\mu^{0}\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle w,\Sigma w\rangle}}>z_{1-\alpha},\qquad\Phi(z_{1-\alpha})=1-\alpha.

\displaystyle h_{m,\lambda}(x):=\frac{\mathrm{P}_{\lambda+x}(m)-\mathrm{P}_{\lambda}(m)}{\mathrm{P}_{\lambda}(m)}=e^{-x}(1+\frac{x}{\lambda})^{m}-1.

We may view

as the relative difference in mass probability of

satisfies

\displaystyle\mu_{m}(Q)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{P}_{nQ_{i}}(m)=\mu_{m}^{0}+P_{\lambda_{0}}(m)\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{m,\lambda_{0}}(nQ_{i}-\lambda_{0}).

(26)

Likewise, under a rate prior
\pi
,

\displaystyle\mu_{m}(\pi)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{Q\sim\pi}\left[\mu_{m}(Q)\right]

\displaystyle=\mu^{0}_{m}+\mathrm{P}_{\lambda_{0}}(m)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{m,\lambda_{0}}(nt-\lambda_{0})\pi_{i}(dt)

\displaystyle=:\mu^{0}_{m}+\Delta_{m}(\pi).

(27)

Namely,
\Delta_{m}(\pi)
is the expected difference in
X_{m}
due to the random perturbations
\pi
of the uniform rate sequence. Notice the identity
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{m}=0
that follows from
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\mu_{m}(Q)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\mu_{m}^{0}=N
.

II-E

Asymptotic normality of a family of linear tests

The following proposition establishes conditions under which linear statistics of the form (

Proposition 3
.

Consider a sequence of multivariate Poisson models (
1
) indexed by
n
and
N
, where
N
and
n
go to infinity with

. Set

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle=\log(N)/(n\sqrt{\log(\log(N))}),

(28a)

\displaystyle r^{2}

\displaystyle=\log(N)/(n\sqrt{N}),

(28b)

Let

be a non-constant sequence that satisfying the conditions:

\langle w,\mu^{0}+\Delta(\pi)\rangle

\langle w,\Sigma w\rangle

(ii)

Set

The Bayes risk of the test

of the form (

\displaystyle\rho(\psi_{w,\alpha^{*}};\pi)=2\Phi\left(-u(w;\pi)/2\right)+o(1),

The proof of Proposition
3
appears in Section

.

We note that the explicit forms of

Condition (

when averaged under the Poisson probability mass function. This condition is invariant under rescaling of

Proposition

establishes conditions under which the test statistic

and variance

\displaystyle\frac{T_{w}-\langle w,\mu^{0}\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle w,\Sigma w\rangle}}\overset{d}{\to}\begin{cases}\mathcal{N}\left(0,1\right),&Q=U\\
\mathcal{N}\left(u(w;\pi),1\right),&Q\sim\pi.\end{cases}

Define the function

\displaystyle g_{m,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{h_{m,\lambda}(x)+h_{m,\lambda}(-x)}{2},

(32)

which symmetrizes

\displaystyle z_{1-\alpha}=\frac{\langle w^{*},\Delta(\pi)\rangle}{2\sqrt{\langle w^{*},\Sigma w^{*}\rangle}}.

The result below characterizes the asymptotic Bayes risk of

under a relevant class of priors, including in particular a specific prior of the form (

.

Consider a sequence of multivariate Poisson models (

III-A

of (

of (

Consider the multivariate Poisson model (

V
Discussions

V-A

Relation to multinomial sampling

As mentioned in the introduction, the problem of multinomial uniformity testing with sample size
n
arises from our setting (
1
) by conditioning on the event
\sum_{i=1}^{N}O_{i}=n
and restricting the set of alternatives to those in
V_{\epsilon}
that also satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{N}Q_{i}=1
. Due to this conditioning and the additional constraint, the minimax risk
R^{*}(V_{\epsilon})
in our setting serves as an upper bound for the minimax risk under multinomial sampling. Consequently, the upper bound in Theorem
5
applies in the multinomial case as well. This bound is known to be tight in several important cases, based on central limit theorem variants showing that the conditioning
\sum_{i=1}^{N}O_{i}=n
does not affect the limiting distribution of the test statistic
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
]
. Some of these works also address asymptotic regimes that give rise to a non-trivial minimax risk, as in Corollary
8
. However, adapting these results precisely to our setting appears to require technical analysis beyond the scope of this paper.

We note that the restriction
\sum_{i=1}^{N}Q_{i}=1
does not affect the minimax risk. Indeed, for any
\delta>0
, the least favorable prior
\pi^{*}
in (
34
) yields alternative sequences
Q
such that
\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}Q_{i}-1\right|<\delta
with probability tending to one. This situation is analogous to the remark in
[
10
, Sec. 4.3.1]
concerning minimax testing in Gaussian noise.

V-B

Removal of non-ball shapes

The similarity between our analysis to those of
[
13
,
39
]
suggests the possibility of generalizing our setting to the removal of other geometric shapes like ellipsoids and Besov bodies
[
48
]
. Such extensions may lead to multi-dimensional versions of the optimization problem considered
in the proof of Theorem (
5
) in which the parameter is a pair of positive sequences
\{\eta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
rather than a pair of positive numbers.

V-C

Sparse alternatives

Another interesting extension is attained by imposing on
V_{\epsilon}
constraints of the form
\|Q_{i}-U_{i}\|_{q}\leq\epsilon^{\prime}
for some
q>0
and
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
. For example, the case of
q
close to zero is related to sparse alternatives as considered in
[
49
]
and
[
50
]
. The methods developed in this paper appears suitable for analyzing such extensions.

Proof of Lemma

Because every sequence

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\epsilon^{-p}\left|NQ_{i}-1\right|^{p}=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\epsilon^{-p}}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|NQ_{i}-1\right|^{p}\right]\geq 1

P_{\pi}
almost surely. Likewise,

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r^{-2}N\left|Q_{i}-1/N\right|^{2}\overset{a.s.}{=}\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{r^{-2}}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}N\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Q_{i}-1/N\right|^{2}\right]\leq 1.

If follows that for some sequences
r_{N}
and
\epsilon_{N}
satisfying

\epsilon_{N}/\epsilon\to 1
,
r_{N}/r\to 1
,

\epsilon_{N}/\epsilon\geq 1
, and

. We now argue similarly to
[

: Define the measure
\tilde{\pi}(A)=\pi(A\cap V_{\epsilon_{N},\xi,r_{N}})/\pi(V_{\epsilon_{N},\xi,r_{N}})
. We have
\tilde{\pi}(V_{\epsilon_{N},\xi,r_{N}})=1
, and hence
\rho^{*}(\tilde{\pi})\leq R^{*}(V_{\epsilon_{N},\xi,r_{N}})
. On the other hand, the total variation distance obeys
\mathrm{TV}(P_{\pi},P_{\tilde{\pi}})\to 0

because
\pi(V_{\epsilon_{N},\xi,r_{N}})\to 1
, implying
\rho^{*}(\pi)=\rho^{*}(\tilde{\pi})+o(1)\leq R^{*}(V_{\epsilon_{N},\xi,r_{N}})+o(1)=R^{*}(V_{\epsilon,\xi,r})+o(1)
, the last transition by continuity of
R^{*}(V_{\epsilon,\xi,r})
in
\epsilon
and
r
.

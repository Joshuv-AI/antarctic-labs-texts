Ryser’s Conjecture and Stochastic matrices


Abstract
Assume that H H is a circulant Hadamard matrix of order n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 . We consider an appropriate stochastic matrix S S of order n n depending on H H . This allows us to prove that n = 4 n=4 . Thus, there are only 10 10 circulant Hadamard matrices.

1
Introduction

A matrix of order
n
is a square matrix with
n
rows.
A
circulant
matrix
A:={\rm{circ}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
of order
n
is a matrix of order
n
, with first row
[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]
, in which each row after the first is
obtained by a cyclic shift of its predecessor by one position. For example, the second row of
A

is
[a_{n},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1}]
. Three useful circulant matrices of order
n
are the matrix
J
with all its entries equal to
1
, i.e.,
J:={\rm{circ}}(1,\ldots,1)
, the identity matrix
I:={\rm{circ}}(1,0,\ldots,0)
, and the zero matrix
0:={\rm{circ}}(0,\ldots,0)
.
A
Hadamard
matrix
H
of order
n
is a matrix of order
n
with entries in
\{-1,1\}
such that

K:=\frac{H}{\sqrt{n}}
is an orthogonal matrix with rational entries. A
circulant Hadamard
matrix of order
n
is a circulant matrix that is Hadamard of order
n
. The
10
known circulant Hadamard matrices are
H_{1}:={\rm{circ}}(1),H_{2}:=-H_{1},H_{3}:={\rm{circ}}(1,-1,-1,-1),H_{4}:=-H_{3},H_{5}:={\rm{circ}}(-1,1,-1,-1),H_{6}:=-H_{5},

H_{7}:={\rm{circ}}(-1,-1,1,-1),H_{8}:=-H_{7},H_{9}:={\rm{circ}}(-1,-1,-1,1),H_{10}:=-H_{9}
.
A
stochastic
matrix
S
of order
n
is a matrix of order
n
with non-negative entries such that the sum of all entries of each row of
S
is equal to
1
. If the transpose of
S
is also stochastic, then
S
is a
doubly stochastic
matrix. We denote by
A^{*}
the conjugate transpose of the matrix
A
. When
A
has real entries
A^{*}
is simply the transpose of
A
.

Ryser proposed in
1963
(see
[
2
, p. 97]
,
[
8
]
) the conjecture of the non-existence of circulant Hadamard matrices of order
>4
. Ryser’s conjecture has since attracted some attention, see, e.g.,
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
9
]

and their bibliographies.

Brualdi
[
1
]
, a student of Ryser, proved the conjecture for every order
n
provided
H
is symmetric, and Turyn
[
9
]
proved the conjecture for all
n
’s of the form
n=4p^{2m}
where
p
is an odd prime number and
m
is a positive integer.
Turyn
[
9
, pp. 329-330]
also
proved the crucial fact that
n=4h^{2}
with
odd

h
.

The object of the present paper is to prove the conjecture. It turns out that this follows immediately when we focus on the following doubly stochastic matrix
S
(see Lemma
3
), associated to a circulant Hadamard matrix
H
of order
4h^{2}
.

S:=\frac{H+J}{2(2h^{2}+h)}.

(1)

The link with the OEIS sequence A091704 (see
[
7
]
) is the following. If Ryser’s Conjecture
holds then there is no Barker codes of length
>13
(see
[
10
]
).

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

There is no circulant Hadamard matrices with more that
4
rows.

Section
2
contains some results useful for the proof of the theorem.
The proof of the theorem appears in section
3
.

Tools

The following is well known. See, e.g.,
[
5
, p. 1193]
,
[
6
, p. 234]
,
[
9
, pp. 329-330]
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
H
be a regular Hadamard matrix of order
n\geq 4
, i.e., a Hadamard matrix whose row and column sums are all equal. Then
n=4h^{2}
for some positive integer
h
.
Moreover, the row and column sums are all equal to
\pm 2h
and each row has
2h^{2}\pm h
positive entries and
2h^{2}\mp h
negative entries. Finally, if
H
is circulant then

h
is odd.

A simple computation follows.

Lemma 3
.

Let
H:={\rm{circ}}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})
be a circulant Hadamard matrix of order
n
. Then, the matrix
S:=\frac{H+J}{n+\sqrt{n}}
is circulant and doubly stochastic.

Proof.

By Lemma
2

n=4h^{2}
. Sums and scalar multiples of circulant matrices are circulant. Thus, both
H+J
and
S
are circulant. Put
s
the sum of all entries in any row of
S
. By Lemma
2
we can assume that the sum
r
of all entries in any row of
H+J
is equal to
2h+4h^{2}
(if not we change
H
into
-H
). Since
n+\sqrt{n}=2(2h^{2}+h)
we obtain that
s=1
. Thus
S
is stochastic. Since
(H+J)^{*}=H^{*}+J^{*}=H^{*}+J
, and

H^{*}+J={\rm{circ}}(h_{1}+1,h_{n}+1,h_{n-1}+1,\ldots,h_{2}+1)

(2)

the sum of all entries in any row of
H^{*}+J
is also equal to
r
. Thus
S^{*}
is stochastic. This proves that
S
is doubly stochastic.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1

Assume that
H
is a circulant Hadamard matrix with at least
4
rows, i.e,
H
has order
n\geq 4
. By Lemma
2

n=4h^{2}
with odd
h
.
We can assume (if not we replace
H
by
-H
) that there are
\frac{n+\sqrt{n}}{2}=2h^{2}+h
entries equal to
1
in row
1
of
H
(see Lemma
2
). By Lemma
3
the matrix
S
defined in (
1
) is circulant and doubly stochastic. It follows from (
1
) that

H=2h(2h+1)S-J.

(3)

Computing the transpose conjugate in both sides of (
3
) we get

H^{*}=2h(2h+1)S^{*}-J.

(4)

since
J
is real and symmetric. But
H
is Hadamard so that

nI=HH^{*}.

(5)

Using (
5
) together with (
3
) and (
4
); using that
I,J,H,H^{*},S,S^{*}
are all circulant so that any product of two of them commute, and using that
J^{2}=nJ
we obtain the following equality:

4h^{2}I=4h^{2}(2h+1)^{2}SS^{*}-2h(2h+1)(S+S^{*})J+4h^{2}J.

(6)

But
SJ=J
and
S^{*}J=J
since
S
is doubly stochastic. Thus, dividing both sides of (
6
) by
h
we get

4hI=4h(2h+1)^{2}SS^{*}-4(2h+1)J+4hJ.

(7)

Reducing both sides of (
7
) modulo
h
we obtain

0=-4J\pmod{h}.

(8)

Therefore, (
8
) says that

h\mid 4.

(9)

But
h
is odd by Turyn’s result in Lemma
2
. Thus, (
9
) implies that

h=1.

(10)

In other words, we have proved that
n=4
. This proves the theorem.

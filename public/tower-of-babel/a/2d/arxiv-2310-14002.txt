Abstract
. In this article, we investigate the class of Hermitian manifolds whose Bismut connection has parallel torsion (BTP for brevity). In particular, we focus on the case where the manifold is (locally) homogeneous with respect to a group of holomorphic isometries and we fully characterize the compact Chern flat BTP manifolds. Moreover we show that certain compact flag manifolds are BTP if and only if the metric is Kähler or induced by the Cartan-Killing form and we then characterize BTP invariant metrics on compact semisimple Lie groups which are Hermitian w.r.t. a Samelson structure and are projectable along the Tits fibration. We state a conjecture concerning the question when the Bismut connection of a BTP compact Hermitian locally homogeneous manifold has parallel curvature, giving examples and providing evidence in some special cases.

1.
Introduction

Given a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, the study of metric connections with skew-symmetric torsion has gained a remarkable interest, as they come up in different meaningful geometric structures and are a powerful tool in Type II string theory and in supergravity theories (see e.g.
[
1
]
). In particular metric connections whose torsion is skew-symmetric and parallel have many interesting properties, e.g. their curvature tensor is a symmetric endomorphism of
\Lambda^{2}(TM)
, and have become an important research topic in the recent years. Basic examples are given by the canonical connections of naturally reductive homogeneous spaces, Sasakian and
3
-Sasakian spaces, nearly Kähler manifolds and nearly parallel
G_{2}
-structures in dimension
7
among others (see e.g.). Some classification results for manifolds carrying connections with parallel skew-symmetric torsion have been recently achieved considering the holonomy action on the tangent space (see
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
).

In the Hermitian setting, it is well-known that a Hermitian manifold
(M^{n},g,J)
admits a unique metric connection
\nabla^{b}
which leaves
J
parallel and whose torsion
T^{b}
is skew-symmetric. The connection
\nabla^{b}
is called the
Bismut connection
(see
[
10
]
), although previously introduced by Strominger (
[
29
]
), and its torsion
3
-form coincides with
-d^{c}\omega
, where
\omega=g(J\cdot,\cdot)
is the corresponding fundamental
2
-form. Hermitian manifolds endowed with the Bismut connection are called
Kähler with torsion (KT)
and when the torsion form is also closed or, equivalently
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
, the KT-structure is called a
strong KT structure (SKT)
and the Hermitian metric
g
is said pluriclosed. In recent years the Bismut connection has turned out to be a very meaningful object in different research topics, including Hermitian curvature flows (see e.g.
[
28
]
) as well as the search for special Hermitian connection with remarkable geometric and algebraic properties (see e.g.
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
17
]
).

In this paper we will focus on the case when the Bismut connection of a Hermitian manifold
(M,g,J)
is supposed to be
\nabla^{b}
-parallel. In this case, according to
[
38
]
, we will call the manifold (and sometimes also the metric)
Bismut torsion parallel
(BTP for brevity). In
[
36
]
it has been proved amongst others that for BTP manifolds the pluriclosed condition is equivalent to saying that the manifold satisfies the Bismut Kähler-like condition, while in
[
38
]
several general properties of BTP manifolds are proved, showing e.g. that on a compact BTP manifold the metric
g
is Gauduchon while the holonomy of
\nabla^{b}
reduces to a subgroup of
U(n-1)
if
g
is not balanced. There are a number of papers which contributed to this observation. As a partial list see
[
5
]
and
[
38
]
for example.

An important class of non-Kähler manifolds are the Vaisman manifolds, namely those compact non-Kähler, locally conformally Kähler manifolds whose Lee form is parallel w.r.t. the Levi-Civita connection. As proved in
[
5
]
, any Vaisman manifold is BTP. Another subset of BTP is the so-called Bismut Kähler-like (or BKL for brevity) manifolds, which are Hermitian manifolds whose Bismut curvature tensors obeys all Kähler symmetries. By the main result of
[
36
]
, any BKL metric is BTP.

When
n=2
, Vaisman, BKL, and BTP conditions are all equivalent, and compact non-Kähler Vaismann surfaces are fully classified by Belgun in
[
8
]
. They are non-Kähler properly elliptic surfaces, Kodaira surfaces, and (Class 1 and elliptic) Hopf surfaces. When
n\geq 3
, however, Vaisman and BKL become disjoint sets: a result proved in
[
35
]
says that for any compact non-Kähler BKL manifold
(M^{n},g)
with
n\geq 3
,
M^{n}
does not admit any Vaisman metric.

For
n\geq 3
, there are other non-balanced BTP manifolds which are not BKL or Vaisman. There are also balanced (non-Kähler) BTP manifolds. We refer the readers to
[
37
]
and
[
38
]
for more examples of BTP manifolds. They form a rich yet highly restrictive class of special Hermitian manifolds.

In this paper, we will be concerned with BTP manifolds that are (locally) homogeneous Hermitian manifolds. By Ambrose-Singer Theorem (see e.g.
[
4
]
,
[
31
]
) and its Hermitian extension due to Sekigawa (
[
27
]
), it is well known that a complete simply-connected
Hermitian manifold
(M,g,J)
is homogeneous if and only if there exists a Hermitian connection
\nabla
, called an
Ambrose-Singer connection
, whose torsion and curvature are
\nabla
-parallel. In particular if there exists a Hermitian Ambrose-Singer connection whose torsion is skew-symmetric, the manifold
(M,g,J)
turns out to be a naturally reductive homogeneous space with respect to the action of a transitive group of biholomorphic isometries.

Clearly, a Hermitian Ambrose-Singer connection with skew-symmetric torsion on a Hermitian manifold
(M,g,J)
coincides with the Bismut connection of the metric
g
. Following
[
24
]
we give the following

Definition 1.1
.

A BTP Hermitian manifold
(M,g,J)
is called
Bismut Ambrose-Singer
(BAS in short) if its Bismut connection has parallel torsion and curvature or, equivalently, if its Bismut connection is an Ambrose-Singer connection.

For instance, every compact flag manifold
F=K/H
where
K
is a compact semisimple Lie group and
H\subset K
is the centralizer of a torus in
K
, can be endowed with many invariant complex structures
J
and the metric
g_{o}
on
F
induced by the Cartan-Killing form of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
of
K
is automatically Hermitian w.r.t.
J
, with its Bismut connection being the associated canonical connection, hence Ambrose-Singer.

The aim of this paper is to investigate the geometry of (locally) homogeneous Hermitian manifolds whose Bismut connection is BTP and in particular to study conditions under which the Bismut connection is Ambrose-Singer.

Our first main result concerns the case of compact Chern flat Hermitian manifolds
(M,g,J)
, which by a well-known result due to Boothby (
[
11
]
) are covered by a complex Lie group endowed with a left-invariant Hermitian metric. We first prove the following characterization

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Chern flat
BTP
manifold. Then its universal cover is a complex reductive Lie group
G
equipped with a left-invariant metric, where
G={\mathbb{C}}^{k}\times G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{r}
is the orthogonal direct product with each
G_{i}
a simple complex Lie group.

Viceversa let
G={\mathbb{C}}^{k}\times G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{r}
where each
G_{i}
is any connected, simply-connected simple complex Lie group. Then there exists a left-invariant Hermitian metric
g
on
G
which is
BTP
.

In Proposition
3.2
we prove that any BTP manifold as in Theorem
1.2
is actually BAS while in Theorem
3.4
we address the problem of describing the moduli space of such BTP metrics.

We then consider the case of a simply-connected generalized flag manifold
F
, namely a homogeneous space
K/H
where
K
is a compact semisimple Lie group and
H
is the centralizer in
K
of a torus. It is well-known that this class of manifolds exhausts the set of all compact simply-connected Kähler homogeneous spaces and we investigate the properties of invariant BTP metrics on such manifolds. After recalling the basic facts concerning the existence of invariant complex structures
J
on a flag manifold
F
, we consider
J
-Hermitian invariant metrics showing that they are automatically balanced and we then prove a characterization of BTP metrics on a special remarkable subclass
\mathcal{C}
of flag manifolds. Indeed a flag manifold
F
belongs to
\mathcal{C}
when the isotropy representation of the isotropy subgroup
H
has at most two, necessarily non-equivalent, submodules; the class
\mathcal{C}
clearly contains all the Hermitian symmetric spaces as a proper subset as well as infinite series of non-symmetric flag manifolds, as it is shown in Table (
1
), which reproduces the full classification obtained in
[
7
]
. Our result is the following

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
F=K/H
be a flag manifold with
K
a compact simple Lie group and let
J
be an invariant complex structure on
F
. If the isotropy representation of
H
has at most two summands, then every invariant Hermitian
BTP
metric is either Kähler or a multiple of the metric induced by
-B
, where
B
denotes the Cartan-Killing form of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
of
K
.

An analogous characterization can also be proved for the class of full flag manifolds for the simple group
\rm{SU}(n)
, namely we establish the following

Theorem 1.4
.

An invariant
BTP
Hermitian metric on the flag manifold
F=\rm{SU}(n\!+\!1)/\rm{T}^{n}
endowed with an invariant complex structure is either Kähler or a multiple of the invariant metric induced by
-B
, where
B
denotes the Cartan-Killing form of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}(n\!+\!1)
.

We note that a general statement for full flag manifolds of a general compact simple Lie group seems to be out of reach at this very moment. Turning then to the case of a
C
-space, namely the case of a compact simply-connected complex manifold which is acted on transitively a compact group of biholomorphisms (see e.g.
[
2
]
), we restrict ourselves to the fundamental subclass given by a compact even-dimensional semisimple Lie group endowed with a left-invariant complex structure, a so-called Samelson complex structure. It is well-known that such a compact Lie group fibers holomorphically onto a full flag manifold endowed with an invariant complex structure via the Tits fibration (see
[
2
]
,
[
26
]
) with a maximal torus as typical fibre. These compact homogeneous complex spaces are particular cases of Wang spaces, namely compact homogeneous spaces
G/K
, where
G
is a compact semisimple Lie group and
K
is a compact subgroup whose semisimple part coincides with the semisimple part of the centralizer of some torus in
G
. Given a compact even-dimensional Lie group
G
endowed with a bi-invariant metric
g_{o}
one can consider any Samelson complex structure
J
w.r.t. which
g_{o}
is Hermitian and the corresponding Bismut connection of
(G,g_{o},J)
turns out to have closed and parallel torsion as well as vanishing curvature. Viceversa it was proved in
[
33
]
that a compact Hermitian manifold whose Bismut connection is flat is finitely covered by a compact Lie group equipped with a bi-invariant metric and a compatible Samelson structure. It is worth mentioning that compact Lie groups with a Samelson structure provide a distinguished ambient where to find special metrics as SKT or CYT (see e.g.
[
16
]
). We provide a characterization of Hermitian BTP metrics on such spaces under the additional hypothesis that the metric is invariant under the right translations by the maximal torus which determines the Samelson structure. More precisely we prove the following

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
G
be a compact even-dimensional simple Lie group endowed with a Samelson left-invariant complex structure
J
. Let
g
be a left-invariant
J
-Hermitian metric on
G
which is
BTP
. If
g
descends to an invariant metric
\bar{g}
on the flag manifold
Q
which is the base of the Tits fibration
j:G\to Q
, then
\bar{g}
is induced by the Cartan-Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
up to a multiple.

Viceversa, given the metric
\bar{g}
induced on
Q
by the Cartan-Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
, then there exists a
BTP
left-invariant
J
-Hermitian metric
g
on
G
so that
j:(G,g)\to(Q,\bar{g})
is a Riemannian submersion.

In the last section we more specifically address the question when a BTP metric turns out to be BAS and we first show that the natural Hermitian metrics on isosceles Hopf manifolds and on Calabi Eckmann manifolds are indeed BAS. We then provide an explicit example of a compact, non-simply connected homogeneous
4
-fold
M
carrying a BTP metric which is not BAS as it is shown to be not naturally reductive w.r.t. any transitive action of isometric biholomorphisms. This naturally leads us to formulate the following

Conjecture 1.6
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Hermitian manifold whose universal cover is a homogeneous Hermitian manifold without Kähler de Rham factor. Assume
g
is
BTP
, and the manifold is one of the following

(1)

M^{n}
has finite fundamental group, or

(2)

the universal cover of
(M^{n},g)
is a Lie group equipped with a left-invariant metric and a compatible left-invariant complex structure.

Then
g
must be
BAS
.

Finally we establish two results to support the conjecture, namely we prove it in some special cases using ad hoc methods. More precisely, in case of
3
-folds we prove

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
(M^{3},g)
be a compact homogeneous Hermitian manifold without Kähler de Rham factor and with finite
\pi_{1}(M)
. If
g
is
BTP
, then it is
BAS
.

We then consider the case of a nilpotent Lie group with a special left-invariant complex structure, which is called
nilpotent
as introduced in
[
13
]
, and we obtain the following result

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
G
be a nilpotent Lie group equipped with a left-invariant nilpotent complex structure
J
and a compatible left-invariant metric
g
. If
g
is
BTP
, then it is
BAS
.

The paper is organized as follows. In §2 we recall some basic facts about the Bismut connection of a Hermitian manifold together with some results in case of BTP metrics. In §3 we consider the case of compact BTP Chern flat manifolds proving the structure result Theorem
1.2
together with other results on such metrics, describing their uniqueness and proving that the Bismut curvature is parallel. In §4 we focus on the case of a compact simply-connected flag manifold endowed with an invariant BTP Hermitian metric. After analyzing this condition in terms of the root system w.r.t. a fixed Cartan subalgebra, we give a proof of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
. In §5 we address the problem of characterizing BTP invariant metric on a Wang space, namely on a simply-connected compact complex homogeneous space under the action of a compact Lie group. We prove a structure result, namely Theorem
1.5
, in the case of Wang spaces given by compact even-dimensional Lie groups with a Samelson structure, endowed with special BTP metrics. In the last section §6 we elaborate on the Conjecture
1.6
providing examples and proving two results, namely Propositions
1.7
and
1.8
, which prove the conjecture in two remarkable special cases.

2.
Preliminaries

Given a Hermitian manifold
(M,g,J)
, a linear connection
\nabla
is called Hermitian if it leaves both
g
and
J
parallel. It is well-known that there exists a distinguished one-parameter family of Hermitian connections
\{{}^{t}\nabla\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, firstly introduced by Gauduchon
[
18
]
and defined as

g({}^{t}\nabla_{X}Y,Z)=g(\nabla^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}_{X}Y,Z)-\frac{t-1}{4}d\omega(JX,JY,JZ)-\frac{t+1}{4}d\omega(JX,Y,Z),

where
X,Y,Z
are vector fields,
\nabla^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}
denotes the Levi-Civita connection and
\omega(X,Y)=g(JX,Y)
is the associated fundamental
2
-form.
Among these one can find the Chern connection
\nabla^{c}
for
t=1
and the Bismut connection
\nabla^{b}
for
t=-1
, also known as the Strominger connection (see
[
29
]
,
[
10
]
), given by

(2.1)

g(\nabla^{b}_{X}Y,Z)=g(\nabla^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}_{X}Y,Z)+\frac{1}{2}d\omega(JX,JY,JZ).

The Bismut connection can also be characterized by the property of being the unique Hermitian connection whose torsion tensor
T^{b}\in\Lambda^{2}TM^{*}\otimes TM
is totally skew-symmetric, namely the tensor
(X,Y,Z)\mapsto g(T^{b}(X,Y),Z)
is a
3
-form on
M
.

In this paper we are mainly concerned with the BTP condition, namely we consider the case where the torsion
T^{b}
is
\nabla^{b}
-parallel. This condition has been thoroughly investigated in
[
36
]
,
[
38
]
, where several important features have been highlighted. In order to describe some of the main properties of BTP manifolds, motivating at the same time their study, we select a local unitary frame
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
of
T^{1,0}(M)
(
n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M
) and we denote by
T
the torsion of the Chern connection, so that

T(e_{i},\overline{e}_{j})=0,\qquad T(e_{i},e_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}T^{k}_{ij}e_{k}.

Note that the symbol
T^{k}_{ij}
in
[
36
]
or
[
38
]
is half of our
T^{k}_{ij}
here. For the Levi-Civita connection of the metric
g
, we may write
De_{j}=\theta^{1}_{jk}e_{k}+\overline{\theta^{2}_{jk}}\overline{e}_{k}
for some
1
-forms
\theta^{1},\theta^{2}
. If we denote by
\theta,\theta^{b}
the connection forms under the unitary frame
e
for the Chern and Bismut connection, respectively, then we have
\theta^{b}=2\theta^{1}-\theta
(see
[
33
]
) and therefore a straightforward computation yields

(2.2)

\nabla^{b}_{e_{i}}e_{j}=2\nabla^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}_{e_{i}}e_{j}-\nabla^{c}_{e_{i}}e_{j},\qquad\nabla^{b}_{\overline{e}_{i}}e_{j}=2\nabla^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}_{\overline{e}_{i}}e_{j}-\sum_{k}T^{i}_{jk}\overline{e}_{k}-\nabla^{c}_{\overline{e}_{i}}e_{j}.

From this it follows that the torsion
T^{b}
of the Bismut connection can be expressed ad follows

T^{b}(e_{i},e_{j})=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}T^{k}_{ij}e_{k},\qquad T^{b}(e_{i},\overline{e}_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(T^{j}_{ik}\overline{e}_{k}-\overline{T^{i}_{jk}}e_{k}\right),

whence one can deduce (see
[
36
]
) that

(2.3)

\nabla^{b}T^{b}=0\qquad{\rm{iff}}\qquad\nabla^{b}T=0.

Again using (
2.2
) one can write down the BTP condition as follows

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{split}\ \sum_{r=1}^{n}\big(T^{\ell}_{ri}T^{r}_{jk}+T^{\ell}_{rj}T^{r}_{ki}+T^{\ell}_{rk}T^{r}_{ij}\big)\ =\ 0\\
\ \sum_{r=1}^{n}\big(T^{j}_{ir}\overline{T^{k}_{\ell r}}-T^{j}_{kr}\overline{T^{i}_{\ell r}}+T^{r}_{ik}\overline{T^{r}_{j\ell}}\big)\ =\ 0\end{split}\right.

for any
1\leq i,j,k,\ell\leq n
.

As for the curvature of the connection
\nabla^{b}
, we first observe that on any Hermitian manifold, the difference between the Bismut and Chern curvature is given by

(2.5)

R^{b}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}-R_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}=T^{\ell}_{ik,\bar{j}}+\overline{T^{k}_{j\ell,\bar{i}}}+\sum_{r}\big(T^{\ell}_{ir}\overline{T^{k}_{jr}}-T^{r}_{ik}\overline{T^{r}_{j\ell}}-T^{j}_{ir}\overline{T^{k}_{\ell r}}-T^{\ell}_{kr}\overline{T^{i}_{jr}}\big)

under any local unitary frame, where the index after comma stands for covariant derivatives with respect to
\nabla^{b}
. We remark here that (
2.5
) is formula (3.2) in
[
38
, Lemma 3.1]
and that the symbol
T^{j}_{ik}
there denote half of the Chern torsion components, which explains the discrepancy in coefficients
2
and
4
on the right hand side. In particular when the torsion
T^{b}
is
\nabla^{b}
-parallel, we immediately obtain that

(2.6)

\nabla^{b}R^{b}=0\qquad{\rm{iff}}\qquad\nabla^{b}R=0.

Moreover under the BTP condition, it has been proved in
[
38
]
that the curvature
R^{b}
has the following remarkable properties, namely

R^{b}(X,Y,Z,\overline{W})=0,\qquad R^{b}(X,\overline{Y},Z,\overline{W})=R(Z,\overline{W},X,\overline{Y})

for every
X,Y,Z,W
tangent vectors of type
(1,0)
. We refer to
[
38
]
for a more detailed analysis of other curvature properties, also in relation to the condition Bismut Kähler-like condition.

As a final note, we briefly discuss the case when the curvature
R^{b}
vanishes identically. Indeed, it has been proved in
[
33
]
that a compact Hermitian manifold whose Bismut connection is flat is locally biholomorphically isometric to an even dimensional Lie group
G
equipped with a bi-invariant metric
g_{o}
and a left-invariant complex structure compatible with
g_{o}
(see
[
33
]
for a more detailed exposition). As the Bismut connection
\nabla^{b}
of
g_{o}
satisfies
\nabla^{b}_{X}Y=0
for every left-invariant vector field on
G
, we see that Bismut flat Hermitian manifold are actually BTP.

3.
Compact Chern flat BTP manifolds

In this section, we focus on the class of compact Chern flat manifolds and prove a characterization of such manifolds in the case where they can carry a metric whose Bismut connect has parallel torsion.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Since
(M^{n},g)
is compact Chern flat, by Boothby’s theorem
[
11
]
, the universal cover of
M
is a unimodular complex Lie group
G
equipped with a compatible left-invariant metric. Denote by
\mathfrak{g}
and
\langle,\rangle
the corresponding Lie algebra and metric. We want to show that
\mathfrak{g}
must be the orthogonal direct sum

(3.1)

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{a}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}

where
\mathfrak{a}
is the center and each
{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}
is a simple complex Lie algebra. Note that in particular this says that
\mathfrak{g}
is reductive.

For this purpose, let us denote by
\nabla
,
\nabla^{b}
the Chern and Bismut connection and
T
,
T^{b}
their torsion tensors. The BTP
condition means that
\nabla^{b}T^{b}=0
, which is equivalent to
\nabla^{b}T=0
(see
[
36
]
,
[
38
]
). Let
e
be a local unitary
\nabla
-parallel frame of type
(1,0)
tangent vectors on
M^{n}
. Lifting to the universal cover, we may extend
e
into a global frame.
Boothby’s theorem says that when
(M^{n},g)
is compact Chern flat one will have
\nabla T=0
, so the torsion components
T^{j}_{ik}
under
e
defined by
T(e_{i},e_{k})=\sum_{j}T^{j}_{ik}e_{j}
are constants, and using them as (minus of the) structure constants one can make the universal cover
G
of
M
into a complex Lie group, where each
e_{i}
is left-invariant. Hence
e
becomes a unitary basis of the complex Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and we have
C^{j}_{ik}=-T^{j}_{ik}
where
C^{j}_{ik}
are the structure constants defined by
[e_{i},e_{k}]=\sum_{j}C^{j}_{ik}e_{j}
.
If we now use formulas (
2.4
), we note that the first one, in terms of
C
, is just the Jacobi identity, so we will focus on the second one. Following a trick in
[
38
]
, in the second equation of (
2.4
), we interchange
i
and
j
, and also interchange
k
and
\ell
, and then take the complex conjugation, it gives us

\sum_{r=1}^{n}\big(T^{\ell}_{kr}\overline{T^{i}_{jr}}-T^{j}_{kr}\overline{T^{i}_{\ell r}}+T^{r}_{ik}\overline{T^{r}_{j\ell}}\big)\ =\ 0.

Subtracting that from the second equation of (
2.4
), we obtain

\sum_{r=1}^{n}\big(T^{j}_{ir}\overline{T^{k}_{\ell r}}-T^{\ell}_{kr}\overline{T^{i}_{jr}}\big)=0,\ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j,k,\ell\leq n.

Choosing
k=j
and
\ell=i
, we get

(3.2)

\sum_{r}|T^{j}_{ir}|^{2}=\sum_{r}|T^{i}_{jr}|^{2},\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

Of course the same equality holds when we replace
T
by
C
, as
C=-T
. Also, the equality holds for any choice of unitary basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Now suppose
{\mathfrak{h}}
is an ideal of the complex Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Denote by
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
its orthogonal complement in
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Take a unitary basis
e=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
so that
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{p}\}
is a basis of
{\mathfrak{h}}
. Since
{\mathfrak{h}}
is an ideal, we have
[{\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{g}}]\subseteq{\mathfrak{h}}
, so

C^{\alpha}_{i\ast}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq p,\ \ \forall\ p+1\leq\alpha\leq n.

By (
3.2
), we have
C^{i}_{\alpha\ast}=0
as well, so
[{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp},{\mathfrak{g}}]\subseteq{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
, thus we have proved that:

For any ideal
{\mathfrak{h}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, its orthogonal complement
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
is also an ideal in
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

This establishes the decomposition (
3.1
) and completes the proof of the first part of Theorem
1.2
.

Conversely, let
G
be a direct product
G={\mathbb{C}}^{k}\times G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{r}
where each
G_{i}
is a connected, simply-connected simple complex Lie group. It suffices to deal with the semisimple case, so in the following we will assume that
G
is a semisimple complex Lie group, with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. If we denote by
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
considered as a real algebra, then a Cartan decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
is given by
{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}}={\mathfrak{u}}+J{\mathfrak{u}}
, where
{\mathfrak{u}}
is the Lie algebra of a compact semisimple Lie group
U
. If
B
denotes the Cartan-Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
we define the Hermitian metric
g
by putting

g|_{\mathfrak{u}\times\mathfrak{u}}=-B,\ g(\mathfrak{u},J\mathfrak{u})=0,\ g|_{J\mathfrak{u}\times J\mathfrak{u}}=B.

This metric depends on the choice of a compact real form
\mathfrak{u}
, but as two such real forms are conjugate by an inner automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
(see e.g.
[
20
]
, p.184), the corresponding metrics are biholomorphically isometric. This construction is well-known (see e.g.
[
20
]
, p.182) and these metrics were used in
[
22
]
, where they are called
canonical
metrics, and in
[
19
]
among others.

In order to compute the Bismut connection using formula (
2.1
), we identify elements of
\mathfrak{g}
with left-invariant vector fields on
G
and we have the following well-known expressions

2\langle\nabla^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}_{x}y,z\rangle=\langle[x,y],z\rangle+\langle[z,x],y\rangle+\langle[z,y],x\rangle,

while

d\omega(x,y,z)=\langle[x,y],Jz\rangle+\langle[z,x],Jy\rangle+\langle[y,z],Jx\rangle,

where
J\in{\rm{End}}(\mathfrak{g})
denotes the complex structure. Using the fact that
{\rm ad}(x)
commutes with
J
for every
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

d\omega(Jx,Jy,Jz)=\langle[x,y],z\rangle+\langle[z,x],y\rangle+\langle[y,z],x\rangle,

so that

\langle\nabla^{b}_{x}y,z\rangle=\langle[x,y],z\rangle+\langle[z,x],y\rangle,\qquad x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

This can also be written as

\nabla^{b}_{x}=\rm{ad}(x)-\rm{ad}(x)^{*},\quad x\in\mathfrak{g}

where
∗
denotes the adjoint with respect to
g
.

Using the fact that
{\rm ad}(x)
is skew-symmetric if
x\in\mathfrak{u}
and symmetric if
x\in J\mathfrak{u}
, we obtain that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\nabla^{b}_{x}=2\ \rm{ad}(x),\ x\in\mathfrak{u},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\nabla^{b}_{x}=0,\ x\in J\mathfrak{u}.

Now we consider the torsion
T^{b}
which is given by

(3.5)

\displaystyle T^{b}(y,z)=3\ [y,z],\quad y,z\in\mathfrak{u},

(3.6)

\displaystyle T^{b}(y,z)=[y,z],\quad y\in\mathfrak{u},\ z\in J\mathfrak{u},

(3.7)

\displaystyle T^{b}(y,z)=-[y,z],\quad y,z\in J\mathfrak{u}.

that
\nabla^{b}T^{b}=0
, we only need to verify
(\nabla^{b}_{x}T^{b})(y,z)=0
for
x\in\mathfrak{u},\ y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
by formula (
3.4
).
When
y,z\in\mathfrak{u}
we have

(\nabla^{b}_{x}T^{b})(y,z)=3\nabla^{b}_{x}[y,z]-T^{b}(2[x,y],z)-T^{b}(y,2[x,z])

=6\left([x,[y,z]]-[[x,y],z]-[y,[x,z]]\right)=0

by Jacobi identity. When
z\in J\mathfrak{u}
and
y\in\mathfrak{u}
(
y\in J\mathfrak{u}
resp.), again by Jacoby identity

(\nabla^{b}_{x}T^{b})(y,z)=2\epsilon\,([x,[y,z]]-[[x,y],z]-[y,[x,z]])=0

with
\epsilon=1
(
\epsilon=-1
resp.).

∎

Remark 3.1
.

For
(M^{n},g)
in Theorem
1.2
, we have
M=G/\Gamma
where
\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of
\,\mbox{Iso}_{h}(G,g)
, the group of holomorphic isometries of
(G,g)
. The latter contains
G
(as left translations) since
g
is left-invariant. Note that in general there might not be a finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
such that
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq G
, and there might not be a finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
such that
\Gamma^{\prime}
is contained in the product of holomorphic isometry groups of the factors of
G
.

We recall that a metric connection with parallel torsion and curvature on a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is called an
Ambrose-Singer
connection, and its existence is a
necessary and sufficient condition for
M
to be locally homogeneous (see
[
4
]
,
[
31
]
). Moreover when
(M,g)
is simply-connected and complete, it admits an Ambrose-Singer connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion if and only if it is a
naturally reductive
homogeneous space with respect to some transitive Lie group
G
of isometries (see e.g.
[
31
]
). By the work of Sekigawa
[
27
]
in the Hermitian case, a complete, simply-connected Hermitian manifold
(M,g,J)
is homogeneous if and only if there exists an Ambrose-Singer connection which is Hermitian. Therefore
(M,g,J)
is a naturally reductive homogeneous space if and only if its Bismut connection is an Ambrose-Singer connection. As introduced in Definition
1.1
, a BTP metric
g
on an Hermitian manifold
(M,g,J)
is called
Bismut Ambrose-Singer
(BAS in short) if its Bismut connection has parallel curvature or equivalently, if its Bismut connection is an Ambrose-Singer connection.

It can be verified that for the metric
g
constructed in Theorem
1.2
, its Bismut connection also has parallel curvature thus is Ambrose-Singer. We have the following observation:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Hermitian manifold that is Chern flat and
BTP
. Then its Bismut connection is Ambrose-Singer, hence its universal covering manifold is naturally reductive.

Proof.

Using formula (
2.5
) we see that when the metric
g
is Chern flat and BTP,
R^{b}
is given by a quadratic expression of
T
, hence
\nabla^{b}R^{b}=0
. (Note that the components
R^{b}_{ijk\bar{\ell}}
always vanish for BTP metrics).
∎

Next let us examine the uniqueness issue, namely, how many Chern flat BTP metrics are there on the same manifold?
Let
M^{n}
be a compact complex manifold. Suppose
g
,
g^{\prime}
are Chern flat and BTP metrics on
M^{n}
. Then the torsion components of
g
and
g^{\prime}
turn the universal cover
\widetilde{M}
into (reductive) complex Lie groups
G
and
G^{\prime}
. First we claim the following

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
M^{n}
be a compact complex manifold. Suppose
g
,
g^{\prime}
are both Chern flat
BTP
metrics on
M^{n}
. The universal cover becomes reductive complex Lie groups
G
and
G^{\prime}
, respectively. Then
G
is isomorphic to
G^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Note that
G
and
G^{\prime}
are reductive simply-connected homeomorphic complex Lie groups. Consider their maximal compact subgroups
K
,
K^{\prime}
, which are semisimple and simply-connected. Clearly
K
and
K^{\prime}
share the same topological invariants and therefore are isomorphic as Lie groups. It then follows that the semisimple parts of
G
and
G^{\prime}
(which are the complexifications of
K
,
K^{\prime}
) are isomorphic. The abelian part then must also be isomorphic. Thus we have proved that
G
is isomorphic to
G^{\prime}
.
∎

The remaining question is to know how many left-invariant (thus Chern flat) BTP metrics are there on the same reductive complex Lie group
G
. By (
3.1
), we know that any left-invariant BTP metric
g
on
G
must preserve the product structure, and it is easy to see that the restriction of
g
on the vector group factor is Kähler and flat. Thus the main issue here rests upon those simple factors.

Suppose
G
is a simple complex Lie group with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Denote by
B
the Cartan-Killing form of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then a
B
-isometry
is a linear isomorphism
f\in GL({\mathfrak{g}})
such that
B(f(X),f(Y))=B(X,Y)
for any
X
,
Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Denote by
\mbox{Iso}(B)
the group all
B
-isometries of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. In general it is bigger than
\mbox{Aut}({\mathfrak{g}})
.

If
g
and
h
are both left-invariant Hermitian metrics on
G
, then they determine a unique linear isomorphism
f\in GL({\mathfrak{g}})
such that
h(X,Y)=g(f(X),Y)
for any
X
,
Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. We have the following

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G={\mathbb{C}}^{k}\times G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{r}
where each
G_{i}
is a connected, simply-connected simple complex Lie group. Then any left-invariant
BTP
Hermitian metric on
G
preserves the product structure, is Kähler flat on the Euclidean factor, and on each simple factor, two such metrics differ by a constant multiple of a
B
-isometry.

Proof.

Let
G
be as above and
g
a left-invariant Hermitian metric on
G
that is BTP. By (
3.1
), we know that
g
must be a product metric. Clearly the vector group factor corresponds to the kernel of the torsion
T
which coincides with the kernel of the Bismut curvature
R^{b}
, hence is Kähler and flat. The main issue here is to determine the metric up to a constant multiple on each simple factor. So in the following let us assume that
G
is a (connected and simply-connected) simple complex Lie group and
g
a left-invariant BTP Hermitian metric on
G
.

We want to understand the relationship between
g
and the group structure of
G
. Consider the Cartan-Killing form
B
of
\mathfrak{g}
, which can be written as

(3.8)

B(X,Y)=\mbox{Tr}(\rm{ad}(X)\circ\rm{ad}(Y))=\sum_{r,s=1}^{n}T^{r}_{sX}T^{s}_{rY}.

As
T
is
\nabla^{b}
-parallel, we have
\nabla^{b}B=0
, so by using formulas (
2.2
) we obtain for any
1\leq i,j\leq n
that

(3.9)

\left\{\begin{split}\ \sum_{l=1}^{n}\big(T^{l}_{ki}B(e_{l},e_{j})+T^{l}_{kj}B(e_{i},e_{l})\big)=0,\\
\ \sum_{i=1}^{l}\big(\overline{T^{i}_{kl}}B(e_{l},e_{j})+\overline{T^{j}_{kl}}B(e_{i},e_{l})\big)=0.\end{split}\right.

It is well known that for any given symmetric complex matrix
P
, there always exists a unitary matrix
Q
such that
{}^{t}\!QPQ
is diagonal with non-negative real elements. So by a constant unitary change of
e
if necessary, we may assume that
B=(B_{ij})=\mbox{diag}\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
with
a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}\geq 0
. Therefore (
3.9
) are written as

a_{j}T^{j}_{ik}+a_{i}T^{i}_{jk}=0,\ \ \ \ a_{j}T^{i}_{jk}+a_{i}T^{j}_{ik}=0,\ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j,k\leq n.

From this we obtain for
1\leq i,j\leq n

a_{j}^{2}\sum_{r}|T^{j}_{ir}|^{2}=a_{i}^{2}\sum_{r}|T^{i}_{jr}|^{2}=a_{i}^{2}\sum_{r}|T^{j}_{ir}|^{2}.

We then conclude that

(3.10)

T^{j}_{i\ast}=T^{i}_{j\ast}=0\ \ \ \mbox{whenever}\ a_{i}\neq a_{j}.

Now suppose that the diagonal elements of
B
are not all equal, say
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{r}>a_{r+1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}\geq 0
. Then for any
1\leq i\leq r
and any
r+1\leq\alpha\leq n
, by (
3.10
), we know that
T^{i}_{\alpha\ast}=T^{\alpha}_{i\ast}=0
. So if we let

{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}={\mathbb{C}}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\},\ \ \ \ {\mathfrak{g}}_{2}={\mathbb{C}}\{e_{r+1},\ldots,e_{n}\},

then both
{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
are ideals in the complex Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
, contradicting with the assumption that
{\mathfrak{g}}
is simple. Therefore the diagonal matrix
B
must be a constant multiple of the identity:
B=a_{1}I
. As
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a semisimple complex Lie algebra, we must have
a_{1}>0
, therefore

B(e_{i},e_{j})=a_{1}\delta_{ij}.

Note that if
\tilde{e}
is another basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, with
\tilde{e}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{ij}e_{j}
, then

B(\tilde{e}_{i},\tilde{e}_{j})=a_{1}\sum_{k}P_{ik}P_{jk},\ \ \ \mbox{while}\ \ g(\tilde{e}_{i},\tilde{e}_{j})=\sum_{k}P_{ik}\overline{P_{jk}},

so we get the following relation between
g
and
B
:

B\,^{t}\!g^{-1}\overline{B}=a_{1}^{2}g,

which holds under any basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Now suppose
h
is another left-invariant Hermitian metric on
G
that is BTP. Then the above equation would hold for
h
as well:

B\,^{t}\!h^{-1}\overline{B}=a_{1}^{\prime 2}h,

where
a_{1}^{\prime}>0
is another constant. Let us denote by
f:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
the isomorphism (as complex linear map) so that
h(X,Y)=\frac{a_{1}}{a_{1}^{\prime}}g(f(X),Y)
for any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then the above relations imply that

(3.11)

B(f(X),f(Y))=B(X,Y),\ \ \ \ \forall\ X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

So a constant multiple of
h
is related to
g
by a
B
-isometry. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.4
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Given a simple complex Lie group
G
, Theorem
1.2
says that there exist left-invariant
BTP
metrics on
G
, and Theorem
3.4
says that such metrics are related by
B
-isometries up to constant multiples. We do not know if such metrics are always related by inner automorphisms (up to constant multiples) or not, although this is indeed the case sometimes, for instance, when
n=3
.

4.
Flag manifolds and the BTP condition

In this section we consider the case of a compact simply-connected (generalized) flag manifold, namely a compact simply-connected Kähler homogeneous manifold
F
. We first recall the construction of invariant complex structures on
F
and then we will investigate the properties of BTP metrics.

Given a compact connected semisimple Lie group
K
and a closed subgroup
H\subset K
, a homogeneous space
F:=K/H
is called a
flag manifold
if
H
is the centralizer in
K
of a torus
Z\subset K
. In this case
H
turns out to be connected and it contains a maximal torus
T
of
K
. At the level of Lie algebras, we may fix a reductive decomposition
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{m}
, with
[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{m}]\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
so that
\mathfrak{m}
identifies with the tangent space
T_{[H]}F
. This identification is obtained using the map
\imath:X\ni\mathfrak{k}\to X^{*}\in\Gamma(TF)
which associates to each element
X
of
\mathfrak{k}
the vector field
X^{*}|_{gH}=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\exp(tX)gH
. Note that
[X^{*},Y^{*}]=-[X,Y]^{*}
for every
X,Y\in\mathfrak{k}
.

It is well-known (see e.g.
[
12
]
) that an invariant almost complex structure
J
on
F
corresponds to an endomorphism
J\in\mbox{End}(\mathfrak{m})
with
J^{2}=-Id
and
[\mbox{Ad}(h),J]=0
for every
h\in H
. If we decompose
\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{m}^{10}+\mathfrak{m}^{01}
into
J
-eigenspaces, then the integrability of
J
is equivalent to the condition

[\mathfrak{m}^{10},\mathfrak{m}^{10}]_{\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbb{C}}}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{10},

where the subscript
{-}_{\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbb{C}}}
denotes the projection onto
\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbb{C}}
with respect to the decomposition
\mathfrak{k}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}+\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Using the
\mbox{Ad}(H)
-invariance of
J
, the integrability condition can also be expressed by saying that
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}+\mathfrak{m}^{10}
is a subalgebra.

If we fix a maximal torus
T\subseteq H
and therefore a maximal abelian subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}
of
\mathfrak{k}
, then we can consider the root space decomposition

\mathfrak{k}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}+\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R}\mathfrak{k}_{\alpha},

where
R\subset(\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}})^{*}
is the set of all roots and
\mathfrak{k}_{\alpha}=\{v\in\mathfrak{k}^{\mathbb{C}}|\ [h,v]=\alpha(h)v,\ h\in\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}\}
is the root space corresponding to the root
\alpha\in R
. As
\mathfrak{h}
contains
\mathfrak{t}
, we have that
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}+\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{h}}}\mathfrak{k}_{\alpha}
for some subset
R_{\mathfrak{h}}
of
R
and
\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}}\mathfrak{k}_{\alpha}
, where
R_{\mathfrak{m}}=R\setminus R_{\mathfrak{h}}
. Moreover as
J
commutes with
\rm{ad}(h)
for every
h\in\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}
, we have that
\mathfrak{m}^{10}
is made of root spaces. Then we can select an ordering of the roots, namely a splitting
R=R^{+}\cup R^{-}
with
R^{-}=-R^{+}
so that for
R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\pm}=R_{\mathfrak{m}}\cap R^{\pm}
we have

(R_{\mathfrak{h}}+R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+})\cap R\subseteq R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+},\quad(R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}+R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+})\cap R\subseteq R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}

and

J|_{\mathfrak{k}_{\alpha}}=i\epsilon_{\alpha}\ \mbox{Id}\ \quad\mbox{where}\qquad\epsilon_{\alpha}=\pm 1\ \mbox{for}\ \alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\pm}.

This means that
\mathfrak{m}^{10}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}}\mathfrak{k}_{\alpha}
.

We now consider an invariant
J
-Hermitian metric
g
. The
\rm{ad}(\mathfrak{t})
-invariance of
g
implies that if
v\in\mathfrak{k}_{\alpha}
and
w\in\mathfrak{k}_{\beta}
for
\alpha,\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}
then

0=(\alpha(h)+\beta(h))\cdot g(v,w),

so that
g(v,w)\neq 0
implies
\alpha=-\beta
. This means that
g
is completely determined by its restriction to the one-dimensional root spaces
\mathfrak{k}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}
. In order to simplify computations, we fix the Weyl basis
\{E_{\alpha}\}
of
\mathfrak{k}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in R
with the property that (see
[
20
]
, p.176)

B(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})=1,\quad[E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha}]=H_{\alpha},

where
\alpha(h)=B(h,H_{\alpha})
for every
h\in\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}
. The real algebra
\mathfrak{k}
is spanned by
\mathfrak{t}
together with the vectors

v_{\alpha}:=E_{\alpha}-E_{-\alpha},\quad w_{\alpha}:=i\ (E_{\alpha}+E_{-\alpha}),\quad\alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}

and
\overline{E_{\alpha}}=-E_{-\alpha}
for every
\alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}
,where the conjugation is with respect to the compact real form
\mathfrak{k}
of
\mathfrak{k}^{\mathbb{C}}
.
It then follows that the metric
g
satisfies
g(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta})\neq 0
if and only if
\beta=-\alpha
and therefore it is completely determined by the real positive numbers

g_{\alpha}:=g(E_{\alpha},\overline{E_{\alpha}})=-g(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})=\frac{1}{2}g(v_{\alpha},v_{\alpha})>0

which are subject to the condition of
\mbox{ad}(\mathfrak{h})
-invariance, namely

g_{\alpha+\gamma}=g_{\alpha},\qquad\alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}},\ \gamma\in R_{\mathfrak{h}},\ \alpha+\gamma\in R.

It is not difficult to see that the metric
g
is Kähler if and only if the coefficients
\{g_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}}
satisfy the relation

g_{\alpha+\beta}=g_{\alpha}+g_{\beta},\quad\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}.

A special Hermitian metric is given by
g_{o}:=-B|_{\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m}}
, where
B
denotes the Cartan-Killing form of
\mathfrak{k}
, and this corresponds to the condition
g_{\alpha}=1
for every
\alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}
, so that
g_{o}
is Kähler if and only if the sum of two positive roots in
R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}
is never a root , hence
[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]_{\mathfrak{m}}=\{0\}
, i.e.
K/H
is a Hermitian symmetric space. We now show that the associated Bismut connection coincides with the canonical connection on the reductive homogeneous space
K/H
. Indeed we can consider the invariant canonical linear connection
\nabla
on
K/H
whose torsion tensor
T^{\nabla}
at the point
[eH]\in K/H
is given by (see e.g.
[
21
]
, p.193)

T^{\nabla}(X,Y)|_{[eH]}=-[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{m}}\qquad X,Y\in\mathfrak{m}.

As any
K
-invariant tensor on
K/H
is
\nabla
-parallel, then
\nabla
leaves the metric
g_{o}
and
J
parallel. Moreover, by the standard property of
B
, we have at
[eH]

g_{o}(T^{\nabla}(X,Y),Z)=B([X,Y]_{\mathfrak{m}},Z)=B([X,Y],Z)=-B([X,Z],Y)=-g_{o}(T^{\nabla}(X,Z),Y)

for any
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{m}
, so that the torsion is skewsymmetric and
\nabla
coincides with the Bismut connection
\nabla^{b}
of
g_{o}
. Note the curvature
R^{\nabla}
is also
\nabla
-parallel, so that
\nabla
is an Ambrose-Singer connection of
(K/H,g_{o})
. In other words,
(K/H,g_{o})
is a
BAS
manifold in the terminology of
[
24
]
, where the word is the abbreviation for
Bismut Ambrose-Singer
, meaning a compact Hermitian manifold whose Bismut connection is Ambrose-Singer. To summarize, we have

Proposition 4.1
.

The Cartan-Killing metric
g_{o}
on the flag manifold
K/H
is
BTP
. In fact it is
BAS
.

We remark that in
[
38
]
, where the classification of three-dimensional compact balanced BTP manifolds were given, the Fano case leads to the flag threefold, and it took a somewhat length computation to verify that the particular metric is balanced and BTP. By the above proposition it suffices to check that the metric is associated with the Cartan-Killing form.

The following proposition shows that all the invariant Hermitian metrics on a flag manifold are balanced. This result is probably already known, but we prefer to include an elementary proof.

Proposition 4.2
.

Every
K
-invariant Hermitian metric on
K/H
is balanced.

Proof.

Let
g
be any invariant Hermitian metric. The balanced condition is given by
d\omega^{n-1}=0
, where
\omega
is the associated fundamental form of
g
and
n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}K/H
. This is equivalent to saying that
\delta\omega=0
, where
\delta
denotes the codifferential w.r.t.
g
. Now,
\delta\omega
is an invariant
1
-form, hence it corresponds to an invariant vector field
X
on
K/H
. This implies that the vector
X_{[eH]}
corresponds to a vector
v\in\mathfrak{m}
which is invariant by the isotropy representation
\lambda:H\to\mbox{SO}(\mathfrak{m},g)
, hence
[h,v]=0
for every
h\in\mathfrak{h}
. In particular
[v,\mathfrak{t}]=0
, contradicting the maximality of the abelian subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}
, unless
v=0
, hence
X=0
by invariance. ∎

We now consider an invariant Hermitian metric
g
on the flag manifold
F=K/H
and compute its Bismut connection. We recall that the Nomizu operator relative to the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}
of
g
, namely
\Lambda^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}:\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})
defined as
\Lambda^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}(X)(Y)=\imath|_{\mathfrak{m}}^{-1}(\nabla^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}_{X^{*}}Y^{*})|_{[H]}+[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{m}}
, is given by (see
[
21
]
)

\Lambda^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}(X)(Y)=\frac{1}{2}([X,Y]_{\mathfrak{m}}+U(X,Y)),

where the bilinear map
U:\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{m}
is defined as

g(U(X,Y),Z)=g([Z,X]_{\mathfrak{m}},Y)+g([Z,Y]_{\mathfrak{m}},X).

From this definition is follows that for roots
\alpha,\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}
, we have that
g(U(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta}),E_{\gamma})\neq 0
only when
\gamma=-\alpha-\beta
, provided this is a root. If we use the standard notation
[E_{\alpha},E_{\beta}]=N_{\alpha,\beta}E_{\alpha+\beta}
, we have

g(U(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta}),E_{-\alpha-\beta})=-N_{-\alpha-\beta,\alpha}\cdot g_{\beta}-N_{-\alpha-\beta,\beta}\cdot g_{\alpha}.

Using the property that
N_{-\alpha-\beta,\alpha}=N_{\alpha,\beta}=N_{\beta,-\alpha-\beta}
(see e.g.
[
20
]
, p.172), we obtain

U(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta})=N_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{g_{\beta}-g_{\alpha}}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}(E_{\alpha+\beta})_{\mathfrak{m}},\quad\alpha,\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}

and therefore

(4.1)

\Lambda^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}(E_{\alpha})(E_{\beta})=\frac{1}{2}N_{\alpha,\beta}\left(1+\frac{g_{\beta}-g_{\alpha}}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\cdot(E_{\alpha+\beta})_{\mathfrak{m}}.

In order to compute the Bismut connection, we need the expression of
d\omega(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta},E_{\gamma})
for
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Using the
\rm{ad}(\mathfrak{t})
-invariance, we see that
d\omega(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta},E_{\gamma})=0
if
\gamma\neq-\alpha-\beta
, while we have (see also
[
3
]
)

d\omega(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta},E_{-\alpha-\beta})=-iN_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\epsilon_{\alpha}g_{\alpha}+\epsilon_{\beta}g_{\beta}-\epsilon_{\alpha+\beta}g_{\alpha+\beta}\right)

and

d\omega(JE_{\alpha},JE_{\beta},JE_{-\alpha-\beta})=-N_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\epsilon_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\beta}g_{\alpha+\beta}-\epsilon_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\alpha+\beta}g_{\beta}-\epsilon_{\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha+\beta}g_{\alpha}\right).

Using formula (
2.1
) for the Bismut connection, we see that the corresponding Nomizu operator
\Lambda^{b}
is given by

\Lambda^{b}(E_{\alpha})E_{\beta}=\Lambda^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}(E_{\alpha})(E_{\beta})-\frac{1}{2g_{\alpha+\beta}}d\omega(JE_{\alpha},JE_{\beta},JE_{-\alpha-\beta})\,(E_{\alpha+\beta})_{\mathfrak{m}}=

=\frac{1}{2}N_{\alpha,\beta}\left((1+\epsilon_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\beta})-(1+\epsilon_{\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha+\beta})\frac{g_{\alpha}}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}+(1-\epsilon_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\alpha+\beta})\frac{g_{\beta}}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\,(E_{\alpha+\beta})_{\mathfrak{m}}.

In particular we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{b}(E_{\alpha})(E_{\beta})=N_{\alpha,\beta}\left(1-\frac{g_{\alpha}}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\,E_{\alpha+\beta},\qquad\mbox{if}\ \alpha,\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{b}(E_{\alpha})(E_{\beta})=0,\qquad\mbox{if}\ \alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+},\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{-},\alpha+\beta\not\in R\ {\rm{or}}\ \alpha+\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{b}(E_{\alpha})(E_{\beta})=N_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\frac{g_{\beta}-g_{\alpha}}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\,E_{\alpha+\beta},\qquad\mbox{if}\ \alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\pm},\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mp},\alpha+\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mp}.

Therefore the torsion
T^{b}
, which is given by
T^{b}(X,Y)=\Lambda^{b}(X)(Y)-\Lambda^{b}(Y)(X)-[X,Y]
for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{m}
, can be written as

(4.5)

\displaystyle T^{b}(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta})=N_{\alpha,\beta}\left(1-\frac{g_{\alpha}+g_{\beta}}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}\right)E_{\alpha+\beta},\qquad\mbox{if}\ \alpha,\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+},

(4.6)

\displaystyle T^{b}(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta})=0,\qquad\mbox{if}\ \alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+},\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{-},\alpha+\beta\not\in R_{\mathfrak{m}},

(4.7)

\displaystyle T^{b}(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta})=N_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\frac{g_{\alpha}-g_{\beta}}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}-1\right)E_{\alpha+\beta},\qquad\mbox{if}\ \alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+},\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{-},\alpha+\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+},

(4.8)

\displaystyle T^{b}(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta})=N_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\frac{g_{\beta}-g_{\alpha}}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}-1\right)E_{\alpha+\beta},\qquad\mbox{if}\ \alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+},\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{-},\alpha+\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{-}.

Using the skew-symmetry of the torsion
T^{b}
, we immediately see that the BTP condition
\nabla^{b}T^{b}=0
boils down to verifying that for every positive roots
\alpha,\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}
we have

\nabla^{b}_{E_{\gamma}}T^{b}(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta})=0,\qquad\mbox{for\ every}\ \gamma\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}
with
\alpha+\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}
. Given the two conditions

i)

\nabla^{b}_{E_{-\alpha}}T^{b}(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta})=0

ii)

\nabla^{b}_{E_{-\beta}}T^{b}(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta})=0

we have:

1.

if
\alpha-\beta\not\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}
then (i) and (ii) imply that either
g_{\alpha+\beta}=g_{\alpha}+g_{\beta}
or
g_{\alpha+\beta}=g_{\alpha}=g_{\beta}
;

2.

if
\alpha-\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}

g_{\alpha+\beta}=g_{\alpha}+g_{\beta}

g_{\alpha+\beta}=g_{\alpha}
;

3.

if
\alpha-\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{-}

g_{\alpha+b}=g_{\alpha}+g_{\beta}
or
g_{\alpha+\beta}=g_{\beta}

Proof.

We have that (i) is equivalent to

0=\Lambda^{b}(E_{-\alpha})(T^{b}(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta}))-T^{b}(\Lambda^{b}(E_{-\alpha})E_{\alpha},E_{b})-T^{b}(E_{\alpha},\Lambda^{b}(E_{-\alpha})E_{\beta}),

In case 3, we use (ii) and the same computation as above with

.

F=K/H
be a flag manifold endowed with an invariant Hermitian metric

\{g_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}}
. We suppose that
\mathfrak{k}

satisfies the

condition. Let
\alpha,\beta\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}
be positive roots with
\alpha+\beta\in R

a)

if there is
\gamma\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}

\beta+\gamma\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}

(a) By Lemma
4.3
, (1), we know that
g_{\alpha}=g_{\beta}=g_{\alpha+\beta}
. Moreover we know that any string of roots has length

\displaystyle 0=\Lambda^{b}(E_{\alpha})(T(E_{\beta},E_{\gamma}))-T(\Lambda^{b}(E_{\alpha})E_{\beta},E_{\gamma})-T(E_{\beta},\Lambda^{b}(E_{\alpha})E_{\gamma})

\mathfrak{k}

The case of a compact Lie group with a Samelson structure

We consider a compact even-dimensional semisimple Lie group
G
with a Samelson structure
J
. We recall that a Samelson structure
J
is a left invariant complex structure on
G
which is constructed starting from a maximal torus
T\subset G
and a system of positive roots
R^{+}
relative to the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
(
[
26
]
). The complex structure
J
acts as
iI
(resp.
-iI
) on each positive (resp. negative) root space, while it maps
\mathfrak{t}
onto itself and the restriction
J|_{\mathfrak{t}}
can be arbitrarily chosen as a complex structure on
\mathfrak{t}
. A well-known result states that every left-invariant complex structure on
G
is of this type for a suitable choice of a maximal torus
T
and therefore it is also invariant under the right action of
T
. This accounts to say that the projection
\pi:G\to G/T
is holomorphic when we endow the full flag manifold
G/T
with the induced complex structure and
\pi
can be seen as the Tits fibration (see e.g.
[
2
]
).

The aim of this section is to prove the result stated in Theorem
1.5
, which provides a characterization of BTP metrics on a compact semisimple Lie group
G
endowed with a Samelson structure under the additional hypothesis that the metric is invariant under right translations by
T
or, equivalently, it descends to an invariant Hermitian metric on the base of the Tits fibration.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

The Samelson invariant complex structure is determined by the choice of a maximal torus
T\subset G
and a set of positive roots relative to the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}
. The condition that the metric
g
descends to a metric on the base
G/T
of the Tits fibration means that the metric
g
is also invariant under right translations by elements of
T
.

Using now the same arguments used in the computation of the Bismut connection on a flag manifold, we see that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\nabla^{b}_{E_{\alpha}}E_{\beta}=N_{\alpha,\beta}\left(1-\frac{g_{\alpha}}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\,E_{\alpha+\beta},\qquad\mbox{if}\ \alpha,\beta\in R^{+},

(5.2)

\displaystyle\nabla^{b}_{E_{\alpha}}E_{\beta}=0,\qquad\mbox{if}\ \alpha\in R^{+},\beta\in R^{-},\alpha\neq-\beta,\quad\alpha+\beta\not\in R\ {\rm{or}}\ \alpha+\beta\in R^{+}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\nabla^{b}_{E_{\alpha}}E_{\beta}=N_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\frac{g_{\beta}-g_{\alpha}}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\,E_{\alpha+\beta},\qquad\mbox{if}\ \alpha\in R^{\pm},\beta\in R^{\mp},\alpha+\beta\in R^{\mp}.

In particular the statement in Lemma
4.3
holds true.
We now compute for
\alpha\in R
and
H\in\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}

d\omega(H,E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})=-\omega([H,E_{\alpha}],E_{-\alpha})+\omega([H,E_{-\alpha}],E_{\alpha})-\omega([E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha}],H)=

=-\alpha(H)\omega(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})-\alpha(H)\omega(E_{-\alpha},E_{\alpha})-\omega(H_{\alpha},H)=g(H_{\alpha},JH).

Moreover

g(U(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha}),H)=g([H,E_{\alpha}],E_{-\alpha})+g([H,E_{-\alpha}],E_{\alpha})=-\alpha(H)g_{\alpha}+\alpha(H)g_{\alpha}=0,

so that

U(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})=0.

This implies that

g(\nabla^{b}_{E_{\alpha}}E_{-\alpha},H)=g(\nabla^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}_{E_{\alpha}}E_{-\alpha},H)+\frac{1}{2}d\omega(JE_{\alpha},JE_{-\alpha},JH)=

=\frac{1}{2}g(H_{\alpha},H)+\frac{1}{2}d\omega(JE_{\alpha},JE_{-\alpha},JH)=

=\frac{1}{2}g(H_{\alpha},H)+\frac{1}{2}d\omega(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha},JH)=\frac{1}{2}g(H_{\alpha},H)-\frac{1}{2}g(H_{\alpha},H)=0

so that

(5.4)

\nabla^{b}_{E_{\alpha}}E_{-\alpha}=0,\qquad T^{b}(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})=-H_{\alpha}.

From the expression of
U
we see that
U(E_{\alpha},H)=c_{\alpha}E_{\alpha}
for some constant
c_{\alpha}
and therefore

g(U(E_{\alpha},H),E_{-\alpha})=g([E_{-\alpha},E_{\alpha}],H)+g([E_{-\alpha},H],E_{\alpha})

-c_{\alpha}g_{\alpha}=-g(H_{\alpha},H)+\alpha(H)g(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})=-g(H_{\alpha},H)-\alpha(H)g_{\alpha}

hence

U(E_{\alpha},H)=(\alpha(H)+\frac{g(H_{\alpha},H)}{g_{\alpha}})E_{\alpha}.

This implies that

\nabla^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}_{H}E_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha(H)E_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha(H)+\frac{g(H_{\alpha},H)}{g_{\alpha}})E_{\alpha}=\big(\alpha(H)+\frac{g(H_{\alpha},H)}{2g_{\alpha}}\big)E_{\alpha},

\nabla^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}_{E_{\alpha}}H=\frac{g(H_{\alpha},H)}{2g_{\alpha}}E_{\alpha}.

It follows that

g(\nabla^{b}_{H}E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})=g(\nabla^{\mbox{\tiny LC}}_{H}E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})+\frac{1}{2}d\omega(JH,JE_{\alpha},JE_{-\alpha})=

=-g_{\alpha}\alpha(H)-\frac{g(H_{\alpha},H)}{2}-\frac{g(H_{\alpha},H)}{2}

so that

(5.5)

\nabla^{b}_{H}E_{\alpha}=\big(\alpha(H)+\frac{g(H,H_{\alpha})}{g_{\alpha}}\big)E_{\alpha}

and similarly,

(5.6)

\nabla^{b}_{E_{\alpha}}H=0.

Therefore

T^{b}(H,E_{\alpha})=\frac{g(H,H_{\alpha})}{g_{\alpha}}E_{\alpha}.

Finally it is immediate to see that

\nabla^{b}_{H_{1}}H_{2}=0,\qquad T^{b}(H_{1},H_{2})=0,\qquad H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in R^{+}
with
\alpha+\beta\in R^{+}
. Then
g_{\alpha+\beta}\neq g_{\alpha}+g_{\beta}
.

Proof.

We suppose by contradiction that
g_{\alpha+\beta}=g_{\alpha}+g_{\beta}
. Using the expressions for
\nabla^{b}
given in (
5.6
) and (
4.2
), we compute for every
H\in\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla^{b}_{E_{\alpha}}T(H,E_{\beta})=\frac{g(H,H_{\beta})}{g_{\beta}}\nabla^{b}_{E_{\alpha}}E_{\beta}-T(H,\nabla^{b}_{E_{\alpha}}E_{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{\alpha,\beta}\left(1-\frac{g_{\alpha}}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{g(H,H_{\beta})}{g_{\beta}}-\frac{g(H,H_{\alpha}+H_{\beta})}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}\right)E_{\alpha+\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{\alpha,\beta}\left(1-\frac{g_{\alpha}}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}\right)\cdot\frac{g(H,g_{\alpha}H_{\beta}-g_{\beta}H_{\alpha})}{g_{\beta}(g_{\alpha}+g_{\beta})}\,E_{\alpha+\beta}.

As
\alpha,\beta
are not proportional, we have
g_{\alpha}H_{\beta}\neq g_{\beta}H_{\alpha}
and therefore there exists
H\in\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}
so that
g(H,g_{\alpha}H_{\beta}-g_{\beta}H_{\alpha})\neq 0
. This implies that
1-\frac{g_{\alpha}}{g_{\alpha+\beta}}=0
, hence
g_{\alpha}+g_{\beta}=g_{\alpha+\beta}=g_{\alpha}
, a contradiction. ∎

We let
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\}
to be the set of simple roots. As
\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\not\in R
for every
i,j=1,\ldots,r
, by Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
5.1
we have that
g_{\alpha_{i}}=g_{\alpha_{j}}
whenever
\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}
is a root and this happens precisely when the two roots are connected in the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
. As the Dynkin diagram is connected, we see that
g_{\alpha_{i}}=g_{\alpha_{j}}
for every
i,j=1,\ldots,r
and we can normalize this value to be
1
.

We now prove that
g_{\alpha}=1
for every
\alpha\in R^{+}
. Indeed, we prove our claim on induction on the height of the root
\alpha
, which is defined as
ht(\alpha):=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}
, where

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}\alpha_{i}
for nonnegative integers
n_{i}
. Given
\alpha
with height

, it is well-known that there exists at least one simple root, say
\alpha_{i}
, with
\alpha-\alpha_{i}=\beta\in R
. As
ht(\beta)=q-1
, by induction hypothesis we have
g_{\beta}=1
. Moreover
\beta-\alpha_{i}
is either not a root or a positive root. Using Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
4.3
we see that
g_{\alpha}=g_{\beta}=g_{\alpha_{i}}=1
and this concludes the proof of the first claim.

To prove the second claim, we fix any
J
-Hermitian metric
g_{o}
on
\mathfrak{t}
and extend it to a metric
g
on
\mathfrak{g}
by taking
-B
on each root space. It the follows that the Bismut connection
\nabla^{b}
satisfies
\nabla^{b}_{E_{\alpha}}X=0
for every root
\alpha
and every
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, while for
H\in\mathfrak{t}
we have
\nabla^{b}_{H}Y=0
for every
Y\in\mathfrak{t}
and

\nabla^{b}_{H}E_{\alpha}=(\alpha(H)+g_{o}(H,H_{\alpha}))E_{\alpha},\qquad\alpha\in R.

To check that
\nabla^{b}_{X}T^{b}=0
for every
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, we only need to compute:

(1)
\nabla^{b}_{H}T^{b}(E_{\alpha},E_{\beta})
with
\beta\neq-\alpha
;

(2)
\nabla^{b}_{H}T^{b}(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})
;

(3)
\nabla^{b}_{H}T^{b}(H^{\prime},E_{\alpha})
.

If we use the fact that
T^{b}(X,Y)=-[X,Y]
unless

and

with
T^{b}(H,E_{\alpha})=g(H,H_{\alpha})E_{\alpha}
, it is straightforward to verify that
\nabla^{b}T^{b}=0
in the cases (1)-(3) and this concludes the proof.∎

Remark 5.2
.

The proof actually shows that the
BTP
property does not depend on the restriction of the metric along the fibers of the Tits fibration. Moreover we easily check that the only possibly non-vanishing component of the curvature tensor
R^{b}
is given by
R^{b}(E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha})E_{\beta}=-[\beta(H_{\alpha})+g(H_{\alpha},H_{\beta})]E_{\beta}
for roots
\alpha,\beta
. From this one concludes that
\nabla^{b}R^{b}=0
and the connection
\nabla^{b}
is Ambrose-Singer.

6.
A conjecture on homogeneous BTP metrics

In this section we focus on BAS metrics on compact Hermitian manifolds. According to Definition
1.1
, we recall that a Hermitian metric is called BAS when its Bismut connection has parallel torsion and curvature. By the aforementioned result of Ambrose-Singer and Sekigawa, we know that a necessary condition for a BTP metric to be BAS is that the metric is locally homogeneous, namely, its universal covering space is a homogeneous Hermitian manifold. Another necessary condition for BAS is that the metric needs to be free of non-locally symmetric Kähler de Rham factors, as Kähler metrics are always BTP, but they are BAS only when they are locally Hermitian symmetric. It is then natural to ask the following:

Question 6.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Hermitian manifold that is
BTP
with its universal cover being a homogeneous Hermitian manifold without Kähler de Rham factor. When will
g
be
BAS
?

The answer is positive when
n=2
as observed in
[
24
]
, namely, any non-Kähler locally Hermitian homogeneous BTP surface is always BAS. This is because BTP surfaces are exactly Vaisman surfaces by
[
36
, Theorem 2]
, and such surfaces (if not Kähler) always admit local unitary frames
e
under which the only possibly non-zero component of the Bismut curvature is
R^{b}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}
, which equals the Bismut scalar curvature
s^{b}
, so
\nabla^{b}R^{b}=0
if and only if
s^{b}
is constant. When the surface is locally homogeneous,
s^{b}
is constant thus it is BAS. The converse is certainly true as well. So one could state that:

Given any non-Kähler
BTP
surface, it is
BAS

\Longleftrightarrow
it has constant scalar curvature
\Longleftrightarrow
it is locally Hermitian homogeneous.

Note that here we did not specify which scalar curvature that was used, as on any BTP surface the scalar curvature of Levi-Civita or Chern or Bismut connection, or the trace of the first or second Ricci curvature of Chern or Bismut connection, all differ by constants. So any one of them equals to constant means all of them are constants.

We now give some examples where the answer to Question
6.1
is positive.

Our first example concerns Hopf manifolds. We remark that in
[
5
]
it is proved that a locally conformally Kähler (LCK) manifold is BTP if and only if it is Vaisman, so that diagonal Hopf manifolds are the only linear Hopf manifolds (see e.g. Cor. 16.16 in
[
25
]
) that can carry a BTP metric.

Example 6.2

(Isosceles Hopf manifolds)
.

Consider the Hopf manifold
M^{n}=({\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus\{0\})/\Gamma
with the deck transformation group
\Gamma\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
generated by the contraction
f
from
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
onto itself given by

f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=(a_{1}z_{1},\ldots,a_{n}z_{n}),

where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
satisfy
0<|a_{1}|=\cdots=|a_{n}|<1
. We will call such Hopf manifolds
isosceles Hopf manifolds
. In this case, the Hermitian metric
g
with Kähler form

\omega=\sqrt{-1}\,\frac{\partial\overline{\partial}|z|^{2}}{|z|^{2}}

descends to
M^{n}
. Here and below we write
|z|^{2}=|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{n}|^{2}
. As is well-known,
(M^{n},g)
is Vaisman, hence by
[
5
]
it is BTP. We claim that it is BAS.

To verify this, let us write
e_{i}=|z|\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}
,
\varphi_{i}=\frac{1}{|z|}dz_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
. Then
e
forms a local unitary frame on
(M^{n},g)
with dual coframe
\varphi
. Under the frame
e
, a straight forward computation leads us to the formula for the matrix of Bismut connection and components of the Chern curvature

\displaystyle\theta^{b}\ =\ \frac{1}{2}(\partial-\overline{\partial})\log|z|^{2}I+\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\big(d\overline{z}\,^{t}\!z-\overline{z}\,d\,^{t}\!z\big),

\displaystyle R_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}\ =\ \xi_{ij}\delta_{k\ell},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \xi\ =\ I-\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\overline{z}\,^{t}\!z.

We compute the matrix commutator

\displaystyle[\theta^{b},\xi]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\,\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\big(d\overline{z}\,^{t}\!z-\overline{z}\,d\,^{t}\!z\big),\,-\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\overline{z}\,^{t}\!z\,]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|z|^{4}}\{-d\overline{z}\,|z|^{2}\,{}^{t}\!z+\overline{z}\,\partial|z|^{2}\,{}^{t}\!z+\overline{z}\,\overline{\partial}|z|^{2}\,{}^{t}\!z-\overline{z}\,|z|^{2}d\,^{t}\!z\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d|z|^{2}}{|z|^{4}}\,\overline{z}\,^{t}\!z-\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\,d(\overline{z}\,^{t}\!z)\ =\ d\xi

Therefore,

\displaystyle dR_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\xi_{ij}\,\delta_{k\ell}\ =\ \big(\theta^{b}\xi-\xi\theta^{b})_{ij}\,\delta_{k\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r}\{R_{r\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}\,\theta^{b}_{ir}-R_{i\bar{r}k\bar{\ell}}\,\theta^{b}_{rj}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r}\{R_{r\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}\,\theta^{b}_{ir}-R_{i\bar{r}k\bar{\ell}}\,\theta^{b}_{rj}+R_{i\bar{j}r\bar{\ell}}\,\theta^{b}_{kr}-R_{i\bar{j}k\bar{r}}\,\theta^{b}_{r\ell}\}

where the last equality is due to the fact that
[\theta^{b},I]=0
. This means that
\nabla^{b}R=0
which is equivalent to
\nabla^{b}R^{b}=0
for BTP manifold, hence
(M^{n},g)
is BAS.

Example 6.3

(Calabi-Eckmann manifolds)
.

We consider the homogeneous space
M=S^{2m_{1}+1}\times S^{2m_{2}+1}=G/H
, where
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
,
H=H_{1}\times H_{2}
and

G_{i}=\rm{SU}(m_{i}+1)
,
H_{i}=\rm{SU}(m_{i})
for
i=1,2
. We also fix a reductive decomposition

\mathfrak{g}_{i}=\mathfrak{h}_{i}+\mathfrak{z}_{i}+\mathfrak{m}_{i},\quad i=1,2,

where
\mathfrak{z}_{i}
is the one-dimensional centralizer of
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
in
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
is an
\rm{ad}(\mathfrak{h}_{i}+\mathfrak{z}_{i})
-invariant subspace. Note that
\mathfrak{h}_{i}+\mathfrak{z}_{i}
is the Lie algebra of the subgroup
K_{i}:=\rm{S}(\rm U(1)\times\rm U(m_{i}))
and that
G_{i}/K_{i}\cong\mathbb{C}P^{m_{i}}
. The subspace
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
is isomorphic to the tangent space
T_{o}\mathbb{C}P^{m_{i}}
at the origin

and it is endowed with a
{\rm Ad}(K_{i})
-invariant complex structure
J_{i}
.

We can endow
M
with an invariant complex structure
J
as follows (see e.g.
[
32
]
,
[
9
]
). We fix generators
z_{i}
of
\mathfrak{z}_{i}
and we put
\mathfrak{q}:={\rm{Span}}(z_{1},z_{2})
. We then define
J
so that
J(\mathfrak{m}_{i})=\mathfrak{m}_{i}
with
J|_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}=J_{i}
and
J|_{\mathfrak{q}}
is the endomorphism which can be expressed by the matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&-\frac{1+\alpha^{2}}{\beta}\\
\beta&-\alpha\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R},\beta\neq 0
, with respect to the basis
\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
. The complex structure
J
is easily seen to define an invariant integrable complex structure on
M
.

Moreover, we can define
J

g
on
M
in the following way. We decree

g(\mathfrak{m}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{j})=0\ {\rm{if}}\,i\neq j,\qquad g(\mathfrak{m}_{i},\mathfrak{q})=0,\,i=1,2,

g|_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}\times\mathfrak{m}_{i}}=-c_{i}B_{i}|_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}\times\mathfrak{m}_{i}},\,i=1,2,

where
c_{i}<0
are negative real numbers and
B_{i}
denotes the Cartan-Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}

g
on the subspace
\mathfrak{q}
to be any
J
-Hermitian metric: any such

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\\

\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
.

We will show that all these homogeneous spaces
(M,g,J)
, which are called Calabi-Eckmann manifolds, are naturally reductive, so that the corresponding Bismut connections have parallel curvature, hence

is (BAS).

In order to do this, we will enlarge the group

and observe that the isometry group of

contains the group
\hat{G}:=G\times A

\hat{H}=\{(g,q)\in\hat{G}|\ g\rho(a)\in H\}=\{(h\rho(a)^{-1},a)|\ a\in A,h\in H\}.

We consider a reductive

\hat{\mathfrak{g}}=\hat{\mathfrak{h}}+V_{f}+\hat{\mathfrak{m}}

It is not difficult to show that we can choose a suitable

Example 6.4

(Vaisman solvmanifolds)
.

If

Example 6.5
.

In the previous sections, we have provided other significant examples, which can be summarized as follows:

i)

Any Chern flat Hermitian metric on a compact complex manifold is BAS whenever it is BTP (see Proposition
3.2
);

ii)

any invariant BTP metric on a generalized flag manifold with at most two irreducible summands or on the full flag manifolds

and Theorem

given a compact Lie group

, Remark

).

At this point in time we do not know whether or not the answer to Question
6.1
is always yes when

. However, we are able to exhibit an example of
a compact homogeneous complex
4
-fold

which is endowed with a BTP metric that is not BAS. The manifold
M^{4}
is defined as the product

, where

3
-fold

is Vaisman and therefore BTP. For the sake of clarity, we will carry out the construction in a detailed way.

Namely, we consider the Wallach
3
-fold

and we will denote by

the root system corresponding to
\rm{T}
and with the standard choice of simple roots
\{\alpha:=\epsilon_{12},\beta:=\epsilon_{23}\}

on

the subspace
\mathfrak{m}^{1,0}
is spanned by the root spaces corresponding to positive roots.

We now select an element

that generates the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{l}
of a one-dimensional torus
\rm L

{\rm{Ad}}(\rm{T})
-invariant decomposition

[

determines an invariant Kähler metric

(6.2)

\omega_{o}(x,y)=B([\ell,x],y),\quad x,y\in\mathfrak{m},

where

\mathfrak{su}(3)

The subspace

The homogeneous manifold

is an

\rm S^{1}

Proposition 6.6
.

The homogeneous Hermitian manifold
(M^{4},J,g)
is
BTP
but not

.

Proof.

First of all we will show that

[
5

If
\omega

(1) When

d\omega(x,y,z)=-\omega([x,y]_{\mathfrak{p}},z)+\omega([x,z]_{\mathfrak{p}},y)+\omega([y,z]_{\mathfrak{p}},x)=0

as

is

is Kähler.

(2) When

\displaystyle d\omega(x,y,\ell)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega([x,y]_{\mathfrak{p}},\ell)+\omega([x,\ell],y)-\omega([y,\ell],x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g([x,y]_{\mathfrak{p}},J\ell)+\omega([x,\ell],y)+\omega(x,[y,\ell])=0

(3) When

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega([x,y]_{\mathfrak{p}},J\ell)+\omega([x,J\ell],y)-\omega([y,J\ell],x)

\displaystyle-g([x,y]_{\mathfrak{p}},\ell)=-g([x,y]_{\mathfrak{l}},\ell)=B([x,y],\ell)=B([\ell,x],y)=\omega(x,y).

(4) When

We will now show that the metric is not BAS by proving that the homogeneous space

is not naturally reductive. To this end, we first characterize the connected component of the full group
\rm G

Lemma 6.7

The group
\rm G
is locally isomorphic to
\rm{SU}(3)\times\rm A\times{\rm S}^{1}
, where
\rm A
is a one-dimensional torus acting on

Proof.

We fix an isomorphism
\rho:\rm A\to\rm L
and we let

act on

by means of right translations by corresponding elements of
\rm L
. This action is holomorphically isometric because the complex structure

and the metric

are both
\rm{ad}(\mathfrak{l})
-invariant. Therefore the compact group
\rm G
contains a subgroup
\rm U
locally isomorphic to
\rm{SU}(3)\times A\times{\rm S}^{1}

is holomorphic and by Blanchard theorem (see
[
2

\rm G
preserves the fibers of the fibration

and descends to a holomorphic transformation of

\rm{SU}(3)
. If

\rm G
is locally isomorphic to
\rm{SU}(3)\cdot N
. The compact group

We now prove that

Now suppose we have a reductive decomposition

When

But then equation (

When

Case 1.

By the work of

\eta_{1}=\cdots=\eta_{n-1}=0,\ \ \eta_{n}=\lambda>0,\ \ \ T^{j}_{in}=a_{i}\delta_{ij},\ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j\leq n,

where

are global constants on

Therefore
\theta^{b}

\displaystyle(\nabla^{b}R^{b})_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(R^{b}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}})-\sum_{r}\big(R^{b}_{r\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}\theta^{b}_{ir}-R^{b}_{i\bar{r}k\bar{\ell}}\theta^{b}_{rj}+R^{b}_{i\bar{j}r\bar{\ell}}\theta^{b}_{kr}-R^{b}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{r}}\theta^{b}_{r\ell}\big)

Case 2.

Assume that

\displaystyle\Theta^{b}_{11}=\Theta^{b}_{22}=d\alpha=x(\varphi_{1\bar{1}}+\varphi_{2\bar{2}})+\sqrt{-1}\,y(\varphi_{2\bar{1}}-\varphi_{1\bar{2}})

\displaystyle\Theta^{b}_{12}=-\Theta^{b}_{21}=d\beta_{0}=-\sqrt{-1}\,y(\varphi_{1\bar{1}}+\varphi_{2\bar{2}})+(x+2)(\varphi_{2\bar{1}}-\varphi_{1\bar{2}})

\nabla^{b}R^{b}=0
if and only if
\nabla^{b}R=0
. For any
1\leq a,b,c,d\leq n
, since all
R_{a\bar{b}c\bar{d}}
are constants and
\theta^{b}
is diagonal, we have

\displaystyle(\nabla^{b}R)_{a\bar{b}c\bar{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(R_{a\bar{b}c\bar{d}})-\sum_{s}\big(R_{s\bar{b}c\bar{d}}\theta^{b}_{as}-R_{a\bar{s}c\bar{d}}\theta^{b}_{sb}+R_{a\bar{b}s\bar{d}}\theta^{b}_{cs}-R_{a\bar{b}c\bar{s}}\theta^{b}_{sd}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-R_{a\bar{b}c\bar{d}}\,\big(\theta^{b}_{aa}-\theta^{b}_{bb}+\theta^{b}_{cc}-\theta^{b}_{dd}\big)

In the last line, the first factor
R_{a\bar{b}c\bar{d}}
is non-trivial only if
(abcd)=(ii\alpha\beta)
or
(ijji)
, but in both of these cases the second factor vanishes by (
6.8
). Thus
\nabla^{b}R=0
, hence
\nabla^{b}R^{b}=0
and
g
is BAS. This completes the proof of Proposition
1.8
.

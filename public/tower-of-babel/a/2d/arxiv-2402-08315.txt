Introduction

Monge–Ampère equations (MAEs) form a distinguished class of nonlinear second–order PDEs. They were introduces by G. Monge in 1784 in his pioneering study of optimal transportation problem and continued by A.M. Ampère in 1820.

The classical MAE has the form

\det\Hess u(x)=f(x,u(x)))\,,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,

where
\Hess u(x)=D^{2}u
is the Hessian and
f(,x,u)
is a given function.

Numerous applications in differential geometry, meteorology, cosmology, hydrodynamics,
economics, optimal mass transportation problem, etc., lead to consideration of a more general class of MAEs, given by

(1)

\det[\Hess u-A(x,u,Du)]=f(x,u,Du),

where
A
is a symmetric matrix.

Monge–Ampère equations are intensively studied
[
32
,
7
,
35
]
. Many deep results about existence and unicity of solutions are obtain in the case when
\Hess u(x)
is positively defined, and as such it may be considered as a Riemannian metric (the so called Hessian metric), see
[
26
,
29
]
.

A short history of MAEs and their complex and quaternionic versions can be found in the notes
[
34
]
of M. Verbitsky.

V.V. Lychagin
[
23
,
24
]
and T. Morimoto
[
27
]
proposed a general construction of a wide class of MAEs in terms of contact and symplectic geometry.
Let
(N,D)
be a
2n+1
dimensional manifold with contact structure
D
, i.e., a codimension–one distribution locally defined by 1–form
\theta
with
d\theta^{n}\wedge\theta\neq 0
. Then any
n
–form

\Omega
defines a MAE
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
. A solution of the equation is a Legendrian submanifold
L\subset N
,
\theta|_{L}=0
, which annihilates
\Omega
.
By Darboux theorem,
(N,\theta)
is locally identified with the space
J^{1}F
of 1-jets of functions on an
n
–dimensional manifold
F
. Then, in terms standard local coordinates
x^{i},p_{j},u
, the MAE reduces to the equation (
1
) on a function
u(x)
. More precisely,
1-jet
j^{1}u(x)\subset N=J^{1}F
is a Legendrian submanifold and it is a solution of MAE if the function
u(x)
satisfies the equation (
1
).

If the
n
–form
\Omega
does not depend of the coordinate
u
, it can be considered as a
n
–form on the symplectic manifold
T^{*}F
and then solutions of the equation
E_{\Omega}\subset T^{*}F
are Lagrangian submanifolds of the symplectic manifold
T^{*}F
which annihilate
\Omega
.

Starting from the paper by G. Monge, the most important application of MAEs remains the application to the optimal transportation problem and related problems.

New geometric approach to this subject had been developed in a series of papers
[
16
,
36
,
37
,
17
,
11
]
by Y.-H. Kim, R. McCann, M. Warren, and Harvey-Lawson. They established a closed relationship between the classical Monge–Kantorovich mass transportation
problem and para–Kähler geometry. More precisely, they proved that under some assumptions the solution of a Monge–Kantorovich problem reduces to the construction of a special Lagrangian submanifold
L
in some
2n
–dimensional para–Kähler manifold
(M,g,\omega)
with para–holomorphic
n
–form
\Phi
, that is an
n
–dimensional real Lagrangian

(\omega|_{L}=0)
submanifold which annihilate the real
n
–form
\Omega=\mathrm{Im}\Phi
.

In this paper we develop an approach for describing invariant Monge–Ampère equations in the sense of Lychagin–Morimoto on homogeneous contact manifolds
N
of semisimple Lie group
G
. More precisely, we consider manifolds
N=G/L
that are contactifications of homogeneous para–Kähler manifolds
M=G/K
, described by
[
2
,
1
]
. The method is applied to the classification of all invariant Monge–Ampère equations on the 11–dimensional contact manifold

N=G_{2}/\SL(2,\mathbb{R})
of the exceptional non–compact Lie group
\mathsf{G}_{2}
, associated to the 10–dimensional para–Kähler real flag manifold
\mathsf{G}_{2}/\GL(2,\mathbb{R})
.

Note that invariant second–order PDEs with
\mathsf{G}_{2}
symmetries had been studied by Yamaguchi
[
15
]
and a description of invariant PDEs with
\mathsf{G}_{2}

symmetry, different from MAE was obtained in the remarkable paper
[
31
]
by Dennis The. Classifications of different classes of MAEs were given in the papers

[
19
,
25
,
20
,
18
,
14
]

and the problem of equivalence and the conditions of linearizability of MAE was treated in

[
21
,
22
]
.

Structure of the paper

In Section
1
we remind that the notion of a bi–Lagrangian manifold is equivalent to the notion of a para–Kähler manifold; then we pass to the
G
–homogeneous case and recall some useful results: an adjoint orbit
\mathrm{Ad}_{G}(Z)
possesses a bi–Lagrangian structure if and only if the element
Z
is splittable, and all the bi–Lagrangian structures on
\mathrm{Ad}_{G}(Z)
are in one–to–one correspondence with the fundamental
\mathbb{Z}
–gradations of
\mathfrak{g}=\Lie(G)
. Next, we introduce the contactification of a homogeneous para–Kähler manifold and, finally, by employing the generalized Gauss decomposition, we prove a theorem that allows to locally identify a para-Kähler manifold with the cotangent bundle to a suitable flag manifold.

In Section
2
we recall the notion of a Monge-Ampère equation in the sense of Lychagin–Morimoto and we extend it to a general contact manifold, thus obtaining the general Monge–Ampère equation associated to an
n
–form (general MAE). The local expression of general MAEs in Darboux coordinates is obtained later, together with the natural interpretation of the fibers of a MAE as hyperplane sections of a Lagrangian Grassmannian.

In Section
3
we work out some examples of fundamental gradations: in particular, we show all fundamental gradations of the algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(V)
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}=\Lie(\mathsf{G}_{2})
.

In Section
4
we focus on the 10–dimensional
\mathsf{G}_{2}
–homogeneous manifold
M=\mathsf{G}_{2}/\mathsf{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and we construct a basis of the space of invariant effective 5–forms on the contactification
N
of
M
. This allows to provide a list of all
\mathsf{G}_{2}
–invariant MAEs on
N
, that are second–order (nonlinear) PDEs in 5 independent variables; we finally establish which of them are contact–equivalent.

1.
Homogeneous para–Kähler manifolds and their contactification

1.1.
Bi–Lagrangian and para-Kähler structures

Below we introduce two equivalent categories made of the objects we will be working with.

Definition 1.1
.

An
almost para–complex structure
on a
2n
–dimensional manifold
M
is a decomposition

(2)

TM=T^{+}M\oplus T^{-}M

of the tangent bundle
TM
into the direct sum of two
n
–dimensional distributions
T^{\pm}M
or, equivalently, a field
I\in\Gamma(\mathrm{End\;}(TM))
of endomorphisms, such that
I|_{T^{\pm}M}=\pm\id_{T^{\pm}M}
. An almost para–complex structure is called a
para–complex structure
if the distributions
T^{\pm}M
are
integrable, i.e., it holds

(3)

[\Gamma(T^{\pm}M),\Gamma(T^{\pm}M)]\subset\Gamma(T^{\pm}M)\,.

Definition 1.2
.

The decomposition (
2
) on a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
is called
almost bi–Lagrangian
if
\omega|_{T^{\pm}_{x}M}
vanishes identically at any point
x\in M
.
If, moreover, the distributions
T^{\pm}M
are integrable, it is called a
bi–Lagrangian structure
.

The integrable submanifolds (of maximal dimension) of the distributions
T^{\pm}M
of a bi–Lagrangian structure on a
2n
–dimensional symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
are Lagrangian submanifolds of
M
, i.e., they are
n
–dimensional and
\omega
vanishes identically on them; see also
[
6
]
.

Definition 1.3
.

An
almost para–Hermitian manifold
is a pseudo–Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
equipped with an almost para–complex structure
I
, such that the distributions
T^{\pm}M

are (absolutely) isotropic, i.e.,
g|_{T_{x}^{\pm}M}
vanishes indentically at any point
x\in M
.

Given an almost para–Hermitian manifold
(M,g,I)
, the (skew–symmetric) two–form

(4)

\omega:=g(\,\cdot\,,I(\,\cdot\,))\,.

is called the
Kähler form
.

Definition 1.4
.

An almost para–Hermitian manifold
(M,g,I)
, such that
I
is a para–complex structure, is called a
para–Hermitian manifold
. A para–Hermitian manifold
(M,g,I)
is called a
para–Kähler manifold
if the para–complex structure
I
is parallel with respect to the Levi–Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
of
g
, i.e.,
\nabla^{g}I=0
.

The next well–known results show that the notion of a bi–Lagrangian manifold
(M,\omega,I)
is equivalent to the notion of a para-Kähler manifold
(M,g,I)
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
(M,g,I)
be a para–Hermitian manifold: then
\nabla^{g}I=0
if and only if the Kähler form
\omega
given by (
4
) is closed, i.e.,
d\omega=0
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the identities

(5)

\displaystyle d\omega(v,w,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\nabla^{g}_{w}(I)(v),u)+g(\nabla^{g}_{u}(I)(w),v)+g(\nabla^{g}_{v}(I)(u),w)\,,

(6)

\displaystyle d\omega(v,w,u)+d\omega(v,I(w),I(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2g(\nabla^{g}_{v}(I)(w),u)\,,

hold for all commuting vector fields
v,w,u,I(w),I(u)
on
M
, see
[
4
, Proposition 4.16]
.

In view of such a commutativity and of (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle d\omega(v,w,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v(\omega(w,u))+w(\omega(u,v))+u(\omega(v,w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v(g(w,I(u)))+w(g(u,I(v)))+u(g(v,I(w)))\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\nabla^{g}_{v}(w),I(u))+g(w,\nabla^{g}_{v}(I(u)))+\ldots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\nabla^{g}_{v}(w),I(u))+g(w,\nabla^{g}_{v}(I)(u))+g(w,I(\nabla^{g}_{v}(u)))+\ldots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(w,\nabla^{g}_{v}(I)(u))+\omega(\nabla^{g}_{v}(w),u)+\omega(w,\nabla^{g}_{v}(u))+\ldots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\nabla^{g}_{w}(I)(v),u)+g(\nabla^{g}_{u}(I)(w),v)+g(\nabla^{g}_{v}(I)(u),w)\,,

where the dots denote cyclic permutations of
(v,w,u)
, that is formula (
5
).

Let us observe now that, in view of the Leibniz rule for the covariant derivative and the fundamental identity
\nabla^{g}(g)=0
, it holds

(7)

\displaystyle 2g(\nabla^{g}_{v}(I)(w),u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2g(\nabla^{g}_{v}(I(w)),u)-2g(I(\nabla^{g}_{v}(w)),u)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2g(\nabla^{g}_{v}(I(w)),u)+2g(\nabla^{g}_{v}(w),I(u))\,.

The Koszul formula, applied to both the addends of (
7
), yields

(8)

\displaystyle 2g(\nabla^{g}_{v}(I(w)),u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v(g(I(w),u))+I(w)(g(v,u))-u(g(v,I(w)))=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v(\omega(u,w))+I(w)(\omega(v,I(u)))+u(\omega(w,v)))

and

(9)

\displaystyle 2g(\nabla^{g}_{v}(w),I(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v(g(w,I(u)))+w(g(v,I(u)))-I(u)(g(v,w))=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v(\omega(w,u))+w(\omega(v,u))+I(u)(\omega(I(w),v)))\,,

respectively. By taking the sum of (
8
) and (
9
) we obtain

(10)

\displaystyle 2g(\nabla^{g}_{v}(I)(w),u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v(\omega(u,w))+I(w)(\omega(v,I(u)))+u(\omega(w,v)))+v(\omega(w,u))+w(\omega(v,u))+I(u)(\omega(I(w),v)))=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I(w)(\omega(v,I(u)))+u(\omega(w,v)))+w(\omega(v,u))+I(u)(\omega(I(w),v)))\,.

It is then easy to see that

\displaystyle d\omega(v,w,u)+d\omega(v,I(w),I(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v(\omega(w,u))+w(\omega(u,v))+u(\omega(v,w)))+

\displaystyle+v(\omega(I(w),I(u)))+I(w)(\omega(I(u),v))+I(u)(\omega(v,I(w))))=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v(\omega(w,u))+w(\omega(u,v))+u(\omega(v,w))+

\displaystyle-v(\omega(w,u))+I(w)(\omega(I(u),v))+I(u)(\omega(v,I(w))))\,,

after switching the arguments in all the instances of
\omega
, coincides with (
10
): this proves (
6
).

\square

Proposition 1.1
.

If
(M,g,I)
is a para–Kähler manifold, then
(M,\omega,I)
is a bi–Lagrangian manifold with
\omega
given by (
4
); conversely, if

(M,\omega,I)
is a bi–Lagrangian manifold, then
(M,g,I)
is a para–Kähler manifold, with
g
given by

(11)

g:=-\omega\circ I\,.

Proof.

If a para–Kähler manifold
(M,g,I)
is given, then the form
\omega

defined by (
4
) is symplectic thanks to Lemma
1.1
; it only remains to show that
T^{\pm}M
is Lagrangian, but this is an immediate consequence of
T^{\pm}M
being
g
–isotropic:

(12)

v,w\in T^{\pm}M\Rightarrow\omega(v,w)=g(v,I(w))=g(v,\pm w)=0\,.

If a bi–Lagrangian manifold on
(M,\omega,I)
is given, then one has to show that (
11
) defines indeed a pseudo–Riemanian metric, such that
T^{\pm}M
are
g
–isotropic and
I
is
g
–parallel.

The first claim is easy and formally mirrors (
12
):

\displaystyle v,w\in T^{\pm}M

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle g(v,w)=\omega(I(v),I(w))=-\omega(\pm v,\pm w)=-\omega(v,w)=0\,,

\displaystyle v\in T^{\pm}M\,,w\in T^{\mp}M

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle g(v,w)=-\omega(\pm v,\mp w)=\omega(\mp v,\pm w)=-\omega(\pm w,\mp v)=g(w,v)\,,

because
T^{\pm}M
is Lagrangian.

The second claim follows again from Lemma
1.1
.

\square

1.2.
Classification of para-Kähler homogeneous manifolds of a semisimple Lie group

In what follows
G
will denote a real Lie group and
Z
an element of its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}:=\Lie(G)
; the Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
will be denoted by the symbol
B
.

Definition 1.5
.

An element
Z\in\mathfrak{g}
is called
semisimple
if such is its adjoint operator
\mathrm{ad}_{Z}
.
A semisimple element
Z\in\mathfrak{g}
is called
splittable
if
\mathrm{ad}_{Z}
has real eigenvalues; it is called
closed
, if it generates a closed 1–parameter subgroup
\{\exp tZ\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}
of
G
.

It is a classical result by Kirillov, Kostant and Souriou that, up to a central extension, any homogeneous symplectic manifold
M=G/H
is a coadjoint orbit in the dual
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
. If
G
is semisimple, then the Killing form
B
is not degenerate and therefore the coadjoint orbit
\mathrm{Ad}_{G}\xi\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
can be identified with the adjoint orbit

\mathrm{Ad}_{G}Z\subset\mathfrak{g}
, with
Z
given by
Z=B^{-1}\circ\xi
.

Theorem 1.1

(Kirillov–Kostant–Souriou)
.

Up to a covering, any homogeneous symplectic manifold
M=(G/H,\omega)
of a semisimple Lie group
G
is isomorphic to an adjoint orbit
\mathrm{Ad}_{G}Z\subset\mathfrak{g}
equipped with the
G
–invariant symplectic form
\omega
given by

(13)

\omega_{x}(X,Y):=B(x,[\mathrm{Ad}_{x^{-1}\ast}(X),\mathrm{Ad}_{x^{-1}\ast}(Y)])\,,

for any
x\in M\subset\mathfrak{g}
and any
X,Y\in T_{x}M\subset T_{x}G
, where
\mathrm{Ad}_{x^{-1}\ast}:T_{x}G\to T_{e}G=\mathfrak{g}
is the tangent map of
\mathrm{Ad}_{x^{-1}}
at the point
x
.

The Kirillov–Konstant–Souriou form (
13
) is one of the many non–equivalent
G
–invariant symplectic forms on an adjoint orbit. Let
M=\mathrm{Ad}_{G}Z=G/H
, where
\mathfrak{h}=\Lie(H)=C_{\mathfrak{g}}(Z)
is the centralizer of
Z\in\mathfrak{g}
in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
, and fix a complementary subspace
\mathfrak{m}
of
C_{\mathfrak{g}}(Z)
in
\mathfrak{g}
:

(14)

\mathfrak{g}=C_{\mathfrak{g}}(Z)+\mathfrak{m}\,.

Then the tangent space
T_{Z}M
identifies with
\mathfrak{m}
.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
G
be a semisimple Lie group and let
\mathfrak{m}
be a
B
–orthogonal complement of
C_{\mathfrak{g}}(Z)
: then the unique
G
–invariant symplectic structure
\omega
on
M
, such that

(15)

\omega_{Z}(X,Y)=B(Z,[X,Y])\,,\quad\forall X,Y\in T_{Z}M\,,

is called
the symplectic structure on
M=\mathrm{Ad}_{G}Z
associated with
Z\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Hou-Deng-Kaneyuki-Nishiyama
[
12
,
2
]
)
.

Let
G
be a semisimple real Lie group and
(M=Ad_{G}Z,\omega)
an adjoint orbit of an element
Z\in g=\Lie(G)
, equipped with the invariant symplectic structure
\omega
associated with
Z
. Then the manifold
M
admits a G–invariant integrable bi–Lagrangian (or, equivalently, para–Kähler) structure if and only if
Z
is a splittable element.

Indeed, if
Z
is splittable, then all the eigenvalues of the operator
\mathrm{ad}_{Z}
are real, so that

(16)

\mathfrak{g}=\sum_{j\in\Eigen}\mathfrak{g}_{j}\,,

where
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
denotes the eigenspace of
\mathrm{ad}_{Z}
that corresponds to the eigenvalue
j
and
\Eigen:=\{j\in\mathbb{R}\mid\det(\mathrm{ad}_{Z}-j\id_{\mathfrak{g}})=0\}
is the set of real eigenvalues of
\mathrm{ad}_{Z}
. In turn, this allows to refine the
B
–orthogonal reductive decomposition (
14
) as follows:

(17)

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}^{-}+\mathfrak{g}^{0}+\mathfrak{n}^{+}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}+\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{n}^{+}\,,

where
\mathfrak{g}^{0}=\mathfrak{h}=C_{\mathfrak{g}}(Z)
, and

(18)

\mathfrak{g}^{\pm}=\mathfrak{n}^{\pm}:=\sum_{j\in\Eigen\cap\mathbb{R}^{\pm}}\mathfrak{g}_{j}\,.

The
\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}
–invariant decomposition (
17
) extends to a
G
–invariant decomposition
TM=T^{-}M+T^{+}M
of
TM
, which turns out to be bi–Lagrangian, because
\mathfrak{n}^{\pm}
is a sub–algebra (which ensures integrability) and from the definition (
15
) of
\omega_{Z}
it follows that the symplectic form
\omega_{Z}
vanishes on
\mathfrak{n}^{\pm}
.

The
\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}
–invariant decomposition (
17
) is called
a generalized Gauss decomposition
, see Definition
1.9
below.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
N^{\pm}
be the nilpotent subgroup generated by the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}^{\pm}
: the integral submanifold of the distribution
T^{\pm}M
passing through the point
x=gZ\in M
is given by
gN^{\pm}Z
.

The classification of homogeneous bi–Lagrangian manifolds of a (complex or real) semisimple Lie group
G
can be reduced to the description of the fundamental gradations of the corresponding Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

A
\mathbb{Z}
–gradation of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a decomposition

(19)

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}^{-k}+\ldots+\mathfrak{g}^{-1}+\mathfrak{g}^{0}+\mathfrak{g}^{l}+\ldots+\mathfrak{g}^{k}\,,

satisfying
[\mathfrak{g}^{i},\mathfrak{g}^{j}]\subset\mathfrak{g}^{i+j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 1.7
.

A
\mathbb{Z}
–gradation (
19
) is called
fundamental
if the subalgebra

(20)

\mathfrak{g}^{-}:=\mathfrak{g}^{-k}+\ldots+\mathfrak{g}^{-1}

is generated by
\mathfrak{g}^{-1}
.

Theorem
1.3
below follows from the remark that the operator
D
given, for any
j
, by
D|_{\mathfrak{g}^{j}}:=j\id_{\mathfrak{g}^{j}}
is a derivation of the Lie algebra (
19
), and any derivation of a semisimple Lie algebra is inner, i.e.,
D=\mathrm{ad}_{d}
, with
d\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem 1.3

(Alekseevsky-Medori
[
1
]
)
.

Let
(M=\mathrm{Ad}_{G}(Z)=G/H,\omega)
be as in Theorem
1.2
and let
\mathfrak{h}=C_{\mathfrak{g}}(Z)=\Lie(H)
. There exists a natural one–to–one correspondence between

invariant bi-Lagrangian structures
TM=T^{+}M+T^{-}M
;

ii)

H
–invariant decompositions (called bi–isotropic) of the Lie algebra

(21)

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}+\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{n}^{+}\,,

where
\mathfrak{n}^{\pm}
given by (

) are subalgebras such that
B|_{\mathfrak{n}^{\pm}}=0
;

iii)

fundamental
H
–invariant
\mathbb{Z}
–gradations
(

) with

.

More precisely, the bi–isotropic decomposition (

), which corresponds to the fundamental gradation (

), is given by

(22)

\mathfrak{n}^{\pm}=\sum_{\pm i>0}\mathfrak{g}^{i}

and
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}^{0}
, while
the bi–Lagrangian decomposition

associated with (

) is
the natural invariant extension of the

–invariant decomposition
T_{o}M=\mathfrak{n}^{+}+\mathfrak{n}^{-}
of the tangent space of

,

, under the standard identification
T_{o}M=\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{n}^{+}+\mathfrak{n}^{-}
.

1.3.
Contactification of a homogeneous para–Kähler manifold

Let
G
be a real semisimple Lie group and
M:=\mathrm{Ad}_{G}Z=G/H
the adjoint orbit of a closed and splittable semisimple element
Z\in\mathfrak{g}
: consider the
B
-orthogonal decomposition
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{l}+{\mathbb{R}}Z
of the stability subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}=\Lie(H)
, where
\mathfrak{l}=({\mathbb{R}}Z)^{\perp}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{l}
generates a closed subgroup
L\subset G
and the homogeneous manifold
N:=G/L
has the reductive decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{l}+\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{l}+({\mathbb{R}}Z+\mathfrak{m}).

The
Ad_{L}
-invariant 1-form
\theta=B\circ Z
defines an invariant contact structure on
N
with the invariant contact form
\theta
which is an invariant extension of the 1-form
\theta=B\circ Z
.

The natural projection

(23)

\pi:N=G/L\longrightarrow M=G/H\,

is a
G
-equivariant principal bundle with structure group
{\mathbb{R}}=\{\exp tZ\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, and
H
identifies with the product
H=L\cdot\{\exp tZ\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}
; moreover, the contact form
\theta:TN\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

Definition 1.8

The

\displaystyle G\,,

Let

\displaystyle N_{F}

\displaystyle T^{+}_{o}N_{M}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}^{+}\,,

2.
Monge–Ampère equations and their generalization

In this section we review some basics fact concerning the contact geometry of Monge–Ampère equations. More details can be found in
[
3
,
20
]
.

2.1.
Monge–Ampère equations via
n
–forms on jet spaces

With any differential form
\Omega
on the
(2n+1)
–dimensional jet space

(33)

J^{1}(n,1):=\{[f]_{x}^{1}\mid f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\}

one can associate its so–called
horizontalization
: such an operation corresponds to the projection of
\Lambda^{\bullet}(J^{1})
onto the quotient

(34)

\overline{\Lambda}^{\bullet}(J^{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def.}}}{{=}}\frac{\Lambda^{\bullet}(J^{1})}{\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}}\,,

where
\Lambda^{\bullet}(J^{1})
is the algebra of differential forms and
{\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}}
is the differential ideal generated by the contact form

(35)

du-\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}dx^{i}\,.

We denote by
\overline{\Omega}
the image of
\Omega\in\Lambda^{\bullet}(J^{1})
in
\overline{\Lambda}^{\bullet}(J^{1})
.

It is not hard to see that

(36)

\overline{\Lambda}^{n}(J^{1})=C^{\infty}(J^{1})\otimes_{C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Lambda^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,,

that is, for any
\Omega\in\Lambda^{n}(J^{1})
there exists a unique function
F_{\Omega}\in C^{\infty}(J^{1})
, such that
\overline{\Omega}=F_{\Omega}\cdot\dd x^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\dd x^{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

For any
n
–form
\Omega
on
J^{1}
, we call
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}:=\{F_{\Omega}=0\}
the
Monge–Ampère equation (MAE)
associated with
\Omega
(see Section
2.2
below). A function
f:\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, such that
[f]_{x}^{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}

\forall x\in\mathcal{U}
, is a
solution
of
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
, whereas each point
[f]_{x}^{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
is called a
formal solution
of
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}

2.2.
Monge–Ampère equations on contact and symplectic manifolds

Given a contact manifold
(N,\mathcal{C})
, that is a
(2n+1)
–dimensional manifold equipped with a completely non–integrable
2n
–dimensional distribution
\mathcal{C}
, formula
\omega:=d\theta|_{\mathcal{C}}
defines a conformal symplectic structure on
\mathcal{C}
, where
\theta
is any
1
–form, such that
\ker(\theta)=\mathcal{C}
. In turn, this conformal symplectic structure allows to introduce
the
Lagrangian Grassmannian
of
(\mathcal{C}_{p},\omega_{p})
, that is the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C}_{p})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def.}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\{L_{p}\mid L_{p}\text{ is a Lagrangian planes of }\mathcal{C}_{p}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{L_{p}\in\Gr(n,\mathcal{C}_{p})\mid\omega_{p}|_{L_{p}}\equiv 0\}\,

of all Lagrangian planes at a given point
p\in N
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
prolongation
of a contact manifold
(N,\mathcal{C})
is the fiber bundle
\pi:N^{(1)}\longrightarrow N
, where

N^{(1)}:=\bigcup_{p\in N}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C}_{p})\,,

and
\pi
is the natural projection.

Points
p^{1}
of
N^{(1)}
can be then understood as Lagrangian planes
L_{p^{1}}
of
(\mathcal{C}_{p},\omega_{p})
, that is, there is a natural correspondence

p^{1}\in N^{(1)}\Longleftrightarrow L_{p^{1}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C}_{p})\,,\quad p=\pi(p^{1})\,.

According to V. Lychagin
[
20
]
and T. Morimoto
[
27
]
, we generalize Definition
2.1
as follows. Given a contact manifold
(N,\mathcal{C})
, with
\mathcal{C}=\ker(\theta)
, let
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}
denote the differential ideal generated by
\theta
; recall also that
L_{{p}^{1}}\subset T_{\pi({p}^{1})}N
is the
Lagrangian plane associated with
{p}^{1}\in N^{(1)}
.

Definition 2.3
.

The hypersurface
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
of
N^{(1)}
given by

(37)

\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}:=\{{p}^{1}\in N^{(1)}\mid\Omega|_{L_{{p}^{1}}}\equiv 0\}\,,

where
\Omega\in\Lambda^{n}(N)
, is called a
(general) Monge–Ampère equation (MAE)
.

MAEs can be defined also on symplectic manifolds rather than on contact ones: it is enough to replace the above contact manifold
N
with a symplectic manifold
M
: details of such a construction are omitted it, see, e.g.,
[
9
,
30
]
.

Remark 2.1
.

Since, in the definition (
37
)
of a general MAE, the restriction
\Omega|_{L_{{p}^{1}}}
is identically zero if the form
\Omega\in\Lambda^{n}(N)
belongs to the contact ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}
, instead of the form
\Omega
one can use the equivalence class

(38)

[\Omega]\in\frac{\Lambda^{n}(N)}{\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}}\,.

Elements of the quotient

(39)

\frac{\Lambda^{\bullet}(N)}{\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}}

are called
effective
forms; accordingly, some speak of MAE associated with an
effective
differential form.

2.3.
MAEs in Darboux coordinates

If
F
is an
n
–dimensional smooth manifold, equipped with coordinates
\{x^{1},\ldots,x^{n}\}
, then the standard symplectic form
\omega_{\mathrm{std}}
on
T^{*}F
reads

(40)

\omega_{\mathrm{std}}=dx^{i}\wedge du_{i}\,,

where
u_{i}
is the momentum conjugate with
x^{i}
; together with the coordinate
u
that corresponds to the value of a function, the
x^{i}
’s and the moments
u_{i}
’s form a Darboux coordinate system of the contact manifold

(41)

J^{1}(F,\mathbb{R})=T^{*}F\times\mathbb{R}\,.

In particular, the contact form
\theta
reads

(42)

\theta=du-u_{i}dx^{i}\,.

The second–order jet space
J^{2}(F,\mathbb{R})
is an affine bundle over
J^{1}(F,\mathbb{R})
, its fiber at
p^{1}\in J^{1}(F,\mathbb{R})
being modeled by the symmetric power
S^{2}(T_{p}^{*}F)
, where
p=\pi(p^{1})
: this allows to extend the Darboux coordinate system by adding the coordinates
u_{ij}
that correspond to second–order derivatives. A point
p^{2}
of the fiber of
J^{2}(F,\mathbb{R})
over
p^{1}\in J^{1}(F,\mathbb{R})
has coordinates
u_{ij}
, if

(43)

u_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}\in S^{2}(T_{p}^{*}F)

is the symmetric form that corresponds to
p^{2}
; the same point can be regarded as a Lagrangian plane of
\mathcal{C}_{p^{1}}
by means of the embedding

\displaystyle S^{2}(T_{p}^{*}F)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\LL(\mathcal{C}_{p^{1}})\,,

(44)

\displaystyle p^{2}=u_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle L_{p^{2}}=\mathrm{span}\left(D_{x^{i}}+u_{ij}\partial_{u_{j}}\mid i=1,\ldots,n\right)\,,

where
D_{x^{i}}=\partial_{x^{i}}+u_{i}\partial_{u}
are the total derivatives.

A coordinate expression of the general MAE
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
defined by an
n
–form
\Omega
on
J^{2}(F,\mathbb{R})
can be then obtained by employing the extended Darboux coordinates introduced above; to this end we shall need the Plücker embedding

\displaystyle\LL(\mathcal{C}_{p^{1}})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Lambda^{n}\mathcal{C}_{p^{1}})\,,

(45)

\displaystyle L=\mathrm{span}\left(\ell_{1}\ldots,\ell_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol\;}(L):=[\ell_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\ell_{n}]\,,

as well as a coordinate expression

(46)

\Omega=Adx^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n}+B_{i}^{j}dx^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{dx^{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n}\wedge du_{j}+\cdots+Cdu_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge du_{n}

of
\Omega
. By combining (
44
) with (
45
), we see that the point
p^{1}
is mapped to the projective class of

(47)

D_{x^{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge D_{x^{n}}+u_{ij}D_{x^{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{D_{x^{i}}}\wedge\cdots\wedge D_{x^{n}}\wedge\partial_{u_{j}}+\cdots+\det(u_{ij})\partial_{u_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\partial_{u_{n}}\,.

Then, by applying the formula (
37
) that defines a MAE, we find out that

(48)

\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}=\{F_{\Omega}(u_{ij})=0\}\,,

where
F_{\Omega}(u_{ij})
is a linear combination of the minors of the matrix
(u_{ij})
, unambiguously defined by

(49)

\Omega|_{L_{p^{2}}}=F_{\Omega}(u_{ij})dx^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n}\,.

Example 2.1
.

If all coefficients in (
46
) are zero, except
C
, then
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
is the Monge–Ampère equation given by
F_{\Omega}(u_{ij})=C\det(u_{ij})
, that is
\det(u_{ij})=0
.

We refer the reader to
[
10
,
8
]
and references therein for more details.

2.4.
Fibers of MAE as hyperplane sections

At each point
p
of the
(2n+1)
–dimensional contact manifold
(N,\mathcal{C})
, let us consider the Plücker embedding (
45
). Then, the fiber

(50)

(\mathcal{E}_{\Omega})_{p}:=\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}\cap N^{(1)}_{p}=\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}\cap\LL(\mathcal{C}_{p})

3.

3.1.
Fundamental gradations of a (complex or real) semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

Let

(56)

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}+\sum_{\alpha\in R}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}

be a root space decomposition of a complex semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}
. We fix a system of simple roots
\Pi=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}\}\subset R
, that is a basis of
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, such that any root
\alpha\in R
has integer coefficients with respect to
\Pi
of the same sign (non–negative or non–positive).

Any disjoint decomposition

(57)

\Pi=\Pi^{0}\cup\Pi^{1}

of
\Pi
defines a fundamental gradation of
\mathfrak{g}
as follows. First, define the function
d:R\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
by

\displaystyle d|_{\Pi^{0}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle 0\,,

\displaystyle d|_{\Pi^{1}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle 1\,,

\displaystyle d(\alpha)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum k_{i}d(\alpha_{i})\,,\quad\forall\alpha=\sum k_{i}\alpha_{i}\in R\,.

Then the fundamental gradation is given by

(58)

\mathfrak{g}^{0}=\mathfrak{a}+\sum_{\alpha\in R_{0}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\,,\quad R_{0}:=\{\alpha\in R\mid d(\alpha)=0\}\,,

and

(59)

\mathfrak{g}^{i}=\sum_{\alpha,\,d(\alpha)=i}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\,,\quad\forall i\neq 0\,.

Notice that any fundamental gradation of
\mathfrak{g}
is conjugated to a unique gradation of such a form.

Any real semisimple Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
is a real form of a complex semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, that is
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
is the fixed point set of some antilinear involution
\sigma
of
\mathfrak{g}
. We can always assume that
\sigma
preserves a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathfrak{g}
and induces an automorphism of the root system
R
. A root

is called

(or
black
) if
\sigma(\alpha)=-\alpha
. It is always possible to choose a system of simple roots
\Pi=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}\}
such that, for any non–compact root
\alpha_{i}\in\Pi
, the corresponding root
\sigma(\alpha_{i})
is a sum of one non–compact root
a_{j}\in\Pi
and a linear combination of compact roots from
\Pi
. The roots
\alpha_{i}
and
\alpha_{j}
are called

.

Proposition 3.1

(Alekseevsky–Medori
[
1
]

.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra,
\sigma:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
an antilinear involution, and
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
the corresponding real form. The gradation of
\mathfrak{g}
, associated with a decomposition
\Pi=\Pi^{0}\cup\Pi^{1}
, defines a gradation
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}=\Sigma(\mathfrak{g}^{i})^{\sigma}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
if and only if
\Pi^{1}
consists of non–compact roots and any two equivalent roots are either both in
\Pi^{0}
or both in
\Pi^{1}
.

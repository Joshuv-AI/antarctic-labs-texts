The sub-Riemannian X-ray Transform on H-type groups: Fourier-Slice Theorems and Injectivity sets
Steven Flynn

Abstract
. We continue the development of X-ray tomography in sub-Riemannian geometry. Using the Fourier Transform adapted to the group structure, we generalize the Fourier Slice Theorem to the class of H-type groups. The Fourier Slice Theorem expresses the X-ray transform as the composition of a Fourier restriction and multiplication operator. We compute the spectral resolution for the resulting operator-valued multiplier, which we use to show that an integrable function on an H-type group is determined by its integrals over sub-Riemannian geodesics. We also relate the support of the X-ray transform I ​ f If to the support of f f in frequency space. These results given explicit answers to the injectivity question in a class of geometries with an abundance of conjugate points.

\lambda\cdot\mu=2k|\lambda|^{3}
and

. Therefore injectivity of

follows once we show that, for almost all

, there exists
\nu\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}
and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
with
\lambda\cdot\mu\in|\lambda|^{3}{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

is injective.

Remark 2.5
.

The condition
\mu\cdot\lambda=2k|\lambda|^{3}
can be understood in the following way: When
G=\mathbb{H}_{n}={\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
is the Heisenberg group, the momenta
\mu,\lambda\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}\cong{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
are real valued. Then
|\lambda|^{-1}=R
is the radius of the circle on the horizontal plane that a sub-Riemannian geodesic
\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}
with charge
\lambda\neq 0
projects to. The condition
\mu\cdot\lambda=2k|\lambda|^{3}
becomes

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{2\pi k}{\pi R^{2}}.

Remark 2.6
.

For and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
the geodesic
\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}(s)
starting at
s=0
reaches its first conjugate point at
s=2\pi R
,
R=|\lambda|^{-1}
, and the two points in conjugacy differ by a group translation of
(0,\pi R^{2}\widehat{\lambda})\in G
with
\widehat{\lambda}=|\lambda|^{-1}\lambda
. Indeed

\displaystyle\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}(s+2\pi R)=(0,\pi R^{2}\widehat{\lambda})\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}(s)

On the other hand, the representation
\pi_{\mu}
which is a map from

to unitary operators on Bargmann-Fock space
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
defined in Section
5.2
, while satisfying the left-invariance

also satisfies

\displaystyle\pi_{\mu}(0,\pi R^{2}\widehat{\lambda})=\pi_{\mu}(0,0)=I

whenever

, and therefore

\displaystyle\pi_{\mu}((x,u)\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}(s+2\pi R\widehat{\lambda}))=\pi_{\mu}((x,u)\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}(s)).

It is in this sense, that the representation
\pi_{\mu}
does not “see” the conjugate points of geodesics

of charge

whenever

In light of the above remark, we make the following definition:

Definition 2.7
.

Let

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}=\{(\lambda,\mu)\in(\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\})\times(\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}):\lambda\cdot\mu=2k|\lambda|^{3},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}

be the relation of all compatible pairs of charges
\lambda
and frequency variables

. Furthermore let

be the subset of

for which

The significance of this relation is the following theorem:

Theorem 2.8
.

Let

is an H-type group and

Remark 2.9
.

By the above discussion, the data of integrals a function

To better understand the next result, define the truncated frequency spaces:

3.
Sub-Riemannian Geometry of H-type groups

3.1.
Hamiltonian Formalism

We define the sub-Riemannian metric on
G
by declaring
X_{1},\ldots X_{2n}
orthonormal, and the length of
U_{1},\ldots U_{m}
to be infinite. This amounts to the restricting the inner product
\langle,\rangle
discussed in Section
1.1
from all of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z}
to the first strata
\mathfrak{v}
. Then any finite length smooth path in
G
must be tangent to the nonintegrable distribution
\mathcal{D}_{q}=TL_{g}(\mathfrak{v})
. We call such a path
horizontal
. The length of a horizontal path equals the length of its projection to the plane by the map

\displaystyle dx:(x,u)\mapsto x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}.

A minimizing Heisenberg geodesic is the shortest horizontal path joining two points of
G
. That any two points in
G
are connected by a horizontal path is guaranteed by Chow’s Theorem and the fact that
\mathcal{D}
satisfies the Hörmander condition (i.e.
\mathcal{D}
is bracket generating.)

The fiber-quadratic Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}G\to{\mathbb{R}}
given in canonical coordinates by

H=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(P_{X_{1}}^{2}+\cdots+P_{X_{2n}}^{2}\right)=\tfrac{1}{2}|\xi+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{\mu}x|^{2}

generates the Heisenberg geodesics. That is, any solution for Hamilton’s equations for
H
projects, via the canonical projection
T^{*}G\to G
, to a sub-Riemannian geodesic, and conversely, all Heisenberg geodesics arise in this way. To obtain geodesics parameterized by arclength, we only take solutions for which
H=1/2
. (Thus, we define the unit cotangent bundle
U^{*}G
as the set of all
(q,p)\in T^{*}G
for which
H(q,p)=1/2
).

Geodesics are best understood by their projection to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
: they are “generalized helices.”
Indeed

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{u}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}=0

so that
\lambda=p_{u}
is a constant of motion.
If we choose a basis for
\mathfrak{v}
as in (
5.1
) that puts
J_{\lambda}
into block diagonal form then
\mathfrak{v}
is a direct sum of two-planes each invariant under
J_{\lambda}
. The geodesics projected to any one of these two-planes are either all circles or lines.
If we interpret
\lambda
at the “generalized charge” of a particle, then Hamiltonian equations for
H
, viewed as a Hamiltonian on
T^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
, are just the Lorentz equations for a particle of charge
\lambda
in a constant unit strength magnetic field.

3.2.
Geodesic Flow

The unique solution to Hamilton’s equations (see the appendix) for a curve starting at the origin with initial momentum
(\nu,\lambda)\in\mathfrak{v}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{z}^{*}
is

\displaystyle s\mapsto\left(x(s),u(s),e^{sJ_{\lambda}}\nu,\lambda\right)\in G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}

with
x(s)
and
u(s)
given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle x(s)=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{sJ_{\lambda}}-1}{J_{\lambda}}{\nu},&\lambda\neq 0\\
s{\nu},&\lambda=0\end{cases},\qquad\qquad u(s)=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{|\lambda|s-\sin{(|\lambda|s)}}{2|\lambda|^{2}}\right)\frac{\lambda}{|\lambda|}|{\nu}|^{2},&\lambda\neq 0\\
0,&\lambda=0\end{cases}.

The solution written in the coordinate
(x,u,\nu,\lambda)
is exactly the Hamiltonian flow written in the left trivialization
T^{*}G\cong G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, that is

\displaystyle\varphi_{s}:G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}

Since
\lambda
is an integral of motion, this flow restricts to the
\lambda
-level sets

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda,s}:G\times\mathfrak{v}^{*}\times\{\lambda\}\to G\times\mathfrak{v}^{*}\times\{\lambda\}

The unique constant speed geodesics starting at the origin with initial momentum

is

(3.2)

\displaystyle s\mapsto(x(s),u(s))\in G.

Motivated by Appendix

, we view the geodesic flow in

; the following left translation shifts the geodesic to one centered around the
\lambda

(3.3)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}(s):=\left(J_{\lambda}^{-1}\nu,0\right)(x(s),u(s))=\left(\frac{e^{sJ_{\lambda}}}{J_{\lambda}}\nu,\;\frac{s\lambda}{2|\lambda|^{2}}|\nu|^{2}\right).

4.
Properties of the X-ray Transform

4.1.
Homogeneity

For
\varepsilon>0
the dilation map
\delta_{\epsilon}(x,u)=(\varepsilon x,\varepsilon^{2}u)
is an isomorphism of the group
G
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\delta_{\varepsilon}:\Gamma_{\varepsilon\lambda}\ni(0,k\pi|\varepsilon\lambda|^{-2}\hat{\lambda})\longmapsto(0,k\pi|\lambda|^{-2}\hat{\lambda})\in\Gamma_{\lambda},

so
\delta_{\varepsilon}:G_{\varepsilon\lambda}\to G_{\lambda}
is well-defined. Denote by
\delta_{\varepsilon}^{*}
the pullback operator on functions

\displaystyle\delta_{\varepsilon}^{*}:L^{1}(G)\to L^{1}(G),

\displaystyle\delta_{\varepsilon}^{*}f=f\circ\delta_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\delta_{\varepsilon}^{*}:L^{1}(G_{\lambda})\to L^{1}(G_{\varepsilon\lambda}),

\displaystyle\delta_{\varepsilon}^{*}g=g\circ\delta_{\varepsilon}.

Proposition 4.1
.

[Homogeneity] For
f\in L^{1}(G)
and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon\lambda}(\delta_{\varepsilon}^{*}f)(x,u)=(1/\varepsilon)\,\delta_{\varepsilon}^{*}\left(I_{\lambda}f\right)(x,u),\quad\forall\varepsilon>0.

Proof.

The main observation is that.

\displaystyle\delta_{\varepsilon}\gamma_{\nu,\varepsilon\lambda}(s)=\gamma_{\nu,\varepsilon\lambda}(\varepsilon s)

Thus,

\displaystyle I_{\nu,\varepsilon\lambda}(\delta_{\varepsilon}^{*}f)(x,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\delta_{\varepsilon}^{*}f((x,u)\gamma_{\nu,\varepsilon\lambda}(s))ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\delta_{\varepsilon}(x,u)\delta_{\varepsilon}\gamma_{\nu,\varepsilon\lambda}(s))ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\delta_{\varepsilon}(x,u)\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}(\varepsilon s))ds

\displaystyle=(1/\varepsilon)\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\delta_{\varepsilon}(x,u)\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}(s))ds

\displaystyle=(1/\varepsilon)(I_{\lambda}f)(\delta_{\varepsilon}(x,u))

as desired.
∎

4.2.
Boundedness and Factorization

Proposition 4.2
.

For any
\nu\in\mathfrak{v}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, the X-ray transform
I_{\nu,0}:L^{1}(G)\to L^{1}(G/{\mathbb{R}}\nu)
is well-defined and bounded.

Proof.

For
f\in C_{c}(G)
,

\displaystyle\|I_{\nu,0}f\|_{L^{1}(G/{\mathbb{R}}\nu)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}\int_{z\perp\nu}|I_{\nu,0}f(x,u)|d\nu^{\perp}(x)du

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}\int_{z\perp\nu}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f((x,u)(s\nu,0))|dsd\nu^{\perp}(x)du

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}\int_{z\perp\nu}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x+s\nu,u+s[x,\nu]/2)|dsd\nu^{\perp}(x)du

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}\int_{z\perp\nu}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x+s\nu,u)|dsd\nu^{\perp}(x)du

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{z}^{*}}\int_{\mathfrak{v}^{*}}|f(x,u)|dxdu=\|f\|_{L^{1}(G)}.

Therefore,
I_{\nu,0}
extends to a bounded map between
L^{1}
spaces.
∎

Here we decompose the X-ray transform into a periodization map and special case of the Pompeiu Transform. (See
[
43
]
).

Proposition 4.3
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
and
\nu\in S(\mathfrak{v}^{*})
, the X-ray transform
I_{\nu,\lambda}:L^{1}(G)\to L^{1}(G_{\lambda})
is well-defined, bounded and factors in the following way:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{1}(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{1}(G_{\lambda})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{1}(G_{\lambda})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{\lambda}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{I}_{\nu,\lambda}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{I}_{\nu,\lambda}}

where the maps which we call central periodization and the holonomy transform are given by

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}f(x,u)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f(x,u+\pi k|\lambda|^{-2}\hat{\lambda}),\quad\quad\widetilde{I}_{\nu,\lambda}g(x,u)=\int_{0}^{2\pi|\lambda|^{-1}}g\left((x,u)\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}(s)\right)ds

for
f\in L^{1}(G)
and
g\in L^{1}(G_{\lambda})
.

Proof.

By the dominated convergence theorem we have

\displaystyle\int_{G_{\lambda}}P_{\lambda}f(x,u)d(x,u)=\int_{G}f(x,u)d(x,u),\quad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G).

In particular

\|P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{1}(G_{\lambda})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(G)}.

Now
\widetilde{I}_{\nu,\lambda}f
is just a group convolution operator on
G_{\lambda}
by a compactly supported convolution kernel. Hence is it bounded. In particular,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{I}_{\nu,\lambda}f(x,u)|\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi|\lambda|^{-1}}|f\left((x,u)\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}(s)\right)|ds

and

\displaystyle\int_{G_{\lambda}}|\widetilde{I}_{\nu,\lambda}f(x,u)|d(x,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi|\lambda|^{-1}}\int_{G_{\lambda}}|f\left(x,u\right)|d(x,u)ds.

Therefore

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{I}_{\nu,\lambda}f\|_{L^{1}(G_{\lambda})}\leq 2\pi|\lambda|^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{1}(G_{\lambda})}.

The decomposition follows from the helical symmetry of the geodesics in (
3.5
). For
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G)

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{\nu,\lambda}\circ P_{\lambda}f(x,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi|\lambda|^{-1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f\left((x,u+\pi k|\lambda|^{-2}\widehat{\lambda})\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}(s)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi|\lambda|^{-1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f\left((x,u)\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}(s+2\pi k|\lambda|^{-1})\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{2\pi k|\lambda|^{-1}}^{2\pi(k+1)|\lambda|^{-1}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f\left((x,u)\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}(s)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\left((x,u)\gamma_{\nu,\lambda}(s)\right)ds=I_{\nu,\lambda}f(x,u)

where the second to last equality follows from the dominated convergence theorem.

By the previous two statements, we have

\displaystyle\|I_{\nu,\lambda}f\|_{L^{1}(G_{\lambda})}\leq 2\pi|\lambda|^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{1}(G)}.

In particular
I_{\nu,\lambda}
extends to a bounded map from
L^{1}(G)
to
L^{1}(G_{\lambda})
.

∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\chi\in M(\mathfrak{z}^{*})
be any compactly supported Radon measure for which

\int_{\mathfrak{z}^{*}}|\lambda|^{-1}d\chi(\lambda)<\infty,

and let

be the measure on the space of geodesics defined by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}F\chi d\mathcal{G}=\int_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}}\int_{\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}Fd\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}d\chi(\lambda),\quad F\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{G}).

The full X-ray transform

is well-defined and bounded.

Proof.

Finally, following Appendix
10.2
,

\displaystyle\|If\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{G},\chi d\mathcal{G})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}}|If|\chi d\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}}\int_{\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}|I_{\nu,\lambda}f|d{\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}}d\chi(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}}\int_{\nu\in{\mathbb{C}}P(\lambda,\mathfrak{v}^{*})}\int_{G_{\lambda}}|I_{\nu,\lambda}f(x,u)|\,d(x,u)d\nu\,d\chi(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi\int_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}}|\lambda|^{-1}\int_{\nu\in{\mathbb{C}}P(\lambda,\mathfrak{v}^{*})}d\nu d\chi(\lambda)\,d\lambda\|f\|_{L^{1}(G)}

\displaystyle=2\pi\,{{\rm vol}}(\mathbb{CP}^{n})\int_{\mathfrak{z}^{*}}|\lambda|^{-1}d\chi(\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{1}(G)}

∎

5.
Representation Theory of
G
and
G_{\lambda}

The Fourier Transform on
G
is defined by means of irreducible unitary representations.
There are many choices of conventions for representations of the Heisenberg group. We choose to use the Bargmann-Fock representation because certain intertwining operators are more explicit than for the Schrödinger representation.

5.1.
Bargmann-Fock Representation on the Heisenberg group

Bargmann-Fock space is the following Hilbert space (equipped with the evident inner product)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n}):=\{F:{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}},\text{ holomorphic: }\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|F(\zeta)|^{2}e^{-|\zeta|^{2}}<\infty\}.

There is an orthonormal basis is given by

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha}=\zeta^{\alpha}/\sqrt{\alpha!},\quad\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n},\;\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}

For each
h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
the Bargmann-Fock representation is the map

\displaystyle{\pi}_{h}:\mathbb{H}_{n}\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n}))

given by

\displaystyle{\pi}_{h}(z,t)F(\zeta):=e^{iht-\sqrt{h/2}\zeta\cdot\overline{z}-\tfrac{h}{4}|z|^{2}}F(\zeta+\sqrt{h/2}z),\quad F\in\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n}),\quad h>0,

and
{\pi}_{h}(z,t)={\pi}_{|h|}(\overline{z},-t)
for
h<0
. Despite the dependence on a parameter
h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
, we simply refer to this as
the
Bargmann-Fock representation. Here
\zeta\cdot\overline{z}=\zeta_{1}\overline{z}_{1}+\cdots+\zeta_{m}\overline{z}_{m}
. The Bargmann-Fock representation is strongly continuous unitary irreducible representations of the Heisenberg group on
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
.

Remark 5.1
.

There are many other normalizations our there, but we have chosen the one for which

•

{\pi}_{h}(z,t)=e^{iht}{\pi}_{h}(z,0)

•

{\pi}_{*}Z=\sqrt{2}\partial_{\zeta}

•

{\pi}_{*}\overline{Z}=\sqrt{2}\zeta

5.2.

To construct representations on a general

(5.1)

(5.2)

\displaystyle J_{\mu}=|\mu|R_{\mu}JR_{\mu}^{t}

with respect to the basis
5.1

Lemma 5.2
.

The mapping

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mu}(x,u)=(R_{\mu}^{t}x,\tfrac{\mu\cdot u}{|\mu|}),\quad(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}

Then define the irreducible representations on
G
on
\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
by

\displaystyle\pi_{\mu}:={\pi}_{|\mu|}\circ\alpha_{\mu}.

When

5.5.
The Unitary Dual

The
unitary dual

\widehat{G}
of a group
G
is the set of its irreducible unitary representations up to unitary equivalence. That is, the set of pairs
(\mathcal{H}_{\pi},\pi)
where
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
is a Hilbert space, and
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
is a strongly continuous group homomorphism from
G
to the set of unitary operators on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
. When
G
is a Nilpotent Lie group, Kirillov theory establishes a bijection between
\widehat{G}
and orbits of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by the coadjoint action.

When
G
is an
H
-type group, the Kirillov correspondence associates to each
\eta\in\mathfrak{v}^{*}
the representation
\pi_{(\eta,0)}
in (
5.3
) acting on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{\eta,0}}=\mathbb{C}
, and to each
\mu\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
the infinite dimensional representation
\pi_{\mu}
acting on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{\mu}}=\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
. Since
\mathfrak{v}^{*}\cup\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
exhaust all representatives of the coadjoint orbits of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, all elements of
\widehat{G}
are obtained in this way [Kirillov Theory]. Therefore
\widehat{G}
is parameterized by
\mathfrak{v}^{*}\cup\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
:

\displaystyle\widehat{G}=\{\text{class of }\pi_{\eta,0}:\eta\in\mathfrak{v}^{*}\}\cup\{\text{class of }\pi_{\mu}:\mu\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}\}.

5.6.
Representations on
G_{\lambda}

Proposition 5.8

(Compatibility of momenta)
.

For
\mu\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
and the associated representation
\pi_{\mu}
of
G
, there exists a representation of
G_{\lambda}
also denoted by
\pi_{\mu}
making following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{C}}^{n}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{\lambda}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\mu}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\mu}}

commute if and only if

\displaystyle\mu\cdot\lambda=2|\lambda|^{3}k,\quad\text{ for some }k\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

That is,
\pi_{\mu}
descends from a irreducible unitary representation of
G
to one of
G_{\lambda}
if and only if
\mu
is an element of the dual of the subgroup
\Gamma_{\lambda}
excluding zero.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. Then
\pi_{\mu}
is a representation of
G_{\lambda}
if and only if
\Gamma_{\lambda}\subset\ker{\pi_{\mu}}
. Since
\pi_{\mu}(x,u)=e^{i\mu\cdot u}\pi_{\mu}(x,0)
, we have

\displaystyle\ker\pi_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\{(x,u)\in G:\pi_{\mu}(x,u)={{\rm Id}}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})}\}

\displaystyle=\{(0,u)\in G:u\cdot\mu\in 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}\}.

Then
\Gamma_{\lambda}=\{(0,l\pi|\lambda|^{-2}\,\widehat{\lambda}):l\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}\subset\ker{\pi_{\mu}}
if and only if

\displaystyle\pi|\lambda|^{-2}\,\widehat{\lambda}\cdot\mu=2\pi k,\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

This is precisely the condition that
\mu\in\Gamma_{\lambda}^{*}:=\{\mu\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}:\mu\cdot\omega\in 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}},\text{for all }\omega\in\Gamma_{\lambda}\}
. We have

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mu\cdot\lambda=2|\lambda|^{3}k,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}

as desired.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

Equation (
5.4
) implies

\displaystyle\mu=\mu_{\perp}+2k|\lambda|\lambda

where
\mu_{\perp}\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}
such that
\mu_{\perp}\cdot\lambda=0
.

Remark 5.10
.

Note
k=0
in Equation (
5.4
) if and only if
\lambda,\mu\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
are perpendicular. This is only possible when
m=\dim\mathfrak{z}>1
.

By Proposition
5.8
, we may view the unitary dual
\widehat{G}_{\lambda}
as a subset of
\widehat{G}
.

\displaystyle\widehat{G}_{\lambda}=\{\text{class of }\pi_{\eta,0}:\eta\in\mathfrak{v}^{*}\}\cup\{\text{class of }\pi_{\mu}:\mu\in\Gamma_{\lambda}^{*}\setminus\{0\}\}\subset\widehat{G}

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\lambda}^{*}:=\{\mu\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}:\mu\cdot\lambda=2k|\lambda|^{3},\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}\subset\mathfrak{z}^{*}.

is the dual to the discrete subgroup
\Gamma_{\lambda}
.

5.7.
Schrödinger Representation on the Heisenberg group

Denote by
\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m}))
the set of unitary operators acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
. For each
h\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
the map

\displaystyle\rho_{h}:\mathbb{H}_{m}\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

\displaystyle[\rho_{h}(x,y,t)\phi](\xi)=e^{ih(t+\frac{1}{2}xy)}e^{i\sqrt{h}y\xi}\phi(\xi+\sqrt{|h|}x),\quad\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

where
\sqrt{h}=\text{sgn}(h)\sqrt{|h|}
. Here we are more or less using the conversions of Strichartz
[
77
, p. 358]
and
[
46
]
.

This is a strongly continuous unitary representation of
\mathbb{H}_{n}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. These
Schrödinger representations
are irreducible and pairwise inequivalent. Moreover, by the Stone-von Neumann Theorem, they exhaust all of the irreducible unitary representations, up to unitary equivalence, that are nontrivial on the center of
\mathbb{H}_{n}
.

Remark 5.11
.

There are many other realizations of the Schrödinger representation abound, but we have chosen the one for which

•

\rho_{h}(z,t)=e^{iht}\rho_{h}(z,0)

•

\rho_{*}X=\partial_{\xi}

•

\rho_{*}Y=i\xi
.

The Bargmann-Fock Representation
{\pi}_{h}
is unitarily equivalent to the Schrödinger representation
\rho_{h}
with the intertwining map
B:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\to\mathcal{H}
given by

(5.5)

\displaystyle Bf(\zeta)=\pi^{-n/4}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|\zeta|^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(x)e^{\sqrt{2}\xi\zeta-\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}}d\xi

That is,
\rho_{h}(z,t)\circ B=B\circ\pi_{h}(z,t).
This can be seen by taking the generating series for the Hermite functions:

\displaystyle\pi^{-n/4}e^{\sqrt{2}\xi\zeta-|\zeta|^{2}/2-|\xi|^{2}/2}=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}}\Phi_{\alpha}(\xi)\omega_{\alpha}(\zeta).

6.
Group Fourier Analysis

Motivated by the Peter Weyl Theorem for compact Lie groups, we consider the unitary dual as a generalized frequency space for the group Fourier Transform. See
[
46
]
for a detailed exposition on the topic.
For any
\pi\in\widehat{G}
, we define the group Fourier Transform of an integrable function
f\in L^{1}(G)
is the operator
\mathcal{F}(f)(\pi)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(f)(\pi)=\int_{G}f(g)\pi(g)^{*}dg,

For
\mu\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
or
\eta\in\mathfrak{v}^{*}
, we write
\mathcal{F}(f)(\mu)=\mathcal{F}(f)(\pi_{\mu})
and
\mathcal{F}(f)(\eta,0)=\mathcal{F}(f)(\pi_{(\eta,0)})
. For
f\in L^{1}(G)
, its Fourier Transform
\pi\mapsto\mathcal{F}(f)(\pi)
is a bounded family of bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
, indexed by
\widehat{G}=\mathfrak{v}^{*}\cup\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. The Fourier Transform extends to an isometry from
L^{2}(G)
onto the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\widehat{G})
of all measurable families
\{A(\mu)\}_{\mu\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}}
of operators on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi_{\mu}}
which are Hilbert-Schmidt for almost every
\mu\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, with norm

\displaystyle\|A\|:=\left(\int_{\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}}\|A(\mu)\|^{2}_{HS(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})}d\pi(\mu)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty

where
d\pi(\mu)=c_{0}|\mu|^{d}d\mu
is the
Plancherel measure
.
This is the unique measure on
\widehat{G}
for which Plancherel formula holds:

\displaystyle\int_{G}|f(g)|^{2}dg=c_{0}\int_{\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}}\|\mathcal{F}(f)(\mu)\|^{2}_{HS(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})}|\mu|^{d}d\mu.

Note that only the
\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
part of the unitary dual appears in the Plancerel formula. This is because the subset of
\widehat{G}
corresponding fo
\mathfrak{v}^{*}
of finite dimensional representations has measure zero.
The Plancherl Formula yields an inversion formula for
f\in\mathcal{S}(G)
:

\displaystyle f(g)=\int_{\widehat{G}}\rm{tr}\left(\pi(g)\mathcal{F}(f)(\pi)\right)d\pi.

6.3.

G_{\lambda}

Define convolution on
G_{\lambda}
by

\displaystyle f*g(x)=\int_{G_{\lambda}}f(y)g(y^{-1}x)dy,\quad f,g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{\lambda})

G_{\lambda}

\displaystyle f*\kappa(x)=\langle\kappa,\pi_{R}(x^{-1})\widetilde{f}\rangle

where

and

\displaystyle Af=f*\kappa.

Then there is a family of operators

indexed by
\pi\in\widehat{G}_{\lambda}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(Af)(\pi)=\widehat{\kappa}(\pi)\circ\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(f)(\pi),\quad\pi\in\widehat{G}_{\lambda}.

In many contexts, the

is called the

of
A

[
46
]
.

If
\kappa=\int_{G_{\lambda}}d\sigma
is just integration against a compactly supported Borel measure

on
G_{\lambda}

is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\kappa}(\pi)=\int_{G_{\lambda}}\pi(x)^{*}d\sigma(x).

The X-ray Transform

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{\nu,\lambda}f=f*\kappa_{\nu,\lambda},\quad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{\lambda}),

Therefore, we may assume without loss of generality that

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

The Fourier Slice Theorem is now a formal consequence of lemmas

and
6.2
, and Proposition

. Let

be given.

For all

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(I_{\nu,\lambda}f)(\eta,0)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(\widetilde{I}_{\nu,\lambda}\circ P_{\lambda}f)(\eta,0)

\displaystyle=2\pi|\lambda|^{-1}J_{0}(\langle\eta,\nu\rangle|\lambda|^{-1})\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(P_{\lambda}f)(\eta,0)

\displaystyle=2\pi|\lambda|^{-1}J_{0}(\langle\eta,\nu\rangle|\lambda|^{-1})\mathcal{F}(f)(\eta,0).

\displaystyle=2\pi|\lambda|^{-1}\mathcal{J}_{\nu,\lambda}(\mu)\circ\mathcal{F}(f)(\mu)

as desired.
∎

6.6.
Geometry on the charge-frequency relation

At this moment, we pause to repackage some information in a more geometric way. Let

\mathfrak{R}_{1}:=\coprod_{|\lambda|=1}\Gamma_{\lambda}^{*}\setminus\{0\}=\{(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\times\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{(0,0)\}:|\lambda|=1,\lambda\cdot\mu\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}\}

be the subset of the momenta relation
\mathfrak{R}
defined in Equation (
2.4
) with
|\lambda|=1
.
The condition
\mu\cdot\lambda=2k
for
\lambda,\mu\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
and
|\lambda|=1
is equivalent to saying that

\lambda\cdot\mu=\mu_{\perp}+2k\lambda,\quad\mu_{\perp}\cdot\lambda=0.

This in turn means that for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, the subset

\mathfrak{R}_{1,k}:=\{(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\times\mathfrak{z}^{*}:|\lambda|=1,\mu\cdot\lambda=2k\}

is diffeomorphic to the tangent bundle to the sphere in
\mathfrak{z}^{*}
. In particular, we may identify

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{1}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow TS^{m-1}\times 2{\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle(\lambda,\mu)

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\lambda,{\rm Pr}_{\lambda}^{\perp}(\mu);\,\lambda\cdot\mu)=(\lambda,\mu_{\perp};\,2k)

where
{\rm Pr}^{\perp}_{\lambda}(\mu)=\mu-(\mu\cdot\lambda)\lambda
is the orthogonal projection of
\mu
onto
\lambda
. Then for fixed
\nu\in\mathfrak{v}^{*}
, the field of operators
\mathcal{J}_{\nu,\lambda}(\mu)
depends on a an integer
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and a point of the tangent bundle of the unit sphere in
\mathfrak{z}^{*}
.

Lemma 7.15
.

Fix
\lambda\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
. If

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}(\mu)\circ\mathcal{F}(f)(\mu)

By the Fourier Slice Theorem

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{J}_{\nu,\lambda}(\mu)\circ\mathcal{F}(f)(\mu).

Applying
\mathcal{J}_{\nu,\lambda}(\mu)^{*}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{N}_{\nu,\lambda}(\mu)\circ\mathcal{F}(f)(\mu).

Integrating over
\nu\in S(\mathfrak{v}^{*})
, we have the result.
∎

Part 2:
\mathfrak{R}_{\lambda,k}=\{\mu\in\mathfrak{z}^{*}\setminus\{0\}:\mu\cdot\lambda=2k\}

Let

Then the Fourier Transform
\mathcal{F}(f)(\mu)
vanishes for all
\mu\in A
where

\displaystyle A=\cup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}A_{k}

Then for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the set

divides the space

into two three disjoint sets

,

and

.

. Furthermore

•

B_{k}^{\infty}\supset B_{k+1}^{\infty}

•

B_{k}^{0}\subset B_{k+1}^{0}

•

B_{k}^{\infty}=kB_{1}^{\infty}

•

B_{k}^{0}=kB_{1}^{0}

•

B_{k}^{0}\cap B_{k}^{\infty}=\emptyset.

8.
Concluding Remarks

The sub-Riemannian geodesics are also of interest because of their relation to the compatible Riemannian and Loentzian geodesics. We remark that these techniques may be used to study the X-ray transforms
I^{\varepsilon}
and
I^{i\varepsilon}
associated to the family of left-invariant Riemannian and Lorentzian metrics

\displaystyle g_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}:=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{v}}\pm\varepsilon^{-2}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{z}}

Since the metric
g_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}
differs from the sub-Riemannian metric
g=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{v}}
by a Casmir element,
\pm\varepsilon^{-2}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{z}}
, the projection of
g^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}
-geodesics from
G
to
\mathfrak{v}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
will be independent of
\epsilon
. Thus, one may parameterize the Riemannian and Lorentzian geodesics in a nearly identical way to
(
3.6
)
.
It is a simple matter to extend the results based on a simple geometric lemma of Montgomery and prove analogous Fourier Slice Theorems as in
[
47
]
. The sub-Riemannian question is therefore a natural starting point to study other left-invariant geometries as a perturbation of the Sub-Riemannian one. We will explore this in a subsequent work.

It is our goal to generalized this work to study X-ray tomography on sub-Riemannian manifolds with less symmetry. Much progress has been made studying classical inverse problems on Riemannian manifolds using tools from microlocal analysis. The success of the group Fourier transform highlights the potential for a pseudodifferential calculus based on the group Fourier transform to treat integral geometric problems in sub-Riemannian geometry.

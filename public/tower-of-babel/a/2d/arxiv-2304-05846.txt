Rationally-extended Dunkl oscillator on the line
C Quesne

Abstract
It is shown that the extensions of exactly-solvable quantum mechanical problems connected with the replacement of ordinary derivatives by Dunkl ones and with that of classical orthogonal polynomials by exceptional orthogonal ones can be easily combined. For such a purpose, the example of the Dunkl oscillator on the line is considered and three different types of rationally-extended Dunkl oscillators are constructed. The corresponding wavefunctions are expressed in terms of exceptional orthogonal generalized Hermite polynomials, defined in terms of the three different types of X m X_{m} -Laguerre exceptional orthogonal polynomials. Furthermore, the extended Dunkl oscillator Hamiltonians are shown to be expressible in terms of some extended Dunkl derivatives and some anharmonic oscillator potentials.



Keywords: quantum mechanics, Dunkl derivative, exceptional orthogonal polynomials

2
The one-dimensional Dunkl oscillator and its relation to the radial oscillator

The one-dimensional Dunkl oscillator model is defined by the Hamiltonian
[
20
]

H_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}(-D_{\mu}^{2}+x^{2})=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-\frac{2\mu}{x}\frac{d}{dx}+\frac{\mu}{x^{2}}(1-R)+x^{2}\right),\qquad-\infty<x<+\infty,

(2.1)

where
D_{\mu}
is the Dunkl derivative

D_{\mu}=\frac{d}{dx}+\frac{\mu}{x}(1-R),\qquad\mu>-\frac{1}{2},

(2.2)

and
R
is the reflection operator.

The corresponding Schrödinger equation reads

H_{\mu}\psi^{(\mu)}_{2n+\epsilon}(x)=\left(2n+\epsilon+\mu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\psi^{(\mu)}_{2n+\epsilon}(x),\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots,\qquad\epsilon=0,1,

(2.3)

where the wavefunctions
\psi^{(\mu)}_{2n+\epsilon}(x)
, which are even for
\epsilon=0
and odd for
\epsilon=1
, can be expressed as

\psi^{(\mu)}_{2n+\epsilon}(x)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}}H^{\mu}_{2n+\epsilon}(x)

(2.4)

in terms of generalized Hermite polynomials
[
20
]
. The latter are defined by

H^{\mu}_{2n+\epsilon}(x)=(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{n!}{\Gamma(n+\mu+\epsilon+1/2)}\right)^{1/2}x^{\epsilon}L^{(\mu-1/2+\epsilon)}_{n}(x^{2}),

(2.5)

in terms of Laguerre polynomials
[
45
]
. The wavefunctions (
2.4
) satisfy the orthonormality property

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left[\psi^{(\mu)}_{2n^{\prime}+\epsilon^{\prime}}(x)\right]^{*}\psi^{(\mu)}_{2n+\epsilon}(x)|x|^{2\mu}dx=\delta_{n^{\prime},n}\delta_{\epsilon^{\prime},\epsilon}.

(2.6)

These results for the Dunkl oscillator on the line can be directly obtained from well-known ones for the radial oscillator (see, e.g., ref.
[
38
]
). The corresponding Schrödinger equation reads

\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V_{l}(x)\right)\phi^{(l)}_{n}(x)=E^{(l)}_{n}\phi^{(l)}_{n}(x),\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

(2.7)

Here

V_{l}(x)=\frac{l(l+1)}{x^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\omega^{2}x^{2},\qquad 0<x<+\infty,

(2.8)

where
l
denotes the angular momentum quantum number and
\omega
the frequency,

E^{(l)}_{n}=\omega\left(2n+l+\frac{3}{2}\right),

(2.9)

and

\phi^{(l)}_{n}(x)=\left(\frac{\omega}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\left(l+\frac{3}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{2n!}{\Gamma(n+l+3/2)}\right)^{1/2}x^{l+1}L^{(l+1/2)}_{n}(z)e^{-\frac{1}{2}z},\qquad z\equiv\frac{1}{2}\omega x^{2},

(2.10)

is such that

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\phi^{(l)}_{n^{\prime}}(x)\right)^{*}\phi^{(l)}_{n}(x)dx=\delta_{n^{\prime},n}.

(2.11)

On starting from the radial oscillator equation (
2.7
), performing the replacements
\omega\to 2
,
l\to\mu-1
, and multiplying the wavefunction by
x^{-\mu}
, we indeed arrive at the equation

\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-\frac{2\mu}{x}\frac{d}{dx}+x^{2}\right)x^{-\mu}\phi^{(\mu-1)}_{n}(x)=\left(2n+\mu+\frac{1}{2}\right)x^{-\mu}\phi^{(\mu-1)}_{n}(x),

(2.12)

agreeing with (
2.3
) for
\epsilon=0
. Similarly, for
\omega\to 2
,
l\to\mu
, we get

\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-\frac{2\mu}{x}\frac{d}{dx}+\frac{2\mu}{x^{2}}+x^{2}\right)x^{-\mu}\phi^{(\mu)}_{n}(x)=\left(2n+\mu+\frac{3}{2}\right)x^{-\mu}\phi^{(\mu)}_{n}(x),

(2.13)

giving back (
2.3
) for
\epsilon=1
. In the transformation, equation (
2.10
) leads to equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) after multiplying the former by an extra
1/\sqrt{2}
factor, which takes the change of normalization from the half-line to the whole line into account (the phase factor in (
2.5
) being optional).

3
Rational extensions of the radial oscillator

The rationally-extended radial oscillators resulting from one-step Darboux transformations read
[
38
,
42
,
43
]

\displaystyle V_{l,{\rm ext}}(x)

\displaystyle=V_{l}(x)+V_{l,{\rm rat}}(x)

\displaystyle=V_{l^{\prime}}(x)-2\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\log\varphi_{l^{\prime}m}(x)-C,

(3.1)

where according to the choice made for
l^{\prime}
and
\varphi_{l^{\prime}m}(x)
, three different types of extension are obtained:

\displaystyle{\rm I:}\quad

\displaystyle l^{\prime}=l-1,\quad\varphi_{l-1,m}(x)\propto x^{l}e^{\frac{1}{4}\omega x^{2}}L^{(l-1/2)}_{m}(-\omega x^{2}/2),\quad C=-\omega;

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\rm II:}\quad

\displaystyle l^{\prime}=l+1,\quad\varphi_{l+1,m}(x)\propto x^{-l-1}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\omega x^{2}}L^{(-l-3/2)}_{m}(\omega x^{2}/2),\quad C=\omega,

\displaystyle\quad m<l+3/2;

\displaystyle{\rm III:}\quad

\displaystyle l^{\prime}=l+1,\quad\varphi_{l+1,m}(x)\propto x^{-l-1}e^{\frac{1}{4}\omega x^{2}}L^{(-l-3/2)}_{m}(-\omega x^{2}/2),\quad C=-\omega,

\displaystyle\quad m<l+3/2,\quad\text{$m$ even}.

The rational term in (
3.1
) can be written as

V_{l,{\rm rat}}(x)=-2\omega\left\{\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}+2z\left[\frac{\ddot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}-\left(\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}\right)^{2}\right]\right\},

(3.3)

where
z=\frac{1}{2}\omega x^{2}
,
\alpha=l+\frac{1}{2}
, a dot denotes a derivative with respect to
z
, and

g^{(\alpha)}_{m}(z)=\begin{cases}L^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}(-z)&\text{for type I},\\[5.69046pt]
L^{(-\alpha-1)}_{m}(z),\quad m<\alpha+1&\text{for type II},\\[5.69046pt]
L^{(-\alpha-1)}_{m}(-z),\quad m<\alpha+1,\quad\text{$m$ even}&\text{for type III}.\end{cases}

(3.4)

The Schrödinger equations obtained by replacing
V_{l}(x)
by
V_{l,{\rm ext}}(x)
in (
2.7
) assume different forms.

For type I, one gets

\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V_{l,{\rm ext}}(x)\right)\phi^{({\rm I},l)}_{n}(x)=E^{({\rm I},l)}_{n}\phi^{({\rm I},l)}_{n}(x),

(3.5)

where

E^{({\rm I},l)}_{n}=\omega\left(2n-2m+l+\frac{3}{2}\right)

(3.6)

and

\phi^{({\rm I},l)}_{n}(x)={\cal N}^{({\rm I},l)}_{n}\frac{x^{l+1}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\omega x^{2}}}{L^{(l-1/2)}_{m}(-\frac{1}{2}\omega x^{2})}L^{{\rm I},l+1/2}_{m,n}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\omega x^{2}\right).

(3.7)

Here
n=m,m+1,m+2,\ldots
and
L^{{\rm I},l+1/2}_{m,n}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\omega x^{2}\right)
is an
n
th-degree
X_{m}
-Laguerre EOP of type I. Such EOPs are known to form an orthogonal and complete polynomial set although 0, 1, …,
m-1
degrees are missing
[
44
]
. The wavefunctions
\phi^{({\rm I},l)}_{n}(x)
satisfy the orthonormality properties

\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\left(\phi^{({\rm I},l)}_{n^{\prime}}(x)\right)^{*}\phi^{({\rm I},l)}_{n}(x)=\delta_{n^{\prime},n},

(3.8)

with the choice

{\cal N}^{({\rm I},l)}_{n}=\left(\frac{\omega}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\left(l+\frac{3}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{2(n-m)!}{(n+l+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(n+l+\frac{1}{2}-m)}\right)^{1/2}

(3.9)

for the normalization factor.

For type II, one gets

\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V_{l,{\rm ext}}(x)\right)\phi^{({\rm II},l)}_{n}(x)=E^{({\rm II},l)}_{n}\phi^{({\rm II},l)}_{n}(x),

(3.10)

where

E^{({\rm II},l)}_{n}=\omega\left(2n-2m+l+\frac{3}{2}\right)

(3.11)

and

\phi^{({\rm II},l)}_{n}(x)={\cal N}^{({\rm II},l)}_{n}\frac{x^{l+1}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\omega x^{2}}}{L^{(-l-3/2)}_{m}(\frac{1}{2}\omega x^{2})}L^{{\rm II},l+1/2}_{m,n}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\omega x^{2}\right),

(3.12)

provided
l>m-\frac{3}{2}
. Here
n=m,m+1,m+2,\ldots
and
L^{{\rm II},l+1/2}_{m,n}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\omega x^{2}\right)
is an
n
th-degree
X_{m}
-Laguerre EOP of type II. Such EOPs have rather similar properties as those of type I with 0, 1, …,
m-1
degrees missing
[
44
]
. The wavefunctions
\phi^{({\rm II},l)}_{n}(x)
satisfy orthonormality properties similar to (
3.8
) provided

{\cal N}^{({\rm II},l)}_{n}=\left(\frac{\omega}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\left(l+\frac{3}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{2(n-m)!}{(n+l+\frac{3}{2}-2m)\Gamma(n+l+\frac{5}{2}-m)}\right)^{1/2}.

(3.13)

From (
2.9
), (
3.6
), and (
3.11
), we note that type I or II extended radial oscillators have exactly the same spectrum as that of the starting radial oscillator
V_{l}(x)
. This is not the case for type III extensions, for which one gets

\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V_{l,{\rm ext}}(x)\right)\phi^{({\rm III},l)}_{n}(x)=E^{({\rm III},l)}_{n}\phi^{({\rm III},l)}_{n}(x),

(3.14)

where

E^{({\rm III},l)}_{n}=\omega\left(2n-2m+l+\frac{3}{2}\right)

(3.15)

and

\phi^{({\rm III},l)}_{n}(x)={\cal N}^{({\rm III},l)}_{n}\frac{x^{l+1}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\omega x^{2}}}{L^{(-l-3/2)}_{m}(-\frac{1}{2}\omega x^{2})}L^{{\rm III},l+1/2}_{m,n}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\omega x^{2}\right),

(3.16)

provided
l>m-\frac{3}{2}
and
m
is even. Here
n=0,m+1,m+2,\ldots
and
L^{{\rm III},l+1/2}_{m,n}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\omega x^{2}\right)
is an
n
th-degree
X_{m}
-Laguerre EOP of type III. For such EOPs, 1, 2, …
m
degrees are indeed missing although they again form an orthogonal and complete polynomial set
[
44
]
. Orthonormality properties similar to (
3.8
) are satisfied with the choice

{\cal N}^{({\rm III},l)}_{n}=\left(\frac{\omega}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\left(l+\frac{3}{2}\right)}\times\begin{cases}\left(\frac{2}{\Gamma(l+\frac{3}{2}-m)m!}\right)^{1/2}&\text{if $n=0$},\\[5.69046pt]
\left(\frac{2(n-m-1)!}{n\Gamma(n+l+\frac{3}{2}-m)}\right)^{1/2}&\text{if $n=m+1,m+2,\ldots$}.\end{cases}

(3.17)

4
Rational extensions of the Dunkl oscillator

To get rational extensions of the Dunkl oscillator from those of the radial oscillator, we are going to proceed as shown in section 2 for the standard problem. In all cases, we replace
\omega
by 2, so that
z=\frac{1}{2}\omega x^{2}
becomes
z=x^{2}
. In the
\epsilon=1
case, which is obtained by introducing an operator
\frac{1}{2}(1-R)
in the Hamiltonian,
l
is replaced by
\mu
, so that the rational part of the potential is defined in terms of
\alpha=\mu+\frac{1}{2}
, whereas in the
\epsilon=0
case, obtained by introducing an operator
\frac{1}{2}(1+R)
,
l
is replaced by
\mu-1
, so that
\alpha-1
will appear instead of
\alpha
in the rational part of the potential. As a result, the three possible extensions of
H_{\mu}
are given by

\displaystyle H_{\mu,{\rm ext}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Biggl\{-D_{\mu}^{2}+x^{2}-2\Biggl[\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}+2z\Biggl(\frac{\ddot{g}^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}-\biggl(\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}\biggr)^{2}\Biggr)\Biggr](1+R)

\displaystyle\quad-2\Biggl[\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}+2z\Biggl(\frac{\ddot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}-\biggl(\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}\biggr)^{2}\Biggr)\Biggr](1-R)\Biggr\},

(4.1)

where
g^{(\alpha)}_{m}(z)
is defined as in (
3.4
) with
\alpha=\mu+\frac{1}{2}
and
z=x^{2}
.

The corresponding Schrödinger equations now read

H_{\mu,{\rm ext}}\hat{\psi}^{(\mu)}_{2n+\epsilon}(x)=\left(2n-2m+\epsilon+\mu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hat{\psi}^{(\mu)}_{2n+\epsilon}(x),

(4.2)

where
n=m,m+1,m+2,\ldots
for type I or II, or
n=0,m+1,m+2,\ldots
for type III and in all cases
\epsilon=0,1
. The corresponding wavefunctions
\hat{\psi}^{(\mu)}_{2n+\epsilon}(x)
result from those of the extended radial oscillator after multiplication by
x^{-\mu}
and a change of normalization from the half-line to the whole line. They may therefore be written as

\hat{\psi}^{(\mu)}_{2n+\epsilon}(x)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}}\hat{H}^{\mu}_{2n+\epsilon}(x),

(4.3)

where
\hat{H}^{(\mu)}_{2n+\epsilon}(x)
are exceptional orthogonal generalized Hermite polynomials, which we define by

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{(\mu)}_{2n+\epsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{(n-m)!}{(n+\mu+\epsilon-\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(n-m+\mu+\epsilon-\frac{1}{2})}\right)^{1/2}\frac{x^{\epsilon}}{L^{(\mu+\epsilon-3/2)}_{m}(-x^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad\times L^{{\rm I},\mu-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}_{m,n}(x^{2}),\qquad\text{if $n=m,m+1,m+2,\ldots$},

(4.4)

for type I,

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{(\mu)}_{2n+\epsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{(n-m)!}{(n-2m+\mu+\epsilon+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(n-m+\mu+\epsilon+\frac{3}{2})}\right)^{1/2}\frac{x^{\epsilon}}{L^{(-\mu-\epsilon-1/2)}_{m}(x^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad\times L^{{\rm II},\mu-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}_{m,n}(x^{2}),\qquad\text{if $n=m,m+1,m+2,\ldots$},

(4.5)

for type II, and

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{(\mu)}_{2n+\epsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{\epsilon}}{L^{(-\mu-\epsilon-1/2)}_{m}(-x^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\begin{cases}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mu-m+\epsilon+\frac{1}{2})m!}\right)^{1/2}&\text{if $n=0$},\\
\left(\frac{(n-m-1)!}{n\Gamma(n-m+\mu+\epsilon+\frac{1}{2})}\right)^{1/2}L^{{\rm III},\mu-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}_{m,n}(x^{2})&\\
\qquad\text{if $n=m+1,m+2,\ldots$}&\end{cases}

(4.6)

for type III (with
m
even). Note that the
\mu
values have to be restricted to
\mu>\frac{1}{2}
for type I or
\mu>m-\frac{1}{2}
for type II or III (in order to get
\mu-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon>0
or
\mu-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon>m-1
, respectively).

5
Rationally-extended Dunkl oscillators in terms of extended Dunkl derivatives

In the present section, we plan to show that the rationally-extended Dunkl oscillator Hamiltonian (
4.1
) can be rewritten in a simpler way

H_{\mu,{\rm ext}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{D}_{\mu}^{2}+x^{2}+G(x)\right)

(5.1)

in terms of an extended Dunkl derivative

\hat{D}_{\mu}=D_{\mu}+F(x)R=\frac{d}{dx}+\frac{\mu}{x}(1-R)+F(x)R,

(5.2)

defined in terms of some odd function
F(x)
, while
G(x)
in (
5.1
) is some even function. The two functions
F(x)
and
G(x)
will of course be different for the three types of extensions.

On inserting definition (
5.2
) in (
5.1
) and comparing the result with equation (
4.1
), one finds that
F(x)
must satisfy the first-order differential equation

F^{\prime}(x)=2\Biggl\{\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}+2z\Biggl[\frac{\ddot{g}^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}-\Biggl(\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}\Biggr)^{2}\Biggr]\Biggr\}-2\Biggl\{\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}+2z\Biggl[\frac{\ddot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}-\Biggl(\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}\Biggr)^{2}\Biggr]\Biggr\},

(5.3)

where a dash denotes a derivative with respect to
x
, while a dot stands for a derivative with respect to
z=x^{2}
. In terms of such a function,
G(x)
will then be given by

\displaystyle G(x)

\displaystyle=-2\Biggl\{\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}+2z\Biggl[\frac{\ddot{g}^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}-\Biggl(\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}\Biggr)^{2}\Biggr]\Biggr\}-2\Biggl\{\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}+2z\Biggl[\frac{\ddot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}-\Biggl(\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}\Biggr)^{2}\Biggr]\Biggr\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\mu}{x}F(x)-F^{2}(x).

(5.4)

Solving equation (
5.3
) is straightforward and leads to

F(x)=2x\left(\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}-\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}\right).

(5.5)

On inserting (
5.5
) in (
5.4
), one gets

G(x)=4(\alpha-1)\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}-4\alpha\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}-4z\left(\frac{\ddot{g}^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}+\frac{\ddot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}\right)+8z\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}\frac{\dot{g}^{(\alpha)}_{m}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}.

(5.6)

To obtain more explicit results for
F(x)
and
G(x)
, one has to use definition (
3.4
) of
g^{(\alpha)}_{m}(z)
in terms of Laguerre polynomials, as well as some known properties of the latter, as summarized in the appendix. The results read

F(x)=\begin{cases}2x\left(\frac{g^{(\alpha)}_{m-1}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}-\frac{g^{(\alpha+1)}_{m-1}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}\right)&\text{for type I},\\[5.69046pt]
2x\left(-\frac{g^{(\alpha-2)}_{m-1}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}+\frac{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m-1}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}\right)&\text{for type II},\\[5.69046pt]
2x\left(\frac{g^{(\alpha-2)}_{m-1}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}-\frac{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m-1}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}\right)&\text{for type III},\end{cases}.

(5.7)

and

G(x)=\begin{cases}-4(\alpha-2+m)\frac{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m-1}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}+4(\alpha-1+m)\frac{g^{(\alpha)}_{m-1}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}&\text{for type I},\\[5.69046pt]
4(\alpha-m)\frac{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m-1}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}-4(\alpha+1-m)\frac{g^{(\alpha)}_{m-1}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}&\text{for type II},\\[5.69046pt]
-4(\alpha-m)\frac{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m-1}}{g^{(\alpha-1)}_{m}}+4(\alpha+1-m)\frac{g^{(\alpha)}_{m-1}}{g^{(\alpha)}_{m}}&\text{for type III}.\end{cases}

(5.8)

For
m=1
, for instance, one gets as usual the same results for types I and II, namely

\displaystyle F(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{4x}{2x^{2}+2\mu-1}-\frac{4x}{2x^{2}+2\mu+1},

\displaystyle G(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4(2\mu-1)}{2x^{2}+2\mu-1}+\frac{4(2\mu+1)}{2x^{2}+2\mu+1},

(5.9)

with

6
Conclusion

In the present work, we have shown that it is possible to combine the extensions of exactly-solvable quantum mechanical problems connected with the replacement of the ordinary derivative by a Dunkl one and with that of COPs by EOPs. For such a purpose, we have explicitly constructed three different types of rationally-extended Dunkl oscillators on the line, whose wavefunctions can be expressed in terms of exceptional orthogonal generalized Hermite polynomials, defined in terms of the three different types of
X_{m}
-Laguerre EOPs.

It is rather obvious that the method described here could be used to generate rationally-extended Dunkl oscillators associated to multi-indexed
X_{m_{1}m_{2}\ldots m_{k}}
-Laguerre EOPs, as well as rationally-extended Dunkl oscillators in more than one dimension.

Considering similar constructions for some other quantum potentials and a possible use of supersymmetric techniques would also be interesting problems for future study.

Global and local asymptotic stability of an epidemic reaction-diffusion model with a nonlinear incidence Journal: ………..
Lamia Djebara a , Redouane Douaifia b , Salem Abdelmalek c , Samir Bendoukha d,∗

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to study the dynamics of a reaction–diffusion SIS (susceptible-infectious-susceptible) epidemic model with a nonlinear incidence rate describing the transmission of a communicable disease between individuals. We prove that the proposed model has two steady states under one condition. By analyzing the eigenvalues and using the Routh–Hurwitz criterion and an appropriately constructed Lyapunov functional, we establish the local and global asymptotic stability of the non negative constant steady states subject to the basic reproduction number being greater than unity and of the disease–free equilibrium subject to the basic reproduction number being smaller than or equal to unity in ODE case. By applying an appropriately constructed Lyapunov functional, we identify the condition of the global stability in the PDE case. Finally, we present some numerical examples illustrating and confirming the analytical results obtained throughout the paper.

2
Preliminary Properties of the Model

Let us assume that the initial conditions
\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
. Note that for
(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
,
we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}F(0,v)=\Lambda>0,\\
G(u,0)=0,\end{array}\right.

which makes the function
(F,G)^{T}
essentially nonnegative. Hence, the
nonnegative quadrant
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
is an invariant set, see
[
12
,
28
, Proposition 2.1]
. By dropping the spatial variable,
the proposed system reduces to the following system of ordinary differential
equations (ODE):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{du}{dt}=F\left(u,v\right)&\ \ \ \ \text{in }(0,\infty),\\
\frac{dv}{dt}=G\left(u,v\right)&\ \ \ \ \text{in }(0,\infty),\end{array}\right.

(6)

with initial conditions

u\left(0\right)=u_{0}\geq 0,\ \ \ v\left(0\right)=v_{0}\geq 0.

(7)

In the following subsections, we define an invariant region for the system,
identify the system’s equilibria and their relation to the basic
reproduction number
R_{0}
, establish the global existence of solutions in
time, and investigate the local stability of the system in the ODE and PDE scenarios.

2.1
Invariant Regions

Throughout this paper,we let
N=u+v
and
\sigma_{0}=\min\left(\sigma,\mu\right)
. We also define the region

D=\left\{(u,v):u,v\geq 0\text{ \ and \ }u+v\leq\frac{\Lambda}{\sigma_{0}}\right\}.

The following proposition shows that
D
is an invariant region of system (
6
)–(
7
).

Proposition 1

The region
D
is non–empty, attracting and positively invariant.

Proof 1

We start by summing the equations of system (
6
)–(
7
),
which yields

\frac{d}{dt}N\left(t\right)=u_{t}+v_{t}\leq\Lambda-\sigma_{0}N.

This implies that

N\left(t\right)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{\sigma_{0}}\left(1-e^{-\sigma_{0}t}\right)+N_{0}e^{-\sigma_{0}t}.

Substituting the value of
N
yields

\left(u+v\right)\left(t\right)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{\sigma_{0}}\left(1-e^{-\sigma_{0}t}\right)+\left(u+v\right)\left(0\right)e^{-\sigma_{0}t},

for
t\geq 0
. If the initial states satisfy
\left(u+v\right)\left(0\right)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{\sigma_{0}}
, then
\left(u+v\right)\left(t\right)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{\sigma_{0}}
and

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}N\left(t\right)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{\sigma_{0}}.

As a result, region
D
is positively invariant and attracting within
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{2}
. Therefore, it is sufficient to consider the
dynamics of the model within
D
as
D
is the biologically feasible region
of the system where the existence and uniqueness results hold for the system.

Existence of Equilibria and the Basic Reproduction Number

In this section, we aim to show the existence of equilibrium solutions for (

Theorem 1

System (

The positive equilibria of model (

Next, we study the existence conditions of an endemic steady state in the

Substituting this into the first equation yields

By applying the intermediate value theorem, there exists a real

holds. Using (

Then, there exists a unique real

, which implies the
existence of a unique

. Note that in

\underset{v\in\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\sigma_{0}},+\infty\right)}{\max}h\left(v\right)\leq h\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\sigma_{0}}\right)<0.

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}v_{t}\\

The Jacobian matrices corresponding to vectors

The second case if where

. The equilibrium

=\left(u^{\ast},v^{\ast}\right)

Lemma 1

Condition (

(14)

such that

\tilde{A}\left(\begin{matrix}u\\
v\end{matrix}\right)=(\tilde{A}_{1}u,\tilde{A}_{2}v)^{T}=(d_{1}\Delta,d_{2}\Delta)^{T},

and

D(\tilde{A})=D(\tilde{A}_{1})\times D(\tilde{A}_{2}),

with

We may write system (

)–(

) in abstract form in

where
U(t)=(u(t,.),v(t,.))^{T}
,
\Upsilon(U(t))=(F(U(t)),G(U(t)))^{T}

and

, in which

is the closure of

in
\mathcal{Y}
for

. The operator

is the infinitesimal
generator of an analytical semigroup of bounded linear operators
\left\{T(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathcal{X}
,
[
39
]
. Since

is locally Lipschitz in

, it follows that system (

)–(
3
) admits a unique local solution

for

]
.

Let us now consider the case

, which
can be formulated as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}-d_{1}\Delta u=\Lambda-\mu u-\lambda u\varphi\left(v\right),&\text{in }(0,T_{max})\times\Omega,\\

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0,&\text{on }(0,T_{max})\times\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(17)

We can easily observe that an upper solution exists for (

) for any
nonnegative function

. This upper solution is given by

N_{1}:=\max\left\{\frac{\Lambda}{\mu},\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\right\}.

Using the comparison principle from
[
41

in

is uniformly bounded in
[0,T_{max})\times\overline{\Omega}
. Integrating the equations of (
1
) over
\Omega

taking the sum of the resulting two identities yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(u(t,x)+v(t,x))dx

\displaystyle=\left|\Omega\right|\Lambda-\int_{\Omega}(\mu u(t,x)+\sigma v(t,x))dx,

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\Omega\right|\Lambda-\sigma_{0}\int_{\Omega}(u(t,x)+v(t,x))dx,

(18)

where
\sigma_{0}=\min\left\{\mu,\sigma\right\}
. From the
well–known Gronwall’s inequality, we have for

,

\int_{\Omega}(u(t,x)+v(t,x))dx\leq N_{2},

(19)

where
N_{2}>0
. Hence, for

\int_{\Omega}v(t,x)dx\leq N_{2}.

(20)

Following the footsteps of
[
42
, Theorem 3.1]
and using the

–equation, we conclude
\exists N_{3}>0
depending on

such that

over
[0,T_{max})\times\overline{\Omega}
. Therefore,
v

is also uniformly bounded in
[0,T_{max})\times\overline{\Omega}
. Using
the standard theory of semilinear parabolic systems described in
[
43
]
, we deduce

.

When

, system (
17
) becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}-d_{1}\Delta u=\Lambda-\mu u-\lambda u\varphi\left(v\right)\leq\Lambda-\mu u,&\text{in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\leq\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})},&\text{on }\Omega,\\

By means of the comparison principle we get

is the unique solution of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{d\omega}{dt}=\Lambda-\mu\omega,&t>0,\\

(22)

Consequently, for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, we have

Therefore, we have an upper bound for

independent of the initial conditions given a
sufficiently large

. By applying
[
44
, Lemma 3.1]
we find that
\left\|v(t,.)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
is also bounded by a
positive constant independent of the initial conditions for a large enough

.

Remark 1

From Lemma

, we obtain

\begin{matrix}\varphi(v)<\varphi^{\prime}(0)e^{v},&\forall v\in(0,\infty).\end{matrix}

(23)

Thus, we can establish the global existence of solutions for system (

)–(
3

) by following the work in
[

.

2.5
The Local PDE Stability

We have already established sufficient conditions for the local asymptotic
stability of system (
5
)–(
7
) in the ODE scenario. Let us
now examine the more general PDE case (
1
)–(
3
).

Theorem 3

For system (
1
)–(
3
):

(i) If
R_{0}\leq 1
, the disease–free equilibrium
\ E_{0}

=\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\mu},0\right)
is locally asymptotically stable.

(ii) If
R_{0}>1
, the positive constant endemic steady equilibrium
E^{\ast}

=\left(u^{\ast},v^{\ast}\right)
is locally asymptotically stable.

Proof 5

(i) In the presence of diffusion, the equilibrium point
E_{0}

=\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\mu},0\right)
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}d_{1}\Delta u+\Lambda-\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)-\mu u^{\ast}=0,\\
d_{2}\Delta v+\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)-\sigma v^{\ast}=0,\end{array}\right.

with Neumann boundaries

\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=0\ \text{on }\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\partial\Omega.

We denote the indefinite sequence of positive eigenvalues for the Laplacian
operator
\Delta
over
\Omega
with Neumann boundary conditions by
0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq...\nearrow^{+\infty}
. Note that the first eigenfunction is a constant,
which is why the corresponding eigenvalue is equal to zero. The
corresponding sequence of eigenfunctions is denoted by
\left(\Phi_{ij}\right)_{j=\overline{1,m}_{i}}
, where
m_{i}\geq 1

\mathtt{is}
the
algebraic multiplicity of
\lambda_{i}
. These functions are the solutions
of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta\Phi_{ij}=\lambda_{i}\Phi_{ij}\text{ \ in }\Omega,\\
\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{ij}}{\partial\nu}=0\text{ \ on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

The eigenfunctions are normalized according to

\left\|\Phi_{ij}\right\|_{2}=\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{ij}^{2}\left(x\right)dx=1.

The set of eigenfunctions
\left\{\Phi_{ij}:i\geq 0,j=\overline{1,m}_{i}\right\}
forms a complete orthonormal basis in
L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
. In order to establish the local asymptotic stability of the
steady states, we must examine all the eigenvalues of the linearizing
operator and if they all have negative real parts, then the solution is
locally asymptotically stable. The linearizing operator may be given by

\mathcal{L}\left(E_{0}\right)\mathcal{=}\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$d_{1}\Delta-\mu$&$-\lambda\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\varphi^{\prime}\left(0\right)$\\
$0$&$d_{2}\Delta+\lambda\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\varphi^{\prime}\left(0\right)-\sigma$\end{tabular}\right).

Using the same method from
[
1
]
, the stability of
E_{0}

reduces to examining the eigenvalues of the matrices

J_{i}\left(E_{0}\right)\mathcal{=}\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$-d_{1}\lambda_{i}-\mu$&$-\lambda\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\varphi^{\prime}\left(0\right)$\\
$0$&$-d_{2}\lambda_{i}+\lambda\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\varphi^{\prime}\left(0\right)-\sigma$\end{tabular}\right)\text{ for all }i\geq 0,

which are given for all
i\geq 0
by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}r_{i1}=-d_{1}\lambda_{i}-\mu,\\
r_{i2}=-d_{2}\lambda_{i}+\lambda\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\varphi^{\prime}\left(0\right)-\sigma.\end{array}\right.

Since the Laplacian eigenvalues are positive and in ascending order, both
r_{i1}
and
r_{i2}
clearly have negative real parts for
R_{0}\leq 1

leading to the local stability of
E_{0}

=\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\mu},0\right)
.

(ii) The second steady state
E^{\ast}=\left(u^{\ast},v^{\ast}\right)

satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}d_{1}\Delta u+\Lambda-\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)-\mu u^{\ast}=0,\\
d_{2}\Delta v+\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)-\sigma v^{\ast}=0,\\
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=0\ \text{on }\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

The corresponding linearization operator is

\mathcal{L}\left(E^{\ast}\right)=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$d_{1}\Delta-\lambda\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)-\mu$&$-\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi^{\prime}\left(v^{\ast}\right)$\\
$\lambda\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)$&$d_{2}\Delta+\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi^{\prime}\left(v^{\ast}\right)-\sigma$\end{tabular}\right).

Hence, the stability of
E^{\ast}
rests on the negativity of the real
parts of the eigenvalues of matrices

J_{i}\left(E^{\ast}\right)=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$-d_{1}\lambda_{i}-\lambda\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)-\mu$&$-\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi^{\prime}\left(v^{\ast}\right)$\\
$\lambda\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)$&$-d_{2}\lambda_{i}+\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi^{\prime}\left(v^{\ast}\right)-\sigma$\end{tabular}\right),\text{ for all }i\geq 0,

which is guaranteed if the trace and determinant of
J_{i}\left(E^{\ast}\right)
satisfies the conditions
tr(J_{i}\left(E^{\ast}\right))<0

and
\det\left(J_{i}\left(E^{\ast}\right)\right)>0,
for all
i\geq 0
. The trace of
J_{i}\left(E^{\ast}\right)
is given by

\displaystyle tr(J_{i}\left(E^{\ast}\right))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-d_{1}\lambda_{i}-d_{2}\lambda_{i}-\lambda\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)-\mu+\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi^{\prime}\left(v^{\ast}\right)-\sigma,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda_{i}\left(d_{1}+d_{2}\right)+tra(J\left(u^{\ast},v^{\ast}\right)).

Since
tr(J\left(u^{\ast},v^{\ast}\right))<0
, it follows that
tr(J_{i}\left(E^{\ast}\right))<0
for all
i\geq 0
. The determinant is
given by

\displaystyle\det\left(J_{i}\left(E^{\ast}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{1}d_{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}+\lambda_{i}\left[-d_{1}\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi^{\prime}\left(v^{\ast}\right)+d_{1}\sigma+\lambda\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)d_{2}+\mu d_{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\lambda\sigma\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)+\mu\sigma-\mu\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi^{\prime}\left(v^{\ast}\right),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{1}d_{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}+\lambda_{i}H_{0}+\det\left(J\left(u^{\ast},v^{\ast}\right)\right)\text{ for all }i\geq 0,

where

H_{0}=-d_{1}\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi^{\prime}\left(v^{\ast}\right)+d_{1}\sigma+\lambda\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)d_{2}+\mu d_{2}.

Using (
5
) and (
12
), it holds that

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-d_{1}\lambda u^{\ast}\frac{\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)}{v^{\ast}}+d_{1}\frac{\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)}{v^{\ast}}+\lambda\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)d_{2}+\mu d_{2},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{2}\left(\lambda\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)+\mu\right),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{2}\frac{\Lambda}{u^{\ast}}>0,

which leads to

\det\left(J_{i}\left(E^{\ast}\right)\right)=d_{1}d_{2}\lambda_{i}^{2}+\lambda_{i}H_{0}+\det\left(J\left(u^{\ast},v^{\ast}\right)\right)>0\text{ for all }i\geq 0.

Hence,
E^{\ast}
is locally asymptotically stable.

, which is why we have decided to treat the scenarios

Lemma 2

Given that

L\left(x\right)=x-1-\ln\left(x\right)\text{, for all }x>0,

where

, holds.

Proof 6

Condition (

) guarantees that
\frac{\varphi\left(v\right)}{v}

is a decreasing function for all

. We may separate the proof into two
regions:

1.

The first region is
v\geq v^{\ast}
. Since
\frac{\varphi\left(v\right)}{v}

\frac{\varphi\left(v\right)}{\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)}\leq\frac{v}{v^{\ast}}.

Note that (

Since

, (

) follows.

L\left(\frac{\varphi(v)}{\varphi(v^{\ast})}\right)\leq L\left(\frac{v}{v^{\ast}}\right)

for all

.

2.

The second region is

. Again, since

and given the non–decreasing nature of

As a result, we get

Since

3.1
Global Asymptotic Stability with
R_{0}\leq 1

To establish the global asymptotic stability of the disease free equilibrium

E_{0}
, we consider

\mathcal{V}_{\theta}\left(t\right)=\int_{\Omega}\left[uv+\frac{\theta}{2}\left(u-\tfrac{\Lambda}{\mu}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}v^{2}+\tfrac{\Lambda}{\sigma}v\right]dx,\text{ where }\theta>0,

as our candidate Lyapunov function. The aim of this subsection is to obtain

Theorem 4

If
R_{0}\leq 1
,
E_{0}
is a globally asymptotically stable
disease–free steady state for system (
1
)–(
3

\varphi^{\prime}\left(0\right)\ \leq\frac{\mu+\sigma}{\lambda\left(\theta\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}+\frac{\Lambda}{\sigma}\right)},

(26)

\theta\geq\frac{\left(d_{1}+d_{2}\right)^{2}}{4d_{1}d_{2}}.

(27)

Proof 7

To prove that
E_{0}=\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\mu},0\right)
is globally
asymptotically stable, we must show that
\mathcal{V}_{\theta}\left(t\right)
is a Lyapunov function. The positive definiteness of
\mathcal{V}_{\theta}\left(t\right)
is evident. Evaluating its derivative with

Substituting the partial derivatives
\tfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}
and
\tfrac{\partial v}{\partial t}
with their respective values from (
1
) and applying Green’s formula with the assumed Neumann boundaries leads to

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{V}_{\theta}\left(t\right)=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

where

I_{2}=-d_{1}\theta\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx-\mu\theta\int_{\Omega}\left(u-\tfrac{\Lambda}{\mu}\right)^{2}dx-\lambda\theta\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\varphi\left(v\right)dx+\lambda\theta\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\int_{\Omega}u\varphi\left(v\right)dx,

and

I_{3}=-d_{2}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla v\right|^{2}dx+\lambda\int_{\Omega}uv\varphi\left(v\right)dx-\sigma\int_{\Omega}v^{2}dx+\lambda\tfrac{\Lambda}{\sigma}\int_{\Omega}u\varphi\left(v\right)dx-\Lambda\int_{\Omega}vdx.

Taking the sum of terms
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
, and
I_{3}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{V}_{\theta}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-d_{1}\theta\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx-\left(d_{1}+d_{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla vdx-d_{2}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla v\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\lambda\left(1-\theta\right)\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\varphi\left(v\right)dx-\left(\sigma+\mu\right)\int_{\Omega}uvdx-\mu\theta\int_{\Omega}\left(u-\tfrac{\Lambda}{\mu}\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\sigma\int_{\Omega}v^{2}dx+\lambda\left(\theta\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}+\tfrac{\Lambda}{\sigma}\right)\int_{\Omega}u\varphi\left(v\right)dx.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+J,

where

where

(28)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{V}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle-d_{1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\left(1-\frac{u^{\ast}}{u}\right)\nabla udx+\Lambda\int_{\Omega}\left(1-\frac{u^{\ast}}{u}\right)dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\left(1-\frac{u^{\ast}}{u}\right)u\varphi\left(v\right)dx

\displaystyle-\sigma\int_{\Omega}\left(1-\frac{v^{\ast}}{v}\right)vdx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+J,

where

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mu u^{\ast}\left(1-\frac{u}{u^{\ast}}\right)\left(1-\frac{u^{\ast}}{u}\right)dx+\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{u\varphi\left(v\right)}{u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)}-\frac{v}{v^{\ast}}\right)\left(1-\frac{v^{\ast}}{v}\right)dx

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ +\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)\int_{\Omega}\left(1-\frac{u\varphi\left(v\right)}{u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)}\right)\left(1-\frac{u^{\ast}}{u}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left[\mu u^{\ast}J_{1}+\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)J_{2}\right]dx,

(32)

where

J_{1}=\left(1-\frac{u^{\ast}}{u}\right)\left(1-\frac{u}{u^{\ast}}\right),

and

J_{2}=\frac{\varphi\left(v\right)}{\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)}-\frac{v}{v^{\ast}}-\frac{u\varphi\left(v\right)v^{\ast}}{u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)v}-\frac{u^{\ast}}{u}+2.

Since
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
can be rewritten in the forms

J_{1}=-L\left(\frac{u}{u^{\ast}}\right)-L\left(\frac{u^{\ast}}{u}\right),

(33)

and

J_{2}=-L\left(\frac{u^{\ast}}{u}\right)+L\left(\frac{\varphi\left(v\right)}{\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)}\right)-L\left(\frac{v}{v^{\ast}}\right)-L\left(\frac{u\varphi\left(v\right)v^{\ast}}{u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)v}\right),

(34)

it follows that

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=-\mu u^{\ast}\int_{\Omega}\left[L\left(\frac{u}{u^{\ast}}\right)+L\left(\frac{u^{\ast}}{u}\right)\right]dx-\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)\int_{\Omega}\left[L\left(\frac{u^{\ast}}{u}\right)+L\left(\frac{u\varphi\left(v\right)v^{\ast}}{u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)v}\right)\right]dx

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\lambda u^{\ast}\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)\int_{\Omega}\left[L\left(\frac{\varphi\left(v\right)}{\varphi\left(v^{\ast}\right)}\right)-L\left(\frac{v}{v^{\ast}}\right)\right]dx.

Taking advantage of the established positivity of
L
and applying Lemma
2
, we end up with

J\leq 0.

Hence,
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{V}\left(t\right)\leq 0
and, consequently,
\mathcal{V}
is non–increasing in time with
\mathcal{V}\left(t\right)=0

only at the steady state
E^{\ast}
. The global asymptotic stability of
E^{\ast}
follows from Lyapunov’s direct method’s.

3.3
Important Remarks

It is important to note that, for system (
1
)–(
2
) in ODE
case, we have the following results:

(i)

For
R_{0}\leq 1
, the disease–free equilibrium
E_{0}
is
globally asymptotically stable. This easy to establish as
d_{1}=d_{2}=0

and, thus, it suffices to choose
\theta=1
in

(ii)

For

) is
satisfied, the endemic equilibrium
E^{\ast}

=\left(u^{\ast},v^{\ast}\right)

Analysis of the feedback particle filter with diffusion map based approximation of the gain
Sahani Pathiraja, Wilhelm Stannat

Abstract
Control-type particle filters have been receiving increasing attention over the last decade as a means of obtaining sample based approximations to the sequential Bayesian filtering problem in the nonlinear setting. Here we analyse one such type, namely the feedback particle filter and a recently proposed approximation of the associated gain function based on diffusion maps. The key purpose is to provide analytic insights on the form of the approximate gain, which are of interest in their own right. These are then used to establish a roadmap to obtaining well-posedness and convergence of the finite N N system to its mean field limit. A number of possible future research directions are also discussed.



Keywords.
nonlinear filtering, diffusion maps, Poisson equation, propagation of chaos, stochastic analysis

1.1
Diffusion map based approximation of the gain

The aforementioned properties of diffusion maps make it an ideal candidate for developing a sample based approximation of
\phi
.
[
TMM20
]
uses the diffusion map approach in a novel way by starting from the semigroup formulation of the Poisson equation (
1.5

[

]
for further details and derivations. The semigroup formulation of (

\displaystyle\phi=P_{\epsilon}\phi+\int_{0}^{\epsilon}P_{s}(h-\hat{h})ds,

(1.6)

where
\{P_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon\geq 0}
is the Markov semigroup associated to the weighted Laplacian
\Delta_{\rho_{t}}
, which must be approximated. In other words,
P_{s}
is the transition operator of a Langevin diffusion with invariant density given by
\rho_{t}
. Adopting

in the diffusion map method corresponds to a Markov chain approximating the Langevin diffusion of interest
[

]
, with anisotropic transition kernel denoted by

and

\displaystyle k_{\epsilon}(x,y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}(x,y)}{(\int\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}(x,z)\rho_{t}(z)dz)^{1/2}(\int\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}(y,z)\rho_{t}(z)dz)^{1/2}},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}(x,y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(4\pi\epsilon)^{-d/2}\exp(-\frac{1}{4\epsilon}|x-y|^{2}),

(1.7)

is a Gaussian kernel with bandwidth matrix
2\epsilon I_{d}
where
I_{d}
denotes the identity matrix of size
d
. This can be used to obtain the following approximation of the transition operator
P_{\epsilon}
, denoted by
{T}_{\epsilon}
,

\displaystyle{T}_{\epsilon}f(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int p_{\epsilon}(x,y)f(y)dy,

(1.8)

where

\displaystyle p_{\epsilon}(x,y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{k_{\epsilon}(x,y)\rho_{t}(y)}{k_{\epsilon}*\rho_{t}(x)},

where we use the shorthand notation
k_{\epsilon}*\rho_{t}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int k_{\epsilon}(x,y)\rho_{t}(y)dy
, also in the remainder of the article.
Additionally, we will use the following equivalent simplified form throughout the article

\displaystyle p_{\epsilon}(x,y)=\frac{g_{\epsilon}(x,y)\nu_{\epsilon}(y)}{g_{\epsilon}*\nu_{\epsilon}(x)},

(1.9)

where
g_{\epsilon}(x,y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\exp(-\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert}^{2})
is the unnormalised Gaussian kernel and

\displaystyle\nu_{\epsilon}(y)=\frac{\rho_{t}(y)}{(g_{\epsilon}*\rho_{t}(y))^{1/2}}.

(1.10)

An infinite dimensional, diffusion map based approximation of
\phi

\displaystyle\phi_{\epsilon}={T}_{\epsilon}\phi_{\epsilon}+\epsilon(h-\hat{h}_{\epsilon});\quad\hat{h}_{\epsilon}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int h(x)\pi_{\epsilon}(x)dx,

(1.11)

where

. Now given

\displaystyle T_{ij}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{k_{\epsilon}^{N}(X^{i},X^{j})}{\sum_{l}{k}_{\epsilon}^{N}(X^{i},X^{l})},

(1.12a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{q}_{\epsilon}(X^{i},X^{j})}{{\sum_{l}\tilde{q}_{\epsilon}(X^{i},X^{l})}},

(1.12b)

where

\displaystyle\quad{k}_{\epsilon}^{N}(X^{i},X^{j})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}(X^{i},X^{j})}{(\sum_{l}\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}(X^{j},X^{l}))^{1/2}(\sum_{l}\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}(X^{i},X^{l}))^{1/2}},

\displaystyle\tilde{q}_{\epsilon}(X^{i},X^{j})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{g_{\epsilon}(X^{i},X^{j})}{(\sum_{l}g_{\epsilon}(X^{j},X^{l}))^{1/2}}.

In the remainder of the article, we will adopt the shorthand notation

\displaystyle s_{\epsilon}^{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{l}\tilde{q}_{\epsilon}(X^{i},X^{l}).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\phi_{\epsilon}^{(N)}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\sum_{i}\tilde{q}_{\epsilon}(x,X^{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{q}_{\epsilon}(x,X^{j})\Phi_{j}+\epsilon(h(x)-\hat{h}^{N}),

Problem statement

2
Analytic properties of
\bar{K}_{\epsilon}(x,\mu)

This section presents some insights on properties of the mean field form of the gain,
\bar{K}_{\epsilon}
, in unbounded domains by making use of a log-concavity assumption. They are mainly of independent interest and maybe utilised for obtaining propagation of chaos type results as in Section
4
without the use of stopping times. The exponential tail decay of the conditional density ensured by the aforementioned assumption plays an important role in uniformly bounding the gain in the spatial argument, which is a rather remarkable result given the covariance type structure of the gain and without boundedness assumptions on
h
. More specifically, in this section we assume the following. Note that we use the notation
\succcurlyeq
to indicate a matrix inequality, i.e.

Assumption 2.1
.

The conditional density of the mean field process (
1.20
) at time

is strongly log-concave, i.e. it takes the form
\exp(-\mathcal{V}_{t}(x))
with

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}^{2}\mathcal{V}_{t}\succcurlyeq c_{v}I,

for some
c_{v}>0

Lemma 2.1
.

Log-concavity of the transition kernel
Under Assumption
2.1
, it holds that the transition kernel
p_{\epsilon}(x,y)
at time
t
is strongly log-concave in

for all

. That is,
p_{\epsilon}(x,y)\propto\exp(-\mathcal{G}_{t}(x,y))
with

\displaystyle\nabla_{y}^{2}\mathcal{G}_{t}\succcurlyeq c_{g}I,

where

\displaystyle c_{g}=\frac{1}{4\epsilon}+c_{v}.

(2.1)

Proof.

The unnormalised form of

can be rewritten as

\displaystyle g_{\epsilon}(x,y)\nu_{\epsilon}(y)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4\epsilon}|x-y|^{2}-\mathcal{V}_{t}(y)-\frac{1}{2}\log(g_{\epsilon}*\bar{\rho}_{t}(y))\right)

The following lipschitz property of

will be used extensively.

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert g_{\epsilon}(x_{1},y_{1})-g_{\epsilon}(x_{2},y_{2})\right\rVert}\leq K_{g}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x_{1}-y_{1}-x_{2}+y_{2}\right\rVert},

Lemma A.1
.

Consider
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
where

. It holds that

is globally lipschitz, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f(z)-f(y)\right\rVert}\leq K_{f}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert z-y\right\rVert},

where

\displaystyle K_{f}=\sqrt{\exp(-2)d^{2}+d(1-\exp(-2))}.

(1.2)

Proof.

We start with
f_{j}(z)=z_{j}\exp(-\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert z\right\rVert}^{2})
which is a scalar function. Then

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\nabla_{j}f_{j}\right\rVert}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\exp(-\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert z\right\rVert}^{2})\mathinner{\!\left\lVert 2\epsilon-z_{j}^{2}\right\rVert}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\exp(-\frac{1}{4\epsilon}z_{j}^{2})\mathinner{\!\left\lVert 2\epsilon-z_{j}^{2}\right\rVert}

\displaystyle\leq 1,

and also

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\nabla_{i}f_{j}\right\rVert}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert z_{j}z_{i}\right\rVert}\exp(-\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert z\right\rVert}^{2}),\quad i\neq j

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert z_{i}\right\rVert}\exp(-\frac{1}{4\epsilon}z_{i}^{2})\mathinner{\!\left\lVert z_{j}\right\rVert}\exp(-\frac{1}{4\epsilon}z_{j}^{2})

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f(z)-f(y)\right\rVert}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j}(f_{j}(y)-f_{j}(z))^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert y-z\right\rVert}^{2}\sum_{j}\left[(\nabla_{j}f_{j})^{2}+\sum_{i\neq j}(\nabla_{i}f_{j})^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\exp(-2)d^{2}+d(1-\exp(-2))}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert y-z\right\rVert}.

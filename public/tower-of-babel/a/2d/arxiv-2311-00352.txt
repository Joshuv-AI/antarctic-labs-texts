1.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a class of groups. The group
G
is said to be meta-
\mathfrak{X}
-Hamiltonian if any of its non-
\mathfrak{X}
subgroups is normal. Also, we say that
G
is para-
\mathfrak{X}
-Hamiltonian if
G
is a non-
\mathfrak{X}
group and every non-normal subgroup of
G
is either an
\mathfrak{X}
-group or a minimal non-
\mathfrak{X}
-group. If the class
\mathfrak{X}
is subgroup closed, then the non-
\mathfrak{X}
condition is necessary.

For a class
\mathfrak{X}
of groups, a group
G
is said to be minimal non-
\mathfrak{X}
if it is not an
\mathfrak{X}
-group but all its proper subgroups belong to
\mathfrak{X}
. Also,
G
is called biminimal non-
\mathfrak{X}
if it is neither an
\mathfrak{X}
-group nor a minimal non-
\mathfrak{X}
-group, but each proper subgroup of
G
either belongs to
\mathfrak{X}
or is a minimal non-
\mathfrak{X}
-group. Para-
\mathfrak{X}
-Hamiltonian groups are a natural extention of biminimal non-
\mathfrak{X}
groups.

If
\mathfrak{A}
is the class of Abelian groups, then the class of meta-
\mathfrak{A}
-Hamiltonian is called metahamiltonian and the class of para-
\mathfrak{A}
-Hamiltonian groups is called parahamiltonian.
The data about metahamiltonian groups is not very precise. These groups were introduced by Romalis in 1962. Then Romalis and Seskin continued the study of these groups.
They proved that if a metahamiltonian group is soluble, then its derived length is at most three and its commutator subgroup is finite of prime power order
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
. Then, these groups attracted the attention of many researchers, as de Giovanni and others. For a number of propertises pertaining metahamiltonian groups one can see
[
4
,
10
]
.

Some researchers have extended the metahamiltonian groups in a different direction. Instead of changing the class of subgroups that are not normal, they changed the normality condition of non-abelian subgroups. In their study,
[
13
]
explored the structure of groups whose non-abelian subgroups are subnormal. Similarly,
[
5
]
focused on groups whose subgroups are either abelian or pronormal. Lastly,
[
6
]
examined locally finite simple groups whose non-abelian subgroups are pronormal.

A group
G
is called locally graded if every non-trivial finitely generated subgroup of
G
contains a proper subgroup of finite index. The results of Romalis and Sesekin
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
show that a locally graded metahamiltonian group is necessarily soluble. Therefore, it has a finite commutator subgroup. Also, an infinite locally graded group whose proper subgroups are metahamiltonian is metahamiltonian
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
. However, the metahamiltonian groups are not necessarily soluble. For example, Tarski groups, i.e. infinite simple groups whose proper non-trivial subgroups have prime order.

Atlihan and de Giovanni showed that every locally graded biminimal non-abelian group is finite and its order is divisible by at most three prime numbers
[
1
, Theorem 1]
. They also showed that
G/\Phi(G)\cong A_{5}
, for any finite insoluble biminimal non-abelian group
G
. We will show that such a group is necessarily isomorphic to
A_{5}
(Corollary
3.8
).

Parahamiltonian groups were first introduced by Atlihan and de Giovanni
[
1
]
. They proved that the commutator subgroup of a locally graded parahamiltonian group is finite
[
1
, Lemma 5]
. They also showed that a parahamiltonian group which is locally graded and insoluble is finite and
\pi(G)=\{2,3,5\}
. We will show that such a group is necessarily isomorphic to
A_{5}
(Corollary
3.9
).

Let
\mathfrak{Nil}
be the class of nilpotent groups. The most natural case for metahamiltonian and parahamiltonian generalization are meta-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian and para-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian groups. In this paper we investigate the class of finitely generated meta-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamilton and para-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamilton groups. It will be proved that every finitely generated locally graded biminimal non-nilpotent group is finite and its order is divisible by at most three prime numbers and every finite insoluble biminimal non-nilpotent group is isomorphic to
A_{5}
. Also, any locally graded insoluble para-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian group is isomorphic to
A_{5}
or
\SL(2,5)
, if it has a finite minimal non-nilpotent subgroup. In particular, this applies to any para-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian group that is insoluble and finite. In the case of locally graded meta-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian groups, we show that they are soluble. Some of our techniques are taken from
[
1
]
.

The finite case

Let
G

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group such that
G/\Phi(G)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, where
p
and
q
are distinct primes. Then, for natural numbers
m
and
n
,
G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{q^{n}}
.

Proof.

Let
P\in\Syl_{p}(G)
and
Q\in\Syl_{q}(G)
. Since
P\Phi(G)\trianglelefteq G
, by Frattini argument,
P\trianglelefteq G
, so
\Phi(P)\leqslant P\cap\Phi(G)
. If
\Phi(P)\neq P\cap\Phi(G)
, then there is a maximal subgroup
M
of
P
such that
P=M(P\cap\Phi(G))
and so
G=PQ=M(P\cap\Phi(G))Q
, thus
G=MQ
, a contradiction. Therefore
\Phi(P)=P\cap\Phi(G)
and so
P/\Phi(P)\cong\dfrac{P\Phi(G)}{\Phi(G)}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, hence
P
is cyclic.

Since
\frac{Q}{Q\cap\Phi(G)}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{q}
,
Q\cap\Phi(G)
is maximal in
Q
, so
\Phi(Q)\leqslant Q\cap\Phi(G)
. Now for any maximal subgroup
M
of
Q
,
PM
is maximal in
G
. As

Q\cap\Phi(G)\leqslant Q\cap PM=(P\cap Q)M=M,

then
Q\cap\Phi(G)\leqslant\Phi(Q)
. Therefore,
\Phi(Q)=Q\cap\Phi(G)
. So, similar to the previous case,
Q/\Phi(Q)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{q}
and so
Q
is cyclic. Hence, for some
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,

G=PQ\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{q^{m}}\;\&\;\Phi(G)=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n-1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q^{m-1}}.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
\Phi(G)\leqslant H\leqslant G
. If
H
and
H/\Phi(G)
are minimal non-nilpotent, then
\Phi(G)\leqslant\Phi(H)
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a maximal subgroup of
H
. If
\Phi(G)\nleqslant M
, then
H=M\Phi(G)
and so
H/\Phi(G)\cong M/(M\cap\Phi(G))
is nilpotent, which is a contradiction. So
\Phi(G)\leqslant\Phi(H)
.
∎

Let

Suppose that

,
\Phi(G)
is contained both in

and in

and

are noncyclic, therefore,
\Phi(H)=\Phi(K)=\Phi(G)

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a finite minimal simple group. If
G
is biminimal non-nilpotent, then
G\cong A_{5}
.

Proof.

Assume that
G\cong\PSL(2,2^{p})
with

prime. According to Theorem
2.1
,
G
has two dihedral subgroups of orders
2(2^{p}-1)
and
2(2^{p}+1)
. These subgroups are minimal non-nilpotent if both

and
2^{p}+1
are prime. Since
2^{p}+1
is prime whenever

, therefore
G\cong\PSL(2,4)\cong A_{5}
.

Suppose that
G\cong\PSL(2,3^{p})
for an odd prime

. Similar to the previous case,
G
has two dihedral subgroups of orders
3^{p}-1
and
3^{p}+1
. So both
(3^{p}\pm 1)/2
are either prime or a power of
2
greater than
8
. Let

(3^{p}+1)/2=2^{\ell}
where
\ell\geq 3
. So
q=3^{p}=2^{\ell+1}-1
. Therefore

q^{2}=3^{2p}=2^{2\ell+2}-2^{\ell+2}+1\equiv 1\pmod{16}.

Thus by Theorem

(iv),

has a subgroup isomorphic to

that is neither nilpotent nor minimal non-nilpotent. Similarly, if

is a power of

S_{4}
. So both
(3^{p}\pm 1)/2
are prime. According to the relation

(3^{p}+1)/2=(3^{p}-1)/2+1

necessarily
(3^{p}-1)/2=2
, a contradiction.

Now suppose that
G\cong\PSL(2,p)
for a prime
p
. Similar to the above argument,
p\pm 1
is not a power of
2
greater than
8
, so
(p\pm 1)/2
is necessarily prime. Since
(p+1)/2=(p-1)/2+1

and so

, which contradicts

(c).

Using the
{\rm GAP}
software
[
23
]
, it is determined that
G\not\cong\PSL(3,3)
. Finally assume that

, where

is an odd prime. By Theorem

,

has two cyclic Hall subgroups

and

of odd orders

and

, respectively, where

. Also,
\mathcal{N}_{G}(U_{i})
for

is a Frobenius group with kernel

, which is non-nilpotent. (The center of a Frobenius group is trivial). Since

\mathcal{N}_{G}(U_{i})

Theorem 3.6

Let

or

Theorem 3.10

Let

be a finite meta-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian group. Then

Although the proof for the above theorem is not necessary, we can provide a straightforward proof.

Since every finite minimal non-nilpotent group is soluble, it can be assumed that

4.
Para-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian and biminimal non-nilpotent groups

Let
G
be a group and

G=\gamma_{1}(G)\geq\gamma_{2}(G)\geq\cdots\geq\gamma_{n}(G)\geq\cdots,

be its lower central series.The intersection
\bigcap_{i\geq 1}\gamma_{i}(G)
is denoted by

Minimal non-nilpotent groups and especially groups which are not nilpotent of class

but whose proper subgroups are nilpotent of class

[
16
]
. The following two important theorems are about finitely generated non-nilpotent groups.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
16
, Lemma 3.2]

Let G be a finitely generated non-nilpotent group all of whose proper subgroups are locally nilpotent. If
G/\gamma_{\infty}(G)
is non-trivial, then
G
is finite.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
16
, Theorem 3.3]

The Frattini factor group of an infinite finitely generated minimal non-nilpotent group is non-abelian and simple.

If the finitely generated minimal non-nilpotent group
G
has a subgroup of finite index, then it has a normal subgroup
N
of finite index. Since
G/N
is finite and soluble, therefore
\gamma_{2}(G)
will be a proper subgroup of
G
and then
G
will be finite by Theorem

. Then, the infinite finitely generated minimal non-nilpotent group has no subgroups of finite index, therefore, it is not locally graded. Thus, we will have the following result.

Corollary 4.3
.

If a finitely generated minimal non-nilpotent group has a subgroup of finite index, then it is finite. In particular, every locally graded finitely generated minimal non-nilpotent group is finite.

Smith
[

, Theorem 1]
showed that
if every proper subgroup of a torsion free locally nilpotent group is nilpotent, then the group must be finite
. Also he proved that,
if

is a soluble minimal non-nilpotent group that has no maximal subgroup, then

has no proper subgroup of finite index

[

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated locally graded group. If

is infinite, then the finite residual

has infinite index.

Proof.

Assume that

is finite. Therefore,

is also finite and so

, as

is finite, then

has finitely many conjugates in

. Hence

is finite, and so

is finite.
∎

If a group

satisfies one of the following conditions, then its commutator subgroup

is finite:

(i)

is finite, for every subgroup

of

.

(ii)

[

.

(iii)

[

.

In case (iii), it follows from Corollary

that

is isomorphic to

.
In the following theorem, we extend the finiteness of

with an additional condition for para-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian groups.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
G
be a para-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian group. If

has a finitely generated minimal non-nilpotent subgroup

is locally graded, then

is finite. Furthermore, if either

is soluble.

Proof.

According to Corollary
4.3
,
X
is finite and hence is soluble. If

, then every subgroup of

is normal, hence

is soluble. Since

is finite, then
G^{\prime}
is finite. Now suppose that
X\ntrianglelefteq G
. As

is maximal in

, according to Lemma

,

is finite. Since every subgroup of

is normal,

is finite and hence

is finite. Suppose that

is infinite. According to Lemma

,

. Therefore,

is soluble and hence

is solvable.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let

be an insoluble para-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian group. If

has a finitely generated minimal non-nilpotent subgroup whose normal closure is locally graded, then

or

Proof.

Let

. According to Theorem

.
∎

Every locally graded biminimal non-abelian group is finite and its order is divisible by at most three prime numbers
[

. The similar result holds for finitely generated locally graded biminimal non-nilpotent groups.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated locally graded biminimal non-nilpotent group. Then
G
is finite, and its order is divisible by at most three prime numbers.

Proof.

Since
G
is locally graded, it has a proper normal subgroup
N
of finite index. It is obvious that
N
is finitely generated. If
N
is non-nilpotent, it is finite by Corollary
4.3
, therefore the statement holds. So assume that
N
is nilpotent. Then
N
is supersoluble by
[
17
, 5.4.6(ii)]
. Since
G
is a biminimal non-nilpotent group, it has a proper subgroup
M
that is minimal non-nilpotent. Also,
M
is a maximal subgroup of
G
. We show that
M
has finite index, thus it is finitely generated, and according to Corollary
4.3
,
G
will be finite.

It can be assumed that
N\nleqslant M
, then
G=NM
and hence
N\cap M\trianglelefteq G
. Let
H
be the maximal subgroup of
N
containing
N\cap M
. Since
N
is supersoluble,
H
has finite index in
N

[
17
, 5.4.7]
, also
N\cap M\leqslant H_{G}
, where
H_{G}={\rm Core}_{G}(H)
. If
N\cap M\neq H_{G}
then
G=H_{G}M
. Now for each
x\in N\backslash H
, there are
h\in H_{G}
and
m\in M
, where
x=hm
. Then
h^{-1}x=m\in N\cap M\leqslant H
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
N\cap M=H_{G}
and
M
has finite index.

The statement about prime divisors holds for any insoluble group by Corollary
3.7
. So suppose that
G
is a soluble group and
H
is a non-nilpotent subgroup of
G
. Therefore,
H
is minimal non-nilpotent and is maximal in
G
. As
|H|
has two prime factors and
|G:H|
is a power of a prime number, so the order of

has at most three prime factors.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
G
be a locally graded biminimal non-nilpotent group. If
G
has a finitely generated non-nilpotent subgroup, then
G
is finite.

Proof.

Let
X
be a finitely generated non-nilpotent subgroup of
G
. According to Theorem

, it can be assumed that

is a proper subgroup of

. Therefore,
X
is minimal non-nilpotent and so it is a maximal subgroup of

. By Corollary

,

is finite, therefore

is finitely generated. Now

is finite by Theorem

.
∎

Suppose that
G
is a locally graded infinite group whose subgroups are either nilpotent or minimal non-nilpotent. According to Corollary
4.9
, if
G
is not nilpotent, then it is neither finite nor does it have any non-nilpotent subgroups that are finitely generated. Therefore, it is locally nilpotent.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let

be an infinite locally graded group whose subgroups are either nilpotent or minimal non-nilpotent. Then

is locally nilpotent.

5.
Meta-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian groups

In this section, we will discuss some properties of meta-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian groups. Firstly, we will prove that if a finitely generated meta-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian group is non-nilpotent but has a nilpotent subgroup of finite index, then it is soluble with a finite derived subgroup. Secondly, we will show that any locally graded meta-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian group is soluble.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G
be a biminimal non-nilpotent locally nilpotent group. Then
G
is meta-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian.

Proof.

If
X
is a proper non-nilpotent subgroup of
G
, then
X
is a maximal subgroup of
G
. Hence
\Phi(G)\leqslant X
. On the other hand, since
G
is a locally nilpotent,
G^{\prime}\leqslant X
and consequently
X
is normal.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G
be a non-nilpotent finitely generated meta-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian group. If every proper subgroup of
G
is locally nilpotent, then either
G
is finite or
G/\Phi(G)
is an infinite simple group.

Proof.

If
G
is an infinite minimal non-nilpotent group, then
G/\Phi(G)
is non-abelian and simple according to Theorem
4.2
. Otherwise
G
has a non-nilpotent proper subgroup
X
. Since
G/X
is a Dedekind group, then
\gamma_{3}(G)\leqslant X
. Therefore,
G/\gamma_{\infty}(G)
is non-trivial. Now the statement follows from Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Therefore, every non-nilpotent finitely generated locally nilpotent group whose non-nilpotent subgroups are normal, is either finite or infinite insoluble.

The following lemma holds with a completely similar proof from
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a class of groups that is subgroup closed. Assume that any non-
\mathfrak{X}
subgroup of
G
contains a finitly generated non-
\mathfrak{X}
subgroup. If every finitely generated subgroup of
G
is meta-
\mathfrak{X}
-Hamiltonian, then
G
is meta-
\mathfrak{X}
-Hamiltonian.

Proof.

Suppose that
X
is a non-
\mathfrak{X}
subgroup of
G
and
\mathfrak{N}
is the set of finitely generated non-
\mathfrak{X}
subgroups of
X
, by assumption
\mathfrak{N}
is non-empty. Now for any
Y\in\mathfrak{N}
,
\langle Y,g\rangle
is finitely generated for all
g\in G
, so
Y^{g}=Y
, then
Y\trianglelefteq G
. Also for any
g\in X
,
\langle Y,g\rangle\in\mathfrak{N}
, so
X=\langle Y\,|\,Y\in\mathfrak{N}\rangle\trianglelefteq G
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a class of groups that is subgroup closed and
G
be a meta-
\mathfrak{X}
-Hamiltonian group. Suppose that
J
is finite residual of
G
,
\mathfrak{N}
is the class of non-
\mathfrak{X}
subgroups of
G
and
I=\bigcap_{X\in\mathfrak{N}}X
.

(i)

\gamma_{3}(G)\leqslant I
and
I
is either an
\mathfrak{X}
-group or a minimal non-
\mathfrak{X}
group.

(ii)

If
G
is not an
\mathfrak{X}
-by-finite, then
\gamma_{3}(G)\leqslant I\leqslant J
.

Proof.

(i) If
\mathfrak{N}=\emptyset
, then
I=G
and there is nothing left to prove. Assume that
\mathfrak{N}\neq\emptyset
and
X\in\mathfrak{N}
. Since
G/X
is a Dedekind group,
\gamma_{3}(G)\leqslant X
. Therefore,
\gamma_{3}(G)\leqslant I
. Now suppose that
X\leqslant I
is a non-
\mathfrak{X}
-group, then
I=X
and so
I
is a minimal non-
\mathfrak{X}
-group.

(ii) Suppose that
G
is not an
\mathfrak{X}
-by-finite group. Let
\mathfrak{F}
be the class of subgroups of finite index of

. Therefore, none of the members of
\mathfrak{F}
is an

\gamma_{3}(G)\leqslant I\leqslant\bigcap_{N\in\mathfrak{F}}N=J.

∎

Notice here that, according to Lemma
5.4
(ii), non-nilpotent meta-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian groups are not residually finite.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
G
be a non-nilpotent finitely generated meta-
\mathfrak{Nil}
-Hamiltonian group. If
G
has a torsion-free nilpotent subgroup
H
of finite index, then
G\cong H_{G}\times K
, where
K
is finite non-nilpotent soluble and
H_{G}
is a free abelian group of finite rank. In particular,
G
is soluble and
G^{\prime}
is finite.

Proof.

Suppose that
H
is a torsion-free nilpotent subgroup of finite index in
G
. Hence
|G:H_{G}|
is also finite. In particular,
N=H_{G}
is finitely generated, torsion-free nilpotent. Suppose that
p
and
q
are primes such that
q>p>|G/N|
. Since
|N:N^{pq}|
is finite,
|G/N^{pq}|
is also finite. Since
|\bar{G}:\bar{N}|=|G/N|<p
, where
\bar{G}=G/N^{pq}
, then
|\bar{G}:\bar{N}|
and
|\bar{N}|
are relatively prime,
\bar{G}\cong\bar{N}\rtimes\bar{K}
where
\bar{K}\cong G/N
, by Schur-Zassenhaus Theorem.

Since Sylow subgroups of
\bar{N}
are characteristic,
{\bar{N}}^{p}\bar{K}
and
{\bar{N}}^{q}\bar{K}
are proper subgroups of
\bar{G}
. If, for example,
{\bar{N}}^{p}\bar{K}
is nilpotent, then
K\cap N^{p}
and
[K,N^{p}]
are both contained in
N^{pq}
. Since
N
is a finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group, according to
[
17
, 5.2.21]
,
\bigcap_{q>p}N^{pq}\leqslant\bigcap_{q>p}N^{q}=1
. Therefore
N^{p}\cap K=[K,N^{p}]=1
and hence
G\cong N^{p}\times K
is nilpotent, because
\bar{K}\cong K
. This is a contradiction. Therefore,
{\bar{N}}^{p}\bar{K}
and
{\bar{N}}^{q}\bar{K}
both are non-nilpotent and so are normal in
\bar{G}
. Hence
\bar{K}\trianglelefteq\bar{G}
, thus
\bar{K}
acts trivially on
\bar{N}
. Then both
K\cap N
and
[K,N]
are contained in
N^{pq}
and hence in
N^{q}
. So again, we will have
G\cong N\times K
. Since
G/K\cong N
is torsion-free Dedekind group, it is abelian. Therefore,
N
is a free abelian group of finite rank. By Theorem
3.10
,
K
is soluble.
∎

The group

is a simplest non-nilpotent example of Proposition

.

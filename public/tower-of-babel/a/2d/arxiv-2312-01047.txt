A New Random Reshuffling Method for Nonsmooth Nonconvex Finite-sum Optimization
Junwen Qiu junwenqiu@link.cuhk.edu.cn, Xiao Li lixiao@cuhk.edu.cn, Andre Milzarek andremilzarek@cuhk.edu.cn

Abstract
Random reshuffling techniques are prevalent in large-scale applications, such as training neural networks. While the convergence and acceleration effects of random reshuffling-type methods are fairly well understood in the smooth setting, much less studies seem available in the nonsmooth case. In this work, we design a new normal map-based proximal random reshuffling ( 𝗇𝗈𝗋𝗆 ​ - ​ 𝖯𝖱𝖱 \mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR} ) method for nonsmooth nonconvex finite-sum problems. We show that 𝗇𝗈𝗋𝗆 ​ - ​ 𝖯𝖱𝖱 \mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR} achieves the iteration complexity 𝒪 ( n − 1 / 3 T − 2 / 3 ) \mathcal{O}(n^{-1/3}T^{-2/3}) where n n denotes the number of component functions f ⁡ ( ⋅ , i ) f(\cdot,i) and T T counts the total number of iterations. This improves the currently known complexity bounds for this class of problems by a factor of n − 1 / 3 n^{-1/3} . In addition, we prove that 𝗇𝗈𝗋𝗆 ​ - ​ 𝖯𝖱𝖱 \mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR} converges linearly under the (global) Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition and in the interpolation setting. We further complement these non-asymptotic results and provide an in-depth analysis of the asymptotic properties of 𝗇𝗈𝗋𝗆 ​ - ​ 𝖯𝖱𝖱 \mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR} . Specifically, under the (local) Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz inequality, the whole sequence of iterates generated by 𝗇𝗈𝗋𝗆 ​ - ​ 𝖯𝖱𝖱 \mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR} is shown to converge to a single stationary point. Moreover, we derive last iterate convergence rates that can match those in the smooth, strongly convex setting. Finally, numerical experiments are performed on nonconvex classification tasks to illustrate the efficiency of the proposed approach.

Introduction

In this work, we consider the composite optimization problem

\min_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\penalty\ \psi(w):=f(w)+\varphi(w),

(1)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is differentiable but not necessarily convex and
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is a weakly convex, lower semicontinuous (lsc.), and proper mapping. Composite models of the form
Eq.

1
are ubiquitous in structured large-scale applications and stochastic optimization, including, e.g., machine learning (
Bottou 2010
;
Bottou et al. 2018
;
LeCun et al. 2015
), statistical learning and sparse regression (
Tibshirani 1996
;
Hastie et al. 2009
;
Shalev-Shwartz and Tewari 2011
), image and signal processing (
Combettes and Pesquet 2011
;
Chambolle and Pock 2011
), etc.

We are interested in the case where the smooth part of the objective function has a finite-sum structure, i.e.,
f
can be represented as follows

f(w):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(w,i).

(2)

In machine learning tasks, the number of component functions,
n
, is typically connected to the number of underlying data points and is often prohibitively large. In this scenario, computing the full gradient
\nabla f
is highly expensive or even impossible. Nonetheless, many classical approaches for
Eq.

1
require exact gradient information in each step, see (
Lions and Mercier 1979
;
Mine and Fukushima 1981
;
Attouch et al. 2010
;
Attouch et al. 2013
). In this paper, we adopt a stochastic approximation-based perspective and assume that the gradient of only one single function
f(\cdot,i)
is available at each iteration. Furthermore, the evaluation of the proximity operator of
\varphi
is assumed to be tractable (as is the case in many applications, (
Combettes and Wajs 2005
)). Our main objective is to develop a novel proximal random reshuffling algorithm for problem
Eq.

1
with convincing practical and theoretical properties.

1.1
Related Work and Motivation

Smooth problems.
When
\varphi\equiv 0
,
Eq.

1
reduces to a standard smooth optimization problem. In this case, the stochastic gradient descent method (
\mathsf{SGD}
) proposed in the seminal work (
Robbins and Monro 1951
) is the prototypical approach for solving
Eq.

1
. The core step of
\mathsf{SGD}
is given by:

w^{k+1}=w^{k}-\alpha_{k}\nabla f(w^{k},i_{k}),\quad\text{where $i_{k}$ is chosen randomly from $[n]$.}

(
\mathsf{SGD}
)

\mathsf{SGD}
has been studied extensively during the past decades; we refer to (
Chung 1954
;
Rakhlin et al. 2012
;
Nguyen et al. 2018
;
Gower et al. 2019
;
Gower et al. 2021
). In the strongly convex setting,
\mathsf{SGD}
was proven to converge to the optimal solution
w^{*}
almost surely with the rate
\|w^{k}-w^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{k})
, (
Chung 1954
). Moreover, the lower bounds derived in (
Nemirovskij and Yudin 1983
;
Woodworth et al. 2020
) indicate that the rate
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{k})
is tight for
\mathsf{SGD}
up to a constant. In the nonconvex case, convergence of
\mathsf{SGD}
can be expressed in terms of complexity bounds,

\min_{k=1,\ldots,T}\;\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(w^{k})\|^{2}]=\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/2}),

see, e.g.,
Ghadimi and Lan 2013
. Recently, empirical evidences in
Bottou 2012
;
Shamir 2016
;
Mishchenko et al. 2020
suggest that variants of
\mathsf{SGD}
with
without-replacement sampling
can attain faster convergence. Incorporating such sampling scheme leads to the so-called random reshuffling method (
\mathsf{RR}
), which is shown below:

\left[\begin{array}[]{l}\text{Set }w_{1}^{k}=w^{k}\text{ and generate a random permutation $\pi^{k}$ of $[n]$;}\\[4.30554pt]
\;\;\;\textbf{For}\;\;i=1,2,\ldots,n\;\;\textbf{do:}\quad w_{i+1}^{k}=w_{i}^{k}-\alpha_{k}\nabla f(w_{i}^{k},\pi_{i}^{k});\\[4.30554pt]
\text{Set }w^{k+1}=w_{n+1}^{k}.\end{array}\right.

(
\mathsf{RR}
)

For problems with the finite-sum structure
Eq.

2
,
\mathsf{RR}
exhibits superior theoretical guarantees compared to
\mathsf{SGD}
(
Gürbüzbalaban et al. 2021
;
Mishchenko et al. 2020
;
Nguyen et al. 2021
;
Li et al. 2023
). In
Gürbüzbalaban et al. 2021
, Gürbüzbalaban et al. provided the first theoretical guarantee for faster convergence of
\mathsf{RR}
. In particular, in the strongly convex case, the sequence of q-suffix averaged iterates is shown to converge to the unique optimal solution at a rate of
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
with high probability. Following this pioneering work, subsequent research has began to explore and understand the theoretical behavior of
\mathsf{RR}
, (
Haochen and Sra 2019
;
Nagaraj et al. 2019
;
Mishchenko et al. 2020
;
Nguyen et al. 2021
). In the nonconvex case and under a general variance bound, the authors in (
Mishchenko et al. 2020
;
Nguyen et al. 2021
) established the complexity bound

{\min}_{k=1,\ldots,T}\;\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(w^{k})\|^{2}]=\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/3}T^{-2/3}),

for
\mathsf{RR}
, when a uniform without-replacement sampling scheme
1
1

1

Every
\nabla f(\cdot,i)
,
i\in[n]
has equal probability to be selected without-replacement.
is applied. In addition, in
Nguyen et al. 2021
, Nguyen et al
.
have shown
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla f(w^{k})\|=0
for step sizes
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
satisfying
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}=\infty
and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{3}<\infty
. This was strengthened to full asymptotic convergence,
\nabla f(w^{k})\to 0
, and, under the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) inequality, to last iterate convergence of the form
\|w^{k}-w^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(1/k)
,
k\to\infty
, in
Li et al. 2023
.

Composite problems.
The proximal stochastic gradient method (
\mathsf{PSGD}
)
is a standard stochastic approach for solving the composite problem
Eq.

1
. The update of
\mathsf{PSGD}
is given by:

w^{k+1}=\mathrm{prox}_{\alpha_{k}\varphi}(w^{k}-\alpha_{k}\nabla f(w^{k},i_{k})),\quad\text{where $i_{k}$ is chosen randomly from $[n]$.}

(
\mathsf{PSGD}
)

In contrast to
\mathsf{SGD}
, the convergence behavior of
\mathsf{PSGD}
is less understood, especially when
f
is nonconvex. In
Davis and Drusvyatskiy 2019
, Davis and Drusvyatskiy present one of the first complexity results for
\mathsf{PSGD}
in the nonconvex setting, i.e., it holds that

{\min}_{k=1,\dots,T}\ \mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(w^{k})\|^{2}]=\mathcal{O}(\textstyle{\sum}_{k=1}^{T}\alpha_{k}^{2}\,/\,\textstyle{\sum}_{k=1}^{T}\alpha_{k}),

(3)

where
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(w):=\lambda^{-1}(w-\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(w-\lambda\nabla f(w)))
is a basic stationarity measure for
Eq.

1
. Earlier studies of
\mathsf{PSGD}
for nonconvex
f
appeared in
Ghadimi et al. 2016
where convergence is shown if the variance vanishes as
k\to\infty
. Asymptotic convergence guarantees are discussed in (
Majewski et al. 2018
;
Duchi and Ruan 2018
;
Davis et al. 2020
;
Li and Milzarek 2022
) under (almost) sure boundedness of the iterates
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
or global Lipschitz continuity of
\varphi
. The easier convex and strongly convex cases have been investigated, e.g., in
Ghadimi et al. 2016
;
Atchadé et al. 2017
;
Rosasco et al. 2020
;
Patrascu and Irofti 2021
. If
f
is convex and
\psi
is strongly convex, then convergence in expectation,
\mathbb{E}[\|w^{k}-w^{*}\|^{2}]=\mathcal{O}(1/k)
, can be ensured,
see (
Rosasco et al. 2020
;
Patrascu and Irofti 2021
).

Naturally and in order to improve the performance of
\mathsf{PSGD}
, we may consider suitable combinations of without-replacement sampling schemes and
\mathsf{PSGD}
. An intuitive and straightforward extension of
\mathsf{RR}
is to perform additional proximal steps “
\mathrm{prox}_{\alpha_{k}\varphi}(\cdot)
” after each inner iteration
i=1,\dots,n
of
\mathsf{RR}
.
However, as highlighted in
(
Mishchenko et al. 2022
, Example 1)
, such a combination prevents the accurate approximation of the full gradient
\nabla f
after one epoch. Based on the this observation, Mishchenko et al
.
develop a different proximal-type
\mathsf{RR}
in
Mishchenko et al. 2022
. Unlike
\mathsf{PSGD}
, the proposed method performs a proximal step only once after each epoch. Therefore, we will refer to this approach as epoch-wise proximal random reshuffling method (
\mathsf{e}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
). The main iterative step of
\mathsf{e}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
is shown below:

\left[\begin{array}[]{l}\text{Set }w_{1}^{k}=w^{k}\text{ and generate a random permutation $\pi^{k}$ of $[n]$;}\\[4.30554pt]
\;\;\;\textbf{For}\;\;i=1,2,\ldots,n\;\;\textbf{do:}\quad w_{i+1}^{k}=w_{i}^{k}-\alpha_{k}\nabla f(w_{i}^{k},\pi_{i}^{k});\\[4.30554pt]
\text{Set }w^{k+1}=\mathrm{prox}_{n\alpha_{k}\varphi}(w_{n+1}^{k}).\end{array}\right.

(
\mathsf{e}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
)

In the nonconvex setting and under an additional bound connecting
\nabla f
and the stationarity measure
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda n}
, Mishchenko et al
.
derive the complexity bound

{\min}_{k=1,\ldots,T}\penalty\ \mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{G}_{\lambda n}(w^{k})\|^{2}]=\mathcal{O}(T^{-2/3}+n^{-1/3}T^{-2/3}).

(4)

for
\mathsf{e}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
, where
\lambda>0
is a step size parameter, see
(
Mishchenko et al. 2022
, Theorem 3)
.

1.2
Contributions

We design a new proximal random reshuffling method (
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
) for nonconvex composite problems. In contrast to existing stochastic proximal methods, our approach is based on the so-called
normal map
which swaps the order of evaluating
\nabla f
and the proximity operator
\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}
. We show that this exchanged order generally exhibits a better compatibility with without-replacement sampling schemes. In particular, similar to
\mathsf{PSGD}
but different from
\mathsf{e}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
,
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
performs proximal steps at each inner iteration which allows maintaining feasibility or the structure induced by
\varphi
. We now list some of our core contributions:

•

We derive finite-time complexity bounds for
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
in the nonconvex, nonsmooth setting. Specifically, under standard assumptions, we establish

\min_{k=1,\ldots,T}\|\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(w^{k})\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(T^{-2/3})\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\min_{k=1,\ldots,T}\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(w^{k})\|^{2}]=\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/3}\,T^{-2/3}),

(5)

where
T
counts the total number of iterations. The first result in
Eq.

5
is a worst-case deterministic complexity bound that is applicable to any shuffling scheme. The second result shown in
Eq.

5
holds for a uniform without-replacement sampling strategy. Both bounds match those of
\mathsf{RR}
in the nonconvex smooth setting, (
Mishchenko et al. 2020
;
Nguyen et al. 2021
). Compared to the complexity
Eq.

4
for
\mathsf{e}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
, the in-expectation bound for
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
has a better dependence on
n
. In addition, we prove that
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
can converge linearly to an optimal solution
w^{*}
under the (global) Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition and in the interpolation setting
\nabla f(w^{*},1)=\dots=\nabla f(w^{*},n)
.

•

We provide an in-depth asymptotic analysis of
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
. For diminishing step sizes
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
with
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}=\infty
and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{3}<\infty
, we derive
\|\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(w^{k})\|\to 0
and
\psi(w^{k})\to\bar{\psi}
.
To our knowledge, this is the first asymptotic convergence guarantee for a proximal-type
\mathsf{RR}
method. Moreover, when
\psi
satisfies the KL inequality, we establish convergence of the whole sequence of iterates, i.e.,
w^{k}\to w^{*}
. We further quantify the asymptotic rate of convergence of
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
when using polynomial step sizes
\alpha_{k}\sim k^{-\gamma}
,
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
. The derived rates depend on the local geometry of
\psi
around
w^{*}
which is captured by the KL exponent
\theta\in[0,1)
. In the case
\gamma=1
,
\theta\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
, we obtain

\|w^{k}-w^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(k^{-1}),\quad\|\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(w^{k})\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(k^{-2}),\quad|\psi(w^{k})-\psi(w^{*})|=\mathcal{O}(k^{-2}).

These rates match existing results in the smooth or strongly convex setting, cf
.
(
Gürbüzbalaban et al. 2021
;
Li et al. 2023
). Comparable asymptotic rates for
\mathsf{PSGD}
and
\mathsf{e}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
do not seem to be available in the general nonconvex case.

•

Finally, we present experiments that corroborate our theoretical findings on feasibility and potential linear convergence of
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
and we conduct numerical comparisons on a nonconvex binary classification and deep learning image classification problem.

2.1
Basic Nonsmooth Concepts and First-Order Optimality

We first recall several useful concepts from nonsmooth and variational analysis. For a function
h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(-\infty,\infty]
, the Fréchet (or regular) subdifferential of
h
at
x
is given by

\partial h(x):=\{g\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:h(y)\geq h(x)+\langle g,y-x\rangle+o(\|y-x\|)\;\;\text{as}\;\;y\to x\},

see, e.g.,
(
Rockafellar and Wets 1998
, Chapter 8)
. If
h
is convex, then the Fréchet subdifferential coincides with the standard (convex) subdifferential. The mapping
h
is said to be
\rho
-weakly convex if
h+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
,
\rho>0
, is convex. The
\rho
-weak convexity of
h
is equivalent to

h(y)\geq h(x)+\langle s,y-x\rangle-\frac{\rho}{2}\|{x-y}\|^{2},\quad\forall\penalty\ x,y,\;\;\forall\penalty\ s\in\partial h(x),

(6)

see, e.g.,
Vial 1983
;
Davis and Drusvyatskiy 2019
.

The first-order necessary optimality condition for the composite problem
Eq.

1
is given by

0\in\partial\psi(w)=\nabla f(w)+\partial\varphi(w).

(7)

A point satisfying this inclusion is called a stationary point and
\mathrm{crit}(\psi):=\{w\in\mathrm{dom\\
}(\varphi):0\in\partial\psi(w)\}
denotes the set of all stationary points of
\psi
. It is well-known that the condition
Eq.

7
can be equivalently represented as a nonsmooth equation,
Rockafellar and Wets 1998
,

F_{\mathrm{nat}}^{\lambda}(x):=\lambda\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(x):=x-\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(x-\lambda\nabla f(x))=0,\quad\lambda>0,

where
F_{\mathrm{nat}}^{\lambda}
is the so-called
natural residual
.
F_{\mathrm{nat}}^{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
are widely used stationarity measures in the analysis of proximal methods.
If
\varphi
is
\rho
-weakly convex, then the proximity operator
\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(w):={\argmin}_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\penalty\ \varphi(y)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|w-y\|^{2}
,
\lambda\in(0,\rho^{-1})
,
is
(1-\lambda\rho)
-cocoercive, i.e.,

\langle w-y,\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(w)-\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(y)\rangle\geq(1-\lambda\rho)\|{\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(w)-\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(y)}\|^{2},\quad\forall\penalty\ w,y,

(8)

see, e.g.,
(
Hoheisel et al. 2020
, Proposition 3.3)
. In particular,
\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}
is Lipschitz continuous with modulus
(1-\lambda\rho)^{-1}
. In this work, we use the
normal map
(
Robinson 1992
),

F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z):=\nabla f(\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z))+\lambda^{-1}(z-\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z))\in\partial\psi(\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z)),\quad\lambda>0,

as an alternative stationarity measure for
Eq.

1
. Here, the condition
F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z)\in\partial\psi(\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z))
follows directly from
z-\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z)\in\lambda\partial\varphi(\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z))
, see
(
Hoheisel et al. 2020
, Proposition 3.1)
. The normal map and the natural residual are closely related via

(1-\lambda\rho)\lambda^{-1}\|F_{\mathrm{nat}}^{\lambda}(\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z))\|\leq\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z)))\leq\|F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z)\|\quad\forall\penalty\ z,

(9)

where the first inequality can be shown applying
Eq.

8
and following the proof of
(
Drusvyatskiy and Lewis 2018
, Theorem 3.5)
. Throughout this work and motivated by
Eq.

9
, we will typically measure and express convergence in terms of the distance
w\mapsto\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(w))
.

2.2
Algorithm Design

We now discuss the core procedures of
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
. Let
\Pi=\{\pi:\pi\text{ is a permutation of }[n]\}
denote the set of all possible permutations of
[n]
. At each iteration
k
, a permutation
\pi^{k}
is sampled from
\Pi
. The algorithm then updates
w^{k}
to
w^{k+1}
through
n
consecutive normal map-type steps by using the stochastic gradients
\{\nabla f(\cdot,\pi^{k}_{1}),\ldots,\nabla f(\cdot,\pi^{k}_{n})\}
sequentially:

z_{i+1}^{k}=z_{i}^{k}-\alpha_{k}({\nabla{f}(w_{i}^{k},\pi^{k}_{i})+\lambda^{-1}(z_{i}^{k}-w_{i}^{k})})\quad\text{and}\quad w_{i}^{k}=\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z_{i}^{k}),\quad i=1,\dots,n.

(10)

Here,
\pi^{k}_{i}
represents the
i
-th element of the permutation
\pi^{k}
and the term
\nabla{f}(w_{i}^{k},\pi^{k}_{i})+\lambda^{-1}(z_{i}^{k}-w_{i}^{k})
approximates
F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z_{i}^{k})
by replacing the true gradient
\nabla f(w_{i}^{k})
with the component gradient
\nabla{f}(w_{i}^{k},\pi^{k}_{i})
. In each step of
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
, only one single gradient component
\nabla f(\cdot,\pi^{k}_{i})
,
i\in[n]
and one proximal operator is evaluated. The pseudocode of
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
is shown in
Algorithm

1
. In the case
\varphi\equiv 0
,
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
coincides with the original
\mathsf{RR}
method.

Based on
Eq.

10
, we can express the update of
z^{k}
compactly via

z^{k+1}=z^{k}-n\alpha_{k}F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})+e^{k},

(11)

where the error term
e^{k}
is given by

e^{k}:=-\;\alpha_{k}\left[{\sum}_{i=1}^{n}(F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k}_{i})-F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k}))+{\sum}_{i=1}^{n}(\nabla f(w^{k}_{i},\pi^{k}_{i})-\nabla f(w^{k}_{i}))\right].

(12)

By
Eq.

9
, the normal map
F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}
is a valid stationarity measure for the composite problem
Eq.

1
that mimics the role of the gradient mapping in smooth optimization. Hence, the procedure
Eq.

11
can be seen as a special proximal gradient-type method with errors.

2.3
Assumptions and Error Estimates

Assumption 1

(Functions & Sampling)
.

We consider the following basic conditions:

(F.1)

Each mapping
\nabla f(\cdot,i)
,
i\in[n]
, is Lipschitz continuous on
\mathrm{dom\\
}(\varphi)
with modulus
{\sf L}>0
.

(F.2)

The function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is
\rho
-weakly convex, lsc., and proper.

(F.3)

There are
f_{\mathrm{lb}},\varphi_{\mathrm{lb}}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
f(w,i)\geq f_{\mathrm{lb}}
and
\varphi(w)\geq\varphi_{\mathrm{lb}}
, for all
w\in\mathrm{dom\\
}(\varphi)
and
i\in[n]
. This also implies
\psi(w)\geq\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}:=f_{\mathrm{lb}}+\varphi_{\mathrm{lb}}
for all
w\in\mathrm{dom\\
}(\varphi)
.

(S.1)

The permutations
\{\pi^{k}\}_{k}
are sampled independently (for each
k
) and uniformly without replacement from
[n]
.

The conditions
(F.1)
and
(F.2)
are standard in nonconvex and nonsmooth optimization.
In
(F.2)
, we only assume
\varphi
to be weakly convex. The class of weakly convex functions is rich
and allows us to cover important nonconvex regularizations, including, e.g., the
student-
t
loss (
Aravkin et al. 2011
), the minimax concave penalty (
Zhang 2010
), and the smoothly clipped absolute deviation penalty (
Fan and Li 2001
).
Combining
(F.1)
and
(F.3)
and using the descent lemma, we can derive the following bound for the gradients
\nabla f(\cdot,i)
:

\|{\nabla f(w,i)}\|^{2}\leq 2{\sf L}[f(w,i)-f_{\mathrm{lb}}],\quad\forall\;i\in[n],

see, e.g.,
Nesterov et al. 2018
or
(
Li et al. 2023
, eqn. (2.5))
. Assumption
(S.1)
is a standard requirement on the sampling scheme utilized in random reshuffling methods,
Mishchenko et al. 2020
;
Mishchenko et al. 2022
;
Nguyen et al. 2021
.

We now establish first estimates for the error term
e^{k}
defined in
Eq.

11
. In particular, we provide a link between the errors
\{e^{k}\}_{k}
, the step sizes
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
, the variance
\sigma_{k}
,

\sigma_{k}^{2}:=\frac{1}{n}{\sum}_{i=1}^{n}\|{\nabla f(w^{k},i)-\nabla f(w^{k})}\|^{2},

(13)

and the normal map
F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}
. To proceed, let us also formally define the filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}_{k}
where
\mathcal{F}_{k}:=\sigma(\pi^{1},\dots,\pi^{k})
is the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the permutations
\pi^{1},\dots,\pi^{k}
. (We may also set
\mathcal{F}_{0}:=\sigma(z^{1})
). Then, it follows
z^{k+1},w^{k+1},e^{k}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}
for all
k\geq 1
. Let us further introduce
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\cdot]:=\mathbb{E}[\cdot\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{k-1}]
.

Lemma 2

(Error Estimates)
.

Let the conditions
(F.1)
–
(F.2)
be satisfied and let
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
and
\{z^{k}\}_{k}
be generated by
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
with
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{\rho})
,
0<\alpha_{k}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mathsf{C}}n}
, and
\mathsf{C}:=2[\frac{3{\sf L}+2\lambda^{-1}-\rho}{1-\lambda\rho}]^{2}
.

(a)

It holds that
\|{e^{k}}\|^{2}\leq\mathsf{C}n^{4}\alpha_{k}^{4}[\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}+\sigma_{k}^{2}\big]
for all
k\geq 1
.

(b)

Additionally, under
(S.1)
, it follows

\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|{e^{k}}\|^{2}]\leq\mathsf{C}n^{4}\alpha_{k}^{4}[\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}+n^{-1}{\sigma_{k}^{2}}]\quad\forall\penalty\ k\geq 1\quad(\text{almost surely}).

The proof of
Lemma

2
is presented in
Section

B.2
. Next, we provide an upper bound for the variance terms
\{\sigma_{k}^{2}\}_{k}
.

Lemma 3

(Variance Bound)
.

Let
\{w^{k}\}_{k}\subseteq\mathrm{dom\\
}(\varphi)
be given and assume that
(F.1)
and
(F.3)
are satisfied. Then, it holds that

\sigma_{k}^{2}\leq 2{\sf L}[f(w^{k})-f_{\mathrm{lb}}]\leq 2{\sf L}[\psi(w^{k})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]\quad\forall\penalty\ k.

Proof.

Using
\|a-b\|^{2}=\|a\|^{2}-2\langle a,b\rangle+\|b\|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla f(w^{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}{\sum}_{i=1}^{n}\|\nabla f(w^{k},i)\|^{2}-\frac{2}{n}{\sum}_{i=1}^{n}\langle\nabla f(w^{k},i),\nabla f(w^{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}{\sum}_{i=1}^{n}\|\nabla f(w^{k},i)\|^{2}\leq 2{\sf L}[f(w^{k})-f_{\mathrm{lb}}]\leq 2{\sf L}[\psi(w^{k})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}],

where the last line is due to
{\sum}_{i=1}^{n}\|\nabla f(w,i)\|^{2}\leq 2{\sf L}{\sum}_{i=1}^{n}[f(w,i)-f_{\mathrm{lb}}]=2{\sf L}n[f(w)-f_{\mathrm{lb}}]
and
f(w)-f_{\mathrm{lb}}\leq\psi(w)-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}
for all
w\in\mathrm{dom\\
}(\varphi)
.
∎

2.4
Merit Function and Approximate Descent

Descent-type properties serve as a fundamental cornerstone when establishing iteration complexity and asymptotic convergence of algorithms. In the classical random reshuffling method and in its proximal version
\mathsf{e}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
, descent is measured directly on the objective function
f
and
\psi
, respectively (
Mishchenko et al. 2020
;
Mishchenko et al. 2022
;
Nguyen et al. 2021
;
Li et al. 2023
). By contrast and motivated by the subgradient condition
F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z)\in\partial\psi(\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z))
, we analyze descent of
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
on an auxiliary merit function
H_{\tau}
that is different from
\psi
. The merit function
H_{\tau}
was initially introduced in
Ouyang and Milzarek 2021
.

Definition 4

(Merit Function)
.

Let the constants
\tau,\lambda>0
be given. The merit function
H_{\tau}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as follows:

H_{\tau}(z):=\psi(\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z))+\frac{\tau\lambda}{2}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z)}\|^{2}.

Provided that
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{4\rho})
, we will typically work with the following fixed choice of
\tau
:

\tau:=\frac{1-4\lambda\rho}{2(1-2\lambda\rho+\lambda^{2}{\sf L}^{2})}\quad

(14)

We now present a first preliminary descent-type property for
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
utilizing
H_{\tau}
. The detailed proof of
Lemma

5
can be found in
Section

B.3
.

Lemma 5
.

Suppose
(F.1)
–
(F.2)
are satisfied and let the iterates
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
and
\{z^{k}\}_{k}
be generated by
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
with
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{4\rho})
and
0<\alpha_{k}\leq\frac{1}{10{\sf L}n}
.
Setting
\tau=\frac{1-4\lambda\rho}{2(1-2\lambda\rho+\lambda^{2}{\sf L}^{2})}
, it holds that

H_{\tau}(z^{k+1})-H_{\tau}(z^{k})\leq-\frac{\tau n\alpha_{k}}{4}\left[{1+\frac{n\alpha_{k}}{\lambda}}\right]\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{8n\alpha_{k}}\|{w^{k+1}-w^{k}}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{n\alpha_{k}}\|{e^{k}}\|^{2}.

Based on
Lemmas

3
,
2
and
5
, we can establish approximate descent of
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
.

Lemma 6

(Approximate Descent)
.

Let
(F.1)
–
(F.2)
hold and let
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
,
\{z^{k}\}_{k}
be generated by
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
with
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{4\rho})
and step sizes
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
satisfying

0<\alpha_{k}\leq\frac{1}{n}\cdot\max\left\{\sqrt{2\mathsf{C}},\;10{\sf L},\;{4\mathsf{C}\lambda\tau^{-1}}\right\}^{-1}=:\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{n}.

Let
\tau
be chosen as in
Eq.

14
and let us define

\Delta(t):=2{\sf L}\mathsf{C}\exp(2{\sf L}\mathsf{C}t)[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]
.

(a)

The following descent-type estimate holds for all
k\geq 1
:

H_{\tau}(z^{k+1})\leq H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\frac{1}{8n\alpha_{k}}\|{w^{k+1}-w^{k}}\|^{2}-\frac{\tau n\alpha_{k}}{4}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}+\mathsf{C}n^{3}\alpha_{k}^{3}\sigma_{k}^{2}.

Furthermore, if
(F.3)
holds and we have
{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{3}<\infty
, then
\mathsf{C}\sigma_{k}^{2}\leq\Delta(n^{3}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}^{3})
.

(b)

Additionally, if the sampling scheme satisfies condition
(S.1)
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}_{k}[H_{\tau}(z^{k+1})]\leq H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\frac{\tau n\alpha_{k}}{4}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}+\mathsf{C}n^{2}\alpha_{k}^{3}\sigma_{k}^{2}\quad\text{(almost surely)}.

Moreover, under
(F.3)
and
{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{3}<\infty
, we have
\mathsf{C}\mathbb{E}[\sigma_{k}^{2}]\leq\Delta(n^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}^{3})
.

Proof.

Applying
Lemma

5
and
Lemma

2
(a), we obtain

\displaystyle H_{\tau}(z^{k+1})-H_{\tau}(z^{k})+\frac{1}{8n\alpha_{k}}\|{w^{k+1}-w^{k}}\|^{2}+\frac{\tau n\alpha_{k}}{4}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}

(15)

\displaystyle\leq\left[{\mathsf{C}n\alpha_{k}-\frac{\tau}{4\lambda}}\right]n^{2}\alpha_{k}^{2}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}+\mathsf{C}n^{3}\alpha_{k}^{3}\sigma_{k}^{2}\leq\mathsf{C}n^{3}\alpha_{k}^{3}\sigma_{k}^{2},

where the last inequality follows from
\alpha_{k}\leq\frac{\tau}{4\mathsf{C}\lambda n}
. Moreover, when
(F.3)
is satisfied, we can use
Lemma

3
in
Eq.

15
. This yields

H_{\tau}(z^{k+1})-H_{\tau}(z^{k})+\frac{1}{8n\alpha_{k}}\|{w^{k+1}-w^{k}}\|^{2}+\frac{\tau n\alpha_{k}}{4}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}\leq 2{\sf L}\mathsf{C}n^{3}\alpha_{k}^{3}[\psi(w^{k})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}].

(16)

Subtracting
\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}
on both sides of
Eq.

16
and noting
\psi(w^{k})\leq H_{\tau}(w^{k})
, this further implies

H_{\tau}(z^{k+1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}\leq(1+2{\sf L}\mathsf{C}n^{3}\alpha_{k}^{3})[H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]\quad\forall\penalty\ k.

Hence, using
1+x\leq\exp(x)
,
x\geq 0
, we can infer

\displaystyle H_{\tau}(z^{k+1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}

\displaystyle\leq{\prod}_{i=1}^{k}\left({1+2{\sf L}\mathsf{C}n^{3}\alpha_{i}^{3}}\right)[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]

\displaystyle\hskip-25.83325pt\leq\exp\left({2{\sf L}\mathsf{C}n^{3}{\sum}_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}^{3}}\right)[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]\leq\exp\left({2{\sf L}\mathsf{C}n^{3}{\sum}_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}^{3}}\right)[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}].

Thus, we have
\psi(w^{k})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}\leq H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}\leq\exp({2{\sf L}\mathsf{C}n^{3}{\sum}_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}^{3}})[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]
for all
k\geq 1
and it follows
\mathsf{C}\sigma_{k}^{2}\leq\Delta(n^{3}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}^{3})
.

We continue with the proof of part (b). Taking the conditional expectation in
Lemma

5
and using
Lemma

2
(b) and
\alpha_{k}\leq\frac{\tau}{4\mathsf{C}\lambda n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\hskip-17.22217pt\mathbb{E}_{k}[H_{\tau}(z^{k+1})]-H_{\tau}(z^{k})+\frac{\tau n\alpha_{k}}{4}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\tau n^{2}\alpha_{k}^{2}}{4\lambda}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{n\alpha_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|{e^{k}}\|^{2}]\leq\mathsf{C}n^{2}\alpha_{k}^{3}\sigma_{k}^{2}.

Taking the total expectation and applying
Lemma

3
, this yields

\mathbb{E}[H_{\tau}(z^{k+1})]\leq\mathbb{E}[H_{\tau}(z^{k})]-\frac{\tau n\alpha_{k}}{4}\mathbb{E}[\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}]+2{\sf L}\mathsf{C}n^{2}\alpha_{k}^{3}\mathbb{E}[H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}].

(17)

Mimicking the previous steps, we can infer
\mathbb{E}[H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]\leq\exp\left({2{\sf L}\mathsf{C}n^{2}{\sum}_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}^{3}}\right)[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]
and
\mathsf{C}\mathbb{E}[\sigma_{k}^{2}]\leq\Delta(n^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}^{3})
which finishes the proof. ∎

3
Iteration Complexity and Global Convergence

Based on the approximate descent properties of the merit function
H_{\tau}
in

Lemma

6
, we now establish the iteration complexity of
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
. Applications to constant and polynomial step sizes are presented in
Corollaries

8
and
11

Theorem 7

(Complexity Bound for
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
)
.

Let the conditions
(F.1)
–
(F.3)
hold and let
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
and
\{z^{k}\}_{k}
be generated by
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
with
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{4\rho})
and step sizes
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
satisfying
\alpha_{k}=\frac{\eta_{k}}{n}
and
0<\eta_{k}\leq\bar{\alpha}
. Then, the following statements are valid:

(a)

If
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\eta_{k}^{3}\leq\frac{1}{2{\sf L}\mathsf{C}}
, then, for all
k\geq 1
, it holds that

\min_{k=1,\dots,T}\;\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(w^{k}))^{2}\leq\frac{4+24{\sf L}\mathsf{C}{\sum}_{k=1}^{T}\eta_{k}^{3}}{\tau{\sum}_{k=1}^{T}\eta_{k}}\cdot[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}].

(b)

In addition, under the sampling condition
(S.1)
and if
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\eta_{k}^{3}\leq\frac{n}{2{\sf L}\mathsf{C}}
, it holds that

\min_{k=1,\dots,T}\;\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(w^{k}))^{2}]\leq\frac{4n+24{\sf L}\mathsf{C}{\sum}_{k=1}^{T}\eta_{k}^{3}}{\tau n{\sum}_{k=1}^{T}\eta_{k}}\cdot[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}].

Here,
the constants
\mathsf{C},\tau,\bar{\alpha}>0
are defined in
Lemma

2
,
Eq.

14
, and
Lemma

6
, respectively.

Proof.

Applying
Lemma

6
(a) with
\alpha_{k}=\frac{\eta_{k}}{n}
and dropping the term
\|w^{k+1}-w^{k}\|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle+\frac{\tau\eta_{k}}{4}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq[H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]+\Delta({\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}}\eta_{i}^{3})\cdot\eta_{k}^{3}

(18)

\displaystyle\leq[H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]+6{\sf L}\mathsf{C}[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]\eta_{k}^{3},

where the last line is due to
\Delta({\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}}\eta_{i}^{3})\leq 2{\sf L}\mathsf{C}\exp(1)[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]\leq 6{\sf L}\mathsf{C}[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]
. Summing
Eq.

18
from
k=1
to
T
, we obtain

\frac{\tau}{4}{\sum}_{k=1}^{T}\eta_{k}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}\leq[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]+6{\sf L}\mathsf{C}[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]{\sum}_{k=1}^{T}\eta_{k}^{3},

which further implies
\min_{k=1,\ldots,T}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]+24{\sf L}\mathsf{C}[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]{\sum}_{k=1}^{T}\eta_{k}^{3}}{\tau{\sum}_{k=1}^{T}\eta_{k}}
. Noticing
\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(w^{k}))\leq\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|
, this completes the proof of part (a).

To prove (b), we invoke
Lemma

6
(b) and set
\alpha_{k}=\frac{\eta_{k}}{n}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[H_{\tau}(z^{k+1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]+\frac{\tau\eta_{k}}{4}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]+\Delta(n^{-1}{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}}\eta_{i}^{3})\cdot n^{-1}\eta_{k}^{3}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]+6{\sf L}\mathsf{C}[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]n^{-1}\eta_{k}^{3}.

The rest of the verification is identical to part (a).
∎

We now study specific complexity results for
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
under constant step size schemes.

Corollary 8
.

Assume
(F.1)
–
(F.3)
and let
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
,
\{z^{k}\}_{k}
be generated by
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
with
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{4\rho})
and
\alpha_{k}\equiv\frac{\alpha}{n}
for all
k
.

(a)

If
\alpha=\frac{\eta}{T^{1/3}}
with
0<\eta\leq\min\{(2{\sf L}\mathsf{C})^{-\frac{1}{3}},\bar{\alpha}T^{\frac{1}{3}}\}
, then we have

\min_{k=1,\ldots,T}\;\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(w^{k}))^{2}\leq\frac{16[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]}{\tau\eta T^{2/3}}=\mathcal{O}({{T^{-2/3}}}).

(b)

Moreover, if the sampling condition
(S.1)
is satisfied and if
\alpha=\frac{\eta n^{1/3}}{T^{1/3}}
with
0<\eta\leq\min\{(2{\sf L}\mathsf{C})^{-\frac{1}{3}},\bar{\alpha}n^{-\frac{1}{3}}T^{\frac{1}{3}}\}
, it holds that

\min_{k=1,\ldots,T}\;\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(w^{k}))^{2}]\leq\frac{16[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]}{\tau\eta n^{1/3}T^{2/3}}=\mathcal{O}({{n^{-1/3}T^{-2/3}}}).

Proof.

We substitute
\eta_{k}=\eta/T^{1/3}
for all
k\leq T
and
\eta_{k}=0
for all
k>T
in
Theorem

7
(a). Observing
\eta^{3}\leq\frac{1}{2{\sf L}\mathsf{C}}
, this yields

\min_{k=1,\ldots,T}\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(w^{k}))^{2}\leq\frac{4+24\eta^{3}{\sf L}\mathsf{C}}{\tau\eta T^{2/3}}\cdot[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]\leq\frac{16[H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi_{\mathrm{lb}}]}{\tau\eta T^{2/3}}.

Similarly, setting
\eta_{k}=\eta n^{1/3}/T^{1/3}
when
k\leq T
and
\eta_{k}=0
when
k>T
in
Theorem

7
(b) allows completing the proof of part (b).
∎

Remark 10
.

\tau=\frac{1}{4}

4
Convergence under the Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) Condition

We now study convergence of
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
under the well-known Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) condition
Polyak 1963
;
Łojasiewicz 1959
;
Łojasiewicz 1963
;
Karimi et al. 2016
.

Definition 13

(PL condition)
.

The function
\psi
is said to satisfy the PL condition if there is
\mu>0

such that the following inequality holds:

\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(w))^{2}\geq 2\mu[\psi(w)-\psi^{*}]\quad\text{where}\quad\psi^{*}:=\inf_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(w).

(20)

The PL condition is a common tool in stochastic optimization; it provides a measure of the “strong convexity-like” behavior of the objective function without requiring convexity of
\psi
. Furthermore, under the PL condition, every stationary point
w^{*}\in\mathrm{crit}(\psi)
is necessarily an optimal solution to the problem
Eq.

1
. To proceed, we introduce the set of optimal solutions and the variance at optimal points:

{\cal W}:=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\psi(w)=\psi^{*}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{*}^{2}:=\sup_{w\in{\cal W}}\,\Big[\frac{1}{n}{\sum}_{j=1}^{n}\|{\nabla f(w,j)-\nabla f(w)}\|^{2}\Big].

(21)

In the following, under the PL condition and utilizing constant step sizes
\alpha_{k}\sim\alpha
, we show that
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
converges linearly to a
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2}\sigma_{*}^{2})
-neighborhood of
{\cal W}
.

Theorem 14
.

Let
(F.1)
–
(F.2)
hold and assume that
\psi
satisfies the PL condition with
\mathcal{W}\neq\emptyset
. Let
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
,
\{z^{k}\}_{k}
be generated by
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
with
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{4\rho})
and

\alpha_{k}\equiv\frac{\alpha}{n},\quad\alpha\leq\min\left\{\bar{\alpha},\;\nu^{-1},\;\frac{\mu\sqrt{\tau}}{2{\sf L}\sqrt{6\mathsf{C}}}\right\},\quad\nu:=\frac{\mu\tau}{3(1+\mu\tau\lambda)}.

(a)

For all
T\geq 1
, it follows
\psi(w^{T+1})-\psi(w^{*})\leq\exp(-T\nu\alpha)\cdot\frac{H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi(w^{*})}{1+\mu\tau\lambda}+\frac{6\mathsf{C}\alpha^{2}\sigma_{*}^{2}}{\mu\tau}
.

(b)

In addition, under the sampling condition
(S.1)
, we have

\mathbb{E}[\psi(w^{T+1})-\psi(w^{*})]\leq\exp(-T\nu\alpha)\cdot\frac{H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi(w^{*})}{1+\mu\tau\lambda}+\frac{6\mathsf{C}\alpha^{2}\sigma_{*}^{2}}{n\mu\tau},\quad\forall\penalty\ T\geq 1.

Here,
\mathsf{C}>0
is defined in
Lemma

2
,
\bar{\alpha}
is defined as in
Lemma

6
, and
\tau
is given in
Eq.

14
.

Remark 15
.

It is possible to express the convergence results shown in
Theorem

14
in terms of the distance,
\mathrm{dist}(w,\mathcal{W})
, to the set
\mathcal{W}
. Indeed, by
(
Bolte et al. 2017
, Theorem 5 and 27)
or
(
Karimi et al. 2016
, Theorem 2)
, the PL condition
Eq.

20
implies the following error bound or quadratic growth condition:

\mathrm{dist}(w,\mathcal{W})^{2}\leq 2\mu^{-1}[\psi(w)-\psi^{*}]\quad\forall\penalty\ w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(22)

If
{\cal W}=\{w^{*}\}
is a singleton (which is the case, e.g., if
\psi
is strongly convex), then
Theorem

14
ensures linear convergence of the iterates
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
to an
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2}\sigma_{*}^{2})
-neighborhood of the optimal solution
w^{*}
.
Hence, in the interpolation setting
\nabla f(w^{*},1)=\dots=\nabla f(w^{*},n)
, the iterates
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
generated by
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
converge linearly to
w^{*}
.

Remark 16

(Comparison with
\mathsf{e}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
)
.

As shown in
(
Mishchenko et al. 2022
, Theorem 2)
,
applying
\mathsf{e}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
with constant step size in the strongly convex case yields linear convergence to an
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2}\sigma_{\text{rad}}^{2})
-neighborhood of the optimal solution
w^{*}
. Here, the shuffling radius
\sigma_{\text{rad}}^{2}
is bounded by
\mathcal{O}(\sigma_{*}^{2})+\mathcal{O}(\|\nabla f(w^{*})\|^{2})
. Hence, even if
f(\cdot,1)=\cdots=f(\cdot,n)
(indicating
\sigma_{*}^{2}=0
), linear convergence of
\mathsf{e}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
can not be guaranteed since
\nabla f(w^{*})
generally does not vanish in composite problems
Eq.

1
. We illustrate this effect numerically in
Section

6.2
.

We now present the proof of
Theorem

14
.

Proof.

Recalling
F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})\in\partial\psi(w^{k})
, the PL condition implies
\|F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})\|^{2}\geq 2\mu(\psi(w^{k})-\psi^{*})
. In addition, using the definition of the merit function,
H_{\tau}(z)=\psi(\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z))+\frac{\tau\lambda}{2}\|F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z)\|^{2}
, we can infer

\psi(w^{k})-\psi^{*}\leq\frac{1}{1+\tau\lambda\mu}[H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\psi^{*}]\quad\text{and}\quad\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}\geq\frac{2\mu}{1+\mu\tau\lambda}[H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\psi^{*}].

(23)

Thus, applying
Lemma

6
(a) with
Eq.

23
and subtracting
\psi^{*}
, it follows

\displaystyle H_{\tau}(z^{k+1})-\psi^{*}

\displaystyle\leq\left[1-\frac{\mu\tau n\alpha_{k}}{2(1+\mu\tau\lambda)}\right][H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\psi^{*}]+\mathsf{C}n^{3}\alpha_{k}^{3}\sigma_{k}^{2}.

(24)

We now bound the variance
\sigma_{k}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}{\sum}_{i=1}^{n}\|{\nabla f(w^{k},i)-\nabla f(w^{k})}\|^{2}
in terms of
\sigma_{*}^{2}
, cf.
Eq.

21
. Setting
w^{*}:=\mathrm{proj}_{\cal W}(w^{k})
and using Young’s inequality,
(F.1)
,
Eq.

22
, and
Eq.

23
,
we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}{\sum}_{i=1}^{n}\|{\nabla f(w^{k},i)-\nabla f(w^{*},i)+\nabla f(w^{*},i)-\nabla f(w^{*})+\nabla f(w^{*})-\nabla f(w^{k})}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}{\sum}_{i=1}^{n}\Big[\|{\nabla f(w^{k},i)-\nabla f(w^{*},i)}\|^{2}+2\langle\nabla f(w^{k},i)-\nabla f(w^{*},i),\nabla f(w^{*},i)-\nabla f(w^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 34.44434pt+2\langle\nabla f(w^{k},i)-\nabla f(w^{*},i),\nabla f(w^{*})-\nabla f(w^{k})\rangle+\|\nabla f(w^{*},i)-\nabla f(w^{*})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 34.44434pt+2\langle\nabla f(w^{*},i)-\nabla f(w^{*}),\nabla f(w^{*})-\nabla f(w^{k})\rangle+\|\nabla f(w^{*})-\nabla f(w^{k})\|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 2{\sf L}^{2}\|w^{k}-w^{*}\|^{2}+2\sigma_{*}^{2}\leq 4{\sf L}^{2}\mu^{-1}[\psi(w^{k})-\psi^{*}]+2\sigma_{*}^{2}\leq\tfrac{4{\sf L}^{2}\mu^{-1}}{1+\tau\lambda\mu}[H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\psi^{*}]+2\sigma_{*}^{2},

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle H_{\tau}(z^{k+1})-\psi^{*}

\displaystyle\leq\left[1-\tfrac{\mu\tau n\alpha_{k}}{2(1+\mu\tau\lambda)}+\tfrac{4\mathsf{C}{\sf L}^{2}n^{3}\alpha_{k}^{3}}{\mu(1+\mu\tau\lambda)}\right][H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\psi^{*}]+2\mathsf{C}n^{3}\alpha_{k}^{3}\sigma_{*}^{2}

(25)

\displaystyle\leq\left[1-\tfrac{\mu\tau n\alpha_{k}}{3(1+\mu\tau\lambda)}\right][H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\psi^{*}]+2\mathsf{C}n^{3}\alpha_{k}^{3}\sigma_{*}^{2},

where we applied
\alpha_{k}^{2}\leq\frac{\mu^{2}\tau}{24\mathsf{C}{\sf L}^{2}n^{2}}
. Fixing
\alpha_{k}=\frac{\alpha}{n}
and unfolding the recursion
Eq.

25
, it follows

\displaystyle H_{\tau}(z^{T+1})-\psi^{*}

\displaystyle\leq[1-\nu\alpha]^{T}(H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi^{*})+2\mathsf{C}\alpha^{3}\sigma_{*}^{2}\cdot{\sum}_{k=0}^{T}[1-\nu\alpha]^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-T\nu\alpha)(H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi^{*})+2\mathsf{C}\nu^{-1}\alpha^{2}\sigma_{*}^{2},

(26)

where we used
(1-x)^{T}=\exp(T\log(1-x))\leq\exp(-Tx)
and
{\sum}_{k=0}^{T}(1-x)^{k}=\frac{1-(1-x)^{T+1}}{x}\leq\frac{1}{x}
in the last line. Thus, invoking
Eq.

23
, we can infer

\displaystyle\psi(w^{T+1})-\psi^{*}\leq\exp(-T\nu\alpha)\cdot\frac{H_{\tau}(z^{1})-\psi^{*}}{1+\mu\tau\lambda}+\frac{6\mathsf{C}\alpha^{2}\sigma_{*}^{2}}{\mu\tau}.

Part (b) can be shown in a similar way utilizing
Lemma

6
(b).

5
Convergence under the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) Condition

The PL condition is generally restrictive and the verification of
Eq.

20
is often not possible in many practical applications. To overcome these limitations, we now study the asymptotic behavior of
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
under a weaker Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) setting.

5.1
KL Inequality and Accumulation Points

We first present the definition of the KL inequality for nonsmooth functions; see, e.g.,
Attouch et al. 2010
;
Attouch and Bolte 2009
;
Attouch et al. 2013
;
Bolte et al. 2014
.

Definition 17

(KL property)
.

The function
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is said to have the KL property at a point
\bar{w}\in\mathrm{dom\\
}(\psi)
if there exist
c>0
,
\eta\in(0,\infty],
and a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{w}

such that for all
w\in U\cap\{w:0<|\psi(w)-\psi(\bar{w})|<\eta\}
, the KL inequality,

|\psi(w)-\psi(\bar{w})|^{\theta}\leq c\cdot\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(w)),

(27)

holds. Here,
\theta\in[0,1)
is the KL exponent of
\psi
at
\bar{w}
.

The KL inequality is satisfied for semialgebraic, subanalytic, and log-exp functions, underlining its broad generality; see
Kurdyka 1998
;
Bolte et al. 2006b
;
Bolte et al. 2006a
;
Bolte et al. 2007
. Particular applications include, e.g., least-squares, logistic and Poisson regression (
Li and Pong 2018
), deep learning loss models (
Davis et al. 2020
;
Dereich and Kassing 2021
), and principal component analysis (
Liu et al. 2019
). Intuitively, the KL inequality implies that
\psi
can be locally reparameterized as a sharp function near its critical points. It provides a quantitative measure of how quickly the function decreases as it approaches a stationary point which is controlled by the constants
c>0
and
\theta\in[0,1)
. In stark contrast to the PL condition, the KL inequality in
Definition

17
is a
local property
. It does not necessitate
\bar{w}\,(\in\mathrm{crit}(\psi))
to be a global solution of
Eq.

1
.

Table

2
, we list several popular and exemplary optimization models together with their corresponding worst-case KL exponent
\theta
.

.

and constant

5.2
Strong Convergence

Based on the KL property formulated in
Definition

17
, our aim is now to show that the whole sequence
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
converges to a stationary point of
\psi
.

Assumption 20

(KL conditions)
.

We consider the following assumptions:

(K.1)

The sequence
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
generated by
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
is bounded.

(K.2)

The KL property holds on
\mathcal{A}_{w}
, i.e.,
Eq.

27
holds for all
\bar{w}\in\mathcal{A}_{w}
.

Condition
(K.1)
is a typical and ubiquitous prerequisite appearing in the application of the KL inequality in establishing the convergence of optimization algorithms; see, e.g.,
Attouch and Bolte 2009
;
Attouch et al. 2010
;
Attouch et al. 2013
;
Bolte et al. 2014
;
Ochs et al. 2014
;
Bonettini et al. 2017
. Moreover,
(K.1)
can be ensured if
\psi
has bounded sub-level sets or
\mathrm{dom\\
}(\varphi)
is compact. As mentioned, condition
(K.2)
is naturally satisfied for subanalytic or semialgebraic functions
(
Kurdyka 1998
, Theorem Ł1)
.

Theorem 21

(Strong iterate convergence)
.

Assume
(F.1)
–
(F.3)
and
(K.1)
–
(K.2)
. Let
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
,
\{z^{k}\}_{k}
be generated by
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
with
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{4\rho})
and step sizes
0<\alpha_{k}\leq\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{n}
satisfying

{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}=\infty\quad\text{and}\quad{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\left[{{\sum}_{i=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}^{3}}\right]^{\xi}<\infty,\quad\text{for some }\xi\in(0,1).

(29)

We then have

{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(w^{k}))<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\|{w^{k+1}-w^{k}}\|<\infty.\vskip-4.30554pt

(30)

In addition, the whole sequence
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
converges to some stationary point
w^{*}\in\mathrm{crit}(\psi)
.

Theorem

21
not only ensures convergence of the iterates
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
, but, invoking
Eq.

30
, we also have
\min_{i=1,\ldots,k}\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(w^{i}))^{2}=\mathcal{O}(1/(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i})^{2})
. This bound is faster compared to the global complexity
\min_{i=1,\ldots,k}\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(w^{i}))^{2}=\mathcal{O}(1/(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}))
obtained in
Theorem

7
.

Proof sketch.
By
Theorem

12
, we conclude that every accumulation point of
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
is a stationary point of
\psi
. Based on the approximate descent property
Lemma

6
(a), the conditions in
Lemma

19
, and the KL assumptions
(K.1)
–
(K.2)
, we can show that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(w^{k}))\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\|F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})\|<\infty
and
{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\|{w^{k+1}-w^{k}}\|<\infty
. The latter result indicates that
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
is a Cauchy sequence and thus,
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
converges to some stationary point of the objective function
\psi
. The full proof and further details are presented in
Section

D.2
.

5.3
Convergence Rates under Polynomial Step Sizes

In this section, we establish convergence rates for the sequences
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
,
\{\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(w^{k}))\}_{k}
, and
\{\psi(w^{k})\}_{k}
under more specific step size strategies. We consider polynomial step sizes,
Robbins and Monro 1951
;
Chung 1954
;
Bottou et al. 2018
, of the form:

\alpha_{k}=\frac{\alpha}{(\beta+k)^{\gamma}},\quad\text{ with }\alpha>0,\quad\beta\geq 0,\quad\gamma\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right].

(31)

Theorem

21
are satisfied when

. We now present several preparatory bounds to facilitate the derivation of the rates.

Lemma 22
.

Let
\xi\in[0,1)
be given and let us consider step sizes
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
of the form
Eq.

31
.

(a)

For all
k\geq 1
, we have

{\sum}_{j=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}^{3}\leq\frac{a_{\gamma}}{(k+\beta)^{3\gamma-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{k}\left[{\sum}_{j=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}^{3}\right]^{2\xi}\leq\frac{a_{\gamma}}{(k+\beta)^{(1+6\xi)\gamma-2\xi}},

(32)

where
a_{\gamma}>0
is a numerical constant depending on
\alpha
and
\gamma
.

(b)

Moreover, if
\xi>\frac{1-\gamma}{3\gamma-1}
, then for all
k\geq 1
, we have

{\sum}_{t=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{t}\left[{\sum}_{j=t}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}^{3}\right]^{\xi}\leq\frac{{a}_{\xi}}{(k+\beta)^{(1+3\xi)\gamma-(1+\xi)}},

(33)

where
{a}_{\xi}>0
is a constant depending on
\xi
.

Proof.

Using the integral test and noting that
3\gamma>1
, we obtain

{\sum}_{j=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}^{3}={\sum}_{j=k}^{\infty}\;\frac{\alpha^{3}}{(j+\beta)^{3\gamma}}\leq\frac{\alpha^{3}}{(k+\beta)^{3\gamma}}+\alpha^{3}\int_{k}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(x+\beta)^{3\gamma}}\;\textup{{d}}x\leq\frac{3\gamma\alpha^{3}}{3\gamma-1}\frac{1}{(k+\beta)^{3\gamma-1}}.

In addition, it follows that
\alpha_{k}[{\sum}_{j=k}^{\infty}\,\alpha_{j}^{3}]^{2\xi}\leq\alpha^{1+6\xi}(\frac{3\gamma}{3\gamma-1})^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{(k+\beta)^{2\xi(3\gamma-1)+\gamma}}
. Noting
\alpha^{1+6\xi}\leq(1+\alpha)^{7}
, this completes the proof of the statement (a). Part (b) can be shown by applying the results in (a) and the integral test; we refer to
(
Li et al. 2023
, Lemma 3.7)
.
∎

Remark 25
.

Theorem

24
establishes the convergence rates of the function values and the stationarity measure for
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time that such rates have been derived for an
\mathsf{RR}
method in the nonconvex setting and under the KL inequality. Moreover,
Theorem

21
and
Theorem

24
show strong convergence of the iterates

, which is the first among proximal-type
\mathsf{RR}
methods. If

aligns with the strongly convex smooth case (cf.
Gürbüzbalaban et al. 2021
).

Remark 26
.

As shown in
Corollary

11
and using polynomial step sizes, the iteration complexity of

is given by

and

(if

is faster than the complexity bound as long as

. Furthermore, in stark contrast to

,

provides last iterate convergence guarantees.

Lemma 27

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a new proximal random reshuffling method (
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
) for large-scale composite problems. Our approach adopts a normal map-based perspective and utilizes stochastic gradient information generated through without-replacement sampling. The obtained complexity results match the existing bounds for
\mathsf{RR}
in the smooth case and improve other known complexity bounds for this class of problems. Under the (global) Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition and in the interpolation setting,
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
provably converges to an optimal solution at a linear rate. In addition, accumulation points of a sequence of iterates
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
generated by
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
are shown to correspond to stationary points of the problem. Finally, under the (local) Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz inequality, we establish last iterate convergence for
\mathsf{norm}\text{-}\mathsf{PRR}
and derive asymptotic rates of convergence. Numerical experiments illustrate that the proposed method effectively maintains feasibility and performs favorably on nonconvex, nonsmooth problems and learning tasks.

\delta_{k}:=\varrho_{\vartheta}(H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\bar{\psi}+u_{k})
.
Due to the monotonicity of the sequence
\{H_{\tau}(z^{k})+u_{k}\}_{k}

H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\bar{\psi}+u_{k}\geq 0

\displaystyle\hskip-4.30554pt\delta_{k}-\delta_{k+1}\geq\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\bar{\psi}+u_{k})[H_{\tau}(z^{k})+u_{k}-H_{\tau}(z^{k+1})-u_{k+1}]

\displaystyle\geq\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(|H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\bar{\psi}|+u_{k})[H_{\tau}(z^{k})+u_{k}-H_{\tau}(z^{k+1})-u_{k+1}]

\displaystyle\geq\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(|H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\bar{\psi}|+u_{k})\left[{\frac{\tau n\alpha_{k}}{4}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{8n\alpha_{k}}\|{w^{k+1}-w^{k}}\|^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\frac{\tau n\alpha_{k}}{4}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{8n\alpha_{k}}\|{w^{k+1}-w^{k}}\|^{2}}{[\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(|H_{\tau}(z^{k})-\bar{\psi}|)]^{-1}+[\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(u_{k})]^{-1}}\geq\frac{\tau n}{4}\frac{\alpha_{k}^{2}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau n^{2}}\|{w^{k+1}-w^{k}}\|^{2}}{\alpha_{k}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|+\alpha_{k}[\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(u_{k})]^{-1}},

(58)

where the first inequality uses the concavity of
\varrho_{\vartheta}
, the second inequality is due to monotonicity of
s\mapsto\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(s)=\hat{c}s^{-\vartheta}
, the third inequality is from
Eq.

57
, the fourth inequality follows from subadditivity of
[\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(s)]^{-1}
– i.e.,
[\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(s_{1}+s_{2})]^{-1}\leq[\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(s_{1})]^{-1}+[\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(s_{2})]^{-1}
for all
s_{1},s_{2}\geq 0
– and the last inequality applies the KL inequality
Eq.

56
. Rearranging the terms in
Section

D.2
, this further yields

\displaystyle\frac{4(\delta_{k}-\delta_{k+1})}{\tau n}\cdot[{\alpha_{k}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|+\alpha_{k}[\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(u_{k})]^{-1}}]

for all
k\geq\bar{k}
, where we used
a^{2}+b^{2}\geq(a+b)^{2}/2

59

8

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\tau}n}\|{w^{k+1}-w^{k}}\|+2\alpha_{k}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{16(\delta_{k}-\delta_{k+1})}{\tau n}\cdot 2[{\alpha_{k}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|+\alpha_{k}[\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(u_{k})]^{-1}}]}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8(\delta_{k}-\delta_{k+1})}{\tau n}+\alpha_{k}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|+\alpha_{k}[\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(u_{k})]^{-1},

(60)

where the last inequality is due to the AM-GM inequality,
\sqrt{ab}\leq(a+b)/2

Summing the inequality

60
from
k=\bar{k}
to

\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\tau}n}{\sum}_{k=\bar{k}}^{T}\|{w^{k+1}-w^{k}}\|+{\sum}_{k=\bar{k}}^{T}\alpha_{k}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|\leq\frac{8(\delta_{\bar{k}}-\delta_{T})}{\tau n}+{\sum}_{k=\bar{k}}^{T}\alpha_{k}[\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(u_{k})]^{-1}.

\varrho_{\vartheta}

29

{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z^{k})}\|<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\|{w^{k+1}-w^{k}}\|<\infty.

The second estimate implies that

∎

D.4
Proof of
Theorem

24
: Rate of
\{w^{k}\}_{k}

Proof.

In this section, we complete the proof of
Theorem

24
and provide rates for the iterates
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
.
Applying
Eq.

61
with
T\to\infty
, we have

\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\tau}n}\|w^{k}-w^{*}\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\tau}n}{\sum}_{i=k}^{\infty}\|{w^{i+1}-w^{i}}\|\leq\frac{8\delta_{k}}{\tau n}+{\sum}_{i=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}[\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(u_{i})]^{-1},

(64)

for all
k\geq\bar{k}
, where
\delta_{k}:=\varrho_{\vartheta}(r_{k})
,
\varrho_{\vartheta}(s):=\frac{\tilde{c}}{1-\vartheta}s^{1-\vartheta}
, and
\vartheta\in[\max\{\frac{1}{2},\theta\},1)
. (As argued in the first parts of the proof, due to
w^{k}\to w^{*}
and
z^{k}\to z^{*}
, we can directly work with the KL exponent
\theta
and adjusted constant
\tilde{c}
instead of using the uniformized versions
\hat{\theta}
and
\hat{c}
). Hence, based on the rate for
\{r_{k}\}_{k}
and using
R(\vartheta,\gamma)\leq R(\theta,\gamma)
and
Lemma

22
(b), we have

\delta_{k}=\mathcal{O}(1/k^{(1-\vartheta)R(\vartheta,\gamma)})\;\;\text{and}\;\;{\sum}_{i=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}[\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(u_{i})]^{-1}=\mathcal{O}({\sum}_{i=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}u_{i}^{\vartheta})=\mathcal{O}(1/k^{(3\gamma-1)\vartheta-(1-\gamma)}).

Note that the adjusted KL exponent
\vartheta
can be selected freely. Thus, we may increase it to ensure
\vartheta>\frac{1-\gamma}{3\gamma-1}
such that
{\sum}_{i=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}[\varrho_{\vartheta}^{\prime}(u_{i})]^{-1}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Hence, provided
\vartheta>\frac{1-\gamma}{3\gamma-1}
, the convergence rate for
\{w^{k}\}_{k}
is

\|w^{k}-w^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(k^{-Q(\vartheta,\gamma)})\quad\text{where}\quad Q(\vartheta,\gamma):=\min\{(1-\vartheta)R(\vartheta,\gamma),(3\gamma-1)\vartheta-(1-\gamma)\}.

We observe that
Q(\vartheta,\gamma)=(3\gamma-1)\vartheta-(1-\gamma)
if
\vartheta\in(\frac{1-\gamma}{3\gamma-1},\frac{\gamma}{3\gamma-1}]
and
Q(\vartheta,\gamma)=\frac{(1-\vartheta)(1-\gamma)}{2\vartheta-1}
if
\vartheta\in(\frac{\gamma}{3\gamma-1},1)
. This means that the mapping
Q(\cdot,\gamma)
is increasing over the interval
(\frac{1-\gamma}{3\gamma-1},\frac{\gamma}{3\gamma-1}]
and decreasing over
(\frac{\gamma}{3\gamma-1},1)
. Therefore, we can choose
\vartheta
in the following way to maximize
Q(\vartheta,\gamma)
: when
\theta\leq\frac{\gamma}{3\gamma-1}
, set
\vartheta=\frac{\gamma}{3\gamma-1}
; otherwise, set
\vartheta=\theta
. In summary, we obtain

\displaystyle\|w^{k}-w^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(k^{-R_{w}(\theta,\gamma)})\quad\text{where}\quad R_{w}(\theta,\gamma):=\begin{cases}2\gamma-1&\text{if}\;\theta\in[0,\frac{\gamma}{3\gamma-1}]\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{(1-\gamma)(1-\theta)}{2\theta-1}&\text{if}\;\theta\in(\frac{\gamma}{3\gamma-1},1)\end{cases}\;\;\gamma\in(\textstyle\frac{1}{2},1),

and

when
\gamma=1,\theta\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
\alpha>\frac{16\tilde{c}^{2}}{\tau n}
. ∎

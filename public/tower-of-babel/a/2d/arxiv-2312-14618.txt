Equivalence principle violation in nonminimally coupled gravity and constraints from Lunar Laser Ranging
Riccardo March, Orfeu Bertolami, Marco Muccino, Simone Dell’Agnello

Abstract
We analyze the dynamics of the Sun-Earth-Moon system in the context of a particular class of theories of gravity where curvature and matter are nonminimally coupled (NMC). These theories can potentially violate the Equivalence Principle as they give origin to a fifth force and a extra non-Newtonian force that may imply that Earth and Moon fall differently towards the Sun. We show, through a detailed analysis, that consistency with the bound on Weak Equivalence Principle arising from 48 years of Lunar Laser Ranging data, for a range of parameters of the NMC gravity theory, can be achieved via the implementation of a suitable screening mechanism.

In the present work we extend the nonlinear computations made in Ref.

;

inside a critical radius,

The chameleon solution for the three-body system is computed by taking into account the appropriate boundary conditions at the boundaries of the
screened zones, and we develop a method of solution based on different linear approximations of the field equations in different zones. The Earth and Moon are modeled by means of
layers of constant density, we solve a Yukawa equation in each layer, a Poisson equation in interplanetary space outside of the screened zones of the three astronomical bodies,
and Laplace equation in the solar neighborhood of the Galaxy.
An analytic solution of the Poisson equation with Dirichlet conditions at the boundaries of the screened zones is computed by means of Green’s function which is in turn approximated
by using an extension of the method of images to a system of three spheres. The screening radii of the bodies are computed by solving a system of integral equations which
result from Neumann boundary conditions.

We compute the equations of motion for the centers of mass of Earth and Moon in the gravitational field of the Sun from first principles, by taking the covariant derivative of the field equations,
then solving the resulting stressed-matter equations of motion.
The Earth and Moon are treated as layered spheres of matter characterized by the energy-momentum tensor of
continuous bodies in a hydrostatic state of stress. The equations of motion exhibit the presence of both the fifth force and the extra force which give rise to deviations from GR.
Such deviations are sourced by the masses contained in the thin shells of the bodies which in turn depend on the density profiles of the bodies themselves,
so that the Earth and Moon fall towards the Sun with different accelerations giving rise to a violation of the Weak Equivalence Principle (WEP).
Such a violation takes place in modified gravity theories which exhibit the chameleon mechanism

;
Kraiselburd

The WEP violation in the Sun-Earth-Moon system makes it possible to constrain the parameters of the NMC gravity model by means of Lunar Laser Ranging (LLR) measurements,
which is the result achieved in the present paper by resorting to most recent LLR data

The paper is organized as follows. In Section II the NMC gravity model is presented. In Section III we consider the field equations inside and around the astronomical bodies (Sun, Earth, Moon) for the chameleon mechanism. In Section IV we compute the solutions for the interior of the astronomical bodies and in the outskirts of the Solar System.
In Section V we determine the boundary conditions for the chameleon solution at the boundaries of the screened zones, then the gravitational field of the astronomical bodies
is evaluated using Green’s function and the method of images for a system of spheres. The integral equations which determine the screening radii are also found.
In Section VI the dynamics of the continuous bodies is considered in order to compute the fifth force and the extra force inside the bodies and to evaluate a yielding jump in the pressure. Both the fifth force and the extra force are shown to be negligible inside the screening radii. In Section VII the acceleration of Earth and Moon due to the fifth force is computed. In Section VIII the acceleration of Earth and Moon due to the extra NMC force is computed.
In Section IX the potential violation of the Equivalence Principle is quantified and in section X the bound on the WEP arising from the LLR data is used to constrain the parameters of the NMC gravity model. It is shown that the screening mechanism is successful in ensuring that the bound on the WEP can be respected for a suitable range of model parameters.
Finally, our conclusions are presented in Section XI. Appendices A to E contain the technical details of the calculations needed to obtain the various results of the paper.

Nonminimally coupled gravity

The action functional of NMC gravity theory here considered is of the form

S=\int\left[\frac{1}{2}f^{1}(R)+[1+f^{2}(R)]\mathcal{L}_{m}\right]\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x,

is considered, and the planet is divided into four homogeneous regions
separated by spherical surfaces of density discontinuities: ocean layer, crust, mantle and core. Numerical values of density and radii of
discontinuity surfaces are reported in Section
.2
of Appendix A.

Since, for
r<r_{E}
, the solution
\eta
has to remain close to the minimizer of
V_{\rm eff}
, then
inside each region of constant density the derivative of the potential
V
is approximated by

\frac{\partial V}{\partial\eta}(\eta,\rho_{E})\approx\frac{\partial V}{\partial\eta}(\eta_{\rm min},\rho_{E})+\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial\eta^{2}}(\eta_{\rm min},\rho_{E})(\eta-\eta_{\rm min}),

(37)

where
\eta_{\rm min}
is the minimizer of
V_{\rm eff}
in the considered region. Since

\frac{\partial V}{\partial\eta}(\eta_{\rm min},\rho_{E})=\frac{1}{3}\rho_{E}c^{2},

(38)

Eq. (
26
) for
\eta
becomes

\nabla^{2}\eta\approx\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}(\rho_{E})}(\eta-\eta_{\rm min}).

(39)

Since in each region the value of density
\rho_{E}
is constant, using (
32
)
\lambda^{2}(\rho_{E})
is also constant,
then Eq. (
39
) admits a closed-form solution that, for a given screening radius
r_{E}
of Earth,
is completely determined by conditions of continuity of
\eta
and its radial derivative at the discontinuity surfaces,
and by the following condition at
r_{E}
given in Ref.
TaTsu
:

\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}(\rho_{E})}\left[\eta(r_{E})-\eta_{\rm min}\right]=-\frac{1}{3}\rho_{E}c^{2},

(40)

where
r_{E}<R_{\oplus}
, with
R_{\oplus}
the Earth’s radius, and
\rho_{E}
is evaluated at the uppermost layer of the Earth’s interior model,
i.e.
, the ocean layer.

Note that the Sun’s interior is modeled by means of a continuously varying density profile (see Section
.1
of Appendix A),
hence Eq. (
39
) in the case of Sun does not admit a closed-form solution, so that we have used a different approximation of the solution
\eta

given by Eq. (
36

prevents us from using a consistency condition of the type (
35

a piecewise constant density profile which permits us to compute an analytic solution that can be proved to be close to the minimizer of the effective potential,
except at the density discontinuities.

In the sequel
R_{E,c}
is the outer radius of the crust,
R_{E,m}
is the outher radius of the mantle, and

\rho_{E,w},\rho_{E,c},\rho_{E,m}

The corresponding values of

By using the numerical values of density of the various Earth’s layers, we find in Section

of Appendix A

The expression of the analytic solution of Eq. (

Ocean layer

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\lambda_{E,c}/\penalty\lambda_{E,m}}{1+\lambda_{E,c}/\penalty\lambda_{E,m}}R_{E,m}(\eta_{E,m}-\eta_{E,c})\exp\left(\frac{R_{E,m}-r}{\lambda_{E,c}}\right)\right].

\displaystyle\eta(r)\approx\eta_{E,m}-\frac{1}{r}\left[\frac{R_{E,m}(\eta_{E,m}-\eta_{E,c})}{1+\lambda_{E,c}/\penalty\lambda_{E,m}}\exp\left(\frac{r-R_{E,m}}{\lambda_{E,m}}\right)\right.

IV.4
Solution in the outskirts of the Solar System

We assume that in the solar neighborhood of the Galaxy the field
\eta
is close to the minimizer of the effective potential
V_{\rm eff}
,
so that the spacetime curvature
R
is approximately given by the GR solution
MBMGDeA
.
This assumption implies that the Milky Way is screened within a distance
of about
8\,{\rm kpc}
from its center, where the Solar System is approximately located. Such a screening condition
may impose additional constraints on the NMC gravity model whose assessment requires the solution for
the gravitational field of the Milky Way, possibly taking also into account the effect of the other
galaxies in the local group. That will be the subject of a future paper.

The galactic mass density
\rho_{g}
in the solar neighborhood of the Milky Way is
\rho_{g}\approx 6.9\times 10^{-24}\,{\rm g}/{\rm cm}^{3}

HoFly
, so that we have
R\approx R_{g}
, with
R_{g}
the GR solution
R_{g}=8\pi G\rho_{g}/c^{2}
.
The minimizer
\eta_{g}
of the effective potential
V_{\rm eff}(\eta,\rho)
, corresponding to
\rho(\mathbf{x},t)=\rho_{g}
, is given by

\eta_{g}=\frac{c^{4}}{8\pi G}-2\alpha q\left(\frac{8\pi G}{c^{2}}\right)^{\alpha-1}\rho_{g}^{\alpha}c^{2}.

(52)

We denote by
r_{g}
a distance from the Sun’s center such that mass density is dominated by the galactic density component at points
\mathbf{x}
such that
|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{S}|>r_{g}
,
where
\mathbf{x}_{S}
is the position vector of Sun’s center.
We choose
r_{g}
at the heliopause, the boundary between the solar wind and the interstellar medium
MBMGDeA
,
corresponding to a heliocentric radial distance of about
120\mbox{ AU }=2.58\times 10^{4}R_{\odot}
, where
R_{\odot}
is the Sun’s radius.

Since
\eta
approximately minimizes
V_{\rm eff}
in the solar neighborhood of the Galaxy,
then Eq. (
26
) becomes

\nabla^{2}\eta\approx\frac{1}{\lambda_{g}^{2}}(\eta-\eta_{g}),\qquad\mbox{for }|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{S}|>r_{g},

(53)

where
\lambda_{g}=\lambda(\rho_{g})
.
The length
\lambda(\rho)
increases as density decreases,
which is a typical property of the chameleon mechanism
KW
,
so that the length
\lambda_{g}
is an upper bound for
\lambda
in the Solar System.
The computations in the present paper will be made under the condition
\lambda_{g}\gg r_{g}
which will permit us
to find analytic estimates of the results. We will find that such a condition is satisfied when the constraint from LLR measurements
is saturated. Then we have

\nabla^{2}\eta=0\qquad\mbox{for }r_{g}<|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{S}|\ll\lambda_{g},

(54)

and, for
|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{S}|
large enough, we assume

\eta(\mathbf{x},t)\approx\eta_{g}.

(55)

Under our assumptions the solution
\eta
is a harmonic function in the outskirts of the Solar System.

IV.5
Solution in interplanetary space

In interplanetary space, where mass density
\rho
is small and gradually approaches the galactic density
\rho_{g}
as
|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{S}|
approaches
r_{g}
,
we proceed as in Ref.
MBMGDeA
and we expand the derivative of the potential
V
around the minimizer
\eta_{g}
:

\frac{\partial V}{\partial\eta}(\eta,\rho)\approx\frac{\partial V}{\partial\eta}(\eta_{g},\rho)+\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial\eta^{2}}(\eta_{g},\rho)(\eta-\eta_{g}).

(56)

Solving the expression (
14
) of
\eta
,

\eta=\frac{c^{4}}{8\pi G}-2\alpha qR^{\alpha-1}\rho c^{2},

(57)

with respect to curvature
R=\omega(\eta,\rho)
we find

\omega(\eta,\rho)=\left(\frac{16\pi G}{c^{2}}\alpha q\rho\right)^{1/\penalty(1-\alpha)}\left(1-\frac{8\pi G}{c^{4}}\eta\right)^{1/\penalty(\alpha-1)},

(58)

from which, using the first equality in Eq. (
25
), we obtain the property

\frac{\partial V}{\partial\eta}(\eta,\rho)=\left(\frac{\rho}{\rho_{g}}\right)^{1/\penalty(1-\alpha)}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\eta}(\eta,\rho_{g}),

(59)

where we note the explicit dependence of
\partial V/\partial\eta
on
\rho
due to the nonminimal coupling.
Taking now into account that at density
\rho_{g}
(in the solar vicinity of the Galaxy) the field
\eta
approximately
minimizes the effective potential
V_{\rm eff}
, so that

\frac{\partial V}{\partial\eta}(\eta_{g},\rho_{g})\approx\frac{1}{3}\,\rho_{g}c^{2},

(60)

we can compute the approximation (
56
) of the derivative of the potential:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V}{\partial\eta}(\eta,\rho)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\rho}{\rho_{g}}\right)^{1/\penalty(1-\alpha)}\left[\frac{\partial V}{\partial\eta}(\eta_{g},\rho_{g})+\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial\eta^{2}}(\eta_{g},\rho_{g})(\eta-\eta_{g})\right]\approx\left(\frac{\rho}{\rho_{g}}\right)^{1/\penalty(1-\alpha)}\left[\frac{1}{3}\,\rho_{g}c^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{g}^{2}}(\eta-\eta_{g})\right]

(61)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\,\rho_{g}c^{2}\left(\frac{\rho}{\rho_{g}}\right)^{1/\penalty(1-\alpha)},

where, taking into account that
\lambda_{g}
is assumed large with respect to
r_{g}
, we have used the inequality

\frac{1}{\lambda_{g}^{2}}(\eta-\eta_{g})\ll\frac{1}{3}\rho_{g}c^{2},

(62)

that will be verified
a posteriori
. Then we have

\frac{\partial V_{\rm eff}}{\partial\eta}=\frac{\partial V}{\partial\eta}-\frac{1}{3}\,\rho c^{2}\approx-\frac{1}{3}\,\widetilde{\rho}c^{2},

(63)

with

\widetilde{\rho}=\rho\left[1-\left(\frac{\rho_{g}}{\rho}\right)^{-\alpha/\penalty(1-\alpha)}\right].

(64)

Then, in interplanetary space we look for the field
\eta
which solves the Poisson equation

\nabla^{2}\eta=-\frac{1}{3}\,\widetilde{\rho}c^{2}.

(65)

As
|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{S}|
tends to
r_{g}
, density
\rho
tends to
\rho_{g}
and the Poisson equation turns to Laplace equation (
54
)
which holds for
|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{S}|\geq r_{g}
.

V
Gravitational field of astronomical bodies

In the thin shells inside the astronomical bodies, the function
\eta
has to interpolate between the solution inside the screening radii computed in the previous section
and the solution in interplanetary space. By adapting to NMC gravity the chameleon mechanism developed in Ref.
KW
, we require the interpolating function
\eta

to satisfy the condition (see also Ref.
MBMGDeA
,
HS
)

0<\frac{\partial V}{\partial\eta}(\eta,\rho)\ll\frac{1}{3}\,\rho c^{2},

(66)

inside the thin shells and in the vicinity of the astronomical bodies where mass density is significantly larger than the galactic density
\rho_{g}
due to the presence of Earth’s
atmosphere and solar wind. Hence the equation for
\eta
becomes the Poisson equation

\nabla^{2}\eta=-\frac{1}{3}\,\rho c^{2}.

(67)

The explicit dependence of
\partial V/\partial\eta
on density
\rho
in condition (
66
) is a distinctive feature of the
application of the chameleon mechanism to NMC gravity with respect to
f(R)
gravity.
Inequality (
66
) will be verified a posteriori in Appendix E for
\alpha<-1/\penalty 2
. Since we have

\alpha<0\implies 0<-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}<1,

(68)

for
|\alpha|
not too small, the second term inside the square bracket in Eq. (
64
) is negligible in comparison to 1
in the thin shells inside the astronomical bodies and in zones where mass density is much larger than the galactic density
\rho_{g}
,
so that
\widetilde{\rho}\approx\rho
and the Poisson equation,

\nabla^{2}\eta=-\frac{1}{3}\,\widetilde{\rho}c^{2},

(69)

is valid with a good approximation everywhere outside of the screening radii. In the next sections we compute an approximate solution
of this equation.

V.1
Boundary conditions

We rewrite the Poisson equation (
69
) in the form

\nabla^{2}\left(\eta-\eta_{g}\right)=-\frac{1}{3}\,\widetilde{\rho}c^{2},

(70)

and we solve this equation in the unbounded domain
\Omega
outside of the screening radii, with the boundary condition (
55
) at large
distance from the Sun’s center. We denote by
\partial\Omega
the boundary of
\Omega
and by
\mathbf{x}_{S},\mathbf{x}_{E},\mathbf{x}_{M}
the centers of the Sun, Earth and Moon, respectively.
Then the boundary
\partial\Omega
consists of the union of the three spherical surfaces with centers at
\mathbf{x}_{S},\mathbf{x}_{E},\mathbf{x}_{M}
and radii given by

r_{S},r_{E},r_{M}
, respectively.

We have to match the solution in
\Omega
with the interior solution computed in Sec.
IV

Using the value (
41
) of
\eta
minimizing the effective potential inside the Earth, and the solution (
IV.2
) for
\eta
in the Earth’s ocean layer,
the value of
\eta
at the Earth’s screening surface is given by

\eta\approx\frac{c^{4}}{8\pi G}-\frac{2-\alpha}{3(1-\alpha)}\lambda^{2}_{E,w}\rho_{E,w}c^{2}.

(71)

Using the expression (
52
) of the minimizer
\eta_{g}
in the Galaxy, and formula (
32
) for
\lambda(\rho)
, we have

\eta_{g}=\frac{c^{4}}{8\pi G}-\frac{\lambda^{2}_{g}}{3(1-\alpha)}\rho_{g}c^{2}.

(72)

In the sequel we assume
|\alpha|
not too close to zero in such a way that, using again the formula for
\lambda(\rho)
, we have

\frac{\lambda^{2}_{E,w}\rho_{E,w}}{\lambda^{2}_{g}\rho_{g}}=\left(\frac{\rho_{E,w}}{\rho_{g}}\right)^{\alpha}\ll 1,

(73)

from which it follows that at the Earth’s screening surface the boundary condition
\eta-\eta_{g}\approx c^{4}/\penalty(8\pi G)-\eta_{g}
holds.
Note that the expression (
57
) of
\eta
, for
R>0
and
\alpha<0
, implies
\eta\neq c^{4}/\penalty(8\pi G)
, so that the
above boundary condition is proposed as an approximate condition in the sense of the inequality
|(8\pi G/\penalty c^{4})\eta-1|\ll 1
.
Analogous considerations can be applied for the boundary conditions at the screening surfaces of the Sun and Moon.

Then we have the following Dirichlet boundary conditions for the Poisson equation (
70
) in the set
\Omega
:

\begin{cases}\eta(\mathbf{x},t)-\eta_{g}\approx\frac{c^{4}}{8\pi G}-\eta_{g}\qquad\mbox{for $\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega$ and any $t$},\\
\eta(\mathbf{x},t)-\eta_{g}\approx 0\quad\mbox{for $r_{g}<|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{S}|$ large and any $t$}.\end{cases}

(74)

Moreover, the existence of
\nabla^{2}\eta
requires the continuity of the first partial derivatives of
\eta
across the screening surfaces.
The normal derivative of
\eta
at the Earth’s screening surface, computed from the interior by using solution (
IV.2

\frac{d\eta}{dr}(r_{E})\approx-\frac{1}{3}\lambda_{E,w}\rho_{E,w}c^{2},

(75)

and an analogous result holds for the Moon. Moreover, using the approximate solution (
36

\eta

at the Sun’s screening surface is given by

\frac{d\eta}{dr}(r_{S})\approx-\frac{\alpha\lambda^{2}(\rho_{S}(r_{S}))}{3(1-\alpha)}\frac{d\rho_{S}}{dr}(r_{S})c^{2}.

(76)

Then, taking into account the smallness of
\lambda(\rho)
in the interior of the bodies,
we neglect the derivatives of
\eta
from the interior of
\partial\Omega
and we impose the Neumann boundary condition:

\nabla\eta(\mathbf{x})\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x})\approx 0\qquad\mbox{for $\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega$ and any $t$},

(77)

where
\hat{\mathbf{n}}(x)
denotes a normal unit vector to the surface
\partial\Omega
at point
\mathbf{x}
. For given screening radii

the Dirichlet condition
uniquely determines the solution of the Poisson equation, while the Neumann condition will be used to find the screening radii.

V.2
Solution by means of Green’s function

In order to compute effects in the Sun-Earth-Moon system it is enough to compute the solution

For a given screening radii and time instant

G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{0})

Haberm

\displaystyle\eta(\mathbf{x},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{g}-\frac{c^{2}}{3}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\rho}(\mathbf{x}_{0},t)G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{0})d^{3}x_{0}

(78)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{c^{4}}{8\pi G}-\eta_{g}\right)\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{0}}G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{0})\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{x}_{0})dS_{0},

provided that, for
|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{S}|

In the next section we compute an analytical approximation of the Green’s function.

V.3
Method of images for a system of spheres

If the set
\partial\Omega
would consist of a single sphere, then the Green’s function could be obtained by using the method of images.
Since in our case the set
\partial\Omega
consists of three spheres, which are the three screening surfaces of Sun, Earth and Moon, respectively,
then we apply to our problem the extension of the method of images to a system of spheres that has been proposed in Ref.
MetzLa
.
This is an iterative method that involves an infinite series of images so that it yields a representation of Green’s function by means of an infinite series:

G(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{0})=G^{(0)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{0})+G^{(1)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{0})+G^{(2)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{0})+\cdots

(81)

The first terms of the series are obtained as follows (see
MetzLa
for further details). The zeroth-order term is

G^{(0)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{0})=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\,\frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|}\,,

(82)

with
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\Omega
, so that
\mathbf{x}_{0}
lies outside of the three screening spheres.

The first order term
G^{(1)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{0})
involves three image points: one image inside each screening sphere.
In each sphere the image point is obtained by applying the usual method of electrostatics to the unitary source at
\mathbf{x}_{0}
:

\displaystyle G^{(1)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{0})=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{r_{S}}{|\mathbf{x}_{S}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|\cdot|\mathbf{x}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{S}|}\right.

(83)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\frac{r_{E}}{|\mathbf{x}_{E}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|\cdot|\mathbf{x}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{E}|}+\frac{r_{M}}{|\mathbf{x}_{M}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|\cdot|\mathbf{x}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{M}|}\right),

where
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{S}
is the image of
\mathbf{x}_{0}
inside the screening sphere of the Sun, which is given by

\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{S}=\mathbf{x}_{S}+r_{S}^{2}\,\frac{\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{x}_{S}}{|\mathbf{x}_{0}-\mathbf{x}_{S}|^{2}}\,,

(84)

and the image points
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{E}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{M}
, inside the screening spheres of Earth and Moon, are obtained
by replacing in the expression of
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{S}
the subscript
S
with
E
and
M
, respectively.

The second order term
G^{(2)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{0})
involves six image points: two images inside each screening sphere. The image points in each sphere are obtained by
iterating the procedure used for the first order term: consider the three sources at points
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{S},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{E},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{M}
with charges

-\frac{r_{S}}{|\mathbf{x}_{S}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|},\quad-\frac{r_{E}}{|\mathbf{x}_{E}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|},\quad-\frac{r_{M}}{|\mathbf{x}_{M}-\mathbf{x}_{0}|},

(85)

respectively. Then, the six image points are obtained by applying the method of electrostatics to the above sources and they are given by:

(i)

the images inside the Sun’s screening sphere of the sources at
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{E}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{M}
;

(ii)

the images inside the Earth’s screening sphere of the sources at
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{S}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{M}
;

(iii)

the images inside the Moon’s screening sphere of the sources at
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{S}
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{E}
.

The resulting expression of
G^{(2)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{0})
is given in Appendix B.
This procedure is repeated iteratively giving rise to an infinite series of images and terms in the Green’s function. The convergence of the series is discussed in Ref.
MetzLa
.
If the distances between the spheres are much greater than the radii, a condition which is satisfied for the Sun-Earth-Moon system, then the contribution
of the higher order terms decreases quickly, and we find that the Green’s function up to the second order will suffice while
higher order terms will not be necessary. Eventually, we observe that notwithstanding the ratio of Sun’s radius and the Earth-Moon distance is not small,
such a ratio never appears in the computations.

Hence, by the maximum principle for harmonic functions, it follows that the harmonic function (with respect to the spatial variables
\mathbf{x}

(115)

is constant inside the sphere of radius

\Psi
is given by

\Psi(\mathbf{x},t)=-\frac{8\pi G}{c^{4}}\left[\eta(\mathbf{x},t)-\eta_{g}\right].

(116)

The solution for
\Psi
then follows immediately from the solution for
\eta
found in the previous sections.
Combining now equations (
21
) and (
22

\Psi
and
\Phi

\nabla^{2}\left(\Phi-\frac{U}{c^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\,\Psi\right)=0.

(117)

Then, applying both boundary conditions (

the solution
\Phi
of this equation is given by

\Phi(\mathbf{x},t)=\frac{1}{c^{2}}\,U(\mathbf{x},t)+\frac{1}{2}\,\Psi(\mathbf{x},t).

(118)

The solutions found for
\Phi
and
\Psi
define the space-time metric (

VI
Dynamics of continuous bodies

We consider the motion of Earth and Moon in the gravitational field of the Sun. The equations describing the dynamics of the system
are obtained by taking the covariant divergence of the energy-momentum tensor and applying Bianchi identities to the gravitational field equations (see Ref.

as given by Eq. (

According to Sec.

Now we compute the right-hand side of Eq. (

R={\cal O}(1/\penalty c^{2})

Then, the integral equations (

|q|=\rho_{g}^{-\alpha}\cdot{\cal O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{2-2\alpha}}\right),

(121)

Using now property (

Using Eq. (

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}(\rho v^{i})=0.

The NMC term on the right-hand side of Eq. (

to the spatial part of this equation that now we compute. Using the components (

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu i}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_{i}}+{\cal O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}}\right).

VI.1
Fifth force inside the screening spheres

The fifth force density is given by

-\frac{1}{2}\rho c^{2}\nabla\Psi=\frac{4\pi G}{c^{2}}\rho\nabla\eta,

(129)

where the expression (
116
) of
\Psi
in terms of the function
\eta
has been used.
In the interior of the screening spheres the magnitude of fifth force is expected to be largest at the surfaces of density discontinuity where the largest
deviations from GR take place. Let us compute such a force at the Moho, the crust-mantle discontinuity in the Earth’s interior.

The radial derivative of
\eta
is continuous, and using the solution for
\eta
inside the Earth’s screening radius, found in Sec.
IV.2
, we have

\frac{d\eta}{dr}(R_{E,m})\approx\frac{\eta_{E,c}-\eta_{E,m}}{\lambda_{E,c}+\lambda_{E,m}}.

(130)

The quantity
\rho_{E}d\eta/dr
is discontinuous at the Moho and the maximum value of its magnitude is
\rho_{E,m}|d\eta/dr(R_{E,m})|
.
Then, using the value (
41
) of
\eta
minimizing the effective potential
V_{\rm eff}
and formula (
32
) for
\lambda
,
the magnitude of the fifth force density is estimated by

\displaystyle\left|F_{\rm f}\right|

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{4}{3}\,\frac{\pi G}{1+|\alpha|}\rho_{E,m}\frac{|\lambda^{2}_{E,c}\rho_{E,c}-\lambda^{2}_{E,m}\rho_{E,m}|}{\lambda_{E,c}+\lambda_{E,m}}

(131)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi}{3}G\lambda_{E,c}\rho_{E,m}\rho_{E,c}.

Now the magnitude of the Newtonian force density at the Moho is

\left|F_{N}\right|=\rho_{E,m}\left|\frac{dU}{dr}(R_{E,m})\right|=\frac{4\pi}{3}GR_{E,m}\rho_{E,m}\overline{\rho}_{E,c},

(132)

where
\overline{\rho}_{E,c}
is the average Earth density below the crust.
Since we have
\lambda(\rho_{E})\ll R_{E,m}
with
\lambda(\rho_{E})
completely negligible (
\lambda(\rho_{E})\lesssim 10^{-7}
m for
\alpha=-1
according to
Section
.2
of Appendix A), it then follows
\left|F_{\rm f}\right|\ll\left|F_{N}\right|
.
The same behavior is found at the other density discontinuities, moreover, far from discontinuities the fifth force is further decreased by exponential suppression
with decay constant
1/\lambda(\rho_{E})
in the various layers.
Eventually, the perturbation of the Newtonian gravitational force, hence of hydrostatic equilibrium, is completely negligible inside the Earth’s screening radius,
confirming the effectiveness of the screening mechanism.

Inside Earth’s thin shell, hence for
r_{E}<r<R_{\oplus}
, the fifth force exerted by the Sun and Moon contributes to the motion of Earth’s center of mass (see Section
VII
),
while the leading contribution from Earth itself is radial, so that it does not contribute to the motion of center mass but only to hydrostatic equilibrium.
Such a contribution to hydrostatic equilibrium is computed in Section
.6
of Appendix E where it is shown that the resulting perturbation is again negligible in
comparison with Newtonian force.

Analogous results hold for the Moon, while for the Sun see Ref.
MBMGDeA
.

VI.2
Jump conditions for the pressure

The expression (
58
) for curvature
R=\omega(\eta,\rho)
, which we rewrite in the form

R=\left(\frac{c^{2}/\penalty(8\pi G)-\eta/\penalty c^{2}}{2\alpha q\rho}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}},

(133)

shows that
R
is discontinuous at surfaces across which mass density is discontinuous, such as the external surfaces of Earth and Moon or the Mohorovičić discontinuity.
Since the extra force is proportional to
\nabla R
, then such a force is concentrated at the surfaces of density discontinuity and the concentration
gives rise to a jump of pressure
p
across these surfaces that we now compute. We will find that such a jump of pressure is undetectable for interesting values
of parameter
\alpha
.

In the following the astronomical body considered is either the Earth or the Moon. Let
R_{\rm d}
be the radius of a discontinuity surface, and let
\rho^{+}
and
\rho^{-}

be the values of density on the two sides of the discontinuity. We adopt the method of Ref.
Obrien-Sy
and we introduce
a boundary layer across which density changes continuously, then we proceed to the limit in which the thickness of the layer tends to zero.
Let
\varepsilon
be a positive small parameter and let
r
be the distance from the center of the body, then we introduce a family of mass density functions
\rho_{\varepsilon}
defined by

\begin{cases}\rho_{\varepsilon}(r)=\rho(r)\quad\mbox{for }r<R_{\rm d}-\varepsilon\mbox{ and }r>R_{\rm d}+\varepsilon,\\
\rho_{\varepsilon}(R_{\rm d}+\varepsilon)=\rho^{+},\quad\rho_{\varepsilon}(R_{\rm d}-\varepsilon)=\rho^{-},\end{cases}

(134)

where
\rho_{\varepsilon}(r)
is continuously differentiable in the interval
(R_{\rm d}-\varepsilon,R_{\rm d}+\varepsilon)
.
Moreover, we denote by means of the subscript
\varepsilon
the functions which are solution of the field equations and equations of motion for assigned density
\rho_{\varepsilon}
, and we assume
that such functions converge as
\varepsilon\to 0
to the solution of the problem with density
\rho
.

Let now
\hat{\mathbf{n}}=(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{o})/\penalty|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{o}|
, where
\mathbf{x}_{o}
is the position vector of the center of the body, then replacing
\rho
with
\rho_{\varepsilon}
in Eqs. (
128
),
taking the scalar product of the resulting equations by the unit vector
\hat{\mathbf{n}}
, and integrating radially over the boundary layer of thickness
2\varepsilon
,
at the time instant
t
we have

\displaystyle\Delta p_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-c^{2}\int_{R_{\rm d}-\varepsilon}^{R_{\rm d}+\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}f^{2}_{R_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial R_{\varepsilon}}{\partial r}dr

(135)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{R_{\rm d}-\varepsilon}^{R_{\rm d}+\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}\left[-\frac{d\mathbf{v}_{\varepsilon}}{dt}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}+\frac{\partial U_{\varepsilon}}{\partial r}-\frac{c^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial\Psi_{\varepsilon}}{\partial r}\right]dr,

where
\Delta p_{\varepsilon}=p_{\varepsilon}(R_{\rm d}+\varepsilon,\theta,\varphi,t)-p_{\varepsilon}(R_{\rm d}-\varepsilon,\theta,\varphi,t)
and

(r,\theta,\varphi)
are spherical coordinates on the discontinuity surface.
Taking the limit as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, and observing that the derivatives in the integrand in the second row of the above equation are bounded
above and below for all
\varepsilon
, we find

\Delta p=-c^{2}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{R_{\rm d}-\varepsilon}^{R_{\rm d}+\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}f^{2}_{R_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial R_{\varepsilon}}{\partial r}dr,

(136)

where
\Delta p=p(R_{\rm d}^{+},\theta,\varphi,t)-p(R_{\rm d}^{-},\theta,\varphi,t)
is the pressure jump.
In order to compute the pressure jump we now compute the above limit of the integral. Using
f^{2}(R_{\varepsilon})=qR_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}
and Eq. (
133
),
the integrand is given by

-\left(2|\alpha|\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+|\alpha|}}|q|^{\frac{1}{1+|\alpha|}}\rho_{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left[\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\alpha}{1+|\alpha|}}\left(\frac{c^{2}}{8\pi G}-\frac{\eta_{\varepsilon}}{c^{2}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\right],

(137)

which can be decomposed into the sum of a bounded term whose contribution vanishes in the limit as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, and an unbounded term which in the
limit gives rise to the pressure jump. The result of the computation is

\Delta p=\mathcal{C}(\alpha,q)\left(\frac{c^{2}}{8\pi G}-\frac{\eta}{c^{2}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\left({\rho^{-}}^{\frac{1}{1+|\alpha|}}-{\rho^{+}}^{\frac{1}{1+|\alpha|}}\right),

(138)

where the coefficient
\mathcal{C}(\alpha,q)
is given by

\mathcal{C}(\alpha,q)=c^{2}2^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\left(\alpha q\right)^{\frac{1}{1+|\alpha|}},

(139)

and the function

\eta=\eta(R_{\rm d},\theta,\varphi,t)
is evaluated on the discontinuity surface at the point with angular coordinates
(\theta,\varphi)
. The jump
\Delta p

does not depend on the angular coordinates on the discontinuity surfaces which are located inside the screening spheres, while the jump varies with position on the
external surfaces of Earth and Moon.

We see that the jump of pressure depends both on the solution
\eta
and, explicitly, on the NMC gravity parameters
\alpha
and
q

(take into account that in Sec.
III.1
we have found that
\alpha<0
implies
q<0
).

IX
Equivalence principle violation

The results of the previous sections imply that the Earth and Moon fall toward the Sun with different accelerations, hence a violation of the universality of free fall (UFF) takes place.
The UFF can be satisfied only if the astronomical bodies are completely screened (the effective masses vanish), a condition that follow from the equations (
101
)
that determine the screening radii only if
\eta_{g}=c^{4}/(8\pi G)
, hence
q=0
from Eq. (
52
), so that NMC gravity reduces to GR.

Using the expressions of the acceleration of Earth and Moon due to fifth force and extra force, computed in Secs.
VII
and
VIII
,
the leading terms of the relative Earth-Moon acceleration are given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{M}-\mathbf{a}_{\oplus}=-GM^{\ast}\frac{\mathbf{x}_{M}-\mathbf{x}_{E}}{|\mathbf{x}_{M}-\mathbf{x}_{E}|^{3}}+\Delta_{\rm ESM}GM_{\odot}\frac{\mathbf{x}_{S}-\mathbf{x}_{E}}{|\mathbf{x}_{S}-\mathbf{x}_{E}|^{3}}

\displaystyle+\left(1+\Delta_{\rm tidal}\right)GM_{\odot}\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}_{E}-\mathbf{x}_{S}}{|\mathbf{x}_{E}-\mathbf{x}_{S}|^{3}}-\frac{\mathbf{x}_{M}-\mathbf{x}_{S}}{|\mathbf{x}_{M}-\mathbf{x}_{S}|^{3}}\right),

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rm ESM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\frac{M_{\odot,{\rm eff}}}{M_{\odot}}\left[(1-Q_{M})\frac{M_{M,{\rm eff}}}{M_{M}}-(1-Q_{\oplus})\frac{M_{\oplus,{\rm eff}}}{M_{\oplus}}\right],

\displaystyle M^{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{\oplus}+M_{M}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\frac{M_{\oplus,{\rm eff}}}{M_{\oplus}}\frac{M_{M,{\rm eff}}}{M_{M}}\left[M_{\oplus}(1-Q_{M})+M_{M}(1-Q_{\oplus})\right],

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}(1-Q_{M})\frac{M_{M,{\rm eff}}}{M_{M}}\frac{M_{\odot,{\rm eff}}}{M_{\odot}}.

(179)

Wi

(i)

(ii)

the second term, which can be written in the form,

\Delta_{\rm ESM}\,\mathbf{g}_{S},

(180)

\mathbf{g}_{S}
being the Newtonian acceleration of Earth due to the Sun, is the UFF violation-related difference between the Earth and the Moon accelerations toward the Sun, hence,

the third term is the solar tidal perturbation of the Moon’s orbit,

The size of the UFF violation is represented by the parameter

If the astronomical bodies are screened with screening radii close enough to radii of the bodies, then
the effective masses and, consequently,
\Delta_{\rm ESM}

cm

XI
Conclusions

In this work we have examined the NMC theories of gravity specified by Eq. (
11
). These theories for certain values of
q
and
\alpha
are potentially inconsistent with the Equivalence Principle given that the fifth force and the extra non-Newtonian force they give rise could imply that Earth and Moon fall differently towards the Sun. We have shown that the screening chameleon-type mechanism, discussed for the first time in the context of these theories in Ref.
MBMGDeA
, can be used to shield any violation of the Equivalence Principle provided the astronomical bodies are screened in such a way that their screening radii are close enough to the radii of the bodies as discussed in section IX. Furthermore, we have shown in section X for which range of values of the parameters
\alpha
and
q
, compatibility with 48 years of LLR data can be ensured. Hence, the screening mechanism here discussed does allow for compatibility with data even of versions of the NMC gravity theories that are potentially problematic. We find that constraints from WEP compatible with the LLR data yield tighter bounds on the model parameters compared than the corresponding bounds from the Cassini measurement of the parametrized post-Newtonian parameter,
\gamma

MBMGDeA
.
Indeed, WEP and LLR data, compatible with lunar ephemerides, do provide stringent bounds on the parameters
q
and
\alpha
, more specifically, as depicted in
Figs.
2
and
2
, for
|\alpha|
and the rescaled value
|\widetilde{q}|
given by Eq. (
185
). Compatibility with data is found for

|\widetilde{q}|<10^{-14}
and for
0.5<|\alpha|<10
. As for the robustness of the screening mechanism, the thin-shell conditions were tested for different model parameters for Earth, Moon and Sun, as shown in Figs.
4
and
4
. As can one inspect, consistency is found for the range of model parameters that are compatible with data.

Recently, WEP has been tested with a precision of
10^{-15}
by the MICROSCOPE mission
Microscope
, nevertheless, the resulting bounds on chameleon gravity are not
competitive with state-of-the-art constraints
Pernot-Borras
since MICROSCOPE was not designed for testing modified gravity theories of this type.

As a final remark, we can state, on general grounds, that our results provide yet another confirmation that the NMC gravity model under study remains a workable alternative to address certain issues for which GR is not fully adequate. Furthermore, given that the particular subclass of NMC model under study requires the development of various techniques for the implementation of the screening mechanism, its relevance goes beyond the specificities of the model as the technical issues addressed in our work can, in principle, be useful in any model of gravity.

Appendix E: thin shell inequality

We give a proof
a posteriori
of inequality (
66
) that has been used in order to compute the solution for function
\eta
.
Particularly, we prove that such a solution is consistent with the inequality for
\alpha<-1/\penalty 2
. We give the proof for the Earth’s thin shell, the case of the Moon
being similar, while the case of the Sun has been proved in Ref.
MBMGDeA
. Since
\eta
is a solution of Eq. (
24
),
we have to prove that for
r>r_{E}
and
r
close to
r_{E}
, the solution
\eta
in the thin shell is such that curvature
R=\omega(\eta,\rho)

quickly becomes much smaller than the GR curvature, so that inequality (
66
) is verified a posteriori.

We use formula (
133
) for curvature which we write in the form

R=(2\alpha q\rho)^{\frac{1}{1+|\alpha|}}\left(\frac{c^{2}}{8\pi G}-\frac{\eta}{c^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{1+|\alpha|}}.

(220)

The term
(c^{2}/\penalty{8\pi G}-\eta/\penalty c^{2})
is computed on the sphere of radius
r
, with
r_{E}<r<R_{\oplus}
, with the same method used to compute the
same expression for
r=R_{\oplus}
in formula (
VIII
) and reported in Appendix D. The leading term depends on
r
and it is given by

\frac{c^{2}}{8\pi G}-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\eta(r)\approx\frac{\delta_{r}}{6r}\int_{r_{E}}^{r}\rho_{E}(r^{\prime})(r^{\prime})^{2}dr^{\prime},

(221)

where

\delta_{r}=\frac{r-r_{E}}{r}=\frac{1-r_{E}/\penalty r}{1-r_{E}/\penalty R_{\oplus}}\,\delta_{E}.

(222)

Now, using the thin shell approximation so that the Earth’s screening surface lies in the ocean layer, we have

\int_{r_{E}}^{R_{\oplus}}\rho_{E}(r)rdr=\frac{1}{2}\rho_{E,w}\left(R_{\oplus}^{2}-r_{E}^{2}\right)\approx\rho_{E,w}R_{\oplus}^{2}\delta_{E}.

(223)

Using then the integral equation (
101
), substituting the expression (
52
) of
\eta_{g}
into the integral equation,
and neglecting factors involving radius-to-distance ratios in the integral equation, we find the approximation

\delta_{E}\approx\frac{6\alpha q}{\rho_{E,w}R_{\oplus}^{2}}\left(\frac{8\pi G}{c^{2}}\right)^{\alpha-1}\rho_{g}^{\alpha}.

(224)

Using again the thin shell approximation we have

\frac{1}{6r}\int_{r_{E}}^{r}\rho_{E}(r^{\prime})(r^{\prime})^{2}dr^{\prime}\approx\frac{1}{6}\rho_{E,w}r^{2}\delta r.

(225)

Moreover, using definition (
32
) of
\lambda
, we have

\alpha q=\frac{\lambda_{g}^{2}}{6(1+|\alpha|)}\left(\frac{8\pi G}{c^{2}}\rho_{g}\right)^{1+|\alpha|}.

(226)

Substituting formulae (
221
)-(
222
) and (
224
-
226
) in expression (
220
) of curvature, we find
for
r_{E}<r<R_{\oplus}
,

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left[2(1+|\alpha|)\right]^{\frac{1}{1+|\alpha|}}\left(\frac{R_{\oplus}-r_{E}}{r-r_{E}}\,\frac{R_{\oplus}}{\lambda_{g}}\right)^{\frac{2}{1+|\alpha|}}

(227)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\rho_{g}}{\rho_{E,w}}\right)^{\frac{|\alpha|-1}{|\alpha|+1}}R_{\rm GR},

where
R_{\rm GR}=(8\pi G/\penalty c^{2})\rho_{E,w}
is the value of GR curvature in the ocean layer. The expression of curvature diverges as
r\rightarrow r_{E}
, which means
that the approximation is not valid in this limit. Nevertheless, there exist
\alpha_{0}
and
r^{\ast}\in(r_{E},R_{\oplus})
, with
r^{\ast}=r^{\ast}(\alpha)
close to
r_{E}
,
such that for any
\alpha<\alpha_{0}
and any
r\in(r^{\ast},R_{\oplus})
we have
R\ll R_{\rm GR}
, so that the desired inequality is verified in almost all the thin shell.
In the following we give a sample of values.

Let us set
\lambda_{g}=10^{2}r_{g}
. We have
R<10^{-3}R_{\rm GR}

\displaystyle\mbox{for}\quad\alpha=-2\quad\mbox{and}\quad r^{\ast}-r_{E}=10^{-15}(R_{\oplus}-r_{E}),

\displaystyle\mbox{for}\quad\alpha=-1\quad\mbox{and}\quad r^{\ast}-r_{E}=10^{-5}(R_{\oplus}-r_{E}).

We have
R<R_{\rm GR}/\penalty 20

\mbox{for}\quad\alpha=-1/\penalty 2\quad\mbox{and}\quad r^{\ast}-r_{E}=3.6\times 10^{-2}(R_{\oplus}-r_{E}).

If we increase
\lambda_{g}
by setting
\lambda_{g}=7.07\times 10^{2}r_{g}
, which is the upper bound permitted by the LLR constraint for
\alpha=-1/\penalty 3

then we have
R<10^{-1}R_{\rm GR}

\mbox{for}\quad\alpha=-1/\penalty 3\quad\mbox{and}\quad r^{\ast}-r_{E}=0.2(R_{\oplus}-r_{E}).

We see that

.6
Fifth force in the thin shell

The fifth force density is given by Eq. (
129
) and the leading term in Earth’s thin shell is computed by using Eqs. (
221
) and (
225

for
r^{\ast}(\alpha)<r<R_{\oplus}
the fifth force density is given by

F_{\rm f}(r)\approx-\frac{4}{3}\pi G(r-r_{E})\left(\rho_{E,w}\frac{R_{\oplus}\delta_{E}}{R_{\oplus}-r_{E}}\right)^{2},

(228)

and it achieves maximum magnitude at
r=R_{\oplus}
. The Newtonian force density at
r=R_{\oplus}
has magnitude

|F_{\rm N}|=(4/3)\pi G\rho_{E,w}\langle\rho_{\oplus}\rangle R_{\oplus}
, where
\langle\rho_{\oplus}\rangle
is Earth’s mean density, so that the ratio of magnitude is

\frac{|F_{\rm f}(R_{\oplus})|}{|F_{\rm N}(R_{\oplus})|}\approx\frac{\rho_{E,w}}{\langle\rho_{\oplus}\rangle}\delta_{E}.

(229)

In Section
X
we have found the upper bound
\delta_{E}<2\cdot 10^{-5}
for
|\alpha|>1/2
which corresponds to the value
3.6\cdot 10^{-6}

for the ratio of magnitude of fifth force and Newtonian force. Since Earth’s thin shell is contained in seawater, then
the fifth force is negligible (see the ocean experiment in
Zum
) in the thin shell for
\alpha<-1/2
and
r>r^{\ast}(\alpha)
.

For values of
r
such that
r_{E}<r<r^{\ast}(\alpha)
the above approximation of
\eta
cannot be used, nevertheless, the radial integral of fifth force density can be evaluated
by using the boundary conditions. Using the Dirichlet condition
\eta(r_{E})\approx c^{4}/\penalty(8\pi G)
, and considering the leading term of
\eta(r^{\ast})
, we find

\displaystyle\int_{r_{E}}^{r^{\ast}}F_{\rm f}(r)dr

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi G}{c^{2}}\rho_{E,w}\left[\eta(r^{\ast})-\eta(r_{E})\right]

(230)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{3}\pi G\rho^{2}_{E,w}\left(r^{\ast}-r_{E}\right)^{2}.

The evaluation of the corresponding integral of Newtonian force density yields

\int_{r_{E}}^{r^{\ast}}F_{\rm N}(r)dr\approx-\frac{4}{3}\pi G\rho^{2}_{E,w}\left(3+\frac{\langle\rho_{\oplus}\rangle}{\rho_{E,w}}\right)r_{E}\left(r^{\ast}-r_{E}\right).

(231)

Since
3+\langle\rho_{\oplus}\rangle/\penalty\rho_{E,w}>8
, the ratio of the integral of fifth force and the integral of Newtonian force is bounded by the ratio

\frac{r^{\ast}-r_{E}}{r_{E}}=\frac{r^{\ast}-r_{E}}{R_{\oplus}-r_{E}}\frac{R_{\oplus}}{r_{E}}\delta_{E},

(232)

so that, by using the values of
r^{\ast}-r_{E}
found in the previous subsection and
\delta_{E}<2\cdot 10^{-5}
, such a ratio is less than
10^{-6}
for
\alpha<-1/2
and
the contribution of the fifth force turns out to be again negligible.

Analogous results can be found for the Moon.

.7
Extra force in the thin shell

Using formula (
133
) for curvature
R
and taking into account that density is constant in the thin shell of both Earth and Moon,
we find for the extra force density
F_{\rm e}=-\rho c^{2}f^{2}_{R}\nabla R
the expression

\displaystyle F_{\rm e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{R}{1+|\alpha|}\nabla\eta=-\frac{R}{(8\pi G/\penalty c^{2})\rho}\frac{F_{\rm f}}{1+|\alpha|},

(233)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{R}{R_{\rm GR}}\frac{F_{\rm f}}{1+|\alpha|}.

Since we have
R\ll R_{\rm GR}
for
r^{\ast}(\alpha)<r<R_{\oplus}
it follows
|F_{\rm e}|\ll|F_{\rm f}|
for such values of radius in Earth,
so that the extra force is also negligible in the thin shell.

For values of
r
such that
r_{E}<r<r^{\ast}(\alpha)
the radial integral of the extra force density can be evaluated by using the boundary conditions:

\displaystyle\int_{r_{E}}^{r^{\ast}}F_{\rm e}(r)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\rho_{E,w}c^{2}\int_{r_{E}}^{r^{\ast}}f^{2}_{R}\frac{dR}{dr}dr

(234)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{E,w}c^{2}|\widetilde{q}|\left[\left(\frac{R(r^{\ast})}{R_{g}}\right)^{\alpha}-\left(\frac{R(r_{E})}{R_{g}}\right)^{\alpha}\right],

with
\widetilde{q}=qR_{g}^{\alpha}
. Using formula (
224
) we have

c^{2}|\widetilde{q}|\approx\frac{4}{3|\alpha|}\pi G\rho_{E,w}R_{\oplus}^{2}\delta_{E},

(235)

from which, being
R(r^{\ast})\ll R_{\rm GR}(r^{\ast})=R(r_{E})
for the boundary condition on Earth’s screening sphere, and
\alpha<0
, we have the inequality

\int_{r_{E}}^{r^{\ast}}F_{\rm e}(r)dr<\frac{4}{3|\alpha|}\pi G\rho^{2}_{E,w}R_{\oplus}^{2}\delta_{E}\left(\frac{R(r^{\ast})}{R_{g}}\right)^{\alpha}.

(236)

Using now the integral (
231
), the absolute value of the ratio of the integral of the extra force and the integral of Newtonian force is bounded by the ratio

\frac{R_{\oplus}^{2}\delta_{E}}{|\alpha|r_{E}(3+\langle\rho_{\oplus}\rangle/\rho_{E,w})}\frac{1}{r^{\ast}-r_{E}}\left(\frac{R(r^{\ast})}{R_{g}}\right)^{\alpha}.

(237)

The ratio increases by decreasing
|\alpha|
so that it is bounded by the value achieved for
\alpha=-1/2
and,
using the values of
r^{\ast}=r^{\ast}(\alpha)
which satisfy the thin shell inequality, we find that the above ratio is less than
10^{-10}
, hence the contribution of the extra force
turns out to be again negligible.

Analogous results can be found for the Moon.

Global tensor polarization of spin 3 / 2 3/2 hadrons and quark spin correlations in relativistic heavy ion collisions
Zhe Zhang, Ji-peng Lv, Zi-han Yu, Zuo-tang Liang

Abstract
We study the global polarization of spin- 3 / 2 3/2 hadrons in relativistic heavy ion collisions. We show in particular that the global tensor polarizations of rank two or three for spin- 3 / 2 3/2 hadrons are sensitive to the local two or three quark spin correlations respectively in the quark gluon plasma produced in the collision processes. We present the relationships between these measurable tensor polarizations and quark spin correlations in the quark matter system.

II
quark spin correlations

The definitions of quark spin polarizations and correlations in QGP have been described systematically in
[
65
]
.
We summarize the main results that are used in the following of this paper in this section.

We consider a quark matter system such as QGP consisting of quarks and anti-quarks.
The spin properties of the system are described by the spin density matrix
\hat{\rho}
.
For single particle states, the spin density matrix is decomposed as

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{2}(1+P_{1i}\hat{\sigma}_{i}),

(1)

where
P_{1i}=\langle\hat{\sigma}_{i}\rangle={\rm Tr}[\hat{\rho}^{(1)}\hat{\sigma_{i}}]
with

i=x,y,z
is the
i
-th component of the quark polarization vector,

\hat{\sigma}_{i}
denotes Pauli matrices and
{\rm Tr}\hat{\rho}^{(1)}=1
is normalized to one.
Also, we use the convention that a sum over repeated indices is implicit through out the paper.

For two particle systems (12),

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{(12)}=\hat{\rho}^{(1)}\otimes\hat{\rho}^{(2)}+\frac{1}{2^{2}}c_{ij}^{(12)}\hat{\sigma}_{1i}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{2j},

(2)

where
c_{ij}^{(12)}
is the spin correlation between particle 1 and 2.
Compared to the conventional decomposition in terms of

\{1\otimes 1,\hat{\sigma}_{1i}\otimes 1,1\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{2j},\hat{\sigma}_{1i}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{2j}\}
,
the decomposition given by Eq. (
2
) has the clear advantage that
c_{ij}^{(12)}=0
if there is no spin correlation, i.e.
\hat{\rho}^{(12)}=\hat{\rho}^{(1)}\otimes\hat{\rho}^{(2)}
.
Here as well as in the following of this paper, we take the same scheme of notations as that in
[
65
]
:
the superscript of the spin density matrix
\hat{\rho}
or the spin correlation
c
denotes the type of particle
where for hadrons we simply use the symbol while for quarks or anti-quarks we put it in a bracket;
the subscript of them denotes the indices of matrix elements or spatial components.
For polarization vectors, we simply use double subscripts to specify particle type and spatial component respectively.

If particles in the system have other degrees of freedom that are denoted by
\alpha
,
we consider the
\alpha
-dependence so that spin density matrices are given by

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{(1)}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\bigl[1+P_{1i}(\alpha)\hat{\sigma}_{i}\bigr],

(3)

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{(12)}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=\hat{\rho}^{(1)}(\alpha_{1})\otimes\hat{\rho}^{(2)}(\alpha_{2})

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +\frac{1}{2^{2}}c_{ij}^{(12)}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\hat{\sigma}_{1i}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{2j}.

(4)

For the system (12) at the state
|\alpha_{12}\rangle
, the effective spin density matrix at
\alpha_{12}
is

\displaystyle\hat{\bar{\rho}}^{(12)}

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha_{12}|\hat{\rho}^{(12)}|\alpha_{12}\rangle=\langle\hat{\rho}^{(12)}\rangle,

(5)

where
\langle\cdots\rangle=\sum_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}(\cdots)|\langle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}|\alpha_{12}\rangle|^{2}
denotes the average over the state
|\alpha_{12}\rangle
.
We decompose it in the same way

\displaystyle\hat{\bar{\rho}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(1+\bar{P}_{1i}\hat{\sigma}_{1i}),

(6)

\displaystyle\hat{\bar{\rho}}^{(12)}

\displaystyle=\hat{\bar{\rho}}^{(1)}\otimes\hat{\bar{\rho}}^{(2)}+\frac{1}{2^{2}}\bar{c}_{ij}^{(12)}\hat{\sigma}_{1i}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{2j},

(7)

and obtain the effective polarization and correlations as

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{1i}=\langle P_{1i}\rangle,

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{ij}^{(12)}=\langle c_{ij}^{(12)}\rangle+\bar{c}_{ij}^{(12;0)},

(8)

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{ij}^{(12;0)}=\langle P_{1i}P_{2j}\rangle-\bar{P}_{1i}\bar{P}_{2j},

where
\bar{c}_{ij}^{(12)}
,
c_{ij}^{(12)}
and
\bar{c}_{ij}^{(12;0)}

are called the effective, genuine and induced quark spin correlations respectively.

Similarly, for three particle system (123) at
|\alpha_{123}\rangle
,

\displaystyle\hat{\bar{\rho}}^{(123)}=

\displaystyle\hat{\bar{\rho}}^{(1)}\otimes\hat{\bar{\rho}}^{(2)}\otimes\hat{\bar{\rho}}^{(3)}+\frac{1}{2^{2}}\Bigl[\bar{c}_{ij}^{(12)}\hat{\sigma}_{1i}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{2j}\otimes\hat{\bar{\rho}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle+\bar{c}_{jk}^{(23)}\hat{\bar{\rho}}^{(1)}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{2j}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{3k}+\bar{c}_{ik}^{(13)}\hat{\sigma}_{1i}\otimes\hat{\bar{\rho}}^{(2)}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{3k}\Bigr]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2^{3}}\bar{c}_{ijk}^{(123)}\hat{\sigma}_{1i}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{2j}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{3k},

(9)

where the effective polarizations such as
\bar{P}_{1i}

and effective two-particle spin correlations such as
\bar{c}_{ij}^{(12)}

have similar expressions as those in the two-particle system given
by Eq. (
8
), and the effective three-particle spin
correlation
\bar{c}_{ijk}^{(123)}
is given by

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{ijk}^{(123)}=

\displaystyle\langle c_{ijk}^{(123)}\rangle+\bar{c}_{ijk}^{(123;1)},

(10)

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{ijk}^{(123;1)}=

\displaystyle\langle P_{1i}P_{2j}P_{3k}+c_{ij}^{(12)}P_{3k}+c_{ik}^{(13)}P_{2j}+c_{jk}^{(23)}P_{1i}\rangle

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{ij}^{(12)}\bar{P}_{3k}-\bar{c}_{ik}^{(13)}\bar{P}_{2j}-\bar{c}_{jk}^{(23)}\bar{P}_{1i}-\bar{P}_{1i}\bar{P}_{2j}\bar{P}_{3k},

where
\bar{c}_{ijk}^{(123)}
reduces to
\bar{c}_{ijk}^{(123;1)}

if
c_{ijk}^{(123)}=0
but there are two-particle spin correlations.
It further reduces to

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{ijk}^{(123;0)}=

\displaystyle\langle P_{1i}P_{2j}P_{3k}\rangle-\langle P_{1i}P_{2j}\rangle\bar{P}_{3k}-\langle P_{1i}P_{3k}\rangle\bar{P}_{2j}

\displaystyle-\langle P_{2j}P_{3k}\rangle\bar{P}_{1i}+2\bar{P}_{1i}\bar{P}_{2j}\bar{P}_{3k},

(11)

if the genuine three and two particle spin correlations all vanish.

\bar{c}_{ijk}^{(123;1)}
and
\bar{c}_{ijk}^{(123;0)}
are called the first and zeroth order induced spin correlations respectively.

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}=\frac{1}{4}\left(1+\frac{4}{5}S^{i}\hat{\Sigma}^{i}+\frac{2}{3}T^{ij}\hat{\Sigma}^{ij}+\frac{8}{9}R^{ijk}\hat{\Sigma}^{ijk}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}(\hat{\Sigma}^{ij}\hat{\Sigma}^{k}+\hat{\Sigma}^{jk}\hat{\Sigma}^{i}+\hat{\Sigma}^{ki}\hat{\Sigma}^{j})

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{15}(\hat{\Sigma}^{i}\delta^{jk}+\hat{\Sigma}^{j}\delta^{ik}+\hat{\Sigma}^{k}\delta^{ij}).

(14)

The vector polarization is
S=(0,S_{T}^{x},S_{T}^{y},S_{L})

is the element of the rank two tensor polarization

These different components of polarizations can be measured in two body decays of these particles and the corresponding formulae

III.2
With other degrees of freedom

If we have other degrees of freedom denoted by
\alpha
, we calculate all quantities at given
\alpha_{B}
.
The results presented in Sec.
III.1
should be replaced by those with all quantities on the rhs being replaced by the effective ones.
For example,
P_{qi}\to\bar{P}_{qi}
,
c^{(qq)}_{ij}\to\bar{c}^{(qq)}_{ij}
and both the effective polarization
\bar{P}_{qi}
and quark spin correlation
\bar{c}^{(qq)}_{ij}
are functions of
\alpha_{B}
as given by

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{qi}(\alpha_{B})=\sum_{\alpha_{n}}|\langle\alpha_{n}|\alpha_{B}\rangle|^{2}P_{qi}(\alpha_{n}),

(27)

\displaystyle\bar{c}^{(qq)}_{ij}(\alpha_{B})=\sum_{\alpha_{n}}|\langle\alpha_{n}|\alpha_{B}\rangle|^{2}c_{ij}^{(qq)}(\alpha_{n})+\bar{c}^{(qq;0)}_{ij}(\alpha_{B}).

(28)

From the results given by Eqs (
19
-
26
), we see clearly that
S_{L}(\alpha_{B})
is sensitive to
\bar{P}_{qi}(\alpha_{B})
,

S_{LL}(\alpha_{B})
is sensitive to
\bar{c}^{(qq)}_{ij}(\alpha_{B})
and
S_{LLL}(\alpha_{B})
is sensitive to
\bar{c}^{(qqq)}_{ijk}(\alpha_{B})
respectively.
As shown by Eqs. (
27
) and (
28
), the effective quantities are results obtained after averaging over those inside the baryon
B
in
\alpha
-space.
Hence we see that
S_{L}(\alpha_{B})
is sensitive to quark polarization averaged inside
B
,
while
S_{LL}(\alpha_{B})
and
S_{LLL}(\alpha_{B})
are sensitive to the local two and three quark spin correlations respectively.
Even if we further average over

IV
Summary and outlook

We extend the study on global polarizations and spin correlations given in
[
65
]
to spin-
3/2
hadrons
and present the results for their global polarizations in relativistic heavy ion collisions.
The polarizations of spin-
3/2
hadrons are decomposed into vector, the second rank and third rank tensor polarizations.
The results show that the vector polarizations are mainly determined by the quark polarization,
the second rank tensor polarizations are determined by the local quark-quark spin correlations
and the third rank tensor polarizations are determined the local spin correlations of three quarks.

As shown in
[
65
]
, when other degrees of freedom besides spin are taken into account,
the global polarizations of produced hadrons are determined by the effective ones of quarks averaged insides the hadron.
The quark spin correlations are mainly determined by those insides the hadron, i.e. the local spin correlations.
Measurements of such polarization properties provide an opportunity to study local quark spin correlations
in relativistic heavy ion collisions.
In this sense, the second and third rank tensor polarizations provide us an ideal place to study
local two and three quark spin correlations in QGP produced in relativistic heavy ion collisions.

Nonabelian embedding tensors
Rong Tang, Yunhe Sheng

Abstract
. In this paper, first we introduce the notion of a nonabelian embedding tensor, which is a nonabelian generalization of an embedding tensor. Then we introduce the notion of a Leibniz-Lie algebra, which is the underlying algebraic structure of a nonabelian embedding tensor, and can also be viewed as a nonabelian generalization of a Leibniz algebra. Next using the derived bracket, we construct a differential graded Lie algebra, whose Maurer-Cartan elements are exactly nonabelian embedding tensors. Consequently, we obtain the differential graded Lie algebra that governs deformations of a nonabelian embedding tensor. Finally, we define the cohomology of a nonabelian embedding tensor and use the second cohomology group to characterize linear deformations.



1.
Introduction

The notion of embedding tensors
[
23
]
can be traced back to the study of the gauged supergravity theory. Embedding tensors were used to construct N =8 supersymmetric gauge theories and to study the Bagger-Lambert theory of multiple M2-branes in
[
4
]
. Embedding tensors were also used to study
E_{11}

[
3
]
and deformations of maximal 9-dimensional supergravity
[
7
]
. Recently, this topic has attracted much attention of the mathematical physics world
[
16
,
20
]
. An embedding tensor on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a representation
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a
linear map
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

[Tu,Tv]=T(\rho(Tu)v),\quad\forall u,v\in V.

An embedding tensors gives rise to a tensor hierarchy algebra
[
26
]
, while the existing constructions
[
19
,
21
]
are still mysterious.

Embedding tensors have been already studied in mathematics under the name of averaging operators since 1960s. Luxuriant studies on averaging operators were done for Banach algebras
[
17
,
22
,
27
]
from the analysis point of view. Recently, there are more and more studies of averaging operators from the algebraic point of view. Averaging operators on various types of algebras were studied in
[
1
]
. In particular, an averaging operator on a Lie algebra gives rise to a Leibniz algebra. More generally, averaging
operators are studied on the algebras over the general operad
\mathcal{P}
and give rise to the di-
\mathcal{P}
algebras and tri-
\mathcal{P}
algebras
[
12
]
.
Averaging operators are related to the
procedure of replication, Hadamard products and Manin white products in the operad theory
[
12
,
24
,
25
,
31
]
, and can be applied to study double
algebras, classical Yang-Baxter equations and Gröbner-Shirshov bases
[
10
,
11
,

.

Note that the above vector space
V
in the definition of an embedding tensor is understood as the space that 1-form fields take values in. Since for the classical gauge field theories, a 1-form field usually takes values in a Lie algebra, so it is natural to replace the vector space
V
by a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
. This will further need an action of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
, and lead to the ‘nonabelian embedding tensor’, which is the main subject that discussed in this paper. Such generalization for averaging operators on associative algebra were already considered in
[
12
,
24
]
, which can be understood as weighted averaging operators.

3.1
). In particular, if the Lie algebra structure is trivial, then it reduces to a Leibniz algebra. So a Leibniz-Lie algebra can be viewed as a nonabelian generalization of a Leibniz algebra. To justify nonabelian embedding tensors are indeed good mathematical structures, we further construct a differential graded Lie algebra such that its Maurer-Cartan elements are exactly nonabelian embedding tensors. As a byproduct, we obtain the differential graded Lie algebra that governs deformations of a nonabelian embedding tensor. The deformation of algebraic structures were originated from the pioneer
work
[
9
]
of Gerstenhaber. In general, deformation theory was developed for algebras over binary quadratic operads by Balavoine
[
2
]
. The deformation theory of embedding tensors were studied in
[
28
]
. In the associative algebra context,
the deformation theory of averaging operators was studied in
[
6
]
via the derived brackets
[
15
,
31
]
, and the deformation theory of averaging
associative algebras was studied in
[
32
]
.

A nonabelian embedding tensor induces a Leibniz-Lie algebra, which further gives rise to a Leibniz algebra, which is called the descendent Leibniz algebra of the nonabelian embedding tensor. We introduce a cohomology theory of nonabelian embedding tensors via the Loday-Pirashvili cohomology of the descendent Leibniz algebras. As applications, we use the second cohomology group to characterize equivalent linear deformations of a nonabelian embedding tensor. Moreover, we introduce the notion of a Nijenhuis element for a nonabelian embedding tensor, and show that trivial linear deformations are generated by Nijenhuis elements.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notion of nonabelian embedding tensors, and show that nonabelian embedding tensors can be characterized by graphs of the nonabelian hemisemidirect product Leibniz algebra. In Section
3
, we define a new algebraic structure, which is called a Leibniz-Lie algebra. We show that a nonabelian embedding tensor naturally induces a Leibniz-Lie algebra. In Section
4
, we construct a differential graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are nonabelian embedding tensors. In Section
5
, we introduce a cohomology theory of nonabelian embedding tensors. In Section
6
, we study linear deformations of nonabelian embedding tensors using the established cohomology theory.

2.
Nonabelian embedding tensors and Leibniz algebras

In this section, we introduce the notion of nonabelian embedding tensors, which are the nonabelian generalization of embedding tensors. We show that nonabelian embedding tensors can be characterized by graphs of the nonabelian hemisemidirect product Leibniz algebra.

A
Leibniz algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{G}
together with a bilinear operation
[-,-]_{\mathfrak{G}}:\mathfrak{G}\otimes\mathfrak{G}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{G}
such that

\displaystyle[x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{G}}]_{\mathfrak{G}}=[[x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}},z]_{\mathfrak{G}}+[y,[x,z]_{\mathfrak{G}}]_{\mathfrak{G}},\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{G}.

A
representation
of a Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{G}})
is a triple
(V;\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
, where
V
is a vector space,
\rho^{L},\rho^{R}:\mathfrak{G}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(V)
are linear maps such that for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{G}
,

\displaystyle\rho^{L}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\rho^{L}(x),\rho^{L}(y)],

\displaystyle\rho^{R}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\rho^{L}(x),\rho^{R}(y)],

\displaystyle\rho^{R}(y)\circ\rho^{L}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\rho^{R}(y)\circ\rho^{R}(x).

Here
[-,-]:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{gl}(V)\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is the commutator Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be Lie algebras. A Lie algebra homomorphism
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
is called an
action
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
. An action
\rho
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
is called a
coherent action
if it satisfies

(1)

[\rho(x)u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0,\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

We denote a coherent action by
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
. Denote by
\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})\subset\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
the Lie subalgebra of the derivation Lie algebra
\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
satisfying

\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})=\{D\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})|[Du,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0,~\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}\}.

The Lie algebra
\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})
is called the
coherent derivation Lie algebra
. A coherent action can be alternatively described by a homomorphism from the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
to the coherent derivation Lie algebra
\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})
.

Example 2.1
.

Recall that a
two-step nilpotent Lie algebra

[
30
]
is a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
such that

(2)

[[x,y],z]=0,\,\,\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

For a two-step nilpotent Lie algebra, the adjoint action
\mathrm{ad}
is a coherent action and
\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
nonabelian embedding tensor
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
is a linear map
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that

(3)

[Tu,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T\big(\rho(Tu)(v)+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}\big),\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two nonabelian embedding tensors on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
. A
homomorphism
from
T^{\prime}
to
T
consists of two Lie algebra homomorphisms
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
and
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
such that

(4)

\displaystyle T\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ T^{\prime},

(5)

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}\rho(x)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in\mathfrak{h}.

In particular, if both
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}
are invertible,
(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
is called an
isomorphism
from
T^{\prime}
to
T
.

Remark 2.4
.

When
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}})
is an abelian Lie algebra, we obtain that
T
is a usual embedding tensor
[
16
,
28
]
. In addition, when
\rho
is the trivial action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
,
T
is a Lie algebra homomorphism from
\mathfrak{h}
to
\mathfrak{g}
.

Consider the direct sum Lie algebra
\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})\oplus\mathfrak{h}
, in which the Lie bracket is given by

[A+u,B+v]=[A,B]+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall A,B\in\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h}),u,v\in\mathfrak{h}

Define
\rho:\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})\to\mathfrak{gl}(\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})\oplus\mathfrak{h})
by

\rho(A)(B+v)=Av,\quad\forall A,B\in\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h}),~v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Then it is straightforward to deduce that
\rho
is a coherent action of the Lie algebra
\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})
on the Lie algebra
\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})\oplus\mathfrak{h}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

The projection
\mathrm{pr}:\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})\oplus\mathfrak{h}\to\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})
is a nonabelian embedding tensor on the Lie algebra
\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})
with respect to the coherent action
(\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})\oplus\mathfrak{h};\rho).

Proof.

It follows from

\mathrm{pr}\Big(\rho(\mathrm{pr}(A+u))(B+v)+[A+u,B+v]\Big)=[A,B]=[\mathrm{pr}(A+u),\mathrm{pr}(B+v)],

for all
A,B\in\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h}),u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
.
∎

Example 2.6
.

The
Heisenberg Lie algebra

H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
is the

\displaystyle[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{3},\quad[e_{1},e_{3}]=0,\quad[e_{2},e_{3}]=0,

with respect to a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
. It is obvious that the Heisenberg Lie algebra

A linear transformation

[Te_{i},Te_{j}]=T([Te_{i},e_{j}]+[e_{i},e_{j}]),~~i,j\in\{1,2,3\}.

First by

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Te_{1},Te_{1}]=T([Te_{1},e_{1}]+[e_{1},e_{1}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T[r_{11}e_{1}+r_{21}e_{2}+r_{31}e_{3},e_{1}]=-r_{21}r_{13}e_{1}-r_{21}r_{23}e_{2}-r_{21}r_{33}e_{3},

we obtain

Similarly, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl rcl rcl}r_{12}r_{13}&=&0,&r_{12}r_{23}&=&0,&r_{12}r_{33}&=&0,\\

Therefore, we have

(i)

If

r_{21}&r_{22}&0\\
r_{31}&r_{32}&0\end{array}\right)

if and only if
r_{11}r_{22}=r_{12}r_{21}
. Moreover, for any

, we deduce that
T=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}c&d&0\\
kc&kd&0\\
a&b&0\end{array}\right)
and

.

(ii)

If

and

, then

,

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be Lie algebras. Let
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be a coherent action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
. On the direct sum vector space
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},
define a bilinear bracket operation
[-,-]_{\rho}
by

(6)

[x+u,y+v]_{\rho}=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho(x)v+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proposition 2.7
.

With the above notations,
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\rho})
is a Leibniz algebra, which is called the
nonabelian hemisemidirect product
, and denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes^{h}_{\rho}\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

By the Jacobi identities for the Lie brackets
[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}}
, and the fact that
\rho
is a homomorphism from the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
to the derivation Lie algebra
\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
, we have

\displaystyle[x+u,[y+v,z+w]_{\rho}]_{\rho}-[[x+u,y+v]_{\rho},z+w]_{\rho}-[y+v,[x+u,z+w]_{\rho}]_{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},z]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[y,[x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[u,[v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}-[[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},w]_{\mathfrak{h}}-[v,[u,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle+\rho(x)[v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}-[\rho(x)v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}-[v,\rho(x)w]_{h}+\rho(x)\rho(y)w-\rho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})w-\rho(y)\rho(x)w

\displaystyle+[u,\rho(y)w]_{\mathfrak{h}}-\rho(y)[u,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[u,\rho(y)w]_{\mathfrak{h}}-\rho(y)[u,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

By (
1
) and the fact that
\rho(y)\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
, we deduce that
[u,\rho(y)w]_{\mathfrak{h}}-\rho(y)[u,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0
. Therefore,
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\rho})
is a Leibniz algebra.
∎

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a linear map. Denote by
G_{T}
the graph of

, i.e.
G_{T}=\{Tu+u|\forall u\in\mathfrak{h}\}

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be Lie algebras. Let
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be a coherent action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
. Then a linear map
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a nonabelian embedding tensor if and only if its graph

is a Leibniz subalgebra of the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\rho})
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle[Tu+u,Tv+v]_{\rho}=[Tu,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho(Tu)v+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h},

which implies that the graph

is a Leibniz subalgebra if and only if (

is a nonabelian embedding tensor.
∎

Since
\mathfrak{h}
and

are naturally isomorphic, we get the following conclusion immediately.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a nonabelian embedding tensor on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
. Then
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{T})
is a Leibniz algebra, where the Leibniz bracket
[-,-]_{T}
is given by

(7)

[u,v]_{T}=\rho(Tu)v+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Moreover,
T
is a Leibniz algebra homomorphism from the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{T})
to the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

The Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{T})
is called the
descendent Leibniz algebra
of the nonabelian embedding tensor
T
.

Example 2.12
.

Consider the nonabelian embedding tensor given in Proposition
2.5
. Then by Corollary
2.11
, one obtain a Leibniz algebra structure on
\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})\oplus\mathfrak{h}:

[A+u,B+v]_{\mathrm{pr}}=[A+u,B+v]+\rho(\mathrm{pr}(A+u))(B+v)=[A,B]+Av+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},

for all
A,B\in\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h}),~u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
, which is exactly the nonabelian hemisemidirect product of
\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})
and
\mathfrak{h}
given in Proposition
2.7
.

The association of a Leibniz algebra from a nonabelian embedding tensor enjoys the functorial property.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two nonabelian embedding tensors on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
and
(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
a homomorphism from
T^{\prime}
to
T
. Then
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}
is a homomorphism of Leibniz algebras from
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{T^{\prime}})
to
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{T})
.

Proof.

For all
u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
, we
have

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}([u,v]_{T^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho(T^{\prime}u)v+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}})=\rho(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(T^{\prime}u))\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(v)+[\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u),\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(T\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u))\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(v)+[\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u),\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}=[\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u),\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(v)]_{T}.

Then
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}
is a homomorphism of Leibniz algebras from
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{T^{\prime}})
to
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{T})
.
∎

3.
Nonabelian embedding tensors and Leibniz-Lie algebras

In this section, we introduce the notion of a Leibniz-Lie algebra as the underlying algebraic structure of a nonabelian embedding tensor. A Leibniz-Lie algebra gives rise to a Leibniz algebra, as well as nonabelian embedding tensors via different approaches.

Definition 3.1
.

A
Leibniz-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}},\rhd)
consists of a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}})
and a binary product
\rhd:\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
such that for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{h},
one has

(8)

\displaystyle x\rhd(y\rhd z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\rhd y)\rhd z+y\rhd(x\rhd z)+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}\rhd z,

(9)

\displaystyle x\rhd[y,z]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x\rhd y,z]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0.

A homomorphism from a Leibniz-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}},\rhd)
to a Leibniz-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h}^{\prime},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}},\rhd^{\prime})
is a Leibniz algebra homomorphism
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}
such that
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x\rhd y)=\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)\rhd^{\prime}\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(y)
.

Remark 3.2
.

A Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{G}})
is naturally a Leibniz-Lie algebra, in which the Lie algebra structure is abelian.

Example 3.3
.

Let
H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
be the Heisenberg Lie algebra. We define a binary product
\rhd:H_{3}(\mathbb{C})\otimes H_{3}(\mathbb{C})\,\rightarrow\,H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
by

\begin{array}[]{rcl rcl rcl rcl}e_{1}\rhd e_{1}&=&-e_{3},&e_{1}\rhd e_{2}&=&e_{3},&e_{2}\rhd e_{2}&=&e_{3},&e_{2}\rhd e_{1}&=&-e_{3}\\
e_{1}\rhd e_{3}&=&0,&e_{2}\rhd e_{3}&=&0,&e_{3}\rhd e_{3}&=&0,&e_{3}\rhd e_{1}&=&e_{3}\rhd e_{2}=0.\end{array}

Then
(H_{3}(\mathbb{C}),[-,-],\rhd)
is a Leibniz-Lie algebra.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}},\rhd)
be a Leibniz-Lie algebra. Then the binary operation
[-,-]:\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
given by

(10)

\displaystyle[x,y]=x\rhd y+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}},\,\,\,\,\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{h},

defines a Leibniz algebra structure on
\mathfrak{h}
, which is denoted by
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathsf{Leib}}
and called the
subadjacent Leibniz algebra
.

Moreover, define
\mathcal{L}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
by

(11)

\mathcal{L}_{x}y=x\rhd y.

Then
(\mathfrak{h};\mathcal{L},0)
is a representation of the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-])
on
\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{h}
, we have

\displaystyle[x,[y,z]=[x,y\rhd z+[y,z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\rhd(y\rhd z)+[x,y\rhd z]_{\mathfrak{h}}+x\rhd[y,z]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Post-2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle x\rhd(y\rhd z)+[x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle[[x,y],z]+[y,[x,z]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x\rhd y+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}},z]+[y,x\rhd z+[x,z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\rhd y)\rhd z+[x\rhd y,z]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}\rhd z+[[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}},z]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle+y\rhd(x\rhd z)+[y,x\rhd z]_{\mathfrak{h}}+y\rhd[x,z]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[y,[x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Post-2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(x\rhd y)\rhd z+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}\rhd z+[[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}},z]_{\mathfrak{h}}+y\rhd(x\rhd z)+[y,[x,z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

By (
8
), we deduce that
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-])
is a Leibniz algebra.

Also by (
8
), we get

\mathcal{L}_{[x,y]}=[\mathcal{L}_{x},\mathcal{L}_{y}],

which implies that
(\mathfrak{h};\mathcal{L},0)
is a representation of the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-])
on
\mathfrak{h}
.
∎

An embedding tensor naturally induces a Leibniz algebra. Now a nonabelian embedding tensor naturally induces a Leibniz-Lie algebra.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a nonabelian embedding tensor on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
. Then there is a Leibniz-Lie algebra structure on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}})
given by

(12)

\displaystyle u\rhd v=\rho(Tu)v,\,\,\,\,\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proof.

For all
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
, we have

\displaystyle u\rhd(v\rhd w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(Tu)\rho(Tv)w=\rho([Tu,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}})w+\rho(Tv)\rho(Tu)w

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:nonET}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\rho\big(T(\rho(Tu)v+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}})\big)w+\rho(Tv)\rho(Tu)w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(u\rhd v)\rhd w+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}\rhd w+v\rhd(u\rhd w),

which implies that (
8
) in Definition
3.1
holds. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle u\rhd[v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}=\rho(Tu)[v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}=[\rho(Tu)v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[v,\rho(Tu)w]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

By (
1
),
u\rhd[v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}=[u\rhd v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0
. Thus,
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}},\rhd)
is a Leibniz-Lie algebra.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The Leibniz algebra determined by the Leibniz-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}},\rhd)
according to Theorem
3.4
is exactly the Leibniz algebra given in Corollary
2.11
.

Example 3.7
.

Consider the nonabelian embedding tensor given in Proposition
2.5
. Then by Theorem
3.5
,
(\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})\oplus\mathfrak{h},[-,-],\rhd)
is a Leibniz-Lie algebra, where
[-,-]
is the direct sum Lie bracket and
\rhd
is given by

(A+u)\rhd(B+v)=\rho(\mathrm{pr}(A+u))(B+v)=Av,

for all
A,B\in\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h}),~u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
.

Furthermore, by Theorem
3.4
, the Leibniz-Lie algebra
(\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})\oplus\mathfrak{h},[-,-],\rhd)
gives rise to a Leibniz algebra which is exactly the nonabelian hemisemidirect product Leibniz algebra given in Example
2.12
.

It is straightforward to obtain the following result, which implies that the association given in Theorem
3.5
is functorial.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two nonabelian embedding tensors on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
and
(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
a homomorphism from
T^{\prime}
to
T
. Then
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}
is a homomorphism of Leibniz-Lie algebras from
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\rhd_{T^{\prime}})
to
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\rhd_{T})
.

At the end of this section, we show that a Leibniz-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}},\rhd)
can give rise to a nonabelian embedding tensor via two different ways such that the subadjacent Leibniz algebra is exactly the descendent Leibniz algebra of the nonabelian embedding tensor. First we make some preparations. Let
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{G}})
be a Leibniz algebra. Denote by
\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G})
the ideal of squares generated by
[x,x]_{\mathfrak{G}}
for all
x\in\mathfrak{G}
. Then

\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}:=\mathfrak{G}/\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G})

is naturally a Lie algebra in which we denote the Lie bracket by
[-,-]_{\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}}
. The equivalence class of
x\in\mathfrak{G}
in
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}
will be denoted by
\bar{x}
. Moreover,
\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G})
is contained in the left center of the Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{G}.
We denote by

(13)

\mathrm{pr}:\mathfrak{G}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}

the natural projection from the Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{G}
to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}
. Obviously, the projection
\mathrm{pr}
preserves the bracket operation, i.e. the following equality holds:

(14)

\mathrm{pr}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}}=[\mathrm{pr}(x),\mathrm{pr}(y)]_{\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{G}.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}},\rhd)
be a Leibniz-Lie algebra. Since
[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}}
is skewsymmetric, the ideal
\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathsf{Leib}})
is generated by
x\rhd x
for all
x\in\mathfrak{h}
. Consider the quotient Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathsf{Leib}})_{\mathrm{Lie}}=\mathfrak{h}^{\mathsf{Leib}}/\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathsf{Leib}}).

Define an action of
(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathsf{Leib}})_{\mathrm{Lie}}
on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}})
by

\rho(\bar{u})v=u\rhd v,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

It is well defined. In fact, if
\bar{u}=\bar{u^{\prime}}
, then
u-u^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathsf{Leib}})
. By (
8
), we obtain
(u-u^{\prime})\rhd v=0.
Moreover, by (
9
), we have

[\rho(\bar{u})v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}=[u\rhd v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0,\quad\rho(\bar{u})[v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}={u}\rhd[v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}},\rhd)
be a Leibniz-Lie algebra. Then the natural projection
\mathrm{pr}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathsf{Leib}})_{\mathrm{Lie}}
is a nonabelian embedding tensor on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathsf{Leib}})_{\mathrm{Lie}}
with respect to the coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
such that the induced descendent Leibniz algebra is exactly the subadjacent Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathsf{Leib}}
.

Proof.

It follows from

\displaystyle[\mathrm{pr}(u),\mathrm{pr}(v)]_{(\mathfrak{h}^{\mathsf{Leib}})_{\mathrm{Lie}}}=\mathrm{pr}[u,v]=\mathrm{pr}([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+u\rhd v)=\mathrm{pr}([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho(\mathrm{pr}({u}))v)

directly.
∎

There is also another approach to construct a nonabelian embedding tensor from a Leibniz-Lie algebra. Let
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}},\rhd)
be a Leibniz-Lie algebra and
\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
the derivation Lie algebra of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}})
.
By (
9
),
\mathcal{L}_{u}\in\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})
, where
\mathcal{L}
is given by (
11
).

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}},\rhd)
be a Leibniz-Lie algebra. Then
\mathcal{L}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})
is a nonabelian embedding tensor on the Lie algebra
\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})
via the natural coherent action on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}})
such that the induced descendent Leibniz algebra is exactly the subadjacent Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathsf{Leib}}
.

Proof.

By (
8
), we have

\displaystyle[\mathcal{L}_{u},\mathcal{L}_{v}]=\mathcal{L}_{[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\mathcal{L}_{u}v},\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h},

which implies that
\mathcal{L}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\overline{\mathrm{Der}}(\mathfrak{h})
is a nonabelian embedding tensor. The other conclusion is obvious.
∎

4.
The controlling differential graded Lie algebra of nonabelian embedding tensors

In this section first we recall the controlling graded Lie algebra that characterize Leibniz algebras as Maurer-Cartan elements. Then we construct the controlling differential graded Lie algebra of nonabelian embedding tensors.

A permutation
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
is called an
(i,n-i)
-shuffle
if
\sigma(1)<\cdots<\sigma(i)
and
\sigma(i+1)<\cdots<\sigma(n)
. If
i=0
or
n
, we assume
\sigma={\rm{Id}}
. The set of all
(i,n-i)
-shuffles will be denoted by
\mathbb{S}_{(i,n-i)}
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a vector space. We consider the graded vector space

C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

It is known that
C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
equipped with the
Balavoine bracket

[
2
]
:

(15)

\displaystyle[P,Q]_{\mathsf{B}}=P\bar{\circ}Q-(-1)^{pq}Q\bar{\circ}P,\,\,\,\,\forall P\in C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),Q\in C^{q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),

is a graded Lie algebra, where
P\bar{\circ}Q\in C^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is defined by

P\bar{\circ}Q=\sum_{k=1}^{p+1}P\circ_{k}Q,

and
\circ_{k}
is defined by

\displaystyle(P\circ_{k}Q)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p+q+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,q)}}(-1)^{(k-1)q}(-1)^{\sigma}P(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(k-1)},Q(x_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(k+q-1)},x_{k+q}),x_{k+q+1},\cdots,x_{p+q+1}).

The following conclusion is straightforward.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
\Omega\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\Omega,\Omega]_{\mathsf{B}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\Omega\bar{\circ}\Omega(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\Omega(\Omega(x_{1},x_{2}),x_{3})-\Omega(x_{1},\Omega(x_{2},x_{3}))+\Omega(x_{2},\Omega(x_{1},x_{3})).

Thus,
\Omega
defines a Leibniz algebra structure if and only if
[\Omega,\Omega]_{\mathsf{B}}=0
, i.e.
\Omega
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
(C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[-,-]_{\mathsf{B}})
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be Lie algebras. Let
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be a coherent action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
.
Consider the graded vector space

C^{*}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}):=\oplus_{n=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
.
Define a linear map
\mathrm{d}:\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})\to\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n+1}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
by

(16)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{d}f)(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{n-1+i}f(v_{1},\cdots,\hat{v}_{i},\cdots,v_{j-1},[v_{i},v_{j}]_{\mathfrak{h}},v_{j+1},\cdots,v_{n+1}),

for all
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
and
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{h}
.
Also define a graded bracket operation

\left\llbracket-,-\right\rrbracket:\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{m}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})\times\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})\to\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{m+n}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),

(17)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\theta,\phi\right\rrbracket(v_{1},\cdots,v_{m+n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,n)}}(-1)^{(k-1)n+1}(-1)^{\sigma}\theta(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k-1)},\rho(\phi(v_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k+n-1)}))v_{k+n},v_{k+n+1},\cdots,v_{m+n})

\displaystyle+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(m,n)}}(-1)^{mn+1}(-1)^{\sigma}[\theta(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(m)}),\phi(v_{\sigma(m+1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(m+n-1)},v_{\sigma(m+n)})]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,m)}}(-1)^{m(k+n-1)}(-1)^{\sigma}\phi(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k-1)},\rho(\theta(v_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(k+m-1)}))v_{k+m},v_{k+m+1},\cdots,v_{m+n}),

for all
\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{m}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),~\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
and
v_{1},\cdots,v_{m+n}\in\mathfrak{h}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be a coherent action of a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Then the triple
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket-,-\right\rrbracket,\mathrm{d})
is a differential graded Lie algebra, whose Maurer-Cartan elements are nonabelian embedding tensors.

Proof.

In short, the graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket-,-\right\rrbracket)
is obtained via the derived bracket
[
15
]
. In fact, the Balavoine bracket
[-,-]_{\mathsf{B}}
associated to the direct sum vector space
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
gives rise to a graded Lie algebra
(\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}),\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}),[-,-]_{\mathsf{B}})
. Obviously
\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k+1}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
is an abelian subalgebra. We denote the Lie brackets
[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}}
by
\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}
respectively, and denote the hemisemidirect product Leibniz algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
by
\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}\boxplus\rho
. Thus
\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}\boxplus\rho
and
\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}
are Maurer-Cartan elements of the graded Lie algebra
(\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}),\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}),[-,-]_{\mathsf{B}})
. Define the derived bracket on the graded vector space
\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
by

\left\llbracket\theta,\phi\right\rrbracket:=(-1)^{m-1}[[\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}\boxplus\rho,\theta]_{\mathsf{B}},\phi]_{\mathsf{B}},\quad\forall\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{m}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),~\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),

which is exactly the bracket given by (
17
). By
[\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}\boxplus\rho,\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}\boxplus\rho]_{\mathsf{B}}=0,
we deduce that
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket-,-\right\rrbracket)
is a graded Lie algebra.

Define a linear map
\mathrm{d}=:[\mu_{\mathfrak{h}},-]_{\mathsf{B}}
on the graded space
\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}),\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h})
. We obtain that
\mathrm{d}
is closed on the subspace
\oplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
, and is given by (
16
).

By
[\mu_{\mathfrak{h}},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathsf{B}}=0
, we obtain that
\mathrm{d}\circ\mathrm{d}=0.
Moreover, by
\mathrm{Im}(\rho)\subset\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
and
[\rho(x)u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0
, we obtain that
[\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}\boxplus\rho,\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathsf{B}}=0.
Then, we deduce that
\mathrm{d}
is a derivation of
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket-,-\right\rrbracket)
. Therefore,
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket-,-\right\rrbracket,\mathrm{d})
is a differential graded Lie algebra.

Finally, for a degree 1 element
T\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
, we have

\displaystyle\big(\mathrm{d}T+\frac{1}{2}\left\llbracket T,T\right\rrbracket\big)(u,v)=-T[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}-T(\rho(Tu)v)+[Tu,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Thus, Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely nonabelian embedding tensors.
∎

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a nonabelian embedding tensor. Since
T
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the differential graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket-,-\right\rrbracket,\mathrm{d})
by Theorem
4.2
, we deduce that

d_{T}:=\mathrm{d}+\left\llbracket T,-\right\rrbracket

is a graded derivation on the graded Lie
algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket-,-\right\rrbracket)
satisfying
d^{2}_{T}=0
.
Therefore,
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket-,-\right\rrbracket,d_{{T}})
is a differential graded Lie algebra.
This differential graded Lie algebra can control deformations of nonabelian embedding tensors.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a nonabelian embedding tensor on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
and
T^{\prime}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
a
linear map. Then
T+T^{\prime}
is still
a nonabelian embedding tensor on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
if and only if
T^{\prime}
is a Maurer-Cartan
element of the differential graded Lie algebra

(C^{*}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket-,-\right\rrbracket,d_{{T}})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.2
,
T+T^{\prime}
is
a nonabelian embedding tensor if and only if

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(T+T^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}\left\llbracket T+T^{\prime},T+T^{\prime}\right\rrbracket=0,

which is equivalent to

d_{T}T^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\left\llbracket T^{\prime},T^{\prime}\right\rrbracket=0.

That is,
T^{\prime}
is a Maurer-Cartan
element of

(C^{*}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket-,-\right\rrbracket,d_{{T}})
.
∎

5.
Cohomologies of nonabelian embedding tensors

In this section, we establish a cohomology theory for nonabelian embedding tensors via the Loday-Pirashvili cohomology of the descendent Leibniz algebra given in Corollary
2.11
.

Definition 5.1
.

(
[
18
]
)
Let
(V;\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
be a representation of a Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{G}})
.
The
Loday-Pirashvili cohomology
of
\mathfrak{G}
with coefficients in
V
is the cohomology of the cochain complex
(C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{G},V)=\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}C^{k}(\mathfrak{G},V),\partial)
, where
C^{k}(\mathfrak{G},V)=\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{G},V)
and the coboundary operator

\partial:C^{k}(\mathfrak{G},V)\to C^{k+1}(\mathfrak{G},V)

is defined by

\displaystyle(\partial f)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}\rho^{L}(x_{i})f(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+1})+(-1)^{k+1}\rho^{R}(x_{k+1})f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k+1}(-1)^{i}f(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{j-1},[x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{G}},x_{j+1},\cdots,x_{k+1}),

for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1}\in\mathfrak{G}
.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a nonabelian embedding tensor on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
.
By Corollary
2.11
,
[u,v]_{T}:=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho(Tu)v
defines a Leibniz algebra structure on
\mathfrak{h}
. Furthermore, define
\rho^{L}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\rho^{R}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
by

(18)

\rho^{L}(u)y:=[Tu,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\rho^{R}(v)x:=[x,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}-T(\rho(x)v).

Lemma 5.2
.

With above notations,
(\mathfrak{g};\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
is a representation of the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{T})
.

Proof.

By (
3
), for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\rho^{L}([u,v]_{T})-[\rho^{L}(u),\rho^{L}(v)]\Big)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[T[u,v]_{T},x]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[Tu,[Tv,x]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Tv,[Tu,x]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[Tu,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}},x]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[Tu,[Tv,x]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Tv,[Tu,x]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

which implies that
\rho^{L}([u,v]_{T})=[\rho^{L}(u),\rho^{L}(v)]
.

By (
1
) and (
3
), for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\rho^{R}([u,v]_{T})-[\rho^{L}(u),\rho^{R}(v)]\Big)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,T[u,v]_{T}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-T(\rho(x)[u,v]_{T})-[Tu,[x,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}-T(\rho(x)v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[[Tu,x]_{\mathfrak{g}},Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}-T(\rho([Tu,x]_{\mathfrak{g}})v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,[Tu,{T}v]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[Tu,[x,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[[Tu,x]_{\mathfrak{g}},Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle-T[u,\rho(x)v]_{\mathfrak{g}}-T\rho(x)\rho(Tu)v+[Tu,T(\rho(x)v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}-T\rho(Tu)\rho(x)v+T\rho(x)\rho(Tu)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

which implies that
\rho^{R}([u,v]_{T})=[\rho^{L}(u),\rho^{R}(v)]
.

Finally, also by (
1
) and (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\Big(\rho^{R}(v)\circ\rho^{L}(u)+\rho^{R}(v)\circ\rho^{R}(u)\Big)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{R}(v)(-T\rho(x))=-[T\rho(x)u,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}+T\rho(T\rho(x)u)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[\rho(x)u,v]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0,

which implies that
\rho^{R}(v)\circ\rho^{L}(u)+\rho^{R}(v)\circ\rho^{R}(u)=0.
Therefore,
(\mathfrak{g};\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
is a representation of the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{T})
.
∎

Let
\partial_{T}:\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})\to\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k+1}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
be the corresponding Loday-Pirashvili coboundary operator of the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{T})
with coefficients in
(\mathfrak{g};\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
. More precisely,
\partial_{T}:\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})\to\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k+1}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
is given by

(19)

\displaystyle\partial_{T}\theta(u_{1},\cdots,u_{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}[Tu_{i},\theta(u_{1},\cdots,\hat{u}_{i},\cdots,u_{k+1})]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k+1}[\theta(u_{1},\cdots,u_{k}),Tu_{k+1}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+(-1)^{k}T(\rho(\theta(u_{1},\cdots,u_{k}))u_{k+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k+1}(-1)^{i}\theta(u_{1},\cdots,\hat{u}_{i},\cdots,u_{j-1},\rho(Tu_{i})(u_{j})+[u_{i},u_{j}]_{\mathfrak{h}},u_{j+1},\cdots,u_{k+1}).

Now we define a cohomology theory governing deformations of a nonabelian embedding tensor
T:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}.
Define the space of
0
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{0}(T)
to be
0
and the space of
1
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{1}(T)
to be
\mathfrak{g}
. For
n\geq 2
, define the space of
n
-cochains
\mathfrak{C}^{n}(T)
as
\mathfrak{C}^{n}(T)=\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n-1}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
be a nonabelian embedding tensor on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
. We define the
cohomology of
the nonabelian embedding tensor
T
to be the cohomology of the cochain complex
(\mathfrak{C}^{\bullet}(T)=\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{C}^{k}(T),\partial_{T})
.

Denote the set of
k
-cocycles by
\mathcal{Z}^{k}(T)
and the set of
k
-coboundaries by
\mathcal{B}^{k}(T)
. Denote by

(20)

\mathcal{H}^{k}(T)=\mathcal{Z}^{k}(T)/\mathcal{B}^{k}(T),\quad k\geq 0,

the corresponding
k
-th cohomology group.

Comparing the coboundary operators
\partial_{T}
given above and the
operators
d_{{T}}=\mathrm{d}+\left\llbracket T,-\right\rrbracket
defined by the Maurer-Cartan element
T
, we have

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
{T}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a nonabelian embedding tensor. Then we have

(21)

\displaystyle\partial_{T}\theta=(-1)^{k-1}d_{T}\theta,\quad\forall\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}),\,\,k=1,2,\cdots,

Proof.

For all
u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{k+1}\in\mathfrak{h}
and
\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
, we have

\displaystyle(d_{T}\theta)(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{d}\theta+\left\llbracket T,\theta\right\rrbracket)(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k+1}(-1)^{k-1+i}\theta(u_{1},\cdots,\hat{u}_{i},\cdots,u_{j-1},[u_{i},u_{j}]_{\mathfrak{h}},u_{j+1},\cdots,u_{k+1})-T\big(\rho(\theta(u_{1},\cdots,u_{k}))u_{k+1}\big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{k+1}(-1)^{i-1}[Tu_{i},\theta(u_{1},\cdots,\hat{u}_{i},\cdots,u_{k+1})]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k+1}(-1)^{(j-1+k-1)}(-1)^{j-i-1}\theta(u_{1},\cdots,\hat{u}_{i},\cdots,u_{j-1},\rho(T(u_{i}))u_{j},u_{j+11},\cdots,u_{k+1}),

which implies that
\partial_{T}\theta=(-1)^{k-1}d_{T}\theta
. The proof is finished.
∎

6.
Linear deformations of nonabelian embedding tensors

In this section, we study linear deformations of a nonabelian embedding tensor using the cohomology theory given in the
previous section. In particular, we introduce the notion of a
Nijenhuis element associated to a nonabelian embedding tensor, which gives
rise to a trivial linear deformation of the
nonabelian embedding tensor.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
T
be a nonabelian embedding tensor on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
and
\mathfrak{T}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
a linear map. If
T_{t}=T+t\mathfrak{T}
is still a nonabelian embedding tensor on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
for all
t\in\mathbb{C}
, we say that
\mathfrak{T}
generates a
linear deformation
of the nonabelian embedding tensor
T
.

By considering the coefficients of
t
and
t^{2}
, one deduce that
T_{t}=T+t\mathfrak{T}
is a linear deformation of a nonabelian embedding tensor
T
if and only if
for any
u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
,

(22)

\displaystyle~[Tu,\mathfrak{T}v]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[\mathfrak{T}u,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(\rho(\mathfrak{T}u)v)+\mathfrak{T}(\rho(Tu)v+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}),

(23)

\displaystyle~[\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}v]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}(\rho(\mathfrak{T}u)v).

Note that Eq. (
22
) means that
\mathfrak{T}
is a 1-cocycle of the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
with coefficients in the representation
(\mathfrak{g};\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
given by Lemma
5.2
and Eq. (
23
) means that
\mathfrak{T}
is an embedding tensor on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the representation
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-])
be a Leibniz algebra and
\omega:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{G}
be a linear map. If for any
t\in\mathbb{C}
, the multiplication
[-,-]_{t}
defined by

[u,v]_{t}=[u,v]+t\omega(u,v),\;\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{G},

also gives a Leibniz algebra structure, we say that
\omega
generates a
linear deformation
of the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-])
.

The two types of linear deformations are related as follows.

Proposition 6.2
.

If
\mathfrak{T}
generates a linear deformation of a nonabelian embedding tensor
T
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
, then the product
\omega_{\mathfrak{T}}
on
\mathfrak{h}
defined by

\omega_{\mathfrak{T}}(u,v)=\rho(\mathfrak{T}u)v,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h},

generates a linear deformation of the associated Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{T})
.

Proof.

We denote by
[-,-]_{t}
the corresponding Leibniz algebra structure associated to the nonabelian embedding tensor
T+t\mathfrak{T}
. Then we have

\displaystyle[u,v]_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho((T+t\mathfrak{T})u)v+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(Tu)v+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+t\rho(\mathfrak{T}u)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[u,v]_{T}+t\omega_{\mathfrak{T}}(u,v),\quad\forall u,v\in V,

which implies that
\omega_{\mathfrak{T}}
generates a linear deformation of
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{T})
.∎

Definition 6.3
.

Let
T
be a nonabelian embedding tensor on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
. Two
linear deformations
T^{1}_{t}=T+t\mathfrak{T}_{1}
and

T^{2}_{t}=T+t\mathfrak{T}_{2}
are said to be

if there exists
an

such that
({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{x},{{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+t\rho(x))
is a
homomorphism from
T^{2}_{t}
to
T^{1}_{t}
. In particular, a
linear deformation
T_{t}=T+t\mathfrak{T}
of a
nonabelian embedding tensor

is said to be
trivial
if there exists
an
x\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{x},{{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+t\rho(x))
is a
homomorphism from
T_{t}
to
T
.

Let
({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{x},{{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+t\rho(x))
be a homomorphism from

T^{2}_{t}
to
T^{1}_{t}
. Then
{{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{x}

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})

(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{x})[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{x})(y),({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{x})(z)]_{\mathfrak{g}},\;\forall y,z\in\mathfrak{g},

\displaystyle({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+t\rho(x))[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+t\rho(x))(u),({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+t\rho(x))(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}},\;\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h},

which implies that
x
satisfies

(24)

[[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},[x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0,\quad\forall y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then by Eq. (
4

(T+t\mathfrak{T}_{1})({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+t\rho(x))(u)=({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{x})(T+t\mathfrak{T}_{2})(u),\quad\forall u\in\mathfrak{h},

which implies

(25)

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{T}_{2}-\mathfrak{T}_{1})(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\rho(x)u-[x,Tu]_{\mathfrak{g}},

(26)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{1}\rho(x)(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,\mathfrak{T}_{2}u]_{\mathfrak{g}},\;\forall u\in\mathfrak{h}.

Finally, Eq. (

({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+t\rho(x))\rho(y)(u)=\rho(({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{x})(y))({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+t\rho(x))(u),\quad\forall y\in\mathfrak{g},u\in\mathfrak{h},

(27)

\rho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})\rho(x)=0,\quad\forall y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Note that Eq. (
25

\mathfrak{T}_{2}-\mathfrak{T}_{1}=\partial_{T}x
. Thus, we have the following result:

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
T
be a nonabelian embedding tensor on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)

If two linear deformations

and

\mathfrak{T}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{T}_{2}
are in the same cohomology class of
\mathcal{H}^{2}(T)
.

Definition 6.5
.

Let
T
be a nonabelian embedding tensor on a Lie algebra

24

Theorem 6.6

It follows from

Now we recall the notion of a Nijenhuis operator on a Leibniz algebra, which gives rise to a trivial linear deformation of a Leibniz algebra.

Definition 6.8
.

(
[
5
]

A linear map

(30)

[Nu,Nv]_{\mathfrak{G}}=N([Nu,v]_{\mathfrak{G}}+[u,Nv]_{\mathfrak{G}}-N[u,v]_{\mathfrak{G}}),\quad u,v\in\mathfrak{G}.

For its connection with a Nijenhuis element associated to
a nonabelian embedding tensor, we have the following result.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
x\in\mathfrak{g}
be a Nijenhuis element associated to a
nonabelian embedding tensor
T
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a coherent action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho)
. Then
\rho(x)
is a Nijenhuis operator
on the descendent Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{T})
.

Proof.

For all
u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(x)([\rho(x)u,v]_{T}+[u,\rho(x)v]_{T}-\rho(x)[u,v]_{T})-[\rho(x)u,\rho(x)v]_{T}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{new-leibniz}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\rho(x)\Big(\rho(T\rho(x)u)v+[\rho(x)u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho(Tu)\rho(x)v+[u,\rho(x)v]_{\mathfrak{h}}-\rho(x)(\rho(Tu)v+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}})\Big)

\displaystyle-\rho(T\rho(x)u)\rho(x)v-[\rho(x)u,\rho(x)v]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:extracon}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\rho(x)\big(\rho(T\rho(x)u)v+\rho(Tu)\rho(x)v-\rho(x)\rho(Tu)v\big)-\rho(T\rho(x)u)\rho(x)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho([x,T\rho(x)u]_{\mathfrak{g}})v+\rho(x)\rho([Tu,x]_{\mathfrak{g}})v

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Nijenhuis}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\rho([x,[x,Tu]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}})v+\rho(x)\rho([Tu,x]_{\mathfrak{g}})v{=}-\rho([x,Tu]_{\mathfrak{g}})\rho(x)v

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Nij3}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 0.

Thus, we deduce that
\rho(x)
is a Nijenhuis operator
on the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{T})
.
∎

We end this section by giving a concrete example of a linear deformation of a nonabelian embedding tensor
T
on the Heisenberg Lie algebra
H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
.

Example 6.10
.

Let
H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
be the Heisenberg Lie algebra. Then
T_{t}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4t}}{2}&0&0\\
0&\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4t}}{2}&0\\
a&b&t\end{array}\right)

is a linear deformation of the nonabelian embedding tensor
T=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
a&b&0\end{array}\right)
on
H_{3}(\mathbb{C})
with respect to the adjoint action for all
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
.

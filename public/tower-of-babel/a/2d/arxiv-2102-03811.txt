Duo property for rings by the quasinilpotent perspective
Abdullah Harmanci, Yosum Kurtulmaz, Burcu Ungor

Abstract
. In this paper, we focus on the duo ring property via quasinilpotent elements which gives a new kind of generalizations of commutativity. We call this kind of ring qnil-duo . Firstly, some properties of quasinilpotents in a ring are provided. Then the set of quasinilpotents is applied to the duo property of rings, in this perspective, we introduce and study right (resp., left) qnil-duo rings. We show that this concept is not left-right symmetric. Among others it is proved that if the Hurwitz series ring H ⁡ ( R , α ) H(R;\alpha) is right qnil-duo, then R R is right qnil-duo. Every right qnil-duo ring is abelian. A right qnil-duo exchange ring has stable range 1. 2010 MSC: 16U80, 16U60, 16N40 Keywords: Quasinilpotent element, duo ring, qnil-duo ring

In

[

]
, the concept of right unit-duo ring was introduced,
namely, a ring

if for every

. Left unit-duo rings are defined
similarly. In
[
9

, the normal property of elements on

is called

for all
a\in R
. Left normal on Jacobson radical rings can be
defined analogously. Also in
[
9
]
, on the one hand, a ring

for all

is

for all
a\in R
where
N_{*}(R)
is
the lower nilradical of
R

nilradical rings are defined similarly. Also, a ring

is called

for every

. Left nilpotent duo rings are defined similarly (see

[
7

).

Motivated by the works on duo property for rings, the goal of this
paper is to approach the notion of duo rings by the way of
quasinilpotent elements, in this regard, we introduce the notion of
qnil-duo rings. Firstly, we investigate some properties of
quasinilpotent elements which we need for the investigation of
qnil-duo property. Then we study some properties of this class of
rings and observe that being a qnil-duo ring need not be
left-right symmetric. It is proved that any right (resp., left)
qnil-duo ring is abelian, and any exchange right (resp., left)
qnil-duo ring has stable range 1. It is observed that regularity
and strongly regularity coincide for right (resp., left) qnil-duo
rings. We also study on some extensions of rings such as Dorroh
extensions, Hurwitz series rings and some subrings of matrix rings
in terms of qnil-duo property.

3.
Qnil-duo Rings

In this section, we deal with the right duo property on the set of
quasinilpotent elements. By this means, we give a generalization
of commutativity from the perspective of quasinilpotents.

Definition 3.1
.

A ring
R
is called
right qnil-duo

if
R^{qnil}a\subseteq aR^{qnil}
for every
a\in R
. Similarly,

R
is called
left qnil-duo
if
aR^{qnil}\subseteq R^{qnil}a
for every
a\in R
. If
R
is both right and left
qnil-duo, then it is called
qnil-duo
, i.e.
R^{qnil}a=aR^{qnil}
for every
a\in R
.

The qnil-duo property of rings is not left-right symmetric as the
following example shows.

Example 3.2
.

Let

Lemma 3.4

Let

Theorem 3.5
.

Let

. Then

,

.
By Proposition

,
b_{i}\in R_{i}^{qnil}
for each

.

for each

. Set
c=(c_{i})
. Then

. Hence

. Conversely,
suppose that

is right qnil-duo. Let

and

. Let

,

-entry of

is

and the other entries are 0

-entry of

and by Proposition

,

. Supposition implies there exists

such that

Recall that a ring

Theorem 3.6
.

Let

be a ring. Then the following hold.

(1)

and

for every

and
e^{2}=e\in R

(2)

(3)

Let R be a ring and

(R)
. If

is right (resp., left) qnil-duo ring, then

and

are right (resp., left) qnil-duo

is central.

(1) Let

comm
(ex-exe)
. Then

.
So we have
(t(xe-exe))^{2}=0
. Hence
1-(ex-exe)t
is

. Similarly,

.

(2) Let

. By (1),

for

. It implies for any

. So

.
Similarly,

(3) It is clear by Theorem

.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Every right (resp., left) qnil-duo ring is directly finite.

Proof.

Let

be a right qnil-duo ring and

,

Theorem 3.11

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
(2), we have

. We prove

.
Let

,

.
If

Therefore

.

(1) Let

a\in R

A=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\

X=\begin{bmatrix}b&0\\
0&b\end{bmatrix}\in K_{0}(R)
. By Proposition
4.8
,
X\in K_{0}(R)^{qnil}
. There exists
X^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}x^{\prime}&y^{\prime}\\
z^{\prime}&t^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\in K_{0}(R)^{qnil}
such that
XA=AX^{\prime}
. Hence
ba=ax^{\prime}
. By Proposition
4.10
,
x^{\prime}\in R^{qnil}
. So
ba=ax^{\prime}\in aR^{qnil}
.

(2) Let
R
be a ring having no nonzero zero divisors. Assume that

K_{0}(R)
is a right qnil-duo ring. Let
a\in R
,
b\in R^{qnil}
.
If
a=0
or
b=0
, there is nothing to do. Let
a\neq 0
and

b\neq 0
and consider
A=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&a\end{bmatrix}
,

B=\begin{bmatrix}b&0\\
0&b\end{bmatrix}\in K_{0}(R)
. By
Proposition
4.8
,
B\in K_{0}(R)^{qnil}
. There exists
B^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}x^{\prime}&y^{\prime}\\
z^{\prime}&t^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\in K_{0}(R)^{qnil}
such
that
BA=AB^{\prime}
. It implies
ba=ax^{\prime}=at^{\prime}
,
ay^{\prime}=0
and
az^{\prime}=0
. Hence
x^{\prime}=t^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}=z^{\prime}=0
. Hence
x^{\prime}\in R^{qnil}
by
Proposition
4.10

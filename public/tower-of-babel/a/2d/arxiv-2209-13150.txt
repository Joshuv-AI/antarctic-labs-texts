Interaction of the Primitive Equations with Sea Ice Dynamics
Tim Binz, Felix Brandt, Matthias Hieber

Abstract
. This article establishes local strong well-posedness and global strong well-posedness close to constant equilibria of a model coupling the primitive equations of ocean and atmospheric dynamics with Hibler’s viscous-plastic sea ice model. In order to treat the coupling conditions, an approach involving the hydrostatic Dirichlet and Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator is developed. Mapping properties of the latter operators are investigated for the first time and are of central importance for showing that the operator associated with the linearized coupled system admits a bounded ℋ ∞ \mathcal{H}^{\infty} -calculus on suitable L q \mathrm{L}^{q} -spaces. Quasilinear methods allow then to obtain the strong well-posedeness results described above.

1.
Introduction

The mathematical analysis of the primitive equations of the large-scale atmosphere and ocean dynamics was pioneered by Lions, Temam and Wang in
[
32
,
33
]
.
Moreover, Lions, Temam and Wang introduced and analyzed a coupled atmosphere-ocean model with wind-driven boundary conditions
[
34
,
35
]
.
Ever since then, the study of coupled atmosphere-ocean models has been an active field of research.
We refer here for instance to the handbook article of Temam and Ziane
[
47
]
.
In this article, for the first time, we rigorously investigate the interaction problem of the atmosphere and the ocean when additionally considering sea ice as a thin layer coupled to the atmosphere and the ocean.

In a seminal paper, Cao and Titi
[
10
]
established global strong well-posedness of the primitive equations for initial data in
\mathrm{H}^{1}
by means of energy methods. For related results, we refer
to the work of Kukavica and Ziane
[
29
]
.
A different approach to the primitive equations by means of evolution equations was developed by Hieber and Kashiwabara
[
26
]
as well as Giga, Gries, Hieber, Hussein and Kashiwabara
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
. Details of this approach and further references can be found in
[
24
]
. We also refer to the article
[
31
]
for a survey of results and further references.

In 1979, Hibler
[
22
]
proposed the governing equations of large-scale sea ice dynamics. They were investigated numerically by different communities,
see e. g.
[
46
,
37
,
38
,
45
]
. For surveys on the modeling of sea ice, we refer to
[
44
]
and
[
20
]
.
We also refer here to the recent article of Piersanti and Temam
[
40
]
on the modeling and analysis of the dynamics of grounded shallow ice sheets.

This article is concerned with the analysis of a coupled atmosphere-sea ice-ocean model.
More precisely, the primitive equations for the ocean and atmosphere dynamics are coupled with the sea ice equations as introduced in
[
22
]
.
For a precise formulation of the coupling conditions, see (
3.1
), (
3.2
)
and (
3.3
) in
Section 3
. For related results on the primitive equations subject to stochastic wind driven boundary conditions, we refer to the recent work
[
7
]
.

It is the aim of this article to show that the coupled system of the ocean, the atmosphere and sea ice, see (
3.4
) below, has a unique, local, strong solution, and that it is
globally strongly well-posed for initial data close to constant equilibria.

To this end, we rewrite the system of equations as a quasilinear evolution equation subject to coupling conditions. In order to treat these coupling conditions, we develop an approach
involving the hydrostatic Dirichlet and Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator. These operators have been studied intensively in the parabolic situation and also for fluids but not for geophysical flows as
the primitive equations. The results on well-posedness of the stationary hydrostatic Stokes problem with inhomogeneous boundary conditions developed in
Section 7
will then be of central
importance for our approach. Indeed, the hydrostatic Dirichlet and Dirichlet-to-Neumann operators allow to show that the operator associated with the linearized coupled system admits a bounded

\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus on suitable
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-spaces. The latter property allows to use modern quasilinear methods for solving the coupled system within the strong setting.

With regard to the global strong well-posedness close to constant equilibria, we make use of the so-called generalized principle of linearized stability, see
[
42
]
and
[
41
]
.
We show that constant equilibria are normally stable by means of energy estimates and absorption arguments. More precisely, energy estimates yield that the spectrum of the total linearization
\mathcal{A}_{0}
besides zero is contained in the left half-plane, that the set of equilibria close to constants is a
\mathrm{C}^{1}
-manifold and that zero is a semi-simple eigenvalue of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
.
We also show that the spectrum of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is independent of
q
and that thus the above properties of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
obtained via energy estimates within the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-setting transfer to
the
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-setting for
q\in(1,\infty)
. Note that due to the quasilinear nature of sea ice dynamics, this range of
q
is important.

For further information concerning quasilinear evolution equations, maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity, bounded
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus and decoupling techniques, we refer e. g. to

[
4
]
,
[
13
]
, and
[
41
]
.

Let us note that the rigorous analysis of the single sea ice component in our model began only recently by the article of Brandt, Disser, Haller-Dintelmann and Hieber
[
9
]

as well as the article of Liu, Thomas and Titi
[
36
]
. The associated system of equations is coupled, degenerate, quasilinear and parabolic-hyperbolic.
The interaction problem of sea ice with a rigid body has been studied in
[
5
]
.

Models describing the coupling of sea ice with certain flows were introduced within the geophysical community e. g. by Hibler and Bryan
[
23
]
or by Timmermann, Beckmann and
Hellmer
[
48
]
.
Let us emphasize that these works are concerned with the development of models and not with its analysis.
For analysis of related models, we refer to
[
14
]
.
Coupled models of the atmosphere and the ocean call for an investigation of boundary layers.
This is left to future study, and we refer to the work of Dalibard and Gérard-Varet
[
11
]
or Dalibard and Saint-Raymond
[
12
]
for boundary layer theory in the context of geophysical flows.

The structure of this article is as follows:
In
Section 2
, we introduce the notation and give basic concepts used throughout the paper.

Section 3
is dedicated to deriving the coupling conditions as well as the complete coupled atmosphere-sea ice-ocean system, and to stating the two main results
on the local strong well-posedness and the global strong well-posedness close to constant equilibria, respectively.
We then reformulate the coupled system of equations (
3.4
) as a quasilinear evolution equation in
Section 4
.
The sea ice equations on the quadratic domain
(0,1)\times(0,1)
are investigated in
Section 5

Section 6
.
The central
Section 7
deals with the stationary hydrostatic Stokes problem and the hydrostatic Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator.
In
Section 8

\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
-calculus of the coupled operator, and we show Lipschitz estimates of the coupled system.

Section 9
and
Section 10
are concerned with the proof of the main results.

We use
x
and

as well as

The sub- or superscripts

,

For instance,

,

represent the full, horizontal and vertical velocity of the atmospheric wind,

With

and

.
For the sea ice part, with

, we also introduce the mean ice thickness

and the mean ice compactness

, defined as the ratio of thick ice per area.
We remark that
\kappa_{1}>0
sufficiently small is a parameter indicating the transition to open water, i. e., a value of

less than

means that at
(x,y)\in G
at time

there is open water.
In contrast,
\kappa_{2}>0

Unless stated otherwise, domains will be denoted by
\Omega
, possibly with a sub- or superscript, while
\Gamma
represents boundaries.
The domains under consideration are
{\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}}=G\times(\kappa_{2},{h}_{\mathrm{atm}})
for the atmosphere, where

{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}=G\times(-{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}},0)
for the ocean.
The upper boundary is denoted by
\Gamma_{u}:=G\times\{{h}_{\mathrm{atm}}\}
, the lower boundary by
\Gamma_{b}:=G\times\{-{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\}

We remark that these boundaries and interfaces will be identified with

in the sequel.
In addition,

The thickness of the sea ice, expressed by

, is relatively small compared to the heights of the ocean and the atmosphere.
For simplicity, we assume that the interfaces between the sea ice and the ocean as well as the atmosphere are flat in this article.
The more involved consideration of free surfaces is left to future studies.

Furthermore,

,

and
\mathrm{div}\,f=\partial_{x}f_{1}+\partial_{y}f_{2}+\partial_{z}f_{3}
represent the Laplacian, the gradient and the divergence of a vector field

, while the respective horizontal objects are denoted by
\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}=\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}
,
\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}=(\partial_{x},\partial_{y})^{\top}
and
\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,g=\partial_{x}g_{1}+\partial_{y}g_{2}
for a vector field

.
The trace and the normal derivative on some boundary
\Gamma
will be denoted by

, respectively.
As it is only used in the context of the sea ice, the deformation tensor
\varepsilon

.

Consider a domain

,

,

.
We denote by

\mathrm{L}^{q}
-spaces, by

[

]

[

]
.
If

For

,

, we denote by

, i. e.,

.
The primitive equations are modeled on the hydrostatic solenoidal

-spaces given by the
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-closure of the smooth hydrostatic functions, so following the approach developed in

{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}(\Omega):=\overline{\{v\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{per}}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{2}\colon\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,\overline{v}=0\}}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}},

and horizontal periodicity is incorporated by the function spaces

and

,

and
\mathrm{B}^{{s}}_{{q}{p},\mathrm{per}}(G)

The Bessel potential spaces
\mathrm{H}^{{s},q}(\Omega)
and Besov spaces
\mathrm{B}^{{s}}_{{q}{p}}(\Omega)

.

.

3.
Main Results for the Coupled System

The main assumptions with regard to the coupling of the atmosphere and the ocean with sea ice are that the first two exert the forces
{\tau}_{\mathrm{atm}}
and
{\tau}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
on the sea ice, and that the velocity of the sea ice coincides with the horizontal velocity of the ocean at the water surface.
More precisely, we assume that the force exerted by the atmosphere is given by

(3.1)

{\tau}_{\mathrm{atm}}={\rho}_{\mathrm{atm}}{C}_{\mathrm{atm}}|\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{i}}{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}|{R}_{\mathrm{atm}}\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{i}}{v}_{\mathrm{atm}},\text{ on }{G}.

The force exerted by the ocean on the sea ice is supposed to be proportional to the shear rate, so it is of the form

(3.2)

{\tau}_{\mathrm{ocn}}=-{\mu}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\partial_{\nu_{\Gamma_{o}}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}},\text{ on }{G}.

This is in accordance with a plane Couette flow for a Newtonian fluid, where the stress tensor is given by
\mathrm{T}-\pi\mathrm{I}_{3}
, with
\mathrm{T}
denoting the usual Cauchy stress tensor,
compare e. g.
[
43
, Section 7.2]
.
The condition on the equality of the velocities on the interface of the ocean and the sea ice can be expressed by

(3.3)

\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{o}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}={v}_{\mathrm{ice}},\text{ on }{G}.

In the above,
{\rho}_{\mathrm{atm}}
represents the density for air,
{C}_{\mathrm{atm}}
denotes an air drag coefficient, and
{R}_{\mathrm{atm}}
is a rotation matrix operating wind vectors, while
{\mu}_{\mathrm{ocn}}>0
is the viscosity associated to the fluid described by the primitive equations of the ocean.

Before providing the complete coupled system, we briefly discuss the internal ice stress.
We follow
[
22
]
and refer e. g. to
[
9
, Section 2]
for a more thorough treatment.
In fact, the viscous-plastic rheology is expressed by a constitutive law linking the internal ice stress
\sigma
and the deformation tensor
\varepsilon
via an internal ice strength
P
as well as nonlinear bulk and shear viscosities such that the principal components of the stress lie on an elliptical yield curve.
With
e>1
denoting the ratio of major to minor axes, the constitutive law is given by

\sigma=\frac{1}{e^{2}}\frac{P}{\triangle(\varepsilon)}\varepsilon+\left(1-\frac{1}{e^{2}}\right)\frac{P}{2\triangle(\varepsilon)}\mathrm{tr}(\varepsilon)\mathrm{I}_{2}-\frac{P}{2}\mathrm{I}_{2},

where for the ice thickness
h
, the ice compactness
a
and given constants
p^{*}>0
and
c>0
, the ice strength
P
takes the shape
P=P(h,a)=p^{*}h\exp(-c(1-a))
.
Moreover, we have

\triangle^{2}(\varepsilon):=\left(\varepsilon_{11}^{2}+\varepsilon_{22}^{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{e^{2}}\right)+\frac{4}{e^{2}}\varepsilon_{12}^{2}+2\varepsilon_{11}\varepsilon_{22}\left(1-\frac{1}{e^{2}}\right).

Even though the above law represents an idealized viscous-plastic material, the viscosities become singular for
\triangle
tending to
0
.
Following
[
9
,
37
]
, for
\delta>0
, we take the regularization
\triangle_{\delta}(\varepsilon):=\sqrt{\delta+\triangle^{2}(\varepsilon)}
into account and define the regularized ice stress by

\sigma_{\delta}:=\frac{1}{e^{2}}\frac{P}{\triangle_{\delta}(\varepsilon)}\varepsilon+\left(1-\frac{1}{e^{2}}\right)\frac{P}{2\triangle_{\delta}(\varepsilon)}\mathrm{tr}(\varepsilon)\mathrm{I}_{2}-\frac{P}{2}\mathrm{I}_{2}.

We follow
[
32
,
33
,
35
]
for the incompressible, viscous primitive equations of the atmosphere and the ocean as well as
[
22
]
for the sea ice equations.
The complete coupled system is then given by

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}-\Delta{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}+({{u}_{\mathrm{atm}}}\cdot\nabla){{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}}+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}{\pi}_{\mathrm{atm}}&={f}_{\mathrm{atm}},&&\text{ on }{{\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}}\times[0,T]},\\
\partial_{z}{\pi}_{\mathrm{atm}}&=0,&&\text{ on }{{\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}}\times[0,T]},\\
\mathrm{div}\,{u}_{\mathrm{atm}}&=0,&&\text{ on }{{\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}}\times[0,T]},\\
\partial_{t}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}-\Delta{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}+({{u}_{\mathrm{ocn}}}\cdot\nabla){{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}}+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}{\pi}_{\mathrm{ocn}}&={f}_{\mathrm{ocn}},&&\text{ on }{{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\times[0,T]},\\
\partial_{z}{\pi}_{\mathrm{ocn}}&=0,&&\text{ on }{{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\times[0,T]},\\
\mathrm{div}\,{u}_{\mathrm{ocn}}&=0,&&\text{ on }{{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\times[0,T]},\\
\partial_{t}{v}_{\mathrm{ice}}-\frac{1}{m}\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,\sigma_{\delta}+({{v}_{\mathrm{ice}}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}){{v}_{\mathrm{ice}}}&=\frac{1}{m}{\tau}_{\mathrm{atm}}+\frac{1}{m}{\tau}_{\mathrm{ocn}}-g\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}H,&&\text{ on }{G\times[0,T]},\\
\partial_{t}h-d_{\mathrm{h}}\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}h+\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,({v}_{\mathrm{ice}}h)&=\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{h}},&&\text{ on }{G\times[0,T]},\\
\partial_{t}a-d_{\mathrm{a}}\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}a+\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,({v}_{\mathrm{ice}}a)&=\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{a}},&&\text{ on }{G\times[0,T]},\\
\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{o}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}&={v}_{\mathrm{ice}},&&\text{ on }{G\times[0,T]}.\end{aligned}\right.

In the above,
{f}_{\mathrm{atm}}
as well as
{f}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
represent external forces,
m={\rho}_{\mathrm{ice}}h
denotes the mass of the sea ice, where
{\rho}_{\mathrm{ice}}>0
is the density,
g
denotes the gravity and
H\colon G\times[0,T]\to[0,\infty)
the sea surface dynamic height,
\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{h}}
and
\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{a}}
are thermodynamic terms discussed in more detail in
Section 5
, and
d_{\mathrm{h}}
,
d_{\mathrm{a}}>0
are constants.
For simplicity, we omit Reynolds numbers and terms associated to the Coriolis force in (
3.4
), but we emphasize that they can be incorporated easily, see
Section 3
.

The system is completed by Neumann boundary conditions for the horizontal velocity of the atmospheric wind
{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}
, Dirichlet boundary conditions for the horizontal velocity of the ocean
{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
on the lower boundary
\Gamma_{b}
and Dirichlet boundary conditions for the vertical velocity of the atmospheric wind and the ocean
{w}_{\mathrm{atm}}
and

, respectively.
This can be summarized by

(3.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu_{\Gamma_{u}}}{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}

\displaystyle=0\text{ on }{\Gamma_{u}},\quad\partial_{\nu_{\Gamma_{i}}}{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}=0\text{ on }{\Gamma_{i}},\quad\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{b}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}=0\text{ on }{\Gamma_{b}},\text{ as well as}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{u}}{w}_{\mathrm{atm}}

\displaystyle=0\text{ on }{\Gamma_{u}},\quad\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{i}}{w}_{\mathrm{atm}}=0\text{ on }{\Gamma_{i}},\quad\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{o}}{w}_{\mathrm{ocn}}=0\text{ on }{\Gamma_{o}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{b}}{w}_{\mathrm{ocn}}=0\text{ on }{\Gamma_{b}}.

On the lateral boundaries, we assume that all variables are periodic, i. e.,
{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}
and
{\pi}_{\mathrm{atm}}
periodic on
\Gamma_{l,\mathrm{atm}}
,
{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
and
{\pi}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
periodic on
\Gamma_{l,\mathrm{ocn}}
and
{v}_{\mathrm{ice}}
,

and
a
periodic on
\partial G
.
The coupling condition (

) is assumed for the horizontal velocity of the ocean on the interface

, and it is already included in (

).

We also consider initial values
{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}(0)=v_{\mathrm{atm},0}
,
{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}(0)=v_{\mathrm{ocn},0}
,
{v}_{\mathrm{ice}}(0)=v_{\mathrm{ice},0}
,

and

.
The ground space is given by

\mathcal{X}_{0}:={\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}})\times{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})\times\mathrm{L}^{q}(G)^{2}\times\mathrm{L}^{q}(G)\times\mathrm{L}^{q}(G).

Equipped with a product norm,

becomes a Banach space.
Moreover, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}:=\{

\displaystyle({v}_{\mathrm{atm}},{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}},{v}_{\mathrm{ice}},h,a)\in(\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{2},q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}})^{2}\cap{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}}))\times{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})\times\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{2},q}(G)^{2}\times\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{2},q}(G)\times\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{2},q}(G):

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{P}}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\Delta{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\in{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}),\kern 5.0pt\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{o}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}={v}_{\mathrm{ice}}\text{ on }{\Gamma_{o}},\text{ and \eqref{eq:boundary conditions v} is satisfied}\},

where
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
denotes the hydrostatic Helmholtz projection associated to the ocean as made precise in

, and
{w}_{\mathrm{atm}}
and
{w}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
can be recovered from
{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}
and
{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
, respectively, see also

.
For the initial data, we introduce the trace space
\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}=(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{X}_{1})_{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}},p}
, which is discussed in more detail in

.
For

,

with
\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}<\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
, we have

if and only if

\displaystyle{\displaystyle v}_{\mathrm{atm}}\in\mathrm{B}_{qp,\mathrm{per}}^{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}})^{2}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}}),\kern 5.0pt{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\in\mathrm{B}_{qp,\mathrm{per}}^{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})^{2}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})\text{ and }({v}_{\mathrm{ice}},h,a)\in\mathrm{B}_{qp,\mathrm{per}}^{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}}(G)^{4},\text{ with}

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu_{\Gamma_{u}}}{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}=\partial_{\nu_{\Gamma_{i}}}{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}=0,\kern 5.0pt\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{b}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}=0\text{ and }\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{o}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}={v}_{\mathrm{ice}}.

We introduce an open set
V=V^{\mathrm{atm}}\times V^{\mathrm{ocn}}\times V^{\mathrm{ice}}\subset\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}
such that for
({v}_{\mathrm{atm}},{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}},{v}_{\mathrm{ice}},h,a)\in V
, we have

(3.7)

\kappa_{1}<h<\kappa_{2}\quad\text{for}\quad 0<\kappa_{1}<\kappa_{2}<\infty\quad\quad\text{and}\quad a\in(0,1).

Below, we provide conditions on the initial data and the external forcing terms for the main results.

Assumption 3.1
.

For
V\subset\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}
from (
3.7
), the initial data satisfy
u_{0}\in V
, so

u_{0}=(v_{\mathrm{atm},0},v_{\mathrm{ocn},0},v_{\mathrm{ice},0},h_{0},a_{0})\in\mathrm{B}_{qp,\mathrm{per}}^{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}})^{2}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}})\times\mathrm{B}_{qp,\mathrm{per}}^{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})^{2}\cap\mathrm{L}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})\times\mathrm{B}_{qp,\mathrm{per}}^{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}}(G)^{4}

subject to (
3.5
) and
\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{o}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}={v}_{\mathrm{ice}}
, and so that
h_{0}\in(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})
and
a_{0}\in(0,1)
.
Besides, for
T>0
, the external forcing terms fulfill
{f}_{\mathrm{atm}}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}})^{2})
,
{f}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})^{2})
and
\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}H\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{q}(G)^{2})
.

Our first main result on the local existence and uniqueness of a strong solution to (
3.4
) reads as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
such that
\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}<\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
, and assume that the initial data
u_{0}
and the external forcing terms
{f}_{\mathrm{atm}}
,
{f}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
and
\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}H
satisfy
Section 3
.
Then there exist
T^{\prime}=T^{\prime}(u_{0})\in(0,T]
and
r=r(u_{0})
with
\overline{B}_{\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}}(u_{0},r)\subset V
such that for for each initial value
u_{1}\in\overline{B}_{\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}}(u_{0},r)
, the coupled system (
3.4
), subject to the boundary conditions (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), admits a unique strong solution

u(\cdot,u_{1})=({v}_{\mathrm{atm}},{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}},{v}_{\mathrm{ice}},h,a)(\cdot,u_{1})\in\mathbb{E}_{T^{\prime}}:=\mathrm{W}^{{1},p}(0,T^{\prime};\mathcal{X}_{0})\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T^{\prime};\mathcal{X}_{1}).

We briefly discuss some further properties of the solution from
Theorem 3.2
.

Corollary 3.3
.

(1)

The solution class satisfies
\mathrm{W}^{{1},p}(0,T^{\prime};\mathcal{X}_{0})\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T^{\prime};\mathcal{X}_{1})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{C}([0,T^{\prime}],\mathcal{X}_{\gamma})
.

(2)

Given an initial value
u_{0}\in V
, there exists
C=C(u_{0})
such that for all
u_{1},u_{2}\in\overline{B}_{\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}}(u_{0},r)
, it holds that

\|u(\cdot,u_{1})-u(\cdot,u_{2})\|_{\mathbb{E}_{T^{\prime}}}\leq C\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}}
.

(3)

The solution regularizes instantly in time, i. e.,
t\partial_{t}v\in\mathrm{W}^{{1},p}(0,T^{\prime};\mathcal{X}_{0})\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T^{\prime};\mathcal{X}_{1})
. In particular,
u\in\mathrm{C}^{1}([b,T^{\prime}];\mathcal{X}_{\gamma})\cap\mathrm{C}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}([b,T^{\prime}];\mathcal{X}_{1})

for every
b\in(0,T^{\prime})
.

(4)

The solution
u=u(u_{0})
exists on a maximal time interval
J(u_{0})=[0,t_{+}(u_{0}))
, and the latter is characterized by the alternatives

(i) global existence, i. e., it holds that
t_{+}(u_{0})=\infty
, or

(ii)
\lim_{t\to t_{+}(u_{0})}\mathrm{dist}(u(t),\partial V)=0
, or

(iii)
\lim_{t\to t_{+}(u_{0})}u(t)
does not exist in
\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}
.

For
h_{*}
and
a_{*}
constant in space and time, it readily follows that
(0,0,0,h_{*},a_{*})
are equilibria to (
3.4
) provided the external forces
{f}_{\mathrm{atm}}
,
{f}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
as well as
g\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}H
vanish and the thermodynamic terms
\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{h}}
and
\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{a}}
are neglected.
Setting
{\tau}_{\mathrm{atm}}=0
, we then verify the normal stability of the above equilibria in the trace space
\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}
, resulting in the existence of a unique global strong solution for initial data close to the equilibrium
(0,0,0,h_{*},a_{*})
.
This leads to the second main result of this article.

Theorem 3.4
.

For
h_{*}\in(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})
and
a_{*}\in(0,1)
constant in space and time,
u_{*}=(0,0,0,h_{*},a_{*})
is a stable equilibrium in
\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}
.
Moreover, there exists
r>0
such that the unique solution
u
of (
3.4
) with
{f}_{\mathrm{atm}}={f}_{\mathrm{ocn}}=g\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}H={\tau}_{\mathrm{atm}}=0
,
\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{h}}=\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{a}}=0
and
u_{0}
fulfilling
Section 3
as well as
\|u_{0}-u_{*}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}}<r
exists on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and converges at an exponential rate in
\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}
to some equilibrium
u_{\infty}
of (
3.4
) as

.

The following remarks on

and

are in order at this point.

.

For simplicity of the presentation we did not introduce time weights

for the solution space to exploit the parabolic regularization.

Assuming that for

such that

lie in an open set

as
in (

), and supposing that the external forcing terms satisfy the above assumptions, we obtain an analogous statement as in

, but the solution

is contained
in the class

.

Remark 3.6
.

(a) The statements of

and

remain valid when including Coriolis terms as in
[
9
, Section 2]
.
They are omitted here for simplicity of the presentation.

(b) Let

,
\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{H},\mathrm{ocn}}
,
\mathrm{Re}_{z,\mathrm{atm}}
and

denote the horizontal and vertical Reynolds numbers in the context of the atmosphere and the ocean.
Instead of

and

4.
Rewriting the Coupled System as a Quasilinear Evolution Equation

The first step in the proof of
Theorem 3.2
is to write the coupled system of PDEs (
3.4
) as a quasilinear evolution equation in the ground space
\mathcal{X}_{0}
.

4.1.
The hydrostatic Helmholtz projection and the hydrostatic Stokes operator

For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<b
, we consider the cylindrical domain
\Omega=G\times(a,b)
.
The incompressible, isothermal primitive equations for the velocity
u=(v,w)
of the fluid and the surface pressure
\pi
are given by

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v-\Delta v+({u}\cdot\nabla){v}+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi&=f,&&\text{ on }{\Omega\times[0,T]},\\
\partial_{z}\pi&=0,&&\text{ on }{\Omega\times[0,T]},\\
\mathrm{div}\,u&=0,&&\text{ on }{\Omega\times[0,T]},\\
v(0)&=u_{0},&&\text{ on }{\Omega}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
f
is an external force term, and
v=(v_{1},v_{2})
denotes the horizontal velocity, whereas
w
represents the vertical velocity.
In the sequel, the remaining surface pressure will be denoted by
\pi_{s}
.
On the upper and bottom parts of the boundary
\partial\Omega
,
v
satisfies Dirichlet or Neumann conditions conditions on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
, respectively, while
w
is subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions.
Both,
v
and
w
, as well as the pressure
\pi_{s}
are assumed to be periodic on the lateral boundary
\Gamma_{l}
.

In conjunction with the boundary conditions
w=0
on
G\times\{a,b\}
, the incompressibility condition
\mathrm{div}\,u=0
yields that the vertical component
w
can be expressed by
w(\cdot,\cdot,z)=-\int_{a}^{z}\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,v(\cdot,\cdot,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi
,
z\in[a,b]
.

Denoting by
\overline{v}
the vertical average of
v
, the fracturing part is given by
\tilde{v}:=v-\overline{v}
.
The vertical average commutes with horizontal or tangential derivation and with the horizontal Laplacian for sufficiently smooth vector fields
v
, i. e.,
\overline{\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}v}=\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{v}
and
\overline{\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}v}=\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{v}
.
In particular, the incompressibility condition can be expressed as
\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,\overline{v}=0
.

We now introduce the associated hydrostatic Helmholtz or Leray projection
\mathcal{P}\colon\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)^{2}\to{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}(\Omega)
.
It is a bounded, linear projection which annihilates the pressure term, i. e.,
\mathcal{P}(\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi)=0
, and the space
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)
can be decomposed into
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)^{2}={\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}(\Omega)\oplus\{\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi\colon\pi\in\widehat{\mathrm{W}}^{1,q}(G)\}
along the hydrostatic Helmholtz projection
\mathcal{P}
.
Furthermore, the Helmholtz projection commutes with the time derivative, i. e.,
\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}\partial_{t}
.
It is related to the two-dimensional classical Helmholtz projection
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{H}}
, see e. g.
[
27
, Section 2]
for further details on the latter one, by

\mathcal{P}\colon\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)^{2}\to{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}(\Omega),\quad\mathcal{P}v=\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{H}}\overline{v}+\tilde{v}.

We define the maximal hydrostatic Stokes operator
\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{m}}\colon D(\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{m}})\subset{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}(\Omega)\to{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}(\Omega)
by
\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{m}}v:=\mathcal{P}\Delta v
, where
D(\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{m}}):=\{v\in{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}(\Omega)\colon\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{m}}v\in{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}(\Omega)\}
.
The hydrostatic Stokes operator with boundary conditions
{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathrm{b.c.}}\colon D({\mathrm{A}}_{\mathrm{b.c.}})\subset{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}(\Omega)\to{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}(\Omega)
is defined by
{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathrm{b.c.}}v:=\mathcal{P}\Delta v
, with
D({\mathrm{A}}_{\mathrm{b.c.}}):=\mathrm{H}^{{2},q}_{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{b.c.}}(\Omega)^{2}\cap{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}(\Omega)
.

For
s\in(0,2]
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
as well as
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
representing the part of the boundary with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions, respectively, we set

\mathrm{H}^{{s},q}_{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{b.c.}}(\Omega):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left\{v\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{s},q}(\Omega)^{2}\colon v|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}=0,\,\partial_{z}v|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}}=0\right\},&&\text{ for }1+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}<s\leq 2,\\
&\left\{v\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{s},q}(\Omega)^{2}\colon v|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}=0\right\},&&\text{ for }\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}<s<1+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},\\
&\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{s},q}(\Omega)^{2},&&\text{ for }0<s<\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}.\end{aligned}\right.

Note that
D(\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{m}})\subset\mathrm{H}^{{s},q}_{\mathrm{per},\mathrm{b.c.}}(\Omega)
only for
s<\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}
but not for
s>\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}
.
As indicated above,
w
can be recovered from
v
, so the bilinearity can be written as
({u}\cdot\nabla){v}=({v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}){v}+w({v})\cdot\partial_{z}{v}
.
Applying the hydrostatic Helmholtz projection to the bilinearity, we obtain

(4.2)

\mathrm{F}(v,v^{\prime}):=\mathcal{P}(({v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}){v^{\prime}}+w({v})\cdot\partial_{z}{v^{\prime}})=\mathcal{P}(({v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}){v^{\prime}})+\mathcal{P}(w(v)\partial_{z}v^{\prime}),\text{ with }\mathrm{F}(v):=\mathrm{F}(v,v).

4.2.
Hibler’s sea ice operator

Proceeding as in
[
9
, Section 3]
, we introduce the map
\mathbb{S}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
with

\mathbb{S}\varepsilon=\begin{pmatrix}(1+\frac{1}{e^{2}})\varepsilon_{11}+(1-\frac{1}{e^{2}})\varepsilon_{22}&\frac{1}{e^{2}}(\varepsilon_{12}+\varepsilon_{21})\\
\frac{1}{e^{2}}(\varepsilon_{12}+\varepsilon_{21})&(1-\frac{1}{e^{2}})\varepsilon_{11}+(1+\frac{1}{e^{2}})\varepsilon_{22}\end{pmatrix}.

Identifying
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
with the vector
(\varepsilon_{11},\varepsilon_{12},\varepsilon_{21},\varepsilon_{22})^{\top}
, we find that the map
\mathbb{S}
corresponds to the positive semi-definite matrix

\mathbb{S}=\left(\mathbb{S}_{ij}^{kl}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}1+\frac{1}{e^{2}}&0&0&1-\frac{1}{e^{2}}\\
0&\frac{1}{e^{2}}&\frac{1}{e^{2}}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{e^{2}}&\frac{1}{e^{2}}&0\\
1-\frac{1}{e^{2}}&0&0&1+\frac{1}{e^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

Setting
S_{\delta}=S_{\delta}(\varepsilon,P):=\frac{P}{2}\frac{\mathbb{S}\varepsilon}{\triangle_{\delta}(\varepsilon)}
, we define Hibler’s operator by
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{H}}{v}_{\mathrm{ice}}:=\frac{1}{{\rho}_{\mathrm{ice}}h}\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,S_{\delta}({v}_{\mathrm{ice}})
and recall

(\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{H}}{v}_{\mathrm{ice}})_{i}=-\sum_{j,k,l=1}^{2}\frac{P}{2{\rho}_{\mathrm{ice}}h}\frac{1}{\triangle_{\delta}(\varepsilon)}\left(\mathbb{S}_{ij}^{kl}-\frac{1}{\triangle_{\delta}^{2}(\varepsilon)}(\mathbb{S}\varepsilon)_{ik}(\mathbb{S}\varepsilon)_{jl}\right)\mathrm{D}_{k}\mathrm{D}_{l}v_{\mathrm{ice},j}+\frac{1}{2{\rho}_{\mathrm{ice}}h\triangle_{\delta}(\varepsilon)}\sum_{j=1}^{2}(\partial_{j}P)(\mathbb{S}\varepsilon)_{ij}

from
[
9
, Section 3]
, where
i=1,2
and
\mathrm{D}_{m}=-\mathrm{i}\partial_{m}
.
The principal part of
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{H}}
has coefficients given by

a_{ij}^{kl}(\varepsilon,h,a):=-\frac{P(h,a)}{2{\rho}_{\mathrm{ice}}h}\frac{1}{\triangle_{\delta}(\varepsilon)}\left(\mathbb{S}_{ij}^{kl}-\frac{1}{\triangle_{\delta}^{2}(\varepsilon)}(\mathbb{S}\varepsilon)_{ik}(\mathbb{S}\varepsilon)_{jl}\right).

For sufficiently smooth initial data
v_{0}=(v_{\mathrm{ice},0},h_{0},a_{0})
, the linearization of Hibler’s operator is of the form

[\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{H}}(v_{0}){v}_{\mathrm{ice}}]_{i}=\sum_{j,k,l=1}^{2}a_{ij}^{kl}(\varepsilon_{0},h_{0},a_{0})\mathrm{D}_{k}\mathrm{D}_{l}v_{\mathrm{ice},j}+\frac{1}{2{\rho}_{\mathrm{ice}}h_{0}\triangle_{\delta}(\varepsilon_{0})}\sum_{j=1}^{2}(\partial_{j}P(h_{0},a_{0}))(\mathbb{S}\varepsilon({v}_{\mathrm{ice}}))_{ij}.

The
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-realization
\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{H}}(v_{0})
of
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{H}}(v_{0})
on
G=(0,1)\times(0,1)
is then defined by
[\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{H}}(v_{0})]{v}_{\mathrm{ice}}:=[\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{H}}(v_{0})]{v}_{\mathrm{ice}}
for
{v}_{\mathrm{ice}}\in D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{H}}(v_{0}))=\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{2},q}(G)^{2}
.

Comparing this definition of Hibler’s operator to the one presented in
[
9
, Section 3]
, we observe that the sign is changed and
\nicefrac{{1}}{{{\rho}_{\mathrm{ice}}h}}
is included in the definition for the sake of consistency with the hydrostatic Stokes operator and to ease notation.
We also introduce the lower-order terms

\mathrm{B}_{h}(h_{0},a_{0})h:=-\frac{\partial_{h}P(h_{0},a_{0})}{2{\rho}_{\mathrm{ice}}h_{0}}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}h\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{B}_{a}(h_{0},a_{0})a:=-\frac{\partial_{a}P(h_{0},a_{0})}{2{\rho}_{\mathrm{ice}}h_{0}}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}a,

originating from
\frac{1}{m}\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,\frac{P}{2}\mathrm{I}
.
Finally, we recall that
\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}
is the horizontal Laplacian on the square
G
with periodic boundary conditions, and its domain is given by
D(\Delta_{\mathrm{H}})=\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{2},q}(G)
.

4.3.
A new formulation of the coupled system

We start the reformulation of (
3.4
) with the primitive equations of the atmosphere.
To this end, we choose
a=\kappa_{2}
and
b={h}_{\mathrm{atm}}
and denote the atmospheric solenoidal
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-space by
{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}})
.
The atmospheric Helmholtz projection
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathrm{atm}}
and the atmospheric Stokes operator
\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{atm}}\colon D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{atm}})\subset{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}})\to{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}})
are defined as in
Subsection 4.1
, i. e.,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{atm}}{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}&={\mathcal{P}}_{\mathrm{atm}}\Delta{v}_{\mathrm{atm}},\\
D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{atm}})&=\left\{{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{2},q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}})^{2}\cap{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{atm}}):\partial_{\nu_{\Gamma_{u}}}{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}=0\text{ on }{\Gamma_{u}}\text{ and }\partial_{\nu_{\Gamma_{i}}}{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}=0\text{ on }{\Gamma_{i}}\right\}\end{aligned}\right.

in view of (
3.5
), and the bilinearity
{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{atm}}
is defined via (
4.2
).

For the primitive equations of the ocean, we choose
a=-{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
and
b=0
.
The oceanic solenoidal
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-space is denoted by
{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})
, and we define the oceanic Helmholtz projection
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
as well as the maximal oceanic Stokes operator
\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{\mathrm{m}}\colon D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{\mathrm{m}})\subset{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})\to{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})
as in
Subsection 4.1
, so

\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{\mathrm{m}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}={\mathcal{P}}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\Delta{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}},\kern 5.0ptD(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{\mathrm{m}})=\{{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\in{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}):{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\Delta{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\in{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})\},

and the bilinearity
{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
via (
4.2
).
The oceanic hydrostatic Stokes operator with homogeneous boundary conditions
\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0}\colon D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0})\subset{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})\to{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})
is given by

(4.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}&={\mathcal{P}}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\Delta{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}},\\
D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0})&=\left\{{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{2},q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})^{2}\cap{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}):\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{b}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}=0\text{ on }{\Gamma_{b}}\text{ and }\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{o}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}=0\text{ on }{\Gamma_{o}}\right\}\end{aligned}\right.

with regard to (
3.5
) and (
3.3
).
Setting
C_{\mathrm{o,i}}(h):=\nicefrac{{{\mu}_{\mathrm{ocn}}}}{{{\rho}_{\mathrm{ice}}h}}
so that
\nicefrac{{1}}{{{\rho}_{\mathrm{ice}}h}}{\tau}_{\mathrm{ocn}}=-C_{\mathrm{o,i}}(h)\partial_{\nu_{\Gamma_{o}}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
, for
(v_{\mathrm{ice},0},h_{0},a_{0})\in V^{\mathrm{ice}}
, we define
\mathcal{A}(v_{\mathrm{ice},0},a_{0},h_{0})\colon\mathcal{X}_{1}\subset\mathcal{X}_{0}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}
by

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(v_{\mathrm{ice},0},h_{0},a_{0})

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{atm}}&0&0&0&0\\
0&\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{\mathrm{m}}&0&0&0\\
0&-C_{\mathrm{o,i}}(h_{0})\partial_{\nu_{\Gamma_{o}}}&\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{H}}(v_{\mathrm{ice},0},h_{0},a_{0})&\mathrm{B}_{h}(h_{0},a_{0})&\mathrm{B}_{a}(h_{0},a_{0})\\
0&0&0&d_{\mathrm{h}}\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}&0\\
0&0&0&0&d_{\mathrm{a}}\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}:=D(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle:=\{({v}_{\mathrm{atm}},{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}},{v}_{\mathrm{ice}},h,a)\in D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{atm}})\times D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{\mathrm{m}})\times D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{H}})\times D(\Delta_{\mathrm{H}})\times D(\Delta_{\mathrm{H}})\colon

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{b}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}=0\text{ on }{\Gamma_{b}}\text{ and }\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{o}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}={v}_{\mathrm{ice}}\text{ on }{\Gamma_{o}}\}.

Note that this operator matrix has
non-diagonal domain
.
Further, we define the nonlinearity by

(4.5)

\mathcal{F}({v}_{\mathrm{atm}},{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}},{v}_{\mathrm{ice}},h,a):=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathrm{atm}}(({{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}){{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}}+w({{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}})\cdot\partial_{z}{{v}_{\mathrm{atm}}})\\
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathrm{ocn}}(({{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}){{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}}+w({{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})\cdot\partial_{z}{{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})\\
({{v}_{\mathrm{ice}}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}){{v}_{\mathrm{ice}}}-\frac{1}{{\rho}_{\mathrm{ice}}h}{\tau}_{\mathrm{atm}}\\
\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,({v}_{\mathrm{ice}}h)-\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{h}}\\
\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,({v}_{\mathrm{ice}}a)-\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{a}}\end{pmatrix},

with
\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{h}}
and
\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{a}}
as in (
5.2
).
Besides, the external force is given by

so it suffices to prove (a).
As
\mathcal{P}\colon\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)^{2}\to{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}(\Omega)
is bounded and
\mathrm{B}^{{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}}}_{{q}{p}}(\Omega)^{2}\cap{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}(\Omega)\subset\mathrm{B}^{{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}}}_{{q}{p}}(\Omega)^{2}
holds true, it is sufficient to bound the
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)
-norms of
({v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}){v}
and
w({v})\cdot\partial_{z}{v}
separately by
C\|v\|_{\mathrm{B}^{{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}}}_{{q}{p}}(\Omega)}^{2}
for some
C>0
.
Next, we deduce from
\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}<\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
the existence of
\eta>0
such that
2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}-\eta-\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}}\geq-\nicefrac{{3}}{{3q}}
and
2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}-\eta-\nicefrac{{3}}{{q}}\geq 1-\nicefrac{{2}}{{q}}
.
It then follows by
[
49
, Theorem 4.6.1]
that

(6.2)

\mathrm{B}^{{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}}}_{{q}{p}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{B}^{{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}-\eta}}_{{q}{3q}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{3q}(\Omega)\text{ and }\mathrm{B}^{{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}}}_{{q}{p}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{B}^{{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}-\eta}}_{{q}{\nicefrac{{3q}}{{2}}}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1,\nicefrac{{3q}}{{2}}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1,\nicefrac{{3q}}{{2}}}(\Omega),

where we used that
\mathrm{H}^{1,\nicefrac{{3q}}{{2}}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1,\nicefrac{{3q}}{{2}}}(\Omega)
by virtue of
q\geq 2
, see e. g.
[
49
, Chapter 2]
.
Combining the embeddings from (
6.2
) with Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\|({v}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}){v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{\mathrm{L}^{3q}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\nicefrac{{3q}}{{2}}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{\mathrm{B}^{{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}}}_{{q}{p}}(\Omega)}^{2},

so the proof for the first addend of the bilinearity is complete.
For the second one, we use (
6.1
) for

\|w\partial_{z}{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}_{z}^{\infty}\mathrm{L}_{xy}^{2q}}\|\partial_{z}{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{z}^{q}\mathrm{L}_{xy}^{2q}},

and we now find estimates for
\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}_{z}^{\infty}\mathrm{L}_{xy}^{2q}}
and
\|\partial_{z}{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{z}^{q}\mathrm{L}_{xy}^{2q}}
separately.
Exploiting
\mathrm{W}^{{1},q}(a,b)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(a,b)
, Poincaré’s inequality, the embedding

\mathrm{B}^{{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}-\eta}}_{{q}{p}}(G)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1,2q}(G)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{1,2q}(G),\text{ $\eta>0$ small},

as well as the above observations concerning embeddings of anisotropic Sobolev spaces, Bessel potential spaces and Besov spaces, and using the assumption that
\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,v+\partial_{z}{w}=0
, we infer that

\displaystyle\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}_{z}^{\infty}\mathrm{L}_{xy}^{2q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|w\|_{\mathrm{W}_{z}^{{1},{q}}\mathrm{L}_{xy}^{2q}}\leq C\|\partial_{z}{w}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{z}^{q}\mathrm{L}_{xy}^{2q}}\leq C\|\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,v\|_{\mathrm{L}_{z}^{q}\mathrm{L}_{xy}^{2q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v\|_{\mathrm{L}_{z}^{q}\mathrm{W}_{xy}^{{1},{2q}}}\leq C\|v\|_{\mathrm{B}^{{\eta}}_{{q}{p},{z}}\mathrm{B}^{{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}-\eta}}_{{q}{p},{xy}}}\leq C\|v\|_{\mathrm{B}^{{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}}}_{{q}{p}}(\Omega)}.

The condition on
p
and
q
yields
1-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}-2\eta-\nicefrac{{2}}{{q}}\geq-\nicefrac{{2}}{{2q}}
for some
\eta>0
, so
[
49
, Theorem 4.6.1]
implies

\mathrm{B}^{{1-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}-\eta}}_{{q}{p}}(G)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{B}^{{1-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}-2\eta}}_{{q}{2q}}(G)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{2q}(G).

Using the above remarks on the relations of anisotropic Sobolev spaces again and invoking the last embedding, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{z}{v}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{z}^{q}\mathrm{L}_{xy}^{2q}}\leq C\|v\|_{\mathrm{W}_{z}^{{1},{q}}\mathrm{L}_{xy}^{2q}}\leq C\|v\|_{\mathrm{B}^{{1+\eta}}_{{q}{p},{z}}\mathrm{B}^{{1-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}-\eta}}_{{q}{p},{xy}}}\leq C\|v\|_{\mathrm{B}^{{2-\nicefrac{{2}}{{p}}}}_{{q}{p}}(\Omega)}.

The claim follows then by concatenating the previous estimates.
∎

We get the following corollary by recalling the concrete shape of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{\gamma}
from (
5.3
).

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
such that
\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}<\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
,
v_{\mathrm{atm},1},v_{\mathrm{atm},2}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{atm}}
and
v_{\mathrm{ocn},1},v_{\mathrm{ocn},2}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{ocn}}
. Then the following holds.

(1)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{atm}}(v_{\mathrm{atm},1})-{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{atm}}(v_{\mathrm{atm},2})\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{atm}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigl(\|v_{\mathrm{atm},1}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{atm}}}+\|v_{\mathrm{atm},2}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{atm}}}\bigr)\|v_{\mathrm{atm},1}-v_{\mathrm{atm},2}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{atm}}}.

(2)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{ocn}}(v_{\mathrm{ocn},1})-{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{ocn}}(v_{\mathrm{ocn},2})\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{ocn}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigl(\|v_{\mathrm{ocn},1}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{ocn}}}+\|v_{\mathrm{ocn},2}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{ocn}}}\bigr)\|v_{\mathrm{ocn},1}-v_{\mathrm{ocn},2}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{ocn}}}.

7.
The Stationary Hydrostatic Stokes Problem

This section is dedicated to the analysis of the stationary hydrostatic Stokes problem with inhomogeneous boundary conditions.
The stationary hydrostatic Stokes problem corresponds to the coupling condition imposed in (
3.3
) as well as the boundary condition for
{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
on the lower boundary as made precise in (
3.5
), and it is given by

(7.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{\mathrm{m}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}&=0,&&\text{ on }{{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}},\\
\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{o}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}&=\varphi,&&\text{ on }{\Gamma_{o}},\\
\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{b}}{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}&=0,&&\text{ on }{\Gamma_{b}},\end{aligned}\right.

on
{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})
with
\varphi\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(G)^{2}
.
We denote the solution operator of (
7.1
) by
\mathrm{L}_{0}\colon\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Gamma_{o})^{2}\to{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})
and call it the
hydrostatic Dirichlet operator
.
In the main part of this section, we show that
\mathrm{L}_{0}
is well-defined and bounded.
This operator and its properties are essential for the decoupling approach in
Section 8
.

7.1.
The hydrostatic Dirichlet operator

We start by solving (
7.1
) for smooth functions on the boundary.
We need the following auxiliary result which guarantees the existence of an extension with vertical average
0
.
To this end, we construct a smooth solution to the associated extension problem

(7.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \overline{g}&=0,&&\text{ on }{{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}},\\
\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{o}}g&=\varphi,&&\text{ on }{\Gamma_{o}},\\
\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{b}}g&=0,&&\text{ on }{\Gamma_{b}}.\end{aligned}\right.

The auxiliary result reads as follows.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{per}}^{\infty}(G)^{2}
. Then there exists a smooth solution
g\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{per}}^{\infty}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})^{2}
of (
7.2
).

Proof.

Note that
{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}=G\times(-{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}},0)
.
The approach for
g
is a splitting into the horizontal and the vertical part
g(x_{\mathrm{H}},z)=r(z)\cdot\varphi(x_{\mathrm{H}})
.
Therefore, the problem (
7.2
) reduces to the construction of a smooth solution
r
to the one-dimensional extension problem with mean value
0
given by

(7.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \overline{r}&=0,&&\text{ on }{(-{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}},0)},\\
r(0)&=1,&&\\
r(-{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}})&=0.&&\end{aligned}\right.

A solution of (
7.3
) is given by
r(z)=\nicefrac{{3}}{{{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}}^{2}}}z^{2}+\nicefrac{{4}}{{{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}}}}z+1
, and it is smooth, i. e.,
g=r\cdot\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{per}}^{\infty}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})^{2}
.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

(1)

In general, the solutions of (
7.2
) are not unique.

(2)

Solving (
7.2
) guarantees in particular that
\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,\overline{g}=0
for all
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{per}}^{\infty}(G)^{2}
.
Functions of the shape
g(x_{\mathrm{H}},z)=r(z)\cdot\varphi(x_{\mathrm{H}})
satisfy
\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,\overline{g}=0
for all
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{per}}^{\infty}(G)^{2}
if and only if
\overline{r}
vanishes.

The regularity and average
0
of the solution
g
of (
7.2
) ensure
g\in D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{\mathrm{m}})
, so
v_{g}:={v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}-g\in D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{\mathrm{m}})
satisfies

(7.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{\mathrm{m}}v_{g}&=f,&&\text{ on }{{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}},\\
\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{o}}v_{g}&=0,&&\text{ on }{\Gamma_{o}},\\
\mathrm{tr}_{\Gamma_{b}}v_{g}&=0,&&\text{ on }{\Gamma_{b}},\end{aligned}\right.

with
f:=-\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{\mathrm{m}}g\in{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})
.
Consequently, (
7.1
) admits a unique solution if and only if (
7.4
) does so.
Note that (
7.4
) is equivalent to
\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0}v_{g}=f
.
The next result follows from
0\in\rho(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0})
in
Subsection 6.1
(b).

Lemma 7.3
.

For
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{per}}^{\infty}(G)^{2}
, there exists a unique solution
u\in D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{\mathrm{m}})
of (
7.1
).

As a consequence of
Subsection 7.1
,
\mathrm{L}_{0}
exists as a densely defined operator with domain
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{per}}^{\infty}(G)^{2}
.
This allows us to define a unique adjoint
\mathrm{L}_{0}^{\prime}\colon D(\mathrm{L}_{0}^{\prime})\subset{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})^{\prime}\to(\mathrm{L}^{q}(G)^{2})^{\prime}
.
Observe that
{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})^{\prime}\cong{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q^{\prime}}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})

and
(\mathrm{L}^{q}(G)^{2})^{\prime}\cong\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}(G)^{2}

with
\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q^{\prime}}}=1
.

We denote by
\partial_{\nu}^{r}\colon D(\partial_{\nu}^{r})\subset{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{r}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})\to\mathrm{L}^{r}(G)^{2}
the (distributional) derivative in the direction of
\nu_{\Gamma_{o}}
with maximal domain
D(\partial_{\nu}^{r}):=\{f\in{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{r}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})\colon\partial_{\nu}^{r}f\in\mathrm{L}^{r}(G)^{2}\}
for
r\in(1,\infty)
.
Moreover, we denote by
\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0,r}
the operator with homogeneous boundary conditions given in (
4.3
) on
{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{r}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})
for
r\in(1,\infty)
.
We remark that
(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0,q})^{\prime}=\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0,q^{\prime}}
with
\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q^{\prime}}}=1
.
The adjoint
\mathrm{L}_{0}^{\prime}
is related to the hydrostatic Stokes operator with homogeneous boundary conditions and the normal derivative in the following way.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
q,q^{\prime}\in(1,\infty)
such that
\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q^{\prime}}}=1
.
The adjoint
\mathrm{L}_{0}^{\prime}
of
\mathrm{L}_{0}
satisfies

(7.5)

\mathrm{L}_{0}^{\prime}=\partial_{\nu}^{q^{\prime}}R(0,\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0,q})^{\prime}=\partial_{\nu}^{q^{\prime}}R(0,\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0,q^{\prime}}).

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{per}}^{\infty}(\Gamma_{o})^{2}
and
k\in{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q^{\prime}}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})
with
\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q^{\prime}}}=1
, so
(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0,q})^{\prime}=\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0,q^{\prime}}
.
We set
f:=\mathrm{L}_{0}\varphi
and
g:=(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0,q^{\prime}})^{-1}k
.
Then we have
f\in D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{\mathrm{m}})\subset{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})
,
g\in D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0,q^{\prime}})\subset{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q^{\prime}}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})
with
\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q^{\prime}}}=1
, and it follows therefrom, also invoking the periodic boundary conditions on the lateral boundary, that

\langle{\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi},{g}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})}=\int_{G}\int_{-{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}}}^{0}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi\cdot g\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x_{\mathrm{H}}={h}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\int_{G}{\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi\cdot\overline{g}}\,\mathrm{d}x_{\mathrm{H}}=-{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\int_{G}{\pi\cdot\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,\overline{g}}\,\mathrm{d}x_{\mathrm{H}}=0,

and likewise
\langle{f},{\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})}=0
.
Green’s second identity and the horizontal periodicity then imply that

\displaystyle\langle{\Delta f+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi},{g}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})}-\langle{f},{\Delta g+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\Delta f},{g}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})}-\langle{f},{\Delta g}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G}{\partial_{z}f(x_{\mathrm{H}},0)g(x_{\mathrm{H}},0)}\,\mathrm{d}x_{\mathrm{H}}+\int_{G}{\partial_{z}f(x_{\mathrm{H}},-{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}})g(x_{\mathrm{H}},-{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}})}\,\mathrm{d}x_{\mathrm{H}}

\displaystyle-\int_{G}{f(x_{\mathrm{H}},0)\partial_{z}g(x_{\mathrm{H}},0)}\,\mathrm{d}x_{\mathrm{H}}-\int_{G}{f(x_{\mathrm{H}},-{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}})\partial_{z}g(x_{\mathrm{H}},-{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}})}\,\mathrm{d}x_{\mathrm{H}}.

Since
g\in D(\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0,q^{\prime}})
, we have
g(x_{\mathrm{H}},0)=g(x_{\mathrm{H}},-{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}})=0
, and it also holds that
f(x_{\mathrm{H}},-{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}})=0
as well as
f(x_{\mathrm{H}},0)=\varphi
.
This yields that

\langle{f},{\Delta g+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})}=\langle{\Delta f+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi},{g}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})}+\int_{G}{\varphi(x_{\mathrm{H}})\partial_{z}g(x_{\mathrm{H}},0)}\,\mathrm{d}x_{\mathrm{H}}.

Making use of the latter identity in conjunction with the definition of the hydrostatic Stokes operator as in
Subsection 4.1
as well as
\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{\mathrm{m}}f=0
on
{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}
by construction, we derive that

\displaystyle\langle{\mathrm{L}_{0}\varphi},{k}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})}

\displaystyle=\langle{f},{\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0,q^{\prime}}g}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})}=\langle{f},{\Delta g+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})}

\displaystyle=\langle{\Delta f+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi},{g}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})}+\int_{G}{\varphi(x_{\mathrm{H}})\partial_{z}g(x_{\mathrm{H}},0)}\,\mathrm{d}x_{\mathrm{H}}

\displaystyle=\langle{\varphi},{(\partial_{z}((\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0,q^{\prime}})^{-1}k))|_{\Gamma_{u}}}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}}})},

so the claim follows.
∎

The next lemma guarantees that the right-hand side of (
7.5
) can be extended to a bounded operator.

Lemma 7.5
.

The normal derivative
\partial_{\nu}^{r}
is relatively
(-\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0,r})^{\delta}
-bounded with

\delta>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2r}

Proof.

From
[
3
, Theorem VIII.1.3.1]
, establishing the result for the half-space, and the observation that horizontally periodic functions can be extended periodically onto a layer and then cut off, we deduce for
r>1+\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}
the embedding
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{r},q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})^{2}\cap{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})\hookrightarrow D(\partial_{\nu}^{q})
.
The claim is then a consequence of
Subsection 6.1
, implying that
-\mathrm{A}^{\mathrm{ocn}}_{0}
has bounded imaginary powers, see also the relation (
8.7
).
∎

Combining
Subsection 7.1
and
Subsection 7.1
, we conclude that
\mathrm{L}_{0}^{\prime}
can be extended to a bounded operator from
\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}_{\overline{\sigma}}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})

Proposition 7.6
.

The operator
\mathrm{L}_{0}
can be extended to a bounded operator from

to
{\mathrm{L}}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})

v

As we see in the next corollary, more regularity of the boundary data implies more regularity of the solution.

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
and
r<\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}

\varphi\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(G)^{2}

of (

s>0

The operator
\mathrm{L}_{0}
is also bounded from
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{s},q}(G)^{2}

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{s},q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})^{2}

of (

Proof.

First, we consider

From

\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi=\frac{1}{{h}_{\mathrm{ocn}}}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{-1}\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{H}}\,\partial_{\nu_{\Gamma_{o}}}v.

.
The general case

.
We first prove the a-priori bound for

.
Using classical Calderón-Zygmund theory, we obtain from (

(7.8)

\|{v}_{\mathrm{ocn}}\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{2},q}({\Omega}_{\mathrm{ocn}})}\leq C(\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{per}}^{{2},q}(G)}+\|\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(G)}).

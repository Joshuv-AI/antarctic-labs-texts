Introduction

In his celebrated paper
[
13
]
, Perelman conjectured that
all 3-dimensional
\kappa
-noncollapsed steady (gradient) Ricci solitons must be rotationally symmetric
.
The conjecture is solved by Brendle in 2012
[
1
]
. For a general dimension
n\geq 3
, under an extra condition that the soliton is asymptotically cylindrical, Brendle also proves that any
\kappa
-noncollapsed steady Ricci soliton with positive sectional curvature must be rotationally symmetric
[
2
]
. In general, it is still open
whether an
n
-dimensional
\kappa
-noncollapsed steady Ricci soliton with positive curvature operator is rotationally symmetric for
n\geq 4
. For
\kappa
-noncollapsed steady Kähler-Ricci solitons with nonnegative bisectional curvature, the authors have recently proved that they must be flat
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
.

Recall from
[
2
]
,

Definition 1.1
.

An
n
-dimensional steady Ricci soliton
(M,g,f)
is called asymptotically cylindrical if the following
holds:

(i) Scalar curvature
R(x)
of
g
satisfies

\frac{C_{0}^{-1}}{\rho(x)}\leq R(x)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\rho(x)},~\forall~\rho(x)\geq r_{0},

where
C_{0}>0
is a constant and
\rho(x)
denotes the distance of
x
from a fixed point
x_{0}
.

(ii) Let
p_{m}
be an arbitrary sequence of marked points going to infinity.
Consider rescaled metrics

g_{m}(t)=r_{m}^{-1}\phi^{*}_{r_{m}t}g,
where

r_{m}R(p_{m})=\frac{n-1}{2}+o(1)
and
\phi_{t}
is a one-parameter subgroup generated by
X=-\nabla f
. As
m\to\infty,

flows
(M,g_{m}(t),p_{m})

converge in the Cheeger-Gromov sense to a family of shrinking cylinders

(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(1),\widetilde{g}(t)),t\in(0,1).
The metric
\widetilde{g}(t)
is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}(t)=dr^{2}+(n-2)(2-2t)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(1)},

where
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(1)
is the unit sphere of euclidean space.

In this note, we discuss 3-dimensional steady (gradient) Ricci solitons without assuming the
\kappa
-noncollapsed conditon.

It is proved by Chen that any 3-dimensional ancient solution has nonnegative sectional curvature
[

.
We prove

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g,f)
be a 3-dimensional steady Ricci soliton. Then, it is rotationally symmetric if the scalar curvature
R(x)
of
(M,g,f)
satisfies

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{C_{0}^{-1}}{\rho(x)}\leq R(x)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\rho(x)},

for some constant
C>0
, where
\rho(x)
denotes the distance from a fixed point
x_{0}
.

Under the condition (
1.2
), we need to check the property (ii) in Definition
1.1
to prove Theorem
1.2
. Actually, we show that for any sequence
p_{i}\rightarrow\infty
, there exists a subsequence
p_{i_{k}}\rightarrow\infty
such that

\displaystyle(M,g_{p_{i_{k}}}(t),p_{i_{k}})\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},\widetilde{g}(t),p_{\infty}),~for~t\in(-\infty,1),

where
g_{p_{i_{k}}}(t)=R(p_{i_{k}})g(R^{-1}(p_{i_{k}})t)
and
(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},\widetilde{g}(t))
is a shrinking cylinders flow, i.e.

\widetilde{g}(t)=dr^{2}+(2-2t)g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}.

As in
[
10
]
, we study the geometry of neighborhood
M_{p,k}=\{x\in M|~f(p)-\frac{k}{\sqrt{R(p)}}\leq f(x)\leq f(p)+\frac{k}{\sqrt{R(p)}}\}
around level set
\Sigma_{r}=\{f(x)=f(p)=r\}
for any
p\in M
. We are able to give a uniform injective radius estimate for
(M,R_{p_{i}}g)
at each sequence of
p_{i}
. Then we can still get a limit flow for rescaled flows
(M,g_{p_{i}}(t))
, which will split off a line. By using a classification result of Daskalopoulos-Hamilton-Sesum for ancient flows on a compact surface
[
8
]
, we finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We remark that the curvature condition in Theorem
1.2
cannot be removed, since there does exist a 3-dimensional non-flat steady Ricci soliton with exponential curvature decay. For example,
(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1},g_{cigar}+ds^{2})
, where
(\mathbb{R}^{2},g_{cigar})
is a cigar soliton.
Also, Theorem
1.2
is not true for dimension
n\geq 4
by Cao’s examples of steady Kähler-Ricci solitons with positive sectional curvature
[
3
]
.

At last, we remark that it is still open
wether there exists a 3-dimensional collapsed steady Ricci soliton with positive curvature
. Hamilton has conjectured that
there should exist a family of collapsed 3-dimensional complete gradient steady Ricci solitons with positive curvature and
S^{1}
-symmetry
(cf.
[
5
]
). Our result shows that
the curvature of Hamilton’s examples could not have a linear decay.

2.
Positivity of Ricci curvature

(M,g,f)
is called a gradient steady Ricci soliton if a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
satisfies

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}(g)=\nabla^{2}f,

for some smooth function
f
. We first show the positivity of Ricci curvature of
(M,g,f)

under (
1.2
) of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Under (
1.2
),
(M,g,f)
has positive sectional curvature.

Proof.

We need to show that
(M,g)
has positive Ricci curvature.
On the contrary,
(M,g)
locally splits off a flat piece of line by Shi’s splitting theorem
[
14
]
.
Then, the universal cover
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g})
of
(M,g)
is isometric to a product Riemannian manifold of a real line and the cigar soliton. Namely,
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g})=(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R},g_{cigar}+ds^{2})
. Let
\pi:\widetilde{M}\to M
be a universal covering. We fix
x_{0}\in M
and
\widetilde{x}_{0}\in\widetilde{M}
such that
\pi(\widetilde{x}_{0})=x_{0}
. For any
x\in M
and
\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{M}
such that
\pi(\widetilde{x})=x
, one sees

(2.2)

\displaystyle\rho(x,x_{0})\leq\widetilde{\rho}(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{x}_{0}),

where
\rho
and
\widetilde{\rho}
are the distance functions w.r.t
g
and
\widetilde{g}
respectively. Let
\{\widetilde{x}_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be a sequence of points so that
\widetilde{x}_{i}=(p_{i},0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
tend to infinity. Then, one may check that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{x}_{i})\rho(\widetilde{x}_{i},\widetilde{x}_{0})\to 0,~{\rm as}~i\to\infty.

Since
R(x_{i})=\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{x}_{i})\to 0
, where
x_{i}=\pi(\widetilde{x}_{i})
, we see
d(x_{i},x_{0})\to\infty
by (
1.2
).
Again by (
1.2
) and (
2.2
), we get

(2.4)

\displaystyle C_{1}\leq R(x_{i})d(x_{i},x_{0})\leq\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{x}_{i})d(\widetilde{x}_{i},\widetilde{x}_{0}).

This is a contradiction to (
2.3
). Hence, the lemma is proved.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

(M,g,f)
in Theorem
1.2
has a unique equilibrium point
o
, i.e.,
\nabla f(o)=0
. As a consequence,
\Sigma_{r}=\{f(x)=r\}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, for any
r>f(o)
.

Proof.

Note that

(2.5)

\displaystyle|\nabla f|^{2}+R=A.

By taking covariant derivatives on both sides of (
2.5
), it follows

(2.6)

\displaystyle 2{\rm Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)=-\langle\nabla R,\nabla f\rangle.

On the the hand, by (
1.2
), there exists a point
o
such that

\sup_{M}R(x)=R(o)=R_{{\rm max}}.

In particular,
\nabla R(o)=0.

Thus

{\rm Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)=0.

By Lemma
2.1
,
\nabla f(o)=0
. The uniqueness also follows from the positivity of Ricci curvature.

By the Morse theorem,
\Sigma_{r}=\{f(x)=r>f(o)\}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
(cf.
[
10
]
, Lemma 2.1).

∎

3.
Geometry of
M_{p,k}

For any
p\in M
and number
k>0
, we set

M_{p,k}=\{x\in M|~f(p)-\frac{k}{\sqrt{R(p)}}\leq f(x)\leq f(p)+\frac{k}{\sqrt{R(p)}}\}.

Let
g_{p}=R(p)g
be a rescaled metric and denote
B(p,r;g_{p})
a
r
-geodesic ball centered at
p
with respect to
g_{p}
.
Then by Corollary
2.2
, we have (cf.
[
10
]
, Lemma 3.3)

Lemma 3.1
.

Under (
1.2
), for any
p\in M
and number
k>0
with
f(p)-\frac{k}{\sqrt{R(p)}}>f(o)
, it holds

(3.1)

\displaystyle B(p,\frac{k}{\sqrt{R_{max}}};g_{p})\subset M_{p,k}.

By Lemma
3.1
, we prove

Lemma 3.2
.

Under (
1.2
), there exists a constant
C
such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{|\Delta R|(p)}{R^{2}(p)}\leq C,~\forall~p\in~M.

Proof.

Fix any
p\in M
with
f(p)\geq r_{0}>>1
. Then

(3.3)

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(p)|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{R(p)}},~\forall~x\in M_{p,1}.

It is known by
[
4
]
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle c_{1}\rho(x)\leq f(x)\leq c_{2}\rho(x),~\forall~\rho(x)\geq r_{0}.

Thus by (
1.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we get

\displaystyle c_{2}\rho(x)\geq f(p)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{R(p)}}\geq c_{1}\rho(p)-\sqrt{C_{0}\rho(p)}.

It follows

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{R(x)}{R(p)}\leq C_{0}^{2}\frac{\rho(p)}{\rho(x)}\leq\frac{2c_{2}C_{0}^{2}}{c_{1}},~\forall x\in M_{p,1}.

On the other hand, by (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle B(p,\frac{1}{\sqrt{R_{\max}}};g_{p})\subseteq M_{p,1}.

Hence

(3.6)

\displaystyle R(x)\leq C^{\prime}R(p),~\forall~x\in B(p,\frac{1}{\sqrt{R_{\max}}};g_{p}).

Let
\phi_{t}
be generated by
-\nabla f
. Then
g(t)=\phi_{t}^{\ast}g
satisfies the Ricci flow,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g(t)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-2{\rm Ric}(g(t)).

Also rescaled flow
g_{p}(t)=R(p)g(R^{-1}(p)t)
satisfies (
3.7
). Since the Ricci curvature is positive,

\displaystyle B(p,\frac{1}{\sqrt{R_{\max}}};g_{p}(-t))\subseteq B(p,\frac{1}{\sqrt{R_{\max}}};g_{p}(0)),~t\in~[-1,0].

Combining with (
3.6
), we get

(3.8)

\displaystyle R_{g_{p}(t)}(x)\leq C^{\prime},~\forall~x\in B(p,\frac{1}{\sqrt{R_{\max}}};g_{p}(0)),~t\in[-1,0].

Thus, by Shi’s higher order estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle|\Delta_{g_{p}(t)}R_{g_{p}(t)}|(x)\leq C_{1}^{\prime},~\forall~x\in B(p,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{R_{\max}}};g_{p}(-1)),~t\in[-\frac{1}{2},0].

It follows

\displaystyle|\Delta R|(x)\leq C_{1}^{\prime}R^{2}(p),~\forall~x\in B(p,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{R_{\max}}};g_{p}(-1)).

In particular, we have

\displaystyle|\Delta R|(p)\leq C_{1}^{\prime}R^{2}(p),~{\rm as}~\rho(p)\geq r_{0}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Under (
1.2
), by the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C(k)
such that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla^{k}R|(p)}{R^{\frac{k+2}{2}}(p)}\leq C(k),~\forall~p\in~M.

Next, we want to show that
M_{p,k}
is bounded by a finite ball
B(p,Ck;g_{p})
, where
C
is a uniform constant. We need
to use the Gauss formula,

\displaystyle R(X,Y,Z,W)=\overline{R}(X,Y,Z,W)+\langle B(X,Z),B(Y,W)\rangle-\langle B(X,W),B(Y,Z)\rangle,

where
X,Y,Z,W\in T\Sigma_{r}
and
B(X,Y)=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{\bot}
. Note that

\displaystyle B(X,Y)=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}Y,\nabla f\rangle\cdot\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\nabla_{X}\langle Y,\nabla f\rangle-\langle Y,\nabla_{X}\nabla f\rangle]\cdot\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\rm Ric}(X,Y)\cdot\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}}.

We choose a normal basis
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
on
(\Sigma_{r},\bar{g})
with the induced metric
\bar{g}
. Then
\{e_{1},e_{2},\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}\}
spans a normal basis of
(M,g)
. Thus

\displaystyle R_{11}=

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{11}+R(\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|},e_{1},e_{1},\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|})-\frac{R_{11}R_{22}-R_{12}R_{21}}{|\nabla f|^{2}},

\displaystyle R_{22}=

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{22}+R(\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|},e_{2},e_{2},\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|})-\frac{R_{11}R_{22}-R_{12}R_{21}}{|\nabla f|^{2}}.

Since
(\Sigma_{r},\bar{g})
is a surface,
K=\overline{R}_{11}=\overline{R}_{22}
. Hence, we get

Lemma 3.4
.

The Gauss curvature of
(\Sigma_{r},\bar{g})
is given by

(3.10)

\displaystyle K=\frac{R}{2}-\frac{{\rm Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)}{|\nabla f|^{2}}+\frac{R_{11}R_{22}-R_{12}R_{21}}{|\nabla f|^{2}}.

Lemma 3.5
.

Under (
1.2
), there exists a uniform
B>0
such that the following is true:
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\bar{r}_{0}=\bar{r}_{0}(k)
such that

(3.11)

\displaystyle M_{p,k}\subset B(p,2\pi\sqrt{B}+\frac{2k}{\sqrt{R_{\max}}};g_{p}),~\forall~\rho(p)\geq\bar{r}_{0}.

Proof.

By (
1.2
) and (
3.4
), we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\frac{R_{\max}}{2}\leq|\nabla f|^{2}(x)\leq R_{\max},~\forall~x\in M_{p,k},

as long as
\rho(p)\geq r_{0}>>1.

Then by Lemma
3.4
and Lemma
3.2
, we get

\displaystyle|K-\frac{R}{2}|=

\displaystyle|-\frac{{\rm Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)}{|\nabla f|^{2}}+\frac{R_{11}R_{22}-R_{12}R_{21}}{|\nabla f|^{2}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|\langle\nabla R,\nabla f\rangle|}{2|\nabla f|^{2}}+\frac{R^{2}}{|\nabla f|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|\Delta R+2|{\rm Ric}|^{2}|}{2|\nabla f|^{2}}+\frac{R^{2}}{|\nabla f|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(C+4)R^{2}}{R_{\max}}.

It follows

(3.13)

\displaystyle\frac{R(x)}{4}\leq K(x)\leq\frac{3R(x)}{4},~\forall~x\in M_{p,k},~\rho(p)\geq r_{0}.

On the other hand, by (
1.2
),
(
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we see

\displaystyle c_{2}^{-1}\Big(c_{1}\rho(p)-k\sqrt{\rho(p)C_{0}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\rho(x)

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}^{-1}(c_{2}\rho(p)+k\sqrt{\rho(p)C_{0}}),~\forall~x\in M_{p,k},

as long as
\rho(p)\geq r_{0}.

Then similar to (
3.5
), there exists a
\bar{r}_{0}\geq r_{0}
such that

(3.15)

\displaystyle R(x)\geq\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{2}C_{0}^{2}}R(p),~\forall~x\in M_{p,k}.

Thus by (
3.13
), we get

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{ij}\geq B^{-1}R(p)\overline{g}_{ij},~\forall~x\in\Sigma_{f(p)},~\rho(p)\geq\bar{r}_{0},

where
B>0
is a uniform constant. By the Myer’s theorem, the diameter of
\Sigma_{f(p)}
is bounded by

\displaystyle{\rm diam}(\Sigma_{f(p)},g)\leq{\rm diam}(\Sigma_{f(p)},\overline{g}_{f(p)})\leq 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{B}{R(p)}}.

As a consequence,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{f(p)}\subset B(p,2\pi\sqrt{B};R(p)g).

For any
q\in M_{p,k}
, there exists
q^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{f(p)}
such that
\phi_{s}(q)=q^{\prime}
for some
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then by (
3.16
) and (
3.12
), we have

\displaystyle d(q,p)\leq

\displaystyle d(q^{\prime},p)+d(q,q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\rm diam}(\Sigma_{f(p)},g)+\mathcal{L}(\phi_{\tau}|_{[0,s]})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{B}{R(p)}}+|\int_{0}^{s}|\frac{d\phi_{\tau}(q)}{d\tau}|d\tau|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{B}{R(p)}}+\int_{0}^{s}|\nabla f(\phi_{\tau}(q))|d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{B}{R(p)}}+\int_{0}^{s}|\nabla f(\phi_{\tau}(q))|^{2}\cdot\frac{2}{\sqrt{R_{\max}}}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{B}{R(p)}}+|\int_{0}^{s}\frac{d(f(\phi_{\tau}(q)))}{d\tau}\cdot\frac{2}{\sqrt{R_{\max}}}d\tau|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{B}{R(p)}}+|f(q)-f(p)|\cdot\frac{2}{\sqrt{R_{\max}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(2\pi\sqrt{B}+\frac{2k}{\sqrt{R_{\max}}}\Big)\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{R(p)}}.

Thus

\displaystyle M_{p,k}\subset B(p,2\pi\sqrt{B}+\frac{2k}{\sqrt{R_{\max}}};R(p)g).

The lemma is proved.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

First we prove the following convergence of rescaled flows.

Lemma 4.1
.

Under (
1.2
), let
p_{i}\to\infty
. Then by taking a subsequence of
p_{i}
if necessary, we have

\displaystyle(M,g_{p_{i}}(t),p_{i})\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}\times N,\widetilde{g}(t);p_{\infty}),~for~t\in(-\infty,0],

where
g_{p_{i}}(t)=R(p_{i})g(R^{-1}(p_{i})t)
,
\widetilde{g}(t)=ds\otimes ds+g_{N}(t)
and
(N,g_{N}(t))
is an ancient solution of Ricci flow on
N
.

Proof.

For a fixed
\overline{r}
, as in (
3.5
), it is easy to see that there exists a uniform
C_{1}
independent of
\overline{r}
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle R(x)\leq C_{1}R(p_{i}),~\forall~x\in M_{p_{i},\overline{r}\sqrt{R_{\max}}}

as long as
i
is large enough. By Lemma
3.1
, it follows

\displaystyle R_{g_{p_{i}}}(x)\leq C_{1},~\forall~x\in B(p_{i},\overline{r};g_{p_{i}}),

where
g_{p_{i}}=g_{p_{i}}(0)
. Since the scalar curvature is increasing along the flow, we get

\displaystyle|{\rm Rm}_{g_{p_{i}}(t)}(x)|_{g_{p_{i}}(t)}

\displaystyle\leq 3R_{g_{p_{i}}(t)}(x)

\displaystyle\leq 3R_{g_{p_{i}}}(x)\leq 3C_{1},~\forall~x\in B(p_{i},\overline{r};g_{p_{i}}),~t\in(-\infty,0].

Thus together with the injective radius estimate in Proposition
3.6
, we can apply the Hamilton compactness theorem to see that
g_{p_{i}}(t)
converges subsequently to a limit flow
(\widetilde{M},\tilde{g}(t);p_{\infty})
on
t\in(-\infty,0]

[
12
]
.
Moreover, the limit flow has uniformly bounded curvature. It remains to prove the splitting property.

By Remark
3.3
, we have

|{\rm Ric}|(x)\leq CR(x),~\forall~x\in B(p_{i},\overline{r};{g_{p_{i}}}).

It follows from (
4.1
),

\displaystyle|{\rm Ric}|(x)\leq CR(p_{i}),~\forall~x\in B(p_{i},\overline{r};{g_{p_{i}}}).

Let
X_{(i)}=R(p_{i})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla f
.
Then

\displaystyle\sup_{B(p_{i},r_{0};{g_{p_{i}}})}|\nabla_{(g_{p_{i}})}X_{(i)}|_{g_{p_{i}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{B(p_{i},r_{0};{g_{p_{i}}})}\frac{|{\rm Ric}|}{\sqrt{R(p_{i})}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{R(p_{i})}\to 0.

On the other hand, by Remark
3.3
, we also have

\sup_{B(p_{i},r_{0};{g_{p_{i}}})}|\nabla^{m}_{(g_{p_{i}})}X_{(i)}|_{g_{p_{i}}}\leq C(n)\sup_{B(p_{i},r_{0};{g_{p_{i}}})}|\nabla^{m-1}_{(g_{p_{i}})}{\rm Ric}({g_{p_{i}})}|_{g_{p_{i}}}\leq C_{1}.

Thus
X_{(i)}
converges subsequently to a parallel vector field
X_{(\infty)}
on
(\widetilde{M},\tilde{g}(0))
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle|X_{(i)}|_{g_{p_{i}}}(x)=|\nabla f|(p_{i})=\sqrt{R_{\rm max}}+o(1)>0,~\forall~x\in B(p_{i},r_{0};{g_{i}}),

as long as
f(p_{i})
is large enough. This implies that
X_{(\infty)}
is non-trivial.
Hence,
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g}(t))
locally splits off a piece of line along
X_{(\infty)}
. It remains to show that
X_{(\infty)}

generates a line through
p_{\infty}
.

By Lemma
3.5
,

\displaystyle M_{p_{i},k}\subset B(p_{i},2\pi\sqrt{B}+\frac{2k}{\sqrt{R_{\max}}};g_{p_{i}}(0)),~\forall~p_{i}\to\infty.

Let
\gamma_{i,k}(s)
,

be an integral curve generated by
X_{(i)}
through
p_{i}
, which restricted in
M_{p,k}
. Then
\gamma_{i,k}(s)
converges to a geodesic
\gamma_{\infty}(s)
generated by
X_{(\infty)}
through
p_{\infty}
, which restricted in

B(p_{\infty},2\pi\sqrt{B}+\frac{2k}{\sqrt{R_{\max}}};\widetilde{g}(0))
. If let
L_{i,k}
be lengths of
\gamma_{i,k}(s)

and
L_{\infty,k}
length of
\gamma_{\infty}(s)
,

\displaystyle L_{i,k}=

\displaystyle\int_{-D_{i,k}}^{E_{i,k}}|\nabla f|_{g_{p_{i}}(0)}ds=\int_{f(p_{i})-\frac{k}{\sqrt{R(p_{i})}}}^{f(p_{i})+\frac{k}{\sqrt{R(p_{i})}}}\sqrt{R(p_{i})}\|\nabla f\|_{g}

\displaystyle\geq R_{max}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{f(p_{i})-\frac{k}{\sqrt{R(p_{i})}}}^{f(p_{i})+\frac{k}{\sqrt{R(p_{i})}}}\sqrt{R(p_{i})}\|\nabla f\|_{g}^{2}ds

. As a consequence,
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g}(0))

∎

Next we estimate the curvature of

Lemma 4.2
.

Under (
1.2

independent of

\displaystyle\frac{R_{N}(x,t)}{R_{N}(y,t)}\leq C,~\forall~x,y\in N,~t\in(-\infty,0].

Proof.

\widetilde{R}(x,t)
be the scalar curvature of
(\mathbb{R}\times N,\widetilde{g}(t))

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{R}(x,t)}{\widetilde{R}(y,t)}\leq C,~\forall~x,y\in\mathbb{R}\times N,~t\in(\infty,0],

for some constant
C
. For any

\overline{r}>0

\displaystyle x_{i},y_{i}\subseteq B(p_{i},\overline{r};{g}_{p_{i}}(0))\subseteq M_{p_{i},\overline{r}\sqrt{R_{\max}}}.

\displaystyle f(x_{i})=(1+o(1))f(p_{i})~{\rm and}~f(y_{i})=(1+o(1))f(p_{i}),~{\rm as}~p_{i}\to\infty.

On the other hand, for a fixed

\displaystyle\frac{f(\phi_{R^{-1}(p_{i})t}(x_{i}))-f(x_{i})}{|R^{-1}(p_{i})t|}=\frac{\int_{R^{-1}(p_{i})t}^{0}|\nabla f|^{2}ds}{|R^{-1}(p_{i})t|}\rightarrow R_{\max},~{\rm as}~p_{i}\to\infty

The proof of Theorem

Lemma 4.3
.

in Lemma
4.1

By Lemma

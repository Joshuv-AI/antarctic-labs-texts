Abstract
. The present paper is devoted to the description of local and 2 2 -local derivations on Cayley algebras over an arbitrary field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} . Given a Cayley algebra 𝒞 \mathcal{C} with norm n , let 𝒞 0 \mathcal{C}_{0} be its subspace of trace 0 0 elements. We prove that the space of all local derivations of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} coincides with the Lie algebra { d ∈ 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 ​ ( 𝒞 , n ) ∣ d ⁡ ( 1 ) = 0 } \{d\in{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C},\textup{n})\mid d(1)=0\} which is isomorphic to the orthogonal Lie algebra 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 ​ ( 𝒞 0 , n ) {\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C}_{0},\textup{n}) . Further we prove that, surprisingly, the behavior of 2 2 -local derivations depends on the Cayley algebra being split or division. Every 2 2 -local derivation on the split Cayley algebra is a derivation, i.e. they form the exceptional Lie algebra 𝔤 2 ​ ( 𝔽 ) {\mathfrak{g}}_{2}(\mathbb{F}) if char ​ 𝔽 ≠ 2 , 3 \textrm{char}\mathbb{F}\neq 2,3 . On the other hand, on division Cayley algebras over a field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} , the sets of 2 2 -local derivations and local derivations coincide, and they are isomorphic to the Lie algebra 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 ​ ( 𝒞 0 , n ) {\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C}_{0},\textup{n}) . As a corollary we obtain descriptions of local and 2 2 -local derivations of the seven dimensional simple non-Lie Malcev algebras over fields of characteristic ≠ 2 , 3 \neq 2,3 .

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an algebra (not necessary associative). Recall that a linear mapping
d:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
is said to be a derivation, if
d(xy)=d(x)y+xd(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
.
A linear mapping
\Delta
is said to be a
local derivation, if for every
x\in\mathcal{A}
there exists a derivation
d_{x}
on
\mathcal{A}
(depending on
x
) such that
\Delta(x)=d_{x}(x)
.
This notion was introduced and investigated independently by R.V. Kadison
[
K90
]
and D.R.
Larson and A.R. Sourour
[
LS90
]
.
The above papers gave rise to a series of works devoted to the description of mappings which are close to automorphisms and derivations of
C^{\ast}
-algebras and operator algebras.

In 1997, P.Šemrl
[
Š97
]
introduced the concepts of

2
-local derivations and
2
-automorphisms.
Recall that a mapping
\Delta:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
(not necessary linear) is said to be a
2
-local derivation, if for every pair
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
there exists a derivation
d_{x,y}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
(depending on
x,y
) such that

\Delta(x)=d_{x,y}(x)
,
\Delta(y)=d_{x,y}(y)
.

P. Šemrl
[
Š97
]
described
2
-local derivations on the algebra
B(H)
of all bounded linear operators on the infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space
H
, by proving that every
2
-local derivation on
B(H)
is a derivation.
A detailed discussion of
2
-local derivations on operator algebras can be found in the survey
[
AKR16
]
.

In
[
AKA21
]
a general form of local derivations on the real Cayley algebra
\mathbb{O}
was obtained.
This description implies that the space of all local derivations on
\mathbb{O}
, when equipped with Lie bracket, is isomorphic
to the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{so}}_{7}(\mathbb{R})
of all real skew-symmetric
7\times 7
-matrices.
Also it is proved that any
2
-local derivation on a Cayley algebra

\mathcal{C}
over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{F}
is a derivation.

The present paper is devoted to the description of local and
2
-local derivations on Cayley algebras over an arbitrary field, and it is organized as follows.

In Section 2 we give the necessary information concerning Cayley algebras and their derivations.

In Section 3 we find a general form for the local derivations on a Cayley algebra
\mathcal{C}
over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
with a norm
n
. Namely, we prove that the space of local derivations of
\mathcal{C}
is the Lie algebra

\{d\in{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C},\textup{n})\mid d(1)=0\}
(Theorem
3.1
).

In Section 4 we consider
2
-local derivations on a Cayley algebra
\mathcal{C}
.
It turns out that the structure of
2
-local derivations on a Cayley algebra
\mathcal{C}
depends on the norm
n
. If the norm
n
is isotropic, then
\mathcal{C}
is the split Cayley algebra and all
2
-local derivations on
\mathcal{C}
are derivations (Theorem
4.1
).
Further, if the norm
n
is anisotropic, then
\mathcal{C}
is a division Cayley algebra, and
n this case the spaces of all
2
-local
derivations and local derivations on
\mathcal{C}
coincide, and both are isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C}_{0},\textup{n})
(Theorem
4.9
).

Section 5 is devoted to applications of the above results to the description of local and
2
-local derivations of the seven dimensional simple Malcev algebras
over a field of characteristic
\neq 2
.

2.
Cayley algebras

Let
\mathbb{F}
be an arbitrary field. Cayley (or octonion) algebras over
\mathbb{F}

constitute a well-known class
of nonassociative algebras. They are unital nonassociative
algebras
\mathcal{C}
of dimension eight over
\mathbb{F}
,
endowed with a nonsingular quadratic multiplicative form (the norm)

\textup{n}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{F}
. Hence

\textup{n}(xy)=\textup{n}(x)\textup{n}(y)

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{C}
, and the polar form

\textup{n}(x,y):=\textup{n}(x+y)-\textup{n}(x)-\textup{n}(y)

is a nondegenerate bilinear form.

Any element in a Cayley algebra
\mathcal{C}
satisfies the degree
2
equation:

\displaystyle x^{2}-\textup{n}(x,1)x+\textup{n}(x)1=0.

(2.1)

The map
x\to\overline{x}=\textup{n}(x,1)1-x
is an involution
and the
trace
t(x)=\textup{n}(x,1)
and norm
\textup{n}(x)
are given by
t(x)1=x+\overline{x}
,

\textup{n}(x)1=x\overline{x}=\overline{x}x
for all
x\in\mathcal{C}
.

Note that two Cayley algebras
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
, with respective norms
\textup{n}_{1}

and
\textup{n}_{2}
, are isomorphic if and only if the norms
\textup{n}_{1}
and
\textup{n}_{2}
are
isometric (see
[
EK13
, Corollary 4.7]
). It must be remarked that Cayley
algebras are alternative, that is, the subalgebra generated by any two elements
is associative.

From (
2.1
), we get

\displaystyle xy+yx-\textup{n}(x,1)y-\textup{n}(y,1)x+\textup{n}(x,y)1=0,

(2.2)

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{C}
.

Recall that the norm
n
is isotropic if there is a non zero element
x\in\mathcal{C}
with
\textup{n}(x)=0
, otherwise its called anisotropic.
Note that any Cayley algebra with anisotropic norm is a division algebra.

It is known that, up to isomorphism, there is a unique Cayley algebra whose
norm is isotropic. It is called the split Cayley algebra. A split Cayley

\mathcal{C}
admits a
canonical basis

\{e_{1},e_{2},u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}

(see

Recall too that
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{F}e_{1}+\mathbb{F}e_{2}
, which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}\times\mathbb{F}
, is the split Hurwitz
algebra of dimension
2
, and with
\mathcal{U}=\mathbb{F}u_{1}+\mathbb{F}u_{2}+\mathbb{F}u_{3}
and
\mathcal{V}=\mathbb{F}v_{1}+\mathbb{F}v_{2}+\mathbb{F}v_{3}
,
the decomposition
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{K}\oplus\mathcal{U}\oplus\mathcal{V}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-grading:

\mathcal{C}_{\overline{0}}=\mathcal{K},\,\mathcal{C}_{\overline{1}}=\mathcal{U},\,\mathcal{C}_{\overline{2}}=\mathcal{V}
.
This induces a
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-grading on the Lie algebra of derivations

{\mathfrak{g}}=\Der(\mathcal{C})
, with

{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}=\left\{d\in{\mathfrak{g}}\mid d(\mathcal{C}_{i})\subset\mathcal{C}_{i+j}\,\,\,\textrm{for all}\,\,\,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}\right\}
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Cayley (or octonion) algebra over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
.
Note that any derivation on
\mathcal{C}
is skew-symmetric with respect to the norm
n
, i.e.,
\textup{n}\bigl(x,d(x)\bigr)=0
for all
x\in\mathcal{C}
, and this implies

\displaystyle\textup{n}(d(x),y)+\textup{n}(x,d(y))=0

(2.3)

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{C}
.

It is well-known that, over a field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
\neq 2,3
, the Lie algebra of all derivations
\Der(\mathcal{C})
of a Cayley algebra

\mathcal{C}

is a central simple Lie algebra of type
G_{2}
and, conversely, any central simple Lie algebra of type
G_{2}
is obtained, up to isomorphism, in this way. (See

[
Se67
, Theorem IV.4.1]
and the references there in.)

Over fields of characteristic
3
,
\Der(\mathcal{C})
is no longer simple, but has a unique
minimal ideal, which is simple and isomorphic to the Lie algebra
\mathcal{C}_{0}
of trace
zero elements with the Lie bracket
[x,y]=xy-yx
, which is a central
simple Lie algebra of type
A_{2}
. The quotient modulo this unique
minimal ideal is again isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}_{0}
. On the other hand, over fields of
characteristic
2
,
\Der(\mathcal{C})
is isomorphic to the split simple Lie algebra

{\mathfrak{psl}}_{4}(\mathbb{F})
of type
A_{3}
, and surprisingly this does not depend on whether

\mathcal{C}
is the split algebra or a division algebra. (See
[
C-RE16
, §§3,4]
and the references there in.)

The arguments in
[
EK13
, §4.4]
show that the coordinate matrix in this basis of any derivation of
\mathcal{C}
is of the form:

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\alpha^{\prime}&\beta^{\prime}&\gamma^{\prime}&-\alpha&-\beta&-\gamma\\

\beta&-\beta&a_{21}&a_{22}&a_{23}&-\gamma^{\prime}&0&\alpha^{\prime}\\

-\alpha^{\prime}&\alpha^{\prime}&0&\gamma&-\beta&-a_{11}&-a_{21}&-a_{31}\\
-\beta^{\prime}&\beta^{\prime}&-\gamma&0&\alpha&-a_{12}&-a_{22}&-a_{32}\\
-\gamma^{\prime}&\gamma^{\prime}&\beta&-\alpha&0&-a_{13}&-a_{23}&-a_{33}\end{pmatrix},

(2.4)

with
a_{11}+a_{22}+a_{33}=0
.

3.
Local derivations on Cayley algebras

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Cayley algebra over an arbitrary field with norm

n
.
Then the space of local derivations of
\mathcal{C}
is the Lie algebra

\{d\in{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C},\textup{n})\mid d(1)=0\}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{0}
be the subspace of trace
0
elements.
If the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
is not
2
, then
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{F}1\oplus\mathcal{C}_{0}
.
Embed
\End_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{C}_{0})
inside
\End_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{C})
by extending any endomorphism
\varphi
of
\mathcal{C}_{0}
to an endomorphism of
\mathcal{C}
by imposing
\varphi(1)=0
. Then
(characteristic not
2
) the Lie algebra in Theorem
3.1
is naturally identified with
{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C}_{0},\textup{n})=\{d\in\End_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{C}_{0})\mid\textup{n}\bigl(v,d(v)\bigr)=0\ \forall v\in\mathcal{C}_{0}\}
. The trace of any element
in
{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C}_{0},\textup{n})
is zero.

However, if the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
is
2
, then
1\in\mathcal{C}_{0}
. Here
the radical of
the restriction of the polarization of
n
to
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is
\mathbb{F}1
. In this case the right definition of
{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C}_{0},\textup{n})
is

\{d\in\End_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{C}_{0})\mid\operatorname{trace}(d)=0\ \text{and}\ \textup{n}\bigl(v,d(v)\bigr)=0\ \forall v\in\mathcal{C}_{0}\}
. It follows that any
element in
{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C}_{0},\textup{n})
annihilates
1
. Moreover, any element

d\in{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C},\textup{n})
with
d(1)=0
leaves
\mathcal{C}_{0}
invariant, and its restriction to
\mathcal{C}_{0}
lies in
{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C}_{0},\textup{n})
. Conversely, pick any element

u\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathbb{F}1
, so that
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{F}u\oplus\mathcal{C}_{0}
. Any element

d\in{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C}_{0},\textup{n})
can be extended to an element of

{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C},\textup{n})
by defining
d(u)
as the unique element of
\mathcal{C}

such that
\textup{n}\bigl(d(u),u\bigr)=0
and

\textup{n}\bigl(d(u),x\bigr)=\textup{n}\bigl(u,d(x)\bigr)
for all
x\in\mathcal{C}_{0}
. Therefore, the restriction

\begin{split}\{d\in{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C},\textup{n})\mid d(1)=0\}&\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C}_{0},\textup{n})\\
d\quad&\mapsto\quad d|_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}\end{split}

is an isomorphism of Lie algebras also in characteristic
2
.

Therefore, in any characteristic, we can identify canonically the Lie algebra

\{d\in{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C},\textup{n})\mid d(1)=0\}
with
{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C}_{0},\textup{n})
.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
is based on two lemmas. The first one
is surely well-known. For Cayley division algebras over fields of characteristic not two, it follows from the Cayley-Dickson doubling process as in

[
SV00
, Corollary 1.7.2]
. We include a proof, valid over arbitrary fields, for completeness.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Cayley over a field
\mathbb{F}
with norm
n
. Any two elements of

\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathbb{F}1
are conjugate under
\Aut\mathcal{C}
if and only if they have the same norm and the same trace.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathbb{F}1
with
t(a)=t(b)=\alpha
, and
\textup{n}(a)=\textup{n}(b)=\mu
.
The minimal polynomial of both
a
and
b
is
p(X)=X^{2}-\alpha X+\mu
.

If
p(X)
is separable, then
\mathcal{K}_{1}=\mathbb{F}1+\mathbb{F}a
and
\mathcal{K}_{2}=\mathbb{F}1+\mathbb{F}b
are isomorphic
two-dimensional composition subalgebras of
\mathcal{C}
, and we use the Cayley-Dickson
doubling process to extend any isomorphim between
\mathcal{K}_{1}
and
\mathcal{K}_{2}
carrying
a
to
b
to an automorphism of
\mathcal{C}
.

Otherwise, either the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
is not
2
and

p(X)=\bigl(X-\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigr)^{2}
, or the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
is
2
and

\alpha=0
.

Note that if
x\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathbb{F}1
satisfies
x^{2}=0
and we pick
y\in\mathcal{C}
with

\textup{n}(x,\bar{y})=1
, then

1=\textup{n}(x,\bar{y})=-\bar{x}y-\bar{y}x=xy-\bar{y}x,
and then

e_{1}=xy
is a nonzero idempotent, because

e_{1}^{2}=xyxy=(1+\bar{y}x)xy=xy+\bar{y}x^{2}y=e_{1}
. Also, we have

e_{1}x=xyx=(1+\bar{y}x)x=x
and
xe_{1}=x^{2}y=0
. Hence
x
lies in the Peirce component

\{u\in\mathcal{C}\mid e_{1}x=x,\ xe_{1}=0\}=e_{1}\mathcal{C}e_{2}
, and as in
[
EK13
, §4.4]
we may take a
canonical basis of
\mathcal{C}
with
x=u_{1}
. In particular, if
\alpha=0=\mu
, then
a
and
b

are both conjugate to
u_{1}
.

Also, if the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
is not
2
and

p(X)=\bigl(X-\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigr)^{2}
, then there are automorphisms
\varphi,\psi
of
\mathcal{C}
such that
\varphi(a-\frac{\alpha}{2}1)=u_{1}=\psi(b-\frac{\alpha}{2}1)
,
and hence
\psi^{-1}\circ\varphi(a)=b
.

Finally, if the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
is
2
and
\alpha=0
but
\mu\neq 0
, we distinguish two cases:

•

If
1\in\mathbb{F}a+\mathbb{F}b
, then
b=\epsilon 1+\delta a
with
\delta\neq 0
, so

\mu=\textup{n}(a)=\textup{n}(b)=\epsilon^{2}+\delta^{2}\textup{n}(a)
. Hence

(1+\delta)^{2}\textup{n}(a)=\epsilon^{2}

and either
\delta=1
,
\epsilon=0
and
a=b
, or
\delta\neq 1
and

\mu=\textup{n}(a)=\textup{n}(b)\in\mathbb{F}^{2}
. Write
\mu=\gamma^{2}
. In this case,

(a+\gamma 1)^{2}=0=(b+\gamma 1)^{2}
and again there are automorphisms

\varphi,\psi
of
\mathcal{C}
such that
\varphi(a+\gamma 1)=u_{1}=\psi(b+\gamma 1)
,
so that
\psi^{-1}\circ\varphi(a)=b
.

•

If, on the contrary,
1\not\in\mathbb{F}a+\mathbb{F}b
, then
(\mathbb{F}a+\mathbb{F}b)^{\perp}\not\subseteq(\mathbb{F}1)^{\perp}
, so we may pick an element
u\in\mathcal{C}
such that

\textup{n}(u,1)=1
and
\textup{n}(u,a)=0=\textup{n}(u,b)
. Then
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{F}1+\mathbb{F}u
is a
two-dimensional composition subalgebra of
\mathcal{C}
and, by the Cayley-Dickson doubling
process, the quaternion subalgebra
\mathcal{Q}_{1}=\mathcal{K}\oplus\mathcal{K}a
and
\mathcal{Q}_{2}=\mathcal{K}\oplus\mathcal{K}b

are isomorphic, under an isomorphism that is the identity on
\mathcal{K}
and takes
a
to

b
. Again, by the Cayley-Dickson doubling process, this isomorphism can be
extended to an automorphism of
\mathcal{C}
. (See, for instance,

[
SV00
, Corollary 1.7.2]
.) ∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Cayley algebra over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
, with Lie algebra of derivations
\Der(\mathcal{C})
, and let
x
be any element in
\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathbb{F}1
. Then

\Der(\mathcal{C})x:=\{d(x)\mid d\in\Der(\mathcal{C})\}=(\mathbb{F}1+\mathbb{F}x)^{\perp}=\bigl(\mathbb{F}x\bigr)^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{C}_{0},

where
\perp
denotes the orthogonal subspace relative to the norm
n
.

Proof.

We may extend scalars and assume that
\mathbb{F}
is algebraically closed. In particular,
\mathcal{C}
is split. Take a canonical basis of
\mathcal{C}
as in Table
1
. If the minimal polynomial of
x
is separable:

p(X)=(X-\alpha)(X-\beta)
, with
\alpha\neq\beta
, then Lemma
3.3

shows that
x
is conjugate to
\alpha e_{1}+\beta e_{2}
. But
Equation (
2.4
) shows that

\Der(\mathcal{C})(\alpha e_{1}+\beta e_{2})=\mathcal{U}\oplus\mathcal{V}=(\mathbb{F}e_{1}+\mathbb{F}e_{2})^{\perp}=\bigl(\mathbb{F}1+\mathbb{F}(\alpha e_{1}+\beta e_{2})\bigr)^{\perp}

and we are done in this case.

Otherwise the minimal polynomial of
x
is of the form

p(X)=(X-\lambda)^{2}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
. By Lemma
3.3
,
x-\lambda 1
is conjugate to
u_{1}
, and hence
x
is conjugate to
\lambda 1+u_{1}
. Now Equation (
2.4
) shows that

\Der(\mathcal{C})(\lambda 1+u_{1})=\Der(\mathcal{C})u_{1}=\mathbb{F}(e_{1}-e_{2})\oplus\mathcal{U}\oplus\mathbb{F}v_{2}\oplus\mathbb{F}v_{3}=(\mathbb{F}1+\mathbb{F}u_{1})^{\perp},

and we are done.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

If
\Delta
is a local derivation of
\mathcal{C}
,

\Delta(1)=0
and for any
x\in\mathcal{C}
, we get
\textup{n}\bigl(x,\Delta(x)\bigr)=0
, as

\Delta(x)=d(x)
for a derivation
d
and
\Der(\mathcal{C})
is contained in

\{\phi\in{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C},\textup{n})\mid\phi(1)=0\}
. Hence the space of local derivations of

\mathcal{C}
is contained in
\{\phi\in{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C},\textup{n})\mid\phi(1)=0\}
.

Conversely, given any
\Delta
in
\{\phi\in{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C},\textup{n})\mid\phi(1)=0\}

and any
x\in\mathcal{C}
, if
x\in\mathbb{F}1
, then
\Delta(x)=0
, while if

x\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathbb{F}1
,

\textup{n}\bigl(x,\Delta(x)\bigr)=0=\textup{n}\bigl(1,\Delta(x)\bigr)
, so

\Delta(x)\in\bigl(\mathbb{F}x\bigr)^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{C}_{0}
.
Hence
\Delta(x)\in\Der(\mathcal{C})x
by Lemma
3.4
, and it follows that there is a derivation
d\in\Der(\mathcal{C})
such that
\Delta(x)=d(x)
.
∎

Note that Theorem
3.1
implies the following result (see
[
AKA21
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Corollary 3.5
.

The space
\LocDer\left(\mathbb{O}\right)
of all local derivations on the real Cayley algebra
\mathbb{O}
equipped with the Lie bracket is isomorphic to the Lie algebra

{\mathfrak{so}}_{7}(\mathbb{R})
of all real skew-symmetric matrices of order
7
.

Remark 3.6
.

Note that the dimensions of the Lie algebras

4.1.

Theorem 4.1

The proof of the following lemma is similar to the proof of

Lemma 4.2

Any
2
-local derivation on an arbitrary Cayley algebra is linear.

Proof.

Note that any
2
-local derivation
\Delta
on
\mathcal{C}
is ‘homogeneous’:
\Delta(\lambda x)=\lambda\Delta(x)
for all

\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
and

Indeed, take a derivation

on
\mathcal{C}
such that
\Delta(x)=d_{x,\lambda x}(x)
and
\Delta(\lambda x)=d_{x,\lambda x}(\lambda x)
. Then

\displaystyle\Delta(\lambda x)

\displaystyle=d_{x,\lambda x}(\lambda x)=\lambda d_{x,\lambda x}(x)=\lambda\Delta(x).

Let us show that
\Delta
is additive.

Let
x,z\in\mathcal{C}
. Take a derivation
d_{x,z}
such that

\Delta(x)=d_{x,z}(x)
and
\Delta(z)=d_{x,z}(z)
.
From Equation (
2.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\textup{n}(\Delta(x),z)=\textup{n}(d_{x,z}(x),z)=-\textup{n}(x,d_{x,z}(z))=-\textup{n}(x,\Delta(z)).

So,

\displaystyle\textup{n}(\Delta(x),z)=-\textup{n}(x,\Delta(z)).

(4.1)

Now replacing the element
x
by
x+y
in (
4.1
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\textup{n}(\Delta(x+y),z)

\displaystyle=-\textup{n}(x+y,\Delta(z))=-\textup{n}(x,\Delta(z))-\textup{n}(y,\Delta(z))

\displaystyle=\textup{n}(\Delta(x),z)+\textup{n}(\Delta(y),z)=\textup{n}(\Delta(x)+\Delta(y),z).

Thus

\displaystyle\textup{n}(\Delta(x+y)-\Delta(x)-\Delta(y),z)

\displaystyle=0

for all
z\in\mathcal{C}
. Since
the bilinear form
n
is nondegenerate, it follows that

\Delta(x+y)=\Delta(x)+\Delta(y)
.
∎

So, any
2
-local derivation on a Cayley algebra is linear and annihilates
1
.

Corollary 4.3

Any

-local derivation on an arbitrary Cayley algebra

Lemma 4.4

Let

4.2.
2-Local derivations on division Cayley algebras

The situation for Cayley division algebras is completely different.
In order to deal with it, some preliminaries are needed.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the split Cayley algebra over an arbitrary field, and take a canonical basis

\{e_{1},e_{2},u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}
as in Table
1
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the split Cayley algebra over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
. Consider the
following subalgebras of
\Der(\mathcal{C})

\mathcal{L}=\{d\in\Der(\mathcal{C})\mid d(e_{1})=d(e_{2})=0\},\quad\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\{d\in\Der(\mathcal{C})\mid d(u_{1}+v_{1})=0\}.

Then the dimension of each of the following subspaces:

\mathcal{L}(u_{1}+v_{1})
,

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u_{1}+\mu v_{1})
with
1\neq\mu\in\mathbb{F}
, and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u_{2}+v_{2})

5
.

Proof.

These subspaces are easily computed using Equation (

•

\mathcal{L}(u_{1}+v_{1})=\espan\langle u_{1}-v_{1},u_{2},u_{3},v_{2},v_{3}\rangle

•

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u_{1}+\mu v_{1})=\espan\langle e_{1}-e_{2},u_{2},u_{3},v_{2},v_{3}\rangle
(

), and

•

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u_{2}+v_{2})=\espan\langle e_{1}-e_{2},u_{1}+v_{1},u_{2}-v_{2},u_{3},v_{3}\rangle
.

The result follows.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Cayley division algebra over an arbitrary field. Let
x,y\in\mathcal{C}
be elements such that
1
,
x
, and
y
, are linearly independent. Consider the following subalgebra of
\Der(\mathcal{C})
:
\mathcal{S}=\{d\in\Der(\mathcal{C})\mid d(x)=0\}
.
Assume that either:

•

The restriction of the norm
n
to
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{F}1+\mathbb{F}x
is nonsingular (that is,
\mathcal{K}
is a two-dimensional composition subalgebra, and note that this is always the case if the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
is not
2

•

The characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
is
2
and
\textup{n}(1,x)=0=\textup{n}(1,y)
.

Then the subspace
\mathcal{S}y
equals
(\mathbb{F}1+\mathbb{F}x+\mathbb{F}y)^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Note first that since
\Der(\mathcal{C})
is contained in the orthogonal Lie algebra

{\mathfrak{so}}(\mathcal{C},\textup{n})
, we always have
\mathcal{S}y\subseteq(\mathbb{F}1+\mathbb{F}x+\mathbb{F}y)^{\perp}

5
because
1
,
x
and
y
are linearly independent. Therefore, it is enough to check that the dimension of
\mathcal{S}y
is
5
.
Also, in order to check this, we may extend scalars to an algebraic closure

\overline{\mathbb{F}}

Assume first that
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{F}1+\mathbb{F}x
is a composition subalgebra of
\mathcal{C}
, then
y=a+y^{\prime}
with

and
0\neq y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}^{\perp}

\mathcal{C}
is a division algebra,

is anisotropic, so
\textup{n}(y^{\prime})\neq 0
. Note that
\mathcal{S}y=\mathcal{S}y^{\prime}
, because
\mathcal{S}
annihilates both

and

. Hence we may assume

. Extending scalars to
\overline{\mathbb{F}}

\overline{\mathcal{C}}

If

Assume now that the characteristic is

\textup{n}(1,x)=0=\textup{n}(1,y)

Let
\mathcal{C}

5.
Local and
2
-local derivations on
7
-dimensional simple Malcev algebras

An algebra
\mathcal{A}
over a field
\mathbb{F}
is called a Malcev algebra if its multiplication is anticommutative and satisfies the following identity:

{\displaystyle(xy)(xz)=((xy)z)x+((yz)x)x+((zx)x)y.}

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Cayley algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic

\neq 2
and define a bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
by

[x,y]=\frac{1}{2}(xy-yx),\,x,y\in\mathcal{C}.

Then the space
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{-}=\left\{x\in\mathcal{C}:t(x)=0\right\}
with the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
, is a simple Malcev algebra (see, e.g.,
[
EM94
, Theorem 4.10]
).

If the characteristic of the ground field
\mathbb{F}
is
\neq 2,3
,

\mathcal{C}_{0}^{-}
is a
central simple non-Lie Malcev algebra and, conversely, any such algebra is obtained, up to
isomorphism, in this way. (See
[
F75b
]
.)

However, if the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
is
3
, then any simple Malcev algebra over

\mathbb{F}
is a Lie algebra (
[
F75a
]
). In this case,
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{-}

is a central simple Lie algebra of type
A_{2}
(i.e., a twisted form of

{\mathfrak{psl}}_{3}(\mathbb{F})
) and, conversely, any central simple Lie algebra of type
A_{2}
is
obtained in this way. (See
[
AEMN02
]
or
[
EK13
, Theorem 4.26]
.)

It is known
[
EM94
, Chapter IV, Lemma 6.1]
that every derivation on a Cayley
algebra
\mathcal{C}
defines a derivation on
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{-}
, and conversely.
More precisely, if
d
is a derivation on
\mathcal{C}
, then the restriction

d|_{\mathcal{C}_{0}^{-}}
is a derivation on
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{-}
. Conversely, if
d
is a derivation on
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{-}
,
then its extension to
\mathcal{C}
defined
as

d(\lambda 1+x)=d(x),\,\lambda\in\mathbb{F},\,x\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{-},

is a derivation on
\mathcal{C}
.
Therefore a similar correspondence between local (
2
-local) derivations
of the Cayley algebra
\mathcal{C}
and the Malcev algebra
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{-}
are also true.

So, from Theorems
3.1
,
4.1
and
4.9
we obtain the following results.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{-}
be the central simple Malcev algebra associated with a Cayley algebra
\mathcal{C}
over a field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
\neq 2

(1)

the space of all local derivations of

is the Lie algebra

(2)

if

(3)

\mathcal{C}

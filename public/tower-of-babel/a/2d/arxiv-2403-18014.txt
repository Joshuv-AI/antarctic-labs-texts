In this article, we focus on establishing the existence of positive ground state solutions for the following

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\Delta u+A_{0}u+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}A_{j}^{2}u=f(u)-a(x)|u|^{p-2}u,\\

Proof of Theorem

Let

and

with

. We denote by

the Schwarz symmetrization of

is radial and non-increasing.
Thanks to the results in
[

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u^{*}|^{2}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx,\penalty\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u^{*}|^{p}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{p}dx

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Phi_{\alpha,j_{0}}(u^{*})dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Phi_{\alpha,j_{0}}(u)dx.

So, without loss of generality, we can suppose that
u\in E
is non-increasing.
Given an
R>0
which will be determined later,
due to Lemma
2.1
, we could deduce that the function
v(x)\triangleq u(x)-u(R)

belongs to
H_{0}^{1}(B_{R}(0))
. Adopting the Young’s inequality, there holds

\displaystyle u^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=v^{2}(x)+2v(x)u(R)+u^{2}(R)\leq v^{2}(x)+[1+v^{2}(x)u^{2}(R)]+u^{2}(R)

\displaystyle=[1+u^{2}(R)]v^{2}(x)+1+u^{2}(R)\triangleq w^{2}(x)+1+u^{2}(R),\penalty\ \forall x\in B_{R}(0).

Obviously,
w\triangleq\sqrt{1+u^{2}(R)}v\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{R}(0))
and
\nabla w=\sqrt{1+u^{2}(R)}\nabla v=\sqrt{1+u^{2}(R)}\nabla u
.
Moreover, we recall from
[
5
, Lemma A.IV]
,
because
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
by Lemma
2.1
, there is a constant
C_{p}>0
which is independent of
u
such that

(2.5)

|u(x)|\leq C_{p}|x|^{-\frac{2}{p}}|u|_{p}\penalty\ \text{for all}\penalty\ x\neq 0.

Consequently, with the help of (
2.5
), we are able to see that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla w|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=[1+u^{2}(R)]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leq[1+u^{2}(R)]\left[1-\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 1-\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}+u^{2}(R)\leq 1-(1-C_{p}^{2}R^{-\frac{4}{p}})\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}.

We then choose the constant
R
to be sufficiently large such that
1-C_{p}^{2}R^{-\frac{4}{p}}\geq 0
and so

|\nabla w|_{2}^{2}\leq 1
. As a consequence,
we can apply the classic Trudinger-Moser inequality explored in
[
7
,
10
]
to arrive at

\int_{B_{R}(0)}\Phi_{4\pi,j_{0}}(u)\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{B_{R}(0)}e^{4\pi u^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\leq e^{4\pi(1+u^{2}(R))}\int_{B_{R}(0)}e^{4\pi w^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\leq C.

From which, one concludes that

(2.6)

\sup_{u\in E:\|u\|\leq 1}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\Phi_{4\pi,j_{0}}(u)\mathrm{d}x\leq C<+\infty.

On the other hand,
we can follow the proof of
[
33
, Theorem 1.1]
to verify that the integral on the complement of

is uniformly bounded. For the sake of the reader’s convenience, we shall show the proof in detail. Using

[
5
, Lemma A.IV]
and Lemma
2.1

for each

, there is a constant

independent of

|u(x)|\leq\bar{C}_{p}|x|^{-\frac{2-p}{p}}|u|_{p^{*}}\penalty\ \text{for all}\penalty\ x\neq 0.

Since

, then

and so applying (

\begin{gathered}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{R}(0)}\Phi_{4\pi,j_{0}}(u)\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{(4\pi)^{j}}{j!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{R}(0)}|u|^{2j}\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{(4\pi)^{j}}{j!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{R}(0)}|u|^{2j-p^{*}}|u|^{p^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\hfill\\
\ \ \ \ \leq\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{(4\pi)^{j}}{j!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{R}(0)}({\bar{C}}_{p}|u|_{p^{*}})^{2j-p^{*}}|u|^{p^{*}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{{\bar{C}}_{p}^{p^{*}}|u|_{p^{*}}^{p^{*}}}\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{(4\pi)^{j}}{j!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{\varrho}(0)}({\bar{C}}_{p}|u|_{p^{*}})^{2j}|u|^{p^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\hfill\\
\ \ \ \ \leq\frac{e^{4\pi({\bar{C}}_{p}|u|_{p^{*}})^{2}}}{{\bar{C}}_{p}^{p^{*}}|u|_{p^{*}}^{p^{*}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{\varrho}(0)}|u|^{p^{*}}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{e^{4\pi({\bar{C}}_{p}|u|_{p^{*}})^{2}}}{{\bar{C}}_{p}^{p^{*}}}\leq\frac{e^{4\pi(\tilde{C}_{p}\|u\|)^{2}}}{\bar{C}_{p}^{p^{*}}}=\frac{e^{4\pi\tilde{C}_{p}^{2}}}{{\bar{C}}_{p}^{p^{*}}}.\hfill\\
\end{gathered}

From this inequality and (

\mathbb{S}(\alpha)\leq C+{e^{4\pi\tilde{C}_{p}^{2}}}{{\bar{C}}_{p}^{-p^{*}}}<+\infty,\penalty\ \forall\alpha\in(0,4\pi].

The remaining parts are totally same as in

∎

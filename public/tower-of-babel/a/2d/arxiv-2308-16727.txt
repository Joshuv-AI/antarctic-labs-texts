On convolution closure properties of subexponentiality approaching from densities Thanks: Muneya Matsui’s research is partly supported by the JSPS Grant-in-Aid for Scientific Research C (23K11019).
Muneya Matsui, Toshiro Watanabe

Abstract
. Non-closedness of subexponentiality by the convolution operation is well-known. We go a step further and show that subexponentiality and non-subexponentiality are generally changeable by the convolution. We also give several conditions, by which (non-) subexponentiality is kept. Most results are given with densities, which are easily converted to those for distributions. As a by-product, we give counterexamples to several past results, which were used to derive the non-closedness of the convolution, and modify the original proof.

Let

\mathbb{R}:=(-\infty,\infty)

F
and

The tail probability of

\overline{F}(x)=1-F(x)
.
Let

be the corresponding probability density functions on
\mathbb{R}
and
denote by

Throughout the paper,

\alpha(x)\sim\beta(x)

means that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\alpha(x)/\beta(x)\to 1
.

We study the following tail equivalence properties for a density
f
and a distribution
F
.

Definition 1.1
.

(\mathrm{i})
A non-negative measurable function
\alpha(x)
belongs to the class
{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma},\,\gamma\geq 0

if

\lim_{x\to\infty}\alpha(x-y)/\alpha(x)=e^{\gamma y},\quad y\in\mathbb{R}.

If
\gamma>0
, the convergence is uniform in
y\leq y^{\prime}
for each
y^{\prime}>0
.
In case
\gamma=0
, we write
{\mathcal{L}}:={\mathcal{L}}_{0}
.

(\mathrm{ii})

F
is
\gamma

\mathbb{R}
, denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}_{\gamma}
if
\overline{F}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma}

and
\overline{F^{\ast 2}}(x)\sim 2\widehat{f}(\gamma)\overline{F}(x)
, where

\widehat{f}(\gamma)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\gamma x}F(dx)<\infty.

In case
\gamma=0
we write
{\mathcal{S}}:={\mathcal{S}}_{0}
.

(\mathrm{iii})
The density
f

is
\gamma

\mathbb{R}
, denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}_{\gamma}
,

. In case

{\mathcal{S}}:={\mathcal{S}}_{0}
.

Notice that the definition of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\gamma}
includes the existence of
\gamma

We abuse notation
{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{\gamma}

Since we specify either

In this note, focusing on densities, we tackle the convolution closure properties of

Recall that an example:

We go a step further and show that

(1)

f,g\notin{\mathcal{S}}\Rightarrow f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}}

(2)

the convolution of a subexponential density and a non-subexponential density could yield
both cases,
f\in{\mathcal{S}},g\notin{\mathcal{S}}\Rightarrow f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
f\in{\mathcal{S}},g\notin{\mathcal{S}}\Rightarrow f\ast g\notin{\mathcal{S}}

This means that we could exhaust all possible patterns.

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq x}\alpha(t)\sim\alpha(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\inf_{x_{0}\leq t\leq x}\alpha(t)\sim\alpha(x).

2.
The closure properties on subexponential density

The closure properties of
{\mathcal{S}}
are theoretically studied.
We separate the results for densities on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
from those for densities on
\mathbb{R}
, which

At the end of this section, we show the equivalent relation between

2.1.
Subexponential density on
\mathbb{R}_{+}

We give two lemmas, which are also used to construct examples in Section
3
.
The first lemma relates the closure property for the mixture and that for the convolution.

Lemma 2.1
.

(\mathrm{i})
Let
f,g\in{\mathcal{L}}
. Then,
f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}}\Leftrightarrow pf+(1-p)g\in{\mathcal{S}}
for some
p\in(0,1)
.
If
f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}}
, then
f\ast g(x)\sim f(x)+g(x)
.

(\mathrm{ii})

Let
f,g\in{\mathcal{S}}
. Then,
f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}}\Leftrightarrow f\ast g(x)\sim f(x)+g(x)
.

Lemma
2.1

(\mathrm{ii})
is the modification of
[
17
, Lemma 2]
, where only
f,g\in{\mathcal{L}}

is assumed, which is not enough (see a counterexample (Ex.4) in the next section).

A motivation for the second lemma is coming from an interesting example given in
[
17
, Section 3]
,
which shows that
f,g\in{\mathcal{S}}
does not always imply
f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}}
. At there we cannot tell which has a dominant tail between

f
and
g
. Then, a natural question arises: if
f\in{\mathcal{S}}
or
{\mathcal{S}}^{c}
and
g(x)=o(f(x))
as
x\to\infty
, then what happens to

f\ast g
. The following lemma gives an answer.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f,g
be densities on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
f\in{\mathcal{L}}
and
g(x)=o\big(f(x)\big)
when
x\to\infty
. Then

(\mathrm{i})

h=f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}}\Leftrightarrow f\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

(\mathrm{ii})
Assume that

\int_{\alpha(x)}^{x-\alpha(x)}f(x-y)g(y)dy=o\big(f(x)\big)

for a function
\alpha(x)
such that
\alpha(x)<x/2,\,\alpha(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
and
f
is
\alpha
-insensitive.
Then
f\in{\mathcal{L}}\setminus{\mathcal{S}}\Leftrightarrow h\in{\mathcal{L}}\setminus{\mathcal{S}}
.
In both cases
f(x)\sim h(x)
holds.

Notice that similar findings for the distribution were pointed by
[
10
, Proposition 1]
,

[
22
, Lemma 2.4]
and
[
18
, Proposition 1.1]
.
Due to Lemma
2.2
, the case
f,g\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
f\ast g\notin{\mathcal{S}}
occurs only if

f(x)\neq o(g(x))
and
g(x)\neq o(f(x))
hold in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, i.e. neither
f
nor
g
dominates the other in the tail.
Indeed, the example in
[
17
]
is included in the case, and so is
the example that
f,g\notin{\mathcal{S}}
but
f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}}
(Ex.3) given in Section

.

2.2.
Subexponential density on
\mathbb{R}

The closure properties of
{\mathcal{S}}
for densities on
\mathbb{R}

are theoretically studied. We need additional assumptions in the two-sided case
to obtain the same conclusions as those for the positive-half case.
We apply the al.d. property
{\mathcal{D}}
(Definition
1.2
), which would be a reasonable assumption.
Indeed, one could see in the following lemma that

{\mathcal{D}}
is inevitable to extend the notion
{\mathcal{S}}
from densities on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to those on
\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f^{+}
and
f^{-}
be densities on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\mathbb{R}_{-}:=\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{+}
, respectively and
for
p\in(0,1)
let
f=pf^{+}+(1-p)f^{-}
. If
f^{+}\in{\mathcal{S}}\cap{\mathcal{D}}^{c}
, then
there exists
f^{-}
such that
f\notin{\mathcal{S}}
.

Take
p=F([0,\infty))
and
f^{+}=f_{+}
, the conditional density of
f
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Then, Lemma
2.3
asserts that even
f_{+}\in{\mathcal{S}}
, without
f\in{\mathcal{D}}

we can not rule out the possibility of
f\notin{\mathcal{S}}
.

The following is the counterpart of Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f,g\in{\mathcal{L}}
. Suppose that
pf+(1-p)g\in{\mathcal{S}}
for some
p\in(0,1)
and then

f\ast g(x)\sim f(x)+g(x)
. If additionally
pf+(1-p)g\in{\mathcal{D}}
for the same

\limsup_{x\to-\infty}\frac{f\ast g(x)}{f(x)+g(x)}\leq C
for some
C>0
, and then
f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

Conversely if
f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
[

f\ast g\in{\mathcal{D}}
or
\liminf_{x\to-\infty}\frac{f\ast g(x)}{f(x)+g(x)}\geq C
for some
C>0

]
, then
f\ast g(x)\sim f(x)+g(x)
, and moreover
2^{-1}(f+g)\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

Remark 2.5
.

From Lemma
2.4
, we have the following fact.

Assume that
f,g\in{\mathcal{L}}
and
[

f\ast g\in{\mathcal{D}}
or
f\ast g(x)\asymp f(x)+g(x)
as
x\to-\infty

]
. Then

pf(x)+(1-p)g(x)\in{\mathcal{S}}
for some
p\in(0,1)
if and only if
f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

The next lemma is a counterpart of Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f,g
be densities on
\mathbb{R}
such that
f\in{\mathcal{L}}\cap{\mathcal{D}}
and
g(x)=o(f(x))
. Then,

(\mathrm{i})

f\in{\mathcal{S}}\Leftrightarrow f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

(\mathrm{ii})

Assume that
\int_{\alpha(x)}^{x-\alpha(x)}f(x-y)g(y)dy=o(f(x))

for a function
\alpha(x)
such that
\alpha(x)<x/2,\,\alpha(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
and

is
\alpha

f\in{\mathcal{L}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}^{c}\Leftrightarrow f\ast g\in{\mathcal{L}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}^{c}
.

Remark 2.7
.

Notice that in view of Lemmas
2.2
and
2.6
,
the tail domination assumption is one option to control the property
{\mathcal{S}}
of the mixture and the convolution,
which are often equivalent. If the tail domination does not hold, then we show by examples that
all possible cases occur in the next section.

Finally, we see the equivalent relation between
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{\gamma}
with
\gamma>0
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
f
be a density on
\mathbb{R}
and let
\gamma>0
.
Assume that
f
has the exponential
-\gamma
moment, i.e.
\widehat{f}(-\gamma)<\infty
.
Then for the exponential tilt
f_{\gamma}
of

, we have

f_{\gamma}(x):=e^{-\gamma x}f(x)/\widehat{f}(-\gamma)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\gamma}\,(\text{resp.}\,{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma})\Leftrightarrow f\in{\mathcal{S}}\,(\text{resp}.\,{\mathcal{L}}).

Notice that the exponential tilt is preserved by the convolution, i.e.

f_{\gamma}\ast g_{\gamma}(x)=e^{-\gamma x}f\ast g(x)/\widehat{g\ast f}(-\gamma),

so that the relation
f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}}\Leftrightarrow f_{\gamma}\ast g_{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\gamma}

This means that our theories in Section

and examples in Section

are also

Examples of convolution of (non-)subexponential densities

In this section we investigate closedness property of the convolution together with that of the mixture

Counterexamples to Lemma 1 of

Recall that an example of [

]
was found by
[
17
]
. Here we step further to
the complete answer of the convolution property. We
construct examples of

such that the other three cases hold, i.e.

\displaystyle f,g\in{\mathcal{L}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}^{c}\,

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\,f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}},\hskip 56.9055pt\text{Example 1 (one-sided)}

\displaystyle f\in{\mathcal{L}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}^{c},g\in{\mathcal{S}}\,

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\,f\ast g\in{\mathcal{L}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}^{c},\hskip 27.03003pt\text{ Example 2 (one-sided)}

\displaystyle f\in{\mathcal{L}}\cap{\mathcal{S}}^{c},g\in{\mathcal{S}}\,

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\,f\ast g\in{\mathcal{S}},\hskip 54.06006pt\text{ Example 3 (two-sided)}

where all examples are
constructed with the condition that the tails of

and

do not dominate the other.
In summary, the classes
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{c}
(within

)
are interchangeable by the convolution operation when the tail domination does not occur.
Notice that from Lemmas
2.2
and

, if a tail of

or

the dominant one is likely to be succeeded.

({\mathcal{D}})
and a.n.i.

or

and a.n.i. Then it remained to be seen that what happens to

or

if

or

In

On one hand, in Ex.2, both
({\mathcal{L}}\setminus{\mathcal{S}})\cap{\mathcal{D}}^{c}
(by

(by

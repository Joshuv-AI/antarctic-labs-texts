Relative Entropy of Hypersurfaces in Hyperbolic Space
Junfu Yao

Abstract
. We study a notion of relative entropy for certain hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space. We relate this quantity to the renormalized area introduced by Graham-Witten [ RW99 ] . We also obtain a monotonicity formula for relative entropy applied to mean curvature flows in hyperbolic space.



1.
Introduction

Let
n\geq 2
and
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
denote the (
n+1
)-dimensional hyperbolic space. We recall that the Poincaré half-space model for
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is the open upper half-space

\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}=\{(\textbf{x},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}:\ y>0\},

equipped with the metric

g_{P}=\frac{1}{y^{2}}(d\textbf{x}\otimes d\textbf{x}+dy\otimes dy)=\frac{1}{y^{2}}g_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}.

In this model, we have the standard compatification:
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{H}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{n+1}=\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\cup(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\})\cup\{\infty\}
. The set
(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\})\cup\{\infty\}=\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
is called the
ideal boundary
of
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and denoted by
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. The ideal boundary of
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is associated to a unique conformal structure. A
boundary defining function

r
on
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{H}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{n+1}
is a function such that the metric
r^{2}g_{P}
extends smoothly to
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{H}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{n+1}
.

Minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
are critical points of the functional

(1.1)

\text{Vol}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}1\cdot d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{y^{n}}\cdot d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

Minimal hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space have been extensively studied, see
[
And82
]
,
[
And83
]
,
[
Gab97
]
,
[
dOS98
]
,
[
MW13
]
,
[
HL87
]
,
[
Lin89
]
,
[
Ton96
]
,
[
Cos06
]
,
[
Cos11
]
,
[
AM10
]
and
[
HW15
]
.

As one may observe, there are no closed minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. So, it is natural to consider minimal hypersurfaces that are asymptotic to the ideal boundary (See Definition
2.1
for the terminology). However, the volume (
1.1
) is always divergent for any
\Sigma
that has asymptotic boundary. Let
r
be any boundary defining function for
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{H}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{n+1}
. In
[
RW99
]
, Graham-Witten showed that for a sufficiently regular minimal hypersurface
\Sigma
and an even integer
n
, the volume of
\{r\geq\epsilon\}\cap\Sigma
has an expansion

(1.2)

\text{Vol}_{\epsilon}(\Sigma;r)=\int_{\Sigma\cap\{r\geq\epsilon\}}1\cdot d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}=\frac{c_{0}}{\epsilon^{n-1}}+\frac{c_{2}}{\epsilon^{n-3}}+\cdots+\frac{c_{n-2}}{\epsilon}+c_{n}+o(1).

If
n
is odd, the expansion takes the following form:

(1.3)

\text{Vol}_{\epsilon}(\Sigma;r)=\int_{\Sigma\cap\{r\geq\epsilon\}}1\cdot d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}=\frac{c_{0}}{\epsilon^{n-1}}+\frac{c_{2}}{\epsilon^{n-3}}+\cdots+\frac{c_{n-3}}{\epsilon^{2}}+c_{n-1}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}+c_{n}+o(1).

The coefficient
c_{n}
is called the
renormalized area
of
\Sigma
relative to
r
, denoted by
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma;r)
. The authors in
[
RW99
]
further showed that when
n
is even,
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma;r)
is conformally invariant. That is,
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma;r)\equiv\mathcal{A}(\Sigma)
is independent of the choice of boundary defining functions. In
[
AM10
]
, Alexakis-Mazzeo proved that for a
C^{2}
-asymptotic surface
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
, if
\Sigma
meets
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}
orthogonally (which holds for all minimal surfaces), then
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma)
can be expressed as

\mathcal{A}(\Sigma)=-2\pi\chi(\Sigma)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\hat{A}_{\Sigma}|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

where
\chi(\Sigma)
is the Euler characteristic and
\hat{A}_{\Sigma}
is the trace-free second fundamental form of
\Sigma
. In
[
Ber22
]
, Bernstein proved an isoperimetric inequality for renormalized area. See also
[
Ngu21
, Theorem 8]
for the same result using a different approach. Recently, Tyrrell
[
Tyr22
]
derived a formula for renormalized area in
\mathbb{H}^{5}
.

We follow
[
BW22
]
to define a
relative entropy
of two minimal hypersurfaces
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
with
\partial_{\infty}\Sigma=\partial_{\infty}\Sigma^{\prime}
. Let
r
be any fixed boundary defining function. We consider

(1.4)

E^{(r)}_{rel}[\Sigma^{\prime},\Sigma]:=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\Big(\text{Vol}_{\epsilon}(\Sigma^{\prime};r)-\text{Vol}_{\epsilon}(\Sigma;r)\Big).

We will show that
E^{(r)}_{rel}[\Sigma^{\prime},\Sigma]
is independent of the choice of boundary defining functions, and that the relative entropy
E^{(r)}_{rel}[\Sigma^{\prime},\Sigma]
is finite and is equal to the difference of
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma;r)
and
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma^{\prime};r)
. It is then worth noting that, although in odd dimensions,
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma;r)
is in general dependent on the choice of boundary defining functions, the difference,
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma^{\prime};r)-\mathcal{A}(\Sigma;r)
, is conformally invariant.

The first result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Let
M
be a
C^{n,\alpha}
submanifold in
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and
r
be a boundary defining function for
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Let
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
be two minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
that are both
C^{n,\alpha}
-asymptotic to
M

(
see
\mathrm{Definition}

2.1
for the precise definition
)
. Then
E^{(r)}_{rel}[\Sigma^{\prime},\Sigma]
is a finite number and is independent of the choice of boundary defining function
r
. That is,
E^{(r)}_{rel}[\Sigma^{\prime},\Sigma]\equiv E_{rel}[\Sigma^{\prime},\Sigma]
. Moreover,

E_{rel}[\Sigma^{\prime},\Sigma]=\mathcal{A}(\Sigma^{\prime};r)-\mathcal{A}(\Sigma;r).

In Theorem
1.1
,
C^{n,\alpha}
-asymptotic regularity is to guarantee the existence of the expansions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
). However, when
n=2
, the authors in
[
AM10
]
used the Gauss-Codazzi equations and the Gauss-Bonnet formula to prove that for all
C^{2}
-asymptotic minimal surfaces, the renormalized area is well-defined. So, we conjecture that
C^{n}
-asymptotic regularity is sufficient to guarantee the existence of the renormalized area.

More generally, we shall prove that the relative entropy
E^{(r)}_{rel}[\Sigma^{\prime},\Sigma]
is well-defined for
\Sigma^{\prime}
in a “thin” neighborhood of
\Sigma
. This will be treated in Section 3. Here, by a “thin” neighborhood, we roughly mean that the volume of the domain bounded by
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
is finite near the ideal boundary. See Definition
2.2
for the precise statement.

It is also useful to study a weighted analog of the relative entropy for hypersurfaces in a thin neighborhood of
\Sigma
. This allows us to develop a variational theory for
E_{rel}
. In particular, a mountain pass theorem for
E_{rel}
is proved in
[
Yao
]
. We will discuss the weighted relative entropy in Section 4.

Finally, we prove a monotonicity formula for the relative entropy of a mean curvature flow.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T)}
be a mean curvature flow in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, so that each
\Sigma_{t}
is trapped between
\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}
. Assume that
\Sigma_{0}
is
C^{2}
-asymptotic to a closed submanifold
M\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Then, there exists an
E_{0}=E_{0}(\Sigma_{0},\Gamma^{\prime}_{-},\Gamma^{\prime}_{+})>0
, so that for any
0\leq t\leq s<T
, we have

E_{rel}[\Sigma_{t},\Sigma]\geq E_{rel}[\Sigma_{s},\Sigma]\geq-E_{0}.

2.
Some Preliminaries

Let
M
be an
(n-1)
-dimensional embedded (smooth) submanifold in
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. If we fix any point
p_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, then there is an isometry
i:\ \mathbb{H}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
, so that
i(p_{0})=(\textbf{0},1)
and
i^{*}g_{P}=g_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}
. Since all such isometries of
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
correspond to the Möbius transformations on
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}=\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
, we can assume that
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}
after a suitable transformation.

Definition 2.1
.

A complete embedded hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is called
C^{k,\alpha}
-
asymptotic
to
M
if
\Sigma\cup M
(denoted by
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Sigma\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
) is a
C^{k,\alpha}
hypersurface in
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
.
M
is also denoted by
\partial_{\infty}\Sigma
.

For convenience, all the unspecified notations below are understood in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
with metric
g_{P}
. Notations with a bar on the top are understood in
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\cup\{\infty\}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
with metric
r^{2}g_{P}
. For example, for a hypersurface
\Sigma
,
g_{\Sigma}
(resp.
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mug_{\Sigma}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
) is the Riemannian metric induced from
g_{P}
(resp.
r^{2}g_{P}
), and
\text{div}_{\Sigma}
(resp.
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\text{div}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\Sigma}
) is referred to the divergence taken with respect to
g_{\Sigma}
(resp.
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mug_{\Sigma}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
). To avoid misunderstanding, for a set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
,
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is referred to the topological closure in
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\cup\{\infty\}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, and the closure taken in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
with metric
g_{P}
is denoted by
\text{cl}(S)
. For any two vectors
X
and
Y
, we think of them as vectors in Euclidean space. So, the addition and substraction make sense. The vector norm in Euclidean space will be denoted by
|\cdot|
and that in hyperbolic space will be denoted by
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{H}}
. The inner product
\left\langle\textbf{X},\textbf{Y}\right\rangle
is taken with respect to
g_{P}
, and
\textbf{X}\cdot\textbf{Y}
is the inner product in Euclidean space.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a hypersurface
\Sigma
which is
C^{2}
-asymptotic to
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and meets
M
orthogonally, a bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
is called a
thin neighborhood of
\Sigma
, if there are constants
C,\epsilon>0
, such that
\Omega\cap\{y=y_{0}\}\subset\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{N}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{C_{1}y_{0}^{n+1}}(\Sigma)
for
0<y_{0}<\epsilon
. Here,
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{N}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\delta}(\Sigma)
is the
\delta
-tubular neighborhood of
\Sigma
considered in Euclidean topology.

Now, let us fix a minimal hypersurface
\Sigma
with
C^{2}
-asymptotic boundary
M
. Let
\Omega
be a thin neighborhood of
\Sigma
. For simplicity, We choose hypersurfaces
\Gamma^{\prime}_{\pm}
and open sets
U_{\Gamma^{\prime}_{\pm}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
satisfying
\partial U_{\Gamma^{\prime}_{\pm}}=\Gamma^{\prime}_{\pm}
and
U_{\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}}\backslash\text{cl}(U_{\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}})=\Omega
. In particular, we can choose
\Gamma^{\prime}_{\pm}
to be
C^{2}
-asymptotic and orthogonal to
M
. Then,
\Omega
is
quasi-convex
in the sense that for any two points
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
in
\Omega
, there is a path
\gamma
connecting them, so that the inequality

\text{Length}(\gamma)\leq C|\textbf{x}(p_{1})-\textbf{x}(p_{2})|

holds for some constant
C
independent of the choice of points. Here, the length is measured with respect to Euclidean metric, and
\textbf{x}(p_{i})
is the position vector. We fix this choice of
\Gamma^{\prime}_{\pm}
throughout the article. With
\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}
illustrated above, we define

\mathcal{C}(\Gamma^{\prime}_{-},\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}):=\{U:\,U\text{ is a Caccioppoli set}\text{ and }U_{\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}}\subset U\subset U_{\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}}\}.

We then discuss the space of admissible weights.

Definition 2.3
.

A function
\psi=\psi(p,\textbf{v})
over
\text{cl}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
is said to be admissible, if

•

\psi
and
\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\psi
are locally Lipschitz functions over
\text{cl}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
;

•

\psi(p,\textbf{v})=\psi(p,-\textbf{v})
;

•

\|\psi\|_{\mathfrak{X}}<\infty
,

where
\|\psi\|_{\mathfrak{X}}:=\|\psi\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\psi\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\psi\|_{\infty}+\|y\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\psi\|_{\infty}+\|y\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\psi\|_{\infty}
. The space of all admissible weights is denoted by
\mathfrak{X}
.

Since
\Omega
is quasi-convex, the sup-norm of the gradient is equivalent to the Lipschitz constant defined by difference quotients. Since
\psi(p,\textbf{v})=\psi(p,-\textbf{v})
, it can be viewed naturally as a function over the Grassman
n
-plane bundle of
\Omega
. It can also be checked that the space
\mathfrak{X}
is a Banach space and an algebra.

The main tool used in the discussion of the relative entropy is the divergence theorem applied to an appropriate vector field. Since
\Sigma
is
C^{2}
-asymptotic to
M
, we choose a small
\delta>0
, so that the normal projection
\Pi:\ \mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{N}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\delta}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Sigma\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\rightarrow\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Sigma\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is
C^{1}
. We fix a small
\epsilon
, so that
\text{cl}(\Omega)\cap\{y<\epsilon\}
is contained in
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{N}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\delta}(\Sigma)
. Then,

(2.1)

\textbf{X}(q)=\textbf{n}_{\Sigma}\circ\Pi(q)=y\big(\Pi(q)\big)\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\Sigma}\circ\Pi(q)

is a vector field that is well-defined in
\text{cl}(\Omega)\cap\{y<\epsilon\}
. Let
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu=\frac{1}{y}\textbf{X}
. This implies
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\text{div}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is bounded in
\Omega\cap\{y<\epsilon\}
. Let
X^{(n+1)}=\textbf{X}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}\cdot(\textbf{n}_{\Sigma}\circ\Pi)
be the (
n+1
)-th component of
X
. Then, the assumption that
\Sigma
meets
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
orthogonally gives

(2.2)

\displaystyle|X^{(n+1)}|=y\big(\Pi(q)\big)|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{(n+1)}_{\Sigma}\circ\Pi(q)|\leq Cy^{2}\big(\Pi(q)\big)\leq C^{\prime}y^{2}(q).

By a direct computation, we have

(2.3)

|\text{div}\textbf{X}|=|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\text{div}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(y\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)-\frac{n+1}{y}X^{(n+1)}|\leq y|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\text{div}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu|+\frac{C}{y}|X^{(n+1)}|\leq Cy.

Since
\Omega
is a thin neighborhood,

(2.4)

\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}(\Omega\cap\{y=y_{1}\})=\frac{1}{y_{1}^{n}}\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Omega\cap\{y=y_{1}\})\leq\frac{1}{y_{1}^{n}}\cdot Cy_{1}^{n+1}=Cy_{1}.

We shall also consider a family of cut-off functions. Let
\phi_{t_{1},\delta}(t)
be a smooth cut-off function that is identically
1
when
t\geq t_{1}
, and vanishes for
t\leq t_{1}-\delta
. We can further require that
|\phi^{\prime}_{t_{1},\delta}|\leq\frac{C}{\delta}
, where
C
is independent of
\delta
and
t_{1}
. Let
\phi_{t_{1},t_{2},\delta}:=\phi_{t_{1},\delta}-\phi_{t_{2}+\delta,\delta}
, for any
t_{1}<t_{2}
with
0<\frac{t_{1}}{2}<t_{1}-\delta
and
t_{2}+\delta<2t_{2}<\epsilon
. Fix a boundary defining function
r
. Define
\phi^{(r)}_{t_{1},\delta}=\phi_{t_{1},\delta}\circ r
and
\phi^{(r)}_{t_{1},t_{2},\delta}=\phi_{t_{1},t_{2},\delta}\circ r
. It is readily checked that
\frac{1}{C}\leq|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mur|\leq C
near
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
for some constant
C>0
. Since
\Sigma
meets
M
orthogonally and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mur
is perpendicular to
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
on
M
, we have
\frac{1}{2C}\leq|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Sigma\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}r|\leq 2C
near
M
.

By choosing
\epsilon
sufficiently small, we can assume that in
\Omega\cap\{r\leq\epsilon\}
,
\frac{y}{C}\leq r(\textbf{x},y)\leq Cy
. Hence, (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) imply that
|\text{div}\textbf{X}|\leq Ct_{1}
and
|\textbf{X}^{(n+1)}|\leq Ct_{1}^{2}
on
\Omega\cap\{r=t_{1}\}
with
t_{1}\leq\epsilon
. Since
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mur
is perpendicular to
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, we also have
\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}(\Omega\cap\{r=t_{1}\})\leq Ct_{1}
on
\Omega\cap\{r=t_{1}\}
.

Let
U_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{C}(\Gamma^{\prime}_{-},\Gamma^{\prime}_{+})
with
\Gamma=\partial^{*}U_{\Gamma}
being the reduced boundary of
U_{\Gamma}
. Consider

E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi^{(r)}_{t_{1},\delta}]:=\int_{\Gamma}\phi^{(r)}_{t_{1},\delta}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}-\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{(r)}_{t_{1},\delta}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}.

Letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
, it follows from the dominated convergence theorem that

E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\chi_{\{r\geq t_{1}\}}]=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi^{(r)}_{t_{1},\delta}]=\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}(\Gamma\cap\{r\geq t_{1}\})-\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}(\Sigma\cap\{r\geq t_{1}\}).

3.
The Existence of Relative Entropy

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. As a first step, we prove that the relative entropy is well-defined for all the reduced boundaries of Caccioppoli sets in
\mathcal{C}(\Gamma^{\prime}_{-},\Gamma^{\prime}_{+})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
U\in\mathcal{C}(\Gamma^{\prime}_{-},\Gamma^{\prime}_{+})
and any boundary defining function
r
,

E^{(r)}_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma]=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\big(\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}(\partial^{*}U\cap\{r\geq\epsilon\})-\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}(\Sigma\cap\{r\geq\epsilon\})\big)

exists
(\in(-\infty,\infty])
and
E^{(r)}_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma]\equiv E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma]
is independent of
r
. Moreover,
E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma]\geq-E_{0}
for
E_{0}=E_{0}(\Sigma,\Omega)
.

Proof.

Since
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Sigma\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is an embedded hypersurface in
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, it is orientable. Thus, we can choose an open set
U_{\Sigma}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
satisfying
\Sigma=\text{cl}(U_{\Sigma})\backslash U_{\Sigma}
and
U_{\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}}\subset U_{\Sigma}\subset U_{\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}}
.

Fix
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
and
\delta
with
0<\frac{t_{1}}{2}<t_{1}-\delta<t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{2}+\delta<2t_{2}<\epsilon
. Combining the computation in Section 2 and applying the divergence theorem to the vector field
X
, we get

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}U}\phi^{(r)}_{t_{1},t_{2},\delta}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}-\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{(r)}_{t_{1},t_{2},\delta}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}\geq\int_{\partial^{*}U}\left\langle\phi^{(r)}_{t_{1},t_{2},\delta}\textbf{X},\textbf{n}_{\partial^{*}U}\right\rangle d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}-\int_{\Sigma}\left\langle\phi^{(r)}_{t_{1},t_{2},\delta}\textbf{X},\textbf{n}_{\Sigma}\right\rangle d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}

(3.2)

(4.1)

\displaystyle E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;\psi]:=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;\psi\chi_{\{y\geq\epsilon\}}]=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\Big(\int_{\partial^{*}U\cap\{y\geq\epsilon\}}\psi d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}-\int_{\Sigma\cap\{y\geq\epsilon\}}\psi d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}\Big)

whenever it exists. Observe that (

Theorem 4.1

exists. If we denote it by

, then

(4.2)

|E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;\psi]|\leq C(|E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma]|+1)\|\psi\|_{\mathfrak{X}},

where the constant
C
only depends on
\Sigma
and
\Omega
. Moreover, for
0<\frac{y_{1}}{2}<y_{1}-\delta<y_{1}<y_{2}<y_{2}+\delta<2y_{2}<\epsilon
,

(4.3)

|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}\psi]|\leq C(|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]|+y_{2})\|\psi\|_{\mathfrak{X}}.

where
\epsilon
is made so small that the vector field
\mathbf{X}
defined in
(\ref{39})
exists.

The arguments in
[
BW22
, Section 4]
can be carried out in our setting. In
[
BW22
]
, the authors first proved that if the weights are quadratic forms, then the weighted relative entropy is well-defined. Then, for a general weight, they showed that by subtracting off an appropriate quadratic form from it, the error term has a reasonably good estimate. We follow the same scheme.

We first prove a weak version of Theorem
4.1
that will be used later on.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
U_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{C}(\Gamma^{\prime}_{-},\Gamma^{\prime}_{+})
with
\Gamma=\partial^{*}U_{\Gamma}
. If
\psi
is a locally Lipschitz function over
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
with
\|\psi\|_{\infty}+\|y\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\psi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, then for
0<\frac{y_{1}}{2}<y_{1}-\delta<y_{1}<y_{2}<y_{2}+\delta<2y_{2}<\epsilon
,

(4.4)

\big|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\psi\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]\big|\leq\big|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]\big|+Cy_{2},

where
\epsilon
is the same as in Theorem
4.1
.

Proof.

We first assume
\psi
is nonnegative. Then, similar to the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\psi\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}-\int_{\Sigma}\psi\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Omega}|\text{div}\left(\psi\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}\textbf{X}\right)|d\mathcal{H}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Omega}\psi\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}|\text{div}\textbf{X}|+\psi|\left\langle\nabla\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta},\textbf{X}\right\rangle|+\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}|\left\langle\nabla\psi,\textbf{X}\right\rangle|d\mathcal{H}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}.

Direct computations show that
\psi\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}|\text{div}\textbf{X}|\leq C\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}
and

\displaystyle|\left\langle\nabla\psi,\textbf{X}\right\rangle|=|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\psi\cdot\textbf{X}|=|\big(y\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\psi\big)\cdot\big(\frac{\textbf{X}}{y}\big)|\leq C,

Thus, applying the divergence theorem to the vector field
X
in a similar way as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\psi\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}-\int_{\Sigma}\psi\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-C\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}\Big(\Omega\cap\{y_{1}-\delta\leq y\leq y_{2}+\delta\}\Big)-C\int_{\Omega\cap\big(\{y_{1}-\delta\leq y\leq y_{1}\}\cup\{y_{2}\leq y\leq y_{2}+\delta\}\big)}\frac{1}{\delta}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-Cy_{2}.

This gives
E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\psi\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]\geq-Cy_{2}
. If we replace
\psi
by
1-\psi
and notice that
1-\psi\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\psi\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]=E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]-E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;(1-\psi)\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]\leq E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]+Cy_{2}.

For a general
\psi\in\mathfrak{X}
with
\|\psi\|_{\mathfrak{X}}\leq 1
, we have
\frac{1+\psi}{2}\geq 0
and
\|\frac{1+\psi}{2}\|_{\mathfrak{X}}\leq 1
. So, (
4.4
) follows if we apply the preceding argument to
\frac{1+\psi}{2}
.
∎

It is then immediate from the next proposition that Theorem
4.1
holds for weights
\psi(p,\textbf{v})\in\mathfrak{X}
that is independent of
v
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
U_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{C}(\Gamma^{\prime}_{-},\Gamma^{\prime}_{+})
with
\Gamma=\partial^{*}U_{\Gamma}
. Assume that
E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma]
is finite and that
\psi
is a locally Lipschitz function over
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
with
\|\psi\|_{w}:=\|\psi\|_{\infty}+\|y\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\psi\|_{\infty}<\infty
. Then,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\big|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma,\psi]\big|\leq C\Big(\big|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma]\big|+1\Big)\|\psi\|_{w},

where
C
is independent of
\Gamma=\partial^{*}U
and
\psi
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we assume that
\|\psi\|_{w}=1
if
\psi
is nonzero. Otherwise, the conclusion follows trivially. In Lemma
4.2
, by letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
, we get

(4.6)

\displaystyle\Big|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\psi\chi_{\{y\geq y_{1}\}}]-E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\psi\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}]\Big|\leq Cy_{2}+\Big|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\chi_{\{y\geq y_{1}\}}]-E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}]\Big|.

Since
E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma]=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\chi_{\{y\geq\epsilon\}}]
exists and is a finite number, the right hand side of (
4.6
) can be made arbitrarily small. So, Cauchy’s criterion implies that

\displaystyle E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\psi]=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\psi\chi_{\{y\geq\epsilon\}}]

is well-defined and is finite valued. Taking
r=r_{1}
in (
3.7
), we see that

E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\chi_{\{y\geq y_{1}\}}]\geq E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}]-Cy_{2}

holds for
y_{1}<y_{2}<\epsilon
. We choose
y_{1}<\epsilon
so small that
\big|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\chi_{\{y\geq y_{1}\}}]\big|\leq 2\big|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma]\big|
. Note that
y_{1}
may depend on
\Gamma
. However, for any
y_{2}<\epsilon
,

E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}]\leq Cy_{2}+E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\chi_{\{y\geq y_{1}\}}]\leq Cy_{2}+2\big|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma]\big|.

Letting
y_{1}\to 0
in (
4.6
), we get

(4.7)

\displaystyle\big|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\psi]\big|

\displaystyle\leq Cy_{2}+\Big|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma]-E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}]\Big|+|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\psi\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}]|

\displaystyle\leq Cy_{2}+3|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma]|+|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\psi\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}]|.

Notice that

(4.8)

\displaystyle|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\psi\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}]|=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Gamma\cap\{y\geq y_{2}\}}\psi d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}-\int_{\Sigma\cap\{y\geq y_{2}\}}\psi d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}\Big|

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\Big\{\big|\int_{\Gamma\cap\{y\geq y_{2}\}}\psi d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}\big|,\big|\int_{\Sigma\cap\{y\geq y_{2}\}}\psi d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}\big|\Big\}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}(\Sigma\cap\{y\geq y_{2}\})+|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}|

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}(\Sigma\cap\{y\geq y_{2}\})+2|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma]|.

Since
y_{2}<\epsilon
is fixed and
\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}(\Sigma\cap\{y\geq y_{2}\})<\infty
, combining (
4.7
)-(
4.11
) gives the desired upper bound. Since
y_{2}
is independent of
\Gamma
, so is the constant
C
in (
4.5
).
∎

Now, we follow
[
BW22
]
to establish the estimate for quadratic forms.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathbf{Y}_{1}
and
\mathbf{Y}_{2}
be two vector fields in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
with

\|\mathbf{Y}_{i}\|_{\infty}+\|y\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\mathbf{Y}_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 1.

Then, for
0<\frac{y_{1}}{2}<y_{1}-\delta<y_{1}<y_{2}<y_{2}+\delta<2y_{2}<\epsilon
, we have

(4.12)

|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}(\mathbf{Y}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{v})(\mathbf{Y}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{v})]|\leq C(y_{2}+|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]|).

Proof.

Let
Y
be a locally Lipschitz vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
with
\|\textbf{Y}\|_{\infty}+\|y\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\textbf{Y}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. We first show that Lemma
4.4
holds for
\textbf{Y}_{1}=\textbf{Y}_{2}=\textbf{Y}
. By a direct computation,

|(\textbf{Y}\cdot\textbf{X})\textbf{Y}^{(n+1)}|\leq Cy,\ \left|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\text{div}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Big((\textbf{Y}\cdot\textbf{X})\textbf{Y}\Big)\right|\leq C.

Applying the divergence theorem to the vector field
(\textbf{X}\cdot\textbf{Y})\textbf{Y}
in a similar way as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}(\textbf{Y}\cdot\textbf{X})\left\langle\textbf{Y},\textbf{n}_{\Gamma}\right\rangle d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}-\int_{\Sigma}\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}(\textbf{Y}\cdot\textbf{X})\left\langle\textbf{Y},\textbf{n}_{\Sigma}\right\rangle d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\geq-2\int_{\Omega}\left|\text{div}\Big(\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}(\textbf{X}\cdot\textbf{Y})\textbf{Y}\Big)\right|d\mathcal{H}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\geq-Cy_{2}.

Using the inequality
(\textbf{Y}\cdot\textbf{X})\left\langle\textbf{Y},\textbf{n}_{\Gamma}\right\rangle=(\textbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)(\textbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\Gamma})\leq\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\Gamma})^{2}
and noticing that
\textbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu=\textbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\Gamma}
over
\Sigma\cap\{y\leq\epsilon\}
, one gets

(4.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}(\textbf{Y}\cdot\textbf{X})\left\langle\textbf{Y},\textbf{n}_{\Gamma}\right\rangle d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}-\int_{\Sigma}\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}(\textbf{Y}\cdot\textbf{X})\left\langle\textbf{Y},\textbf{n}_{\Sigma}\right\rangle d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}

(4.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\Gamma})^{2}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}-\int_{\Sigma}\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\Sigma})^{2}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}

(4.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\frac{1}{2}\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}(\textbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)^{2}]+E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\frac{1}{2}\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}(\textbf{Y}\cdot\textbf{v})^{2}].

Notice that
\frac{1}{2}(\textbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)^{2}
satisfies

\|(\textbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)^{2}\|_{\infty}+\|y\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\big(\textbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\big)^{2}\|_{\infty}\leq C.

So, applying Lemma
4.2
to the first term in (
4.15
), it is bounded by
Cy_{2}+C|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]|
. Hence,

(4.16)

E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}(\textbf{Y}\cdot\textbf{v})^{2}]\geq-Cy_{2}-C|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]|.

If we are given two vector fields
\textbf{Y}_{1}
and
\textbf{Y}_{2}
with

\displaystyle\|\textbf{Y}_{i}\|_{\infty}+\|y\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\textbf{Y}_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 1\text{ for }i=1,2,

then, using (
4.16
) we have

\displaystyle E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}\big((\textbf{Y}_{1}+\textbf{Y}_{2})\cdot\textbf{v}\big)^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{2}E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}(\textbf{Y}_{1}\cdot\textbf{v})^{2}]-4E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}\big(\frac{\textbf{Y}_{1}-\textbf{Y}_{2}}{2}\cdot\textbf{v}\big)^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{2}E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}(\textbf{Y}_{1}\cdot\textbf{v})^{2}]+4\Big(Cy_{2}+C|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]|\Big).

Now, assume
\textbf{Y}_{i}
(
i=1,\cdots,k
) are vector fields with
\|\textbf{Y}_{i}\|_{\infty}+\|y\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\textbf{Y}_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. By induction, it is immediate that

(4.17)

\displaystyle E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}\big((\sum_{i=1}^{k}\textbf{Y}_{i})\cdot\textbf{v}\big)^{2}]\leq 2^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}\big(\textbf{Y}_{i}\cdot\textbf{v}\big)^{2}]+C_{k}(|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]|+y_{2}).

If we write
\textbf{Y}=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}Y_{i}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{i}
, then

\big((Y_{i}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{i})\cdot\textbf{v}\big)^{2}=Y_{i}^{2}-\sum_{m\neq i}\big((Y_{i}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{m})\cdot\textbf{v}\big)^{2}.

If we take
\psi=Y_{i}^{2}
in Lemma
4.2
and use (
4.16
) with
(\textbf{Y}\cdot\textbf{v})^{2}
replaced by
((Y_{i}\textbf{e}_{m})\cdot\textbf{v})^{2}
, then

\displaystyle E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}\big((Y_{i}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{i})\cdot\textbf{v}\big)^{2}]\leq C(y_{2}+|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]|).

Together with (
4.17
), we get

\displaystyle E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}\big(\textbf{Y}\cdot\textbf{v}\big)^{2}]=E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}\big((\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}Y_{i}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{i})\cdot\textbf{v}\big)^{2}]\leq C(y_{2}+|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]|).

Combining this with (
4.16
), we see that

\displaystyle\big|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}\big(\textbf{Y}\cdot\textbf{v}\big)^{2}]\big|\leq C(y_{2}+\big|E_{rel}[\Gamma,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]\big|).

Then, (
4.12
) follows from the following observation:

(\textbf{Y}_{1}\cdot\textbf{v})(\textbf{Y}_{2}\cdot\textbf{v})=\left(\frac{\textbf{Y}_{1}+\textbf{Y}_{2}}{2}\cdot\textbf{v}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{\textbf{Y}_{1}-\textbf{Y}_{2}}{2}\cdot\textbf{v}\right)^{2}.

∎

Now, we discuss the quadratic approximation for a general weight
\psi\in\mathfrak{X}
and establish the estimate for the error term. Fix a small
\epsilon>0
. Let
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p,\textbf{v}):=\psi(p,\textbf{v})-(1-\phi_{\frac{\epsilon}{2},\epsilon})(\textbf{Y}_{\psi}(p)\cdot\textbf{v})(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p)\cdot\textbf{v})
, where
\textbf{Y}_{\psi}(p):=(\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\psi)(p,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p))
. Notice that if
\epsilon
is sufficiently small, then
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is well-defined. Fix a point
p
in
\{y<\frac{\epsilon}{2}\}
. Notice that
\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p,\textbf{v})\big|_{\textbf{v}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p)}=0
. Let
\gamma(t)
(
t\in[0,\ell]
) be a geodesic on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
connecting
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p)
and
v
, where
\ell
is the length of
\gamma
. If we let
\varphi(t)=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p,\gamma(t))
, then

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(0)=\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\big|_{\textbf{v}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p)}\cdot\gamma^{\prime}(0)=0.

Hence,

(4.18)

\displaystyle|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p,\textbf{v})-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p))|=

\displaystyle|\varphi(\ell)-\varphi(0)|=|\int_{0}^{\ell}\varphi^{\prime}(t)dt|=|\int_{0}^{\ell}\int_{0}^{t}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(s)dsdt|

(4.19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\int_{0}^{\ell}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\psi(\gamma^{\prime}(s),\gamma^{\prime}(s))dsdt|

(4.20)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\psi\|_{\infty}\ell^{2}=\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\psi\|_{\infty}d^{2}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\textbf{v},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p)).

Since
\psi
is an even function in
v
, so is
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. Thus,

(4.21)

\displaystyle|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p,\textbf{v})-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p))|=|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p,-\textbf{v})-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p))|\leq\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\psi\|_{\infty}d^{2}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(-\textbf{v},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p)).

Combining (
4.20
) and (
4.21
), we get

\displaystyle|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p,\textbf{v})-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p))|\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\psi\|_{\infty}\min\Big\{d^{2}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\textbf{v},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p)),d^{2}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(-\textbf{v},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{X}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(p))\Big\}

(4.22)

\displaystyle\leq

. The lemma below shows that the corresponding relative entropy also converges. By the same reasoning, we have

E_{rel}[\Sigma_{s},\Sigma;\eta_{\epsilon}f]\to E_{rel}[\Sigma_{s},\Sigma;f],\ E_{rel}[\Sigma_{t},\Sigma;\eta_{\epsilon}f]\to E_{rel}[\Sigma_{t},\Sigma;f]

as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
. The monotone convergence theorem gives

\int_{t}^{s}dr\int_{\Sigma_{r}}\eta_{\epsilon}f\left\langle\textbf{H},\textbf{H}\right\rangle d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}\to\int_{t}^{s}dr\int_{\Sigma_{r}}f\left\langle\textbf{H},\textbf{H}\right\rangle d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Putting them together, we get (
5.7
).
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

If
f
and
f_{i}
are functions in
\mathfrak{X}
with uniformly bounded
\mathfrak{X}
-norms and
f_{i}
converges pointwise to
f
, then we have
E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;f]=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;f_{i}]
for any
U\in\mathcal{C}(\Gamma^{\prime}_{-},\Gamma^{\prime}_{+})
with
E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma]<\infty
.

Proof.

Assume
\|f\|_{\mathfrak{X}}\leq C
and
\|f_{i}\|_{\mathfrak{X}}\leq C
for all
i
. For any
y_{1},y_{2}
small with
0<\frac{y_{1}}{2}<y_{1}-\delta<y_{1}<y_{2}<y_{2}+\delta<2y_{2}<\epsilon
, where
\epsilon
is the same as in Theorem
4.1
,

\displaystyle|E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;(f_{i}-f)\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]|

\displaystyle\leq C(|E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]|+y_{2})\|f_{i}-f\|_{\mathfrak{X}}

\displaystyle\leq C(|E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;\phi_{y_{1},y_{2},\delta}]|+y_{2}).

Letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
and
y_{1}\to 0^{+}
,

\displaystyle|E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;(f_{i}-f)]-E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;(f_{i}-f)\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}]|

\displaystyle\leq C(|E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma]-E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}]|+y_{2}).

This implies

\displaystyle\big|E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;(f_{i}-f)]\big|\leq

\displaystyle\big|E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;(f_{i}-f)\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}]\big|

\displaystyle+C(|E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma]-E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}]|+y_{2}).

For any
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, we fix
y_{2}<\min\{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}\}
so small that
|E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma]-E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}]|<\epsilon^{\prime}
. By the dominated convergence theorem, for any fix
y_{2}
,
\big|E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;(f_{i}-f)\chi_{\{y\geq y_{2}\}}]\big|<\epsilon^{\prime}
for
i
sufficiently large. So, putting these together, we see that for any
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
,
\big|E_{rel}[\partial^{*}U,\Sigma;(f_{i}-f)]\big|\leq C\epsilon^{\prime}
for
i
sufficienly large. This concludes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
A computation for geodesics in hyperbolic place

We verify the relative entropy is conformally invariant for
n=1
by computing out the explicit formula of the relative entropy for geodesics in hyperbolic plane. Specifically, consider the upper half space model, (
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},\frac{1}{y^{2}}(dx\otimes dx+dy\otimes dy)
), of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Let
a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}<a_{4}
be four points on the real line. Let
\gamma_{1}
be the union of the geodesic joining
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
(denoted by
\gamma_{1,1}
) and the geodesic joining
a_{3}
and
a_{4}
(denoted by
\gamma_{1,2}
). Let
\gamma_{2}
be the union of the geodesic joining
a_{1}
and
a_{3}
(denoted by
\gamma_{2,1}
) and the geodesic joining
a_{2}
and
a_{4}
(denoted by
\gamma_{2,2}
). We will give an explicit formula for
E_{rel}[\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}]
.

It is easy to check that
\gamma_{1,1}
is the upper half circle defined implicitly by the equation

(A.1)

y^{2}+(x-a_{1})(x-a_{2})=0,\ y\geq 0.

Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}(\gamma_{1,1}\cap\{y\geq\epsilon\})=\int_{\gamma_{1,1}\cap\{y\geq\epsilon\}}\frac{1}{y}d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}=\int_{x_{1}(\epsilon)}^{x_{2}(\epsilon)}\frac{1}{y}\sqrt{1+\big(y^{\prime}(x)\big)^{2}}dx,

where
y
is thought of as a function in
x
and
x_{1}(\epsilon)<x_{2}(\epsilon)
are the roots to the equation
\epsilon^{2}+(x-a_{1})(x-a_{2})=0
. Differentiating (
A.1
), we get
yy^{\prime}+(x-\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2})=0
. Hence,

\sqrt{1+\big(y^{\prime}(x)\big)^{2}}=\frac{1}{y}\sqrt{y^{2}+\left(x-\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}=\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{2y}.

As a result,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}(\gamma_{1,1}\cap\{y\geq\epsilon\})=\int_{x_{1}(\epsilon)}^{x_{2}(\epsilon)}\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{2y^{2}}dx=\int_{x_{1}(\epsilon)}^{x_{2}(\epsilon)}\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{2(a_{2}-x)(x-a_{1})}dx.

Notice that
x_{i}(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2}\pm\sqrt{(a_{2}-a_{1})^{2}-4\epsilon^{2}})
. So,

\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}(\gamma_{1,1}\cap\{y\geq\epsilon\})=\ln\left(\frac{x_{2}(\epsilon)-a_{1}}{x_{1}(\epsilon)-a_{1}}\right)=2\ln\left(\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}+\sqrt{(a_{2}-a_{1})^{2}-4\epsilon^{2}}}{2\epsilon}\right).

Then, we do similar computations for
\gamma_{1,2}
,
\gamma_{2,1}
and
\gamma_{2,2}
. As explained in Section 4, we can pick a boundary defining function
r_{1}
satisfying
r_{1}(\textbf{x},y)=y
in a compact subset of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
containing
\Omega
. So,

\displaystyle E_{rel}[\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}]

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}(\gamma_{1,i}\cap\{y\geq\epsilon\})-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}(\gamma_{2,i}\cap\{y\geq\epsilon\})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}2\ln\left(\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1}+\sqrt{(a_{2}-a_{1})^{2}-4\epsilon^{2}})(a_{4}-a_{3}+\sqrt{(a_{4}-a_{3})^{2}-4\epsilon^{2}})}{(a_{3}-a_{1}+\sqrt{(a_{3}-a_{1})^{2}-4\epsilon^{2}})(a_{4}-a_{2}+\sqrt{(a_{4}-a_{2})^{2}-4\epsilon^{2}})}\right)

\displaystyle=2\ln\left(\frac{(a_{2}-a_{1})(a_{4}-a_{3})}{(a_{3}-a_{1})(a_{4}-a_{2})}\right)=2\ln[a_{1},a_{4};a_{2},a_{3}],

where
[a_{1},a_{4};a_{2},a_{3}]
is the cross ratio. It is preserved under the Möbius transformations of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Let
\gamma_{3,1}
be geodesic joining
a_{1}
and
a_{3}
, and let
\gamma_{3,2}
be the geodesic joining
a_{2}
and
a_{4}
. Let
\gamma_{3}=\gamma_{3,1}\sqcup\gamma_{3,2}
. Then,
\gamma_{3}
is stationary in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
but has a singular point. Consider
E_{rel}[\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}]
with
1\leq i,j\leq 3
and
i\neq j
. This gives
6
different values of
E_{rel}
in general. So, the relative entropy gives every permutation of the cross ratio of
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
a_{3}
and
a_{4}
.

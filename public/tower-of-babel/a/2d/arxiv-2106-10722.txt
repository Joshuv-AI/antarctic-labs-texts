Ricci solitons and certain related metrics on 3-dimensional trans-Sasakian manifold The first author is the corresponding author and this work was financially supported by UGC Senior Research Fellowship of India, Sr. No. 2061540940. Ref. No:21/06/2015(i)EU-V.
Sumanjit Sarkar, Santu Dey, Arindam Bhattacharyya

Abstract
. In this paper we study certain types of metrics such as Ricci soliton, ∗ * -conformal Ricci soliton in 3-dimensional trans-Sasakian manifold. First we have shown that a 3-dimensional trans-Sasakian manifold of type ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) admits a Ricci soliton where the covariant derivative of potential vector field in the direction of unit vector field ξ \xi is orthogonal to ξ \xi . It is also shown that if the structure functions satisfy α 2 = β 2 \alpha^{2}=\beta^{2} then the covariant derivative of the potential vector field in the direction of ξ \xi is a constant multiple of ξ \xi . Further, we have evolved the nature of scalar curvature when the manifold satisfies ∗ * -conformal Ricci soliton of type ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) , provided α ≠ 0 \alpha\neq 0 . Finally, we present an example to verify our findings.

Introduction

Richard S. Hamilton introduced the concept of Ricci flow (for details see
[
15
]

\frac{\partial g(t)}{\partial t}=-2S(g(t))

(1.1)

where

is a one-parameter family of metrices on M.

A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is called a Ricci soliton if there exists a vector field
V
and a constant
\lambda
such that the following equation holds,

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{V}g+S+\lambda g=0

(1.2)

where
\mathcal{L}_{V}
denotes Lie derivative along the direction
V
and
\lambda
is a non-zero constant. The vector field
V
is called potential vector field and
\lambda
is called soliton constant. Ricci soliton which is a natural extension of Einstein manifold is a self-similar solution of Ricci flow. The potential vector field
V
and soliton constant
\lambda
play vital roles while determining the nature of the soliton. A soliton is said to be shrinking, steady or expanding according as
\lambda<0
,
\lambda=0
or
\lambda>0
. Now if
V
is Killing then the Ricci soliton reduces to Einstein manifold. Compact Ricci solitons are the fixed points of the Ricci flow (
1.1
) projected from the space of metrics onto its quotient modulo diffeomorphisms and scalings, and often arise as blow-up limits for the Ricci flow on compact manifolds.

4.

In this section we provide an example to verify our outcomes.

Example 4.1
.

We consider the manifold as

, where
(x,y,z)

\displaystyle e_{1}

\displaystyle=e^{2z}\frac{\partial}{\partial x},

\displaystyle e_{2}

\displaystyle=e^{2z}\frac{\partial}{\partial y},

\displaystyle e_{3}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}

\displaystyle\eta(e_{1})=\eta(e_{2})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\eta(e_{3})

\displaystyle=1.

\displaystyle\phi e_{1}

\displaystyle=e_{2},

\displaystyle\phi e_{2}

\displaystyle=-e_{1},

\displaystyle\phi e_{3}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\phi^{2}(Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-Z+\eta(Z)e_{3},

\displaystyle g(\phi Z,\phi W)

\displaystyle g(Z,W)-\eta(Z)\eta(W)

\displaystyle[e_{1},e_{2}]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle[e_{1},e_{3}]

\displaystyle g(Y,[X,Z])+g(Z,[X,Y]),

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}

\displaystyle=-2e_{1},

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle R(e_{1},e_{2})e_{2}

\displaystyle R(e_{1},e_{2})e_{3}

\displaystyle R(e_{1},e_{3})e_{2}

\displaystyle R(e_{1},e_{3})e_{3}

\displaystyle=-4e_{1},

\displaystyle R(e_{2},e_{3})e_{1}

Symplectic Extra-gradient Type Method for Solving General Non-monotone Inclusion Problem Thanks: This work was partially supported by grant 12288201 from NSF of China.
Ya-xiang Yuan, Yi Zhang

Abstract
In recent years, accelerated extra-gradient methods have attracted much attention by researchers, for solving monotone inclusion problems. A limitation of most current accelerated extra-gradient methods lies in their direct utilization of the initial point, which can potentially decelerate numerical convergence rate. In this work, we present a new accelerated extra-gradient method, by utilizing the symplectic acceleration technique. We establish the inverse of quadratic convergence rate by employing the Lyapunov function technique. Also, we demonstrate a faster inverse of quadratic convergence rate alongside its weak convergence property under stronger assumptions. To improve practical efficiency, we introduce a line search technique for our symplectic extra-gradient method. Theoretically, we prove the convergence of the symplectic extra-gradient method with line search. Numerical tests show that this adaptation exhibits faster convergence rates in practice compared to several existing extra-gradient type methods.

Preliminaries

2.1
Basic Concepts

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real Hilbert space, and let
T:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is a set-valued operator. The operator
T
is said to be
\rho-
comonotone
if

\left\langle u-v,x-y\right\rangle\geqslant\rho\left\|u-v\right\|^{2},\quad\forall x,\ y\in\mathcal{H},\ u\in T(x),\ v\in T(y).

When
\rho=0
, the concept of
0-
comonotone coincide with the concept of monotone. If
\rho>0
,
\rho-
comonotone is the same as
\rho-
cocoercive. A monotone operator
G
is
maximally
if the graph of
G
is not a proper subset of the graph of another monotone operator. The Minty surjectivity theorem
[
18
]
shows that a monotone operator
G
is maximal if and only if the domain of resolvent
J_{G}=(I+G)^{-1}
of
G
is
\mathcal{H}
. Also, a continuous and monotone single-value operator is a maximally monotone operator. For further theories on comonotone operator, we refer to
[
2
]
.

2.2
Extra-gradient Method and Extra-gradient+ Method

The extra-gradient method for solving zero-point of
L-
Lipschitz continuous and monotone operator
F
, i. e. solving

0=F(z),

is given as follows:

\begin{split}z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}&=z_{k}-sF(z_{k}),\\
z_{k+1}&=z_{k}-sF(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}).\end{split}

(2)

In
[
15
]
and
[
32
]
, the proof of convergence results of EG method is based on the following inequality:

\left\|z_{k+1}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}\leqslant\left\|z_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}-(1-s^{2}L^{2})\left\|F(z_{k})\right\|^{2},\quad\forall 0=F(z^{*}).

By summing the above inequality respect to
k
, one can easily show that the ergodic convergence rate of the EG method on
\left\|F(z_{k})\right\|^{2}
is
O(1/k)
when
0<s<\dfrac{1}{L}
. The result that the sequence
\{z_{k}\}
converges weakly to a zero-point of
F
relies on the following propositions.

Proposition 1

(Lemma 2.47 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\{z_{k}\}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{H}
and let
C
be a nonempty subset of
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that, for every
z\in C
,
\left\|z_{k}-z\right\|
converges and that
every weak sequential cluster point of
\{z_{k}\}
belongs to
C
. Then
\{z_{k}\}
converges weakly to a point in
C
.

Proposition 2

(Proposition 20.38 (ii) in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
T:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
be a maximally monotone operator,
\{z_{k}\}
and
\{v_{k}\}
be the sequences in
\mathcal{H}
such that
v_{k}\in T(z_{k})
. If
z_{k}\rightharpoonup z
and
v_{k}\to v
. Then
v\in T(z)
.

The details of the proof of weak convergence property of the EG method can be found in
[
22
]
. The EG+ method proposed in
[
5
]
for solving zero-point of
L-
Lipschitz continuous and
\rho-
comonotone operator
F
is given as follows:

\begin{split}z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}&=z_{k}-\frac{s}{\beta}F(z_{k}),\\
z_{k+1}&=z_{k}-sF(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}).\end{split}

(3)

Similar to the EG method,
[
5
]
shows that if
\beta=\dfrac{1}{2},s=\dfrac{1}{2L}
,
\rho>-\dfrac{1}{8L}
, then we have

\left\|z_{k+1}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}\leqslant\left\|z_{k}-z^{*}\right\|-\frac{1}{4L}\left(\frac{1}{4L}+2\rho\right)\left\|F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2},\quad\forall 0=F(z^{*}).

By using the above inequality respect to
k
, one can prove the ergodic convergence rate of the EG+ method.

2.3
Anchor Acceleration

Since
[
20
]
suggested that the sequence generated by the EG method is closed to the sequence generated by the proximal point algorithm, recent study of acceleration form of the EG method is based on existing acceleration technique for proximal point algorithm. Among most acceleration form of proximal point algorithm, the Halpern’s iteration
[
10
]
is one of the most wildly studied acceleration technique. The Halpern’s iteration for solving zero-point of maximally monotone operator
F
is

z_{k+1}=\alpha_{k}z_{0}+(1-\alpha_{k})J_{F}(z_{k}).

(4)

Replacing the proximal point term by the EG step, we obtain the extra anchored gradient algorithm
[
37
]
:

\begin{split}z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}&=\alpha_{k}z_{0}+(1-\alpha_{k})z_{k}-\beta_{k}F(z_{k}),\\
z_{k+1}&=\alpha_{k}z_{0}+(1-\alpha_{k})z_{k}-\beta_{k}F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}).\end{split}

(5)

In
[
37
]
, (
5
) was proven to be
O(1/k^{2})
convergence rate if
F
is an
L-
Lipschitz continuous and monotone operator and the parameters in (
5
) satisfy several assumptions. Combining the EG+ method and (
5
),
[
16
]
gave the fast EG (FEG) method as follows:

\begin{split}z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}&=\alpha_{k}z_{0}+(1-\alpha_{k})z_{k}-(1-\alpha_{k})\left(\frac{1}{L}+2\rho\right)F(z_{k}),\\
z_{k+1}&=\alpha_{k}z_{0}+(1-\alpha_{k})z_{k}-\frac{1}{L}F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-(1-\alpha_{k})2\rho F(z_{k}).\end{split}

(6)

If
\alpha_{k}=\dfrac{1}{k+1}
, (
6
) admits
O(1/k^{2})
convergence rate. The convergence results of accelerated forward-backward splitting method in
[
4
]
is similar to the convergence results (
6
).

2.4
Symplectic Acceleration

The symplectic acceleration technique proposed in
[
38
]
is a new way to accelerate proximal point algorithm. The symplectic proximal point algorithm for solving zero-point of maximally monotone operator
F
is given as follows:

\begin{split}\tilde{z}_{k+1}&=\frac{k}{k+r}z_{k}+\frac{r}{k+r}u_{k},\\
z_{k+1}&=J_{F}(z_{k}),\\
u_{k+1}&=u_{k}+\frac{D}{r}(z_{k+1}-\tilde{z}_{k+1}).\end{split}

(7)

The reason why (
7
) is called symplectic proximal point algorithm is that (
7
) is obtained by applying first-order implicit symplectic method to a first-order ODEs system. If
r>1
,
0<D<r-1
, then (
7
) admits
O(1/k^{2})
convergence rate. Moreover,
o(1/k^{2})
convergence rate and weak convergence property of (
7
) can be established by considering two auxiliary functions.

3
Symplectic Extra-gradient Type Method

In this section, we introduce the symplectic extra-gradient+ framework for solving general inclusion problem

0\in T(z):=F(z)+G(z).

At first, we explain how to propose symplectic extra-gradient framework by considering the zero-point problem
0=F(z)
. In Section
2.4
, we introduce the symplectic proximal point algorithm (
7
). (
7
) can be generalized as follows:

\begin{split}\tilde{z}_{k+1}&=(1-\alpha_{k})z_{k}+\alpha_{k}u_{k},\\
z_{k+1}&=J_{F}(\tilde{z}_{k+1}),\\
u_{k+1}&=u_{k}+C_{k}(z_{k+1}-\tilde{z}_{k+1}).\end{split}

(8)

To formulate the desired algorithm, we need a two-step modification to equation (
8
). Due to the fact that
\tilde{z}_{k+1}-z_{k+1}=F(z_{k+1})
, the update rule for
u_{k+1}
is transformed to

u_{k+1}=u_{k}-C_{k}F(z_{k+1}).

Moreover, similar to (
5
), we replace the proximal point step by the EG step and obtain the
symplectic extra-gradient (SEG) method
described as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{z}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{k})z_{k}+\alpha_{k}u_{k},

(9a)

\displaystyle z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{z}_{k+1}-\beta_{k}F(z_{k}),

(9b)

\displaystyle z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\tilde{z}_{k+1}-\beta_{k}F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}),

(9c)

\displaystyle u_{k+1}

\displaystyle=u_{k}-C_{k}F(z_{k+1}).

(9d)

The coefficients
\{\alpha_{k}\},\{\beta_{k}\}
in (
9
) are positive for all
k\geqslant 1
. First, we notice that if we does not impose any constrains on
\{\alpha_{k}\}
and
\{C_{k}\}
, the EG method and EAG algorithm can be included in (
9
). Specifically, let
\alpha_{k}\equiv 1,\beta_{k}\equiv\dfrac{1}{L}
, (
9
) can be simplified as (
2
), and let
C_{k}\equiv 0
, (
9
) is the same as (
5
). However, it is noteworthy that despite the apparent requirement of three gradient evaluations per iteration in (
9
), a strategic reordering of the iterative process can economize computations to merely two gradient evaluations per loop. This is achieved through the adoption of the following alternative iteration rule:

\displaystyle u_{k+1}

\displaystyle=u_{k}-C_{k}F(z_{k}),

\displaystyle\tilde{z}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{k})z_{k}+\alpha_{k}u_{k+1},

\displaystyle z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{z}_{k+1}-\beta_{k}F(z_{k}),

\displaystyle z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\tilde{z}_{k+1}-\beta_{k}F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}).

The employment of such a reordering methodology can decrease the quantity of necessary gradient evaluations within subsequent algorithms. Consequently, further expounding on this technique will be omitted in the remaining text.

It should be noted that the SEG method is only suitable for solving zero-point of
F
under monotone assumption, coupled with complicate convergence results discussed in Section
A
. As such, we focus on the symplectic extra-gradient+ framework in the remaining text. Inspired by the FEG method (
6
) proposed in
[
16
]
, the corresponding
symplectic extra-gradient+ (SEG+) method
for solving zero-point of
L-
Lipschitz continuous and
\rho-
comonotone operator
F
is given as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{z}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha_{k})z_{k}+\alpha_{k}u_{k},

(10a)

\displaystyle z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{z}_{k+1}-(1-\alpha_{k})\left(\frac{1}{L}+2\rho\right)F(z_{k}),

(10b)

\displaystyle z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\tilde{z}_{k+1}-\dfrac{1}{L}F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-(1-\alpha_{k})2\rho F(z_{k}),

(10c)

\displaystyle u_{k+1}

\displaystyle=u_{k}-C_{k}F(z_{k+1}).

(10d)

Motivated by Tseng’s splitting algorithm
[
33
]
and the accelerated forward-backward splitting method
[
4
]
, we can further generalize the SEG+ method and propose the
symplectic forward-backward splitting (SFBS) method
with
\alpha_{k}=\dfrac{r}{k+r}
and
C_{k}=\dfrac{D}{r}
, which is described in Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm 1

Symplectic Forward-Backward Splitting Method, SFBS

Input:
Operators
F
and
G
,
L\in(0,+\infty),\rho\in\left(-\dfrac{1}{2L},+\infty\right)
;

Initialization:
z_{0}
,
u_{0}=z_{0}
,
\tilde{G}_{0}=0
;

for

k=0,1,\cdots

do

\tilde{z}_{k+1}=\dfrac{k}{k+r}z_{k}+\dfrac{r}{k+r}u_{k}
;

z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}=\tilde{z}_{k+1}-\dfrac{k}{k+r}\left(\dfrac{1}{L}+2\rho\right)(F(z_{k})+\tilde{G}_{k})
;

z_{k+1}=J_{L^{-1}G}\left(\tilde{z}_{k+1}-\dfrac{1}{L}F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-\dfrac{2\rho k}{k+r}(F(z_{k})+\tilde{G}_{k})\right)
;

\tilde{G}_{k+1}=L\left[\tilde{z}_{k+1}-z_{k+1}-\dfrac{1}{L}F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-\dfrac{2\rho k}{k+r}(F(z_{k})+\tilde{G}_{k})\right]
;

u_{k+1}=u_{k}-\dfrac{D}{r}(F(z_{k+1})+\tilde{G}_{k+1})
.

Next, we apply the Lyapunov function technique to demonstrate the convergence rates of (
10
). Here, inspired by the Lyapunov function in
[
16
]
for proving the convergence rates of the FEG method and the Lyapunov function in
[
38
]
for proving the convergence rates of the symplectic proximal point algorithm, the Lyapunov function for Algorithm
1
is

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}(k):=&\ \left[\frac{Dk^{2}}{2L}+\rho Dk(k-r)\right]\left\|F(z_{k})+\tilde{G}_{k}\right\|^{2}\\
&\ +Drk\left\langle F(z_{k})+\tilde{G}_{k},z_{k}-u_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{r^{3}-r^{2}}{2}\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2},\end{split}

(11)

where
z^{*}\in\mathrm{zero}(T)
. Given the fundamental role that the difference of the Lyapunov function, i. e.
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)
, plays in establishing convergence rates for (
10
), it becomes imperative to estimate the upper bound of the difference. By estimating the upper bound of
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)
, we can give the convergence results of Algorithm
1
in Theorem
1
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\{z_{k}\}
and
\{u_{k}\}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm
1
. If
r>1
and
0<D\leqslant(r-1)\left(\dfrac{1}{L}+2\rho\right)
, then we have

\left(\frac{1}{2L}+\rho-\frac{D}{2(r-1)}\right)k^{2}\mathrm{dist}(0,T(z_{k}))^{2}\leqslant\frac{r^{3}-r^{2}}{2D}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(z_{0},\mathrm{zero}(T))^{2},\quad\forall k\geqslant 1.

Thus if
D<(r-1)\left(\dfrac{1}{L}+2\rho\right)
, the last-iterate convergence rate of Algorithm
1
is

\mathrm{dist}(0,T(z_{k}))^{2}\leqslant\frac{(r-1)^{2}r^{2}}{\left[(r-1)\left(\dfrac{1}{L}+2\rho\right)D-D^{2}\right]k^{2}}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(z_{0},\mathrm{zero}(T))^{2},\quad\forall k\geqslant 1.

In addition, we have

\displaystyle\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2}\left[(r-1)\left(\frac{1}{L}+2\rho\right)-D\right](2k+r+1)\left\|F(z_{k+1})+\tilde{G}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \frac{r^{3}-r^{2}}{2D}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(z_{0},\mathrm{zero}(T))^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{T}(z_{k})=F(z_{k})+\tilde{G}_{k}
. By the definition of
\tilde{G}_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\tilde{z}_{k+1}-\frac{1}{L}[F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{G}_{k+1}]-\frac{2\rho k}{k+r}\tilde{T}(z_{k}).

With above equation, we obtain the following useful equations

\displaystyle k(z_{k+1}-z_{k})

\displaystyle=r(u_{k}-z_{k+1})-\frac{k+r}{L}[F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{G}_{k+1}]-2\rho k\tilde{T}(z_{k}),

(12)

\displaystyle r(z_{k}-u_{k})

\displaystyle=-(k+r)(z_{k+1}-z_{k})-\frac{k+r}{L}[F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{G}_{k+1}]-2\rho k\tilde{T}(z_{k}).

(13)

Also, by
L-
Lipschitz continuity of
F
, we have

\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}\leqslant L^{2}\left\|z_{k+1}-z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}.

Since

z_{k+1}-z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{L}\left[\frac{k}{k+r}\tilde{T}(z_{k})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-\tilde{G}_{k+1}\right],

we have

\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}\leqslant\left\|\frac{k}{k+r}\tilde{T}(z_{k})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-\tilde{G}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}.

(14)

Recall the Lyapunov function

\mathcal{E}(k)=\left[\frac{Dk^{2}}{2L}+\rho Dk(k-r)\right]\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k})\right\|^{2}+Drk\left\langle\tilde{T}(z_{k}),z_{k}-u_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{r^{3}-r^{2}}{2}\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}.

First, we divide the difference
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)
into three parts.

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \underbrace{\left[\frac{D(k+1)^{2}}{2L}+\rho D(k+1)(k+1-r)\right]\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})\right\|^{2}-\left[\frac{Dk^{2}}{2L}+\rho Dk(k-r)\right]\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k})\right\|^{2}}_{\text{I}}

\displaystyle\ +\underbrace{Dr(k+1)\left\langle\tilde{T}(z_{k+1}),z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}\right\rangle-Drk\left\langle\tilde{T}(z_{k}),z_{k}-u_{k}\right\rangle}_{\text{II}}

\displaystyle\ +\underbrace{\frac{r^{3}-r^{2}}{2}\left[\left\|u_{k+1}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}-\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}\right]}_{\text{III}}.

Secondly, we reckon the upper bound of II and III. First we consider II.

\displaystyle\text{II}=

\displaystyle\ Dr\left\langle\tilde{T}(z_{k+1}),z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}\right\rangle+Drk\left\langle\tilde{T}(z_{k+1}),z_{k+1}-z_{k}-u_{k+1}+u_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +Drk\left\langle\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})-\tilde{T}(z_{k}),z_{k}-u_{k}\right\rangle.

Due to (
12
) and the equality
u_{k+1}-u_{k}=-\dfrac{D}{r}[F(z_{k+1})+\tilde{G}_{k+1}]
, we have

\begin{split}&\ Drk\left\langle\tilde{T}(z_{k+1}),z_{k+1}-z_{k}-u_{k+1}+u_{k}\right\rangle\\

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{Dk}{\sqrt{r-1}}[F(z_{k})+\tilde{C}_{k}]-\sqrt{r^{3}-r^{2}}(u_{k}-z^{*})\right\|^{2}.

Since
F
is monotone,
\mathcal{E}(k)
can be represented by the sum of three non-negative terms. Then we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{D}{2L}-\frac{D^{2}}{2(r-1)}\right)k^{2}\mathrm{dist}(0,T(z_{k}))^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{E}(k)\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0)=\frac{r^{3}-r^{2}}{2}\left\|z_{0}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}.

\displaystyle Drk\left\langle F(z_{k})+\tilde{C}_{k},z_{k}-z^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{E}(k)\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0)=\frac{r^{3}-r^{2}}{2}\left\|z_{0}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}.

Taking infimum respect to all
z^{*}\in\mathrm{zero}(T)
on both sides of the first above inequality and using the inequality
\mathrm{dist}(0,T(z_{k}))\leqslant\left\|F(z_{k})+\tilde{C}_{k}\right\|
, we obtain the desire result. Moreover, by summing
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)
from
0
to
\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{D}{2}\left(\frac{r-1}{L}-D\right)(2k+r+1)\left\|F(z_{k+1})+\tilde{C}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}\leqslant\frac{r^{3}-r^{2}}{2}\left\|z_{0}-z^{*}\right\|^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+r)^{2}}{2L}\left\|\tilde{C}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{k}{k+r}\tilde{C}_{k}\right\|^{2}\leqslant\frac{r^{3}-r^{2}}{2}\left\|z_{0}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}.

Also, taking infimum respect to all
z^{*}\in\mathrm{zero}(T)
, we obtain the desire results.
∎

4
Sharper Rate

In this section, we will build up the
o(1/k^{2})
convergence rate of the SEG type method. It is noteworthy that the symplectic proximal point algorithm admits an
o(1/k^{2})
convergence rate if the assumption
D<r-1
holds. Analogously, one may suggest that the symplectic EG type method admits
o(1/k^{2})
convergence rate when
D<(r-1)\left(\dfrac{1}{L}+2\rho\right)
. However, the assumption
D<(r-1)\left(\dfrac{1}{L}+2\rho\right)
alone is not enough. The main reason is that the EG type method tries to use
F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})
to approximate
F(z_{k+1})
, but the error term
\left\|F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-F(z_{k+1})\right\|^{2}
can not be estimated directly. To overcome this problem, we need additional assumption on the step-size in the SEG type method. Here, we make use of Algorithm
1
to illustrate the desire result.

Lemma 3

(Estimation of error)
.

Let
\{z_{\frac{k}{2}}\}
,
\{\tilde{z}_{k+1}\}
and
\{u_{k}\}
be the sequences generated by

\displaystyle\tilde{z}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{k+r}z_{k}+\frac{r}{k+r}u_{k},

(30)

\displaystyle z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{z}_{k+1}-\frac{k}{k+r}(s+2\rho)(F(z_{k})+\tilde{G}_{k}),

(31)

\displaystyle z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=J_{\frac{1}{s}G}\left[\tilde{z}_{k+1}-sF(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-\frac{k}{k+r}2\rho(F(z_{k})+\tilde{G}_{k})\right],

(32)

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=s^{-1}\left[\tilde{z}_{k+1}-z_{k+1}-sF(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-\frac{k}{k+r}2\rho(F(z_{k})+\tilde{G}_{k})\right],

(33)

\displaystyle u_{k+1}

\displaystyle=u_{k}-\frac{D}{r}[F(z_{k+1})+\tilde{G}_{k+1}].

(34)

If
F
is
L-
Lipschitz continuous and
s<\dfrac{1}{L}
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}\leqslant C\left\|F(z_{k+1})+\tilde{G}_{k+1}-F(z_{k})-\tilde{G}_{k}\right\|^{2}.

Proof.

First, we show that for all
C_{1}>1
, there exists a constant
C_{2}>1
such that

\left\|x+y\right\|^{2}\leqslant C_{2}\left\|x\right\|^{2}+C_{1}\left\|y\right\|^{2},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

(35)

By using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|x+y\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|x\right\|^{2}+\left\|y\right\|^{2}+2\left\langle x,y\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\|x\right\|^{2}+\left\|y\right\|^{2}+2\left\langle(C_{1}-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}x,\sqrt{C_{1}-1}y\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C_{1}}{C_{1}-1}\left\|x\right\|^{2}+C_{1}\left\|y\right\|^{2},\quad\forall C_{1}>1.

Let
C_{2}=\dfrac{C_{1}}{C_{1}-1}
, we obtain the desire result. Let
\tilde{T}(z_{k})=F(z_{k})+\tilde{G}_{k}
. Due to
L-
Lipschitz continuity of
F
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}\leqslant L^{2}s^{2}\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})+\tilde{T}(z_{k})\right\|^{2}.

Let
y=F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})
and
x=-\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})+\tilde{T}(z_{k})
. Since
s<\dfrac{1}{L}
, there exists a constant
C_{1}
such that
C_{1}>1
and
L^{2}s^{2}C_{1}<1
. By applying (
35
), we have

\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}\leqslant L^{2}s^{2}C_{2}\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})-\tilde{T}(z_{k})\right\|^{2}+L^{2}s^{2}C_{1}\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}.

Let
C=\dfrac{L^{2}s^{2}C_{2}}{1-L^{2}s^{2}C_{1}}
, we have

\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}\leqslant C\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})-\tilde{T}(z_{k})\right\|^{2}.

∎

With Lemma
3
, we can demonstrate the
o(1/k^{2})
convergence rate of the SFBS method.

Theorem 4

(Faster convergence rate)
.

Let
\{z_{\frac{k}{2}}\}
,
\{\tilde{z}_{k+1}\}
and
\{u_{k}\}
be the sequences generated by (
30
)-(
34
). If
F
is
L-
Lipschitz continuous,
G
is maximally monotone,
\rho-\dfrac{1}{2L}
,
T=F+G
is
\rho-
comonotone,
\max\{0,-2\rho\}<s<\dfrac{1}{L}
, and
0<D<(r-1)(s+2\rho)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|z_{k}-u_{k}\right\|^{2}=0,\qquad

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}\text{ exists for all }z^{*}\in\mathrm{zero}(T),

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}k^{2}\mathrm{dist}(0,T(z_{k}))^{2}=0,\qquad

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}k^{2}\left\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\right\|^{2}=0.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{T}(z_{k})=F(z_{k})+\tilde{G}_{k}
. Owing to
s<\dfrac{1}{L}
, the operator
F
can be regarded as
s^{-1}-
Lipschitz continuous. By Theorem
1
, we have

\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k})\right\|^{2}\leqslant O\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right),\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k})\right\|^{2}<+\infty.

Due to Lemma
3
, we have

\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}\leqslant O\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right),\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}<+\infty.

Next, we show that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left\|z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}\right\|^{2}}{2k}<+\infty.

To obtain such result, we need to study the following auxiliary Lyapunov function:

\mathcal{G}(k)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|z_{k}-u_{k}\right\|^{2}+\frac{r}{2D}\cdot\frac{(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}}\left(s+2\rho-\frac{D}{r}\right)\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}.

First, consider the difference
\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|z_{k}-u_{k}\right\|^{2}
.

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|z_{k}-u_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\langle z_{k+1}+z_{k}-u_{k+1}-u_{k},z_{k+1}-z_{k}-u_{k+1}+u_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left(2+\frac{r}{k}\right)z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}-\left(1+\frac{r}{k}\right)u_{k}+\frac{k+r}{k}s[F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{G}_{k+1}]+2\rho\tilde{T}(z_{k}),\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\ \left.-\frac{r}{k}z_{k+1}+\left(1+\frac{r}{k}\right)u_{k}-u_{k+1}-\frac{k+r}{k}s[F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{G}_{k+1}]-2\rho\tilde{T}(z_{k})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(2k+r)r}{2k^{2}}\left\|z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}}s\left\langle F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}),z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ -\frac{k+r}{k}2\rho s\left\langle F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-F(z_{k+1}),\tilde{T}(z_{k})-\frac{k+r}{k}\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}}\left(s+2\rho-\frac{D}{r}\right)\left\langle\tilde{T}(z_{k+1}),z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ -\frac{k+r}{k}\left(s+2\rho-\frac{D}{r}\right)2\rho s\left\langle\tilde{T}(z_{k})-\frac{k+r}{k}\tilde{T}(z_{k+1}),\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(2k+r)(r-1)}{2k^{2}}\left\|z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{(k+r)^{4}s^{2}}{2k^{2}(2k+r)}\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}}\left(s+2\rho-\frac{D}{r}\right)\left\langle\tilde{T}(z_{k+1}),z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\frac{k+r}{k}\rho s\left(\left\|F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-F(z_{k+1})\right\|^{2}+\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k})-\frac{k+r}{k}\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{k+r}{k}\left(s+2\rho-\frac{D}{r}\right)\rho s\left(\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k})-\frac{k+r}{k}\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})\right\|^{2}+\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})\right\|^{2}\right).

Also, since
\dfrac{(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}}
is non-increasing, we have

\displaystyle\ \frac{r}{2D}\cdot\frac{(k+1+r)^{2}}{(k+1)^{2}}\left(s+2\rho-\frac{D}{r}\right)\left\|u_{k+1}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}-\frac{r}{2D}\cdot\frac{(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}}\left(s+2\rho-\frac{D}{r}\right)\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \frac{r}{2D}\cdot\frac{(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}}\left(s+2\rho-\frac{D}{r}\right)\left(\left\|u_{k+1}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}-\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{r}{2D}\cdot\frac{(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}}\left(s+2\rho-\frac{D}{r}\right)\left(2\left\langle u_{k+1}-u_{k},u_{k+1}-z^{*}\right\rangle-\left\|u_{k+1}-u_{k}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{r}{2D}\cdot\frac{(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}}\left(s+2\rho-\frac{D}{r}\right)\left(-\frac{2D}{r}\left\langle\tilde{T}(z_{k+1}),u_{k+1}-z^{*}\right\rangle-\frac{D^{2}}{r^{2}}\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})\right\|^{2}\right).

Let

\displaystyle\varphi(k)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(k+r)^{4}s^{2}}{2k^{2}(2k+r)}\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(k+r)^{2}}{k^{2}}\left(s+2\rho-\frac{D}{r}\right)\left(\rho+\frac{D}{2r}\right)\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{k+r}{k}\rho s\left(\left\|F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-F(z_{k+1})\right\|^{2}+\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k})-\frac{k+r}{k}\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{k+r}{k}\left(s+2\rho-\frac{D}{r}\right)\rho s\left(\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k})-\frac{k+r}{k}\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})\right\|^{2}+\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})\right\|^{2}\right).

By previous argument, we have

\mathcal{G}(k+1)-\mathcal{G}(k)\leqslant-\frac{(2k+r)(r-1)}{2k^{2}}\left\|z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\varphi(k).

Since
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\varphi(k)<+\infty
, we have

\mathcal{G}(k+1)+\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\varphi(i)\leqslant\mathcal{G}(k)+\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}\varphi(k)-\frac{(2k+r)(r-1)}{2k^{2}}\left\|z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}\right\|^{2}.

Then we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{G}(k)\text{ exists, }\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left\|z_{k}-u_{k}\right\|^{2}}{k}\leqslant+\infty.

The next thing we need to do is to show that for all
z^{*}\in\mathrm{zero}(T)
,
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}
exists. If
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}
exists,
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{G}(k)
exists and
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{\left\|z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}\right\|^{2}}{2k}<+\infty
, we can easily show that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|z_{k}-u_{k}\right\|^{2}=0.

Calculating the difference of
\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|u_{k+1}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ -\frac{D}{r}\left\langle\tilde{T}(z_{k+1}),u_{k+1}-z^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \frac{D}{2r}\left(k\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})\right\|^{2}+k^{-1}\left\|u_{k+1}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}\right)-\frac{D\rho}{r}\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})\right\|^{2}.

Since

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{D}{2r}\left(k\left\|T_{k+1}\right\|^{2}+k^{-1}\left\|u_{k+1}-z_{k+1}\right\|^{2}\right)-\frac{D\rho}{r}\left\|T_{k+1}\right\|^{2}<+\infty,

we prove the existence of the limitation
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}
.

Finally, we can prove the results presented in Theorem
1
. Since

\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k})\right\|^{2}\leqslant O\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right),\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|z_{k}-u_{k}\right\|^{2}=0,

we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}Drk\left\langle\tilde{T}(z_{k}),z_{k}-u_{k}\right\rangle=0.

Consider the following Lyapunov function

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(k)=

\displaystyle\ \left[\frac{Dk^{2}s}{2}+\rho Dk(k-r)\right]\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +Drk\left\langle\tilde{T}(z_{k}),z_{k}-u_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{r^{3}-r^{2}}{2}\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|.

By Theorem
4
, we have
\{\mathcal{E}(k)\}
is non-increasing and non-negetive. Thus,
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(k)
exists. Also, by the existence of
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left[\frac{Dk^{2}s}{2}+\rho Dk(k-r)\right]\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k})\right\|^{2}\text{ exists}.

Since
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}k\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k})\right\|^{2}<+\infty
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}k^{2}\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k})\right\|^{2}=0.

By the inequality
0\leqslant\mathrm{dist}(0,T(z_{k}))^{2}\leqslant\left\|\tilde{T}(z_{k})\right\|^{2}
, we can obtain

\lim_{k\to\infty}k^{2}\mathrm{dist}(0,T(z_{k}))^{2}=0.

Also, by the inequality

k(z_{k+1}-z_{k})=r(u_{k+1}-z_{k+1})-[(k+r)s-D]\tilde{T}(z_{k+1})-(k+r)s[F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-F(z_{k+1})]-2\rho k\tilde{T}(z_{k}),

we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}k^{2}\left\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\right\|^{2}=0.

∎

Next, we discuss the weak convergence property.

Lemma 5
.

Let
F:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be a
L
-Lipschitz continuous operator,
G
be a maximally monotone operator, and
T=F+G
be a
\rho
-comonotone operator. If
\rho>-\dfrac{1}{2L}
, then
\frac{1}{2L}(F+G)
is a maximally
2L\rho
-comonotone.

Proof.

Because of
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
F
, we have

\left\langle F(x)-F(y),x-y\right\rangle\geq-\left\|F(x)-F(y)\right\|_{2}\left\|x-y\right\|_{2}\geq-L\left\|x-y\right\|_{2}^{2},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

Then
LI+F
is a monotone operator. Also, by Corollary 20.28 in
[
1
]
,
LI+F
is a maximally monotone operator. Then according to Minty’s surjective theorem and Theorem 25.2 in
[
1
]
, we have

\mathrm{ran}\left(2LI+F+G\right)=\mathrm{ran}\left(LI+\left(LI+F+G\right)\right)=\mathcal{H}.

Because
\frac{1}{2L}(F+G)
is
2L\rho
-comonotone and
2L\rho>-1
, then by Proposition 2.16 in
[
2
]
, we have that
\frac{1}{2L}(F+G)
is maximally
2L\rho
-comonotone.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
T
be a maximally
\rho
-comonotone operator. If there exists two sequences
\{z_{k}\}
and
\{u_{k}\}
such that
u_{k}\in T(z_{k})
,
u_{k}\to u
and
z_{k}\rightharpoonup z
, then
z\in T(x)
.

Above lemma is a direct generalization of Proposition 20.38 in
[
1
]
.

Proof.

Let
(y,v)
be any point such that
v\in T(y)
. Then we have

\left\langle u_{k}-v,z_{k}-y\right\rangle\geq\rho\left\|u_{k}-v\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Let
k\to\infty
, we have

\left\langle u-v,z-y\right\rangle\geq\rho\left\|u-v\right\|_{2}^{2},\quad\forall v\in T(x).

Because
T
is a maximally
\rho
-comonotone operator, then
u\in T(x)
. If
u\notin T(z)
, then consider the following operator

\overline{T}(y):=\begin{cases}T(y),&y\neq z;\\
T(z)\cup\{u\},&y=z.\end{cases}

Then it is easy to verify that
\overline{T}
is
\rho
-comonotone and the graph of
T
is a proper subset of the graph of
\tilde{T}
, which is contradict to the assumption that
T
be a maximally
\rho
-comonotone operator.
∎

Theorem 7

(Weak convergence property)
.

Let
\{z_{\frac{k}{2}}\}
,
\{\tilde{z}_{k+1}\}
and
\{u_{k}\}
be the sequences generated by (
30
)-(
34
). If
F
is
L-
Lipschitz continuous,
G
is maximally monotone,
T=F+G
is
\rho-
comonotone,
\rho>-\dfrac{1}{2L}
,
\max\{0,-2\rho\}<s<\dfrac{1}{L}
, and

, then both of the sequences

and
\{u_{k}\}
converge weakly to the same point in
\mathrm{zero}(T)
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
4
and Proposition
1
, we only need to prove that every weak cluster points of
\{u_{k}\}
belong to
\mathrm{zero}(T)
. Let
u_{\infty}
be any weak cluster point of
\{u_{k}\}
, and
\{u_{k_{j}}\}
be the subsequence of
\{u_{k}\}
such that
u_{k_{j}}\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}
.

Then by Theorem
4
, we have
z_{k_{j}}\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}
. Because
\tilde{T}(z_{k_{j}})\in T(z_{k_{j}})
and
\tilde{T}(z_{k_{j}})\to 0

\frac{1}{2L}\tilde{T}(z_{k_{j}})\to 0

The method to demonstrate the
o(1/k^{2})
convergence rate and the weak convergence property of the SPEG+ method is similar to the proof for Theorem
4
and Theorem

.

Corollary 8
.

Let
\{z_{\frac{k}{2}}\}
,
\{\tilde{z}_{k+1}\}
and
\{u_{k}\}
be the sequences generated by

\displaystyle\tilde{z}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{k}{k+r}z_{k}+\dfrac{r}{k+r}u_{k},

\displaystyle z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=P_{C}\left(\tilde{z}_{k+1}-\dfrac{ks}{(k+r)}F(z_{k})\right),

\displaystyle z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=P_{C}\left(\tilde{z}_{k+1}-sF(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right),

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=s^{-1}\left(\tilde{z}_{k+1}-sF(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-z_{k+1}\right),

\displaystyle u_{k+1}

\displaystyle=u_{k}-\dfrac{D}{r}[F(z_{k+1})+\tilde{C}_{k+1}].

If
F
is
L-
Lipschitz continuous and monotone,
0<s<\dfrac{1}{L}
,
r>1
and
0<D<(r-1)s
, then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|z_{k}-u_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|u_{k}-z^{*}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\text{ exists for all }z^{*}\in\mathrm{zero}(T),

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}k^{2}\mathrm{dist}(0,T(z_{k}))^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}k\left\langle F(z_{k})+\tilde{C}_{k},z_{k}-z^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall z^{*}\in\mathrm{zero}(T),

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}k^{2}\left\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=0.

Moreover, both of the sequences
\{z_{k}\}
and
\{u_{k}\}
converge weakly to a point in
\mathrm{zero}(T)
.

Proof.

The main difficultly is to estimate the error term
\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|
. If

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}<+\infty

is true, then by applying the proofing technique in the proof of Theorem
4
, we can obtain the results in Corollary
8
. Since
F
is
L-
Lipschitz continuous, we have

\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}\leqslant L^{2}s^{2}\left\|F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})+\tilde{C}_{k+1}-\frac{k}{k+r}F(z_{k})-\tilde{C}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}.

Since
s<\dfrac{1}{L}
, there exists a constant
C_{1}>1
such that
L^{2}s^{2}C_{1}<1
. By (
35
), there exists a constant
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\ \left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ L^{2}s^{2}C_{1}\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +L^{2}s^{2}C_{2}\left\|F(z_{k+1})+\tilde{C}_{k+1}-\frac{k}{k+r}F(z_{k})-\tilde{C}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ L^{2}s^{2}C_{1}\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}+2L^{2}s^{2}C_{2}\left\|\frac{k}{k+r}\tilde{C}_{k}-\tilde{C}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +2L^{2}s^{2}C_{2}\left\|F(z_{k+1})+\tilde{C}_{k+1}-\frac{k}{k+r}F(z_{k})-\frac{k}{k+r}\tilde{C}_{k}\right\|^{2}.

The proof of Theorem
2
shows that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+r)^{2}\left\|\frac{k}{k+r}\tilde{C}_{k}-\tilde{C}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}<+\infty.

In conclusion,

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k\left\|F(z_{k+1})-F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right\|^{2}<+\infty

holds.
∎

5
Line Search Framework

The theorems presented in Section
3
indicate that the SEG+ type method exhibits its maximum theoretical convergence speed when
D=(r-1)\left(\dfrac{1}{2L}+\rho\right)
. This prompts the question as to whether the SEG+ type method with
D=(r-1)\left(\dfrac{1}{2L}+\rho\right)
indeed converges most rapidly in practical applications. We conduct a straightforward numerical experiment aimed at verifying this question. Here we consider the following min-max problem:

\min_{x}\max_{y}f(x,y)=-\frac{1}{6}x^{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}xy+\frac{1}{6}y^{2}.

Such min-max problem is considered in section 4.4 in
[
16
]
. The first-order criteria of above min-max problem is

0=F(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}-\dfrac{1}{3}x+\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}y\\
-\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}x-\dfrac{1}{3}y\end{pmatrix}.

(36)

It is easily to verify that

\displaystyle\left\|F(x,y)-F(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|(x,y)-(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right\|,\quad\forall(x,y),\ (x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\left\langle F(x,y)-F(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),x-y\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}\left\|F(x,y)-F(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right\|^{2},\quad\forall(x,y),\ (x^{\prime},y^{\prime}).

We apply Algorithm
1
with parameter
r=2
and different parameters
D
to above zero-point problem until the termination condition is satisfied. The required number of iterations of Algorithm
1
respect to
D
is plotted in Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
The required number of iterations Algorithm
1
with
G=0
respect to
D
. The termination condition is
\left\|F(x_{k},y_{k})\right\|\leqslant 10^{-6}
.

As we can see from Figure
1
, when
D=(r-1)\left(\dfrac{1}{2L}+\rho\right)
, i. e.
D=\dfrac{1}{6}
, the corresponding instance of Algorithm
1
requires least number of iterations. However, the Lipschitz constant
L
and the comonotone index
\rho
of the operator
F
described in (
36
) can be deduced in advance. In practice, the Lipschitz constant of most of the operators
F
and the comonotone index of most of the operators
T
can not be determined. In addition, even though we can determine the Lipschitz constant and the comonotone index in advance, the operator
F
may have smaller local Lipschitz constant or the operator
T
may have larger local comonotone index as
z_{k}
approaching
\mathrm{zero}(T)
. Algorithm
1
can not catch these local information. To overcome these problems, we apply the line search framework to the SFBS method to estimate these local information.

Since there is a term
\left\langle F(z_{k})+\tilde{G}_{k},z_{k}-u_{k}\right\rangle
in the Lyapunov function, when we consider the difference of
\left\langle F(z_{k})+\tilde{G}_{k},z_{k}-u_{k}\right\rangle
,
\left\langle F(z_{k+1})+\tilde{G}_{k+1}-F(z_{k})-\tilde{G}_{k},z_{k+1}-z_{k}\right\rangle
appears. This is why we need to assume comonotonicity for the operator
T=F+G
. Thus we propose the termination condition for
\rho_{k}
is

\rho_{k}\leqslant\frac{\left\langle F(z_{k+1})+\tilde{G}_{k+1}-F(z_{k})+\tilde{G}_{k},z_{k+1}-z_{k}\right\rangle}{\left\|F(z_{k+1})+\tilde{G}_{k+1}-F(z_{k})-\tilde{G}_{k}\right\|^{2}}.

Also, the idea that
F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})
can be seen as an approximation of
F(z_{k+1})
inspires us that the Lipschitz continuity on
F
helps us give an estimation of the error
\left\|F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-F(z_{k+1})\right\|
. Because the parameter
L_{k}
serves as an estimation of local Lipschitz constant, the termination condition for
L_{k}
should be

L_{k}\geqslant\frac{\left\|F(z_{k+\frac{1}{2}})-F(z_{k+1})\right\|}{\left\|z_{k+\frac{1}{2}}-z_{k+1}\right\|}.

By summarizing previous argument, we obtain Algorithm
3
. The convergence results of Algorithm
3
is given in the following theorems.

7
Conclusion and Further Discussion

In our paper, we introduce the symplectic extra-gradient type methods based on the symplectic proximal point algorithm. By using the Lyapunov function approach, we show these methods have a convergence rate of
O(1/k^{2})
. We also prove they can achieve a faster
o(1/k^{2})
convergence rate and show weak convergence property under stronger assumptions. To improve computational speed, we incorporate a line search technique into our symplectic extra-gradient method.

Several theoretical questions around SEG type method remain. With the heightened need for efficient Generative Adversarial Networks training, studying a stochastic version of our method is intriguing. Furthermore, inspired by Tseng’s method for more general monotone inclusion problems discussed in
[
1
]
, developing a more general SEG type method is of interest. Finally, we are intrigued to explore whether symplectic acceleration can be useful to derive a broader range of acceleration forms of algorithms that designed to solve either minimax problems or inclusion problems.

Practically, creating efficient algorithms to find good values for
L_{k}
and
\rho_{k}
in Algorithm
3
, or just
L_{k}
as in Corollary
12
, is a concern. Our LASSO problem tests indicate that the current line search for
\rho_{k}
does not speed up calculations significantly. One suggestion from our analysis in Corollary
11
is that
\rho_{k}
shouldn’t greatly exceed the comonotone index. Therefore, improving when to stop adjusting
\rho_{k}
could be beneficial.

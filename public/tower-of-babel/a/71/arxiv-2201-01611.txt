BGK model for multi-component gases near a global Maxwellian
Gi-Chan Bae, Christian Klingenberg, Marlies Pirner, Seok-Bae Yun

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish the existence of the unique global-in-time classical solutions to the multi-component BGK model suggested in [ 47 ] when the initial data is a small perturbation of global equilibrium. For this, we carefully analyze the dissipative nature of the linearized multi-component relaxation operator, and observe that the partial dissipation from the intra-species and the inter-species linearized relaxation operators are combined in a complementary manner to give rise to the desired dissipation estimate of the model We also observe that the convergence rate of the distribution function increases as the momentum-energy interchange rate between the different components of the gas increases.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the existence and the asymptotic behavior of the BGK model for multi-component gases suggested in
[
47
]
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}F_{1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F_{1}&=n_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{11}-F_{1})+n_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{12}-F_{1}),\cr\partial_{t}F_{2}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F_{2}&=n_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{22}-F_{2})+n_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{21}-F_{2}),\cr F_{1}(x,v,0)=F_{10}&(x,v),\qquad F_{2}(x,v,0)=F_{20}(x,v).\end{aligned}

The distribution function
F_{i}(x,v,t)
denotes the number density of
i

(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
at time

for

\mathcal{M}_{ii}
are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{ii}=\frac{n_{i}}{\sqrt{2\pi\frac{T_{i}}{m_{i}}}^{3}}\exp\left(-\frac{|v-U_{i}|^{2}}{2\frac{T_{i}}{m_{i}}}\right),\quad(i=1,2).

Here
m_{i}

(i=1,2)

i

which we assume that

The number density

, the bulk velocity

, and the temperature

i

\displaystyle n_{i}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{i}(x,v,t)dv,

\displaystyle U_{i}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{i}(x,v,t)vdv,

\displaystyle T_{i}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3n_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{i}(x,v,t)m_{i}|v-U_{i}|^{2}dv.

The inter-species Maxwellian distributions are defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{12}=\frac{n_{1}}{\sqrt{2\pi\frac{T_{12}}{m_{1}}}^{3}}\exp\left(-\frac{|v-U_{12}|^{2}}{2\frac{T_{12}}{m_{1}}}\right),\qquad\mathcal{M}_{21}=\frac{n_{2}}{\sqrt{2\pi\frac{T_{21}}{m_{2}}}^{3}}\exp\left(-\frac{|v-U_{21}|^{2}}{2\frac{T_{21}}{m_{2}}}\right),

where the inter-species bulk velocities
U_{12},U_{21}
and the inter-species temperatures
T_{12},T_{21}
are defined by

\displaystyle U_{12}

\displaystyle=\delta U_{1}+(1-\delta)U_{2},

\displaystyle U_{21}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}(1-\delta)U_{1}+\left(1-\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}(1-\delta)\right)U_{2},

and

\displaystyle T_{12}

\displaystyle=\omega T_{1}+(1-\omega)T_{2}+\gamma|U_{2}-U_{1}|^{2},

\displaystyle T_{21}

\displaystyle=(1-\omega)T_{1}+\omega T_{2}+\left(\frac{1}{3}m_{1}(1-\delta)\left(\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}(\delta-1)+1+\delta\right)-\gamma\right)|U_{2}-U_{1}|^{2}.

Here, the free parameter
\delta
and
\omega
denote the momentum interchange rate and the temperature interchange rate, respectively.
In (
1.1
),
n_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{ii}-F_{i})

(i=1,2)
are the intra-species relaxation operators for
i
-th gas component, while

n_{j}(\mathcal{M}_{ij}-F_{i})

(i\neq j)
are the inter-species relaxation operators between different components of the gas. We note that the inter-species relaxation operators describe the interchange of the macroscopic momentum and the temperature between two different species of gas.
These relaxation operators satisfy the following cancellation properties:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathcal{M}_{ii}-F_{i})\left(1,m_{i}v,m_{i}|v|^{2}\right)dv=0,\quad i=1,2\cr&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathcal{M}_{12}-F_{1})dv=0,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathcal{M}_{21}-F_{2})dv=0,\cr&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{12}-F_{1})m_{1}vdv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{21}-F_{2})m_{2}vdv=0,\cr&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{12}-F_{1})m_{1}|v|^{2}dv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{21}-F_{2})m_{2}|v|^{2}dv=0,\end{split}

leading to the following conservation laws of the density, total momentum, and total energy:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{1}(x,v,t)dvdx=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{2}(x,v,t)dvdx=0,\cr&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(F_{1}(x,v,t)m_{1}v+F_{2}(x,v,t)m_{2}v\right)dvdx=0,\cr&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(F_{1}(x,v,t)m_{1}|v|^{2}+F_{2}(x,v,t)m_{2}|v|^{2}\right)dvdx=0.\end{split}

To ensure the positivity of all temperatures, the free parameters
\omega
,
\delta
, and
\gamma
are restricted to

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}-1}{1+\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}}\leq\delta<1,\qquad 0\leq\omega<1,

and

\displaystyle 0\leq\gamma\leq\frac{m_{1}}{3}(1-\delta)\left[\left(1+\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}\right)\delta+1-\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}\right].

The main goal of this paper is to establish the global-in-time classical solution of the mixture BGK model when the initial data is close to global equilibrium. For this, we consider the following global equilibrium for each particle distribution function:

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(v)=n_{10}\frac{\sqrt{m_{1}}^{3}}{\sqrt{2\pi}^{3}}e^{-\frac{m_{1}|v|^{2}}{2}},\qquad\mu_{2}(v)=n_{20}\frac{\sqrt{m_{2}}^{3}}{\sqrt{2\pi}^{3}}e^{-\frac{m_{2}|v|^{2}}{2}}.

We then define the perturbations
f_{k}

(k=1,2)
by
F_{k}=\mu_{k}+\sqrt{\mu_{k}}f_{k}
and rewrite the mixture BGK model (
1.1
) in terms of
f_{k}
as

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}f_{1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}&=L_{11}(f_{1})+L_{12}(f_{1},f_{2})+\Gamma_{11}(f_{1})+\Gamma_{12}(f_{1},f_{2}),\cr\partial_{t}f_{2}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}&=L_{22}(f_{2})+L_{21}(f_{1},f_{2})+\Gamma_{22}(f_{2})+\Gamma_{21}(f_{1},f_{2}).\end{split}

On the R.H.S,
L_{11}
and
L_{22}
denote the linearized part of the intra-species relaxation operators:

L_{kk}(f_{k})=n_{k0}(P_{k}f_{k}-f_{k}),\quad(k=1,2),

where

is the

projection onto the linear space spanned by

\left\{\sqrt{\mu_{k}},v\sqrt{\mu_{k}},|v|^{2}\sqrt{\mu_{k}}\right\}.

The linearized operators for inter-species interactions
L_{12}
and
L_{21}
are given by

\displaystyle L_{12}(f_{1},f_{2})

\displaystyle=n_{20}(P_{1}f_{1}-f_{1})

\displaystyle\quad+n_{20}\bigg[(1-\delta)\sum_{2\leq i\leq 4}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}}\langle f_{2},e_{2i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{1},e_{1i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)e_{1i}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\omega)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\langle f_{2},e_{25}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{1},e_{15}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)e_{15}\bigg],

\displaystyle L_{21}(f_{1},f_{2})

\displaystyle=n_{10}(P_{2}f_{2}-f_{2})

\displaystyle\quad+n_{10}\bigg[\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}(1-\delta)\sum_{2\leq i\leq 4}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{20}}{n_{10}}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}}\langle f_{1},e_{1i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{2},e_{2i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)e_{2i}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\omega)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{20}}{n_{10}}}\langle f_{1},e_{15}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{2},e_{25}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)e_{25}\bigg],

for
0\leq\delta,\omega<1
and
\{e_{ki}\}_{1\leq i\leq 5}
is an orthonormal basis spanned by
\left\{\sqrt{\mu_{k}},v\sqrt{\mu_{k}},|v|^{2}\sqrt{\mu_{k}}\right\}
for
k=1,2
.
Finally,
\Gamma_{11}
,
\Gamma_{22}
,
\Gamma_{12}
, and

are nonlinear perturbations.

We define an

•

The standard

norm of a vector denotes

\displaystyle\|\left(f(x,v),g(x,v)\right)\|_{L^{2}_{v}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f(v)|^{2}+|g(v)|^{2}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\|\left(f(x,v),g(x,v)\right)\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f(x,v)|^{2}+|g(x,v)|^{2}dvdx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We use the following notations for multi-indices differential operators:

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}=\partial_{t}^{\alpha_{0}}\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{x_{3}}^{\alpha_{3}}\partial_{v_{1}}^{\beta_{1}}\partial_{v_{2}}^{\beta_{2}}\partial_{v_{3}}^{\beta_{3}}.

We employ the following convention for simplicity.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
. We set the macroscopic quantities of the initial data to the same with that of the global equilibria:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{k0}(x,v)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\cr m_{k}v\cr m_{k}|v|^{2}\end{array}\right)dvdx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu_{k}(v)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\cr m_{k}v\cr m_{k}|v|^{2}\end{array}\right)dvdx,

for
k=1,2
.
We define
f_{k0}
as
F_{k0}=\mu_{k}+\sqrt{\mu_{k}}f_{k0}
.
Then there exists
\epsilon>0
such that if
\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},N_{2}}(f_{10},f_{20})<\epsilon
, then there exists a unique global-in-time classical solution of (
1.1
) satisfying

•

The two distribution functions are non-negative:

F_{k}(x,v,t)=\mu_{k}+\sqrt{\mu_{k}}f_{k}\geq 0.

•

The conservation laws hold (
1.2
).

•

The distribution functions converge exponentially to the global equilibrium:

\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},N_{2}}(f_{1},f_{2})(t)\leq Ce^{-\eta t}\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},N_{2}}(f_{10},f_{20}).

In the case of
N_{2}=0
, that is, if
\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},0}(f_{10},f_{20})<\epsilon
, we have the following more detailed convergence estimate:

\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},0}(f_{1},f_{2})(t)\leq Ce^{-\eta\min\left\{(1-\delta),(1-\omega)\right\}t}\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},0}(f_{10},f_{20}).

•

Let
(f_{1},f_{2})
and
(\bar{f}_{1},\bar{f}_{2})
be solutions corresponding to the initial data
(f_{10},f_{20})
and
(\bar{f}_{10},\bar{f}_{20})
, respectively, then the system satisfies the following
L^{2}
stability:

\|(f_{1}-\bar{f}_{1},f_{2}-\bar{f}_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq C\|(f_{10}-\bar{f}_{10},f_{20}-\bar{f}_{20})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

Remark 1.2
.

The convergence rate in the case of
N_{2}=0
shows that the higher interchange rate (
\delta
and
\omega
close to
0
) gives the faster convergence rates.

The most important step is the identification of the dissipation mechanism of the linearized multi-component relaxation operator. To investigate the dissipative property of
L
, we decompose the linearized inter-species relaxation operator
L_{ij}

(i\neq j)
further into the mass interaction part
L^{1}_{ij}
and the momentum-energy interaction part
L^{2}_{ij}
:

\displaystyle L_{12}^{1}(f_{1})=n_{20}(P_{1}f_{1}-f_{1}),\quad L_{21}^{1}(f_{2})=n_{10}(P_{2}f_{2}-f_{2}),

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}L_{12}^{2}(f_{1},f_{2})&=n_{20}\bigg[(1-\delta)\sum_{2\leq i\leq 4}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}}\langle f_{2},e_{2i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{1},e_{1i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)e_{1i}\cr&\quad+(1-\omega)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\langle f_{2},e_{25}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{1},e_{15}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)e_{15}\bigg],\cr L_{21}^{2}(f_{1},f_{2})&=n_{10}\bigg[\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}(1-\delta)\sum_{2\leq i\leq 4}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{20}}{n_{10}}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}}\langle f_{1},e_{1i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{2},e_{2i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)e_{2i}\cr&\quad+(1-\omega)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{20}}{n_{10}}}\langle f_{1},e_{15}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{2},e_{25}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)e_{25}\bigg],\end{split}

so that
L_{12}=L_{12}^{1}+L_{12}^{2}
and
L_{21}=L_{21}^{1}+L_{21}^{2}
. We first derive from an explicit computation that the intra-species operator
L_{ii}
and the mass interaction part of the inter-species operator
L^{1}_{12}
and
L^{1}_{21}
give rise to the following partial dissipative estimate:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle(L_{11}+L_{12}^{1})f_{1},f_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle(L_{22}+L_{21})f_{2},f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\cr&\hskip 85.35826pt=-(n_{10}+n_{20})\|(I-P_{1},I-P_{2})(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.\end{split}

We note that the dissipation estimate above is too weak in that it involves 10-dimensional degeneracy,
which is 4-dimensional bigger than the 6-dimensional conservation laws in (
1.2
).
It is the additional dissipation from the momentum-energy interaction parts
L_{12}^{2}
,
L_{21}^{2}
of the inter-species operators
L_{12}
and
L_{21}
that make up for the deficiency:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle L_{12}^{2},f_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle L_{21}^{2},f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}&\leq-\min\left\{(1-\delta),(1-\omega)\right\}(n_{10}+n_{20})\cr&\quad\times\left(\|(P_{1},P_{2})(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}-\|P(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\right),\end{split}

where
P
is an orthonormal
L^{2}\times L^{2}
projection on the space spanned by the following
6
-dimensional basis

\displaystyle\{(\sqrt{\mu_{1}},0),(0,\sqrt{\mu_{2}}),(m_{1}v\sqrt{\mu_{1}},m_{2}v\sqrt{\mu_{2}}),\left((m_{1}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{1}},(m_{2}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{2}}\right)\}.

Then partial dissipation estimates (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) complement each other to give rise to the following multi-component dissipation estimate for
L
:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle L(f_{1},f_{2}),(f_{1},f_{2})\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}&\leq-(n_{10}+n_{20})\Big(\max\{\delta,\omega\}\|(I-P_{1},I-P_{2})(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\cr&\quad+\min\left\{(1-\delta),(1-\omega)\right\}\|(I-P)(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\Big).\end{split}

The dissipation estimate (
1.6
), together with further analysis on the degeneracy part through the standard micro-macro decomposition, provides the following full coercivity depending on the interchange rates:

\displaystyle\langle L(\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2})),\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2})\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq-\eta\min\left\{(1-\delta),(1-\omega)\right\}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|(\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

Due to the presence of the momentum interchange rate
\delta
and the energy interchange rate
\omega
between different components in the dissipation estimate, we see that the larger interchange rate (when
\delta
and
\omega
are close to zero) leads to the stronger dissipation, and therefore, the faster convergence to the global equilibrium:

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t))\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\leq e^{-\eta\min\left\{(1-\delta),(1-\omega)\right\}t}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0))\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

2.1.
Linearization of the mixture Maxwellian

In this part, we linearize the inter-species Maxwellian

Definition 2.1
.

We define the macroscopic projection operator
P_{k}
in
L^{2}_{v}
for
k=1,2

\displaystyle P_{k}f

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n_{k0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\sqrt{\mu_{k}}dv\sqrt{\mu_{k}}+\frac{m_{k}}{n_{k0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fv\sqrt{\mu_{k}}dv\cdot v\sqrt{\mu_{k}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{6n_{k0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(m_{k}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{k}}dv(m_{k}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{k}}.

We denote
5
-dimensional basis as
(i=2,3,4)

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}e_{k1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{k0}}}\sqrt{\mu_{k}},\qquad e_{ki}=\sqrt{\frac{m_{k}}{n_{k0}}}v_{i-1}\sqrt{\mu_{k}},\qquad e_{k5}=\frac{m_{k}|v|^{2}-3}{\sqrt{6n_{k0}}}\sqrt{\mu_{k}}.\end{split}

The
5

and
\{e_{2i}\}_{i=1,\cdots,5}

\displaystyle P_{1}f=\sum_{1\leq i\leq 5}\langle f,e_{1i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}e_{1i},\quad\textit{and}\quad P_{2}f=\sum_{1\leq i\leq 5}\langle f,e_{2i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}e_{2i}.

Lemma 2.2
.

The mono-species BGK Maxwellian

for k=1,2. The function

The linearization of the mono-species BGK Maxwellian

3.
Dissipative property of the linearized relaxation operator

In this part, we investigate the dissipative property of the linearized multi-component relaxation operator. For simplicity of the notation, we denote the linear operator and the nonlinear perturbation as the vector forms:

\displaystyle L_{1}

\displaystyle=L_{11}(f_{1})+L_{12}(f_{1},f_{2}),

\displaystyle L_{2}

\displaystyle=L_{22}(f_{2})+L_{21}(f_{1},f_{2}),

and

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{11}(f_{1})+\Gamma_{12}(f_{1},f_{2}),

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{22}(f_{2})+\Gamma_{21}(f_{1},f_{2}),

then we can write (

) and (

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x})(f_{1},f_{2})&=L(f_{1},f_{2})+\Gamma(f_{1},f_{2}),\end{split}

where

and

We also define the following

-dimensional orthonormal basis:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&E_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{10}}}(\sqrt{\mu_{1}},0),\quad E_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{20}}}(0,\sqrt{\mu_{2}}),\cr&E_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{1}n_{10}+m_{2}n_{20}}}\left(m_{1}v_{i-2}\sqrt{\mu_{1}},m_{2}v_{i-2}\sqrt{\mu_{2}}\right),\quad(i=3,4,5),\cr&E_{6}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6n_{10}+6n_{10}}}\left((m_{1}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{1}},(m_{2}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{2}}\right).\end{split}

We also denote

for

The following is the main result of this section.

Proposition 3.1
.

We have the following dissipation property for the linear operator
L
:

\displaystyle\langle L(f_{1},f_{2}),(f_{1},f_{2})\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq-(n_{10}+n_{20})\Big(\max\{\delta,\omega\}\|(I-P_{1},I-P_{2})(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\min\left\{(1-\delta),(1-\omega)\right\}\|(I-P)(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\Big).

Proof.

By an explicit computation, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle L(f_{1},f_{2})&,(f_{1},f_{2})\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}=\langle L_{1}f_{1},f_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle L_{2}f_{2},f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\cr&=-(n_{10}+n_{20})\|(I-P_{1},I-P_{2})(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle L_{12}^{2},f_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle L_{21}^{2},f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.\end{split}

We decompose the proof in the following 4-steps.

(
Step 1:
) We consider the dissipation from the momentum and temperature interchange part of the inter-species linearized relaxation operator. We claim that

\displaystyle\langle L_{12}^{2},f_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}+\langle L_{21}^{2},f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq 0,

and the equality holds if and only if

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{10}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{1}v\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv=\frac{1}{n_{20}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{2}v\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv,

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{10}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{1}(m_{1}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv=\frac{1}{n_{20}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{2}(m_{2}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv.

\bullet
Proof of the claim:
By the definition of

\displaystyle\langle L_{12}^{2},f_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle=(1-\delta)\sum_{2\leq i\leq 4}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}}\langle f_{2},e_{2i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{1},e_{1i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)\langle f_{1},e_{1i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}n_{20}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\omega)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\langle f_{2},e_{25}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{1},e_{15}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)\langle f_{1},e_{15}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}n_{20}

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\langle L_{21}^{2},f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}(1-\delta)\sum_{2\leq i\leq 4}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{20}}{n_{10}}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}}\langle f_{1},e_{1i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{2},e_{2i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)\langle f_{2},e_{2i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}n_{10}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}(1-\omega)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{20}}{n_{10}}}\langle f_{1},e_{15}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{2},e_{25}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)\langle f_{2},e_{25}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}n_{10}

\displaystyle=I_{3}+I_{4}.

By an explicit computation, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{1}+I_{3}&=-(1-\delta)n_{20}\sum_{2\leq i\leq 4}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}}\langle f_{2},e_{2i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{1},e_{1i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)^{2}\cr&=-(1-\delta)m_{1}n_{10}n_{20}\left(\frac{1}{n_{20}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{2}v\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv-\frac{1}{n_{10}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{1}v\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv\right)^{2}\leq 0,\end{split}

and

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2}&+I_{4}=-(1-\omega)n_{20}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\langle f_{2},e_{25}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{1},e_{15}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)^{2}\cr&=-(1-\omega)\frac{n_{10}n_{20}}{6}\left(\frac{1}{n_{20}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{2}(m_{2}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv-\frac{1}{n_{10}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{1}(m_{1}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv\right)^{2}\cr&\leq 0.\end{split}

which proves the claim of this step.

(
Step 2:
) To estimate the gap of the macroscopic projection
(P_{1},P_{2})
with

, we compute the following term:

\displaystyle\|(P_{1},P_{2})(f_{1},f_{2})-P(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

We note that the element of
(P_{1},P_{2})(f_{1},f_{2})
can be written as the linear combination of the following
10
-dimensional basis:

\displaystyle\{(\sqrt{\mu_{1}},0),(0,\sqrt{\mu_{2}}),(v\sqrt{\mu_{1}},0),(0,v\sqrt{\mu_{2}}),\left(|v|^{2}\sqrt{\mu_{1}},0\right),\left(0,|v|^{2}\sqrt{\mu_{2}}\right)\}

so that

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|(P_{1},P_{2})(f_{1},f_{2})-P(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}=\|(P_{1},P_{2})(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}-\|P(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

Since we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|P_{k}f_{k}|^{2}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n_{k0}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{k}\sqrt{\mu_{k}}dv\right)^{2}+\frac{m_{k}}{n_{k0}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{k}v\sqrt{\mu_{k}}dv\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{6n_{k0}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{k}(m_{k}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{k}}dv\right)^{2},

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|P(f_{1},f_{2})|^{2}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n_{10}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{1}\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{n_{20}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{m_{1}n_{10}+m_{2}n_{20}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{1}m_{1}v\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{2}m_{2}v\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{6n_{10}+6n_{20}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(m_{1}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(m_{2}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv\right)^{2},

which follows directly from explicit computations, we can write

\displaystyle\|(P_{1}f_{1},P_{2}f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}-\|P(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}=II_{1}+II_{2},

where

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}II_{1}&=\frac{1}{m_{1}n_{10}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{1}m_{1}v\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{m_{2}n_{20}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{2}m_{2}v\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv\right)^{2}\cr&\quad-\frac{1}{m_{1}n_{10}+m_{2}n_{20}}\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{1}m_{1}v\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{2}m_{2}v\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv\right)^{2}\right)\\

and

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}II_{2}&=\frac{1}{6n_{10}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{1}(m_{1}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{6n_{20}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{2}(m_{2}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv\right)^{2}\cr&\quad-\frac{1}{6n_{10}+6n_{20}}\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(m_{1}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(m_{2}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv\right)^{2}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{6n_{10}+6n_{20}}\left[\sqrt{\frac{n_{20}}{n_{10}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{1}(m_{1}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv-\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{2}(m_{2}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv\right]^{2}.\end{split}

(
Step 3:
) In this step, we compare
\langle L_{12}^{2},f_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle L_{21}^{2},f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}
with

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle L_{12}^{2},f_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle L_{21}^{2},f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}&\leq-\min\left\{(1-\delta),(1-\omega)\right\}(n_{10}+n_{20})\cr&\quad\times\left(\|(P_{1},P_{2})(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}-\|P(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\right).\end{split}

which is equivalent to

(3.8)

\displaystyle(n_{10}+n_{20})\left(II_{1}+II_{2}\right)\leq-\max\left\{\frac{1}{1-\delta},\frac{1}{1-\omega}\right\}\left[(I_{1}+I_{3})+(I_{2}+I_{4})\right],

where
I_{i}~(i=1,2,3,4)
are defined in Step 1, and
II_{i}~(i=1,2)
are defined in (
3.5
) and (
3.6
).
We first compare
II_{2}
with
I_{2}+I_{4}
. Multiplying
(n_{10}+n_{20})
on (

) yields

|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N
. For sufficiently small
\mathcal{E}(t)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma_{ij},g\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|\leq|\alpha|}\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{\beta}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v}},

for
(i,j)=(1,2)
or
(2,1)
.

Proof.

We only consider the
\Gamma_{12}
since the estimate of
\Gamma_{21}
is similar.
Therefore, we focus on the estimates of the nonlinear terms
\Gamma_{12}
and
\Gamma_{21}
. For convenience, we divide
\Gamma_{12}
into three parts:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{12}=\Gamma_{12A}+\Gamma_{12B}+\Gamma_{12C},

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{12A}

\displaystyle=(n_{2}-n_{20})(P_{1}f_{1}-f_{1}),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{12B}

\displaystyle=n_{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{1}}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{M}_{12}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)(1-\theta)d\theta,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{12C}

\displaystyle=(n_{2}-n_{20})\bigg[(1-\delta)\sum_{2\leq i\leq 4}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}}\langle f_{2},e_{2i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{1},e_{1i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)e_{1i}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\omega)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\langle f_{2},e_{25}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle f_{1},e_{15}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)e_{15}\bigg].

We first write
\Gamma_{12B}
in a concise form before we delve into the estimate. For this, compute applying the chain rule twice on
\mathcal{M}_{ij}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{ij}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{d}{d\theta}\bigg(\frac{dn_{\theta 1}}{d\theta}\frac{d\mathcal{M}_{ij}}{dn_{\theta 1}}+\frac{d(n_{\theta 1}U_{\theta 1})}{d\theta}\frac{d\mathcal{M}_{ij}}{d(n_{\theta 1}U_{\theta 1})}+\frac{dG_{\theta 1}}{d\theta}\frac{d\mathcal{M}_{ij}}{dG_{\theta 1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{dn_{\theta 2}}{d\theta}\frac{d\mathcal{M}_{ij}}{dn_{\theta 2}}+\frac{d(n_{\theta 2}U_{\theta 2})}{d\theta}\frac{d\mathcal{M}_{ij}}{d(n_{\theta 2}U_{\theta 2})}+\frac{dG_{\theta 2}}{d\theta}\frac{d\mathcal{M}_{ij}}{dG_{\theta 2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad=(n_{1}-n_{10},n_{1}U_{1},G_{1},n_{2}-n_{20},n_{2}U_{2},G_{2})^{T}\left\{\nabla^{2}_{(n_{1\theta},n_{1\theta}U_{1\theta},G_{1\theta},n_{2\theta},n_{2\theta}U_{2\theta},G_{2\theta})}\mathcal{M}_{ij}(\theta)\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\times(n_{1}-n_{10},n_{1}U_{1},G_{1},n_{2}-n_{20},n_{2}U_{2},G_{2}).

Therefore, if we define

(4.3)

\displaystyle H_{k}=(n_{k},n_{k}U_{k},G_{k}),\quad\textit{and}\quad H_{k\theta}=(n_{k\theta},n_{k\theta}U_{k\theta},G_{k\theta}),

we can rewrite
\Gamma_{12B}
as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{12B}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{2}}{\sqrt{\mu_{1}}}(H_{1}-H_{10},H_{2}-H_{20})^{T}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{0}^{1}\left\{\nabla^{2}_{(H_{1\theta},H_{2\theta})}\mathcal{M}_{ij}(\theta)\right\}(1-\theta)d\theta(H_{1}-H_{10},H_{2}-H_{20}).

Now we estimate each part of
\Gamma_{12}
.

\bullet

Estimate of
\Gamma_{12A}
:
We take a derivative
\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}
on
\Gamma_{12A}
:

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma_{12A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha}C_{\alpha_{1}}\partial^{\alpha_{1}}(n_{2}-n_{20})\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{\beta}(P_{1}f_{1}-f_{1}).

From (
2.12
), we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}(n_{2}-n_{20})\leq C\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}.

For an estimate of the macroscopic projection
P_{1}f_{1}
, since
\partial_{\beta}e_{1i}
has an exponential decay, we get

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}P_{1}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}=\|\partial_{\beta}P_{1}\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq C_{\beta}\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}.

Thus we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}(P_{1}f_{1}-f_{1}),g\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq C\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v}}.

Combining (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\beta}^{\alpha}\Gamma_{12A},g\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|+|\beta|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}+\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{\beta}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v}}.

\bullet

Estimate of
\Gamma_{12C}
:
We take a derivative
\partial_{\beta}^{\alpha}
on
\Gamma_{12C}
:

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma_{12C}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha}C_{\alpha_{1}}\partial^{\alpha_{1}}(n_{2}-n_{20})

\displaystyle\quad\times\bigg[(1-\delta)\sum_{2\leq i\leq 4}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}}\langle\partial^{\alpha_{2}}f_{2},e_{2i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle\partial^{\alpha_{2}}f_{1},e_{1i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)\partial_{\beta}e_{1i}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\omega)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\langle\partial^{\alpha_{2}}f_{2},e_{25}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle\partial^{\alpha_{2}}f_{1},e_{15}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)\partial_{\beta}e_{15}\bigg].

Since each
e_{1i}
and
e_{2i}
has exponential decay for
i=1,\cdots,5
, we can have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\langle\partial^{\alpha}f_{1},e_{1i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq C\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}},\qquad\langle\partial^{\alpha}f_{2},e_{2i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq C\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{v}},

and

(4.7)

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\beta}e_{1i},g\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\qquad\langle\partial_{\beta}e_{2i},g\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v}}.

Thus by using (
4.4
), (
4.6
), and (
4.7
), we get

\displaystyle\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma_{12C},g\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|\leq|\alpha|}\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v}}.

\bullet

Estimate of
\Gamma_{12B}
:
Taking
\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}
on
\Gamma_{12B}
gives

(4.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma_{12B}&=\sum_{\sum\alpha_{i}=\alpha}C_{\alpha_{i}}\partial^{\alpha_{0}}n_{2}\partial^{\alpha_{1}}(H_{1}-H_{10},H_{2}-H_{20})^{T}\cr&\times\int_{0}^{1}\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{\beta}\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{1}}}\nabla^{2}_{(H_{1\theta},H_{2\theta})}\mathcal{M}_{12}(\theta)\right\}(1-\theta)d\theta\partial^{\alpha_{3}}(H_{1}-H_{10},H_{2}-H_{20}).\end{split}

By the definition of
H_{k}
in (
4.3
), applying (
2.7
) yields

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}(H_{k}-H_{k0})

\displaystyle=\partial^{\alpha}(n_{k}-n_{k0},n_{k}U_{k},G_{k})=\left(\langle\partial^{\alpha}f_{k},e_{k1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}},\cdots,\langle\partial^{\alpha}f_{k},e_{k5}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right),

for
k=1,2
.
Thus we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}(H_{k}-H_{k0})|\leq C\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}.

For notational simplicity, we set

\displaystyle A_{lm}=\int_{0}^{1}\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{\beta}\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{1}}}\nabla^{2}_{(H_{1\theta},H_{2\theta})}\mathcal{M}_{12}(\theta)\right\}_{l,m}(1-\theta)d\theta.

Then by Lemma
4.3
, we can write it as

(4.10)

\displaystyle A_{lm}=\int_{0}^{1}\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{\beta}\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{1}}}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{lm}(n_{1\theta},n_{2\theta},U_{1\theta},U_{2\theta},T_{1\theta},T_{2\theta},v-U_{12\theta})}{n_{1\theta}^{\lambda}n_{2\theta}^{\nu}T_{12\theta}^{\xi}}\mathcal{M}_{12}(\theta)\right\}(1-\theta)d\theta.

By the product rule, we have

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\left\{\frac{\mathcal{P}_{lm}(n_{1\theta},n_{2\theta},U_{1\theta},U_{2\theta},T_{1\theta},T_{2\theta},v-U_{12\theta})}{n_{1\theta}^{\lambda}n_{2\theta}^{\nu}T_{12\theta}^{\xi}}\right\}

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}\sum_{\sum\alpha_{i}=\alpha}\bigg\{\mathcal{P}_{lm}(\partial^{\alpha_{1}}n_{1\theta},\partial^{\alpha_{2}}n_{2\theta},\partial^{\alpha_{3}}U_{1\theta},\partial^{\alpha_{4}}U_{2\theta},\partial^{\alpha_{5}}T_{1\theta},\partial^{\alpha_{6}}T_{2\theta},\partial^{\alpha_{7}}_{\beta}(v-U_{12\theta}))

\displaystyle\quad\times\partial^{\alpha_{8}}\frac{1}{n_{1\theta}^{\lambda}n_{2\theta}^{\nu}T_{12\theta}^{\xi}}\bigg\}

If
|\alpha_{i}|\leq N-2
, then by Sobolev embedding
H^{2}\subset\subset L^{\infty}
and Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}n_{k\theta}(x,t)|+|\partial^{\alpha}U_{k\theta}(x,t)|+|\partial^{\alpha}T_{k\theta}(x,t)|\leq C\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}.

Since
N\geq 3
, there is at most one
\alpha_{i}
that exceeds
N-2
. Thus, for sufficiently small
\mathcal{E}(t)
, we have

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\left\{\frac{\mathcal{P}_{lm}(n_{1\theta},n_{2\theta},U_{1\theta},U_{2\theta},T_{1\theta},T_{2\theta},v-U_{12\theta})}{n_{1\theta}^{\lambda}n_{2\theta}^{\nu}T_{12\theta}^{\xi}}\right\}\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\mathcal{P}_{lm}(v).

Substituting it in (
4.10
) yields

\displaystyle A_{lm}\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\mathcal{P}_{lm}(v)\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\exp\left(-\frac{|v-U_{12\theta}|^{2}}{2\frac{T_{12\theta}}{m_{1}}}+\frac{m_{1}|v|^{2}}{4}\right).

Similarly, the derivative of the exponential part can be bounded as follows:

\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\exp\left(-\frac{|v-U_{12\theta}|^{2}}{2\frac{T_{12\theta}}{m_{1}}}+\frac{m_{1}|v|^{2}}{4}\right)\cr\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\mathcal{P}_{lm}(v)\exp\left(-\frac{|v-U_{12\theta}|^{2}}{2\frac{T_{12\theta}}{m_{1}}}+\frac{m_{1}|v|^{2}}{4}\right).

By Lemma
4.1
(3), a sufficiently small
\mathcal{E}(t)
guarantees
T_{12\theta}\leq 3/2
, so that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle A_{lm},g\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}&\leq C\left\|P(v)\exp\left(-\frac{2m_{1}|v-U_{12\theta}|^{2}}{3}+\frac{m_{1}|v|^{2}}{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\cr&\leq C\left\|P(v)\exp\left(-\frac{m_{1}|v-4U_{12\theta}|^{2}}{6}+2m_{1}|U_{12\theta}|^{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\cr&\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v}},\end{split}

where we used
e^{2m_{1}|U_{12\theta}|^{2}}\leq C
for sufficiently small
\mathcal{E}(t)
. Substituting (
4.9
) and (
4.11
) on (
4.8
) gives the desired result.
∎

4.3.
Local existence

In this part, we prove the existence of a local-in-time classical solution of the mixture BGK model (
1.1
).

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
F_{10}=\mu_{1}+\sqrt{\mu_{1}}f_{10}\geq 0
and
F_{20}=\mu_{2}+\sqrt{\mu_{2}}f_{20}\geq 0
. There exists
T_{*}>0
and
M_{0}>0
such that if
\mathcal{E}(0)\leq\frac{M_{0}}{2}
, then there exists a unique local-in-time solution
(F_{1},F_{2})
of (
1.1
) such that

(1)

The distribution functions
F_{1}(x,v,t)
and
F_{2}(x,v,t)
are non-negative.

(2)

The high-order energy
\mathcal{E}(t)
is uniformly bounded:

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{*}}\mathcal{E}(t)\leq M_{0}.

(3)

The high-order energy is continuous in
t\in[0,T_{*})
.

(4)

The conservation laws (
2.17
) hold for all
t\in[0,T_{*})
.

Proof.

We define an iteration of the mixture BGK model (
1.1
) as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}F_{1}^{n+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F_{1}^{n+1}&=n_{1}(F_{1}^{n})(\mathcal{M}_{11}(F_{1}^{n})-F_{1}^{n+1})\cr&\quad+n_{2}(F_{2}^{n})(\mathcal{M}_{12}(F_{1}^{n},F_{2}^{n})-F_{1}^{n+1}),\cr\partial_{t}F_{2}^{n+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F_{2}^{n+1}&=n_{2}(F_{2}^{n})(\mathcal{M}_{22}(F_{2}^{n})-F_{2}^{n+1})\cr&\quad+n_{1}(F_{1}^{n})(\mathcal{M}_{21}(F_{1}^{n},F_{2}^{n})-F_{2}^{n+1}),\end{aligned}

and
F_{1}^{n+1}(x,v,0)=F_{10}(x,v)
and
F_{2}^{n+1}(x,v,0)=F_{20}(x,v)
for all
n\geq 0
. We start the iteration with
F_{1}^{0}(x,v,t)=F_{10}(x,v)
and
F_{2}^{0}(x,v,t)=F_{20}(x,v)
.

We split
F_{1}^{n}=\mu_{1}+\sqrt{\mu_{1}}f_{1}^{n}
, and
F_{2}^{n}=\mu_{2}+\sqrt{\mu_{2}}f_{2}^{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and use the linearization of the Maxwellian given in Proposition
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
to get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{1}^{n+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=(n_{10}+n_{20})(P_{1}f_{1}^{n}-f_{1}^{n+1})+L_{12}^{2}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n})+\Gamma_{11}(f_{1}^{n})+\Gamma_{12}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n}),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{2}^{n+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=(n_{10}+n_{20})(P_{2}f_{2}^{n}-f_{2}^{n+1})+L_{21}^{2}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n})+\Gamma_{22}(f_{2}^{n})+\Gamma_{21}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n}).

Then the local existence can be constructed by the standard argument as in
[
39
]
.
The key ingredient is the uniform control of the high-order energy norm in each iteration step. So we only prove the following auxiliary lemma below.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{E}(0)<\frac{M_{0}}{2}
. Then there exists
T_{*}>0
and
M_{0}>0
such that
\mathcal{E}(f^{n}(t))<M_{0}
for all
n\geq 0
and
t\in[0,T_{*}].

Proof.

We take
\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}
on each side of (
2.13
) and (
2.15
):

\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\partial_{t}f_{1}^{n+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}f_{1}^{n+1}=(n_{10}+n_{20})(\partial_{\beta}P_{1}\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}^{n}-\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1})\cr+\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}L_{12}^{2}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n})+\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma_{11}(f_{1}^{n})+\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma_{12}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n}),

and

\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\partial_{t}f_{2}^{n+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{2}^{n+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}f_{2}^{n+1}=(n_{10}+n_{20})(\partial_{\beta}P_{2}\partial^{\alpha}f_{2}^{n}-\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{2}^{n+1})\cr+\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}L_{21}^{2}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n})+\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma_{22}(f_{2}^{n})+\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma_{21}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n}),

where
k_{1}=(1,0,0)
,
k_{2}=(0,1,0)
,
k_{3}=(0,0,1)
, and
\bar{k}_{1}=(0,1,0,0)
,
\bar{k}_{2}=(0,0,1,0)
,
\bar{k}_{3}=(0,0,0,1)
. We then take the inner product with
\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}
:

(4.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}&+(n_{10}+n_{20})\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}=-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\langle\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}f_{1}^{n+1},\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\cr&\quad+\langle\partial_{\beta}P_{1}\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}^{n},\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}L_{12}^{2}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n}),\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\cr&\quad+\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma_{11}(f_{1}^{n}),\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma_{12}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n}),\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\cr&\quad=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+I_{5}.\end{split}

Applying the Hölder inequality on
I_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\langle\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}f_{1}^{n+1},\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{3}\|\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}f_{1}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

Since
\partial_{\beta}e_{1i}
and
\partial_{\beta}e_{2i}
have exponential decay,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\beta}P_{1}\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}^{n}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq C_{\beta}\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}^{n}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

Thus Young’s inequality implies

\displaystyle I_{2}=\langle\partial_{\beta}P_{1}\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}^{n},\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}^{n}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+C\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

To estimate
I_{3}
, we take
\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}
on
L_{12}^{2}
:

\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}L_{12}^{2}(f_{1},f_{2})=n_{20}\bigg[(1-\delta)\sum_{2\leq i\leq 4}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\sqrt{\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}}\langle\partial^{\alpha}f_{2},e_{2i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle\partial^{\alpha}f_{1},e_{1i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)\partial_{\beta}e_{1i}\cr+(1-\omega)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{10}}{n_{20}}}\langle\partial^{\alpha}f_{2},e_{25}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle\partial^{\alpha}f_{1},e_{15}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)\partial_{\beta}e_{15}\bigg],

and apply the Hölder inequality:

\displaystyle I_{3}=\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}L_{12}^{2}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n}),\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{2}^{n}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}+C\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}^{n}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\right)\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

Since
I_{4}
and
I_{5}
are similar, we only consider
I_{5}
. Applying Lemma
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle I_{5}=\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma_{12}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n}),\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|\leq|\alpha|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n})\|_{L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n})\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}dx.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
|\alpha_{1}|\leq|\alpha_{2}|
. Then the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}\subset\subset L^{\infty}
implies

\displaystyle I_{5}

\displaystyle=\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma_{12}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n}),\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|\leq|\alpha|}\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}(f_{1}^{n},f_{2}^{n})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\bigg)^{2}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

Combining the estimate from
I_{1}
to
I_{5}
, and taking
\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N}
on (
4.12
), we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N}&\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+(n_{10}+n_{20})\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\cr&\leq C\mathcal{E}^{n}(t)+C\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)+C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{n}(t)}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)}+C\mathcal{E}^{n}(t)\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)}.\end{split}

Similarly,

(4.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N}&\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{2}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N}(n_{10}+n_{20})\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{2}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\cr&\leq C\mathcal{E}^{n}(t)+C\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)+C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{n}(t)}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)}+C\mathcal{E}^{n}(t)\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)}.\end{split}

Combining (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)+(n_{10}+n_{20})\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{E}^{n}(t)+C\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{n}(t)}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)}+C\mathcal{E}^{n}(t)\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)}.

We integrate in time to get

(4.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{n+1}&(t)\leq\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(0)\cr&+\int_{0}^{t}\left(C\mathcal{E}^{n}(s)+C\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(s)+C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{n}(t)}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)}+C\mathcal{E}^{n}(t)\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)}\right)ds.\end{split}

We now apply an induction argument. We have
\mathcal{E}^{0}(0)<\frac{M_{0}}{2}
from the assumption. Assume we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{n}(t)\leq M_{0},\quad\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(0)\leq M_{0}/2.

Then, from (
4.15
), we see that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M_{0}}{2}+CT_{*}M_{0}+CT_{*}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+CT_{*}\sqrt{M_{0}}\sqrt{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)}+CT_{*}M_{0}\sqrt{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)}.

By using Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle(1-3CT_{*})\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{*}}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{n+1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M_{0}}{2}+2CT_{*}M_{0}+CT_{*}M_{0}^{2}.

Therefore, for sufficiently small
T_{*}
and
M_{0}>0
, we can derive

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{*}}\mathcal{E}^{n+1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq M_{0}.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Coercivity estimate

We write the macroscopic part
P(f_{1},f_{2})
of the distribution function
(f_{1},f_{2})
as

\displaystyle P(f_{1},f_{2})

\displaystyle=a_{1}(x,t)\left(\sqrt{\mu_{1}},0\right)+a_{2}(x,t)\left(0,\sqrt{\mu_{2}}\right)+b(x,t)\cdot v\left(m_{1}\sqrt{\mu_{1}},m_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+c(x,t)|v|^{2}\left(m_{1}\sqrt{\mu_{1}},m_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{2}}\right),

where

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{k}(x,t)&=\frac{1}{n_{k0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{k}\sqrt{\mu_{k}}dv\cr&\quad-\frac{1}{2n_{10}+2n_{20}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{1}(m_{1}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{2}(m_{2}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv\right),\cr b(x,t)&=\frac{1}{m_{1}n_{10}+m_{2}n_{20}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{1}m_{1}v\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{2}m_{2}v\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv\right),\cr c(x,t)&=\frac{1}{6n_{10}+6n_{20}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{1}(m_{1}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{1}}dv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{2}(m_{2}|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu_{2}}dv\right),\end{split}

for
k=1,2
.
We substitute

(f_{1},f_{2})=(I-P)(f_{1},f_{2})+P(f_{1},f_{2}),

into (
3.1
) to get

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\}P(f_{1},f_{2})&=-\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}-L\}(I-P)(f_{1},f_{2})\cr&\quad+(\Gamma_{11}(f_{1})+\Gamma_{12}(f_{1},f_{2}),\Gamma_{22}(f_{2})+\Gamma_{21}(f_{1},f_{2})).\end{split}

We write L.H.S. of (
5.2
) in the following form:

\bigg\{\left(\partial_{t}a_{1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}a_{1}\right)(\sqrt{\mu_{1}},0)+\left(\partial_{t}a_{2}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}a_{2}\right)(0,\sqrt{\mu_{2}})\cr+v\cdot\partial_{t}b(m_{1}\sqrt{\mu_{1}},m_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{2}})+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}v_{i}v_{j}(\partial_{x_{i}}b_{j}+\partial_{x_{j}}b_{i})(m_{1}\sqrt{\mu_{1}},m_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{2}})\cr+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}(\partial_{x_{i}}b_{i}+\partial_{t}c)v_{i}^{2}(m_{1}\sqrt{\mu_{1}},m_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{2}})+|v|^{2}v\cdot\nabla_{x}c(m_{1}\sqrt{\mu_{1}},m_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{2}})\bigg\},

as a linear expansion with respect to the following
17
basis:

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\{(\sqrt{\mu_{1}},0),(0,\sqrt{\mu_{2}}),v(\sqrt{\mu_{1}},0)&,v(0,\sqrt{\mu_{2}}),\cr&v_{i}v_{j}(m_{1}\sqrt{\mu_{1}},m_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{2}}),v|v|^{2}(m_{1}\sqrt{\mu_{1}},m_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{2}})\}.\end{split}

Therefore, comparing both sides of (
5.2
), we obtain the following system:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}a_{1}

\displaystyle=l_{a1}+h_{a1},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}a_{2}

\displaystyle=l_{a2}+h_{a2},

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}a_{1}+m_{1}\partial_{t}b_{i}

\displaystyle=l_{b1i}+h_{b1i},

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}a_{2}+m_{2}\partial_{t}b_{i}

\displaystyle=l_{b2i}+h_{b2i},

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}b_{j}+\partial_{x_{j}}b_{i}

\displaystyle=l_{bbi}+h_{bbi},\quad(i\neq j)

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}b_{i}+\partial_{t}c

\displaystyle=l_{bci}+h_{bci},

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}c

\displaystyle=l_{ci}+h_{ci},

where
(l_{a1},l_{a2},l_{b1i},l_{b2i},l_{bbi},l_{bci},l_{ci})
, and
(h_{a1},h_{a2},h_{b1i},h_{b2i},h_{bbi},h_{bci},h_{ci})
are the coefficients corresponding to the expansion of
l
and
h
:

\displaystyle l(f_{1},f_{2})=-\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}-L\}(I-P)(f_{1},f_{2}),

\displaystyle h(f_{1},f_{2})=(\Gamma_{11}(f_{1})+\Gamma_{12}(f_{1},f_{2}),\Gamma_{22}(f_{2})+\Gamma_{21}(f_{1},f_{2})),

with respect to (
5.3
). For brevity, we denote

\displaystyle\tilde{l}

\displaystyle=l_{a1}+l_{a2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(l_{b1i}+l_{b2i}+l_{bbi}+l_{bci}+l_{ci}\right)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}

\displaystyle=h_{a1}+h_{a2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(h_{b1i}+h_{b2i}+h_{bbi}+h_{bci}+h_{ci}\right).

Lemma 5.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}a_{1}(x,t)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}a_{2}(x,t)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}b(x,t)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}c(x,t)dx=0.

Proof.

This follows from the conservation laws (
2.17
) and the definition of
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
b
, and
c
in (
5.1
).
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

[
39
]
Let
0\leq|\alpha|\leq N
with
N\geq 3
, then we have

\|\partial^{\alpha}a_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}a_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}b\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}c\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right).

Proof.

The proof can be found in
[
39
, page 620, Proof of Theorem 3]
. We omit it.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

For sufficiently small energy norm
\mathcal{E}(t)
, we have

\displaystyle(1)\ \sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|(I-P)\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}},

\displaystyle(2)\ \sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq C\sqrt{M}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

Proof.

(1) The proof can be found in
[
39
, page 616, Lemma 7]
. We omit it.

(2) Let us define
\{e_{i}^{*}\}_{i=1}^{17}
be the orthonormal basis corresponding to the basis (
5.3
). Then we can write

\displaystyle e_{i}^{*}=\sum_{j=1}^{17}C_{ij}e_{j},\qquad h(f_{1},f_{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{17}\langle h,e_{i}^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}e_{i}^{*},

so that

\displaystyle\langle h,e_{n}^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 17}C_{ij}C_{ni}\langle h,e_{i}^{*}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}},

for
n=1,\cdots,17
. For the estimate of
h
, we compute

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int\partial^{\alpha}h(f_{1},f_{2})e_{i}^{*}dv\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\|\int\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma_{11}(f_{1})(|v|^{k}\sqrt{\mu_{1}})dv\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\bigg\|\int\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma_{12}(f_{1},f_{2})(|v|^{k}\sqrt{\mu_{1}})dv\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle+\bigg\|\int\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma_{22}(f_{2})(|v|^{k}\sqrt{\mu_{2}})dv\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\bigg\|\int\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma_{21}(f_{1},f_{2})(|v|^{k}\sqrt{\mu_{2}})dv\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

for
k=0,1,2,3
. For sufficiently small
\mathcal{E}(t)
, by Lemma
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma_{mm}(f_{m})|v|^{k}\sqrt{\mu_{m}}dv\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|\leq|\alpha|}\bigg\|\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f_{m}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}f_{m}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Similarly, we have from Lemma
4.5

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma_{lm}(f_{l},f_{m})|v|^{k}\sqrt{\mu_{l}}dv\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|\leq|\alpha|}\bigg\|\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}(f_{l},f_{m})\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}(f_{l},f_{m})\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

for
l\neq m
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
|\alpha_{1}|\leq|\alpha_{2}|
and apply the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}\subset\subset L^{\infty}
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|\leq|\alpha_{2}|}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}}\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\sum_{|\alpha_{2}|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}},

which gives desired result.
∎

We are now ready to derive the full coercivity estimate. By Lemma
5.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}P(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}a_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\|\partial^{\alpha}a_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\|\partial^{\alpha}b\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\|\partial^{\alpha}c\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\right).

We then apply Lemma
5.3
to get

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}P(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|(I-P)\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+C\sqrt{M}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

Adding
\displaystyle{\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|(I-P)\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}}
on each side, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\leq\frac{C+1}{1-C\sqrt{M}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|(I-P)(\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2}))\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

Combining it with the estimate in Proposition
3.1
, we derive the following full coercivity estimate

(5.4)

\displaystyle\langle L\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2}),\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2})\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq-\eta\min\left\{(1-\delta),(1-\omega)\right\}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2},

when
\mathcal{E}(t)
is sufficiently small.

6.
Global existence

In this section, we extend the local-in-time solution to the global one by establishing a uniform energy estimate. Let
(f_{1},f_{2})
be the classical local-in-time solution constructed in Theorem
4.6
.
We take
\partial^{\alpha}
on (
2.13
) and take inner product with
\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}
in
L^{2}_{x,v}
to have

(6.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}&=\langle\partial^{\alpha}L_{11}(f_{1}),\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle\partial^{\alpha}L_{12}(f_{1},f_{2}),\partial^{\alpha}f_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\cr&\quad+\langle\partial^{\alpha}f_{1},\partial^{\alpha}(\Gamma_{11}+\Gamma_{12})\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.\end{split}

Similarly, we get from (
2.15
) that

(6.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}&=\langle\partial^{\alpha}L_{22}(f_{2}),\partial^{\alpha}f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle\partial^{\alpha}L_{21}(f_{1},f_{2}),\partial^{\alpha}f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\cr&\quad+\langle\partial^{\alpha}f_{2},\partial^{\alpha}(\Gamma_{22}+\Gamma_{21})\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.\end{split}

Combining (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1,2}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle L\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2}),\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2})\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\quad+\langle\partial^{\alpha}f_{1},\partial^{\alpha}(\Gamma_{11}+\Gamma_{12})\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle\partial^{\alpha}f_{2},\partial^{\alpha}(\Gamma_{22}+\Gamma_{21})\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

Then the first term of the R.H.S is controlled by the full coercivity estimate (
5.4
), and the nonlinear terms on the second line are estimated by Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
:

\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\sum_{k=1,2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\eta\min\left\{(1-\delta),(1-\omega)\right\}\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\right)\cr\leq C_{0}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},0}(t)}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

For
M_{0}
satisfying Theorem
4.6
and (
5.4
), we define

\displaystyle M=\left\{\frac{M_{0}}{2},\frac{\eta^{2}\min\left\{(1-\delta)^{2},(1-\omega)^{2}\right\}}{4C_{0}^{2}}\right\},\qquad T=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}\{t~|~\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},0}(t)\leq 2M\}>0.

We restrict our initial data to satisfy the following energy bound:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},0}(0)\leq M\leq 2M_{0}.

Once we define

\displaystyle y(t)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\sum_{k=1,2}\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2},

then
y(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(t)+2\eta\min\left\{(1-\delta),(1-\omega)\right\}y(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{0}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},0}(t)}y(t)

\displaystyle\leq\eta\min\left\{(1-\delta),(1-\omega)\right\}y(t).

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle y(t)\leq e^{-\eta\min\left\{(1-\delta),(1-\omega)\right\}t}y(0)\leq y(0)\leq M<2M,

and which is possible only when
T=\infty
. Note that this also gives

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t))\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\leq e^{-\eta\min\left\{(1-\delta),(1-\omega)\right\}t}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0))\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

Now we consider the general case of
f
having momentum derivatives. Taking
\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}
on (
2.13
) and (
2.15
) and applying an inner product with
\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}
and
\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{2}
, respectively, we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}&+(n_{10}+n_{20})\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}=-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\langle\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}f_{1},\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\cr&\quad+(n_{10}+n_{20})\langle\partial_{\beta}P_{1}\partial^{\alpha}f_{1},\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}L_{12}^{2}(f_{1},f_{2}),\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\cr&\quad+\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}(\Gamma_{11}(f_{1})+\Gamma_{12}(f_{1},f_{2})),\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}},\end{split}

and

(6.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}&+(n_{10}+n_{20})\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}=-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\langle\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}f_{2},\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\cr&\quad+(n_{10}+n_{20})\langle\partial_{\beta}P_{2}\partial^{\alpha}f_{2},\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}L_{21}^{2}(f_{1},f_{2}),\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\cr&\quad+\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}(\Gamma_{22}(f_{2})+\Gamma_{21}(f_{1},f_{2})),\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.\end{split}

Combining (
6.3
) and (
6.4
), and applying the Hölder inequality and Young’s inequality, we can obtain

\sum_{k=1,2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+(n_{10}+n_{20}-2\epsilon)\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\right)\cr\leq\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\sum_{k=1,2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\|\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\frac{C}{2\epsilon}\sum_{k=1,2}\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+C\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},|\beta|}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t),

for some positive constant
\epsilon
satisfying
(n_{10}+n_{20})/2>\epsilon>0
. We sum this over
|\beta|=m+1
and multiply both sides with
\epsilon\eta_{m}
:

\sum_{|\beta|=m+1}\left[\sum_{k=1,2}\left(\frac{\epsilon\eta_{m}}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\epsilon\eta_{m}(n_{10}+n_{20}-2\epsilon)\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\right)\right]\cr\leq\sum_{|\beta|=m+1}\left[\frac{\eta_{m}}{2}\sum_{k=1,2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\|\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\frac{C\eta_{m}}{2}\sum_{k=1,2}\|\partial^{\alpha}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+C\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},|\beta|}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)\right].

Combining the previous cases
|\beta|\leq m
, the R.H.S of the inequality can be bounded by the energy
\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},|\beta|}
with
|\beta|\leq m
and
\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},0}
. Thus, we can conclude from induction that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N\cr|\beta|\leq m+1\end{subarray}}\sum_{k=1,2}\left(C_{m+1}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\eta_{m+1}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\right)\leq C_{m+1}^{*}\mathcal{E}_{N_{1},|\beta|}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t).

Applying the same continuity argument as to when
\beta=0
, we can construct the global-in-time classical solution. We mention that when
|\beta|=0
, the parameter
\eta_{0}
depends on
1-\delta
and
1-\omega
, and
C_{0}=1/2
. But when
|\beta|\geq 1
, both
C_{m+1}
and
\eta_{m+1}
depend on the parameter
\eta_{m}
. That is why we cannot extract a decay rate depending explicitly on the parameter
\delta
and
\omega
when the velocity derivatives are involved.
For the uniqueness of the solution and
L^{2}
stability, we can follow the standard arguments in
[
38
,
39
,
40
,
71
]
. This completes the proof.

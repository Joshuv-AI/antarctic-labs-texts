Comment on “First-order methods almost always avoid strict saddle points” Thanks: This research was supported by the Beijing Natural Science Foundation under grant Z180005, and the National Natural Science Foundation of China under grants 12171021 and 11822103.
Jinyang Zheng, Yong Xia

Abstract
The analysis on the global stability of Riemannian gradient descent method in manifold optimization (i.e., it avoids strict saddle points for almost all initializations) due to Lee et al. (Math. Program. 176:311-337) is corrected. Moreover, an explicit condition on the step-size is presented by the newly introduced retraction L-smooth property.



∎

1
Introduction

Based on results from dynamic system
shub13
,
Lee et al.
lee19
first established the global stability of first-order methods for unconstrained optimization, i.e., they can avoid saddle points for almost all initializations. More precisely,
the first-order iteration method
x_{k+1}=g(x_{k})
almost converges to minimizers under the assumptions that
g(x)
is a local diffeomorphsim and the derivative
Dg(x)
has a negative eigenvalue at every strict saddle points. Mathematically, we have

Theorem 1.1
(
lee19
)

Given a
d
-dimensional manifold
\mathcal{X}
, and a
C^{1}
mapping
g:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
, the set of initial points that converge to an unstable fixed point has measure zero,

\mu\left(\left\{x_{0}:~\lim x_{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}\right\}\right)=0
, if
\operatorname{det}(\mathrm{D}g(x))\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
,

where
\operatorname{det}(\mathrm{D}g(x))
is the determinant of the matrix representing
\mathrm{D}g(x)
with respect to an arbitrary basis, then
\operatorname{det}(\mathrm{D}g(x))\neq 0
if and only if
g(x)
is a local diffeomorphsim, the set of unstable fixed points
\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}
is defined as

\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}=\left\{x:~g(x)=x,~\max_{i}\left|\lambda_{i}(Dg(x))\right|>1\right\},

and
\lambda_{i}(\cdot)
returns the
i
-th largest eigenvalue of matrix
(\cdot)
.

As a natural extension, for the problem of minimizing
f(x)
on the manifold
\mathcal{M}
, Lee et al.
(
lee19
, section 5.5)
established the global stability of
the Riemannian gradient method
absil12
with projection-like retraction reads as:

x_{k+1}=g(x_{k})\triangleq P_{\mathcal{M}}\left(x_{k}-\alpha P_{Tx_{k}M}\left(\nabla f\left(x_{k}\right)\right)\right),

(1)

where
\nabla f
is the gradient of function
f
, the orthogonal projection operator
P_{\mathcal{N}}
onto the manifold
\mathcal{N}
is defined by

P_{\mathcal{N}}(x):=\operatorname{argmin}\{\|x-y\|:~y\in\mathcal{N}\}.

The strict saddle assumption in manifold version requires that Riemannian Hessian has at least one negative eigenvalue. Following the proof of Theorem
1.1
, Lee et al.
lee19
verified the unstability of the strict saddle points when calling the iteration (
1
) by the following two key results.

Proposition 1

(
(
lee19
, Prop. 8)
)

At a strict saddle point
x^{*}
,
Dg(x^{*})
has an eigenvalue of magnitude larger than 1.

Proposition 2

(
(
lee19
, Prop. 9)
)

For a compact submanifold
\mathcal{M}
, there is a strictly positive
\alpha
such that
\operatorname{det}(\mathrm{D}g(x))\neq 0
.

We point out in this note that the proof of Proposition
2
due to Lee et al.
lee19
is incorrect. In fact, we need additional assumptions in Proposition
2
. Our goal is to provide such a reasonable assumption together with a correct proof. As a by-product, we can establish an explicit bound of the step-size
\alpha
for the globally stable iteration (
1
).

.

\lambda_{i}(A)

the

A
are denoted by
\sigma_{\max}(A)
and
\sigma_{\min}(A)
, respectively. Then
\sigma_{1}(A)=\sigma_{\max}(A)
, which is equal to the spectral norm
\|A\|
.
\sigma_{\min}(A)=\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(A^{T}A)}
. For a vector
a
,
\|a\|
returns the Euclidean norm.

Correction

2.1
Incorrectness

Notice that

\mathrm{D}g(x)=\mathrm{D}P_{\mathcal{M}}\left(x-\alpha P_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}\nabla f(x)\right)\left(I-\alpha\mathrm{D}\left(P_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}\nabla f\right)(x)\right).

In order to prove Proposition
2
,
Lee et al.
lee19
introduced

h_{x}(\alpha)=\operatorname{det}\left(\mathrm{D}g(x)\right),

which is the determinant of the matrix representing
Dg(x)
with respect to a basis.
Let
(U,\phi)
and
(V,\psi)
be charts for
x
and
g(x)
on manifold
\mathcal{M}
, respectively, that is,

\phi:U\subseteq\mathcal{M}\rightarrow R^{{\rm dim}(\mathcal{M})},x\in U,~\psi:V\subseteq\mathcal{M}\rightarrow R^{{\rm dim}(\mathcal{M})},g(x)\in V.

Then we can calculate
\operatorname{det}(Dg)
under differential structures
(U,\phi)
and
(V,\psi)
:

\operatorname{det}(Dg|_{x})=\operatorname{det}(J(\psi\circ g\circ\phi^{-1})|_{\phi(x)}),

(2)

where
J|_{\phi(x)}
denotes Jacobian matrix at
\phi(x)\in R^{{\rm dim}(\mathcal{M})}
.

Lee et al.
lee19
first proved that

h_{x}(0)=\operatorname{det}\left(\mathrm{D}P_{\mathcal{M}}\left(x\right)\right)=\operatorname{det}\left(P_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}\right)=1,

where the second equality holds since
\mathrm{D}P_{\mathcal{M}}\left(x\right)|_{x\in\mathcal{M}}=P_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}
due to Lewis and Malick
(
lewis08
, lemma 4)
.

For any compact smooth manifold
\mathcal{M}
, there is a
r>0
such that
P_{\mathcal{M}}
is unique and smooth in a neighborhood of radius
r

absil12
. Thus, for any
\alpha<r/\max_{x\in\mathcal{M}}\|\nabla f(x)\|
, it holds that
P_{\mathcal{M}}\left(x-\alpha P_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}\nabla f(x)\right)
is differentiable.
As shown by Lee et al.
(
lee19
, Prop.9)
that

h_{x}(\alpha)>0,~\forall\alpha<C_{\mathcal{M},f}\triangleq\min\left(\frac{r}{\max_{x\in\mathcal{M}}\|\nabla f(x)\|},~\frac{1}{B}\right),

(3)

where

B:=\max_{x\in\mathcal{M},\alpha<\frac{r}{\max_{x\in\mathcal{M}}\|\nabla f(x)\|}}\left|D_{\alpha}h_{x}(\alpha)\right|<\infty.

(4)

It implies from (
3
) that
\mathrm{D}g
is invertible, which completes the proof of Proposition
2
.

Lee et al.’s above proof implicitly assumes that
h_{x}(\alpha)
is differentiable for any
\alpha<r/\max_{x\in\mathcal{M}}\|\nabla f(x)\|
.
Unfortunately, this is in general incorrect.
As shown in the following counterexample,

h_{x}(\alpha)
could be even noncontinuous in the feasible interval.

Example 1

Let
\mathcal{M}
be unit sphere
S^{n-1}
. The projection operator is
P_{\mathcal{M}}\left(x\right)=x/||x||
and the projection onto the tangent space of
\mathcal{M}
is
P_{T_{x}M}=I-xx^{T}
.

Choose local parameterizations
(U,\phi)
and
(V,\psi)
such that:

\displaystyle U=S^{n-1}\setminus\{(0,0,\cdots,0,1)\},~V=S^{n-1}\setminus\{(0,0,\cdots,0,-1)\},

\displaystyle\phi(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})=\left(\dfrac{x_{1}}{1-x_{n}},\dfrac{x_{2}}{1-x_{n}},\cdots,\dfrac{x_{n-1}}{1-x_{n}}\right),

\displaystyle\psi(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})=\left(\dfrac{x_{1}}{1+x_{n}},\dfrac{x_{2}}{1+x_{n}},\cdots,\dfrac{x_{n-1}}{1+x_{n}}\right).

It is not difficult to see that

D\phi\neq D\psi.

(5)

Let
x,\alpha_{0}
be such that
x\in U
and
g(x)|_{\alpha=\alpha_{0}}=(0,0,\cdots,0,1)
. Then, for
\alpha<\alpha_{0}
, we have

h_{x}(\alpha)=\operatorname{det}(D\phi Dg(x)D\phi^{-1}).

However, when
\alpha=\alpha_{0}
, we can only use
(V,\psi)
as local parameterization, i.e.,

h_{x}(\alpha_{0})=\operatorname{det}(D\psi Dg(x)D\phi^{-1}).

The discontinuity of
h_{x}(\alpha)
follows from (
5
).
On the other hand, in this case, the radius
r
in (
4
) can be set arbitrarily large. It follows that the constants
B
(
4
) is not well-defined. Consequently, the claim (
3
) is incorrect.

Remark 1

It should be noted that if we always take
(V,\psi)
as the local parameterization in Example
1
, then
h_{x}(\alpha)
becomes differentiable. It remains unknown whether there are proper local parameterizations to guarantee the differentiability of
h_{x}(\alpha)
. Even if the answer is positive, it is difficult to determine step-size and find the correct local parameterizations.

2.2
A corrective proof

The local diffeomorphism (embedding) between manifolds is an
immersion
, which is sufficient to check the non-singularity of
Dg(x)
.
The basic idea of our correction in showing that
g(x)
is immersion is to verify
\operatorname{rank}(Dg(x))=\operatorname{dim}\mathcal{M}
(or
\sigma_{\min}(Dg(x)|_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}})>0
) rather than
\det(Dg(x))\neq 0
, though they are equivalent.

Notice that

J(\psi\circ g\circ\phi^{-1})|_{\phi(x)}=J(\psi)|_{g(x)}J(g(x))J(\phi^{-1})|_{\phi(x)},

where
J(\phi^{-1}):\mathbb{R}^{{\rm dim}(\mathcal{M})}\rightarrow T_{x}\mathcal{M}
. Since
\phi
and
\psi
are local diffeomorphism,
J(\phi^{-1})
and
J(\psi)
are both of full rank.

We first present a useful technical lemma, which shows that
x
and the tangent vector
u
can be decoupled when
Dg
is restricted in the tangent space.

Lemma 1

For
\alpha>0
, we have

\displaystyle Jg(x)[\xi]=D_{u}R_{x}[u](I-\alpha Dgradf(x))[\xi],~\xi\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}.

where
u=-\alpha gradf(x)
and
R_{x}[u]=P_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u)
is the projection-like retraction.

Proof

∎For any
\xi\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}
, we have

\displaystyle D_{x}g(x)[\xi]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{x}P_{\mathcal{M}}(x-\alpha gradf(x))[\xi]

(6)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{P_{\mathcal{M}}(x+t\xi-\alpha gradf(x+t\xi))-P_{\mathcal{M}}(x-\alpha gradf(x))}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{P_{\mathcal{M}}(x+t\xi-\alpha gradf(x+t\xi))-P_{\mathcal{M}}(x+t\xi-\alpha gradf(x))}{t}

\displaystyle~~~~+\dfrac{P_{\mathcal{M}}(x+t\xi-\alpha gradf(x))-P_{\mathcal{M}}(x-\alpha gradf(x))}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{DP_{\mathcal{M}}(x+t\xi-\alpha gradf(x))(\alpha gradf(x)-\alpha gradf(x+t\xi))}{t}

\displaystyle~~~~+D_{u}R_{x}[u]\xi|_{u=-\alpha gradf(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{y}P_{\mathcal{M}}(y)(-\alpha Dgradf(x))[\xi]+D_{u}R_{x}[u]\xi|_{u=-\alpha gradf(x)},

where
y
denotes
x-\alpha gradf(x)
.

Since
D_{u}P_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u)=D_{u+x}P_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u)
when
x
is fixed and
u
is independent of
x
, we have

D_{y}P_{\mathcal{M}}(y)=D_{u}P_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u)=D_{u}R_{x}[u]|_{u=-\alpha gradf(x)}

(7)

Combining (
6
) with (
7
) yields that

Jg(x)[\xi]=D_{u}R_{x}[u](I-\alpha Dgradf(x))[\xi]|_{u=-\alpha gradf(x)},

which completes the proof.
∎

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a compact manifold and
r
be a positive constant so that
P_{\mathcal{M}}
is unique and smooth in a neighborhood of radius
r

absil12
. Let
R_{x}[u]
be a projection-like retraction at any
x\in\mathcal{M}
.
Absil
absil09
has shown that
DR_{x}[u]
is smooth with respect to
u\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}
. We now further introduce the Retraction L-smooth property for
DR_{x}[u]
.

Assumption 1
(Retraction L-smooth)

R_{x}[u]
is assumed to be Retraction L-smooth around
0
if there is a constant
L>0
such that

\|DR_{x}[u]-DR_{x}[0]\|\leq L\|u\|,~\forall x\in\mathcal{M},~\forall u\in T_{x}\mathcal{M},~\|u\|<r.

Remark 2

Clearly, the Retraction L-smooth property naturally holds for any smooth projection-like retraction. No access to information beyond retraction (such as
f(x)
) is necessary to determine the Lipschitz constant
L
.

Under the above Retraction L-smooth assumption,
base on Lemma
1
and the following two well-known lemmas, we can establish the main result.

Lemma 2
(Poincaré separation theorem)

Let
A\in R^{n\times n}
be symmetric and
U\in R^{n\times k}
be column-wise orthogonal, i.e.,
U^{T}U=I_{k}
. It holds that

\lambda_{n-k+i}(A)\leqslant\lambda_{i}\left(U^{*}AU\right)\leqslant\lambda_{i}(A),\quad i=1,\cdots,k.

Lemma 3
(Weyl’s eigenvalue perturbation inequality)

For any matrices
M,\Delta\in R^{n\times p}
, it holds that

\left|\sigma_{k}(M+\Delta)-\sigma_{k}(M)\right|\leq\sigma_{1}(\Delta).

Theorem 2.1

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a compact submanifold in
R^{n}
. Under the Retraction L-smooth assumption,
g(x)
is an immersion (or equivalently,
\operatorname{det}(\mathrm{D}g(x))\neq 0
) as long as the step-size
\alpha
satisfies that

\alpha<\min\left(\dfrac{1}{2H},\frac{1}{3LG},\frac{r}{G}\right),

(8)

where
H=\max_{x\in\mathcal{M}}\|Dgradf(x)\|
and
G=\max_{x\in\mathcal{M}}\|gradf(x)\|
.

Proof

According to Lemma
1
, for any
\xi\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}
, we have

Dg(x)[\xi]=D_{u}R_{x}[u](I-\alpha Dgradf(x))[\xi]|_{u=-\alpha gradf(x)}.

Let
m=\operatorname{dim}(\mathcal{M})
. Then
m\leq n
. Let
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{m}\}
be an orthonormal basis of the tangent space
T_{x}\mathcal{M}
.
Denote
E
by the matrix
(e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{m})\in R^{n\times m}
.
Under this parameterazation,
Dg(x)[\xi]
is of full rank if and only if

\operatorname{\sigma_{min}}\left(D_{u}R_{x}[u](I-\alpha Dgradf(x))E|_{u=-\alpha gradf(x)}\right)>0.

(9)

First, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\sigma_{min}^{2}}\left(D_{u}R_{x}[0](I-\alpha Dgradf(x))E\right)=\operatorname{\sigma_{min}^{2}}\left(P_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(I-\alpha Dgradf(x))E\right)

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{\sigma_{min}^{2}}\left((I-\alpha P_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}Dgradf(x))E\right)=\operatorname{\sigma_{min}^{2}}\left((I-\alpha Hessf(x))E\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{\lambda}_{m}\left(E^{T}(I-\alpha Hessf(x))^{T}(I-\alpha Hessf(x))E\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\operatorname{\lambda}_{n}((I-\alpha Hessf(x))^{T}(I-\alpha Hessf(x)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{i}(1-\alpha\lambda_{i}(Hessf(x)))^{2}\geq(1-\alpha\|Hessf(x)\|)^{2}\geq 1/4,

where the first inequality is due to Lemma
2
, and the last inequality
holds since it follows from (
8
) that

1/2-\alpha\|Hessf(x)\|>0
.

Take
u=-\alpha gradf(x)
.
For
\alpha
satisfying (
8
), we have

\|u\|=\|-\alpha gradf(x)\|\leq\alpha G<r.

(11)

Let
V_{D}:=(I-\alpha Dgradf(x))E
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{\min}(D_{u}R_{x}[0]V_{D})-\sigma_{\min}(D_{u}R_{x}[u]V_{D})\leq||D_{u}R_{x}[u]V_{D}-D_{u}R_{x}[0]V_{D}||

\displaystyle\leq||D_{u}R_{x}[u]-D_{u}R_{x}[0]||\cdot||V_{D}||\leq L||u||\cdot||V_{D}||\leq LG\alpha||V_{D}||,

(12)

where the first inequality holds according to Lemma
3
, the third inequality follows from the Retraction L-smooth assumption, and the last inequality is due to (
11
). It follows from (
12
) that

\sigma_{\min}(D_{u}R_{x}[u]V_{D})\geq\sigma_{\min}(D_{u}R_{x}[0]V_{D})-LG\alpha\|V_{D}\|.

(13)

According to Lemma
2
, we have

\displaystyle\|V_{D}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{\max}((I-\alpha Dgradf(x))E)\leq\sigma_{\max}(I-\alpha Dgradf(x))

(14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\alpha\sigma_{\max}(Dgradf(x))=1+\alpha\|Dgradf(x)\|\leq 3/2,

where the last inequality follows from (
8
).
Substituting (
10
) and (
14
) into (
13
) yields that

\sigma_{\min}(D_{u}R_{x}[u]V_{D})\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2}LG\alpha>0,

where the last inequality holds due to (
8
).
Thus, we have proved (
9
). Then we have

\operatorname{det}(\mathrm{D}g(x))\neq 0
and hence
g(x)
is an immersion.
∎

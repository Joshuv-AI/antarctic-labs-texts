Moment Generating Stirling Numbers of the first kind and Applications
Ludwig Frank

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper we introduce and investigate moment generating Stirling numbers of the first kind, ”‘MSN1”’. They are inverses of MSN2’s, which make the representation of the moments for a lot of statistical distributions in closed formulas possible. Both MSN1’s and MSN2’s are related to the r-Stirling numbers, and extend their properties to any real third parameter. If the third parameter is a nonnegative integer, r-Stirling numbers can be converted to MSN’s and vice versa.

Keywords:
Generalized Stirling numbers, phase type distribution, factorial moments, central moments, r-Stirling numbers

AMS Subject Classification:
11B73, 05A19, 05A10, 60C05

1
Introduction

In
[
6
]
moment generating Stirling numbers of the second kind were defined, ”‘MSN2”’. They make the representation of the moments for a lot of statistical distributions in closed formulas possible. In the present paper we introduce the moment generating Stirling numbers of the first kind, ”‘MSN1”, and investigate their properties. In some way, they are inverses of MSN2’s. Both MSN1’s and MSN2’s are related to the r-Stirling numbers, (cf.
[
3
]
) and extend their properties to any real third parameter. If the third parameter is a nonnegative integer, r-Stirling numbers can be converted to MSN’s and vice versa. While r-Stirling numbers are useful for combinatorial applications, MSNs allow simpler representations of formulas in statistical applications.

In Section 2 we present the definition of MSN1’s, their basic properties and their interaction with the MSN2’s. Finally, Section 3 shows how central, factorial and ordinary moments can be converted into one another using MSN1’s (and MSN2’s). If the moments of a statistical distribution can be expressed by means of MSN2’s, MSN1’s make it possible to reconstruct their parameters.

Moment Generating Stirling Numbers of the first kind

2.1
Definitions and Basic Concepts

The purpose of this section is to show some simple but basic properties of MSN1’s and to present the ordinary and exponential generating function, which allow to derive further properties and identities. They show the relation to ordinary SN1’s.

If
s_{i,j}
are the (ordinary) Stirling numbers of the first kind (with sign) (cf. e.g.
[
1
]

i\geq 0

c_{i,j,k}=\sum_{r=j}^{i}\binom{r}{j}\cdot(-k)^{r-j}\cdot s_{i,r}.

(2.1)

For
i<0

j<0

.

The following lemma summarizes some obvious and often used properties:

Lemma 2.1
.

For integers

,

and real

a)

\displaystyle c_{i,j,0}=s_{i,j},

(2.2)

b)

\displaystyle c_{i,j,k}=0,\text{ if }j>i,

(2.3)

c)

\displaystyle c_{i,i,k}=1,

(2.4)

d)

\displaystyle c_{i+1,i,k}=s_{i+1,i}-k\cdot(i+1)=-\binom{i+1}{2}-k\cdot(i+1)

(2.5)

Proof.
a) and b) follow from the definition, c) holds due to

(cf.
[
1
]
) and d) follows from (

) and
s_{i+1,i}=-\binom{i+1}{2}
(cf. e.g.
[

]
).
∎

Now, we turn to the ordinary generating function, which is closely related to the generating function of the ordinary Stirling numbers. With

(2.9)

\displaystyle\text{c) }G_{i+1,k}(x)=G_{1,k}(x)\cdot G_{i,k+1}(x),

(2.11)

\displaystyle\text{e) }G_{i,k}(x)\cdot F_{k}(x)=G_{k,i}(x)\cdot F_{i}(x),

b) Due to a)

\displaystyle G_{i+n,k}(x)=(x-k)\cdot(x-k-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot(x-k-i-n+1)=

\displaystyle(x-k)\cdot\ldots\cdot(x-k-n+1)\cdot(x-k-n)\cdot\ldots\cdot(x-k-i-n+1)=

\displaystyle G_{n,k}(x)\cdot G_{i,k+n}(x).

f) follows from b) for
k=0

\displaystyle F_{i+n}(x)=G_{i+n,0}(x)=G_{n,0}(x)\cdot G_{i,n}(x)=F_{n}(x)\cdot G_{i,n}(x).

Set

2.16
), for the exponential generating function

\displaystyle E_{j_{1},k_{1}}(x)\cdot E_{j_{2},k_{2}}(x)=\binom{j_{1}+j_{2}}{j_{1}}\cdot E_{j_{1}+j_{2},k_{1}+k_{2}}(x).

By differentiating
i
-times, due to
c_{i,j,k}=E_{j,k}^{(i)}(0)
, we obtain the equation

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{i}\binom{i}{r}\cdot E_{j_{1},k_{1}}^{(i-r)}(0)\cdot E_{j_{2},k_{2}}^{(r)}(0)=\binom{j_{1}+j_{2}}{j_{1}}\cdot E_{j_{1}+j_{2},k_{1}+k_{2}}^{(i)}(0),

which implies the assertion.
∎

For integers

,
j\geq 0
and real

\displaystyle\text{ a) }\sum_{r=j}^{i}\binom{i}{r}(k)_{i-r}\cdot c_{r,j,k}=s_{i,j},

\displaystyle\text{ b) }\sum_{r=j}^{i}(i)_{r}\cdot(-1)^{i-r}\cdot c_{r,j,k}=c_{i,j,k+1}.

Proof.
a) follows from the preceding theorem for

(cf. (

\displaystyle\sum_{r=j}^{i}\binom{i}{r}\cdot(-k_{1})_{i-r}\cdot c_{r,j_{2},k_{2}}=c_{i,j_{2},k_{1}+k_{2}}.

Setting

\displaystyle c_{i,j,k+1}=\sum_{r=j}^{i}\binom{i}{r}c_{i-r,0,1}\cdot c_{r,j,k}=\sum_{r=j}^{i}\binom{i}{r}(-1)_{i-r}\cdot c_{r,j,k}=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=j}^{i}(i)_{r}\cdot(-1)^{i-r}\cdot c_{r,j,k}.\qed

Theorem 2.13

For integers
i\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{r=j}^{i}\binom{r}{j}\cdot k_{2}^{r-j}\cdot c_{i,r,k_{1}}=c_{i,j,k_{1}-k_{2}}

3
Applications

In
[
6
]
binomial coefficients and the MSN2’s allowed to express the central moments

\displaystyle C_{m}(X)=M_{1}\left((X-M_{1}(X))^{m}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\cdot\left(-M_{1}(X)\right)^{m-j}\cdot M_{j}(X)

(3.1)

of any random variable
X
on {0, 1, 2,…} by the ordinary moments
M_{m}(X)
and to express the ordinary moments by the factorial moments

\displaystyle F_{m}(X)=M_{1}(X\cdot(X-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot(X-m+1))

(3.2)

as

\displaystyle M_{m}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}b_{m,j,0}\cdot\frac{1}{j!}\cdot F_{j}(X).

(3.3)

Additionally it was shown that

\displaystyle C_{m}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}b_{m,j,-M_{1}(X)}\cdot\frac{1}{j!}\cdot F_{j}(X).

(3.4)

The following theorem, which inverts these equations, shows that the MSN1’s make it possible to convert any kind of moment to any other one.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
X
is a random variable on
{
0, 1, 2, …
}
with existing moments of any order, then

\displaystyle\text{ a) }F_{m}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{m,j,0}\cdot M_{j}(X),

(3.5)

\displaystyle\text{ b) }F_{m}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{m,j,-M_{1}(X)}\cdot C_{j}(X),

(3.6)

\displaystyle\text{ c) }M_{m}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\cdot\frac{1}{j!}\cdot\left(M_{1}(X)\right)^{m-j}\cdot C_{j}(X).

(3.7)

Proof.
a) Due to (
2.41
) and (
3.3
)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{m,j,0}\cdot M_{j}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{m,j,0}\cdot\sum_{r=0}^{j}\frac{b_{j,r,0}}{r!}\cdot F_{r}(X)=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{m}\sum_{j=r}^{m}c_{m,j,0}\cdot\frac{b_{j,r,0}}{r!}\cdot F_{r}(X)=F_{m}(X).

b) Due to (
2.41
) and (
3.4
)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{m,j,-M_{1}(X)}\cdot C_{j}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{m,j,-M_{1}(X)}\cdot\sum_{r=0}^{j}b_{j,r,-M_{1}(X)}\cdot\frac{1}{r!}\cdot F_{r}(X)=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{m}F_{r}(X)\cdot\frac{1}{r!}\cdot\sum_{j=r}^{m}c_{m,j,-M_{1}(X)}\cdot b_{j,r,-M_{1}(X)}=F_{m}(X).

c) Due to (
3.3
), b) and Theorem 2.18 in
[
6
]

\displaystyle M_{m}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{b_{m,j,0}}{j!}\cdot F_{j}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{b_{m,j,0}}{j!}\cdot\sum_{r=0}^{j}c_{j,r,-M_{1}(X)}\cdot C_{r}(X)=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{m}C_{r}(X)\cdot\sum_{j=r}^{m}\frac{b_{m,j,0}}{j!}\cdot c_{j,r,-M_{1}(X)}=\sum_{r=0}^{m}C_{r}(X)\cdot\binom{m}{r}\cdot M_{1}(X)^{m-r}.\qed

Just as in
[
6
]
the central moments for a lot of probability distributions were possible in closed formulas, here the factorial moments can be determined in simple calculations by means of Theorem 3.1.

Example 3.2
.

If the random variable
X
is Poisson distributed with parameter
\lambda
, then

\displaystyle M_{m}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}S_{m,j}\cdot\lambda^{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}b_{m,j,0}\cdot\frac{\lambda^{j}}{j!}.

By Item a) of the preceding theorem we obtain due to (
2.41
)

\displaystyle F_{m}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{m,j,0}\cdot M_{j}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{m,j,0}\cdot\sum_{r=0}^{j}b_{j,r,0}\cdot\frac{\lambda^{r}}{r!}=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{m}\frac{\lambda^{r}}{r!}\cdot\sum_{j=r}^{m}c_{m,j,0}\cdot b_{j,r,0}=\lambda^{m}.

PH(\textbf{{p}},\textbf{{P}})

]
,
[

]

[

]

]

\displaystyle M_{m}(X)=\sum_{r=0}^{m}b_{m,r,1}\cdot\textbf{{p}}\cdot\left(\textbf{{P}}\cdot(\textbf{{I-P}})\right)^{r}\cdot\textbf{{e}},

with a suitable column vector

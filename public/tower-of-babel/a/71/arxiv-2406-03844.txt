PREX and CREX: Evidence for Strong Isovector Spin-Orbit Interaction
Tong-Gang Yue, Zhen Zhang, Lie-Wen Chen

Abstract
The recent PREX-2 and CREX data on the model-independent extraction of the charge-weak form factor difference Δ ​ F CW \Delta F_{\rm CW} in 208 Pb and 48 Ca challenge modern nuclear energy density functionals (EDFs) as well as our present understanding on the neutron skin and nuclear symmetry energy. Within the Skyrme-like EDFs, we demonstrate that the isovector spin-orbit interaction can strongly change the Δ ​ F CW \Delta F_{\rm CW} in 48 Ca while it has essentially no influence on the Δ ​ F CW \Delta F_{\rm CW} in 208 Pb, mainly due to the eight spin-orbit unpaired 1 ​ f 7 / 2 1f_{7/2} neutrons in 48 Ca. To simultaneously describe PREX-2 and CREX data in 1 ​ σ 1\sigma error, we find the strength of isovector spin-orbit interaction should be larger than about four times of that in the conventional Skyrme-like EDFs, implying the neutrons and protons have significantly different spin-orbit interaction. To further reconcile the data on electric dipole polarizability in 208 Pb and 48 Ca, we obtain L ≈ 55 L\approx 55 MeV for the slope parameter of the symmetry energy, Δ r np ( 208 Pb ) ≈ 0.19 \Delta r_{\rm np}(^{208}\rm{Pb})\approx 0.19 fm and Δ r np ( 48 Ca ) ≈ 0.12 \Delta r_{\rm np}(^{48}\rm{Ca})\approx 0.12 fm for the neutron skin thickness. The implications of the strong isovector spin-orbit interaction are discussed.

It is clear that
208
Pb and
48

In particular,
compared to

12
spin-orbit (SO) unpaired

neutrons,

8
unpaired

that the isospin-sensitive
\Delta F_{\rm{CW}}^{48}
may depend on the state-dependent isovector spin-orbit (IVSO) interaction.
Note the SO interaction is also more important for lighter nuclei due to the relatively more diffused surface.
It is interesting to note that
the
\Delta r_{\rm{np}}
of
48
Ca depends on the total SO interaction but that of
208
Pb does not

.
In addition, the
SO contribution to the electroweak skin in
48
Ca is significant compared to the tiny one in
208
Pb

.
These observations
suggest that the IVSO interaction might
be the key to the PREX-CREX puzzle.
It is well-known that
the strong SO interaction is of fundamental importance for nuclear structure, e.g., it is responsible for nuclear magic numbers
Mayer 1949
;
Haxel et al. 1949
.
While the strong SO coupling
naturally emerges in the relativistic models
Duerr 1956
;
Furnstahl and Serot 2000

and Gogny
Berger et al. 1984
EDFs incorporate the SO coupling via a phenomenological way.
Although the isocalar SO interaction is relatively well determined by analyzing properties of finite nuclei, the IVSO interaction is poorly known due to the lack of effective experimental probes and the complex interplay of the SO interaction with, e.g., tensor forces and nucleon effective masses

;
Lesinski et al. 2007
;
Klupfel et al. 2009

In this Letter, within the Skyrme-like EDFs, we demonstrate that the IVSO interaction can significantly affect the
\Delta F_{\rm{CW}}^{48}
while it has negligible influence on the
\Delta F_{\rm{CW}}^{208}
, and a strong IVSO interaction with its strength larger than about four times of that in the conventional Skyrme-like EDFs can solve the PREX-CREX puzzle.

Model and method.
—

In the (extended) Skyrme EDFs, we further include the zero-range tensor force

Results and discussions.
—
We begin by comparing in Fig.
1
the
predictions on
\Delta F_{\rm{CW}}^{48}
and
\Delta F_{\rm{CW}}^{208}

from various modern nonrelativistic and relativistic nuclear EDFs

;
Yue et al. 2022
with the PREX-2 and CREX results.
One clearly sees that
to simultaneously describe the PREX-2 and CREX results is a challenge for modern nuclear EDFs.
Also included in Fig.

the predictions from six new (extended) Skyrme EDFs, i.e., S240, eS240, S

Remarkably, with the inclusion of a strong IVSO interaction, all the six new EDFs can simultaneously

Among the six new EDFs, the

The effects of the IVSO coupling

\displaystyle E_{\rm{so}}=\int d^{3}r\left[\frac{b_{\rm{IS}}}{2}\bm{J}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\rho+\frac{b_{\rm{IV}}}{2}(\bm{J}_{n}-\bm{J}_{p})\cdot\bm{\nabla}(\rho_{n}-\rho_{p})\right].

(2)

For spherical nuclei like
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 48$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ca}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi r^{3}}\sum_{i}v_{i}^{2}\left(2j_{i}+1\right)

\displaystyle\times\left[j_{i}\left(j_{i}+1\right)-l_{i}\left(l_{i}+1\right)-\frac{3}{4}\right]R_{i}^{2}(r),

Compared to

Within the RMF model,

;

;

;

Given the importance

Conclusions.
—Within the Skyrme-like EDFs, we have demonstrated that the PREX-CREX puzzle can be solved by including a strong isovector spin-orbit interaction, which can significantly affect the
\Delta F_{\rm{CW}}
in
48
Ca but has essentially no effects on the
\Delta F_{\rm{CW}}
in
208
Pb.
To reconcile the PREX-2 and CREX data, the strength of the isovector spin-orbit interaction should be larger than about four times of that in the conventional Skyrme-like EDFs.
To further
reconcile the measured electric dipole polarizabilities in

208
Pb and
48
Ca, we obtain
L\approx 55
MeV,
\Delta r_{\rm np}(^{208}\rm{Pb})\approx 0.19
fm and
\Delta r_{\rm np}(^{48}\rm{Ca})\approx 0.12
fm.
The strong isovector spin-orbit interaction suggested in our present work will have profound impacts on many topics in nuclear physics.

Our results suggest that while the
\Delta F_{\rm{CW}}
in
stable closed-shell nuclei like
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 208$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Pb}
and
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 60$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ni}
can uniquely constrain the density dependence of the symmetry energy near saturation density,
the
\Delta F_{\rm{CW}}
in stable closed-shell nuclei with unpaired neutrons like
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 48$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ca}
and
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 90$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Zr}
provides an ideal probe of the isovector spin-orbit interaction once the density behavior of the symmetry energy near saturation density is well constrained. It is thus interesting to measure the
\Delta F_{\rm{CW}}
of these nuclei in future parity-violation experiments, such as the Mainz Radius Experiment (MREX)
Becker et al. 2018
;
Schlimme et al. 2024
, which can help to determine the neutron skin, the symmetry energy and isovector spin-orbit interaction with minimal
model dependence.

I. Extended Skyrme energy density functional with the inclusion of tensor force

The conventional standard Skyrme interaction is given by (see, e.g., Ref.
Chabanat et al. 1997
):

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &v\left(\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2}\right)=t_{0}\left(1+x_{0}P_{\sigma}\right)\delta(\mathbf{r})+\frac{1}{2}t_{1}\left(1+x_{1}P_{\sigma}\right)\left[\mathbf{k}^{\prime 2}\delta(\mathbf{r})+\delta(\mathbf{r})\mathbf{k}^{2}\right]+t_{2}\left(1+x_{2}P_{\sigma}\right)\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\cdot\delta(\mathbf{r})\mathbf{k}\\
&+\frac{1}{6}t_{3}\left(1+x_{3}P_{\sigma}\right)[\rho(\mathbf{R})]^{\alpha}\delta(\mathbf{r})+iW_{0}(\bm{\sigma}_{1}+\bm{\sigma}_{2})\cdot\left[\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\times\delta(\mathbf{r})\mathbf{k}\right],\end{aligned}

(4)

where
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2}
,
\mathbf{R}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}+\mathbf{r}_{2}\right)
,
\mathbf{k}=\frac{1}{2i}\left(\bm{\nabla}_{1}-\bm{\nabla}_{2}\right)
is the relative momentum with
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
being its conjugate acting on the left, the
\bm{\sigma}
are Pauli spin operators,
P_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\bm{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{2}\right)
is the spin exchange operator,
and
\rho(\bm{R})=\rho_{n}(\bm{R})+\rho_{p}(\bm{R})
is the total local density with
\rho_{n}
and
\rho_{p}
being neutron and proton densities, respectively. The
t_{0}\sim t_{3}
and
x_{0}\sim x_{3}
are the standard “Skyrme parameters”, and
W_{0}
is the spin-orbit coupling parameter.
In this work, we also use the extended Skyrme interaction with the inclusion of additional effective momentum-dependent many-body force (see, e.g., Ref.
Chamel et al. 2009
), that is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} v^{\prime}=&v+\frac{1}{2}t_{4}\left(1+x_{4}P_{\sigma}\right)\left[\mathbf{k}^{\prime 2}\rho(\bm{R})^{\beta}\delta(\mathbf{r})+\delta(\mathbf{r})\rho(\bm{R})^{\beta}\mathbf{k}^{2}\right]+t_{5}\left(1+x_{5}P_{\sigma}\right)\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\cdot\rho(\bm{R})^{\gamma}\delta(\mathbf{r})\mathbf{k}.\end{aligned}

(5)

Here the additional
t_{4}
and
t_{5}
terms are density-dependent generalization of the momentum-dependent
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
terms, then the extended Skyrme interaction has additional six parameters:

t_{4}
,
x_{4}
,
t_{5}
,
x_{5}
,
\beta
and
\gamma
.
For the tensor force, we use the following zero-range form
Stancu et al. 1977

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} V_{T}=&\frac{1}{2}T\left\{\left[\left(\bm{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\right)\left(\bm{\sigma}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\right)-\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{k}^{\prime 2}\left(\bm{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{2}\right)\right]\delta\left(\mathbf{r}\right)+\delta\left(\mathbf{r}\right)\left[\left(\bm{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{k}\right)\left(\bm{\sigma}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{k}\right)-\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{k}^{2}\left(\bm{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{2}\right)\right]\right\}\\
&+U\left\{\left(\bm{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\right)\delta\left(\mathbf{r}\right)\left(\bm{\sigma}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{k}\right)-\frac{1}{3}\left(\bm{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{2}\right)\left[\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\cdot\delta\left(\mathbf{r}\right)\mathbf{k}\right]\right\},\end{aligned}

(6)

where
T
and

quantify the magnitude of the tensor force in states of even and odd relative motion, respectively.

Based on the extended Skyrme interaction with a zero-range tensor force, we can obtain the extended Skyrme potential energy density functional from Hartree-Fock calculation. \rho_{q}

\displaystyle\rho_{q}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}v_{i}^{2}\left|\varphi_{i}(\mathbf{r})\right|^{2},

(7)

\displaystyle\tau_{q}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}v_{i}^{2}\left|\nabla\varphi_{i}\mathbf{r})\right|^{2},

(8)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\sum_{i}v_{i}^{2}\varphi_{i}^{+}(\mathbf{r})\nabla\times\hat{\sigma}\varphi_{i}(\mathbf{r}).

II. Charge and weak form factors

The normalized nuclear form factors
F_{C}(q)
and
F_{W}(q)
are calculated by folding the nucleon form factor
F_{t}(q)~(t=n,p)
and the spin-orbit current form factor (
F_{t}^{ls}
) with the intrinsic nucleon electromagnetic form factor
G_{E/M,t}
and weak form factor
G^{(W)}_{E/M,t}
by
Reinhard et al. 2021a

\displaystyle F_{C}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z}\sum_{t=p,n}\left[G_{E,t}(q)F_{t}(q)+G_{M,t}(q)F_{t}^{(ls)}(q)\right],

(19)

\displaystyle F_{W}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=p,n}\frac{\left[G_{E,t}^{(W)}(q)F_{t}(q)+G_{M,t}^{(W)}(q)F_{t}^{(ls)}(q)\right]}{ZQ_{p}^{(W)}+NQ_{n}^{(W)}},

(20)

where
N(Z)
is the neutron(proton) number, and
Q_{p}^{(W)}=0.0713
and
Q_{n}^{(W)}=-0.9888
are proton and neutron weak charges, respectively.
The
G_{E/M,t}
are derived from the isospin-coupled Sachs form factors, the relativistic Darwin correction has been included and the center-of-mass corrections are taken into account by simply renormalizing the nucleon mass
m_{N}
to
(1-1/A)m_{N}
in the HF calculation (see, e.g., Ref.
Bender et al. 2003
for details). The weak intrinsic nucleon form factors
G^{(W)}_{E/M,t}
are then determined by
Reinhard et al. 2021a

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &G_{E/M,n}^{(W)}=Q_{n}^{(W)}G_{E/M,p}+Q_{p}^{(W)}G_{E/M,n}+Q_{n}^{(W)}G_{E/M,s},\\
&G_{E/M,p}^{(W)}=Q_{p}^{(W)}G_{E/M,p}+Q_{n}^{(W)}G_{E/M,n}+Q_{n}^{(W)}G_{E/M,s},\end{aligned}

(21)

with the strange-quark electromagnetic form factors
G_{E/M,s}
given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} G_{E,s}(q)&=\rho_{s}\frac{\hbar^{2}q^{2}/\left(4c^{2}m_{N}^{2}\right)}{1+4.97\hbar^{2}q^{2}/\left(4c^{2}m_{N}^{2}\right)},\\
G_{M,s}(q)&=\kappa_{s}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{\left(4c^{2}m_{N}^{2}\right)}.\end{aligned}

(22)

The strange quark electric coupling
\rho_{s}
and the strange quark magnetic moment
\kappa_{s}
are respectively taken to be -0.24 and -0.017
Reinhard et al. 2021a
.

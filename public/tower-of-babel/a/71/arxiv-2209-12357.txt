Abstract
. This paper deals with fractional Sobolev spaces on a compact Riemannian manifold. We prove a Sobolev inequality in the critical range with an optimal constant for these fractional Sobolev spaces. We use this result to study the existence of a non-trivial solution for equations driven by a non-local integro-differential operator ℒ 𝒦 \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{K}} with critical non-linearity.



1.
Introduction

Equations involving non-local operators on an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are the subject of an intense research activity which focuses on the classical questions of existence, uniqueness, regularity, and qualitative properties of
the solutions, for both linear and non-linear operators, like the fractional and the p-fractional Laplacian (see
[
7
,
20
,
23
,
26
]
). This acute effort to understand this type of equation is due to its multiple applications in several contexts: continuum mechanics, phase transition, population dynamics, optimal control, game theory and image processing, as is explained in
[
8
,
15
]
and references therein.

In this paper, we are particularly interested in equations on Riemannian manifolds involving a non-linearity whose growth is critical from the point of view of the Sobolev embedding. The first results in this direction started in the ’60s in the context of the Yamabe problem, a classical problem in differential geometry formulated as the question of finding a non-trivial solution to a particular critical equation with the Laplace-Beltrami operator. The problem was solved completely three decades ago, and since then, many authors have devoted their work to extend the techniques used for its resolution in different directions (e.g.
[
5
,
13
]
).
Recently an analogous theory has been developed for the so-called fractional Yamabe problem,
which relies on finding a solution to a critical equation with a specific fractional operator on manifolds (
[
9
,
16
,
19
]
).

To the best of our knowledge, there is still no work dealing with critical equations on manifolds for general linear and non-linear integro-differential operators.
The present work aims to provide a functional framework suitable to extend to the p-fractional Laplacian on a compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
many results obtained for the standard Laplacian.
More precisely, we start defining natural fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}(M)
, and then we prove an optimal Sobolev inequality. Moreover, we deduce from there an existence result for critical equations involving a singular non-local operator.

The question of suitable natural fractional Sobolev spaces on manifolds is not trivial.
Indeed we found two different definitions of fractional Sobolev spaces in the literature,
both equally natural and appealing but slightly different.
H. Triebel introduced in
[
28
,
29
]
the whole scales of Besov
B^{s}_{p,q}(M)
and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces
F^{s}_{p,q}(M)
on manifolds.
The Triebel-Lizorkin spaces on manifolds are modelled on the classical Triebel-Lizorkin spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

through exponential charts and partition of unity. On the other hand, the Besov spaces on manifolds are defined by interpolation of the Triebel-Lizorki spaces, as in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Both spaces have attracted considerable attention in the last decades.
For example in
[
6
]
, the authors study the theory of Besov and
Triebel–Lizorkin spaces on general non-compact Lie groups endowed with a sub-Riemannian structure.
Furthermore, it was recently proved in
[
3
,
4
]
that there exists a Brezis-Bourgain-Mironescu type result for these spaces:

\lim_{s\to 1}(1-s)[u]_{s,p}^{p}=C\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}.

In the Euclidean setting, the fractional Sobolev spaces are defined for
s\in(0,1),\,sp<n
, as

(1)

W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,[u]^{p}_{s,p}<\infty\},

endowed with the norm
\|u\|_{s,p}^{p}=\|u\|_{p}^{p}+[u]_{s,p}^{p}
, where

(2)

[u]_{s,p}^{p}:=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dxdy,

known as the Galgliardo seminorm.
Basic properties of
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be found in
[
12
]
,
whereas the complete theory of Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be found in
[
27
]
. In particular, it holds

F^{s}_{p,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=B^{s}_{p,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

H. Triebel extended part of this theory to
B^{s}_{p,q}(M)
and
F^{s}_{p,q}(M)
in
[
28
,
29
]
.
Furthermore, he proved that when
p=q
, we have
F^{s}_{p,p}(M)=B^{s}_{p,p}(M)
, which are the spaces we are interested in, and we call
W^{s,p}(M)
.
More recently, L. Guo, B. Zhang, and Y. Zhang
[
17
]
straightforwardly adapted (
1
)-(
2
) on a compact Riemannian manifold

(M,g)
in the following way. For
s\in(0,1),\,sp<n
, they define

(3)

\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(M):=\{u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,[u]^{p}_{s,p}<\infty\},

where

(4)

[u]_{s,p}^{p}:=\iint_{M\times M}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{d_{g}(x,y)^{n+sp}}\,dv_{g}(x)dv_{g}(y).

Both fractional spaces
W^{s,p}(M)
and
\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(M)
are pretty natural and satisfy the usual properties of Sobolev spaces
(such as Banach, reflexivity, the density of smooth functions and embedding Theorems).
So, it is logical to investigate if they define different spaces or not.
Our first main result shows that they indeed coincide:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary of dimension
n
,
s\in(0,1)
and
sp<n
.
Then the fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}(M)
and
\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(M)
introduced by H. Triebel and
L. Guo, B. Zhang, and Y. Zhang coincide with norm equivalence.

From now on, we fix a compact Riemannian manifold boundaryless of dimension
n
and denote

W^{s,p}(M)=\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(M)
.
As a consequence of H. Triebel and L. Guo, B. Zhang, and Y. Zhang’s work, we know in particular that
the embedding
W^{s,p}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(M)
is continuous for
q\leq p^{*}
and compact for
q<p^{*}
,
where
p^{*}
is the fractional critical Sobolev exponent given by

(5)

p^{*}=p^{*}(n,s)=\frac{np}{n-sp}.

The p-fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}:W^{s,p}(M)\to(W^{s,p}(M))^{\prime}
appears naturally when looking for critical points of the semi-norm
[u]_{s,p}^{p}
. So we consider the equation

(6)

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u+h|u|^{p-2}u=f|u|^{q-2}u

where
f
and
h
are smooth functions on
M
, with
q\leq p^{*}
.
Solutions to (
6
) can be found as critical points of the functional

J(u):=\frac{1}{p}[u]_{s,p}^{p}+\frac{1}{p}\int_{M}h|u|^{p}\,dv_{g}-\frac{1}{q}\int_{M}f|u|^{q}\,dv_{g},\qquad u\in W^{s,p}(M).

Actually, we shall consider a more general non-local operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{K}}
on
W^{s,p}(M)
defined as follows.
Denote
\mathcal{D}=\{(x,x):x\in M\}
. Let
g_{\varepsilon}
be the metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
obtained blowing-up
g
at
x_{0}
, namely

g_{\varepsilon}(x)=((\exp_{x_{0}})^{*}g)(\varepsilon x)
.
Let
\mathcal{K}(\cdot,\cdot\ ;g):(M\times M)\backslash\mathcal{D}\rightarrow(0,\ +\infty)
be a function which satisfies:

(K1)

m\mathcal{K}\in L^{1}(M\times M)
, where
m=m(x,y)=\displaystyle\min\{d_{g}(x,y)^{p},1\};

(K2)

\mathcal{K}(x,y;g)=\mathcal{K}(y,x;g)
for any
(x,y)\in(M\times M)\backslash\mathcal{D}
.

(K3)

There is a constant
\Lambda>1
such that

\Lambda^{-1}<\mathcal{K}(x,y;g)d_{g}(x,y)^{n+ps}<\Lambda\,\text{ for all }x,y\in(M\times M)\backslash\mathcal{D}.

(K4)

Let
x_{0}\in M
and
G:T_{x_{0}}M\to M
be a smooth function. If we denote

\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(X,Y,G^{*}g):=\mathcal{K}(G(X),G(Y);g)\qquad\text{for all }X\neq Y\in T_{x_{0}}M,

then it holds

\varepsilon^{n+sp}\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(X,Y;\varepsilon^{2}g_{\varepsilon})\to|X-Y|^{-(n+ps)}

as
\varepsilon\to 0
uniformly on compacts.

Then,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{K}}
is defined weakly by

(7)

(\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{K}}u,v)=\iint_{M\times M}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))\mathcal{K}(x,y;g)\,dv_{g}(x)dv_{g}(y).

We thus look for a non-trivial solution to a non-local equation like

(8)

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{K}}u+h|u|^{p-2}u=f|u|^{q-2}u,

distinguishing two cases: the critical and sub-critical problems. In the sub-critical case
q<p^{*}
, an application of the Mountain Pass Theorem gives us the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
s\in(0,1),\,sp<n
, and
f\geq 0
and
h
be smooth functions on
M
. Assuming the coercivity condition

(9)

[u]_{s,p}^{p}+\int_{M}h|u|^{p}\,dv_{g}\geq C\|u\|_{s,p}^{p}\qquad\text{for any }u\in W^{s,p}(M),

the equation (
8
) with
1<q<p^{*}
has a non-trivial solution.

Let us denote by
K(n,s,2)
the best constant in the classical embedding
W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
For the critical case, we shall prove the existence of a solution to the problem

(10)

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{K}}u+hu=f|u|^{p^{*}-2}u

where
h
and
f
are smooth functions on
M
,
p=2.

This problem is related to the following fractional Sobolev inequality, which is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(11)

\left(\int_{M}|u|^{2^{*}}\,dv_{g}\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\leq(K(n,s,2)+\varepsilon)\iint_{M\times M}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\mathcal{K}(x,y;g)\,dv_{g}(x)dv_{g}(y)+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{M}u^{2}\,dv_{g}

holds for any
u\in W^{s,2}(M)
. Moreover,
K(n,s,2)
is the least possible constant.

In this sense, the present work may be seen as the extension of some classical results
for the Laplacian to the case of non-local fractional operators.
Unfortunately, in the non-local and non-linear case
p\neq 2
, there is no
information about the asymptotic behaviour at infinity of optimizers of the Sobolev
inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and we could not prove this Theorem for
p\neq 2
.
Recall that, for
p=2
, the extremals are of the explicit form
cU(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{\varepsilon^{2}})

with

(12)

U(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{-\frac{n-2s}{2}},

see
[
10
]
.
Although it has been conjectured that this extremal has a similar
explicit form for the general case, it is still an open problem.

Consider the functional

J_{\mathcal{K}}:W^{s,2}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

(13)

J_{\mathcal{K}}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{M\times M}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\mathcal{K}(x,y;g)dv_{g}(x)dv_{g}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}h|u(x)|^{2}dv_{g}(x)

that we will minimize over

(14)

H=\{u\in W^{s,2}(M):\,\int_{M}f|u|^{2^{*}}\,dv_{g}=1\}.

The solutions to (
10
), with
p=2
, can be found as the critical points of
J_{\mathcal{K}}
restricted to
H
.
As a corollary of the main result, we have the following Theorem: the non-local counterpart of a result well-known in the local setting.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
f\geq 0
and
h
be smooth functions on
M
. Assume the coercivity condition

(15)

J_{\mathcal{K}}(u)\geq C\|u\|_{s,2}^{2}\qquad\text{for any }u\in W^{s,2}(M)

for some positive constant
C
. If

(16)

\inf_{H}J_{\mathcal{K}}<\left(2\left(\max\,f\right)^{2/2^{*}}K(n,s,2)\right)^{-1},

then the infimum in the l.h.s. of (
16
) is attained at some nonzero
u_{0}\in H
.
In particular,
u_{0}
is a non-trivial solution to (
10
).

The rest of this paper is organized as follows:
In Sect. 2, we set down some notation that we will use throughout the paper and we prove Theorem

\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(M)

and

.
In Sect. 3, we prove Theorem

In Sect. 4, we find the optimal Sobolev embedding given by Theorem

In Sect. 5, we apply the results of Sect. 4. to

).
In Sect. 6, we give some technical computations related to the function
U
defined in (
12
).

For further details on the fractional Sobolev spaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

]

2.1.
Preliminaries and notation

Here we collect some
elementary results, which will be helpful in the main estimates of the paper.

Given
(M,\ g)
a smooth Riemannian manifold and
\gamma
:
[a,\ b]\rightarrow M
, a curve of class
C^{1}
,
the length of
\gamma
is

L(\gamma)=\int_{\alpha}^{b}\sqrt{g(\gamma(t))\left(\frac{d\gamma(t)}{dt},\frac{d\gamma(t)}{dt}\right)}dt.

For
x,y\in M
let
\mathcal{C}
be the space of piecewise
C^{1}
curves
\gamma
:
[a,\ b]\rightarrow M
such that
\gamma(a)=x
and
\gamma(b)=y
. Then
d_{g}(x,y)=\inf_{\mathcal{C}}L(\gamma)
is the distance associated with
g.

We denote by
dv_{g}(x)=\sqrt{\det(g_{ij})}dx
the Riemannian volume element on
(M,\ g)
, where the
g_{ij}
are the components of the Riemannian metric
g
in the chart and
dx
is the Lebesgue volume element of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For any
x\in M
consider the exponential map
\exp_{x}:T_{x}M\to M
. Then, we can fix
r>0

such that
\exp_{x}\big|_{B_{r}}:B_{r}\to B_{r}(x)
is a diffeomorphism for any
x\in M
.
Throughout the paper, we will denote by
B_{R}
the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centred
at
0
with radius
R
and by
B_{R}(x)
the ball in
M
centred at
x
with radius
R
for the
distance
d_{g}
. Also, we denote by
(\exp_{x}^{*})g
the metric in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined as the pullback of
g
via the exponential map.

We shall need the elementary (see
[
21
, Lemma 2.53]
)

Lemma 2.1
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, then there exist a
\delta>0
smaller than the injectivity radius of
(M,g)
and a covering of
M
by balls
\{B_{\delta}(x_{i}),i=1,\dots,N\}
, such that for any
i=1,\dots,N
,
the following properties hold in the exponential chart
(B_{\delta}(x_{i}),\text{exp}_{x_{i}}^{-1})
:

(17)

(1-\varepsilon)\ dv_{\xi}\leq dv_{\exp_{x_{i}}^{*}g}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\ dv_{\xi},\qquad\text{ and }

(18)

(1-\varepsilon)\ d_{\xi}(x,y)\leq d_{g}(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(x),\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(y))\leq(1+\varepsilon)\ d_{\xi}(x,y),

where
\xi
is the Euclidean metric.

In the following, for any
\alpha>0
, we will say that
I_{\varepsilon}=O(\varepsilon^{\alpha})
as
\varepsilon\to 0

if there exists a
C>0
such that
|I_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\varepsilon^{\alpha}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Additionally, if
(a_{i})
and
(b_{i})
are two real sequences then
a_{i}=o(b_{i})
means that for any
\varepsilon>0
and
i
big enough one has
|a_{i}|\leq\varepsilon|b_{i}|
.

2.2.
Triebel-Lizorkin and Besov spaces on Riemannian manifolds

Consider a smooth connected complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with positive injectivity radius

r_{0}
. Assume moreover that there exist
c>0
and, for any multi-index
\alpha
, constants
c_{\alpha}>0
such that

\det(g_{ij})\geq c,\qquad|D^{\alpha}g_{ij}|\leq c_{\alpha}

in the normal chart at any point in
M
.

H. Triebel defines in
[
28
, Definition 3]
the Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
F^{s}_{p,q}(M)
and the Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}(M)
on
M
using exponential charts at points
x_{j}
covering
M
and a subordinate partition of unity
(\psi_{j})_{j}
.
For simplicity, we suppose that
p,q\geq 1
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. They are defined by

F^{s}_{p,q}(M)=\{f\in D^{\prime}(M):\,\|f\|_{F^{s}_{p,q}(M)}:=\left(\sum_{j}\|\psi_{j}f\circ\exp_{x_{j}}\|_{F^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\right)^{1/p}<\infty\},

B^{s}_{p,q}(M)=(F^{s_{0}}_{p,p}(M),F^{s_{1}}_{p,p}(M))_{\theta,q}\qquad s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1},

where
D^{\prime}(M)
denotes the dual space of
C^{\infty}(M)
.
He proves in
[
28
]
that the definition of
F^{s}_{p,q}(M)
is independent of the choice of the points
x_{j}
and the partition of unity and that the definition of
B^{s}_{p,q}(M)
is independent of the choice of
s_{0}
and
s_{1}
. Moreover, they are Banach spaces when
p,q\geq 1
.
He then gives equivalent norms of these spaces to mimic the existing theory in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular, he proves that
F^{s}_{p,p}(M)=B^{s}_{p,p}(M)
and that

(19)

\|f\|_{F^{s}_{p,q}(M)}^{p}\simeq\sum_{j}\|\psi_{j}f\|_{F^{s}_{p,q}(M)}^{p}\qquad\text{if }p<\infty

(and usual modification when
p=\infty
).
Then in
[
29
]
, he proves the characterization of these spaces using finite differences but, as he mentions himself, with unnatural restrictions on the coefficient. In particular, he cannot consider the case
s\in(0,1)
.

2.3.
Equivalence

Next, we prove that the spaces
\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(M)
satisfy a property analogous to the one satisfied by the Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
F^{s}_{p,q}(M)
described in (
19
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold that we cover with a finite number of exponential charts at points
\{x_{i}:i=1,\dots,N\}
. Let
\{\eta_{i}:i=1,\dots,N\}
be a partition of unity associated to this covering. There exist constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
such that for any
u\in\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(M)
,

(20)

C^{\prime}\|u\|_{s,p}^{p}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\eta_{i}u\|_{s,p}^{p}\leq C\|u\|_{s,p}^{p}.

Proof.

First, we observe that

\displaystyle|\eta_{i}u(x)-\eta_{i}u(y)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}(\eta_{i}(x)^{p}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}+|u(y)|^{p}|\eta_{i}(x)-\eta_{i}(y)|^{p})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}(|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}+|u(y)|^{p}|\eta_{i}(x)-\eta_{i}(y)|^{p}).

Then we get

\displaystyle\iint_{M\times M}\frac{|\eta_{i}u(x)-\eta_{i}u(y)|^{p}}{d_{g}(x,y)^{n+sp}}\ dv_{g}(x)dv_{g}(y)\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\left(\iint_{M\times M}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{d_{g}(x,y)^{n+sp}}\ dv_{g}(x)dv_{g}(y)+\int_{M}|u(y)|^{p}\int_{M}\frac{|\eta_{i}(x)-\eta_{i}(y)|^{p}}{d_{g}(x,y)^{n+sp}}\ dv_{g}(x)dv_{g}(y)\right).

Since

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{|\eta_{i}(x)-\eta_{i}(y)|^{p}}{d_{g}(x,y)^{n+sp}}\ dv_{g}(x)<\infty\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ and }\hskip 14.22636pt\int_{M}|\eta_{i}u(x)|^{p}\ dv_{g}(x)<C\int_{M}|u(x)|^{p}\ dv_{g}(x),

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\eta_{i}u\|_{s,p}^{p}\leq C\|u\|_{s,p}^{p},

with
C>0
depending on
N,p,|M|
and
\|\eta_{i}\|_{\infty}
, for all
i=1,\dots,N
.

On the other hand, we note that
\|u\|_{p}=\|\sum_{i}\eta_{i}u\|_{p}\leq\sum_{i}\|\eta_{i}u\|_{p}
.
By Jensen’s inequality we have

(21)

\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq N^{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\eta_{i}u\|_{p}^{p}.

Furthermore, we can write

|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}=|\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\eta_{i}u(x)-\eta_{i}u(y))|^{p}\leq N^{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\eta_{i}u(x)-\eta_{i}u(y)|^{p}

and then

(22)

\iint_{M\times M}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{d_{g}(x,y)^{n+sp}}\,dv_{g}(x)dv_{g}(y)\leq C^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\iint_{M\times M}\frac{|\eta_{i}u(x)-\eta_{i}u(y)|^{p}}{d_{g}(x,y)^{n+sp}}\,dv_{g}(x)dv_{g}(y).

Thus, from (
21
) and (
22
), it follows

\|u\|_{s,p}^{p}=[u]_{s,p}^{p}+\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq C^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{N}([\eta_{i}u]_{s,p}^{p}+\|\eta_{i}u\|_{p}^{p})=C^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|u\|_{s,p}^{p}.

with
C^{\prime}>0
depending on
N,p
.
∎

As a corollary of Proposition
2.1
we can prove the Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For
\varepsilon>0
, let
\{\eta_{i},\,i=1,\dots,N\}
be a partition of unity adapted to the covering

\{B_{\delta}(x_{i}),\,i=1,\dots,N\}
given by Lemma (
2.1
) and let
v_{i}=(\eta_{i}u)\circ\text{exp}_{x_{i}}
.
In order to prove the Theorem, we have to check first that

\|\eta_{j}u\|_{s,p}\simeq\|v_{j}\|_{s,p}
.
Indeed we have

(1-\varepsilon)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v_{i}|^{p}\,dv_{\xi}\leq\int_{M}|\eta_{i}u|^{p}\,dv_{g}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v_{i}|^{p}\,dv_{\text{exp}_{x_{i}}^{*}g}\\
\leq(1+\varepsilon)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v_{i}|^{p}\,dv_{\xi}.

So we have

(23)

(1-\varepsilon)\|v_{i}\|_{p}^{p}\leq\|\eta_{i}u\|_{p}^{p}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|v_{i}\|_{p}^{p}.

Now we shall estimate
[\eta_{i}u]_{s,p}
.
Denote
U_{i}:=\text{exp}_{x_{i}}^{-1}(B_{\delta}(x_{i}))
,
i=1,\dots,N
.
Since
\text{supp}(v_{i})\subset U_{i}
,
we can write

(24)

[v_{i}]_{s,p}^{p}=2\iint_{x\in U_{i},y\not\in U_{i}}\frac{|v_{i}(x)-v_{i}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dxdy+\iint_{x\in U_{i},y\in U_{i}}\frac{|v_{i}(x)-v_{i}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dxdy.

Recall that
\text{supp}\,\eta_{i}
is a compact subset of
B_{\delta}(x_{i})
.
Denote
K_{i}:=\text{exp}_{x_{i}}^{-1}(\text{supp}(\eta_{i}))
.
Since
K_{i}
is compact, we can take some
\alpha>0
such that

\displaystyle+\iint_{|x-y|<\delta^{\prime},\,x\not\in U_{i},y\in U_{i}}\frac{|v_{i}(x)-v_{i}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dxdy+\iint_{|x-y|<\delta^{\prime},\,x,y\in U_{i}}\frac{|v_{i}(x)-v_{i}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=2\iint_{|x-y|<\delta^{\prime},\,x\in U_{i},y\not\in U_{i}}\frac{|v_{i}(x)-v_{i}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dxdy+\iint_{|x-y|<\delta^{\prime},\,x,y\in U_{i}}\frac{|v_{i}(x)-v_{i}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dxdy.

Recall that
\text{supp}\,\eta_{i}
is a compact subset of
B_{\delta}(x_{i})
.
Denote
K_{i}:=\text{exp}_{x_{i}}^{-1}(\text{supp}(\eta_{i}))
.
Since

is a compact, we can take some

such that

\displaystyle\iint_{|x-y|<\delta^{\prime},\,x\in U_{i},\,y\not\in U_{i}}\frac{|v_{i}(x)-v_{i}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{|x-y|<\delta^{\prime},\,x\in K_{i},\,y\not\in U_{i}}\frac{|v_{i}(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{K_{i}}|v_{i}(x)|^{2}\left(\int_{|x-y|<\delta^{\prime}}\frac{dy}{\alpha^{n+2s}}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,n,\delta^{\prime}}\int_{K_{i}}|v_{i}(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,n,\delta^{\prime},\varepsilon}\int_{M}\eta_{i}|u|^{2}\,dv_{g}.

We thus obtain

\displaystyle\|\eta_{i}u\|_{2^{*}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,n,p,\delta^{\prime},\varepsilon}\int_{M}\eta_{i}|u|^{2}\,dv_{g}+\ (1+\varepsilon)A\iint_{|x-y|<\delta^{\prime},\,x,y\in U_{i}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dxdy.

To bound the 2nd integral in the r.h.s. we write

\displaystyle v_{i}(x)-v_{i}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{i}(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(x))[u(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(x))-u(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(y))]

\displaystyle+u(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(y))[\eta_{i}(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(x))-\eta_{i}(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(y))].

Using the inequality
(a+b)^{2}\leq(1+\varepsilon)a^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}b^{2}
,

, we deduce

\displaystyle\iint_{|x-y|<\delta^{\prime},\,x,y\in U_{i}}\frac{|v_{i}(x)-v_{i}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\iint_{|x-y|<\delta^{\prime},\,x,y\in U_{i}}|\eta_{i}(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(x))|^{2}\frac{|u(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(x))-u(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(y))|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle+C_{\varepsilon}\iint_{|x-y|<\delta^{\prime},\,x,y\in U_{i}}\frac{|\eta_{i}(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(x))-\eta_{i}(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(y))|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}|u(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(y))|^{2}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I+II.

Using that
\eta_{i}\circ\text{exp}_{x_{i}}
is Lipschitz with a Lipschitz constant that can be chosen to depend on
\delta
, and thus on
\varepsilon
, and not on

, we can bound II:

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon,\delta}\int_{U_{i}}|u(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(y))|^{2}\left(\int_{|x-y|<\delta^{\prime}}\frac{dx}{|x-y|^{n-(1-s)2}}\right)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{\varepsilon,\delta}}{1-s}\int_{U_{i}}|u(\text{exp}_{x_{i}}(y))|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon,\delta,s}\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{i})}|u|^{2}\,dv_{g}.

Concerning

,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+C\varepsilon)\iint_{x,y\in B_{\delta}(x_{i}),\,|\text{exp}_{x_{i}}^{-1}(x)-\text{exp}_{x_{i}}^{-1}(y)|<\delta^{\prime}}\eta_{i}(x)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|\text{exp}_{x_{i}}^{-1}(x)-\text{exp}_{x_{i}}^{-1}(y)|^{n+2s}}dv_{g}(x)dv_{g}(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+C\varepsilon)\iint_{x,y\in B_{\delta}(x_{i})}\eta_{i}(x)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{d_{g}(x,y)^{n+2s}}\,dv_{g}(x)dv_{g}(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Lambda(1+C\varepsilon)\iint_{x,y\in B_{\delta}(x_{i})}\eta_{i}(x)|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\mathcal{K}(x,y;g)\,dv_{g}(x)dv_{g}(y)

where we used (

We thus obtain

\displaystyle\|\eta_{i}u\|_{2^{*}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{M}\eta_{i}|u|^{2}\,dv_{g}+C\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{i})}|u|^{2}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle+\ \Lambda(1+C\varepsilon)A\int_{M}\eta_{i}(x)\left(\int_{M}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\mathcal{K}(x,y;g)\,dv_{g}(y)\right)\,dv_{g}(x)

where

.
Since the balls

,

overlap a finite number of times, summing this inequality over

∎

5.
Applications to equations with a fractional non-local operator

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
.
The proof is similar to the classical one in the local case (see, e.g.
[
18
]
), but we prefer to give a full demonstration as some technical differences arise.

We consider the critical equation (
10
) for
p=2
, which is

(32)

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{K}}u+hu=f|u|^{2^{*}-2}u.

We are interested in the weak formulation of that equation given by the following problem:

\iint_{M\times M}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))\mathcal{K}(x,y;g)\,dv_{g}(x)dv_{g}(y)\ +\ \int_{M}huv=\int_{M}f|u|^{2^{*}-2}uvdv_{g}(x),

for all
v\in W^{s,2}(M),
and
u\in W^{s,2}(M).

Critical points of
J_{\mathcal{K}}
in
H
are solutions to the problem (
32
), where
J_{\mathcal{K}}
and
H
are defined in (
13
) and (
14
).

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Denote
\mu_{0}:=\inf_{u\in H}J_{\mathcal{K}}(u)
. We shall prove that there is a
u_{0}\in H
which attained the infimum in
\mu_{0}
.
We approximate the minimization problem by the subcritical problem

\mu_{q}:=\inf_{u\in H_{q}}J_{\mathcal{K}}(u)
where

H_{q}=\{u\in W^{s,2}(M):\,\int_{M}f|u|^{q}=1\}
,
1\leq q<2^{*}
.
Since
q<2^{*}
, this infimum is attained at some
u_{q}\in H_{q}
due to Theorem
1.2
.

Using the constant test-function
1/(\int_{M}fdv_{g})^{1/q}\in H_{q}
to estimate
\mu_{q}
, we obtain

\mu_{q}\leq J_{\mathcal{K}}\left(\frac{1}{(\int_{M}fdv_{g})^{1/q}}\right)\leq\dfrac{\|h\|_{\infty}}{(\int_{M}fdv_{g})^{2/q}}\leq C.

Since
J_{\mathcal{K}}
is coercive, we deduce that
(u_{q})
is bounded in
W^{s,2}(M)
.
Then
u_{q}\to u_{0}
as
q\to 2^{*}
weakly in
W^{s,2}(M)
and strongly in
L^{2}(M)
.
Thus
u_{0}
is a weak solution of (
32
).

An easy claim is that
\mu_{q}\to\mu_{0}
as
q\to 2^{*}
. Indeed, given
\varepsilon>0
there is a
u\in H
such that

J_{\mathcal{K}}(u)\leq\mu_{0}+\varepsilon
. It follows that

\mu_{q}\leq J_{\mathcal{K}}\left(\frac{u}{(\int_{M}f|u|^{q})^{1/q}}\right).

It is clear that
(\int_{M}f|u|^{q})^{1/q}\to(\int_{M}f|u|^{2^{*}})^{1/{2^{*}}}=1
as
q\to 2^{*}
. Then

\limsup\mu_{q}\leq J_{\mathcal{K}}(u)\leq\mu_{0}+\varepsilon.

Hence,
\limsup\mu_{q}\leq\mu_{0}
as
q\to 2^{*}
.

Conversely, it follows from Hölder’s inequality that

1=\int_{M}f|u_{q}|^{q}dv_{g}=\int_{M}f^{1-q/2^{*}}\left(f^{1/2^{*}}|u_{q}|\right)^{q}dv_{g}\leq\left(\int_{M}fdv_{g}\right)^{1-q/2^{*}}\left(\int_{M}f|u_{q}|^{2^{*}}dv_{g}\right)^{q/2^{*}}.

Thus we have
1\leq\liminf\left(\int_{M}f|u_{q}|^{2^{*}}\right)
as
q\to 2^{*}
. Noting that

\mu_{0}\leq J_{\mathcal{K}}\left(\frac{u_{q}}{\left(\int_{M}f|u_{q}|^{2^{*}}dv_{g}\right)^{1/2^{*}}}\right)=\frac{\mu_{q}}{\left(\int_{M}f|u_{q}|^{2^{*}}dv_{g}\right)^{2/2^{*}}},

we then get that
\mu_{0}\leq\liminf\mu_{q}
as
q\to 2^{*}
.
It follows that
\lim\limits_{q\to 2^{*}}\mu_{q}=\mu_{0}
, and the above claim is proved.

To prove that
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
, we use Theorem
1.3
. We write

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}f|u_{q}|^{q}dv_{g}\right)^{2/q}\leq\left(\max\,f\right)^{2/q}\left(\int_{M}|u_{q}|^{2^{*}}dv_{g}\right)^{2/2^{*}}|M|^{(1-q/2^{*})2/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\max\,f\right)^{2/q}|M|^{(1-q/2^{*})2/q}\left(\left(K(n,s,2)+\varepsilon\right)\left(\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{K}}u_{q},u_{q}\right)^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|u_{q}\|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\max\,f\right)^{2/q}|M|^{(1-q/2^{*})2/q}\left((K(n,s,2)+\varepsilon)2\mu_{q}+(C_{\varepsilon}+(K(n,s,2)+\varepsilon)\|h\|_{\infty})\|u_{q}\|_{2}^{2}\right).

Since
\limsup_{q\to 2^{*}}\mu_{q}\leq\mu_{0}<(2(\max\,f)^{2/2^{*}}K(n,s,2))^{-1}
we deduce that

\|u_{0}\|_{2}\geq C>0
so that
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
.

It remains to prove that
u_{0}\in H
i.e. that
\int_{M}f|u_{0}|^{2^{*}}=1
. Indeed,
from the weak convergence
u_{q}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
on
W^{s,2}(M)
, and the fundamental property of
the weak limit (the norm of a weak limit is less than or equal to the infimum limit of
the norms of the sequence), we have

\|u_{0}\|_{s,2}\leq\liminf\ \|u_{q}\|_{s,2},

from which it follows

(\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{K}}u_{0},u_{0})^{2}\leq\liminf\ (\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{K}}u_{q},u_{q})^{2}.

As
u_{q}\to u_{0}
strongly on
L^{2}(M)
, we know that
\|u_{q}\|_{2}\to\|u_{0}\|_{2}
and

\int_{M}h|u_{q}|^{2}\to\int_{M}h|u_{0}|^{2}
. Then it follows

(33)

(\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{K}}u_{0},u_{0})^{2}+\int_{M}h|u_{0}|^{2}\leq\liminf\left((\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{K}}u_{q},u_{q})^{2}+\int_{M}h|u_{q}|^{2}\right).

Taking
u_{0}
as a test function in the equation where
u_{0}
is a weak solution, we have
that the l.h.s. of (
33
) is equal to
\int_{M}f|u_{0}|^{2^{*}}dv_{g}
.
Analogously, the r.h.s. is
\lim_{q\to 2*}\int_{M}f|u_{q}|^{q}dv_{g}=1.

Finally we obtain

\int_{M}f|u_{0}|^{2^{*}}dv_{g}\leq 1.

To prove
\int_{M}f|u_{0}|^{2^{*}}dv_{g}\geq 1,
we note that

\mu_{0}\leq\frac{J_{\mathcal{K}}(u_{0})}{\left(\int_{M}f|u_{0}|^{2^{*}}dv_{g}\right)^{2/2^{*}}}.

Since

J_{\mathcal{K}}(u_{0})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}f|u_{0}|^{2^{*}}dv_{g},

we get then

\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{M}f|u_{0}|^{2^{*}}dv_{g}\right)^{1-2/2^{*}}\geq\mu_{0}=\lim_{q\to 2*}\mu_{q}=\lim_{q\to 2*}J_{\mathcal{K}}(u_{q})=\lim_{q\to 2*}\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}f|u_{q}|^{q}dv_{g}=\frac{1}{2},

and the Theorem is proved.

∎

Using the constant test-function
v=1/\left(\int_{M}fdv_{g}\right)^{1/2^{*}}\in H
we obtain

Corollary 5.1
.

If
f\geq 0
and
h
are smooth functions on
M
such that

J_{\mathcal{K}}
satisfies the coercivity condition (

) and

\frac{\left(\max\,f\right)^{2/2^{*}}\left(\int_{M}hdv_{g}\right)}{\left(\int_{M}fdv_{g}\right)^{2/2^{*}}}<K(n,s,2)^{-1},

then (

) holds and thus (
32
) has a non-trivial solution.

Proposition 6.1

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \,dv_{T^{*}(\exp_{x_{0}})^{*}g}(x)dv_{T^{*}(\exp_{x_{0}})^{*}g}(y)

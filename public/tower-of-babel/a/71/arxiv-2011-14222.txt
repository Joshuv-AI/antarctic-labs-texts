The Brown measure of unbounded variables with free semicircular imaginary part
Ching-Wei Ho

Abstract
. Let x 0 x_{0} be an unbounded self-adjoint operator such that the Brown measure of x 0 x_{0} exists in the sense of Haagerup and Schultz. Also let σ ~ α \tilde{\sigma}_{\alpha} and σ β \sigma_{\beta} be semicircular variables with variances α ≥ 0 \alpha\geq 0 and β > 0 \beta>0 respectively. Suppose x 0 x_{0} , σ α \sigma_{\alpha} , and σ ~ β \tilde{\sigma}_{\beta} are all freely independent. We compute the Brown measure of x 0 + σ ~ α + i ​ σ β x_{0}+\tilde{\sigma}_{\alpha}+i\sigma_{\beta} , extending the recent work which assume x 0 x_{0} is a bounded self-adjoint random variable. We use the PDE method introduced by Driver, Hall and Kemp to compute the Brown measure. The computation of the PDE relies on a charaterization of the class of operators where the Brown measure exists. The Brown measure in this unbounded case has the same structure as in the bounded case; it has connections to the free convolution x 0 + σ α + β x_{0}+\sigma_{\alpha+\beta} . We also compute the example where x 0 x_{0} is Cauchy-distributed.

1.
Introduction

An elliptic variable
c_{\alpha,\beta}
is an element in a
W^{*}
-probability space
(\mathscr{A},\tau)
of the form
\tilde{\sigma}_{\alpha}+i\sigma_{\beta}
where
\tilde{\sigma}_{\alpha}
and
\sigma_{\beta}
are free semicircular random variables with variances
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
respectively. In this paper, we also consider the case
\alpha=0
; in this case,
c_{\alpha,\beta}
is a “degenerate” elliptic variable, and is just a imaginary multiple of the semicircular variable. We do not consider the case
\alpha>0
and
\beta=0
in this paper. In this case,
c_{\alpha,\beta}
is Hermitian — a semicircular variable. The elliptic variable
c_{\alpha,\beta}
is the limit in
\ast
-distribution of the Gaussian random matrix
\sqrt{\alpha}\tilde{X}_{N}+i\sqrt{\beta}X_{N}
where
\tilde{X}_{N}
and
X_{N}
are independent Gaussian unitary ensembles (GUEs); see
[
24
]
and the book
[
20
]
. Also, the empirical eigenvalue distribution of the random matrix
\sqrt{\alpha}\tilde{X}_{N}+i\sqrt{\beta}X_{N}
converges to the Brown measure of the elliptic variable
c_{\alpha,\beta}

[
11
]
(See
[
7
,
12
]
for the definition of Brown measure). The Brown measure of
c_{\alpha,\beta}
has a simple structure; it is the uniform measure in a region bounded by a certain ellipse
[
5
]
.

Let
x_{0}
be a self-adjoint noncommutative random variable in the
W^{*}
-probability space
\mathscr{A}
containing
c_{\alpha,\beta}
that is freely independent from
c_{\alpha,\beta}
. In particular,
x_{0}
is a
bounded
random variable. The author computed the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
in
[
16
]
. In this paper, we extend the computation to an
unbounded
self-adjoint random variable
x_{0}
affiliated with
\mathscr{A}
for which the Brown measure exists in the sense of Haagerup and Schultz
[
12
]
; we refer this class of unbounded random variables as the Haagerup–Schultz class. Our results show that the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
has the same structure as in the bounded case; in particuar, it has direct connections to the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{s}
, where
c_{s}=c_{s/2,s/2}
is the circular variable with
s=\alpha+\beta
, and the law of
x_{0}+\sigma_{s}
. We also compute, as an example, the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
for
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta>0
when
x_{0}
has a Cauchy distribution.

When
x_{0}
is a bounded random variable and is the limit in distribution of a sequence
A_{N}
of Hermitian random matrix independent from the GUEs
\tilde{X}_{N}
and
X_{N}
, Śniady
[
22
]
shows that the empirical eigenvalue distribution of

A_{N}+\sqrt{\alpha}\tilde{X}_{N}+i\sqrt{\beta}X_{N},\quad\alpha>0,\beta>0

converges to the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
, as
N\to\infty
. When
x_{0}
is unbounded, computer simulations show that the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
is a reasonable candidate of the limiting eigenvalue distribution of the random matrix model
A_{N}+\sqrt{\alpha}\tilde{X}_{N}+i\sqrt{\beta}X_{N}
, where the empirical eigenvalue distribution of
A_{N}
converges to the law of
x_{0}
.

The computation of the Brown measure in this paper is based on the PDE method introduced by Driver, Hall and Kemp in
[
8
]
. In
[
8
]
, Driver, Hall and Kemp computed the Brown measure of the free multiplicative Brownian motion using the Hamilton–Jacobi method. The author computes in later work with collaborators the Brown measures of other variables using the same PDE method
[
13
,
16
,
17
]
.
The results in these papers assume the boundedness of
x_{0}
in the process of computing the PDE. The key observation in this paper is to show that the function

S(t,\lambda,\varepsilon)=\tau[\log((x_{0}+i\sigma_{t}-\lambda)^{*}(x_{0}+i\sigma_{t}-\lambda)+\varepsilon)],\quad t>0,\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\varepsilon>0

(1.1)

satisfies the same PDE derived in
[
13
]
when
x_{0}
is an unbounded random variable affiliated with
\mathscr{A}
in the Haagerup–Schultz class. The solution of the PDE yields the Brown measure of
x_{0}+i\sigma_{t}
. By considering the free additive convolution
x_{0}+\tilde{\sigma}_{\alpha}
in place of
x_{0}
, we derive the family of the Brown measures of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
for
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta>0
. In particular, by taking
\alpha=\beta>0
, we extend the results in
[
17
]
to unbounded self-adjoint
x_{0}
.

The method of deriving the PDE (
1.1
) in
[
13
]
for the bounded
x_{0}
case is to apply the free Itô formula; the partial derivatives
\partial S/\partial\lambda
and
\partial S/\partial\varepsilon
of
S
can also be computed easily using the formula given in
[
7
, Lemma 1.1]
. When
x_{0}
is unbounded, the free Itô formula does not apply; furthermore, since the spectrum of
(x_{0}+i\sigma_{t}-\lambda)^{*}(x_{0}+i\sigma_{t}-\lambda)+\varepsilon
may be an unbounded set on the positive half-line, it takes extra work to verify that the partial derivatives of
S
are given by the “same” formula as in the bounded
x_{0}
case. To overcome these issues, in this paper, we use the characterization of
x_{0}=AB^{-1}
for some
A,B\in\mathscr{A}
given in
[
12
, Lemma 2.4]
to write

S(t,\lambda,\varepsilon)=\tau[\log(|x_{0}|^{2}+1)]+\tau[\log((\lambda B-C_{t})^{*}(\lambda B-C_{t})+B^{2}\varepsilon)]

where
C_{t}=A+i\sigma_{t}B
. The point is that the above form of
S
is written in terms of bounded operators in
\mathscr{A}
, except for the constant
\tau[\log(|x_{0}|^{2}+1)]
. And then we prove that the partial derivatives and the time derivative of
S
are all well-approximated by the corresponding partial derivatives of the function

T_{\eta}(t,\lambda,\varepsilon)=\tau[\log((\lambda B-C_{t})^{*}(\lambda B-C_{t})+B^{2}\varepsilon+\eta)].

The extra regularization
\eta
allows us to apply the free Itô formula and the derivative formula
[
7
, Lemma 1.1]
.

1.1.
Statement of results

In this subsection, we list out the Brown measures computed in this paper according to the complexity of their structures. Let
(\mathscr{A},\tau)
be a
W^{*}
-probability space and
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
be the algebra of closed densely defined operators affiliated with
\mathscr{A}
. Let
x_{0}\in\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
be self-adjoint satisfying the following standing assumption throughout the paper.

1.1
.

The law
\mu_{x_{0}}
of
x_{0}
is not a Dirac mass at a point on
\mathbb{R}
, and satisfies

\int_{1}^{\infty}\log|x|\,d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)<\infty.

(1.2)

The assumption (
1.2
) means that

is in the Haagerup–Schultz class

; that is, the Brown measure of
x_{0}
exists in the sense of Haagerup and Schultz (See Definition
2.5
). If
\mu_{x_{0}}
is a Dirac mass, then the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
is just the translation of the elliptic law in the case
\alpha>0
, or just the translation of the semicircular law on the imaginary axis in the case
\alpha=0
.

We start by Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{t}
, where
c_{t}=c_{t/2,t/2}
is the circular variable, freely independent from
x_{0}
. Recall that
\sigma_{t}
denotes a semicircular variable of variance
t
, freely independent from
x_{0}
. The following theorem is proved in Corollaries
6.6
and
6.7
(2). When
x_{0}
is bounded, this theorem is established in Theorems 3.13 and 3.14 in
[
17
]
.

1.2
.

The following statements about the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{t}
hold.

(1)

The Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{t}
has full measure on an open set
\Lambda_{x_{0},t}
of the form

\Lambda_{x_{0},t}=\{\left.u+iv\in\mathbb{C}\right||v|<v_{x_{0},t}(u)\}

for a certain function

that appears in the study of the free convolution
x_{0}+\sigma_{t}
. Then the density of the Brown measure on

has the special form

w_{t}(u+iv)=\frac{1}{\pi t}\left(1-\frac{t}{2}\frac{d}{du}\int\frac{x\,d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u-x)^{2}+v_{x_{0},t}(u)^{2}}\right)

for all
u+iv\in\Lambda_{x_{0},t}
; in particular, the density is strictly positive and constant along vertical segments in
\Lambda_{x_{0},t}
.

(2)

Consider the function

\psi_{x_{0},t}(u)=u+t\int\frac{(u-x)\,d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u-x)^{2}+v_{x_{0},t}(u)^{2}},\quad u\in\mathbb{R}

(1.3)

that appears in the study of the free convolution

x_{0}+c_{t}
by the map

x_{0}+\sigma_{t}
.

The Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{s}
“generates” the family of the Brown measures of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
with
\alpha+\beta=s
in the sense of push-forward measure. The following theorem is established in Theorem
6.4
and Corollary

. This result extends Theorems 3.7 and 4.1 in

]

affiliated with
\mathscr{A}
.

1.3
.

The following about the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
, for
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta>0
holds.

(1)

The Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
has full measure on an open set

of the form

\Omega_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}=\{\left.u+iv\in\mathbb{C}\right||v|<\varphi_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}(u)\}

(1.4)

for a certain function

. There exists a strictly increasing homeomorphism

on

for all

2.
Background and previous results

2.1.
Free probability

We first introduce noncommutative random variables and freeness (or free independence).

2.1
.

(1)

A
W^{*}
-probability space

(\mathscr{A},\tau)
is a finite von Neumann algebra
\mathscr{A}
with a normal, faithful, tracial state
\tau
.

(2)

The algebra
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
affiliated with
\mathscr{A}
is the set of closed, densely defined operators such that every
a\in\mathscr{A}
has a polar decomposition

a=u|a|=u\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda\,dE_{|a|}(\lambda)

where
u\in\mathscr{A}
is unitary and the projection-valued spectral measure
E_{|a|}
takes values in
\mathscr{A}
. The elements in
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
are called (noncommutative) random variables. When we distinguish elements from
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
and
\mathscr{A}
, any
a\in\mathscr{A}
is called a bounded random variable and any
a\in\tilde{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\mathscr{A}
is called an unbounded random variable.

(3)

Given any self-adjoint random variable
a\in\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
with spectral measure
E_{a}
, the law
\mu_{a}
of
a
is the probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
defined by

\mu_{a}(B)=\tau(E_{a}(B))

for any Borel set
B
.

(4)

The von Neumann subalgebras
\mathscr{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{A}_{n}\subset\mathscr{A}
are said to be free, or freely independent, if given any
i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
i_{k}\neq i_{k+1}
and
a_{i_{k}}\in\mathscr{A}_{i_{k}}
for all
k
satisfying
\tau(a_{i_{k}})=0
for all
1\leq k\leq m
, we have

\tau(a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{m}})=0.

The random variables
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
are free, or freely independent, if the von Neumann subalgebras
\mathscr{A}_{k}
generated by all the spectral projections of
a_{k}
are free.

In this paper, we denote
\sigma_{t}
or
\tilde{\sigma}_{t}
by the semicircular variable with variance
t
; that is, the law
\mu_{\sigma_{t}}
is given by

\frac{1}{2\pi t}\sqrt{4t-x^{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{|x|\leq 2\sqrt{t}}\,dx.

One way to study the law of
a\in\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
is to use the
Cauchy transform

G_{a}
of
a
defined by

G_{a}(z)=\tau[(z-a)^{-1}]=\int\frac{d\mu_{a}(x)}{z-x},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}^{+},

where
\mathbb{C}^{+}
is the upper half plane. The Cauchy transform is determined by the law
\mu_{a}
of
a
. Given any finite measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
, one can define the Cauchy transform
G_{\mu}
of
\mu
by replacing
\mu_{a}
in the right hand side of the above equation by
\mu
. We can recover the law
\mu_{a}
from
G_{a}
by the Stieltjes inversion formula

d\mu_{a}(x)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathrm{Im}\,G_{a}(x+i\varepsilon)\,dx

where the limit is in weak sense.

The Cauchy transform
G_{a}
of
a
is invertible on some truncated Stolz angle

\Gamma_{\alpha,\beta}=\{\left.x+iy\in\mathbb{C}\right|y>\beta\textrm{ and }x<\alpha y\}

for positive
\alpha
and
\beta
. The inverse of
G_{a}
is called the
K
-transform
of
a
and is denoted by
K_{a}
. The
R
-transform

R_{a}
is defined as

R_{a}(z)=K_{a}(z)-\frac{1}{z}.

The following fact about
R
-transform was first discovered by Voiculescu
[
23
]
for bounded random variables, then by Maassen
[
19
]
for possibly unbounded random variables with finite variance, and by Bercovici and Voiculescu
[
2
]
for arbitrary unbounded random variables.

2.2
.

If
a,b\in\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
are self-adjoint freely independent random variables, then, on the common domain where the
R
-transforms
R_{a}
,
R_{b}
, and
R_{a+b}
are defined,

R_{a+b}=R_{a}+R_{b}.

2.2.
Free addition convolution

When
x_{0}\in\mathscr{A}
is a bounded self-adjoint random variable free from
\sigma_{t}
, the Brown measure of
x_{0}+i\sigma_{t}
has direct connections to
x_{0}+\sigma_{t}
, by
[
13
,
17
]
. In this paper, we will show that this is also the case for unbounded self-adjoint random variable
x_{0}
. In this section, we review the free additive convolution of
x_{0}
and
\sigma_{t}
, where
x_{0}
can be taken to be unbounded.

Biane
[
3
]
computed the free additive convolution of a self-adjoint random variable
x_{0}\in\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
and the semicircular variable
\sigma_{t}
. Before we state the results, we first introduce some notations.

2.3
.

Denote by
\mu_{x_{0}}
the law of the self-adjoint random variable
x_{0}\in\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
.

(1)

Let
H_{x_{0},t}
be defined by

H_{x_{0},t}(z)=z+t\int\frac{d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{z-x},\quad z\not\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{x_{0}})

(2.1)

where
\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{x_{0}})
denotes the support of
\mu_{x_{0}}
.

(2)

Define a nonnegative function on
\mathbb{R}
by

v_{x_{0},t}(u)=\inf\left\{v>0\left|\int\frac{d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u-x)^{2}+v^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{t}\right.\right\}.

This means, for a given
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
v_{x_{0},t}(u)
is the unique positive number (if exists) such that
\int\frac{d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u-x)^{2}+v_{x_{0},t}(u)^{2}}=\frac{1}{t}
; if such a number does not exist,
v_{x_{0},t}(u)=0
.

(3)

Let
\Gamma_{x_{0},t}=\{u+iv\in\mathbb{C}\left|v>v_{x_{0},t}(u)\right.\}
be the region in the upper half plane
\mathbb{C}^{+}
that is above the graph of
v_{x_{0},t}
.

Recall that
G_{a}
denotes the Cauchy transform of
a\in\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
. The following theorem, due to Biane
[
3
]
, computes the free additive convolution of a self-adjoint random variable
x\in\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
and the semicircular variable
\sigma_{t}
.

2.4
[
3
]
.

(1)

The function
H_{x_{0},t}
is an injective conformal map from
\Gamma_{x_{0},t}
onto the upper half plane
\mathbb{C}^{+}
. The function extends to a homeomorphism from
\overline{\Gamma}_{x_{0},t}
onto
\mathbb{C}^{+}\cup\mathbb{R}
. Hence, for any
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
H_{x_{0},t}(u+iv_{x_{0},t}(u))
is real.

(2)

The function
H_{x_{0},t}
satisfies

G_{x_{0}+\sigma_{t}}(H_{x_{0},t}(z))=G_{x_{0}}(z).

(2.2)

(3)

The law of
x_{0}+\sigma_{t}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure; its density
p_{x_{0},t}
can be computed using the function
\psi_{x_{0},t}(u)=H_{x_{0},t}(u+iv_{x_{0},t}(u))
. The function
\psi_{x_{0},t}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a homeomorphism, and

p_{x_{0},t}(\psi_{x_{0},t}(u))=\frac{v_{x_{0},t}(u)}{\pi t}.

When
a
and
b
are arbitrary self-adjoint random variables, the free additive convolution
a+b
can be studied using Cauchy transforms in a way similar to (
2.2
) (the method of subordination functions). The subordination functions are first discovered by Voiculescu
[
25
]
for bounded random variables
a
and
b
. Then it is further developed by Biane
[
4
]
, and again by Voiculescu
[
26
]
to a even more general setting. Belinschi and Bercovici
[
1
]
use a complex analysis approach to show the existence of subordination functions (where they do not assume the random variables to be bounded).

2.3.
The Brown measure

In this section, we review the definition of the Brown measure, first introduced by Brown
[
7
]
for operators in a von Neumann algebra with a faithful, semifinite, normal trace, then extended to a class
\mathscr{A}^{\Delta}
of unbounded operators by Haagerup and Schultz
[
12
]
. We refer this class of unbounded operators as the Haagerup–Schultz class. In the following, we introduce the class
\mathscr{A}^{\Delta}
.

2.5
.

We denote by
\mathscr{A}^{\Delta}
the set of operators
a
in
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
(see Definition
2.1
(2)) such that

\tau(\log^{+}|a|)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\log^{+}(\lambda)\,d\mu_{|a|}(\lambda)

where
\log^{+}

6.1
.

(1)

Given any self-adjoint
a\in\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
and

H_{a,r}(z)=z+rG_{a}(z),\quad z\not\in\sigma(a).

This definition is consistent with (

is an injective conformal map on

(2)

Let
a\in\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
be self-adjoint. Also let
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta>0
and write
s=\alpha+\beta

f_{a,\alpha,\beta}(u)=\mathrm{Re}[H_{\alpha-\beta}(u+iv_{x_{0},s}(u))].

This function is strictly increasing and is a homeomorphism onto
\mathbb{R}

f_{a,\alpha,\beta}^{\prime}(u)>0
for all

(3)

Let
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
and write
s=\alpha+\beta

\varphi_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}(u)=2\beta v_{a,s}(f_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}^{-1}(u))/s

\Omega_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}=\left.\{u+iv\in\mathbb{C}\right||v|<\varphi_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}(u)\}.

By Definition 3.1 and Proposition 3.6 of
[

\Omega_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}=[H_{x_{0},\alpha-\beta}(\Lambda_{x_{0},s}^{c})]^{c}
. Remark that
\varphi_{x_{0},0,t}=\varphi_{x_{0},t}
, which is defined in Theorem
5.1
(4).

We will see that
\Omega_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}
is an open set of full measure with respect to the Brown measure of

.

6.2
.

The author uses a different parametrization for the elliptic variable in

If we apply Theorem

6.3
Theorem 3.4 of

Let

. Then, by writing

f_{a,\beta}^{-1}(u)=u+\beta\int\frac{(u_{0}-x)\,d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u_{0}-x)^{2}+v_{x_{0},s}(u_{0})^{2}},\quad u\in\mathbb{R}.

We are ready to state the main result in this case.

6.4
.

The Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on the plane. The open set
\Omega_{\alpha,\beta}
is a set of full measure of the Brown measure. The density of the Brown measure on

is given by

w_{\alpha,\beta}(u+iv)=\frac{1}{4\pi\beta}\left(1+2\beta\frac{d}{du}\int\frac{(u_{0}-x)\,d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u_{0}-x)^{2}+v_{x_{0},s}(u_{0})^{2}}\right),\quad u+iv\in\Omega_{\alpha,\beta},

where
f_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}(u_{0})=u
. The density is strictly positive and constant along the vertical segments in
\Omega_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}
.

6.5
.

If
\alpha=0
and
\beta=t

6.3
implies that

w_{\alpha,\beta}(u+iv)=\frac{1}{4\pi t}\left(1+2\frac{d}{du}(f_{x_{0},t}^{-1}(u)-u)\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi t}\left(\frac{d}{du}f_{x_{0},\beta}^{-1}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\right),

giving the density of the Brown measure of
x_{0}+i\sigma_{t}

When

is a bounded self-adjoint random variable, the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{t}
is computed in
[

\Lambda_{x_{0},t}
, and the density can be written as terms of the derivative of the function

in Theorem

6.6
.

The Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{t}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on the plane. The open set
\Lambda_{x_{0},t}
is a set of full measure of the Brown measure. The density on
\Lambda_{x_{0},t}
has the form

\displaystyle w_{t/2,t/2}(u+iv)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi t}\frac{d\psi_{x_{0},t}}{du}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi t}\left(1-\frac{t}{2}\frac{d}{du}\int\frac{x\,d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u-x)^{2}+v_{x_{0},t}(u)^{2}}\right),\quad u+iv\in\Lambda_{x_{0},t},

where
\psi_{x_{0},t}
is defined in Theorem
2.4
. The density is strictly positive and constant along the vertical segments in
\Lambda_{x_{0},t}
.

Proof.

Take
\alpha=\beta=t/2
in Theorem
6.4
. Then,
\alpha+\beta=t
and
2\beta/(\alpha+\beta)=1
, so that
f_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}
is the identity map, and

\Omega_{x_{0},t/2,t/2}=\left.\{u+iv\in\mathbb{C}\right||v|<v_{x_{0},t}(u)\}=\Lambda_{x_{0},t}.

Then we can compute the density using the formula in Theorem
6.4

\displaystyle w_{t/2,t/2}(u+iv)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi t}\left(1+t\frac{d}{du}\int\frac{(u-x)\,d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u-x)^{2}+v_{x_{0},t}(u)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi t}\frac{d\psi_{x_{0},t}}{du},\quad u+iv\in\Lambda_{x_{0},t};

the last equality follows from the definition of
\psi_{x_{0},t}(u)=H_{x_{0},t}(u+iv_{x_{0},t}(u))
. By Definiton
2.3
(2) of

\int\frac{(u-x)\,d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u-x)^{2}+v_{x_{0},t}(u)^{2}}=\frac{u}{t}-\int\frac{x\,d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u-x)^{2}+v_{x_{0},t}(u)^{2}}.

The other claimed formula of
w_{t}
follows from a straightforward algebraic computation.
∎

The following corollary establishes the push-forward properties between the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
, the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{s}
, and the law of
x_{0}+\sigma_{t}
, where
s=\alpha+\beta
is the total variance of the elliptic variable
c_{\alpha,\beta}
.

6.7
.

Write
s=\alpha+\beta
. Let
U_{\alpha,\beta}:\Lambda_{x_{0},s}\to\Omega_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}
be defined by

U_{\alpha,\beta}(u+iv)=f_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}(u)+i\frac{2\beta v}{s}.

(6.1)

Then
U_{\alpha,\beta}
extends to a homeomorphism from
\overline{\Lambda}_{x_{0},s}
to
\overline{\Omega}_{\alpha,\beta}
and agrees with
H_{x_{0},\alpha-\beta}
on the boundary of
\Lambda_{x_{0},s}
. Furthermore, the following push-forward properties hold.

(1)

The push-forward of the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{s}
under the map
U_{\alpha,\beta}
is the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
.

(2)

The push-forward of the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
by the map

Q_{\alpha,\beta}(u+iv)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\alpha-\beta}[su-2\beta f_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}^{-1}(u)],\quad&\textrm{if $\alpha\neq\beta$}\\
H_{x_{0},s}(u+iv_{x_{0},s}(u)),\quad&\textrm{if $\alpha=\beta=s/2$}.\end{cases}

is the law of
x_{0}+\sigma_{s}
.

6.8
.

Write
u=f_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}(u_{0})
. When
\alpha\neq\beta
, we can compute that

Q_{\alpha,\beta}(u+iv)=u_{0}+s\int\frac{(u_{0}-x)\,d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u_{0}-x)^{2}+v_{x_{0},s}(u_{0})^{2}}=H_{x_{0},s}(u_{0}+iv_{x_{0},s}(u)).

If
\alpha=\beta=s/2
, then
u=u_{0}
and the above equation reduces to
H_{x_{0},s}(u+iv_{x_{0},s}(u))
, which is the definition of
Q_{\alpha,\beta}
when
\alpha=\beta=s/2
.

Proof.

That
U_{\alpha,\beta}
extends to a homeomorphism from
\overline{\Lambda}_{x_{0},s}
to
\overline{\Omega_{\alpha,\beta}}
and agrees with
H_{x_{0},\alpha-\beta}
on the boundary of
\Lambda_{x_{0},s}
follows from the proof of Proposition 4.2 of
[
16
]
.

For
\alpha\neq\beta
, the proof follows from the one given for Theorem 4.1 of
[
16
]
. For the case
\alpha=\beta=s/2
, the proof follows from the one given for Theorem 3.13 of
[
17
]
.
∎

Using the push-forward property in Point 1 of Corollary

in terms of the density of the Brown measure of

6.9
.

For any

w_{\alpha,\beta}(u+iv)=\frac{1}{r}\frac{w_{s/2,s/2}(u_{0}+iv_{0})}{r+2\pi(1-r)s\cdot w_{s/2,s/2}(u_{0}+iv_{0})}.

Proof.

This follows from a direct computation of integration of substitution using Corollary

6.10

Writing

f_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}^{\prime\prime}(u_{0})=2\pi(\alpha-\beta)w_{s/2,s/2}^{\prime}(u_{0})=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{s}\psi_{x_{0},s}^{\prime\prime}(u_{0})

for all
u_{0}\in\Lambda_{x_{0},s}\cap\mathbb{R}
, where
\psi_{x_{0},s}(u_{0})=H_{x_{0},s}(u_{0}+iv_{x_{0},s}(u_{0}))
is defined in Theorem

Proof.

Using the formula

f_{x_{0},\alpha,\beta}(u_{0})=u_{0}+(\alpha-\beta)\int\frac{(u_{0}-x)\,d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u_{0}-x)^{2}+v_{x_{0},s}(u_{0})^{2}}=u_{0}+\frac{\alpha-\beta}{s}(\psi_{x_{0},s}(u_{0})-u_{0}),

Since the density of

7.1.
Adding a circular variable

In this section, we compute the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{t}
as in the following theorem. Some of the computations will be used again when we compute the Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{\alpha,\beta}
.

7.1
.

When
x_{0}
is Cauchy distributed, the boundary of the domain
\Lambda_{x_{0},t}
has the form

\partial\Lambda_{x_{0},t}=\left\{u\pm iv\left|u^{2}=\frac{1}{v}(1+v)(t-v-v^{2}),v>0\right.\right\}.

(7.1)

The upper boundary
(\partial\Lambda_{x_{0},t})\cap(\mathbb{C}^{+}\cup\mathbb{R})
of
\Lambda_{x_{0},t}
is the graph of a positive unimodal function with peak
\frac{-1+\sqrt{1+4t}}{2}
at
0
.
The Brown measure of
x_{0}+c_{t}
has full measure on
\Lambda_{x_{0},t}
, with density

w_{t/2,t/2}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi t}\frac{t+4v^{2}(1+v)^{2}}{(1+v)(t+2v^{2}(1+v))}

where
v=v_{x_{0},t}(u)
.

7.2
.

As
|u|\to\infty
, the density
w_{t}(u)
does not approach
0
. In fact, as
|u|\to\infty
,
v_{x_{0},t}(u)\to 0
, so
w_{t}(u)
approaches
1/(2\pi t)
. The density
w_{t}(\lambda)
on
\Lambda_{x_{0},t}
still defines a probability measure because the function
v_{x_{0},t}(u)\approx t/u^{2}
as

7.3
.

For each
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
v_{x_{0},t}(u)
is the unique positive number
v
satisfying

u^{2}=\frac{1}{v}(1+v)(t-v-v^{2}).

(7.2)

Thus,
u
and

have opposite sign; in particular,
v_{x_{0},t}
is unimodal with peak at

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathbb{R}
. We can compute (computer software such as Mathematica could be helpful) that

\frac{1}{t}=\int\frac{d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u-x)^{2}+v_{x_{0},t}(u)^{2}}=\frac{1+v_{x_{0},t}(u)}{v_{x_{0},t}(u)(u^{2}+(1+v_{x_{0},t}(u))^{2})},

so that

u^{2}=\frac{1}{v_{x_{0},t}(u)}[1+v_{x_{0},t}(u)][t-v_{x_{0},t}(u)-v_{x_{0},t}(u)^{2}].

This proves (
7.2
).

Differentiating both sides of (
7.2
) with respect to
u
, we have

2u=\left(-\frac{t}{v_{x_{0},t}(u)^{2}}-2(1+v_{x_{0},t}(u))\right)\frac{dv_{x_{0},t}}{du}

(7.3)

which shows
u
and
dv_{x_{0},t}/du
have oppposite sign since we must have
-t/v_{x_{0},t}(u)-2(1+v_{x_{0},t}(u))<0
. It follows that

. The value

comes from solving the equation (

.
∎

7.4
.

We have

\frac{d\psi_{x_{0},t}}{du}=\frac{t+4v^{2}(1+v)^{2}}{(1+v)(t+2v^{2}(1+v))},

where
v=v_{x_{0},t}(u)
.

Proof.

Since

\frac{1+v}{v(u^{2}+(1+v)^{2})}=\int\frac{d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u-x)^{2}+v_{x_{0},t}(u)^{2}}=\frac{1}{t},

we can compute (again, computer software could be useful)

\psi_{x_{0},t}(u)=u+t\int\frac{(u-x)\,d\mu_{x_{0}}(x)}{(u-x)^{2}+v_{x_{0},t}(u)^{2}}=u+t\frac{u}{u^{2}+(1+v)^{2}}=u+\frac{uv}{1+v}.

(7.4)

\frac{d}{du}\frac{uv}{1+v}=\frac{v}{1+v}+\frac{u}{(1+v)^{2}}\frac{dv}{du}=\frac{v(-t+2v(1+v)^{2})}{(1+v)(t+2v^{2}(1+v))}

\frac{d\psi_{x_{0},t}}{du}=\frac{t+4v^{2}(1+v)^{2}}{(1+v)(t+2v^{2}(1+v))},

completing the proof.
∎

7.9
.

7.10
.

(4) can be computed as

\varphi_{x_{0},t}(u)=\frac{4t}{\sqrt{u^{2}+1}(\sqrt{u^{2}+1}+\sqrt{u^{2}+1+4t})},\quad u\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

By putting

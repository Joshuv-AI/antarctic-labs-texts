Minimal surfaces containing an epitrochoid as a geodesic
Shin Kaneda

Abstract
We show a complete minimal immersion cannot have a certain kind of epitrochoid as a geodesic.



Introduction

The Björling problem for minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
asks for the existence of a minimal surface containing a given curve in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and a given unit normal coincides with its Gauss map.
The representation formula of the surface of this problem’s solution is given by H. A. Schwarz.
See for instance,
[
M
]
,
[
LW
]
,
[
MW
]
, and so on.

We consider minimal surfaces containing planar curves as geodesics.
For example, a catenoid contains a circle and catenaries as geodesics (See Figure
1
), Catalan surface contains a cycloid and a parabola (See Figure
2
).
The minimal surfaces containing a conic curve as a geodesic are studied in
[
AGS
]
,
[
P
]
, and so on.
Note that the Catalan surface and the minimal surfaces containing a conic curve as a geodesic are not immersed, while the catenoid is immersed and complete.

Figure 1:

Catenoid and planar curves, a circle and catenaries, contained as geodesics.

Figure 2:

Catalan surface and planar curves, a cycloid and a parabola, contained as geodesics.

In
[
FS
]
, Fujimori and Shoda constructed complete minimal immersions with finite total curvature.
These surfaces contain a planar curve as a geodesic.
These curves really look like epitrochoids (See Figure
3
).
So the author tried to prove that these curves are indeed epitrochoids.
Then, surprisingly, we found that these curves are different from the epitrochoids.
In fact, we prove the following :

Main Theorem
.

A complete minimal immersion in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
cannot have a single-wrapped epitrochoid as a geodesic.

For the definition of single-wrapped epitrochoid, see Definition
4
.
By this theorem, we see that the surfaces constructed in
[
FS
]
do not contain an epitrochoid as a geodesic because these surfaces are immersed and complete.

1
Preliminaries

Theorem 1

(The Weierstrass representation
[
AGS
, Theorem 22.27]
)
.

Let
(g,\eta)
be a pair of a meromorphic function
g
and a holomorphic differential
\eta
on a Riemann surface
M
such that

(1+|g|^{2})^{2}\eta\bar{\eta}

(1)

gives a Riemannian metric on
M
. We set

\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{2}\\
\phi_{3}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}(1-g^{2})\eta\\
i(1+g^{2})\eta\\
2g\eta\end{pmatrix}.

(2)

Suppose that

\operatorname{Re}\oint_{\gamma}\Phi={\bf{0}}

(3)

holds for any
\gamma\in H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z}).
Then

f(z)=\operatorname{Re}\int_{z_{0}}^{z}\Phi:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3},\qquad(z_{0}\in M)

(4)

defines a minimal immersion in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

The pair
(g,\eta)
in Theorem
1
is called the Weierstrass data of
f
.

Remark 2
.

Let
(g,\eta)
be the Weierstrass data of
f
. Then
(g,\eta)
can be written as

g=\frac{\phi_{3}}{\phi_{1}-i\phi_{2}},\qquad\eta=\phi_{1}-i\phi_{2}.

(5)

Moreover,
g
coincides with the composition of the unit normal
\nu
of
f
and the stereographic projection
\sigma
from the north pole, that is,
g=\sigma\circ\nu
.
Thus we call
g
the Gauss map of
f
.

Theorem 3

(Schwarz
[
AGS
, Section 22.6]
)
.

Let
c:I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a real analytic regular curve and
n:I\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
a real analytic vector field along
c
such that

\langle{n(t)},{n(t)}\rangle=1,\qquad\langle{n(t)},{c^{\prime}(t)}\rangle=0,\qquad t\in I,

where
I
is an interval in
\mathbb{R}
.
Then, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
there exists a unique minimal immersion
f:I\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying

f(t)=c(t),\qquad\nu(t)=n(t),\qquad t\in I,

where
\nu
is the unit normal of
f
.
Moreover,
f
can be written on
I\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
as

f(z)=\operatorname{Re}\left\{c(z)-i\int_{z_{0}}^{z}n(w)\times c^{\prime}(w)dw\right\},

(6)

where
c(z),n(z)
are extensions of
c(t),n(t)
to functions of a complex variable
z=t+is\in I\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
.

We consider minimal immersions containing a real analytic regular planar curve that is not a straight line
c(t)=(x(t),y(t),0)
as a geodesic.
We also suppose that
c(t)
is regular, that is,
x^{\prime}(t)^{2}+y^{\prime}(t)^{2}>0
for any
t\in I
.
Since
c^{\prime\prime}(t)
is contained in the
x_{1}x_{2}
-plane and
c
is a geodesic of
f
, the unit normal
n
of
c
can be written as

n(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^{\prime}(t)^{2}+y^{\prime}(t)^{2}}}(-y^{\prime}(t),x(t),0).

Applying (
6
) to
c
and
n
, we have

f(z)=\operatorname{Re}\int_{z_{0}}^{z}\left(x^{\prime}(w),y^{\prime}(w),i\sqrt{x^{\prime}(w)^{2}+y^{\prime}(w)^{2}}\right)dw.

(7)

Moreover, from (
5
), we have

g=i\sqrt{\frac{x^{\prime}(z)+iy^{\prime}(z)}{x^{\prime}(z)-iy^{\prime}(z)}},\qquad\eta=(x^{\prime}(z)-iy^{\prime}(z))dz.

(8)

Let
c(t)=(x(t),y(t),0)
be an epitrochoid.
Then it is written by

\begin{cases}x(t)=(r_{c}+r_{m})\cos t-\lambda\cos\left(\dfrac{r_{c}+r_{m}}{r_{m}}t\right),\vskip 4.0pt\\
y(t)=(r_{c}+r_{m})\sin t-\lambda\sin\left(\dfrac{r_{c}+r_{m}}{r_{m}}t\right),\end{cases}

(9)

where
r_{c}>0
and
r_{m}>0
are radii of a fixed circle and a rolling circle around outside of a fixed circle respectively,
\lambda>0
is a distance between a center of a rolling circle and a point on the epitrochoid (See Figure
4
).
For the curve
c(t)
to be the regular, we assume that
\lambda\neq r_{m}
.

Figure 4:
The thick curve indicates an epitrochoid, the big circle is a fixed circle, and the small circle is a rolling circle.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
r_{c}=1
.

Definition 4
.

An epitrochoid (
9
) is called
single-wrapped
if there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
r_{m}=1/(k+1)
.
Note that if an epitrochoid is single-wrapped, then it is closed when the rolling circle wraps once around the fixed circle.

We consider a single-wrapped epitrochoid, that is, we set
r_{m}=1/(k+1),k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Then (
9
) becomes

\begin{cases}x(t)=\left(1+\dfrac{1}{k+1}\right)\cos t-\lambda\cos\left(\dfrac{1+1/(k+1)}{1/(k+1)}t\right),\vskip 4.0pt\\
y(t)=\left(1+\dfrac{1}{k+1}\right)\sin t-\lambda\sin\left(\dfrac{1+1/(k+1)}{1/(k+1)}t\right).\end{cases}

Figure 5:
Single-wrapped epitrochoids (left :
k=2
, middle :
k=3
, right :
k=4
) (
{\lambda}>r_{m}
)

Scaling up this curve, we can set

\begin{cases}x(t)=\left(k+2\right)\cos t-(k+1)\lambda\cos\left(k+2\right)t\vskip 4.0pt\\
y(t)=\left(k+2\right)\sin t-(k+1)\lambda\sin\left(k+2\right)t.\end{cases}

(10)

See Figure
5
.

2
The proof of the main theorem

We consider a complete minimal immersion containing a single-wrapped epitrochoid as a geodesic.
Applying (
7
) and (
10
) , we have

f(z)=\operatorname{Re}\int_{z_{0}}^{z}\begin{pmatrix}-(k+2)\sin z+(k+1)(k+2)\lambda\sin(k+2)z\\
(k+2)\cos z-(k+1)(k+2)\lambda\cos(k+2)z\\
i(k+2)\sqrt{1+(k+1)^{2}\lambda^{2}-2\lambda(k+1)\cos(k+1)z}\end{pmatrix}dz.

(11)

By (
8
),

g=i\sqrt{\frac{e^{iz}-\lambda(k+1)e^{(k+2)iz}}{-e^{-iz}+\lambda(k+1)e^{-(k+2)iz}}},\qquad\eta=(k+2)(e^{-iz}+\lambda(k+1)e^{-(k+2)iz})dz.

After the substitution
v=e^{iz}
and rotating the surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by
-\pi/2
about the
x_{3}
-axis, we have

g=\sqrt{\frac{v^{k+3}(1-\lambda(k+1)v^{k+1})}{-v^{k+1}+\lambda(k+1)}},\qquad\eta=(k+2)\frac{v^{k+1}-\lambda(k+1)}{v^{k+3}}dv.

We set

w^{2}=\begin{cases}v(1-\lambda(k+1)v^{k+1})(\lambda(k+1)-v^{k+1})\qquad\text{ ($k$ is even),}\\
(1-\lambda(k+1)v^{k+1})(\lambda(k+1)-v^{k+1})\qquad\text{ ($k$ is odd).}\end{cases}

Case 1.

k
is even.

Let
\overline{M}
be the compact Riemann surface of genus
k+1
defined by

\overline{M}=\{(v,w)\in(\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\})^{2}\>|\>w^{2}=v(1-\lambda(k+1)v^{k+1})(\lambda(k+1)-v^{k+1})\},

and set
M=\overline{M}\backslash\{(0,0),(\infty,\infty)\}.

Then the Weierstrass data of
f
can be written as

g=-\frac{wv^{\frac{k+2}{2}}}{v^{k+1}-\lambda(k+1)},\qquad\eta=\frac{v^{k+1}-\lambda(k+1)}{v^{k+1}}dv.

It is easy to check that (
1
) degenerates at
v=\sqrt[k+1]{\lambda(k+1)}
and
v=\sqrt[k+1]{1/(\lambda(k+1))}
.
Therefore
f
is not an immersion at these points.

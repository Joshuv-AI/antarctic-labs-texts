Proton-unbound states in 𝐀 𝟐𝟒 {}^{\makebox[9.0611pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[9.0611pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{A} l relevant for the 𝐌 𝟐𝟑 {}^{\makebox[9.0611pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 23$}}_{\makebox[9.0611pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{M} g ( 𝒑 , 𝜸 ) (p,\gamma) reaction in novae Preprint: APS/123-QED
E. C. Vyfers, V. Pesudo, S. Triambak, M. Kamil, P. Adsley, B. A. Brown, H. Jivan, D. J. Marin-Lambarri, R. Neveling, J. C. Nzobadila Ondze, P. Papka, L. Pellegri, B. M. Rebeiro, B. Singh, F. D. Smit, G. F. Steyn

Abstract
Background: The nucleosynthesis of several proton-rich nuclei is determined by radiative proton-capture reactions on unstable nuclei in nova explosions. One such reaction is Mg 23 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 23$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Mg} ( p , γ ) (p,\gamma) Al 24 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Al} , which links the NeNa and MgAl cycles in oxygen-neon (ONe) novae. Purpose: To extract Mg 23 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 23$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Mg} ( p , γ ) (p,\gamma) resonance strengths from a study of proton-unbound states in Al 24 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Al} , produced via the 24 Mg( 3 He, t t ) reaction. Methods: A beam of He 3 {}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 3$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{He} 2+ ions at 50.7 MeV was used to produce the states of interest in Al 24 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Al} . Proton-triton angular correlations were measured with a K = 600 K=600 QDD magnetic spectrometer and a silicon detector array, located at iThemba LABS, South Africa. Results: We measured the excitation energies of the four lowest proton-unbound states in 24 Al and place lower-limits on Γ p / Γ \Gamma_{p}/\Gamma values for these four states. Together with shell-model calculations of partial gamma widths, the experimental data are also used to determine resonance strengths for the four lowest Mg 23 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 23$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Mg} ( p , γ ) (p,\gamma) Al 24 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Al} resonances. Conclusions: The energy of the dominant first Mg 23 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 23$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Mg} ( p , γ ) (p,\gamma) resonance is determined to be E r = 478 ± 4 E_{r}=478\pm 4 keV, with a resonance strength ω ​ γ = 19 ± 9 \omega\gamma=19\pm 9 meV.



I
Introduction

The
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 23$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{M}
g
(p,\gamma)
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{A}
l reaction is a crucial link between the NeNa and MgAl cycles in oxygen-neon (ONe) novae. Therefore, an accurate determination of this stellar reaction rate is critical for an improved understanding of elemental abundances up to Ca
Kelly et al. 2013
;
José et al. 2004
. At peak nova temperatures
(T_{9}=0.2-0.4)
, the dominant contribution to this process is through the first resonance above the proton threshold in
24
Al (c.f. Fig.
1
).

Direct measurements of the
23
Mg(
p,\gamma)
reaction rate are challenging, primarily because of small cross sections at astrophysically relevant energies and the difficulty in producing intense
23
Mg radioactive ion beams with sufficient purity. As a result, there have been several attempts to determine the
23
Mg(
p,\gamma)
reaction rate indirectly
Wallace and Woosley 1981
;
Herndl et al. 1998
;
Wiescher et al. 1986
;
Kubono et al. 1995
;
Visser et al. 2007
, using theoretical estimates of the partial proton and gamma widths of relevant states in
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Al}
.
For isolated narrow resonances, the resonant contribution for each level can be obtained from its resonance strength

\omega\gamma=\frac{(2J_{r}+1)}{8}\frac{\Gamma_{p}\Gamma_{\gamma}}{\Gamma},

(1)

where
J_{r}
is the total angular momentum of the resonant state,
\Gamma_{p}
and
\Gamma_{\gamma}
are its partial proton and gamma widths, and
\Gamma=\Gamma_{p}+\Gamma_{\gamma}
is the total width.

To date, the only direct
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 23$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Mg}
(p,\gamma)
measurement was performed by Erikson
et al.

Erikson et al. 2010
at the TRIUMF-ISAC facility, where a radioactive
23
Mg ion beam was incident on a hydrogen gas target at the DRAGON recoil spectrometer
Hutcheon et al. 2003
. Prompt
\gamma
rays were detected with an array of bismuth germanate (BGO) scintillators surrounding the target, while the recoils were identified using a combination of ionization chamber and microchannel plate (MCP) detectors located at the focal plane of the spectrometer. The experiment was severely affected by a dominant time-varying
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 23$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{N}
a contamination in the beam
Erikson et al. 2010
. Nevertheless, the energy of the lowest resonance and its corresponding resonance strength were determined to be

E_{r}=485.7_{-1.8}^{+1.3}
keV and
\omega\gamma=37.8_{-15.4}^{+20.5}
meV, respectively
Erikson et al. 2010
. The former translates to an excitation energy of
E_{\rm x}=2349.8^{+1.3}_{-1.8}
keV in
{}^{24}{\rm Al}
, a value that is in tension with an independent
\gamma
ray measurement,
E_{\rm x}=2345.1\pm 1.4
keV
Lotay et al. 2008
.

Taking the above into consideration, in this work we report complementary studies of proton-unbound states in
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{A}
l, produced via the
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{M}
g
(^{3}{\rm He},t)
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{A}
l charge-exchange reaction.

II
APPARATUS

The experiment was performed at the iThemba LABS cyclotron facility, where a 10 pnA, 50.7-MeV dispersion-matched
3
He
2+
beam was bombarded on an
\approx 300~\mu\text{g}/\text{cm}^{2}
-thick MgF
2
target on a carbon backing. The target was located in the scattering chamber of the
K=600
QDD magnetic spectrometer, which was configured in
0^{\circ}
mode, with the beam stop located inside the first dipole magnet
Neveling et al. 2011
. Momentum-analyzed reaction ejectiles were detected in a
1/4^{\prime\prime}
-thick plastic scintillator at the spectrometer focal plane, after passing through two vertical drift chambers (VDCs). The VDCs determined the horizontal and vertical positions of the ejectiles crossing the focal plane, while the plastic scintillator was used for particle identification (PID) purposes, and to generate the data acquisition (DAQ) trigger. The PID plots were generated using the relative time difference between the cyclotron RF and DAQ trigger signals, together with the energy deposited in the scintillator. Fig.
2
shows the PID plot obtained for this experiment, with the triton group explicitly highlighted.

Figure 2:
PID spectrum from energy deposited in the scintillator vs relative time of flight (tof), as discussed in the text.

An array of five 400-
\mu
m-thick MMM-type Double-sided Silicon Strip Detectors (DSSDs) called CAKE
Adsley et al. 2017
was used to detect protons from unbound states in
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{A}
l, in coincidence with triton focal-plane events. The detectors were placed upstream of the target location in a backward-facing lampshade configuration and covered
\approx 25\%
of the total solid-angle. Each DSSD comprised 16 ring channels and 8 sector channels. The rings had an angular range of
\theta_{lab}=115\degree-165\degree
for the whole array.

V
Summary

To summarize, this work reports a study of states in
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Al}
, which are important to determine the
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 23$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Mg}
(p,\gamma)
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Al}
nuclear reaction rate in ONe novae. Our measured excitation energy for the dominant lowest resonance agrees with a previous higher-precision
\gamma
-ray measurement
Lotay et al. 2008
. Together with shell-model calculations of partial gamma widths, our experimental data are also used to extract key
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 23$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Mg}
(p,\gamma)
resonance strengths. For the lowest resonance, we determine a resonance strength that is in reasonable with a previous direct measurement
Erikson et al. 2010
. It is noted however, that our extracted resonance strength is lower than the median value reported Ref.
Erikson et al. 2010
. This is consistent with results from other independent studies
Wrede et al. 2010
;
Herndl et al. 1998
;
Kubono et al. 1995
. Similarly, it can also be argued that at face-value, our results favor the energy reported by Lotay
et al.

Lotay et al. 2008
, for the first
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 23$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Mg}
(p,\gamma)
{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Al}
resonance.

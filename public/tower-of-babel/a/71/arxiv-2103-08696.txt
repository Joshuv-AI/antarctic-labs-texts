Nonlocal strong forms of thin plate, gradient elasticity, magneto-electro-elasticity and phase field fracture by nonlocal operator method Thanks: huilong.ren@uni-weimar.de; zhuang@ikm.uni-hannover.de;timon.rabczuk@tdt.edu.vn
Huilong Ren, Xiaoying Zhuang

Abstract
The derivation of nonlocal strong forms for many physical problems remains cumbersome in traditional methods. In this paper, we apply the variational principle/weighted residual method based on nonlocal operator method for the derivation of nonlocal forms for elasticity, thin plate, gradient elasticity, electro-magneto-elasticity and phase field fracture method. The nonlocal governing equations are expressed as integral form on support and dual-support. The first example shows that the nonlocal elasticity has the same form as dual-horizon non-ordinary state-based peridynamics. The derivation is simple and general and it can convert efficiently many local physical models into their corresponding nonlocal forms. In addition, a criterion based on the instability of the nonlocal gradient is proposed for the fracture modelling in linear elasticity. Several numerical examples are presented to validate nonlocal elasticity and the nonlocal thin plate .

Introduction

Classical continuum mechanics has achieved great success in describing the macro-scale properties of solid material based on the continuous medium hypothesis that the material is a continuous mass rather than as discrete particles. The assumption indicates that the substance of the object completely fills the space it occupies, without considering the inherent micro-structure of the material. Such a continuous medium hypothesis is not always valid in solid medium. Over the years, researchers found that many phenomena, such as size effect

In contrast with local theory whose mathematical language is partial differential derivatives defined at an infinitesimal point, nonlocal theory is formulated as integral form in a domain.

Classical continuum mechanics is regarded as a local theory. For solid mediums of multiple materials with material interface or discontinuity such as fracture, the partial differential operator is no longer well defined. Around the fracture front tip, the stress singularity happens for local theory. In order to model fracture and its evolution, various local theories have been proposed, for example, finite element method (FEM)

In section 4, an instability criterion of nonlocal gradient is presented in the fracture modeling of linear elastic solid. The implementation of nonlocal solid and nonlocal thin plate is discussed in section 5. Several numerical examples for solid and thin plate are used to demonstrate the accuracy and efficiency of the current method in section 6. Last but not the least, some concluding remarks are presented.

2
Second-order nonlocal operator method

NOM uses the integral form to replace the partial differential derivatives of different orders. Although NOM can solve higher order linear/nonlinear problems in 2D/3D, we restrict our discussion in second-order NOM, which is sufficient for the nonlocal derivation of the physical problems to be studied in section 3.

\bm{x}_{i}

\bm{x}_{j}

\bm{v}_{i}:=\bm{v}(\bm{x}_{i},t)

Support

\mathcal{S}_{i}

is the neighbourhood of point
\bm{x}_{i}

\bm{x}_{j}
in support
\mathcal{S}_{i}
forms the spatial vector
\bm{r}(=\bm{x}_{j}-\bm{x}_{i})

2.2
Dual property of dual-support

For point
j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
, let
f_{ij}
be a physical quantity, work conjugate to field difference
(u_{j}-u_{i})
, the dual property of dual-support is

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}(u_{j}-u_{i})\,dV_{j}\,dV_{i}=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}f_{ji}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}\,dV_{j}\Big)u_{i}\,dV_{i}

Proof
:

Let the domain
\Omega
be divided into
N
non-overlapping particles, so that
\Omega=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta V_{i}
, where
\Delta V_{i}
is the volume assigned to particle
i
. Herein,
N
can be arbitrarily large so that the
\Delta V_{i}
is infinitesimal and the double summations of discrete form converge to the double integrals in continuous form.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}(u_{j}-u_{i})\,dV_{j}\,dV_{i}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{\Delta V_{i}\in\Omega}\sum_{\Delta V_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}(u_{j}-u_{i})\Delta V_{j}\Delta V_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\Delta V_{i}\in\Omega}\sum_{\Delta V_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}u_{j}\Delta V_{j}\Delta V_{i}-\sum_{\Delta V_{i}\in\Omega}\sum_{\Delta V_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}u_{i}\Delta V_{j}\Delta V_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\Delta V_{i}\in\Omega}\sum_{\Delta V_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}f_{ji}u_{i}\Delta V_{j}\Delta V_{i}-\sum_{\Delta V_{i}\in\Omega}\sum_{\Delta V_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}u_{i}\Delta V_{j}\Delta V_{i}

(3)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}f_{ji}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}\,dV_{j}\Big)u_{i}\,dV_{i}

In the third step, the dual-support is considered as follows. The term
f_{ij}
with
u_{j}
is the physical quantity from
i
’s support, but is added to particle
j
; since
j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
,
i
belongs to the dual-support
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{j}
of
j
; all terms
f_{ji}
with
u_{i}
are collected from any material point
j
whose support contains
i
and hence form the dual-support of
i
. Therefore, the dual property of the dual-support is proved.

When all points have the same size of support domains, i.e.
j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}\leftrightarrow i\in\mathcal{S}_{j}
, we have
\mathcal{S}_{i}=\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}
for any point
i
and then the dual property of dual-support by Eq.
2
becomes

(4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}(u_{j}-u_{i})\,dV_{j}\,dV_{i}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}(f_{ji}-f_{ij})u_{i}\,dV_{j}\,dV_{i}

Above equation is widely used in the derivation of nonlocal strong form from weak form. Such expression is valid in the continuum form as well as in discrete form. The dual property of dual-support is also proved in the dual-horizon peridynamics
[
45
]
. A simple example with
N=4
to illustrate this property is given in
A
.

2.3
Nonlocal gradient and Hessian operator

The local gradient operator and Hessian operator for a scalar-valued function
u
have the forms in 2D

\displaystyle\nabla u=\Big(u_{,x},u_{,y}\Big)^{T},\quad\nabla^{2}u=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{,xx}&u_{,xy}\\
u_{,xy}&u_{,yy}\\
\end{array}\right)

and in 3D

\displaystyle\nabla u=\Big(u_{,x},u_{,y},u_{,z}\Big)^{T},\quad\nabla^{2}u=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}u_{,xx}&u_{,xy}&u_{,xz}\\
u_{,xy}&u_{,yy}&u_{,yz}\\
u_{,xz}&u_{,yz}&u_{,zz}\\
\end{array}\right)

where
u_{,xx}
denotes the partial derivative of
u
with respect to
x
twice.

In the framework of NOM, the partial derivatives can be constructed as follows. The Taylor series expansion of scalar-valued field
u_{j}
in 2D can be written as

(10)

\displaystyle u_{j}=u_{i}+(u_{i,x},u_{i,y},u_{i,xx},u_{i,xy},u_{i,yy})\cdot(x_{ij},y_{ij},x_{ij}^{2}/2,x_{ij}y_{ij},y_{ij}^{2}/2)+O(|\bm{r}_{ij}|^{3})

where
\bm{r}_{ij}=(x_{ij},y_{ij})^{T}=\bm{x}_{j}-\bm{x}_{i}

Let

(11)

\displaystyle u_{ij}

\displaystyle=u_{j}-u_{i}

(12)

\displaystyle\bm{p}_{ij}

\displaystyle=(x_{ij},y_{ij},x_{ij}^{2}/2,x_{ij}y_{ij},y_{ij}^{2}/2)^{T}

(13)

\displaystyle\partial u_{i}

\displaystyle=(u_{i,x},u_{i,y},u_{i,xx},u_{i,xy},u_{i,yy})^{T}

The Taylor series expansion of Eq.
10

(14)

\displaystyle u_{ij}=\partial u_{i}^{T}\bm{p}_{ij}

Tensor product with
\bm{p}_{ij}^{T}
on both sides of Eq.

\displaystyle u_{ij}\bm{p}_{ij}^{T}=\partial u_{i}^{T}\bm{p}_{ij}\bm{p}_{ij}^{T}

Considering the weighted integration in the support

(16)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})u_{ij}\bm{p}_{ij}^{T}\,dV_{j}=\partial u_{i}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{p}_{ij}\bm{p}_{ij}^{T}\,dV_{j}

where
\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})

Then the nonlocal operators can be obtained as

(17)

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}u_{i}:=\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{K}_{i}\cdot\bm{p}_{ij}u_{ij}\,dV_{j}

where

(18)

\displaystyle\bm{K}_{i}=\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{p}_{ij}\otimes\bm{p}_{ij}^{T}\,dV_{j}\Big)^{-1}

Here, we use
\tilde{\square}
to denote the nonlocal form of the local operator
\square

The Taylor series expansion of a vector field
\bm{u}
can be obtained in the similar manner as

(19)

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{ij}^{T}

\displaystyle=\bm{p}_{ij}^{T}\cdot\partial\bm{u}_{i}

(20)

\displaystyle\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{p}_{ij}\otimes\bm{u}_{ij}^{T}

\displaystyle=\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{p}_{ij}\otimes\bm{p}_{ij}^{T}\cdot\partial\bm{u}_{i}

(21)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{p}_{ij}\otimes\bm{u}_{ij}^{T}\,dV_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{p}_{ij}\otimes\bm{p}_{ij}^{T}\cdot\partial\bm{u}_{i}\,dV_{j}

That is

(22)

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}\bm{u}_{i}:=\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{K}_{i}\cdot\bm{p}_{ij}\otimes\bm{u}_{ij}^{T}\,dV_{j}

For example, consider the displacement field
\bm{u}=(u,v)^{T}
in two dimensional space, the relative displacement vector and the nonlocal partial derivatives have the explicit forms

(23)

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{j}-u_{i}\\
v_{j}-v_{i}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\tilde{\partial}\bm{u}_{i}=(\tilde{\partial}u_{i},\tilde{\partial}v_{i})=\begin{pmatrix}u_{i,x}&v_{i,x}\\
u_{i,y}&v_{i,y}\\
u_{i,xx}&v_{i,xx}\\
u_{i,xy}&v_{i,xy}\\
u_{i,yy}&v_{i,yy}\\
\end{pmatrix},

Let
\bm{K}_{i}\cdot\bm{p}_{ij}
be denoted by

(24)

\displaystyle(g_{1j},g_{2j},h_{1j},h_{2j},h_{3j})^{T}=\bm{K}_{i}\cdot\bm{p}_{ij}

In 3D case, the polynomial vector based on relative coordinates

\displaystyle\bm{p}_{ij}=(x_{ij},y_{ij},z_{ij},x_{ij}^{2}/2,x_{ij}y_{ij},x_{ij}z_{ij},y_{ij}^{2}/2,y_{ij}z_{ij},z_{ij}^{2})^{T}

in Eq.

Let

Stability of the second-order nonlocal operators

According to Ref

, the energy functional for second-order nonlocal operator in discrete form can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{i}(\bm{u})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{p^{hg}}{m_{i}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\big(u_{ij}-\bm{p}_{j}^{T}\tilde{\partial}u_{i}\big)^{2}\,dV_{j}

The operator in Eq.

\displaystyle-\frac{p^{hg}}{m_{i}}\Big(\underbrace{\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{p}_{j}u_{ij}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r})\bm{p}_{j}\bm{p}_{j}^{T}\,dV_{j}\cdot\tilde{\partial}u_{i}}_{=0\text{ since Eq.\ref{eq:nomeq}}}\Big)\cdot\tilde{\partial}\delta u_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

Therefore,

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{F}_{i}(\bm{u})=\frac{p^{hg}}{m_{i}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\big(u_{ij}-\bm{p}_{j}^{T}\tilde{\partial}u_{i}\big)(\delta u_{j}-\delta u_{i})\,dV_{j}

Consider integration of
\delta\mathcal{F}_{i}(\bm{u})
in domain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta\mathcal{F}_{i}\,dV_{i}=p^{hg}\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\frac{\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})}{m_{i}}\big(u_{ij}-\bm{p}_{j}^{T}\tilde{\partial}u_{i}\big)(\delta u_{j}-\delta u_{i})\,dV_{j}\,dV_{i}}_{\text{by Eq.\ref{eq:dualprop}}}

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\frac{p^{hg}}{m_{j}}\big(u_{ji}-\bm{p}_{i}^{T}\tilde{\partial}u_{j}\big)\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r})\frac{p^{hg}}{m_{i}}\big(u_{ij}-\bm{p}_{j}^{T}\tilde{\partial}u_{i}\big)\,dV_{j}\Big)\delta u_{i}\,dV_{i}

For any
\delta u_{i}
,
\int_{\Omega}\delta\mathcal{F}_{i}\,dV_{i}=0
leads to the internal force due to the stability of the nonlocal operator

(41)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\frac{p^{hg}}{m_{j}}\big(u_{ji}-\bm{p}_{i}^{T}\tilde{\partial}u_{j}\big)\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r})\frac{p^{hg}}{m_{i}}\big(u_{ij}-\bm{p}_{j}^{T}\tilde{\partial}u_{i}\big)\,dV_{j}

Eq.
41
is the expression for a scalar-valued field. For vector-valued field, the internal force due to the stability of nonlocal operator is

(42)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})\frac{p^{hg}}{m_{j}}\big(\bm{u}_{ji}-\bm{p}_{i}^{T}\tilde{\partial}\bm{u}_{j}\big)\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\frac{p^{hg}}{m_{i}}\big(\bm{u}_{ij}-\bm{p}_{j}^{T}\tilde{\partial}\bm{u}_{i}\big)\,dV_{j}

Nonlocal governing equations based on NOM

This section is devoted to the variational derivation of nonlocal strong forms of solid mechanics, including hyperelasticity, thin plate, gradient elasticity, electro-magnetic-elasticity theory and phase field fracture method. The strong form is suitable for theoretical analysis as well as explicit time integration. For the fully implicit simulation of various PDEs, the reader is referred to NOM for PDEs
[
59
,
60
,
61
,
62
,
63
]
.

3.1
Nonlocal form for hyperelasticity

Consider the energy density of a hyperelasticity as
\phi:=\phi(\bm{F})
, where
\bm{F}=\nabla\bm{u}+\bm{I}
.
The balance equation for the hyperelastic solid is

(43)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{P}+\bm{b}=0\mbox{ on }\Omega

with boundary conditions
\bm{u}=\bm{u}_{0}\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{D}
and
\bm{P}\cdot\bm{n}=\bm{t}_{0}\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{N}
, where
\bm{u}_{0}
is the specified displacement and
\bm{t}_{0}
is the prescribed traction load,
\bm{P}=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\bm{F}}
, the first Piola-Kirchhoff stress,
\bm{b}
is the body force density.

3.1.1
Derivation based on variational principle

The variation of strain energy over the domain is

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\delta\phi(\bm{F})\,dV=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\bm{F}}:\delta\bm{F}\,dV

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bm{P}:\nabla(\delta\bm{u})\,dV

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bm{P}_{i}:\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\delta\bm{u}_{ij}\otimes\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}\,dV_{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{P}_{i}:\delta\bm{u}_{ij}\otimes\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}\,dV_{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})(\bm{P}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}_{ij})\cdot\delta\bm{u}_{ij}\,dV_{j}\,dV_{i}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})(\bm{P}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}_{ij})\cdot(\delta\bm{u}_{j}-\delta\bm{u}_{i})\,dV_{j}\,dV_{i}}_{\text{by Eq.\ref{eq:dualprop}}}

(44)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})\bm{P}_{j}\cdot\bm{g}_{ji}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{P}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}\Big)\cdot\delta\bm{u}_{i}\,dV_{i}

In above derivation, we replace the gradient operator with nonlocal gradient, e.g.
\tilde{\nabla}\otimes\bm{u}_{i}\to\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{u}_{ij}\otimes\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}
in Eq.
30
, and the relation
\bm{A}:\bm{a}\otimes\bm{b}=(\bm{A}\cdot\bm{b})\cdot\bm{a}
for second-order tensor
\bm{A}
and vectors
\bm{a},\bm{b}
is employed.

The variational of external body force energy

(45)

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{F}_{ext}=\int_{\Omega}\delta\bm{u}\cdot\bm{b}\,dV

For any
\delta\bm{u}_{i}
,
\delta\mathcal{F}-\delta\mathcal{F}_{ext}=0
leads to the nonlocal governing equations for elasticity

(46)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{P}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})\bm{P}_{j}\cdot\bm{g}_{ji}\,dV_{j}+\bm{b}=0

Considering the effect of inertial force
\rho\ddot{\bm{u}}_{i}
per unit volume, and replacing the dual-support with dual-horizon, we obtain the equations of motion for dual-horizon peridynamics

(47)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{P}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{H}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})\bm{P}_{j}\cdot\bm{g}_{ji}\,dV_{j}+\bm{b}_{i}=\rho\ddot{\bm{u}}_{i}

If the sizes of horizons for all material points are the same, the dual-horizon peridynamics degenerates to the conventional constant horizon peridynamics.

For any specific strain energy density (for example, isotropic/anisotropic linear/nonlinear elasticity), the explicit form of
\bm{P}
can be derived straightforwardly.

3.1.2
Derivation based on weighted residual method

Beside the derivation based on strain energy density, the nonlocal strong form can be derived by weighted residual method.
Consider the governing equations for hyperelasticity
, the weak form of Eq.
43
for any trial vector becomes

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bm{v}\cdot\nabla\cdot\bm{P}+\bm{v}\cdot\bm{b}\,dV

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}-\nabla\bm{v}:\bm{P}+\bm{v}\cdot\bm{b}\,dV+\int_{\Gamma}\bm{P}\cdot\bm{n}\cdot\bm{v}\,dS

(48)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}-\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{v}_{ij}\otimes\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}\Big):\bm{P}_{i}+\bm{v}_{i}\cdot\bm{b}\,dV_{i}+\int_{\Gamma}\bm{P}\cdot\bm{n}\cdot\bm{v}\,dS

Let us focus on the integral in
\Omega
, the first term in above equation can be written as

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{v}_{ij}\otimes\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}\Big):\bm{P}_{i}\,dV_{i}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}-\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{P}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}_{ij}\cdot(\bm{v}_{j}-\bm{v}_{i})\,dV_{j}\Big)\,dV_{i}}_{\text{by Eq.\ref{eq:dualprop}}}

(49)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{P}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})\bm{P}_{j}\cdot\bm{g}_{ji}\,dV_{j}\Big)\cdot\bm{v}_{i}\,dV_{i}

For any
\bm{v}_{i}
, the weak form being zero leads to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{P}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})\bm{P}_{j}\cdot\bm{g}_{ji}\,dV_{j}+\bm{b}=0

which is identical to Eq.
46
. As being more general than the energy method, the weighted residual method can be used to convert PDEs that have no energy functional to nonlocal integral forms.

3.2
Nonlocal thin plate theory

The thin plate theory is widely used in engineering applications
[
66
]
. The basic assumption of thin plate include: 1) the thickness of the plate is much smaller than the length inside the mid-plane; 2) the deflection is much smaller than the thickness of the plate so that higher order effect is neglect-able; 3) the stress along the thickness direction is assumed as zero, e.g.
\sigma_{z}\approx 0
and the points in the midplane have no displacement parallel to the midplane, e.g.
u(x,y,0)=v(x,y,0)\approx 0
; 4) the normal of the mid-plane remains perpendicular to the mid-plane after deformation. Then the plate bending can be simplified into 2D problem and the displacements, strain and stress can be described by the deflection on the mid-plane

(50)

\displaystyle u(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=-z\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}

(51)

\displaystyle v(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=-z\frac{\partial w}{\partial y}

(52)

\displaystyle w(x,y,z)

\displaystyle\simeq w(x,y,0)\cong w(x,y)

The generalized strain is the Hessian operator on the deflection

(53)

\displaystyle\bm{\kappa}=\nabla^{2}w=\begin{pmatrix}w_{,xx}&w_{,xy}\\
w_{,xy}&w_{,yy}\end{pmatrix}

with nonlocal correspondence and its variation

(54)

\displaystyle\bm{\kappa}=\tilde{\nabla}^{2}w:=\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{h}_{ij}w_{ij}\,dV_{j}

(55)

\displaystyle\delta\bm{\kappa}=\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{h}_{ij}\delta w_{ij}\,dV_{j}

The momentum tensor
\bm{M}
, the general stress for isotropic thin plate, is given by

\displaystyle\bm{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M_{xx}&M_{xy}\\
M_{xy}&M_{yy}\end{array}\right)=D_{0}\big(\nu\mbox{ tr}(\bm{\kappa})\bm{I}_{2\times 2}+(1-\nu)\bm{\kappa}\big)

where
D_{0}=\frac{Et^{3}}{12\left(1-\nu^{2}\right)}
and
t
is the thickness of the plate.

Based on the principle of minimum potential energy, the energy functional for the governing equation is

(58)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{int}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\bm{M}:\bm{\kappa}-qw\,dS

and for the boundary condition can be expressed as

(59)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{ext}=\int_{S_{3}}\bar{V}_{n}w\,d\Gamma-\int_{S_{2}+S_{3}}\bar{M}_{n}\frac{\partial w}{\partial n}\,d\Gamma

where
q
is the external transverse load on the mid-plane,
\bar{V}_{n}
is the shear force load on boundary
S_{3}
and
\bar{M}_{n}
is the prescribed moment on boundary
S_{2}+S_{3}
. For simplicity, we leave the integral on the boundary for later consideration. The variation of the internal energy functional is

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{F}_{int}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bm{M}:\delta\bm{\kappa}-q\delta w\,dS

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bm{M}_{i}:\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{h}_{ij}\delta w_{ij}\,dS_{j}-q_{i}\delta w_{i}\,dS_{i}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{M}_{i}:\bm{h}_{ij}(\delta w_{j}-\delta w_{i})\,dS_{j}}_{\text{by Eq.\ref{eq:dualprop}}}-\int_{\Omega}q_{i}\delta w_{i}\,dS_{i}

(60)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{M}_{j}:\bm{h}_{ji}\,dS_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{M}_{i}:\bm{h}_{ij}\,dS_{j}-q_{i}\Big)\delta w_{i}\,dS_{i}

The variation of the external energy function is

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{F}_{ext}

\displaystyle=\int_{S_{3}}\bar{V}_{n}\delta w\,d\Gamma-\int_{S_{2}+S_{3}}\bar{M}_{n}\frac{\partial\delta w}{\partial n}\,d\Gamma

\displaystyle=\int_{S_{3}}\bar{V}_{n}\delta w\,d\Gamma-\int_{S_{2}+S_{3}}\bar{M}_{n}\nabla\delta w\cdot\bm{n}\,d\Gamma

\displaystyle=\int_{S_{3}}\bar{V}_{n}\delta w\,d\Gamma-\int_{S_{2}+S_{3}}\bar{M}_{ni}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\delta w_{ij}\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}\cdot\bm{n}_{i}\,d\Gamma_{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{S_{3}}\bar{V}_{n}\delta w\,d\Gamma-\int_{S_{2}+S_{3}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bar{M}_{ni}\bm{g}_{ij}\cdot\bm{n}_{i}\delta w_{ij}\,dV_{j}\,d\Gamma_{i}

(61)

\displaystyle=\int_{S_{3}}\bar{V}_{n}\delta w\,d\Gamma-\int_{S_{2}+S_{3}}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})\bar{M}_{nj}\bm{g}_{ji}\cdot\bm{n}_{j}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bar{M}_{ni}\bm{g}_{ij}\cdot\bm{n}_{i}\,dV_{j}\Big)\delta w_{i}\,d\Gamma_{i}

For any
\delta w_{i}
,
\delta\mathcal{F}_{int}-\delta\mathcal{F}_{ext}=0
leads to the nonlocal thin plate equation for material point in domain
\Omega

(62)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{M}_{i}:\bm{h}_{ij}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{M}_{j}:\bm{h}_{ji}\,dV_{j}+q_{i}=0

The additional nonlocal form for material point applied with the moment boundary condition is

(63)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bar{M}_{ni}\bm{g}_{ij}\cdot\bm{n}_{i}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})\bar{M}_{nj}\bm{g}_{ji}\cdot\bm{n}_{j}\,dV_{j}=0

Based on the D’Alembert’s principle, the equation of motion considering the effect of inertial force
\rho t\ddot{w}_{i}
per unit area is

(64)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{M}_{j}:\bm{h}_{ji}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{M}_{i}:\bm{h}_{ij}\,dV_{j}+q_{i}=t\rho\ddot{w}_{i}

For clamped boundary condition
w_{,n}=\nabla w\cdot\bm{n}=0
, the nonlocal form is

(65)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})w_{ij}\bm{g}_{ij}\cdot\bm{n}_{i}\,dV_{j}=0

Compared with the local governing equation for thin plate
\nabla^{2}:\bm{M}+q=t\rho\ddot{w}
, we can find the correspondence between local and nonlocal formulation

(66)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}:\bm{M}\to\tilde{\nabla}^{2}:\bm{M}_{i}:=\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})\bm{M}_{j}:\bm{h}_{ji}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{M}_{i}:\bm{h}_{ij}\,dV_{j}

The nonlocal derivation for thin plate can be extended to composite plate and functional gradient plate theories.

3.3
Nonlocal gradient elasticity

Gradient theories emerge from considerations of the microstructure in the material at micro-scale, where a mass point after homogenization is not the center of a micro-volume and the rotation of the micro-volume depends on the moment stress/couple stress as well as the Cauchy stress.
Gradient elasticity generalizes the elasticity theory by employing higher order terms of the deformation gradient or the gradient of the strain tensor. Generally, the energy density functional can be assumed as
\psi:=\psi(\bm{F},\nabla\bm{F})=\psi(\nabla\bm{u},\nabla^{2}\bm{u})
, where
\bm{F}=\nabla\bm{u}+\bm{I}
. The total potential energy in domain is

(67)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\int_{\Omega}\psi-\bm{b}\cdot\bm{u}\,dV

The stress tensor and generalized stress tensor of first Piola-Kirchhoff type are defined as

(68)

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\bm{F}}

(69)

\displaystyle\bm{\Sigma}=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nabla\bm{F}}

The variation of the total internal energy is

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\bm{F}}:\nabla\delta\bm{u}+\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nabla\bm{F}}\dot{:}\nabla^{2}\delta\bm{u}-\bm{b}\cdot\delta\bm{u}\Big)\,dV

(70)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\bm{\sigma}:\nabla\delta\bm{u}+\bm{\Sigma}\dot{:}\nabla^{2}\delta\bm{u}-\bm{b}\cdot\delta\bm{u}\Big)\,dV

Based on the integration by parts, the local form can be derived by

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\sigma}\cdot\delta\bm{u}+\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\Sigma}{:}\nabla\delta\bm{u}\Big)\,dS-\int_{\Omega}\Big(\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}\cdot\delta\bm{u}+\nabla\cdot\bm{\Sigma}{:}\nabla\delta\bm{u}+\bm{b}\cdot\bm{u}\Big)\,dV

(71)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\sigma}\cdot\delta\bm{u}+\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\Sigma}:\nabla\delta\bm{u}-\bm{n}\cdot\nabla\cdot\bm{\Sigma}\cdot\delta\bm{u}\Big)\,dS-\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}-\nabla^{2}:\bm{\Sigma}+\bm{b})\cdot\delta\bm{u}\,dV

Based on D’Alembert’s principle, the governing equations for dynamic gradient elasticity can be written as

(72)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}-\nabla^{2}:\bm{\Sigma}+\bm{b}=\rho\ddot{\bm{u}}\mbox{ in }\Omega

On the other hand, do the substitutions
\nabla\delta\bm{u}\to\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{g}_{ij}\otimes\delta\bm{u}_{ij}\,dV_{j},\mbox{ and }\nabla^{2}\delta\bm{u}\to\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{h}_{ij}\otimes\delta\bm{u}_{ij}\,dV_{j}
, we get

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\bm{\sigma}:\nabla\delta\bm{u}+\bm{\Sigma}\dot{:}\nabla^{2}\delta\bm{u}-\bm{b}\cdot\delta\bm{u}\,dV

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\bm{\sigma}_{i}:\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{g}_{ij}\otimes\delta\bm{u}_{ij}\,dV_{j}+\bm{\Sigma}_{i}\dot{:}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{h}_{ij}\otimes\delta\bm{u}_{ij}\,dV_{j}-\bm{b}\cdot\delta\bm{u}\Big)\,dV_{i}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{\sigma}_{i}{:}(\delta\bm{u}_{j}-\delta\bm{u}_{j})\otimes\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}\,dV_{i}}_{\text{by Eq.\ref{eq:dualprop}}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{\Sigma}_{i}\dot{:}(\delta\bm{u}_{j}-\delta\bm{u}_{j})\otimes\bm{h}_{ij}\,dV_{j}\,dV_{i}}_{\text{by Eq.\ref{eq:dualprop}}}-\int_{\Omega}\bm{b}\cdot\delta\bm{u}_{i}\,dV_{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})\bm{\sigma}_{j}\cdot\bm{g}_{ji}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{\sigma}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}\Big)\cdot\delta\bm{u}_{i}\,dV_{i}

(73)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})\bm{\Sigma}_{j}:\bm{h}_{ji}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{\Sigma}_{i}:\bm{h}_{ij}\,dV_{j}\Big)\cdot\delta\bm{u}_{i}\,dV_{i}-\int_{\Omega}\bm{b}\cdot\delta\bm{u}_{i}\,dV_{i}

In the above derivation, we used
\bm{\Sigma}\dot{:}\bm{u}\otimes\bm{h}=(\bm{\Sigma}:\bm{h})\cdot\bm{u}
.
For any
\delta\bm{u}_{i}
,
\delta\mathcal{F}=0
leads to the nonlocal form of gradient elasticity

(74)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})(\bm{\sigma}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}_{ij}+\bm{\Sigma}_{i}:\bm{h}_{ij})\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})(\bm{\sigma}_{j}\cdot\bm{g}_{ji}+\bm{\Sigma}_{j}:\bm{h}_{ji})\,dV_{j}+\bm{b}=\rho\ddot{\bm{u}}_{i}

The inertia force term is added based on D’Alembert’s principle.

Comparing Eq.
71
and Eq.
73
, the correspondence from local form to nonlocal form is

(75)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}:\bm{\Sigma}_{i}\to\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})\bm{\Sigma}_{j}:\bm{h}_{ji}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{\Sigma}_{i}:\bm{h}_{ij}\,dV_{j}

3.4
Nonlocal form of magneto-electro-elasticity

In accordance with reference
[
67
]
, let us postulate the following form of internal energy for the energy function
\psi:=\psi(\bm{F},\nabla\bm{F},\bm{p},\nabla\bm{p},\bm{m},\nabla\bm{m})
, a function depends on the displacement gradient
\bm{F}=\nabla\bm{u}+\bm{I}
and its second gradient
\nabla\bm{F}=\nabla^{2}\bm{u}
, polarization vector
\bm{p}
and its gradient
\nabla\bm{p}
, magnetic field
\bm{m}
and its gradient
\nabla\bm{m}
. The total potential energy in the domain can be written as

(76)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\int_{\Omega}\psi(\bm{F},\nabla\bm{F},\bm{p},\nabla\bm{p},\bm{m},\nabla\bm{m})\,dV

This model has a strong physical background, for example, the nonlinear electro-gradient elasticity for semiconductors
[
68
]
and flexoelectricity
[
69
]
.

The first variation of
\mathcal{F}
is

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{F}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta\psi\,dV

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\bm{F}}:\nabla\delta\bm{u}+\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nabla\bm{F}}\dot{:}\nabla^{2}\delta\bm{u}+\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\bm{p}}\cdot\delta\bm{p}+

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nabla\bm{p}}:\nabla\delta\bm{p}+\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\bm{m}}\cdot\delta\bm{m}+\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nabla\bm{m}}:\nabla\delta\bm{m}\,dV

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bm{\sigma}:\nabla\delta\bm{u}+\bm{\Sigma}\dot{:}\nabla^{2}\delta\bm{u}+\bm{e}\cdot\delta\bm{p}

(77)

\displaystyle+\bm{E}:\nabla\delta\bm{p}+\bm{s}\cdot\delta\bm{m}+\bm{S}:\nabla\delta\bm{m}\,dV

where

(78)

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\bm{F}},\bm{\Sigma}=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nabla\bm{F}},\bm{e}=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\bm{p}}

(79)

\displaystyle\bm{E}=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nabla\bm{p}},\bm{s}=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\bm{m}},\bm{S}=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nabla\bm{m}}

Doing substitutions
\nabla\delta\bm{u}_{i}\to\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\delta\bm{u}_{ij}\otimes\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}
,
\nabla^{2}\delta\bm{u}_{i}\to\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\delta\bm{u}_{ij}\otimes\bm{h}_{ij}\,dV_{j}
,
\nabla\delta\bm{p}_{i}\to\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\delta\bm{p}_{ij}\otimes\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}
,
\nabla\delta\bm{m}_{i}\to\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\delta\bm{m}_{ij}\otimes\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}
and following the same operations in prior sections, the functional becomes

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})(\bm{\sigma}_{j}\cdot\bm{g}_{ji}+\bm{\Sigma}_{j}:\bm{h}_{ji})\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})(\bm{\sigma}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}_{ij}+\bm{\Sigma}_{i}:\bm{h}_{ij})\,dV_{j}\Big)\cdot\delta\bm{u}_{i}\,dV_{i}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})(\bm{E}_{j}\cdot\bm{g}_{ji})\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{E}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}+\bm{e}_{i}\Big)\cdot\delta\bm{p}_{i}\,dV_{i}+

(80)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ji})(\bm{S}_{j}\cdot\bm{g}_{ji})\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\omega(\bm{r}_{ij})\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}_{ij}\,dV_{j}+\bm{s}_{i}\Big)\cdot\delta\bm{m}_{i}\,dV_{i}

For any
\delta\bm{u}_{i},\delta\bm{p}_{i},\delta\bm{m}_{i}
,
\delta\mathcal{F}=0
leads to general nonlocal governing equation for mechanical field, electrical field and magnetic field, respectively

4
Instability criterion for fracture modelling

Typical methods for fracture modelling are either based on diffusive crack domain in phase field methods or on direct topological modification on meshes in XFEM or bonds in PD. Direct topological modification on meshes often leads to instability issues. For example, in NOSBPD, the breakage of a bond based on the quantities derived from stress state or strain state often introduces too much perturbation to the scheme, which may abort the calculation because of the singularity in shape tensors. These criteria include critical stretch
[
28
,
71
]
, energy based
[
30
]
or stress based criterion
[
32
,
33
]
. Another issue in NOSBPD is that the strain energy carried by a bond is not independent with other bonds. It also depends on the direction, the length of the bond, the choice of influence functions. Removing one neighbour often gives rise to catastrophic result on the calculation. A criterion on how to remove the neighbours safely from the neighbour list remains unclear.

Damage is a process deviated from the robust mathematical expression, where the transition happens in a very narrow zone, such as the crack tip front. It is observed that around the crack tip, the gradient or strain undergoes a sharp transition within a very small zone. Most conventional numerical methods for fracture modelling focus on accurate description of the singularity occurring around the crack tip, such a description is very hard to tackle and its evolution is inconvenient to update. This dilemma can be handled when something different from continuous function is introduced.

In NOM, the gradient operator is defined in a “redundant” way. Around the crack tip, the deformation is irregular and the part due to hourglass energy is comparable to the strain energy carried by a particle. More specifically, the operator energy in nonlocal operator method describes the irregularity of a function around the crack tip. The irregularity is the part that cannot be described by the continuous function. For continuous domain, the strain energy density is much larger than the operator energy density. However, for particles around the crack tip, the operator energy density is far from zero and the irregularity due to the singularity around the crack tip increases comparably to the strain energy density. In this sense, the operator energy density can be viewed as an indicator for the crack tip.

Unlike the strain energy density, the hourglass energy density describes the irregular deformation around the crack tip. It depends on the penalty for the strain energy. Larger penalty improves the continuity of deformation, but the extent of hourglass energy compared with the strain energy density is hard to estimate. In this paper, we propose a special manner to estimate the critical hourglass strain. Let the critical bond strain be denoted by
s_{max}
, which may depend on the characteristic length scale of the support, critical energy release rate and the elastic modulus. When the maximal strain reached
s_{max}
, the damage process is activated and the critical hourglass strain
s^{hg}_{max}
is set as the maximal hourglass strain
s^{hg}_{ij}
for all bonds in the computational model. In the sequential calculation, when the hourglass strain of a bond is larger than
s^{hg}_{max}
, the damage on that bond occurs, which is mathematically described as

(91)

\displaystyle d_{ij}=\begin{cases}0\mbox{ if }s^{hg}_{ij}(t)>s^{hg}_{max},t\in[0,T]\\
1\mbox{ otherwise }\end{cases}

where
d_{ij}
denotes the damage status between particle
i
and particle
j
.

The damage of a particle is calculated as

(92)

\displaystyle d_{i}=\frac{\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}d_{ij}\,dV_{j}}{\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}1\,dV_{j}}

Every time one particle is removed from the neighbour list, the nonlocal gradient for the central particle should be recalculated based on the remaining “healthy” neighbour. We will apply this rule to model fractures in 2D and 3D linear elastic material.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we employ the recent proposed NOM to derive the nonlocal strong forms for various physical models, including elasticity, thin plate, gradient elasticity, electro-magneto-elastic coupled model and phase field fracture model. These models require second order partial derivative at most and we make use of the second-order NOM scheme, which contains the nonlocal gradient and nonlocal Hessian operator. Considering the fact that most physical models are compatible with the variational principle/weighted residual method, we start from the energy form/weak form of the problem, by inserting the nonlocal expression of the gradient/Hessian operator into the weak form, based on the dual property of the dual-support in NOM, the nonlocal strong form is obtained with ease. Such a process can be extended to many other physical problems in other fields. The derived strong forms are variationally consistent and allow elegant description for inhomogeneous nonlocality in both theoretical derivation and numerical implementation.

We also propose an instability criterion in nonlocal elasticity or dual-horizon state-based peridynamics for the fracture modeling. The criterion is formulated as the functional of nonlocal gradient in support, which minimizes the zero-energy deformation that cannot be described by the nonlocal gradient. Such an operator functional approaches zero for continuous fields but has comparable value to the strain energy density for the deformation around the crack tip. During the fracture modeling by removing particles from the neighbor list, it is safer to delete the particle with larger zero-energy deformation. The numerical examples for 2D/3D fracture modeling confirm the feasibility and robustness of this criterion. The instability criterion is possible applicable for anisotropic elastic material and hyperelastic materials.

A simple example to illustrate dual-support

In order to facilitate the comprehension of dual-support, let us consider 4 particles in Fig.
22
, each with particle volume
\Delta V_{i},i=\{1,2,3,4\}
and
\Omega=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Delta V_{i}
. Obviously, the support and dual-support can be listed as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}=\{2,3,4\},\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}=\{3,4\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}=\{3\},\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\prime}=\{1,3\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{3}=\{1,2\},\mathcal{S}_{3}^{\prime}=\{1,2,4\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{4}=\{1,3\},\mathcal{S}_{4}^{\prime}=\{1\}

Here we neglect whether the shape tensor is invertible or not.

The most common formula in the derivation based on NOM and variational principle is the double integrations in support and whole domain.
Consider the double integrations

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}(u_{j}-u_{i})\,dV_{j}\,dV_{i}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}(u_{j}-u_{i})\Delta V_{j}\Big)\Delta V_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}u_{j}\Delta V_{j}\Big)\Delta V_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}u_{i}\Delta V_{j}\Big)\Delta V_{i}

Expand the double summations

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}u_{j}\Delta V_{j}\Big)\Delta V_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(f_{12}\Delta V_{2}\Delta V_{1}u_{2}+f_{13}\Delta V_{3}\Delta V_{1}u_{3}+f_{14}\Delta V_{4}\Delta V_{1}u_{4}\Big)+\Big(f_{23}\Delta V_{2}\Delta V_{3}u_{3}\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Big(f_{31}\Delta V_{1}\Delta V_{3}u_{1}+f_{32}\Delta V_{2}\Delta V_{3}u_{2}\Big)+\Big(f_{41}\Delta V_{1}\Delta V_{4}u_{1}+f_{43}\Delta V_{3}\Delta V_{4}u_{3}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(f_{31}\Delta V_{3}+f_{41}\Delta V_{4}\Big)u_{1}\Delta V_{1}+\Big(f_{12}\Delta V_{1}+f_{32}\Delta V_{3}\Big)u_{2}\Delta V_{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Big(f_{13}\Delta V_{1}+f_{23}\Delta V_{2}+f_{43}\Delta V_{4}\Big)u_{3}\Delta V_{3}+\Big(f_{14}\Delta V_{1}\Big)u_{4}\Delta V_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in S_{1}^{\prime}}f_{j1}\Delta V_{j}\Delta V_{1}u_{1}+\sum_{j\in S_{2}^{\prime}}f_{j2}\Delta V_{j}\Delta V_{2}u_{2}+\sum_{j\in S_{3}^{\prime}}f_{j3}\Delta V_{j}\Delta V_{3}u_{3}+\sum_{j\in S_{4}^{\prime}}f_{j4}\Delta V_{j}\Delta V_{4}u_{4}

(109)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}f_{ji}\Delta V_{j}\Big)u_{i}\Delta V_{i}

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}u_{j}\Delta V_{j}\Big)\Delta V_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}u_{i}\Delta V_{j}\Big)\Delta V_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}f_{ji}\Delta V_{j}\Big)u_{i}\Delta V_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}u_{i}\Delta V_{j}\Big)\Delta V_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}f_{ji}\Delta V_{j}-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}\Delta V_{j}\Big)u_{i}\Delta V_{i}

(110)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}f_{ji}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}\,dV_{j}\Big)u_{i}\,dV_{i}

At last, we obtain

(111)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}(u_{j}-u_{i})\,dV_{j}\,dV_{i}=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\prime}}f_{ji}\,dV_{j}-\int_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}f_{ij}\,dV_{j}\Big)u_{i}\,dV_{i}

Above equation is widely used in the derivation of nonlocal strong form from weak form. Such expression is valid in the continuum form as well as in discrete form.

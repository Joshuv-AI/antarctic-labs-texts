ON COSILTING HEARTS OVER THE KRONECKER ALGEBRA
Alessandro Rapa

Abstract
This paper is about the hearts arising from torsion pairs of finite type in the category of modules over the Kronecker algebra. After a characterization of the simple objects in these hearts, we describe their atom spectrum and compute their Gabriel dimension.



1
Introduction

With the introduction of
\tau
-tilting theory for finite dimensional algebras by Adachi, Iyama and Reiten, in
[
1
]
, and the proof of the bijection between support
\tau
-tilting modules and functorially finite torsion classes, a renewed focus has been estabilished on the study of torsion pairs in the category of finite dimensional modules over a finite dimensional algebra. After that, in
[
20
]
, the authors looked at the bigger picture by studying the collection of all the torsion pairs, not only the functorially finite ones, in the category of finite dimensional modules over a finite dimensional algebra, exploring its lattice theoretical properties.

For a finite dimensional algebra
\Lambda
, the torsion pairs in the category
\Lambda
-mod
of finite dimensional
\Lambda
-modules are in bijection with the torsion pairs of finite type in the category
\Lambda
-Mod
of all
\Lambda
-modules (see
[
18
]
). A torsion pair is of
finite type
if the torsionfree class is closed under direct limits. In this paper we direct our attention on the torsion pairs of finite type of
\Lambda
-Mod
and on the hearts of the t-structures arising from them, describing properties that comes from the characterization of their simple objects.

Being closed under direct limits, the torsionfree class of a torsion pair of finite type is clearly a
definable
class, meaning that it is closed under direct products, direct limits and pure submodules. This leads to one of the fundamental results stating that definable torsionfree classes, and so, torsion pairs of finite type, bijectively corresponds to cosilting classes, and so, torsion pairs cogenerated by a cosilting
\Lambda
-module (cf.
[
15
]
and
[
39
]
).

Buan and Krause
[
13
]
gave a classification of all the cotilting modules over a tame hereditary artin algebra and, moreover, a complete classification of all the silting modules over the Kronecker algebra has been given in
[
8
]
. With this information, we can list all the cosilting
\Lambda
-modules and so all the torsion pairs of finite type in
\Lambda
-Mod
. There are basically two types of torsion pairs of finite type in
\Lambda
-Mod
: the ones that are cogenerated by a finite dimensional cosilting
\Lambda
-module, which are in bijection with the functorially finite torsion pairs in
\Lambda
-mod
, and the ones that are cogenerated by an infinite dimensional cosilting
\Lambda
-module, which correspond to the torsion pairs generated either by a nonempty subset of the tubular family
\mathbf{t}
or by the preinjective component
\mathbf{q}
.

The construction of a t-structure arising from a torsion pair goes back to Happel, Reiten and Smalø
[
23
]
. It is well known that the heart of a t-structure is always an abelian category and, in our specific case, since the considered torsion pairs are cogenerated by cosilting modules (equivalently, they are of finite type), the heart is always a Grothendieck category (see
[
27
]
).

First of all, we give a complete description of the simple objects in these hearts and their injective envelopes. In order to do so we use the notion of
torsionfree, almost torsion
object defined in
[
4
]
(together with the dual concept of
torsion, almost torsionfree
object) and introduced also in
[
12
]
under the name of
minimal extending modules
. Torsionfree, almost torsion objects, with respect to a given torsion pair, are torsionfree objects whose proper quotients are torsion (torsion, almost torsionfree objects are defined dually). In a sense, one can think about torsionfree, almost torsion objects as objects very close to the ”border” of the torsion pair.

After this, we use the description of the simples to compute the
atom spectrum
of the different hearts. The atom spectrum has been introduced by Kanda, in
[
24
]
, for a general abelian category and it is a generalization of the prime spectrum for commutative rings. Accordingly, it has a structure of topological space and, for a Grothendieck category, it is strongly related to the spectrum of the indecomposable injective objects. The elements of the atom spectrum are called
atoms
and they are built as equivalence classes of
monoform
objects, which are objects
X
of the category such that, for any subobject
Y
, there are no common nonzero subobjects between
X
and
X/Y
. Simple objects are indeed the first example of monoform objects. For a monoform object
X
, the corresponding atom is denoted by
\overline{X}
. We obtain the following:

Theorem

(Theorem
7.5
)
.

Let
\Lambda
be the Kronecker algebra (over a field
k
). Denote by
P_{1}
and
Q_{1}
the simple projective and the simple injective
\Lambda
-modules, respectively. For a torsion pair of finite type
\mathsf{t}
in
\Lambda
-Mod
, we denote by
\mathcal{A}
the heart of the t-structure arising from
\mathsf{t}
.

If
\mathsf{t}
is cogenerated by a finite dimensional cosilting
\Lambda
-module, we get:

•

\Aspec(\mathcal{A})\cong\Aspec(k\mathrm{-Mod})=\{\overline{k}\}
, for the torsion pair cogenerated by
Q_{1}
.

•

\Aspec(\mathcal{A})\cong\Aspec(\mbox{\rm$\Lambda$-Mod})=\{\overline{P_{1}},\overline{Q_{1}}\}
, for all the other torsion pairs.

Let
\mathbf{t}
be the family of homogeneous tubes in
\Lambda
-Mod
, indexed by the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
. Consider
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
and denote by
G
the generic
\Lambda
-module. If
\mathsf{t}
is generated by
\mathbf{q}
or by a nonempty subset of
\mathbf{t}
indexed by
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
, then:

\displaystyle\Aspec(\mathcal{A})=\overline{G[1]}

\displaystyle\cup\{\overline{S}\mid S\textrm{ simple regular in a tube indexed by }\mathcal{U}\}\,\cup

\displaystyle\cup\{\overline{S[1]}\mid S\textrm{ simple regular in a tube indexed by }\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{U}\}.

In the last part of the paper we give a direct computation of the
Gabriel dimension
of the different hearts. The notion of Gabriel dimension has been introduced by Gabriel, in
[
21
]
, under the name of Krull dimension, as a way to understand the complexity of a Grothendieck category using an iterated localization procedure. Whenever the Grothendieck category is a Gabriel category, ie. a category with finite Gabriel dimension, the atom spectrum is in bijective correspondence with the spectrum of the indecomposable injective objects (see
[
36
]
). Our result is that all the hearts arising from the torsion pairs of finite type are Gabriel categories, giving an a posteriori evidence of the fact that the atom spectrum of every heart bijectively corresponds to the spectrum of the indecomposable injective objects in that heart.

Torsion pairs and t-structures

.

A

and

(1)

.

(2)

(resp. the

is:

•

: if every short exact sequence

T\in\mathcal{T}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}

•

\mathcal{T}

•

{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp_{1}}=\Ker\Ext^{1}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{M},-)

Definition 2.4
.

A pair of full subcategories of
\mathcal{D}
,
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})
, is called a

if it satisfies the properties below. We use the following notation:
\mathcal{D}^{\leq n}=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}[-n]
and
\mathcal{D}^{\geq n}=\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}[-n]
.

(1)

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 1})=0
,

(2)

\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\leq 1}
(and
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}\supseteq\mathcal{D}^{\geq 1}
),

(3)

For every object
X\in\mathcal{D}
, there is a triangle
A\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow A[1]
, with
A\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
and
B\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq 1}
.

For a t-structure
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})
, the full subcategory defined as:

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}

is called the
heart
of the t-structure.
\mathcal{A}
is always an abelian category and its abelian structure comes from the triangulated structure of
\mathcal{D}
(ie. a short exact sequence
0\to X\to Y\to Z\to 0
in
\mathcal{A}
is exact if and only if there is a triangle
X\to Y\to Z\to X[1]
in
\mathcal{D}
with
X,Y,Z\in\mathcal{A}
).

Example 2.5
.

Consider a Grothendieck category
\mathcal{G}
and a torsion pair
(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{C})
in it. The full subcategories of
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{G})
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}=\{X\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{G})\mid H^{0}(X)\in\mathcal{Q},H^{i}(X)=0\text{ for }i>0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}=\{X\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{G})\mid H^{-1}(X)\in\mathcal{C},H^{i}(X)=0\text{ for }i<-1\}

form a t-structure

, called the

, see

. Its heart is the category:

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}=\{X\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{G})\mid H^{0}(X)\in\mathcal{Q},H^{-1}(X)\in\mathcal{C},H^{i}(X)=0\text{ for }i\neq-1,0\}

and it is called the
HRS-heart
. In the sequel, we will denote by
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{C})
the HRS-heart of the HRS-t-structure arising from the torsion pair
(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{C})
on the category
\mathcal{G}
.

For any two objects

there are functorial isomorphisms

, for

. Moreover, the pair

is a torsion pair in

2.2
Silting and cosilting modules

In this section we recall, mainly from

]

[

]

Let

(resp.

-Mod

Let

\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}=\{X\in\mbox{\rm Mod-$A$}\mid\Hom_{A}(\sigma,X)\text{ is surjective}\}

Definition 2.7
.

An

if it admits a projective presentation

such that
\Gen T=\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}
. In this case, the torsion class
\Gen T
is called

.

is called

if
{T}^{\perp_{1}}=\Gen T
.

Two silting modules

and

are
equivalent
if they generate the same silting class or, equivalently, if
\Add T=\Add T^{\prime}
. An
A
-module

is tilting if and only if
T
is silting with respect a projective presentation

, with

[

, Proposition 3.12]
).

Remark 2.8
.

[
7
, Remark 3.11]

If

is a silting module, then
(\Gen T,{T}^{\perp_{0}})
is a torsion pair.

In a dual fashion, let

be a map between two injective
A
-modules and consider:

\mathcal{C}_{\omega}=\{X\in\mbox{\rm Mod-$A$}\mid\Hom_{A}(X,\omega)\text{ is surjective}\}

Definition 2.9
.

An
A
-module
C
is called
cosilting
if it admits an injective copresentation

such that
\Cogen C=\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
. In this case, the torsionfree class

is called

.

is called

if
{}^{\perp_{1}}{C}=\Cogen C
.

Two cosilting modules

are

if they generate the same cosilting class or, equivalently, if
\Prod C=\Prod C^{\prime}
. An
A
-module

is cotilting if and only if

is cosilting with respect an injective copresentation

]

Remark 2.10
.

[

If

A class

in

4
The Atom Spectrum

In this section we introduce the notion of atom spectrum for a Grothendieck category. This is a generalization of the prime spectrum for a commutative ring and, in a similar fashion, it is endowed with a topological space structure. The notion of atom spectrum has been introduced by Kanda in
[
24
]
, in the more general setting of abelian categories. Moreover, some properties will appear in a forthcoming work by Vámos and Virili,
[
36
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an Grothendieck category and let
X
be an object of
\mathcal{G}
.
X
is said to be
monoform
if, given any subobject
H
of
X
and a morphism
\varphi\in\Hom_{\mathcal{G}}(H,X)
,
\varphi
is non-zero if and only if it is a non-zero monomorphism.

Lemma 4.2
.

[
36
, Lemma 2.10]

Let
X\in\mathcal{G}
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

X
is monoform.

(ii)

for any non-zero subobject
H\subseteq X
, the unique object isomorphic to both a subobject of
X
and to a subobject of
X/H
is the zero object.

(iii)

X
is uniform and, for any non-zero subobject
H\subseteq X
, the unique object isomorphic to both a subobject of
H
and to a subobject of
X/H
is the zero object.

Any simple object is monoform. We say that two monoform objects
X
and
X^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{G}
are
atom-equivalent
if there exist a nonzero
H\in\mathcal{G}
such that
X\supseteq H\subseteq X^{\prime}
. Clearly, two non isomorphic simple objects are not atom-equivalent.

Example 4.3
.

Let
\Lambda
be the Kronecker algebra. As mentioned before, the two simple
\Lambda
-modules
P_{1}
and
Q_{1}
are monoform. On the contrary, the generic module
G
is not monoform, indeed we have a short exact sequence
0\to P_{1}\to G\to\bigoplus S_{\infty}\to 0
(see
[
29
, Theorem 6.1]
) and
P_{1}
is a submodule of any Prüfer module.

Lemma 4.4
.

[
24
, Lemma 5.8]
[
36
, Lemma 2.13]

Two monoform objects
X
and
X^{\prime}
are atom-equivalent if and only if
E(X)\cong E(X^{\prime})
.

As proven in
[
24
, Proposition 2.8]
, the atom-equivalence relation is an equivalence relation. We denote by
\overline{X}
the class of all monoform objects atom-equivalent to the monoform object
X
, these equivalence classes are called
atoms
.

Definition 4.5
.

The
atom spectrum
of an abelian category
\mathcal{G}
, denoted by
\Aspec(\mathcal{G})
, is the class of all atoms in
\mathcal{G}
.

Recall that, if
\mathsf{t}=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is a hereditary torsion pair in
\mathcal{G}
, then
\mathcal{T}
is a localizing subcategory of
\mathcal{G}
. Hence, we have the so called localization sequence:

\textstyle{\mathcal{T}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{inc}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{Q}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{S}}

where
inc
is the inclusion functor,
\mathsf{T}
is the left-exact torsion radical (see
[
33
, §VI.3]
), the Grothendieck category
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}
is the localization of
\mathcal{G}
at
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathsf{Q}
and
\mathsf{S}
are the quotient functor and the section functor, respectively, of the localization.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\mathsf{t}=(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{C})
be a torsion pair in
\mathcal{G}
. An object
Y
in
\mathcal{G}
is
\mathsf{t}
-

if

and all proper quotients of

are in
\mathcal{Q}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\mathsf{t}=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
be a hereditary torsion pair and let
\mathsf{Q}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}
be the quotient functor. The following are equivalent for

:

(i)

X
is
\mathsf{t}
-cocritical

(ii)

\mathsf{Q}(X)
is simple in
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}
and
X\in\mathcal{F}

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Let
Y
be a nonzero subobject of
\mathsf{Q}(X)\in\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}
. Then, applying the section functor
\mathsf{S}\colon\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{G}
, we have
\mathsf{S}(Y)\subseteq\mathsf{S}\mathsf{Q}(X)
. Moreover,
\mathsf{S}\mathsf{Q}(X)\subseteq E(X)
, indeed, by
[
21
, Proposition III.3.6]
,
\mathsf{S}\mathsf{Q}(X)\subseteq\mathsf{S}\mathsf{Q}(E(X))\cong E(X)
.

X
is essential in

, therefore we have that
\mathsf{S}(Y)\neq 0
if and only if
\mathsf{S}(Y)\cap X\neq 0
, then, since

is
\mathsf{t}
-cocritical,
X/(\mathsf{S}(Y)\cap X)\in\mathcal{T}
. Applying the functor
\mathsf{Q}
to the short exact sequence:

we obtain that
\mathsf{Q}(X)\cong\mathsf{Q}(\mathsf{S}(Y)\cap X)\subseteq\mathsf{Q}\mathsf{S}(Y)=Y\subseteq\mathsf{Q}(X)
. Therefore

.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Let
Y
be a nonzero subobject of
X
, then

and
\mathsf{Q}(Y)
is nonzero. Since
\mathsf{Q}
is an exact functor and
\mathsf{Q}(X)
is simple,
\mathsf{Q}(Y)=\mathsf{Q}(X)
. Hence, applying
\mathsf{Q}
to the sequence

, we get
\mathsf{Q}(X/Y)=0
hence

.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

[
36
, Proposition 2.12]

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Grothendieck category and let

. Consider the torsion pair
\mathsf{t}_{X}=(\mathcal{T}_{X},\mathcal{F}_{X})

. The followings are equivalent:

(i)

(ii)

\mathsf{Q}_{X}(X)
is the unique simple object in
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}_{X}
, up to isomorphisms (where
\mathsf{Q}_{X}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}_{X}
denotes the quotient functor relative to

).

(iii)

there exists a hereditary torsion pair
\mathsf{t}=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
such that

and
\mathsf{Q}(X)
is simple in
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}
.

Moreover, if the above conditions are verified, a monoform object

is atom-equivalent to

if and only if

is isomorphic to a subobject of
\mathsf{S}_{X}\mathsf{Q}_{X}(X)
(where
\mathsf{S}_{X}\colon\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}_{X}\to\mathcal{G}
denotes the section functor relative to

).

.

Notice that, by Lemma

, we can say that an object

is monoform if and only if there exists a hereditary torsion pair
\mathsf{t}=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
such that

is
\mathsf{t}
-cocritical.

The atom spectrum of an abelian category may not be a set. On the other hand, we have that

[

[

\textstyle{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{G})}
\textstyle{\overline{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

.

For the Kronecker algebra

Topology and partial order on the atom spectrum

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Grothendieck category. In
[
24
]
, the author defines a topological space structure on
\Aspec(\mathcal{G})
by means of the notion of atom support.

Definition 4.12
.

Let
M
be an object of
\mathcal{G}
. Define the
atom support
of
M
as the set:

\Asupp M=\{\alpha\in\Aspec(\mathcal{G})\mid\alpha=\overline{H}\text{ for a monoform subquotient $H$ of }M\}

Let
\Phi
be a subset of
\Aspec(\mathcal{G})
.
\Phi
is
open
if for any atom
\alpha\in\Phi
, there exists a monoform object
H\in\mathcal{G}
such that
\overline{H}=\alpha
and
\Asupp H\subset\Phi
.

It is clear that, for a simple object
S
,
\Asupp S=\{\overline{S}\}
. By
[
25
, Proposition 3.2]
, the set of all open sets in
\Aspec(\mathcal{G})
is given by the family
\{\Asupp M\mid M\in\mathcal{G}\}
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{G}
is locally noetherian, the family restricts to
\{\Asupp M\mid M\text{ noetherian in }\mathcal{G}\}
.

Open singletons in
\Aspec(\mathcal{G})
are characterized by the following:

Proposition 4.13
.

[
25
, Proposition 3.7]

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a locally noetherian Grothendieck category and let
\alpha\in\Aspec(\mathcal{G})
. The set
\{\alpha\}
is open if and only if there exists a simple object
S
in
\mathcal{G}
such that
\overline{S}=\alpha
.

By
[
25
, Proposition 3.5]
, the atom spectrum of
\mathcal{G}
is a
T_{0}
-space
(or
Kolmogorov space
), ie. a space in which, for any distinct points
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
in it, there exists an open subset containing exactly one of them.
It is well known that, if
X
is a
T_{0}
-space, it is possible to define a partial order
\preceq
on it, called
specialization order
, in the following way: for any
x,y\in X
, we define
x\preceq y
if and only if
x\in\overline{\{y\}}
, where
\overline{\{y\}}
is the topological closure of
\{y\}
in
X
. Conversely, a partially ordered set
P
can be seen as a topological space as follows: a subset
\Phi
of
P
is open if and only if for any
p,q\in P
such that
p\preceq q
,
p\in\Phi
implies
q\in\Phi
. These correspondences are mutually inverse.

\Aspec(\mathcal{G})
becomes a partially ordered set defining a specialization order
\leq
on it. We have the following:

Proposition 4.14
.

[
25
, Proposition 4.2]

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Grothendieck category and
\alpha,\beta\in\Aspec(\mathcal{G})
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\alpha\leq\beta
, i.e.
\alpha\in\overline{\{\beta\}}
.

(2)

If
\Phi
is an open subset of
\Aspec(\mathcal{G})
such that
\alpha\in\Phi
, then
\beta\in\Phi
. In other words,
\beta
belongs to the intersection of all the open subsets containing
\alpha
.

(3)

For any object
M
in
\mathcal{G}
such that
\alpha\in\Asupp M
, we have
\beta\in\Asupp M
.

(4)

For any monoform object
H
in
\mathcal{G}
such that
\overline{H}=\alpha
, we have
\beta\in\Asupp H
.

\mathsf{t}_{\bar{\mathcal{U}}}

∎

6
On Gabriel categories

Consider
\mathsf{t}=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
a hereditary torsion pair in
\mathcal{G}
together with the localization sequence, as we have mentioned in Section
4
:

\textstyle{\mathcal{T}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{inc}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{Q}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{S}}

For a set
\mathcal{X}
of objects in
\mathcal{G}
, we denote by
{\mathopen{\langle}\mathcal{X}\mathclose{\rangle}_{\mathrm{htor}}}
the smallest hereditary torsion class containing
\mathcal{X}
.

Definition 6.1
.

The
Gabriel filtration
of
\mathcal{G}
is a transfinite chain of hereditary torsion classes of
\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{G}_{-1}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\subseteq\dots

where:

•

\mathcal{G}_{-1}=\{0\}

•

suppose that
\alpha
is an ordinal for which
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
has already been defined. Let
\mathsf{Q}_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
be the quotient functor. We define
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}
as:

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}={\mathopen{\langle}\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\cup\{X\in\mathcal{G}\mid\mathsf{Q}_{\alpha}(X)\text{ is simple in }\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\}\mathclose{\rangle}_{\mathrm{htor}}}

•

if
\lambda
is a limit ordinal, then:

\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}={\mathopen{\langle}\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\mathclose{\rangle}_{\mathrm{htor}}}

Let
\alpha
be an ordinal. An object
X
in
\mathcal{G}
is said to be
\alpha
-torsion
(resp.
\alpha
-torsionfree
) if and only if
X\in\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
(resp.
X\in{\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}}^{\perp_{0}}
).

Remark 6.2
.

The hereditary torsion pair
\mathsf{t}_{\alpha}=(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha},{\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}}^{\perp_{0}})
induces the localization sequence:

\textstyle{\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{inc}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{T}_{\alpha}}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{Q}_{\alpha}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{S}_{\alpha}}

and, for simplicity, a
\mathsf{t}_{\alpha}
-cocritical object in
\mathcal{G}
will be called
\alpha
-cocritical
.

Notice that, given the class
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
in the Gabriel filtration, we can define
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}
, using Lemma
4.7
, as

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}={\mathopen{\langle}\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\cup\{X\in\mathcal{G}\mid X\text{ is }\alpha\text{-cocritical}\}\mathclose{\rangle}_{\mathrm{htor}}}.

Remark 6.3
.

[
37
, Remark 2.12]

Let
G
be a generator of the Grothendieck category
\mathcal{G}
. It is known that
G
has just a set of quotient objects. One can show that:

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}={\mathopen{\langle}\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\cup\{X\in\mathcal{G}\mid X\text{ is a quotient of }G,\mathsf{Q}_{\alpha}(X)\text{ is simple in }\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\}\mathclose{\rangle}_{\mathrm{htor}}}

This means that the Gabriel filtration eventually stabilizes, ie. there is a cardinal
\kappa
such that
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
for all
\alpha\geq\kappa
, just take
\kappa=\sup\{\alpha\mid\text{ there is }H\subseteq G\text{ such that }\mathsf{Q}_{\alpha}(G/H)\text{ is simple}\}
.

By virtue of the previous Remark, it is meaningful to consider the union
\bar{\mathcal{G}}=\bigcup_{\alpha}\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
of all the localizing subcategories in the Gabriel filtration.

Definition 6.4
.

For an object
X\in\mathcal{G}
, we say that
X
has
Gabriel dimension
if there is a minimal ordinal
\delta
such that
X\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
, and we write
\Gdim(X)=\delta
. If
\bar{\mathcal{G}}=\mathcal{G}
, we say that
\mathcal{G}
is a
Gabriel category
with Gabriel dimension
\Gdim(\mathcal{G})=\kappa
, where
\kappa
is the smallest ordinal such that
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}=\mathcal{G}
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Every locally noetherian Grothendieck category is a Gabriel category.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a locally noetherian Grothendieck category and consider its Gabriel filtration:

\{0\}=\mathcal{G}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\subseteq\dots

By Remark
6.3
this filtration stabilizes, ie. there is a cardinal
\kappa
such that
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
for all
\alpha\geq\kappa
.
Let
\mathcal{N}
be the set of all the noetherian generators of
\mathcal{G}
. We prove that
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
. Indeed: suppose that there is
N\in\mathcal{N}
such that
N\notin\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
. Consider the set:

\mathcal{I}=\{X\subseteq N\mid N/X\notin\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}\}

which is not empty since
0\in\mathcal{I}
. Since
N
is noetherian,
\mathcal{I}
has a maximal element
\bar{X}
. Therefore, for any proper subobject
Y
such that
\bar{X}\subseteq Y\subseteq N
, we have
N/Y\in\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
. Moreover,
N/\bar{X}
is
\kappa
-torsionfree, indeed: if it is not, it has a nonzero
\kappa
-torsion part
\mathsf{T}_{\kappa}(N/\bar{X})
such that
(N/\bar{X})/\mathsf{T}_{\kappa}(N/\bar{X})
is
\kappa
-torsionfree, but all the proper quotients of
N/\bar{X}
are
\kappa
-torsion, by the maximality of
\bar{X}
. This means that
N/\bar{X}
is
\kappa
-cocritical, since it is
\kappa
-torsionfree and any proper quotient of
N/\bar{X}
is in
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
. Hence,
\mathsf{Q}_{\kappa}(N/\bar{X})
is simple in
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
and then
N/\bar{X}\in\mathcal{G}_{\kappa+1}=\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
. Contradiction.
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}
, therefore
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa}=\mathcal{G}
.
∎

Example 6.6
.

The Gabriel dimension of
\Lambda
-Mod
, where
\Lambda
is the Kronecker algebra, is 0. Indeed:
\mathcal{G}_{0}={\mathopen{\langle}\{P_{1},Q_{1}\}\mathclose{\rangle}_{\mathrm{htor}}}
, where
P_{1}
and

are the simple
\Lambda
-modules, and there is a short exact sequence
0\to P_{1}\oplus P_{1}\to P_{2}\to Q_{1}\to 0
, since

is the projective cover of

. Therefore the module
\Lambda_{\Lambda}
is in
\mathcal{G}_{0}
. Meaning that
\mathcal{G}_{0}=\mbox{\rm$\Lambda$-Mod}
.

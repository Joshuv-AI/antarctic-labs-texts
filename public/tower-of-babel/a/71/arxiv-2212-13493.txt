1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\neq\emptyset
be an unbounded open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
We study solutions of the inequality

{\mathcal{L}}_{\varphi}u\geq F(x,u)\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,

(1.1)

satisfying the condition

\left.u\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(1.2)

where
F
is a non-negative function and

{\mathcal{L}}_{\varphi}u=\operatorname{div}\left(\frac{\varphi(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\right)

(1.3)

is the
\varphi
-Laplace operator with some increasing one-to-one function

\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

such that

\left(\frac{\varphi(|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\xi-\frac{\varphi(|\zeta|)}{|\zeta|}\zeta\right)(\xi-\zeta)>0

(1.4)

for all
\xi,\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
\xi\neq\zeta
. As is
customary, by
B_{r}
and
S_{r}
we mean the open ball and the sphere
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of radius
r>0
centered at zero. Let us denote

\Omega_{r,\sigma}=\Omega\cap B_{\sigma r}\setminus\overline{B_{r/\sigma}},

r>0
,
\sigma>1
. Following
[
16
]
, we say that
f\in W_{p,loc}^{1}(\Omega)
,
p\geq 1
, if
f\in W_{p}^{1}(\Omega\cap B_{r})
for any real number
r>0
. The space
L_{p,loc}(\Omega)
,

p\geq 1
, is defined in a similar way.

The phenomenon of the absence of solutions of differential
equations and inequalities also known as blow-up has been
intensively studied by many mathematicians [1–6, 8–15, 17].
Among the inequalities with nonlinearity in the principal part,
inequalities containing the
\varphi
-Laplacian are most general.
This operator was introduced by J. Serrin. It appears in various
applications, for instants, in the theory of Riemannian
manifolds
[
18
]
.

In the paper presented to your attention, we manage to obtain
sufficient blow-up conditions for solutions of inequalities with the

\varphi
-Laplacian and a nonlinearity of a very general form in the
right-hand side. The exactness of these conditions is shown in
Examples 1 and 2.

A function
u\in W_{1,loc}^{1}(\Omega)\cap L_{\infty,loc}(\Omega)
is called a weak solution of (
1.1
) if
F(x,u)\in L_{1,loc}(\Omega),

\varphi(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|\in L_{1,loc}(\Omega),

and

-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\nabla\psi\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\psi\,dx

for any non-negative function

\psi\in{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm\scriptscriptstyle o}}{{W}}\!\!{}_{1}^{1}(\Omega)}\cap L_{\infty}(\Omega)

with a compact support such that

\int_{\Omega}\varphi(|\nabla u|)|\nabla\psi|\,dx<\infty.

(1.5)

Analogously, a function
u\in W_{1,loc}^{1}(\Omega)\cap L_{\infty,loc}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of the inequality

{\mathcal{L}}_{\varphi}u\leq F(x,u)\quad\mbox{in }\Omega

if

F(x,u)\in L_{1,loc}(\Omega),

\varphi(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|\in L_{1,loc}(\Omega),

and

-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\nabla\psi\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\psi\,dx

for any non-negative function

\psi\in{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm\scriptscriptstyle o}}{{W}}\!\!{}_{1}^{1}(\Omega)}\cap L_{\infty}(\Omega)

with a compact support such that (
1.5
) holds.
In its turn, a function
u\in W_{1,loc}^{1}(\Omega)\cap L_{\infty,loc}(\Omega)
is called a weak solution of the equation

{\mathcal{L}}_{\varphi}u=F(x,u)\quad\mbox{in }\Omega

if

F(x,u)\in L_{1,loc}(\Omega),

\varphi(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|\in L_{1,loc}(\Omega),

and

-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\nabla\psi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\psi\,dx

for any function

\psi\in{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm\scriptscriptstyle o}}{{W}}\!\!{}_{1}^{1}(\Omega)}\cap L_{\infty}(\Omega)

with a compact support satisfying relation (
1.5
).

Condition (
1.2
) means that

\gamma u\in{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm\scriptscriptstyle o}}{{W}}\!\!{}_{1}^{1}(\Omega)}

for all
\gamma\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Note that, for
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, this condition is obviously valid.

Remark 1.
We understand the support of the function

\psi
in the sense of distributions from
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. In so doing, we extend
\psi
by zero outside the set

\Omega
. The inclusion
\varphi(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|\in L_{1,loc}(\Omega),

obviously, implies that

\varphi(|\nabla u|)\in L_{1,loc}(\Omega).

Indeed, for any real number
r>0
we have

\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r}}\varphi(|\nabla u|)\,dx=\int_{\{x\in\Omega\cap B_{r}:|\nabla u(x)|\leq 1\}}\varphi(|\nabla u|)\,dx+\int_{\{x\in\Omega\cap B_{r}:|\nabla u(x)|>1\}}\varphi(|\nabla u|)\,dx,

where

\int_{\{x\in\Omega\cap B_{r}:|\nabla u(x)|\leq 1\}}\varphi(|\nabla u|)\,dx\leq\varphi(1)\operatorname{mes}\Omega\cap B_{r}<\infty

and

\int_{\{x\in\Omega\cap B_{r}:|\nabla u(x)|>1\}}\varphi(|\nabla u|)\,dx\leq\int_{\{x\in\Omega\cap B_{r}:|\nabla u(x)|>1\}}\varphi(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|\,dx<\infty.

A function
g:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is called semi-multiplicative if

g(t_{1})g(t_{2})\geq g(t_{1}t_{2})

for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in(0,\infty)
. In the case where the inequality
in the last formula can be replaced by equality, the function
g

is multiplicative. Using the isomorphism
t\mapsto e^{t}
between the
additive group of real numbers and the multiplicative group of
positive reals, one can assert that a function
g
is
semi-multiplicative if and only if
G(t)=\ln g(e^{t})
is
semi-additive, i.e.,

G(t_{1})+G(t_{2})\geq G(t_{1}t_{2})

for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
In so doing, a function
g:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is almost semi-multiplicative if

g(t_{1})g(t_{2})\geq cg(t_{1}t_{2})

(1.6)

with some constant
c>0
for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in(0,\infty)
.
In its turn,
G:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is almost semi-additive function if

G(t_{1})+G(t_{2})\geq G(t_{1}t_{2})-C

with some constant
C>0
for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. It
can easily be seen that a function
g
is almost
semi-multiplicative if and only if
G(t)=\ln g(e^{t})
is almost
semi-additive.

The simplest example of an almost semi-multiplicative function is
the power function
g(t)=t^{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
This function is also multiplicative. As another example, we can
take
g(t)=t^{\lambda}\ln^{\nu}(2+t)
, where
\lambda\geq 0
and

\nu\geq 0
are real numbers.

Note that the product and superposition of almost
semi-multiplicative functions is also an almost semi-multiplicative
function. Taking
t_{1}=\tau_{1}\tau_{2}
and
t_{2}=1/\tau_{2}

in (
1.6
), we obtain

g(\tau_{1}\tau_{2})\geq\frac{cg(\tau_{1})}{g(1/\tau_{2})}

(1.7)

for all real numbers
\tau_{1}>0
and
\tau_{2}>0
. According
to (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), for any almost semi-multiplicative
function
g
and real number
c>0
there exist constants
c_{1}>0

and
c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}g(t)\leq g(ct)\leq c_{2}g(t)

for all real numbers
t>0
.

Below we assume that

\Omega\cap S_{r}\neq\emptyset
for all
r\geq r_{0}
, where
r_{0}>0
is a real number.
In addition, let

\inf_{x\in\Omega_{r,\sigma}}F(x,t)\geq f(r)g(t)

(1.8)

with some real number
\sigma>1

for all
r\geq r_{0}
and
t>0
, where

f\in L_{\infty,loc}([r_{0},\infty))

and

g\in L_{\infty,loc}(0,\infty)

are non-negative functions with

\inf_{K}g>0

for any compact set
K\subset(0,\infty)
. We also assume that the
function
g
in (
1.8
) and the function inverse to
\varphi

in (
1.3
) are almost semi-multiplicative.

Denote

\eta(t)=\frac{t}{\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)},\quad t>0.

It is easy to see that
\eta
is an increasing one-to-one mapping
of the set
(0,\infty)
into itself. In so doing, since

is an almost semi-multiplicative function, for any

there exist constants
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0

such that

(1.9)

c_{1}\eta^{-1}(t)\leq\eta^{-1}(ct)\leq c_{2}\eta^{-1}(t)

2.
Main results

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\eta
be a convex function and, moreover,

\int_{1}^{\infty}\eta^{-1}\left(\frac{t}{\varphi^{-1}(g(t))}\right)\frac{dt}{t}<\infty

(2.1)

and

\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\eta\left(\frac{r}{\eta^{-1}\left(\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{f(r)}\right)\right)}\right)\frac{dr}{r}=\infty.

(2.2)

Then, any non-negative weak solution of (
1.1
), (
1.2
) is
identically equal to zero.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let condition (
2.1
) be valid and, moreover,

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{\varphi^{-1}(g(t))}<\infty

(2.3)

and

\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\min\left\{\frac{\eta(r)}{r\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{f(r)}\right)},\frac{1}{\eta^{-1}\left(\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{f(r)}\right)\right)}\right\}dr=\infty.

(2.4)

Then, any non-negative weak solution of (
1.1
), (
1.2
) is
identically equal to zero.

Remark 2.
In the case of
f(r)=0
, we assume by
definition that the integrands in the left-hand side
of (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) are equal to zero.

Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
are proved in Section
3
.

(2.6)

i.e.,

c_{1}|x|^{s}\leq c(x)\leq c_{2}|x|^{s}

with some constants
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0
for all

By Theorem

\lambda>p-1\quad\mbox{and}\quad s\geq-p,

then any non-negative weak solution of (

It can be shown that condition (

]

Example 2.

Proof of Theorems
2.1
and

We say that

u_{1}\leq u_{2}\quad\mbox{on }\partial\omega,

(3.1)

where
u_{i}\in W_{1,loc}^{1}(\omega)\cap L_{\infty,loc}(\omega)
,

, are some functions and
\omega
is an open subset of

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, if
\gamma\max\{u_{1}-u_{2},0\}\in{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm\scriptscriptstyle o}}{{W}}\!\!{}_{1}^{1}(\omega)}
for all
\gamma\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. If
\omega
is a

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then condition (

)
obviously means that
\max\{u_{1}-u_{2},0\}\in{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm\scriptscriptstyle o}}{{W}}\!\!{}_{1}^{1}(\omega)}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u_{i}\in W_{1}^{1}(\omega)\cap L_{\infty}(\omega)
,
i=1,2
, be
weak solutions of the inequalities

{\mathcal{L}}_{\varphi}u_{1}\geq\rho(x,u_{1})\quad\mbox{in }\omega

(3.2)

and

{\mathcal{L}}_{\varphi}u_{2}\leq\rho(x,u_{2})\quad\mbox{in }\omega,

(3.3)

satisfying condition (
3.1
), where
\omega
is an open bounded
subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\rho
is some function
non-decreasing with respect to the last argument. If
\varphi(|\nabla u_{i}|)|\nabla u_{j}|\in L_{1}(\omega)

and

\rho(x,u_{i})u_{j}\in L_{1}(\omega),

i,j=1,2
, then

u_{1}\leq u_{2}\quad\mbox{a.e. in }\omega.

(3.4)

Proof.

From (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), it follows that

-\int_{\tilde{\omega}}\frac{\varphi(|\nabla u_{1}|)}{|\nabla u_{1}|}\nabla u_{1}\nabla\psi\,dx\geq\int_{\tilde{\omega}}\rho(x,u_{1})\psi\,dx

and

(3.6)

where

\psi=\max\{u_{1}-u_{2},0\}

and
\tilde{\omega}=\{x\in\omega:u_{1}(x)>u_{2}(x)\}

Thus, subtracting (

\int_{\tilde{\omega}}\left(\frac{\varphi(|\nabla u_{1}|)}{|\nabla u_{1}|}\nabla u_{1}-\frac{\varphi(|\nabla u_{2}|)}{|\nabla u_{2}|}\nabla u_{2}\right)(\nabla u_{1}-\nabla u_{2})\,dx\leq 0.

In view of (

).

We need the following generalization of well-known Kato

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u\in W_{1,loc}^{1}(\omega)\cap L_{\infty,loc}(\omega)
be a weak solution of the problem

{\mathcal{L}}_{\varphi}u\geq a(x)\quad\mbox{in }\omega,\quad u\leq 0\quad\mbox{on }\partial\omega,

(3.7)

where
\omega
is an open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
a\in L_{1,loc}(\omega)
. We denote
\tilde{\omega}=\{x\in\omega:u(x)>0\}
and

\tilde{a}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &a(x)&&x\in\tilde{\omega},\\
&0,&&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\tilde{\omega}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then, the function

\tilde{u}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &u(x),&&x\in\tilde{\omega},\\
&0,&&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\tilde{\omega},\end{aligned}\right.

is a weak solution of the inequality

{\mathcal{L}}_{\varphi}\tilde{u}\geq\tilde{a}(x)\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Proof.

The proof, with minor differences, repeats the reasoning given in
[
12
, Lemma 4.2]
.
In order not to be
unfounded, we present this proof in full. Let us take a
non-decreasing function
\zeta\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
such
that
\zeta=0
on the interval
(-\infty,1/4]
and
\zeta=1

on
[3/4,\infty)
. Put
\zeta_{\varepsilon}(t)=\zeta(t/\varepsilon),

where
\varepsilon>0
is a real number.
In view of (
3.7
), we have

-\int_{\omega}\frac{\varphi(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\nabla(\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)\psi)\,dx\geq\int_{\omega}a(x)\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)\psi\,dx

(3.8)

for any non-negative function

\psi\in{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm\scriptscriptstyle o}}{{W}}\!\!{}_{1}^{1}(\Omega)}\cap L_{\infty,loc}(\omega)

with a compact support such that

\int_{\omega}\varphi(|\nabla u|)|\nabla\psi|\,dx<\infty.

Note that

\varphi(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|\in L_{1,loc}(\Omega)
by the definition of solutions of (
3.7
); therefore,

\int_{\omega}\varphi(|\nabla u|)|\nabla(\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)\psi)|\,dx<\infty.

We also have

\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)\psi\in{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm\scriptscriptstyle o}}{{W}}\!\!{}_{1}^{1}(\omega)}\cap L_{\infty,loc}(\omega).

Hence,
\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)\psi
can be used as a test function in (
3.8
).

Expanding the brackets in the left-hand side of (
3.8
), we obtain

-\int_{\omega}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)\frac{\varphi(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\nabla\psi\,dx\geq\int_{\omega}\varphi(|\nabla u|)|\nabla u|\zeta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u)\psi\,dx+\int_{\omega}a(x)\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)\psi\,dx.

(3.9)

The first summand on the right in the last expression is non-negative, whereas

\int_{\omega}\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)\frac{\varphi(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\nabla\psi\,dx\to\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\varphi(|\nabla\tilde{u}|)}{|\nabla\tilde{u}|}\nabla\tilde{u}\nabla\psi\,dx

and

\int_{\omega}a(x)\zeta_{\varepsilon}(u)\psi\,dx\to\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\tilde{a}(x)\psi\,dx

as
\varepsilon\to+0
. Thus, passing to the limit
in (
3.9
) as
\varepsilon\to+0
, we arrive at the
inequality

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\varphi(|\nabla\tilde{u}|)}{|\nabla\tilde{u}|}\nabla\tilde{u}\nabla\psi\,dx\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\tilde{a}(x)\psi\,dx.

The proof is completed.

Proof.

By direct differentiation, it can be verified that

.
Let us shown that

w(r_{2})\geq C(r_{2}-r_{1})\varphi^{-1}((r_{2}-r_{1}){\mathcal{F}}).

(3.13)

Really, in accordance with Lagrange’s theorem

\tau^{n}-r_{1}^{n}=n\tau_{*}^{n-1}(\tau-r_{1})

for some
\tau_{*}\in(r_{1},\tau)
; therefore,

\frac{\tau^{n}-r_{1}^{n}}{n\tau^{n-1}}\geq\frac{\tau-r_{1}}{\sigma^{n-1}}

for all
\tau\in(r_{1},r_{2})
, whence it follows that

w(r_{2})\geq\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{\tau-r_{1}}{\sigma^{n-1}}{\mathcal{F}}\right)d\tau\geq\int_{(r_{1}+r_{2})/2}^{r_{2}}\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{\tau-r_{1}}{\sigma^{n-1}}{\mathcal{F}}\right)d\tau\geq\frac{r_{2}-r_{1}}{2}\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{r_{2}-r_{1}}{2\sigma^{n-1}}{\mathcal{F}}\right).

Thus, to establish the validity of (
3.13
), it remains to note that

\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{r_{2}-r_{1}}{2\sigma^{n-1}}{\mathcal{F}}\right)\geq C\varphi^{-1}((r_{2}-r_{1}){\mathcal{F}})

in accordance with property (
1.7
) of almost semi-multiplicative functions.

We put

v(x)=\tilde{w}(x)+\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\limits_{B_{r_{2}}}\,(\tilde{u}-\tilde{w}),

where

\tilde{w}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &w(|x|),&&x\in B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}},\\
&0,&&x\in B_{r_{1}}.\end{aligned}\right.

It can easily be see that

v\geq\tilde{u}\quad\mbox{a.e. in }B_{r_{2}}.

(3.14)

Really, we have

\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\limits_{B_{r_{2}}}\,(\tilde{u}-\tilde{w})\geq\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{w}(x)

for almost all
x\in B_{r_{2}}
. Hence,

v(x)=\tilde{w}(x)+\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\limits_{B_{r_{2}}}\,(\tilde{u}-\tilde{w})\geq\tilde{w}(x)+\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{w}(x)=\tilde{u}(x)

for almost all
x\in B_{r_{2}}
.
Since
\tilde{w}
is a non-negative function, it is also obvious that

v(x)\geq\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\limits_{B_{r_{2}}}\,(\tilde{u}-\tilde{w})\geq\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\limits_{B_{r_{1}}}\,(\tilde{u}-\tilde{w})=M(r_{1})

(3.15)

for all
x\in B_{r_{2}}
.

Let us establish the validity of the equality

\left.v\right|_{S_{r_{2}}}=M(r_{2}).

(3.16)

Indeed, taking into account (
3.14
), we obtain

\left.v\right|_{S_{r_{2}}}=\sup_{B_{r_{2}}}v\geq\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\limits_{B_{r_{2}}}\tilde{u}=M(r_{2});

therefore, if (
3.16
) is not valid, then

\left.(v-\varepsilon)\right|_{S_{r_{2}}}>M(r_{2})

for some real number
0<\varepsilon<M(r_{1})/2
. In view
of (
3.12
), the function
\tilde{v}=v-\varepsilon
is a
weak solution of the equation

\Delta_{\varphi}\tilde{v}=\chi_{B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}}}(x){\mathcal{F}}\quad\mbox{in }B_{r_{2}},

where

\chi_{B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}}}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&&x\in B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}},\\
&0,&&x\not\in B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}},\end{aligned}\right.

is the characteristic function of the set
B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}}
.
At the same time, according to (
3.15
), we have

\tilde{v}(x)\geq M(r_{1})-\varepsilon>M(r_{1})/2

for all
x\in B_{r_{2}}
.
Thus,
\tilde{v}
is a weak solution of the equation

\Delta_{\varphi}\tilde{v}=\rho(x,\tilde{v})\quad\mbox{in }B_{r_{2}}

and, applying Lemma
3.1
, we arrive at the inequality

\tilde{u}\leq\tilde{v}\quad\mbox{a.e. in }B_{r_{2}}

or, in other words,

\tilde{u}(x)\leq\tilde{w}(x)+\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\limits_{B_{r_{2}}}\,(\tilde{u}-\tilde{w})-\varepsilon

for almost all
x\in B_{r_{2}}
,
whence it follows that

\tilde{u}(x)-\tilde{w}(x)\leq\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\limits_{B_{r_{2}}}\,(\tilde{u}-\tilde{w})-\varepsilon

for almost all
x\in B_{r_{2}}
.
This contradiction proves (
3.16
).

Since

\left.v\right|_{S_{r_{1}}}=\sup_{B_{r_{1}}}v\geq\operatorname*{ess\,sup}\limits_{B_{r_{1}}}\tilde{u}=M(r_{1})

in accordance with (
3.14
), equality (
3.16
) yields

M(r_{2})-M(r_{1})\geq\left.v\right|_{S_{r_{2}}}-\left.v\right|_{S_{r_{1}}}=w(r_{2}).

Combining this with (
3.13
), we complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
r_{*}\leq r_{1}<r_{2}
be real numbers such that
r_{2}\leq\sigma r_{1}
and
M(r_{2})\leq 2M(r_{1})
. Then,

\frac{M(r_{2})-M(r_{1})}{\varphi^{-1}(g(\tau))}\geq\frac{C\eta(r_{2}-r_{1})}{\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{f(r)}\right)}

(3.17)

for all
r\in[r_{2}/\sigma,\sigma r_{1}]
and
\tau\in[M(r_{1})/2,2M(r_{2})]
.

Proof.

Take some
r\in[r_{2}/\sigma,\sigma r_{1}]
and
\tau\in[M(r_{1})/2,2M(r_{2})]
. From (
1.8
), it follows that

F(x,t)\geq f(r)g(t)

(3.18)

for all

x\in\Omega\cap B_{r_{2}}\setminus B_{r_{1}}

and

t\in[M(r_{1})/2,M(r_{2})]
.
Putting
\tau_{1}=\tau
and
\tau_{2}=t/\tau
in (
1.7
), we have

g(t)\geq{Cg(\tau)}/{g(\tau/t)}.

Since the function
g
is bounded from above on any compact subset
of the interval
(0,\infty)
and, moreover,

\tau/t\in[1/4,8]
for all
t\in[M(r_{1})/2,M(r_{2})]
,
the last inequality yields

g(t)\geq Cg(\tau)

for all
t\in[M(r_{1})/2,M(r_{2})]
.
Thus, (
3.18
) implies the estimate

{\mathcal{F}}\geq Cf(r)g(\tau),

where
{\mathcal{F}}
is given by (
3.11
).
Combining this with formula (
3.10
) of Lemma
3.3
, we obtain

M(r_{2})-M(r_{1})\geq C(r_{2}-r_{1})\varphi^{-1}((r_{2}-r_{1})f(r)g(\tau)).

(3.19)

As
\varphi^{-1}
is an almost semi-multiplicative function, one can assert that

\varphi^{-1}((r_{2}-r_{1})f(r)g(\tau))\geq\frac{C\varphi^{-1}(g(\tau))}{\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{(r_{2}-r_{1})f(r)}\right)}

and

\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{(r_{2}-r_{1})f(r)}\right)\leq C\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\right)\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{f(r)}\right).

Thus, (
3.19
) leads to the inequality

M(r_{2})-M(r_{1})\geq\frac{C(r_{2}-r_{1})\varphi^{-1}(g(\tau))}{\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\right)\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{f(r)}\right)}

which is obviously equivalent to (
3.17
).
∎

The following statement is simple but useful.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
a>0
and
b>0
be real numbers. Then,

\eta(a)b\geq C\eta\left(\frac{a}{\eta^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{b}\right)}\right).

(3.20)

Proof.

Taking into account the relation

\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}\right)\geq\frac{C\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)}{\varphi^{-1}(\beta)},

we obtain

\eta(\alpha\beta)=\frac{\alpha\beta}{\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}\right)}\leq\frac{C\alpha\beta\varphi^{-1}(\beta)}{\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)}

(3.21)

for all real numbers
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
.
Let us denote

h(t)=t\varphi^{-1}(t),\quad t>0,

It can be verified that

h(t)=\frac{1}{\eta\left({1}/{t}\right)},\quad t>0.

We also note that
h
is an increasing one-to-one mapping of the
set
(0,\infty)
into itself with

h^{-1}(t)=\frac{1}{\eta^{-1}\left({1}/{t}\right)},\quad t>0.

Formula (
3.21
) can obviously be written in the form

\eta(\alpha\beta)\leq C\eta(\alpha)h(\beta),

whence, putting
\alpha=a
and
\beta=h^{-1}(b)
, we
immediately arrive at (
3.20
).
∎

3.4

is an

for all

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
r_{*}\leq r_{1}<r_{2}
be real numbers such that
r_{2}=\sigma^{1/2}r_{1}
and
M(r_{2})\geq 2M(r_{1})
. Then,

\int_{M(r_{1})}^{M(r_{2})}\eta^{-1}\left(\frac{t}{\varphi^{-1}(g(t))}\right)\frac{dt}{t}\geq C\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\Phi(r)\frac{dr}{r},

(3.22)

where

\Phi(r)=\sup_{s\in(\sigma^{-1/2}r,\,r\sigma^{1/2})}\,\frac{s}{\eta^{-1}\left(\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{f(s)}\right)\right)}.

(3.23)

Proof.

s_{1}>s_{2}>\ldots>s_{k}

s_{1}=r_{2}
. Assume further
that
s_{i}
is already known for some positive integer
i
. If
s_{i}=r_{1}
, we put
k=i
and stop; otherwise we take

s_{i+1}=\inf\{r\in[r_{1},s_{i}]:M(s_{i})\leq 2M(r)\}.

It is obvious that

M(s_{i})\leq 2M(s_{i+1}+0),\quad i=1,2,\ldots,k-1.

Since
M(r)=M(r-0)
for all
r\in(r_{1},r_{2}]
, we also have

2M(s_{i+1})\leq M(s_{i}),\quad i=1,2,\ldots,k-2.

(3.24)

By Lemma
3.4
,

\frac{M(s_{i})-M(s_{i+1}+0)}{\varphi^{-1}(g(\tau))}\geq\frac{C\eta(s_{i}-s_{i+1})}{\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{f(r)}\right)}

for all
r\in(s_{i}/\sigma,\sigma s_{i+1})
and
\tau\in(M(s_{i+1}+0)/2,2M(s_{i}))
,

s=1,2,\ldots,k-1
.
At the same time, taking into account Lemma
3.5
, we obtain

\frac{\eta(s_{i}-s_{i+1})}{\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{f(r)}\right)}\geq C\eta\left(\frac{s_{i}-s_{i+1}}{\eta^{-1}\left(\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{f(r)}\right)\right)}\right);

therefore, one can assert that

\frac{M(s_{i})-M(s_{i+1}+0)}{\varphi^{-1}(g(\tau))}\geq C\eta\left(\frac{s_{i}-s_{i+1}}{\eta^{-1}\left(\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{f(r)}\right)\right)}\right),

for all
r\in(s_{i}/\sigma,\sigma s_{i+1})
and
\tau\in(M(s_{i+1}+0)/2,2M(s_{i}))
,
s=1,2,\ldots,k-1
.
By (
1.10
) and the fact that
\eta^{-1}
is an increasing
function, this implies the inequality

\eta^{-1}\left(\frac{M(s_{i})-M(s_{i+1}+0)}{\varphi^{-1}(g(\tau))}\right)\geq\frac{C(s_{i}-s_{i+1})}{\eta^{-1}\left(\varphi^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{f(r)}\right)\right)}

r\in(s_{i}/\sigma,\sigma s_{i+1})
and
\tau\in(M(s_{i+1}+0)/2,2M(s_{i}))
,

, whence it

\eta^{-1}\left(\frac{M(s_{i})}{2\varphi^{-1}(g(\tau))}\right)\geq C(s_{i}-s_{i+1})\frac{\Phi(r)}{r}

(3.25)

for all
r\in(s_{i+1},s_{i})
and
\tau\in(M(s_{i+1}+0)/2,2M(s_{i}))
,

s=1,2,\ldots,k-1
.

Combining (
3.25
) with the evident inequalities

\int_{M(s_{i})/2}^{M(s_{i})}\eta^{-1}\left(\frac{t}{\varphi^{-1}(g(t))}\right)\frac{dt}{t}\geq\ln 2\inf_{\tau\in(M(s_{i})/2,M(s_{i}))}\eta^{-1}\left(\frac{M(s_{i})}{2\varphi^{-1}(g(\tau))}\right)

and

(3.26)

we arrive at the estimate

\int_{M(s_{i+1})}^{M(s_{i})}\eta^{-1}\left(\frac{t}{\varphi^{-1}(g(t))}\right)\frac{dt}{t}\geq C\int_{s_{i+1}}^{s_{i}}\Phi(r)\frac{dr}{r},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,k-2.

Summing the last expression over all

, we obtain

\int_{M(s_{k-1})}^{M(r_{2})}\eta^{-1}\left(\frac{t}{\varphi^{-1}(g(t))}\right)\frac{dt}{t}\geq C\int_{s_{k-1}}^{r_{2}}\Phi(r)\frac{dr}{r}.

and

.

obviously exists.

therefore, taking into account (
3.25
) and (
3.26
)
with

, we have

Finally, summing the last inequality and (
3.27
), we arrive
at (
3.22
).

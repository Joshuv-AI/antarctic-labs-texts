Abstract
. Ramanujan, in his lost notebook, gave an interesting identity, which generates infinite families of solutions to Euler’s Diophantine equation A 3 + B 3 = C 3 + D 3 A^{3}+B^{3}=C^{3}+D^{3} . In this paper, we produce a few infinite families of solutions to the aforementioned Diophantine equation as well as for the Diophantine equation A 4 + B 4 + C 4 + D 4 + E 4 = F 4 A^{4}+B^{4}+C^{4}+D^{4}+E^{4}=F^{4} in the spirit of Ramanujan.



1.
Introduction

Littlewood once remarked that
“Every natural number was one of Ramanujan’s personal friends”.

1729
is one such special number which was dear to Ramanujan. This number is popularly known as Ramanujan taxicab number or Hardy-Ramanujan number due to the following incident.

The story begins in 1918, when Ramanujan was admitted to Matlock Sanitorium in Derbyshire. Hardy went to see him and communicated the following story about his visit:

I remember once going to him when he was lying ill at Putney. I had ridden in taxi cab number 1729 and remarked that the number seemed to me rather a dull one, and that I hoped it was not an unfavourable omen. No, he replied, it is a very interesting number; it is the smallest number expressible as the sum of two cubes in two different ways.

One can easily verify that
1729
is the smallest natural number to be represented as sum of two cubes in two different ways i.e.,
1729=1^{3}+12^{3}=9^{3}+10^{3}
. Once we know this special property of
1729
, this prompts one to think are there more numbers satisfying this property? This is analogous to solving the Diophantine equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle A^{3}+B^{3}=C^{3}+D^{3},

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose we have the following power series:

3.
Proof of Main Results

Let
(p,q,r,s)
be a known solution to
A^{3}+B^{3}+C^{3}=D^{3}
. Then consider a straight line passing through
(p,q,r,s)
as

\displaystyle\frac{A-p}{a}=\frac{B-q}{b}=\frac{C-r}{-a}=\frac{D-s}{b}=\theta.

This gives

(3.1)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=a\theta+p,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=b\theta+q,

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=-a\theta+r,

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=b\theta+s.

Substituting (
3.1
) in

, we get

(3.2)

\displaystyle\theta=-\frac{a(p^{2}-r^{2})+b(q^{2}-s^{2})}{a^{2}(p+r)+b^{2}(q-s)}.

Plugging the value (
3.2
) of
\theta

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=a^{2}r(p+r)-ab(q^{2}-s^{2})-b^{2}p(s-q),

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=a^{2}q(p+r)-ab(p^{2}-r^{2})+b^{2}s(s-q),

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=a^{2}p(p+r)+ab(q^{2}-s^{2})-b^{2}r(s-q),

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=a^{2}s(p+r)-ab(p^{2}-r^{2})+b^{2}q(s-q).

This is the most general solution. Now, we make a change of variable

\displaystyle a\rightarrow\frac{a}{\sqrt{p+r}},~~~b\rightarrow\frac{b}{\sqrt{s-q}}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=a^{2}r+ab(s+q)\sqrt{\frac{s-q}{p+r}}-b^{2}p,

\displaystyle=-ab(p^{2}-s^{2})+b^{2}t(q+t)-bc(r^{2}-u^{2})+a^{2}q(p+s)c^{2}q(r-u),

\displaystyle=a^{2}s+b^{2}p+c^{2}p-ab(q-t)\sqrt{\frac{q+t}{p+s}}-ac(r+u)\sqrt{\frac{r-u}{p+s}},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=a^{2}q+b^{2}t+c^{2}q-ab(p-s)\sqrt{\frac{p+s}{q+t}}-bc(r+u)\sqrt{\frac{r-u}{q+t}},

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=a^{2}r+b^{2}r-c^{2}u-ac(p-s)\sqrt{\frac{p+s}{r-u}}-bc(q-t)\sqrt{\frac{q+t}{r-u}},

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=a^{2}p+b^{2}s+c^{2}s+ac(r+u)\sqrt{\frac{r-u}{p+s}}+ab(q-t)\sqrt{\frac{q+t}{p+s}},

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=a^{2}t+b^{2}q+c^{2}t+ab(p-s)\sqrt{\frac{p+s}{q+t}}+bc(r+u)\sqrt{\frac{r-u}{q+t}},

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=a^{2}u+b^{2}u-c^{2}r-ac(p-s)\sqrt{\frac{p+s}{r-u}}-bc(q-t)\sqrt{\frac{q+t}{r-u}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

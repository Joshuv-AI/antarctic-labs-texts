Abstract
We study the Morse-Smale property for the following scalar semilinear parabolic equation on the circle S 1 S^{1} , u t = u x ​ x + f ⁡ ( t , u , u x ) , t > 0 , x ∈ S 1 = ℝ / 2 ​ π ​ ℤ , u_{t}=u_{xx}+f(t,u,u_{x}),\,\,t>0,\,x\in S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}, where f f is a C 2 C^{2} function and T T -periodic in t t . Introduction

The study of the structural stability of differential equations can be traced back to the work of Poincaré on the three-body problem in celestial mechanics and was first appeared in Andronov and Pontrjagin
[
1
]
. Later, the structural stability was extensively studied in the frame of vector fields on compact smooth manifolds of finite dimensions. A system or equation is structurally stable means that the qualitative behavior of the trajectories can persistent by small perturbations(to be exact
C^{1}
-small perturbations). In 1960(see
[
34
]
), Smale proposed the concept of Morse-Smale system: (1) the non-wandering set consists only in a finite number of hyperbolic equilibria and hyperbolic orbits; (2) the stable and unstable manifolds of all the connecting hyperbolic fixed points and periodic orbits are all intersect transversely. The Morse-Smale system on any finite dimensional compact manifold is structurally stable was proved by Palis and Smale
[
26
,
27
,
36
]
. Particularly, for a 2-dimensional compact manifold, Morse-Smale property is established for generic
C^{1}
-vector fields; under some dissipative assumptions, the generic Morse-Smale property is also correct for vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Nevertheless, Morse-Smale vector fields no longer dense for a general 3-dimensional system. In fact, the existence of transverse homoclinic connecting hyperbolic orbits lead to chaotic behaviors(see
[
35
]
).

As for infinite systems that generated by partial differential equations, results related to structural stability and local stability are more recent. Similar to the finite dimensional case of vector fields on compact manifold or on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one can also define Morse-Smale property for infinite systems. It is proved that Morse-Smale systems generated by disspative parabolic equations or more generally by disspative in finite time smoothing equations are sturctural stable
(see
[
25
,
15
]
). To our best knowledge, the first relevant result on the structural stability of parabolic equations should belong to Henry
[
17
]
for the following equation

u_{t}=u_{xx}+f(x,u,u_{x}),\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times(0,1),

with Dirichlet, Neumann or Robin boundary conditions. It was proved in
[
17
]
(see also Angenent
[
3
]
) that the stable and unstable manifolds of two connecting hyperbolic fixed points of the above equation intersect transversely; and this result combine with already proved gradient structure in
[
39
]
imply that the generic Morse-Smale property of such equation. After then, Chen, et al. in
[
8
]
obtained the Morse-Smale property for
f=f(t,x,u,u_{x})
of
t
is periodic under the assumption that all the fixed points of the corresponding Poincaré map are hyperbolic. It is worth pointing out that scalar semilinear parabolic equation defined on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, is no longer gradient in general. Nonetheless, for special nonlinear term
f=f(x,u)
, the system is also a gradient system, and hence, Morse-Smale property for some special equations on bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
established(see
[
6
]
). For general
f=f(x,u,\nabla u)
, the generic tranversality of heteroclinic and homoclinic orbits was proved in the recent work of Brunovský, et al in
[
5
]
.

As it mentioned before, Morse-Smale system is an important class of structural stable system. In spite of this fact, there are not many results that can be used to verify if Morse-Smale properties holds for a given dynamical system, especially for that high-dimensional or infinite-dimensional systems. In this situation, it is necessary and important to select some typical systems to study their Morse-Smale properties. Here, we focus on the following typical infinite-dimensional system

\begin{cases}u_{t}=u_{xx}+f(t,u,u_{x}),\quad t>0,x\in S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z},\\

We have some comments in the following.

Firstly, our result is not a simple extension for autonomous systems in
[

,
18
]
to periodic systems. In fact, the main difficulty to prove the transversality of invariant manifolds between two hyperbolic fixed points is to construct suitable spaces such that the spaces can span the full space. In autonomous case, for hyperbolic periodic orbit, the whole orbit is both belong to the local stable manifolds and local unstable manifolds of itself, which is very important for the proving of transversality for connecting hyperbolic equilibrium elements(hyperbolic equilibrium or hyperbolic periodic orbit). While in our periodic case, this property no longer holds true, as we need to consider the transversality on Poincaré section. The proof of Theorem

Secondly, although the methods in

Thirdly, the recent characterization of non-wandering points in

Fourth, the zero number function plays an essential role in characterization the global stability and longtime behavior of (

20

21

37
,

In Section 3, we study the long time behavior of solutions of linear aysmptotic periodic parabolic equations on the circle. In Section 4, we present our main results and give detailed proof, we first exclude the existence of homoclinic connecting hyperbolic periodic orbits; and then prove the transversality of stable and unstable manifolds between heteroclinic connecting hyperbolic periodic orbits; finally obtain the Morse-Smale property under the assumption that all

Let

\displaystyle v_{t}=v_{xx}+\bar{c}(t,x)v_{x}+\bar{d}(t,x)v,

\displaystyle v(0)=v_{0},

(2.8b)

\bar{d}(t,x)=\partial f_{2}(t,u(t,x;u_{0}),u_{x}(t,x;u_{0})).

Let

be the solution operator of (

), then for each

,

. In the following, we give an equivalent characterization about tangent spaces of stable and unstable manifolds for hyperbolic fixed points.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume
\varphi
is a hyperbolic fixed point of

and

are

immersed submanifolds of

(i)

at

(ii)

for any
u_{0}\in W^{u}(\varphi)
, the tangent space of
W^{u}(\varphi)
at
u_{0}
is given by

\displaystyle T_{u_{0}}W^{u}(\varphi)=\{v_{0}\in X^{\alpha}|

\displaystyle v_{0}\quad\mbox{has a backward continuation for \eqref{tranver-line-eq} and}

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|S(-nT,0;u_{0})v_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}_{X^{\alpha}}<1\}.

Note that the Fréchet derivative

, the proof of Lemma

. To prove Lemma
2.3

Lemma 2.4
.

The map

and

Proof.

Let

be two solutions of equation (

) with

. Suppose

, that is,

. Let

, then

satisfies the following equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}+Av=G(t,u)(x),\quad t>0,x\in S^{1},\\
&v(0)=u_{0}-u_{1},\end{aligned}\right.

where
G(t,u)(x)=F(t,u(t,x;u_{0}))-F(t,u(t,x;u_{1}))=c_{1}(t,x)v_{x}+d_{1}(t,x)v,

c_{1}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{1}\partial f_{3}(t,su(t,x;u_{0})+(1-s)u(t,x;u_{1}),su_{x}(t,x;u_{0})+(1-s)u_{x}(t,x;u_{1}))ds,

\displaystyle\|G(t,u)\|_{X^{0}}

\displaystyle=(\int_{S^{1}}|c_{1}(t,x)v_{x}+d_{1}(t,x)v|^{2}dx)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\leq L\parallel v\parallel_{X^{\frac{1}{2}}},\quad t\in[0,\infty),

particularly,

,

Consider the following two linear equations

\displaystyle v_{t}=v_{xx}+c(t,x)v_{x}+d(t,x)v,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times S^{1},

(3.1a)

\displaystyle v(0)=v_{0},

(3.1b)

and

\displaystyle v^{p}_{t}=v^{p}_{xx}+\tilde{c}(t,x)v^{p}_{x}+\tilde{d}(t,x)v^{p},\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times S^{1},

(3.2a)

\displaystyle v^{p}(0)=v^{p}_{0},

(3.2b)

Assume that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\pm\infty}\|c-\tilde{c}\|_{L^{\infty}([nT,(n+1)T]\times S^{1})}+\|d-\tilde{d}\|_{L^{\infty}([nT,(n+1)T]\times S^{1})}=0,

3.2

Let
S(t,0):X^{\alpha}\rightarrow X^{\alpha}
and

be solution operators of (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), then

,

are solutions of (
3.1
) and (

) respectively. For simplicity, write

The following lemma tells us that
S_{p}
and

can be arbitrarily close as

.

Lemma 3.1
.

S_{p}
has the following relationship with

in

,

(i)
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|S((n+1)T,nT)-S_{p}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X^{\alpha})}=0
,

(ii)
\lim_{n\rightarrow-\infty}\|S((n+1)T,nT)-S_{p}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X^{\alpha})}=0

Proof.

Consider the following abstract equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v^{n}_{s}+Av^{n}=h^{n}(s)v^{n},\quad 0<s\leq T,\quad x\in S^{1},\\
&v^{n}(0)=v^{n}_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v^{p}_{s}+Av^{p}=h^{p}(s)v^{p},\quad 0<s\leq T,\quad x\in S^{1},\\
&v^{p}(0)=v^{p}_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.4)

where
Au=-u_{xx}
,
h^{n}(s)v^{n}=c(nT+s,x)v^{n}_{x}+d(nT+s,x)v^{n},

h^{p}(s)=\tilde{c}(s,x)v^{p}_{x}+\tilde{d}(s,x)v^{p}
. It is not hard to see that
h^{n}(s),h^{p}(s)\in\mathcal{L}(X^{\alpha},X^{0})
are Hölder continuous in
s
. By standard theory for analytic semigroups(see
[
16
, Theorem 3.3.3]
), equations (
3.3
) and (

) admit classical solutions in

.

Let
v^{n}_{0}=v^{p}_{0}=\psi\in X^{\alpha}
, then
v^{n}(s,\cdot;\psi)=S(nT+s,nT)\psi
,
v^{p}(s,\cdot;\psi)=S_{p}(s,0)\psi
respectively. Note that
L^{\infty}(S^{1})\hookrightarrow X^{0},
and
X^{\alpha}\hookrightarrow C^{1}(S^{1})
, there exists
C_{\alpha}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in(0,T]}\|h^{n}(s)-h^{p}(s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X^{\alpha},X^{0})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\in(0,T]}\frac{\|c(nT+s,\cdot)\psi_{x}+d(nT+s,\cdot)\psi-\tilde{c}(s,\cdot)\psi_{x}-\tilde{d}(s,\cdot)\psi\|_{X^{0}}}{\|\psi\|_{X^{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{s\in(0,T]}\frac{\|c(nT+s,\cdot)-\tilde{c}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{1})}\|\psi_{x}\|_{X^{0}}+\|d(nT+s,\cdot)-\tilde{d}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{1})}\|\psi\|_{X^{0}}}{\|\psi\|_{X^{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\sup_{s\in(0,T]}[\|c(nT+s,\cdot)-\tilde{c}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{1})}+\|d(nT+s,\cdot)-\tilde{d}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{1})}].

By asymptotic periodicity, one has

\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|h^{n}(s)-h^{p}(s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X^{\alpha},X^{0})}\to 0,\quad n\to\infty.

(3.5)

By standard theory of analytic semigroups, we know that

\|A^{\alpha}e^{-At}\|\leq M_{1}t^{-\alpha},

where

A
and
\alpha

depends on

. It then follows from the Gronwall’s inequality (see Exercise 4* in

where

is a constant depending on

. By the variation of constants formula, we have

\displaystyle v^{n}(s,x;\psi)-v^{p}(s,x;\psi)=\int_{0}^{s}

\displaystyle e^{-A(s-\xi)}\{(h^{n}(\xi)-h^{p}(\xi))v^{p}(\xi,x;\psi)

\displaystyle+h^{n}(\xi)(v^{n}(\xi,x;\psi)-v^{p}(\xi,x;\psi))\}d\xi.

In view of (

\begin{split}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|h^{n}(s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X^{\alpha},X^{0})}\leq M_{p}+1,\quad n\gg 1,\\
\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|h^{n}(s)-h^{p}(s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X^{\alpha},X^{0})}<\epsilon,\quad n\gg 1.\end{split}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|v^{n}(s,\cdot;\psi)-v^{p}(s,\cdot;\psi)\|_{X^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{s}\frac{M_{1}}{(s-\xi)^{\alpha}}\{\parallel(h^{n}(\xi,\cdot)-h^{p}(\xi,\cdot))\parallel_{\mathcal{L}(X^{\alpha},X^{0})}\parallel v^{p}(\xi,\cdot;\psi)\parallel_{X^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle+\parallel h^{n}(\xi,\cdot)\parallel_{\mathcal{L}(X^{\alpha},X^{0})}\parallel(v^{n}(\xi,\cdot;\psi)-v^{p}(\xi,\cdot;\psi))\parallel_{X^{\alpha}}\}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M_{1}M_{4}s^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\varepsilon\parallel\psi\parallel_{X^{\alpha}}+{M_{1}}(M_{p}+1)\int_{0}^{s}(s-\xi)^{-\alpha}\parallel v^{n}(\xi,\cdot;\psi)-v^{p}(\xi,\cdot;\psi)\parallel_{X^{\alpha}}d\xi,\quad n\gg 1.

Again by the Gronwall’s inequality,

\parallel v^{n}(s,\cdot;\psi)-v^{p}(s,\cdot;\psi)\parallel_{X^{\alpha}}\leq M_{5}\varepsilon\parallel\psi\parallel_{X^{\alpha}},\quad n\gg 1,

where
M_{5}
is a constant depending on
\alpha,T,M_{1},M_{4},M_{p}
.

Thus

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|S((n+1)T,nT)-S_{p}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X^{\alpha})}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\|\psi\|_{X^{\alpha}}=1}\|v^{n}(s,\cdot;\psi)-v^{p}(s,\cdot;\psi)\|_{X^{\alpha}}=0.

Similarly, replacing

with

\lim_{n\rightarrow-\infty}\|S((n+1)T,nT)-S_{p}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X^{\alpha})}=0.

∎

Wijesekera-style constructive modal logics
Tiziano Dalmonte

Abstract
We define a family of propositional constructive modal logics corresponding each to a different classical modal system. The logics are defined in the style of Wijesekera’s constructive modal logic [ 38 ] , and are both proof-theoretically and semantically motivated. On the one hand, they correspond to the single-succedent restriction of standard sequent calculi for classical modal logics. On the other hand, they are obtained by incorporating the hereditariness of intuitionistic Kripke models into the classical satisfaction clauses for modal formulas. We show that, for the considered classical logics, the proof-theoretical and the semantical approach return the same constructive systems.



keywords

Constructive modal logic, intuitionistic modal logic, sequent calculus, neighbourhood semantics.

resp. with axiom
{D}
, resp. with axioms
{T_{\Box}}
and
{T_{\Diamond}}
.
As usual,
we say that
A
is a theorem of
\mathsf{W^{*}}
, written
\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash A
, if there is a finite sequence of formulas
ending with
A
in which every formula is an axiom of
\mathsf{W^{*}}
, or it is obtained from previous formulas by
the application
of a rule
of
\mathsf{W^{*}}
.
Moreover,
we say that
A
is deducible in
\mathsf{W^{*}}
from a set of formulas
\Sigma
,
written

\Sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{W^{*}}}A
, if there is a finite set
\{B_{1},...,B_{n}\}\subseteq\Sigma
such that

\vdash_{\mathsf{W^{*}}}B_{1}\land...\land B_{n}\supset A
.
Furthermore, given two axiomatic systems
\mathsf{L_{1}}
and
\mathsf{L_{2}}
,
we say that
\mathsf{L_{1}}
is included in
\mathsf{L_{2}}
if
\mathsf{L_{1}}\vdash A
entails

\mathsf{L_{2}}\vdash A
for all
A\in\mathcal{L}
,
and that
\mathsf{L_{1}}
and
\mathsf{L_{2}}
are equivalent if they derive exactly the same theorems.

Concerning W-logics specifically, note
that
{C_{\Diamond}}
is not an axiom of
\mathsf{WMC}
because it is not derivable in
\mathsf{S.{WMC}}
,

{C_{\Diamond}}
must be replaced by
{K_{\Diamond}}
which is instead derivable in the calculus.
Note also that
{P_{\Diamond}}
must be included in the axiomatisation of
\mathsf{WMD}
and
\mathsf{WMCD}

as it is not derivable from
{D}
in the intuitionistic systems.
The relations among the W-logics are displayed in Fig.
6
.

We prove some basic results about W-logics.

Proposition 3.5
.

(i)

\mathsf{WM}\vdash\textup{${Rdual_{\land}}$}
.

(ii)

\mathsf{WMN}\vdash\textup{${nec}$}
.

(iii)

\mathsf{WMN}\vdash\textup{${N_{\Diamond}}$}
.

(iv)

\mathsf{WMC}\vdash\textup{${K_{\Box}}$}
.

(v)

\mathsf{WMP}\vdash\textup{${P_{\Box}}$}
.

(vi)

\mathsf{WMND}\vdash\textup{${P_{\Diamond}}$}
.

Proof 3.6
.

(i) From
\neg(A\land B)
, by
\mathsf{IPL}
we obtain
B\supset\neg A
, then by
{mon_{\Diamond}}
,
\Diamond B\supset\Diamond\neg A
,
thus
\neg\Diamond\neg A\supset\neg\Diamond B
.
Moreover from
{dual_{\land}}
we have
\Box A\supset\neg\Diamond\neg A
,
thus
\Box A\supset\neg\Diamond B
, therefore
\neg(\Box A\land\Diamond B)
.
(ii) From
A
, by
\mathsf{IPL}
we have
\top\supset A
, then by
{mon_{\Box}}
,
\Box\top\supset\Box A
,
then by
{N_{\Box}}
,
\Box A
.
(iii) By
{Rdual_{\land}}
,
\Box\top\supset\neg\Diamond\bot
,
then by
{N_{\Box}}
,
\neg\Diamond\bot
.
(iv) By
{C_{\Box}}
,
\Box(A\supset B)\land\Box A\supset\Box((A\supset B)\land A)
,
and by
{mon_{\Box}}
,
\Box((A\supset B)\land A)\supset\Box B
,
thus by
\mathsf{IPL}
,
\Box(A\supset B)\supset(\Box A\supset\Box B)
.
(v) By
{dual_{\land}}
,
\Diamond\top\supset\neg\Box\bot
, then by
{P_{\Diamond}}
,
\neg\Box\bot
.
(vi) By
{D}
,
\Box\top\supset\Diamond\top
, then by
{N_{\Box}}
,
\Diamond\top
.

We recall that Wijesekera’s original axiomatisation of
\mathsf{WK}

[
38
]
was given by

\mathsf{IPL}+\textup{${nec}$}+\textup{${K_{\Box}}$}+\textup{${K_{\Diamond}}$}+\textup{${N_{\Diamond}}$}
.
It is easy to verify that
{dual_{\land}}
,
{mon_{\Box}}
,
{mon_{\Diamond}}
,
{C_{\Box}}
, and
{N_{\Box}}
are all derivable
in Wijesekera’s original system.
Then from Proposition
3.5

it follows in particular that the axiomatisation of
\mathsf{WK}

considered here
is equivalent to Wijesekera’s one.

We now prove that the systems
\mathsf{W^{*}}
are equivalent to the corresponding calculi.

Theorem 3.7
.

\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\vdash\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
if and only if
\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash\and\Gamma\supset\bigvee\Delta
.

Proof 3.8
.

From right to left, it is easy to see that all modal axioms are derivable in the corresponding calculi
(cf. derivations in Fig.
5
),
observing that initial sequents
\mathsf{init}
can be generalised as usual to arbitrary formulas
A
.
For
{mon_{\Box}}
, from
\Rightarrow A\supset B
and
A\supset B,A\Rightarrow B
, by
\mathsf{cut}
(which has been proved admissible)
we obtain
A\Rightarrow B
,
then by
\mathsf{M_{\Box}^{i}}
,
\Box A\Rightarrow\Box B
, and by
\mathsf{\supset^{i}_{R}}
,
\Rightarrow\Box A\supset\Box B
.

{mon_{\Diamond}}
is derived similarly.
The derivations of the intuitionistic axioms are standard, moreover modus ponens is simulated by
\mathsf{cut}
in the usual way.

For the other direction,
we can consider standard derivations of the propositional rules.
Here we show that for every modal sequent rule of
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}

with premiss
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
and conclusion
\Gamma^{\prime}\Rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}
,
the Hilbert-style
rule

\and\Gamma\supset\bigvee\Delta
/
\and\Gamma^{\prime}\supset\bigvee\Delta^{\prime}

is derivable
in the corresponding system
\mathsf{W^{*}}
. We only consider some relevant examples,
the other derivations are similar.

(
\mathsf{C_{\Box}^{i}}
)

From
\and\Gamma\land A\supset B
, by
{mon_{\Box}}
we get
\Box(\and\Gamma\land A)\supset\Box B
,
moreover by
{C_{\Box}}
,
\and\Box\Gamma\land\Box A\supset\Box(\and\Gamma\land A)
,
then
\and\Gamma^{\prime}\land\and\Box\Gamma\land\Box A\supset\Box B
.

(
\mathsf{C_{\Diamond}^{i}}
)

From
\and\Gamma\land A\supset B
, we get
\and\Gamma\supset(A\supset B)
, then by
{mon_{\Box}}
,

\Box\and\Gamma\supset\Box(A\supset B)
.
By
{C_{\Box}}
we have
\and\Box\Gamma\supset\Box(A\supset B)
, then by
{K_{\Diamond}}
,

\and\Box\Gamma\supset(\Diamond A\supset\Diamond B)
,
thus
\and\Gamma^{\prime}\land\and\Box\Gamma\land\Diamond A\supset\Diamond B
.

(
\mathsf{\land\textup{-}dual_{C}^{i}}
)

From
\and\Gamma\land A\land B\supset\bot
, we get
\and\Gamma\land A\supset\neg B
,
then by
{mon_{\Box}}
,
\Box(\and\Gamma\land A)\supset\Box\neg B
,
and by
{C_{\Box}}
,
\and\Box\Gamma\land\Box A\supset\Box\neg B
.
Moreover by
{dual_{\land}}
,
\Box\neg B\supset\neg\Diamond B
,
thus
\and\Gamma^{\prime}\land\and\Box\Gamma\land\Box A\land\Diamond B\supset C
for any
C
.

(
\mathsf{CD^{i}}
)

If
\Gamma\not=\emptyset
, then from
\and\Gamma\supset B
, by
{mon_{\Box}}
we get
\Box\and\Gamma\supset\Box B
,
then by
{C_{\Box}}
,
\and\Box\Gamma\supset\Box B
, and by
{D}
,
\and\Box\Gamma\supset\Diamond B
,
thus
\and\Gamma^{\prime}\land\and\Box\Gamma\supset\Diamond B
.
If
\Gamma=\emptyset
, then from
B
we get
\top\supset B
, then
by
{mon_{\Diamond}}
,
\Diamond\top\supset\Diamond B
, and by
{P_{\Diamond}}
,
\Diamond B
,
thus
\and\Gamma^{\prime}\supset\Diamond B
.

(
\mathsf{CD_{\Box}^{i}}
)

From
\and\Gamma\supset\bot
we get
\and\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\supset\neg B
for some

B\in\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}=\Gamma\setminus B
.
Then by
{mon_{\Diamond}}
,
\Diamond\and\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\supset\Diamond\neg B
,
thus by
{D}
,
\Box\and\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\supset\Diamond\neg B
,
by
{C_{\Box}}
,
\and\Box\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\supset\Diamond\neg B
,
and by
{dual_{\land}}
,
\and\Box\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\supset\neg\Box B
,
thus
\and\Gamma^{\prime}\land\and\Box\Gamma\supset C
for any
C
.

3.2
Some properties of W-logics

Cut-free sequent calculi are a very powerful tool for the analysis of logical systems.
In this subsection, we present some fundamental properties of W-logics
that easily follow from those of their sequent calculi.

We start considering the following result, which establishes
how classical and Wijesekera-style modal logics are related
from the point of view of the axiomatic systems.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathsf{L}
be any classical modal logic from Sec.
2
,
and
\mathsf{WL}
be the corresponding W-logic.
Then
\mathsf{L}
is equivalent to
\mathsf{WL}+A\lor\neg A+\Box A\lor\Diamond\neg A
.

Proof 3.10
.

From left to right, it is easy to verify that all axioms of
\mathsf{WL}
, as well as

A\lor\neg A
and
\Box A\lor\Diamond\neg A
, are derivable in
\mathsf{L}
.
For the opposite direction, adding
A\lor\neg A
one derives as usual all theorems of
\mathsf{CPL}
,
while adding
\Box A\lor\Diamond\neg A
one derives
{dual}
.

Note that
\mathsf{WL}+A\lor\neg A+\Box A\lor\Diamond\neg A
is a proper extension of

\mathsf{WL}
, as it is stated by the following proposition.

Proposition 3.11
.

For every W-logic
\mathsf{W^{*}}
,
\mathsf{W^{*}}\not\vdash p\lor\neg p
and
\mathsf{W^{*}}\not\vdash\Box p\lor\Diamond\neg p
.

Proof 3.12
.

If
\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash p\lor\neg p
, then
by Theorem
3.7
,
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\vdash\ \Rightarrow p\lor\neg p
.
The only rule of
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}
with a consequence of the form
\Rightarrow p\lor\neg p
is
\mathsf{\lor^{i}_{R}}
,
thus
\mathsf{\lor^{i}_{R}}
must be the last rule applied in the derivation, with premiss either
\Rightarrow p
or
\Rightarrow\neg p
.
However, by inspecting the rules of
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}
it is easy to verify that

\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\not\vdash\ \Rightarrow p
and
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\not\vdash\ \Rightarrow\neg p
,
therefore
\mathsf{W^{*}}\not\vdash p\lor\neg p
.

\mathsf{W^{*}}\not\vdash\Box p\lor\Diamond\neg p

is proved similarly, observing that

\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\not\vdash\ \Rightarrow\Box p
and
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\not\vdash\ \Rightarrow\Diamond\neg p
.

In a similar way we can prove that W-logics
satisfy the disjunction property.

Proposition 3.13

(Disjunction property)
.

For all W-logics
\mathsf{W^{*}}
and all formulas
A
,
B
of
\mathcal{L}
,
if
\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash A\lor B
, then
\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash A
or
\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash B
.

Proof 3.14
.

If
\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash A\lor B
, then
by Theorem
3.7
,
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\vdash\ \Rightarrow A\lor B
.
The only rule of
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}
with a consequence of the form
\Rightarrow A\lor B
is
\mathsf{\lor^{i}_{R}}
,
thus
\mathsf{\lor^{i}_{R}}
must be the last rule applied in the derivation, with premiss either
\Rightarrow A
or
\Rightarrow B
.
Then
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\vdash\ \Rightarrow A
or
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\vdash\ \Rightarrow B
,
therefore
\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash A
or
\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash B
.

Furthermore, we can prove that derivability in W-logics is decidable.
To this aim, observe that for every rule
R
of
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}
,
the premisses of
R
have a smaller complexity than its conclusion,
with the only exceptions of
\mathsf{\supset_{L}}
and
\mathsf{T_{\Box}^{i}}
which copy the principal formula into one premiss.
It follows that
bottom-up proof search in
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}
is not strictly terminating,
however,
similarly to
[
37
, Ch. 4]
,
termination can be gained
by controlling the applications of
\mathsf{\supset_{L}}
and
\mathsf{T_{\Box}^{i}}
with a simple loop-checking in order
to avoid redundant applications of these rules,
preserving at the same time the completeness of the calculi.
Adopting this restriction,
it turns out that every proof tree for a root sequent
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
is finite,
moreover there are only finitely many distinct proof trees for it.
Then, given the equivalence between
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}
and
\mathsf{W^{*}}
, it follows that
derivability of
A
in
\mathsf{W^{*}}
is decidable for any
A
:
the decision procedure trivially consists in checking all possible derivations of
\Rightarrow A
in
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}
.

Theorem 3.15

(Decidability)
.

Given a W-logic
\mathsf{W^{*}}
and a formula
A
of
\mathcal{L}
,
it is decidable whether
A
is derivable in
\mathsf{W^{*}}
.

Finally, we can prove that all W-logics enjoy Craig interpolation.
For every formula
A
of
\mathcal{L}
and every multiset
\Gamma=B_{1},...,B_{n}
,
we define
var(A)=\{\bot\}\cup\{p\in Atm\mid p\textup{ occurs in }A\}
,
and
var(\Gamma)=var(B_{1})\cup...\cup var(B_{n})
.
Then Craig interpolation
amounts to the following property.

Definition 3.16
.

A logic
\mathsf{W^{*}}
enjoys
Craig interpolation

if for all
A,B\in\mathcal{L}
,
if
\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash A\supset B
, then there is
C\in\mathcal{L}
such that

\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash A\supset C
,

\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash C\supset B
,
and
var(C)\subseteq var(A)\cap var(B)
.

The proof of Craig interpolation is based on the following lemma.

Lemma 3.17
.

For every calculus
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}
, if
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\vdash\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\Rightarrow\Delta
,
then there is
C\in\mathcal{L}
such that

\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\vdash\Gamma_{1}\Rightarrow C
,

\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\vdash C,\Gamma_{2}\Rightarrow\Delta
,
and
var(C)\subseteq var(\Gamma_{1})\cap var(\Gamma_{2},\Delta)
.

Proof 3.18
.

By induction on the height
h
of the derivation of
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\Rightarrow\Delta
,
taking into account the last rule applied in the derivation.
If
h=0
or the last rule applied is propositional we refer to
[
30
]
.
Here we consider just one significant case involving a modal rule,
for the other rules the proof is analogous.

Let
\mathsf{C_{\Diamond}^{i}}
be the last rule applied in the derivation.
Then
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\Rightarrow\Delta
has the form
\Gamma_{1}^{\prime},\Box\Gamma,\Diamond A,\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}\Rightarrow\Diamond B

and it is obtained from the premiss
\Gamma,A\Rightarrow B
.
There are four possible partitions of
\Gamma_{1}^{\prime},\Box\Gamma,\Diamond A,\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}
into
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
.

(i)

\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{1}^{\prime}
and
\Gamma_{2}=\Box\Gamma,\Diamond A,\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}
.
Then
\top
is an interpolant:
\Gamma_{1}^{\prime}\Rightarrow\top
is derivable, and from
\Gamma,A\Rightarrow B
, by
\mathsf{C_{\Diamond}^{i}}
we obtain
\top,\Box\Gamma,\Diamond A,\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}\Rightarrow\Diamond B
.

(ii)

\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{1}^{\prime},\Box\Gamma,\Diamond A
and
\Gamma_{2}=\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}
.
By i.h., there is
C
such that
\Gamma,A\Rightarrow C
and
C\Rightarrow B
are derivable, and

var(C)\subseteq var(\Gamma,A)\cap var(B)
.
Then by
\mathsf{C_{\Diamond}^{i}}
we obtain
\Gamma_{1}^{\prime},\Box\Gamma,\Diamond A\Rightarrow\Diamond C
and
\Diamond C,\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}\Rightarrow\Diamond B
.
Since
var(\Diamond C)=var(C)
,
\Diamond C
is an interpolant.

The following two partitions are possible if
\Gamma=D_{1},...,D_{n}
and
n\geq 2
.

(iii)

\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{1}^{\prime},\Box D_{1},...,\Box D_{k}
and
\Gamma_{2}=\Box D_{k+1},...,\Box D_{n},\Diamond A,\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}

(for
1\leq k<n
).
By i.h., there is
C
such that
D_{1},...,D_{k}\Rightarrow C
and
C,D_{k+1},...,D_{n},A\Rightarrow B
are derivable, and

var(C)\subseteq var(D_{1},...,D_{k})\cap var(D_{k+1},...,D_{n},A,B)
.
Then by
\mathsf{C_{\Box}^{i}}
,
\Gamma_{1}^{\prime},\Box D_{1},...,\Box D_{k}\Rightarrow\Box C
is derivable,
and by
\mathsf{C_{\Diamond}^{i}}
,
\Box C,\Box D_{k+1},...,\Box D_{n},\Diamond A,\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}\Rightarrow\Diamond B
is derivable.
Then
\Box C
is an interpolant.

(iv)

\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{1}^{\prime},\Box D_{1},...,\Box D_{k},\Diamond A
and
\Gamma_{2}=\Box D_{k+1},...,\Box D_{n},\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}

(for
1\leq k<n
).
By i.h., there is
C
such that
D_{1},...,D_{k},A\Rightarrow C
and
C,D_{k+1},...,D_{n}\Rightarrow B
are derivable, and

var(C)\subseteq var(D_{1},...,D_{k},A)\cap var(D_{k+1},...,D_{n},B)
.
Then by
\mathsf{C_{\Diamond}^{i}}
,
\Gamma_{1}^{\prime},\Box D_{1},...,\Box D_{k},\Diamond A\Rightarrow\Diamond C
and

\Diamond C,\Box D_{k+1},...,\Box D_{n},\Gamma_{2}^{\prime}\Rightarrow\Diamond B
are derivable.
Then
\Diamond C
is an interpolant.

Theorem 3.19
.

Every W-logic
\mathsf{W^{*}}
enjoys Craig interpolation.

Proof 3.20
.

Suppose that
\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash A\supset B
.
Then
\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\vdash A\Rightarrow B
.
By Lemma
3.17
,
there is
C\in\mathcal{L}
such that

var(C)\subseteq var(A)\cap var(B)
,

\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\vdash A\Rightarrow C
, and

\mathsf{S.{W^{*}}}\vdash C\Rightarrow B
,
thus

\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash A\supset C
and

\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash C\supset B
.

4
Semantics

We now define constructive neighbourhood models (CNMs) that characterise the constructive modal logics defined in Sec.
3
.
CNMs
are defined analogously to Wijesekera’s relational models
[
38
]
:
we enrich intuitionistic Kripke models with a neighbourhood function
(rather than a binary relation as in Wijesekera’s models),
moreover we generalise the classical satisfaction clauses for modal formulas to all
\leq
-successors,
so that hereditariness is built into the clauses:
in order that
w
satisfies
\Box A
,
we require that
for all successors of
w
there is a neighbourhood
\alpha
such that
\alpha\Vdash^{\forall}A
,
and similarly for
\Diamond A
.
We show that, for every classical logic characterised by neighbourhood models
satisfying some conditions from Def.
2.2
,
the corresponding W-logic is characterised by the CNMs
satisfying exactly the same conditions.
CNMs are defined as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

A
constructive neighbourhood model
(CNM) is a tuple
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{W},\leq,\mathcal{N},\mathcal{V}\rangle
,
where

\mathcal{W}
is a non-empty set of worlds,

\leq
is a preorder on
\mathcal{W}
,

\mathcal{N}:\mathcal{W}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{W}))
is a neighbourhood function, and

\mathcal{V}:Atm\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{W})
is a hereditary valuation function
(i.e., if
w\in\mathcal{V}(p)
and
w\leq v
, then
v\in\mathcal{V}(p)
).
The forcing relation
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash A
is defined as in Def.
2.2
for

A=p,\bot,B\land C,B\lor C
, otherwise it is as follows:

\mathcal{M},w\Vdash B\supset C

iff

for all
v\geq w
,
\mathcal{M},v\Vdash B
implies
\mathcal{M},v\Vdash C
.

\mathcal{M},w\Vdash\Box B

iff

for all
v\geq w
, there is
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(v)
such that
\alpha\Vdash^{\forall}B
.

\mathcal{M},w\Vdash\Diamond B

iff

for all
v\geq w
, for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(v)
,
\alpha\Vdash^{\exists}B
.

We consider the following properties on CNMs:

(C) If
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{N}(w)
, then
\alpha\cap\beta\in\mathcal{N}(w)
.

(N)
\mathcal{N}(w)\not=\emptyset
.

(D) If
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{N}(w)
, then
\alpha\cap\beta\not=\emptyset
.

(P)
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{N}(w)
.

(T) If
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(w)
, then
w\in\alpha
.

We say that
\mathcal{M}
is a model for a logic
\mathsf{W^{*}}
, or is a
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-model,
if for every modal axiom
{X}
of
\mathsf{W^{*}}

(among
{C_{\Box}}
,
{N_{\Box}}
,
{T_{\Box}}
,
{D}
,
{P_{\Diamond}}
),

\mathcal{M}
satisfies the corresponding condition (X).

CNMs represent the simplest way of combining intuitionistic Kripke models and neighbourhood models.
From the definition of
\mathcal{V}
and of the satisfaction clauses, it immediately follows that
CNMs enjoy the hereditary property.

Proposition 4.2

(Hereditary property)
.

For all
A\in\mathcal{L}
and all CNMs
\mathcal{M}
, if
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash A
and
w\leq v
, then
\mathcal{M},v\Vdash A
.

Proof 4.3
.

By induction on the construction of
A
.
We only consider the inductive cases
A=\Box B,\Diamond B
as the other cases are standard.
(
A=\Box B
) If
w\Vdash\Box B
, then for all
u\geq w
, there is
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(u)
such that
\alpha\Vdash^{\forall}B
,
then for all
u\geq v
, there is
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(u)
such that
\alpha\Vdash^{\forall}B
, thus
v\Vdash\Box B
.
(
A=\Diamond B
) If
w\Vdash\Diamond B
, then for all
u\geq w
, for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(u)
,
\alpha\Vdash^{\exists}B
,
then for all
u\geq v
, for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(u)
,
\alpha\Vdash^{\exists}B
, thus
v\Vdash\Diamond B
.

We show that, for any classical modal logic characterised by neighbourhood models
satisfying some conditions among (C), (N), (T), (D), (P),
the corresponding W-logic is characterised by the CNMs satisfying the same conditions.
We first show that W-logics are sound with respect to their classes of CNMs,
then prove their completeness by a canonical model construction.

Theorem 4.4

(Soundness)
.

For every W-logic
\mathsf{W^{*}}
,
if
\mathsf{W^{*}}\vdash A
, then
\mathcal{M}\models A

for all
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-models
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof 4.5
.

As usual, we need to show that the axioms of
\mathsf{W^{*}}
are valid in all
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-models,
and that the rules of
\mathsf{W^{*}}
preserve the validity in
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-models.

(
\mathsf{M_{\Box}}
)

Assume that
\mathcal{M}\models A\supset B
and
w\Vdash\Box A
.
Then for all
v\geq w
, there is
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(v)
such that
\alpha\Vdash^{\forall}A
.
If follows that
\alpha\Vdash^{\forall}B
. Therefore
w\Vdash\Box B
.

(
\mathsf{M_{\Diamond}}
)

Assume that
\mathcal{M}\models A\supset B
and
w\Vdash\Diamond A
.
Then for all
v\geq w
, for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(v)
,
\alpha\Vdash^{\exists}A
.
If follows that
\alpha\Vdash^{\exists}B
. Therefore
w\Vdash\Diamond B
.

(
{dual_{\land}}
)

Assume that
w\Vdash\Box A\land\Diamond\neg A
.
Then for all
v\geq w
, there is
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(v)
such that
\alpha\Vdash^{\forall}A
, and for all
\beta\in\mathcal{N}(v)
,
\beta\Vdash^{\exists}\neg A
.
Thus there is
\gamma\in\mathcal{N}(w)
such that
\gamma\Vdash^{\exists}A\land\neg A
, which is impossible.
Therefore
\mathcal{M}\models\neg(\Box A\land\Diamond\neg A)
.

(
{C_{\Box}}
)

Assume that
\mathcal{M}
satisfies condition (C) and
w\Vdash\Box A\land\Box B
.
Then for all
v\geq w
, there is
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(v)
such that
\alpha\Vdash^{\forall}A
,
and there is
\beta\in\mathcal{N}(v)
such that
\beta\Vdash^{\forall}B
.
By (C),
\alpha\cap\beta\in\mathcal{N}(v)
, moreover
\alpha\cap\beta\Vdash^{\forall}A\land B
.
Thus
w\Vdash\Box(A\land B)
.

(
{K_{\Diamond}}
)

Assume by contradiction that
\mathcal{M}
satisfies (C),
w\Vdash\Box(A\supset B)
,
w\Vdash\Diamond A

and
w\not\Vdash\Diamond B
.
By
w\not\Vdash\Diamond B
, there are
v\geq w
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(v)
such that
\alpha\not\Vdash^{\exists}B
.
Then by
w\Vdash\Box(A\supset B)
, there is
\beta\in\mathcal{N}(v)
such that
\beta\Vdash^{\forall}A\supset B
.
Thus by (C),
\alpha\cap\beta\in\mathcal{N}(v)
.
It follows
\alpha\cap\beta\not\Vdash^{\exists}B
and
\alpha\cap\beta\Vdash^{\forall}A\supset B
.
However by
w\Vdash\Diamond A
,
\alpha\cap\beta\Vdash^{\exists}A
,
thus
\alpha\cap\beta\Vdash^{\exists}B
, which gives a contradiction.

(
{N_{\Box}}
)

If
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the condition (N), then for all
w
there is
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(w)
. Moreover,
\alpha\Vdash^{\forall}\top
,
therefore
\mathcal{M}\models\Box\top
.

(
{P_{\Diamond}}
)

If
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the condition (P), then for all
w
and
all
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(v)
,
\alpha\not=\emptyset
, thus
\alpha\Vdash^{\exists}\top
. Therefore
\mathcal{M}\models\Diamond\top
.

(
{D}
)

Assume by contradiction that
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the condition
(D),
w\Vdash\Box A
, and
w\not\Vdash\Diamond A
.
Then there are
v\geq w
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(v)
such that
\alpha\not\Vdash^{\exists}A
.
Moreover, there is
\beta\in\mathcal{N}(v)
such that
\beta\Vdash^{\forall}A
.
By (D), there is
u\in\alpha\cap\beta
. Thus
u\not\Vdash A
and
u\Vdash A
.
Therefore
w\Vdash\Diamond A
.

(
{T_{\Box}}
,
{T_{\Diamond}}
)

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the condition (T). Then if
w\Vdash\Box A
,
there is
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(w)
such that
\alpha\Vdash^{\forall}A
. By (T),
w\in\alpha
, thus
w\Vdash A
.
Moreover, if
w\Vdash A
, then by Prop.
4.2
,
v\Vdash A
for all
v\geq w
.
By (T),
v\in\alpha
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{N}(v)
, thus
\alpha\Vdash^{\exists}A
.
Therefore
w\Vdash\Diamond A
.

4.1
Completeness

We now prove that W-logics are complete with respect to the corresponding classes of CNMs.
As usual, for every logic
\mathsf{W^{*}}
, we call
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-
prime
any set
\Sigma
of formulas of
\mathcal{L}

such that

\Sigma\not\vdash_{\mathsf{W^{*}}}\bot
(consistency), if
\Sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{W^{*}}}A
, then
A\in\Sigma
(closure under derivation), and
if
A\lor B\in\Sigma
, then
A\in\Sigma
or
B\in\Sigma
(disjunction property).
Moreover, for every set of formulas
\Sigma
,
we denote
\Box^{-}\Sigma
the set
\{A\mid\Box A\in\Sigma\}
.
One can prove in a standard way the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6

(Lindenbaum)
.

If
\Sigma\not\vdash_{\mathsf{W^{*}}}A
, then there is a
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-prime set
\Pi
such that
\Sigma\subseteq\Pi
and
A\notin\Pi
.

We also consider the following notion of segment
(we adopt the terminology of
[
38
]
),
and prove the subsequent lemma that will be needed in the following.

Definition 4.7
.

For every logic
\mathsf{W^{*}}
,
a
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-
segment
is a pair
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})
,
where
\Sigma
is a
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-prime set, and
\mathscr{C}
is a class of sets of
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-prime sets such that:

•

if
\Box A\in\Sigma
, then there is
\mathscr{U}\in\mathscr{C}
such that for all
\Pi\in\mathscr{U}
,
A\in\Pi
; and

•

if
\Diamond A\in\Sigma
, then for all
\mathscr{U}\in\mathscr{C}
, there is
\Pi\in\mathscr{U}
such that
A\in\Pi
.

\mathsf{WC^{*}}
-,
\mathsf{WD^{*}}
- and
\mathsf{WT^{*}}
-segments must satisfy also the following conditions:
(
\mathsf{WC^{*}}
) If
\mathscr{U},\mathscr{U}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}
, then
\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{U}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}
.
(
\mathsf{WD^{*}}
) If
\mathscr{U},\mathscr{U}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}
, then
\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{U}^{\prime}\not=\emptyset
.
(
\mathsf{WT^{*}}
) For all
\mathscr{U}\in\mathscr{C}
,
\Sigma\in\mathscr{U}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

For every
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-prime set
\Sigma
, there exists a
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-segment
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})
.

Proof 4.9
.

Given a
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-prime set
\Sigma
, we construct a
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-segment
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})
as follows.
If there is no
\Box A\in\Sigma
, we put
\mathscr{C}=\emptyset
.
If there is no
\Diamond A\in\Sigma
, we put
\mathscr{C}=\{\emptyset\}
.
Otherwise we distinguish two cases.

(i)

\mathsf{W^{*}}
does not contain
{C_{\Box}}
,
{K_{\Diamond}}
.
Let
\Box A,\Diamond B\in\Sigma
. Then
A,B\not\vdash_{\mathsf{W^{*}}}\bot

(otherwise by
{Rdual_{\land}}
,
\Box A,\Diamond B\vdash_{\mathsf{W^{*}}}\bot
, against the consistency of
\Sigma
).
Then by Lemma
4.6
, there is
\Sigma^{\prime}_{AB}

\mathsf{W^{*}}
-prime such that
A,B\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{AB}
.
For all
\Box A\in\Sigma
,
we define
\mathscr{U}_{A}=\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{AB}\mid\Diamond B\in\Sigma\}

if
\mathsf{W^{*}}
does not contain
{T_{\Box}}
, and

\mathscr{U}_{A}=\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{AB}\mid\Diamond B\in\Sigma\}\cup\{\Sigma\}
if it contains
{T_{\Box}}
. Moreover we define
\mathscr{C}=\{\mathscr{U}_{A}\mid\Box A\in\Sigma\}
.
Then
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})
is a
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-segment:
if
\Box A\in\Sigma
, then
\mathscr{U}_{A}\in\mathscr{C}
and for all
\Sigma^{\prime}\in\mathscr{U}_{A}
,
A\in\Sigma^{\prime}
.
If
\Diamond B\in\Sigma
, then for all
\mathscr{U}_{A}\in\mathscr{C}
, there is
\Sigma^{\prime}_{AB}\in\mathscr{U}_{A}
such that
B\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{AB}
.
Moreover for
\mathsf{WD^{*}}
, if
\mathscr{U}_{A},\mathscr{U}_{B}\in\mathscr{C}
,
\mathscr{U}_{A}\not=\mathscr{U}_{B}
,
then
\Box A,\Box B\in\Sigma
,
then by axiom
{D}
,
\Diamond A,\Diamond B\in\Sigma
,
thus
\Sigma^{\prime}_{AB}\in\mathscr{U}_{A}\cap\mathscr{U}_{B}
.

(ii)

\mathsf{W^{*}}
contains
{C_{\Box}}
,
{K_{\Diamond}}
.
Let
\Diamond B\in\Sigma
. Then
\Box^{-}\Sigma\cup\{B\}\not\vdash_{\mathsf{W^{*}}}\bot

(otherwise by
{Rdual_{\land}}
and
{C_{\Box}}
,
\Sigma\vdash_{\mathsf{W^{*}}}\bot
).
Then by Lemma
4.6
, there is
\Sigma^{\prime}_{B}

\mathsf{W^{*}}
-prime such that
\Box^{-}\Sigma\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}_{B}
and
B\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{B}
.
We define
\mathscr{U}=\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{B}\mid\Diamond B\in\Sigma\}

if
\mathsf{W^{*}}
does not contain
{T_{\Box}}
, and

\mathscr{U}=\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{B}\mid\Diamond B\in\Sigma\}\cup\{\Sigma\}
if it contains
{T_{\Box}}
. Moreover we define
\mathscr{C}=\{\mathscr{U}\}
.
It is easy to verify that
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})
is a
\mathsf{WC^{*}}
-segment.

We consider the following definition of canonical model.

Definition 4.10
.

For every logic
\mathsf{W^{*}}
,
the
canonical model
for
\mathsf{W^{*}}
is the tuple
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{W},\leq,\mathcal{N},\mathcal{V}\rangle
,
where:

•

\mathcal{W}
is the class of all
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-segments;

•

(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\leq(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
if and only if
\Sigma\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}
;

•

for every set
\mathscr{U}
of
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-prime sets,
\alpha_{\mathscr{U}}=\{(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\mid\Sigma\in\mathscr{U}\}
;

•

\alpha_{\mathscr{U}}\in\mathcal{N}((\Sigma,\mathscr{C}))
if and only if
\mathscr{U}\in\mathscr{C}
;

•

(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\in\mathcal{V}(p)
if and only if
p\in\Sigma
.

We prove the following two lemmas which entail completeness of
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-logics.

Lemma 4.11
.

The canonical model for
\mathsf{W^{*}}
is a CNM for
\mathsf{W^{*}}
.

Proof 4.12
.

We show that the canonical model for
\mathsf{W^{*}}
satisfies the conditions of CNMs for
\mathsf{W^{*}}
.
(C), (D), (T) and hereditariness of
\mathcal{V}
are immediate by
Defs.
4.7
and
4.10
.
(N)
For all
\mathsf{WN^{*}}
-prime sets
\Sigma
,
\Box\top\in\Sigma
,
then for all
\mathsf{WN^{*}}
-segments
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})
,
\mathscr{C}\not=\emptyset
,
thus
\mathcal{N}((\Sigma,\mathscr{C}))\not=\emptyset
.
(P)
For all
\mathsf{WP^{*}}
-prime sets
\Sigma
,
\Diamond\top\in\Sigma
,
then for all
\mathsf{WP^{*}}
-segments
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})
, for all
\mathscr{U}\in\mathscr{C}
,
\mathscr{U}\not=\emptyset
,
thus
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{N}((\Sigma,\mathscr{C}))
.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
\mathsf{W^{*}}
be a W-logic, and
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{W},\leq,\mathcal{N},\mathcal{V}\rangle

be the canonical model for
\mathsf{W^{*}}
. Then for
all
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\in\mathcal{W}
,

(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\Vdash A
if and only if
A\in\Sigma
.

Proof 4.14
.

By induction on the construction of
A
.
If
A=p
or
A=\bot
, the proof is immediate by definition of
\mathcal{V}
or by consistency of
\Sigma
,
moreover for
A=B\land C,B\lor C
the proof is immediate by applying the inductive hypothesis.
We consider the
remaining
cases.

\bullet

A=B\supset C
:
If
B\supset C\in\Sigma
, then suppose
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\leq(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
and
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\Vdash B
.
Then
\Sigma\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}
, thus
B\supset C\in\Sigma^{\prime}
.
Moreover by i.h.,
B\in\Sigma^{\prime}
, then
C\in\Sigma^{\prime}
, thus by i.h.,
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\Vdash C
.
Therefore
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\Vdash B\supset C
.
If instead
B\supset C\notin\Sigma
, then
\Sigma\not\vdash B\supset C
, thus
\Sigma\cup\{B\}\not\vdash C
.
By Lemma
4.6
, there is
\Sigma^{\prime}

\mathsf{W^{*}}
-prime such that
\Sigma\cup\{B\}\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}
and
C\notin\Sigma^{\prime}
.
Then by Lemma
4.8
and Def.
4.10
, there is a
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-segment
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{W}
.
By definition,
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\leq(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
, and by i.h.,
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\Vdash B
and
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\not\Vdash C
.
Therefore
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\not\Vdash B\supset C
.

\bullet

A=\Box B
:
If
\Box B\in\Sigma
, then for all
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\geq(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})
,
\Box B\in\Sigma^{\prime}
.
By definition of segment,
there is
\mathscr{U}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
such that for all
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}\in\mathscr{U}^{\prime}
,
B\in\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
.
Then
\alpha_{\mathscr{U^{\prime}}}\in\mathcal{N}((\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime}))
, moreover by i.h.,
(\Sigma^{\prime\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime})\Vdash B
for all
(\Sigma^{\prime\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime})\in\alpha_{\mathscr{U^{\prime}}}
.
Therefore
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\Vdash\Box B
.
Now suppose that
\Box B\notin\Sigma
.
If there is no
\Box C\in\Sigma
, then
(\Sigma,\emptyset)
is a
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-segment,
moreover
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\leq(\Sigma,\emptyset)
and
\mathcal{N}((\Sigma,\emptyset))=\emptyset
,
thus
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\not\Vdash\Box B
.
If instead there is
\Box C\in\Sigma
,
we distinguish two cases:

(i)

\mathsf{W^{*}}
does not contain
{C_{\Box}}
,
{K_{\Diamond}}
.
Then for all
\Box D\in\Sigma
,
D\not\vdash B

(otherwise by
{mon_{\Box}}
,
\Box D\vdash\Box B
, whence
\Box B\in\Sigma
).
Then there is
\Sigma^{\prime}_{D}

\mathsf{W^{*}}
-prime such that
D\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{D}
and
B\notin\Sigma^{\prime}_{D}
.
Moreover, for all
\Diamond C\in\Sigma
,
C,D\not\vdash\bot

(otherwise by
{Rdual_{\land}}
,
\Diamond C,\Box D\vdash\bot
, whence
\bot\in\Sigma
).
Then there is
\Sigma^{\prime}_{CD}

\mathsf{W^{*}}
-prime such that
C,D\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{CD}
.
For all
\Box D\in\Sigma
, we define

\mathscr{U}^{\prime}_{D}=\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{D}\}\cup\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{CD}\mid\Diamond C\in\Sigma\}

if
\mathsf{W^{*}}
does not contain
{T_{\Box}}
,
and
\mathscr{U}^{\prime}_{D}=\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{D}\}\cup\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{CD}\mid\Diamond C\in\Sigma\}\cup\{\Sigma\}
if it contains
{T_{\Box}}
. Moreover, we define

\mathscr{C}^{\prime}=\{\mathscr{U}^{\prime}_{D}\mid\Box D\in\Sigma\}
.
It is easy to verify that
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
is a
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-segment.
Moreover, for all
\mathscr{U}^{\prime}_{D}\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
,
\Sigma^{\prime}_{D}\in\mathscr{U}^{\prime}_{D}
and
B\notin\Sigma^{\prime}_{D}
,
thus by i.h.,
(\Sigma^{\prime}_{D},\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime})\not\Vdash B
for any
(\Sigma^{\prime}_{D},\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{W}
.
It follows that for all
\alpha_{\mathscr{U}}\in\mathcal{N}((\Sigma,\mathscr{C}^{\prime}))
,
\alpha_{\mathscr{U}}\not\Vdash^{\forall}B
.
Thus
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\not\Vdash\Box B
, and since
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\leq(\Sigma,\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
,

(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\not\Vdash\Box B
.

(ii)

\mathsf{W^{*}}
contains
{C_{\Box}}
,
{K_{\Diamond}}
.
Then
\Box^{-}\Sigma\not\vdash B

(otherwise by
{mon_{\Box}}
and
{C_{\Box}}
,

\Sigma\vdash\Box B
),
then there is
\Sigma^{\prime}

\mathsf{WC^{*}}
-prime such that
\Box^{-}\Sigma\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}
and
B\notin\Sigma^{\prime}
.
Moreover, for all
\Diamond C\in\Sigma
,
\Box^{-}\Sigma\cup\{C\}\not\vdash\bot
,
then there is
\Sigma^{\prime}_{C}

\mathsf{WC^{*}}
-prime such that
\Box^{-}\Sigma\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}_{C}
and
C\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{C}
.
We define

\mathscr{U}^{\prime}=\{\Sigma^{\prime}\}\cup\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{C}\mid\Diamond C\in\Sigma\}

if
\mathsf{WC^{*}}
does not contain
{T_{\Box}}
,
and
\mathscr{U}^{\prime}=\{\Sigma^{\prime}\}\cup\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{C}\mid\Diamond C\in\Sigma\}\cup\{\Sigma\}
if it contains
{T_{\Box}}
. Moreover, we define

\mathscr{C}^{\prime}=\{\mathscr{U}^{\prime}\}
.
It is easy to verify that
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
is a
\mathsf{WC^{*}}
-segment.
Moreover, since
B\notin\Sigma^{\prime}
,
by i.h.,
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime})\not\Vdash B
for any
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{W}
,
it follows that for all
\alpha_{\mathscr{U}}\in\mathcal{N}((\Sigma,\mathscr{C}^{\prime}))
,
\alpha_{\mathscr{U}}\not\Vdash^{\forall}B
.
Thus
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\not\Vdash\Box B
, and since
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\leq(\Sigma,\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
,

(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\not\Vdash\Box B
.

\bullet

A=\Diamond B
:
If
\Diamond B\in\Sigma
, then for all
(\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\geq(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})
,
\Diamond B\in\Sigma^{\prime}
.
By definition of segment, for all
\mathscr{U}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, there is
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}\in\mathscr{U}^{\prime}
such that
B\in\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
.
Then for all
\alpha_{\mathscr{U^{\prime}}}\in\mathcal{N}((\Sigma^{\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime}))
, there is
(\Sigma^{\prime\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime})\in\alpha_{\mathscr{U^{\prime}}}
such that
B\in\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
,
thus by i.h.,
(\Sigma^{\prime\prime},\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime})\Vdash B
.
It follows that
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\Vdash\Diamond B
.
Now suppose that
\Diamond B\notin\Sigma
.
If there is no
\Diamond C\in\Sigma
, then
(\Sigma,\{\emptyset\})
is a
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-segment,
moreover
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\leq(\Sigma,\{\emptyset\})
and
\mathcal{N}((\Sigma,\{\emptyset\}))=\{\emptyset\}
,
thus
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C})\not\Vdash\Diamond B
.
If instead there is
\Diamond C\in\Sigma
,
we distinguish two cases:

(i)

\mathsf{W^{*}}
does not contain
{C_{\Box}}
,
{K_{\Diamond}}
.
Then for all
\Diamond C\in\Sigma
,
C\not\vdash B

(otherwise by
{mon_{\Diamond}}
,
\Diamond C\vdash\Diamond B
, whence
\Diamond B\in\Sigma
).
Then there is
\Sigma^{\prime}_{C}

\mathsf{W^{*}}
-prime such that
C\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{C}
and
B\notin\Sigma^{\prime}_{C}
.
Moreover, for all
\Box D\in\Sigma
,
C,D\not\vdash\bot

(otherwise by
{Rdual_{\land}}
,
\Diamond C,\Box D\vdash\bot
, whence
\bot\in\Sigma
).
Then there is
\Sigma^{\prime}_{CD}

\mathsf{W^{*}}
-prime such that
C,D\in\Sigma^{\prime}_{CD}
.
If in addition
\mathsf{W^{*}}
does not contain
{T_{\Box}}
, we define

\mathscr{U}^{\prime}=\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{C}\mid\Diamond C\in\Sigma\}
, and
for all
\Box D\in\Sigma
,
\mathscr{U}^{\prime}_{D}=\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{CD}\mid\Diamond C\in\Sigma\}
;
otherwise we define

\mathscr{U}^{\prime}=\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{C}\mid\Diamond C\in\Sigma\}\cup\{\Sigma\}
, and
for all
\Box D\in\Sigma
,
\mathscr{U}^{\prime}_{D}=\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{CD}\mid\Diamond C\in\Sigma\}\cup\{\Sigma\}
.
Moreover, we define

\mathscr{C}^{\prime}=\{\mathscr{U}^{\prime}\}\cup\{\mathscr{U}^{\prime}_{D}\mid\Box D\in\Sigma\}
.
It is easy to verify that
(\Sigma,\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
is a
\mathsf{W^{*}}
-segment:
for instance for
\mathsf{WD^{*}}
,
if
\mathscr{U}^{\prime}_{D},\mathscr{U}^{\prime}_{E}\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, then
\mathscr{U}^{\prime}_{D}\cap\mathscr{U}^{\prime}_{E}\not=\emptyset

(cf. proof of Lemma
4.8
), moreover
by
{P_{\Diamond}}
,
\Diamond\top\in\Sigma

5
Discussion and future work

In this paper, we have defined a family of 14 constructive modal logics
both proof-theoretically and semantically motivated,
corresponding each to a different classical modal logic.
On the one hand, the logics correspond to the single-succedent restriction
of standard sequent calculi for classical modal logics.
On the other hand, the same logics are obtained
by considering over intuitionistic Kripke models a natural generalisation of
the classical satisfaction clauses for modal formulas in the neighbourhood semantics.
The main result of this paper is that,
despite being mutually independent,
for the considered logics
the two approaches return exactly the same systems.

In addition, we have provided some preliminary analysis of W-logics.
First, we have shown how W-logics
are related to
the corresponding classical modal logics from the point of view of the axiomatic systems:
each classical modal logic considered in this paper can be obtained by extending the corresponding W-logic
with both excluded middle
A\lor\neg A
and disjunctive duality
\Box A\lor\Diamond\neg A
.
Moreover, basing on their sequent calculi we have proved some fundamental properties of W-logics, such as the disjunction property, decidability and Craig interpolation.

Simpson
[
35
, Ch. 3]
listed some
requirements that one
expects to be satisfied by
any intuitionistic modal logic:
they must be conservative over
\mathsf{IPL}
;
they must contain all axioms of
\mathsf{IPL}
(over the whole language) and be closed under modus ponens;
they must satisfy the disjunction property;
the modalities must be independent;
the addition of the axiom
A\lor\neg A
must yield a standard classical modal logic.
Basing on the results presented in this paper,
it is easy to verify that
all W-logics satisfy the first four requirements, by contrast they do not satisfy the last one.
5
5

5

This requirement has been sometimes criticised as being too strong,
see
[
22
]
for an argument against this requirement
based on negative translations.

However,
it comes natural to ask whether there could be some modal principle,
additional to excluded middle,
that distinguishes between constructive and classical modalities.
As a matter of fact,
it is easy to identify such a principle for W-logics:
as we observed above this principle
is precisely

\Box A\lor\Diamond\neg A
.

This relation between classical and W-logics is not entirely trivial.
For instance, the same relation
does not hold between
\mathsf{CK}
and
\mathsf{K}
, in particular
\mathsf{CK}

must be
extended also with
\neg(\Box A\land\Diamond\neg A)
(or equivalently with
\neg\Diamond\bot
)
in order to obtain classical
\mathsf{K}
.
Moreover,
we believe that failure of
\Box A\lor\Diamond\neg A
is justifiable
from a constructive perspective,
as it can be seen as a modalised form of excluded middle.

Concerning the semantics of W-logics, the choice of considering neighbourhood models is motivated by the possibility to uniformly cover all considered logics,
which include both normal and non-normal systems.
However,
\mathsf{WK}
,
\mathsf{WKD}
and
\mathsf{WKT}
have
an equivalent characterisation in terms of constructive bi-relational models
[
38
]
,
and we conjecture that
an analogous characterisation can be given for
\mathsf{WMC}
and its extensions in terms of relational models with non-normal worlds
(cf. e.g.
[
34
]
).
As a byproduct of this work,
we have provided a new semantics for
\mathsf{WK}
alternative to
its original relational semantics
[
38
]

and to the neighbourhood semantics in
[
18
,
9
]
.

The possibility to define constructive counterparts of both normal and non-normal classical
logics
can be seen as providing
additional justification for
the present approach.
To make a comparison, it is not obvious how to extend the family
of intuitionistic modal logics (
\mathsf{IK}
and extensions) to non-normal systems,
given that their definition
ultimately relies
on the standard translation of modal formulas into first-order sentences, which in turn is based on the relational semantics.
Interestingly,
the constructive counterparts of non-normal logics that we have obtained are not entirely new.
In particular,
\mathsf{WM}
and
\mathsf{WMN}
coincide with the logics
\mathsf{IM}
and
\mathsf{IMN_{\Box}}
introduced in
[
9
]
,
where they are given an alternative semantics
with distinct neighbourhood functions for
\Box
and
\Diamond
.
By contrast,
\mathsf{WMC}
is not equivalent to
\mathsf{IMC}
in

[
9
]
,
since
\mathsf{WMC}
contains
{K_{\Diamond}}
which is not a theorem of
\mathsf{IMC}
.

The results presented in this paper can be extended in several directions.
In future work we plan to study the complexity of W-logics, possibly extending some optimal calculi for
\mathsf{IPL}

(G3-style calculi are not adequate to establish good complexity bounds
for constructive logics).
Moreover, we would like to study whether Iemhoff’s proof-theoretical method for proving
uniform interpolation
[
16
]
can be adapted to W-logics.
We would also like to define calculi for W-logics
that allow for a direct extraction of countermodels from failed proofs,
along the lines of
[
17
,
10
,
11
]
.

Furthermore,
one can extend the
present analysis to further classical modal logics in order to enrich the family of W-logics,
but also to inspect the limits of our approach.
An obvious
limit concerns the logics for which no standard cut-free Gentzen calculi exist,
such as
\mathsf{S5}
.
For these logics one can study whether
a similar analysis could be based on
alternative kinds of calculi,
like hyper- or nested sequent calculi.
At the same time, it is known that
incorporating hereditariness into the satisfaction clauses is not sufficient to provide a semantics for some
constructive systems,
this is the case for instance of the logics
with axiom
{4}

[
2
]
.
Concerning instead
weaker systems,
it seems that for non-normal logic
\mathsf{E}

[
8
]

this approach returns a very weak form of duality analogous to the one of
\mathsf{IE_{1}}
in
[
9
]
,
but this requires further study.

A regularity criterion in multiplier spaces to Navier-Stokes equations via the gradient of one velocity component
Ahmad M. Alghamdi, Sadek Gala and Maria Alessandra Ragusa

Abstract
In this paper, we study regularity of weak solutions to the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in ℝ 3 × ( 0 , T ) \mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T) . The main goal is to establish the regularity criterion via the gradient of one velocity component in multiplier spaces.



keywords
Navier-Stokes equations; regularity criterion; one component;
multiplier spaces.

Introduction

In this paper we consider the following Cauchy problemma for the
incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}u+\left(u\cdot\nabla\right)u-\Delta u+\nabla\pi=0,\\
\nabla\cdot u=0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
u=(u_{1}(x,t),u_{2}(x,t),u_{3}(x,t))
denotes the unknown velocity
vector and
\pi=\pi(x,t)
denotes the hydrostatic pressure respectively.
While
u_{0}
is the prescribed initial data for the velocity with
properties
\nabla\cdot u_{0}=0
.
The global existence of smooth solutions for the 3D incompressible
Navier-Stokes equations is one of the most outstanding open problemmas in
fluid mechanics. Different criteria for regularity of the weak solutions
have been proposed and many interesting results were established (see, for
example,

Z5
]
,
[
ZG1

,
[

]
and references therein).
Recently, many authors became interested in the regularity criteria
involving only one velocity component, or its gradient, even though most of
which are not scaling invariant (see, for example,
[
CT0
]
,
[
CT1

,
[

]
,
[

[

and the references cited therein). In particular,
Zhou
[

showed that the solution is regular if

u_{3}\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\text{\ \ with \ }\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=\frac{1}{2},\text{\ \ }6<q\leq\infty.

(2)

Later, Cao and Titi
[
CT1
]
obtained the regularity criterion

u_{3}\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\text{\ \ with \ }\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=\frac{2}{3}+\frac{2}{3q},\text{\ \ }q>\frac{7}{2}.

(3)

Motivated by the above work, Zhou and Pokorný
[
ZP1
]
showed the
following regularity condition

u_{3}\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\text{\ \ with \ }\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2q},\text{\ \ }q>\frac{10}{3},

(4)

while the limiting case
u_{3}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{10}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
was covered in
[
JZ1
]
. Inspired by the work
[
Bei
]
, we are
interested in criteria involving the gradient of one velocity component
\nabla u_{3}
. In fact, He
[
He
]
first verified the following regularity
result

\nabla u_{3}\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\text{\ \ with \ }\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=1,\text{\ \ }3\leq q\leq\infty.

(5)

The above result was significantly improved by Pokorný
[
Pok
]
and
Zhou
[
Z2
]
independently (see also
[
ZP2

). More precisely, it reads
as follows

\nabla u_{3}\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\text{\ \ with \ }\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=\frac{3}{2},\text{\ \ }2\leq q<\infty.

(6)

Very recently, Ye
[
Ye2
]
improves the previous work of Zhou and Pokorný
[
ZP2
]
by using of a new anisotropic Sobolev inequality, and
proved the following regularity criterion

\nabla u_{3}\in L^{\frac{16q}{15q-23}}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\quad\textup{ with }q\in[2,3].

(7)

Note that

\frac{2}{\frac{16q}{15q-23}}+\frac{3}{q}=\frac{15q+1}{8q}>\frac{23}{12},\quad\textup{for any }2\leq q<3.

Consequently, (
7
) can be regarded as a further improvement of
[
ZP2
]
. Moreover, the endpoint case
q=3
recovers the
result of
[
ZY

. For some other interesting regularity criteria, we
refer the readers to
[
Ye1

,
[
Ye2

The purpose of this work is to extend the regularity criterion of weak
solutions in terms of one gradient of velocity component to the multiplier
space which is larger than the Lebesgue space. The method is based on the
following interpolation inequality

\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{\gamma}}\leq C\left\|\partial_{3}\varphi\right\|_{L^{\mu}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\left\|\nabla_{h}\varphi\right\|_{L^{\lambda}}^{\frac{2}{3}},

where
\mu,\lambda
and
\gamma
satisfy

1\leq\mu,\text{\ \ }\lambda<+\infty,\text{\ \ }\frac{1}{\mu}+\frac{2}{\lambda}>1\text{\ and \ }1+\frac{3}{\gamma}=\frac{1}{\mu}+\frac{2}{\lambda}.

The detailed proof of this inequality can be found in the appendix of Cao
and Wu
[
CW
]
.
In order to prove our theorem, let us recall the definition of weak
solutions.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
T>0,u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\nabla\cdot u_{0}=0
in
the sense of distributions. A measurable function
u(x,t)
is called a weak
solution to the Navier-Stokes equations (
1
) on
[0,T]
if the
following conditions hold:

1.

u(x,t)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
;

2.

system (
1
) is satisfied in the sense of distributions;

3.

the energy inequality, that is,

\left\|u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\leq\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By a strong solution, we mean that a weak solution
u
of the Navier-Stokes
equations (
1
) satisfies

(u(x,t),\theta(x,t))\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

It is well known that the strong solution is regular and unique.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the Homogeneous Sobolev Space
\dot{H}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is the space of tempered distributions
f
for which

\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}=\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\xi\right|^{2\alpha}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi}<+\infty.

For Homogeneous Sobolev Spaces, we refer to the book
[
BCD
]
. For
instance, the following basic interpolation inequality holds:

Lemma 1.2
.

For
0<\alpha\leq\beta
, the space
L^{2}\cap\dot{H}^{\beta}
is a
subset of
\dot{H}^{\alpha}
, and we have

\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}}^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}.

(8)

Proof 1.3
.

This is a particular case of
[
BCD
]
, Proposition 1.32.

We say that a function belongs to the multiplier spaces
\dot{X}_{1+\alpha}:=M(\dot{H}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\rightarrow\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
if it maps, by pointwise multiplication,
\dot{H}^{\alpha}
in
\dot{H}^{-1}:

\dot{X}_{1+\alpha}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\left\|fg\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\leq\left\|g\right\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}\right\}.

\dot{H}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
denotes the homogeneous Sobolev space.
The space
\dot{X}_{1+\alpha}
has been characterized in
[
Maz1
]
,
[
Maz2
]

(see also
[
GL
]
). Now our regularity criterion for system (
1
)
reads

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\nabla\cdot u_{0}=0

in the sense of distributions. Assume that
u
is a weak solution to system (
1
). If
\nabla u_{3}
satisfies the following condition

\nabla u_{3}\in L^{\frac{8}{3-4\alpha}}(0,T;\dot{X}_{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\text{\ for some \ }0\leq\alpha<\frac{3}{4},

(9)

then the solution
u
is regular on
(0;T]
.

Remark 1.5
.

Since
L^{\frac{3}{1+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset\dot{X}_{1+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(see e.g.
[
ZG1
]
for details), it is clear that our
result improves that in
[
Ye2
]
and extend the regularity criterion (
4
) from Lebesgue space
L^{\alpha}
to multiplier space
\dot{X}_{1+\alpha}
.

Thanks to

\left\|f\right\|_{BMO}\leq C\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\dot{X}_{1}}

(see e.g.
[
G08
, Proposition 2]
), where
BMO
denotes the homogeneous
space of bounded mean oscillations, it is easy to deduce the following
regularity criterion.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\nabla\cdot u_{0}=0
in the
sense of distributions. Assume that
u
is a weak solution to system (
1
). If
u_{3}
satisfies the following condition

u_{3}\in L^{\frac{8}{3}}(0,T;BMO(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

(10)

then the solution
u
is regular on
(0;T]
.

Remark 1.7
.

Since
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow BMO(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, our
result recovers the limiting case
q=\infty
in (
4
), that is,

u_{3}\in L^{\frac{8}{3}}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

Consequently, (
10
) can be regarded as a further improvement of the
previous work
[

]

2
Proof of main result.

In this section, under the assumptions of the Theorem
1.4
, we prove
our main result. Before proving our result, we recall the following
muliplicative Sobolev imbedding inequality in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(see, for example
[
CT1
]
):

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{6}}\leq C\left\|\nabla_{h}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|\partial_{3}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}},

(11)

where
\nabla_{h}=(\partial_{x_{1}},\partial_{x_{2}})
is the horizontal
gradient operator. We are now give the proof of our main theorem.

Proof 2.1
.

To prove our result, it suffices to show that for any fixed
T>T^{\ast}
,
there holds

\underset{0\leq t\leq T^{\ast}}{\sup}\left\|\nabla u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{T},

where
T^{\ast}
, which denotes the maximal existence time of a strong
solution and
C_{T}
is an absolute constant which only depends on
T
and
u_{0}
.
The method of our proof is based on two major parts. The first one
establishes the bounds of
\left\|\nabla_{h}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
,
while the second gives the bounds of the
H^{1}-
norm of velocity
u
in
terms of the results of part one.

Step I.
Taking the inner product of (
1
)
1
with
-\Delta_{h}u
, we obtain after integrating by parts that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\nabla_{h}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\nabla_{h}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta_{h}udx=I.

(12)

where
\Delta_{h}=\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}+\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}
is the
horizontal Laplacian. For the notational simplicity, we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\underset{\tau\in[\Gamma,t]}{\sup}\left\|\nabla_{h}u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\Gamma}^{t}\left\|\nabla\nabla_{h}u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\underset{\tau\in[\Gamma,t]}{\sup}\left\|\nabla u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\Gamma}^{t}\left\|\Delta u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau,

for
t\in[\Gamma,T^{\ast})
. In view of (
9
), we choose
\epsilon>0
to be precisely determined subsequently and then select
\Gamma<T^{\ast}
sufficiently close to
T^{\ast}
such that for all
\Gamma\leq t<T^{\ast}
,

\int_{\Gamma}^{t}\left\|\nabla u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\leq\epsilon\ll 1.

(13)

Integrating by parts and using the divergence-free condition, it follows
that

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\nabla u_{3}\right|\left|\nabla u\right|\left|\nabla_{h}u\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left|\nabla u_{3}\right|\left|\nabla u\right|\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\left\|\nabla_{h}u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla u_{3}\right\|_{\dot{X}_{1+\alpha}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}\left\|\nabla\nabla_{h}u\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla u_{3}\right\|_{\dot{X}_{1+\alpha}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\alpha}\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\alpha}\left\|\nabla\nabla_{h}u\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla u_{3}\right\|_{\dot{X}_{1+\alpha}}^{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2(1-\alpha)}\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla\nabla_{h}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

by Young’s inequality and (
8
). Inserting the above estimate into (
12
) and integrating with respect to time, we deduce for every
\tau\in[\Gamma,t]
:

\displaystyle\underset{\tau\in[\Gamma,t]}{\sup}

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{h}u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\Gamma}^{t}\left\|\nabla\nabla_{h}u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla_{h}u(\Gamma)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int_{\Gamma}^{t}\left\|\nabla u_{3}(\tau)\right\|_{\dot{X}_{1+\alpha}}^{2}\left\|\nabla u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2(1-\alpha)}\left\|\Delta u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2\alpha}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla_{h}u(\Gamma)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\underset{\tau\in[\Gamma,t]}{\sup}\left\|\nabla u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}-2\alpha}\right)\int_{\Gamma}^{t}\left\|\nabla u_{3}(\tau)\right\|_{\dot{X}_{1+\alpha}}^{2}\left\|\nabla u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\Delta u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2\alpha}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C+C\left(\underset{\tau\in[\Gamma,t]}{\sup}\left\|\nabla u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}-2\alpha}\right)\left(\int_{\Gamma}^{t}\left\|\nabla u_{3}(\tau)\right\|_{\dot{X}_{1+\alpha}}^{\frac{8}{3-4\alpha}}d\tau\right)^{\frac{3}{4}-\alpha}\left(\int_{\Gamma}^{t}\left\|\nabla u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{\Gamma}^{t}\left\|\Delta u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq C+C\mathcal{J}^{\frac{3}{2}-2\alpha}(t)\left(\int_{\Gamma}^{t}\left\|\nabla u_{3}(\tau)\right\|_{\dot{X}_{1+\alpha}}^{\frac{8}{3-4\alpha}}d\tau\right)^{\frac{3}{4}-\alpha}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{J}^{2\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{J}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t),

which leads to

\mathcal{L}^{2}(t)\leq C+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{J}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t).

(14)

Step II.
Now, we will establish the bounds of
H^{1}-
norm of the
velocity field. In order to do it, taking the inner product of (
1
)
1
with
-\Delta u
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then, integration by
parts gives the following identity:

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta udx

Integrating by parts and using the divergence-free condition, one can easily
deduce that (see e.g.
[
ZP1
]
)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta udx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\nabla_{h}u\right|\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx\leq C\left\|\nabla_{h}u\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla_{h}u\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{6}}^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla_{h}u\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\nabla_{h}\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

by Hölder’s inequality, Nirenberg-Gagliardo’s interpolation inequality
and (
11
). Integrating this last inequality in time, we deduce that
for all
\tau\in[\Gamma,t]

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{2}(t)\leq{}

\displaystyle{}\left\|\nabla u(\Gamma)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle{\ }+C\underset{\tau\in[\Gamma,t]}{\sup}\left\|\nabla_{h}u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left(\int_{\Gamma}^{t}\left\|\nabla u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{\Gamma}^{t}\left\|\nabla\nabla_{h}u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Gamma}^{t}\left\|\Delta u(\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle{}\left\|\nabla u(\Gamma)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2C\mathcal{L}(t)\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{L}(t)\mathcal{J}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle{}\left\|\nabla u(\Gamma)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{L}^{2}(t)\mathcal{J}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t).

(15)

Inserting (
14
) into (
15
) and taking
\epsilon
small enough,
then it is easy to see that for all
\Gamma\leq t<T^{\ast}
, there holds

\mathcal{J}^{2}(t)\leq\left\|\nabla u(\Gamma)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{J}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{J}^{2}(t)<\infty,

which proves

\underset{\Gamma\leq t<T^{\ast}}{\sup}\left\|\nabla u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<+\infty.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

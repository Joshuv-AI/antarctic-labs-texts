Inducing spectral gaps for the cohomological Laplacians of SL n ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}) and SAut ⁡ ( F n ) \operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
Piotr Mizerka

Abstract
. The technique of inducing spectral gaps for cohomological Laplacians in degree zero was used by Kaluba, Kielak and Nowak [ 9 ] to prove property (T) for Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \operatorname{Aut}(F_{n}) and SL n ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}) . In this paper, we adapt this technique to Laplacians in degree one. This allows to provide a lower bound for the cohomological Laplacian in degree one for SL n ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}) for every unitary representation. In particular, one gets in that way an alternative proof of property (T) for SL n ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}) whenever n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 .

1.
Introduction

Cohomological Laplacians were introduced by Bader and Nowak
[
1
]
in the context of vanishing of group cohomology with unitary coefficients. Such vanishing of the first cohomology is known to be equivalent to Kazhdan’s property (T) and an algebraic characterization for it was found by Ozawa
[
17
]
for finitely generated groups. It turns out that property (T) can be described in terms of a specific degree zero
Laplacian

\Delta
: Ozawa showed that a group
G
has property (T) if and only if
\Delta^{2}-\lambda\Delta
is a sum of squares in the real group ring
\mathbb{R}G
for some positive spectral gap
\lambda
. Using this characterization, Kaluba, Kielak, Nowak, and Ozawa
[
11
,
9
]
were able to show property (T) for
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n})
for
n\geq 3
and
n\geq 5
respectively. Estimating the spectral gap
\lambda
from below has another advantage of prividing a lower bound for
\kappa(G,S)
, the
Kazhdan constant
associated to a generating set
S
of a group
G
. It is known that
G
has property (T) if and only if
\kappa(G,S)>0
and this inequality does not depend on the generating set. For a symmetric generating set
S=S^{-1}
, the lower bound for Kazhdan constants is given by the formula (see
[
3
, Remark 5.4.7]
and
[
13
]
)

\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{|S|}}\leq\kappa(G,S),

where
\lambda
is a positive constant such that
\Delta^{2}-\lambda\Delta
is a sum of squares.

We focus on providing lower bounds for the spectral gap of
\Delta_{1}
, the one-degree Laplacian (
\Delta_{1}
can be in fact any group ring matrix which can be used to compute the first group cohomology – details can be found in
[
1
]
– and we focus on its particular model here, defined by
Fox derivatives

[
6
,
7
,
15
]
). The existence of such a positive spectral gap for a group
G
is equivalent to vanishing of the first cohomology of
G
with unitary coefficients and
reducibility
of the second (reducibility means that the images of the differentials in the chain complex of Hilbert spaces computing the cohomology for a given unitary representation are closed), see
[
1
, The Main Theorem]
. Once a positive spectral gap is found for
\Delta_{1}
, it turns out to be a lower bound for the Ozawa’s spectral gap, see Remark
2.2
. We acknowledge that it has been recently announced by U. Bader and R. Sauer that the reducibility condition is a consequence of vanishing of cohomology one degree lower, see
[
2
]
. Thus,
\Delta_{1}
having a positive spectral gap is equivalent to a group having property (T). By
[
1
]
and Theorem 3.11 from
[
2
]
and the paragraph below it, we know actually that a more general statement holds: for any
n\geq 1
, the existence of a spectral gap for some model of
\Delta_{n}
is equivalent to the vanishing of the
n
-th cohomology of a group with unitary coefficients.

Recently, M. Kaluba, P. W. Nowak and the author showed that for
\Delta_{1}
, the first cohomological Laplacian of
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
, and
\lambda=0.32
, the matrix
\Delta_{1}-\lambda I
is a sum of squares (
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
). In this paper we show how to use a slightly stronger statement for
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
to induce the spectral gap for
G_{n}=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, whenever
n\geq 3
. Our technique works as well for the case
G_{n}=\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
. We remark however that in such a case we were unable to obtain such a spectral gap for any particular
n
. On the other other hand, in his preprint
[
16
]
M. Nitsche shows property (T) for
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{4})
. This, in combination with the work of Bader and Nowak
[
1
]
and the announcement of Bader and Sauer, would yield the desired gap for the Laplacian in degree one for
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{4})
. Nevertheless, this will still not allow us to induce the spectral gap since we would need a spectral gap for a specific summand of the Laplacian of
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{4})
.

Our induction technique is inspired by the idea of decomposing the generating set of
G_{n}
into three parts: square, adjacent, and opposite, see
[
9
]
. This allows us to decompose
\Delta_{1}
for any
G_{n}
into the sum of the appropriate parts. Denoting the adjacent part by
\operatorname{Adj}_{n}
for any
n\geq 3
, and the generating set for
G_{n}
by
\altmathcal{S}_{n}
, the main theorem can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(cf.
Corollary

5.5
)
.

Suppose
\operatorname{Adj}_{m}-\lambda I_{|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|}
is a sum of squares in
\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|\times|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{m})
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Adj}_{n}-\frac{n-2}{m-2}\lambda I_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}

is a sum of squares in
\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|\times|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
for any
n\geq m
.

Bounding the spectral gap for the adjacent part
\operatorname{Adj}_{3}
of
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
, we get the following

Corollary 1.2

(cf.
Corollary

6.2
)
.

Let
\Delta_{1}
be the cohomological Laplacian in degree one of
G_{n}=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then
\Delta_{1}-\lambda I_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}
is a sum of squares for
\lambda=0.217(n-2)
.

To our knowledge, the lower bounds for the spectral gaps we obtained are the first examples of explicit computations for
\Delta_{1}
for an infinite family of property (T) groups.

This article is organized as follows. In
section

2
we introduce Fox derivatives which provide a setting to calculate one-degree Laplacians and define the positivity in the group ring setting in terms of sums of squares. In the next section we introduce the presentions for
G_{n}
: using the
elementary matrices
for
G_{n}=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
Nielsen transvections
for
G_{n}=\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
. Next, we decompose the Laplacian in degree one into the three parts mentioned above. In
section

5
we show
Theorem

1.1
using the technique of symmetrization and apply this result in
section

6
to obtain a lower bound for the spectral gap of
\Delta_{1}
for
G_{n}=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
whenever
n\geq 3
.

2.
Fox derivatives and sums of squares

We describe the model of
\Delta_{1}
which appears in Theorem
1.1
. This can be achieved by means of
Fox derivatives
. Further details can be found in the papers of Fox and Lyndon,
[
6
,
7
,
15
,
14
]
.

Let
G=\langle s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}|r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}\rangle
be a finitely presented group. The Fox derivatives
\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{j}}
are the endomorphisms of
\mathbb{Z}F_{n}
, the group ring of the free group
F_{n}=\langle s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\rangle
, given by the following conditions:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial 1}{\partial s_{j}}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial s_{i}}{\partial s_{j}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(uv)}{\partial s_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial u}{\partial s_{j}}+u\frac{\partial v}{\partial s_{j}},

where
\delta_{ij}
denotes the Kronecker delta. The
Jacobian
of
G
is then the matrix
J=\left[\frac{\partial r_{i}}{\partial s_{j}}\right]\in\mathbb{M}_{m\times n}(\mathbb{Z}G)
obtained by quotienting the Fox derivatives into the group ring
\mathbb{Z}G
. Denoting by
d
the vertical vector
\left[1-s_{j}\right]\in\mathbb{M}_{n\times 1}(\mathbb{Z}G)
, the one-degree Laplacian
\Delta_{1}
associated to the presentation
G=\langle s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}|r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}\rangle
is defined by

\Delta_{1}=\Delta_{1}^{+}+\Delta_{1}^{-},

where
\Delta_{1}^{+}=J^{*}J
and
\Delta_{1}^{-}=dd^{*}
, and, for any matrix
M
with entries in
\mathbb{Z}G
, the matrix
M^{*}
is the composition of the matrix transposition with the
*
-involution on
\mathbb{Z}G
given by
g\mapsto g^{-1}
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a matrix
M\in\mathbb{M}_{k\times k}(\mathbb{R}G)
is a
sum of squares
if there exist matrices
M_{1},\ldots,M_{l}\in\mathbb{M}_{k\times k}(\mathbb{R}G)
such that
M=M_{1}^{*}M_{1}+\ldots M_{l}^{*}M_{l}
.

It follows from
[
1
, Lemma 14]
that
\Delta_{1}^{\pm}
, and therefore
\Delta_{1}
as well, are sums of squares.

By saying that a matrix
M\in\mathbb{M}_{k\times k}(\mathbb{R}G)
has a positive
spectral gap
we mean that there exists a positive
\lambda
such that
M-\lambda I_{k}
is a sum of squares.

As noted in
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
, it turns out that we have some freedom in choosing the relations which build up
\Delta_{1}^{+}
. Denote

J_{i}=\left[\frac{\partial r_{i}}{\partial s_{1}}\cdots\frac{\partial r_{i}}{\partial s_{n}}\right]

and put
\Delta_{I}^{+}=\sum_{i\in I}J_{i}^{*}J_{i}
for any subset of indices
I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Then, once
\Delta_{I}^{+}+\Delta_{1}^{-}\in\mathbb{M}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{R}G)
has a spectral gap then
\Delta_{1}=\Delta_{I}^{+}+\Delta_{\{1,\ldots,m\}\setminus I}^{+}+\Delta_{1}^{-}\in\mathbb{M}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{R}G)
has at least the same spectral gap.

Computing lower bounds for sepctral gaps for the first Laplacians provides the bounds for Ozawa’s spectral gap:

Remark 2.2
.

Suppose the matrix
C\Delta_{I}^{+}+\Delta_{1}^{-}-\lambda I
is a sum of squares for some subset of relator indices
I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}
and a positive constant
C
and let
\Delta=d^{*}d
. Then
\Delta^{2}-\lambda\Delta=d^{*}\left(C\Delta_{I}^{+}+\Delta_{1}^{-}-\lambda I\right)d
is a sum of squares as well (note that
d^{*}\Delta_{I}^{+}d
is always zero). Note that in the case the generating set
\altmathcal{S}
and the set of its inverses,
\altmathcal{S}^{-1}
, are disjoint, then
\Delta
is the zero degree Laplacian
|\altmathcal{S}|-\sum_{s\in\altmathcal{S}}s
for the symmetric generating set
\altmathcal{S}\cup\altmathcal{S}^{-1}
.

3.
Presentations of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})

In this section, we introduce the presentations of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
which we shall use to perform the Fox calculus and prove property (T) for these groups. The presentations can be found in
[
5
]
and
[
8
]
for
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
respectively. For the latter, we provide an equivalent presentation which is easy to obtain from
[
8
]
.

The presentation for
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
is given below.

\displaystyle\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})=\big\langle E_{ij},1\leq i\neq j\leq n\big|

\displaystyle[E_{ij},E_{kl}],i\neq l,j\neq k,(i,j)\neq(k,l),

\displaystyle[E_{ij},E_{jk}]E_{ik}^{-1}\text{ for distinct }i,j,k,

\displaystyle\left(E_{12}E_{21}^{-1}E_{12}\right)^{4}\big\rangle.

The generator
E_{ij}
represents the elementary matrix with
1
at the
(i,j)
-th entry and the diagonal and zeroes elsewhere. Using
Remark

2.2
, we shall exclude the relation
\left(E_{12}E_{21}^{-1}E_{12}\right)^{4}
from further considerations.

The generators of
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
are denoted by
\lambda_{ij}
and
\rho_{ij}
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
and are called left and right
Nielsen transvections
respectively. The relator set contains commutators and pentagonal relations as well, along with other relations which we shall not consider, again by
Remark

2.2
. We only present the commutator and pentagonal relations therefore. These relations are defined for distinct indices
i,j,k,l\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
as follows:

\displaystyle[\lambda_{i,j},\rho_{ij}],\quad[\lambda_{ij},\lambda_{kl}],\quad[\rho_{ij},\rho_{kl}],\quad[\lambda_{ij},\rho_{kl}],

\displaystyle\quad[\lambda_{ij},\lambda_{kj}],\quad[\rho_{ij},\rho_{kj}],\quad[\lambda_{ij},\rho_{ik}],\quad[\lambda_{i,j},\rho_{kj}],

\displaystyle\quad\lambda_{ik}^{\pm}[\lambda_{jk}^{\pm},\lambda_{ij}^{-1}],\quad\rho_{ik}^{\pm}[\rho_{jk}^{\pm},\rho_{ij}^{-1}],\quad\lambda_{ik}^{\pm}[\rho_{jk}^{\mp},\lambda_{ij}],\quad\rho_{ik}^{\pm}[\lambda_{jk}^{\mp},\rho_{ij}].

The transvection generators correspond in
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
to the following automorphisms of
F_{n}=\langle s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\rangle
1
1

1

We take in
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
the group operation order given by composition of automorphisms, i.e.
\alpha*\beta=\alpha\circ\beta
. Note that this is different than the convention of Gersten
[
8
]
. In his paper the convention is:
\alpha*\beta=\beta\circ\alpha
. We had to reverse the order in pentagonal relators for that account.
:

\displaystyle\lambda_{ij}(s_{k})=\begin{cases}s_{j}s_{i}&\text{ if $k=i$,}\\
s_{k}&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\quad\rho_{ij}(s_{k})=\begin{cases}s_{i}s_{j}&\text{ if $k=i$,}\\
s_{k}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

4.
Decomposition into square, adjacent and opposite part

Let
n\geq 3
be an integer and
G_{n}
be
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
or
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
. We introduce the following decompositions of the Laplacians
\Delta_{1}^{\pm}
of
G_{n}
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}^{\pm}=\operatorname{Sq}_{n}^{\pm}+\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{\pm}+\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{\pm}.

Our decomposition is inspired by
[
9
, pp. 543 – 545]
.

The generators of
G_{n}
take form
\omega_{ij}
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
and
\omega
denoting either elementary matrices or Nielsen transvections. Then, for any
3\leq m\leq n
, there exists a natural embedding
G_{m}\hookrightarrow G_{n}
given by
\omega_{ij}\mapsto\omega_{ij}
. From now on, when talking about the relations of
G_{n}
, we shall restrict ourselves to commutator and pentagonal relations only.

Let
C_{n}
be the
(n-1)
-simplex with the vertices
\{1,\ldots,n\}
and let
F(C_{n})
denote the set of its faces, that is, the subsets of the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Denoting by
\altmathcal{S}_{n}
the generator set of
G_{n}
and by
\operatorname{Free}(\altmathcal{S}_{n})
the free group on
\altmathcal{S}_{n}
, one can define the map

\displaystyle\phi:\operatorname{Free}(\altmathcal{S}_{n})\longrightarrow F(C_{n})

by sending an element of
\operatorname{Free}(\altmathcal{S}_{n})
to the face on the indices of the generators occurring in that element, i.e.

\phi(\omega_{i_{1}j_{1}}\ldots\omega_{i_{k}j_{k}})=\{i_{1},j_{1},\ldots,i_{k},j_{k}\}.

The generators
\omega_{i_{l}j_{l}}
can be either elementary matrices or left or right Nielsen transvections.

In order to define
\operatorname{Sq}_{n}^{\pm}
,
\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{\pm}
and
\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{\pm}
we distinguish from
F(C_{n})
the following three subsets of faces

\displaystyle\operatorname{Edg}_{n}

\displaystyle=\{f\in F(C_{n})||f|=2\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tri}_{n}

\displaystyle=\{f\in F(C_{n})||f|=3\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tet}_{n}

\displaystyle=\{f\in F(C_{n})||f|=4\},

that is,
\operatorname{Edg}_{n}
,
\operatorname{Tri}_{n}
and
\operatorname{Tet}_{n}
constitute respectively the sets of edges, triangles, and tetrahedra of
C_{n}
.

The decomposition
\Delta_{1}^{-}=\operatorname{Sq}_{n}^{-}+\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{-}+\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{-}
is obtained as follows:

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Sq}^{-}_{n}\right)_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\left(\Delta^{-}_{1}\right)_{s,t}\text{ for }\phi(s)\cup\phi(t)\in\operatorname{Edg}_{n}\text{ and }0\text{ otherwise},

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Adj}^{-}_{n}\right)_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\left(\Delta^{-}_{1}\right)_{s,t}\text{ for }\phi(s)\cup\phi(t)\in\operatorname{Tri}_{n}\text{ and }0\text{ otherwise},

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Op}^{-}_{n}\right)_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\left(\Delta^{-}_{1}\right)_{s,t}\text{ for }\phi(s)\cup\phi(t)\in\operatorname{Tet}_{n}\text{ and }0\text{ otherwise}.

We remark that the group ring elements
d^{*}\operatorname{Sq}^{-}_{n}d
,
d^{*}\operatorname{Adj}^{-}_{n}d
, and
d^{*}\operatorname{Op}^{-}_{n}d
are equal to the elements
\operatorname{Sq}_{n}
,
\operatorname{Adj}_{n}
, and
\operatorname{Op}_{n}
as defined in
[
9
]
.

The decomposition
\Delta_{1}^{+}=\operatorname{Sq}^{+}_{n}+\operatorname{Adj}^{+}_{n}+\operatorname{Op}^{+}_{n}
is defined, in turn, by summing over specific relators of
G_{n}
.

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Sq}^{+}_{n}\right)_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi(r)\in\operatorname{Edg}_{n}}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t},

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Adj}^{+}_{n}\right)_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi(r)\in\operatorname{Tri}_{n}}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t},

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Op}^{+}_{n}\right)_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi(r)\in\operatorname{Tet}_{n}}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}.

Note that
\left(\operatorname{Adj}^{+}_{n}\right)_{s,t}
and
\left(\operatorname{Op}^{+}_{n}\right)_{s,t}
can be non-zero only if
s=t
or
\phi(s)\cup\phi(t)
belongs to
\operatorname{Tri}_{n}
and
\operatorname{Tet}_{n}
respectively. Since we do not consider relation
\left(E_{12}E_{21}^{-1}E_{12}\right)^{4}
for
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, there are no relators
r
such that
\phi(r)\in\operatorname{Edg}_{n}
for this case and so
\operatorname{Sq}_{n}^{+}
is the zero matrix for
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
. This is not the case for
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
due to the presence of relations
[\lambda_{i,j},\rho_{ij}]
- this is, however, not a problem for us, since we only need
\operatorname{Sq}_{n}^{+}
to be a sum of squares which is obvious by definition.

We shall call
\operatorname{Sq}_{n}^{\pm}
,
\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{\pm}
and
\operatorname{Op}_{n}^{\pm}
the
square
,
adjacent
and
opposite
part respectively.

4.1.
Sum of squares decomposition for square and opposite part

It turns out that the square and opposite parts behave well with respect to sum of squares decompositions. The precise statement is given below.

Lemma 4.3
.

The matrices
\operatorname{Sq}^{-}_{n}
and
\operatorname{Op}^{+}_{n}+2\operatorname{Op}^{-}_{n}
are sums of squares.

Proof.

For any
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, define
d^{i,j}\in\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|\times 1}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
to be the column vector indexed by the generators of
G_{n}
with the only non-zero entries being
1-s
, indexed by the generators
s
satisfying
\phi(s)=\{i,j\}
. That
\operatorname{Sq}^{-}_{n}
is a sum of squares follows from the decomposition below:

\operatorname{Sq}^{-}_{n}=\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}d^{i,j}\left(d^{i,j}\right)^{*}.

We have already observed that both
\operatorname{Op}^{+}_{n}
and
\operatorname{Op}^{-}_{n}
(in this case, just by the definition) have vanishing
(s,t)
-entries whenever
\phi(s)\cup\phi(t)\in\operatorname{Tri}_{n}
or the generator
t
has the same indices as
s
but with the reversed order. In addition, the diagonal vanishes for
\operatorname{Op}^{-}_{n}
as well. This is not the case for
\operatorname{Op}^{+}_{n}
. However, since
\operatorname{Op}^{+}_{n}
is a sum of squares by its definition, it follows that the same applies to its diagonal part. On the other hand, the non-diagonal parts of
\operatorname{Op}^{+}_{n}
and
2\operatorname{Op}^{-}_{n}
cancel out – for distinct
1\leq i,j,k,l\leq n
we have:

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Op}^{+}_{n}\right)_{\alpha_{ij},\beta_{kl}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial[\alpha_{ij},\beta_{kl}]}{\partial\alpha_{ij}}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial[\alpha_{ij},\beta_{kl}]}{\partial\beta_{kl}}+\left(\frac{\partial[\beta_{kl},\alpha_{ij}]}{\partial\alpha_{ij}}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial[\beta_{kl},\alpha_{ij}]}{\partial\beta_{kl}}

\displaystyle=-2(1-\alpha_{ij})(1-\beta_{kl})^{*}.

∎

5.
Symmetrization of the adjacent part

In this section we show how to get the lower bound for spectral gap of
\operatorname{Adj}_{n}=\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{+}+\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{-}
for
G_{n}
once we assume that the lower bound for the corresponding spectral gap for
G_{m}
is known for some
n\geq m\geq 3
. Our method is inspired by the symmetrization method used in
[
9
]
.

5.1.
General setting for symmetrization

Note that the symmetric group
\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
acts by automorphisms on
G_{k}
by sending the generator
\omega_{i,j}
to
\omega_{\sigma(i),\sigma(j)}
for any permutation
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
. This induces an action of
\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
on
\mathbb{R}G_{k}
by automorphisms which preserves the
*
-involution. Using this action we can define the action on
\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{k}|\times|\altmathcal{S}_{k}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{k})
by the formula

\left(\sigma A\right)_{s,t}=\sigma\left(A_{\sigma^{-1}(s),\sigma^{-1}(t)}\right),

for any matrix
A
over
\mathbb{R}G_{k}
indexed by the generators
\altmathcal{S}_{k}
.
This is also the action by
*
-algebra automorphisms (the multiplication being the group ring matrix multiplication). Thus, for any matrix
A\in\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{k}|\times|\altmathcal{S}_{k}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{k})
and
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
, the matrix
\sigma A
is a sum of squares provided
A
was a sum of squares.

5.2.
Invariance of Laplacians

Let us show first that the adjacent Laplacians
\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{+}
and
\operatorname{Adj}^{-}_{k}
for
G_{k}
are
\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
-invariant. The statement for
\operatorname{Adj}^{-}_{k}
is straightforward:

\displaystyle\left(\sigma\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{-}\right)_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\sigma\left(\left(\operatorname{Adj}^{-}_{k}\right)_{\sigma^{-1}(s),\sigma^{-1}(t)}\right)=\sigma\left(\left(1-\sigma^{-1}(s)\right)\left(1-\sigma^{-1}(t)\right)^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=(1-s)(1-t)^{*}=\left(\operatorname{Adj}^{-}_{k}\right)_{s,t}.

Let us show the invariance of
\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{+}
. Let
a=|\altmathcal{S}_{k}|
be the number of generators of
G_{k}
and
b
be the number of its relations
r
such that
\phi(r)\in\operatorname{Tri}_{k}
. Suppose moreover that this set of relations is invariant with respect to the action of
\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
, that is, for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
and
r
satisfying
\phi(r)\in\operatorname{Tri}_{k}
, the word
\sigma(r)
is another relator in
\phi^{-1}(\operatorname{Tri}_{k})
. Notice that each permutation
\sigma
from
\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
defines permutations of
\{1,\ldots,a\}
and
\{1,\ldots,b\}
(we fix the order of generators
\altmathcal{S}_{k}
and relations
r
such that
\phi(r)\in\operatorname{Tri}_{k}
). Having this, we can endow both products
\left(\mathbb{R}G_{k}\right)^{a}
and
\left(\mathbb{R}G_{k}\right)^{b}
with the action of
\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
as follows:

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{a}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sigma\xi_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\ldots,\sigma\xi_{\sigma^{-1}(a)}\right),

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{b}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sigma\xi_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\ldots,\sigma\xi_{\sigma^{-1}(b)}\right).

Finally, denote by
J
the Jacobian map from
\left(\mathbb{R}G_{k}\right)^{a}
to
\left(\mathbb{R}G_{k}\right)^{b}
given by the relations
r
such that
\phi(r)\in\operatorname{Tri}_{k}
, that is
\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{+}=J^{*}J
. It turns out that the invariance of
\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{+}
follows from the
\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
-equivariance of
J
:

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
we have
\sigma\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{+}=\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{+}
provided
J
is equivariant.

Proof.

Suppose
J
is equivariant. Let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
. Since we have ordered the generators in
\altmathcal{S}_{k}
, we can identify them with the corresponding indices from the set
\{1,\ldots,a\}
. Thus, we must show that
\left(\sigma\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{+}\right)_{i,j}=\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{+}\right)_{i,j}
for any
1\leq i,j\leq a
.

It is easy to check that the equivariance of
J
is equivalent to the following condition:

J_{y,\sigma^{-1}(x)}=\sigma^{-1}J_{\sigma(y),x}

for any
1\leq x\leq a
and
1\leq y\leq b
. Using this relationship, we get

\displaystyle\left(\sigma\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{+}\right)_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sigma\left(\sum_{l}\left(J_{l,\sigma^{-1}(i)}\right)^{*}J_{l,\sigma^{-1}(j)}\right)=\sigma\left(\sum_{l}\sigma^{-1}\left(\left(J_{\sigma(l),i}\right)^{*}J_{\sigma(l),j}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\left(J_{\sigma(l),i}\right)^{*}J_{\sigma(l),j}=\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{+}\right)_{i,j}.

∎

The following lemma is the key tool to prove the equivariance of
J
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{N}
,
s\in\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}\}
and
r
a word in the free group
F_{N}
generated by
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}\}
, the following holds:

\frac{\partial r}{\partial(\sigma(s))}=\sigma\left(\frac{\partial\left(\sigma^{-1}(r)\right)}{\partial s}\right),

where the action of
\operatorname{Sym}_{N}
on
F_{N}
is given by
\tau(s_{i})=s_{\tau(i)}
for any
\tau\in\operatorname{Sym}_{N}
.

Proof.

We prove the assertion by induction on the word length of the relation
r
. Note first that both sides of the equation in question vanish for
r=1
. Suppose
r
is a single generator
s^{\prime}
(if
r
is the inverse of a generator, the proof is analogous). In the case
\sigma(s)\notin\{s^{\prime},(s^{\prime})^{-1}\}
both sides of the equation in question vanish. If
\sigma(s)=s^{\prime}
, then

\frac{\partial r}{\partial(\sigma(s))}=\frac{\partial s^{\prime}}{\partial s^{\prime}}=1=\sigma\left(\frac{\partial s}{\partial s}\right)=\sigma\left(\frac{\partial\left(\sigma^{-1}(r)\right)}{\partial s}\right),

while if
\sigma(s)=(s^{\prime})^{-1}
, then

\frac{\partial r}{\partial(\sigma(s))}=\frac{\partial s^{\prime}}{\partial(s^{\prime})^{-1}}=-s^{\prime}=\sigma\left(\frac{\partial s^{-1}}{\partial s}\right)=\sigma\left(\frac{\partial\left(\sigma^{-1}(r)\right)}{\partial s}\right).

Suppose now the assertion follows for all words of length
k\geq 1
. Take a word
r
of length
k+1
. Then
r=uv
for some words
u
and
v
of length at most
k
. Using the induction assumption, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial r}{\partial(\sigma(s))}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial u}{\partial(\sigma(s))}+u\frac{\partial v}{\partial(\sigma(s))}

\displaystyle=\sigma\left(\frac{\partial\left(\sigma^{-1}(u)\right)}{\partial s}+\sigma^{-1}(u)\frac{\partial\left(\sigma^{-1}(v)\right)}{\partial s}\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma\left(\frac{\partial\left(\sigma^{-1}(uv)\right)}{\partial s}\right)=\sigma\left(\frac{\partial\left(\sigma^{-1}(r)\right)}{\partial s}\right).

∎

Corollary 5.3
.

The Jacobian homomorphism
J:(\mathbb{R}G)^{a}\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}G)^{b}
is an equivariant map.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
, for any
1\leq x\leq a
and
1\leq y\leq b
, we get

\displaystyle J_{y,\sigma^{-1}(x)}=\frac{\partial(r_{y})}{\partial\left(\sigma^{-1}(s_{x})\right)}=\sigma^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial\left(\sigma(r_{y})\right)}{\partial s_{x}}\right)=\sigma^{-1}J_{\sigma(y),x}.

∎

5.3.
Adjacent part symmetrizes well

In this section we show how to express
\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{\pm}
in terms of
\operatorname{Adj}_{m}^{\pm}
and the action of the symmetric group
\operatorname{Sym}_{n}
, for any
n\geq m\geq 3
. This allows us to induce spectral gaps for
\operatorname{Adj}
parts (cf.
Corollary

5.5
).

For any
n\geq 3
and
\theta\in\operatorname{Tri}_{n}
, we denote by
\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{\theta}\in\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|\times|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
the embedding of the adjacent part given by the traingle
\theta
of
C_{n}
, i.e., for any generators
s
and
t
of
G_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{\theta}^{-}\right)_{s,t}=\begin{cases}(1-s)(1-t)^{*}&\text{if }\phi(s)\cup\phi(t)=\theta\\
0&\text{if }\phi(s)\cup\phi(t)\neq\theta\end{cases},\quad\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{\theta}^{+}\right)_{s,t}=\sum_{\phi(r)=\theta}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}.

Since it shall be clear from the context, given
m\leq n
, we shall also denote by
\operatorname{Adj}_{m}^{\pm}
the canonical embedding of
\operatorname{Adj}_{m}^{\pm}\in\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|\times|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{m})
into
\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|\times|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
:

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{m}^{-}\right)_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(1-s)(1-t)^{*}&\text{if }\phi(s)\cup\phi(t)\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\\
0&\text{if }\phi(s)\cup\phi(t)\nsubseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\end{cases},

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{m}^{+}\right)_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi(r)\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}.

Due to the same reason, we decided to remove the grading
n
from the notation
\operatorname{Adj}_{\theta}^{\pm}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

For
n\geq m\geq 3
, one has

\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\sigma\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{m}^{\pm}\right)=m(m-1)(m-2)(n-3)!\operatorname{Adj}_{n}^{\pm}.

Proof.

The idea of the proof is to transfer the action of the symmetric group
\operatorname{Sym}_{n}
from the matrices over group rings to the simplex
C_{n}
and apply the invariance of
\operatorname{Adj}_{m}^{\pm}
under the action of
\operatorname{Sym}_{m}
. The transfer is possible due to the following relationships:

(1)

\sigma\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{\theta}^{\pm}\right)=\operatorname{Adj}_{\sigma(\theta)}^{\pm},

holding for every permutation
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}
and a triangle
\theta\in\operatorname{Tri}_{n}
. Let us show (
1
) for
\operatorname{Adj}^{+}
only since the proof for
\operatorname{Adj}^{-}
is a direct application of its definition and the action of
\operatorname{Sym}_{n}
on
\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|\times|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
. Pick two generators
s
and
t
of
G_{n}
. Then, it follows by
Lemma

5.2
that

\displaystyle\left(\sigma\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{\theta}^{+}\right)\right)_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\sigma\left(\sum_{\phi(r)=\theta}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial\sigma^{-1}(s)}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial r}{\partial\sigma^{-1}(t)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi(r)=\sigma(\theta)}\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial s}\right)^{*}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}=\operatorname{Adj}_{\sigma(\theta)}^{+}.

Note that, for any
k\geq 3
, we can express
\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{\pm}
by summing its projections to the triangles of
C_{k}
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{\pm}=\sum_{\theta\in\operatorname{Tri}_{k}}\operatorname{Adj}_{\theta}^{\pm}.

Applying (
1
) and the orbit-stabilizer theorem applied to the action of
\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
on the triangles
\operatorname{Tri}_{k}
, we get

(2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{\pm}=\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{k}}\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Stab}\{1,2,3\}|}\operatorname{Adj}_{\sigma(\{1,2,3\})}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{(k-3)!}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{k}}\sigma\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{3}^{\pm}\right).

Let
\tau_{i}\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}
,
i=1,\ldots,\frac{n!}{m!}
be the representatives of the cosets
\operatorname{Sym}_{n}/\operatorname{Sym}_{m}
. Applying (
2
) first for
k=n
and then for
k=m
, we get

\displaystyle(n-3)!\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\sigma\left(\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{3}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\tau_{i}\sum_{\tau\in\operatorname{Sym}_{m}}\tau\left(\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{3}\right)

\displaystyle=(m-3)!\sum_{i}\tau_{i}\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{m}.

We conclude the proof by applying
\operatorname{Sym}_{m}
-invariance of
\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{m}
(cf.
Lemma

5.1
and
Corollary

5.3
):

\displaystyle(n-3)!\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{n}

\displaystyle=(m-3)!\sum_{i}\tau_{i}\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{m}

\displaystyle=(m-3)!\sum_{i}\tau_{i}\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{m}}\sigma\left(\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(m-1)(m-2)}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\sigma\left(\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{m}\right).

∎

For any
k\geq 3
, let
\operatorname{Adj}_{k}=\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{+}+\operatorname{Adj}_{k}^{-}
. We have the following

Corollary 5.5

(cf.
Theorem

1.1
)
.

The matrix

\displaystyle\operatorname{Adj}_{n}-\frac{n-2}{m-2}\lambda I_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}

is a sum of squares in
\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|\times|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
, provided
\operatorname{Adj}_{m}-\lambda I_{|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|}
is a sum of squares in
\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|\times|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{m})
.

Proof.

As in the case of
\operatorname{Adj}^{\pm}_{m}
, let us also use the notation
I_{|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|}
for the canonical embedding of
I_{|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|}\in\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|\times|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{m})
into
\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|\times|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
. We show first that

(3)

\displaystyle(n-2)!m(m-1)I_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}=\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}_{n}}\sigma I_{|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|}.

Note that the right-hand side of the expression above is a multiple of the identity matrix. Let
\omega_{0}
be
1
in the case
G_{n}=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
2
in the case
G_{n}=\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
. Then each summand
\sigma I_{|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|}
contributes
|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|=m(m-1)\omega_{0}
neutral elements to the diagonal which yields
n!m(m-1)\omega_{0}
neutral elements altogether. Since the diagonal of
I_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}
has
|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|=n(n-1)\omega_{0}
neutral elements, the multiplication factor is equal to
(n-2)!m(m-1)
.

Suppose
\operatorname{Adj}_{m}-\lambda I_{|\altmathcal{S}_{m}|}
is a sum of squares. Combining Lemma
Lemma

5.4
and (
3
), we conclude that the following matrix is a sum of squares in
\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|\times|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
:

m(m-1)(m-2)(n-3)!\operatorname{Adj}_{n}-(n-2)!m(m-1)\lambda I_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}.

Dividing the expression above by
(n-3)!m(m-1)
, we get the assertion.
∎

G_{n}=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})

In this section, let
G_{n}=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
for any

Lemma 6.1
.

The matrix
\operatorname{Adj}_{3}-0.217I_{6}
is a sum of squares in
\mathbb{M}_{6\times 6}(\mathbb{R}\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}))
.

.

Corollary 6.2

(cf.
Corollary

1.2
)
.

Let
\Delta_{1}
be the cohomological Laplacian in degree one of
G_{n}
. Then
\Delta_{1}-\lambda I_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}
is a sum of squares in
\mathbb{M}_{|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|\times|\altmathcal{S}_{n}|}(\mathbb{R}G_{n})
for
\lambda=0.217(n-2)
.

Proof.

Lemma

6.1
together with

5.5
show that

is a sum of squares in

. It follows by

4.3
that the matrices

and

are sums of squares. From this we conclude that

is a sum of squares in

:

∎

.

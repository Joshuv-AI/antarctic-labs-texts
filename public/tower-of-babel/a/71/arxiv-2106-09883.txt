On Logics of Perfect Paradefinite Algebras
Joel Gomes Vitor Greati, Sérgio Marcelino, João Marcos Umberto Rivieccio

Abstract
The present study shows how to enrich De Morgan algebras with a perfection operator that allows one to express the Boolean properties of negation-consistency and negation-determinedness. The variety of perfect paradefinite algebras thus obtained ( PP-algebras ) is shown to be term-equivalent to the variety of involutive Stone algebras, introduced by R. Cignoli and M. Sagastume, and more recently studied from a logical perspective by M. Figallo-L. Cantú and by S. Marcelino-U. Rivieccio. This equivalence plays an important role in the investigation of the 1 1 -assertional logic and of the order-preserving logic associated to PP-algebras. The latter logic (here called 𝒫 ​ 𝒫 ≤ \mathcal{PP}_{\leq} ) is characterized by a single 6-valued matrix and is shown to be a Logic of Formal Inconsistency and Formal Undeterminedness. We axiomatize 𝒫 ​ 𝒫 ≤ \mathcal{PP}_{\leq} by means of an analytic finite Hilbert-style calculus, and we present an axiomatization procedure that covers the logics corresponding to other classes of De Morgan algebras enriched by a perfection operator.

The variety of De Morgan algebras consists of all bounded distributive lattices equipped with a primitive involutive negation operation
{\sim}
satisfying the
well-known De Morgan laws.
Such negation need not be Boolean, that is, it may fail to satisy the equations
x\lor{\sim}x\approx\top
and
x\land{\sim}x\approx\bot
, respectively expressing the classical ‘negation-determinedness’ and the ‘negation-consistency’ assumptions.
Involutive Stone algebras (henceforth referred to as IS-algebras) are obtained by enriching De Morgan algebras with a further unary operation
\nabla
that allows for the definition of a pseudo-complement operator
\neg
satisfying the Stone equation
\neg x\lor\neg\neg x\approx\top
(as well as its dual,
\neg x\land\neg\neg x\approx\bot
).

While the order-preserving logic canonically induced by De Morgan algebras, namely Dunn-Belnap’s 4-valued logic
[
6
]
,
has been extensively studied over the last four decades, the logic similarly induced by IS-algebras (which we call
\mathcal{IS}_{\leq}
) has only recently attracted due attention
[
8
,
9
,
21
]
.
The latter studies make (but do not pursue to any significant length) an observation that we shall take as the starting point of our present work, namely that, by replacing
\nabla
with a unary ‘consistency operator’ (here denoted by
\circ
), it is possible to view
\mathcal{IS}_{\leq}
as a Logic of Formal Inconsistency (
[
11
]
).

From the point of view of non-classical logics,
some of the most prominent features of
\mathcal{IS}_{\leq}
are the facts that it is paradefinite
[
3
]
(it is, indeed, at once
{\sim}
-paraconsistent and
{\sim}
-paracomplete, both properties being inherited from the Dunn-Belnap logic), and yet, with the help of the single connective
\circ
, it may be seen to be expressive enough so as to fully recover the ‘lost perfection’ of classical negation,
by being at once
{\sim}
-gently explosive and
{\sim}
-gently implosive
[
23
]
;
in other words,
\mathcal{IS}_{\leq}
may be seen as a Logic of Formal Inconsistency (
LFI
) and a Logic of Formal Undeterminedness (
LFU
),
in the sense of
[
24
]
.
These features, however, are somehow concealed by the usual presentation of IS-algebras in terms of
\nabla
, an algebraic operator whose significance and philosophical motivations are unclear.

Logics that allow for the internalization of the very notions of negation-consistency and negation-determinedness at the object-language level have been extensively studied in the last two decades (cf.
[
5
]
, for example, for the so-called ‘classicality’, ‘restoration’, ‘recapture’, or ‘recovery’ operators).
In order to establish a fruitful dialogue with the logical study of negation, we thus propose
an alternative rendition of IS-algebras in terms of structures that we christen ‘perfect paradefinite algebras’ (or more briefly PP-algebras), obtained by replacing
\nabla
with a primitive perfection operation
{\circ}
.
The significance of such an approach, we believe, is not only nor primarily technical; instead, it lies mainly in a clarification of the intuitive meanings associated to the propositional connectives employed to present
\mathcal{IS}_{\leq}
. As explained below, another important consequence of our work will be the possibility of singling out new meaningful logical axioms (expressed in the alternative language we propose for
\mathcal{IS}_{\leq}
) that present more general logics than (i.e. weakenings of)
\mathcal{IS}_{\leq}
.

The equational characterization we present for PP-algebras will not only guarantee that the corresponding variety is term-equivalent to the variety of IS-algebras
but also highlight the expressive paradefinite character of the order-preserving logic thereby induced (
\mathcal{PP}_{\leq}
). The latter logic will be shown,
more specifically,
to constitute a fully self-extensional and non-protoalgebraic member of the families of logics known as
C
-systems and
D
-systems (detailed explanations and discussions about the latter classes may be found in
[
23
]
).
A procedure for constructing a PP-algebra using a De Morgan algebra as material is introduced and the logic
\mathcal{PP}_{\leq}
is shown to be characterizable, like
\mathcal{IS}_{\leq}

Lastly, we also provide a well-behaved symmetrical Hilbert-style calculus for the
Set-Set
logics determined by logical matrices based on De Morgan algebras enriched with
{\circ}
, as well as conventional Hilbert-style calculi for the
Set-Fmla
logics determined by logical matrices with prime filters based on De Morgan algebras enriched with

3
Perfect paradefinite algebras and their logics

3.1
Involutive Stone and PP-algebras

We propose in this section
to enrich De Morgan algebras by the addition of a perfection
operator
{\circ}
, which will allow us to recover
the classical properties of
{\sim}
-consistency and
{\sim}
-determinedness. In the sequel,
we will
prove that the variety
of the algebras thus obtained is term-equivalent to the variety of IS-algebras.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a
\Sigma^{\mathsf{PP}}
-algebra whose

\Sigma^{\mathsf{DM}}
-reduct is a De Morgan
algebra, we say that it
constitutes a
perfect paradefinite algebra
(
PP-algebra
) if it
satisfies the equations:

(PP1)

{\circ}{\circ}x\approx\top

(PP2)

\circ x\approx\circ{\sim}x

(PP3)

{\circ}\top\approx\top

(PP4)

x\land{\sim}x\land{\circ}x\approx\bot

(PP5)

\circ(x\land y)\approx(\circ x\lor\circ y)\land(\circ x\lor{\sim}y)\land(\circ y\lor{\sim}x)

Example 3.2
.

An example of PP-algebra is
\mathbf{PP}_{6}\,{\coloneqq}\,\langle\mathcal{V}_{6},\cdot^{\mathbf{PP}_{6}}\rangle
,
the
\Sigma^{\mathsf{PP}}
-algebra
defined as
\mathbf{IS}_{6}

in Example
2.4
,
differing only in that,
instead of containing an interpretation for

\nabla
, it interprets
{\circ}

as follows:

{\circ}^{\mathbf{PP}_{6}}a\,{\coloneqq}\,\begin{cases}\hat{\mathbf{f}}&a\in\mathcal{V}_{6}\setminus\{\hat{\mathbf{f}},\hat{\mathbf{t}}\}\\
\hat{\mathbf{t}}&a\in\{\hat{\mathbf{f}},\hat{\mathbf{t}}\}\\
\end{cases}

Other examples are the algebras
\mathbf{PP}_{i}
, for
2\leq i\leq 5
, the
subalgebras
of
\mathbf{PP}_{6}

having, respectively, the same lattice structures of the algebras
\mathbf{IS}_{i}
exhibited in Figure
1(b)
.

As it occurs with IS-algebras, in the language of PP-algebras
we may define,
by setting
\neg x\;\,{\coloneqq}\,\;{\circ}x\land{\sim}x
,
a pseudo-complement satisfying the Stone equation.
We denote by
\mathbb{PP}

the variety of
PP-algebras.
The following result
illustrates some useful
equations satisfied by the members
of
\mathbb{PP}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Every PP-algebra satisfies:

0

{\sim}{\circ}x\lor(x\lor{\sim}x)\approx\top

’

{\circ}x\land{\sim}{\circ}x\approx\bot

{\circ}x\approx{\circ}x\land(x\lor{\sim}x)

’

Proof.

Notice that 1 is a straightforward consequence
of
(PP4)
,
and 2 is a consequence of

(PP4)
using
{\circ}x

in place of

and invoking
(PP1)
.
Finally, 3 may be easily proved
using 1 and 2.
∎

Given
\varphi\in L_{\Sigma^{\mathsf{IS}}}(P)

(resp.
\varphi\in L_{\Sigma^{\mathsf{PP}}}(P)

let

(resp.

(resp. of

)

(resp. Theorem

Extend this notion
to sets of formulas
in the usual way.

.

Lemma 3.9

Given

and

1.

h

\mathbf{A}

4.

if

h

\mathbf{B}

then
the mapping
h^{\nabla}\in\mathsf{Hom}(\mathbf{L}_{\Sigma^{\mathsf{IS}}}(P),\mathbf{B}^{\nabla})

such that
h^{\nabla}(p)=h(p)

for all

h^{\nabla}(\varphi^{\nabla})=h(\varphi)
for all

Proof.

We will first discuss the proofs of items 1 and 3,
which may then be easily adapted, respectively, for proving items 2 and 4.
Both proofs are by structural induction on the set of formulas. Starting with 1, when
\varphi\in P
,
the result trivially
holds, as propositional variables are not affected
by translations.
In case
\varphi=\nabla\psi
,
if
h((\psi^{\circ})^{\nabla})=h(\psi)

h((\varphi^{\circ})^{\nabla}))=h(((\nabla\psi)^{\circ})^{\nabla})

From the argument in the proof of Theorem
3.6

we know that
((\nabla\psi)^{\circ})^{\nabla}

and
\nabla\psi

h(((\nabla\psi)^{\circ})^{\nabla})=\nabla(h(\psi))=h(\varphi)

The proof is analogous for
the cases of
\land,\lor,{\sim},\top
and
\bot

Now, for item 3, the base case is again obvious,
and, in case

,
we have
h^{\circ}((\nabla\psi)^{\circ})=h^{\circ}({\sim}{\circ}\psi^{\circ}\lor\psi^{\circ})={\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}h^{\circ}(\psi^{\circ})\lor^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}h^{\circ}(\psi^{\circ})

and, by the induction hypothesis,
the latter is equal to

{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}h(\psi)\lor^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}h(\psi)

this is the same as
h(\nabla\psi)

in
(\mathbf{A}^{\circ})^{\nabla}

with
\mathbf{A}
by Theorem
3.6

The proof is again analogous for

\land,\lor,{\sim},\top
and
\bot

∎

The former result allows us to prove the following auxiliary facts:

Proposition 3.10

\Phi^{\nabla}\;\rhd_{\mathcal{IS}_{\leq}}\;\Psi^{\nabla}

4.

\Phi\;{\rhd^{\top}_{\mathbb{PP}}}\;\Psi
iff
\Phi^{\nabla}\;{\rhd^{\top}_{\mathbb{IS}}}\;\Psi^{\nabla}

Proof.

We start by proving item 1. From the left to the right, suppose that

The other direction

3.3

We now present a recipe for constructing a perfect paradefinite algebra by endowing a De Morgan algebra with a perfection operator.

Definition 3.15
.

Let
\mathbf{A}

be a
\Sigma^{\mathsf{DM}}
-algebra. Given
\hat{\mathbf{f}},\hat{\mathbf{t}}\notin A
, we define the
\Sigma^{\mathsf{PP}}
-algebra
\mathbf{A}^{\circ}\,{\coloneqq}\,\langle A\cup\{\hat{\mathbf{f}},\hat{\mathbf{t}}\},\cdot^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}\rangle
by letting:

\displaystyle a\land^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}b

\displaystyle\,{\coloneqq}\,\begin{cases}a\land^{\mathbf{A}}b&\text{if }a,b\in A\\
\hat{\mathbf{t}}&\text{if }a=b=\hat{\mathbf{t}}\\
\hat{\mathbf{f}}&\text{if }a=\hat{\mathbf{f}}\text{ or }b=\hat{\mathbf{f}}\\
c&\text{if }\{a,b\}=\{\hat{\mathbf{t}},c\}\text{ with }c\in A\end{cases}

\displaystyle a\lor^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}b

\displaystyle\,{\coloneqq}\,\begin{cases}a\lor^{\mathbf{A}}b&\text{if }a,b\in A\\
\hat{\mathbf{f}}&\text{if }a=b=\hat{\mathbf{f}}\\
\hat{\mathbf{t}}&\text{if }a=\hat{\mathbf{t}}\text{ or }b=\hat{\mathbf{t}}\\
c&\text{if }\{a,b\}=\{\hat{\mathbf{f}},c\}\text{ with }c\in A\end{cases}

\displaystyle{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a

\displaystyle\,{\coloneqq}\,\begin{cases}{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}}a&\text{if }a\in A\\
\hat{\mathbf{f}}&\text{if }a=\hat{\mathbf{t}}\\
\hat{\mathbf{t}}&\text{if }a=\hat{\mathbf{f}}\end{cases}

\displaystyle{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a

\displaystyle\,{\coloneqq}\,\begin{cases}\hat{\mathbf{t}}&\text{if }a=\hat{\mathbf{f}}\text{ or }a=\hat{\mathbf{t}}\\
\hat{\mathbf{f}}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\bot^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}

\displaystyle\,{\coloneqq}\,\hat{\mathbf{f}}

\displaystyle\top^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}

\displaystyle\,{\coloneqq}\,\hat{\mathbf{t}}

In addition, we define
the
\Sigma^{\mathsf{IS}}
-algebra
\mathbf{A}^{\nabla}\,{\coloneqq}\,\langle A\cup\{\hat{\mathbf{f}},\hat{\mathbf{t}}\},\cdot^{\mathbf{A}^{\nabla}}\rangle

interpreting the connectives in
\Sigma^{\mathsf{DM}}
as above,
while letting
\nabla^{\mathbf{A}^{\nabla}}a\,{\coloneqq}\,\hat{\mathbf{f}}
if
a=\hat{\mathbf{f}}
and
\nabla^{\mathbf{A}^{\nabla}}a\,{\coloneqq}\,\hat{\mathbf{t}}
otherwise (cf.
[
21
]
).

Proposition 3.16
.

If

\mathbf{A}
is a De Morgan algebra,
then

\mathbf{A}^{\circ}
is
a PP-algebra.

Proof.

When
\mathbf{A}
is a De Morgan algebra,
it is clear that the
\Sigma^{\mathsf{DM}}
-reduct of
\mathbf{A}^{\circ}
is also a De Morgan algebra.
Moreover, the operation
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
defined above
satisfies all equations presented in Definition
3.1
, as we confirm below:

(PP1)

By the definition of
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
, we have either
(1)
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a=\hat{\mathbf{t}}
or (2)
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a=\hat{\mathbf{f}}
.
In both cases we have
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a=\hat{\mathbf{t}}=\top^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
.

(PP2)

By the definition of
{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
, we have that

a\in\{\hat{\mathbf{f}},\hat{\mathbf{t}}\}
iff
{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a\in\{\hat{\mathbf{f}},\hat{\mathbf{t}}\}
.
Also, we have either (1)
a\in\{\hat{\mathbf{f}},\hat{\mathbf{t}}\}
or (2)
a\notin\{\hat{\mathbf{f}},\hat{\mathbf{t}}\}

If (1) is the case, then
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a={\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a=\hat{\mathbf{t}}
;
alternatively, if (2) is the case, then
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a={\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a=\hat{\mathbf{f}}
.

(PP3)

By the definition of
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
and
\top^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
, we have that

{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}\top^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}={\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}\hat{\mathbf{t}}=\hat{\mathbf{t}}=\top^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
.

(PP4)

By the definition of
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
, we have either
(1)
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a=\hat{\mathbf{t}}
or (2)
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a=\hat{\mathbf{f}}
.
If (1) is the case, we have either (1.1)
a=\hat{\mathbf{f}}
or (1.2)
a=\hat{\mathbf{t}}
, then:
If (1.2) is the case, then, by the definition of
{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
,
{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a=\hat{\mathbf{f}}
.
In all cases we have that at least one among
a,{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a
and
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a
is
\hat{\mathbf{f}}
. Then, by the definition of
\land^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
, we have
a\land{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a\land^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a=\hat{\mathbf{f}}=\bot^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
.

(PP5)

By the definition of
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
, we have that either
(1)
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}(a\land^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}b)=\hat{\mathbf{t}}
or (2)
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}(a\land^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}b)=\hat{\mathbf{f}}
.
If (1) is the case, we have either (1.1)
a\land^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}b=\hat{\mathbf{f}}
or (1.2)
a\land^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}b=\hat{\mathbf{t}}
.
If (1.1) is the case, then, by the definition of
\land^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
, we have either (1.1.1)
a=\hat{\mathbf{f}}
or (1.1.2)
b=\hat{\mathbf{f}}
.
If (1.1.1) is the case, then, by the definition of
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
and
{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}

{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a=\hat{\mathbf{t}}
and
{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a=\hat{\mathbf{t}}
.
The case (1.1.2) is similar to (1.1.1).
If (1.2) is the case, then, by the definition of
\land^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}

a=b=\hat{\mathbf{t}}

By the definition of
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
, we have
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a={\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}b=\hat{\mathbf{t}}
.
Hence, in all subcases of (1) we have, by the definition of
\lor^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}

{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a\lor^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}b={\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a\lor^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}b={\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}b\lor{\sim}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a=\hat{\mathbf{t}}
.
If (2) is the case, we have,
a\land^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}b\notin\{\hat{\mathbf{f}},\hat{\mathbf{t}}\}
.
Then, by the definition of
\land^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}

a,b\notin\{\hat{\mathbf{f}},\hat{\mathbf{t}}\}
or (2.2) both
a=\hat{\mathbf{t}}
and
b\not\in\{\hat{\mathbf{f}},\hat{\mathbf{t}}\}

b=\hat{\mathbf{t}}
and
a\not\in\{\hat{\mathbf{f}},\hat{\mathbf{t}}\}
.

{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}
, we have
{\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}a={\circ}^{\mathbf{A}^{\circ}}b=\hat{\mathbf{f}}
.
If (2.2) is the case, then, by the definitions of

and

\Sigma^{\mathsf{DM}}
-reduct of
\mathfrak{M}^{{\circ}}

Furthermore, given a class of

Whenever
\vdash
is a super-Belnap logic, denote by
\vdash^{\circ}
the logic determined by the family of matrices

\{\mathfrak{M}^{{\circ}}\mid\mathfrak{M}\text{ is a that non-trivial model of }\vdash\}

The series of results presented in

just defined

of extensions of the logic
\mathcal{PP}_{\leq}

This allows us, in particular, to

\mathcal{B}

Before introducing the results,
we recall some helpful
definitions from abstract algebraic logic
[
16
]
.
Given a
\Sigma
-matrix

\mathfrak{M}=\langle\mathbf{A},D\rangle

a congruence
\theta\in\ConSet\mathbf{A}

is said to be
compatible with

\mathfrak{M}

when
b\in D
whenever both
a\in D

and
a\theta b

a,b\in A

We denote by
\Omega^{\mathfrak{M}}

associated to
\mathfrak{M}
,
namely
the greatest congruence of

\mathbf{A}

\mathfrak{M}

The matrix
{\mathfrak{M}}^{\ast}=\langle\mathbf{A}/\Omega^{\mathfrak{M}},D/\Omega^{\mathfrak{M}}\rangle

is the
reduced version
of
\mathfrak{M}
.
We say that
\mathfrak{M}
is

reduced
when it coincides with its own reduced version
(or, equivalently, when its Leibniz
congruence is the identity relation
on
A
).
It is well known that

\rhd_{\mathfrak{M}}=\rhd_{{\mathfrak{M}}^{\ast}}

(and thus
\vdash_{\mathfrak{M}}\;=\;\vdash_{{\mathfrak{M}}^{\ast}}
)
and, since every logic is determined by a class of matrix models, we have that every logic coincides
with the logic determined by
its reduced matrix models.
The class of all reduced
matrix models for a logic
\vdash

is denoted by
\mathsf{Mat^{\ast}}(\vdash)
.

Let

Corollary 3.22

5
Final remarks

We have seen how to endow with a perfection connective logics characterized by matrices having a De Morgan algebraic reduct, offering two possible directions: either by appropriately expanding the corresponding matrices or by adding new rules of inference to an existing Hilbert-style axiomatization.
In particular, by so enriching Dunn-Belnap’s 4-valued logic we obtained the 6-valued order-preserving logic
\mathcal{PP}_{\leq}
, associated to the variety of expanded algebras, which we called ‘perfect paradefinite algebras’ and proved to be term-equivalent with the variety of involutive Stone algebras. It is worth mentioning that the one-one correspondence between both varieties can be used to introduce back-and-forth functors
that establish a categorical equivalence
between the corresponding algebraic categories.

By providing a Derivability Adjustment Theorem for
\mathcal{PP}_{\leq}
and its extensions, we have also shown that Boolean reasoning is fully recovered using De Morgan negation and the perfection operator.
Notice, indeed, that adding the equation
{\circ}x\approx\top
to a perfect paradefinite algebra, intuitively stating
that every element is
Boolean,
what results
in an
algebra that is (term-equivalent to) a Boolean algebra.

The equational basis of the variety of PP-algebras studied here was conceived having in mind the expected term-equivalence with the variety of IS-algebras.
A natural path for future work is to drop this constraint and study De Morgan algebras enriched with perfection operators satisfying weaker equations,
and the corresponding logics.
Within such more general algebraic structures and the logics based thereon, and taking also into account the proposed comparison between
\mathcal{PP}_{\leq}
and other
C
-systems and
D
-systems in the literature, one may for instance consider two distinct negations (not necessarily respecting all De Morgan laws) instead of a single one that is at once paraconsistent and paracomplete, and possibly also two separate ‘recovery connectives’, as, e.g., in
[
14
,
19
]
.

Yet another direction for future investigation would be to enrich
\mathcal{PP}_{\leq}
with an implication connective;
similar paths have recently been explored in
[
15
]
, but considering involutive distributive residuated lattices instead of De Morgan algebras.
A promising starting point for this research could be provided by the following observation.
The algebra
\mathbf{PP_{6}}
, as a finite distributive lattice, has
an implicitly definable (and unique) intuitionistic implication given by the relative pseudo-complement. By adding this operation to the propositional language and considering the corresponding logical matrix, one thus obtains a conservative extension of
\mathcal{PP}_{\leq}
by the intuitionistic implication. We expect this logic to be algebraizable, but not necessarily self-extensional. On the other hand, a (weaker) self-extensional conservative extension of
\mathcal{PP}_{\leq}
may be obtained by considering the logic determined by the family of all matrices of type
\langle\mathbf{PP_{6}},D\rangle

based on
\mathbf{PP_{6}}
(endowed with the relative pseudo-complement operation), with
D
a lattice filter.
Logics obtained in this way will have
as algebraic counterparts (subclasses of)
algebras that
carry both
a De Morgan negation and an intuitionistic implication: these structures have been studied in the literature under the names of

symmetric Heyting algebras
(A. Monteiro
[
26
]
)
and
De Morgan-Heyting algebras

(H.P. Sankappanavar
[
29
]
). From a technical point of view, an advantage of the proposed approach is thus one may hope
to be able to
import results
from the well-developed theory of the above-mentined classes of algebras.

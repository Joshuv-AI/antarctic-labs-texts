Stephen Ebert, Hao-Yu Sun, and Zhengdi Sun

Abstract
We calculate the 𝒮 \mathcal{S} -multiplets for two-dimensional Euclidean 𝒩 = ( 0 , 2 ) \mathcal{N}=(0,2) and 𝒩 = ( 2 , 2 ) \mathcal{N}=(2,2) superconformal field theories under the T ​ T ¯ T\overline{T} deformation at leading order of perturbation theory in the deformation coupling. Then, from these 𝒩 = ( 0 , 2 ) \mathcal{N}=(0,2) deformed multiplets, we calculate two- and three-point correlators. We show the 𝒩 = ( 0 , 2 ) \mathcal{N}=(0,2) chiral ring’s elements do not flow under the T ​ T ¯ T\overline{T} deformation. Specializing to integrable supersymmetric seed theories, such as 𝒩 = ( 2 , 2 ) \mathcal{N}=(2,2) Landau-Ginzburg models, we use the thermodynamic Bethe ansatz to study the S-matrices and ground state energies. From both an S-matrix perspective and Melzer’s folding prescription, we show that the deformed ground state energy obeys the inviscid Burgers’ equation. Finally, we show that several indices independent of D D -term perturbations including the Witten index, Cecotti-Fendley-Intriligator-Vafa index and elliptic genus do not flow under the T ​ T ¯ T\overline{T} deformation.

Background

Recent attention has been drawn toward irrelevant deformations of two-dimensional quantum field theories (QFTs) and applications in holography. Unlike marginal or relevant deformations, irrelevant deformations in QFTs are notoriously arduous to study due to the requirement of including infinitely many counterterms to the action, and understanding the ultraviolet physics of the system becomes highly ambiguous. It was not until Zamolodchikov shed light on this subject matter by deriving his novel composite operator

2.3
Deformed two-point correlators

We extract the SCFT two-point functions from the OPEs (
21
) and (
22
):

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{S}_{z}(Z_{1})\mathcal{S}_{z}(Z_{2})\rangle_{0}=\frac{c}{3Z_{12}^{2}}.

(24)

In components, (
24
) is

\displaystyle\langle T_{zz}(z)T_{zz}(0)\rangle_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2z^{4}},

(25)

\displaystyle\langle T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(z)T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(0)\rangle_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2\overline{z}^{4}},

\displaystyle\langle S_{z}(z)\overline{S}_{z}(0)\rangle_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2c}{3z^{3}},

\displaystyle\langle j_{z}(z)j_{z}(0)\rangle_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{3z^{2}}.

To derive the deformed two-point functions, we follow the method in
Kraus:2018xrn
and §
2.1
. Since we are working in perturbation theory, all new terms appearing in the correlation functions must be a function of
y=\frac{z\overline{z}}{\lambda}
and vanish in the limit
\lambda\rightarrow 0
. Also, all the correlation functions we consider are at separate spacetime points, so we will drop present contact terms. The operator equations

\mathcal{R}_{\overline{z}}=-\pi\lambda\mathcal{S}^{(0)}_{z}T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}^{(0)}+O(\lambda^{2})

(26)

allow us to derive the deformed two-point functions in superspace formalism

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{R}_{\overline{z}}(Z_{1})\mathcal{R}_{\overline{z}}(Z_{2})\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}\langle\mathcal{R}_{z}(Z_{1})\mathcal{R}_{z}(Z_{2})\rangle_{0}\langle T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(z_{1})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(z_{2})\rangle_{0}+O(\lambda^{3})

(27)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}c^{2}}{6}\frac{1}{Z_{12}^{2}\overline{z}_{12}^{4}}+O(\lambda^{3}).

Then, from the conservation equation
\partial_{z}\mathcal{R}_{\overline{z}}+\partial_{\overline{z}}\mathcal{R}_{z}=0
, we find

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{R}_{z}(Z_{1})\mathcal{R}_{\overline{z}}(Z_{2})\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}c^{2}}{9}\frac{1}{Z_{12}^{3}\overline{z}_{12}^{3}}+O(\lambda^{3}),

(28)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{R}_{z}(Z_{1})\mathcal{R}_{z}(Z_{2})\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{3Z_{12}^{2}}-\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}c^{2}}{6}\frac{1}{Z_{12}^{4}\overline{z}_{12}^{2}}+O(\lambda^{3}).

We collect the non-zero two-point functions in component fields below:

\displaystyle\langle j_{\overline{z}}(z)j_{\overline{z}}(0)\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}c^{2}}{6}\frac{1}{z^{2}\overline{z}^{4}}+O(\lambda^{3}),

(29)

\displaystyle\langle j_{z}(z)j_{\overline{z}}(0)\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}c^{2}}{9}\frac{1}{z^{3}\overline{z}^{3}}+O(\lambda^{3}),

\displaystyle\langle j_{z}(z)j_{z}(0)\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{3z^{2}}+\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}c^{2}}{6}\frac{1}{z^{4}\overline{z}^{2}}+O(\lambda^{3}),

\displaystyle\langle S_{\overline{z}}(z)\overline{S}_{\overline{z}}(0)\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}c^{2}\lambda^{2}}{3}\frac{1}{z^{3}\overline{z}^{4}}+O(\lambda^{3}),

(30)

\displaystyle\langle S_{z}(z)\overline{S}_{\overline{z}}(0)\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi^{2}c^{2}\lambda^{2}}{3}\frac{1}{z^{4}\overline{z}^{3}}+O(\lambda^{3}),

\displaystyle\langle S_{\overline{z}}(z)\overline{S}_{z}(0)\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi^{2}c^{2}\lambda^{2}}{3}\frac{1}{z^{4}\overline{z}^{3}}+O(\lambda^{3}),

\displaystyle\langle S_{z}(z)\overline{S}_{z}(0)\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{2c}{3z^{3}}+\frac{2\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}c^{2}}{3}\frac{1}{z^{5}\overline{z}^{2}}+O(\lambda^{3}),

\displaystyle\langle T_{zz}(z)T_{zz}(0)\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2z^{4}}+\frac{5\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}c^{2}}{6}\frac{1}{z^{6}\overline{z}^{2}}+O(\lambda^{3}),

(31)

\displaystyle\langle T_{zz}(z)T_{z\overline{z}}(0)\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}c^{2}}{3}\frac{1}{z^{5}\overline{z}^{3}}+O(\lambda^{3}),

\displaystyle\langle T_{zz}(z)T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(0)\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}c^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{z^{4}\overline{z}^{4}}+O(\lambda^{3}),

\displaystyle\langle T_{z\overline{z}}(z)T_{z\overline{z}}(0)\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}c^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{z^{4}\overline{z}^{4}}+O(\lambda^{3}).

We can see there is no space-filling brane current
C
at leading order in
\lambda
from the vanishing of the two point function

\langle S_{z}(z)S_{\overline{z}}(0)\rangle_{\lambda}=0,

(32)

because as pointed out in
Dumitrescu:2011iu
,

appears in the two-point function as

\langle S_{z}(z)S_{\overline{z}}(0)\rangle\sim\frac{C}{\overline{z}}.

(33)

This is consistent with the existence of

.

2.4
Deformed three-point correlators

Before we calculate the three-point correlators for the deformed
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
SCFT, we first write down all the non-zero three-point correlators in the undeformed case. Reading off from the OPEs (
22
), the undeformed three-point correlators are:

\displaystyle\langle T_{zz}(x_{1})T_{zz}(x_{2})T_{zz}(x_{3})\rangle_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{z_{12}^{2}z_{23}^{2}z_{31}^{2}},

(34)

\displaystyle\langle T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{1})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{2})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{3})\rangle_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{\overline{z}_{12}^{2}\overline{z}_{23}^{2}\overline{z}_{31}^{2}},

\displaystyle\langle T_{zz}(x_{1})S_{z}(z_{2})\overline{S}_{z}(z_{3})\rangle_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{z_{12}^{2}z_{31}^{2}z_{31}},

\displaystyle\langle j_{z}(z_{1})S_{z}(z_{2})\overline{S}_{z}(z_{3})\rangle_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2c}{3}\frac{1}{z_{12}z_{31}z_{23}^{2}},

\displaystyle\langle T_{zz}(z_{1})j_{z}(z_{2})j_{z}(z_{3})\rangle_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{3}\frac{1}{z_{12}^{2}z_{31}^{2}}.

Next, we consider the deformed three-point correlators
\langle O_{1}(z_{1})O_{2}(z_{2})O_{3}(z_{3})\rangle_{\lambda}
at
O(\lambda)
. The three-point correlators fall into two separate classes:

1.

We could have one of
O_{i}
, say
O_{1}
, being an operator which vanishes in the SCFT, or at the superconformal point
(e.g.,
T_{z\overline{z}}
,
j_{\overline{z}}
,
\mathcal{R}_{\overline{z}}
),
and the rest of them being the operators which survive in the
SCFT (e.g.,
T_{zz}
,
T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}
,
\mathcal{R}_{z}
).

2.

We could have all three of them being the operators which survive in the
SCFT.

For the first case, at the first order in perturbation theory, we obtain a four-point correlator in the original SCFT because the first operator in the three-point correlator is a product of holomorphic and anti-holomorphic operators
T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}
. The rest of the two operators must be the same holomorphic operator and
T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}
in order to have a non-vanishing result.

For the first case, correlators can be easily computed using superspace notation:

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{R}_{\overline{z}}(Z_{1})\mathcal{R}_{z}(Z_{2})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(z_{3})\rangle_{\lambda}&=-\pi\lambda\langle\mathcal{R}_{z}(Z_{1})\mathcal{R}_{z}(Z_{2})\rangle\langle T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(z_{1})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(z_{3})\rangle+O(\lambda^{2})\\
&=-\frac{\pi\lambda c^{2}}{6Z_{12}^{2}\overline{z}_{31}^{4}}+O(\lambda^{2}),\end{split}

(35)

and we collect the three-point functions in component fields at the end of this subsection.

For the second case, we obtain a five-point correlator in the undeformed SCFT because one of the operators has to be
T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}
which is anti-holomorphic. We must have another one or two operators being
T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}
to see a non-vanishing result since the five-point function factorizes into a product of correlation functions of holomorphic and anti-holomorphic operators. If the deformed three-point correlator contains two
T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}
, then

\displaystyle\langle O_{1}(x_{1})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{2})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{3})\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\pi\lambda\int d^{2}x\langle O_{1}(x_{1})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{2})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{3})T_{zz}(x)T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x)\rangle_{0}+O(\lambda^{2})

(36)

\displaystyle=-\pi\lambda\int d^{2}x\langle O_{1}(x_{1})T_{zz}(x)\rangle_{0}\langle T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{2})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{3})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x)\rangle_{0}+O(\lambda^{2}).

To calculate a non-zero result, we must set
O_{1}=T_{zz}
and obtain what
Kraus:2018xrn
found :

\langle T_{zz}(x_{1})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{2})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{3})\rangle_{\lambda}=-\frac{\pi\lambda c^{2}}{3}\frac{1}{z_{12}^{3}}\frac{1}{\overline{z}_{23}^{5}}-\frac{\pi\lambda c^{2}}{3}\frac{1}{z_{31}^{3}\overline{z}_{23}^{5}}+O(\lambda^{2}).

(37)

If it contains only one of
T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}
, then we have

\displaystyle\langle O_{1}(x_{1})O_{2}(x_{2})\overline{T}_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{3})\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\pi\lambda\int d^{2}x\langle O_{1}(x_{1})O_{2}(x_{2})\overline{T}_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{3})T_{zz}(x)\overline{T}_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x)\rangle_{0}+O(\lambda^{2})

(38)

\displaystyle=-\pi\lambda\int d^{2}x\langle O_{1}(x_{1})O_{2}(x_{2})T_{zz}(x)\rangle_{0}\langle\overline{T}_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{3})\overline{T}_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x)\rangle_{0}+O(\lambda^{2}).

To obtain a non-zero result, we must have
\langle O_{1}(x_{1})O_{2}(x_{2})T(x)\rangle_{0}\neq 0
. Using (
34
) and the trace flow equation as systematically carried out in
Kraus:2018xrn
for the deformed three-point correlators at
O(\lambda)
, we summarize all the non-vanishing three-point correlators:

\displaystyle\langle j_{\overline{z}}(x_{1})j_{z}(x_{2})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{3})\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi\lambda c^{2}}{6}\frac{1}{z_{12}^{2}\overline{z}_{31}^{4}}+O(\lambda^{2}),

(39)

\displaystyle\langle S_{\overline{z}}(x_{1})\overline{S}_{z}(x_{2})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{3})\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi\lambda c^{2}}{6}\frac{1}{z_{12}^{3}\overline{z}_{31}^{4}}+O(\lambda^{2}),

\displaystyle\langle\overline{S}_{\overline{z}}(x_{1})S_{z}(x_{2})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{3})\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi\lambda c^{2}}{6}\frac{1}{z_{12}^{3}\overline{z}_{31}^{4}}+O(\lambda^{2}),

\displaystyle\langle j_{z}(x_{1})j_{z}(x_{2})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{3})\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\pi\lambda c^{2}}{9}\bigg(\frac{1}{\overline{z}_{23}^{3}z_{12}^{3}}+\frac{1}{z_{12}^{3}\overline{z}_{31}^{3}}\bigg)+O(\lambda^{2}),

\displaystyle\langle S_{z}(x_{1})\overline{S}_{z}(x_{2})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{3})\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi\lambda c^{2}}{3}\bigg(\frac{1}{z_{12}^{5}\overline{z}_{31}^{3}}+\frac{1}{\overline{z}_{23}^{3}z_{12}^{5}}\bigg)+O(\lambda^{2}),

\displaystyle\langle T_{zz}(x_{1})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{2})T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(x_{3})\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi\lambda c^{2}}{3}\bigg(\frac{1}{z_{12}^{3}}\frac{1}{\overline{z}_{23}^{5}}+\frac{1}{z_{31}^{3}\overline{z}_{23}^{5}}\bigg)+O(\lambda^{2}).

2.5
Deformed
n
-point correlators of other operators

We briefly comment on how to compute
n
-point correlators between
n
generic supermultiplets using conformal perturbation theory. An analysis for the deformed
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
and
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
supersymmetric correlators were done by
He:2019ahx
. Recall that
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
superspace are described by holomorphic and anti-holomorphic coordinates
(Z,\widetilde{Z})=(z,\theta,\bar{\theta},\bar{z},\widetilde{\theta},\bar{\widetilde{\theta}})
. To obtain
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
, we simply set these two fermionic coordinates to zero
\widetilde{\theta}=\bar{\widetilde{\theta}}=0
in
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
superspace. A generic
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
superfield is given by
Kiritsis:1987np
:

\Phi_{i}(Z_{i})=\phi_{i}(z_{i})+\theta_{i}\overline{\psi}_{i}(z_{i})+\overline{\theta}_{i}\psi_{i}(z_{i})+\theta\overline{\theta}g_{i}(z_{i}),

(40)

where
\phi_{i}(z),g_{i}(z_{i})
are complex scalar fields and
\psi_{i}(z_{i})
is a complex spinor. Therefore, at leading order in
\lambda
, the deformed
n
-point correlators are simply given by

\left\langle\prod^{n}_{i=1}\Phi_{i}(Z_{i},\overline{Z}_{i})\right\rangle_{\lambda}=-\lambda\int d^{2}z\int d^{2}\theta\left\langle\mathcal{S}^{(0)}(Z)T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(\overline{z})\prod^{n}_{i=1}\Phi_{i}(Z_{i},\overline{Z}_{i})\right\rangle_{0},

(41)

\int d^{2}\theta~\mathcal{S}^{(0)}(Z)=T_{zz}(z).

(42)

\mathcal{N}=(2,2)

by setting
\tilde{\theta}=\bar{\tilde{\theta}}=0
.

2.6
Renormalized correlators and universality

An interesting question to ask is if one can define
renormalized
operators as in
Cardy:2019qao
;
Kraus:2018xrn
while respecting supersymmetry. This proof is quite obvious in superfield formalism inspired from
He:2019ahx
. The singular piece in the dimension regularization parameter
\epsilon
for the deformed two-point correlator is

\langle\Phi_{1}(Z_{1},\overline{z}_{1})\Phi_{2}(Z_{2},\overline{z}_{2})\rangle_{\lambda}=-\frac{16\pi\lambda}{\epsilon}\bigg(\frac{2h}{Z_{12}}+Q_{2}\frac{\theta_{12}\overline{\theta}_{12}}{z_{12}^{2}}\bigg)\frac{2\overline{h}}{\overline{z}_{12}}\langle\Phi_{1}(Z_{1},\overline{z}_{1})\Phi_{2}(Z_{2},\overline{z}_{2})\rangle_{0},

(43)

where the undeformed two-point correlator is

\langle\Phi_{1}(Z_{1},\overline{z}_{1})\Phi_{2}(Z_{2},\overline{z}_{2})\rangle_{0}=\frac{1}{Z_{12}^{2h}\overline{z}_{12}^{2\overline{h}}}e^{Q_{2}\frac{\theta_{12}\overline{\theta}_{12}}{Z_{12}}}\delta_{Q_{1}+Q_{2},0}.

(44)

Here
(h_{1},\overline{h}_{1})=(h_{2},\overline{h}_{2})=(h,\overline{h})
are the scaling dimensions and
Q_{1}+Q_{2}=\overline{Q}_{1}+\overline{Q}_{2}=0
are the charges of
\Phi_{1}(Z_{1},z_{1})
and
\Phi_{2}(Z_{2},z_{2})
.

We define the
renormalized
superfield as

\Phi_{R}=\Phi-\frac{A\lambda}{\epsilon}\{D,\overline{D}\}\partial_{\overline{z}}\Phi=\Phi-\frac{2A\lambda}{\epsilon}\partial_{z}\partial_{\overline{z}}\Phi

(45)

in order to remove the above singular piece. In
Kraus:2018xrn
, for a general operator
\mathcal{O}
with scaling dimension
(h_{\mathcal{O}},\overline{h}_{\mathcal{O}})
, we have

\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}(x_{2})T(x)\overline{T}(x)\rangle_{0}=h_{\mathcal{O}}\overline{h}_{\mathcal{O}}\bigg(\frac{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}{(x-x_{1})^{2}(x-x_{2})^{2}}\bigg)^{2}\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}(x_{2})\rangle_{0}.

(46)

So, the deformed two-point correlators differ only by an overall coefficient
h_{\mathcal{O}}\overline{h}_{\mathcal{O}}
and this matches the coefficients one obtains from acting on the undeformed two-point correlators with
\partial_{z}\partial_{\overline{z}}
:

\partial_{z}\partial_{\overline{z}}\frac{1}{z^{2h_{\mathcal{O}}}\overline{z}^{2\overline{h}_{\mathcal{O}}}}=\frac{4h_{\mathcal{O}}\overline{h}_{\mathcal{O}}}{z^{2h_{\mathcal{O}}+1}\overline{z}^{2\overline{h}_{\mathcal{O}}+1}},

(47)

which implies the coefficient
A
is universal for every operator. This allows us to embed the
T\overline{T}
deformation into superfield formalism, and thus preserve supersymmetry. As a preview to the upcoming subsection, we will see that such a renormalization is absent for the
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
chiral ring elements
\Phi
which satisfy
\partial_{\overline{z}}\Phi=0
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{z}^{A}(Z_{1})\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{z}^{B}(Z_{2})

0&\text{ otherwise }\end{array}\quad k=c_{R}-c_{L}\right.

(51)

Under the
T\overline{T}
deformation, we need to accompany
\mathcal{J}_{z}^{(0)}
with

\mathcal{J}_{\overline{z}}=-\pi\lambda\mathcal{J}_{z}^{(0)}T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}+O(\lambda^{2})

(52)

such that
7
7

7

As one can easily check
\overline{D}\mathcal{J}_{\overline{z}}=D\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{\overline{z}}=0
remains chiral.

\partial_{z}\mathcal{J}_{\overline{z}}+\partial_{\overline{z}}\mathcal{J}_{z}=0.

(53)

Then, just as before in the previous subsections, one can write down the deformed current two-point correlator

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{\overline{z}}^{A}(Z_{1})\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{\overline{z}}^{B}(Z_{2})\right\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{z}^{A}(Z_{1})\overline{\mathcal{J}}_{z}^{B}(Z_{2})\right\rangle_{0}\left\langle\overline{T}_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(z_{1})\overline{T}_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(z_{2})\right\rangle_{0}+O(\lambda^{3})

(54)

\displaystyle=\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}\frac{k^{AB}}{\overline{z}^{4}_{12}}\bigg(\frac{1}{Z_{12}}-\frac{\theta_{12}\overline{\theta}_{12}}{Z_{12}^{2}}\bigg)+O(\lambda^{3}).

Likewise, how conservation equations were used to find the other
\mathcal{R}
correlators in (
28
) in principle, one can perform the same trick for the other current correlators
\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{z}^{A}(Z_{1})\mathcal{J}_{z}^{B}(Z_{2})\right\rangle_{\lambda}
and
\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{z}^{A}(Z_{1})\mathcal{J}_{\overline{z}}^{B}(Z_{2})\right\rangle_{\lambda}
.

2.7.2
Deformed two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
chiral ring

Another interesting question to analyze is the
T\overline{T}
deformed chiral ring elements for two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
SCFTs. These operators are superconformal primaries that are annihilated by half of the superconformal charges,
G^{+}_{n}
or
G^{-}_{n}
, and saturate the unitarity bound
h=|q|/2
. Under the deformation, superconformal symmetry is broken and the full short representation splits into representations of the surviving supersymmetry. Hence, a natural question one can address is whether the resulting representations are chiral? To show whether half of the supercharges annihilate these deformed operators, one can compute OPE between the deformed supercurrent and the deformed chiral operators at leading order in
\lambda
. Let
\Phi^{(0)}
be an element of the two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
chiral ring and is annihilated by
G^{-}_{z}
:
G_{-1/2}^{-}|\Phi^{(0)}\rangle=0
. Then, we have the OPEs

\displaystyle G^{-}_{z}{}^{(0)}(z)\Phi(0)

\displaystyle\sim 0,

(55)

\displaystyle T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}^{(0)}(z)\Phi(0)

\displaystyle\sim 0.

Now by using the deformation equation,
G^{-}_{\overline{z}}=-\pi\lambda G^{-}_{z}{}^{(0)}T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}{}^{(0)}+O(\lambda^{2})
, we find

G^{-}_{\overline{z}}(z)\Phi(0)=-\pi\lambda G^{-}_{z}{}^{(0)}(z)T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}(z)\Phi(0)^{(0)}+O(\lambda^{2})\sim O(\lambda)^{2}.

(56)

From also using

\delta\Phi(0)=\pi\lambda\int_{0}^{X}d\overline{z}^{\prime}T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}{}^{(0)}(z^{\prime})\partial_{z}\Phi(0)+O(\lambda^{2})

(57)

where
X
is an arbitrary reference point and

\delta T_{zz}(z)=\pi\lambda\int_{z}^{X}d\overline{z}^{\prime}T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}^{(0)}(z^{\prime})\partial_{z}G^{-}_{z}{}^{(0)}(z)+O(\lambda^{2}),

(58)

we obtain

\delta(G_{z}^{-}(z)\Phi(0))=[\delta G_{z}^{-}(z)]\Phi^{(0)}(0)+G_{z}^{-}{}^{(0)}(z)\delta\Phi(0)\sim 0+O(\lambda^{2}).

(59)

As a result,

G_{z}^{-}(z)\Phi(0)\sim G_{\overline{z}}^{-}(z)\Phi(0)\sim O(\lambda^{2})

(60)

which implies
\Phi(x)
and its super-partner will form a chiral superfield.

Another interesting perspective is to check the chiral ring relation under the
T\overline{T}
deformation. The deformed OPE coefficients for a general operator is derived in
Cardy:2019qao
:

\delta C^{l}{}_{mn}(x_{1}-x_{2})=2\pi\lambda\epsilon^{ab}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}T_{ai}(x^{\prime}+\epsilon)\epsilon^{ij}dx_{j}^{\prime}\partial_{b}C^{l}{}_{mn}(x_{1}-x_{2}),

(61)

but since the OPE coefficients inside the chiral ring are constant, i.e.,
\partial_{x^{i}}C^{l}{}_{mn}(x)=0
, we must have
\delta C^{l}{}_{mn}=0
. Therefore, the
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
chiral ring relation is preserved under the deformation.

3.1
A brief review of two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)

\mathcal{S}
-multiplets

In this subsection, we briefly review two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)

\mathcal{S}
-multiplets following
Dumitrescu:2011iu
. A generic
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)

S
-multiplet without conformal symmetry consists of two real superfields
\mathcal{S}_{\pm\pm}
together with chiral superfields
\chi_{\pm}
and twisted chiral superfields
\mathcal{Y}_{\pm}
satisfying the following constraints

\overline{D}_{\pm}\mathcal{S}_{\mp\mp}=\pm(\chi_{\mp}+\mathcal{Y}_{\mp}),

(62)

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{D}_{\pm}\chi_{\pm}=0,&D_{\pm}\mathcal{Y}_{\pm}=0,\\
\overline{D}_{\pm}\chi_{\mp}=\pm C^{(\pm)},&\overline{D}_{\pm}\mathcal{Y}_{\mp}=\mp C^{(\pm)},\\
D_{+}\chi_{-}-\overline{D}_{-}\overline{\chi}_{+}=k,&D_{+}\mathcal{Y}_{-}+D_{-}\mathcal{Y}_{+}=k^{\prime}.\end{array}

(63)

Here
k,k^{\prime}
and
C^{(\pm)}
are real and complex constants respectively.

In components, the
\mathcal{S}
-multiplets are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\pm\pm}=

\displaystyle j_{\pm\pm}-i\theta^{\pm}S_{\pm\pm\pm}-i\theta^{\mp}(S_{\mp\pm\pm}\mp 2\sqrt{2}i\overline{\psi}_{\pm})-i\overline{\theta}^{\pm}S_{\pm\pm\pm}-i\overline{\theta}^{\mp}\left(\overline{S}_{\mp\pm\pm}\pm 2\sqrt{2}i\psi_{\pm}\right)

(64)

\displaystyle-\theta^{\pm}\overline{\theta}^{\pm}T_{\pm\pm\pm\pm}+\theta^{\mp}\overline{\theta}^{\mp}\left(A\mp{k+\frac{k^{\prime}}{2}}\right)+i\theta^{+}\theta^{-}\overline{Y}_{\pm\pm}+i\overline{\theta}^{+}\overline{\theta}^{-}Y_{\pm\pm}

\displaystyle\pm i\theta^{+}\overline{\theta}^{-}\overline{G}_{\pm\pm}\mp i\theta^{-}\overline{\theta}^{+}G_{\pm\pm}\mp\frac{1}{2}\theta^{+}\theta^{-}\overline{\theta}^{\pm}\partial_{\pm\pm}S_{\mp\pm\pm}\mp\frac{1}{2}\theta^{+}\theta^{-}\overline{\theta}^{\mp}\partial_{\pm\pm}\left(S_{\pm\mp\mp}\pm 2\sqrt{2}i\overline{\psi}_{\mp}\right)

\displaystyle\mp\frac{1}{2}\overline{\theta}^{+}\overline{\theta}^{-}\theta^{\pm}\partial_{\pm\pm}\overline{S}_{\mp\pm\pm}\mp\frac{1}{2}\overline{\theta}^{+}\overline{\theta}^{-}\theta^{\mp}\partial_{\pm\pm}\left(\overline{S}_{\pm\mp\mp}\mp 2\sqrt{2}i\psi_{\mp}\right)+{\frac{1}{4}}\theta^{+}\theta^{-}\overline{\theta}^{+}\overline{\theta}^{-}\partial_{\pm\pm}^{2}j_{\mp\mp}

and

\displaystyle\chi_{+}

\displaystyle=-i\lambda_{+}(y)-i\theta^{+}\overline{G}_{++}(y)+\theta^{-}\left(E(y)+{\frac{k}{2}}\right)+\overline{\theta}^{-}C^{(-)}+\theta^{+}\theta^{-}\partial_{++}\overline{\lambda}_{-}(y)~,

(65)

\displaystyle\chi_{-}

\displaystyle=-i\lambda_{-}(y)-\theta^{+}\left(\overline{E}(y)-\frac{k}{2}\right)+i\theta^{-}G_{--}(y)-\overline{\theta}^{+}C^{(+)}-\theta^{+}\theta^{-}\partial_{--}\overline{\lambda}_{+}(y),

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\pm\overline{S}_{\mp\pm\pm}+\sqrt{2}i\psi_{\pm},

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(T_{++--}-A\right)+\frac{i}{4}\left(\partial_{++}j_{--}-\partial_{--}j_{++}\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{++}G_{--}

\displaystyle=\partial_{--}G_{++},

\displaystyle y^{\pm\pm}

\displaystyle=x^{\pm\pm}+4i\theta^{\pm}\overline{\theta}^{\pm},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{+}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\psi_{+}(\overline{\tilde{y}})+\theta^{-}\left(F(\overline{\tilde{y}})+{\frac{k^{\prime}}{2}}\right)-i\overline{\theta}^{+}Y_{++}(\overline{\tilde{y}})-\overline{\theta}^{-}C^{(-)}+\sqrt{2}i\theta^{-}\overline{\theta}^{+}\partial_{++}\psi_{-}(\overline{\tilde{y}}),

(66)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{-}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\psi_{-}(\tilde{y})-\theta^{+}\left(F(\tilde{y})-\frac{k^{\prime}}{2}\right)+\overline{\theta}^{+}C^{(+)}-i\overline{\theta}^{-}Y_{--}(\tilde{y})+\sqrt{2}i\theta^{+}\overline{\theta}^{-}\partial_{--}\psi_{+}(\tilde{y}),

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(T_{++--}+A\right)-\frac{i}{4}\left(\partial_{++}j_{--}+\partial_{--}j_{++}\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{++}Y_{--}

\displaystyle=\partial_{--}Y_{++},

\displaystyle\tilde{y}^{\pm\pm}

\displaystyle=x^{\pm\pm}\pm 4i\theta^{\pm}\overline{\theta}^{\pm}.

It is important to identify the conserved brane currents in the generic
\mathcal{S}
-multiplet:
\mp i\overline{Y}_{\pm\pm}
and
\pm i\overline{G}_{\pm\pm}
are the zero-brane currents that give rise to the central charges
Z
and
\tilde{Z}
, while the constants
C^{\pm}
and
k-k^{\prime}
are the space-filling brane currents which can lead to partial supersymmetry-breaking.

The
\mathcal{S}
-multiplet can be modified by an improvement transformation by a real superfield
U
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\pm\pm}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\pm\pm}+[D_{\pm},\overline{D}_{\pm}]U,

(67)

\displaystyle\chi_{\pm}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\chi_{\pm}-\overline{D}_{+}\overline{D}_{-}D_{\pm}U,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{\pm}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{\pm}-D_{\pm}\overline{D}_{+}\overline{D}_{-}U.

Like in the
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
case, we are interested in the various possibilities where the
\mathcal{S}
-multiplet can be improved to a smaller multiplet:

1.

If
k=C^{(\pm)}=0
and there is a well-defined
U
such that
\chi_{\pm}=\overline{D}_{+}\overline{D}_{-}D_{\pm}U
, then an improvement transformation can be used to set
\chi_{\pm}=0
. The resulting multiplet is the FZ-multiplet, whose bottom component is a conserved axial current.

2.

If instead
k^{\prime}=C^{(\pm)}=0
and there is a well-defined
U
such that
\mathcal{Y}_{\pm}=D_{\pm}\overline{D}_{+}\overline{D}_{-}U
, then a improvement transformation will set
\mathcal{Y}=0
. This leads to the
\mathcal{R}
-multiplet, whose bottom component is a conserved vector current. Notice that the FZ- and
\mathcal{R}
-multiplets are related by the following mirror automorphisms:

\mathcal{S}_{\pm\pm}\leftrightarrow\pm\mathcal{S}_{\pm\pm},\,\,\,\chi_{+}\leftrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{+},\,\,\,\chi_{-}\leftrightarrow-\mathcal{Y}_{-},\,\,\,k\leftrightarrow-k^{\prime},\,\,\,C^{(\pm)}\leftrightarrow\overline{C}^{(\pm)}.

(68)

3.

If
k=k^{\prime}=C^{(\pm)}=0
and both
\chi_{\pm}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{\pm}
can be removed by an improvement transformation, then the theory is superconformal.

3.2
Deformation of the generic
\mathcal{S}
-multiplet in
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
SCFT

We derive the deformed
\mathcal{S}
-multiplet by supersymmetrizing the flow equation (
4
). The trick is that we can apply the supercharge
Q
on both sides of the deformation equation. We have to be careful since the supercharge
Q
in the deformed theory would be different from the supercharge
Q^{(0)}
in the seed theory by
O(\lambda)
corrections. Since
T_{z\overline{z}}
is already of the first order in
\lambda
, at leading order of the perturbation theory, the extra correction of
\lambda
will not contribute. We have

[Q,T_{z\overline{z}}]=[Q^{(0)},-\pi\lambda T_{zz}T_{\overline{z}\overline{z}}]+O(\lambda^{2}).

(69)

One might worry that supersymmetry acts on operators which do not vanish at the superconformal point, such as
T_{zz}
, will receive contribution like
[\lambda Q^{(1)},T_{zz}^{(0)}]
; however, all the leading order contributions can be acquired by acting the supercharge on the operators which do vanish at the superconformal point together with the Ward identity. Thus, we do not have to worry about such a contribution. Using this method, we can determine the deformed
\mathcal{S}
-multiplet. The collected results are rather extensive and we refer the reader to appendix
B
.

The advantage of this calculation is that neither
Y_{\pm\pm}
or
G_{\pm\pm}
vanishes so that we acquire a generic
\mathcal{S}
-multiplet with no conserved
R
-currents. In particular, the deformation of the bottom components
j_{\pm\pm}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\pm\pm}
are given by

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\partial_{++}j_{--}=-\frac{\pi\lambda}{16}\partial_{--}j_{--}^{(0)}T_{++++}^{(0)}+O(\lambda^{2}),

(70)

\displaystyle\partial_{--}j_{++}=-\frac{\pi\lambda}{16}\partial_{++}j_{++}^{(0)}T_{----}^{(0)}+O(\lambda^{2}).

The above result clearly indicates that neither the vector current nor axial current are conserved.

However, the appearance of the total derivative on the R.H.S. suggests it is possible to consider an improvement transformation to acquire a conserved
U(1)_{A}
current or
U(1)_{V}
current. Supersymmetrizing such improvement transformation leads to either the FZ-multiplet or
\mathcal{R}
-multiplet. For instance, one can consider the improvement transformation which acts on
j_{\pm\pm}
via

j_{++}\rightarrow j_{++}-\frac{\pi\lambda}{16}j_{--}^{(0)}T_{++++}^{(0)},\,\,\,j_{--}\rightarrow j_{--}-\frac{\pi\lambda}{16}j_{++}^{(0)}T_{----}^{(0)}.

(71)

As one can check, after this improvement transformation, the vector current is now conserved at the leading order of the perturbation theory in
\lambda
:

\partial_{++}j_{--}+\partial_{--}j_{++}=O(\lambda^{2}).

(72)

Using
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
supersymmetry, one can find the improvement transformation for the result of the components in the
\mathcal{S}
-multiplets and verify that the twisted chiral superfields
\mathcal{Y}_{\pm}
indeed vanish. A new phenomenon is that this improvement transformation will lead to an additional correction to the trace flow equations:

T_{++--}=\frac{\pi\lambda}{16}T_{----}^{(0)}T_{++++}^{(0)}+\frac{\pi\lambda}{64}\partial_{--}j_{--}^{(0)}\partial_{++}j_{++}^{(0)}+O(\lambda^{2}).

(73)

Notice that central currents
Y_{\pm\pm}
and
G_{\pm\pm}
will be generated under the
T\overline{T}
deformation. Even after the improvement transformation, only one of them can be removed and the one left takes form of a total derivative. For instance, if we choose to remove
Y_{\pm\pm}
by an improvement transformation, we will find:

\displaystyle G_{++}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi\lambda}{16}\partial_{++}\overline{S}_{+++}^{(0)}S_{---}^{(0)}+O(\lambda^{2}),

(74)

\displaystyle G_{--}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi\lambda}{16}\overline{S}_{+++}^{(0)}\partial_{--}S_{---}^{(0)}+O(\lambda^{2}),

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{++}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\lambda}{16}\partial_{++}S_{+++}^{(0)}\overline{S}_{---}^{(0)}+O(\lambda^{2}),

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{--}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\lambda}{16}S_{+++}^{(0)}\partial_{--}\overline{S}_{---}^{(0)}+O(\lambda^{2}).

Being total derivatives itself does not suggest
G_{\pm\pm}
leads to trivial charge, but rather it suggests one must understand non-perturbative effects of the
T\overline{T}
deformation to fully understand the perturbative
\mathcal{S}
-multiplet for the deformed theory. Similar scenarios occur, such as calculating the instanton number in four-dimensional gauge theory from integrating the total derivative
\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\operatorname{Tr}F\wedge F
.

It might be tempting to conclude that either chiral ring or twisted chiral ring will cease to exist in the deformed theory. However, there is a caveat. There is no guarantee that all the would-be chiral ring or twisted chiral ring elements would actually be charged under the non-zero central current preventing one to simply draw such a conclusion. We believe understanding the non-perturbative effect of the
T\overline{T}
deformation and perhaps a model dependent analysis are required to determine the ultimate fate of the chiral ring and twisted chiral ring in the deformed theory.

In the previous section, we studied the

\mathcal{N}=(2,2)

E
for any state.

However, for

Fendley:1991ve
;
Fendley:1991xn
;
Fendley:1992dm
;
Fendley:1993pi
;

. Unfortunately, rather than just a single integrable equation, the TBA system usually contains several coupled integrable equations.
8
8

8

There are hybrid approaches combining the TBA and NLIE. These approaches have been carried out in
Hegedus:2005bg
;
Suzuki:2011dj
;
Balog:2012zt

4.1
Review of TBA system for Landau-Ginzburg models

In this subsection, we will provide a brief review on acquiring TBA for undeformed LG models. We skip detailed derivations and refer the reader to pioneering work by Fendley and Intriligator
Fendley:1991ve
;
Fendley:1992dm
.

These LG models have
n
supersymmetric vacua given by the
n
solutions from
X^{n}=\beta
. There are BPS solitons which interpolate between different vacua. For a soliton connecting the vacuum
X_{i}
and
X_{f}=e^{2\pi ir/n}X_{i}
, it has mass

m_{r}=M\sin(r\mu),\,\,\,r=1,\dots,n-1,

(76)

where
M=\frac{2n}{n+1}
and
\mu=\pi/n
. For each mass
m_{r}
, there are a pair of solitons
(u_{r},d_{r})
related by
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
supersymmetry.

LG models’ S-matrices are diagonal under
r,s
labels with the incoming and outgoing states being type-
r,s
solitons. The S-matrix
S_{r,s}(\theta)
is

\bordermatrix{&d_{s}u_{r}&u_{s}d_{r}\cr u_{r}d_{s}&b_{r,s}(\theta)&{\tilde{c}}_{r,s}(\theta)\cr d_{r}u_{s}&c_{r,s}(\theta)&{\tilde{b}}_{r,s}(\theta)\cr}\qquad\qquad\bordermatrix{&u_{s}u_{r}&d_{s}d_{r}\cr u_{r}u_{s}&a_{r,s}(\theta)&0\cr d_{r}d_{s}&0&{\tilde{a}}_{r,s}(\theta)\cr},

(77)

where the S-matrix elements are

\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{r,s}(\theta)=Z_{r,s}(\theta)\sinh\left(\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{i\mu}{2}(r+s)\right),&\tilde{a}_{r,s}(\theta)=-Z_{r,s}(\theta)\sinh\left(\frac{\theta}{2}-\frac{i\mu}{2}(r+s)\right),\\
b_{r,s}(\theta)=Z_{r,s}(\theta)\sinh\left(\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{i\mu}{2}(s-r)\right),&\tilde{b}_{r,s}(\theta)=Z_{r,s}(\theta)\sinh\left(\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{i\mu}{2}(r-s)\right),\\
c_{r,s}(\theta)=Z_{r,s}(\theta)ie^{i\mu(r-s)/2}\left(\sin(r\mu)\sin(s\mu)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},&\tilde{c}_{r,s}(\theta)=Z_{r,s}(\theta)ie^{i\mu(s-r)/2}(\sin(r\mu)\sin(s\mu))^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{array}

(78)

and satisfy the condition

a_{r,s}(\theta)\tilde{a}_{r,s}(\theta)+b_{r,s}(\theta)\tilde{b}_{r,s}(\theta)-c_{r,s}(\theta)\tilde{c}_{r,s}(\theta)=0.

(79)

The specific form of
Z_{r,s}(\theta)
is unimportant in our analysis for the deformed theory and is thus omitted.

The basic idea of TBA is to place the system on a torus of length
L
(in the longitude direction) at temperature
T\equiv 1/R
. Then, working in the grand canonical ensemble, one finds the energy spectrum and the particular filling of the energy levels which minimize the free energy in the thermodynamic limit
L\rightarrow\infty
.

Now, consider
N
particles with rapidity
\theta_{i},i=1,\dots,N
on a circle
S^{1}(R)
. The allowed wave function must be invariant under the transformation which brings the particle with rapidity
\theta_{k}
around the circle and back to its original position. In other words, the wave function obeys the Yang equation (see e.g.,
zamolodchikov1991thermodynamic
)

e^{im_{k}\sinh\theta_{k}L}T(\theta_{k}|\theta_{k},\cdots,\theta_{N},\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{k-1})\psi=\psi,

(80)

where
T(\theta_{k}|\theta_{k+1},\cdots,\theta_{k-1})
is the transfer matrix. The transfer matrix’s components can be expressed in terms of the S-matrix:

\left(T_{ab}(\theta)\right)_{c_{i}}^{d_{i}}\equiv\sum_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{N-1}}S_{ac_{1}}^{d_{1}k_{1}}\left(\theta-\theta_{1}\right)S_{k_{1}c_{2}}^{d_{2}k_{2}}\left(\theta-\theta_{2}\right)\cdots S_{k_{N-1}c_{N}}^{d_{N}b}\left(\theta-\theta_{N}\right).

(81)

Once we obtain the transfer matrix’s eigenvalues, the single-valuedness condition (
80
) will lead to constraint equations. Minimizing the free energy under these constraints from (
80
) provides the following TBA equations

\epsilon_{a}(\theta)=m_{a}R\cosh(\theta)-\sum_{b}\int\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{2\pi}\phi_{ab}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})\ln(1+e^{-\epsilon_{b}(\theta^{\prime})}),

(82)

where
\phi_{ab}(\theta)
is a kernel and the distribution of type-
a
solitons in the thermodynamic limit is
\rho_{a}(\theta)=\ln(1+e^{-\epsilon_{a}(\theta)})
. Therefore, the ground state energy of the system can be written as

E(R)=-\sum_{a}\frac{m_{a}}{2\pi}\int d\theta\cosh(\theta)\ln(1+e^{-\epsilon_{a}(\theta)})

(83)

and the ground state momentum
P(R)
can be computed by replacing
\cosh\theta
with
\sinh\theta
. Due to the symmetry in
\epsilon(\theta)=\epsilon(-\theta)
, one can show
P(R)\equiv 0
and it is consistent with the expectation that the ground state has vanishing momentum.

Solving for the transfer matrix’s eigenvalues is usually a difficult task. For the special case where the S-matrix is diagonal, the only process are two type
a,b
solitons scattering into two type
a,b
solitons:

S_{ab}^{cd}(\theta)=S_{ab}(\theta)\delta_{a}^{c}\delta_{b}^{d}.

(84)

This implies that the transfer matrix is also diagonal and the TBA system then greatly simplifies. First, each
\epsilon_{a}(\theta)
corresponds to a physical soliton of mass
m_{a}
. The kernel
\phi_{ab}
also has a simple expression in terms of the S-matrix:

\phi_{ab}(\theta)=-i\frac{\partial\ln S_{ab}(\theta)}{\partial\theta}.

(85)

However, in (
77
), the S-matrix for LG models are not diagonal. Fortuitously, the S-matrix is diagonal for the type-
r,s
soliton. The S-matrix is of the 6-vertex model form and its eigenvalues can be solved by the so-called algebraic Bethe ansatz (ABA). From computing the eigenvalues of the S-matrix for fixed
r,s
, we construct the transfer matrix’s eigenvalues.

A detailed discussion on how ABA works can be found in
Fendley:1991ve

and setting
\alpha=\pi
, we arrive at the CFIV index, which is invariant under
D
-term perturbations introduced in
Cecotti:1992qh
,

I_{1}(\beta)=\text{Tr}(-1)^{F}Fe^{-\beta H}.

(135)

One can consider generalization of
I_{1}(\beta)
by taking more derivatives,

I_{l}(\beta)=\text{Tr}(-1)^{F}F^{l}e^{-\beta H},\quad l\geq 2,

(136)

however, as shown in
Cecotti:1992qh
, these quantities are not invariant under
D
-term perturbations and are not indices.

For a theory with a mass gap, sometimes there is a vacuum degeneracy. Then we can consider soliton configurations interpolating between the vacuum labelled by
a
on the left and the vacuum labelled by
b
on the right. Thus, we can define
I_{l}
for each pairwise soliton configuration

(I_{l})_{ab}=\text{Tr}_{ab}(-1)^{F}F^{l}e^{-\beta H}

(137)

so that we now have
n\times n
(
n
is the number of vacua) matrices rather than a number.

For the Witten index,
(I_{0})_{ab}
is just a diagonal matrix. This is exactly because for
a\neq b
, a BPS soliton is required to connect the two different vacua. However, as we know, this is a two-dimensional representation of
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
supersymmetry with non-zero energy and will not contribute to the Witten index. Furthermore, since in our case there is a
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
symmetry that relates the
n
vacua,
(I_{0})_{ab}
is proportional to the identity matrix.

For the CFIV index, we obtain a general
n\times n
matrix and are interested in their eigenvalues. In practice, we keep track of the eigenvalues by introducing a weight for each soliton type. For instance, if the soliton is charged under some topological symmetry with charge
\mathcal{T}
, then we can introduce a chemical potential
e^{i\Theta\mathcal{T}}
, expand the final answer in terms of
e^{im\Theta}
with coefficients being the eigenvalues of
(I_{1})_{ab}
. For this model, we consider the superpotential
W(X,\beta)=\frac{X^{n+1}}{n+1}-\beta X
which leads to
n
vacua at
X=e^{2\pi ij/n}\beta^{1/n},j=1\cdots n
. As usual, we call a soliton type-
r
if the two vacua are related to each other by satisfying
X_{a}=e^{2\pi ir/n}X_{b}
. The
p
-th eigenvalues of
(I_{1})_{ab}
are found by introducing a weight
e^{2\pi irpN_{r}/n}
, where
N_{r}
are the number of type-
r
solitons.

To make connections to integrable theories, we consider the free energy
\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{a}}(\beta)
with chemical potential
\mu_{a}
:

-\beta\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{a}}(\beta)=\text{ln}\text{Tr}(e^{\beta\sum_{a}\mu_{a}N_{a}}e^{-\beta H}).

(138)

The exact expression
\beta\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{a}}(\beta)
can be computed via TBA using the exact S-matrix

\ln\text{Tr}(e^{\beta\sum_{a}\mu_{a}F_{a}}e^{-\beta H})=-\sum_{a}m_{a}L\int\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\cosh\theta\ln(1+e^{\beta\mu_{a}-\epsilon_{a}(\theta)}),

(139)

where the
\epsilon_{a}(\theta)
are the solutions to the coupled integral equations:

\epsilon_{a}(\theta)=m_{a}\beta\cosh(\theta)-\sum_{b}\int\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{2\pi}\phi_{ab}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})\ln(1+e^{\beta\mu_{b}-\epsilon_{b}(\theta^{\prime})}).

(140)

Recall from previous subsections that the ground state’s momentum vanishes

-\sum_{a}m_{a}L\int\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\sinh(\theta)\ln(1+e^{\beta\mu_{a}-\epsilon(\theta)})=0.

(141)

In the vanishing chemical potential case (
\mu_{a}=0
), we argue that this integral vanishes because of
\epsilon_{a}(\theta)=\epsilon_{a}(-\theta)
. The same occurrence with vanishing momentum happens here since the chemical potential only favors particular species of particles but not the rapidity. Thus the
\theta\rightarrow-\theta
parity symmetry is unaffected by the chemical potential. For a vanishing chemical potential, we similarly derive a flow equation of
-\beta\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{a}}(\beta)
by introducing the quantity

F_{p}(\beta,\lambda,\alpha)=-\sum_{a}m_{a}\beta\int\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\cosh(\theta)\ln(1+e^{\beta\mu_{a}-\epsilon_{a}(\theta)})=\frac{\beta}{L}\ln\text{Tr}(e^{i\alpha F+2\pi irpN_{r}/N}e^{-\beta H}),

(142)

where in the last step we have chosen
\mu_{a}
such that
\beta\sum_{a}\mu_{a}N_{a}=i\alpha F+2\pi irpN_{r}/N
, and
p=0,\dots,n-1
. Then we find the flow equation for
F_{p}(\beta,\lambda,\alpha)
:

\partial_{\lambda}F_{p}(\beta,\lambda,\alpha)+F_{p}(\beta,\lambda,\alpha)\partial_{\beta}F_{p}(\beta,\lambda,\alpha)=0.

(143)

Unlike the Witten index, when being placed on
S^{1}
with radius
L
,
I_{1}
from (
135
) does scale linearly with
L
. It is useful to introduce another quantity

Q_{p}(\beta,\lambda)=\partial_{\alpha}F_{p}(\beta,\lambda,\alpha)_{\alpha=\pi}=\frac{\beta}{L}\partial_{\alpha}\ln\text{Tr}(e^{i\alpha F+2\pi irpN_{r}/N-\beta H})_{\alpha=\pi},

(144)

which is clear in the thermodynamic limit
L\rightarrow\infty
. Taking a derivative of the flow equation with respect to
\alpha
evaluated at
\alpha=\pi
, we find

\partial_{\lambda}Q_{p}(\beta,\lambda)+Q_{p}(\beta,\lambda)\partial_{\beta}\bigg(\frac{\beta}{L}\ln I_{0}\bigg)+\frac{\beta}{L}(\ln I_{0})\partial_{\beta}Q_{p}(\beta,\lambda)=0.

(145)

After being simplified, the flow equation for
Q_{p}(\beta,\lambda)
is

\partial_{\lambda}Q_{p}(\beta,\lambda)+\frac{1}{L}(\ln I_{0})Q_{p}(\beta,\lambda)+\frac{\beta}{L}(\ln I_{0})\partial_{\beta}Q_{p}(\beta,\lambda)=0,

(146)

where we have the same
I_{0}
for all
p
since
(I_{0})_{ab}
is proportional to the identity and can be simultaneously diagonalized with
(I_{1})_{ab}
with
N
identical eigenvalues.
14
14

14

We will denote the eigenvalues as
I_{0}
and we have used the fact that
I_{0}
is independent of
\beta
and
\lambda
.
In the thermodynamic limit
L\rightarrow\infty
, we find

\partial_{\lambda}Q_{p}(\beta,\lambda)=0.

(147)

In other words, the CFIV index does not flow under
T\overline{T}
deformation and, perhaps, is not a surprise after all. As argued in
Cecotti:1992qh
, the CFIV index is dependent of
F
-term perturbations and is independent of
D
-term perturbations. These two facts combined imply that the CFIV index will not flow under
T\overline{T}
deformation.

Now, let us consider
I_{l}
for
l\geq 2
. These are not invariant under
D
-term perturbations making them not indices. Therefore, it is natural to expect these
I_{l}
’s would flow under the
T\overline{T}
deformation. Indeed, TBA allows one to derive a set of recursive flow equations for these
I_{l}
’s. For instance, consider
I_{2}
and define

Q_{2,p}=\partial_{\alpha}^{2}F_{p}(\beta,\lambda,\alpha)|_{\alpha=\pi}=\frac{\beta}{L}\bigg(\frac{I_{1,p}^{2}}{I_{0}^{2}}-\frac{I_{2,p}}{I_{0}}\bigg).

(148)

Taking two derivatives with respect to
\alpha
on both sides of the flow equation for
F_{p}(\beta,\lambda,\alpha)
, we obtain

\partial_{\lambda}Q_{2,p}+(Q_{2,p}+\beta\partial_{\beta}Q_{2,p})\frac{\ln I_{0}}{L}+2Q_{1,p}\partial_{\beta}Q_{1,p}=0.

(149)

If
I_{2,p}
scales linearly with
L
in the thermodynamic limit
L\rightarrow\infty
, then
\frac{Q_{2,p}}{L}\rightarrow\beta\frac{I_{1,p}^{2}}{L^{2}}=-\frac{1}{\beta}Q_{1,p}^{2}
. As a result, we have

\beta\partial_{\lambda}(I_{2}/L)-2I_{0}(1-\ln I_{0})Q_{1,p}\partial_{\beta}Q_{1,p}=0,

(150)

where we used
\partial_{\lambda}I_{0}=\partial_{\lambda}I_{1,p}=0
.

Another interesting index to study in
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)

\lambda=0
, the theory has a special solution
Fendley:1993pi

e^{-\epsilon_{R}(\theta)}=\epsilon_{\pm F_{R}}(\theta)=0,

(163)

where by
\pm F_{R}
, we mean the two nodes to the right of the node
R
in (
159
). To see this, in the integral equations (
158
) of
\epsilon_{\pm F_{R}}(\theta)
, we have

\epsilon_{\pm F_{R}}(\theta)=-\int\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\cosh(\theta-\theta^{\prime})}\ln(1+e^{-\epsilon_{R}(\theta)})=0

(164)

while for
e^{-\epsilon_{R}(\theta)}
, we find

\displaystyle e^{-\epsilon_{R}(\theta)}

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg[\int\frac{d\theta^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\cosh(\theta-\theta^{\prime})}\ln(2+2\cos\alpha_{R})+\text{finite}\bigg]

(165)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2+2\cos\alpha_{R}}\times(\text{finite})=0\,\,\,\text{for $\alpha_{R}=\pi$}.

In this solution, the right-movers completely decouple from the left-movers as
\ln(1+e^{-\epsilon_{R}(\theta)})=0
. In other words, the terms related to
\epsilon_{R}(\theta)
vanishes in the integral equation (
158
) of
\epsilon_{L}(\theta)
. It is straightforward to check that this remains true under the
T\overline{T}
deformation. This is again not a surprise. What the
T\overline{T}
deformation does is simply to introduce a non-trivial coupling between left-movers and right-movers. If the two sectors are already decoupled in the TBA system for the elliptic genus, then
T\overline{T}
deformation simply will not have any effects on the TBA system.

It is not difficult to see that this argument generalizes to the superpotential
W=gX^{k+2}
for
k\geq 2
. The only difference in the TBA system is now there are extra
k-1
open nodes linearly connecting nodes
L
and
R
. However, since
\alpha_{R}=\pi
, the right-moving sector would decouple anyways so the
T\overline{T}
deformation will not change the TBA system for the remaining left-moving sector.

5
Conclusion and future directions

In this paper, we have further explored several aspects of
T\overline{T}
deformed SCFTs, integrable supersymmetric models and their indices. We have calculated the deformed two- and three-point correlators for two-dimensional Euclidean
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)
SCFTs in the spirit of
Kraus:2018xrn
. However, unlike in
Kraus:2018xrn
, we did not look at the bulk AdS
3
supergravity side
Achucarro:1987vz
;
Kraus:2007vu
as an alternative method to obtain the deformed correlators. Additionally, studying how the correlators change under different solvable irrelevant deformations, such as using
Jiang:2019trm
’s construction for the deformed multiplets in
J\overline{T}
,
\bar{J}T
and
He:2019vzf
’s systematic methods to evaluate
J\overline{T},\bar{J}T
correlators via conformal perturbation theory, seems tractable. Also, it would be interesting to non-perturbatively calculate the deformed SCFT correlators á la Cardy
Cardy:2019qao
.

Our perturbative analysis of the
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)

\mathcal{S}
-multiplets near the superconformal fixed point does not completely explain the full feature of the central current generated under the
T\overline{T}
deformation and does not allow one to convincingly determine the fate of the chiral ring and twisted ring in the deformed theory. We believe a non-perturbative study of the
T\overline{T}
deformation is required for further exploration.

We also studied the
T\overline{T}
deformed
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
and
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
supersymmetric integrable models’ S-matrices, ground state energies and common indices via TBA. We derived a flow equation for the deformed ground state energy and showed that several
D
-term independent indices do not flow. Among these indices, the CFIV index is special as it is not a topological index but can be derived solely from the topological data (topological-anti-topological fusions), i.e., the deformed
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
chiral ring, via the deformed
tt^{*}
equations. It would be interesting to study more aspects of the
T\overline{T}
deformed (twisted) chiral ring in relation to TQFTs
Witten:1988xj
;
Witten:1991zz
;
Santilli:2018xux
;
Santilli:2020qvd
.

While we have analyzed a class of deformed
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
two-dimensional integrable models via TBA or ABA, there are other interesting models one can consider such as the following:
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
generalizations of the supersymmetric sine-Gordon model, supersymmetric
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
sigma models and
SU(2)_{l}\otimes SU(2)_{k}/SU(2)_{k+l}
coset models. Also, it would be interesting to use TBA to numerically study excited states in these models. Numerical solutions for the excited states of the TBA equations have been studied in
Dorey:1996re
, which is extended to supersymmetric integrable models in
Fendley:1997ys
.

We studied the
T\overline{T}
deformed S-matrices for a certain class of
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
and
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
two-dimensional integrable models, and a natural question is to consider these same theories when there is a boundary present to determine, with suitable boundary conditions, the boundary reflection matrices on how much integrability and supersymmetry are preserved. In the case for two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
integrable models
Moriconi:1996tn
, reminiscent of the S-matrix, the R-matrix is

R^{[ij]}(\theta)=R^{[ij]}_{BF}(\theta)R^{[ij]}_{B}(\theta),

(166)

where
R^{[ij]}_{B}(\theta)
is the reflection matrix for the bosonic part and
R^{[ij]}_{BF}(\theta)
describes the relative amplitudes for bosons and fermions when scattering off the boundary. The deformed R-matrix obeys the usual unitarity condition, boundary Yang-Baxter equations and crossing symmetry in terms of the deformed S-matrix. It would be interesting to study more on boundary supersymmetric integrable models in the context of
Fendley:1990zj
;
Fendley:1993zt
;
Fendley:1994cz
;
Warner:1995ay
;
Inami:1995np
;
Moriconi:1996tn
under the
T\overline{T}
deformation.

We hope to return to these open problems in future works.

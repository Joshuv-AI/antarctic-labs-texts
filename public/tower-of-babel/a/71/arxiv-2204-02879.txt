Combinatorics of integer partitions with prescribed perimeter
Zhicong Lin, Huan Xiong, Sherry H.F. Yan

Abstract
. We prove that the number of even parts and the number of times that parts are repeated have the same distribution over integer partitions with a fixed perimeter. This refines Straub’s analog of Euler’s Odd-Distinct partition theorem. We generalize the two concerned statistics to these of the part-difference less than d d and the parts not congruent to 1 1 modulo d + 1 d+1 and prove a distribution inequality, that has a similar flavor as Alder’s ex-conjecture, over partitions with a prescribed perimeter. Both of our results are proved analytically and combinatorially.

Introduction

Integer partitions

1

n

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k})

\lambda_{i}

\lambda

\ell(\lambda)
. The

\lambda
is defined to be

\Gamma(\lambda)=\lambda_{1}+\ell(\lambda)-1

and the
minimal part-difference
of
\lambda
is

\min\{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}:1\leq i<\ell(\lambda)\}.

One of the most famous results in partition theory is Euler’s Odd-Distinct partition theorem, which asserts that the number of partitions of
n
with odd parts is equal to the number with distinct parts. Two beautiful bijective proofs (see
[
4
, pp. 63-65]
) of Euler’s partition theorem with different refinements were constructed respectively by Sylvester and Glaisher. Recently, Straub
[
5
, Thereom 1.4]
considered the set
\mathcal{H}_{n}
of partitions with perimeter
n
, rather than the set of all partitions of
n
, and proved the following analog of Euler’s partition theorem.

Theorem 1

(Straub)
.

There are as many partitions in
\mathcal{H}_{n}
with odd parts as with distinct parts.

For a fixed positive integer
d
, Alder’s ex-conjecture (see
[
1
, Sec. 4.3]
and
[
2
]
) states that there are not more partitions of
n
into parts congruent to
\pm 1
modulo
d+3
than into parts with minimal part-difference at least
d
. Indeed, Alder’s ex-conjecture is Euler’s partition theorem (resp. Rogers–Ramanujan identity) for
d=1
(resp.
d=2
).
Inspired by Alder’s ex-conjecture, Fu and Tang
[
2
, Theorem 2.15]
proved the following
d

Theorem 2

(Fu–Tang)
.

There are as many partitions in
\mathcal{H}_{n}
with parts congruent to
1
modulo
d+1
as with minimal part-difference at least
d
.

The purpose of this paper is to study further the combinatorics of integer partitions with a prescribed perimeter after the aforementioned works by Straub and Fu–Tang.

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})

\mathrm{rep}(\lambda)=|\{1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)-1:\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}\}|\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{even}(\lambda)=|\{1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda):\lambda_{i}\text{ is even}\}|,

2.
Three proofs of Theorem
3

2.1.
A recurrence relation proof

Let
A(n,k)
(resp.
B(n,k)
) be the number of partitions
\lambda
in
\mathcal{H}_{n}
with
\mathrm{rep}(\lambda)=k
(resp.
\mathrm{even}(\lambda)=k
). In the following two lemmas, we prove that
A(n,k)
and
B(n,k)
share the same (binomial-like) recurrence relation, which proves Theorem
3
.

Lemma 5
.

The number
A(n,k)
satisfies the recurrence relation

(2.1)

A(n,k)=A(n-1,k)+A(n-1,k-1)+A(n-2,k)-A(n-2,k-1)

for
n\geq 2
with the initial value
A(1,0)=1
.

Proof.

Suppose that
n\geq 2
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots)
is a
partition in
\mathcal{H}_{n}
with
\mathrm{rep}(\lambda)=k
. To obtain the recursion formula for
A(n,k)
, we consider three cases:

(i)

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
. In this case, by deleting the largest part of
\lambda
, we obtain
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots)
. Then
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{n-1}
and
\mathrm{rep}(\lambda^{\prime})=k-1
. Furthermore,
\lambda^{\prime}
can be any of
the partitions satisfying
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{n-1}
and
\mathrm{rep}(\lambda^{\prime})=k-1
. Therefore, the number of partitions
\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
satisfying
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
and
\mathrm{rep}(\lambda)=k
is equal to
A(n-1,k-1)
.

(ii)

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}+1
. In this case, again by deleting the largest part of
\lambda
, we obtain
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots)
. It is clear that
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{n-2}
and
\mathrm{rep}(\lambda^{\prime})=k
. Furthermore,
\lambda^{\prime}
can be any of the partitions satisfying
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{n-2}
and
\mathrm{rep}(\lambda^{\prime})=k
. Therefore, the number of partitions
\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
satisfying
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}+1
and
\mathrm{rep}(\lambda)=k
is equal to
A(n-2,k)
.

(iii)

\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}+2
. In this case, by removing one box in the first row of the Young diagram of
\lambda
, we obtain
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{1}-1,\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots)
. It is clear that
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{n-1}
,
\mathrm{rep}(\lambda^{\prime})=k
and
{\lambda^{\prime}}_{1}\geq{\lambda^{\prime}}_{2}+1
. By (i), the number of partitions
\lambda^{\prime}
satisfying
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{n-1}
,
\mathrm{rep}(\lambda^{\prime})=k
and
{\lambda^{\prime}}_{1}={\lambda^{\prime}}_{2}
is equal to
A(n-2,k-1)
. Thus, the number of partitions
\lambda^{\prime}
satisfying
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{n-1}
,
\mathrm{rep}(\lambda^{\prime})=k
and
{\lambda^{\prime}}_{1}\geq{\lambda^{\prime}}_{2}+1
is equal to
A(n-1,k)-A(n-2,k-1)
. Finally, this implies that the number of partitions
\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
satisfying
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}+2
and
\mathrm{rep}(\lambda)=k
is equal to
A(n-1,k)-A(n-2,k-1)
.

Combining the above three cases gives (
2.1
).
∎

Lemma 6
.

The number
B(n,k)
satisfies the recurrence relation

(2.2)

B(n,k)=B(n-1,k)+B(n-1,k-1)+B(n-2,k)-B(n-2,k-1)

for
n\geq 2
with the initial value
B(1,0)=1

Proof.

Suppose that
n\geq 2
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots)

\mathcal{H}_{n}
with
\mathrm{even}(\lambda)=k
. Let

(resp.

\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
with
\lambda_{1}
odd (resp. even) and
\mathrm{even}(\lambda)=k
. To obtain the recursion formula for

is more involved and we again consider three cases:

(i)

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
. In this case, consider the deletion of the largest part of
\lambda
, we see that the number of partitions
\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
satisfying
\lambda_{1}
odd (resp. even) and
\mathrm{even}(\lambda)=k
is equal to

(resp.

).

(ii)

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}+1
. In this case, again by considering the deletion of the largest part of
\lambda
, we see that the number of partitions
\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
satisfying
\lambda_{1}

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}+1

is equal to

(resp.
B_{o}(n-2,k-1)
).

(iii)

\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}+2
. In this case, by removing two boxes in the first row of the Young diagram of
\lambda
, we obtain
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{1}-2,\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots)
. It is clear that
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{n-2}
and
\mathrm{even}(\lambda^{\prime})=k
. Furthermore,
\lambda^{\prime}
can be any of
the partitions satisfying
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{n-2}
and
\mathrm{even}(\lambda^{\prime})=k
. Therefore, the number of partitions
\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
satisfying
\lambda_{1}

(resp.

).

Combining the above three cases together, we derive that

(2.3)

B(n,k)=B(n-2,k)+B_{o}(n-1,k)+B_{o}(n-2,k-1)+B_{e}(n-1,k-1)+B_{e}(n-2,k).

On the other hand, by deleting the first part of
\lambda
when
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
or by removing one box in the first row of the Young diagram of
\lambda
when
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}+1
, we see that

(2.4)

B_{o}(n-1,k-1)=B_{o}(n-2,k-1)+B_{e}(n-2,k)

and

(2.5)

B_{e}(n-1,k)=B_{e}(n-2,k-1)+B_{o}(n-2,k-1)=B(n-2,k-1).

Now (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) together results in (
2.2
), as desired.

For positive integers

and

1\leq k\leq n
, a subset

of

is called a

-extraordinary subset
if

equals the
k
-th smallest element of

. For example, all the

-extraordinary subsets of
[5]
are

\{1,2\},\,\,\{1,3,4\},\,\,\{1,3,5\},\,\,\{2,3,4\},\,\,\{2,3,5\}.

Let

be the number of

-extraordinary subsets of
[n]
. In
[

]
, Grimaldi showed that

C(n,k)=C(n-1,k)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}C(n-k-2+i,i)

for

and the initial value

.
It follows from the above recursion that

\displaystyle C(n,k)

\displaystyle=C(n-1,k)+C(n-2,k)+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}C(n-k-2+i,i)

\displaystyle=C(n-1,k)+C(n-2,k)+C(n-1,k-1)-C(n-2,k-1).

Comparing with the recursions for

and

derived in the above two lemmas (and their initial values) yields the following result.

Proposition 7
.

For

A(n,k)=B(n,k)=C(n,k+1).

2.2.
A generating function proof

We will compute the generating function for the joint distribution of
(\mathrm{rep},\mathrm{even})
on
\mathcal{H}_{n}
.
For a partition
\lambda
, let
\mathrm{dist}(\lambda)
be the number of
distinct parts
of
\lambda
.
Then,
\mathrm{dist}(\lambda)=\ell(\lambda)-\mathrm{rep}(\lambda)
.
A partition
\lambda
can also be represented as
\lambda=1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\cdots
, where
m_{i}
is the number of parts equating
i
of
\lambda
. Consider the weight function

\mathrm{wt}(\lambda)=p^{\mathrm{dist}(\lambda)}q^{\mathrm{even}(\lambda)}x_{1}^{m_{1}}x_{2}^{m_{2}}\cdots.

Then,

(2.6)

1+\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}\mathrm{wt}(\lambda)=\biggl(1+\frac{px_{1}}{1-x_{1}}\biggr)\biggl(1+\frac{pqx_{2}}{1-qx_{2}}\biggr)\biggl(1+\frac{px_{3}}{1-x_{3}}\biggr)\biggl(1+\frac{pqx_{4}}{1-qx_{4}}\biggr)\cdots,

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the set of all integer partitions.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{n}
be the set of partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots)
such that
\lambda_{1}=n
. Then,

(2.7)

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{2n}}\mathrm{wt}(\lambda)=\biggl(1+\frac{px_{1}}{1-x_{1}}\biggr)\biggl(1+\frac{pqx_{2}}{1-qx_{2}}\biggr)\cdots\biggl(1+\frac{px_{2n-1}}{1-x_{2n-1}}\biggr)\biggl(\frac{pqx_{2n}}{1-qx_{2n}}\biggr)

and

(2.8)

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{2n+1}}\mathrm{wt}(\lambda)=\biggl(1+\frac{px_{1}}{1-x_{1}}\biggr)\biggl(1+\frac{pqx_{2}}{1-qx_{2}}\biggr)\cdots\biggl(1+\frac{pqx_{2n}}{1-qx_{2n}}\biggr)\biggl(\frac{px_{2n+1}}{1-x_{2n+1}}\biggr).

It follows from (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) that

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}p^{\mathrm{dist}(\lambda)}q^{\mathrm{even}(\lambda)}x^{\ell(\lambda)}y^{\lambda_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{pxy}{1-x}+\biggl(\frac{pqx}{1-qx}+\frac{pxy(1-qx+pqx)}{(1-x)(1-qx)}\biggr)\sum_{n\geq 1}\biggl(1+\frac{px}{1-x}\biggr)^{n}\biggl(1+\frac{pqx}{1-qx}\biggr)^{n-1}y^{2n}

\displaystyle=\frac{pxy}{1-x}+\frac{\biggl(\frac{pqx}{1-qx}+\frac{pxy(1-qx+pqx)}{(1-x)(1-qx)}\biggr)\biggl(1+\frac{px}{1-x}\biggr)y^{2}}{1-\biggl(1+\frac{px}{1-x}\biggr)\biggl(1+\frac{pqx}{1-qx}\biggr)y^{2}}.

Therefore, we have

Theorem 8
.

The generating function for partitions by size of first parts, number of parts and the pair
(\mathrm{dist},\mathrm{even})
is

(2.9)

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}p^{\mathrm{dist}(\lambda)}q^{\mathrm{even}(\lambda)}x^{\ell(\lambda)}y^{\lambda_{1}}=\frac{pxy}{1-x}+\frac{\biggl(\frac{pqx}{1-qx}+\frac{pxy(1-qx+pqx)}{(1-x)(1-qx)}\biggr)\biggl(1+\frac{px}{1-x}\biggr)y^{2}}{1-\biggl(1+\frac{px}{1-x}\biggr)\biggl(1+\frac{pqx}{1-qx}\biggr)y^{2}}.

Consequently (
p\leftarrow p^{-1},x\leftarrow px,y\leftarrow x
),

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}p^{\mathrm{rep}(\lambda)}q^{\mathrm{even}(\lambda)}x^{\Gamma(\lambda)}=\frac{x(1-(p-1)q(x^{2}+x))}{1-p(1+q)x-(1-p^{2}q)x^{2}-(1-p)(1+q)x^{3}-(p-1)^{2}qx^{4}}.

In particular,

(2.10)

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}p^{\mathrm{rep}(\lambda)}x^{\Gamma(\lambda)}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}p^{\mathrm{even}(\lambda)}x^{\Gamma(\lambda)}=\frac{x}{1-(1+p)x-(1-p)x^{2}}.

Remark 9
.

Dividing both sides of (
2.9
) by
x
and then substituting
p\leftarrow p^{-1},q\leftarrow 1,x\leftarrow-px,y\leftarrow x
yields

(2.11)

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}(-1)^{\ell(\lambda)}p^{\mathrm{rep}(\lambda)}x^{\Gamma(\lambda)}=\frac{x}{(p-1)(x^{2}-x)-1}.

Thus, if we denote

h_{n}(p)=\sum_{\Gamma(\lambda)=n}(-1)^{\ell(\lambda)}p^{\mathrm{rep}(\lambda)}=\sum_{\Gamma(\lambda)=n\atop\ell(\lambda)\text{ even}}p^{\mathrm{rep}(\lambda)}-\sum_{\Gamma(\lambda)=n\atop\ell(\lambda)\text{ odd}}p^{\mathrm{rep}(\lambda)},

then
\sum_{n\geq 1}h_{n}(p)x^{n}=\frac{x}{(p-1)(x^{2}-x)-1}
, which is equivalent to the recurrence relation

h_{n}(p)=(1-p)(h_{n-1}(p)-h_{n-2}(p))

for

with initial values

and

. In particular, we have

h_{n}(0)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{m},\quad&\text{if $n=(6m-3\pm 1)/2$};\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

This was first proved by Fu and Tang

•

•

It can be easily deduced from (

m=n+1
. Define
\tilde{\phi}^{-1}(w)
to be the concatenation
01w_{2}\cdots w_{m-1}\tilde{\phi}^{-1}(w_{m}\cdots w_{n})
.

It is easy to check by induction on
n
that
\tilde{\phi}
and
\tilde{\phi}^{-1}
are inverse to each other.
∎

3.
Three proofs of Theorem
4

For a partition
\lambda
, let

\mathrm{mod}_{d}(\lambda):=|\{i:\lambda_{i}\equiv 1\text{($\mathrm{mod}\,d+1$)}\}|\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{mod}^{\prime}_{d}(\lambda):=\ell(\lambda)-\mathrm{mod}_{d}(\lambda).

Then,
\mathrm{mod}^{\prime}_{d}(\lambda)
is
d
-generalization of
\mathrm{even}(\lambda)
as
\mathrm{mod}^{\prime}_{1}(\lambda)=\mathrm{even}(\lambda)
. Introduce the
d
-generalization of
\mathrm{rep}(\lambda)
by

\mathrm{dif}_{d}(\lambda):=|\{i:1\leq i<\ell(\lambda),\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}<d\}|.

It is clear that Theorem
4
is equivalent to

(3.1)

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\mathrm{dif}_{d}(\lambda)\geq\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\mathrm{mod}^{\prime}_{d}(\lambda).

3.1.
A generating function proof

First we compute the generating function for partitions by the perimeter and the statistic
\mathrm{mod}_{d}
.

Lemma 13
.

We have

(3.2)

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}t^{\mathrm{mod}_{d}(\lambda)}x^{\ell(\lambda)}y^{\lambda_{1}}=\frac{txy(1-x)^{d}+\frac{xy}{x+y-1}(y^{d+1}-y(1-x)^{d})}{(1-tx)(1-x)^{d}-y^{d+1}}.

In particular,

(3.3)

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}t^{\mathrm{mod}_{d}^{\prime}(\lambda)}x^{\Gamma(\lambda)}=\frac{x(tx^{d+1}+(1-tx)^{d}(x-1))}{(1-x-tx)((1-tx)^{d}(x-1)+x^{d+1})}.

Proof.

Since every integer partition
\lambda
can be written as
\lambda=1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\cdots
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}t^{m_{d}(\lambda)}x^{\ell(\lambda)}y^{\lambda_{1}}

\displaystyle=tx\sum_{n\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\,d+1)}y^{n}\biggl(\frac{1}{1-tx}\biggr)^{\lceil n/(d+1)\rceil}\biggl(\frac{1}{1-x}\biggr)^{n-\lceil n/(d+1)\rceil},

\displaystyle\qquad+x\sum_{n\not\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\,d+1)}y^{n}\biggl(\frac{1}{1-tx}\biggr)^{\lceil n/(d+1)\rceil}\biggl(\frac{1}{1-x}\biggr)^{n-\lceil n/(d+1)\rceil}

\displaystyle=tx\sum_{k\geq 0}\biggl(\frac{y}{1-tx}\biggr)^{k+1}\biggl(\frac{y}{1-x}\biggr)^{dk}

\displaystyle\qquad+x\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k\geq 0}\biggl(\frac{y}{1-tx}\biggr)^{k+1}\biggl(\frac{y}{1-x}\biggr)^{dk+i},

which is simplified to (
3.2
).
∎

Next we compute the generating function for partitions by the perimeter and the statistic
\mathrm{dif}_{d}
.

Lemma 14
.

We have

(3.4)

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}t^{\mathrm{dif}_{d}(\lambda)}x^{\ell(\lambda)}y^{\lambda_{1}}=\frac{xy}{1-y-tx(1-y^{d})-xy^{d}}.

In particular,

(3.5)

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}t^{\mathrm{dif}_{d}(\lambda)}x^{\Gamma(\lambda)}=\frac{x}{1-x-tx(1-x^{d})-x^{d+1}}.

Proof.

Notice that in the 01-sequence
w(\lambda)
of
\lambda
, each maximal segment
00\cdots 01
(
k
0’s followed by one 1) after the leftmost
1
contributes a
txy^{k}
in the following generating function if
0\leq k\leq d-1
; and contributes a
xy^{k}
if
k\geq d
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}t^{\mathrm{dif}_{d}(\lambda)}x^{\ell(\lambda)}y^{\lambda_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{xy}{1-y}\cdot\left(1+\biggl(\frac{tx(1-y^{d})}{1-y}+\frac{xy^{d}}{1-y}\biggr)+\biggl(\frac{tx(1-y^{d})}{1-y}+\frac{xy^{d}}{1-y}\biggr)^{2}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{xy}{1-y-tx(1-y^{d})-xy^{d}},

as desired.
∎

Take the derivative with respect to
t
and then set
t=1
in (
3.3
) gives

(3.6)

\sum_{n\geq 1}x^{n}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\mathrm{mod}^{\prime}_{d}(\lambda)=\frac{x(1-x)(x^{d+1}-x(1-x)^{d})}{(1-2x)^{2}(x^{d+1}-(1-x)^{d+1})}.

On the other hand, the same operation on (
3.5
) yields

(3.7)

\sum_{n\geq 1}x^{n}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\mathrm{dif}_{d}(\lambda)=\frac{x^{2}(1-x^{d})}{(1-2x)^{2}}.

To finish the proof of Theorem
4
(or equivalently, inequality (
3.1
)), it remains to show that

(3.8)

\frac{x^{2}(1-x^{d})}{(1-2x)^{2}}-\frac{x(1-x)(x^{d+1}-x(1-x)^{d})}{(1-2x)^{2}(x^{d+1}-(1-x)^{d+1})}=\frac{x^{d+2}(1-2x+x^{d+1}-(1-x)^{d+1})}{(1-2x)^{2}((1-x)^{d+1}-x^{d+1})}

has nonnegative coefficients for each
d\geq 1
. Since

\frac{1-2x+x^{d+1}-(1-x)^{d+1}}{(1-2x)^{2}((1-x)^{d+1}-x^{d+1})}=\frac{1}{1-2x}\biggl(\frac{1}{(1-x)^{d+1}-x^{d+1}}-\frac{1}{1-2x}\biggr),

Theorem
4
then follows from the following interesting positivity result.

Theorem 15
.

The rational function

\frac{1}{(1-x)^{d+1}-x^{d+1}}-\frac{1}{1-2x}=:\Delta_{d}(x)

has nonnegative coefficients for each
d\geq 1
.

Note that
\Delta_{0}(x)=\Delta_{1}(x)=0
and

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}x^{n}\sum_{i\geq 0}{n\choose 3i+1}=x+2x^{2}+3x^{3}+5x^{4}+10x^{5}+21x^{6}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\Delta_{3}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}x^{n}\sum_{i\geq 0}2{n+1\choose 4i+2}=2x+6x^{2}+12x^{3}+20x^{4}+32x^{5}+56x^{6}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\Delta_{4}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}x^{n}\sum_{i\geq 0}\biggl(3{n+2\choose 5i+3}-{n\choose 5i+2}\biggr)=3x+11x^{2}+27x^{3}+54x^{4}+95x^{5}+156x^{6}+\cdots.

For two polynomials
f(x),g(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
, we write

f(x)\geq_{x}g(x)\Leftrightarrow f(x)-g(x)\text{ has nonnegative coefficients}.

To prove Theorem
15
, we need the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 16
.

For
d\geq 1
, we have

\frac{x}{(1-x)^{d}}\geq_{x}\frac{x^{d}}{(1-x)^{d}}.

Proof.

This follows from the binomial theorem

(1-x)^{-d}=\sum_{k\geq 0}{d+k-1\choose k}x^{k}

and the monotonicity
{d+k\choose k+1}\geq{d+k-1\choose k}
for
k\geq 0
.
∎

We can now prove Theorem
15
.

Proof of Theorem
15
.

By Lemma
16
and by induction on
d
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{(1-x)^{d}}-\frac{1}{1-x}\biggl(1+\frac{x}{1-x}+(\frac{x}{1-x})^{2}+\cdots+(\frac{x}{1-x})^{d-1}\biggr)

\displaystyle\geq_{x}\frac{1}{(1-x)^{d}}-\frac{x}{(1-x)^{d}}-\frac{1}{1-x}\biggl(1+\frac{x}{1-x}+(\frac{x}{1-x})^{2}+\cdots+(\frac{x}{1-x})^{d-2}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{d-1}}-\frac{1}{1-x}\biggl(1+\frac{x}{1-x}+(\frac{x}{1-x})^{2}+\cdots+(\frac{x}{1-x})^{d-2}\biggr)

\displaystyle\geq_{x}0

for each
d\geq 2
. It then follows from the above assertion that

\displaystyle\Delta_{d-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{d}-x^{d}}-\frac{1}{1-2x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{d}}\frac{1}{1-(\frac{x}{1-x})^{d}}-\frac{1}{1-x}\frac{1}{1-\frac{x}{1-x}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-x)^{d}}\biggl(1+(\frac{x}{1-x})^{d}+(\frac{x}{1-x})^{2d}+(\frac{x}{1-x})^{3d}+\cdots\biggr)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{1-x}\biggl(1+\frac{x}{1-x}+(\frac{x}{1-x})^{2}+(\frac{x}{1-x})^{3}+\cdots\biggr)

\displaystyle=\biggl(\sum_{k\geq 0}(\frac{x}{1-x})^{kd}\biggr)\biggl(\frac{1}{(1-x)^{d}}-\frac{1}{1-x}\biggl(1+\frac{x}{1-x}+(\frac{x}{1-x})^{2}+\cdots+(\frac{x}{1-x})^{d-1}\biggr)\biggr)

\displaystyle\geq_{x}0,

which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

3.2.
An injective proof

This section is motivated by finding an interpretation for

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\mathrm{dif}_{d}(\lambda)-\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n}}\mathrm{mod}^{\prime}_{d}(\lambda).

A partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{i}^{*},\cdots)
whose
i
-th part,
1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda)
, receives a star is called a
labeled partition
. It is convenience to represent such a labeled partition by
(\lambda,i)
. Let us consider the two sets of labeled partitions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{n,d}:=\{(\lambda,i):\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n},0\leq\lambda_{i-1}-\lambda_{i}<d\}\quad\text{and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n,d}:=\{(\lambda,i):\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n},\lambda_{i}\not\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\,d+1)\}.

For example,

\mathcal{D}_{4,2}=\{32^{*},33^{*},211^{*},21^{*}1,221^{*},22^{*}1,222^{*},22^{*}2,1111^{*},111^{*}1,11^{*}11\}

and

\mathcal{M}_{4,2}=\{3^{*}1,32^{*},3^{*}2,33^{*},3^{*}3,2^{*}11,22^{*}1,2^{*}21,222^{*},22^{*}2,2^{*}22\}.

It is clear that Theorem
4
is equivalent to
|\mathcal{D}_{n,d}|\geq|\mathcal{M}_{n,d}|
for
n,d\geq 1
. The purpose of this section is to provide an injective proof of Theorem
4
that leads to a partition interpretation of
|\mathcal{D}_{n,d}|-|\mathcal{M}_{n,d}|
.

Theorem 17
.

For any fixed
n,d\geq 1
, there exists an injection
\xi
from
\mathcal{M}_{n,d}
to
\mathcal{D}_{n,d}
such that

(3.9)

\mathcal{D}_{n,d}\setminus\xi(\mathcal{M}_{n,d})=\{(\lambda,i)\in\mathcal{D}_{n,d}:\lambda_{i-1}\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\,d+1),\lambda_{i}\not\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\,d+1)\}.

Proof.

For a labeled partition
(\lambda,i)\in\mathcal{M}_{n,d}
, we define
\xi(\lambda,i)\in\mathcal{D}_{n,d}
according to the following two cases.
Suppose that
\lambda_{i}=l(d+1)+k
for some
2\leq k\leq d+1
and
l\geq 0
. Note that
0\leq k-2<d
and we use the convention
\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)+1}=0
.

•

If
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}\leq k-2
, then set
\xi(\lambda,i)=(\lambda,i+1)
. As
0\leq k-2<d
, it is clear that
\xi(\lambda,i)\in\mathcal{D}_{n,d}
. In this case,
\lambda_{i+1}\not\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\,d+1)
.

•

If
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}>k-2
, then set
\xi(\lambda,i)=(\lambda^{\prime},i+1)
, where
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\lambda^{\prime}_{2}\cdots\lambda^{\prime}_{\ell(\lambda)+1}
is a partition with one more part than
\lambda
defined as

\lambda^{\prime}_{j}=\begin{cases}\lambda_{j}-1,&\quad\text{if $j\leq i$};\\
l(d+1)+1,&\quad\text{if $j=i+1$};\\
\lambda_{j-1},&\quad\text{if $i+1<j\leq\ell(\lambda)+1$}.\end{cases}

Since
\lambda^{\prime}_{i}-\lambda^{\prime}_{i+1}=l(d+1)+k-1-l(d+1)-1=k-2
, we see
\xi(\lambda,i)\in\mathcal{D}_{n,d}
.
In this case,
\lambda_{i+1}=l(d+1)+1\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\,d+1)
.

For example, if
(\lambda,3)=(14,13,11^{*},10,5,2)\in\mathcal{M}_{19,5}
then
\xi(\lambda,3)=(14,13,11,10^{*},5,2)\in\mathcal{D}_{19,5}
is constructed in the first case, and if
(\lambda,3)=(14,13,11^{*},5,5,2)\in\mathcal{M}_{19,5}
then
\xi(\lambda,3)=(13,12,10,7^{*},5,5,2)\in\mathcal{D}_{19,5}
is constructed in the second case.

To see that the mapping
\xi
is an injection, observe that for any
(\lambda,i+1)\in\mathcal{D}_{n,d}
:

•

(\lambda,i+1)
is the image under
\xi
from the first case above if
\lambda_{i+1}\not\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\,d+1)
and
\lambda_{i}\not\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\,d+1)
;

•

(\lambda,i+1)
is the image under
\xi
from the second case above if
\lambda_{i+1}\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\,d+1)
;

•

(\lambda,i+1)
is not an image under
\xi
if
\lambda_{i+1}\not\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\,d+1)
but
\lambda_{i}\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\,d+1)
.

Since the above two cases of
\xi
are reversible,
\xi
is an injection and (
3.9
) holds.
∎

Example 18
.

As an example of (
3.9
), we see that the number
|\mathcal{D}_{6,2}|-|\mathcal{M}_{6,2}|=4
counts the labeled partitions
(4,3^{*},1)
,
(4,3^{*},2)
,
(4,4,3^{*})
and
(4,3^{*},3)
.

3.3.
A bijective proof

The following stronger version of Theorem
4
was originally suggested by numerical computations.

Theorem 19
.

Fix
d\geq 1
. There exists a bijection
\phi_{d}:\mathcal{H}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{n}
such that

\mathrm{dif}_{d}(\lambda)\geq\mathrm{mod}^{\prime}_{d}(\phi_{d}(\lambda))

for every
\lambda\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
. Moreover,
\mathrm{dif}_{d}(\lambda)=0
if and only if
\mathrm{mod}^{\prime}_{d}(\phi_{d}(\lambda))=0
.

Remark 20
.

Theorem
19
is a common generalization of Theorems
2
and
4
. At the beginning, we attempted to prove Theorem
19
by using generating function but failed. To find such a proof remains an interesting open problem.

The construction of
\phi_{d}
is a
d
-extension of
\phi
defined in Theorem
11
.

Proof of Theorem
19
.

Under the correspondence
\lambda\mapsto w=w(\lambda)
in Lemma
12
, the two statistics
\mathrm{dif}_{d}(\lambda)
and
\mathrm{mod}^{\prime}_{d}(\lambda)
are transformed to

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{dif}}_{d}(w)

\displaystyle=|\{i\in[n-1]:w_{i}=w_{i+k}=1\text{ and $w_{i+1}=\cdots=w_{i+k-1}=0$ for some $k\in[d]$}\}|,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{mod}}^{\prime}(w)

\displaystyle=|\{2\leq i\leq n:w_{i}=1\text{ and }\#\{0\leq j<i:w_{j}=0\}\not\equiv 1(\mathrm{mod}\,d+1)\}|.

So it is sufficient to define recursively a bijection
\tilde{\phi}_{d}:\Lambda_{n}\rightarrow\Lambda_{n}
such that

(3.10)

\widetilde{\mathrm{dif}}_{d}(w)\geq\widetilde{\mathrm{mod}}^{\prime}_{d}(\tilde{\phi}_{d}(w))

for each
w\in\Lambda_{n}
and then set
\phi_{d}=w^{-1}\circ\tilde{\phi}_{d}\circ w
.

Set
\tilde{\phi}_{d}(\emptyset)=\emptyset
and
\tilde{\phi}_{d}(01)=01
. For each
w\in\Lambda_{n}
(
n\geq 2
), we distinguish the following two cases:

•

If
w=00w_{2}\cdots w_{n}
, then
\tilde{\phi}_{d}(w)
is obtained from
\tilde{\phi}_{d}(0w_{2}\cdots w_{n})
by inserting
1
immediately after the initial
0
.

•

If
w=01w_{2}\cdots w_{n}
, then suppose
w_{m}=0
is the
(d+1)
-th
0
in
w
if exists, otherwise set
m=n+1
. Define
\tilde{\phi}_{d}(w)
to be the concatenation
00w_{2}\cdots w_{m-1}\tilde{\phi}_{d}(w_{m}\cdots w_{n})
.

For example, we have

\displaystyle 001\mapsto 011\mapsto 001;\,\,0101\mapsto 0001\mapsto 0111\mapsto 0011\mapsto 0101;

\displaystyle 00001\mapsto 01111\mapsto 00111\mapsto 01011\mapsto 00011\mapsto 01101\mapsto 00101\mapsto 01001\mapsto 00001.

under the mapping
\tilde{\phi}_{2}
.
We need to verify that
\tilde{\phi}_{d}
satisfies (
3.10
) by induction on
n
depending on two cases of
\tilde{\phi}_{d}
:

•

Since inserting
1
after the initial
0
of
\tilde{\phi}_{d}(0w_{2}\cdots w_{n})
dose not change
\widetilde{\mathrm{mod}}^{\prime}
, we have

\widetilde{\mathrm{dif}}_{d}(w)=\widetilde{\mathrm{dif}}_{d}(0w_{2}\cdots w_{n})\geq\widetilde{\mathrm{mod}}^{\prime}_{d}(\tilde{\phi}_{d}(0w_{2}\cdots w_{n}))=\widetilde{\mathrm{mod}}^{\prime}_{d}(\tilde{\phi}_{d}(w)).

•

As the two statistics
\widetilde{\mathrm{dif}}_{d}
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{mod}}^{\prime}_{d}
can be extended to any
01
sequence and notice that there are exactly
d+1

0
’s in the prefix
00w_{2}\cdots w_{m-1}
of
\tilde{\phi}_{d}(w)
whenever
m\neq n+1
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{dif}}_{d}(w)

\displaystyle\geq\widetilde{\mathrm{dif}}_{d}(01w_{2}\cdots w_{m-1})+\widetilde{\mathrm{dif}}_{d}(w_{m}\cdots w_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\widetilde{\mathrm{mod}}^{\prime}_{d}(00w_{2}\cdots w_{m-1})+\widetilde{\mathrm{mod}}^{\prime}_{d}(\tilde{\phi}_{d}(w_{m}\cdots w_{n}))

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathrm{mod}}^{\prime}_{d}(\phi_{d}(w)).

For the case
m=n+1
, we have

\widetilde{\mathrm{dif}}_{d}(w)=\widetilde{\mathrm{dif}}_{d}(01w_{2}\cdots w_{n})=\widetilde{\mathrm{mod}}^{\prime}_{d}(00w_{2}\cdots w_{n})=\widetilde{\mathrm{mod}}^{\prime}_{d}(\tilde{\phi}_{d}(w)).

To see that
\tilde{\phi}_{d}
is a bijection, we construct its inverse
\tilde{\phi}_{d}^{-1}
explicitly. Given
w\in\Lambda_{n}
(
n\geq 2
), we consider the following two cases:

•

If
w=01w_{2}\cdots w_{n}
, then
\tilde{\phi}_{d}^{-1}(w)
is constructed from
\tilde{\phi}_{d}^{-1}(0w_{2}\cdots w_{n})
by inserting
0
immediately after the initial
0
.

•

If
w=00w_{2}\cdots w_{n}
, then suppose
w_{m}=0
is the
(d+2)
-th
0
in
w
if exists, otherwise set
m=n+1
. Define
\tilde{\phi}_{d}^{-1}(w)
to be the concatenation
01w_{2}\cdots w_{m-1}\tilde{\phi}_{d}^{-1}(w_{m}\cdots w_{n})
.

It can be checked routinely that
\tilde{\phi}_{d}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{d}^{-1}
are inverse to each other, which proves that
\tilde{\phi}_{d}
is a bijection.

Finally, we need to verify that whenever
\mathrm{mod}^{\prime}_{d}(w)=0
then
\mathrm{dif}_{d}(\tilde{\phi}_{d}^{-1}(w))=0
by induction on
n
according to the two cases of
\tilde{\phi}_{d}^{-1}
:

•

In the first case, we have

0=\mathrm{mod}^{\prime}_{d}(w)=\mathrm{mod}^{\prime}_{d}(0w_{2}\cdots w_{n})=\mathrm{dif}_{d}(\tilde{\phi}_{d}^{-1}(0w_{2}\cdots w_{n}))=\mathrm{dif}_{d}(\tilde{\phi}_{d}^{-1}(w)).

•

In the second case, since
\mathrm{mod}^{\prime}_{d}(w)=0
, we must have

and the prefix

of

The proof of the theorem is now complete.
∎

Further remarks

We will conclude this paper with the following three remarks.

(1)

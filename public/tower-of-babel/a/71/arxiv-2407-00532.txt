Subspaces spanned by eigenforms with nonvanishing twisted central L L -values
June Kayath, Connor Lane, Ben Neifeld, Tianyu Ni, Hui Xue

Abstract
. In this paper, we construct explicit spanning sets for two spaces. One is the subspace generated by integral-weight Hecke eigenforms with nonvanishing quadratic twisted central L L -values. The other is a subspace generated by half-integral weight Hecke eigenforms with certain nonvanishing Fourier coefficients. Along the way, we show that these subspaces are isomorphic via the Shimura lift.

1.6

The twisted

This section proves our main results, assuming the necessary results to be proved later. We begin by proving Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Recall that
\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}
(
1.15
) and
\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}
(
1.14
) are cuspforms. Write

\mathcal{S}_{D}(\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}(z))=\sum_{n\geq 1}g_{D,k,e}(n)q^{n}\quad{\rm and}\quad\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}f_{D,k,e}(n)q^{n}.

Comparing the Fourier coefficients
f_{D,k,e}(n)
and
g_{D,k,e}(n)
that are respectively given by Lemma
6.5
and Lemma
6.6
, it suffices to show for each nonnegative integer pair
(a_{1},a_{2})
with
a_{1}+a_{2}=n|D_{1}|
that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\binom{k+e-1}{e}\sum_{r=0}^{2e}(-1)^{r}a_{1}^{r}a_{2}^{2e-r}\binom{2e+k-1}{2e-r}\binom{2e+k-1}{r}

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\binom{k+2e-1}{2e}\sum_{r+s=e}(-1)^{r}\binom{k+e-1}{s}\binom{e-1/2}{r}4^{r}(a_{2}-a_{1})^{2s}(a_{1}a_{2})^{r}.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
R\leq S
and compare the coefficients of the monomial
a_{1}^{R}a_{2}^{S}
of the two sides of (
2.2
). The
a_{1}^{R}a_{2}^{S}
-coefficient on the left hand side of (
2.2
) is

(2.3)

\displaystyle(-1)^{R}\binom{k+e-1}{e}\binom{2e+k-1}{2e-R}\binom{2e+k-1}{R},

and the right hand side of (
2.2
) has
a_{1}^{R}a_{2}^{S}
-coefficient

(2.4)

\displaystyle\binom{k+2e-1}{2e}\sum_{r=0}^{R}(-1)^{r}\binom{k+e-1}{e-r}\binom{e-1/2}{r}4^{r}\binom{2e-2r}{R-r}(-1)^{R-r}

(2.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{R}\binom{k+2e-1}{2e}\sum_{r=0}^{R}\binom{k+e-1}{e-r}\binom{e-1/2}{r}4^{r}\binom{2e-2r}{R-r}.

Using Lemma
2.1
, we finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
R\leq e
be nonnegative and
k\geq 4
. Then we have the following identity

\binom{k+e-1}{e}\binom{k+2e-1}{2e-R}\binom{k+2e-1}{R}=\binom{k+2e-1}{2e}\sum_{r=0}^{R}4^{r}\binom{k+e-1}{e-r}\binom{e-1/2}{r}\binom{2e-2r}{R-r},

where fractional binomial coefficients are defined by the
\Gamma
function.

Proof.

We reproduce the proof of
[
21
, Proposition 2.1]
. By definition we have

\binom{e-1/2}{r}=\frac{\Gamma(e+1/2)}{\Gamma(r+1)\Gamma(e+1/2-r)}.

By Legendre’s duplication formulas we have

\Gamma(e+1/2)=\frac{(2e)!}{4^{e}e!}\sqrt{\pi},\qquad\Gamma(e-r+1/2)=\frac{(2(e-r))!}{4^{e-r}(e-r)!}\sqrt{\pi}.

These together yield

\binom{e-1/2}{r}=\frac{(2e)!4^{e-r}(e-r)!\sqrt{\pi}}{r!4^{e}e!(2(e-r))!\sqrt{\pi}}=\frac{(2e)!4^{r}(e-r)!}{r!e!(2(e-r))!},

which yields the following formula for each term on the left hand side

\displaystyle 4^{r}\hskip-1.0pt\binom{k+2e-1}{2e}\hskip-2.0pt\binom{k+e-1}{e-r}\hskip-2.0pt\binom{e-1/2}{r}\hskip-2.0pt\binom{2e-2r}{R-r}\hskip-1.0pt=\hskip-1.0pt\frac{(k+2e-1)!(k+e-1)!}{(k-1)!\hskip-1.0pt(k+r-1)!\hskip-1.0pt(R-r)!\hskip-1.0pt(2e-R-r)!e!r!}.

The right hand side expands in to

\binom{k+e-1}{e}\binom{k+2e-1}{2e-R}\binom{k+2e-1}{R}=\frac{(k+e-1)!(k+2e-1)!(k+2e-1)!}{e!(k-1)!(2e-R)!(k+R-1)!R!(k+2e-1-R)!}.

If we cancel
(k+e-1)!(k+2e-1)!
from both sides, and multiply by
R!(k+2e-R-1)
, we see that it suffices to show

\sum_{r=0}^{R}\binom{R}{R-r}\binom{k+2e-R-1}{k+r-1}=\binom{k+2e-1}{k+R-1}.

After applying the involution
r\mapsto R-r
, this is then the Vandermonde’s identity
[
17
, p.11]

\sum_{j=0}^{t}\binom{n}{j}\binom{m}{t-j}=\binom{n+m}{t}

for the case of
n=R
,
m=k+2e-R-1
, and
t=k+R-1
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

be an odd fundamental discriminant with

\mathcal{S}_{D}
restricts to an isomorphism

Proof.

Recall that by
[

or
[
8

\mathcal{S}_{D}

Thus, in order to show

\dim S_{\ell+1/2}^{0,D}(4)=\dim S_{2\ell}^{0,D}(1)

Note that

is the number of Hecke eigenforms in
S_{2\ell}(1)
with nonzero central twisted

\dim S_{\ell+1/2}^{0,D}(4)
is the number of Hecke eigenforms in
S^{+}_{\ell+1/2}(4)
with nonzero

[
8

, these two nonvanishing conditions are the same under the Shimura correspondence, thus we conclude that
\dim S_{\ell+1/2}^{0,D}(4)=\dim S_{2\ell}^{0,D}(1)
.
∎

Remark

In the

Here the

Proposition 2.3

The set

By Proposition

\{\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}\}_{2k+4e=2\ell}
. So it suffices to show that the orthogonal complement of the span of
\{\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}\}_{2k+4e=2\ell}
is contained in
S_{2\ell}^{-,D}(1)
.

We will show that any modular form
G=\sum_{j}c_{j}g_{j}
which is a linear combination of normalized Hecke eigenforms in
g_{j}\in S_{2\ell}^{0,D}(1)
such that
\langle G,\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}\rangle=0
for all
\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}
must be zero.

Note that Proposition
5.6
and (
2.6
) imply that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e},g_{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma(2k+4e-1)\Gamma(k+2e)}{(2e)!(4\pi)^{2k+4e-1}\Gamma(k)}\frac{L_{D}(1-k)}{L_{D}(k)}\frac{(-2\pi i)^{2k+2e-1}}{(2k+2e-2)!}L(g_{j},D,k+2e)r_{2k+2e-2}(g_{j}).

Thus, the orthogonality condition
\langle G,\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}\rangle=0
is equivalent to

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}c_{j}L(g_{j},D,k+2e)r_{2k+2e-2}(g_{j})=0.

Following an idea from the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 1]
, we define another form in
S_{2\ell}(1)
by

F=\sum_{j}c_{j}L(g_{j},D,k+2e)g_{j}.

Hence (

) implies that

(2.9)

\displaystyle r_{2k+2e-2}(F)=\sum_{j}c_{j}L(g_{j},D,k+2e)r_{2k+2e-2}(g_{j})=0.

As
1\leq e\leq\lfloor\frac{\ell-4}{2}\rfloor
and
k+2e=\ell
, we have
\ell-2\geq k\geq 4
. Then
t=2k+2e-2
ranges through all even values
\ell+2\leq t\leq 2\ell-4
, so
r_{t}(F)=0
for all even
\ell+2\leq t\leq 2\ell-4
.
As a result of the following lemma, we have
F=0
. Since
L(g_{j},D,k+2e)\neq 0
as
g_{j}\in S_{2\ell}^{0,D}(1)
, we must have
c_{j}=0
for all
j
, and thus
G=0
.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
F\in S_{2\ell}(1)
and
\ell\geq 6
, and let
r_{t}(F)
be the
t
-th Period of
F
. If
r_{t}(F)=0
for all even
t
such that
\ell+2\leq t\leq 2\ell-4
, then
F=0
.

Proof.

We follow the idea of
[
22
]
.
By the Eichler-Shimura theory
[
13
, Proposition 2.3 (b)]
and
[
22
, Remark 2.4]
, we know that
F=0
if and only if
r_{t}(F)=0
for all even
2\leq t\leq 2\ell-4
.
By the Eichler-Shimura relation

(2.10)

\displaystyle r_{t}(F)+(-1)^{t}r_{2\ell-2-t}(F)=0,

and the assumption that
r_{t}(F)=0
for all even
\ell+2\leq t\leq 2\ell-4
, we know that
r_{t}(F)=0
also for all even
2\leq t\leq\ell-4
. To show that the Periods
\ell-4<t<\ell+2
are zero, we split into cases based on the parity of
\ell
.

(1)

If
\ell
is even, it suffices to show that
r_{\ell}(F)=r_{\ell-2}(F)=0
.
Since
\ell
is even, by (
2.10
)

(2.11)

\displaystyle r_{\ell}(F)+r_{\ell-2}(F)=0.

Substituting
t=\ell-2
into the Eichler-Shimura relation

(2.12)

\displaystyle(-1)^{t}r_{t}(F)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq t\\
m\equiv 0\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\binom{t}{m}r_{2\ell-2-t+m}(F)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq 2\ell-2-t\\
m\equiv t\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\binom{2\ell-2-t}{m}r_{m}(F)=0

and noting that
r_{0}(F)+r_{2\ell-2}(F)=0
, we obtain

(2.13)

\displaystyle\left(\binom{\ell}{2}+1\right)r_{\ell-2}(F)+2r_{\ell}(F)

\displaystyle=0.

This equation, along with (
2.11
) implies that
r_{\ell}(F)=r_{\ell-2}(F)=0
for
\ell\geq 6
.

(2)

If
\ell
is odd, it suffices to show that
r_{\ell-3}(F)=r_{\ell-1}(F)=r_{\ell+1}(F)=0
. Substituting
t=\ell-1
into (
2.12
), we get

3r_{\ell-1}(F)+\binom{\ell-1}{2}r_{\ell+1}(F)+\binom{\ell-1}{\ell-3}r_{\ell-3}(F)=0.

Since
\binom{\ell-1}{2}=\binom{\ell-1}{\ell-3}
, and we know by (
2.10
) that
r_{\ell-3}(F)+r_{\ell+1}(F)=0
, we conclude that
r_{\ell-1}(F)=0
.
Substituting
t=\ell+1
into (
2.12
) yields

(2.14)

\displaystyle 2r_{\ell-3}(F)+\binom{\ell+1}{2}r_{\ell-1}(F)+\left(1+\binom{\ell+1}{4}\right)r_{\ell+1}(F)=0,

and noting that
r_{\ell-1}(F)=0
, we conclude by (
2.10
) that

(2.15)

\displaystyle r_{\ell-3}(F)=r_{\ell+1}(F)=0.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Finally, we construct a spanning set for
S_{\ell+1/2}^{0,D}(4)
and finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 2.5
.

The set
\{\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}\}_{k+2e=\ell}
for
1\leq e\leq\lfloor\frac{\ell-4}{2}\rfloor
spans the subspace
S_{\ell+1/2}^{0,D}(4)

\ell\geq 6

For a Hecke eigenform

. So

(2.16)

\displaystyle\Span\{\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}\}_{k+2e=\ell}\subseteq S_{\ell+1/2}^{0,D}(4).

Note that Theorem
1.1
implies that

(2.17)

\displaystyle\dim\Span\{\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}\}_{k+2e=\ell}\geq\dim\Span\{\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}\}_{k+2e=\ell}.

2.2

2.3

(2.18)

\displaystyle\quad\Span\{\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}\}_{k+2e=\ell}=S_{2\ell}^{0,D}(1)\quad{\rm and}\dim S_{2\ell}^{0,D}(1)=\dim S_{\ell+1/2}^{0,D}(4).

∎

Combining Propositions
2.2

2.3

Lemma 3.1

And the outer sums are over all factorizations of

\displaystyle\{(16|D_{1}|\,:\,16|D_{1}|\mu-4|D_{1}|+1)\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\ZZ/4D\ZZ):\mu\text{ mod}|D_{2}|\},

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\{(c:d)\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\ZZ/4|D|\ZZ)\,:\,(c,4D)=4|D_{1}|\}.

Noting that the second set has size

and that there are
|D_{2}|

(D)=(-1)^{k}
by our assumption, we can simplify this to

(3.86)

\displaystyle\Tr^{16D}_{4D}(V(h))

\displaystyle=\left[G_{k,D}(4z)-2^{-k}\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)G_{k,D}\left(2z+\frac{1}{2}\right),(1-i(-1)^{k})\theta(|D|z)\right]_{e}

(3.87)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt+\left[G_{k,D}(4z)-G_{k,D}(8z),\varepsilon_{|D|}^{-1}(2i)^{1/2}\theta(|D|z)\right]_{e}.

Now we can finally compute the projection.

(3.88)

\displaystyle g_{D}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-(-1)^{k}i}{6}\Tr^{16D}_{4D}(V(h(z)))+\frac{1}{3}h(z)

(3.89)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-i(-1)^{k}}{6}\left[G_{k,D}(4z)-2^{-k}\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)G_{k,D}\left(2z+\frac{1}{2}\right),(1+i(-1)^{k})\theta(|D|z)\right]_{e}

(3.90)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt+\frac{1-i(-1)^{k}}{6}\left[G_{k,D}(4z)-G_{k,D}(8z),\varepsilon_{|D|}^{-1}(2i)^{1/2}\theta(|D|z)\right]_{e}

(3.91)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt+\frac{1}{3}\left[G_{k,D}(4z)-2^{-k}\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)G_{k,D}(2z),\theta(|D|z)\right]_{e}

(3.92)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left[G_{k,D}(4z)-2^{-k}\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)G_{k,D}\left(2z+\frac{1}{2}\right),\theta(|D|z)\right]_{e}

(3.93)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt+\frac{1}{3}\left[G_{k,D}(4z)-G_{k,D}(8z),\theta(|D|z)\right]_{e}

(3.94)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt+\frac{1}{3}\left[G_{k,D}(4z)-2^{-k}\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)G_{k,D}(2z),\theta(|D|z)\right]_{e}

(3.95)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left[G_{k,D}(4z)-G_{k,D}(8z)-2^{-k}\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)\left(G_{k,D}\left(2z+\frac{1}{2}\right)+G_{k,D}(2z)\right),\theta(|D|z)\right]_{e}

(3.96)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt+\frac{2}{3}[G_{k,D}(4z),\theta(|D|z)]_{e}.

Using Lemma
3.4
, we can simplify the first term to get

(3.97)

\displaystyle g_{D}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left[-\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)2^{-k+1}G_{k,D}(4z),\theta(|D|z)\right]_{e}+\frac{2}{3}[G_{k,D}(4z),\theta(|D|z)]_{e}

(3.98)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\left(1-\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)2^{-k}\right)\left[G_{k,D}(4z),\theta(|D|z)\right]_{e}.

Plugging (
3.98
) into (
3.77
) gives the desired result.
∎

4.
Eisenstein Series

In this section, we define various Eisenstein series and show that
G_{k,4D}(z)
(
1.8
) is an Eisenstein series for the cusp at infinity of level
4|D|
.
We recall the theory of Eisenstein series as developed in Miyake
[
14
, §7]
. Let
\chi
and
\psi
be Dirichlet characters mod
L
and mod
M
, respectively. For
k\geq 3
, we put

(4.1)

\displaystyle E_{k}(z;\chi,\psi)=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}\limits_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi(m)\psi(n)(mz+n)^{-k}.

Here
\mathop{\mathchoice{{\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}\kern-2.80556pt}\limits\kern 2.80556pt}}{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}}{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}}{\sum\nolimits^{\prime}}}\nolimits
is the summation over all pairs of integers
(m,n)
except
(0,0)
. For a fundamental discriminant
D
, we write
\chi_{D}(\cdot)=\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D}{\cdot}\hskip-1.0pt\right)
and
L_{D}(k)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi_{D}(n)n^{-k}
.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
14
, Theorem 7.1.3]
)
.

Assume
k\geq 3
. Let
\chi
and
\psi
be Dirichlet characters mod
L
and mod
M
, respectively, satisfying
\chi(-1)\psi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. Let
m_{\psi}
be the conductor of
\psi
, and
\psi^{0}
be the primitive character associated with
\psi
. Then

(4.2)

\displaystyle E_{k}(z;\chi,\psi)=C+A\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n)e^{2\pi inz/M},

where

(4.3)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\psi^{0})/M^{k}(k-1)!,

(4.4)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2L_{M}(k,\psi)&\chi:{\rm the~principal~character},\\
0&{\rm otherwise},\end{cases}

(4.5)

\displaystyle a(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<c\mid n}\chi(n/c)c^{k-1}\sum_{0<d\mid(l,c)}d\mu(l/d)\psi^{0}(l/d)\overline{\psi^{0}}(c/d).

Here
l=M/m_{\psi}
,
\mu
is the Möbius function,
L_{M}(k,\psi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi(n)n^{-k}
is the Dirichlet series, and
G(\psi^{0})
is the Gauss sum of
\psi^{0}
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
D
be a fundamental discriminant and
\mathbf{1}
be the principal character. Then

(4.6)

\displaystyle E_{k}(z;\mathbf{1},\chi_{D})=2L_{D}(k)+\frac{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\chi_{D})}{(k-1)!|D|^{k}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{d\mid n}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D}{d}\hskip-1.0pt\right)d^{k-1}\right)q^{2\pi inz/|D|}.

Example 4.3
.

If
D=D_{1}D_{2}
is a product of relatively prime fundamental discriminants then

(4.7)

\displaystyle E_{k}(z;\chi_{D_{2}},\chi_{D_{1}}):=C+\frac{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\chi_{D_{1}})}{|D_{1}|^{k}(k-1)!}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}>0\\
d_{1}d_{2}=n\end{subarray}}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{1}}{d_{1}}\hskip-1.0pt\right)\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{d_{2}}\hskip-1.0pt\right)d_{1}^{k-1}\right)e^{2\pi inz/|D_{1}|},

where
C
is zero unless
D_{2}=1
.

We shall compare our Eisenstein series
G_{k,D}(z)
(
1.7
) and
G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(z)
, defined below in (
4.9
)
[
8
, p. 193]
with the ones above given in Miyake
[
14
]
. Comparing the Fourier coefficients of
G_{k,D}(z)
and
E_{k}(z;\mathbf{1},\chi_{D})
gives

(4.8)

\displaystyle G_{k,D}(z)=\frac{(k-1)!|D|^{k}}{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\chi_{D})}E_{k}(|D|z,\mathbf{1},\chi_{D}).

Recall that
[
8
, p. 193]
for
D_{1},D_{2}
relatively prime fundamental discriminants with
(-1)^{k}D_{1}D_{2}>0
:

(4.9)

\displaystyle G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(n)q^{n},

(4.10)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(n)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-L_{D_{1}}(1-k)L_{D_{2}}(0)&n=0,\\
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}>0\\
d_{1}d_{2}=n\end{subarray}}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{1}}{d_{1}}\hskip-1.0pt\right)\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{d_{2}}\hskip-1.0pt\right)d_{1}^{k-1}&n>0.\end{cases}

where the constant term is zero unless
D_{2}=1
. Hence by comparing the Fourier coefficients of
G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(z)
and
E_{k}(z;\chi_{D_{2}},\chi_{D_{1}})
, we get

(4.11)

\displaystyle G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(z)=\frac{|D_{1}|^{k}(k-1)!}{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\chi_{D_{1}})}E_{k}(|D_{1}|z;\chi_{D_{2}},\chi_{D_{1}}).

The following expression of
G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(z)
is useful for Lemma
6.1
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
D=D_{1}D_{2}
be a product of coprime fundamental discriminants. Then

(4.12)

\displaystyle G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(z)=\frac{|D_{1}|^{k}(k-1)!}{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\chi_{D_{1}})}\chi_{D_{2}}(|D_{1}|)\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D_{1}\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D_{2}}(m)\chi_{D_{1}}(n)}{(mz+n)^{k}}.

Proof.

Note that

(4.13)

\displaystyle E_{k}(|D_{1}|z;\chi_{D_{2}},\chi_{D_{1}})

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}\limits_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{D_{2}}(m)\chi_{D_{1}}(n)(m|D_{1}|z+n)^{-k}

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\chi_{D_{2}}(|D_{1}|)\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D_{1}\mid\ell\end{subarray}}\chi_{D_{2}}(\ell)\chi_{D_{1}}(n)(\ell z+n)^{-k}.

Thus the result follows from (
4.11
).
∎

Let
k\geq 3
and
\chi
be a Dirichlet character mod
N
. We define the Eisenstein series for the cusp at infinity
[
14
, p. 272]
as

(4.15)

\displaystyle E_{k,N}^{\ast}(z;\chi)=\sum_{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(N)}\frac{\chi(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}},

where
\Gamma_{\infty}=\{\pm\begin{bmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.

Now, we are ready to prove that
G_{k,4D}
is an Eisenstein series for the cusp at infinity of level
4|D|
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathbf{1}
denote the principal Dirichlet character. Then

(4.16)

\displaystyle 2L_{N}(k,\chi)E_{k,N}^{\ast}(z;\chi)=E_{k}(Nz;\mathbf{1},\chi).

Proof.

Recall the following bijection
[
14
, Lemma 7.1.6]
,

\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(N)\rightarrow\{(c,d)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}:N\mid c,(c,d)=1,d>0\}:\Gamma_{\infty}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\mapsto(c,d),

which allows us to rewrite
E^{*}_{k,N}(z;\chi)
as

E^{*}_{k,N}(z;\chi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c,d)=1\\
d>0,N\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c,d)=1\\
N\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}.

It follows that

(4.17)

\displaystyle 2L_{N}(k,\chi)E^{*}_{k,N}(z;\chi)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{k}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c,d)=1\\
N\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}\right)

(4.18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c,d)=1\\
N\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(nd)}{(ncz+nd)^{k}}

(4.19)

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,d\in\mathbb{Z}\\
N\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}

(4.20)

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{c,d\in\ZZ}\frac{\chi(d)}{(Ncz+d)^{k}}

(4.21)

\displaystyle=E_{k}(Nz;\mathbf{1},\chi),

as desired.
∎

From (
4.8
) and Lemma
4.5
, we know that
G_{k,D}(z)
is an Eisenstein series at infinity. Comparing the constant terms of the Fourier expansions of
G_{k,D}(z)
and
E_{k,|D|}^{\ast}(z;\chi_{D})
gives

(4.22)

\displaystyle G_{k,D}(z)=\frac{L_{D}(1-k)}{2}E_{k,|D|}^{\ast}(z;\chi_{D}).

Note also that (
4.8
) and the proof of Lemma 4.5 imply that

(4.23)

\displaystyle G_{k,D}(z)=\frac{(k-1)!|D|^{k}}{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\chi_{D})}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,d\in\ZZ\\
D\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D}(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}.

In fact, equation (

) will be more convenient for us to compute the Fourier expansion of
G_{k,D}(z)
at different cusps. We need the following lemma; see also
[
4
, p. 271]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
L_{D}^{(4)}(k)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,4)=1\\
n\geq 1\end{subarray}}\chi_{D}(n)n^{-k}
. Then

(4.24)

\displaystyle E_{k,4|D|}^{\ast}(z;\chi_{D})=\frac{L_{D}(k)}{L_{D}^{(4)}(k)}\left(E_{k,|D|}^{\ast}(4z;\chi_{D})-2^{-k}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D}{2}\hskip-1.0pt\right)E_{k,|D|}^{\ast}(2z;\chi_{D})\right).

Proof.

Observe that

(4.25)

\displaystyle 2L_{D}^{(4)}(k)E_{k,4|D|}^{\ast}(z;\chi_{D})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
(4,n)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D}(n)}{n^{k}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c,d)=1\\
4D\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D}(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}\right)

(4.26)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
(4,n)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c,d)=1\\
4D\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D}(nd)}{(ncz+nd)^{k}}

(4.27)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d^{\prime},4D)=1\\
4D\mid c^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D}(d^{\prime})}{(c^{\prime}z+d^{\prime})^{k}},

where
nc=c^{\prime}
and
nd=d^{\prime}
. Note that we can replace
(d^{\prime},4D)=1
by
(d^{\prime},4)
since
\chi_{D}(d^{\prime})=0
otherwise. It follows that

(4.28)

\displaystyle 2L_{D}^{(4)}(k)E_{k,4|D|}^{\ast}(z;\chi_{D})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(d,4)=1\\
4D\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D}(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}

(4.29)

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,d\in\mathbb{Z}\\
4D|c\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{e|(d,4),e>0}\mu(e)\right)\frac{\chi_{D}(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}

(4.30)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\mid 4,e>0}\mu(e)\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,d\in\mathbb{Z}\\
4D\mid c,e\mid d\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D}(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}},

where we used
\sum\limits_{e\mid(d,4)}\mu(e)=0
for
(d,4)>1
in the second equality. Substituting
d=ey
and
c=4x
,

(4.31)

\displaystyle 2L_{D}^{(4)}(k)E_{k,4|D|}^{\ast}(z;\chi_{D})

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\mid 4,e>0}\mu(e)\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D\mid x\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D}(ey)}{(4xz+ey)^{k}}

(4.32)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\mid 4,e>0}\mu(e)e^{-k}\chi_{D}(e)\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D|x\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D}(y)}{(x4z/e+y)^{k}}

(4.33)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\mid 4,e>0}\mu(e)e^{-k}\chi_{D}(e)2L_{D}(k)E_{k,D}^{\ast}(4z/e,\chi_{D})

(4.34)

\displaystyle=2L_{D}(k)\left(E_{k,|D|}^{\ast}(4z;\chi_{D})-2^{-k}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D}{2}\hskip-1.0pt\right)E_{k,|D|}^{\ast}(2z;\chi_{D})\right),

where the second to last equality is from the proof of Lemma
4.5
.
∎

From Lemma
4.6
and (
4.22
), we know that
G_{k,4D}(z)
is an Eisenstein series for the cusp at infinity in
M_{k}(4|D|,\chi_{D})
. Comparing the constants of
G_{k,4D}(z)
and
E_{k,4D}^{\ast}(z;\chi_{D})
, we have

(4.35)

\displaystyle G_{k,4D}(z)=\frac{L_{D}(1-k)}{2}\left(1-2^{-k}\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)\right)E_{k,4D}^{\ast}(z;\chi_{D}).

5.
The Rankin-Selberg convolution

The purpose of this section is to prove Propositions
5.6
and
5.7
. For two elements
f
and
g
of
M_{k}(N)
such that
fg
is a cuspform, the Petersson inner product is given by

(5.1)

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{\Gamma_{0}(N)}=\int_{\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}\im(z)^{k}d\mu,

where
z=x+iy
and
d\mu=dxdy/y^{2}
. We use
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to denote
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Gamma_{0}(N)}
if the level is clear from the context. For
f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n)q^{n}\in S_{k}(N,\chi)
, we put
f_{\rho}(z):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\overline{a_{f}(n)}q^{n}
. Note that
f_{\rho}(z)=f(z)
if
f
is a newform.

We now review the classical result on the Rankin-Selberg convolution, which was reformulated and generalized in Zagier
[
23
]
, keeping in mind the difference between our definition of the Rankin-Cohen bracket and the one used therein.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
23
, Propsition 6]
)
.

Let
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
be real numbers with
k_{2}\geq k_{1}+2>2
. Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n)q^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b(n)q^{n}
be modular forms in
S_{k}(N,\chi)
and
M_{k_{1}}(N,\chi_{1})
, where
k=k_{1}+k_{2}+2e,e\geq 0
and
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
.
Then

(5.2)

\displaystyle\langle f,[g,E^{*}_{k_{2},N}(\cdot;\chi_{2})]_{e}\rangle=\frac{(-1)^{e}}{e!}\frac{\Gamma(k-1)\Gamma(k_{2}+e)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}\Gamma(k_{2})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)\overline{b(n)}}{n^{k_{1}+k_{2}+e-1}}.

To obtain Proposition
5.6
, we need to deal with the case
k_{1}=k_{2}
, which can be done by following Shimura
[
18
]
and Lanphier’s work
[
10
]
. For
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n)q^{n}\in S_{k}(N,\chi)
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b(n)q^{n}\in M_{\ell}(N,\psi)
, we put

D(s,f,g)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n)b(n)n^{-s},\quad\re(s)\gg 0.

We are particularly interested in the following case.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n)q^{n}\in S_{2\ell}(1)
be a normalized eigenform with
\ell=k+2e,e>0
and
k\geq 4
integers, and let
D
be an odd fundamental discriminant. Then

(5.3)

\displaystyle D(s,f,G_{k,D})=\frac{L(f,s)L(f,D,s-k+1))}{L_{D}(2s-3k-4e+2)},\quad\re(s)\gg 0.

Proof.

Note that for
\re(s)\gg 0
, we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle D(s,f,G_{k,D})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma_{k-1,\mathbf{1},\chi_{D}}(n)a(n)}{n^{s}},

where
\sigma_{k-1,\mathbf{1},\chi_{D}}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\chi_{D}(d)d^{k-1}
. A standard computation (see
[
20
, Proposition 4.1]
) gives

(5.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma_{k-1,\mathbf{1},\chi_{D}}(n)a(n)}{n^{s}}=\frac{L(f,s)L(f,D,s-(k-1))}{L_{D}(2s-(k-1)+1-(2k+4e))},

as desired.
∎

From Shimura
[
18
, p. 786-789]
,
D(s,f,G_{k,D})
has a meromorphic continuation to the whole complex plane and
D(s,f,G_{k,D})
is holomorphic at
s=2k+2e-1
, see
[
18
, p. 789]
.

The Maass-Shimura operators
[
18
, p. 788 (2.8)]
are defined by

(5.6)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2iy}+\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right),\quad 0<\lambda\in\mathbb{Z},

(5.7)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}^{(r)}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\lambda+2r-2}\cdots\delta_{\lambda+2}\delta_{\lambda},\quad 0\leq r\in\mathbb{Z},

where we understand that
\delta^{(0)}_{\lambda}
is the identity operator.
A relation between Maass-Shimura operators and the Rankin-Cohen bracket is given by

(5.8)

\displaystyle\delta_{k}^{(n)}f(z)\times g(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}\binom{k+n-1}{n-j}}{\binom{k+\ell+2j-2}{j}\binom{k+\ell+n+j-1}{n-j}}\delta^{(n-j)}_{k+\ell+2j}[f,g]_{j}(z),

where
f\in M_{k}(\Gamma)
and
g\in M_{\ell}(\Gamma)
for any congruence subgroup
\Gamma
; see
[
10
, Theorem 1]
.

We recall the following two results.

Lemma 5.3

(
[
18
, Lemma 6]
)
.

Suppose
f\in S_{k}(N,\chi),g\in M_{l}(N,\overline{\chi})
and
k=l+2r
with a positive integer
r.
Then
\langle\delta^{(r)}g,f_{\rho}\rangle=0
.

Lemma 5.4

(
[
18
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Suppose
f\in S_{2\ell}(|D|)
with
\ell=k+2e,e>0
and
k\geq 4
, and
D
is a fundamental discriminant. Then

(5.9)

\displaystyle D(2k+4e-1-2e),f,G_{k,D})=c\pi^{2k+4e-1}\langle G_{k,D}\delta_{k}^{(2e)}E_{k,|D|}^{\ast}(z;\chi_{D}),f_{\rho}\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the non-normalized Petersson inner product on defined in (
5.1
) and

(5.10)

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(2k+4e-k-2(2e))}{\Gamma(2k+4e-1-2e)\Gamma(2k+4e-k-2e)}(-1)^{2e}4^{2k+4e-1}.

(5.11)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(k)}{\Gamma(2k-1+2e)\Gamma(k+2e)}4^{2k+4e-1}

We apply these two results in our situation to obtain the following.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
f\in S_{2\ell}(1)
be a normalized eigenform with
\ell=k+2e,e>0
and
k\geq 4
. Then

(5.12)

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
k\geq 3
. Suppose
D
is an odd fundamental discriminant and
D=D_{1}D_{2}
is a product of two fundamental discriminants. Let
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\gcd(c,D)=|D_{1}|
. Then

(6.1)

\displaystyle G_{k,D}(z)\bigg|_{k}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}=\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{c}\hskip-1.0pt\right)\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{1}}{d|D_{2}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{|D_{1}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)\frac{\varepsilon_{|D_{1}|}}{\varepsilon_{|D|}}|D_{2}|^{-1/2}G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{z+c^{\ast}d}{|D_{2}|}\right),

where
c^{\ast}
is an integer with
cc^{\ast}\equiv 1\pmod{|D_{2}|}
, and
\varepsilon_{n}
is given by

(6.2)

\varepsilon_{n}=\begin{cases}1&n\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
i&n\equiv 3\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We follow the idea in Gross-Zagier
[
4
, pp. 273-275]
. By equation (
4.23
), we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle\frac{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\chi_{D})}{(k-1)!|D|^{k}}G_{k,D}(z)\bigg|_{k}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l,r\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D\mid l\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D}(r)}{(l(az+b)+r(cz+d))^{k}}

(6.4)

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\ZZ\\
md\equiv nc~\text{mod}~|D|\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D}(an-bm)}{(mz+n)^{k}},

where
(m,n)=(l,r)\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}
. Since
md\equiv nc\pmod{|D|}
, we have

(6.5)

\displaystyle d(an-bm)

\displaystyle=adn-bmd\equiv adn-bcn\equiv n\pmod{|D|},

(6.6)

\displaystyle c(an-bm)

\displaystyle=anc-bcm\equiv adm-bcm\equiv m\pmod{|D|}.

Note also that
\gcd(D_{1},D_{2})=1
. Then (
6.5
) and (
6.6
) imply that

(6.7)

\displaystyle\chi_{D}(an-bm)

\displaystyle=\chi_{D_{1}}(an-bm)\chi_{D_{2}}(an-bm)

(6.8)

\displaystyle=\chi_{D_{1}}(d)\chi_{D_{1}}(n)\chi_{D_{2}}(c)\chi_{D_{2}}(m).

Since
D_{1},D_{2}\mid(md-nc)
,
(d,D_{1})=1,(c,D_{2})=1
and
(c,D)=|D_{1}|
, we must have
D_{1}\mid m
; and
n\equiv c^{\ast}md\pmod{|D_{2}|}
. By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, we can choose
c^{\ast}
such that
D_{1}\mid c^{\ast}
. Now we write
n=c^{\ast}md+l|D_{2}|
. It follows that

(6.9)

\displaystyle\frac{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\chi_{D})}{(k-1)!|D|^{k}}G_{k,D}(z)\bigg|_{k}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,l\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D_{1}\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D_{1}}(d)\chi_{D_{1}}(c^{\ast}md+l|D_{2}|)\chi_{D_{2}}(c)\chi_{D_{2}}(m)}{(mz+mc^{\ast}d+l|D_{2}|)^{k}}

(6.10)

\displaystyle=\chi_{D_{2}}(c)\chi_{D_{1}}(d|D_{2}|)\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,l\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D_{1}\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D_{2}}(m)\chi_{D_{1}}(l)}{(mz+mc^{\ast}d+l|D_{2}|)^{k}}

(6.11)

\displaystyle=\chi_{D_{2}}(c)\chi_{D_{1}}(d|D_{2}|)|D_{2}|^{-k}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,l\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D_{1}\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D_{2}}(m)\chi_{D_{1}}(l)}{\left(m\frac{z+c^{\ast}d}{|D_{2}|}+l\right)^{k}}.

Note that (
4.12
) implies that

(6.12)

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,l\in\mathbb{Z}\\
D_{1}\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi_{D_{2}}(m)\chi_{D_{1}}(l)}{\left(m\frac{z+c^{\ast}d}{|D_{2}|}+l\right)^{k}}=\frac{2(-2\pi i)^{k}G(\chi_{D_{1}})}{|D_{1}|^{k}(k-1)!}\chi_{D_{2}}(|D_{1}|)G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{z+c^{\ast}d}{|D_{2}|}\right).

Plugging (
6.12
) into (
6.11
) gives

(6.13)

\displaystyle G_{k,D}(z)\bigg|_{k}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\chi_{D_{2}}(c)\chi_{D_{1}}(d|D_{2}|)\chi_{D_{2}}(|D_{1}|)\frac{G(\chi_{D_{1}})}{G(\chi_{D})}G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{z+c^{\ast}d}{|D_{2}|}\right).

From
[
3
, Proposition 2.2.24. p. 49]
we know that

(6.14)

\displaystyle G(\chi_{D_{1}})=\varepsilon_{|D_{1}|}|D_{1}|^{1/2}\quad{\rm and}\quad G(\chi_{D})=\varepsilon_{|D|}|D|^{1/2},

which implies that

(6.15)

\displaystyle G_{k,D}(z)\bigg|_{k}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}=\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{c}\hskip-1.0pt\right)\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{1}}{d|D_{2}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{|D_{1}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)\frac{\varepsilon_{|D_{1}|}}{\varepsilon_{|D|}}|D_{2}|^{-1/2}G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{z+c^{\ast}d}{|D_{2}|}\right),

as desired.
∎

\displaystyle\left\{\gamma_{D_{1},\mu}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
4|D_{1}|&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&\mu\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}:\qquad\text{where }D=D_{1}D_{2},\quad\mu\pmod{|D_{2}|}\right\},

where
D=D_{1}D_{2}
is a product of fundamental discriminants. By a simple casework we have

(6.33)

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{|D_{1}|}}{\varepsilon_{|D|}\cdot\varepsilon_{|D_{2}|}}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{1}}{|D_{2}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)=\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{-|D_{1}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right).

Now Lemmas
6.1
and
6.2
together with (
6.33
) imply that

\displaystyle\quad\,\sum_{D_{1}D_{2}=D}\sum_{\mu~\text{mod}~|D_{2}|}[G_{k,D}(4z),\theta(|D|z)]_{e}\big|_{k+2e+\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{D_{1},\mu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{D_{1}D_{2}=D}\sum_{\mu~\text{mod}~|D_{2}|}\left[G_{k,D}(4z)|_{k}\gamma_{D_{1},\mu},\theta(|D|z)\big|_{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{D_{1},\mu}\right]_{e}

\displaystyle=\sum_{D_{1}D_{2}=D}\sum_{\mu~\text{mod}~|D_{2}|}\frac{\varepsilon_{|D_{1}|}}{\varepsilon_{|D|}\cdot\varepsilon_{|D_{2}|}}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{1}}{|D_{2}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)|D_{2}|^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{4z+|D_{1}|^{*}+4\mu}{|D_{2}|}\right),\theta\left(\frac{|D_{1}|z+4^{*}+|D_{1}|\mu}{|D_{2}|}\right)\right]_{e}

\displaystyle=\sum_{D_{1}D_{2}=D}\sum_{\mu~\text{mod}~|D_{2}|}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{-|D_{1}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)|D_{2}|^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{4(z+4^{\ast}|D_{1}|^{*}+\mu)}{|D_{2}|}\right),\theta\left(\frac{|D_{1}|(z+4^{*}|D_{1}|^{*}+\mu)}{|D_{2}|}\right)\right]_{e}

\displaystyle=\hskip-13.0pt\sum_{D_{1}D_{2}=D}\sum_{\nu~\text{mod}~|D_{2}|}\hskip-5.0pt\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{-|D_{1}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)\hskip-3.0pt|D_{2}|^{-1+k/2+1/4}\hskip-2.0pt\left[G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(4z)\bigg|_{k}\begin{bmatrix}1&\nu\\
0&|D_{2}|\end{bmatrix},\theta(|D_{1}|z)\bigg|_{\frac{1}{2}}\begin{bmatrix}1&\nu\\
0&|D_{2}|\end{bmatrix}\hskip-2.0pt\right]_{e}

\displaystyle=\hskip-5.0pt\sum_{D_{1}D_{2}=D}\sum_{\nu~\text{mod}~|D_{2}|}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{-|D_{1}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)|D_{2}|^{-1+k/2+1/4}\left[G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(4z),\theta(|D_{1}|z)\right]_{e}\bigg|_{2e+k+\frac{1}{2}}\begin{bmatrix}1&\nu\\
0&|D_{2}|\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sum_{D_{1}D_{2}=D}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{-|D_{1}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)|D_{2}|^{-e}U_{D_{2}}[G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(4z),\theta(|D_{1}|z)]_{e},

as desired.
∎

We are now ready to compute the Fourier expansions of
\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}
and
\mathcal{S}_{D}(\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e})
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
k\geq 4
,
e>0
and let
D
be an odd fundamental discriminant with
(-1)^{k}D>0
. Then we have the Fourier expansion

(6.34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}f_{D,k,e}(n)q^{n},

where

(6.35)

\displaystyle f_{D,k,e}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{-1}\hskip-1.0pt\right)|D_{2}|^{-2e}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=n|D_{2}|\end{subarray}}\sum_{d|(a_{1},a_{2})}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D}{d}\hskip-1.0pt\right)d^{k-1}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{a_{1}a_{2}}{d^{2}}\right)C_{e,a_{1},a_{2}},

(6.36)

\displaystyle C_{e,a_{1},a_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{2e}(-1)^{r}a_{1}^{r}a_{2}^{2e-r}\binom{2e+k-1}{2e-r}\binom{2e+k-1}{r}.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.3
, we have

(6.37)

\displaystyle f_{D,k,e}(n)=\sum_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{-1}\hskip-1.0pt\right)|D_{2}|^{-2e}F_{D_{1},D_{2},e}(n),

where
F_{D_{1},D_{2},e}(n)
is the
n|D_{2}|
-th Fourier coefficient of

[G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(z),G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(z)]_{2e}.

Note that

(6.38)

\displaystyle G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(z)^{(r)}=\sum_{n\geq 0}n^{r}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(n)q^{n},

which implies that the
n|D_{2}|
-th Fourier coefficient of
G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}^{(r)}(z)G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}^{(2e-r)}(z)
is

(6.39)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=n|D_{2}|\end{subarray}}a_{1}^{r}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(a_{1})a_{2}^{2e-r}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(a_{2}).

It follows that
F_{D_{1},D_{2},e}(n)=

(6.40)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.0pt\sum_{r=0}^{2e}(-1)^{r}\binom{2e+k-1}{2e-r}\binom{2e+k-1}{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=n|D_{2}|\end{subarray}}a_{1}^{r}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(a_{1})a_{2}^{2e-r}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(a_{2})

(6.41)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=n|D_{2}|\end{subarray}}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(a_{1})\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(a_{2})\sum_{r=0}^{2e}a_{1}^{r}a_{2}^{2e-r}(-1)^{r}\binom{2e+k-1}{2e-r}\binom{2e+k-1}{r}

(6.42)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=n|D_{2}|\end{subarray}}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(a_{1})\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(a_{2})C_{e,a_{1},a_{2}}

(6.43)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=n|D_{2}|\end{subarray}}\sum_{d|(a_{1},a_{2})}\left(\frac{D}{d}\right)d^{k-1}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{a_{1}a_{2}}{d^{2}}\right)C_{e,a_{1},a_{2}}.

where the last equality is given by the Hecke multiplicative relation
[
8
, p. 194]

(6.44)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(a_{1})\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(a_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{d|(a_{1},a_{2})}\left(\frac{D}{d}\right)d^{k-1}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{a_{1}a_{2}}{d^{2}}\right).

This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
k\geq 4
,
e>0
and let
D
be an odd fundamental discriminant with
(-1)^{k}D>0
. Then we have the Fourier expansion

(6.45)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{D}\left(\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}(z))\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}g_{D,k,e}(n)q^{n},

where

(6.46)

g_{D,k,e}(n)=|D|^{e}\sum\limits_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\left(\frac{D_{2}}{-1}\right)|D_{2}|^{-2e}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=n|D_{2}|\end{subarray}}\sum_{d|(a_{1},a_{2})}\left(\frac{D}{d}\right)d^{k-1}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{a_{1}a_{2}}{d^{2}}\right)E(a_{1},a_{2}),

(6.47)

E(a_{1},a_{2})=\sum\limits_{r=0}^{e}(-1)^{r}\binom{e+k-1}{e-r}\binom{e-1/2}{r}4^{r}\left(a_{1}a_{2}\right)^{r}(a_{2}-a_{1})^{2(e-r)}.

Proof.

From Proposition
6.4
and the definition of the Rankin-Cohen bracket (
1.12
), we have
\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}(z)=

(6.48)

\displaystyle\sum_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{-|D_{1}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)|D_{2}|^{-e}U_{|D_{2}|}\left(\sum_{r=0}^{e}(-1)^{r}\binom{e+k-1}{e-r}\binom{e-1/2}{r}G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(4z)^{(r)}\theta(|D_{1}|z)^{(e-r)}\right).

Hence
\mathcal{S}_{D}(\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}(z))=

(6.49)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\hskip-2.0pt\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{-|D_{1}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)|D_{2}|^{-e}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{e}(-1)^{r}\binom{e+k-1}{e-r}\binom{e-1/2}{r}\mathcal{S}_{D}\hskip-2.0pt\left(U_{|D_{2}|}(G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(4z)^{(r)}\theta(|D_{1}|z)^{(e-r)})\right)\hskip-1.0pt,

where we abuse notation to move the Shimura operator
\mathcal{S}_{D}
into the sums. Now taking the Fourier expansions of
G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}^{(r)}
and
\theta(|D_{1}|z)^{(e-r)}
, we get

G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(4z)^{(r)}=\sum_{n\geq 0}(4n)^{r}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(n)q^{4n}

\theta(|D_{1}|z)^{(e-r)}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(n^{2}|D_{1}|)^{e-r}q^{n^{2}|D_{1}|}

where
\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(n)
is defined in
[
8
, p. 193]
. This allows us to rewrite the product

(6.50)

\displaystyle G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(4z)^{(r)}\theta(|D_{1}|z)^{(e-r)}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{s\geq 0}(4s)^{r}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(s)q^{4s}\right)\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(m^{2}|D_{1}|)^{e-r}q^{m^{2}|D_{1}|}\right)

(6.51)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{r}(n)q^{n},

where

c_{r}(n)=\sum_{m\equiv n\mod 2}\left(n-m^{2}|D_{1}|\right)^{r}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{n-m^{2}|D_{1}|}{4}\right)(m^{2}|D_{1}|)^{e-r},

taking the convention that
\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(x)=0
if
x\notin\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}
or
x<0
.
It follows that

(6.52)

\displaystyle U_{|D_{2}|}\left(G_{k,D_{1},D_{2}}(4z)^{(r)}\theta(|D_{1}|z)^{(e-r)}\right)=U_{|D_{2}|}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}c_{r}(n)q^{n}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}c_{r}(n|D_{2}|)q^{n}.

Now we compute the
D
-th Shimura lift of (
6.52
). If we write

(6.53)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{D}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}c_{r}(n|D_{2}|)q^{n}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{r,D_{2}}(n)q^{n}

for some
a_{r,D_{2}}(n)
, then by the definition of
\mathcal{S}_{D}
(
1.2
), we have
a_{r,{D_{2}}}(n)=

(6.54)

\displaystyle\sum_{d|n}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D}{d}\hskip-1.0pt\right)d^{k+2e-1}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(|D_{2}||D|\frac{n^{2}}{d^{2}}-|D_{1}|m^{2}\right)^{r}\left(m^{2}|D_{1}|\right)^{e-r}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{|D_{2}||D|\frac{n^{2}}{d^{2}}-m^{2}|D_{1}|}{4}\right).

Note that we can write

(6.55)

\displaystyle\frac{|D_{2}||D|\frac{n^{2}}{d^{2}}-m^{2}|D_{1}|}{4}=|D_{1}|a_{1}a_{2},\quad{\rm where}~a_{1}=\frac{|D_{2}|\frac{n}{d}+m}{2}~{\rm and}~a_{2}=\frac{|D_{2}|\frac{n}{d}-m}{2}.

It follows that
a_{r,D_{2}}(n)=

(6.56)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.0pt\sum_{d|n}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D}{d}\hskip-1.0pt\right)d^{k+2e-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=\frac{n}{d}|D_{2}|\end{subarray}}\left(4|D_{1}|a_{1}a_{2}\right)^{r}(a_{2}-a_{1})^{2(e-r)}|D_{1}|^{e-r}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}(|D_{1}|a_{1}a_{2})

(6.57)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=n|D_{2}|\end{subarray}}\sum_{d|(a_{1},a_{2})}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D}{d}\hskip-1.0pt\right)d^{k+2e-1}\left(4|D_{1}|\frac{a_{1}a_{2}}{d^{2}}\right)^{r}\left(\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{d}\right)^{2(e-r)}|D_{1}|^{e-r}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(|D_{1}|\frac{a_{1}a_{2}}{d^{2}}\right)

(6.58)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=n|D_{2}|\end{subarray}}\sum_{d|(a_{1},a_{2})}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D}{d}\hskip-1.0pt\right)\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{|D_{1}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)d^{k-1}|D_{1}|^{e}\left(4a_{1}a_{2}\right)^{r}(a_{2}-a_{1})^{2(e-r)}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{a_{1}a_{2}}{d^{2}}\right).

Now we substitute (
6.58
) back into our equation for
\mathcal{S}_{D}(\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}(z))
. Let

\mathcal{S}_{D}(\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}(z))=\sum_{n\geq 1}g_{D,k,e}(n)q^{n}.

Then we have
g_{D,k,e}(n)=

(6.59)

\displaystyle\sum_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{-|D_{1}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)|D_{2}|^{-e}\sum_{r=0}^{e}\binom{e+k-1}{e-r}\binom{e-1/2}{r}a_{r,D_{2}}(n)

(6.60)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{-|D_{1}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)|D_{2}|^{-e}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{e}(-1)^{r}\binom{e+k-1}{e-r}\binom{e-1/2}{r}

(6.61)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=n|D_{2}|\end{subarray}}\sum_{d|(a_{1},a_{2})}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D}{d}\hskip-1.0pt\right)\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{|D_{1}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)d^{k-1}|D_{1}|^{e}\left(4a_{1}a_{2}\right)^{r}(a_{2}-a_{1})^{2(e-r)}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{a_{1}a_{2}}{d^{2}}\right)

(6.62)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\left(\frac{D_{2}}{-|D_{1}|}\right)\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D_{2}}{|D_{1}|}\hskip-1.0pt\right)|D_{2}|^{-e}|D_{1}|^{e}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{e}(-1)^{r}\binom{e+k-1}{e-r}\binom{e-1/2}{r}

(6.63)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=n|D_{2}|\end{subarray}}\sum_{d|(a_{1},a_{2})}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{D}{d}\hskip-1.0pt\right)d^{k-1}\left(4a_{1}a_{2}\right)^{r}(a_{2}-a_{1})^{2(e-r)}\sigma_{k-1,D_{1},D_{2}}\left(\frac{a_{1}a_{2}}{d^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

as desired.
∎

7.
Discussion

It is a folklore conjecture that
S_{2\ell}^{0,D}(1)=S_{2\ell}(1)
. Luo
[
12
]
showed that for
\ell
sufficiently large one has
\dim S_{2\ell}^{0,1}(1)\gg\ell
. Our Theorem
1.2
(the case
D=1
was proved earlier by Xue
[
21
, Proposition 3.5]
) provides a possible different approach to the conjecture. By studying the linear independence of
\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}
or
\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}
, one could obtain lower bounds on the dimension of
S_{2\ell}^{0,D}(1)
.

Conjecture 7.1
.

For
\ell
even,
D
a positive fundamental discriminant, the set
\{\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}~|~k+2e=\ell,1\leq e\leq\lfloor\frac{\ell}{6}\rfloor\}
is linearly independent.

We checked this conjecture computationally in the
D=1
case up to
\ell=1000
and for prime
D
less than 50 up to
\ell=100
, using code written in Pari/GP
[
6
]
. In particular, we computationally verified that the matrix

\begin{bmatrix}g_{D,\ell-2,1}(4)&g_{D,\ell-2,1}(8)&\ldots&g_{D,\ell-2,1}(4\lfloor\frac{\ell}{6}\rfloor)\\
g_{D,\ell-4,2}(4)&g_{D,\ell-4,2}(8)&\ldots&g_{D,\ell-4,2}(4\lfloor\frac{\ell}{6}\rfloor)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
g_{D,\ell-2\lfloor\frac{\ell}{6}\rfloor,\lfloor\frac{\ell}{6}\rfloor}(4)&g_{D,\ell-2\lfloor\frac{\ell}{6}\rfloor,\lfloor\frac{\ell}{6}\rfloor}(8)&\ldots&g_{D,\ell-2\lfloor\frac{\ell}{6}\rfloor,\lfloor\frac{\ell}{6}\rfloor}(4\lfloor\frac{\ell}{6}\rfloor)\\
\end{bmatrix},

where
\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}=\sum_{n\geq 1}g_{D,k,e}(n)q^{n}
, has nonzero determinant. Further work in this area should try to prove that this determinant is nonzero in general.

The conjecture would have several interesting consequences. Using the isomorphism between
S^{0,D}_{\ell+1/2}(4)
and
S^{0,D}_{2\ell}(1)
given by the
D
-th Shimura lift, we find that the dimension of
S^{0,D}_{2\ell}
would be at least
\lfloor\frac{\ell}{6}\rfloor
. Since the dimension of
S_{2\ell}(1)
for even
\ell
is
\lfloor\frac{2\ell}{12}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{\ell}{6}\rfloor
and
S^{0,D}_{2\ell}(1)\subseteq S_{2\ell}(1)
, we would conclude that
S^{0,D}_{2\ell}(1)=S_{2\ell}(1)
, settling the conjecture on the non-vanishing of twisted central
L
-values for Hecke eigenforms.

This would then imply that
S^{0,D}_{\ell+1/2}(4)=S^{+}_{\ell+1/2}(4)
, so the Kohnen plus space for
k
even is generated by Hecke eigenforms whose
D
-th coefficients are nonzero for all fundamental discriminants
D
. Further, we would conclude that
\{\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}\}_{k+2e=\ell,1\leq e\leq\lfloor\frac{\ell}{6}\rfloor}
is a basis for
S^{+}_{\ell+1/2}(4)
, and the set
\{\mathcal{G}_{D,k,e}\}_{k+2e=\ell,0\leq e\leq\lfloor\frac{\ell}{6}\rfloor}
is a basis for
M^{+}_{\ell+1/2}(4)
(since the
0
-th Rankin-Cohen bracket produces a modular form which is non-cuspidal but still in the Kohnen plus space). To the best of our knowledge, a similar basis was first mentioned by Henri Cohen in a MathOverflow post.

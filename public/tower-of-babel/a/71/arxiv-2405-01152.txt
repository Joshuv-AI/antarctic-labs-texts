Relative cluster tilting theory and τ \tau -tilting theory
Yu Liu, Jixing Pan and Panyue Zhou

Abstract
. Let 𝒞 \mathcal{C} be a Krull-Schmidt triangulated category with shift functor [ 1 ] [1] and ℛ \mathcal{R} be a rigid subcategory of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . We are concerned with the mutation of two-term weak ℛ ⁡ [ 1 ] \mathcal{R}[1] -cluster tilting subcategories. We show that any almost complete two-term weak ℛ ⁡ [ 1 ] \mathcal{R}[1] -cluster tilting subcategory has exactly two completions. Then we apply the results on relative cluster tilting subcategories to the domain of τ \tau -tilting theory in functor categories and abelian categories.

1.
Introduction

Tilting theory emerges as a versatile method for establishing equivalences between categories. Initially developed in the framework of module categories over finite dimensional algebras, tilting theory has now evolved into an indispensable tool across various mathematical domains. Applications have been found in diverse fields such as finite and algebraic group theory, commutative and non-commutative algebraic geometry, as well as algebraic topology.

Let
\Lambda
be a finite dimensional hereditary algebra over an algebraically closed field.
All modules are regarded as finite dimensional left
\Lambda
-modules.
We assume that
\Lambda
has
n
isomorphism classes of simple modules.
A partial tilting module
M
is called an almost complete tilting module, if
M

has
n-1
non-isomorphic indecomposable direct summands.
An indecomposable module
X
is called a complement to
M
,
if
M\oplus X
is a tilting module and
X
is not isomorphic to any direct summand of
M
.
It has been shown in
[
HR
]
that there always exists a complement to an almost complete tilting module
M
, and further research
[
RS
,
U
,
HU
]
showed that such a partial tilting module allows (up to isomorphism) at most two complements and there are two such complements if and only if the partial tilting module is sincere.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional algebra over a field. Adachi, Iyama and Reiten
[
AIR
]

introduced
\tau
-tilting theory
, which is a
generalization of classical tilting theory.
The impetus to explore
\tau
-tilting theory arises from various sources, with a key focus on the mutation of tilting modules. It’s worth noting that one limitation of tilting module mutation is its occasional infeasibility. This limitation serves as the driving force behind the exploration of
\tau
-tilting theory.
Support
\tau
-tilting (resp.
\tau
-rigid) modules extend the scope of tilting (resp. partial tilting) modules by incorporating the Auslander-Reiten translation. In essence, support
\tau
-tilting modules offer a natural extension of the class of tilting module with respect to mutation. Notably, as demonstrated in
[
AIR
]
, a basic almost complete support
\tau
-tilting module emerges as a direct summand of exactly two basic support
\tau
-tilting modules. This means that mutation
of support
\tau
-tilting modules is always possible.

Cluster categories were introduced by Buan, Marsh, Reineke, Reiten, and Todorov
[
BMRRT
]
in order to categorify Fomin-Zelevinsky’s cluster algebras
[
FZ1
,
FZ2
]
. Consider
\Lambda
, a finite dimensional hereditary algebra over an algebraically closed field with
n
non-isomorphic simple modules. The cluster category of
\Lambda
, denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}=\mathrm{D}^{b}({\rm mod}\Lambda)/\tau^{-1}[1]
, arises as the Verdier quotient category.
In a triangulated category, rigid objects manifest in three distinct forms: cluster tilting, maximal rigid, and complete rigid. Generally speaking, these forms are not equivalent
[
BIKR
,
KZ
]
, yet in the cluster category
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}
, they are demonstrated to be equivalence
[
BMRRT
]
. In contrast to classical tilting modules, cluster tilting objects in cluster categories exhibit favorable characteristics
[
BMRRT
]
.
For instance, any almost complete cluster tilting object in a cluster category can be completed to a cluster tilting object in exactly two ways.
Adachi, Iyama and Reiten
[
AIR
]
established a
bijection between cluster tilting objects in a
2
-Calabi-Yau triangulated category and
support
\tau
-tilting modules over a cluster-tilted algebra (also see
[
CZZ
,
YZ
,
FGL
]
for various versions of this bijection).

Mutation of
n
-cluster tilting subcategories was introduced by Iyama and Yoshino
[
IY
]
,
they also studied almost complete
n
-cluster
tilting subcategories. They proved that any almost complete cluster tilting subcategory of a
2
-Calabi-Yau triangulated category is contained in exactly two cluster tilting subcategories.
We know that cluster tilting subcategories are maximal rigid subcategories, but the converse is
not true in general. Zhou and Zhu
[
ZZ
]
proved that
any almost complete maximal rigid subcategory of a
2
-Calabi-Yau triangulated category is contained in exactly two maximal rigid subcategories.
In
[
IJY
]
, Iyama, Jørgensen, and Yang introduced a functorial version of
\tau
-tilting theory. They extended the concept of support
\tau
-tilting modules from the module categories of finite dimensional algebras to essentially small additive categories. Their results demonstrated that for a triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
with a silting subcategory
\mathcal{S}
, there exists a one-to-one correspondence between the set of two-term silting subcategories in
\mathcal{S}*\mathcal{S}[1]
and the set of support
\tau
-tilting subcategories of
\mathsf{mod}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{S}
.

For a Krull-Schmidt, Hom-finite triangulated category with a Serre functor
and a cluster tilting object
T
, let
\Lambda
be the endomorphism algebra of
T
. Yang and Zhu
[
YZ
]
proved that there exists a bijection between the set of isomorphism classes of basic
T[1]
-cluster tilting objects in this triangulated category and the set of isomorphism classes of basic support
\tau
-tilting
\Lambda
-modules.
Yang, Zhou, and Zhu
[
YZZ
]
introduced the notion of (weak)
\mathcal{T}[1]
-cluster tilting subcategories for a triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
with a cluster tilting subcategory
\mathcal{T}
. They established a correspondence between the set of weak
\mathcal{T}[1]
-cluster tilting subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
and the set of support
\tau
-tilting subcategories of
\mathsf{mod}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{T}
, which extends the above correspondence by Yang and Zhu in
[
YZ
]
. This correspondence resembles the one presented in
[
IJY
]
.
Consider a triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
with shift functor
[1]
and a rigid subcategory
\mathcal{R}
. Zhou and Zhu
[
ZhZ
]
introduced the concepts of two-term
\mathcal{R}[1]
-rigid subcategories, two-term (weak)
\mathcal{R}[1]
-cluster tilting subcategories, and two-term maximal
\mathcal{R}[1]
-rigid subcategories. They proved that there is a bijection between the set of two-term
\mathcal{R}[1]
-rigid subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
and the set of
\tau
-rigid subcategories of
\mathsf{mod}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{R}

\mathcal{R}[1]
-cluster tilting subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
and the set of support
\tau
-tilting subcategories of
\mathsf{mod}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{R}

[

when

2.
Preliminaries

The class of cluster tilting subcategories is a significant class in triangulated categories, boasting a plethora of desirable properties. Let’s delve into the definition of cluster tilting subcategories and related subcategories, as outlined in seminal works such as
[
BMRRT
,
KR
,

For convenience, we introduce the following notions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a triangulated category with shift functor
[1]
.

(i)

A subcategory
\mathcal{R}
of
\mathcal{C}
is called
rigid
if
{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{R}[1])=0
.

(ii)

A subcategory
\mathcal{R}
of
\mathcal{C}
is called
maximal rigid
if it is rigid and maximal with respect to the property: If
M\in\mathcal{C}
satisfies

{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{R}\cup\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229ptM,(\mathcal{R}\cup\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229ptM)[1])=0
, then
M\in\mathcal{R}
.

(iii)

A functorially finite subcategory
\mathcal{R}
of
\mathcal{C}
is called cluster tilting if

\mathcal{R}=\{M\in\mathcal{C}\ |\ {\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{R},M[1])=0\}=\{M\in\mathcal{C}\ |\ {\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(M,\mathcal{R}[1])=0\}.

(iv)

An object
R
in
\mathcal{C}
is called rigid, maximal rigid, or cluster tilting if
\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229ptR
is rigid, maximal rigid, or cluster tilting respectively.

For any subcategory
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
, denote by
[\mathcal{D}](A,B)
the subset of
{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,B)
consisting of the morphisms that factors through objects in
\mathcal{D}
. Now we recall the notion of two-term relative cluster tilting subcategories and related notations from
[

,
YZZ
,
ZhZ
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a rigid subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.

(i)

A subcategory
\mathscr{X}
in

is called

-rigid if

.

(ii)

\mathscr{X}\subseteq\mathcal{R}\ast\mathcal{R}[1]

\mathcal{R}[1]
-rigid, two-term weak
\mathcal{R}[1]

\mathcal{R}[1]

For convenience, we introduce the following notion: for any subcategory

, denote

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be two-term
\mathcal{R}[1]
-rigid. If

, then

\mathcal{R}[1]
-rigid.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{R}
is rigid, we have

and
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime}[1],\mathcal{R}^{\prime}[2])=0
. Since
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{R}(\mathscr{X})
, we have
0=\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime}[1],\mathscr{X}[1])=[\mathcal{R}[1]](\mathcal{R}^{\prime}[1],\mathscr{X}[1])
. By definition,
\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229pt(\mathscr{X}\cup\mathcal{R}^{\prime}[1])
is still
\mathcal{R}[1]
-rigid. Since
\mathscr{X}\subseteq\mathcal{R}\ast\mathcal{R}[1]
,
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}[1]\subseteq\mathcal{R}*\mathcal{R}[1]
, by Lemma
2.6
,
\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229pt(\mathscr{X}\cup\mathcal{R}^{\prime}[1])\subseteq\mathcal{R}\ast\mathcal{R}[1]
.
∎

On the other hand, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be
\mathcal{R}[1]
-rigid. Then

is two-term maximal

-rigid, then
\mathscr{X}\cap\mathcal{R}[1]=\mathcal{R}(\mathscr{X})[1]
.

Let

. Since

is
\mathcal{R}[1]
-rigid, we have

Hence

and
\mathscr{X}\cap\mathcal{R}[1]\subseteq\mathcal{R}(\mathscr{X})[1]
. The “Moreover” part is followed by the argument above and Lemma

∎

.

4.
Almost complete relative cluster tilting subcategories

In this section, we give the definition of almost complete two-term weak
\mathcal{R}[1]
-cluster tilting subcategories, and prove that such subcategories
has exactly (up to isomorphism) two completions.

Definition 4.1
.

We call a two-term
\mathcal{R}[1]
-rigid subcategory
\mathscr{X}
an almost complete two-term weak
\mathcal{R}[1]
-cluster tilting subcategory if the following conditions are satisfied:

(a)

\mathscr{X}
is
\mathcal{R}[1]
-functorially finite;

(b)

There exists an indecomposable object

such that
\mathsf{add}\hskip 0.72229pt(\mathscr{X}\cup\{W\})
is two-term weak
\mathcal{R}[1]
-cluster tilting.

Any two-term weak
\mathcal{R}[1]
-cluster tilting subcategory which has the form in
(b)
is called a completion of
\mathscr{X}
,

is called a complement of
\mathscr{X}
.

For convenience, if
\mathscr{X}
is an almost complete two-term weak
\mathcal{R}[1]
-cluster tilting subcategory, we can just say
\mathscr{X}
is almost complete.

Lemma 4.2

\mathscr{X}
be an
\mathcal{R}[1]
-functorially finite two-term
\mathcal{R}[1]
-rigid subcategory. Assume

\mathscr{X}

Let

be an indecomposable object in

\overline{A}\simeq\mathsf{mod}\hskip 0.72229pt\mathcal{R}

Proof.

We show the “if” part first.

By Lemma
2.10
, any object

admits a triangle

R_{1}\xrightarrow{f}R_{2}\xrightarrow{u_{1}}U\xrightarrow{u_{2}}R_{1}[1]

R_{1}\xrightarrow{\overline{f}}R_{2}\xrightarrow{\overline{u}_{1}}U\to 0.

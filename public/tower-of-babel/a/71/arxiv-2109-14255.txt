Abstract
. We study weighted inequalities of Hardy and Hardy–Poincaré type and find necessary and sufficient conditions on the weights so that the considered inequalities hold. Examples with the optimal constants are shown. Such inequalities are then used to quantify the convergence rate of solutions to doubly nonlinear fast diffusion equation towards the Barenblatt profile.



1.
Introduction

We investigate functional inequalities of Hardy and Poincaré type. Our main objective is to provide new constructive methods of derivation of such inequalities with explicit constants that are optimal in some cases. Our study starts with finding necessary and sufficient conditions on the weights so that the following
Poincaré inequality
on the real line is valid for any compactly supported
f\in W^{1,\infty}({\mdmathbb{R}})

\int_{{\mdmathbb{R}}}|f-(f)_{w_{1}}|^{q}w_{1}(s)\,ds\leq C_{P}\int_{{\mdmathbb{R}}}|f^{\prime}|^{q}w_{2}(s)\,ds\,,\qquad\text{where $1<q<\infty$,}

(f)_{w_{1}}
is a weighted average with respect to
w_{1}
, and
C_{P}=C_{P}(q,w_{1},w_{2})>0
, see Theorem
1
.
Having
Poincaré inequality
on the real line, we prove some compatibility conditions to be necessary and sufficient for
Hardy–Poincaré inequality on
{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}
,
N>2
, of a form

\int_{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}\ |{\varphi}-\overline{{\varphi}}|^{q}w_{1}(x)\,dx\leq C_{HP}\int_{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}\ |\nabla{\varphi}|^{q}w_{2}(x)\,dx,\qquad\text{where $1<q<N$,}

to hold for all compactly supported
{\varphi}\in W^{1,\infty}({\mdmathbb{R}^{N}})
, where
\overline{{\varphi}}=\frac{1}{H_{1}}\int_{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}{\varphi}w_{1}(x)\,dx
, and
C_{HP}=C_{HP}(q,w_{1},w_{2})>0
, see Theorem
2
. Our next result is Theorem
3
providing a constructive PDE-based method of obtaining admissible weights to the following
Hardy inequality

\int_{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}\ |{\varphi}|^{q}w_{1}(x)\,dx\leq C_{H}\int_{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}\ |\nabla{\varphi}|^{q}w_{2}(x)\,dx,\qquad\text{where $1<q<\infty$,}

for every compactly supported
{\varphi}\in W^{1,\infty}({\mdmathbb{R}^{N}})
and
C_{H}=C_{H}(q,w_{1},w_{2})>0
.

The investigation of the inequalities mentioned above is motivated by the study of nonlinear diffusion equations where they play a critical role in understanding the asymptotic behaviour of solutions to Cauchy problems. The constants in the inequalities dictate the convergence rates of a solution towards a self-similar one. We apply such an inequality to investigate the long-time asymptotics of solutions to the Cauchy problem

In order to present our first result let us define the
median
of the measure
w_{1}(s)\,ds
being a point

Theorem 1

(Poincaré inequality on
{\mdmathbb{R}}
)
.

Let

\frac{(2^{\frac{q-1}{q}}-1)^{q}}{2^{q-1}}\,\max\left(B_{\eta}^{+},B_{\eta}^{-}\right)\leq C_{P}\leq\frac{(2q)^{q}}{(q-1)^{q-1}}\,\max\left(B_{\eta}^{+},B_{\eta}^{-}\right)

In the case

Theorem 3

(General Hardy inequality)
.

Suppose

,

w_{2}(x)={|\nabla g|^{q(\theta-1)}}{|\Delta_{\theta}g|^{1-q}}

We will employ the classical result of Muckenhoupt.

Lemma 2.1

(Muckenhoupt’s inequality, Theorem 1,
[

.

Let

0\leq w_{1},w_{2}
such that

is true for every compactly supported

if and only if

\displaystyle H_{M}:=

\displaystyle\sup_{\rho>0}\left(\int_{\rho}^{\infty}w_{1}(r)\,dr\left[\int_{0}^{\rho}{(w_{2}(r))^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}dr}\right]^{q-1}\right)<\infty

Moreover, then the optimal constant

We are in a position to prove Poincaré inequality (

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We assume first that
B_{\eta}^{+}
and
B_{\eta}^{-}
are both finite. For any

we have that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f-(f)_{w_{1}}|^{q}w_{1}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f-f(m)|^{q}w_{1}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=2^{q}\int_{-\infty}^{m}|f-f(m)|^{q}w_{1}(s)\,ds+2^{q}\int_{m}^{\infty}|f-f(m)|^{q}w_{1}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=:2^{q}(L_{1}(m)+L_{2}(m)).

Let us define

J_{1}(m):=\int_{-\infty}^{m}|f^{\prime}|^{q}w_{2}(s)\,ds\quad\mbox{and}\quad J_{2}(m):=\int_{m}^{\infty}|f^{\prime}|^{q}w_{2}(s)\,ds\,,

and let us call
A_{m}^{-}
(
A_{m}^{+}
resp.) the optimal constant of the inequality
L_{1}(m)\leq A_{m}^{-}J_{1}(m)
(of inequality
L_{2}(m)\leq A_{m}^{+}J_{2}(m)
resp.). In particular, if we consider

, we obtain that

B_{\eta}^{-}\leq A_{\eta}^{-}\leq{q}{(q^{\prime})^{q-1}}\,B_{\eta}^{-}\quad\mbox{and}\quad B_{\eta}^{+}\leq A_{\eta}^{+}\leq{q}{(q^{\prime})^{q-1}}\,B_{\eta}^{+}

as a consequence of a change of variables and Lemma
2.1
. By summing up the previous inequalities, we find that

\begin{split}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f-(f)_{w_{1}}|^{q}w_{1}(s)\,ds&\leq 2^{q}\,(L_{1}(m)+L_{2}(m))\\
&\leq 2^{q}\,\max\{A_{\eta}^{+}\,A_{\eta}^{-}\}(J_{1}(m)+J_{2}(m))\\
&\leq 2^{q}\,{q}{(q^{\prime})^{q-1}}\,\max\left(B_{\eta}^{+},B_{\eta}^{-}\right)\,\int_{{\mdmathbb{R}}}|f^{\prime}|^{q}w_{2}(s)\,ds\,,\end{split}

which proves inequality (
1
) with the wanted upper bound on the constant

.

Assume now that inequality (

. Let us first restrict our attention to the case when

(so that, by the optimality of

Let us recall that

is the optimal constant for inequality

\int_{\eta}^{\infty}\left|\int_{\eta}^{s}f_{\varepsilon}(t)dt\right|^{q}w_{1}(s)\,ds\geq(A_{\eta}^{+}-\varepsilon)\int_{\eta}^{\infty}|f_{\varepsilon}(s)|^{q}w_{2}(s)ds\,.

Note that, without loss of generality, we can assume that

\begin{split}\int_{{\mdmathbb{R}}}|F_{\varepsilon}(t)-(F_{\varepsilon})_{w_{1}}|^{q}\,{w_{1}}(t)dt\geq&\left(\left(\int_{{\mdmathbb{R}}}|F_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{q}\,{w_{1}}(t)dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}-(F_{\varepsilon})_{w_{1}}\right)^{q}\\

.

We prove the inequality with radial weights as an application of the Poincaré inequality (
1
). For this we introduce the standard change of variables from Cartesian to spherical coordinates, that is

r=|x|\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\vartheta=x/|x|.

In these coordinates, the gradient can be written as
(\partial r,\frac{1}{r}\nabla_{\vartheta})
where
\partial r=\frac{x}{r}\cdot\nabla
is the partial derivative with respect to the radial variable
r
and
\nabla_{\vartheta}
is the derivative with respect to the angular variables. Then

(14)

|\partial_{r}{f}(r,\vartheta)|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{r^{2}}|\nabla_{\vartheta}{f}(r,\vartheta)|^{2}=|\nabla f(x)|^{2}.

By
S^{N-1}\subset{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}
we denote the unit sphere and parametrize it with the variable
\vartheta
. Moreover, for every compactly supported function
f\in W^{1,\infty}({\mdmathbb{R}^{N}})
we denote the directional average as

(15)

\widetilde{f}(\vartheta):=\frac{|S^{N-1}|}{H_{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r,\vartheta){r^{N-1}}h(r)\,dr.

Recall that the global average with respect to
h
is given by

(16)

\overline{f}=\frac{1}{|S^{N-1}|}\int_{S^{N-1}}\widetilde{f}(\vartheta)d\vartheta=\frac{1}{H_{1}}\int_{{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}}f(x)h(|x|)\,dx.

We recall the Poincaré inequality on the sphere.

Lemma 2.2

(Spherical Poincaré inequality)
.

Let
1\leq q<N
if
N\geq 3,
and
1\leq q\leq 2
when
N=2
. Then there exists
C_{\rm sph}>0
such that

(17)

\int_{S^{N-1}}|\widetilde{f}(\vartheta)-\overline{f}|^{q}\,d\vartheta\leq C_{\rm sph}\int_{S^{N-1}}|\nabla_{\vartheta}\widetilde{f}(\vartheta)|^{q}\,d\vartheta

for every
f\in W^{1,q}(S^{N-1})
.

In the case
N\geq 3
, inequality (
17
) is a direct consequence of
[
46
, Theorem 2.10]
formulated for a smooth and compact Riemannian manifold. In the case
q=2
and
N=2
, it follows from a decomposition in spherical harmonics which then coincides with the Fourier basis, see
[
5
]
. To our best knowledge its optimal value of the constant from (
17
) is known only in the case of
q=2
, when
C_{\rm sph}=\frac{1}{N-1}
, see
[
23
, Chapter 4, Proposition 1]
.

The following result would be instrumental in the proof of Theorem
2
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose assumptions of Theorem
2
are satisfied. If for every compactly supported function
f\in W^{1,\infty}({\mdmathbb{R}^{N}})
the following inequality holds

(18)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{N-1}}|f(r,\vartheta)-\widetilde{f}(\vartheta)|^{q}r^{N-1}h(r)\,d\vartheta\,dr\leq C_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{N-1}}|\partial_{r}{f}(r,\vartheta)|^{q}r^{N-1+q}h(r)d\vartheta\,dr,

then there exist
C_{HP}>0
, such that for the same
f
one has (
1
) with
w_{1}(x)=h(|x|)
,
w_{2}(x)=|x|^{q}h(|x|)
, and a positive constant
C_{HP}\leq 2^{q-1}\,\max\{C_{1},H_{1}^{q}\,C_{\rm sph}\,|S^{N-1}|^{-q}\}
.

Proof.

By (
16
) and Jensen’s inequality it holds

\begin{split}\int_{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}|f(x)-\overline{f}|^{q}h(|x|)\,dx&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{N-1}}|f(r,\vartheta)-\overline{f}|^{q}r^{N-1}h(r)\,{d\vartheta}\,dr\\
&\leq 2^{q-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{N-1}}|f(r,\vartheta)-\widetilde{f}(\vartheta)|^{q}r^{N-1}h(r)\,{d\vartheta}\,dr\\
&\quad+2^{q-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{N-1}}|\widetilde{f}(\vartheta)-\overline{f}|^{q}r^{N-1}h(r)\,{d\vartheta}\,dr=:I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

Since the first term on the right-hand side above can be estimated due to (
18
) and then by properties of the gradient (
14
), we get

I_{1}\leq 2^{q-1}C_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{N-1}}|\partial_{r}{f}(r,\vartheta)|^{q}r^{N-1+q}h(r)\,{d\vartheta}\,dr\leq 2^{q-1}C_{1}\int_{{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}}|\nabla{f}(x)|^{q}|x|^{q}h(|x|)\,dx\,.

As for the second one we use (
4
) to obtain

I_{2}=2^{q-1}\frac{H_{1}}{|S^{N-1}|}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\widetilde{f}(\vartheta)-\overline{f}|^{q}\,{d\vartheta}

and we apply the spherical Poincaré inequality (Lemma
2.2
) to get

I_{2}=2^{q-1}\frac{H_{1}}{|S^{N-1}|}\int_{S^{N-1}}|\widetilde{f}(\vartheta)-\overline{f}|^{q}\,{d\vartheta}\leq 2^{q-1}\frac{H_{1}}{|S^{N-1}|}C_{\rm sph}\int_{S^{N-1}}|\nabla_{\vartheta}\widetilde{f}(\vartheta)|^{q}\,{d\vartheta}=:J.

As
r^{N-1}h(r)|S^{N-1}|/H_{1}\,dr
is a probability measure by Jensen’s inequality we have that

\begin{split}J&=2^{q-1}\frac{H_{1}}{|S^{N-1}|}C_{\rm sph}\int_{S^{N-1}}\left|\nabla_{\vartheta}\int_{0}^{\infty}{f}(r,\vartheta)r^{N-1}h(r)\,dr\right|^{q}\,{d\vartheta}\\
&=2^{q-1}\left(\frac{H_{1}}{|S^{N-1}|}\right)^{q+1}C_{\rm sph}\int_{S^{N-1}}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\nabla_{\vartheta}{f}(r,\vartheta)r^{N-1}h(r)\frac{|S^{N-1}|}{H_{1}}\,dr\right|^{q}\,{d\vartheta}\\
&\leq 2^{q-1}\left(\frac{H_{1}}{|S^{N-1}|}\right)^{q}C_{\rm sph}\int_{S^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\nabla_{\vartheta}{f}(r,\vartheta)\right|^{q}r^{N-1}h(r)\,dr\,{d\vartheta}=:K.\end{split}

Due to (
14
) we have
r^{-q}\,|\nabla_{\vartheta}f(r,\vartheta)|^{q}\leq|\nabla f(x)|^{q}

and we infer that

\begin{split}K\leq 2^{q-1}\left(\frac{H_{1}}{|S^{N-1}|}\right)^{q}C_{\rm sph}\int_{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla f(x)|^{q}\,|x|^{q}h(|x|)\,dx.\end{split}

By summing up the previous computations we have the claim.
∎

We are in the position to prove the Hardy–Poincaré inequality.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

By Corollary
2.1
with

(f)=\frac{|S^{N-1}|}{H_{1}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r)\,r^{N-1}\,h(r)\,dr,

and
H_{1}
from (
4
), we have the following inequality

\int_{0}^{\infty}|f-(f)|^{q}r^{N-1}h(r)\,dr\leq 2^{q}C_{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}|f^{\prime}|^{q}r^{N-1+q}h(r)\,dr

for all compactly supported
f\in W^{1,\infty}([0,\infty))
. Hence, also the following radial inequality follows

\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(r,\vartheta)-\widetilde{f}(\vartheta)|^{q}r^{N-1}h(r)\,dr\leq C_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\partial_{r}{f}(r,\vartheta)|^{q}r^{N-1+q}h(r)\,dr\quad\mbox{for a.e.}\,\,\vartheta\in S^{N-1}

with
C_{1}=2^{q}C_{M}
. The above inequality implies (
18
), which by Proposition
2.1
completes the proof of (
1
) with
w_{1}(x)=h(|x|)
and
w_{2}(x)=|x|^{q}h(|x|)
. Bounds on the constant result from the estimates on
c_{P}
provided in Theorem
1
.∎

2.3.
Hardy inequality on
{\mdmathbb{R}}^{N}
. Proof of Theorem
3

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Let us observe that integration by parts and the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality imply

\int_{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}|{\varphi}|^{q}|\Delta_{\theta}g|\,dx\leq q\int_{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}|{\varphi}|^{q-1}|\nabla{\varphi}||\nabla g|^{\theta-1}\,dx=:I.

By the Hölder inequality, we have

\begin{split}I&=q\int_{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|{\varphi}|^{q-1}{|\Delta_{\theta}g|^{\frac{q-1}{q}}}\right)\left(\frac{|\nabla{\varphi}||\nabla g|^{\theta-1}}{{|\Delta_{\theta}g|^{\frac{q-1}{q}}}}\right)\,dx\\
&\leq q\left(\int_{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}|{\varphi}|^{q}|\Delta_{\theta}g|dx\right)^{(q-1)/q}\left(\int_{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\varphi}|^{q}\frac{|\nabla g|^{q(\theta-1)}}{|\Delta_{\theta}g|^{q-1}}\,dx\right)^{1/q}.\end{split}

Summing up the above remarks, we get

\left(\int_{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}|{\varphi}|^{q}|\Delta_{\theta}g|\,dx\right)^{1/q}\leq q\left(\int_{\mdmathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\varphi}|^{q}\frac{|\nabla g|^{q(\theta-1)}}{|\Delta_{\theta}g|^{q-1}}\,dx\right)^{1/q}.

∎

(negative values of
m
are admitted) by Blanchet, Bonforte, Dolbeault, Grillo and Vázquez in
[
12
]
. The aforementioned results are often based on the study of a linearised problem performed in
[
33
]
, see also the monograph
[
32
]
.

In the case
m=1
and
p\neq 2
less is known. In the range
2N/(N+1)+1/(N+1)\leq p<N
, (non-optimal) rates of convergence were obtain by Del Pino and Dolbeault in
[
30
]
. Similarly, in the case of the doubly nonlinear equation (non-optimal) rates of convergence were obtain by Del Pino and Dolbeault in
[
31
]
, by Agueh
[
3
]
and by Agueh, Blanchet and Carrillo in
[
4
]
.

Here we only sketch the proof of Theorem
4
and emphasize where Theorem
2
is applied. For details we refer to
[
4
]
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

We restrict our analysis to the case
p\neq 2
which is fully covered by
[
12
]
.

By the scaling properties of the equation (
1
), we can assume that
D_{\star}=1
and let us define
\mathfrak{B}=\mathfrak{B}_{1}
. Let us define the
self-similar
change of variables

(27)

v(\tau,y):=R(t)^{N}\,u(t,x)\,,\quad\mbox{where}\quad\tau:=\log\left(\mathrm{R}(t)\right)\,,\quad y:=x/R(t)\,.

The main advantage of the change of variables is that the Barenblatt profile
B_{1}(t,x)
is transformed into
\mathfrak{B}
which is stationary in time. The equation satisfied by
v
is now

(28)

\frac{\partial v}{\partial\tau}=\mathrm{div}\left(m^{p-1}\,v^{(1-m)(1-p)}\,|\nabla v|^{p-2}\,\nabla v+v\,y\right)\,.

The
relative entropy
with respect to the Barenblatt profile
\mathfrak{B}
is defined by

(29)

\mathcal{E}[v|\mathfrak{B}]:=\frac{m}{\sigma(\sigma-1)}\int_{{\mdmathbb{R}}^{N}}\left(v^{\sigma}(y)-\mathfrak{B}^{\sigma}(y)-\sigma\,\mathfrak{B}^{\sigma-1}\left(v(y)-\mathfrak{B}(y)\right)\right)dy

for any
0\leq v\in L^{1}({\mdmathbb{R}}^{N})
where
\sigma=m+(p-2)/(p-1)
. Notice that, by the convexity of the function
u\mapsto m\,u^{\sigma}/\sigma(\sigma-1)
, the relative entropy is nonnegative functional
\mathcal{E}[v|\mathfrak{B}]\geq 0
. The derivative of
\mathcal{E}[v(\tau),\mathfrak{B}]
along the flow (
28
) is called
Fisher information
and is formally given by

(30)

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}[v|\mathfrak{B}]&:=-\frac{d\mathcal{E}[v(\tau)|\mathfrak{B}]}{d\tau}\\
&=m^{p}\int_{{\mdmathbb{R}}^{N}}v(\tau)\left(\frac{\nabla v(\tau)}{v(\tau)^{2-\sigma}}-\frac{\nabla\mathfrak{B}}{\mathfrak{B}^{2-\sigma}}\right)\cdot\left(v(\tau)^{(\sigma-2)(p-1)}\frac{\nabla v}{|\nabla v|^{2-p}}-\mathfrak{B}^{(\sigma-2)(p-1)}\frac{\nabla\mathfrak{B}}{|\nabla\mathfrak{B}|^{2-p}}\right)dy\,,\end{split}

where
a\cdot b
is the standard scalar product between
a,b\in{\mdmathbb{R}}^{N}
. Since
(a-b)\cdot(|a|^{p-2}a-|b|^{p-2}b)\geq 0
for
p>1
and any
a,b\in{\mdmathbb{R}}^{N}
, we infer that
\mathcal{I}[v(\tau)|\mathfrak{B}]\geq 0
.

The convergence of
v(\tau)
to
\mathfrak{B}
as
\tau\to\infty
follows from the decaying in time of the entropy functional. Once the inequality

(31)

\mathcal{E}[v(\tau)|\mathfrak{B}]\leq e^{-\mu\,\tau}\,\mathcal{E}[v(0)|\mathfrak{B}]\quad\forall\tau\geq\tau_{0}

is established for some
\mu,\tau_{0}>0
, then inequality (
10
) can be obtained by combining (
31
) with the Csiszár-Kullback inequality

\|v(\tau)-\mathfrak{B}\|^{2}_{L^{1}({\mdmathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq C\,\mathcal{E}[v(\tau)|\mathfrak{B}]\,,

for which we refer to
[
19
,
3
]
. Finally, to obtain the algebraic rate in the
t
variable, one should take into account the self-similar change of variables (
27
) which also gives the relation
\lambda=\mu/\vartheta
.

In order to prove (
31
), it suffices to have

(32)

\mu\,\mathcal{E}[v(\tau)|\mathfrak{B}]\leq\mathcal{I}[v(\tau)|\mathfrak{B}]\,.

for
\tau
large enough. Indeed, (
31
) follows by combining a Gronwall-type argument with inequality (
32
) and (
30
). In the range of parameters under consideration, inequality (
32
) does not hold for any function
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mdmathbb{R}}^{N})
, due to scaling arguments. This is very different from the case,
1>m(p-1)>1-(p-1)/N
, where (
32
) is equivalent to a class of Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequalities, see
[
3
,
15
,
36
,
29
]
. However, when
v(\tau)
is close enough to
\mathfrak{B}
, then (
32
) holds. In order to prove so, let us introduce the following weights:

w_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{m}\,\left(1+\frac{(1-\sigma)\,(p-1)}{p\,m}|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{2-\sigma}{\sigma-1}},\quad w_{2}(x)=|x|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\,\left(1+\frac{(1-\sigma)\,(p-1)}{p\,m}|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}

and for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

w_{2,\varepsilon}(x)=\left(1+\frac{(1-\sigma)\,(p-1)}{p\,m}|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}\,\left(\varepsilon+|x|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)^{-(2-p)}.

Let us define the
linearised relative entropy
as

E[{\varphi}]:=\int_{{\mdmathbb{R}}^{N}}|{\varphi}-\overline{{\varphi}}|^{2}\,w_{1}(x)\,dx

and the
linearised Fisher information
that for
p>2
takes a form

I[{\varphi}]:=\int_{{\mdmathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla{{\varphi}}|^{2}\,w_{2}(x)\,dx\,.

When
1<p<2
, the role of the
linearised Fisher information
is played by the quantity
I_{\varepsilon}[{\varphi}]
defined as
I[{\varphi}]
with the weight
w_{2,\varepsilon}
instead of
w_{2}
. The inequalities

(33)

C_{p,m}^{(1)}\,E[{\varphi}]\leq I[{\varphi}]\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad C_{p,m}^{(2)}\,E[{\varphi}]\leq I_{\varepsilon}[{\varphi}]

hold within the range of parameters (
7
) and for any function
{\varphi}\in C^{1,\alpha}({\mdmathbb{R}}^{N})
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. The inequalities in (
33
) follow from Example
3.1
with
\gamma=0
,
\beta=p/(p-1)
and
\alpha=(2-\sigma)/(\sigma-1)
and the fact that
c_{1}\,w_{1}(|x|)\leq(1+|x|^{\beta})^{\alpha}
,
|x|^{2}(1+|x|^{\beta})^{\alpha}\leq c_{2}\,w_{2}(|x|)
and
|x|^{2}(1+|x|^{\beta})^{\alpha}\leq c_{2,\varepsilon}\,w_{2,\varepsilon}(|x|)
, for some finite
c_{1},c_{2},c_{2,\varepsilon}>0
depending on
m,p
.

By
[
4
, Proposition 4.2]
, if
p>2
there exist
\tau_{0}
,
\kappa_{1}
,
\kappa_{2}>0
such that, for all
\tau>\tau_{0}

(34)

I[v(\tau)-\mathfrak{B}]\leq\kappa_{1}\,\mathcal{I}[v(\tau)|\mathfrak{B}]+\kappa_{2}\,E[v(\tau)-\mathfrak{B}]\,.

For
1<p<2
, the same inequality holds but with
I_{\varepsilon}[v(\tau)-\mathfrak{B}]
instead of
I[v(\tau)-\mathfrak{B}]
in the left-hand-side. The constant
\kappa_{2}
can be taken arbitrary small provided that
\tau_{0}
is large enough. By
[
4
, Proposition 4.1]
, it holds for any
\tau>0
that

(35)

\tfrac{1}{m}\,D_{1}^{\frac{1}{m(p-1)-1}}\,\mathcal{E}[v(\tau)|\mathfrak{B}]\leq E[v(\tau)-\mathfrak{B}]\,.

Combining inequalities (
35
), (
34
), (
33
) and provided
\tau_{0}
is large enough, we find (
32
) with
\mu=D_{1}^{\frac{1}{m(p-1)-1}}\left(C_{p,m}^{(i)}-\kappa_{2}\right)/m\kappa_{1}
with
i=1
when
p>2
and
i=2
when
1<p<2
. Choosing
\tau_{0}
large enough so that
C_{p,m}^{(i)}-\kappa_{2}\geq C_{p,m}^{(i)}/2
, we conclude that the rate of convergence
\lambda
can be taken as

\lambda=\frac{D_{1}^{\frac{1}{m(p-1)-1}}\,C_{p,m}^{(i)}}{2\,m\,\kappa_{2}\,(p-N[1-m(p-1)])}\,.

The proof is concluded.
∎

An Operator-Splitting Finite Element Method for the Numerical Solution of Radiative Transfer Equation
Sashikumaar Ganesan, Maneesh Kumar Singh

Abstract
An operator-splitting finite element scheme for the time-dependent, high-dimensional radiative transfer equation is presented in this paper. The streamline upwind Petrov-Galerkin finite element method and discontinuous Galerkin finite element method are used for the spatial-angular discretization of the radiative transfer equation, whereas the implicit backward Euler scheme is used for temporal discretization. Error analysis of the proposed numerical scheme for the fully discrete radiative transfer equation is presented. The stability and convergence estimates for the fully discrete problem are derived. Moreover, an operator-splitting algorithm for numerical simulation of high-dimensional equations is also presented. The validity of the derived estimates and implementation is illustrated with suitable numerical experiments.

Introduction

Radiation plays a significant role, both as a detectable and dominant mechanism for transmitting energy inside and outside a system, in several areas, including optics, astrophysics, atmospheric science, and remote sensing.
Consequently, the propagation of radiation through a medium is one of the most critical processes studied extensively. Analyzing the released radiation from an object provides insight into the radiative source, the medium between the object and the observer, and its surroundings. Modeling this physical process results in a time-dependent, six-dimensional Partial Differential Equation (PDE). The higher dimension of PDE is one of the challenges associated with the solution of the radiative transfer equation (RTE).
Besides, considerable uncertainty is added due to radiation’s ability to
affect the medium’s state, which is the source of the radiation itself. Although analytic solutions to RTE exist for simple cases, numerical solutions are often sought for more realistic, complex applications.
Therefore, it is exciting and, at the same time challenging to develop numerical schemes for the radiative model. More details on the radiative transfer model can be found in
chandrasekhar
;
kanschatnumerical
. The design and implementation of the computational method for time-dependent, high-dimensional radiative transfer equations remain a challenging task in computational science, even though tremendous advances have been made in this area over the past few years.

1.1
Model problem

Let

the unit outward normal for

\mathbf{s}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})\in S^{2}

\partial\Omega_{\mathbf{s},-}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega:\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x})<0\},\quad\partial\Omega_{\mathbf{s},+}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega:\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{x})\geq 0\}.

Let

as the inlet and outlet boundaries.
In this article, the high-dimensional radiative transfer equation (RTE) is formally defined by
an initial-boundary-value problem:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla u+\sigma_{\tau}u-\sigma_{s}\int_{S^{2}}u(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s}^{\prime})\Phi(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s}^{\prime})d\mathbf{s}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=f,\,\mathrm{in}~(0,T]\times\Omega\times S^{2},

\displaystyle u(t=0,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})

\displaystyle=u_{0},\,\mathrm{in}~\Omega\times S^{2},

(1)

\displaystyle u(t,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})

\displaystyle=0,~\,\mathrm{on}~(0,T]\times\Gamma_{-},

where

\sigma_{a}(\mathbf{x})

For simplicity, the particle speed is assumed to be one.
Here, the scattering phase function

that originally propagates in the direction
\mathbf{s}
, and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
as its new propagation direction after the scattering event. Note that the angular variable
\mathbf{s}
in the spherical coordinate system is denoted as

\mathbf{s}=(\sin{\theta}\cos{\phi},\sin{\theta}\sin{\phi},\cos{\theta})^{T}.

Also, we make the following assumptions on the data of the model problem (

Further, the data

The existing numerical schemes for RTE models can be classified into (i) stochastic approach and (ii) deterministic approach. Among all stochastic approaches, the Monte Carlo method is often used to solve the radiative transfer equation, see, for example,
gentile2001implicit
;
halton
;

and the references therein.
Nevertheless, the Monte-Carlo simulation’s computational cost is very high due to its iterative design, and it increases when the optical depth becomes large.

Several deterministic numerical schemes have been proposed in the literature for the stationary RTE, see for example,
avila2011spatial
;
badri1
;

;

;

;

. A low-rank approximation for time-dependent radiation transport in one- and two-dimensional Cartesian geometries has been discussed in
peng2020low
. In
dehghanian2020transient
, a variable discrete ordinates method has been used to solve the transient radiation heat transfer in a semi-transparent slab. Despite several numerical schemes proposed in the literature, numerical solution of the time-dependent high-dimensional RTE is still challenging and is an active research field.

The operator-splitting finite element methods have been developed in the recent past for many high-dimensional physical and mathematical models. For example, an operator-splitting numerical method for the micro-macro dilute polymeric fluid model has been provided in
knezevic_ESIAM
. For a high-dimensional
the convection-diffusion problem, an operator-splitting method with detailed numerical implementation, has been presented in

ganeshan_CEC

ganesan2017finite
.

For high-dimensional time-dependent RTE, we present an operator-splitting heterogeneous FEM. Further,
a priori
error estimate for the proposed numerical scheme is presented, which is the main contribution of this research article.
The key idea is to split the RTE model problem concerning the internal (angular) and the external (spatial) directions, resulting in a transient transport problem and a time-dependent integro-differential equation. The transient transport problem is numerically approximated using the streamline upwind Petrov-Galerkin (SUPG) finite element method, whereas the discontinuous Galerkin method with piecewise constant polynomials (DG(
0
)) is used for the integro-differential equation. The proposed numerical scheme uses this tailor-made spatial discretization method and the implicit backward Euler scheme. The stability estimate of the fully discrete form of the proposed scheme is first derived. A convergence analysis is then established under the assumption of certain regularity conditions on the data and the stabilization parameter. Finally, an array of numerical experiments is provided to support the theoretical error estimates of the numerical approximation.

The rest of the article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we briefly discuss the weak formulation of the operator-splitting method for the model problem, and later, we discuss the finite element approximation of RTE. Further, a fully discrete form is derived in this section. Next, the stability estimate of the discrete problem and the convergence analysis of the numerical approximation is presented in Section
3
. Further, the implementation of the numerical scheme is discussed in Section
4
. Finally, a concluding remark is discussed in Section
5
.

2.1
Operator-Splitting Method

The gradient operator in the RTE model (
1

\mathbf{x}

Using the Lie’s operator-splitting method, in the time

Step 1. (

(4)

by considering

-direction. Then, this solution

Step 2. (

-direction)

For given

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla u=f,\,\,(t^{n},t^{n+1})\times\Omega\times S^{2},\\[8.0pt]

(5)

by considering the variable
\mathbf{s}
as a parameter. Here, the solution

-direction (

Angular and spatial discretization

v_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathbf{s}}}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{\mathbf{x}}}v_{il}^{n}\phi_{i}(\mathbf{s})\psi_{l}(\mathbf{x})

\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla v_{h}
is expressed as

\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla v_{h}=\mathbf{s}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathbf{s}}}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{\mathbf{x}}}v_{il}^{n}\phi_{i}(\mathbf{s})\nabla\psi_{l}(\mathbf{x}).

The efficient way to handle these entries in the associated mass and stiffness matrices from the resulting finite element approximation is presented in Section
4
.

Now, by
using the finite element space
\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h}
, the respective semi-discrete form (
6
) and (
7
) read:

Step 1.
Find
\tilde{u}_{h_{\mathbf{x}},h_{\mathbf{s}}}:(t^{n},t^{n+1})\rightarrow\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h}
with
\tilde{u}_{h_{\mathbf{x}},h_{\mathbf{s}}}(t^{n})=u_{h_{\mathbf{x}},h_{\mathbf{s}}}(t^{n})
such that

\int_{S^{2}}(\tilde{u}_{t,h_{\mathbf{x}},h_{\mathbf{s}}},\zeta)_{\mathbf{x}}+\int_{S^{2}}(\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}\tilde{u}_{h_{\mathbf{x}},h_{\mathbf{s}}},\zeta)_{\mathbf{x}}=0,\,\,\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h}.

(13)

Step 2.
Find
u_{h_{\mathbf{x}},h_{\mathbf{s}}}:(t^{n},t^{n+1})\rightarrow\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h}
with
u_{h_{\mathbf{x}},h_{\mathbf{s}}}(t^{n})=\tilde{u}_{h_{\mathbf{x}},h_{\mathbf{s}}}(t^{n+1})
such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}}(u_{t,h_{\mathbf{x}},h_{\mathbf{s}}},\zeta)_{\mathbf{x}}+\int_{S^{2}}a_{SUPG}(u_{h_{\mathbf{x}},h_{\mathbf{s}}},\zeta)=\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}}(f,\zeta)_{\mathbf{x}}\\[8.0pt]
\hskip 113.81102pt\displaystyle+\int_{S^{2}}\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\delta_{K}\left(f-u_{t,h_{\mathbf{x}},h_{\mathbf{s}}},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\zeta\right)_{K},\,\,\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h}.\end{array}

(14)

To simplify notations, we denote
v_{h_{\mathbf{x}},h_{\mathbf{s}}}
by
v_{h}
and also use similar notations throughout this paper.

2.4
Temporal discretization

We consider a uniform partition of the time interval
[0,T]
with
\Delta t=T/N
, i.e.,
t_{n}=n\Delta t,\,\,n=0,1,\ldots,N
.
After discretizing the temporal variable by the implicit backward Euler scheme, the fully discrete operator-split form of the model problem (
1
) reads:

Step 1.
For a given
u^{n}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h}
, find
\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h}
such that

\int_{S^{2}}\left(\partial_{\Delta t}\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h},\zeta\right)_{\mathbf{x}}+\int_{S^{2}}(\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h},\zeta)_{\mathbf{x}}=0,\,\,\zeta\in\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h},

(15)

where
\partial_{\Delta t}\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}=(\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}-u^{n}_{h})/\Delta t
.

Step 2.
Update the solution
\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}
from (
15
) by finding
u^{n+1}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h}
such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}}\left(\partial_{\Delta t}u^{n+1}_{h},\zeta\right)_{\mathbf{x}}+\int_{S^{2}}a_{SUPG}(u^{n+1}_{h},\zeta)_{\mathbf{x}}=\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}}(f^{n+1},\zeta)_{\mathbf{x}}\\[8.0pt]
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle+\int_{S^{2}}\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\delta_{K}\left(f^{n+1}-\partial_{\Delta t}u^{n+1}_{h},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\zeta\right)_{K},\,\,\zeta\in\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h},\end{array}

(16)

where
\partial_{\Delta t}u^{n+1}_{h}=(u^{n+1}_{h}-\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h})/\Delta t
.

3
A
priori
error estimate : stability and convergence analysis

We now discuss the stability and the convergence analysis for the proposed numerical scheme. We first establish interpolation error estimates and then discuss the local truncation error of the two-step method. After that, both the local errors are combined to obtain a global error estimate.

3.1
Stability result

The stability estimate of the two-step operator-splitting method (
15
)-(
16
) is derived here.

Theorem 3.1

Assume that the stabilization parameter
\delta_{K}
satisfy

\delta_{K}\leq\dfrac{\Delta t}{4},\quad\delta=\max\{\delta_{K}\},\quad\Delta t\leq\dfrac{1}{2}.

(17)

Then, the solutions
\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}
and
u^{n+1}_{h}
of the two-step algorithm (
15
) and (
16
) satisfy

\|u^{n}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\int_{S^{2}}\|u^{m+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{SUPG}\leq e^{2T}\left(\|u^{0}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+2\Delta t(1+4\delta\Delta t)\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\|f^{m+1}\|^{2}_{0}\right).

(18)

Proof.

Setting
\zeta=\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}
in (
15
) and using
2(a-b)a=a^{2}-b^{2}+(a-b)^{2}
with Lemma
2
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}-u^{n}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\Delta t\underline{\sigma}_{a}\|\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u^{n}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}.\end{array}

(19)

By neglecting the positive terms from the left hand side of above equation to deduce that

\|\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\leq\|u^{n}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}.

(20)

Next, setting
\zeta=u^{n+1}_{h}
in (
16
), we get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\|u^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}-\dfrac{1}{2}\|\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|u^{n+1}_{h}-\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{2}\int_{S^{2}}\|u^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{SUPG}\\[8.0pt]
\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\displaystyle\Delta t\left|\int_{S^{2}}(f^{n+1},u^{n+1}_{h})_{\mathbf{x}}\right|+\Delta t\left|\int_{S^{2}}\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\delta_{K}(f^{n+1},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1}_{h})_{K}\right|\\[8.0pt]
\hskip 56.9055pt+\displaystyle\left|\int_{S^{2}}\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\delta_{K}(u^{n+1}_{h}-\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1}_{h})_{K}\right|.\end{array}

(21)

Using C-S inequality and Young inequality,
first two terms of the right hand side are bounded by

\Delta t\left|\int_{S^{2}}(f^{n+1},u^{n+1}_{h})_{\mathbf{x}}\right|\leq\dfrac{\Delta t}{2}\|f^{n+1}\|^{2}_{0}+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\|u^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}.

(22)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Delta t\left|\int_{S^{2}}\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\delta_{K}(f^{n+1},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1}_{h})_{K}\right|&\displaystyle\leq 2\Delta t\int_{S^{2}}\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\delta_{K}\|f^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}\\[20.0pt]
&\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{\Delta t}{8}\int_{S^{2}}\|u^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{SUPG}.\end{array}

(23)

Again, employing C-S and Young inequalities with assumptions (
17
) to deduce that

\left|\int_{S^{2}}\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\delta_{K}(u^{n+1}_{h}-\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla u^{n+1}_{h})_{K}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u^{n+1}_{h}-\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\frac{\Delta t}{8}\int_{S^{2}}\|u^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{SUPG}.

(24)

Then, combing (
22
)-(
24
), we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\|u^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}-\|\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\int_{S^{2}}\|u^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{SUPG}\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\leq\Delta t\|u^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\Delta t\|f^{n+1}\|^{2}_{0}+4\Delta t\int_{S^{2}}\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\delta_{K}\|f^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}.\end{array}

(25)

This can be reduced as follows

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt(1-\Delta t)\|u^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\int_{S^{2}}\|u^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{SUPG}\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\Delta t\|f^{n+1}\|^{2}_{0}+4\Delta t\int_{S^{2}}\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\delta_{K}\|f^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K)},\end{array}

(26)

using
1/(1-\Delta t)\leq 1+2\Delta t\leq 2
.
Employ (
26
) in (
25
), we deduce that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\Delta t\int_{S^{2}}\|u^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{SUPG}\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left(1+2\Delta t\right)\|\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+2\Delta t(1+4\delta\Delta t)\|f^{n+1}\|^{2}_{0}.\end{array}

(27)

Adding stability results of both the steps (
20
) and (
27
), we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\Delta t\int_{S^{2}}\|u^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{SUPG}\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left(1+2\Delta t\right)\|u^{n}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+2\Delta t(1+4\delta\Delta t)\|f^{n+1}\|^{2}_{0}.\end{array}

(28)

Now summing over
m=0,1,\ldots,n-1
, we get that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\|u^{n}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\int_{S^{2}}\|u^{m+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{SUPG}\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\|u^{m}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\|u^{0}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+2\Delta t(1+4\delta\Delta t)\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\|f^{m+1}\|^{2}_{0}.\end{array}

(29)

By using discrete Grownwall’s lemma, we obtain the stated stability result of the lemma.

Remark 1

In Theorem
3.1
, the stability condition of the discrete method (
15
) and (
16
) is established with the stability parameter
\delta_{K}
satisfies
\delta_{K}=\mathcal{O}(\Delta t)
. From (
12
), we would be able to take

.
For more details on the choice of stabilization parameter, one may see the detailed discussion in
john2008finite

Eril_CMAME1
.

3.2

Convergence analysis

In this subsection, error approximation for the numerical solution.
To derive the error estimate of the operator-splitting finite element discretization(
15
)-(
16
), we denote

\displaystyle\Pi_{h}=\pi_{h_{\mathbf{x}}}\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}=\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}\pi_{h_{\mathbf{x}}}.

Here,
\pi_{h_{\mathbf{x}}}v\in V_{h}
, the elliptic projection of
v\in V
and
\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}w\in W_{h}
, the angular interpolant of
w\in W
.
By applying the argumentation form
(
apel2005clement
, Lemma 4.2)
, we have

\|w-\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(S^{2})}\leq C\,\,h^{2}_{\mathbf{s}}|w|^{2}_{H^{1}(S^{2})},\,\,\forall\,w\in H^{1}(S^{2}).

(30)

Using Galerkin orthogonality,
\pi_{h_{\mathbf{x}}}u
satisfies

a_{SUPG}(\pi_{h_{\mathbf{x}}}u,v_{h_{\mathbf{x}}})=a_{SUPG}(u,v_{h_{\mathbf{x}}})\,\,\forall\,v_{h_{\mathbf{x}}}\in V_{h}.

Applying as in
johnson1984finite
, we have

\|u-\pi_{h_{\mathbf{x}}}u\|^{2}_{SUPG}\leq C\,h_{\mathbf{x}}^{3}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)},\,\,\forall\,u\in V\cap H^{2}(\Omega).

(31)

Now, we discuss the error estimate of the discrete problems (
15
)-(
16
).
The local truncation error in the first step (
15
) is denoted by

\tilde{E}^{n}_{h}=\tilde{u}^{n}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\tilde{u}(t^{n}),

where
\tilde{u}^{n}_{h}
is the fully discrete solution of the first step (
15
) and
\tilde{u}
is the weak solution of (
6
). The error term
\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}
solves the following equation

\int_{S^{2}}(\partial_{\Delta t}\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h},\zeta)_{\mathbf{x}}+\int_{S^{2}}\mathcal{A}(\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h},\zeta)=\int_{S^{2}}(I_{1},\zeta)_{\mathbf{x}}+\int_{S^{2}}\mathcal{A}(I_{2},\zeta),\,\,\zeta\in\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h},

(32)

where

I_{1}=\tilde{u}_{t}(t^{n+1})-\partial_{\Delta t}\Pi_{h}\tilde{u}(t^{n+1}),\quad I_{2}=\tilde{u}(t^{n+1})-\Pi_{h}\tilde{u}(t^{n+1}),

and
\mathcal{A}(v,w)=(\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}v,w)_{\mathbf{x}},

for any discrete function
v
and
w
.

Next, we discuss the error estimates
for both steps (
15
) and (
16
) subsequently in the upcoming lemmas.

Lemma 3

The local truncation error
\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}
associated with the angular discretization satisfies

\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left(\|\tilde{E}^{m+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}-\|E^{m}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\right)\leq C\Delta t\bigg[\Delta t\int_{0}^{T}\|\tilde{u}_{tt}\|^{2}_{0}+h_{\mathbf{s}}^{2}\|\tilde{u}_{t}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(L^{2})}+h_{\mathbf{x}}^{3}\|\tilde{u}_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}\bigg].

(33)

Proof.

Setting
\zeta=\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}
in (
32
), we get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\|E^{n}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\Delta t\int_{S^{2}}\mathcal{A}\left(\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h},\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\right)\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\leq\Delta t\int_{S^{2}}\left|\left(I_{1},\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\right)_{\mathbf{x}}\right|+\Delta t\int_{S^{2}}\left|\mathcal{A}\left(I_{2},\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\right)\right|.\end{array}

(34)

By using the argumentation from (
19
), it can be deduced that

\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\|E^{n}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\Delta t\underline{\sigma}_{a}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\leq\Delta t\int_{S^{2}}\left|\left(I_{1},\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\right)_{\mathbf{x}}\right|+\Delta t\int_{S^{2}}\left|\mathcal{A}\left(I_{2},\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\right)\right|.

(35)

Let us consider the first term on right hand side. Applying C-S and Young inequalities, we deduce that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Delta t\int_{S^{2}}\left|\left(I_{1},\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\right)_{\mathbf{x}}\right|&\leq 4\Delta t\|\tilde{u}_{t}(t^{n+1})-\partial_{\Delta t}\Pi_{h}\tilde{u}(t^{n+1})\|^{2}_{0}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{16}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\\[8.0pt]
&\leq 8\Delta t\|\Pi_{h}\tilde{u}_{t}(t^{n+1})-\partial_{\Delta t}\Pi_{h}\tilde{u}(t^{n+1})\|^{2}_{0}\\[8.0pt]
&\hskip 28.45274pt+8\Delta t\|\tilde{u}_{t}(t^{n+1})-\Pi_{h}\tilde{u}_{t}(t^{n+1})\|^{2}_{0}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{16}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\\[8.0pt]
&\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t^{2}\int_{t^{n}}^{t^{n+1}}\|\Pi_{h}\tilde{u}_{tt}(t^{n+1})\|^{2}_{0}+C\Delta t\|\tilde{u}_{t}(t^{n+1})-\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}\tilde{u}_{t}(t^{n+1})\|^{2}_{0}\\[8.0pt]
&\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+C\Delta t\|\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}\tilde{u}_{t}(t^{n+1})-\Pi_{h}\tilde{u}(t^{n+1})\|^{2}_{0}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{16}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\\[8.0pt]
&\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t^{2}\int_{t^{n}}^{t^{n+1}}\|\Pi_{h}\tilde{u}_{tt}(t^{n+1})\|^{2}_{0}+C\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{s}}^{2}\|\tilde{u}_{t}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(L^{2})}\\[8.0pt]
&\hskip 28.45274pt+C\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{x}}^{3}\|\tilde{u}_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{16}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}.\end{array}

(36)

Next, the second term is decomposed as

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}}\mathcal{A}\left(I_{2},\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\right)=\int_{S^{2}}\mathcal{A}\left(\tilde{u}(t^{n+1})-\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}\tilde{u}(t^{n+1}),\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\right)\\[8.0pt]
\hskip 113.81102pt\displaystyle+\int_{S^{2}}\mathcal{A}\left(\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}\tilde{u}(t^{n+1})-\Pi_{h}\tilde{u}(t^{n+1}),\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\right).\end{array}

Using Lemma
2
with the interpolation and projection estimates, the first term can be deduced by means of C-S and Young inequalities,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Delta t\int_{S^{2}}\left|\mathcal{A}\left(\tilde{u}(t^{n+1})-\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}\tilde{u}(t^{n+1}),\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\right)\right|&\\[8.0pt]
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle\leq\Delta t(2\overline{\sigma}_{\mathbf{s}}+\overline{\sigma}_{a})\left\|\tilde{u}(t^{n+1})-\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}\tilde{u}(t^{n+1})\right\|_{0}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|_{0}\\[8.0pt]
\hskip 56.9055pt\leq C\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{s}}^{2}\|\tilde{u}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(L^{2})}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{32}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}.\end{array}

(37)

Similarly, one can obtain that

\Delta t\int_{S^{2}}\left|\mathcal{A}\left(\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}\tilde{u}\left(t^{n+1}\right)-\Pi_{h}\tilde{u}\left(t^{n+1}\right),\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\right)\right|\leq C\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{x}}^{3}\|\tilde{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{32}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}.

(38)

Combing estimates (
37
) and (
38
), we get

\Delta t\int_{S^{2}}\left|\mathcal{A}\left(I_{2},\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\right)\right|\leq C\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{s}}^{2}\|\tilde{u}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(L^{2})}+C\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{x}}^{3}\|\tilde{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{16}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}.

(39)

Next, employing (
36
) and (
39
) in (
35
), it can be devised that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\|E^{n}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\Delta t\underline{\sigma}_{a}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\leq C\Delta t^{2}\int_{t^{n}}^{t^{n+1}}\|\Pi_{h}\tilde{u}_{tt}(t^{n+1})\|^{2}_{0}\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+C\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{s}}^{2}\|\tilde{u}_{t}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(L^{2})}+C\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{x}}^{3}\|\tilde{u}_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{8}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}.\end{array}

(40)

By using assumption
2
, it is reduced as

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\|E^{n}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\leq C\Delta t^{2}\int_{t^{n}}^{t^{n+1}}\|\Pi_{h}\tilde{u}_{tt}(t^{n+1})\|^{2}_{0}\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+C\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{s}}^{2}\|\tilde{u}_{t}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(L^{2})}+C\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{x}}^{3}\|\tilde{u}_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}.\end{array}

(41)

Finally, summing over

, we obtain the required results.

Remark 2

Note that the coefficient of
\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}
in the left side of (
40
) can be taken smaller as per our convenience by the means of C-S and Young inequalities.

Next, we discuss the bound for the local truncation error term for the second step, which is given by

E^{n}_{h}=u^{n}_{h}-\Pi_{h}u(t^{n}).

Let
\pi_{h_{\mathbf{x}}}u\in V_{h}
be the elliptic projection of
u\in V
, we have

a_{SUPG}(\pi_{h_{\mathbf{x}}}u,v_{h})=a_{SUPG}(u,v_{h}),\,\,\forall\,v_{h}\in V_{h}.

Then, one can claim that

a_{SUPG}(\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}\pi_{h_{\mathbf{x}}}u,v_{h})=\left(\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}f-\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}u_{t},v_{h}+\delta\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\right),\,\,\forall\,v_{h}\in V_{h}.

And, we have

\int_{S^{2}}a_{SUPG}(\Pi_{h}u,\zeta)=\int_{S^{2}}\left(\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}f-\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}u_{t},\zeta+\delta\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\zeta\right)_{\mathbf{x}},\,\,\forall\,\zeta\in\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h}.

(42)

The error term
E^{n+1}_{h}
satisfies the following equation

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}}(\partial_{\Delta t}E^{n+1}_{h},\zeta)_{\mathbf{x}}+\int_{S^{2}}a_{SUPG}(E^{n+1}_{h},\zeta)\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle=\int_{S^{2}}\left(\Lambda^{n+1}_{u}+\Lambda^{n+1}_{f},\zeta+\delta\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\zeta\right)_{\mathbf{x}}-\delta\int_{S^{2}}(\partial_{\Delta t}E^{n+1}_{h},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\zeta)_{\mathbf{x}},\,\,\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h},\end{array}

(43)

where functions
\Lambda^{n+1}_{u}
and
\Lambda^{n+1}_{f}
are defined as

\Lambda^{n+1}_{u}=\left(\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}u_{t}(t^{n+1})-\partial_{\Delta t}u(t^{n+1})\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Lambda^{n+1}_{f}=f^{n+1}-\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}f(t).

Lemma 4

The local truncation error
E^{n+1}_{h}
associated with the spatial discretization satisfies

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left(\|E^{m+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}-\|\tilde{E}^{m+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\right)+\dfrac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\int_{S^{2}}\|E^{m+1}_{h}\|_{SUPG}^{2}\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq C\bigg[\Delta t^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|u_{tt}\|^{2}_{0}+\|u_{ttt}\|^{2}_{0}\right)\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{x}}^{3}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}+\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}+\|u_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}\right)\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{s}}^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(H^{1})}+\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(H^{1})}+\|u_{tt}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(H^{1})}\right)\bigg].\end{array}

(44)

Proof.

Setting
\zeta=E^{n+1}_{h}
in (
43
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(\|E^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}-\|\tilde{E}^{n+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{S^{2}}\|E^{n+1}_{h}\|_{SUPG}^{2}\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\leq\int_{S^{2}}\left|\left(\Lambda^{n+1}_{u}+\Lambda^{n+1}_{f},E^{n+1}_{h}+\delta\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla E^{n+1}_{h}\right)_{\mathbf{x}}\right|+\delta\int_{S^{2}}\left|(\partial_{\Delta t}E^{n+1}_{h},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla E^{n+1}_{h})_{\mathbf{x}}\right|.\end{array}

Applying C-S inequality, Young inequality and summing over
m=0,1,\ldots,n-1
, we get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left(\|E^{m+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}-\|\tilde{E}^{m+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\right)+\dfrac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\int_{S^{2}}\|E^{m+1}_{h}\|_{SUPG}^{2}\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq 10(1+\delta)\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\Delta t\left(\|\Lambda^{m+1}_{u}\|^{2}_{0}+\|\Lambda^{m+1}_{f}\|^{2}_{0}\right)+10\delta\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\Delta t\|\partial_{\Delta t}E^{m+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}.\end{array}

(45)

Next, we discuss estimate for last term of the right hand side,
i.e.
,
\|\partial_{\Delta t}E^{m+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}
.
Consider
\chi^{n+1}=\partial_{\Delta t}E^{n+1}_{h}
in (
43
), it satisfies the following equation

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}}\left(\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1},\zeta\right)_{\mathbf{x}}+\int_{S^{2}}a_{SUPG}(\chi^{n+1},\zeta)\\[16.0pt]
=\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}}\left(\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{n+1}_{u}+\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{n+1}_{f},\zeta+\delta\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\zeta\right)_{\mathbf{x}}-\delta\int_{S^{2}}\left(\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\zeta\right)_{\mathbf{x}},\end{array}

(46)

where
\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1}=(\chi^{n+1}-\chi^{n})/\Delta t
and
\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{n+1}_{u}
,
\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{n+1}_{f}
are defined in a similar way.

Further, assigning
\zeta=\chi^{n+1}+\delta\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1}
in (
46
) and after small simplification, we deduce

\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{1}{2\Delta t}\bigg(\|\chi^{n+1}\|^{2}_{0}-\|\chi^{n}\|^{2}_{0}\bigg)+\delta\|\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1}\|^{2}_{0}+\delta\|\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\chi^{n+1}\|^{2}_{0}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|(\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{n})\chi^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{+})}\\[16.0pt]
\displaystyle+\dfrac{\delta^{2}}{2\Delta t}\bigg(\|\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\chi^{n+1}\|^{2}_{0}-\|\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\chi^{n}\|^{2}_{0}\bigg)+2\delta\int_{S^{2}}\bigg(\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\chi^{n+1}\bigg)_{\mathbf{x}}\\[16.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\int_{S^{2}}\bigg(\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{n+1}_{u}+\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{n+1}_{f},\chi^{n+1}+\delta\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\chi^{n+1}\bigg)_{\mathbf{x}}\\[16.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\delta\int_{S^{2}}\bigg(\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{n+1}_{u}+\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{n+1}_{f},\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1}+\delta\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1}\bigg)_{\mathbf{x}}\\[16.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt-\delta^{2}\int_{S^{2}}\big(\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla(\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1})\big)_{\mathbf{x}}.\end{array}

(47)

Next, we define the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{s}}
as follows:

\|\chi^{n+1}\|_{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{s}}^{2}\equiv\delta\left\|\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\chi^{n+1}+\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1}\right\|_{0}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|(\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{n})\chi^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{+})}.

By using the above norm definition, we deduce

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(\|\chi^{n+1}\|^{2}_{0}-\|\chi^{n}\|^{2}_{0}\right)+\|\chi^{n+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{s}}+\dfrac{\delta^{2}}{2\Delta t}\left(\|\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\chi^{n+1}\|^{2}_{0}-\|\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\chi^{n}\|^{2}_{0}\right)\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\int_{S^{2}}\left(\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{n+1}_{u}+\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{n+1}_{f},\chi^{n+1}+\delta\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\chi^{n+1}\right)_{\mathbf{x}}\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 34.14322pt+\delta\int_{S^{2}}\left(\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{n+1}_{u}+\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{n+1}_{f},\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1}+\delta\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1}\right)_{\mathbf{x}}\\[16.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 34.14322pt-\delta^{2}\bigg(\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla(\partial_{\Delta t}\chi^{n+1})\bigg)_{\mathbf{x}}.\end{array}

(48)

By using C-S and Young inequalities, we have estimated the right side terms of (
48
). After small mathematical simplification, we obtain the following inequality

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|\chi^{n+1}\|^{2}_{0}-\|\chi^{n}\|^{2}_{0}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{2}\|\chi^{n+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{s}}+\delta^{2}\left(\|\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\chi^{n+1}\|^{2}_{0}-\|\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\chi^{n}\|^{2}_{0}\right)\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\Delta t\|\chi^{n+1}\|^{2}_{0}+C\Delta t\left(\|\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{m+1}_{u}\|^{2}_{0}+\|\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{m+1}_{f}\|^{2}_{0}\right).\end{array}

(49)

By summing over
m=1,\ldots,n-1
and repeating the argument from Theorem
3.1
, we deduce that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\|\chi^{n}\|^{2}_{0}+\Delta t\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\|\chi^{m+1}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{s}}+\delta^{2}\|\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\chi^{n}\|^{2}_{0}\leq C\exp^{2T}\bigg(\|\chi^{1}\|^{2}_{0}\\[12.0pt]
\hskip 85.35826pt\displaystyle+C\delta^{2}\|\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\chi^{1}\|^{2}_{0}+C\Delta t\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\left(\|\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{m+1}_{u}\|^{2}_{0}+\|\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{m+1}_{f}\|^{2}_{0}\right)\bigg).\end{array}

(50)

Now substituting the estimate of
\|\partial_{\Delta t}E^{m+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}
from (
50
) in (
45
) and using the above inequality to get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left(\|E^{m+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}-\|\tilde{E}^{m+1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\right)+\dfrac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\int_{S^{2}}\|E^{m+1}_{h}\|_{SUPG}^{2}\leq C\delta\|\partial_{\Delta t}E^{1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 17.07164pt+C\Delta t\left[\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left(\|\Lambda^{m+1}_{u}\|^{2}_{0}+\|\Lambda^{m+1}_{f}\|^{2}_{0}\right)+\delta\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left(\|\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{m+1}_{u}\|^{2}_{0}+\|\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{m+1}_{f}\|^{2}_{0}\right)\right].\end{array}

(51)

Applying the standard interpolation results and Taylor’s theorem with integral
remainder term, we deduce

\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\|\Lambda^{m+1}_{u}\|^{2}_{0}\leq C\Delta t^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\int_{t^{n}}^{t^{n+1}}\|\Pi_{h}u_{tt}\|^{2}_{0}+C\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{x}}^{3}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\|\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}u_{t}(t^{m+1})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}.

(52)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\hskip-28.45274pt\displaystyle\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\|\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{m+1}_{u}\|^{2}_{0}\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\int_{t^{n}}^{t^{n+1}}\|\Pi_{h}u_{ttt}\|^{2}_{0}+C\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{x}}^{3}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\|\pi_{h_{\mathbf{s}}}u_{tt}(t^{m+1})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}.\end{array}

(53)

In a similar way, we have

\displaystyle\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\|\Lambda^{m+1}_{f}\|^{2}_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{s}}^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\|\partial_{\mathbf{s}}f(t^{m+1})\|^{2}_{0}.

(54)

\displaystyle\Delta t\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\|\partial_{\Delta t}\Lambda^{m+1}_{f}\|^{2}_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Delta t^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|f_{tt}\|^{2}_{0}+C\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{s}}^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\|\partial_{\mathbf{s}}f_{t}(t^{m+1})\|^{2}_{0}.

(55)

Next, we need to evaluate bounds for term
\|\partial_{\Delta t}E^{1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}
. By using the argument from
ahamd_ANM
and following similar technique as in (
50
), we devise that

\delta\|\partial_{\Delta t}E^{1}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}\leq C(\Delta t^{2}+h^{3}_{\mathbf{x}}+h^{2}_{\mathbf{s}}).

(56)

By combing the estimates (
52
)-(
56
) in (
51
), we obtain
the desired result (
44
).
This completes the proof.

Next, we present the convergence estimate of the operator-splitting finite element method (
15
)-(
16
) for the model problem (
1
).

Theorem 3.2

The global error
e^{n}=u-u^{n}_{h}
satisfies

\|e^{n}\|^{2}_{0}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\int_{S^{2}}\|e^{m+1}\|^{2}_{SUPG}\leq C(u,\tilde{u})(\Delta t^{2}+h^{3}_{\mathbf{x}}+h^{2}_{\mathbf{s}}).

(57)

Proof.
By combining the local error estimates (
33
) and (
44
), we obtain that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\|E^{n}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\int_{S^{2}}\|E^{m+1}_{h}\|_{SUPG}^{2}\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\|E^{0}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+C\Delta t\bigg[\Delta t\int_{0}^{T}\|\tilde{u}_{tt}\|^{2}_{0}+h_{\mathbf{s}}^{2}\|\tilde{u}_{t}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(L^{2})}+h_{\mathbf{x}}^{3}\|\tilde{u}_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}\bigg]\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+C\bigg[\Delta t^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|u_{tt}\|^{2}_{0}+\|u_{ttt}\|^{2}_{0}\right)\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{x}}^{3}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}+\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}+\|u_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(H^{2})}\right)\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\Delta t\,h_{\mathbf{s}}^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(H^{1})}+\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(H^{1})}+\|u_{tt}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(H^{1})}\right)\bigg].\end{array}

(58)

Noting that
E^{0}_{h}=0
. Then, the estimate (
58
) can be further simplified as

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\|E^{n}_{h}\|^{2}_{0}+\dfrac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\int_{S^{2}}\|E^{m+1}_{h}\|_{SUPG}^{2}\leq C(u,\tilde{u})(\Delta t^{2}+h^{3}_{\mathbf{x}}+h^{2}_{\mathbf{s}}),\end{array}

(59)

where
C(u,\tilde{u})
is the positive constant, depending upon
u,\tilde{u}
in (
58
). Finally, by employing the approximation results (
30
) and (
31
) and using above argumentation, the main convergence result (
57
) is devised. This completes the proof.

Computational Results

In this section, we present the numerical algorithm for the proposed discrete scheme and a validation of the theoretical estimates.

4.1

Numerical implementation

We briefly discuss the operator-splitting algorithm for the radiative transfer equation solution, whereas more details on implementation for general scalar equations can be found in
ganeshan_AMC1
and
barrenechea_ANM2017
. All the numerical experiments are performed in our in-house finite element package
ganesan2016object
;
wilbrandt2017parmoon
.

In Section
2
, the finite element space
\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h}
is given as follows

\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h}:=W_{h}\otimes V_{h}=\bigg\{\lambda\colon\lambda=\sum_{l=1}^{N_{s}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{x}}\lambda_{il}\phi_{i}\psi_{l},\,\,\lambda_{il}\in\mathbb{R}\bigg\}.

Further, the discrete solution
u^{n}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}^{1,0}_{h}
and its gradient are expressed by

u^{n}_{h}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{s})=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{s}}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{x}}u_{il}^{n}\phi_{i}(\mathbf{s})\psi_{l}(\mathbf{x}),\quad\nabla u^{n}_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{s}}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{x}}u_{il}^{n}\phi_{i}(\mathbf{s})\nabla\psi_{l}(\mathbf{x}),

where
u_{il}
are the unknown degrees of freedoms (DOFs).
Define the mass matrices
M^{1}_{\mathbf{s}},\,M^{2}_{\mathbf{s}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathbf{s}}\times N_{\mathbf{s}}}
, where the
(i,j)^{th}
entries of these matrices are given by

(M^{1}_{\mathbf{s}})_{ij}=\int_{S^{2}}\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\,d\mathbf{s},\qquad(M^{2}_{\mathbf{s}})_{ij}=\int_{S^{2}}\mathcal{K}\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\,d\mathbf{s}.

Further, the
(l,m)^{th}
entries of the matrices
M_{\mathbf{x}},\,A_{\mathbf{x}},\,M^{\delta}_{\mathbf{x}},\,A^{\delta}_{\mathbf{x}}
are given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle(M_{\mathbf{x}})_{lm}=\int_{\Omega}\psi_{l}\psi_{m}\,d\mathbf{x},\qquad\left(M^{\delta}_{\mathbf{x}}\right)_{lm}=\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\delta_{K}(\psi_{l},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\psi_{m})_{K}\,d\mathbf{x},\\
\displaystyle(A_{\mathbf{x}})_{lm}=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\psi_{l}\psi_{m}\,d\mathbf{x},\qquad\left(A^{\delta}_{\mathbf{x}}\right)_{lm}=\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\delta_{K}(\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\psi_{l},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\psi_{m})_{K}\,d\mathbf{x},\end{array}

and
m^{th}
component of the load vectors
F^{n}_{\mathbf{x}}
and
F^{\delta,n}_{\mathbf{x}}
are given by

\displaystyle\left(F^{n}_{\mathbf{x}}\right)_{m}=\int_{\Omega}f^{n}\psi_{m}\,d\mathbf{x},\qquad\left(F^{\delta,n}_{\mathbf{x}}\right)_{m}=\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\delta_{K}(f^{n},\mathbf{s}\cdot\nabla\psi_{m})_{K}.

Here the matrices
M^{\delta}_{\mathbf{x}},\,A^{\delta}_{\mathbf{x}}
and
F^{\delta,n}_{\mathbf{x}}
are due to the SUPG stabilization terms.
Further, we use
\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{n+1}_{k}
to denote an array of unknown solution coefficients (DOFs)
\{\tilde{u}^{n+1}_{k,i}\},\,\,k=1,2,\ldots,N_{\mathbf{x}},\,i=1,2,\ldots,N_{\mathbf{s}}
.
With these notations, the system matrix of the
\mathbf{s}
-direction step in the time interval
(t^{n},t^{n+1}]
becomes:

For given
\mathbf{u}^{n}_{k}
, solve

\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{s}}\,\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{n+1}_{k}=M^{1}_{\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{u}^{n}_{k},\qquad\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{s}}=\left(M^{1}_{\mathbf{s}}+\Delta t\,\sigma_{\tau}M^{1}_{\mathbf{s}}-\Delta t\,\sigma_{s}M^{2}_{\mathbf{s}}\right),

(60)

for
k=1,2,\ldots,N_{\mathbf{x}}
. In this way, we solve numerical solution in the
\mathbf{s}
-direction.

We next discuss the
\mathbf{x}
-direction
step, where the updated solution from the
\mathbf{s}
-direction is used to compute the solution of (
15
). In the
\mathbf{x}
-direction step, we first need to transpose the vector
\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{n+1}_{k}
to obtain
\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{n+1}_{\ell}
and then solve the linear system

\left(M_{\mathbf{x}}+M^{\delta}_{\mathbf{x}}+\Delta t(A_{\mathbf{x}}+A^{\delta}_{\mathbf{x}})\right)\mathbf{u}^{n+1}_{\ell}=\Delta t(F^{n}_{\mathbf{x}}+F^{\delta,n}_{\mathbf{x}})+(M_{\mathbf{x}}+M^{\delta}_{\mathbf{x}})\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}^{n+1}_{\ell},

(61)

for
\ell=1,2,\ldots,N_{\mathbf{s}}
. Though the mass matrix
M_{\mathbf{x}}
is independent of
\mathbf{s}
, all other matrices in (
61
) depend on
\mathbf{s}
. Therefore, all these
\mathbf{s}
-dependent matrices need to be assembled for each
\ell
in all time steps. However, the matrix assembling can be avoided for every
\ell
by assembling and storing the
\mathbf{s}
-dependent matrices in a component form. For example,
A_{\mathbf{x}}
can be split as

\displaystyle\displaystyle(A_{\mathbf{x}})_{lm}

\displaystyle=s_{1}\int_{\Omega_{h}}\frac{\partial\psi_{l}}{\partial x}\psi_{m}\,d\mathbf{x}+s_{2}\int_{\Omega_{h}}\frac{\partial\psi_{l}}{\partial y}\psi_{m}\,d\mathbf{x}+s_{3}\int_{\Omega_{h}}\frac{\partial\psi_{l}}{\partial z}\psi_{m}\,d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=s_{1}(A_{\mathbf{x}}^{I})_{lm}+s_{2}(A_{\mathbf{x}}^{II})_{lm}+s_{3}(A_{\mathbf{x}}^{III})_{lm}.

Hence, it is enough to assemble matrices
A_{\mathbf{x}}^{I}
,
A_{\mathbf{x}}^{II}
and
A_{\mathbf{x}}^{III}
only once and then multiply it with
s_{1}
,
s_{2}
and
s_{3}
, respectively, for every
\ell
in each time step. Following a similar technique for
\mathbf{s}
-dependent matrices, matrix assembling for each
\ell
in every time step can completely be avoided, and it is enough to assemble all these component matrices only once at the beginning of the computation. In this way, we can solve the linear system (
61
) very efficiently.

5

Conclusion and Discussion

An operator-splitting finite element method for the time-dependent, high-dimensional radiative transfer equation is proposed in this paper.
The numerical scheme combines the backward Euler scheme, SUPG method, and DG(
0
) for time, space, and angular discretization. The stability and consistency are established for the fully discrete scheme. Further, the convergence estimate with optimal order is derived. Moreover, the operator-splitting algorithm to compute the solution is also presented. An array of numerical experiments are performed to support the theoretical estimates and validate the proposed algorithm. The computed numerical results validate the implementation and confirm the derived error estimate.

Ill-posedness for the stationary Navier-Stokes equations in critical Besov spaces
Jinlu Li, Yanghai Yu, Weipeng Zhu

Abstract




Abstract:
This paper presents some progress toward an open question which proposed by Tsurumi (Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal. 234:2, 2019): whether or not the stationary Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is well-posed from
\dot{B}_{p,q}^{-2}
to
\mathbb{P}\dot{B}_{p,q}^{0}
with
p=d
and
1\leq q\leq 2
. In this paper, we prove that for the case
1\leq q<\frac{d}{2}
with
d\geq 4
the stationary Navier-Stokes equations is ill-posed from
\dot{B}_{d,q}^{-2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathbb{P}\dot{B}_{d,q}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by showing that a sequence of external forces is constructed to show discontinuity of the solution map at zero. Indeed in such case of
q
, there exists a sequence of external forces which converges to zero in
\dot{B}_{d,q}^{-2}
and yields a sequence of solutions which does not converge to zero in
\dot{B}_{d,q}^{0}
. In particular, we also prove that the stationary Navier-Stokes equations is well-posed from
\dot{B}_{d,2}^{-2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathbb{P}\dot{B}_{d,2}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
d=3,4
. Based on these two cases, we demonstrate that the above open question for the dimension
d\geq 4
has been solved completely.

Keywords:
Stationary Navier-Stokes equations, Besov spaces, Ill-posedness

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the forced stationary Navier-Stokes equations describing the motion of incompressible fluid in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla\Pi=f,\\
\mathrm{div}u=0,\end{cases}

(SNS)

where
u=u(x)=\big(u^{1}(x),\ldots,u^{d}(x)\big)
and
\Pi=\Pi(x)
denote the unknown velocity vector and the unknown pressure at the point
x=\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively, while
f=f(x)=\big(f^{1}(x),\ldots,f^{d}(x)\big)
denotes the given external force.

1.1
Known Well/Ill-posedness (WP/IP) results

There have been various studies on strong solutions
u
to (SNS) for given data
f

on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Leray
[
10
]
and Ladyzhenskaya
[
9
]
showed the existence
of strong solutions to (SNS), and Heywood
[
5
]
constructed solutions of (SNS) as a limit of
solutions of the non-stationary Navier-Stokes equations:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla\Pi=0,\;&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ t>0,\\
\mathrm{div}u=0,\;&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ t>0,\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0},\;&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{cases}

(NNS)

We should mention that Koch and Tataru
[
7
]
obtained the global well-posedness of the 3D (NNS) for small initial data in the space
\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}=\dot{F}_{\infty,2}^{-1}
. On the other hand, Bourgain and Pavlović
[
2
]
showed the ill-posedness of (NNS) in
\dot{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{-1}
(which includes
\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
). Later on, the ill-posedness in
\dot{B}_{\infty,q}^{-1}
with
1\leq q<\infty
was also showed by Yoneda in
[
15
]

(2<q<\infty)
and Wang in
[
14
]

(1\leq q\leq 2)
. These spaces play a crucial role since these are scaling invariant for the initial data
u_{0}
in (NNS).

Chen
[
4
]
showed that for every small external force having a divergence-form
f=\mathrm{div}F
with
F\in L^{d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists an
unique strong solution
u
of (SNS) in
L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Secchi
[
11
]
investigated existence and regularity of solutions to (SNS) in
L^{d}\cap L^{p}
with
p>d
. As for the well-posedness of (SNS) in homogeneous Besov spaces, Kaneko-Kozono-Shimizu in
[
8
]
showed the well-posed result as follows:

Theorem 1.1
(see
[
8
]
)

Let
d\geq 3
. Suppose that
1\leq p<d
and
1\leq q\leq\infty.
Then
(\mathrm{SNS})
is well-posed from
E=\dot{B}_{p,q}^{-3+\frac{d}{p}}
to
S=\mathbb{P}\dot{B}_{p,q}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}
.

Moreover, in the case
p=d
, (SNS) is also well-posed from
E=\dot{B}_{d,q}^{-2}
to
S=\mathbb{P}L^{d}
if
1\leq q\leq 2
. These spaces
E
and
S
are scaling invariant for the external force
f
and the velocity
u
in (SNS) respectively. Precisely speaking, the corresponding scaling transform is
\{u,\pi,f\}\mapsto

\left\{u_{\lambda},\Pi_{\lambda},f_{\lambda}\right\}
with
u_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda u(\lambda x),\Pi_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{2}\Pi(\lambda x),f_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{3}f(\lambda x)
, and we see that

\left\|f_{\lambda}\right\|_{E}=\|f\|_{E},\quad\left\|u_{\lambda}\right\|_{S}=\|u\|_{S},\quad\forall\lambda>0.

There are other previous results on the well-posedness in the case
p=d
. Bjorland et al.
[
3
]
showed the well-posedness with more general space of external forces. In fact, they proved that there are constants
\varepsilon,\delta>0
such that if
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
satisfies
\left\|(-\Delta)^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{d,\infty}}<\varepsilon
, then there exists a unique solution
u\in B_{\mathbb{P}L^{d,\infty}}(\delta)
to (SNS), which belongs to
L^{d}
if and only if
\mathbb{P}f\in\dot{H}^{-2,d}
. Phan and Phuc
[
16
]
showed the well-posedness in the largest critical space of external forces including
\dot{H}^{-2,d}
.

A nature question to ask is: Whether or not (SNS) is well-posed from
E=\dot{B}_{p,q}^{-3+\frac{d}{p}}
to
S=\mathbb{P}\dot{B}_{p,q}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}
when
d\leq p\leq\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
?

Recently, Tsuruni gave a partial answer to the above problem. More precisely, Tsuruni
[
12
]
proved the ill-posedness of (SNS) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see
[
13
]
for the Torus case
\mathbb{T}^{d}
), namely,

Theorem 1.2
(see
[
12
]
)

Let
d<p\leq\infty,1\leq q\leq\infty
, and if
p=d,2<q\leq\infty
, then (SNS) is ill-posed from
E=\dot{B}_{p,q}^{-3+\frac{d}{p}}
to
S=

\mathbb{P}\dot{B}_{p,q}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}
in the sense that the solution map
f\in E\mapsto u\in S
is, even if it exists, not continuous. More precisely, under such a condition, there exists a sequence
\left\{f_{N}\right\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
of external forces with
f_{N}\rightarrow 0
in
E
such that there exists a unique solution
u_{N}\in\mathbb{P}L^{d}
of (SNS) for each
f_{N}
, which never converges to zero in
S
(actually, even in
\dot{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{-1}
).

Obviously, Tsuruni’s result makes it clear that the well-posedness and ill-posedness can be divided between the case
(p,q)\in[1,d)\times[1,\infty]
(Theorem
1.1
) and the case
(p,q)\in(d,\infty]\times[1,\infty]\cup\{d\}\times(2,\infty]
(Theorem
1.2
), respectively. However, we remark that there is a gap between the global well-posedness in Theorem
1.1
and the ill-posedness in Theorem
1.2
. In other words, for the case
p=d
and
1\leq q\leq 2
, it is still unknown whether (SNS) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is well-posed or ill-posed from
\dot{B}_{d,q}^{-2}
to
\mathbb{P}\dot{B}_{d,q}^{0}
. In this paper, we are devoted to answer the question.

1.2
New Results

Now we return to the equation (SNS). Let us write the
i
-th component of
\mathbf{v}
as
\mathbf{v}^{(i)}
. For the vector fields
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{u}
, we define the tensor product
\mathbf{v}\otimes\mathbf{u}
as the
(i,j)
-th component
\mathbf{v}^{(i)}\mathbf{u}^{(j)}
with
1\leq i,j\leq d
. Thus, if
\mathbf{v}
is divergence free (that is if
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0
) we have
\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{v}\otimes\mathbf{u})=(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}
. Let us rewrite it to the generalized form so that we can apply successive approximation. First, we note that since
\mathrm{div}u=0
, there holds

u\cdot\nabla u=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}\left(u^{(i)}u\right)=\mathrm{div}(u\otimes u).

We next introduce the projection
\mathbb{P}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow L_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\equiv\overline{\left\{f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d});\mathrm{div}f=0\right\}}^{\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}
. In
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathbb{P}
can be defined by
\mathbb{P}=\mathrm{Id}+\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\mathrm{div}
, or equivalently,
\mathbb{P}=(\mathbb{P}_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant d}
with
\mathbb{P}_{ij}\equiv\delta_{ij}+R_{i}R_{j}
.

Applying
\mathbb{P}
to
(\mathrm{SNS})
, we obtain

-\Delta u+\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)=\mathbb{P}f,

implied by
\mathbb{P}u=u
and
\mathbb{P}(\nabla\Pi)=0
since
\mathrm{div}u=0
.
Also, since
\mathbb{P}
commutes with
-\Delta
, the solution
u
of
(\mathrm{SNS})
can be expressed as

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=(-\Delta)^{-1}\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)+(-\Delta)^{-1}\mathbb{P}f

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(-\Delta)^{-1}\mathrm{div}(u\otimes u)+\mathbb{P}(-\Delta)^{-1}f

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}(u,u)+g,

(rSNS)

here and in what follows, we shall denote the bilinear form

\mathcal{B}(u,v)\equiv\mathbb{P}(-\Delta)^{-1}\mathrm{div}(u\otimes v).

Our main results now read as follows:

Theorem 1.3

Let
d=3,4
.
(\mathrm{SNS})
is well-posed from
E=\dot{B}_{d,2}^{-2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
S=\mathbb{P}\dot{B}_{d,2}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Theorem 1.4

Let
d\geq 4
and
1\leq q<\frac{d}{2}
. (rSNS) is ill-posed from
\dot{B}^{0}_{d,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathbb{P}\dot{B}^{0}_{d,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the following sense:
There exist
\left\{g_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\dot{B}_{d,q}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that a sequence of solutions
u_{n}\in\dot{B}_{d,q}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to (rSNS) which satisfies

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|g_{n}\right\|_{\dot{B}_{d,q}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0

and for some positive constant
\varepsilon_{0}

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{{\dot{B}}_{d,q}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

Remark 1.1

Theorem
1.4
demonstrates that if
d\geq 4
and
1\leq q<\frac{d}{2}
, there exists a sequence of external forces which converges to zero in
\dot{B}_{d,q}^{0}
and yields a sequence of solutions to (rSNS) which does not converge to zero in
\dot{B}_{d,q}^{0}
. In other words, the (SNS) is ill-posed from
\dot{B}_{d,q}^{-2}
to
\mathbb{P}\dot{B}_{d,q}^{0}
due to the discontinuity of the solution map at zero.

Main Ideas

To illustrate our main idea, we introduce

\begin{cases}G\equiv\mathcal{B}(g,g),\\
U\equiv\mathcal{B}(u,u)-G,\end{cases}

(1.1)

then we have form (rSNS)

u=g+G+U,

(1.2)

Obviously,

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}(U,g)+\mathcal{B}(g,U)+\mathcal{B}(U,U)+\mathcal{B}(U,G)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{B}(G,U)+\mathcal{B}(g,G)+\mathcal{B}(G,g)+\mathcal{B}(G,G).

Lemma 1.1

Let
d=3,4
. Then for
u,v\in\dot{B}^{0}_{d,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have
\mathcal{B}(u,v)\in L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\dot{B}^{0}_{d,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the estimate

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(u,v)\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
C
is a positive constant.

Proof.
Due to the embedding
L^{\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{0}_{\frac{d}{2},2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{-1}_{d,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\dot{B}^{0}_{d,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\hookrightarrow L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(u,v)\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\otimes v\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Remark 1.3

With Lemma
1.1
at our disposal, we can prove Theorem
1.3
by successive approximation method. Since the procedure is standard (see
[
8
]
), we shall not go into details.

Lemma 1.2

Let
d\geq 4
. Then for
u,v\in L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have
\mathcal{B}(u,v)\in L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\dot{B}^{0}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the estimate

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(u,v)\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\mathcal{B}(u,v)\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
C
is a positive constant.

Proof.
We have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(u,v)\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u\otimes v\|_{H^{-1,d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u\otimes v\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(u,v)\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u\otimes v\|_{\dot{B}^{-1}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u\otimes v\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u\otimes v\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We should emphasize that, Lemma
1.2
tells us that if there exists small enough
\delta
such that
\|g\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\delta
, then we have a unique solution
u\in L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to (rSNS) such that
\|u\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\delta
, which implies that the solution map
L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ni g\mapsto u\in L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of (rSNS) is well-posed.

Corollary 1.1

Let
d\geq 4
and
1\leq q<\frac{d}{2}
. Assume that
g\in L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\dot{B}^{1}_{\frac{d}{2},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\|g\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\dot{B}^{0}_{d,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\delta
for small enough
\delta
, then there exists a solution
u\in L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\dot{B}^{0}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to (rSNS). Moreover,
u\in\dot{B}^{0}_{d,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.
Due to
\dot{B}^{1}_{\frac{d}{2},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{0}_{d,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{0}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, using Lemma
1.2
yields that
u\in L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\dot{B}^{0}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
It is easy to deduce that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(u,u)\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\frac{d}{2},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\otimes u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\frac{d}{2},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\frac{d}{2},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

from which and (rSNS), we obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\frac{d}{2},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\frac{d}{2},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\frac{d}{2},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\delta\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\frac{d}{2},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{\frac{d}{2},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

which implies
u\in\dot{B}^{1}_{\frac{d}{2},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{0}_{d,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Furthermore, we have

Corollary 1.2

Let
d\geq 4
. For small enough
\delta
such that
\|g\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\delta
, then we have

\displaystyle\|U\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|U\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{3},

where
C
is a positive constant.

Proof.

Using Lemma
1.2
yields

\displaystyle\|U\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|U\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|U\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|U\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|G\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|G\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|U\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|U\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{3}+\|g\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{4}\right),

which enables us to complete the proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Remark 1.4

From (
1.2
) and Corollary
1.2
, we expect that the primarily affect which leads to the discontinuity to the solution of (rSNS) is that the worst term from nonlinear interactions
\mathcal{B}(g,g)

2
Preliminaries

Firstly, let us recall that for all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, the Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
, is defined by

(\mathcal{F}f)(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for any}\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The inverse Fourier transform of any
g
is given by

(\mathcal{F}^{-1}g)(x)=\check{g}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(\xi)e^{ix\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi.

Next, we will recall some facts about the Littlewood-Paley (L-P) decomposition, the homogeneous Besov spaces and their some useful properties.

Proposition 2.1
(L-P decomposition, See
[
1
]
)

Let
\mathcal{B}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|\leq 4/3\}
and
\mathcal{C}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:3/4\leq|\xi|\leq 8/3\}.

Choose a radial, non-negative, smooth function
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto[0,1]
such that it is supported in
\mathcal{B}
and
\chi\equiv 1
for
|\xi|\leq 3/4
. Setting
\varphi(\xi):=\chi(\xi/2)-\chi(\xi)
, then we deduce that
\varphi
is supported in
\mathcal{C}
and
\varphi(\xi)\equiv 1
for
4/3\leq|\xi|\leq 3/2
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,\quad\forall\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi^{2}(2^{-j}\xi)\leq 1,\quad\forall\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

For every
u\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the homogeneous dyadic blocks
{\dot{\Delta}}_{j}
is defined as follows

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}_{j}u=\varphi(2^{-j}D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\mathcal{F}u\big)=2^{dj}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\check{\varphi}\big(2^{j}(x-y)\big)u(y)\mathrm{d}y,\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\dot{\Delta}}_{j}u=\widetilde{\varphi}(2^{-j}D)u=\sum_{|k-j|\leq 1}{\dot{\Delta}}_{k}u.

Moreover, the dyadic blocks
{\dot{\Delta}}_{j}
satisfies the property of almost orthogonality:

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}_{j}\dot{\Delta}_{k}u\equiv 0\quad\text{if}\quad|j-k|\geq 2.

In the homogeneous case, the following Littlewood-Paley decomposition makes sense

u=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\quad\text{for any}\;u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}:=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\;\lim_{j\rightarrow-\infty}\|\chi(2^{-j}D)u\|_{L^{\infty}}=0\Big\}.

We turn to the definition of the Besov Spaces and norms which will come into play in our paper.

Definition 2.1
(
[
1
]
)

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
(p,q)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
. The homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
consists of all tempered distribution
f
such that

\displaystyle\dot{B}_{p,q}^{s}=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\;\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty\Big\},

where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{sjr}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\|^{r}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{1/q},

if
1\leq q<\infty
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{sj}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

if
q=\infty
.

Remark 2.1

We point out that the following properties will be used in the sequel.

•

For the homogeneous Besov spaces, we have the embedding properties as follows:

\dot{B}_{p,q_{1}}^{s}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{p,q_{2}}^{s},\;s\in\mathbb{R},1\leq p\leq\infty,1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq\infty

and

\dot{B}_{p_{1},q}^{s_{1}}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{p_{2},q}^{s_{2}},\;-\infty<s_{2}\leq s_{1}<\infty,1\leq q\leq\infty,1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty

\displaystyle\equiv\widetilde{c}\varepsilon^{1-\frac{2}{d}}2^{2\ell},

thus

\displaystyle\mathbf{J_{1}}=\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{-2\ell q}\left\|{\Delta}_{\ell}\left((\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})H_{1,1}\right)\right\|^{q}_{L^{d}(\mathbf{B}_{\ell})}\geq\widetilde{c}\varepsilon^{(1-\frac{2}{d})q}n.

(3.18)

Estimation of
\mathbf{J_{2}}
.

By direct computations, we get

\displaystyle\quad\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}(\partial_{1}(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})^{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}H_{1,1})\|_{L^{d}(\mathbf{B}_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}(\partial_{1}(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})^{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}H_{1,1})\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}2^{-2\ell}\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}(\partial_{1}(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})^{2}H_{1,1})\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}\varepsilon^{3}2^{3\ell}\|[(\partial_{1}(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})^{2}\phi^{2}]\big(2^{\ell}A(y-2^{2n+\ell}\vec{e})\big)\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}\varepsilon^{3-\frac{2}{d}}2^{2\ell}\|(\partial_{1}(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})^{2}\phi^{2}\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}\varepsilon^{3-\frac{2}{d}}2^{2\ell},

where we have used

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}(\partial_{1}(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})^{2}H_{1,1})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{3}2^{5\ell}[(\partial_{1}(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})^{2}\phi^{2}]\big(2^{\ell}A(y-2^{2n+\ell}\vec{e})\big).

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbf{J_{2}}=\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{-2\ell q}\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}\left(\partial_{1}(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})^{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}H_{1,1}\right)\right\|^{q}_{L^{d}(\mathbf{B}_{\ell})}\leq\widetilde{C}\varepsilon^{(3-\frac{2}{d})q}n.

(3.19)

Remark 3.2

It is worthwhile pointing out that both the constants
\widetilde{c}
and
\widetilde{C}
do not depend on the parameter
\varepsilon
. Combining (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), we need to take
\varepsilon
sufficiently small such that
\mathbf{J_{2}}
can be absorbed by
\mathbf{J_{1}}
. This is why in the present paper we set the parameter
\varepsilon
in the matric
A
.

Next, we deal with the last two terms which are much less than the first one.
By making fully use of the information of decreasing rapidly functions
\check{\varphi}
and
\phi
when
k\neq\ell
, we can deal with the third term.

Estimation of
\mathbf{J_{3}}
.

Noting the fact that for
100d<N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

|\check{\varphi}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{-N},

then we have

\displaystyle\quad\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}\left((\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})H_{1,2}\right)\right\|_{L^{d}(\mathbf{B}_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}(n),k\neq\ell}2^{3k}2^{d\ell}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\check{\varphi}(2^{\ell}(x-y))[(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})\phi^{2}]\big(2^{k}A(y-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})\big)\mathrm{d}y\right\|_{L^{d}(\mathbf{B}_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}(n),k\neq\ell}2^{3k}2^{d\ell}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(1+2^{\ell}|x-y|\Big)^{-N}\Big(1+2^{k}|A(y-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})|\Big)^{-2N}\mathrm{d}y\right\|_{L^{d}(\mathbf{B}_{\ell})}.

(3.20)

Dividing the integral region in terms of
y
into the following two parts to estimate:

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle=\left\{y:\;|A(y-2^{\ell+2n}\vec{e})|\leq\varepsilon 2^{2n}\right\}\cup\left\{y:\;|A(y-2^{\ell+2n}\vec{e})|\geq\varepsilon 2^{2n}\right\}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathbf{A}_{1}\cup\mathbf{A}_{2},

For
x\in\mathbf{B}_{\ell}
and
y\in\mathbf{A}_{1}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left|A(y-2^{k+2n}\vec{e})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|A(y-2^{\ell+2n}\vec{e})+A(2^{\ell+2n}\vec{e}-2^{k+2n}\vec{e})\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|(2^{\ell+2n}-2^{k+2n})A\vec{e}\right|-\left|A(y-2^{\ell+2n}\vec{e})\right|

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon 2^{2n}.

For
x\in\mathbf{B}_{\ell}
and
y\in\mathbf{A}_{2}
, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle|x-y|

\displaystyle\geq\left|A(y-2^{\ell+2n}\vec{e})\right|-\left|A(x-2^{\ell+2n}\vec{e})\right|\geq\varepsilon 2^{2n}-2^{-\ell}\geq\varepsilon 2^{2n-1}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(1+2^{\ell}|x-y|\Big)^{-N}\Big(1+2^{k}|A(y-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})|\Big)^{-2N}\mathrm{d}y\right\|_{L^{d}(\mathbf{B}_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C2^{-2(k+2n)N}\left\|\int_{\mathbf{A}_{1}}\Big(1+2^{\ell}|x-y|\Big)^{-N}\mathrm{d}y\right\|_{L^{d}(\mathbf{B}_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\quad+C2^{-(\ell+2n)N}\left\|\int_{\mathbf{A}_{2}}\Big(1+2^{k}|A(y-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})|\Big)^{-2N}\mathrm{d}y\right\|_{L^{d}(\mathbf{B}_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C\left(2^{-d\ell}2^{-2(k+2n)N}+2^{-(\ell+2n)N}2^{-dk}\right)2^{-\frac{\ell}{d}}.

Plugging the above into (
3.1
) yields

\displaystyle\left\|{\dot{\Delta}}_{\ell}\left((\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})H_{1,2}\right)\right\|_{L^{d}(\mathbf{B}_{\ell})}\leq C\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}(n),k\neq\ell}2^{3k}2^{d\ell}\left(2^{-d\ell}2^{-2(k+2n)N}+2^{-(\ell+2n)N}2^{-dk}\right)2^{-\frac{\ell}{d}}\leq C2^{-n}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{J_{3}}=\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}(n)}2^{-2\ell q}\left\|{\dot{\Delta}}_{\ell}\left((\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})H_{1,2}\right)\right\|^{q}_{L^{d}(\mathbf{B}_{\ell})}\leq Cn2^{-qn}.

Estimation of
\mathbf{J_{4}}
.

Notice that

\displaystyle\phi^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\theta^{2}(x_{i})+\frac{1}{2}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\theta^{2}(x_{i})\cos\left(\frac{17}{12}x_{d}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\Theta_{1}(x)+\Theta_{2}(x),

then we have for
k\neq\ell
,

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}\left(\Theta_{2}\big(2^{k}A(x-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})\big)\right)=0,

which implies

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}\Big(\partial_{1}(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})^{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}[\phi^{2}\big(2^{k}A(x-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})\big)]\Big)

\displaystyle=\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}\Big(\partial_{1}(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})^{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}[\Theta_{1}\big(2^{k}A(x-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})\big)]\Big)

\displaystyle=\dot{\Delta}_{\ell}\widetilde{\dot{\Delta}}_{\ell}\Psi(x)

where we denote

\Psi(x)\equiv U\big(2^{k}A(x-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})\big).

and

U(x)=\varepsilon^{3}2^{k}\partial_{1}(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})^{2}(-\varepsilon^{2}\partial^{2}_{1}-\varepsilon^{2}\partial^{2}_{2}-\partial^{2}_{3}-\cdots-\partial^{2}_{d})^{-1}[\Theta_{1}](x),

we can show that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\dot{\Delta}}_{\ell}\Psi(x)

\displaystyle=2^{d\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{h}(2^{\ell}(x-y))U\big(2^{k}A(y-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})\big)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=2^{d\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{h}(2^{\ell}(x-z-2^{2n+k}\vec{e}))U\big(2^{k}Az\big)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-2}2^{d(\ell-k)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{h}(2^{\ell}(x-2^{-k}A^{-1}y-2^{2n+k}\vec{e}))U\big(y\big)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-2}2^{d(\ell-k)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{h}(2^{\ell-k}A^{-1}(2^{k}A[x-2^{2n+k}\vec{e}]-y))U\big(y\big)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=(P_{\ell}*U)\big(2^{k}A(x-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})\big),

where

{P_{\ell}*U}=\varepsilon^{-2}2^{d(\ell-k)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{h}(2^{\ell-k}A^{-1}(x-y))U\big(y\big)\mathrm{d}y.

Notice that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\tilde{h}(2^{\ell-k}A^{-1}\cdot)]=\varepsilon^{2}2^{-d(\ell-k)}\tilde{\varphi}(2^{k-\ell}A\xi),

we have

\displaystyle(P_{\ell}*U)(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\varepsilon^{3}2^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\cdot\frac{-i\xi_{1}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}\xi_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\xi_{2}^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}+\cdots+\xi^{2}_{d}}\widehat{\Theta_{1}}(\xi)\tilde{\varphi}(2^{k-\ell}A\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi.

Since
\tilde{\varphi}(x)=\varphi(\frac{1}{2}x)+\varphi(x)+\varphi(2x)
, then we have
\tilde{\varphi}(x)
is supported in
\mathcal{C}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:3/8\leq|\xi|\leq 16/3\}.
Therefor, if
|\xi|\leq\frac{3}{8}2^{\ell-k}
, we can deduce that
\tilde{\varphi}(2^{k-\ell}A\xi)=0
.
Then, using

\displaystyle(1+|x|^{2})^{N}(P_{\ell}*U)(x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d}\varepsilon^{3}2^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{N}(e^{ix\cdot\xi})\frac{-i\xi_{1}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}\xi_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\xi_{2}^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}+\cdots+\xi^{2}_{d}}\widehat{\Theta_{1}}(\xi)\tilde{\varphi}(2^{k-\ell}A\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d}\varepsilon^{3}2^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{N}\left(\frac{-i\xi_{1}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}\xi_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\xi_{2}^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}+\cdots+\xi^{2}_{d}}\widehat{\Theta_{1}}(\xi)\tilde{\varphi}(2^{k-\ell}A\xi)\right)\mathrm{d}\xi,

we have

\displaystyle|(1+|x|^{2})^{N}(P_{\ell}*U)(x)|\leq C2^{k}2^{(k-\ell)2N},

which implies

\displaystyle\Big|\widetilde{\dot{\Delta}}_{\ell}\Psi(x)\Big|\leq C2^{k}2^{(k-\ell)2N}\Big(1+2^{k}|A(x-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})|\Big)^{-2N}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\quad\left\|{\dot{\Delta}}_{\ell}\left(\partial_{1}(\partial_{1}-\partial_{2})^{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}H_{1,2}\right)\right\|_{L^{d}(\mathbf{B}_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}(n),k\neq\ell}2^{2k}2^{d\ell}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\check{\varphi}(2^{\ell}(x-y))\Big(\widetilde{\dot{\Delta}}_{\ell}(\Psi(y))\Big)\mathrm{d}y\right\|_{L^{d}(\mathbf{B}_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}(n),k\neq\ell}2^{3k}2^{d\ell}2^{(k-\ell)2N}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(1+2^{\ell}|x-y|\Big)^{-N}\Big(1+2^{k}|A(y-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})\big|\Big)^{-2N}\mathrm{d}y\right\|_{L^{d}(\mathbf{B}_{\ell})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}(n),k\neq\ell}2^{3k}2^{d\ell}2^{(k-\ell)2N}\left(2^{-d\ell}2^{-2(k+2n)N}+2^{-(\ell+2n)N}2^{-dk}\right)2^{-\frac{\ell}{d}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-n},

this gives that

\displaystyle\mathbf{J_{4}}\leq Cn2^{-qn}.

Combining the above estimates yields

\displaystyle\left\|\big(\mathbb{P}(E_{n})\big)^{(1)}\right\|^{q}_{\dot{B}^{-2}_{d,q}(\mathbb{N}(n))}

\displaystyle\geq(\widetilde{c}-\widetilde{C}\varepsilon^{2})\varepsilon^{(1-\frac{2}{d})q}-C2^{-qn},

which implies

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(g_{n},g_{n})\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,q}(\mathbb{N}(n))}\geq c.

(3.21)

Thus, we complete the proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Combining Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
, we shall prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.
Letting
u_{n}=g_{n}+\mathcal{B}(g_{n},g_{n})+U_{n}
, then we deduce from Proposition
3.1
that

\displaystyle\|g_{n}\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,q}(\mathbb{N}(n))}\leq C\|g_{n}\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,1}}\leq Cn^{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2q}}

and Corollary
1.2
that

\displaystyle\|U_{n}\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,q}(\mathbb{N}(n))}\leq n^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{2}{d}}\|U_{n}\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{N}(n))}\leq Cn^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{2}{d}}\|g_{n}\|^{3}_{L^{d}}\leq Cn^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{2}{d}}n^{\frac{3}{d}-\frac{3}{2q}}\leq Cn^{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2q}}.

Therefore, we obtain for large
n
enough

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,q}}

\displaystyle\geq\|u_{n}\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,q}(\mathbb{N}(n))}

\displaystyle\geq\|\mathcal{B}(g_{n},g_{n})\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,q}(\mathbb{N}(n))}-\|g_{n}\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,q}(\mathbb{N}(n))}-\|U_{n}\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{d,q}(\mathbb{N}(n))}

\displaystyle\geq c-Cn^{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2q}}\geq\frac{c}{2}.

Thus, we have completed the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Lemma 4.1

Let
b_{n}
be defined by (
3.3
). Then there holds

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \widehat{b_{n}}(\xi)\subset\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \frac{33}{24}2^{n}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{35}{24}2^{n}\right\}.

Proof.
For the sake of simplicity, we denote

\Phi_{k}:=\phi\left(2^{k}A(x-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})\right)\sin\left(\lambda_{n}\vec{e}\cdot x\right)\quad\text{with}\quad\lambda_{n}:=\frac{17}{12}2^{n}.

Using the fact
\sin\alpha=\frac{i}{2}(e^{-i\alpha}-e^{i\alpha})
from Euler’s formula, we deduce easily that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left(\Phi_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\phi\left(2^{k}A(x-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})\right)\sin\left(\lambda_{n}\vec{e}\cdot x\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(e^{-ix\cdot\left(\xi+\lambda_{n}\vec{e}\right)}-e^{-ix\cdot\left(\xi-\lambda_{n}\vec{e}\right)}\right)\phi\left(2^{k}A(x-2^{2n+k}\vec{e})\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\left(\Phi_{k}^{1}-\Phi_{k}^{2}\right),

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}^{1}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i(x+2^{2n+k}\vec{e})\cdot\left(\xi+\lambda_{n}\vec{e}\right)}\phi\left(2^{k}Ax\right)\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i(x+2^{2n+k}\vec{e})\cdot\left(\xi-\lambda_{n}\vec{e}\right)}\phi\left(2^{k}Ax\right)\mathrm{d}x.

Let
\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}:=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\lambda_{n}
. Then by change of variables, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}^{1}=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}2^{dk}}e^{-i2^{2n+k}\vec{e}\cdot\left(\xi+\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}\vec{e}\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix_{1}\cdot\varepsilon^{-1}2^{-k}\left(\xi_{1}+\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}\right)}\theta(x_{1})\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix_{2}\cdot\varepsilon^{-1}2^{-k}\left(\xi_{2}+\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}\right)}\theta(x_{2})\mathrm{d}x_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix_{3}\cdot 2^{-k}\xi_{3}}\theta(x_{3})\mathrm{d}x_{3}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix_{d-1}\cdot 2^{-k}\xi_{d-1}}\theta(x_{d-1})\mathrm{d}x_{d-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix_{d}\cdot 2^{-k}\xi_{d}}\theta(x_{d})\sin\left(\frac{17}{24}x_{d}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{i}{\varepsilon^{2}2^{dk+1}}e^{-i2^{2n+k}\vec{e}\cdot\left(\xi+\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}\vec{e}\right)}\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{1}+\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}}{\varepsilon 2^{k}}\right)\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{2}+\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}}{\varepsilon 2^{k}}\right)\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{3}}{2^{k}}\right)\cdots\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{d-1}}{2^{k}}\right)\left[\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{d}}{2^{k}}+\frac{17}{24}\right)-\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{d}}{2^{k}}-\frac{17}{24}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{i}{\varepsilon^{2}2^{dk+1}}e^{-i2^{2n+k}\vec{e}\cdot\left(\xi+\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}\vec{e}\right)}\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{1}+\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}}{\varepsilon 2^{k}}\right)\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{2}+\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}}{\varepsilon 2^{k}}\right)\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{3}}{2^{k}}\right)\cdots\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{d-1}}{2^{k}}\right)\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{d}}{2^{k}}+\frac{17}{24}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle~\frac{i}{\varepsilon^{2}2^{dk+1}}e^{-i2^{2n+k}\vec{e}\cdot\left(\xi+\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}\vec{e}\right)}\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{1}+\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}}{\varepsilon 2^{k}}\right)\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{2}+\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}}{\varepsilon 2^{k}}\right)\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{3}}{2^{k}}\right)\cdots\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{d-1}}{2^{k}}\right)\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{d}}{2^{k}}-\frac{17}{24}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\Phi_{k}^{+,+}-\Phi_{k}^{+,-}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}^{2}=

\displaystyle~\frac{i}{\varepsilon^{2}2^{dk+1}}e^{-i2^{2n+k}\vec{e}\cdot\left(\xi-\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}\vec{e}\right)}\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{1}-\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}}{\varepsilon 2^{k}}\right)\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{2}-\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}}{\varepsilon 2^{k}}\right)\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{3}}{2^{k}}\right)\cdots\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{d-1}}{2^{k}}\right)\widehat{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi_{d}}{2^{k}}+\frac{17}{24}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\Phi_{k}^{-,+}-\Phi_{k}^{-,-}.

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\ \Phi_{k}^{\pm,\pm}\subset\Big\{\xi:

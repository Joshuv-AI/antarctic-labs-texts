Twisted bilayer graphene reveals its flat bands under spin pumping
Sonia Haddad 1,2,3, Takeo Kato 2, Jihang Zhu 3, Lassaad Mandhour 1

Abstract
The salient property of the electronic band structure of twisted bilayer graphene (TBG), at the so-called magic angle (MA), is the emergence of flat bands around the charge neutrality point. These bands are associated with the observed superconducting phases and the correlated insulating states. Scanning tunneling microscopy combined with angle resolved photoemission spectroscopy are usually used to visualize the flatness of the band structure of TBG at the MA. Here, we theoretically argue that spin pumping (SP) provides a direct probe of the flat bands of TBG and an accurate determination of the MA. We consider a junction separating a ferromagnetic insulator and a heterostructure of TBG adjacent to a monolayer of a transition metal dichalcogenide. We show that the Gilbert damping of the ferromagnetic resonance experiment, through this junction, depends on the twist angle of TBG, and exhibits a sharp drop at the MA. We discuss the experimental realization of our results which open the way to a twist switchable spintronics in twisted van der Waals heterostructures.

Around the MA, the bands are almost flat and the statistical weights

and

, are small compared to the case of a twist angle away from the MA, where the band dispersion is larger.

Let us now turn to the case of a dirty interface where the spin transfer should now also include the non-conserved momentum processes. The corresponding self-energy [Eq. (
9
)] can also be expressed in terms of the thermal weight
\Delta f(E)
governing the interband transitions (see Sec. IV of the Supplemental Material
supp
).

Regarding the flatness of the bands, the dirty processes at the MA acquire, as in the clean limit, small thermal weights compared to the twist angles away from the MA, where the band are dispersive.
In the dirty limit, the Gilbert damping correction is, then, expected to drop at the MA as found in the case of a clean interface.

It comes out that the twist angle dependence of
\delta\alpha_{G}
is a direct probe of the emergence of the flat bands in TBG. On the other hand, the temperature dependence of the fine structure around the MA provides an accurate measurement of the MA, with a precision below
0.005^{\circ}
(see Fig.

It is worth stressing that in our model we did not take into account the electron-electron interactions which significantly distort the electronic band structure of TBG
Guinea18
;
Guinea20
;
Guinea21
;
Guinea22
. Near the MA, the dominant electron-electron interaction is found to be the Coulomb interaction with an amplitude estimated to be 10-15 meV

\sim 2-5
meV and the SOC considered in the present work. How are the results of Fig.2 modified in the presence of Coulomb interaction?
Treating this interaction within the Hartree-Fock approximation revealed that the Hartree term considerably widens the bands while the exchange term leads, basically, to broken-symmetry phases. At the charge neutrality, the Hartree term vanishes and the exchange potential, which concerns bands with identical spins, opens a gap of 4 meV
Guinea20
;
Guinea21
;
Guinea22

\Delta f(E)
of the bands with opposite

Guinea20
;
Guinea21
;
Guinea22
, but remains smaller than 3 meV, which preserve the flatness of the bands. It comes out, that our results hold in undoped TBG under Coulomb interaction, and can be used to extract the value of the MA at which the Gilbert damping correction drops.
Away from the neutrality, the bands are substantially distorted by the Coulomb interaction

;
Guinea21
;
Guinea22
and our results should be taken with a grain of salt since they account for filling
\nu

Besides interactions, strain is found to be a key parameter in the emergence of flat bands in TBG

Marwa

Experimental realization. –

Our proposed setup consists of an interface between a FI and a fully hBN encapsulated TBG/
\mathrm{WSe_{2}}
heterostructure (Fig.
1
).
The hBN layer acts as a tunnel barrier which prevents the diffusion of the FI atoms into the graphene layer
SP-Gr

On the other hand, the encapsulation provides a clean interface and prevents the graphene degradation

It should be stressed that the hBN encapsulated TBG/

Furthermore, the spin transport through a clean interface between a FI and 2D material has been experimentally achieved

;
SP-TMD

or covered by a thin layer of an oxide insulator (as MgO)

Our proposed technique to measure the MA can, then, be implemented experimentally with a clean interface and at room temperature.
Moreover, an

Conclusion. –
To conclude, we have proposed an experiment to probe the flat bands of TBG and to measure its MA accurately. The experiment is based on a spin pumping measurement through a junction separating a FI and a TBG adjacent to a monolayer of
\mathrm{WSe_{2}}
.
We first derived the continuum model of TBG with SOC, which constitutes a first step to develop an analytical understanding of the emergence of a stable superconducting state at small twist angles observed in TBG in proximity to
\mathrm{WSe_{2}}

Alex
.
We then determined analytically the Gilbert damping correction
\delta\alpha_{G}
induced by the presence of the TBG/
\mathrm{WSe_{2}}
heterostructure.
Our results show that the twist angle dependence of
\delta\alpha_{G}
exhibits a drop at the MA with a temperature-dependent fine structure. This feature provides an accurate determination of the MA and an estimation of the SOC induced in TBG by its proximity to the TMD layer. Our proposed set-up can be readily implemented regarding the state-of-the art of the experimental realizations of SP in 2D materials and TBG-based heterostructure.
Our work opens the gate to a twist tunable spintronics in twisted layered heterostructures.

IV. Magnon Green function and Gilbert damping

S.4.1

Interfacial exchange coupling between a ferro-
magnetic insulator (FI) and a TBG

We consider the Hamiltonian of the ferromagnetic insulator (FI) in the independent magnon approximation as

\displaystyle H_{\rm FI}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\hbar\omega_{\bm{k}}b_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}b_{\bm{k}},

(S.41)

where
\hbar\omega_{\bm{k}}\simeq{\cal D}|{\bm{k}}|^{2}+\hbar\gamma h_{\rm dc}
is a dispersion of magnons,
{\cal D}
is a spin stiffness,
\gamma
is the gyromagnetic ratio,
h_{\rm dc}
is a static magnetic field.
In the spin pumping setup, only the static part associated to
\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{0}
is relevant.
Considering only the uniform spin precession, the Hamiltonian of the FI can be simply written as

\displaystyle H_{\rm FI}=\hbar\omega_{\bm{0}}b_{\bm{0}}^{\dagger}b_{\bm{0}},

(S.42)

where
b_{\bm{q}}
is the Fourier transformation of the site representation
b_{i}
defined as

\displaystyle b_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\rm FI}}}\sum_{\bm{q}}e^{i{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{i}}b_{\bm{q}}\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\rm FI}}}b_{\bm{0}},

(S.43)

\displaystyle b_{i}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\rm FI}}}\sum_{\bm{q}}e^{-i{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{i}}b^{\dagger}_{\bm{q}}\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\rm FI}}}b^{\dagger}_{\bm{0}},

(S.44)

where
N_{\rm FI}
is the number of unit cells in the FI.

We consider the (retarded) magnon Green function as

\displaystyle G^{R}({\bm{q}},\omega)

\displaystyle=\int dt\,G^{R}({\bm{q}},t)e^{i\omega t},

(S.45)

\displaystyle G^{R}({\bm{q}},t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\theta(t)\langle[S^{+}_{\bm{q}}(t),S^{-}_{\bm{q}}(0)]\rangle=-\frac{2iS_{0}}{\hbar}\theta(t)\langle[b_{\bm{q}}(t),b_{\bm{q}}^{\dagger}(0)]\rangle.

(S.46)

In the absence of the junction, the magnon Green function is

\displaystyle G_{0}^{R}({\bm{q}},\omega)=\frac{2S_{0}/\hbar}{\omega-\omega_{\bm{q}}+i\delta}.

(S.47)

We introduce spin relaxation of the bulk FI phenomenologically as

\displaystyle G_{0}^{R}({\bm{q}},\omega)=\frac{2S_{0}/\hbar}{\omega-\omega_{\bm{q}}+i\alpha_{\rm G}\omega},

(S.48)

where
\alpha_{\rm G}
is a dimensionless strength of the Gilbert damping, which is of order of
10^{-4}
–
10^{-3}
.
We note that a line shape of the ferromagnetic resonance is proportional to
{\rm Im}\,G_{0}^{R}({\bm{q}}={\bm{0}},\omega)

Funato
.

In the presence of the interfacial coupling and for a uniform spin precession, the magnon Green function is given by the Dyson equation as

\displaystyle G_{0}^{R}({\bm{q=0}},\omega)=\frac{2S_{0}/\hbar}{\omega-\omega_{\bm{q}=0}+i\alpha_{\rm G}\omega-(2S_{0}/\hbar)\Sigma^{R}(\bm{q=0},\omega)},

(S.49)

where
\Sigma^{R}(\omega)
is the self-energy.
Although the real part of
\Sigma^{R}(\omega)
is related to the shift of the ferromagnetic resonance, we neglect it for simplicity.
Then, the magnon Green function is rewritten as

\displaystyle G_{0}^{R}({\bm{0}},\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{2S_{0}/\hbar}{\omega-\omega_{\bm{q}=0}+i(\alpha_{\rm G}+\delta\alpha_{\rm G})\omega},

(S.50)

\displaystyle\delta\alpha_{\rm G}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2S_{0}}{\hbar\omega}\rm{Im}\Sigma^{R}(\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{0},\omega).

(S.51)

We note that
\delta\alpha_{\rm G}(\omega)
depends on
\omega
in general.
However, since the ferromagnetic resonance peak is sharp enough (
\alpha_{\rm G}+\delta\alpha_{\rm G}\ll 1
), we can replace
\omega
with
\omega_{\rm 0}=\Omega
(the peak position of the ferromagnetic resonance):

\displaystyle\delta\alpha_{\rm G}

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{2S_{0}}{\hbar\Omega}\Sigma^{R}(\bm{q=0},\Omega).

(S.52)

The Hamiltonian of the interfacial coupling is given as

\displaystyle H_{\rm int}=\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}T_{ij}(S^{+}_{i}s^{-}_{j}+{\rm h.c.}).

(S.53)

Here,
S^{\pm}_{i}
is a spin ladder operator of the FI and is described by magnon annihilation/creation operators (
b_{i}
and
b_{i}^{\dagger}
) as

\displaystyle S^{+}_{i}=\sqrt{2S_{0}}b_{i},\hskip 11.74988ptS^{-}_{i}=\sqrt{2S_{0}}b_{i}^{\dagger},

(S.54)

where
S_{0}
is an amplitude of the localized spin in the FI.

s^{\pm}_{j}
is a spin ladder operator of electrons in two-dimensional electron systems (twisted bilayer graphene) and is described by the electron annihilation/creation operators (
c_{j\sigma}
and
c_{j\sigma}^{\dagger}
) as

\displaystyle s^{+}_{j}=c_{j\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{j\downarrow},\hskip 11.74988pts^{-}_{j}=c_{j\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{j\uparrow}.

(S.55)

We define the Fourier transformation as

\displaystyle c_{j\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\bm{k}}e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}}c_{{\bm{k}}\sigma},

(S.56)

\displaystyle c^{\dagger}_{j\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\bm{k}}e^{-i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}}c^{\dagger}_{{\bm{k}}\sigma},

(S.57)

where
N
is the number of unit cells and
{\bm{r}}_{j}
is the position of the site
j
in TBG.
Then, we obtain

\displaystyle s^{+}_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}e^{-i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}+i{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}}c_{{\bm{k}}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\downarrow},

(S.58)

\displaystyle s^{-}_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}e^{-i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}+i{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}}c_{{\bm{k}}\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\uparrow}.

(S.59)

We define the Fourier transformation of
s^{\pm}_{j}
as

\displaystyle s^{+}_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}e^{i{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}}s^{+}_{\bm{q}},

(S.60)

\displaystyle s^{-}_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}e^{i{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}}s^{-}_{\bm{q}}.

(S.61)

From
s^{-}_{j}=(s^{+}_{j})^{\dagger}
, we obtain the relation
s^{-}_{\bm{q}}=(s^{+}_{-{\bm{q}}})^{\dagger}
.
The inverse Fourier transformation is given as

\displaystyle s^{+}_{\bm{q}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}e^{-i{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}}s^{+}_{j},

(S.62)

\displaystyle s^{-}_{\bm{q}}

\displaystyle=(s^{+}_{-{\bm{q}}})=\sum_{j}e^{-i{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}}s^{-}_{j}.

(S.63)

Especially for
{\bm{q}}={\bm{0}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle s^{+}_{\bm{0}}=\sum_{j}s^{+}_{j},\hskip 11.74988pts^{-}_{\bm{0}}=\sum_{j}s^{-}_{j},

(S.64)

Using Eqs. (
S.56
) and (
S.57
), we obtain

\displaystyle s^{+}_{\bm{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j}e^{-i{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}}\sum_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}e^{-i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}+i{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}}c_{{\bm{k}}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\downarrow}=\sum_{{\bm{k}}}c_{{\bm{k}}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}\downarrow},

(S.65)

\displaystyle s^{-}_{\bm{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j}e^{-i{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}}\sum_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}e^{-i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}+i{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{j}}c_{{\bm{k}}\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}\uparrow}=\sum_{{\bm{k}}}c_{{\bm{k}}\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}\uparrow}.

(S.66)

For a clean interface, we can set
T_{ij}=T
.
Then, using Eqs. (
S.43
) and (
S.44
), the Hamiltonian of the interface is written as

\displaystyle H_{\rm int}

\displaystyle=\frac{T\sqrt{2S_{0}}}{\sqrt{N_{\rm FI}}}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}(b_{\bm{0}}s_{j}^{-}+b_{\bm{0}}^{\dagger}s_{j}^{+})\simeq\frac{T\sqrt{2S_{0}}}{\sqrt{N_{\rm FI}}}\left[b_{\bm{0}}\Bigl(\sum_{j}s_{j}^{-}\Bigr)+b_{\bm{0}}^{\dagger}\Bigl(\sum_{j}s_{j}^{+}\Bigr)\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2S_{0}}b_{\bm{0}}\tilde{s}^{-}+\sqrt{2S_{0}}b_{\bm{0}}\tilde{s}^{+},

(S.67)

where
\tilde{s}^{\pm}
is defined as

\tilde{s}^{\pm}=(T/\sqrt{N_{\rm FI}})s_{\bm{0}}^{\pm}
.

By the second-order perturbation, the self-energy of the magnon at
{\bm{q}}={\bm{0}}
is calculated as

\displaystyle\Sigma^{R}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int dt\,\Sigma^{R}(t)e^{i\omega t},

(S.68)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{R}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\theta(t)\langle[\tilde{s}^{+}(t),\tilde{s}^{-}(0)]\rangle.

(S.69)

The self-energy can be related to a retarded component of the dynamic spin susceptibility
per unit cell
as

\displaystyle\Sigma^{R}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{T^{2}N}{N_{\rm FI}}\chi({\bm{0}},\omega),

(S.70)

\displaystyle\chi({\bm{q}},\omega)

\displaystyle=\int dt\,\chi({\bm{q}},t)e^{i\omega t},

(S.71)

\displaystyle\chi({\bm{q}},t)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{N\hbar}\theta(t)\langle[s_{-{\bm{q}}}^{+}(t),s_{\bm{q}}^{-}(0)]\rangle.

(S.72)

\chi({\bm{q}},t)
is calculated for one-band of TBG without spin-orbit interaction as

\displaystyle\chi({\bm{q}},t)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{f(\xi_{\bm{k}})-f(\xi_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}})}{\hbar\omega+i\delta+\xi_{{\bm{k}}+{\bm{q}}}-\xi_{\bm{k}}},

(S.73)

where
\xi_{\bm{k}}=\epsilon_{\bm{k}}-\mu
,
\epsilon_{\bm{k}}
is a dispersion of electrons,
\mu
is a chemical potential.
This is just a Lindhard function.
We note that
\chi({\bm{q}},t)
is independent of the system size (area).
For systems with spin-orbit interaction, we have to extend the Lindhard function into the spin-dependent one.

Then, the enhancement of the Gilbert damping is written as

\displaystyle\delta\alpha_{\rm G}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2S_{0}}{\hbar\Omega}\,{\rm Im}\Sigma^{R}(\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{0},\Omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{2S_{0}T^{2}N}{N_{\rm FI}\hbar\omega_{\bm{0}}}\chi(\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{0},\Omega).

(S.74)

We note that the number of the unit cell of twisted bilayer graphene is written as
N=S/A
where
S
is a area of the junction and
A
is an area of a unit cell of twisted bilayer graphene.
We also note that the number of the unit cell of the FI is written as
N_{\rm FI}=Sd/a^{3}
where
d
is a thickness of the FI,
a
is a lattice constant of the FI.
Using these parameters, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta\alpha_{\rm G}

\displaystyle=\frac{2S_{0}T^{2}a^{3}}{Ad\hbar\omega_{\bm{0}}}\chi(\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{0},\Omega).

(S.75)

We note that
\delta\alpha_{\rm G}
is proportional to
1/d
in consistent with experimental results.

S.4.3

Magnon self-energy

In the second order perturbation with respect to the interfacial exchange interaction
T_{\mathbf{q}}
, the interfacial self-energy is given by
Yama

\displaystyle\Sigma(\mathbf{q},i\omega_{n})=\frac{|T_{\mathbf{q}}|^{2}}{4\beta}\sum_{\mathbf{k},i\omega_{m}}\text{Tr}\left[\sigma_{s}^{x^{\prime},-}\;\hat{g}(\mathbf{k},i\omega_{m})\sigma_{s}^{x^{\prime},+}\;\hat{g}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},i\omega_{m}+i\omega_{n})\right]

(S.78)

where
\hat{g}(\mathbf{k},i\omega_{m})
is the electronic Green function given by Eq.
S.22
.

The trace term is of the form:

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left[\mathbf{a}^{\ast}\cdot\mathbf{\sigma}_{s}\left(\hat{g}_{0}+\hat{\mathbf{g}}\cdot\mathbf{\sigma}_{s}\right)\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{\sigma}_{s}\left(\hat{g}^{\prime}_{0}+\hat{\mathbf{g}}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{\sigma}_{s}\right)\right]

(S.79)

where the vector
\mathbf{a}=\left(-\sin\Phi_{m}+i\sin\theta_{m}\cos\Phi_{m},\cos\Phi_{m}+i\sin\theta_{m}\sin\Phi_{m},i\cos\theta_{m}\right)
is written in the laboratory frame
(x,y,z)
.

We set
\hat{g}=\hat{g}(\mathbf{k},i\omega_{m})
and
\hat{g}^{\prime}=\hat{g}(\mathbf{k},i\omega_{m}+i\omega_{n})
.
Taking into account the operator character of
\hat{g}
one could use the identity

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{\sigma}_{s}\right)\left(\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{\sigma}_{s}\right)=\left(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}\right)\mathbb{I}+i\left(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}\right)\cdot\mathbf{\sigma}_{s}

(S.80)

Given the expressions of
\hat{g}
and
\hat{g}^{\prime}
in Eq.
S.23
, the trace term (Eq.
S.79
) reduces to:

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left[\mathbf{a}^{\ast}\cdot\mathbf{\sigma}_{s}\left(\hat{g}_{0}+\hat{\mathbf{g}}\cdot\mathbf{\sigma}_{s}\right)\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{\sigma}_{s}\left(\hat{g}^{\prime}_{0}+\hat{\mathbf{g}}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{\sigma}_{s}\right)\right]=\sum_{i=0,1,2}F_{i}(\mathbf{k},i\omega_{m},i\omega_{n})

(S.81)

where

\displaystyle F_{0}(\mathbf{k},i\omega_{m},i\omega_{n})=4\left(A_{01}A^{\prime}_{01}+A_{0z}A^{\prime}_{0z}+C_{0x}C^{\prime}_{0x}+C_{0y}C^{\prime}_{0y}\right)

\displaystyle F_{1}(\mathbf{k},i\omega_{m},i\omega_{n})=-2\left\{\cos\theta_{m}\cos\Phi_{m}\right.

\displaystyle\left(A_{01}B^{\prime}_{x1}-B_{x1}A^{\prime}_{01}+A_{0z}B^{\prime}_{xz}-A^{\prime}_{0z}B_{xz}+C_{0x}D^{\prime}_{xx}-C^{\prime}_{0x}D_{xx}+C_{0y}D^{\prime}_{xy}-C^{\prime}_{0y}D_{xy}\right)

\displaystyle+\cos\theta_{m}\sin\Phi_{m}\left(A_{01}B^{\prime}_{y1}-B_{y1}A^{\prime}_{01}+A_{0z}B^{\prime}_{yz}-A^{\prime}_{0z}B_{yz}+C_{0x}D^{\prime}_{yx}-C^{\prime}_{0x}D_{yx}+C_{0y}D^{\prime}_{yy}-C^{\prime}_{0y}D_{yy}\right)

\displaystyle\left.-\sin\theta_{m}\left(A_{01}A^{\prime}_{z1}-A^{\prime}_{01}A_{z1}+A_{0z}A^{\prime}_{zz}-A^{\prime}_{0z}A_{zz}+C_{0x}C^{\prime}_{zx}-C^{\prime}_{0x}C_{zx}+C_{0y}C^{\prime}_{zy}-C^{\prime}_{zy}C_{0y}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle F_{2}(\mathbf{k},i\omega_{m},i\omega_{n})=-2\cos^{2}\theta_{m}\cos^{2}\Phi_{m}\left(B_{x1}B^{\prime}_{x1}+B_{xz}B^{\prime}_{xz}+D_{xx}D^{\prime}_{xx}+D_{xy}D^{\prime}_{xy}\right)

\displaystyle-2\cos^{2}\theta_{m}\sin^{2}\Phi_{m}\left(B_{y1}B^{\prime}_{y1}+B_{yz}B^{\prime}_{yz}+D_{yx}D^{\prime}_{yx}+D_{yy}D^{\prime}_{yy}\right)

\displaystyle-2\sin^{2}\theta_{m}\left(A_{z1}A^{\prime}_{z1}+A_{zz}A^{\prime}_{zz}+C_{zx}C^{\prime}_{zx}+C_{zy}C^{\prime}_{zy}\right)

\displaystyle-\cos^{2}\theta_{m}\sin 2\Phi_{m}\left(B_{x1}B^{\prime}_{y1}+B_{xz}B^{\prime}_{yz}+D_{xx}D^{\prime}_{yx}+D_{xy}D^{\prime}_{yy}+B^{\prime}_{x1}B_{y1}+B^{\prime}_{xz}B_{yz}+D^{\prime}_{xx}D_{yx}+D^{\prime}_{xy}D_{yy}\right)

\displaystyle+\cos\Phi_{m}\sin 2\theta_{m}\left(B_{x1}A^{\prime}_{z1}+B_{xz}A^{\prime}_{zz}+D_{xx}C^{\prime}_{zx}+D_{xy}C^{\prime}_{zy}B^{\prime}_{x1}A_{z1}+B^{\prime}_{xz}A_{zz}+D^{\prime}_{xx}C_{zx}+D^{\prime}_{xy}C_{zy}\right)

\displaystyle+\sin\Phi_{m}\sin 2\theta_{m}\left(B_{y1}A^{\prime}_{z1}+B_{yz}A^{\prime}_{zz}+D_{yx}C^{\prime}_{zx}+D_{yy}C^{\prime}_{zy}B^{\prime}_{y1}A_{z1}+B^{\prime}_{yz}A_{zz}+D^{\prime}_{yx}C_{zx}+D^{\prime}_{yy}C_{zy}\right)

(S.82)

The terms with a prime are expressed in terms of
i\omega_{n}^{\prime}=i\omega_{n}+i\omega_{m}
.

Regarding the
\mathbf{k}
dependence of the

and

operators (Eqs.
S.25

S.40

According to Eq.

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}}F(\mathbf{k})\sum_{\omega_{m}}\frac{1}{i\hbar\omega_{m}-E_{i}}\frac{1}{i\hbar\omega_{m}^{\prime}-E_{j}},

(S.83)

where

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{i\hbar\omega_{n}-(E_{j}-E_{i})}\frac{1}{k_{B}T}\int_{c}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}h(z)f(z)

S.4.4

Gilbert damping correction

For a uniform spin precession, the Gilbert damping correction
\delta\alpha_{G}
, at the FMR frequency
\Omega
, can be expressed as
Yama

\displaystyle\delta\alpha_{G}=-\frac{2S_{0}}{\hbar\Omega}\mathrm{Im}\Sigma(\mathbf{q=0},\Omega)

(S.87)

The imaginary part of the self-energy is of the form
\mathrm{Im}\Sigma(\mathbf{q=0},\Omega)=\frac{T_{0}^{2}}{\hbar\Omega}\tilde{\Sigma}(\mathbf{q=0},\Omega)

\tilde{\Sigma}(\mathbf{q=0},\Omega)

Introducing the average SOC
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{I}+\lambda_{R})

can be written as

\displaystyle\delta\alpha_{G}=-\alpha_{G}^{0}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\hbar\Omega}\right)^{2}\tilde{\Sigma}(\mathbf{q=0},\Omega)

where
\alpha_{G}^{0}=2S_{0}\frac{T_{0}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}

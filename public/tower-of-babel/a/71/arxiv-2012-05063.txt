Infinitesimal extension of pluricanonical forms
Junyan CAO, Mihai PAUN

Abstract




1.
Introduction

Let
p:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic family of smooth,
n
-dimensional compact manifolds whose central fiber -denoted by
X
- is assumed to be Kähler. We denote by
\displaystyle K_{\mathcal{X}}
the canonical bundle of
\mathcal{X}
and let
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{X}
be an arbitrary line bundle.

We are interested in the extension properties of global sections of the adjoint bundle
K_{\mathcal{X}}+\mathcal{L}
defined over the
k^{\rm th}
infinitesimal neighborhood of the central fiber of
p
, where
k\geqslant 1
is a positive integer.
In other words, we consider the sheaves

(1.0.1)

\mathcal{F}_{k}:=\left(K_{\mathcal{X}}+\mathcal{L}\right)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}/t^{k+1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}},

and the corresponding spaces of sections
H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F}_{k})
.

In this article we are establishing sufficient criteria for a given
\displaystyle s\in H^{0}\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F}_{k}\right)
to be in the image of the map induced by the projection

(1.0.2)

\pi_{k}:\mathcal{F}_{k+1}\to\mathcal{F}_{k}.

We are particularly interested in the case

\mathcal{L}=(m-1)K_{\mathcal{X}}
where
m\geqslant 1
is a positive integer,
so we state next our main result in this setting. In order to do so we have to introduce further notations. Let
s
be a holomorphic section of
\mathcal{F}_{k}
; we denote by

h_{L}
the metric on the bundle
L:=(m-1)K_{X}
induced by the restriction of
s
to the central fiber
X
.

The following statement is the main result of the first part of our paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s
be a section of the sheaf (
1.0.1
) with
\mathcal{L}=(m-1)K_{\mathcal{X}}
, which admits
a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
extension
s_{k}
so that if we write

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}s_{k}=t^{k+1}\Lambda_{k},

the integral

(1.1.1)

\int_{X}\left|\frac{\Lambda_{k}}{dt}\right|^{2}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\varphi_{L}}dV<\infty

converges for any positive
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exists a section
\widehat{s}
of
\mathcal{F}_{k+1}
such that
s=\pi_{k}(\widehat{s})
.

The notations in (
1.1.1
) are as follows: we measure the
(n,1)
-form
\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda_{k}}{dt}\Big|_{X}
with respect to an arbitrary Kähler metric on
X
and the metric
e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\varphi_{L}-\varepsilon\phi_{L}}
where
e^{-\phi_{L}}
is a smooth metric on
L
and
e^{-\varphi_{L}}=h_{L}
is the singular metric induced by
s
.

Coming back to the general setting, let
\mathcal{E}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{K}
be a set of line bundles
on
\mathcal{X}
such that

(1.1.2)

(1-r_{0})c_{1}(\mathcal{L})=r_{0}c_{1}(K_{\mathcal{X}})+\sum_{i=1}^{K}r_{i}c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i}),

where
0\leqslant r_{0}<1
and
r_{j}\geqslant 0
for
j=2,\dots,K
are rational numbers.
In (
1.1.2
) we denote by
c_{1}(\mathcal{E})
the first Chern class of
\mathcal{E}
.
Let
s
be a section of
\mathcal{F}_{k}
and and for each
i=1,\dots K
let
\sigma_{i}
be a section of the sheaf
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}/t^{k+2}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
(notice that this is one order higher than
s
). We denote by
h_{L}
the metric induced on the bundle
L:=\mathcal{L}|_{X}
by the set of holomorphic sections
(s,\sigma_{i})
restricted to the central fiber
–notice that this makes sense thanks to (
1.1.2
).

Then we have the following version of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\displaystyle(s,\sigma_{i})_{i=1,\dots,K}
be a family of sections of
\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}_{k},\mathcal{E}_{i})_{i=1,\dots,K}
respectively as above. We assume that
s
admits a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
extension
s_{k}
such that if we write

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}s_{k}=t^{k+1}\Lambda_{k}

then

(1.2.1)

\int_{X}\left|\frac{\Lambda_{k}}{dt}\right|^{2}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\varphi_{L}}dV<\infty

for any positive
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a section
\widehat{s}
of
\mathcal{F}_{k+1}
such that
s=\pi_{k}(\widehat{s})
.

A very interesting result in connection to Theorem
1.2
was established by Cao, Demailly and Matsumura in
[
4
]
, as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
4
]
Consider
p:{\mathcal{X}}\to\Delta
a Kähler family,
(\mathcal{L},h_{\mathcal{L}})
a line bundle on
\mathcal{X}

and let
u\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F}_{k})
be a holomorphic section such that:

•

We have
\displaystyle i\Theta(\mathcal{L},h_{\mathcal{L}})\geqslant 0
on
\mathcal{X}
.

•

u
admits a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
extension
u_{k}
to
\mathcal{X}
such that
\displaystyle\overline{\partial}u_{k}=t^{k+1}\Lambda_{k}
together with

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left|{\Lambda_{k}}\right|^{2}e^{-\varphi_{L}}dV<\infty

Then
u
extends to
\mathcal{X}
(no estimates available).

We remark that a-priori in Theorem
1.1
we do not have any semi-positively curved metric on the bundle
\mathcal{L}=(m-1)K_{\mathcal{X}}
.
Moreover, the
L^{2}
condition in Theorem
1.3
shows that our hypothesis (
1.1.1
) is natural, at least from the point of view of extension theorems.

Theorem
1.2
is equally motivated by an important conjecture in Kähler geometry that we next recall. After Y.-T. Siu’s ”invariance of plurigenera” articles
[
24
,
25
]
(see also
[
22
]
) concerning the extension of
s
in case of a
projective family

\mathcal{X}
,
the following very important problem is still open (despite of the
avalanche
of articles and crucial achievements that followed Siu’s work).

Conjecture 1.4
.

[
25
]

Let
p:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be a family of smooth,
n
-dimensional compact manifolds whose central fiber is denoted by
X
. We assume that the total space
\mathcal{X}
admits a Kähler metric. Then any holomorphic pluricanonical section defined on
X
extends holomorphically to
\mathcal{X}
.

Among the articles dedicated to Conjecture
1.4
we first mention here
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
, due to M. Levine. A direct consequence of Theorem
1.1
is the following statement, which represents a more general version of the results in
[
15
,
16
]
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
p:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be a smooth holomorphic family whose central fiber
X
is Kähler, and let
\displaystyle s\in H^{0}(X,mK_{X})

be a pluricanonical section defined on
X
. We assume that the set of zeros of the
ideal

\mathfrak{I}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{I}\left((1-\varepsilon)\frac{m-1}{m}\Sigma\right)

is discrete,
where
\Sigma
is the divisor corresponding to
s
.
Then any section
\tau\in H^{0}(X,mK_{X})
admits a holomorphic extension to a neighborhood of the center fiber
X
.
If moreover the total space
\mathcal{X}
is Kähler, then any section
\tau\in H^{0}(X,mK_{X})
admits a holomorphic extension to
\mathcal{X}
.

Furthermore we obtain a new proof of the next statement, which is the main result in
[
15
]
(cf. Section 6). Our main objective in doing that is to understand better the set-up in which the hypothesis (
1.2.1
) is verified.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
15
]

Let
\displaystyle s\in H^{0}(X,mK_{X})
be a pluricanonical section defined on the
central fiber. We assume that the set of zeroes
(s=0)\subset X
of
s
is non-singular.
Then
s
admits an extension to
\mathcal{X}
.

We highlight next some parts of the proof of Theorem
1.1
which seem interesting to us. By hypothesis the section
s
admits a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
extension
s_{k}
such that

(1.6.1)

\bar{\partial}s_{k}=t^{k+1}\Lambda_{k}

for some form
\Lambda_{k}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{n+1,1}(\mathcal{X},(m-1)K_{\mathcal{X}})
whose restriction to
X
is
\bar{\partial}
-closed. The first step is to show that there exist forms
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}

which are smooth in the complement of the support of
(s=0)\subset X
and such that

(1.6.2)

\frac{\Lambda_{k}}{dt}\Big|_{X}=\bar{\partial}\alpha_{k}+D^{\prime}(\beta_{k})

holds pointwise in the complement of
s=0
, where
D^{\prime}
is the
(1,0)
-part of the Chern connection on
L:=(m-1)K_{X}

induced by the given section
\displaystyle s=s_{k}|_{X}
.

Note that the equality (
1.6.2
) is true in general, i.e. without any
L^{2}
assumption. But the problem is that the
L
-valued forms
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
have singularities of type
\displaystyle\frac{\Phi}{s^{k+1}}
, where
\Phi
is smooth. We therefore find ourselves in a rather strange situation, in which the LHS of (
1.6.2
) is non-singular but the primitives appearing in
the RHS are meromorphic. Of course, this is -so to speak- the price to pay in order to have an intrinsic decomposition as in (
1.6.2
).

Anyway, in order to prove Theorem
1.1
, it would be sufficient to establish the following result which we state separately since we find it interesting in itself.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\gamma
be a
\bar{\partial}
-closed smooth
(n,1)
-form on a compact Kähler manifold
X
with values in
L
such that the following hold.

(a)

The bundle
L
is endowed with a singular metric
h_{L}=e^{-\varphi_{L}}
such that

\varphi_{L}=\sum_{i}q_{i}\log|f_{i}|^{2}+C^{\infty},

for some rational coefficients
q_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
, where
f_{i}
are holomorphic functions and such that the curvature current
i\Theta_{h_{L}}
is supported in a proper subvariety of
X
.

(b)

We suppose that

\int_{X}|\gamma|^{2}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\varphi_{L}}<+\infty

for every
\varepsilon>0
.

(c)

Set
Y:=\{h_{L}=+\infty\}
.
There exists a
(n-1,1)
-form
\beta
and a
(n,0)
-form
\alpha
with poles along
Y
such that

\gamma=\bar{\partial}\alpha+D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}(\beta)

on
X\setminus Y
, where
D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}
is induced by the singular metric
h_{L}
on
L
.

Then
\gamma
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact.

The assumption (c) above means that the coefficients of the two forms can be locally written as

\displaystyle\frac{g}{\prod f_{i}^{N}}
, where
g
is smooth. For the proof of Theorem
1.7
, an important point is that after the log-resolution of
\big(X,\mathrm{Div}(s)\big)
combined with a few other reductions the singularities of
\alpha
and
\beta
can be improved significantly.

The last step of the proof consists in showing that Theorem
1.7
is a consequence of the following general vanishing theorem, established in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1.8
.

[
5
]

Let
X
be a
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold and let
E
be a snc divisor on
X
,
s_{E}
be the canonical section of
E
. Let
(L,h_{L})
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
such that

i\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)=\sum q_{i}[Y_{i}]+\theta_{L},

where
q_{i}\in]0,1[\cap\mathbb{Q}
,
E+\sum Y_{i}
is snc, and the form
\theta_{L}
is smooth, semi-positive.
Let
\lambda
be a
\bar{\partial}
-closed smooth
(n,q)
-form with value in
L+E
. If there exists
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
, two
L
-valued
(n-1,q)
-form and
(n-1,q-1)
-form with log poles along
E+\sum Y_{i}
respectively, such that

(1.8.1)

\frac{\lambda}{s_{E}}=D^{\prime}_{h_{L}}\beta_{1}+\theta_{L}\wedge\beta_{2}\qquad\text{ on }X\setminus(E+\sum Y_{i}),

then the form
\lambda
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact, i.e,
the class
[\lambda]=0\in H^{q}(X,K_{X}+L+E)
.

In the second part of our article we are concerned more specifically with (extensions of) sections of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
for
i=0,1
. The majority of our results can be seen as applications of Theorem
1.1
, as follows.

\bullet
In subsection
4.2
, we provide an alternative argument for Theorem
1.6
. The main
point is to show that if the divisor
s=0
is smooth, then for each order
k
we can construct a particular vector field

\Xi
, so that the resulting forms
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
are automatically smooth (hence in particular
L^{2}
, given that the multiplier ideal sheaf corresponding to
\frac{m-1}{m}\mathrm{Div}(s)
is trivial). Then we can extend
s
over infinitesimal nbd’s of any order and the conclusion follows.

\bullet
Even if the hypothesis (
1.1.1
) in statement
1.1
fits perfectly in the
L^{2}
-landscape, it
is somehow unnatural in the context of Siu’s conjecture.
Nevertheless in subsection
4.3
, we formulate
a sufficient condition for (
1.1.1
) to hold, as follows. Consider a pluricanonical section
s
on the central fiber of
p:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{D}
, such that the next requirement is satisfied.

Hypothesis 1.9
.

There exists a divisor
\Sigma
on
\mathcal{X}
such that we have.

(i)

The divisor
\Sigma+X
is snc.

(ii)

The support of
\mathrm{Div}(s)
is contained in the restriction
\displaystyle\Sigma|_{X}
.

In Section 5, among many other results we show that the following holds true.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
s\in H^{0}(X,mK_{X})
be a pluricanonical section such that Hypothesis
1.9
is satisfied. Then for each
k\geqslant 0

the section
s
admits a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
extension
s_{k}
to
\mathcal{X}
, such that (
1.1.1
) holds true. In particular,
s
admits a holomorphic extension to
\mathcal{X}
.

Remark 1.11
.

Given a pluricanonical section
s
defined on the central fiber
X
of the family
p
, we can always find a birational map
\pi:\widehat{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathcal{X}

such that the support of the inverse image of the set
(s=0)
is a divisor, transverse to the proper transform
\overline{X}
of
X
. The map
\widehat{p}:=p\circ\pi:\widehat{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathbb{D}

is now singular, but the support of the central fiber is snc. The said support is the union of
\overline{X}
with
\pi
-exceptional divisors,
and the inverse image of
s
becomes a pluricanonical section of
\overline{X}
, whose set of zeros is contained in a divisor defined globally.
In other words, the context is vaguely similar to that in Hypothesis
1.9
, but the new map
\widehat{p}
is singular, so the methods used in proving Theorem
1.10
do not apply (at least, not directly).

\bullet
In subsection
4.4
, we discuss the second order extension. The problem extending sections of the sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is already very interesting, since we have to deal with an unreduced space. The partial result we obtain in this direction is the following.

Let

be a pluricanonical section defined on the

of a family

on the total space

such that

(1.12.1)

where

(s=0)
.
Then there exists a section

such that

\displaystyle s_{2}|_{X}=s
and

(1.12.2)

\bar{\partial}s_{2}=t^{3}\Lambda_{2},

where
\Lambda_{2}
belongs to the space
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0,1}(\mathcal{X},mK_{\mathcal{X}})
.

. The general setup is as follows.
Let
(L,h_{L})
be a Hermitian line bundle on

Let

be an

is

.

This paper is organized as follows.

2.
First order differential operators

In this subsection we will recall a few facts from differential geometry of line
bundles. We take this opportunity to fix some notations as well.

2.1.
Connection induced by a smooth section

Notation 2.1
.

In the setting of our article, let
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{X}
be a holomorphic line
bundle endowed with a connection whose
(0,1)
-component is given by the

\bar{\partial}
operator,

(2.1.1)

\nabla=D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}+\bar{\partial}.

Since no confusion is likely, we will use the same symbol to denote the
induced operator on the space of smooth,
\mathcal{L}
-valued
(p,q)
-forms,

(2.1.2)

D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{X},\Omega_{\mathcal{X}}^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\right)\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{X},\Omega_{\mathcal{X}}^{p+1,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\right).

Construction 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{X}
be a holomorphic line bundle, and let

\widetilde{s}
be a smooth section of
\mathcal{L}
, with vanishing locus
Z\subseteq\mathcal{X}
.
Assume that the open cover
(U_{i})_{i}
trivializes the bundle
\mathcal{L}
and choose
trivialization,
L|_{U_{i}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{U_{i}}
. The section
\widetilde{s}
will
therefore give rise to smooth functions
\widetilde{s}_{i}
on
U_{i}
. Set

U^{\circ}_{i}:=U_{i}\setminus Z
.

Given any index
i
we can now define a differential operator

D^{\prime}_{i}:\mathcal{O}_{U^{\circ}_{i}}\to\Omega^{1}_{U^{\circ}_{i}}
on
U^{\circ}_{i}
as
follows,

(2.2.1)

D^{\prime}_{i}:\mathcal{O}_{U^{\circ}_{i}}\to\Omega^{1}_{U^{\circ}_{i}},\qquad\sigma\mapsto D^{\prime}_{t}(\sigma_{i})dt+\sum_{\alpha}D^{\prime}_{\alpha}(\sigma_{i})dz_{i}^{\alpha}

where

\displaystyle D^{\prime}_{t}(\sigma_{i})

\displaystyle:=\partial_{t}\sigma_{i}-\frac{\partial_{t}\widetilde{s}_{i}}{\widetilde{s}_{i}}\sigma_{i},

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\sigma_{i})

\displaystyle:=\partial_{\alpha}\sigma_{i}-\frac{\partial_{\alpha}\widetilde{s}_{i}}{\widetilde{s}_{i}}\sigma_{i}.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}

\displaystyle:=\partial/\partial t

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\partial/\partial z_{i}^{\alpha}

Using the trivialization chosen above, we can view
D^{\prime}_{i}
as differential
operators

D^{\prime}_{i}:\mathcal{L}|_{U^{\circ}_{i}}\to\mathcal{L}|_{U^{\circ}_{i}}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{U^{\circ}_{i}}
on

U^{\circ}_{i}
. One computes without much pain that these differential operators
glue to give a globally defined operator

D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}

on
\mathcal{X}\setminus Z
. In particular, we obtain a connection

\nabla:=D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}+\bar{\partial}
on
\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{X}\setminus Z}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Setting as in Construction
2.2
. Then, we have the following graded
commutator identity

(2.3.1)

[D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}},\bar{\partial}]=-\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{\partial\widetilde{s}}{\widetilde{s}}\right)\wedge

Proof.

Direct computation that we skip. We note that the symbol
\displaystyle\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{\partial\widetilde{s}}{\widetilde{s}}\right)
which appears in (
2.3.1
)
is a global
(1,1)
form on the complement of the set
\widetilde{s}=0
, as we now explain. The global section
\widetilde{s}
corresponds to local smooth functions denoted by
\widetilde{s}_{i}
such that we have

(2.3.2)

\widetilde{s}_{i}=g_{ij}\widetilde{s}_{j}

on overlapping subsets of
\mathcal{X}
. Then we have

(2.3.3)

\frac{\partial\widetilde{s}_{i}}{s_{i}}=\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{g_{ij}}+\frac{\partial\widetilde{s}_{j}}{s_{j}}

and since
\displaystyle\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{g_{ij}}\right)=0
, we obtain

(2.3.4)

\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{\partial\widetilde{s}_{i}}{s_{i}}\right)=\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{\partial\widetilde{s}_{j}}{s_{j}}\right)

which proves our claim. We note that if
\widetilde{s}
is holomorphic, then
the
(1,1)
-from is simply zero on
\mathcal{X}\setminus Z
.
∎

)

2.2.
Lie derivative and commutation relations

Let

(2.5.1)

s\in H^{0}(X,mK_{X})

be a holomorphic section of the pluricanonical bundle, where
m\geqslant 1
is a positive integer.

Let
\widetilde{s}
be arbitrary smooth extension of the section
s
. As already hinted, we can use the section
\widetilde{s}
in order to
define a connection on the bundle

\mathcal{L}:=(m-1)K_{\mathcal{X}}
restricted to the complement of the set of zeros of

\widetilde{s}
. The local differential operators corresponding to
D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}

are given by

(2.5.2)

D^{\prime}_{t}(\sigma):=\partial_{t}\sigma-\frac{m-1}{m}\frac{\partial_{t}\widetilde{s}_{i}}{\widetilde{s}_{i}}\sigma,\quad D_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\sigma):=\partial_{\alpha}\sigma-\frac{m-1}{m}\frac{\partial_{\alpha}\widetilde{s}_{i}}{\widetilde{s}_{i}}\sigma.

where
\partial_{t}:=\partial/\partial t
and

\partial_{\alpha}:=\partial/\partial z_{i}^{\alpha}

for each
\alpha=1,\dots,n
. In (
2.5.2
) the symbol
\sigma
represents a local section of the bundle
\displaystyle(m-1)K_{\mathcal{X}/D}
. The sum

D^{\prime}_{t}(\sigma)dt+\sum_{\alpha}D_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\sigma)dz_{i}^{\alpha}

corresponds to a global connection of
(1,0)
type on
L:=(m-1)K_{\mathcal{X}}
. If
\widetilde{s}
would have been holomorphic, then this is nothing but the Chern connection; in any case, this is well (i.e. globally) defined. It is clear that we have

(2.5.3)

D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}=0.

We consider next a smooth vector field
\displaystyle\Xi
on the total space
\mathcal{X}

such that
dp(\Xi)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
on
\mathbb{D}
.
It can be written in local coordinates as follows

(2.5.4)

\Xi|_{U_{i}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}v_{i}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}^{\alpha}}

where
v_{i}^{\alpha}
for
i=1,\dots,n
are smooth functions.

Our vector field induces a Lie derivative operator
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}
as follows

(2.5.5)

\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}(\sigma):=D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(\Xi\rfloor\sigma)

where
\sigma
is any
(n+1,q)
-form with values in
L
. The result
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}(\sigma)

is a form of the same type as
\sigma
, and it will play an important role in what follows.

Let
i_{\Xi}
be the operator of degree (-1, 0) given by the contraction with the vector field
\Xi
. Since we have
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}=[D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}},i_{\Xi}]
on
(n+1,q)
-differential forms, the following Jacobi identity

[\bar{\partial},\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}]+[D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}},[i_{\Xi},\bar{\partial}]]+[i_{\Xi},[\bar{\partial},D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}]]=0

holds true, and therefore we obtain the next formula over
(n+1,q)
-forms

(2.5.6)

[\bar{\partial},\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}]=-D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ(\bar{\partial}\Xi)\rfloor-[i_{\Xi},[\bar{\partial},D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}]].

Our next statement is playing an important role in the “algebra” part of the proof of our main results.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\rho
be a
(n-1,1)
-form with values in
L
on
\mathcal{X}
. Then we have the equality

(2.6.1)

\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}\big(D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(dt\wedge\rho)\big)=D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\left(dt\wedge\Big(\Xi\rfloor D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\Xi\rfloor(dt\wedge\rho)\big)\Big)\right)

on
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

The argument is quite clear; we start with the left hand side and we remark that we have

(2.6.2)

D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(dt\wedge\rho)=-dt\wedge D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(\rho)

and so

(2.6.3)

\Xi\rfloor\big(D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(dt\wedge\rho)\big)=-D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(\rho)+dt\wedge\big(\Xi\rfloor D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(\rho)\big)

since the contraction with the vector field
\Xi
is a derivation. The LHS of (
2.6.1
) is therefore equal
to

(2.6.4)

-dt\wedge D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\Xi\rfloor D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(\rho)\big).

For the RHS, we have

(2.6.5)

\Xi\rfloor(dt\wedge\rho)=\rho-dt\wedge(\Xi\rfloor\rho)

so

(2.6.6)

D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\Xi\rfloor(dt\wedge\rho)\big)=D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\rho+dt\wedge D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(\Xi\rfloor\rho).

A contraction with
\Xi
gives

(2.6.7)

\Xi\rfloor\Big(D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\Xi\rfloor(dt\wedge\rho)\big)\Big)\equiv\Xi\rfloor\big(D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\rho\big)+D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(\Xi\rfloor\rho)

modulo a term in
dt\wedge\cdot
, so we have

(2.6.8)

dt\wedge\left(\Xi\rfloor\Big(D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\Xi\rfloor(dt\wedge\rho)\big)\Big)\right)=dt\wedge\Big(\Xi\rfloor\big(D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\rho\big)\Big)+dt\wedge D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(\Xi\rfloor\rho)

and a further derivative of (
2.6.8
) shows that the LHS of (
2.6.1
) equals

(2.6.9)

-dt\wedge D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\Xi\rfloor D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(\rho)\big),

so our statement is proved. In the argument just finished, we have used many times the fact that
D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}=0
.
∎

3.1.
Evaluation of the obstruction form

Let
\displaystyle\bigcup U_{i}
be a Stein cover of
\mathcal{X}
. We consider a section
\displaystyle s\in H^{0}(X,K_{\mathcal{X}}+\mathcal{L}|_{X})
on the central fiber of
p
. We denote by
s_{i}
an arbitrary holomorphic extension of

\displaystyle s|_{U_{i}\cap X}
. Then we have

(3.0.2)

s_{i}dz_{i}\wedge dt\otimes e_{i}=s_{j}dz_{j}\wedge dt\otimes e_{j}+t\Lambda_{ij}dz_{j}\wedge dt\otimes e_{j}.

on overlapping coordinate sets
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
. Hence we interpret
s
as a top form on
\mathcal{X}
with values in
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}
. In (
3.0.2
) the symbol
e_{i}
stands for the local frame of
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}
(which, in our case
is simply
\displaystyle(dz_{i}\wedge dt)^{\otimes(m-1)}

We can reformulate this data as follows:
there exists a smooth section
s_{1}
of the bundle
K_{\mathcal{X}}+\mathcal{L}
such that

(3.0.3)

\bar{\partial}s_{1}=t\Lambda_{1}

on
\mathcal{X}
, and whose restriction to the central fiber is equal to
s
.

Consider next the Lie derivative operator
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}
associated to a vector field
\Xi
such that
\displaystyle dp(\Xi)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
. If we apply
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}
to (
3.0.3
)
on the RHS we obtain

(3.0.4)

\Lambda_{1}+t\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}(\Lambda_{1})

By the commutation relation (
2.5.6
), the LHS of (
3.0.3
) becomes

(3.0.5)

\bar{\partial}\big(\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}(s_{1})\big)+D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\bar{\partial}\Xi\rfloor s_{1}\big)+[i_{\Xi},[\bar{\partial},D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}]]s_{1}

Modulo a factor divisible with
t
, the last term of (
3.0.5
) is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{m-1}{m}\Lambda_{1}
on
X\setminus Z
. Therefore we get

(3.0.6)

\frac{1}{m}\Lambda_{1}\equiv\bar{\partial}\big(\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}(s_{1})\big)+D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\bar{\partial}\Xi\rfloor s_{1}\big)

on
X\setminus Z
,
modulo a form which is divisible by
t
and non-singular on
X\setminus Z
.

Another interesting observation is that the form
\bar{\partial}\Xi\rfloor s_{1}
can be written as follows

(3.0.7)

\bar{\partial}\Xi\rfloor s_{1}=dt\wedge\rho_{1}

given the shape of the vector field
\Xi
, cf. (
2.5.4
). We have therefore obtained the
first step of the next statement.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
s_{k}
be a smooth section of the bundle
K_{\mathcal{X}}+\mathcal{L}
, such that

(3.1.1)

\bar{\partial}s_{k}=t^{k+1}\Lambda_{k}

for some
(n+1,1)
-form
\Lambda_{k}
. We assume that the
connection on
L=(m-1)K_{\mathcal{X}}
is induced by the section
s_{k}
. Then we can find the forms
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}

such that we have

(3.1.2)

c_{k}\Lambda_{k}\equiv\bar{\partial}\alpha_{k}+D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(dt\wedge\beta_{k})\qquad\text{on }X\setminus Z

modulo the ideal generated by
t
. Moreover, the forms
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
are
smooth in the complement of the set
s_{k}=0
and
c_{k}
is a positive constant.

Proof.

This is obtained as follows: we take the Lie derivative in (
3.1.2
) and use the commutation relation (
2.5.6
). The result of this first operation is that we have

(3.1.3)

\frac{k+1}{m}t^{k}\Lambda_{k}\equiv\bar{\partial}\big(\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}(s_{k})\big)+D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\bar{\partial}\Xi\rfloor s_{k}\big)\qquad\text{on }X\setminus Z

modulo the ideal generated by
t^{k+1}
. In order to start the inductive process which will prove our statement, we rewrite (
3.1.3
) as follows

(3.1.4)

\frac{k+1}{m}t^{k}\Lambda_{k}\equiv\bar{\partial}u_{1}+D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(dt\wedge v_{1}\big)\qquad\text{on }X\setminus Z,

modulo
t^{k+1}
. Here we use the notations

(3.1.5)

u_{1}:=\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}(s_{k}),\qquad dt\wedge v_{1}:=\bar{\partial}\Xi\rfloor s_{k}.

We show next that if we apply the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}
to the RHS of (
3.1.5
)
the result is an expression of a similar type. Indeed, we have

(3.1.6)

\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}(\bar{\partial}u_{1})=\bar{\partial}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}(u_{1})\right)+D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\bar{\partial}\Xi\rfloor u_{1}\big)\qquad\text{on }X\setminus Z

modulo the curvature term which is of order

: this is one higher than we have to take into account, so we just drop it.

Also, we have

(3.1.7)

\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}\big(D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(dt\wedge v_{1})\big)=D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\left(dt\wedge\Big(\Xi\rfloor D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\Xi\rfloor(dt\wedge v_{1})\big)\Big)\right)

thanks to Lemma
2.6
, so summing up we get

(3.1.8)

\frac{k(k+1)}{m}t^{k-1}\Lambda_{k}\equiv\bar{\partial}u_{2}+D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(dt\wedge v_{2}\big),

modulo

, where we use the notations

(3.1.9)

u_{2}:=\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}(u_{1}),\qquad dt\wedge v_{2}:=\bar{\partial}\Xi\rfloor u_{1}+dt\wedge\Big(\Xi\rfloor D^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\Xi\rfloor(dt\wedge v_{1})\big)\Big)

Our statement follows by induction on
k
–and moreover we have

(3.1.10)

c_{k}:=\frac{(k+1)!}{m}

as we see by taking successive derivatives of (
3.1.8
).
∎

3.2.
The
L^{2}
hypothesis

Let
\pi:\widehat{X}\to X
be a birational map such that

\pi^{*}\varphi_{L}=\sum m_{i}\log|s_{E_{i}}|+\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}\log|s_{F_{j}}|+C^{\infty},

holds true where the convention is that
m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
q_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
,
s_{E_{i}},s_{F_{j}}
are the canonical sections associated to the divisors
E_{i},F_{j}
, and
\sum E_{i}+\sum F_{j}
is simple normal crossing.
We can write thus

(3.3.1)

\widehat{L}:=\pi^{\star}L=\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}E_{i}+\sum_{j\in J}q_{j}F_{j}+L_{0},

where
L_{0}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle equipped with a smooth metric
h_{0}
such that
i\Theta_{h_{0}}(L_{0})=0
.

We consider the form
\gamma
in (
3.0.1
). Its inverse image
\pi^{\star}\gamma
is a
(n,1)
-form with values in
\widehat{L}
, such that

(3.3.2)

\int_{X}\left|\pi^{\star}\gamma\right|^{2}_{\widehat{\omega}}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\varphi_{\widehat{L}}}dV_{\widehat{\omega}}<+\infty

holds true for every positive
\varepsilon
, where
\widehat{\omega}
is any (non-singular) metric on
\widehat{X}
.

As consequence of (
3.3.2
), we claim that the form

(3.3.3)

\lambda:=\frac{\pi^{\star}\gamma}{\prod_{i\in I}s_{E_{i}}^{m_{i}-1}\prod_{j\in J}s_{F_{j}}^{\lfloor q_{j}\rfloor}}

is smooth -and of course,
\bar{\partial}
-closed. Indeed, let
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
be a coordinate system defined on some open subset
\Omega\subset\widehat{X}
of
\widehat{X}
. We write

\pi^{\star}\gamma|_{\Omega}=\sum a_{\overline{i}}dz\wedge d\overline{z}_{i}

and then (
3.3.2
) is equivalent to the following relations

\int_{\Omega}\left|a_{\overline{i}}\right|^{2}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\varphi_{\widehat{L}}}dV<+\infty

for all indexes
i
, so our claim follows.

The equality

(3.3.4)

\gamma=\bar{\partial}\alpha_{k}+D^{\prime}\beta_{k}

established in Lemma
3.1
becomes

(3.3.5)

\lambda=\bar{\partial}\alpha+D^{\prime}\beta

on
\widehat{X}\setminus(E\cup F)
, where
D^{\prime}
is the
(1,0)
-part of the Chern connection for
singular metric on the line bundle
\mathcal{O}\left(E+\sum(q_{j}-\lfloor q_{j}\rfloor)F_{j}+L_{0}\right)
, and
\alpha,\beta
are some forms with value in
\mathcal{O}\left(E+\sum(q_{j}-\lfloor q_{j}\rfloor)F_{j}+L_{0}\right)
and have some poles along
E+F
.

Thus, by changing the notations we have the following setup.

(1)

\lambda
is a
\bar{\partial}
-closed form of type
(n,1)
and values in
E+L
on a compact Kähler manifold
X
.

(2)

E+F
is a snc divisor on
X
and
L=\mathcal{O}\left(\sum(q_{j}-\lfloor q_{j}\rfloor)F_{j}+L_{0}\right)

is a line bundle which we endow with the singular metric
h_{L}
induced by
F
and
L_{0}
.

(3)

\alpha
and
\beta
are two
E+L
-forms of type
(n,0)
and
(n-1,1)
, respectively
with poles at most along
E+F
, i.e.

(3.3.6)

\alpha=\frac{1}{s^{N}}\mathcal{O}(1),\qquad\beta=\frac{1}{s^{N}}\mathcal{O}(1)

where
s=s_{E}\cdot s_{F}
and such that (
3.3.5
) holds in the complement of
the support of
E+F
.

Our next step would be to improve the ”shape” of the two forms in (
3.3.6
): we show in the next subsection that we can replace them with forms having logarithmic poles on
E+F
.

3.3.
Improving the pole order of
\alpha
and
\beta
(cosmetics)

Notice that by hypothesis (3) above, the meromorphic form

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{s_{E}}

In this set-up, we formulate the next statement.

Proposition 3.4
.

There exists
\alpha_{1}
and
\beta_{1}

(3.4.1)

\bar{\partial}\alpha_{1}+D^{\prime}\beta_{1}=\frac{\lambda}{s_{E}}

holds in the complement of

is the
(1,0)
-part of the Chern connection for
(L,h_{L})

Proof.

We start by choosing a finite covering
\displaystyle(\Omega_{i})_{i\in I}
of
X
, together with corresponding coordinates
(z_{i}^{1},\dots,z_{i}^{n})
such that for every index

Consider next the component

V_{1}:=\sum\theta_{i}(z)z_{i}^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}^{1}}

Notice that since

V_{1}|_{\Omega_{i}}=\big(z_{i}^{1}+\mathcal{O}(z_{i}^{1})^{2}\big)\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}^{1}}+\sum_{k=2}^{r+s}\mathcal{O}(z_{i}^{1}z_{i}^{k})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}^{k}}+\sum_{k>r+s}\mathcal{O}(z_{i}^{1})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}^{k}}

(3.4.5)

z_{i}^{\alpha}=g_{ij}^{\alpha}(z_{j})z_{j}^{\alpha}

the expression (
3.4.4
) obviously corresponds to the restriction of
V_{1}
to
\Omega_{i}
.

Notice also that the local expression of the covariant derivative
D^{\prime}
induced by the singular metric on

(3.4.6)

We first modify the form
\alpha
.
Assume that
\alpha
has a pole of order
N

E_{1}
for some

.
We define

(3.4.7)

\alpha_{N}:=\alpha+\frac{1}{N}D^{\prime}\left(V_{1}\rfloor\alpha\right)

(1,0)

,

3.4.6
). By a direct computation, we show now that the pole order of

. Indeed, we write
\displaystyle\alpha|_{\Omega}=\frac{f}{z_{1}^{N}}dz
(where
\Omega

) and then we have

(3.4.8)

V_{1}\rfloor\alpha=\left(z_{1}+\mathcal{O}(z_{1})^{2}\right)\frac{f}{z_{1}^{N}}\widehat{dz_{1}}+\sum_{j\geqslant 2}(-1)^{j-1}\mathcal{O}(z_{1})\frac{f}{z_{1}^{N}}\widehat{dz_{j}}.

By the formula (

) we have

(3.4.9)

D^{\prime}\left(V_{1}\rfloor\alpha\right)\equiv-N\frac{f}{z_{1}^{N}}dz

modulo a form whose pole order is smaller than

, so our claim is proved

Moreover, we have

(3.4.10)

\lambda=\bar{\partial}\alpha_{N}+D^{\prime}(\beta+\frac{1}{N}\bar{\partial}\left(V_{1}\rfloor\alpha\right))

in the complement of
E\cup F
. We repeat this procedure until the exponent

is equal to zero. At this moment we have

(3.4.11)

\lambda=\bar{\partial}\alpha_{0}+D^{\prime}(\beta_{0})

where
\alpha_{0}
has no poles along
E_{1}
and the coefficients of
\beta_{0}
are similar to the ones of our initial
\beta
.

We are dealing with the other components of

and

we proceed as follows. We define the vector field

(3.4.12)

V_{2}:=\sum\theta_{i}(z)z_{i}^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}^{2}}

constructed by using coordinate system (
3.4.2
). It then follows that locally the
V_{2}
can be written as

(3.4.13)

V_{2}|_{\Omega_{i}}=\big(z_{i}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(z_{i}^{2})^{2}\big)\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(z_{i}^{1}z_{i}^{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}^{1}}+\sum_{j\neq 1,2}\mathcal{O}(z_{i}^{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}^{k}}.

With this choice of vector field, if
\alpha
is a smooth form along
E_{1}
then the same is true for

(3.4.14)

\alpha+\frac{1}{N}D^{\prime}\left(V_{2}\rfloor\alpha\right)

in other words, we are not loosing what we have gained at the first step.
Furthermore, the pole order of (
3.4.14
) along
E_{2}
has dropped by at least one unit if
\alpha
is of pole order
N\geqslant 1
along
E_{2}
. The same procedure as before applies.

Finally we get some new
\alpha
and
\beta
such that

(3.4.15)

\lambda=\bar{\partial}{\alpha}+D^{\prime}{\beta}

holds in the complement of
E+F
and such that the form
\displaystyle{\alpha}
is smooth on
X
and the coefficients of

are of type
\displaystyle\frac{b}{s^{k}}
for some smooth function

and positive integer
k
.

We now turn to the form
\beta
, and prove that modulo the image of the

-operator it has no poles along
E+F
.
Assume that
\beta
has a pole of order
N\geqslant 1
along
E_{1}
. Then we define

(3.4.16)

\beta_{N}:=\beta+\frac{1}{N}D^{\prime}(V_{1}\rfloor\beta)

and the same type of calculations as before will show that it has a pole of order at most
N-1
along

.

To see this, we write

(3.4.17)

\beta|_{\Omega}=\sum_{j\geqslant 2,k}\frac{\eta_{j\overline{k}}}{z_{1}^{N}}dz_{1}\wedge\widehat{dz_{j}}\wedge d\overline{z}_{k}+\sum_{k}\frac{\eta_{\overline{k}}}{z_{1}^{N}}\widehat{dz_{1}}\wedge d\overline{z}_{k}

in local coordinates. Since
\lambda
and
\alpha
in (

More precisely, we have

\Xi_{k}\cdot(f,t^{k+1})\subset(f,t^{k})

Indeed we have

In conclusion, after the first derivative of the relation (

∎

4.3.
A sufficient condition

In this subsection we assume that the divisor

corresponding to the section

Hypothesis 4.6

There exists a divisor

\mathcal{X}
such that we have.

(i)

The divisor

is snc.

(ii)

The support of
\mathrm{Div}(s)
is contained in the restriction
\displaystyle\Sigma|_{X}

Then we show that the following holds true.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let

be a pluricanonical section such that Hypothesis

is satisfied. Then

admits a holomorphic extension to
\mathcal{X}

Proof.

The role of the two conditions (i) and (ii) above is to furnish a special coordinate system on
\mathcal{X}
which we will use during all the proof.

As consequence of these requirements we have a finite covering
\displaystyle\big(\Omega,(t,z_{i})\big)_{i\in I}
of an open subset of the central fiber
X
of
p
such that for each

we have
\Sigma\cap\Omega_{i}=(z_{i}^{1}\dots z_{i}^{r}=0)
together with
the transition functions

(4.7.1)

z_{i}^{\gamma}=g_{ij}^{\alpha}(t,z_{j})z_{j}^{\gamma},\qquad z_{i}^{\beta}=\phi_{ij}^{\beta}(t,z_{j})

for each

and

.

These coordinates are inducing a trivialization of the canonical bundle of

with respect to which our section

corresponds locally to holomorphic functions

is a nowhere vanishing holomorphic function on

and

.

We formulate the next statement.

Claim 4.8
.

For every order

there exists a section

s_{k}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F}_{k})
–with
\mathcal{L}=(m-1)K_{\mathcal{X}}
– such that
\displaystyle s_{k}|_{X}=s
and such that it can be written locally as follows

(4.8.1)

f_{i}(t,z_{i})=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{k}\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}f_{i\alpha}(z_{i})

where

are holomorphic, such that for every positive real
\varepsilon>0
and for every

\displaystyle\int_{X\cap\Omega_{i}}\frac{|f_{i\alpha}|^{2}}{|f_{i0}|^{2(1-\varepsilon)\frac{m-1}{m}}}<\infty
.

We prove next Claim
4.8
by induction on

. For

this is simply the hypothesis, so let us assume that a section

as in (

) exists. We are constructing next

the transition functions of the bundle

(4.8.2)

f_{i}(t,z_{i})=\xi_{ij}(t,z_{j})\left(f_{j}(t,z_{j})+t^{k+1}\Lambda_{ij}(t,z_{j})\right)

on the overlapping sets
\Omega_{i}\cap\Omega_{j}
. By the equalities (

) it follows that

(4.8.3)

\frac{\partial^{l}f_{i\alpha}\big(\Phi(t,z_{j})\big)}{\partial t^{l}}\Big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle\frac{d\lambda(z_{i})}{|f_{i0}|^{2(1-\varepsilon)\frac{m-1}{m}}}
for any positive integer

\varepsilon>0
. Indeed, given (

) if some monomial, say,

divides

as in (

The conclusion is that we have

for every positive

which means precisely that the
(n,1)
form
(m-1)K_{X}
-valued form

associated to the cocycle

is verifies the hypothesis of Theorem

.

The proof of Theorem

shows that

-exact, where

\displaystyle\nu_{q}:=\frac{m-1}{m}m_{q}-1

This can be re-interpreted in terms of cocycles as follows: there exists holomorphic

∎

This will be done by an explicit evaluation of

Coming back to the integrals above, for (

We define the linear form

Lemma 4.12

Let

(4.12.1)

\psi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}

according to

and its orthogonal. Then

Proof.

Note that

Indeed, (

Therefore, we infer that

\int_{X}\langle\rho,D^{\prime\star}\xi_{2}\rangle_{\omega_{E}}e^{-\varphi_{L}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{X}\langle\mu_{\varepsilon}D^{\prime}\rho,\xi_{2}\rangle_{\omega_{E}}e^{-\varphi_{L}}.

4.5.
An application of Theorem
1.12

In this subsection we consider the simplest case of a pluricanonical section whose corresponding divisor is unreduced. Let
s\in H^{0}(X,mK_{X})
such that

(4.12.27)

\mathrm{Div}(s)=2Z

where
Z
is a non-singular hypersurface of the central fiber
X
of our family.

We have the following result.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
s\in H^{0}(X,mK_{X})
be a pluricanonical section, such that (
4.12.27
) is satisfied. Then there exists a section
s_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X},mK_{\mathcal{X}})
such that the following hold

(4.13.1)

s_{2}|_{X}=s,\qquad\bar{\partial}s_{2}=t^{3}\Lambda_{2}.

Proof.

We will show that under the hypothesis (
4.12.27
) we can construct the vector field
\Xi
as requested in Theorem
1.12
.

To this end, we first use Subsection
4.1
, so that the first order extension
s_{1}
of
s
can be written as

(4.13.2)

s_{1}|_{\Omega}\simeq\left(z_{1}^{2}+tz_{1}f_{1}(z)\right)(dt\wedge dz)^{\otimes m}

modulo
t^{2}
, where
\Omega
is one of the
\Omega_{i}
,
z_{1}=0
is the equation of the divisor
Z
on the central fiber
X
and
f_{1}
is holomorphic.

We change the coordinates on
\Omega
by taking

(4.13.3)

w_{1}:=z_{1}+\frac{t}{2}f_{1}(z),\qquad w_{i}=z_{i}

for
i=2,\dots,n
. The new local expression for the section
s_{1}
reads as

(4.13.4)

s_{1}|_{\Omega}\simeq w_{1}^{2}\big(1+tg(w)\big)(dt\wedge dw)^{\otimes m}

where
g
is holomorphic, again modulo smooth terms divisible by monomials in
t

and
\overline{t}
of degree at least 2.

Let
h_{s}
be the Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{X}\setminus(s_{1}=0)
defined as follows

(4.13.5)

h_{s}:=\omega+\frac{\sqrt{-1}D^{\prime}s_{1}\wedge\overline{D^{\prime}s_{1}}}{|s_{1}|^{2}}

where
\omega
is an arbitrary smooth metric on
\mathcal{X}
and
D^{\prime}
is the covariant derivative given by the Chern connection induced by a smooth metric on
mK_{\mathcal{X}}
.

The metric
h_{s}
is singular and in general not Kähler, but its task is to allow us to define
\Xi
as the canonical lifting of the vector field

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
in an intrinsic, computable manner.
Recall that with respect to the coordinates
(t,w)
as above we have

(4.13.6)

\Xi|_{\Omega}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\sum v_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial w_{i}}

where the coefficients
\displaystyle v_{i}:=\sum_{\alpha}h^{\overline{\alpha}i}h_{t\overline{\alpha}}
are
expressed by the usual formula with respect to the coefficients of
h_{s}
.

We evaluate next briefly the coefficients of
h_{s}
; we write

(4.13.7)

D^{\prime}s_{1}|_{\Omega}=2w_{1}dw_{1}+\mathcal{O}(w_{1}^{2})+\mathcal{O}(t,\overline{t})

where
\mathcal{O}(w_{1}^{2})
and
\mathcal{O}(t,\overline{t})
denote forms of type (1,0) on
\Omega
whose coefficients belong to the ideal
(w_{1}^{2})
and
(t,\overline{t})
, respectively. Therefore, at points
\Omega\cap X
we can write

(4.13.8)

h_{t\overline{1}}=g_{t\overline{1}}+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{1}{\overline{w}_{1}}\Big),\qquad h_{t\overline{\alpha}}=g_{t\overline{\alpha}}+\mathcal{O}(1)

for each
\alpha=2,\dots,n
, where
g_{t\overline{\alpha}}
are the coefficients of the non-singular metric
\omega
Moreover, the determinant of the matrix corresponding to
h_{s}
is clearly equal to
\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{O}(1)}{|w_{1}|^{2}}
, where here
\mathcal{O}(1)
is smooth,
positive and bounded away from zero.

Thus the coefficients of the inverse matrix can be written as

(4.13.9)

h^{\overline{1}1}=|w_{1}|^{2}\big(1+\mathcal{O}(1)\big),\qquad h^{\overline{\alpha}1}=|w_{1}|^{2}\Big(1+\frac{\mathcal{O}(1)}{\overline{w}_{1}}\Big)

for each
\alpha=2,\dots,n

and hence we have

(4.13.10)

v_{1}=\mathcal{O}(w_{1}),\qquad v_{i}=\mathcal{O}(1)

for each
i=2,\dots,n
.

This case-by case analysis shows that the requirements of Theorem
1.12
are satisfied, since in our present case the Poincaré metric is quasi-isometric to

(4.13.11)

\frac{\sqrt{-1}dw_{1}\wedge d\overline{w}_{1}}{|w_{1}|^{2}\log^{2}|w_{1}|^{2}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\sqrt{-1}{dw_{i}\wedge d\overline{w}_{i}}.

This ends the proof of our theorem.
∎

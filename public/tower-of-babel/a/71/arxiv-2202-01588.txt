Pentagon relation in quantum cluster scattering diagrams
Tomoki Nakanishi

Abstract
. We formulate the pentagon relation for quantum dilogarithm elements in the structure group of a quantum cluster scattering diagram (QCSD). As an application, we establish the nonpositivity of a certain class of nonskew-symmetric QCSDs. Also, we explicitly present various consistency relations and their positivity for QCSDs of rank 2 completely or up to some degree, most of which are new in the literature.

1.
Introduction

In
[
GHKK18
]

the scattering diagram method
was employed
to study cluster algebras
[
FZ02
]
,
where all information of a cluster algebra or a cluster pattern is encoded in the corresponding
cluster
scattering diagram
(CSD, for short).
It was clarified implicitly in
[
GHKK18
]
and more explicitly in
[
Nak23
]
that
the
dilogarithm elements

\Psi[n]
,
where
n
is its normal vector,
and the
pentagon relation
among them play prominent roles
in a CSD.
Meanwhile,
it has been known that
cluster algebras
admit natural quantizations
[
BZ05
,
Tra11
,
FG09a
,
FG09b
]
,
where the
quantum dilogarithm
plays a key role.
Naturally,
quantum cluster scattering diagrams
(QCSD, for short),
which are quantum analogs of CSDs,
were introduced and studied in
[
Man21
,
DM21
]
,
where
quantum dilogarithm elements

play a primary role.
Here and below, we distinguish the quantum dilogarithm
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{q}(x)
and the quantum dilogarithm elements
\Psi_{a,b}[n]
.
The former is a formal power series of an indeterminate
x
with a parameter
q
due to
[
Sch53
,
FK94
,
FG09a
]
, while the latter
are certain elements of the structure group
G
of a QCSD.
It is well known that the quantum dilogarithm satisfies the
pentagon identity

[
FK94
,
CF99
]
,
which is a quantum analog of the pentagon identity (more often called Abel’s identity or the five-term relation) of the dilogarithm
[
Lew81
]
.

Having this development in mind,
in this paper we formulate the
pentagon relation
among
the quantum dilogarithm elements (Theorem
2.7
),
which is the counterpart of the pentagon relation for the dilogarithm elements for a CSD in
[
Nak23
]
.
The definition of QCSDs is in parallel with the one for CSDs
thanks to a more general formulation of scattering diagrams by
[
KS14
]
.
However, each quantum dilogarithm element
\Psi_{a,b}[n]

carries some additional rational parameters (the
quantum data
)
a
and
b

compared with its classical counterpart
\Psi[n]
,
and this is the source of the complication and the richness
in the quantum case.
(The situation is somewhat similar to the
fusion
of representations of quantum affine algebras
with the
spectral parameter
.)

As an application, we study the positivity of QCSDs in Section 5.
We first formulate the positivity of CSDs and QCSDs (Definitions
5.1
and
5.4
) based on the structure of the walls therein.
It is known that any CSD is positive
[
GHKK18
]
and it implies the positivity of the theta functions for each CSD.
It is also known that any QCSD with the
skew-symmetric
initial exchange matrix is positive
[
DM21
]
.
On the other hand, there are examples of nonpositive theta functions for QCSDs
[
LLZ12
,
CFMM24
]
.
This implies that QCSDs are not always positive in the
nonskew-symmetric
case.
Using the pentagon relation, we clarify the origin of the nonpositivity in the combinatorics of quantum dilogarithm elements,
and we establish the
nonpositivity
of a certain class of nonskew-symmetric QCSDs
(Theorem
5.10
and
Corollary

5.12
).
This extends the results of
[
LLRZ14
,
DM21
,
CFMM24
]
systematically.
Also, we explicitly present various consistency relations
and their positivity for QCSDs of rank 2
completely (Section
3.2
) or up to degree 4 (Section
5.4
).
Most of these formulas are new in the literature and exhibit the complexity of the positivity phenomenon.

2.
Quantum dilogarithm elements and pentagon relation

We introduce quantum dilogarithm elements and the pentagon relation among them.

2.1.
Structure group
G

Here we introduce the underlying
structure group

G
of a QCSD
following
[
KS14
,
Man21
,
DM21
,
CFMM24
]
.
See also
[
GHKK18
,
Nak23
]
for parallel notions for a CSD.

Let
\Gamma
be a
fixed data
consisting of the following:

•

a lattice
N
of rank
r
,

•

a skew-symmetric bilinear form
\{\cdot,\cdot\}\colon N\times N\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
,

•

a sublattice
N^{\circ}\subset N
of rank
r
such that
\{N^{\circ},N\}\subset\mathbb{Z}
,

•

positive integers
\delta_{1}
, …,
\delta_{r}
such that
there is a basis
(e_{1},\dots,e_{r})
of
N
, where
(\delta_{1}e_{1},\dots,\delta_{r}e_{r})

is a basis of
N^{\circ}
,

•

M=\mathrm{Hom}(N,\mathbb{Z})
and
M^{\circ}=\mathrm{Hom}(N^{\circ},\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
M_{\mathbb{R}}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.
Let

\langle n,m\rangle
denote the canonical paring either for
N^{\circ}\times M^{\circ}

or for
N\times M_{\mathbb{R}}
.
For
n\in N
,
n\neq 0
, let
n^{\perp}:=\{\xi\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\langle n,\xi\rangle=0\}
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}=(e_{1},\dots,e_{r})
be a
seed
for
\Gamma
,
which is a basis of
N
such that
(\delta_{1}e_{1},\dots,\delta_{r}e_{r})
is a basis of
N^{\circ}
.
The dual bases of
M
and
M^{\circ}
are given by

(e_{1}^{*},\dots,e_{r}^{*})
and

(f_{1},\dots,f_{r}):=(e_{1}^{*}/\delta_{1},\dots,e_{r}^{*}/\delta_{r})
, respectively.
The initial exchange matrix
B=(b_{ij})
of the corresponding quantum cluster algebra
is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle b_{ij}=\{\delta_{i}e_{i},e_{j}\}.

Let

(2.2)

\displaystyle N^{+}=\Biggl\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}e_{i}\mid a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\,\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}>0\Biggr\}

be the positive vectors of
N
with respect to
\mathfrak{s}
.
Let
N^{+}_{\mathrm{pr}}
denote the set of primitive elements in
N^{+}
.
The degree function
\deg\colon N^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is defined by
\deg(\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}e_{i}):=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}
.

For
n\in N^{+}
, let
\delta(n)
be the smallest positive rational number
such that
\delta(n)n\in N^{\circ}
,
which is called the
normalization factor
of
n
.
For example,
\delta(e_{i})=\delta_{i}
.
We have
\delta(n)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
\delta(tn)=\delta(n)/t
for any
n\in N^{+}_{\mathrm{pr}}
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
We set
\delta_{0}
as the least common multiple of
\delta_{1}
, …,
\delta_{r}
.
Then,
\delta_{0}n\in N^{\circ}
for any
n\in N^{+}
. It implies that

\{\cdot,\cdot\}\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z}

and that
\delta_{0}/\delta(n)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

Let
q
be an indeterminate, and let
\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})
be the rational function field of
q^{1/\delta_{0}}
.
For any
\alpha\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z}
, let

(2.3)

\displaystyle[\alpha]_{q}:=\frac{q^{\alpha}-q^{-\alpha}}{q-q^{-1}}\in\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})

be the
q
-number
, which has the limit

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}[\alpha]_{q}=\alpha.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the
N^{+}
-graded Lie algebra over
\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})
defined by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{n\in N^{+}}\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})X_{n},\quad[X_{n},X_{n^{\prime}}]:=[\{n,n^{\prime}\}]_{q}X_{n+n^{\prime}},

where the Jacobi identity is easily verified.
Let
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
be the completion of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
\deg
, and let
G=\exp(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})

be the exponential group of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
whose product is defined by the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula
(e.g.,
[
Jac79
, §V.5]
).
We call
G
the
structure group
for the forthcoming scattering diagrams.

Let
(N^{+})^{>\ell}=\{n\in N^{+}\mid\deg(n)>\ell\}
.
Let
G^{>\ell}
be the normal subgroup of
G

consisting elements

\exp(\sum_{n\in(N^{+})^{>\ell}}c_{n}X_{n})
, where the sum may be infinite,
and let
G^{\leq\ell}:=G/G^{>\ell}
be its quotient.
For each
n\in N_{\mathrm{pr}}^{+}
, let
G_{n}^{\parallel}
be the abelian subgroup of
G

consisting of elements
\exp(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}X_{jn})
.
For any
g=\exp(X)
and
c\in\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})
,
the power
g^{c}\in G
is defined by
\exp(cX)
.

Remark 2.1
.

So far,
the only difference from the classical case is the Lie bracket
in (
2.5
).
It is a quantum analog of the Lie bracket

(2.6)

\displaystyle[X_{n},X_{n^{\prime}}]:=\{n,n^{\prime}\}X_{n+n^{\prime}}.

for a CSD.
In what follows all expressions involving
q
converge to their classical counterparts
in the limit
q\rightarrow 1
.

2.2.
y
-representation

Let
y
be a symbol.
Let
\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})[y]_{q}
be the noncommutative and associative algebra over

\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})
with generators
y^{n}
(
n\in N^{+}\sqcup\{0\})

and the
q
-commutative elations

(2.7)

\displaystyle y^{n}y^{n^{\prime}}=q^{\{n,n^{\prime}\}}y^{n+n^{\prime}}.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{q}(y)
be the completion of
\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})[y]_{q}
with respect to
\deg
.
In other words,
any element of
\mathcal{R}_{q}(y)
is expressed as an infinite sum

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in N^{+}\sqcup\{0\}}c_{n}y^{n}\quad(c_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})).

Then, we define the action of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\mathcal{R}_{q}(y)
by

(2.9)

\displaystyle X_{n}(y^{n^{\prime}}):=

\displaystyle\ [\{n,n^{\prime}\}]_{q}y^{n^{\prime}+n}

(2.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{q^{2\{n,n^{\prime}\}}-1}{q-q^{-1}}y^{n^{\prime}}y^{n}.

It is easy to check that this is indeed an action of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}

and also a derivation.
Thus,
it induces the action of
G
on
\mathcal{R}_{q}(y)
defined by

(2.11)

\displaystyle(\exp X)(y^{n})=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!}X^{j}(y^{n})\quad(X\in\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}).

Moreover,
\exp X
is an algebra automorphism

[
Jac79
, §I.2]
.
We call the resulting representation

\rho_{y}:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{R}_{q}(y))

the
(quantum)
y
-representation
of
G
.
It is faithful if and only if
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
is
nondegenerate.

Remark 2.2
.

If we replace the relation (
2.7
) with
the standard one

y^{n}y^{n^{\prime}}=y^{n+n^{\prime}}
,
the action (
2.9
) is still an action of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
,
but it is not a derivation.
This explains the necessity of the relation (
2.7
).

2.3.
Quantum dilogarithm elements and pentagon relation

Let
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{q}(x)
be the
quantum dilogarithm
by
[
Sch53
,
FV93
,
FK94
,
FG09a
]
,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{q}(x):=

\displaystyle\ \exp\biggr(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j(q^{j}-q^{-j})}x^{j}\biggl)\quad\in\mathbb{Q}(q)[[x]]

(2.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \prod_{j=0}^{\infty}(1+q^{2j+1}x)^{-1}.

This convention is due to
[
FG09a
]
.
The equality holds, because
both expressions satisfy the property

(2.14)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{q}(q^{2}x)=(1+qx)\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{q}(x),\quad\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{q}(0)=1,

which uniquely determines
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{q}(x)
.
See, for example,
[
Kir95
,
FG09a
]
for further information.

The following definition is similar to the first expression (
2.12
)
of
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{q}(x)
.

Definition 2.3

(Quantum dilogarithm element)
.

For any
n\in N^{+}
,
a\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and
b\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z}
, we define

(2.15)

\displaystyle\Psi_{a,b}[n]:=\exp\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j[ja]_{q}}q^{jb}X_{jn}\Biggr)\in G_{n_{0}}^{\parallel},

where
n_{0}\in N_{\mathrm{pr}}^{+}
is the one such that
n=jn_{0}
for some
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
We call it a
quantum dilogarithm element
.
We call the parameters
a
and
b
the
quantum data
of
\Psi_{a,b}[n]
.
They control the
interval
and the
shift
in the sum, respectively.
For simplicity, we also write

(2.16)

\displaystyle\Psi_{a}[n]:=\Psi_{a,0}[n].

Remark 2.4
.

The above
\Psi_{a,b}[n]
is a quantum analog of the dilogarithm element
in
the classical case
[
Nak23
, §III.1.4]

(2.17)

\displaystyle\Psi[n]=\exp\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j^{2}}X_{jn}\Biggr).

We have

(2.18)

\displaystyle\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}\Psi_{a,b}[n]=\Psi[n]^{1/a}.

Note that the interval
a
appears in the RHS as the inverse.

Proposition 2.5
.

The group
G
is generated by
\Psi_{a,b}[n]^{c}

(
n\in N^{+}
,

a\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
b\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z}
,
c\in\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})
)

admitting the infinite product.

Proof.

By (
2.15
), any element of

G_{n_{0}}^{\parallel}
is expressed as an infinite product of

\Psi_{a,b}[jn_{0}]^{c}
’s.
∎

By (
2.10
),
each dilogarithm element
\Psi_{a,b}[n]
acts on
\mathcal{R}_{q}(y)
under
\rho_{y}
as

(2.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Psi_{a,b}[n](y^{n^{\prime}})&=\exp\Biggl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j[ja]_{q}}q^{jb}X_{jn}\Biggr)(y^{n^{\prime}})\\
&=y^{n^{\prime}}\exp\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2j\{n,n^{\prime}\}}-1}{q^{2ja}-1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j}q^{ja}q^{jb}y^{jn}\Biggr).\end{split}

We consider two special situations,
where the expression (
2.19
) is simplified.

(i). Fix
n
in (
2.19
). Let
a=1/s\delta(n)
, where
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is a divisor of
\delta_{0}/\delta(n)
.
Then,
a\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
and also
\alpha:=\{s\delta(n)n,n^{\prime}\}
is an integer.
Then, we have

(2.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{q^{2j\{n,n^{\prime}\}}-1}{q^{2ja}-1}&=\frac{q^{2\alpha j/s\delta(n)}-1}{q^{2j/s\delta(n)}-1}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{\alpha-1}q^{2jp/s\delta(n)}&\alpha>0,\\
0&\alpha=0,\\
\displaystyle-\sum_{p=1}^{-\alpha}q^{-2jp/s\delta(n)}&\alpha<0.\end{cases}\end{split}

Thus, we obtain

(2.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\ \Psi_{1/s\delta(n),b}[n](y^{n^{\prime}})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle y^{n^{\prime}}\prod_{p=1}^{\alpha}(1+q^{(2p-1)/s\delta(n)}q^{b}y^{n})&\alpha>0,\\
y^{n^{\prime}}&\alpha=0,\\
\displaystyle y^{n^{\prime}}\prod_{p=1}^{-\alpha}(1+q^{-(2p-1)/s\delta(n)}q^{b}y^{n})^{-1}&\alpha<0.\end{cases}\end{split}

Observe that
this is the automorphism part of the Fock-Goncharov decomposition
of mutations of the quantum
y
-variables in
[
FG09a
, §3.3]
.

(ii). Fix
n
and
n^{\prime}
in (
2.19
).
Suppose that
\{n,n^{\prime}\}\neq 0
,
and let
a=|\{n,n^{\prime}\}|
.
Then,
we have

(2.22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{q^{2j\{n,n^{\prime}\}}-1}{q^{2ja}-1}&=\begin{cases}1&\{n,n^{\prime}\}>0,\\
\displaystyle-q^{-2ja}&\{n,n^{\prime}\}<0.\end{cases}\end{split}

Thus, we obtain

(2.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\ \Psi_{a,b}[n](y^{n^{\prime}})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle y^{n^{\prime}}(1+q^{a}q^{b}y^{n})&\{n,n^{\prime}\}>0,\\
\displaystyle y^{n^{\prime}}(1+q^{-a}q^{b}y^{n})^{-1}&\{n,n^{\prime}\}<0.\end{cases}\end{split}

Remark 2.6
.

The same formula in the RHS of (
2.21
)
is also obtained by the

adjoint action
of the
quantum dilogarithm

\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{q^{1/s\delta(n)}}(q^{b}y^{n})
on
\mathcal{R}_{q}(y)

[
FG09a
]
.
In this sense, we have the correspondence between

\Psi_{1/s\delta(n),b}[n]
and
the quantum dilogarithm

\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{q^{1/s\delta(n)}}(q^{b}y^{n})
.

We have the following important application of the formula
(
2.23
).

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
n_{1},n_{2}\in N^{+}
.
The following relations hold in
G
.

(a). Suppose that
\{n_{2},n_{1}\}=0
.
Then,
for any
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
b_{1}
, and
b_{2}
,
we have

(2.24)

\displaystyle\Psi_{a_{2},b_{2}}[n_{2}]\Psi_{a_{1},b_{1}}[n_{1}]=\Psi_{a_{1},b_{1}}[n_{1}]\Psi_{a_{2},b_{2}}[n_{2}].

(b). (Pentagon relation. Cf.
[
FK94
, §2]
).
Suppose that
\{n_{2},n_{1}\}=c

(c\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z}_{>0})
.
Then,
for any
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
,
we have

(2.25)

\displaystyle\Psi_{c,b_{2}}[n_{2}]\Psi_{c,b_{1}}[n_{1}]=\Psi_{c,b_{1}}[n_{1}]\Psi_{c,b_{1}+b_{2}}[n_{1}+n_{2}]\Psi_{c,b_{2}}[n_{2}].

Proof.

The relation (
2.24
) is clear by (
2.5
).
Let us prove the relation (
2.25
).
Since it only involves

\Psi_{a,b}[n]
’s for the rank 2 sublattice
N^{\prime}
of
N

generated by
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
,
we concentrate on the subgroup
G^{\prime}
of
G
corresponding to
N^{\prime}
.
Accordingly, we consider
the
y
-representation of
G^{\prime}
acting on the subalgebra of
\mathcal{R}_{q}(y)

generated by
y^{n_{1}}
and
y^{n_{2}}
.
By the assumption
\{n_{2},n_{1}\}\neq 0
, this is faithful.
Thus, one can prove (
2.25
) with this representation.

First, we consider the action on
y^{n_{1}}
.
Note that
\{n_{1}+n_{2},n_{1}\}=-\{n_{1}+n_{2},n_{2}\}=c>0
.
Then, thanks to the formula (
2.23
),
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\Psi_{c,b_{2}}[n_{2}]\Psi_{c,b_{1}}[n_{1}])(y^{n_{1}})&=\Psi_{c,b_{2}}[n_{2}](y^{n_{1}})\\
&=y^{n_{1}}(1+q^{c+b_{2}}y^{n_{2}}),\end{split}

and also

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\ (\Psi_{c,b_{1}}[n_{1}]\Psi_{c,b_{1}+b_{2}}[n_{1}+n_{2}]\Psi_{c,b_{2}}[n_{2}])(y^{n_{1}})\\
&=(\Psi_{c,b_{1}}[n_{1}]\Psi_{c,b_{1}+b_{2}}[n_{1}+n_{2}])(y^{n_{1}}(1+q^{c+b_{2}}y^{n_{2}}))\\
&=\Psi_{c,b_{1}}[n_{1}](y^{n_{1}}(1+q^{c+b_{1}+b_{2}}y^{n_{1}+n_{2}})\\
&\qquad\times(1+q^{c+b_{2}}y^{n_{2}}(1+q^{-c+b_{1}+b_{2}}y^{n_{1}+n_{2}})^{-1}))\\
&=\Psi_{c,b_{1}}[n_{1}](y^{n_{1}}(1+q^{c+b_{2}}y^{n_{2}}+q^{c+b_{1}+b_{2}}y^{n_{1}+n_{2}}))\\
&=y^{n_{1}}(1+q^{c+b_{2}}y^{n_{2}}(1+q^{-c+b_{1}}y_{1})^{-1}+q^{c+b_{1}+b_{2}}y^{n_{1}+n_{2}}(1+q^{-c+b_{1}}y_{1})^{-1})\\
&=y^{n_{1}}(1+q^{-c+b_{1}}y_{1}+q^{c+b_{2}}y^{n_{2}}+q^{b_{1}+b_{2}}y^{n_{2}}y^{n_{1}})(1+q^{-c+b_{1}}y_{1})^{-1}\\
&=y^{n_{1}}(1+q^{c+b_{2}}y^{n_{2}}).\end{split}

Thus, they coincide.
Next, we consider the action on
y^{n_{2}}
.
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\ (\Psi_{c,b_{2}}[n_{2}]\Psi_{c,b_{1}}[n_{1}])(y^{n_{2}})\\
&=\Psi_{c,b_{2}}[n_{2}](y^{n_{2}}(1+q^{-c+b_{1}}y^{n_{1}})^{-1})\\
&=y^{n_{2}}(1+q^{-c+b_{1}}y^{n_{1}}(1+q^{c+b_{2}}y^{n_{2}}))^{-1}\\
&=y^{n_{2}}(1+q^{-c+b_{1}}y^{n_{1}}+q^{b_{1}+b_{2}}y^{n_{1}}y^{n_{2}})^{-1},\end{split}

and also

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\ (\Psi_{c,b_{1}}[n_{1}]\Psi_{c,b_{1}+b_{2}}[n_{1}+n_{2}]\Psi_{c,b_{2}}[n_{2}])(y^{n_{2}})\\
&=(\Psi_{c,b_{1}}[n_{1}]\Psi_{c,b_{1}+b_{2}}[n_{1}+n_{2}])(y^{n_{2}})\\
&=\Psi_{c,b_{1}}[n_{1}](y^{n_{2}}(1+q^{-c+b_{1}+b_{2}}y^{n_{1}+n_{2}})^{-1})\\
&=y^{n_{2}}(1+q^{-c+b_{1}}y^{n_{1}})^{-1}(1+q^{-c+b_{1}+b_{2}}y^{n_{1}+n_{2}}(1+q^{-c+b_{1}}y_{1})^{-1})^{-1}\\
&=y^{n_{2}}(1+q^{-c+b_{1}}y^{n_{1}}+q^{b_{1}+b_{2}}y^{n_{1}}y^{n_{2}})^{-1}.\end{split}

Again, they coincide.
∎

Also, the following operations to decompose and unify quantum dilogarithm elements
are important for our purpose.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
p
be a positive integer.
The following equalities hold,
where we assume
a/p\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
in the second equality:

(2.26)

\displaystyle\text{\rm(fission)}\quad\Psi_{a,b}[n]=\prod_{t=1}^{p}\Psi_{pa,b+(2t-p-1)a}[n],

(2.27)

\displaystyle\text{\rm(fusion)}\quad\prod_{t=1}^{p}\Psi_{a,b+(2t-p-1)a/p}[n]=\Psi_{a/p,b}[n].

Proof.

Two equalities (
2.26
) and (
2.27
)
are equivalent.
The equality (
2.26
) is shown as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Psi_{a,b}[n]&=\exp\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j[ja]_{q}}\frac{q^{jpa}-q^{-jpa}}{q^{jpa}-q^{-jpa}}q^{bj}X_{jn}\Biggr)\\
&=\exp\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j[jpa]_{q}}\frac{q^{jpa}-q^{-jpa}}{q^{ja}-q^{-ja}}q^{bj}X_{jn}\Biggr)\\
&=\exp\Biggl(\sum_{t=1}^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j[jpa]_{q}}q^{j(2t-p-1)a}q^{bj}X_{jn}\Biggr)\\
&=\prod_{t=1}^{p}\Psi_{pa,b+(2t-p-1)a}[n].\end{split}

∎

3.1.
Quantum cluster scattering diagrams

The following definitions for (quantum) scattering diagrams
are just the same as the classical one in
[
GHKK18
]
,
by replacing the structure group
G
with the one in
Section
2.1
.

A
wall

\mathbf{w}=(\mathfrak{d},g)_{n}
for
\mathfrak{s}
is a triplet with

n\in N_{\mathrm{pr}}^{+}
, a cone
\mathfrak{d}\subset n^{\perp}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
of codimension 1, and
g\in G_{n}^{\parallel}
.
We call
n
,
\mathfrak{d}
,
g
, the
normal vector
, the
support
, the
wall element

of
\mathbf{w}
, respectively.
Let
p^{*}\colon N\rightarrow M^{\circ}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
,
n\mapsto\{\cdot,n\}
.
We say that a wall
\mathbf{w}=(\mathfrak{d},g)_{n}
is
incoming
if
p^{*}(n)\in\mathfrak{d}
holds.

Definition 3.1

(Scattering diagram)
.

A
scattering diagram

\mathfrak{D}=\{\mathbf{w}_{\lambda}=(\mathfrak{d}_{\lambda},g_{{\lambda}})_{n_{\lambda}}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
for
\mathfrak{s}
is a collection of walls for
\mathfrak{s}

satisfying the following finiteness condition:
For any degree
\ell
,
there are only finitely many walls such that
\pi_{\ell}(g_{\lambda})\neq\mathrm{id}
,
where
\pi_{\ell}\colon G\rightarrow G^{\leq\ell}
is the canonical projection.

For a scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}
, we define

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Supp}(\mathfrak{D})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{d}_{\lambda},\quad\mathrm{Sing}(\mathfrak{D})=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\partial\mathfrak{d}_{\lambda}\cup\bigcup_{\scriptstyle\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda\atop\scriptstyle\dim\mathfrak{d}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{d}_{\lambda^{\prime}}=r-2}\mathfrak{d}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{d}_{\lambda^{\prime}}.

A curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\rightarrow M_{\mathbb{R}}
is

admissible
for
\mathfrak{D}

if it satisfies the following properties:

(1)

The endpoints of
\gamma
are in
M_{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(\mathfrak{D})
.

(2)

It is a smooth curve, and it intersects
\mathrm{Supp}(\mathfrak{D})
transversally.

(3)

\gamma
does not intersect
\mathrm{Sing}(\mathfrak{D})
.

For any admissible curve
\gamma
,
the path-ordered product
\mathfrak{p}_{\gamma,\mathfrak{D}}\in G
is defined
as the product of
the wall elements
g_{{\lambda}}^{\epsilon_{\lambda}}
of walls
\mathbf{w}_{\lambda}
of
\mathfrak{D}
intersected by
\gamma

in the order of intersection, where
\epsilon_{\lambda}
is the
intersection sign

defined by

(3.2)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\lambda}=\begin{cases}1&\langle n_{\lambda},\gamma^{\prime}\rangle<0,\\
-1&\langle n_{\lambda},\gamma^{\prime}\rangle>0,\end{cases}

and
\gamma^{\prime}
is the velocity vector of
\gamma
at the wall
\mathbf{w}_{\lambda}
.
The product
\mathfrak{p}_{\gamma,\mathfrak{D}}
is infinite in general,
and it is well-defined in
G
due to the finiteness condition.
We say that a pair of scattering diagrams
\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
are
equivalent
if
\mathfrak{p}_{\gamma,\mathfrak{D}}=\mathfrak{p}_{\gamma,\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}}

for any admissible curve
\gamma
for both
\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
.
We say that a scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}
is
consistent
if
\mathfrak{p}_{\gamma,\mathfrak{D}}=\mathrm{id}

for any admissible loop (i.e., closed curve)
\gamma
for
\mathfrak{D}
.

The following definition is due to
[
Man21
]
.

(Quantum cluster scattering diagram)
.

A
quantum cluster scattering diagram

(QCSD, for short) for
\mathfrak{s}
is a consistent scattering diagram
whose set of incoming walls are given by

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{In}_{\mathfrak{s}}:=\{(e_{i}^{\perp},\Psi_{1/\delta_{i}}[e_{i}])_{e_{i}}\mid i=1,\dots,r\}.

(
[

[

, Theorem 2.1.6]

[

Proof.

The proof of
[

, Theorem 1.12]
, which originates in
[

See also
[

Thanks to Proposition

we may assume that, up to equivalence, wall elements of

4.
Principal

We introduce another representation of

which we call
the
principal

-representation
.
It is closely related to the quantization of cluster variables (

-variables) in
[

]
.
Then, we obtain the reduction property of wall elements for a QCSD,
which is important in our application of the pentagon relation to the positivity problem.

4.1.
Principal
x
-representation

Following
[
GHKK18
]
,
we introduce

(4.1)

\displaystyle\tilde{N}=N\oplus M^{\circ},\quad\tilde{M}^{\circ}=M^{\circ}\oplus N,

which are “dual” to each other twisted by
\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{r}
.
An element of
\tilde{N}
is denoted by

\tilde{n}=(n,m)
(
n\in N
,
m\in M^{\circ}
).
Similarly,
an element of
\tilde{M}^{\circ}
is denoted by

\tilde{m}=(m,n)
(
m\in M^{\circ}
,
n\in N
).
Below we use the notation such as
\tilde{n}_{1}=(n_{1},m_{1})
,

\tilde{m}^{\prime}=(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})
without explanation.
Meanwhile, we also use the internal-sum notation.
For example, for
n\in N
,
we write
n=(n,0)\in\tilde{N}
and

n=(0,n)\in\tilde{M}^{\circ}
.
Both spaces
\tilde{N}
and
\tilde{M}^{\circ}
are equipped with
(essentially common) bilinear forms

(4.2)

\displaystyle\{\tilde{n},\tilde{n}^{\prime}\}_{\tilde{N}}

\displaystyle:=\{n,n^{\prime}\}+\langle n^{\prime},m\rangle-\langle n,m^{\prime}\rangle,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\{\tilde{m},\tilde{m}^{\prime}\}_{\tilde{M}^{\circ}}

\displaystyle:=-\{n,n^{\prime}\}-\langle n^{\prime},m\rangle+\langle n,m^{\prime}\rangle,

where
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

in the RHSs
are the ones in Section
2.1
.
We also have the canonical paring

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\tilde{N}\times\tilde{M}^{\circ}\rightarrow(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z}
defined by

(4.4)

\displaystyle\langle(n,m),(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})\rangle:=\langle n,m^{\prime}\rangle+\langle n^{\prime},m\rangle.

Let
\tilde{p}^{*}
be the group homomorphism defined by

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\tilde{p}^{*}\colon&N&\rightarrow&\tilde{M}^{\circ}\\
&n&\mapsto&\{\cdot,n\}_{\tilde{N}}\end{matrix},\quad\{(n^{\prime},m^{\prime}),n\}_{\tilde{N}}=\{n^{\prime},n\}+\langle n,m^{\prime}\rangle.

Note that the map
\tilde{p}^{*}
is injective.
Let
p^{*}\colon N\rightarrow M^{\circ}
be the one in Section

3.1
.
Then, we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\tilde{p}^{*}(n)=(p^{*}(n),n)\in\tilde{M}^{\circ}.

The following duality relation holds:

(4.7)

\displaystyle\{\tilde{p}^{*}(n),\tilde{m}\}_{\tilde{M}^{\circ}}=\langle n,\tilde{m}\rangle.

Indeed, by (
4.6
),

(4.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\{\tilde{p}^{*}(n),\tilde{m}\}_{\tilde{M}^{\circ}}&=\{(p^{*}(n),n),(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})\}_{\tilde{M}^{\circ}}\\
&=-\{n,n^{\prime}\}-\langle n^{\prime},p^{*}(n)\rangle+\langle n,m^{\prime}\rangle\\
&=\langle n,m^{\prime}\rangle=\langle n,\tilde{m}\rangle.\end{split}

Remark 4.1
.

The bilinear form
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\tilde{M}^{\circ}}
in (
4.3
) has the representation matrix
with respect to the basis
f_{1}
, …,
f_{r}
,
e_{1}
, …,
e_{r}

of
\tilde{M}^{\circ}
as

(4.9)

\displaystyle\Lambda:=\begin{pmatrix}O&-D\\
D&-DB\end{pmatrix},\quad D=\mathrm{diag}(\delta_{1}^{-1},\cdots,\delta_{r}^{-1}).

Let
\tilde{B}
be the principal extension of the matrix
B
,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\tilde{B}=\begin{pmatrix}B\\
I\end{pmatrix}.

They satisfy the relation

(4.11)

\displaystyle-\Lambda\tilde{B}=\begin{pmatrix}D\\
O\end{pmatrix},

which is equivalent to the duality relation (
4.7
).
Such a pair
(\Lambda,\tilde{B})
is called a
compatible pair
in
[
BZ05
]
.

Following
[
GHKK18
]
,
let
\tilde{P}\subset\tilde{M}^{\circ}
be a (not unique) monoid satisfying the following conditions:

(i).

\tilde{P}=\sigma\cap\tilde{M}^{\circ}
,
where
\sigma
is a
2r
-dimensional strongly convex cone in
\tilde{M}^{\circ}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

(ii).

\tilde{p}^{*}(e_{1})
, …,
\tilde{p}^{*}(e_{r})\in\tilde{P}
.

(iii).

f_{1}
, …,
f_{r}\in\tilde{P}
.

For example, we take
\sigma
as the cone generated by
f_{1}
, …,
f_{r}
,
\tilde{p}^{*}(e_{1})
, …,
\tilde{p}^{*}(e_{r})
,
which are a basis of
\tilde{M}^{\circ}
.
(In
[
GHKK18
]
, the condition (iii) was not assumed.)

Now we are ready to define the
(quantum) principal
x
-representation
of
G
,
which is defined in a parallel way to the
y
-representation.

Let
x
be a formal symbol.
Let
\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})[x]_{q}
be the noncommutative and associative algebra over

\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})
with generators
x^{\tilde{m}}
(
\tilde{m}\in\tilde{P})

and the
q
-commutative relations

(4.12)

\displaystyle x^{\tilde{m}}x^{\tilde{m}^{\prime}}=q^{\{\tilde{m},\tilde{m}^{\prime}\}_{\tilde{M}^{\circ}}}x^{\tilde{m}+\tilde{m}^{\prime}}.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{q}(x)
be the completion of
\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})[x]_{q}

with respect to the maximal ideal generated by

x^{\tilde{m}}
’s (
\tilde{m}\in\tilde{P}\setminus\{0\})
.
Any element of
\mathcal{R}_{q}(x)
is expressed as an infinite sum

(4.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{\tilde{m}\in\tilde{P}}c_{\tilde{m}}x^{\tilde{m}}\quad(c_{\tilde{m}}\in\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})).

We define the action of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\mathcal{R}_{q}(x)
by

(4.14)

\displaystyle X_{n}(x^{\tilde{m}}):=

\displaystyle\ [\langle n,\tilde{m}\rangle]_{q}x^{\tilde{m}+\tilde{p}^{*}(n)}

(4.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{q^{2\langle n,\tilde{m}\rangle}-1}{q-q^{-1}}x^{\tilde{m}}x^{\tilde{p}^{*}(n)},

where in the second equality we used
the duality relation (
4.7
).
It is easy to check that this is an action of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
and also a derivation.
Thus,
it induces the action of
G
on
\mathcal{R}_{q}(x)
defined by

(4.16)

\displaystyle(\exp X)(x^{\tilde{m}})=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!}X^{j}(x^{\tilde{m}})\quad(X\in\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}).

Moreover,
\exp X
is an algebra automorphism.
We call the resulting representation

\rho_{x}^{\mathrm{pr}}:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{R}_{q}(x))

the
(quantum) principal
x
-representation
of
G
.
It is always a faithful representation,
even when
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
is degenerate.

4.2.
Action of dilogarithm elements

Let us present an analogous result in
Section
2.3
for the principal
x
-representation.

Any
\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})
-power of a dilogarithm element
\Psi_{a,b}[n]^{c}
(
c\in\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}}))
acts on
\mathcal{R}_{q}(x)
under
\rho_{x}^{\mathrm{pr}}
as

(4.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Psi_{a,b}[n]^{c}(x^{\tilde{m}})&=\exp\Biggl(\,c\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j[ja]_{q}}q^{jb}X_{jn}\Biggr)(x^{\tilde{m}})\\
&=x^{\tilde{m}}\exp\Biggl(c\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2j\langle n,\tilde{m}\rangle}-1}{q^{2ja}-1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j}q^{ja}q^{jb}x^{j\tilde{p}^{*}(n)}\Biggr).\end{split}

Let us consider a parallel situation to (
2.20
)
and (
2.21
).
Fix
n
in (
4.17
),
and
let
a=1/s\delta(n)
, where
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is a divisor of
\delta_{0}/\delta(n)
.
Then,
\alpha:=\langle s\delta(n)n,\tilde{m}\rangle
is an integer,
and
we have

(4.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{q^{2j\langle n,\tilde{m}\rangle}-1}{q^{2ja}-1}&=\frac{q^{2\alpha j/s\delta(n)}-1}{q^{2j/s\delta(n)}-1}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{\alpha-1}q^{2jp/s\delta(n)}&\alpha>0,\\
0&\alpha=0,\\
\displaystyle-\sum_{p=1}^{-\alpha}q^{-2jp/s\delta(n)}&\alpha<0.\end{cases}\end{split}

Thus, we obtain

(4.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\ \Psi_{1/s\delta(n),b}[n](x^{\tilde{m}})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle x^{\tilde{m}}\prod_{p=1}^{\alpha}(1+q^{(2p-1)/s\delta(n)}q^{b}x^{\tilde{p}^{*}(n)})&\alpha>0,\\
x^{\tilde{m}}&\alpha=0,\\
\displaystyle x^{\tilde{m}}\prod_{p=1}^{-\alpha}(1+q^{-(2p-1)/s\delta(n)}q^{b}x^{\tilde{p}^{*}(n)})^{-1}&\alpha<0.\end{cases}\end{split}

Observe that
this is indeed the automorphism part of the Fock-Goncharov decomposition
of mutations of the quantum
x
-variables
with principal coefficients in
[
BZ05
]
.
See also
[
Man21
,
DM21
,
CFMM24
]

for an alternative approach, where the same representation is described through the adjoint action
of the quantum dilogarithm on
\mathcal{R}_{q}(x)
as in Remark
2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{q}(x)
be the subset of
\mathcal{R}_{q}(x)

consisting of the elements
such that the coefficients

in
(
4.13
) are in
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1/\delta_{0}}]
.
Then,
\mathcal{S}_{q}(x)
is an algebra over
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1/\delta_{0}}]
by the same relation
(
4.12
).
The following lemma is a quantum analog of
the one in
[
GHKK18
, Appendix C.3 Step II]
,

[
Nak23
, Lemma III.5.8]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

A
\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}})
-power of a
quantum dilogarithm element
\Psi_{a,b}[n]^{c}

(c\in\mathbb{Q}(q^{1/\delta_{0}}))

acts on
\mathcal{S}_{q}(x)

if and only if
a=1/s\delta(n)
for a divisor
s
of
\delta_{0}/\delta(n)

and
c\in\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1/\delta_{0}}]
.

Proof.

First,
we prove the if-part.
By the assumption
c\in\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1/\delta_{0}}]
,

\Psi_{1/s\delta(n),b}[n]^{c}
is decomposed into a product of

\Psi_{1/s\delta(n),b_{i}}[n]^{c_{i}}
(
b_{i}\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z}
,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
).
By (
4.19
), each factor acts on
\mathcal{S}_{q}(x)
.
Thus,
\Psi_{a,b}[n]^{c}
acts on
\mathcal{S}_{q}(x)
.
Next,
we prove the only-if-part.
Assume that
\Psi_{a,b}[n]^{c}
acts on
\mathcal{S}_{q}(x)
.
Then,
by (
4.17
),
we have a necessary condition

(4.20)

\displaystyle c\frac{q^{2j\langle n,\tilde{m}\rangle}-1}{q^{2ja}-1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j}q^{ja}q^{jb}\in\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 1/\delta_{0}}]\quad(j\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}).

Let
a=1/s\delta(n)
, where
s
is a positive rational number.
Then,
(
4.20
) implies the condition

s\langle\delta(n)n,\tilde{m}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}
for any
\tilde{m}\in\tilde{P}
.
By the assumption for
\tilde{P}
,
\tilde{P}
contains a basis of
\tilde{M}^{\circ}
.
It follows that

s\langle\delta(n)n,\tilde{m}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}
for any
\tilde{m}\in\tilde{M}^{\circ}
.
Due to the definition of
\delta(n)
, there is some
\tilde{m}\in\tilde{M}^{\circ}

such that
\langle\delta(n)n,\tilde{m}\rangle=1
. Thus, we have
s\in\mathbb{Z}
.
On the other hand,
let
t=\delta_{0}/\delta(n)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Then,
a=t/s\delta_{0}
.
Since we require that
a\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,

s
is a divisor of
t
.
This finishes the condition for
a
.
Meanwhile, the condition (
4.20
) also implies
c\in\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 1/\delta_{0}}]
.
Then,

\Psi_{a,b}[n]^{c}
is decomposed into a product of

\Psi_{1/s\delta(n),b_{i}}[n]^{c_{i}}
(
b_{i}\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z}
,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
).
By (
4.19
), we have
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Thus, we have
c\in\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1/\delta_{0}}]
.
∎

Below, we only use the if-part of Lemma
4.2
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Any QCSD
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q}

is equivalent to a consistent scattering diagram whose wall elements have
the form

(4.21)

\displaystyle\Psi_{1/\delta(n),b}[n]^{c}\quad(n\in N^{+},\ b\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z},\ c\in\mathbb{Z}).

Proof.

We follow Step 2 in the proof of
[
GHKK18
, Prop. C.13]
.
This is proved inductively on the degree of
n
, by considering the consistency relations around the perpendicular joints.
Here, we only describe the key point of the inductive step.
Suppose that a scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}_{\ell}
has the
walls with wall elements of the form (
4.21
)
with
\deg n\leq\ell
and that,
for a small loop
\gamma
around a perpendicular joint
\mathfrak{j}
,
the consistency relation
\mathfrak{p}_{\gamma,\mathfrak{D}_{\ell}}=\mathrm{id}
holds
modulo
G^{>\ell}
.
By Lemma
4.2
,
\mathfrak{p}_{\gamma,\mathfrak{D}_{\ell}}
acts on
\mathcal{S}_{q}(x)
.
By adding walls around
\mathfrak{j}
with wall elements

\Psi_{a,b}[n]^{c}
(
\deg n=\ell+1
) to
\mathfrak{D}_{\ell}
,
we have a scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}_{\ell+1}

such that
\mathfrak{p}_{\gamma,\mathfrak{D}_{\ell+1}}=\mathrm{id}
modulo
G^{>\ell+1}
.
Since we consider the action of
\Psi_{a,b}[n]^{c}
modulo
G^{>\ell+1}
,
the second expression of (
4.17
) is simplified as

(4.22)

\displaystyle x^{\tilde{m}}\biggl(1+c\frac{q^{2\langle n,\tilde{m}\rangle}-1}{q^{2a}-1}q^{a}q^{b}x^{\tilde{p}^{*}(n)}\biggr).

We may further concentrate on
\tilde{m}
with
\langle\delta(n)n,\tilde{m}\rangle=1
,
because other cases are propotional to it with factors that are independent of
\Psi_{a,b}[n]^{c}
.
In particular, the action of
\Psi_{1/\delta(n),0}[n]^{c}

on such
\tilde{m}
is given by

(4.23)

\displaystyle x^{\tilde{m}}(1+cq^{a}x^{\tilde{p}^{*}(n)}).

This means that,
for each
n\in N^{+}
(
\deg n=\ell+1
),
the (possibly multiple) added wall elements
\Psi_{a,b_{i}}[n]^{c_{i}}
(
i=1
, 2, …) in
\mathfrak{D}_{\ell+1}
is
replaced with a single wall element

\Psi_{1/\delta(n),0}[n]^{c}
; moreover, we have
c\in\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1/\delta_{0}}]
by the induction assumption.
Then, it is further decomposed
into a product of

\Psi_{1/\delta(n),b}[n]^{c}
’s (
c\in\mathbb{Z}
).
∎

5.
Application to positivity and nonpositivity of QCSDs

In the rest of the paper
we apply the pentagon relation,
together with the combinatorics of quantum data,
to study the positivity and nonpositivity of QCSDs.

5.1.
Positivity of QCSDs

Let us briefly summarize known results concerning the positivity and nonpositivity of CSDs and QCSDs.

Let us introduce the following notion.

Definition 5.1
.

A CSD
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}
is a
positive realization
if
the wall element of any wall has the form

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Psi[n]^{c\delta(n)}\quad(n\in N^{+},\ b\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z},\ c\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}).

We say that a CSD
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q}
is
positive

if it is equivalent to a CSD that is a positive realization.

The following fact is one of the main results of
[
GHKK18
]
.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
GHKK18
, Theorem 1.13]
)
.

Every CSD
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}
is positive.

For each CSD,
a
theta function
is defined by
broken lines

[
GHKK18
]
.
The definition of a broken line and Theorem
5.2
immediately imply
the following positivity result.

Corollary 5.3

(Positivity of theta functions
[
GHKK18
]
)
.

For any CSD
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}
, every nonzero coefficients of any theta function is
a positive integer.

Based on Proposition
4.3
,
we introduced a parallel notion for QCSDs.

Definition 5.4
.

A QCSD
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q}
is a
positive realization
if
the wall element of any wall has the form

(5.2)

\displaystyle\Psi_{1/\delta(n),b}[n]^{c}\quad(n\in N^{+},\ b\in(1/\delta_{0})\mathbb{Z},\ c\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}).

We say that a QCSD
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q}
is
positive

if it is equivalent to a QCSD that is a positive realization.

Example 5.5
.

All examples of QCSDs in Section
3
are positive realizations.

A
quantum theta function
for a QCSD
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q}
is defined by
broken lines

[
DM21
]

in the same way as in the classical one
[
GHKK18
]
.
The following fact follows from the definition of a broken line.

Proposition 5.6

(
[
DM21
, Theorem 3.9]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q}
is positive.
Then,
every quantum theta function of a QCSD
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q}

has a formal power series of the form

(5.3)

\displaystyle\theta_{Q,\tilde{m}_{0}}=x^{\tilde{m}_{0}}\biggl(1+\sum_{n\in N^{+}}c_{n}x^{\tilde{p}^{*}(n)}\biggr)\quad(c_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[q^{\pm 1/\delta_{0}}]).

Observing the parallelism between the CSDs and QCSDs so far,
it is natural to expect that Theorem
5.2
also holds for QCSDs.
However, this is not true in general.
We say that a QCSD is
skew-symmetric
if its initial exchange matrix
B
is skew-symmetric.
The following positivity result is known.

Theorem 5.7

(
[
DM21
, Theorem 2.15]
)
.

Any skew-symmetric QCSD
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q}
is positive.

The proof of
[
DM21
]
is based on the Donaldson-Thomas theory
on quiver-representations, which applies exclusively to the skew-symmetric case.

On the other hand, the following example suggests the abundance of the nonpositive QCSDs.

Example 5.8

(
[
LLRZ14
,
CFMM24
]
)
.

Consider a cluster algebra of rank 2 with the initial exchange matrix

(5.4)

\displaystyle B=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\delta_{1}\\
\delta_{2}&0\end{pmatrix}\quad(\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}).

The (classical)
greedy elements
were introduced in
[
LLZ12
]
.
Later they were identified with the theta functions
[
CGM+17
]
.
Meanwhile, the
quantum greedy elements
were studied in
[
LLRZ14
]
.
It turned out that the positivity of the coefficients of the quantum greedy elements
fails

for
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\allowbreak=(2,3)
,
(2,5)
,
(3,4)
,
(4,6)
, for example.
Motivated by this result,
the nonpositivity of
quantum theta functions
for
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})=(2,3)
was shown by
[
CFMM24
]
.
Thus, the corresponding QCSD is
nonpositive
due to Proposition
5.6
.
Also, it was conjectured
[
LLRZ14
, Conj. 14]
that
the positivity holds for the quantum greedy basis
if
\delta_{1}|\delta_{2}
or
\delta_{2}|\delta_{1}
.

Thus, there is an intriguing discrepancy of the positivity
between CSDs and QCSDs especially in the
nonskew-symmetric case
.
Then, we have the following natural questions.

Q1:

Exactly when and why does the positivity of a nonskew-symmetric QCSD
hold or fail?

Q2:

When the positivity fails for a QCSD, how
is the positivity restored in the classical limit
q\rightarrow 1
?

Below we study the above problems for the nonskew-symmetric QCSDs of rank 2
using the pentagon relation.
Before that, let us present one immediate and important consequence of the positivity of QCSDs.
We say that a CSD
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}
is
with minimal support
if

\mathrm{Supp}(\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}})
is minimal among all CSDs that are equivalent to
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}
.
If
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}
is a positive realization, then
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}
is with minimal support
because it is impossible to cancel the wall elements of any walls due to the positivity.

Proposition 5.9
.

Suppose that a QCSD
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q}
is a positive realization.
Then,
\mathrm{Supp}(\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q})
coincides with
\mathrm{Supp}(\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}})
,
where
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}
is a CSD with minimal support.

Proof.

By replacing each wall element
\Psi_{1/\delta(n),b}[n]^{c}
in
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{q}
with
\Psi[n]^{c\delta(n)}
,
we obtain a CSD
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathfrak{s}}
,
which is a positive realization.
∎

5.3.
Nonpositivity result

Consider a cluster algebra
of rank 2
such that its exchange matrix
B

has the form in (
5.4
).
As stated in Example
5.8
,
it was conjectured in
[
LLRZ14
, Conj. 14]
that the positivity
of the quantum greedy basis
holds if
\delta_{1}|\delta_{2}
or
\delta_{2}|\delta_{1}
.
The following result is motivated by the converse of this conjecture.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}^{q}_{\mathfrak{s}}
be a QCSD of rank 2
such that
b_{12}\neq 0
for
B
in (
2.1
).
Then,
\mathfrak{D}^{q}_{\mathfrak{s}}
is nonpositive if

\delta_{1}\nmid\delta_{2}
and
\delta_{2}\nmid\delta_{1}
.

Proof.

Let us show that under the condition the nonpositivity already emerges at degree 2
just as above Examples (a) and (d).
We continue to assume that
\{e_{2},e_{1}\}=1
.
By a similar consideration to the examples in Section
5.2
,
we have, modulo
G^{>2}
,

(5.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix}0\\

1\end{bmatrix}_{\frac{1}{\delta_{2}},0},\end{split}

(5.10)

\displaystyle b(i,j)=(2\delta_{1}\delta_{2}+(1-2j)\delta_{1}+(1-2i)\delta_{2})/\delta_{1}\delta_{2}.

Let

, and let

\delta^{\prime}_{2}=1

This means

\delta_{2}\mid\delta_{1}

&\quad\times\biggl(\,\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\prod_{p\geq 1}}\limits^{\longleftarrow}}[pn+(2p+1)n^{\prime}]_{c/4,pb+(2p+1)b^{\prime}}[(2p-1)n+4pn^{\prime}]_{c,(2p-1)b+4pb^{\prime}}\biggr)\\
&\quad\times[n^{\prime}]_{c/2,b^{\prime}-c/4}.\end{split}

Then, after a little manipulation, we obtain the desired expression
in the RHS of (
A.18
).
∎

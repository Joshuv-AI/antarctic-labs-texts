Isoperimetric sets in nonnegative scalar curvature and their role through various concepts of mass
Luca Benatti, Mattia Fogagnolo

Abstract
. We review some recent results about the relations among isoperimetric sets, Penrose inequalities and related concepts in the analysis of 3 3 -manifolds of nonnegative scalar curvature. We also show that if the isoperimetric sets of big volume have connected boundaries, the equivalence among suitable notions of mass hold.

Keywords
: isoperimetric sets, Penrose inequality, positive mass theorem, isoperimetric mass, isocapacitary mass, inverse mean curvature flow, nonnegative scalar curvature.

1.
Introduction

We will discuss topics gravitating around isoperimetric properties of Riemannian manifolds of dimension
3
with nonnegative scalar curvature that are asymptotically flat in some suitable sense, usually endowed with a closed, minimal and outermost boundary. With the latter adjective, we indicate that no other closed, minimal surface exists enclosing
\partial M
. We will occasionally refer to boundaries with these properties with the word
horizon
, or
horizon boundary
.

One of the classical results in this class of manifolds is the Riemannian Penrose inequality. Leaving all the discussion and the details to the main body of the work, we just point out that Penrose inequalities read as a bound from above of the area of the minimal boundary of
(M,g)
in terms of suitable global geometric invariants, which are interpreted as physical “global”
masses
. In
[
BFM22a
]
, together with Mazzieri, we showed an isoperimetric version of the Riemannian Penrose inequality holding in a very large class of manifolds. We will review such result focusing on its relation with the isoperimetric sets and on the techniques that led to the proof of their existence for any volume in this context
[
CCE16
]
. We will also deal with the equivalence of various, apparently very different, notions of mass. Particular attention will be put in being as sharp as possible in the decay requirements for the asymptotically flat condition on the manifolds considered.

In
section

2
, we review the main properties of the Inverse Mean Curvature Flow we are going to employ, mainly obtained by Huisken-Ilmanen
[
HI01
]
. In

-manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature, obtained by Carlotto-Chodosch-Eichmair

, after the fundamental insight of Shi

. In doing so, we sensibly weaken the decay assumptions on the metric to

-asymptotic flatness. In

[
Hui06
]
, we review the proof of the related isoperimetric Penrose inequality, obtained in collaboration with Mazzieri
[

]
. In
section

5
, we analyze the relations with other notions of mass, most notably the classical
\ADM

[

. This also gives us the occasion for discussing the physical relevance of these concepts. The other notions of mass that will be taken into account are the

]

where
\accentset{\circ}{\h}_{t}
is the trace-less second fundamental form of
\Sigma_{t}
. As
\mathrm{R}\geq 0
and

(2.14)

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\mathrm{R}^{\Sigma_{t}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{t}=4\pi\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\chi$}(\Sigma_{t})\leq 8\pi

by the

we have that the time derivative of the Hawking mass is nonnegative.

The analysis of the monotonicity in the weak formulation constitutes a central part of Huisken-Ilmanen’s work. However, once accepted the heuristic description of the level set flow given above, one realizes that, at jump times, part of the evolving boundary should be replaced by a piece of minimal surface, hence the

(Geroch monotonicity along the weak IMCF

.

Let

3.
Isoperimetry in nonnegative scalar curvature

3.1.
Nonnegative scalar curvature and the reverse isoperimetric inequality

To see clearly the role played by the monotonicity of the Hawking mass in isoperimetric issues, we focus on the case of manifolds without boundary. It is not difficult to conceive that, shrinking and shrinking an initial geodesic ball
B(r,o)
of radius
r
centered at a desired
o\in M
, one can build through a limiting procedure as
r\to 0^{+}
a weak IMCF originating from
o
. This will in turn be described by a function
w\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M\setminus\{o\})
solving in the weak sense
eq.

2.2
, with the boundary condition replaced by
w(x)\to-\infty
as
\mathrm{d}(x,o)\to 0^{+}
. This can actually be obtained with a similar argument as in
[
HI01
, Proposition 7.2]
. In fact, arguing as in
[
HI01
, Blowdown Lemma 7.1]
,
w(x)
can be shown to asymptotically behave as the Euclidean model
(n-1)\log(\mathrm{d}(x,o))
around
o
. In particular, one immediately observes that
\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\partial\Omega_{t})\to 0
when
\Omega_{t}=\left\{w\leq t\right\}
is approaching
o
, that is when
t\to-\infty
. Consequently, being
\mathfrak{m}_{H}
nondecreasing along the flow by
theorem

2.3
, recalling its expression
eq.

2.7
the level sets of
w
are seen to satisfy a
reverse Willmore inequality
, meaning

(3.1)

\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\H^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq 16\pi.

This suggests that the value of the Euclidean isoperimetric quotient of such sets are smaller than the value of the isoperimetric quotient of a round ball in flat
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This can be obtained, in the smooth case, as follows.

Let
v(t)={\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\Omega_{t}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}}
. Then, by the coarea formula and equation
eq.

2.2
solved by
w
, one has

(3.2)

v^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\frac{1}{\H}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

On the other hand, by Hölder’s inequality,

(3.3)

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial\Omega_{t}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}}=\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\H^{\alpha}\H^{-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq\left(\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\H^{\alpha p}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\H^{-\alpha\frac{p}{(p-1)}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}},

hence, letting
\alpha=2/3
and
p=3
, one finds out that

(3.4)

\left(\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\frac{1}{\H}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{-1}\leq\frac{\left(\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\H^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial\Omega_{t}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

Now, the numerator at the right-hand side is estimated by inequality
eq.

3.1
, while, as far as the denominator is concerned, we recall that the evolution forces the area
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial\Omega_{t}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}}
to be
\mathrm{e}^{t}
times a constant. This is due to formula
eq.

2.8
(see
[
HI01
, Lemma 1.6]
for the computation in the setting of weak solutions). By the asymptotic behaviour at the “pole” pointed out above, the constant is in fact
4\pi
, that is the area of the round unit
2
-sphere, so

(3.5)

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial\Omega_{t}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}}=4\pi\mathrm{e}^{t}.

Consequently, combining these pieces of information with inequality
eq.

3.4
and equation
eq.

3.2
we conclude

(3.6)

v^{\prime}(t)\geq 2\pi\mathrm{e}^{\frac{3}{2}t}.

Integrating from
t\to-\infty
(which corresponds to the pole) where both the volume and the area of the level sets vanishes, and taking into account again equality
eq.

3.5
, we get

(3.7)

\frac{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial\Omega_{t}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}}}{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\Omega_{t}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Omega_{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}}^{\frac{2}{3}}}\leq(36\pi)^{\frac{1}{3}}=\frac{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial B\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert B\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{\frac{2}{3}}},

where in the right-hand side
B
denotes the round ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
In other words, the level sets of

The following result of Shi

]

Theorem 3.1

(Shi’s reverse Isoperimetric inequality)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-asymptotically flat Riemannian
3
-manifold, satisfying

, and let
o\in M
. For
o\in M

be the weak IMCF issuing from

(3.8)

t(v)=\inf\left\{\tau\,\,\middle|\,\,{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\left\{w_{o}\leq t\right\}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\left\{w_{o}\leq t\right\}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\left\{w_{o}\leq t\right\}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\{w_{o}\leq t\right\}$}}\right\rvert$}}}\geq v\right\}.

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial\{w_{o}\leq t(v)\}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial\{w_{o}\leq t(v)\}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial\{w_{o}\leq t(v)\}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial\{w_{o}\leq t(v)\}$}}\right\rvert$}}}\leq(36\pi)^{\frac{1}{3}}v^{\frac{2}{3}}.

The scalar curvature influences the existence of isoperimetric sets substantially by means of the above result.

We find worth comparing the isoperimetric property of sublevel sets of the weak IMCF in nonnegative scalar curvature with those of the geodesic balls in nonnegative Ricci curvature.

3.2.
Comparison with nonnegative Ricci curvature

When the nonnegative scalar curvature assumption is strengthened to nonnegative Ricci curvature, we have that geodesic balls satisfy the very same reverse isoperimetric inequality
eq.

3.7
. This in turn relies on the basic Laplacian comparison and Bishop-Gromov theorems. Such classical results respectively state that, in a suitable weak sense, the mean curvature of a geodesic ball of radius
r
is bounded from above by
(n-1)/r
, while its area
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial B(r)$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial B(r)\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial B(r)$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial B(r)$}}\right\rvert$}}}
is controlled from above by
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.07002pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-3.07002pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$}}\right\rvert$}}}r^{n-1}
.

We focus on dimension
3
, in order to facilitate the comparison with
eq.

3.7
, although the argument works unchanged also in higher dimension.
Fix a point
o
in a manifold
(M,g)
endowed with a metric of nonnegative Ricci curvature
g
.
Letting
r(V)
be the radius of the ball
B(r(V))
centered in
o
of volume
V
, it can be easily computed, through classical variation formulas, that

(3.10)

r^{\prime}(V)=\frac{1}{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial B(r(V))$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial B(r(V))\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial B(r(V))$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial B(r(V))$}}\right\rvert$}}}}\geq\frac{1}{4\pi r(V)^{2}},

where the inequality is the Bishop-Gromov control on
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial B(r)$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial B(r)\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial B(r)$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial B(r)$}}\right\rvert$}}}
.
Integrating such inequality, one gets

(3.11)

r(V)\geq\left(\frac{3}{4\pi}V\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}.

On the other hand, we have

(3.12)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial B(r(V))$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial B(r(V))\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial B(r(V))$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial B(r(V))$}}\right\rvert$}}}=\frac{1}{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial B(r(V))$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial B(r(V))\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial B(r(V))$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial B(r(V))$}}\right\rvert$}}}}\int_{\partial B(r(V))}\H\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq\frac{2}{r(V)}\leq\frac{2}{V^{\frac{1}{3}}}\left(\frac{4\pi}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},

where the first inequality is due to the Laplacian comparison and the second one to
eq.

3.11
. Integrating this other differential inequality, we conclude

(3.13)

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial B(r)$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial B(r)\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial B(r)$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial B(r)$}}\right\rvert$}}}\leq(36\pi)^{\frac{1}{3}}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle B(r)$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert B(r)\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle B(r)$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle B(r)$}}\right\rvert$}}}^{\frac{2}{3}}

for any radius
r\geq 0
. Observe that (
3.13
) fully mirrors
eq.

3.7
, but for a different exhaustion. It should be clear that no particular features of the dimension three have been exploited here, contrarily to the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem
eq.

2.14
utilized to infer the monotonicity of the Hawking mass, that in turn led to
eq.

3.7

The reverse isoperimetric inequality in nonnegative Ricci curvature was first pointed out by Morgan-Johnson

]

3.3.
Isoperimetric analysis on manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature

The following is a version of Nardulli’s generalized compactness principle
[
Nar14
]
, crafted for an asymptotically flat framework. It substantially implies that in an isoperimetric minimizing sequence the portion of volume that is escaping at infinity is in fact converging to a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The sublevel sets of the IMCF satisfying, as previously explained, a reverse isoperimetric inequality, can thus be exploited to replace such runaway volume and provide an isoperimetric set. In the remainder of this work, given an ambient manifold
M
, we denote with
I
its isoperimetric profile, defined as the function
I:[0,{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle M$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert M\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle M$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle M$}}\right\rvert$}}})\to[0,\infty)
given

(3.14)

I(V)=\inf\left\{P(E)\,\,\middle|\,\,E\subset M,\,{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}}=V\right\}.

Isoperimetric sets
E_{V}
of volume
V
are exactly those satisfying
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E_{V}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E_{V}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E_{V}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E_{V}$}}\right\rvert$}}}=I(V)
.

Theorem 3.2

(Asymptotic decomposition of Isoperimetric minimizing sequences)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth,
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-asymptotically flat Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with Ricci curvature bounded from below, and let
I
be its isoperimetric profile. Then, for any
V>0
, there exists a possibly empty,
bounded

E\subset M
and a ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
V={\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}}+{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert B\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}}
and

(3.15)

I(V)={\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}}+{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial B\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}}.

The version above is actually a consequence of the way more general
[

.

Clearly, the number of the balls drifting away being at most one is due to the fact that a union of balls in

is manifestly isoperimetrically less convenient than a single one.

Remark 3.3
.

Nardulli’s earlier work has been recently vastly exploited and empowered in the context of possibly nonsmooth metric spaces with Ricci lower bounds (see
[

,
Ant+22
,

,

We can now present the overall argument leading to the existence of isoperimetric sets. By
theorem

3.2
, the only way a minimizing sequence for the isoperimetric problem at volume

may fail to provide an isoperimetric set is by losing part of its volume at infinity, that, on the other hand, by the asymptotic flatness converges to a round ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. However, the nonnegative scalar curvature, by
theorem

, determines that a sublevel set

of a weak IMCF emanating from some point

, enclosing the same volume as

.
Thus, if such
B
actually exists, than

would provide an isoperimetric set of volume

sitting in

.

Remark 3.4
.

In the case of nonnegative Ricci curvature, a scheme like this has been worked out in
[

]
, taking advantage of the isoperimetric properties of the geodesic balls described in

. Variations on this argument have been proposed also in
[

]
and in
[

]
.

There is a last issue to be taken into account before safely running the above argument. As already pointed out, the weak IMCF can jump, and in particular there could be some value

such that for no

the set

encloses a volume exactly equal to

. This may cause trouble in case such volume is exactly the volume of the ball at infinity that we would like to replace. This problem is bypassed by the strict monotonicity of the isoperimetric profile, holding when

is endowed with a minimal outermost boundary.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-asymptotically flat Riemannian

We first of all recall that the isoperimetric profile is continuous in this case, see
[

]

V

theorem

3.2
, we know that

(3.16)

I(V)={\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}}+{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial B\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}

for a possibly empty
E\subset M
and for a Euclidean ball
B
satisfying
V={\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}}+{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert B\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
. Clearly,
E
must be isoperimetric for its own volume, i.e.
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}}=I({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}})
, and in particular it is smooth. Moreover, we recall that on a Riemannian manifold isoperimetric sets are bounded anytime an Euclidean-like isoperimetric inequality is available, at least for small volumes (see e.g.
[
AFP21
, Theorem B.1]
). In particular, this holds in our case: due to asymptotic flatness, an uniform Euclidean-like isoperimetric inequality can be checked to hold outside some compact set, so
[
PST14
, Theorem 3.2]
ensures its validity on the whole manifold with boundary. We can thus infer that
E
is bounded.

We assume first that
E
is nonempty. We perform inward variations
E_{t}
of
E
obtained through a normal deformation with a velocity such that, at
t=0
, is given by
\varphi\nu_{\partial E}
, for a nonpositive
\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(E\setminus\partial M)
, and where
\nu_{\partial E}
points towards infinity. For a fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we let
t(\varepsilon)
be such that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{1.97223pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-1.97223pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E_{t(\varepsilon)}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E_{t(\varepsilon)}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.45557pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.45557pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E_{t(\varepsilon)}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.11111pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.11111pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E_{t(\varepsilon)}$}}\right\rvert$}}}={\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}}-\varepsilon
. We can thus compute

\begin{split}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{I(V)-I(V-\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}&\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}}-{\mathchoice{\raisebox{1.97223pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-1.97223pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E_{t(\varepsilon)}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E_{t(\varepsilon)}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.45557pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.45557pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E_{t(\varepsilon)}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.11111pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.11111pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E_{t(\varepsilon)}$}}\right\rvert$}}}}{\varepsilon}=t^{\prime}(\varepsilon)_{|\varepsilon=0}\int_{\partial E}\varphi\H_{E}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\\
&=\frac{\int_{\partial E}\varphi\H_{E}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\int_{\partial E}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\sigma}=\H_{E},\end{split}

where
\H_{E}
is the
constant
mean curvature of
\partial E\setminus\partial M
. In the first inequality in
section

3.3
, we used

(3.17)

I(V-\varepsilon)\leq{\mathchoice{\raisebox{1.97223pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-1.97223pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E_{t(\varepsilon)}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E_{t(\varepsilon)}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.45557pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.45557pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E_{t(\varepsilon)}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.11111pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.11111pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E_{t(\varepsilon)}$}}\right\rvert$}}}+{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial B\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}},

holding because the sets
E_{\varepsilon}\cup B_{j}
, with
B_{j}\subset M
satisfying
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.31946pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.31946pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle B_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert B_{j}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle B_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.45885pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.45885pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle B_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}}={\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert B\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
approaching the round ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
in the
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-topology as

, form a valid family of competitors for the isoperimetric problem of volume
V-\varepsilon
. The monotonicity of
I
thus follows by showing that the constant
\H_{E}
is strictly positive. To see this, we can argue as in
[
BFM22a
, Lemma 2.8]
, after
[
HI01
, Remark, p. 394]
. Namely, one can flow a geodesic ball in the asymptotic region, that is mean-convex by
[
BFM22b
, Lemma 4.3]
, through the Mean Curvature Flow of mean-convex surfaces with surgery in Riemannian
3
-manifolds
[
BH18
]
; as proved in such paper, this flow smoothly converges to the outermost minimal boundary
\partial M
. One can then find a surface in the evolution “touching”
\partial E\setminus\partial M
. If
\H_{E}
were nonpositive, this would result in a contradiction with the maximum principle. This completes the proof in neighbourhoods of volumes
V
such that
E
as above is nonempty.

In case
E
were empty, deforming
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
inwardly as above yields
section

3.3
, this time in terms of the mean curvature of
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that is obviously strictly positive. This concludes the proof.
∎

With
theorem

3.1
,
theorem

3.2
and
proposition

3.5
at hand, we can prove that isoperimetric sets exist in any
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-asymptotically flat Riemannian

-manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature and horizon boundary, for any volume, provided some lower bound on the Ricci curvature is in force. This is a refinement of
[

, Proposition K.1]
. Useful insights about the strategy employed were actually proposed by Brendle-Chodosh
[
BC14
]
, including the key computations leading to
theorem

3.1
.

Theorem 3.6

(Existence of isoperimetric sets in nonnegative scalar curvature)
.

Let
(M,g)
be
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-asymptotically flat Riemannian
3
-manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature and with Ricci curvature bounded from below, endowed with a closed, minimal outermost boundary. Then, for any
V>0
, there exists an isoperimetric set of volume
V
.

Let
V>0
. As above, we have

(3.18)

I(V)={\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}}+{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial B\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial B$}}\right\rvert$}}}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}

for a possibly empty
E\subset M
and for a round ball
B
in flat
\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying
V={\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}}+{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert B\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
. We can assume that
B
is nonempty, otherwise
E
is already the isoperimetric set of volume
V
sought for.

Let now
o\in M
be far away from the boundary. We can assume that there exists a weak IMCF
w_{o}
issuing from
o
, although this is not obtained through flows of hypersurfaces homologous to
\partial M
, as
theorem

2.2
would require. In fact, we can attach a cap to
\partial M
, and extend smoothly the metric
g
to this new complete manifold without boundary
[
PV20
]
. Then,
theorem

2.2
, coupled with the limiting procedure already mentioned above, yields a weak IMCF
w_{o}
issuing from
o
. It is known that, in our assumptions,
H_{2}(M,\partial M,\mathbb{Z})=\left\{0\right\}
, see e.g.
[
BFM22a
, Lemma 2.8]
and the references contained in the proof there, consequently, the manifold without boundary obtained attaching the cap and extending the metric satisfies
H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})=\left\{0\right\}
.

By
[
MRS22
, Theorem 1.3]
, there exist functions
f_{1}f_{2},:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
growing to infinity at infinity, and such that

(3.19)

f_{1}(\mathrm{d}(x,o))\leq w_{o}(x)\leq f_{2}(\mathrm{d}(x,o))

for any
o\in M
.
Such result requires a Ricci lower bound and the validity of a global, possibly weighted Sobolev inequality. In the
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-asymptotically flat case this holds with no weight, as a direct consequence of the uniform Euclidean-like isoperimetric inequality that can be directly obtained outside some sufficiently large compact set, then, by
[
PST14
, Theorem 3.2]
, on the whole manifold.

In light of
eq.

3.19
, given
v>0
we can always find
o
sufficiently far in space such that
\{w_{o}\leq t(v)\}
is disjoint from the bounded
E
and
\partial M
. Since such sublevel set is contained in a nonnegative scalar curved region of a boundaryless Riemannian manifold satisfying

, we can apply
theorem

3.1
and infer that Shi’s reverse isoperimetric inequality

3.9
holds for
t(v)
. Choose now
v={\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert B\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle B$}}\right\rvert$}}}
, and
o
as above. Let
F=\left\{w_{o}\leq t(v)\right\}
, and observe that, by definition,
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert F\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}}\geq v
. Now, if
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert F\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}}=v
, then by (
3.9
) the set

is isoperimetric of volume

. If instead
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert F\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}}>v
, then by the strict monotonicity of

shown in
proposition

3.5
and (

) we have

(3.20)

I({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}}+{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert F\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}})>I({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}}+v)=I(V)={\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}}+(36\pi)^{\frac{1}{3}}v^{\frac{2}{3}}.

On the other hand, we also have

(3.21)

I({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E$}}\right\rvert$}}}+{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert F\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle F$}}\right\rvert$}}})\leq{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}}+{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial F\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial F$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial F$}}\right\rvert$}}}\leq{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E$}}\right\rvert$}}}+(36\pi)^{\frac{1}{3}}v^{\frac{2}{3}},

where we used again (
3.9
). The chaining of (
3.20
) with (

) leads to a contradiction, that concludes the proof.
∎

4.
The isoperimetric mass and the isoperimetric Penrose inequality

So far, we did not mention any particular example of
3
-manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature and minimal, outermost boundary. Let us focus now on the archetypal one, that will actually constitute the model for the geometric inequalities that we are going to present. The Schwarzschild manifold of dimension
3
of (positive) mass
\mathfrak{m}
is the space
\mathbb{R}^{3}\smallsetminus\left\{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert x\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}}<2\mathfrak{m}\right\}
endowed with the rotationally symmetric metric

(4.1)

g=\left(1+\frac{\mathfrak{m}}{2{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert x\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}}}\right)^{4}\delta_{ij}\,\mathrm{d}x^{i}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{j}.

This Riemannian manifold has zero scalar curvature, the boundary
\partial M=\{{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert x\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}}=2\mathfrak{m}}\}
is minimal, and, since any other level set of
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert x\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}}
has constant mean curvature, such boundary is also outermost, since the presence of any other closed minimal surface would result in a contradiction with the Maximum Principle.

It is known from early work of Bray
[
Bra97
]
, later generalized in various directions
[
BM02
,
Bre13
]
, that the isoperimetric sets in this warped products are only the Euclidean annuli given by
E_{R}=\{2\mathfrak{m}\leq{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert x\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}}\leq R\}
. Coherently with Shi’s reverse isoperimetric inequality and with the fact that balls form an Inverse Mean Curvature Flow, one checks that

(4.2)

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E_{R}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}}-\frac{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E_{R}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}}{6\sqrt{\pi}}\geq 0.

Moreover, the quantity at the left-hand side is seen to be asymptotic, as
R\to+\infty
, to
R^{2}
. Interestingly enough, one computes that the mass parameter
\mathfrak{m}
is in fact recovered as the limit

(4.3)

\mathfrak{m}=\limsup_{R\to+\infty}\frac{2}{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E_{R}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}}}\left({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E_{R}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}}-\frac{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E_{R}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E_{R}$}}\right\rvert$}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}}{6\sqrt{\pi}}\right).

These observations serve as a motivation for the notion of
isoperimetric mass
, first introduced by Huisken
[
Hui06
]
.

Definition 4.1

(Isoperimetric mass)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian
3
-manifold possibly with boundary, and with infinite volume. Then, its
isoperimetric mass
is defined as

(4.4)

\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}=\sup_{(\Omega_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}}\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\frac{2}{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial\Omega$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial\Omega\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial\Omega$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial\Omega$}}\right\rvert$}}}}\left({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\Omega$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Omega$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Omega$}}\right\rvert$}}}-\frac{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial\Omega$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial\Omega\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial\Omega$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial\Omega$}}\right\rvert$}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}}{6\sqrt{\pi}}\right),

where the supremum is taken among all exhaustions
(\Omega_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
consisting of bounded domains with
\mathscr{C}^{1,\alpha}
-boundary.

It is immediately checked from the above definition that, if an exhaustion consists of isoperimetric sets, this automatically realizes the required supremum. In particular, if the scalar curvature is nonnegative, the following holds.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
3
-dimensional
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-asymptotically flat Riemannian manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature and Ricci curvature bounded from below, having a closed, outermost minimal boundary.
Then, we have

(4.5)

5.
The
\ADM
mass and comparison with other masses

In order to better understand the heuristics behind the definition of the
{\ADM}
mass below, we start by recalling why manifolds fulfilling the assumptions considered above are so natural in the context of General Relativity. This groundbreaking physical theory is formulated in the context of Lorentzian geometry and, specifically, in a
4
-manifold
(L,\mathfrak{g})
satisfying the
vacuum
Einstein Field Equations

(5.1)

\Ric_{\mathfrak{g}}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{R}_{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathrm{T},

where
\mathrm{T}
is called
stress-energy
tensor and should be thought of as a physical datum. A largely accepted assumption on
\mathrm{T}
is the
dominant energy condition
, amounting simply to
\mathrm{T}(V,V)\geq 0
for any
timelike
vector
V
, that is
\mathfrak{g}(V,V)<0
. Consider now a
spacelike

3
-hypersurface
(M,g)
, with
g
induced by
\mathfrak{g}
, that is
\mathfrak{g}(X,X)=g(X,X)>0
for any
X\in T_{p}M
and any
p\in M
. We also consider the simplest case of a
time symmetric

(M,g)
, consisting in the vanishing of the second fundamental form of the immersion
M\hookrightarrow L
. By means of the Gauss-Codazzi equations,
eq.

5.1
, coupled with the dominant energy condition
\mathrm{T}\geq 0
induces on
(M,g)
the identity

(5.2)

\mathrm{R}_{g}=16\pi\rho\geq 0,

where
\rho=\mathrm{T}(V,V)
for some timelike vector
V
, hence the inequality follows from the dominant energy condition. We address the reader to
[
Car21
, Section 1.1]
for a detailed derivation of
eq.

5.2
.

The
event horizon
of a
black hole
in
(L,\mathfrak{g})
manifests itself in
(M,g)
as a minimal outermost boundary. Finally, when modeling an
isolated gravitational system
, it becomes natural to assume some kind of asymptotic flatness. With a physical language, this condition says that the gravitational field is not influenced by the presence of some mass “at infinity”.
We address the interested reader to
[
HE73
,
Lee19
]
for a comprehensive treatment of these relativistic concepts.

5.1.
The
\ADM
mass

To get an idea of the
\ADM
mass, we briefly stick with the more familiar Newtonian framework.
Suppose to have a mass density
\rho
in a
3
D-time snapshot represented by an isolated gravitational system
(M,g)
. Newton’s formulation models the gravitational field by a function
V
called
gravitational potential
, which satisfies the relation
\Delta V=4\pi\rho
. Hence, using the divergence theorem, one can reconstruct the total mass of the system by observing the effect of the gravitational potential at infinity, since

(5.3)

\mathfrak{m}=\int_{M}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{M}\frac{\Delta V}{4\pi}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\int_{\partial B_{r}}\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

On the other hand, we just recalled that, in Einstein’s formulation, the gravitational field is modelled by a metric
g
on
M
which is only constrained (in the time-symmetric case) by the equation
\mathrm{R}_{g}=16\pi\rho
. One then would like to compute the system’s total mass simply by knowing the metric in this case as well. A first idea may be to integrate the quantity
\mathrm{R}_{g}/16\pi
as we did in the Newtonian case. However, this approach has at least two main issues. The first one is that, even in a special paradigmatic case such as the Schwarzschild manifold, the total mass would be zero, which does not correspond to what we expect. The second problem is that the “superposition principle” does not hold, since the scalar curvature is not a linear operator of
g
. In the case
(M,g)
is close to the Euclidean flat space, one could try to circumvent this by replacing the scalar curvature with its first-order linearization, that is

(5.4)

\mathrm{R}_{g}\approx\mathrm{R}_{\delta}+\mathrm{D}\kern 0.28436pt\mathrm{R}|_{\delta}(g-\delta)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}\mathrm{R}(\delta+\varepsilon(g-\delta))\big|_{\varepsilon=0},

since
\mathrm{R}_{\delta}=0
. Observe that, in the above formula,
h=\delta+\varepsilon(g-\delta)
is still a metric for
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\varepsilon$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\varepsilon\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\varepsilon$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\varepsilon$}}\right\rvert$}}}
small enough,
thus it makes sense to compute the scalar curvature of
h
, which is

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{h}

\displaystyle=h^{ij}(\partial_{k}H^{k}_{ij}-\partial_{i}H^{k}_{kj}+H^{k}_{ij}H^{s}_{ks}-H^{k}_{is}H^{s}_{jk})

(5.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\delta^{ij}\delta^{ks}\partial_{k}(-\partial_{s}g_{ij}+\partial_{j}g_{is}+\partial_{i}g_{js})-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\delta^{ij}\delta^{ks}\partial_{i}(-\partial_{s}g_{kj}+\partial_{j}g_{ks}+\partial_{k}g_{js})+O_{1}(\varepsilon^{2})

(5.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\delta^{ij}\delta^{ks}\partial_{k}(-\partial_{s}g_{ij}+2\partial_{j}g_{is})-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\delta^{ij}\delta^{ks}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}g_{ks}+O_{1}(\varepsilon^{2})

(5.8)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\delta^{ij}\delta^{ks}(\partial_{k}\partial_{i}g_{js}-\partial_{s}\partial_{k}g_{ij})+O_{1}(\varepsilon^{2})

where
H
are the Christoffel symbols of
h
. We are employing the normal coordinates of flat
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Hence,

(5.9)

\mathrm{R}_{g}\approx\delta^{ij}\delta^{ks}(\partial_{k}\partial_{i}g_{js}-\partial_{k}\partial_{s}g_{ij}).

By means of the divergence theorem, one has

(5.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{M}\mathrm{R}_{g}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{g}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{M}\delta^{ij}\delta^{ks}(\partial_{k}\partial_{i}g_{js}-\partial_{k}\partial_{s}g_{ij})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\delta}

(5.11)

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\partial B_{r}}\delta^{ij}(\partial_{i}g_{jk}-\partial_{k}g_{ij})\frac{x^{k}}{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert x\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\delta}

(5.12)

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\partial B_{r}}g^{ij}(\partial_{i}g_{jk}-\partial_{k}g_{ij})\nu^{k}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}.

The quantity appearing on the right-hand side is called Arnowitt-Deser-Misner’s mass
\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}

[
ADM61
]
.
If the metric
g
is sufficiently close to the flat metric, the above computation shows that the integral of the scalar curvature is approximated by such mass. Clearly, the two quantities are not, in general, the same, since the first one depends on the global behaviour of the metric, while the second one depends only on its behaviour at large distances. On the other hand, it has been showed in
[
ADM61
]
that, in a suitable asymptotic setting, the finiteness of the one is equivalent implies the finiteness of the other. To see this, one can consider the vector field,

(5.13)

Y^{k}=g^{kl}g^{ij}(\partial_{i}g_{jl}-\partial_{l}g_{ij})=g^{ij}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}-g^{ik}\Gamma^{j}_{ij},

inspired by the fact that, formally,
\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\int_{\partial B_{r}}\langle Y,\nu\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}
. This suggests to compute the divergence of
Y
with respect to
g
, that is

\begin{split}\mathrm{div}Y&=\partial_{k}Y^{k}+\Gamma^{l}_{lk}Y^{k}\\
&=\partial_{k}g^{ij}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}+g^{ij}\partial_{k}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}-\partial_{k}g^{ik}\Gamma^{j}_{ij}-g^{ik}\partial_{k}\Gamma^{j}_{ij}+g^{ij}\Gamma^{l}_{lk}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}-g^{ik}\Gamma^{l}_{lk}\Gamma^{j}_{ij}\\
&=\mathrm{R}_{g}+\partial_{k}g^{ij}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}-\partial_{k}g^{ik}\Gamma^{j}_{ij}.\end{split}

The asymptotic conditions we assume are

(5.14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}^{-1}\delta\leq g\leq\mathrm{C}\delta

\displaystyle\text{ in $M\smallsetminus B_{R}$},

\displaystyle\int_{M\smallsetminus B_{R}}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial g$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial g\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial g$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial g$}}\right\rvert$}}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\delta}<+\infty

and we show that the limit defining the
\ADM
mass exists. Appealing to the divergence theorem and to
section

5.1
, we have

(5.15)

\displaystyle{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.31946pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.31946pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{r}}{\mathchoice{\left\langle{Y\,}\middle|{\,\nu}\right\rangle}{\langle{Y\,}|{\,\nu}\rangle}{\scriptstyle\langle{Y\,}|{\,\nu}\rangle}{\scriptscriptstyle\langle{Y\,}|{\,\nu}\rangle}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}-\int_{B_{s}\smallsetminus B_{r}}{\mathchoice{\left\langle{Y\,}\middle|{\,\nu}\right\rangle}{\langle{Y\,}|{\,\nu}\rangle}{\scriptstyle\langle{Y\,}|{\,\nu}\rangle}{\scriptscriptstyle\langle{Y\,}|{\,\nu}\rangle}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}-\int_{B_{s}\smallsetminus 5.2.
Equivalence between masses

A large amount of literature deals with geometric inequalities involving the
\ADM
mass. Most notably, Schoen-Yau’s Positive Mass Theorem
[
SY79
,
SY81
]
, ensures, under suitable decay conditions on the metric, that
\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}\geq 0
in nonnegatively scalar curved complete manifolds, and that it vanishes only on flat
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Solving a special case of a conjecture of Penrose
[
Pen73
]
, Huisken-Ilmanen
[
HI01
]
sharpened such result in the connected boundary case, obtaining the (Riemannian) Penrose inequality

(5.20)

\sqrt{\frac{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial M$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial M\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial M$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial M$}}\right\rvert$}}}}{16\pi}}\leq\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}.

With the description given above of
\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}
as an actual (candidate) for a global physical mass, the validity of the (Riemannian) Penrose inequality should appear more natural. In fact, the Schwarzschild metric, where the inequality is checked to hold as an equality, represents a space where the whole matter is shielded by the horizon
\partial M
. Consequently, any other space should have a larger global mass, in relation with the area of the boundary, as
eq.

5.20
actually states.

Huisken-Ilmanen’s proof of
eq.

5.20
exploits their
theorem

2.3
coupled with an involved asymptotic analysis resulting in
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\partial\Omega_{t})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}
, along the weak IMCF of the horizon boundary. Bray
[
Bra01
]
, with a completely different proof based on an
ad hoc
developed conformal flow of metrics, removed the assumption of connected boundary. In both approaches, the decay assumptions are strictly stronger than in
definition

5.1
t namely
\mathscr{C}^{1}_{1}
-asymptotically flatness and
\Ric\geq-\mathrm{C}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert x\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle x$}}\right\rvert$}}}^{-2}
in
[
HI01
]
, and
\mathscr{C}^{2}_{1}
-asymptotically flatness in
[
Bra01
]
.

The question about the validity of
eq.

5.20
in the optimal
\mathscr{C}^{1}_{\tau}
-asymptotically flat regime arises then naturally, and has been answered in collaboration with Mazzieri
[
BFM22a
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Another even more natural (and more general) question regards the relation between
\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}
. Sharpening the earlier result envisioned by Huisken
[
Hui06
]
and rigorously obtained (and strengthened) by Jauregui-Lee
[

]
, we do obtain also
[

, Theorem 4.13]
the stronger conclusion

(5.21)

\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}=\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}

in
\mathscr{C}^{1}_{\tau}
-asymptotically flat Riemannian manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature and horizon boundary, for
\tau>1/2
. We observe that such identity provides also a proof of the well-posedness of
\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}
in this asymptotic regime completely different from the ones of Bartnik and Chrùsciel
[
Bar86
,
Chr86
]
, in the case of nonnegative scalar curvature. Indeed, the isoperimetric mass is manifestly independent on the choice of the coordinate chart.

The proofs of both the Penrose inequality
eq.

5.20
and of
eq.

5.21
under optimal decay assumptions in
[
BFM22a
]
are based on some new insights about the asymptotic behaviour of the weak IMCF. The sharpening of the decay assumptions in the Penrose inequality substantially stems from the asymptotic comparison
eq.

4.9
and on its relation with the asymptotic behaviour of the harmonic functions that takes advantage of the potential-theoretic version of the Hawking mass introduced in
[
AMO21
]
. The equality between the two masses
eq.

5.21
fundamentally relies also on a combination with Huisken-Jauregui-Lee argument
[
Hui06
,
JL19
]
building on the Mean Curvature Flow.

In what follows, we
outline an alternative strategy devised by Chodosh-Eichmair-Shi-Yu
[
Cho+21
]
that can be useful to prove
eq.

5.21

Before going on, we point out that the inequality

(5.22)

\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}\leq\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}

can be obtained in the optimal asymptotic regime through a direct yet not trivial computation that has been carried out in
[
FST09
]
, so we will deal with the reverse one only.
A key observation is the following, deduced by the proof of
[
Cho+21
, Theorem C.1]
. When isoperimetric sets are known to exist, as in the setting of
theorem

3.6
, they do realize, in the limit of infinite volume, by

4.2
, the isoperimetric mass. Since each of them has obviously constant mean curvature, the asymptotic comparison argument carried out in
[
BFM22a
]
can be reversed in some sense, allowing to estimate the isoperimetric mass
from above
with the Hawking mass. This inspiring idea is expressed in the computation
section

5.2
.

Starting from the next proposition and for all results up to the end of
section

5
, we will drop the outermost condition on the boundary.
This is due to known properties of asymptotically flat
3
-manifolds of nonnegative scalar curvature, see
[
BFM22a
, Lemma 2.8]
, asserting, in particular, that if the manifold contains minimal surfaces, then one can find a minimal, outermost
\Sigma
enclosing
\partial M
and the analysis is applied on the new manifold with the minimal outermost boundary
\Sigma
. The masses are obviously the same as those of the original manifold. If
(M,g)
possesses no closed minimal surfaces, and, in particular, it is boundaryless, then one can safely work in such complete manifold.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete
\mathscr{C}^{1}_{\tau}
-asymptotically flat Riemannian
3
-manifold, for
\tau>1/2
, with nonnegative scalar curvature and (possibly empty) closed, minimal boundary. Assume that there exists
V_{0}>0
such that, for any
V\geq V_{0}
, there exists an isoperimetric set

of volume
V
with connected boundary. Then,

(5.23)

\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}=\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}.

As already pointed out, we only have to show
\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}\leq\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}
.
Assume
\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}<+\infty

\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}>0

[
BFM22a
, Theorem 1.3]
implies that
(M,g)
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the result trivially holds.

Let

(M,g)
, and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}&=\limsup_{V\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}(E_{V})=\limsup_{V\to+\infty}\frac{2}{I(V)}\left(V-\frac{I(V)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{6\sqrt{\pi}}\right)\\

,

5.2

.

Let then
(V_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

5.2

5.24

that in
\mathscr{C}^{1}_{\tau}
-asymptotically flat
3
-manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature and closed minimal boundary there holds

\mathfrak{m}^{{\text{\tiny$(p)$}}}_{\iso}=\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}=\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}

for any

. This was already known for

only, under the stronger assumption of harmonic flatness at infinity
[

, Corollary 8]
, meaning that

outside a suitable compact set, where

is harmonic with respect to the Euclidean Laplacian.

Here, we observe that the same assumption of connectedness of isoperimetric sets considered in

allows to get the identity
\mathfrak{m}^{{\text{\tiny$(p)$}}}_{\iso}=\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}
, for
1<p\leq 2
, in
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-asymptotically flat manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature and minimal boundary. This is particularly interesting due to the fact that, in this asymptotic regime, the notion of
\mathfrak{m}_{\ADM}
is not a priori available, so one cannot go through computations involving its expression.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-asymptotically flat Riemannian
3
-manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature and (possibly empty) closed, minimal boundary. Assume that there exists
V_{0}>0
such that, for any
V\geq V_{0}
, there exists an isoperimetric set
E_{V}
of volume
V
with connected boundary. Then,

(5.32)

\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}=\mathfrak{m}^{{\text{\tiny$(p)$}}}_{\iso}

for every
1<p\leq 2
.

The inequality
\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}\geq\mathfrak{m}^{{\text{\tiny$(p)$}}}_{\iso}
has already been pointed out to hold more in general, so we focus on the reverse one
\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}\leq\mathfrak{m}^{{\text{\tiny$(p)$}}}_{\iso}
.

For every
V>0
large enough, denote
E_{V}
the isoperimetric set of volume
V
. Assume that
\mathfrak{m}^{{\text{\tiny$(p)$}}}_{\iso}<+\infty
and that that
\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}>0
, otherwise
[
BFM22a
, Theorem 1.3]
implies that
(M,g)
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the conclusion trivially holds. Along a sequence
(V_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
realising the superior limit in
section

5.2
, there holds
\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\partial E_{V_{k}})\geq 0
. Evolve
E_{V_{k}}
using the weak IMCF and denote
E_{V_{k}}^{t}
its sublevels. We briefly point out that, since the isoperimetric sets are not known to be homologous to the boundary, the evolving hypersurfaces may in principle touch the boundary
\partial M
. If this happens, one should consider the weak IMCF
with jumps
, that is, its modification described in
[
HI01
, Section 6]
. By
eq.

4.9
and the Geroch monotonicity formula,
theorem

2.3
, we have

(5.33)

\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\partial E_{V_{k}})\leq\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\partial E_{V_{k}}^{t})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}.

Observe now that
\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\partial E_{V_{k}}^{t})\geq 0
and, for large
t
, the set
E_{V_{k}}^{t}
has a connected boundary which is homologous to
\partial M
. Applying
eq.

5.30
, we have

(5.34)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{p}(\partial E^{t}_{V_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.93669pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.93669pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E^{t}_{V_{k}}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.37224pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.37224pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.02779pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.02779pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}}}{4\pi}\right)^{\mathrlap{\frac{3-p}{2}}}\kern 5.69046pt{\vphantom{F}}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3-p}{p-1},\frac{2}{p-1};1-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\partial E^{t}_{V_{k}}}\H^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{\mathrlap{-(p-1)}}\kern 19.91684pt\leq\left(\frac{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.93669pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.93669pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E^{t}_{V_{k}}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.37224pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.37224pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.02779pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.02779pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}}}{4\pi}\right)^{\mathrlap{\frac{3-p}{2}}},

since
{\vphantom{F}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3-p}{p-1},\frac{2}{p-1};t\right)\geq 1
if

.
In particular, this implies

(5.35)

\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}(E_{V_{k}}^{t})\leq\liminf_{t\to+\infty}p\frac{1}{2p\pi\mathfrak{c}_{p}(\partial E_{V_{k}}^{t})^{\frac{2}{3-p}}}\left({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.93669pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.93669pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E_{V_{k}}^{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E_{V_{k}}^{t}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.37224pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.37224pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E_{V_{k}}^{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.02779pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.02779pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E_{V_{k}}^{t}$}}\right\rvert$}}}-\frac{4\pi}{3}\mathfrak{c}_{p}(\partial E_{V_{k}}^{t})^{\frac{3}{3-p}}\right)\leq p\mathfrak{m}^{{\text{\tiny$(p)$}}}_{\iso}.

Combining
eqs.

5.33
and
5.35
, we get that
\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\partial E_{V_{k}})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}^{{\text{\tiny$(p)$}}}<+\infty
.

Suppose now that, up to a subsequence,
I^{\prime}(V_{k})^{2}I(V_{k})\leq 16\pi-\varepsilon
for some positive

and every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we would have

\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\partial E_{V_{k}})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{32\pi}\sqrt{I(V_{k})},

that diverges, since the isoperimetric constant is positive, contradicting
\mathfrak{m}^{{\text{\tiny$(p)$}}}_{\iso}<+\infty

(5.37)

\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}\leq\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{32\pi\,\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\partial E_{V_{k}})}{4\sqrt{\pi}I^{\prime}(V_{k})\sqrt{I(V_{k})}+I^{\prime}(V_{k})^{2}I(V_{k})}=\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\partial E_{V_{k}})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}.

For every

\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}-\frac{1}{j}\leq\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\partial E_{V_{k_{j}}}^{t})\leq\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\frac{2}{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.93669pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.93669pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E^{t}_{V_{k}}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.37224pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.37224pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.02779pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.02779pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}}}\left({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.93669pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.93669pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert E^{t}_{V_{k}}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.37224pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.37224pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.02779pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.02779pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}}-\frac{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.93669pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.93669pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial E^{t}_{V_{k}}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.37224pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.37224pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.02779pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.02779pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial E^{t}_{V_{k}}$}}\right\rvert$}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}}{6\sqrt{\pi}}\right)\leq\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}.

Therefore, there exists an exhaustion made of subsets
\Omega_{j}=E_{V_{k_{j}}}^{t_{j}}
of

that are bounded with connected boundaries homologous to

, and such that

(5.39)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}-\frac{1}{j}\leq\mathfrak{m}_{H}(\partial\Omega_{j})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}-\frac{1}{j}\leq\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}(\Omega_{j})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}.

Appealing again to
eq.

5.30
and using Taylor’s expansion of
{\vphantom{F}}{}_{2}F_{1}
around

, we get

(5.41)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{p}(\partial\Omega_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.31946pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.31946pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial\Omega_{j}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.45885pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.45885pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}}}{4\pi}\right)^{\frac{3-p}{2}}{\vphantom{F}}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3-p}{p-1},\frac{2}{p-1};\frac{4\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.31946pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.31946pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial\Omega_{j}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.45885pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.45885pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}}}}(1+o(1))\right)^{-(p-1)}

(5.42)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.31946pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.31946pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial\Omega_{j}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.45885pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.45885pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}}}{4\pi}\right)^{\frac{3-p}{2}}\left(1+\frac{(3-p)\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.31946pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.31946pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\partial\Omega_{j}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\partial\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.45885pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.45885pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\partial\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}}}}\mathfrak{m}_{\iso}(1+o(1))\right)^{-(p-1)}

\mathfrak{m}^{{\text{\tiny$(p)$}}}_{\iso}=\sup_{(\Omega_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}}\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\frac{2\mathfrak{c}_{p}(\partial\Omega)^{\frac{p-2}{3-p}}}{p(3-p)}\left(\left(\frac{3{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.31946pt}{$\displaystyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.31946pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\lvert\Omega_{j}\rvert}{\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\scriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}{\raisebox{2.45885pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\left\lvert\raisebox{-2.45885pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Omega_{j}$}}\right\rvert$}}}}{4\pi}\right)^{\frac{3-p}{3}}-\mathfrak{c}_{p}(\partial\Omega_{j})\right).

5.41

6.
Questions and open problems

In this last section, we collect some natural problems and questions connected with the previous topics.

(1)

Connectedness of isoperimetric sets.
As showed above, knowing that the isoperimetric sets of big volume have connected boundaries allows us to set the equivalence among isocapacitary, isoperimetric, and
\ADM
masses. It would be thus desirable to know if such property is true at least for
\mathscr{C}^{1}_{\tau}
-asymptotically flat
3
-manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature, with
\tau>1/2
. It is likely that, suitably reworking the computations of Nerz
[
Ner15
]
without taking into account the behaviour of second derivatives of the metric, this can be accomplished. Some of the insights contained in
[
BFM22a
]
about the asymptotic behaviour of the
2
-Hawking mass and its relation with the Hawking mass, in this optimal asymptotic regime, could play a role.

(2)

Higher dimensional analysis
. All the results presented here are proved through computations that are very peculiar to dimension
3
. They are all substantially based on the application of the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem
eq.

2.14
in the monotonicity calculation performed in
section

2.1
. On the other hand, the fundamental Positive Mass Theorem has been proved through Schoen-Yau’s
[
SY79
]
contradiction argument up to dimension
7
(see
[
Sch89
]
), and consequently also Bray’s approach to the Penrose inequality
[
BL09
]
. It would be very interesting to understand whether, possibly with related arguments, the existence
theorem

3.6
and the various results on the isoperimetric/isocapacitary masses hold in higher dimensions.

(3)

A
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-notion of
\ADM
mass.
A weakened notion of
\ADM
mass, resulting well posed, in particular, on
3
-manifolds that are
\mathscr{C}^{0}_{\tau}
-asymptotically flat with
\tau>1/2
, and that act as initial datum for a Ricci flow of metrics with nonnegative scalar curvature, has been recently devised by Burkhardt-Guim
[
Bur22
]
. It would be interesting to check if such quantity still coincides with the isoperimetric mass. The existence of isoperimetric sets in this class of potentially nonsmooth metrics would be of interest too.

(4)

A conjecture of Huisken.
Strongly related to the previous point, we mention a famous and formidable conjecture by Huisken (see e.g.
[
Ced+21
, p. 2221-2223]
) on an isoperimetric Positive Mass Theorem on
\mathscr{C}^{0}
manifolds of dimension
3
admitting some suitable notion of nonnegative scalar curvature. We believe that the asymptotic comparison between the Hawking mass and the isoperimetric mass devised in
[
BFM22a
]
and discussed here may serve as a useful tool, as it strongly weakens the regularity requirements at least on the asymptotic decay of the metric. Manifolds of nonnegative scalar curvature in the Ricci-flow-related sense considered by Burkhardt-Guim
[
Bur22
]
, might be a good family of metrics to test Huisken’s conjecture on.

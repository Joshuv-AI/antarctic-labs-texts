1.
Introduction

Let
G(k,n)
be the set of
m
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
V\in G(k,n)
, define
\pi_{V}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow V
to be the orthogonal projection onto
V
.
Throughout the paper,
we use
\dim X
to denote the Hausdorff dimension of the set
X
.

There is a classical result proved by Marstrand
[
11
]
, who showed that if
A
is a Borel set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then the projection of
A
onto almost every line through the origin has Hausdorff dimension
\min\{1,\dim A\}
. This was generalized to higher dimensions by Mattila
[
12
]
, who showed that if
A
is a Borel set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the projection of
A
onto almost every
k
-dimensional plane through the origin has Hausdorff dimension
\min\{k,\dim A\}
.

There is a finer question known as the exceptional set estimate. It is formulated as follows. Fix integers
1\leq k<n
. For
a,s
satisfying
0<s<\min\{k,a\}
, find

(1)

T(a,s):=\sup_{A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \textup{dim}A=a}\textup{dim}(\{V\in G(k,n):\textup{dim}(\pi_{V}(A))<s\}).

To avoid the measurability issue, we assume the set
A
is Borel throughout the paper. We remark that
T(a,s)
also depends on another two parameters
k,n
, but since
k,n
are fixed, we just drop them from the notation for simplicity.

We call the set

(2)

E_{s}(A):=\{V\in G(k,n):\textup{dim}(\pi_{V}(A))<s\}

the exceptional set.
Roughly speaking, the exceptional set is the set of directions where the projection is small. We call
T(a,s)
the dimension of the exceptional set. Of course, we trivially have
T(a,s)\leq\dim(G(k,n))=k(n-k)
. Here are some nontrivial results on the upper bound of
T(a,s)
.

(i)

T(a,s)\leq k(n-k)+s-k
.

(ii)

T(a,s)\leq\max\{k(n-k)+s-a,0\}.

(iii)

T(a,\frac{k}{n}a)\leq k(n-k)-1
.

The estimate
(i)
was first proved by Kaufman in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(see
[
9
]
), and proved by Mattila in higher dimensions (see
[
12
]
).
For the the estimate
(ii)
, it was originally obtained by Falconer (see
[
3
]
) in a slightly different formulation. Peres-Schlag also proved the result (see
[
16
]
).
The estimate
(iii)
was obtained by He (see
[
8
]
). We remark that estimate
(iii)
beats estimate
(i)
and
(ii)
if
k=1,a\in(0,1+\frac{1}{n-1})
or
k=n-1,a\in(n-1-\frac{1}{n-1},n)
.

In the special case
n=2,k=1
, there were some work by Bourgain
[
1
]
, Orponen and Shmerkin
[
15
]
,
[
14
]
. Shortly after this paper, this special case was completely resolved by Ren and Wang
[
17
]
. They proved that
T(a,s)=\max\{0,2s-a\}
when
n=2,k=1
.

In this paper, we will explore a new type of the exceptional set estimate, and as one of the result, we can improve
(iii)
.

1.1.
Statement of the main result

We fix two integers
1\leq k<n
throughout this paper.
The first theorem is an improvement over the estimate
(iii)
.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
T(a,s)
is defined in (
1
). We have

(3)

T(a,\frac{k}{n}a)\leq k(n-k)-\min\{k,n-k\}.

We would like to compare the estimate of
T(a,\frac{k}{n}a)
given by Theorem
1
with that given by
(i)
and
(ii)
.

When
k\leq n/2
, Theorem
1
gives
T(a,\frac{k}{n}a)\leq k(n-k)-k
. This automatically beats
(i)
. And it beats
(ii)
in the range
a\in(0,\frac{nk}{n-k})
.

When
k\geq\frac{n}{2}
, Theorem
1
gives
T(a,\frac{k}{n}a)\leq k(n-k)-(n-k)
. It beats
(i)
if
a>\frac{(2k-n)n}{k}
, and it beats
(ii)
if
a<n
. Therefore, it beats both
(i)
and
(ii)
in the range
a\in(\frac{(2k-n)n}{k},n)
.

To prove Theorem
1
, we will prove a more technical theorem.
To handle the numerology, we need to set up some notation.

Lemma 2
.

Let
0\leq m\leq n,1\leq k,l\leq n
be integers. Let
L\in G(l,n)
. We have

\displaystyle\dim(\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(\pi_{L}(V))\leq m\})=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}0&m<\max\{0,l+k-n\}\\
k(n-k)-(k-m)(l-m)&\max\{0,l+k-n\}\leq m\leq\min\{l,k\}\\
k(n-k)&m>\min\{l,k\}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Since
\dim(\pi_{L}(V))\geq\max\{0,l+k-n\}
, we have

\dim(\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(\pi_{L}(V))\leq m\})=0

for
m<\max\{0,l+k-n\}
.

Since
\dim(\pi_{L}(V))\leq\min\{l,k\}
, we have

\dim(\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(\pi_{L}(V))\leq m\})=k(n-k)

for
m>\min\{l,k\}
.

It remains to consider the case when
\max\{0,l+k-n\}\leq m\leq\min\{l,k\}
. First by Lemma
35
, we have
\dim(\pi_{L}(V))=\dim(\pi_{V}(L))
. We are going to apply Lemma
36
with
W=L
and the roles of
l
and
m
switched. We can verify the conditions of Lemma
36
are satisfied:
n-k\geq l-m
,
m\leq k,l
. Therefore,

(7)

\dim(\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(\pi_{L}(V))\leq m\})=d(k-m,n-l)+d(m,n-(k-m)).

Here,
d(k,n)=k(n-k)
.
We can calculate

\dim(\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(\pi_{L}(V))\leq m\})=k(n-k)-(k-m)(l-m).

∎

Definition 3

(Definition of
E(m,l)
)
.

We define
E(m,l)
to be the number in (
2
). We remark that
E(m,l)
also depends on
n,k
, but
n,k
are fixed throughout the paper so we drop them from the notation for simplicity.

Definition 4

(Definition of
m(t,l)
)
.

For
0\leq t<k(n-k)
and integer
1\leq l\leq n
, we define
m(t,l)
to be the smallest non-negative integer so that

E(m(t,l),l)>t.

By (

), we see
0\leq m(t,l)\leq\min\{l,k\}+1
. We also see that
m(\lfloor t\rfloor,l)=m(t,l)
, since
E(m,l)\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Now, we state our technical theorem.

Theorem 5
.

Fix integers
1\leq k<n
. For
0<s<\min\{k,a\}
and
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\dim A=a
, define

E_{s}(A):=\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(\pi_{V}(A))<s\}.

If
s,t
satisfy

(8)

s=\sup_{1\leq p\leq\infty}\inf_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,n]}\bigg(\frac{a-l}{p}+m(t,l)\bigg),

then

\dim(E_{s}(A))\leq t.

Remark 6
.

The relationship between
s
and
t

in (
8
) has a somewhat weird expression in terms of
\sup
and
\inf
, because we will apply Brascamp-Lieb inequality during the proof, and the Brascamp-Lieb constant has this form (see (
70
)).

It seems hard to find an explicit way to express the right hand side of (
8
) (without using
\sup
,
\inf
). In this paper, we only calculate a special case
s=\frac{k}{n}a
which gives Theorem
1
. It is conceivable that we may obtain some other interesting exceptional set estimates by carefully analyzing (
8
).

In Section
6
, we will find
the lower bound of
T(a,s)
by constructing examples. Together with Theorem
1
, we will see for certain range of
(a,s)
, the upper bound and lower bound of

are the same, which gives the exact value of
T(a,s)
. This is the following theorem.

Theorem 7
.

When
k\leq\frac{n}{2}
,
\beta\in(0,1]
and
\gamma\in(\beta,\frac{k}{n}(1+\beta)]
, we have

T(1+\beta,\gamma)=k(n-k)-k.

When
k\geq\frac{n}{2}
,
\beta\in(0,1]
and
\gamma\in(\beta,(1-\frac{k}{n})+\frac{k}{n}\beta]
, we have

T(n-1+\beta,k-1+\gamma)=k(n-k)-(n-k).

Remark 8
.

From Theorem
7
, we see that Theorem
1
is sharp for
a\in(1,2]
if
k\leq\frac{n}{2}
, and for
a\in(n-1,n]
if
k\geq\frac{n}{2}
,

Here is the structure of the paper. In Section

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

Proof of Theorem
1
using Theorem
5
.

We set

u=\min\{k,n-k\},\ t=k(n-k)-u,\ s=\sup_{1\leq p\leq\infty}\inf_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,n]}\bigg(\frac{a-l}{p}+m(t,l)\bigg).

Theorem
5
implies that

T(a,s)\leq t.

To prove Theorem
1
, it suffices to show

(9)

\frac{k}{n}a\leq\sup_{1\leq p\leq\infty}\inf_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,n]}\bigg(\frac{a-l}{p}+m(t,l)\bigg).

By choosing
p=\frac{n}{k}
, it is further reduced to

(10)

\frac{k}{n}a\leq\inf_{l\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,n]}\bigg(\frac{(a-l)k}{n}+m(t,l)\bigg).

By the definition of
u
, we have

(11)

k\in\{u,n-u\},\ n\geq 2u.

We will find an explicit expression for
m(t,l)
. Recall the definition of
m(t,l)
in Definition
4
.
m=m(t,l)
is the smallest non-negative integer satisfying

k(n-k)-(k-m)(l-m)>t,

or equivalently,

m^{2}-(k+l)m+kl+t-k(n-k)<0.

We obtain that

m=\sup\{0,\lfloor\frac{k+l-\sqrt{(k-l)^{2}+4(k(n-k)-t)}}{2}\rfloor+1\}.

Plugging
t=k(n-k)-u
, we get

(12)

m=\sup\{0,\lfloor\frac{k+l-\sqrt{(k-l)^{2}+4u}}{2}\rfloor+1\}.

To prove (
10
), we just need to prove for any
0\leq l\leq n
,

\frac{k}{n}a\leq\frac{(a-l)k}{n}+\lfloor\frac{k+l-\sqrt{(k-l)^{2}+4u}}{2}\rfloor+1.

Equivalently,

(13)

\frac{lk}{n}\leq\lfloor\frac{k+l-\sqrt{(k-l)^{2}+4u}}{2}\rfloor+1.

We discuss two cases.

Case 1:
0\leq l\leq k
We write
l=k-\mu
with
\mu\geq 0
. Then the right hand side of (
13
) is

\lfloor\frac{-\mu-\sqrt{\mu^{2}+4u}}{2}\rfloor+k+1.

Let
\alpha
be the biggest integer such that
\alpha<\frac{\mu+\sqrt{\mu^{2}+4u}}{2}
, or in other words,
\alpha=-\lfloor\frac{-\mu-\sqrt{\mu^{2}+4u}}{2}\rfloor-1
.
Then (
13
) boils down to

(14)

(k-\mu)k\leq n(k-\alpha).

There are two subcases.

When
u\leq\mu
, then
\alpha<\frac{\mu+\sqrt{\mu^{2}+4u}}{2}\leq\frac{2\mu+2}{2}
, so
\alpha\leq\mu
. We see (
14
) holds since
n>k
.

When
u\geq\mu+1
, we just need to verify (
14
) at two endpoints
k=u,k=n-u
. For
k=u
, (
14
) becomes
(u-\mu)u\leq n(u-\alpha)
. Using the bound
n\geq 2u
, we just need to show

u+\mu\geq 2\alpha
. Noting
\mu^{2}+4u\leq(u+1)^{2}
, we have
\alpha<\frac{\mu+u+1}{2}
, and hence
\alpha\leq\frac{\mu+u}{2}
. For
k=n-u
, (
14
) becomes
(n-u-\mu)(n-u)\leq n(n-u-\alpha)
, which is equivalent to
u(u+\mu)\leq n(u+\mu-\alpha)
. Using the bound
n\geq 2u
, it boils down to
u+\mu\geq 2\alpha
which has been proved.

Case 2:
k\leq l\leq n
We write
l=k+\mu
with
\mu\geq 0
. Then the right hand side of (
13
) is

\lfloor\frac{\mu-\sqrt{\mu^{2}+4u}}{2}\rfloor+k+1.

Let
\alpha
be the biggest integer such that
\alpha<\frac{\sqrt{\mu^{2}+4u}-\mu}{2}
, or in other words,
\alpha=-\lfloor\frac{\mu-\sqrt{\mu^{2}+4u}}{2}\rfloor-1
.
Then (
13
) boils down to

(15)

(k+\mu)k\leq n(k-\alpha).

There are two subcases.

When
u\leq\mu
, then
\alpha<\frac{\sqrt{\mu^{2}+4u}-\mu}{2}\leq\frac{\mu+2-\mu}{2}
, so
\alpha\leq 0
. We see (
15
) holds since
n\geq l=k+\mu
.

When
u\geq\mu+1
, we just need to verify (
15
) at two endpoints
k=u,k=n-u
. For
k=u
, (
15
) becomes
(u+\mu)u\leq n(u-\alpha)
. Using the bound
n\geq 2u
, we just need to show

u-\mu\geq 2\alpha
. Noting
\mu^{2}+4u\leq(u+1)^{2}
, we have
\alpha<\frac{u+1-\mu}{2}
, and hence
\alpha\leq\frac{u-\mu}{2}
. For
k=n-u
, (
15
) becomes
(n-u+\mu)(n-u)\leq n(n-u-\alpha)
, which is equivalent to
u(u-\mu)\leq n(u-\mu-\alpha)
. Using the bound
n\geq 2u
, it boils down to
u-\mu\geq 2\alpha
which has been proved.

∎

3.
A heuristic proof of Theorem
5

The main idea in the proof of Theorem
5
is as follows. Suppose there is a set
A
satisfying the condition in Theorem
5
such that

\dim(E_{s}(A))=\tau>t,

then we will derive a contradiction.

We consider the following heuristic setting.
A
is a disjoint union of
\delta
-balls with cardinality
\delta^{-a}
. Our exceptional set
E
is a
\delta
-separated subset of
G(k,n)
with cardinality
\delta^{-\tau}
. We also assume
E
satisfies the
(\delta,\tau)
-condition:

(16)

\#(E\cap Q_{r})\leq(r/\delta)^{\tau},

for any
Q_{r}\subset G(k,n)
being a ball of radius
r
.
The condition that
\dim(\pi_{V}(A))<s
is characterized in the following way. For each
V\in E
, we have a set of
(n-k)
-dimensional
\delta
-slabs
\mathbb{T}_{V}=\{T\}
orthogonal to
V
. In other words, each
T\in\mathbb{T}_{V}
is a rectangle of dimensions
\delta\times\dots\times\delta\times 1\times\dots\times 1
(with

n-k
many
1
’s), and the longest
n-k
directions are orthogonal to
V
. We also assume
A\subset\bigcup_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{V}}T
and
\#\mathbb{T}_{V}\leq\delta^{-s+\varepsilon_{0}}
. Under these conditions, one is able to deduce some relationships among the parameters
n,k,a,s,t
. We consider the integral

(17)

\int_{A}(\sum_{V\in E}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{V}}1_{T})^{p},

where
1\leq p\leq\infty
is to be determined later. Since
A\subset\bigcup_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{V}}T
, a straightforward observation gives

\int_{A}(\sum_{V\in E}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{V}}1_{T})^{p}\sim|A|(\#E)^{p}\sim\delta^{n-a-\tau p}.

On the other hand, we will also obtain an upper bound for the integral.
Obtaining an upper bound for the integral is more complicated. Let us first take a look at a single
\delta
-ball
B_{\delta}\subset A
. By our assumption, for each
V\in E
, there is a
T\in\mathbb{T}_{V}
such that
T\cap B_{\delta}\neq\emptyset
. Let
K
be a scale such that
\delta\ll K^{-1}\ll 1
, and we partition
G(k,n)
into
K^{-1}
-balls
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q\}
(here
K^{-1}
-ball means ball of radius
K^{-1}
). Denote the number
J=\lfloor K^{\tau}/2\rfloor
. The key observation is the following inequality.

(18)

\delta^{-\tau}=\#E\lesssim K^{O(1)}\sum_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{J}\in\mathcal{Q}}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{J}\#(Q_{j}\cap E)\bigg)^{1/J}.

Here,
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{J}
are different.

We give a proof for (
18
). Label the balls
Q_{1},Q_{2},\dots,Q_{J},\dots
in
\mathcal{Q}
so that
\#(Q_{1}\cap E)\geq\#(Q_{2}\cap E)\geq\dots\geq\#(Q_{J}\cap E)\geq\dots
. We just need to prove
\#(Q_{J}\cap E)\gtrsim K^{-O(1)}\#E
. By (
16
),
\sum_{j=1}^{J}\#(Q_{j}\cap E)\leq J/(K\delta)^{\tau}\leq\#E/2
. Therefore,

\#(Q_{J}\cap E)\geq\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{j\geq J+1}\#(Q_{j}\cap E)\geq\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{Q}}\#E/2\gtrsim K^{-O(1)}\#E.

By (
18
), we can bound the integral (
17
) by the multilinear form

(19)

\int_{A}(\sum_{V\in E}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{V}}1_{T})^{p}\lesssim K^{O(1)}\sum_{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{J}\in\mathcal{Q}}\int\prod_{j=1}^{J}\bigg(\sum_{V\in Q_{j}\cap E}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{V}}1_{T}\bigg)^{p/J}.

The transversality property of these
J
patches allows us to obtain a nice upper bound. The main tool we use to handle this
J
-linear integral is the Brascamp-Lieb inequality. In the end, we will check the numerology and see the contradiction if
\tau>t
.

Let us work on a very easy case when
n=2,k=1
. We give a heuristic proof for the estimate

\dim(E_{a/2}(A))=0.

We just follow the argument as above. We will choose
p=2
.

Suppose by contradiction that
\dim(E_{a/2}(A))>0
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
\dim(E_{a/2-\varepsilon_{0}}(A))=\tau>0
. On the one hand,

\int_{A}(\sum_{V\in E}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{V}}1_{T})^{2}\sim\delta^{2-a-2\tau}.

For the upper bound, we notice that
J=\lfloor K^{\tau}/2\rfloor\geq 2
(if
K
is large), so we could just use the bilinear integral as an upper bound:

\int_{A}(\sum_{V\in E}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{V}}1_{T})^{2}\lesssim\int\bigg(\sum_{V\in Q_{1}\cap E}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{V}}1_{T}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{V\in Q_{2}\cap E}\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{V}}1_{T}\bigg)\lesssim\delta^{2}(\#E\delta^{-a/2+\varepsilon_{0}})^{2},

where
Q_{1},Q_{2}
are two arcs of
S^{1}
with distance
\sim 1
from each other. Note that
\#E=\delta^{-\tau}
. Combining the two estimates gives
1\lesssim\delta^{2\varepsilon_{0}}
which is a contradiction.

For the proof of general
n
and
k
, our argument is similar. The full details will be given in the next section.

4.1.
Preliminary

We recall some properties of affine Grassmannians from
[
5
, Section 1.2]
.

Let
G(k,n)
be the set of
k
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For every
k
-plane
V
, we can uniquely write it as

V=\textup{dir}(V)+x_{V},

where
\textup{dir}(V)\in G(k,n)
and
x_{V}\in V^{\perp}
.
\textup{dir}(V)
refers to the direction of
V
, as can be seen that
\textup{dir}(V)=\textup{dir}(V^{\prime})\Leftrightarrow V\parallel V^{\prime}
.

In this paper, we use
A(k,n)
to denote the set of
k
-planes
V
such that
x_{V}\in B^{n}(0,1/2)
. (
B^{n}(0,1/2)
is the ball of radius
1/2
centered at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.)

A(k,n)=\{V:V\textup{~is~a~}k\textup{~dimensional~plane~},x_{V}\in B^{n}(0,1/2)\}.

Usually
A(k,n)
denotes all the
k
-planes in other references, but for our purpose we only care about those
V
that lie near the origin.

Next, we discuss the metrics on
G(k,n)
and
A(k,n)
.
For
V_{1},V_{2}\in G(k,n)
, we define

d(V_{1},V_{2})=\|\pi_{V_{1}}-\pi_{V_{2}}\|.

Here,
\pi_{V_{1}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow V_{1}
is the orthogonal projection. We have another characterization for this metric. Define
\rho(V_{1},V_{2})
to be the smallest number
\rho

\displaystyle II

5.2.

The version of Brascamp-Lieb inequality we are going to use is due to Maldague. See Theorem 2 in

Theorem 25

(Maldague)
.

Let

for

\textup{BL}(\{W_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J},p):=\sup_{L\leq\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\dim L-\frac{p}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\dim\pi_{W_{j}}(L)).

Here,
L\leq\mathbb{R}^{n}
means
L
is a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\pi_{W_{j}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow W_{j}
is the orthogonal projection.

There exists
\nu>0
depending on

\varepsilon>0
and for any

By a compactness argument, we deduce the following result.

Theorem 26
.

Define

\textup{BL}(\{\mathcal{W}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J},p):=\sup_{W_{1}\in\mathcal{W}_{1},\dots,W_{J}\in\mathcal{W}_{J}}\textup{BL}(\{W_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J},p).

\varepsilon>0
.
Then for any

\int_{[-1,1]^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{J}\bigg(\sum_{V_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{j}}1_{V_{j,\delta}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{J}}\leq C(\{\mathcal{W}_{j}\},p,\varepsilon)\cdot\delta^{n-\varepsilon}\delta^{-\textup{BL}((\mathcal{W}_{j})_{j=1}^{J},p)}\prod_{j=1}^{J}\bigg(\#\mathcal{V}_{j}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{J}}.

6.2.
Examples in higher dimensions

We discuss the examples of the exceptional sets in higher dimensions. There will be four types of the examples. We will use
d(m,n)
to denote
\dim(G(m,n))
, namely
d(m,n)=m(n-m)
. We first list some well known facts.

Lemma 35
.

Suppose
V,W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are two subspaces. Then

\dim(\pi_{V}(W))=\dim(\pi_{W}(V)),

and

\dim(\pi_{V}(W))=\dim(W)-\dim(W\cap V^{\perp}).

Proof.

Since the linear map
\pi_{V}:W\rightarrow V
has kernel
W\cap V^{\perp}
, we have

\dim(\pi_{V}(W))=\dim(W)-\dim(W\cap V^{\perp}).

To prove the first equality, we note

\displaystyle\dim(\pi_{V}(W))

\displaystyle=\dim(W)-\dim(W\cap V^{\perp})

\displaystyle=\dim W-\bigg(n-\dim\big((W\cap V^{\perp})^{\perp}\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\dim W-n+\dim(W^{\perp}+V)

\displaystyle=\dim W-n+\dim W^{\perp}+\dim V-\dim(W^{\perp}\cap V)

\displaystyle=\dim V-\dim(V\cap W^{\perp})

\displaystyle=\dim(\pi_{W}(V)).

∎

Lemma 36
.

Let
0\leq l\leq n
,
1\leq k,m\leq n
be integers satisfying
n-k\geq m-l
and
l\leq k,m
. If
W\in G(m,n)
, then

\dim\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(\pi_{V}(W))\leq l\}=d(k-l,n-m)+d(l,n-(k-l)).

Proof.

By Lemma
35
, we have

\displaystyle\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(\pi_{V}(W))\leq l\}

\displaystyle=\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(V\cap W^{\perp})\geq k-l\},

By condition
l\leq k
, we see
\dim(V\cap W^{\perp})\geq k-l
makes sense.
Actually,
\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(V\cap W^{\perp})\geq k-l\}
is a Schubert cycle whose dimension is not hard to calculate (see
[
7
]
Chapter 1, Section 5). Here, we provide a sketch of proof. The condition
n-k\geq m-l
says that
\dim(W^{\perp})\geq k-l
. The condition
l\leq m
yields that generic
V\in G(k,n)
satisfies
\dim(V\cap W^{\perp})=\dim(V)+\dim(W^{\perp})-n=k+n-m-n\leq k-l
(imagine the generic case is when
V
and
W^{\perp}
are as transverse as possible). Therefore, generic
V\in\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(V\cap W^{\perp})\geq k-l\}
will satisfy
\dim(V\cap W^{\perp})=k-l
. It is equivalent to calculate the dimension of

\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(V\cap W^{\perp})=k-l\}.

Note that we can identify
V
with
(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
, where
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
are orthonormal basis of
V
. We first choose
v_{1},\dots,v_{k-l}\in W^{\perp}
, which is equivalent to choosing an
(k-l)
-plane in
W^{\perp}
. It gives
d(k-l,n-m)
-dimensional choices. Next, we choose
v_{k-l+1},\dots,v_{k}
from
\textup{span}\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-l}\}^{\perp}
, which gives
d(l,n-(k-l))
-dimensional choices.
Adding together, gives the dimension
d(k-l,n-m)+d(l,n-(k-l))
.
∎

We state the key result.

Proposition 37
.

Fix
0<k<n
. For

, define

E_{s}(A):=\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(\pi_{V}(A))<s\}.

For

and

, define

Then we have the following lower bound for

. We write
a=m+\beta,s=l+\gamma
where

and
\beta,\gamma\in(0,1]
.

(1)

Type 1:

1\leq m\leq n+l-k,\gamma>\frac{\beta+1}{2}

\displaystyle\geq d(k-l,n-m)+d(l,n-(k-l))+2\gamma-(\beta+1)

\displaystyle=k(n-k)-(m-l)(k-l)+2\gamma-(\beta+1).

(2)

Type 2:

m\leq n+l-k,\gamma>\beta

(88)

\displaystyle T(m+\beta,l+\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq d(k-l,n-m)+d(l,n-(k-l))

\displaystyle=k(n-k)-(m-l)(k-l).

(3)

Type 3:

m\leq n+l-k-1,\gamma>\frac{\beta}{2}

(89)

\displaystyle T(m+\beta,l+\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq d(k-l,n-m-1)+d(l,n-(k-l))+2\gamma-\beta

\displaystyle=k(n-k)-(m+1-l)(k-l)+2\gamma-\beta.

(4)

Type 4:

\displaystyle\geq d(k-(l-1),n-m)+d(l-1,n-(k-l+1))

Proof.

Type 2

Type 3

\displaystyle T(m+\beta,l+\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq d(k-l,n-m-1)+d(l,n-(k-l))+2\gamma-\beta

\displaystyle=k(n-k)-(m+1-l)(k-l)+2\gamma-\beta.

We choose
A=\boldsymbol{A}_{\gamma,\beta}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\{\underbrace{(0,\dots,0)}_{(n-m-2)\times 0}\}

28

\mathbb{R}^{m}
to denote
\{(0,0)\}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\{(0,\dots,0)\}

\mathbb{R}^{2}

but orthogonal to
\theta

\mathcal{W}_{\theta}=\{V\in\mathcal{V}_{\theta}:\dim(\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{m}))\leq l-1\}\cup\{V\in\mathcal{V}_{\theta}:\dim(\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})\leq l\}.

It is not hard to see

. To get some intuition on these sets, we suggest readers to look back at (

\mathcal{U}_{\theta},\mathcal{V}_{\theta}

are higher dimensional analog of
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{*\},\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
and

.

Let
\mathbf{E}_{\gamma,\beta}=\{\theta\in S^{1}:\dim(\pi_{l_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{A}_{\gamma,\beta}))<\gamma\}
. We claim that if
\theta\in\mathbf{E}_{\gamma,\beta}
, then
\mathcal{V}_{\theta}\subset E_{l+\gamma}(A)
. In other words, any
V\in\mathcal{V}_{\theta}
satisfies
\dim(\pi_{V}(A))<l+\gamma
. We first note that
\boldsymbol{A}_{\gamma,\beta}\subset\pi_{l_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{A}_{\gamma,\beta})+\mathbb{R}\theta
, so

A=\boldsymbol{A}_{\gamma,\beta}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\subset(\pi_{l_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{A}_{\gamma,\beta})+\mathbb{R}\theta)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}=\pi_{l_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{A}_{\gamma,\beta})+(\mathbb{R}\theta\oplus\mathbb{R}^{m}).

Therefore,

\dim(\pi_{V}(A))\leq\dim(\pi_{l_{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{A}_{\gamma,\beta}))+\dim(\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}\theta\oplus\mathbb{R}^{m}))<\gamma+l.

We see that
E_{l+\gamma}(A)\supset\bigcup_{\theta\in\mathbf{E}_{\gamma,\beta}}\mathcal{V}_{\theta}.

\mathcal{V}_{\theta}
may intersect with each other, so we delete a tiny portion
\mathcal{W}_{\theta}
from
\mathcal{V}_{\theta}
, so that the remaining part
\mathcal{U}_{\theta}=\mathcal{V}_{\theta}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{\theta}
are disjoint with each other. Then we have

E_{l+\gamma}(A)\supset\bigsqcup_{\theta\in\mathbf{E}_{\gamma,\beta}}\mathcal{U}_{\theta}.

To make the argument precise, we first apply Lemma
36
to see

\dim(\mathcal{V}_{\theta})=d(k-l,n-m-1)+d(l,n-(k-l))=k(n-k)-(m+1-l)(k-l).

To calculate
\dim(\mathcal{W}_{\theta})
, we apply Lemma
36
again to see

\dim(\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{m}))\leq l-1\})=k(n-k)-(m-l+1)(k-l+1),

and

\dim(\{V\in G(k,n):\dim(\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}))\leq l\})=k(n-k)-(m+2-l)(k-l).

Therefore,
\dim(\mathcal{W}_{\theta})<\dim(\mathcal{V}_{\theta})
and hence

\dim(\mathcal{U}_{\theta})=\dim(\mathcal{V}_{\theta})=d(k-l,n-m-1)+d(l,n-(k-l)).

Next we show
\mathcal{U}_{\theta}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\theta^{\prime}}=\emptyset
for
\theta\neq\theta^{\prime}
.
By contradiction, suppose
V\in\mathcal{U}_{\theta}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\theta^{\prime}}
. By definition,
V
satisfies

\displaystyle\dim(\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}\theta\oplus\mathbb{R}^{m}))\leq l,

\displaystyle\dim(\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}\theta^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{m}))\leq l,

\displaystyle\dim(\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{m}))=l,

\displaystyle\dim(\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}))=l+1.

The first and third conditions imply
\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}\theta)\subset\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Similarly, the second and third conditions imply
\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}\theta^{\prime})\subset\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Noting that
\textup{span}\{\mathbb{R}\theta,\mathbb{R}\theta^{\prime}\}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have

\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})\subset\pi_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),

which has dimension
=l
, contradicting the fourth condition.

We have shown that
E_{l+\gamma}(A)\supset\bigsqcup_{\theta\in\mathbf{E}_{\gamma,\beta}}\mathcal{U}_{\theta}
, which has dimension

\dim(\mathbf{E}_{\gamma,\beta})+\dim(\mathcal{U}_{\theta})

=2\gamma-\beta+d(k-l,n-m-1)+d(l,n-(k-l)).

This gives (
92

Type 1
: When
m\geq 1,n-m\geq k-l,\gamma>\frac{\beta+1}{2}
, then

(93)

\displaystyle T(m+\beta,l+\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq d(k-l,n-m)+d(l,n-(k-l))+2\gamma-(\beta+1)

(94)

\displaystyle=k(n-k)-(m-l)(k-l)+2\gamma-(\beta+1).

The trick is to write
T(m+\beta,l+\gamma)=T((m-1)+(\beta+1),l+\gamma)
and then apply
Type
2 construction. We will choose

A=\boldsymbol{A}_{\gamma,\beta+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{m-1}\times\{(0,\dots,0)\}.

It will give the bound (
93
).

Type 4
: When
l\geq 1,n-m\geq k-l+1
, then

(95)

\displaystyle T(m+\beta,l+\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq d(k-(l-1),n-m)+d(l-1,n-(k-l+1))

\displaystyle=k(n-k)-(m-l+1)(k-l+1).

The idea is to apply
Type
1 construction. Note that here we do not have the condition
\gamma>\beta
, so we cannot directly apply
Type
1. The trick is to write

T(m+\beta,l+\gamma)\geq T(m+\beta,(l-1)+1).

Now our new
l
becomes
l-1
, and our new
\gamma
becomes
1
. We apply
Type 1
, and obtain (
95
).
∎

Given
a=m+\beta,s=l+\gamma
, we compare the lower bounds of these four types and see that

{\bf Type\ 1}\geq{\bf Type\ 2}\geq{\bf Type\ 3}\geq{\bf Type\ 4}.

Therefore, the strategy to find the lower bound of
T(a,s)
is by testing the requirement of
Type 1
,
Type 2
,… one by one, and use the lower bound as soon as the requirement is satisfied. If neither requirement is satisfied, then we only expect the trivial lower bound
T(a,s)\geq 0
.

Type 2

6.3.
Proof of Theorem
7

We will use
Type 2
bound in Proposition
37
. We consider the case
k\leq\frac{n}{2}
. Suppose
\beta\in(0,1],\gamma\in(\beta,\frac{k}{n}(1+\beta)]
. By Theorem
1
, we have

(96)

T(1+\beta,\gamma)\leq T(1+\beta,\frac{k}{n}(1+\beta))\leq k(n-k)-k.

To obtain a lower bound, we use
Type 2
. We choose
m=1,l=0
, so
a=1+\beta
and
s=\gamma
. We can verify the condition that
m\leq n+l-k,\gamma>\beta
. Therefore, we obtain

(97)

T(1+\beta,\gamma)\geq k(n-k)-k.

This finishes the proof that

(98)

T(1+\beta,\gamma)=k(n-k)-k.

Next, we consider the case
k\geq\frac{n}{2}
. Suppose
\beta\in(0,1],\gamma\in(\beta,(1-\frac{k}{n})+\frac{k}{n}\beta]
. By Theorem
1
, we have

(99)

T(n-1+\beta,k-1+\gamma)\leq T(n-1+\beta,\frac{k}{n}(n-1+\beta))\leq k(n-k)-(n-k).

To obtain a lower bound, we use
Type 2
. We choose
m=n-1,l=k-1
, so
a=n-1+\beta
and
s=k-1+\gamma
. We can verify the condition that
m\leq n+l-k,\gamma>\beta
. Therefore, we obtain

(100)

T(n-1+\beta,k-1+\gamma)\geq k(n-k)-(n-k).

This finishes the proof that

(101)

T(n-1+\beta,k-1+\gamma)=k(n-k)-(n-k).

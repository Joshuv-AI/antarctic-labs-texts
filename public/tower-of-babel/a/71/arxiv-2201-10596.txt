Small oscillations of a heavy symmetric top when magnitudes of conserved angular momenta are equal
V. Tanrıverdi

Abstract
Small oscillations of a heavy symmetric top are studied when magnitudes of conserved angular momenta are equal to each other. Results show that the small oscillation approximation can be used in these cases.

These are necessary to explain some of the observed motions of a heavy symmetric top and can be helpful in teaching.

2
Heavy symmetric top when
|b|=|a|

In most of the works on heavy symmetric top, the situation when conserved angular momenta are different is studied.
In the previous work, we have studied small oscillations of a heavy symmetric top when
|b|\neq|a|
by using
[
7
]

\ddot{\theta}=\frac{\sin\theta}{I_{x}}\left[Mgl+I_{x}\dot{\phi}^{2}\cos\theta-I_{z}\dot{\phi}^{2}\cos\theta-I_{z}\dot{\phi}\dot{\psi}\right].

(1)

where
a=L_{z}/I_{x}
and
b=L_{z^{\prime}}/I_{x}
are two constants defined from conserved angular momenta.
This equation is written in terms of Euler angles and their derivatives, and
I_{x}
and
I_{z}
are moments of inertia,
M
is the mass,
l
is the distance between the center of mass and
g
is the gravitational acceleration.

When magnitudes of conserved angular momenta are equal to each other, which is equivalent to
|b|=|a|
, there are some changes, and at the following part, we will consider these cases.
We will consider
Mgl
as positive unless stated otherwise.

2.1
Heavy symmetric top when
b=a

When
b=a
, the effective potential can be written as
[
12
]

U_{eff}(\theta)=\frac{I_{x}}{2}\frac{a^{2}(1-\cos\theta)}{1+\cos\theta}+Mgl\cos\theta.

(2)

In this case, the infinity at
\theta=0
disappears and the motion at the upright position becomes possible.
When the motion at
\theta=0
is possible, one may need to extend the domain from
\theta\in[0,\pi]
to
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
to avoid the discontinuity in
\dot{\theta}

[
12
]
.
We will use such extended domains for these types of motion.

The derivative of the effective potential, equation (
2
), can be obtained as

\frac{dU_{eff}(\theta)}{d\theta}=\left[\frac{I_{x}a^{2}}{(1+\cos\theta)^{2}}-Mgl\right]\sin\theta.

(3)

The terms in the parentheses can only be equal to zero when
|a|<\sqrt{4Mgl/I_{x}}
.
When
|a|>\sqrt{4Mgl/I_{x}}
, i.e. fast top,
\theta=0
corresponds to the minimum which is equal to
Mgl
.
When
|a|<\sqrt{4Mgl/I_{x}}
, i.e. slow top,
\theta=0
corresponds to the local maximum with value
Mgl
,
and the minimum occurs at
\theta_{r}=\arccos(\sqrt{I_{x}a^{2}/Mgl}-1)
with value
U_{eff_{min}}=2\sqrt{MglI_{x}a^{2}}-Mgl-I_{x}a^{2}/2
.
These two situations can be seen in figure
1
(a) and
1
(b).

For slow top, turning angles can be found by considering
E^{\prime}=U_{eff}
with
\dot{\theta}=0
.
In this case, by changing varible
u=\cos\theta
, one gets a quadratic equation for
u
as

u^{2}+\left(1-\frac{I_{x}a^{2}+2E^{\prime}}{2Mgl}\right)u+\frac{I_{x}a^{2}-2E^{\prime}}{2Mgl}=0,

(4)

From the roots of this equation one can obtain turning angles by using
\theta_{min,max}=\arccos(u_{1,2})
, see figure
1
(b).

For fast tops, there exists only one root, i.e.
\theta_{max}
, between
0
and
\pi
which corresponds to turning angle,
and for the extended domain, the other turning angle can be taken as
-\theta_{max}
, see figure
1
(a).

When
b=a
, angular velocities
\dot{\phi}
and
\dot{\psi}
can be found as
[
12
]

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a}{1+\cos\theta},

(5)

\displaystyle\dot{\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\left(\frac{I_{x}}{I_{z}}-\frac{\cos\theta}{1+\cos\theta}\right).

(6)

2.2
Heavy symmetric top when
b=-a

When
b=-a
, the effective potential can be written as
[
12
]

U_{eff}(\theta)=\frac{I_{x}}{2}\frac{a^{2}(1+\cos\theta)}{1-\cos\theta}+Mgl\cos\theta.

(7)

In this case, the infinity at
\theta=\pi
disappears, and the motion at there becomes possible.
Again, one may need to use an extended domain to avoid discontinuity in
\dot{\theta}
and use the extended domain
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
for motions including
\theta=\pi
.

The derivative of the effective potential, equation (
7
), can be obtained as

\frac{dU_{eff}(\theta)}{d\theta}=-\left[\frac{I_{x}a^{2}}{(1-\cos\theta)^{2}}+Mgl\right]\sin\theta.

(8)

The terms in the parentheses can not be equal to zero since both terms are always positive.
This time,
\theta=\pi
corresponds to the minimum of the effective potential with value
-Mgl
.
This situation can be seen in figure
1
(c).

Similar to the previous case by considering
E^{\prime}=U_{eff}
with
\dot{\theta}=0
and changing variable, one can obtain a quadratic equation for
u
as

u^{2}-\left(1+\frac{I_{x}a^{2}+2E^{\prime}}{2Mgl}\right)u+\frac{2E^{\prime}-I_{x}a^{2}}{2Mgl}=0,

(9)

One of the roots of this equations gives turning angle, i.e.
\theta_{min}
, and the other one can be defined as
2\pi-\theta_{min}
for extended potential, see figure
1
(c).

Angular velocities
\dot{\phi}
and
\dot{\psi}
can be found as
[
12
]

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-a}{1-\cos\theta},

(10)

\displaystyle\dot{\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\left(\frac{I_{x}}{I_{z}}+\frac{\cos\theta}{1-\cos\theta}\right).

(11)

3
Small oscillations when conserved angular momenta are equal

We will consider the small oscillations in two parts since there are two possible situations, i.e.
b=a
and
b=-a
.

For examples, we will take
I_{x}=I_{y}=22.8\times 10^{-5}\,kg\,m^{2}
,
I_{z}=5.72\times 10^{-5}\,kg\,m^{2}
and
Mgl=0.068J
.
We will compare the results of the small oscillation approximation with the results of the numerical integration of angular accelerations, whose details can be found in a previous work
[
12
]
.

3.1
Small oscillations when
b=a

When
b=a
, equation (
1
) can be written as

\ddot{\theta}=-\frac{a^{2}}{\sin^{3}\theta}(1-\cos\theta)^{2}+\frac{Mgl}{I_{x}}\sin\theta.

(12)

For small oscillations, one can write
\theta=\theta_{0}+\eta
and
\sin\theta\approx\sin\theta_{0}+\eta\cos\theta_{0}
and
\cos\theta\approx\cos\theta_{0}-\eta\sin\theta_{0}
where
\eta
is small.
By using these in equation (
12
), expanding denominator and ignoring higher-order terms, one can obtain

\ddot{\eta}+\eta\left[\frac{a^{2}(2-\cos\theta_{0})}{(1+\cos\theta_{0})^{2}}-\frac{Mgl}{I_{x}}\cos\theta_{0}\right]\approx\frac{Mgl}{I_{x}}\sin\theta_{0}-\frac{a^{2}(1-\cos\theta_{0})}{\sin\theta_{0}(1+\cos\theta_{0})}.

(13)

One can define
w_{1}^{2}=a^{2}(2-\cos\theta_{0})/(1+\cos\theta_{0})^{2}-Mgl\cos\theta_{0}/I_{x}
and
C_{1}=Mgl\sin\theta_{0}/I_{x}-a^{2}(1-\cos\theta_{0})/[\sin\theta_{0}(1+\cos\theta_{0})]
.
Then, by considering
\ddot{\eta}+\eta w_{1}^{2}\approx C_{1}
,
\dot{\eta}(t=0)=0
and
\eta(t=0)=0
, one can obtain the solution as

\eta(t)=\frac{C_{1}}{w_{1}^{2}}(1-\cos(w_{1}t)).

(14)

Then by considering
\theta=\theta_{0}+\eta
, one can obtain

\displaystyle\theta(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\theta_{0}+\frac{C_{1}}{w_{1}^{2}}(1-\cos(w_{1}t)),

(15)

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}}{w_{1}}\sin(w_{1}t),

(16)

and by using equations for
\dot{\phi}=(b-a\cos\theta)/\sin^{2}\theta
and
\dot{\psi}=I_{x}a/I_{z}-(b-a\cos\theta)\cos\theta/\sin^{2}\theta
and small oscillations, one can obtain

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{0}+\frac{\dot{\phi}_{0}(1-\cos\theta_{0})}{\sin\theta_{0}}\frac{C_{1}}{w_{1}^{2}}(1-\cos(w_{1}t)),

(17)

\displaystyle\dot{\psi}(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\dot{\psi}_{0}+\frac{\dot{\phi}_{0}(1-\cos\theta_{0})}{\sin\theta_{0}}\frac{C_{1}}{w_{1}^{2}}(1-\cos(w_{1}t)),

(18)

\displaystyle\phi(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{0}t+\frac{\dot{\phi}_{0}(1-\cos\theta_{0})}{\sin\theta_{0}}\frac{C_{1}}{w_{1}^{2}}\left(t-\frac{\sin(w_{1}t)}{w_{1}}\right),

(19)

\displaystyle\psi(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\dot{\psi}_{0}t+\frac{\dot{\phi}_{0}(1-\cos\theta_{0})}{\sin\theta_{0}}\frac{C_{1}}{w_{1}^{2}}\left(t-\frac{\sin(w_{1}t)}{w_{1}}\right).

(20)

\phi
and
\psi
are obtained by integrating corresponding angular velocities and considering
\phi(t=0)=0
and
\psi(t=0)=0
.
These equations are adequate for small oscillations when the minimum of the effective potential is between
0
and
\pi
.

3.2
Small oscillations when
b=-a

When
b=-a
, equation (
1
) can be written as

\ddot{\theta}=\frac{a^{2}}{\sin^{3}\theta}(1+\cos\theta)^{2}+\frac{Mgl}{I_{x}}\sin\theta.

(21)

Similar to the previous case from equation (
21
), one can obtain

\ddot{\eta}+\eta\left[\frac{a^{2}(2+\cos\theta_{0})}{(1-\cos\theta_{0})^{2}}-\frac{Mgl}{I_{x}}\cos\theta_{0}\right]\approx\frac{Mgl}{I_{x}}\sin\theta_{0}+\frac{a^{2}(1+\cos\theta_{0})}{\sin\theta_{0}(1-\cos\theta_{0})}.

(22)

One can define
w_{2}^{2}=a^{2}(2+\cos\theta_{0})/(1-\cos\theta_{0})^{2}-Mgl\cos\theta_{0}/I_{x}
and
C_{2}=Mgl\sin\theta_{0}/I_{x}+a^{2}(1+\cos\theta_{0})/[\sin\theta_{0}(1-\cos\theta_{0})]
.
Then, by considering
\ddot{\eta}+\eta w_{2}^{2}\approx C_{2}
,
\dot{\eta}(t=0)=0
and
\eta(t=0)=0
, one can obtain

\eta(t)=\frac{C_{2}}{w_{2}^{2}}(1-\cos(w_{2}t)).

(23)

Then, similar to previous case, by considering
\theta=\theta_{0}+\eta
, one can obtain

\displaystyle\theta(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\theta_{0}+\frac{C_{2}}{w_{2}^{2}}(1-\cos(w_{2}t)),

(24)

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}}{w_{2}}\sin(w_{2}t),

(25)

and similarly by considering equations
\dot{\phi}
and
\dot{\psi}
and small oscillations, one can obtain

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{0}-\frac{\dot{\phi}_{0}(1+\cos\theta_{0})}{\sin\theta_{0}}\frac{C_{2}}{w_{2}^{2}}(1-\cos(w_{2}t)),

(26)

\displaystyle\dot{\psi}(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\dot{\psi}_{0}+\frac{\dot{\phi}_{0}(1+\cos\theta_{0})}{\sin\theta_{0}}\frac{C_{2}}{w_{2}^{2}}(1-\cos(w_{2}t)),

(27)

\displaystyle\phi(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{0}t-\frac{\dot{\phi}_{0}(1+\cos\theta_{0})}{\sin\theta_{0}}\frac{C_{2}}{w_{2}^{2}}\left(t-\frac{\sin(w_{2}t)}{w_{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\psi(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\dot{\psi}_{0}t+\frac{\dot{\phi}_{0}(1+\cos\theta_{0})}{\sin\theta_{0}}\frac{C_{2}}{w_{2}^{2}}\left(t-\frac{\sin(w_{2}t)}{w_{2}}\right),

(29)

4
Conclusion

We have studied small oscillations of a heavy symmetric top when conserved angular momenta are equal to each other, i.e.
|b|=|a|
.
The small oscillation approximation is done by using the angular acceleration for inclination angle
\ddot{\theta}
when
b=a
and
b=-a
(
Mgl>0
).
Overall results are consistent with the numerical solution.

In the appendix, we studied the situation when
Mgl
is negative.
The results have shown that the small oscillation approximation can also be used in this case.

To obtain results closer to the exact one, it is needed to choose
E^{\prime}
values very close to the minimum of the effective potential.
As the difference between
E^{\prime}
and
U_{eff_{min}}
becomes greater, the similarity between the results of the small oscillation approximation and the numerical solution disappears as expected.

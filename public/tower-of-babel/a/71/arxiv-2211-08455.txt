Point-wise Symmetry of Birkhoff-James Orthogonality and Geometry of 𝔹 ⁡ ( ℓ ∞ n , ℓ 1 m ) \mathbb{B}(\ell_{\infty}^{n},\ell_{1}^{m}) Thanks: The research of Babhrubahan Bose is funded by PMRF research fellowship under the supervision of Professor Apoorva Khare and Professor Gadadhar Misra.

Abstract
. We study the relationship between the point-wise symmetry of Birkhoff-James orthogonality and the geometry of the space of operators 𝔹 ⁡ ( ℓ ∞ n , ℓ 1 m ) \mathbb{B}(\ell_{\infty}^{n},\ell_{1}^{m}) . We show that any non-zero left-symmetric point in this space is a smooth point. We also show that for n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 , any unit norm right-symmetric point of this space is an extreme point of the closed unit ball. This marks the first step towards characterizing the extreme points of these unit balls and finding the Grothendieck constants G ⁡ ( m , n ) G(m,n) using Birkhoff-James orthogonality techniques.

Introduction

In recent times, Birkhoff-James orthogonality and its point-wise symmetry has been used to understand the geometry of a normed linear space. Characterization of Birkhoff-James orthogonality and its local symmetry has been done for finite-dimensional
\ell_{p}
spaces in
[
6
]
, while that for the sequence spaces
\ell_{p}
and the function spaces
L_{p}
have been done in
[
4
]
,
[
5
]

In this article, we use this idea to establish a relationship between the point-wise symmetry of Birkhoff-James orthogonality and the geometry of the space of operators from
\ell_{\infty}^{n}
into
\ell_{1}^{m}
over
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, denoted by
\mathbb{B}(\ell_{\infty}^{n},\ell_{1}^{m})
. We show that any non-zero left-symmetric point of this space is a smooth point and any unit norm right-symmetric point of this space is an extreme point of the closed unit ball.

Recall that these extreme points play a crucial role in understanding the
Grothendieck constant
for a given pair of natural numbers
(m,n)
, given by

(1)

\displaystyle G^{\mathbb{K}}(m,n):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|T\|_{op}=1,\\
\|x_{i}\|_{2}=\|y_{j}\|_{2}=1\end{subarray}}\penalty\ \left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\langle x_{i},y_{j}\rangle\right|,

where

over
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. The Grothendieck constant
G^{\mathbb{K}}
for the field
\mathbb{K}
can then be obtained as the supremum of all

, where

The Grothendieck constant emerged from a celebrated result by Grothendieck
[
7

, which in effect says that

(defined in (

and

12
]

[
11

[
14

and the memoir by Blei
[
3

The goal of this short note is to approach the Grothendieck inequality via the framework of Birkhoff-James orthogonality. Since the right hand side of (
1

\mathbb{B}(\ell_{\infty}^{n},\ell_{1}^{m})
. Hence characterization of these extreme points, which we call the
extreme contractions

[
8

(m,n)

In this spirit, the first step towards better bounding the Grothendieck constant would be to characterize the extreme contractions. This has been done for

\mathbb{R}

Theorem 1

Let

or

For proving this result, we first study Birkhoff-James orthogonality in the Banach space

\displaystyle\|f\|=1,\penalty\ f(x)=\|x\|.

A non-zero element

Given two elements

James proved in

\displaystyle x\perp_{B}y,\penalty\ x\perp_{B}z\penalty\ \Rightarrow\penalty\ x\perp_{B}(y+z).

James proved in
[

However, the importance of studying the point-wise symmetry of Birkhoff-James orthogonality in describing the geometry of normed linear spaces has been illustrated in

An element

\displaystyle x\perp_{B}y\;\Rightarrow\;y\perp_{B}x\penalty\ \penalty\ (\textit{resp.\penalty\ }y\perp_{B}x\;\Rightarrow\;x\perp_{B}y),

Note that by the term

A

2.
Birkhoff-James orthogonality in
\mathbb{X}_{\infty}^{n}

Throughout this section and the next one, all the spaces are to be considered over
\mathbb{R}
. Begin by observing that the dual of
\mathbb{X}_{\infty}^{n}
is
{\mathbb{X}^{*}}_{1}^{n}
, with the functional
\Psi_{(f_{1},f_{2},\dots f_{n})}
corresponding to
(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})\in{\mathbb{X}^{*}}_{1}^{n}
given by

\displaystyle\Psi_{(f_{1},f_{2},\dots f_{n})}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}):=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(x_{i}),\penalty\ (x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{X}_{\infty}^{n}.

We now characterize the support functional of any non-zero element of
\mathbb{X}_{\infty}^{n}
.

Proposition 3
.

Given
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{X}_{\infty}^{n}
non-zero,
(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})\in{\mathbb{X}^{*}}_{1}^{n}
is a support functional of
x
if and only if

\displaystyle f_{i}=\begin{cases}\lambda_{i}g_{i},\penalty\ \|x_{i}\|=\|x\|,\\
0,\penalty\ \|x_{i}\|<\|x\|,\end{cases}

where
g_{i}
is a support functional of
x_{i}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
and
\sum\limits_{i}\lambda_{i}=1
.

Proof.

To prove the sufficiency, note that if
(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})
satisfies the given condition, then

\displaystyle\|(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})\|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\|g_{i}\|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1,

and

\displaystyle\Psi_{(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}g_{i}(x_{i})=\sum\limits_{\|x_{i}\|=\|x\|}\lambda_{i}g_{i}(x_{i})=\sum\limits_{\|x_{i}\|=\|x\|}\lambda_{i}\|x_{i}\|=\|x\|,

since
\lambda_{i}=0
if
\|x_{i}\|\neq\|x\|
.

For the necessity, note that if
\Psi_{(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})}
is a support functional of
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
, then

\displaystyle\|x\|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(x_{i})\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|\|x_{i}\|\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|\|x\|=\|x\|\|(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})\|.

Since
\|(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})\|=1
, equality must hold in all the inequalities giving
f_{i}=0
if
\|x_{i}\|<\|x\|
and
f_{i}(x_{i})=\|f_{i}\|\|x_{i}\|
if
\|x_{i}\|=\|x\|
. Hence,
f_{i}=\lambda_{i}g_{i}
for some support functional
g_{i}
of
x_{i}
and
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
if
\|x_{i}\|=\|x\|
. Finally,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=\sum\limits_{\|x_{i}\|=\|x\|}\lambda_{i}=\sum\limits_{\|x_{i}\|=\|x\|}\lambda_{i}\|g_{i}\|=\sum\limits_{\|x_{i}\|=\|x\|}\|f_{i}\|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|=1.

∎

Theorem 5

Given two elements

(2)

Proof.

We begin with the sufficiency. If condition 1 holds then by James’ characterization of Birkhoff-James orthogonality, there exists a support functional

\displaystyle\Gamma((z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n})):=f(z_{i}),\penalty\ (z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{X}_{\infty}^{n}.

Then by Proposition

\displaystyle f_{i}(z):=[z,x_{i}]_{1},\penalty\ f_{j}(z):=[z,x_{j}]_{2},\penalty\ z\in\mathbb{X}.

Then

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\prime}((z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n})):=\lambda f_{i}(z_{i})+(1-\lambda)f_{j}(z_{j}).

Then again by Proposition

For proving the necessity, consider

3.
Point-wise symmetry of Birkhoff-James orthogonality and the geometry of
\mathbb{B}(\ell_{\infty}^{n},\ell_{1}^{m})

In this section, we show that any non-zero left-symmetric point of
\mathbb{B}(\ell_{\infty}^{n},\ell_{1}^{m})
over
\mathbb{R}
is smooth and any unit norm right-symmetric point of the space is an extreme point of the closed unit ball. We begin by characterizing the smooth points of the space of operators between two finite-dimensional Banach spaces.

Theorem 9
.

Given finite-dimensional real Banach spaces
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
, an operator
T\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
is smooth if and only if
M_{T}=\{x_{0},-x_{0}\}
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{X}
and
Tx_{0}
is a smooth point of
\mathbb{Y}
.

Proof.

For proving the sufficiency, note that if
M_{T}=\{x_{0},-x_{0}\}
, then by
[
16

,
T\perp_{B}T^{\prime}
for some
T^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
if and only if
Tx_{0}\perp_{B}T^{\prime}x_{0}
. Hence by smoothness of
Tx_{0}
, we get, for any
T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
,

\displaystyle T\perp_{B}T^{\prime},\penalty\ T\perp_{B}T^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow Tx_{0}\perp_{B}T^{\prime}x_{0},\penalty\ Tx_{0}\perp_{B}T^{\prime\prime}x_{0}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow Tx_{0}\perp_{B}(T^{\prime}x_{0}+T^{\prime\prime}x_{0})\Leftrightarrow T\perp_{B}(T^{\prime}+T^{\prime\prime}).

For proving the necessity, note that if
x\in M_{T}
and
Tx\in\mathbb{Y}
is not smooth, then there exist

distinct support functionals of
Tx
giving two distinct support functionals

\Phi
of

\displaystyle\Psi(T^{\prime}):=f\left(T^{\prime}x\right),\penalty\ \Phi(T^{\prime}):=g\left(T^{\prime}x\right),\penalty\ T^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}).

Also if

contains two linearly independent points

and

and

(not necessarily distinct) of
Tx
and
Ty
respectively to get two distinct support functionals
\Psi
and
\Phi
of
T
via

\displaystyle\Psi(T^{\prime}):=f\left(T^{\prime}x\right),\penalty\ \Phi(T^{\prime}):=g\left(T^{\prime}y\right),\penalty\ T^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}).

∎

We now prove the relationship between the left-symmetric points and smoothness in
\mathbb{B}(\ell_{\infty}^{n},\ell_{1}^{m})

Theorem 10
.

A non-zero element
T\in\mathbb{B}(\ell_{\infty}^{n},\ell_{1}^{m})
over
\mathbb{R}
is smooth if it is left-symmetric.

Proof.

Let
x\in M_{T}
and assume that
Tx
is not a left-symmetric point of
\ell_{1}^{m}
. Then find

such that
Tx\perp_{B}y
and
y\not\perp_{B}Tx
. Construct
T^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\ell_{\infty}^{n},\ell_{1}^{m})
such that

and
T^{\prime}x=y
. Hence by
[
16
, Corollary 2.2.1]
, we get
T\perp_{B}T^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime}\not\perp_{B}T
. Thus
Tx
must be a smooth point of
\mathbb{Y}
, whenever
x\in M_{T}
.

Now, if
M_{T}
contains more than two points, then
M_{T}
must contain at least two extreme points. Since
x\perp_{B}y
for any two linearly independent extreme points
x,y\in M_{T}
, we can find
T^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
such that
M_{T^{\prime}}=\{x,-x\}
,
T^{\prime}x=Tx
and
T^{\prime}y=0
for every extreme point
y\in M_{T}
linearly independent to
x
. Hence again by
[
16
, Corollary 2.2.1]
,
T\perp_{B}T^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime}\not\perp_{B}T
.

Thus
M_{T}=\{x,-x\}
and
Tx
is a left-symmetric point of
\ell_{1}^{m}
. However, from
[
6
]
, we get that every left-symmetric point of
\ell_{1}^{m}
is smooth and hence
T
must be smooth by Theorem

∎

We now come to our main result, where we relate the notion of right-symmetry of Birkhoff-James orthogonality with extreme contractions. Recall from
[
8
]
that these are simply defined to be the extreme points of the closed unit ball in
\mathbb{B}(\ell_{\infty}^{n},\ell_{1}^{m})
.

Proof of Theorem
1
for the real case.

Let us denote
\ell_{\infty}^{n}
and
\ell_{1}^{m}
by
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
respectively. Begin by observing that if
M_{T}
contains
n
linearly independent extreme points of
\ell_{\infty}^{n}
, then
T
is an extreme contraction since if
T=\frac{1}{2}(T_{1}+T_{2})
for some
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
having norm 1, then
Tx=\frac{1}{2}(T_{1}x+T_{2}x)
for every extreme point
x
of
X
contained in
M_{T}
, giving
T_{1}x=T_{2}x=Tx
. Since there are
n
-many linearly independent
x
in
M_{T}
, clearly
T
,
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
agree on
\mathbb{X}
proving
T
to be an extreme contraction.

Now, assume the contrary. As
T
is not an extreme contraction,
T
cannot attain norm at more than
n-1
linearly independent extreme points. Let
T
attain norm at
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k}
, which are linearly independent extreme points of
\mathbb{X}
. Extend
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k}\}
to a basis
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}
of
\mathbb{X}
consisting of extreme points. Also, by composing
T
with a suitable signed permutation operator, we can assume that
x_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}e_{j}-2e_{i}
, where
e_{i}
denotes the
i
-th standard basis vector.

Define a map
\Gamma:\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})\to\mathbb{Y}_{\infty}^{n}
given by

\displaystyle\Gamma(T^{\prime}):=(T^{\prime}x_{1},T^{\prime}x_{2},\dots,T^{\prime}x_{n}),\penalty\ T^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}).

Clearly,
\Gamma
is a bijective bounded linear map having norm 1 and it attains norm at
T
. Now, let
T\perp_{B}T^{\prime}
, for some
T^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
. Then by
[
16
, Theorem 2.2]
, there exist

such that

\displaystyle\|Tx+\lambda T^{\prime}x\|\geq\|Tx\|,\penalty\ \|Tx^{\prime}-\lambda T^{\prime}x^{\prime}\|\geq\|Tx^{\prime}\|,\penalty\ \textit{for every}\penalty\ \lambda\geq 0.

Now, if
\|Tx+\lambda T^{\prime}x\|\geq\|Tx\|
for every
\lambda\geq 0
then either
Tx\perp_{B}T^{\prime}x
or
[Tx,T^{\prime}x]>0
for every semi-inner product
[\cdot,\cdot]
on
\mathbb{Y}
since otherwise, there exists
\delta>0
such that
f(T^{\prime}x)\leq-\delta
for every support functional
f
of
Tx
. However, this means that for any support functional
f
of
Tx
,

\displaystyle\|Tx\|=f(Tx)\geq f(Tx+\lambda T^{\prime}x)-\delta\lambda.

Since the support functionals of
Tx
are limit points of the set of support functionals of
Tx+\lambda T^{\prime}x
for every
\lambda>0
, this violates
\|Tx+\lambda T^{\prime}x\|\geq\|Tx\|
for every
\lambda\geq 0
.

Now, if
x\in M_{T}
, then
x
lies in the convex hull of
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k}\}
. If
S
is the smallest subset of
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k}\}
, such that

, then

for every
x_{i}\in S

and

,
\lambda_{i}>0
,
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=1
, then find a support functional
f
of
Tx
such that
f(T^{\prime}x)=0
. Since
f
is a support functional of
Tx_{i}
for every

,

\displaystyle\Psi:y\mapsto\sum\limits_{x_{i}\in S}\lambda_{i}f\left(y_{i}\right),\penalty\ y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{Y}_{\infty}^{n},

is a support functional of
\Gamma(T)
by Proposition
3
, such that
f\left(\Gamma(T^{\prime})\right)=0
. Also, if
[Tx,T^{\prime}x]>0
, then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{x_{i}\in S}\lambda_{i}[T^{\prime}x_{i},Tx_{i}]>0,

. Similarly, if
[Tx,T^{\prime}x]<0

[Tx_{i},T^{\prime}x_{i}]<0

x_{i}\in S

T\perp_{B}T^{\prime}

Tx\perp_{B}T^{\prime}
for some

or

[Ty,T^{\prime}y]<0

[\cdot,\cdot]

\mathbb{X}
for some

\Gamma(T)\perp_{B}\Gamma(T^{\prime})

Now, by Theorem
7

\Gamma(T)

and

and

be any extreme point of
\mathbb{X}

\displaystyle T^{\prime}x=\frac{1}{n-2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}T^{\prime}x_{i},\penalty\ \textit{for every}\penalty\ T^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}).

Else if

\displaystyle T^{\prime}x=\frac{|A|-|B|}{2(n-2)}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}T^{\prime}x_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{i\in A}T^{\prime\prime}x_{i}-\sum\limits_{i\in B}Tx_{i}\right),\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ T^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}).

∎

4.
Analysis for the complex case

Our goal in this final section is to prove Theorem
1
over the complex numbers. For analyzing the geometry of the space of operators
\mathbb{B}(\ell_{\infty}^{n},\ell_{1}^{m})
over

3
holds for the space
\mathbb{X}_{\infty}^{n}
over the complex numbers as well. We now restate Theorem
5
for the complex case:

Theorem 11
.

Given
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})
elements of
\mathbb{X}_{\infty}^{n}
,

if and only if

\displaystyle 0\in\conv\{[y_{i},x_{i}]:\|x_{i}\|=\|x\|,\penalty\ [\cdot,\cdot]\penalty\ \textit{semi-inner product on}\penalty\ \mathbb{X}\}.

Proof.

For the sufficiency, observe that if

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}[x_{k},y_{k}]_{k}=0,

and
[\cdot,\cdot]_{k}
semi-inner products on
\mathbb{X}
for

\displaystyle z=(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n})\mapsto\frac{1}{\|x\|}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}[x_{k},z_{k}]_{k},\penalty\ z\in\mathbb{X}_{\infty}^{n}.

Clearly, the image of

For the necessity, assume

and consider a support functional of

given by
f=(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})
such that

. Construct semi-inner products by extending:

\displaystyle[x_{i},z_{i}]_{i}:=\|x\|g_{i}(z_{i}),

a support functional of

and

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i}\lambda_{i}[y_{i},x_{i}]_{i}=0,

Note that Corollary

Theorem

is restated as:

Given finite-dimensional Banach spaces

and

over

Theorems
1
and

also hold in the complex case, but for proving them, we need the characterization of Birkhoff-James orthogonality in the space of operators over the complex field.

Theorem 13
.

Given finite-dimensional Banach spaces
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
over
\mathbb{C}
and
T,S\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
,
T\perp_{B}S
if and only if

\displaystyle 0\in\conv\{[Sx,Tx]:x\in M_{T},\penalty\ [\cdot,\cdot]\penalty\ \text{semi-inner product on}\penalty\ \mathbb{X}\}.

Proof.

For the sufficiency, note that if

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}[Sx_{i},Tx_{i}]_{i}=0,

then

\displaystyle S^{\prime}\mapsto\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}[S^{\prime}x_{i},Tx_{i}]_{i},\penalty\ S^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}),

is a support functional of
T
that annihilates
S
.

For the necessity, note that we can embed
\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
within
C(S_{\mathbb{X}},\mathbb{Y})

\mathbb{Y}

S_{\mathbb{X}}
equipped with the supremum norm. Now, applying the Riesz representation theorem, since

is finite, the dual of

is given by the space of all
\mathbb{Y}^{*}

S_{\mathbb{X}}

Elementary computation yields the characterization of the support functional of a non-zero element

as

such that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{X}Gd\mu=\int\limits_{M_{F}}\Psi(x)\left(G(x)\right)d|\mu|(x),\penalty\ G\in C(S_{\mathbb{X}},\mathbb{Y}),

where
\Psi(x)
is some support functional of

for

, the set of points in
S_{\mathbb{X}}
where

attains its norm and

is the total variation of the measure
\mu
with

Hence if

in
\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})

as elements of

as well. However, the characterization of the support functionals in

gives that

in
C(S_{\mathbb{X}},\mathbb{Y})
implies

\displaystyle 0\in\conv\{[Tx,Sx]:x\in M_{T},\penalty\ [\cdot,\cdot]\penalty\ \text{semi-inner product on}\penalty\ \mathbb{X}\}.

The proof is similar to the proof of
[
5

.
∎

This characterization of Birkhoff-James orthogonality in

immediately allows us to prove Theorem

We finish by proving Theorem

Proof of Theorem

Begin by noting that given any complex Banach space

∎

The coupled-cluster self-energy
Christopher J. N. Coveney, David P. Tew

Abstract
Coupled-cluster and Green’s function theories are highly successful in treating many-body electron correlation and there has been significant interest in identifying and leveraging connections between them. Here we present a diagrammatic definition of the irreducible coupled-cluster self-energy that directly embeds coupled-cluster theory within the framework of many-body field theory. The EOM-CC treatment emerges naturally from our definition via the Dyson and Bethe-Salpeter equations, providing a unified description of RPA, G ​ W GW -BSE and CC theory for ground state and excitation energies. This clarifies the origin of previously established connections between RPA, G ​ W GW -BSE and coupled-cluster theory, and exposes the relationship between vertex corrections and the coupled-cluster amplitude equations.



I
Introduction

The single-particle Green’s function is one of the central objects in the quantum theory of many-particle systems.
Fetter and Walecka 1971
;
Mahan 2000
;
Stefanucci and Van Leeuwen 2013
From knowledge of the single-particle Green’s function we can obtain the exact addition and removal energies, the expectation value of any single-particle observable and, in the case of a two-body interaction, the exact ground state energy of a system of many interacting particles. In many-body field theory, the irreducible self-energy has equal importance.
Dyson 1949
The self-energy connects a non-interacting to an interacting Green’s function through the Dyson equation and implicitly contains all the many-body interactions a single-particle experiences. The usefulness of the self-energy stems from the fact that it may be defined in terms of a Feynman diagram series expansion without
a priori
knowledge of the Green’s function.

Hedin’s equations define a formally exact procedure for generating the many-body self-energy by expressing its infinite Feynman diagram series in terms of the vertex function.
Hedin 1965
However, no known functional form for the vertex exists and inclusion of vertex corrections to access higher-order many-body correlation effects is neither rigorous nor trivial.
Pokhilko et al. 2021
;
Martin et al. 2016
;
Verdozzi et al. 1995
;
Von Friesen et al. 2009
;
Romaniello et al. 2009
;
von Friesen et al. 2010
;
Romaniello et al. 2012
The lack of a controllable and systematically improvable expression for the self-energy in traditional field theory arises due to the fact that these approaches do not assume a closed form expression for the exact many-body ground state wavefunction.
Kohn and Luttinger 1960
;
Luttinger and Ward 1960
;
Luttinger 1961
;
Potthoff 2014
;
van Setten et al. 2013

Ground state coupled-cluster (CC) theory represents a reformation of the Configuration Interaction that preserves the correct many-body product structure of the electronic ground state wavefunction. As a result, CC theory allows for proper parameterisation of
N
-body correlation effects that lead to a hierarchical, systematically improvable set of equations for the exact ground state energy and wavefunction. Commonly, ionization potentials, electron affinities and neutral excitation energies in coupled-cluster theory are obtained from application of linear response equation-of-motion theory to the coupled-cluster ansatz (EOM-CC). This is in contrast to the field theoretic approach which is not based on a wavefunction representation but instead works with dynamical correlation functions known as many-body Green’s functions.

Nooijen and Snijders’ formulation of the coupled-cluster Green’s function (CCGF) was based on the direct construction of the Green’s function using quantities obtained from ground state and EOM-CC theory and has led to many practical advantages over conventional many-body theory for the computation of addition and removal energies.
Nooijen and Snijders 1992
;
Nooijen and Snijders 1993
;
Peng and Kowalski 2018
;
Shee and Zgid 2019
;
Backhouse and Booth 2022
However, this formulation does not involve the explicit construction of the irreducible coupled-cluster self-energy and is not yet fully connected to the conventional formulation of many-body field theory. As a result, the connections between the CCGF and the CC Bethe-Salpeter equation have remained elusive.

The recent analysis of the supermatrix formulation of the quasiparticle equation within the
GW
and GF2 approximations has led to significant advances in our understanding of the dynamical effects contained in the many-body self-energy.
Bintrim and Berkelbach 2021
;
Bintrim and Berkelbach 2022
;
Scott et al. 2023
;
Quintero-Monsebaiz et al. 2022
;
Monino and Loos 2023
;
Tölle and Kin-Lic Chan 2023
The relationship between the random-phase approximation (RPA) and the coupled-cluster doubles (CCD) amplitude equations by retaining only ‘ring coupled-cluster’ diagrams is now well established.
Scuseria et al. 2008
;
Berkelbach 2018
;
Rishi et al. 2020
;
Quintero-Monsebaiz et al. 2022
Recent work on uncovering formal and numerical similarities between the
G_{0}W_{0}
supermatrix and
approximate
forms of the ionization potential/electron affinity EOM-CC singles and doubles (IP/EA-EOM-CCSD) eigenvalue problem has also demonstrated how improved formulations of the quasiparticle equation may be constructed.
Lange and Berkelbach 2018
;
Tölle and Kin-Lic Chan 2023

However, no diagrammatic definition of the coupled-cluster self-energy has been introduced that makes clear its relationship to conventional Green’s function theory. This is the subject of the present paper. Drawing on the preceding work, here we reveal the structure of the coupled-cluster self-energy along with its relationship to EOM-CC and the Green’s function formalism. This allows us to define the notion of a coupled-cluster
self-energy that retains the functional role of the self-energy in conventional
many-body theory in how it is used to define equations governing
different observables such as charged and neutral excitation energies. The power of this approach rests in the formally exact coupled-cluster ansatz for the ground state wavefunction and a self-energy that directly connects to that of conventional many-body field theory.

II
single-particle Green’s function and Coupled-cluster theory

In this section we present a brief overview of Green’s function theory, ground state coupled-cluster theory and equation-of-motion coupled-cluster theory. In the following, indices and wavevectors
i\mathbf{k}_{i},j\mathbf{k}_{j},\cdots
denote occupied (valence band) spin-orbitals,
a\mathbf{k}_{a},b\mathbf{k}_{b},\cdots
virtuals (conduction band) and
p\mathbf{k}_{p},q\mathbf{k}_{q},\cdots
general spin-orbitals. The wavevector dependence is omitted for clarity.

II.1
Electronic Green’s function and self-energy

The single-particle electronic Green’s function is defined as

~iG_{pq}(t_{1}-t_{2})=\braket{\Psi_{0}|\mathcal{T}\left\{a_{p}(t_{1})a^{\dagger}_{q}(t_{2})\right\}|\Psi_{0}},

(1)

where
a^{\dagger}_{p}(t)/a_{p}(t)
create/annihilate electrons in spin-orbital
\ket{\phi_{p}}
in the Heisenberg representation,
\mathcal{T}
is the time-ordering operator and
\ket{\Psi_{0}}
is the normalized, exact ground state.
Fetter and Walecka 1971
The poles of the exact Green’s function correspond to the exact ionization potentials and electron affinities of the system. From the equation of motion of the Green’s function, the exact ground state energy can be obtained from the Galitskii-Migdal formula.
Galitskii and Migdal 1958
Therefore, the Green’s function simultaneously contains both the exact single-particle spectrum and ground state energy. From the Dyson equation, it can be shown that the ionization potentials, electron affinities and Dyson orbitals can be obtained by solution of the quasiparticle equation
Hybertsen and Louie 1986
;
Fetter and Walecka 1971

[f+\Sigma^{c}(\omega=\varepsilon_{p})]\ket{\psi_{p}}=\varepsilon_{p}\ket{\psi_{p}}.

(2)

Here,
f
is the Fock operator and
\Sigma^{c}
is the many-body self-energy. The frequency-dependence of the self-energy captures the higher-order interactions whilst remaining in the single-particle spin-orbital basis. In contradistinction with the analytic diagrammatic expansion, the exact self-energy may also be defined by direct inversion of the Dyson equation

\displaystyle\begin{split}~\Sigma^{c}_{pq}(\omega)=[G^{0}_{pq}(\omega)]^{-1}-G^{-1}_{pq}(\omega)\ ,\end{split}

(3)

where
G^{0}_{pq}
is the ‘non-interacting’ Green’s function obtained by replacing
\ket{\Psi_{0}}
in Eq.
1
with the reference,
\ket{\Phi_{0}}
, where the time-dependence of the field operators is governed by the Fock operator. This is not a functional relationship as it requires
a priori
knowledge of the exact Green’s function; the power of the Dyson equation turns on the separate existence of a diagrammatic expansion for the self-energy which preserves the analytic pole structure of the Green’s function.

The spectral form of the electronic many-body self-energy is given by
Schirmer et al. 1983
;
Caruso et al. 2013
;
Raimondi and Barbieri 2018
;
Schirmer 2018

\displaystyle\begin{split}~\Sigma^{c}_{pq}(\omega)&=\Sigma^{\infty}_{pq}+\sum_{JJ^{\prime}}U^{\dagger}_{pJ}\left[(\omega+i\eta)\mathbbm{1}-(\mathbf{K}^{>}+\mathbf{C}^{>})\right]^{-1}_{JJ^{\prime}}U_{J^{\prime}q}\\
&+\sum_{AA^{\prime}}V_{pA}\left[(\omega-i\eta)\mathbbm{1}-(\mathbf{K}^{<}+\mathbf{C}^{<})\right]^{-1}_{AA^{\prime}}V^{\dagger}_{A^{\prime}q}\ .\end{split}

(4)

Using this expression, the frequency-dependent quasiparticle equation (Eq.
2
) is equivalent to diagonalization of the Dyson supermatrix
Raimondi and Barbieri 2018
;
Schirmer 2018

\displaystyle\begin{split}~\mathbf{D}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}f_{pq}+\Sigma^{\infty}_{pq}&U^{\dagger}_{pJ^{\prime}}&V_{pA^{\prime}}\\
U_{Jq}&(\mathbf{K}^{>}_{JJ^{\prime}}+\mathbf{C}^{>}_{JJ^{\prime}})&\mathbf{0}\\
V^{\dagger}_{Aq}&\mathbf{0}&(\mathbf{K}^{<}_{AA^{\prime}}+\mathbf{C}^{<}_{AA^{\prime}})\end{array}\right).\end{split}

(5)

The indices
JJ^{\prime}/AA^{\prime}
label forward-time/backward-time Intermediate State Configurations (ISCs) and outline the character of the different multi-particle-hole configurations that a single-particle state may be connected to. ISCs are excited state configurations containing
(N\pm 1)
-electrons that mediate between exact energy eigenstates and electronic configurations with respect to the reference.
Schirmer 2018
The matrices
(\mathbf{K}^{>}_{JJ^{\prime}}+\mathbf{C}^{>}_{JJ^{\prime}})
and
(\mathbf{K}^{<}_{AA^{\prime}}+\mathbf{C}^{<}_{AA^{\prime}})
represent the interactions between the different ISCs. The quantities
U_{qJ}
and
V_{pA}
represent the coupling matrices that link initial and final single-particle states to the ISCs.
The upper left block of
\mathbf{D}
is defined over the complete set of single-particle spin-orbitals, with the coupling and interaction matrices containing coupling to all possible ISCs. The zero entries of the Dyson supermatrix are present as the forward and backward time self-energy contributions are coupled only through the initial and final single-particle states. Diagonalization of the Dyson supermatrix yields the complete set of ionization potentials and electron affinities.

From knowledge of the electronic self-energy and single-particle Green’s function, the ground state correlation energy may be found from the Galitskii-Migdal formula as
Galitskii and Migdal 1958
;
Coveney and Tew 2023
;
Backhouse and Booth 2020

\displaystyle\begin{split}~E_{c}&=-\frac{i}{2}\lim_{\eta\to 0^{+}}\sum_{pq}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{i\eta\omega}\Sigma^{c}_{pq}(\omega)G_{qp}(\omega)\\
&=-\frac{i}{2}\int d3\ \Sigma(1,3)G(3,1^{+})\ ,\end{split}

(6)

where the second equality stems from the space-spin-time representation,
1\equiv(\mathbf{r}_{1}\sigma_{1},t_{1})
is the composite spin-space-time coordinate and
\int d1\equiv\int d\mathbf{r}_{1}d\sigma_{1}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}dt_{1}
.
At zero temperature, the self-energy is given by the functional derivative of the ground state correlation energy with respect to the Green’s function
Luttinger and Ward 1960
;
Luttinger 1961

\displaystyle\begin{split}~\Sigma^{c}(1,2)=i\frac{\delta E_{c}}{\delta G(2,1)}\ .\end{split}

(7)

Eq.
7
highlights the fact that the self-energy may be constructed as a functional of the dressed Green’s function and two-body coulomb interaction.
Stefanucci and Van Leeuwen 2013
;
Hedin 1965
Finally, the relationship between the one-body reduced density matrix and the single-particle Green’s function is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{pq}(t-t^{+})=i\braket{\Psi_{0}|a^{\dagger}_{q}a_{p}|\Psi_{0}}\ ,\end{split}

(8)

where the notation,
G_{pq}(t-t^{+})
, denotes evaluation of the second time argument of the Green’s function with a positive infinitesimal,
i.e.

t^{+}=t+\eta
, where
\eta\to 0
from above.

II.2
Overview of coupled-cluster theory

In CC theory, the many-body electronic ground state is expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\ket{\Psi^{\mathrm{CC}}_{0}}=e^{T}\ket{\Phi},\end{split}

(9a)

where
T
creates all excitations with respect to a reference determinant
\ket{\Phi}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}T&=\sum_{ai}t^{a}_{i}a^{\dagger}_{a}a_{i}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{abij}t^{ab}_{ij}a^{\dagger}_{a}a^{\dagger}_{b}a_{j}a_{i}\\
&+\frac{1}{36}\sum_{abcijk}t^{abc}_{ijk}a^{\dagger}_{a}a^{\dagger}_{b}a^{\dagger}_{c}a_{k}a_{j}a_{i}+\cdots\end{split}

(9b)

Here,
\{t^{a}_{i},t^{ab}_{ij},t^{abc}_{ijk},\cdots\}
are the cluster amplitudes. The ground state energy and cluster amplitudes are obtained by projection onto the manifold of Slater determinants

\displaystyle\begin{split}\braket{\Phi|\bar{H}|\Phi}=E^{\mathrm{CC}}_{0}\end{split}

(10a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\braket{\Phi^{a}_{i}|\bar{H}|\Phi}=0\end{split}

(10b)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\braket{\Phi^{ab}_{ij}|\bar{H}|\Phi}=0\end{split}

(10c)

and so on, where

\bar{H}=e^{-T}He^{T}

(10d)

is the similarity transformed Hamiltonian. The manifold of excited Slater determinants are given by
\ket{\Phi^{a}_{i}}=a^{\dagger}_{a}a_{i}\ket{\Phi}
etc. When
T
is not truncated, the ground state energy obtained from this procedure is formally exact. Importantly, the similarity transformed Hamiltonian
\bar{H}
, is non-Hermitian, possessing different left and right eigenstates.

In standard coupled-cluster theory, the reference determinant,
\ket{\Phi}
, corresponds to the Hartree–Fock wavefunction constructed from spin-orbitals that are determined by separate optimization of the reference. However, coupled-cluster theory may also be expressed with respect to an arbitrary Slater determinant in an alternative spin-orbital basis. One of the most useful reformulations of coupled-cluster theory is Brueckner coupled-cluster (BCC), whereby the spin-orbitals are determined simultaneously with the optimization of the cluster amplitudes.
Scuseria 1995
;
Helgaker et al. 2013
This is performed by an alternative parametrization of the singles amplitudes by rotating the spin-orbital basis via

\displaystyle\begin{split}\ket{\Phi_{0}}=\exp\left(-\kappa\right)\ket{\Phi},\end{split}

(11)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\kappa=\sum_{ai}\kappa_{ai}\left(a^{\dagger}_{a}a_{i}-a^{\dagger}_{i}a_{a}\right)\end{split}

(12)

is anti-Hermitian
\kappa=-\kappa^{\dagger}
, and
\kappa_{ai}
is the orbital-rotation matrix to be determined. The orbital-rotation results in the corresponding unitary transformation of the creation (annihilation) operators

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{p}=e^{-\kappa}a^{\dagger}_{p}e^{\kappa}\end{split}

(13)

to the new spin-orbital basis. Within Brueckner theory, the orbital-rotation parameters,
\{\kappa_{ai}\}
, are determined by the singles amplitude equations. The result is that the BCC ground state wavefunction has the form of a standard CC wavefunction, but where the singles amplitudes,
\{t^{a}_{i}\}
, are eliminated by construction as they are already ‘accounted’ for by transformation to the Brueckner basis. For simplicity, in the rest of this paper we will work in the Brueckner basis where we have already accounted for the orbital-rotation. However, our formalism can be easily generalized to include singles amplitudes.

As first proposed in Refs
18
and
19
, one may construct the CCGF from the corresponding EOM-CC eigenvalue problem. The IPs and EAs are the eigenvalues of the similarity transformed Hamiltonian
\bar{H}
in the basis of all Slater determinants containing (
N\pm 1
)-electrons. For example, the IP-EOM-CC eigenvalue problem is written for the right eigenstates as

~-\bar{H}_{N}R^{\mathrm{IP}}_{\nu}\ket{\Phi_{0}}=\varepsilon^{N-1}_{\nu}R^{\mathrm{IP}}_{\nu}\ket{\Phi_{0}},

(14)

where
\bar{H}_{N}=\bar{H}-E^{\mathrm{CC}}_{0}
is the normal-ordered similarity transformed Hamiltonian and the state
R^{\mathrm{IP}}_{\nu}\ket{\Phi_{0}}
is the exact
N-1
electron, right eigenstate of the similarity transformed Hamiltonian. Importantly, Eq.
14
implicitly requires
[R^{\mathrm{IP}}_{\nu},T]=0
. When
T
is not truncated, the eigenvalues
\varepsilon_{\nu}^{N-1}
are the exact ionization potentials of the system and correspond to the poles of the exact Green’s function.
Nooijen and Snijders 1992
;
Nooijen and Snijders 1993
;
Lange and Berkelbach 2018
In IP-EOM-CC theory, the equation-of-motion operator creates all determinants consisting of
(N-1)
-electrons and corresponds to the choice

~R^{\mathrm{IP}}_{\nu}=\sum_{i}r_{i}(\nu)a_{i}+\sum_{i<j,a}r^{a}_{ij}(\nu)a^{\dagger}_{a}a_{j}a_{i}+\cdots

(15)

With this definition of the equation-of-motion operator, Eq.
14
is re-written in terms the IP-EOM-CC supermatrix

\displaystyle\begin{split}~-\mathbf{\bar{H}}&{}^{\text{IP-EOM-CC}}=\\
&\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\braket{\Phi_{i}|\bar{H}_{N}|\Phi_{j}}&\braket{\Phi_{i}|\bar{H}_{N}|\Phi^{a}_{kl}}&\cdots\\
\braket{\Phi^{b}_{mn}|\bar{H}_{N}|\Phi_{j}}&\braket{\Phi^{b}_{mn}|\bar{H}_{N}|\Phi^{a}_{kl}}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{array}\right)\end{split}

(16)

Diagonalization of this supermatrix yields the left and right
(N-1)
-electron eigenstates of
\bar{H}
. The left and right eigenstates of the IP/EA-EOM-CC eigenvalue problem form a complete biorthogonal set in the
(N\pm 1)
-electron space, and can be used to construct the CCGF from the Lehmann representation. However, the associated coupled-cluster self-energy exists only numerically by explicit inversion of the Dyson equation (Eq.
3
) and does not admit a diagrammatic definition.
Nooijen and Snijders 1992
;
Nooijen and Snijders 1993
Thus, this approach to the coupled-cluster self-energy does not expose its relationship to the Bethe-Salpeter kernel and the general formalism of many-body field theory. Additionally, calculation of the ground state energy at any level of approximation for the CCGF results in a different ground state energy from that obtained at the same level of approximation of the ground state coupled-cluster equations.
Nooijen and Snijders 1992
;
Nooijen and Snijders 1993

In this work, we introduce an alternative formalism that focuses on constructing the coupled-cluster self-energy based on the universal relationships between the interaction kernels of many-body field theory.
Hedin 1965
;
Kohn and Luttinger 1960
;
Luttinger and Ward 1960

III
Relationship between the similarity transformed Hamiltonian and many-body Green’s functions

To expose the relationship between the effective interactions generated by the similarity transformed Hamiltonian and many-body Green’s functions, it is necessary to outline the relationship between normal-ordering and Green’s functions.

III.1
Relationship between the Fock operator and ‘non-interacting’ Green’s function

The electronic structure Hamiltonian is written in second quantization as

\begin{split}H=\sum_{pq}h_{pq}a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{q}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{pq,rs}\braket{pq\|rs}a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{q}a_{s}a_{r}\end{split}

(17)

where
h_{pq}
is the one-body term that includes the kinetic energy and attractive interaction due to the potential of the nuclei, whilst
\braket{pq\|rs}=\braket{pq|rs}-\braket{pq|sr}
is the antisymmetrized Coulomb interaction. The electronic structure Hamiltonian may be normal-ordered with respect to a reference determinant to give

\begin{split}H=E^{\mathrm{ref}}_{0}+\sum_{pq}f_{pq}\{a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{q}\}_{0}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{pq,rs}\braket{pq\|rs}\{a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{q}a_{s}a_{r}\}_{0}\end{split}

(18)

where the notation
\{\cdots\}_{0}
indicates a normal-ordered string of operators with respect to the reference,
\ket{\Phi_{0}}
. The reference energy is given by the expectation value of the Hamiltonian over the reference state,
E^{\mathrm{ref}}_{0}=\braket{\Phi_{0}|H|\Phi_{0}}
. The Fock operator,
f_{pq}
, is generated by the procedure of normal-ordering by contraction of the Coulomb interaction with the ‘non-interacting’ one-body reduced density matrix via

~f_{pq}=h_{pq}-i\sum_{rs}\braket{pr\|qs}G^{0}_{sr}(t-t^{+})\ .

(19)

In what follows, we find it useful to introduce the following notation

\displaystyle\begin{split}~\tilde{G}_{pq}=G^{0}_{pq}(t-t^{+})=i\braket{\Phi_{0}|a^{\dagger}_{q}a_{p}|\Phi_{0}}\end{split}

(20)

to denote the equal-time ‘non-interacting’ Green’s function. Therefore, we re-write the Fock operator as

f_{pq}=h_{pq}-i\sum_{rs}\braket{pr\|qs}\tilde{G}_{sr}\ ,

(21)

with the property that

\displaystyle\begin{split}~i\frac{\delta f_{pr}}{\delta\tilde{G}_{sq}}=\braket{pq\|rs}\ .\end{split}

(22)

III.2
Normal-ordering the similarity transformed Hamiltonian

The similarity transformed Hamiltonian, in its full generality, is an
N
-body operator when
T
is not truncated and is written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{H}&=\sum_{pq}\bar{h}_{pq}a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{q}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{pq,rs}\bar{h}_{pq,rs}a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{q}a_{s}a_{r}\\
&+\frac{1}{36}\sum_{pqr,stu}\bar{h}_{pqr,stu}a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{q}a^{\dagger}_{r}a_{u}a_{t}a_{s}+\cdots\ ,\end{split}

(23)

where
\{\bar{h}_{pq,rs},\bar{h}_{pqr,stu},\cdots\}
are antisymmetrized two-body, three-body and so on, matrix elements. Normal-ordering
\bar{H}
with respect to the reference determinant gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}~\bar{H}&=E^{\mathrm{CC}}_{0}+\sum_{pq}F_{pq}\{a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{q}\}_{0}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{pq,rs}\chi_{pq,rs}\{a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{q}a_{s}a_{r}\}_{0}\\
&+\frac{1}{36}\sum_{pqr,stu}\chi_{pqr,stu}\{a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{q}a^{\dagger}_{r}a_{u}a_{t}a_{s}\}_{0}+\cdots\end{split}

(24)

The explicit expressions for the effective interaction matrix elements,
\{F_{pq},\chi_{pq,rs},\chi_{pqr,stu},\cdots\}
, up to the four-body interaction elements can be found in Refs
43
and
44
. The amplitude equations are contained in the effective interaction elements as

\displaystyle\begin{split}~F_{ai}=\braket{\Phi^{a}_{i}|\bar{H}_{N}|\Phi_{0}}\end{split}

(25a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}~\chi_{ab,ij}=\braket{\Phi^{ab}_{ij}|\bar{H}_{N}|\Phi_{0}}\end{split}

(25b)

\displaystyle\begin{split}~\chi_{abc,ijk}=\braket{\Phi^{abc}_{ijk}|\bar{H}_{N}|\Phi_{0}}\end{split}

(25c)

and so on, which all evaluate to zero at convergence.
Shavitt and Bartlett 2009

For what follows, it is central to note that the following relationships hold (see Appendix
A
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}~\chi_{pq,rs}=i\frac{\delta F_{pr}}{\delta\tilde{G}_{sq}}\ ,\end{split}

(26a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}~\chi_{pqr,stu}=i\frac{\delta\chi_{pq,st}}{\delta\tilde{G}_{ur}}\ ,\end{split}

(26b)

and so on for the higher-order effective interactions appearing in the normal-ordered similarity transformed Hamiltonian. Eqs
26a
and
26b
and so on for the higher-order effective interactions can also be viewed as generalizations of Eq.
22
for the relationship between the Fock operator and the two-body Coulomb interaction.

By the normal-ordering procedure, the corresponding effective interaction matrix elements arise by contraction with density matrices which are related to many-body Green’s functions as follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}~F_{pq}&=\bar{h}_{pq}-i\sum_{rs}\bar{h}_{pr,qs}G^{0}_{sr}(t-t^{+})\\
&+\frac{i}{4}\sum_{rs,tu}\bar{h}_{prs,qtu}G^{2\mathrm{p}\mathrm{h},(0)}_{tu,rs}(t-t^{+})+\cdots\end{split}

(27a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}~\chi_{pq,rs}&=\bar{h}_{pq,rs}-i\sum_{tu}\bar{h}_{pqt,rsu}G^{0}_{ut}(t-t^{+})\\
&+\frac{i}{4}\sum_{tuwv}\bar{h}_{pqtu,rswv}G^{2\mathrm{p}\mathrm{h},(0)}_{wv,tu}(t-t^{+})+\cdots\end{split}

(27b)

\displaystyle\begin{split}~\chi_{pqr,stu}&=\bar{h}_{pqr,stu}-i\sum_{wv}\bar{h}_{pqrw,stuv}G^{0}_{vw}(t-t^{+})\\
&+\frac{i}{4}\sum_{wvo\sigma}\bar{h}_{pqrwv,stuo\sigma}G^{2\mathrm{p}\mathrm{h},(0)}_{o\sigma,wv}(t-t^{+})+\cdots\end{split}

(27c)

and so on, where
G^{2\mathrm{p}\mathrm{h},(0)}
is the non-interacting two-particle-hole Green’s function defined in Appendix
A
. Implicitly, all expressions for the effective interaction matrix elements continue with the contraction of the higher-order matrix elements with the corresponding higher-order Green’s functions. These expressions represent a generalization of Eq.
19
in the presence of higher-body interactions.

III.3
The coupled-cluster self-energy of the Lagrangian

The BCC energy is given by the extremum of the Lagrangian equation

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{BCC}}_{c}=\braket{\Phi_{0}|\bar{H}_{N}|\Phi_{0}}+\sum_{\mu}\lambda_{\mu}\braket{\Phi_{\mu}|\bar{H}_{N}|\Phi_{0}}\ ,

(28)

where
\braket{\Phi_{\mu}|\bar{H}_{N}|\Phi_{0}}
represent the complete set of amplitude equations, with
\bar{H}_{N}=\bar{H}-E^{\mathrm{ref}}_{0}
, and
\lambda_{\mu}=\{\lambda^{i}_{a},\lambda^{ij}_{ab},\lambda^{ijk}_{abc},\cdots\}
are the associated Lagrange multipliers used to determine the left ground eigenstate of
\bar{H}
. The first term on the right hand side of Eq.
28
is the Brueckner correlation energy,
E^{\mathrm{BCC}}_{c}
. At convergence, the second term of the right hand side of Eq.
28
vanishes as the amplitude equations are satisfied,
\braket{\Phi_{\mu}|\bar{H}_{N}|\Phi_{0}}=0
. The ground state correlation energy is expanded to give
Shavitt and Bartlett 2009

V
Relationship to IP/EA-EOM-CC theory

To uncover the relationship between the coupled-cluster self-energy and IP/EA-EOM-CC theory, we first introduce the spectral form of the coupled-cluster self-energy and the coupled-cluster Dyson supermatrix, whose eigenvalues give the exact charged excitation energies. We then proceed to show how replacing the interaction kernels derived in this work with their ‘zeroth-order’ counterparts results in the equivalence between the IP/EA-EOM-CCSD eigenvalue problem and the coupled-cluster self-energy.

V.1
The dynamical coupled-cluster self-energy

The spectral form of the non-hermitian dynamical coupled-cluster self-energy is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}~\tilde{\Sigma}_{pq}(\omega)&=\tilde{\Sigma}^{\infty}_{pq}+\sum_{JJ^{\prime}}\tilde{U}_{pJ}\left[(\omega+i\eta)\mathbbm{1}-(\mathbf{\bar{K}}^{>}+\mathbf{\bar{C}}^{>})\right]^{-1}_{JJ^{\prime}}\bar{U}_{J^{\prime}q}\\
&+\sum_{AA^{\prime}}\bar{V}_{pA}\left[(\omega-i\eta)\mathbbm{1}-(\mathbf{\bar{K}}^{<}+\mathbf{\bar{C}}^{<})\right]^{-1}_{AA^{\prime}}\tilde{V}_{A^{\prime}q}\ .\end{split}

(72)

Using Eq.
72
, the frequency-dependent quasiparticle equation (Eq.
2
) is equivalent to diagonalization of the coupled-cluster Dyson supermatrix

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{\tilde{D}}^{\mathrm{CC}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}f_{pq}+\tilde{\Sigma}^{\infty}_{pq}&\tilde{U}_{pJ^{\prime}}&\bar{V}_{pA^{\prime}}\\
\bar{U}_{Jq}&(\mathbf{\bar{K}}^{>}_{JJ^{\prime}}+\mathbf{\bar{C}}^{>}_{JJ^{\prime}})&\mathbf{0}\\
\tilde{V}_{Aq}&\mathbf{0}&(\mathbf{\bar{K}}^{<}_{AA^{\prime}}+\mathbf{\bar{C}}^{<}_{AA^{\prime}})\end{array}\right).\end{split}

(73)

The indices
JJ^{\prime}/AA^{\prime}
label forward-time/backward-time ISCs. Here, the coupling matrices mediate the coupling of the initial and final single-particle states to the ISCs induced by the similarity transformed Hamiltonian,
\bar{H}
. The interaction matrices,
(\mathbf{\bar{K}}^{>}_{JJ^{\prime}}+\mathbf{\bar{C}}^{>}_{JJ^{\prime}})
and
(\mathbf{\bar{K}}^{<}_{AA^{\prime}}+\mathbf{\bar{C}}^{<}_{AA^{\prime}})
, represent the interactions between the different ISCs mediated by
\bar{H}
. We will subsequently demonstrate that the ‘interaction matrices’ appearing in IP/EA-EOM-CCSD are given by 2h1p/2p1h matrix elements of the similarity transformed Hamiltonian (see Eq.
81
). Importantly, for the non-hermitian coupled-cluster self-energy, the coupling matrices,
\bar{U}_{pJ}/\tilde{U}_{Jp}
and
\bar{V}_{pA}/\tilde{V}_{Ap}
, are not simply related to each other via hermitian conjugation. This is to be contrasted with the coupling matrices of the electronic Dyson supermatrix defined in Eq.
5
.
Similarly, the upper left block of
\mathbf{\tilde{D}}^{\mathrm{CC}}
is defined over the complete set of single-particle spin-orbitals.

V.2
Connection to EOM-CCSD eigenvalue problem

Now, we identify the relationship between the interaction kernels derived in this work and the IP-EOM-CCSD eigenvalue problem in order to show the general relationship between IP-EOM-CC theory and the coupled-cluster self-energy for a particular case. To expose the connection to IP-EOM-CCSD theory, we truncate the ISCs to be of 2h1p/2p1h character only and restrict our analysis to the occupied-occupied block of the self-energy. In this case, the coupling matrices that connect the initial and final single-particle states to the ISCs are given by variation of the quasiparticle Hamiltonian defined in Eq.
47
. Focusing on the coupling matrices that connect the ISCs to initial and final single-particle states in the occupied-occupied subspace, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{V}_{i,kla}&=i\frac{\delta\tilde{F}_{ik}}{\delta\tilde{G}_{la}}=\tilde{\Xi}_{ia,kl}\end{split}

(74a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{V}_{mnb,j}&=i\frac{\delta\tilde{F}_{mj}}{\delta\tilde{G}_{bn}}=\tilde{\Xi}_{mn,jb}\end{split}

(74b)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{U}_{i,kab}=i\frac{\delta\tilde{F}_{ia}}{\delta\tilde{G}_{bk}}=\tilde{\Xi}_{ik,ab}\end{split}

(74c)

\displaystyle\begin{split}~\bar{U}_{lcd,j}=i\frac{\delta\tilde{F}_{cj}}{\delta\tilde{G}_{ld}}=\tilde{\Xi}_{cd,jl}\ ,\end{split}

(74d)

where the quantities
\tilde{\Xi}_{pq,rs}
are defined in Eq.

&+\cdots\\

(75)

At ‘zeroth-order’, using the expression for
\tilde{\Sigma}^{\infty(0)}_{pq}

and

VI
Conclusions and Outlook

In summary, we have introduced a natural definition of the coupled-cluster self-energy that unifies elements of coupled-cluster theory with the Green’s function formalism. By appealing to the coupled-cluster similarity transformed Hamiltonian, we present a closed form expression for the self-energy that is systematically improvable via the coupled-cluster amplitude equations. We have discovered that the EOM-CC treatment naturally emerges as a consequence of our formalism, providing us with a unified description of RPA,
GW
-BSE and coupled-cluster theory for ground state and excitation energies. Finally, we have explicitly demonstrated how the IP/EA-EOM-CC eigenvalue problem emerges from our definition of the coupled-cluster self-energy. The connections uncovered here represent a major step towards a unified description of the seemingly disconnected approaches of coupled-cluster theory and the Green’s function formalism. We hope that our findings will stimulate renewed work towards an improved formulation of Green’s function theory for correlated excited state phenomena.
Coveney and Tew 2025

(90)

Using this relationship, the extended Fock operator (Eq.

\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{pr}&=\bar{h}_{pr}-i\sum_{ut}\bar{h}_{pu,rt}\tilde{G}_{tu}\\

(93)

Now, taking the functional derivative of this expression with respect to the equal-time non-interacting Green’s function, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}~i\frac{\delta F_{pr}}{\delta\tilde{G}_{sq}}&=\bar{h}_{pq,rs}+\frac{i}{4}\sum_{ut,vw}\bar{h}_{put,rwv}(\delta_{vs}\delta_{uq}\tilde{G}_{wt}+\delta_{tq}\delta_{ws}\tilde{G}_{vu}\\

(94)

The second term of Eq.

\displaystyle\begin{split}i\frac{\delta F_{pr}}{\delta\tilde{G}_{sq}}&=\bar{h}_{pq,rs}-i\sum_{ut}\bar{h}_{pqu,rst}\tilde{G}_{tu}\\

On Isolated Gaps of Numerical Semigroups of embedding dimension two
Shubh N. Singh, Ranjan K. Ram

Abstract
. We explicitly describe all the isolated gaps of any numerical semigroup of embedding dimension two, and we give an exact formula for the number of isolated gaps of these numerical semigroups.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
20M14, 11D04

1.
Introduction

Let
\N
denote the set of all nonnegative integers. A
numerical semigroup
is a nonempty subset
S
of
\N
such that
S
is closed under addition,
0\in S
, and the set
\N\setminus S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon n\notin S\}
is finite. The
set of gaps

G(S)
of a numerical semigroup
S
is the finite set
\N\setminus S
. Given positive integers
a_{1}<\cdots<a_{m}
, let
\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\rangle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\lambda_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{m}a_{m}\colon\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\in\N\}
. It is well-known that
\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\rangle
is a numerical semigroup if and only if the greatest common divisor of
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
is one (cf.
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
), and every numerical semigroup has this form (cf.
[
6
, Theorem 2.7]
).

In
2019
, Moreno-Frías and Rosales
[
2
, p. 1742]
defined an
isolated gap
of a numerical semigroup
S
to be an element
x\in G(S)
such that
\{x-1,x+1\}\subseteq S
, and called a numerical semigroup
perfect
if it does not contain any isolated gaps. Moreno-Frías and Rosales proved that any numerical semigroup of embedding dimension two is not perfect
[
3
, Corollary 4]
. Further, Moreno-Frías and Rosales characterized perfect numerical semigroups of embedding dimension three
[
3
, Proposition 8]
. Smith studied various properties of isolated gaps of any non-perfect numerical semigroup
[
7
]
. Smith proved that if
h
is the smallest isolated gap of a numerical semigroup
\langle a,b\rangle
, then the numerical semigroup
\langle a,b,h\rangle
is perfect
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
.

One of the main results of this paper is Theorem
3.4
, which determines the number of isolated gaps of
S
. Theorem
3.4
permit us to explicitly describe all the isolated gaps of these numerical semigroups.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we recall preliminary notions, introduce notation, and state some well known results. In Section 3, we determine the number of isolated gaps of any numerical semigroup of embedding dimension two. In Section 4, we describe all the isolated gaps of any numerical semigroup of embedding dimension two. We further give an exact formula for the smallest isolated gap of these numerical semigroups.

2.
Preliminaries and Notation

Let
X
be a nonempty set. We denote by
\#X
the cardinality of
X
. A
partition
of
X
is a collection of pairwise disjoint nonempty subsets, called
blocks
, whose union is
X
. A partition is
uniform
if all its blocks have the same cardinality. Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a function. We write
f(x)
to denote the image of an element
x\in X
under
f
. For a subset
A
of
X
and a subset
B
of
Y
, let
f(A)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{f(x)\colon x\in A\}
and
f^{-1}(B)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in X\colon f(x)\in B\}
. The absolute value of a real number
x
is denoted by
|x|
.

The set of all integers is denoted by
\Z
. For an integer
m\geq 2
, the greatest common divisor of the positive integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
is denoted by
\gcd(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})
. For integers
x
and
y
with
x<y
, let
[x,y]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{z\in\Z\colon x\leq z\leq y\}
. For an integer
k
and a set
A
of integers, let
kA\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{ka\colon a\in A\}
. We denote by
\min A
(resp.
\max A
) the smallest (resp. greatest) element of a finite nonempty set
A
of integers. For integers
a
,
b
,
m
with
m\geq 1
, we say that
a
is congruent to
b
modulo
m
, written
a\equiv b\pmod{m}
, if
m
divides
a-b
. A
complete residue system modulo
m
is a set of
m
integers such that every integer is congruent modulo
m
to exactly one element of the set. Let
a
and
b
be relatively prime positive integers. A solution
(u,v)
of the Diophantine equation
ax+by=1
is a
definitely least solution
(
d.l.s.
) if both
|u|
and
|v|
attain their least possible values (cf.
[
1
, p. 646]
). It is known that there is a unique d.l.s. of the Diophantine equation
ax+by=1

[
1
, Theorem 3]
.

Let
S
be a numerical semigroup. We denote by
I(S)
the set of all isolated gaps of
S
. We use
F(S)
to denote the Frobenius number of
S
. Let
N(S)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{s\in S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}s<F(S)\}
. It is well-known that every numerical semigroup admits a unique minimal (under inclusion) finite generating set (cf.
[
6
, Theorem 2.7]
). The
embedding dimension
of
S
is the cardinality of the minimal generating set of
S
. We say that
S
is
symmetric
if
F(S)-x\in S
for all
x\in\Z\setminus S
. It is well-known that every numerical semigroup of embedding dimension two is symmetric (cf.
[
6
, Corollary 4.7]
). For an element
n\in S\setminus\{0\}
, the
Apéry set
of
n
in
S
is
\text{Ap}(S,n)=\{s\in S\colon s-n\notin S\}
.

We refer the reader to
[
6
]
for any undefined notions and results on numerical semigroups. In the rest of the paper, we will henceforth assume that
a
and
b
are relatively prime positive integers such that
1<a<b
, and
(u,v)
is the d.l.s. of the equation
ax+by=1
.

We end this section by listing two known lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
1
, Lemma]

A solution
(x_{0},y_{0})
of the Diophantine equation
ax+by=1
is the d.l.s. if and only if
|x_{0}|\leq\frac{b}{2}
and
|y_{0}|\leq\frac{a}{2}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
5
, Lemma 1]

Let
x\in\Z
. Then
x\notin\langle a,b\rangle
if and only if
x=ab-\ell a-kb
for some
\ell,k\in\N\setminus\{0\}
.

3.
Combinatorial results

Our first aim in this section is to prove Theorem
3.4
, which determines an exact formula for the number of isolated gaps of a numerical semigroup of embedding dimension two.

Given a numerical semigroup
S
, we define

T(S)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{s\in N(S)\colon s-1,s+1\notin S\}.

The following proposition shows that the sets
I(S)
and
T(S)
have the same cardinality if the numerical semigroup
S
is symmetric.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
S
is a symmetric numerical semigroup, then
\#I(S)=\#T(S)
.

Proof.

Define a function
\alpha\colon T(S)\to I(S)
by
\alpha(x)=F(S)-x
. First, we show that
\alpha(x)\in I(S)
whenever
x\in T(S)
. Let
x\in T(S)
. Then
\alpha(x)=F(S)-x\in G(S)
. Now since
x-1,x+1\notin S
and
S
is symmetric, we obtain
F(S)-(x-1),F(S)-(x+1)\in S
. Thus
F(S)-x\in I(S)
, and so
\alpha(x)\in I(S)
.

It is clear from the definition that
\alpha
is injective. Finally, we show that
\alpha
is surjective. Let
y\in I(S)
. Since
S
is symmetric, it follows that
F(S)-y\in N(S)
. Now since
y-1,y+1\in S
, we obtain
F(S)-(y-1),F(S)-(y+1)\notin S
. Thus
F(S)-y\in T(S)
. Moreover, observe that
\alpha\left(F(S)-y\right)=y
, as desired.
∎

Notation 3.2
.

Throughout the rest of the paper, we will use the symbol
\alpha
to denote the bijection defined as in the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

The following lemma determines the cardinality of the finite set
T(\langle a,b\rangle)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
S=\langle a,b\rangle
, then
\#T(S)=|uv|
.

Proof.

Let
s\in T(S)
. Then
s=a\lambda_{1}+b\lambda_{2}
for some
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
1=au+bv
, we deduce that
s-1=a(\lambda_{1}-u)+b(\lambda_{2}-v)
and
s+1=a(\lambda_{1}+u)+b(\lambda_{2}+v)
. Note that exactly one of the integers
u
and
v
is negative.

Case 1: Suppose
u<0
. Write
u^{\prime}=-u
. Then
s-1=a(\lambda_{1}+u^{\prime})+b(\lambda_{2}-v)
and
s+1=a(\lambda_{1}-u^{\prime})+b(\lambda_{2}+v)
. Since
s-1,s+1\notin S
, it follows that
\lambda_{2}<v
and
\lambda_{1}<u^{\prime}
.

We now show that if
\lambda_{1}<u^{\prime}
and
\lambda_{2}<v
, then
s-1,s+1\notin S
. Since
u^{\prime}\leq\frac{b}{2}
and
v\leq\frac{a}{2}
by Lemma
2.1
, we obtain
\lambda_{1}+u^{\prime}<2u^{\prime}\leq b
and
\lambda_{2}+v<2v\leq a
. Put
\ell=a-(\lambda_{2}+v)
,
k=b-(\lambda_{1}+u^{\prime})
,
r=u^{\prime}-\lambda_{1}
, and
t=v-\lambda_{2}
. Then

s-1=a(b-k)+b(-t)=ab-ak-bt
and
s+1=a(-r)+b(a-\ell)=ab-ar-b\ell
.
Since
\ell,k,r,t\in\N\setminus\{0\}
, we obtain
s-1,s+1\notin S
by Lemma
2.2
.

Case 2: Suppose
v<0
. Write
v^{\prime}=-v
. Then
s-1=a(\lambda_{1}-u)+b(\lambda_{2}+v^{\prime})
and
s+1=a(\lambda_{1}+u)+b(\lambda_{2}-v^{\prime})
. Since
s-1,s+1\notin S
, it follows that
\lambda_{1}<u
and
\lambda_{2}<v^{\prime}
. By using the similar argument as in Case 1, we can readily show that if
\lambda_{1}<u
and
\lambda_{2}<v^{\prime}
, then
s-1,s+1\notin S
.

In each case, we have
s-1,s+1\notin S
whenever
\lambda_{1}<|u|
and
\lambda_{2}<|v|
. Hence we conclude that
\#T(S)=|u||v|=|uv|
.
∎

Recall that every numerical semigroup of embedding dimension two is symmetric. By combining Proposition
3.1
and Lemma
3.3
, we thus obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

If
S=\langle a,b\rangle
, then
\#I(S)=|uv|
.

We illustrate Theorem
3.4
with the next two examples.

Example 3.5
.

Consider the numerical semigroup
S=\langle 9,13\rangle
. Observe that
(3,-2)
is a solution of the equation
9x+13y=1
. Moreover, the solution
(3,-2)
is the d.l.s. by Lemma
2.1
. Therefore
u=3
and
v=-2
. By using Theorem
3.4
, we thus obtain
\#I(S)=|uv|=6
. Indeed, a modest computation confirms that
I(S)=\{64,73,77,82,86,95\}
.

Example 3.6
.

Consider the numerical semigroup
S=\langle 8,13\rangle
. Observe that
(5,-3)
is a solution of the equation
9x+13y=1
. Moreover, the solution
(5,-3)
is the d.l.s. by Lemma
2.1
. Therefore
u=5
and
v=-3
. By using Theorem
3.4
, we thus obtain
\#I(S)=|uv|=15
. Indeed, a modest computation confirms that
I(S)=\{25,33,38,41,46,49,51,54,57,59,62,67,70,75,83\}
.

Let
S
be a non-perfect numerical semigroup, and let
m\in S\setminus\{0\}
. For each
i\in[1,m-1]
, Smith
[
7
, p. 124]
defined the set
I_{i,m}(S)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{s\in I(S)\colon s\equiv i\pmod{m}\}
. It is easy to verify that the collection
\left\{I_{i,m}(S)\colon I_{i,m}(S)\neq\varnothing,\;i\in[1,m-1]\right\}
is a partition of
I(S)
. Smith
[
7
, Theorem 2.7]
proved that the partition

\left\{I_{i,a}(\langle a,b\rangle)\colon I_{i,a}(\langle a,b\rangle)\neq\varnothing,\;i\in[1,a-1]\right\}
of
I(\langle a,b\rangle)
is uniform.

To determine the cardinality of
\left\{I_{i,a}(\langle a,b\rangle)\colon I_{i,a}(\langle a,b\rangle)\neq\varnothing,\;i\in[1,a-1]\right\}
, we require the next two lemmas.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
S
be a symmetric numerical semigroup such that
I(S)\neq\varnothing
, and let
m\in S\setminus\{0\}
. Then:

(i)

\left\{\alpha^{-1}(I_{i,m}(S))\colon I_{i}(S)\neq\varnothing,\;i\in[1,m-1]\right\}
is a partition of
T(S)
,

(ii)

any two elements of
\alpha^{-1}(I_{i,m}(S))
are congruent modulo
m
.

Proof.

Note that
\alpha\colon T(S)\to I(S)
is a bijection.

(i)

This follows from the facts that the inverse image of a finite union (resp. intersection) equals the union (resp. intersection) of the inverse images.

(ii)

Let
x,y\in\alpha^{-1}(I_{i,m}(S))
. Then
\alpha(x),\alpha(y)\in I_{i,m}(S)
, and so
\alpha(x)\equiv\alpha(y)\pmod{m}
. Then, by the definition of
\alpha
we obtain
F(S)-x\equiv F(S)-y\pmod{m}
. This implies that
x\equiv y\pmod{m}
, as desired.

∎

Lemma 3.8
.

If
S=\langle a,b\rangle
, then
\#\left\{\alpha^{-1}(I_{i,a}(S))\colon I_{i,a}(S)\neq\varnothing,\;i\in[1,a-1]\right\}=|v|
.

Proof.

Note that
\text{Ap}(S,a)=\{0,b,\cdots,(a-1)b\}
(cf.
[
6
, p. 10]
). Moreover, the Apéry set
\text{Ap}(S,a)
is a complete
residue system modulo
a
(cf.
[
4
, Theorem 2.6]
). Thus, by Lemma
3.7
it suffices to count elements in
T(S)
that has the form
bk
for some
k\in\{0,\ldots,a-1\}
.

Let
bk\in T(S)
. Since
1=au+bv
, we deduce that

bk+1=au+b(k+v)
and
bk-1=-au+b(k-v)
. From Lemma
2.1
, we know that
|u|<b
and
|v|<a
. Note that exactly one of
u
and
v
is negative.

Case 1: Suppose
u<0
. Write
u^{\prime}=-u
and
\ell=b-u^{\prime}
. Then

bk-1=a(b-\ell)+b(k-v)=ab-a\ell-b(v-k)
. Since
bk-1\notin S
and
\ell\in\N\setminus\{0\}
, it follows that
k<v
by Lemma
2.2
.

We now show that if
k<v
, then
bk+1\notin S
. Put
t=a-(k+v)
. Observe that
k+v<2v\leq a
, and so
t\in\N\setminus\{0\}
. Then

bk+1=-au^{\prime}+b(a-t)=ab-au^{\prime}-at
. Since
u^{\prime},t\in\N\setminus\{0\}
, we obtain
bk+1\notin S
by Lemma
2.2
.

Case 2: Suppose
v<0
. Write
v^{\prime}=-v
and
\ell=b-u
. Then

bk+1=a(b-\ell)+b(k-v^{\prime})=ab-a\ell-b(v^{\prime}-k)
. Since
bk+1\notin S
and
\ell\in\N\setminus\{0\}
, we obtain
k<v^{\prime}
by Lemma
2.2
.

We now show that if
k<v^{\prime}
, then
bk-1\notin S
. Put
t=a-(k+v^{\prime})
. Observe that
k+v^{\prime}<2v^{\prime}\leq a
, and so
t\in\N\setminus\{0\}
. Then

bk-1=-au+b(a-t)=ab-au-at
. Since
u,t\in\N\setminus\{0\}
, we obtain
bk-1\notin S
by Lemma
2.2
.

In each case, we have
k<|v|
. Hence
\#\left\{\alpha^{-1}(I_{i,a}(S))\colon I_{i,a}(S)\neq\varnothing,\;i\in[1,a-1]\right\}=|v|
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

If
S=\langle a,b\rangle
, then
\#\left\{I_{i,a}(S)\colon I_{i,a}(S)\neq\varnothing,\;i\in[1,a-1]\right\}=|v|
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
3.7
(i) and Lemma
3.8
, since
\alpha\colon T(S)\to I(S)
is a bijection.
∎

We illustrate Proposition
3.9
with the following example.

Example 3.10
.

Consider the numerical semigroup
S=\langle 7,16\rangle
. Observe that
(7-3)
is a solution of the equation
7x+16y=1
. Moreover, the solution
(7,-3)
is the d.l.s. by Lemma
2.1
. Therefore
|v|=3
. By using Proposition
3.9
, we thus obtain
\#\left\{I_{i,7}(S)\colon I_{i,7}(S)\neq\varnothing,\;i\in[1,6]\right\}=3
. Indeed, a modest computation confirms that

I(S)=\{15,22,29,31,36,38,43,45,47,50,52,54,57,59,61,66,68,73,75,82,89\}

and

•

I_{1,7}(S)=\{15,22,29,36,43,50,57\}

•

I_{2,7}(S)=\varnothing

•

I_{3,7}(S)=\{31,38,45,52,59,66,73\}

•

I_{4,7}(S)=\varnothing

•

I_{5,7}(S)=\{47,54,61,68,75,82,89\}

•

I_{6,7}(S)=\varnothing
.

Smith proved that the partition
\left\{I_{i,a}(\langle a,b\rangle)\colon I_{i,a}(\langle a,b\rangle)\neq\varnothing,\;i\in[1,a-1]\right\}
of
I(\langle a,b\rangle)
is uniform
[
7
, Theorem 2.7]
. Combining this fact with Theorem
3.4
and Proposition
3.9
, we immediately obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 3.11
.

If
S=\langle a,b\rangle
, then the cardinality of each block of the partition
\left\{I_{i,a}(S)\colon I_{i,a}(S)\neq\varnothing,\;i\in[1,a-1]\right\}
of
I(S)
is
|u|
.

We illustrate Proposition
3.11
with the following example.

Example 3.12
.

Consider the numerical semigroup
S=\langle 7,16\rangle
. From Example
3.10
, we know that
|u|=7
. By using Proposition
3.11
, we thus obtain that the cardinality of each block of
\left\{I_{i,7}(S)\colon I_{i,7}(S)\neq\varnothing,\;i\in[1,6]\right\}
is
|u|=7
. Indeed, Example
3.10
says that the cardinality of each block of
\left\{I_{i,7}(S)\colon I_{i,7}(S)\neq\varnothing,\;i\in[1,6]\right\}
is
7
.

4.
Isolated Gaps

We assume throughout this section that
S=\langle a,b\rangle
, and let
h\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\min I(S)
.

Smith proved that every element of
I(S)
has the form
h+s
for some
s\in S

[
7
, Theorem 2.11]
. To identify the value of
h
, Smith showed that either
h=1
or
\left(h\pmod{a},h\pmod{b}\right)\in\left\{(1,-1),(-1,1)\right\}

[
7
, Proposition 3.3]
.

In this section, we explicitly describe all the isolated gaps of
S
, and find an exact formula for the smallest isolated gap
h
of
S
.

For each
i\in[1,a-1]
such that
I_{i,a}(S)\neq\varnothing
, the smallest element of
I_{i,a}(S)
, written
h_{i}
, is a
minimal isolated gap modulo
a
of
I(S)

[
7
, p. 124]
. Smith
[
7
]
gave the following theorem, which determines all the minimal isolated gaps modulo
a
of
I(S)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
7
, Theorem 2.11]

Let
n
be the smallest positive integer such that
h+nb\in a\N
. Then
h,h+b,\cdots,h+(n-1)b
are all the minimal isolated gaps modulo
a
of
I(S)
.

By combining Proposition
3.9
with Theorem
4.1
, we immediately obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

There are exactly
|v|
minimal isolated gaps modulo
a
of
I(S)
, namely,

h,h+b,\cdots,h+(|v|-1)b
.

To determine all elements of any block of
\left\{I_{i,a}(S)\colon I_{i,a}(S)\neq\varnothing,\;i\in[1,a-1]\right\}
, we require a list of lemmas.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
I_{i,a}(S)\neq\varnothing
for some
i\in[1,a-1]
. Then
x\in I_{i,a}(S)
if and only if
x=h_{i}+ka
for some
k\in\N
.

Proof.

Assume that
x\in I_{i,a}(S)
. Since
h_{i}\in I_{i,a}(S)
, we deduce that
x\equiv h_{i}\pmod{a}
. Note that
h_{i}=\min I_{i,a}(S)
, and hence
x=h_{i}+ka
for some
k\in\N
.

Conversely, assume that
x=h_{i}+ka
for some
k\in\N
. Clearly
ka\equiv 0\pmod{a}
. Since
h_{i}\in I_{i,a}(S)
, we find
h_{i}\equiv i\pmod{a}
. Thus
x=h_{i}+ka\equiv i\pmod{a}
, and hence
x\in I_{i,a}(S)
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

If
x\in I_{i,a}(S)
for some
i\in[1,a-1]
, then either
x+a\in S
or
x+a\in I_{i,a}(S)
.

Proof.

Assume that
x+a\notin S
. Since
x\in I_{i,a}(S)
, we obtain
x-1,x+1\in S
. Therefore
(x+a)-1,(x+a)+1\in S
. Hence
x+a\in I_{i,a}(S)
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
x\in I(S)
. If
n
is the smallest positive integer such that
x+na\in S
, then
x+na\in b\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Assume that
n
is the smallest positive integer such that
x+na\in S
. Since
x+na\in S
, there exist
\ell,k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x+na=\ell a+kb
. We first claim that
\ell<n
. Suppose to the contrary that
\ell\geq n
. Then
x=(\ell-n)a+kb
, and so
s\in S
. This leads to a contradiction, since
x\in I(S)
.

Now since
kb\in S
and
x+na=\ell a+kb
, we deduce that
x+(n-\ell)a\in S
. Since
n
is the smallest positive integer such that
x+na\in S
, it follows that
\ell=0
. Thus
x+na=kb
. Note that
bk\in b\mathbb{N}
, and hence
x+na\in b\mathbb{N}
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
I_{i,a}(S)\neq\varnothing
for some
i\in[1,a-1]
. If
n
is the smallest positive integer such that
h_{i}+na\in b\mathbb{N}
, then
I_{i,a}(S)=\{h_{i},h_{i}+a,\cdots,h_{i}+(n-1)a\}
.

Proof.

Assume that
n
is the smallest positive integer such that
h_{i}+na\in b\mathbb{N}
. Then, by Lemma
4.5
we deduce that
h_{i},h_{i}+a,\cdots,h_{i}+(n-1)a\notin S
. Therefore
\{h_{i},h_{i}+a,\cdots,h_{i}+(n-1)a\}\subseteq I_{i,a}(S)
by Lemma
4.4
.

For the reverse inclusion, let
x\in I_{i,a}(S)
. Then, by Lemma
4.3
we obtain
x=h_{i}+ka
for some
k\in\N
.
To prove that
x\in\{h_{i},h_{i}+a,\cdots,h_{i}+(n-1)a\}
, it suffices to show that
k<n
. Suppose to the contrary that
k\geq n
. Then we can write
x=h_{i}+na+(k-n)a
. Since
h_{i}+na\in b\N
by the hypothesis, it follows that
x\in S
. This leads to a contradiction, since
x\in I_{i,a}(S)
. Hence
k<n
, as desired.
∎

By combining Lemma
4.6
with Proposition
3.11
, we immediately obtain the following Proposition.

Proposition 4.7
.

If
I_{i,a}(S)\neq\varnothing
for some
i\in[1,a-1]
, then

I_{i,a}(S)=\{h_{i},h_{i}+a,\cdots,h_{i}+(|u|-1)a\}.

The next theorem determines all the isolated gaps of
S
.

Theorem 4.8
.

The set of isolated gaps of
S
is given as

\displaystyle I(S)

\displaystyle=\{h,h+a,\ldots,h+\left(|u|-1\right)a\}\cup\{h+b,h+b+a,\ldots,h+b+(|u|-1)a\}

\displaystyle\quad\cup\cdots\cup\{h+(|v|-1)b,h+(|v|-1)b+a,\ldots,h+(|v|-1)b+(|u|-1)a\}.

Proof.

This follows from Propositions
4.2
and
4.7
.
∎

Theorem
4.8
suggests that the identifying the value of the smallest isolated gap of
S
is crucial in calculating all the isolated gaps of
S
. The following proposition gives an exact formula for the smallest isolated gap of
S
.

Proposition 4.9
.

If
S=\langle a,b\rangle
, then
\min I(S)=F(S)-(|u|-1)a-(|v|-1)b
.

Proof.

Observe that
\max I(S)=h+(|u|-1)a+(|v|-1)b
. From
[
7
, p. 125]
, we know that
F(S)\in I(S)
, and so
\max I(S)=F(S)
. Thus
h+(|u|-1)a+(|v|-1)b=F(S)
, and hence
h=F(S)-(|u|-1)a-(|v|-1)b
.
∎

We illustrate Proposition
4.9
with the next two examples.

Example 4.10
.

Consider the numerical semigroup
S=\langle 9,13\rangle
. By
[
6
, Proposition 2.13]
, we obtain
F(S)=9\times 13-9-13=95
.
We know from Example
3.5
that
|u|=3
and
|v|=2
. By using Proposition
4.9
, we thus obtain
\min I(S)=95-2\times 9-1\times 13=64
.

Example 4.11
.

Consider the numerical semigroup
S=\langle 7,11\rangle
. By
[
6
, Proposition 2.13]
, we obtain
F(S)=7\times 11-7-11=59
. Observe that
(-3,2)
is a solution of the equation
7x+11y=1
. Moreover, the solution
(-3,2)
is the d.l.s. by Lemma
2.1
. Therefore
|u|=3
and
|v|=2
. By using Proposition
4.9
, we thus obtain
\min I(S)=59-2\times 7-1\times 11=34
. Indeed, a modest computation confirms that
I(S)=\{34,41,45,48,52,59\}
.

To facilitate further computations on
I(S)
, we construct the
|v|\times|u|
matrix
L(S)
whose
(i,j)
-entry is given by
L(S)(i,j)=h+(i-1)b+(j-1)a
. That is,

L(S)=\begin{bmatrix}h&h+a&\cdots&h+\left(|u|-1\right)a\\
h+b&h+b+a&\cdots&h+b+(|u|-1)a\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
h+(|v|-1)b&h+(|v|-1)b+a&\cdots&h+(|v|-1)b+(|u|-1)a\end{bmatrix}.

For example, consider the numerical semigroup
S=\langle 7,16\rangle
. From Example
3.12
, we know that
h=15
,
|u|=7
, and
|v|=3
. Thus

L(S)=\begin{bmatrix}15&22&29&36&43&50&57\\
31&38&45&52&59&66&73\\
47&54&61&68&75&82&89\end{bmatrix}.

By using Proposition
4.9
, we can alternatively calculate any
(i,j)
-entry of the matrix
L(S)
as follows.

Corollary 4.12
.

The
(i,j)
-entry of the
|v|\times|u|
matrix
L(S)
is given by the rule:

L(S)(i,j)=F(S)-(|u|-j)a-(|v|-i)b.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.9
, we know that
h=F(S)-(|u|-1)a-(|v|-1)b
. Therefore

\displaystyle L(S)(i,j)

\displaystyle=h+(i-1)b+(j-1)a

\displaystyle=F(S)-(|u|-1)a-(|v|-1)b+(i-1)b+(j-1)a

\displaystyle=F(S)-(|u|-j)a-(|v|-i)b.

∎

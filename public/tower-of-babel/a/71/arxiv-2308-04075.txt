Boundary-preserving Lamperti-splitting schemes for some stochastic differential equations
Johan Ulander

Abstract
. We propose and analyse boundary-preserving schemes for the strong approximations of some scalar SDEs with non-globally Lipschitz drift and diffusion coefficients whose state-space is bounded. The schemes consists of a Lamperti transform followed by a Lie–Trotter splitting. We prove L p ​ ( Ω ) L^{p}(\Omega) -convergence of order 1 1 , for every p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 , of the schemes and exploit the Lamperti transform to confine the numerical approximations to the state-space of the considered SDE. We provide numerical experiments that confirm the theoretical results and compare the proposed Lamperti-splitting schemes to other numerical schemes for SDEs.

2.1.
Description of the SDE

We first discuss some preliminaries and introduce the main assumptions needed for the definition and analysis of the proposed Lamperti-splitting (LS) schemes. The general idea of the Lamperti transform is to transform an SDE into another SDE with state-independent diffusion coefficient
[
20
,
24
]
. More precisely, provided that everything is well-defined, the Lamperti transform of the SDE in equation (
1
) with
x\in\mathring{D}
is given by

\Phi(x)=\int_{w_{0}}^{x}\frac{1}{g(w)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w

(2)

where
\mathring{D}=(l,r)
for some
l,r\in\mathbb{R}
and for some
w_{0}\in\mathring{D}
. We also let
\bar{D}=[l,r]
. By Itô’s formula, the process
Y(t)=\Phi(X(t))
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Y(t)=\tilde{H}(Y(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(t),\\
&Y(0)=\Phi(x_{0}),\end{aligned}\right.

(3)

where we define

\tilde{H}(x)=\frac{f(\Phi^{-1}(x))}{g(\Phi^{-1}(x))}-\frac{1}{2}g^{\prime}(\Phi^{-1}(x)).

We require
x_{0},w_{0}\in\mathring{D}
because, by Assumption
2
below,
\Phi
has singularities on
\partial D
. Also observe that
w_{0}=x_{0}
is a valid choice for the lower integration limit in the Lamperti transform. Let us also denote by

H(x)=\tilde{H}(x)-\mu

(4)

for some
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. The introduction of
H
allows us to transfer the constant
\mu
between the ODE part and the SDE part of the splitting scheme (see Section
3.1
). We now list the assumptions that we need to guarantee that the above is well-defined.

Assumption 1
.

The drift coefficient
f\in C^{2}\left(\bar{D}\right)
.

Assumption 2
.

The diffusion coefficient
g\in C^{3}\left(\bar{D}\right)
and is strictly positive on
\mathring{D}=(l,r)
, and, for any
w_{0}\in\mathring{D}
, the following non-integrability conditions are satisfied

\int_{w_{0}}^{l}\frac{1}{g(w)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w=-\infty,\ \int_{w_{0}}^{r}\frac{1}{g(w)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w=\infty.

(5)

Assumption 3
.

The drift coefficient
f
decays at least as fast as the diffusion coefficient
g
near the boundary points
\partial D
; that is, the following limits exist and are finite

\left|\lim_{x\searrow l}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\right|+\left|\lim_{x\nearrow r}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\right|<\infty.

(6)

The above assumptions implies regularity properties of the inverse of the Lamperti transform
\Phi^{-1}
and of the modified drift
H
in equation (
4
) that we summarise in the following two propositions.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose Assumption
2
is satisfied. Then
\Phi^{-1}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathring{D}
is bounded, bijective, continuously differentiable and has bounded derivative. In particular,
\Phi^{-1}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathring{D}
is globally Lipschitz continuous and we denote the Lipschitz constant of
\Phi^{-1}
by
L_{\Phi^{-1}}
.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

The assumption that
g(x)>0
for every
x\in\mathring{D}
and the non-integrability conditions in equation (
5
) implies that
\Phi^{-1}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathring{D}
is bijective and differentiable. The latter follows from the inverse function theorem since
\Phi^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{g\left(\Phi^{-1}(x)\right)}>0
for every
x\in\mathring{D}
. For boundedness of
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}\Phi^{-1}(x)
, we can compute, using the inverse function theorem and the chain rule,

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}\Phi^{-1}(x)=\frac{1}{\Phi^{\prime}\left(\Phi^{-1}(x)\right)}=g\left(\Phi^{-1}(x)\right),

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
g
is continuous on
\bar{D}
, by Assumption
2
, and
\Phi^{-1}(x)\in\mathring{D}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the above expressions implies that
\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}\Phi^{-1}(x)
is continuous and uniformly bounded. Since any differentiable function that has a uniformly bounded derivative is globally Lipschitz continuous, we conclude that
\Phi^{-1}
is globally Lipschitz continuous.
∎

Observe that we could continue the argument in the proof of Proposition
1
to conclude that
\Phi^{-1}\in C^{4}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
with bounded derivatives up to order
4
.

Proposition 2
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
,
2
and
3
are satisfied. Then
H\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
and

L_{H}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|H(x)+\mu|+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|H^{\prime}(x)|+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|H^{\prime\prime}(x)|<\infty

and is independent of
\mu
. In particular,
H,H^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are globally Lipschitz continuous and
L_{H}
is an upper bound for the Lipschitz constants of
H
and
H^{\prime}
.

Observe that we include

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|H(x)+\mu|=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\tilde{H}(x)|

in the definition of

in order for it to be independent of

(but dependent on

). Also note that Proposition

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|H(x)|\leq L_{H}+|\mu|.

Proof of Proposition
2
.

Recall that

H(x)=\frac{f(\Phi^{-1}(x))}{g(\Phi^{-1}(x))}-\frac{1}{2}g^{\prime}(\Phi^{-1}(x))-\mu.

(7)

By the quotient rule, the chain rule and by the inverse function theorem, we can compute

for every

2.2.
Boundary classification

We dedicate this section to a short discussion about whether or not the process
X
can hit the boundary points
\partial D
, where the Lamperti transform
\Phi
is not well-defined. The boundary points are unattainable by
X
if and only if the stopping time

\tau=\inf\{t\in(0,T]:\ X(t)\in\partial D\}

is infinite almost surely. In the considered setting, we have the following characterisation of when
\tau=\infty
almost surely:

{\mathbb{P}}(\tau=\infty)=1\Longleftrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(Y\text{ blows up})=0.

By Proposition
2
, the drift coefficient
H
of the transformed process
Y
is Lipschitz continuous. Thus,
Y
does not blow up in finite time with probability
1
, and we conclude that
\tau=\infty
almost surely. Alternatively, Feller’s boundary classification provides a general theory on boundary behaviour of solutions to Itô SDEs and characterises this in terms of the drift and diffusion coefficients
f
and
g
. For a detailed exposition of Feller’s boundary classification we refer the interested reader to
[
15
]
.

Y^{LS}_{m}
for
m=0,\ldots,M
. We let
Y^{LS}_{0}=\Phi(x_{0})
and we define
Y^{LS}_{m}
, for
m=1,\ldots,M
, recursively as follows: Suppose
Y^{LS}_{m}
at time
t_{m}=m\Delta t
is given. First we let
y_{m}
solve the nonlinear ODE

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{m}(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}=H(y_{m}(t)),\\
&y_{m}(t_{m})=Y^{LS}_{m},\end{aligned}\right.

(13)

on the interval
[t_{m},t_{m+1}]
with initial value
Y^{LS}_{m}
. Second we let
Z_{m}
solve the SDE for Brownian motion with drift
\mu\in\mathbb{R}

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Z_{m}(t)=\mu\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(t),\\
&Z_{m}(t_{m})=y_{m}(t_{m+1}),\end{aligned}\right.

(14)

on the interval
[t_{m},t_{m+1}]
with initial value
y_{m}(t_{m+1})
. We define
Y^{LS}_{m+1}
at the next time grid point
t_{m+1}=(m+1)\Delta t
as

Y^{LS}_{m+1}=Z_{m}(t_{m+1})\equiv y_{m}(t_{m+1})+\mu\Delta t+B(t_{m+1})-B(t_{m})

(15)

and we define the approximation
X^{LS}_{m+1}=\Phi^{-1}(Y^{LS}_{m+1})
of the solution of equation (
11
) at the time grid point
t_{m+1}
.

Remark 3
.

The same scheme could have been constructed by considering the opposite order of the splitting; that is, first solve the SDE in equation (
14
) and then solve the ODE in equation (
13
). Then we would directly obtain
L^{2}(\Omega)
-convergence of order
1
by applying Theorem
3.1
in
[
25
]
. However, Theorem
3.1
in
[
25
]
does not yield the stronger results of
L^{p}(\Omega)
-convergence rate, for every
p\geq 1
, with the supremum inside the expected value: see Theorem
7
below.

For the proof of the convergence result in this section (see Theorem
7
), the following integral expression for
Y^{LS}_{m+1}
, for
m=0,\ldots,M-1
, will be used

Y^{LS}_{m+1}=Y^{LS}_{m}+\int_{t_{m}}^{t_{m+1}}H(y_{m}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\mu\Delta t+B(t_{m+1})-B(t_{m}).

(16)

The definition of
X^{LS}
as
\Phi^{-1}(Y^{LS})
combined with Assumptions
1
,
2
and
3
guarantee that the scheme is boundary-preserving. This is the content of the next proposition.

Proposition 4
.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
T>0
,
\Delta t=T/M
and let
x_{0}\in\mathring{D}
.
Suppose that Assumptions
1
,
2
and
3
are satisfied.
Let
Y^{LS}
be given by the splitting scheme in equation (
15
) and let
X^{LS}=\Phi^{-1}(Y^{LS})
be the numerical approximation of the original SDE in (
11
). Then

X^{LS}_{m}\in\mathring{D},

almost surely, for every
m\in\{0,\ldots,M\}
.

Proof of Proposition
4
.

Recall that
\Phi^{-1}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathring{D}
is bijective and continuous, by Proposition
1
. Thus, if
Y^{LS}_{m}
, for
m=0,\ldots,M
, does not blow up, with probability
1
, then the statement holds. Suppose that
Y^{LS}_{m}
is finite with probability
1
for some
m=0,\ldots M-1
. Then
y_{m}(t_{m+1})
, in equation (
13
), is finite with probability
1
as it is the solution of the globally well-defined ODE in (
13
) at time
t_{m+1}
. Since
Z_{m}(t)
in equation (
14
), for
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1}]
, is a Brownian motion with drift
\mu
and unit diffusion starting at
y_{m+1}(t_{m+1})
, we have that
Y^{LS}_{m+1}=Z_{m}(t_{m+1})
is finite with probability

. This gives the desired result.
∎

3.2.
Convergence result

In the following we prove that the proposed semi-analytic LS scheme has
L^{p}(\Omega)
-convergence of order
1
, for every
p\geq 1
, for the considered SDE in equation (
11
).

We would like to make a few remarks and comparisons to other related papers before we state and prove the convergence result.

Remark 5
.

The positive moments of the exact solution and of the LS approximation are immediately bounded as both are confined to the bounded domain
\mathring{D}
.

Remark 6
.

In contrast to the papers
[
6
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
, we do not need to bound the inverse moments of the exact solution and of the LS approximation to obtain our convergence result.

Theorem 7
.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
T>0
,
\Delta t=T/M
and let
x_{0}\in\mathring{D}
.
Suppose Assumptions
1
,
2
and
3
are satisfied. Let
X^{LS}=\Phi^{-1}(Y^{LS})
, where
Y^{LS}
is defined by the splitting scheme in equation (
15
), and let
X
be the exact solution of the considered SDE in equation (
11
). Then, for every
p\geq 1
, it holds

\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M}|X^{LS}_{m}-X(t_{m})|^{p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C(p,\Phi^{-1},H,T,\mu)\Delta t.

The dependence of the constant
C(p,\Phi^{-1},H,T,\mu)
in Theorem
7
on the parameters can be found in the proof.

Proof of Theorem
7
.

As
X^{LS}_{0}=X(0)
, it suffices to consider the case
m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
. Since
\Phi^{-1}
is globally Lipschitz continuous by Proposition
1
, the proof reduces to proving the corresponding
L^{p}(\Omega)
-estimate for
|Y^{LS}_{m}-Y(t_{m})|
:

|X^{LS}_{m}-X(t_{m})|=|\Phi^{-1}(Y^{LS}_{m})-\Phi^{-1}(Y(t_{m}))|\leq L_{\Phi^{-1}}|Y^{LS}_{m}-Y(t_{m})|.

Recall the following integral expression for
Y^{LS}_{m}
stated in equation (
16
) in Section
3.1

\begin{split}Y^{LS}_{m}&=Y^{LS}_{m-1}+\int_{t_{m-1}}^{t_{m}}H(y_{m-1}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\mu\Delta t+B(t_{m})-B(t_{m-1})\\
&=Y^{LS}_{m-1}+\int_{t_{m-1}}^{t_{m}}\left(H(y_{m-1}(s))+\mu\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+B(t_{m})-B(t_{m-1}).\end{split}

By recursively applying the above formula, we obtain that

Y^{LS}_{m}=Y(0)+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left(H(y_{k}(s))+\mu\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+B(t_{m})

(17)

and we re-write the integral representation of
Y
on a similar form

\begin{split}Y(t_{m})&=Y(0)+\int^{t_{m}}_{0}\left(H(Y(s))+\mu)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+B(t_{m})\\
&=Y(0)+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left(H(Y(s))+\mu\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+B(t_{m})\end{split}

at
t=t_{m}
. Therefore

|Y^{LS}_{m}-Y(t_{m})|\leq\left|\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(y_{k}(s)\right)-H\left(Y(s)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|,

(18)

We first split the integral as follows

\begin{split}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(y_{k}(s)\right)-H\left(Y(s)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s&=\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(y_{k}(s)\right)-H\left(Y^{LS}_{k}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(Y^{LS}_{k}\right)-H\left(Y(t_{k})\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(Y(t_{k})\right)-H\left(Y(s)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

(19)

and consider each term separately. Observe that the second term on the right hand side of equation (
19
) is the term that we need for Grönwall’s lemma after the estimate and simplification

\begin{split}\left|\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(Y^{LS}_{k}\right)-H\left(Y(t_{k})\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|&\leq L_{H}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|Y^{LS}_{k}-Y(t_{k})|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&=L_{H}\Delta t|Y^{LS}_{k}-Y(t_{k})|,\end{split}

where we used that
H
is globally Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant bounded by
L_{H}
by Proposition
2
. Recall now that
y_{k}(s)=Y^{LS}_{k}+\int_{t_{k}}^{s}H(y_{k}(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r
is the exact solution to the ODE in equation (
13
) starting at
t_{k}
with initial value
Y^{LS}_{k}
. Therefore, the first term on the right hand side in equation (
19
) can be estimated by

\begin{split}\left|\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(y_{k}(s)\right)-H\left(Y^{LS}_{k}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|&\leq L_{H}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|y_{k}(s)-Y^{LS}_{k}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq L_{H}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}|H(y_{k}(r))|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq L_{H}\left(L_{H}+|\mu|\right)\Delta t^{2},\end{split}

since
H
is globally Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant bounded by
L_{H}
and uniformly bounded by
L_{H}+|\mu|
by Proposition
2
. It remains to consider the third term on the right hand side in equation (
19
)

\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(Y(t_{k})\right)-H\left(Y(s)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

By Proposition
2
,
H\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
and we can thus apply Itô’s formula

\begin{split}H(Y(s))=H(Y(t_{k}))+\int_{t_{k}}^{s}H(Y(r))H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}H^{\prime\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\\
&+\int_{t_{k}}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\end{split}

to see that

\begin{split}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(Y(t_{k})\right)-H\left(Y(s)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s&=\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}H(Y(r))H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{k+1}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}H^{\prime\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(20)

To obtain convergence order
1
, we have to sum over
k=0,\ldots,m-1
before estimating the separate terms in equation (
20
)

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(Y(t_{k})\right)-H\left(Y(s)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s&=\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H(Y(r))H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&+\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

(21)

where we let
\ell(s)=t_{k}
whenever
s\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})
. The first two terms on the right hand side of equation (
21
) can estimated by

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H(Y(r))H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|&\leq\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}|H(Y(r))H^{\prime}(Y(r))|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\left(L_{H}+|\mu|\right)L_{H}t_{m}\Delta t\end{split}

and

\left|\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}|H^{\prime\prime}(Y(r))|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{1}{2}L_{H}t_{m}\Delta t,

where we used that
H
and
H^{\prime},H^{\prime\prime}
are uniformly bounded by
L_{H}+|\mu|
and
L_{H}
, respectively, by Proposition
2
. The third term on the right hand side of equation (
21
) is a random variable with finite
p
th moments for every
p\geq 1
, see Appendix
A
for a proof. Inserting everything back into equation (
18
) and estimating
m\Delta t=t_{m}\leq T
gives us

\begin{split}|Y^{LS}_{m}-Y(t_{m})|&\leq\left(L_{H}+|\mu|\right)L_{H}T\Delta t+\left(L_{H}+|\mu|\right)L_{H}T\Delta t+\frac{1}{2}L_{H}T\Delta t\\
&+\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|+L_{H}\Delta t\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}|Y^{LS}_{k}-Y(t_{k})|,\end{split}

where we have tried to group the terms to make it clear where the terms come from. An application of a discrete Grönwall’s lemma now yields

\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M}|Y^{LS}_{m}-Y(t_{m})|\leq\Delta t\eta_{\Delta t}e^{L_{H}T}

where
\eta_{\Delta t}
is the random variable

\eta_{\Delta t}=2\left(L_{H}+|\mu|\right)L_{H}T+\frac{1}{2}L_{H}T+\left|\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|.

(22)

Observe that the random variable
\eta_{\Delta t}
is not finite almost sure uniformly in
\Delta t
. However,
\Delta t\eta_{\Delta t}
is finite almost surely uniformly in
\Delta t
. To finish the proof we need to make sure that
\eta_{\Delta t}
has finite
p
th moments for every
p\geq 1
, since then

\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M}|Y^{LS}_{m}-Y(t_{m})|^{p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\Delta t\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\eta_{\Delta t}^{p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}e^{L_{H}T}=C(p,H,T,\mu)\Delta t.

The only random term in the definition of
\eta_{\Delta t}
in equation (
22
) is the third. An application of stochastic Fubini’s theorem and the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) inequality gives us that the third term indeed has finite
p
th moments

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|^{p}\right]\leq C(p)L_{H}T^{p/2},

where the constant
C(p)
here is the constant in the BDG inequality. We provide the proof of the above
p
th moment estimates in Appendix
A
. This concludes the proof.
∎

By applying Lemma
2.1
in
[
19
]
, we obtain, as a corollary to Theorem
7
, almost sure pathwise convergence with rate
1-\epsilon
for every
\epsilon>0
.

Corollary 8
.

Under the same assumptions and notation as in Theorem
7
, there exists for every
\epsilon>0
a random variable
\zeta_{\epsilon}
, with
{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\zeta_{\epsilon}|^{p}\right]<\infty
for every
p\geq 1
, such that

\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M}|X^{LS}_{m}-X(t_{m})|\leq\zeta_{\epsilon}\Delta t^{1-\epsilon}

almost surely.

4.
A boundary-preserving integrator

Here we extend the results in the previous section to the case where we do not assume that we can integrate the nonlinear ODE in (
13
) exactly. More precisely, instead of assuming that there exists an explicit solution to the nonlinear ODE in (
10
) we assume that we can integrate it using some deterministic integrator with local error of order
\Delta t^{2}
.

Assumption 4
.

For any
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, we can find an approximation
\hat{y}
to the exact solution
y
of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y(t)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}=H(y(t)),\\
&y(0)=y_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(23)

with local error of order
\Delta t^{2}
on the interval
[0,\Delta t]
; that is,

\sup_{t\in[0,\Delta t]}|\hat{y}(t)-y(t)|\leq K\sqrt{1+|y_{0}|^{2}}\Delta t^{2}

for some parameter
K>0
.

Observe that the classical Euler scheme and the exact solution
y
both satisfies Assumption
4
. The latter means that the setting in Section
3
also fits into the setting of the current section. At this point, we also mention that we in this section for the convergence result in Theorem
11
will impose a time-step restriction; that is, we will assume that there exists
\gamma>0
such that
KT\Delta t\leq\gamma
. Observe that this is in contrast to Section
3
where no time-step restriction was imposed. The main reason for the time-step restriction in this section is to obtain estimates where the only dependence on
\Delta t
is in the convergence rate. We only use the time-step restriction in the concluding lines of the proofs.

4.1.
Description of the integrator

Let
\hat{Y}^{LS}_{0}=\Phi(x_{0})
and suppose that the approximation
\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}
at time point
t_{m}=m\Delta t
is given. We let
\hat{y}_{m}(t)

to equation (

) starting at

\hat{Y}^{LT}_{m}

(24)

where

We define

(26)

where

, for

) starting at
t_{k}
with initial value
\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}

\tilde{y}_{m}(t_{m+1})

and then insert that

to obtain

\begin{split}\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m+1}&=\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}+\hat{y}_{m}(t_{m+1})-\tilde{y}_{m}(t_{m+1})+\int_{t_{m}}^{t_{m+1}}\left(H(\tilde{y}_{m}(s))+\mu\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\

4.2.
Convergence result

We start with proving that
\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M}|\hat{Y}^{LS}|
is almost surely bounded. This is the content of the following proposition and is proved by a standard Grönwall argument. Observe that moment bounds of
\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}|
would have been enough for the proof of the

-convergence in Theorem

because of the assumption of an exact solution to the ODEs in equation (

Proposition 10
.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
T>0
,
\Delta t=T/M
and let
x_{0}\in\mathring{D}
. Suppose Assumptions
1
,
2
,
3
and
4
are satisfied. Let
\hat{Y}^{LS}
be given by the splitting scheme in equation (
24
) with initial value
\hat{Y}^{LS}_{0}=Y(0)=\Phi(x_{0})
. Then it holds

\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}|\leq\left(KT\Delta t+L_{H}T+|Y(0)|+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|B(t)|\right)e^{KT\Delta t}

almost surely.

We remark the time-step restriction
KT\Delta t\leq\gamma
, for some constant
\gamma>0
, in the convergence result in Theorem
11
below comes from Propostion
10
. Observe that Proposition
10
provides a bound on
\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}|
that is independent of
\mu
.

Proof of Proposition
10
.

We first apply the triangle inequality to the representation formula for
\hat{Y}^{LS}
in equation (
26
) to estimate

\begin{split}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}|&\leq|Y(0)|+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}|\hat{y}_{k}(t_{k+1})-\tilde{y}_{k}(t_{k+1})|\\
&+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|H(\tilde{y}_{k}(s))+\mu|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+|B(t_{m})|.\end{split}

Next, since we have the estimate

|\hat{y}_{k}(t_{k+1})-\tilde{y}_{k}(t_{k+1})|\leq K\sqrt{1+|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}|^{2}}\Delta t^{2}\leq K\left(1+|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}|\right)\Delta t^{2}

by Assumption
4
and since
|H(x)+\mu|
is uniformly bounded by
L_{H}
by Proposition
2
, we can further estimate

\begin{split}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}|&\leq|Y(0)|+K\Delta t^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\left(1+|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}|\right)+L_{H}t_{m}+|B(t_{m})|\\
&\leq|Y(0)|+\left(K\Delta t+L_{H}\right)T+K\Delta t^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}|+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|B(t)|\end{split}

where we used that
m\Delta t=t_{m}\leq T
. We then apply Grönwall’s lemma to conclude that

|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}|\leq\left(KT\Delta t+L_{H}T+|Y(0)|+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|B(t)|\right)e^{KT\Delta t},

(27)

almost surely. Applying
\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M}
to equation (
27
) gives the desired result.
∎

We now prove the main convergence theorem in this section. The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem
7
, but with the different representation formula in equation (
26
). We provide both proofs for completeness.

Theorem 11
.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
T>0
,
\Delta t=T/M
and let
x_{0}\in\mathring{D}
.
Suppose Assumptions
1
,
2
,
3
and
4
are satisfied and that there exists some constant
\gamma>0
such that
KT\Delta t\leq\gamma
. Let
\hat{X}^{LS}=\Phi^{-1}(Y^{LS})
, where
\hat{Y}^{LS}
is defined by the splitting scheme in equation (
24
), and let
X
be the exact solution of the considered SDE in equation (
11
). Then, for every
p\geq 1
, it holds

\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M}|\hat{X}^{LS}_{m}-X(t_{m})|^{p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C(p,\Phi^{-1},H,K,T,\mu,\gamma)\Delta t.

The dependence of the constant
C(p,\Phi^{-1},H,K,T,\mu,\gamma)
on the parameters can be found in the proof of Theorem
11
.

Proof of Theorem
11
.

Since
\Phi^{-1}
is globally Lipschitz continuous by Proposition
1
, the proof reduces to proving the corresponding
L^{p}(\Omega)
-estimate for
|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}-Y(t_{m})|
:

|\hat{X}^{LS}_{m}-X(t_{m})|=|\Phi^{-1}(\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m})-\Phi^{-1}(Y(t_{m}))|\leq L_{\Phi^{-1}}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}-Y(t_{m})|.

The rest of the proof is based on comparing the following representation formulas for
\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}
and
Y(t_{m})
:

\begin{split}\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}&=Y(0)+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\hat{y}_{k}(t_{k+1})-\tilde{y}_{k}(t_{k+1})\\
&+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\int^{t_{k+1}}_{t_{k}}\left(H(\tilde{y}_{k}(s))+\mu\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+B(t_{m}),\end{split}

introduced in equation (
26
), and the integral representation of
Y
that we re-write on similar form

\begin{split}Y(t_{m})&=Y(0)+\int^{t_{m}}_{0}\left(H(Y(s))+\mu)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+B(t_{m})\\
&=Y(0)+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left(H(Y(s))+\mu\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+B(t_{m})\end{split}

at
t=t_{m}
. Therefore

\begin{split}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}-Y(t_{m})|&\leq\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}|\hat{y}_{k}(t_{k+1})-\tilde{y}_{k}(t_{k+1})|\\
&+\left|\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(\tilde{y}_{k}(s)\right)-H\left(Y(s)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|,\end{split}

(28)

We first estimate the first term in equation (
28
). By Assumption
4
, we can bound

\begin{split}|\hat{y}_{k}(t_{k+1})-\tilde{y}_{k}(t_{k+1})|&\leq K\sqrt{1+|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}|^{2}}\Delta t^{2}\\
&\leq K\left(1+|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}|\right)\Delta t^{2}\\
&\leq K\left(1+\sup_{k=0,\ldots,M}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}|\right)\Delta t^{2},\end{split}

almost surely. Recall that
\sup_{k=0,\ldots,M}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}|
is finite, almost surely, by Proposition
10
and that the bound can be made independent of
\Delta t
because of the time-step restriction
KT\Delta t\leq\gamma
. Thus

\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}|\hat{y}_{k}(t_{k+1})-\tilde{y}_{k}(t_{k+1})|\leq Kt_{m}\left(1+\sup_{k=0,\ldots,M}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}|\right)\Delta t.

Let us now estimate the second term on the right hand side of equation (
28
). We first split the integral as follows

\begin{split}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(\tilde{y}_{k}(s)\right)-H\left(Y(s)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s&=\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(\tilde{y}_{k}(s)\right)-H\left(\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}\right)-H\left(Y(t_{k})\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(Y(t_{k})\right)-H\left(Y(s)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

(29)

and consider each term separately. Observe that the second term on the right hand side of equation (
29
) is the term that we need for Grönwall’s lemma after the simplification

\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}\right)-H\left(Y(t_{k})\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\Delta t\left(H\left(\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}\right)-H\left(Y(t_{k})\right)\right)

and after estimating it

\left|\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}\right)-H\left(Y(t_{k})\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq L_{H}\Delta t\left|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}-Y(t_{k})\right|

using that
H
is globally Lipschitz continuous by Proposition
2
. Recall now that
\tilde{y}_{k}(s)=\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}+\int_{t_{k}}^{s}H(\tilde{y}_{k}(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r
, for
s\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
, is the exact solution to the ODE in equation (
23
) starting at
t_{k}
with initial value
\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}
. Therefore, the first term on the right hand side in equation (
29
) can be estimated by

\begin{split}\left|\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(\tilde{y}_{k}(s)\right)-H\left(\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|&\leq L_{H}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}|\tilde{y}_{k}(s)-\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq L_{H}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}|H(\tilde{y}_{k}(r))|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

since
H
is Lipschitz continuous and by using that
H
is uniformly bounded by
L_{H}+|\mu|
by Proposition
2
we can further estimate

L_{H}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}|H(\tilde{y}_{k}(r))|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq L_{H}\left(L_{H}+|\mu|\right)\Delta t^{2}.

It remains to consider the third term in equation (
29
)

\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(Y(t_{k})\right)-H\left(Y(s)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Recall now that
H\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
by Proposition
2
. Thus we can use Itô’s formula as such:

\begin{split}H(Y(s))&=H(Y(t_{k}))+\int_{t_{k}}^{s}H(Y(r))H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}H^{\prime\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r+\int_{t_{k}}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\end{split}

to see that

\begin{split}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(Y(t_{k})\right)-H\left(Y(s)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s&=\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}H(Y(r))H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{k+1}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}H^{\prime\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&+\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(30)

To obtain convergence order
1
, we have to sum over
k=0,\ldots,m-1
before estimating the separate terms in equation (
30
)

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}H\left(Y(t_{k})\right)-H\left(Y(s)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s&=\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H(Y(r))H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&+\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

(31)

where we recall that
\ell(s)=t_{k}
whenever
s\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})
. The first two terms on the right hand side of equation (
31
) can estimated by

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H(Y(r))H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|&\leq\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}|H(Y(r))H^{\prime}(Y(r))|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq L_{H}\left(L_{H}+|\mu|\right)t_{m}\Delta t\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\left|\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}|H^{\prime\prime}(Y(r))|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}L_{H}t_{m}\Delta t,\end{split}

where we used that
H
and
H^{\prime},H^{\prime\prime}
are uniformly bounded by
L_{H}+|\mu|
and
L_{H}
, respectively, by Proposition
2
. The third term on the right hand side of equation (
31
) is a random variable with finite
p
th moments for every
p\geq 1
, see Appendix
A
for a proof. Inserting everything back into equation (
28
) and estimating
m\Delta t=t_{m}\leq T
gives us

\begin{split}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}-Y(t_{m})|&\leq KT\left(1+\sup_{k=0,\ldots,M}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}|\right)\Delta t+2L_{H}\left(L_{H}+|\mu|\right)T\Delta t\\
&+\frac{1}{2}L_{H}T\Delta t+\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|\\
&+L_{H}\Delta t\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}-Y(t_{k})|,\end{split}

where we have tried to group the terms to make it clear where the terms come from. An application of a discrete Grönwall’s lemma followed by applying
\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M}
now yields

\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}-Y(t_{m})|\leq\Delta t\eta_{\Delta t}e^{L_{H}T}

(32)

where
\eta_{\Delta t}
is the random variable

\begin{split}\eta_{\Delta t}&=KT\left(1+\sup_{k=0,\ldots,M}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{k}|\right)+2L_{H}\left(L_{H}+|\mu|\right)T+\frac{1}{2}L_{H}T\\
&+\left|\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|.\end{split}

Note that the random variable
\eta_{\Delta t}
is not finite almost sure uniformly in
\Delta t
, but
\Delta t\eta_{\Delta t}
is finite almost surely uniformly in
\Delta t
. To finish the proof we need to make sure that
\eta_{\Delta t}
has finite
p
th moments for every
p\geq 1
, since then

\begin{split}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M}|\hat{Y}^{LS}_{m}-Y(t_{m})|^{p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\leq\Delta t\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\eta_{\Delta t}^{p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}e^{L_{H}T}\\
&=C(p,H,K,T,\mu,\gamma)\Delta t.\end{split}

By Proposition
10
, the first three terms constituting
\eta_{\Delta t}
are almost surely bounded and thus in particular have finite
p
th moments for every
p\geq 1
. An application of the stochastic Fubini’s theorem followed by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) inequality gives us that the remaining term has finite
p
th moments

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|^{p}\right]\leq C(p)L_{H}T^{p/2},

where the constant
C(p)
is the constant in the BDG inequality. We provide the proof of the above
p
th moment estimate in Appendix
A
. This concludes the proof.
∎

In the same manner as almost sure pathwise convergence of
X^{LS}
towards
X
given in Corollary
8
, we obtain, as a corollary to Theorem
11
, almost sure pathwise convergence of
\hat{X}^{LS}
towards
X
with rate
1-\epsilon
for every
\epsilon>0
.

Corollary 12
.

Under the same assumptions and notation as in Theorem
11
, there exists for every
\epsilon>0
a random variable
\zeta_{\epsilon}
, with
{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\zeta_{\epsilon}|^{p}\right]<\infty
for every

, such that

\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M}|\hat{X}^{LS}_{m}-X(t_{m})|\leq\zeta_{\epsilon}\Delta t^{1-\epsilon}

Appendix A
Proof of a lemma

Lemma 13
.

Recall that
\ell(s)=t_{k}
whenever
s\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})
and that
\left(B(t)\right)_{t\in[0,T]}
denotes a standard Brownian motion. Then, for every
p\geq 1
, it holds that

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|^{p}\right]\leq C(p)L_{H}T^{p/2}\Delta t^{p}

where
C(p)
is the BDG constant.

Proof of Lemma
13
.

The proof essentially consists of applying the stochastic Fubini theorem followed by the BDG inequality. We first re-write the integral as follows

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{1}_{\{r\in(\ell(s),s)\}}(r,s)H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

where
\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x)=1
if
x\in A
and
\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x)=0
if
x\not\in A
. We now apply the stochastic Fubini’s theorem

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|^{p}\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{1}_{\{r\in(\ell(s),s)\}}(r,s)H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\right|^{p}\right]

followed by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) inequality to estimate

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{1}_{\{r\in(\ell(s),s)\}}(r,s)H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\right|^{p}\right]\\
\leq C(p){\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{1}_{\{r\in(\ell(s),s)\}}(r,s)H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p/2}\right],

(34)

where
C(p)
is the constant from the BDG inequality. We now use that the inner integral can be expressed as

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{1}_{\{r\in(\ell(s),s)\}}(r,s)H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s&=H^{\prime}(Y(r))\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{1}_{\{r\in(\ell(s),s)\}}(r,s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&=H^{\prime}(Y(r))\left(\ell(r+\Delta t)-r\right)\end{split}

and so we can estimate

\begin{split}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{1}_{\{r\in(\ell(s),s)\}}(r,s)H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}&=|H^{\prime}(Y(r))|^{2}\left(\ell(r+\Delta t)-r\right)^{2}\\
&\leq|H^{\prime}(Y(r))|^{2}\Delta t^{2}.\end{split}

Collecting everything gives us the desired estimate

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\ell(s)}^{s}H^{\prime}(Y(r))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|^{p}\right]&\leq C(p){\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}|H^{\prime}(Y(r))|^{2}\Delta t^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p/2}\right]\\
&=C(p)\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|H^{\prime}(x)|T^{p/2}\Delta t^{p}\\
&\leq C(p)L_{H}T^{p/2}\Delta t^{p},\end{split}

where we in the last inequality used Proposition
2
.
∎

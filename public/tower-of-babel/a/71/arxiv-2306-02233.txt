Bulk and film synthesis pathways to ternary magnesium tungsten nitrides
Christopher L. Rom, Rebecca W. Smaha, Callan A. Knebel, Karen N. Heinselman, James R. Neilson, Sage R. Bauers, Andriy Zakutayev

Abstract
Bulk solid state synthesis of nitride materials usually leads to thermodynamically stable, cation-ordered crystal structures, whereas thin film synthesis tends to favor disordered, metastable phases. This dichotomy is inconvenient both for basic materials discovery, where non-equilibrium thin film synthesis methods can be useful to overcome reaction kinetic barriers, and for practical technology applications where stable ground state structures are sometimes required. Here, we explore the uncharted Mg-W-N chemical phase space, using rapid thermal annealing to reconcile the differences between thin film (on ambient or heated substrates) and bulk powder syntheses. Combinatorial co-sputtering synthesis from Mg and W targets in a N 2 \text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} environment yielded cation-disordered Mg-W-N phases in the rocksalt ( 0.1 < 0.1< Mg/(Mg+W) < 0.9 <0.9 ), and hexagonal boron nitride ( 0.7 < 0.7< Mg/(Mg+W) < 0.9 <0.9 ) structure types. In contrast, bulk synthesis produced a cation-ordered polymorph of MgWN 2 \text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} that consists of alternating layers of rocksalt-like [ MgN 6 \text{MgN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}} ] octahedra and nickeline-like [ WN 6 \text{WN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}} ] trigonal prisms (denoted “rocksaline”). Thermodynamic calculations corroborate these observations, showing rocksaline MgWN 2 \text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} is stable while other polymorphs are metastable. We also show that rapid thermal annealing can convert disordered rocksalt films to this cation-ordered polymorph, near the MgWN 2 \text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} stoichiometry. Electronic structure calculations suggest that this rocksalt-to-rocksaline structural transformation should also drive a metallic-to-semiconductor transformation, but our resistivity measurements were only able to confirm the semiconducting behavior of rocksaline MgWN 2 \text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} and rocksalt Mg 3 ​ WN 4 \text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{WN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}} . In addition to revealing three new phases (rocksalt MgWN 2 \text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} and Mg 3 ​ WN 4 \text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{WN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}} , hexagonal boron nitride Mg 3 ​ WN 4 \text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{WN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}} , and rocksaline MgWN 2 \text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} ), these findings highlight how rapid thermal annealing can control polymorphic transformations, adding a new strategy for exploration of thermodynamic stability in uncharted phase spaces.

Introduction

Ternary nitrides are an emerging class of ceramic materials with applications in solid-state lighting, electrochemical energy storage, optoelectronics, piezoelectrics, ferroelectrics, and wide-bandgap semiconductor devices.
Greenaway et al. 2021

However, nitrides are underexplored, lagging behind oxides with an order of magnitude fewer scientific publications and known structures.
Greenaway et al. 2021
;
Zakutayev 2016
;
Sun et al. 2019

Therefore, exploring chemical phase space to find new ternary nitrides will open avenues to identifying novel materials with intriguing functional properties that may underlie future technologies.
Recent breakthroughs in high-throughput computational techniques successfully predicted many new ternary nitrides,
Sun et al. 2019
and combinatorial co-sputtering has proven to be a powerful tool for experimentally realizing these predicted materials.
Zakutayev et al. 2022
;
Zhuk et al. 2021
;
Heinselman et al. 2019

;
Kikuchi et al. 2021

In particular, Mg and Zn can be combined with early transition metals and main-group elements to form ternary nitrides that are structurally-related to the binary
\text{\text{$M$}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}\text{N}
nitrides (e.g., wurtzite GaN or rocksalt TiN).
Zakutayev et al. 2022

With

M
as a main group or transition metal, the stoichiometries
\text{\text{$A$}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{2+}}\text{\text{$M$}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{4+}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
,
\text{\text{$A$}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{2+}}\text{\text{$M$}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{5+}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
, and
\text{\text{$A$}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{2+}}\text{\text{$M$}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{6+}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
have the 1:1 cation:anion ratio of
\text{\text{$M$}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}\text{N}
compounds, and semiconducting properties emerge when
M
is a
d^{0}
transition metal or a main-group element.
Martinez et al. 2017
;
Zakutayev et al. 2022

Furthermore, Zn and Mg favor 4- or 6-fold coordination, respectively, and therefore tend to produce the respective wurtzite-derived or rocksalt-derived structures.
For example, exploration of the Zn-Mo-N phase space by combinatorial sputtering revealed a wurtzite-like structure across a range of compositions, from metallic
\text{ZnMoN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
to semiconducting wurtzite-like
\text{Zn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{MoN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
(with a bandgap of 2.4 eV).
Arca et al. 2018

Similarly, the Mg-W-N phase space is a promising area of exploration because W has multiple possible oxidation states (between 0 and 6+, inclusive), potentially leading to varied structures and properties.

Combinatorial co-sputtering is a good choice to rapidly survey this potentially complex phase space.
However, materials discovered by combinatorial sputtering often deviate from those predicted by computational methods or synthesized in bulk on a key detail: cation (dis)order.
Zakutayev et al. 2022
;
Schnepf et al. 2020

This discrepancy can potentially be beneficial, such as when cation-disorder lowers the bandgap into the visible range.
Schnepf et al. 2020
;
Greenaway et al. 2022

In other cases, cation disorder negatively impacts optoelectronic properties by localizing charge carriers
Schnepf et al. 2020
;
Lany et al. 2017
or even leading to polymorphism.
Woods-Robinson et al. 2022

How to control this structural polymorphism and cation disorder is still an open question. For example, annealing conditions are known to affect the degree of cation (dis)order,
Blanton et al. 2017
;
Fetzer et al. 2002
but that control is often material-specific and difficult to explore in a high-throughput manner.
Schnepf et al. 2020

Therefore, understanding metastable phase formation and cation (dis)order in ternary nitrides remains a pressing challenge for the field to fully realize the tunable properties of this promising class of materials.

In this report, we describe the discovery of several new Mg-W-N compounds in this previously-unexplored ternary phase space.
We show that thin film combinatorial co-sputtering methods yielded cation-disordered rocksalt (RS, space group
Fm\bar{3}m
,
0.1<
Mg/(Mg+W)
<0.9
) and hexagonal boron nitride structures (h-BN, space group
P6_{3}/mmc
,
0.7<
Mg/(Mg+W)
<0.9
) covering the
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
and
\text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{WN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
stoichiometries.
In contrast, our bulk ceramic methods yielded cation-ordered
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
with space group
P6_{3}/mmc
.
We call this cation-ordered structure “rocksaline” (RL) as a portmanteau of the rocksalt-like Mg and nickeline-like W layers.
Thermodynamic calculations confirm that the RL polymorph is the ground state of the
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
composition.
Rapid thermal annealing (RTA) of thin films converted the disordered RS structure to this ordered RL structure, in a narrow composition window near the
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
stoichiometry, resolving this difference between the thin film and the bulk synthesis results.
The
\text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{WN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
was only produced in thin films, and formed in either the RS or h-BN structure.
Thermodynamic calculations reveal these polymorphs to be close in energy to one another and slightly metastable.
Electronic structure calculations suggest that RL
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
should be a semiconductor, while RS
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
should be metallic.
Resistivity measurements of the synthesized films as a function of composition and temperature show both RL
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
and RS
\text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{WN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
are semiconducting, but were unable to verify the charge transport behavior of RS
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
.
These findings show how RTA treatment of disordered films can build upon existing combinatorial co-sputtering techniques to rapidly assess the thermodynamic synthesizability of a predicted cation-ordered phase.

Methods

2.1
Bulk structural measurements and analysis

Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) measurements were performed using a Bruker DaVinci diffractometer with Cu K
\alpha
X-ray radiation. All samples were prepared for PXRD from within the glovebox by placing powder on off-axis cut silicon single crystal wafers to reduce the background, and then covered with polyimide tape to slow exposure to the atmosphere.
However, as PXRD showed that the product (
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
) is air stable, a PXRD pattern was collected without tape to minimize the large scattering background (Figure
1
).

Full-pattern fitting of thin film XRD, GIWAXS, and PXRD data was performed using TOPAS v6.
Coelho 2018

For thin film samples, 2D diffraction images showed texturing (i.e., preferred orientation), meaning that integrated peak intensities may not directly correspond to electron density.
Therefore, we performed LeBail fits using the appropriate space group and refined lattice parameters and crystallite size broadening.
For the
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
phase in the RL structure, a model was created by substituting W for Mo in the previously reported
\text{MgMoN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
structure in space group
P6_{3}/mmc
.
Verrelli et al. 2017
Rietveld analysis was then performed to refine the lattice parameters, crystallite size broadening, and site occupancy.
In all cases, 10-term polynomial functions were refined to fit the background.
Structural visualizations were performed with VESTA.
Momma and Izumi 2011

2.2
Thin film synthesis and annealing experiments

Combinatorial co-sputtering of Mg-W-N film libraries were conducted in two custom vacuum chambers, both with base pressures of
<10^{-7}
Torr.
Mg and W targets (2 inch diameter, Kurt J. Lesker, 99.95% purity) were angled towards a stationary substrate and sputtered using radiofrequency (RF) excited plasma of the Ar/
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
gas mixture in the chamber.
Sputter powers ranged from 30 W to 90 W for each target, to shift the Mg/(Mg+W) ratio across the whole composition window.
Gases were introduced at 50 sccm Ar and 50 sccm
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
, with a 10 Torr process pressure during deposition.
The N plasma intensity was enhanced by RF plasma source at 350 W.
Most samples were deposited on 2 inch by 2 inch (001)-oriented Si substrates.
Select samples were deposited on insulating substrates (e.g., 100 nm
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
on Si or 100 nm
SiN
x
on Si) for electronic property measurements, as indicated in the text.
Select samples were coated with a 15 nm TiN capping layer, sputtered from a 2 inch diameter Ti target, to protect against atmospheric exposure.
During these capping depositions, the substrate was rotated to ensure a homogeneous capping layer.
A diagram for this experimental setup is shown in Figure
S1
A.

Rapid thermal annealing (RTA) experiments were conducted on individual compositionally-graded library rows in flowing
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
atmosphere at ambient pressure. Heating profiles started with a +100 °C/min ramp to 100 °C and a 3 min dwell to drive off adsorbed water, followed by a +100 °C/min ramp to a
T_{\mathrm{anneal}}
set-point in the 600-1200 °C range for a 3 min dwell. Samples were cooled by turning off the heating source. A diagram for this experimental setup is shown in Figure
S1
B.

2.4
Thin film property measurements

Room temperature resistivity was measured on thin films using a custom-built collinear four-point probe instrument by sweeping current between the outer two pins while measuring voltage between the inner pins (1 mm between each pin). Conventional geometric corrections were applied to convert the measured resistance into sheet resistance and then resistivity.
Smits 1958
The measured films were deposited on insulating substrates (either 100 nm thick
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
on Si or 100 nm thick
SiN
x

Temperature-dependent electrical resistivity was measured on thin films using a Lake Shore Cryotronics Model 8425. Small squares (5 mm
\times
5 mm) were cleaved out of libraries deposited on insulating substrates.
For compositions near
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
, indium contacts were pressed into the film near the corners of the squares. Indium contacts were non-ohmic on
\text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{WN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
films, so Ti/Au contacts were deposited by evaporation.
Temperature-dependent sheet resistance was measured from 104 K to 298 K for most samples, with RL
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
measured from 36 K to 298 K.
Resistivity was calculated using XRF-measured film thickness.

As these reagents are air sensitive, they were prepared and stored in an argon-filled glovebox (

A Zr foil cap was fit into the mouth of the alumina crucible to decrease
\text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}

Without air exposure, the samples were reacted in a tube furnace under flowing purified
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
(ca. 20 mL/min flow rate).
A diagram for this system is shown in Figure
S1
C.
Reactions were conducted by heating the sample at +10 °C/min to the dwell temperature, dwelling for approximately 5–20 h at various temperatures up to 1100 °C, and then cooling by switching off the furnace.
Samples were recovered into the Ar glovebox.
This procedure was adapted from the strategy used by Verrelli, et al., to synthesize
\text{MgMoN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}

Conclusions

We synthesized three new polymorphs of magnesium tungsten nitrides by bulk and film synthesis methods in a previously empty ternary phase space, and demonstrated how rapid thermal annealing can be a powerful tool to reconcile thermodynamic and non-equilibrium synthesis pathways.
Combinatorial co-sputtering yielded cation-disordered rocksalt structures across a wide composition range including
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
, while samples near the
\text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{WN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
stoichiometry crystallized in either a cation-disordered rocksalt or a cation-disordered hexagonal boron nitride structure.
Rapid thermal annealing treatments of these combinatorial libraries show that rocksalt
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
converts to a cation-ordered rocksaline structure at
T_{\mathrm{anneal}}
= 900 °C, in a narrow composition window around the nominal stoichiometry.
This cation-ordered
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
phase also appeared in bulk ceramic syntheses and was predicted as the ground state structure by theoretical calculations, indicating that annealing of thin film libraries can potentially access the thermodynamically stable ternary nitrides.
Density of state calculations suggest cation-disordered rocksalt
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
should exhibit metallic properties while cation-ordered rocksaline
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
should exhibit semiconducting behavior.
Resistivity measurements show that rocksaline
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
and rocksalt
\text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{WN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
are semiconductors, but we were unable to experimentally confirm the metallic behavior of rocksalt
\text{MgWN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
.
Resistivity varies by six orders of magnitude as a function of Mg content.
In sum, these findings expand the toolkit through which combinatorial co-sputtering experiments can explore the thermodynamic landscape in search of new nitride compounds.

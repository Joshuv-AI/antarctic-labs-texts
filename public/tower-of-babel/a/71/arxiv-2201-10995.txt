Instability of the ferromagnetic quantum critical point in strongly interacting 2D and 3D electron gases with arbitrary spin-orbit splitting
Dmitry Miserev, 1∗ Daniel Loss, 1 and Jelena Klinovaja 1

Abstract
In this work we revisit itinerant ferromagnetism in 2D and 3D electron gases with arbitrary spin-orbit splitting and strong electron-electron interaction. We identify the resonant scattering processes close to the Fermi surface that are responsible for the instability of the ferromagnetic quantum critical point at low temperatures. In contrast to previous theoretical studies, we show that such processes cannot be fully suppressed even in presence of arbitrary spin-orbit splitting. A fully self-consistent non-perturbative treatment of the electron-electron interaction close to the phase transition shows that these resonant processes always destabilize the ferromagnetic quantum critical point and lead to a first-order phase transition. Characteristic signatures of these processes can be measured via the non-analytic dependence of the spin susceptibility on magnetic field both far away or close to the phase transition.

The problem becomes even more complicated if a spin-orbit (SO) splitting

In this work, we consider the general case of a

In this work we apply the dimensional reduction of the electron Green function which we developed earlier in Ref.

In order to access the strongly interacting regime, we treat the resonant scattering processes near the Fermi surface

Within our model, the non-analyticities are strongly enhanced close to the FQPT and remain negative at arbitrary SO splitting.

this calculation to the case of a strongly interacting
electron gas with a singularity (not necessarily a pole) at the Fermi surface
of arbitrary geometry.
The Appendix
A
also contains details for nearly spherical Fermi surfaces.
The case of spherical Fermi surfaces
is covered in Ref.
miserev
.

IV
Non-analyticities in
\Omega
: limit of weak interaction

In this section we calculate the non-analytic corrections
to the thermodynamic potential
\Omega

in the limit of weak electron-electron interaction.
The calculation is performed within second order perturbation theory
and it is valid in the paramagnetic Fermi liquid phase
far away from the FQPT.
However, this calculation is important
because it allows us to identify the
resonant scattering processes
close to the Fermi surface which are responsible for the non-analytic terms in
\Omega
.
In the following sections we treat these processes
non-perturbatively
and find that the non-analytic terms
are strongly enhanced close to the FQPT.
The results of this section extend existing theories

belitz1
;
belitz2
;
chubukov1
;
maslov
;
zak1

to the case of arbitrary spin splitting.
In particular, we show that arbitrary SO splitting
is not able to gap out all soft fluctuation modes
and the non-analyticity in
\Omega
with respect to the
magnetic field
\bm{B}
survives,
in contrast to predictions of Ref.
kirk
.
In this section we are only after
the non-analytic terms in
\Omega
,
all analytic corrections will be dropped.

IV.1
First-order interaction correction to
\Omega

Let us start from the first-order interaction correction to
\Omega
, see Fig.
2
(a):

\displaystyle\Omega^{(1)}=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\int dz\,V_{0}(z)P_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(z),

(20)

\displaystyle P_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(z)=-{\rm Tr}\left\{G^{(0)}_{\sigma}(z)G^{(0)}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(-z)\right\},

(21)

where
z=(\tau,\bm{r})
,

{\rm Tr}
stands for the spin trace, and

P_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(z)
is the particle-hole
bubble. Here,

V_{0}(z)
is the Coulomb interaction,

\displaystyle V_{0}(\tau,\bm{r})=\frac{e^{2}}{\epsilon r}\delta(\tau),

(22)

where
e
is the elementary charge,

\epsilon
the dielectric constant,
and
\delta(\tau)
is due to the instantaneous nature of the Coulomb interaction
(the speed of light is much larger than the Fermi velocity).
Using the asymptotics of the
Green function
G^{(0)}_{\sigma}(\tau,\bm{r})
, see Eq. (
18
),
we find the asymptotics of the
particle-hole bubble:

\displaystyle P_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{r})=P^{L}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{r})+P^{K}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{r}),

(23)

\displaystyle P^{L}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{r})\approx\frac{\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}{2\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{F}r}\right)^{D-1}\frac{v_{F}^{2}\tau^{2}-r^{2}}{(r^{2}+v_{F}^{2}\tau^{2})^{2}},

(24)

\displaystyle P^{K}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{r})\approx\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{r^{2}+v_{F}^{2}\tau^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{F}r}\right)^{D-1}

\displaystyle\times\left[e^{-2i\vartheta}e^{ir(k_{\sigma}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})+k_{\sigma^{\prime}}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}))}\left|M_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})\right|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+e^{2i\vartheta}e^{-ir(k_{\sigma}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})+k_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}))}\left|M_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})\right|^{2}\right],

(25)

where the matrix elements
M_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\pm\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})
are given by Eq. (
8
).
Here
P^{L}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{r})
is the Landau damping contribution to the
particle-hole bubble
coming from the forward scattering.
It is clear that this contribution is insensitive to the spin splitting.
The second contribution,
P^{K}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\bm{r})
,
is the Kohn anomaly coming from the backscattering with the momentum transfer close to
2k_{F}
.
The Kohn anomaly is sensitive to the spin splitting through the oscillatory factors
containing the Fermi momenta
k_{\sigma}(\pm\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})
, see Eq. (
9
).

As only the Kohn anomaly is sensitive to the spin splitting
\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{p}})
,
we can simplify Eq. (
20
):

\displaystyle\Omega^{(1)}=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\int\limits_{S_{D-1}}d\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dr\,r^{D-1}\frac{e^{2}}{\epsilon r}P^{K}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(0,\bm{r}),

(26)

where
S_{D-1}
is the
(D-1)
-dimensional unit sphere,

d\bm{r}=r^{D-1}\,dr\,d\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}
.
The integral over
r
is divergent at small
r

(the ultraviolet divergence)
because it takes the following form:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{3}}e^{ir\Delta}\to\infty,

(27)

where
\Delta
is either equal to

\Delta=k_{\sigma}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})+k_{\sigma^{\prime}}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})

or to
\Delta=-k_{\sigma}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})-k_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})
.
This divergence comes from
the asymptotics of the particle-hole bubble, see Eq. (
25
),
that is only valid at
r\gg\lambda_{F}
.
Therefore, the lower limit for
r
in Eq. (
27
)
is bounded by
r\sim\lambda_{F}
.
This divergence can also be cured
via the analytical continuation to the Euler gamma function
\Gamma(x)

using the following identity:

\displaystyle\hskip-12.0ptI_{\alpha}(\Delta)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{\alpha}}e^{ir\Delta}=\frac{\pi|\Delta|^{\alpha-1}}{\sin(\pi\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}(\alpha-1){\rm sgn}(\Delta)}.

(28)

In our case
\alpha=3

and the integral is indeed divergent due to

\sin(3\pi)=0
in the denominator.
Therefore, we consider
\alpha=3+\delta

and take the limit
\delta\to 0
:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{3}}e^{ir\Delta}=\frac{\Delta^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}+\ln\left|\Delta\right|-i\frac{\pi}{2}{\rm sgn}\left(\Delta\right)\right).

(29)

Now it is clear that the physical dimension
of
\Delta
under the logarithm
has to be compensated by the ultraviolet scale

p_{0}\sim 2k_{F}

which is equivalent to cutting the lower limit in Eq. (
27
) at
r\sim\lambda_{F}
:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\sim\lambda_{F}}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{3}}e^{ir\Delta}=\frac{\Delta^{2}}{2}\left(\ln\left|\frac{\Delta}{p_{0}}\right|-i\frac{\pi}{2}{\rm sgn}\left(\Delta\right)\right).

(30)

Now, we come back to
\Omega^{(1)}

where
\Delta
is either equal to

\Delta=k_{\sigma}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})+k_{\sigma^{\prime}}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})

or to
\Delta=-k_{\sigma}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})-k_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})
,
so using Eq. (
9
) we find:

\displaystyle|\Delta|\approx 2k_{F}+\frac{\sigma\beta(\pm\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})+\sigma^{\prime}\beta(\mp\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})}{v_{F}}.

(31)

As the spin splitting is much smaller than
the Fermi energy,
we can expand the logarithm

\ln|\Delta/p_{0}|

in the analytic Taylor series with respect to the
spin splitting.
Hence, we see that
\Omega^{(1)}

does not contain any non-analyticities
for arbitrary spin splitting.

Here we have performed the calculations for the
long-range Coulomb interaction Eq. (
22
).
Finite electron density results in the Thomas-Fermi screening of the
long range Coulomb tail on the scale of the screening length
r_{0}
.
The weak coupling limit that we consider in this section
corresponds to
r_{0}\gg\lambda_{F}
.
However, the
integral over
r
in
\Omega^{(1)}

converges at
r\sim\lambda_{F}\ll r_{0}
, because
\Delta\approx 2k_{F}
here.
Therefore, we can indeed neglect the Thomas-Fermi screening in this section.

IV.2
Second-order interaction corrections to
\Omega

We see from the calculation of
\Omega^{(1)}

that the non-analytic terms
may come from the oscillatory integrals
like the one in Eq. (
28
).
However, we have to subtract the
2k_{F}

factor first, such that
\Delta

in Eq. (
28
) becomes proportional to the spin splitting.
One way to achieve this
is to consider
\Omega^{(1)}
, see Eq. (
20
),
with the interaction
V(\tau,\bm{r})

which has oscillatory components
e^{\pm i2k_{F}r}
.
In fact, the electron-electron interaction
acquires such components upon
the dynamic screening by the particle-hole bubble.
One consequence of this is the Thomas-Fermi screening which we already discussed
and concluded that it is not important if the interaction is weak.
However, there is another much more important consequence of such dressing
that results in
2k_{F}
harmonics in the interaction
due to backscattering of electrons near the Fermi surface, the effect known as Friedel oscillations.
As we consider the correlations at large
r\sim v_{F}/\beta\gg\lambda_{F}
, where
\beta
is a characteristic value of the spin splitting at the Fermi surface,
the interaction matrix elements at the momentum transfer
2k_{F}
are effectively local,
so we can use the effective contact interaction:

\displaystyle V_{2k_{F}}(z)=u\,\delta(\bm{r})\delta(\tau)=u\,\delta(z),

(32)

\displaystyle u\approx V_{0}(2k_{F})=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{D}{2}}\Gamma(D-1)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)}\frac{e^{2}}{\epsilon(2k_{F})^{D-1}},

(33)

where
\delta(\bm{r})
is the
D
-dimensional delta function,

V_{0}(q)
is the Fourier transform of the Coulomb
interaction Eq. (
22
).

If we dress the interaction line in Fig.
2
(a)
by a single particle-hole bubble,
we get the second-order diagram for
\Omega

shown in Fig.
2
(b):

\displaystyle\Omega^{(2)}=-\frac{1}{4}\sum\limits_{\sigma_{i}}\int dz_{1}dz_{2}dz_{3}\,V_{2k_{F}}(z_{2})V_{2k_{F}}(z_{3}-z_{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0pt\times P_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}(z_{1})P_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}(z_{3}-z_{2}).

(34)

Using the contact approximation Eq. (
32
),
we simplify
\Omega^{(2)}
to the following expression:

\displaystyle\Omega^{(2)}=-\frac{u^{2}}{4}\sum\limits_{\sigma_{i}}\int dz\,P_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}(z)P_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}(z),

(35)

where
z=(\tau,\bm{r})
.
From Eqs. (
23
)–(
25
) we find that only the product of Kohn anomalies
contains slowly oscillating terms
on the scale
v_{F}/\beta
, where
\beta
stands for the characteristic spin splitting at the Fermi surface:

\displaystyle P_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}(z)P_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}(z)=\frac{e^{ir\Delta^{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})}\left|M_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})M_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})\right|^{2}+e^{-ir\Delta^{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})}\left|M_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})M_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})\right|^{2}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{4}\left(\lambda_{F}r\right)^{2(D-1)}\left(r^{2}+v_{F}^{2}\tau^{2}\right)^{2}}+\dots,

(36)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})=k_{\sigma_{1}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})+k_{\sigma_{2}}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})-k_{\sigma_{3}}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})-k_{\sigma_{4}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})\approx\frac{\left(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{4}\right)\beta(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})+\left(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{3}\right)\beta(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})}{v_{F}},

(37)

where dots in Eq. (
36
) stand for
the rapidly oscillating terms on the scale of
2k_{F}
and
4k_{F}

and also the forward scattering contribution
which does not contain any non-analytic dependence on the spin splitting.
We used Eq. (
9
) to express
\Delta^{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})

in terms of the spin splitting.

Then we substitute Eq. (
36
) into Eq. (
35
)
and evaluate the integral over
z=(\tau,\bm{r})
.
The integral over
\tau
is elementary:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\tau}{\left(r^{2}+v_{F}^{2}\tau^{2}\right)^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{2v_{F}r^{3}}.

(38)

The integral over
r
can be represented using
the integral
I_{\alpha}(\Delta)
defined in Eq. (
28
):

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\Omega^{(2)}=-\frac{u^{2}}{2^{6}\pi^{3}v_{F}\lambda_{F}^{2(D-1)}}\sum\limits_{\sigma_{i}}\int\limits_{S_{D-1}}d\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\left|M_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})M_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})\right|^{2}{\rm Re}\left(I_{D+2}\left(\Delta^{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})\right)\right),

(39)

where
{\rm Re}
stands for the real part.
Here we used that
I_{\alpha}(-x)=I^{*}_{\alpha}(x)
,
where the star corresponds to the complex conjugation.

At this point it is convenient to introduce the
dimensionless interaction parameter
g
:

\displaystyle g=uN_{F}=\frac{umk_{F}^{D-2}}{2^{D-1}\pi^{\frac{D}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)},

(40)

where
N_{F}
is the density of states per band at the Fermi level.
Substituting Eq. (
33
) into Eq. (
40
),
we find an estimate for the dimensionless coupling constant
g
:

\displaystyle g\approx\frac{\Gamma(D-1)}{2^{2D-3}\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)}\frac{1}{k_{F}a_{B}},\,\,a_{B}=\frac{\epsilon}{me^{2}},

(41)

where
a_{B}
is the effective Bohr radius.
The weak coupling regime corresponds to high densities such that
k_{F}a_{B}\gg 1

or
g\ll 1
.

Then Eq. (
39
) can be represented in the following form:

\displaystyle\Omega^{(2)}=L_{D}\frac{v_{F}^{D+1}}{2^{D+2}}\sum\limits_{\sigma_{i}}\int\limits_{S_{D-1}}d\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}\,

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt\times\left|M_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})M_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})\right|^{2}\left|\Delta^{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})\right|^{D+1},

(42)

\displaystyle L_{D}=\frac{g^{2}}{32}\left(\frac{2}{\pi v_{F}}\right)^{D}\frac{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(D+2\right)}\frac{1}{\cos\left(\pi\frac{D}{2}\right)}.

(43)

We perform the summation over the band indexes
\sigma_{i}
explicitly:

\displaystyle\Omega^{(2)}=L_{D}\int\limits_{S_{D-1}}d\bm{n}\left[\left|M_{+-}(\bm{n})M_{+-}(-\bm{n})\right|^{2}\left|\beta(\bm{n})-\beta(-\bm{n})\right|^{D+1}+\left|M_{++}(\bm{n})M_{--}(\bm{n})\right|^{2}\left|\beta(\bm{n})+\beta(-\bm{n})\right|^{D+1}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 140.0pt\left.+2\left(\left|M_{++}(\bm{n})M_{-+}(\bm{n})\right|^{2}+\left|M_{--}(\bm{n})M_{+-}(\bm{n})\right|^{2}\right)\left|\beta(\bm{n})\right|^{D+1}\right].

(44)

Here we dropped the index
\bm{r}
in
\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}
,
such that
\bm{n}
can be also interpreted as the
unit vector
\bm{n}_{\bm{p}}=\bm{p}/p
,
p\approx k_{F}
,
in the momentum space.
This interpretation makes sense
because the asymptotics of the Green function, see Eq. (
18
),
comes from small vicinities of two points on the
Fermi surface whose outward normals are collinear with
\bm{r}
.
So,
\bm{r}
and
\bm{p}
are in a way pinned to each other.

Finally, we have to check that
the second-order diagrams in Fig.
3
(a),(b)
do not contribute to the non-analytic terms in
\Omega
:

\displaystyle\Omega_{a}=\frac{u^{2}}{2}\sum\limits_{\sigma_{i}}\int dz\,

\displaystyle\hskip 30.0pt{\rm Tr}\left\{G_{\sigma_{1}}^{(0)}(0)G_{\sigma_{2}}^{(0)}(z)G_{\sigma_{3}}^{(0)}(0)G_{\sigma_{4}}^{(0)}(-z)\right\},

(45)

\displaystyle\Omega_{b}=\frac{u^{2}}{4}\sum\limits_{\sigma_{i}}\int dz\,

\displaystyle\hskip 30.0pt{\rm Tr}\left\{G_{\sigma_{1}}^{(0)}(z)G_{\sigma_{2}}^{(0)}(-z)G_{\sigma_{3}}^{(0)}(z)G_{\sigma_{4}}^{(0)}(-z)\right\}.

(46)

Here
G_{\sigma}^{(0)}(0)=G_{\sigma}^{(0)}(\tau=-0,\bm{r}=0)

due to the ordering of the field operators within the interaction Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}^{(0)}(0)=\int\frac{d\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\theta(k_{\sigma}(\bm{n}_{\bm{p}})-p)|\sigma,\bm{n}_{\bm{p}}\rangle\langle\sigma,\bm{n}_{\bm{p}}|,

(47)

where
|\sigma,\bm{n}_{\bm{p}}\rangle

are the eigenvectors of the single-particle Hamiltonian, see Eq. (
5
),
and
k_{\sigma}(\bm{n}_{\bm{p}})
is given by Eq. (
9
).

The diagram
\Omega_{a}
has a single particle-hole bubble in it
due to the Green functions
G_{\sigma_{2}}^{(0)}(z)
and
G_{\sigma_{4}}^{(0)}(-z)
,
see Eq. (
45
).
The product of these Green functions
contains weakly oscillating terms
and
\approx 2k_{F}
harmonics.
As in the case of
\Omega^{(1)}
,
the
2k_{F}
harmonics do not produce any non-analyticities.
The weakly oscillating terms originate from the
Landau damping part of the particle-hole bubble
but these terms vanish due to the integral over
\tau
:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\tau}{(v_{F}\tau\pm ir)^{2}}=0.

(48)

The diagram
\Omega_{b}
is more complicated.
Let us consider two matrix products
G_{\sigma_{1}}^{(0)}(z)G_{\sigma_{2}}^{(0)}(-z)

and
G_{\sigma_{3}}^{(0)}(z)G_{\sigma_{4}}^{(0)}(-z)
,
spin traces are not taken here.
As usual, we are after the slowly oscillating terms in Eq. (
46
).
One possibility for this is
the product of the forward scattering contributions coming from

G_{\sigma_{1}}^{(0)}(z)G_{\sigma_{2}}^{(0)}(-z)

and
G_{\sigma_{3}}^{(0)}(z)G_{\sigma_{4}}^{(0)}(-z)
.
From Eq. (
18
)
it is clear that the forward scattering contributions
are non-zero only if
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}

and
\sigma_{3}=\sigma_{4}
,
matrix products of corresponding projectors vanish otherwise.
However, in this case the oscillating factors are canceled exactly
and thus, this contribution is analytic.
Another way to obtain slowly oscillating terms in Eq. (
46
)
is the product of Kohn anomalies
contained in
G_{\sigma_{1}}^{(0)}(z)G_{\sigma_{2}}^{(0)}(-z)

and
G_{\sigma_{3}}^{(0)}(z)G_{\sigma_{4}}^{(0)}(-z)
.
In this case, we have to look at the spin trace in Eq. (
46
)
which is non-zero only if
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{4}

and
\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{3}
.
This condition becomes obvious
if we notice that the product of Kohn anomalies
of
G_{\sigma_{1}}^{(0)}(z)G_{\sigma_{2}}^{(0)}(-z)

and
G_{\sigma_{3}}^{(0)}(z)G_{\sigma_{4}}^{(0)}(-z)

is actually equivalent to the product of the
forward scattering contributions of

G_{\sigma_{2}}^{(0)}(-z)G_{\sigma_{3}}^{(0)}(z)

and
G_{\sigma_{4}}^{(0)}(-z)G_{\sigma_{1}}^{(0)}(z)

which is analytic for the reasons we discussed above.

Hence, only the diagram in Fig.
2
(b)
contains non-analytic terms
and, therefore, Eq. (
44
) describes
the non-analytic corrections to
\Omega

due to arbitrary spin splitting
\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}(\bm{p})

within second-order perturbation theory.

Even though Eq. (
44
) is true in arbitrary number
D
of spatial dimensions,
we give explicit expressions for
D=2
and
D=3
.
For 2DEG the coefficient
L_{2}

is negative, see Eq. (
43
) for
D=2
:

\displaystyle L_{2}=-\frac{g^{2}}{48\pi^{2}v_{F}^{2}}.

(49)

The integral over
d\bm{n}
can be parametrized
by a single angle
\phi\in(0,2\pi]
,
so the non-analytic correction Eq. (
44
)
for 2DEG then reads:

\displaystyle\Omega^{(2)}=-\frac{g^{2}}{24\pi v_{F}^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\left[\left|M_{+-}(\phi)M_{-+}(\phi)\right|^{2}\left|\beta(\phi)-\beta(\phi+\pi)\right|^{3}+\left|M_{++}(\phi)M_{--}(\phi)\right|^{2}\left|\beta(\phi)+\beta(\phi+\pi)\right|^{3}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 95.0pt\left.+2\left(\left|M_{++}(\phi)M_{-+}(\phi)\right|^{2}+\left|M_{--}(\phi)M_{+-}(\phi)\right|^{2}\right)\left|\beta(\phi)\right|^{3}\right],\,\,\,\,D=2.

(50)

Our result Eq. (
50
)
agrees
with previous studies
zak1
;
zak2
;
maslov

and extends them to the case of arbitrary
spin splitting.
Equation (
50
)
together with Eq. (
8
)
for the matrix elements
allows one to find the non-analytic terms in
\Omega

directly from the spin splitting

\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}(\bm{n})
.

The case of
D=3
is marginal because
the non-analytic terms in Eq. (
44
)
are proportional to the fourth power of the
spin splitting.
The non-analyticity itself
comes from the divergence of the
L_{D}
prefactor
at
D=3
, see Eq. (
43
),
which results in an additional logarithm.
This is best seen from the dimensional regularization:

\displaystyle D=3-\delta,\,\,\delta\to+0.

(51)

The dimension
D
enters Eq. (
44
)
in the following form:

\displaystyle L_{D}\Delta^{D+1}=-\frac{g^{2}\Delta^{4}}{192\pi^{3}v_{F}^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}-\ln\Delta\right)+\mathcal{O}(\delta),

(52)

where
\Delta
takes one of the following
values:
\Delta=|\beta(\bm{n})\pm\beta(-\bm{n})|

or

\Delta=|\beta(\bm{n})|
.
Here we expanded the expression at
\delta\to+0
.
The divergent
1/\delta
contribution is actually analytic
and can be represented by
\ln\Lambda

factor,
\Lambda\sim E_{F}
,
which compensates the physical dimension of
\Delta
:

\displaystyle L_{D}\Delta^{D+1}\to\frac{g^{2}}{48\pi^{2}v_{F}^{3}}\frac{\Delta^{4}}{4\pi}\ln\left|\frac{\Delta}{\Lambda}\right|,\,\,\Lambda\sim E_{F}.

(53)

Using the regularization Eq. (
53
),
we find the non-analytic correction to the spin-split 3DEG:

\displaystyle\Omega^{(2)}=\frac{g^{2}}{48\pi^{2}v_{F}^{3}}\int\limits_{S_{2}}\frac{d\bm{n}}{4\pi}\left[\left|M_{+-}(\bm{n})M_{-+}(\bm{n})\right|^{2}\left|\beta(\bm{n})-\beta(-\bm{n})\right|^{4}\ln\left|\frac{\beta(\bm{n})-\beta(-\bm{n})}{\Lambda}\right|\right.

\displaystyle+\left|M_{++}(\bm{n})M_{--}(\bm{n})\right|^{2}\left|\beta(\bm{n})+\beta(-\bm{n})\right|^{4}\ln\left|\frac{\beta(\bm{n})+\beta(-\bm{n})}{\Lambda}\right|

\displaystyle\left.+2\left(\left|M_{++}(\bm{n})M_{-+}(\bm{n})\right|^{2}+\left|M_{--}(\bm{n})M_{+-}(\bm{n})\right|^{2}\right)\left|\beta(\bm{n})\right|^{4}\ln\left|\frac{\beta(\bm{n})}{\Lambda}\right|\right],\,\,\,\,\Lambda\sim E_{F},\,\,\,\,D=3.

(54)

Here, integration over the unit sphere
S_{2}

means
d\bm{n}=\sin\phi_{1}\,d\phi_{1}d\phi_{2}
,

\phi_{1}\in[0,\pi]
,
\phi_{2}\in(0,2\pi]
.
The non-analytic correction is negatively defined for arbitrary spin splitting
due to the logarithms.
In particular,
if
\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}(\bm{n})=\bm{B}
,
we get the well-known result, see Ref.
maslov
:

\displaystyle\Omega^{(2)}=\frac{g^{2}B^{4}}{3\pi^{2}v_{F}^{3}}\ln\left|\frac{2B}{\Lambda}\right|,\,\,\Lambda\sim E_{F}.

(55)

IV.3
Large SO splitting and small magnetic field

Here, we consider the important special case
of arbitrary SO splitting and small magnetic field:

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}(\bm{n}_{\bm{p}})=\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n}_{\bm{p}})+\bm{B},\,\,B\ll\beta_{SO},

(56)

where
\bm{n}_{\bm{p}}=\bm{p}/p
,
p\approx k_{F}
,

\beta_{SO}
is a characteristic
value of the SO splitting at the Fermi surface.
As any SO splitting respects time reversal symmetry,
it has to be an odd vector function of
\bm{n}_{\bm{p}}
:

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{p}})=-\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n}_{\bm{p}}).

(57)

As we consider
B\ll\beta_{SO}
, then
we can expand
\beta(\bm{n})
with respect to
\bm{B}
:

\displaystyle\beta(\bm{n})\approx\beta_{SO}(\bm{n})+\frac{\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n})\cdot\bm{B}}{\beta_{SO}(\bm{n})},

(58)

where
\beta_{SO}(\bm{n})=|\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n})|
.
Together with the symmetry condition Eq. (
57
), we
conclude that only the very first term in Eq. (
44
)
contributes to the non-analyticity with respect to
\bm{B}

due to the following identity:

\displaystyle\beta(\bm{n})-\beta(-\bm{n})\approx 2\frac{\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n})\cdot\bm{B}}{\beta_{SO}(\bm{n})}.

(59)

As we only consider the leading non-analyticity,
we calculate the matrix elements
at
\bm{B}=0
:

\displaystyle M_{\sigma\sigma}(\bm{n})=0,\,\,M_{\sigma-\sigma}(\bm{n})=-1.

(60)

Substituting Eqs. (
59
), (
60
)
in Eq. (
44
), we find
the non-analytic in
\bm{B}
correction to
\Omega
in case of arbitrary SO splitting:

\displaystyle\delta\Omega(\bm{B})=L_{D}\int\limits_{S_{D-1}}d\bm{n}\,\left|2\frac{\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n})\cdot\bm{B}}{\beta_{SO}(\bm{n})}\right|^{D+1},

(61)

where
\delta\Omega(\bm{B})
indicates that
only the non-analytic terms with respect to
\bm{B}
are included.
Thus, we see that the non-analyticity in
magnetic field

\bm{B}
cannot be eliminated even by arbitrary SO splitting,
in contrast to the predictions of Ref.
kirk
.

The elementary processes that are responsible for the non-analyticity given by Eq. (
61
)
are shown in Fig.
4
.
These processes describe the
resonant scattering of a pair of electrons with
the band index
\sigma

and opposite momenta
\pm\bm{k}_{\sigma}

into a pair of electrons in the other band with index
-\sigma

and momenta
\pm\bm{k}_{-\sigma}

that are collinear with momenta of initial electrons
\pm\bm{k}_{\sigma}
.
The momentum transfer in such a scattering processes is close to
2k_{F}
, see Fig.
4
(b).
The scattering with small momentum transfer is forbidden
due to the orthogonality condition Eq. (
6
).
The considered processes are resonant due to the
time reversal symmetry, see Eq. (
57
).
The collinearity condition comes from
the
local nesting

when the momentum transfer between the resonantly
scattering states
also matches small vicinities
around these states.
This matching is satisfied when the outward normals
in the scattering states are collinear
such that the mismatch comes only from different curvatures of the Fermi surface
in the considered points.
The local nesting strongly enhances
corresponding scattering processes
because not only the considered states are in resonance but also small vicinities of states around them.
For example, the Kohn anomaly in the particle-hole bubble
is a result of such a local nesting
for the states scattering with the
2k_{F}
momentum transfer.
The perfect local nesting
corresponds to the Landau damping of the particle-hole excitations
with energy and momentum around zero,
in this case the scattered region in the particle-hole bubble is mapped onto itself.

(a) Fermi surfaces at arbitrary SO splitting, red (blue)

\sigma=+1
(
\sigma=-1
).
The arrows show the resonant scattering processes.
(b) The interaction matrix element

It is also instructive to
write down Eq. (
61
)
for 2DEG
and 3DEG explicitly:

\displaystyle\delta\Omega(\bm{B})=-\frac{g^{2}|B|^{3}}{3\pi v_{F}^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\left|\frac{\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\phi)\cdot\bm{b}}{\beta_{SO}(\phi)}\right|^{3},\,\,D=2,

(62)

\displaystyle\delta\Omega(\bm{B})=\frac{g^{2}B^{4}}{3\pi^{2}v_{F}^{3}}\ln\left|\frac{2B}{\Lambda}\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0pt\times\int\limits_{S_{2}}\frac{d\bm{n}}{4\pi}\left|\frac{\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n})\cdot\bm{b}}{\beta_{SO}(\bm{n})}\right|^{4},\,\,D=3,

(63)

where
\bm{b}=\bm{B}/B
is the unit vector along
\bm{B}
.
We neglected the term
\ln|\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n})\cdot\bm{b}/\beta_{SO}(\bm{n})|
in Eq. (
63
)
because it just slightly renormalizes the regular
B^{4}
term.
Here it is convenient to introduce the angular form-factor
F_{D}(\bm{b})
which depends
on the direction
\bm{b}
of the magnetic field
and on the SO splitting:

\displaystyle F_{D}(\bm{b})=\int\limits_{S_{D-1}}\frac{d\bm{n}}{S_{D-1}}\left|\frac{\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n})\cdot\bm{b}}{\beta_{SO}(\bm{n})}\right|^{D+1},

(64)

where
S_{D-1}
is the area of a unit
(D-1)
-dimensional sphere.

The form-factors
F_{D}(\bm{b})

can only be positive or zero, see Eq. (
64
).
If we demand

F_{D}(\bm{b})=0
for any unit vector
\bm{b}
,
it is equivalent to say that
\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n})\cdot\bm{b}=0

for any
\bm{b}
and also
\beta_{SO}(\bm{n})\neq 0
from Eq. (
56
).
As this is clearly impossible,
we conclude that
F_{D}(\bm{b})

can never vanish for all unit vectors
\bm{b}

even at arbitrary SO splitting
\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n})
.
Therefore,
the non-analyticity with respect to
\bm{B}

cannot be cut by any SO splitting neither in 2DEG nor in 3DEG.

Nevertheless, the SO splitting is important because it
leads to strong anisotropy
of the non-analytic term, see Eq. (
61
),
which is described by the form-factor
F_{D}(\bm{b})
.
If we extrapolate this result to the vicinity of a FQPT,
we conclude that
the direction of spontaneous magnetization
must coincide with the maximum of
F_{D}(\bm{b})
.
In particular,
we predict a first-order Ising FQPT
in electron gas with a general SO splitting which breaks the spin rotational symmetry
down to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

As an example, we consider a 2DEG with Rashba and Dresselhaus SO splittings:

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\left((\alpha_{D}+\alpha_{R})k_{F}\sin\phi,(\alpha_{D}-\alpha_{R})k_{F}\cos\phi,0\right),

(65)

where the
x
and
y
axes correspond to the
[110]
and
[1\overline{1}0]
crystallographic directions,

\alpha_{R}
and
\alpha_{D}
are the Rashba and the Dresselhaus coupling constants, respectively.
The qualitative picture of the SO-split Fermi surfaces
is shown in Fig.
4
(a).
It is more convenient to introduce the following SO couplings:

\displaystyle a_{\pm}\equiv\left(\alpha_{R}\pm\alpha_{D}\right)k_{F}.

(66)

Then we find the angular form-factor
F_{2}(\bm{b})
, see Eqs. (
62
), (
64
):

\displaystyle F_{2}(\bm{b})=\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\phi}{\pi}\frac{\left|a_{+}b_{x}\sin\phi\,-a_{-}b_{y}\cos\phi\,\right|^{3}}{\left(a_{+}^{2}\sin^{2}\phi+a_{-}^{2}\cos^{2}\phi\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}},

(67)

where
\bm{b}=\bm{B}/B
is the unit vector along
\bm{B}
.
We want to identify the directions
\bm{b}^{*}
where

F_{2}(\bm{b}^{*})
is maximal.
It is clear that all such directions have
b^{*}_{z}=0
.
Then
b_{x}^{*}
and
b_{y}^{*}
can be parametrized by a single angle
\Psi
:

\displaystyle b_{x}^{*}=\cos\Psi,\,\,b_{y}^{*}=\sin\Psi.

(68)

The integral in Eq. (
67
)
is quite cumbersome but elementary:

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}F_{2}(\zeta,\Psi)=-\frac{\zeta\cos\left(2\Psi\right)}{\zeta^{2}-1}+\frac{\zeta\cos\Psi}{(\zeta^{2}-1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\zeta^{2}\cos^{2}\Psi-3\sin^{2}\Psi\right)\arctan\left(\sqrt{\zeta^{2}-1}\cos\Psi\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\sin\Psi}{(\zeta^{2}-1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\sin^{2}\Psi-3\zeta^{2}\cos^{2}\Psi\right)\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{\zeta^{2}\cos^{2}\Psi+\sin^{2}\Psi}}{\sqrt{\zeta^{2}-1}\sin\Psi+\zeta}\right),\,\,\zeta\equiv\left|\frac{a_{+}}{a_{-}}\right|=\left|\frac{\alpha_{R}+\alpha_{D}}{\alpha_{R}-\alpha_{D}}\right|>1.

(69)

We added
\zeta
as additional argument of
F_{2}(\bm{b})
for convenience.
Equation (
69
) is true only if
\zeta>1
.
If
\zeta<1
, we use the following identity:

\displaystyle F_{2}(\zeta,\Psi)=F_{2}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta},\frac{\pi}{2}-\Psi\right).

(70)

The extremal values of the
\pi
-periodic function
F_{2}(\zeta,\Psi)

correspond to
\Psi=0
and
\Psi=\pi/2
:

\displaystyle F_{2}(\zeta,0)=\frac{3}{2}\left[\frac{\zeta^{3}\arctan\left(\sqrt{\zeta^{2}-1}\right)}{(\zeta^{2}-1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{\zeta}{\zeta^{2}-1}\right],

(71)

\displaystyle F_{2}\left(\zeta,\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=\frac{3}{2}\left[\frac{\zeta}{\zeta^{2}-1}-\frac{\ln\left(\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^{2}-1}\right)}{(\zeta^{2}-1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right],

(72)

where
\zeta>1
.
It is straightforward to see that at
\zeta>1

the maximum of
F_{2}(\zeta,\Psi)
corresponds to
\Psi=0
.
If
\zeta<1
, we use Eq. (
70
)
and find that the maximum corresponds to
\Psi=\pi/2
.
If these calculations are extrapolated to
the vicinity of the FQPT,
we predict an Ising ferromagnetism
in 2DEG with Rashba and Dresselhaus SO splitting.
The direction of spontaneous magnetization
here coincides
with the spin quantization axis
of the states that are maximally split by the SO coupling,
namely, along
[110]
(
[1\overline{1}0]
)
if
\alpha_{R}
and
\alpha_{D}
have the same (opposite) signs.
The case
\zeta=1
is realized when either Rashba or Dresselhaus SO splitting is zero,
in this case all in-plane directions are equivalent
which corresponds to the easy-plane ferromagnet, the result predicted in Refs.
zak1
;
zak2
.

V
Strong interaction regime

In this section we consider the electron gas
with strong electron-electron interaction, i.e.

g\gg 1
or
k_{F}a_{B}\ll 1
, see Eq. (
41
).
In this section we concentrate
on the case of SO splitting with
small magnetic field
B\ll\beta
,
where
\beta
is a characteristic
SO splitting at the Fermi surface.
In this case
we already know that only the scattering processes that
are schematically shown in Fig.
4
(b)
contribute to the non-analytic correction
in the regime of weak interaction.
In preceding sections we already discussed
that the scattering processes are nearly collinear in order
to support the
local nesting
,
see the paragraph after Eq. (
61
).
Here we consider the effective interaction Hamiltonian
whose matrix elements are only given by these
processes, see Fig.
4
(b).
Note that the standard backscattering
within the same band is forbidden
due to Eq. (
60
).
The momentum transfer in the diagram in Fig.
4
(b) is about
2k_{F}
,
so we can use the approximation of contact interaction, see Eq. (
32
).
The only difference from the previous sections is that here
we consider the regime of strong electron-electron interactions
g\gg 1
,
i.e. we have to account for the processes in Fig.
4
(b)
fully self-consistently.

There is one more reason why we consider the
case of finite SO splitting here.
As we found earlier, the SO splitting
results in strong anisotropy of the non-analytic terms, see Eq. (
61
).
For the case of general SO splitting
which breaks the spin rotational symmetry
down to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
the magnetic order parameter is Ising,
i.e. all fluctuation modes of the order parameter are gapped
and can be neglected even close to the FQPT.
At the same time, the resonant scattering processes
shown in Fig.
4

result in the negative non-analytic terms
in
\Omega(\bm{B})
and, thus,
destabilize the FQCP.
We show that the non-analyticity is enhanced parametrically in the limit of strong interaction
g\gg 1
.
This effect can be measured experimentally
from the strongly non-analytic magnetic field dependence of the spin susceptibility close to the FQPT.

V.1
Self-consistent Born approximation

Next, we apply the strategy that
we used before in Ref.
miserev

where we predicted non-Fermi-liquid phases
(they correspond to certain magnetic quantum critical points)
in strongly interacting Fermi gases
with multiple Fermi surfaces.
In fact, the case that we consider here
is very similar to a special case of Ref.
miserev
.
One difference here is that the Fermi surfaces are not spherical due to the anisotropic spin splitting.
Another difference here is that we analyze the stability
of a FQCP via considering the non-analytic terms in the thermodynamic potential.

,
because there is no explicit dependence on
\bm{n}
in Eq. (
87
)
if
\Delta(\bm{n})=0
:

\displaystyle g_{\sigma}(\tau,x,\bm{n})=g_{\sigma}(\tau,x).

(88)

Second, we observe
that Eq. (
87
)
is free from any energy or momentum scales
which results in the scale invariance
(conformal symmetry)
of Eq. (
87
)
with respect to independent
reparametrization of time and coordinate:

\displaystyle\tau\to\ell_{1}\tau,\,\,x\to\ell_{2}x,

(89)

where
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
are arbitrary real numbers.
Applying this reparametrization to Eq. (
87
), one can show
that
the Green function with rescaled
coordinates
g_{\sigma}(\ell_{1}\tau,\ell_{2}x)

is just proportional to
g_{\sigma}(\tau,x)
:

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0ptg_{\sigma}(\ell_{1}\tau,\ell_{2}x)=\frac{g_{\sigma}(\tau,x)}{|\ell_{1}|^{2d_{1}}|\ell_{2}|^{2d_{2}}},\,\,d_{1}=\frac{1}{4},\,\,d_{2}=\frac{3-D}{8},

(90)

where
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are the temporal and
spatial scaling dimensions.
Equation (
90
) implies
the power-law scaling of the
Green function
g_{\sigma}(\tau,x)
with respect to time and coordinate:

\displaystyle g_{\sigma}(\tau,x)\propto\frac{1}{|\tau|^{2d_{1}}}\frac{1}{|x|^{2d_{2}}}.

(91)

Thus, we see that
we can separate the variables
and represent Eq. (
87
)
at
\Delta(\bm{n})=0

as the product of two independent Dyson equations:

\displaystyle g_{\sigma}(\tau,x)=\mathfrak{g}(\tau)\gamma_{\sigma}(x),

(92)

\displaystyle\delta(\tau)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau^{\prime}\,\mathfrak{g}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\mathfrak{g}(-\tau^{\prime})\mathfrak{g}^{2}(\tau^{\prime}),

(93)

\displaystyle\delta(x)=u^{2}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx^{\prime}\,\gamma_{\sigma}(x-x^{\prime})\frac{\gamma_{\sigma}(-x^{\prime})\gamma^{2}_{-\sigma}(x^{\prime})}{|\lambda_{F}x^{\prime}|^{D-1}}.

(94)

Equation (
93
) is the well-known
Dyson equation of the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model

ye
;
parcollet
;
sachdev

which can be applied to describe
the strange metal phase in cuprates
sachdev
;
chowdhury
.
In Ref.
miserev

we found that this model can emerge
in strongly interacting
electron systems
with multiple Fermi surfaces.
The zero temperature solution of this equation is the following:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(\tau)=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{1}{4}}}\frac{{\rm sgn}(\tau)}{|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

(95)

In fact, this solution can be easily
generalized to the case of finite temperature
T
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(\tau)=\frac{{\rm sgn}(\tau)}{(4\pi)^{\frac{1}{4}}}\left|\frac{\pi T}{\sin(\pi T\tau)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}},

(96)

where
\tau\in(-1/T,1/T)
.
The factor
{\rm sgn}(\tau)

is needed here to satisfy
the antiperiodicity of the electron Green function:

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}\left(\tau+\frac{1}{T},\bm{r}\right)=-G_{\sigma}(\tau,\bm{r}).

(97)

Equation (
94
) is
similar to the SYK equation (
93
)
and can be solved in terms of the power-law functions:

\displaystyle\gamma_{\sigma}(x)=C_{D}\frac{i\,{\rm sgn}(x)}{|x|^{2d_{2}}},\,\,d_{2}=\frac{3-D}{8},

(98)

\displaystyle C_{D}=\left[\frac{D+1}{8\pi u^{2}}\lambda_{F}^{D-1}\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{3-D}{4}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}.

(99)

Here we assume that the proportionality coefficient
C_{D}

is the same for both spin-split bands, so
\gamma_{\sigma}(x)
is independent of
\sigma
in this case.
This assumption is reasonable because the spin splitting is much smaller than the Fermi energy.

Substituting Eqs. (
96
) and (
98
)
into Eq. (
76
), we find the asymptotic form of the SCBA Green function:

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}(\tau,\bm{r})\approx\frac{C_{D}}{\lambda_{F}^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\frac{\mathfrak{g}(\tau)}{r^{\frac{D+1}{4}}}\left[e^{i\left(k_{\sigma}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r-\vartheta+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)}|\sigma,\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}\rangle\langle\sigma,\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}|\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt+\left.e^{-i\left(k_{\sigma}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r-\vartheta+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)}|\sigma,-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}\rangle\langle\sigma,-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}|\right],

(100)

where the
\pi/2
phase is due to the
i\,{\rm sgn}(x)
phase term in Eq. (
98
).
Equation (
100
) has a certain phase freedom upon a small translation

r\to r+a
,
a\sim\lambda_{F}
.
Such a translation does not change the asymptotics of the power-law tail
because
r\gg\lambda_{F}

but it results in the phase shift
in the oscillatory factors.
In other words,
the phase cannot be determined self-consistently
from the long-range infrared limit.
Here we derive it from the particle-hole symmetry
which is exact close to the Fermi surface,
so we
conclude that the phase has to be just
-\vartheta
.
The true asymptotics of the
particle-hole symmetric Matsubara Green function
with positively defined spectral function
then reads:

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}(\tau,\bm{r})\approx\frac{C_{D}}{\lambda_{F}^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\frac{\mathfrak{g}(\tau)}{r^{\frac{D+1}{4}}}\left[e^{i\left(k_{\sigma}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r-\vartheta\right)}|\sigma,\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}\rangle\langle\sigma,\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}|\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt+\left.e^{-i\left(k_{\sigma}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r-\vartheta\right)}|\sigma,-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}\rangle\langle\sigma,-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}|\right].

(101)

The numerical coefficient
C_{D}>0

is given by Eq. (
99
).

Here we would like to comment on the case
D=3

because
C_{D}
vanishes in this case.
In order to resolve this issue,
we use the dimensional regularization:

\displaystyle C_{D}\approx\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{F}}{4u}}\delta^{\frac{1}{4}},\,\,D=3-\delta,\,\,\delta\to+0.

(102)

Substituting it into Eq. (
98
)
and forgetting about the phase factor (see the paragraph above),
we find:

\displaystyle\gamma_{\sigma}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{F}}{4u}}\left(\frac{|x|^{\delta}}{\delta}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\approx\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{F}}{4u}}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}+\ln|x|\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

(103)

Here
1/\delta
comes from the ultraviolet
scale
x\sim\lambda_{F}
,
so we can write
1/\delta=\ln p_{0}
,
where
p_{0}\sim k_{F}

compensates the dimensionality of
x
:

\displaystyle\gamma_{\sigma}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{F}}{4u}}\frac{1}{\left(\ln|p_{0}x|\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}},\,\,p_{0}\sim k_{F}.

(104)

As a summary, we provide the Matsubara Green function of
strongly interacting 2D and 3D electron gases
with arbitrary SO splitting:

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}(\tau,\bm{r})\approx\left(\frac{3(\sqrt{2}-1)m^{2}k_{F}}{\pi g^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\frac{e^{i\left(k_{\sigma}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)}|\sigma,\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}\rangle\langle\sigma,\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}|+e^{-i\left(k_{\sigma}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)}|\sigma,-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}\rangle\langle\sigma,-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}|}{4\pi r^{\frac{3}{4}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 280.0pt\times\left|\frac{\pi T}{\sin(\pi T\tau)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm sgn}(\tau),\,\,D=2,

(105)

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}(\tau,\bm{r})\approx\frac{k_{F}}{4\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{4\pi m^{2}}{g^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\frac{e^{i\left(k_{\sigma}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)}|\sigma,\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}\rangle\langle\sigma,\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}|+e^{-i\left(k_{\sigma}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)}|\sigma,-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}\rangle\langle\sigma,-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}|}{r\left|\ln(p_{0}r)\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 280.0pt\times\left|\frac{\pi T}{\sin(\pi T\tau)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm sgn}(\tau),\,\,D=3,

(106)

where
g
is the dimensionless interaction coupling constant, see Eq. (
41
).
We also provide the Fourier transforms:

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}(i\omega,\delta p,\bm{n})=\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\frac{(\sqrt{2}-1)^{\frac{3}{4}}\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)}{2\pi\sqrt{2gE_{F}T}}

\displaystyle\hskip 60.0pt\times\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\delta p}\right|^{\frac{3}{4}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\omega}{2\pi T}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\omega}{2\pi T}+\frac{3}{4}\right)},\,\,D=2,

(107)

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}(i\omega,\delta p,\bm{n})=\frac{\pi^{\frac{1}{4}}}{16\sqrt{gE_{F}T}}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt\times\frac{k_{F}}{|\delta p|}\left(\ln\left|\frac{p_{0}}{\delta p}\right|\right)^{-\frac{5}{4}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\omega}{2\pi T}+\frac{1}{4}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\omega}{2\pi T}+\frac{3}{4}\right)},\,\,D=3,

(108)

where
\omega=\pi T(2n+1)
is the Matsubara frequency, with
n
being an integer,
and, again,
p_{0}\sim k_{F}
.

These calculations
have been performed within the SCBA.
However, the emergent conformal symmetry
makes this approximation exact,
see Ref.
miserev
for details.
In particular,
the interaction vertex correction and the dressing of the
effective interaction
only lead to the renormalization of the interaction parameter
g
,
while the scaling dimensions
d_{1}
and
d_{2}

remain the same.
In this paper we consider
g
as a phenomenological parameter,
so its renormalizations are not important for us.
The only condition that is implied here is that

g\gg 1
close to the FQPT, so we can apply the limit of strong interaction.

V.4
Solutions at finite
\bm{B}

If
\bm{B}
is finite, then Eq. (
87
)
contains an oscillatory term
making the self-energy irrelevant at
large distance
x\gg 1/\Delta(\bm{n})
,
so we expect Fermi liquid
behavior
at such distances.
At small distances
x\ll 1/\Delta(\bm{n})

the oscillatory term is not important
and the solutions, given by Eqs. (
105
)–(
106
),
are still valid.
Following this reasoning,
the Fourier transforms
Eqs. (
107
)–(
108
)
are valid if
\delta p\gg\Delta(\bm{n})
,
where
\bm{n}
here labels the direction of
\bm{p}
.

Another important limitation of Eqs. (
105
)–(
108
)
comes from the assumption that the self-energy

\Sigma_{\sigma}(i\omega,\delta p,\bm{n})

dominates over
the single-particle terms
i\omega-v_{F}\delta p

which we neglected in Eq. (
85
).
All in all,
the non-Fermi liquid regime
corresponds to the following constrained region:

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\Delta(\bm{n})\ll\delta p\ll k_{F},\,\,w(\delta p)\ll\omega\ll W(\delta p),

(109)

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0ptw(\delta p)=\frac{E_{F}}{g}\left|\frac{\delta p}{k_{F}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}},\,W(\delta p)=gE_{F}\left|\frac{\delta p}{k_{F}}\right|^{\frac{3}{2}},\,D=2,

(110)

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0ptw(\delta p)=\frac{E_{F}}{g}\left(\ln\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\delta p}\right|\right)^{-\frac{5}{2}},

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0ptW(\delta p)=gE_{F}\left(\frac{\delta p}{k_{F}}\right)^{2}\left(\ln\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\delta p}\right|\right)^{\frac{5}{2}},\,D=3,

(111)

where we used
p_{0}\approx k_{F}
for
D=3
.
Here, it is required that
W(\delta p)\gg w(\delta p)

which further constrains
\delta p
:

\displaystyle\delta p\gg\delta p^{*},

(112)

\displaystyle\delta p^{*}=\frac{k_{F}}{g^{2}},\,D=2,

(113)

\displaystyle\delta p^{*}=\frac{k_{F}}{g\left(\ln g\right)^{\frac{5}{2}}},\,D=3.

(114)

As
g\gg 1
close to the FQPT,
the scale
\delta p^{*}
is very small.
Here we see that at the very deep infrared limit

\delta p\lesssim\delta p^{*}

the Fermi liquid is restored.
This also means that at
\Delta(\bm{n})\ll\delta p^{*}

the non-analyticity can be correctly estimated by the perturbative approach that we considered
in the previous section.
The most interesting regime corresponds to

\Delta(\bm{n})\gg\delta p^{*}

where the dominant contribution to the thermodynamic potential comes from the
quantum states that are strongly affected by the interaction.
In the next section we calculate the thermodynamic potential

\Omega

in this regime:

\displaystyle\Delta(\bm{n})\gg\delta p^{*}.

(115)

VI
Thermodynamic potential

In the preceding section
we calculated the electron Green function
of 2DEG and 3DEG with arbitrary SO splitting in
the strongly interacting regime
g\gg 1
,
see Eqs. (
105
)–(
108
),
and we also
defined the crossover region with
the Fermi liquid,
see Eqs. (
109
) and (
115
).
The Fermi liquid contribution to the
non-analyticity is calculated in Sec. IV.
Here we address the contribution
coming from the strongly correlated region
of the phase space, see Eqs. (
109
) and (
115
).

In order to calculate the thermodynamic potential
\Omega
,
we consider the following energy functional
\Omega(\Sigma,G)
:

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\Omega(\Sigma,G)=-{\rm Tr}\ln\left(G_{0}^{-1}-\Sigma\right)-{\rm Tr}\left(\Sigma G\right)-\Phi(G),

(116)

where
{\rm Tr}
is the trace over all indexes and

\Phi(G)
the Luttinger-Ward functional.
The saddle point of
\Omega(\Sigma,G)
yields
the Dyson equation and the equation for the electron self-energy:

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\delta\Omega(\Sigma,G)}{\delta\Sigma_{\sigma}}=\left(G_{0}^{-1}-\Sigma_{\sigma}\right)^{-1}-G_{\sigma},

(117)

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\delta\Omega(\Sigma,G)}{\delta G_{\sigma}}=-\Sigma_{\sigma}-\frac{\delta\Phi(G)}{\delta G_{\sigma}}.

(118)

Comparing Eq. (
74
) with Eq. (
118
), we reconstruct the Luttinger-Ward functional:

\displaystyle\Phi(G)=\frac{u^{2}}{2}\int dz\,\left[{\rm Tr}\left\{G_{+}(z)G_{-}(-z)\right\}\right]^{2}.

(119)

The thermodynamic potential
\Omega
is given by the saddle-point value of the
functional
\Omega(\Sigma,G)
:

\displaystyle\Omega={\rm Tr}\ln G+3\Phi(G),

(120)

where
G
is the Matsubara Green function.
Here we used that
\Sigma
satisfies Eq. (
74
), so that
-{\rm Tr}(\Sigma G)=4\Phi(G)
.

VI.1
The logarithmic term

Let us first calculate
{\rm Tr}\ln G
:

\displaystyle\hskip-18.0pt\Omega_{1}\equiv{\rm Tr}\ln G=\sum\limits_{\sigma}T\sum\limits_{\omega}\int\frac{d\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\ln\left(G_{\sigma}(i\omega,\delta p)\right).

(121)

Here we assume that Eq. (
115
) is satisfied,
so the quantum critical region
is given by the constraints Eqs. (
109
)–(
111
).

We start from the low temperature regime:

\displaystyle T\ll W(\Delta(\bm{n})).

(122)

In this case there are many Matsubara frequencies
in the window
\omega\in(w(\Delta(\bm{n})),W(\Delta(\bm{n}))
,
so we can approximate the sum over frequencies by an integral.
For the same reason, we can also expand the gamma functions in Eqs. (
107
)–(
108
)
with respect to the large ratio
\omega/(2\pi T)

such that
\ln G_{\sigma}(i\omega,\delta p)

takes the following form:

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\ln G_{\sigma}(i\omega,\delta p)=\frac{3}{4}\ln\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\delta p}\right|-\frac{1}{2}\ln|\omega|+{\rm const},\,D=2,

(123)

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\ln G_{\sigma}(i\omega,\delta p)=\ln\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\delta p}\right|-\frac{5}{4}\ln\ln\left|\frac{p_{0}}{\delta p}\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 90.0pt-\frac{1}{2}\ln|\omega|+{\rm const},\,D=3.

(124)

The sum over
\sigma
in Eq. (
121
) yields just a factor of
2
.
As
\ln G_{\sigma}(i\omega,\delta p)
is an even function of
\omega
and

\delta p
, we
can integrate over the positive intervals,
i.e.
\delta p\in(\Delta(\bm{n}),k_{F})

and
\omega\in(0,W(\delta p))
.
Note that we extend the integration over
\omega

all the way to zero frequency
because
W(\delta p)\gg T
and
W(\delta p)\gg w(\delta p)
.
This allows us to represent Eq. (
121
) in the following form:

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}(\bm{B})=2\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}2\int\limits_{\Delta(\phi)}^{k_{F}}\frac{k_{F}\,d\delta p}{2\pi}2\int\limits_{0}^{W(\delta p)}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\hskip 60.0pt\times\left(\frac{3}{4}\ln\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\delta p}\right|-\frac{1}{2}\ln|\omega|\right),\,D=2,

(125)

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}(\bm{B})=2\int\frac{d\bm{n}}{4\pi}2\int\limits_{\Delta(\bm{n})}^{k_{F}}\frac{k_{F}^{2}\,d\delta p}{2\pi^{2}}2\int\limits_{0}^{W(\delta p)}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt\times\left(\ln\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\delta p}\right|-\frac{5}{4}\ln\ln\left|\frac{p_{0}}{\delta p}\right|-\frac{1}{2}\ln|\omega|\right),\,D=3.

(126)

Next, we take the integral over
\omega

with logarithmic accuracy.
In particular, we neglect the
\ln\ln|\delta p|
term in the 3D case compared to the
\ln|\delta p|
term:

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}(\bm{B})=\frac{2k_{F}^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\Delta(\phi)}^{k_{F}}\frac{d\delta p}{k_{F}}W(\delta p)

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt\times\left(\frac{3}{4}\ln\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\delta p}\right|-\frac{1}{2}\ln|W(\delta p)|\right),\,D=2,

(127)

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}(\bm{B})=\frac{2k_{F}^{3}}{\pi^{3}}\int\frac{d\bm{n}}{4\pi}\int\limits_{\Delta(\bm{n})}^{k_{F}}\frac{d\delta p}{k_{F}}W(\delta p)

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt\times\left(\ln\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\delta p}\right|-\frac{1}{2}\ln|W(\delta p)|\right),\,D=3.

(128)

Then substituting
W(\delta p)
, see Eqs. (
110
)–(
111
),
we find:

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}(\bm{B})=\frac{3k_{F}^{2}gE_{F}}{\pi^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\hskip 50.0pt\times\int\limits_{\Delta(\phi)}^{k_{F}}\frac{d\delta p}{k_{F}}\left|\frac{\delta p}{k_{F}}\right|^{\frac{3}{2}}\ln\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\delta p}\right|,\,D=2,

(129)

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}(\bm{B})=\frac{4k_{F}^{3}gE_{F}}{\pi^{3}}\int\frac{d\bm{n}}{4\pi}

\displaystyle\hskip 50.0pt\times\int\limits_{\Delta(\bm{n})}^{k_{F}}\frac{d\delta p}{k_{F}}\left|\frac{\delta p}{k_{F}}\right|^{2}\ln\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\delta p}\right|^{\frac{7}{2}},\,D=3.

(130)

Calculating the integrals over
\delta p

with the logarithmic accuracy
and subtracting the part which is independent of
\Delta(\bm{n})
,
we find the non-analytic contribution to the thermodynamic potential:

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}(\bm{B})=-\frac{6k_{F}^{2}gE_{F}}{5\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt\times\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\left|\frac{\Delta(\phi)}{k_{F}}\right|^{\frac{5}{2}}\ln\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\Delta(\phi)}\right|,\,D=2,

(131)

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}(\bm{B})=-\frac{4k_{F}^{3}gE_{F}}{3\pi^{3}}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt\times\int\frac{d\bm{n}}{4\pi}\left|\frac{\Delta(\bm{n})}{k_{F}}\right|^{3}\left(\ln\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\Delta(\bm{n})}\right|\right)^{\frac{7}{2}},\,D=3.

(132)

Finally, we have to calculate the Luttinger-Ward
contribution to
\Omega
.

VI.2
Luttinger-Ward contribution

Here we calculate the second part of the thermodynamic potential, see Eq. (
120
):

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\Omega_{2}(\bm{B})=3\Phi(G)=\frac{3u^{2}}{2}\!\!\int dz\left[{\rm Tr}\left\{G_{+}(z)G_{-}(-z)\right\}\right]^{2}.

(133)

In this case it is more convenient to use
the real space representation of the
Green function,
see Eqs. (
105
)–(
106
).
Here we will show that
\Omega_{2}(\bm{B})

yields a subleading correction compared to
\Omega_{1}(\bm{B})
.

Using Eqs. (
105
)–(
106
),
we find:

\displaystyle\left[{\rm Tr}\left\{G_{+}(z)G_{-}(-z)\right\}\right]^{2}\approx\frac{3(\sqrt{2}-1)m^{2}k_{F}}{2^{7}\pi^{5}g^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 30.0pt\times\frac{e^{ir\Delta(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})}}{r^{3}}\left|\frac{\pi T}{\sin(\pi T\tau)}\right|^{2},\,D=2,

(134)

\displaystyle\left[{\rm Tr}\left\{G_{+}(z)G_{-}(-z)\right\}\right]^{2}\approx\frac{m^{2}k_{F}^{4}}{2^{5}\pi^{7}g^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 30.0pt\times\frac{e^{ir\Delta(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})}}{r^{4}\ln(p_{0}r)}\left|\frac{\pi T}{\sin(\pi T\tau)}\right|^{2},\,D=3,

(135)

First, we have to translate the
conditions given by Eqs. (
109
)–(
111
)
to constraints on
\tau
and
r
.
We do it via the correspondence
\omega\sim 1/\tau
,
\delta p\sim 1/r
.
Thus, in our case the integration region is
defined by the following constraints:

\displaystyle\tau\gg\overline{\tau}(r)=\frac{1}{W(r^{-1})},\,\,r\ll\frac{1}{\Delta(\bm{n})}.

(136)

Here we show only the most important conditions.
As in the previous case, we consider small
temperatures
T\ll W(\Delta(\bm{n}))
,
so we can actually use the
T=0
approximation.
First, we integrate over
\tau
:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\overline{\tau}(r)}^{\frac{1}{T}-\overline{\tau}(r)}d\tau\,\left|\frac{\pi T}{\sin(\pi T\tau)}\right|^{2}\approx 2\int\limits_{\overline{\tau}(r)}^{\infty}\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{2}}=\frac{2}{\overline{\tau}(r)}.

(137)

Substituting this in
\Omega_{2}(\bm{B})
,
we find:

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}(\bm{B})=\frac{9(\sqrt{2}-1)}{16\pi^{2}}gE_{F}k_{F}^{2}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0pt\times\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{k_{F}dr}{(k_{F}r)^{\frac{7}{2}}}e^{ir\Delta(\phi)},\,D=2,

(138)

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}(\bm{B})=\frac{3}{2\pi^{2}}gE_{F}k_{F}^{3}\int\frac{d\bm{n}}{4\pi}

\displaystyle\hskip 30.0pt\times\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{k_{F}dr}{(k_{F}r)^{4}}\left(\ln(k_{F}r)\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{ir\Delta(\bm{n})},\,D=3.

(139)

We substituted
p_{0}\approx k_{F}
for
D=3
case.
Note that here we do not even have to use the condition

r\ll 1/\Delta(\bm{n})

because it is effectively satisfied due to the
oscillatory term
e^{ir\Delta(\bm{n})}
.
The integral over
k_{F}r
in Eq. (
138
) is calculated via Eq. (
28
).
As
\Delta(\phi+\pi)=-\Delta(\phi)
, see Eq. (
83
),
then only the real part does not vanish after the integration over
\phi
.
The integral in Eq. (
139
)
can be calculated similarly.
Here we calculate it with logarithmic accuracy:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{k_{F}dr}{(k_{F}r)^{4+\delta}}\left(\ln(k_{F}r)\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{ir\Delta(\bm{n})}

\displaystyle\approx\left(\ln\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\Delta(\bm{n})}\right|\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\left|\frac{\Delta(\bm{n})}{k_{F}}\right|^{3+\delta}\frac{i\,{\rm sgn}(\Delta(\bm{n}))+\frac{\pi}{2}\delta}{6\delta},

(140)

where we introduced some small
\delta\to 0
.
As
\Delta(-\bm{n})=-\Delta(\bm{n})
,
the divergent
1/\delta
part cancels out
after the integration over
\bm{n}
,
so the physical contribution is finite.
In conclusion, we find
\Omega_{2}(\bm{B})
:

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}(\bm{B})=\frac{3(\sqrt{2}-1)}{5(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}gE_{F}k_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0pt\times\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\left|\frac{\Delta(\phi)}{k_{F}}\right|^{\frac{5}{2}},\,D=2,

(141)

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}(\bm{B})=\frac{gE_{F}k_{F}^{3}}{8\pi}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt\times\int\frac{d\bm{n}}{4\pi}\left|\frac{\Delta(\bm{n})}{k_{F}}\right|^{3}\left(\ln\left|\frac{k_{F}}{\Delta(\bm{n})}\right|\right)^{\frac{3}{2}},\,D=3.

(142)

Indeed, compared to
\Omega_{1}(\bm{B})
, see Eqs. (
131
) and (
132
),

\Omega_{2}(\bm{B})
is only subleading,
so we can neglect it.

Summing over, the leading non-analyticity
in
\Omega
in the strongly interacting regime

g\gg 1
close to FQPT
is given by the
\Omega_{1}
contribution, see Eqs. (
131
) and (
132

Here we represent the answer using the
angular form-factors

\overline{F}_{D}(\bm{b})

that are different from the form-factors

F_{D}(\bm{b})
that appear in the weak coupling regime, see Eq. (
64

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\delta\Omega(\bm{B})=\frac{6k_{F}^{2}gE_{F}}{5\pi^{2}}\left|\frac{2B}{E_{F}}\right|^{\frac{5}{2}}\ln\left|\frac{2B}{E_{F}}\right|\overline{F}_{2}(\bm{b}),\,D=2,

(143)

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\delta\Omega(\bm{B})=-\frac{32k_{F}^{3}g|B|^{3}}{3\pi^{3}E_{F}^{2}}\left|\ln\left|\frac{E_{F}}{2B}\right|\right|^{\frac{7}{2}}\overline{F}_{3}(\bm{b}),\,D=3,

(144)

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\overline{F}_{D}(\bm{b})=\int\limits_{S_{D-1}}\frac{d\bm{n}}{S_{D-1}}\left|\frac{\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n})\cdot\bm{b}}{\beta_{SO}(\bm{n})}\right|^{\frac{D+3}{2}},

(145)

where
\bm{b}=\bm{B}/B
.
First, we notice that the non-analytic terms are negative
and that they are parametrically larger
than the Fermi liquid non-analyticity,
see Eqs. (
62

63

The angular form-factor
\overline{F}_{D}(\bm{b})
due to the SO splitting
is different from the one that we considered

64
),
but it still results in the same physical effect:
if the SO splitting breaks the spin rotational symmetry down
to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
then the ferromagnetic ground state is Ising
and the magnetization axis is defined by the
direction where the SO splitting
and, thus,
\overline{F}_{D}(\bm{b})
are maximal.

Equations (
143
) and (
144

are valid at very low temperatures
when
T\ll W(\Delta(\bm{n}))
.
This condition can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle B\gg B^{*}(T),

(146)

\displaystyle B^{*}(T)=E_{F}\left|\frac{T}{gE_{F}}\right|^{\frac{2}{3}},\,\,D=2,

(147)

\displaystyle B^{*}(T)=\frac{E_{F}}{\left(\ln|gE_{F}/T|\right)^{\frac{5}{4}}}\sqrt{\frac{T}{gE_{F}}},\,\,D=3.

(148)

If

\omega\sim B

and

The regime

144

The regime

and the non-Fermi-liquid regimes.

In this section we treated the resonant backscattering processes
shown in Fig.

non-perturbatively
and found that
the non-analytic terms in
\Omega

remain negative close to the FQPT and
that they
are strongly enhanced in magnitude compared to the regime of weak interaction.
This means
that the FQCP separating paramagnetic and ferromagnetic metals
is intrinsically unstable even in the presence of arbitrarily complex SO splitting.

VII
Spin susceptibility far away and close to FQPT

The non-analytic corrections destabilizing
the FQCP separating ferromagnetic and paramagnetic phases
can be measured experimentally via the
magnetic field dependence of the spin susceptibility
\chi_{ij}(\bm{B})

in the paramagnetic phase:

\displaystyle\chi_{ij}(\bm{B})=-\frac{\partial^{2}\Omega(\bm{B})}{\partial B_{i}\partial B_{j}}.

(149)

Deep inside the paramagnetic phase
where
g\ll 1
or
\lambda_{F}\ll a_{B}
, see Eq. (
41
),
the non-analytic correction
is given by Eqs. (
62
)–(
63
),
so the corresponding non-analytic corrections to the
spin susceptibility are the following:

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\delta\chi_{ij}(\bm{B})=\frac{2g^{2}|B|}{\pi v_{F}^{2}}\kappa^{(2)}_{ij}(\bm{b}),\,\,D=2,

(150)

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\delta\chi_{ij}(\bm{B})=-\frac{4g^{2}B^{2}}{\pi^{2}v_{F}^{3}}\ln\left|\frac{2B}{\Lambda}\right|\kappa^{(3)}_{ij}(\bm{b}),\,\,D=3,

(151)

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\kappa^{(D)}_{ij}(\bm{b})

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt=\int\limits_{S_{D-1}}\frac{d\bm{n}}{S_{D-1}}\left|\frac{\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n})\cdot\bm{b}}{\beta_{SO}(\bm{n})}\right|^{D-1}\frac{\beta_{SO}^{i}(\bm{n})\beta_{SO}^{j}(\bm{n})}{\beta^{2}_{SO}(\bm{n})},

(152)

where
\Lambda\sim E_{F}
,

\beta_{SO}^{i}(\bm{n})

is the
i^{{\rm th}}
component of the vector
\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n})
.
These results are valid for
B\gg T
.
In the opposite regime
B\ll T
,
the non-analyticity is regularized by the temperature.
An important feature here
is the non-trivial angular dependence of the
spin susceptibility
on
\bm{b}
due to the SO splitting, see the angular tensor
\kappa^{(D)}_{ij}(\bm{b})
.
Measuring
\kappa^{(D)}_{ij}(\bm{b})

can resolve the
angular dependence of the SO splitting.
However, it is not an easy task to measure
the non-analytic corrections to the spin susceptibility
in the weak coupling regime
because they are proportional to
g^{2}
,
where
g\ll 1
.

It is much more promising
to measure the non-analyticities in the spin susceptibility
when the paramagnetic phase is close to the FQPT,
such that
g\gg 1
and we can use the results of Sec. VI,
see Eqs. (
143
)–(
144

\displaystyle\delta\chi_{ij}(\bm{B})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{36\sqrt{2}}{\pi^{2}}mg\ln\left|\frac{E_{F}}{2B}\right|\sqrt{\frac{|B|}{E_{F}}}\,\overline{\kappa}_{ij}^{(2)}(\bm{b}),\,\,D=2,

(153)

\displaystyle\delta\chi_{ij}(\bm{B})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{256mg}{\pi^{3}v_{F}}\left|\ln\left(\frac{E_{F}}{2B}\right)\right|^{\frac{7}{2}}|B|\,\overline{\kappa}_{ij}^{(3)}(\bm{b}),\,\,D=3,

(154)

\displaystyle\overline{\kappa}^{(D)}_{ij}(\bm{b})

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt=\int\limits_{S_{D-1}}\frac{d\bm{n}}{S_{D-1}}\left|\frac{\mbox{\boldmath{$\beta$}}_{SO}(\bm{n})\cdot\bm{b}}{\beta_{SO}(\bm{n})}\right|^{\frac{D-1}{2}}\frac{\beta_{SO}^{i}(\bm{n})\beta_{SO}^{j}(\bm{n})}{\beta^{2}_{SO}(\bm{n})}.

(155)

These equations are valid if
B\gg B^{*}(T)
,
where
B^{*}(T)
is given by Eqs. (
147
) and (
148
).
If
B\ll B^{*}(T)
,
then the electron self-energy is irrelevant
on the scale
\omega\sim B

and the non-analyticity is given by
Eqs. (
150
) and (
151
).
Here we see that at
B\gg B^{*}(T)

the non-analytic corrections to the susceptibility
are strongly enhanced.
In particular,
\delta\chi_{ij}(\bm{B})\propto\sqrt{|B|}

in a 2DEG and
\delta\chi_{ij}(\bm{B})\propto|B|
in a 3DEG,
which is much easier to detect experimentally
than the much weaker non-analyticities

150
) and (
151

stano_RKKY

VIII
Conclusions

In this paper we revisited
FQPT in strongly interacting clean
2DEG and 3DEG with arbitrary SO splitting.
First of all, we calculated
the non-analytic corrections to the
thermodynamic potential
\Omega

with respect to arbitrary spin splitting
in the limit of weak electron-electron interaction.
So far, this has been done in the literature only for very limited
number of special cases

maslov
;
zak1
;
zak2
;
kirk
.
Here we generalized the calculation for arbitrary
spin splitting and arbitrary spatial dimension
D>1
, see Eq. (
44
).
The most important outcome of this calculation
is that even arbitrarily complex
SO splitting is not able to cut the non-analyticity in
\Omega
with respect to
the magnetic field, see Eq. (
61
).
This is a direct consequence of the
backscattering processes shown in Fig.
4
.
Such processes
were not taken into account
in Ref.
kirk

where a complicated enough SO splitting
is predicted to cut the non-analyticity.

The weak coupling regime cannot be extended
to the phase transition point
where the electron-electron interaction is very strong.
In order to address this regime,
we treat the processes in Fig.
4

non-perturbatively
and find that there is a sector in the phase space
where the electron Green function exhibits
strong non-Fermi-liquid behavior.
We find that this non-Fermi-liquid sector
dominates the non-analytic response at low temperatures.
We also find that the non-analyticity
remains negative
but it is parametrically enhanced
from
\delta\Omega\propto-|B|^{D+1}

in the weak interaction limit
to
\delta\Omega\propto-|B|^{\frac{D+3}{2}}
in the strong coupling regime.
In particular, the non-analyticity in interacting 3DEG
is enhanced from the marginal

\propto B^{4}\ln|B|
term
in the weak interaction regime
to
\propto-|B|^{3}
close to the FQPT
where the interaction is strong.
In addition, SO splitting makes the
non-analytic corrections strongly anisotropic.
In particular,
if the SO splitting breaks the spin symmetry down to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
then the ferromagnetic ground state is necessarily Ising-like.
In this case, all soft fluctuations of the magnetic order parameter are gapped
which validates the mean-field treatment of the magnetization.
Nevertheless,
there is still a gapless fluctuation channel
originating from the resonant backscattering processes shown in Fig.
4
(b)
which contributes to
the negative non-analytic terms in
\Omega
destabilizing the FQCP.
Based on our analysis,
we conclude that the
FQPT in clean metals is always first
order regardless of the SO splitting.
The non-analytic terms leading to the instability of the FQCP in clean metals
can be measured via the non-analytic dependence
of the spin susceptibility
on the magnetic field
both far away, see Eqs. (
150
) and (
151
),
or close to the FQPT, see Eqs. (
153
) and (
154
).
The candidate materials are the pressure-tuned 3D ferromagnets ZrZn
2

uhlarz
,
UGe
2

taufour
, and many others
brando
,
or density-tuned 2D quantum wells
hossain
.

Appendix A
ASYMPTOTICS OF THE GREEN FUNCTION

We start from the Fourier representation of the Green function:

\displaystyle G(\tau,\bm{r})=\int\frac{d\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{D}}e^{i\bm{p}\cdot\bm{r}}G(\tau,\bm{p}),

(156)

where
\tau
is the imaginary time,

\bm{r}
a
D
-dimensional position vector,
and
\bm{p}
a
D
-dimensional momentum vector.
Here we do not indicate
any band index because it is fixed.
The asymptotics at large
\tau\gg 1/E_{F}
and large
r=|\bm{r}|\gg\lambda_{F}

is dominated by the vicinity of the Fermi surface
\mathcal{FS}
,
so we can expand the momentum
\bm{p}
into
the momentum
\bm{k}
on the Fermi surface
\mathcal{FS}
and
the momentum along the outward normal
\bm{n}(\bm{k})
at
\bm{k}
,
see Fig.
1
(a):

\displaystyle\bm{p}=\bm{k}+\bm{n}(\bm{k})\delta p,\,\,\,\,\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS},

(157)

where
\delta p
is the distance from
\bm{p}

to the Fermi surface
\mathcal{FS}
.
Notice that
\delta p>0
(
\delta p<0
) corresponds to
the states above (below) the Fermi surface.
At large
r\gg 1/k_{F}
,
k_{F}
is the typical momentum scale on the Fermi surface,
we have
\delta p\sim 1/r\ll k_{F}
,
so we can approximate the integral over
\bm{p}

by the integration over a thin layer around the Fermi surface:

\displaystyle G(\tau,\bm{r})\approx\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\delta p}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}}\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{D-1}}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt\times e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}e^{i\delta p\,\bm{n}(\bm{k})\cdot\bm{r}}G(\tau,\delta p,\bm{k}),

(158)

\displaystyle G(\tau,\delta p,\bm{k})\equiv G(\tau,\bm{k}+\bm{n}(\bm{k})\delta p).

(159)

Here we extended the integral over
\delta p
to the interval
(-\infty,\infty)
because the
convergence radius of this integral is very short at
r\to\infty
, namely
\delta p\sim 1/r
.
Hence, we approximated the initial Fourier
transform Eq. (
156
)
by the integral over the fiber bundle

\mathcal{FS}\times(-\infty,\infty)
.

As
r\to\infty
, we can use the stationary phase method to find the asymptotics.
First, we evaluate the integral over the Fermi surface.
The stationary condition for the phase of the rapidly oscillating factor
e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}
in Eq. (
158
)
reads:

\displaystyle d\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}=0,

(160)

where
d\bm{k}
is an arbitrary infinitesimal (but non-zero) element
of the tangent space
\mathcal{T}(\bm{k})
attached to the Fermi surface
\mathcal{FS}
at the point
\bm{k}
.
Hence,
\bm{r}
has to be orthogonal to the
whole linear space
\mathcal{T}(\bm{k})

which has codimension one.
This means that the stationary phase condition is satisfied
at the points
\bm{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{FS}

where
\bm{r}
is collinear with the normals
\bm{n}(\bm{k}^{\prime})
:

\displaystyle\bm{n}(\bm{k}^{\prime})=s(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})\bm{n}_{\bm{r}},\,\,\,\,\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}=\frac{\bm{r}}{r},

(161)

where
s(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})=+1
(
s(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})=-1
)
if the outward normal
\bm{n}(\bm{k}^{\prime})
and
the radius vector
\bm{r}

are parallel (anti-parallel).
We include all such points
\bm{k}^{\prime}

into a set
\mathcal{P}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})=\{\bm{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{FS}|\bm{n}(\bm{k}^{\prime})=\pm\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}\}.

(162)

It is clear that
\mathcal{P}(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})=\mathcal{P}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})

and
s(\bm{k}^{\prime},-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})=-s(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})
.

At this point we can take the integral over
\delta p
in Eq. (
158
):

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0pt\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\delta p}{2\pi}e^{i\delta p\,\bm{n}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\cdot\bm{r}}G(\tau,\delta p,\bm{k}^{\prime})=\mathcal{G}(\tau,s(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r,\bm{k}^{\prime}),

(163)

where we used that
\bm{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})

and
s(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})=\bm{n}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\cdot\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}=\pm 1
, see Eq. (
161
).
Here
\mathcal{G}(\tau,x,\bm{k})
is the 1D Fourier transform of the Green function:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\tau,x,\bm{k})=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\delta p}{2\pi}e^{i\delta p\,x}G(\tau,\delta p,\bm{k}),\,\,\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}.

(164)

Note that such a 1D Fourier transform is generally dependent on
the point
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}
.
Substituting this into Eq. (
158
), we find:

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0ptG(\tau,\bm{r})\approx\sum\limits_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})}e^{i\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{r}}J_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(r)\mathcal{G}(\tau,s(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r,\bm{k}^{\prime}),

(165)

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0ptJ_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(r)=\int\limits_{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}}\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{D-1}}e^{i(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\cdot\bm{r}}.

(166)

The function
J_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(r)
appears due to the integration over a small vicinity of a point
\bm{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})
.

The integral
J_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(r)
converges due to the finite curvature of the Fermi surface at
\bm{k}^{\prime}
.
This is true even if the interaction is very strong such that the Fermi surface becomes critical.
In order to evaluate this integral,
it is convenient to introduce an auxiliary function

\varepsilon(\bm{p})
with the following properties:

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\bm{p})=0\,\,{\rm if}\,\,\bm{p}\in\mathcal{FS},

(167)

\displaystyle\bm{v}(\bm{p})=\frac{\partial\varepsilon(\bm{p})}{\partial\bm{p}}\neq 0\,\,{\rm if}\,\,\bm{p}\in\mathcal{FS}.

(168)

We notice here that there are infinitely many choices for such a function but
we will show that the result is independent
of the choice.
In case of a free electron gas the natural choice for
\varepsilon(\bm{p})
is the electron dispersion.
The condition Eq. (
168
) is required to generate the outward normal
\bm{n}(\bm{k})
at each point
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}
:

\displaystyle\bm{v}(\bm{k})=v(\bm{k})\bm{n}(\bm{k}),\,\,\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}.

(169)

Here
v(\bm{k})\neq 0
for all
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}
.

Consider two close points
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}

and
\bm{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{FS}
.
According to Eq. (
167
),
we can write:

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\bm{k})=\varepsilon(\bm{k}^{\prime})=0.

(170)

Using the Taylor series expansion,
we find:

\displaystyle 0=\varepsilon(\bm{k})\approx\varepsilon(\bm{k}^{\prime})+(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\cdot\bm{v}(\bm{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})^{T}R(\bm{k}^{\prime})(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}),

(171)

\displaystyle R_{ij}(\bm{p})=\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon(\bm{p})}{\partial p_{i}\partial p_{j}},

(172)

where we used the matrix notations in Eq. (
171
), and the superscript

T
stands for transposition.
Substituting Eq. (
169
) into Eq. (
171
),
we find:

\displaystyle(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\cdot\bm{n}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\approx-(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})^{T}\frac{R(\bm{k}^{\prime})}{2v(\bm{k}^{\prime})}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}).

(173)

In Eq. (
166
)
\bm{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})
,
so
\bm{n}(\bm{k}^{\prime})
satisfies Eq. (
161

This allows us to write:

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\cdot\bm{r}\approx-s(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})^{T}\frac{R(\bm{k}^{\prime})}{2v(\bm{k}^{\prime})}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}).

(174)

The expression is quadratic with respect to the
small difference
\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}
.
At large
r
the convergence radius of the integral
J_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(r)

scales as
|\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}|\propto 1/\sqrt{r}

so we indeed have to integrate over a small

\bm{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})

Equation (
174

shows that the component of
\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}

along the normal
\bm{n}(\bm{k}^{\prime})
is only quadratic with respect to

\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}

so at a given accuracy we can approximate

\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}
on the right-hand side of Eq. (

by its orthogonal projection onto the

\mathcal{T}(\bm{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hskip-7.0ptJ_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(r)\approx\int\limits_{\mbox{\boldmath{$\kappa$}}\in\mathcal{T}(\bm{k}^{\prime})}\frac{d\mbox{\boldmath{$\kappa$}}}{(2\pi)^{D-1}}e^{-irs(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})\mbox{\boldmath{$\kappa$}}^{T}A(\bm{k}^{\prime})\mbox{\boldmath{$\kappa$}}},

(175)

\displaystyle\hskip-7.0ptA(\bm{k}^{\prime})=\frac{R_{\mathcal{T}}(\bm{k}^{\prime})}{2v(\bm{k}^{\prime})},

(176)

where
R_{\mathcal{T}}(\bm{k}^{\prime})

R(\bm{k}^{\prime})

\mathcal{T}(\bm{k}^{\prime})

We reduced the initial integral

\displaystyle J_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(r)\approx\left(\frac{1}{4\pi r}\right)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}\frac{e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}s(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})\mathfrak{S}(A(\bm{k}^{\prime}))}}{\sqrt{|\det A(\bm{k}^{\prime})|}},

(177)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(A(\bm{k}^{\prime}))=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{D-1}{\rm sgn}(a_{i}),

(178)

where
a_{i}

D-1

symmetric matrix
A(\bm{k}^{\prime})
.
In all cases that we consider in this paper

\det A(\bm{k})\neq 0
at any point
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}
.
In other words, we consider only Fermi surfaces
with non-zero Gauss curvature at each point.

Let us check
that the matrix
A(\bm{k})
,
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}

\varepsilon(\bm{p})
.
For this, we consider another parametrization:

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}(\bm{p})=f(\bm{p})\varepsilon(\bm{p}),

(179)

where

As

Let us find the velocity

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\tilde{v}(\bm{k})\bm{n}(\bm{k})=\frac{\partial\tilde{\varepsilon}(\bm{k})}{\partial\bm{k}}=\frac{\partial f(\bm{k})}{\partial\bm{k}}\varepsilon(\bm{k})+f(\bm{k})v(\bm{k})\bm{n}(\bm{k}),

\displaystyle\tilde{v}(\bm{k})=f(\bm{k})v(\bm{k}),\,\,\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}.

(181)

Similarly, we can calculate the tensor
\tilde{R}(\bm{k})
,
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{ij}(\bm{k})=\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{\varepsilon}(\bm{k})}{\partial k_{i}\partial k_{j}}=f(\bm{k})R_{ij}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt+v(\bm{k})\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial k_{i}}\bm{n}_{j}(\bm{k})+\frac{\partial f}{\partial k_{j}}\bm{n}_{i}(\bm{k})\right),\,\,\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS},

(182)

where
R_{ij}(\bm{k})
is defined in Eq. (
172
).
Second line in Eq. (
182
)
contains a term which vanishes in all
products
\mbox{\boldmath{$\kappa$}}^{T}\tilde{R}(\bm{k})\mbox{\boldmath{$\kappa$}}
where
\mbox{\boldmath{$\kappa$}}\in\mathcal{T}(\bm{k})
i.e.
\mbox{\boldmath{$\kappa$}}\cdot\bm{n}(\bm{k})=0
.
This means that the restriction to the tangent space
\mathcal{T}(\bm{k})
is especially simple:

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(\bm{k})=f(\bm{k})R_{\mathcal{T}}(\bm{k}),\,\,\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}.

(183)

Using Eqs. (
181
) and (
183
),
we indeed find that the operator
A(\bm{k})
,
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}
,
is invariant with respect to different choices of
\varepsilon(\bm{p})
:

\displaystyle\tilde{A}(\bm{k})=\frac{\tilde{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(\bm{k})}{2\tilde{v}(\bm{k})}=\frac{R_{\mathcal{T}}(\bm{k})}{2v(\bm{k})}=A(\bm{k}),\,\,\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}.

(184)

All in all, the long range asymptotics of the Green function of an interacting Fermi gas
is the following:

\displaystyle G(\tau,\bm{r})\approx\sum\limits_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})}\frac{C(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})}{(4\pi r)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}e^{i\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{r}}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt\times\mathcal{G}(\tau,s(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r,\bm{k}^{\prime}),

(185)

\displaystyle C(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})=\frac{e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}s(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})\mathfrak{S}(A(\bm{k}^{\prime}))}}{\sqrt{|\det A(\bm{k}^{\prime})|}}.

(186)

This is the general result which is suitable for a Fermi surface of arbitrary geometry.
Next, we give examples for spherical or nearly spherical Fermi surfaces.

A.1
Spherical Fermi surface

The simplest example is the spherical Fermi surface with the Fermi momentum
k_{F}
.
We considered this case in Ref.
miserev
.
For an arbitrary direction
\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}

there are exactly two points on the Fermi surface
whose normals are collinear with
\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})=\{\pm k_{F}\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}\}.

(187)

In this case, the sum over
\bm{k}^{\prime}
in Eq. (
185
)
contains only two terms, namely,

\bm{k}^{\prime}=\pm k_{F}\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}
.
In order to calculate the matrix
A(\bm{k}^{\prime})
,
we consider the function:

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\bm{p})=\frac{p^{2}-k_{F}^{2}}{2}.

(188)

The velocity
v(\bm{p})
and the tensor
R(\bm{p})
are then the following:

\displaystyle v(\bm{p})=p,

(189)

\displaystyle R_{ij}(\bm{p})=\delta_{ij}.

(190)

This allows us to identify the matrix
A(\bm{k})
,
|\bm{k}|=k_{F}
:

\displaystyle A(\bm{k})=\frac{I}{2k_{F}},\,\,|\bm{k}|=k_{F},

(191)

where
I
is the
(D-1)\times(D-1)

identity matrix on the tangent space
\mathcal{T}(\bm{k})
.
Substituting this into Eq. (
185
),
we find the asymptotics of the Green function
in case of the spherical Fermi surface:

\displaystyle G(\tau,\bm{r})\approx\left(\frac{k_{F}}{2\pi r}\right)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 5.0pt\times\left(e^{i(k_{F}r-\vartheta)}\mathcal{G}(\tau,r)+e^{-i(k_{F}r-\vartheta)}\mathcal{G}(\tau,-r)\right),

(192)

\displaystyle\vartheta=\frac{\pi}{4}(D-1),

(193)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\tau,x)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\delta p}{2\pi}e^{i\delta p\,x}G(\tau,\delta p).

(194)

Here we also used the spherical symmetry, i.e.

G(\tau,\bm{p})=G(\tau,p)
,
so
G(\tau,\delta p,\bm{k})
is independent of
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}
.

A.2
Nearly spherical Fermi surface

Here we consider another example when the Fermi surface is nearly spherical
and can be modeled by the following dispersion:

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\bm{p})=\frac{p^{2}-k_{F}^{2}}{2m}-\beta(\bm{p}).

(195)

We denote points on the Fermi surface
\mathcal{FS}

by
\bm{k}
, they satisfy the equation
\varepsilon(\bm{k})=0
:

\displaystyle k^{2}=k_{F}^{2}+2m\beta(\bm{k}).

(196)

In this part we make the following assumptions about the smooth function

\beta(\bm{p})
:

\displaystyle|\beta(\bm{k})|\ll E_{F},\,\,\,\mbox{\boldmath{$\nabla$}}\beta(\bm{k})\equiv\frac{\partial\beta(\bm{p})}{\partial\bm{p}}\left.\vphantom{\frac{\partial\beta(\bm{p})}{\partial\bm{p}}}\right|_{\bm{p}=\bm{k}}\ll v_{F},

(197)

where
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}
,

2E_{F}=k_{F}v_{F}
is the Fermi energy, and

v_{F}=k_{F}/m
the Fermi velocity.
Using the first condition in Eq. (
197
),
we find the approximate Fermi surface equation:

\displaystyle k(\bm{e})\approx k_{F}+\frac{\beta(\bm{e})}{v_{F}},\,\,k(\bm{e})\bm{e}\in\mathcal{FS},

(198)

where
\bm{e}
is an arbitrary unit vector
and
\beta(\bm{e})
stands for
\beta(k_{F}\bm{e})
.

The outward normal
\bm{n}(\bm{k})
at
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{FS}

is defined though the gradient
of
\varepsilon(\bm{p})
at
\bm{p}=\bm{k}
:

\displaystyle v(\bm{k})\bm{n}(\bm{k})=\frac{\bm{k}}{m}-\mbox{\boldmath{$\nabla$}}\beta(\bm{k}),

(199)

\displaystyle v^{2}(\bm{k})=\frac{k^{2}}{m^{2}}-2\frac{\bm{k}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$\nabla$}}\beta(\bm{k})}{m}+\left(\mbox{\boldmath{$\nabla$}}\beta(\bm{k})\right)^{2},

(200)

where the second equation here is just the first one squared.
Here is where we use the second condition in Eq. (
197
).
In linear order in
\beta(\bm{k})
we find:

\displaystyle v(\bm{k})\approx\frac{k(\bm{n})}{m}-\bm{n}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$\nabla$}}\beta(\bm{n}),

(201)

\displaystyle\bm{k}\cdot\bm{n}(\bm{k})\approx k(\bm{n})=k_{F}+\frac{\beta(\bm{n})}{v_{F}},

(202)

where
\beta(\bm{n})
stands for
\beta(k_{F}\bm{n})
.

We are only interested in the points
\bm{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{FS}

with normals
\bm{n}(\bm{k}^{\prime})=s\bm{n}_{\bm{r}}
,
s=\pm 1
.
Using Eq. (
202
), we find the oscillating phase:

\displaystyle\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{r}=sr\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{n}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\approx sk(s\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r,

(203)

where we used Eq. (
202
).
Neglecting the weak dependence of the prefactor
C(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})
on
\beta(\bm{k}^{\prime})
,
see Eq. (
186
),
we find the asymptotic behavior of the Green function in case of a nearly spherical Fermi surface:

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0ptG(\tau,\bm{r})\approx\left(\frac{k_{F}}{2\pi r}\right)^{\frac{D-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\times\left(e^{i(k(\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r-\vartheta)}\mathcal{G}(\tau,r)+e^{-i(k(-\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r-\vartheta)}\mathcal{G}(\tau,-r)\right),

(204)

where
k(\bm{n})
is given by Eq. (
198
)
and
\vartheta
by Eq. (
193
).
Here,
\mathcal{G}(\tau,x)
is calculated at
\beta(\bm{p})=0
, i.e. it coincides with
the spherically symmetric case.
Importantly, the oscillatory factors

e^{\pm ik(\pm\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})r}

in Eq. (
204
) depend explicitly on

\beta(\pm\bm{n}_{\bm{r}})
,
which is crucial for the resonant scattering processes near the Fermi surface.

Duality for Powerset Coalgebras
Guram Bezhanishvili \rsuper a, Luca Carai \rsuper b, Patrick J. Morandi \rsuper a

Abstract
. Let 𝖢𝖠𝖡𝖠 \sf{CABA} be the category of complete atomic boolean algebras and complete boolean homomorphisms, and let 𝖢𝖲𝖫 {\sf{CSL}} be the category of complete meet-semilattices and complete meet-homomorphisms. We show that the forgetful functor from 𝖢𝖠𝖡𝖠 \sf{CABA} to 𝖢𝖲𝖫 {\sf{CSL}} has a left adjoint. This allows us to describe an endofunctor ℋ \mathcal{H} on 𝖢𝖠𝖡𝖠 \sf{CABA} such that the category 𝖠𝗅𝗀 ⁡ ( ℋ ) {\sf{Alg}}(\mathcal{H}) of algebras for ℋ \mathcal{H} is dually equivalent to the category 𝖢𝗈𝖺𝗅𝗀 ⁡ ( 𝒫 ) {\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{P}) of coalgebras for the powerset endofunctor 𝒫 \mathcal{P} on 𝖲𝖾𝗍 {\sf{Set}} . As a consequence, we derive Thomason duality from Tarski duality, thus paralleling how Jónsson-Tarski duality was derived from Stone duality in [ Abr88 , KKV04 ] .

]
and Goldblatt
[

]

Jónsson-Tarski duality is a generalization of the celebrated Stone duality between the category

{\sf{Stone}}

Descriptive frames can be thought of as Kripke frames

In

Thomason proved a “discrete version” of Jónsson-Tarski duality, establishing that the category
{\sf{KFr}}

{\sf{CAMA}}

The same way Jónsson-Tarski duality generalizes Stone duality, Thomason duality generalizes Tarski duality between the category
\sf{CABA}
of complete atomic boolean algebras and the category
{\sf{Set}}
of sets. It is natural to try to obtain Thomason duality from Tarski duality using algebra/coalgebra methods in the same vein Jónsson-Tarski duality was obtained from Stone duality in
[
Abr88
,
KKV04
]
. Surprisingly, such an approach has not yet been undertaken. Our aim is to fill in this gap.

For this purpose, it is natural to replace the Vietoris endofunctor
\mathcal{V}
on
{\sf{Stone}}
with the powerset endofunctor
\mathcal{P}
on
{\sf{Set}}
. It is known (see, e.g.,
[
Ven07
, Sec. 9]
) that
{\sf{KFr}}
is isomorphic to
{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{P})
. Thus, the key is to construct an endofunctor
\mathcal{H}
on
\sf{CABA}
that is an analogue of the endofunctor
\mathcal{K}:{\sf{BA}}\to{\sf{BA}}
. We recall that
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{L}\mathcal{U}
where
\mathcal{L}:{\sf{SL}}\to{\sf{BA}}
is left adjoint to the forgetful functor
\mathcal{U}:{\sf{BA}}\to{\sf{SL}}
. A natural analogue of
{\sf{SL}}
in the complete case is the category
{\sf{CSL}}
of complete meet-semilattices.
Our main contribution is to show that the forgetful functor
\mathcal{U}:\sf{CABA}\to{\sf{CSL}}
has a left adjoint
\mathcal{L}:{\sf{CSL}}\to\sf{CABA}
. We then define
\mathcal{H}:\sf{CABA}\to\sf{CABA}
as the composition
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{L}\mathcal{U}
, and prove that
{\sf{Alg}}(\mathcal{H})
is dually equivalent to
{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{P})
. Since
{\sf{CAMA}}
is isomorphic to
{\sf{Alg}}(\mathcal{H})
and
{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{P})
is isomorphic to
{\sf{KFr}}

2.
Coalgebraic approach to Jónsson-Tarski duality

In this section we give a brief account of Jónsson-Tarski duality, and provide a construction of the left adjoint
\mathcal{L}:{\sf{SL}}\to{\sf{BA}}
of the forgetful functor
\mathcal{U}:{\sf{BA}}\to{\sf{SL}}
which is alternative to
[
KKV04
, Prop. 3.12]
. This we do by utilizing Pontryagin duality for semilattices
[
HMS74
]
. We start by recalling the definition of a modal algebra.

{defi}

A
modal algebra
is a pair
(B,\Box)
where
B
is a boolean algebra and
\Box
is a unary function on
B
preserving finite meets. A
modal algebra homomorphism
between modal algebras
(B_{1},\Box_{1})
and
(B_{2},\Box_{2})
is a boolean homomorphism
\alpha:B_{1}\to B_{2}
such that
\alpha(\Box_{1}a)=\Box_{2}\alpha(a)
for each
a\in B_{1}
. Let
{\sf{MA}}
be the category of modal algebras and modal algebra homomorphisms.

A subset of a topological space
X
is
clopen
if it is both closed and open, and
X
is
zero-dimensional
if
X
has a basis of clopen sets. A
Stone space
is a zero-dimensional compact Hausdorff space.

For a binary relation
R
on
X
, we write

R[x]:=\{y\in X\mid xRy\}\ \mbox{ and }\ R^{-1}[U]:=\{x\in X\mid\exists u\in U\mbox{ with }xRu\}

for the
R
-image of
x\in X
and
R
-inverse image of
U\subseteq X
.

{defi}

A
descriptive frame
is a pair
(X,R)
where
X
is a Stone space and
R
is a binary relation on
X
such that
R[x]
is closed for each
x\in X
and
R^{-1}[U]
is clopen for each clopen
U\subseteq X
.

Such relations are often called
continuous relations
for the following reason. Let
\mathcal{V}(X)
be the
Vietoris space
of
X
. We recall (see, e.g.,
[
Joh82
, Sec. III.4]
) that
\mathcal{V}(X)
is the set of closed subsets of
X
topologized by the subbasis
\{\Box_{U},\Diamond_{V}\mid U,V\mbox{ open in }X\}
where

\Box_{U}=\{F\in\mathcal{V}(X)\mid F\subseteq U\}\ \mbox{ and }\ \Diamond_{V}=\{F\in\mathcal{V}(X)\mid F\cap V\neq\varnothing\}.

Then
R
is continuous iff the associated map
\rho_{R}:X\to\mathcal{V}(X)
, given by
\rho_{R}(x)=R[x]
, is a well-defined continuous map (that
\rho_{R}
is well defined follows from (i), and that it is continuous from (ii)).

Let
{\sf{DFr}}
be the category of descriptive frames and continuous p-morphisms, where a
p-morphism
between
(X_{1},R_{1})
and
(X_{2},R_{2})
is a map
f:X_{1}\to X_{2}
satisfying
f[R_{1}[x]]=R_{2}[f(x)]
for each
x\in X_{1}
.

3.
Two constructions of the left adjoint
\mathcal{L}:{\sf{CSL}}\to\sf{CABA}

Let
{\sf{KFr}}
be the category of Kripke frames and p-morphisms. Forgetting the topology of a descriptive frame yields the forgetful functor
\mathcal{U}:{\sf{DFr}}\to{\sf{KFr}}
. To describe the modal algebras corresponding to Kripke frames, we recall the notion of a completely multiplicative modal operator.

{defi}

A modal operator
\Box
on a complete boolean algebra
B
is
completely multiplicative
if
\Box\left(\bigwedge S\right)=\bigwedge\{\Box s\mid s\in S\}
for each
S\subseteq B
. Let
{\sf{CAMA}}
be the category whose objects are complete atomic modal algebras with completely multiplicative
\Box
, and whose morphisms are complete modal algebra homomorphisms.

Theorem 4

(Thomason duality)
.

{\sf{CAMA}}
is dually equivalent to
{\sf{KFr}}
.

Thomason duality generalizes Tarski duality between
\sf{CABA}
and
{\sf{Set}}
the same way Jónsson-Tarski duality generalizes Stone duality. We recall that
\sf{CABA}
is the category of complete atomic boolean algebras and complete boolean homomorphisms and
{\sf{Set}}
is the category of sets and functions. The contravariant functors of Tarski duality are
\wp:{\sf{Set}}\to^{\sf{op}}\sf{CABA}
and
{\mathfrak{at}}:\sf{CABA}\to^{\sf{op}}{\sf{Set}}
. The functor
\wp
assigns to each set
X
the powerset
\wp(X)
and to each function
f:X\to Y
its inverse image
f^{-1}:\wp(Y)\to\wp(X)
. The functor
{\mathfrak{at}}
assigns to each
A\in\sf{CABA}
its set of atoms. If
\alpha:A\to B
is a complete boolean homomorphism, it has a left adjoint
\alpha^{*}:B\to A
, which sends atoms to atoms, and the functor
{\mathfrak{at}}
assigns to
\alpha
the function
\alpha^{*}:{\mathfrak{at}}(B)\to{\mathfrak{at}}(A)
. One unit
\varepsilon:1_{{\sf{Set}}}\to{\mathfrak{at}}\circ\wp
of this dual equivalence is given by
\varepsilon_{X}(x)=\{x\}
for each
x\in X\in{\sf{Set}}
, and the other unit
\vartheta:1_{\sf{CABA}}\to\wp\circ{\mathfrak{at}}
by
\vartheta_{A}(a)={\downarrow}a\cap{\mathfrak{at}}(A)
for each
a\in A\in\sf{CABA}
.

To derive Thomason duality from Tarski duality the same way Jónsson-Tarski duality was derived from Stone duality, we need to replace the Vietoris endofunctor
\mathcal{V}
on
{\sf{Stone}}
with the powerset endofunctor
\mathcal{P}
on
{\sf{Set}}
. We recall that the endofunctor
\mathcal{P}:{\sf{Set}}\to{\sf{Set}}
associates to each set
X
its powerset
\mathcal{P}(X)
and to each function
f:X\to Y
the function
\mathcal{P}(f):\mathcal{P}(X)\to\mathcal{P}(Y)
that maps each subset
S\subseteq X
to its direct image
f[S]
. We also need to replace the endofunctor
\mathcal{K}:{\sf{BA}}\to{\sf{BA}}
with an appropriate endofunctor
\mathcal{H}:\sf{CABA}\to\sf{CABA}
.

To describe
\mathcal{H}
, we need to construct the left adjoint to
\mathcal{U}:\sf{CABA}\to{\sf{CSL}}
, where
{\sf{CSL}}
is the category of complete meet-semilattices and complete meet-homomorphisms. As in the previous section, this can be done purely algebraically or using duality. To construct
\mathcal{H}
algebraically, we need that free objects exist in
\sf{CABA}
. Care is needed here since
it is a well-known result of Gaifman
[
Gai64
]
and Hales
[
Hal64
]
that free objects do not exist in the category of complete boolean algebras and complete boolean homomorphisms. On the other hand, free objects do exist in
\sf{CABA}
, and this can be seen by observing that the Eilenberg-Moore algebras of the double contravariant powerset monad are exactly the objects of
\sf{CABA}

[
Tay02
]
, and that categories of algebras for monads have free objects
[
AHS06
, Prop. 20.7(2)]
.

A more concrete construction of free objects in
\sf{CABA}
can be given utilizing the theory of canonical extensions. It is well known that free objects over any set exist in the category of complete and completely distributive lattices (see Markowski
[
Mar79
]
and Dwinger
[
Dwi81
, Thm. 4.2]
). By
[
BHJ21
, Cor. 2.3]
, the free complete and completely distributive lattice over a set
X
is the canonical extension of the free bounded distributive lattice over
X
. We show that the same is true in
\sf{CABA}
.
For this we need to recall the definition of a canonical extension of a boolean algebra.

{defi}

[
JT51
,
GH01
]

A

of a boolean algebra

is a complete boolean algebra

together with a boolean embedding

satisfying:

(1)

(Density) Each

is a join of meets (and hence also a meet of joins) of

.

(2)

(Compactness) For

, from
\bigwedge e[S]\leq\bigvee e[T]
it follows that
\bigwedge S_{0}\leq\bigvee T_{0}
for some finite
S_{0}\subseteq S
and
T_{0}\subseteq T
.

It is well known that canonical extensions are unique up to isomorphism, and that the correspondence

extends to a covariant functor
(-)^{\sigma}:{\sf{BA}}\to\sf{CABA}
. It can conveniently be described as the composition
\wp\circ\mathcal{U}\circ{\mathfrak{uf}}
, where
\mathcal{U}:{\sf{Stone}}\to{\sf{Set}}
is the forgetful functor.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\sf{BA}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\sf{Stone}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\sf{Set}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sf{CABA}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\sigma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{uf}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wp}

Thus, we can think of

as the Stone map

y\in\zeta_{A}((\tau_{\Box})^{*}(x))\ \mbox{iff}\ y\leq\bigwedge\{a\in A\mid x\leq\Box a\}\ \mbox{iff}\ (\forall a\in A)(x\leq\Box a\Rightarrow y\leq a).

Thus,
xR_{\zeta_{A}\circ{\mathfrak{at}}(\tau_{\Box})}y
iff
xR_{\Box}y
, so
\mathcal{F}\,\widehat{{\mathfrak{at}}}\,\mathcal{A}(A,\Box)={\mathfrak{at}}(A,\Box)
, and hence
\mathcal{F}\,\widehat{{\mathfrak{at}}}\,\mathcal{A}={\mathfrak{at}}
.

Remark 24
.

We conclude the paper by connecting the coalgebraic approaches to Jónsson-Tarski and Thomason dualities. As follows from
[
KKV04
]
,
{\sf{Alg}}(\mathcal{K})
is dually equivalent to
{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{V})
, from which Jónsson-Tarski duality follows. By Theorem
21
,
{\sf{Alg}}(\mathcal{H})
is dually equivalent to
{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{P})
, from which Thomason duality follows.

Let
\mathcal{U}:{\sf{Stone}}\to{\sf{Set}}
be the forgetful functor. For each
X\in{\sf{Stone}}
, viewing the underlying set of the Vietoris space
\mathcal{V}(X)
as a subset of
\mathcal{P}(X)
, we have an inclusion map
i:\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V}(X)\to\mathcal{P}\mathcal{U}(X)
. We extend
\mathcal{U}
to a forgetful functor on the level of coalgebras. Let
(X,g)\in{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{V})
, so
g:X\to\mathcal{V}(X)
is a continuous map. Set
\mathcal{U}(X,g):=(\mathcal{U}(X),g^{\prime})
where
g^{\prime}:\mathcal{U}(X)\to\mathcal{PU}(X)
is given by
g^{\prime}=i\circ\mathcal{U}(g)
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}\mathcal{U}(X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}(g)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}

Then
(\mathcal{U}(X),g^{\prime})\in{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{P})
. If
\alpha:(X_{1},g_{1})\to(X_{2},g_{2})
is a
{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{V})
-morphism, it is straightforward to see that
\mathcal{U}(\alpha):(\mathcal{U}(X_{1}),g_{1}^{\prime})\to(\mathcal{U}(X_{2}),g_{2}^{\prime})
is a
{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{P})
-morphism. This yields a functor
\mathcal{U}:{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{V})\to{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{P})
. If we identify
X
with
\mathcal{U}(X)
and
g:X\to\mathcal{V}(X)
with
g^{\prime}:\mathcal{U}(X)\to\wp\mathcal{U}(X)
, then
\mathcal{U}(X,g)=(X,g)
. We have thus forgotten the topological structure of
X
and the continuity of
g
. This justifies thinking about
\mathcal{U}:{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{V})\to{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{P})
as a forgetful functor.

We next define a functor
(-)^{\sigma}:{\sf{Alg}}(\mathcal{K})\to{\sf{Alg}}(\mathcal{H})
which can be thought of as the canonical extension functor for algebras for
\mathcal{K}
. Use Lemma
20
to lift the contravariant functors of Stone duality to
\widehat{{\mathfrak{uf}}},\widehat{{\mathfrak{clop}}}
and those of Tarski duality to
\widehat{{\mathfrak{at}}},\widehat{\wp}
and set
(-)^{\sigma}=\widehat{\wp}\circ\mathcal{U}\circ\widehat{{\mathfrak{uf}}}
.
Thus,
(-)^{\sigma}\circ\widehat{{\mathfrak{clop}}}
and
\widehat{\wp}\circ\mathcal{U}
are naturally isomorphic, and hence so are
\mathcal{U}\circ\widehat{{\mathfrak{uf}}}
and
\widehat{{\mathfrak{at}}}\circ(-)^{\sigma}
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\sf{Alg}}(\mathcal{K})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{V})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\sf{Alg}}(\mathcal{H})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{P})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{{\mathfrak{uf}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\sigma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{{\mathfrak{clop}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{{\mathfrak{at}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\wp}}

The functor
\mathcal{K}
satisfies
{\mathfrak{uf}}\mathcal{K}(A)\cong\mathcal{V}{\mathfrak{uf}}(A)

[
KKV04
, Cor. 3.11]
. By identifying these two Stone spaces, for
(A,\alpha)\in{\sf{Alg}}(\mathcal{K})
we describe
(A,\alpha)^{\sigma}
directly. Since
\alpha:\mathcal{K}(A)\to A
is a
{\sf{BA}}
-morphism,
{\mathfrak{uf}}(\alpha):{\mathfrak{uf}}(A)\to{\mathfrak{uf}}\mathcal{K}(A)=\mathcal{V}{\mathfrak{uf}}(A)
is a continuous map. Therefore,
({\mathfrak{uf}}(A),{\mathfrak{uf}}(\alpha))\in{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{V})
. The forgetful functor (after appropriate identifications) sends this to
({\mathfrak{uf}}(A),{\mathfrak{uf}}(\alpha))\in{\sf{Coalg}}(\mathcal{P})
. Finally,
\widehat{\wp}
sends
({\mathfrak{uf}}(A),{\mathfrak{uf}}(\alpha))
to
(\wp{\mathfrak{uf}}(A),\wp{\mathfrak{uf}}(\alpha))=(A^{\sigma},\alpha^{\sigma})
. Because of these calculations, we can view the functor
{\sf{Alg}}(\mathcal{K})\to{\sf{Alg}}(\mathcal{H})
as the canonical extension functor for algebras for
\mathcal{K}
.

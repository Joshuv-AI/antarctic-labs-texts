Volterra type operators on minimal Möbius invariant space Thanks: This work was supported by NNSF of China (Grant No. 11801094).
Huayou Xie, Junming Liu, Saminathan Ponnusamy

Abstract
. In this note, we mainly study operator-theoretic properties on Besov space B 1 B_{1} on the unit disc. This space is the minimal Möbius invariant space. Firstly, we consider the boundedness of Volterra type operators. Secondly, we prove that Volterra type operators belong to the Deddens algebra of composition operator. Thirdly, we obtain estimates for the essential norm of Volterra type operators. Finally, we give a complete characterization of spectrum of Volterra type operators.

Introduction and preparation

In this paper,
\mathbb{D}
denotes the open unit disc and
\mathbb{T}
be the unit circle.
Let
H(\mathbb{D})
be the space of all analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
.
For
0<p<\infty
, the Hardy space
H^{p}
consists of analytic functions
f\in\mathbb{D}
such that

\|f\|^{p}_{H^{p}}=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{2\pi}_{0}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta<\infty.

If
p=\infty
, then
H^{\infty}
is the space of bounded analytic functions
f
in
H(\mathbb{D})
with

\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup\{|f(z)|:z\in\mathbb{D}\}.

For
0<p<\infty
, the Bergman space
A^{p}
consists of all function
f
analytic in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\|f\|^{p}_{A^{p}}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\,dA(z)<\infty,

where
dA(z)
is the normalized Lebesgue area measure on
\mathbb{D}
. It is clear that
H^{p}\subset A^{p}
. Moreover,
H^{p}\subset A^{2p}
and
\|f\|_{A^{2p}}\leq\|f\|_{H^{p}}
for
0<p<\infty
. See
[
43
]
, for example.

The Dirichlet type space
\mathcal{D}^{p}
is the set of all functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with

\|f\|^{p}_{\mathcal{D}^{p}}=|f(0)|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\,dA(z)<\infty.

The space of all conformal automorphisms of
\mathbb{D}
forms a group, called the Möbius group and is denoted by
{\rm Aut}\,(\mathbb{D})
. It is well-known that
\varphi
belongs to

{\rm Aut}\,(\mathbb{D})
if and only if there exists a real number
\theta
and a point
a\in\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle\varphi(z)=e^{i\theta}\sigma_{a}(z)~\mbox{ and }~\sigma_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Let
X
be a Banach space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. Then
X
is said to be Möbius invariant whenever
f\circ\varphi\in X
for all
f\in X
and
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}\,(\mathbb{D})
and
\|f\circ\varphi\|_{X}=\|f\|_{X}
.

For
1<p<\infty
, the Besov space
B_{p}
consists of analytic functions
f
in

\mathbb{D}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\,dA(z)<\infty.

The norm of
B_{p}
is defined by

\|f\|_{B_{p}}=|f(0)|+\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\,dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

When
p=\infty
,
B_{\infty}=:\mathcal{B}
is called the classical Bloch space. We define a norm in
\mathcal{B}
by

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}=|f(0)|+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|<\infty.

When
p=2
,
B_{2}=:\mathcal{D}
is the classical Dirichlet space. When
p=1
, we get the analytic Besov space
B_{1}
which is the minimal Möbius invariant space consisting of all function
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with

f(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}c_{k}\sigma_{a_{k}}(z),

where the sequence
\{c_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\in\ell^{1}
and
\{a_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\in\mathbb{D}
. An equivalent norm of
B_{1}
is defined as

\|f\|_{B_{1}}=|f(0)|+|f^{\prime}(0)|+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(z)|\,dA(z).

Arazy et al.
[
5
]
first studied minimal Möbius invariant space systematically. More results related to minimal Möbius invariant space may be seen from
[
6
,
7
,
9
,
31
,
32
,
44
]
.

Now, we define several operators on
B_{1}
. For
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
the multiplication operator
M_{g}
on
B_{1}
is defined by

(M_{g})f(z)=f(z)g(z),~f\in B_{1},~z\in\mathbb{D}.

The differentiation operator is given by
Df=f^{\prime}
for each
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
.
Given
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
, the Volterra type operator
T_{g}
is defined by

(T_{g}f)(z)=\int^{z}_{0}f(w)g^{\prime}(w)\,dw,~\mbox{ for all }f\in B_{1}.

When
g(z)=z
, the operator
T_{z}f(z)=\int^{z}_{0}f(w)dw
becomes the simplest Volterra operator. An integral operator related to
T_{g}
, denoted by
I_{g}
, is defined by

(I_{g}f)(z)=\int^{z}_{0}f^{\prime}(w)g(w)\,dw,~\mbox{ for all }f\in B_{1}.

The Volterra type operator
T_{g}
was originally studied by Pommerenke

In

Lin et al.

generalized some of the works of

to the general case when

, and obtained the boundedness of the Volterra type operator

and

on derivative Hardy space

. And then they also considered strict singularity of Volterra type operators on Hardy spaces in

2.
Volterra type operators on
B_{1}

Lemma 1
.

If
f\in H^{1}
and
f(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}a_{n}z^{n}
, then

\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{|a_{n}|}{n+1}\leq\pi\|f\|_{H^{1}}.

The following lemma is a classic exercise in mathematical analysis, but it might be worth to give a brief details of the proof for completeness.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that
f(x)
is a continuously increasing function on
[a,b]
. Then

\int^{b}_{a}xf(x)dx\geq\frac{a+b}{2}\int^{b}_{a}f(x)\,dx.

Proof.

As
f(x)
is monotonically increasing on
[a,b]
, the “integral mean value theorem” shows that there exists a
\xi\in[a,b]
such that

\displaystyle\int^{b}_{a}\left(x-\frac{a+b}{2}\right)f(x)\,dx=

\displaystyle f(a)\int^{\xi}_{a}\left(x-\frac{a+b}{2}\right)dx+f(b)\int^{b}_{\xi}\left(x-\frac{a+b}{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(f(b)-f(a))(a\xi+b\xi-\xi^{2}-ab)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(f(b)-f(a))(b-\xi)(\xi-a)\geq 0.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3
.

If
f\in\mathcal{D}^{1}
, then

\|f\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{D}^{1}.
Then we can see that

|f(e^{i\theta})|-|f(0)|\leq|f(e^{i\theta})-f(0)|\leq\left|\int^{1}_{0}f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})\,dr\right|\leq\int^{1}_{0}|f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|\,dr.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H_{1}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{2\pi}_{0}|f(e^{i\theta})|\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{2\pi}_{0}\left[|f(0)|+\int^{1}_{0}|f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|\,dr\right]d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|f(0)|+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{2\pi}_{0}\int^{1}_{0}|f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|\,dr\,d\theta.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}=|f(0)|+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|\,dA(z)=

\displaystyle|f(0)|+\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{2\pi}_{0}\int^{1}_{0}r|f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|\,dr\,d\theta.

By Hardy’s convexity theorem (see,
[
26
,
45
]
), we find that

F(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{2\pi}_{0}|f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta,\,~0<r<1,

is a nondecreasing function of
r
.
It follows from Lemma
2
that

\int^{1}_{0}F(r)dr\leq 2\int^{1}_{0}rF(r)\,dr.

This shows that
\|f\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}
.
∎

Remark 1
.

In Lemma
3
, set
f(z)=z
. Then we see that
1=\|z\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|z\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}=1
showing that the norm estimate is sharp. This improves the previous conclusion, namely,
\|f\|_{H^{1}}\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}
from the works of Girela and Merchán
[
17
]
.

Lemma 4
.

If
f\in B_{1}
, then
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\pi\|f\|_{B_{1}}
and
B_{1}\subset H^{\infty}
.

Proof.

Assume that
f\in B_{1}
and write
f(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}a_{k}z^{k}
. By Lemmas
1
and
3
, we have

\displaystyle|f(z)|=\left|\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}a_{k}z^{k}\right|\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}|a_{k}z^{k}|\leq

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}|a_{k}|\leq\pi\|f^{\prime}\|_{H^{1}}+|f(0)|\leq\pi\|f^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}+|f(0)|\leq\pi\|f\|_{B_{1}},

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
Hence, we obtain that
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\pi\|f\|_{B_{1}}
.
∎

Remark 2
.

In
[
21
, Theorem 1]
, they obtained that
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\pi\|f\|_{S^{1}}
for each
f\in S^{1}
, where the space
S^{1}
is defined by
S^{1}=\{f\in H^{1}:f^{\prime}\in H^{1}\}

In the following, we discuss the boundedness of
T_{g}
and
I_{g}
on minimal Möbius invariant space.

Theorem 1
.

The operator
T_{g}
is bounded on
B_{1}
if and only if
g\in B_{1}
. Moreover,

\|g-g(0)\|\leq\|T_{g}\|\leq(1+\pi)\|g-g(0)\|_{B_{1}}.

Proof.

Let
f\in B_{1}
. By Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
3
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)g^{\prime}(z)|\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\,dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\,dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\|f^{\prime}\|_{A^{2}}\|g^{\prime}\|_{A^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f^{\prime}\|_{H^{1}}\|g^{\prime}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}\|g^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{B_{1}}\|g-g(0)\|_{B_{1}}.

Hence, we get

\displaystyle\|T_{g}f\|_{B_{1}}=

\displaystyle\|fg^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}(\mathbb{D})}=|f(0)g^{\prime}(0)|+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)g^{\prime}(z)|\,dA(z)+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)g^{\prime\prime}(z)|\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B_{1}}\|g-g(0)\|_{B_{1}}+\|f\|_{\infty}\|g-g(0)\|_{B_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\pi)\|f\|_{B_{1}}\|g-g(0)\|_{B_{1}}

showing that
T_{g}
is a bounded operator on
B_{1}
.

Conversely, assume that
T_{g}
is a bounded operator on
B_{1}
and let
f=1
. Then we obtain

\|T_{g}\|\geq\|T_{g}1\|_{B_{1}}\geq\|g-g(0)\|_{B_{1}}

which gives that
g\in B_{1}
. Thus,

\|g-g(0)\|\leq\|T_{g}\|\leq(1+\pi)\|g-g(0)\|_{B_{1}}.

∎

For
0<p<\infty
,
-2<q<\infty
, and
0\leq s<\infty
, we define the general family of function spaces
F(p,q,s)
as the set of all analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\|f\|^{p}_{p,q,s}=|f(0)|+\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{q}g^{s}(z,a)\,dA(z)<\infty,

where
g(z,a)=\log\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|}
. These spaces were introduced by Zhao in
[
46
]
. In 2003, Rättyä provided the following
n
-th derivation characterization of functions in spaces

.

Lemma 5
.

[
38
, Theorem 3.2]

Let
f
be an analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
and let
0<p<\infty,-2<q<\infty
and
0\leq s<\infty
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
q+s>-1
; or
n=0
and
q+s-p>-1
. Then

if and only if

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{(n)}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{np-p+q}(1-|\varphi_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}\,dA(z)<\infty.

For
p>0
, the space
Z_{p}
consists of all analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\|f\|_{Z_{p}}=|f(0)|+\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|(f\circ\sigma_{a}(z))^{\prime}|(1-|z|^{2})^{p-1}\,dA(z)<\infty.

It is clear that
Z_{1}=F(1,-1,1)
. For more results about

and

.

Theorem 2
.

The operator
I_{g}
is bounded on
B_{1}
if and only if
g\in Z_{1}\cap H^{\infty}
.

Proof.

Assume that
g\in Z_{1}\cap H^{\infty}
.
Using Lemma
6
of
[
23
]
, we obtain that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(w)|\cdot|g^{\prime}(w)|\,dA(w)\lesssim\|g\|_{Z_{1}}\left(|f^{\prime}(0)|+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(w)|\,dA(w)\right).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|(I_{g}f)(z)\|_{B_{1}}=

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}g\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|f^{\prime}(0)g(0)|+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(w)|\cdot|g(w)|\,dA(w)+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(w)|\cdot|g^{\prime}(w)|\,dA(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|f^{\prime}(0)g(0)|+\|g\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(w)|\,dA(w)+\|g\|_{Z_{1}}\left(|f^{\prime}(0)|+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(w)|\,dA(w)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(\|g\|_{\infty}+\|g\|_{Z_{1}})\|f\|_{B_{1}}

for all
f\in B_{1}
. This implies that
I_{g}
is a bounded operator on
B_{1}
.

Conversely, suppose that
I_{g}
is a bounded operator on
B_{1}
. For each
a\in\mathbb{D}
,
let
f(z)=\sigma_{a}(z)
. Then
\|f\|_{B_{1}}\lesssim 1
and

\displaystyle\|I_{g}\|\gtrsim

\displaystyle\|I_{g}f\|_{B_{1}}=\|g(w)f^{\prime}(w)\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|g(w)f^{\prime}(w)\|_{H^{1}}=\int^{2\pi}_{0}|g(e^{i\theta})|\cdot|\sigma^{\prime}_{a}(e^{i\theta})|\,d\theta=\int^{2\pi}_{0}|g(\sigma_{a}(e^{i\theta}))|\,d\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|g(\sigma_{a}(0))|=|g(a)|

which shows that
\|I_{g}\|\gtrsim\|g\|_{\infty}
.
Hence,
g\in H^{\infty}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\|I_{g}\|

\displaystyle\gtrsim\|I_{g}\sigma_{a}\|_{B_{1}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\sigma^{\prime}_{a}(w)|\cdot|g^{\prime}(w)|\,dA(w)-\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\sigma^{\prime\prime}_{a}(w)|\cdot|g(w)|\,dA(w)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\sigma^{\prime}_{a}(w)|\cdot|g^{\prime}(w)|\,dA(w)-\|g\|_{\infty}\|\sigma_{a}\|_{B_{1}},

from which it follows that

\|I_{g}\|+\|g\|_{\infty}\|\sigma_{a}\|_{B_{1}}\gtrsim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\sigma^{\prime}_{a}(w)|\cdot|g^{\prime}(w)|\,dA(w).

This implies that
g\in Z_{1}
. Therefore,
g\in Z_{1}\cap H^{\infty}
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

The following theorem gives the connection between the Volterra operator
T_{z}
on
\mathcal{D}^{1}
and the multiplication operator
M_{z}
on
B^{0}_{1}
.

Theorem 3
.

The Volterra type operator
T_{z}:\mathcal{D}^{1}\rightarrow B^{0}_{1}
is bounded and invertible with
T^{-1}_{z}=D.

Proof.

First, we show that
T_{z}(\mathcal{D}^{1})=B^{0}_{1}.
For
f\in\mathcal{D}^{1}
, we consider

F(z):=(T_{z}f)(z)=\int^{z}_{0}f(w)\,dw.

Clearly,
F^{\prime}=f\in\mathcal{D}^{1}
so that
F\in B^{0}_{1}
and
T_{z}(\mathcal{D}^{1})\subseteq B^{0}_{1}.

Conversely, for each
F\in B^{0}_{1}
, we have
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}^{1}
. Then

(T_{z}(F^{\prime}(z)))=\int^{z}_{0}F^{\prime}(w)\,dw=F(z)-F(0)=F(z).

Then
B^{0}_{1}\subseteq T_{z}(\mathcal{D}^{1})
. This implies that
T_{z}(\mathcal{D}^{1})=B^{0}_{1}
.

Secondly, we show that
T_{z}:\mathcal{D}^{1}\rightarrow B^{0}_{1}
is a bounded isomorphism, and its inverse
T^{-1}_{z}=D.
Recall from the above
discussion that
(T_{z}(F^{\prime}(z)))=F(z)
for
F\in B^{0}_{1}
. Then, for each
f\in\mathcal{D}^{1}
, we have that
(D(T_{z}f))(z)=f(z)
. This implies that
T_{z}
is a bijection from
\mathcal{D}^{1}
onto
B^{0}_{1}
. Since
T_{z}
is linear and
T_{z}
is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{D}^{1}
onto
B^{0}_{1}
.

Finally, we need to prove that
T_{z}
is a bounded operator on
\mathcal{D}^{1}
. For
f\in\mathcal{D}^{1}
, we have

\|T_{z}f\|_{B_{1}}\lesssim(|(T_{z}f)(0)|+\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}})\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}.

Therefore,
T_{z}
is a bounded isomorphism from
\mathcal{D}^{1}
onto
B^{0}_{1}
.
∎

Let us introduce an addition operator
P
defined by

(Pf)(z)=(M_{z}f)(z)+(T_{z}f)(z)\ \mbox{ for $f\in H(\mathbb{D})$ and $z\in\mathbb{D}$.}

Theorem 4
.

Let
T_{z}:\mathcal{D}^{1}\rightarrow B^{0}_{1}
and
M_{z}:B^{0}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{1}
be the Volterra type operator and the multiplication operator, respectively. Then
P
is an operator on
\mathcal{D}^{1}
with

P=T^{-1}_{z}M_{z}T_{z}.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{D}^{1}
and
F=T_{z}f
. Then we get

\displaystyle(Pf)(z)=

\displaystyle(M_{z}f)(z)+(T_{z}f)(z)=zf(z)+F(z)=D(zF(z))=(T_{z}^{-1}M_{z}T_{z}f)(z),

which shows that
P=T^{-1}_{z}M_{z}T_{z}
.

Theorem 5
.

If
f,g\in B_{1}
, then
\|fg\|_{B_{1}}\leq(2\pi+2)\|f\|_{B_{1}}\|g\|_{B_{1}}
.

Proof.

For
f,g\in B_{1}
, we get

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{B_{1}}=

\displaystyle|f(0)g(0)|+|f^{\prime}(0)g^{\prime}(0)|

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(w)g(w)+2f^{\prime}(w)g^{\prime}(w)+f(w)g^{\prime\prime}(w)|\,dA(w)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(|f(0)|+|f^{\prime}(0)|)(|g(0)|+|g^{\prime}(0)|)+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(w)g(w)|\,dA(w)

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(w)g^{\prime}(w)|\,dA(w)+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(w)g^{\prime\prime}(w)|\,dA(w)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|g\|_{B_{1}}(|f(0)|+|f^{\prime}(0)|)+\|g\|_{\infty}(\|f\|_{B_{1}}-|f(0)|-|f^{\prime}(0)|)

\displaystyle+2\|f\|_{B_{1}}\|g\|_{B_{1}}+\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime\prime}(w)|\,dA(w)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|g\|_{B_{1}}(|f(0)|+|f^{\prime}(0)|)+\pi\|g\|_{B_{1}}(\|f\|_{B_{1}}-|f(0)|-|f^{\prime}(0)|)

\displaystyle+2\|f\|_{B_{1}}\|g\|_{B_{1}}+\pi\|f\|_{B_{1}}\|g\|_{B_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2\pi+2)\|f\|_{B_{1}}\|g\|_{B_{1}}

and the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 4
.

In
[
5
, Theorem10]
, Arazy et al. obtained
\|fg\|\leq 7\|f\|\|g\|
for
f,g\in B_{1}
, in which they defined the norm of
f\in B_{1}
as

\|f\|=\inf\left\{\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}|c_{k}|:f(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}c_{k}\sigma_{a_{k}}(z)\right\}.

Inspired by their work, we derived Theorem
5
and we are not sure whether the constant
2\pi+2
in Theorem
5
is optimal or not.

3.
Deddens algebras

Let
\mathcal{L}(X)
denote the algebra of all bounded linear operators on a complex Banach space
X
. A nontrivial invariant subspace of an operator
A\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is, by definition, a closed subspace
M
of
X
such that
M\neq\{0\}
,
M\neq X
, and
Ax\in M
for every
x\in M
; or, briefly,
A(M)\subset M
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{L}(X)
. The operator
T
is said to belong to Deddens algebra
\mathcal{D}_{A}
if there exists
M=M(T)>0
such that

\|A^{n}Tf\|\leq M\|A^{n}f\|

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in X
.

The study of Deddens algebra was originally introduced by Deddens
[
14
]
, where he assumed that
A
is an invertible operator and
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|A^{n}TA^{-n}\|<\infty
. Later, it received the attention of many, see
[
14
,
42
,
18
,
34
,
35
,
33
,
19
,
41
,
36
]
. Recently,
Petrovic
[
36
]
studied the Deddens algebra associated to compact composition operators
C_{\varphi}
on Hardy spaces, where
A
is not necessarily be invertible. And they have demonstrated that the operators
M_{g}
and
T_{z}
belong to the Deddens algebra
\mathcal{D}_{C_{\varphi}}
. It is worth to point out that compact operator on
H^{2}
is not invertible.

Let us begin to present the boundedness of composition operator and multiplication operator on
B_{1}
.

Lemma 6
.

[
44
]

Let
\varphi
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
. Then the composition operator
C_{\varphi}
is bounded on
B_{1}
if and only if

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\|C_{\varphi}\sigma_{a}\|_{B_{1}}<\infty.

In particular, we give another description of sufficiency condition for the boundedness of the composition operator in the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\varphi
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
such that
\varphi(0)=0
. Then the composition operator
C_{\varphi}
is bounded on
B_{1}
whenever
\varphi^{\prime}\in Z_{1}\cap H^{\infty}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\varphi^{\prime}\in Z_{1}\cap H^{\infty}
. Then we get

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi}f\|_{B_{1}}=

\displaystyle\|f(\varphi(z))\|_{B_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|f(\varphi(0))|+|f^{\prime}(\varphi(0))\varphi^{\prime}(0)|+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(\varphi(w))\cdot(\varphi^{\prime}(w))^{2}+f^{\prime}(\varphi(w))\cdot\varphi^{\prime\prime}(w)|\,dA(w)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|f(\varphi(0))|+|f^{\prime}(\varphi(0))\varphi^{\prime}(0)|+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(\varphi(w))\cdot(\varphi^{\prime}(w))^{2}|\,dA(w)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(\varphi(w))\cdot\varphi^{\prime\prime}(w)|\,dA(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\infty}+\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{B_{1}}+\|\varphi^{\prime}\|^{2}_{\infty}\|f\|_{B_{1}}+\|\varphi\|_{Z_{1}}\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{B_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(\|\varphi^{\prime}\|^{2}_{\infty}+\|\varphi\|_{Z_{1}}\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+1)\|f\|_{B_{1}},

which implies that
C_{\varphi}
is bounded on
B_{1}
if
\varphi^{\prime}\in Z_{1}\cap H^{\infty}
.
∎

Theorem 7
.

Suppose that
M_{g}
is multiplication operator on
B_{1}
. Then
M_{g}
is bounded if and only if
g\in B_{1}
.

Proof.

For any
f\in B_{1}
, if
g\in B_{1}
, we have
M_{g}
is bounded on
B_{1},
by Theorem
5
.

Conversely, let
M_{g}
be a bounded operator on
B_{1}.
Then with
f=1
, we get
\|M_{g}\|\geq\|M_{g}1\|_{B_{1}}=\|g\|_{B_{1}}
which implies that
g\in B_{1}
.
∎

In the following theorem, we will consider the algebra
\mathcal{D}_{C_{\varphi}}
, in which the operator
C_{\varphi}
is a bounded composition operator.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it clear that
C^{n}_{\varphi}f=f\circ\varphi\circ\cdots\circ\varphi
. For simplicity of the notation, we write

\varphi_{n}
instead of
\varphi\circ\cdots\circ\varphi
.

Theorem 8
.

Let
g\in B_{1}
and
\varphi
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
with
\varphi(0)=0
such that
C_{\varphi}
is bounded on
B_{1}
. Then the operators
M_{g}
,
T_{g}
and
I_{g}
belong to the Deddens algebra
\mathcal{D}_{C_{\varphi}}
.

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we see that

\displaystyle C^{n}_{\varphi}M_{g}f=

\displaystyle C^{n}_{\varphi}(gf)=(g\circ\varphi_{n})(f\circ\varphi_{n})=M_{g\circ\varphi_{n}}C^{n}_{\varphi}f.

Since
\varphi_{n}(\mathbb{D})\subset\mathbb{D}
, it follows that

\|M_{g\circ\varphi_{n}}f\|_{B_{1}}=\|(g\circ\varphi_{n})f\|_{B_{1}}\lesssim\|g\|_{B_{1}}\|f\|_{B_{1}}

and therefore,

\|C^{n}_{\varphi}M_{g}f\|_{B_{1}}=\|M_{g\circ\varphi_{n}}C^{n}_{\varphi}f\|_{B_{1}}\lesssim\|g\|_{B_{1}}\|C^{n}_{\varphi}f\|_{B_{1}},

where
f\in B_{1}(\mathbb{D})
. This implies that
M_{g}\in\mathcal{D}_{C_{\varphi}}
.

Next, we have

C^{n}_{\varphi}T_{g}f(z)=C^{n}_{\varphi}\left(\int^{z}_{0}f(w)g^{\prime}(w)dw\right)=\int^{\varphi_{n}(z)}_{0}f(w)g^{\prime}(w)\,dw.

Since
\varphi
is an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
satisfying
\varphi(0)=0
, we have
\varphi_{n}(0)=0
and therefore,

\displaystyle T_{g\circ\varphi_{n}}C^{n}_{\varphi}f(z)=

\displaystyle T_{g\circ\varphi_{n}}\left(f(\varphi_{n}(z))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{z}_{0}f(\varphi_{n}(w))g^{\prime}(\varphi_{n}(w))\varphi_{n}^{\prime}(w)\,dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\varphi_{n}(z)}_{0}f(w)g^{\prime}(w)\,dw,

where
f\in B_{1}
.
It shows that
C^{n}_{\varphi}T_{g}=T_{g\circ\varphi_{n}}C^{n}_{\varphi}
.
By Theorem
1
, we have

\displaystyle\|C^{n}_{\varphi}T_{g}f(z)\|_{B_{1}}=

\displaystyle\|T_{g\circ\varphi_{n}}C^{n}_{\varphi}f(z)\|_{B_{1}}\lesssim\|g\circ\varphi_{n}\|_{B_{1}}\|C^{n}_{\varphi}f(z)\|_{B_{1}}\lesssim\|g\|_{B_{1}}\|C^{n}_{\varphi}f(z)\|_{B_{1}}.

which gives
T_{g}\in\mathcal{D}_{C_{\varphi}}
.

Finally, we have

C^{n}_{\varphi}I_{g}f(z)=C^{n}_{\varphi}\left(\int^{z}_{0}f^{\prime}(w)g(w)\,dw\right)=\int^{\varphi_{n}(z)}_{0}f^{\prime}(w)g(w)\,dw

and

\displaystyle I_{g\circ\varphi_{n}}C^{n}_{\varphi}f(z)=

\displaystyle I_{g\circ\varphi_{n}}(f(\varphi_{n}(z)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{z}_{0}f^{\prime}(\varphi_{n}(w))g(\varphi_{n}(w))\varphi_{n}^{\prime}(w)\,dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\varphi_{n}(z)}_{0}f^{\prime}(w)g(w)\,dw,

where
f\in B_{1}
.
Therefore,
C^{n}_{\varphi}I_{g}=I_{g\circ\varphi_{n}}C^{n}_{\varphi}
.
By Theorem
2
and Remark
3
, we find that

\|C^{n}_{\varphi}I_{g}f(z)\|_{B_{1}}=\|I_{g\circ\varphi_{n}}C^{n}_{\varphi}f(z)\|_{B_{1}}\lesssim\|g\circ\varphi_{n}\|_{B_{1}}\|C^{n}_{\varphi}f(z)\|_{B_{1}}\lesssim\|g\|_{B_{1}}\|C^{n}_{\varphi}f(z)\|_{B_{1}}

for all
f\in B_{1}
.
We thus deduce that
I_{g}\in\mathcal{D}_{C_{\varphi}}
.
∎

4.
Essential norms of Volterra type operators on
B_{1}

Suppose
X
is a Banach space and
T
is a bounded linear operator on
X
. The essential norm of
T
is defined to be

\|T\|_{e}=\inf\{\|T-K\|:K~\text{is a compact operator on }B_{1}\}.

Obviously, the essential norm of
T
is 0 if and only if
T
is compact. For more results, we invite the reader to refer to
[
40
,
25
]
.
In this section, we characterize the essential norm of linear operator on
B_{1}
, which generalizes the conclusion of Liu et al.
[
24
]

Theorem 9
.

Every bounded operator
T_{g}
on
B_{1}
is compact.

Proof.

By definition, we know
\|T_{g}\|_{e}\geq 0
.

Next we show that
\|T_{g}\|_{e}\leq 0
. To do this we define the following operators:

T_{g_{r}}f:=\int^{z}_{0}rf(w)g_{r}^{\prime}(w)\,dw,

where
g_{r}(z)=g(rz)
and
r\in(0,1)
. It is easy to see that
T_{g_{r}}
is a compact operator on
B_{1}
for
g\in B_{1}
.
In fact, if
T_{g}
is a bounded operator in
B_{1}
, then
g\in B_{1}
.
Suppose that
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset B_{1}
with
\|f_{n}\|_{B_{1}}\leq 1
, and
f_{n}\rightarrow 0
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
.
Then

\displaystyle\|T_{g_{r}}f_{n}\|_{B_{1}}=

\displaystyle\|f_{n}(rz)g^{\prime}(rz)\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}_{n}(rz)g^{\prime}(rz)|\,dA(z)+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{n}(rz)g^{\prime\prime}(rz)|\,dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\delta_{1}}}|f^{\prime}_{n}(rz)g^{\prime}(rz)|\,dA(z)+\int_{\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathbb{D}_{\delta_{1}}}|f^{\prime}_{n}(rz)g^{\prime}(rz)|\,dA(z)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\delta_{1}}}|f_{n}(rz)g^{\prime\prime}(rz)|\,dA(z)+\int_{\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathbb{D}_{\delta_{1}}}|f_{n}(rz)g^{\prime\prime}(rz)|\,dA(z),

where
\delta_{1}<1,
and
\mathbb{D}_{\delta_{1}}=\{z:|z|<\delta_{1}\}
are compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
.

Note that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}_{n}(rz)g^{\prime}(rz)|\,dA(z)\lesssim\|f_{n}\|_{B_{1}}\|g\|_{B_{1}}~\text{ and }~\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{n}(rz)g^{\prime\prime}(rz)|\,dA(z)\lesssim\|f_{n}\|_{B_{1}}\|g\|_{B_{1}}.

Using the theorem of absolute continuity of Lebegue measure, we conclude that

\int_{\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathbb{D}_{\delta_{1}}}|f^{\prime}_{n}(rz)g^{\prime}(rz)|\,dA(z)<\epsilon~\text{ and }~\int_{\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathbb{D}_{\delta_{1}}}|f_{n}(rz)g^{\prime\prime}(rz)|\,dA(z)<\epsilon.

On the other hand, using a basic result of complex analysis (see [p. 151 of
[
12
]
])
we know that if
f_{n}\rightarrow 0,
then
f_{n}^{\prime}\rightarrow 0
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. Consequently,

\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\delta_{1}}}|f^{\prime}_{n}(rz)g^{\prime}(rz)|\,dA(z)~\text{ and }~\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\delta_{1}}}|f_{n}(rz)g^{\prime\prime}(rz)|\,dA(z)

converge to
0
when
n\rightarrow\infty
. This implies that
T_{g,r}
is compact.

Meanwhile, we have

\|T_{g}\|_{e}\leq\|T_{g}-T_{g_{r}}\|=\|T_{g-g_{r}}\|\lesssim\|g-g_{r}\|_{B_{1}}

for
r\in(0,1)
. Similarly with the above computation, we have

\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\|g-g_{r}\|_{B_{1}}=0

so that
\|T_{g}\|_{e}\leq 0
. Hence we deduce that
\|T_{g}\|_{e}=0
, which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 10
.

If
I_{g}
is bounded operator on
B_{1}
, then
\|I_{g}\|_{e}\approx\|g\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

From the proof of Theorem
2
, we have

\|I_{g}\|_{e}=\inf\|I_{g}-K\|\leq\|I_{g}\|\leq C\|g\|_{\infty},

where
C
is a positive constant.

We next show that
\|I_{g}\|_{e}\geq\|g\|_{\infty}
.
Choose
a_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
such that
|a_{n}|\rightarrow 1
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Let
f_{n}(z)=\sigma_{a_{n}}(z)-a_{n}
.
It is obvious that
\|f_{n}\|_{B_{1}}=1
. Since
\{f_{n}\}
converges to zero uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, for every compact operator
K
on
B_{1}
, we obtain
\|Kf_{n}\|_{B_{1}}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|I_{g}-K\|

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup\|(I_{g}-K)f_{n}\|_{B_{1}}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup(\|I_{g}f_{n}\|_{B_{1}}-\|Kf_{n}\|_{B_{1}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup\|I_{g}f_{n}\|_{B_{1}}.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
2
, we get

\displaystyle\|I_{g}f_{n}\|_{B_{1}}=\|g(z)f^{{}^{\prime}}_{n}(z)\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}\geq|g(\sigma_{a_{n}}(0))|=|g(a_{n})|.

As the choice of the sequence
\{a_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
is arbitrary, we have
\|I_{g}\|_{e}\geq\|g\|_{\infty}
, which is completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 11
.

Every bounded operator
M_{g}
on
B_{1}
is compact.

Proof.

The proof is similar to Theorem
9
, so we omit its details.
∎

5.
Spectrum of Volterra type operators on
B_{1}(\mathbb{D})

The spectrum of integral operators in different spaces has attracted the attention of many scholars. The spectrum of integral operator on weighted Bergman space are characterized by Aleman and Constantin
[
2
]
.
Later, Constantin
[
10
]
obtained the spectrum of Volterra type operators on Fock spaces.
Mengestie
[
28
]
studied of the spectrum of Volterra type operators on Fock-Sobolev spaces. Mengestie
[
29
]
also obtained the spectrum of
T_{g}
in
terms of a closed disk of radius twice the coefficient of the highest degree term in a polynomial expansion of
g
. For more results, see
[
27
,
8
]
.
Recently, Lin et al. described the spectrum of the multiplication operator and Volterra type operator
I_{g}
in
[
22
]
, respectively.
Inspired by the above results, it is natural to discuss the spectrum of the multiplication operator and the Volterra type operators in
B_{1}
.

Theorem 12
.

Suppose that
M_{g}
is bounded on
B_{1}
. Then we have

\sigma(M_{g})=\overline{g(\mathbb{D})}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\lambda\notin\sigma(M_{g})
. Then
M_{g}-\lambda I
is invertible. As
1\in B_{1},
there exists an
f\in B_{1}
such that
(g(z)-\lambda)f(z)=1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, which implies that
\lambda\notin g(\mathbb{D})
. Thus,
g(\mathbb{D})\subset\sigma(M_{g})
.

For the other way inclusion, we let
\lambda\notin\overline{g(\mathbb{D})}
. Then we can choose a
t>0
such that
|g(z)-\lambda|>t
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. This shows that

h=(g-\lambda)^{-1}
is a bounded analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
. For all
g\in B_{1}
, we get

\displaystyle\|h\|_{B_{1}}\leq

\displaystyle t(|h(0)|+\|h^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|g(0)-\lambda|}+\|\frac{g^{\prime}}{(g-\lambda)^{2}}\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|g(0)-\lambda|}+\frac{g^{\prime}(0)}{(g(0)-\lambda)^{2}}+\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{(g(z)-\lambda)^{2}}-\frac{2(g^{\prime})^{2}}{(g(z)-\lambda)^{3}}\right|\,dA(z)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|g\|_{B_{1}}+\|g\|^{2}_{B_{1}}.

Hence,
h\in B_{1}
. Then
M_{h}
is bounded on
B_{1},
by Theorem
7
.
Since
M_{h}=M_{(g-\lambda)^{-1}}=M_{g-\lambda}^{-1}
, we see that
M_{g-\lambda}
is invertible and thus,
\lambda\notin\sigma(M_{g})
.
Therefore,
\sigma(M_{g})\subset\overline{g(\mathbb{D})}
. Since the spectrum set is closed, we conclude that
\sigma(M_{g})=\overline{g(\mathbb{D})}
.
∎

Lemma 7
.

[
39
]

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a Banach space
X
, and
T
be compact. If
\dim X=\infty
, then
\sigma(T)=\{0\}\cup\{\mbox{eigen values of $T$}\}
.

Theorem 13
.

Suppose that
T_{g}
is a bounded operator in
B_{1}
. Then
\sigma(T_{g})=\{0\}.

Proof.

Let
T_{g}
be a bounded operator on
B_{1}.
Then
T_{g}
is compact, by Theorem
9
. By Lemma
7
, we obtain
0\in\sigma(T)
.

Next we prove that
T_{g}
has no non-zero eigenvalue. Assume that
T_{g}
has an eigenvalue
\lambda\neq 0
with eigenvector
f.

Then

(1)

T_{g}f(z)=\int^{z}_{0}f(w)g^{\prime}(w)\,dw=\lambda f(z).

Differentiating the equation (
1
) with respect to
z
, we get

f(z)g^{\prime}(z)=\lambda f^{\prime}(z).

All nonzero solutions of this equation are of the form
f(z)=ce^{\frac{g(z)}{\lambda}}
for some
c\neq 0
. Setting
z=0
in (
1
) shows that
0=\lambda f(0)
,
which contradicts the last relation about
f.
Therefore, there is no non-zero eigenvalue for
T_{g}
. From this, we deduce that
\sigma(T_{g})=\{0\}
.
∎

Theorem 14
.

If
I_{g}
is a bounded operator in
B_{1}
, then

\sigma(I_{g})=\{0\}\cup\overline{g(\mathbb{D})}.

Proof.

For any constant function
a
, we have

(I_{g}a)(z)=\int_{0}^{z}a^{\prime}(w)g(w)\,dw=0

which gives
0\in\sigma(I_{g})
.

Suppose that
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
. Note that the equation

f-\frac{1}{\lambda}I_{g}f=h,~\hbox{for}~h\in B_{1},

has a unique analytic solution

f(z)=R_{\lambda,g}h(z)=\int^{z}_{0}\frac{h^{\prime}(\xi)}{1-\frac{1}{\lambda}g(\xi)}\,d\xi+h(0)=I_{(1-\frac{1}{\lambda}g)^{-1}}h(z)+h(0).

(see
[
11
]
for more details). Hence, the resolvent set
\rho(I_{g})
of the bounded operator
I_{g}
consists precisely of all points
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
for which
R_{\lambda,g}
is a bounded operator on
B_{1}
.

If
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash(\{0\}\cup\overline{g(D)})
, then it is clear that
1-\frac{1}{\lambda}g(z)
is bounded away from
0
, which implies that
\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{\lambda}g(z)}\in H^{\infty}
. If
I_{g}
is a bounded operator in
B_{1}
, then
g\in H^{\infty}\bigcap Z_{1}
by Theorem
2
.
Moreover, it is easy to show that
\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{\lambda}g(z)}\in Z_{1}.

This implies that the operator
R_{\lambda,g}
is a bounded operator on
B_{1}
. It follows that
\mathbb{C}\backslash(\{0\}\cup\overline{g(\mathbb{D})})\subset\rho(I_{g})
. Thus,
\sigma(I_{g})\subset(\{0\}\cup\overline{g(\mathbb{D})})
.

On the other hand, if
\lambda\in g(\mathbb{D})
and
\lambda\neq 0
, then
\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{\lambda}g(\xi)}
is not bounded, which shows that the operator
R_{\lambda,g}
is not bounded on
B_{1}
. Therefore, we obtain that
g(\mathbb{D})\backslash\{0\}\subset\sigma(I_{g})
. This together with the fact that
0\in\sigma(I_{g})
shows that

g(\mathbb{D})\cup\{0\}\subset\sigma(I_{g})\subset\overline{g(\mathbb{D})}\cup\{0\}.

Since the spectrum
\sigma(I_{g})
is closed, we deduce that
\sigma(I_{g})=\overline{g(\mathbb{D})}\cup\{0\}
.
∎

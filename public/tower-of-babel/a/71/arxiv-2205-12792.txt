Abstract
. This article is part of an ongoing investigation of the two-dimensional Jacobian conjecture. In the first paper of this series, we proved the generalized Magnus’ formula. In this paper, inspired by cluster algebras, we introduce a sequence of new conjectures including the remainder vanishing conjecture. This makes the generalized Magnus’ formula become a useful tool to show the two-dimensional Jacobian conjecture. In the forthcoming paper(s), we plan to prove the remainder vanishing conjecture.

Jacobian conjecture.

Let

\pi(x)=f

\pi(y)=g

If

A useful tool to study this conjecture is the Newton polygon. One source for this is
[

]

f=\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq 0}f_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
be a polynomial in
\mathcal{R}

. The

Define the

for

, denoted

\operatorname{supp}(f)
. The
augmented Newton polygon
for
f\in\mathcal{R}\setminus\{0\}
, denoted

, is the convex hull of
\operatorname{supp}(f)\cup\{(0,0)\}
.

Conjecture A
.

Let

Suppose that

(1)

In

Conjecture 6.1
.

Let

be such that
a\not|b
and

(2)
\text{deg}(C)=1

As suggested by Christian Valqui, it would be interesting to see if the generalized Magnus’ formula could help solve the systems of polynomial equations obtained from the condition (4).

Condensation, boundary conditions, and effects of slow sites in zero-range systems
Sunder Sethuraman, Jianfei Xue

Abstract
. We consider the space-time scaling limit of the particle mass in zero-range particle systems on a 1 1 D discrete torus ℤ / N ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z} with a finite number of defects. We focus on two classes of increasing jump rates g g , when g ⁡ ( n ) ∼ n α g(n)\sim n^{\alpha} , for 0 < α ≤ 1 0<\alpha\leq 1 , and when g g is a bounded function. In such a model, a particle at a regular site k k jumps equally likely to a neighbor with rate g ⁡ ( n ) g(n) , depending only on the number of particles n n at k k . At a defect site k j , N k_{j,N} , however, the jump rate is slowed down to λ j − 1 ​ N − β j ​ g ​ ( n ) \lambda_{j}^{-1}N^{-\beta_{j}}g(n) when g ⁡ ( n ) ∼ n α g(n)\sim n^{\alpha} , and to λ j − 1 ​ g ​ ( n ) \lambda_{j}^{-1}g(n) when g g is bounded. Here, N N is a scaling parameter where the grid spacing is seen as 1 / N 1/N and time is speeded up by N 2 N^{2} . Starting from initial measures with O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) relative entropy with respect to an invariant measure, we show the hydrodynamic limit and characterize boundary behaviors at the macroscopic defect sites x j = lim N ↑ ∞ k j , N / N x_{j}=\lim_{N\uparrow\infty}k_{j,N}/N , for all defect strengths. For rates g ⁡ ( n ) ∼ n α g(n)\sim n^{\alpha} , at critical or super-critical slow sites ( β j = α \beta_{j}=\alpha or β j > α \beta_{j}>\alpha ), associated Dirichlet boundary conditions arise as a result of interactions with evolving atom masses or condensation at the defects. Differently, when g g is bounded, at any slow site ( λ j > 1 \lambda_{j}>1 ), we find the hydrodynamic density must be bounded above by a threshold value reflecting the strength of the defect. Moreover, due to interactions with masses of atoms stored at the slow sites, the associated boundary conditions bounce between being periodic and Dirichlet.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this article is to understand the macroscopic boundary conditions which arise in hydrodynamic scaling limits for the space-time ‘bulk’ mass evolution of zero-range processes with a finite number of ‘defects’, and also related effects of ‘condensation’ at these defect locations. Such an aim more broadly fits into the larger study of how macroscopic boundary conditions emerge from inhomogeneous microscopic interactions.

In this view, there has been much interesting work on
one-dimensional (
1
D) exclusion models where a site or bond is ‘slowed’ down.
In
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
, in computing the hydrodynamic limit in symmetric systems, different boundary conditions from Dirichlet to Neumann, and also Robin have been derived; see also
[
10
]
.
There are however only a few works with respect to different interactions, in particular zero-range systems, which do not limit the particle numbers at a location.
Among these,
[
17
]
studies the hydrodynamic limit for a system of
1
D symmetric independent particles moving in a (random) trap environment. In
[
19
]
, with respect to a
1
D totally asymmetric zero-range process, with bounded, increasing rate function
g(\cdot)
, effects of a slow site and a slow particle are found when starting from a ‘flat’ initial measure.
In
[
4
]
(see also
[
5
]
), with respect to a class of such
1
D totally asymmetric zero-range systems, however with a nontrivial density of site disorders, hydrodynamics is shown with respect to an effective flux function, constant at supercritical densities; see also the conjectures in Section 3.3 in
[
4
]
about slow sites in asymmetric models.

We also mention that
1
D systems with ‘reservoir’ boundaries have been studied; see
[
9
]
for a discussion of ‘hydrostatics’, and related references, with respect to exclusion models.
In zero-range processes, ‘static’ reservoir effects are studied and dynamical conjectures are discussed in
[
15
]
.

In this context, the general goal of our work is to consider the effect of a finite number of ‘slow’ sites in a class of
1
D symmetric zero-range systems in a torus
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
.
In such a model, particles would ‘condense’ on a defect site if jump rates from it are ‘slow’ enough. One would expect that for the particle continuum mass, different boundary conditions would result depending on how ‘slow’ the defect is.
Our main results describe corresponding hydrodynamic limits, in terms of a nonlinear parabolic PDE and evolving point masses, with specified boundary conditions at defects reflecting different types of condensation. To our knowledge, these are the first results for a general class of zero-range interactions, with bounded and unbounded rate functions
g
. In particular, the behaviors found differ depending on the structure of the rate
g
. The proof method, in the scheme of the ‘entropy’ method, specifies local ‘replacements’ which may be useful in other problems.

By specifying the locations of the ‘slow’ defects in the system, we fix the macroscopically separated points where ‘condensation’ can occur. There seems to be little work on the dynamical structure in such systems.
This is in contrast to the well-developed study of ‘condensation’, which ‘spontaneously’ forms at a random location by introducing more particles in a zero-range system with a bounded decreasing rate function than is allowed to equilibriate.
See
[
2
]
for a discussion of both mechanisms with respect to canonical and grand canonical invariant measures of a bounded rate zero-range process with a single defect.

In passing, we mention, among the recent literature on the ‘spontaneous’ formation of ‘condensation’,
[
3
]
considers, with respect to a thermodynamic limit in
1
D symmetric zero-range models,
the evolution of the random ‘condensate’ in a certain time-scale.
In
[
20
]
, with respect to
1
D asymmetric dynamics in a set of
L
fixed sites, motion of the ‘condensate’ is described. In
[
6
]
, a ‘martingale problem’ approach for the condensate dynamics is developed. In
[
23
]
, some partial results on a hydrodynamic limit is given. See also references therein in these papers for a more complete history of the subject.
For related notions of ‘metastability’, see books and surveys
[
7
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
24
]
.

1.1.
Sketch of results

To describe our results,
we consider zero-range processes on the torus

{\mathbb{T}}_{N}:=\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}
where the site
N
(the right neighbor of
N-1
) is identified as
0
.
The jump rate functions
g:\mathbb{N}_{0}\mapsto[0,\infty)
focused upon satisfy
\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}g(n)/n^{\alpha}=1
(to fix a time-scale) and
\alpha\in[0,1]
.
Moreover, we classify the
g
’s into two families: (1)
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}
when
0<\alpha\leq 1
, and (2)
g
is bounded.
In both settings, we will assume also that
g
is a Lipschitz, increasing function, which rules out the case
\alpha>1
. Such an assumption is convenient to bound the rate
g
in estimates, and also allows ‘attractive’ process couplings; see Section
3.2

for a discussion of the use of ‘attractive’ coupling.

In this process, at a regular site
k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
, if there are
n
particles there, one of them leaves with rate
g(n)
to a neighbor, jumping either to
k-1
or
k+1
, with equal probability.
However, at a defect site
k
, the departure rate is altered as follows: Let
\lambda>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.
If there are
n
particles at the defect
k
, one of them leaves at rate
\tfrac{1}{\lambda N^{\beta}}g(n)
.
We will say the rate is ‘slow’ when
\beta>0
, or
\beta=0
and
\lambda>1
.

The zero-range system tracks the evolution of the unlabeled particles on
\mathbb{T}_{N}
. We denote by
\xi_{t}=\{\xi_{t}(k):k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\}
the configuration of the process, where
\xi_{t}(k)
is the number of particles at site
k
at time
t\geq 0
. Given the symmetric transitions, it will be useful to define also the speeded-up process
\eta_{t}=\xi_{N^{2}t}
.
For this Markov system, there is a family of product (reversible) invariant measures
\mathscr{R}^{N}_{c}
, indexed by an interval of ‘density’ parameters
c
;
see Section
2.1
.

For the system with rate
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}
, we will start the process from measures
\mu^{N}
, associated to an initial macroscopic measure
\pi_{0}
on the unit torus
\mathbb{T}=[0,1)
, with
O(N)
relative entropy with respect to an invariant distribution
\mathscr{R}^{N}_{c_{0}}
, and stochastically bounded by another invariant distribution
\mathscr{R}^{N}_{c^{\prime}}
. With respect to bounded rates
g
,
\mu^{N}
satisfies a similar but slightly different criteria, since when

\|g\|_{\infty}=g_{\infty}<\infty
, there will be a finite effective critical density above which the invariant measure is not defined; see Condition
3.1
.

Here, in the setting
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}
, the initial profile
\pi_{0}
will be in form

\pi_{0}(dx)=\rho_{0}(x)dx+\sum_{j:\beta_{j}=\alpha}{\mathfrak{m}}_{0,j}\delta_{x_{j}}(dx),

with a similar formulation in the
g
bounded setting.
Examples of suitable initial measures
\mu^{N}
are given by local equilibrium product measures; see Section
3.3
.

1.1.1.
Rates
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}

To describe the main result in the setting
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}
, it will be helpful to get a sense of the ‘condensation’ of particles, under an invariant measure
\mathscr{R}^{N}_{c}
.
Typically at a slow site, (1) the number of particles will be
O(N^{\beta/\alpha})
when
\beta>\alpha
, referred to as ‘super-slow’ or ‘super-critical’, (2) order
O(N)
when
\beta=\alpha
, called ‘critical’, and (3)
o(N)
when
\beta<\alpha
, named as ‘sub-critical’. So, with a finite number of slow sites, if one of the
k_{j,N}
’s is ‘super-slow’,
there will be a superlinear

O(N^{\beta_{j}/\alpha})
number of particles at that location. Whereas, when all the
\beta_{j}\leq\alpha
, there will be
O(N)
particles on
\mathbb{T}_{N}
. See Section
2.2
for precise statements.

Let
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}
be the set of super-slow sites
k_{j,N}
,
and let
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}
be the corresponding set of continuum points
x_{j}\sim k_{j,N}/N
. Since, at these locations, the particle numbers are superlinear, we omit them in the definition of the empirical measure with respect to the diffusively scaled system:

\pi^{N}_{t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}}\eta_{t}(k)\delta_{k/N}.

By a hydrodynamic limit, we mean, with respect to test functions
G\in C(\mathbb{T})
, that

\langle G,\pi^{N}_{t}\rangle
converges in probability to
\langle G,\pi_{t}\rangle
, where
\pi_{t}
is a measure-valued weak solution of a specified macroscopic evolution.

Our first result (Theorem
4.1
) is that three different behaviors and boundary conditions, at the macroscopic defect sites
x_{j}
, arise in the hydrodynamic limit depending on when
\beta_{j}>\alpha
,
\beta_{j}=\alpha
and
\beta_{j}<\alpha
. As might be suspected, when
\beta_{j}<\alpha
, the defect is not ‘slow’ enough to be seen in the continuum limit. However, defects where
\beta_{j}\geq\alpha
on the other hand do register. To describe the bulk mass macroscopic flow, segregate the unit torus
\mathbb{T}
into intervals with endpoints
x_{j}
corresponding to
\beta_{j}\geq\alpha
. In the interior of each interval, the hydrodynamic limit is described by a nonlinear heat equation

(1.1)

\partial_{t}\rho=\partial_{xx}\Phi(\rho).

Here, the ‘fugacity’ function
\Phi
is a continuum homogenization of the microscopic rate
g
(cf. (
2.4
)).
We note this equation also arises in the context of the zero-range system without disorder. (cf. Chapter 4
[
18
]
).

However, at a defect point
x_{j}
where
\beta_{j}=\alpha
, an atom evolves with
a mass given by
\mathfrak{m}_{j}(t)=\big\{\lambda_{j}\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))\big\}^{1/\alpha}
, in terms of
\rho
. Such a statement is natural, given that under
\mu^{N}
, not far from
\mathscr{R}^{N}_{c_{0}}
, there are
O(N)
particles at
k_{j,N}
.
Differently, when
\beta_{j}>\alpha
, we will observe initially a superlinear
O(N^{\beta_{j}/\alpha})
number of particles at
k_{j,N}
due to the relative entropy bound assumption.
This level of condensation will not evolve much for
t>0
and, as a result, leads to a macroscopic boundary condition
\rho(t,x_{j})=c_{0}
where
c_{0}
is the parameter of the reference measure

\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}
, reflecting a level of initial condensation at
x_{j}
. The derivation of such Dirichlet boundary condition at super-slow sites (
\beta_{j}>\alpha
), to our knowledge, is novel.

Taken together, the hydrodynamic limit
\pi_{t}
on
\mathbb{T}
will be in form

\pi_{t}=\rho(t,x)dx+\sum_{j:\beta_{j}=\alpha}\mathfrak{m}_{j}(t)\delta_{x_{j}}(dx),\quad\text{for $t>0$.}

At time
t=0
,
\pi_{t}
reduces to
\pi_{0}
mentioned earlier.
In general, the PDE (
1.1
) for the bulk mass
\rho
will not be closed without
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)
being specified.
In fact, the limit
\pi_{t}
is characterized as the unique weak solution to a system in terms of
(\rho(t,x),\{\mathfrak{m}_{j}(t)\}_{j})
:

(1.2)

\partial_{t}\pi_{t}=\partial_{xx}\Phi(\rho)

with boundary behaviors of
\rho(t,x)
at slow sites
x_{j}
given earlier;
see Definition
4.4
and uniqueness Theorem
11.1
. See Example
4.7
, for a specific discussion when there is only one defect in the system.
We mention in
[
17
]
, when
g(n)\equiv n

(
\alpha=1
), that is for independent particles, a case of the above hydrodynamic limit may be inferred when
\beta_{j}\equiv\alpha=1
, with
\Phi(u)\equiv u
.

1.1.2.
Bounded rates
g

Our second result (Theorem
4.2
) concerns the process with
a rate function
g(n)
, bounded and
increasing say to level
g_{\infty}=1
in the limit as
n\uparrow\infty
.
Slow sites
k_{j,N}
that we consider will be those where the jump rate is
\tfrac{1}{\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}}g(n)=\tfrac{1}{\lambda_{j}}g(n)
, when
\beta_{j}=0
and
\lambda_{j}>1
,
when there are
n
particles at
k
. As before, with a finite number of slow sites
k_{j,N}
, there is a family of product invariant measures
\{\mathscr{R}^{N}_{c}\}
, but now with densities
c
limited to
0\leq\max_{j}\{\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}\vee 1\}\Phi(c)\leq g_{\infty}
. There is no non-trivial product invariant measure for
c
above this level. With this understanding, it is natural to focus on
\beta_{j}=0
, as if we would slow down with

\beta_{j}>0
, there would be no such non-trivial invariant measure, as the fugacity
\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}\Phi(c)
would exceed
g_{\infty}
for a finite
N
.

Phenomenologically, the slow site effect is different here than when
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}

in that the system and in particular the slow sites under
\mathscr{R}^{N}_{c}
have at most
O(N)
particles on them. As before, between macroscopic defects
\{x_{j}\sim k_{j,N}/N\}
, the hydrodynamic limit satisfies (
1.1
). An atom of mass
\mathfrak{m}_{j}(t)
may also form though at macroscopic site
x_{j}\sim k_{j,N}/N
where
\beta_{j}=0
and
\lambda_{j}>1
.

However, unlike in the case

g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}
, this atomic mass may be evanescent: We show that, at such a defect
x_{j}
, the hydrodynamic density
\rho(t,x_{j})
satisfies a bound

\rho(t,x_{j})\leq c_{j,\max}
where

c_{j,\max}:=\Phi^{-1}(g_{\infty}/\lambda_{j})
for all
t>0
.
When this inequality is strict, it holds necessarily that
\mathfrak{m}_{j}(t)=0
.
In particular, the boundary condition near
x_{j}
may bounce between being periodic and Dirichlet.

For an informal description, suppose that
\rho(t,x_{j})
is strictly less than
c_{j,\max}
at
t=t_{0}
and

{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t_{0})=0
.
Here, the macroscopic flow will evolve as if the defect at
x_{j}
were not present,
namely, we will observe a periodic boundary condition at
x=x_{j}
.
This will last until the first moment
t=t_{1}
when the density
\rho(t,x_{j})
tends toward exceeding the threshold value
c_{j,\max}
.
The defect is then ‘set off’ to keep
\rho(t,x_{j})
at the value
c_{j,\max}
, with any excess mass stored at the defect to form a delta mass.
In other words, we start to observe
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)>0
as well as a Dirichlet boundary condition
\rho(t,x_{j})=c_{j,\max}
after
t=t_{1}
. Besides being a place of storage, the slow site
x_{j}
also serves as a source of mass to maintain the mentioned Dirichlet boundary when needed as long as
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)>0
. When the stored mass is used up (when
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)
becomes
0
) and the density
\rho(t,x_{j})
tends to drop below
c_{j,\max}
, say at
t=t_{3}
, the boundary condition switches to the periodic one again after
t=t_{3}
. Such a ‘bouncing’ phenomenon between boundary conditions takes place at all slow sites.
Finally, characterization of the limit

\pi_{t}=\rho(t,x)dx+\sum_{j:\beta_{j}=0,\lambda_{j}>0}\mathfrak{m}_{j}(t)\delta_{x_{j}}(dx)

is given through a weak formulation (
1.2
) with boundary conditions described earlier, shown to have a unique solution; see Definition
4.5
and uniqueness Theorem
11.2
.

We remark that a sufficient condition to not feel the defects
\{x_{j}\}
in the limit would be to start the process from initial (local equilibrium) measures
\mu^{N}
associated to
\pi_{0}(dx)=\rho_{0}(x)dx
, where
\|\rho_{0}\|_{\infty}<\Phi^{-1}\big([\max_{j}\{\lambda_{j}\}]^{-1}\big)
, so that by say attractiveness
\rho(t,x)
also satisfies this bound. On the other hand, a sufficient condition so that atom masses form is that the initial density
\rho_{0}(x)>\Phi^{-1}\big([\max_{j}\{\lambda_{j}\}]^{-1}\big)
on
\mathbb{T}
: Indeed, if no atoms are formed, by the maximum principle,
\rho(t,x)>\Phi^{-1}\big([\max_{j}\{\lambda_{j}\}]^{-1}\big)
on
\mathbb{T}
, a contradiction. We believe the results in Theorem
4.2
are the first for a general class of bounded symmetric zero-range processes with defects.

1.2.
Proof ideas for Theorems
4.1
and
4.2

We now discuss ideas in the proofs. We use the general scheme of the ‘entropy’ method, discussed in
[
18
]
, although there are several departures since the dynamics is not translation invariant. In particular, mixing in time estimates are used to make microscopic to macroscopic homogenizations in the ‘bulk’. However, to capture the boundary effects and correspondences with macroscopic mass of atoms, we need to estimate the local behavior near defects, for which we give a more refined argument.

Let
G
be a test function on
\mathbb{T}
when
g
is bounded, and with compact support away from super-slow sites
x_{j}\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}
when
g\sim n^{\alpha}
. Consider the stochastic differential

d\langle G,\pi^{N}_{t}\rangle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\Delta_{N}G\big(k/N\big)g_{k}(\eta_{t}(k))dt+dM^{N}(t).

Here,
g_{k}=\tfrac{1}{\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}}g
when
k=k_{j,N}
and
g_{k}=g
otherwise.
Also,
M^{N}(t)
is a martingale, which will vanish as
N\uparrow\infty
.
We state a ‘bulk’ replacement in Lemma
8.4
. The proof that we give makes use of local replacements, as these will be useful to deduce boundary behaviors at the defects.

In particular, we may replace the local function
g(\eta_{t}(k))
at regular sites
k
by
\Phi\big(\eta^{\theta N}_{t}\big)
where
\eta^{\ell}
is the
\ell
-window average
\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\sum_{|y-k|\leq\ell}\eta(y)
, by use of an entropy inequality and a ‘Rayleigh’ estimate. The ‘Rayleigh’ estimate controls the difference between expectations of a local function under non-equilibrium and equilibrium measures in terms of a Dirichlet form and a term written in terms of the spectral gap of the process localized to an interval. No special bound is required–this gap needs only to be positive, or equivalently that the localized dynamics is ergodic. We remark, as a technical device, attractiveness is used to handle large densities, difficult to analyze otherwise, in the proofs of these local replacement, ‘1-block’ Lemma
8.1
and ‘2-block’ Lemma
8.2
. As a consequence of these estimates, the hydrodynamic equation (
1.1
) may be derived in the ‘bulk’. We now discuss derivation of the boundary conditions in the two settings.

1.2.1.
Boundary derivations for
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}

In the setting where

g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}

at a defect
k_{j,N}
, when
0<\beta_{j}<\alpha
, under
\mu^{N}
, the number of
particles
\eta_{t}(k_{j,N})=O(N^{\beta_{j}/\alpha})
is sublinear in
N
. Hence, with respect to the empirical measure, the term

\langle G,N^{-1}\delta_{k_{j,N}/N}\rangle=N^{-1}\eta_{t}(k_{j,N})G(k_{j,N}/N)\leq\|G\|_{\infty}N^{-1}\eta_{t}(k_{j,N})=O(N^{-1+\beta_{j}/\alpha})

vanishes as
N\uparrow\infty
. In effect, we can ignore such defects in the continuum limit.

However, when
\beta_{j}\geq\alpha
, to deduce a non-trivial boundary condition, we consider the rate
g_{k_{j,N}}(\eta_{t}(k_{j,N}))=\tfrac{1}{\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}}g(\eta_{t}(k_{j,N})
at the slow site
k_{j,N}
. The following discussion leading to (
1.3
) will hold actually for both
g
of
n^{\alpha}
type and
g
bounded settings. To fix ideas, let us say
k_{j,N}=0
, the origin. Under
\mu^{N}
, not far from the reversible
\mathscr{R}^{N}_{c_{0}}
in terms of relative entropy, we show that this rate is close to the neighboring rate
g(\eta_{t}(1))
say; see Lemma
9.1
. By the local ‘1-block’ estimate, we show that
g(\eta_{t}(1))
is close to
\Phi(\eta^{\ell,+}_{t}(0))
, where
\eta_{t}^{\ell,+}(0)=\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{y=1}^{\ell}\eta_{t}(y)
is the average in the
\ell
-block to the right of the defect site
0
; see Lemma
9.2
. Now, by the local ‘2-block’ bound, we have
\Phi(\eta^{\ell,+}_{t}(0))
is close to the macroscopic quantity
\Phi((\eta^{\theta N,+}_{t}(0))
for
\theta>0
small. Together, we conclude the fundamental relation,

(1.3)

\Phi(\eta^{\theta N,+}_{t}(0))\sim g_{0}(\eta_{t}(0)).

After establishment of tightness of the empirical measure trajectories, and absolute continuity estimates in Lemmas
6.1
and
7.1
, we may consider
a subsequential limit
\pi
on
\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}
in the form
\pi=\rho(t,x)dx+\sum_{j:\beta_{j}=\alpha}\mathfrak{m}_{j}(t)
. From (
1.3
), one has for this limit point that

\Phi(\rho(t,0))\sim g_{0}(\eta_{t}(0)).

When
\beta_{j}=\alpha
, we have

\Phi(\rho(t,0))\sim g_{0}(\eta_{t}(0))\sim\big(N^{-1}\eta_{t}(0)\big)^{\alpha}\lambda_{j}^{-1}\sim\big({\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)\big)^{\alpha}\lambda_{j}^{-1},

which is the boundary condition relating the atom strength to the local density in Theorem
4.1
; see Lemma
7.4
.

When
\beta_{j}>\alpha
, we may take the same passage to obtain

\Phi(\rho(t,0))\sim g_{0}(\eta_{t}(0))\sim\big(N^{-\beta_{j}/\alpha}\eta_{t}(0)\big)^{\alpha}\lambda_{j}^{-1}.

But, in our time-scale, we show
N^{-\beta_{j}/\alpha}\eta_{t}(0)
does not vary much from the time
t=0
quantity
N^{-\beta_{j}/\alpha}\eta_{0}(0)
. For instance, if
k_{j,N}=0
were the only defect in the system, by mass conservation,

|\eta_{t}(0)-\eta_{0}(0)|=\big|\sum_{k\neq 0}\big(\eta_{t}(k)-\eta_{0}(k)\big)\big|.

Starting under
\mu^{N}
, by use of the entropy inequality,
\sum_{k\neq 0}\eta_{t}(k)=O(N)
for all
t\geq 0
. Hence,

N^{-\beta_{j}/\alpha}\big|\eta_{t}(0)-\eta_{0}(0)\big|=O(N^{-\beta_{j}/\alpha+1})

vanishes as
N\uparrow\infty
. Moreover, by a calculation with respect to the initial measure
\mu^{N}
, we may show that

\big(N^{-\beta_{j}/\alpha}\eta_{0}(0)\big)^{\alpha}\sim\lambda_{j}\Phi(c_{0}).

As a consequence, we have

\Phi(\rho(t,0))\sim\big(N^{-\beta_{j}/\alpha}\eta_{0}(0)\big)^{\alpha}\lambda_{j}^{-1}\sim\Phi(c_{0}),

yielding the boundary behavior in Theorem
4.1
;
see Lemma
7.2
which addresses the multiple defect setting.

1.2.2.
Boundary derivations for bounded rate
g

We now discuss the case of bounded rate function
g
:
\|g\|_{\infty}=g_{\infty}<\infty
. There are subtleties here with respect to boundary estimates, different than in the
n^{\alpha}
rate setting, although the bulk hydrodynamic limit between defects
x_{j}\sim k_{j,N}/N
follows the same procedure as above to derive the equation (
1.1
).

At a slow site
k_{j,N}
, say again at the origin,
slowed down by
\lambda_{j}^{-1}
(and
\beta_{j}=0
), we deduce

\Phi(\rho(t,0))\sim\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}g(\eta_{t}(0))\leq\frac{g_{\infty}}{\lambda_{j}}.

Hence, the density near the slow site
x_{j}=0
is restricted,

\rho(t,0)\leq\Phi^{-1}(g_{\infty}/\lambda_{j}).

When the restriction is strict, the intuition is that the particle mass is not high enough to impact much the
flow of particle numbers through the slow site
0
. However, when equality in the above relation holds,
an atom at the slow site
0
may form to store excess mass while maintaining the boundary condition. More precisely, we have
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)
, the atomic mass accumulated by
x_{j}=0
, satisfies
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)={\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{\rho(t,0)=\Phi^{-1}(g_{\infty}/\lambda_{j})}
; this is established by showing a microscopic version of the following key relation

{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)\big(\Phi^{-1}(g_{\infty}/\lambda_{j})-\rho(t,0)\big)=0;

see Lemma
7.5
.
These prescriptions give the boundary conditions specified in Theorem
4.2
.

2.
Model description

We will consider symmetric zero-range processes on the discrete torus
\mathbb{T}_{N}:=\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}=\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}
with a finite number
n_{0}\geq 0
of defects located in
\mathbb{T}_{N}
.
We will always assume that
N>n_{0}
so that there is enough space in
\mathbb{T}_{N}
to record the defects.

More carefully, the structure of the defects is the following:
Let
J
be the index set
\{1,2,\ldots,n_{0}\}
.
For each
j\in J
, fix
(x_{j},\beta_{j},\lambda_{j})
such that

x_{j}\in\mathbb{T}
,
\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda_{j}\in(0,\infty)
.
Here,
\mathbb{T}
stands for
[0,1)
viewed as the unit torus.
We will assume that all
x_{j}
’s are different, that is macroscopically separated.
For each
j
, the point
x_{j}
denotes the macroscopic location of a defect and
(\beta_{j},\lambda_{j})
characterizes its strength.
Let
{\mathfrak{D}}:=\{x_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be the set of all macroscopic defect locations.
For each
j\in J
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
we now define
k_{j,N}=\lfloor x_{j}N\rfloor
be the integer part of
x_{j}N
.
Then, the set
{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}:=\{k_{j,N}\}_{j\in J}

stands for the set of microscopic locations of the defects.

Let now
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
.
For each
N
, let
\Omega_{N}:=\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\mathbb{T}_{N}}
be the space of all particle configurations on
\mathbb{T}_{N}
.
With respect to
\xi\in\Omega_{N}
,
at a normal site
k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
, a particle jumps to neighboring sites
k\pm 1
equally likely at rate
g(\xi(k))/\xi(k)
where
\xi(k)
is the number of particles at site
k
, and
g:\mathbb{N}_{0}\mapsto[0,\infty)
is a jump rate function. At a defect site
k=k_{j,N}
, the jump rate is
(\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}})^{-1}g(\xi(k))/\xi(k)
.
In particular, the site
k_{j,N}
is a slow site if
\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}>1
and a fast or normal site if
\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}\leq 1

for
N
large. Let

g_{k,N}(\cdot)=\begin{cases}g(\cdot)&k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{N},\\
\dfrac{g(\cdot)}{\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}}&k=k_{j,N},\ j\in J.\end{cases}

The zero-range process is a Markov process
\xi_{t}
with the generator

(2.1)

\begin{split}L_{N}f(\xi)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\Big\{g_{k,N}(\xi(k))\big(f(\xi^{k,k+1})-f(\xi)\big)+g_{k,N}(\xi(k))\big(f(\xi^{k,k-1})-f(\xi)\big)\Big\}\end{split}

where

(2.2)

\xi^{x,y}(k)=\begin{cases}\xi(x)-1&k=x,\\
\xi(y)+1&k=y,\\
\xi(k)&k\neq x,y.\end{cases}

So that the process is irreducible, we will assume that the jump rate function
g
is such that
g(n)=0
exactly when
n=0
. We will also assume the following condition.

Condition 2.1
.

The jump rate function
g(\cdot)
satisfies

(1)

Lipschitz: there exists
g^{*}>0
such that
|g(n+1)-g(n)|\leq g^{*}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

(2)

Power Interaction: there exists
\alpha\in[0,1]
such that
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}
, that is

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{g(n)}{n^{\alpha}}=1.

(3)

Monotonicity:
g(n)\leq g(n+1)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

When the constant
\alpha=0
, in the power interaction condition, we note the function
g(n)
increases to
g_{\infty}:=1
as
n\to\infty
. We will refer to this case as
‘
g
is of bounded type’
or simply,
g
is
bounded
.
On the other hand, when
\alpha\in(0,1]
, we will say
‘
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type’.

We remark that, when
g(n)\equiv n
, the dynamics is non-interactive, a superposition of independent random walks. The Lipschitz condition, which rules out the case
\alpha>1
, is convenient to bound
g(n)
by
g^{*}n
, and in using the entropy inequality in places.
Moreover, the assumption that
g
is increasing, an ‘attractive’ dynamics assumption, allows use of the ‘basic coupling’ in our proofs; for more discussion of this point; see Section
3.2
.

Also, as discussed in the introduction, we will suppose in the
g
bounded setting that, at a defect site
k_{j,N}
, the parameter
\beta_{j}\leq 0
. Such a condition ensures existence of a non-trivial family of invariant measures; see Section
2.1
.

Condition 2.2
.

In the
g
bounded setting, we will assume
\beta_{j}\leq 0
for a defect site
k=k_{j,N}
.

Our goal in this work is to study hydrodynamic limits of the dynamics generated by
L_{N}
. In particular,
in the limit macroscopic flow, there will be different behaviors at the defect locations depending on the associated strength parameters.
To prepare for these statements, it will be helpful to introduce a partition on the index set
J=J_{s}\cup J_{c}\cup J_{b}
. Here, the subscripts
s
,
c
, and
b
stand for super-critical or ‘super-slow’, critical, and sub-critical respectively.

There will be different partitions of
J
depending on whether
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type or bounded.
When
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type, we let

•

J_{s}:=\{j\in J:\beta_{j}>\alpha\}
,

•

J_{c}:=\{j\in J:\beta_{j}=\alpha\}
, and

•

J_{b}:=\{j\in J:\beta_{j}<\alpha\}
.

When
g
is bounded, we take

•

J_{s}:=\{j\in J:\beta_{j}>0\}
(=
\emptyset
),

•

J_{c}:=\{j\in J:\beta_{j}=0,\ \lambda_{j}>1\}
, and

•

J_{b}:=\{j\in J:\beta_{j}<0\text{ or }\beta_{j}=0\text{ with }\lambda_{j}<1\}
.

In terms of the partition of
J
, the sets
{\mathfrak{D}}
and
{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
, the macroscopic and microscopic locations of the defects, are then divided into corresponding subsets.
For example, we have
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}:=\{x_{j}\in{\mathfrak{D}}:j\in J_{s}\}
and

{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}:=\{k_{j,N}\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}:j\in J_{s}\}
.
The sets
{\mathfrak{D}}_{c}
,
{\mathfrak{D}}_{b}
,
{\mathfrak{D}}_{c,N}
, and
{\mathfrak{D}}_{b,N}
are defined in the same fashion.

2.1.
Invariant measures

The construction of invariant measures under

L_{N}
is based on
\{\pazocal{P}_{\phi}\}
, a
family of Poisson-like distributions indexed by ‘fugacities’
\phi
.
In order to define
\pazocal{P}_{\phi}
, we first introduce the partition function:

Z(\phi):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\phi^{n}}{g(n)!}.

Let
r_{g}
be the convergence radius of
Z(\cdot)
.
It holds that
r_{g}=\lim_{n\to\infty}g(n)
.
In particular, when
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type, we have
r_{g}=\infty
, namely, the “FEM” condition (cf. p. 69,
[
18
]
) is satisfied.
When
g
is bounded,
r_{g}=g_{\infty}=1
.
In either case, it holds that
\lim_{\phi\uparrow r_{g}}Z(\phi)=\infty
.

For each
\phi\in[0,r_{g})
, define
\pazocal{P}_{\phi}
by

(2.3)

\pazocal{P}_{\phi}(n)=\dfrac{1}{Z(\phi)}\dfrac{\phi^{n}}{g(n)!},\quad\text{for }n\geq 0.

Here,
g(0)!:=1
and
g(n)!:=\prod_{k=1}^{n}g(k)
for
n\geq 1
.
Let
R(\phi)=E_{\pazocal{P}_{\phi}}[X]
, where
X(n)=n
, be the mean of
the distribution
\pazocal{P}_{\phi}
. A direct computation yields that

R(0)=0
and
\lim_{\phi\uparrow r_{g}}R(\phi)=\infty
.
Moreover, it holds
R^{\prime}(\phi)=\sigma^{2}(\phi)/\phi

and
\lim_{\phi\downarrow 0}R^{\prime}(\phi)=1/g(1)

where

\sigma^{2}(\phi)={\rm Var}_{\pazocal{P}_{\phi}}[X]
is the variance of
X
under
\pazocal{P}_{\phi}
.

Since

R
is strictly increasing, it has an inverse, denoted by

\Phi:[0,\infty)\mapsto[0,r_{g})
. We may parametrize the family of
distributions
\pazocal{P}_{\phi}
by their means: For
\rho\geq 0
, let

\pazocal{Q}_{\rho}=\pazocal{P}_{\Phi(\rho)}
, so that

E_{\pazocal{Q}_{\rho}}[X]=E_{\pazocal{P}_{\Phi(\rho)}}[X]=R(\Phi(\rho))=\rho
.
A straightforward computation yields that

E_{\pazocal{P}_{\phi}}[g(X)]=\phi
for
\phi\geq 0
. Thus,

(2.4)

\Phi(\rho)=E_{\pazocal{P}_{\Phi(\rho)}}[g(X)]=E_{\pazocal{Q}_{\rho}}[g(X)],\quad\rho\,\geq\,0.

As
g(n)\leq g^{*}n
, we have that

\Phi(\rho)\leq g^{*}\rho
.
As
\Phi(\cdot)=R^{-1}(\cdot)
, we have
\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)=\Phi(\rho)/\sigma^{2}(\Phi(\rho))
.
Under our assumptions on
g
, in fact, it holds that
0\leq\Phi^{\prime}(\rho)\leq g^{*}

for all
\rho\geq 0
(cf. p. 33,
[
18
]
). In particular,

\Phi\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
is an increasing function with a uniformly bounded derivative.
We note, in the case
g(n)\equiv n
, that
\Phi(\rho)\equiv\rho
and
\pazocal{P}_{\phi}
is a Poisson measure with mean
\phi
.

We now introduce the invariant measures.
For each
N
, let

q_{N}=\max\{1,\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}|j\in J\}.

For
c
so that
\Phi(c)\in[0,r_{g}/q_{N})
, denote by
\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}
the product measure on
\Omega_{N}
whose
marginals are given by

(2.5)

\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}(\xi(k)=n)=\begin{cases}\pazocal{P}_{\Phi(c)}(n)&\text{ for }k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}\text{ and }n\geq 0,\\
\pazocal{P}_{\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}\Phi(c)}(n)&\text{ for }k=k_{j,N},\ j\in J\text{, and }n\geq 0.\end{cases}

Notice that the condition
\Phi(c)\in[0,r_{g}/q_{N})
is needed since the distributions
\{\pazocal{P}_{\phi}\}
are defined for
\phi\in[0,r_{g})
.
When
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type, as
r_{g}=\infty
, we have that
\{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}\}
are defined for all
c\in[0,\infty)
.
However, when
g
is bounded, we have
r_{g}=g_{\infty}=1
, and thus the measures
\{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}\}
are defined only for
c\in[0,R(1/q_{N}))
. Moreover, in the
g
bounded setting, we have that
r_{g}/q_{N}
is bounded away from
0
for all large
N
exactly when
J_{s}=\emptyset
, that is
\beta_{j}\leq 0
, the reason for the assumption
J_{s}=\emptyset
in this case (cf. Condition
2.2
).

With
\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}
defined, it is straightforward (cf.
[
1
]
,
[
11
]
) to check the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
c
so that
\Phi(c)\in[0,r_{g}/q_{N})
,

\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}
is invariant and reversible with respect to the generator
L_{N}
in (
2.1
).

2.2.
Static limit

Before studying the hydrodynamic limits, it will be useful to understand the particle mass behavior under an invariant measure
\mathscr{R}^{N}_{c}
.
For a configuration
\xi\in\Omega_{N}
,
define the associated scaled mass empirical measure:

(2.6)

\widehat{\pi}^{N}(dx):=\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\xi(k)\delta_{k/N}(dx).

In this formulation, each particle has mass
N^{-1}
. Here and in the sequel,
\delta_{z}
refers to a delta point mass at
z
.

For a test function
G\in C(\mathbb{T})
, let

\langle G,\widehat{\pi}^{N}\rangle:=N^{-1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\xi(k)G(k/N).

More generally, we will use the notation
\langle G,\mu\rangle:=\int Gd\mu
.
We now compute the limit of

\langle G,\widehat{\pi}^{N}\rangle
with respect to a sequence of invariant measures
\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}
with
c
fixed as
N\to\infty
.

We assume first
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type
and
c>0
.
Because of the product structure of
\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}
,

\{\xi(k)\}_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}
are independent
and have a commmon marginal
\pazocal{P}_{\Phi(c)}
for all
k\neq{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
. As
\pazocal{P}_{\Phi(c)}
has expectation
c
and finite variance, we have

N^{-1}\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}}\xi(k)G(k/N)

converges in probability to

\int_{\mathbb{T}}G(x)c\,dx
as
N\to\infty
.

It remains to investigate the behavior of
N^{-1}\xi(k)
for
k=k_{j,N}\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
.
As
\xi(k_{j,N})
has distribution
\pazocal{P}_{\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}\Phi(c)}
, by the later Lemma
2.6
, for all
\beta_{j}>0
,

E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}[\xi(k_{j,N})]\sim(\lambda_{j}\Phi(c))^{1/\alpha}N^{\beta_{j}/\alpha},\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm Var}_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}[\xi(k_{j,N})]=o(N^{2\beta_{j}/\alpha}).

Then, according to the value of
\beta_{j}
, there are three different types of behaviors at
k\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
:

(1)

if
\beta_{j}<\alpha
,

N^{-1}\xi(k_{j,N})\to 0
in probability;

(2)

if
\beta_{j}=\alpha
,

N^{-1}\xi(k_{j,N})\to\left(\lambda_{j}\Phi(c)\right)^{1/\alpha}
in probability;

(3)

if
\beta_{j}>\alpha
,

N^{-\beta_{j}/\alpha}\xi(k_{j,N})\to\left(\lambda_{j}\Phi(c)\right)^{1/\alpha}

in probability.

In other words, the defect site
k_{j,N}
becomes macroscopically invisible when
\beta_{j}<\alpha
as typically it contains
o(N)
number of particles and each particle has mass
N^{-1}
.
In the case
\beta_{j}=\alpha
, typically the number of particles at
k_{j,N}
is of order
N
and a delta mass of magnitude
\left(\lambda_{j}\Phi(c)\right)^{1/\alpha}
emerges. When the site is super-slow, that is
\beta_{j}>\alpha
, the particle number at
k_{j,N}
is of order
N^{\beta_{j}/\alpha}
, corresponding to an infinite macroscopic mass. Recall, the partition
J=J_{b}\cup J_{c}\cup J_{s}
in Section
2
matches with this classification of the
\beta_{j}
’s.

As the macroscopical mass at
x_{j}\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}
explodes, to consider the remaining mass, we define microscopic empirical measures which exclude the super-critical defect set
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}
:

(2.7)

\pi^{N}(dx):=\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}}\xi(k)\delta_{k/N}(dx).

Then, we may summarize the above discussion as follows.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type. Then,
for any
G\in C(\mathbb{T})
,
c\geq 0
, and
\delta>0
:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}\big[\,\big|\langle G,\pi^{N}\rangle-\langle G,\pi\rangle\big|>\delta\,\big]=0

where
\pi(dx)=c\,dx+\sum_{j\in J_{c}}\left(\lambda_{j}\Phi(c)\right)^{1/\alpha}\delta_{x_{j}}(dx)
.
Moreover, for all
j\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}\big[\,\big|N^{-\beta_{j}/\alpha}\xi(k_{j,N})-\left(\lambda_{j}\Phi(c)\right)^{1/\alpha}\big|>\delta\,\big]=0.

Now we turn to the case when
g
is bounded.
Recall, in this case,
\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}
is defined for
c
such that
\Phi(c)\in[0,1/q_{N})
where

q_{N}=\max\{1,\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}|j\in J\}
. Recall also, in this setting, that
\beta_{j}\leq 0
, that is
J_{s}=\emptyset
.
When
J_{c}\neq\emptyset
, we will define
\lambda_{\max}:=\max_{j\in J_{c}}\{\lambda_{j}\}
. Otherwise, when
J_{c}=\emptyset
, we will take
\lambda_{\max}=1
. Hence, the domain for
c
so that
\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}
is defined for all
N
is
[0,R(1/\lambda_{\max})
.

For
c\in[0,R(1/\lambda_{\max}))
, we also have

N^{-1}\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}}\xi(k)G(k/N)

converges in probability to

\int_{\mathbb{T}}G(x)c\,dx
as
N\to\infty
.
Given
\xi(k)
has finite
\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}
-expectation and variance for all
k\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
, the following is easily obtained.

Proposition 2.5
.

Assume
g
is bounded and
J_{s}=\emptyset
. Then,
for
c\in[0,R(1/\lambda_{\max}))
,
for any
G\in C(\mathbb{T})
and
\delta>0
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}\big[\,\big|\langle G,\pi^{N}\rangle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}G(x)c\,dx\big|>\delta\,\big]=0.

We finish this section with a technical lemma used in Proposition
2.4
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume
g(k)\sim k^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
For each
\varphi>0
, let
X
be a random variable with distribution
\pazocal{P}_{\varphi}
(cf. (
2.3
)).
Then, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
E[X^{n}]\sim\varphi^{n/\alpha}
as
\varphi\to\infty
.
As a result,
\ln Z(\varphi)\sim\alpha\varphi^{1/\alpha}

and
{\rm Var}[X]=o\big(\varphi^{2/\alpha}\big)
as
\varphi\to\infty
.

Proof.

We first show
E[X^{n}]\sim\varphi^{n/\alpha}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
To this end, let us assume for now

(2.8)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{k^{n\alpha}\varphi^{k}}{g(k)!}\sim\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}\dfrac{\varphi^{k}}{g(j-n)!}.

Let
Y=X^{\alpha}
. Then,

E[Y^{n}]=\dfrac{1}{Z(\varphi)}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{k^{n\alpha}\varphi^{k}}{g(k)!}\sim\dfrac{1}{Z(\varphi)}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\dfrac{\varphi^{k}}{g(k-n)!}=\varphi^{n}.

As
\alpha\in(0,1]
, we may find
p\in[0,1)
and
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha^{-1}=p+(1-p)l
.
Also, since
E[X^{n}]=E[Y^{n/\alpha}]
, by Jensen’s and Hölder’s inequalities,

E[Y^{n}]^{1/\alpha}\leq E[X^{n}]\leq E[Y^{n}]^{p}E[Y^{nl}]^{1-p}.

Since
E[Y^{n}]\sim\varphi^{n}
and
E[Y^{nl}]\sim\varphi^{nl}
, we obtain

E[X^{n}]\sim\varphi^{n/\alpha}
.

For the limit behavior of
E[X^{n}]
, it remains to show the claim (
2.8
).
As
g(k)\sim k^{\alpha}
, for any
A>0
, we may find
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}(A)
and

\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{2}(A)
, such that

\lambda_{1}k^{\alpha}\leq g(k)\leq\lambda_{2}k^{\alpha}
for all
k\geq A
and
\lim_{A\to\infty}\lambda_{1}=\lim_{A\to\infty}\lambda_{2}=1
.
Then, for all
k\geq A+n
,

\lambda_{2}^{-n}\leq\dfrac{k^{n\alpha}}{\prod_{k-n<k^{\prime}\leq k}g(k^{\prime})}\leq\Big(\dfrac{A+n}{A}\Big)^{n}\lambda_{1}^{-n}.

Therefore,

\lambda_{2}^{-n}\sum_{k=A+n}^{\infty}\dfrac{\varphi^{k}}{g(k-n)!}\leq\sum_{k=A+n}^{\infty}\dfrac{k^{n\alpha}\varphi^{k}}{g(k)!}\leq\Big(\dfrac{A+n}{A}\Big)^{n}\lambda_{1}^{-n}\sum_{k=A+n}^{\infty}\dfrac{\varphi^{k}}{g(k-n)!}

Notice that, if
\varphi
is sent to infinity in the above display, we may replace
\sum_{k\geq A+n}
by either
\sum_{k\geq 0}
or
\sum_{k\geq n}
.
Then the claim (
2.8
) follows from taking
\varphi\to\infty
and then
A\to\infty
.

We have shown
E[X^{n}]\sim\varphi^{n/\alpha}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, it follows that

{\rm Var}[X]=o\big(\varphi^{2/\alpha}\big)

as
E[X^{2}]\sim{E[X]}^{2}\sim\varphi^{2/\alpha}
.
To prove
\ln Z(\varphi)\sim\alpha\varphi^{1/\alpha}
, notice
\dfrac{d}{d\varphi}\ln Z(\varphi)=\varphi^{-1}E[X]\sim\varphi^{1/\alpha-1}

and then apply L’Hospital’s rule, to finish the argument.
∎

3.
Initial measures

In this section, we specify the assumptions on the initial measures
\{\mu^{N}\}
we use to start our dynamics.
Roughly speaking,
\{\mu^{N}\}
should be associated with a macroscopic profile which gives the initial condition for the hydrodynamic limit.
We will also require
\mu^{N}
to possess certain relative entropy estimates and to be stochastically bounded with respect to invariant measures.

To specify these conditions, recall, for
\mu
,
\nu
, two probability measures on
\Omega_{N}
, we say that
\mu\leq\nu
, that is
\mu
is stochastically bounded by
\nu
, if for all
f:\Omega_{N}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
coordinately increasing, we have
E_{\mu}(f)\leq E_{\nu}(f)
.
Fix also
\pi
, a nonnegative measure on
\mathbb{T}
, such that

(3.1)

\pi(dx)=\rho_{0}(x)dx+\sum_{j\in J_{c}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{0,j}\,\delta_{x_{j}}(dx)

where
\rho_{0}(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j,0}\geq 0
for
j\in J_{c}
.
Recall also, the empirical measure
\pi^{N}
defined in (
2.7
).

Throughout this work, we will assume the following on the sequence of initial measures
\{\mu^{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
on
\Omega_{N}
.

Condition 3.1
.

The following hold:

(1)

\{\mu^{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
has macroscopic profile
\pi
on
\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}
, i.e. for all
G(x)\in C(\mathbb{T})
and
\delta>0
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mu^{N}\Big[\,\Big|\langle G,\pi^{N}\rangle-\langle G,\pi\rangle\Big|>\delta\,\Big]=0.

(2)

There exists
c_{0}>0
such that
the relative entropy of
\mu^{N}
with respect to
\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}
is of order
N
: Let
f_{N}:=d\mu^{N}/d\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}
. Then,
H(\mu^{N}|\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}):=\int f_{N}\ln f_{N}d\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}=O(N)
.

(3)

When
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type,
there exists
c^{\prime}

such that
\mu^{N}
is stochastically bounded by
\mathscr{R}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}
for all
N
.

(3
′
)

When
g
is bounded,
there exists
c^{\prime}
such that,
when restricted to
\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{c,N}
,
\mu^{N}
is stochastically bounded by
\kappa_{c^{\prime}}^{N}
, that is
E_{\mu^{N}}[f]\leq E_{\kappa_{c^{\prime}}^{N}}[f]
for coordinate increasing functions supported on
\{\eta(k):k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{c,N}\}
.
Here,

\kappa_{c^{\prime}}^{N}=\prod_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{b,N}}\pazocal{P}_{\Phi(c^{\prime})}\times\prod_{k=k_{j,N}\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{b,N}}\pazocal{P}_{\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}\Phi(c^{\prime})}.

We comment that
item (3) is sufficiently general in the
n^{\alpha}
setting to allow
\{\mu^{N}\}
to be associated with profiles of the form
\pi_{0}(dx)=\rho_{0}(x)dx+\sum_{j\in J_{c}}\mathfrak{m}_{0,j}\delta_{x_{j}}(dx)
, for densities
\rho_{0}
and masses
\{\mathfrak{m}_{0,j}\}
, as demonstrated in Section
3.3
. In the bounded
g
case, however, if
\{\mu^{N}\}
satisfied item (3), then
O(N)
accumulations at points
{\mathfrak{D}}_{c,N}
would not be allowed. In this case, the only profiles allowed would be of form
\pi_{0}(dx)=\rho_{0}(x)dx
, where
\|\Phi(\rho_{0})\|_{\infty}\leq\min_{j\in J_{c}}\tfrac{1}{\lambda_{j}}
(cf. Section
3.2
). As mentioned in the introduction, hydrodynamic evolution from such profiles would not see any defects.

In this context, item (3
′
) is formulated so that, with respect to
\{\mu^{N}\}
,
O(N)
accumulations are possible on
{\mathfrak{D}}_{c,N}
as well as later point masses on
{\mathfrak{D}}_{c}
in the hydrodynamic evolution.

In fact, conditions
(3)
and
(3^{\prime})
can be made to accommodate a larger class of initial measures
\{\mu^{N}\}
.
For example, one may remove stochastic bounded assumptions on coordinates in
{\mathfrak{D}}_{b,N}
. We have however chosen to state the
(3)
and
(3^{\prime})
in the forms given to streamline arguments and avoid more piecemeal calculations.

Define now
\mu_{t}^{N}
as the distribution of the process
\xi_{t}
, with initial measure
\mu^{N}
.

In the rest of this section, we make remarks on
the consequences of the
relative entropy bound in terms of
\mu^{N}_{t}
-particle numbers, discuss the use of attractiveness, and also
provide a large class of examples of
\mu^{N}
which satisfy Condition
3.1
.

3.1.
\mu^{N}_{t}
-particle numbers and relative entropy

We first comment on the
\mu_{t}^{N}
-particle numbers in the system.
Denote
H_{N}:=H(\mu^{N}|\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N})=O(N)
. Notice that the entropy does not increase in
t
, that is
H(\mu_{t}^{N}|\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N})\leq H_{N}
for

t\geq 0

(cf. pp 340,
[
18
]
).

Assume now that
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type.
By the entropy inequality

E_{\mu}[f]\leq H(\mu|\nu)+\ln E_{\nu}[e^{f}]
(cf. p.338
[
18
]
),

E_{\mu_{t}^{N}}\Big[\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}}\xi(k)\Big]\leq H(\mu_{t}^{N}|\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N})+\ln E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\big[e^{\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}}\xi(k)}\big].

As
\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}
is product measure, the
\ln
term is written as
\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}}\ln E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}[e^{\xi(k)}]

which is
O(N)
by Lemma
2.6
.
Thus, the condition
H_{N}=O(N)
specifies
O(N)
particles on the sites
\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}
for times
t\geq 0
.

On the other hand, at a super-slow site
k_{j,N}\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}
, the
\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}
-particle number is typically of order
O(N^{\beta_{j}/\alpha})
, as discussed in Section
2.2
. Then, because
H_{N}=O(N)
, as consequence of the entropy inequality, we may conclude,
with respect to
\mu_{t}^{N}
, that

N^{-\beta_{j}/\alpha}\xi(k_{j,N})
converges in probability to
\left(\lambda_{j}\Phi(c_{0})\right)^{1/\alpha}
; see near (
7.6
) for a proof.

Therefore,
the net exchange of particle numbers between super-slow sites
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}
and the rest of the system is of order
N
and
the total particle number on
\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}
remains
O(N)
for all times
t\geq 0
.

We now turn to case when
g
is bounded.
Since in this setting
J_{s}=\emptyset
, that is
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}=\emptyset
(Condition
2.2
),
by the previous entropy inequality discussion, it follows that the total number of particles in
\mathbb{T}_{N}
is of order
N
for all times
t\geq 0
.

3.2.
Basic coupling

We discuss the use of the stochastic boundedness assumption (3) and (3
′
) in Condition
3.1
.
Since
g(n)
is an increasing function in
n
, the dynamics generated by
L_{N}
is ‘attractive’:
if initially
\xi_{0}
is distributed according to measures
\mu\leq\nu
, then we have
\mu_{t}\leq\nu_{t}
for all
t\geq 0
where
\mu_{t}
and
\nu_{t}
are distributions of
\xi_{t}

(cf.
[
1
]
, Chapter II in
[
22
]
).

In the case when
g
of
n^{\alpha}
type, as
\mathscr{R}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}
is invariant, the assumption
\mu^{N}\leq\mathscr{R}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}

implies that
\mu_{t}^{N}\leq\mathscr{R}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}
for all
t\geq 0
.
However, when
g
is bounded,
the domain for
c
in
\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}
is
c<R(1/\lambda_{\max})
(cf. Proposition
2.5
).
Then, an assumption

\mu^{N}\leq\mathscr{R}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}

would imply that
\pi
, the macroscopic profile associated to
\mu^{N}
, is
\pi(dx)=\rho_{0}(x)dx
with
\|\rho_{0}\|_{\infty}<R(1/\lambda_{\max})<\infty
.
To accommodate more initial profiles
\pi
(and observe more involved limit evolutions),
we have assumed
(3^{\prime})
in Condition
3.1
instead, that is, for all coordinately increasing
f:\Omega_{N}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
depending only on
\{\xi(k)\}_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{c,N}}
,
we have
E_{\mu_{t}^{N}}[f(\xi)]\leq E_{\kappa_{c^{\prime}}^{N}}[f(\xi)]
.

We now illustrate how we will apply attractiveness under assumption
(3^{\prime})
. Instead of an evolution with respect to particle numbers in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and configurations in
\Omega_{N}^{\mathbb{T}_{N}}
, we may consider a dynamics corresponding to
\overline{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\overline{\Omega}_{N}:=\overline{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{\mathbb{T}_{N}}
.
Recall the constant
c^{\prime}
in
(3^{\prime})
. Define
\overline{g}_{k,N}(\cdot)
by
\overline{g}_{k,N}(n)=g_{k,N}(n)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\overline{g}_{k,N}(\infty)=\Phi(c^{\prime})
. Consider now the following generator on
\overline{\Omega}_{N}

\overline{L}_{N}f(\xi)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\Big\{\overline{g}_{k,N}(\xi(k))\big(f(\xi^{k,k+1})-f(\xi)\big)+\overline{g}_{k,N}(\xi(k))\big(f(\xi^{k,k-1})-f(\xi)\big)\Big\}.

Here,
\xi^{x,y}
is defined as in (
2.2
) by using the convention
\infty\pm 1=\infty
.
When starting with configurations in
\Omega_{N}
,
\overline{L}_{N}
coincides with
L_{N}
. Once a site starts with
\infty
particles, it will serve as a ‘reservoir’ which pumps particles into its neighbors at the rate of
\Phi(c^{\prime})
.
Let
\delta_{\infty}
be the Dirac measure on the extended number
\infty
. Define
\overline{\kappa}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}
as the product measure on
\overline{\Omega}_{N}
that coincides with
\kappa_{c^{\prime}}^{N}
on
k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{c,N}
and has marginal
\delta_{\infty}
for
k\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{c,N}
.
It is straightforward to check that
\overline{\kappa}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}
is invariant under
\overline{L}_{N}
. Also, we have that
\mu^{N}\leq\overline{\kappa}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}
by
(3^{\prime})
in Condition
3.1
.
As attractiveness and the basic coupling are still in effect for
\overline{L}_{N}
, we obtain that the later time
t
distribution satisfies
\mu_{t}^{N}\leq\overline{\kappa}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}
for any
t\geq 0
. In particular, for a coordinate-increasing function
f(\xi)
which does not depend on
\{\xi(k)\}_{k\in\mathscr{D}_{c,N}}
, we have
E_{\mu^{t}_{N}}[f]\leq E_{\overline{\kappa}^{N}_{c^{\prime}}}[f]=E_{\kappa^{N}_{c^{\prime}}}[f]
.

Finally, as a general remark, we make technical use of ‘attractiveness’ most essentially in the cutoff of large densities in the local replacements (Lemma
8.1
and Lemma
8.2
),
and in proving the limit mass profile has
L^{2}
absolutely continuous part (Lemma
7.1
).
The latter property is needed in the proof of uniqueness of weak solutions (Theorem
11.1
and
11.2
).

Proof.

As

that

Let

We define

4.1.
Hydrodynamic limits

We now state our main results. Recall the initial profile
\pi
and the constant

from the beginning of this section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Asssume
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type.
Then, for any
t>0
, test function

G\in C(\mathbb{T})
, and
\delta>0
, we have

(4.2)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{N}\Big[\Big|\langle G,\pi^{N}_{t}\rangle-\langle G,\pi_{t}\rangle\Big|>\delta\Big]=0,

where
\pi_{t}(dx)=\rho(t,x)dx+\sum_{j\in J_{c}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)\,\delta_{x_{j}}(dx)
is the unique weak solution to

(4.3)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\pi_{t}=\partial_{xx}\Phi(\rho(t,x)),&x\in\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s},\ t\in(0,T),\\
\pi_{t}|_{t=0}=\pi,\quad\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))=\Phi(c_{0}),&t\in(0,T),\ j\in J_{s},\\
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)=\left(\lambda_{j}\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))\right)^{1/\alpha},&t\in(0,T),\,j\in J_{c}.\end{cases}

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume
g
is bounded.
Then, for any
t>0
, test function

G\in C(\mathbb{T})
, and
\delta>0
, we have

(4.4)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{N}\Big[\Big|\langle G,\pi^{N}_{t}\rangle-\langle G,\pi_{t}\rangle\Big|>\delta\Big]=0,

where
\pi_{t}(dx)=\rho(t,x)dx+\sum_{j\in J_{c}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)\,\delta_{x_{j}}(dx)
is the unique weak solution to

(4.5)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\pi_{t}=\partial_{xx}\Phi(\rho(t,x)),&x\in\mathbb{T},\ t\in(0,T),\\
\pi_{t}|_{t=0}=\pi,\quad\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))\leq 1/\lambda_{j},&t\in(0,T),\,j\in J_{c},\\
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)={\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))=1/\lambda_{j}},&t\in(0,T),\,j\in J_{c}.\end{cases}

We now define the weak solutions to the limit PDE (
4.3
) and (
4.5
).

Definition 4.3
.

Let
f(t,x)
and
g(t,x)
be in
L_{loc}^{1}([0,T]\times D)
where
D
is a domain of
x
. We say
f
is weakly differentialble with respect to

if for all

G(t,x)\in C_{c}^{0,1}([0,T]\times D)
that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}G(t,x)f(t,x)dxdt=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}G(t,x)g(t,x)dxdt;

The weak derivative will be denoted by
\partial_{x}f(t,x)
and
\partial_{x}f(t,x):=g(t,x)
.

We comment that if
f(t,x)
is weakly differentiable with respect to
x\in D
as defined above,
then for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,
f(t,\cdot)
is absolutely continuous and
f(t,b)-f(t,a)=\int_{a}^{b}\partial_{x}f(t,x)\,dx
for all connected
a,b\in D
. In particular, the evaluation of

f(t,x^{\prime})=f(t,x)|_{x=x^{\prime}}
at a given value
x^{\prime}\in D
is well defined.

Definition 4.4
.

We say
\pi_{t}(dx)=\rho(t,x)dx+\sum_{j\in J_{c}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)\,\delta_{x_{j}}(dx)

is a weak solution to the system (
4.3
)
if

(1)

\rho(t,x)
is in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
and
\Phi(\rho(t,x))
is weakly differentiable with respect to

with

\partial_{x}\Phi(\rho(t,x))\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
;

(2)

\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))=\Phi(c_{0})
, for almost all

and all

;

(3)

{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)=\left(\lambda_{j}\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))\right)^{1/\alpha}
for almost all
t\in(0,T)
and
j\in J_{c}
;

(4)

for all
G(t,x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left([0,T)\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s})\right)

(4.6)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{t}G(t,x)\pi_{t}(dx)dt+\int_{\mathbb{T}}G(0,x)\pi(dx)+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{xx}G(t,x)\Phi(\rho(t,x))dxdt=0.\end{split}

Definition 4.5
.

We say
\pi_{t}(dx)=\rho(t,x)dx+\sum_{j\in J_{c}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)\,\delta_{x_{j}}(dx)

is a weak solution to the system (

)
if

(1)

\rho(t,x)
is in

and
\Phi(\rho(t,x))
is weakly differentiable with respect to

with

\partial_{x}\Phi(\rho(t,x))\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
;

(2)

\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))\leq 1/\lambda_{j}
, for almost all
t\in(0,T)
and all
x_{j}\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{c}
;

(3)

{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)={\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))=1/\lambda_{j}}
for almost all

and

;

(4)

the weak formulation (

) holds
for all
G(t,x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left([0,T)\times\mathbb{T}\right)
.

Remark 4.6
.

In Definitions
4.4
and
4.5
,
we have assumed

\Phi(\rho(t,x))
to be weakly differentialbe with respect to
x\in\mathbb{T}
.
Note that
\Phi(\cdot)
is invertible
and the inverse function

\Phi^{-1}(\cdot)
is a continuous mapping from
[0,r_{g})
to
[0,\infty)

where
r_{g}=\infty
for
g
of
n^{\alpha}
type and
r_{g}=1
for
g
bounded (cf. Section
2.1
).
Then
\rho(t,x)=\Phi^{-1}\left(\Phi(\rho(t,x))\right)
is continuous in
x
for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
(viewed as
[0,\infty]
valued function for
g
bounded).
In particular, the evaluation of
\Phi(\rho(t,x))
at
x=x_{j}
may be written in terms

\rho(t,x_{j})
instead.

Moreover, notice that, under the settings of Theorems

4.1
and
4.2
, it holds
\|\rho\|_{\infty}<\infty
; see Lemma
7.1
.
As
\Phi^{-1}(\cdot)
is Lipschitz on

[0,\phi]
where
\phi=\Phi(\|\rho\|_{\infty})
(cf. Section
2.1
), we have that

\rho(t,x)
is also weakly differentiable with respect to
x\in\mathbb{T}
and

\partial_{x}\rho(t,x)\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
.

To illustrate the relation between boundary conditions and effects on defect sites, we consider the case with only a single defect site.

Example 4.7

(Effects of a single slow site)
.

As an example to illustrate how defects affect the macroscopic bulk evolution, we look at systems with a single defect site.
Without loss of generality, we may assume the defect location is at
0
and the altered jump rate is
(\lambda N^{\beta})^{-1}g(\xi(0))
.

Consider first
g
of
n^{\alpha}
type.
By Theorem
4.1
, the hydrodynamic limit
\pi_{t}
is governed by the PDE (
4.3
).
As the defect site is at
x=0
, we have
\pi_{t}(dx)=\rho(t,x)dx
when restricted to open interval
(0,1)
.
Indeed, (
4.3
) is now reduced to a nonlinear heat equation regarding only
\rho(t,x)
with different boundary conditions at
x=0
and
x=1
depending on the value of
\beta
.
Precisely, we have the following.

(1)

When
\beta<\alpha
, the defect site is invisible in the limit and (
4.3
) becomes

(4.7)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)=\partial_{xx}\Phi(\rho(t,x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{T},\ t\in(0,T),\\
\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x),\end{cases}

that is
\rho(t,x)
satisfies periodic boundary conditions.

(2)

When
\beta=\alpha
, we have
\pi_{t}(dx)=\rho(t,x)dx+{\mathfrak{m}}(t)\delta_{0}(dx)
with

{\mathfrak{m}}(t)=(\lambda\Phi(\rho(t,0)))^{1/\alpha}
.
The atomic mass
{\mathfrak{m}}(t)
can also be expressed in terms of the its initial value
{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}
and net change of the bulk mass as the total mass is conserved:

{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}+\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0}(x)dx={\mathfrak{m}}(t)+\int_{0}^{1}\rho(t,x)dx,\quad\text{for all }t>0.

Therefore, we have

\Phi(\rho(t,0))=\lambda^{-1}\big[{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}+\int_{0}^{1}(\rho_{0}(x)-\rho(t,x))dx\big]^{\alpha}
.
Noticing that
x=0
and
x=1
coincide on
\mathbb{T}
,
we obtain

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)=\partial_{xx}\Phi(\rho(t,x)),\quad x\in(0,1),\ t\in(0,T),\\
\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x),\\
\Phi(\rho(t,0))=\Phi(\rho(t,1))=\lambda^{-1}\big[{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}+\int_{0}^{1}\left(\rho_{0}(x)-\rho(t,x)\right)dx\big]^{\alpha}.\end{cases}

We comment that, in a system with more than one critical slow sites (
\beta=\alpha
), conservative of mass is not sufficient to determine the atomic masses individually. For a closed equation, one needs to stay with a form such as (
4.3
).

(3)

When
\beta>\alpha
, as
{\mathfrak{D}}={\mathfrak{D}}_{s}=\{0\}
, the PDE (
4.3
) is

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)=\partial_{xx}\Phi(\rho(t,x)),\quad x\in(0,1),\ t\in(0,T),\\
\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x),\\
\rho(t,0)=\rho(t,1)=c_{0}.\end{cases}

We now turn to case when
g
is bounded.
By Theorem
4.2
, the macroscopic flow of
\pi_{t}
is described by (
4.4
).
Notice that here we have
\beta\leq 0
.
When the defect is a fast site, that is
\beta<0
or
\beta=0
with
\lambda<1
, the defect site is invisible macroscopically and the limit evolution is the usual nonlinear heat equation (
4.7
) with periodic boundary condition. When the defect is a slow site, that is
\beta=0
and
\lambda>1
,
we can write
\mathfrak{m}(t)
, the atomic mass at the slow site, in term of ‘mass conservation’ as in part (2) above to form a closed equation of
\rho(t,x)
:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)=\partial_{xx}\Phi(\rho(t,x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{T},\ t\in(0,T),\\
\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x),\quad\rho(t,0)=\rho(t,1)\leq\Phi^{-1}(1/\lambda),\\
{\mathfrak{m}}(t)={\mathfrak{m}}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{\rho(t,0)=\Phi^{-1}(1/\lambda)},\quad{\mathfrak{m}}(t)={\mathfrak{m}}_{0}+\int_{0}^{1}\left(\rho_{0}(x)-\rho(t,x)\right)dx.\end{cases}

Informally, during the evolution, we observe periodic boundary conditions when
{\mathfrak{m}}(t)=0
and Dirichlet boundary conditions
\rho(t,0)=\rho(t,1)=\Phi^{-1}(1/\lambda)
when
{\mathfrak{m}}(t)>0
.

4.2.
Remarks on general defects

In our model, we have assumed that the jumping rate at a defect site
k_{j,N}
is slowed down by a factor of
\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}
. However, the form of
\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}
is only a choice for convenience. In fact,
Theorems
4.1
and
4.2

hold for more general choices of slow down factors, as can be perused.

We may assume the jumping rate at a defect
k_{j,N}
is
g(\cdot)/\beta_{j,N}
. (In terms of the current setting
\beta_{j,N}=\lambda_{j}N^{\beta_{j}}
.) Assume that
\beta_{j,N}>0
and
\lim_{N\to\infty}\beta_{j,N}/g(N)
exists (might be
\infty
though). To extend Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
to the new setting, we need to clarify the sets of
J_{s}
,
J_{c}
and
\lambda_{j}
for
j\in J_{c}
.
When
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type,
J_{c}=\{j\in J:\beta_{j,N}\sim N^{\alpha}\}
and when
g
is bounded,
J_{c}=\{j\in J:\lim_{N\to\infty}\beta_{j,N}>1\}
.
In either case of
g
, the super-critical index set
J_{s}
consists of
j
’s such that

\beta_{j,N}\gg g(N)
and
J_{b}=J\setminus\{J_{s}\cup J_{c}\}
.
The constant
\lambda_{j}
for
j\in J_{c}
can be defined as
\lambda_{j}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\beta_{j,N}/g(N)
.

5.
Proof outline

In this section, we prove
Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
.
We begin with analyzing
\langle G,\pi^{N}_{t}\rangle
by computing its stochastic
differential in terms of certain martingales.

5.1.
Stochastic differentials

Let
G
be a smooth function on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}
and we write
G_{t}(x):=G(t,x)
.
For
t\geq 0
, consider
\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{N}
, the emprical measure associated with
\eta_{t}
on
\mathbb{T}_{N}
:

(5.1)

\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{N}(dx):=\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\eta_{t}(k)\delta_{k/N}(dx).

Note that if
G
has compact support on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}
,
it holds
\langle G_{t},\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{N}\rangle=\langle G_{t},\pi_{t}^{N}\rangle
for all
N
large (cf. (
4.1
)).

We have

M^{N,G}_{t}=\left\langle G_{t},\widehat{\pi}^{N}_{t}\right\rangle-\left\langle G_{0},\widehat{\pi}^{N}_{0}\right\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{\left\langle\partial_{s}G_{s},\widehat{\pi}_{s}^{N}\right\rangle+N^{2}L_{N}\left\langle G_{s},\widehat{\pi}^{N}_{s}\right\rangle\Big\}ds

is a mean zero martingale.
Denote the discrete Laplacian
\Delta_{N}
by

\Delta_{N}G\Big(\frac{k}{N}\Big):=N^{2}\Big(G\Big(\frac{k+1}{N}\Big)+G\Big(\frac{k-1}{N}\Big)-2G\Big(\frac{k}{N}\Big)\Big).

Then, for
N
large, we compute

(5.2)

N^{2}L_{N}\left\langle G,\widehat{\pi}^{N}_{s}\right\rangle=\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\Delta_{N}G_{s}\Big(\frac{k}{N}\Big)g_{k,N}(\eta_{s}(k)).

The quadratic variation of
M^{N,G}_{t}
is given by

\langle M^{N,G}\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\left\{N^{2}L_{N}\left(\left\langle G_{s},\widehat{\pi}^{N}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}\right)-2\left\langle G_{s},\widehat{\pi}^{N}_{s}\right\rangle N^{2}L_{N}\left\langle G_{s},\widehat{\pi}^{N}_{s}\right\rangle\right\}ds

which by standard calculation equals

\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}g_{k,N}(\eta_{s}(k))\left\{\Big(G_{s}\big(\frac{k+1}{N}\big)-G_{s}\big(\frac{k}{N}\big)\Big)^{2}+\Big(G_{s}\big(\frac{k-1}{N}\big)-G_{s}\big(\frac{k}{N}\big)\Big)^{2}\right\}ds.

This variation may be bounded as follows.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any test function
G(x)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, there is a constant
C
independent of
N
such that, for all
N
large,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}_{N}\langle M^{N,G}\rangle_{t}\leq CN^{-1}.

Proof.

Since
G
is smooth, we obtain, for
N
large

\mathbb{E}_{N}\langle M^{N,G}\rangle_{t}\leq 2(\|\partial_{x}G\|_{\infty})^{2}N^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}g_{k,N}(\eta_{s}(k))ds\Big].

Then, the result follows from
(
5.6
) in
the next Lemma
5.2
.
∎

It will be useful to bound local particle numbers and rates. Although there are different ways to prove parts of the following statements, say using the entropy inequality and
g(n)/n^{\alpha}<C
for
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
, we will use attractiveness to facilitate a short proof.

Lemma 5.2
.

We have the following:

(1)

The expectation of total mass at all but super-critical sites is uniformly bounded:

(5.3)

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}}\eta_{t}(k)\Big]<\infty;

(2)

The expected particle number at each regular site
k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
is uniformly bounded:

(5.4)

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq 0}\sup_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\eta_{t}(k)\Big]<\infty;

(3)

The expectation of weighted jump rate
N^{-1}g_{k,N}
vanishes uniformly as
N\to\infty
:

(5.5)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{t\geq 0}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\dfrac{1}{N}g_{k,N}\left(\eta_{t}(k)\right)\Big]=0;

(4)

The expectation of total weighted jump rate is uniformly bounded:

(5.6)

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}g_{k,N}(\eta_{t}(k))\Big]<\infty.

Proof.

First, in both rate
g
settings,
the total mass estimate (
5.3
) follow directly from the initial bound of the entropy
H(\mu^{N}|\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N})=O(N)
and the entropy inequality; see Section
3.1
for an explicit proof.

Moreover, (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) follow by straightforwardly applying attractiveness,
\mu_{t}^{N}\leq\mathscr{R}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}
and

\mu_{t}^{N}\leq\overline{\kappa}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}
for
g
of
n^{\alpha}
type and bounded type respectively
(see Section
3.2
).
Indeed, in the
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}
case,, we have, for all
k\not\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
, that
\mathbb{E}_{N}[\eta_{t}(k)]\leq E_{\mathscr{R}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}}[\xi(k)]=c^{\prime}
. Also, as
g
is increasing,

\mathbb{E}_{N}[g_{k,N}(\eta_{t}(k))]\leq E_{\mathscr{R}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}}[g_{k,N}(\xi(k))]=\Phi(c^{\prime})
(cf. (
2.4
)).
When
g
is bounded, we have
\mathbb{E}_{N}[\eta_{t}(k)]\leq E_{\overline{\kappa}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}}[\xi(k)]=c^{\prime}
for regular sites
k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
. For the bound of
\mathbb{E}_{N}[g_{k,N}(\eta_{t}(k))]
, it holds trivially that
\mathbb{E}_{N}[g_{k,N}(\eta_{t}(k))]\leq g_{\infty}=1
when
k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
and
\mathbb{E}_{N}[g_{k,N}(\eta_{t}(k))]\leq 1/\lambda_{j}
when
k=k_{j,N}
with
\beta_{j}=0
. When
k=k_{j,N}
with
\beta_{j}<0
, we use attractiveness again
\mathbb{E}_{N}[g_{k,N}(\eta_{t}(k))]\leq E_{\overline{\kappa}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}}[g_{k,N}(\xi(k))]=\Phi(c^{\prime})
.

Finally, to show (
5.6
),
we separate the sum of
k
into two sums consisting of the defect sites
{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
and the regular sites respectively: The sum over
{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
is bounded via (
5.5
) and that
{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
has finite cardinality
n_{0}
independent of
N
.
Recall that
g_{k,N}(\cdot)=g(\cdot)
for
k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
.
Now, bounding
g(n)\leq g^{*}n
, the sum over the regular sites is bounded due to (
5.3
).
∎

Note that

\begin{split}&\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{\theta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}Q^{N}\Big[\Big|\left\langle G_{0},\pi^{N}_{0}\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{\left\langle\partial_{s}G_{s},\pi^{N}_{s}\right\rangle\\

Notice that the discrete sum on

As the absolute value term in (

(5.11)

\begin{split}&\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{\theta\to 0}Q\Big[\Big|\left\langle G_{0},\pi_{0}\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{\left\langle\partial_{s}G_{s},\pi_{s}\right\rangle\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{\varepsilon}(s,x)\Phi\big(\langle\iota_{\theta}(\cdot-x),\pi_{s}\rangle\big)dx\Big\}ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0.\end{split}

Step 4.
We show in

that

is supported on trajectories

\pi_{s}(dx)=\rho(s,x)dx+\sum_{j\in J_{c}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(s)\delta_{x_{j}}(dx).

Then, for
x\notin{\mathfrak{D}}^{\varepsilon}
and
\theta<\varepsilon
,

\langle\iota_{\theta}(\cdot-x),\pi_{s}\rangle=(2\theta)^{-1}\int_{x-\theta}^{x+\theta}\rho(s,u)du
.
Note that
\Phi
is Lipschitz and
\rho
is integrable on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}
. Hence, for all
\varepsilon
small, we have,
Q
-almost surely

\lim_{\theta\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{\varepsilon}(s,x)\Phi(\langle\iota_{\theta}(\cdot-x),\pi_{s}\rangle)dxds=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{\varepsilon}(s,x)\Phi(\rho(s,x))dxds.

Since almost sure convergence impiies convergence in probability, we obtain from (
5.11
) that, for all
\delta>0

\begin{split}&\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}Q\Big[\Big|\left\langle G_{0},\pi_{0}\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{\left\langle\partial_{s}G_{s},\pi_{s}\right\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{\varepsilon}(s,x)\Phi(\rho(s,x))dx\Big\}ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0.\end{split}

Taking
\varepsilon\to 0
we may also replace
F_{\varepsilon}
by
\partial_{xx}G
.
As
\delta
is arbitrary, we have

\begin{split}Q\Big[\left\langle G_{0},\pi_{0}\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{\left\langle\partial_{s}G_{s},\pi_{s}\right\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{xx}G_{s}\left(x\right)\Phi(\rho(s,x))dx\Big\}ds=0\Big]=1.\end{split}

By Condition
3.1
, the initial condition
\pi_{0}=\pi
holds.
Thus, we conclude the limit measure
Q
is concentrated on trajectories
\pi_{\cdot}
that satisfies the weak formulation (
4.6
).

Step 5.

That
\rho(s,x)\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
follows from Lemma
7.1
.
The weak spatial differentiability of
\Phi(\rho(t,x))
and the
L^{2}
integrability of the weak derivative are addressed in Proposition
10.1
and Remark
10.2
.
When
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type,
by Lemma
7.2
and Lemma
7.4
, we obtain the boundary conditions
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)=\left(\lambda_{j}\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))\right)^{1/\alpha}
for all
j\in J_{c}
and
\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))=\Phi(c_{0})
for all
j\in J_{s}
.
When
g
is bounded, by Lemma
7.5
, it holds that

\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))\leq 1/\lambda_{j}
and

{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)={\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))=1/\lambda_{j}}
for all
j\in J_{c}
.
Therefore,
\pi_{\cdot}
is a weak solution to (
4.3
) or (
4.5
) when
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type or bounded respectively (cf. Definitions
4.4
and
4.5
).

In Section
11
, we show that there is at most one weak solution

\pi_{\cdot}
to (
4.3
) or (
4.5
).
We conclude then that the sequence of
Q^{N}
converges weakly to the Dirac
measure on
\pi_{\cdot}
. Finally, as
Q^{N}
converges to
Q
with
respect to the uniform topology, we have weak convergence of
\pi^{N}_{t}
for each
0\leq t\leq T
.
That is, for all
G
in
C_{c}(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s})
,

\langle G,\pi_{t}^{N}\rangle
weakly converges to the constant

\langle G,\pi_{t}\rangle
, and therefore convergence in probability as
stated in (
4.2
) and (
4.4
).
To finish the proof, it remains to extend the test function from
G\in C_{c}(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s})
to
G\in C(\mathbb{T})
. When
g
is bounded, it holds that
C_{c}(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s})=C(\mathbb{T})
as
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}=\emptyset
.
When
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type,
notice that
\rho(t,\cdot)
is in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
for all
t\geq 0
(cf. Lemma
7.1
). Then, the desired extension follows from standard approximations and (
5.4
) of Lemma
5.2
.

6.
Tightness

In this section, we address the tightness of the probability measures associated with the trajectories of empirical measures
\pi_{\cdot}^{N}
. Recall that
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}
is the macroscopic locations of the super-slow sites; in particular,
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}=\emptyset
when
g
is bounded.
To unify treatmentss, in both rate
g
settings, we let
\pazocal{M}
be the space of locally finite nonnegative measures on
\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}
.
Let
C_{c}(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s})
be the space of continuous functions with compact support on
\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}
. Let
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a countable dense set in
C_{c}(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s})
in the sense that for all
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s})
there exists a subsequence
\{f_{n_{k}}\}
such that
{\rm supp\,}f_{n_{k}}\subset{\rm supp\,}f
for all
k
and
f_{n_{k}}
converges uniformly to
f
.
Equipped with the distance

d(\mu,\nu)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}\frac{\left|\int_{0}^{1}f_{k}(d\mu-d\nu)\right|}{1+\left|\int_{0}^{1}f_{k}(d\mu-d\nu)\right|}.

the space
(\pazocal{M},d(\cdot,\cdot))
is a complete separable metric space.
The metric
d(\cdot,\cdot)
realizes the vague topology,
that is,
\lim_{n\to\infty}d(\mu_{n},\mu)=0
if and only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int fd\mu_{n}=\int fd\mu
for all
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s})
.

Let
Q^{N}
be the
probability measure on the trajectory space
D([0,T],\pazocal{M})
governing

\pi_{\cdot}^{N}
when the process starts from
\mu^{N}
.
We show that
\left\{Q^{N}\right\}
is tight with respect to the
uniform topology, stronger than the Skorokhod topology on

D([0,T],\pazocal{M})
.

Lemma 6.1
.

\left\{Q^{N}\right\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is relatively compact with respect to the uniform topology.
As a consequence, all limit points
Q
are supported on trajectories
\{\pi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
vaguely continuous on
\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}
, that is, for all
G\in C_{c}(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s})
, the mapping
t\in[0,T]\mapsto\langle G,\pi_{t}\rangle
is continuous.

Proof.

To deduce that
\{Q^{N}\}
is relatively compact with respect to uniform
topology, we show the following items (cf. Theorem 15.5 in

[
8
]
).

(1)

For each
t\in[0,T]
,
\epsilon>0
, there exists a compact set
K_{t,\epsilon}\subset\pazocal{M}
such that

(6.1)

\sup_{N}Q^{N}\left[\pi^{N}_{\cdot}:\pi^{N}_{t}\notin K_{t,\epsilon}\right]\leq\epsilon.

(2)

For every
\epsilon>0
,

(6.2)

\limsup_{r\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}Q^{N}\Big[\pi^{N}_{\cdot}:\sup_{|t-s|<r}d(\pi^{N}_{t},\pi^{N}_{s})>\epsilon\Big]=0.

Step 1.

We first consider (
6.1
).
Notice that, for any
A>0
, the set
\left\{\mu\in\pazocal{M}:\langle 1,\mu\rangle\leq A\right\}
is compact in
\pazocal{M}
.
Also, by (
5.3
), we have
\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[N^{-1}\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}}\eta_{t}(k)\Big]\leq C
for some constant
C<\infty
independent of
N
. As

Q^{N}\big[\langle 1,\pi^{N}_{t}\rangle>A\big]\leq A^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{N}\big[N^{-1}\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}}\eta_{t}(k)\big]
, the first condition (
6.1
) is checked by taking
A
large.

Step 2.

To verify the second condition (
6.2
), it is enough
to show a counterpart of the condition for the distributions of

\langle G,\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\rangle
where
G
is any smooth test
function compactly supported on
\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}
(cf. p. 54,
[
18
]
). In other words, we need to
show, for every
\epsilon>0
,

(6.3)

\limsup_{r\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}Q^{N}\Big[\pi^{N}_{\cdot}:\sup_{|t-s|<r}\Big|\langle G,\pi^{N}_{t}\rangle-\langle G,\pi^{N}_{s}\rangle\Big|>\epsilon\Big]=0.

Note that

\left\langle G,\pi^{N}_{t}\right\rangle=\left\langle G,\pi^{N}_{0}\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}N^{2}L_{N}\left\langle G,\pi^{N}_{s}\right\rangle ds+M^{N,G}_{t}
.
Then, we only need to consider the oscillations of
\int_{0}^{t}N^{2}L_{N}\left\langle G,\pi^{N}_{s}\right\rangle ds

and
M^{N,G}_{t}
respectively.

Step 3.

For the oscillations of the martingale
M^{N,G}_{t}
,
by
\big|M^{N,G}_{t}-M^{N,G}_{s}\big|\leq\big|M^{N,G}_{t}\big|+\big|M^{N,G}_{s}\big|
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{N}\big[\sup_{|t-s|<r}\big|M^{N,G}_{t}-M^{N,G}_{s}\big|>\epsilon\big]\leq 2\mathbb{P}_{N}\big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big|M^{N,G}_{t}\big|>\epsilon/2\big]
.
Using Chebyshev and Doob’s inequality, we further bound it by

\dfrac{8}{\epsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big|M^{N,G}_{t}\big|\Big)^{2}\Big]\leq\dfrac{32}{\epsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\big(M^{N,G}_{T}\big)^{2}\Big]=\dfrac{32}{\epsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{N}\langle M^{N,G}\rangle_{T}.

By Lemma
5.1
,
\mathbb{E}_{N}\langle M^{N,G}\rangle_{T}=O(N^{-1})
.
Then, we conclude

\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{N}\Big[\sup_{|t-s|<r}\left|M^{N,G}_{t}-M^{N,G}_{s}\right|>\epsilon\Big]=0.

Step 4.

To conclude (
6.3
), it suffices to show

\limsup_{r\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}Q^{N}\Big[\sup_{|t-s|<r}\int_{s}^{t}\big|N^{2}L_{N}\left\langle G,\pi^{N}_{\tau}\right\rangle\big|d\tau>\epsilon\Big]=0.

The absolute value term is bounded above by

\|\Delta G\|_{\infty}\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}}g_{k,N}(\eta_{\tau}(k))
(cf. (
5.2
) applied to
\pi^{N}_{\cdot}
).
Then, by Markov’s inequality, it remains to show

\limsup_{r\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\sup_{|t-s|<r}\int_{s}^{t}\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s,N}}g_{k,N}(\eta_{\tau}(k))d\tau\Big]=0.

By Lemma
5.2
,

\mathbb{E}_{N}\big[\int_{0}^{T}N^{-1}g_{k,N}(\eta_{t}(k))dt\big]

vanishes as
N\to\infty
for defect sites
k\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{c,N}
or
{\mathfrak{D}}_{b,N}
.
Therefore, we may restrict the summation term in the previous display over
k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
.
Note that for each such
k
we have
g_{k,N}(\cdot)=g(\cdot)
.
When
g
is bounded, as

N^{-1}\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}}g(\eta_{\tau}(k))\leq\|g\|_{\infty}
, the lemma is proved for this case.

The rest of this proof focuses on the case when
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type. Since
g(\cdot)
grows at most linearly, we are left to show

(6.4)

\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\sup_{|t-s|<r}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}}\eta_{\tau}(k)d\tau\Big|\Big]=0.

Step 5.

To show (
6.4
). we introduce a truncation
\mathbbm{1}_{\eta(k)\leq A}
with
A>0
.
Notice that

\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\sup_{|t-s|<r}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}}\eta_{\tau}(k)\mathbbm{1}_{\eta_{\tau}(k)\leq A}d\tau\Big|\Big]\leq\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\sup_{|t-s|<r}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}Ad\tau\Big|\Big]\leq rA

which vanishes when taking

N\to\infty
,
r\to 0
, and
A\to\infty
in order.
It remains to show the error term with
\mathbbm{1}_{\eta(k)>A}
also vanishes in the limit.
Note that the error term is estimated by

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\sup_{|t-s|<r}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}}\eta_{\tau}(k)\mathbbm{1}_{\eta_{\tau}(k)>A}d\tau\Big|\Big]\leq\ &\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}}\eta_{\tau}(k)\mathbbm{1}_{\eta_{\tau}(k)>A}d\tau\Big]\\
=&\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}}\eta_{\tau}(k)\mathbbm{1}_{\eta_{\tau}(k)>A}\Big]d\tau.\end{split}

Recall the initial entropy bound
H(\mu^{N}|\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N})<CN
. By entropy inequality, for any
\tau\in[0,T]
and
B>0
, the term
\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[N^{-1}\sum\eta_{\tau}(k)\mathbbm{1}_{\eta_{\tau}(k)>A}\Big]
is further bounded above by

(6.5)

\dfrac{C}{B}+\dfrac{1}{BN}\ln E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\Big[\exp\Big\{B\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}}\eta(k)\mathbbm{1}_{\eta(k)>A}\Big\}\Big].

Since, with respect to
\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}
, the
\eta(k)
’s in (
6.5
) are i.i.d. with common distribution
\pazocal{P}_{\varphi}
for
\varphi=\Phi(c_{0})
,
we have (
6.5
) is equal to

\dfrac{C}{B}+\dfrac{N-n_{0}}{BN}\ln E_{\pazocal{P}_{\varphi}}\left[e^{BX\mathbbm{1}_{X>A}}\right]\leq\dfrac{C}{B}+B^{-1}\ln E_{\pazocal{P}_{\varphi}}\left[e^{BX\mathbbm{1}_{X>A}}\right].

Here,
n_{0}
is the number of defect sites and the distribution of
X
is
\pazocal{P}_{\varphi}
. As
E_{\pazocal{P}_{\varphi}}[e^{BX}]<\infty
for all
B>0
,
we have
B^{-1}\ln E_{\pazocal{P}_{\varphi}}\big[e^{BX\mathbbm{1}_{X>A}}\big]\to 0
when taking

A\to\infty
and then
B\to\infty
.
Hence, we obtain

\lim_{A\to\infty}\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\sup_{|t-s|<r}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}}\eta_{\tau}(k)\mathbbm{1}_{\eta_{\tau}(k)>A}d\tau\Big|\Big]=0

which completes the proof.
∎

7.
Properties of limit measures

By Lemma
6.1
, the sequence
\left\{Q^{N}\right\}
is relatively compact with respect to the uniform topology.
Consider any convergent subsequence of
Q^{N}
and relabel so that
Q^{N}\Rightarrow Q
.
We now consider absolute continuity and boundary behaviors at defect sites for trajectories under
Q
.

7.1.
Absolute continuity

We now address absolute continuity.

Lemma 7.1
.

We have

Q
is supported on trajectories whose restriction on
\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{c}
is absolutely continuous and the density is in
L^{\infty}
uniformly in
t
: there exists
c\geq 0
such that

Q\Big[\pi_{\cdot}:\pi_{t}(dx)=\rho(t,x)dx+\sum_{j\in J_{c}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)\delta_{x_{j}}(dx)\text{ with }\|\rho(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}<c\text{ for all }0\leq t\leq T\Big]=1.

Proof.

Let
D:=\mathbb{T}\setminus\{{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}\cup{\mathfrak{D}}_{c}\}

and
C_{c}^{+}(D)
be the space of nonnegative continuous functions with compact support on
D
,
equipped with topology of uniform convergence on compact sets.
Take
\left\{G_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a dense sequence of
C_{c}^{+}(D)
.
The lemma will follow if we have, for some
c\geq 0
,

Q\Big[\pi_{\cdot}:\langle G_{n},\pi_{t}\rangle\leq\langle G_{n},c\rangle\text{ for all }0\leq t\leq T\text{ and }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big]=1.

Here
\langle G,c\rangle
is shorthand for
c\int_{\mathbb{T}}G(x)dx
.

To this end, recall
\kappa_{c^{\prime}}^{N}
defined in Condition
3.1
. Let
\nu^{N}
denote
\mathscr{R}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}
when
g
is of
n^{\alpha}
type and
\kappa_{c^{\prime}}^{N}
when
g
is bounded. By the product structure of
\nu^{N}
and Chebyshev inequality, for each
\delta>0
, we have
\nu^{N}[|\langle G_{n},\pi^{N}\rangle-\langle G_{n},c^{\prime}\rangle|>\delta]\to 0
as
N\to\infty
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
t\in[0,T]
. By attractiveness (cf. Section
3.2
),

Q^{N}\big[\langle G_{n},\pi^{N}_{t}\rangle\leq\langle G_{n},c^{\prime}\rangle+\varepsilon\big]
is bounded from below by

\nu^{N}\big[\langle G_{n},\pi^{N}\rangle\leq\langle G_{n},c^{\prime}\rangle+\varepsilon\big].

Then, we have, for all
t\geq 0
and
\varepsilon>0

\lim_{N\to\infty}Q^{N}\left[\langle G_{n},\pi_{t}^{N}\rangle\leq\langle G_{n},c^{\prime}\rangle+\varepsilon\right]=1.

As compactness of
\{Q^{N}\}
was shown in the uniform topology in Lemma
6.1
, the distribution of
\langle G_{n},\pi^{N}_{t}\rangle
under
Q^{N}
converges weakly to
\langle G_{n},\pi_{t}\rangle
under
Q
.
Hence, for any
\varepsilon>0
and
t\in[0,T]
, we have

Q\Big[\langle G_{n},\pi_{t}\rangle\leq\langle G_{n},c^{\prime}\rangle+\varepsilon\Big]\geq\limsup_{N\to\infty}Q^{N}\Big[\langle G_{n},\pi_{t}^{N}\rangle\leq\langle G_{n},c^{\prime}\rangle+\varepsilon\Big]=1.

Since
Q
is supported on vaguely continuous trajectories by Lemma
6.1
, we obtain for all
\varepsilon>0
that

Q\Big[\langle G_{n},\pi_{t}\rangle\leq\langle G_{n},c^{\prime}\rangle+\varepsilon\text{ for all }0\leq t\leq T,\,n\in\mathbb{N}\Big]=1.

Then, we conclude the lemma by taking
\varepsilon\to 0
and choosing
c=c^{\prime}
.
∎

7.2.
Boundary behavior

We proceed by showing the behavior of the limit density
\rho(t,x)
around the defects
x_{j}
in
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}
or
{\mathfrak{D}}_{c}
.
Notice that, part of the results presented here are in terms of
\rho(t,x)
as
x\to x_{j}+
.
The corresponding results for
x\to x_{j}-
follow from similar arguments. In the following, we will make use of a ‘local’ replacement lemma Lemma
9.2
proved in Section
9
.

For each
\theta\in(0,1)
,
let
\hat{\iota}_{\theta}:(0,\theta)\mapsto[0,1]

be a compactly supported continuous function such that
\hat{\iota}_{\theta}(x)=1
for
x\in

(\theta^{2},\theta-\theta^{2})
.
Let
\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|
denote the
L^{1}
norm
\int_{0}^{\theta}\hat{\iota}_{\theta}(x)dx
and
\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|^{-1}:=(\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|)^{-1}
.
Notice that
\big|\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|^{-1}-\theta^{-1}\big|
is bounded from above for all
\theta
small since

\big|\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|^{-1}-\theta^{-1}\big|

is less than
\big|(\theta-2\theta^{2})^{-1}-\theta^{-1}\big|
which approaches
2
as
\theta\to 0
.

We first describe behavior near ‘super-slow’ sites in the
n^{\alpha}
setting.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
g
be of
n^{\alpha}
type.
Then, for any
j\in J_{s}
,
\delta>0
, and
G\in C[0,T]
,
we have

(7.1)

\lim_{\theta\to 0}Q\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\big(\Phi\big(\rho^{+,\theta}_{x_{j}}(s)\big)-\Phi(c_{0})\big)ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0

where
\rho^{+,\theta}_{x_{j}}(s):=\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|^{-1}\langle\hat{\iota}_{\theta}(\cdot-x_{j}),\rho\rangle=\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{\iota}_{\theta}(x-x_{j})\rho(s,x)dx
.
Consequently,

(7.2)

Q\big[\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))=\Phi(c_{0})\text{ for a.e.~$t\in[0,T]$ and all $j\in J_{s}$}\big]=1.

Proof.

The proof of is split into steps. We show (
7.1
) in the first four steps and the last step derives (
7.2
) from (
7.1
).

Step 1.

Fix
j\in J_{s}
and also a test function
G\in C[0,T]
for later use. We first show that

(7.3)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{s\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{N}\big[\big|N^{-\beta_{j}}g(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))-N^{-\beta_{j}}(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))^{\alpha}\big|\big]=0.

Note that
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}
.
For each
\epsilon>0
, let
A=A(\epsilon)
be such that

|n^{\alpha}/g(n)-1|<\epsilon
for all
n>A
.
Then, the expectation term in (
7.3
) is bounded above by

E_{1}+E_{2}
where
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are the same expectation as in (
7.3
) with the integrand multiplied by indicators

\mathbbm{1}_{\eta_{s}(k_{j,N})\leq A}
and

\mathbbm{1}_{\eta_{s}(k_{j,N})>A}
respectively.
For each
A
, the term
E_{1}
vanishes as
N\to\infty
.
By the definition of
A
and Lemma
5.2
, the term
E_{2}
is further bounded above by

\epsilon\sup_{s\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{N}\big[N^{-\beta_{j}}g(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))\big]=O(\epsilon)
.
Letting
\epsilon\to 0
, we conclude (
7.3
).

Step 2.

We now argue that
N^{-\beta_{j}}(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))^{\alpha}
may be replaced by
N^{-\beta_{j}}(\eta_{0}(k_{j,N}))^{\alpha}
.
Let
r_{N,s}=N^{-\beta_{j}/\alpha}(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N})-\eta_{0}(k_{j,N}))
.
Let
\tau>0
be a constant such that there are no other defects within a
\tau
-neighborhood of
x_{j}
.
Take a test function
F:\mathbb{T}\mapsto[0,1]
such that
F
has compact support in
(x_{j}-\tau,x_{j}+\tau)
and
F(x)=1
for
|x-x_{j}|\leq\tau/2
.
Recall
\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{N}
from (
5.1
) and notice that

|r_{N,s}|\leq N^{1-\beta_{j}/\alpha}\big|\langle F,\widehat{\pi}_{s}^{N}\rangle-\langle F,\widehat{\pi}_{0}^{N}\rangle\big|+N^{-\beta_{j}/\alpha}{\sum_{k}}^{\circ}(\eta_{s}(k)+\eta_{0}(k)):=I_{1}+I_{2}.

where the
\sum_{k}^{\circ}
term is summation over
k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
such that
|k/N-x_{j}|\leq\tau
and
k\neq k_{j,N}
.

As
\beta_{j}>\alpha
, (
5.3
) of Lemma
5.2
asserts that

\sup_{s\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{N}[I_{2}]
vanishes as
N\to\infty
.
To show
\mathbb{E}_{N}[I_{1}]
vanishes, notice that

\left\langle F,\widehat{\pi}^{N}_{s}\right\rangle-\left\langle F,\widehat{\pi}^{N}_{0}\right\rangle=\int_{0}^{s}N^{2}L_{N}\left\langle F,\widehat{\pi}^{N}_{t}\right\rangle dt+M^{N,F}_{s}
.
Using the generator computation
(
5.2
) and the jump rate bound (
5.6
), we have

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}_{N}\big[N^{2}L_{N}\left\langle F,\widehat{\pi}^{N}_{t}\right\rangle\big]\leq\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\Delta_{N}F(\cdot)\|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}g_{k,N}(\eta_{t}(k))\Big]<\infty.

Also, by Lemma
5.1
, the martingale term vanishes as
its variance
\sup_{0\leq s\leq T}\mathbb{E}_{N}\langle M^{N,G}\rangle_{s}=o(1)
.
Thus, as
\beta_{j}>\alpha
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{N}[I_{1}]
and, therefore,
\mathbb{E}_{N}[|r_{N,s}|]

vanishes as
N\to\infty
uniformly for all
s\in[0,T]
.

As
\alpha\in(0,1]
, by the elementary inequality
(|x|+|y|)^{\alpha}\leq|x|^{\alpha}+|y|^{\alpha}
, we have

N^{-\beta_{j}}|(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))^{\alpha}-(\eta_{0}(k_{j,N}))^{\alpha}|\leq|r_{N,s}|^{\alpha}.

Therefore, we conclude

(7.4)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq s\leq T}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[N^{-\beta_{j}}\big|(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))^{\alpha}-(\eta_{0}(k_{j,N}))^{\alpha}\big|\Big]=0.

In particular,
it follows from (
7.3
)
and (
7.4
) that,
for any
\delta>0
,

(7.5)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{N}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\big(N^{-\beta_{j}}g(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))-N^{-\beta_{j}}(\eta_{0}(k_{j,N}))^{\alpha}\big)ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0.

Step 3.

In this step, we show that
N^{-\beta_{j}}(\eta_{0}(k_{j,N}))^{\alpha}
may be replaced by

\varphi=\lambda_{j}\Phi(c_{0})
.
Precisely, as
\eta_{0}
has distribution
\mu^{N}
, we show that

(7.6)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mu^{N}\big[\big|N^{-\beta_{j}/\alpha}\xi(k_{j,N})-\varphi^{1/\alpha}\big|>\delta\big]=0.

Indeed, let

\pazocal{U}_{N}:=\big\{\big|N^{-\beta_{j}/\alpha}\xi(k_{j,N})-\varphi^{1/\alpha}\big|\geq\delta\big\}
.
Fix
r_{0}\in(1,\beta_{j}/\alpha)
.
By the entropy inequality,

\mu^{N}\left[\pazocal{U}_{N}\right]=E_{\mu^{N}}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\pazocal{U}_{N}}\right]\leq N^{-r_{0}}H(\mu^{N}|\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N})+N^{-r_{0}}\ln E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\big[e^{N^{r_{0}}\mathbbm{1}_{\pazocal{U}_{N}}}\big].

Since
H(\mu^{N}|\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N})=O(N)
(cf. Condition
3.1
) and
E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\big[e^{N^{r_{0}}\mathbbm{1}_{\pazocal{U}_{N}}}\big]\leq 1+e^{N^{r_{0}}}\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}\left[\pazocal{U}_{N}\right]
,
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mu^{N}\left[\pazocal{U}_{N}\right]\leq\max\big\{0,\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-r_{0}}\ln\big(e^{N^{r_{0}}}\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}\left[\pazocal{U}_{N}\right]\big)\big\}.

By the following Lemma
7.3
, we have
\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-r_{0}}\ln\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}\left[\pazocal{U}_{N}\right]=-\infty
,
and therefore, (
7.6
) holds.

Step 4.

Putting together (
7.5
) and (
7.6
), we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{N}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\big(N^{-\beta_{j}}g(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))-\lambda_{j}\Phi(c_{9})\big)ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0.

For each
\theta>0
, let
\eta_{s}^{\theta N,+}(k)=(\theta N)^{-1}\sum_{0<j-k\leq\theta N}\eta_{s}(k)
.
By Lemma
9.2
, we may further replace
N^{-\beta_{j}}g(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))
by
\lambda_{j}\Phi(\eta_{s}^{\theta N,+}(k_{j,N}))
and obtain

(7.7)

\lim_{\theta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{N}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\big(\Phi(\eta_{s}^{\theta N,+}(k_{j,N}))-\Phi(c_{0})\big)ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0.

Notice that

\big|\eta_{s}^{\theta N,+}(k_{j,N})-\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|^{-1}\langle\hat{\iota}_{\theta}(\cdot-x_{j}),\pi_{s}^{N}\rangle\big|

is bounded above by

\big|\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|^{-1}-\theta^{-1}\big|N^{-1}\sum_{0<k/N-x_{j}\leq\theta}\eta_{s}(k)+(\theta N)^{-1}\sum_{(k/N-x_{j})\in(0,\theta^{2})\cup(\theta-\theta^{2},\theta]}\eta_{s}(k).

Using the particle numbers bound (
5.4
) and that
\big|\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|^{-1}-\theta^{-1}\big|
is bounded for all
\theta
small, the
\mathbb{E}_{N}
expectation of the previous display vanishes as
N\to\infty
.
Since
\Phi(\cdot)
is Lipschitz, we may replace
\eta_{s}^{\theta N,+}(k_{j,N})
in (
7.7
) by
\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|^{-1}\langle\hat{\iota}_{\theta}(\cdot-x_{j}),\pi_{s}^{N}\rangle
to obtain

\lim_{\theta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}Q^{N}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\big(\Phi\big(\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|^{-1}\langle\hat{\iota}_{\theta}(\cdot-x_{j}),\pi_{s}^{N}\rangle\big)-\Phi(c_{0})\big)ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0.

Since the absolute value term in the previous display is continuous (in the uniform topology) when viewed as a function on the trajectory space
D([0,T],\pazocal{M})
, we obtain

\lim_{\theta\to 0}Q\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\big(\Phi\big(\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|^{-1}\langle\hat{\iota}_{\theta}(\cdot-x_{j}),\pi_{s}\rangle\big)-\Phi(c_{0})\big)ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0.

By Lemma
7.1
,
\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|^{-1}\langle\hat{\iota}_{\theta}(\cdot-x_{j}),\pi_{s}\rangle
is recognized as
\rho^{+,\theta}_{x_{j}}(s)
; therefore (
7.1
) follows.

Step 5.

We now conclude the proof of the lemma by showing (
7.2
) from (
7.1
).
By Lemma
7.1
and Proposition
10.1
(see also Remarks
4.6
and
10.2
), it holds almost surely under
Q
that
\|\rho(\cdot,\cdot)\|_{\infty}<\infty
and
\rho(t,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{T})
for almost all
t\in[0,T]
. Then, we have,
Q
-almost surely,
\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\Phi\big(\rho^{+,\theta}_{x_{j}}(s)\big)ds
converges to

\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\Phi(\rho(s,x_{j}))ds
.
As we have shown in (
7.1
) that
\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\Phi\big(\rho^{+,\theta}_{x_{j}}(s)\big)ds
also converges to

\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\Phi(c_{0})ds
in probability, we obtain,
for all
G\in C[0,T]
and
j\in J_{s}

Q\Big[\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\big(\Phi\big(\rho(s,x_{j})\big)-\Phi(c_{0})\big)ds=0\Big]=1.

As the continuous function space
C[0,T]
is separable with respect to the unform norm and
J_{s}
is a finite set, we further obtain

Q\Big[\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\big(\Phi\big(\rho(s,x_{j})\big)-\Phi(c_{0})\big)ds=0\text{ for all $G\in C[0,T]$ and $j\in J_{s}$}\Big]=1

which implies (
7.2
), finishing the proof.
∎

We now show the estimate used in Step 3 of the above argument.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
g
be of
n^{\alpha}
type. Let
X_{N}
be distributed according to
\pazocal{P}_{N^{\beta}\varphi}

for some
\beta>\alpha
and
\varphi\geq 0
.
Then, for any
r_{0}<\beta/\alpha

and
\delta>0

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-r_{0}}\ln P\big[\big|N^{-\beta/\alpha}X_{N}-\varphi^{1/\alpha}\big|\geq\delta\big]=-\infty.

Proof.

Let
\phi=\varphi^{1/\alpha}
.
To prove the lemma, it suffices to show that

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-r_{0}}\ln P\big[X_{N}\geq(\phi+\delta)N^{\beta/\alpha}\big]=\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-r_{0}}\ln P\big[X_{N}\leq(\phi-\delta)N^{\beta/\alpha}\big]=-\infty.

To this end, we notice that, for any
x>0
,

\begin{split}\ln P\big[X_{N}\geq(\phi+\delta)N^{\beta/\alpha}\big]\leq&\ln\big(e^{-(\phi+\delta)N^{\beta/\alpha}x}E[e^{xX_{N}}]\big)\\
=&-(\phi+\delta)N^{\beta/\alpha}x+\ln\dfrac{Z(e^{x}N^{\beta}\varphi)}{Z(N^{\beta}\varphi)}.\end{split}

By Lemma
2.6
,

\ln\dfrac{Z(e^{x}N^{\beta}\varphi)}{Z(N^{\beta}\varphi)}\sim\alpha\phi N^{\beta/\alpha}(e^{x/\alpha}-1)
.
Then, for

K_{\delta,\phi,\alpha}:=\max_{x>0}\big\{(\phi+\delta)x-\alpha\phi(e^{x/\alpha}-1)\big\}

we have

\limsup_{N\to\infty}N^{-\beta/\alpha}\ln P\big[X\geq(\phi+\delta)N^{\beta/\alpha}\big]\leq-K_{\delta,\phi,\alpha}<0.

Since
r_{0}<\beta/\alpha
, we obtain

(7.8)

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-r_{0}}\ln P\big[X_{N}\geq(\phi+\delta)N^{\beta/\alpha}\big]=-\infty.

On the other hand, for all
x>0
, we also have

\ln P\big[X_{N}\leq(\phi-\delta)N^{\beta/\alpha}\big]\leq\ln\big(e^{(\phi-\delta)N^{\beta/\alpha}x}E[e^{-xX_{N}}]\big).

By a similar argument employed to prove (
7.8
), we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-r_{0}}\ln P\big[X_{N}\leq(\phi-\delta)N^{\beta/\alpha}\big]=-\infty.

The lemma is now proved.
∎

We now consider the behavior near ‘critical’ slow sites in the
n^{\alpha}
setting used in Step 5.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
g
be of
n^{\alpha}
type.
Then, for any
j\in J_{c}
,
\delta>0
, and
G\in C[0,T]
, we have

(7.9)

\lim_{\theta\to 0}Q\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\Big(\lambda_{j}\Phi\big(\rho^{+,\theta}_{x_{j}}(s)\big)-({\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(s))^{\alpha}\Big)ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0,

where
\rho^{+,\theta}_{x_{j}}(s)=\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|^{-1}\langle\hat{\iota}_{\theta}(\cdot-x_{j}),\rho\rangle
.
Consequently,

(7.10)

Q\big[{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)=\big(\lambda_{j}\Phi\big(\rho(t,x_{j})\big)\big)^{1/\alpha}\text{ for a.e.~$t\in[0,T]$ and all $j\in J_{c}$}\big]=1.

Proof.

Fix
j\in J_{c}
and
G\in C[0,T]
.
As
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}
, arguments as in Step
1
of the proof of
Lemma
7.2

show that
N^{-\alpha}g(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))
may be replaced by
N^{-\alpha}(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))^{\alpha}
. Hence, we have

(7.11)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{N}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\Big(N^{-\alpha}g(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))-(N^{-1}\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))^{\alpha}\Big)ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0.

Let
\{F_{\theta}(x)\}
be a sequence of nonnegative smooth functions such that
F_{\theta}
’s are supported on
(x_{j}-\theta,x_{j}+\theta)
,
\|F_{\theta}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, and
F_{\theta}(x)=1
for
|x-x_{j}|\leq\theta/2
.
Then,

|\langle F_{\theta},\pi^{N}_{s}\rangle-N^{-1}\eta_{s}(k_{j,N})|\leq B_{\theta,N}(\eta_{s})

where

B_{\theta,N}(\eta)=N^{-1}\sum_{k\notin{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}}\mathbbm{1}_{(-\theta,\theta)}(k/N-x_{j})\,\eta(k)

for
\theta
small and
N
large.
As
0<\alpha\leq 1
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{N}\big[\big|\langle F_{\theta},\pi^{N}_{s}\rangle^{\alpha}-(N^{-1}\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))^{\alpha}\big|\big]\leq\mathbb{E}_{N}\big[(B_{\theta,N})^{\alpha}\big]\leq\mathbb{E}_{N}\big[B_{\theta,N}\big]^{\alpha},

which vanishes as
N\to\infty
and
\theta\to 0
by (
5.4
) in Lemma
5.2
.
Therefore, we may replace

N^{-1}\eta_{s}(k_{j,N})
in (
7.11
) to obtain

\lim_{\theta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{N}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\Big(N^{-\alpha}g(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))-\langle F_{\theta},\pi^{N}_{s}\rangle^{\alpha}\Big)ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0.

By Lemma
9.2
,
N^{-\alpha}g(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))
may be replaced by
\lambda_{j}\Phi(\eta_{s}^{\theta N,+}(k_{j,N}))
.
Moreover, following Step
4
in the proof of Lemma
7.2
to replace
\eta_{s}^{\theta N,+}(k_{j,N})
with
\rho^{+,\theta}_{x_{j}}(s)
, we obtain

\lim_{\theta\to 0}Q\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\Big(\lambda_{j}\Phi\big(\rho^{+,\theta}_{x_{j}}(s)\big)-\langle F_{\theta},\pi_{s}\rangle^{\alpha}\Big)ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0.

Moreover, by Lemma
7.1
,
\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\langle F_{\theta},\pi_{s}\rangle^{\alpha}ds
converges to

\int_{0}^{T}G(s)(\pi_{s}(\{x_{j}\}))^{\alpha}ds=\int_{0}^{T}G(s)({\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(s))^{\alpha}ds

almost surely with respect to
Q
. Hence, with these observations,
(
7.9
) in the lemma holds.
Following the argument in Step
5
of Lemma
7.2
, we obtain
(
7.10
) from
(
7.9
), finishing the proof.
∎

We now turn to the behavior near ‘critical’ slow sites in the
g
bounded setting.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
g
be bounded.
Then, for any
j\in J_{c}
,
\delta>0
, and
G\in C[0,T]
, we have

(7.12)

\lim_{\theta\to 0}Q\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|G(s)|\big(\Phi\big(\rho^{+,\theta}_{x_{j}}(s)\big)-\phi_{j}\big)ds>\delta\Big]=0

and

(7.13)

\lim_{\theta\to 0}Q\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)({\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(s)\wedge 1)\big(\phi_{j}-\Phi\big(\rho^{+,\theta}_{x_{j}}(s)\big)\big)ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0.

where
\rho^{+,\theta}_{x_{j}}(s):=\|\hat{\iota}_{\theta}\|^{-1}\langle\hat{\iota}_{\theta}(\cdot-x_{j}),\rho\rangle
,

\phi_{j}=\lambda^{-1}_{j}
,
and

a\wedge b=\min\{a,b\}
.
Consequently,

(7.14)

\displaystyle Q\big[\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))\leq\lambda_{j}^{-1},\,{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)={\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{\Phi(\rho(t,x_{j}))=\lambda_{j}^{-1}}.

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{ for a.e.~$t\in[0,T]$ and all $j\in J_{c}$}\big]=1.

Proof.

Fix
j\in J_{c}
and
G\in C[0,T]
.
We first address (
7.12
).
As
g(\cdot)\leq 1
, it is trivial that, for all
N

\mathbb{P}_{N}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|G(s)|\left(\lambda_{j}^{-1}g(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))-\phi_{j}\right)ds>\delta\Big]=0.

We now use the local ‘replacement’ Lemma
9.2
to replace

\lambda_{j}^{-1}g(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))
by

\Phi(\eta_{s}^{\theta N,+}(k_{j,N}))
, to obtain

\lim_{\theta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{N}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|G(s)|\left(\Phi(\eta_{s}^{\theta N,+}(k_{j,N}))-\phi_{j}\right)ds>\delta\Big]=0.

Then, (
7.12
) follows by taking
N\to\infty
,
cf. Step
4
in Lemma
7.2
.

To show (
7.13
),
we take the same sequence
\{F_{\theta}(x)\}
from the proof of Lemma
7.4
.
We now observe a key microscopic boundary relation:

(7.15)

\lim_{\theta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\big|\left(\langle F_{\theta},\pi^{N}_{s}\rangle\wedge 1\right)\big(\phi_{j}-\lambda_{j}^{-1}g(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))\big)\big|ds\Big]=0.

Indeed, as
\lim_{n\to\infty}g(n)=1
, let
A=A(\varepsilon)
be such that
|g(n)-1|<\varepsilon
for all
n\geq A
.
Then, in the above display, on the one hand, the absolute value is bounded above by
\varepsilon
when
\eta_{s}(k_{j,N})\geq A
. On the other hand,
\langle F_{\theta},\pi^{N}_{s}\rangle
is less than
N^{-1}(A+\sum_{k}^{\circ,\theta}\eta_{s}(k))

when
\eta_{s}(k_{j,N})<A
.
Here, the sum
\sum_{k}^{\circ,\theta}
is over all
k
such that
|k/N-x_{j}|\leq\theta
and
k\neq k_{j,N}
. In considering these cases, when

\eta_{s}(k_{j,N})<A
and
\eta_{s}(k_{j,N})\geq A
respectively,
the estimate (
7.15
) follows from (
5.4
) of the ‘particle numbers’ Lemma
5.2
.

Now, as
|\langle F_{\theta},\pi^{N}_{s}\rangle\wedge 1|
is bounded, we observe that the local ‘replacement’ Lemma
9.2

remains effective when the test function is taken in form
G(s)\left(\langle F_{\theta},\pi^{N}_{s}\rangle\wedge 1\right)
.
Thus, we may replace
\lambda_{j}^{-1}g(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))
by

\Phi(\eta_{s}^{\theta N,+}(k_{j,N}))
and obtain

\lim_{\theta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{N}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\left(\langle F_{\theta},\pi^{N}_{s}\rangle\wedge 1\right)\big(\phi_{j}-\Phi(\eta_{s}^{\theta N,+}(k_{j,N}))\big)ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0.

Again, by following Step
4
in Lemma
7.2
, we have

\lim_{\theta\to 0}Q\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\left(\langle F_{\theta},\pi_{s}\rangle\wedge 1\right)\big(\phi_{j}-\Phi\big(\rho^{+,\theta}_{x_{j}}(s)\big)\big)ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0.

Also, by Lemma
7.1
,
it holds that

\lim_{\theta\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\left|\left(\langle F_{\theta},\pi_{s}\rangle\wedge 1\right)-\left({\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(s)\wedge 1\right)\right|ds=0
almost surely with respect to
Q
.
As
\phi_{j}
and
\Phi(\cdot)
are bounded,
we conclude the proof of (
7.13
).

It remains to show (
7.14
). Following Step
5
in Lemma
7.2
, we obtain from (
7.12
) that,
Q
-almost surely,

\Phi(\rho(s,x_{j}))\leq\phi_{j}

for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
and all
j\in J_{c}
.
To finish the proof, it suffices to show that
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(s)=0
whenever
\Phi(\rho(s,x_{j}))<\phi_{j}
. In fact,
by (
7.13
), we have

({\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)\wedge 1)\big(\phi_{j}-\Phi\big(\rho(s,x_{j})\big)\big)=0
.
As we have
\Phi(\rho(s,x_{j}))\leq\phi_{j}
, we have that
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(s)\wedge 1=0
(therefore
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(s)=0
) when
\Phi(\rho(s,x_{j}))<\phi_{j}
, completing the proof.
∎

8.
Local
1
and
2
-block estimates of bulk sites

In this section we address the
1
and
2
-block estimates for non-defect sites
\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
.
These estimates are obtained through a Rayleigh-type estimation of a
variational eigenvalue expression derived from a Feynman-Kac bound. These bounds will hold in either
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}
or
g
bounded settings.

8.1.
Local
1
-block estimate

We start from recalling the concept of spectral gap which is used to prove our local
1
-block estimate.
For
k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
and
l\geq 1
, define the set
\Lambda_{k,l}=\left\{k-l,k-l+1,\ldots,k+l\right\}\subset\mathbb{T}_{N}
.
Let
\Omega_{k,l}=\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\Lambda_{k,l}}
be the state space of
configurations restricted on sites
\Lambda_{k,l}
.
Define the state space
of configurations with exactly
j
particles on the sites
\Lambda_{k,l}
:

\Omega_{k,l,j}=\Big\{\eta\in\Omega_{k,l}:\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{k,l}}\eta(x)=j\Big\}.

Consider the generator
L_{k,l}
on
\Omega_{k,l}
given by

\begin{split}L_{k,l}f(\eta)=&\sum_{x,x+1\in\Lambda_{k,l}}\Big\{g(\eta(x))\left[f\left(\eta^{x,x+1}\right)-f(\eta)\right]+g(\eta(x))\left[f\left(\eta^{x+1,x}\right)-f(\eta)\right]\Big\}.\end{split}

Recall the generator
L_{N}
from (
2.1
).
Notice that, for each
k,l
such that
\Lambda_{k,l}\cap{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}=\emptyset
, the generator
L_{k,l}
coincides with
L_{N}
localized on
\Lambda_{k,l}
.

For any
\rho>0
, let
\nu_{\rho}
be the product measure on

\Omega=\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\mathbb{T}_{N}}
with common marginal

\pazocal{P}_{\Phi(\rho)}
on each site
k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
,
and let
\nu^{\rho}_{k,l}
be its restriction to
\Omega_{k,l}
.
Let
\nu_{k,l,j}
be
\nu_{k,l}^{\rho}
conditioned on total number of particles on
\Lambda_{k,l}
being
j
.
Notice that
\nu_{k,l,j}
does not depend on
\rho
. It is well-known that both
\nu^{\rho}_{k,l}
and
\nu_{k,l,j}
are invariant measures with respect to the localized generator
L_{k,l}
(cf.
[
1
]
).
For
\kappa=\nu_{k,l}^{\rho}
or
\nu_{k,l,j}
,
the corresponding Dirichlet form is given by

(8.1)

\begin{split}E_{\kappa}\left[f(-L_{k,l}f)\right]=&\sum_{x,x+1\in\Lambda_{k,l}}E_{\kappa}\left[g(\eta(x))\left(f\left(\eta^{x,x+1}\right)-f(\eta)\right)^{2}\right].\end{split}

For
j\geq 1
, let
b_{l,j}
be the spectral gap of
-L_{k,l}
on
\Omega_{k,l,j}
(cf. p. 374,
[
18
]
):

(8.2)

b_{l,j}:=\inf_{f}\dfrac{E_{\nu_{k,l,j}}[f(-L_{k,l}f)]}{{\rm Var}_{\nu_{k,l,j}}(f)}

where the infimum is taken over all
L^{2}(\nu_{k,l,j})
functions
f
from
\Omega_{k,l,j}
to
\mathbb{R}
.
For all
l,j\geq 1
, as
\Omega_{k,l,j}
is a finite space and the localized process is irreducible, we have
b_{l,j}>0
.
As a consequence, we have the following Poincaré inequality: for all
f\in L^{2}(\nu_{k,l,j})

(8.3)

{\rm Var}_{\nu_{k,l,j}}(f)\leq C_{l,j}E_{\nu{k,l,j}}\left[f(-L_{k,l}f)\right]

where

C_{l,j}:=b_{l,j}^{-1}<\infty
for
j\geq 1
and
C_{l,0}=0
.
We remark that even though, for a large class of
g(\cdot)
’s, sharp estimates of

b_{l,j}
are available in the literature,
we will only need that
b_{l,j}
is strictly positive for all
l,j\geq 1
.

We now prove the local
1
-block estimate for regular sites:

Lemma 8.1

(Local
1
-block estimate)
.

For any bounded function
G
on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}
, we have

\limsup_{l\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{k,k^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s,k^{\prime}/N)\left(g(\eta_{s}(k^{\prime}))-\Phi(\eta_{s}^{l}(k))\right)ds\Big|\Big]=0

where the
\sup
is taken over all
k
and
k^{\prime}
such that
k^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{k,l}
and
\Lambda_{k,l}\cap{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

We separate the argument into
5
steps.

Step 1.

We first introduce a cutoff of large densities.
Let

V_{k,k^{\prime},l}(s,\eta):=G(s,k^{\prime}/N)\left(g(\eta(k^{\prime}))-\Phi(\eta^{l}(k))\right).

As
g(n)\leq g^{*}n
and
\Phi(x)\leq g^{*}x
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}V_{k,k^{\prime},l}(s,\eta_{s})\mathbbm{1}_{\eta_{s}^{l}(k)>A}ds\Big|\Big]\leq g^{*}\|G\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{N}\left[\left(\eta_{s}(k^{\prime})+\eta_{s}^{l}(k)\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\eta_{s}^{l}(k)>A}\right]ds.

By attractiveness (cf. Section
3.2
), in both
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}
and bounded settings (as
\Lambda_{k,l}\cap{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}=\emptyset
, the stochastic bound
\kappa^{N}_{c^{\prime}}
and
\mathscr{R}^{N}_{c^{\prime}}
agree with
\nu_{c^{\prime}}
on
\Lambda_{k,l}
), the last expectation is bounded above by

\begin{split}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c^{\prime}}^{N}}\left[\left(\eta(k^{\prime})+\eta^{l}(k)\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\eta^{l}(k)>A}\right]\leq&A^{-1}E_{\nu_{c^{\prime}}}\left[\left(\eta(k^{\prime})\eta^{l}(k)\right)+\left(\eta^{l}(k)\right)^{2}\right]\\
\leq&A^{-1}E_{\nu_{c^{\prime}}}\left[\left(\eta(k^{\prime})\right)^{2}+2\left(\eta^{l}(k)\right)^{2}\right].\end{split}

Notice that
\left(\eta^{l}(k)\right)^{2}\leq(2l+1)^{-1}\sum_{j\in\Lambda_{k,l}}\left(\eta(j)\right)^{2}

and, under
\nu_{c^{\prime}}
,
\left\{\eta(j)\right\}_{j\in\Lambda_{k,l}}
has common distribution
\pazocal{P}_{\Phi(c^{\prime})}
.
We obtain
\mathbb{E}_{N}\big[\big|\int_{0}^{T}V_{k,k^{\prime},l}(s,\eta_{s})\mathbbm{1}_{\eta_{s}^{l}(k)>A}ds\big|\big]\to 0
as
N
,
l
, and
A
approach
\infty
in this order.

Therefore, to prove the lemma, it will be enough to show, for all
A>0
, that

\limsup_{l\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{k,k^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}(s,\eta_{s})ds\Big|\Big]=0

where
V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}(s,\eta):=V_{k,k^{\prime},l}(s,\eta)\mathbbm{1}_{\{\eta^{l}(k)\leq A\}}
.

Step 2.

As
H(\mu^{N}|\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N})\leq CN
for some
C<\infty
, it follows from the entropy inequality

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}(s,\eta_{s})ds\Big|\Big]\leq\dfrac{C}{\gamma}+\dfrac{1}{\gamma N}\ln\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\gamma N\Big|\int_{0}^{T}V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}(s,\eta_{s})ds\Big|\Big\}\Big].\end{split}

The absolute value in the right hand side of last inequality can be dropped by using
e^{|x|}\leq e^{x}+e^{-x}
.
By Feynman-Kac formula (cf. p.336,
[
18
]
),

\begin{split}\dfrac{1}{\gamma N}\ln\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\gamma N\int_{0}^{T}V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}(s,\eta_{s})ds\Big\}\Big]\leq\dfrac{1}{\gamma N}\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{N,l}(s)ds\end{split}

where
\lambda_{N,l}(s)
is the largest eigenvalue of

N^{2}L_{N}+\gamma NV_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}(s,\eta)
.

Step 3.

Fix
s\in[0,T]
; we will omit the argument
s
in

\lambda_{N,l}(s)
to simplify notation, that is
\lambda_{N,l}=\lambda_{N,l}(s)
.
Note the variational formula for
\lambda_{N,l}
:

(\gamma N)^{-1}\lambda_{N,l}=\sup_{f}\big\{E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\big[V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}f\big]-\gamma^{-1}NE_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\big[\sqrt{f}(-L_{N}\sqrt{f})\big]\big\},

where the supremum is over all
f
which are densities with respect to
\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}
(cf.
[
18
]
, p. 377).

Let
f_{k,l}=E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\big[f|\Omega_{k,l}\big]
, be the conditional expectation of
f
given the variables on
\Lambda_{k,l}
.
For
\mu=\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}
let
\mu_{k,l}
be
\mu
restricted on
\Lambda_{k,l}
.
Clearly,
\mu_{k,l}=\nu_{k,l}^{c_{0}}
.
Since the Dirichlet form
E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\left[\sqrt{f}(-L_{N}\sqrt{f})\right]
is convex, we have

(\gamma N)^{-1}\lambda_{N,l}\leq\sup_{f_{k,l}}\left\{E_{\mu_{k,l}}\left[V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}f_{k,l}\right]-\gamma^{-1}NE_{\mu_{k,l}}\left[\sqrt{f_{k,l}}(-L_{k,l}\sqrt{f_{k,l}})\right]\right\}.

Step 4.

We now decompose
f_{k,l}d\mu_{k,l}
with respect to sets
\Omega_{k,l,j}
of configurations with total particle number
j
on
\Lambda_{k,l}
:

(8.4)

E_{\mu_{k,l}}\left[V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}f_{k,l}\right]=\sum_{j\geq 0}c_{k,l,j}(f)\int V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}f_{k,l,j}d\mu_{k,l,j},

where

c_{k,l,j}(f)=\int_{\Omega_{k,l,j}}f_{k,l}d\mu_{k,l}
and

f_{k,l,j}=c_{k,l,j}(f)^{-1}\mu_{k,l}\left(\Omega_{k,l,j}\right)f_{k,l}
. Here,
\sum_{j\geq 0}c_{k,l,j}=1
and
f_{k,l,j}
is a density with respect to
\mu_{k,l,j}
.

Straightforwardly, on
\Omega_{k,l,j}
, we have

\begin{split}\dfrac{L_{k,l}\sqrt{f_{k,l}}}{\sqrt{f_{k,l}}}=\dfrac{L_{k,l}\sqrt{f_{k,l,j}}}{\sqrt{f_{k,l,j}}}.\end{split}

Using (
8.4
), we write

E_{\mu_{k,l}}\Big[\sqrt{f_{k,l}}(-L_{k,l}\sqrt{f_{k,l}})\Big]=\sum_{j\geq 0}c_{k,l,j}(f)E_{\mu_{k,l,j}}\Big[\sqrt{f_{k,l,j}}(-L_{k,l}\sqrt{f_{k,l,j}})\Big].

Then, we get

(\gamma N)^{-1}\lambda_{N,l}\leq\sup_{0\leq j\leq A(2l+1)}\sup_{f}\left\{E_{\mu_{k,l,j}}\left[V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}f\right]-\gamma^{-1}NE_{\mu_{k,l,j}}\left[\sqrt{f}(-L_{k,l}\sqrt{f})\right]\right\},

where the second supremum is on all densities
f
with respect to
\mu_{k,l,j}
.

Step 5.

Let

\widehat{V}_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}=V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}-E_{\mu_{k,l,j}}\left[V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}\right].

Let
C_{l,A,G}
be such that
\|\widehat{V}_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{l,A,G}
. Recall
C_{l,j}
the inverse spectral gap of
L_{k,l}
(cf. (
8.3
)).
We now use the Rayleigh expansion (cf.
[
18
]
, pp. 375–376, Appendix 3, Theorem 1.1)

(8.5)

\begin{split}&E_{\mu_{k,l,j}}\left[\widehat{V}_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}f\right]-\gamma^{-1}NE_{\mu_{k,l,j}}\big[\sqrt{f}(-L_{k,l}\sqrt{f})\big]\\
\leq&\dfrac{\gamma N^{-1}}{1-2C_{l,A,G}C_{l,j}\,\gamma N^{-1}}E_{\mu_{k,l,j}}\left[\widehat{V}_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}(-L_{k,l})^{-1}\widehat{V}_{k,l,A}\right].\end{split}

The spectral gap of
L_{k,l}
also implies that
\|L_{k,l}^{-1}\|_{2}
, the
L^{2}(\mu_{k,l,j})
norm of the operator
L_{k,l}^{-1}
on
mean zero functions, is less than or equal to
C_{l,j}
.
Now, by Cauchy-Schwarz and the estimate of
\|L_{k,l}^{-1}\|_{2}
, we have

\begin{split}E_{\mu_{k,l,j}}\left[\widehat{V}_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}(-L_{k,l})^{-1}\widehat{V}_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}\right]\leq C_{l,j}E_{\mu_{k,l,j}}\left[\widehat{V}_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}^{2}\right]\leq C_{l,j}C^{2}_{l,A,G}.\end{split}

Accordingly, retracing our steps, noting (
8.5
), we have that
\mathbb{E}_{N}\big[\big|\int_{0}^{T}V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}(\eta_{s})ds\big|\big]

is less than or equal to

\begin{split}\dfrac{C_{0}}{\gamma}+\sup_{0\leq j\leq A(2l+1)}\dfrac{T\gamma N^{-1}C_{l,j}C_{l,A,G}^{2}}{1-2C_{l,A,G}C_{l,j}\,\gamma N^{-1}}+T\sup_{0\leq j\leq A(2l+1)}E_{\mu_{k,l,j}}\left[V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}\right].\end{split}

Taking
N\to\infty
, first
\sup
term vanishes. Notice that the expression
\sup E_{\mu_{k,l,j}}\left[V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}\right]
is independent of
N
and vanishes as
l\to\infty
.
In fact, as
\mu_{k,l,j}=\nu_{k,l,j}
is translation-invariant

\big|E_{\mu_{k,l,j}}\left[V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}\right]\big|\leq\|G\|_{\infty}\Big|E_{\nu_{0,l,j}}[g(\eta(0))]-E_{\nu_{j/(2l+1)}}[g(\eta(0))]\Big|.

By equivalence of ensembles (cf. p.355,
[
18
]
),
the right hand side of the above disaplay vanishes as
l\to\infty
, uniformly for
\rho=j/(2l+1)\in[0,A]
.
The lemma now is proved by letting
\gamma\to\infty
.
∎

8.2.
Local
2
-block estimate

We now detail the local
2
-block estimate following the outline of the local
1
-block estimate.
Recall the notation
\Lambda_{k,l}
from the
1
-block estimate and let
\Lambda_{k,k^{\prime},l}=\Lambda_{k,l}\cup\Lambda_{k^{\prime},l}
for
|k-k^{\prime}|>l
.
Define the generator
L_{k,k^{\prime},l}
on
\Omega_{k,k^{\prime},l}=\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\Lambda_{k,k^{\prime},l}}
:

\begin{split}L_{k,k^{\prime},l}f(\eta)&=L_{k,l}f(\eta)+L_{k^{\prime},l}f(\eta)\\
&+g(\eta(k+l))\big[f\big(\eta^{k+l,k^{\prime}-l}\big)-f(\eta)\big]+g(\eta(k^{\prime}-l))\big[f\big(\eta^{k^{\prime}-l,k+l}\big)-f(\eta)\big].\end{split}

When
|k-k^{\prime}|
is large, the process governed by
L_{k,k^{\prime},l}
in effect treats the blocks
\Lambda_{k,l}
and
\Lambda_{k^{\prime},l}
as adjacent, with a connecting bond.

Let
\Omega_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}:=\{\eta\in\Omega_{k,k^{\prime},l}:\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{k,k^{\prime},l}}\eta(x)=j\}
.
As before, the localized measure
\nu^{\rho}_{k,k^{\prime},l}
defined by
\nu_{\rho}
limited to sites in
\Lambda_{k,k^{\prime},l}
,
as well as
\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}
, the canonical measure of
\nu^{\rho}_{k,k;,l}
on
\Omega_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}
, are both invariant and reversible with respect to
L_{k,k^{\prime},l}
.

The corresponding Dirichlet form, with measure
\kappa
given by
\mu_{k,k^{\prime},l}
or
\mu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}
, is given by

(8.6)

\begin{split}E_{\kappa}\left[f(-L_{k,k^{\prime},l}f)\right]=&\sum_{x,x+1\in\Lambda_{k,k^{\prime},l}}E_{\kappa}\left[g(\eta(x))\left[f\left(\eta^{x,x+1}\right)-f(\eta)\right]^{2}\right]\\
&+E_{\kappa}\Big[g(\eta(k+l))\left[f\left(\eta^{k+l,k^{\prime}-l}\right)-f(\eta)\right]^{2}\Big].\end{split}

For
l,j\geq 1
, let
b_{l,l,j}
be the spectral gap of
-L_{k,k^{\prime},l}
on
\Omega_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}
( cf. (
8.2
)). As
b_{l,l,j}
is strictly postive, we have the following
Poincaré inequality (cf. (
8.3
)):
for all
f\in L^{2}(\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j})

(8.7)

{\rm Var}_{\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}}(f)\leq C_{l,l,j}E_{\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}}\left[f(-L_{k,k^{\prime},l}f)\right]

where

C_{l,l,j}:=b_{l,l,j}^{-1}
for
j\geq 1
and
C_{l,l,0}=0
.

We now state and show a local
2
-blocks estimate. The scheme is similar to that of the local
1
-block estimate.

Lemma 8.2

(Local
2
-block estimate)
.

We have

(8.8)

\limsup_{l\to\infty}\limsup_{\theta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{k,k^{\prime\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\left|\Phi(\eta_{s}^{l}(k))-\Phi(\eta_{s}^{\theta N}(k^{\prime\prime}))\right|ds\Big]=0

where the
\sup
is taken over all
k
and
k^{\prime\prime}
such that
\Lambda_{k,l}\subset\Lambda_{k^{\prime\prime},\theta N}
and
\Lambda_{k^{\prime\prime},\theta N}\cap{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

We separate the argument into steps.

Step 1.

Since
\Phi(\cdot)
is Lipschitz,
to prove the lemma, it suffices to show (
8.8
) with
\Phi(\eta_{s}^{l}(k))-\Phi(\eta_{s}^{\theta N}(k^{\prime\prime}))
replaced by
\eta_{s}^{l}(k)-\eta_{s}^{\theta N}(k^{\prime\prime})
.
We may further replace
\eta_{s}^{\theta N}(k^{\prime\prime})
by

(2\theta N+1)^{-1}\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{k^{\prime\prime},\theta N-l}}\eta^{l}(k^{\prime})
. Indeed, the replacement error is

\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\Big|\eta_{s}^{\theta N}(k^{\prime\prime})-\dfrac{1}{2\theta N+1}\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{k^{\prime\prime},\theta N-l}}\eta_{s}^{l}(k^{\prime})\Big|\Big]\leq\dfrac{2l+1}{2\theta N+1}\mathbb{E}_{N}\big[\eta_{s}^{l}(k^{\prime\prime}-\theta N+l)+\eta_{s}^{l}(k^{\prime\prime}+\theta N-l)\big].

By attractiveness (as explained in Step 1 of the proof of the local 1-block Lemma
8.1
), for either case
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}
or
g
bounded, the expectation term
\mathbb{E}_{N}
is bounded uniformly in
t
,
l
, and
N
, the right-hand side of the above display vanishes as
N\uparrow\infty
first.

Therefore, the lemma will follow if we show

(8.9)

\limsup_{l\to\infty}\limsup_{\theta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{k,k^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\eta_{s}^{l}(k)-\eta_{s}^{l}(k^{\prime})\Big|ds\Big]=0

where the
\sup
is taken over all
k,k^{\prime}
such that
\Lambda_{k,k^{\prime},l}\cap{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}=\emptyset
and
2l+1\leq k^{\prime}-k\leq\theta N
.

Step 2.

By a similar coupling argument as in the Step 1 of the proof of local
1
-block Lemma
8.1
,
we may apply a cutoff of large densities.
Therefore, to prove the lemma, it suffices to show

\limsup_{l\to\infty}\limsup_{\theta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{k,k^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\eta_{s}^{l}(k)-\eta_{s}^{l}(k^{\prime})\Big|\mathbbm{1}_{\{\eta_{s}^{l}(k,k^{\prime})\leq A\}}ds\Big]=0

where
\eta_{s}^{l}(k,k^{\prime})=\eta_{s}^{l}(k)+\eta_{s}^{l}(k^{\prime})

and the
\sup
is over
k,k^{\prime}
as in (
8.9
).

Let
U_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}(\eta):=\left|\eta^{l}(k)-\eta^{l}(k^{\prime})\right|\mathbbm{1}_{\{\eta^{l}(k,k^{\prime})\leq A\}}
.
Following the proof of Lemma
8.1
, for fixed
l,\theta,N,k,k^{\prime}
, in order to estimate

\mathbb{E}_{N}\big[\int_{0}^{T}U_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}(\eta_{s})ds\big]
,
it suffices to bound

(8.10)

(\gamma N)^{-1}\lambda_{N,l}=\sup_{f}\left\{E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\left[U_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}f\right]-\gamma^{-1}NE_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\left[\sqrt{f}(-L_{N}\sqrt{f})\right]\right\}.

where the supremum is over all
f
which are densities with respect to
\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}
.

Step 3.

Recall the generator
L_{k,k^{\prime},l}
and its Dirichlet form defined in the beginning of this subsection.
We now argue the following Dirichlet form inequality

(8.11)

E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\big[\sqrt{f}(-L_{k,k^{\prime},l}\sqrt{f})\big]\leq\theta NE_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\big[\sqrt{f}(-L_{N}\sqrt{f})\big].

The Dirichlet form with respect to the full generator
L_{N}
under
\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}
is given by

(8.12)

E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\left[f(-L_{N}f)\right]=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\Big[g_{k,N}(\eta(k))\big(f(\eta^{k,k+1})-f(\eta)\big)^{2}\Big].

First, writing out the Dirichlet form in (
8.6
), in terms of the product measure
\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}
, we have

\begin{split}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\left[f(-L_{k,k^{\prime},l}f)\right]=&\sum_{x,x+1\in\Lambda_{k,k^{\prime},l}}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\Big[g(\eta(x))\big(f(\eta^{x,x+1})-f(\eta)\big)^{2}\Big]\\
&+E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\Big[g(\eta(k+l))\big(f(\eta^{k+l,k^{\prime}-l})-f(\eta)\big)^{2}\Big].\end{split}

Next, by adding and subtracting at most
\theta N
terms, we have

\begin{split}\left[f\left(\eta^{k+l,k^{\prime}-l}\right)-f(\eta)\right]^{2}\leq(k^{\prime}-k-2l)\sum_{q=0}^{k^{\prime}-k-2l-1}\left[f\left(\eta^{k+l,k+l+q+1}\right)-f\left(\eta^{k+l,k+l+q}\right)\right]^{2}.\end{split}

By the change of variables
\xi=\eta^{k+l,k+l+q}
, which takes away a particle at
k+l
and adds one at
k+l+q
, we have

\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}(\eta)=\dfrac{g(\eta(k+l+q)+1)}{g(\eta(k+l))}\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}(\xi)
.
Then

\displaystyle E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\left[g(\eta(k+l))\left[f\left(\eta^{k+l,k+l+q+1}\right)-f\left(\eta^{k+l,k+l+q}\right)\right]^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi}\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}(\eta)g(\eta(k+l))\left[f\left(\xi^{k+l+q,k+l+q+1}\right)-f\left(\xi\right)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\Big[g(\eta(k+l+q))\Big[f\left(\eta^{k+l+q,k+l+q+1}\right)-f\left(\eta\right)\Big]^{2}\Big].

From these observations, (
8.11
) follows.

Step 4.

Let
\mu_{k,k^{\prime},l}
be the restriction of
\mu=\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}
to
\Lambda_{k,k^{\prime},l}
. Clearly,
\mu_{k,k^{\prime},l}=\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l}
.
Inputting (
8.11
) into (
8.10
), and considering the conditional expectation of
f
with respect to
\Omega_{k,k^{\prime},l}
as in the
1
-block estimate proof, we have

(\gamma N)^{-1}\lambda_{N,l}\leq\sup_{f_{k.k^{\prime},l}}\Big\{E_{\mu_{k,k^{\prime},l}}\left[U_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}f_{k,k^{\prime},l}\right]-\dfrac{1}{\theta\gamma}E_{\mu_{k,k^{\prime},l}}\Big[\sqrt{f_{k,k^{\prime},l}}(-L_{k,k^{\prime},l}\sqrt{f_{k,k^{\prime},l}})\Big]\Big\},

where the supremum is over densities
f_{k,k^{\prime},l}
with respect to
\mu_{k,k^{\prime},l}
.

Again, as in the proof of the
1
-block estimate, decomposing
f_{k,k^{\prime},l}d\mu_{k,k^{\prime}.l}
along configurations with common total number
j
, we need only to bound

\sup_{0\leq j\leq A(2l+1)}\sup_{f}\left\{E_{\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}}\left[U_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}f\right]-\dfrac{1}{\theta\gamma}E_{\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}}\left[\sqrt{f}(-L_{k,k^{\prime},l}\sqrt{f})\right]\right\},

where the supremum is over densities
f
with respect to
\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}
.

Step 5.

Consider the centered object

\widehat{U}_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}=U_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}-E_{\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}}\left[U_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}\right].

Recall
C_{l,l,j}
, the inverse spectral gap of
L_{k,k^{\prime},l}
from (
8.7
) and note that
\|\widehat{U}_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}\|_{\infty}\leq A
.
Using the Rayleigh expansion (cf. p.375,
[
18
]
), we have

\begin{split}&E_{\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}}\big[\widehat{U}_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}f\big]-(\theta\gamma)^{-1}E_{\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}}\big[\sqrt{f}(-L_{k,k^{\prime},l}\sqrt{f})\big]\\
\leq&\dfrac{\theta\gamma}{1-2AC_{l,l,j}\,\theta\gamma}E_{\nu_{k,k;,l,j}}\big[\widehat{U}_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}(-L_{k,k^{\prime},l})^{-1}\widehat{U}_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}\big]\\
\leq&\dfrac{\theta\gamma C_{l,l,j}}{1-2AC_{l,l,j}\,\theta\gamma}E_{\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}}\big[\widehat{U}_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}^{2}\big]\to 0\text{ as }\theta\to 0.\end{split}

Step 6.
Recall the definition of
U_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}
in Step 2. To finish, we still need to estimate the centering term

E_{\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}}\left[U_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}\right]
.
By adding and subtracting
j/(4l+2)
, we
need only bound

E_{\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}}\left[\big|\eta^{l}(k)-j/(4l+2)\big|\right]
. By exchangeability and
an equivalence of ensemble estimate (cf. p. 355
[
18
]
), the canonical variance

\displaystyle E_{\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}}\left[\big|\eta^{l}(k)-j/(4l+2)\big|^{2}\right]=O(l^{-1})E_{\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}}\left[(\eta(k)-j/(4l+2))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +O(1)E_{\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}}\left[(\eta(k)-j/(4l+2))(\eta(k+1)-j/(4l+2))\right]

and is further bounded by

C(A){\rm Var}_{\nu^{j/(4l+2)}_{k,k^{\prime},l}}\left(\eta^{l}(k)\right)
for some constant
C(A)
depending only on
A
.
This variance is of order
O(l^{-1})
, since the single
site variance

{\rm Var}_{\nu^{j/(4l+2)}_{k,k^{\prime},l}}\left(\eta(k)\right)
is uniformly
bounded for
j/(4l+2)\leq A
.
Hence,

\sup_{0\leq j\leq A(4l+2)}E_{\nu_{k,k^{\prime},l,j}}\left[V_{k,k^{\prime},l,A}\right]
is of order

O(l^{-1/2})
, vanishing as
l\uparrow\infty
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 8.3
.

We comment that Lemmas
8.1
and
8.2
with the choice
k=k^{\prime}=k^{\prime\prime}

g(\eta_{s}(k))

.

8.3.
Bulk Replacement Lemma

Let
G(t,x)
be a bounded function on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}
with compact support on
[0,T]\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}})
.
As in Remark
8.3
,
Lemma
8.1
implies that

\limsup_{l\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s,k/N)\left(g(\eta_{s}(k))-\Phi(\eta_{s}^{l}(k))\right)ds\Big|\Big]=0

and by Lemma
8.2
,

\limsup_{l\to\infty}\limsup_{\theta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\int_{0}^{T}G(s,k/N)\Big|\Phi\left(\eta_{s}^{l}(k)\right)-\Phi\left(\eta_{s}^{\theta N}(k)\right)\Big|ds\Big]=0.

By Markov’s inequality and triangle inequality, we obtain

Lemma 8.4

(Bulk Replacement Lemma)
.

For each bounded function
G(t,x)
on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}
with compact support on
[0,T]\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}})
, and
\delta>0
, we have

\begin{split}\limsup_{\theta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{N}\Big[\Big|\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\int_{0}^{T}G\big(s,\frac{k}{N}\big)\Big(g\left(\eta_{s}(k)\right)-\Phi\left(\eta_{s}^{\theta N}(k)\right)\Big)ds\Big|>\delta\Big]=0.\end{split}

.

We comment, in the
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}

[
18
]
holds), the “attractiveness” assumption used in the local

2
-block estimates to introduce cutoffs of large densities in a local region, may be dropped in the statement of the ‘global average’ Lemma

9.
Replacement at the boundary

In this section, we show a local replacement near the defect sites, used in Subsection
7.2
, in two steps. In Lemma
9.1
, we show that the jump rate
g_{k,N}(\eta_{s}(k))
at any site
k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
is close to
g_{k+1,N}(\eta_{s}(k+1))
, that of its neighbor site
k+1
.

In later use, when
k
is a defect site in
{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
, this neighbor site will be a non-defect or regular site for
N
large.
Then, we may apply local
1
and
2
-blocks estimates from last section to obtain our local replacement Lemma
9.2
near the defect sites.

In both lemmas, the replacements will hold in the
g(n)\sim n^{\alpha}
and
g
bounded settings.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
G(\cdot):[0,T]\mapsto\mathbb{R}
be bounded.
Then, we have

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\big(g_{k,N}(\eta_{s}(k))-g_{k+1,N}(\eta_{s}(k+1))\big)ds\Big|\Big]=0.

Proof.

Let

U_{s,k}(\eta)=\,2N^{-1}(G(s))^{2}\big(g_{k,N}(\eta(k))+g_{k+1,N}(\eta(k+1))\big).

By Lemma
5.2
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\mathbb{E}_{N}\big[\int_{0}^{T}U_{s,k}(\eta_{s})ds\big]=0
.
Let

V_{s,k}(\eta)=\,G(s)\big(g_{k,N}(\eta(k))-g_{k+1,N}(\eta(k+1))\big).

Then, to prove the lemma, it suffices to show that

(9.1)

\limsup_{\kappa\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{T}V_{s,k}(\eta_{s})ds\Big|-\kappa\int_{0}^{T}U_{s,k}(\eta_{s})ds\Big]=0.

Recall the initial entropy bound
H(\mu^{N}|\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N})\leq CN
. Then, for
c=c_{0}
and
\kappa>0
, by the entropy inequality, the expectaton in the previous display is bounded from above by

\dfrac{C}{\kappa}+\dfrac{1}{\kappa N}\ln\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\kappa N\Big|\int_{0}^{T}V_{s,k}(\eta_{s})ds\Big|-\kappa^{2}N\int_{0}^{T}U_{s,k}(\eta_{s})ds\Big\}\Big].

The absolute value in the right hand side of last inequality can be dropped by using
e^{|x|}\leq e^{x}+e^{-x}
.
By Feynman-Kac formula (cf. p.336,
[
18
]
), we have

\begin{split}\dfrac{1}{\kappa N}\ln\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\kappa N\int_{0}^{T}(V_{s,k}-\kappa U_{s,k})ds\Big\}\Big]\leq\dfrac{1}{\kappa N}\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{N,k}(s)ds\end{split}

where
\lambda_{N,k}(s)
is the largest eigenvalue of

N^{2}L_{N}+\kappa N(V_{s,k}(\eta)-\kappa U_{s,k}(\eta))
.
Fix
s\in[0,T]
and note the variational formula for
\lambda_{N,k}
:

(\kappa N)^{-1}\lambda_{N,k}=\sup_{f}\left\{E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\left[(V_{s,k}-\kappa U_{s,k})f\right]-\kappa^{-1}NE_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\big[\sqrt{f}(-L_{N}\sqrt{f})\big]\right\}

where the supremum is over all
f
which are densities with respect to
\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}
(cf.
[
18
]
, p. 377).
Thus, to prove (
9.1
), it remains to show, for any density
f
,

(9.2)

E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}[V_{s,k}f]\leq E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}[\kappa U_{s,k}f]+\kappa^{-1}NE_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\big[\sqrt{f}(-L_{N}\sqrt{f})\big].

By the product structure of
\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}
, we have

g_{k,N}(\eta(k))\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}(\eta)=g_{k+1,N}(\eta(k+1)+1)\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}(\eta^{k,k+1}).

Thus, we compute that

\begin{split}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\left[V_{s,k}f\right]=&E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[G(s)\Big(g_{k,N}(\eta(k))-g_{k+1,N}(\eta(k+1))\Big)f(\eta)\Big]\\
=&E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[G(s)g_{k,N}(\eta(k))\Big(f(\eta)-f(\eta^{k,k+1})\Big)\Big]\\
=&E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[G(s)g_{k,N}(\eta(k))\Big(\sqrt{f(\eta)}-\sqrt{f(\eta^{k,k+1})}\Big)\Big(\sqrt{f(\eta)}+\sqrt{f(\eta^{k,k+1})}\Big)\Big].\end{split}

By Cauchy-Schwarz, for any
A>0
, the above display is estimated from above by

\begin{split}&AE_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[g_{k,N}(\eta(k))\Big(\sqrt{f(\eta)}-\sqrt{f(\eta^{k,k+1})}\Big)^{2}\Big]\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+A^{-1}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[G(s)^{2}g_{k,N}(\eta(k))\Big(\sqrt{f(\eta)}+\sqrt{f(\eta^{k,k+1})}\Big)^{2}.\end{split}

Notice that the first expectation in the above display is bounded by
E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\big[\sqrt{f}(-L_{N}\sqrt{f})\big]
(cf. (
8.12
)).
Take
A=\kappa^{-1}N
.
The second summand is estimated from above by

\begin{split}&2\kappa N^{-1}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[(G(s))^{2}g_{k,N}(\eta(k))\big(f(\eta)+f(\eta^{k,k+1})\big)\Big]\\
=\,&2\kappa N^{-1}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[(G(s))^{2}\big(g_{k,N}(\eta(k))+g_{k+1,N}(\eta(k+1))\big)f(\eta)\Big]\\
=\,&E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\big[\kappa U_{s,k}f\big].\end{split}

Retracing the terms, we obtain (
9.2
), finishing the proof.
∎

We now finish this section with a local replacement lemma at defect sites:

Lemma 9.2

(Local replacement at defect sites)
.

Let
G(\cdot):[0,T]\mapsto\mathbb{R}
be bounded.
Then, for each defect site
k_{j,N}\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
,
we have

\lim_{\theta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{N}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}G(s)\left(g_{k_{j,N},N}(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))-\Phi(\eta_{s}^{\theta N,+}(k_{j,N}))\right)ds\right|\right]=0

where
\eta^{l,+}(k):=(2l+1)^{-1}\sum_{k+1\leq x\leq k+2l+1}\eta(x)
.

Proof.

Lemma
9.1

shows we may replace

g_{k_{j,N},N}(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}))

by
g_{k_{j,N}+1,N}(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}+1))
.
To finish, notice
k_{j,N}+1\in\mathbb{T}_{N}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{N}
for
N
large. Then, we may further replace
g_{k_{j,N}+1,N}(\eta_{s}(k_{j,N}+1))
by
\Phi(\eta_{s}^{\theta N,+}(k_{j,N}))
using Lemma
8.1
and Lemma
8.2
; see Remark
8.3
.
The proof is now complete.
∎

10.
Energy estimate

By Lemma
7.1
, we know that
Q
is supported on paths

\pi_{t}(dx)
which can be decomposed into an absolute continuous part
\rho(t,x)dx
and atoms
\sum_{j\in J_{c}}{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}(t)\delta_{x_{j}}(dx)
.
In this section, we prove an energy estimate for
\rho(t,x)
.

Proposition 10.1
.

Q
is supported on paths
\pi_{t}(dx)
such that
\Phi(\rho(t,x))
is weakly differentiable with respect to
x
on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}
and
\partial_{x}\Phi(\rho(t,x))
, the weakly derivative, satisfies

(10.1)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\dfrac{(\partial_{x}\Phi(\rho(t,x)))^{2}}{\Phi(\rho(t,x)}dx\,dt<\infty.

Remark 10.2
.

As
\rho(t,x)\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
(see Lemma
7.1
) and
\Phi(\cdot)
is Lipschitz, it follows from (
10.1
) that
\partial_{x}\Phi(\rho(t,x))\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
.

Proof of Proposition
10.1
.

The proof presented here is based on the one of Theorem 7.1, p. 102,
[
18
]
. However, because of the presence of the defect sites and the difference in the underlying topology, many details are different in subtle ways.

Let
\{H_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a dense sequence in
C_{c}^{0,1}([0,T]\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}))
under the norm
\|H\|_{\infty}+\|\partial_{x}H\|_{\infty}
.
We split the proof into steps.
In the first of two steps,
we show there is a constant
K_{0}
such that, for all
\epsilon
small,

(10.2)

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{N}\Big[\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}W_{N}(\epsilon,H_{j}(s,\cdot),\eta_{s})ds\Big\}\Big]\leq K_{0}

where

\begin{split}W_{N}(\epsilon,H(\cdot),\eta):=&\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\dfrac{H(x/N)}{\epsilon N}\left(g_{x,N}(\eta(x))-g_{x+\epsilon N,N}(\eta(x+\epsilon N))\right)\\
&-\dfrac{2}{N}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\dfrac{H^{2}(x/N)}{\epsilon N}\sum_{0\leq k\leq\epsilon N}g_{x+k,N}(\eta(x+k)).\end{split}

Step 1.

Let
c=c_{0}
. By the entropy inequality, the expectation in (
10.2
) is bounded from above by

\begin{split}\dfrac{H(\mu^{N}|\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N})}{2N}+\dfrac{1}{2N}\ln\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{R}_{c_{0}}^{N}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\Big\{2N\int_{0}^{T}W_{N}(\epsilon,H_{j}(s,\cdot),\eta_{s})ds\Big\}\Big\}\Big].\end{split}

For convenience, we set
c=c_{0}
.
Using
H(\mu^{N}|\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N})\leq CN
and
e^{\max a_{j}}\leq\sum e^{a_{j}}
, the limsup in
N
of the previous display is estimated from above by

\begin{split}\dfrac{C}{2}+\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2N}\ln\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[\exp\Big\{2N\int_{0}^{T}W_{N}(\epsilon,H_{j}(s,\cdot),\eta_{s})ds\Big\}\Big].\end{split}

By Feynman-Kac formula, for any fixed index
j
, the limsup term in the previous display is less than or equal to

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{f}\left\{E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\left[W_{N}(\epsilon,H_{j}(s,\cdot),\eta)f(\eta)\right]-\dfrac{N}{2}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[\sqrt{f}(-L_{N}\sqrt{f})\Big]\right\}ds

where the supremum is over all
f
which are densities with respect to

\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}
.

Step 2.

To show (
10.2
), it now remains to show, for all
H
in
C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}})
, that

(10.3)

\begin{split}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\left[W_{N}(\epsilon,H(\cdot),\eta)f(\eta)\right]-NE_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[\sqrt{f}(-L_{N}\sqrt{f})\Big]\leq 0.\end{split}

We first compute that
E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\left[W_{N}(\epsilon,H(\cdot),\eta)f(\eta)\right]
equals

(10.4)

\begin{split}&E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\dfrac{H(x/N)}{\epsilon N}(g_{x,N}(\eta(x))-g_{x+\epsilon N,N}(\eta(x+\epsilon N)))f(\eta)\Big]\\
&-\dfrac{2}{N}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\dfrac{H^{2}(x/N)}{\epsilon N}\sum_{0\leq k\leq\epsilon N}g_{x+k,N}(\eta(x+k))f(\eta)\Big].\end{split}

Let
\delta_{x}
be the configuration with the only particle at
x
and
\eta+\delta_{x}
be the configuration obtaining from adding one particle at
x
to
\eta

By the definition of
\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}
, we have, for each
x
,

(10.5)

E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}[g_{x,N}(\eta(x))f(\eta)]=\varphi E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}[f(\eta+\delta_{x})]

where
\varphi=\Phi(c)
. Then, the first expectation in
(
10.4
) is written as

(10.6)

\begin{split}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\dfrac{\varphi H(x/N)}{\epsilon N}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[f(\eta+\delta_{x})-f(\eta+\delta_{x+\epsilon N})\Big].\end{split}

which is rewritten as

(10.7)

\begin{split}&E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\sum_{0\leq k\leq\epsilon N-1}\dfrac{\varphi H(x/N)}{\epsilon N}\big(\sqrt{f(\eta+\delta_{x+k})}+\sqrt{f(\eta+\delta_{x+k+1})}\big)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\big(\sqrt{f(\eta+\delta_{x+k})}-\sqrt{f(\eta+\delta_{x+k+1})}\big)\Big].\end{split}

Using
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
, for any
A>0
, (
10.7
) is bounded from above by

(10.8)

\begin{split}&E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\sum_{0\leq k\leq\epsilon N-1}\dfrac{\varphi H^{2}(x/N)}{2\epsilon NA}\big(\sqrt{f(\eta+\delta_{x+k})}+\sqrt{f(\eta+\delta_{x+k+1})}\big)^{2}\Big]\\
&\qquad+E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\sum_{0\leq k\leq\epsilon N-1}\dfrac{\varphi A}{2\epsilon N}\big(\sqrt{f(\eta+\delta_{x+k})}-\sqrt{f(\eta+\delta_{x+k+1})}\big)^{2}\Big]:=I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

The
I_{2}
term in (
10.8
) is rewritten as

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\dfrac{\varphi A}{2}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\big[\big(\sqrt{f(\eta+\delta_{x})}-\sqrt{f(\eta+\delta_{x+1})}\big)^{2}\big]
which, by the change of variable formula (
10.5
), is further recognized as the Dirichelet form

\dfrac{A}{2}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\big[\sqrt{f}(-L_{N}\sqrt{f})\big]
, cf. (
8.12
).
For the first expectation
I_{1}
in (
10.8
), using first
(\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b})^{2}\leq 2(a+b)
and then (
10.5
), it is bounded from above by

(10.9)

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}\dfrac{2H^{2}(x/N)}{\epsilon NA}\sum_{0\leq k\leq\epsilon N}E_{\mathscr{R}_{c}^{N}}\Big[g_{x+k,N}(x+k)f(\eta)\Big].

Notice that the summation of
k
is ranging from
0\leq k\leq\epsilon N
instead of
0\leq k\leq\epsilon N-1
.
Now, we set
A=N
.
Putting together (
10.4
) and (
10.9
),
we obtain (
10.3
) with
K_{0}=C/2
.

Step 3.

Recall that
H_{j}
’s have compact support in
[0,T]\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}})
.
Let
\iota_{\delta}=(2\delta)^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{[-\delta,\delta]}
.
Applying the Bulk Replacement Lemma (Lemma
8.4
) to (
10.2
) and taking
N\to\infty
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\limsup_{\delta\to 0}E_{Q}\Big[\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}H_{j}(s,x)\epsilon^{-1}\big(\Phi(\iota_{\delta}*\rho_{s}(x))-\Phi(\iota_{\delta}*\rho_{s}(x+\epsilon))\big)dxds\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}H_{j}^{2}(s,x)\epsilon^{-1}\int_{x}^{x+\epsilon}\Phi(\iota_{\delta}*\rho_{s}(u))dudxds\Big\}\Big]\leq K_{0},\end{split}

Here
\iota_{\delta}*\rho_{s}(\cdot)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\iota_{\delta}(x-\cdot)\rho(s,x)dx

and
\rho(s,x)\in L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
is the absolute continuous part of each limit path
\pi_{s}
(cf. Lemma
7.1
).

Sending
\delta\to 0
, applying a discrete integration by parts, and then taking
\epsilon\to 0
, we have

\begin{split}&E_{Q}\Big[\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}H_{j}(s,x)\Phi(\rho(s,x))dxds\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}H_{j}^{2}(s,x)\Phi(\rho(s,x))dxds\Big\}\Big]\leq K_{0},\end{split}

By monotone convergence, the
\max_{1\leq j\leq m}
above can be replaced by
\max_{1\leq j<\infty}
.
Furthermore, as
H_{j}
is dense in
C_{c}^{0,1}([0,T]\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}))
with respect to the norm
\|H\|_{\infty}+\|\partial_{x}H\|_{\infty}
, we conclude

(10.10)

E_{Q}\Big[\sup_{H}\Big\{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}H(s,x)\Phi(\rho(s,x))dxds-2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}H^{2}(s,x)\Phi(\rho(s,x))dxds\Big\}\Big]\leq K_{0},

where the
\sup
is over
H\in C_{c}^{0,1}([0,T]\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}))
.

Step 4.

As a result of (
10.10
), for
Q
-a.e. path
\pi_{t}(dx)
, there exists
B=B(\pi_{t})
such that, for all
H\in C_{c}^{0,1}([0,T]\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}))
,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}H(s,x)\Phi(\rho(s,x))dxds-2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}H^{2}(s,x)\Phi(\rho(s,x))dxds\leq B.\end{split}

Define on
C_{c}^{0,1}([0,T]\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}))
a linear functional
l(H):=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}H(s,x)\Phi(\rho(s,x))dxds
.
Also define
\|H\|_{2,\rho}:=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}H^{2}(s,x)\Phi(\rho(s,x))dxds\right)^{1/2}
.
Then we have, for all
a\in\mathbb{R}

a\,l(H)-2a^{2}\left(\|H\|_{2,\rho}\right)^{2}\leq B.

Maximizing the left hand side over
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain
l(\cdot)
is a bounded linear functional under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{2,\rho}
. By Riesz representation theorem, there exists
F
such that
\|F\|_{2,\rho}<\infty
and it holds that
l(H)=\langle H,F\rangle_{\rho}:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}H(s,x)F(s,x)\Phi(\rho(s,x))dxds
.
Define
\partial_{x}\Phi(\rho(t,x)):=-F(t,x)\Phi(\rho(t,x))
. Then, we have shown that
\Phi(\rho(t,x))
is weakly differentialble with respect to
x
on
[0,T]\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}})
and the weak derivative
\partial_{x}\Phi(\rho(t,x))
satisfies (
10.1
).

To finish, we argue that the weak differentiability of
x
may be extended from
\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}
to
\mathbb{T}
with the same weak derivative
\partial_{x}\Phi(\rho(t,x))
.
The weak differentialbility on
[0,T]\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}})
implies that, for almost all
t\in[0,T]
,
\Phi(\rho(t,\cdot))
is absolutely continuous on
\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}
.
It suffices to show that, for almost all
t\in[0,T]
,
\Phi(\rho(t,\cdot))
is continuous on the defects
{\mathfrak{D}}
.
Notice that, as
(\partial_{x}\Phi(\rho))^{2}/\Phi(\rho)
and
\Phi(\rho)
are both in
L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
, by Cauchy-Schwarz, we have
\partial_{x}\Phi(\rho)
is in
L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
as well.
Then, for almost all
t\in[0,T]
, we have that the limits

\lim_{x\to x_{j}\pm}\Phi(\rho(t,x))

exist and are finite for all
x_{j}\in{\mathfrak{D}}

(cf. Remark
4.6
).
Moreover, by Lemma
9.2
, the left and right limits match.
Therefore, we conlude the continuity of

\Phi(\rho(t,\cdot))
on
{\mathfrak{D}}
,
finishing the proof.
∎

11.
Uniqueness

In this section, we present the uniqueness of the weak solutions to equations (
4.3
) and (
4.5
).
The proof is based on an energy argument (cf.
[
17
]
).
Recall Definitions
4.4
and
4.5
.

Theorem 11.1
.

There exists at most one weak solution to
(
4.3
).

Proof.

Let
\pi^{(1)}_{t}
and
\pi^{(2)}_{t}
be two weak solutions of (
4.3
) such that

\pi^{(i)}_{t}=\rho_{i}(t,x)dx+\sum_{j\in J_{c}}{\mathfrak{m}}^{(i)}_{j}(t)\delta_{x_{j}}(dx)
for
i=1,2
.
As
{\mathfrak{m}}^{(i)}_{j}(t)=\big[\lambda_{j}\Phi(\rho_{i}(t,x_{j}))\big]^{1/\alpha}
,
to prove the theorem, it suffices to show
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}
for almost all
t
.

Step 1.

Define

\overline{\Phi}(t,x):=\Phi(\rho_{1}(t,x))-\Phi(\rho_{2}(t,x))
.
By Definition
4.4
,
we have that
\overline{\Phi}
is weakly differentiable with respect to
x\in\mathbb{T}
and

\overline{\partial}_{x}\Phi
(and therefore

\overline{\Phi}
) is in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
.
As
\Phi(\rho_{i}(t,x_{j}))=\Phi(c_{0})
for each
i=1,2
and
j\in J_{s}
, we have
\overline{\Phi}(t,x_{j})=0
for for all
j\in J_{s}
.
We first show that
\overline{\Phi}
can be approximated ‘well’ by a sequence
\{\overline{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
in the sense that (1)
\overline{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}
is smooth and compactly supported on
[0,T]\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s})
;
(2)
\overline{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\to\overline{\Phi}
and
\partial_{x}\overline{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\to\partial_{x}\overline{\Phi}
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
;
and (3)

\overline{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}(t,x_{j})\to\overline{\Phi}(t,x_{j})
in
L^{2}[0,T]
for all
j\in J_{c}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Indeed, to verify this approximation, for
\delta>0
, let
{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}^{\delta}=\cup_{j\in J_{s}}(x_{j}-\delta,x_{j}+\delta)
.
We define
F_{\delta}
be such that

F_{\delta}(t,x)=0
on
[0,T]\times{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}^{\delta}
and

F_{\delta}(t,x)=\overline{\Phi}(t,x)
on
[0,T]\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}^{2\delta})
.
For
(t,x)\in{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}^{2\delta}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}^{\delta}
, define

F_{\delta}(t,x)=\begin{cases}\overline{\Phi}(t,2x-x_{j}-2\delta)&{\rm on\ }[0,T]\times[x_{j}+\delta,x_{j}+2\delta),\\
\overline{\Phi}(t,2x-x_{j}+2\delta)&{\rm on\ }[0,T]\times(x_{j}-2\delta,x_{j}-\delta].\\
\end{cases}

We note that
F_{\delta}
has compact support in
[0,T]\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s})
.
It is easy to check that
F_{\delta}
and
\partial_{x}F_{\delta}
approximate
\overline{\Phi}
and
\partial_{x}\overline{\Phi}
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
respectively.
Since
F_{\delta}
and
\overline{\Phi}
match at
x=x_{j}
for all
j\in J_{c}
,
to find a desired sequence
\{\overline{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\}
,
it suffices to show that, for each small
\delta
, there exists
\{F_{\delta,\varepsilon}\}
that approximates
F_{\delta}
‘well’.

To this end, let
\tau_{\varepsilon}(x)
be a standard mollifier supported on
[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
.
With
F_{\delta}(t,x)
extended to be
0
for
t\notin[0,T]
, we define

F_{\delta,\varepsilon}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}F_{\delta}(t-s,x-u)\tau_{\varepsilon}(s)\tau_{\varepsilon}(u)\,duds.

Notice that
F_{\delta,\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}))
for
\varepsilon<\delta
.
When restricted on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}
,
it is standard that
\overline{F}_{\delta,\varepsilon}
and

\partial_{x}F_{\delta,\varepsilon}
approximates

F_{\delta}
and
\partial_{x}F_{\delta}
respectively in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
It remains to show
that
F_{\delta,\varepsilon}(t,x_{j})

approximates
F_{\delta}(t,x_{j})

in
L^{2}[0,T]
.
Write that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(F_{\delta,\varepsilon}(t,x_{j})-F_{\delta}(t,x_{j})\right)^{2}dt

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ =\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(F_{\delta}(t-s,x_{j}-u)-F_{\delta}(t,x_{j})\right)\tau_{\varepsilon}(s)\tau_{\varepsilon}(u)duds\Big]^{2}dt.

By adding and subtracting

F_{\delta}(t-s,x_{j})
, the above is bounded above by
I_{1}+I_{2}
where

\begin{split}I_{1}&:=2\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(F_{\delta}(t-s,x_{j}-u)-F_{\delta}(t-s,x_{j})\right)\tau_{\varepsilon}(s)\tau_{\varepsilon}(u)duds\Big]^{2}dt,\\
I_{2}&:=2\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(F_{\delta}(t-s,x_{j})-F_{\delta}(t,x_{j})\right)\tau_{\varepsilon}(s)ds\Big]^{2}dt\end{split}

As
\int_{\mathbb{R}}F_{\delta}(t-s,x_{j})\tau_{\varepsilon}(s)ds
approximates
F_{\delta}(t,x_{j})
in
L^{2}[0,T]
, the term
I_{2}
vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
For the term
I_{1}
, using

F_{\delta}(t-s,x_{j}-u)-F_{\delta}(t-s,x_{j})=\int_{x_{j}}^{x_{j}-u}\partial_{x}F_{\delta}(t-s,x)dx
, we have

I_{1}\leq 2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big(\int_{x_{j}}^{x_{j}-u}\partial_{x}F_{\delta}(t-s,x)dx\Big)^{2}\tau_{\varepsilon}(s)\tau_{\varepsilon}(u)dudsdt

The square term above is further bounded by
u^{2}\big|\int_{x_{j}}^{x_{j}-u}(\partial_{x}F_{\delta}(t,x))^{2}dx\big|
.
As
\tau_{\varepsilon}(u)=0
for
u\notin[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
, we have that
I_{1}\leq 2\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\partial_{x}F_{\delta}(t,x))^{2}dxdt
which vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Step 2.

We now proceed to the uniqueness of weak solutions.
Let
\overline{\rho}:=\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}
and

\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}}_{j}(t):={\mathfrak{m}}_{j}^{(1)}(t)-{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}^{(2)}(t)
for each
j\in J_{c}
.
As
\pi^{(1)}_{t}
and
\pi^{(2)}_{t}
both satisfy (
4.6
), we have,
for all
G(t,x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left([0,T)\times(\mathbb{T}\setminus{\mathfrak{D}}_{s})\right)
,

(11.1)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{t}G(t,x)\overline{\rho}(t,x)dxdt+\sum_{j\in J_{c}}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}G(t,x_{j})\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}}_{j}(t)dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}G(t,x)\partial_{x}\overline{\Phi}(t,x)dxdt.\end{split}

Let
\overline{\Phi}
be approximated ‘well’ by some
\{\overline{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}\}
as in Step
1
.
Taking
G(t,x)=-\int_{t}^{T}\overline{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}(s,x)ds
and then leting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain

(11.2)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\overline{\Phi}(t,x)\overline{\rho}(t,x)dxdt+\sum_{j\in J_{c}}\int_{0}^{T}\overline{\Phi}(t,x_{j})\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}}_{j}(t)dt\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}\partial_{x}\overline{\Phi}(s,x)ds\Big]\partial_{x}\overline{\Phi}(t,x)dtdx.\end{split}

The right hand side of the above is computed as
-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big[\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}\overline{\Phi}(t,x)dt\big]^{2}dx\leq 0
.
However, for the left hand side, we have
\overline{\Phi}(t,x)\overline{\rho}(t,x)\geq 0
and
\overline{\Phi}(t,x_{j})\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}}_{j}(t)\geq 0
for all
t
,
x
, and
j
.
Then, we deduce that
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\overline{\Phi}(t,x)\overline{\rho}(t,x)dxdt=0

which implies
\overline{\Phi}(t,x)\overline{\rho}(t,x)=0
a.e. and, therefore,
\overline{\rho}(t,x)=0

a.e. Since
\overline{\rho}
is continuous in
x
for almost all
t
(cf. Remark
4.6
),
the theorem is proved.
∎

Theorem 11.2
.

There exists at most one weak solution to
(
4.5
).

Proof.

For
i=1,2
, let
\pi^{(i)}_{t}=\rho_{i}(t,x)dx+\sum_{j\in J_{c}}{\mathfrak{m}}^{(i)}_{j}(t)\delta_{x_{j}}(dx)

be two weak solutions to (
4.5
).
Notice that
{\mathfrak{m}}^{(i)}_{j}(t)={\mathfrak{m}}^{(i)}_{j}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{\rho(t,x_{j})=c_{j}}

and
\rho(t,x_{j})\leq c_{j}
implies
\overline{\Phi}(t,x_{j})\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}}_{j}(t)\geq 0
where

\overline{\Phi}:=\Phi(\rho_{1})-\Phi(\rho_{2})
and
\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}}_{j}:={\mathfrak{m}}_{j}^{(1)}-{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}^{(2)}
.
Following the proof of Theorem
11.1
, we have
\overline{\rho}:=\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}=0
for almost all
t
.
To conclude, it remains to show
\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}}_{j}=0
for each
j\in J_{c}
for almost all
t
.
To this end, we fix any
j\in J_{c}
and take
F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
such that
F(x_{j})=1
and
\text{supp}\,F\cap J_{c}=x_{j}
. Letting
G(t,x)=\int_{t}^{T}h(s)F(x)ds
in (
11.1
)
for any
h(t)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,T)

and using
\overline{\rho}=\overline{\Phi}=0
,
we obtain

\int_{0}^{T}h(t)\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}}_{j}(t)dt=0
, and therefore,
\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}}_{j}(t)=0
for almost all
t
, finishing the proof.
∎

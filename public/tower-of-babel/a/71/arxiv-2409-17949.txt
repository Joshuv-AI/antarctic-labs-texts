The 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -connection and the 4-dimensional Einstein spaces
A. García-Parrado

Abstract
We introduce a new family of operators in 4-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian manifolds with a non-vanishing Weyl scalar (non-degenerate spaces) that keep the conformal covariance of conformally covariant tensor concomitants . A particular case that arises naturally is the 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -connection that is a Weyl connection that keeps conformal invariance . Using the 𝒞 − \mathcal{C}- connection we give a new characterization of non-degenerate spaces that are conformal to an Einstein space.



Keywords:
Conformal Geometry, Conformal covariance, Einstein spaces.

Introduction

Conformal geometry has become a crucial area of study in both physics and mathematics due to its role in
describing fundamental interactions. All fundamental interactions in physics, except for gravity,
are governed by
conformally covariant
dynamical equations.
This naturally leads to the important mathematical question of
identifying and classifying geometric objects that are
conformally covariant
. Despite the significant
attention this question has received, there is no unified theory of conformal covariance. Instead, research has
progressed through targeted approaches, with a variety of distinct perspectives and applications.

A comprehensive introduction to conformal geometry is provided in
[
1
]
,
where the role of
tractor calculus
is emphasized.
Another interesting outcome is
[
23
]
where
conformal tensors

are classified and their properties as metric concomitants are fully investigated.

Higher-order differential operators, such as the Paneitz operator
[
20
]
, have played a
significant role in conformal geometry. The Paneitz operator, a fourth-order conformally invariant operator,
generalizes the Laplacian and is crucial in geometric analysis. Similarly,

[
13
]
provides a geometric construction of conformally invariant powers of the Laplacian,
particularly using the Fefferman-Graham ambient metric. Their work highlights the existence of conformally
invariant operators in various dimensions.

Other relevant contributions are
[
4
]
, on the conformal differential
geometry of subspaces and the book
[
14
]
, exploring the connections between conformal
geometry, higher-order differential operators, and physical theories such as holography.

In general relativity, the introduction of the concept of the
conformal
boundary
by Penrose in the 1960s
[
21
]
marked the
birth of a new field with significant impact on theoretical physics and pure
mathematics. In physics, this concept
is vital for studying isolated systems and their radiative properties, while in
mathematics, it helps define the
causal boundary of a Lorentzian manifold.

Another major advancement in general relativity
came with H. Friedrich’s work on the
conformal equations

[
5
,
10
]
.
Friedrich’s equations have been extensively studied (see
[
9
,
16
]
) and have led to significant
applications
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
.

Still within the framework of general relativity, an important question,
also addressed in the Friedrich formalism is to determine the
class of space-times that are conformal to a vacuum solution of the
Einstein field equations. A number of approaches to answer this question
can be found in the literature

[
15
,
17
,
2
,
12
,
22
]
.

In this work we perform an analysis of conformally
covariant
metric concomitants
defined in four-dimensional
pseudo-Riemannian manifolds (see section
3
).
If the pseudo-Riemannian manifold is
non-degenerate
(the
Weyl scalar
C_{abcd}C^{abcd}
is no-where vanishing) then
we can define the notion of
conformal pseudo-differential
(see

),
(

)). Using

out of a given one as explained in

3.1

we can introduce the

\mathcal{C}-
connection (see subsection

).
This is a Weyl connection that keeps the conformal invariance
as stated by Theorem

This characterization is

2
Geometric preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a 4-dimensional smooth manifold (unless otherwise stated, all structures are assumed to be smooth) and suppose that we define on
\mathcal{M}
a
pseudo-Riemannian metric
\tilde{g}_{ab}
(referred to as the physical metric) and
a second pseudo-Riemannian metric,
g_{ab}
(called the unphysical metric) of the same signature
which is
conformally related
to the first in the following fashion

g_{ab}=\Theta^{2}\tilde{g}_{ab}.

(1)

The conformal factor
\Theta
is assumed to be a positive smooth function which does not vanish
on
\mathcal{M}
.
The previous relation can be used to define two pseudo-Riemannian spaces:

(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})
and
(\mathcal{M},\tilde{g}_{ab})
.
The conformal relation (
1
)
can be used to set up an equivalence relation between
conformally related pseudo-Riemannian metrics. The resulting equivalence classes are called

conformal structures
on
\mathcal{M}
.
In this way, if (
1
)
holds then
g_{ab}
and
\tilde{g}_{ab}
belong to the same conformal structure.

The bundle of
r
-contravariant and
s
-covariant tensors

T^{r}_{s}(\mathcal{M})
is introduced in the standard way
(the union of all these bundles will be denoted by
T(\mathcal{M})
).
Tensor fields (or just tensors) are sections of these bundles and
the corresponding set of sections is
{\mathcal{T}}^{r}_{s}(\mathcal{M})
,

{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{M})
.
We shall use small Latin letters to denote abstract indices
of tensors in
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{M})
.
Indices are always raised and lowered with respect to the unphysical metric
g_{ab}

with the exception of
\tilde{g}^{ab}
, where we follow
the traditional convention that it represents the inverse
of
\tilde{g}_{ab}

\tilde{g}^{ac}\tilde{g}_{cb}=\delta_{b}{}^{a}.

(2)

From (
1
), the explicit relation between
the physical and the unphysical contravariant metric tensors becomes

g^{ab}=\frac{\tilde{g}^{ab}}{\Theta^{2}}.

(3)

Also each metric tensor has its own Levi-Civita connection denoted respectively
by
\nabla_{a}
,
\tilde{\nabla}_{a}
which are used to define the connection coefficients and the curvature
tensors in the standard fashion. Our conventions for the (unphysical) Riemann, Ricci and Weyl tensors
are

\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\omega_{c}-\nabla_{b}\nabla_{a}\omega_{c}=R_{abc}{}^{d}\omega_{d}\;,

(4)

R_{ac}\equiv R_{abc}{}^{b}\;,

(5)

C_{abc}{}^{d}\equiv R_{abc}{}^{d}-2L^{d}{}_{[b}g_{a]c}-2\delta^{d}{}_{[b}L_{a]c}\;,

(6)

where the unphysical Schouten tensor is defined by

L_{ab}\equiv\tfrac{1}{2}(R_{ab}-\tfrac{1}{6}Rg_{ab}).

(7)

Tensors defined in terms of the physical metric
\tilde{g}_{ab}
will be denoted with a tilde over
the symbol employed for an unphysical spacetime tensor.
The difference between the Levi-Civita connection
coefficients of the physical and the unphysical metrics
is the
transition tensor

\Gamma[\nabla,\tilde{\nabla}]^{e}{}_{ac}
given by

\Gamma[\nabla,\tilde{\nabla}]^{e}{}_{ac}=2\delta_{(a}{}^{e}\Upsilon_{c)}-g_{ca}\Upsilon^{e},

(8)

where we defined

\Upsilon_{a}\equiv\frac{\nabla_{a}\Theta}{\Theta}.

(9)

Any (tensorial) object
Q
defined exclusively in terms of the unphysical metric

g_{ab}
and its Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
will be
called an
unphysical metric concomitant
.
Elementary examples
of metric concomitants are the Riemann curvature of the Levi-Civita connection,
its non-trivial metric contractions (Ricci tensor and Ricci scalar) and the
Weyl and Schouten tensors.

Of course, any unphysical metric concomitant will always admit a physical
counterpart (
physical metric concomitant
),
denoted with a tilde on the top,
\tilde{Q}
. We will just speak of a
metric concomitant if the context makes it clear what the involved metric is.
Sometimes we may stress the dependencies on the metric and the Levi-Civita
connection in the notation (such as in Definition
10
).

3
Conformal pseudo-differential
of conformally covariant metric concomitants

Definition 1

(Conformally covariant metric concomitant)
.

We will say that a metric concomitant
Q(g,\nabla)
is
conformally covariant of weight
s\in\mathbb{Z}
if

Q(g,\nabla)
and its physical
counterpart
\tilde{Q}(\tilde{g},\tilde{\nabla})
are related to each other
in the following way

\tilde{Q}(\tilde{g},\tilde{\nabla})=\Theta^{s}Q(g,\nabla),\quad s\in\mathbb{Z}.

(10)

Classical examples of conformally covariant metric concomitants are the metric tensor
itself and its inverse (see eqs. (
1
),
(
3
)), the Weyl tensor

\tilde{C}_{abc}{}^{d}=C_{abc}{}^{d}
and the Bach tensor

B_{bc}\equiv\nabla^{a}\nabla^{d}C_{abdc}-\frac{1}{2}R^{ad}C_{abcd},

(11)

which is conformally invariant
\tilde{B}_{ab}(\tilde{g},\tilde{\nabla})=B_{ab}(g,\nabla)
.
See
[
23
]
for an in-depth study of
conformally covariant metric concomitants in pseudo-Riemannian
manifolds of arbitrary dimension.

Define the following metric concomitant

C\cdot C\equiv C_{abcd}C^{abcd}
.
Unless otherwise stated we will assume that

(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})
fulfills the condition
(C\cdot C)\neq 0

at every point.
Let us define the following unphysical metric concomitant:

\Lambda_{d}=\frac{8C_{d}{}^{amp}\nabla_{[m}L_{p]a}}{(C\cdot C)}.

(12)

The following lemma is key for our computations.

Lemma 1
.

The unphysical
\Lambda_{d}
just defined and its physical
counterpart
\tilde{\Lambda}_{d}
fulfill the relation

\Upsilon_{a}=\Lambda_{a}-\widetilde{\Lambda}_{a}.

(13)

Proof.

The relation between the
unphysical and physical Schouten tensors can be always written in the form (see e.g.
[
16
]
)

L_{ab}=\tilde{L}_{ab}-\Upsilon_{a}\Upsilon_{b}+\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}\Upsilon_{c}\Upsilon^{c}-\nabla_{b}\Upsilon_{a}.

(14)

Computing the unphysical covariant derivative of the previous equation and using (
4
) with
\omega_{d}
replaced by

\Upsilon_{d}
we get the following integrability condition

2\tilde{L}_{b[p}g_{m]a}\Upsilon^{b}+2\tilde{L}_{a[m}\Upsilon_{p]}+\Upsilon^{b}C_{abmp}+2\nabla_{[p}\tilde{L}_{m]a}+2\nabla_{[m}L_{p]a}=0.

(15)

Expressing
\nabla
in terms of
\tilde{\nabla}
by means of (
9
)
we obtain the following equivalent form of (
15
)

\tilde{\nabla}_{[b}\tilde{L}_{e]a}=\tfrac{1}{2}\Upsilon^{c}C_{acbe}+\nabla_{[b}L_{e]a}.

(16)

On the other hand the relation between the
unphysical Weyl tensor
C_{abcd}
and its
physical counterpart, denoted by
\tilde{W}_{abcd}
, is

C_{abcd}=\Theta^{2}\tilde{W}_{abcd}.

(17)

Now, take eq. (
16
), multiply both sides by

\tilde{g}^{ap}\tilde{g}^{bq}\tilde{g}^{er}\tilde{W}_{dpqr}
, use (
17
)
and the following identity, valid only in dimension four,
(see e.g.
[
3
]
),

C_{abmp}C^{dbmp}=\tfrac{1}{4}\delta_{a}{}^{d}C_{cbmp}C^{cbmp}.

(18)

The sought result follows after elementary algebraic manipulations.
∎

3.1
Conformal differentiation of scalar
conformally covariant metric concomitants

Let us consider the particular case of a conformally covariant
metric concomitant defined by a scalar function
w\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.
In this case and following the notation of Definition
10

we have

\widetilde{w}=\Theta^{s}w.

(19)

Taking the
\tilde{\nabla}
covariant derivative of this
equation and replacing it by
\nabla
where appropriate
we get

\widetilde{\nabla}_{a}\widetilde{w}=s\Theta^{s-1}\nabla_{a}\Theta w+\Theta^{s}\nabla_{a}w.

(20)

This equation shows that the Levi-Civita connection is not

conformally covariant
. However, we can use it as the starting
point to construct a
conformally covariant operator
.
Write (
20
) in terms of
\Upsilon_{a}

and use Lemma
13

\widetilde{\nabla}_{a}\widetilde{w}=\Theta^{s}\nabla_{a}w+s\Theta^{s}\Upsilon_{a}w,

\Leftrightarrow\widetilde{\nabla}_{a}\widetilde{w}=s\Theta^{s}(\Lambda_{a}-\widetilde{\Lambda}_{a})w+\Theta^{s}\nabla_{a}w,

\Leftrightarrow\widetilde{\nabla}_{a}\widetilde{w}+s\Theta^{s}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{a}w=\Theta^{s}(\nabla_{a}w+s\Lambda_{a}w),

The previous equation can be written in terms of
the following operator
D^{s}_{a}:C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})

\quad D^{s}_{a}w\equiv\nabla_{a}w+s\Lambda_{a}w.

(21)

The previous considerations lead us to the proof
of the following result.

Theorem 1
.

The operator
D^{s}_{a}
is conformally covariant when acting on
conformally covariant scalars.

\widetilde{D}^{s}_{a}\widetilde{w}=\Theta^{s}D^{s}_{a}w.

(22)

∎

Another straightforward property of
D^{s}_{a}

is contained in the following proposition.

Proposition 1

(Generalized Leibnitz rule)
.

The operator

D_{a}^{s}:C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})

is linear and fulfills the following property (generalized Leibnitz rule)

D^{s}_{a}(w_{1}w_{2})=(D^{s_{1}}_{a}w_{1})w_{2}+w_{1}(D_{a}^{s_{2}}w_{2}),\quad s=s_{1}+s_{2},\quad\forall w_{1},w_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}).

(23)

Proof.

The operator
D^{s}_{a}
is obviously linear, so we only need
to show that it verifies (
23
). To that end we work out
its left hand side and obtain its right hand side.

\displaystyle D^{s}_{a}(w_{1}w_{2})=\nabla_{a}(w_{1}w_{2})+s\Lambda_{a}(w_{1}w_{2})=

\displaystyle w_{2}\nabla_{a}w_{1}+w_{1}\nabla_{a}w_{2}+s_{1}\Lambda_{a}w_{1}w_{2}+s_{2}\Lambda_{a}w_{1}w_{2}=

\displaystyle w_{1}(\nabla_{a}w_{2}+s_{2}\Lambda_{a}w_{2})+w_{2}(\nabla_{a}w_{1}+s_{1}\Lambda_{a}w_{1})=(D^{s_{1}}_{a}w_{1})w_{2}+w_{1}(D_{a}^{s_{2}}w_{2}).

∎

3.2
Conformal pseudo-differential of conformally covariant tensors

Consider now a metric concomitant that is a conformally covariant tensor of rank
p

and conformal weight
s
. Then, according to definition
10
one has

\tilde{K}_{a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{p}}=\Theta^{s}K_{a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{p}}.

(24)

Take the physical covariant derivative of eq. (
24
)

\tilde{\nabla}_{b}\tilde{K}_{a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{p}}=s\Theta^{s-1}\nabla_{b}\Theta K_{a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{p}}+\Theta^{s}\tilde{\nabla}_{b}K_{a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{p}}.

Next, use the transition tensor (
8
)
on the right hand side to write
\tilde{\nabla}

in terms of
\nabla

s\Theta^{s-1}\nabla_{b}\Theta K_{a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{p}}+\Theta^{s}\left(\nabla_{b}K_{a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{p}}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Gamma[\nabla,\tilde{\nabla}]^{c_{j}}{}_{ba_{j}}K_{a_{1}a_{2}\ldots c_{j}\ldots a_{p}}\right)

=\Theta^{s}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{p}M^{c_{j}}{}_{ba_{j}}({\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}},{\boldsymbol{g}})K_{a_{1}\ldots c_{j}\ldots a_{p}}+\nabla_{b}K_{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}\right],

(25)

where in the last step we defined (we use boldface to mean that the covariant
form of a tensor is understood)

M^{c}{}_{ba}({\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}},{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)\equiv\frac{s}{p}\Upsilon_{b}\delta_{a}{}^{c}+\Gamma[\nabla,\tilde{\nabla}]^{c}{}_{ba}.

(26)

Expanding out the transition tensor (see (
8
))
we get the equivalent form

M^{c}{}_{ba}({\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}},{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)=\frac{s}{p}\Upsilon_{b}\delta_{a}{}^{c}+\Upsilon_{a}\delta_{b}{}^{c}+\Upsilon_{b}\delta_{a}{}^{c}-g_{ab}g^{cr}\Upsilon_{r}

=\left(\frac{s+p}{p}\right)\Upsilon_{b}\delta_{a}{}^{c}+\Upsilon_{a}\delta_{b}{}^{c}-g_{ab}g^{cr}\Upsilon_{r}

Note that
M^{c}{}_{ba}({\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}},{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)
is
linear in its first argument and conformally invariant in the second one.
Hence using the relation
\Upsilon_{a}=\Lambda_{a}-\tilde{\Lambda}_{a}

proven by Lemma
13

we get:

M^{c}{}_{ba}({\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}},{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)=M^{c}{}_{ba}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)=M^{c}{}_{ba}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)-M^{c}{}_{ba}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s).

(27)

Putting this back in (
25
)
we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{b}\tilde{K}_{a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{p}}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}M^{c_{j}}{}_{ba_{j}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)\Theta^{s}K_{a_{1}\ldots c_{j}\ldots a_{p}}=

\displaystyle\Theta^{s}\left[\nabla_{b}K_{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}M^{c_{j}}{}_{ba_{j}}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)K_{a_{1}\ldots c_{j}\ldots a_{p}}\right]

(28)

The previous relation can be cast
in terms of a new conformally covariant operator

D_{b}^{s,(0,p)}:\mathcal{T}^{0}_{p}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathcal{T}^{0}_{p+1}(\mathcal{M})
:

\tilde{D}_{b}^{s,(0,p)}\tilde{K}_{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}=\Theta^{s}D_{b}^{s,(0,p)}K_{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}.

(29)

Here, the operator
{D}_{b}^{s,(0,p)}
is defined by (we
shorten the notation by suppressing the covariant indices of
K

which are those of (
28
))

\displaystyle D_{b}^{s,(0,p)}\cdot K\equiv\nabla_{b}\cdot K+\sum_{j=1}^{p}M^{c}{}_{ba}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)\cdot K=

(30)

\displaystyle\nabla_{b}\cdot K+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left(\left(\frac{s+p}{p}\right)\Lambda_{b}\delta_{a}{}^{c}+\Lambda_{a}\delta_{b}{}^{c}-g_{ab}g^{cd}\Lambda_{d}\right)\cdot K.

(31)

Equation (
29
) shows that the operator
{D}_{b}^{s,p}

is conformally covariant for conformally covariant tensors of rank
p

and conformal weight
s
.

In a similar fashion we can carry out the computations
for conformally contravariant tensorial
metric concomitants. The result is

\tilde{\nabla}_{b}\tilde{K}^{a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{p}}+\sum_{j}\bar{M}^{a_{j}}{}_{bc_{j}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},\tilde{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)\Theta^{s}K^{a_{1}\ldots c_{j}\ldots a_{p}}=

\Theta^{s}\left[\nabla_{b}K^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bar{M}^{a_{j}}{}_{bc_{j}}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},{\boldsymbol{g}})K^{a_{1}\ldots c_{j}\ldots a_{p}}\right],

where

\bar{M}^{c}{}_{ba}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)\equiv\left(\frac{s-p}{p}\right)\Lambda_{b}\delta_{a}{}^{c}-\Lambda_{a}\delta_{b}{}^{c}+g_{ab}g^{cr}\Lambda_{r}

(32)

Again, the previous relation can be cast
in terms of a new conformally covariant operator

D_{b}^{s,(p,0)}:\mathcal{T}_{0}^{p}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{0}^{p+1}(\mathcal{M})
:

\tilde{D}_{b}^{s,(p,0)}\tilde{K}^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}=\Theta^{s}D_{b}^{s,(p,0)}K^{a_{1}\ldots a_{p}}.

(33)

Here, the operator
\bar{D}_{b}^{s,(p,0)}
is defined by
(again we suppress irrelevant contravariant indices to shorten the notation)

\displaystyle D_{b}^{s,(p,0)}\cdot K\equiv\nabla_{b}\cdot K+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bar{M}^{c}{}_{ba}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)\cdot K=

\displaystyle\nabla_{b}\cdot K+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left(\left(\frac{s-p}{p}\right)\Lambda_{b}\delta_{a}{}^{c}-\Lambda_{a}\delta_{b}{}^{c}+g_{ab}g^{cd}\Lambda_{d}\right)\cdot K.

(34)

Equation (
33
) shows that the operator
\bar{D}_{b}^{s,(p,0)}

is conformally covariant for conformally contravariant tensors of rank
p

and conformal weight
s
.

Finally, we can also extend these notions to mixed conformally covariant tensors.
For example, if
K^{p}{}_{q}
is a metric concomitant conformally covariant

\tilde{K}^{p}{}_{q}=\Theta^{s}K^{p}{}_{q},

(35)

then

\tilde{D}_{b}^{s,(1,1)}\tilde{K}^{p}{}_{q}=\Theta^{s}D_{b}^{s,1,1}K^{p}{}_{q},

(36)

where

D_{b}^{s,(1,1)}K^{p}{}_{q}=\nabla_{b}K^{p}{}_{q}+\bar{M}^{p}{}_{br}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},{\boldsymbol{g}})K^{r}{}_{q}+M^{r}{}_{bq}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},{\boldsymbol{g}})K^{p}{}_{r}.

(37)

We summarize our previous findings in the following Theorem.

Theorem 2

(Conformal pseudo-differential)
.

Let the tensor
K^{a_{1}\dots a_{p}}{}_{b_{1}\dots b_{q}}
be an unphysical metric
concomitant and assume that it is conformally covariant
of weight
s\in\mathbb{Z}

\widetilde{K}^{a_{1}\dots a_{p}}{}_{b_{1}\dots b_{q}}=\Theta^{s}K^{a_{1}\dots a_{p}}{}_{b_{1}\dots b_{q}}.

(38)

Then its
conformal pseudo-differential

D_{a}^{s,(p,q)}:\mathcal{T}^{p}_{q}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathcal{T}^{p}_{q+1}(\mathcal{M})
,

(p,q)\neq(0,0)
defined by

\displaystyle D_{a}^{s,(p,q)}K^{a_{1}\dots a_{p}}{}_{b_{1}\dots b_{q}}\equiv\nabla_{a}K^{a_{1}\dots a_{p}}{}_{b_{1}\dots b_{q}}+

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\bar{M}^{a_{j}}{}_{ac}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)K^{a_{1}\dots a_{j-1}ca_{j+1}\dots a_{p}}{}_{b_{1}\dots b_{q}}+

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{q}M^{c}{}_{ab_{j}}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)K^{a_{1}\dots a_{p}}{}_{b_{1}\dots b_{j-1}cb_{j+1}\dots b_{q}}.

(39)

is also conformally covariant with the same weight

\widetilde{D}_{a}^{s,(p,q)}\tilde{K}^{a_{1}\dots a_{p}}{}_{b_{1}\dots b_{q}}=\Theta^{s}D_{a}^{s,(p,q)}K^{a_{1}\dots a_{p}}{}_{b_{1}\dots b_{q}},

(40)

where

\displaystyle M^{c}{}_{ba}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)\equiv\left(\frac{s+p}{p}\right)\Lambda_{b}\delta_{a}{}^{c}+\Lambda_{a}\delta_{b}{}^{c}-g_{ab}g^{cd}\Lambda_{d},

(41)

\displaystyle\bar{M}^{c}{}_{ba}({\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},{\boldsymbol{g}},p,s)\equiv\left(\frac{s-p}{p}\right)\Lambda_{b}\delta_{a}{}^{c}-\Lambda_{a}\delta_{b}{}^{c}+g_{ab}g^{cr}\Lambda_{r}.

(42)

∎

If
(p,q)=(0,0)
(scalar quantity) then we make the definition

D_{a}^{s,(0,0)}\equiv D^{s}_{a},

(43)

where
D^{s}_{a}
is the operator defined in (
21
).

3.3
The
\mathcal{C}
-connection

Note that when
s=0
(conformal invariance)
the operators
D^{0,(0,p)}_{a}
,
\bar{D}^{0,(p,0)}_{a}
are actually independent from

p
as it follows from (
41
)-(
42
).
Therefore we can make the following definitions

\mathcal{C}_{a}\equiv D_{a}^{0,(0,p)},\quad\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\equiv D_{a}^{0,(p,0)}.

(44)

These operators are true covariant derivatives as one can easily
check from (
39
). In fact, we also deduce from (
39
)
that
\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{a}=\mathcal{C}_{a}
, so we only need to describe one
of them (we pick
\mathcal{C}_{a}
). Using eq. (
31
) we have that

\Gamma[\mathcal{C},\nabla]^{c}{}_{ab}=g^{cd}g_{ab}\Lambda_{d}-\delta_{b}{}^{c}\Lambda_{a}-\delta_{a}{}^{c}\Lambda_{b}.

(45)

We shall denote the Riemann and Ricci tensors defined by the
C
-connection
as
\mathcal{R}_{abc}{}^{d}
,
\mathcal{R}_{ab}\equiv\mathcal{R}_{adb}{}^{d}

respectively. Given that
\nabla
has no torsion and
\Gamma[\mathcal{C},\nabla]^{c}{}_{ab}

is symmetric, the connection
\mathcal{C}
has no torsion either.
Hence, the standard formula that relates the Riemann curvature tensors of

\nabla
and
\mathcal{C}
is

\mathcal{R}_{abc}{}^{d}=2\Gamma[\mathcal{C},\nabla]^{d}{}_{e[a}\Gamma[\mathcal{C},\nabla]^{e}{}_{b]c}+R_{abc}{}^{d}-2\mathcal{C}_{[a}\Gamma[\mathcal{C},\nabla]^{d}{}_{b]c}.

(46)

Inserting (
45
) in (
46
) we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{abc}{}^{d}=R_{abc}{}^{d}-2\Lambda^{d}\Lambda_{[b}g_{a]c}+2\delta_{[a}{}^{d}(\Lambda_{b]}\Lambda_{c}-g_{b]c}\Lambda_{e}\Lambda^{e}-\mathcal{C}_{b]}\Lambda_{c})+

Theorem 3

The

(49)

Proof.

Theorem 4

Let

,
(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})

covariant concomitant of
g_{ab}
vanishes

E_{ab}(g_{ab},\nabla)\equiv\mbox{\rm TF}_{g}\big[L_{ab}+\nabla_{a}\Lambda_{b}+\Lambda_{a}\Lambda_{b}\big],

(54)

where
{\rm TF}_{g}
stands for “trace-free part” with respect to
g_{ab}
.
Moreover
E_{ab}
is
conformally invariant
according to

\tilde{E}_{ab}(\tilde{g}_{ab},\tilde{\nabla})=E_{ab}(g_{ab},\nabla).

Proof.

See

11
]

Using the previous theorem as the starting point
we obtain the main result of the present work.

Theorem 5
.

A 4-dimensional
manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
is conformally related to an Einstein manifold

(\tilde{\mathcal{M}},\tilde{g}_{ab})
, if and only if its
\mathcal{C}
-Ricci
tensor fulfills the following algebraic conditions

\mathcal{R}_{[ab]}=0,\quad\mathcal{R}_{ab}-\frac{1}{4}g_{ab}\mathcal{R}=0.

(55)

Proof.

Using (
47
)
one can find the following relations

\displaystyle R_{ac}=2\Lambda_{a}\Lambda_{c}+\mathcal{R}_{ac}-2(\Lambda_{b}\Lambda^{b})g_{ac}-3\mathcal{C}_{a}\Lambda_{c}+\ \mathcal{C}_{c}\Lambda_{a}-g_{ac}(g^{db}\mathcal{C}_{d}\Lambda_{b}),

(56)

\displaystyle R=-6\Lambda_{a}\Lambda^{a}+\mathcal{R}_{a}{}^{a}-6g^{ab}\ \mathcal{C}_{b}\Lambda_{a}

(57)

From this we can obtain the relation between the Schouten tensor

L_{ab}
and
\mathcal{R}_{ab}
by means of (
7
)

L_{ab}=\Lambda_{a}\Lambda_{b}-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{c}\Lambda^{c}\ g_{ab}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{R}_{(ab)}-\frac{1}{12}\mathcal{R}_{c}{}^{c}g_{ab}-\mathcal{C}_{(a}\Lambda_{b)}.

(58)

Next we use the relation between the
\nabla
covariant derivative
and the
\mathcal{C}
covariant derivative

\nabla_{b}\Lambda_{a}=-\Gamma[\nabla,\mathcal{C}]^{c}{}_{ba}\ \Lambda_{c}+\mathcal{C}_{b}\Lambda_{a}.

(59)

Since the connection tensor
\Gamma[\nabla,\mathcal{C}]^{c}{}_{ba}

is known (see (
45
)) we can replace it
getting

\nabla_{b}\Lambda_{a}=-2\Lambda_{a}\Lambda_{b}+\Lambda_{c}\Lambda^{c}g_{ab}+\ \mathcal{C}_{b}\Lambda_{a}.

(60)

Putting together all the pieces we can compute
E_{ab}
as defined by
(
54
) but written now in terms of the

\mathcal{C}
-connection. The result is

E_{ab}(g_{ab},\nabla)=\mbox{\rm TF}_{g}\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{R}_{(ab)}+(\tfrac{1}{2}\Lambda_{c}\Lambda^{c}-\tfrac{1}{12}\ \mathcal{R}_{c}{}^{c})g_{ab}-\mathcal{C}_{[a}\Lambda_{b]}\right].

(61)

From (
56
) we deduce

\mathcal{C}_{[a}\Lambda_{b]}=\tfrac{1}{4}\mathcal{R}_{[ab]}.

(62)

Thus

E_{ab}(g_{ab},\nabla)=\mbox{\rm TF}_{g}\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{R}_{(ab)}\right]-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{R}_{[ab]}.

(63)

Hence
E_{ab}
vanishes if and only if

\mathcal{R}_{[ab]}=0,\quad\mathcal{R}_{ab}-\frac{1}{4}g_{ab}\mathcal{R}=0,

(64)

as desired.

5
Conclusions

In this work, we have studied the structure of a 4-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian manifold in the context where

C_{abcd}C^{abcd}\neq 0
. A key outcome of our research is the introduction of the
conformal pseudo-
differential
, a tool that enables the construction of new tensorial conformally covariant metric concomitants
from pre-existing ones.

Building on the conformal pseudo-differential, we defined the
\mathcal{C}-
connection, a specialization of the
Weyl connection. This connection possesses the notable property that its Riemann tensor remains conformally
invariant, offering a new approach to understanding the conformal structure of such manifolds. Furthermore, we
presented a novel characterization of Einstein spaces through the
\mathcal{C}-
connection, as detailed in Theorem

4
, providing an alternative perspective on their geometric structure.

A particularly intriguing open problem emerging from this work is whether established conformal operators, such as
the Yamabe or Paneitz operators, can be reformulated using the conformal pseudo-differential that we have
introduced. Another interesting question is the formulation of an initial value
problem for the conformal vacuum Einstein equations (
55
).
These problems will be addressed elsewhere.

The Analytic Stockwell Transform and its Zeros
Ali Moukadem, Barbara Pascal, Jean-Baptiste Courbot, Nicolas Juillet

Abstract
The Stockwell Transform is a time–frequency representation resulting from an hybridization between the Short-Time Fourier Transform and the Continuous Wavelet Transform. Instead of focusing on energy maxima, an unorthodox line of research has recently shed the light on the zeros of time–frequency transforms, leading to fruitful theoretical developments combining probability theory, complex analysis and signal processing. While the distributions of zeros of the Short-Time Fourier Transform and of the Continuous Wavelet Transform of white noise have been precisely characterized, that of the Stockwell Transform of white noise zeros remains unexplored. To fill this gap, the present work proposes a characterization of the distribution of zeros of the Stockwell Transform of white noise taking advantage of a novel generalized Analytic Stockwell Transform. First of all, an analytic version of the Stockwell Transform is designed. Then, analyticity is leveraged to establish a connection with the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function, whose zero set is invariant under the isometries of the Poincaré disk. Finally, the theoretical spatial statistics of the zeros of the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function and the empirical statistics of the zeros the Analytic Stockwell Transform of white noise are compared through intensive Monte Carlo simulations, showing the practical relevance of the established connection. A documented Python toolbox has been made publicly available by the authors.



Keywords
– Time–frequency analysis; Stockwell transform; Gaussian Analytic Functions; Point processes; Hyperbolic geometry

2
The Analytic Stockwell Transform

First, the formal definition of the Generalized Stockwell Transform is reminded.
Then, connecting the Stockwell Transform to the Wavelet Transform and leveraging analyticity results obtained for Cauchy wavelet, an Analytic Stockwell Transform is constructed.

2.1
Preliminary: definition of the Generalized Stockwell Transform

Definition 1
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
The
Generalized Stockwell Transform
with analysis window
\varphi
of

f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is defined as

S_{\varphi}f:\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}&\rightarrow&\mathbb{C}\\
(x,\xi)&\mapsto&\xi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\overline{\varphi(\xi(t-x))}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}t\xi}\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{array}\right.

(1)

Remark 1
.

The seminal Stockwell transform introduced in
[
39
]
corresponds to the standard Gaussian analysis window
\varphi(t)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-t^{2}/2}
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\xi,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, let the
translation
,
modulation
and
dilatation
automorphisms of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be respectively defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{T}_{x}h(t)=h(t-x);\quad\mathbf{M}_{\xi}h(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\xi t}h(t);\quad\mathbf{D}_{\gamma}h(t)=\gamma h(\gamma t),\end{split}

(2)

Then, the Generalized Stockwell Transform of Equation (
1
) can be interpreted as the decomposition of the signal onto family of functions generated by translations, modulations and dilatations of the analysis window, that is:

S_{\varphi}f(x,\xi)=\langle f,\mathbf{M}_{\xi}\mathbf{T}_{x}\mathbf{D}_{\xi}\varphi\rangle.

(3)

Note the modulation operator
\mathbf{M}_{\xi}
, which essentially differentiates the Stockwell transform from the Wavelet transform and provides an absolute reference for the phase information
[
39
]
.

2.2
Existence of an Analytic Stockwell Transform

The design of an Analytic Stockwell Transform is based on the
Cauchy
wavelet, also referred to as the
Daubechies-Paul
wavelet in the literature
[
12
]
.

Definition 2
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
\beta>0
.
The
Cauchy
wavelet of parameters
\beta
is defined as the unique function
\psi_{\beta}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
whose Fourier transform reads

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}_{\beta}(\nu)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\nu^{\beta}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\nu}&\text{if }\nu\geq 0\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Remark 2
.

The condition
\beta>0
ensures the admissibility of the wavelet, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\widehat{\psi}_{\beta}(\nu)\right\rvert^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\lvert\nu\rvert}<\infty

(6)

and that
\widehat{\psi}_{\beta}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Then, the bijectivity of the Fourier transform from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
yields the existence of a unique
\psi_{\beta}
satisfying (
2
).

Theorem 1
.

Let
\beta>0
and
\psi_{\beta}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be the Cauchy wavelet of parameter
\beta
. Define the
modulated
Cauchy wavelet
\varphi_{\beta}(t)=\psi_{\beta}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}t}
.
There exists a smooth nonvanishing function
\lambda:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and a conformal mapping
\vartheta:\mathbb{C}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
such that any signal
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
can be written as

\displaystyle\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\quad S_{\varphi_{\beta}}f(x,\xi)=\lambda(x,\xi)\times F\left(\vartheta(x+\mathrm{i}\xi^{-1})\right)

(7)

with
F:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
an
analytic
function on the unit disk.

In words, Theorem
1
states that, up to multiplication by a smooth nonvanishing factor and composition with a conformal transformation, the Generalized Stockwell Transform of analyzing window
\varphi_{\beta}
of any finite energy signal is an analytic function on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

First, remark that for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\varphi_{\beta}}f(x,\xi)&=\xi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\overline{\varphi_{\beta}(\xi(t-x))}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\xi t}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\xi\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\xi x}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\overline{\varphi_{\beta}(\xi(t-x))\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\xi(t-x)}}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\xi\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\xi x}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\overline{\psi_{\beta}(\xi(t-x))}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\sqrt{\xi}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\xi x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\xi^{-1}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\overline{\psi_{\beta}\left(\frac{t-x}{\xi^{-1}}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\sqrt{\xi}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\xi x}W_{\psi_{\beta}}f(x,\xi^{-1})\end{split}

(8)

where
W_{\psi_{\beta}}f
is the Cauchy
Wavelet
Transform of
f
.

Then, from
[
27
, Section 2.2]
and
[
21
, Theorem 2]
, the Cauchy Wavelet Transform of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is analytic on the upper-half complex plane, that is
G:z=x+\mathrm{i}y\mapsto y^{-\beta-1/2}W_{\psi_{\beta}}f(x,y)
is an analytic function on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
.
Moreover, let
\vartheta:\mathbb{C}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be the
Cayley
transform defined as

\displaystyle\vartheta(z)=\frac{z-\mathrm{i}}{z+\mathrm{i}}.

(9)

Then,
\vartheta
is a conformal and invertible mapping from the upper-half plane to the unit disk whose inverse is also conformal and writes

\displaystyle\vartheta^{-1}(w)=\frac{\mathrm{i}+\mathrm{i}w}{1-w}.

(10)

Define
F:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
as

\forall w\in\mathbb{D},\,F(w)=G(\vartheta^{-1}(w))
.
Since
F
is the composition of an analytic function
G
, and of a conformal, hence holomorphic, mapping
\vartheta^{-1}
,
F
is analytic.
Finally, let
\lambda(x,\xi)=\xi^{-\beta}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\xi x}
, then
\lambda:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a smooth nonvanishing function.

By construction of
\vartheta
,
\lambda
and
F
they satisfy all the conditions enumerated in Theorem
1
and
\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\,S_{\varphi_{\beta}}f(x,\xi)=\lambda(x,\xi)\times F\left(\vartheta(x+\mathrm{i}\xi^{-1})\right)
which concludes the proof.
∎

3
The Zeros of the Analytic Stockwell Transform of White Noise

The purpose of this section is to characterize the probability distribution of the zeros of the newly introduced Analytic Stockwell Transform of white noise.
To this purpose, the link between the Analytic Stockwell Transform and the Cauchy Wavelet Transform, established in the proof of Theorem
1
, and the one-to-one correspondence between the zeros of the Cauchy Wavelet Transform of white noise and the zeros of the so-called
hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function
, proven in
[
25
,
7
]
, are combined to yield Theorem
3
below.

For the sake of completeness, before proving the connection with the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function,
some necessary elements of hyperbolic and stochastic geometry are presented; the definition of the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function is reminded accompanied with its main interesting properties so that the present section is self-contained.
The interested reader can refer to
[
22
]
for a thorough presentation of the theory of Gaussian analytic functions and a detailed study of their zeros.

3.1
Gaussian analytic functions and point processes

The
hyperbolic plane
is the Riemaniann surface, i.e, two-dimensional manifold, that is homeomorphic to a plane and has constant negative curvature equal to
-1
.
Among its classical representations are the hyperboloid model, the Klein model, the Poincaré half-plane model, and the Poincaré disk model.
The present work uses the Poincaré disk model.

Definition 3
.

The Poincaré disk model is the triplet
(\mathbb{D},\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{D}},\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{D}})
constituted of the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\lvert z\rvert<1\}
, the
hyperbolic metric
defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathsf{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(z)=\frac{4\mathrm{d}z}{\left(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2}\right)^{2}}

(11)

where
\mathrm{d}z
refers to the standard Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}
, and the associated
hyperbolic distance
defined for
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
by

\displaystyle\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)=2\mathrm{tanh}^{-1}(\mathsf{p}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)),\quad\mathsf{p}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)=\frac{\lvert z-w\rvert}{\lvert 1-\overline{w}z\rvert}

(12)

with
\mathsf{p}_{\mathbb{D}}
the
pseudo-hyperbolic
distance.

The isometries under the hyperbolic distance
\mathsf{d}_{\mathbb{D}}
of the Poincaré disk are exactly the homographies of the form

\displaystyle z\mapsto\frac{az+b}{\overline{b}z+\overline{a}},\quad a,b\in\mathbb{C},\,\lvert a\rvert^{2}-\lvert b\rvert^{2}=1.

(13)

Definition 4
.

Let
\alpha>0
be a real number, the
hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function
is defined on the Poincaré disk
\mathbb{D}
by

\displaystyle\forall z\in\mathbb{D},\quad\mathsf{GAF}_{\mathbb{D}}^{(\alpha)}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{n}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+n)}{n!}}z^{n}

(14)

where
\left(\zeta_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of independent and identically distributed standard Gaussian random variables,
\zeta_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,1)
.
By
[
22
, Lemma 2.2.3]
, for
\alpha>0
,
\mathsf{GAF}_{\mathbb{D}}^{(\alpha)}
is well-defined and almost-surely an analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
.

Gaussian analytic functions have received much interest from the probability and spatial statistics communities, in particular due to the
remarkable properties of their zeros
[
22
]
.
Indeed, since Gaussian analytic functions are almost-surely analytic, with probability one, their zeros constitute a random set of isolated points in
\mathbb{C}
.
Further,
[
22
, Lemma 2.4.1]
ensures that the random zeros of a Gaussian analytic function are almost-surely
simple
.
Hence, the zero set of a Gaussian analytic function constitutes a
simple point process

[
11
,
29
]
, defined as a random variable taking values in the configurations of points in
\mathbb{C}
.
Several Gaussian analytic functions, among which the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function, are distinguished by the fact that their zeros are distributed very uniformly in their definition domain, which is mathematically formalized as an
invariance
under isometries
[
22
, Section 2.3]
.
This invariance property is the cornerstone of the zero-based signal processing procedures designed for detection
[
6
,
31
]
, denoising
[
25
,
6
]
and component separation
[
16
]
. The interested reader can refer to
[
32
]
for a review of the use of spatial statistics in time–frequency analysis.

Theorem 2
.

[
22
, Proposition 2.3.4]

For
\alpha>0
, the point process formed by the zeros of
\mathsf{GAF}_{\mathbb{D}}^{(\alpha)}
is invariant under the isometries of the hyperbolic disk.

3.2
Link with the zeros of the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function

A major consequence of Theorem
1
is that, since
\vartheta
is invertible and
\lambda
never vanishes, the zeros of the Analytic Stockwell Transform of a finite energy signal are in one-to-one correspondance with the zeros of an analytic function
F
on the unit disk.
As so, the zeros of the Analytic Stockwell Transform form a collection of isolated points in
\mathbb{D}
.
Further, in the presence of noise in the signal,
f
is random and then the zeros of
S_{\varphi_{\beta}}f
are random isolated points in
\mathbb{D}
, and thus form a point process.

In line with
[
3
,
7
,
31
]
, the purpose of this section is to study a remarkable instance of such point processes, that is the zeros of the Analytic Stockwell Transform of
white noise
, and to demonstrate that it coincides in law with the zeros of the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function introduced in Section
3.1
.
Following
[
3
,
25
,
6
,
7
]
, this connection is established in two steps: first the continuous white noise adapted to the considered transform is rigorously constructed; second the equality, in law, of the point process of the zeros of the transform of white noise and of the zeros of the associated Gaussian analytic function is proven.
The following lemma will appear as an important building block for the design of the continuous white noise adapted to the Analytic Stockwell Transform.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\beta>0
and consider the family of functions
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defined through their Fourier transforms such that,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\forall\nu\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\quad\widehat{f}_{n}(\nu)=\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\Gamma(2\beta+n+1)}}\,2\pi\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\nu}(2\pi)^{2\beta}\nu^{\beta}L_{n}^{(2\beta)}(4\pi\nu),

(15)

where
L_{n}^{(2\beta)}
denotes the
Laguerre polynomial
of order
n
with parameter
2\beta
, defined by the Rodrigues formula

\displaystyle L_{n}^{(2\beta)}(\mu)=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mu}\mu^{-2\beta}}{n!}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}\mu^{n}}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\mu}\mu^{2\beta+n}\right](\mu).

(16)

There exists a smooth nonvanishing function
\eta_{\beta}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
z=x+\mathrm{i}\xi^{-1}\in\mathbb{C}
, the Analytic Stockwell Transform of
f_{n}
has the closed-form expression:

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\varphi_{\beta}}f_{n}(x,\xi)=\eta_{\beta}(x,\xi)\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(2\beta+n+1)}{n!}}\,\vartheta(z)^{n}.\end{split}

(17)

Remark 3
.

The above lemma is an original contribution of the present work.
Indeed, first, the orthonormal basis used to connect the Cauchy Wavelet Transform of white noise to the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function in
[
3
,
25
,
7
]
has to be adapted to the Analytic Stockwell Transform of Section
2.2
.
Second, in
[
3
,
25
]
the orthonormal basis used is only defined implicitly.
Third, it seems that the explicit derivations performed in
[
7
, Theorem 2.3]
, relying on
[
7
, Equation (2.15)]
, together with the computations in
[
1
, Section 4]
, contains erroneous prefactors that might impact the definition of white noise adapted to the Cauchy Wavelet Transform used to prove
[
7
, Theorem 2.3]
.
Hence, since Equation (
17
) is the cornerstone of the demonstration of the connection between the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function and the Analytic Stockwell Transform of white noise, for the sake of completeness, the proof below details the entire calculation, including the proposed corrected prefactors and normalizations.

Proof.

First, remark that for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the analytic factor of the Stockwell transform, corresponding to

\displaystyle\xi^{\beta+1/2}W_{\psi_{\beta}}f(x,\xi^{-1})

\displaystyle=\xi^{\beta+1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\overline{\psi_{\beta}\left(\frac{t-x}{\xi^{-1}}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}t,

involves the convolution between
f
and
h
defined by
h(t)=\overline{\psi_{\beta}(-t/\xi^{-1})}
.
Then, using that the Fourier transform of the convolution between two functions is the product of the Fourier transforms of these functions leads to

\displaystyle\xi^{\beta+1/2}W_{\psi_{\beta}}f(x,\xi^{-1})

\displaystyle=\xi^{\beta+1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(\nu)\widehat{h}(\nu)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\nu x}\,\mathrm{d}\nu.

(18)

Using the symmetries of the Fourier transform and the expression of the Fourier transform of the Cauchy wavelet provided in Equation (
2
) yields

\displaystyle\widehat{h}(\nu)=\xi^{-1}\overline{\widehat{\psi}_{\beta}(\xi^{-1}\nu)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\xi^{-1-\beta}\nu^{\beta}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\xi^{-1}\nu}&\text{if }\nu>0\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

which, when injected into Equation (
18
), leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\xi^{\beta+1/2}W_{\psi_{\beta}}f(x,\xi^{-1})&=\xi^{\beta+1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(\nu)\widehat{h}(\nu)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\nu x}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(\nu)\nu^{\beta}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\xi^{-1}\nu}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\nu x}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(\nu)\nu^{\beta}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\nu(x+\mathrm{i}\xi^{-1})}\,\mathrm{d}\nu.\end{split}

(19)

Then, injecting the expression of the Fourier transform of
f_{n}
provided in Equation (
15
) into the expression of the analytic wavelet transform of Equation (
19
) and setting
z=(x+\mathrm{i}\xi^{-1})
, one obtains

\displaystyle\xi^{\beta+1/2}W_{\psi_{\beta}}f_{n}(x,\xi^{-1})=\hskip 10.10072pt

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}_{n}(\nu)\nu^{\beta}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\nu z}\,\mathrm{d}\nu

(20)

\displaystyle=\hskip 9.95845pt

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\Gamma(\beta+n+1)}}\,2\pi\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\nu(\mathrm{i}z-1)}(2\pi\nu)^{2\beta}L_{n}^{(2\beta)}(4\pi\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\nu

\displaystyle\overset{\mu\,=\,2\pi\nu}{=}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\Gamma(\beta+n+1)}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\mu(\mathrm{i}z-1)}\mu^{2\beta}L_{n}^{(2\beta)}(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

Replacing the expression of the Laguerre polynomial by its Rodrigues formula of Equation (
16
) one gets

5
Conclusion and Perspectives

After evidencing the existence of an Analytic Stockwell Transform, whose analysis window have been designed leveraging a modulated Cauchy wavelet, the zeros of the Analytic Stockwell Transform of white noise are characterized.
To that aim a well-suited construction of continuous white noise has been presented, enabling to establish a strong connection between the Analytic Stockwell Transform of white noise and the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function.
This link permitted to transfer the remarkable properties of the zeros of the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function, among which the invariance under isometries of the Poincaré disk, to the zeros of the Analytic Stockwell Transform of white noise.
Finally, intensive Monte Carlo simulations are implemented to illustrate and support this result; notably the empirical pair correlation function of the zeros of the Analytic Stockwell Transform of white noise is shown to coincide
accurately with the expected theoretical pair correlation function of the zero set of the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function.
A documented Python toolbox reproducing all the experiments has been produced and made publicly available.
9
9

9

https://github.com/courbot/ast

The characterization of the distribution of zeros under a white-noise hypothesis is the cornerstone of recent original zero-based detection procedures
[
6
,
31
,
28
]
.
Hence, the direct continuation of the present work would consists in leveraging the knowledge gained on the zeros Analytic Stockwell Transform to perform signal detection in high-noise contexts.
Though, application of spatial statistics in signal processing do not restrict to detection and zero-based denoising and separation strategies have shown promising results
[
16
,
6
,
28
]
.
The nice resolution properties and great versatility of the Analytic Stockwell Transform make it a good candidate for the development of zero-based component separation algorithms. Although in this work, the phase information of the Stockwell Transform, which differs from the phase information contained in the Wavelet Transform, has not been exploited, it could interestingly be used to complete the information of zeros with the aim of consolidating and complementing methods for signal detection and denoising since it seems that both information are intimately related
[
5
]
.
Finally, as the accuracy of zero-based procedures relies on the precise localization of zeros, novel zero detection techniques will be explored. To go beyond the Minimal Grid Neighbor algorithm, two paths will be investigated: first, an hyperbolic counterpart of the Adaptive Minimal Grid Neighbor algorithm
[
13
]
will be derived, and second, the off-the-grid algorithm developed in
[
10
]
will be generalized to the hyperbolic geometry framework with the overarching goal of enhancing the quality of spatial statistics estimators, and hence reaching state-of-the-art detection and reconstruction performance.

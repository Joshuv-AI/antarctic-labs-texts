Commutative homotopical algebra embeds into non-commutative homotopical algebra
Ricardo Campos, Dan Petersen, Daniel Robert-Nicoud, Felix Wierstra

Abstract
. Over a field of characteristic zero, we show that the forgetful functor from the homotopy category of commutative dg algebras to the homotopy category of dg associative algebras is faithful. In fact, the induced map of derived mapping spaces gives an injection on all homotopy groups at any basepoint. We prove similar results both for unital and non-unital algebras, and also Koszul dually for the universal enveloping algebra functor from dg Lie algebras to dg associative algebras. An important ingredient is a natural model for these derived mapping spaces as Maurer–Cartan spaces of complete filtered dg Lie algebras (or curved Lie algebras, in the unital case).

Primary 18M70; secondary 13D10, 13D03, 16E40, 17B35, 55P62

1.
Commutativity and associativity

1.1
.

The forgetful functor from commutative rings to associative algebras is a canonical example of a
fully faithful functor
: the notion of a homomorphism
A\to A^{\prime}
does not depend on whether
A
and
A^{\prime}
are considered as commutative or just associative algebras.

1.2
.

In the setting of homotopical algebra, the analogous statement is emphatically false. Namely, when a forgetful functor
C\to D
is fully faithful, this can be informally understood as saying that the category
C
consists of objects of
D
satisfying some
property
, and the forgetful functor merely forgets about this property. Examples could be the forgetful functor from finite sets to all sets, or as just mentioned, the forgetful functor from commutative rings to associative algebras. But in a homotopy-theoretic situation we should not be asserting the
property
that
xy=yx
; rather, we should supply a
particular
homotopy between
xy
and
yx
(and then higher homotopies, etc.), and these homotopies are additional
structure
on the algebra.

1.3
.

To be more explicit, let us consider the two categories
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{CDGA})
and
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGA})
of non-unital commutative dg algebras, and non-unital dg algebras respectively, localized at the class of quasi-isomorphisms. We always work over a field
\mathbf{K}
of characteristic zero in what follows. In this case both categories can be modeled by the Hinich model structure
[
Hin97b
]
, in which all objects are fibrant. This means in particular that the set of morphisms
A\to A^{\prime}
in the homotopy category can be computed as the set of homotopy classes of morphisms
\mathrm{Q}A\to A^{\prime}
, where
\mathrm{Q}A
denotes a cofibrant replacement of
A
. Now on the one hand a cofibrant object of
\mathsf{CDGA}
very rarely remains cofibrant in
\mathsf{DGA}
; on the other hand, the notions of homotopy in the two categories are also different. The consequence is that if
A,A^{\prime}
are commutative dg algebras, then morphisms
A\to A^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGA})
are given by an a priori larger set of maps modulo an a priori coarser notion of homotopy than morphisms in
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{CDGA})
. We denote similarly by
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{CDGA}^{u})
and
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGA}^{u})
the homotopy categories of unital commutative dg algebras and unital dg algebras, respectively. Everything we have said in this paragraph remains true in the unital case, too.

1.4
.

In fact, the functor
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGA}^{u})\to\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGA}^{u})
is
not
full. Take for example
A
to be the polynomial ring
\mathbf{K}[x,y]
, considered as a commutative dg algebra concentrated in degree
0
. It is cofibrant in the category
\mathsf{CDGA}^{u}
, but not in
\mathsf{DGA}^{u}
. A cofibrant replacement in
\mathsf{DGA}^{u}
is given by non-commutative polynomial ring
\mathbf{K}\langle x,y,z\rangle
with
|z|=1
and
dz=xy-yx
. Therefore, for any
A^{\prime}
in
\mathsf{CDGA}^{u}
, we have

\hom_{\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{CDGA}^{u})}(A,A^{\prime})\cong H_{0}(A^{\prime})\times H_{0}(A^{\prime}),

but

\hom_{\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGA}^{u})}(A,A^{\prime})\cong H_{0}(A^{\prime})\times H_{0}(A^{\prime})\times H_{1}(A^{\prime}).

The same example would work just as well if we had worked with non-unital algebras.
Our main result is the following.

1.5 Theorem
.

Over a base field of characteristic zero, the forgetful functors

\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{CDGA})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGA})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{CDGA}^{u})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGA}^{u})

are faithful.

1.6
.

The argument we will use for this result will in fact prove a stronger result at the level of simplicial mapping spaces. We denote by
\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal{C}}
the derived mapping space of a model category
\mathcal{C}
.

1.7 Theorem
.

Over a base field of characteristic zero, the natural maps

\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{CDGA}}(A,A^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{DGA}}(A,A^{\prime})

and

\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{CDGA}^{u}}(A,A^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{DGA}^{u}}(A,A^{\prime})

induced by the forgetful functors give rise to an injection on all homotopy groups at any basepoint, for any
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathsf{CDGA}
, resp.
\mathsf{CDGA}^{u}
.

1.8
.

For the homotopy groups
\pi_{n}
with
n>0
this recovers a theorem of Amrani
[
Amr15
]
, so only the result about
\pi_{0}
(i.e.
1.5
) is new. The assertion about
\pi_{0}
is significantly trickier to prove.

1.9
.

Although the structure of the proof is very similar in both the unital and non-unital case, the argument is technically simpler when dealing with not necessarily unital algebras. For the rest of the introduction we focus on this case.

1.10 Remark
.

The set of homotopy classes of maps
A\to A^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGA})
can also be understood in terms of
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-morphisms. This has the advantage that one does not have to cofibrantly replace
A
. Namely,
\hom_{\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGA})}(A,A^{\prime})
can be identified with the set of
\infty
-morphisms of
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-algebras from
A
to
A^{\prime}
, modulo
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-homotopy. Similarly
\hom_{\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{CDGA})}(A,A^{\prime})
is the set of
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-morphisms from
A
to
A^{\prime}
modulo
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-homotopy. Thus,
1.5
can be reformulated as saying that if two
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-morphisms are
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-homotopic, then they are also
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-homotopic. Such a result seems potentially quite useful since the notion of
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-homotopy is much simpler than the notion of
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-homotopy, the reason being that the
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-operad is non-symmetric. Indeed, suppose that
f,g:A\to A^{\prime}
are
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-morphisms between
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-algebras, and let

\mathrm{B}f,\mathrm{B}g:\mathrm{B}A\to\mathrm{B}A^{\prime}

be the corresponding maps of coassociative coalgebras. By a homotopy between
f
and
g
we mean a
(\mathrm{B}f,\mathrm{B}g)
-coderivation
H:\mathrm{B}A\to\mathrm{B}A^{\prime}
such that
\partial H=\mathrm{B}f-\mathrm{B}g
. We refer to
[
LH03
, Section 1.3.4]
for more details. The notion of homotopy of
\infty
-morphisms over a symmetric operad is more complicated and is discussed in detail in
[
DP16
]
.

1.11
.

This paper may be considered as a sequel, or coda, to our previous paper
[
CPRNW19
]
. We say that a functor
F\colon C\to D
is
injective on isomorphism classes
if
F(X)\cong F(Y)
implies
X\cong Y
. In Theorem A of
[
CPRNW19
]
we proved the result that
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{CDGA})\to\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGA})
is injective on isomorphism classes over a field of characteristic zero (and similarly in the unital case). This result and
1.5
are morally similar in that both try to probe the failure of the forgetful functor to be fully faithful (as clearly a fully faithful functor is injective on isomorphism classes). Putting the two papers together we see that the homotopy theory of commutative dg algebras embeds faithfully into the non-commutative homotopy theory in a precise sense. What is also striking is that the essential structure of the two arguments is extremely similar.

1.12
.

Let us try to bring out this similarity. The proof of
[
CPRNW19
, Theorem A]
quickly reduces to the following statement: given two
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-algebra structures on the same chain complex, and an
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-isotopy between them, there must also exist a
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-isotopy between them. Similarly
1.5
reduces to the statement that given two
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-morphisms between
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-algebras and an
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-homotopy between them, there also exists a
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-homotopy between them. To treat both situations on the same footing, we say simply that we are given two
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-objects
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}
related by an
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-gauge, and we want to find a
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-gauge from
\xi
to
\xi^{\prime}
. More precisely, in both cases the problems are best described in terms of deformation problems governed by dg Lie algebras. In our previous paper we considered two dg Lie algebras whose Maurer–Cartan elements correspond to
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-algebra structures (resp.
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-algebra structures) on some fixed chain complex, such that gauges correspond to
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-isotopies (resp.
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-isotopies). Similarly, in the current paper we have two dg Lie algebras that describe the set of
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-morphisms (resp.
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-morphisms) between two fixed such algebras, with gauges now corresponding to
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-homotopies (resp.
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-homotopies). In both cases there is an inclusion of the Lie algebra encoding
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-structures into the Lie algebra encoding
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-structures.

1.13
.

Once the problem is formulated in terms of
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
- and
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-structures, it is natural to try to work one coefficient at a time, which amounts to noting that the dg Lie algebras in question come with natural filtrations (or gradings) by arity of the
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
- and
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-operations. Let
\tau_{\leq n}\xi
be the truncation of
\xi
to a
\mathsf{C}_{n}
-object, discarding all operations of arity bigger than
n
. By an inductive obstruction theory argument one can in both cases show that
\tau_{\leq n}\xi
and
\tau_{\leq n}\xi^{\prime}
are
\mathsf{C}_{n}
-gauge equivalent for every
n
. Indeed, if we have an equivalence between
\tau_{\leq n}\xi
and
\tau_{\leq n}\xi^{\prime}
, then there is a well defined obstruction class for finding a gauge equivalence between
\tau_{\leq(n+1)}\xi
and
\tau_{\leq(n+1)}\xi^{\prime}
. More specifically, there are
two
classes
\mathrm{o}_{n+1}^{\mathsf{A}}\in H^{\mathsf{A}}
and
\mathrm{o}_{n+1}^{\mathsf{C}}\in H^{\mathsf{C}}
, which are obstructions for finding an
\mathsf{A}_{n+1}
-gauge and a
\mathsf{C}_{n+1}
-gauge, respectively, both lying in certain cohomology groups. Now the great news in both situations is that
H^{\mathsf{C}}
is a direct summand of
H^{\mathsf{A}}
, and
\mathrm{o}_{n+1}^{\mathsf{C}}
is the projection of
\mathrm{o}_{n+1}^{\mathsf{A}}
onto
H^{\mathsf{C}}
. But
\mathrm{o}_{n+1}^{\mathsf{A}}
must vanish, since we had an
\mathsf{A}_{\infty}
-gauge from
\xi
to
\xi^{\prime}
by assumption, and then the obstruction
\mathrm{o}_{n+1}^{\mathsf{C}}
vanishes, too. (In
[
CPRNW19
]
the groups
H^{\mathsf{C}}
and
H^{\mathsf{A}}
are Harrison and Hochschild cohomology groups, respectively, and Harrison cohomology is a direct summand of Hochschild cohomology by a result of Barr
[
Bar68
]
.) But at this point one gets stuck: there is no natural way to deduce from the fact that
\tau_{\leq n}\xi
is
\mathsf{C}_{n}
-gauge equivalent to
\tau_{\leq n}\xi^{\prime}
for all
n
, that
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}
are
\mathsf{C}_{\infty}
-gauge equivalent. Although we can find a gauge at each step, this does not imply that transfinite composition of all these gauges is well defined or convergent.

1.14
.

To make the argument work one needs to be more careful in the construction. Instead of saying at each step that a certain obstruction class vanishes in cohomology, so that
some
gauge exists, we need to be able to choose a
specific
gauge, which is small enough in a suitable sense, so that the sequence of gauges converges in the limit. To accomplish this, we pass from cohomology to cochains and consider the natural complete filtrations on everything in sight. The difficulties involved in making this strategy work can be bundled up into a criterion which we proved in
[
CPRNW19
]
.

1.15 Theorem

(
[
CPRNW19
, Theorem 1.7]
)
.

Let
i\colon\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a morphism of complete filtered dg Lie algebras over a field of characteristic zero. Suppose that there exists
r\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
such that
r\circ i=\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{h}}
, and
r
is a morphism of filtered
\mathfrak{h}
-modules. Then the map
\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{h})\longrightarrow\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
induces an injection on all homotopy groups for all basepoints. In particular, the set of gauge equivalence classes of Maurer–Cartan elements of
\mathfrak{h}
injects into the gauge equivalence classes of Maurer–Cartan elements of
\mathfrak{g}
.

1.16
.

The assumption that
\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
admits a retraction as
\mathfrak{h}
-modules immediately implies that for any Maurer–Cartan element
\alpha
of
\mathfrak{h}
, there is a split injection in homology
H_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{h}^{\alpha})\to H_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha})
, where
(-)^{\alpha}
denotes the effect of twisting the differential by the Maurer–Cartan element
\alpha
. In terms of the heuristic outline of §
1.13
, this corresponds to the fact that
H^{\mathsf{C}}
is a direct summand of
H^{\mathsf{A}}
, and that all obstruction classes vanish. Having the retraction be filtration-preserving is what gives the extra leverage and allows passage to the limit. We can now also justify the remark made in §
1.8
, that the assertion about higher homotopy groups is easier than the assertion about
\pi_{0}
in
1.7
. Indeed, for
n>0
a theorem of Berglund
[
Ber15
]
furnishes an identification
\pi_{n}(\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{h}),\alpha)\cong H_{n-1}(\mathfrak{h}^{\alpha})
, so for the injection on higher homotopy groups it is not necessary to suppose compatibility of the retraction with the filtrations, and the convergence problems are irrelevant.

1.17
.

In order to apply
1.15
we need suitable models for mapping spaces in
\mathsf{CDGA}
and
\mathsf{DGA}
in terms of Maurer–Cartan spaces of complete filtered dg Lie algebras. Before stating the result let us recall some notation and preliminaries. If
A
is a dg algebra, we denote by
\mathrm{B}A
its bar construction. If furthermore
A
is commutative, we write
\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Com}}A
for its “commutative” bar construction, i.e. the Koszul dual Lie coalgebra of
A
. Recall also that the space of linear maps from a dg Lie coalgebra to a commutative dg algebra is itself a dg Lie algebra. (This is the “linear dual” of the perhaps more familiar fact that the tensor product of a Lie algebra and a commutative ring is a Lie algebra.) Similarly the space of linear maps from a dg coalgebra to a dg algebra is itself a dg algebra, and in particular a dg Lie algebra.

1.18 Proposition
.

Let
A
and
A^{\prime}
be dg algebras over a field
\mathbf{K}
of characteristic zero. Then:

\diamond

\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{DGA}}(A,A^{\prime})\simeq\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet}(\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}A,A^{\prime}))
.

\diamond

If
A
and
A^{\prime}
are commutative, then
\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{CDGA}}(A,A^{\prime})\simeq\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet}(\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Com}}A,A^{\prime}))
.

Here, both
\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}A,A^{\prime})
and
\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Com}}A,A^{\prime})
are considered as complete filtered dg Lie algebras, with the filtrations induced by the coradical filtrations of
\mathrm{B}A
and
\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Com}}A
respectively. In the commutative case, the projection
\mathrm{B}A\to\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Com}}A
induces the natural map
\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{DGA}}(A,A^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{CDGA}}(A,A^{\prime}).

1.19 Remark
.

Versions of
1.18
are already in the literature, see in particular Berglund
[
Ber15
, Proposition 6.1]
. But note the lack of boundedness or finite-dimensionality assumptions here.

1.20 Remark
.

We expect the first equivalence
\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{DGA}}(A,A^{\prime})\simeq\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet}(\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}A,A^{\prime}))
to remain valid over fields of arbitrary characteristic, cf.
[
dKW21
]
.

1.21
.

The proof of
1.7
is now nearly complete: by
1.15
and
1.18
, all that is left to do is to construct a certain left inverse to the map

\hom_{\mathbf{K}}\left(\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Com}}A,A^{\prime}\right)\longrightarrow\hom_{\mathbf{K}}\left(\mathrm{B}A,A^{\prime}\right).

We will obtain it as follows. We can identify
\mathrm{B}A
with the
universal enveloping conilpotent coalgebra
of
\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Com}}A
. By a dual version of the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem, we can identify
\mathrm{B}A\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{c}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Com}}A)
as
\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Com}}A
-comodules. From this, one can in particular construct a splitting of the projection
\mathrm{B}A\to\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Com}}A
.

1.22
.

Again we see that the arguments of this paper follow closely those of
[
CPRNW19
]
, where it also turned out that the retraction needed to apply
1.15
was obtained from the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem.

1.23
.

As indicated in §
1.9
, there are additional complications in the unital case: namely, when the algebras have units, we need to replace dg Lie algebras with
curved
Lie algebras throughout, as we explain in
Section

5
.

1.24
.

In our previous paper
[
CPRNW19
]
we did not only study the forgetful functor
\mathsf{CDGA}\to\mathsf{DGA}
. A major portion of the paper concerned instead the
Koszul dual
of this functor. Since associative algebras are Koszul self-dual, and commutative algebras are Koszul dual to Lie algebras, this Koszul dual is the universal enveloping algebra functor
\mathsf{DGLA}\to\mathsf{DGA}
. In
[
CPRNW19
]
the results we obtained about the universal enveloping algebra functor were slightly weaker than those for the forgetful functor. Indeed, we could prove that
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{CDGA})\to\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGA})
is injective on isomorphism classes, but we could only prove that the universal enveloping algebra functor
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGLA})\to\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGA})
is injective on isomorphism classes up to
derived completion
of the dg Lie algebras involved. By contrast, in this paper we are able to prove a “Koszul dual” of
1.5
with no additional completeness hypotheses.

1.25 Theorem
.

Let
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}
be two dg Lie algebras over a field of characteristic zero. For any choice of basepoint, the natural map

\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{DGLA}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})\longrightarrow\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{DGA}}(U\mathfrak{g},U\mathfrak{h})

induces an injection on all homotopy groups.

1.26
.

This theorem is less striking than its commutative and associative counterpart
1.5
in that there is no longer a large difference in behavior between the classical (non-dg) and the homotopical situation. Indeed, consider the classical universal enveloping algebra functor from Lie algebras to associative algebras over a field of characteristic zero. This functor is clearly not full (consider e.g.
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}
a free Lie algebra). However, the universal enveloping algebra functor
does
turn out to be faithful, but it is not automatic: it is a consequence of the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem. This is also exactly what happens in the homotopical situation.

1.27
.

Some experts will perhaps not find anything surprising beyond this point: what remains of the paper is mostly to fill in details in the proof of
1.7
sketched in §
1.21
. We first explain the dual form of the PBW theorem mentioned in §
1.21
in
Section

2
, and verify that the retraction obtained from dual PBW satisfies the conditions of
1.15
in
Section

3
. After that we give the proof of
1.18
in
Section

4
. In
Section

5
we then treat the unital case, proving in particular a unital version of
1.18
involving curved Lie algebras. Finally, in Section
6
we consider the Koszul dual situation which concerns the universal enveloping algebra functor from dg Lie algebras to dg algebras.

1.28
.

We mostly follow the same conventions as in
[
CPRNW19
]
. We always work over a field
\mathbf{K}
of characteristic
0
. All the filtrations that we consider are concentrated in positive filtration degree.

1.29
.

As in
[
CPRNW19
]
we systematically use the language of algebraic operads, and the results are obtained by studying the interplay between the operads
\mathsf{Lie}
,
\mathsf{Ass}
and
\mathsf{Com}
. In fact, the only property of these operads that we end up using (besides their Koszulness) is that the natural morphism
\mathsf{Lie}\to\mathsf{Ass}
admits a left inverse in the category of infinitesimal bimodules over the operad
\mathsf{Lie}
. The methods of this paper extend to show the following more general theorem, whose proof we leave to the interested reader:

1.30 Theorem
.

Let
f\colon\mathsf{P}\to\mathsf{Q}
be a morphism of binary Koszul operads in characteristic zero with
\mathsf{P}(n)
and
\mathsf{Q}(n)
finite dimensional for all
n
. Let
\mathsf{Q}^{!}\to\mathsf{P}^{!}
be the induced morphism between the Koszul dual operads. Suppose that there exists a morphism of infinitesimal
\mathsf{P}
-bimodules
s\colon\mathsf{Q}\to\mathsf{P}
such that
s\circ f=\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{P}}
. Then both the forgetful functor from
\mathsf{Q}^{!}
-algebras to
\mathsf{P}^{!}
-algebras and the derived operadic pushforward functor
\mathbf{L}f_{!}
from
\mathsf{P}
-algebras to
\mathsf{Q}
-algebras have the property that they induce injections on all homotopy groups of derived mapping spaces for all basepoints.

2.
On the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem

2.1
.

In this section we will explain some generalities around the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem. Our perspective will be that the statement of the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem is that there is an isomorphism of infinitesimal
\mathsf{Lie}
-bimodules

(
\dagger
)

\mathsf{Ass}\cong\mathsf{Com}\circ\mathsf{Lie}.

We may also write the right hand side as
\mathrm{Sym}(\mathsf{Lie})
, which makes the infinitesimal
\mathsf{Lie}
-bimodule structure more transparent (cf.
[
CPRNW19
, Remark 2.9]
). In
[
CPRNW19
, Section 2]
we explained how to deduce (

†

‣
2.1
) from (an abstract form of) the usual PBW theorem, i.e. that for a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
there is an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
U\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})
. We will now explain how to deduce the standard form of the PBW theorem from (

†

‣
2.1
), and then explain that the exact same reasoning also implies the following dual form of the PBW theorem.

2.2 Proposition
.

Let
L
be a conilpotent dg Lie coalgebra, and denote by
U^{c}L
its universal enveloping conilpotent coalgebra. There is an isomorphism of
L
-comodules
U^{c}(L)\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{c}(L)
.

2.3
.

The material in this section is known to experts, but we do not know a reference which states it explicitly, and the perspective is perhaps somewhat novel. The original dual PBW theorem is due to Michaelis
[
Mic80
,
Mic85
]
, who considers more generally any
locally finite
Lie coalgebra, not necessarily conilpotent. The fact that the isomorphism (

†

‣
2.1
) can be promoted to an isomorphism of infinitesimal bimodules seems to have been first noticed by Griffin
[
Gri14
]
. Other closely related works are Dotsenko–Tamaroff
[
DT21
]
and Fresse
[
Fre09
, Section 10.2]
.

2.4
.

Let
\mathsf{P}\to\mathsf{Q}
be a morphism of operads. The restriction functor from
\mathsf{Q}
-algebras to
\mathsf{P}
-algebras has a left adjoint, which we denote
\mathscr{U}_{\mathsf{Q}}
. In the case of
\mathsf{Lie}\to\mathsf{Ass}
, the functor
\mathscr{U}_{\mathsf{Ass}}
is the usual universal enveloping algebra functor.

2.5
.

In general, we can identify algebras over an operad
\mathsf{P}
with left
\mathsf{P}
-modules concentrated in arity
0

\mathscr{U}_{\mathsf{Q}}

3.
Construction of a retraction

3.1
.

We write
\hom_{\mathbf{K}}
for the internal hom of chain complexes over
\mathbf{K}
. If

is a dg algebra, then

has a natural dg algebra structure defined by the

. If we write the coproduct of

using Sweedler notation

\Delta(x)=\sum x_{(1)}\otimes x_{(2)},

then the convolution product of

(f\ast g)(x)\coloneqq\sum f(x_{(1)})g(x_{(2)})

for

is a right

-comodule and

\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(M,N)
is functorially a right
\hom_{K}(C,A)

3.2

In a similar way, if

is a commutative dg algebra, then

is a dg Lie algebra. Again using Sweedler’s notation for the Lie cobracket of

is given by

[f,g](x)\coloneqq\sum f(x_{(1)})g(x_{(2)})

for

M
is an
L
-comodule and
N
an
A
-module, then
\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(M,N)
is functorially a
\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(L,A)
-module.

3.3
.

If
\mathsf{D}
is a reduced cooperad, then any
\mathsf{D}
-coalgebra comes equipped with a canonical filtration as follows. The cooperad
\mathsf{D}
has a natural increasing filtration by arity

0=\tau_{\leq 0}\mathsf{D}\xhookrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt}\tau_{\leq 1}\mathsf{D}\xhookrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt}\tau_{\leq 2}\mathsf{D}\xhookrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt}\cdots

where
\tau_{\leq k}\mathsf{D}
denotes the truncation of
\mathsf{D}
at arity
k

\mathsf{D}
-cobimodule in a natural way. If
C
is a
\mathsf{D}
-coalgebra, then

inherits a filtration given by
\tau_{\leq k}\mathsf{D}\circ^{\mathsf{D}}C
in filtration degree
k-1
, called the
coradical filtration
of

\mathsf{D}
-coalgebra

is said to be

if this filtration is exhaustive. With this description of the coradical filtration, it becomes obvious that if
\mathsf{E}\to\mathsf{D}
is a morphism of reduced cooperads, then the natural map

\mathscr{U}^{c}_{\mathsf{E}}C=\mathsf{E}\circ^{\mathsf{D}}C\longrightarrow\mathsf{D}\circ^{\mathsf{D}}C\cong C

is compatible with the respective coradical filtrations, where
\mathscr{U}^{c}_{\mathsf{E}}C
is endowed with the filtration induced by the arity filtration of
\mathsf{E}
. More generally, if
\mathsf{E}_{1}\to\mathsf{E}_{2}
is a morphism of right
\mathsf{D}
-comodules,
\mathsf{D}
-bicomodules, or infinitesimal
\mathsf{D}
-bicomodules, then the induced morphism

\mathscr{U}^{c}_{\mathsf{E}_{1}}C\longrightarrow\mathscr{U}^{c}_{\mathsf{E}_{2}}C

is compatible with the filtrations induced by the arity filtrations of
\mathsf{E}_{1}
and
\mathsf{E}_{2}
.

4.
Models for mapping spaces of algebras

4.1
.

Hinich
[
Hin97b
]
has constructed a simplicial model structure on unbounded dg algebras over an operad
\mathsf{P}
over a field
\mathbf{K}
of characteristic zero. This model structure is right transferred from the model structure on unbounded chain complexes along the forgetful functor. The resulting weak equivalences are the morphisms of
\mathsf{P}
-algebras that are quasi-isomorphisms of chain complexes, and the fibrations are the morphisms that are degree-wise surjections. Any triangulated algebra
[
LV12
, Section B.6.7]
is cofibrant. In particular, if
\mathsf{P}
is a Koszul operad, then a canonical cofibrant replacement functor is given by the bar-cobar resolution. The simplicial enrichment is given by

\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{P}}(A,A^{\prime})\coloneqq\hom_{\mathsf{P}\text{-}\mathsf{alg}}(A,A^{\prime}\otimes\Omega_{\bullet})\in\mathsf{sSet},

where
\Omega_{\bullet}
is Sullivan’s algebra of polynomial differential forms on the simplices.

4.2
.

For a complete filtered dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, Hinich
[
Hin97a
]
has also introduced the
Maurer–Cartan simplicial set

\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})\coloneqq\mathrm{MC}(\mathfrak{g}\penalty\ \widehat{\otimes}\penalty\ \Omega_{\bullet}),

where
\mathfrak{g}\penalty\ \widehat{\otimes}\penalty\ \Omega_{\bullet}\coloneqq\varprojlim(\mathfrak{g}/F^{n}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Omega_{\bullet})
. It is a non-trivial result that this simplicial set is in fact a Kan complex. Note in particular that
\mathrm{MC}_{0}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{MC}(\mathfrak{g})
. Since
\mathfrak{g}
has a complete filtration, we may also exponentiate
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
to get a
gauge group
acting on
\mathrm{MC}(\mathfrak{g})
, and two Maurer–Cartan elements of
\mathfrak{g}
lie in the same connected component of
\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
if and only if they are gauge equivalent.

4.3
.

We can now prove

5.
The unital case

5.1
.

In this section, we show the unital version of Theorem
1.5
. Recall that we denote by
\mathsf{DGA}^{u}
(resp.
\mathsf{CDGA}^{u}
) the categories of unital associative (resp. commutative) dg algebras over our fixed base field
\mathbf{K}
.

5.2 Theorem
.

Over a base field of characteristic zero, the forgetful functor

\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{CDGA}^{u})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ho}(\mathsf{DGA}^{u})

is faithful. More generally, the map
\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{CDGA}^{u}}(A,A^{\prime})\to\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{DGA}^{u}}(A,A^{\prime})

induced by the forgetful functor induces an injection on all homotopy groups, for any
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathsf{CDGA}^{u}
.

5.3
.

The main difference in the unital case is the following. Our strategy for proving
1.5
was to model the two mapping spaces as Maurer–Cartan simplicial sets of two dg Lie algebras, and then to apply our criterion
1.15
. However, in the categories of unital (commutative) dg algebras, it is simply not possible that mapping spaces can be modeled by dg Lie algebras in this way. Indeed, dg Lie algebras intrinsically model
pointed
spaces, since the element
0
provides a canonical basepoint of the Maurer–Cartan space. The space of maps between two non-unital rings is canonically pointed by the zero map, but the space of maps between two unital rings has no natural basepoint. In fact the space of maps may even be empty: for example, there does not exist any homomorphism
\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{R}
, derived or otherwise. To overcome this we need to systematically replace dg Lie algebras with
curved Lie algebras
.

5.4
.

Indeed, from the point of view of rational homotopy theory, the choice of an augmentation of a commutative dg algebra corresponds to fixing a basepoint of a space. Thus, one may think that unital algebras without augmentations should correspond to unbased spaces. It is a remarkable insight of Positselski
[
Pos11
]
that Koszul duality can be extended to unital algebras by introducing a
curvature
term on the other side of the duality: the Koszul dual of a unital commutative dg algebra is a
curved
Lie coalgebra. Similarly counital coalgebras are Koszul dual to curved algebras. It is then natural that Maurer–Cartan spaces of curved Lie algebras can be fruitfully used to model unbased spaces. Further operadic and homotopical studies of curved Lie (or
\mathsf{L}_{\infty}
-) algebras have been pursued by
[
HM12
,
Bra12
,
Mau17
,
LG19
,
LGL18
,
CCN21
,
RiL22
]
.

5.5
.

The proof of
5.2
will closely follow the proof of
1.5
, except that we need to replace
1.15
and
1.18
with the following curved counterparts.

.

Let

\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{h})\longrightarrow\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})

Let

\diamond

\diamond

Here, both
\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{uAss}}A,A^{\prime})

\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{uCom}}A,A^{\prime})

5.8

Once

5.9

The proof of

is a complete filtered dg Lie algebra and

by

and a point

. If

is instead a complete filtered

by an

\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}_{-1}

[

is a pointed space.

\diamond

6.1

In this section we prove

. We write
\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}
for the bar construction from dg Lie algebras to dg cocommutative coalgebras and

for its left adjoint cobar construction.

6.2 Lemma
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be dg Lie algebras over a field of characteristic zero. There is a commutative diagram

\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet}(\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}))
\mathrm{MC}_{\bullet}(\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g},U\mathfrak{h})).
\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{DGLA}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{DGA}}(U\mathfrak{g},U\mathfrak{h}).
\sim
\sim

in which the vertical arrows are homotopy equivalences, the upper horizontal arrow is induced by the natural map
\mathfrak{h}\to U\mathfrak{h}
, and the lower horizontal arrow is induced from the universal enveloping algebra functor.

Proof.

We remark that the left vertical arrow is constructed by the exact same arguments as in
4.4
. As in the proof of
4.4
, the two simplicial mapping spaces depend on the choice of a cofibrant replacement of
\mathfrak{g}
and
U\mathfrak{g}
, respectively. For
\mathfrak{g}
we choose the bar-cobar resolution
\Omega_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{g}
. But for
U\mathfrak{g}
we choose instead to take the resolution

\Omega\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}U\mathfrak{g}.

This map is constructed as follows: the universal enveloping algebra functor preserves quasi-isomorphisms by the PBW theorem, so from the bar-cobar resolution of
\mathfrak{g}
we get a quasi-isomorphism
U\Omega_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}U\mathfrak{g}
. But if
C
is a cocommutative coalgebra, then
\Omega C\cong U\Omega_{\mathsf{Lie}}C

[
CPRNW19
, Lemma 4.7]
, so
U\Omega_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g}\cong\Omega\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g}
. The algebra
\Omega\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g}
is triangulated, hence cofibrant, by the same argument that shows that any bar-cobar resolution is cofibrant.

With these choices of cofibrant replacement, the simplicial enrichment constructed by Hinich (§
4.1
) gives

\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{DGLA}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})_{n}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{DGLA}}(\Omega_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}\otimes\Omega_{n})=\mathrm{MC}(\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}\otimes\Omega_{n}))

and

\mathbf{R}\mathrm{map}_{\mathsf{DGA}}(U\mathfrak{g},U\mathfrak{h})_{n}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{DGA}}(\Omega\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g},U\mathfrak{h}\otimes\Omega_{n})=\mathrm{MC}(\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g},U\mathfrak{h}\otimes\Omega_{n}))

with the map between them given by the universal enveloping algebra functor. But now we have in each simplicial degree a commuting diagram

\mathrm{MC}(\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})\penalty\ \widehat{\otimes}\penalty\ \Omega_{n})
\mathrm{MC}(\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g},U\mathfrak{h})\penalty\ \widehat{\otimes}\penalty\ \Omega_{n})
\mathrm{MC}(\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}\otimes\Omega_{n}))
\mathrm{MC}(\hom_{\mathbf{K}}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\mathfrak{g},U\mathfrak{h}\otimes\Omega_{n}))

with the horizontal arrows induced by
\mathfrak{h}\to U\mathfrak{h}
, which produces the commuting square of the lemma. The vertical arrows realize to homotopy equivalences by
[
RNW19a
, Theorem 4.1]
.
∎

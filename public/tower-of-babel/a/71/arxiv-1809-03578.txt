Studies of \cmsSymbolFace ​ B \cPqs ​ 2 ∗ ​ ( 5840 ) 0 \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}(5840)^{0} and \cmsSymbolFace ​ B \cPqs ​ 1 ​ ( 5830 ) 0 \cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}(5830)^{0} mesons including the observation of the \cmsSymbolFace ​ B \cPqs ​ 2 ∗ ​ ( 5840 ) 0 → \cmsSymbolFace ​ B 0 ​ \PK S 0 \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}(5840)^{0}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}} decay in proton-proton collisions at s = 8 ​ \TeV \sqrt{s}=8\TeV


Abstract
Measurements of \cmsSymbolFace ​ B \cPqs ​ 2 ∗ ​ ( 5840 ) 0 \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}(5840)^{0} and \cmsSymbolFace ​ B \cPqs ​ 1 ​ ( 5830 ) 0 \cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}(5830)^{0} mesons are performed using a data sample of proton-proton collisions corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 19.6 ​ \fbinv 19.6\fbinv , collected with the CMS detector at the LHC at a centre-of-mass energy of 8 ​ \TeV 8\TeV . The analysis studies P P -wave \cmsSymbolFace ​ B \cPqs 0 \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}_{\cPqs} meson decays into \cmsSymbolFace ​ B ( ∗ ) + ​ \PK − \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{(*)+}\PK^{-} and \cmsSymbolFace ​ B ( ∗ ) ​ 0 ​ \PK S 0 \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{(*)0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}} , where the \cmsSymbolFace ​ B + \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+} and \cmsSymbolFace ​ B 0 \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0} mesons are identified using the decays \cmsSymbolFace ​ B + → \cmsSymbolFace ​ J / ψ ​ \PK + \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\to\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi\PK^{+} and \cmsSymbolFace ​ B 0 → \cmsSymbolFace ​ J / ψ ​ \PK ∗ ​ ( 892 ) 0 \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\to\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi\PK^{*}(892)^{0} . The masses of the P P -wave \cmsSymbolFace ​ B \cPqs 0 \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}_{\cPqs} meson states are measured and the natural width of the \cmsSymbolFace ​ B \cPqs ​ 2 ∗ ​ ( 5840 ) 0 \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}(5840)^{0} state is determined. The first measurement of the mass difference between the charged and neutral \cmsSymbolFace ​ B ∗ \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*} mesons is also presented. The \cmsSymbolFace ​ B \cPqs ​ 2 ∗ ​ ( 5840 ) 0 \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}(5840)^{0} decay to \cmsSymbolFace ​ B 0 ​ \PK S 0 \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}} is observed, together with a measurement of its branching fraction relative to the \cmsSymbolFace ​ B \cPqs ​ 2 ∗ ​ ( 5840 ) 0 → \cmsSymbolFace ​ B + ​ \PK − \cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}(5840)^{0}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\PK^{-} decay.

0.1
Introduction

The
P
-wave
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}_{\cPqs}
states are the bound states of
\cPqb
and
\cPqs
quarks
with an orbital angular momentum
L=1
. Since the b quark is considerably
heavier than the strange quark, heavy-quark effective theory
(HQET)
[
1
,
2
]

can be applied to describe this system. In the HQET framework, the state
can be described by
L
and the spin of the
light quark, providing a total angular momentum
of the light subsystem
j=L\pm\frac{1}{2}
.
In the case of
L=1
, this results in
j=\frac{1}{2}
or
j=\frac{3}{2}
.
Including the additional splitting from the spin of the heavy b quark
results in a total angular momentum
J=j\pm\frac{1}{2}
,
yielding two doublets, with the four states denoted as:

\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 0}
(
j=\frac{1}{2}
,
J^{P}=0^{+}
),

\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 1}
(
j=\frac{1}{2}
,
J^{P}=1^{+}
),

\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}
(
j=\frac{3}{2}
,
J^{P}=1^{+}
), and

\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}
(
j=\frac{3}{2}
,
J^{P}=2^{+}
).
The two former states have not been observed to date, while the latter two
are known as the
\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}(5830)^{0}
and
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}(5840)^{0}
mesons, respectively.
For simplicity in this paper, shortened symbols are used
to denote the following particles:

\PK^{*0}\equiv\PK^{*}(892)^{0}
,
\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{1}\equiv\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{1}(5721)^{0}
,

\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{2}\equiv\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{2}(5747)^{0}
,
\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}\equiv\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}(5830)^{0}
,

\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\equiv\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}(5840)^{0}
, and
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{(*)}_{\cPqs 1,2}
refers to either
\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}
or
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}
.
Charge-conjugate states are implied throughout the paper.
According to HQET, the decays
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\PK^{-}
,
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*+}\PK^{-}
, and
\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*+}\PK^{-}

are allowed and should proceed through a
D
-wave transition,
while the decay
\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\PK^{-}
is forbidden. Similar conclusions
apply to the decays into
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{(*)0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
.

Orbitally excited states of the
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}_{\cPqs}
meson were observed by the CDF and D0
Collaborations via the decays into
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{(*)+}\PK^{-}

[
3
,
4
]
.
More recently, the LHCb Collaboration presented a more precise study of these
states and observed the decay
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}(5840)^{0}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*+}\PK^{-}

[
5
]
,
favouring the spin-parity assignment
J^{P}=2^{+}
for the
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}(5840)^{0}
state.
The CDF Collaboration subsequently presented a study of excited
\PB
meson
states
[
6
]
that included measurements of the

\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{(*)}_{\cPqs 1,2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{(*)+}\PK^{-}
decays.

\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{(*)}_{\cPqs 1,2}
results.

\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
decay and a measurement

\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\PK^{-}

The
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}
and
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}
candidates are reconstructed using the

\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\to\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})\PK^{+}

\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\to\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})\PK^{*0}(\PK^{+}\pi^{-})

.

19.6\fbinv
of
proton-proton collisions at
\sqrt{s}=8\TeV
, collected by
the CMS experiment
[
7
]
at the CERN LHC in 2012.

0.2
The CMS detector

The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid
of 6
\unit
m internal diameter, providing a magnetic field of 3.8
\unit
T.
Within the solenoid volume are a silicon pixel and strip tracker, a lead
tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter, and a brass and
scintillator hadron calorimeter, each composed of a barrel and two
endcap sections. Muons are detected
in the pseudorapidity range
\abs{\eta}<2.4

in gas-ionization chambers embedded in
the steel flux-return yoke outside the solenoid.
The main subdetectors used for the present analysis are
the silicon tracker and the muon detection system.
The silicon tracker measures charged particles within the
range
\abs{\eta}<2.5
.
For nonisolated particles with transverse momentum
1<\pt<10\GeV

and
\abs{\eta}<1.4
,
the track resolutions are typically 1.5% in
\pt
and 25–90 (45–150)
\mum
in the transverse (longitudinal) impact parameter
[
8
]
.
Matching muons to tracks measured in the silicon tracker
results in a relative
\pt
resolution for muons
with
\pt<10\GeV
of 0.8–3.0% depending on

\abs{\eta}

[
9
]
.
A more detailed description of the CMS detector, together with a definition
of the coordinate system used and the relevant kinematic variables,
can be found in Ref.
[
7
]
.

Events of interest are selected using a two-tiered trigger
system
[
10
]
. The first level, composed of custom
hardware processors, uses information from the calorimeters and muon detectors
to select events at a rate of around 100
\unit
kHz within a time interval
of less than 4
\mus
.
The second level, known as the high-level trigger
(HLT), consists of a farm of processors running a version of the full event
reconstruction software optimized for fast processing, and reduces the event
rate to around 1
\unit
kHz before data storage.

0.3
Event reconstruction and selection

The data sample is collected with an HLT algorithm
designed to select events with two muons consistent with originating
from a charmonium resonance decaying at a significant distance from the beam axis.
The requirements imposed at the trigger level include

\pt(\mu^{\pm})>3.5\GeV
,
\abs{\eta(\mu^{\pm})}<2.2
,
\pt(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})>6.9\GeV
,
dimuon vertex
\chi^{2}
fit probability
P_{\text{vtx}}(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})>10\%
,
dimuon invariant mass
1.0<M(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})<4.8\GeV
,
distance between the beam axis and the reconstructed dimuon vertex position
in the transverse plane
L_{xy}(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})>3\sigma_{L_{xy}(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})}
,
where
\sigma_{L_{xy}(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})}
is the uncertainty in
L_{xy}(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
,
and the cosine of the dimuon candidate pointing angle to the beam axis

\cos(\vec{L}_{xy}(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
,
\vec{\pt}(\mu^{+}\mu^{-}))>0.9
.
The pointing angle is the angle between the
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
candidate momentum in
the transverse (
x
–
y
) plane and the vector from the
beam axis position to the reconstructed dimuon vertex in the transverse plane.

The reconstruction and selection of the
\PB
meson candidates are similar
to those described in Ref.
[
11
]
.
The analysis requires two muons of opposite charge
that must match those that triggered the event readout.
The trigger requirements are confirmed and the
\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi
candidates are selected
by tightening the dimuon mass region to
[3.04,\,3.15]\GeV
.

The
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\to\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi\PK^{+}
candidates are constructed by combining the selected
\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi

candidates with a track having
\pt>1\GeV
to which the kaon mass is assigned.
The muon candidates must also satisfy the soft-muon identification
criteria described in Ref.
[
9
]
, and
the kaon candidates must pass the high-purity track requirements
detailed in Ref.
[
8
]
.
A kinematic fit to the three tracks is performed that constrains
the dimuon invariant mass to the world-average
\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi
mass
[
12
]
.
From all the reconstructed
\mathrm{pp}
collision vertices in an event, the primary vertex (PV)
is chosen as the one with the smallest
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}
pointing angle.
This pointing angle is the angle between the
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}
candidate momentum and
the vector from the PV to the reconstructed
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}
candidate vertex.
Furthermore, in this procedure, if any of the three tracks used in the

\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}
candidate reconstruction are included in the fit of the chosen
PV, they are removed, and the PV is refitted.
The
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}
candidates are required to have
\pt(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+})>10\GeV
,

P_{\text{vtx}}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+})>1\%
,
L_{xy}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+})>5\sigma_{L_{xy}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+})}
,
and
\cos(\vec{L}_{xy}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+})
,
\vec{\pt}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}))>0.99
.
The invariant mass distribution of the
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\to\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi\PK^{+}
candidates
is shown in Fig.
1
(a).
An unbinned extended maximum-likelihood fit is performed to this
distribution using a triple-Gaussian function
with common mean for the signal,
an exponential function for the combinatorial background, and a fixed-shape function,
derived from simulation, accounting for the Cabibbo-suppressed
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\to\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi\pi^{+}
decay.
The parameters of the signal and the combinatorial background contributions, as well
as the yields of the different components, are free in the fit.
The effective resolution of the signal function (
\sigma_{M_{\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}}}
) found from
simulation of about 24
\MeV
is consistent with the resolution measured in data.
The invariant mass
M(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+})
returned by the vertex fit is
required to lie in the range
[5.23,5.33]\GeV
, corresponding to
a
\pm 2\sigma_{M_{\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}}}
window around the
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}
mass.

The selected
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}
candidates are combined with each track originating
from the chosen PV with the charged kaon mass assigned to it.
The track charge must be opposite to that of the reconstructed

\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}
meson candidate (in the following, this track is referred to as
\PK^{-}
).
The kaon candidate is required to fulfill the standard high-purity track
requirements
[
8
]
and have
\pt(\PK^{-})>1\GeV
.

The reconstruction of
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\to\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})\PK^{*0}(\PK^{+}\pi^{-})
candidates
is similar to the one used for the charged decay mode.
The dimuon combinations forming
\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi
candidates are
obtained using the same algorithm.
The
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}
candidates are constructed from the selected
\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi

candidates and two tracks of opposite charge, assumed to be from a
kaon and a pion. The tracks are required to satisfy standard high-purity
track requirements
[
8
]
and have
\pt>1\GeV
. Those kaon and pion
candidates that can be matched to a signal in the muon chambers are rejected.

The
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}
candidates are obtained by performing a kinematic vertex fit
to the four tracks described above that constrains the dimuon invariant mass
to that of the
\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi
meson
[
12
]
.
The candidates are required to have
L_{xy}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0})>5\sigma_{L_{xy}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0})}
,

P_{\text{vtx}}(\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\PK^{+}\pi^{-})>1\%
,
\cos(\vec{L}_{xy}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}),\vec{\pt}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}))>0.99
,
and
\pt(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0})>10\GeV
.
To reject the contribution from
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}_{\cPqs}\to\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi\phi
decay, the invariant
mass of the two hadron tracks, if both are assigned the kaon mass, is required
to be above 1.035
\GeV
.
We demand that the
\PK^{+}\pi^{-}
invariant mass is within 90
\MeV
of the
\PK^{*0}

mass
[
12
]
. If both the
\PK^{+}\pi^{-}
and
\PK^{-}\pi^{+}
hypotheses pass this
selection, then the
\PK^{+}\pi^{-}
invariant mass must be closer to the
\PK^{*0}

mass than the
\PK^{-}\pi^{+}
invariant mass.
The invariant mass distribution of the selected
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\to\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi\PK^{+}\pi^{-}
candidates
is shown in Fig.
1
(b).

0.5
Efficiencies and systematic uncertainties

The efficiency for each decay channel is calculated using simulated signal samples.
It is defined as the number of reconstructed signal events from the
simulation divided by the number of generated events. The efficiency includes
the detector acceptance, trigger, and candidate reconstruction efficiencies.
Only the ratios of such efficiencies for different decay modes are needed
in formulae (
1
)–(
6
), which reduces the systematic uncertainties
in those ratios. The resulting efficiency ratios used
in the measurements of the ratios of the branching fractions are:

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\PK^{-})}{\epsilon(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}})}

\displaystyle=15.77\pm 0.18,~~~\frac{\epsilon(\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*+}\PK^{-})}{\epsilon(\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}})}=16.33\pm 0.20,

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\PK^{-})}{\epsilon(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*+}\PK^{-})}

\displaystyle=0.961\pm 0.010,~~~\frac{\epsilon(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}})}{\epsilon(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}})}=0.970\pm 0.012,

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\PK^{-})}{\epsilon(\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*+}\PK^{-})}

\displaystyle=0.953\pm 0.010,~~~\frac{\epsilon(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}})}{\epsilon(\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}})}=0.987\pm 0.012,

Studies of simulated events show that the mass differences measured in the

\MeV

[
21

[

\MeV

\MeV

Results

The decay
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
is observed for the
first time with a corresponding statistical significance of 6.3 standard deviations.
The first evidence (3.9 standard deviations) for the decay
\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
is found.
In the measurements presented below of the relative branching fractions,
cross sections multiplied by branching fractions, masses, mass differences,
and natural width, the first uncertainty is statistical, the second is systematic,
and if there is a third, it is related to the uncertainties in the
world-average values of the branching fractions, masses, and mass differences
[
12
]
.

Formulae (
1
)–(
4
) are used with the branching
fractions
[
12
]

\mathcal{B}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\to\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi\PK^{+})=(1.026\pm 0.031)\,10^{-3},

\mathcal{B}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\to\cmsSymbolFace{J}\hskip-0.80002pt/\hskip-1.4pt\psi\PK^{*0})=(1.28\pm 0.05)\,10^{-3},

\mathcal{B}(\PK^{*0}\to\PK^{+}\pi^{-})=(0.99754\pm 0.00021),
and

\mathcal{B}(\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-})=(0.6920\pm 0.0005)

to determine the following ratios of branching fractions:

\displaystyle R^{0\pm}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{B}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}})}{\mathcal{B}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\PK^{-})}

\displaystyle=0.432\pm 0.077\pm 0.075\pm 0.021,

\displaystyle R^{0\pm}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{B}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}})}{\mathcal{B}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*+}\PK^{-})}

\displaystyle=0.49\pm 0.12\pm 0.07\pm 0.02,

\displaystyle R^{\pm}_{2*}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{B}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*+}\PK^{-})}{\mathcal{B}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}\PK^{-})}

\displaystyle=0.081\pm 0.021\pm 0.015,

\displaystyle R^{0}_{2*}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{B}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}})}{\mathcal{B}(\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}})}

\displaystyle=0.093\pm 0.086\pm 0.014.

Summary

The
P
-wave
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}_{\cPqs}
meson states are studied using a data
sample corresponding to an integrated luminosity of

19.6\fbinv
of proton-proton collisions collected by the CMS experiment
at
\sqrt{s}=8\TeV
in 2012. Observation and evidence are reported
for the decays
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}(5840)^{0}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
and
\cmsSymbolFace{B}_{\cPqs 1}(5830)^{0}\to\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}\PK^{0}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
, respectively.
Four ratios of branching fractions and two ratios of production cross
sections multiplied by the branching fractions of the
P
-wave
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}_{\cPqs}
mesons
into a
\PB
meson and kaon are measured. In addition, the differences between the

\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{(*)}_{\cPqs 1,2}
mass and the sum of the
\PB
meson and kaon mass are determined,
as well as the
\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*}_{\cPqs 2}(5840)^{0}
natural width.
Finally, using a new approach, the mass differences
M_{\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{0}}-M_{\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{+}}
and

M_{\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*0}}-M_{\cmsSymbolFace{B}^{*+}}
are measured, where the latter is determined for the first time.

Characteristic polynomials and finitely dimensional representations of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 , ℂ ) \mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
Tianyi Jiang, Shoumin Liu

Abstract
In this paper, we obtain a general formula for the characteristic polynomial of a finitely dimensional representation of Lie algebra 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 , ℂ ) \mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C}) and the form for these characteristic polynomials, and prove there is one to one correspondence between representations and their characteristic polynomials. We define a product on these characteristic polynomials, endowing them with a monoid structure.

The finitely dimensional representations of

2
Conjugation automorphisms on
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
is a basic object in Lie theory, and it is well known that it has a canonical basis

h=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}\right),\quad e_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
0&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad e_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
1&0\\
\end{array}\right),

with their Lie bracket relations:

[e_{1},e_{2}]=h,\quad[h,e_{1}]=2e_{1},\quad[h,e_{2}]=-2e_{2}.

(2.1)

By the Lie algebra automorphisms of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
, there are many possible choices for this kind of basis, the following theorem implies more choices for them.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that we have a matrix
h^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
with
{\rm det}(h)=-1
, then there exists a
2\times 2
invertible matrix
A
,
such that

AhA^{-1}=h^{{}^{\prime}}
. Let
Ae_{1}A^{-1}=e_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}
,
Ae_{2}A^{-1}=e_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}
. Therefore,
h^{{}^{\prime}}
,
e_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}
,
e_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}
satisfy the relations (
2.1
) by replacing

h
,
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
, respectively.

Proof.

Suppose that the eigenvalues of
h^{{}^{\prime}}
are
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
.
Since we have
h^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
with
{\rm det}(h)=-1
, it follows that

\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=0,\quad\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}=-1,

therefore, we have
\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}=\{\pm 1\}.
So there is a
2\times 2
invertible matrix
A
,
such that

AhA^{-1}=h^{{}^{\prime}}
. Easily, we can check that the conjugation map,

\displaystyle\sigma_{A}:\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C}),

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle AxA^{-1}

is a Lie algebra automorphism on
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
. Hence the three elements

h^{{}^{\prime}}
,
e_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}
,
e_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}
satisfy the relations (
2.1
) by replacing

h
,
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
, respectively.
∎

Hu-Zhang’s conjecture by conjugation automorphisms

Remark 3.1

It is well known that

,

Definition 3.2

The polynomial

In

f_{m}(z_{0},z_{1})=f_{\phi}(z_{0},z_{1},1,1)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(z_{0}-(m-2i)\sqrt{z_{1}^{2}+1}\right).

The formula plays an important role in showing (
1.1
) in
[

It is proved by showing the eigenvalues and eigenvectors directly in

Proof.

Let

h^{{}^{\prime}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\\
\end{array}\right)=e_{1}+e_{2}.

Then we can see that

satisfies the condition in Theorem
2.1
, then we can find the

and
e_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}
for Theorem
2.1
, where

e_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1/2&-1/2\\
1/2&-1/2\\
\end{array}\right),\quad e_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1/2&1/2\\
-1/2&-1/2\\
\end{array}\right).

So
h=e_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}+e_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}
, and

z_{1}h+e_{1}+e_{2}=z_{1}(e_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}+e_{2}^{{}^{\prime}})+h^{{}^{\prime}}
.
Up to some base change on

,
h^{{}^{\prime}}
,
e_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}
, and
e_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}
have the same matrices
as
h
,
e_{1}
, and
e_{2}
, respectively. Therefore, it follows that

f_{\phi}(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})={\rm det}(z_{0}I+z_{1}\phi(h^{{}^{\prime}})+z_{2}\phi(e_{1}^{{}^{\prime}})+z_{3}\phi(e_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}))

(3.1)

Hence we have

\displaystyle f_{m}(z_{0},z_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\phi}(z_{0},z_{1},1,1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm det}(z_{0}I+z_{1}\phi(h)+\phi(e_{1})+\phi(e_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm det}(z_{0}I+z_{1}\phi(h^{{}^{\prime}})+\phi(e_{1}^{{}^{\prime}})+\phi(e_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm det}(z_{0}I+z_{1}\phi(e_{1}+e_{2})+\phi(h))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm det}(z_{0}I+z_{1}\phi(e_{1})+z_{1}\phi(e_{2})+\phi(h))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\phi}(z_{0},1,z_{1},z_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm det}\left(z_{0}I+\phi(h+z_{1}(e_{1}+e_{2}))\right).

We have

h+z_{1}(e_{1}+e_{2})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&z_{1}\\
z_{1}&-1\\
\end{array}\right).

It follows that

. When

, we can check that

h^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{h+z_{1}(e_{1}+e_{2})}{\sqrt{1+z_{1}^{2}}}

satisfies the Theorem
2.1

and Remark

, we can have that

has eigenvalues

-m
,
-m+2
,

,
m
, which implies that

has eigenvalues

,
-\sqrt{1+z_{1}^{2}}(m-2)
,
\cdots
,
\sqrt{1+z_{1}^{2}}m
.
Hence when we consider the
z_{0}
and
z_{1}
are complex numbers, and

f_{m}(z_{0},z_{1})=\prod_{i=0}^{m}\left(z_{0}+(m-2i)\sqrt{1+z_{1}^{2}}\right),

or

f_{m}(z_{0},z_{1})=\begin{cases}z_{0}\prod_{l=1}^{m/2}\left(z_{0}^{2}-4l^{2}(1+z_{1}^{2})\right)\quad 2\mid m.\\
\\
\prod_{l=0}^{(m-1)/2}\left(z_{0}^{2}-(2l+1)^{2}(1+z_{1}^{2})\right)\quad 2\nmid m.\end{cases}

(3.2)

It is known that

4
Characteristic polynomials of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})

In
[
2
]
, the authors obtain the
f_{\phi}(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
by proving the Hu’s conjecture.
Now we consider a
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
representation
\phi:\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, where
V
is a finite dimension complex vector space.
It is known that
\phi(h)
is semisimple (diagonalizable) with integer eigenvalues. For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we use
d_{n,\phi}
denote the dimension of eigenvector space
\phi(h)
of the eigenvalue
n
, namely

d_{n,\phi}=\dim\{v\in V\mid\phi(h)v=nv\}.

For the representation
\phi
, the following theorem holds.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
be a representation of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
, then
the characteristic polynomial

f_{\phi}(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=z_{0}^{d_{0,\phi}}\prod_{n\geq 1}(z_{0}^{2}-n^{2}(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}))^{d_{n,\phi}}.

(4.1)

Proof.

By (
1.1
), it is easy to verify that the formula (
4.1
) holds for the finite dimensional irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
.
By the semisimplity of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
, then we have the decomposition

\phi\simeq\oplus_{t=1}^{s}\phi_{t},

Where each

\phi_{t}
is an irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
.
By the definition of characteristic polynomial, we have

f_{\phi}(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=\prod_{t=1}^{s}f_{\phi_{t}}(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}).

Furthermore, we have
d_{n,\phi}=\sum_{t=1}^{s}d_{n,\phi_{t}}
. It follows that

\displaystyle f_{\phi}(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{s}f_{\phi_{t}}(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{s}\left(z_{0}^{d_{0,\phi_{t}}}\prod_{n\geq 1}(z_{0}^{2}-n^{2}(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}))^{d_{n,\phi_{t}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{0}^{\sum_{t=1}^{s}d_{0,\phi_{t}}}\prod_{n\geq 1}(z_{0}^{2}-n^{2}(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}))^{\sum_{t=1}^{s}d_{n,\phi_{t}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{0}^{d_{0,\phi}}\prod_{n\geq 1}(z_{0}^{2}-n^{2}(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}))^{d_{n,\phi}}.

∎

On the other direction, the structure of a finite dimensional
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
-module is totally decided by its characteristic polynomial.

Theorem 4.2
.

Two finite dimensional representations
\phi
and
\psi
of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
are isomorphic if and only if they have the same characteristic polynomial.

Proof.

The necessarity is obvious. Suppose that the characteristic polynomial
f_{\phi}(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
is fixed,
we will show that the representation
\phi
can be reconstructed in a unique way.
Because the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}]
is a unique factorization ring, by Theorem
4.1
, we can suppose that

f_{\phi}(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=z_{0}^{d_{0}}\prod_{n=1}^{N}(z_{0}^{2}-n^{2}(z_{1}+z_{2}z_{3}))^{d_{n}},

(4.2)

with
d_{N}\geq 1
.
Let
\varphi_{m}
be the irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
of dimension
m+1
with highest weight
m
, for
m\geq 0
.
Suppose that

\phi\simeq\oplus_{m\geq 0}l_{m}\varphi_{m},

where
l_{m}
is the multiplicity of
\varphi_{m}
in
\phi
. By the equation (
4.2
), we see that

\phi\simeq\oplus_{m=0}^{N}l_{m}\varphi_{m}.

For Characteristic polynomials, it is known that

f_{\phi}=\prod_{m=0}^{N}f_{\varphi_{m}}^{l_{m}}.

Therefore by the equation (
4.2
),
we see that
l_{N}=d_{N}
.
Therefore

f_{\oplus_{m=0}^{N-1}l_{m}\varphi_{m}}=\frac{f_{\phi}}{f_{\varphi_{N}}^{d_{N}}},

thus the
l_{m}
for
m\leq N-1
can be decided by induction.
∎

From the algorithm in the Proof of Theorem
4.2
, it is easy to obtain the following description for the characteristic polynomials of all finite dimensional representations of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
.

Corollary 4.3
.

A polynomial
f_{(}z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\in\mathbb{C}[z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}]
is a characteristic polynomial of a finite dimensional representation of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
if and only if

f_{\phi}(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=z_{0}^{d_{0}}\prod_{n=1}^{N}(z_{0}^{2}-n^{2}(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}))^{d_{n}}

with
d_{n}\geq d_{n+2}
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}.

5
A monoid structure on
\mathbf{CP}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})}

We denote characteristic polynomials of all finite dimensional representations of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
by
\mathbf{CP}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})}
in the Section
4
, which is a subset of
\mathbb{C}[z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}]
.
In this section, a monoid structure on
\mathbf{CP}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})}
will be defined.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
f_{\phi}
,
f_{\psi}
be two polynomials in
\mathbf{CP}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})}
. Considering
z_{0}=z_{0}-0\sqrt{z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}}
in Theorem
4.1
, for two representations
\phi
and
\psi
,
we can write

\displaystyle f_{\phi}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(z_{0}+\alpha_{i}\sqrt{z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}}\right),

\displaystyle f_{\psi}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(z_{0}+\beta_{j}\sqrt{z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}}\right),

with
\alpha_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,n
being all the eigenvalues of
\phi(h)
and

\beta_{j}
,
j=1,\cdots,m
being all the eigenvalues of
\psi(h)
.
Define
f_{\phi}*f_{\psi}\in\mathbb{C}[\sqrt{z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}},z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}]
by

f_{\phi}*f_{\psi}=\prod_{i.j=1}^{n,m}\left(z_{0}+(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{j})\sqrt{z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}}\right)

(5.1)

and we call
f_{\phi}*f_{\psi}
the
resolution product
of
f_{\phi}
and
f_{\psi}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
f_{\phi}
,
f_{\psi}
be two polynomials in
\mathbf{CP}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})}
as in the Definition
5.1
. It follows that

f_{\phi}*f_{\psi}=f_{\phi\otimes\psi}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a basis of representation
\phi
such that

\phi(h)(v_{i})=\alpha_{i}v_{i},

and

\{w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
is a basis of representation
\psi
such that

\psi(h)(w_{j})=\beta_{j}w_{j}.

Let
\lambda_{v_{i}}=\alpha_{i}
and
\lambda_{w_{j}}=\beta_{j}
, for
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq m
.
It is known that
\{v_{i}\otimes w_{j}\}_{i=1,j=1}^{n,m}
is a basis of
\phi\otimes\psi
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\phi\otimes\psi(h)(v_{i}\otimes w_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(h)\otimes I+I\otimes\psi(h)(v_{i}\otimes w_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(h)(v_{i})\otimes w_{j}+v_{i}\otimes\psi(h)(w_{j})=(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{j})(v_{i}\otimes w_{j}).

Let
\lambda_{v_{i}\otimes w_{j}}=\alpha_{i}+\beta_{j}
, for
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq m
.
By
\lambda_{v_{i}}
,
\lambda_{w_{j}}
and
\lambda_{v_{i}\otimes w_{j}}
, we can rewrite the
f_{\phi}
,
f_{\psi}
and
f_{\phi\otimes\psi}
as the follows,

\displaystyle f_{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(z_{0}+\lambda_{v_{i}}\sqrt{z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}}\right),

\displaystyle f_{\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(z_{0}+\lambda_{w_{j}}\sqrt{z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}}\right),

\displaystyle f_{\phi\otimes\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i.j=1}^{n,m}\left(z_{0}+\lambda_{v_{i}\otimes w_{j}}\sqrt{z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i.j=1}^{n,m}\left(z_{0}+(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{j})\sqrt{z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}}\right)=f_{\phi}*f_{\psi}.

∎

Theorem 5.3
.

The set
\mathbf{CP}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})}
is a commutative monoid under the resolution product with the unit element
z_{0}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.2
, the set
\mathbf{CP}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})}
is closed under the resolution product.
For three representations
\phi
,
\psi
,
\varphi
of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
, we have

\phi\otimes(\psi\otimes\varphi)\simeq(\phi\otimes\psi)\otimes\varphi
. By Proposition
5.2
,
it follows that

f_{\phi}*(f_{\psi}*f_{\varphi})=f_{\phi\otimes(\psi\otimes\varphi)}=f_{(\phi\otimes\psi)\otimes\varphi}=(f_{\phi}*f_{\psi})*f_{\varphi}.

Let
\varphi_{0}
denote one dimensional trivial representation of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
, then
f_{\varphi_{0}}=z_{0}
.
For each representation
\phi
of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
, we have
\phi\simeq\phi\otimes\varphi_{0}\simeq\varphi_{0}\otimes\phi
, so it follows that

f_{\phi}=f_{\phi}*z_{0}=z_{0}*f_{\phi}
. Similarly, the equation
f_{\phi}*f_{\psi}=f_{\psi}*f_{\phi}
holds for
\phi\otimes\psi\simeq\psi\otimes\phi
.
∎

Let
\varphi_{m}
be the irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
of dimension
m+1
with highest weight
m
, for
m\geq 0
. For
0\leq n\leq m
,
it is well known that

\varphi_{m}\otimes\varphi_{n}\simeq\oplus_{k=0}^{n}\varphi_{m-n+2k}.

By Proposition
5.2
, the corollary below holds.

Corollary 5.4
.

For
0\leq n\leq m
, we have

f_{\varphi_{m}}*f_{\varphi_{n}}=\prod_{k=0}^{n}f_{\varphi_{m-n+2k}}.

Remark 5.5
.

Under the tensor product, the category of finitely dimensional representations of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
is a monoidal category, hence it is natural that the set
\mathbf{CP}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})}
should have a monoid structure.
Considering that the
{\rm det}(A\otimes I_{m}-I_{n}\otimes B)
is the resolution of characteristic polynomials of
A
and
B
, where
A
is a
n\times n
matrix, and
B
is a
m\times m
matrix, we name the product resolution product. There are some definitions of
resolution or determinant of multivariable polynomials in algebraic geometry, so there might be some necessary research for their relations from the view of algebraic geometry.

6
The adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
on
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})

Let us recall the canonical basis of
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
first. Suppose that
e_{ij}
is the complex
n\times n
matrix, with entry
1
at the
i
th row and
j
th column and
0
otherwise.
The
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
is the simple Lie algebra of type
{\rm A}_{n-1}
with a basis

h_{i}=e_{ii}-e_{i+1,i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, and
e_{ij}
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
be the Lie algebra homomorphism defined by
\phi(h)=h_{1}
,
\phi(e_{1})=e_{12}
,
\phi(e_{2})=e_{21}
.
Suppose that
\mathrm{ad}\circ\phi
be the composition of
\phi
and the adjoint representation
\mathrm{ad}
of
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
. Then

f_{\mathrm{ad}\circ\phi}(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=z_{0}^{n^{2}-5n+6}(z_{0}^{2}-(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}))^{2n-4}(z_{0}^{2}-4(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}z_{3}))

(6.1)

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
, we need to compute the eigenvalues of
\mathrm{ad}h_{1}
and their multiplicities.

We recall the root system
\Phi
of type
{\rm A}_{n-1}
. The set
\Phi
can be realized in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
\Phi=\{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}\mid 1\leq i\neq j\leq n\}
with
\{\epsilon_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
being the canonical orthonormal basis of

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
\mathrm{ad}h_{1}
, we have

[h_{1},h_{i}]=0,\quad for\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1

[h_{1},e_{ij}]=(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1})e_{ij}\quad for\quad 1\leq i\neq j\leq n,

where
(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1})
is the canonical inner product of
\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}
and
\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}
.
Hence we get the eigenvalues of
\mathrm{ad}h_{1}
are
2
,
-2
,
1
,
-1
and
0
with their multiplicities

1
,
1
,
2n-4
,
2n-4
and
n^{2}-5n+6
, respectively. Therefore, the theorem holds for Theorem
4.1
.
∎

In
[
11
]
, the characteristic polynomial of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
is defined as
Q_{\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})}(z)
, the determinant
of the linear pencil
z_{0}I+z_{1}\mathrm{ad}h_{1}+\cdots+z_{n-1}\mathrm{ad}h_{n-1}+z_{12}\mathrm{ad}e_{12}+\cdots+z_{21}\mathrm{ad}e_{21}+\cdots+z_{n-1,n}\mathrm{ad}e_{n,n-1}
. By the Theorem
6.1
, the following holds.

Corollary 6.2
.

For the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
,Let

f_{i}(z_{0},z_{i},z_{i,i+1},z_{i+1,i})={\rm det}(z_{0}I+z_{i}\mathrm{ad}h_{i}+z_{i,i+1}\mathrm{ad}e_{i,i+1}+z_{i+1,i}\mathrm{ad}e_{i+1,i}).

Then it follows that

f_{i}(z_{0},z_{i},z_{i,i+1},z_{i+1,i})==f_{\mathrm{ad}\circ\phi}(z_{0},z_{i},z_{i,i+1},z_{i+1,i}).

Proof.

We can apply the analogous argument by constructing a Lie algebra isomorphism
\phi_{i}
from
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
to the
subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
generated by
h_{i}
,
e_{i,i+1}
and
e_{i+1,i}
. By
[
11
, Theorem 2.3]
, there is another way to prove this. From
[
11
, Theorem 2.3]
, it is known that the characteristic polynomial
Q_{\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})}(z)
is invariant under the Lie algebra automorphism.
In the Lie algebra, the image of
\phi_{i}
is corresponding to the simple root
\alpha_{i}=\epsilon_{i+1}-\epsilon_{i+1}
as in the proof of Theorem
6.1
, namely,

\phi(\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C}))=\mathbb{C}h_{\alpha_{i}}+\mathbb{C}e_{\alpha_{i}}+\mathbb{C}e_{-\alpha_{i}}
. Under the action Weyl group
W
of type
{\rm A}_{n-1}
associated to
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
, all these roots are on the same orbit of
W
. So it implies that there exists a
\sigma\in W
, such that

\sigma(\alpha_{i})=\alpha_{j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n-1
. It can be interpreted as a Lie algebra automorphism of
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
such that

\sigma(h_{\alpha})=h_{\sigma(\alpha)},\quad\sigma(e_{\alpha})=e_{\sigma(\alpha)}.

Therefore, the conclusion follows just by considering the case in Theorem
6.1
.

Remark 6.3
.

From
[
11
, Theorem 2.3]
and the proof of the Corollary
6.2
, for a complex simple Lie algebra
L
, its characteristic polynomial is invariant under its Weyl group, hence the invariant theory of Weyl groups might be helpful to compute the characteristic polynomial.

∎

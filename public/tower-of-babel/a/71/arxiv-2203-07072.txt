A Representation-Theoretic Approach to 𝒒 ​ 𝒒 \bm{qq} -Characters
Henry LIU

Abstract
We raise the question of whether (a slightly generalized notion of) q ​ q qq -characters can be constructed purely representation-theoretically. In the main example of the quantum toroidal 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 1 \mathfrak{gl}_{1} algebra, geometric engineering of adjoint matter produces an explicit vertex operator 𝖱𝖱 \mathsf{RR} which computes certain q ​ q qq -characters, namely Hirzebruch χ y \chi_{y} -genera, completely analogously to how the R-matrix 𝖱 \mathsf{R} computes q q -characters. We give a geometric proof of the independence of preferred direction for the refined vertex in this and more general non-toric settings.

Introduction

From a high vantage point, one could say this paper studies the
character theory of representations
V
of quantum affine (or
affinized) algebras
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
. In the pioneering work

[
12
]
, from the R-matrix defining
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
,
Frenkel and Reshetikhin constructed the
q
-character

\chi_{\mathrm{q}}(V)
, a
q
-analogue of the ordinary character for
representations of classical Lie algebras. Later Nakajima
[
25
]
provided a geometric construction of
\chi_{\mathrm{q}}(V)

using his quiver varieties. More recently, in
[
30
]
,
Nekrasov used an analogous geometric construction to produce a
one-parameter deformation of
\chi_{\mathrm{q}}(V)
called the
qq
-character

\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}(V)
. Our main goal will be to bring this
collection of ideas full circle back to representation theory, and
attempt to fill in the remaining cell of the following table:

\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}\text{Construction}&\chi_{\mathrm{q}}&\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}\\
\hline\cr\text{Rep. theory}&\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Frenkel1999}{}{}]}&??\\
\text{Geometry}&\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Nakajima2001}{}{}]}&\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Nekrasov2016}{}{}]}\end{array}

To this end, Section
2
reviews the aforementioned
material, proposes a generalized notion of
qq
-character, and poses a
sequence of questions on how
\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}
might be described purely in
terms of representation theory, namely using only the operators in

U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
(as can be done for
q
-characters).
Schematically, we ask if there is an operator
\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}
, analogous
to the R-matrix
\mathsf{R}_{W,V}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}_{W,V}\leadsto\chi_{\mathrm{q}}(V),\qquad\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}\leadsto\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}(V)

(1.1)

are analogous procedures. We anticipate that such a
definition/description of
\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}
will be useful, among other
applications, for studying the enumerative geometry of curves in
3-folds
[
22
]
.

From a much lower, down-to-earth vantage point, this paper actually
mainly studies
geometric engineering

[
15
,
20
]
,
a procedure by which certain K-theoretic Nekrasov partition functions
can be computed using networks of refined topological vertices

C_{\lambda\mu\nu}(q,t)
. This is relevant because

•

a specific Nekrasov partition function
Z_{r}
(for 5d
\mathcal{N}=1^{*}

supersymmetric
\SU(r)
Yang–Mills theory) is the Hirzebruch

\chi_{y}
-genus of the moduli of rank-
r
instantons, which by our
definition is a form of
qq
-character for (the
r
-fold tensor
product of) the Fock module
\mathsf{F}
of the quantum toroidal algebra

U_{q,t}\big(\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.5pt}}\smash{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}}_{1}\big)
;

•

refined vertices have a history of descriptions
[
3
,
16
]
using operators in
U_{q,t}\big(\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.5pt}}\smash{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}}_{1}\big)
.

Specifically, in Section
3
we build from
refined vertices an operator
\mathsf{RR}_{\mathsf{F},\mathsf{F}}
which produces
Z_{r}
in
much the same way that the R-matrix
\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{F},\mathsf{F}}
produces

\chi_{\mathrm{q}}(\mathsf{F}^{\otimes r})
. In this way, we answer a question from
Section
2
for the (important!) example of

U_{q,t}\big(\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.5pt}}\smash{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}}_{1}\big)
. This result can be degenerated to
U_{q}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}\big)
.

The main challenge in finding an operator
\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}
is that

\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}(V)
is morally quadratic in
V
, while
\chi_{\mathrm{q}}(V)
is linear
(see Question
2.6
). For
U_{q,t}\big(\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.5pt}}\smash{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}}_{1}\big)
, the
desired quadratic terms naturally occur in (traces of) the so-called

refined
4
-point function
, see, e.g.,
[
6
]
. This is our

\mathsf{RR}_{\mathsf{F},\mathsf{F}}
. Compositions of these 4-point functions are similar
to but
distinct
from higher-rank Carlsson–Nekrasov–Okounkov
Ext operators
[
9
]
, which lack nice closed-form
formulas despite an excellent representation-theoretic
characterization
[
27
,
29
]
. In contrast, in
Section
3.3
, using a standard operator
formalism, we give an explicit formula for
\mathsf{RR}_{\mathsf{F},\mathsf{F}}
involving
vertex operators with a curious interaction of the horizontal and
vertical subalgebras of
U_{q,t}(\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.5pt}}\smash{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}}_{1})
. Similar results
hold for other toroidal algebras if adjoint matter can be engineered.

Of significant independent interest is
Section
3.4
, where we prove the
independence of preferred direction
, also known as
slicing
invariance
, of the network of refined vertices which computes

Z_{r}
. This is a commonly-assumed property of appropriate networks

[
5
,
17
,
24
]
. Recent work
[
2
]
of Arbesfeld contains a geometric proof if the
network is the toric skeleton of a smooth toric
3
-fold. We use a
degenerating family of abelian varieties to relax the toric
constraint, to allow for suitable non-toric gluings of edges. This
should cover all networks in current literature for which independence
of preferred direction is expected. The strategy in
[
2
]
, and for us as well, is to identify refined
vertices and partition functions as specific limits of equivariant
K-theoretic Pandharipande–Thomas (PT) vertices
[
36
]
and partition functions. We believe this
geometric approach to be cleaner than the recent purely algebraic
proof of
[
13
]
, despite a dependence on the conjectural
K-theoretic DT/PT correspondence.

In accordance with
the analogy (
1.1
), in
Section
3.6
we consider
\mathsf{RR}^{\PT}
, the lift of

\mathsf{RR}
to PT theory, and collect some (conjectural) properties of

\mathsf{RR}
and
\mathsf{RR}^{\PT}
which we propose are analogues of certain
properties of the R-matrix
\mathsf{R}
.

\bm{qq}
-characters

2.1
The geometric definition

2.1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a quiver with vertices indexed by a set
I
.
Associated to a doubled and framed version of
\Gamma
is the Nakajima
quiver variety

X_{\Gamma}(\bm{w})=\bigsqcup_{\bm{v}}X_{\Gamma}(\bm{v},\bm{w}),

where
\bm{v}
and
\bm{w}
are dimension vectors of the ordinary and
framing vertices respectively in the quiver representation. Recall
that
X_{\Gamma}
is a smooth algebraic symplectic variety. Let

\mathsf{T}=\mathsf{A}\times\mathbb{C}_{\hbar}^{\times}\subset\Aut(X_{\Gamma})
be a
(possibly maximal) torus such that
\mathbb{C}_{\hbar}^{\times}
scales the
symplectic form with weight
\hbar
and
\mathsf{A}
preserves the symplectic
form.

The important work
[
25
]
, and later
[
23
,
35
]
, showed that the equivariant K-theory group (of
coherent algebraic sheaves)

V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w})\coloneqq K_{\mathsf{T}}(X_{\Gamma}(\bm{w}))

(2.1)

is a highest-weight module for a quantum group
\mathscr{A}_{\Gamma}
which is
essentially a quantum affinized algebra. For example, when
\Gamma
is
of finite ADE type,
\mathscr{A}_{\Gamma}=U_{q}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\Gamma}\big)
is the
quantum affine algebra for (a mild central extension of) the classical
Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\Gamma}
. More relevant for us, if
\Gamma
is the
Jordan quiver, with one vertex and one edge loop, then

\mathscr{A}_{\Gamma}=U_{q,t}\big(\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.5pt}}\smash{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}}_{1}\big)
is the quantum toroidal
\mathfrak{gl}_{1}
algebra, which is morally (but not literally) the quantum
affinization of
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{1}
.

The geometric realization
(
2.1
) is useful for studying
the representation theory of quantum groups, e.g., if
\Gamma
of finite ADE
type then modules of the form
V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w})
form a basis in the
Grothendieck ring of all finite-dimensional
\mathscr{A}_{\Gamma}
-modules.

2.1.2
.

Associated to each module
V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w})
is the
qq
-character

\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}(V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w}))
, originally introduced in
[
30
]
to
study the BPS/CFT correspondence.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Taut
denote the tautological bundle of
X_{\Gamma}
, and let
f(-)

be a function on
K_{\mathsf{T}}(X_{\Gamma})
such that
f(\mathcal{E}_{1}+\mathcal{E}_{2})=f(\mathcal{E}_{1})f(\mathcal{E}_{2})
. Set

\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}^{(f)}(V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w});m,Q)\coloneqq\sum_{\bm{v}}Q^{\bm{v}}\chi_{\mathsf{T}}\big(X_{\Gamma}(\bm{v},\bm{w}),\wedge_{-m}^{\bullet}\big(\mathcal{T}^{\vee}\big)\otimes f(\Taut)\big).

(2.2)

Here
m
and
Q=(Q_{i})_{i\in I}
are formal variables, with

Q^{\bm{v}}
being short for
\prod_{i\in I}Q_{i}^{v_{i}}
, and

\wedge_{-m}^{\bullet}(-)\coloneqq\sum_{i}(-m)^{i}\wedge^{i}(-)
is an
alternating sum of exterior powers,
\mathcal{T}
is the tangent bundle, and

\chi_{\mathsf{T}}(X,\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\sum(-1)^{i}H^{i}(X,\mathcal{F})\in K_{\mathsf{T}}(\mathrm{pt})_{\mathrm{loc}}

is the
\mathsf{T}
-equivariant Euler characteristic of a coherent sheaf

\mathcal{F}
. (When
X
is non-compact but the fixed locus
X^{\mathsf{T}}
is,

\chi_{\mathsf{T}}
is defined via
\mathsf{T}
-equivariant localization, hence the
subscript
\mathrm{loc}
.)

2.1.3
.

Note that (
2.2
) is
not
the original
(combinatorial!) characterization of
\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}
from
[
30
, Section 6.1]
, and instead we have used the geometric formula
from
[
30
, Sections 8.3 and 8.4]
. The geometric formula arises
from integration over a certain
moduli of crossed instantons
,
see
[
31
]
for an ADHM-style construction.
Algebro-geometrically, this moduli space admits a description and
virtual cycle in the style of Oh–Thomas
[
33
]
.

2.1.4
.

We have allowed for an

multiplicative function
f

(

), while the original

use a specific function
f_{\psi}
(namely the product of all

from (

)). We feel that

qq
-characters with more general

should be studied on equal

[

between
\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}^{(f)}
and quantities in the enumerative geometry of

3

f
corresponds exactly to a
descendent
insertion
.

An example, which may be of independent interest, is when
f(-)=\mathcal{O}

is the trivial constant function, which we denote
f=1
. In this

) is nothing but the
(equivariant) Hirzebruch
\chi_{y}

\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}^{(1)}(V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w});m,Q)=\chi_{\mathsf{T},m}(X_{\Gamma}(\bm{w});Q)\coloneqq\sum_{\bm{v}}Q^{\bm{v}}\chi_{\mathsf{T}}\big(X_{\Gamma}(\bm{v},\bm{w}),\wedge_{-m}^{\bullet}\big(\mathcal{T}^{\vee}\big)\big),

m

2.2
The question(s)

2.2.1
.

We will now pose a sequence of successively more precise questions
about the representation-theoretic nature of
qq
-characters. Let

V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w})
be a geometric representation of
\mathscr{A}_{\Gamma}
as
in Section
2.1.1
.

Question 2.2
.

Can the quantity
\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}^{(f)}(V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w});m,Q)
be expressed
purely in terms of operators in
\mathscr{A}_{\Gamma}
acting on
V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w})
?

This question is motivated by the following observation. The
specialization
m=1
for
qq
-characters gives

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}^{(f)}(V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w});1,Q)

\displaystyle=\chi_{\mathsf{T}}\left(X_{\Gamma}(\bm{w}),Q^{\cdots}\cdot\wedge_{-1}^{\bullet}\left(\mathcal{T}^{\vee}\right)\otimes f(\Taut)\right)

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\chi_{\mathsf{T}}\left(X_{\Gamma}(\bm{w})\times X_{\Gamma}(\bm{w}),Q^{\cdots}\cdot\iota_{\Delta}(f(\Taut))\right),

(2.4)

where in (
2.3
) we abbreviated
\sum_{\bm{v}}Q^{\bm{v}}
as
Q^{\cdots}
, and in
(
2.4
)
\iota_{\Delta}
is the inclusion of the
diagonal. Hence (
2.4
) is the trace, in

V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w})
, of the operator of multiplication by
f(\Taut)
. It is
known that such operators always live in a commutative subalgebra of

\mathscr{A}_{\Gamma}\subset\End(V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w}))
; see
[
23
, Section 5.4]

(written for cohomology/Yangians, but the general principle still
applies).

2.2.2
.

For a specific choice of
f
, the
m=1
specialization is exactly a
q
-character.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be of finite ADE type, and let
\big\{\psi_{i}^{\pm}(u)\big\}_{i\in I}

be the Drinfeld generators of the loop Cartan in

\mathscr{A}_{\Gamma}=U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\Gamma})
. Let
\Taut=\bigoplus_{i}\Taut_{i}

be the decomposition across vertices of
\Gamma
, and consider

f_{\psi,i}(\Taut)\coloneqq\widehat{S}^{\bullet}\big(\big(1-\hbar^{-1}\big)\otimes\Taut_{i}\big),

(2.5)

where
\widehat{S}^{\bullet}(V)\coloneqq\Sym^{\bullet}(V)\otimes(\det V)^{1/2}
is a “symmetrized” version of the symmetric algebra.
Then

\psi_{i}^{\pm}(u)=f_{\psi,i}(u\otimes\Taut),

(2.6)

as operators expanded in series around
u^{\pm 1}\to 0
, see, e.g.,

[
25
, Theorem 9.4.1]
. Therefore

\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}^{(f_{\psi,i})}(V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w});1,Q)
equals

\chi_{q}^{(i)}(V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w});Q)\coloneqq\tr_{V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w})}Q^{\cdots}\psi_{i}^{\pm}(u),

(2.7)

also known as the (
i
-th)
q
-character
of
V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w})
. Up
to some syntactic repackaging, (
2.7
) is essentially
the
q
-character originally introduced in
[
12
]
for
quantum affine algebras. This explains the nomenclature
“
qq
-character”.

2.2.3
.

Whenever the canonical bundle
\mathcal{K}_{X}
admits a square root, there is a
natural pairing on
K_{\mathsf{T}}(X)
given by

\langle\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\rangle_{X}\coloneqq\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\Ext^{i}_{\mathsf{T}}\big(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{X}^{1/2}\big),

(2.8)

and

\langle\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}\rangle_{X}=(-1)^{\dim X}\langle\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{1}\rangle_{X}^{\vee}

if Serre duality applies. Since our
X
will always be smooth and
symplectic, (
2.8
) becomes a Hermitian
form. We therefore use bra-ket notation for elements of
K_{\mathsf{T}}(X)
,
along with the shorthand

\langle v|A|w\rangle^{\prime}\coloneqq\frac{\langle v|A|w\rangle}{\langle v|w\rangle}.

2.2.4
.

For our purposes, it is useful to repackage (
2.7
) as
follows. Let
\bm{\delta}_{i}=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)
be the
dimension vector with
1
in the
i
-th position only, and consider
the highest-weight modules
V_{i}\coloneqq V_{\Gamma}(\bm{\delta}_{i})
. Let

\ket{\varnothing}
denote the highest weight vector (up to scalars).

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be of finite ADE type. Let
V
be a finite-dimensional

\mathscr{A}_{\Gamma}
-module and
\mathsf{R}_{V_{i},V}\in\End(V_{i}\otimes V)
be the
R-matrix. Then

\psi_{i}^{\pm}(u)=\langle\varnothing|\mathsf{R}_{V_{i},V}|\varnothing\rangle_{1}^{\prime}\in\End(V),

(2.9)

where
\langle-\rangle_{1}
means to take the matrix element in the first
tensor factor
V_{i}
.

In fact, in general the entire Hopf algebra
\mathscr{A}_{\Gamma}
, not just
its elements
\psi_{i}^{\pm}(u)
, is constructed from the data of
R-matrices
\{\mathsf{R}_{V,V^{\prime}}\}
where
V
,
V^{\prime}
range over the modules (
2.1
) (
[
37
]
,

[
35
, Section 3]
or
[
23
, Section 5]
for the
cohomological case). When
\Gamma
has loops, in general

\psi_{i}^{\pm}(u)
is a product of the matrix elements in the r.h.s. of (
2.9
).

Recall
\mathsf{R}=\Stab_{-}^{-1}\circ\Stab_{+}
where
\Stab_{\pm}
are
upper-/lower-triangular stable envelopes. So only the diagonal
terms, normalized exactly to give (
2.6
),
contribute to
\langle\varnothing|\mathsf{R}|\varnothing\rangle
; see the
factorization of
\mathsf{R}
in
[
35
, Section 2.3]
or the
cohomological argument in
[
23
, Section 4.7]
.
∎

Therefore

\chi_{\mathrm{q}}^{(i)}(V;Q)=\tr_{V}Q^{\cdots}\langle\varnothing|\mathsf{R}_{V_{i},V}|\varnothing\rangle_{1}^{\prime}.

2.2.5
.

In complete analogy with (
2.10
), a natural refinement
of Question
2.2
is the following.

Question 2.5
.

Does there exist a highest-weight module
W
and an operator

\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}(m)
such that, for appropriate
f
,

\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}^{(f)}(V;m,Q)=\tr_{V}Q^{\cdots}\frac{\langle\varnothing|\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}(m)|\varnothing\rangle_{1}^{\prime}}{\langle\varnothing\otimes\varnothing|\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}(m)|\varnothing\otimes\varnothing\rangle^{\prime}},

(2.11)

with
\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}(1)=\mathsf{R}_{W,V}
?

With our normalization conventions,

\langle\varnothing\otimes\varnothing|\mathsf{R}_{W,V}|\varnothing\otimes\varnothing\rangle^{\prime}=1

and therefore this factor was not present in
(
2.10
). For brevity, let

\langle\varnothing|\mathsf{RR}|\varnothing\rangle_{1}^{\prime\prime}

denote the normalized operator in the r.h.s. of
(
2.11
), so

\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}^{(f)}=\tr Q^{\cdots}\langle\varnothing|\mathsf{RR}|\varnothing\rangle_{1}^{\prime\prime}
.

2.2.6
.

We take a very specific and somewhat naive approach to answering
Question
2.5
for the modules
V=V_{\Gamma}(\bm{w})
. Let

X=X_{\Gamma}(\bm{w})
, and let
\{\ket{\mathcal{O}_{p}}\}_{p\in X^{\mathsf{T}}}
be the
basis of structure sheaves of fixed points. The operator of
multiplication by
\Taut
acts diagonally in this basis.

Question 2.6
.

Does there exist a highest-weight module
W
and an operator

\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}(m)
such that

\frac{\wedge_{-m}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{T}_{p}^{\vee})}{\wedge_{-1}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{T}_{p}^{\vee})}=\frac{\left\langle\varnothing\otimes\mathcal{O}_{p}|\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}(m)|\varnothing\otimes\mathcal{O}_{p}\right\rangle^{\prime}}{\left\langle\varnothing\otimes\varnothing|\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}(m)|\varnothing\otimes\varnothing\right\rangle^{\prime}}

for every
p\in X^{\mathsf{T}}
?

Such an operator would essentially answer Question
2.5
since, by

\mathsf{T}
-equivariant localization,

\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}^{(f)}(V;m,Q)=\tr_{V}Q^{\cdots}f(\Taut)\left\langle\varnothing|\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}(m)|\varnothing\right\rangle_{1}^{\prime\prime}.

(2.12)

2.2.7
.

In Section
3
, we answer the following variant
of Question
2.6
in the affirmative for the Jordan quiver
\Gamma
,
for which the Nakajima quiver variety
X_{\Gamma}(r)=\mathcal{M}_{r}
is the moduli
of rank-
r
instantons.

Question 2.7
.

Does there exist a highest-weight module
W
and an operator

\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}(m)
such that

\frac{\wedge_{-m}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{T}_{p}^{\vee})}{\wedge_{-1}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{T}_{p}^{\vee})}=\frac{\left\langle\varnothing\otimes\mathcal{F}_{p}|\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}(m)|\varnothing\otimes\mathcal{F}_{p}\right\rangle^{\prime}}{\left\langle\varnothing\otimes\varnothing|\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}(m)|\varnothing\otimes\varnothing\right\rangle^{\prime}}

for every
p\in X^{\mathsf{T}}
, for some basis

\{\ket{\mathcal{F}_{p}}\}_{p\in X^{\mathsf{T}}}
with nice properties?

One reason to look beyond the basis
\{\ket{\mathcal{O}_{p}}\}
of fixed points
is that fixed points do not behave nicely with respect to tensor
product; see Section
3.1.4
for details in the
case of
\mathcal{M}_{r}
. For example, any operator
\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}
satisfying the
original Question
2.6
has little hope of satisfying the fusion
property of the original R-matrices
\mathsf{R}_{W,V}
(see
Section
3.6.2
), but our operator
\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}
will
satisfy Question
2.7
for a basis
\{\ket{\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}_{p}}\}

preserving this fusion property.

2.2.8
.

The discrepancy between Questions
2.6
and
2.7
is exactly
the obstruction to incorporating a non-trivial insertion
f
. Namely,
we answer Question
2.7
in a basis
\{\ket{\mathcal{O}_{p}^{\otimes}}\}

where
\Taut
does
not
act diagonally (see
Section
3.1.5
), and therefore the
resulting
\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}(m)
no longer answers Questions
2.5
or

2.6
; only the specific

\chi_{\mathrm{qq}}^{(1)}(V;m,Q)=\tr_{V}Q^{\cdots}\left\langle\varnothing|\mathsf{RR}_{W,V}(m)|\varnothing\right\rangle_{1}^{\prime\prime}

of (
2.12
) continues to hold. To
incorporate a general
f
, one could try to rewrite
\mathsf{RR}(m)
in the
true fixed-point basis
\{\ket{\mathcal{O}_{p}}\}
. Put differently, if
\mathsf{S}
is
the change of basis from
\{\ket{\mathcal{O}_{p}}\}
to

\{\ket{\mathcal{O}_{p}^{\otimes}}\}
, then
\mathsf{S}^{-1}\cdot\mathsf{RR}\cdot\mathsf{S}

satisfies Question
2.6
if the operator
\mathsf{RR}
satisfies
Question
2.7
. However, it is unclear whether there is a
representation-theoretic interpretation or formula for
\mathsf{S}
.

2.2.9
.

To be clear, the conditions imposed by Questions
2.6
and

2.7
are on the
spectrum
of the operator
\mathsf{RR}
, while
the condition of Question
2.5
is merely on the trace of
\mathsf{RR}
.
One therefore expects the former to be far more stringent than the

in Section

, where we find many different

such that

is the Hirzebruch

3
Geometric engineering

3.1
The setup

3.1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be the Jordan quiver, with one vertex and
one edge loop. The Nakajima quiver variety
X_{\Gamma}(r)
is the moduli

\mathcal{M}_{r}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}_{r}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\coloneqq\big\{E\in\mathsf{Coh}(\mathbb{P}^{2})\text{ torsion-free}\colon E\big|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\infty}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\infty}}^{\oplus r}\big\}

of rank-
r
instantons on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. It admits natural actions induced
by
\GL_{2}
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, and by
\GL_{r}
acting on the framing

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\infty}}^{\oplus r}
. Let

\mathsf{T}\coloneqq\mathsf{T}_{\text{framing}}\times\mathsf{T}_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\coloneqq(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}\ni(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},q,t)

be the maximal torus of this
\GL_{r}\times\GL_{2}
, with coordinates
written as above. The
r=1
case is the Hilbert scheme
\mathcal{M}_{1}=\Hilb
of points on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

3.1.2
.

Let

\mathbbm{k}\coloneqq K_{\mathsf{T}}(\mathrm{pt})_{\mathrm{loc}}=\mathbb{Z}\big[a_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,a_{r}^{\pm},q^{\pm},t^{\pm}\big]_{\mathrm{loc}}

where
\mathrm{loc}
means to adjoin
(1-w)^{-1}
for all non-zero monomials

w\in K_{\mathsf{T}}(\mathrm{pt})
. All our modules and computations are implicitly
over this base ring. Set

\mathsf{F}\coloneqq K_{\mathsf{T}}(\Hilb).

The quantum group associated to
\Gamma
is the quantum toroidal

\mathfrak{gl}_{1}
algebra
\mathscr{A}_{\Gamma}=U_{q,t}\big(\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.5pt}}\smash{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}}_{1}\big)
, and
\mathsf{F}

is its standard Fock module. See
Section
3.3.1
for some more details. By
general principles
[
23
]
, stable envelopes provide an
isomorphism of
\mathscr{A}_{\Gamma}
-modules

\Stab\colon\ \mathsf{F}^{\otimes r}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\xrightarrow{\sim}K_{\mathsf{T}}(\mathcal{M}_{r})\otimes\mathbbm{k}.

(3.1)

Stable envelopes can be viewed as “corrected” versions of (the
pushforward along) the inclusion

\iota\colon\ (\mathcal{M}_{r})^{\mathsf{T}_{\text{framing}}}=\Hilb^{\times r}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{r}

of the
\mathsf{T}_{\text{framing}}
-fixed locus, which itself only induces
an isomorphism of
\mathbbm{k}
-modules.

3.1.3
.

The
\mathsf{T}_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}
-fixed points of
\Hilb
, and therefore basis
elements of
\mathsf{F}
, are labeled by partitions
\lambda
. The

\mathsf{T}
-fixed points of
\mathcal{M}_{r}
are therefore
r
-tuples
\bm{\lambda}=\big(\lambda^{(1)},\ldots,\lambda^{(r)}\big)
of partitions. So here we fix
some notation for partitions.

We will use the letters
\lambda
,
\mu
,
\nu
to denote
partitions. Occasionally it is useful to view a partition

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots)
in terms of its Young
diagram. Let
(i,j)=(i(\square),j(\square))
be the position of a square
\square\in\lambda
in the Young diagram. If
\lambda^{t}
is
the conjugate partition, set

a(\square)\coloneqq\lambda_{i(\square)}-j(\square),\qquad\ell(\square)\coloneqq\big(\lambda^{t}\big)_{j(\square)}-i(\square).

Let
|\lambda|\coloneqq\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}
denote the size of
\lambda
.

3.1.4
.

We identify
\mathsf{F}
with the algebra of symmetric functions. From
(
2.8
), there is a standard inner
product
\langle-,-\rangle
on
\mathsf{F}
. We will use two bases in
\mathsf{F}
:

•

the basis of Schur polynomials
s_{\lambda}
, which are
orthonormal;

•

the basis of fixed points
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
, which are orthogonal
with norm

\langle\mathcal{O}_{\lambda},\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\rangle=(qt)^{\frac{|\lambda|}{2}}\prod_{\square\in\lambda}\big(1-q^{-\ell(\square)-1}t^{a(\square)}\big)\big(1-q^{\ell(\square)}t^{-a(\square)-1}\big).

This can also be taken as the definition of
\langle-,-\rangle
if
desired. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}
be the unit vector normalization of

\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
, so they form an ortho
normal
basis.

The elements
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\in\mathsf{F}
are Haiman’s normalization of
Macdonald polynomials, e.g., denoted
\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}
in
[
14
]
.

3.1.5
.

We identify
\mathsf{F}^{\otimes r}
with
K_{\mathsf{T}}(\mathcal{M}_{r})
using
(
3.1
). The tensor product

\mathsf{F}^{\otimes r}
inherits the inner product from
\mathsf{F}
; note that
this is
not
the inner product on
K_{\mathsf{T}}(\mathcal{M}_{r})
from
(
2.8
). We consider two bases in

\mathsf{F}^{\otimes r}
:

•

the basis of generalized Schur polynomials

s_{\bm{\lambda}}\coloneqq\otimes_{i=1}^{r}s_{\lambda^{(i)}}
, which
are orthonormal;

•

the basis of fixed points
\mathcal{O}_{\bm{\lambda}}
, also known as

generalized
Macdonald polynomials (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
,
cf.
[
39
]
), which are not orthogonal.

The latter is not the same as

\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}_{\bm{\lambda}}\coloneqq\otimes_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda^{(i)}}\in\mathsf{F}^{\otimes r},

which do form an orthonormal basis. This is because although

\mathcal{O}_{\bm{\lambda}}=\iota_{*}\boxtimes_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda^{(i)}}
as
sheaves on
\mathcal{M}_{r}
, the identification
(
3.1
) is not
\iota_{*}
. A crucial
distinguishing property is that, in general,

\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda^{(1)},\lambda^{(2)})}\neq\mathcal{O}_{\lambda^{(1)}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\lambda^{(2)}}.

3.2
The
\bm{\chi_{y}}
-genus

3.2.1
.

Our primary goal is to study the
\chi_{y}
-genus

\chi_{\mathsf{T},m}(\mathcal{M}_{r};Q)
, and various representation-theoretic
description of it in terms of operators in the algebra

U_{q,t}\big(\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.5pt}}\smash{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}}_{1}\big)
. The strategy is to compute the

\chi_{y}
-genus via a form of
geometric engineering
, which
equates it to the refined partition functions of certain special toric

3
-folds. One consequence, among others, of our computations is an
affirmative answer (Theorem
3.3
) to
Question
2.7
.

3.2.2
.

Refined partition functions in the toric setting are (sums of)
products of contributions
C_{\lambda\mu\nu}(q,t)
, called
refined vertices
, from each toric chart. One labels each edge of
the toric
1
-skeleton with a partition and performs certain
combinatorial sums over them; see
[
17
]
for details. For
example,

\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{images/refined-vertex.pdf}}}=C_{\lambda\mu\nu}(q,t),\qquad\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{images/refined-vertex-glued-2.pdf}}}=\sum_{\lambda}Q^{|\lambda|}C_{\lambda\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(q,t)C_{\lambda^{t}\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(t,q).

Here a marked half-edge

labels
the “preferred direction”
\nu
of the vertex
C_{\lambda\mu\nu}
,
which is necessary because it is not symmetric in
\lambda
,
\mu
,
\nu
.
(This asymmetry is evident in the explicit formula (
3.9
) later.) An unlabeled half-edge is set to

\varnothing
, and any other edge not explicitly labeled by a partition
is summed over. Each edge may be labeled with a so-called
Kähler variable
, e.g.,
Q
, indicating a term
Q^{|\cdot|}

recording the size of the partition on the edge. The result is a function of
q
,
t
, and various Kähler variables.

Remark 3.1
.

For the experts, all our edges will have normal bundles

\mathcal{O}(-1)^{\oplus 2}
, i.e., everything is locally a conifold, so we
neglect framing factors when gluing refined vertices.

3.2.3
.

Different choices of toric diagram engineer different quantities on

\mathcal{M}_{r}
, or more generally
\mathcal{M}_{r_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{M}_{r_{k}}
,
and a general recipe is given in
[
20
]
. However, it is
important that the diagram does
not
need to be the
1
-skeleton
of an actual toric
3
-fold for geometric engineering to work. In
particular, the
\chi_{y}
-genus involves gluing edges in the following
non-toric way.

Proposition 3.2
.

With the substitution
t\mapsto t^{-1}
,

\chi_{\mathsf{T},\kappa}(\mathcal{M}_{r};Q)=\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{images/adjoint-matter-diagram.pdf}}}\bigg/\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{images/adjoint-matter-perturbative-diagram.pdf}}}

(3.2)

where the half-edges with the same variable
Q_{i}
are glued
together, i.e., there is an additional overall sum
\sum_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}}Q_{1}^{|\lambda_{1}|}\cdots Q_{r}^{|\lambda_{r}|}
,
and there are identifications

Q_{1}=\cdots=Q_{r}=Q,\qquad A_{1}=\cdots=A_{r}=\kappa,\qquad A_{k}B_{k}=a_{k+1}/a_{k}.

(3.3)

Proof.

By explicit calculation using the formalism in
[
15
]
(or otherwise).
∎

In physics language, Proposition

gives the “toric” diagram for engineering adjoint
U(r)
matter. The
denominator is referred to as the
perturbative
term, and has an
explicit closed form formula unimportant to us.

3.2.4
.

Let
Z_{r}\big(q,t^{-1};Q,A,\bm{B}\big)
be the quantity encoded by the
numerator of (
3.2
); since all the
Q_{i}

are specialized to be equal, we retain only a single variable denoted

, and similarly for the

and

Let

be the partitions labeling the horizontal legs of (

Z_{r}(q,t;Q,A,\bm{B})\eqqcolon\sum_{\bm{\lambda}}Q^{|\bm{\lambda}|}Z_{r}(q,t;A,\bm{B})_{\bm{\lambda}}.

For example, the denominator of (

Z_{r}(\cdots)_{\bm{\varnothing}}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

There is an operator
\mathsf{RR}\in\End(\mathsf{F}\otimes\mathsf{F})[[Q,A]]
, with
explicit formula given by (

\displaystyle\big({-}\sqrt{qt}\big)^{|\bm{\lambda}|}Z_{r}\big(q,t^{-1};A,A\bm{B}\big)_{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\big\langle\varnothing\otimes\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}_{\bm{\lambda}}\big|\mathsf{RR}^{(10)}B_{1}^{|\cdot|}\mathsf{RR}^{(20)}B_{2}^{|\cdot|}\cdots B_{r-1}^{|\cdot|}\mathsf{RR}^{(r0)}\big|\varnothing\otimes\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}_{\bm{\lambda}}\big\rangle,

(3.4)

where
\mathsf{RR}^{(ij)}

j

factors and the

3.2.5
.

In fact, the proof of
Proposition

proceeds by identifying

\frac{\wedge^{\bullet}_{-\kappa}\big(\mathcal{T}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\vee}\big)}{\wedge^{\bullet}_{-1}\big(\mathcal{T}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\vee}\big)}=\frac{Z_{r}(\cdots)_{\bm{\lambda}}}{Z_{r}(\cdots)_{\bm{\varnothing}}}

up to the identifications (

Hence Theorem

resolves

in the affirmative for
\mathcal{M}_{r}

is closely related to but is
not
exactly the higher-rank

[

which no explicit vertex operator formula is known. The diagonal

[

3.2.6
.

Taking the trace of (
3.4
)
gives

Z_{r}\big(q,t^{-1};-Q\sqrt{qt},A,A\bm{B}\big)=\tr_{\mathsf{F}^{\otimes r}}Q^{|\cdot|}\big\langle\varnothing\big|\mathsf{RR}^{(10)}B_{1}^{|\cdot|}\mathsf{RR}^{(20)}B_{2}^{|\cdot|}\cdots B_{r-1}^{|\cdot|}\mathsf{RR}^{(r0)}\big|\varnothing\big\rangle_{0},

(3.5)

where
\left\langle-,-\right\rangle_{0}
means the matrix element is taken in the

0
-th tensor factor. We will construct different operators
\mathsf{RR}^{H}

and
\mathsf{RR}^{V}
which both satisfy (

up to mild changes of variables (nb. the discussion of

). The key idea
(Theorem
3.13
)
is that the diagrams in (

) remain the
same under
any
choice of preferred direction for the refined
vertices. In particular,
\mathsf{RR}^{H}
(resp.
\mathsf{RR}^{V}
) arises from
horizontal (resp. vertical) preferred direction. The effects, not
always trivial, of changing the preferred direction can be
investigated quite generally for toric diagrams possibly with some
pairs of parallel half-edges glued together (in a non-toric way), and
so Section
3.4
is of independent interest.

3.3
An explicit operator formula

3.3.1
.

The algebra
U_{q,t}\big(\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.5pt}}\smash{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}}_{1}\big)
is complicated; see

[
38
]
for various presentations. For us, it suffices
to know that it contains two special subalgebras:

•

the “horizontal” Heisenberg subalgebra, with generators

\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
which in terms of power-sum polynomials

p_{k}\in\mathsf{F}
act as

\alpha_{n}=n\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{n}},\qquad\alpha_{-n}=p_{n},\qquad\forall n>0;

•

the “vertical” commutative subalgebra, with generators

\{H_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
which act on fixed points as
H_{n}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}=h_{n}(\lambda;q,t)\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
with eigenvalue

h_{n}(\lambda;q,t)\coloneqq\sign(n)\left(-\chi_{\lambda}(q^{n},t^{n})+\frac{1}{(1-q^{n})(1-t^{n})}\right)

(3.6)

where

\chi_{\lambda}(q,t)\coloneqq\sum_{\square\in\lambda}q^{i(\square)}t^{j(\square)}
.
One recognizes this as the weight of the tautological bundle of

\Hilb
at the fixed point
\lambda
.

Experts will notice that the action of
\alpha_{-n}
for
n>0
is
scaled by a factor
-\big(q^{n/2}-q^{-n/2}\big)\big(t^{n/2}-t^{-n/2}\big)
from the
usual horizontal generators.

3.3.2
.

Let

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}(z)\coloneqq\exp\left(\sum_{n>0}(qt)^{\frac{n}{2}}\big(t^{\frac{n}{2}}-t^{-\frac{n}{2}}\big)(H_{n}\otimes\alpha_{n})\frac{z^{n}}{n}\right),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(z)\coloneqq\exp\left(-\sum_{n>0}(qt)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\big(q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}}\big)(H_{-n}\otimes\alpha_{-n})\frac{z^{-n}}{n}\right).

(3.7)

Furthermore let
\mathsf{D}
be the diagonal operator whose entries are

\big\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}|\mathsf{D}|\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}\big\rangle\coloneqq\langle\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}|\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\rangle^{-1}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

\mathsf{RR}=\mathsf{RR}^{H}\coloneqq\mathsf{D}^{(1)}\Gamma_{-}\big(\sqrt{qt}\big)^{-1}\Gamma_{+}(1)^{-1}\Gamma_{-}(-A)\Gamma_{+}\big({-}1/A\sqrt{qt}\big),

(3.8)

where a superscript
(-)^{(1)}
means to act on the first tensor
factor.

3.3.3
.

Explicit formulas like (
3.8
),
particularly the “vertex operators”
\Gamma_{\pm}(z)
, have some
precursors in the literature under the name of
Awata–Feigin–Shiraishi
or
Ding–Iohara–Miki intertwiners

[
3
]
. In some sense, the main novelty in our computation
is the introduction of the auxiliary factor of
\mathsf{F}
where the
operators
H_{\pm n}
(in (
3.7
)) from the
vertical subalgebra of
U_{q,t}\big(\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.5pt}}\smash{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}}_{1}\big)
act. The
operators
\Gamma_{\pm}(z)
involve a curious interaction of the
vertical and horizontal subalgebras, in contrast to objects living
only in one slope subalgebra

U_{q,t}\big(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{1}\big)\subset U_{q,t}\big(\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.5pt}}\smash{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}}_{1}\big)
, e.g.,
the vertex operators in the R-matrix
[
26
]
. Alternatively,
one can view them as single-slope objects evaluated on

\mathsf{F}^{V}\otimes\mathsf{F}
instead of
\mathsf{F}\otimes\mathsf{F}
, where

\Phi\colon U_{q,t}\big(\widehat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.5pt}}\smash{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}}_{1}\big)\to\End\big(\mathsf{F}^{V}\big)
is the
“vertical” Fock representation
[
29
]
.

3.3.4
.

We now proceed with the proof of
Theorem
3.4
.
The refined vertex with preferred direction
\nu
is

\displaystyle C_{\lambda\mu\nu}(q,t)\coloneqq\left(\frac{t}{q}\right)^{\frac{\|\mu\|^{2}+\|\nu\|^{2}}{2}}\!\!q^{\frac{\kappa(\mu)}{2}}P_{\nu^{t}}(q,t)\sum_{\eta}\left(\frac{t}{q}\right)^{\frac{|\eta|+|\lambda|-|\mu|}{2}}\!\!s_{\lambda^{t}/\eta}\big(q^{-\rho}t^{-\nu}\big)s_{\mu/\eta}\big(t^{-\rho}q^{-\nu^{t}}\big),\!\!\!

(3.9)

where the
s_{\lambda/\mu}(\bm{x})
are skew Schur functions,

q^{-\rho}t^{-\nu}
means

\big(q^{1/2}t^{-\nu_{1}},q^{3/2}t^{-\nu_{2}},q^{5/2}t^{-\nu_{3}},\ldots\big)
,

\|\mu\|^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{i}\mu_{i}^{2}
and

\kappa(\mu)\coloneqq\|\mu\|^{2}-\|\mu^{t}\|^{2}
, and

P_{\nu^{t}}(q,t)\coloneqq q^{\frac{\|\nu\|^{2}}{2}}\prod_{\square\in\nu}\frac{1}{1-q^{\ell(\square)+1}t^{a(\square)}}.

Setting
\nu^{(0)}\coloneqq\nu^{(r)}\coloneqq\varnothing
, the
desired quantity is

Z_{r}\big(q,t^{-1};Q,A,\bm{B}\big)=\sum_{\nu^{(1)},\ldots,\nu^{(r-1)}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}B_{i}^{|\nu^{(i)}|}Z_{\nu^{(i-1)},\nu^{(i)t}}^{H}\big(q,t^{-1};Q,A\big),

(3.10)

where
Z_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}^{H}
is the so-called
four-point diagram

\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{images/four-point-diagram-horizontal.pdf}}}\eqqcolon{}

\displaystyle Z_{\nu^{(1)},\nu^{(2)t}}^{H}\big(q,t^{-1};Q,A\big)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda,\mu}A^{|\mu|}Q^{|\lambda|}C_{\mu\nu^{(1)}\lambda}\big(q,t^{-1}\big)C_{\mu^{t}\nu^{(2)t}\lambda^{t}}\big(t^{-1},q\big)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda,\mu,\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}Q^{|\lambda|}A^{|\nu^{(2)}|}(qt)^{\frac{\|\lambda^{t}\|^{2}-\|\lambda\|^{2}}{2}}P_{\lambda}\big(t^{-1},q\big)P_{\lambda^{t}}\big(q,t^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\ {}\times s_{\mu^{t}/\eta_{1}}\big(q^{-\rho}t^{\lambda}\big)s_{\nu^{(1)}/\eta_{1}}\big((qt)^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{-\lambda^{t}}t^{\rho}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\ {}\times s_{\mu/\eta_{2}}\big(Aq^{-\lambda^{t}}t^{\rho}\big)s_{\nu^{(2)t}/\eta_{2}}\big(A^{-1}(qt)^{-\frac{1}{2}}q^{-\rho}t^{\lambda}\big).

(3.11)

The superscript

reminds us that the horizontal direction is
preferred. In the second equality above, we used the homogeneity

to absorb some factors of
A
and
\sqrt{qt}
.

3.3.5

The following key tool converts a skew Schur function into a matrix

\mathsf{F}

Lemma 3.5

(
[

)

s_{\lambda/\mu}(\bm{x})=\left\langle s_{\mu}\bigg|\exp\left(\sum_{n>0}p_{n}(\bm{x})\frac{\alpha_{n}}{n}\right)\bigg|s_{\lambda}\right\rangle.

3.3.6
.

For arguments
\bm{x}=q^{-\rho}t^{\nu}
or similar,

Lemma 3.6
.

\displaystyle s_{\lambda/\mu}(q^{-\rho}t^{\nu})=\left\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\nu}\otimes s_{\mu}\bigg|\exp\left(-\sum_{n>0}\big(qt)^{n/2}(t^{n/2}-t^{-n/2}\big)\frac{H_{n}\otimes\alpha_{n}}{n}\right)\bigg|\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\nu}\otimes s_{\lambda}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle s_{\lambda/\mu}(q^{-\nu^{t}}t^{\rho})=\left\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\nu}\otimes s_{\mu}\bigg|\exp\left(\sum_{n>0}(qt)^{-n/2}\big(q^{n/2}-q^{-n/2}\big)\frac{H_{-n}\otimes\alpha_{n}}{n}\right)\bigg|\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\nu}\otimes s_{\lambda}\right\rangle.

Proof.

For

\displaystyle p_{n}\big(q^{-\rho}t^{\nu}\big)=q^{n/2}\sum_{k>0}q^{n(k-1)}t^{n\nu_{k}}=-(qt)^{n/2}\big(t^{n/2}-t^{-n/2}\big)h_{n}(\nu;q,t),

where

but quite clearly

∎

3.3.7
.

It is clear that the terms in the last two lines of (

the vertex operators, via Lemma
3.6
, so the terms in the
first line must be absorbed somewhere. Compute that

\displaystyle(qt)^{\frac{\|\lambda^{t}\|^{2}-\|\lambda\|^{2}}{2}}P_{\lambda^{t}}\big(q,t^{-1}\big)P_{\lambda}\big(t^{-1},q\big)

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{\|\lambda^{t}\|^{2}}{2}}t^{-\frac{\|\lambda\|^{2}}{2}}\prod_{\square\in\lambda}\frac{1}{1-q^{\ell(\square)+1}t^{-a(\square)}}\frac{1}{1-q^{\ell(\square)}t^{-a(\square)-1}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\lambda|}(qt)^{\frac{|\lambda|}{2}}\frac{1}{\langle\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}|\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\rangle}

using that
\sum_{\square\in\lambda}\ell(\square)=\big(\|\lambda^{t}\|^{2}-|\lambda|\big)/2
and similarly for

. The resulting

\big({-}\sqrt{qt}\big)^{|\lambda|}
term is absorbed into the Kähler variable

. Finally, the term
\langle\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}|\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\rangle^{-1}
comes
from
\mathsf{D}
.

3.4
Dependence on preferred direction

3.4.1
.

The way in which diagrams such as the ones in
(

direction has been raised
[
17
]
and studied, e.g.,
[
5
]
, since the introduction of the refined vertex. A good
way to study this dependence is to relate the refined vertex

C_{\lambda\mu\nu}(q,t)
to the more symmetric K-theoretic
Pandharipande–Thomas (PT) vertex
V_{\lambda\mu\nu}(x,y,z;\mathfrak{q})
.

For a review of (equivariant) K-theoretic DT and PT theory,

[
34
]
should suffice.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\mathsf{T}\coloneqq(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{3}
with coordinates
(x,y,z)
.
Given a function
f(x,y,z)
on
\mathsf{T}
and a cocharacter

\sigma(u)=\big(u^{a},u^{b},u^{c}\big)\in\mathsf{T}
, let

\lim_{\sigma}f\coloneqq\lim_{u\to 0}f(\sigma(u)).

If
a+b+c=0
, i.e.,
\sigma
preserves
\kappa\coloneqq xyz
, and
there is some permutation of
a
,
b
,
c
so that
a\gg b>0
, then

\sigma
is called an

.

Theorem 3.8

(
[
32

)
.

Assume the DT/PT conjecture
[
32
, formula (16)]
for
equivariant K-theoretic vertices. Then

(\text{\rm prefactor})\cdot C_{\lambda\mu\nu}\big({-}\mathfrak{q}\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}},-\mathfrak{q}\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)=\lim_{\sigma_{V}}V_{\lambda\mu\nu}(x,y,z;\mathfrak{q})

for the index limit
\sigma_{V}(u)\coloneqq\big(u^{N},u^{-N-1},u\big)
with

.

The PT vertex

is fully symmetric in its three
legs, upon permuting the variables
x
,
y
,
z
accordingly, so different
permutations of components of the cocharacter

produce refined
vertices with different preferred direction.

In general, therefore, refined partition functions

are index
limits of analogous
PT partition functions

Z^{\PT}(x,y,z;\mathfrak{q},Q,A,\bm{B})\in\mathbb{Q}\big(x^{\frac{1}{2}},y^{\frac{1}{2}},z^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)((\mathfrak{q}))[[Q,A,\bm{B}]],

built from the PT vertex
V_{\lambda\mu\nu}
(and PT edge
contributions) in the same way that

is built from the refined
vertex
C_{\lambda\mu\nu}
. Changing preferred direction in

corresponds to changing the index limit

for

, which

[
2

for toric geometries, and we now review (a mild,

3.4.2

3.4.4
.

The proof the following theorem
will occupy the remainder of this subsection.

Theorem 3.13
.

With the specializations (

we have

\sigma

Note that the

and its independence of

more generally applicable to any toric diagram with appropriate

. In particular there

.

3.4.5
.

The PT vertex
V_{\lambda\mu\nu}(x,y,z;\mathfrak{q})
(and PT edges) enjoys a
number of nice properties, chief among which is that it is a sum of
so-called “balanced” rational functions of the form

\prod_{i}\frac{(\kappa w_{i})^{1/2}-(\kappa w_{i})^{-1/2}}{w_{i}^{1/2}-w_{i}^{-1/2}},\qquad\kappa\coloneqq xyz,

(3.17)

for monomials
w_{i}=w_{i}(x,y,z)
. (This follows immediately from the
construction of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathrm{vir}}
.) We refer to the
w_{i}
as
poles
of the rational function.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
\sigma
and
\tau
be cocharacters such that

\kappa(\sigma(u))
and
\kappa(\tau(u))
are independent of
u
.
Let
f
be a balanced rational function, as in (
3.17
). Then

\lim_{\sigma}f=\lim_{\tau}f

if
\lim_{\sigma}w_{i}=\lim_{\tau}w_{i}
for every pole
w_{i}
of
f
.

Being built from PT vertices (and edges),
Z_{r}^{\PT}(x,y,z)
is also a
sum of balanced rational functions. Hence the behavior of

\lim_{\sigma}Z_{r}^{\PT}
is controlled by the poles of
Z_{r}^{\PT}
, and
it suffices to locate these poles.

Note that individual components, e.g., the PT vertices

V_{\lambda\mu\nu}^{\PT}
comprising the sum
Z_{r}^{\PT}
, may have
more poles than
Z_{r}^{\PT}
does; there will generally be some pole
cancellation which we will now explain.

3.4.6
.

Pole-cancellation is controlled by the following geometric observation.

Proposition 3.15

(
[
2
, Proposition 3.2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a space with action by a torus
\mathsf{T}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a
(
virtual
)
sheaf on
\mathcal{M}
and assume that
(
virtual
)

\mathsf{T}
-equivariant
localization is applicable to
\chi_{\mathsf{T}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{F})
. Then its poles
occur only at weights
w\in\Hom(\mathsf{T},\mathbb{C}^{\times})
such that the
fixed locus
\mathcal{M}^{\ker w}
is non-compact.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{M}^{\ker w}
were compact, then equivariant localization with
respect to the maximal torus
\mathsf{T}_{w}\subset\ker w\subset\mathsf{T}

produces poles only at
\mathsf{T}
-weights occuring in the (virtual)
normal bundle
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{M}^{\mathsf{T}w}}
, none of which vanish on

\mathsf{T}_{w}
(by definition of the normal bundle).
∎

In the case of PT theory of a
3
-fold
X
, the only way for

\PT(X)^{\ker w}
to be non-compact is if
\ker w
leaves some
non-compact direction in
X
invariant – e.g., if
X
is toric and

w
is an integer power of the weight of some half-edge in the toric
diagram – and there are complete curves in
X
which can escape to
infinity along that direction. This is a reflection of the fact that
there is a Hilbert–Chow map

\pi\colon\ \PT(X)\to\Chow(X),

which is proper on each component of the Chow variety of

1
-dimensional cycles on
X
, and so non-compact directions in

\PT(X)
must arise from
\Chow(X)
. We have arrived at the conclusion
of
[
2
]
: weights of such non-compact directions in
X

form walls in the cocharacter lattice, and
\lim_{\sigma}Z^{\PT}
can
only change when
\sigma
crosses a wall.

3.4.7
.

We now move beyond the results of
[
2
]
, where this
pole-cancellation principle is applied only to toric
3
-folds. Our

X_{r}
is not toric, but the same argument as in
Section
3.4.6
applies. The only non-compact
direction in
X_{r}
is along the base
\Spec\mathbb{C}[[\kappa]]
, and so all
poles of
Z_{r}^{\PT}
occur only at integer powers of
\kappa
. By
Lemma
3.14
, such poles do not
affect index limits, which by definition leave
\kappa
constant.
Hence
\lim_{\sigma}Z_{r}^{\PT}
is independent of index limit
\sigma
,
as desired.

3.4.8
.

Finally, it remains to verify that
Z_{r}=\lim_{\sigma}Z_{r}^{\PT}
. The
only non-trivial step is with computing the index limit of PT edge
contributions in
X_{r}
, for the half-edges which are glued together in
a non-toric way. This is done via the following trivial observation.

Lemma 3.16
.

In the setting of
Proposition
3.14
, modifying
any weight
w_{i}
by multiples of
\kappa
does not affect

\lim_{\sigma}f
.

In particular, this applies to coordinate changes

(x,y,z)\mapsto\big(\kappa^{a}x,\kappa^{b}y,\kappa^{c}z\big)
with

a+b+c=0
, and the action of
\Lambda_{r}
on
\widetilde{X}
is generated
by substitutions of this form. In other words,
Z_{r}^{\PT}
may change
under the substitutions of (
3.15
), but

\lim_{\sigma}Z_{r}^{\PT}
does not. Hence, for each pair of half-edges
glued in a non-toric way, e.g., one in coordinates
(x,y,z)
and
another in coordinates
(\kappa^{a}x,\kappa^{-a}y,z)
, we are free
to pick either of the two coordinate charts to write the edge term.

Example 3.17
.

In our setting, all edges are local conifolds, i.e., having normal
bundle
\mathcal{O}(-1)^{\oplus 2}
. Let
E_{\lambda}
be the PT edge
contribution for a curve of class
\lambda
on such an edge. Then in
rank

,

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma}\left(\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{images/four-point-1.pdf}}}\right)\eqqcolon\lim_{\sigma}\big(Z_{1}^{\PT}(x,y,z;\mathfrak{q},Q,A,C=0)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad=\lim_{\sigma}\bigg(\sum_{\lambda,\mu}Q^{|\lambda|}A^{|\mu|}V_{\lambda\mu\varnothing}(x,y,z;\mathfrak{q})V_{\lambda^{t}\mu^{t}\varnothing}\big(yz,y^{-1},xy;\mathfrak{q}\big)E_{\lambda}(x,y,z;\mathfrak{q})E_{\mu}(y,z,x;\mathfrak{q})\bigg),

where, equally well,
E_{\lambda}(x,y,z;\mathfrak{q})
could be have been
replaced by
E_{\lambda^{t}}\big(yz,y^{-1},xy;\mathfrak{q}\big)
.

In general, by Theorem
3.8
, the PT vertex

V_{\lambda\mu\nu}
becomes the refined vertex

(with appropriate preferred direction) up to some prefactors which are
combinatorial quantities in
\lambda
,
\mu
, and
\nu
. One can check
by explicit computation that

\lim_{\sigma}E_{\lambda}=q^{\frac{\|\lambda\|^{2}}{2}}t^{\frac{\|\lambda^{t}\|^{2}}{2}}

exactly cancels these prefactors, e.g., the part of the prefactor from
the vertex which depends on
\lambda
is

q^{-\|\lambda\|^{2}/2}(q/t)^{|\lambda|}
, and the vertex on the other
end of the edge contributes

t^{-\|\lambda^{t}\|^{2}/2}(t/q)^{|\lambda^{t}|}
. See

[
2
, Section 4.3]
for details. We conclude that prefactors
and edges don’t matter, and
{\lim_{\sigma}Z_{r}^{\PT}=Z_{r}}

\displaystyle Z_{r}\big(q,t^{-1};QA,A,-\bm{B}\sqrt{qt}\big)=\tr_{\mathsf{F}^{\otimes r}}Q^{|\cdot|}\big\langle\varnothing\big|\big(\mathsf{RR}^{V}\big)^{(01)}\!B_{1}^{|\cdot|}\big(\mathsf{RR}^{V}\big)^{(02)}\!B_{2}^{|\cdot|}\cdots B_{r-1}^{|\cdot|}\big(\mathsf{RR}^{V}\big)^{(0r)}\!\big|\varnothing\big\rangle,

3.5.3

3.6

3.6.1

We collect here some properties of R-matrices – fusion
(Section

), unitarity
(Section
3.6.3
), and the Yang–Baxter equation
(Section

) – and discuss their analogues for the
operator
\mathsf{RR}
. In fact, it is productive to discuss more generally
the fully-equivariant four-point function in K-theoretic PT theory, so
let

\mathsf{RR}^{\PT}(A;x,y,z)\coloneqq\big(\big(\mathsf{RR}^{\PT}\big)^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}_{\mu\nu}\big)\coloneqq\left(\vbox{\hbox{\includegraphics[scale]{images/four-point-PT.pdf}}}\right)\in\End(\mathsf{F}\otimes\mathsf{F})((A))

denote the PT partition function associated to the four-point diagram
shown. Explicitly, in the notation of

\big(\mathsf{RR}^{\PT}\big)^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}_{\mu\nu}\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda}A^{|\lambda|}V_{\mu\lambda\nu}(x,y,z;\mathfrak{q})V_{\mu^{\prime}\lambda^{t}\nu^{\prime}}\big(yz,y^{-1},xy;\mathfrak{q}\big)E_{\lambda}(y,z,x;\mathfrak{q}).

3.6.2
.

Recall that if
\mathsf{R}_{W,V_{1}}\in\End(W\otimes V_{1})
and
\mathsf{R}_{W,V_{2}}\in\End(W\otimes V_{2})
are R-matrices, then

\mathsf{R}_{W,V_{1}\otimes V_{2}}=\mathsf{R}_{W,V_{1}}^{(12)}\mathsf{R}_{W,V_{2}}^{(13)}\in\End(W\otimes V_{1}\otimes V_{2}).

In this way,

-characters of tensor products arise from R-matrices
of each tensor factor. Completely analogously, the factorization of
(
3.2
) into four-point diagrams indicates
that we should define

\mathsf{RR}^{\PT}_{\mathsf{F},\mathsf{F}\otimes\mathsf{F}}\coloneqq\big(\mathsf{RR}^{\PT}_{\mathsf{F},\mathsf{F}}\big)^{(12)}B^{|\cdot|}\big(\mathsf{RR}^{\PT}_{\mathsf{F},\mathsf{F}}\big)^{(13)},

and
qq
-characters of tensor products therefore also arise from

\mathsf{RR}
of each tensor factor. Note that the Kähler variable
B

becomes some combination of evaluation parameters or equivariant
variables under the identification
(
3.3

3.6.3
.

Recall that if
\mathsf{R}(u)\in\End(V\otimes V)
is a trigonometric
R-matrix with spectral parameter
u

whether

\mathsf{R}^{(21)}\big(u^{-1}\big)=\mathsf{R}(u),

called

. We propose that the following is the analogue
for
\mathsf{RR}^{\PT}
.

Conjecture 3.19

(flop invariance)
.

Let

\widetilde{\mathsf{RR}}^{\PT}(A;x,y,z)\coloneqq\frac{\mathsf{RR}^{\PT}(A;x,y,z)}{\mathsf{RR}^{\PT}(A;x,y,z)^{\varnothing\varnothing}_{\varnothing\varnothing}}

be the normalization, as in (

\widetilde{\mathsf{RR}}^{\PT}\big(A^{-1};x,xy,z/x\big)^{\mu^{\prime}\nu}_{\mu\nu^{\prime}}=\widetilde{\mathsf{RR}}^{\PT}(A;x,y,z)^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}_{\mu\nu}.

The change of variables
(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,xy,z/x)
is to ensure
that each of the four half-edges
\mu
,
\mu^{\prime}
,
\nu
,
\nu^{\prime}
retains the
same weight. (Only the weight of the internal edge changes.)

This conjecture is known, by the explicit computation in

[
21
]
, when either
\mu=\mu^{\prime}=\varnothing
or
\nu=\nu^{\prime}=\varnothing
, i.e., only one set of half-edges is non-trivial. In
general it is a question about the behavior of DT (or PT) invariants
under flops, and such general questions have been addressed
non-equivariantly, see, e.g.,
[
8
]
. In the refined
limit, the conjecture is known in full generality by explicit
computation
[
4
]
.

3.6.4
.

Recall that (trigonometric) R-matrices satisfy the Yang–Baxter
equation

\mathsf{R}^{(12)}(u)\mathsf{R}^{(13)}(uv)\mathsf{R}^{(23)}(v)=\mathsf{R}^{(23)}(v)\mathsf{R}^{(13)}(uv)\mathsf{R}^{(12)}(u),

from which one obtains the RTT relation

T^{(13)}T^{(12)}\mathsf{R}^{(23)}=\mathsf{R}^{(23)}T^{(13)}T^{(12)},\qquad T\coloneqq(\mathsf{Z}\otimes 1)\mathsf{R}

for any operator
\mathsf{Z}
such that
[\mathsf{Z}\otimes\mathsf{Z},\mathsf{R}]=0
.

Conjecture 3.20

(
[
6
, Section 3]

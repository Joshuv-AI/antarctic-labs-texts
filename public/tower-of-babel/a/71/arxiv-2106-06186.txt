Real-Value Power-Voltage Formulations of, and Bounds for, Three-Wire Unbalanced Optimal Power Flow
Frederik Geth and Hakan Ergun

Abstract
Unbalanced optimal power flow refers to a class of optimization problems subject to the steady state physics of three-phase power grids with nonnegligible phase unbalance. Significant progress on this problem has been made on the mathematical modelling side of unbalanced OPF, however there is a lack of information on implementation aspects as well as data sets for benchmarking. One of the key problems is the lack of definitions of current and voltage bounds across different classes of representations of the power flow equations. Therefore, this tutorial-style paper summarizes the structural features of the unbalanced (optimal) power problem for three-phase systems. The resulting nonlinear complex-value matrix formulations are presented for both the bus injection and branch flow formulation frameworks, which typically cannot be implemented as-is in optimization toolboxes. Therefore, this paper also derives the equivalent real-value formulations, and discusses challenges related to the implementation in optimization modeling toolboxes. The derived formulations can be re-used easily for continuous and discrete optimization problems in distribution networks for a variety of operational and planning problems. Finally, bounds are derived for all variables involved, to further the development of benchmarks for unbalanced optimal power flow, where consensus on bound semantics is a pressing need. We believe benchmarks remain a cornerstone for the development and validation of scalable and reproducible optimization models and tools. The soundness of the derivations is confirmed through numerical experiments, validated w.r.t. OpenDSS for IEEE test feeders with 3 × 3 3\times 3 impedance matrices.

Introduction

Driven by the increased rollout of distributed energy resources (DERs) such as PV, battery storage as well as electric vehicles, power distribution grids are facing a number of challenges associated with the large scale integration of these technologies.
In low-voltage grids specifically, one can observe phase unbalance readily, due to the presence of a significant amount of single-phase loads and DERs. Many of the current electric vehicles use single phase charging and small rooftop solar systems are connected via single phase inverters to the low voltage grid, further increasing the unbalance. Furthermore, phase unbalance can stem from insufficient conductor transposition in radial distribution networks.
Unbalance implies an underutilization of the grid’s transfer capacity, as it leads to higher losses and to faster-than-expected congestion. Simulation techniques, i.e. deriving solutions to the power flow (PF) equations in unbalanced networks has long been a topic of interest
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, and is used in deriving hosting capacity by means of scenario analysis. The
accuracy
of modeling of low-voltage grids has been studied in-depth by Urquhart
[
4
]
.

Unbalanced
optimal
power flow (OPF) refers to the mathematical optimization of problems subject to the physics of unbalanced grids, and serves as the core for a variety of problem classes such as benchmarking centralized or distributed optimal control solutions
[
5
]
, determination of expansion options and hosting capacity analysis considering control actions.

1.1
State of the Art on Unbalanced (O)PF

Similar to the balanced (positive sequence) modeling, one can distinguish between the bus injection model (BIM) and the branch flow model (BFM) formulations of the unbalanced PF equations. BIM forms eliminate all current variables, which leads to active and reactive power flows being expressed purely as a function of the voltage differences between connected buses. The series impedance is consequently used in admittance form, which makes it impossible to represent zero-impedance branches. Conversely, the BFM forms keep (a representation of) the current variable through the series impedance. In this case, series impedance is represented in impedance form, therefore the edge case of zero series impedance remains representable.

Most OPF problems are developed in the complex power-voltage variable space, instead of the current-voltage variable space common in PF solvers.

1.2
Bound definitions and data

In an OPF problem, authors typically assume
[
6
,
7
,
20
]
the power flow is subject to a subset of:

•

apparent power, upper bound;

•

current magnitude, upper bound;

•

voltage magnitude, lower and upper bound;

•

voltage angle difference between adjacent buses, lower and upper bound.

These bounds can be generalized to the case with phase unbalance. Furthermore, in the context of phase unbalance, additional limits have been discussed
[
21
]
. The most obvious missing bound is that of phase angle differences at a certain bus.
Note that bounds represent a key aspect of optimization problems, however currently there are few unbalanced power flow data sets that include all of these parameters. This makes benchmarking
optimality
of different unbalanced OPF engines challenging.

Scope, Contributions and Report Structure

In summary, we observe the following gaps in the literature:

•

polar and rectangular real forms in the power-voltage variable space for branches
with shunts

\Pi

2
Notation and Basic Relationships

2.1
Scalar and Matrix Variables

The voltage
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}
of a bus
i
is a complex value, vector variable, encapsulating variables for each conductor:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{a}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{b}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{c}}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}U_{{i},{a}}^{\text{mag}}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\angle}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{a}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}U_{{i},{b}}^{\text{mag}}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\angle}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{b}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}U_{{i},{c}}^{\text{mag}}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\angle}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{c}}}\end{bmatrix}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{mag}}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\angle}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{\Theta}_{{i}}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}U_{{i},{a}}^{\text{re}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}j}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}U_{{i},{a}}^{\text{im}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}U_{{i},{b}}^{\text{re}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}j}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}U_{{i},{b}}^{\text{im}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}U_{{i},{c}}^{\text{re}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}j}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}U_{{i},{c}}^{\text{im}}}\end{bmatrix}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{re}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}j}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{im}}}

(1)

Similarly, the total current of a branch
l
connecting a pair of buses
i
and
j
is a complex value vector variable,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I_{{l}{i}{j},{a}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I_{{l}{i}{j},{b}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I_{{l}{i}{j},{c}}}\end{bmatrix}.

The sending-side current (
i\rightarrow j
) through the series element in the
\Pi
-model is defined as
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}
, the sending-side shunt current as
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}}
, respectively.
The complex power flow in branch
l
from bus
{i}
to
{j}
depends on the bus voltage of the sending-side
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}
and the conjugate transpose (indicated with superscript
H
) of the current
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}}
,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}})^{\text{H}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{a}{a}}}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{a}{b}}}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{a}{c}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{b}{a}}}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{b}{b}}}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{b}{c}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{c}{a}}}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{c}{b}}}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{c}{c}}}\end{array}\right].

We observe that
\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}})={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}\circ({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}})^{*}
,
where
\circ
is the element-wise (Hadamard) product.
It is noted that
\rank({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}})=1
, as it is defined as the outer product of two vectors.
The off-diagonals relate to the diagonal elements according to,

\displaystyle\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{a}{a}}}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{a}}}}=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{b}{a}}}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{b}}}}=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{c}{a}}}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{c}}}}=({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I_{{l}{i}{j},{a}}})^{*},

(6a)

\displaystyle\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{b}{b}}}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{b}}}}=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{a}{b}}}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{a}}}}=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{c}{b}}}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{c}}}}=({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I_{{l}{i}{j},{b}}})^{*},

(7a)

\displaystyle\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{c}{c}}}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{c}}}}=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{a}{c}}}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{a}}}}=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{b}{c}}}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{b}}}}=({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I_{{l}{i}{j},{c}}})^{*},

(8a)

which means that the off-diagonals of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}}
are scaled and rotated versions of the more easily interpretable diagonal elements.

2.2
Variable Bounds

Voltage magnitudes have minimum and maximum operational limits, which are specific to each bus and phase,

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}\leq\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}U_{{i},{a}}^{\text{min}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}U_{{i},{b}}^{\text{min}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}U_{{i},{c}}^{\text{min}}}\\
\end{bmatrix}}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{min}}}}\leq\begin{bmatrix}|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{a}}}|\\
|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{b}}}|\\
|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{c}}}|\\
\end{bmatrix}\leq\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}U_{{i},{a}}^{\text{max}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}U_{{i},{b}}^{\text{max}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}U_{{i},{c}}^{\text{max}}}\\
\end{bmatrix}}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{max}}}}.

(9)

In this report, we overload ‘
\leq
’ for ‘
\geq
’ for vectors
and
matrices to indicate
element-wise inequality
(conversely, ‘
\succeq
’ is used as the symbol for matrix positive semidefiniteness).
Recognizing we obtain the magnitude squared by multiplying complex numbers with their own conjugates, the nodal voltage bounds can also be presented quadratically as:

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{min}}}\circ{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{min}}}\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}\circ({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}})^{*}\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{max}}}\circ{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{max}}}.

(10)

Note that the upper bound constraints are convex (space inside a circle), but the lower bound constraints are nonconvex (space outside of a circle).
Next, apparent power limits
are defined using the absolute value of the diagonals of the branch flow matrix,

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}\leq\begin{bmatrix}|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{a}{a}}}|\\
|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{b}{b}}}|\\
|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{c}{c}}}|\\
\end{bmatrix}\leq\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}S_{{l}{i}{j},{a}}^{\text{rated}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}S_{{l}{i}{j},{b}}^{\text{rated}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}S_{{l}{i}{j},{c}}^{\text{rated}}}\\
\end{bmatrix}={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{rated}}},

(11)

which is equivalent to second order cone (SOC) constraints,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{a}{a}}}|^{2}\\
|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{b}{b}}}|^{2}\\
|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{c}{c}}}|^{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}P_{{l}{i}{j},{a}{a}}})^{2}+({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}Q_{{l}{i}{j},{a}{a}}})^{2}\\
({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}P_{{l}{i}{j},{b}{b}}})^{2}+({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}Q_{{l}{i}{j},{b}{b}}})^{2}\\
({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}P_{{l}{i}{j},{c}{c}}})^{2}+({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}Q_{{l}{i}{j},{c}{c}}})^{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}\leq\begin{bmatrix}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}S_{{l}{i}{j},{a}}^{\text{rated}}})^{2}\\
({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}S_{{l}{i}{j},{b}}^{\text{rated}}})^{2}\\
({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}S_{{l}{i}{j},{c}}^{\text{rated}}})^{2}\\
\end{bmatrix},

(12)

and can succinctly be written as,

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}\leq\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}})\circ\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}})^{*}\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{rated}}}\circ{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{rated}}}.

(13)

The magnitudes of the branch current should stay below rated values and are bounded for the diagonals of the branch current matrix,

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}\leq\begin{bmatrix}|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I_{{l}{i}{j},{a}}}|\\
|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I_{{l}{i}{j},{b}}}|\\
|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I_{{l}{i}{j},{c}}}|\\
\end{bmatrix}\leq\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}I_{{l}{i}{j},{a}}^{\text{rated}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}I_{{l}{i}{j},{b}}^{\text{rated}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}I_{{l}{i}{j},{c}}^{\text{rated}}}\\
\end{bmatrix}={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{rated}}},

(14)

which again can be written as a set of SOC constraints,

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}}\circ({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}})^{*}\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{rated}}}\circ{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{rated}}}.

(15)

A valid SOC representation of current magnitude limits (
14
) that does not require explicit current variables
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}}
is useful for BIM forms.
Using the nodal voltage magnitudes, the branch current limits can directly be enforced on the power flows,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{a}{a}}}|^{2}\\
|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{b}{b}}}|^{2}\\
|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{{l}{i}{j},{c}{c}}}|^{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}\leq\begin{bmatrix}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}I_{{l}{i}{j},{a}}^{\text{rated}}})^{2}\\
({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}I_{{l}{i}{j},{b}}^{\text{rated}}})^{2}\\
({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}I_{{l}{i}{j},{c}}^{\text{rated}}})^{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}\circ\begin{bmatrix}|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{a}}}|^{2}\\
|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{b}}}|^{2}\\
|{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}U_{{i},{c}}}|^{2}\\
\end{bmatrix},

(16)

which can also be developed in matrix notation as,

\displaystyle\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}})\circ\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}})^{*}\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{rated}}}\circ{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{rated}}}\circ{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}\circ({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}})^{*}.

(17)

Valid bounds on all elements of the power flow matrix
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{P}_{{l}{i}{j}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}j}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{Q}_{{l}{i}{j}}}
are,

\displaystyle-{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{max}}}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{rated}}})^{\text{T}}\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{P}_{{l}{i}{j}}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{Q}_{{l}{i}{j}}}\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{max}}}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{rated}}})^{\text{T}},

(18)

both for active and reactive power components, respectively.
The voltage angle differences between connected buses
i
and
j
are bounded,

\displaystyle\!\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{\pi}{2}\\
-\frac{\pi}{2}\\
-\frac{\pi}{2}\end{bmatrix}\leq\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i}{j},{a}{a}}^{\text{min}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i}{j},{b}{b}}^{\text{min}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i}{j},{c}{c}}^{\text{min}}}\end{bmatrix}}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{\Theta}_{{i}{j}}^{\text{min}}}}\leq\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{a}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{j},{a}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{b}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{j},{b}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{c}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{j},{c}}}\end{bmatrix}\leq\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i}{j},{a}{a}}^{\text{max}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i}{j},{b}{b}}^{\text{max}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i}{j},{c}{c}}^{\text{max}}}\end{bmatrix}}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{\Theta}_{{i}{j}}^{\text{max}}}}\leq\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\pi}{2}\\
\frac{\pi}{2}\\
\frac{\pi}{2}\end{bmatrix}.

(19)

For a relatively balanced voltage phasor, we expect
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{a}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{b}}}\approx{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{b}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{c}}}\approx{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{c}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{a}}}\approx\frac{2\pi}{3}
.
The voltage angle differences between phases on buses
i
can be bounded to enforce angle balance relative to the expected 120 degrees,

\displaystyle\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i},{a}{a}}^{\text{min}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i},{b}{b}}^{\text{min}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i},{c}{c}}^{\text{min}}}\end{bmatrix}}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{\Theta}_{{i}}^{\text{min}}}}\leq\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{a}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{b}}}-\frac{2\pi}{3}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{b}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{c}}}-\frac{2\pi}{3}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{c}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\theta_{{i},{a}}}-\frac{2\pi}{3}\end{bmatrix}\leq\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i},{a}{a}}^{\text{max}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i},{b}{b}}^{\text{max}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i},{c}{c}}^{\text{max}}}\end{bmatrix}}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{\Theta}_{{i}}^{\text{max}}}}.

(20)

The voltage phasor in reference buses
i\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{I}}_{\text{ref}}\subset{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{I}}
is assumed fixed, e.g.,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}={\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{ref}}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}U_{{i},{a}}^{\text{ref}}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\angle}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i},{a}}^{\text{ref}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}U_{{i},{b}}^{\text{ref}}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\angle}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i},{b}}^{\text{ref}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}U_{{i},{c}}^{\text{ref}}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\angle}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\theta_{{i},{c}}^{\text{ref}}}\end{bmatrix}.

(21)

2.3
Branch Impedance

The circuit series impedance matrix is defined as a full matrix with no assumption on the particular structure:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{z}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}j}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{x}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}z_{{l},{a}{a}}^{\text{{s}}}}&{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}z_{{l},{a}{b}}^{\text{{s}}}}&{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}z_{{l},{a}{c}}^{\text{{s}}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}z_{{l},{b}{a}}^{\text{{s}}}}&{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}z_{{l},{b}{b}}^{\text{{s}}}}&{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}z_{{l},{b}{c}}^{\text{{s}}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}z_{{l},{c}{a}}^{\text{{s}}}}&{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}z_{{l},{c}{b}}^{\text{{s}}}}&{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}z_{{l},{c}{c}}^{\text{{s}}}}\\
\end{bmatrix},

(22)

where each element consists of a series resistive and reactive impedance. It is noted that in physical systems, we expect
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{r}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}\succeq 0,{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{x}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}\succeq 0
.
The impedance matrix can be rewritten in the corresponding admittance form,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}={({\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{z}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}})^{-1}}={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{g}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}j}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{b}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}.

where
{({\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{z}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}})^{-1}}
is the matrix inverse of
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{z}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}
. In case of missing conductors, e.g. single, or two-conductor connections,
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{z}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}
is not invertible but it is valid to use the Moore-Penrose inverse instead.
The shunt admittances at the sending and receiving sides, respectively
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}},{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}{j}{i}}^{\text{{sh}}}}
, are defined,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}}={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{g}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}j}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{b}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}y_{{l}{i}{j},{a}{a}}^{\text{{sh}}}}&{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}y_{{l}{i}{j},{a}{b}}^{\text{{sh}}}}&{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}y_{{l}{i}{j},{a}{c}}^{\text{{sh}}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}y_{{l}{i}{j},{b}{a}}^{\text{{sh}}}}&{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}y_{{l}{i}{j},{b}{b}}^{\text{{sh}}}}&{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}y_{{l}{i}{j},{b}{c}}^{\text{{sh}}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}y_{{l}{i}{j},{c}{a}}^{\text{{sh}}}}&{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}y_{{l}{i}{j},{c}{b}}^{\text{{sh}}}}&{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}y_{{l}{i}{j},{c}{c}}^{\text{{sh}}}}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

(23)

Although for typical distribution lines and cables the shunt admittances are diagonal and have equal values for both sides, in this generalized derivation they can be considered as a two different and full matrices. This also allows for re-use of the representation for other elements, such as transformers.

2.4
Loads and Generators as Units

We define units
{u}\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{U}}
to generalize loads, generators and storage elements. The current flowing from the connected bus into the unit is

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{u}}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{I}_{{u},{a}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{I}_{{u},{b}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{I}_{{u},{c}}}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

(24)

The unit current is bounded by the corresponding current rating
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{I}_{{u}}^{\text{rated}}}
similarly to (
14
).
Tuples of units and the buses they are connected to are defined in the connectivity set
{u}{i}\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T^{\text{units}}}}
.
The active and reactive power consumed by a unit is defined
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{u}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{P}_{{u}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}j}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{Q}_{{u}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{u}}})^{\text{H}}
. We define bounds on active/reactive power dispatch separately,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{P}_{{u}}^{\text{min}}}\leq\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{P}_{{u}}})\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{P}_{{u}}^{\text{max}}},

(25a)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{Q}_{{u}}^{\text{min}}}\leq\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{Q}_{{u}}})\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{Q}_{{u}}^{\text{max}}}.

(26a)

We do not provide any further detail on the specific modeling of delta or wye-connected units and refer to

,
17
]
.

2.5
Shunts

A shunt element, e.g. shunt capacitance, inductance or resistance, with index
{h}
has an admittance
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{h}}}={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{g}_{{h}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}j}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{b}_{{h}}}
.
Tuples of shunts and the bus they are connected to are defined in the connectivity set
{h}{i}\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T^{\text{shunts}}}}
.
The current from the bus to the shunt is,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{h}}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{I}_{{h},{a}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{I}_{{h},{b}}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{I}_{{h},{c}}}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

(27)

and is bounded by the rated current
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{I}_{{h}}^{\text{rated}}}
.

3

This section illustrates how Kirchhoff’s and Ohm’s laws are used in the context of multi-conductor branches with matrix impedances for the representation of the circuit physics, providing the relationship between the voltage, current and power variables.

3.1
Branch Model

Ohm’s law for branch
lij
representing the voltage drop along the branch is formulated in matrix form using nodal voltages, the branch current and the impedance matrix,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{j}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}-{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{z}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}.

(28)

Kirchhoff’s current law (KCL) is used to split up the series and shunt (to ground) currents in the
\Pi
-section,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{j}{i}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{j}{i}}^{\text{{s}}}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{j}{i}}^{\text{{sh}}}},

(29)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{j}{i}}^{\text{{s}}}}=0.

(30)

defines the harmonized single-wire equivalent
vector/matrix

3.2
Shunts and Units at Nodes

The unit model can be adapted for a variety of optimization problems, e.g. optimal dispatch of distributed generation, optimal scheduling of storage and electric vehicle charging and optimal demand management. Such extensions essentially define feasible sets dependent on
\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{P}_{{u}}}),\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{Q}_{{u}}})
. Without loss of generality, in this work, we focus on the feasible sets defined by (
25a
)-(
26a
).

Similarly, the shunt power
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{h}}}
is defined as

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{h}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{P}_{{h}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}j}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{Q}_{{h}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{h}}})^{\text{H}}.

(31)

The shunt current relates to the voltage through Ohm’s law,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{h}}}={\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{h}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}.

(32)

Substituting (
32
) into (
31
), the shunt power consumption can be expressed depending on the nodal voltage only,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{h}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}})^{\text{H}}({\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{h}}})^{\text{H}}.

(33)

3.3
Bus Model: Kirchhoff’s Current Law

Kirchhoff’s Current Law (KCL) is conventionally expressed in current variables, but can also be lifted to the complex power variable space.
KCL in current variables at each bus
i
is,

\displaystyle\sum_{lij\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}}+\sum_{{u}{i}\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T^{\text{units}}}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{u}}}+\sum_{bi\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T^{\text{shunts}}}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{h}}}=0.

(34)

KCL in the complex power variable space is obtained by taking the conjugate transpose of (
34
) and element-wise multiplying with
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}\neq 0
on the left,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sum_{lij\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T}}}\!\!\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}})+\!\!\!\!\sum_{{u}{i}\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T^{\text{units}}}}}\!\!\!\!\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{u}}})+\!\!\!\!\sum_{bi\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T^{\text{shunts}}}}}\!\!\!\!\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{h}}})=0,

(35)

which means that the diagonal elements of the apparent power matrices of the connected branch flows, units and shunt need to sum to zero for each bus. Commonly, the shunt power expression (
33
) is substituted into this equation. The real-value equivalent forms are obtained,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sum_{lij\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T}}}\!\!\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{P}_{{l}{i}{j}}})+\!\!\!\!\sum_{{u}{i}\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T^{\text{units}}}}}\!\!\!\!\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{P}_{{u}}})+\!\!\!\!\sum_{bi\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T^{\text{shunts}}}}}\!\!\!\!\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{P}_{{h}}})=0,

(36a)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sum_{lij\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T}}}\!\!\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{Q}_{{l}{i}{j}}})+\!\!\!\!\sum_{{u}{i}\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T^{\text{units}}}}}\!\!\!\!\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{Q}_{{u}}})+\!\!\!\!\sum_{bi\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T^{\text{shunts}}}}}\!\!\!\!\diag({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{Q}_{{h}}})=0.

(37a)

4
Unbalanced Branch Flow Model

This section illustrates how the unbalanced BFM is derived.
First, we define a variable for the complex power flow
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}
through the
series
element of the
\Pi
-section,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{P}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}j}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{Q}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}})^{\text{H}}.

(38)

4.1
Power Flow Model

Passive components in electrical circuits cause losses. In the BFM we distinguish between the losses associated with the from-side shunt, i.e.
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{loss}},\text{{sh}}}}
, the series impedance, i.e.
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}}^{\text{{loss}},\text{{s}}}}
, and the to-side shunt, i.e.
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{j}{i}}^{\text{{loss}},\text{{sh}}}}
.
The series losses depend on the voltage drop over the impedance and the current flow through it,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}}^{\text{{loss}},\text{{s}}}}=({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{j}}})({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}})^{\text{H}}.

(39)

The voltage drop itself is can be derived from Ohm’s law,

\displaystyle({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{j}}})={\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{z}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}.

(40)

Substituting (
40
) into (
39
) we obtain the series loss

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}}^{\text{{loss}},\text{{s}}}}={\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{z}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}})^{\text{H}}={\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{z}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{j}{i}}^{\text{{s}}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{j}{i}}^{\text{{s}}}})^{\text{H}},

(41)

which is symmetric because of (
30
).
The shunt losses
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{loss}},\text{{sh}}}}
are derived from the current through the shunt, i.e.
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}}
, and the voltage at the shunt, i.e.
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}
,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{loss}},\text{{sh}}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}})^{\text{H}},

(42)

The shunt current relates to the voltage through Ohm’s law,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}}={\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}.

(43)

Substituting (
43
) into (
42
), the shunt losses are,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{loss}},\text{{sh}}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}})^{\text{H}}({\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}})^{\text{H}},\,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{j}{i}}^{\text{{loss}},\text{{sh}}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{j}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{j}}})^{\text{H}}({\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}{j}{i}}^{\text{{sh}}}})^{\text{H}}.

(44)

As the the sum of the sending and receiving side power flows need to equal the branch losses, the branch loss balance (see Fig.
3
) can be written using the different loss components (
41
) and (
44
),

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{j}{i}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{loss}},\text{{sh}}}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}}^{\text{{loss}},\text{{s}}}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{j}{i}}^{\text{{loss}},\text{{sh}}}}

\displaystyle={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}})^{\text{H}}({\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}})^{\text{H}}+{\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{z}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}})^{\text{H}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{j}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{j}}})^{\text{H}}({\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}{j}{i}}^{\text{{sh}}}})^{\text{H}}.

(45)

5.1
Power Flow Model

Therefore, we write (
40
) in admittance form,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}={\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{j}}}).

(46)

The sending end apparent power flow can be calculated using the sending end voltage and the sum of the series current and the sending end shunt current, respectively,

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}})^{\text{H}}.

(47)

By substituting (
43
) and (
46
) into (
47
) we derive,

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}})^{\text{H}}({\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}})^{\text{H}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{j}}})^{\text{H}}({\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}})^{\text{H}},

(48)

which is the nonlinear complex matrix form of the BIM. Note that this constraint is defined
\forall{l}{i}{j}\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathcal{T}}
, i.e. for both the sending end
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}}
and the receiving end
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{j}{i}}}
, respectively.
We now choose rectangular coordinates for the voltage and power variables and obtain,

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{P}_{{l}{i}{j}}}=\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{re}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{re}}})^{\text{T}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{im}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{im}}})^{\text{T}}\right)({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{g}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}})^{\text{T}}+\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{im}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{re}}})^{\text{T}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{im}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{im}}})^{\text{T}}\right)({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{b}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}})^{\text{T}}

\displaystyle+\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{re}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{re}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{j}}^{\text{re}}})^{\text{T}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{im}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}^{\text{im}}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{U}_{{j}}^{\text{im}}})^{\text{T}}\right)({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{g}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}})^{\text{T}}

7.1.1

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{L}_{{l}{i}{j}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{L}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{re}}}+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}j}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{L}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{im}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}})^{\text{H}}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{L}_{{l}{i}{j}}}\succeq 0,\rank({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{L}_{{l}{i}{j}}})=1

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{I}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}+({\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}}){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{U}_{{i}}}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{L}_{{l}{i}{j}}}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{L}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}+({\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}}){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{W}_{{i}}}({\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}})^{\text{H}}+({\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}}){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}}+({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{S}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{s}}}})^{\text{H}}({\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}\mathbf{y}_{{l}{i}{j}}^{\text{{sh}}}})^{\text{H}},

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{L}_{{l}}^{\text{{s}}}}

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbf{W}_{{i}}}
and

9
Conclusions

In this report, the electrical physics of three-phase grids are derived in complex-power matrix variables and different voltage variable spaces, i.e. polar, rectangular and lifted.
Using the different variable spaces, the unbalanced power flow formulations are derived for generic
\Pi
-model branches with asymmetric shunt impedance (full matrices, no structure assumed).
The real-value matrix and scalar equivalent formulations are derived, and implemented in an open-source software package.
The derived mathematical framework can easily be extended and applied to a variety of continuous and discrete optimization problems in distribution networks.
Expressions are presented to enforce voltage magnitude, angle, power and current bounds across the formulations in an exact manner, without needing to resort to auxiliary variables.
Nevertheless, including auxiliary variables instead of eliminating them does not by default increase computational effort.
Comparing these variants numerically requires the development of an extensive unbalanced OPF test case library, which includes values for the different bounds discussed, not just voltage magnitudes.
Future work includes adding more bounds, e.g. for sequence voltage magnitudes and unbalance metrics
[
21
]
.

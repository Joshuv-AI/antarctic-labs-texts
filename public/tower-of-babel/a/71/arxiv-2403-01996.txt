Global shift symmetry on an ADM hypersurface: Toward emergent gravity
Ki-Seok Kim a,b , Arpita Mitra a , Debangshu Mukherjee b and Mitsuhiro Nishida a

Abstract
Generalized symmetries and their spontaneous breakdown serve as the fundamental concept to constrain the many-body entanglement structure, which allows us to characterize quantum phases of matter and emergent collective excitations. For example, emergent photons may be understood by spontaneous 1-form symmetry breaking, which results from a long-ranged entanglement structure between UV microscopic degrees of freedom. In this study, we show that emergent “gravity” may also arise in a similar fashion, where quotes have been used to emphasize that the symmetry-constrained gravitons show unconventional properties compared to usual gravitons. As the electric 1-form symmetry in Maxwell theory is realized as a global shift symmetry of the spatial component of the U(1) gauge field, generated by the electric field, we demonstrate that a constant shift of the Arnowitt-Deser-Misner (ADM) metric on the spatial hypersurface can be viewed as a global symmetry, generated by the ADM canonical momentum. Deriving a vector-type conserved charge from the variation of action, we construct a shift symmetry operator. Considering a Wick rotation, we demonstrate that a gravitational Wilson loop is charged under the action of this shift symmetry operator, which thus confirms the existence of a generalized global symmetry on the ADM hypersurface. Based on the Ward identity, we show that the spontaneous breaking of this global shift symmetry may give rise to a nonpropagating massless symmetric gauge field at the boundary of the hypersurface.

Review of shift symmetry in electromagnetism

First, we briefly review the 1-form symmetry

To understand this more concretely, we begin with the Lorentz invariant Maxwell Lagrangian density in four spacetime dimensions,

{\cal L}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}.

(1)

We consider the field theory to be on a constant time slice with an inertial frame coordinate (

). We also work in the Coulomb gauge given by

\displaystyle\pi^{i}=\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{A}_{i}}=-E^{i}.

(2)

The conjugate momentum corresponding to the temporal component,
\pi^{0}=\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{A}_{0}}=0
is a primary constraint of the theory. In addition, we have a Gauss law constraint that has to be imposed to retain the states invariant under gauge transformation. Imposing this constraint as the selection rule for physical states, we write the commutation relation between the conjugate variables as

[A^{i}(\vec{x}),E^{j}(\vec{y})]=-i\delta^{ij}\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})\ .

(3)

Under a constant shift of the spatial component of gauge field,
A_{i}\rightarrow A_{i}+\lambda_{i}

\delta S_{EM}=\int dt\ d^{3}x\left(\partial_{t}F^{0i}+\partial_{j}{F}^{ji}\right)\lambda_{i}\equiv\int dt\ d^{3}x\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu i}\lambda_{i}\ .\qquad(\mu=0,j)

the first cohomology group of the spacetime manifold

Another important point to remember is that the associated current has to be gauge invariant so that one can have a well-defined charge operator in the theory. Interestingly, here the 2-form current

III
Shift symmetry of ADM metric on the hypersurface

Carrying out an expansion for the gravitational Wilson line up to the linear order of
\kappa
,
\Phi
can be written as

\displaystyle\Phi=\exp\left[\frac{i\kappa}{2}\int_{C}ds\frac{dX^{\mu}}{ds}\frac{dX^{\nu}}{ds}\gamma_{\mu\nu}(x(s))\right].

(33)

Here, the mass

was absorbed into the length parameter
s
.
The issue of square root “singularity” can be avoided with the interpretation of
\Phi
as the Euclidean action of a relativistic point particle. We can rewrite the point-particle action in the following form
Polchinski

\displaystyle S_{\text{PP}}={1\over 2}\int_{C}e(s)~ds\left({1\over e^{2}(s)}g_{\mu\nu}^{E}\frac{dX^{\mu}}{ds}\frac{dX^{\nu}}{ds}+m^{2}\right)\ ,

(34)

where
e(s)
is an auxiliary field acting as a vielbein on the worldline. Substituting
e
with the solution of its equation of motion, one can get back (
31
).
e(s)
essentially plays the role of Lagrange multiplier and the action enjoys reparametrization invariance. One can use this symmetry to fix
e(s)=1
which leads to the following Wilson line operator,

W_{g}=\exp\left[{i\over 2}\int_{C}ds~\left(g_{\mu\nu}\frac{dX^{\mu}}{ds}\frac{dX^{\nu}}{ds}+m^{2}\right)\right]\ .

(35)

Considering the ADM foliation and taking the vanishing mass limit, (
35
) gives,

W_{h}=\exp\left[{i\over 2}\int_{C}ds\left(\frac{dy^{0}}{ds}\frac{dy^{0}}{ds}+\frac{dy^{a}}{ds}\frac{dy^{b}}{ds}h_{ab}(\vec{y}(s))\right)\right].

(36)

Under the symmetry operation (

) transforms in the following manner,

\displaystyle W_{h}^{\prime}=U_{h}~W_{h}~U^{\dagger}_{h}\ .

(37)

To compute the above after a Wick rotation
\pi_{ab}\rightarrow i\pi_{E}^{ab}
, we need to use the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula, which further requires the following commutator

\displaystyle\left[-2\xi_{d}\int d^{3}x~\partial_{c}\pi^{cd}_{E}(\vec{x}),{i\over 2}\int_{C}ds\frac{dy^{a}}{ds}\frac{dy^{b}}{ds}h_{ab}^{E}(\vec{y}(s))\right]

\displaystyle=i\xi_{b}\int d^{3}x\int_{C}ds\frac{dy^{a}}{ds}\frac{dy^{b}}{ds}~\partial_{a}\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}-\vec{y}(s)).

(38)

In deriving the above, we have used the commutation relation resulting from the uplifting of Poisson bracket between the conjugate variables (
63
). Generally speaking, within the canonical quantization framework, after solving the constraint equations one can construct the reduced phase space and then upgrade the Poisson bracket (

) to a commutator. After a Wick rotation the commutation relation becomes,

[h_{ab}^{E}(\vec{x},\tau),\pi^{cd}_{E}(\vec{y},\tau)]=\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{c}_{a}\delta^{d}_{b}+\delta^{d}_{a}\delta^{c}_{b}){\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})}.

(39)

Under the action of the shift symmetry operator, we observe that the Wilson line acquires a phase under the shift (similar to the case of free Maxwell theory) as

W_{h}^{\prime}=\exp\left(i~\xi_{b}\int_{C}ds\frac{dy^{a}}{ds}\frac{dy^{b}}{ds}\chi_{a}(\vec{y})\right)W_{h}.

(40)

where we have introduced

, following

as

\chi_{a}(\vec{y})=\int d^{3}x~\partial_{a}\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})=\int d^{2}x~n_{a}\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}-\vec{y}).

(41)

The above confirms that the shift symmetry is indeed a “global” symmetry for the Wilson line on the ADM hypersurface.

This generalized global symmetry would give rise to a Ward identity, regarded as a consequence of Noether’s theorem at the quantum level. The Ward identity can be computed from the invariance of correlators under this symmetry. We consider the expectation value of the Wilson line in the path integral framework as

\displaystyle\langle W_{h}[C]\rangle\sim\int{\cal D}h_{E}~W_{h}[C]\exp(-S_{E}[h]).

Ensuring the invariance of the expectation value under the shift of the induced metric (in the absence of anomalies)

\displaystyle\langle W_{h}[C]\rangle=\int{\cal D}h^{\prime}_{E}W_{h}^{\prime}[C]\exp(-S_{E}[h^{\prime}])=\int{\cal D}h_{E}W_{h}^{\prime}[C](1-\delta S_{E})\exp(-S_{E}[h]).

Here, we expand the transformed Wilson line as follows

\displaystyle W_{h}^{\prime}=\left(1+i~\xi_{b}\int_{C}ds\frac{dy^{a}}{ds}\frac{dy^{b}}{ds}\chi_{a}(\vec{y})\right)W_{h}.

Recalling the variation of action under the shift after a Wick rotation,

\delta S_{E}=\int d\tau\ d^{3}x\partial_{\tau}J^{0b}\xi_{b},

, we find the Ward identity as follows

\displaystyle\left\langle~\partial_{\tau}J^{0b}(\tau,\vec{x})~W_{h}[C]\right\rangle=\left\langle i\int_{C}ds~\partial_{a}\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})~\frac{dy^{a}}{ds}\frac{dy^{b}}{ds}~W_{h}[C]\right\rangle\Big|_{\tau=y_{0}}\ ,

The spontaneous breaking of this generalized global symmetry is described by the condensation of the Wilson line implying a non vanishing vacuum expectation value. The expectation value of the Wilson line may be regarded as an order parameter for the phase transition induced by the symmetry breaking. The Ward identity when written in momentum space will explicitly demonstrate the appearance of gapless Goldstone modes - a “Goldstone theorem” in the present context.

We first perform the Fourier transformation of the rank 2 tensorial current,

\tilde{J}^{0b}(p^{0},\vec{p})=\int d\tau~d^{3}x~e^{ip^{0}\tau+i\vec{p}.\vec{x}}J^{0b}(\tau,\vec{x})\ ,

which comes from (

(48)

Therefore, we can observe the existence of a pole at

This may be regarded as a Goldstone theorem, which implies the existence of gapless immobile particles on the ADM hypersurface. It would be interesting to consider how this Goldstone boson may be related to fractons to represent immobile excitations

Here, one may think that the Weinberg-Witten theorem disallows the emergence of gravity from a tensor gauge theory. Nevertheless, the theorem relies on two key assumptions: Lorentz invariance and the presence of asymptotic momentum eigenstates for particles. For fractonic theories, Lorentz invariance is broken and due to immobility isolated fractons do not possess a description in terms of momentum eigenstates, making the theorem not readily applicable in this scenario. Interestingly, these two conditions also hold for the ADM case. In a related direction,

investigates construction of low-energy effective theories characterized by the emergence of symmetries that give rise to fractonic modes from the spontaneous breaking of spatial translations, particularly those that exhibit gaplessness.

IV
Conclusion

This study originated from the observation that 0 and 1-form symmetries can also be studied from the commutation relation between the canonical conjugate pairs, where the canonical momentum plays the role of a generator of a shift transformation of the conjugate field. Our aim was to investigate if an analogous description exists for gravity within the Hamiltonian formulation. Interestingly, we find a tensorial rank 2 current which is conserved on-shell provided one has “nice” falloffs for arbitrary diffeomorphism of the boundary i.e. diffeomorphism that does not disturb the asymptotic structure of the spacetime at spatial infinity on any arbitrary Cauchy slice. The associated charge is subsequently used to define an extended topological operator on a codimension-2 surface at the boundary of spatial hypersurface. We subsequently see the effect of this symmetry operator on a nonlocal gauge invariant observable, namely the Wilson line in a theory of Euclidean gravity. We have not considered any possible large gauge transformations at the spatial boundary, which do not die off in asymptotic regions. Typically, these kinds of diffemorphisms are parametrized by an infinite family of functions that depend on angular coordinates at the boundary. The charges associated with global shift symmetry of the induced metric on the ADM hypersurface are not invariant under such large diffeomorphisms.

We emphasize the significant departure of our analysis from the usual 0 and 1-form symmetries that one sees in
U(1)
gauge theory.

•

Recall that, in free
U(1)
gauge theory, the equation of motion itself is identified as the current conservation equation associated with 1-form symmetry. In contrast to Maxwell electrodynamics, in the context of Einstein gravity in ADM foliation, the extended topological symmetry generator is defined at the boundary of the ADM hypersurface.

•

In Lorentzian spacetimes, we cannot have any physical particles (massless or massive) reaching spatial infinity–which is precisely where we eventually defined the topological symmetry generators. However, they will have nontrivial effect on Wilson lines in Euclidean spacetime where all asymptotic points are essentially spacelike.

Furthermore, we derived the corresponding Ward identity and showed that there appears a massless localized excitation on the ADM hypersurface when the gravitational Wilson line is condensed. One might be curious as to the applicability and the physical scenario where such operators and their effect on Euclidean Wilson lines might become relevant. The kind of conserved charges and extended operators discussed in this work will presumably play an important role in the physics of thermal systems, which typically requires one to Wick rotate the temporal coordinate and further compactify it on a circle. However, more sharp and concrete statements require more analysis, which we relegate to future work.

Appendix A
ADM formalism in (3+1) dimensions

In ADM formalism, the (3+1)-dimensional metric is foliated according to,

ds^{2}=-N^{2}dt^{2}+h_{ab}(dx^{a}+\mathcal{N}^{a}dt)(dx^{b}+\mathcal{N}^{b}dt),

(51)

where the Latin indices
a,b
run over all spatial indices.The inverse metric components are given by

g^{tt}=-\frac{1}{N^{2}}\ ;\quad g^{at}=\frac{\mathcal{N}^{a}}{N^{2}}\ ;\quad g^{ab}=h^{ab}-\frac{1}{N^{2}}\mathcal{N}^{a}\mathcal{N}^{b}\ .

(52)

With this decomposition, the three dimensional hypersurfaces at constant time are associated with an intrinsic curvature given by 3D Riemann tensor and an extrinsic curvature
K_{ab}
defined as

K_{ab}=\frac{1}{2N}(D_{a}N_{b}+D_{b}N_{a}-\dot{h}_{ab}),\quad K=h^{ab}K_{ab},

(53)

where
D_{a}
is the covariant derivative with respect to the induced spatial metric
h_{ab}
. The extrinsic curvature arises from the embedding of hypersurfaces in 4D spacetimes. As a result, we can rewrite the Einstein-Hilbert action in terms of the ADM variables as follows

S_{ADM}^{\text{bulk}}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\int dt\ d^{3}x\sqrt{h}N(\bar{R}+K^{ab}K_{ab}-K^{2}),

(54)

where
\bar{R}
is the Ricci scalar of the hypersurface and
\kappa=16\pi G_{N}
and will set to be unity in the future onward.
This ADM action can be also expressed in terms of the DeWitt metric,

G_{abcd}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{h}}(h_{ac}h_{bd}+h_{ad}h_{bc}-h_{ab}h_{cd})\ ,

(55)

as follows

\displaystyle S_{ADM}^{\text{bulk}}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\int dt\ d^{3}x\ \left[N\sqrt{h}(G^{abcd}K_{ab}K_{cd}+\bar{R})\right].

(56)

The Hamiltonian can be derived by performing the Legendre transformation of the ADM action. This transformation requires a set of conjugate momenta
\pi^{ab}
that describes how the spatial metric changes over time and is given by

\pi^{ab}=\frac{\delta L}{\delta\dot{h}_{ab}}=-\sqrt{h}(K^{ab}-h^{ab}K).

(57)

The phase space will be described in terms of conjugate variables (
h_{ab},\pi^{ab}
). Consequently, we can express the Hamiltonian arising from the action in Eq. (
54
) as

H_{ADM}=\int_{\Sigma}d^{3}x~(N{\cal H}+{\cal N}^{a}{\cal H}_{a}),

(58)

where

{\cal H}=\sqrt{h}(K^{ab}K_{ab}-K^{2}-\bar{R})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}G_{abcd}\pi^{ab}\pi^{cd}-\sqrt{h}~\bar{R}\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\cal H}^{b}=-2D_{a}\pi^{ab}.

(59)

Taking into consideration the definition of the Hamiltonian, we can also write the ADM action (
54
) in first order form as

S_{ADM}^{\text{bulk}}=\int dt\ d^{3}x\ \left[\pi^{ab}\dot{h}_{ab}-N\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{N}^{a}\mathcal{H}_{a}\right],

(60)

which, upon variation with respect to the lapse and shift, yields the well-known Hamiltonian and momentum constraints,

{\cal H}=0,\qquad{\cal H}_{a}=0.

(61)

Variation with respect to
\pi^{ab}
and
h_{ab}
, results in Hamilton’s equations of motion,

\displaystyle\dot{h}_{ab}=\frac{\delta H}{\delta\pi^{ab}}=\{h_{ab},H_{\text{ADM}}\},

\displaystyle\dot{\pi}^{ab}=-\frac{\delta H}{\delta h_{ab}}=\{\pi^{ab},H_{\text{ADM}}\},

(62)

where the equal time Poisson bracket between the canonical variables is given by

\{h_{ab}(\vec{x}),\pi^{cd}(\vec{y})\}=\frac{1}{2}(\delta^{c}_{a}\delta^{d}_{b}+\delta^{d}_{a}\delta^{c}_{b}){\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})}.

(63)

In the presence of a boundary, one has to add a boundary term to the bulk Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle H_{ADM}^{\rm Bdy}=-\frac{2}{\kappa}\oint d^{2}x(N\sqrt{s}K_{S}-{\mathcal{N}}_{a}\pi^{ab}r_{b}),

(64)

where
s_{ab}
is the metric on the sphere at the boundary of the spacelike hypersurface,
K_{S}
is the extrinsic curvature of this boundary, and
r^{a}
is the unit normal to the boundary. One can think of this piece as the on-shell
boundary Hamiltonian
after imposing the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints. The boundary piece in (
64
) is associated with asymptotic time translation and is related to the ADM energy

\displaystyle E_{\text{ADM}}=-\frac{2}{\kappa}\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\oint d^{2}x\sqrt{s}(K_{S}-K_{S}^{0})

(65)

for static spacetimes. Note that, in the above, one has to regularize the ADM energy by subtracting out the “infinity” coming from the vacuum, denoted by
K_{S}^{0}
.

An Improved Approximation for Maximum Weighted k k -Set Packing
Theophile Thiery, Justin Ward

Abstract
We consider the weighted k k -set packing problem, in which we are given a collection of weighted sets, each with at most k k elements and must return a collection of pairwise disjoint sets with maximum total weight. For k = 3 k=3 , this problem generalizes the classical 3-dimensional matching problem listed as one of the Karp’s original 21 NP-complete problems. We give an algorithm attaining an approximation factor of 1.786 1.786 for weighted 3-set packing, improving on the recent best result of 2 − 1 63,700,992 2-\frac{1}{63,700,992} due to Neuwohner. Our algorithm is based on the local search procedure of Berman that attempts to improve the sum of squared weights rather than the problem’s objective. When using exchanges of size at most k k , this algorithm attains an approximation factor of k + 1 2 \frac{k+1}{2} . Using exchanges of size k 2 ​ ( k − 1 ) + k k^{2}(k-1)+k , we provide a relatively simple analysis to obtain an approximation factor of 1.811 when k = 3 k=3 . We then show that the tools we develop can be adapted to larger exchanges of size 2 ​ k 2 ​ ( k − 1 ) + k 2k^{2}(k-1)+k to give an approximation factor of 1.786. Although our primary focus is on the case k = 3 k=3 , our approach in fact gives slightly stronger improvements on the factor k + 1 2 \frac{k+1}{2} for all k > 3 k>3 . As in previous works, our guarantees hold also for the more general problem of finding a maximum weight independent set in a ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -claw free graph.

Berman’s algorithm in fact applies to the more general problem of finding a

In this vocabulary, Berman’s local search algorithm squares the weight of all vertices of the graph and then considers a restricted set of “claw swaps.” For each vertex

Our Results

Given this stream of recent progress in the asymptotic approximability of the weighted
k
-set packing for large
k
, it is natural to ask whether it is possible to obtain significant improvements in approximation specifically in the case of small
k
.
Here we answer this question affirmatively, by giving two new approximation guarantees for the weighted
k
-set packing problem by using a variant of Berman’s squared-weight local search with larger exchanges. We first present a relatively simple analysis showing that exchanges adding up to
k^{2}(k-1)+k
sets is sufficient to obtain a factor 1.811 for weighted 3-set packing, improving on the factor
\frac{k+1}{2}=2
by 0.189. We then show that by refining our basic analysis, it is possible to attain a 1.786-approximation using swaps of size
2k^{2}(k-1)+k
. Our results imply better improvements for
k>3
, and we show that our algorithms’ guarantees improve asymptotically to
(k+\frac{1}{2})/2
and
(k+\frac{1}{3})/2
, respectively, as
k
grows. We summarize our results in the following theorem.

Further related work

Nearly all algorithmic results for both the
k
-set packing problem and the maximum independent set problem in
(k+1)
-claw free graphs are based on variants of local search and greedy algorithms. In the unweighted setting, a simple local-search attempting to swap at most
2
vertices into the current solution yields a
\frac{k+1+\varepsilon}{2}
-approximation. Hurkens and Schrijver
[
13
]
showed that by considering swaps that add
O(\varepsilon^{-1})
vertices gives a
\frac{k+\varepsilon}{2}
-approximation. They also show that their analysis is tight, in the sense that any local-search which swaps a constant number of vertices has approximation factor at least
\frac{k+\varepsilon}{2}

[
13
]
.
In contrast, Halldórson
[
11
]
proved that a pure local search algorithm performing non-constant size swaps
\Omega(\log n)
achieves a
\frac{k+2+\varepsilon}{3}
-approximation. This analysis was refined by Cygan et al.
[
8
]
to obtain a ratio equal to
\frac{k+1+\varepsilon}{3}
. Due to the large swap sizes, the previous two results yield quasi-polynomial time algorithms. Sviridenko and Ward
[
19
]
and Cygan
[
7
]
designed polynomial-time local search algorithms with approximation factors of
\frac{k+2+\varepsilon}{3}
and
\frac{k+1+\varepsilon}{3}
, respectively, by using techniques from fixed-parameter tractability. Fürer and Yu
[
10
]
gave a
\frac{k+1+\varepsilon}{3}
approximation algorithm with improved dependence on
\varepsilon
and also gave an instance with locality gap
\frac{k+1}{3}
for any algorithm using swaps of size
O(n^{1/5})
. For all algorithms considering swaps of size
O(\log n)
rely on the underlying structure specific to the
k
-set packing problem to find swaps in polynomial time, and thus do not generalize to the maximum independent set problem in
(k+1)
-claw free graphs.

In the weighted setting, Arkin and Hassin showed that the standard weighted local-search algorithm performing swaps of size
O(\varepsilon^{-1})
yields only a
k-1+\varepsilon
approximation
[
1
]
. Chandra and Halldórson
[
6
]
showed that the associated locality gap could be circumvented by combining a greedy algorithm followed by a local-improvement strategy that always selects the best improvement at each stage, yielding a
\frac{2(k+1)+\varepsilon}{3}
-approximation. As we have already noted, Berman
[
2
]
obtained a
\frac{k+1}{2}
approximation by considering a local search guided by the squared weights and swaps of size
k
. For smaller swaps of size 2, Berman and Krysta
[
4
]
showed that a local search guided by
w^{\alpha}
, for an appropriately chosen
1<\alpha<2
has an approximation factor of
0.667k
,
0.651k
, and
0.646k
for
k=3
,
k=4
, and
k>4
, respectively.

The
k
-set packing problem has also been studied via linear programming hierarchies. In this context, Chan and Lau
[
5
]
give an LP-rounding algorithm with approximation ratio
k-1+\frac{1}{k}
for
k
-set packing and
k-1
for
k
-dimensional matching. They also show that even after the linear program is strengthened by a linear number of rounds of the Sherali-Adams lifting procedure, its integrality gap remains at least
k-2
. In contrast, they show that by including a polynomial number of extra constraints, the integrality gap can be reduced to
\frac{k+1}{2}
. Singh and Talwar
[
18
]
showed that the same integrality gap of
\frac{k+1}{2}
can be achieved by applying
O(k^{2})
rounds of Chvátal-Gomory cuts to natural LP for the
k
-set packing problem.

for all

for all

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose

2w(O)\leq w(A)+\sum_{o\in O}w(N(o,A))-\sum_{a\in A}\left[\frac{\Delta_{a}}{w_{a}}+\frac{\Psi_{a}}{w_{a}}\right].

Proof.

Fix a single claw
C_{a}
and

. Then,

\displaystyle 2w_{o}w_{a}

\displaystyle=w_{o}^{2}+w_{a}^{2}-(w_{o}-w_{a})^{2}

(2)

\displaystyle=w_{o}^{2}+w_{a}^{2}-(w_{o}-w_{a})^{2}-w^{2}(N(o,A-a))+w^{2}(N(o,A-a))

\displaystyle\qquad-w_{a}w(N(o,A-a))+w_{a}w(N(o,A-a))

\displaystyle=w_{o}^{2}+w_{a}^{2}-w^{2}(N(o,A-a))+w_{a}w(N(o,A-a))-\psi_{a,o}.

Equation (
2
) holds for every

. Summing over all

\displaystyle 2w_{a}w(C_{a})

\displaystyle=|C_{a}|w_{a}^{2}+w^{2}(C_{a})-\sum_{o\in C_{a}}w^{2}(N(o,A-a))+w_{a}\sum_{o\in C_{a}}w(N(o,A-a))-\Psi_{a}

\displaystyle\leq(|C_{a}|+1)w_{a}^{2}+w^{2}(C_{a})-w^{2}(N^{+}_{a})+w_{a}\sum_{o\in C_{a}}w(N(o,A-a))-\Psi_{a}

Lemma 4.4
.

For any undirected tree

\sum_{(a,b)\in E(T)}w_{a}\geq\frac{(t-1)(1-\varepsilon)}{t-\varepsilon}\sum_{v\in V(T)}w_{v}.

Proof.

Let
r
be a vertex of
T
with minimum weight, and fix some edge
(r,x)\in E(T)
. For each remaining arc
(a,b)\in E(T)-(r,x)
, consider the unique

be the vertex at the end of this path. Note that every vertex of

. Moreover, if

, then

, and if

. Let

. Then, by the above discussion,

. Consider the function:

f(z)=\frac{w_{r}+z}{w_{r}+w_{x}+(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}z}\leq\frac{\sum_{(a,b)\in E(T)}w_{a}}{\sum_{v\in V(T)}w_{v}}.

To complete the proof it suffices to show that

. Observe that since

(1-\varepsilon)w_{x}\leq w_{r}\leq w_{x}
, and so

. Thus, for

where the second inequality follows again from

.

Lemma 4.5

Suppose that

and for
0\leq t\leq k
, define
\rho_{t}\triangleq\frac{t(\varepsilon-\delta)}{1-\delta}-\frac{\varepsilon-\delta}{1-\varepsilon}
. Then, for any
a\in I,

\frac{\Psi_{a}}{w_{a}}\geq\rho_{|C_{a}|}w_{a}+\delta\sum_{o\in C_{a}}w(N(o,A-a)).

The crucial observation that Lemma
4.5
captures is that the slack for isolated vertices is non-trivial. While this observation may seem obvious, we point out a subtle difficulty in handling it. Looking at bound in Lemma
3.1
, we see that for any
a\in A
,
w_{a}
appears once in the bound for each
o\in N(a,O)
. In particular, the weight
w_{a}
appears
\left|C_{a}\right|+\left|N(a,O\backslash C_{a})\right|\leq k
times. If
a
is isolated, then (
7
) implies that our bound will be instead
(\left|C_{a}\right|+\left|N(a,O\backslash C_{a})\right|(1-\varepsilon))w_{a}
. The issue is that when
\left|N(a,O\backslash C_{a})\right|
is small, we do not save enough to make any progress. To handle this situation, each vertex will keep a fraction of the slack equal to the parameter
\rho_{\left|C_{a}\right|}
in Lemma
4.5
, leaving a smaller amount
(1-\delta)w_{b}
for other isolated vertices
b\in N_{a}^{+}\backslash\{a\}
, where
\delta\leq\varepsilon
. The parameter
\delta
can intuitively be thought of as a way of dividing the slack from
\Psi_{a}
into one portion that pays for
a
and another that will help pay for other isolated claws.

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

Fix
a\in I
and consider any
o\in C_{a}
. Since
a\in I
, Equation (
7
) implies that

\psi_{a,o}=(w_{a}-w_{o})^{2}+w_{a}w(N(o,A-a))-w^{2}(N(o,A-a))\geq(w_{a}-w_{o})^{2}+\varepsilon w_{a}w(N(o,A-a))

for every
o\in C_{a}
and so

\Psi_{a}\geq\sum_{o\in C_{a}}(w_{a}-w_{o})^{2}+\varepsilon w_{a}\sum_{o\in C_{a}}w(N(o,A-a)).

Define
\alpha_{o}\triangleq w_{o}/w_{a}
and
\beta\triangleq\sum_{o\in C_{a}}w(N(o,A-a))/w_{a}
. Then we can reformulate the above inequality as

\displaystyle\Psi_{a}\geq\sum_{o\in C_{a}}(w_{a}-\alpha_{o}w_{a})^{2}+\varepsilon w_{a}\beta w_{a}

\displaystyle=w_{a}^{2}\left(\sum_{o\in C_{a}}(1-\alpha_{o})^{2}+\varepsilon\beta\right)

(8)

\displaystyle=w_{a}^{2}\left(\sum_{o\in C_{a}}(1-\alpha_{o})^{2}+(\varepsilon-\delta)\beta\right)+w_{a}^{2}\delta\beta.

We now lower bound the bracketed expression on the right. Since
A
is locally optimal with respect to 1-exchanges, (
1
) and (
7
) imply that

\sum_{o\in C_{a}}w^{2}_{o}=w^{2}(C_{a})\leq w^{2}(N^{+}_{a})\leq w_{a}^{2}+\sum_{\mathclap{o\in C_{a}}}w^{2}(N(o,A-a))\leq w_{a}^{2}+\sum_{\mathclap{o\in C_{a}}}w_{a}(1-\varepsilon)w(N(o,A-a)).

Reformulating this inequality in terms of the values
\alpha_{o}
and
\beta
, gives us the following constraint:

\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\alpha_{o}^{2}w_{a}^{2}\leq w_{a}^{2}+(1-\varepsilon)w_{a}^{2}\beta.

Dividing through by
w_{a}^{2}
and then rearranging, we obtain
\beta\geq\frac{\left(\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\alpha_{o}^{2}\right)-1}{1-\varepsilon}
. Then, since
\varepsilon-\delta\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{o\in C_{a}}(1-\alpha_{o})^{2}+(\varepsilon-\delta)\beta

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{o\in C_{a}}(1-\alpha_{o})^{2}+(\varepsilon-\delta)\frac{\left(\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\alpha_{o}^{2}\right)-1}{1-\varepsilon}

(9)

\displaystyle=|C_{a}|-2\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\alpha_{o}+\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\alpha^{2}_{o}+(\varepsilon-\delta)\frac{\left(\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\alpha_{o}^{2}\right)-1}{1-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=|C_{a}|-2\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\alpha_{o}+\frac{1-\delta}{1-\varepsilon}\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\alpha_{o}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon-\delta}{1-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq|C_{a}|-2\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\alpha_{o}+\frac{1-\delta}{1-\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{1}{|C_{a}|}\left(\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\alpha_{o}\right)^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon-\delta}{1-\varepsilon},

where the last inequality follows from Cauchy-Schwarz. We can express the above lower bound as
f(x)
where
f(x)=\frac{1-\delta}{|C_{a}|(1-\varepsilon)}x^{2}-2x+|C_{a}|-\frac{\varepsilon-\delta}{1-\varepsilon}
and
x=\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\alpha_{o}
. Then, note that
\frac{d^{2}f}{dx^{2}}=\frac{2(1-\delta)}{|C_{a}|(1-\varepsilon)}>0
, so
f
is convex in
x
, and when
x=\frac{|C_{a}|(1-\varepsilon)}{1-\delta}
, we have

\frac{df}{dx}=\frac{2(1-\delta)x}{|C_{a}|(1-\varepsilon)}-2=0
. Thus,

|C_{a}|-2\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\alpha_{o}+\frac{1-\delta}{|C_{a}|(1-\varepsilon)}\left(\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\alpha_{o}\right)^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon-\delta}{1-\varepsilon}\\
=f\left(\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\alpha_{o}\right)\geq f\left(\frac{|C_{a}|(1-\varepsilon)}{1-\delta}\right)=\frac{|C_{a}|(\varepsilon-\delta)}{1-\delta}-\frac{\varepsilon-\delta}{1-\varepsilon}=\rho_{|C_{a}|}.

(10)

Combining the inequalities (
8
), (
9
), and (
10
) we finally have

\frac{\Psi_{a}}{w_{a}}\geq\frac{\rho_{|C_{a}|}w_{a}^{2}+\delta\beta w_{a}^{2}}{w_{a}}=\rho_{|C_{a}|}w_{a}+\delta\sum_{o\in C_{a}}w(N(o,A-a)),

as required.
∎

We now combine the previous results into the following bound for the total slack associated with all vertices
a\in I
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose that
A
is locally optimal with respect to 1-exchanges.
Let
0\leq\delta\leq\varepsilon
, let
B
be any set of vertices in
H_{\varepsilon}
, and for
0\leq t\leq k
, define
\rho_{t}\triangleq\frac{t(\varepsilon-\delta)}{1-\delta}-\frac{\varepsilon-\delta}{1-\varepsilon}
. Then,

\sum_{a\in I}\left[\frac{\Delta_{a}}{w_{a}}+\frac{\Psi_{a}}{w_{a}}\right]\geq\sum_{a\in I}\left(\rho_{|C_{a}|}-\delta|C_{a}|\right)w_{a}+\sum_{a\in I}\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\delta w(N(o,B)).

Proof.

For each
a\in I
, Lemma
4.5
gives

\frac{\Delta_{a}}{w_{a}}+\frac{\Psi_{a}}{w_{a}}\geq\rho_{|C_{a}|}w_{a}+\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\delta w(N(o,A-a))\\
=\rho_{|C_{a}|}w_{a}-\delta|C_{a}|w_{a}+\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\delta w(N(o,A))\geq\rho_{|C_{a}|}w_{a}-\delta|C_{a}|w_{a}+\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\delta w(N(o,B)).

(11)

The lemma then follows by summing (

) over all

.
∎

4.3
Combining the Bounds

We now combine the bounds on the slack for isolated and non-isolated claws given by Lemmas
4.4
and
4.6
with Lemma
3.1
to obtain a guarantee for Algorithm
2.1
. In the following, we will set the parameter
\delta
according to
\varepsilon
as
\delta\triangleq 1-\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}
. Then, straightforward algebraic manipulations (given in Appendix
A.1
) lead to the following:

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
0\leq\varepsilon<1
and set
\delta=1-\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}
. Then,
\delta\leq\varepsilon
and for all
0\leq t\leq k
,

\rho_{t}\triangleq\frac{t(\varepsilon-\delta)}{1-\delta}-\frac{\varepsilon-\delta}{1-\varepsilon}=\left(t-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}}\right)(1-\sqrt{1-\varepsilon})=\left(t-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}}\right)\delta.

In particular, for all
0\leq t\leq k
,
\rho_{t}=\rho_{k}-(k-t)\delta
.

Having set
\delta=1-\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}
, we can now state our main result for this section. Theorem
4.8
proves that the approximation factor is a tradeoff between isolated and non-isolated claws.

Theorem 4.8
.

For any
0\leq\varepsilon\leq 1/2
, if
A
is locally optimal under
s
-exchanges for
s\geq 1+k(k-1)
then,

w(O)\leq\left[\tfrac{k+1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\min\left\{\tfrac{1-\varepsilon}{2-\varepsilon},\rho_{k}\right\}\right]w(A),

where
\rho_{k}=(k-1/\sqrt{1-\varepsilon})(1-\sqrt{1-\varepsilon})
.

Proof.

Set
\delta=1-\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}
and observe that
\delta\leq\varepsilon
. Then, by Lemma
4.2
,

\sum_{a\in D}\left[\frac{\Delta_{a}}{w_{a}}+\frac{\Psi_{a}}{w_{a}}\right]\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2-\varepsilon}w(D)+\sum_{a\in D}\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\varepsilon w(N(o,I))\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2-\varepsilon}w(D)+\sum_{a\in D}\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\delta w(N(o,I)),

and by Lemma
4.6
(with
B=I
),

\sum_{a\in I}\left[\frac{\Delta_{a}}{w_{a}}+\frac{\Psi_{a}}{w_{a}}\right]\geq\sum_{a\in I}\left(\rho_{|C_{a}|}-\delta|C_{a}|\right)w_{a}+\sum_{a\in I}\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\delta w(N(o,I)).

Recall that, by Lemma
4.7
, when
\delta=1-\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}
,
\rho_{t}=\rho_{k}-(k-t)\delta
for
0\leq t\leq k
. Thus,
\rho_{|C_{a}|}-\delta|C_{a}|=\rho_{k}-\delta(k-|C_{a}|)-\delta|C_{a}|=\rho_{k}-k\delta
. Combining this with the 2 bounds above and recalling that every
o\in O
appears in
C_{a}
, for exactly one
a\in A
, then gives

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in A}\left[\frac{\Delta_{a}}{w_{a}}+\frac{\Psi_{a}}{w_{a}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2-\varepsilon}w(D)+\sum_{a\in I}(\rho_{|C_{a}|}-|C_{a}|\delta)w_{a}+\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{o\in C_{a}}\delta w(N(o,I))

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2-\varepsilon}w(D)+(\rho_{k}-k\delta)w(I)+\sum_{o\in O}\delta w(N(o,I)).

Combining this bound with Lemma
3.1
we then have:

\displaystyle 2w(O)

\displaystyle\leq w(A)+\sum_{o\in O}w(N(o,A))-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2-\varepsilon}w(D)-(\rho_{k}-k\delta)w(I)-\sum_{o\in O}\delta w(N(o,I))

\displaystyle=w(A)+\sum_{o\in O}w(N(o,A\backslash I))+(1-\delta)\sum_{o\in O}w(N(o,I))-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2-\varepsilon}w(D)-(\rho_{k}-k\delta)w(I)

\displaystyle\leq w(A)+kw(A\backslash I)+k(1-\delta)w(I)-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2-\varepsilon}w(D)-(\rho_{k}-k\delta)w(I)

\displaystyle=w(A)+kw(A)-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2-\varepsilon}w(D)-\rho_{k}w(I)\leq(k+1)w(A)-\min\left\{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2-\varepsilon},\rho_{k}\right\}w(A),

as required.
∎

5
Further improving the bound

The main bottleneck in our analysis of non-isolated claws occurs when

for all

(12)

Lemma 5.2
.

Consider the connected components of

. For each connected component that is not an isolated vertex or an isolated edge, there is a collection of subgraphs

Lemma 5.3

A.2
Bounding on the number of swaps performed by Algorithm

In all of our preceding analysis, we have relied only on local optimality of the set
A
produced by Algorithm
2.1
, without considering the time required to converge to such a local optimum. Here, we show that the weight-scaling argument used by Berman
[

[
2

.

Suppose that any
A
that is locally optimal with respect to the improvements considered by Algorithm
2.1
for a weight function
w
satisfies
\alpha w(A)\geq w(O)
for some approximation factor

. Let

be a given claw-free graph with weights
w_{v}

and then set

\tilde{w}_{v}\triangleq\left\lfloor dw_{v}\right\rfloor

2.1
to this new instance, starting from the solution

2.1

\tilde{w}(S)\leq dw(S)\leq dw(O)

\tilde{w}^{2}(O)-\tilde{w}^{2}(S_{0})\leq\tilde{w}^{2}(O)\leq k\tilde{w}^{2}(S_{0})\leq k\tilde{w}(S_{0})^{2}\leq k\left(dw(S_{0})\right)^{2}=kn^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}

w
is also greedy solution for weight function
\tilde{w}^{2}
. The inequality then follows since the greedy algorithm has an approximation factor of at most
k

(k+1)
-claw free graphs.

A

2.1

G

\tilde{w}

\tilde{w}

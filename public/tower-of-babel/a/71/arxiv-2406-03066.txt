Statistics for 3 3 -isogeny induced Selmer groups of elliptic curves
Pratiksha Shingavekar

Abstract
. Given a sixth power free integer a a , let E a E_{a} be the elliptic curve defined by y 2 = x 3 + a y^{2}=x^{3}+a . We prove explicit results for the lower density of sixth power free integers a a for which the 3 3 -isogeny induced Selmer group of E a E_{a} over ℚ ⁡ ( μ 3 ) \mathbb{Q}(\mu_{3}) has dimension ≤ 1 \leq 1 . The results are proven by refining the strategy of Davenport–Heilbronn, by relating the statistics for integral binary cubic forms to the statistics for 3 3 -isogeny induced Selmer groups.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and historical background

The Mordell–Weil rank of an elliptic curve
E
defined over a number field
F
is of natural arithmetic interest. Given
n>0
, the
n
-Selmer group of
E/F
gives an upper bound on the rank of the Mordell–Weil group
E(F)
. These Selmer groups can be viewed as
random objects
, as the elliptic curves in question vary in natural families, ordered according to their discriminant. This average behaviour of Selmer groups have generated significant interest in recent years, thanks to the celebrated works of Bhargava–Shankar
[
BS15a
,
BS15b
,
BS13
]
, Bhargava–Elkies–Schnidman
[
BES20
]
, Alpöge–Bhargava–Schnidman
[
ABS22
]
, etc.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with a rational
3
-isogeny
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
. Then
E
can be written either in the form
E_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+a
where
a\in\mathbb{Z}
or
E_{a,b}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+a(x-b)^{2}
where
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
. Over the field
\mathbb{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{3})
, the curve
E_{a}
is
3
-isogenous to itself. The isogeny
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E_{a}\rightarrow E_{a}
is explicitly given as follows

\varphi(x,y)=\left(\frac{x^{3}+4a}{\varpi^{2}x^{2}},\frac{y(x^{3}-8a)}{\varpi^{3}x^{3}}\right),

where
\varpi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1-e^{2\pi i/3}
. Associated with
\varphi
, the
\varphi
-Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}^{\varphi}(E_{a}/\mathbb{K})
is a global Galois cohomology group satisfying local conditions, and sits in a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{E^{\prime}(\mathbb{K})}/{\varphi(E(\mathbb{K}))}\longrightarrow{\rm Sel}^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{K})\longrightarrow\Sh(E/\mathbb{K})[\varphi]\longrightarrow 0.

The advantage of studying these Selmer groups over the field
\mathbb{K}
is that even when the elliptic curve
E_{a}
has rank
0
, one can still obtain interesting lower and upper bounds for the
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-dimensions of these Selmer groups in terms of
3
-ranks of class groups of certain quadratic extensions of
\mathbb{K}
, cf.
[
JMS22
]
for further details. In this article, we shall study distribution questions for these
\varphi
-Selmer groups over
\mathbb{K}
, as
a
ranges over all
6
-th power free integers.

1.2.
Main results

We state our main results. Given a sixth power free integer
a
, set
L_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{3},\sqrt{a})
and let
r_{3}(L_{a})
be the
3
-rank of the class group of
L_{a}
. Note that the discriminant of
E_{a}
is
\Delta_{E_{a}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-432a^{2}
. Given a real number
X>0
, we set
\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)
to be the set of all isomorphism classes of elliptic curves
E_{a}
with
|\Delta_{E_{a}}|\leq X
. We identify
\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)
with

(1.1)

\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{a\in\mathbb{Z}\mid a\text{ is sixth power free and }432a^{2}\leq X\}.

Theorem A
.

[Theorem
4.3
]
There is a positive density of
6
-th power free integers
a
, such that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}\operatorname{Sel}^{\varphi}(E_{a}/\mathbb{K})\in\{r_{3}(L_{a}),r_{3}(L_{a})+1\}.

More precisely,

\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\#\left\{a\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)\mid\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}\operatorname{Sel}^{\varphi}(E_{a}/\mathbb{K})\in\{r_{3}(L_{a}),r_{3}(L_{a})+1\}\right\}}{\#\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)}\right)\geq\frac{\zeta(6)}{64\zeta(2)}.

Theorem B
.

[Theorem
4.9
]
There is a positive density of sixth power free integers
a
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}\operatorname{Sel}^{\varphi}(E_{a}/\mathbb{K})\leq 1
. In greater detail,

\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\left\{a\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)\mid\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}\operatorname{Sel}^{\varphi}(E_{a}/\mathbb{K})\leq 1\right\}}{\#\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)}\geq\frac{3\zeta(6)}{128\pi^{2}}.

Theorem C
.

[Theorem
4.10
]
There is a positive density of sixth power free integers
a
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}\operatorname{Sel}_{3}(E_{a}/\mathbb{K})\leq 2
. More precisely, one has

\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\left\{a\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)\mid\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}\operatorname{Sel}_{3}(E_{a}/\mathbb{K})\leq 2\right\}}{\#\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)}\geq\frac{3\zeta(6)}{128\pi^{2}}.

1.3.
Methodology and related work

Certain upper and lower bounds for
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}\operatorname{Sel}^{\varphi}(E_{a}/\mathbb{K})
and

were recently obtained by Jha, Majumdar and Shingavekar
[

Families of elliptic curves with rational

Throughout this article, we let

In the second case when

where

Refer to such curves as

. In other words,

\mathcal{C}_{2}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mid abd\neq 0\text{, }\operatorname{gcd}(a,b)\text{ is squarefree and }16a^{2}b^{3}d\leq X\}.

[
V7́1
]

2.2.
Selmer groups and Tate–Shafarevich groups

The algebraic structure of the Mordell-Weil group of an elliptic curve can be studied using Galois theoretic techniques. Associated to an elliptic curve
E
, one considers Selmer groups, which are Galois cohomology groups with prescribed local conditions. In this subsection, we introduce the Selmer groups associated to an isogeny between elliptic curves. Let
F
be a number field and denote by
\Sigma_{F}
the set of places of
F
. Given
v\in\Sigma_{F}
, set
F_{v}
to denote the completion of
F
at
v
. Let
\bar{F}
be a choice of algebraic closure of
F
and likewise
\bar{F}_{v}
an algebraic closure of
F_{v}
. Set
\operatorname{G}_{F}
(resp.
\operatorname{G}_{F_{v}}
) to denote the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{F}/F)
(resp.
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{F}_{v}/F_{v})
). For each
v\in\Sigma_{F}
, choose an embedding
\iota_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bar{F}\hookrightarrow\bar{F}_{v}
. Note that this induces an embedding
\iota_{v}^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{F_{v}}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{G}_{F}
of Galois groups. Thus we get a restriction map on Galois cohomology

\operatorname{res}_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{F},\cdot)\rightarrow H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{F_{v}},\cdot).

Let
E
,
E^{\prime}
be elliptic curves over
F
and
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\to E^{\prime}
be an isogeny over
F
. For
\mathcal{F}\in\{F,F_{v}\}
, consider the long exact sequence
\operatorname{G}_{\mathcal{F}}
-modules associated to

0\rightarrow E[\varphi]\rightarrow E\xrightarrow{\varphi}E^{\prime}\rightarrow 0.

This induces the following Kummer map

\delta_{\mathcal{F}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E^{\prime}(\mathcal{F})\longrightarrow E^{\prime}(\mathcal{F})/\varphi(E(\mathcal{F}))\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\overline{\delta}_{\mathcal{F}}}H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathcal{F}},E[\varphi]).

From this, we arrive at the commutative diagram

We now give the definition of the
\varphi
-Selmer group of

over

.

Definition 2.1
.

With respect to notation above, we set

{\rm Sel}^{\varphi}(E/F)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{c\in H^{1}(G_{F},E[\varphi])\mid{\rm res}_{v}(c)\in{\rm Im}\ \delta_{F_{v}}\text{ for every }v\in\Sigma_{F}\}.

On the other hand, recall that the

of

\Sh(E/F)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\text{Ker}\big(H^{1}(G_{F},E)\to\underset{v\in\Sigma_{F}}{\prod}H^{1}(G_{F_{v}},E)\big).

(2.4)

0\longrightarrow{E^{\prime}(F)}/{\varphi(E(F))}\longrightarrow{\rm Sel}^{\varphi}(E/F)\longrightarrow\Sh(E/F)[\varphi]\longrightarrow 0

3.
Class group statistics

In this section, we discuss the method of Davenport
[
Dav51a
,
Dav51b
]
and Davenport–Heilbronn
[
DH69
,
DH71
]
who study the statistics for the
3
-parts of class numbers of quadratic number fields. Given real numbers
\xi<\eta
, let
N_{3}(\xi,\eta)
be the number of conjugacy classes of cubic fields

with discriminant
\Delta_{K}\in(\xi,\eta)

Theorem 3.1

(Davenport–Heilbronn)
.

With respect to notation above,

\begin{split}&\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}X^{-1}N_{3}(0,X)=\left(12\zeta(3)\right)^{-1},\\
&\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}X^{-1}N_{3}(-X,0)=\left(4\zeta(3)\right)^{-1}.\\
\end{split}

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a cubic extension and
\widetilde{K}
be the Galois closure of
K
. Then, either
K=\widetilde{K}
and
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
, or
\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{K}/\mathbb{Q})\simeq S_{3}
. In the latter case, there is a triplet of fields that are conjugate to
K
. This triple is counted once. Let
\Phi
be the set of all equivalence classes of irreducible primitive binary cubic forms

F(x,y)=ax^{3}+bx^{2}y+cxy^{2}+dy^{3},

where
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z}
. Two forms
F(x,y)
and
G(x,y)
are equivalent if there exists an integral matrix
A=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}e&f\\
g&h\\
\end{array}}\right)
with determinant
\pm 1
which transforms
G(x,y)
to
F(x,y)
. In other words,

F(x,y)=G(ex+fy,gx+hy).

The discriminant of

is

D=b^{2}c^{2}+18abcd-27a^{2}d^{2}-4b^{3}d-4c^{3}a.

Let
K
be a cubic field over
\mathbb{Q}
and let

be an integral basis of
K
. For
\alpha\in K
, let
\mathfrak{d}(\alpha)
denote the discriminant of

. Then following
[

]

The form
F_{K}
is irreducible and integral. Moreover, it satisfies local conditions at each prime
p
, which we proceed to now describe. Given
F(x,y),G(x,y)\in\Phi
and a natural number
m
, we write

F(x,y)\equiv G(x,y)\pmod{m}

if the coefficients of
F(x,y)
are congruent to those of
G(x,y)
modulo
m
. Given a prime
p
define a symbol
(F,p)
as follows. Write
(F,p)=(111)

where
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
and
\lambda_{3}
are three linear forms that have no constant quotient. Likewise, write
(F,p)=(12)
to mean that

F(x,y)\equiv\lambda(x,y)\kappa(x,y)\pmod{p},

where
\lambda(x,y)
is a linear form and
\kappa(x,y)
is an irreducible quadratic form. Write
(F,p)=(3)

itself is irreducible modulo

It conveniences us to prove a general result about the density of a set of integers defined by congruence conditions at every prime
p
. When there are only finitely many primes at which local conditions are defined, the density is easy to work out, and is a consequence of the Chinese remainder theorem. When there are infinitely many conditions, we require that some additional conditions are satisfied, which we now describe in greater detail.

Let
c,d
be real numbers such that
c<d
and
\Omega(X)
be the set of integers
a\in(cX,dX)
. At each prime
p
, we consider a local condition
\Phi_{p}
defined by a finite set of congruence classes
\{\phi_{1}^{(p)},\dots,\phi_{t}^{(p)}\}
modulo
p^{r_{p}}
. Setting

\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{t}{p^{r_{p}}},

say that
a\in\Phi_{p}
if
a\mod{p^{r_{p}}}
belongs to the set
\{\phi_{1}^{(p)},\dots,\phi_{t}^{(p)}\}
. We define
\Phi_{p}(X)
to be the set of
a\in\Omega(X)
such that
a
satisfies
\Phi_{p}
. Let
\Phi(X)
be the set of
a\in\Omega(X)
satisfying the conditions
\Phi_{p}
for all primes
p
. In other words,

\Phi(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcap_{p}\Phi_{p}(X),

where
p
ranges over all prime numbers.

Proposition 3.13
.

With respect to notation above, suppose that

(1)

\sum_{p}\left(1-\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p})\right)<\infty
,

(2)

the product
\prod_{p}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p})
converges.

Then, we have that

\#\Phi(X)\sim(d-c)\left(\prod_{p}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p})\right)X.

Proof.

Given a positive real number
Z
, set
\Phi_{Z}(X)
to be the set of integers
a\in\Omega(X)
such that
a
satisfies
\Phi_{p}
for all
p\leq Z
. Since there are only finitely many local conditions defining
\Phi_{Z}(X)
, we have that

\lim_{Y\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi_{Z}(X)}{(d-c)X}=\prod_{p\leq Z}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p}).

On the other hand, since
\Phi(X)
is contained in
\Phi_{Z}(X)
, we get that

\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi(X)}{(d-c)X}\leq\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi_{Z}(X)}{(d-c)X}=\prod_{p\leq Z}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p}).

Thus, taking
Z\rightarrow\infty
, we find that

\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi(X)}{(d-c)X}\leq\prod_{p}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p}).

Given a prime
p
, set
\Phi_{p}^{\prime}(X)
to be the set of integers
a\in\Omega(X)
such that
a
does not satisfy
\Phi_{p}
. Since

\Phi_{Z}(X)\subseteq\Phi(X)\cup\left(\bigcup_{p>Z}\Phi_{p}^{\prime}(X)\right),

we find that

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi_{Z}(X)}{(d-c)X}\leq\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi(X)}{(d-c)X}+\sum_{p>Z}\limsup_{Y\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi_{p}^{\prime}(X)}{(d-c)X}.

Note that
\#\Phi_{p}^{\prime}(X)=\left(1-\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p})\right)(d-c)X+O(1)
. Thus, we find that

\sum_{p>Z}\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi_{p}^{\prime}(X)}{(d-c)X}\leq\sum_{p\geq Z}\left(1-\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p})\right).

This quantity goes to
0
as
Z\rightarrow\infty
. On the other hand,

\lim_{Z\rightarrow\infty}\left(\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi_{Z}(X)}{(d-c)X}\right)=\prod_{p}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p}).

Thus, we have shown that

\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi(X)}{(d-c)X}=\prod_{p}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p}).

Since
\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi(X)}{(d-c)X}
and
\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\Phi(X)}{(d-c)X}
both exist and are equal to
\prod_{p}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p})
, we conclude that

\#\Phi(X)\sim(d-c)\left(\prod_{p}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p})\right)X.

∎

4.

In this section, we prove the main results of this article. Let

Recall that

It is easy to see that

\end{split}

The density of curves of Type I for which

\begin{split}\mathfrak{d}_{\leq 1}=&\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\#\{a\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)\mid\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}\operatorname{Sel}^{\varphi}(E_{a}/\mathbb{K})\leq 1\}}{\#\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)}\right)\\
=&\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\#\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\prime}(X)}{\#\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)}\right).\\
\end{split}

Upon replacing
Y
with
\frac{\sqrt{X}}{12\sqrt{3}}
, it is clear that

\mathfrak{d}_{\leq 1}=\lim_{Y\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\#\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(Y)}{\#\mathcal{S}(Y)}\right).

Let
\mathcal{T}(Y)
be the set of
a\in\mathcal{S}(Y)
such that

•

a>0
,

•

,

Proposition 4.1
.

For
a\in\mathcal{T}(Y)
, we have that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}\operatorname{Sel}^{\varphi}(E_{a}/\mathbb{K})\in\{r_{3}(L),r_{3}(L)+1\}.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
S_{a}=\emptyset
. Recall that

S_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{\mathbb{K}}\mid a\in{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{*2}\text{ and }\upsilon_{\mathfrak{q}}(4a)\not\equiv 0\pmod{6}\}.

Let
q
be the rational prime such that
\mathfrak{q}|q
. First consider the case when
q\neq 2,3
. In this case,
a
is not divisible by
q^{2}
since it is squarefree. Moreover,
q
is unramified in
\mathbb{K}
. Suppose that
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4a)\not\equiv 0\mod{6}
. Then, in particular, this would imply that
q
divides
a
exactly once. Let
\pi_{\mathfrak{q}}
be the uniformizer of
{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
. We find that
\pi_{\mathfrak{q}}
also divides
a
exactly once and hence,
a\notin({\mathbb{K}}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{*})^{2}
, which implies that
\mathfrak{q}\notin S_{a}
. Next, suppose that
q=2
, since
2
is unramified in
\mathbb{K}
, and the exact power of
2
that divides
a
is
4
, we find that
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(a)=4
. As a result,
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4a)=6
and thus,
\mathfrak{q}\notin S_{a}
. Finally, we get to the case when
q=3
. In this case, since
3\nmid a
, we find that
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(4a)=0
and hence,
\mathfrak{q}\notin S_{a}
. We have thus shown that
S_{a}
is empty and the result thus follows from Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

With respect to the notation above, the following assertions hold

(1)

\#\mathcal{T}(Y)\sim\frac{1}{32\zeta(2)}Y
,

(2)

\#\mathcal{S}(Y)\sim\frac{2}{\zeta(6)}Y
,

(3)

\operatorname{lim}_{Y\to\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{T}(Y)}{\#\mathcal{S}(Y)}=\frac{\zeta(6)}{64\zeta(2)}.

Proof.

We begin by proving (
1
). At each prime
p
, we put a local condition
\Phi_{p}
at
p
defined as follows. For
p=2
, we say that
a
satisfies
\Phi_{2}
if
a\equiv 16\pmod{32}
. At
p=3
, say that
a
satisfied
\Phi_{3}
if
3\nmid a
. Finally for
p\geq 5
,
a
satisfied
\Phi_{p}
if
p^{2}\nmid a
. The local densities
\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p})
are as follows

\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{32}&\text{ if }p=2;\\
\frac{2}{3}&\text{ if }p=3;\\
1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}&\text{ if }p\geq 5.\end{cases}

It follows from Proposition
3.13
that

\begin{split}\lim_{Y\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{T}(Y)}{Y}&=\prod_{p}\mathfrak{d}(\Phi_{p})\\

The proof of part (

Theorem 4.7

Recall that
\mathcal{M}^{-}(Y)
be the set of all discriminants

\mathcal{N}^{\prime}(Y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{M}^{-}(Y)\setminus\mathcal{N}(Y)

\begin{split}\sum_{\Delta_{2}\in\mathcal{M}^{-}(Y)}h_{3}^{*}(\Delta_{2})&=\sum_{\Delta_{2}\in\mathcal{N}(Y)}h_{3}^{*}(\Delta_{2})+\sum_{\Delta_{2}\in\mathcal{N}^{\prime}(Y)}h_{3}^{*}(\Delta_{2})\\

&=\#\mathcal{N}(Y)+3\#\mathcal{N}^{\prime}(Y)\\
&=3(\#\mathcal{N}(Y)+\#\mathcal{N}^{\prime}(Y))-2\#\mathcal{N}(Y)\\
&=3\#\mathcal{M}^{-}(Y)-2\#\mathcal{N}(Y).\end{split}

∎

Random sampling and reconstruction in reproducing kernel subspace of mixed Lebesgue spaces
Prashant Goyal a Dhiraj Patel a Sivananthan Sampath a

Abstract
In this article, we consider the random sampling in the image space V V of mixed Lebesgue space L p , q ​ ( ℝ n + 1 ) L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}) under an idempotent integral operator. We assume some decay and regularity conditions of the kernel and approximate the unit sphere in V V on a bounded cube C R , S C_{R,S} by a finite-dimensional subspace of V V . Consequently, the set of concentrated functions is totally bounded. We prove with an overwhelming probability that the random sample set uniformly distributed over C R , S C_{R,S} is a stable set of sampling for the set of concentrated functions on C R , S C_{R,S} . Moreover, we propose an iterative scheme to reconstruct the concentrated signal from its random measurements.

Introduction

A
signal
is an electrical current that carries data or information from one system to another. An
analog signal
is a continuous time-varying function that works with physical values and natural phenomena such as temperature, sound, lighting, earth-quack, speed of wind, etc. A
digital signal
is a discrete sequence that represents values of data at any point in time. In modern days, analog signals are analyzed and manipulated digitally. Therefore, digital data processing of signals uses the discretized version of the analog signal. This raises the question of whether we can recover the original signal from its discrete samples. The fundamental problem in sampling theory is to reconstruct or approximate a function
f
uniquely and stably from its sampled values
\{f(x_{j}):j\in J\}
on some discrete set
X=\{x_{j}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Sampling theory is the most basic tool in signal analysis and modern pulse code modulation communication. It is one of the most active research due to its influential applications in digital signal processing, image processing, and wireless communication. As the characteristic properties of the signals are different for distinct signals, the sampling problem cannot be solved in general. In this article, we consider the function space as an image of an idempotent integral operator in mixed Lebesgue space
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
.

Mixed Lebesgue space
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
is a generalization of Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
which contains measurable functions with independent variables having different properties. In particular, time-varying functions belong to mixed Lebesgue space. The mixed Lebesgue space
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
is the collection of all measurable functions
f=f(x,y)
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}^{q}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x,y)|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,dy<\infty,~~~\text{for }1\leq p,q<\infty.

The initial realization of mixed Lebesgue space traced back to the year 1960 of an interesting article by Hörmander
[
15
]
and first introduced by Benrdek and Panzone
[
6
]
. They considered mixed Lebesgue space
L^{\vec{p}}(X)
of multi-variable measurable functions and norm on
L^{\vec{p}}(X)
defined as iterative
L^{p}
norm of the function with each variables. Mixed Lebesgue space
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
inherits many properties of the standard
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
, such as the Plancherel-Polya inequality
[
26
]
, Leibniz-type rule
[
16
]
, and some classical conjectures in harmonic analysis
[
9
]
. The usefulness of mixed Lebesgue spaces motivates to study numerous other function spaces like Besov spaces, Sobolev spaces, and Bessel potential spaces with mixed norms
[
7
]
. The classical spaces such as parabolic function space, Lorentz space, and Orlicz space were studied with mixed norms
[

,

,
23
]
. Hart et al.
[

]
introduced the mixed-norm Hardy space

to improve the regularity of the bilinear operator. Moreover, due to the flexibility of independent variables of the function domain, interesting results were studied for the functions in mixed Lebesgue space in the context of partial differential equations. For example, to study the solution of partial differential equations involving both time and space variables, such as the heat or the wave equations. Functions in mixed Lebesgue space give precise information on the estimation of the parameters and induce better regularity for the solution of linear or non-linear equations
[
2

In random sampling, sample points are randomly taken according to the probability distribution. The stability of the random sample set over unbounded set

In this section, we define independent random variable from uniformly distributed random sample set on

Using the Bernstein inequality on the independent random variables, we prove the random sampling result.

Let

(

]
)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(C_{R,S})}^{pq}\leq R^{(p-1)n}S^{q-1}D^{pq-1}\|f\|_{L^{1,1}(C_{R,S})}.

(b)

Proof.

(a)

Lemma
4.1
and reproducing properties of function in
V
implies

\displaystyle Var\big(Z_{i,j}(f)\big)

\displaystyle=E\Big(\big(|f(x_{i},y_{j})|-E\big(|f(x_{i},y_{j})|\big)\big)^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=E\big(|f(x_{i},y_{j})|^{2}\big)-\big(E\big(|f(x_{i},y_{j})|\big)\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq E\big(|f(x_{i},y_{j})|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{R^{n}S}\iint_{C_{R,S}}|f(x,y)|^{2}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{R^{n}S}\|f\|_{L^{\infty,\infty}(C_{R,S})}\|f\|_{L^{1,1}(C_{R,S})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{R^{\frac{n}{p}}S^{\frac{1}{q}}}\sup\limits_{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\|K(x,y,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}.

(b)

Reproducing properties of function in

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}\Big\{\big||f(x_{i},y_{j})|-\frac{1}{R^{n}S}\iint_{C_{R,S}}|f(x,y)|\,dxdy\big|\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\|K(x,y,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}.

(c)

Similar argument as in part (a) implies

\displaystyle Var\big(Z_{i,j}(f)-Z_{i,j}(g)\big)=

\displaystyle E\Big(\big(|f(x_{i},y_{j})|-|g(x_{i},y_{j})|\big)^{2}\Big)-\Big(E\big(|f(x_{i},y_{j})|-|g(x_{i},y_{j})|\big)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R^{n}S}\iint_{C_{R,S}}\big||f(x,y)|-|g(x,y)|\big|\big(|f(x,y)|+|g(x,y)|\big)\,dxdy

Theorem 4.6

f_{0}=S_{X}f,\hskip 28.45274ptf_{r}=f_{0}+f_{r-1}-S_{X}f_{r-1},\hskip 14.22636ptr\geq 1,

where
\displaystyle S_{X}f=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{l}f(x_{i},y_{j})T\beta_{i,j}
and
\beta_{i,j}
is the partition of unity supported on
B_{\theta}(x_{i},y_{j})
. Moreover,
f_{r}
converges to

exponentially, and

\displaystyle\|f_{r}-f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\leq\frac{1+\|K\|_{W}(\|w_{\theta}(K)\|_{W}+\delta)}{1-\|K\|_{W}(\|w_{\theta}(K)\|_{W}+\delta)}\big[\|K\|_{W}(\|w_{\theta}(K)\|_{W}+\delta)\big]^{r+1}\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}.

Proof.

From the regularity condition of the kernel
K
,
\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\|w_{\epsilon}(K)\|_{W}=0
. Therefore, for
\frac{1}{\|K\|_{W}}-\delta>0
there exists
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
such that

\|w_{\epsilon}(K)\|_{W}<\frac{1}{\|K\|_{W}}-\delta,\hskip 17.07164pt\text{whenever }0<\epsilon<\epsilon^{\prime}.

We consider
\eta<\min\{\frac{2}{n},\epsilon^{\prime}\}
.
X
is a finite collection of sample set in
C_{R,S}
with gap
\theta\big(<\eta\text{ as }lm>\mathcal{O}\big(N_{0}(\Gamma)(R+S)^{n+1}\big)\big).
The collection
\{B_{\theta}(x_{i},y_{j}):1\leq i\leq l,1\leq j\leq m\}
is a cover for
C_{R,S}
and
\beta_{i,j}
is the partition of unity with
supp(\beta_{i,j})\subseteq B_{\theta}(x_{i},y_{j})
and satisfies

1.

0\leq\beta_{i,j}\leq 1.

2.

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}\beta_{i,j}\equiv 1.

Now consider the iterative algorithm

f_{0}=S_{X}f,\hskip 28.45274ptf_{r}=f_{0}+f_{r-1}-S_{X}f_{r-1},\hskip 14.22636ptr\geq 1.

For every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we consider
\displaystyle Q_{X}f(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{l}f(x_{i},y_{j})\beta_{i,j}(x,y),
then
S_{X}=TQ_{X}
. Now, for
f\in V^{\star}(R,S,\delta),
we have

\displaystyle\|f-S_{X}f\|_{L^{p.q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}

\displaystyle=\|Tf-TQ_{f}f\|_{L^{p.q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}

\displaystyle\leq\|K\|_{W}(\|f-Q_{f}f\|_{L^{p.q}(C_{R,S})}+\|f\|_{L^{p.q}(C_{R,S}^{c})}).

For
(x,y)\in C_{R,S}

\displaystyle|f(x,y)-Q_{X}f(x,y)|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{l}|f(x,y)-f(x_{i},y_{j})|\beta_{i,j}(x,y)\leq w_{\theta}(f)(x,y).

\displaystyle w_{\theta}(f)(x,y)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w_{\theta}(K)(x,y,s,t)f(s,t)\,dsdt,

\displaystyle\|w_{\theta}(f)\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\leq\|w_{\theta}(K)\|_{W}\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}.

\displaystyle\|f-S_{X}f\|_{L^{p.q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\leq\|K\|_{W}\big(\|w_{\theta}(K)\|_{W}+\delta\big)\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}.

f_{r}-f_{r-1}=(I-S_{X})f_{0},~~\text{and }f_{r}=S_{X}f+\sum_{h=1}^{r}(T-S_{X})^{h}S_{X}f,

where

\displaystyle\|Rf\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|K\|_{W}}{1-\|K\|_{W}\big(\|w_{\theta}(K)\|_{W}+\delta\big)}\|f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}.

Therefore,

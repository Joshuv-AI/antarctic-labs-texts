A determinantal identity
Tony C. Dorlas

Abstract
We prove an interesting identity for the sum of determinants, which is a generalization of the sum of a geometric progression. The proof is quite long and a number of other identities are proved along the way. Some of the more elementary ones are deferred to another section at the end.



1
The identity

We prove an identity for determinants which generalizes the sum of a geometric progression. It was inspired by the problem of calculating scalar products of Bethe Ansatz eigenfunctions, see
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
.
The proof is quite long and a sequence of lemmas used in the proof is deferred to a second section.
For general properties of determinants, see
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1

For any
n,N\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
, the following identity holds.

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq x_{1}<\dots<x_{n}\leq N}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1}^{x_{1}}&a_{1}^{x_{2}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{x_{n}}\\
a_{2}^{x_{1}}&a_{2}^{x_{2}}&\cdots&a_{2}^{x_{n}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{n}^{x_{1}}&a_{n}^{x_{2}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{x_{n}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{a_{k}}{a_{k}-1}\sum_{J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}}(-1)^{\nu(J^{c})}\gamma(J)\gamma(J^{c})\prod_{j\in J}a_{j}^{N},

(6)

where

\gamma(J)=\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{\{i,j\}\subset J}(a_{i}a_{j}-1)}\Delta(J),

(7)

where

\Delta(J)=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&a_{j_{1}}&\cdots&a_{j_{1}}^{k-1}\\
1&a_{j_{2}}&\cdots&a_{j_{2}}^{k-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{j_{k}}&\cdots&a_{j_{k}}^{k-1}\end{array}\right|

(8)

is a Van der Monde determinant if
J=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\}
with
j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}

(and
\gamma(\emptyset)=\gamma(\{k\})=1
), and where

\nu(I)=\sum_{k\in I}k.

(9)

The proof uses a number of elementary results about determinants of this type, which are stated in
Section
2
.

Proof.
We first sum over
x_{1}
to write

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{a_{k}-1}{a_{k}}\sum_{1\leq x_{1}<\dots<x_{n}\leq N}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1}^{x_{1}}&a_{1}^{x_{2}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{x_{n}}\\
a_{2}^{x_{1}}&a_{2}^{x_{2}}&\cdots&a_{2}^{x_{n}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{n}^{x_{1}}&a_{n}^{x_{2}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{x_{n}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq x_{2}<\dots<x_{n}\leq N-1}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}a_{1}^{x_{2}}-1&a_{1}^{x_{2}+1}-1&a_{1}^{x_{3}}(a_{1}-1)&\cdots&a_{1}^{x_{n}}(a_{1}-1)\\
a_{2}^{x_{2}}-1&a_{2}^{x_{2}+1}-1&a_{2}^{x_{3}}(a_{2}-1)&\cdots&a_{2}^{x_{n}}(a_{2}-1)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{n}^{x_{2}}-1&a_{n}^{x_{2}+1}-1&a_{n}^{x_{3}}(a_{n}-1)&\cdots&a_{n}^{x_{n}}(a_{n}-1)\end{array}\right|.

For
n=2
this becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{2}=1}^{N-1}\left\{(a_{1}a_{2})^{x_{2}}\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}1&a_{1}\\
1&a_{2}\end{array}\right|-\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}1&a_{1}^{x_{2}+1}-a_{1}^{x_{2}}\\
1&a_{2}^{x_{2}+1}-a_{2}^{x_{2}}\end{array}\right|\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(a_{1}a_{2})^{N}-a_{1}a_{2}}{a_{1}a_{2}-1}\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}1&a_{1}\\
1&a_{2}\end{array}\right|-\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}1&a_{1}^{N}-a_{1}\\
1&a_{2}^{N}-a_{2}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(a_{1}a_{2})^{N}-1}{a_{1}a_{2}-1}\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}1&a_{1}\\
1&a_{2}\end{array}\right|+a_{1}^{N}-a_{2}^{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{J\subset\{1,2\}}(-1)^{\nu(J^{c})}\gamma(J)\gamma(J^{c})\prod_{j\in J}a_{j}^{N}.

In general, we want to prove that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq x_{2}<\dots<x_{n}\leq N-1}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}a_{1}^{x_{2}}-1&a_{1}^{x_{2}+1}-1&a_{1}^{x_{3}}(a_{1}-1)&\cdots&a_{1}^{x_{n}}(a_{1}-1)\\
a_{2}^{x_{2}}-1&a_{2}^{x_{2}+1}-1&a_{2}^{x_{3}}(a_{2}-1)&\cdots&a_{2}^{x_{n}}(a_{1}-1)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{n}^{x_{2}}-1&a_{n}^{x_{2}+1}-1&a_{n}^{x_{3}}(a_{n}-1)&\cdots&a_{n}^{x_{n}}(a_{n}-1)\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}}(-1)^{\nu(J^{c})}\gamma(J)\gamma(J^{c})\prod_{j\in J}a_{j}^{N}.

(28)

We proceed by induction on
n
.
First note that if
x_{i}=x_{i-1}
, the
i
-th column and the
i-1
th columns are equal (except for
i=3
, in which case
the third column equals the difference of the second and first columns). We can therefore extend the sums to those cases and write the left-hand side of (
28
) as

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq x_{2}\leq\dots\leq x_{n}\leq N-1}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}a_{1}^{x_{2}}-1&a_{1}^{x_{2}+1}-1&a_{1}^{x_{3}}(a_{1}-1)&\cdots&a_{1}^{x_{n}}(a_{1}-1)\\
a_{2}^{x_{2}}-1&a_{2}^{x_{2}+1}-1&a_{2}^{x_{3}}(a_{2}-1)&\cdots&a_{2}^{x_{n}}(a_{2}-1)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{n}^{x_{2}}-1&a_{n}^{x_{2}+1}-1&a_{n}^{x_{3}}(a_{n}-1)&\cdots&a_{n}^{x_{n}}(a_{n}-1)\end{array}\right|\!.

(34)

Expanding the left-hand side of (
34
) according to the last column it becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}(a_{k}-1)\sum_{x_{n}=0}^{N-1}a_{k}^{x_{n}}\sum_{0\leq x_{2}\leq\dots\leq x_{n-1}\leq x_{n}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}a_{1}^{x_{2}}-1&a_{1}^{x_{2}+1}-1&a_{1}^{x_{3}}(a_{1}-1)&\cdots&a_{1}^{x_{n-1}}(a_{1}-1)\\
a_{2}^{x_{2}}-1&a_{2}^{x_{2}+1}-1&a_{2}^{x_{3}}(a_{2}-1)&\cdots&a_{2}^{x_{n-1}}(a_{2}-1)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\left[a_{k}^{x_{2}}-1\right.&a_{k}^{x_{2}+1}-1&a_{k}^{x_{3}}(a_{k}-1)&\cdots&\left.a_{k}^{x_{n-1}}(a_{k}-1)\right]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{n}^{x_{2}}-1&a_{n}^{x_{2}+1}-1&a_{n}^{x_{3}}(a_{n}-1)&\cdots&a_{n}^{x_{n-1}}(a_{n}-1)\end{array}\right|,

where the square brackets around the
k
-th row indicate that
this row is omitted. By the induction hypothesis, this equals

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}(a_{k}-1)\sum_{x_{n}=0}^{N-1}a_{k}^{x_{n}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k\}}(-1)^{\nu_{k}(J^{c})}\,\gamma(J)\,\gamma(J^{c}\setminus\{k\})\prod_{j\in J}a_{j}^{x_{n}},

(42)

where
\nu_{k}(J^{c})
is given by

\nu_{k}(I)=\sum_{i\in I:\,i<k}i+\sum_{i\in I:\,i>k}(i-1).

(43)

Now let us first consider the case that
|J|=n-1
, that is
J=\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k\}
. In that case

\nu_{k}(J^{c})=0
for all
k
. The corresponding term is

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}(a_{k}-1)\sum_{x_{n}=0}^{N-1}a_{k}^{x_{n}}\gamma(\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k\})\prod_{j\neq k}a_{j}^{x_{n}}.

Multiplying by
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(a_{i}a_{j}-1)
this becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}(a_{k}-1)\sum_{x_{n}=0}^{N-1}a_{k}^{x_{n}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{j\neq k}a_{j}^{x_{n}}\prod_{j\neq k}(a_{j}a_{k}-1)\,\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\left[1\right.&a_{k}&\cdots&\left.a_{k}^{n-1}\right]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-2}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{n}=0}^{N-1}(a_{1}\dots a_{n})^{x_{n}}\,\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-2}&(a_{1}-1)\prod_{j=2}^{n}(a_{1}a_{j}-1)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{k}&\cdots&a_{k}^{n-2}&(a_{k}-1)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}(a_{j}a_{k}-1)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-2}&(a_{n}-1)\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(a_{j}a_{n}-1)\end{array}\right|.

Using Lemma
2.4
and summing over
x_{n}
this equals

\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{N}-1\right)\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-2}&a_{1}^{n-1}\\
1&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{2}^{n-2}&a_{2}^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-2}&a_{n}^{n-1}\end{array}\right|.

The term with
\prod_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{N}
is just the
term
|J|=n
of the right-hand side of (
28
) when divided again by

\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(a_{i}a_{j}-1)
. The second term contributes to
J=\emptyset
.

Next consider the case
|J|=n-2
in the expression (
42
). This equals

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}(a_{k}-1)\sum_{x_{n}=0}^{N-1}a_{k}^{x_{n}}\sum_{l\neq k}(-1)^{\nu_{k}(\{k,l\})}\gamma(\{k,l\}^{c})\prod_{j\neq k,l}a_{j}^{x_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{k\neq l}(-1)^{n-k}(a_{k}-1)\sum_{x_{n}=0}^{N-1}\prod_{j\neq l}a_{j}^{x_{n}}(-1)^{\nu_{k}(\{k,l\})}\gamma(\{k,l\}^{c}).

Here
\nu_{k}(J)
is given by (
43
). Multiplying the
l
-th term by

\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i,j\neq l\end{subarray}}(a_{i}a_{j}-1)
the resulting
expression is

\displaystyle\sum_{k\neq l}(-1)^{n-k}(a_{k}-1)\sum_{x_{n}=0}^{N-1}\prod_{j\neq l}a_{j}^{x_{n}}(-1)^{\nu_{k}(\{k,l\})}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq k,l\end{subarray}}^{n}(a_{j}a_{k}-1)\,\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-3}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\left[1\right.&a_{k}&\cdots&\left.a_{k}^{n-3}\right]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\left[1\right.&a_{l}&\cdots&\left.a_{l}^{n-3}\right]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-3}\end{array}\right|.

Now,

\nu_{k}(\{k,l\})=\begin{cases}l&\text{if $l<k$;}\\
l-1&\text{if $l>k$.}\end{cases}

But, in the case
l>k
, the
l
-th row is below the
k
-th row so the number of rows below
the
k
-th is only
n-k-1
. Performing the sums over
k
and
x_{n}
, we therefore get

(-1)^{l}\frac{\prod_{j\neq l}a_{j}^{N}-1}{\prod_{j\neq l}a_{j}-1}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-3}&(a_{1}-1)\prod_{j\neq 1,l}(a_{1}a_{j}-1)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\left[1\right.&a_{l}&\cdots&a_{l}^{n-3}&\left.(a_{l}-1)\prod_{j\neq l}(a_{j}a_{l}-1)\right]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-3}&(a_{n}-1)\prod_{j\neq l,n}(a_{j}a_{n}-1)\end{array}\right|.

We set
J^{\prime}=J\cup\{k\}=\{l\}^{c}
and note that
\nu(J^{\prime c})=\nu(\{l\})=l
. Using Lemma
2.4
again, we obtain

\left(\prod_{j\neq l}a_{j}^{N}-1\right)(-1)^{\nu(\{l\})}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-3}&a_{1}^{n-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\left[1\right.&a_{l}&\cdots&a_{l}^{n-3}&\left.a_{l}^{n-2}\right]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-3}&a_{n}^{n-2}\end{array}\right|.

Dividing again by
\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i,j\neq l\end{subarray}}(a_{i}a_{j}-1)
and summing over
l
this yields

\sum_{l=1}^{n}(-1)^{\nu(\{l\})}\gamma(\{l\}^{c})\left(\prod_{j\neq l}a_{j}^{N}-1\right).

The term with
\prod_{j\neq l}a_{j}^{N}
is just the term
J^{\prime}=\{l\}^{c}
in the right-hand side of (
28
). The other term contributes to the case
J^{\prime}=\emptyset
.

We now consider the general case in (
42
). Again, we want to put
J^{\prime}=J\cup\{k\}
.
Then
(J^{\prime})^{c}=J^{c}\setminus\{k\}
. Summing over
x_{n}
in (
42
) we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\\
J^{c}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\sum_{k\in J^{c}}\left(\frac{a_{k}^{N}\prod_{j\in J}a_{j}^{N}-1}{a_{k}\prod_{j\in J}a_{j}-1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times(-1)^{n-k}(a_{k}-1)(-1)^{\nu_{k}(J^{c})}\gamma(J)\gamma(J^{c}\setminus\{k\}).

With
J^{\prime}=J\cup\{k\}
this is

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J^{\prime}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\\
J^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{\prod_{j\in J^{\prime}}a_{j}^{N}-1}{\prod_{j\in J^{\prime}}a_{j}-1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{k\in J^{\prime}}(-1)^{n-k}(a_{k}-1)(-1)^{\nu_{k}(J^{\prime c}\cup\{k\})}\gamma(J^{\prime}\setminus\{k\})\gamma((J^{\prime})^{c}).

As in the case
|J|=n-2
,
\nu_{k}(J^{c})=\nu(J^{\prime c})-p
, where
p
is the number of
i\in J^{c}
with
i>k
,
which compensates for the number of rows below the
k
-th row omitted in the determinant for
J
. Applying
Lemma
2.4
, we therefore obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J^{\prime}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\\
J^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j\in J^{\prime}}a_{j}^{N}-1\right)(-1)^{\nu(J^{\prime c})}\gamma(J^{\prime})\gamma(J^{\prime c}).

(57)

The terms corresponding to
\prod_{j\in J^{\prime}}a_{j}^{N}
agree with those in the right-hand side of equation (
28
) with
J\neq\emptyset
, so it remains to show that

-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J^{\prime}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\\
J^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\nu(J^{\prime c})}\gamma(J^{\prime})\gamma(J^{\prime c})=(-1)^{\nu(\{1,\dots,n\})}\gamma(\{1,\dots,n\}).

(58)

Equivalently, with
I=J^{\prime c}
,

\sum_{I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}}(-1)^{\nu(I)}\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{j\in I^{c}}(a_{i}a_{j}-1)\Delta(I)\Delta(I^{c})=0,

(59)

where

\Delta(I)=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&a_{i_{1}}&\dots&a_{i_{1}}^{p-1}\\
1&a_{i_{2}}&\dots&a_{i_{2}}^{p-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{i_{p}}&\dots&a_{i_{p}}^{p-1}\end{array}\right|\quad\mbox{ for }I=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{p}\}.

(60)

If
\nu(\{1,\dots,n\})
is odd, the identity (
59
) is obvious by interchanging
I
and
I^{c}
. If
\nu(\{1,\dots,n\})
is even, the terms
I
and
I^{c}
are equal to each other, so, by symmetry, we can assume that
|I|\leq|I^{c}|
.
Then we can expand
\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{j\in I^{c}}(a_{i}a_{j}-1)
as follows.

\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{j\in I^{c}}(a_{i}a_{j}-1)=\sum_{p=0}^{|I|\,|I^{c}|}\sum_{K\subset I\times I^{c}:|K|=p}(-1)^{|I|\,|I^{c}|-|K|}\prod_{(i,j)\in K}a_{i}a_{j}.

(61)

Set
k=|I|
so that
|I^{c}|=n-k
. We can reorder
the points
i\in I
such that the number
n_{i}
of points
(i,j)\in K
for given
i\in I
, is non-decreasing. Given a
non-decreasing sequence
(n_{r})_{r=1}^{k}
, put
k_{m}=\#\{r:\,n_{r}=m\}
. Clearly,
n_{r}\leq n-k
so
m\leq n-k
.
Moreover,
\sum_{m=0}^{n-k}k_{m}=k
. We can then write

\displaystyle\sum_{K\subset I\times I^{c}:|K|=p}\prod_{(i,j)\in K}a_{i}a_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq\dots\leq n_{k}\leq n-k\\
\sum_{r=1}^{k}n_{r}=p\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(I_{m})_{m=0}^{k}\in\Pi(I):\\
|I_{m}|=k_{m}\end{subarray}}\prod_{m=1}^{k}\prod_{i\in I_{m}}a_{i}^{m}\prod_{r=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J_{r}\subset I^{c}:\\
|J_{r}|=n_{r}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\in J_{r}}a_{j},

where
\Pi(I)
is the set of partitions of
I
. We define

S_{l}=\sum_{J\subset I^{c}:\,|J|=l}\prod_{j\in J}a_{j}\mbox{ and }S_{0}=1,

(63)

and

A_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}=\sum_{(I_{m})_{m=0}^{k}\in\Pi(I):\,|I_{m}|=k_{m}}\,\prod_{m=1}^{k}\prod_{i\in I_{m}}a_{i}^{m},

(64)

so that

\sum_{K\subset I\times I^{c}:|K|=p}\prod_{(i,j)\in K}a_{i}a_{j}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq\dots\leq n_{k}\leq n-k\\
\sum_{r=1}^{k}n_{r}=p\end{subarray}}A_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}\prod_{r=1}^{k}S_{n_{r}}.

(65)

Denote
|\underline{n}|=\sum_{r=1}^{k}n_{r}
and

\mathbb{N}^{k}_{\uparrow}=\{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}:\,0\leq n_{1}\leq\dots\leq n_{k}\leq n-k\}

(66)

and introduce a lexicographic ordering according to

\underline{n}<\underline{m}\mbox{ if }n_{r}=m_{r}\mbox{ for }r>r_{0},\ n_{r_{0}}<m_{r_{0}}.

(67)

We define a map
\phi:\mathbb{N}^{k}_{\uparrow}\to\mathbb{N}^{n-k}_{\uparrow}
by

\phi(\underline{n})_{m}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $1\leq m\leq n-k-n_{k}$,}\\
1&\text{ if $n-k-n_{k}+1\leq m\leq n-k-n_{k-1}$,}\\
\vdots\\
k&\text{ if $m\geq n-k-n_{1}+1$.}\end{cases}

(68)

That is,

\phi(\underline{n})_{m}=\min\{r\geq 0:\,n_{k-r}\leq n-k-m\}.

(69)

For example, if
n=10
and
k=4
, then

\phi(0,3,3,5)=(0,1,1,3,3,3)
. A pictorial representation of this map is obtained by filling
squares of a
k\times(n-k)
grid with beads;
n_{i}
on
column
i
and
\phi(\underline{n})_{j}
on row
j
. We order the rows from bottom to top:

Let us also define, for

and

) and (
73
) into (
65
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{K\subset I\times I^{c}:|K|=p}\prod_{(i,j)\in K}a_{i}a_{j}\Delta(I)\Delta(I^{c})

(85)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{\uparrow}:\\
|\underline{n}|=p\end{subarray}}A_{\underline{n}}\Delta(I)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{m}\geq\underline{n}:\\
|\underline{m}|=p\end{subarray}}R_{\underline{n},\underline{m}}\Delta_{\psi(\underline{m})}(I^{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{\uparrow}:\\
|\underline{m}|=p\end{subarray}}\Delta_{\psi(\underline{m})}(I^{c})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{n}\leq\underline{m}:\\
|\underline{n}|=p\end{subarray}}R_{\underline{n},\underline{m}}A_{\underline{n}}\Delta(I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{\uparrow}:\\
|\underline{m}|=p\end{subarray}}\Delta_{\tilde{\underline{m}}}(I)\Delta_{\psi(\underline{m})}(I^{c}).

Inserting this into (
61
) and (
59
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}}(-1)^{\nu(I)}\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{j\in I^{c}}(a_{i}a_{j}-1)\Delta(I)\Delta(I^{c})

(86)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}}(-1)^{\nu(I)}\sum_{p=0}^{|I|\,|I^{c}|}(-1)^{|I|\,|I^{c}|-p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{\uparrow}:\\
|\underline{m}|=p\end{subarray}}\Delta_{\tilde{\underline{m}}}(I)\Delta_{\psi(\underline{m})}(I^{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{p=0}^{k(n-k)}(-1)^{k(n-k)-p}\sum_{\underline{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{\uparrow};\,|\underline{m}|=p}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}:\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\nu(I)}\Delta_{\tilde{\underline{m}}}(I)\Delta_{\psi(\underline{m})}(I^{c}).

The last sum is an expansion of

\Delta_{\tilde{\underline{m}},\psi(\underline{m})}(\{1,\dots,n\})
with respect to the first
k
columns.
In general,

\displaystyle\Delta_{m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}(\{1,\dots,n\})=

(87)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k(k+1)/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}:\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\nu(I)}\Delta_{m_{1},\dots,m_{k}}(I)\Delta_{m_{k+1},\dots,m_{n}}(I^{c}).

Indeed, for
k=1
we have

\Delta_{m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}(\{1,\dots,n\})=\sum_{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}}(-1)^{i-1}a_{i}^{m_{1}}\Delta_{m_{2},\dots,m_{n}}(\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i\}),

where
a_{i}^{m_{1}}=\Delta_{m_{1}}(\{i\})
. By induction this yields

\displaystyle\Delta_{m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}(\{1,\dots,n\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i-1}a_{i}^{m_{1}}\Delta_{m_{2},\dots,m_{n}}(\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i-1}a_{i}^{m_{1}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i\}:\\
|I|=k-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\nu^{\prime}_{i}(I)}\Delta_{m_{2},\dots,m_{k}}(I)\Delta_{m_{k+1},\dots,m_{n}}(I^{c}),

where
\nu^{\prime}_{i}(I)=\sum_{j\in I}j-\#\{j\in I:\,j>i\}
. Thus

\displaystyle\Delta_{m_{1},\dots,m_{n}}(\{1,\dots,n\})=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}:\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{\nu(I)-1-\#\{j\in I:\,j>i\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times a_{i}^{m_{1}}\Delta_{m_{2},\dots,m_{k}}(I)\Delta_{m_{k+1},\dots,m_{n}}(I^{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}:\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{\nu(I)-k+\#\{j\in I:\,j<i\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times a_{i}^{m_{1}}\Delta_{m_{2},\dots,m_{k}}(I)\Delta_{m_{k+1},\dots,m_{n}}(I^{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k(k+1)/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}:\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\nu(I)}\Delta_{m_{1},\dots,m_{k}}(I)\Delta_{m_{k+1},\dots,m_{n}}(I^{c}).

Hence, in order to prove (
59
), we want to show that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{p=0}^{k(n-k)}(-1)^{k(n-k)-p+k(k+1)/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{\uparrow}:\\
|\underline{m}|=p\end{subarray}}\Delta_{\tilde{\underline{m}},\psi(\underline{m})}(\{1,\dots,n\})=0.

(88)

First note that
\Delta_{\tilde{\underline{m}},\psi(\underline{m})}(\{1,\dots,n\})=0
unless
\tilde{\underline{m}}
and
\psi(\underline{m})

have nothing in common and make up
\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
. In particular,
|\underline{m}|+|\phi(\underline{m})|+\frac{1}{2}k(k-1)+\frac{1}{2}(n-k)(n-k-1)=\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)
, i.e.

2p=\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)-\frac{1}{2}k(k-1)-\frac{1}{2}(n-k)(n-k-1)=k(n-k).

If
k(n-k)
is odd, there is no nonzero term, so if
n
is even then
k
must also be even.
We therefore need

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k(n-k)/2+k(k+1)/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{\uparrow}:\\
|\underline{m}|=k(n-k)/2\end{subarray}}\Delta_{\tilde{\underline{m}},\psi(\underline{m})}(\{1,\dots,n\})=0.

(89)

Next we argue that
\Delta_{\tilde{\underline{m}},\psi(\underline{m})}(\{1,\dots,n\})=0
unless
m_{i}+m_{k-i+1}=n-k
for

i=1,\dots,k
. Consider the case
i=1
. In order that all the numbers below
m_{1}
are present, we need
\phi(\underline{m})_{j}=0
for
j=1,\dots,m_{1}
, while
\phi(\underline{m})_{m_{1}+1}\geq 1
. This means that the number of zeros in
\phi(\underline{m})

equals
m_{1}
, so
m_{k}=n-k-m_{1}
. The converse also holds. Similarly, for
i>1
, we must have
\psi(\underline{m})_{j}=j+i-1
, i.e.
\phi(\underline{m})_{j}=i
, for
j=m_{i-1}+1,\dots,m_{i}
and
\phi(\underline{m})_{m_{i}+1}\geq i+1
. This implies that

m_{k-i+2}-m_{k-i+1}=m_{i}-m_{i-1}
. By induction, therefore
m_{i}+m_{k-i+1}=m_{i-1}+m_{k-i+2}=n-k
. In
particular, if
k
is odd, then
n-k
is even and
m_{(k+1)/2}=(n-k)/2
.

Consider first the case that
n
is even, and hence also
k
is even.
Then we can count the number of possible solutions as follows. We choose the values of
\tilde{m}_{i}
with
i=1,\dots,n/2
arbitrarily between 1 and
n/2
. These are strictly increasing and determine
uniquely
m_{1},\dots,m_{k/2}
. The remaining
m_{i}
(
i=k/2+1,\dots,k
) are then given by the condition
m_{i}+m_{k-i+1}=n-k
, and the values of
\psi(\underline{m})_{j}
are given by the interstices.
The number of possible solutions is therefore
\displaystyle{{n/2\choose k/2}}
.

Note also, that if we move the
k/2
last elements
m_{i}
(
i=k/2+1,\dots,k
) across all
\phi(\underline{m})_{j}
(
j=1,\dots,n-k
), then in order to put the

\tilde{m}_{i}
and
\psi(\underline{m})_{j}
in increasing order, it remains to move each
m_{i}
with

i\leq k/2
across equally many
\phi(\underline{m})_{j}
to the right as we need to move
m_{k-i+1}

across
\phi(\underline{m})_{j}
to the left. This means that in each case, the determinant

\Delta_{\tilde{\underline{m}},\psi(\underline{m})}=(-1)^{k(n-k)/2}\Delta(\{1,\dots,n\})
.
Inserting this into the left-hand side of (
88
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k(n-k)/2+k(k+1)/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{\uparrow}:\\
|\underline{m}|=k(n-k)/2\end{subarray}}\Delta_{\tilde{\underline{m}},\psi(\underline{m})}(\{1,\dots,n\})=

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\,{\rm even}\end{subarray}}^{n}(-1)^{k/2}{n/2\choose k/2}\Delta(\{1,\dots,n\})=0.

(90)

(Note
that if
k
is even ,then
(-1)^{k(k+1)/2}=(-1)^{k/2}
.)

Analogously, if
n
is odd, then if
k
is even, the number of possibilities is
\displaystyle{{(n-1)/2\choose k/2}}
,
and if
k
is odd then the number of possibilities is
\displaystyle{{(n-1)/2\choose(k-1)/2}}
. The sign is again

(-1)^{k(n-k)/2}
and we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(n-1)/2}(-1)^{k(k+1)/2}{(n-1)/2\choose[k/2]}\Delta

(91)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{(n-1)/2}(-1)^{l}{(n-1)/2\choose l}\Delta+\sum_{l=0}^{(n-1)/2}(-1)^{l+1}{(n-1)/2\choose l}\Delta=0.

In both cases therefore (
88
) holds. The claim (
59
) is
thus proved. This completes the proof of the theorem.

Lemma 2.1

Let
\cal R
be a commutative ring. For
n\geq 3
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{\cal R}
, and for

0\leq k+l\leq n-2
,

\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-2}&a_{1}^{l}\sum_{2\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\\
1&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{2}^{n-2}&a_{2}^{l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 2\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-2}&a_{n}^{l}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n-1}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\end{array}\right|=0.

(92)

Proof.
For
k=0
this is obvious.

We now proceed by induction on
k
:

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-2}&a_{1}^{l}\sum_{2\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\\
1&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{2}^{n-2}&a_{2}^{l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 2\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-2}&a_{n}^{l}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n-1}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-2}&a_{1}^{l}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\\
1&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{2}^{n-2}&a_{2}^{l}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-2}&a_{n}^{l}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle\quad-\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-2}&a_{1}^{l+1}\sum_{2\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k-1}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k-1}}\\
1&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{2}^{n-2}&a_{2}^{l+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k-1}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 2\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-2}&a_{n}^{l+1}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k-1}\leq n-1}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k-1}}\end{array}\right|=0

provided
k+l\leq n-2
. Indeed, the first term
equals zero because the last column is a constant multiple of the
l+1
-th column, where
l\leq n-2
. The second term
equals zero by the induction hypothesis.

Similarly, we have also

Lemma 2.2

Let
\cal R
be a commutative ring. For
n\geq 3
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{\cal R}
, and for
1\leq k,l\leq n-1
,
such that
k+l\geq n
,

\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-2}&a_{1}^{l}\sum_{2\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\\

(96)

Proof.
For
l\geq 1
and
k=n-1
the final element in the
i
-th row equals
a_{i}^{l-1}a_{1}\dots a_{n}
so the determinant

Lemma 2.3

Let
\cal R
be a commutative ring. For
n\geq 3
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{\cal R}
, and for

,

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-2}&a_{1}^{n-1-k}\sum_{2\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\\

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-2}&a_{1}^{n-1}\\

\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\

Proof.
For
k=0
the identity is tautological. For
k\geq 1
we write

\displaystyle a_{i}^{n-1-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\

Lemma 2.4

n\geq 3
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{\cal R}
,

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-2}&(a_{1}-1)\prod_{j=2}^{n}(a_{1}a_{j}-1)\\

.

Lemma 2.5

For
n\geq 2
,
1\leq k\leq n-1
, and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{\cal R}
,

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sum_{2\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-1}\\

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\

\displaystyle=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-k-1}&a_{1}^{n-k+1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n}\\

\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-k-1}&a_{n}^{n-k+1}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n}\end{array}\right|.

Proof.
For
k=n-1
, we have, expanding,

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{2}\dots a_{n}&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-1}\\

\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}a_{1}\dots a_{j-1}a_{j+1}\dots a_{n}\,\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\left[a_{j}\right.&\cdots&\left.a_{j}^{n-2}\right]\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-1}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{1}^{2}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\left[a_{j}^{2}\right.&\cdots&\left.a_{j}^{n}\right]\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{n}^{2}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n}\end{array}\right|=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&a_{1}^{2}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}^{2}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n}\end{array}\right|.

We proceed by induction and write similarly,

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 1\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-1}\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 2\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{2}^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq n\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-1}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\,\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\left[a_{j}\right.&\cdots&\left.a_{j}^{n-1}\right]\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-1}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\prod_{i\neq j}a_{i}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-2}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\big[\qquad"&a_{j}&\cdots&a_{j}^{n-2}\big]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-2}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\prod_{i\neq j}a_{i}^{2}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k-1}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 1,j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k-1}}&1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-3}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\bigg[\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&1&a_{j}&\cdots&a_{j}^{n-3}\bigg]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k-1}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j,n\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k-1}}&1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-3}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\prod_{i\neq j}a_{i}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-2}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\big[\qquad"&a_{j}&\cdots&a_{j}^{n-2}\big]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n-1\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-2}\end{array}\right|.

The first term equals zero by Lemma
2.1
since
k-1\leq n-3
. By the induction hypothesis, the second term equals

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\prod_{i\neq j}a_{i}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-k-2}&a_{1}^{n-k}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-1}\\
1&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{2}^{n-k-2}&a_{2}^{n-k}&\cdots&a_{2}^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\left[1\right.&a_{j}&\cdots&a_{j}^{n-k-2}&a_{n}^{n-k}&\cdots&\left.a_{j}^{n-1}\right]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-k-2}&a_{n}^{n-k}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-1}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-k-1}&a_{1}^{n-k+1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n}\\
1&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{2}^{n-k-1}&a_{2}^{n-k+1}&\cdots&a_{2}^{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-k-1}&a_{n}^{n-k+1}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n}\end{array}\right|.

Corollary 2.1

For
n\geq 2
,
1\leq k\leq n-1
, and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{\cal R}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-1}\\
1&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{2}^{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-1}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n-k-1}&a_{1}^{n-k+1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{n}\\
1&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{2}^{n-k-1}&a_{2}^{n-k+1}&\cdots&a_{2}^{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n-k-1}&a_{n}^{n-k+1}&\cdots&a_{n}^{n}\end{array}\right|.

Proof.
Writing

\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}=a_{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k-1}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k-1}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}},

we see that the first term vanishes if
k\leq n-1
by Lemma
2.1
.

We generalise Lemma
2.5
further:

Lemma 2.6

Let
\cal R
be a commutative ring and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{\cal R}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
m_{1},\dots,m_{n-1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
1\leq m_{1}<\dots<m_{n-1}
. Then, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leq k\leq n-1
,

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sum_{2\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{1}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 2\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{2}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{2}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq n\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{n}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m_{n-1}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}\leq m^{\prime}_{1}<\dots<m^{\prime}_{n-1}:\,(\forall i)m^{\prime}_{i}-m_{i}=0,1\\
\#\{i:\,m^{\prime}_{i}=m_{i}+1\}=k\end{subarray}}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{n-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{n-1}}\end{array}\right|.

Proof.
We proceed as in the previous lemma and first note that

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sum_{2\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{n-1}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{1}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{n-1}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 2\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{2}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{2}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{n-1}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq n\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{n}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m_{n-1}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{2}\dots a_{n}&a_{1}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\prod_{i\neq 2}a_{i}&a_{2}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{2}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{1}\dots a_{n-1}&a_{n}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m_{n-1}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{1}^{m_{1}+1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m_{n-1}+1}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\big[\,a_{j}^{m_{1}+1}&\cdots&a_{j}^{m_{n-1}+1}\,\big]\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{n}^{m-1+1}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m_{n-1}+1}\end{array}\right|=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&a_{1}^{m_{1}+1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m_{n-1}+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}^{m_{1}+1}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m_{n-1}+1}\end{array}\right|.

Next we continue by induction as before:

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 1\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{1}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 2\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{2}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{2}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq n\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{n}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m_{n-1}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\,\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{1}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\left[\,a_{j}^{m_{1}}\right.&\cdots&\left.a_{j}^{m_{n-1}}\,\right]\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{n}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m_{n-1}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\prod_{i\neq j}a_{i}^{m_{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{1}^{m_{2}-m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m_{n-1}-m_{1}}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\bigg[\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{j}^{m_{2}-m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{j}^{m_{n-1}-m_{1}}\bigg]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{n}^{m_{2}-m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m_{n-1}-m_{1}}\end{array}\right|

Distinguishing the cases
j_{r}=i
for some
r
and
j_{r}\neq i
for all
i
, this becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\prod_{i\neq j}a_{i}^{m_{1}+1}

\displaystyle\times\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k-1}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 1,j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k-1}}&a_{1}^{m_{2}-m_{1}-1}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m_{n-1}-m_{1}-1}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k-1}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k-1}}\right.&a_{j}^{m_{2}-m_{1}-1}&\cdots&\left.a_{j}^{m_{n-1}-m_{1}-1}\right]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k-1}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j,n\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k-1}}&a_{n}^{m_{2}-m_{1}-1}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m_{n-1}-m_{1}-1}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\prod_{i\neq j}a_{i}^{m_{1}}

\displaystyle\times\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{1}^{m_{2}-m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m_{n-1}-m_{1}}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{j}^{m_{2}-m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{j}^{m_{n-1}-m_{1}}\bigg]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n-1\\
j_{r}\neq j\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{n}^{m_{2}-m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m_{n-1}-m_{1}}\end{array}\right|.

Both determinants in the last expression are of the same form as
the original, but of smaller size. By the induction hypothesis we therefore have that

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 1\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{1}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 2\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{2}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{2}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq n\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{n}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m_{n-1}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\prod_{i\neq j}a_{i}^{m_{1}+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{2}-m_{1}-1\leq m^{\prime}_{1}<\dots<m^{\prime}_{n-2}:\\
(\forall i)m^{\prime}_{i}-m_{i+1}+m_{1}=0,-1\\
\#\{i:\,m^{\prime}_{i}=m_{i+1}-m_{1}\}=k-1\end{subarray}}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\big[\,1&a_{j}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{j}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\big]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\prod_{i\neq j}a_{i}^{m_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{2}-m_{1}\leq m^{\prime}_{1}<\dots<m^{\prime}_{n-2}:\\
(\forall i)m^{\prime}_{i}-m_{i+1}+m_{1}=0,1\\
\#\{i:\,m^{\prime}_{i}=m_{i+1}-m_{1}+1\}=k\end{subarray}}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\big[\,1&a_{j}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{j}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\big]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{2}\leq m^{\prime}_{1}<\dots<m^{\prime}_{n-2}:\\
(\forall i)m^{\prime}_{i}-m_{i+1}=0,1\\
\#\{i:\,m^{\prime}_{i}=m_{i+1}+1\}=k-1\end{subarray}}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1}^{m_{1}+1}&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\big[\,a_{1}^{m_{1}+1}&a_{j}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{j}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\big]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{n}^{m_{1}+1}&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{2}\leq m^{\prime}_{1}<\dots<m^{\prime}_{n-2}:\\
(\forall i)m^{\prime}_{i}-m_{i+1}=0,1\\
\#\{i:\,m^{\prime}_{i}=m_{i+1}+1\}=k\end{subarray}}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1}^{m_{1}}&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\big[\,a_{j}^{m_{1}}&a_{j}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{j}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\big]\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{n}^{m_{1}}&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\end{array}\right|.

Summing over
j
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 1\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{1}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq 2\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{2}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{2}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n\\
j_{r}\neq n\end{subarray}}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}&a_{n}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m_{n-1}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{2}\leq m^{\prime}_{1}<\dots<m^{\prime}_{n-2}:\\
(\forall i)m^{\prime}_{i}-m_{i+1}=0,1\\
\#\{i:\,m^{\prime}_{i}=m_{i+1}+1\}=k-1\end{subarray}}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1}^{m_{1}+1}&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}^{m_{1}+1}&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{2}\leq m^{\prime}_{1}<\dots<m^{\prime}_{n-2}:\\
(\forall i)m^{\prime}_{i}-m_{i+1}=0,1\\
\#\{i:\,m^{\prime}_{i}=m_{i+1}+1\}=k\end{subarray}}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&a_{1}^{m_{1}}&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}^{m_{1}}&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{n-2}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}\leq m^{\prime}_{1}<\dots<m^{\prime}_{n-1}:\\
(\forall i)m^{\prime}_{i}-m_{i}=0,1\\
\#\{i:\,m^{\prime}_{i}=m_{i}+1\}=k\end{subarray}}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{n-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{n-1}}\end{array}\right|.

Iterating once more we get

Corollary 2.2

Let
\cal R
be a commutative ring and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{\cal R}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
m_{1},\dots,m_{n-1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
1\leq m_{1}<\dots<m_{n-1}
. Then, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leq k\leq n
,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq n}a_{j_{1}}\dots a_{j_{k}}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&a_{1}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m_{n-1}}\\
1&a_{2}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{2}^{m_{n-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
1&a_{n}^{m_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m_{n-1}}\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m^{\prime}_{1}<\dots<m^{\prime}_{n}:\,(\forall i)m^{\prime}_{i}-m_{i-1}=0,1\\
\#\{i:\,m^{\prime}_{i}=m_{i-1}+1\}=k\end{subarray}}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{1}^{m^{\prime}_{n}}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{n}}\end{array}\right|,

where
m_{0}=0
.

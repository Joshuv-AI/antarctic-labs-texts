First and second-order Cucker-Smale models with non-universal interaction, time delay and communication failures
Chiara Cicolani, Elisa Continelli, &, Cristina Pignotti

Abstract
In this paper, we deal with first and second-order alignment models with non-universal interaction, time delay and possible lack of connection between the agents. More precisely, we analyze the situation in which the system’s agents do not transmit information to all the other agents and also agents that are linked to each other can suspend their interaction at certain times. Moreover, we take into account of possible time lags in the interactions. To deal with the considered "non-universal" connection, a graph topology over the structure of the model has to be considered. Under a so-called Persistence Excitation Condition, we establish the exponential convergence to consensus for both models whenever the digraph that describes the interaction between the agents is strongly connected.



Keywords:
alignment models; Cucker-Smale model; non-universal interaction; communication failures; time delay.

In multiagent systems, it is important to consider the presence of time delay effects. Indeed, in the applications, one has to take into account of certain time lags due to propagation of information among the agents or to reaction times.

The analysis of the Hegselmann-Krause model and of the Cucker-Smale model in presence of time delays (that can be constant or, more realistically, varying in time), has been carried out by many authors,

[

the asymptotic flocking for the Cucker-Smale model with constant time delay without requiring any smallness condition on the time delay size (see also
[

]
for a consensus result for the Hegselmann-Krause model). Generalizing and extending the arguments employed in
[
33
]
, the exponential convergence to consensus for the Hegselmann-Krause model and the exhibition of asymptotic flocking for the Cucker-Smale model with time-variable time delay have been proved in

[

14
]
monotonicity assumptions on the influence function, which are usually required when dealing with the Hegselmann-Krause model and with the Cucker-Smale model, are removed, namely the influence function is assumed to be just positive bounded and continuous. In the same spirit, the asymptotic synchronization of the Kuramoto oscillators with time delay and non-universal interaction has been achieved in
[
7
]
.

A possible scenario that could occur in the analysis of such models is the one in which the system’s particles suspend the interactions they have with the other agents. As a consequence, there is a lack of connection between the system’s elements that, of course, obstacles the convergence to consensus, for the first-order model, or the flocking for the second-order one. Then, it is important to find conditions guaranteeing the system’s alignment.

In
[
3
]
, the convergence to consensus and the asymptotic flocking for a class of Cucker-Smale systems under communication failures among the system’s agents have been proved under suitable assumptions in the case of symmetric interaction coefficients. Also, in
[
1
]
, the approach to consensus for a first-order alignment system involving weights depending on the couple of agents that can eventually degenerate has been proved under a Persistence Excitation Condition. In the case of nonsymmetric interaction coefficients, the exponential convergence to consensus for the Hegselmann-Krause model with time delay and lack of interaction has been obtained in
[
15
]
.

Also, it could happen that the agents involved in an opinion formation or flocking process are not able to exchange information with all the other components of the system. In this case, we are in presence of a non-universal interaction, so that the agents are able to influence only the opinions or the velocities of the particles they are linked to. To deal with this kind of interaction, a graph topology over the structure of the model has to be considered (see
[
7
]
).

In this paper, we analyze the asymptotic behavior of the solutions to first and second-order alignment models, i.e. Hegselmann-Krause and Cucker-Smale models, in presence of (pair and time-dependent) time delays, non-universal interaction, and lack of connection. Namely, in these models, weight functions depending on the agents’ pair and that can degenerate at certain times are considered. In this case, the interaction is lacking not only among agents that are not linked each other but also among agents that are generally able to exchange information. Under a Persistence Excitation Condition, we establish the exponential consensus and flocking for the Hegselmann-Krause opinion formation model and for the Cucker-Smale model whenever the digraph that describes the interaction among the agents is strongly connected.

This is done dealing with a very general influence function and without requiring any smallness asssumption on the time delay size. Our result is very general and greatly improves
previous related works
[
3
,
15
,
1
]
. Indeed, in
[
1
]

only the first-order model is analyzed and the convergence to consensus is not achieved exponentially fast. Furthermore, time delay effects are not considered. Here, we work in a more general setting since there are particles that could never communicate with each other, and we consider time delays, pair and time depending. This wide generality requires finer and sophisticated arguments.
With respect to
[
15
]
, where only the first-order model is analyzed, the main novelties are the more general weight and time delay functions (now pair-dependent). Moreover, here, we work in the network topology setting. Finally,
[
3
]
deals with the second-order model too. However, the analysis requires symmetry conditions on the weight functions and all-to-all interaction. Moreover, no time delays are included.

The present paper is so organized. In Section
2
, we establish the exponential convergence to consensus for the solutions of the Hegselmann-Krause model, namely the first-order alignment model.
In Section
3
, the discussion is extended to the second-order version, namely the Cucker-Smale model. The proof of the exponential flocking for the Cucker-Smale model requires a more careful analysis with respect to the one carried out in Section
2
, since in this case we have to prove that the agents uniform their velocities and the distances between the agents’ positions are bounded.

2
The first-order alignment model

Consider a finite set of
N\in\mathbb{N}
agents, with
N\geq 2
. Let
x_{i}(t)\in{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
be the opinion of the
i
-th agent at time
t
. We shall denote with
\lvert\cdot\rvert
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the usual norm and scalar product on
{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
, respectively. Let us denote with
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.
The interactions between the elements of the system are described by the following Hegselmann-Krause type model:

\frac{d}{dt}x_{i}(t)=\underset{j:j\neq i}{\sum}\chi_{ij}\alpha_{ij}(t)b_{ij}(t)(x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))-x_{i}(t)),\quad t>0,\,\,\forall i=1,\dots,N.

(2.1)

where the time delay functions
\tau_{ij}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
are assumed to be continuous and satisfy the following:

0\leq\tau_{ij}(t)\leq\tau,\quad\forall t\geq 0,\,\forall i,j=1,\dots,N,

(2.2)

for a suitable positive constant
\tau
.

Here, the communication rates
b_{ij}
are of the form

b_{ij}(t):=\frac{1}{N-1}\psi(x_{i}(t),x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))),\quad\forall t>0,\,\forall i,j=1,\dots,N,

(2.3)

where the influence function
\psi:{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}\times{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}\rightarrow{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}
is positive, bounded and continuous and

K:=\lVert\psi\rVert_{\infty}.

(2.4)

The terms
\chi_{ij}
are so defined

\begin{cases}1,\quad\text{if }j\text{ transmits information to }i,\\
0,\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.5)

The weight functions
\alpha_{ij}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
are
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-measurable and satisfy the following Persistence Excitation Condition (cf.
[
3
,
1
]
):

(PE)

there exist two positive constants
T
and
\tilde{\alpha}
such that

\int_{t}^{t+T}\alpha_{ij}(s)ds\geq\tilde{\alpha},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

(2.6)

for all
i,j=1,\dots,N
such that
\chi_{ij}=1
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\tilde{\alpha}K\leq 1
.

Let us note that (
2.6
) becomes relevant when
T
is large and
\tilde{\alpha}
is small. In this case, the agents could eventually suspend their interaction for long enough. We also point out that, in the case in which
\alpha_{ij}(t)=1
, for a.e.
t\geq 0
and for any
i,j=1,\dots,N
, i.e. in the case in which the agents do not interrupt their exchange of information, the condition (
2.6
) is of course satisfied.

Due to the presence of the time delay, the initial conditions are functions defined in the interval
[-\tau,0].
The initial conditions

x_{i}(s)=x^{0}_{i}(s),\quad\forall s\in[-\tau,0],\,\forall i=1,\dots,N,

(2.7)

are assumed to be continuous functions.

We set

C_{0}:=\max_{i=1,\dots,N}\,\,\max_{s\in[-\tau,0]}\lvert x_{i}(s)\rvert,

(2.8)

\psi_{0}:=\min_{|y|,|z|\leq C_{0}}\psi(y,z).

(2.9)

We will consider a graph topology over the model structure. Let
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
be a digraph consisting of a finite set
\mathcal{V}=\{1,...,N\}
of vertices and a set
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}
of arcs. We assume that opinions of the agents are located at the vertices and interact each other via the underlying network topology. For each vertex
i
, we denote by
\mathcal{N}_{i}
the set of vertices that directly influence the vertex
i
, namely

\mathcal{N}_{i}:=\{j=1,\dots,N:\chi_{ij}=1\}.

(2.10)

The set
\mathcal{N}_{i}
can also be defined in the following way:
j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}
if and only if
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}
. Also, we denote with

N_{i}:=|\mathcal{N}_{i}|.

(2.11)

Throughout the paper, we will exclude self loops, i.e. we assume that
i\notin\mathcal{N}_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq N.
We also denote the network topology via its
(0,1)
-adjacency matrix
(\chi_{ij})_{ij}
. A
path
in a digraph
\mathcal{G}
from
i_{0}
to
i_{p}
is a finite sequence
i_{0},i_{1},\dots,i_{p}
of distinct vertices such that each successive pair of vertices is an arc of
\mathcal{G}.
The integer p is called
length
of the path. If there exists a path from
i
to
j
, then vertex
j
is said to be
reachable
from vertex
i
and we define the distance from
i
to
j
, in notation
\text{dist}(i,j)
, as the length of the shortest path from
i
to
j
. A digraph
\mathcal{G}
is said to be
strongly connected
if each vertex is reachable from any other vertex. We assume that our digraph
\mathcal{G}
is strongly connected. We define the
depth

\gamma
of the digraph as follows:

\gamma:=\max_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\text{dist}(i,j).

(2.12)

Thus, any particle can be connected to the other individuals of the system via no more than
\gamma
intermediate agents. By definition, since
i\notin\mathcal{N}_{i}
, for all
i=1,\dots,N
, we have that
\gamma\leq N-1.
Also, since the digraph is strongly connected, the depth
\gamma\geq 1
.

Now, we give the rigorous definition of convergence to consensus for solutions of the Hegselmann-Krause model (
2.1
). We define the diameter
d(\cdot)
of the solution as

d(t):=\max_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\lvert x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)\rvert,\quad\forall t\geq-\tau.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a solution
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,N}
to system (
2.1
) converges to
consensus
if
d(t)\to 0
, as
t\to\infty
.

We will prove the following exponential convergence to consensus result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume (
2.2
) and that the digraph
\mathcal{G}
is strongly connected. Let
\psi:{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}\times{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}\rightarrow{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}
be a positive, bounded, continuous function. Assume that the weight functions
\alpha_{ij}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
are
{\mathcal{L}^{1}}
-measurable and satisfy
(PE)
. Let
x_{i}^{0}:[-\tau,0]\rightarrow{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
be a continuous function, for any
i=1,\dots,N
. Then, every solution
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,N}
to (
2.1
) with the initial conditions (
2.7
) satisfies the following exponential decay estimate

d(t)\leq\left(\max_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\max_{r,s\in[-\tau,0]}\lvert x_{i}(r)-x_{j}(s)\rvert\right)e^{-C(t-\gamma(T+\tau)-\tau)},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

(2.13)

where
\gamma>0
is the depth of the digraph,
T
is the positive constant in (
2.6
) and
C
is a suitable positive constant.

2.1
Preliminary lemmas

Let
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,N}
be solution to (
2.1
) under the initial conditions (
2.7
). We assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.1
are satisfied. We present some auxiliary lemmas.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we define

I_{n}:=[n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)-\tau,n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)]

m_{n}^{v}:=\min_{i=1,\dots,N}\min_{s\in I_{n}}\,\langle x_{i}(s),v\rangle,

M_{n}^{v}:=\max_{j=1,\dots,N}\max_{s\in I_{n}}\,\langle x_{j}(s),v\rangle.

Also, we define, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\tilde{m}_{n}^{v}:=\min_{i=1,\dots,N}\langle x_{i}(n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)),v\rangle,

\tilde{M}_{n}^{v}:=\max_{j=1,\dots,N}\langle x_{j}(n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)),v\rangle.

Lemma 2.2
.

For each vector
v\in{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
, we have that

m_{0}^{v}\leq\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle\leq M_{0}^{v},

(2.14)

for all
t\geq-\tau
and for any
i=1,\dots,N
.

Proof.

First of all, we note that the inequalities in (
2.14
) are satisfied for every
t\in[-\tau,0]
.

Now, let
v\in{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
. For all
\epsilon>0
, we define

K^{\epsilon}:=\left\{t>0:\max_{i=1,\dots,N}\langle x_{i}(s),v\rangle<M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon,\,\forall s\in[0,t)\right\},

and

S^{\epsilon}:=\sup K^{\epsilon}.

By continuity, we have that
K^{\epsilon}\neq\emptyset
and
S^{\epsilon}>0
.

We claim that
S^{\epsilon}=+\infty
. Indeed, suppose by contradiction that
S^{\epsilon}<+\infty
. By definition of
S^{\epsilon}
, it turns out that

\max_{i=1,\dots,N}\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle<M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon,\quad\forall t\in(0,S^{\epsilon}),

(2.15)

\lim_{t\to S^{\epsilon-}}\max_{i=1,\dots,N}\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle=M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon.

(2.16)

For all
i=1,\dots,N
and
t\in(0,S^{\epsilon})
, we have that

\frac{d}{dt}\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j:j\neq i}\chi_{ij}\alpha_{ij}(t)\psi(x_{i}(t),x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t)))\langle x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))-x_{i}(t),v\rangle.

Now, being
t\in(0,S^{\epsilon})
, it holds that
t-\tau_{ij}(t)\in(-\tau,S^{\epsilon})
. Then, from (
2.15
)

\langle x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t)),v\rangle<M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon,\quad\forall j=1,\dots,N,

(2.17)

where hare we have used the fact that the second inequality in (
2.14
) is satisfied in
[-\tau,0]
.

Therefore, using (
2.4
), (
2.15
), (
2.17
) and recalling that
\chi_{ij},\alpha_{ij}\leq 1
, for a.e.
t\in(0,S^{\epsilon})
we can write

\frac{d}{dt}\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle\leq\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j:j\neq i}\chi_{ij}\alpha_{ij}(t)\psi(x_{i}(t),x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t)))(M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon-\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle)

\leq K(M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon-\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle).

Thus, the Gronwall’s inequality yields

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69046pt\displaystyle{\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle\leq e^{-Kt}\langle x_{i}(0),v\rangle+K(M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-K(t-s)}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 48.36958pt=e^{-Kt}\langle x_{i}(0),v\rangle+(M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon)e^{-Kt}(e^{Kt}-1)}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 48.36958pt=e^{-Kt}\langle x_{i}(0),v\rangle+(M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon)(1-e^{-Kt})}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 48.36958pt\leq e^{-Kt}M_{0}^{v}+M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon-M_{0}^{v}e^{-Kt}-\epsilon e^{-Kt}}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 48.36958pt=M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon-\epsilon e^{-Kt}}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 48.36958pt\leq M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon-\epsilon e^{-KS^{\epsilon}},}\end{array}

for all
t\in(0,S^{\epsilon})
. We have so proved that,
\forall i=1,\dots,N,

\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle\leq M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon-\epsilon e^{-KS^{\epsilon}},\quad\forall t\in(0,S^{\epsilon}).

Thus, we get

\max_{i=1,\dots,N}\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle\leq M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon-\epsilon e^{-KS^{\epsilon}},\quad\forall t\in(0,S^{\epsilon}).

(2.18)

Letting
t\to S^{\epsilon-}
in (
2.18
), from (
2.16
) we have that

M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon\leq M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon-\epsilon e^{-KS^{\epsilon}}<M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon,

which is a contradiction. Thus,
S^{\epsilon}=+\infty
and

\max_{i=1,\dots,N}\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle<M_{0}^{v}+\epsilon,\quad\forall t>0.

From the arbitrariness of
\epsilon
we can conclude that

\max_{i=1,\dots,N}\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle\leq M_{0}^{v},\quad\forall t>0,

from which

\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle\leq M_{0}^{v},\quad\forall t>0,\,\forall i=1,\dots,N.

So, the second inequality in (
2.14
) is proven.

Now, to show that the other inequality holds, fix
v\in{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
. Then, for all
i=1,\dots,N
and
t>0
, by applying the second inequality in (
2.14
) to the vector
-v\in{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
we get

-\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle=\langle x_{i}(t),-v\rangle\leq\max_{j=1,\dots,N}\max_{s\in[-\tau,0]}\langle x_{j}(s),-v\rangle

=-\min_{j=1,\dots,N}\min_{s\in[-\tau,0]}\langle x_{j}(s),v\rangle=-m_{0}^{v},

from which

\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle\geq m_{0}^{v},\quad\forall t\geq 0,\,\forall i=1,\dots,N.

Thus, also the first inequality in (
2.14
) is fulfilled.
∎

Using the same arguments employed in the proof of the previous lemma, one can prove the following more general result.

Lemma 2.3
.

For each vector
v\in{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have that

m_{n}^{v}\leq\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle\leq M_{n}^{v},

(2.19)

for all
t\geq n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)-\tau
and for any
i=1,\dots,N
.

Now, we define the following quantities.

Definition 2.3
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

D_{n}:=\max_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\max_{r,s\in I_{n}}\lvert x_{i}(r)-x_{j}(s)\rvert.

Let us note that, for
n=0
,

D_{0}:=\max_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\max_{r,s\in I_{0}}\lvert x_{i}(r)-x_{j}(s)\rvert=\max_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\max_{r,s\in[-\tau,0]}\lvert x_{i}(r)-x_{j}(s)\rvert.

So, the exponential decay estimate in (
2.13
) can be written as

d(t)\leq e^{-C(t-\gamma(T+\tau)-\tau)}D_{0},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

Lemma 2.4
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have that

\lvert x_{i}(s)-x_{j}(t)\rvert\leq D_{n},

(2.20)

for all
s,t\geq n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)-\tau
and for any
i,j=1,\dots,N
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Let
i,j=1,\dots,N
and
s,t\geq n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)-\tau
. Then, if
\lvert x_{i}(s)-x_{j}(t)\rvert=0
, (
2.20
) is obviously satisfied. So we can assume
\lvert x_{i}(s)-x_{j}(t)\rvert>0
. Let us define the unit vector

v=\frac{x_{i}(s)-x_{j}(t)}{\lvert x_{i}(s)-x_{j}(t)\rvert}.

Then, using (
2.19
) and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have that

\lvert x_{i}(s)-x_{j}(t)\rvert=\langle x_{i}(s)-x_{j}(t),v\rangle=\langle x_{i}(s),v\rangle-\langle x_{j}(t),v\rangle\leq M_{n}^{v}-m_{n}^{v}

\leq\max_{k,l=1,\dots,N}\max_{r,\sigma\in I_{n}}\lvert x_{k}(r)-x_{l}(\sigma)\rvert=D_{n}.

∎

Remark 2.5
.

Note that (
2.20
) yields

d(t)\leq D_{n},\quad\forall t\geq n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)-\tau.

(2.21)

Moreover, from (
2.20
) it comes that

D_{n+1}\leq D_{n},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(2.22)

Next, we show that the agents’ opinions are bounded by a constant that depends of the initial data.

Lemma 2.6
.

For every
i=1,\dots,N,
we have that

\lvert x_{i}(t)\rvert\leq C_{0},\quad\forall t\geq-\tau,

(2.23)

where
C_{0}
is the constant defined in (
2.8
).

Proof.

Given
i=1,\dots,N
and
t\geq-\tau
, if
\lvert x_{i}(t)\rvert=0
, then trivially
C_{0}\geq\lvert x_{i}(t)\rvert
. On the contrary, if
\lvert x_{i}(t)\rvert>0
, we define

v=\frac{x_{i}(t)}{\lvert x_{i}(t)\rvert},

which is a unit vector. Then, by applying (
2.14
) and by using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

\lvert x_{i}(t)\rvert=\langle x_{i}(t),v\rangle\leq M_{0}^{v}=\max_{j=1,\dots,N}\max_{s\in[-\tau,0]}\langle x_{j}(s),v\rangle

\leq\max_{j=1,\dots,N}\max_{s\in[-\tau,0]}\lvert x_{j}(s)\rvert\lvert v\rvert=\max_{j=1,\dots,N}\max_{s\in[-\tau,0]}\lvert x_{j}(s)\rvert=C_{0},

and (
2.23
) is satisfied.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

From the estimate (
2.23
), since the influence function

is continuous, we deduce that

\psi(x_{i}(t),x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t)))\geq\psi_{0},

for all

for all

2.2
Consensus estimate

In order to prove the consensus result, we need the following crucial proposition, inspired by a previous argument in
[
21
]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

For all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

Proposition 2.10

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Fix
v\in{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
. Let us define the quantities

\mathcal{D}^{v}_{n}:=M^{v}_{n}-m^{v}_{n},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where
M_{n}^{v}
,
m_{n}^{v}
are the constants introduced in Definition
2.2
. Note that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have
\mathcal{D}^{v}_{n}\geq 0
, being
M_{n}^{v}\geq m_{n}^{v}
.

Let
\Gamma\in(0,1)
be the constant in (
2.26
). We claim that

\mathcal{D}^{v}_{n+1}\leq(1-\Gamma)\mathcal{D}_{n}^{v},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(2.34)

Indeed, fix
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Let
i,j=1,\dots,N
and
s,t\in I_{n+1}
be such that
\langle x_{i}(s),v\rangle=M^{v}_{n+1}
and
\langle x_{j}(t),v\rangle=m^{v}_{n+1}
. Then, applying Lemma
2.10
, we can write

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 42.67912pt\mathcal{D}^{v}_{n+1}=M^{v}_{n+1}-m^{v}_{n+1}=\langle x_{i}(s),v\rangle-\langle x_{j}(t),v\rangle}\\

(2.35)

Now, we distinguish four cases.

Case I)
Assume that
M_{n}^{v}-\tilde{m}_{n}^{v}=0
and
\tilde{M}_{n}^{v}-m_{n}^{v}=0
. Then, since from (
2.19
)

m_{n}^{v}\leq\tilde{m}_{n}^{v}\leq\tilde{M}_{n}^{v}=m_{n}^{v},

2.35
) becomes

\mathcal{D}^{v}_{n+1}\leq 0=(1-\Gamma)\mathcal{D}^{v}_{n}.

Case II)

M_{n}^{v}-\tilde{m}_{n}^{v}=0

\tilde{M}_{n}^{v}-m_{n}^{v}>0

2.35

\mathcal{D}^{v}_{n+1}\leq M_{n}^{v}-m_{n}^{v}-\Gamma\tilde{M}_{n}^{v}+\Gamma m_{n}^{v}=(1-\Gamma)(M_{n}^{v}-m_{n}^{v})=(1-\Gamma)\mathcal{D}^{v}_{n}.

M_{n}^{v}-\tilde{m}_{n}^{v}>0
and
\tilde{M}_{n}^{v}-m_{n}^{v}=0
. Then, from (
2.19
) we have

m_{n}^{v}\leq\tilde{m}_{n}^{v}\leq\tilde{M}_{n}^{v}=m_{n}^{v},

\tilde{m}_{n}^{v}=m_{n}^{v}.

2.35

\mathcal{D}^{v}_{n+1}\leq M_{n}^{v}-m_{n}^{v}-\Gamma M_{n}^{v}+\Gamma\tilde{m}_{n}^{v}=(1-\Gamma)(M_{n}^{v}-m_{n}^{v})=(1-\Gamma)\mathcal{D}^{v}_{n}.

\tilde{M}_{n}^{v}-m_{n}^{v}>0

3
The second-order alignment model

Consider a finite set of
N\in\mathbb{N}
particles, with
N\geq 2
. Let
x_{i}(t)\in{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
and
v_{i}(t)\in{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
denote the position and the velocity of the
i
-th particle at time
t
, respectively. We shall denote with
\lvert\cdot\rvert
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the usual norm and scalar product in
{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
, respectively. The interactions between the elements of the system are described by the following Cucker-Smale type model with a variable time delay

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}x_{i}(t)=v_{i}(t),\quad&t>0,\,\,\forall i=1,\dots,N,\\
\frac{d}{dt}v_{i}(t)=\underset{j:j\neq i}{\sum}\chi_{ij}\alpha_{ij}(t)c_{ij}(t)(v_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))-v_{i}(t)),\quad&t>0,\,\,\forall i=1,\dots,N,\end{cases}

(3.39)

where the time delay functions
\tau_{ij}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
are as in (
2.2
), the terms
\chi_{ij}
are defined as in (
2.5
) and the weight functions
\alpha_{ij}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
are
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-measurable and satisfy the Persistence Excitation Condition
(PE)
.

Here, the communication rates
c_{ij}
of the form

c_{ij}(t):=\frac{1}{N-1}\tilde{\psi}(\lvert x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))\rvert),\quad\forall t>0,\,\forall i,j=1,\dots,N,

(3.40)

where
\tilde{\psi}:{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}\rightarrow{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}
is a positive, bounded and continuous function and we denote by

\tilde{K}:=\lVert\tilde{\psi}\rVert_{\infty}.

The initial conditions

x_{i}(s)=x^{0}_{i}(s),\quad v_{i}(s)=v^{0}_{i}(s),\quad\forall s\in[-\tau,0],\,\forall i=1,\dots,N,

(3.41)

are assumed to be continuous functions.

We set

C^{V}_{0}:=\max_{i=1,\dots,N}\,\,\max_{s\in[-\tau,0]}\lvert v_{i}(s)\rvert,

(3.42)

M_{0}^{X}:=\max_{i=1,\dots,N}\,\,\max_{s,t\in[-\tau,0]}\lvert x_{i}(s)-x_{i}(t)\rvert.

(3.43)

We define the space and velocity diameters as follows

d_{X}(t):=\max_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\lvert x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)\rvert,\quad\forall t\geq-\tau

d_{V}(t):=\max_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\lvert v_{i}(t)-v_{j}(t)\rvert,\quad\forall t\geq-\tau.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a solution
\{(x_{i},v_{i})\}_{i=1,\dots,N}
to system (
3.39
) exhibits
asymptotic flocking
if it satisfies the two following conditions:

1.

there exists a positive constant
d^{*}
such that

\sup_{t\geq-{\tau}}d_{X}(t)\leq d^{*};

2.

\underset{t\to\infty}{\lim}d_{V}(t)=0.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume (
2.2
) and that the digraph
\mathcal{G}
is strongly connected. Let
\tilde{\psi}:{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}\rightarrow{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}
be a positive, bounded, continuous function that satisfies

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\min_{r\in[0,t]}\tilde{\psi}(r)\right)^{\gamma}dt=+\infty,

(3.44)

where
\gamma
is the depth of the digraph. Assume that the weight functions
\alpha_{ij}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
are

{\mathcal{L}^{1}}
-measurable and satisfy
(PE)
. Moreover, let
x^{0}_{i},v_{i}^{0}:[-{\tau},0]\rightarrow{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
be continuous functions, for any
i=1,\dots,N
.
Then, for every solution
\{(x_{i},v_{i})\}_{i=1,\dots,N}
to (
3.39
) with the initial conditions (
3.41
), there exists a positive constant
d^{*}
such that

\sup_{t\geq-{\tau}}d_{X}(t)\leq d^{*},

(3.45)

and there exists a positive constant
\mu
for which the following exponential decay estimate holds

d_{V}(t)\leq\left(\max_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\,\,\max_{r,s\in[-\tau,0]}\lvert v_{i}(r)-v_{j}(s)\rvert\right)e^{-\mu(t-\gamma(T+\tau)-\tau)},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

(3.46)

Remark 3.2
.

Let us note that, if the function
\tilde{\psi}
is nonincreasing and the interaction is universal, i.e.
\gamma=1
, then the condition (
3.44
) reduces to

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{\psi}(t)dt=+\infty,

which is the classical assumption to obtain the unconditional flocking (see e.g.
[
33
]
). Since here we deal with an influence function not necessarily monotonic and the interaction is not universal, we require the stronger assumption (
3.44
) (cf.
[
13
]
for the case of universal interaction).

3.1
Preliminary lemmas

Let
\{x_{i},v_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,N}
be solution to (
3.39
) under the initial conditions (
3.41
). We assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
are satisfied. The following lemmas hold. We omit their proofs since they can be proved using the same arguments employed in Section
2
.

Definition 3.2
.

Given a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we define

{r}_{n}^{v}:=\min_{j=1,\dots,N}\min_{s\in I_{n}}\,\langle v_{j}(s),v\rangle,

{R}_{n}^{v}:=\max_{j=1,\dots,N}\max_{s\in I_{n}}\,\langle v_{j}(s),v\rangle,

where, as in the previous section,

I_{n}=[n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)-\tau,n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)].

Also, we define, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\tilde{r}_{n}^{v}:=\min_{j=1,\dots,N}\langle v_{j}(n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)),v\rangle,

\tilde{R}_{n}^{v}:=\max_{j=1,\dots,N}\langle v_{j}(n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)),v\rangle.

Lemma 3.3
.

For each vector
v\in{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{d}
and for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have that

r_{n}^{v}\leq\langle v_{i}(t),v\rangle\leq R_{n}^{v},

(3.47)

for all
t\geq n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)-\tau
and for any
i=1,\dots,N
.

Definition 3.3
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we define

F_{n}:=\max_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\max_{r,s\in I_{n}}\lvert v_{i}(r)-v_{j}(s)\rvert.

Remark 3.4
.

Let us note that

F_{0}:=\max_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\max_{r,s\in I_{0}}\lvert v_{i}(r)-v_{j}(s)\rvert=\max_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\max_{r,s\in[-\tau,0]}\lvert v_{i}(r)-v_{j}(s)\rvert.

Then, the exponential decay estimate in (
3.46
) can be written as

d_{V}(t)\leq e^{-\mu(t-\gamma(T+\tau)-\tau)}F_{0},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

Lemma 3.5
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have that

\lvert v_{i}(s)-v_{j}(t)\rvert\leq F_{n},

(3.48)

for all
s,t\geq n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)-\tau
and for any
i,j=1,\dots,N
.

Remark 3.6
.

Let us note that (
3.48
) yields

d_{V}(t)\leq F_{n},\quad\forall t\geq n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)-\tau.

(3.49)

Furthermore, from (
3.48
) it follows that

F_{n+1}\leq F_{n},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.50)

Also, arguing as in Section
2
, we can find a bound on the velocities
|v_{i}(t)|,
which is uniform with respect to
t
and
i=1,\dots,N.

Lemma 3.7
.

For every
i=1,\dots,N,
we have that

\lvert v_{i}(t)\rvert\leq C^{V}_{0},\quad\forall t\geq-\tau,

(3.51)

where
C^{V}_{0}
is the constant defined in (
3.42
).

Now, we provide the following result in which an estimate on the position diameters is established.

Lemma 3.8
.

For every
i,j=1,\dots,N
, we get

\lvert x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))\rvert\leq\tau C_{0}^{V}+M^{X}_{0}+d_{X}(t),\quad\forall t\geq 0,

(3.52)

where
C_{0}^{V}
and
M^{X}_{0}
are the positive constants in (
3.42
) and (
3.43
), respectively.

Proof.

Given
i,j=1,\dots,N
and
t\geq 0
, we have

\begin{split}\lvert x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))\rvert&\leq\lvert x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)\rvert+\lvert x_{j}(t)-x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))\rvert\\
&\leq d_{X}(t)+\lvert x_{j}(t)-x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))\rvert.\end{split}

(3.53)

Now, we estimate

\lvert x_{j}(t)-x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))\rvert.

If
t-\tau_{ij}(t)>0
, from (
2.2
) and (
3.51
) we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\lvert x_{j}(t)-x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))\rvert\leq\int_{t-\tau_{ij}(t)}^{t}\lvert v_{j}(s)\rvert ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt\leq C^{V}_{0}\tau_{ij}(t)\leq\tau C^{V}_{0}.}\end{array}

On the other hand, if
t-\tau_{ij}(t)\leq 0
, then
t\leq\tau
and

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\lvert x_{j}(t)-x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))\rvert\leq\lvert x_{j}(0)-x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))\rvert+\int_{0}^{t}\lvert v_{j}(s)\rvert ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt\leq M^{X}_{0}+tC^{V}_{0}\leq M^{X}_{0}+\tau C^{V}_{0}.}\end{array}

Therefore, in both cases,

\lvert x_{j}(t)-x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))\rvert\leq M^{X}_{0}+\tau C^{V}_{0},

from which (
3.53
) becomes

\lvert x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))\rvert\leq M^{X}_{0}+\tau C^{V}_{0}+d_{X}(t).

∎

3.2
The flocking estimate

To prove the flocking result we need, as before, a crucial proposition.
First of all, we give the following definition.

Definition 3.4
.

We define

\tilde{\phi}(t):=\min\left\{\tilde{\psi}(r):r\in\left[0,\tau C^{V}_{0}+M_{X}^{0}+\max_{s\in[-\tau,t]}d_{X}(s)\right]\right\},

for all
t\geq-\tau
.

Remark 3.9
.

Let us note that from (
3.52
)

\tilde{\psi}(\lvert x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t-\tau_{ij}(t))\rvert)\geq\tilde{\phi}(t),\quad\forall t\geq 0,\,\forall i,j=1,\dots,N.

from which

c_{ij}(t)\geq\frac{1}{N-1}\tilde{\phi}(t),\quad\forall t\geq 0,\,\forall i,j=1,\dots,N.

(3.54)

Proposition 3.10
.

For all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds

r_{0}^{v}+\Gamma_{1}(\tilde{R}^{v}_{0}-r^{v}_{0})\leq\langle v_{i}(t),v\rangle\leq R^{v}_{0}-\Gamma_{1}(R^{v}_{0}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v}),

(3.55)

for all
t\in[\gamma(T+\tau),\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau]
and for all
i=1,\dots,N
, where
\Gamma_{1}
is the positive constant defined as follows

\Gamma_{1}:=e^{-K(\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{2}+3\gamma)(T+\tau)+\tau)}\left(\frac{\tilde{\phi}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)\tilde{\alpha}}{N-1}\right)^{\gamma}.

(3.56)

Remark 3.11
.

Let us note that, from
(PE)
,
\Gamma_{1}\in(0,1)
since
\tilde{\alpha}K\leq 1
.

Proof.

Fix
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
L=1,\dots,N
be such that
\langle v_{L}(0),v\rangle=\tilde{r}_{0}^{v}
. Note that from (
3.47
),
R^{v}_{0}\geq\tilde{r}^{v}_{0}
. Then, for a.e.
t\in[0,\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau]
, from (
3.47
)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\frac{d}{dt}\langle v_{L}(t),v\rangle=\sum_{j:j\neq L}\chi_{Lj}\alpha_{Lj}(t)c_{Lj}(t)(\langle v_{j}(t-\tau_{Lj}(t)),v\rangle-\langle v_{L}(t),v\rangle)}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 42.67912pt\leq\sum_{j:j\neq L}\chi_{Lj}\alpha_{Lj}(t)c_{Lj}(t)(R_{0}^{v}-\langle v_{L}(t),v\rangle)}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 42.67912pt\leq\frac{\tilde{K}}{N-1}\sum_{j:j\neq L}(R_{0}^{v}-\langle v_{L}(t),v\rangle)=\tilde{K}(R_{0}^{v}-\langle v_{L}(t),v\rangle).}\end{array}

Thus, the Gronwall’s inequality yields

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\langle v_{L}(t),v\rangle\leq e^{-\tilde{K}t}\langle v_{L}(0),v\rangle+R_{0}^{v}(1-e^{-\tilde{K}t})}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 42.67912pt=R_{0}^{v}-e^{-\tilde{K}t}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 42.67912pt\leq R_{0}^{v}-e^{-\tilde{K}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v}).}\end{array}

Therefore, we have

\langle v_{L}(t),v\rangle\leq R_{0}^{v}-e^{-\tilde{K}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v}),\quad\forall t\in[0,\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau].

(3.57)

Now, let
i_{1}=1,\dots,N\setminus\{L\}
be such that
\chi_{i_{1}L}=1
. Such an index
i_{1}
exists since the digraph is strongly connected. Then, for a.e.
t\in[\tau,\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau]
, from (
3.57
) we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\frac{d}{dt}\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle=\sum_{j\neq i_{1},L}\chi_{i_{1}j}\alpha_{i_{1}j}(t)c_{i_{1}j}(t)(\langle v_{j}(t-\tau_{i_{1}j}(t)),v\rangle-\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle)}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt+\alpha_{i_{1}L}(t)c_{i_{1}L}(t)(\langle v_{L}(t-\tau_{i_{1}L}(t)),v\rangle-\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle)}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\sum_{j\neq i_{1},L}\alpha_{i_{1}j}(t)\chi_{i_{1}j}c_{i_{1}j}(t)(R_{0}^{v}-\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle)}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt+\alpha_{i_{1}L}(t)c_{i_{1}L}(t)\left(R_{0}^{v}-e^{-\tilde{K}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})-\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle\right)}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt=(R_{0}^{v}-\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle)\sum_{j\neq i_{1},L}\chi_{i_{1}j}\alpha_{i_{1}j}(t)c_{i_{1}j}(t)}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt+\alpha_{i_{1}L}(t)c_{i_{1}L}(t)\left(R_{0}^{v}-e^{-\tilde{K}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})-\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle\right).}\end{array}

Note that

\sum_{j\neq i_{1},L}\chi_{i_{1}j}\alpha_{i_{1}j}(t)c_{i_{1}j}(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i_{1}\end{subarray}}\chi_{i_{1}j}\alpha_{i_{1}j}(t)c_{i_{1}j}(t)-\alpha_{i_{1}L}(t)c_{i_{1}L}(t)

\leq\frac{\tilde{K}}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i_{1},L}\chi_{i_{1}j}-\alpha_{i_{1}L}(t)c_{i_{1}L}(t)=\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{1}}}{N-1}-\alpha_{i_{1}L}(t)c_{i_{1}L}(t).

Thus, from (
3.54
) it comes that

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\frac{d}{dt}\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle\leq\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{1}}}{N-1}(R_{0}^{v}-\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle)-\alpha_{i_{1}L}(t)c_{i_{1}L}(t)(R_{0}^{v}-\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle)}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 85.35826pt+\alpha_{i_{1}L}(t)c_{i_{1}L}(t)\left(R_{0}^{v}-e^{-\tilde{K}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})-\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle\right)}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{1}}}{N-1}(R_{0}^{v}-\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle)-\alpha_{i_{1}L}(t)\frac{\tilde{\phi}(t)}{N-1}e^{-\tilde{K}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt=\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{1}}}{N-1}R_{0}^{v}-\alpha_{i_{1}L}(t)\frac{\tilde{\phi}(t)}{N-1}e^{-\tilde{K}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})-\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{1}}}{N-1}\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle).}\end{array}

Hence, the Gronwall’s estimate yields

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle\leq e^{-\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{1}}}{N-1}(t-\tau)}\langle v_{i_{1}}(\tau),v\rangle)+R^{v}_{0}(1-e^{-\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{1}}}{N-1}(t-\tau)})}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt-e^{-\tilde{K}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})\frac{1}{N-1}\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{\phi}(s)\alpha_{i_{1}L}(s)e^{-\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{1}}}{N-1}(t-s)}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leq e^{-\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{1}}}{N-1}(t-\tau)}R_{0}^{v}+R^{v}_{0}(1-e^{-\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{1}}}{N-1}(t-\tau)})}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt-e^{-\tilde{K}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})e^{-\tilde{K}\gamma(T+\tau)}\frac{1}{N-1}\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{\phi}(s)\alpha_{i_{1}L}(s)ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt=R^{v}_{0}-e^{-\tilde{K}(2\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})\frac{1}{N-1}\int_{\tau}^{t}\tilde{\phi}(s)\alpha_{i_{1}L}(s)ds,}\end{array}

for all
t\in[\tau,\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau]
. Note that, since
\tilde{\phi}
is a nonincreasing function,

\tilde{\phi}(t)\geq\tilde{\phi}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau),\quad\forall t\in[0,\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau].

(3.58)

Then, we can write

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle\leq R^{v}_{0}-e^{-\tilde{K}(2\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})\frac{\tilde{\phi}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}{N-1}\int_{\tau}^{t}\alpha_{i_{1}L}(s)ds,}\end{array}

for all
t\in[\tau,\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau]
. In particular, for
t\in[T+\tau,\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau]
, we find

\langle v_{i_{1}}(t),v\rangle\leq R^{v}_{0}-e^{-\tilde{K}(2\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})\frac{\tilde{\phi}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}{N-1}\tilde{\alpha},

(3.59)

where here we have used the fact that (
2.6
) implies the following inequality

\int_{\tau}^{t}\alpha_{i_{1}L}(s)ds\geq\int_{\tau}^{T+\tau}\alpha_{i_{1}L}(s)ds\geq\tilde{\alpha}.

Now, if
\gamma=1
, (
3.59
) holds true for each agent. On the other hand, if
\gamma>1
, let us consider an index
i_{2}\in\{1,\dots,N\}\setminus\{i_{1}\}
such that
\chi_{i_{2}i_{1}}=1
. Then, for a.e.
t\in[T+2\tau,\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau]
, from (
3.59
) it comes that

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\frac{d}{dt}\langle v_{i_{2}}(t),v\rangle=\sum_{j\neq i_{1},i_{2}}\chi_{i_{2}j}\alpha_{i_{2}j}(t)c_{i_{2}j}(t)(\langle v_{j}(t-\tau_{i_{2}j}(t)),v\rangle-\langle v_{i_{2}}(t),v\rangle)}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt+\alpha_{i_{2}i_{1}}(t)c_{i_{2}i_{1}}(t)(\langle v_{i_{1}}(t-\tau_{i_{2}i_{1}}(t)),v\rangle-\langle v_{i_{2}}(t),v\rangle)}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leq(R_{0}^{v}-\langle v_{i_{2}}(t),v\rangle)\sum_{j\neq i_{1},i_{2}}\chi_{i_{2}j}\alpha_{i_{2}j}(t)c_{i_{2}j}(t)}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt+\alpha_{i_{2}i_{1}}(t)c_{i_{2}i_{1}}(t)\left(R^{v}_{0}-e^{-\tilde{K}(2\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})\frac{\tilde{\phi}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}{N-1}\tilde{\alpha}-\langle v_{i_{2}}(t),v\rangle\right).}\end{array}

Hence, arguing as above we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\frac{d}{dt}\langle v_{i_{2}}(t),v\rangle\leq\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{2}}}{N-1}(R_{0}^{v}-\langle x_{i_{2}}(t),v\rangle)-\alpha_{i_{2}i_{1}}(t)c_{i_{2}i_{1}}(t)(R_{0}^{v}-\langle v_{i_{2}}(t),v\rangle)}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt+\alpha_{i_{2}i_{1}}(t)c_{i_{2}i_{1}}(t)\left(R^{v}_{0}-e^{-\tilde{K}(2\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})\frac{\tilde{\phi}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}{N-1}\tilde{\alpha}-\langle x_{i_{2}}(t),v\rangle\right)}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{2}}}{N-1}(R_{0}^{v}-\langle v_{i_{2}}(t),v\rangle)-\alpha_{i_{2}i_{1}}(t)e^{-\tilde{K}(2\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})\frac{\tilde{\phi}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}{(N-1)^{2}}\tilde{\phi}(t)\tilde{\alpha}.}\end{array}

Again, using Gronwall’s estimate it comes that

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\langle v_{i_{2}}(t),v\rangle\leq e^{-\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{2}}}{N-1}(t-T-2\tau)}\langle v_{i_{2}}(T+2\tau),v\rangle)+R^{v}_{0}(1-e^{-\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{2}}}{N-1}(t-T-2\tau)})}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt-e^{-\tilde{K}(2\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})\frac{\tilde{\phi}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}{(N-1)^{2}}\tilde{\alpha}\int_{T+2\tau}^{t}\tilde{\phi}(s)\alpha_{i_{2}i_{1}}(s)e^{-\frac{\tilde{K}N_{i_{2}}}{N-1}(t-s)}ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 14.22636pt\leq R^{v}_{0}-e^{-\tilde{K}(3\gamma(T+\tau)-T)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})\frac{\tilde{\phi}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}{(N-1)^{2}}\tilde{\alpha}\int_{T+2\tau}^{t}\tilde{\phi}(s)\alpha_{i_{2}i_{1}}(s)ds,}\end{array}

for all
t\in[T+2\tau,\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau]
. In particular, for
t\in[2T+2\tau,\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau]
, the condition (
2.6
) and the inequality (
3.58
) imply that

\langle v_{i_{2}}(t),v\rangle\leq R^{v}_{0}-e^{-\tilde{K}(3\gamma(T+\tau)-T)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})\left(\frac{\tilde{\phi}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}{N-1}\right)^{2}\tilde{\alpha}^{2}.

(3.60)

Finally, iterating the above procedure along the path
i_{0},i_{1},\dots,i_{r},
with
r\leq\gamma,
starting from
i_{0}=L
we find the following upper bound

\langle v_{i_{k}}(t),v\rangle\leq R^{v}_{0}-e^{-K((k+1)\gamma(T+\tau)-(T+\tau)\left(\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}l\right)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})\left(\frac{\tilde{\phi}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)\tilde{\alpha}}{N-1}\right)^{k},

(3.61)

for all
1\leq k\leq r
and for all
t\in[k(T+\tau),\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau]
. In particular, if the path has length
\gamma
, for
k=\gamma
, since
\sum_{l=0}^{\gamma-1}l=\frac{\gamma(\gamma-1)}{2}
, inequality (
3.61
) reads as

\langle v_{i_{\gamma}}(t),v\rangle\leq R^{v}_{0}-e^{-K(\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{2}+3\gamma)(T+\tau)+\tau)}(R_{0}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{0}^{v})\left(\frac{\tilde{\phi}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)\tilde{\alpha}}{N-1}\right)^{\gamma},

(3.62)

for all
t\in[\gamma(T+\tau),\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau]
.
Arguing as in Proposition
2.8
, we can say that (
3.62
) holds for every
i=1,\dots,N
.

Now, let
R=1,\dots,N
be such that
\tilde{R}_{0}^{v}=\langle v_{R}(0),v\rangle
. Then, arguing as before, we get

\langle v_{R}(t),v\rangle\geq r^{v}_{0}+e^{-K(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}(\tilde{R}_{0}^{v}-r_{0}^{v}),\quad\forall t\in[0,\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau].

(3.63)

Employing the same arguments used above, we can conclude that

\langle v_{i}(t),v\rangle\geq r^{v}_{0}+e^{-K(\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{2}+3\gamma)(T+\tau)+\tau)}(\tilde{R}_{0}^{v}-r_{0}^{v}))\left(\frac{\tilde{\phi}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)\tilde{\alpha}}{N-1}\right)^{\gamma},

for all
t\in[\gamma(T+\tau),\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau]
and for all
i=1,\dots,N
. Finally, we can deduce that estimate (
3.55
) holds.
∎

The following proposition extends the previous one in successive time intervals. We omit its proof since it is analogous to the previous one.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds

r^{v}_{n}+\Gamma_{n+1}(\tilde{R}^{v}_{n}-r^{v}_{n})\leq\langle v_{i}(t),v\rangle\leq R^{v}_{n}-\Gamma_{n+1}(R^{v}_{n}-\tilde{r}^{v}_{n}),

(3.64)

for all
t\in I_{n+1}
and for all
i=1,\dots,N
, where
\Gamma_{n+1}
is the positive constant defined as

\Gamma_{n+1}:=e^{-K(\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{2}+3\gamma)(T+\tau)+\tau)}\left(\frac{\tilde{\phi}((n+1)(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau))\tilde{\alpha}}{N-1}\right)^{\gamma}.

(3.65)

Remark 3.13
.

Let us note that from (
3.64
) it comes that

R_{n+1}^{v}-r_{n+1}^{v}\leq(1-\Gamma_{n+1})(R_{n}^{v}-r_{n}^{v}),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.66)

where
\Gamma_{n+1}\in(0,1)
is the constant in (
3.65
).

Indeed, given
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let
i,j=1,\dots,N
and
s,t\in I_{n+1}
be such that
\langle v_{i}(s),v\rangle=R^{v}_{n+1}
and
\langle v_{j}(t),v\rangle=r^{v}_{n+1}
. Then, applying Lemma
3.12
, we can write

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 42.67912ptR^{v}_{n+1}-r^{v}_{n+1}=\langle v_{i}(s),v\rangle-\langle v_{j}(t),v\rangle}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 85.35826pt\leq R_{n}^{v}-r_{n}^{v}-\Gamma_{n+1}(R_{n}^{v}-\tilde{r}_{n}^{v})-\Gamma_{n+1}(\tilde{R}_{n}^{v}-r_{n}^{v}).}\end{array}

(3.67)

Then, arguing as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we get that estimate (
3.66
) holds true.

Also, setting
C^{*}:=e^{-K(\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{2}+3\gamma)(T+\tau)+\tau)}\left(\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{N-1}\right)^{\gamma},

it holds that

\Gamma_{n+1}=C^{*}(\tilde{\phi}((n+1)(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)))^{\gamma},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.68)

As a consequence, (
3.66
) can be written as

R_{n+1}^{v}-r_{n+1}^{v}\leq(1-C^{*}(\tilde{\phi}((n+1)(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)))^{\gamma})(R_{n}^{v}-r_{n}^{v}),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.69)

In particular, from (
3.66
) and (
3.69
), arguing as in Theorem
2.1
, it comes that

F_{n+1}\leq(1-\Gamma_{n+1})F_{n},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(3.70)

or, equivalently,

F_{n+1}\leq(1-C^{*}(\tilde{\phi}((n+1)(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)))^{\gamma})F_{n},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(3.71)

Now, we are able to prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\{(x_{i},v_{i})\}_{i=1,\dots,N}
be solution to (
3.39
) under the initial conditions (
3.41
). Let us define

\tilde{\Gamma}_{n+1}=\frac{\Gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Then, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
it holds
\tilde{\Gamma}_{n+1}<\Gamma_{n+1}
.

Let us introduce the function
\mathcal{E}:[-\tau,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty),

\mathcal{E}(t):=\begin{cases}F_{0},\hskip 128.0374ptt\in[-\tau,\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau],\\
\mathcal{E}(n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau))\left(1-\tilde{\Gamma}_{n+1}(t-n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau))\right),\\
\vskip 5.69046pt\hskip 142.26378ptt\in(n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau),(n+1)(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)],\,n\geq 1.\end{cases}

By definition,
\mathcal{E}
is continuous, positive and nonincreasing. Moreover, we claim that

F_{n}\leq\mathcal{E}(t),\quad\forall t\in[-\tau,n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)],\,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.72)

We prove this by induction. For
n=1
, from (
3.50
) we can immediately say that

F_{1}\leq F_{0}=\mathcal{E}(t),\quad\forall t\in[-\tau,\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau].

Now, assume that (
3.72
) holds for some
n\geq 1
. We have to show that (
3.72
) is true also for
n+1
. From the induction hypothesis and by using again (
3.50
), we have that

F_{n+1}\leq F_{n}\leq\mathcal{E}(t),

for all
t\in[-\tau,n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)]
. It lasts to prove that
F_{n+1}\leq\mathcal{E}(t)
, for all
t\in(n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau),(n+1)(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)]
. From (
3.70
), it comes that

\mathcal{E}(t)\geq\mathcal{E}((n+1)(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau))=\mathcal{E}(n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau))(1-\tilde{\Gamma}_{n+1}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau))=(1-\Gamma_{n+1})F_{n}\geq F_{n+1},

for all
t\in(n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau),(n+1)(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)]
, where in the above inequalities we have used the fact that
\mathcal{E}
is nonincreasing. Hence, (
3.72
) is proven.

Now, for almost all time (see
[
13
]
for further details)

\frac{d}{dt}\max_{s\in[-\bar{\tau},t]}d_{X}(s)\leq\left\lvert\frac{d}{dt}d_{X}(t)\right\rvert\leq d_{V}(t).

(3.73)

Next, let us define the function
\mathcal{W}:[-\tau,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
,

\mathcal{W}(t):=(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)\mathcal{E}(t)+C^{*}\int_{0}^{\tau C^{V}_{0}+M^{X}_{0}+\underset{s\in[-\tau,t+\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau]}{\max}d_{X}(s)}\left(\min_{\sigma\in[0,r]}\tilde{\psi}(\sigma)\right)^{\gamma}\,dr,

for all
t\geq-\tau
. By construction,
\mathcal{W}
is continuous. Also, for each
n\geq 1
and for a.e.
t\in(n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau),(n+1)(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)
, from (
3.49
), (
3.72
) and (
3.73
) it follows that

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{W}(t)=(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(t)+C^{*}(\tilde{\phi}(t+\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau))^{\gamma}\,\frac{d}{dt}\underset{s\in[-\tau,t+(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)]}{\max}d_{X}(s)}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 14.22636pt\leq-\mathcal{E}(n\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)C^{*}(\tilde{\phi}((n+1)(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)))^{\gamma}}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\hskip 99.58464pt\displaystyle{+C^{*}(\tilde{\phi}(t+\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau))^{\gamma}d_{V}(t+(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau))}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 14.22636pt\leq C^{*}F_{n}(-(\tilde{\phi}((n+1)(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)))^{\gamma}+(\tilde{\phi}((n+1)(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau))^{\gamma}))=0.}\end{array}

Then,

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{W}(t)\leq 0,\quad\text{a.e. }t>\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau,

(3.74)

which implies

\mathcal{W}(t)\leq\mathcal{W}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau),\quad\forall t\geq\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau.

(3.75)

Now, by definition of
\mathcal{W}
, being
\mathcal{E}
a nonnegative function, we have

C^{*}\int_{0}^{\tau C^{V}_{0}+M^{X}_{0}+\underset{s\in[-\tau,t+\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau]}{\max}d_{X}(s)}\left(\min_{\sigma\in[0,r]}\tilde{\psi}(\sigma)\right)^{\gamma}\,dr\leq\mathcal{W}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau),

for all
t\geq\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau
. Letting
t\to\infty
in the above inequality, we can conclude that

C^{*}\int_{0}^{\tau C^{V}_{0}+M^{X}_{0}+\underset{s\in[-\tau,+\infty)}{\sup}d_{X}(s)}\left(\min_{\sigma\in[0,r]}\tilde{\psi}(\sigma)\right)^{\gamma}\,dr\leq\mathcal{W}(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)).

(3.76)

Finally, since the function
\tilde{\psi}
satisfies property (
3.44
), from (
3.76
), we can conclude that there exists a positive constant
d^{*}
such that

\tau C^{V}_{0}+M^{X}_{0}+\underset{s\in[-\tau,+\infty)}{\sup}d_{X}(s)\leq d^{*}.

(3.77)

Now, let us define

\hat{\phi}:=\min_{r\in[0,d^{*}]}\tilde{\psi}(r).

Note that
\tilde{\phi}^{*}>0
. Also, (
3.77
) yields

\hat{\phi}\leq\tilde{\phi}(t),\quad\forall t\geq-\tau.

(3.78)

Then, from (
3.71
)
and (
3.78
) we have

F_{n+1}\leq(1-C^{*}\hat{\phi}^{\gamma})F_{n},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.79)

Thus, thanks to an induction argument, we can write

F_{n}\leq(1-C^{*}\hat{\phi}^{\gamma})^{n}F_{0},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Note that the above inequality can be rewritten as

F_{n}\leq e^{-n\mu(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}F_{0},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(3.80)

where

\mu=\frac{1}{\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-C^{*}\hat{\phi}^{\gamma}}\right).

Finally, let
t\geq 0
. Then,
t\in[n(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau),(n+1)(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)]
, for some
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then, using (
3.49
) and (
3.80
)

d_{V}(t)\leq F_{n}\leq e^{-n\mu(\gamma(T+\tau)+\tau)}F_{0}\leq e^{-\mu(t-\gamma(T+\tau)-\tau)}F_{0},

which concludes our proof.
∎

The SLE loop via conformal welding of quantum disks
Morris Ang, Nina Holden, and Xin Sun

Abstract
. We prove that the SLE κ loop measure arises naturally from the conformal welding of two γ \gamma -Liouville quantum gravity (LQG) disks for γ 2 = κ ∈ ( 0 , 4 ) \gamma^{2}=\kappa\in(0,4) . The proof relies on our companion work on conformal welding of LQG disks and uses as an essential tool the concept of uniform embedding of LQG surfaces. Combining our result with work of Gwynne and Miller, we get that random quadrangulations decorated by a self-avoiding polygon converge in the scaling limit to the LQG sphere decorated by the SLE 8/3 loop. Our result is also a key input to recent work of the first and third coauthors on the integrability of the conformal loop ensemble. Finally, our result can be viewed as the random counterpart of an action functional identity due to Viklund and Wang.

Introduction

The Schramm-Loewner evolution (SLE) and Liouville quantum gravity (LQG) are central objects in random conformal geometry. It was shown by Sheffield
[
She16
]
and Duplantier-Miller-Sheffield
[
DMS21
]
that SLE curves arise as the interfaces of LQG surfaces under conformal welding. This phenomenon
is a cornerstone of the mating-of-trees framework
[
DMS21
]
for the SLE/LQG coupling and is a fundamental input to the link between LQG and the scaling limits of random planar maps. See e.g.
[
Law05
,
GHS19
,
Gwy20
,
BP21
,
She22
]
for an introduction to SLE, LQG, and their interactions.

Conformal welding results in
[
She16
,
DMS21
]
mainly focus on infinite-volume LQG surfaces. Recently in
[
AHS20b
]
, we showed that the conformal welding of finite-volume LQG surfaces called two-pointed quantum disks can give rise to some canonical variants of SLE curves with two marked points. In this paper, we show in Theorem
1.1
that when conformally welding two quantum disks without marked points, the interface is another canonical variant of SLE called the
SLE loop
. Moreover, the resulting LQG surface is the so-called
quantum sphere
(without marked point), which describes the scaling limit of classical random planar map models with spherical topology. For example, in the pure gravity case it corresponds to the Brownian map
[
Le 13
,
Mie13
,
MS15
]
.

As reviewed in Section

, the SLE loop is an important one-parameter family of conformally invariant random Jordan curves whose existence was conjectured by Kontsevitch and Suhov
[

]
and established by Zhan
[

]
. In particular, the

loop introduced by Werner
[

]
describes the conjectural scaling limit of self-avoiding polygons on planar lattices. Our conformal welding result Theorem

combined with earlier work of Gwynne and Miller

]
yields that uniform quadrangulations decorated by a self-avoiding loop converge to the Brownian map decorated by the SLE

. For

, the SLE

,

]
.

and

1.1.
The SLE loop via conformal welding

Kontsevitch and Suhov
[
KS07
]
, inspired by Malliavin
[
Mal99
]
, formulated a natural conformal restriction covariance property for loop measures, and they conjectured that there is a unique (up to multiplicative factor) one-parameter family of loop measures satisfying this property. We call loop measures satisfying this property a
Malliavin-Kontsevitch-Suhov (MKS) loop measure
and we index the measures by
\kappa\in(0,4]
.

Werner
[
Wer08
]
proved the existence and uniqueness of the MKS loop measure for
\kappa=8/3
and constructed the loop measure via the boundaries of Brownian loops. Kemppainen and Werner
[
KW16
]
constructed an MKS loop measure for
\kappa\in(8/3,4]

using the density measure of a nested simple CLE.
For
\kappa=2
, Benoist and Dubédat
[
BD16
]
proved that a measure constructed in
[
KK17
]
is an MKS loop measure. Finally, Zhan
[
Zha21
]
constructed an MKS loop measure for all
\kappa\in(0,4]
via SLE
κ
equipped with its natural parametrization and also extended the construction to
\kappa\in(4,8)
.
We denote Zhan’s MKS loop measure by
\SLE_{\kappa}^{\operatorname{loop}}
. See Section
2.5
for the precise definition.
For
\kappa\in(8/3,4]
, the loop measures of Zhan and Kemppainen-Werner agree; see
[
AS21
, Section 2.3]
.
We emphasize that
\SLE_{\kappa}^{\operatorname{loop}}
is an infinite measure for each
\kappa\in(0,4]
.

is a random field on

such that
the distribution of

viewed as a quantum surface is

Let

be a sample

Theorem 1.1
.

For

The proof of Theorem

builds on a welding result from
[

Given this result and the construction of Zhan’s MKS loop measure, Theorem

[

HRV18

Based on the integrability of LQG from mating-of-trees

[

In a forthcoming work of the first and third coauthors, it will be used to compute the renormalized probability that three given points are close to the same

Viklund and Wang
[

proved a beautiful identity between the Loewner energy of a Jordan curve

on the sphere and the sum of the Dirichlet energies of

[

[

Theorem

1.2.
The scaling limit of random planar maps decorated by self-avoiding loop

It is believed that the MKS loop with
\kappa=8/3
, namely Werner’s loop measure
[
Wer08
]
,
is the scaling limit of the critical self-avoiding loop on a regular planar lattice. We will argue that this result holds in an annealed sense in the setting of planar maps. Namely, the critical Boltzmann measure on quadrangulations decorated with a self-avoiding loop converges to the MKS-loop-decorated quantum sphere with
\gamma=\sqrt{8/3}
as curve-decorated metric measure spaces. The measure is called critical since the weight assigned to a loop-decorated quadrangulation has been tuned precisely so that the number of vertices of the quadrangulation and the length of the loop have a power-law behavior.

To state this result we will first introduce some notation; see Section
4
for more precise definitions. Let
\mathrm{MS}^{n}\otimes\operatorname{SAW}^{n}
denote the measure on pairs
(M,\eta)
where
M
is a quadrangulation,
\eta
is a self-avoiding loop on
M
, and a pair
(M,\eta)
has weight

n^{2.5}12^{-\#\mathcal{F}(M)}54^{-\#\eta},

where
\#\mathcal{F}(M)
is the number of faces of
M
and
\#\eta
is the number of edges of
\eta
. Note that the parameter
n
does not correspond to any quantity in the quadrangulation beyond the weights we use to define MS
n
and as a scaling factor for distances and areas (see Section
4
for the latter). We let
\mathrm{QS}\otimes\SLE_{8/3}^{\operatorname{loop}}
be as in Theorem
1.1

with
\gamma^{2}=\kappa=8/3
.
As we will explain in Section
4
, samples from

\mathrm{MS}^{n}\otimes\operatorname{SAW}^{n}
and
\mathrm{QS}\otimes\SLE_{8/3}^{\operatorname{loop}}
can be viewed
as loop-decorated metric measure spaces. In this setting the natural topology for weak convergence is the Gromov-Hausdorff-Prokhorov-uniform (GHPU) topology; see Section
2.6
for a precise definition. For a loop-decorated metric measure space and
c\in(0,1)
we let
A(c)
denote the event that the length of the loop is in
[c,c^{-1}]
. We use the notation
M|_{A(c)}
to stand for the restriction of the measure
M
to the event
A(c)
, and
use the symbol
\Rightarrow
to indicate weak convergence of finite measures.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists
c_{0}>0
such that for all
c\in(0,1)
,

\mathrm{MS}^{n}\otimes\operatorname{SAW}^{n}|_{A(c)}\Rightarrow c_{0}\cdot\mathrm{QS}\otimes\SLE_{8/3}^{\operatorname{loop}}|_{A(c)}\quad\textrm{as }n\rightarrow\infty

with respect to the Gromov-Hausdorff-Prokhorov-uniform topology.

Here, we restrict to
A(c)
to make all measures in Theorem
1.2
finite.
Gwynne and Miller proved the counterpart of the theorem in the setting of chordal self-avoiding paths on half-planar quadrangulations
[
GM16
,
GM19b
]
, and results from their papers are key inputs to our proof. The other inputs are Theorem
1.1
and an exact discrete counterpart (also observed in
[
GM19a
,
CC19
]
) of Theorem
1.1
given in Observation
4.2
.

It is a classical result that the quantum sphere for
\gamma=\sqrt{8/3}
(also known as the Brownian map) arises as the scaling limit of uniformly sampled planar maps
[
Le 13
,
Mie13
]
. By contrast, our Theorem
1.2
gives the analogous result for a family of
non-uniform
planar maps in the sense that two planar maps of the same size do not have the same probability of being sampled. Indeed, if
(M,\eta)
is sampled from
\mathrm{MS}^{n}\otimes\operatorname{SAW}^{n}
then the marginal law of
M
has been reweighted according to the (weighted) number of self-avoiding loops that the map admits. On the other hand, Theorem
1.2
means that this reweighting does not change the scaling limit of the planar map.

For concreteness Theorem
1.2
is stated and proved for quadrangulations, but we remark that the result also holds for random triangulations building on
[
AHS20a
]
.

Define the horizontal cylinder

Definition 2.5
.

Let

Definition 2.6

Remark 2.7

For

Finally, we will need an area-weighted variant of

2.2.
The Liouville field

In this section we recall the Liouville field which was constructed in
[
DKRV16
]
.
Let
\exp:\mathcal{C}\to{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
be the exponential map
z\mapsto e^{z}
. Let
h_{\mathcal{C}}
be the GFF on the cylinder as defined in the previous section, and let
h_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}=h_{\mathcal{C}}\circ\exp
. Then
h_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
is the
GFF on
{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
with average zero on the unit circle
. We write
P_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
for the law of
h_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
. Its covariance kernel is

G_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}(z,w)=-\log|z-w|+\log|z|_{+}+\log|w|+,\qquad|z|_{+}:=\max(|z|,1).

Definition 2.9
.

Sample
(h,\mathbf{c})
from
P_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}\times[e^{-2Qc}\,dc]
and let
\phi=h-2Q\log|\cdot|_{+}+\mathbf{c}
. We call
\phi
the
Liouville field
on
{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
and denote its law by
\mathrm{LF}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
.

For a finite collection of weights
\alpha_{i}
and points
z_{i}\in\mathbbm{C}
, we want to define “
\mathrm{LF}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\alpha_{i},z_{i})_{i}}=\prod_{i}e^{\alpha_{i}\phi(z_{i})}\mathrm{LF}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}(d\phi)
”. This can be understood via regularization and renormalization, see e.g.
[
AHS22
, Lemma 2.6]
. We give a direct definition below.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
(\alpha_{i},z_{i})\in\mathbbm{R}\times\mathbbm{C}
for
i=1,\dots,m
, where
m\geq 1
and the
z_{i}
are distinct. Sample
(h,\mathbf{c})
from
C_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\alpha_{i},z_{i})_{i}}P_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}\times[e^{(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}-2Q)c}\,dc]
where

C_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\alpha_{i},z_{i})_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}|z_{i}|_{+}^{-\alpha_{i}(2Q-\alpha_{i})}e^{\sum_{j=i+1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}G_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}(z_{i},z_{j})}.

Let
\phi=h-2Q\log|\cdot|_{+}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}G_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}(\cdot,z_{i})+\mathbf{c}
. We call
\phi
the
Liouville field on
{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
with insertions
(\alpha_{i},z_{i})
, and denote its law by
\mathrm{LF}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\alpha_{i},z_{i})_{i}}
.

For a conformal automorphism
f:\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}
and a measure
M
on the space of distributions on
\mathbbm{C}
, let
f_{*}M
be the pushforward of
M
under the map
\phi\mapsto\phi\circ f^{-1}+Q\log|(f^{-1})^{\prime}|
. The following change-of-coordinates result is
[
DKRV16
, Theorem 3.5]
with different notation. We present the version stated in
[
AHS22
, Proposition 2.29]
.

Proposition 2.11

(
[
DKRV16
, Theorem 3.5]
)
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbbm{R}
let
\Delta_{\alpha}:=\frac{\alpha}{2}(Q-\frac{\alpha}{2})
. Let
f
be a conformal automorphism of
\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}
and let
(\alpha_{i},z_{i})\in\mathbbm{R}\times\mathbbm{C}
satisfy
f(z_{i})\neq\infty
for
i=1,\dots,m
. Then

\mathrm{LF}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}=f_{*}\mathrm{LF}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}},\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{LF}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\alpha_{i},f(z_{i}))_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}|f^{\prime}(z_{i})|^{-2\Delta_{\alpha_{i}}}f_{*}\mathrm{LF}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\alpha_{i},z_{i})_{i}}.

As the next lemma illustrates,
sampling points from quantum measures of the Liouville field corresponds to adding insertions to the Liouville field. We recall the proof for the reader’s convenience since a closely related argument will be used later.

Lemma 2.12

(
[
AHS22
, Lemma 2.31]
)
.

We have
\mu_{\phi}(d^{2}u)\mathrm{LF}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\alpha_{i},z_{i})_{i}}(d\phi)=\mathrm{LF}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\gamma,u),(\alpha_{i},z_{i})_{i}}(d\phi)d^{2}u
.

Proof.

Sample
h\sim P_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
.
Let
h_{\varepsilon}(u)
be the average of
h
on
\partial B_{\varepsilon}(u)
and write
G_{{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}},\varepsilon}(z,u):=\mathbbm{E}[h(z)h_{\varepsilon}(u)]
. Let
f
be a non-negative continuous function on the Sobolev space
H^{-1}(\mathbbm{C})
, and
g
a non-negative measurable function on
\mathbbm{R}
. Girsanov’s theorem gives

\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[f(h)\varepsilon^{\gamma^{2}/2}e^{\gamma h_{\varepsilon}(u)}}}\right]=\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[f(h+\gamma G_{{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}},\varepsilon}(\cdot,u))}}\right]\mathbbm{E}[\varepsilon^{\gamma^{2}/2}e^{\gamma h_{\varepsilon}(u)}].

With
\mu_{h}^{\varepsilon}(d^{2}u):=\varepsilon^{\gamma^{2}/2}e^{\gamma h_{\varepsilon}(u)}\,d^{2}u
and
\rho_{\varepsilon}(u):=\mathbbm{E}[\varepsilon^{\gamma^{2}/2}e^{\gamma h_{\varepsilon}(u)}]
, integrating against
g(u)\,d^{2}u
gives

\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\int_{\mathbbm{C}}f(h)g(u)\mu_{h}^{\varepsilon}(d^{2}u)}}\right]=\int_{\mathbbm{C}}\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[f(h+\gamma G_{{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}},\varepsilon}(\cdot,u))}}\right]g(u)\rho_{\varepsilon}(u)\,d^{2}u.

Taking the
\varepsilon\to 0
limit yields, with
\rho(u)
defined by
\rho(u)\,d^{2}u=\mathbbm{E}[\mu_{h}(d^{2}u)]
,

\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\int f(h)g(u)\,\mu_{h}(d^{2}u)}}\right]=\int\mathbbm{E}[f(h+\gamma G_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}(\cdot,u))]g(u)\rho(u)\,d^{2}u.

See, e.g.,
[
BP21
, Section 2.4]
or
[
AHS22
, Lemma 2.31]
for details on taking this limit.

Let
c\in\mathbbm{R}
and
q(z)=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}G_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}(z,z_{i})-2Q\log|z|_{+}
. For
\widetilde{f}
any non-negative continuous function on
H^{-1}(\mathbbm{C})
and
\widetilde{g}
any non-negative measurable function on
\mathbbm{R}
, choose
f=\widetilde{f}(\cdot+q+c)
and
g=e^{\gamma(q+c)}\widetilde{g}
. The above equation, together with
\mu_{h+p}=e^{\gamma p}\mu_{h}
for any continuous function
p:\mathbbm{C}\to\mathbbm{R}
, gives

\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\int\widetilde{f}(h+q+c)\widetilde{g}(u)\mu_{h+q+c}(d^{2}u)}}\right]=\int\mathbbm{E}[\widetilde{f}(h+\gamma G_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}(\cdot,u)+q+c)]\widetilde{g}(u)e^{\gamma q(u)+\gamma c}\rho(u)\,d^{2}u.

On the other hand, we have

C_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\gamma,u),(\alpha_{i},z_{i})}=C_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\alpha_{i},z_{i})_{i}}C_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\gamma,u)}e^{\gamma q(u)+2\gamma Q\log|u|_{+}}=C_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\alpha_{i},z_{i})_{i}}e^{\gamma q(u)}\rho(u)
, where the first equality holds by definition and the second follows from a direct calculation; see
[
AHS22
, Lemma 2.12]
for a similar calculation. Thus, multiplying the previous identity by
C_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\alpha_{i},z_{i})_{i}}
gives

C_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\alpha_{i},z_{i})_{i}}\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\int\widetilde{f}(h+q+c)\widetilde{g}(u)\mu_{h+q+c}(d^{2}u)}}\right]=\int C_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\gamma,u),(\alpha_{i},z_{i})_{i}}\mathbbm{E}[\widetilde{f}(h+\gamma G_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}(\cdot,u)+q+c)]\widetilde{g}(u)e^{\gamma c}\,d^{2}u.

Multiplying by
e^{(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}-2Q)c}
and integrating over
c
gives

\mathrm{LF}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\alpha_{i},z_{i})_{i}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\int_{\mathbbm{C}}\widetilde{f}(\phi)\widetilde{g}(u)\mu_{\phi}(d^{2}u)}}\right]=\int_{\mathbbm{C}}\mathrm{LF}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\gamma,u),(\alpha_{i},z_{i})_{i}}[\widetilde{f}(\phi)]\widetilde{g}(u)\,d^{2}u.

The functions
\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g}
are arbitrary so the desired result holds.
∎

We need the following Liouville field description of
\mathcal{M}_{2,\bullet}^{\mathrm{sph}}(W)
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Fix
W>0
, let
\alpha=Q-\frac{W}{2\gamma}
and sample
\phi
from
\frac{2\pi\gamma}{(Q-\alpha)^{2}}\mathrm{LF}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}^{(\alpha,0),(\alpha,1),(\gamma,-1)}
. Then the law of
({\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}},\phi,0,1,-1)/{\sim_{\gamma}}
is
\mathcal{M}_{2,\bullet}^{\mathrm{sph}}(W)
.

Proof.

[
AHS22
, Proposition B.7]
describes the field of
\mathcal{M}_{2,\bullet}^{\mathrm{sph}}(W)
in terms of the Liouville field on
\mathcal{C}
, then
[
AHS22
, Lemma B.4]
gives the coordinate change from
\mathcal{C}
to
{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
.
∎

2.3.
Uniform embedding of quantum surfaces

Let
\operatorname{conf}(\widehat{\mathbbm{C}})
denote the space of automorphisms of the Riemann sphere

\operatorname{conf}(\widehat{\mathbbm{C}})
is unimodular the measure is also left-invariant. Let
\mathbf{m}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
be such a Haar measure.
The following gives an explicit description of
\mathbf{m}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
; see e.g.
[
AHS22
, Lemma 2.28]
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}
be sampled from
\mathbf{m}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}
. Then there is a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that the law of
(\mathfrak{f}(0),\mathfrak{f}(1),\mathfrak{f}(-1))
is
C|(p-q)(q-r)(r-p)|^{-2}\,d^{2}p\,d^{2}q\,d^{2}r
.

Suppose
M
is a measure on the space of quantum surfaces which can be embedded in
\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}
. Sample a pair
(\mathfrak{f},S)
from the product measure
\mathbf{m}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}\times M
, and let
\phi_{0}
be a distribution on
\mathbbm{C}
chosen in a way measurable with respect to
S
such that
S=(\widehat{\mathbbm{C}},\phi_{0})/{\sim_{\gamma}}
. We define
\mathbf{m}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}\ltimes M
to be the law of
\mathfrak{f}\bullet_{\gamma}\phi_{0}
. We call
\mathbf{m}_{\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}}\ltimes M
the
uniform embedding of

. Note that the definition of uniform embedding does not depend on the choice of
\phi_{0}
. Recall that
\mathrm{QS}
is the law of the quantum sphere from Definition
2.6
.

Proposition 2.15

(
[

There is a constant

.

2.4.
Conformal welding

Let
\kappa>0
and let
(D,p,q)
be a simply-connected domain with two marked boundary points.

\SLE_{\kappa}
is a conformally invariant random curve in
D
from
p
to
q
introduced by Schramm
[
RS05
]
, which describes the scaling limits of many statistical physics models. When
\kappa<4
, almost surely
\SLE_{\kappa}
is simple and only intersects
\partial D
at
\{p,q\}
. We will also need a spherical variant of
\SLE
: for distinct points
p,q\in\mathbbm{C}
and
\rho>-2
, there is a random curve from
p
to
q
called
whole-plane
\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho)
, see e.g.
[
MS17
, Section 2.1.3]
for its definition.

For
\kappa\in(0,8)
and distinct points
p,q\in\mathbbm{C}
, the
two-sided whole-plane SLE
, which we denote by
\SLE^{p\rightleftharpoons q}_{\kappa}
, is the probability measure on pairs of curves
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
on
\mathbbm{C}
connecting
p
and
q
where
\eta_{1}
is a whole-plane
\SLE_{\kappa}(2)
from
p
to
q
, and conditioning on
\eta_{1}
, the curve
\eta_{2}
is a chordal
\SLE_{\kappa}
on the complement of
\eta_{1}
from
q
to
p
. This pair of curves
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
satisfies the following resampling property: conditioning on one, the other has the law of
chordal
\SLE_{\kappa}
in the complement, see e.g.
[
Zha21
, Section 2.2]
.

We need a special case of
[
AHS20b
, Theorem 2.4]
. Let
\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}=\mathbbm{C}\cup\{\infty\}
be the Riemann sphere. Let
\{\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(2;\ell_{1},\ell_{2})\}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
be a disintegration of
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(2)
on its two boundary arc lengths, i.e.
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(2)=\iint\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(2;\ell_{1},\ell_{2})\,d\ell_{1}\,d\ell_{2}
, and a sample from
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(2;\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
a.s. has boundary lengths
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
.

Proposition 2.16
.

Fix distinct
p,q\in\mathbbm{C}
and let
(\widehat{\mathbbm{C}},\phi,p,q)
be an embedding of a sample from
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{sph}}(4)
. Independently sample
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
from the probability measure
\SLE^{p\rightleftharpoons q}_{\kappa}
, and let
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
be the connected components of
\widehat{\mathbbm{C}}\backslash(\eta_{1}\cup\eta_{2})
lying to the left and right of
\eta_{1}
respectively. Then there is a constant
C
such that the joint law of
(D_{1},\phi,p,q)/{\sim_{\gamma}}
and
(D_{2},\phi,p,q)/{\sim_{\gamma}}
is

C\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(2;\ell_{1},\ell_{2})\times\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(2;\ell_{2},\ell_{1})\,d\ell_{1}\,d\ell_{2}.

The above statement of Proposition
2.16
is in terms of cutting a sphere to get two disks. It can be equivalently expressed in terms of gluing two disks to get a loop-decorated sphere.
For fixed
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}>0
, a pair of quantum disks sampled from
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(2;\ell_{1},\ell_{2})\times\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(2;\ell_{2},\ell_{1})
can be
conformally welded
along their boundary arcs according to quantum boundary length, to get a quantum surface with the sphere topology decorated by two points and a loop passing through them.
For more details on the conformal welding of quantum surfaces, see e.g.
[
She16
,
DMS21
,
GHS19
]
, and see
[
AHS20b
]
for more information on the conformal welding of quantum disks.

We now give a more precise definition of the measure
\mathrm{Weld}(\mathrm{QD},\mathrm{QD})
appearing in Theorem
1.1
. Let
\ell>0
and let
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})\sim\mathrm{QD}(\ell)\times\mathrm{QD}(\ell)
. For
i=1,2
, let
\phi_{i}:\mathbb{S}^{1}_{\ell}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{i}
be a parametrization of the boundary of
\mathcal{D}_{i}
according to its quantum boundary length such that
\phi_{i}
traces the boundary in counterclockwise direction when the disk is embedded in
\mathbbm{D}
. Namely, for
0<s<t<1
,
\phi_{i}([s,t])
is an arc on the boundary of
\mathcal{D}_{i}
with quantum length
t-s
,
where we represent
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{\ell}
as the interval
[0,\ell]
with endpoints identified. Let
U
be a uniform point on
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{\ell}
independent of everything else. Let
\mathrm{Weld}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
be the curve-decorated quantum surface obtained by conformally welding
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
along their boundaries where
\phi_{1}(t)
is identified with
\phi_{2}(U-t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{S}^{1}_{\ell}
. In words,
\mathrm{Weld}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
means we conformally weld
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
according to their boundary length uniformly at random. Let
\mathrm{Weld}(\mathrm{QD}(\ell),\mathrm{QD}(\ell))
be the law of
\mathrm{Weld}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
, and define
\mathrm{Weld}(\mathrm{QD},\mathrm{QD})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\ell\cdot\mathrm{Weld}(\mathrm{QD}(\ell),\mathrm{QD}(\ell))
.

2.5.
Zhan’s construction of the SLE loop measure

Given a simple loop
\eta
and some
d\in[0,2]
, let
\mathrm{Cont}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
be
\varepsilon^{d-2}
times Lebesgue area measure restricted to the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
\eta
.
If
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathrm{Cont}_{\eta,\varepsilon}
exists for the weak topology then we denote the limit by
\mathrm{Cont}_{\eta}
and call it the
d
-dimensional Minkowski content
of
\eta
.

We can view
\SLE^{p\rightleftharpoons q}_{\kappa}
as a measure on oriented loops by concatenating
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
. Given a loop
\eta
sampled from
\SLE^{p\rightleftharpoons q}_{\kappa}
, with probability 1 the dimension of
\eta
is
d=1+\frac{\kappa}{8}

[
Bef08
]
and its
d
-dimensional Minkowski content
\mathrm{Cont}_{\eta}
exists
[
LR15
]
. The
(unrooted) SLE loop measure

\SLE_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{loop}}
on
\mathbbm{C}
is an infinite measure on oriented loops defined by (see
[
Zha21
, Theorem 4.2]
)

(2.2)

\SLE_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{loop}}(d\eta)=|\mathrm{Cont}_{\eta}|^{-2}\iint_{\mathbbm{C}\times\mathbbm{C}}|p-q|^{-2(2-d)}\SLE^{p\rightleftharpoons q}_{\kappa}(d\eta)\,d^{2}p\,d^{2}q.

The operation of forgetting
p
and
q
in (
2.2
) is natural, since given
\eta
, the points
p,q
are conditionally independent points sampled from the Minkowski content measure
\mathrm{Cont}_{\eta}
on
\eta
; precisely,
[
Zha21
, Theorem 4.2 (i)]
states

(2.3)

\SLE_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{loop}}(d\eta)\,\mathrm{Cont}_{\eta}(dp)\,\mathrm{Cont}_{\eta}(dq)=|p-q|^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}-2}\,\SLE_{\kappa}^{p\rightleftharpoons q}(d\eta)\,d^{2}p\,d^{2}q.

For
\kappa\in(0,4]
, Zhan
[
Zha21
]
shows that
\SLE_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{loop}}

is an example of a Malliavin-Kontsevich-Suhov (MKS) loop measure.

2.6.
The Gromov-Hausdorff-Prokhorov-uniform metric

In this subsection we will define precisely the space of compact loop-decorated metric measure spaces and the Gromov-Hausdorff-Prokhorov-uniform metric. This, along with definitions in Section
3
, will make precise the statement of Theorem
1.2
. We remark that the analogous definitions in the setting of curve-decorated metric measure spaces were first made in
[
GM17
]
; see also
[
Gro99
,
BBI01
,
GPW09
,
Mie09

ADH13

For a metric space
(X,d)
let
C_{0}(X)
denote the space of parametrized and oriented loops on
X
with no distinguished starting point. More precisely, identifying the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{\ell}
of length
\ell
with the interval
[0,\ell]
with endpoints identified,

is the space of continuous functions

\eta
and
\widetilde{\eta}
if
\widetilde{\eta}(t)=\eta(t+r)

and all

. Let
\mathbbm{d}_{d}^{\operatorname{H}}
denote the
d
-Hausdorff metric on compact subsets of
X
and let
\mathbbm{d}_{d}^{\operatorname{P}}
denote the
d
-Prokhorov metric on finite measures on
X
. Finally, let
\mathbbm{d}_{d}^{\operatorname{U}}
denote the
d
-uniform metric on

\mathbbm{d}_{d}^{\operatorname{U}}(\eta,\widetilde{\eta})=\inf_{r\in[0,\ell)}\sup_{t\in[0,\ell)}d(\eta(t),\widetilde{\eta}(t+r)).

Let
\mathbbm{M}^{\operatorname{GHPU}}
be the set of compact
loop-decorated metric measure spaces
, i.e.,
\mathbbm{M}^{\operatorname{GHPU}}
is the set of 4-tuples

(X,d)
is a compact metric space,
\mu
is a finite Borel measure on

\eta\in C_{0}(X)

\mathbbm{M}^{\operatorname{GHPU}}

\mathbbm{d}^{\operatorname{GHPU}}(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\mathfrak{X}_{2})=\inf_{(W,d),\iota_{1},\iota_{2}}\mathbbm{d}_{D}^{\operatorname{H}}(\iota_{1}(X_{1}),\iota_{2}(X_{2}))+\mathbbm{d}_{D}^{\operatorname{P}}((\iota_{1})_{*}\mu_{1},(\iota_{2})_{*}\mu_{2})+\mathbbm{d}_{D}^{\operatorname{U}}(\iota_{1}\circ\eta_{1},\iota_{2}\circ\eta_{2}),

where we take the infimum over all compact metric spaces
(W,D)
and isometric embeddings
\iota_{1}:X_{1}\to W
and
\iota_{2}:X_{2}\to W
. It is shown in
[

that this defines a complete separable metric in the setting of curve-decorated (rather than loop-decorated) metric measure spaces if we identify two elements of this space which differ by a measure- and curve-preserving isometry. The analogous statement holds in the setting of loop-decorated metric measure spaces since we obtain a loop by considering a curve that forms a loop and identifying two such curves which differ by a time shift.

4.
The scaling limit on random quandragulation decorated by self-avoiding loop

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
. We start by introducing more precisely the objects appearing in the theorem. Recall that a planar map is a connected graph drawn on the sphere
\mathbbm{S}^{2}
such that no two edges cross, viewed modulo an orientation-preserving homeomorphisms from the sphere to itself. A quadrangulation is a planar map such that all faces have four edges. Le Gall and Miermont
[
Mie13
,
Le 13
]
proved that uniformly sampled quadrangulations converge in the scaling limit to the metric measure space known as the Brownian map for the so-called Gromov-Hausdorff-Prokhorov topology
[
ADH13
]
.

Define the following constants:

(4.1)

\lambda=12,\qquad\theta=54,\qquad a=5/2\qquad b=1/2.

The constants are chosen such that the number of quadrangulations of a
2p
-gon with
m
faces is of order
\theta^{p}p^{-b}\lambda^{m}m^{-a}
for
m\geq cp^{2}
for arbitrary fixed
c>0

[
Bro65
]
.
3
3

3

Our quadrangulated
2p
-gons are unrooted. If we consider maps with a root edge on its boundary then the number of maps is of order
\theta^{p}p^{-b+1}\lambda^{m}m^{-a}
instead.

Let
\mathrm{MS}^{n}
denote the measure on quadrangulations such that a quadrangulation
M
with
m
faces has weight
n^{a}\lambda^{-m}
.
For
M
sampled from
\mathrm{MS}^{n}
, we view
M
as a metric measure space by considering the graph metric rescaled by
2^{-1/2}n^{-1/4}
and by giving each vertex mass
2(9n)^{-1}
. With this choice of rescaling, the measure of the set of quadrangulations with mass of order 1 will be of order 1 since the number of quadrangulations with
m

\lambda^{m}m^{-a-1}

[
Tut63
]
.

If
M
is a quadrangulation we say that

is a

on

if

is an ordered set of edges

such

and

share an end-point if and only if

A planar map

For

If

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For constants

(4.4)

\begin{split}\operatorname{Weld}(\mathrm{MD}^{n},\mathrm{MD}^{n})|_{A(c)}&=\sum_{k=\lceil c\sqrt{n}\rceil}^{\lceil c^{-1}\sqrt{n}\rceil}2kZ_{n}(k)^{2}\operatorname{Weld}(\mathrm{MD}^{n}(k)^{\#},\mathrm{MD}^{n}(k)^{\#})\\

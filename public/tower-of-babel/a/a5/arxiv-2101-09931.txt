Nonreciprocal Transmission and Entanglement in a cavity-magnomechanical system
Zhi-Bo Yang 1, Jin-Song Liu 1, Ai-Dong Zhu 1, Hong-Yu Liu 1, Rong-Can Yang 2,3

Abstract
Quantum entanglement, a key element for quanum information is generated with a cavity magnomechanical system. It comprises of two microwave cavities, a magnon mode and a vibrational mode, and the last two elements come from a YIG sphere trapped in the second cavity. The two microwave cavities are connected by a superconducting transmission line, resulting in a linear coupling between them. The magnon mode is driven by a strong microwave field and coupled to cavity photons via magnetic dipole interaction, and at the same time interacts with phonons via magnetostrictive interaction. By breaking symmetry of the configuration, we realize nonreciprocal photon transmission and one-way bipartite quantum entanglement. By using current experimental parameters for numerical simulation, it is hoped that our results may reveal a new strategy to built quantum resources for the realization of noise-tolerant quantum processors, chiral networks, and so on.

Strangeness S = − 3 S=-3 and − 4 -4 baryon-baryon interactions in relativistic chiral effective field theory
Zhi-Wei Liu, Jing Song, Kai-Wen Li, Li-Sheng Geng

Abstract
The strangeness S = − 3 S=-3 and − 4 -4 baryon–baryon interactions are investigated in the relativistic chiral effective field theory at leading order. First, the 12 tree-level low-energy constants contributing to the S = − 1 S=-1 hyperon-nucleon interaction are fixed by fitting to the 36 hyperon-nucleon scattering data. Then the S = − 3 S=-3 and − 4 -4 baryon-baryon interactions are derived from that of S = − 1 S=-1 assuming that the corresponding low-energy constants are related to each other via SU(3) flavor symmetry. The comparison with the state-of-the-art lattice QCD simulations, show, however, that SU(3) flavor symmetry breaking effects can not be neglected. In order to take into account these effects, we redetermine two sets of low-energy constants by fitting to the lattice QCD data in the Ξ ​ Σ \Xi\Sigma and Ξ ​ Ξ \Xi\Xi channels respectively. The fitting results demonstrate that the lattice QCD S S -waves phase shifts for both channels can be described rather well. Without any additional free low-energy constants, the predicted phase shifts for the D 1 3 {}^{3}D_{1} channel and the mixing angle ε 1 \varepsilon_{1} are also in qualitative agreement with the lattice QCD data for the S = − 3 S=-3 channel, while the results for the S = − 4 S=-4 channel remain to be checked by future lattice QCD simulations. With the so-obtained low-energy constants, the S S -wave scattering lengths and effective ranges are calculated for these two channels at the physical point. Finally, in combination with the S = 0 S=0 and − 2 -2 results obtained in our previous works, we study the evolution of the irreducible representation “ 27 27 ” in the baryon-baryon interactions as a function of increasing strangeness. It is shown that the attraction decreases dramatically as strangeness increases from S = 0 S=0 to S = − 2 S=-2 , but then remains relatively stable until S = − 4 S=-4 . The results indicate that the existence of bound states in the Ξ ​ Σ \Xi\Sigma and Ξ ​ Ξ \Xi\Xi channels is rather unlikely.

II
Theoretical Framework

In this section, the essential ingredients of the relativistic ChEFT will be briefly recalled for baryon-baryon interactions, especially for the strangeness
S=-3
and
-4
sectors at leading order (LO). For more details of the relativistic ChEFT, we refer the reader to Refs.
Ren et al. 2018
;
Li et al. 2018a
;
Li et al. 2018b
;
Song et al. 2018
. In order to maintain Lorentz invariance, the complete baryon spinor is retained in the relativistic ChEFT approach, namely,

\displaystyle u_{B}(\bm{p},s)=N_{p}\binom{1}{\frac{\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{p}}{E_{p}+M_{B}}}~\chi_{s},\qquad N_{p}=\sqrt{\frac{E_{p}+M_{B}}{2M_{B}}},

(1)

where
E_{p}=\sqrt{\bm{p}^{2}+M_{B}^{2}}
, and
M_{B}
is the averaged baryon mass, while a non-relativistic reduction of
u_{B}
is employed in the non-relativistic ChEFT approach.

The LO baryon-baryon potentials consist of four-baryon contact terms (CT) without derivatives and one-pseudoscalar-meson exchange terms. The CT potential in momentum space reads,

\displaystyle V_{\rm CT}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}=C_{i}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\left(\bar{u}_{3}\Gamma_{i}u_{1}\right)\left(\bar{u}_{4}\Gamma_{i}u_{2}\right),

(2)

where
C_{i}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}
are the LECs corresponding to independent four-baryon terms. The superscript
{\rm YY^{\prime}}
denotes the hyperons in the reaction of
YN\rightarrow Y^{\prime}N
.
\Gamma_{i}
denote the elements of the Clifford algebra,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}=1,\qquad\Gamma_{2}=\gamma^{\mu},\qquad\Gamma_{3}=\sigma^{\mu\nu},\qquad\Gamma_{4}=\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5},\qquad\Gamma_{5}=\gamma_{5}.

(3)

The contact potentials are first calculated in the helicity basis, and then projected into different partial waves in the
|LSJ\rangle
basis

\displaystyle V_{\rm CT}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}(^{1}S_{0})

\displaystyle=\xi_{B}\left[\left(C_{1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+C_{2}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-6C_{3}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+3C_{4}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\right)\left(1+R_{p}^{2}R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right)+\left(3C_{2}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-6C_{3}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+C_{4}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+C_{5}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\right)\left(R_{p}^{2}+R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\equiv\xi_{B}\left[C_{1S0}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\left(1+R_{p}^{2}R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right)+\hat{C}_{1S0}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\left(R_{p}^{2}+R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right)\right],

(4a)

\displaystyle V_{\rm CT}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}(^{3}S_{1})

\displaystyle=\xi_{B}\left[\frac{1}{9}\left(C_{1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+C_{2}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+2C_{3}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-C_{4}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\right)\left(9+R_{p}^{2}R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\left(C_{2}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-2C_{3}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-C_{4}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-C_{5}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\right)\left(R_{p}^{2}+R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\equiv\xi_{B}\left[\frac{1}{9}C_{3S1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\left(9+R_{p}^{2}R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\hat{C}_{3S1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\left(R_{p}^{2}+R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right)\right],

(4b)

\displaystyle V_{\rm CT}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}(^{3}P_{0})

\displaystyle=\xi_{B}\left[-2\left(C_{1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-4C_{2}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-12C_{3}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-4C_{4}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+C_{5}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\right)R_{p}R_{p^{\prime}}\right]\equiv\xi_{B}\left[-2C_{3P0}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}R_{p}R_{p^{\prime}}\right],

(4c)

\displaystyle V_{\rm CT}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}(^{3}P_{1})

\displaystyle=\xi_{B}\left[-\frac{4}{3}\left(C_{1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-2C_{2}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+2C_{4}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-C_{5}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\right)R_{p}R_{p^{\prime}}\right]=\xi_{B}\left[-\frac{4}{3}\left(C_{1S0}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-\hat{C}_{1S0}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\right)R_{p}R_{p^{\prime}}\right],

(4d)

\displaystyle V_{\rm CT}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}(^{1}P_{1})

\displaystyle=\xi_{B}\left[-\frac{2}{3}\left(C_{1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+4C_{3}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+C_{5}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\right)R_{p}R_{p^{\prime}}\right]=\xi_{B}\left[-\frac{2}{3}\left(C_{3S1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-\hat{C}_{3S1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\right)R_{p}R_{p^{\prime}}\right],

(4e)

\displaystyle V_{\rm CT}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}(^{3}D_{1})

\displaystyle=\xi_{B}\left[\frac{8}{9}\left(C_{1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+C_{2}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+2C_{3}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-C_{4}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\right)R_{p}^{2}R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right]=\xi_{B}\left[\frac{8}{9}C_{3S1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}R_{p}^{2}R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right],

(4f)

\displaystyle V_{\rm CT}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}(^{3}SD_{1})

\displaystyle=\xi_{B}\left[\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{9}\left(C_{1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+C_{2}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+2C_{3}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-C_{4}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\right)R_{p}^{2}R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\left(C_{2}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-2C_{3}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-C_{4}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-C_{5}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\right)R_{p}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\xi_{B}\left[\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{9}C_{3S1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}R_{p}^{2}R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\hat{C}_{3S1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}R_{p}^{2}\right],

(4g)

\displaystyle V_{\rm CT}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}(^{3}DS_{1})

\displaystyle=\xi_{B}\left[\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{9}\left(C_{1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+C_{2}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}+2C_{3}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-C_{4}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\right)R_{p}^{2}R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\left(C_{2}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-2C_{3}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-C_{4}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}-C_{5}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}\right)R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\xi_{B}\left[\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{9}C_{3S1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}R_{p}^{2}R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\hat{C}_{3S1}^{\rm YY^{\prime}}R_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right],

(4h)

where

.

phase shifts, indicating the need to take into account SU(3) breaking effects.

From the above comparisons with the LQCD results, we conclude that SU(3) symmetry breaking is large when one moves from the
S=-1
system to the
S=-3,-4
sectors. As a result, we refrain from using the LECs tabulated in Table
2
to predict the corresponding cross sections for these two sectors.

t=14

III.3

27
” irreducible representation in two-octet-baryon interactions

Up to now we have systematically studied two-octet-baryon interactions in the relativistic ChEFT at leading order. Our results show that the

(
I=1
)
{}^{1}S_{0}

Ren et al. 2018

\Sigma N
(

is moderately attractive, and the
\Sigma\Sigma
(
I=2

\Xi\Sigma
(

\Xi\Xi

27

Ideally the behaviors of these five states should be the same under strict SU(3) symmetry, but in practice SU(3) symmetry is broken due to the mass difference of octet baryons and pseudoscalar mesons. Thus it offers an ideal place to understand SU(3) symmetry breaking via the evolution of the irreducible representation “
27

S=-4

\Sigma\Sigma

-1

IV
Summary and Outlook

In this work, we have studied the strangeness
S=-3
and
-4
baryon–baryon interactions in the relativistic ChEFT at leading order. In order to be self-consistent, we first redetermined the hyperon-nucleon interactions by fitting the 12 LECs to the 36
YN
scattering data with cutoff
\Lambda_{F}=550-700
MeV. By assuming strict SU(3) flavor symmetry, the
S=-1
baryon-baryon interactions were extended to the
S=-3
and
-4
sectors. Compared to the state-of-the-art LQCD simulations, it is found that there is an appreciable SU(3) flavor symmetry breaking from strangeness
S=-1
to
S=-3
and
-4
sectors. In order to consider these effects, we redetermined two sets of LECs by fitting to the LQCD data for the
\Xi\Sigma
and
\Xi\Xi
channels respectively. The fitting results demonstrated that the
S
-waves phase shifts of LQCD can be described rather well. In addition, without any additional free LECs, the predicted phase shifts for the
{}^{3}D_{1}
channel and the mixing angle
\varepsilon_{1}
are also found to be in qualitative agreement with the LQCD data in the
S=-3
sector. Quantitatively, the relativistic ChEFT predicted a stronger coupling than the LQCD for the
{}^{3}S_{1}-{}^{3}D_{1}
channel. With the obtained LECs, the
S
-wave scattering lengths and effective ranges were also calculated for these two systems at the physical point. Finally, using the
S=0
and
-2
results obtained in our previous works, we studied the evolution of the singlet
S
-wave scattering lengths with the increase of strangeness, which belong to the same irreducible representation “
27
”. It was shown that the attraction decreases fast as strangeness increases from
S=0
to
S=-2
, but then remains almost unchanged from
S=-2
to
S=-4
, which indicates that the existence of bound states in the
\Xi\Sigma
and
\Xi\Xi
systems is rather unlikely.

Although experimental studies of baryon-baryon interactions of strangeness
S=-3
and
-4
are rather challenging, it has been recently demonstrated that it is possible to extract information on such interactions from correlation measurements in heavy-ion collisions at RHIC or CERN
Adamczyk and et al. 2015
. In addition, baryon-baryon systems with
S=-2,-3,-4
can also be produced in photon induced reactions on the deuteron at JLab
Miller 2013
or
K^{-}
induced reactions at J-PARC
Ahn et al. 2011
. In the not so far future, these studies might substantially advance our understanding of multi-strangeness systems.

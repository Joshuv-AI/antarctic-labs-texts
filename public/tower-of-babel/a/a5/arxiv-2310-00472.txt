Factorization of Algebraic p p -adic L L -functions Attached to Adjoint Representations of Coleman Families: Non-critical Case
Fırtına Küçük

Abstract
. The main objective of this article is to establish the p p -adic Artin formalism for the algebraic p p -adic L L -functions attached to the adjoint representations of Coleman families of modular forms. In particular, we prove a factorization formula involving the determinants of appropriate Selmer complexes, using tools from rigid geometry, homological algebra, Euler systems, and p p -adic Hodge theory. This work extends an earlier result of Palvannan to the p p -non-ordinary setting.

Introduction

1.1.
Introduction and Background

The main objective of this article is to prove the factorization of the algebraic
p
-adic
L
-function attached to the representation:

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Ad}}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathcal{F}})(\psi\chi_{\rm cyc}^{j})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathrm{End}_{A}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathcal{F}})(\psi\chi_{\rm cyc}^{j}),

\displaystyle{{\rm Ad}^{0}}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathcal{F}})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\ker({\mathrm{Ad}}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathcal{F}})\xrightarrow{{\rm Tr}}A).

We then have the following decomposition of the representations:

{\mathrm{Ad}}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathcal{F}})(\psi\chi_{\rm cyc}^{j})\cong{{\rm Ad}^{0}}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathcal{F}})(\psi\chi_{\rm cyc}^{j})\oplus A(\psi\chi_{\rm cyc}^{j}).

Let

denote the Deligne’s (cohomological) representation attached to

p
-adic field

. Artin formalism asserts that due to the definition of motivic

-function attached to
{\mathrm{Ad}}(f)

L({\mathrm{Ad}}(V_{f})(\psi),s)=L({{\rm Ad}^{0}}(V_{f})(\psi),s)L(\psi,s).

Establishing the
p
-adic Artin formalism is, however, far from obvious, as
{\mathrm{Ad}}(V_{f})
does not have any critical

[
Gro80
]
) is due to Dasgupta in
[
Das16
]
, where the modular form of interest is

Theorem

(Dasgupta)
.

Let
f
be a
p
-ordinary cuspidal eigenform and
\psi
be a finite order Dirichlet character with conductor coprime to
p

p
-adic
L
-functions

\displaystyle L_{p}(f\otimes f\otimes\psi,\sigma)=L_{p}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{2}(f)\otimes\psi,\sigma)L_{p}(\varepsilon\psi,\sigma/(z\cdot\kappa)),

where
\kappa(z)=z^{k}
.

In
[
Pal17
]
, Palvannan proved that the analogous factorization holds for the associated algebraic
p
-adic
L
-functions (i.e. corresponding Greenberg Selmer groups and their divisors). In
[
AL21
]
, Arlandini and Loeffler generalized the result of Dasgupta in
[
Das16
]
to the case where
f
is
p
-non-ordinary and
\psi
is trivial (see Theorem
6.1
).

Our main goal in this article is to establish the
p
-adic Artin formalism for the algebraic counterparts of these
p
-adic
L
-functions, where
f
is
p
-non-ordinary and
\psi
is non-trivial. In order to accomplish this, we will appeal to Selmer complexes, which were developed by Nekovář in
[
Nek06
]
and generalized by Pottharst (in
[
Pot13
]
and
[
Pot12
]
) and Benois (see
[
Ben14
]
,
[
Ben21
]
) to the case where the local conditions at
p
come from
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules, and the coefficients of the representations arise from affinoid algebras. In an upcoming work, following the spirit of this article, we will be interested in the case where
f
is
\theta
-critical, which will require a further investigation.

We will end this section by pointing out some key differences between the result of Palvannan and our theorems. Firstly, Palvannan assumes that
f
is
p
-ordinary, in this case there is a
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p}/{{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}})
-stable subspace of
V_{f}
(and
\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{f}}
, where
\mathbf{f}
is the Hida family passing through
f
) which satisfies the Panchishkin condition. Palvannan forms the local conditions attached to the relevant representations by using this subspace. In the
p
-non ordinary case, which we assume,

and
\mathbf{V}_{\mathcal{F}}
are irreducible as
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{p}/{{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}})
representations, hence there is no such subspace. This is why we need to work with Pottharst style Selmer complexes, which allows us to choose our local conditions as
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules that comes from the “triangulation” of

(see Theorem
4.7

(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules are very closely related to
p
-adic Hodge theory. We also use the advantage of appealing to the theory of Selmer complexes, since it is very convenient to use the language of derived categories.

Another technical difference is that in the non-ordinary case, the
p
-adic
L
-functions are no longer integral, this is why we use
\mathcal{H}
, the algebra of locally analytic distributions on
\Gamma_{0}
, for cyclotomic deformations. Note that
\mathcal{H}
is a non-Noetherian ring. Fortunately there is a rich theory of coadmissible modules over
\mathcal{H}
thanks to Schneider and Teitelbaum
[
ST03
]
, which is applied to the theory of Selmer complexes by Pottharst and Benois. We also require some tools from rigid geometry, as our coefficient ring is an affinoid algebra and

is the global sections of the structure sheaf on rigid open unit ball.

Finally we utilize Beilinson-Flach Euler system in Section
6
to establish the analogue of the AD-TOR assumption of
[
Pal17
]
in our setting. In order to do this, we adapt the results of Loeffler and Zerbes in
[

]
to our setting, combining with the ideas from
[
LZ19
]
and
[
BLV21
]

2.
Affinoid Spaces and the Weight Space

In this section, we will review definitions and some properties of affinoid algebras, their spectra and the weight space. The details on affinoid algebras and affinoid spaces can be found in
[
Bos14
]
, and the details on the weight space can be found in
[
Bel21
]
and
[
BB20
]
.

2.1.
Affinoid Algebras

Definition 2.1

(Tate algebra)
.

Let
E
be a finite extension of
{{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}
. The
E
-algebra

E\langle x_{1},...,x_{n}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbf{N}}^{n}}c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}\in E[[x_{1},...,x_{n}]]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert c_{\alpha}\right\rvert}\rightarrow 0\textrm{\ as }\alpha\rightarrow\infty\right\}

is called the Tate algebra.

We will also denote
E\langle x_{1},...,x_{n}\rangle
by
T_{n}
, if
E
is inferred from the context. Tate algebras are also characterized by the following property given in
[
Bos14
, §2.2 Lemma 1]
:

Proposition 2.2
.

A formal power series with
n
variables

f=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbf{N}}^{n}}c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}\in E[[x_{1},...,x_{n}]]

lies in
T_{n}
iff
f
converges on the closed unit ball

B^{n}(\overline{E})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{x=(x_{1},...,x_{n})\in\overline{E}^{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x_{i}\right\rvert}\leq 1\}.

Some properties of the Tate algebras are as follows: For each
n
,
T_{n}
is UFD, Noetherian, Jacobson, and regular of Krull dimension
n
. For each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
T_{n}
, the residue field
T_{n}/\mathfrak{m}
is a finite extension of
E
. The Tate algebra
T_{n}
is also complete with respect to the Gauss norm:

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\rm max}_{\alpha}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert c_{\alpha}\right\rvert},

where

f(x)=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}\in T_{n}.

In particular,
T_{1}
is an Euclidean domain, hence it is a PID. This last property will be useful when we discuss nice affinoid neighborhoods in Definition
4.1
.

Definition 2.3

(Affinoid algebra)
.

An
E
-algebra
A
is called an affinoid algebra over
E
if there exists an
E
-algebra epimorphism
e\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}T_{n}\twoheadrightarrow A
from a Tate algebra
T_{n}
to
A
.

In other words, affinoid algebras are quotients of Tate algebras by some ideals. Affinoid algebras are Noetherian and Jacobson. There is a norm on affinoid algebras induced by the Gauss norm on Tate algebras, and all
E
-algebra morphisms between affinoid algebras are continuous. Affinoid algebras also satisfy Noether normalization theorem, i.e. for some
d
there exists a finite monomorphism
T_{d}\rightarrow A
, and in particular the Krull dimension is
d
.

There exists a category of affinoid
E
-algebras with morphisms given by
E
-algebra maps which admits pushouts, where we give the universal property of pushouts in the following (see
[
Bos14
, §3.1 Proposition 2, Appendix A Proposition 6]
):

Proposition 2.4

(Completed tensor product)
.

Let
R
,
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
be affinoid
E
-algebras such that
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are
R
-algebras. Then there exists a completed tensor product
A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{R}A_{2}
of
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
over
R
satisfying the following universal property: There are
R
-algebra homomorphisms
i_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A_{1}\rightarrow A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{R}A_{2}
and
i_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A_{2}\rightarrow A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{R}A_{2}
, and for any
R
-algebra
A
lying in this category and
R
-algebra homomorphisms

and

2.2.
Affinoid and Rigid Spaces

Definition 2.5

(Affinoid space)
.

X
is called an affinoid space over
E
if
X=\mathrm{Spm}(A)
for some affinoid algebra
A
over
E
, where
\mathrm{Spm}(A)
is the maximal spectra of
A
(i.e. the set of maximal ideals of
A
).
X
is equipped with the following data:

•

X
has the Zariski topology, where the closed subsets of
X
are precisely the following:

V(\mathfrak{a})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{x\in\mathrm{Spm}(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{x}\},

where
\mathfrak{a}
is an ideal of
A
, and
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
is the corresponding maximal ideal in
A
to the point
x
,

•

A
is the ring of “functions” on
X
viewed as follows: if
f\in A
and
x\in\mathrm{Spm}(A)
, then
f(x)=f{\rm\ mod\ }\mathfrak{m}_{x}\in A/\mathfrak{m}_{x}
.

Note that in this case, for an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
A
, the closed subset
V(\mathfrak{a})
of
\mathrm{Spm}(A)
is precisely

V(\mathfrak{a})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{x\in\mathrm{Spm}(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f(x)=0{\rm\ for\ all\ }f\in\mathfrak{a}\}.

The morphisms of affinoid spaces are defined so that the category of affinoid spaces is the opposite category of the category of affioid algebras. For instance, the pullbacks (fibered products) are defined as
\mathrm{Spm}(A_{1})\times_{\mathrm{Spm}(R)}\mathrm{Spm}(A_{2})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathrm{Spm}(A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{R}A_{2})
and the natural maps
p_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X_{1}\times_{X}X_{2}\rightarrow X_{1}
and
p_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X_{1}\times_{X}X_{2}\rightarrow X_{2}
(which correspond to
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
in the opposite category) are the usual projections
(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto x_{1}
and
(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto x_{2}
respectively.

Zariski topology on
\mathrm{Spm}(A)
enjoys many similar properties to the topology on the prime spectrum
{\rm Spec}(A)
. For instance, the sets of the form

D_{f}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{x\in\mathrm{Spm}(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f(x)\neq 0\}\cong\mathrm{Spm}(A_{f})

for
f\in A
forms a basis for this topology. There are more interesting topologies, such as canonical topology, weak and strong Grothendieck topologies, which are finer than the Zariski topology. The details can be found on Chapter 3 and 4 of
[
Bos14
]
.

Definition 2.6

(Affinoid subdomain)
.

A subset
U\subseteq X=\mathrm{Spm}(A)
is called an affinoid subdomain of
X
if there exists a morphism of affinoid spaces
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
such that
\iota(X^{\prime})\subseteq U
and this map is universal with this property, i.e. for any morphism
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y=\mathrm{Spm}(B)\rightarrow X
with
\varphi(Y)\subseteq U
, there exists a unique morphism
\varphi^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\rightarrow X^{\prime}
such that
\varphi=\iota\varphi^{\prime}
.

It turns out that in this case,
\iota
is a bijection from
X^{\prime}
onto
U
, and
X^{\prime}
is unique up to unique isomorphism, hence we can regard
U=X^{\prime}=\mathrm{Spm}(A_{U})
. Also for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{x}\in U
, we have
A_{\iota(\mathfrak{m}_{x})}\cong{A_{U}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{x}}
and
A/{\iota(\mathfrak{m}_{x})}^{n}\cong{A_{U}}/{\mathfrak{m}_{x}}^{n}
for any
n\in{\mathbf{N}}
(see
[
Bos14
, §3.3 Lemma 10]
).

An important property of affinoid subdomains for our discussions is as follows (see
[
Bos14
, §4.1 Corollary 5]
):

Proposition 2.7
.

If
U\subseteq X=\mathrm{Spm}(A)
is an affinoid subdomain of
X
, then the restriction map
A\rightarrow A_{U}
is flat.

Definition 2.8

(Admissible open subset)
.

A subset
U\subseteq X
is called admissible open if there exists a covering of
U
by affinoid subdomains
\{U_{i}\}_{i}
such that for each morphism of affinoid spaces
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\rightarrow X
with
\varphi(Y)\subseteq U
, there is a finite refinement of the covering
\{\varphi^{-1}(U_{i})\}_{i}
of
Y
.

For example, all Zariski open subsets of
X
are admissible, in particular they are covered by finitely many affinoid subdomains of
X
. Another important example is the open unit ball. For
n\in{\mathbf{N}}
, let

W_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathrm{Spm}\left(E\langle X/p^{1/n}\rangle\right)

be the closed ball with radius
p^{-1/n}
. Then the open unit ball is admissibly covered by these closed balls
W_{n}
.

4.1.
The Weight Space

Let
\Gamma
be a profinite group, and let
H
run through open subgroups of
\Gamma
. Recall that we define the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda_{E}(\Gamma)
as

\Lambda_{E}(\Gamma)=\mathcal{O}_{E}[[\Gamma]]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{H}\mathcal{O}_{E}[\Gamma/H].

The particular cases in which we will be most interested are
\Gamma\cong{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
or
{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}
. Note that we have

\Lambda_{E}({\mathbf{Z}}_{p}^{\times})\cong \mathcal{O}_{E}[\Delta]\otimes\Lambda_{E}({{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}),

and
\Lambda_{E}({{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}})\cong\mathcal{O}_{E}[[T]]
, where the last isomorphism is given by sending a topological generator
\gamma\in{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}
to
1+T
.

The weight space
\mathcal{W}
is defined as the Hom-set of continuous group homomorphism from
{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
to the multiplicative group scheme, i.e. if
R
is a Banach
E
-algebra then

\mathcal{W}(R)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\mathrm{Hom}}_{\rm cts,grp}({\mathbf{Z}}_{p}^{\times},R^{\times}).

This definition is equivalent to saying that

\mathcal{W}(R)={\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\rm cts},\mathcal{O}_{E}{\rm-alg}}(\Lambda_{E}({\mathbf{Z}}_{p}^{\times}),R).

Applying the functor of points idea, we can see
\mathcal{W}
as the generic fiber of
{\rm Spf}(\Lambda_{E}({\mathbf{Z}}_{p}^{\times}))
. Note that this is not an affinoid space but it is a rigid analytic space: it is the disjoint union of
p-1
open balls of radius
1
. Each of these balls are admissibly covered by affinoid disks
W_{n}
defined in Section
2.2
.

For every integer
k
, the character
\kappa_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}z\mapsto z^{k}
is an element of the weight space, thus we have an injection
{\mathbf{Z}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}
. Moreover, the integers form a dense subset of the weight space. Thus we can identify
{\mathbf{Z}}
with the subset of the weight space
\mathcal{W}
via
\kappa
.

If we fix one of these components (i.e. a character
\chi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}({\mathbf{Z}}/p)^{\times}\rightarrow R^{\times}
, without loss of generality we can assume it is the trivial character) and call the component
\mathcal{W}_{\chi}
, it is an open ball of radius
1
, hence it is admissibly covered by closed affinoid balls of radius
p^{-1/n}
, each of which is isomorphic to

W_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathrm{Spm}\left(E\langle X/p^{1/n}\rangle\right).

Denote

\mathcal{H}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=E\langle X/p^{1/n}\rangle=\Gamma(W_{n},\mathcal{O}_{W_{n}}),

so that we define the ring of locally analytic
E
-valued distributions on
{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}
as

\mathcal{H}_{E}({{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{H}_{n}=\Gamma(\mathcal{W}_{\chi},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}).

We also define

\mathcal{H}_{E}({\mathbf{Z}}_{p}^{\times})\cong E[\Delta]\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E}({\mathbf{Z}}_{p}),

and note that
\mathcal{H}_{E}({\mathbf{Z}}_{p})
contains the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda_{E}({{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}})\otimes E
. We will denote
\mathcal{H}_{E}({{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}})
by
\mathcal{H}
. If
A
is an affinoid algebra over
E
, we will write
\mathcal{H}_{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{H}\widehat{\otimes}A
and
\Lambda_{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Lambda\widehat{\otimes}A
.

If
\Gamma
is a quotient of
G_{\mathbf{Q}}
or
G_{p}
isomorphic to
{{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}}
or
{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
, and
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma
is the involution that maps
\gamma\in\Gamma
to
\gamma^{-1}
, then
\Lambda(\Gamma)^{\iota}
will denote the rank one
\Lambda(\Gamma)
-module on which
G_{\mathbf{Q}}
acts via the inverse of the tautological character, see
[
Nek06
, §8]
and
[
Ben14
, §2.2 ]
for details. This action naturally extends to
\mathcal{H}_{A}
, and we denote this rank 1
\mathcal{H}_{A}
-module by
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{\iota}
.

Definition 4.1

(Nice affinoid neighborhood,
[
Bel12
]
)
.

Let

be an admissible affinoid neighborhood of the weight space

, and

denote the unit ball for the supremum norm for

. Then

is called nice if

and the residue ring

are PID, and the subset

is dense in

For example, any closed ball with center

is nice. This will be important when we discuss the perfectness of the Selmer complexes that we are interested in.

4.2.
Eigencurve and Coleman Families

In this subsection we will give the definitions of Coleman families, the big representations attached to these families and state the result that these representations are trianguline (due to Berger). We will also make a quick review of the eigencurve for the sake of completeness, even though we will not need this for this article, we will need this theory for the sequel article.

Let
U\subseteq\mathcal{W}
be a wide open disk centered at a
\kappa(k_{0})\in\mathcal{W}
for some

, i.e. a rigid analytic space

such that its

\{x\in\mathcal{W}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x-\kappa(k_{0})\right\rvert}<p^{-r}\}
for some

. Let

denote the power bounded analytic functions on

, i.e.

\Lambda_{U}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{f\in E[[T]]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f\textnormal{ converges on }U,\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}\leq 1\}.

Note that
\Lambda_{U}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{E}[[T]]
, where the isomorphism is non-canonical (hence the notation

is used). For

, let
\Gamma_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Gamma_{1}(N)\cap\Gamma_{0}(p)
. The following definition is from
[

(also see
[

, Theorem 3.8]
).

Definition 4.2

(Coleman family)

A Coleman Family

is a power series

\mathcal{F}=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}(\mathcal{F})q^{n}\in\Lambda_{U}[[q]],

where

, and

is invertible in

such that for all but finitely many weights

Remark 4.3

Definition 4.5

(Cuspidal Coleman-Mazur-Buzzard eigencurve)
.

The cuspidal eigencurve with tame level
N

\mathcal{C}^{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{C}^{0}_{N}
is a rigid analytic space of equidimension
1
, which is equipped with a weight morphism
\kappa\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{C}^{0}\to\mathcal{W}
that is locally finite and flat, and a universal eigenform
\mathcal{F}^{\rm univ}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}[[q]]
such that for all affinoid
U
with a weight morphism

\mathcal{F}
over

The eigencurve
\mathcal{C}
is equipped with a finite flat weight map, which parametrizes modular forms, and contains

Note that

is

at

4.3.
Motives and Galois Representations Attached to Modular Forms

Let
f\in S_{k+2}(\Gamma_{1}(N),\varepsilon)
be a normalised newform of weight
k+2
and level
\Gamma_{1}(N)
with nebentype
\varepsilon
. Suppose that
p\nmid N
. In
[
Sch90
]
, Scholl constructs a motive
M(f)
such that its
p
-adic realization is the Deligne’s cohomological
G_{\mathbf{Q}}
representation
V_{f}
, which is irreducible, crystalline at
p
(since
p\nmid N
) and the action of
\varphi
on
\mathbf{D}_{\rm cris}(V_{f})
is given by the characteristic polynomial
X^{2}-a_{p}(f)X+\varepsilon(p)p^{k+1}
. If
\chi
is a Dirichlet character, we have

V_{f_{\chi}}\cong V_{f}\otimes\chi.

Note that for
g\in\{f_{\alpha},f_{\beta}\}
we have the isomorphisms of Galois representations
V_{g}\cong V_{f}
, and
V_{g^{c}}\cong V_{f^{*}}
. Note also that due to the Poincaré duality, we have the isomorphism:

(4.1)

V_{f}^{*}\cong V_{f}(k+1)\otimes\varepsilon^{-1}\cong V_{f^{*}}(k+1).

The Hodge-Tate weights of
V_{f}
are
0
and
-1-k
. Note also that the action of geometric Frobenius
{\rm Frob}^{-1}_{p}
at
p
on the
\ell
-adic realizations (for
\ell\neq p
) is given by the characteristic polynomial
X^{2}-a_{p}(f)X+\varepsilon(p)p^{k+1}
. Let
\alpha
and
\beta
denote the roots of this characteristic polynomial, and let
E
be a finite extension of
{{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}
which contains
\alpha
,
\beta
, and the Hecke field of

.

We say that

is ordinary at
p
if
v_{p}(a_{p}(f))=0
, otherwise we say

is
p
-non-ordinary. In this article we are interested in the non-ordinary case. This implies that
v_{p}(\alpha),v_{p}(\beta)>0
, therefore none of the
p
-stabilizations of

are at critical slope (i.e.
v_{p}(\alpha),v_{p}(\beta)\neq k+1
). Thus they are not
\theta
-critical, i.e.
V_{f}|_{G_{p}}
does not split into the direct sum of two characters. Indeed, in the non-ordinary case, the Panchishkin ordinarity condition for
V_{f}^{*}
fails (even though it satisfies the criticality condition in Definition
3.6
since
d^{+}(V_{f}^{*})=1
and
V_{f}^{*}
has Hodge-Tate weights
0
and
k+1
) as

is irreducible as a
G_{p}
representation.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Coleman family passing through
f_{\alpha}
over a nice affinoid subdomain
U=\mathrm{Spm}(A)
of the weight space. Let
x_{0}
be the point corresponding to
f_{\alpha}
in the eigencurve. Let
\mathcal{X}
be the preimage of
U
under the weight map
\kappa
of the eigencurve, and let

denote the classical points of
\mathcal{X}
, i.e. the points which are sent to non-negative integers under the weight map
\kappa
of the eigencurve.

Theorem 4.6

(The big Galois representation attached to a Coleman family)
.

There exists a free

\mathbf{V}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbf{V}_{\mathcal{F}}
of rank

In the non

(and of

\displaystyle C^{\bullet}_{{\rm Iw},S}(F,\mathbf{V})

Cohomology of

Definition 5.1

(Fontaine-Herr complex)
.

Let
K
be a finite extension of
{{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}
, and let
\mathbf{D}
be a
(\varphi,\Gamma_{K})
-module over
\mathcal{R}_{A}
. We define the Fontaine-Herr complex
C^{\bullet}_{\varphi,\gamma_{n}}(\mathbf{D})
, which is concentrated at degrees [0,2], as follows:

\left[\mathbf{D}^{\Delta}\xrightarrow{d_{0}}\mathbf{D}^{\Delta}\oplus\mathbf{D}^{\Delta}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}\mathbf{D}^{\Delta}\right],

where
d_{0}(z)=(\gamma_{n}(z)-z,\varphi(z)-z)
and
d_{1}(x,y)=(\gamma_{n}(x)-x)+(y-\varphi(y))
.

We denote the cohomologies of this complex by
H^{i}(K_{n},\mathbf{D})
. For

, we will denote the complex by
C^{\bullet}_{\varphi,\Gamma_{K}}(\mathbf{D})
, and the corresponding object in the derived category

\mathbf{R\Gamma}(K,\mathbf{D})
(or simply by
\mathbf{R\Gamma}(\mathbf{D})
, when

).

Definition 5.2

(Iwasawa cohomology)
.

Let
C^{\bullet}_{\psi}(\mathbf{D})
be the following complex that is concentrated in degrees

and
2
:

\mathbf{D}^{\Delta}\xrightarrow{\psi-1}\mathbf{D}^{\Delta},

where
\psi
is the unique continuous map (and a left inverse of

such that:

\varphi(\psi(f(\pi)))\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\xi\in\mu_{p}}f(\xi(1+\pi)-1).

We denote the corresponding object in the derived category
\mathscr{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A})
by
\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm Iw}(K,\mathbf{D})
(or simply by
\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{D})
, when

), and its cohomologies by

The following theorem relates the Galois cohomologies of a

-representation

and the cohomologies of its associated

Proposition 5.3
.

If

is a

representation over

then we have

\displaystyle H^{i}(K,\mathbf{V})

\displaystyle\cong H^{i}(K,\mathbf{D}_{{\rm rig},A}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{V})),

\displaystyle H^{i}_{{\rm Iw}}(K,\mathbf{V})

\displaystyle\cong H^{i}_{{\rm Iw}}(K,\mathbf{D}_{{\rm rig},A}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{V})).

(Local conditions)

5.3.
Selmer Complexes

In this subsection we will give a brief summary of Nekovář’s Selmer Complexes, details of which can be found in
[
Nek06
]
. We will here focus on the rings
R
which are complete local Noetherian rings with finite residue field, and give the generalizations to affinoid algebras in the following subsection.

Let
F
be a finite extension of
{\mathbf{Q}}
, and let
S
be a finite set of primes as before.

Definition 5.6

(Selmer complex)
.

Let
X
be an admissible
R[G_{F}]
-module (
[
Nek06
, Definition 3.2.1]
), and for each
v\in S
, let
U_{v}^{+}(X)
be a complex satisfying some finiteness conditions (see
[
Nek06
, §4.2]
for the details), and let
i_{v}^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}U_{v}^{+}(X)\rightarrow C^{\bullet}(G_{v},X)
be a morphism of complexes. The local conditions attached to
X
is the collection of these morphisms
\Delta(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{i_{v}^{+}\}
. Let
U_{S}^{+}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{v\in S_{f}}U_{v}^{+}(X)
.

The Selmer complex attached to the data
(G_{F},X,\Delta(X))
is the following mapping cone:

\widetilde{C}_{f}^{\bullet}(F,X,\Delta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\rm Cone}\left(C^{\bullet}_{S}(X)\oplus U_{S}^{+}(X)\xrightarrow{res_{S_{f}}-i_{S}^{+}}\bigoplus_{v\in S_{f}}C^{\bullet}(G_{v},X)\right)[-1],

where
i_{S}^{+}=\oplus i_{v}^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}U_{S_{f}}^{+}(X)\to\bigoplus_{v\in S_{f}}C^{\bullet}(G_{v},X)
. The corresponding object in the derived category
\mathscr{D}_{\rm ft}^{b}(R)
will be denoted by
\widetilde{\mathbf{R\Gamma}}_{f}(F,X,\Delta)
, or simply by omitting
F
and
\Delta
when they are clear from the context.

We set

U_{S}^{-}(F,X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\rm Cone}\left(U_{S}^{+}(F,X)\to\bigoplus_{v\in S_{f}}C^{\bullet}(F_{v},X)\right),

and

C^{\bullet}_{\rm c}(G_{F,S},X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\rm Cone}\left(C^{\bullet}_{S}(F,X)\to\bigoplus_{v\in S_{f}}C^{\bullet}(F_{v},X)\right)[-1].

Then by definition of these complexes, we have the following exact triangles (see
[
Nek06
, 6.1.3.2]
):

(Tr1)

\displaystyle U_{S}^{+}(F,X)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S_{f}}\mathbf{R\Gamma}(F_{v},X)\rightarrow U_{S}^{-}(F,X) \rightarrow U_{S}^{+}(F,X)[1],

(Tr2)

\displaystyle U_{S}^{+}(F,X)[-1]\rightarrow\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm c}(G_{F,S},X)\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathbf{R\Gamma}}_{f}(F,X) \rightarrow U_{S}^{+}(F,X),

(Tr3)

\displaystyle U_{S}^{-}(F,X)[-1]\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathbf{R\Gamma}}_{f}(F,X)\rightarrow\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{S}(F,X) \rightarrow U_{S}^{-}(F,X).

In particular, we will focus on the case where the local conditions (called Greenberg’s local conditions) are as follows: for
v\nmid p
we will set

U_{v}^{+}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=C^{\bullet}(G_{v}/I_{v},X^{I_{v}}),

and for
v\mid p
we choose a certain subrepresentation
F^{+}_{v}X\subseteq X
and set
U_{v}^{+}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=C^{\bullet}(G_{v},F^{+}_{v}X)
.

In the case where
F^{+}_{v}X=X
(resp.
F^{+}_{v}X=0
) for all
v\mid p
we will denote the associated Selmer complex by
\widetilde{C}^{\bullet}_{(p)}(X)
(resp.
\widetilde{C}^{\bullet}_{\rm str}(X)
).

Theorem 5.7

(Exact triangle of Selmer complexes)
.

Let
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
,
X_{3}
be admissible
G_{F}
-representations forming a split exact sequence as follows:

(5.1)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow X_{1}\xlongrightarrow{f}X_{2}\xlongrightarrow{g}X_{3}\longrightarrow 0.

Suppose also that for each
v\mid p
, this exact sequence induce the following exact sequences:

(5.2)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow U_{v}^{+}(X_{1})\xrightarrow{U_{v}^{+}(f)}U_{v}^{+}(X_{2})\xrightarrow{U_{v}^{+}(g)}U_{v}^{+}(X_{3})\longrightarrow 0.

Then we have the short exact sequence of the Selmer complexes:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\widetilde{C}^{\bullet}_{f}(F,X_{1},\Delta(X_{1}))\longrightarrow\widetilde{C}^{\bullet}_{f}(F,X_{2},\Delta(X_{2}))\longrightarrow\widetilde{C}^{\bullet}_{f}(F,X_{3},\Delta(X_{3}))\longrightarrow 0.

Moreover, if the splitting of (
5.1

(Perfectness of Selmer complexes)

Lemma 7.7
.

Let

-module, and for each

Proof.

Since
M
is a coadmissible
\mathcal{H}_{A}
-module,

for finitely generated
\mathcal{H}_{n}\widehat{\otimes}_{E}A
-modules

for each

For an arbitrary

, let

(7.11)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{x,n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\ker((\mathcal{H}_{A})_{n}\rightarrow(\mathcal{H}_{x})_{n}).

Recall that

M_{n}\cong M\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}(\mathcal{H}_{A})_{n},

thus

M_{n}\widehat{\otimes}_{(\mathcal{H}_{A})_{n}}(\mathcal{H}_{x})_{n}\cong(M\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}\mathcal{H}_{x})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}(\mathcal{H}_{A})_{n}.

By our running assumption, we have

Let
n\in{\mathbf{N}}
and a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
(\mathcal{H}_{A})_{n}=\mathcal{H}_{n}\widehat{\otimes}A
be arbitrary but fixed. We claim that there exists an
x\in U
such that
\mathfrak{m}\supseteq\mathfrak{p}_{x,n}
. Set
Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=W_{n}\times_{E}U=\mathrm{Spm}(\mathcal{H}_{n}\widehat{\otimes}_{E}A)
. Denote by

of affinoid algebras sending

to

. Set
x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi(\mathfrak{m})
(where

We claim that the fiber

Thus for all
\mathfrak{m}\in Y

with

, and recall that we already obtained

) and (
7.5
).

These computations show that the cohomologies of
\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V},\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{V}})
and
\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V}^{*}(1),\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{V}}^{\perp})
vanishes except

2

By the control theorem (
CTRL-2
) we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V},\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{V}})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}\mathcal{H}_{A}/{\mathfrak{p}_{x_{0}}}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm Iw}(V,D_{V}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V}^{*}(1),\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{V}}^{\perp})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}\mathcal{H}_{A}/{\mathfrak{p}_{x_{0}}}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm Iw}(V^{*}(1),D_{V}^{\perp}),

hence applying the determinant functor and the fact that

\det(\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm Iw}(V,D_{V}))={\rm char}_{\mathcal{H}}(H^{2}_{\rm Iw}(V,D_{V}))

and

\det(\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm Iw}(V^{*}(1),D_{V}^{\perp}))={\rm char}_{\mathcal{H}}(H^{2}_{\rm Iw}(V^{*}(1),D_{V}^{\perp}))

(see the last paragraph of the proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\det(\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V},\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{V}}))\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}\mathcal{H}_{A}/{\mathfrak{p}_{x_{0}}}

\displaystyle\cong{\rm char}_{\mathcal{H}}H^{2}_{\rm Iw}(V,D_{V}),

\displaystyle\det(\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V}^{*}(1),\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{V}}^{\perp}))\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{H}_{A}}\mathcal{H}_{A}/{\mathfrak{p}_{x_{0}}}

\displaystyle\cong{\rm char}_{\mathcal{H}}H^{2}_{\rm Iw}(V^{*}(1),D_{V}^{\perp}).

Finally, the factorizations follow from applying the determinant functor to the exact triangles (

∎

Theorem 7.9
.

Suppose that

is a finitely generated
\mathcal{H}_{B}
-module. There exists an

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathbf{V}_{3}^{\prime},\mathbf{D}_{3}^{\prime})=0,

\displaystyle H^{1}((\mathbf{V}_{3}^{\prime})^{*}(1),(\mathbf{D}_{3}^{\prime})^{\perp})=0,

where
\mathbf{V}_{3}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbf{V}_{3}\widehat{\otimes}_{B}(\mathcal{H}_{B})_{f}^{\iota}
and
\mathbf{D}_{3}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbf{D}_{3}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{R}_{B}}{(\mathcal{H}_{B})}_{f}^{\iota}

p
-adic
L
-functions:

\displaystyle\det(\mathbf{R\Gamma}(\mathbf{V}_{3}^{\prime},\mathbf{D}_{3}^{\prime}))\otimes_{(\mathcal{H}_{B})_{f}}\mathcal{H}_{A}

\displaystyle\cong\det(\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{Ad}},\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{Ad}})),

\displaystyle\det(\mathbf{R\Gamma}((\mathbf{V}_{3}^{\prime})^{*}(1),(\mathbf{D}_{3}^{\prime})^{\perp}))\otimes_{(\mathcal{H}_{B})_{f}}\mathcal{H}_{A}

\displaystyle\cong\det(\mathbf{R\Gamma}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{Ad}}^{*}(1),\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{Ad}}^{\perp})).

Proof.

From now on we will prove the theorem for
\mathbf{V}_{3}
and
\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{Ad}}
, the exact same arguments will prove the results for
(\mathbf{V}_{3})^{*}(1)
and
\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{Ad}}^{*}(1)
. By control theorem
[
Pot13
, Theorem 1.12]
we have

\mathbf{R\Gamma}(\mathbf{V}_{3},\mathbf{D}_{3})\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{\widetilde{\pi}}\mathcal{H}_{A}\cong\mathbf{R\Gamma}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{Ad}},\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{Ad}}).

Since
\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}
is a height
1
prime, the higher Tor terms
{\rm Tor}_{i}^{\mathcal{H}_{B}}({-},\mathcal{H}_{A})
vanishes for

, and the corresponding spectral sequence degenerates into the following information:

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V}_{3},\mathbf{D}_{3})[\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=0,

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow H^{1}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V}_{3},\mathbf{D}_{3})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}}\mathcal{H}_{B}/\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{Ad}},\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{Ad}})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V}_{3},\mathbf{D}_{3})[\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}]\rightarrow 0,

\displaystyle H^{2}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V}_{3},\mathbf{D}_{3})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}}\mathcal{H}_{B}/\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle\cong H^{2}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{Ad}},\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{Ad}}).

H^{1}_{\rm Iw}(\mathbf{V}_{3},\mathbf{D}_{3})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{H}_{B}}\mathcal{H}_{B}/\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}=0

,

(\mathcal{H}_{B})_{f}

\mathcal{H}_{B}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}_{B}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\pi}}

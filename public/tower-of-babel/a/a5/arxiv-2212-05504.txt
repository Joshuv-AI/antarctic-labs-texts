Correlation matrix of equi-correlated normal population: fluctuation of the largest eigenvalue, scaling of the bulk eigenvalues, and stock market
Yohji Akama

Abstract
Given an N N -dimensional sample of size T T and form a sample correlation matrix 𝐂 \mathbf{C} . Suppose that N N and T T tend to infinity with T / N T/N converging to a fixed finite constant Q > 0 Q>0 . If the population is a factor model, then the eigenvalue distribution of 𝐂 \mathbf{C} almost surely converges weakly to Marčenko-Pastur distribution such that the index is Q Q and the scale parameter is the limiting ratio of the specific variance to the i i -th variable ( i → ∞ ) (i\to\infty) . For an N N -dimensional normal population with equi-correlation coefficient ρ \rho , which is a one-factor model, for the largest eigenvalue λ \lambda of 𝐂 \mathbf{C} , we prove that λ / N \lambda/N converges to the equi-correlation coefficient ρ \rho almost surely. These results suggest an important role of an equi-correlated normal population and a factor model in (Laloux et al. Random matrix theory and financial correlations, Int. J. Theor. Appl. Finance , 2000): the histogram of the eigenvalue of sample correlation matrix of the returns of stock prices fits the density of Marčenko-Pastur distribution of index T / N T/N and scale parameter 1 − λ / N 1-\lambda/N . Moreover, we provide the limiting distribution of the largest eigenvalue of a sample covariance matrix of an equi-correlated normal population. We discuss the phase transition as to the decay rate of the equi-correlation coefficient in N N .

Introduction

For risk management and asset allocation, a sample correlation matrix is
essential. One of the pillars of Markowitz portfolio
theory
(
Markowitz 1959
;
Elton et al. 2014
)
is the study of correlation (or covariance) matrices, e.g.,
the optimal weight of each financial asset, given a set of assets with
average returns and risks, so that the overall portfolio offers the best
return for a fixed level of risk or, alternatively, the smallest risk
for a given overall return. For these optimizations, the eigenvalues of
covariance/correlating matrices are important. However, the eigenvalues
of a sample covariance matrix/correlation matrix in finance are
dispersed with noise, so various ways to eliminate noisy eigenvalues
from them are studied by
Laloux et al. 2000
,

El Karoui 2008
,
Ledoit & Wolf 2004
;
Ledoit & Wolf 2012
,

Ledoit & Péché 2011
,
Donoho et al. 2018
, to cite a few. For
this purpose,
Laloux et al. 2000
proposed to approximate
heuristically the distribution of noisy eigenvalues of a sample
covariance/correlation matrix, with
Marčenko-Pastur
distribution

(
Marčenko & Pastur 1967
)
of random matrix theory. Their idea influenced
on the statistical methods on vaccine design
(
Quadeer et al. 2018
)
.
We will discuss the
heuristic of
Laloux et al. 2000

mathematically, with an
equi-correlated normal population
, which plays an important role in various risk
models such as
dynamic equicorrelation

(

)

and
constant correlation model

(

)
.

The corresponding eigenvector

4
Phase transition of limiting distribution of largest eigenvalue

In our setting, all the correlation coefficients among

variables
being the positive constant
\rho
. It is, however, somehow unnatural in
the limit
N\to\infty.
We assume that

N>T

and the equi-correlation coefficient

converges to 0 from above as

For this setting with

of the largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{S})
and the asymptotic normality
of
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{S})
for a nonnormal population follows from

. Moreover, the limiting

\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{S})
for an equi-correlated normal population

coefficient

eigenvalue of
\mathbf{S}
formed by a spiked population model does so with
respect to the largest eigenvalue of the population covariance

Baik et al. 2005
)
.

4.1
Consistency and asymptotic normality of

In

(

)

Proposition 4.1
.

Let a data matrix

[\bm{X}_{1},\ldots,\bm{X}_{T}]

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}=c_{i}N^{\delta_{i}}\ (i=1,\ldots,m),\ \mbox{and}

Here

\mathbf{Z}

(i)

\delta_{i}\in(0,\,1]

Then, we have

2.

Assume also that the largest

of
{\mathbf{\Sigma}}

The condition (ii) is relieved for

(
(

)
.

In the assertion (

By (

Suppose that a population is possibly nonnormal, the population

{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{\rho}

4.2
Phase transition of limiting distribution of

\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{S})
for normal population as to decay rate of equi-correlation
coefficient

The limiting distribution of an appropriate scaling of a translation of the largest eigenvalue

of a sample
covariance matrix

is Tracy-Widom distribution if the

\rho
is 0, by

(

)
, and a normal

\rho
is a positive constant by

Theorem
4.3
(
2
),
we turn our attention to the case
\lim_{N\to\infty}N\rho<\infty
.

First, we recall
(
Baik et al. 2005
, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.1)
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume
Q>1
and the population is

\mathrm{N}_{N}(\bm{\mu},\,{\mathbf{\Sigma}})
, and
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{r}\geq\lambda_{r+1}=\cdots=\lambda_{N}=1
be the
eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{\Sigma}}
.

(a)

Suppose

,

\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{k}=1+1/\sqrt{Q}
, and
\set{\lambda_{k+1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}}\subset(0,\,1+1/\sqrt{Q})
. Then,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{S})\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\to}}}{(1+\sqrt{1/Q})^{2}},\ \mbox{and}

\displaystyle\Prb\left(\left(\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{S})-{(1+\sqrt{1/Q})^{2}}\right)\cdot\frac{\sqrt{Q}}{(1+\sqrt{Q})^{4/3}}T^{2/3}\leq x\right)\to F_{\mathrm{TW},k}(x),

(6)

where

is the distribution function of a generalization of the Tracy-Widom distribution
(
Baik et al. 2005
, Definition 1.1)
.

(b)

Suppose

,
\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{k}>1+1/\sqrt{Q}
, and

\set{\lambda_{k+1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}}\subset(0,\lambda_{1})
. Then,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{S})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\to}}\lambda_{1}+\frac{Q^{-1}\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}-1},\ \mbox{and}

\displaystyle\Prb\left(\left(\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{S})-\left(\lambda_{1}+\frac{Q^{-1}\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}-1}\right)\right)\cdot\frac{\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{2}-Q^{-1}\lambda_{1}^{2}/(\lambda_{1}-1)^{2}}}\leq x\right)\to F_{\mathrm{G},k}(x),

where

is the distribution function of a generalization of the Gaussian distribution
(
Baik et al. 2005
, Definition 1.2)
.

This proposition suggests the following phase transition: For a normal population, if

, then the largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{S})

satisfies the asymptotic normality in a sense of (

then an appropriate scaling of a translation of
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{S})
converges to
the Tracy-Widom distribution
G_{1}
in a sense of (
7
). This holds
for
\lim_{N\to\infty}N\rho=\infty
by Theorem
4.3
.

The detail of this phase transition will be studied elsewhere.

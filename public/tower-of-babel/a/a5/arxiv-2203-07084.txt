Projective dimension and Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of t t –spread ideals
Luca Amata, Marilena Crupi, Antonino Ficarra

Abstract
We study some algebraic invariants of t t –spread ideals, t ≥ 1 t\geq 1 , such as the projective dimension and the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity, by means of well–known graded resolutions. We state upper bounds for these invariants and, furthermore, we identify a special class of t t --spread ideals for which such bounds are optimal. † † Keywords: monomial ideals, minimal graded resolution, Taylor resolution, Koszul resolution, t t –spread ideals, regularity, edge ideals. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05E40, 13B25, 13D02, 16W50.

Introduction

Let
K
be a field and let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard polynomial ring in
n
variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
with coefficients in
K
. The study of the algebraic invariants of graded ideals in
S
is a central topic in commutative algebra. Among the graded ideals, monomial ideals play an essential role. Indeed,
for any graded ideal
I
of
S
, if
K
is a field of characteristic
0
, the generic initial ideal of
I
,
\operatorname{gin}(I)
, which is a monomial ideal, preserves the extremal Betti numbers of
I
, and consequently, it preserves the projective dimension and the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity
[
8
]
. On the other hand, a particular subclass of monomial ideals, the squarefree monomial ideals, are related to many combinatorial structures such as partial ordered sets, simplicial complexes, graphs and hypergraphs. Thus, to study the extremal combinatorics of such objects, one can use tools from commutative and homological algebra and examine the defining squarefree ideals associated to these objects.

Recently, Ene, Herzog and Qureshi,
[
13
]
, have generalized the notion of squarefree ideals. Given an integer
t\geq 1
, a monomial
u=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{d}}
,
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{d}\leq n
is said
t
–spread
if
i_{j+1}-i_{j}\geq t
, for all
j=1,\dots,d-1
. A
t
–spread ideal is a monomial ideal of
S
generated by
t
–spread monomials. If
t=1
, a
t
–spread ideal is just a squarefree ideal. This latter class of ideals has been vastly studied by many authors. One expects that many of the results which hold for squarefree ideals have “
t
–spread” analogues. In this direction, C. Andrei–Ciobanu has generalized the classical Kruskal–Katona theorem,
[
6
]
. Many other authors, including the ones of this paper, have examined
t
–spread ideals, and especially the class of
t
–spread strongly stable ideals, discussing some algebraic invariants
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
10
]
.

In this paper we focus our attention on the computation of projective dimension and regularity of
t
–spread ideals, for
t\geq 1
. Section
2
contains basic notations and
terminology from commutative algebra and combinatorics that will be used in the paper.
In Section
3
, if
I
is a squarefree ideal of
S
, we rewrite both a result on an upper bound for the projective dimension of
I

[
9
, Corollary 8]
and a result on an upper bound for the regularity of
I
given in
[
18
, Corollary 2.4]
,
via
homological tools and handling of monomials (Theorem
3.3
). Our techniques are completely different from those in
[
9
,
18
]
and we believe that they can be further improved in order to achieve
sharper
bounds for both the projective dimension and the regularity of a
t
–spread ideal,
t\geq 1
.
Hence, we obtain a bound for the regularity of a
t
–spread ideal, for all
t\geq 1
, generalizing the classical bound due to Hochster
[
19
]
for the squarefree case (
t=1
). It is
n-(t-1)
(Theorem
4.3
).
An example of a
t
–spread ideal whose regularity is given by such a value is the
Pascal ideal of type
(n,t)
, where
n
is the number of the indeterminates of
S
(Definition
4.5
).
Finally, in Section
5
, we provide some applications of the main results for edge ideals and some related classes of ideals.

2
Preliminaries

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
with the standard grading,
i.e.
, each
\deg x_{i}=1
. For any graded ideal
I
of
S
, there exists the unique minimal graded free
S
–resolution

{\mathbb{F}}:0\rightarrow F_{s}\xrightarrow{\ d_{s}\ }F_{s-1}\xrightarrow{d_{s-1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\ d_{2}\ }F_{1}\xrightarrow{\ d_{1}\ }F_{0}\xrightarrow{\ d_{0}\ }S/I\rightarrow 0,

with
F_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{i,j}}
. The numbers
\beta_{i,j}=\beta_{i,j}(S/I)
are called the graded Betti numbers of
S/I
, while
\beta_{i}(S/I)=\sum_{j}\beta_{i,j}(S/I)
are called the total Betti numbers of
S/I
. Recall, that the
projective dimension
and the
Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity
of
S/I
are defined, respectively, as follows:

\displaystyle{\operatorname{pd}}(S/I)

\displaystyle=\max\{i:\beta_{i}(S/I)\neq 0\},

\displaystyle{\operatorname{reg}}(S/I)

\displaystyle=\max\big\{\mbox{$j-i$ : $\beta_{i,j}(S/I)\neq 0$, for some $i$ and $j$}\big\}

\displaystyle=\max\big\{\mbox{$j$ : $\beta_{i,i+j}(S/I)\neq 0$, for some $i$}\big\}.

Note that
{\operatorname{pd}}(I)={\operatorname{pd}}(S/I)-1
and
{\operatorname{reg}}(I)={\operatorname{reg}}(S/I)+1
.
More precisely, the projective dimension
{\operatorname{pd}}(I)
is the length of a minimal graded free resolution of the graded ideal
I
.
Furthermore, the regularity of
I
is at least the smallest total degree of a
generator of
I
, and if all the minimal generators of
I
lie in the same degree, then
I
has linear free resolution precisely when that degree equals
{\operatorname{reg}}(I)

[
21
, Lemma 5.55]
.

The invariants above have been generalized in
[
8
]
by the notion of
extremal Betti number
.

Definition 2.1

An
i
–th Betti number
\beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0
is extremal if
\beta_{p,q}(I)=0
for all
p
and
q
satisfying the
following three conditions: (i)
p\geq i
, (ii)
p-q\geq i-j
, and (iii)
q\geq j+1
.

Now, let
I
be a graded ideal of the polynomial ring
S
.

I
is a (monomial) squarefree ideal of
S
if it is generated by squarefree monomials of
S
.

For a positive integer
n
, we denote by
[n]
the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Recall that a
simplicial complex

\Delta
on the vertex set
[n]
is a family of subsets of
[n]
, such that

–

\{i\}\in\Delta
for all
i\in[n]
, and

–

if
F\subseteq\Delta
,
G\subseteq F
, we have
G\in\Delta
.

The dimension of
\Delta
is the number
d=\max\{|F|-1:F\in\Delta\}
. It is well known that for any squarefree ideal
I
of
S
there exists a unique simplicial complex
\Delta
on
[n]
such that
I=I_{\Delta}
, where
I_{\Delta}
is the Stanley–Reisner ideal of
\Delta
,
i.e.
, the ideal of
S
generated by all squarefree monomials
x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{r}}
,
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n
, with
\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{r}\}\notin\Delta

[
15
]
. The Stanley–Reisner ideal,

, above defined is a squarefree ideal of
S
.

The notion of squarefree ideal has been generalized in
[
13
]
by the concept of
t
–spread ideal.

Given an integer
t\geq 0
, we say that a monomial
x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{d}}
of

1\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\dots\leq i_{d}\leq n

, for all

. If

, every
t
–spread monomial is a squarefree monomial. We say that a monomial ideal

is
t
–spread if

is generated by
t
–spread monomials. If

, every

Using the same notations as in
[
6
]
, we denote by

the set of all

–spread monomials of degree

of a monomial ideal

of
S
and by

the set of all
t
–spread monomials of degree

of the ring

. From
[

we have that

|M_{n,d,t}|\ =\ \binom{n-(d-1)(t-1)}{d}.

If

is a non empty subset of

, we define the

–shadow
of

to be the set

{\operatorname{Shad}}_{t}(T)\ =\ \big\{x_{i}w\,:\,w\in T\,\mbox{and $x_{i}w$ is $t$--spread monomial, $i=1,\dots,n$}\big\}.

Let

. To an arbitrary monomial ideal
I
of
S
, one can associate the following vector of integers:

f_{t}(I)=(f_{t,-1}(I),f_{t,0}(I),\dots),

with

–vector of

.

We close the section with the following definitions from
[
6
,
13
]
.

For a monomial

, the set
{\operatorname{supp}}(u)=\{i:x_{i}\ \text{divides}\ u\}
is called the

of

.

Definition 2.2

A non empty subset

A

–spread monomial ideal

–spread strongly stable if

–spread strongly stable set for all

.

Let

be the usual squarefree lex order
[

]

.

A

–spread ideal

If

, such that

[

. In general, a

–spread ideal

–spread lexsegment ideal with the same

–vector
[

. Indeed, it is still open the problem of classifying all

–spread lexsegment ideal with the same

–vector.

Set

\max(u)=\max\big\{i:i\in{\operatorname{supp}}(u)\big\},\,\,\mbox{for $u\neq 1$},

and
\max(u)=0
, if

.

For a

–spread strongly stable ideal

, one has

13
]
:

{\operatorname{pd}}(I)=\max\{\max(u)-t(\deg(u)-1)-1:u\in G(I)\},

.

3
Projective dimension and regularity

In this section, if
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, we discuss some optimal bounds for the projective dimension and the regularity of squarefree ideals of
S
. It is well–known by a result of Hochster
[
19
]
that for squarefree ideals the maximum value of the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity is given by
n
.
Indeed,
x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
is the squarefree monomial of the largest degree in a polynomial ring in
n
variables.
For
t
–spread ideals with
t\geq 2
this bound can be refined, as we will see in the sequel.

Let us start by quoting a result from
[
12
]
.

Theorem 3.1

[
12
, Theorem 4.26]
Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
–module,

{\mathbb{F}}:\cdots\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0,

a minimal graded free resolution of
M
with
F_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{i,j}}
, and

\mathbb{G}:\cdots\rightarrow G_{1}\rightarrow G_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

a graded free
S
–resolution of
M
with
G_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}S(-j)^{\textup{b}_{i,j}}
. Then

\beta_{i,j}(M)\ \leq\ \textup{b}_{i,j}

for all
i
and
j
.

Let
I
be a squarefree ideal of
S
with minimal set of monomial generators given by
G(I)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{p}\}
.

Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}

\displaystyle=\big\{{\operatorname{supp}}(u):u\in G(I)\big\}=\big\{{\operatorname{supp}}(u_{1}),\dots,{\operatorname{supp}}(u_{p})\big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{i+1}{\operatorname{supp}}(u_{j_{\ell}}):\{j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{i+1}\}\subseteq[p]\Big\},\,\mbox{for $i\geq 1$}.

The integer

s=\min\Big\{i:\mbox{ for all $A\in\mathcal{A}_{i}$},A=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{p}{\operatorname{supp}}(u_{\ell})\Big\}

will be called the
support index
of
I
.

One can quickly observe that if the sets in
\mathcal{A}_{0}
are pairwise disjoint, then the support index of
I
is equal to
p-1=|G(I)|-1
.

Example 3.2

Let
S={\mathbb{Q}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{11}]
and consider the following squarefree ideal
I=(x_{2}x_{4},x_{1}x_{5}x_{7},x_{3}x_{7}x_{9}x_{11})
of
S
. Setting
u_{1}=x_{2}x_{4}
,
u_{2}=x_{1}x_{5}x_{7}
and
u_{3}=x_{3}x_{7}x_{9}x_{11}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{{\operatorname{supp}}(u_{1}),{\operatorname{supp}}(u_{2}),{\operatorname{supp}}(u_{3})\big\}=\big\{\{2,4\},\{1,5,7\},\{3,7,9,11\}\big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{2}{\operatorname{supp}}(u_{j_{\ell}}):\{j_{1}<j_{2}\}\subseteq[3]\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{\{1,2,4,5,7\},\{2,3,4,7,9,11\},\{1,3,5,7,9,11\}\big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{3}{\operatorname{supp}}(u_{j_{\ell}}):\{j_{1}<j_{2}<j_{3}\}\subseteq[3]\Big\}=\big\{\{1,2,3,4,5,7,9,11\}\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{3}{\operatorname{supp}}(u_{\ell})\Big\}.

Hence,
s=2
is the support index of the ideal
I
.

If one identifies every squarefree monomial
u\in S
with its support, then if
I
is a squarefree ideal of
S
with
G(I)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{p}\}
, one obtains an equivalent definition of the support index
s
defined above. More precisely, let

\ell
be the smallest number with the property that for all integers
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{\ell}\leq p
, one has

{\operatorname{lcm}}(u_{j_{1}},u_{j_{2}},\ldots,u_{j_{\ell}})={\operatorname{lcm}}(u_{1},\dots,u_{p}).

Such an integer introduced in
[
18
]
is called
{\operatorname{b-cosize}}_{S}(I)
. Hence, the support index
s
of
I
is equal to the integer
{\operatorname{b-cosize}}_{S}(I)-1
. In
[
9
, Corollary 8]
, the authors proved that the projective dimension of a squarefree ideal
I
of
S
can be bounded by
{\operatorname{b-cosize}}_{S}(I)-1
.

In
[
18
]
, the notion of cosize of a squarefree ideal
I
of
S
with minimal system of generators given by
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{p}\}
has been introduced too. More precisely, let
w
be the smallest number
\ell
with the property that there exist
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{\ell}\leq p
such that

{\operatorname{lcm}}(u_{j_{1}},u_{j_{2}},\ldots,u_{j_{\ell}})={\operatorname{lcm}}(u_{1},\dots,u_{p}),

the number

\deg{\operatorname{lcm}}(u_{1},\dots,u_{p})-w

is called cosize of
I
and denoted by
{\operatorname{cosize}}(I)
. Moreover, in
[
18
, Corollary 1.4]
, the authors proved that
{\operatorname{reg}}(I)\leq{\operatorname{cosize}}(I)+1
.

Both the cited bounds were obtained by some results due to Lyubeznik
[
20
]
and applying the Alexander duality.
We will show how to compute the projective dimension and the regularity of squarefree ideals (thus, also for
t
–spread ideals) by using only set–theoretic operations. A fundamental tool will be the Taylor complex, which for each monomial ideal provides a graded free resolution, but which in general is not minimal
[
15
]
.

Theorem 3.3

Let
I
be a squarefree ideal of
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
with minimal generating set
G(I)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{p}\}
. Let
s
be the support index of
I
, then

(a)

{\operatorname{pd}}(I)\leq\min\{s,n\}=\min\{{\operatorname{b-cosize}}_{S}(I)-1,n\}
,

(b)

{\operatorname{reg}}(I)\leq{\operatorname{cosize}}(I)+1
.

Proof.
In order to simplify the notation, we set

\Omega_{i}={\operatorname{supp}}(u_{i}),\,i=1,\dots,p

and

\Omega=\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}\Omega_{i}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}

\displaystyle=\big\{\Omega_{1},\dots,\Omega_{p}\big\},

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{i+1}\Omega_{j_{\ell}}:\{j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{i+1}\}\subseteq[p]\Big\},\,\mbox{for $i=1,\dots,p-1$}.

To get the statements, we use the
Taylor resolution
. Following the same notations as in
[
15
]
, to the sequence
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{p}\}
of monomial generators of
I
, we associate a complex
\mathbb{T}
of free
S
–modules defined as follows: let
T_{1}
be a free
S
–module with basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{p}\}
. Then

–

T_{i}\ =\ {\bigwedge}^{i}T_{1}
. More precisely,
T_{i}
is a free
S
–module with basis the elements

{\bf e}_{F}\ =\ e_{j_{1}}\wedge e_{j_{2}}\dots\wedge e_{j_{i}},\,\,F=\{j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{i}\}\subseteq[p];

–

the differentials
\partial_{i}:T_{i}\rightarrow T_{i-1}
, for
i=1,\dots,p
, are defined by

\partial_{i}({\bf e}_{F})\ =\ \sum_{i\in F}(-1)^{\sigma(F,i)}\frac{{\operatorname{lcm}}(u_{j}:j\in F)}{{\operatorname{lcm}}(u_{j}:j\in F\setminus\{i\})}{\bf e}_{F\setminus\{i\}},

where
\sigma(F,i)=\big|\{j\in F:j<i\}\big|
.

To each basis element
{\bf e}_{F}
, with
F=\{j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{i}\}\subseteq[p]
, we assign degree

\deg({\bf e}_{F})=\deg({\operatorname{lcm}}(u_{j}:j\in F))=|A|,

(2)

with
A=\Omega_{j_{1}}\cup\Omega_{j_{2}}\cup\dots\cup\Omega_{j_{i}}\in\mathcal{A}_{i-1}
.
The differentials
\partial_{i}
are homogeneous maps of graded free
S
–modules and so

\mathbb{T}:0\rightarrow T_{p}\xrightarrow{\ \partial_{p}\ }T_{p-1}\xrightarrow{\ \partial_{p-1}\ }\cdots\xrightarrow{\ \partial_{3}\ }T_{2}\xrightarrow{\ \partial_{2}\ }T_{1}\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0

(3)

is a graded free resolution of
I

[
15
, Theorem 7.1.1]
.
We can write
T_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}S(-j)^{\text{b}_{i,j}}
, for some positive integers
\text{b}_{i,j}
,
and consider the unique minimal graded free
S
–resolution of
I

{\mathbb{F}}:0\rightarrow F_{r}\xrightarrow{\ d_{r}\ }F_{r-1}\xrightarrow{d_{r-1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\ d_{3}\ }F_{2}\xrightarrow{\ d_{2}\ }F_{1}\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0,

with
F_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{i,j}}
, thus (Theorem
3.1
)

\beta_{i,j}(I)\leq\text{b}_{i,j},\quad\mbox{for all $i$ and $j$}.

(4)

(a) Consider the support index
s=\min\big\{i:\mbox{ for all $A\in\mathcal{A}_{i}$},A=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{p}\Omega_{\ell}=\Omega\big\}
of the squarefree ideal
I
, then

{\operatorname{pd}}(I)=r\leq s.

Indeed, by the meaning of
s
,
T_{s}
is a graded free
S
–module with a basis whose elements have degrees
\leq|\Omega|
, whereas
T_{s+1}
is a graded free
S
–module with a basis whose elements all have degrees equal to
|\Omega|
. The same reasoning holds for all
T_{j}
with
s+1\leq j\leq p
.

By (
4
),
F_{s+2}
is a graded free
S
–submodule of
T_{s+2}
. If
F_{s+2}\neq 0
,
then, by the minimality of
{\mathbb{F}}
,
{\operatorname{im}}(F_{s+2})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}F_{s+1}
, where
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
. Thus there exists a basis element of
F_{s+2}
whose degree is greater than
|\Omega|
. An absurd. Hence,
{\operatorname{pd}}(I)\leq s
.

On the other hand, by Hilbert Syzygy’s Theorem,
{\operatorname{pd}}(I)\leq n
. Hence,
{\operatorname{pd}}(I)\leq\min\{s,n\}
.

(b) From (
4
), we have

\displaystyle{\operatorname{reg}}(I)

\displaystyle=\max\big\{j-i:\beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0,\ \text{for some}\ i\ \text{and}\ j\big\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\big\{j-i:\text{b}_{i,j}\neq 0,\ \text{for some}\ i\ \text{and}\ j\big\}.

From the Taylor resolution (
3
), one gets that the number

\max\big\{j-i:\text{b}_{i,j}\neq 0,\ \text{for some}\ i\ \text{and}\ j\big\}

is determined by the
first
step of the resolution
\mathbb{T}
in which the free
S
–module has at least one direct summand
S(-j)
with
j=|\Omega|
. More precisely, if
1\leq\ell\leq p
is the smallest integer such that the free
S
–module
T_{\ell}=\bigoplus_{j}S(-j)^{\text{b}_{\ell,j}}
has the property that there exists a direct summand
S
shifted by
j
with
j=|\Omega|
(for some
j
), then

\max\big\{j-i:\text{b}_{i,j}\neq 0,\ \text{for some}\ i\ \text{and}\ j\big\}=|\Omega|-(\ell-1)={\operatorname{cosize}}(I)+1.

The assertion follows.

\Box

Remark 3.4

\displaystyle\deg({\bf x}_{n,t,j})\

\displaystyle=\ \begin{cases}\big|\{j,j+t,\dots,j+kt\}\big|&\text{if}\,\,\,j=1,\dots,i,\\
\big|\{j,j+t,\dots,j+(k-1)t\}\big|&\text{if}\,\,\,j=i+1,\dots,t.\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\ \begin{cases}\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{t}\right\rfloor+1&\text{if}\ j=1,\dots,i,\\
\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{t}\right\rfloor&\text{if}\ j=i+1,\dots,t.\end{cases}

(12)

One can observe that

. Hence, setting

.

one obtains

and

.

Since

{\operatorname{Shad}}_{t}(\{{\bf x}_{n,t,i+1},\dots,{\bf x}_{n,t,t}\})=\varnothing
,
(c) follows.

(d) Since

\displaystyle\textup{Hilb}_{S/I}(z)\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{\prod_{u\in G(I)}(1+z+\ldots+z^{\deg(u)-1})}{(1-z)^{n-|G(I)|}}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{{(1+z+\ldots+z^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{t}\rfloor})}^{i}{(1+z+\ldots+z^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{t}\rfloor-1})}^{t-i}}{{(1-z)}^{n-t}},

and (d) holds.

5
Some Applications

In this section we consider some applications of the previous results. In particular, we analyze the regularity of
t
–spread ideals equigenerated in degree two by tools from the graph theory.

A
simple graph

G
is an ordered pair of disjoint finite sets
(V(G),E(G))
such that
E(G)
is a
subset of the set of unordered pairs of
V(G)
. The set
V(G)
is the set of
vertices

and the set
E(G)
is called the set of
edges
.

If
e=\{u,v\}
is an edge of
G
one says that the vertices
u
and
v
are
adjacent
.

A
walk
of length
n
in
G
is an alternating sequence of vertices and edges,
written as
w=\{v_{0},z_{1},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1},z_{n},v_{n}\}
, where
z_{i}=\{v_{i-1},v_{i}\}
is the edge joining the vertices
v_{i-1}
and
v_{i}
. A walk may also be written
\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{n}\}

with the edges understood, or
\{z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}\}
with the vertices understood.
If
v_{0}=v_{n}
, the walk
w
is called a
closed walk
. A
path
is a walk with all its
vertices distinct. A
cycle
of length
n
is a closed path
\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{n}\}
in which
n\geq 3
.
A
forest
is an acyclic graph.

To each simple graph
G
on the vertex set
V(G)
we can associate a squarefree ideal
I(G)
of the polynomial ring
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, called the
edge ideal
associated to
G
, defined as follows
[
24
]
:

I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}:\textrm{$x_{i}$ is adjacent to $x_{j}$})=(x_{i}x_{j}:\{i,j\}\in E(G)).

We quote the next definition from
[
24
]
.

Definition 5.1

An
induced matching
in a graph
G
is a set of pairwise disjoint edges
e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}
such that the only edges of
G
contained in
\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}e_{i}
are
e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}
. The
induced matching number
, denoted by
{\operatorname{im}}(G)
, is the
number of edges in the largest induced matching.

Proposition 5.2

[
24
, Corollary 7.5.6.]
If
G
is a forest, then

{\operatorname{reg}}(I(G))={\operatorname{im}}(G)+1.

See
[
24
]
for detailed information on this subject.

Corollary 5.3

Let
n
be a positive integer and let
G
be a simple graph on
[n]
. Assume

E(G)=\begin{cases}\big\{e_{i}=\big\{i,i+\tfrac{n}{2}\big\}:i=1,\dots,\tfrac{n}{2}\big\}&\text{if $n$ is even},\\
\\
\big\{e_{i}=\big\{i,i+\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\big\}:i=1,\dots,\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\big\}\cup\big\{\{1,n\}\big\}&\text{if $n$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Then

{\operatorname{reg}}(I(G))=\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1.

Proof.
Indeed, in both cases
G
is a forest and the assertion follows from Proposition
5.2
.

\Box

Now, we are ready to state and prove the analogous of Theorem
4.3
for the regularity of
t
–spread ideals generated in degree two.

Corollary 5.4

Let
n,t\geq 1
with
n\geq 2t
and let
I
be a
t
–spread ideal equigenerated in degree two of
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. Then

{\operatorname{reg}}(I)\ \leq\ \left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1.

Proof.
We distinguish two cases:
n
even and
n
odd.

Case 1.
Let
n
be even. Following Theorem
4.3
, it suffices to find
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor=\tfrac{n}{2}

t
–spread monomials of degree two, whose supports are pairwise disjoint and such that their union is
[n]
. For this purpose, we can consider the graph
G
on
[n]
with

E(G)\ =\ \Big\{e_{i}=\big\{i,i+\tfrac{n}{2}\big\}:i=1,\dots,\tfrac{n}{2}\Big\}.

The ideal
I(G)
is the
t
–spread ideal minimally generated by the required
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor=\tfrac{n}{2}

t
–spread monomials. From Corollary
5.3
(see also Theorem
4.1
) and Theorem
4.3
, we have

{\operatorname{reg}}(I)\leq{\operatorname{reg}}(I(G))=\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1=n-(\tfrac{n}{2}-1).

Case 2.
Let
n
be odd.
Let us consider the graph
G
on the vertex set
[n]
, with

E(G)\ =\ \Big\{e_{i}=\big\{i,i+\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\big\}:i=1,\dots,\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\Big\}\cup\big\{\{1,n\}\big\}.

From Corollary
5.3
, we have that

{\operatorname{reg}}(I(G))=\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1.

On the other hand, since the structure of

I(G)=(x_{i}x_{i+\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor}:i=1,\dots,\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor)+(x_{1}x_{n}),

from Theorem
4.3
, it follows that

{\operatorname{reg}}(I)\leq{\operatorname{reg}}(I(G))=\left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1.

\Box

Corollary 5.5

Let
n\geq 2
and
I
an edge ideal of
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. Then

{\operatorname{reg}}(I)\ \leq\ \left\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\right\rfloor+1.

We close the section with a slight modification of the proof of Corollary
5.4
that, given three positive numbers
n,d,t\geq 1
such that
n\geq 1+(d-1)t
, allows us to obtain an optimal upper bound for the regularity of a
t
–spread ideal generated in degrees at most
d\geq 1
.

Theorem 5.6

Let
n,d,t\geq 1
with
n\geq 1+(d-1)t
and let
I
be a
t
–spread ideal of
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
generated in degrees at most
d
. Then

{\operatorname{reg}}(I)\ \leq\ n+1-\max\{\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil,t\}.

(15)

Proof.
Following Theorem
4.3
, it suffices to find the minimum possible number of
t
–spread monomials of degrees at most
d
whose supports are pairwise disjoint and such that the union of their supports is
[n]
. We prove that this number is
\max\{\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil,t\}
.
We distinguish two cases.

Case 1.

\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil\geq t
.
Suppose

n=\ell+d\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil
,
0\leq\ell\leq d-1
.
Using analogous techniques as in Proposition
4.9
, we consider the following
t
–spread monomials

u_{i}\ =\ \begin{cases}x_{i}x_{i+\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil}\cdots x_{i+(d-2)\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil}x_{i+(d-1)\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil}&\text{if}\ i=1,\dots,\ell,\\
x_{i}x_{i+\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil}\cdots x_{i+(d-2)\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil}&\text{if}\ i=\ell+1,\dots,\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil.\\
\end{cases}

Since
\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil\geq t
, the monomials
u_{i}
are
t
–spread monomials of degrees at most
d
.
Let us consider the ideal
J=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil})
. Since
J
is the Pascal ideal of type
(n,\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil)
,
then by Theorem
4.1
,

{\operatorname{reg}}(J)\ =\ n-(\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil-1)\ =\ n+1-\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil.

Therefore,
J
is the
t
–spread ideal generated in degrees at most
d
with the maximum possible regularity. Indeed, the minimum possible number of generators of a squarefree ideal generated in degrees at most
d
, is
\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil
.

Case 2.

\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil<t
. In such case,
n<dt
, thus
1+(d-1)t\leq n<dt
.

Hence,

d-1\leq\tfrac{n-1}{t}<d-\tfrac{1}{t}
, and so

d-1\leq\left\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{t}\right\rfloor<d.

Finally,
d\leq\left\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{t}\right\rfloor+1<d+1
. Therefore,
\left\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{t}\right\rfloor+1=d
. By (
10

\ell=\left\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{t}\right\rfloor+1=d
is the maximum degree of a
t
–spread monomial of
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

(n,t)
gives the maximum regularity of a
t
–spread ideal of
S
generated in degree at most
\ell=d
. Since
\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil<t
, we have
\max\{\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil,t\}=t
. Hence,
n+1-\max\{\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil,t\}=n-(t-1)
and the bound given in (
15
) is true. The statement is proved.

\Box

Remark 5.7

Theorem
5.6
generalizes Theorem
4.3
. Let

, then

\tfrac{n}{d}\leq t
. Thus
\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil\leq t
and
\max\{\left\lceil\tfrac{n}{d}\right\rceil,t\}=t
. Therefore, by equation (

t
–spread ideal of
S
,
i.e.
,
n+1-t
, and we obtain again Theorem
4.3
.

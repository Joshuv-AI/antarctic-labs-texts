Finite-rank complex deformations of random band matrices: sigma-model approximation
Mariya Shcherbina, Tatyana Shcherbina

Abstract
We study the distribution of complex eigenvalues z 1 , … , z N z_{1},\ldots,z_{N} of random Hermitian N × N N\times N block band matrices with a complex deformation of a finite rank. Assuming that the width of the band W W grows faster than N \sqrt{N} , we proved that the limiting density of ℑ ⁡ z 1 , … , ℑ ⁡ z N \Im z_{1},\ldots,\Im z_{N} in a sigma-model approximation coincides with that for the Gaussian Unitary Ensemble. The method follows the techniques of [ 16 ] .

According to the works

One of the most interesting questions about the spectral statistics of

In contrast to the classical non-Hermitian models such as Ginibre ensemble (random matrices with iid entries), if

fluctuations on the

For the case

Let us mention also that the cases of non-Hermitian symmetry, and in particular real symmetric case, are much more involved,

In this paper we consider

The main feature of RBM is that their local spectral statistics is conjectured to exhibit the crossover at

the eigenvectors are expected to be
delocalized and the local spectral statistics is governed by the Wigner-Dyson (GUE/GOE) statistics, and for

and the local spectral statistics is Poisson. The conjecture is supported by the physical derivation due to Fyodorov and Mirlin (see

[
3

first in the so-called sigma-model approximation
[
16

[
17

by the application of the supersymmetric transfer matrix approach.

The main advantage of the SUSY techniques here is that the main spectral
characteristics of the model (

combine the SUSY techniques with a transfer matrix approach. However, the rigorous application of the techniques to the main spectral characteristics of RBM is quite difficult due to
the complicated structure and non self-adjointness of the corresponding transfer operator. So it is easier to apply it first to the so-called sigma-model approximation,
which is often used by physicists to study complicated statistical mechanics systems. In such approximation spins of the statistical mechanics model take values in
some symmetric space (

5.1
Grassmann integration

Let us consider two sets of formal variables

\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{\overline{\psi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
, which satisfy the anticommutation
conditions

\psi_{j}\psi_{k}+\psi_{k}\psi_{j}=\overline{\psi}_{j}\psi_{k}+\psi_{k}\overline{\psi}_{j}=\overline{\psi}_{j}\overline{\psi}_{k}+\overline{\psi}_{k}\overline{\psi}_{j}=0,\quad j,k=1,\ldots,n.

Note that this definition implies
\psi_{j}^{2}=\overline{\psi}_{j}^{2}=0

These two sets of variables
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
and
\{\overline{\psi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}

\mathfrak{A}
. Taking into account that
\psi_{j}^{2}=0
, we have that all elements of
\mathfrak{A}

are polynomials of
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
and
\{\overline{\psi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
of degree at most one
in each variable. We can also define functions of
the Grassmann variables. Let
\chi
be an element of
\mathfrak{A}

\chi=a+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}(a_{j}\psi_{j}+b_{j}\overline{\psi}_{j})+\sum\limits_{j\neq k}(a_{j,k}\psi_{j}\psi_{k}+b_{j,k}\psi_{j}\overline{\psi}_{k}+c_{j,k}\overline{\psi}_{j}\overline{\psi}_{k})+\ldots.

(5.2)

For any
sufficiently smooth function
f
we define by

the element of
\mathfrak{A}
obtained by substituting
\chi-a

in the Taylor series of
f
at the point
a
. Since
\chi
is a polynomial of
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
,

\{\overline{\psi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
of the form (

), according to (

) there exists such

.

Following Berezin
[

]
, we define the operation of
integration with respect to the anticommuting variables in a formal

\displaystyle\int d\,\psi_{j}=\displaystyle\int d\,\overline{\psi}_{j}=0,\quad\displaystyle\int\psi_{j}d\,\psi_{j}=\displaystyle\int\overline{\psi}_{j}d\,\overline{\psi}_{j}=1,

and then extend the definition to the general element of
\mathfrak{A}
by
the linearity. A multiple integral is defined to be a repeated

and

anticommute with each other and with the
variables
\psi_{j}

\overline{\psi}_{k}

then

\displaystyle\int f(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{k})d\,\psi_{k}\ldots d\,\psi_{1}=p_{1,2,\ldots,k}.

Let

be an ordinary Hermitian matrix with a positive real part. The following Gaussian
integral is well-known

\displaystyle\int\exp\Big\{-\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{n}A_{jk}z_{j}\overline{z}_{k}\Big\}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\dfrac{d\,\Re z_{j}d\,\Im z_{j}}{\pi}=\dfrac{1}{\mathrm{det}A}.

(5.3)

(5.4)

where
A

Let
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be some function that depends only on combinations

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}\phi:=\Big\{\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{p}\bar{\phi}_{l\alpha}\phi_{s\alpha}\Big\}_{l,s=1}^{2},

and set

d\Phi=\prod\limits_{l=1}^{2}\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{p}d\Re\phi_{l\alpha}d\Im\phi_{l\alpha}.

Assume also that
p\geq 2

\int F\left(\bar{\phi}\phi\right)d\Phi=\dfrac{\pi^{2p-1}}{(p-1)!(p-2)!}\int F(B)\cdot\mathrm{det}^{p-2}B\,dB,

\displaystyle dB

On the universal family of Hilbert schemes of points on a surface
Lei Song

Abstract
. For a smooth quasi-projective surface X X and an integer n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , we show that the universal family Z n Z^{n} over the Hilbert scheme Hilb n ​ ( X ) \text{Hilb}^{n}(X) of n n points has non ℚ \mathbb{Q} -Gorenstein, rational singularities, and that the Samuel multiplicity μ \mu at a closed point on Z n Z^{n} can be computed in terms of the dimension of the socle. We also show that μ ≤ n \mu\leq n .



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective surface over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
. Let
\text{Hilb}^{n}(X)
denote the Hilbert scheme of zero dimensional closed subschemes of
X
of length
n
. Fogarty’s fundamental result
[
5
]
claims that
\text{Hilb}^{n}(X)
is a smooth, irreducible variety of dimension
2n
. When
X
is complete, one may consider
\text{Hilb}^{n}(X)
as a natural compactification of the space of
n
unlabeled distinct points on
X
. For this reason,
\text{Hilb}^{n}(X)
is a quite useful tool for dealing with infinitely near points on
X
.

The universal family
Z^{n}\subset\text{Hilb}^{n}(X)\times X
, with the induced projection
\pi:Z^{n}\rightarrow\text{Hilb}^{n}(X)
, is a finite flat cover of degree
n
over
\text{Hilb}^{n}(X)
. The importance of
Z^{n}
lies in its role in inductive approaches for the study of Hilbert schemes, e.g. in deduction of the Picard scheme of
\text{Hilb}^{n}(X)

[
6
]
, and in calculation of the Nakajima constants
[
4
]
. While
Z^{2}
is simply the blow up of
X\times X
along the diagonal,
Z^{n}
is complicated in general. It is well known that
Z^{n}
is irreducible, singular (except
n
=2), and Cohen-Macaulay. By
[
6
]
,
Z^{n}
is also normal with
R_{3}
condition. More precisely, we prove in this note

Theorem 1.1
.

The universal family
Z^{n}
is non
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein, and has rational singularities. For a closed point
\zeta=(\xi,p)\in Z^{n}
, the Samuel multiplicity
\mu={b_{2}+1\choose 2}
, where
b_{2}=b_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\xi,p})
is the dimension of the socle of
\mathcal{O}_{\xi,p}
.

We note that
b_{2}+1
equals the minimal number of generators of the ideal
\mathscr{I}_{\xi,p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
; see Lemma
2.1
.

For
i>0
, let
V^{i}=\left\{(\xi,p)\in Z^{n}\;|\;b_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\xi,p})=i\;\right\}
. The locally closed subsets
V^{i}
form a stratification of
Z^{n}
. A proposition due to Ellingsrud and Lehn
[
3
]
says that
\text{codim}(V^{i},\text{Hilb}^{n}(X)\times X)\geq 2i
. This kind of codimension estimate is an ingredient in proving the irreducibility of
\text{Hilb}^{n}(X)
and more generally, that of certain quot schemes. An immediate consequence of their estimate is that
b_{2}\leq n+1
. In fact, we have

Theorem 1.2
.

b_{2}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{1+8n}-1}{2}\right\rfloor,

and the bound is optimal. Consequently, one has
\mu\leq n
.

Haiman
[
8
, Prop 3.5.3]
proves a similar result on multiplicity, namely
\dim_{k}\mathcal{O}_{\xi,p}\geq{b_{2}+1\choose 2}
, which implies that
\mu\leq n
. We need to point out that
\mu\neq\dim_{k}\mathcal{O}_{\xi,p}
in general.

For a point

\mathfrak{m}_{p}

. By Nakayama lemma
e(\mathscr{I}_{\xi,p})=\dim_{k(p)}{\mathscr{I}_{\xi,p}\otimes k(p)}
.

Since

is smooth and of dimension two,
\mathcal{O}_{\xi,p}
has a minimal free resolution

(2.1)

0\rightarrow{\overset{b_{2}}{\oplus}\mathcal{O}_{X,p}}\xrightarrow{\varphi}{\overset{b_{1}}{\oplus}\mathcal{O}_{X,p}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}_{X,p}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\xi,p}\rightarrow 0,

where obviously

, and

\varphi
is represented by a matrix with entries in
\mathfrak{m}_{p}
. The Hilbert-Burch theorem
[
2

says that all the
b_{2}\times b_{2}
minors of
\varphi
generate the ideal
\mathscr{I}_{\xi,p}
. A standard computation of homological algebra shows

Lemma 2.1
.

With the notations above,
\dim_{k(p)}\text{Soc}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\xi,p}\right)=b_{2}=e(\mathscr{I}_{\xi,p})-1
.∎

3.

We denote the discriminant divisor of the finite map
\pi:Z^{n}\rightarrow\text{Hilb}^{n}(X)
by
B^{n}
, which is a prime divisor parameterizing all non-reduced subschemes of

of length

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In the proof of
[
6

, Fogarty shows that there are precisely two prime Weil divisors on
Z^{n}
dominating
B^{n}
, which are described as

\displaystyle E^{n}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\{(\xi,p)\;|\;\xi\;\text{has type}\;(2,1,\cdots,1),\text{and}\;l_{p}(\xi)=2\}},

\displaystyle E^{n}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\{(\xi,p)\;|\;\xi\;\text{non reduced},l_{p}(\xi)=1\}},

with the reduced induced closed subscheme structure. Moreover he proves that neither
E^{n}_{1}
nor
E^{n}_{2}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.

Let
r_{i}
be the ramification index of
E^{n}_{i}
over
B^{n}
, and
K(\ast)
the field of functions of
\ast
. From the identity

r_{1}[K(E^{n}_{1}):K(B^{n})]+r_{2}[K(E^{n}_{2}):K(B^{n})]=n,

, we see that

and
r_{2}=1
.

Then Riemann-Hurwitz formula for finite maps claims that

(3.1)

K_{Z^{n}}+(2c-1)E^{n}_{1}+cE^{n}_{2}=\pi^{*}(K_{\text{Hilb}^{n}(X)}+cB^{n}),

for all

. Thereby we deduce that
K_{Z^{n}}
cannot be
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier by setting

or
0
.

It thus remains to show the rational singularities of

and compute the Samuel multiplicity.
Let

be the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{\text{Hilb}^{n}(X)\times X,\zeta}

Z^{n}
is a codimension two Cohen-Macaulay subscheme of

0\rightarrow{\overset{b_{2}}{\oplus}R}\rightarrow{\overset{b_{1}}{\oplus}R}\rightarrow{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z^{n},\zeta}\rightarrow 0.

Claim A

Proof of Claim A.

Let

\displaystyle\text{Tor}^{R}_{i}(\mathcal{O}_{Z^{n},\zeta},\mathcal{O}_{\{\xi\}\times{X},p})

Remark 3.1
.

As a smooth surface varies, the universal families over Hilbert schemes of
n
points easily patch together. More precisely, let
f:X\rightarrow C
be a smooth, projective morphism from a 3-fold
X
to a smooth curve
C
over
k
. By
[
5
, Thm. 2.9]
, the relative Hilbert scheme of
n
points
\text{Hilb}^{n}(X/C)
is smooth over
C
. The composite morphism
h:Z^{n}_{X/C}\rightarrow\text{Hilb}^{n}(X/C)\rightarrow C
from the universal family is a flat morphism, so one gets a flat family of algebraic varieties with non
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein and rational singularities.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We can assume that
\textrm{supp}(\xi)=\{p\}

(A,\mathfrak{m})
denote the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
and

the defining ideal of
\xi
. Since

is Artinian local

-algebra, one has

as

-algebras, where
\hat{A}
, the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion of

, is isomorphic to

.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{n})
be the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{2},O}
of

at the origin. For

, one has the surjection
R/{{\mathfrak{n}}^{r}}\simeq\hat{A}/{\hat{\mathfrak{m}}^{r}}\rightarrow\hat{A}/{\hat{I}}\rightarrow 0
, so there exists an ideal

such that

as

-algebras. Therefore it suffices to consider the case that

and
\textrm{supp}(\xi)=\{O\}
.

Fix a monomial order

and let

be the initial ideal of

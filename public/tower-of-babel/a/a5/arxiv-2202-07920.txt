Spectroscopic characterization of singlet-triplet doorway states of aluminum monofluoride Preprint: AIP/123-QED
N. Walter, J. Seifert, S. Truppe, H.C. Schewe, B.G. Sartakov, G. Meijer

Abstract
Aluminum monofluoride (AlF) possesses highly favorable properties for laser cooling, both via the A Π 1 {}^{1}\Pi and a Π 3 {}^{3}\Pi states. Determining efficient pathways between the singlet and the triplet manifold of electronic states will be advantageous for future experiments at ultralow temperatures. The lowest rotational levels of the A Π 1 , v = 6 {}^{1}\Pi,v=6 and b Σ + 3 , v = 5 {}^{3}\Sigma^{+},v=5 states of AlF are nearly iso-energetic and interact via spin-orbit coupling. These levels thus have a strongly mixed spin-character and provide a singlet-triplet doorway. We here present a hyperfine resolved spectroscopic study of the A Π 1 , v = 6 {}^{1}\Pi,v=6 // b Σ + 3 , v = 5 {}^{3}\Sigma^{+},v=5 perturbed system in a jet-cooled, pulsed molecular beam. From a fit to the observed energies of the hyperfine levels, the fine and hyperfine structure parameters of the coupled states, their relative energies as well as the spin-orbit interaction parameter are determined. The standard deviation of the fit is about 15 MHz. We experimentally determine the radiative lifetimes of selected hyperfine levels by time-delayed ionization, Lamb dip spectroscopy and accurate measurements of the transition lineshapes. The measured lifetimes range between 2 ns and 200 ns, determined by the degree of singlet-triplet mixing for each level.

Appendix A
Lamb dip experiments

In a Lamb dip experiment, a dip with a Lorentzian profile is observed, centered on top of a Doppler broadened line. The depth of the dip and the width of the Lorentzian profile contain information on the peak absorption cross section of the transition and on the lifetime of the upper level. The functional dependence of the depth and width of the Lamb dip on the cross section and lifetime can be found in textbooks for a fully closed two-level system.
Demtröder 2015
Here the equations for a fully open two-level system are derived.

A.1
Rate equation modelling of a general two-level system

We start with a certain number of molecules
N_{a}(t=0)=N^{0}_{a}
at time
t=0
in level
a
. This lower level is connected with a laser to an upper level
b
, that is originally empty, i.e.
N_{b}(t=0)=0
. The energy separation between the levels is given by

\Gamma_{\text{r}}
(in s

\Gamma
(in s
-1

\Gamma=1/\tau
, where
\tau

\sigma(\nu)=\sigma_{0}\,\frac{(\gamma/2)^{2}}{(\nu-\nu_{0})^{2}+(\gamma/2)^{2}}\equiv\sigma_{0}\,\mathcal{L}(\nu_{0},\gamma)

(4)

where
\sigma_{0}

\nu_{0}

\nu

A.2
Lamb dips in an open two-level system

In an open two-level system, the relation
\Gamma_{\text{r}}\ll\Gamma
holds. In the situation that
\sigma_{0}\Phi\ll\Gamma
we can approximate
\lambda_{+}\approx-\sigma(\nu)\Phi
and
\lambda_{-}\approx-\Gamma
. The number of fluorescence photons,
n_{\textrm{fl}}(\nu,T)
, is given by

n_{\textrm{fl}}(\nu,T)\propto N^{0}_{a}\sigma(\nu)\Phi\int_{0}^{T}\left(e^{-\sigma(\nu)\Phi t}-e^{-\Gamma t}\right)\,\textrm{d}\,t.

(9)

When the relaxation is fast, the second term under the integral can be neglected, and we find

n_{\textrm{fl}}(\nu,T)\propto N^{0}_{a}(1-e^{-\sigma(\nu)\Phi T})=N^{0}_{a}(1-e^{-\sigma_{0}\Phi T\mathcal{L}(\nu_{0},\gamma)}).

(10)

Using that we can write

\frac{1-e^{-\sigma(\nu)\Phi T}}{1-e^{-\sigma_{0}\Phi T}}=\frac{e^{\sigma_{0}\Phi T/2}(e^{\sigma(\nu)\Phi T/2}-e^{-\sigma(\nu)\Phi T/2})}{e^{\sigma(\nu)\Phi T/2}(e^{\sigma_{0}\Phi T/2}-e^{-\sigma_{0}\Phi T/2})}.

(11)

It is seen from a series expansion, including all terms linear and quadratic in
\sigma(\nu)\Phi T
and
\sigma_{0}\Phi T
, that the expression for
n_{\textrm{fl}}(\nu,T)
can be approximated by

n_{\textrm{fl}}(\nu,T)\propto N^{0}_{a}(1-e^{-\sigma_{0}\Phi T})\mathcal{L}(\nu_{0},\gamma_{\textrm{fl}})

(12)

with

\gamma_{\textrm{fl}}=\gamma\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{0}\Phi T+\frac{1}{8}(\sigma_{0}\Phi T)^{2}}.

(13)

This is a broadened Lorentzian profile that is a very good approximation for
n_{\textrm{fl}}(\nu,T)
given by Equation (
10
) as long as
\sigma_{0}\Phi T<2
.

In a typical Lamb dip experiment, there is inhomogeneous broadening as well as saturation broadening of the Lorentzian line-profile, and the absorption line is described by a convolution of these profiles. When we retroreflect a mononchromatic laser beam with flux
\Phi
, i.e. when we have two counter-propagating laser beams, each with flux
\Phi
, the total number of spontaneous fluorescence photons that is emitted when the laser beams interact with different velocity-groups of molecules, is given by two times the value of
n_{\textrm{fl}}(\nu,T)
given in Equation (
12
). When both laser beams interact with the same velocity-group of molecules, then this results in a total number of spontaneous fluorescence photons given by the value of
n_{\textrm{fl}}(\nu_{0},T)
for the doubled laser flux,
2\Phi
, which is lower. This causes the appearance of the Lamb dip. In an open two-level system, the relative depth,
D(\nu_{0})
, of the Lamb dip is thus given by

D(\nu_{0})=1-\frac{(1-e^{-2\sigma_{0}\Phi T})}{2(1-e^{-\sigma_{0}\Phi T})}=\frac{1}{2}(1-e^{-\sigma_{0}\Phi T})

(14)

The shape is Lorentzian, saturation broadened due to the excitation rate
2\sigma_{0}\Phi
, and the full spectral profile of the Lamb dip is given by

D(\nu)=\frac{(1-e^{-2\sigma_{0}\Phi T\mathcal{L}(\nu_{0},\gamma)})}{2(1+e^{-\sigma_{0}\Phi T})}\approx D(\nu_{0})\mathcal{L}(\nu_{0},\gamma_{\textrm{s}})

(15)

with

\gamma_{\textrm{s}}=\gamma\sqrt{1+\sigma_{0}\Phi T+\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{0}\Phi T)^{2}}.

(16)

The only experimental observables in a Lamb dip experiment are the relative depth of the Lamb dip (
0<D(\nu_{0})<1/2
) and its full width at half maximum
\gamma_{\textrm{s}}
. In an open two-level system, the absorption cross section
\sigma_{0}
is given by

\sigma_{0}=\frac{1}{\Phi T}\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{1}{1-2D(\nu_{0})}\right)\,\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle D(\nu_{0})\ll 1}}{{\approx}}\,\,\frac{2D(\nu_{0})}{\Phi T}

(17)

and the lifetime
\tau
is given by

\displaystyle\tau=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\gamma_{\textrm{s}}}\sqrt{1+\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{1}{1-2D(\nu_{0})}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{1}{1-2D(\nu_{0})}\right)\right)^{2}}

(18)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle D(\nu_{0})\ll 1}}{{\approx}}\,\,\frac{1+D(\nu_{0})}{2\pi\gamma_{\textrm{s}}}.

Abstract
Location based services are expected to play a major role in future generation cellular networks, starting from the incoming 5G systems. At the same time, localization technologies may be severely affected by attackers capable to deploy low cost fake base stations and use them to alter localization signals. In this paper, we concretely focus on two classes of threats: noise-like jammers, whose objective is to reduce the signal-to-noise ratio, and spoofing/meaconing attacks, whose objective is to inject false or erroneous information into the receiver. Then, we formulate the detection problems as binary hypothesis tests and solve them resorting to the generalized likelihood ratio test design procedure as well as the Latent Variable Models, which involves the expectation-maximization algorithm to estimate the unknown data distribution parameters. The proposed techniques can be applied to a large class of location data regardless the subsumed network architecture. The performance analysis is conducted over simulated data generated by using measurement models from the literature and highlights the effectiveness of the proposed approaches in detecting the aforementioned classes of attacks.

\times\left(\bm{M}_{0,1}^{-1}\bm{s}_{0,1}+\bm{M}_{1,2}^{-1}\bm{s}_{1,2}\right),

(44)

where

\displaystyle\bm{M}_{0,1}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tilde{q}_{k}^{(h-1)}(1)\bm{z}_{k}\bm{z}_{k}^{T}-\frac{1}{q^{(h-1)}(1)}\bm{s}_{0,1}\bm{s}_{0,1}^{T},

\displaystyle\bm{M}_{1,2}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tilde{q}_{k}^{(h-1)}(2)\bm{z}_{k}\bm{z}_{k}^{T}-\frac{1}{q^{(h-1)}(2)}\bm{s}_{1,2}\bm{s}_{1,2}^{T},

\displaystyle\bm{s}_{0,1}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tilde{q}_{k}^{(h-1)}(1)\bm{z}_{k},\quad\bm{s}_{1,2}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tilde{q}_{k}^{(h-1)}(2)\bm{z}_{k}.

The iterations of the EM algorithm terminate when a stopping criterion is satisfied. The latter
can be related to the maximum number of iterations (dictated by computational power)
and/or the variation of the likelihood function at each iteration.

As for the maximization under
H_{0}
, since the latter is unaltered with respect to the previous case, we can clearly
use the
MLE

\bar{\bm{m}}_{0}
and

\bar{\bm{\Sigma}}_{0}
and the final decision rule can be written as

\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\tilde{\pi}_{a}^{(h)}f_{1}(\bm{z}_{k};\tilde{\bm{m}}_{1}^{(h)},\tilde{\bm{\Sigma}}_{a}^{(h)})}{\displaystyle f_{0}(\bm{Z};\bar{\bm{m}}_{0},\bar{\bm{\Sigma}}_{0})}\mbox{$\begin{array}[]{c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle H_{1}}}{{\textstyle>}}}}{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\textstyle H_{0}}}}}\end{array}$}\eta.

(45)

The above decision rule will be referred to as LVM-based NLJ Detector (LVM-NLJ-D).

Spoofing Detection Architectures

Recall that in the presence of a spoofing attack, the decision problem to be addressed is (
3

has the following form

\frac{\displaystyle\max_{K_{0}\in\Omega_{0}}\displaystyle\max_{\bm{m}_{1}}\displaystyle\max_{\bm{m}_{2}}\displaystyle\max_{\bm{\Sigma}_{1}}f_{1}(\bm{Z};\bm{m}_{1},\bm{m}_{2},\bm{\Sigma}_{1},K_{0})}{\displaystyle\max_{\bm{m}_{0}}\displaystyle\max_{\bm{\Sigma}_{0}}f_{0}(\bm{Z};\bm{m}_{0},\bm{\Sigma}_{0})}\mbox{$\begin{array}[]{c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle H_{1}}}{{\textstyle>}}}}{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\textstyle H_{0}}}}}\end{array}$}\eta.

IV-A

GLRT for Uncorrelated Measurements

Let us assume that the covariance matrix is diagonal, namely
\bm{\Sigma}_{0}=\mbox{\rm\bf diag}\,\{\sigma_{0,1}^{2},\sigma_{0,2}^{2},\ldots,\sigma_{0,N}^{2}\}
,
then, under
H_{0}
, the likelihood can be maximized resorting to (
7
).

On the other, under
H_{1}
,
the resulting log-likelihood function has the following expression (neglecting the irrelevant constants)

\mbox{$\mathcal{L}$}_{1}(\bm{m}_{1},\bm{m}_{2},\bm{\Sigma}_{1},K_{0};\bm{Z})\approx-\frac{K}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log\sigma_{1,n}^{2}\\
-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{(z_{k,n}-{m}_{1,n})^{2}}{\sigma_{1,n}^{2}}\\
-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=K_{0}+1}^{K}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{({z}_{k,n}-{m}_{2,n})^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{1,n}},

(47)

where
m_{i,n}
,
i=1,2
and
n=1,\ldots,N
, is the
n
th entry of
\bm{m}_{i}
.
Now, since
\mbox{$\mathcal{L}$}_{1}(\bm{m}_{1},\bm{m}_{2},\bm{\Sigma}_{1},K_{0};\bm{Z})
is radially unbounded with respect to
\|\bm{m}_{i}\|
,
i=1,2
, we
can compute the stationary points by setting to zero its derivatives with respect to
m_{i,n}
,
n=1,\ldots,N
,
obtaining the following estimates

\widehat{m}_{1,n}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{K_{0}}\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}z_{k,n},\quad\widehat{m}_{2,n}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{K_{1}}\sum_{k=K_{0}+1}^{K}z_{k,n}.

(48)

As for the estimates of
\bm{\Sigma}_{1}
, let us define
\bm{\sigma}_{1}=[\sigma_{1,1},\ldots,\sigma_{1,N}]^{T}
and observe that

\displaystyle\lim_{\|\bm{\sigma}_{1}\|\rightarrow+\infty}\mbox{$\mathcal{L}$}_{1}(\bm{m}_{1},\bm{m}_{2},\bm{\Sigma}_{1},K_{0};\bm{Z})

\displaystyle=-\infty,

(49)

\displaystyle\lim_{\|\bm{\sigma}_{1}\|\rightarrow 0}\mbox{$\mathcal{L}$}_{1}(\bm{m}_{1},\bm{m}_{2},\bm{\Sigma}_{1},K_{0};\bm{Z})

\displaystyle=-\infty.

(50)

Therefore, searching the stationary points of
\mbox{$\mathcal{L}$}_{1}(\bm{m}_{1},\bm{m}_{2},\bm{\Sigma}_{1},K_{0};\bm{Z})
in the interior of its
domain leads to
6
6

6

The sign of the derivative allows to establish that the solution represents a maximum point.

\widehat{\sigma}^{2}_{1,n}=\frac{1}{K}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}(z_{k,n}-\widehat{m}_{1,n})^{2}+\sum_{k=K_{0}+1}^{K}(z_{k,n}-\widehat{m}_{2,n})^{2}\right]

(51)

and the final decision rule is given by

\displaystyle\max_{K_{0}}\Bigg\{\frac{K}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log\left[\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}(z_{k,n}-\widehat{m}_{0,n})^{2}\right]\\
-\frac{K}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log\Bigg[\frac{1}{K}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}(z_{k,n}-\widehat{m}_{1,n})^{2}\\
+\sum_{k=K_{0}+1}^{K}(z_{k,n}-\widehat{m}_{2,n})^{2}\Bigg)\Bigg]\Bigg\}\mbox{$\begin{array}[]{c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle H_{1}}}{{\textstyle>}}}}{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\textstyle H_{0}}}}}\end{array}$}\eta.

(52)

In the next sections, we will refer to this architecture as SPoofing Detector for Uncorrelated Measurements (SP-D-UM).

IV-B

GLRT for Correlated Measurements

In this case, the covariance matrix
\bm{\Sigma}_{i}
,
i=0,1
, exhibits a general symmetric structure. Thus, under
H_{0}
,
the
MLE
s of
\bm{m}_{0}
and
\bm{\Sigma}_{0}
are given by
\bar{\bm{m}}_{0}
and

\bar{\bm{\Sigma}}_{0}
, whereas, under
H_{1}
, it is not difficult to show that

\mbox{$\mathcal{L}$}_{1}(\bm{m}_{1},\bm{m}_{2},\bm{\Sigma}_{1},K_{0};\bm{Z})\leq\mbox{$\mathcal{L}$}_{1}(\bm{m}_{1},\bm{m}_{2},\bar{\bm{\Sigma}}_{1},K_{0};\bm{Z})

with

\bar{\bm{\Sigma}}_{1}(\bm{m}_{1},\bm{m}_{2})=\frac{1}{K}\Bigg[\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}(\bm{z}_{k}-\bm{m}_{1})(\bm{z}_{k}-\bm{m}_{1})^{T}\\
+\sum_{k=K_{0}+1}^{K}(\bm{z}_{k}-\bm{m}_{2})(\bm{z}_{k}-\bm{m}_{2})^{T}\Bigg].

(53)

Now, approximate the compressed log-likelihood as

\displaystyle\mbox{$\mathcal{L}$}_{1}(\bm{m}_{1},\bm{m}_{2},\bar{\bm{\Sigma}}_{1},K_{0};\bm{Z})

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{K}{2}\log\det\Bigg\{\Bigg[\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}(\bm{z}_{k}-\bm{m}_{1})(\bm{z}_{k}-\bm{m}_{1})^{T}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=K_{0}+1}^{K}(\bm{z}_{k}-\bm{m}_{2})(\bm{z}_{k}-\bm{m}_{2})^{T}\Bigg]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{K}{2}\log\det\{\bm{M}_{0}+\bm{M}_{1}+\bm{u}_{0}\bm{u}_{0}^{T}+\bm{u}_{1}\bm{u}_{1}^{T}\}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{K}{2}\log\det\{\bm{M}_{0}+\bm{M}_{1}\},

(54)

where the inequality is due to the fact that
\bm{u}_{0}\bm{u}_{0}^{T}+\bm{u}_{1}\bm{u}_{1}^{T}
is positive semidefinite
[
44
]
;
the equality holds when
\bm{u}_{0}=\bm{u}_{1}=0
, namely

\bar{\bm{m}}_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{K_{0}}\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\bm{z}_{k},\qquad\bar{\bm{m}}_{2}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{K_{1}}\sum_{k=K_{0}+1}^{K}\bm{z}_{k}.

(55)

Finally, the logarithm of the GLRT has the following form

\displaystyle\max_{K_{0}}\Bigg\{-\log\det\Bigg[\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}(\bm{z}_{k}-\bar{\bm{m}}_{1})(\bm{z}_{k}-\bar{\bm{m}}_{1})^{T}\\
+\sum_{k=K_{0}+1}^{K}(\bm{z}_{k}-\bar{\bm{m}}_{2})(\bm{z}_{k}-\bar{\bm{m}}_{2})^{T}\Bigg]\Bigg\}\\
+\log\det\left[\sum_{k=1}^{K}(\bm{z}_{k}-\bar{\bm{m}}_{0})(\bm{z}_{k}-\bar{\bm{m}}_{0})^{T}\right]\mbox{$\begin{array}[]{c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle H_{1}}}{{\textstyle>}}}}{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\textstyle H_{0}}}}}\end{array}$}\eta.

(56)

The above decision scheme is referred to in the following as SPoofing Detector for Correlated Measurements (SP-D-CM).

1
LVM
for Correlated Measurements

The
K
discrete random variables
\omega_{k}
s introduced in Subsection
III-B1
now are such that
when
\omega_{k}=a
,
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
, then
\bm{z}_{k}\sim\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}_{N}(\bm{m}_{a},\bm{\Sigma})
under
H_{1}
.
Therefore, the
PDF
of
\bm{z}_{k}
can be written exploiting the Total Probability Theorem as

f_{1}(\bm{z}_{k};\bm{\pi},\bm{m}_{1},\bm{m}_{2},\bm{\Sigma}_{1})=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\pi_{a}f_{1}(\bm{z}_{k};\bm{m}_{a},\bm{\Sigma}).

(57)

The estimates of the unknown parameters are obtained by applying the
EM
algorithm and suitably leveraging the results of
Subsection
III-B1
. Specifically, the E-step is accomplished by noticing that

\log f_{1}(\bm{Z};\bm{\pi},\bm{m}_{1},\bm{m}_{2},\bm{\Sigma}_{1})\\
\geq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\log\left[\frac{\pi_{a}f_{1}(\bm{z}_{k};\bm{m}_{a},\bm{\Sigma}_{1})}{q_{k}(a)}\right]q_{k}(a),

(58)

where the equality holds if

q_{k}(a)=\frac{\pi_{a}f_{1}(\bm{z}_{k};\bm{m}_{a},\bm{\Sigma}_{1})}{\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{A}}}\pi_{m}f_{1}(\bm{z}_{k};\bm{m}_{a},\bm{\Sigma}_{1})}.

(59)

It follows that

\tilde{q}^{(h-1)}_{k}(a)=\frac{\tilde{{\pi}}_{a}^{(h-1)}f_{1}(\bm{z}_{k};\tilde{\bm{m}}_{a}^{(h-1)},\tilde{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(h-1)}_{1})}{\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{A}}}\tilde{{\pi}}_{m}^{(h-1)}f_{1}(\bm{z}_{k};\tilde{\bm{m}}_{a}^{(h-1)},\tilde{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(h-1)}_{1})},

(60)

\tilde{\bm{\pi}}^{(h-1)}
,
\tilde{\bm{m}}_{1}^{(h-1)}
,
\tilde{\bm{m}}_{2}^{(h-1)}
,
and
\tilde{\bm{\Sigma}}_{1}^{(h-1)}
are the available estimates at the
(h-1)
th step.

As for the M-step, it leads to the following problem

\displaystyle\displaystyle\max_{\bm{\pi}}\displaystyle\max_{\bm{m}_{1}}\displaystyle\max_{\bm{m}_{2}}\displaystyle\max_{\bm{\Sigma}_{1}}\Bigg\{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\tilde{q}^{(h-1)}_{k}(a)\log({f_{1}(\bm{z}_{k};\bm{m}_{a},\bm{\Sigma}_{1})})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\tilde{q}^{(h-1)}_{k}(a)\log(\pi_{a})\Bigg\}.

(61)

The maximization over
\bm{\pi}
is the same as in Subsection
III-B1
and the estimate update is given by
(
39
). In order to complete the M-step, we have to solve

\displaystyle\max_{\bm{m}_{a}\atop a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\displaystyle\max_{\bm{\Sigma}_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\tilde{q}^{(h-1)}_{k}(a)\log({f_{1}(\bm{z}_{k};\bm{m}_{a},\bm{\Sigma}_{1})}).

(62)

To this end, notice that the objective function can be recast as

\frac{K}{2}\Bigg\{\log\det[\bm{\Sigma}_{1}^{-1}]-\mbox{\rm Tr}\,\Bigg[\bm{\Sigma}_{1}^{-1}\\
\times\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\tilde{q}^{(h-1)}_{k}(a)(\bm{z}_{k}-\bm{m}_{a})(\bm{z}_{k}-\bm{m}_{a})^{T}\Bigg]\Bigg\},

(63)

and, hence, the maximum with respect to
\bm{\Sigma}_{1}
is attained at

\tilde{\bm{\Sigma}}_{1}^{(h)}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\tilde{q}^{(h-1)}_{k}(a)(\bm{z}_{k}-\bm{m}_{a})(\bm{z}_{k}-\bm{m}_{a})^{T}.

(64)

Therefore, the last optimization problem is given by

\displaystyle\max_{\bm{m}_{a}\atop a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\Bigg\{-\frac{K}{2}\log\det\Bigg[\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\tilde{q}^{(h-1)}_{k}(a)\\
\times(\bm{z}_{k}-\bm{m}_{a})(\bm{z}_{k}-\bm{m}_{a})^{T}\Bigg]\Bigg\},

(65)

which can be solved by exploiting the procedure used to obtain (
55
).
As a consequence, the maximum is attained at

\tilde{\bm{m}}_{a}^{(h)}=\frac{1}{q^{(h-1)}(a)}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tilde{q}^{(h-1)}_{k}(a)\bm{z}_{k}.

(66)

Gathering the above results, we can write the modified GLRT as

\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\tilde{\pi}_{a}^{(h)}f_{1}(\bm{z}_{k};\tilde{\bm{m}}_{a}^{(h)},\tilde{\bm{\Sigma}}_{1}^{(h)})}{\displaystyle f_{0}(\bm{Z};\bar{\bm{m}}_{0},\bar{\bm{\Sigma}}_{0})}\mbox{$\begin{array}[]{c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle H_{1}}}{{\textstyle>}}}}{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle<}}{{\textstyle H_{0}}}}}\end{array}$}\eta.

(67)

Finally, we will refer to this decision rule as LVM-based SPoofing Detector (LVM-SP-D).

VI
Conclusions

In this paper we have addressed the problem of detecting an attack to the location services provided by the next generation
communication networks. The proposed strategies have been conceived for high-level location data in agreement with 5G standardization work. At the design stage, this problem has been formulated as a binary hypothesis
test accounting for possible correlation among the measurements and solved resorting to GLRT-based design procedures as well as the
LVM
.
The analysis conducted on simulated data has singled out the NLJ-D-CM and the SP-D-CM as an effective means to cope with NLJ and spoofing
attacks since they represent a good compromise between detection performance and capability of operating without any assumption on
data correlation.

Future research tracks might include the development of an architecture capable of classifying and detecting which kind of attack
is in course also accounting for more sophisticated jamming attackers.

Fixed Point Theorem: Variants, Affine Context and Some Consequences
Anderson L. A. de Araujo, Edir J. F. Leite

Abstract




In this work, we will present variants Fixed Point Theorem for the affine and classical contexts, as a consequence of general Brouwer’s Fixed Point Theorem. For instance, the affine results will allow working on affine balls, which are defined through the affine
\displaystyle L^{p}
functional
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}^{p}
introduced by Lutwak, Yang and Zhang in the work
Sharp affine
\displaystyle L_{p}
Sobolev inequalities
, J. Differential Geom. 62 (2002), 17-38 for
\displaystyle p>1
that is non convex and does not represent a norm in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Moreover, we address results for discontinuous functional at a point. As an application, we study critical points of the sequence of affine functionals
\displaystyle\Phi_{m}
on a subspace
\displaystyle W_{m}
of dimension
\displaystyle m
given by

\Phi_{m}(u)=\frac{1}{p}\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}^{p}(u)-\frac{1}{\alpha}\|u\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\alpha}(\Omega)}-\int_{\Omega}f(x)udx,

where
\displaystyle 1<\alpha<p
,
\displaystyle[W_{m}]_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is dense in
\displaystyle W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\displaystyle f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, with
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.

Keywords:

Fixed Point Theorems; Affine Balls; Affine
\displaystyle L^{p}
Energy; Nontrivial Critical Point

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

Lemma 2.1

Let
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{N}
. The affine
\displaystyle L^{p}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}

\displaystyle W_{m}

Let
\displaystyle u_{k}\rightarrow u

\displaystyle W_{m}

\displaystyle u=0

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}(u_{k})\rightarrow 0=\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}(0)

\displaystyle u\neq 0

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}(u)>0

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\xi}u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\rightarrow\|\nabla_{\xi}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\xi}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}>c_{1}

The following lemma will be important to apply the general Brouwer’s Fixed Point Theorem. This theorem states that if
\displaystyle E
is a bounded and closed subset in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{m}
and homeomorphic to the closed unit ball, then any continuous mapping
\displaystyle S:E\rightarrow E

Lemma 2.2
.

The affine ball
\displaystyle\overline{B}^{m}_{\varrho}(\zeta_{0})=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{m};\lfloor z-\zeta_{0}\rfloor_{m}\leq\varrho\}
and
\displaystyle\overline{B}^{p,m}_{1}(0)=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{m};\|z\|_{1,p,m}\leq 1\}
are homeomorphic in
\displaystyle(\mathbb{R}^{m},\|\cdot\|_{1,p,m})
.

Proof.

Let us consider the applications
\displaystyle T:\overline{B}^{m}_{\varrho}(\zeta_{0})\to\overline{B}^{p,m}_{1}(0)

T(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{x-\zeta_{0}}{\varrho}\frac{\lfloor x-\zeta_{0}\rfloor_{m}}{\|x-\zeta_{0}\|_{1,p,m}}&\mbox{ if }&x\neq\zeta_{0},\\
0&\mbox{ if }&x=\zeta_{0}\end{array}\right.

and
\displaystyle G:\overline{B}^{p,m}_{1}(0)\to\overline{B}^{m}_{\varrho}(\zeta_{0})
defined by

By standard computations we obtain that
\displaystyle G=T^{-1}
, that is,
\displaystyle T\circ G=G\circ T=Id
. By the continuity of the functional
\displaystyle u\in W_{m}\mapsto\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}(u)
follows the continuity of the function
\displaystyle T
in
\displaystyle x\neq\zeta_{0}
and
\displaystyle G
in
\displaystyle x\neq 0
. Now,
\displaystyle T
is continuous in
\displaystyle x=\zeta_{0}
since applying inequality (

\|T(x)-T(\zeta_{0})\|_{1,p,m}=\left\|\frac{x-\zeta_{0}}{\varrho}\frac{\lfloor x-\zeta_{0}\rfloor_{m}}{\|x-\zeta_{0}\|_{1,p,m}}\right\|_{1,p,m}=\frac{1}{\varrho}\lfloor x-\zeta_{0}\rfloor_{m}\leq\frac{1}{\varrho}\|x-\zeta_{0}\|_{1,p,m}.

To proof the continuity of
\displaystyle G

\displaystyle x=0

\displaystyle(x_{k})
in
\displaystyle\overline{B}^{p,m}_{1}(0)

\displaystyle x_{k}\to 0

Hence, by continuity of

\lim_{j\to\infty}x_{k_{j}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}T\circ G(x_{k_{j}})=T(\theta)\neq 0

\displaystyle x_{k}\to 0

Before proving our application, we will show the following continuity result. Namely:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle A:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a function such that

A(\zeta)=(A_{1}(\zeta),A_{2}(\zeta),\dots,A_{m}(\zeta)),

where
\displaystyle\zeta=(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},...,\zeta_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}A_{j}(\zeta)=\int_{\Omega}H_{u}^{p-1}(\nabla u)\nabla H_{u}(\nabla u)\cdot\nabla w_{j}\;dx\end{array}

\displaystyle j=1,2,\dots,m
, and
\displaystyle u=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\zeta_{i}w_{i}\in W_{m}
. Then, the function
\displaystyle A
is continuous.

Proof.

For this, let
\displaystyle\zeta^{k}\rightarrow\zeta^{0}
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\displaystyle u_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\zeta^{k}_{i}w_{i}\in W_{m}
,
\displaystyle k=0,1,2,\dots
. We fixed
\displaystyle j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
. Note that

A_{j}(\zeta^{k})=\gamma_{n,p}^{-n}\;\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}^{n+p}(u_{k})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\|\nabla_{\xi}u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{-n-p}\int_{\Omega}\{\nabla_{\xi}u_{k}\}^{p-1}\langle\nabla w_{j},\xi\rangle dxd\xi.

If
\displaystyle\zeta^{0}=0
, then by inequality (
4
), we have
\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\xi}u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
uniform on
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Note that, using inequality (
5
), there exists
\displaystyle D_{0}>0
independent of
\displaystyle\xi
such that

|A_{j}(\zeta^{k})|\leq\gamma_{n,p}^{-n}\;D_{0}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\Omega}|\langle\nabla u_{k},\xi\rangle|^{p-1}|\langle\nabla w_{j},\xi\rangle|dxd\xi.

Now, by Hölder and Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities and dominated convergence theorem, we have

|A_{j}(\zeta^{k})|\leq\gamma_{n,p}^{-n}\;D_{0}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\nabla w_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\|\nabla_{\xi}u_{k}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}d\xi\rightarrow 0=A_{j}(\zeta^{0}).

If
\displaystyle\zeta^{0}\neq 0
, then by Lemma
2.1
, we have
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}^{n+p}(u_{k})\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}^{n+p}(u)
. Finally, using the same arguments used in the proof of Lemma
2.1
and in the proof of the previous case, we have
\displaystyle A_{j}(\zeta^{k})\rightarrow A_{j}(\zeta^{0})
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
\displaystyle f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
with
\displaystyle f\neq 0
in
\displaystyle\Omega
,
\displaystyle 1<\alpha<p
. Let
\displaystyle\Phi_{m}
be the functional defined in (
6
). Then there exists a nontrivial solution
\displaystyle u\in W_{m}
satisfying
\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}_{m}(u)=0
.

Proof.

Define the function
\displaystyle F:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

F(\zeta)=(F_{1}(\zeta),F_{2}(\zeta),\dots,F_{m}(\zeta)),

where
\displaystyle\zeta=(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},...,\zeta_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}F_{j}(\zeta)&=&\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}H_{u}^{p-1}\left(\nabla u\right)\nabla H_{u}\left(\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla w_{j}dx\\
&&-\int_{\Omega}|u|^{\alpha-2}uw_{j}dx-\int_{\Omega}f(x)w_{j}dx,\end{array}

\displaystyle j=1,2,\dots,m
, and
\displaystyle u=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\zeta_{i}w_{i}\in W_{m}
.

By Proposition
3.1
, we have
\displaystyle F
is a continuous function. Therefore, by Hölder’s inequality, we get

\begin{array}[]{lll}\left\langle F(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle&=&\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}^{p}(u)-\|u\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\alpha}(\Omega)}-\int_{\Omega}f(x)udx\\
&\geq&\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}^{p}(u)-\|u\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\alpha}(\Omega)}-\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\end{array}

where
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{p}=1
. By affine Poincaré-Sobolev inequality (see inequality (
2
)), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lll}\left\langle F(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle&\geq&\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}^{p}(u)-\left(\mu_{p,\alpha}^{\cal A}\right)^{-\alpha}\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}^{\alpha}(u)-\left(\mu_{p,p}^{\cal A}\right)^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}(u).\end{array}

Now, with the notations of Theorem
1.1
, let
\displaystyle\lfloor\zeta\rfloor_{m}=\varrho
for some
\displaystyle\varrho>0
to be taken below. Thus, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\left\langle F(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle&\geq&\displaystyle\varrho^{p}-\left(\mu_{p,\alpha}^{\cal A}\right)^{-\alpha}\varrho^{\alpha}-\left(\mu_{p,p}^{\cal A}\right)^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\varrho.\end{array}

If
\displaystyle\varrho
is such that

\varrho>\max\left\{\left[2\left(\mu_{p,p}^{\cal A}\right)^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}},\left[2\left(\mu_{p,\alpha}^{\cal A}\right)^{-\alpha}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-\alpha}}\right\}

then

\left\langle F(\zeta),\zeta\right\rangle>0.

We take

\varrho:=\max\left\{\left[2\left(\mu_{p,p}^{\cal A}\right)^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}},\left[2\left(\mu_{p,\alpha}^{\cal A}\right)^{-\alpha}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-\alpha}}\right\}+1.

Thus, Theorem
1.1
ensure the existence of
\displaystyle z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\displaystyle\lfloor y\rfloor_{m}\leq\varrho
and such that
\displaystyle F(z_{0})=0
. In other words, there exists
\displaystyle u_{m}\in W_{m}
verifying

\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}(u_{m})\leq\varrho,

and such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}H_{u_{m}}^{p-1}\left(\nabla u_{m}\right)\nabla H_{u_{m}}\left(\nabla u_{m}\right)\cdot\nabla wdx=\int_{\Omega}|u_{m}|^{\alpha-2}u_{m}wdx+\int_{\Omega}f(x)wdx

\displaystyle u_{m}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{p,\Omega}^{p}(u_{m})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|u_{m}\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\alpha}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}f(x)u_{m}dx.

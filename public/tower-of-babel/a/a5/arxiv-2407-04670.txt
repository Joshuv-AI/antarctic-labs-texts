Neutrinoless Double Beta Decay from Scalar Leptoquarks: Interplay with Neutrino Mass and Flavor Physics
P. S. Bhupal Dev, Srubabati Goswami, Chayan Majumdar, Debashis Pachhar

Abstract
We perform a comprehensive analysis of neutrinoless double beta ( 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta ) decay and its interplay with low-energy flavor observables in a radiative neutrino mass model with scalar leptoquarks S 1 ​ ( 3 ¯ , 1 , 1 / 3 ) S_{1}(\overline{3},1,1/3) and R ~ 2 ​ ( 3 , 2 , 1 / 6 ) \widetilde{R}_{2}(3,2,1/6) . We carve out the parameter region consistent with constraints from neutrino mass and mixing, collider searches, as well as measurements of several flavor observables, such as muon and electron anomalous magnetic moments, charged lepton flavor violation and rare (semi)leptonic kaon and B B -meson decays, including the recent anomalies in R D ( ∗ ) R_{D^{(*)}} and B → K ​ ν ​ ν ¯ B\to K\nu\overline{\nu} observables. We perform a global analysis to all the existing constraints and show the (anti)correlations between all relevant Yukawa couplings satisfying these restrictions. We find that the most stringent constraint on the parameter space comes from μ → e \mu\to e conversion in nuclei and K + → π + ​ ν ​ ν ¯ K^{+}\rightarrow\,\pi^{+}\nu\overline{\nu} decay. We also point out a tension between the muon and electron ( g − 2 ) (g-2) anomalies in this context. Taking benchmark values from the combined allowed regions, we study the implications for 0 ​ ν ​ β ​ β 0\nu\beta\beta decay including both the canonical light neutrino and the leptoquark contributions. We find that for normal ordering of neutrino masses, the leptoquark contribution removes the cancellation region that occurs for the canonical case. The effective mass in presence of leptoquarks can lie in the desert region between the standard normal and inverted ordering cases, and this can be probed in future ton-scale experiments like LEGEND-1000 and nEXO.

Introduction

The Standard Model (SM) of elementary particle physics successfully describes the short-distance physics probed so far in collider experiments up to the TeV scale. Nevertheless, there exist several empirical observations, as well as theoretical arguments, for the existence of some beyond-the-SM (BSM) physics. Perhaps the strongest piece of evidence comes from the discovery of neutrino oscillations
[
1

2
]

B-L

A simple way to generate nonzero neutrino mass is by breaking the
B-L
symmetry of the SM via higher-dimensional non-renormalizable operators, suppressed by a high energy scale that characterizes lepton number violation (LNV). At dimension five, there is only one such operator, namely, the Weinberg operator

3

The Model

The model we consider consists of the SM particle content augmented with two scalar leptoquarks

\displaystyle S_{1}(\overline{3},1,1/3)\equiv S_{1}^{1/3},\qquad\widetilde{R}_{2}(3,2,1/6)\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{R}_{2}^{+2/3}\\

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{\rm LQ}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle Y_{1}^{L}\overline{Q}_{L}^{c}\epsilon\ell_{L}S_{1}+Y_{1}^{R}\overline{u}_{R}^{c}e_{R}S_{1}+Y_{2}\overline{d}_{R}\widetilde{R}_{2}^{T}\epsilon\ell_{L}+\text{H.c.}

\displaystyle Y_{1}^{L}\,\overline{u}_{L}^{c}\,e_{L}\,S_{1}-Y_{1}^{L}\,\overline{d}_{L}^{c}\,\nu_{L}\,S_{1}+Y_{1}^{R}\,\overline{u}_{R}^{c}\,e_{R}\,S_{1}+Y_{2}\,\overline{d}_{R}\,\widetilde{R}_{2}^{2/3}\,e_{L}-Y_{2}\,\overline{d}_{R}\,\widetilde{R}_{2}^{-1/3}\,\nu_{L}+\text{H.c.}

In Fig.

On the other hand, diagrams (c) and (d), being dependent on the neutrino momentum, give important contribution to

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\,\epsilon_{S+P}^{S+P},

(15)

corresponding to the

\displaystyle\left(T^{0\nu}_{1/2}\right)_{\rm total}^{-1}

V.1.1

In this model, leptoquark can mediate

V.1.4
\ell_{\alpha}\to\ell_{\beta}\ell_{\gamma}\ell_{\delta}

The four-lepton LFV amplitude is mediated by penguin and box diagrams involving leptoquarks and quarks. The amplitude typically scales as
y^{2}/m_{\rm LQ}^{2}
for the penguin diagrams and as
y^{4}/m_{\rm LQ}^{2}
for the box diagrams, where
y
is the relevant leptoquark coupling. As the Yukawa couplings involved in this processes are small due to the constraints from cLFVs, the bounds coming from the box diagrams turn out to be subdominant to those from the penguins. However, typically the photon penguin contribution to
\ell_{\alpha}\to\ell_{\beta}\ell_{\gamma}\ell_{\delta}
is dominant over the
Z
penguins. Now, as the leptoquark couples to both left and the right chiral fermions, the dipole contributions are dominant for
\ell_{\alpha}\to\ell_{\beta}\ell_{\gamma}\ell_{\delta}
. The branching ratio of
\ell_{\alpha}\to 3\ell_{\beta}
decay is found as
[
120
,
121
]

\displaystyle\frac{BR\left(\ell_{\alpha}\to 3\,\ell_{\beta}\right)}{BR\left(l_{\alpha}\rightarrow\,l_{\beta}\,\gamma\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}}{3\pi}\left[\ln\left(\frac{m_{\alpha}}{m_{\beta}}\right)-\frac{11}{4}\right]\,.

(47)

This is to be compared with the current limis:

{\rm BR}\left(\mu\rightarrow\,3\,e\right)<1.0\times 10^{-12}

SINDRUM
[
122
]
,

{\rm BR}\left(\tau\rightarrow\,3\,\mu\right)<2.1\times 10^{-8}

Belle
[
123
]
,

{\rm BR}\left(\tau\rightarrow\,3\,e\,\right)<2.7\times 10^{-8}

Belle
[
123
]
.

We find that the corresponding limits in the leptoquarks are weaker than those from
\mu\to e
conversion in nuclei. Therefore, we do not further discuss the results for
\ell_{\alpha}\to 3\,\ell_{\beta}
type processes here.

\displaystyle\qquad+q^{\mu}\,\left\{F_{3}^{V}(q^{2})+F_{3}^{A}(q^{2})\,\gamma_{5}\,\right\}\Big]\,,

where
q
is the momentum of the outgoing photon,
F_{1}^{V},F_{1}^{A},F_{2}^{V},F_{2}^{A}
are the electric charge, anapole, magnetic dipole and electric dipole form factors, respectively. The MDM and EDM of the neutrino are then given as,

\displaystyle\mu^{ij}_{\nu}

\displaystyle=e\,\frac{F_{2}^{V}(0)}{2\,m_{\nu}}\,,\qquad\qquad d^{ij}_{\nu}=-i\,e\,\frac{F_{2}^{A}(0)}{2\,m_{\nu}}\,.

(49)

If the neutrinos are majorana, the dipole moments are anti-symmetric which means only the transition elements i.e off-diagonal elements are non-vanishing.

Stringent limits on the neutrino MDM come from the brightness measurement of the tip of the red giant branch in
\omega
-Centauri globular cluster using Gaia DR2 data. Ref.
[
129
]
quotes this limit as
\mu_{\nu}<1.2\times 10^{-12}\,\mu_{B}
, where
\mu_{B}=\frac{e}{2\,m_{e}}
is the Bohr magneton. As far as the neutrino EDM is concerned, no experimental limit exists yet, but several theoretical and indirect bounds have been obtained. The most stringent limit on electron and muon neutrino EDM is quoted as
d_{\nu_{e},\nu_{\mu}}<10^{-21}e
-cm
[
130
]
whereas, for the tau neutrino,
d_{\nu_{\tau}}<10^{-17}e
-cm
[
131
]
.

In our leptoquark model, there are two 1-loop diagrams, similar to Fig.
1
, that contribute to MDM and EDM, depending on whether the photon radiates off either from the scalar leptoquark with charge 1/3 or from the down-type quark.
If the photon is emitted from the leptoquark, the amplitude can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal{M}}^{ij}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\,e\int\frac{d^{4}\,k}{\left(2\pi\right)^{4}}\,\frac{\left[\overline{u}(q_{2})\,P_{L}\,\left(\not{k}+m_{d_{m}}\right)\,P_{L}\,u(q_{1})\,\left(q_{1}^{\mu}+q_{2}^{\mu}\right)\right]}{\left(k^{2}-m_{d_{m}}^{2}\right)\,\left((q_{1}-k)^{2}-m_{X_{i}}^{2}\right)\,\left((q_{2}-k)^{2}-m_{X_{i}}^{2}\right)}\,

(50)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\alpha,\beta,m}\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}\right)_{m\alpha}\,\left(Y_{2}\right)_{m\beta}\,U_{j\alpha}\,U_{i\beta}\,,

where
i,j
denote the initial and final neutrino state,
k
is the loop momentum,
q_{1}
,
q_{2}
are the momenta of external neutrinos,
m_{d_{m}}
denotes the mass of down-type quark with flavor
m
, and
\left(\alpha,\,\beta\right)
run over the flavors of neutrinos, i.e.
\left\{e,\mu,\tau\right\}
.
Similarly, the amplitude of the diagram where the photon is emitted from the down-quark leg can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal{M}}^{ij}_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ie\int\frac{d^{4}\,k}{\left(2\pi\right)^{4}}\frac{\left[\overline{u}(q_{2})\,P_{L}\,\left(\not{q_{2}}-\not{k}+m_{d_{m}}\right)\gamma^{\mu}\,\left(\not{q_{1}}-\not{k}+m_{d_{m}}\right)\,P_{L}\,u(q_{1})\right]}{\left(k^{2}-m_{X_{i}}^{2}\right)\,\left((q_{1}-k)^{2}-m_{d_{m}}^{2}\right)\,\left((q_{2}-k)^{2}-m_{d_{m}}^{2}\right)}

(51)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\alpha,\beta,m}\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}\right)_{m\alpha}\left(Y_{2}\right)_{m\beta}\,U_{j\alpha}\,U_{i\beta}\,.

Note that the electromagnetic charge of
1/3
is canceled by the color factor of 3 in both Eqs. (
50
) and (
51
).

From the total amplitude,
{\cal{M}}={\cal M}_{a}+{\cal M}_{b}
, we identify the dominant contribution to the MDM as

\displaystyle\mu_{\nu}^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m_{b}\,m_{e}}{32\,\pi^{2}}\,\sin\left(2\theta_{\rm LQ}\right)\,L(x_{b})\,\left(\frac{1}{m_{X_{1}}^{2}}-\frac{1}{m_{X_{2}}^{2}}\right)\,{\rm Im}\left[\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}\right)_{3\alpha}\left(Y_{2}\right)_{3\beta}\,\left(\,U_{i\beta}\,U^{\ast}_{j\alpha}-U^{\ast}_{i\alpha}\,U_{j\beta}\right)\right]\,,

where,
x_{b}=m_{b}^{2}/m_{\rm LQ}^{2}
. Note that as MDM depends on the mass of the down-type quark, the dominant contribution comes from the bottom quark. The loop function
L(x)
is defined as

\displaystyle L(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\log x}{\left(1-x\right)^{2}}+\frac{1}{\left(1-x\right)}\,.

(53)

Similarly, the dominant contribution to the EDM can be identified as

\displaystyle d_{\nu}^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{m_{b}\,\sin\left(2\theta_{\rm LQ}\right)}{32\,\pi^{2}}\,L(x_{b})\left(\frac{1}{m_{X_{1}}^{2}}-\frac{1}{m_{X_{2}}^{2}}\right){\rm Re}\,\left[\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}\right)_{3\alpha}\left(Y_{2}\right)_{3\beta}\left(U_{i\beta}U^{\ast}_{j\alpha}-U^{\ast}_{i\alpha}U_{j\beta}\right)\right].

(54)

Assuming
{\cal O}(1)
Yukawa coupling, we can express both MDM and EDM in terms of neutrino masses, as follows:

\displaystyle\mu^{ij}_{\nu}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 5\times 10^{-20}\,\mu_{B}\,\left(\frac{m_{\nu}}{0.01\,{\rm eV}}\right)\left(\frac{{\rm TeV}}{m_{\rm LQ}}\right)^{2}\,\,,\quad\left|d^{ij}_{\nu}\right|\approx 2\times 10^{-30}e\textrm{-cm}\,\left(\frac{m_{\nu}}{0.01\,{\rm eV}}\right)\left(\frac{{\rm TeV}}{m_{\rm LQ}}\right)^{2}.

(55)

This implies that for TeV-scale LQs, both MDM and EDM of neutrinos are far below the current experimental limits.

We should also comment here that there are no leptoquark contributions to the electron, proton, or neutron EDM in our model, if we assume the leptoquark Yukawa couplings to be real
[
132
,
133
]
.

V.3
Rare Meson Decays

Here we discuss the leptoquark contributions to the (semi)leptonic rare decays of
B
and
K
mesons. We will consider the neutral-current
B
decays involving
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
and charged-current
B
decays involving
b\to c\ell\overline{\nu}
, in particular the LFUV observables parameterized in terms of the ratios of BRs,

\displaystyle R_{D^{(*)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm BR}(\overline{B}\to D^{(*)}\tau\overline{\nu})}{{\rm BR}(\overline{B}\to D^{(*)}\ell\overline{\nu})}\quad{\rm with}~\ell=e,\mu,

(56)

\displaystyle R_{K}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm BR}(B^{+}\to K^{+}\mu^{+}\mu^{-})}{{\rm BR}(B^{+}\to K^{+}e^{+}e^{-})},

(57)

\displaystyle R_{K^{*}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm BR}(B^{0}\to K^{*0}\mu^{+}\mu^{-})}{{\rm BR}(B^{0}\to K^{*0}e^{+}e^{-})},

(58)

which are theoretically clean observables with strongly suppressed hadronic and CKM-angle uncertainties. We also consider other rare decays such as
B\to K\nu\overline{\nu}
,
K^{+}\to\pi^{+}\nu\overline{\nu}
, and the rare kaon decays
K_{L}\to\mu^{\pm}e^{\mp}
and
K^{+}\to\pi^{+}\mu^{\pm}e^{\mp}
.

V.3.1
R_{K}\text{ and }R_{K^{*}}

The relevant effective Hamiltonian for the process can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal{H}_{\rm eff}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4\,G_{F}\,}{\sqrt{2}}\,\lambda_{t}^{bs}\,\left[\sum_{X=9,10}\left(C_{X}^{ll}\,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{ll}+C_{X^{\prime}}^{ll}\,\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}^{ll}\right)\right]\,,

(59)

where
\lambda^{bs}_{q}=V_{qb}\,V_{qs}^{\ast}
,
C_{X^{(^{\prime})}}
is the Wilson coefficients corresponding to the operator
\mathcal{O}_{X^{(^{\prime})}}
, defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{9}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}}{4\pi}\left(\overline{s}\,\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\,b\right)\,\left(\overline{l}\,\gamma_{\mu}\,l\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{10}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}}{4\pi}\left(\overline{s}\,\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\,b\right)\,\left(\overline{l}\,\gamma_{\mu}\,\gamma_{5}\,l\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{9^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}}{4\pi}\left(\overline{s}\,\gamma^{\mu}P_{R}\,b\right)\,\left(\overline{l}\,\gamma_{\mu}\,l\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{10^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}}{4\pi}\left(\overline{s}\,\gamma^{\mu}P_{R}\,b\right)\,\left(\overline{l}\,\gamma_{\mu}\,\gamma_{5}\,l\right)\,.

(60)

At tree level,
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}
operators can be generated by the mediation of
\tilde{R}_{2}^{2/3}
and
\mathcal{O}_{X}
operators are generated at 1-loop from the EW penguin and box diagrams where
S_{1}
leptoquark is circulating inside the loop
[
134
]
. When
y_{1}^{L}
Yukawa couplings are
\mathcal{O}(1)
,
y_{2}
needs to be very small to generate a small neutrino mass for TeV scale LQ mass. In this case, the EW penguin and box contributions are greater than the tree-level contribution.
At the tree-level, the Wilson coefficients are

\displaystyle\delta C^{\prime}_{9}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\delta C^{\prime}_{10}=\frac{\pi\,v^{2}}{\alpha_{\rm em}\,V_{tb}\,V^{*}_{ts}}\,\frac{\left(Y_{2}\right)_{s\ell}\left(Y_{2}^{*}\right)_{b\ell}}{2\,m^{2}_{\widetilde{R}_{2}}}.

(61)

For the penguin and box diagrams, the NP Wilson coefficients are given by

\displaystyle\delta C^{\rm penguin}_{9\,ll}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v^{2}}{16\,\lambda_{t}^{bs}}\sum_{i=1,3}\,\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}\right)_{2i}\,\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}^{*}\right)_{3i}\,\left(\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{m_{X_{1}}^{2}}+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{m_{X_{2}}^{2}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\delta C^{\rm box}_{9\,ll}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v^{2}}{64\,\pi\,\alpha_{\rm em}\,\lambda_{t}^{bs}}\sum_{i=1,3}\,\sum_{j=u,c,t}\,\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}\right)_{2i}\,\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}^{*}\right)_{3i}\,\left(\left|(Y_{1}^{L})_{jl}\right|^{2}+\left|(Y_{1}^{R})_{jl}\right|^{2}\right)\,\left(\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{m_{X_{1}}^{2}}+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{m_{X_{2}}^{2}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\delta C^{\rm box}_{10\,ll}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v^{2}}{64\,\pi\,\alpha_{\rm em}\,\lambda_{t}^{bs}}\sum_{i=1,3}\,\sum_{j=u,c,t}\,\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}\right)_{2i}\,\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}^{*}\right)_{3i}\,\left(\left|(Y_{1}^{L})_{jl}\right|^{2}-\left|(Y_{1}^{R})_{jl}\right|^{2}\right)\,\left(\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{m_{X_{1}}^{2}}+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{m_{X_{2}}^{2}}\right).

The LHCb experiment recently presented new measurements of the
R_{K^{(*)}}
ratios which turned out to be compatible with the SM
[
76
,
77
]
. From a recent global analysis using all available
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
data
[
135
]
, it was found that the primed operators
{\cal O}^{\prime}_{9}
and
{\cal O}^{\prime}_{10}
(with right chiral quark currents) are loosely constrained with the best-fit values

\displaystyle\delta C_{9}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1.18\pm 0.19\,\qquad\delta C_{10}=0.23\pm 0.20\,

\displaystyle\delta C_{9^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.06\pm 0.31\,\qquad\delta C_{10^{\prime}}=-0.05\pm 0.19\,

(63)

Therefore,
R_{K^{(*)}}
does not impose a stringent constraint on our leptoquark model parameter space.

V.3.2
R_{D}\text{ and }R_{D^{*}}

Both
S_{1}
and
\widetilde{R}_{2}
leptoquarks contribute to
R_{D^{(*)}}
. The effective Hamiltonian can be written as
[
136
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\,G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{cb}\left[\left(\delta_{\ell\tau}+C_{V_{L}}\right)\,{\cal O}_{V_{L}}+C_{S_{L}}\,{\cal O}_{S_{L}}+C_{T_{L}}\,{\cal O}_{T_{L}}\right],

(64)

where the operators are defined as

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{V_{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\overline{c}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}b\right)\left(\overline{\ell}\gamma_{\mu}P_{L}\nu\right),

(65)

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{S_{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\overline{c}P_{L}\,b\right)\left(\overline{\ell}\,P_{L}\,\nu\right),

(66)

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{T_{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\overline{c}\,\sigma^{\mu\nu}\,P_{L}\,b\right)\left(\overline{\ell}\,\sigma_{\mu\nu}\,P_{L}\,\nu\right),

(67)

and the
\delta_{\ell\tau}
term denotes the SM-dominant contribution for
b\rightarrow c\tau^{-}\overline{\nu}
. The Wilson coefficients can be found as

\displaystyle C_{V_{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v^{2}}{V_{cb}}\,\frac{\left(Y_{1}^{L}\right)^{\ast}_{c\ell}\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}\right)_{b\ell}}{4}\,\left(\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{\rm LQ}}{m_{X_{1}}^{2}}+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta_{\rm LQ}}{m_{X_{2}}^{2}}\right),

(68)

\displaystyle C_{S_{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v^{2}}{V_{cb}}\,\frac{\left(Y_{1}^{R}\right)^{\ast}_{c\ell}\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}\right)_{b\ell}}{4}\,\left(\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{\rm LQ}}{m_{X_{1}}^{2}}+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta_{\rm LQ}}{m_{X_{2}}^{2}}\right),

(69)

\displaystyle C_{T_{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{C_{S_{L}}}{4}.

(70)

The Wilson coefficients, mentioned above are extracted at
\mu=\mu_{\rm ew}
. To compare with the experimental observables, we need to run them down to
\mu=m_{b}=4.18
GeV using the renormalization group equations, i.e.

\displaystyle C_{i}(\mu=m_{b})\,=\,\Omega_{N}(\mu,\mu_{\mathrm{ew}})\;C_{i}(\mu_{\mathrm{ew}})\,,

(71)

where at the lowest order (leading logarithm), the evolution operator is given by
[
137
,
138
,
139
,
140
]

\displaystyle\Omega_{N}(\mu,\mu_{\mathrm{ew}})\,=\,\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}^{(5)}(m_{b})}{\alpha^{(5)}_{s}(m_{t})}\right)^{-\gamma^{J}_{1}/\beta_{1}^{(5)}}\,

(72)

with QCD running
\beta
-function,
\beta_{1}^{(n_{f})}=(2n_{f}-33)/6
, where
n_{f}
is the relevant number of quark flavors at the hadronic scale. The anomalous dimensions for the currents are

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}^{V}=0\,,\qquad\qquad\gamma_{1}^{S}=2\,,\qquad\qquad\gamma_{1}^{T}=-2/3\,.

(73)

Note that the vector currents are not affected, while the scalar and tensor currents renormalize multiplicatively.

With the given effective operators, we can express the ratio of
R_{D}\,(R_{D^{*}})
to the SM prediction
[
141
]
:

\displaystyle r_{D}=\frac{R_{D}}{R_{D}^{\rm SM}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left|1+C_{V_{L}}\right|^{2}+1.01\,\left|C_{S_{L}}\right|^{2}+0.84\,\left|C_{T_{L}}\right|^{2}+1.49\,\,{\rm Re}\left[(1+C_{V_{L}})\,C_{S_{L}}^{*}\right]

(74)

\displaystyle\qquad+1.08\,\,\rm Re\left[(1+C_{V_{L}})\,C_{T_{L}}^{*}\right],

\displaystyle r_{D^{*}}=\frac{R_{D^{*}}}{R_{D^{*}}^{\rm SM}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left|1+C_{V_{L}}\right|^{2}+0.04\left|C_{S_{L}}\right|^{2}+16.07\left|C_{T_{L}}\right|^{2}

(75)

\displaystyle\qquad-0.11\,\,\rm Re\left[(1+C_{V_{L}})\,C_{S_{L}}^{*}\right]-5.17\,\,{\rm Re}\left[(1+C_{V_{L}})\,C_{T_{L}}^{*}\right].

Using a recent global fit to the experimental results, Ref.
[
141
]
found a
4.3\sigma
deviation from the SM prediction:

\displaystyle r_{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.186\pm 0.072,\quad\quad r_{D^{*}}=1.149\pm 0.04.

(76)

Treating this
R_{D^{(*)}}
anomaly at the face value, we will analyze the parameter space which can successfully fit this anomaly. Given the volatile situation with flavor anomalies, we will also present our results for a hypothetical case where this anomaly disappears with more data in the future, i.e. both
R_{D}
and
R_{D^{*}}
are consistent with the SM predictions.

V.3.3
B^{+}\to K^{+}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu}

Leptoquarks can also induce rare semileptonic
B
decays like
B^{+}\rightarrow K^{+}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu}
and
B^{0}\rightarrow{K^{*}}^{0}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu}
, governed by
(\overline{q}\,q\,\overline{\nu}\nu)
effective interactions.
The SM predictions for these decays are
{\rm BR}\left(B^{+}\rightarrow\,K^{+}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu}\right)\,=\,(4.65\pm 0.62)\times 10^{-6}
and
{\rm BR}\left(B^{0}\rightarrow\,{K^{*}}^{0}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu}\right)\,=\,(10.13\pm 0.92)\times 10^{-6}

[
142
]
. Belle-II recently reported the first observation of
B^{+}\rightarrow K^{+}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu}
decay
[
78
]
.
Using the weighted average of BABAR, Belle and Belle-II data, Ref.
[
143
]
quoted an experimental value for
{\rm BR}\left(B^{+}\rightarrow\,K^{+}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu}\right)_{\rm exp}=(1.3\pm 0.4)\times\,10^{-5}
.
As for
B^{0}\rightarrow\,{K^{*}}^{0}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu}
, the current experimental upper limit is
{\rm BR}\left(B^{0}\rightarrow\,{K^{*}}^{0}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu}\right)<2.7\times\,10^{-5}
at 90% CL by the Belle collaboration
[
144
]
. The constraints on new physics (NP) contributions are expressed in terms of the ratios
R^{\nu\nu}_{K^{(*)}}
, defined as

\displaystyle R^{\nu\nu}_{K^{(*)}}=\frac{{\rm BR}^{\rm SM+NP}\left(B^{+(0)}\rightarrow K^{+(*)}\nu\,\overline{\nu}\right)}{{\rm BR}^{\rm SM}\left(B^{+(0)}\rightarrow K^{+(*)}\nu\,\overline{\nu}\right)}.

(77)

From the recent Belle-II measurement
[
78
]
, we get
R_{K}^{\nu\nu}=(2.8\pm 0.94)
and from the Belle upper limit
[
144
]
, we get
R_{K^{*}}^{\nu\nu}<2.7
.

The relevant effective Hamiltonian governing these decays can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\,G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{tb}\,V^{*}_{ts}\left(C^{ij}_{L}\,\mathcal{O}^{ij}_{L}+C^{ij}_{R}\mathcal{O}^{ij}_{R}\right),

(78)

where the operators are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{ij}_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}}{4\pi}\left(\overline{d}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}s\right)\left(\overline{\nu}_{i}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\nu_{j}\right),

(79)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{ij}_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}}{4\pi}\left(\overline{d}\gamma^{\mu}P_{R}s\right)\left(\overline{\nu}_{i}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\nu_{j}\right).

(80)

The corresponding Wilson coefficients are given as

\displaystyle C^{ij}_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\rm SM}\delta^{ij}+\delta C_{L}^{ij}

(81)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\rm SM}\delta^{ij}+\frac{\pi\,v^{2}}{2\,\alpha_{\rm em}\,V_{tb}\,V^{*}_{ts}}\,\left(\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{m_{X_{1}}^{2}}+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{m_{X_{2}}^{2}}\right)\,\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}\right)_{2j}\,\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}^{\ast}\right)_{3i},

\displaystyle C^{ij}_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta C_{R}^{ij}=-\frac{\pi\,v^{2}}{2\,\alpha_{\rm em}\,V_{tb}\,V^{*}_{ts}}\,\left(\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{m_{X_{1}}^{2}}+\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{m_{X_{2}}^{2}}\right)\,\left(Y_{2}\right)_{2j}\,\left(Y_{2}^{*}\right)_{3i}.

(82)

As the experiments cannot tag the neutrino flavor, we need to sum over all possible flavor combinations while calculating the ratio of branching fractions in Eq. (
77
). The ratios can be expressed as
[
145
,
146
]

\displaystyle R_{K}^{\nu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3\left|C_{\rm SM}\right|^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\left|C_{L}^{ij}+C_{R}^{ij}\right|^{2},

(83)

\displaystyle R_{K^{*}}^{\nu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3\left|C_{\rm SM}\right|^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\left|C_{L}^{ij}+C_{R}^{ij}\right|^{2}-\frac{2}{3\left|C_{\rm SM}\right|^{2}}\left(1+\eta\right)\sum_{i,j}\,{\rm Re}\,\left[C_{L}^{ij}\,C_{R}^{*ij}\right]

(84)

The numerical value of
\eta
was found to be
0.33\pm 0.09
using the Light Cone Sum Rule (LCSR) method
[
147
]
.

V.3.4
K^{+}\to\pi^{+}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu}

Apart from
B
meson decays, leptoquark models are also constrained from the rare
K
meson decays. One such example is
K^{+}\rightarrow\,\pi^{+}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu}
which gets tree-level contribution from the scalar leptoquarks
[
148
,
37
,
149
,
143
]
. The experimentally determined value is
{\rm BR}\left(K^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\nu\,\overline{\nu}\right)_{\rm exp}=\left(10.6^{+4.1}_{-3.5}\times 10^{-11}\right)
by NA62
[
150
]
which is consistent with the SM prediction
{\rm BR}\left(K^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\nu\,\overline{\nu}\right)_{\rm SM}=(7.73\pm 0.61)\times 10^{-11}

[
151
]
. Thus, there is a very small room for the NP contribution for
K^{+}\rightarrow\,\pi^{+}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu}
because the SM value is already well inside the experimental range.
The BR of
K^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\nu\,\overline{\nu}
can be written as
[
152
,
148
]

\displaystyle{\rm BR}\left(K^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\nu\,\overline{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa_{+}\frac{\left(1+\Delta_{\rm em}\right)}{3\,\lambda^{10}}\sum_{i,j=e,\mu,\tau}\left[{\rm Im}^{2}\left(\lambda_{t}^{sd}\,X_{t}^{ij}\right)+{\rm Re}^{2}\left(\lambda_{c}^{sd}\,X_{c}^{ii}+\lambda_{t}^{sd}\,X_{t}^{ij}\right)\right],

(85)

where
i,j
denote the flavors of the emitted neutrinos,
\lambda=0.225
is the Wolfenstein parameter of the CKM matrix,
\Delta_{\rm em}=-0.003
,
\kappa_{+}=0.52\times 10^{-10}\left(\frac{\lambda}{0.225}\right)
and
\lambda^{sd}_{q}=V_{qs}\,V_{qd}^{\ast}
, where
q
is the quark flavor. The short-distance contribution
X
is defined as
X_{t}^{ij}=X_{\rm SM}(x_{t})\delta_{ij}+X_{\rm LQ}^{ij}
, where
x_{t}=m_{t}^{2}/m_{\rm LQ}^{2}
. In the SM, the dominant contribution to the branching ratio comes from the top quark and the corresponding
X_{\rm SM}(x_{t})=1.481\pm 0.009
and
X_{c}\,\approx\,10^{-3}
is the charm quark contribution
[
152
]
. NP contributions in our leptoquark model can be written as

\displaystyle X_{\rm LQ}^{ij}=-\frac{\sin^{2}\theta_{w}\,\pi v^{2}\,}{\alpha_{\rm em}}\,\frac{\left(C_{L}^{ij}+C_{R}^{ij}\right)}{\lambda_{t}^{sd}},

(86)

where
\theta_{w}
is the weak mixing angle, and

\displaystyle C_{L}^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}\right)_{2i}\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}\right)_{1j}\,\left(\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{\rm LQ}}{m_{X_{1}}^{2}}+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta_{\rm LQ}}{m_{X_{2}}^{2}}\right),

(87)

\displaystyle C_{R}^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(Y_{2}\right)_{2i}\left(Y_{2}\right)_{1j}\,\left(\frac{\sin^{2}\theta_{\rm LQ}}{m_{X_{1}}^{2}}+\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{\rm LQ}}{m_{X_{2}}^{2}}\right).

(88)

We can also consider the analogous decay of the neutral kaon,
K_{L}\to\pi^{0}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu}
. However, this has not been observed yet, and there only exists an upper bound on
{\rm BR}(K_{L}\to\pi^{0}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu})_{\rm exp}<4.9\times 10^{-9}
from KOTO
[
153
]
. This is more than two orders of magnitude larger than the SM prediction:
{\rm BR}(K_{L}\to\pi^{0}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu})_{\rm SM}=(2.94\pm 0.15)\times 10^{-11}

[
154
]
. Therefore, this process cannot put any meaningful constraint on the NP scenario at the moment.

V.3.5
K_{L}\to\mu^{\pm}e^{\mp}
and
K^{+}\to\pi^{+}\mu^{\pm}e^{\mp}

The effective Hamiltonian for these purely leptonic decays of the pseudoscalar meson
P\rightarrow\,\ell_{i}\,\ell_{j}
and
P\rightarrow\,P^{\prime}\,\ell_{i}\,\ell_{j}
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm LEFT}=\sqrt{2}\,G_{F}\sum_{I\in\left\{V,S,T\right\}}C_{I_{XY}}\,\mathcal{O}_{I_{XY}},

(89)

where
X,Y\in\{L,R\}
. The relevant operator generated at tree level
K_{L}\rightarrow\,\ell^{-}_{i}\,\ell^{+}_{j}
and
K^{+}\rightarrow\,\pi^{+}\,\ell^{-}_{i}\,\ell^{+}_{j}
is

\mathcal{O}_{V_{LR}}^{ij21}=\left(\overline{\ell}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\,P_{L}\,\ell_{j}\right)\,\left(\overline{s}\gamma_{\mu}\,P_{R}\,d\right)
and the corresponding Wilson coefficient is denoted as

\displaystyle C_{V_{LR}}^{ij}=\frac{1}{2\,G_{F}\,m^{2}_{\widetilde{R}_{2}^{2/3}}}\left(\left(Y_{2}\right)_{2j}\left(Y_{2}\right)_{1i}^{\ast}+\left(Y_{2}\right)_{1j}\left(Y_{2}\right)_{2i}^{\ast}\right).

(90)

Here
1,2
correspond to the first (down) and second (strange) generation quarks. Besides the tree level diagram, one loop box diagrams mediated by
S_{1}
leptoquark also contribute to these processes. The penguin diagrams are not possible in this case because of the absence of flavor-changing neutral current (FCNC) for
Z_{\mu}
and
A_{\mu}
.
The Wilson coefficients generated from the box diagrams are

\displaystyle C_{V_{LL}}^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v^{2}}{64\,\sqrt{2}\,\pi^{2}}\sum_{l=1,3}\sum_{m=u,c,t}\,\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}\right)_{2l}\,\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}^{*}\right)_{3l}\,\left(Y_{1}^{L}\right)_{mi}\,\left(Y_{1}^{L}\right)_{mj}^{\ast}\,\left(\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{m_{X_{1}}^{2}}+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{m_{X_{2}}^{2}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle C_{V_{RL}}^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v^{2}}{64\,\sqrt{2}\,\pi^{2}}\sum_{l=1,3}\sum_{m=u,c,t}\,\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}\right)_{2l}\,\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}^{*}\right)_{3l}\,\left(Y_{1}^{R}\right)_{mi}\,\left(Y_{1}^{R}\right)_{mj}^{\ast}\,\left(\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{m_{X_{1}}^{2}}+\frac{\sin^{2}\theta}{m_{X_{2}}^{2}}\right)\,.

(91)

The branching ratio of
K_{L}\rightarrow\ell_{i}^{-}\ell_{j}^{+}
can be computed as
[
104
]

\displaystyle{\rm BR}\left(K_{L}\rightarrow\ell_{i}^{-}\ell_{j}^{+}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{K}\frac{f_{K}^{2}\,m_{K}\,m^{2}_{\ell}}{128\,\pi\,v^{4}}\left(1-\frac{m_{\ell}^{2}}{m_{K}^{2}}\right)^{2}\left\{\left|C_{VA}^{ij}\right|^{2}+\left|C_{AA}^{ij}\right|^{2}\right\},

(92)

where
m_{K}
is the kaon mass,
\tau_{K}
is its lifetime, and
f_{K}
is the kaon decay constant.
The Wilson coefficients in Eq. (
92
) are defined as

\displaystyle C_{VA}^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{V_{LR}}^{ij}-C_{V_{LL}}^{ij}-C_{V_{RL}}^{ij}\,,

\displaystyle C_{AA}^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{V_{LL}}^{ij}-C_{V_{LR}}^{ij}-C_{V_{RL}}^{ij}\,.

(93)

Similarly, the branching ratio for
K^{+}\rightarrow\,\pi^{+}\,\ell_{i}\,\ell_{j}
can be written as
[
104
]

\displaystyle{\rm BR}\left(K^{+}\rightarrow\,\pi^{+}\,\ell_{i}\,\ell_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{VV}\left|C^{ij}_{VV}\right|^{2}+a_{AV}\,\left|C^{ij}_{AV}\right|^{2}\,,

(94)

where
a_{VV}\simeq a_{AV}\simeq 0.157
are kaon form factors in the limit where the light lepton mass is neglected, and the Wilson coefficients are given as

\displaystyle C^{ij}_{VV}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{ij}_{V_{LR}}+C^{ij}_{V_{LL}}+C^{ij}_{V_{RL}}\,,\qquad C^{ij}_{AV}=C^{ij}_{V_{RL}}-C^{ij}_{V_{LL}}-C_{V_{LR}}^{ij}.

(95)

These BRs are subjected to stringent experimental limits:
{\rm BR}\left(K_{L}\rightarrow\mu^{\pm}e^{\mp}\right)<4.7\times 10^{-12}

[
155
]
and
{\rm BR}\left(K^{+}\rightarrow\,\pi^{+}\,\mu^{+}\,e^{-}\right)<1.3\times 10^{-11}

[
156
]
at 90% CL.

VI
Numerical Analysis

In this section, we use all the above mentioned constraints and perform a multidimensional scan over all the leptoquark parameters to carve out the allowed parameter space.

VI.1
Parametrization of the Yukawa Coupling Matrix

In the canonical seesaw mechanism, the Yukawa couplings can be expressed in terms of the PMNS mixing matrix elements and light neutrino masses using the Casas-Ibarra (CI) parametrization
[
157
]
. However, in the leptoquark scenario with multiple Yukawa couplings, the standard CI approach does not work. However, one can use a similar approach to parameterize one Yukawa coupling in terms of the other Yukawa couplings, PMNS matrix elements and neutrino masses
[
158
]
, as follows:

\displaystyle Y_{2}=\frac{1}{\mathbf{C}_{1}}\,\mathcal{M}_{d}^{-1}\,\left(\widetilde{Y_{1}^{L}}^{T}\right)^{-1}\,U^{*}\,\sqrt{\mathcal{M}_{\nu}^{\rm diag}}\,\mathbb{R}\,\sqrt{\mathcal{M}_{\nu}^{\rm diag}}\,U^{\dagger}\,,

(96)

where
\mathbf{C}_{1}=\frac{3\sin 2\theta_{\rm{LQ}}}{32\pi^{2}}\ln\left(\frac{m_{X_{1}}^{2}}{m_{X_{2}}^{2}}\right)
is the prefactor in the neutrino mass matrix [cf. Eq. (
9
)], and
\mathbb{R}
is an arbitrary
3\times 3
complex matrix with
\mathbb{R}+\mathbb{R}^{T}=\mathbb{I}
.
Thus, using Eq. (
96
),
Y_{2}
can be determined in terms of
Y_{1}^{L}
,
\mathbb{R}
, neutrino masses and PMNS matrix elements. In general, the complex
\mathbb{R}
matrix can be parameterized in several ways; one such choice is
[
158
]

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}&r_{1}&r_{2}\\
-r_{1}&\frac{1}{2}&r_{3}\\
-r_{2}&-r_{3}&\frac{1}{2}\end{pmatrix}.

In general,
r_{1}
,
r_{2}
and
r_{3}
can be any complex number. For simplicity, we assume

\displaystyle r_{1}=r_{2}=r_{3}=r\,,

(97)

which is an additional unknown parameter in the model.
For our numerical analysis, we vary
r
within the range
(-10^{2}:10^{2})
. For concreteness, we also choose a benchmark point for the leptoquark masses, which satisfies the current LHC constraints
[
28
,
159
]
:

\displaystyle m_{1}=m_{2}=m_{\rm LQ}=2.0\,{\rm TeV},\quad\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha^{\prime}_{2}=0.2,\quad\kappa=m_{\rm LQ}/10\,,

(98)

which yields

\displaystyle\theta_{\rm LQ}=0.7420\,\,{\rm rad},\quad m_{X_{1}}=1.99\,{\rm TeV},\quad m_{X_{2}}=2.011\,{\rm TeV},\quad m_{\widetilde{R}_{2}^{2/3}}=2.00\,{\rm TeV}\,.

(99)

Later, we will relax this condition to examine the dependence of the observables on the leptoquark mass. As we will see later, for suitable benchark values of Yukawa couplings, large values of
r
will be constrained from observables like
\mu\rightarrow
e conversion and
B\rightarrow\,K^{+}\,\nu\,\overline{\nu}
, and for the benchmark value of
m_{\rm LQ}
chosen here,
r
cannot be larger than
{\cal O}(1)
.

In order to determine the Yukawa matrix
Y_{2}
, we take the following textures for
Y_{1}^{L}
and
Y_{1}^{R}
:

\displaystyle Y_{1}^{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}{y^{L}_{11}}&{y^{L}_{12}}&{y^{L}_{13}}\\
0&0&{y^{L}_{23}}\\
{y^{L}_{31}}&{y^{L}_{32}}&{y^{L}_{33}}\end{pmatrix},\quad\quad Y_{1}^{R}=\begin{pmatrix}{y^{R}_{11}}&{y^{R}_{12}}&{y^{R}_{13}}\\
0&0&{y^{R}_{23}}\\
{y^{R}_{31}}&{y^{R}_{32}}&{y^{R}_{33}}\end{pmatrix}.

(100)

Here, we explicitly assume
{y^{L}_{21}},{y^{L}_{22}},{y^{R}_{21}},{y^{R}_{22}}
as zero to prevent NP contributions to
B\to D\ell\nu
(with
\ell=e,\mu
), which does not give a good fit to the
b\to c\ell\nu
observables, as noted in Ref.
[
160
]
. With the above textures, we vary the elements of the matrices
Y_{1}^{L}
and
Y_{1}^{R}
randomly between their perturbative limits
\left(-\sqrt{4\pi}\,:\,\sqrt{4\pi}\right)
. The oscillation parameters required in Eq. (
96
) are given as inputs in their currently allowed
3\,\sigma
range
[
161
,
4
]

U
and are varied in the range
(0:\pi)
and the lightest neutrino mass is varied over
(10^{-5}:1)
eV. The
Y_{2}
matrix determined with these input values is subjected to perturbativity bounds and as well as constraints from rare meson decays, cLFV processes and lepton

VII
Conclusions

Scalar leptoquarks provide an attractive framework for neutrino mass generation radiatively. At the same time, the new leptoquark interactions give rise to new contributions to various lepton number and flavor violating processes. We have considered the Standard Model augmented with two scalar leptoquarks. While a single leptoquark cannot generate correct neutrino mass, the combination of singlet-doublet leptoquarks (
S_{1}-\tilde{R}_{2}
) can generate the neutrino mass radiatively. Such models have a rich phenomenology since leptoquarks can couple to both quarks and leptons. Our main aim was to study the implications of this combination in context of
0\nu\beta\beta
decay. To find the allowed values of the Yukawa couplings, we imposed the
constraints from neutrino mass and mixing, as well as demanded compliance with bounds coming from charged lepton flavor violation, lepton flavor universality violation and low-energy rare meson decays. We performed a comprehensive global parameter scan satisfying all available experimental data arising from the above-mentioned constraints, as well as existing anomalies in
R_{D^{(*)}}
and
\Delta a_{\ell}
to constrain the allowed parameter space for various Yukawa matrix elements with a TeV-scale leptoquark mass. Our combined analysis reveals some interesting interplay and tensions coming from different constraints. For instance, in this two leptoquark scenario, the muon and electron
g-2
values are in tension with each other. We found that the most stringent constraints on the allowed Yukawa couplings are obtained from
\mu\rightarrow e
conversion in nuclei. We obtained the prediction for
m_{ee}^{\rm eff}
, arising from the combined contributions of standard and leptoquark contributions, for the Yukawa coupling values that pass all the above mentioned constraints. We find that the contribution from leptoquark mediated diagrams to
m_{ee}^{\rm eff}
can be significant and even greater than the standard contribution for normal ordering of the light neutrinos and the cancellation region is no longer present around
m_{\rm{lightest}}\sim{\rm meV}
. The total value of
m_{ee}^{\rm eff}
can lie in the desert region between the standard NO and IO regions and hence can be probed by future experiments like LEGEND-1000 and nEXO. On the other hand, for IO, we find that most of the points otherwise allowed by low-energy flavor constraints are disfavored by the current
0\nu\beta\beta
limit from KamLAND-Zen.

We have also varied the leptoquark mass fixing some benchmark values of Yukawa couplings and discussed the constraints on this from different observables, as well as from the perturbativity bounds. For the chosen benchmark values, we find very stringent constraint on leptoquark mass,
m_{\rm LQ}>6.6
TeV for IO, from the
0\nu\beta\beta
decay.

Appendix C
Loop Functions for cLFV and g-2

The loop functions
\mathcal{F}(x)
and
\mathcal{G}(x)
appearing in Eqs. (
45
)-(
46
) can be defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{S}f_{S}(x)-f_{F}(x)\,,\qquad\mathcal{G}(x)=Q_{S}\,g_{S}(x)-g_{F}(x),

(122)

where
Q_{S}
is the electromagnetic charge for the scalar leptoquark and

\displaystyle f_{S}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x+1}{4(1-x)^{2}}+\frac{x\,\ln x}{2(1-x)^{3}},

(123)

\displaystyle f_{F}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x^{2}-5x-2}{12(x-1)^{3}}+\frac{x\,\ln x}{2(x-1)^{4}},

(124)

\displaystyle g_{S}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{\ln x}{(x-1)^{2}},

(125)

\displaystyle g_{F}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x-3}{2(x-1)^{2}}+\frac{\ln x}{(x-1)^{3}}.

(126)

For
x\ll 1
, these loop functions can be approximated as

\displaystyle f_{S}(x)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4},\quad f_{F}(x)\simeq\frac{1}{6},\quad g_{S}(x)\simeq-\ln(x),\quad g_{F}(x)\simeq-\ln(x)\,.

(127)

Note that for leptoquark
\widetilde{R}_{2}^{2/3}
with
Q_{S}=2/3
, we have
\mathcal{F}(x)\sim\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{6}=0
.

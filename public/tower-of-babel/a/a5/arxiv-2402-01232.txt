Scattering-Passive Structure-Preserving Finite Element Method for the Boundary Controlled Transport Equation with a Moving Mesh Corresponding author: Jesus-Pablo Toledo-Zucco. E-mail: jtoledoz@onera.fr
Jesus-Pablo Toledo-Zucco, Denis Matignon, Charles Poussot-Vassal

Abstract
A structure-preserving Finite Element Method (FEM) for the transport equation in one- and two-dimensional domains is presented. This Distributed Parameter System (DPS) has non-collocated boundary control and observation, and reveals a scattering-energy preserving structure. We show that the discretized model preserves the aforementioned structure from the original infinite-dimensional system. Moreover, we analyse the case of moving meshes for the one-dimensional case. The moving mesh requires less states than the fixed one to produce solutions with a comparable accuracy, and it can also reduce the overshoot and oscillations of Gibbs phenomenon produced when using the FEM. Numerical simulations are provided for the case of a one-dimensional transport equation with fixed and moving meshes.

Introduction

Simulation and control design of distributed parameter systems (DPS) described by Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) requires spatial and time discretizations methods. The field of computational methods seeks to develop high-fidelity models with a low computational effort in such a way that they are implementable on available computers.

One way to reduce computational efforts when multi-physical systems are involved is to use a modular approach. A modular approach allows to represent the overall system as the interconnection of subsystems, which are more simple to deal with. The port-Hamiltonian (pH) approach, introduced in
(
Maschke and van der Schaft 1992
)
, aims at representing complex multi-physical systems as the interconnection between sub-systems by mean of conservation laws. This approach has shown a great success during the last two decades concerning modelling and control of infinite-dimensional systems
(
Rashad et al. 2020
)
.

For boundary controlled and observed infinite-dimensional systems with an impedance-passive structure, the pH approach has been widely used for the structure-preserving discretization. In the literature, one can find structure-preserving discretization methods using discrete exterior calculus
(
Seslija et al. 2012
)
, mixed finite-elements
(
Golo et al. 2004
)
, finite volume
(
Kotyczka 2016
)
, finite-differences
(
Trenchant et al. 2018
)
, among others. Recently, the Partitioned Finite Element
Method (PFEM)
(
Cardoso-Ribeiro et al. 2018
)
,
(
Serhani et al. 2019
)
,
(
Cardoso-Ribeiro et al. 2020
)
has shown a wide number of applications in one, two and three dimensional domains
(
Brugnoli et al. 2019
)
,
(
Haine et al. 2022
)
. The methods mentioned above are mainly focused in
impedance-energy preserving
systems and not much attention has been paid to
scattering-energy preserving
systems.

In this paper, the application of the FEM to
scattering systems
is presented for the cases of the one- and two-dimensional transport equations. We follow the approach proposed in
(
Cardoso-Ribeiro et al. 2020
)
since it has shown to be adaptable to deal with different spatial domains, it uses a single mesh, it provides sparse matrices and it allows distributed parameters. Similar as in the PFEM, in which the
impedance-energy preserving
structure is preserved at the discrete level, in the FEM proposed in this paper, the
scattering-energy preserving
structure is preserved at the discrete level.

A second contribution of this paper is the extension of the structure-preserving FEM to deal with moving meshes. It is known that the efficiency of numerical methods for solving PDEs can be improved concentrating the nodes in the areas of rapid variations of the solution
(
Huang et al. 1994
)
,
(
Huang et al. 2002
)
,
(
Aydoğdu et al. 2019
)
. In this paper, some first steps towards the structure-preserving FEM using moving meshes is presented for the case of the one-dimensional transport equation. The discretized model is a time-variant finite-dimensional system, in which the time-variying matrices depend on the mesh dynamic through sparse matrices as well. We show that the
scattering
structure is also preserved on the finite-dimensional model when the velocity density is constant in space.

In Section
2
, we recall the transport equation in one- and two-dimensional domains, showing it
scattering
structure with respect to a quadratic Hamiltonian. In Section
3
, we develop the structure-preserving discretization scheme for both cases, showing that the discretized models are also
scattering-energy systems
. In Section
4
, we present some first steps towards moving meshes using the toy example of the one-dimensional transport equation. In Section
5
, we show some simulations on the one-dimensional transport equation with fix and moving meshes. Finally, in Section
6
we give some conclusions and perspectives.

2
Transport Equation

2.1
One-dimensional transport equation

We consider the following transport equation in one-dimensional space:

\begin{cases}\dot{x}(\zeta,t)+c\dfrac{\partial e}{\partial\zeta}(\zeta,t)=0,\\
x(\zeta,0)=x_{0}(\zeta),\\
e(\zeta,t)=\mathcal{H}(\zeta)x(\zeta,t),\end{cases}

(1)

in which
\zeta\in[a,b]=:\Omega
represents the space,
t\geq 0
the time,
x(\zeta,t)\in\mathbb{R}
the state,
e(\cdot,t)\in H^{1}(\Omega)
the co-energy variable,
c\in\{-1,+1\}
the velocity direction,
\mathcal{H}(\zeta)>0
the Hamiltonian density, and
x_{0}(\zeta)
the initial condition. The input
u(t)
and output
y(t)
are chosen at the spatial boundaries and they depend on the velocity direction as follows:

c=-1:\begin{cases}e(b,t)=u(t),\\
e(a,t)=y(t),\end{cases}\,c=+1:\begin{cases}e(a,t)=u(t),\\
e(b,t)=y(t),\end{cases}

(2)

The Hamiltonian associated to (
1
) is defined as

H(t):=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}\mathcal{H}(\zeta)x(\zeta,t)^{2}d\zeta=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}x(\zeta,t)e(\zeta,t)d\zeta,

(3)

and one can show that the following balance equation is satisfied:

\dot{H}(t)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(u(t)^{2}-y(t)^{2}\right).

(4)

This balance guarantees that the system (
1
)-(
2
) is
scattering energy preserving
.

Note that, when
\mathcal{H}(\zeta)=\mathcal{H}_{0}
, then the analytic solution is known as
y(t)=u(t-\tau)
, with
\tau=\frac{\ell}{\mathcal{H}_{0}}
and
\ell=b-a
. This is a simple delay system.

2.2
Two-dimensional transport equation

Now, a two-dimensional transport equation is considered. The PDE is:

\dfrac{\partial x}{\partial t}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},t)+\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})x(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},t))=0

(5)

in which
(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\in\Omega
is the space with
\Omega
a bounded domain,
t\geq 0
is the time,
x
is the state variable, and
\mathbb{c}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})=\begin{bmatrix}c_{1}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\\
c_{2}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\end{bmatrix}
is the velocity distribution vector. We define the boundary of
\Omega
as
\Gamma
and the normal vector
\mathbb{n}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})=\begin{bmatrix}n_{1}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\\
n_{2}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\end{bmatrix}
which is perpendicular to
\Gamma
(pointing outside of
\Omega
). We define
\Gamma_{i}
and
\Gamma_{u}
as the sets of the input and output, respectively. These subsets are defined as:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{i}

\displaystyle=\{(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\in\Gamma,\quad\mathbb{c}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})^{\top}\mathbb{n}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})<0\},

(6)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{o}

\displaystyle=\{(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\in\Gamma,\quad\mathbb{c}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})^{\top}\mathbb{n}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})>0\}.

(7)

These subsets are such that
\Gamma=\Gamma_{i}\cup\Gamma_{o}
, and the input and output are:

\begin{split}u(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},t)&=x(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},t),\quad(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\in\Gamma_{i},\\
y(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},t)&=x(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},t),\quad(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\in\Gamma_{o}.\end{split}

(8)

The Hamiltonian associated to (
5
) is defined as:

H(t):=\dfrac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega}x(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},t)^{2}d\zeta,

(9)

with
\zeta=(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})
. In the following, for simplicity, we omit the time and spatial dependency. The time derivative of the Hamiltonian is computed as follows:

\begin{split}\dot{H}&=\iint_{\Omega}x\dfrac{\partial x}{\partial t}d\zeta=-\iint_{\Omega}x\,\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c}x)d\zeta,\\
&=\iint_{\Omega}x\,\mathbb{grad(x)}^{\top}\mathbb{c}\,d\zeta-\int_{\Gamma}x^{2}\mathbb{c}^{\top}\mathbb{n}d\zeta,\end{split}

(10)

in which we have applied integration by part using Stokes theorem.

Now, we use the divergence rule for a product:

\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c}\,x)=\mathbb{c}^{\top}\,\mathbb{grad(x)}+x\,\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c})

(11)

to highlight the
scattering energy preserving
structure of the two-dimensional transport equation. To this end, we multiply (
11
) by
x
as follows:

x\,\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c}\,x)=x\,\mathbb{c}^{\top}\,\mathbb{grad(x)}+x^{2}\,\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c})

(12)

implying the following:

\begin{split}\dot{H}&=-\iint_{\Omega}x\,\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c}x)d\zeta\\
&=-\iint_{\Omega}\left[x\,\mathbb{c}^{\top}\,\mathbb{grad(x)}+x^{2}\,\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c})\right]d\zeta,\\
&=-\iint_{\Omega}\left[x\,\,\mathbb{grad(x)}^{\top}\mathbb{c}+x^{2}\,\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c})\right]d\zeta.\end{split}

(13)

From the previous equation, we use the following identity:

\iint_{\Omega}x\,\,\mathbb{grad(x)}^{\top}\mathbb{c}\,d\zeta=-\dot{H}-\iint_{\Omega}x^{2}\,\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c})d\zeta

(14)

and replace it in (
10
)

\begin{split}\dot{H}&=\iint_{\Omega}x\,\mathbb{grad(x)}^{\top}c\,d\zeta-\int_{\Gamma}x^{2}\mathbb{c}^{\top}\mathbb{n}d\zeta,\\
&=-\dot{H}-\iint_{\Omega}x^{2}\,\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c})d\zeta-\int_{\Gamma}x^{2}\mathbb{c}^{\top}\mathbb{n}d\zeta,\end{split}

(15)

implying

\dot{H}=-\dfrac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega}x^{2}\,\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c})d\zeta-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}x^{2}\mathbb{c}^{\top}\mathbb{n}d\zeta.

(16)

Notice that, if
\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c})=0
, the system is
scattering energy preserving
with the following balance:

\begin{split}\dot{H}&=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}u^{2}\,|\mathbb{c}^{\top}\mathbb{n_{i}}|d\zeta-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{o}}y^{2}\,\mathbb{c}^{\top}\mathbb{n_{o}}d\zeta,\\
\end{split}

(17)

with
\mathbb{n_{i}}
and
\mathbb{n_{o}}
the normal vectors associated to
\Gamma_{i}
and
\Gamma_{o}
, respectively, and
|\mathbb{c}^{\top}\mathbb{n_{i}}|
and
\mathbb{c}^{\top}\mathbb{n_{o}}
a density on the surface
\Gamma
.

and

1\end{bmatrix}
for all
(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\in\Omega
. We define the following boundary domains:

•

•

(18)

and since

,

3
Finite Element Method

In this section, the FEM is applied to the one- and two dimensional transport equations introduced previously. We show that in both cases, the discretized Hamiltonian mimics the Hamiltonian of the infinite-dimensional system, implying that the discretized models are also
scattering energy systems
.

3.1
One-dimensional FEM for the transport equation

We approximate the state and co-energy variable using the following projections:

\begin{split}x(\zeta,t)\approx x^{ap}(\zeta,t)&:=\Phi(\zeta)^{\top}x_{d}(t),\\
e(\zeta,t)\approx e^{ap}(\zeta,t)&:=\Phi(\zeta)^{\top}e_{d}(t).\end{split}

(19)

The approximated functions
x^{ap}(\zeta,t)
and
e^{ap}(\zeta,t)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
\zeta\in\Omega
and
t\geq 0
are both projected on the same basis functions

\Phi(\zeta)=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}(\zeta)\\
\vdots\\
\phi_{N}(\zeta)\end{pmatrix}

(20)

with
\phi_{i}(\cdot)\in H^{1}(\Omega)
for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
. The discretized state and co-energy variables are
x_{d}(t)
and
e_{d}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
, respectively.

We apply the following three operations to the equations in (
1
):
(i)
we multiply by
\Phi(\zeta)
from the right to the left,
(ii)
integrate over
\zeta\in\Omega
, and
(iii)
replace the approximation functions from (
19
). For simplicity, from now on, we consider
c=-1
, meaning that the signals travel from the right to the left. The weak form of (
1
)-(
2
) writes:

\begin{cases}\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta)\left[\Phi(\zeta)^{\top}\dot{x}_{d}(t)-\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}(\zeta)^{\top}e_{d}(t)\right]d\zeta=0,\\
\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta)\Phi(\zeta)^{\top}x_{d}(0)d\zeta=\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta)x_{0}(\zeta)d\zeta,\\
\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta)\Phi(\zeta)^{\top}e_{d}(t)d\zeta=\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta)\mathcal{H}(\zeta)\Phi(\zeta)^{\top}x_{d}(t)d\zeta,\\
y(t)=\Phi(a)^{\top}e_{d}(t).\end{cases}

(21)

which can be equivalently written as:

\begin{cases}E\dot{x}_{d}(t)=Fe_{d}(t)+Bu(t),\\
x_{d}(0)=x_{d0},\\
Ee_{d}(t)=Qx_{d}(t),\\
y(t)=Ce_{d}(t),\end{cases}

(22)

with

\begin{split}E&=\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta)\Phi(\zeta)^{\top}d\zeta,\\
F&=-\Phi(a)\Phi(a)^{\top}-\int_{a}^{b}\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}(\zeta)\Phi(\zeta)^{\top}d\zeta,\\
Q&=\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta)\mathcal{H}(\zeta)\Phi(\zeta)^{\top}d\zeta,\\
B&=\Phi(b),\\
C&=\Phi(a),\\
x_{d0}&=E^{-1}\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta)x_{0}(\zeta)d\zeta.\end{split}

(23)

Discretized Hamiltonian: one-dimensional case

We replace the approximations (
19
) in the Hamiltonian (
3
) to obtain the discretized Hamiltonian as follows:

\begin{split}H(t)\approx H_{d}(t)&:=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}x^{ap}(\zeta,t)e^{ap}(\zeta,t)d\zeta,\\
&=\dfrac{1}{2}x_{d}(t)^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta)\Phi(\zeta)^{\top}d\zeta\right)\,e_{d}(t),\\
&=\dfrac{1}{2}x_{d}(t)^{\top}Ee_{d}(t)=\dfrac{1}{2}x_{d}(t)^{\top}Qx_{d}(t).\end{split}

(24)

The main properties that we use to develop the time derivative of (
24
) are:

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=E^{\top},\quad Q=Q^{\top},

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=\Phi(a)^{\top}e_{d}(t),

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=\Phi(b)^{\top}e_{d}(t),

\displaystyle E\dot{x}_{d}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta)\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}(\zeta)^{\top}d\zeta e_{d}(t).

For simplicity, we omit time and space dependency. The Hamiltonian balance is:

\begin{split}\dot{H}_{d}&={x}_{d}^{\top}Q\dot{x}_{d},\\
&={x}_{d}^{\top}QE^{-1}E\dot{x}_{d},\\
&=(Q{x}_{d})^{\top}E^{-1}(Fe_{d}+Bu),\\
&=(E{e}_{d})^{\top}E^{-1}((-\Phi_{a}\Phi_{a}^{\top}-\int_{a}^{b}\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}\Phi^{\top}d\zeta)e_{d}+\Phi_{b}u),\\
&=-{e}_{d}^{\top}\Phi_{a}\Phi_{a}^{\top}e_{d}-e_{d}^{\top}\int_{a}^{b}\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}\Phi^{\top}d\zeta e_{d}+e_{d}^{\top}\Phi_{b}u,\\
&=-y^{\top}y-e_{d}^{\top}\int_{a}^{b}\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}\Phi^{\top}d\zeta e_{d}+u^{\top}u,\\
&=-y^{2}-e_{d}^{\top}\int_{a}^{b}\Phi\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}^{\top}d\zeta e_{d}+u^{2},\\
&=-y^{2}-e_{d}^{\top}E\dot{x}_{d}+u^{2},\\
&=-y^{2}-(Ee_{d})^{\top}\dot{x}_{d}+u^{2},\\
&=-y^{2}-(Qx_{d})^{\top}\dot{x}_{d}+u^{2},\\
&=-y^{2}-x_{d}^{\top}Q\dot{x}_{d}+u^{2},\\
&=-y^{2}-\dot{H}_{d}+u^{2},\\
\Leftrightarrow\dot{H}_{d}&=\dfrac{1}{2}(u^{2}-y^{2}),\end{split}

(25)

implying that the discretized system (
22
) preserves the
scattering energy preserving
structure of the infinite-dimensional system.

3.2
Two-dimensional FEM for the transport equation

We approximate the state variable using the following projection:

x(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},t)\approx x^{ap}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},t):=\Phi(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})^{\top}x_{d}(t),

(26)

The approximated function
x^{ap}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},t)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\in\Omega
and
t\geq 0
is projected on the following basis functions

\Phi(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\\
\vdots\\
\phi_{N}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\end{pmatrix}

(27)

with
\phi_{i}(\cdot,\cdot)\in H^{1}(\Omega)
for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
. The discretized state variable is
x_{d}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 1}
.

We apply the following three operations to the equations in (
5
):
(i)
we multiply by
\Phi(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})
from the right to the left,
(ii)
integrate over
\zeta\in\Omega
, and
(iii)
replace the approximation functions from (
26
). The weak form of (
5
) writes:

\iint_{\Omega}\Phi\left[\Phi^{\top}\dot{x}_{d}+\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c}\,\Phi^{\top}x_{d})\right]d\zeta=0

(28)

which can be equivalently written as:

M\dot{x}_{d}(t)=F_{1}x_{d}(t)

(29)

with

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega}\Phi\Phi^{\top}d\zeta,

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=-\iint_{\Omega}\Phi\begin{bmatrix}\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c}\phi_{1})\cdots\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c}\phi_{N})\end{bmatrix}d\zeta

or as:

M\dot{x}_{d}(t)=F_{2}x_{d}(t)-\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\Phi\,u\,\mathbb{c}^{\top}\mathbb{n_{i}}\,ds

(30)

with

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega}\begin{bmatrix}\grad(\phi_{1})^{\top}\mathbb{c}\\
\vdots\\
\grad(\phi_{N})^{\top}\mathbb{c}\end{bmatrix}\Phi^{\top}d\zeta-\int_{\Gamma_{o}}\left(\Phi\Phi^{\top}\right)\,\mathbb{c}^{\top}\mathbb{n_{o}}\,ds,

in which we have applied integration by parts making appear the input term and keeping the output term inside the internal dynamic through
F_{2}
.

Discretized Hamiltonian: two-dimensional case

We replace the approximation (
26
) in the Hamiltonian (
9
) to obtain the discretized Hamiltonian as follows:

\begin{split}H(t)\approx H_{d}(t)&:=\dfrac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega}x^{ap}(\zeta,t)x^{ap}(\zeta,t),\\
&=\dfrac{1}{2}x_{d}(t)^{\top}\left(\iint_{\Omega}\Phi(\zeta)\Phi(\zeta)^{\top}d\zeta\right)x_{d}(t),\\
\end{split}

(31)

Notice that
H_{d}
in (
33
) is defined as
H
from (
9
) but evaluated at
x=x^{ap}
, with
x^{ap}
from (
26
). Then, following subsection
2.2
, one can also apply the identities (
11
)-(
12
) to obtain the following discretized Hamiltonian balance:

\dot{H}=-\dfrac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega}x^{ap}x^{ap}\,\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c})\,d\zeta-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}x^{ap}x^{ap}\mathbb{c}^{\top}\mathbb{n}\,ds.

(32)

Then, replacing
x^{ap}=\Phi^{\top}x_{d}
from (
26
), we obtain the following balance:

\begin{split}{\dot{H}}_{d}(t)&=-\dfrac{1}{2}x_{d}(t)^{\top}\left(\iint_{\Omega}\Phi(\zeta)\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c})\Phi(\zeta)^{\top}d\zeta\right)x_{d}(t)\\
&+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}{u}^{2}\,|\mathbb{c}^{\top}\mathbb{n_{i}}|\,ds-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{o}}{y}^{2}\,\mathbb{c}^{\top}\mathbb{n_{o}}\,ds,\\
\end{split}

(33)

where we have replaced the inputs and outputs from (
8
). The previous balance equation shows that at the discretized model preserves the
scattering-energy preserving
structure.

Remark 1

For a divergence-free velocity field, the scattering energy preserving structure is exactly preserved. However, in a non divergence-free case, a dissipative or accumulative effect is appreciated depending on the sign of
\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c})
(See (
16
)) The dissipative or accumulative effect is also preserved at the discrete level through the matrix
Q_{c}=\iint_{\Omega}\Phi\operatorname{div}(\mathbb{c})\Phi^{\top}d\zeta

Remark 2

For the numerical implementation of the discretized model of the 2D transport equation, the input and output have to be projected on two different finite-dimensional bases as well. These projections lead to a new energy balance which is now approximated. The same holds for the scattering energy preserving structure.

4
Moving Mesh for the 1D case

Now, we consider a moving mesh. The state and co-energy variables are approximated as follows:

\begin{split}x(\zeta,t)\approx x^{ap}(\zeta,t)&:=\Phi(\zeta,t)^{\top}x_{d}(t),\\
e(\zeta,t)\approx e^{ap}(\zeta,t)&:=\Phi(\zeta,t)^{\top}e_{d}(t)\end{split}

(34)

with
x^{ap}(\zeta,t)
and
e^{ap}(\zeta,t)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
\zeta\in\Omega
and
t\geq 0
,

\Phi(\zeta,t)=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}(\zeta,t)\\
\vdots\\
\phi_{N}(\zeta,t)\end{pmatrix}

(35)

the vector with the approximation basis functions
\phi_{i}(\cdot,t)\in H^{1}(\Omega)
for all
t\geq 0
and
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
.

The main difference with respect to the previous case is that, in a moving mesh, the time derivative of the approximation function
x^{ap}(\zeta,t)
has the following two terms:

\dot{x}^{ap}(\zeta,t)=\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}(\zeta,t)^{\top}x_{d}(t)+\Phi(\zeta,t)^{\top}\dot{x}_{d}(t).

(36)

We apply the following three operations to the transport equation in (
1
):
(i)
we multiply by
\Phi(\zeta,t)
from the right to the left,
(ii)
integrate over
\zeta\in\Omega
, and
(iii)
replace the approximation function from (
34
)-(
36
). Considering
c=1
, the weak form of (
1
)-(
2
) writes:

\begin{cases}\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta,t)f(\zeta,t)d\zeta=0,\\
f=\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}(\zeta,t)^{\top}x_{d}(t)+\Phi(\zeta,t)^{\top}\dot{x}_{d}(t)-\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}(\zeta,t)^{\top}e_{d}(t),\\
\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta,0)\Phi(\zeta,0)^{\top}x_{d}(0)d\zeta=\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta,0)x_{0}(\zeta)d\zeta,\\
\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta,t)\Phi(\zeta,t)^{\top}e_{d}(t)d\zeta\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad=\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta,t)\mathcal{H}(\zeta)\Phi(\zeta,t)^{\top}x_{d}(t)d\zeta,\\
y(t)=\Phi(a,t)^{\top}e_{d}(t).\end{cases}

(37)

The realization (
37
) can be equivalently written as:

\begin{cases}E(t)\dot{x}_{d}(t)=F(t)e_{d}(t)+B(t)u(t),\\
x_{d}(0)=x_{d0},\\
E(t)e_{d}(t)=Q(t)x_{d}(t),\\
y(t)=C(t)x_{d}(t),\end{cases}

(38)

with

\begin{split}E(t)&=\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta,t)\Phi(\zeta,t)^{\top}d\zeta,\\
F(t)&=-\Phi(a,t)\Phi(a,t)^{\top}-\int_{a}^{b}\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}(\zeta,t)\Phi(\zeta,t)^{\top}d\zeta\\
&\quad-\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta,t)\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}(\zeta,t)^{\top}d\zeta\right)Q(t)^{-1}E(t),\\
Q(t)&=\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta,t)\mathcal{H}(\zeta)\Phi(\zeta,t)^{\top}d\zeta,\\
B(t)&=\Phi(b,t),\\
C(t)&=\Phi(a,t)^{\top},\\
x_{d0}&=E(0)^{-1}\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta,0)x_{0}(\zeta)d\zeta.\end{split}

(39)

Remark 3

Notice that for
\mathcal{H}(\zeta)=\mathcal{H}_{0}
(constant in space), the term
Q(t)^{-1}E(t)
in the expression of
F(t)
from (
39
) becomes the identity matrix times
\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

Discretized Hamiltonian using a moving mesh

In this case, we replace the approximations (
34
) in the Hamiltonian (
3
) to obtain the discretized Hamiltonian as follows:

\begin{split}H_{d}(t)&:=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}x^{ap}(\zeta,t)e^{ap}(\zeta,t),\\
&=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta,t)^{\top}x_{d}(t)\Phi(\zeta,t)^{\top}e_{d}(t)d\zeta,\\
&=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}x_{d}(t)^{\top}\Phi(\zeta,t)\Phi(\zeta,t)^{\top}e_{d}(t)d\zeta,\\
&=\dfrac{1}{2}x_{d}(t)^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta,t)\Phi(\zeta,t)^{\top}d\zeta\right)e_{d}(t),\\
&=\dfrac{1}{2}x_{d}(t)^{\top}E(t)e_{d}(t)=\dfrac{1}{2}x_{d}(t)^{\top}Q(t)x_{d}(t).\end{split}

(40)

The main properties that we use to develop the time derivative of (
40
) are:

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle=E(t)^{\top},\quad Q(t)=Q(t)^{\top},

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=\Phi(a,t)^{\top}e_{d}(t),

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=\Phi(b,t)^{\top}e_{d}(t),

\displaystyle E(t)\dot{x}_{d}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta,t)\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}(\zeta,t)^{\top}d\zeta\right)e_{d}(t)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad-\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi(\zeta,t)\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}(\zeta,t)^{\top}d\zeta\right)x_{d}(t).

In the following, we omit time and space dependency and we introduce the following notation:
\Phi_{a}:=\Phi(a,t)
and
\Phi_{b}:=\Phi(b,t)
. Then, we compute the time derivative of the discretized Hamiltonian (
40
) as follows:

\begin{split}2\dot{H}_{d}&={x}_{d}^{\top}Q\dot{x}_{d}+x_{d}^{\top}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(Qx_{d}\right)\\
&={e}_{d}^{\top}E\dot{x}_{d}+x_{d}^{\top}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(Qx_{d}\right)\\
&={e}_{d}^{\top}\left(Fe_{d}+Bu\right)+x_{d}^{\top}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(Qx_{d}\right),\\
&=-{e}_{d}^{\top}\Phi_{a}\Phi_{a}^{\top}e_{d}+{e}_{d}^{\top}\Phi_{b}u-{e}_{d}^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}\Phi^{\top}d\zeta\right)e_{d}\\
&\quad-{e}_{d}^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}^{\top}d\zeta\right)x_{d}+x_{d}^{\top}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(Qx_{d}\right),\\
&=-y^{2}+u^{2}-{e}_{d}^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}\Phi^{\top}d\zeta\right)e_{d}\\
&\quad-{e}_{d}^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}^{\top}d\zeta\right)x_{d}+x_{d}^{\top}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(Qx_{d}\right).\\
\end{split}

(41)

Notice that if we are able to show that

\begin{split}x_{d}^{\top}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(Qx_{d}\right)&={e}_{d}^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}\Phi^{\top}d\zeta\right)e_{d}\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+{e}_{d}^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}^{\top}d\zeta\right)x_{d}\end{split}

(42)

then,

2\dot{H}_{d}=-y^{2}+u^{2}\Leftrightarrow\dot{H}_{d}=\dfrac{1}{2}(u^{2}-y^{2}).

To do this, we use the following facts:

\begin{split}&{e}_{d}^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}\Phi^{\top}d\zeta\right)e_{d}={e}_{d}^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}^{\top}d\zeta\right)e_{d},\\
&E\dot{x}_{d}=\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}^{\top}d\zeta\right)e_{d}-\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}^{\top}d\zeta\right)x_{d}.\end{split}

(43)

Then, (
42
) is equivalently written as:

\begin{split}x_{d}^{\top}&\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(Qx_{d}\right)={e}_{d}^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\zeta}^{\top}d\zeta\right)e_{d}\\
&-{e}_{d}^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}^{\top}d\zeta\right)x_{d}+2e_{d}^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}^{\top}d\zeta\right)x_{d},\\
&={e}_{d}^{\top}E\dot{x}_{d}+2e_{d}^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}^{\top}d\zeta\right)x_{d},\\
&={x}_{d}^{\top}Q\dot{x}_{d}+2e_{d}^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}^{\top}d\zeta\right)x_{d},\\
\end{split}

(44)

Finally, if
\mathcal{H}
is constant for all
\zeta\in\Omega
, one can show that
e_{d}=\mathcal{H}x_{d}
.Then, the previous equation becomes:

\begin{split}x_{d}^{\top}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(Ex_{d}\right)&=x_{d}^{\top}E\dot{x}_{d}+2x_{d}^{\top}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\Phi\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}d\zeta\right)x_{d},\\
&=x_{d}^{\top}E\dot{x}_{d}+x_{d}^{\top}\dfrac{\partial E}{\partial t}x_{d},\\
&=x_{d}^{\top}\left(E\dot{x}_{d}+\dfrac{\partial E}{\partial t}x_{d}\right),\\
\end{split}

(45)

which prove (
42
) and thus
\dot{H}_{d}=\dfrac{1}{2}(u^{2}-y^{2})
.

Remark 4

Notice that by making
\tfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}=0
, the moving mesh becomes the fixed one developped in subsection
3.1

Remark 5

If the Hamiltonian density is uniform in space, i.e., the transport velocity is the same for all
\zeta\in\Omega
, then the proposed discretization scheme preserves the structure for any time parametrization of the test functions.

6
Conclusions and perspectives

In this paper we have presented a structure-preserving Finite Element Method (FEM) for the transport equation in one- and two-dimensional spatial domains. The main advantages of the proposed method is that the finite-dimensional models preserve the
scattering energy preserving
structure of the initial Partial Differential Equation (PDE). Moreover, for the one-dimensional case, we have extended this approach to deal with moving meshes, which can improve the numerical solution behaviour with respect to fixed meshes. This is a first step towards using adaptive meshes whereas preserving the structure. Further investigations on how the nodes should be adapted will be requiered. The extension of the moving mesh approach to
passive-energy preserving
is part of a future work in order to deal with waves and beams, for instance. Additionally, the implementation of this discretization scheme in predictive control techniques
(
Deng et al. 2022
)
as the well-known Smith predictor is part of the future work of the authors. The fact of preserving the energy structure of the transport phenomena provides a natural Lyapunov function that can be used to guarantee closed-loop stability at the continuos and discrete level
(
Krstic and Smyshlyaev 2008
)
,
(
Bastin and Coron 2016
)
.

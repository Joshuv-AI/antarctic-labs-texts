High-rank subtensors of high-rank tensors
Thomas Karam

Abstract
Let d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 be a positive integer. We show that for a class of notions R R of rank for order- d d tensors, which includes in particular the tensor rank, the slice rank and the partition rank, there exist functions F d , R F_{d,R} and G d , R G_{d,R} such that if an order- d d tensor has R R -rank at least G d , R ​ ( l ) G_{d,R}(l) then we can restrict its entries to a product of sets X 1 × ⋯ × X d X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{d} such that the restriction has R R -rank at least l l and the sets X 1 , … , X d X_{1},\dots,X_{d} each have size at most F d , R ​ ( l ) F_{d,R}(l) . Furthermore, our proof methods allow us to show that under a very natural condition we can require the sets X 1 , … , X d X_{1},\dots,X_{d} to be pairwise disjoint.

Introduction

The last few years have seen a sequence of successes in using notions of ranks for higher-dimensional tensors to solve combinatorial problems. A central idea from the breakthrough solution to the cap-set problem by Ellenberg and Gijswijt
[
3
]
, which was based on a technique of Croot, Lev, and Pach
[
2
]
, was reformulated by Tao
[
20
]
in terms of the notion of slice rank for tensors, leading to what is now known as the slice rank polynomial method. The slice rank was further studied by Sawin and Tao
[
19
]
, and bounds shown there on the slice rank involving orderings on the coordinates were later used by Sauermann
[
18
]
to prove under suitable conditions the existence of solutions with pairwise distinct variables to systems of equations in subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
that are not exponentially sparse. Another fruitful generalisation of the idea underlying the slice rank has been the partition rank, which was defined by Naslund
[
17
]
in order to prove a polynomial upper bound on the size of subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}^{n}
not containing any
k
-right corners (with
p
a prime integer and
r\geq 1
a positive integer) and very recently used again by Naslund
[
16
]
to prove exponential lower bounds on the chromatic number of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with multiple forbidden distances.

In this paper we will focus on high-rank subtensors of tensors: it is a standard fact from linear algebra that if
A
is a matrix of rank
k
then
A
has a
k\times k
submatrix with rank
k
, and we will study here the extent to which this statement can be generalised to notions of rank for higher-order tensors, in particular to the tensor rank, to the slice rank and to the partition rank. The results that we obtain in this direction as well as the methods that we use in their proofs will also allow us to prove that under a very natural assumption we can find a subtensor such that the coordinates take values in pairwise disjoint sets. As we explain in a few paragraphs, the formulation of this result also arises naturally as an analogue of the standard inequality that every oriented graph has a bipartition such that at least a quarter of the edges go from the first part to the second.

We now define the relevant notions of higher-dimensional ranks for tensors and state our main theorems.

Let

We are now in a position to state our first main theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
d\geq 2
be an integer, and let
R
be a non-empty family of partitions of
[d]
. There exist functions
F_{d,R}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}

each with size at most

\Rrk T(X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{d})\geq l
.

Another independent starting point is the following standard statement.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
G
be an oriented graph with vertex set

. There exists an ordered bipartition
(X,Y)
of

such that the number of edges
(u,v)\in X\times Y
of

is at least a quarter of the total number of edges of

.

This statement can be seen to be equivalent to the following: given a matrix

of

such that the restriction

has at least a quarter as many support elements as

has outside the diagonal. A first step will be to obtain an analogue of this statement for ranks of matrices: this will be the aim of Section

Let
E
be the set of points

that do

Definition 1.6
.

Let

be an integer, let

\eRrk T=\min_{V}\Rrk(T+V)

, and the

\dRrk T=\max_{X_{1},\dots,X_{d}}\Rrk(T(X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{d}))

In the

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
d\geq 2
be an integer, and let
R
be a non-empty family of partitions of
[d]
. For every order

\dRrk T\leq\eRrk T.

Proof.

Let
X_{1}\subset Q_{1},\dots,X_{d}\subset Q_{d}
be pairwise disjoint sets and let
V:Q_{1}\times\dots\times Q_{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be an order-
d

E

is contained in

, we have

T(X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{d})=(T-V)(X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{d}).

Moreover, taking a restriction of a tensor cannot increase its

\Rrk(T-V)(X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{d})\leq\Rrk(T-V).

Therefore

\Rrk T(X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{d})\leq\Rrk(T-V),

so taking the maximum over the

of pairwise disjoint sets and the minimum over the tensors

Our second main result is a weak converse to this last inequality.

Theorem 1.8

Let

be an integer, and let

2
Optimal bounds for tensor rank subtensors using spanning techniques

In this section we prove Theorem
1.4
in the case of the tensor rank. Given a vector space
V
over a field
\mathbb{F}
, and a subset
U
of
V
, we write
\langle U\rangle
for the linear subspace of
V
spanned by
U
. The steps of the proof will be modelled after those of the following standard proof of the existence of full-rank submatrices of matrices. Let
A:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be a matrix.

1.

We characterise
\rk A
as the smallest
k\geq 0
such that there exist vectors
L_{1},\dots,L_{k}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{2}}
such that
A_{x}\in\langle L_{1},\dots,L_{k}\rangle
for each
x\in Q_{1}
.

2.

We extract a basis
(A_{x}:x\in X)
of the linear subspace
\langle A_{x}:x\in Q_{1}\rangle
spanned by all rows of
A
, with a set
X
of size
\dim\langle A_{x}:x\in Q_{1}\rangle=\rk A
.

3.

It follows from the two previous steps that
\rk A(X\times Q_{2})=\rk A
. Iterating again on the second coordinate we are done.

As an analogue of step 1 we begin by expressing the tensor rank of an order-
d
tensor in terms of a notion of rank for families of order-
(d-1)
tensors.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}
and let
S,F\subset V
. The
spanning rank

\sprk_{S}F
of
F
with respect to
S
is the smallest nonnegative integer
k
such that there exist vectors
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in S
satisfying
F\subset\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{s}\rangle
.

When
S=V
, we have
\sprk_{S}F=\dim\langle F\rangle
for every
F\subset V
. For any fixed
F\subset V
,
S\mapsto\sprk_{S}F
is decreasing for inclusion, so in particular we always have
\sprk_{S}F\geq\dim\langle F\rangle
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
d\geq 3
be a positive integer, and let
S_{d-1}
be the family of order-
(d-1)
tensors
\prod_{\alpha=2}^{d}Q_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
with order-
(d-1)
tensor rank equal to
1
. Then every order-
d
tensor
T:\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}Q_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
satisfies

\tr T=\sprk_{S_{d-1}}\{T_{x_{1}}:x_{1}\in Q_{1}\}.

Proof.

Let
k
be an nonnegative integer. If
\tr T=k
then there exist functions
a_{i,\alpha}:Q_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
for each
i\in[k]
and each
\alpha\in[d]
such that

T(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}a_{i,\alpha}(x_{\alpha})

for every
(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}Q_{\alpha}
. Then the order-
(d-1)
tensors
T^{i}=\prod_{\alpha=2}^{d}a_{i,\alpha}
for each
i\in[k]
each have order-
(d-1)
tensor rank at most
1
and
T_{x_{1}}\in\langle T^{1},\dots,T^{k}\rangle
for each
x_{1}\in Q_{1}
. Conversely if
\sprk_{S_{d-1}}T=k
then there exist order-
(d-1)
tensors
T^{1},\dots,T^{k}:\prod_{\alpha=2}^{d}Q_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
with tensor rank at most
1
such that for each
x_{1}\in Q_{1}
there exist
a_{1}(x_{1}),\dots,a_{k}(x_{1})\in\mathbb{F}
satisfying
T_{x_{1}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}(x_{1})T^{i}
, so we can write

T(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}(x_{1})T^{i}(x_{2},\dots,x_{d})

for every
(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}Q_{\alpha}
, which shows that
T
has tensor rank at most
k
. ∎

Our next lemma is an adaptation of the linear-algebra fact underlying step 2: the claim that a finite family of vectors of a vector space has a subfamily with the same rank as that of the original family and with the same size as its rank.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
k\geq 1
be a positive integer, let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}
, and let
S
and
F
be families of elements of
V
. Assume that
\sprk_{S}F\geq k
. Then there exists a subfamily
F^{\prime}
of
F
with size at most
k
such that
\sprk_{S}F^{\prime}\geq k
.

Proof.

We distinguish two cases depending on the dimension of the linear subspace
\langle F\rangle
. If
\dim\langle F\rangle\geq k
then we take
F^{\prime}
to be a linearly independent family of
k
elements of
F
; because
\dim\langle F^{\prime}\rangle=k
, in particular
\sprk F^{\prime}\geq k
. If on the other hand
\dim\langle F\rangle\leq k
then we take
F^{\prime}
to be a maximal linearly independent family of elements of
F
; the family
F^{\prime}
has size at most
k
, and since
\langle F\rangle=\langle F^{\prime}\rangle
, a family of elements of
S
spans all elements of
F
if and only if it spans all elements of
F^{\prime}
, so
\sprk F^{\prime}=\sprk F
.∎

We are now ready to deduce our subtensors result for the tensor rank.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
d\geq 3
,
k\geq 1
be positive integers and let
T:\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}Q_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be an order-
d
tensor. Assume that
\tr T\geq k
. Then there exist sets
X_{1}\subset Q_{1},\dots,X_{d}\subset Q_{d}
with size at most
k
such that
\tr T(X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{d})\geq k
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
we have
\sprk_{S_{d-1}}\{T_{x_{1}}:x_{1}\in Q_{1}\}=\tr T
. Since
\tr T\geq k
, by Lemma
2.3
there exists a subset
X_{1}
of
Q_{1}
with size at most
k
such that
\sprk_{S_{d-1}}\{T_{x_{1}}:x_{1}\in X_{1}\}\geq k
, so applying Lemma
2.2
again we have

\tr T(X_{1}\times\prod_{j=2}^{d}Q_{j})\geq k.

Iterating this argument
d-1
more times, which we can, since the roles of the
d
coordinates are the same in the definition of the tensor rank, we obtain the desired sets
X_{2},\dots,X_{d}
. ∎

The remainder of this section is devoted to proving a generalisation of Proposition
2.4
to linear subspaces spanned by a fixed number of tensors. We begin by formulating such a generalisation for matrices.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
s,k\geq 1
be positive integers, and let
A_{1},\dots,A_{s}:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be matrices. Then there exists a subset
X\subset Q_{1}
with size at most
sk
such that

\rk(a.A)(X\times Q_{2})\geq\min(\rk(a.A),k)

for every
a\in\mathbb{F}^{s}
. Iterating a second time, there also exists a subset
Y\subset Q_{2}
with size at most
sk
such that

\rk(a.A)(X\times Y)\geq\min(\rk(a.A),k)

for every
a\in\mathbb{F}^{s}
.

Proof.

We remove rows one by one until there are only at most
sk
remaining rows, with the inductive step being as follows. Assume that there are still
m>sk
remaining rows and let

be the set of remaining rows. Let

(a^{1},\dots,a^{r})
of elements of
\mathbb{F}^{s}
such that

and

. For each

of

th row from

. ∎

We now apply Proposition

to obtain a multidimensional version of Lemma

Lemma 2.6

be a positive integer, let

be a family of elements of

We fix an arbitrary choice of basis

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field. Then there exists an order-

\sr T=4

,

For

(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}),(x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime\prime},z^{\prime\prime})\in U

If

[n_{1}]\times[n_{2}]\times[n_{3}]

\scc U

.

Let

Using the additivity of the slice rank over diagonal sums recently proved by Gowers

Proposition 3.2

For every positive integer

For each

We take

\displaystyle=(X\cap I_{i,11})-\{11(i-1)\}

\displaystyle Y_{i}

\displaystyle=(Y\cap I_{i,4})-\{4(i-1)\}

\displaystyle Z_{i}

4
Partition rank subtensors in the finite fields case using projections and analytic rank

Throughout this section and only in this section we assume that the field
\mathbb{F}
is finite. We will prove Theorem
1.4
for the partition rank in this special case, and our main result will be Theorem
4.1
. Although our upper bound on the function
G_{d,\pr}^{|\mathbb{F}|}
there turns out to be independent of
|\mathbb{F}|
, we shall nonetheless keep the notation of this function in order to distinguish it from the function
G_{d,\pr}
from Theorem
1.4
, where the finiteness of the field is not assumed. For
f_{d},g_{d}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
two sequences of functions, we shall write
f_{d}=O_{d}(g_{d})
for the statement that for every

there exist quantities
C_{d},L_{d}>0
(depending on

) such that
f_{d}(l)\leq C_{d}g_{d}(l)
for all
l\geq L_{d}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
be a positive integer and let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field. Then there exist functions
F_{d,\pr}^{|\mathbb{F}|}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
,
G_{d,\pr}^{|\mathbb{F}|}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
satisfying

F_{d,\pr}^{|\mathbb{F}|}(l)\leq|\mathbb{F}|^{d!l}\text{ and }G_{d,\pr}^{|\mathbb{F}|}(l)=O_{d}(l\log^{d}(l))

such that the following property holds. If
T:Q_{1}\times\dots\times Q_{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
is an order-
d
tensor with
\pr T\geq G_{d,\pr}^{|\mathbb{F}|}(l)
then there exist
X_{1}\subset Q_{1},\dots,X_{d}\subset Q_{d}
each with size at most
F_{d,\pr}^{|\mathbb{F}|}(l)
and such that
\pr T(X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{d})\geq l
.

We will rely on a connection between the partition rank and the analytic rank which we begin by explaining.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
T:\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}Q_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be an order-
d
tensor. The
multilinear form
m(T):\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}\mathbb{F}^{Q_{\alpha}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
associated with

is defined by the formula

m(T)(u_{1},\dots,u_{d})=\sum_{x_{1}\in Q_{1},\dots,x_{d}\in Q_{d}}T(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})(u_{1})_{x_{1}}\dots(u_{d})_{x_{d}}

for each
u_{1}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}},\dots,u_{d}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{d}}
.

For each
u\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}}
let
u.T:\prod_{\alpha=2}^{d}Q_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be the order-
(d-1)
tensor defined by

(u.T)(x_{2},\dots,x_{d})=\sum_{x_{1}\in Q_{1}}u(x_{1})T(x_{1},\dots x_{d})

for every
x_{2}\in Q_{2}
, …,
x_{d}\in Q_{d}
. It is straightforward to check that the
(d-1)
-linear form
m(u.T):\prod_{\alpha=2}^{d}\mathbb{F}^{Q_{\alpha}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
satisfies

m(u.T)(u_{2},\dots,u_{d})=m(T)(u,u_{2},\dots,u_{d})

for every
u_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{2}},\dots,u_{d}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{d}}
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
be a positive integer, and let

be an order-

tensor. The

of

is defined by

\bias(T)=\mathbb{E}_{u_{1}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}},\dots,u_{d}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{d}}}\chi(m(T)(u_{1},\dots,u_{d}))

(1)

for any arbitrary non-trivial character
\chi

\mathbb{F}
.

Indeed the following interpretation shows that the right-hand side of (

\chi

\bias(T)
is always a positive real number. We can write

\displaystyle\bias(T)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{u_{1}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}},\dots,u_{d-1}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{d-1}}}(\mathbb{E}_{u_{d}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{d}}}\chi(m_{(u_{1},\dots,u_{d-1})}(T)(u_{d})))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{u_{1}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}},\dots,u_{d-1}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{d-1}}}(m_{(u_{1},\dots,u_{d-1})}(T)\equiv 0)

where for each

, the linear form

for each

. The main property of the
\bias
that we will use is that we can write

\bias(T)=\mathbb{E}_{u_{1}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}}}\bias(u_{1}.T).

(2)

If

\bias T=\mathbb{F}^{-\rk T}
. Proving qualitatively that for a fixed integer

and a fixed finite field
\mathbb{F}

of a tensor

[

It is known since works of Lovett
[

(3)

At the time that the first version of this manuscript was written, the best bounds

(4)

[

The averaging identity (

) we can rewrite

|\mathbb{F}|^{-\ar T}=\mathbb{E}_{u\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}}}|\mathbb{F}|^{-\ar(u.T)}

and hence obtain

|\mathbb{F}|^{-A_{d,\mathbb{F}}^{-1}(\pr T)}\geq\mathbb{E}_{u\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}}}|\mathbb{F}|^{-\pr(u.T)}.

(5)

We now begin the proof of our subtensors result. Our first step will be to use inequality (
5
) to show that, in a sense that we are about to make precise, if a tensor has all its projections over the first coordinate approximately spanned by a family of order-
(d-1)
tensors that has bounded size, then the tensor has bounded partition rank.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
d\geq 3
,
q,l\geq 1
be positive integers. If
T:\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}Q_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
is an order-
d
tensor over a finite field
\mathbb{F}
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{l}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}}
are such that for every
u\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}}
there exists
v\in\mathbb{F}^{l}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying

\pr(u.T-\sum_{h=1}^{l}v_{h}u_{h}.T)\leq q,

then

\pr T\leq A_{d,\mathbb{F}}(l+q).

Proof.

There are only at most
|\mathbb{F}|^{l}
linear combinations of
u_{1},\dots,u_{l}
, so by the assumption and the pigeonhole principle there is some
v\in\mathbb{F}^{l}
such that for a proportion at least
|\mathbb{F}|^{-l}
of the
u\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}}
,

\pr(u.T-\sum_{h=1}^{l}v_{h}u_{h}.T)\leq q.

The change of variables
u\mapsto u-\sum_{h=1}^{l}v_{h}u_{h}
therefore shows that a proportion at least
|\mathbb{F}|^{-l}
of the
u\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}}
is such that
\pr(u.T)\leq q
. Hence,

\mathbb{E}_{u\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}}}|\mathbb{F}|^{-\pr(u.T)}\geq|\mathbb{F}|^{-l}|\mathbb{F}|^{-q},

so by the inequality (
5
) we have

|\mathbb{F}|^{-A_{d,\mathbb{F}}^{-1}(\pr T)}\geq|\mathbb{F}|^{-l}|\mathbb{F}|^{-q},

from which it follows that
\pr T\leq A_{d,\mathbb{F}}(l+q)
.∎

We can without much effort deduce from Proposition
4.4
that a tensor with high partition rank has a large separated family of projections over the first coordinate.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
q,l\geq 1
be positive integers. Let
T:\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}Q_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be an order-
d
tensor over a finite field
\mathbb{F}
such that
\pr T\geq A_{d,\mathbb{F}}(l+q)
. Then there exist
u_{1},\dots,u_{l}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}}
such that

\pr(\sum_{h=1}^{l}v_{h}u_{h}.T)\geq q

for every
v\in\mathbb{F}^{l}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

Using the contrapositive of Proposition
4.4
we can construct the
u_{h}
by induction on
h=1,\dots,l
as follows: we find
u_{1}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}}
such that
\pr u_{1}.T\geq q
and more generally at the
h
th step we find
u_{h}\in\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}}
such that

\pr((u_{h}-\sum_{h^{\prime}=1}^{h-1}v_{h^{\prime}}u_{h^{\prime}}).T)\geq q

for every
v\in\mathbb{F}^{h-1}\setminus\{0\}
.∎

In our next lemma we show that, conversely, if a tensor has a large separated set of projections then it has high partition rank. Unlike the proof of Proposition
4.4
, the proof of the following lemma does not resort to the connection between partition rank and analytic rank (and hence works in an arbitrary field).

Lemma 4.6

Let

Proof.

Assume for a contradiction that

. Then there exist nonnegative integers

with

,

for every

be arbitrary fixed elements of
\mathbb{F}^{Q_{1}}
. For each

The right-hand side has partition rank at most

, a contradiction. ∎

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
A:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be a matrix. We have
\drk A\geq(\erk A)/3
.

Proof.

Let
k=\drk A
. There exist disjoint subsets
X\subset Q_{1}
,
Y\subset Q_{2}
such that
\rk A(X\times Y)=k
. By the standard result on full-rank submatrices of matrices we can furthermore require
X,Y
to have size
k
. Let
z\in Q_{1}\setminus(X\cup Y)
and
w\in Q_{2}\setminus(X\cup Y)
. If
z,w
are distinct then
X\cup\{z\}
and
Y\cup\{w\}
are disjoint, so by definition of the disjoint rank,

\rk A((X\cup\{z\})\times(Y\cup\{w\}))=\rk A(X\times Y)

whence

A(z,w)=A(\{z\}\times Y)A(X\times Y)^{-1}A(X\times\{w\}).

If
z=w
then there exists a unique element
d(z)\in\mathbb{F}
such that

A(z,z)+d(z)=A(\{z\}\times Y)A(X\times Y)^{-1}A(X\times\{w\}).

Let
D:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be the matrix defined by
D(z,w)=0
if
z\neq w
and
D(z,w)=d(z)
if
z=w
. We get that for all
z\in Q_{1}\setminus(X\cup Y)
and all
w\in Q_{2}\setminus(X\cup Y)
,

\displaystyle(A+D)(z,w)

\displaystyle=A(\{z\}\times Y)A(X\times Y)^{-1}A(X\times\{w\})

\displaystyle=(A+D)(\{z\}\times Y)(A+D)(X\times Y)^{-1}(A+D)(X\times\{w\})

where the last equality holds since
D
is identically
0
on all three sets
\{z\}\times Y
,
X\times Y
,
X\times\{w\}
. Therefore

\rk(A+D)((X\cup(Q_{1}\setminus(X\cup Y)))\times(Y\cup(Q_{2}\setminus(X\cup Y)))=\rk(A+D)(X\times Y).

Simplifying both sides we get

\rk(A+D)((Q_{1}\setminus Y)\times(Q_{2}\setminus X))=\rk A(X\times Y)=k.

Moreover by subadditivity

\rk(A+D)\leq\rk(A+D)((Q_{1}\setminus Y)\times(Q_{2}\setminus X))+\rk(A+D)(Y\times(U_{2}\setminus X))+\rk(A+D)((Q_{1}\setminus Y)\times X).

The first term of the right-hand side is at most
k
as we have just shown, and the second and third terms are each at most
k
since
X,Y
have size
k
. So
\rk(A+D)\leq 3k
, whence
\erk A\leq 3\drk A
as desired. ∎

.

We proceed by induction on

5.1
proves the

there exist disjoint subsets

,

\mathbb{F}^{s}
such that

6
Disjoint tensor rank using the flattening rank

Let

be a positive integer. For

for the set

\{x(I)\in\prod_{\alpha\in I}Q_{\alpha}:x_{\alpha^{\prime}}=x_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\text{ for some distinct }\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime}\in I\}.

In particular, the set

is the set

of elements that do not have pairwise distinct coordinates.

6.1
The order-3 case

Our proof will make use of the following notion of rank.

Definition 6.1
.

For

an order-
3
tensor, let the

of

, which we write as

defined by

In other words
\frank_{1}T
is defined as
\Rrk T
for

. Likewise we define the

for this same

and denote it by
\efrank_{1}T=\eRrk T
.

The structure of our proof will be as follows: starting with an order-
3
tensor

with high essential tensor rank, either the tensor
T
has an order-
2
slice with high essential rank, in which case we conclude using Proposition
5.1
, or all order-
2
slices of

have bounded essential rank, in which case the essential tensor rank can be shown to be equivalent to the essential

-flattening rank, and we will then show (using again our assumption that all order-

has high essential

-flattening rank, it must have high disjoint

-flattening rank, which suffices to ensure that

has high disjoint tensor rank.

We begin by proving our equivalence statement between the essential ranks.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
T:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\times Q_{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be an order-

tensor such that
\erk T_{x}\leq m

\efrank_{1}T\leq l

\etr T\leq(m+2)l^{2}
.

Proof.

Let

. There exists a tensor

supported inside

, functions

and functions
b_{1},\dots,b_{r}:Q_{2}\times Q_{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
such that we can write the decomposition

(T-V)(x,y,z)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}(x)b_{i}(y,z).

(6)

The functions

are linearly independent, so by Gaussian elimination there exists a subset
U\subset Q_{1}
with size
r
and functions

for all

. For a fixed

a_{i}^{*}
to both sides of

(7)

\{y=x\}

\{z=x\}
and the diagonal

\etr(T-V)\leq r^{2}(m+2)\leq l^{2}(m+2)

\etr T\leq l^{2}(m+2)
. ∎

7
Proofs for the slice rank of order-3 tensors

7.1
Proof for order-3 slice rank subtensors

We here prove Theorem
1.4
in the case of the slice rank of order-3 tensors. Our proof can be summarised as follows: given an order-
3
tensor
T
with high slice rank we distinguish two cases: either there exists a large separated set of slices
T_{x}
, in which case we can find sets
Y,Z
such that this is still the case after we restrict these slices to
Y\times Z
, which suffices to guarantee a high rank subtensor, or there does not exist such a large separated set, in which case we construct a projected tensor for which the slice rank and tensor rank are equivalent in the sense that they are large simultaneously, and conclude using our subtensors result in the tensor rank case.

We begin by proving a lemma showing that having a large separated set of slices guarantees a high slice rank.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
T:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\times Q_{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be an order-
3
tensor, and
l\geq 1
be an integer. If there exist
x_{1}\dots,x_{l}\in Q_{1}
such that

\rk(\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}T_{x_{i}})\geq l

for every
(a_{1},\dots,a_{l})\in\mathbb{F}^{l}\setminus\{0\}
, then
\sr T\geq l
.

Proof.

We show the contrapositive. Assume that
\sr T\leq l-1
. Then there exist nonnegative integers
r,s,t
with
r+s+t=l
and functions
a_{i}:Q_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
,
b_{i}:Q_{2}\times Q_{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
,
i\in[r]
,
c_{j}:Q_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
,
d_{j}:Q_{1}\times Q_{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
,
j\in[s]
,
e_{k}:Q_{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
,
f_{k}:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
,
k\in[t]
such that

T(x,y,z)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}(x)b_{i}(y,z)+\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_{j}(y)d_{j}(x,z)+\sum_{k=1}^{t}e_{k}(z)f_{k}(x,y)

Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\in Q_{1}
. Then we can write

T_{x_{h}}(y,z)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}(x_{h})b_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_{j}(y)d_{j}(x_{h},z)+\sum_{k=1}^{t}e_{k}(z)f_{k}(x_{h},y)

for each
h\in[l]
. Because
l\geq r+1
there exists a function
a:Q_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
supported inside
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\}
such that
a\neq 0
but
\sum_{h=1}^{l}a(x_{h})a_{i}(x_{h})=0
for each
i\in[r]
, so we can write

\sum_{h=1}^{l}a(x_{h})T_{x_{h}}(y,z)=\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_{j}(y)(\sum_{h=1}^{l}a(x_{h})d_{j}(x_{h},z))+\sum_{k=1}^{t}e_{k}(z)(\sum_{h=1}^{l}a(x_{h})f_{k}(x_{h},y)).

The right-hand side has rank at most
s+t\leq l-1
, a contradiction.∎

We next show a partial converse to the inequality
\sr T\leq\tr T
which holds in the situation where all slices of
T
of all three kinds have bounded rank.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
T:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\times Q_{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be an order-
3
tensor. Let
m\geq 1
be an integer. Assume that for all

we have
\rk T_{x},\rk T_{y},\rk T_{z}\leq m

Then
\tr T\leq m(\sr T)^{2}
.

Proof.

There exists a decomposition

\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}(x)b_{i}(y,z)+\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_{j}(y)d_{j}(x,z)+\sum_{k=1}^{t}e_{k}(z)f_{k}(x,y)

(11)

of
T
, with
r,s,t
nonnegative integers such that
r+s+t=\sr T
. The family
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\}
is necessarily linearly independent. By Gaussian elimination there exists a set
X\subset Q_{1}
of size at most
r
and for each
i\in[r]
a function
a_{i}^{*}:Q_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
supported inside
X
such that
a_{i}^{*}.a_{i^{\prime}}=\delta_{i,i^{\prime}}
for all
i,i^{\prime}\in[r]
. For a given
i\in[r]
, applying
a_{i}^{*}
to both sides of (
11
) we get

b_{i}(y,z)=(a_{i}^{*}.T)(y,z)-\sum_{j=1}^{s}c_{j}(y)(a_{i}^{*}.d_{j})(z)-\sum_{k=1}^{t}e_{k}(z)(a_{i}^{*}.f_{k})(y).

Because
\rk T_{x}\leq m
for each
x\in Q_{1}
and
a_{i}^{*}
is supported in a set of size at most
r
we get by subadditivity of the rank that
\rk(a_{i}^{*}.T)\leq mr
and hence
\rk b_{i}\leq mr+s+t
. Similarly
\rk d_{j}\leq ms+r+t
for every
j\in[s]
and
\rk f_{k}\leq mt+r+s
for every

. By subadditivity

\tr T\leq r(mr+s+t)+s(ms+r+t)+t(mt+r+s)\leq m(\sr T)^{2}.\qed

We are now ready to prove our subtensors result.

\displaystyle\pr T

\displaystyle\pr_{(2,2)}T

Lemma 9.1

Let

\displaystyle T(x,y,z,w)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{r_{3}}a_{3,i}(z)b_{3,i}(x,y,w)+\sum_{i=1}^{r_{4}}a_{4,i}(w)b_{4,i}(x,y,z)+U(x,y,z,w)

Lemma 9.4

Proposition 9.7

10
Additional difficulties for order-4 tripartition rank subtensors

For
T
an order-
4
tensor, let the
tripartition rank

\trp T
of
T
be the value of
\Rrk T
for
R
the set of partitions of
\{1,2,3,4\}
into three (non-empty) parts.

Throughout this section we prove Theorem
1.4
for
(d,R)=(4,\trp)
. The structure of the proof will be as follows. Let
T
be an order-
4
tensor with large tripartition rank. Rather than the two cases involved in the proofs of the previous section, the proof will distinguish between three cases (the second of our steps below is a preparation for the last two cases).

1.

If the tensor
T
has a large separated set of order-
2
slices, without loss of generality of the type
T_{(x_{i},y_{i})}
, then we can restrict them to a product
Z\times W
with
Z,W
of bounded size such that the restrictions are still separated, and containing the
(x_{i},y_{i})
in a box
X\times Y
with bounded size ensures that the tripartition rank of
T(X\times Y\times Z\times W)
is large.

2.

If the tensor
T
does not have such a large separated set, then we find a bounded number of
(x_{i},y_{i})
and of functions
A_{i}:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
such that we can approximate each slice
T_{x,y}
by

\sum_{i}A_{i}(x,y)T_{(x_{i},y_{i})}(z,w)

and similarly for the five other kinds of order-
2
slices. Using a sequence of scales to define the approximation we can ensure that the functions
A_{i}
can to be thought of as independent coordinates.

3.

If at least one of the functions
A_{i}
has high rank, or similarly for their analogues for one of the five other kinds of order-
2
slices, then taking subtensors
X\times Y
and
Z\times W
such that
A_{i}(X\times Y)
has high rank and the slices
T_{(x_{i},y_{i})}(Z\times W)
are still separated with thresholds in a sequence of scales ensures that the tripartition rank of
T(X\times Y\times Z\times W)
is large.

4.

If all the functions
A_{i}
have bounded rank, and similarly for their analogues for all five other kinds of order-
2
slices, then we can decompose
T=S+U
with
S
a tensor with bounded tripartition rank and
U
a tensor such that all slices
U_{(x,y)}
with
(x,y)\in Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
have bounded rank. Using an equivalence between the tripartition rank and the (order-
4
) tensor rank we are then able to conclude by applying order-
4
tensor rank subtensors.

We begin as usual with the relevant separation result. Because the tripartition rank is at least as big as the
(2,2)
-partition rank, the following lemma follows from Lemma
9.4
.

Lemma 10.1
.

Let
T
be an order-
4
tensor, and suppose that there exist
(x_{1},y_{1}),\dots,(x_{l},y_{l})\in Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
such that

\rk(\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}T_{(x_{i},y_{i})})\geq l(l-1)+1

for every
a\in\mathbb{F}^{l}\setminus\{0\}
. Then
\trp T\geq l
.

We next want to obtain another condition which guarantees that a tensor has high tripartition rank and will allow us to conclude in the second of the three cases of the proof. To prepare for the proof of this condition we first prove a lemma that states that the order-
2
slices of a tensor with bounded tripartition rank can be well-approximated by a linear combination of a bounded number of order-
2
tensors.

Lemma 10.2
.

Let
l\geq 1
be a positive integer and let
T
be an order-
4
tensor with
\trp T\leq l
. Then there exist functions
A_{1},\dots,A_{l}
:
Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
, each with rank at most
1
, and functions
B_{1},\dots,B_{l}:Q_{3}\times Q_{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
such that

\rk(T_{(x,y)}-\sum_{i=1}^{l}A_{i}(x,y)B_{i})\leq l

for every
(x,y)\in Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
.

Proof.

There exists a decomposition

T(x,y,z,w)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}(x)b_{i}(y)c_{i}(z,w)+\sum_{i=1}^{r^{\prime}}F_{i}(x,y,z,w)

for some nonnegative integers
r,r^{\prime}
with
r+r^{\prime}=\trp T\leq l
, where for each
i\in[r^{\prime}]
, the function
F_{i}
is of one of the five types

\displaystyle a(x)b(z)c(y,w)\text{, }a(x)b(w)c(y,z)\text{, }a(y)b(z)c(x,w)\text{, }a(y)b(w)c(x,z)\text{, }a(z)b(w)c(x,y).

We take
A_{i}=a_{i}
and
B_{i}=c_{i}d_{i}
for each
i\in[r]
. For any given
(x,y)\in Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
and each
i\in[r^{\prime}]
the rank of
(F_{i})_{(x,y)}
is at most
1
, so the desired inequality follows by subadditivity. ∎

We can now deduce our second condition that guarantees that an order-
4
tensor has high tripartition rank.

Proposition 10.3
.

Let
M,m,l^{\prime},l\geq 1
be four integers, let
T:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\times Q_{3}\times Q_{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be an order-
4
tensor, and let functions
A_{1},\dots,A_{l^{\prime}}:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
, functions
B_{1},\dots,B_{l^{\prime}}:Q_{3}\times Q_{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
be such that the three following conditions hold.

(i)

For all
a\in\mathbb{F}^{l^{\prime}}\setminus\{0\}
,
\rk(\sum_{i=1}^{l^{\prime}}a_{i}B_{i})\geq M
.

(ii)

There exists
j\in[l^{\prime}]
such that
\rk A_{j}\geq l
.

(iii)

For all
y\in Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
,
\rk(T_{(x,y)}-\sum_{i=1}^{l^{\prime}}A_{i}(x,y)B_{i})\leq m
.

If
M\geq(m+l)l
, then
\trp T\geq l
.

Proof.

Assume for contradiction that
\trp T<l
. By Lemma
10.2
there exist functions
C_{1},\dots,C_{l-1}:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
and functions
D_{1},\dots,D_{l-1}:Q_{3}\times Q_{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
such that
\rk C_{i}=1
for each
i\in[l-1]
and such that

\rk(T_{(x,y)}-\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}C_{i}(x,y)D_{i})\leq l-1

(13)

for every
(x,y)\in Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
. Then
\rk C_{i}\leq 1
for each
i\in[l-1]
, but by assumption (ii)
\rk A_{j}\geq l
, so by subadditivity of the rank,
A_{j}
does not belong to the linear span of
C_{1},\dots,C_{l-1}
. It follows that there exists a function
u:Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
supported in a subset
U
of
Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
with size at most
l
such that
u.C_{i}=0
for each

but
u.A_{j}\neq 0
. We can write

. On the one hand, applying (
13
), subadditivity, and the fact that
u.C_{i}=0
for each
i\in[l-1]
we obtain

\rk(u.T)\leq(l-1)|U|\leq(l-1)l.

(14)

On the other hand, by assumption (iii) and subadditivity of the rank, we have

\rk(u.T-\sum_{i=1}^{l^{\prime}}(u.A_{i})B_{i})\leq m|U|\leq ml.

Since
u.A_{j}\neq 0
, by applying assumption (i) to
(a_{1},\dots,a_{l^{\prime}})=(u.A_{1},\dots,u.A_{l^{\prime}})
we have
\rk\sum_{i=1}^{l^{\prime}}(u.A_{i})B_{i}\geq M
, and therefore by subadditivity

\rk u.T\geq M-ml.

From this inequality and (
14
) we obtain
(l-1)l\geq M-ml
and therefore
M\leq(m+l-1)l
, a contradiction. ∎

In order to conclude in the third case of our argument we prove an equivalence between the tripartition rank and the tensor rank for order-
4

Proposition 10.4
.

Let
T
be an order-
4
tensor such that
\rk T_{(x,y)}\leq m
,
\rk T_{(x,z)}\leq m
,
\rk T_{(x,w)}\leq m
,
\rk T_{(y,z)}\leq m
,
\rk T_{(y,w)}\leq m

\rk T_{(z,w)}\leq m
for respectively all
(x,y)\in Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
, all
(x,z)\in Q_{1}\times Q_{3}
, all
(x,w)\in Q_{1}\times Q_{4}
, all
(y,z)\in Q_{2}\times Q_{3}
, all
(y,w)\in Q_{2}\times Q_{4}
, all
(z,w)\in Q_{3}\times Q_{4}

Proof.

We start with a decomposition

\displaystyle T(x,y,z,w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r_{12}}A_{12,i}(x,y)B_{12,i}(z,w)+\sum_{i=1}^{r_{13}}A_{13,i}(x,z)B_{12,i}(y,w)+\sum_{i=1}^{r_{14}}A_{14,i}(x,w)B_{12,i}(y,z)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{r_{23}}A_{23,i}(y,z)B_{12,i}(x,w)+\sum_{i=1}^{r_{24}}A_{24,i}(y,w)B_{12,i}(x,z)+\sum_{i=1}^{r_{34}}A_{34,i}(z,w)B_{12,i}(x,y)

where the functions

The functions

are linearly independent, so we can find functions

such that

for every

. Applying

to the decomposition we obtain that for each

,
\rk B_{12,i}\leq r_{12}m+(r_{13}+r_{14}+r_{23}+r_{24}+r_{34})l
, and similar bounds for the ranks of the functions

11
The inductive proof in the general case

11.1
Definitions

In this section we prove Theorem
1.4
in full. For
d\geq 2
an integer and
R
a non-empty family of partitions of
[d]
, we shall say that a set
C\subset[d]
is a
largest part
for
R
if there exists
P\in R
with
C\in P
and if moreover
|C|=\max_{P\in R,I\in P}|I|
. We shall write
R_{\tr}=\{\{\{1\},\dots,\{d\}\}\}
for the family of partitions corresponding to the tensor rank.

Our proof strategy is to proceed by induction, with the base case of the induction being the case
R=R_{\tr}
, for which we already know by Proposition
2.4
that Theorem
1.4
holds. Otherwise, we choose a largest part
C\subset[d]
for
R
that will remain fixed throughout the inductive step. The following notions of rank will be relevant to the proof:

\displaystyle R_{+}

\displaystyle=\{P\in R:C\in P\}

\displaystyle R_{-}

\displaystyle=\{P\in R:C\notin P\}

\displaystyle\Rcomp

\displaystyle=\{P\setminus\{C\}:P\in R_{+}\}

\displaystyle\Rnew

\displaystyle=\{P\cup\{I,J\}:P\in\Rcomp,\{I,J\}\text{ a bipartition of }C\}

\displaystyle R^{\prime}

\displaystyle=R_{-}\cup\Rnew.

We emphasize that although
R_{+},R_{-},R^{\prime},\Rnew
are notions of rank for order-
d
tensors
\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}Q_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
,
\Rcomp
is a notion of rank for tensors
\prod_{\alpha\in C^{c}}Q_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
. We will refer to
R^{\prime}
as the
down-shadow
of
R
(with respect to
C
). Informally, the set
R^{\prime}
is the set
R
, modified such that whenever
C
appears in a partition
P
of
R
, the partition
P
is replaced by all partitions which are identical to
P
in all ways except that the part
C
is split into two non-empty parts. Starting from a non-empty family
R
of partitions of
[d]
, choosing a largest part

for

of

are forbidden by decreasing size.

We now summarise the structure of the inductive step, which can largely be viewed as a generalisation of the structure of the proof in Section
10
. Let

be a non-empty family of partitions of

, and let

tensor with large

-rank.

1.

If the tensor

with

, then by applying Proposition

for
(|C|,\pr)
we can restrict this set of slices to a product

such that the restrictions of the slices to this product are still separated and the sets

,
\alpha\in C

2.

If the tensor

If at least one of the functions

4.

If all the functions

Whenever

\matfrank A(X^{1}\times X^{2})\geq l
. Let
X_{1,\alpha}
be the projection of
X^{1}
on to the
(1,\alpha)
th coordinate axis for each
\alpha=1,\dots,d_{1}
, and similarly let
X_{2,\alpha}
be the projection of
X^{2}
on to the
(2,\alpha)
th coordinate axis for each
\alpha=1,\dots,d_{2}
. The tensor

T^{\prime}=T(X_{1,1}\times\dots\times X_{1,d_{1}}\times X_{2,1}\times\dots\times X_{2,d_{2}})

satisfies
\matfrank T^{\prime}\geq l
and hence

\Rprodrk T^{\prime}\geq l.

Case 2
: We have
\matfrank T\leq l
. We then let
l^{\prime}=\matfrank T
. There exist tensors
T_{1,1},\dots,T_{1,l^{\prime}}:\prod_{\alpha=a}^{d_{1}}Q_{1,\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
and tensors
T_{2,1},\dots,T_{2,l^{\prime}}:\prod_{\alpha=a}^{d_{2}}Q_{2,\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
such that we can write

T(x_{1,1}\dots,x_{1,d_{1}},x_{2,1},\dots,x_{2,d_{2}})=\sum_{i=1}^{l^{\prime}}T_{1,i}(x_{1,1},\dots,x_{1,d_{1}})T_{2,i}(x_{2,1},\dots,x_{2,d_{2}})

for all
x_{1,1}\in Q_{1,1},\dots,x_{1,d_{1}}\in Q_{1,d_{1}},x_{2,1}\in Q_{2,1},\dots,x_{2,d_{2}}\in Q_{2,d_{2}}
. Moreover, the families of tensors
\{T_{1,1},\dots T_{1,l^{\prime}}\}
and
\{T_{2,1},\dots T_{2,l^{\prime}}\}
are both linearly independent (if they were not, then we would have
\matfrank T<l^{\prime}
). For each
i\in[l^{\prime}]
, by definition of the
(R_{1}\times R_{2})
-rank we have

\Rprodrk(T_{1,i}T_{2,i})\leq\Ronerk T_{1,i}\Rtwork T_{2,i}

so by subadditivity

\Rprodrk T\leq\sum_{i=1}^{l^{\prime}}\Ronerk T_{1,i}\Rtwork T_{2,i}.

Since
\Rprodrk T\geq lG_{d_{1},R_{1}}(l)G_{d_{2},R_{2}}(l)
and
l^{\prime}\leq l
, there exists
i\in[l^{\prime}]
such that
\Ronerk T_{1,i}\geq G_{d_{1},R_{1}}(l)
or
\Rtwork T_{2,i}\geq G_{d_{2},R_{2}}(l)
. Without loss of generality let us assume that
\Ronerk T_{1,i}\geq G_{d,R_{1}}(l)
. Because the family
\{T_{2,1},\dots,T_{2,l^{\prime}}\}
is linearly independent there exists a function
u:\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d_{2}}Q_{2,\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
supported inside a subset
U
of
\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d_{2}}Q_{2,\alpha}
with size at most
l^{\prime}\leq l
such that
u.T_{2,i^{\prime}}=1_{i^{\prime}=i}
for all
i^{\prime}\in[l^{\prime}]
, so
u.T=T_{1,i}
. For each
\alpha\in[d_{2}]
let
X_{2,\alpha}
be the projection of
U
on the
(2,\alpha)
th coordinate axis. The sets
X_{2,\alpha}
all have size at most
l^{\prime}\leq l
. By Theorem
1.4
for
(d_{1},R_{1})
there exist sets
X_{1,\alpha},\alpha\in[d_{1}]
with size at most
F_{d_{1},R_{1}}(l)
and such that

\Ronerk T_{1,i}(X_{1,1}\times\dots\times X_{1,d_{1}})\geq l.

Letting

T^{\prime}=T(X_{1,1}\times\dots\times X_{1,d_{1}}\times X_{2,1}\times\dots\times X_{2,d_{2}})

we have
\Ronerk u.T^{\prime}\geq l
. This ensures that
\Rprodrk T\geq l
: indeed if we can write

T^{\prime}(x_{1,1}\dots,x_{1,d_{1}},x_{2,1},\dots,x_{2,d_{2}})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}T_{1,i}^{\prime}(x_{1,1}\dots,x_{1,d_{1}})T_{2,i}^{\prime}(x_{2,1},\dots,x_{2,d_{2}})

for some tensors
T_{1,i}^{\prime}:\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d_{1}}Q_{1,\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
,
T_{2,i}^{\prime}:\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d_{2}}Q_{2,\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
and some positive integer
r
then

u.T^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}T_{i,1}^{\prime}(u.T_{i,2}^{\prime})

and hence
\Ronerk u.T^{\prime}\leq r
.∎

Using induction on
D
by applying the previous proposition to
R_{1}\times\dots\times R_{D-1}
and
R_{D}
we obtain the following bounds.

Corollary 12.4
.

Let
D\geq 1
,
d_{1},\dots,d_{D}\geq 2
be positive integers, let
R_{1},\dots,R_{D}
be families of non-empty partitions of respectively
[d_{1}]
,…,
[d_{D}]
, and let
R=R_{1}\times\dots\times R_{D}
. If for each
j\in[D]
Theorem
1.4
holds for
(d_{j},R_{j})
with bounds
F_{d_{j},R_{j}}
and

then Theorem
1.4

(d_{1}+\dots+d_{D},R_{1}\times\dots\times R_{D})
with the bounds

F_{d_{1}+\dots+d_{D},R_{1}\times\dots\times R_{D}}(l)=\max(l,\max_{1\leq j\leq D}F_{d_{j},R_{j}}(l))

Let

13
Open problems

The results in this paper still leave open a number of related strengthenings. The bounds that we obtain for Theorem
1.4
are very probably suboptimal: the functions
F_{d,R}
and
G_{d,R}
that we obtain are merely those resulting from the current proof, and nothing particularly suggests that they are close to the optimal bounds. On the contrary, our guess would be that it is possible to take both
F_{d,R}(l)
and
G_{d,R}(l)
to be linear in
l
.

Conjecture 13.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
be an integer and let
R
be a non-empty family of partitions of
[d]
. Then there exist constants
A(d,R),B(d,R)>0
such that whenever
T:\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}Q_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
is an order-
d
tensor with
\Rrk T\geq l
, there exist
X_{1}\subset Q_{1},\dots,X_{d}\subset Q_{d}
with size at most
A(d,R)l
such that

\Rrk T(X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{d})\geq B(d,R)l.

Aside from an improvement of our current bounds to linear bounds, there are two additional statements involving subtensors that we would expect to be true. The first involves obtaining a subtensor of bounded size with the same rank as the original tensor, and the second involves obtaining a full-rank subtensor (that is, of rank equal to the sizes of each of the
d
sets) of size tending to infinity with the rank of the original tensor.

Conjecture 13.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
be an integer, and let
R
be a non-empty family of partitions of
[d]
. Then there exists a function
F_{d,R,\mathrm{same}}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that whenever
T:\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}Q_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
is an order-
d
tensor with
\Rrk T\geq l
, there exist
X_{1}\subset Q_{1},\dots,X_{d}\subset Q_{d}
with size at most
F_{d,R,\mathrm{same}}(l)
such that

\Rrk T(X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{d})\geq l.

Conjecture 13.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
be an integer, and let
R
be a non-empty family of partitions of
[d]
. There exists a function
G_{d,R,\mathrm{full}}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that whenever
T:\prod_{\alpha=1}^{d}Q_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
is an order-
d
tensor with
\Rrk T\geq G_{d,R,\mathrm{full}}(l)

X_{1}\subset Q_{1},\dots,X_{d}\subset Q_{d}

It is worth noting that conjectures
13.1

13.2

13.3

Let

Conjecture 13.6
.

Let
d\geq 2
be an integer, and let
R

[d]

In the case of the slice rank (and, similarly, of the partition rank) a better bound than
\dsr T\geq\esr T/d

\esr T

Q_{1}=\dots=Q_{d}=[n]

d

d

In the case of the tensor rank, for any positive integer

(d+1)!

and for every positive integer

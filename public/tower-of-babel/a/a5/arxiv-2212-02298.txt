Modular Structure and Inclusions of Twisted Araki-Woods Algebras
Ricardo Correa da Silva, Gandalf Lechner

Abstract
. In the general setting of twisted second quantization (including Bose/Fermi second quantization, S S -symmetric Fock spaces, and full Fock spaces from free probability as special cases), von Neumann algebras on twisted Fock spaces are analyzed. These twisted Araki-Woods algebras ℒ T ​ ( H ) \mathcal{L}_{T}(H) depend on the twist operator T T and a standard subspace H H in the one-particle space. Under a compatibility assumption on T T and H H , it is proven that the Fock vacuum is cyclic and separating for ℒ T ​ ( H ) \mathcal{L}_{T}(H) if and only if T T satisfies a standard subspace version of crossing symmetry and the Yang-Baxter equation (braid equation). In this case, the Tomita-Takesaki modular data are explicitly determined. Inclusions ℒ T ​ ( K ) ⊂ ℒ T ​ ( H ) \mathcal{L}_{T}(K)\subset\mathcal{L}_{T}(H) of twisted Araki-Woods algebras are analyzed in two cases: If the inclusion is half-sided modular and the twist satisfies a norm bound, it is shown to be singular. If the inclusion of underlying standard subspaces K ⊂ H K\subset H satisfies an L 2 L^{2} -nuclearity condition, ℒ T ​ ( K ) ⊂ ℒ T ​ ( H ) \mathcal{L}_{T}(K)\subset\mathcal{L}_{T}(H) has type III relative commutant for suitable twists T T . Applications of these results to localization of observables in algebraic quantum field theory are discussed.



1.
Introduction

Second quantization von Neumann algebras play a prominent role in many areas of mathematical physics and mathematics and exist in numerous variations: Weyl algebras and their weak closures describe Bosonic interaction-free systems such as Bose gases or free quantum field theories, and similarly CAR algebras describe the corresponding Fermionic models
[
BR97
,
Pet90
]
. Whereas these algebras are naturally represented on Bose/Fermi Fock spaces, there exist also more general Fock spaces that are useful for describing generalized statistics, including anyons
[
LM95a
,
DKLP20
]
. Very similar spaces – sometimes called
S
-symmetric Fock spaces – arise from other representations of symmetric groups on tensor powers and form convenient representation spaces for integrable quantum field theories with prescribed two-body scattering operator
S
, and carry families of von Neumann algebras generalizing the CCR/CAR setting
[
Lec08
,
AL17
]
. Related constructions also occur in the representation theory of Wick algebras, where the canonical (anti-)commutation relations are deformed
[
JSW95
,
JPS01
]
.

Another variation of second quantization algebras arises in free probability: Here one considers representations of free group factors
[
Voi85
]
and other von Neumann algebras
[
Shl97
]
(free Araki-Woods factors) on unsymmetrized (“Boltzmann” or “full”) Fock spaces, and this generalizes to
q
-deformed Araki-Woods von Neumann algebras, interpolating between the Bose
(q=1)
and Fermi
(q=-1)
situations, and containing the free factors at
q=0
as a special case.

In this article, we adopt a general framework that includes all these examples and goes beyond the setting of Fock spaces symmetrized by symmetric group actions. Our main goal is to study natural families of von Neumann algebras and their modular and inclusion properties in this setting. As our analysis is motivated from quantum field theory, we focus on properties of the Fock vacuum state on these algebras, and consider the structure of inclusions of such von Neumann algebras rather than their internal structure.

The basic setup we use is due to Bożejko and Speicher
[
BS94
]
and Jørgensen, Schmitt, and Werner
[
JSW95
]
and can briefly be described as follows: Starting from a one-particle Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and a bounded selfadjoint operator

satisfying a certain positivity condition, one can construct a “

-twisted Fock space”

which specializes to the Bose, Fermi, Boltzmann, or

-symmetric Fock spaces for suitable choices of

Interesting von Neumann algebras

are generated by “field operators”

. Here

For general twist

In Section

We discuss our results from this point of view, review the resulting perspectives on constructive algebraic quantum field theory, and give an outlook to ongoing research.

2.
Twisted Fock spaces and Araki-Woods algebras

2.1.
Twists and twisted Fock spaces

The von Neumann algebras we are interested in are defined on twisted Fock spaces. These Fock spaces arise as natural representation spaces of algebras defined by a quadratic exchange relation (Wick algebras) and exist in various versions. The version that we use here is the most general one and due to Bożejko and Speicher

]

3.1.

For

Note that

In the present section, we do not rely on the diagram notation in our proofs, but still regard it as a helpful tool to keep track of the various contributions to

.

Let

\displaystyle W_{2n}^{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{2n}}(t-i)

\displaystyle=W_{2n}^{\Delta_{H}^{it}\xi_{2n},\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{2n-1}}=:(W_{2n}^{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{2n}})^{\prime}(t),\hskip 20.00003ptt\in\mathbb{R}.

Subsequently we will often be concerned with analytic functions of the form (

We write
B(\mathbb{R},{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}))
for the set of bounded functions

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
Z_{i}:\mathcal{D}(Z_{i})\to\mathcal{H}
,
i=1,2
be closed antilinear operators,
D_{i}\subset\mathcal{D}(Z_{i})
subsets of the topological space
\mathcal{D}(Z_{i})
provided with the graph norm,
E_{1},E_{2}\subset\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
,
\mathcal{F}\subset B(\mathbb{R},{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes(n+1)}))
subsets, and

f:\mathcal{F}\times D_{1}\times E_{1}\times D_{2}\times E_{2}\to{\mathbb{H}}^{\infty}_{\rm c.bv.}({\mathbb{S}}_{\alpha}).

If there exists a continuous map
\Theta:\mathcal{F}\to B(\mathbb{R},{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes(n+1)}))
such that

(3.13)

\displaystyle f(R,\xi_{1},\Psi,\xi_{2},\Phi)(t)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\xi_{1}\otimes\Psi,R(t)\Phi\otimes\xi_{2}\right\rangle,

(3.14)

\displaystyle f(R,\xi_{1},\Psi,\xi_{2},\Phi)(t+i\alpha)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Psi\otimes Z_{2}\xi_{2},\Theta(R)(t)Z_{1}\xi_{1}\otimes\Phi\right\rangle,

then
f
is separately continuous. In particular,
f
can be uniquely extended to

\overline{\operatorname{span}\mathcal{F}}\times\overline{\operatorname{span}D_{1}}\times\overline{\operatorname{span}E_{1}}\times\overline{\operatorname{span}D_{2}}\times\overline{\operatorname{span}E_{2}},

with closures taken in the respective topologies, and the extension still satisfies (
3.13
) and (
3.14
).

Proof.

By linearity and antilinearity, it is obvious that we can extend
f
to
\operatorname{span}\mathcal{F}\times\operatorname{span}D_{1}\times\operatorname{span}E_{1}\times\operatorname{span}D_{2}\times\operatorname{span}E_{2}
. Hence, we can suppose,
\mathcal{F}
,
D_{1},D_{2},E_{1}
, and
E_{2}
are vector spaces.

Fix
R
and, for the sake of notation, let us omit the dependence on
R
and denote
M=(\|\xi_{1}\|+\|Z_{1}\xi_{1}\|)(\|\xi_{2}\|+\|Z_{2}\xi_{2}\|)\|\Psi\|\|\Phi\|
. By the three lines theorem it follows that,
z\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle|f(\xi_{1},\Psi,\xi_{2},\Phi)(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{|f(\xi_{1},\Psi,\xi_{2},\Phi)(t)|\right\},\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\left|f(\xi_{1},\Psi,\xi_{2},\Phi)(t+i\alpha)\right|\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|R(t)\|+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\Theta(R)(t)\|\right)M.

Since
f
depends linearly or antilinearly on all its four variables, it is continuous in them separately in the appropriate topologies. Therefore, we can continuously extend
f
to
\overline{\operatorname{span}D_{1}}\times\overline{\operatorname{span}E_{1}}\times\overline{\operatorname{span}D_{2}}\times\overline{\operatorname{span}E_{2}}
, since
{\mathbb{H}}^{\infty}_{\rm c.bv.}({\mathbb{S}}_{\alpha})
is a Banach space.

For the continuity in
R
, let us now omit the dependence on the fixed vectors
\xi_{1}
,
\Psi
,
\xi_{2}
, and
\Phi
and let
M
be as above. Again by the three line theorem, it follows that

\displaystyle|f(R)(z)-f(S)(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{|f(R)(t)-f(S)(t)|\right\},\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{|f(R)(t+i\alpha)-f(S)(t+i\alpha)|\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|R(t)-S(t)\|,\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|(\Theta(R)-\Theta(S))(t)\|\right\}M,

and the conclusion follows from the continuity of
\Theta
.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

In quantum field theoretic scattering theory, crossing symmetry is a property stating that the scattering amplitude of particles is related to the amplitude of the corresponding antiparticles by analytic continuation
[
Mar69
, Sect. IV]
. Whereas this property has not been proven in general quantum field theory (see
[
BEG65
]
for a proof of crossing for two-particle amplitudes, and
[
Miz21
]
for recent work towards general crossing conditions in perturbative QFT), it is well established – and often taken as an axiom – in integrable QFT on two-dimensional Minkowski spacetime
[
Iag78
,
Smi92
,
AAR01
,
Sch10
]
.

In the setting of Example
3.14
, our abstract form of crossing symmetry specializes to the crossing symmetry of scattering theory in integrable models, with
S
playing the role of elastic two-body scattering matrix,
\theta
is the rapidity and the modular conjugation
J_{K}
corresponds to conjugating a particle into an antiparticle
[
AL17
]
. See also
[
BT15
,
HL18
]
for previous work relating standard subspaces and crossing symmetry, and
[
Nie98
]
for a proof of the cyclic formfactor equation, related to crossing symmetry, from modular theory.

We now proceed to show that the Yang-Baxter equation and crossing symmetry are not only necessary, but also
sufficient
conditions for
\Omega
being separating for
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)
. This amounts to establishing a large commutant of
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)
.

It is instructive to first look at the case of zero twist
T=0
, with
\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathcal{H})
the full Fock space over
\mathcal{H}
. In this case, one has in addition to the “left” creation and annihilation operators (

) also “right” creation and annihilation operators, namely (
\xi,\psi_{j}\in\mathcal{H}
)

(3.37)

\displaystyle a_{R}^{*}(\xi)(\psi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\psi_{n})

\displaystyle:=\psi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\psi_{n}\otimes\xi,

(3.38)

\displaystyle a_{R}(\xi)(\psi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\psi_{n})

\displaystyle:=\langle\xi,\psi_{n}\rangle\cdot\psi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\psi_{n-1}.

It is easy to see that the left field opeartors
\phi_{0,L}(h)=a_{L}(h)+a_{L}^{*}(h)
and right field operators
\phi_{0,R}(h^{\prime})=a_{R}(h^{\prime})+a_{R}^{*}(h^{\prime})
commute if and only if
\operatorname{Im}\langle h,h^{\prime}\rangle=0
, i.e. if
h\in H
and
h^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}
for some standard subspace
H
. Hence
\mathcal{L}_{0}(H)^{\prime}
contains the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{R}_{0}(H^{\prime})
generated by right fields
\phi_{0,R}(h^{\prime})
,
h^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}
. As
\Omega
is also cyclic for
\mathcal{R}_{0}(H^{\prime})
, it is separating for
\mathcal{L}_{0}(H)
.

Furthermore, the natural unitary involution

(3.39)

\displaystyle Y:\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathcal{H})

(3.40)

\displaystyle Y(\psi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\psi_{n})

\displaystyle:=\psi_{n}\otimes\ldots\otimes\psi_{1},

relates the left and right operators according to
Ya_{L}^{\#}(\xi)Y=a^{\#}_{R}(\xi)
, which leads to
Y\mathcal{L}_{0}(H)Y=\mathcal{R}_{0}(H)
; this involution enters the modular conjugation of
(\mathcal{L}_{0}(H),\Omega)
in this case
[
Shl97
, p. 341]
.

In comparison, in our general
T
-twisted setting there exist no “right” operators. This is due to the fact that the very definition of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})
is biased towards the left because of the appearance of
(1\otimes P_{T,n})
instead of
(P_{T,n}\otimes 1)
in the recursive definition of

(

).

However, in the case of a

twist, the symmetry between left and right is restored. This observation will be a key ingredient to proving that
\Omega
separates
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)
.

Lemma 3.16

Let

a)

For any

\displaystyle P_{T,n+1}

c)

Let

\displaystyle=a_{R,T}^{\#}(Z\xi),\hskip 20.00003pt\xi\in\mathcal{D}(Z).

Proof.

a) We begin with a calculation in the group algebras of the symmetric groups

Remark 3.18

a)

In complete analogy to Lemma

b)

Definition 3.19
.

A twist

Lemma 3.20
.

Let
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\geq}
and suppose
\Psi_{n}\otimes\xi\in\operatorname{ker}P_{T,n+1}
whenever
\Psi_{n}\in\operatorname{ker}P_{T,n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, for
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, and
\Psi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}

(3.50)

\displaystyle[a_{L,T}^{\star}(\xi),a_{R,T}^{\star}(\eta)]

\displaystyle=0,\hskip 20.00003pt[a_{L,T}(\xi),a_{R,T}(\eta)]=0,

(3.51)

\displaystyle[a_{L,T}(\xi),a_{R,T}^{\star}(\eta)]\Omega

\displaystyle=\left\langle\xi,\eta\right\rangle\Omega,

(3.52)

\displaystyle[a_{L,T}(\xi),a_{R,T}^{\star}(\eta)][\Psi_{n}]

\displaystyle=[a_{L}(\xi)(T_{1}\cdots T_{n})(\Psi_{n}\otimes\eta)].

Furthermore if

is a braided twist, we have in addition

\displaystyle[a_{R,T}(\xi),a_{L,T}^{\star}(\eta)][\Psi_{n}]

\displaystyle=[a_{R}(\xi)T_{n}\cdots T_{1}(\eta\otimes\Psi_{n})],

(3.53)

\displaystyle[\phi_{L,T}(\xi),\phi_{R,T}(\eta)]\Omega

\displaystyle=2i\operatorname{Im}\left\langle\xi,\eta\right\rangle\Omega,

\displaystyle[\phi_{L,T}(\xi),\phi_{R,T}(\eta)][\Psi_{n}]=[a_{L}(\xi)T_{1}

\displaystyle\cdots T_{n}(\Psi_{n}\otimes\eta)-a_{R}(\eta)T_{n}\cdots T_{1}(\xi\otimes\Psi_{n})].

Proof.

As discussed above, the condition involving kernels guarantees
a_{R,T}^{\star}(\xi)
to exist as an operator on
\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})
. It is easy to see that the left and right creation operators commute and, by taking adjoints, the left and right annihilation also commute. For the mixed relation we get

\displaystyle[a_{L,T}(\xi),a_{R,T}^{\star}(\eta)][\Psi_{n}]

\displaystyle=[a_{L}(\xi)R_{T,n+1}(\Psi_{n}\otimes\eta)]-[(a_{L}(\xi)R_{T,n}\Psi_{n})\otimes\eta]

\displaystyle=[a_{L}(\xi)T_{1}\cdots T_{n}(\Psi_{n}\otimes\eta)],

where we have used (
2.16
) and
R_{T,n+1}=R_{T,n}\otimes 1+T_{1}\cdots T_{n}
.

In case
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\geq}
is a braided twist, Lemma
3.16
ensures the existence of the right creation operators and fields. One can easily calculate the commutator acting on the vacuum directly. In addition, we have (
3.44
) and
\tilde{R}_{T,n+1}=1\otimes\tilde{R}_{T,n}+T_{n}\cdots T_{1}
. Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle[a_{R,T}(\eta),a_{L,T}^{\star}(\xi)][\Psi_{n}]

\displaystyle=[a_{R}(\eta)\tilde{R}_{T,n+1}(\xi\otimes\Psi_{n})]-[\xi\otimes(a_{R}(\eta)\tilde{R}_{T,n}\Psi_{n})]

\displaystyle=[a_{R}(\eta)T_{n}\cdots T_{1}(\xi\otimes\Psi_{n})].

Combining these two mixed commutators immediately yields the claimed formula for
[\phi_{L,T}(\xi),\phi_{R,T}(\eta)][\Psi_{n}]
.
∎

One can easily see that whenever the right fields are well defined on
\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})
—
i
.e.,
\Psi_{n}\in\operatorname{ker}P_{T,n}
implies
\Psi_{n}\otimes\xi\in\operatorname{ker}P_{T,n+1}

, and

—it follows from the fields commutator in Lemma

that

is local if, and only if, for all

,

and

,

(3.54)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}\ni a_{L}(h)T_{1}\cdots T_{n}(\Psi_{n}\otimes h^{\prime})-a_{R}(h^{\prime})T_{n}\cdots T_{1}(h\otimes\Psi_{n})\in\operatorname{ker}P_{T,n}.

Comparing to the zero twist case, this imposes an additional constraint on an operator

to be local. An example worth mentioning is

, for which

for all

Proposition 3.21
.

For a braided twist
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\geq}
to be local w.r.t. a standard subspace

it is sufficient that

\displaystyle\langle h\otimes\Psi_{n},T_{1}\cdots T_{n}(\Phi_{n}\otimes h^{\prime})\rangle=\langle\Psi_{n}\otimes h^{\prime},T_{n}\cdots T_{1}(h\otimes\Phi_{n})\rangle

for all

,

,
h^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}
, and all

.
Furthermore, in case

, (
3.55

Proof.

First notice that (
3.55
) is equivalent to, for all
\Phi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
,

, and
h^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}\ni a_{L}(h)T_{1}\cdots T_{n}(\Phi_{n}\otimes h^{\prime})-a_{R}(h^{\prime})T_{n}\cdots T_{1}(h\otimes\Phi_{n})=0.

Now, Lemma
3.16
ensures the existence of the right fields and Lemma
3.20
yields

\displaystyle[\phi_{L,T}(h),\phi_{R,T}(h^{\prime})][\Phi_{n}]=[a_{L}(h)T_{1}\cdots T_{n}(\Phi_{n}\otimes h^{\prime})-a_{R}(h^{\prime})T_{n}\cdots T_{1}(h\otimes\Phi_{n})]=0.

In case

.

Proof.

a) We will verify (

4.1.
Half-sided modular inclusions

Half-sided modular inclusions were introduced by Wiesbrock
[
Wie93b
,
Wie93a
]
and are of prominent importance in conformal quantum field theory. They are defined as follows.

Definition 4.3
.

An inclusion of two von Neumann algebras
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
with a joint cyclic separating vector
\Omega
is called half-sided modular if the modular unitaries
\Delta^{it}
of
(\mathcal{M},\Omega)
satisfy

(4.9)

\displaystyle\Delta^{it}\mathcal{N}\Delta^{-it}\subset\mathcal{N},\hskip 20.00003ptt\leq 0.

For a non-trivial half-sided inclusion
(\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M},\Omega)
, there exists no conditional expectation
\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
(hence no meaningful notion of index), and the inclusion is not split. Hence no standard methods for investigating the relative commutant of a half-sided inclusion are available.

The structure of a half-sided modular inclusion is closely related to that of a one-dimensional Borchers triple, that we also recall here
[
BLS11
]
.

Definition 4.4
.

A one-dimensional Borchers triple
(\mathcal{M},U,\Omega)
consists of a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, a strongly continuous unitary one-parameter group
U
with positive generator
{\mathrm{P}}
, and a unit vector

such that

a)

\Omega
is cyclic and separating for
\mathcal{M}
, and
U(x)\Omega=\Omega
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

b)

U(x)\mathcal{M}U(x)^{-1}\subset\mathcal{M}
for
x\geq 0
.

Let us recall that for a one-dimensional Borchers triple
(\mathcal{M},U,\Omega)
, Borchers’ Theorem
[
Bor92
]
asserts that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\Delta^{it}U(x)\Delta^{-it}=U(e^{-2\pi t}x),\hskip 10.00002ptJU(x)J=U(-x),\hskip 20.00003ptt,x\in\mathbb{R},

where

are the modular data of
(\mathcal{M},\Omega)
. Thus

extends to a (anti)unitary representation of the affine group of

(the “

As a consequence of (
4.10

{\mathcal{N}}:=U(1)\mathcal{M}U(-1)\subset\mathcal{M}
coming from a Borchers triple is half-sided modular. Conversely, given a half-sided modular inclusion
({\mathcal{N}}\subset\mathcal{M},\Omega)
, there exists a strongly continuous unitary one-parameter group

such that

is a one-dimensional Borchers triple, and
{\mathcal{N}}=U(1)\mathcal{M}U(-1)

[
Wie93b
,

. This one-parameter group is related to the modular unitaries of

and

by

(4.11)

\displaystyle U(e^{2\pi t}-1)=\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}^{-it}\Delta_{\mathcal{N}}^{it}.

We may therefore use the structure of one-dimensional Borchers triples to define half-sided modular inclusions.

We will say that a half-sided inclusion has

if the subspace of

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((a,b)):=U(a)\mathcal{M}U(a)^{*}\cap U(b)\mathcal{M}^{\prime}U(b)^{*},

as well as the

,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\infty}:=\bigcap_{I}\mathcal{A}(I)^{\prime}.

Then the following holds.

Proposition 4.7
.

[
LS22
]

Let
(\mathcal{M},U,\Omega)
be a one-dimensional Borchers triple with unique vacuum. Let

denote the modular data of

, and
\mathcal{N}:=U(1)\mathcal{M}U(1)^{-1}\subset\mathcal{M}

a)

The relative commutants
\mathcal{A}((0,x))=\mathcal{M}\cap U(x)\mathcal{M}^{\prime}U(x)^{*}
,

b)

If

is such that

exists, then

.

We now apply this technique to the half-sided inclusions
\mathcal{L}_{T}(K)\subset\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)
,

, defined by Prop.
4.6
. For

, the relative commutant

is known to be non-trivial for

, and for various symmetric twists it is expected to be non-trivial.

We begin with a preparatory lemma which does not rely on the half-sided structure. The lemma says that an operator
A
commuting with left
and
right fields is necessarily quite complicated in the sense that

cannot be a finite particle number vector. Here we only need to assume that

is strict (Def.

).

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
H,K\subset\mathcal{H}
be standard subspaces with
\operatorname{dim}K\geq 2
and let
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{>}(H)
be a strict twist. Let
A\in\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{R}_{T}(K)^{\prime}
,
A\neq 0
. If

(4.14)

\displaystyle n(A):=\sup\{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\,\colon\,[A\Omega]_{n}\neq 0\}<\infty,

then
A\in\mathbb{C}1
.

Proof.

The supremum
n(A)
exists because
A\neq 0
and
\Omega
separates the intersection
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{R}_{T}(K)^{\prime}
(it even separates
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)^{\prime}
because

is standard, see Lemma

), hence
A\Omega\neq 0
. We have to show
n(A)=0
and proceed by contradiction, assuming
n(A)>0
.

By assumption,
A
commutes with
\phi^{H}_{L,T}(\xi)
and
\phi^{K}_{R,T}(\eta)
for all
\xi\in H+iH
and all
\eta\in K+iK
, i.e.

\displaystyle A\xi

\displaystyle=A\phi^{H}_{L,T}(\xi)\Omega=\phi^{H}_{L,T}(\xi)A\Omega=a_{L,T}^{\star}(\xi)A\Omega+a_{L,T}(S_{H}\xi)A\Omega,

\displaystyle A\eta

\displaystyle=A\phi^{K}_{R,T}(\eta)\Omega=\phi^{K}_{R,T}(\eta)A\Omega=a_{R,T}^{\star}(\eta)A\Omega+a_{R,T}(S_{K}\eta)A\Omega.

Since
[A\Omega]_{n(A)+2}=0
, projecting these equations onto
\mathcal{H}_{T,n(A)+1}
gives (note that
\operatorname{ker}P_{T,m}=0
for all

because

, hence we don’t have to project onto a quotient)

\displaystyle(A\xi)_{n(A)+1}

\displaystyle=a_{L,T}^{\star}(\xi)(A\Omega)_{n(A)}+a_{L,T}(S_{H}\xi)(A\Omega)_{n(A)+2}=\xi\otimes(A\Omega)_{n(A)},

\displaystyle(A\eta)_{n(A)+1}

\displaystyle=a_{R,T}^{\star}(\eta)(A\Omega)_{n(A)}+a_{R,T}(S_{K}\eta)(A\Omega)_{n(A)+2}=(A\Omega)_{n(A)}\otimes\eta.

As
H
is standard, for each
\eta\in K+iK
we can find a sequence
(\xi^{\eta}_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H+iH
such that
\|\xi^{\eta}_{l}-\eta\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0
as

. By continuity and the first of the two equations above, we then have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(A\xi^{\eta}_{l})_{n(A)+1}-\xi^{\eta}_{l}\otimes(A\Omega)_{n(A)}\longrightarrow(A\eta)_{n(A)+1}-\eta\otimes(A\Omega)_{n(A)}

for

. Comparing this equation with the second equation above yields

\displaystyle\eta\otimes(A\Omega)_{n(A)}=(A\Omega)_{n(A)}\otimes\eta\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }\,\eta\in K+iK,

which is impossible because
(A\Omega)_{n(A)}\neq 0
and
\operatorname{dim}K>1
.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
H\subset\mathcal{H}
be a standard subspace and
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\geq}(H)
a braided crossing-symmetric twist with
\|T\|<1
. Let
U
be a translation group compatible with
H
and
T
, and set
K:=U(1)H\subset H
.

Then the half-sided modular inclusion
\mathcal{L}_{T}(K)\subset\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)
has trivial relative commutant.

Proof.

The idea is to use Prop.
4.7
, namely to establish that the vacuum projection
P_{\Omega}
lies in the algebra at infinity. The easiest way to achieve this would be to realize it as a weak limit of
\Delta^{it}\phi_{L,T}(k)\phi_{R,T}(k^{\prime})\Delta^{-it}
as
t\to-\infty
(with
k\in K
,
k^{\prime}\in J_{H}K
), see Prop.
4.7

b)
.

Hence we take
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
such that
k:=U(1)h_{1}\in K
and
k^{\prime}:=U(-1)J_{H}h_{2}
satisfy
\langle k,k^{\prime}\rangle=1
(which is possible because
H
is cyclic), and consider the field operators

\displaystyle\phi_{L,T}(k)

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{L}_{T}(K)=\Gamma_{T}(U(1))\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)\Gamma_{T}(U(1))^{\star},

\displaystyle\phi_{R,T}(k^{\prime})

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{R}_{T}(J_{H}U(1)H)=J(\Gamma_{T}(U(1))\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)\Gamma_{T}(U(1))^{\star})J;

these are bounded operators because
\|T\|<1
.

In view of the form of the modular unitaries
\Delta^{it}
of
(\mathcal{L}_{T}(H),\Omega)
(Prop.
3.25
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{it}\phi_{L,T}(k)\phi_{R,T}(k^{\prime})\Delta^{-it}

\displaystyle=\phi_{L,T}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k)\phi_{R,T}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=a_{L,T}^{\star}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k)a_{R,T}^{\star}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k^{\prime})+a_{L,T}^{\star}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k)a_{R,T}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+a_{L,T}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k)a_{R,T}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k^{\prime})+a_{L,T}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k)a_{R,T}^{\star}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k^{\prime}).

To investigate the limit
t\to-\infty
, we first recall that
\Delta_{H}
has purely absolutely continuous spectrum and hence
\Delta_{H}^{it}\to 0
weakly as
t\to-\infty
by the Riemann-Lebesgue–Lemma. This is a consequence of the commutation relations (
4.10
) and
\operatorname{ker}{\mathrm{P}}=\{0\}
, which imply that
\log P
and
\log\Delta_{H}
satisfy canonical commutation relations and are hence a multiple of the Schrödinger representation
[
Lon08
, Prop. 1.7.1]
.

This implies that for any
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
, we have
a_{L,T}(\Delta_{H}^{it}\xi)\to 0
and
a_{R,T}(\Delta_{H}^{it}\xi)\to 0
in the strong operator topology as
t\to-\infty
(evident on vectors of the form
\psi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\psi_{n}
, then apply uniform boundedness – note that our assumptions imply that
T
is strict, i.e.
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}=\mathcal{H}_{T,n}
).

Taking into account the uniform bound (
2.18
), we see that the first three summands converge weakly to zero as
t\to-\infty
. With Lemma
3.20
, the last term may be rewritten on a vector
\Psi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
as

\displaystyle a_{L,T}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k)a_{R,T}^{\star}(

\displaystyle\Delta_{H}^{it}k^{\prime})\Psi_{n}=a_{R,T}^{\star}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k^{\prime})a_{L,T}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k)\Psi_{n}

\displaystyle\;+\langle\Delta_{H}^{it}k,\Delta_{H}^{it}k^{\prime}\rangle E_{0}\Psi_{n}+a_{L}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k)T_{1}\cdots T_{n}(E_{0}^{\perp}\Psi_{n}\otimes\Delta_{H}^{it}k^{\prime}).

Here the first term
a^{\star}a
converges weakly to zero as
t\to-\infty
, and the second term equals
E_{0}\Psi_{n}
for all
t
.

Since
\phi_{L,T}(k)\phi_{R,T}(k^{\prime})
is a bounded operator and the unit ball in
{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H}))
is compact in the weak operator topology,
\Delta^{it}\phi_{L,T}(k)\phi_{R,T}(k^{\prime})\Delta^{-it}
has a weak limit for

along a suitably chosen net
(t_{j})_{j}
going to

. By construction, the limit lies in the algebra at infinity

(

Hence we conclude that

\displaystyle W:=\wlim_{j\to\infty}a_{L}(\Delta_{H}^{it_{j}}k)\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}T_{1}\cdots T_{n}\,a_{R}^{\star}(\Delta_{H}^{it_{j}}k^{\prime})E_{0}^{\perp}

exists as a bounded operator and

. Moreover,

is selfadjoint because
\Delta^{it}\phi_{L,T}(k)\phi_{R,T}(k^{\prime})\Delta^{-it}
is selfadjoint for any

. Clearly,
\Omega
is an eigenvector of

. Hence the spectral projection
\Pi^{Q}_{1}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H}))
of

for eigenvalue

also lies in

and satisfies
\Pi^{Q}_{1}\geq E_{0}
.

If we even have

, Prop.
4.7

a)
immediately concludes the proof of the theorem. But in general, the equality

is not clear and we have to use Lemma

. In order to be able to do so, we now show that there exists

such that

satisfies

(4.15)

\displaystyle\Pi^{Q}_{1}\mathcal{H}_{T,n}=\{0\},\hskip 20.00003ptn\geq n_{T}.

To prove this claim, it will be advantageous to work with the
T

\|\cdot\|
instead of
\|\cdot\|_{T}
. Recall that
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}=\mathcal{H}_{T,n}
as vector spaces because

is dense in the norm

and the two norms

are equivalent because of our assumptions on

As the operators

\displaystyle W_{n}(t):=a_{L}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k)T_{1}\cdots T_{n}\,a_{R}^{*}(\Delta_{H}^{it}k^{\prime})|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}

are well defined operators on

Now any

satisfies

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{n}\|=\|Q\Psi_{n}\|=\|W_{n}\Psi_{n}\|\leq\|k\|\|\|k^{\prime}\|\|T\|^{n}\|\Psi_{n}\|.

As

for

(and hence

) for sufficiently large

To finish the proof of the theorem, let

be an element of the relative commutant of the half-sided modular inclusion

. Then

commutes with any element of the algebra at infinity and in particular with

. So we obtain

\displaystyle\Pi^{Q}_{1}A\Omega=A\Pi^{Q}_{1}\Omega=A\Omega.

But the vector
\Pi^{Q}_{1}A\Omega
is contained in

, whereas Lemma
4.8
tells us that

is not an element of this space unless

.
∎

The significance of this result in quantum field theory will be explained in Section
5
. Here we note that the following instability result: If we take

, then Theorem
4.9
shows that the half-sided inclusion

has trivial relative commutant for any

, whereas the relative commutant equals

(which has

as a standard vector) for

because of the familiar structure of second quantization factors. Such instability phenomena of inclusions under deformations are in line with the observations in
[

,

,

]
.

4.2.
L^{2}
-nuclearity and quasi-split inclusions

So far we have obtained results about (half-sided) inclusions
\mathcal{L}_{T}(K)\subset\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)
of twisted Araki-Woods algebras having trivial relative commutant
\mathcal{R}_{L,T}(H,K)=\mathbb{C}
. We now add a complementary result, showing that other inclusions of the form
\mathcal{L}_{T}(K)\subset\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)
can also have large relative commutants.

The mechanism behind this discussion is the
L^{2}
-nuclearity condition of Buchholz, D’Antoni, and Longo
[
BDL07
]
and the theory of (quasi-)split inclusions
[
DL84
,
Fid01
]
.

Definition 4.10
.

An inclusion
{\mathcal{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}
of von Neumann algebras with joint cyclic separating vector and corresponding modular operators
\Delta_{\mathcal{N}}
,
\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}
is said to satisfy
L^{2}
-nuclearity if
\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}^{1/4}\Delta_{\mathcal{N}}^{-1/4}
is of trace class.

If
{\mathcal{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}
satisfies
L^{2}
-nuclearity, then it is quasi-split, namely there exist type I factors
\mathcal{I}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}
such that
{\mathcal{N}}\otimes\mathbb{C}\subset\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{M}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{I}}
. If
{\mathcal{N}}
or
\mathcal{M}
is a factor, we even have the split property, namely
{\mathcal{N}}\subset\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{M}
with some type I factor
\mathcal{I}
.

A consequence of
{\mathcal{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}
being quasi-split is that whenever
\mathcal{M}
is of type III, then also the relative commutant
{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}
is of type III. These results are generalizations of
[
DL84
]
to the non-factor case
[
Fid01
]
, see
[
BM22
, Cor. 3.11]
.

We now state our non-triviality result for the relative commutant
\mathcal{C}_{T}(K,H)
under quite strong assumptions. This result is closely related to the work of D’Antoni, Longo, and Radulescu
[
DLR01
]
who considered the case
T=0
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
K\subset H
be an inclusion of standard subspaces and
T
a braided twist such that

a)

L^{2}
-nuclearity holds on the standard subspace level, i.e.
\|\Delta_{H}^{1/4}\Delta_{K}^{-1/4}\|_{1}<1
, where
\|\cdot\|_{1}
is the trace norm on
\mathcal{H}
,

b)

T
is crossing-symmetric w.r.t.
H
,

c)

T
is compatible with
K
and
H
, and has norm
\|T\|<1
.

Then
\mathcal{L}_{T}(K)\subset\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)
satisfies
L^{2}
-nuclearity and is quasi-split. If
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)
is of type III, also the relative commutant
\mathcal{C}_{T}(K,H)
is of type III.

Proof.

We first note that in view of the assumptions,
\Omega
is cyclic and separating for
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)
and hence also separating for the subalgebra
\mathcal{L}_{T}(K)
. It is also cyclic for the subalgebra because
K
is a standard subspace, so we have
\Omega
as a joint cyclic separating vector.

Thanks to the compatibility assumption for
K
and
H
, the modular operators
\hat{\Delta}_{K}
and
\hat{\Delta}_{H}
of
\mathcal{L}_{T}(K)
and
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)
are of second quantized form (Prop.
3.25
). Hence the trace norm of
\hat{\Delta}_{H}^{1/4}\hat{\Delta}_{K}^{-1/4}
is

(4.18)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\Delta}_{H}^{1/4}\hat{\Delta}_{K}^{-1/4}\|_{T,1}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\|(\Delta_{H}^{1/4}\Delta_{K}^{-1/4})^{\otimes n}\|_{T,1};

where
\|\cdot\|_{T,1}
denotes the trace norm of the trace class in
{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}_{T,n})
.

As
\|T\|<1
is a strict twist, the operator
P_{T,n}^{1/2}:\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}\to\mathcal{H}_{T,n}
is unitary. Hence
\|(\Delta_{H}^{1/4}\Delta_{K}^{-1/4})^{\otimes n}\|_{T,1}=\|P_{T,n}^{-1/2}(\Delta_{H}^{1/4}\Delta_{K}^{-1/4})^{\otimes n}P_{T,n}^{1/2}\|_{1}
, where
\|\cdot\|_{1}
denotes the trace norm of the trace class in
{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})
. But as
T
is compatible with
K
and
H
, the operators
P_{T,n}^{\pm 1/2}
commute with
(\Delta_{H}^{1/4}\Delta_{K}^{-1/4})^{\otimes n}
and thus drop out. Now we may use the tensor structure of
\|\cdot\|_{1}
to conclude

(4.19)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\Delta}_{H}^{1/4}\hat{\Delta}_{K}^{-1/4}\|_{T,1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|\Delta_{H}^{1/4}\Delta_{K}^{-1/4}\|_{1}^{n}=\frac{1}{1-\|\Delta_{H}^{1/4}\Delta_{K}^{-1/4}\|_{1}}<\infty.

∎

Let us comment on the assumptions entering into this proposition. The compatibility condition for
T
with
H
and
K
is quite strong in general, but automatically satisfied for
T=qF
,
-1<q<1
. In this case, it is also known from Hiai’s work that
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H)
is a type III
1
factor if
\Delta_{H}
does not have eigenvalue
1

[
Hia01
, Prop. 3.2]
.

The
L^{2}
-nuclearity assumption on the level of the standard subspaces is also a strong assumption, but it is known to hold (including the norm inequality
\|\Delta_{H}^{1/4}\Delta_{K}^{-1/4}\|_{1}<1
) for certain inclusions of standard subspaces arising QFT (see Section
5
).

5.
Applications to quantum field theory

In this section we explain some applications of our constructions in algebraic quantum field theory
[
Haa96
,
BDFY15
]
, where the main object of interest are families of von Neumann algebras labeled by open regions in some spacetime manifold, subject to several physically motivated conditions.

Since we will consider quantum field theories on a variety of different spacetimes, we use an abstract formulation. From the structure of spacetime as a globally hyperbolic Lorentzian manifold
{\mathscr{M}}
we only need that
{\mathscr{M}}
has a family
{\mathscr{O}}
of “good” subsets (to be thought of as the causally complete convex regions), a notion of causal complement, mapping subsets
\mathcal{O}\in{\mathscr{O}}
to their complements
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{O}}
such that
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime})^{\prime}=\mathcal{O}
for all
\mathcal{O}
. Furthermore, we require that there exists a Lie group
G
acting on
{\mathscr{M}}
such that
g\mathcal{O}\in{\mathscr{O}}
for
\mathcal{O}\in{\mathscr{O}}
and preserving complementation,
g\mathcal{O}^{\prime}=(g\mathcal{O})^{\prime}
for all
g\in G
,
\mathcal{O}\in{\mathscr{O}}
.

In this general setting, we require the existence of a reference region with the following properties.

(reference wedge):

There exists
W_{0}\in{\mathscr{O}}
such that there is a one-parameter group
\lambda_{0}(t)\in G
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and an involution
j_{0}\in G
such that
\lambda_{0}(t)W_{0}=W_{0}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and
j_{0}W_{0}=W_{0}^{\prime}
as well as
j_{0}\lambda_{0}(t)=\lambda_{0}(t)j_{0}
.

We will refer to the reference region
W_{0}
as “reference wedge” although its geometrical shape can be quite different (see examples below), and to elements of the
G
-orbit
{\mathscr{W}}:=GW_{0}\subset{\mathscr{O}}
as “wedges”.

(wedge separation property):

For any
\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2}\in{\mathscr{O}}
with
\mathcal{O}_{1}\subset\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\prime}
, there exists a wedge
W\in{\mathscr{W}}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{1}\subset W\subset\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\prime}
.

(wedge intersection property):

For any
\mathcal{O}\in{\mathscr{O}}
, there holds

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}=\bigcap_{W\in{\mathscr{W}}:W\supset\mathcal{O}}W.

Two examples of this setting are the following.

a)

Minkowski spacetime
{\mathscr{M}}=\mathbb{R}^{1+s}
,
s\geq 1
, with
{\mathscr{O}}
the set of all convex causally complete regions,
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
the causal complement,
G
the Poincaré group, and
W_{0}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{1+s}\,:\,x_{1}>|x_{0}|\}
the Rindler wedge. Here
\lambda_{0}(t)
are the Lorentz boosts in
x_{1}
-direction with parameter
t
, and
j_{0}
is the reflection at the edge of
W_{0}
.

b)

The lightray
{\mathscr{M}}=\mathbb{R}
with
{\mathscr{O}}
the set of all open intervals and half-lines,
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
the set-theoretic complement,
G
the affine group of
\mathbb{R}
, and
W_{0}=\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Here
\lambda_{0}(t)
are the dilations
x\mapsto e^{t}x
and
j_{0}
is the reflection
x\mapsto-x
.

Further examples include the circle
{\mathscr{M}}=S^{1}
with Möbius group symmetry or de Sitter spacetime with Lorentz group symmetry. These examples are well known, and recently a general group-theoretic formulation has been developed which includes these and further cases
[
MN21
,
MNO22
]
.

The first step to a quantum field theory on
{\mathscr{M}}
consists in describing the one-particle localization structure, which we shall do by modular localization. As explained in the work of Brunetti, Guido and Longo
[
BGL02
]
and later generalizations
[
Mun03
,
MN21
]
, we consider an (anti)unitary representation
U
of
G
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, namely a strongly continuous group homomorphism
U
from
G
to the group of (anti)unitary operators on
\mathcal{H}
such that
U(j_{0})
is antiunitary. Denoting the connected component of the identity by
G^{\uparrow}\subset G
, we then have
G=G^{\uparrow}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, where
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
acts by conjugation with
j_{0}
. We also write
G^{\downarrow}:=G\backslash G^{\uparrow}=G^{\uparrow}j_{0}
.

Then
t\mapsto U(\lambda_{0}(t))=e^{itB}
is a unitary one-parameter group with some selfadjoint generator
B
, and
U(j_{0})
is an antiunitary involution. Setting

(5.2)

\displaystyle H_{0}:=\operatorname{ker}(U(j_{0})e^{\frac{1}{2}B}-1),\hskip 20.00003pt\Delta_{H_{0}}^{it}=U(\lambda_{0}(t)),\hskip 20.00003ptJ_{H_{0}}=U(j_{0}),

we obtain a standard subspace
H_{0}\subset\mathcal{H}
with the modular data specified above.

We shall assume that

is
localized in

in the sense that

\displaystyle g\in G,\;gW_{0}\subset W_{0}\;\Rightarrow\;U(g)H_{0}\subset H_{0}.

Once (

, and obtain a net of standard subspaces for wedges, namely

\displaystyle gW_{0}\mapsto H(gW_{0}):=U(g)H_{0},\hskip 20.00003ptg\in G.

Note that this map is well-defined thanks to the inclusion property (

fixes

in the representation

\displaystyle H(W^{\prime})=H(W)^{\prime},\hskip 20.00003ptW\in{\mathscr{W}}.

For

in (

, and for general wedges by covariance.

To connect to the twisted Araki-Woods algebras, we need to amplify

for suitable twists

Definition 5.1

Let

\displaystyle[U(g)\otimes U(g),T]=0,\hskip 10.00002ptg\in G^{\uparrow},\hskip 20.00003pt[F(J_{H_{0}}\otimes J_{H_{0}}),T]=0,

and crossing-symmetric w.r.t.

The

,
-1\leq q\leq 1

(and hence any

,

), in the sense of Def.

For admissible

\displaystyle U_{T}(g):=\Gamma_{T}(U_{1}(g)),\hskip 10.00002ptg\in G^{\uparrow},\hskip 20.00003ptU_{T}(j_{0}):=\Gamma_{T}^{Y}(J_{H}),

are well-defined (anti)unitary operators on

Given a representation

of

\mathcal{F}_{T}(\mathcal{H})
and two maps

\displaystyle W\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W)),\hskip 20.00003ptW\mapsto\mathcal{R}_{T}(H(W))

to von Neumann algebras on

.

Let

a)

\displaystyle U_{T}(g)\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W))U_{T}(g)^{-1}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(gW))&g\in G^{\uparrow}\\

and analogously with
\mathcal{L}_{T}
and
\mathcal{R}_{T}

\mathcal{L}_{T}(W)
and
\mathcal{R}_{T}(W)
,

.

(5.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W_{1}))\subset\mathcal{R}_{T}(H(W_{2}))^{\prime},

and analogously with
\mathcal{L}_{T}
and

) and Prop.

. Item c) is contained in Thm.

, and d) follows from

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W_{1}))\subset\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W_{2}^{\prime}))=\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W_{2})^{\prime})=\mathcal{R}_{T}(H(W_{2}))^{\prime}.

∎

We may interpret this structure as a quantum field theory in two different ways. In the first version, we consider
G^{\uparrow}
as our symmetry group. Then both nets

and

are
G^{\uparrow}
-covariant and
relatively local
in the sense of item d) above. However, for
T\neq F
typically neither

nor

is local (see Lemma

).

In the second version, we consider
G
as our symmetry group. Then the transformation behavior from item b) above imposes an additional constraint in case there exists
g_{0}\in G^{\uparrow}
with
g_{0}W_{0}=W_{0}^{\prime}
. Namely, in that case we must have
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H_{0}^{\prime})=\mathcal{R}_{T}(H_{0}^{\prime})
for

to be

-covariant. Apart from

, this is typically not the case (see Lemma
4.2
). However, this conflict does not occur in several situations of interest, such as two-dimensional Minkowski space, the lightray example, or higher-dimensional Minkowski space with a restricted symmetry group (see
[
BS07
]
for similar considerations). If
W_{0}^{\prime}\not\in G^{\uparrow}W_{0}
, we may take

(5.11)

\displaystyle gW_{0}\mapsto\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{T}(H(gW_{0})):=U_{T}(g)\mathcal{L}_{T}(H_{0})U_{T}(g^{-1}),\hskip 20.00003ptg\in G,

as the definition of our net, which is then

\mathcal{R}_{T}
instead of
\mathcal{L}_{T}
.

We will adopt this second point of view because it leads to local nets
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{T}
, and refer to
[
Lec12
]
for a scheme (for special twists) in which the restriction to low-dimensional spacetime or smaller symmetry groups can be overcome.

The net
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{T}
is so far defined on the set of wedges
{\mathscr{W}}
, but naturally extends to the larger set
{\mathscr{O}}
of localization regions. Here the assumptions of wedge separation and wedge intersection enter.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let

be a representation with the inclusion property (

),

an admissible twist, and assume that

does not contain

. Then the map

(5.12)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{O}}\ni\mathcal{O}\longmapsto\mathcal{A}_{U,T}(\mathcal{O}):=\bigcap_{W\in{\mathscr{W}}:W\supset\mathcal{O}}\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{T}(H(W))

is well-defined,

a)

G
-covariant, namely

,
g\in G
,
\mathcal{O}\in{\mathscr{O}}
,

b)

isotonous, namely
\mathcal{A}_{U,T}(\mathcal{O}_{1})\subset\mathcal{A}_{U,T}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
for
\mathcal{O}_{1}\subset\mathcal{O}_{2}
,

c)

local, namely
\mathcal{A}_{U,T}(\mathcal{O}_{1})\subset\mathcal{A}_{U,T}(\mathcal{O}_{2})^{\prime}
for
\mathcal{O}_{1}\subset\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The map is well-defined because any
\mathcal{O}\in{\mathscr{O}}
is contained in a wedge by the wedge intersection property. a) and b) are obvious. For c), note that for
\mathcal{O}_{1}\subset\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\prime}
there exists a wedge

such that
\mathcal{O}_{1}\subset W
and
\mathcal{O}_{2}\subset W^{\prime}
(wedge separation property). Hence either

or

lies in the

-orbit of
W_{0}
. In case
W=gW_{0}
,

, we have
\mathcal{A}_{U,T}(\mathcal{O}_{1})\subset\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W))
and

\mathcal{A}_{U,T}(\mathcal{O}_{2})\subset\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W^{\prime}))=\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W)^{\prime})=\mathcal{R}_{T}(H(W))^{\prime},

which implies the claim. The case
W^{\prime}=gW_{0}
,

, is analogous.
∎

Given a representation
U
and a twist
T
satisfying our assumptions, we therefore get a net
\mathcal{A}_{U,T}
satisfying the basic properties of a quantum field theory. In particular, this construction recovers the free field for
T=F
.

The representation
U
entering the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{U,T}
encodes the single particle structure (such as particle masses and spins), and the twist
T
is expected to be characteristic of the interaction in the underlying theory. This is well understood in case
T
comes from a Yang-Baxter solution with spectral parameter (Example
2.4
), in which case it precisely encodes the two-particle scattering operator and leads to a scheme for the operator-algebraic construction of integrable QFTs
[
Lec08
,
AL17
]
. In this case, our notion of crossing symmetry also coincides with the scattering theory definition of crossing symmetry (Remark
3.15
).

In general, it is expected that the local algebras (
5.12
) may fail to have
\Omega
as a cyclic vector, or be too small to allow for interesting observables localized in
\mathcal{O}
. In some cases, we will give proofs of these expectations below. One should therefore view
\mathcal{A}_{U,T}
as a “germ” of a quantum field theory (also called “wedge-local quantum field theory”). The class of all wedge-local models contains both, “strictly local” models in which
\Omega
is cyclic for all
\mathcal{A}_{U,T}(\mathcal{O})
,
\mathcal{O}\in{\mathscr{O}}
, as well as non-local models in which this property does not hold.

The challenge to efficiently decide which class a given tuple
U,T
corresponds to constitutes the QFT part of the motivation for the present article. We here comment on models with
\|T\|<1
which have not been investigated so far.

Typically for a given region
\mathcal{O}\in{\mathscr{O}}
, there will exist two wedges
W_{1}=g_{1}W_{0}
,
W_{2}=g_{2}W_{0}
,
g_{1},g_{2}\in G^{\uparrow}
, such that

(5.13)

\displaystyle W_{1}\subset W_{2},\hskip 20.00003pt\mathcal{O}\subset W_{1}^{\prime}\cap W_{2}.

This is for instance the case for
\mathcal{O}
a double cone in Minkowski space, or an interval on the lightray, where
W_{1}
is a wedge (half-line) open to the right, and
W_{2}^{\prime}
is a wedge (half-line) open to the left. In this situation,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{U,T}(\mathcal{O})

\displaystyle\subset\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{T}(H(W_{1}^{\prime}))\cap\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{T}(H(W_{2}))=\mathcal{R}_{T}(H(W_{1}))\cap\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W_{2}))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W_{1}))^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W_{2})),

namely
\mathcal{A}_{U,T}(\mathcal{O})
is a subalgebra contained in the relative commutant of the inclusion
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W_{1}))\subset\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W_{2}))
. In the cases mentioned above (two-dimensional Minkowski space or lightray), we actually have equality between
\mathcal{A}_{U,T}(\mathcal{O})
and the relative commutant.

Our results on relative commutants (Theorem
4.9
and Proposition
4.11
) then apply as follows.

•

Lightlike inclusions of wedges or lightrays
.

Consider
W_{0}+a\subset W_{0}
a lightlike inclusion of wedges (i.e.
a=(\lambda,\lambda,0,\ldots,0)
,
\lambda>0
) in the Minkowski example a), or
\mathbb{R}_{+}+\lambda\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\lambda>0
, an inclusion of half lines in the lightray example b).
In a positive energy representation, we are then in the situation of Def.
4.5
and hence the corresponding inclusions
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W_{0}+a))\subset\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(W_{0}))
and
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(\mathbb{R}_{+}+\lambda))\subset\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
are half-sided modular.

Now consider an arbitrary admissible twist with
\|T\|<1
(for example,
T=qF
with
|q|<1
, or a scaled Yang-Baxter solution
T=qT_{S}
as in Example
2.5
). Then Theorem
4.9
implies that the half-sided inclusion has trivial relative commutant.

In the context of the net
\mathcal{A}_{U,T}
, the interpretation of this fact is that there are no observables strictly localized in the relative complement of the inclusion, i.e. on the lightfront
\overline{W_{0}^{\prime}+a}\cap\overline{W}_{0}
or the lightlike interval
(0,\lambda)
. Hence
\mathcal{A}_{U,T}
is non-local from this point of view.

•

L^{2}
-modular inclusions for massless theories
.

Consider Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
,
d\geq 1
, two double cones
\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2}
with
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{1}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{2}
, and the representation
U
defining the free massless scalar field. Then the corresponding standard subspaces
H(\mathcal{O}_{1})\subset H(\mathcal{O}_{2})
satisfy
L^{2}
-nuclearity including the trace norm bound
\|\Delta_{H(\mathcal{O}_{2})}^{1/4}\Delta_{H(\mathcal{O}_{1})}^{-1/4}\|_{1}<1
if the distance between
\mathcal{O}_{1}
and
\partial\mathcal{O}_{2}
is sufficiently large
[
BDL07
]
.

Now consider an arbitrary admissible twist with
\|T\|<1
; for example,
T=qF
with
|q|<1
. As the modular operators do not have eigenvalue
1
, in this case
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(\mathcal{O}_{i}))
are type III
1
factors and it follows from Prop
4.11
implies that the relative commutant of the inclusion
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(\mathcal{O}_{1}))\subset\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(\mathcal{O}_{2}))
is of type III as well.

The interpretation of this fact is that there are lots of elements of
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(\mathcal{O}_{2}))
that are localized in
\mathcal{O}_{1}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{O}_{2}
in the sense that they commute with the subalgebra
\mathcal{L}_{T}(H(\mathcal{O}_{1}))
.

The first result can be understood as a sign that the models with
\|T\|<1
are strongly non-local. It complements results establishing large relative commutants for special twists with
\|T\|=1
, and we view such examples as counterexamples to the construction of strictly local theories. The second result does not strictly fit to the wedge-local setting because of the different geometry (double cone inclusions), but does indicate that nets which appear strongly non-local might contain more local observables than anticipated.

We expect to learn from both scenarios and other examples how to better control the local observable content of twisted models in future work on constructive algebraic quantum field theory.

A Strong Form of the Quantitative Wulff Inequality for Crystalline Norms
Kenneth DeMason

Abstract
. Quantitative stability for crystalline anisotropic perimeters, with control on the oscillation of the boundary with respect to the corresponding Wulff shape, is proven for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . This extends a result of [ Neu16 ] in n = 2 n=2 .



1.
Introduction

Wulff’s construction, based on the classical theory of thermodynamics presented by Gibbs, provides a method for determining equilibrium shapes for crystals in media
[
Wul01
]
. In this setting, the crystalline interfaces determine favorable orientations to minimize the so-called
anisotropic perimeter

\Phi
. Given a
surface tension

f:S^{n-1}\to[0,\infty)
, the anisotropic perimeter of a region
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined as

\Phi(E)=\int_{\partial^{*}E}f(\nu_{E}(x))\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

(1.1)

Here we use the standard notation and terminology, see
[
Mag12
]
, where
E
is a set of finite perimeter,
\partial^{*}E
is its reduced boundary, and
\nu_{E}
is the measure-theoretic outer unit normal.

This notion of perimeter arises naturally in crystallography when the
1
-homogeneous extension of
f
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is piecewise linear; in this case we call
f
a
crystalline
surface tension. The study of crystalline surface tensions is interesting from both a physical viewpoint due to its crystallographic connections
[
GM67
,
RW84
]
and from a mathematical viewpoint
[
Her51
,
McC98
,
Tay78
]
.

From the perspective of statistical mechanics, identifying low-energy states of
\Phi
is as important as identifying absolute minimizers, since they are interpreted as the most likely observable states of the system. Indeed, entropic contributions vary proportionally with temperature and thus always play a role in nature. It is therefore equally as interesting to know that the Wulff shape is the absolute minimizer of
\Phi
at fixed volume as it is to describe, as precisely as possible, the shape of low-energy states.

The Wulff inequality, the anisotropic analogue of the isoperimetric inequality,

\Phi(E)\geq n|K|^{1/n}|E|^{(n-1)/n}

(1.2)

characterizes the volume-constrained minimizers of the anisotropic perimeter as the corresponding
Wulff shape

K
, see
[
Mag12
, Chapter 20]
. Equality holds in (
1.2
) if and only if
|E\Delta(rK+x)|=0
for some
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Due to this rigidity we can use the
anisotropic deficit

\delta_{\Phi}(E):=\frac{\Phi(E)}{n|K|^{1/n}|E|^{(n-1)/n}}-1

(1.3)

to interpret the low-energy states as the regions
E
for which
\delta_{\Phi}(E)
is small. Indeed, by definition
\delta_{\Phi}(E)=0
if and only if
E
saturates (
1.2
). Note that
\delta_{\Phi}
is scale invariant and thus detects deviations from
K
due to shape alone.

In
[
FMP10
]
the first identification of low-energy states of
\Phi
in terms of control via
\delta_{\Phi}
was obtained. Here the authors show the existence of a universal constant
C(n)>0
such that for any set of finite perimeter
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0<|E|<\infty
,

\alpha_{\Phi}(E)^{2}\leq C\delta_{\Phi}(E).

(1.4)

In the above
\alpha_{\Phi}
denotes the
anisotropic Fraenkel asymmetry
,

\alpha_{\Phi}(E):=\inf\left\{\frac{|E\Delta(rK+x)|}{|E|}\ \bigg|\ |rK|=|E|,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}.

(1.5)

Heuristically, this says that the low-energy states of
\Phi
are close to the absolute minimizer in an
L^{1}
sense. Due to the anisotropic nature of the problem
[
FMP10
]
relied on a Sobolev-Poincaré type trace inequality in conjunction with optimal transport methods as opposed to the symmetrization techniques available in the isotropic case, see the seminal result by
[
FMP08
]
. More recently,
[
CL12
]
used a new technique known as the selection principle to re-prove the sharp quantitative isoperimetric inequality without appealing to symmetry; notably, this approach is applicable to the anisotropic setting.

A particularly important class of surface tensions are the
\lambda
-elliptic surface tensions, for which the corresponding Wulff shape is
C^{2}
and uniformly convex (with uniformity controlled by
\lambda>0
). In this case
[
Neu16
]
, using the ideas of
[
CL12
]
and
[
FJ13
]
, improved the sharp stability (
1.4
) by showing that there exists
C(n,\lambda,\varepsilon_{\Phi},\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{C^{0}(\partial K)})>0

for every set of finite perimeter
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0<|E|<\infty
,

\alpha_{\Phi}(E)^{2}+\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}\leq C\delta_{\Phi}(E).

(1.6)

Here
\beta_{\Phi}
denotes the
anisotropic oscillation index

\beta_{\Phi}(E):=\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{n|K|^{1/n}|E|^{(n-1)/n}}\int_{\partial^{*}E}f(\nu_{E}(x))-\frac{\langle x-y,\nu_{E}(x)\rangle}{f_{*}(x-y)}\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\right)^{1/2},

(1.7)

where
f_{*}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
is the
gauge function
, a
1
-homogeneous convex function related to the surface tension in a dual sense by

f_{*}(x)=\sup\{\langle x,\nu\rangle\ |\ f(\nu)\leq 1\}.

(1.8)

The quantity
\beta_{\Phi}(E)
was first introduced in the isotropic setting by
[
FJ13
]
and controls the oscillation of the boundary with respect to a reference Wulff shape. Heuristically, (
1.6
) says that low-energy states of
\Phi
are close in an
H^{1}
sense, see
[
Neu16
, Proposition 1.9]
.

We now turn to the crystalline setting, which as mentioned occurs when the
1
-homogeneous extension of
f
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is piecewise linear. Particularly,
\nabla^{2}f=0
a.e., in contrast to the
\lambda
-elliptic case, and the problem becomes degenerate elliptic. This degeneration also causes the Wulff shape
K
to develop flat sides and become a polytope. Each facet
F_{i}
of
K
has a constant unit normal
\nu_{i}
and lies at a distance
{\bf d_{\it{i}}}=f(\nu_{i})
from the origin. In this case
K
is represented by

K=\bigcap_{i\in\mathscr{I}}\,\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ \langle x,\nu_{i}\rangle<{\bf d_{\it{i}}}\}.

(1.9)

In the above,
\mathscr{I}=\{1,...,N\}
indexes the
N
many facets of
K
.

Recall as defined in
[
FZ22
]
that
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
\varepsilon
-minimizer for
\Phi
if for every set of finite perimeter
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|F|=|E|
it holds

\Phi(E)\leq\Phi(F)+\varepsilon|E\Delta F|.

(1.10)

In particular when
|E|=|K|
we have

\delta_{\Phi}(E)\leq\varepsilon\left(\frac{|E\Delta K|}{\Phi(K)}\right)\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{n}.

(1.11)

Thus
\varepsilon
-minimizers are low-energy states of
\Phi
. In fact,
\varepsilon
-minimizers of
\Phi
have been completely characterized, first for
n=2
in
[
FM07
, Theorem 7]
and later for
n\geq 3
in
[
FZ22
, Theorem 1.1]
, as polytopes with sides parallel to
K
.

What remains then is to understand the remaining low-energy states which are not
\varepsilon
-minimizers. Due to the anisotropic nature of the problem, little progress has been made in this direction from the lack of symmetrization techniques. The planar case was proven in
[
Neu16
, Theorem 1.5]
, but the case for
n\geq 3
remains open. The following theorem, the main result of this paper, proves this and therefore provides a natural conclusion to the above discussion.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
be a crystalline surface tension. There exists
C(n,K)>0
such that for any
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a set of finite perimeter with
0<|E|<\infty
,

\alpha_{\Phi}(E)^{2}+\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}\leq C\delta_{\Phi}(E).

Let us comment briefly on the proof strategy. The planar case of this theorem proven in
[
Neu16
]
relied on the characterization of
\varepsilon
-minimizers in
[
FM07
]
. Analogously, our result relies on this characterization extended to
n\geq 3
by
[
FZ22
]
which crucially uses the following theorem.

Theorem A

(
[
FZ22
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

There exist
\sigma(n,K)>0
and
\gamma(n,K)>0
such that for any set of finite perimeter
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|E|=|K|
and
|E\Delta K|\leq\sigma
there exists
K^{\bf a}
a polytope parallel to
K
with
|K^{\bf a}|=|K|
and

\Phi(E)-\Phi(K^{\bf a})\geq\gamma|E\Delta K^{\bf a}|.

To expand on the notation above, we write
K^{\bf a}
to denote the polytope obtained by perturbing
K
via a vector
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}<1
as follows:

K^{\bf a}=\bigcap_{i\in\mathscr{I}}\,\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ \langle x,\nu_{i}\rangle<{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}})\}.

(1.12)

When
{\bf a}=0
we have
K^{\bf a}=K
. The above procedure may produce a polytope having fewer sides than
K
, for example by perturbing the short side of a trapezoid to produce a triangle. This presents an issue as we frequently compare facets
F_{i}
of
K
with those of
K^{\bf a}
, necessitating they have the same unit normal. To ensure this occurs we need the technical condition that
K^{\bf a}
is
parallel
to
K
, defined by having the same number of sides or, equivalently, the same set of unit normals
\{\nu_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
. The collection of all such polytopes is denoted
\mathcal{C}_{\text{par}}(K)
.

In light of Theorem
A
, for sets of finite perimeter
E
sufficiently close to
K
, we are able to essentially project them onto a polytope
K^{\bf a}
also close to
K
in terms of both having comparable volume and unit normals. This reduction allows us to leverage Theorem
A
and add control over
\beta_{\Phi}
.

Section
2
introduces some relevant results from
[
Neu16
]
and
[
FZ22
]
. Section
3
proves Proposition
3.7
, the result for parallel polytopes. This is a delicate explicit computation involving estimates comparing the sizes of parallel facets, see Proposition
3.1
. Section
4
proves Theorem
1.1
. It first introduces some technical lemmas allowing us to control
\gamma_{\Phi}
, a quantity related to the oscillation index, in a strong way, via Proposition
4.2
. It then proves the main result using the selection principle.

2.
Summary of Relevant Results

Properties of anisotropic quantities

We first introduce
\gamma_{\Phi}
, which will allow us to more conveniently estimate
\beta_{\Phi}^{2}
. By applying the divergence theorem we can rewrite
\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}
as

\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}=\frac{\Phi(E)-(n-1)\gamma_{\Phi}(E)}{n|K|^{1/n}|E|^{(n-1)/n}},\ \ \ \gamma_{\Phi}(E):=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{E}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x-y)}\ dx.

(2.1)

Remark 2.1
.

Note that
\alpha_{\Phi}(E)
,
\beta_{\Phi}(E)
, and
\gamma_{\Phi}(E)
are all translation invariant. Moreover
\alpha_{\Phi}(E)
and
\beta_{\Phi}(E)
are scale invariant whereas
\gamma_{\Phi}(rE)=r^{n-1}\gamma_{\Phi}(E)
.

The supremum defining
\gamma_{\Phi}(E)
in (
2.1
) may not be attained at a unique point. We call any such point a
center of

E
, denoted
y_{E}
. The following lemma lets us estimate the norm of
y_{E}
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
Neu16
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\eta>0
such that if
|E\Delta K|\leq\eta
then
|y_{E}|<\varepsilon
for any center
y_{E}
of
E
.

We summarize the important properties of
\beta_{\Phi}
and
\gamma_{\Phi}
here.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
Neu16
, Prop. 2.1]
)
.

The function
\gamma_{\Phi}
is Hölder continuous with respect to
L^{1}
convergence, that is

|\gamma_{\Phi}(E)-\gamma_{\Phi}(F)|\leq\frac{n|K|}{(n-1)}|E\Delta F|^{(n-1)/n}.

Furthermore, for any sequence
\{E_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
such that
E_{j}
converges to
E
in
L^{1}
and
\{f^{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
a sequence of surface tensions converging locally uniformly to
f
, we have

i)

\gamma_{\Phi_{\bullet}}
is continuous;

\lim_{j\to\infty}\gamma_{\Phi_{j}}(E_{j})=\gamma_{\Phi_{j}}(E).

ii)

\beta_{\Phi_{\bullet}}
and
\Phi
are lower semi-continuous,

\beta_{\Phi}(E)\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\beta_{\Phi_{j}}(E_{j}),\ \ \ \Phi(E)\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\Phi(E_{j}).

Additionally if
\Phi(E_{j})\to\Phi(E)
then
\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})\to\beta_{\Phi}(E)
too.

Technical results

The following results come from
[
Neu16
]
and
[
FZ22
]
.

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
we will need to apply the selection principle. To do this we will assume Theorem
1.1
is false and generate a sequence
\{E_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
. This sequence ends up being too generic to use, and we must replace it with a new sequence
\{F_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
with more regularity, which will be obtained as minimizers of a certain functional. The first step in this is the following lemma, which allows us to restrict our attention to those sets
E
contained in some ball.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
Neu16
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

There exist constants
R_{0}(n,K)>0
and
C(n,K)>0
such that for any set of finite perimeter
E
with
|E|=|K|
we can find
E^{\prime}\subset B_{R_{0}}
such that
|E^{\prime}|=|K|
and

\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}\leq\beta_{\Phi}(E^{\prime})^{2}+C\delta_{\Phi}(E),\ \ \ \delta_{\Phi}(E^{\prime})\leq C\delta_{\Phi}(E).

To replace the
E_{j}
we construct
F_{j}
as minimizers of the functional

Q(E)=\Phi(E)+\varepsilon_{\Phi}|K||\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}|+\Lambda\big||E|-|K|\big|

for
0<\varepsilon<1
and
\Lambda>0
; in practice we take
\varepsilon=\beta_{\phi}(E_{j})
. Here,
\varepsilon_{\Phi}=m_{\Phi}/M_{\Phi}
the so-called eccentricity, where

M_{\Phi}=\sup_{\nu\in S^{n-1}}f(\nu)\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ m_{\Phi}=\inf_{\nu\in S^{n-1}}f(\nu).

(2.2)

To explain the relevance of
Q
, the latter two terms are penalty terms to control the oscillation and volume. Since these penalizations should be small, minimizers of
Q
are almost minimizers of
\Phi
, and thus should be comparable to
K
. Indeed by the rigidity of (
1.2
) we know that volume-constrained minimizers of
\Phi
are the Wulff shape
K
up to translation. We can reformulate this without the volume constraint as

Lemma 2.5

(
[
Neu16
, Lemma 3.5]
)
.

Let
R_{0}>\operatorname{Diam}(K)
and
\Lambda>n
. Then up to translation
K
is the unique minimizer of the functional

\Phi(E)+\Lambda\big||E|-|K|\big|

over
E\subset B_{R_{0}}
.

Note the similarity between the functional in Lemma
2.5
and
Q
. The following lemma guarantees that minimizers of
Q
exist.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
Neu16
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

A minimizer for the problem

\min\{Q(E)\ |\ E\subset B_{R_{0}}\}

exists for
\Lambda>4n
and
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small. Moreover, any minimizer
F
satisfies

|F|\geq\frac{|K|}{2},\ \ \ \ \Phi(F)\leq 2n|K|=2\Phi(K).

(2.3)

The former condition in (
2.3
) tells us that
F
does not degenerate, whereas the latter gives us a nontrivial way to compare
\Phi(F)
with
\Phi(K)
. This will be crucial in producing a contradiction.

We say that
E
satisfies the following
uniform density estimate
if there exists
r_{0}(n,K)>0
and
0<c_{0}(n)<1/2
such that for any
x\in\partial^{*}E
and
r<r_{0}
it holds

c_{0}\varepsilon_{\Phi}^{n}\omega_{n}r^{n}\leq|B_{r}(x)\cap E|\leq\left(1-c_{0}\varepsilon_{\Phi}^{n}\right)\omega_{n}r^{n}.

(2.4)

Typically
L^{1}
convergence is not enough to control Hausdorff convergence. However, combined with uniform density estimates one can obtain Hausdorff convergence of the boundaries, which in turn controls the Hausdorff distance. This is a standard technique; see for example (
4.2
) of Lemma
4.4
. Indeed, notably uniform density estimates help rule out cases like having long, thin tentacles which can extend arbitrarily far.

Importantly, minimizers of
Q(E)
satisfy the uniform density estimates.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
Neu16
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

Any minimizer
F
of
Q(E)
among
E\subset B_{R_{0}}
satisfies the uniform density estimates (
2.4
).

Remark 2.8
.

In (
2.4
) we require that
r_{0}
depends on
n
and
K
. In actuality, in Lemma
2.7
it also depends on
\Lambda
. However, we will take
\Lambda
to be some fixed constant
\Lambda>4n
, so this will not matter.

Also, the uniform density estimates allow us to upgrade the Hölder continuity from Proposition
2.3
to a kind of Lipschitz continuity; see Proposition
4.2
. In order to achieve this, we need the following lemma from
[
FZ22
]
.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
FZ22
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

There exists a modulus of continuity
\omega:(0,1)\to(0,\infty)
such that if
|{\bf a}|+|{\bf a^{\prime}}|<1
then

|\Phi(K^{{\bf a}})-\Phi(K^{{\bf a^{\prime}}})|\leq\omega(|{\bf a}|+|{\bf a^{\prime}}|)|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}|.

Finally we note the following useful property of
\mathcal{C}_{\text{par}}(K)
.

Remark 2.10
.

As
\mathcal{C}_{\text{par}}(K)
is parametrized by
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
it is finite dimensional. We expect the metrics induced by the
L^{1}
distance, Hausdorff distance, and
\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}
are all equivalent up to a constant
C(n,K)>0
; indeed, this is the content of Lemma
3.4
below. Explicit computations show that, in fact, these constants can be chosen to depend only on
n,N,\varepsilon_{\Phi},\csc(\theta_{ij})
where
\cos(\theta_{ij})=-\langle\nu_{i},\nu_{j}\rangle
. As in
[
FMP10
]
, it is likely that the dependence on
\varepsilon_{\Phi}
can be removed by an application of John’s lemma.

One may hope to use Theorem
1.1
to re-prove the uniformly elliptic result (see
[
Neu16
, Theorem 1.3]
) by approximating a smooth Wulff shape
K
with polytopes
K_{j}
, thereby removing the dependence on
\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{C^{0}(\partial K)}
of the universal constant. However, this necessarily means both
\csc(\theta_{ij})\to\infty
and
N\to\infty
. For this reason, we do not include the extra work to explicitly compute these constants in terms of these parameters.

3.
Parallel Deformations of Polytopes

Comparison of parallel facets

Given
K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{par}}(K)
, since they are parallel they have the same set of unit normals
\{\nu_{i}\}_{i\in\mathscr{I}}
. Let
F_{i}^{\bf a},F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}
be the facets of
K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}}
respectively with outer unit normal
\nu_{i}
. Formally,
F_{i}^{\bf a}=\partial K^{\bf a}\cap\Sigma_{\nu_{i}}^{1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}}}
, where

\Sigma_{\nu_{i}}^{r}:=\{\langle x,\nu_{i}\rangle=r{\bf d_{\it{i}}}\}.

(3.1)

Notice that
\Sigma_{\nu_{i}}^{1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}}}
is the supporting hyperplane of
K^{\bf a}
in the direction
\nu_{i}\in S^{n-1}
.

One can ask how
F_{i}^{\bf a}
and
F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}
are related in terms of, say,
\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}
. Indeed as in Lemma
3.4
we see that if
\{K^{\bf a_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\text{par}}(K)
is such that
{\bf a_{j}}\xrightarrow{\ell^{\infty}}{\bf a}
then
d_{H}(K^{\bf a_{j}},K^{\bf a})\to 0
. This is a strong condition, and suggests that
F_{i}^{\bf a}
and
F_{i}^{\bf a_{j}}
can be compared for sufficiently large
j
. In fact, we will show that, up to a suitable translation,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a_{j}}\Delta F_{i}^{\bf a})\to 0
and quantify the rate of convergence. More precisely we prove:

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists
C(n,K)>0
such that for any
K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{par}}(K)
and any
i\in\mathscr{I}
,

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a}\Delta(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i}))\leq CM_{\Phi}^{n-1}\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

(3.2)

where
u_{i}={\bf d_{\it{i}}}({\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}})\nu_{i}
.

In particular, applying Proposition
3.1
with
K
and
K^{\bf a}
immediately yields

Corollary 3.2

(Asymptotics of Parallel Facet Areas)
.

There exists
C(n,K)>0
such that for any
K^{\bf a}\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{par}}(K)
,

|\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a})-\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i})|\leq CM_{\Phi}^{n-1}\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

(3.3)

that is
|\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a})-\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i})|=O(\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}).

We start by showing that the class
\mathcal{C}_{\text{par}}(K)
is non-empty for
\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}
sufficiently small.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists
a_{0}(n,K)>0
such that if
\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq a_{0}
and
K^{\bf a}
is the Wulff shape associated to the gauge function

f_{*}^{\bf a}(x)=\max_{i\in\mathscr{I}}\frac{\langle x,\nu_{i}\rangle}{{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}})},

(3.4)

then
K^{\bf a}
is parallel to
K
.

Proof.

The idea is to show we can find a neighborhood
U
of
{\bf 0}
such that for any
{\bf a}\in U
and
i\in\mathscr{I}
we have
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a})>0
. To do this we first prove the map
{\bf a}\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a})
for fixed
i\in\mathscr{I}
is continuous. Note that Corollary
3.2
determines a precise modulus of continuity, but this requires the assumption that
K^{\bf a}
is parallel to
K
.

Fix
i\in\mathscr{I}
and consider
{\bf a}
such that
|{\bf a_{\it{i}}}|<1
and
{\bf a_{\it{j}}}=0
for
j\neq i
. Throughout we denote
F_{j}^{\bf a}
instead as
F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}}
to emphasize that
{\bf a}
depends only on its
i
-th component
{\bf a_{\it{i}}}
. First let
j\neq i
and observe that
F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}}\subset F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}
for
{\bf a_{\it{i}}}\leq{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}
. Since
|{\bf a_{\it{i}}}|<1
we see that
F_{j}^{1}
is maximal, in the sense that
F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}}\subset F_{j}^{1}
for all
|{\bf a_{\it{i}}}|<1
. Furthermore, it follows that

F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}}=F_{j}^{1}\cap\{\langle x,\nu_{i}\rangle\leq{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}})\}.

(3.5)

In particular, for
s\in[{\bf a_{\it{i}}},{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}]
the slices
(F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}\setminus F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}})\cap\Sigma_{\nu_{i}}^{1+s}
and
F_{j}^{1}\cap\Sigma_{\nu_{i}}^{1+s}
coincide. Consequently by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}})-\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}\setminus F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}})

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\int_{\bf a_{\it{i}}}^{{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}((F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}\setminus F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}})\cap\Sigma_{\nu_{i}}^{1+s})\ ds=\int_{\bf a_{\it{i}}}^{{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(F_{j}^{1}\cap\Sigma_{\nu_{i}}^{1+s})\ ds

(3.7)

and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}})
is absolutely continuous in
{\bf a_{\it{i}}}
by choosing
{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}=0
and reversing roles if necessary.

It remains to show that
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}})
is continuous. By
[
Sch13
, Lemma 5.1.1]
we have

|K^{\bf a_{\it{i}}}|=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j\neq i}{\bf d_{\it{j}}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}})+\frac{{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}})}{n}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}}).

(3.8)

By the same slicing argument above we see that
|K^{\bf a_{\it{i}}}|
is continuous. Hence by (
3.8
), so too is
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a_{\it{i}}})
. We may now choose
a_{0}
so that
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{{\bf a_{\it{i}}}})>\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j})/2
for all
j\in\mathscr{I}
.

The previous argument works whenever we perturb one of the facets. Now, given arbitrary
{\bf a}
we construct
{\bf a_{1}},...,{\bf a_{N}}
so that the
j
-th components satisfy
{\bf a_{i,\it{j}}}={\bf a_{\it{j}}}
for
j\leq i
and
{\bf a_{i,\it{j}}}=0
for
j>i
. Notice that
{\bf a_{N}}={\bf a}
, and set
{\bf a_{0}}={\bf 0}
. By the above, if each
{\bf a_{\it{i}}}
is sufficiently small then

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a})=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a_{N}})\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a_{N-1}})\geq\frac{1}{2^{N}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a_{0}})=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j})>0.

(3.9)

In principle the choice of
{\bf a_{\it{i}}}
depends on that of
{\bf a_{\it{1}}},...,{\bf a_{\it{i}-1}}
, but since this holds for any
i\in\mathscr{I}
, they all depend on
{\bf a_{\it{1}}}
, which in turn depends on
n
and
K
.
∎

We will also need to determine a modulus of continuity for the maps
{\bf a}\mapsto|K^{\bf a}\Delta K|
and
{\bf a}\mapsto d_{H}(K^{\bf a},K)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists
C(n,K)>0
such that for any
K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{par}}(K)
we have

\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq C|K^{\bf a}\Delta K^{\bf a^{\prime}}|\leq C^{2}d_{H}(K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}})\leq C^{3}\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}.

(3.10)

In particular, the metrics induced by
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{\infty}}
,
d_{H}
, and
L^{1}
are all strongly equivalent.

Proof.

We first show there exists a
C(n,K)
such that
d_{H}(K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}})\leq C\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}
. From
[
Sch13
, Theorem 1.8.11]
the Hausdorff distance between convex sets
K
and
K^{\prime}
with surface tensions
f
and
f^{\prime}
respectively can be computed as

d_{H}(K,K^{\prime})=\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{n-1})}.

(3.11)

Fix
K^{\bf a}
and
K^{\bf a^{\prime}}
(not necessarily parallel). Then without loss of generality there exists
\nu\in S^{n-1}
such that

d_{H}(K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}})=f^{\bf a}(\nu)-f^{\bf a^{\prime}}(\nu)=\sup_{x\in K^{\bf a}}\langle x,\nu\rangle-\sup_{x\in K^{\bf a^{\prime}}}\langle x,\nu\rangle.

(3.12)

Let
x^{\bf a}\in\partial K^{\bf a}
attain the first supremum. Recall that
x\in\partial K^{\bf a^{\prime}}
if and only if
f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(x)=1
. By 1-homogeneity it follows that
x^{\bf a}/f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(x^{\bf a})\in\partial K^{\bf a}
, and is a valid candidate in the latter supremum. Thus,

d_{H}(K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}})\leq\langle x^{\bf a},\nu\rangle-\left\langle\frac{x^{\bf a}}{f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(x^{\bf a})},\nu\right\rangle=\left\langle\left[\frac{f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(x^{\bf a})-1}{f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(x^{\bf a})}\right]x^{\bf a},\nu\right\rangle\leq\bigg|\frac{f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(x^{\bf a})-1}{f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(x^{\bf a}/|x^{\bf a}|)}\bigg|.

(3.13)

Appealing to the facts that
f_{*}^{\bf a}(x^{\bf a})=1
,
M_{\Phi}^{\bf a^{\prime}}=\sup_{x\in S^{n-1}}1/f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(x)
, and
K^{\bf a}\subset B_{M_{\Phi}^{\bf a}}(0)
we have

\displaystyle d_{H}(K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leq M_{\Phi}^{\bf a^{\prime}}|f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(x^{\bf a})-f_{*}^{\bf a}(x^{\bf a})|

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq|x^{\bf a}|M_{\Phi}^{\bf a^{\prime}}\left|f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}\left(\frac{x^{\bf a}}{|x^{\bf a}|}\right)-f_{*}^{\bf a}\left(\frac{x^{\bf a}}{|x^{\bf a}|}\right)\right|\leq M_{\Phi}^{\bf a}M_{\Phi}^{\bf a^{\prime}}\|f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}-f_{*}^{\bf a}\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{n-1})}.

(3.15)

Finally observe that
K^{\bf a}\subset K^{\bf 1}
, where
{\bf 1}=(1,...,1)
. Letting
R(n,K)>0
denote the smallest
R>0
such that
K^{\bf 1}\subset B_{R}(0)
, we finally have

d_{H}(K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}})\leq R^{2}\|f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}-f_{*}^{\bf a}\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{n-1})}.

(3.16)

It now suffices to show there exists
C(n,K)>0
such that
\|f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}-f_{*}^{\bf a}\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{n-1})}\leq C\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}
. As before, without loss of generality we may find
\nu\in S^{n-1}
such that

\|f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}-f_{*}^{\bf a}\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{n-1})}=f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(\nu)-f_{*}^{\bf a}(\nu)=\max_{i\in\mathscr{I}}\frac{\langle\nu,\nu_{i}\rangle}{{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}})}-\max_{i\in\mathscr{I}}\frac{\langle\nu,\nu_{i}\rangle}{{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}})}.

(3.17)

Letting
\nu_{i}
attain the first maximum, we have

\|f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}-f_{*}^{\bf a}\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{n-1})}\leq\frac{\langle\nu,\nu_{i}\rangle}{{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}})}-\frac{\langle\nu,\nu_{i}\rangle}{{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}})}=\left[\frac{{\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}}{{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}})(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}})}\right]\langle\nu,\nu_{i}\rangle.

(3.18)

In particular, choosing
a_{0}\leq 1/2
we have that
{\bf a_{\it{i}}},{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}\geq-1/2
and

\|f_{*}^{\bf a^{\prime}}-f_{*}^{\bf a}\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{n-1})}\leq\frac{4}{{\bf d_{\it{i}}}}|{\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}|\leq\frac{4}{m_{\Phi}}\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

(3.19)

as
m_{\Phi}=\inf_{\nu\in S^{n-1}}f(\nu)
, and
f(\nu_{i})={\bf d_{\it{i}}}
by definition.

Next we show there exists
C(n,K)>0
such that
\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq C|K^{\bf a}\Delta K^{\bf a^{\prime}}|
. Fix
K^{\bf a}
and
K^{\bf a^{\prime}}
, and consider
K^{\bf a_{s}}
where
{\bf a_{s}}=(1-s){\bf a}+s{\bf a^{\prime}}
. Let

E_{j}=\bigcup_{s\in[0,1]}F_{j}^{\bf a_{s}}\cap(K^{\bf a}\Delta K^{\bf a^{\prime}})

(3.20)

and observe the
E_{j}
partition
K^{\bf a}\Delta K^{\bf a^{\prime}}
(away from sets of measure zero), having parallel facets
F_{j}^{\bf a}\cap(K^{\bf a}\Delta K^{\bf a^{\prime}})
and
F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}\cap(K^{\bf a}\Delta K^{\bf a^{\prime}})
. Next by slicing,

|K^{\bf a}\Delta K^{\bf a^{\prime}}|=\sum_{j\in\mathscr{I}}|E_{j}|=\sum_{j\in\mathscr{I}}\bigg|\int_{{\bf a_{\it{j}}}}^{\bf a_{\it{j}}^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E_{j}\cap\Sigma_{\nu_{j}}^{1+s})\ ds\bigg|\geq\sum_{j\in\mathscr{I}}c_{j}|{\bf a_{\it{j}}}-{\bf a_{\it{j}}^{\prime}}|\geq c\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

(3.21)

where
c_{j}=\min_{s\in[0,1]}\{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E_{j}\cap\Sigma_{\nu_{j}}^{1+s})\}
and
c=\min_{j\in\mathscr{I}}\{c_{j}\}
. In principle these depend on
{\bf a}
and
{\bf a^{\prime}}
, but once more we can take a minimum over
[-a_{0},a_{0}]^{N}
to remove this dependence.

Finally we show
|K^{\bf a}\Delta K^{\bf a^{\prime}}|\leq Cd_{H}(K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}})
. The main theorem of
[
Gro00
]
, part i) states that: If
D=\max\{\operatorname{Diam}(K^{\bf a}),\operatorname{Diam}(K^{\bf a^{\prime}})\}
, then

|K^{\bf a}\Delta K^{\bf a^{\prime}}|\leq C\,d_{H}(K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}}),\ \ \ C=\frac{2\omega_{n}}{2^{1/n}-1}\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)^{n-1}.

(3.22)

But, with
R
defined earlier, we have that
\operatorname{Diam}(K^{\bf a})\leq 2R
for any
K^{\bf a}
, and so we may choose
C=2\omega_{n}R^{n-1}/(2^{1/n}-1)
.
∎

We now consider parallel perturbations in one direction. That is, we suppose that
K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{par}}(K)
are such that there exists
i\in\mathscr{I}
where for all
j\in\mathscr{I}
with
j\neq i
we have
{\bf a_{\it{j}}}={\bf a_{\it{j}}^{\prime}}
.
We will call this a
parallel perturbation in the direction

\nu_{i}
.

We first estimate
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}})
, up to suitable translation, in terms of the
L^{1}
norm of the other facets.

Lemma 3.5

(Bound on parallel facets in the perturbed direction)
.

Let
K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{par}}(K)
be a parallel perturbation in the direction
\nu_{i}
. Then,

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a}\Delta(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i}))\leq\sum_{j\neq i}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}})

(3.23)

where
u_{i}={\bf d_{\it{i}}}({\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}})\nu_{i}
.

Proof.

Step 1:
We first note that if
E,F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are Borel then,

|E\Delta F|=\sup_{\varphi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),|\varphi|\leq 1}\left[\int_{E}\varphi(x)\ dx-\int_{F}\varphi(x)\ dx\right].

(3.24)

By the Riesz Representation theorem,
(C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}})^{*}\simeq(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}),\|\cdot\|_{TV})
where
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the set of (signed) Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
|E\Delta F|=\|\mu_{E}-\mu_{F}\|_{TV}
where
\mu_{E}=\mathcal{H}^{n}\mathbin{\vrule height=4.30554pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.43057pt\vrule height=0.43057pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}E
, (
3.24
) is then a consequence of computing
\|\mu_{E}-\mu_{F}\|_{TV}
via Riesz Representation as above. We will actually apply this on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
instead of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, but for readability it is easier to state as above.

Step 2:
We next simplify the computation by showing that

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a}\Delta(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i}))=\sup_{\varphi\in C_{\nu_{i}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),|\varphi|\leq 1}\left[\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)-\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\right]

(3.25)

where

C_{\nu_{i}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{\varphi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\ |\ \varphi(x)=\varphi(x+t\nu_{i})\ \text{for all}\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

First notice that

\displaystyle\sup_{\varphi\in C_{\nu_{i}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),|\varphi|\leq 1}\left[\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)-\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ =\sup_{\phi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),|\phi|\leq 1}\left[\int_{L(F_{i}^{\bf a})}\phi(y)\ dy-\int_{L(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i})}\phi(y)\ dy\right]

(3.26)

where
\Sigma_{\nu_{i}}^{1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}}}:=\{\langle x,\nu_{i}\rangle={\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}})\}
and
L:\Sigma_{\nu_{i}}^{1+{\bf a_{i}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is a fixed isometry. This proves (
3.25
) since

\displaystyle\sup_{\phi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),|\phi|\leq 1}\left[\int_{L(F_{i}^{\bf a})}\phi(y)\ dy-\int_{L(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i})}\phi(y)\ dy\right]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{n-1}(L(F_{i}^{\bf a})\Delta L(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i}))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{n-1}(L(F_{i}^{\bf a}\Delta(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i})))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a}\Delta(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i}))

by Step 1 and the fact that
L
is an isometry. To show (
3.26
), given
\varphi\in C_{\nu_{i}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
|\varphi|\leq 1
,

\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}}\varphi(x-u_{i})\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x),

owing to the invariance of
\varphi
along
\nu_{i}
. Then, setting
\phi(y)=\varphi(L^{-1}(y))
we have

\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)-\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=\int_{L(F_{i}^{\bf a})}\phi(y)\ dy-\int_{L(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i})}\phi(y)\ dy.

Since
\phi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
and
|\phi|\leq 1
, we necessarily get

\displaystyle\sup_{\varphi\in C_{\nu_{i}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),|\varphi|\leq 1}\left[\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)-\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \leq\sup_{\phi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}),|\phi|\leq 1}\left[\int_{L(F_{i}^{\bf a})}\phi(y)\ dy-\int_{L(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i})}\phi(y)\ dy\right].

We now prove equality by showing each
\phi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
with
|\phi|\leq 1
can be obtained this way. Given
\phi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
with
|\phi|\leq 1
, define
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\varphi(x)=\phi(L(x-\langle x,\nu_{i}\rangle\nu_{i}))=\phi(L(x)-\langle x,\nu_{i}\rangle L(\nu_{i})).

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\varphi(x+t\nu_{i})=\phi(L(x+t\nu_{i})-\langle x+t\nu_{i},\nu_{i}\rangle L(\nu_{i}))=\phi(L(x)-\langle x,\nu_{i}\rangle L(\nu_{i}))=\varphi(x)

so that
\varphi\in C_{\nu_{i}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
|\varphi|\leq 1
.

Step 3:
We now show (
3.23
). Let
\varphi\in C_{\nu_{i}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
\langle\nabla\varphi(x),\nu_{i}\rangle\equiv 0
and since
\nu_{i}
is a constant vector field,
\operatorname{Div}(\nu_{i})\equiv 0
. Hence,

\int_{K^{\bf a}}\operatorname{Div}(\varphi(x)\nu_{i})\ dx=\int_{K^{\bf a}}\langle\nabla\varphi(x),\nu_{i}\rangle+\varphi(x)\operatorname{Div}(\nu_{i})\ dx=0.

In particular, applying the divergence theorem on
K^{\bf a}
with the vector field
X(x)=\varphi(x)\nu_{i}
yields

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{K^{\bf a}}\operatorname{Div}(\varphi(x)\nu_{i})\ dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial^{*}K^{\bf a}}\langle\varphi(x)\nu_{i},\nu_{K^{\bf a}}(x)\rangle\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathscr{I}}\int_{F_{j}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\langle\nu_{i},\nu_{j}\rangle\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)-\sum_{j\neq i}\cos(\theta_{ij})\int_{F_{j}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

since
\nu_{K^{\bf a}}(x)\equiv\nu_{j}
on
\operatorname{Int}(F_{j}^{\bf a})
and
\cos(\theta_{ij}):=-\langle\nu_{i},\nu_{j}\rangle
. Hence,

\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=\sum_{j\neq i}\cos(\theta_{ij})\int_{F_{j}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

(3.27)

By Step 2, we need only bound the difference

I:=\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)-\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

(3.28)

With the exact same logic we prove (
3.27
) with
{\bf a^{\prime}}
in place of
{\bf a}
. Combining this fact with (
3.27
) we see

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq i}\cos(\theta_{ij})\left[\int_{F_{j}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)-\int_{F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\neq i}\left[\int_{F_{j}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)-\int_{F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\right]

(3.29)

for all
\varphi\in C_{\nu_{i}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Notice that (
3
) relies only on the
C^{0}
data of
\varphi
. Approximating
\varphi\in C_{\nu_{i}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by
\varphi\in C_{\nu_{i}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
shows that (
3
) holds for
\varphi\in C_{\nu_{i}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In particular, taking the supremum over such
\varphi
with
|\varphi|\leq 1
and applying Step 2 on both sides,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a}\Delta(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i}))

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varphi\in C_{\nu_{i}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),|\varphi|\leq 1}\left[\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)-\int_{F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\neq i}\sup_{\varphi\in C_{\nu_{i}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),|\varphi|\leq 1}\left[\int_{F_{j}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)-\int_{F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\neq i}\sup_{\varphi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),|\varphi|\leq 1}\left[\int_{F_{j}^{\bf a}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)-\int_{F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}}\varphi(x)\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq i}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}),

(3.30)

where the third line follows from the second as
C_{\nu_{i}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Having found a bound for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a}\Delta(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i}))
in terms of
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}})
for
j\neq i
, we now bound the latter by
\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}
.

Lemma 3.6

(Bound on remaining facets)
.

With the setup in Lemma
3.5
, for
j\neq i

\begin{cases}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}})=0\ &\ \text{if}\ j\in\mathscr{N}^{\bf a}(i)^{c}\cap\mathscr{N}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(i)^{c},\\
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}})\leq CM_{\Phi}^{n-1}\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\ &\ \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3.31)

Here and later
\mathscr{N}^{\bf a}(i)
denotes the collection of indices

\mathscr{N}^{\bf a}(i):=\{j\in\mathscr{I}\ |\ F_{i}^{\bf a}\cap F_{j}^{\bf a}\neq\varnothing\}.

(3.32)

I.e., those facets which neighbor
F_{i}^{\bf a}
(on a possibly lower dimensional set).

Proof.

Suppose first that
j\in\mathscr{N}^{\bf a}(i)^{c}\cap\mathscr{N}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(i)^{c}
, so that
F_{i}^{\bf a}\cap F_{j}^{\bf a}=F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}\cap F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}=\varnothing
. We show
F_{j}^{\bf a}=F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}
. Recall that
F_{j}^{\bf a}=\operatorname{Co}(V(F_{j}^{\bf a}))
, where
V(F_{j}^{\bf a})
is the set of vertices of
F_{j}^{\bf a}
and
\operatorname{Co}(\cdot)
is the convex hull. It suffices then to show
V(F_{j}^{\bf a})=V(F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}})
. Let
v_{j}^{\bf a}\in V(F_{j}^{\bf a})
. By definition there exists a maximal collection

\mathscr{I}_{v_{j}^{\bf a}}:=\{l\in\mathscr{I}\ |\ \langle v_{j}^{\bf a},\nu_{l}\rangle={\bf d_{\it{l}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{l}}})\}

(3.33)

with
|\mathscr{I}_{v_{j}^{\bf a}}|\geq n
; again these index the facets of
K^{\bf a}
which contain
v_{j}^{\bf a}
. Since
F_{i}^{\bf a}\cap F_{j}^{\bf a}=\varnothing
, it follows that
i\not\in\mathscr{I}_{v_{j}^{\bf a}}
, and since
{\bf a_{\it{j}}}-{\bf a_{\it{j}}^{\prime}}=a_{i}\delta_{ij}
we can have in particular
{\bf a_{\it{l}}}-{\bf a_{\it{l}}^{\prime}}=0
for
l\in\mathscr{I}_{v_{j}^{\bf a}}
. Hence also

\mathscr{I}_{v_{j}^{\bf a}}=\{l\in\mathscr{I}\ |\ \langle v_{j}^{\bf a},\nu_{l}\rangle={\bf d_{\it{l}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{l}}^{\prime}})\},

(3.34)

implying
v_{j}^{\bf a}\in V(F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}})
, as it is contained in at least
n
facets of
K^{\bf a^{\prime}}
. The reverse containment follows by an analogous argument.

Next, without loss of generality we assume
{\bf a_{\it{i}}}>{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}
and let
j\in\mathscr{N}^{\bf a}(i)\cup\mathscr{N}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(i)
,
j\neq i
. Recall
x\in F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}
if and only if
\langle x,\nu_{j}\rangle={\bf d_{\it{j}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{j}}^{\prime}})
and
\langle x,\nu_{l}\rangle\leq{\bf d_{\it{l}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{l}}^{\prime}})
for all
l\in\mathscr{I}
. Additionally

{\bf a_{\it{l}}^{\prime}}={\bf a_{\it{l}}}\ \ \text{for}\ l\neq i\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ {\bf a_{\it{i}}}>{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}.

(3.35)

In particular, since
j\neq i
,
x\in F_{j}^{\bf a}
too and we have that
F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}\subset F_{j}^{\bf a}
. So,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}})=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a}\setminus F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}})
.

The idea now is to construct a nice set
E
such that
F_{j}^{\bf a}\setminus F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}\subset E
and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E)\lesssim\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}
. Consider the following sets:

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\left\{\bigg|\langle x,\nu_{i}\rangle-{\bf d_{\it{i}}}\left(1+\frac{{\bf a_{\it{i}}}+{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}}{2}\right)\bigg|\leq\frac{{\bf d_{\it{i}}}}{2}|{\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}|\right\},

\displaystyle Q_{R}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{l=1}^{n}\{|\langle x,u_{l}\rangle|\leq R\},\ \text{and}

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\nu_{j}}^{1+{\bf a_{\it{j}}}}

\displaystyle=\{\langle x,\nu_{j}\rangle={\bf d_{\it{j}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{j}}})\}

where
\{u_{1},...,u_{n-2}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
\operatorname{Span}(\{\nu_{i},\nu_{j}\})^{\perp}
,

u_{n-1}=\frac{\nu_{i}-\langle\nu_{i},\nu_{j}\rangle\nu_{j}}{|\nu_{i}-\langle\nu_{i},\nu_{j}\rangle\nu_{j}|}=\csc(\theta_{ij})\nu_{i}+\cot(\theta_{ij})\nu_{j},

the co-normal to
F_{j}
in the direction of
\nu_{i}
, and
u_{n}=\nu_{j}
. We let
E=S\cap Q_{R}\cap\Sigma_{\nu_{j}}^{1+{\bf a_{\it{j}}}}
with
R
to be decided momentarily. The purpose of the slab
S
is to restrict the height of
E
, of
Q_{R}
to restrict the
(n-2)
-dimensional “width”, and of
\Sigma_{\nu_{j}}^{1+{\bf a_{\it{j}}}}
to ensure that
E
is
(n-1)
-dimensional and
F_{j}^{\bf a}\setminus F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}\subset E
. Indeed, it follows readily from the definition of a facet, together with the fact that we consider only a perturbation in the
\nu_{i}
direction, that

F_{j}^{\bf a}\setminus F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}\subset S\cap\Sigma_{\nu_{j}}^{1+{\bf a_{\it{j}}}}.

Of course, this set is unbounded and hence not a good candidate for
E
. On the other hand,
K^{\bf a}\subset B_{M_{\Phi}^{\bf a}}(0)\subset B_{R}(0)\subset Q_{R}
, which is bounded, for any
R\geq M_{\Phi}^{\bf a}
. Thus we let
E=S\cap Q_{M_{\Phi}^{\bf a}}\cap\Sigma_{\nu_{j}}^{1+{\bf a_{\it{j}}}}
, which evidently satisfies the desired properties. Finally, the specific choice of
Q_{M_{\Phi}^{\bf a}}
allows us to easily bound
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E)
. It is oriented according to
\{u_{1},...,u_{n}\}
so that, in essence,
E
is a box with
(n-2)
-dimensional width
2M_{\Phi}^{\bf a}
a (slant) height
\csc(\theta_{ij})\cdot{\bf d_{\it{i}}}({\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}})
. It follows that
E
is isometric to a box in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
having
n-2
sides with length
2M_{\Phi}^{\bf a}
and one side of length
\csc(\theta_{ij})\cdot{\bf d_{\it{i}}}({\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}})
. The formal verification of this claim is deferred to the end of this proof. Figure
1
elucidates the situation.

Figure 1.
Left: A parallel perturbation in the direction
\nu_{i}
, with
F_{j}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}
emphasized. Right: The set
F_{j}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}
is contained within a slab
S
of height
|{\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}|
and the plane
\Sigma_{\nu_{j}}^{1+{\bf a_{\it{j}}}}
. As
F_{j}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}}
is slanted (according to
\nu_{j}
), its height is bounded by
\csc(\theta_{ij})\cdot{\bf d_{\it{i}}}|{\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}|
.

Thus,

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}})\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E)={\bf d_{\it{i}}}(2M_{\Phi}^{\bf a})^{n-2}\csc(\theta_{ij})|{\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}|.

(3.36)

Recall
[
Sch13
, Theorem 1.8.11]
that (
3.11
) holds and so

d_{H}(K,K^{\bf a})=\|f^{\bf a}-f\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{n-1})}.

(3.37)

Thus for any
\nu\in S^{n-1}
,

f^{\bf a}(\nu)\leq|f^{\bf a}(\nu)-f(\nu)|+f(\nu)\leq d_{H}(K,K^{\bf a})+M_{\Phi}.

(3.38)

Taking the supremum over
\nu\in S^{n-1}
and applying Lemma
3.4
, there exists
C(n,K)>0
such that

M_{\Phi}^{\bf a}\leq M_{\Phi}+d_{H}(K,K^{\bf a})\leq CM_{\Phi}

(3.39)

as
\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}<1
. Of course, we can absorb
M_{\Phi}
into this constant but we separate them to highlight the scaling. In total,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leq{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(2CM_{\Phi})^{n-2}\csc(\theta_{ij})|{\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}|

(3.40)

\displaystyle\leq CM_{\Phi}^{n-1}\csc(\theta_{ij})\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

(3.41)

since
{\bf d_{\it{i}}}=f(\nu_{i})\leq\sup_{\nu\in S^{n-1}}f(\nu)=M_{\Phi}
.

To prove that
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(E)={\bf d_{\it{i}}}(2M_{\Phi}^{\bf a})^{n-2}\csc(\theta_{ij})|{\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}|
we show
\partial E
contains orthogonal segments of the desired lengths, and that the segments cannot be extended. Set

v=v_{1}\nu_{i}+v_{2}\nu_{j},\ \ \ \ \ \begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\csc(\theta_{ij})\begin{pmatrix}\csc(\theta_{ij})&\cot(\theta_{ij})\\
\cot(\theta_{ij})&\csc(\theta_{ij})\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}})\\
{\bf d_{\it{j}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{j}}})\end{pmatrix}.

The vector
v
is chosen precisely so that
\langle v,u_{1}\rangle=...=\langle v,u_{n-2}\rangle=0
and

\displaystyle\langle v,\nu_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=v_{1}-\cos(\theta_{ij})v_{2}={\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}})

\displaystyle\langle v,\nu_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\cos(\theta_{ij})v_{1}+v_{2}={\bf d_{\it{j}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{j}}}).

Together these imply that
tu_{l}+v\in\partial S\cap Q_{M_{\Phi}^{\bf a}}\cap\Sigma_{\nu_{j}}^{1+{\bf a_{\it{j}}}}\subset\partial E
for
t
sufficiently small. With this choice, the maximal domain for
l=1,...,n-2
is
[-M_{\Phi}^{\bf a},M_{\Phi}^{\bf a}]
. Now consider for
t\in[t^{-},t^{+}]
the segment
\gamma_{n-1}(t)=-tu_{n-1}+v
with
v
as before. We wish to find the maximal interval
[t^{-},t^{+}]
such that
\gamma_{n-1}([t^{-},t^{+}])\subset E
. That is, we find
t^{-},t^{+}
such that

\gamma_{n-1}(t^{-})={\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}}),\ \ \ \ \ \ \gamma_{n-1}(t^{+})={\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}).

Notice that at
t=0
we have

\gamma_{n-1}(0)=\langle v,\nu_{i}\rangle={\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}}).

On the other hand, by construction of
u_{n-1}
,

\displaystyle\langle\gamma_{n-1}(t),\nu_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle-tu_{n-1}+v,\nu_{i}\rangle

(3.42)

\displaystyle=-t\langle\csc(\theta_{ij})\nu_{i}+\cot(\theta_{ij})\nu_{j},\nu_{i}\rangle+{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}})=-t\sin(\theta_{ij})+{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}})

(3.43)

so that at
t^{+}=\csc(\theta_{ij})\cdot{\bf d_{\it{i}}}({\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}})>0
we have

\gamma_{n-1}(t^{+})={\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}}).

So, the maximal interval is
[0,\csc(\theta_{ij})\cdot{\bf d_{\it{i}}}({\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}})]
. It follows that
E
is isometric to a box in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
having
n-2
sides with length
2M_{\Phi}^{\bf a}
and one side of length
\csc(\theta_{ij})\cdot{\bf d_{\it{i}}}({\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}})
, as desired.
∎

We are now ready to generally bound the
L^{1}
norm of parallel facets under a parallel perturbation.

Proof of Proposition 3.1.

First notice that if
{\bf a}
and
{\bf a^{\prime}}
are a parallel perturbation in the direction
\nu_{i}
then for some
C(n,K)>0
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{if}\ j\in\mathscr{N}^{\bf a}(i)^{c}\cap\mathscr{N}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(i)^{c}\ \text{and}

(3.44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leq CM_{\Phi}^{n-1}\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}},\ \ \ \text{if}\ j\in\mathscr{N}^{\bf a}(i)\cup\mathscr{N}^{\bf a^{\prime}}(i),\ j\neq i

(3.45)

by Lemma
3.6
. So, we are only missing an
\ell^{\infty}
bound on
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a}\Delta(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i}))
. However, by Lemma
3.5
, we can estimate this in terms of the contributions from
j\neq i
, so that

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a}\Delta(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i}))\leq\sum_{j\neq i}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{j}^{\bf a}\Delta F_{j}^{\bf a^{\prime}})\leq CM_{\Phi}^{n-1}\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}.

(3.46)

Now given generic
K^{\bf a},K^{\bf a^{\prime}}
we set
{\bf a_{0}}={\bf a^{\prime}}
and define
\bf a_{i}
for
i=1,...,N
by

{\bf a_{i,\it{j}}}-{\bf a_{i-1,\it{j}}}=({\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}})\delta_{ij},\ \ \ j=1,...,N.

Note that
{\bf a_{i}}
and
{\bf a_{i-1}}
are a parallel perturbation in the direction
\nu_{i}
. So we can apply the estimates (
3.44
), (
3.45
), and (
3.46
) as above for each pair
K^{\bf a_{i-1}}
and
K^{\bf a_{i}}
. Defining
u_{i}={\bf d_{\it{i}}}({\bf a_{\it{i}}}-{\bf a_{\it{i}}^{\prime}})\nu_{i}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a}\Delta(F_{i}^{\bf a^{\prime}}+u_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}((F_{i}^{\bf a_{j}}+u_{i})\Delta(F_{i}^{\bf a_{j-1}}+u_{i}))+\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a_{j}}\Delta F_{i}^{\bf a_{j-1}})

(3.47)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a_{i}}\Delta(F_{i}^{\bf a_{i-1}}+u_{i}))

(3.48)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a_{i}}\Delta(F_{i}^{\bf a_{i-1}}+u_{i}))+\sum_{j\neq i}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a_{j}}\Delta F_{i}^{\bf a_{j-1}}).

(3.49)

By (

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a_{i}}\Delta(F_{i}^{\bf a_{i-1}}+u_{i}))\leq CM_{\Phi}^{n-1}\|{\bf a}-{\bf a^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

(3.51)

Together, (

Strong quantitative stability for parallel polytopes

Our goal in this section is to prove a version of Theorem
1.1
applicable to polytopes
K^{\bf a}
obtained from Theorem
A
. This is achieved if we can prove the result among the class
\mathcal{C}(K)\subset\mathcal{C}_{\text{par}}(K)
of parallel polytopes to
K
with the same volume as
K
. Precisely, we prove:

Proposition 3.7
.

There exists an
a_{0}(n,K)>0
and a constant
C(n,K)>0
such that whenever
K^{\bf a}\in\mathcal{C}(K)
with
\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq a_{0}
, and
|K\Delta K^{\bf a}|=\inf\{|K\Delta(K^{\bf a}+x)|\ |\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
, it holds

\beta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})^{2}\leq C\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a}).

Before proving Proposition
3.7
let us introduce some notation and technical lemmas. Set

{\bf m}=(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{1}),...,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{N})),\ \ {\bf m^{a}}=(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{1}^{\bf a}),...,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{N}^{\bf a})).

In terms of these quantities, the following initial estimate on
\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})
is easy to show.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
K^{\bf a}\in\mathcal{C}(K)
. Then,

\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})=-\frac{1}{n|K|}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf d_{\it{i}}}{\bf a_{\it{i}}}{\bf m_{\it{i}}^{a}}.

(3.52)

Furthermore, there exists a
C(n,K)>0
such that if
|K\Delta K^{\bf a}|=\inf\{|K\Delta(K^{\bf a}+x)|\ |\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
then

\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})\geq C(n,K)\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}^{2}.

(3.53)

We write
\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})\geq O(\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}^{2})
to denote this property.

The latter, (
3.53
), says precisely that
\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})
is non-degenerate with respect to
\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}
.

Proof.

First we can express the anisotropic perimeter and volume of
K
and
K^{\bf a}
in terms of the above quantities as

\Phi(K)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf d_{\it{i}}}{\bf m_{\it{i}}},\ \ \ \Phi(K^{\bf a})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf d_{\it{i}}}{\bf m_{\it{i}}^{a}},\ \ \ |K|=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf d_{\it{i}}}{\bf m_{\it{i}}},\ \ \ |K^{\bf a}|=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}}){\bf m_{\it{i}}^{a}}.

Since
K^{\bf a}\in\mathcal{C}(K)
we have by definition
|K^{\bf a}|=|K|
. Applying this volume constraint yields

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf d_{\it{i}}}{\bf m_{\it{i}}}=|K|=|K^{\bf a}|=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf d_{\it{i}}}{\bf m_{\it{i}}^{a}}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf d_{\it{i}}}{\bf a_{\it{i}}}{\bf m_{\it{i}}^{a}}.

(3.54)

Next, by (
3.54
) and the definition of
\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})
,

n|K|\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})=\Phi(K^{\bf a})-\Phi(K)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf d_{\it{i}}}({\bf m_{\it{i}}^{a}}-{\bf m_{\it{i}}})=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf d_{\it{i}}}{\bf a_{\it{i}}}{\bf m_{\it{i}}^{a}},

which is exactly (
3.52
).

As for (
3.53
), by the quantitative stability with the Fraenkel asymmetry, (
1.4
), we have that there exists
C_{1}(n)>0
such that

\frac{|K\Delta K^{\bf a}|^{2}}{|K|^{2}}=\frac{1}{|K|^{2}}\inf\{|K\Delta(K^{\bf a}+x)|\ |\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}^{2}=\alpha_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})^{2}\leq C_{1}\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a}),

(3.55)

where we have used the fact that
K^{\bf a}\in\mathcal{C}(K)
satisfies
|K^{\bf a}|=|K|
. Next by Lemma
3.4
there exists a constant
C_{2}(n,K)>0
such that
\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq C_{2}|K\Delta K^{\bf a}|
. Combined with (
3.55
) we have
C\|{\bf a}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}^{2}\leq\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})
, with
C=1/(C_{1}C_{2}^{2}|K|^{2})
, as desired.
∎

Finally we provide an initial estimate on
\beta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
K^{\bf a}\in\mathcal{C}(K)
. Then

\beta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})^{2}\leq\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})+\frac{n-1}{n|K|}\left(A-B\right)

(3.56)

where

A=\sum_{i\in\mathscr{I}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{{\bf d_{\it{i}}}}{r}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(rF_{i}\cap(K\setminus K^{\bf a}))\ dr,\ \ \ \ \ B=\sum_{i\in\mathscr{I}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{{\bf d_{\it{i}}}}{r}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(rF_{i}\cap(K^{\bf a}\setminus K))\ dr.

(3.57)

Proof.

Recall from (
2.1
) that we can write
\beta_{\Phi}
in terms of
\gamma_{\Phi}
,

\beta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})^{2}=\frac{\Phi(K^{\bf a})-(n-1)\gamma_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})}{n|K|^{1/n}|K^{\bf a}|^{(n-1)/n}}=\frac{\Phi(K^{\bf a})}{n|K|}-\frac{(n-1)\gamma_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})}{n|K|},

where
\gamma_{\Phi}(E)
is defined by

\gamma_{\Phi}(E)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{E}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x-y)}\ dx.

Testing at the origin we get

\displaystyle\beta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Phi(K^{\bf a})}{n|K|}-\frac{n-1}{n|K|}\int_{K^{\bf a}}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x)}\ dx

(3.58)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\Phi(K^{\bf a})-\Phi(K)}{n|K|}\right)+\frac{\Phi(K)}{n|K|}-\frac{n-1}{n|K|}\int_{K^{\bf a}}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x)}\ dx.

(3.59)

Note that
\Phi(K)=(n-1)\gamma_{\Phi}(K)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\beta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})+\frac{n-1}{n|K|}\left(\int_{K}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x)}\ dx-\int_{K^{\bf a}}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x)}\ dx\right)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a})+\frac{n-1}{n|K|}\left(\int_{K\setminus K^{\bf a}}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x)}\ dx-\int_{K^{\bf a}\setminus K}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x)}\ dx\right).

The weighted anisotropic co-area formula lets us compute these integrals more directly. Indeed, recall that for any Borel
g:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
and Lipschitz
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, and open
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
it states that

\int_{\Omega}f(\nabla u(x))g(f_{*}(x))\ dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(\{u<r\};\Omega)g(r)\ dr,

(3.60)

where the relative anisotropic perimeter
\Phi(E;\Omega)
is defined as

\Phi(E;\Omega)=\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega}f(\nu_{E}(x))\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

(3.61)

In particular, selecting
g(r)=1/r
and
u(x)=f_{*}(x)
we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x)}\ dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r}\Phi(rK;\Omega)\ dr

(3.62)

as
K=\{f_{*}<1\}
and the Fenchel inequality,

\langle x,\nu\rangle\leq f_{*}(x)f(\nu)

(3.63)

implies that
f(\nabla f_{*}(x))\equiv 1
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For more details see
[
Neu16
]
. Applying (
3.62
) with
\Omega=K\setminus K^{\bf a}
and
\Omega=K^{\bf a}\setminus K
gives

\displaystyle\int_{K\setminus K^{\bf a}}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x)}\ dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r}\Phi(rK;K\setminus K^{\bf a})\ dr=\sum_{i\in\mathscr{I}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{{\bf d_{\it{i}}}}{r}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(rF_{i}\cap(K\setminus K^{\bf a}))\ dr

\displaystyle\int_{K^{\bf a}\setminus K}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x)}\ dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r}\Phi(rK;K^{\bf a}\setminus K)\ dr=\sum_{i\in\mathscr{I}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{{\bf d_{\it{i}}}}{r}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(rF_{i}\cap(K^{\bf a}\setminus K))\ dr.

In the latter integrals we make use of the fact that
\partial^{*}K=\operatorname{Int}(F_{1})\cup...\cup\operatorname{Int}(F_{N})
and that
\nu_{K}\equiv\nu_{i}
on
\operatorname{Int}(F_{i})
, so
f(\nu_{K})\equiv{\bf d_{\it{i}}}
on
\operatorname{Int}(F_{i})
.
∎

In light of (
3.57
), to find the appropriate bounds on
A
and
B
we effectively only need to study

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{r}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(rF_{i}\cap(K\setminus K^{\bf a}))\ dr\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(rF_{i}\cap(K^{\bf a}\setminus K))\ dr

for
i\in\mathscr{I}
. The idea now is that whether or not these integrals contribute to
A
and
B
for a particular
i\in\mathscr{I}
depends on its inclusion in one of the following collections:

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}^{-}=\{i\in\mathscr{I}\ |\ {\bf a_{\it{i}}}<0\}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \mathscr{I}^{+}=\mathscr{I}\setminus\mathscr{I}^{+}.

(3.64)

To control the first integral we will subdivide these collections so as to estimate
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(rF_{i}\cap(K\setminus K^{\bf a}))
easily. As for the latter integral, we will show that
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(rF_{i}\cap(K\setminus K^{\bf a}))
can be written as
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(F_{i}^{\bf a_{1}}\cap F_{i}^{\bf a_{2}})
for judicious choices of
{\bf a_{1}}
and
{\bf a_{2}}
, then make use of Proposition
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
.

Let us observe the following simple facts. By convexity
rF_{i}\subset K
if and only if
0\leq r<1
. On the other hand,

K^{\bf a}\subset\{\langle x,\nu_{i}\rangle<{\bf d_{\it{i}}}(1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}})\}
while

rF_{i}\subset\{\langle x,\nu_{i}\rangle=r{\bf d_{\it{i}}}\}
. It follows that
rF_{i}\not\subset K^{\bf a}
whenever
1+{\bf a_{\it{i}}}\leq r
.

Focusing on the case
0<r<1

∎

From Lemma
4.4
we are ready to prove Proposition
4.2
.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

We prove something more general. Let
0<\varepsilon<1/2
and choose
\varepsilon_{0}<\eta
as in Lemma
2.2
. Up to decreasing
\varepsilon_{0}
(requiring it to now also depend on
n
and
K
), the bounds
(
4.2
) and (
4.3
) on the Hausdorff distances of
E
and
K^{\bf a*}
to
K
respectively imply that

(1-\varepsilon)K\subset E,K^{{\bf a}*}\subset(1+\varepsilon)K.

(4.5)

Next, by the control on the center from Lemma
2.2
we have
|y_{E}|<\varepsilon
. However, by Remark
4.3
, up to further decreasing
\varepsilon_{0}
, we can also apply Lemma
2.2
to
K^{\bf a*}
and conclude that
|y_{K^{{\bf a}*}}|<\varepsilon
too.

Recall that
f_{*}(x)=\sup\{\langle x,\nu\rangle\ |\ f(\nu)\leq 1\}
. We estimate
f_{*}(x-y)
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by a direct application of Cauchy-Schwarz to obtain

f_{*}(x)-|y|\leq f_{*}(x-y)\leq f_{*}(x)+|y|.

(4.6)

Now, by (
4.5
) we see that if
x\in E\cup K^{{\bf a}*}
then
f_{*}(x)\leq 1+\varepsilon
owing to the 1-homogeneity of
f_{*}
. Similarly, if
x\in(E\cap K^{{\bf a}*})^{c}
then
f_{*}(x)\geq 1-\varepsilon
. Accordingly, for all
x\in E\Delta K^{{\bf a}*}
,

(1-\varepsilon)-|y|\leq f_{*}(x-y)\leq(1+\varepsilon)+|y|.

(4.7)

If
\gamma_{\Phi}(E)\leq\gamma_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*})
then, choosing
y=y_{K^{{\bf a}*}}
we get

\displaystyle\gamma_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*})-\gamma_{\Phi}(E)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{K^{{\bf a}*}}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x-y_{K^{{\bf a}*}})}\ dx-\int_{E}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x-y_{K^{{\bf a}*}})}\ dx

\displaystyle=\int_{K^{{\bf a}*}\setminus E}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x-y_{K^{{\bf a}*}})}\ dx-\int_{E\setminus K^{{\bf a}*}}\frac{1}{f_{*}(x-y_{K^{{\bf a}*}})}\ dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{K^{{\bf a}*}\setminus E}\frac{1}{1-2\varepsilon}\ dx-\int_{E\setminus K^{{\bf a}*}}\frac{1}{1+2\varepsilon}\ dx\leq 2\varepsilon|E\Delta K^{{\bf a}*}|,

where we have applied (
4.7
) in both directions. The same inequality holds if
\gamma_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*})\leq\gamma_{\Phi}(E)
using
y_{E}
; it is for this reason that we need control on the containment of both
E
and
K^{{\bf a}*}
. Thus,

|\gamma_{\Phi}(E)-\gamma_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*})|<2\varepsilon|E\Delta K^{{\bf a}*}|

and setting
\varepsilon=1/4
gives the result.
∎

We are finally ready to prove Proposition
4.1
.

Proof.

We use the properties of
\beta_{\Phi}
and
\gamma_{\Phi}
, together with Theorem
A
and the Lipschitz continuity of
\gamma_{\Phi}
from Proposition
4.2
to conclude the result.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}
be small enough to invoke both Proposition
4.2
and Theorem
A
. Then, by the latter, for some
C_{1}(n,K)>0

\Phi(E)-\Phi(K^{{\bf a}*})\geq C_{1}|E\Delta K^{{\bf a}*}|.

(4.8)

By definition of
\delta_{\Phi}
we can rewrite (
4.8
) as

\frac{C_{1}}{n|K|}|E\Delta K^{{\bf a}*}|\leq\frac{\Phi(E)}{n|K|}-1+1-\frac{\Phi(K^{{\bf a}*})}{n|K|}=\delta_{\Phi}(E)-\delta_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*})

(4.9)

as
|E|=|K^{{\bf a}*}|=|K|
. Notice immediately (
4.9
) implies

\delta_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*})\leq\delta_{\Phi}(E).

(4.10)

Now let us turn to estimating
\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}
. Again by definition,

\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}=\frac{\Phi(E)}{n|K|}-\frac{(n-1)\gamma_{\Phi}(E)}{n|K|}.

(4.11)

To handle the
\gamma_{\Phi}(E)
term we appeal to the Lipschitz continuity of
\gamma_{\Phi}
in Proposition
4.2
to find

|\gamma_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*})-\gamma_{\Phi}(E)|\leq\frac{1}{2}|E\Delta K^{{\bf a}*}|.

(4.12)

Rearranging (
4.12
) and substituting in (
4.11
) gives

\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}\leq\frac{\Phi(E)}{n|K|}-\frac{(n-1)\gamma_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*})}{n|K|}+\frac{n-1}{2n|K|}|E\Delta K^{{\bf a}*}|.

(4.13)

We must bound each term on the right-hand side of (
4.13
) by
\delta_{\Phi}(E)
. We start with the first two terms. By adding and subtracting
\Phi(K^{{\bf a}*})/(n|K|)
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(E)}{n|K|}-\frac{(n-1)\gamma_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*})}{n|K|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi(E)}{n|K|}-\frac{\Phi(K^{{\bf a}*})}{n|K|}+\beta_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*})^{2}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\Phi}(E)-\delta_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*})+\beta_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*})^{2}.

(4.14)

Since
K^{{\bf a}*}
is parallel to
K
, up to translating by some
x^{\bf a*}
we can apply Proposition
3.7
and find
C_{2}(n,K)
such that
\beta_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*}+x^{\bf a*})^{2}\leq C_{2}\delta_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*}+x^{\bf a^{*}})
. However, both
\beta_{\Phi}
and
\delta_{\Phi}
are translation invariant as seen in Remark
2.1
. Consequently,
\beta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a*})^{2}\leq C_{2}\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a*})
. Applying this together with (
4.14
) in (
4.13
) yields

\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}\leq\delta_{\Phi}(E)+(C_{2}-1)\delta_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*})+\frac{n-1}{2n|K|}|E\Delta K^{{\bf a}*}|.

(4.15)

As for the remaining term, we directly apply our bound (
4.9
) on
|E\Delta K^{\bf a*}|
to conclude

\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}\leq\left(1+\frac{(n-1)}{2C_{1}}\right)\delta_{\Phi}(E)+\left(\frac{(n-1)}{2C_{1}}+C_{2}-1\right)\delta_{\Phi}(K^{{\bf a}*}).

Letting
C_{3}=\max\{0,(n-1)/(2C_{1})+C_{2}-1\}
we apply
\delta_{\Phi}(K^{\bf a*})\leq\delta_{\Phi}(E)
from (
4.10
) to conclude

\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}\leq\left(1+C_{3}+\frac{(n-1)}{2C_{1}}\right)\delta_{\Phi}(E).

So setting

C=1+\max\left\{0,\frac{(n-1)}{2C_{1}(n,K)}+C_{2}(n,K)-1\right\}+\frac{(n-1)}{2C_{1}(n,K)},

which evidently depends only on
n
and
K
and is positive, the proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1

We are finally ready to prove the main theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists
C(n,K)>0
such that for any
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a set of finite perimeter with
0<|E|<\infty
,

\alpha_{\Phi}(E)^{2}+\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}\leq C\delta_{\Phi}(E).

Following
[
Neu16
]
, we use the same selection principle argument. The proof essentially follows that in
[
Neu16
, Theorem 1.1]
; we include it here for the sake of completeness.

Proof.

By (
1.2
) it suffices to show there exists
C(n,K)>0
such that

\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}\leq C\delta_{\Phi}(E).

(4.16)

Moreover, by Lemma
2.4
we may assume
E\subset B_{R_{0}}
.

Step 1: Generating a convergent sequence.
We will show Theorem
1.1
with a proof by contradiction. The first step is to take a minimizing sequence along which the claim is supposedly false and show it converges to (a translation of)
K
.

Suppose (
4.16
) is false. Then we can find a sequence of sets
\{E_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
in
B_{R_{0}}
such that
|E_{j}|=|K|
,
\Phi(E_{j})\to\Phi(K)
, but

\Phi(E_{j})<\Phi(K)+C_{2}\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}

(4.17)

for
C_{2}>0
to be chosen later. The sequence
\{E_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
will be replaced with a new one
\{F_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
with more regularity to exploit. Since
E_{j}\subset B_{R_{0}}
for all
j
and
P(E)\leq\Phi(E)/m_{\Phi}<\infty
,
we have by compactness, see
[
Mag12
, Theorem 12.26]
, that
E_{j}\xrightarrow{L^{1}}E_{\infty}
up to subsequence. Since
|E_{j}|=|K|
for all
j
we also have
|E_{\infty}|=|K|
. By (
1.2
) and Proposition
2.3
part ii),

n|K|\leq\Phi(E_{\infty})\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\Phi(E_{j})=\Phi(K)=n|K|,

so that
E_{\infty}=K
up to translation. Futhermore, by Proposition
2.3
parts i) and ii) and (
2.1
),

\lim_{j\to\infty}\gamma_{\Phi}(E_{j})=\gamma_{\Phi}(K)=\frac{n|K|}{n-1},\ \ \ \lim_{j\to\infty}\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})=\lim_{j\to\infty}\left[\frac{\Phi(E_{j})}{n|K|}-\frac{n-1}{n|K|}\gamma_{\Phi}(E_{j})\right]=0.

Step 2: Replacing
E_{j}
with almost-minimizers of
\Phi
.
We replace the sequence
\{E_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
with a new sequence
\{F_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
which minimize
Q_{j}
among sets
E\subset B_{R_{0}}
, where

Q_{j}(E):=\Phi(E)+\frac{\varepsilon_{\Phi}|K|}{8}|\beta_{\Phi}(E)^{2}-\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}|+\Lambda\big||E|-|K|\big|

(4.18)

and
\Lambda>4n
is fixed. Since the functionals
Q_{j}
are slight modifications of the (volume-constrained) anisotropic perimeter, the
F_{j}
are almost minimizers. The regularity theory of almost minimizers then allows us to show that for some
R_{0}>0

i)

The following bounds hold for
\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})
and
\Phi(F_{j})
:

\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}\leq 2\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})^{2}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \Phi(F_{j})\leq\Phi(E_{j}).

(4.19)

In particular, with the contradictory hypothesis (
4.17
),

\Phi(F_{j})<\Phi(K)+2C_{2}\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})^{2}.

(4.20)

ii)

|F_{j}|=|K|
for
j
sufficiently large, and

iii)

F_{j}
converges in
L^{1}
to
K
,

Items i) and ii) allow us to replace
E_{j}
with
F_{j}
and item iii) recovers the same information obtained in Step 1.

To show (
4.19
), observe first that Lemma
2.6
guarantees a minimizer of
Q_{j}
among
E\subset B_{R_{0}}
exists – let
F_{j}\in\argmin\{Q_{j}(E)\ |\ E\subset B_{R_{0}}\}
. Since
F_{j}
minimizes
Q_{j}
, testing with
E_{j}
yields

\Phi(F_{j})+\frac{\varepsilon_{\Phi}|K|}{8}|\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})^{2}-\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}|+\Lambda\big||F_{j}|-|K|\big|\leq\Phi(E_{j})

(4.21)

where we have used the fact that
Q_{j}(E_{j})=\Phi(E_{j})
by construction. In particular,
\Phi(F_{j})\leq\Phi(E_{j})
. As for the bound
\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}\leq 2\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})^{2}
, combining (
4.21
) with (
4.17
) we get

\Phi(F_{j})+\frac{\varepsilon_{\Phi}|K|}{8}|\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})^{2}-\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}|+\Lambda\big||F_{j}|-|K|\big|\leq\Phi(E_{j})\leq\Phi(K)+C_{2}\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}.

(4.22)

On the other hand, recall Lemma
2.5
, which quantifies the fact that
K
minimizes the volume-constrained minimization of
\Phi
. This lets us bound
\Phi(K)
in terms of
F_{j}
in (
4.22
) and gives

\Phi(F_{j})+\frac{\varepsilon_{\Phi}|K|}{8}|\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})^{2}-\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}|+\Lambda\big||F_{j}|-|K|\big|\leq\Phi(F_{j})+\Lambda\big||F_{j}|-|K|\big|+C_{2}\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}.

(4.23)

It immediately follows that

\frac{\varepsilon_{\Phi}|K|}{8}|\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})^{2}-\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}|\leq C_{2}\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}.

(4.24)

From Step 1 we have
\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})\to 0
, and so
\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})\to 0
too. Next, rearranging (
4.24
) and choosing
C_{2}(n,K)
sufficiently small,

\frac{\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}}{2}\leq\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}-\frac{8C_{2}}{\varepsilon_{\Phi}|K|}\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}\leq\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})^{2},\ \ \ \text{i.e.}\ \ \beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}\leq 2\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})^{2}.

(4.25)

This completes the proof of i).

We now show item ii). Let
r_{j}>0
be such that
|r_{j}F_{j}|=|K|
. By (
2.3
) we see that
r_{j}^{n}|K|/2\leq r_{j}^{n}|F_{j}|=|K|
, and so
r_{j}^{n}\leq 2
. By Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
4.4
, for
j
sufficiently large we have
F_{j}\subset 2K
. Combined with
r_{j}\leq 2
, we see
2F_{j}\subset 4K
. Then, as long as
R_{0}>4M_{\Phi}
we have
r_{j}F_{j}\subset 4K\subset B_{R_{0}}
. This allows us to test
r_{j}F_{j}
against
F_{j}
in
Q_{j}
and obtain

\displaystyle\Phi(F_{j})+\frac{\varepsilon_{\Phi}|K|}{8}|\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})^{2}-\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}|+\Lambda\big||F_{j}|-|K|\big|

(4.26)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(r_{j}F_{j})+\frac{\varepsilon_{\Phi}|K|}{8}|\beta_{\Phi}(r_{j}F_{j})^{2}-\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}|=r^{n-1}\Phi(F_{j})+\frac{\varepsilon_{\Phi}|K|}{8}|\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})^{2}-\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}|

(4.27)

owing to the fact that
|r_{j}F_{j}|=|K|
and the scale invariance of
\beta_{\Phi}
(see Remark
2.1
). Thus,

\Phi(F_{j})+\Lambda\big||F_{j}|-|K|\big|\leq r_{j}^{n-1}\Phi(F_{j}).

(4.28)

Since the latter term on the left-hand side of (
4.28
) is non-negative, we see that
r_{j}\geq 1
for all
j
. Moreover, as
F_{j}\xrightarrow{L^{1}}K
we have
|F_{j}|\to|K|
, i.e.
r_{j}\to 1
. Suppose that, up to a subsequence,
r_{j}>1
. Since
|F_{j}|=|K|/r_{j}^{n}
, rearranging (
4.28
) gives

\Lambda|K|\leq\left(\frac{r_{j}^{n}(r_{j}^{n-1}-1)}{r_{j}^{n}-1}\right)\Phi(F_{j}).

(4.29)

Next, since
r_{j}^{n}(r_{j}^{n-1}-1)/(r_{j}^{n}-1)\to(n-1)/n
as
r_{j}\to 1^{+}
we have for any
0<\varepsilon<1/n
and
j
sufficiently large that the right-hand side of (
4.29
) is bounded by
(1-\varepsilon)\Phi(F_{j})
. Finally, testing
F_{j}
against
K
in
Q_{j}
gives

\Phi(F_{j})\leq Q_{j}(F_{j})\leq Q_{j}(K)=n|K|+\frac{\varepsilon_{\Phi}|K|}{8}\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}.

(4.30)

Combining (
4.29
) with (
4.30
) for
j
sufficiently large and
\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})\to 0
,

\Lambda|K|\leq(1-\varepsilon)\Phi(F_{j})\leq(1-\varepsilon)\left(n|K|+\frac{\varepsilon_{\Phi}|K|}{8}\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}\right)\leq n|K|.

However, we assumed that
\Lambda>4n
, a contradiction to the supposition that
r_{j}>1
.

To show iii) we need a uniform upper bound on
P(F_{j})
. From (
2.3
) we can control
\Phi(F_{j})
in terms of
\Phi(K)
, and so

P(F_{j})\leq\frac{1}{m_{\Phi}}\Phi(F_{j})\leq\frac{2\Phi(K)}{m_{\Phi}}<\infty.

Thus up to subsequence
F_{j}\xrightarrow{L^{1}}F_{\infty}
for some
F_{\infty}\subset B_{R_{0}}
. Appealing to (
4.22
), since
\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})\to 0
,

\displaystyle\Phi(F_{\infty})\hskip-2.0pt+\hskip-2.0pt\Lambda\liminf_{j\to\infty}\big||F_{j}|-|K|\big|

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\left[\Phi(F_{j})\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\frac{\varepsilon_{\Phi}|K|}{8}|\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})-\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})|^{2}\hskip-2.0pt+\hskip-2.0pt\Lambda\big||F_{j}|-|K|\big|\right]

(4.31)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\left[\Phi(K)+C_{2}\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}\right]=\Phi(K),

(4.32)

which implies that
F_{\infty}=K
up to translation by (
1.2
). Without loss of generality, since all the involved quantities are translation invariant (see Remark
2.1
), we may translate each
F_{j}
so that
|F_{j}\Delta K|=\inf\{|F_{j}\Delta(K+x)\ |\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
. Thus,
F_{j}\xrightarrow{L^{1}}K
.

Step 3: Deriving a contradiction to (
4.17
).
We now use the properties of
F_{j}
established in Step 2 to show that
F_{j}=K
for
j
sufficiently large. Since
\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})
controls
\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})
, and we assumed that
\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})>0
in (
4.17
), this gives us a contradiction.

Recall that the
F_{j}
minimize
Q_{j}
, and so by Lemma
2.7
they satisfy the uniform density estimates. Invoking Proposition
4.1
, we obtain
\varepsilon_{0}(n,K)>0
and
C(n,K)>0
, so that for
j
sufficiently large as to ensure
|F_{j}\Delta K|\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, we have

\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})^{2}\leq C_{1}(n,K)\delta_{\Phi}(F_{j}).

(4.33)

Since the
F_{j}
minimize
Q_{j}
, we may test against
E_{j}
and see that

\Phi(F_{j})\leq\Phi(F_{j})+\frac{\varepsilon_{\Phi}|K|}{8}|\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})^{2}-\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}|\leq\Phi(E_{j}).

Recalling (
4.20
) from Step 2 item i), we can replace
F_{j}
with
E_{j}
:

\Phi(F_{j})\leq\Phi(E_{j})\leq\Phi(K)+C_{2}\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})^{2}\leq\Phi(K)+2C_{2}\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})^{2}.

Finally, we use the control on the oscillation index by the deficit established in (
4.33
) to yield

\Phi(F_{j})\leq\Phi(K)+2C_{2}C_{1}(n,K)\delta_{\Phi}(F_{j}).

Letting
C_{2}=n|K|/(2C_{1})
we see

\Phi(F_{j})\leq\Phi(K)+2C_{1}C_{2}\delta_{\Phi}(F_{j})=\Phi(K)+n|K|\left(\frac{\Phi(F_{j})}{n|K|}-1\right)=\Phi(F_{j}),

implying that
F_{j}=K
for
j
sufficiently large (since we translated the
F_{j}
to minimize the
L^{1}
distance to
K
). However, this means that
\beta_{\Phi}(F_{j})=0
for
j
sufficiently large, a contradiction to (
4.19
) as
\beta_{\Phi}(E_{j})>0
.
∎

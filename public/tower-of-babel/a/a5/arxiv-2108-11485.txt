Chung-type law of the iterated logarithm and exact moduli of continuity for a class of anisotropic Gaussian random fields
Cheuk Yin Lee, Yimin Xiao

Abstract
. We establish a Chung-type law of the iterated logarithm and the exact local and uniform moduli of continuity for a large class of anisotropic Gaussian random fields with a harmonizable-type integral representation and the property of strong local nondeterminism. Compared with the existing results in the literature, our results do not require the assumption of stationary increments and provide more precise upper and lower bounds for the limiting constants. The results are applicable to the solutions of a class of linear stochastic partial differential equations driven by a fractional-colored Gaussian noise, including the stochastic heat equation.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification

60G15, 60G60, 60G17.

Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to establish a general framework that is useful for studying
the regularity properties of sample functions of anisotropic Gaussian random fields and
can be directly applied to the solutions of linear SPDEs. This is mainly motivated by

[
6
]
and
[
27
]
.
We consider a class of Gaussian random fields
\{v(x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\}
that satisfy Assumption
2.1 in
[
6
]
(see Assumption
2.1
below) and the property of strong local
nondeterminism, or strong LND for short, with respect to an anisotropic metric (see
Assumption
2.2
below). For these Gaussian random fields, we prove some limit
theorems that provide precise information about the oscillation behavior of the sample
function
x\mapsto v(x)
.

The main results of this paper are as follows. We prove a Chung-type law of the iterated
logarithm (LIL) in Theorem
4.4
, the exact local and uniform moduli of
continuity in Theorems
5.2
and
6.1
, respectively. Our strategy is
to first prove a zero–one law for each of the limit theorems (see Lemma
3.1
),
showing that the limit is equal to a constant in
[0,\infty]
almost surely. Then, we prove
that the constant is in fact positive and finite by establishing a finite upper bound and
positive lower bound for the limit, and therefore, the corresponding modulus function
in the limit theorem is sharp. We give an application of the main results to the solutions
of a class of linear SPDEs

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)=\mathscr{L}u(t,x)+\dot{W}(t,x)

driven by a fractional-colored Gaussian noise, including the stochastic heat equation

[
3
,
7
]
. It is also a notable result of this paper that
u(t,x)
satisfies the
strong LND property (see Lemma
7.3
), which strengthens a result of

[
7
]
.

In general, there are different ways to describe the sample path variation of random
fields. The Chung-type LIL characterizes the lower envelope (
\liminf
) for the local
oscillations of the sample functions at a fixed point. The local modulus of continuity at
a fixed point is, for many Gaussian random fields, given by the ordinary Khinchin-type
LIL, which complements the Chung-type LIL by characterizing the upper envelope
(
\limsup
) for the local oscillations at a fixed point. On the other hand, the uniform
modulus of continuity specifies the maximum oscillation of the sample functions over
certain sets such as a compact interval.

The Chung-type LIL for a class of isotropic and anisotropic Gaussian random fields
with stationary increments has been studied by Li and Shao
[
14
]
(see also

[
18
,
25
]
) and Luan and Xiao
[
15
]
. The exact local and uniform moduli
of continuity for a class of anisotropic Gaussian random fields have been studied by
Meerschaert et al.
[
19
]
. The novelty of the present paper is a general
framework based on a harmonizable-type representation and the strong LND
property of a Gaussian random field that may not have stationary increments, which
is employed to extend and improve some of the results of
[
14
,
15
,
19
]
, and can be directly applied to the solutions of SPDEs.
In particular, with a harmonizable-type
representation, we are able to decompose the random field and create independence,
making it possible to establish general zero–one laws (Lemma
3.1
) which can
be strengthened to prove the Chung-type LIL as well as the exact local and uniform
moduli of continuity. The independence structure from the harmonizable-type representation
also allows the use of the second Borel–Cantelli lemma, which facilitates a simple proof
of one of the bounds for the Chung-type LIL and the exact local modulus of continuity.

Let us summarize the major differences and improvements in our results compared
to the existing results in the literature. The Chung-type LIL results in
[
14
]
,

[
18
]
,
[
25
]
and
[
15

and, in particular, it is enough for them to consider the Chung-type LIL at the origin.
Our Theorem

that may not necessarily have stationary increments, and we prove a Chung-type LIL

Moreover, our Theorem

LIL is given in terms of the small ball constant provided it exists, see (
4.8
)
below. We remark that the connection between the bounds on the limiting constant
in Chung’s LIL and the small ball estimates is also given in Theorem 7.1 of

For exact local and uniform moduli of continuity of Gaussian processes with stationary

Also, Meerschaert et al.

We prove exact local and uniform moduli of continuity under two metrics

2.
Assumptions

Consider a real-valued continuous centered Gaussian random field
v=\{v(x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\}
.
Let

. We introduce some assumptions for

. Notice
that Assumption

is from
[

]
and Assumption

[

Assumption 2.1
.

There exists a centered Gaussian random field
\{v(A,x),A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),x\in T\}
, where
\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is the
Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty)
, such that the following
properties hold:

(a) For every
x\in T

is an independently scattered Gaussian
noise such that

and
the processes

are independent whenever

and
B

are disjoint.

(b) There exist constants
c_{0}>0
,

, and
\gamma_{j}>0
,

,
such that for all
a_{0}\leq a<b\leq\infty

(2.1)

\displaystyle\big\|v([a,b),x)-v(x)-v([a,b),y)+v(y)\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{0}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{k}a^{\gamma_{j}}|x_{j}-y_{j}|+b^{-1}\bigg)

(2.2)

\displaystyle\big\|v([0,a_{0}),x)-v([0,a_{0}),y)\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{k}|x_{j}-y_{j}|.

In the above,
\|X\|_{L^{2}}:=[{\mathbb{E}}(X^{2})]^{1/2}
for a random variable
X
.

Define
\alpha_{j}

(j=1,\ldots,k
) by the relation
\gamma_{j}=\alpha_{j}^{-1}-1

\alpha_{j}=(\gamma_{j}+1)^{-1}
. Note that

The parameters

characterize the Hölder regularity of

(see Lemma
2.4

Let

and define the metric
\Delta
by

(2.3)

\Delta(x,y):=\sum_{j=1}^{k}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}},\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}.

We will also use the canonical metric

associated with

.
It is defined by

(2.4)

d(x,y)=d_{v}(x,y):=\big\|v(x)-v(y)\big\|_{L^{2}},\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}.

Assumption 2.2
.

There exists a constant
c_{2}>0

such that for all integers
n\geq 1
,
for all
x,x^{1},\dots,x^{n}\in T
,

\mathrm{Var}\big(v(x)|v(x^{1}),\dots,v(x^{n})\big)\geq c_{2}\min_{0\leq i\leq n}\Delta^{2}(x,x^{i}),

where
x^{0}=0
.

Assumption 2.3
.

There exists a constant
c_{3}>0
such that for all
x,y\in T
,

\big\|v(x)-v(y)\big\|_{L^{2}}\geq c_{3}\Delta(x,y).

The following are some remarks about these assumptions. Assumption
2.1
above
is the same as Assumption 2.1 in
[
6
]
and
[
5
]
, and is satisfied by
many Gaussian random fields that have a spectral or harmonizable-type representation,
or more generally, a stochastic integral representation. For example, it is shown in

[

]
that the solutions of linear stochastic heat and wave equations admit
harmonizable-type representations and satisfy Assumption
2.1
.
The same is true for fractional Brownian sheets
[
5
]
.
Assumption

implies an upper bound for the increments of

in
L^{2}
-norm in
terms of the metric
\Delta

Lemma 2.4
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, there exist constants
\varepsilon_{1}>0
and
c_{1}

such that for all
x,y\in T
with
\Delta(x,y)\leq\varepsilon_{1}
,

(2.5)

\|v(x)-v(y)\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{1}\Delta(x,y).

This is a consequence of Proposition 2.2 of

\varepsilon_{1}=\min\{a_{0}^{-1},1\}

.

Assumption
2.2
is known as the property of strong local nondeterminism (strong LND)
with respect to the metric
\Delta
, which has found various applications in studying probabilistic,
analytic and fractal properties of Gaussian random fields (cf.
[
26
,
27
]
).
For Gaussian random fields with stationary
increments,

provides sufficient conditions in terms of their spectral measures for

Assumption

implies the lower bound in Assumption
2.3
if

is compact: for all
x,y\in T\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\backslash\{0\}
,

(2.6)

\|v(x)-v(y)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\sqrt{c_{2}}\Delta(x,y).

The strong LND property (Assumption

) will be used to prove optimal bounds for
the small ball probability, which is the main ingredient of the proof of Chung’s LIL (Theorem

). This property will also be needed in the proof of the lower bound

). Assumption

is much weaker than Assumption

. To establish the exact local modulus of continuity,
or the ordinary LIL (Theorem

,
but not Assumption

.

In Lemma

of this paper, we will prove that the solutions of a class of

,
12
]
. For this reason, the Chung-type LIL for the stochastic

]

[

,
12
]

For the local modulus of continuity, our Theorem

3.
Zero–one laws

In Lemma
3.1
below, we establish zero–one laws
for the Chung-type LIL and the local and uniform moduli of continuity,
showing that the limit in each of these laws is equal to a constant almost surely.
At this stage, we do not rule out the possibility that the constant could be zero
or infinity. Later in our main theorems, we will strengthen these zero–one laws
and prove that the limiting constants are indeed positive and finite.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following statements hold under Assumption
2.1
.

(i)

For any fixed
x_{0}\in T
,
there exists a constant
0\leq\kappa_{1}\leq\infty
which may depend on
x_{0}
such that

(3.1)

\liminf_{r\to 0+}\sup_{x\in T:\,\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq r}\frac{|v(x)-v(x_{0})|}{r(\log\log(1/r))^{-1/Q}}=\kappa_{1}\quad\text{a.s.}

(ii)

For any fixed
x_{0}\in T
, there exists a constant
0\leq\kappa_{2}\leq\infty

which may depend on
x_{0}
such that

(3.2)

\lim_{r\to 0+}\sup_{x\in T:\,0<\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq r}\frac{|v(x)-v(x_{0})|}{\Delta(x,x_{0})\sqrt{\log\log(\Delta(x,x_{0})^{-1})}}=\kappa_{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

(iii)

There exists a constant
0\leq\kappa_{3}\leq\infty
such that

(3.3)

\lim_{r\to 0+}\sup_{x,y\in T:\,0<\Delta(x,y)\leq r}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{\Delta(x,y)\sqrt{\log(\Delta(x,y)^{-1})}}=\kappa_{3}\quad\text{a.s.}

Moreover, under Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
,
(
3.2
) and (
3.3
)
also hold when
\Delta
is replaced by the canonical metric
d
,
with possibly different constants.

Proof.

By Assumption
2.1
,
v(x)
can be represented as the infinite sum

(3.4)

v(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}v_{n}(x),

where
v_{n}(x)=v([n,n+1),x)
and
v_{n}=\{v_{n}(x),x\in T\}
(
n=0,1,\dots
)
is a sequence of independent Gaussian random fields.
Let
\mathscr{F}_{n}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the processes

\{v_{m},m\geq n\}
and the null events, and let

\mathscr{F}_{\infty}=\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathscr{F}_{n}
be the
\sigma
-algebra
of all tail events.
By Kolmogorov’s zero–one law,
{\mathbb{P}}(A)=0
or
1
for
A\in\mathscr{F}_{\infty}
.

To prove (i), we will show that for any fixed
x_{0}\in T
, the random variable

X:=\liminf_{r\to 0+}\sup_{x\in T:\,\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq r}\frac{|v(x)-v(x_{0})|}{r(\log\log(1/r))^{-1/Q}}

is measurable with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{F}_{\infty}
.
For any

and

, let

Y_{n}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}v_{m}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad Z_{n}(x)=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}v_{m}(x).

Note that
v(x)=Y_{n}(x)+Z_{n}(x)
and
Y_{n}(x)=v([0,n),x)
.
Consider

, where
a_{0}
is the constant in Assumption
2.1
.
Then by (
2.1
) with
a=n
and
b=\infty
,
for all
x,y\in T
, we have

\displaystyle\|Y_{n}(x)-Y_{n}(y)\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{k}n^{\gamma_{j}}|x_{j}-y_{j}|.

Since
Y_{n}
is Gaussian, this implies that for any

, there is a finite constant
C

which depends on

such that for all
x,y\in T
,

{\mathbb{E}}(|Y_{n}(x)-Y_{n}(y)|^{p})\leq C|x-y|^{p}.

Then, by Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem, for any

,
with probability one,

is
\beta

T
.
If we choose

\max\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\}<\beta<1

then for a.e.

(3.5)

|Y_{n}(x)-Y_{n}(y)|\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{k}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{\beta}

For (ii) and (iii), notice that the limits on the left-hand side of

Moreover, similarly to the above arguments,
for any

, by (

), we have

(3.6)

\lim_{r\to 0+}\sup_{x\in T:\,0<\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq r}\frac{|Y_{n}(x)-Y_{n}(x_{0})|}{\Delta(x,x_{0})\sqrt{\log\log(\Delta(x,x_{0})^{-1})}}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

(3.7)

\lim_{r\to 0+}\sup_{x,y\in T:\,0<\Delta(x,y)\leq r}\frac{|Y_{n}(x)-Y_{n}(y)|}{\Delta(x,y)\sqrt{\log(\Delta(x,y)^{-1})}}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

Finally, to see that (

) and (

) also hold when

is replaced by

) and Assumption

for a.e.

, there exists

such that

, we have

where

Therefore, (

4.
Chung-type law of the iterated logarithm

This section is devoted to proving the Chung-type LIL.
It is well known that the small ball probability is a key step in establishing
Chung’s LIL (
[
18
,
14
]
).
The following lemma is a reformulation of Talagrand’s
lower bound for small ball probabilities of Gaussian processes

[
23
, Lemma 2.2]
. See Ledoux
[
9
, p.257]
for a proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\{X(t),t\in S\}
be a separable, real-valued, mean-zero Gaussian process
indexed by a bounded set
S
with canonical metric

d_{X}(s,t)=\|X(s)-X(t)\|_{L^{2}}
.
Let
N(S,d_{X},\varepsilon)
denote the smallest number of
d_{X}
-balls of
radius
\varepsilon
needed to cover the set
S
.
Suppose there is a decreasing function
\psi:(0,\delta)\to(0,\infty)

such that

N(S,d_{X},\varepsilon)\leq\psi(\varepsilon)
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
and there are constants

a_{2}\geq a_{1}>1
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)
,

(4.1)

a_{1}\psi(\varepsilon)\leq\psi(\varepsilon/2)\leq a_{2}\psi(\varepsilon).

Then, there is a finite constant
K
depending only on
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
such that
for all
u\in(0,\delta)
,

(4.2)

{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\sup_{s,t\in S}|X(s)-X(t)|\leq u\bigg\}\geq\exp\left(-K\psi(u)\right).

Recall the metric
\Delta
defined in (
2.3
). Let
B_{\Delta}(x,r)=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}:\Delta(x,y)\leq r\}
be
the closed
\Delta
-ball centered at
x
of radius
r
.
In the proposition below, we prove optimal bounds for the small ball probability
of
v
around a fixed point
x_{0}
, which generalizes Theorem 5.1 in
[
27
]

(or Lemma 2.2 of
[
15
]
), where the case of
x_{0}=0
and

was considered.

Proposition 4.2
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
, there exist positive finite constants
C_{1},C_{2}

and
r_{0}>0
small such that for any
0<u<r\leq r_{0}
and
x_{0}\in T

with
B_{\Delta}(x_{0},r)\subset T
, we have

(4.3)

\exp\left(-C_{1}(r/u)^{Q}\right)\leq{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\sup_{x\in B_{\Delta}(x_{0},r)}|v(x)-v(x_{0})|\leq u\bigg\}\leq\exp\left(-C_{2}(r/u)^{Q}\right).

Proof.

We first prove the lower bound in (
4.3
).
Consider the Gaussian random field
\{v(x),x\in T\}
and the canonical metric

d_{v}(x,y)=\|v(x)-v(y)\|_{L^{2}}
.
By Assumption
2.1
and Lemma
2.4
,
we can find some small
r_{0}>0
such that
d_{v}(x,y)\leq c_{1}\Delta(x,y)

for all
x,y\in T
with
\Delta(x,y)\leq r
and
0<r\leq r_{0}
.
This implies
N(B_{\Delta}(x_{0},r),d_{v},\varepsilon)\leq C_{0}(r/\varepsilon)^{Q}
for all
\varepsilon>0
small,
where
C_{0}
does not depend on
r
or
\varepsilon
.
Take
S=B_{\Delta}(x_{0},r)
and
\psi(\varepsilon)=C_{0}(r/\varepsilon)^{Q}
.
Then
\psi
satisfies (
4.1
) with
a_{1}=a_{2}=2^{Q}>1
.
Hence, the lower bound in (
4.3
) follows from Lemma
4.1
.

The proof of the upper bound in (
4.3
) is based on Assumption
2.2
and a
conditioning argument. Suppose
0<u<r\leq r_{0}
and
B_{\Delta}(x_{0},r)\subset T
.
Notice that, for
x_{0}=(x_{0,1},\dots,x_{0,k})
, the rectangle
I:=\prod_{j=1}^{k}[x_{0,j},\,x_{0,j}+(k^{-1}r)^{1/\alpha_{j}}]
is contained in
B_{\Delta}(x_{0},r)
.
For simplicity, we consider the case where
x_{0}
lies in the orthant

[0,\infty)^{k}
, so that the interior of
I
does not contain the origin (otherwise,
in order to retain this latter property for
I
, we can modify the definition of
I
by
using the interval
[x_{0,j}-(k^{-1}r)^{1/\alpha_{j}},\,x_{0,j}]
for
x_{0,j}<0

and the rest of the proof is similar).
It suffices to prove that

(4.4)

{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\sup_{x\in I}|v(x)-v(x_{0})|\leq u\bigg\}\leq\exp\left(-C_{2}(r/u)^{Q}\right).

Since
r/u>1
, we can find an integer
n\geq 2

such that
n-1<r/u\leq n
(in particular,
n/2<r/u
).
Divide
I
into sub-rectangles of side lengths
\big(r/(kn)\big)^{1/\alpha_{j}}
(
j=1,\dots,k
).
The number of sub-rectangles is
N\sim n^{Q}
.
Let
x_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq N
) denote the upper-right vertices of the sub-rectangles in any order.
For each
1\leq j\leq N
, let

A_{j}=\bigg\{\max_{1\leq i\leq j}|v(x_{i})-v(x_{0})|\leq u\bigg\}.

Then by conditioning,

(4.5)

{\mathbb{P}}(A_{j})={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[{\bf 1}_{A_{j-1}}\,{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{|v(x_{j})-v(x_{0})|\leq u\,\Big|\,v(x_{i}):0\leq i\leq j-1\Big\}\bigg].

By Assumption
2.2
, the property that the
x_{i}
’s are separated by a

\Delta
-distance of at least
r/(kn)
and
that the interior of
I
does not contain the origin, we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{Var}\big(v(x_{j})\big|v(x_{i}):0\leq i\leq j-1\big)&\geq c_{2}\min_{0\leq i\leq j-1}\Delta^{2}(x_{j},x_{i})\geq c_{2}(r/(kn))^{2}.\end{split}

Since the random field
v
is Gaussian, the conditional distribution of
v(x_{j})
given
all the
v(x_{i})
, with
0\leq i\leq j-1
, is a Gaussian distribution with conditional variance

\mathrm{Var}(v(x_{j})|v(x_{i}):0\leq i\leq j-1)
.
Then, (
4.6
) and Anderson’s inequality
[
2
, Theorem 2]
imply that

(4.7)

\begin{split}{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{|v(x_{j})-v(x_{0})|\leq u\,\Big|\,v(x_{i}):0\leq i\leq j-1\Big\}&\leq{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{|Z|\leq\frac{u}{\sqrt{c_{2}}(r/(kn))}\Big\}\leq\exp(-C),\end{split}

where
Z
is a standard Gaussian random variable
and the last inequality holds for some constant
C>0

since
k\leq knu/r\leq 2k
.
Then, based on (
4.5
) and (
4.7
),
we can use induction to deduce that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|v(x_{i})-v(x_{0})|\leq u\bigg\}={\mathbb{P}}(A_{N})\leq\exp(-CN).

Since
N\sim n^{Q}
and
n-1<r/u\leq n
,
this implies (
4.4
) and completes the proof.
∎

The next lemma is an isoperimetric inequality for general Gaussian processes.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
10
, p.302]

There is a universal constant
K_{0}
such that the following statement holds.
Let
S
be a bounded set and
\{X(s),s\in S\}
be a separable Gaussian process.
Let
D=\sup\{d_{X}(s,t):s,t\in S\}
be the diameter of
S
in metric
d_{X}
.
Then for any
u>0
,

{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\sup_{s,t\in S}|X(s)-X(t)|\geq K_{0}\Big(u+\int_{0}^{D}\sqrt{\log N(S,d_{X},\varepsilon)}d\varepsilon\Big)\bigg\}\leq\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}}{D^{2}}\right).

Now, we are ready to prove the Chung-type LIL.

Theorem 4.4
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
, for any fixed
x_{0}\in T
, there exists a
positive finite constant
\kappa
which may depend on
x_{0}
such that

\liminf_{r\to 0+}\sup_{x\in T:\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq r}\frac{|v(x)-v(x_{0})|}{r(\log\log(1/r))^{-1/Q}}=\kappa^{1/Q}\quad\text{a.s.}

and
C_{2}\leq\kappa\leq C_{1}
, where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are the constants in
Proposition
4.2
. In particular,
\kappa
coincides with
the following limit, which is called the small ball constant of
v
on
\{x\in T:\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq r\},

if it exists:

(4.8)

\kappa=-\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\to 0,u/r\to 0\end{subarray}}{\left(\frac{u}{r}\right)}^{Q}\log{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\sup_{x\in T:\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq r}|v(x)-v(x_{0})|\leq u\bigg\}.

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

Fix
x_{0}\in T
. To simplify notations, define
h(r):=r(\log\log(1/r))^{-1/Q}
and

L(r):=\sup_{x\in T:\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq r}\frac{|v(x)-v(x_{0})|}{h(r)}.

By Lemma
3.1
,
\liminf_{r\to 0+}L(r)=\kappa_{1}
a.s.
for some constant
0\leq\kappa_{1}\leq\infty
.
To prove the theorem, we will show that

(4.9)

\liminf_{r\to 0+}L(r)\geq C_{2}^{1/Q}\quad\text{a.s.}

and

(4.10)

\liminf_{r\to 0+}L(r)\leq C_{1}^{1/Q}\quad\text{a.s.}

We first prove the lower bound (
4.9
). Let
a>1
be a constant.
For each
n\geq 1
, let
r_{n}=a^{-n}
.
Consider a constant
K
such that
0<K<a^{-1}C_{2}^{1/Q}
and
consider the event

A_{n}=\bigg\{\sup_{x\in T:\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq r_{n}}|v(x)-v(x_{0})|\leq Kh(r_{n-1})\bigg\}.

By the small ball probability estimates in Proposition
4.2
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-C_{2}(aK)^{-Q}\log\log(1/r_{n-1})\right)

\displaystyle=((n-1)\log a)^{-C_{2}(aK)^{-Q}}.

Then
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})<\infty
since
C_{2}(aK)^{-Q}>1
.
By using the Borel–Cantelli lemma and letting

K\uparrow a^{-1}C_{2}^{1/Q}
along a rational sequence, we get that

(4.11)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in T:\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq r_{n}}\frac{|v(x)-v(x_{0})|}{h(r_{n-1})}\geq a^{-1}C_{2}^{1/Q}\quad\text{a.s.}

Note that
h
is increasing for
r>0
small.
For any
r>0
small, we can find
n
large enough such that
r_{n}\leq r\leq r_{n-1}

and
h(r)\leq h(r_{n-1})
. Then, by (
4.11
), we have

\liminf_{r\to 0+}L(r)\geq a^{-1}C_{2}^{1/Q}\quad\text{a.s.},

which implies (
4.9
) since
a>1
is arbitrary.

Now, we turn to the proof of the upper bound (
4.10
).
It relies on Assumption
2.1
which allows us to create independence.
Fix
\delta>0
small.
For any
n\geq 1
, let
\rho_{n}=\exp(-(n^{\delta}+n^{1+\delta}))
and

b_{n}=\exp(n^{1+\delta})
.
For any
x\in T
, let
v_{n}(x)=v([b_{n},b_{n+1}),x)
and

\widetilde{v}_{n}(x)=v({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\setminus[b_{n},b_{n+1}),x)

so that
v(x)=v_{n}(x)+\widetilde{v}_{n}(x)
.
By Assumption
2.1
(a), the processes
v_{1},v_{2},\dots
are
independent, and for each
n\geq 1
,
v_{n}
and
\widetilde{v}_{n}
are also independent.

Let
K:=((1+\delta)C_{1})^{1/Q}
.
Since
v(x)=v_{n}(x)+\widetilde{v}_{n}(x)
and
v_{n}
and
\widetilde{v}_{n}
are
independent, we can apply Anderson’s inequality
[
2
, Theorem 2]
to get that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\sup_{x\in T:\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq\rho_{n}}|v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(x_{0})|\leq Kh(\rho_{n})\bigg\}\geq{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\sup_{x\in T:\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq\rho_{n}}|v(x)-v(x_{0})|\leq Kh(\rho_{n})\bigg\}.

Then, by Proposition
4.2
, the right-hand side is at least

\displaystyle\exp\left(-C_{1}K^{-Q}\log\log(1/\rho_{n})\right)

\displaystyle=(n^{\delta}+n^{1+\delta})^{-C_{1}K^{-Q}}

\displaystyle\geq(2n^{1+\delta})^{-C_{1}K^{-Q}}.

Since
(1+\delta)C_{1}K^{-Q}=1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\sup_{x\in T:\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq\rho_{n}}|v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(x_{0})|\leq Kh(\rho_{n})\bigg\}=\infty.

Since
v_{1},v_{2},\dots
are independent, the second Borel–Cantelli lemma implies

(4.12)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in T:\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq\rho_{n}}\frac{|v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(x_{0})|}{h(\rho_{n})}\leq((1+\delta)C_{1})^{1/Q}\quad\text{a.s.}

To complete the proof of (
4.10
), we claim that

(4.13)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in T:\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq\rho_{n}}\frac{|\widetilde{v}_{n}(x)-\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{0})|}{h(\rho_{n})}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

We prove this by using Lemma
4.3
. Consider the process
\widetilde{v}_{n}

on the set
S_{n}:=B_{\Delta}(x_{0},\rho_{n})
.
By (
2.1
) of Assumption
2.1
, for all
x,y\in S_{n}
,

(4.14)

\|\widetilde{v}_{n}(x)-\widetilde{v}_{n}(y)\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{0}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{n}^{\gamma_{j}}|x_{j}-y_{j}|+b_{n+1}^{-1}\bigg).

Recall that
\gamma_{j}=\alpha_{j}^{-1}-1
. Let
D_{n}
be the diameter of
S_{n}
in the metric

d_{\widetilde{v}_{n}}
. Then

(4.15)

D_{n}\leq C\rho_{n}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{k}(b_{n}\rho_{n})^{\alpha_{j}^{-1}-1}+(b_{n+1}\rho_{n})^{-1}\bigg).

Note that
b_{n}\rho_{n}=\exp(-n^{\delta})
.
Also, by the mean value theorem,

(n+1)^{1+\delta}-n^{1+\delta}\geq(1+\delta)n^{\delta}
, which implies

b_{n+1}\rho_{n}\geq\exp(\delta n^{\delta})
.
Provided
\delta\leq\min\{\alpha_{1}^{-1}-1,\dots,\alpha_{k}^{-1}-1\}
,
we have

(4.16)

D_{n}\leq C\rho_{n}\exp(-\delta n^{\delta}).

Also, by the independence of
v_{n}
and
\widetilde{v}_{n}
and Lemma
2.4
,
for
n
large, for all
x,y\in S_{n}
,

\|\widetilde{v}_{n}(x)-\widetilde{v}_{n}(y)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|v(x)-v(y)\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{1}\Delta(x,y).

This implies
N(S_{n},d_{\widetilde{v}_{n}},\varepsilon)\leq C(\rho_{n}/\varepsilon)^{Q}
for
\varepsilon>0
small.
Then for
n
large,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{D_{n}}\sqrt{\log N(S_{n},d_{\widetilde{v}_{n}},\varepsilon)}\,d\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{C\rho_{n}\exp(-\delta n^{\delta})}\sqrt{\log(\rho_{n}/\varepsilon)}\,d\varepsilon

\displaystyle=C\rho_{n}\int_{0}^{C\exp(-\delta n^{\delta})}\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\,d\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq C\rho_{n}\exp(-\delta n^{\delta})\sqrt{\delta n^{\delta}}.

The last inequality can be verified using the change of variable
\varepsilon=e^{-u^{2}}

and the elementary inequality
\int_{x}^{\infty}u^{2}e^{-u^{2}}\,du\leq Cxe^{-x^{2}}

for
x
large. Let
\zeta>0
.
Then for
n
large, we have

2K_{0}\zeta h(\rho_{n})\geq K_{0}\Big(\zeta h(\rho_{n})+\int_{0}^{D_{n}}\sqrt{\log N(S_{n},d_{\widetilde{v}_{n}},\varepsilon)}\,d\varepsilon\Big),

where
K_{0}
is the universal constant in Lemma
4.3
.
Then, by that lemma and (
4.16
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\sup_{x\in T:\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq\rho_{n}}|\widetilde{v}_{n}(x)-\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{0})|\geq 2K_{0}\zeta h(\rho_{n})\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{\zeta^{2}h(\rho_{n})^{2}}{D_{n}^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{\zeta^{2}\exp(2\delta n^{\delta})}{C^{2}(\log(n^{\delta}+n^{1+\delta}))^{2/Q}}\bigg).

Hence

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\sup_{x\in T:\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq\rho_{n}}|\widetilde{v}_{n}(x)-\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{0})|\geq 2K_{0}\zeta h(\rho_{n})\bigg\}<\infty.

By the Borel–Cantelli lemma,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in T:\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq\rho_{n}}\frac{|\widetilde{v}_{n}(x)-\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{0})|}{h(\rho_{n})}\leq 2K_{0}\zeta\quad\text{a.s.}

Since
\zeta>0
is arbitrary, we get (
4.13
).

Finally, recall that
v(x)=v_{n}(x)+\widetilde{v}_{n}(x)
.
Combining (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) yields

\liminf_{r\to 0+}L(r)\leq((1+\delta)C_{1})^{1/Q}\quad\text{a.s.}

Since
\delta>0
is arbitrary, we get (
4.10
).
The proof of Theorem
4.4
is complete.
∎

5.
The exact local modulus of continuity

In this section, we are going to prove the exact local modulus of continuity,
which takes the form of the ordinary LIL.
First, let us recall the following result of Talagrand
[
22
, Theorem 2.4]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\{X(t),t\in S\}
be a mean-zero continuous Gaussian process.
Let

\sigma^{2}:=\sup_{t\in S}\|X(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Consider the canonical metric
d_{X}
on
S
defined by

d_{X}(s,t)=\|X(s)-X(t)\|_{L^{2}}
.
Assume that for some constant
M>\sigma
, some
p>0
and
some
0<\varepsilon_{0}\leq\sigma
, we have

N(S,d_{X},\varepsilon)\leq(M/\varepsilon)^{p}\quad\text{for all }\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}.

Then for
u>\sigma^{2}[(1+\sqrt{p})/\varepsilon_{0}]
, we have

{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\sup_{t\in S}X(t)\geq u\bigg\}\leq\left(\frac{KMu}{\sqrt{p}\,\sigma^{2}}\right)^{p}\Phi\left(\frac{u}{\sigma}\right),

where
\Phi(x)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-y^{2}/2}\,dy

and
K
is a universal constant.

The following Gaussian estimate is standard:

(5.1)

\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}x}e^{-x^{2}/2}\leq\Phi(x)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}\quad\text{for all }x\geq 1.

Recall that
d(x,y)=\|v(x)-v(y)\|_{L^{2}}
is the canonical metric of
v
.
The following theorem gives the exact local modulus of continuity
under the metrics
d
and
\Delta
, respectively.
Note that the strong LND property (Assumption
2.2
) is not required
for this result.

Theorem 5.2
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
, for any fixed
x_{0}\in T
, we have

(5.2)

\lim_{r\to 0+}\sup_{x\in T:\,0<d(x,x_{0})\leq r}\frac{|v(x)-v(x_{0})|}{d(x,x_{0})\sqrt{\log\log(d(x,x_{0})^{-1})}}=\sqrt{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

and

(5.3)

\lim_{r\to 0+}\sup_{x\in T:\,0<\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq r}\frac{|v(x)-v(x_{0})|}{\Delta(x,x_{0})\sqrt{\log\log(\Delta(x,x_{0})^{-1})}}=\kappa\quad\text{a.s.}

for some positive finite constant
\kappa
satisfying

\sqrt{2}\,c_{3}\leq\kappa\leq\sqrt{2}\,c_{1},

where
c_{1}
is the constant in (
2.5
) and

c_{3}
is the constant in Assumption
2.3
.

Remark 5.3
.

Meerschaert et al.
[
19
]
have considered Gaussian random fields
that have stationary increments and satisfy
d(x,y)\asymp\Delta(x,y)
,
but only proved that
the limit in (
5.2
) is equal to some finite constant
\kappa_{1}\geq\sqrt{2}
.
Our theorem does not require stationarity of increments and we obtain
the exact constant
\kappa_{1}=\sqrt{2}
.
Meerschaert et al.
[
19
]
also proved another form of LIL:

\limsup_{|\varepsilon|\to 0+}\sup_{s:\,|s_{j}|\leq|\varepsilon_{j}|}\frac{|v(x_{0}+s)-v(x_{0})|}{d(s,0)\sqrt{\log\log(1+\prod_{j=1}^{k}|s_{j}|^{-\alpha_{j}})}}=\kappa_{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Fix
x_{0}\in T
. For
r>0
, define

L(r):=\sup_{x\in T:\,0<d(x,x_{0})\leq r}\frac{|v(x)-v(x_{0})|}{d(x,x_{0})\sqrt{\log\log(d(x,x_{0})^{-1})}}.

By Lemma
3.1
,
\lim_{r\to 0+}L(r)=\kappa
a.s.
for some constant
0\leq\kappa\leq\infty
.
To prove (
5.2
), we claim that

(5.4)

\limsup_{r\to 0+}L(r)\leq\sqrt{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

and

(5.5)

\limsup_{r\to 0+}L(r)\geq\sqrt{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

We first prove the upper bound (
5.4
).
Let
a>1
and
\zeta>0
be constants.
For each
n\geq 1
, let

r_{n}=a^{-n}\quad\text{and}\quad u_{n}=(1+\zeta)r_{n}\sqrt{2\log\log(1/r_{n})}.

Consider the event

A_{n}=\bigg\{\sup_{x\in T:d(x,x_{0})\leq r_{n}}|v(x)-v(x_{0})|>u_{n}\bigg\}.

We are going to use Lemma
5.1
to derive an upper bound for
{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})
.
Fix a large
n
.
Consider
S:=\{x\in T:d(x,x_{0})\leq r_{n}\}
and
X(x):=v(x)-v(x_{0})
for
x\in S
.
Then,

\sigma^{2}:=\sup_{x\in S}\|X(x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=r_{n}^{2}

and by Lemma
2.4
, for all
x,y\in S
,

d_{X}(x,y)\leq c_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}.

Then
N(S,d_{X},\varepsilon)\leq C_{0}(r_{n}/\varepsilon)^{Q}
for
0<\varepsilon<\sigma
,
where
C_{0}
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon
or
n
,
and can be chosen such that
M:=C_{0}^{1/Q}r_{n}>\sigma
.
For
n
large enough,
u_{n}>r_{n}(1+\sqrt{Q})
.
Take
\varepsilon_{0}=\sigma
and
p=Q
.
Then by Lemma
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigg(\frac{KC_{0}^{1/Q}r_{n}u_{n}}{\sqrt{Q}\,r_{n}^{2}}\bigg)^{Q}\Phi(u_{n}/r_{n}).

Using the estimate (
5.1
),
we get that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})

\displaystyle\leq C{(\log n)}^{Q/2}\,n^{-(1+\zeta)^{2}}.

Hence,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})<\infty
.
By the Borel–Cantelli lemma,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in T:\,0<d(x,x_{0})\leq r_{n}}\frac{|v(x)-v(x_{0})|}{r_{n}\sqrt{\log\log(1/r_{n})}}\leq(1+\zeta)\sqrt{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

and thus

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in T:\,r_{n+1}\leq d(x,x_{0})\leq r_{n}}\frac{|v(x)-v(x_{0})|}{r_{n+1}\sqrt{\log\log(1/r_{n})}}\leq a(1+\zeta)\sqrt{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

This implies that

\limsup_{r\to 0+}L(r)\leq a(1+\zeta)\sqrt{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

Letting
a\downarrow 1
and
\zeta\downarrow 0
along rational sequences,
we get (
5.4
).

We turn to the proof of the lower bound (
5.5
).
Fix
0<\varepsilon<1
.
Let
0<\delta<1
be a small fixed number (depending on
\varepsilon
) to be determined.
Write
x_{0}=(x_{0,1},\dots,x_{0,k})
.
For each
n\geq 1
, let

x_{n}=\big(x_{0,1}+\rho_{n}^{\alpha_{1}^{-1}},\dots,x_{0,k}+\rho_{n}^{\alpha_{k}^{-1}}\big),

where
\rho_{n}=\exp(-(n^{\delta}+n^{1+\delta}))
.
With Assumption
2.1
, we can write
v(x)=v_{n}(x)+\widetilde{v}_{n}(x)
, where

v_{n}(x)=v([b_{n},b_{n+1}),x),\quad\widetilde{v}_{n}(x)=v({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\setminus[b_{n},b_{n+1}),x),

and
b_{n}=\exp(n^{1+\delta})
.
We aim to prove that

(5.6)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|v_{n}(x_{n})-v_{n}(x_{0})|}{d(x_{n},x_{0})\sqrt{\log\log(d(x_{n},x_{0})^{-1})}}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

and

(5.7)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{n})-\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{0})|}{d(x_{n},x_{0})\sqrt{\log\log(d(x_{n},x_{0})^{-1})}}\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{a.s.}

To prove (
5.6
), we consider for each
n\geq 1
the event

B_{n}=\left\{|v_{n}(x_{n})-v_{n}(x_{0})|\geq(1-\varepsilon)d(x_{n},x_{0})\sqrt{2\log\log(d(x_{n},x_{0})^{-1})}\right\}.

Similarly to (
4.14
)–(
4.16
) in the proof of
Theorem
4.4
, we can deduce from Assumption
2.1
that,
provided
\delta\leq\min\{\alpha_{1}^{-1}-1,\dots,\alpha_{k}^{-1}-1\}
,

(5.8)

\begin{split}\|\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{n})-\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{0})\|_{L^{2}}&\leq c_{0}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{n}^{\gamma_{j}}|x_{n,j}-x_{0,j}|+b_{n+1}^{-1}\Big)\\
&\leq K_{1}\rho_{n}\exp(-\delta n^{\delta}).\end{split}

Note that
\Delta(x_{n},x_{0})=k\rho_{n}
.
By Assumption
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
,

(5.9)

c_{3}\Delta(x,x_{0})\leq d(x,x_{0})\leq c_{1}\Delta(x,x_{0})

for all
x
in a neighbourhood of
x_{0}
.
Then, for
n
large,

(5.10)

c_{3}k\rho_{n}\leq d(x_{n},x_{0})\leq c_{1}k\rho_{n}.

Therefore, by the triangle inequality, (
5.8
) and (
5.10
), we have

(5.11)

\begin{split}\|v_{n}(x_{n})-v_{n}(x_{0})\|_{L^{2}}&\geq\|v(x_{n})-v(x_{0})\|_{L^{2}}-\|\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{n})-\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\geq(1-K_{2}\exp(-\delta n^{\delta}))d(x_{n},x_{0}).\end{split}

Now, (
5.11
) and (
5.10
) imply that for
n
large,

\displaystyle B_{n}\supset\left\{|v_{n}(x_{n})-v_{n}(x_{0})|\geq(1-\varepsilon/2)\|v_{n}(x_{n})-v_{n}(x_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\sqrt{2\log\log(C/\rho_{n})}\right\},

where
C
is a suitable constant.
Then, by the standard Gaussian estimate (
5.1
),
we get that, for
n
large,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(B_{n})

\displaystyle\geq K(\log n)^{-1/2}n^{-(1-\varepsilon/2)^{2}(1+\delta)}.

By choosing
\delta
small enough such that
(1-\varepsilon/2)^{2}(1+\delta)\leq 1
,
we have
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(B_{n})=\infty
.
Hence, by the independence among
v_{1},v_{2},\dots
and
the second Borel–Cantelli lemma, we get (
5.6
).

For (
5.7
), we use (
5.8
) and (
5.10
)
above to get that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left\{|\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{n})-\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{0})|\geq\varepsilon d(x_{n},x_{0})\sqrt{\log\log(d(x_{n},x_{0})^{-1})}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left\{|\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{n})-\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{0})|\geq K\varepsilon\|\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{n})-\widetilde{v}_{n}(x_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\exp(\delta n^{\delta})\sqrt{\log\log(C/\rho_{n})}\right\}.

This probability, by standard Gaussian estimate, is bounded above by

C^{\prime}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}K^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\exp(2\delta n^{\delta})\log\log(C/\rho_{n})\right)\leq C^{\prime}n^{-2}

for
n
large.
Thus, the Borel–Cantelli lemma implies (
5.7
).
Since
v(x)=v_{n}(x)+\widetilde{v}_{n}(x)
, combining
(
5.6
) and (
5.7
) yields

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|v(x_{n})-v(x_{0})|}{d(x_{n},x_{0})\sqrt{\log\log(d(x_{n},x_{0})^{-1})}}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon\quad\text{a.s.}

Since
d(x_{n},x_{0})\to 0
, this implies

\limsup_{r\to 0+}L(r)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon
a.s.
Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
along a rational sequence, we get (
5.5
).
This completes the proof of (
5.2
).
Finally, (
5.3
) is a direct consequence of
Lemma
3.1
, (
5.2
) and (
5.9
).
∎

6.
The exact uniform modulus of continuity

The following theorem establishes the exact uniform modulus of continuity for
v
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
,
2.2
, and
2.3
, we have

(6.1)

\lim_{r\to 0+}\sup_{x,y\in T:\,0<\Delta(x,y)\leq r}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{\Delta(x,y)\sqrt{\log(\Delta(x,y)^{-1})}}=\kappa\quad\text{a.s.}

and

(6.2)

\lim_{r\to 0+}\sup_{x,y\in T:\,0<d(x,y)\leq r}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{d(x,y)\sqrt{\log(d(x,y)^{-1})}}=\kappa^{\prime}\quad\text{a.s.}

for some positive finite constants
\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
satisfying

(6.3)

\sqrt{2Qc_{2}}\leq\kappa\leq\sqrt{2Q}\,c_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\sqrt{2Qc_{2}}\,c_{1}^{-1}\leq\kappa^{\prime}\leq\sqrt{2Q},

where
Q=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}^{-1}
,
c_{1}
is the constant in (
2.5
) and

c_{2}
is the constant in Assumption
2.2
.

Remark 6.2
.

Our Assumption
2.2
of strong LND is stronger than condition
(A2)
in
Theorem 4.1 of Meerschaert et al.
[
19
]
, which is known as the sectorial
LND property. But our estimates (
6.3
) for the constants are sharper
than the estimates
(4.3)
in
[
19
]
.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

For any
r>0
, let

L(r):=\sup_{x,y\in T:\,0<\Delta(x,y)\leq r}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{\Delta(x,y)\sqrt{\log(\Delta(x,y)^{-1})}}.

By Lemma
3.1
,
\lim_{r\to 0+}L(r)=\kappa
a.s.
for some constant
0\leq\kappa\leq\infty
.
To prove (
6.1
), we aim to show that

(6.4)

\lim_{r\to 0+}L(r)\leq\sqrt{2Q}\,c_{1}\quad\text{a.s.}

and

(6.5)

\lim_{r\to 0+}L(r)\geq\sqrt{2Qc_{2}}\quad\text{a.s.}

For the upper bound (
6.4
), we first prove that
there is a finite constant
C_{0}
such that for any fixed
b>1
,

(6.6)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x,y\in T:\,\Delta(x,y)\leq 2kb^{-n}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{b^{-n}\sqrt{\log b^{n}}}\leq C_{0}\quad\text{a.s.}

Indeed, by Theorem 1.3.5 in
[
1
]
, there exists a universal constant
K_{0}
such that
for a.e.
\omega
, there exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(\omega)
such that for all
0<r<r_{0}
,

\sup_{x,y\in T:\,d(x,y)\leq r}|v(x)-v(y)|\leq K_{0}\int_{0}^{r}\sqrt{\log N(T,d,\varepsilon)}\,d\varepsilon,

where
d
is the canonical metric of
v
.
By Lemma
2.4
,
N(T,d,\varepsilon)\leq C\varepsilon^{-Q}

for all
\varepsilon>0
small, thus for
r>0
small,

\int_{0}^{r}\sqrt{\log N(T,d,\varepsilon)}\,d\varepsilon\leq Cr\sqrt{\log(1/r)}.

Also, by Lemma
2.4
,
if
\Delta(x,y)\leq 2kb^{-n}
and if
n\geq n_{0}(\omega)
is large enough,
then
d(x,y)
would be less than
r_{0}(\omega)
.
Hence, (
6.6
) follows immediately.

Of course, (
6.6
) implies
\limsup_{r\to 0+}L(r)\leq\kappa
for some
finite constant
\kappa
.
In order to improve this and get the sharper bound (
6.4
),
we use an approximation argument based on anisotropic lattice points.
Let
\varepsilon>0
and
1<a<2
.
Choose
b
such that
a<b<a^{1+\varepsilon/(2Q)}
.
Let
n\geq 1
be an integer.
For each
i=(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}
and
m=(m_{1},\dots,m_{k})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}
,
define the anisotropic lattice points
z_{n,i}
and
h_{n,m}

in
\prod_{j=1}^{k}b^{-n/\alpha_{j}}{\mathbb{Z}}
by

\displaystyle z_{n,i}=(i_{1}b^{-n/\alpha_{1}},\dots,i_{k}b^{-n/\alpha_{k}})\quad\text{and}\quad h_{n,m}=(m_{1}b^{-n/\alpha_{1}},\dots,m_{k}b^{-n/\alpha_{k}}).

Let

\displaystyle I_{n}=\{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}:z_{n,i}\in T\}\quad\text{and}\quad M_{n}=\bigg\{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}:\sum_{j=1}^{k}|m_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}b^{-n}\leq a^{-n}\bigg\}.

Consider the event

A_{n}=\bigg\{\max_{i\in I_{n}}\max_{m\in M_{n}}|v(z_{n,i}+h_{n,m})-v(z_{n,i})|>c_{1}a^{-n}\sqrt{2(Q+\varepsilon)\log a^{n}}\bigg\}.

By Lemma
2.4
, for
i\in I_{n}
,
m\in M_{n}
and
n
large,

\|v(z_{n,i}+h_{n,m})-v(z_{n,i})\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}|m_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}b^{-n}\leq c_{1}a^{-n}.

Also, the cardinality of
I_{n}
is
\leq Cb^{Qn}
and
that of
M_{n}
is
\leq C(b/a)^{Qn}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})

\displaystyle\leq Cb^{Qn}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{Qn}\max_{i\in I_{n}}\max_{m\in M_{n}}{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\frac{|v(z_{n,i}+h_{n,m})-v(z_{n,i})|}{\|v(z_{n,i}+h_{n,m})-v(z_{n,i})\|_{L^{2}}}>\sqrt{2(Q+\varepsilon)\log a^{n}}\bigg\}.

Then by the standard Gaussian estimate (
5.1
), for
n
large,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{b^{2}}{a}\right)^{Qn}\exp(-(Q+\varepsilon)\log a^{n})=C\left(\frac{b^{2}}{a^{2+\varepsilon/Q}}\right)^{Qn}.

The choice of
b
implies that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})<\infty
.
By the Borel–Cantelli lemma,

(6.7)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max_{i\in I_{n}}\max_{m\in M_{n}}\frac{|v(z_{n,i}+h_{n,m})-v(z_{n,i})|}{a^{-n}\sqrt{\log a^{n}}}\leq c_{1}\sqrt{2(Q+\varepsilon)}\quad\text{a.s.}

To prove (
6.4
),
we consider
x,y\in T
such that
a^{-n-1}\leq\Delta(x,y)\leq a^{-n}
,
and approximate them by lattice points.
Write
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
and
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})
.
Choose
i\in I_{n}
such that
z_{n,i}=z_{n,i}(x)=(i_{1}b^{-n/\alpha_{1}},\dots,i_{k}b^{-n/\alpha_{k}})
is the lattice point that is closest to
x
.
In particular, for all
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, we have

|x_{j}-i_{j}b^{-n/\alpha_{j}}|\leq b^{-n/\alpha_{j}}
.
Since
\Delta(x,y)\leq a^{-n}
, we can also find
m\in M_{n}
such that
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
,

\displaystyle m_{j}b^{-n/\alpha_{j}}\leq y_{j}-x_{j}\leq(m_{j}+1)b^{-n/\alpha_{j}}\quad\text{if }y_{j}-x_{j}\geq 0,

\displaystyle(m_{j}-1)b^{-n/\alpha_{j}}\leq y_{j}-x_{j}\leq m_{j}b^{-n/\alpha_{j}}\quad\text{if }y_{j}-x_{j}<0.

Let
h_{n,m}=h_{n,m}(x,y)=(m_{1}b^{-n/\alpha_{1}},\dots,m_{k}b^{-n/\alpha_{k}})

and write

v(y)-v(x)=[v(y)-v(z_{n,i}+h_{n,m})]+[v(z_{n,i}+h_{n,m})-v(z_{n,i})]+[v(z_{n,i})-v(x)].

Note that

\Delta(z_{n,i},x)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}|x_{j}-i_{j}b^{-n/\alpha_{j}}|^{\alpha_{j}}\leq kb^{-n}

and

\displaystyle\Delta(y,z_{n,i}+h_{n,m})

\displaystyle\leq\Delta(y,x+h_{n,m})+\Delta(x,z_{n,i})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}|y_{j}-x_{j}-m_{j}b^{-n/\alpha_{j}}|^{\alpha_{j}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}|x_{j}-i_{j}b^{-n/\alpha_{j}}|^{\alpha_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq 2kb^{-n}.

Then, since
b>a
, (
6.6
) implies that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x,y\in T:\,a^{-n-1}\leq\Delta(x,y)\leq a^{-n}}\frac{|v(y)-v(z_{n,i}+h_{n,m})|}{a^{-n}\sqrt{\log a^{n}}}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x,y\in T:\,a^{-n-1}\leq\Delta(x,y)\leq a^{-n}}\frac{|v(z_{n,i})-v(x)|}{a^{-n}\sqrt{\log a^{n}}}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

Therefore, together with (
6.7
), we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x,y\in T:\,a^{-n-1}\leq\Delta(x,y)\leq a^{-n}}\frac{|v(y)-v(x)|}{a^{-n}\sqrt{\log a^{n}}}\leq c_{1}\sqrt{2(Q+\varepsilon)}\quad\text{a.s.}

This implies that

\limsup_{r\to 0+}\sup_{x,y\in T:\,0<\Delta(x,y)\leq r}\frac{|v(y)-v(x)|}{\Delta(x,y)\sqrt{\log(\Delta(x,y)^{-1})}}\leq ac_{1}\sqrt{2(Q+\varepsilon)}\quad\text{a.s.}

Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
and
a\downarrow 1
along rational sequences,
we get the upper bound (
6.4
).

Next, we prove the lower bound (
6.5
) using
the strong LND property from Assumption
2.2
.
Let
T=\prod_{j=1}^{k}[t_{j}-s_{j},t_{j}+s_{j}]
.
For simplicity, we consider the case where
(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})\in[0,\infty)^{k}

(the proof is similar for other cases).
In this case, it is enough to prove (
6.5
) with
T
being replaced by

\tilde{T}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}[t_{j},t_{j}+s_{j}]
, whose interior does not contain the origin.
For each
n\geq 1
, let
r_{n}=2^{-n}
.
For each
i=(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}
, denote

i-1^{*}=(i_{1}-1,i_{2}\dots,i_{k}).

Define the lattice points
x_{n,i}
by

\displaystyle x_{n,i}=(i_{1}2^{-n/\alpha_{1}},\dots,i_{k}2^{-n/\alpha_{k}}).

Let

\displaystyle I_{n}=\{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}:x_{i}\in\tilde{T}\text{ and }x_{i-1^{*}}\in\tilde{T}\}\quad\text{and}\quad I^{\prime}_{n}=\{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}:x_{i}\in\tilde{T}\}.

Note that
\Delta(x_{n,i},x_{n,i-1^{*}})=r_{n}
and the function

r\mapsto r\sqrt{\log(1/r)}
is increasing for
r>0
small.
Then

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0+}L(r)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x,y\in\tilde{T}:\,0<\Delta(x,y)\leq r_{n}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{\Delta(x,y)\sqrt{\log(\Delta(x,y)^{-1})}}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}L_{n},

where

L_{n}:=\max_{i\in I_{n}}\frac{|v(x_{n,i})-v(x_{n,i-1^{*}})|}{r_{n}\sqrt{\log(1/r_{n})}}.

To prove (
6.5
), it suffices to prove that

(6.8)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}L_{n}\geq\sqrt{2Qc_{2}}\quad\text{a.s.}

To this end, let
0<K<\sqrt{2Qc_{2}}
.
Fix a large integer
n
and write
x_{i}=x_{n,i}
for simplicity.
We claim that there is a constant
C
independent of
n
or
i
such that
for all
i\in I_{n}
,

(6.9)

{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\frac{|v(x_{i})-v(x_{i-1^{*}})|}{r_{n}\sqrt{\log(1/r_{n})}}\leq K\,\bigg|\,v(x_{j}):j\in I^{\prime}_{n}\setminus\{i\}\bigg\}\leq\exp\left(-\frac{C2^{-nK^{2}/(2c_{2})}}{\sqrt{n}}\right).

Indeed, for all
i\in I_{n}
, by Assumption
2.2
and the property that
the interior of
\tilde{T}
does not contain the origin,

(6.10)

\mathrm{Var}\big(v(x_{i})\big|v(x_{j}):j\in I^{\prime}_{n}\setminus\{i\}\big)\geq c_{2}\min_{j\in I^{\prime}_{n}\cup\{0\}\setminus\{i\}}\Delta^{2}(x_{i},x_{j})=c_{2}\,r_{n}^{2}.

The conditional distribution of
v(x_{i})
given
\{v(x_{j}):j\in I^{\prime}_{n}\setminus\{i\}\}

is Gaussian with conditional variance

\mathrm{Var}(v(x_{i})|v(x_{j}):j\in I^{\prime}_{n}\setminus\{i\})
,
and
v(x_{i-1^{*}})
is constant given
\{v(x_{j}):j\in I^{\prime}_{n}\setminus\{i\}\}
.
Then, by Anderson’s inequality
[
2
]
and (
6.10
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\frac{|v(x_{i})-v(x_{i-1^{*}})|}{r_{n}\sqrt{\log(1/r_{n})}}\leq K\,\bigg|\,v(x_{j}):j\in I^{\prime}_{n}\setminus\{i\}\bigg\}\leq{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{|Z|\leq K\sqrt{c_{2}^{-1}\log(1/r_{n})}\,\Big\},

where
Z
is a standard Gaussian random variable.
Hence, we can derive (
6.9
)
using the Gaussian estimate (
5.1
) and
the elementary inequality
1-x\leq\exp(-x)
.

Let
N=N(n)
be the cardinality of
I_{n}
.
Order the members of
I_{n}
by
i(1),\dots,i(N)
in a way such that
the value of the first coordinate of
i
is nondecreasing.
For each
m\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, let
I_{n}(m)=\{i(1),\dots,i(m)\}
and
consider the event

B_{m}=\bigg\{\max_{i\in I_{n}(m)}\frac{|v(x_{i})-v(x_{i-1^{*}})|}{r_{n}\sqrt{\log(1/r_{n})}}\leq K\bigg\}.

Notice that, for each
2\leq m\leq N
,
the event
B_{m-1}
depends on the value of process
v

at points among
\{x_{i(1)-1^{*}},x_{i(1)},\dots,x_{i(m-1)-1^{*}},x_{i(m-1)}\}
,
none of which coincides with
x_{i(m)}

because of the way we order the members of
I_{n}
.
Therefore,
B_{m-1}\in\sigma\{v(x_{j}):j\in I^{\prime}_{n}\setminus\{i(m)\}\}
.
It follows from (
6.9
) that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(B_{m})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[{\bf 1}_{B_{m-1}}\,{\mathbb{P}}\bigg\{\frac{|v(x_{i(m)})-v(x_{i(m)-1^{*}})|}{r_{n}\sqrt{\log(1/r_{n})}}\leq K\,\bigg|\,v(x_{j}):j\in I^{\prime}_{n}\setminus\{i(m)\}\bigg\}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}(B_{m-1})\exp\left(-\frac{C2^{-nK^{2}/(2c_{2})}}{\sqrt{n}}\right).

Note that
N\sim C2^{nQ}
. By induction, we get that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(L_{n}\leq K)={\mathbb{P}}(B_{N})\leq\exp\left(-C2^{nQ}\frac{2^{-nK^{2}/(2c_{2})}}{\sqrt{n}}\right).

Since
Q-K^{2}/(2c_{2})>0
, we have
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(L_{n}\leq K)<\infty
.
Hence, by the Borel–Cantelli lemma,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}L_{n}\geq K\quad\text{a.s.}

Now, we let
K\uparrow\sqrt{2Qc_{2}}
along a rational sequence
to get (
6.8
). This proves (
6.1
).

We turn to the proof of (
6.2
). Let

\tilde{L}(r):=\sup_{x,y\in T:\,0<d(x,y)\leq r}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{d(x,y)\sqrt{\log(d(x,y)^{-1})}}.

By Lemma
3.1
,
\lim_{r\to 0+}\tilde{L}(r)=\kappa^{\prime}
a.s. for some constant

0\leq\kappa^{\prime}\leq\infty
.
Moreover, (
6.1
) and Lemma
2.4
imply that

\lim_{r\to 0+}\tilde{L}(r)\geq\sqrt{2Qc_{2}}\,c_{1}^{-1}\quad\text{a.s.}

It remains to prove that

(6.11)

\lim_{r\to 0+}\tilde{L}(r)\leq\sqrt{2Q}\quad\text{a.s.}

This can be proved by a similar argument that led to (
6.4
) above.
In fact, the proof of (
6.6
) above shows that for any fixed
b>1
,

(6.12)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x,y\in T:\,d(x,y)\leq b^{-n}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{b^{-n}\sqrt{\log b^{n}}}\leq C\quad\text{a.s.}

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
1<a<2
and
b
be such that
a<b<a^{1+\varepsilon/(2Q)}
.
We modify the above approximation argument as follows.
For fixed
n
, choose any minimal cover
\{B_{d}(z_{n,i},b^{-n})\}_{i}
of
T

consisting of
d
-balls with centers
z_{n,i}\in T
, and define
I_{n}=\{z_{n,i}\}_{i}
.
For each
z_{n,i}
, define
M_{n,i}=\{h_{n,i,m}\}_{m}
such that

\{B_{d}(z_{n,i}+h_{n,i,m},b^{-n})\}_{m}
is a minimal cover
of
B_{d}(z_{n,i},(1+\varepsilon)a^{-n})
.
Consider the event

A_{n}=\Big\{\max_{z_{n,i}\in I_{n}}\max_{h_{n,i,m}\in M_{n,i}}|v(z_{n,i}+h_{n,i,m})-v(z_{n,i})|>(1+\varepsilon)a^{-n}\sqrt{2(Q+\varepsilon)\log a^{n}}\Big\}.

Since
d
is comparable to
\Delta
by Assumption
2.3
and Lemma

2.4
, the cardinality of
I_{n}
is
\leq Cb^{nQ}
and
that of
M_{n,i}
is
\leq C(b/a)^{nQ}
.
Also,
\|v(z_{n,i}+h_{n,i,m})-v(z_{n,i})\|_{L^{2}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)a^{-n}
.
Then, as before, we can show that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})<\infty
and

(6.13)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max_{z_{n,i}\in I_{n}}\max_{h_{n,i,m}\in M_{n,i}}\frac{|v(z_{n,i}+h_{n,i,m})-v(z_{n,i})|}{a^{-n}\sqrt{\log a^{n}}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{2(Q+\varepsilon)}\quad\text{a.s.}

Consider
x,y\in T
such that
a^{-n-1}\leq d(x,y)\leq a^{-n}
.
Then, we can find
z_{n,i}=z_{n,i}(x)\in I_{n}
such that
d(z_{n,i},x)\leq b^{-n}
.
Since
d(z_{n,i},y)\leq d(z_{n,i},x)+d(x,y)\leq b^{-n}+a^{-n}\leq(1+\varepsilon)a^{-n}

for
n
large, we can also find
h_{n,i,m}=h_{n,i,m}(x,y)\in M_{n,i}
such that

d(z_{n,i}+h_{n,i,m},y)\leq b^{-n}
. Then, by (
6.12
) and
b>a
,
we get

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x,y\in T:\,a^{-n-1}\leq d(x,y)\leq a^{-n}}\frac{|v(z_{n,i})-v(x)|}{a^{-n}\sqrt{\log a^{n}}}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x,y\in T:\,a^{-n-1}\leq d(x,y)\leq a^{-n}}\frac{|v(z_{n,i}+h_{n,i,m})-v(y)|}{a^{-n}\sqrt{\log a^{n}}}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

Combining this with (
6.13
), we get

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x,y\in T:\,a^{-n-1}\leq d(x,y)\leq a^{-n}}\frac{|v(y)-v(x)|}{a^{-n}\sqrt{\log a^{n}}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{2(Q+\varepsilon)}\quad\text{a.s.}

which implies that

\limsup_{r\to 0+}\sup_{x,y\in T:\,0<d(x,y)\leq r}\frac{|v(y)-v(x)|}{d(x,y)\sqrt{\log(d(x,y)^{-1})}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)a\sqrt{2(Q+\varepsilon)}\quad\text{a.s.}

Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
and
a\downarrow 1
yields (
6.11
).
This completes the proof of Theorem
6.1
.
∎

7.
Linear SPDEs driven by fractional-colored noise

In this section, we give an application of our main results to a class of linear SPDEs.
Consider the equation

(7.1)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)=\mathscr{L}u(t,x)+\dot{W}(t,x),\quad t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

with zero initial condition
u(0,x)=0
. Here,
\mathscr{L}
is the infinitesimal
generator of a symmetric Lévy process
X=\{X(t),t\geq 0\}
taking values
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
\dot{W}
is a fractional-colored (or white-colored) centered Gaussian
noise with Hurst index
1/2\leq H<1
in time and spatial covariance
f
, i.e.,

{\mathbb{E}}[\dot{W}(t,x)\dot{W}(s,y)]=\rho_{H}(t-s)f(x-y),

where

\rho_{H}(t-s)=\begin{cases}a_{H}|t-s|^{2H-2}&\text{if }1/2<H<1,\\
\delta(t-s)&\text{if }H=1/2,\end{cases}

and where
a_{H}=H(2H-1)
and
\delta
is the delta function. When
X
is a Brownian motion,

\mathscr{L}
is the Laplace operator and (
7.1
) is the stochastic heat equation.
Furthermore, when
H=1/2
, (
7.1
) is the stochastic heat equation considered in
[
6
]
.

The existence of the solution to (
7.1
) has been studied in
[
3
,
7
]

(and in
[
4
]
for
H=1/2
), and the space-time regularity of the solution has been
studied in
[
24
,
7
]
. Herrell et al.
[
7
]
used the the idea of string
processes of Mueller and Tribe
[
20
]
and showed that
\{u(t,x),t\geq 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\}

admits the decomposition

(7.2)

u(t,x)=U(t,x)-Y(t,x),

where
\{U(t,x),t\geq 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\}
has stationary increments and satisfies
the property of
strong LND, while
\{Y(t,x),t\geq 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\}
has smooth sample
paths. Consequently, certain regularity properties of
u(t,x)
can be deduced
from those of
U(t,x)
. Now we can deal with
\{u(t,x),t\geq 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\}
directly.

We assume that
f
is the Fourier transform of a tempered measure
\mu

which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
with density
h
, i.e.,
\mu(d\xi)=h(\xi)d\xi
.
A typical example is
h(\xi)=|\xi|^{-\beta}
,
0<\beta<d
.
In this case,
f
is called the Riesz kernel:
f(x)=C|x|^{\beta-d}
,
where
C
is some suitable constant depending on
\beta
and
d
;
see
[
21
, §V]
.

Let
\Psi(\xi)
be the characteristic exponent of
X
given by

{\mathbb{E}}[e^{i\xi\cdot X(t)}]=e^{-t\Psi(\xi)},\quad t\geq 0,\ \xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Note that
\Psi(\xi)=\Psi(-\xi)\geq 0
for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

since
X
is assumed to be symmetric.
Assume that
X(t)
has a probability density function given by

(7.3)

p_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}e^{-t\Psi(\xi)}d\xi,\quad t>0,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Let
T_{0}>0
. Recall that the Gaussian noise
W
defines a linear isometry
from the Hilbert space completion
\mathcal{HP}
of the space
C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T_{0})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
of compactly supported smooth functions with respect
to the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{HP}}
into the Gaussian
space in
L^{2}({\mathbb{P}})
:

(7.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \varphi&\mapsto W(\varphi):=\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(s,y)W(ds,dy),\\
&\qquad{\mathbb{E}}[W(\varphi)W(\psi)]=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{HP}}.\end{aligned}

For test functions
\varphi,\psi
on
(0,T_{0})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the inner product

{\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle}_{\mathcal{HP}}
is defined by

(7.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &{\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle}_{\mathcal{HP}}:=\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}ds\,dr\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}dy\,dz\,\varphi(s,y)\rho_{H}(s-r)f(y-z)\psi(r,z)\\
&=b_{H}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}d\tau\,|\tau|^{1-2H}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}dy\,dz\,f(y-z)\mathcal{F}(\varphi(\cdot,y){\bf 1}_{[0,T_{0}]}(\cdot))(\tau)\overline{\mathcal{F}(\psi(\cdot,z){\bf 1}_{[0,T_{0}]}(\cdot))(\tau)}\\
&=c_{H,d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}d\tau\,|\tau|^{1-2H}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}d\xi\,h(\xi)\mathcal{F}(\varphi{\bf 1}_{[0,T_{0}]})(\tau,\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}(\psi{\bf 1}_{[0,T_{0}]})(\tau,\xi)},\end{aligned}

where
b_{H}=a_{H}(2^{2(1-H)}\sqrt{\pi}\,)^{-1}\Gamma(H-1/2)/\Gamma(1-H)

and
c_{H,d}=b_{H}(2\pi)^{-d}
, for
1/2<H<1
; see
[
3
,
7
]
.
In fact, the equalities in (
7.5
) also hold for
H=1/2
with

b_{1/2}=(2\pi)^{-1}
.
In the above,
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform defined,
for any functions
g:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
and
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, by

\mathcal{F}g(\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\tau s}g(s)\,ds,\quad\mathcal{F}\varphi(\tau,\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}}e^{-i\tau s-i\xi\cdot y}\varphi(s,y)\,ds\,dy.

If follows from
[
7
]
and
[
4
]

(for
1/2<H<1
and
H=1/2
respectively) that if

(7.6)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\mu(d\xi)}{1+\Psi(\xi)^{2H}}<\infty,

then (
7.1
) has a random field solution on
[0,T_{0}]
which is given by

u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}p_{t-s}(x-y)W(ds,dy).

Denote
G_{t,x}(s,y)=p_{t-s}(x-y){\bf 1}_{[0,t]}(s)
. It follows from (
7.5
) that
for any
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, for any
t^{1},\dots,t^{n}\in[0,T_{0}]
and

x^{1},\dots,x^{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we have

(7.7)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}u(t^{j},x^{j})\bigg)^{2}\Bigg]=c_{H,d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}d\tau\,|\tau|^{1-2H}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}d\xi\,h(\xi)\,|\mathcal{F}G(\tau,\xi)|^{2},

where
G=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}G_{t^{j},x^{j}}
.
Note that
p_{t}(\cdot)
is equal to the inverse Fourier transform of

\xi\mapsto e^{-t\Psi(\xi)}
since
\Psi(\xi)=\Psi(-\xi)
.
Hence, it can be verified that the Fourier transform of
G_{t,x}(\cdot,\cdot)
is

(7.8)

\mathcal{F}G_{t,x}(\tau,\xi)=\frac{e^{-i\xi\cdot x}(e^{-i\tau t}-e^{-t\Psi(\xi)})}{\Psi(\xi)-i\tau},\quad\tau\in{\mathbb{R}},\ \xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Dalang et al.
[
6
]
have established a harmonizable representation for the
solution of the stochastic heat equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+\dot{W}(t,x),

where
\dot{W}
is a spatially homogeneous Gaussian noise that is white in time
and colored in space. In the following, we follow the approach of
[
6
]
to
establish a similar representation for the solution of equation (
7.1
) driven
by the fractional-colored Gaussian noise.

Let
\tilde{W}_{1},\tilde{W}_{2}
be independent space-time Gaussian white
noise on
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Let
\tilde{W}=\tilde{W}_{1}+i\tilde{W}_{2}
.
For each
(t,x)\in[0,T_{0}]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, define

(7.9)

v(t,x)=c_{H,d}^{1/2}\,{\rm Re}\,\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathcal{F}G_{t,x}(\tau,\xi)\,|\tau|^{\frac{1-2H}{2}}h^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)\tilde{W}(d\tau,d\xi).

The following lemma verifies that
v(t,x)
has the same law as the solution
u(t,x)

of equation (
7.1
). We will call (
7.9
) the harmonizable representation
of
u(t,x)
.

Lemma 7.1
.

The Gaussian random field
v=\{v(t,x),(t,x)\in[0,T_{0}]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}

has the same law as the solution
u=\{u(t,x),(t,x)\in[0,T_{0}]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}

of equation (
7.1
).

Proof.

It is clear that
v
is Gaussian.
By (
7.5
), for any
(t,x),(s,y)\in[0,T_{0}]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[v(t,x)v(s,y)]

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathcal{F}G_{t,x}(\tau,\xi)\,\overline{\mathcal{F}G_{s,y}(\tau,\xi)}\,|\tau|^{1-2H}h(\xi)\,d\tau\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\langle G_{t,x},G_{s,y}\rangle_{\mathcal{HP}}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}[u(t,x)u(s,y)].

Hence
v
and
u
have the same law.
∎

From now on, suppose that there exist positive finite constants
c_{\Psi}
and
C_{\Psi}

such that

(7.10)

c_{\Psi}|\xi|^{\alpha}\leq\Psi(\xi)\leq C_{\Psi}|\xi|^{\alpha}\text{ for all }\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\quad\text{where }0<\alpha\leq 2,

and there exist positive finite constants
c_{h}
and
C_{h}
such that

(7.11)

c_{h}|\xi|^{-\beta}\leq h(\xi)\leq C_{h}|\xi|^{-\beta}\text{ for all }\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\quad\text{where }0<\beta<d.

Define

\theta_{1}:=H-\frac{d-\beta}{2\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{2}:=\alpha\theta_{1}=\alpha H-\frac{d-\beta}{2}.

These are the Hölder exponents of
u(t,x)
in time and space respectively.
By (
7.6
), if
\beta>d-2\alpha H
, or equivalently,
\theta_{1}>0
,
then (
7.1
) has a solution.
Consider the following metric on
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
:

(7.12)

\Delta((t,x),(s,y))=|t-s|^{\theta_{1}}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{\theta_{2}}.

We always have
\theta_{1}<1
since
H<1
and
\beta<d
.
Furthermore, we assume the following condition:

(7.13)

\theta_{1}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{2}<1.

The condition
\theta_{2}<1
is equivalent to
\beta<d-2\alpha H+2
.

We now verify Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
for the solution of (
7.1
)
using the harmonizable representation (
7.9
).

Lemma 7.2
.

Suppose
\Psi
and
h
satisfy conditions (
7.10
) and (
7.11
)
respectively. Suppose
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
satisfy condition (
7.13
).
Let
T
be a compact rectangle in
(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Then the Gaussian random field
\{v(A,t,x),A\in\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}),(t,x)\in T\}

defined by

(7.14)

v(A,t,x)=c_{H,d}^{1/2}\,{\rm Re}\,\iint_{\{(\tau,\xi):\max(|\tau|^{\theta_{1}},|\xi|^{\theta_{2}})\in A\}}\mathcal{F}G_{t,x}(\tau,\xi)\,|\tau|^{\frac{1-2H}{2}}h^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)\tilde{W}(d\tau,d\xi)

satisfies Assumption
2.1
(a).
Moreover, there exists a finite constant
c_{0}
such that for all
0\leq a<b\leq\infty

and all
(t_{0},x_{0}),(t,x)\in T
,

(7.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &{\|v([a,b),t,x)-v(t,x)-v([a,b),t_{0},x_{0})+v(t_{0},x_{0})\|}_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq c_{0}\bigg(a^{\gamma_{1}}|t-t_{0}|+a^{\gamma_{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|x_{j}-x_{0,j}|+b^{-1}\bigg),\end{aligned}

where
\gamma_{1}=\theta_{1}^{-1}-1
and
\gamma_{2}=\theta_{2}^{-1}-1
.
In particular, Assumption
2.1
(b) is satisfied for
a_{0}=0
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
v(A,t,x)
satisfies part (a) of Assumption
2.1
.
For part (b), the proof is similar to that of Lemma 7.3 in
[
6
]
:
First,

\displaystyle v([a,b),t,x)-v(t,x)-v([a,b),t_{0},x_{0})+v(t_{0},x_{0})

\displaystyle=v([0,a),t_{0},x_{0})-v([0,a),t,x)+v([b,\infty),t_{0},x_{0})-v([b,\infty),t,x).

By (
7.7
) and (
7.8
),

(7.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}[(v([0,a),t,x)-v([0,a),t_{0},x_{0}))^{2}]\\
&=C\iint_{D_{1}(a)}\bigg|\frac{(e^{-i\tau t}-e^{-t\Psi(\xi)})-e^{-i\xi\cdot(x_{0}-x)}(e^{-i\tau t_{0}}-e^{-t_{0}\Psi(\xi)})}{\Psi(\xi)-i\tau}\bigg|^{2}|\tau|^{1-2H}h(\xi)\,d\tau\,d\xi\\
&=C\iint_{D_{1}(a)}\frac{\varphi_{1}(t,x,\tau,\xi)^{2}+\varphi_{2}(t,x,\tau,\xi)^{2}}{\Psi(\xi)^{2}+|\tau|^{2}}|\tau|^{1-2H}h(\xi)\,d\tau\,d\xi\end{split}

and

(7.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}[(v([b,\infty),t,x)-v([b,\infty),t_{0},x_{0}))^{2}]\\
&=C\iint_{D_{2}(b)}\frac{\varphi_{1}(t,x,\tau,\xi)^{2}+\varphi_{2}(t,x,\tau,\xi)^{2}}{\Psi(\xi)^{2}+|\tau|^{2}}|\tau|^{1-2H}h(\xi)\,d\tau\,d\xi,\end{split}

where

\displaystyle D_{1}(a)=\{(\tau,\xi)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\max(|\tau|^{\theta_{1}},|\xi|^{\theta_{2}})<a\},

\displaystyle D_{2}(b)=\{(\tau,\xi)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\max(|\tau|^{\theta_{1}},|\xi|^{\theta_{2}})\geq b\},

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(t,x,\tau,\xi)=\cos(\tau t)-e^{-t\Psi(\xi)}-\cos(\xi\cdot(x_{0}-x)+\tau t_{0})+e^{-t_{0}\Psi(\xi)}\cos(\xi\cdot(x_{0}-x)),

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}(t,x,\tau,\xi)=-\sin(\tau t)+\sin(\xi\cdot(x_{0}-x)+\tau t_{0})-e^{-t_{0}\Psi(\xi)}\sin(\xi\cdot(x_{0}-x)).

Consider (
7.16
).
Note that
\varphi_{1}(t_{0},x_{0},\tau,\xi)=0=\varphi_{2}(t_{0},x_{0},\tau,\xi)
, and

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\varphi_{j}|\leq|\tau|+\Psi(\xi)\quad\text{and}\quad|\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}|\leq 2|\xi|,\quad j=1,2.

Then, by the mean value theorem,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[(v([0,a),t,x)-v([0,a),t_{0},x_{0}))^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C\iint_{D_{1}(a)}\Big(4(|\tau|^{2}+\Psi(\xi)^{2})|t-t_{0}|^{2}+8|\xi|^{2}|x-x_{0}|^{2}\Big)\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{\Psi(\xi)^{2}+|\tau|^{2}}d\tau\,d\xi

\displaystyle=4C|t-t_{0}|^{2}\iint_{D_{1}(a)}|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}d\tau\,d\xi+8C|x-x_{0}|^{2}\iint_{D_{1}(a)}\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{2-\beta}}{\Psi(\xi)^{2}+|\tau|^{2}}d\tau\,d\xi

\displaystyle=:4C|t-t_{0}|^{2}I_{1}+8C|x-x_{0}|^{2}I_{2}.

Using polar coordinates
r=|\xi|
,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=C\iint_{\{(\tau,r)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}:\max(|\tau|^{\theta_{1}},r^{\theta_{2}})<a\}}|\tau|^{1-2H}r^{d-\beta-1}d\tau\,dr

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{-a^{\theta_{1}^{-1}}}^{a^{\theta_{1}^{-1}}}d\tau\,|\tau|^{1-2H}\int_{0}^{a^{\theta_{2}^{-1}}}dr\,r^{d-\beta-1}

\displaystyle=Ca^{(2-2H)\theta_{1}^{-1}+(d-\beta)\theta_{2}^{-1}}.

Since
\theta_{2}=\alpha\theta_{1}
and
\gamma_{1}=\theta_{1}^{-1}-1
,
we get that
I_{1}\leq Ca^{2\gamma_{1}}
.

For
I_{2}
, we use the condition
c|\xi|^{\alpha}\leq\Psi(\xi)\leq C|\xi|^{\alpha}
,
polar coordinates
r=|\xi|
, and symmetry of the integrand in
\tau
to get that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\iint_{\{(\tau,r)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}:\max(\tau^{\theta_{1}},r^{\theta_{2}})<a\}}\frac{\tau^{1-2H}r^{d-\beta-1}}{(c^{2}\wedge 1)(r^{2\alpha}+\tau^{2})}d\tau\,dr.

Putting
\rho=r^{\alpha}
and
|z|
the Euclidean norm of

z=(\tau,\rho)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we get that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\iint_{\{(\tau,\rho)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}:\max(\tau,\rho)<a^{\theta_{1}^{-1}}\}}\frac{\tau^{1-2H}\rho^{\alpha^{-1}(d-\beta+2)-1}}{\rho^{2}+\tau^{2}}d\tau\,d\rho

\displaystyle\leq C\iint_{\{z\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}:|z|<\sqrt{2}a^{\theta_{1}^{-1}}\}}|z|^{-2H+\alpha^{-1}(d-\beta+2)-2}dz

\displaystyle=Ca^{\theta_{1}^{-1}(-2H+\alpha^{-1}(d-\beta+2))}=Ca^{2\gamma_{2}}.

Therefore, we have

(7.18)

{\mathbb{E}}[(v([0,a),t,x)-v([0,a),t_{0},x_{0}))^{2}]\leq C\left(a^{2\gamma_{1}}|t-t_{0}|^{2}+a^{2\gamma_{2}}|x-x_{0}|^{2}\right).

For (
7.17
), we can use the bounds
|\varphi_{1}|\leq 4
and
|\varphi_{2}|\leq 3

to deduce that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[(v([b,\infty),t,x)-v([b,\infty),t_{0},x_{0}))^{2}]\leq C\iint_{D_{2}(b)}\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{c^{2}|\xi|^{2\alpha}+|\tau|^{2}}d\tau\,d\xi.

To estimate the above integral, we split
D_{2}(b)
into two parts:

D_{2}(b)=\big\{|\tau|^{\theta_{1}}\leq|\xi|^{\theta_{2}},|\xi|^{\theta_{2}}\geq b\big\}\cup\big\{|\tau|^{\theta_{1}}>|\xi|^{\theta_{2}},|\tau|^{\theta_{1}}\geq b\big\}.

Passing to polar coordinates, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{D_{2}(b)}\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{c^{2}|\xi|^{2\alpha}+|\tau|^{2}}d\tau\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\iint_{\{|\tau|^{\theta_{1}}\leq r^{\theta_{2}},r^{\theta_{2}}\geq b\}}\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}r^{d-\beta-1}}{c^{2}r^{2\alpha}}d\tau\,dr+C\iint_{\{|\tau|^{\theta_{1}}>r^{\theta_{2}},|\tau|^{\theta_{1}}\geq b\}}\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}r^{d-\beta-1}}{|\tau|^{2}}d\tau\,dr

\displaystyle=C\int_{b^{\theta_{2}^{-1}}}^{\infty}dr\,r^{d-\beta-1-2\alpha}\int_{-r^{\alpha}}^{r^{\alpha}}d\tau\,|\tau|^{1-2H}+C\int_{b^{\theta_{1}^{-1}}}^{\infty}d\tau\,|\tau|^{-1-2H}\int_{0}^{|\tau|^{\alpha^{-1}}}dr\,r^{d-\beta-1}

\displaystyle=Cb^{-2}.

We have shown that

(7.19)

{\mathbb{E}}[(v([b,\infty),t,x)-v([b,\infty),t_{0},x_{0}))^{2}]\leq Cb^{-2}.

Therefore, (
7.15
) follows immediately from (
7.18
) and (
7.19
).
∎

The following result shows that
u(t,x)
satisfies strong LND with respect to
the metric
\Delta
defined in (
7.12
) above, thus
verifies Assumption
2.2
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Suppose
\Psi
and
h
satisfy conditions (
7.10
) and (
7.11
)
respectively. Suppose
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
satisfy (
7.13
).
Let
T
be a compact rectangle in
(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Then there exists a constant
c_{2}>0
such that for any
n\geq 1
, for any

(t,x),(t^{1},x^{1}),\dots,(t^{n},x^{n})\in T
, we have

\mathrm{Var}(u(t,x)|u(t^{1},x^{1}),\dots,u(t^{n},x^{n}))\geq c_{2}\min_{1\leq j\leq n}\Delta^{2}((t,x),(t^{j},x^{j})).

In particular, this implies that

\|u(t,x)-u(s,y)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\sqrt{c_{2}}\Delta((t,x),(s,y))
for all
(t,x),(s,y)\in T
.

Proof.

We may assume that
T=[a,a^{\prime}]\times[-b,b]^{d}
,
where
0<a<a^{\prime}<\infty
and
0<b<\infty
.
It suffices to show that there exists a positive constant
C
such that

{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[\bigg(u(t,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}u(t^{j},x^{j})\bigg)^{2}\Bigg]\geq Cr^{2\theta_{2}},

for any
n\geq 1
, any
(t,x),(t^{1},x^{1}),\dots,(t^{n},x^{n})\in T
,
and any
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, where

r=\min_{1\leq j\leq n}(|t-t^{j}|^{1/\alpha}\vee|x-x^{j}|).

From (
7.7
) and (
7.8
), we see that

(7.20)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[\bigg(u(t,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}u(t^{j},x^{j})\bigg)^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\geq K_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}d\tau\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}d\xi\,\bigg|e^{-i\xi\cdot x}(e^{-i\tau t}-e^{-t\Psi(\xi)})-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}e^{-i\xi\cdot x^{j}}(e^{-i\tau t^{j}}-e^{-t^{j}\Psi(\xi)})\bigg|^{2}\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{C_{\Psi}^{2}|\xi|^{2\alpha}+|\tau|^{2}},

where
K_{0}=c_{H,d}\,c_{h}
.
Let
M
be a finite constant such that

|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/\alpha}\vee|x-x^{\prime}|\leq M
for all
(t,x),(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in T
.
Let
\rho=\min\{a/M^{\alpha},1\}
.
Choose and fix two nonnegative smooth test functions

f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

which vanish outside
[-\rho,\rho]
and the unit ball respectively, and
satisfy
f(0)=g(0)=1
.
Let
f_{r}(\tau)=r^{-\alpha}f(r^{-\alpha}\tau)
,
g_{r}(\xi)=r^{-d}g(r^{-1}\xi)
,
and denote the Fourier transforms of
f_{r}
and
g_{r}
by

\widehat{f_{r}}
and
\widehat{g_{r}}
respectively.
Consider the integral

\displaystyle I:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}d\tau\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}d\xi\bigg[e^{-i\xi\cdot x}(e^{-i\tau t}-e^{-t\Psi(\xi)})-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}e^{-i\xi\cdot x^{j}}(e^{-i\tau t^{j}}-e^{-t^{j}\Psi(\xi)})\bigg]e^{i\xi\cdot x}e^{i\tau t}\widehat{f}_{r}(\tau)\widehat{g}_{r}(\xi).

By inverse Fourier transform and (
7.3
), we have

\displaystyle I=(2\pi)^{1+d}\bigg[f_{r}(0)g_{r}(0)-

\displaystyle f_{r}(t)(p_{t}\ast g_{r})(0)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\Big(f_{r}(t-t^{j})g_{r}(x-x^{j})-f_{r}(t)(p_{t^{j}}\ast g_{r})(x-x^{j})\Big)\bigg].

By the definition of
r
, for every
j
, either
|t-t^{j}|\geq r^{\alpha}
or
|x-x^{j}|\geq r
,
thus
f_{r}(t-t^{j})g_{r}(x-x^{j})=0
. Moreover, since
t/r^{\alpha}\geq a/M^{\alpha}\geq\rho
,
we have
f_{r}(t)=0
and hence

(7.21)

I=(2\pi)^{1+d}r^{-\alpha-d}.

On the other hand, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and (
7.20
),

\displaystyle I^{2}\leq\frac{1}{K_{0}}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\bigg(u(t,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}u(t^{j},x^{j})\bigg)^{2}\bigg]\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big|\widehat{f}_{r}(\tau)\widehat{g}_{r}(\xi)\big|^{2}\big(C_{\Psi}^{2}|\xi|^{2\alpha}+|\tau|^{2}\big)|\tau|^{2H-1}|\xi|^{\beta}d\tau\,d\xi.

Note that
\widehat{f}_{r}(\tau)=\widehat{f}(r^{\alpha}\tau)
and

\widehat{g}_{r}(\xi)=\widehat{g}(r\xi)
. Then by scaling, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big|\widehat{f}_{r}(\tau)\widehat{g}_{r}(\xi)\big|^{2}\big(C_{\Psi}^{2}|\xi|^{2\alpha}+|\tau|^{2}\big)|\tau|^{2H-1}|\xi|^{\beta}d\tau\,d\xi

\displaystyle=r^{-2\alpha-2\alpha H-\beta-d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big|\widehat{f}(\tau)\widehat{g}(\xi)\big|^{2}\big(C_{\Psi}^{2}|\xi|^{2\alpha}+|\tau|^{2}\big)|\tau|^{2H-1}|\xi|^{\beta}d\tau\,d\xi

\displaystyle=:r^{-2\alpha-2\alpha H-\beta-d}C_{0},

where
C_{0}
is a finite constant since
\widehat{f}
and
\widehat{g}
are
rapidly decreasing functions. It follows that

(7.22)

I^{2}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{K_{0}}\,r^{-2\alpha-2\alpha H-\beta-d}\,\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[\bigg(u(t,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}u(t^{j},x^{j})\bigg)^{2}\Bigg].

Combining (
7.21
) and (
7.22
), we conclude that

{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[\bigg(u(t,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}u(t^{j},x^{j})\bigg)^{2}\Bigg]\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2+2d}K_{0}}{C_{0}}\,r^{2\theta_{2}}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
7.3
.
∎

7.10
), (
7.11
) and (
7.13

2.4
,
7.2

7.3

Theorem 7.4
.

,

Theorem 7.6
.

Suppose
\Psi(\xi)=|\xi|^{\alpha}
and
h(\xi)=|\xi|^{-\beta}
, where

0<\alpha\leq 2
,
0<\beta<d
.
Suppose
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
satisfy condition (
7.13
).
Then, for any fixed
(t_{0},x_{0})\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, almost surely,

(7.24)

\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\frac{|u(t_{0}+\delta,x_{0})-u(t_{0},x_{0})|}{|\delta|^{\theta_{1}}\sqrt{\log\log(1/|\delta|)}}=\left(2c_{H,d}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|e^{-i\tau}-1|^{2}\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{|\tau|^{2}+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\,d\tau\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}

and

(7.25)

\limsup_{|\varepsilon|\to 0}\frac{|u(t_{0},x_{0}+\varepsilon)-u(t_{0},x_{0})|}{|\varepsilon|^{\theta_{2}}\sqrt{\log\log(1/|\varepsilon|)}}=\left(2c_{H,d}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|e^{-i\xi_{1}}-1|^{2}\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{|\tau|^{2}+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\,d\tau\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

Let
\kappa_{5}
and
\kappa_{6}
denote the quantity on the right-hand sides of
(
7.24
) and (
7.25
) respectively.
Fix
(t_{0},x_{0})\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
We claim that

(7.26)

\left\|(u(t_{0}+s,x_{0})-u(t_{0},x_{0})\right\|_{L^{2}}=|s|^{\theta_{1}}\big(\kappa_{5}+o(1)\big)\quad\text{as }s\to 0,

and

(7.27)

\left\|(u(t_{0},x_{0}+y)-u(t_{0},x_{0})\right\|_{L^{2}}=|y|^{\theta_{2}}\big(\kappa_{6}+o(1)\big)\quad\text{as }|y|\to 0.

Once the claims are proved to be true, we can consider a sequence of
neighborhoods converging to the point
t_{0}
and
x_{0}
respectively, and apply
Theorem
5.2
to the processes
\{u(t,x_{0}),t>0\}
and

\{u(t_{0},x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}
to obtain (
7.24
) and (
7.25
) respectively.

To prove (
7.26
), for
s>0
, we use (
7.7
) to get that

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0}+s,x_{0})-u(t_{0},x_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{H,d}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big|(e^{-i\tau(t_{0}+s)}-e^{-(t_{0}+s)|\xi|^{\alpha}})-(e^{-i\tau t_{0}}-e^{-t_{0}|\xi|^{\alpha}})\big|^{2}\,\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{|\tau|^{2}+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\,d\tau\,d\xi

\displaystyle=c_{H,d}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big|e^{-i\tau t_{0}}(e^{-i\tau s}-1)-e^{-t_{0}|\xi|^{\alpha}}(e^{-s|\xi|^{\alpha}}-1)\big|^{2}\,\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{|\tau|^{2}+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\,d\tau\,d\xi.

Then the change of variables

\tau\mapsto s^{-1}\tau
and
\xi\mapsto s^{-1/\alpha}\xi
leads to

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0}+s,x_{0})-u(t_{0},x_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{H,d}\,s^{2\theta_{1}}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big|(e^{-i\tau}-1)-e^{s^{-1}(i\tau t_{0}-t_{0}|\xi|^{\alpha})}(e^{-|\xi|^{\alpha}}-1)\big|^{2}\,\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{|\tau|^{2}+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\,d\tau\,d\xi.

Similarly, for
s>0
small,

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0}-s,x_{0})-u(t_{0},x_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{H,d}\,s^{2\theta_{1}}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big|(e^{-i\tau}-1)-e^{s^{-1}(i\tau(t_{0}-s)-(t_{0}-s)|\xi|^{\alpha})}(e^{-|\xi|^{\alpha}}-1)\big|^{2}\,\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{|\tau|^{2}+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\,d\tau\,d\xi.

Since
0\leq 1-e^{-|\xi|^{\alpha}}\leq\min(1,|\xi|^{\alpha})
and

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\min(1,|\xi|^{2\alpha})\,\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{|\tau|^{2}+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\,d\tau\,d\xi<\infty,

by the dominated convergence theorem, we have

|s|^{-2\theta_{1}}\|u(t_{0}+s,x_{0})-u(t_{0},x_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to\kappa_{5}^{2}
as
s\to 0
,
which is exactly (
7.26
).

For (
7.27
), we let
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}

and use (
7.7
) again to get that

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0},x_{0}+y)-u(t_{0},x_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{H,d}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big|e^{-i\xi\cdot(x_{0}+y)}(e^{-i\tau t_{0}}-e^{-t_{0}|\xi|^{\alpha}})-e^{-i\xi\cdot x_{0}}(e^{-i\tau t_{0}}-e^{-t_{0}|\xi|^{\alpha}})\big|^{2}\,\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{|\tau|^{2}+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\,d\tau\,d\xi

\displaystyle=c_{H,d}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big|(e^{-i\xi\cdot y}-1)(1-e^{i\tau t_{0}-t_{0}|\xi|^{\alpha}})\big|^{2}\,\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{|\tau|^{2}+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\,d\tau\,d\xi.

By the change of variables

\tau\mapsto|y|^{-\alpha}\tau
and
\xi\mapsto|y|^{-1}\xi
,
the above expression is equal to

\displaystyle c_{H,d}\,|y|^{2\theta_{2}}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big|\big(e^{-i\xi\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}}-1\big)\big(1-e^{|y|^{-\alpha}(i\tau t_{0}-t_{0}|\xi|^{\alpha})}\big)\big|^{2}\,\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{|\tau|^{2}+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\,d\tau\,d\xi.

Then, by a rotation of the variable
\xi
which takes the unit vector

\frac{y}{|y|}
to the basis vector
\mathrm{\bf e}_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)
,
we deduce that

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0},x_{0}+y)-u(t_{0},x_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{H,d}\,|y|^{2\theta_{2}}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big|\big(e^{-i\xi_{1}}-1\big)\big(1-e^{|y|^{-\alpha}(i\tau t_{0}-t_{0}|\xi|^{\alpha})}\big)\big|^{2}\,\frac{|\tau|^{1-2H}|\xi|^{-\beta}}{|\tau|^{2}+|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\,d\tau\,d\xi.

By the dominated convergence theorem, we get (
7.27
) as
y\to 0
.
The proof is complete.

8.
Strongly LND anisotropic Gaussian fields with non-stationary increments

Finally, we construct a class of anisotropic Gaussian random fields
that have strong LND property but do not have stationary increments.
Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a nonnegative function such that for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
,

(8.1)

\frac{C_{1}}{(\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}})^{Q+2}}\leq f(\xi)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{(\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}})^{Q+2}},

where
0<\alpha_{j}<1
,
Q=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}^{-1}
and

C_{1},C_{2}
are positive finite constants. It can be verified that
f
satisfies

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}\min\{1,|\xi|^{2}\}f(\xi)d\xi<\infty.

Define the Gaussian random field
v=\{v(x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\}
by

(8.2)

v(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(e^{ix_{j}\xi_{j}}-1)W(d\xi),

where
W
is a centered complex-valued Gaussian random measure whose control
measure has density
f
, meaning that for all Borel sets
A,B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
,

{\mathbb{E}}[W(A)\overline{W(B)}]=\int_{A\cap B}f(\xi)d\xi,\quad\text{and}\quad W(-A)=\overline{W(A)}.

This implies that
v
is real-valued.
Note that
v
does not have stationary increments.
Still, we can verify that
v
satisfies Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
in Lemmas

8.1
and
8.2
below.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
T
be a compact rectangle in
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
.
Then the process
\{v(A,x),A\in\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}),x\in T\}

defined by

v(A,x)=\int_{\{\max_{j}|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\in A\}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(e^{ix_{j}\xi_{j}}-1)W(d\xi)

satisfies Assumption
2.1
(a). Moreover, there exists a finite constant
c_{0}

such that for all
0\leq a<b\leq\infty
and all
x,y\in T
,

(8.3)

\|v(x)-v([a,b),x)-v(y)+v([a,b),y)\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{0}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{k}a^{\gamma_{j}}|x_{j}-y_{j}|+b^{-1}\bigg),

where
\gamma_{j}=\alpha_{j}^{-1}-1
.
In particular, Assumption
2.1
(b) is satisfied for
a_{0}=0
.

Proof.

It is clear that
v(A,x)
satisfies Assumption
2.1
(a).
For (
8.3
), by writing
v(x)-v(y)
as the telescoping sum

\displaystyle[v(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})-v(y_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k})]

\displaystyle+[v(y_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k})-v(y_{1},y_{2},x_{3},\dots,x_{k})]

\displaystyle+\cdots+[v(y_{1},\dots,y_{k-1},x_{k})-v(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})]

and similarly for
v([a,b),x)-v([a,b),y)
, it is enough to prove (
8.3
)
for
x
and
y
that only differ in one coordinate.
By re-arranging coordinates, we only need to consider the case that

x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
and
y=(y_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k})
.
Note that

\displaystyle v(x)-v([a,b),x)-v(y)+v([a,b),y)

\displaystyle=[v([0,a),x)-v([0,a),y)]+[v([b,\infty),x)-v([b,\infty),y)].

We estimate the two terms separately.
By
|e^{iz}-e^{iz^{\prime}}|\leq|z-z^{\prime}|
,
|e^{iz}-1|\leq 2
and (
8.1
),

\displaystyle\|v([0,a),x)-v([0,a),y)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{\max_{j}|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}<a\}}\bigg|\prod_{j=1}^{k}(e^{ix_{j}\xi_{j}}-1)-(e^{iy_{1}\xi_{1}}-1)\prod_{j=2}^{k}(e^{ix_{j}\xi_{j}}-1)\bigg|^{2}f(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2k-2}C_{2}|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{2}\int_{\{\max_{j}|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}<a\}}\frac{|\xi_{1}|^{2}}{(\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}})^{Q+2}}\,d\xi.

Note that
|\xi_{1}|^{2}=(|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}})^{2+{2(1-\alpha_{1})}/{\alpha_{1}}}\leq(\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}})^{2+{2(1-\alpha_{1})}/{\alpha_{1}}}
.
Then, by the change of variables
\xi_{j}\mapsto a^{\alpha_{j}^{-1}}\xi_{j}
,
followed by another change
\xi_{j}\mapsto z_{j}^{2/\alpha_{j}}
,
the last integral is equal to

\displaystyle a^{2\alpha_{1}^{-1}-2}\int_{\{\max_{j}|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}<1\}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\Big)^{-Q+{2(1-\alpha_{1})}/{\alpha_{1}}}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq a^{2\alpha_{1}^{-1}-2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(2/\alpha_{j})\int_{\{\max_{j}|z_{j}|^{2}<1\}}|z|^{-k+{4(1-\alpha_{1})}/{\alpha_{1}}}dz

\displaystyle\leq Ca^{2\alpha_{1}^{-1}-2},

where
C
is a finite constant, so we get the estimate

\|v([0,a),x)-v([0,a),y)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Ca^{2\gamma_{j}}|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{2}
.
On the other hand, by
|e^{iz}-1|\leq 2
and (
8.1
),

\displaystyle\|v([b,\infty),x)-v([b,\infty),y)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2^{2k}C_{2}\int_{\{\max_{j}|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\geq b\}}\frac{1}{(\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}})^{Q+2}}\,d\xi.

Now, by similar changes of variables
\xi_{j}\mapsto b^{\alpha_{j}^{-1}}\xi_{j}

and then
\xi_{j}\mapsto z_{j}^{2/\alpha_{j}}
, we get that

\|v([b,\infty),x)-v([b,\infty),y)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Cb^{-2}
.
Combining the two estimates above finishes the proof of (
8.3
).
∎

Lemma 8.2
.

Define
\Delta(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}
.
Let
T
be a compact rectangle in
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
away from the axes.
Then, there exists a positive finite constant
c_{2}
such that
for all
n\geq 1
, for all
x,x^{1},\dots,x^{n}\in T
,

\mathrm{Var}(v(x)|v(x^{1}),\dots,v(x^{n}))\geq c_{2}\min_{1\leq\ell\leq n}\Delta^{2}(x,x^{\ell}).

In particular, this implies that
\|v(x)-v(y)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\sqrt{c_{2}}\Delta(x,y)

for all
x,y\in T
.

Proof.

We may assume that
a\leq|x_{j}|\leq b
for all
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in T
,
where
0<a<1<b<\infty
are constants.
It suffices to prove that there exists a positive finite constant
c

such that for all
n\geq 1
, for all
x,x^{1},\dots,x^{n}\in T
and
all
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

(8.4)

{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\Big(v(x)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{\ell}v(x^{\ell})\Big)^{2}\bigg]\geq cr^{2},\quad\text{where }r=\min_{1\leq\ell\leq n}\max_{1\leq j\leq k}|x_{j}-x^{\ell}_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}.

By (
8.1
),

(8.5)

\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\Big(v(x)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{\ell}v(x^{\ell})\Big)^{2}\bigg]\\
&\geq C_{1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}\bigg|\prod_{j=1}^{k}(e^{ix_{j}\xi_{j}}-1)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{\ell}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(e^{ix^{\ell}_{j}\xi_{j}}-1)\bigg|^{2}\frac{d\xi}{(\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}})^{Q+2}}.\end{split}

Let
\rho=\min\{1,a(2b)^{-\alpha^{*}/\alpha_{*}}\}
,
where
\alpha^{*}=\max\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\}
and

\alpha_{*}=\min\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\}
.
For each
j=1,\dots,k
, let
\phi^{j}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be a nonnegative smooth
function supported on
[-\rho,\rho]
satisfying
\phi^{j}(0)=1
.
Let
\phi^{j}_{r}(z)=r^{-\alpha_{j}^{-1}}\phi^{j}(r^{-\alpha_{j}^{-1}}z)

and let
\widehat{\phi}^{j}_{r}
denote the Fourier transform of
\phi^{j}_{r}
.
Consider the integral

I:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}\bigg[\prod_{j=1}^{k}(e^{ix_{j}\xi_{j}}-1)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{\ell}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(e^{ix^{\ell}_{j}\xi_{j}}-1)\bigg]\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big[e^{-ix_{j}\xi_{j}}\widehat{\phi}^{j}_{r}(\xi_{j})\big]\,d\xi.

Then, by inverse Fourier transform,

I=(2\pi)^{k}\bigg[\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\phi^{j}_{r}(0)-\phi^{j}_{r}(x_{j}))-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{\ell}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\phi^{j}_{r}(x_{j}-x_{j}^{\ell})-\phi^{j}_{r}(x_{j}))\bigg].

Note that
\phi^{j}_{r}(0)=r^{-Q}
.
Since
r\leq(2b)^{\alpha^{*}}
, we have

r^{-\alpha_{j}^{-1}}|x_{j}|\geq a(2b)^{-\alpha^{*}/\alpha_{*}}\geq\rho
,
thus
\phi^{j}_{r}(x_{j})=0
.
Also, for each
\ell
, by the definition of
r
,
\max_{j}|x_{j}-x^{\ell}_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\geq r
,
so there exists some
j
such that
|x_{j}-x^{\ell}_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\geq r
.
For this
j
,
r^{-\alpha_{j}^{-1}}|x_{j}-x^{\ell}_{j}|\geq 1\geq\rho
, and hence

\phi^{j}_{r}(x_{j}-x^{\ell}_{j})=0
.
This implies that for each
\ell
,

\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\phi^{j}_{r}(x_{j}-x_{j}^{\ell})-\phi^{j}_{r}(x_{j}))=0.

Therefore, we have

(8.6)

I=(2\pi)^{k}r^{-Q}.

On the other hand, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and (
8.5
) above,

I^{2}\leq\frac{1}{C_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\Big(v(x)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{\ell}v(x^{\ell})\Big)^{2}\bigg]\times\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\widehat{\phi}^{j}_{r}(\xi_{j})|^{2}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\Big)^{Q+2}d\xi.

By
\widehat{\phi}^{j}_{r}(\xi_{j})=\widehat{\phi}^{j}(r^{\alpha_{j}^{-1}}\xi_{j})
and the change of
variables
\xi_{j}\mapsto r^{-\alpha_{j}^{-1}}\xi_{j}
, the integral on the right-hand side is
equal to

\displaystyle r^{-2Q-2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\widehat{\phi}^{j}(\xi_{j})|^{2}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\Big)^{Q+2}d\xi=C_{0}r^{-2Q-2}.

Therefore, together with (
8.6
), we have

(2\pi)^{2k}r^{-2Q}=I^{2}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{C_{1}}r^{-2Q-2}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\Big(v(x)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{\ell}v(x^{\ell})\Big)^{2}\bigg]

and (
8.4
) follows. The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 8.3
.

Let
T
be a compact rectangle in
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
away from the axes.
Then, Theorems
4.4
,
5.2
and
6.1

can be applied to the Gaussian random field
v
defined by (
8.2
).

We point out that even though the Gaussian random field
v=\{v(x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\}

defined by (
8.2
) and the fractional Brownian sheet with parameters

(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\in(0,1)^{k}
share some similarity in their definitions
and many sample path properties, some of their other fine properties such as
Chung’s LILs and exact Hausdorff measure functions are rather different (see Lee
[
11
]
).
Instead, it can be proved that these latter properties of
v=\{v(x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\}

are similar to those in
[
15
,
16
]
for Gaussian random fields with
stationary increments and spectral density
f
that satisfies (
8.1
).

A Note on the Finite Convergence of Alternating Projections Journal: Operations Research Letters
Hoa T. Bui, Ryan Loxton, Asghar Moeini

Abstract
We establish sufficient conditions for finite convergence of the alternating projections method for two non-intersecting and potentially nonconvex sets. Our results are based on a generalization of the concept of intrinsic transversality, which until now has been restricted to sets with nonempty intersection. In the special case of a polyhedron and closed half space, our sufficient conditions define the minimum distance between the two sets that is required for alternating projections to converge in a single iteration.

Introduction

Throughout this paper,
\displaystyle X
is a Hilbert space with inner product
\displaystyle\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
and associated norm
\displaystyle\left\|\cdot\right\|
.
The method of
alternating projections
for two nonempty sets
\displaystyle A,B\subset X
involves iterating the following steps, starting with
\displaystyle x_{0}\in A
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle y_{n}\in P_{B}(x_{n}),

\displaystyle\displaystyle x_{n+1}\in P_{A}(y_{n}).

Here,
\displaystyle P_{B}(x_{n})
is the set of all projections of
\displaystyle x_{n}
onto
\displaystyle B
and
\displaystyle P_{A}(y_{n})
is the set of all projections of
\displaystyle y_{n}
onto
\displaystyle A
.
The study of the convergence of this method in the
consistent case
(i.e.
\displaystyle A\cap B\neq\emptyset
) has a long history that can be traced back to von Neumman; see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
for historical comments. In particular, for convex settings, Bregman
[
5
]
showed that the method always converges,
and a
linear convergence
rate was established by Gubin et al.
[
6
]
and Bauschke
& Borwein
[
7
]
.
For nonconvex settings, conditions such as
superregularity
and
intrinsic transversality
can be imposed to ensure linear convergence (see the results by Dao et al.
[
8
]
and Drusvyatskiy et al.
[
2
]
).
Furthermore, Noll and Rondepierre in
[
4
]
studied a general setting that allows
for nonlinear convergence under more subtle nonlinear regularity assumptions.

For the inconsistent case, when
\displaystyle A\cap B=\emptyset
, the method of alternating projections does not converge to a single point, but under certain conditions it will converge to a pair of points of minimum distance.
For example,
Cheney and Goldstein showed in
[
9
]
that in Euclidean spaces when the two sets are closed and convex, and one of the sets is compact, the method converges and attains the minimum distance between the two sets.
In particular, this result holds for two polytopes.
Because of this important convergence property, alternating projections for inconsistent cases has been widely applied
[
6
,
10
,
11
]
; see also
[
12
,
13
]
for reviews.

There has been a handful of research papers aimed at establishing certain convergence rates for the alternating projections method in the inconsistent case (see for example
[
14
]
), but almost all results consider convergence in the limit rather than finite convergence. To the best of our knowledge the only exception is a recent paper by Behling et al.
[
15
]
, which considers
finite convergence
for two non-intersecting closed convex sets satisfying some error bound conditions. Our work in this paper also aims at finding sufficient conditions to ensure finite convergence, but for the general nonconvex setting.

Our approach is to extend the concept of intrinsic transversality, first defined for consistent cases in
[
2
]
,
to the more general setting when the intersection can be empty or nonempty (Conditions 1 and 2 in Section
3
).
Under either condition, we show that the number of iterations depends on the distance between the two sets, the starting point and the maximum angle between vectors of type
\displaystyle(a-b)
, where
\displaystyle a\in A
and
\displaystyle b\in B
, and the proximal normal cones
\displaystyle N_{A}^{\text{prox}}(a)
and
\displaystyle N_{B}^{\text{prox}}(b)
.
In particular, these results are applicable when the two sets are a polyhedron and a closed half space.

The alternating projections method can be used to solve linear programming problems. Indeed, a minimization linear program
can be formulated as finding the closest points of the following sets:

(1)

the problem’s feasible region; and

(2)

the closed half space containing all vectors whose objective function value is strictly less than a specified lower bound for the objective over the feasible region.

Finite convergence of the alternating projections method for this setting is guaranteed by our sufficient conditions. This idea of solving linear programming problems using alternating projections was also independently considered in
[
15
]
. However, our approach has some advantages. First, we can estimate the number of iterations required for convergence. Second, we can also determine the minimum distance (or lower bound), relative to the starting point that is needed to ensure convergence after one iteration (see Figure
1
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some essential results that will be used in subsequent sections, and we provide a new proof for one of these results. Section
3
contains our main results on the finite convergence of the alternating projections method. Finally, Section
4
explores the new finite convergence results for the special case of a polyhedron and a closed half space, which is related to linear programming.

Preliminaries and auxiliary results

Let
\displaystyle\mathbb{B}
,
\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{*}
denote the open unit balls in the primal and dual spaces
\displaystyle X
,
\displaystyle X^{*}
and furthermore let
\displaystyle B_{\delta}(x)
and
\displaystyle\overline{B}_{\delta}(x)
denote, respectively, the open and closed balls with center
\displaystyle x
and radius
\displaystyle\delta>0
. We use
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}
to denote the real line (with the usual norm) and the non-negative subset of the real line. We use the notation
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}(v)
for the cone
\displaystyle\{\lambda v:\lambda\geq 0\}
generated by a vector
\displaystyle v
in
\displaystyle X
.
The boundary and interior of a set
\displaystyle A
are denoted as
\displaystyle{\rm bd}\,A
and
\displaystyle{\rm int}\,A
, respectively.
The distance from a point
\displaystyle x
to a set
\displaystyle A
is defined by
\displaystyle d(x,A):=\inf_{u\in A}\|u-x\|
, and we use the convention
\displaystyle d(x,\emptyset)=+\infty
. The set of all projections of
\displaystyle x
onto
\displaystyle A
is

P_{A}(x):=\{a\in A:d(x,a)=d(x,A)\}.

If
\displaystyle A
is a closed subset of a finite dimensional space, then
\displaystyle P_{A}(x)\neq\emptyset
.
Additionally, if
\displaystyle A
is a closed convex set of an Euclidean space, then
\displaystyle P_{A}(x)
is a singleton.
The identity
\displaystyle d\left(v/\left\|v\right\|,\mathbb{R}_{+}(u)\right)=d\left(u/\left\|u\right\|,\mathbb{R}_{+}(v)\right)
for
\displaystyle u,v\in X\setminus\{0\}
is used in subsequent sections. This assertion is proved as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left[d\left(\frac{v}{\left\|v\right\|},\mathbb{R}_{+}(u)\right)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\min_{\lambda\geq 0}\left\|\frac{v}{\left\|v\right\|}-\lambda u\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\min_{\lambda\geq 0}\left(1+\lambda^{2}\left\|u\right\|^{2}-\frac{2\lambda}{\left\|v\right\|}\left\langle v,u\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1-\frac{\left\langle v,u\right\rangle^{2}}{\left\|u\right\|^{2}\left\|v\right\|^{2}},&\text{if }\left\langle u,v\right\rangle>0,\\
1,&\text{if }\left\langle u,v\right\rangle\leq 0,\end{cases}

and similarly

\left[d\left(\frac{u}{\left\|u\right\|},\mathbb{R}_{+}(v)\right)\right]^{2}=\begin{cases}1-\frac{\left\langle v,u\right\rangle^{2}}{\left\|u\right\|^{2}\left\|v\right\|^{2}},&\text{if }\left\langle u,v\right\rangle>0,\\
1,&\text{if }\left\langle u,v\right\rangle\leq 0.\end{cases}

As defined in
[
16
]
, the
proximal normal cone
to
\displaystyle A
at
\displaystyle a\in A
is:

\displaystyle\displaystyle N^{\text{prox}}_{A}(a)

\displaystyle\displaystyle:={\rm cone}\,(P^{-1}_{A}(a)-a)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\lambda(x-a):\lambda\geq 0,a\in P_{A}(x)\right\}.

For convenience, we will use the notation
\displaystyle N_{A}(a)
instead of
\displaystyle N^{\text{prox}}_{A}(a)
throughout.
Observe that if
\displaystyle a\in P_{A}(x)
, then
\displaystyle x-a\in N_{A}(a)
. The proximal normal cone is related to the proximal subdifferential of a proper semicontinuous function
\displaystyle f
, denoted
\displaystyle\partial_{P}f
, or
\displaystyle\partial f
for simplicity. Indeed,
\displaystyle\partial\mathbbm{1}_{A}(a)=N_{A}(a)
for any
\displaystyle a\in A
, where here
\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{A}
is the indicator function of the set
\displaystyle A
defined by
\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x)=0
if
\displaystyle x\in A
and
\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x)=+\infty
, otherwise. The proximal subdifferential satisfies the following fuzzy sum rule (see
[
16
]
, page 240).

Lemma 1

(Fuzzy sum rule)
.

Suppose
\displaystyle f_{1}
is lower semicontinuous and

\displaystyle f_{2}
is Lipschitz continuous
in a neighbourhood of
\displaystyle\bar{x}
.
Then, for any
\displaystyle x^{*}\in\partial(f_{1}+f_{2})(\bar{x})
and
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
, there exist
\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2}\in X
with
\displaystyle\|x_{i}-\bar{x}\|<\varepsilon
,
\displaystyle|f_{i}(x_{i})-f_{i}(\bar{x})|<\varepsilon

\displaystyle(i=1,2)
, such that

\displaystyle x^{*}\in\partial f_{1}(x_{1})+\partial f_{2}(x_{2})+(\varepsilon\mathbb{B}^{*}).

The following result has been proved in
[
2
]
and here we give an alternative proof. Our proof consists of two key ingredients: (1) Ekeland’s Variational Principle
[
17
]
, and (2) the fuzzy sum rule in Lemma
1
.

Theorem 2

(Distance decrease)
.

[
2
, Theorem 5.2]

Consider a Hilbert space
\displaystyle X
, a closed set
\displaystyle A
, and points
\displaystyle a\in A
,
\displaystyle b\notin A
with
\displaystyle\rho:=\left\|a-b\right\|
and
\displaystyle\alpha>0
. If there is
\displaystyle\delta>0
such that

\inf\left\{d\left(\frac{b-x}{\left\|b-x\right\|},N_{A}(x)\right):x\in B_{\rho}(b)\cap B_{\delta}(a)\cap A\right\}\geq\alpha,

(1)

then
\displaystyle d(b,A)\leq\left\|a-b\right\|-\alpha\delta
.

Proof.

Consider the function
\displaystyle f(x)=\left\|x-b\right\|
and suppose to the contrary that (
1
) holds but

\displaystyle d(b,A)>\left\|a-b\right\|-\alpha\delta
.
Take
\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}\in(0,\alpha)
such that
\displaystyle d(b,A)>\left\|a-b\right\|-\alpha^{\prime}\delta
.
This is equivalent to
\displaystyle\inf_{x\in A}f(x)>f(a)-\alpha^{\prime}\delta
.
By Ekeland’s Variational Principle, there is a vector
\displaystyle x_{0}\in A\cap B_{\delta}(a)
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle f(x_{0})

\displaystyle\displaystyle<f(a),

(2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle f(x_{0})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq f(x)+\alpha^{\prime}\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|,\quad\forall x\in A.

(3)

Due to (
2
),
\displaystyle\left\|x_{0}-b\right\|<\left\|a-b\right\|=\rho
, or
\displaystyle x_{0}\in A\cap B_{\rho}(b)
. By (
3
), it follows that
\displaystyle x_{0}
is a global minimizer of the sum function
\displaystyle f(x)+\alpha^{\prime}\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x)
. Thus,

0\in\partial\left(f(x)+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x)+\alpha^{\prime}\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|\right)\mid_{x=x_{0}}.

Take
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
such that

\epsilon<\min\left\{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime},\rho-\left\|x_{0}-b\right\|,\delta-\left\|x_{0}-a\right\|\right\}.

By the fuzzy sum rule applied at
\displaystyle x_{0}
for the functions
\displaystyle f(x)+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x)
and
\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|
, there exist
\displaystyle\bar{x},x^{\prime}\in B_{\epsilon}(x_{0})
such that
\displaystyle\left|f(\bar{x})+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\bar{x})-f(x_{0})-\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x_{0})\right|<\epsilon
and

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle\in\partial(f(x)+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x))\mid_{x=\bar{x}}+\partial(\alpha^{\prime}\left\|x-x_{0}\right\|)\mid_{x=x^{\prime}}+\epsilon\mathbb{B}^{*}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\subset\partial(f(x)+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x))\mid_{x=\bar{x}}+\alpha^{\prime}\mathbb{B}^{*}+\epsilon\mathbb{B}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\subset\partial(f(x)+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x))\mid_{x=\bar{x}}+\alpha\mathbb{B}^{\ast}.

Therefore,
\displaystyle\bar{x}\in A\cap B_{\epsilon}(x_{0})
and
\displaystyle\partial(f(x)+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x))\mid_{x=\bar{x}}\cap(\alpha\mathbb{B}^{*})\neq\emptyset
.
On the other hand, since
\displaystyle X
is a Hilbert space and

f(\bar{x})=\left\|\bar{x}-b\right\|\geq d(b,A)>0,

the function
\displaystyle f
is differentiable at
\displaystyle\bar{x}
and
\displaystyle\nabla f(\bar{x})=\frac{\bar{x}-b}{\left\|b-\bar{x}\right\|}
.
Thus,

\partial(f(x)+\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x))\mid_{x=\bar{x}}=\frac{\bar{x}-b}{\left\|b-\bar{x}\right\|}+N_{A}(\bar{x}).

Recall that
\displaystyle\left(\frac{\bar{x}-b}{\left\|b-\bar{x}\right\|}+N_{A}(\bar{x})\right)\cap(\alpha\mathbb{B}^{\ast})\neq\emptyset
, or

d\left(\frac{b-\bar{x}}{\left\|b-\bar{x}\right\|},N_{A}(\bar{x})\right)<\alpha.

Furthermore, by the choice of
\displaystyle\epsilon
,
\displaystyle\bar{x}\in A\cap B_{\rho}(b)
and

\left\|\bar{x}-a\right\|\leq\left\|\bar{x}-x_{0}\right\|+\left\|x_{0}-a\right\|<\epsilon+\left\|x_{0}-a\right\|<\delta,

which shows that
\displaystyle\bar{x}\in B_{\delta}(a)
, and hence the previous inequality contradicts (
1
).
∎

The next proposition is a supplementary result that provides a characterization for two points in disjoint convex sets that are of minimum distance apart.

Proposition 3
.

Consider two closed convex subsets
\displaystyle A,B
of a Hilbert space
\displaystyle X
with
\displaystyle d(A,B)>0
and
\displaystyle a\in A,b\in B
. Then
\displaystyle\left\|a-b\right\|=d(A,B)
if and only if

(b-a)\in N_{A}(a),\and(a-b)\in N_{B}(b).

(4)

Proof.

If
\displaystyle\left\|a-b\right\|=d(A,B)>0
with
\displaystyle a\in A
,
\displaystyle b\in B
, then
\displaystyle a\in P_{A}(b)
and
\displaystyle b\in P_{B}(a)
. By the definition of the proximal normal cones,
\displaystyle b-a\in N_{A}(a)
and
\displaystyle a-b\in N_{B}(b)
.

Consider the distance function restricted to the sets
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle B
defined by
\displaystyle f(x,y):=\left\|x-y\right\|+\mathbbm{1}_{A\times B}(x,y)
, where
\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{A\times B}
is the indicator function of the set
\displaystyle A\times B
. The product space
\displaystyle X\times X
is equipped with the usual
\displaystyle L_{2}
norm.
When the two sets
\displaystyle A,B
are convex, the function
\displaystyle f
is convex. The pair
\displaystyle(a,b)
is the global minimizer of
\displaystyle f
, or equivalently a pair of shortest distance between
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle B
, if and only if

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0\in\partial f(a,b)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\partial\left(\left\|x-y\right\|\right)\mid_{(x,y)=(a,b)}+N_{A\times B}(a,b)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\partial\left(\left\|x-y\right\|\right)\mid_{(x,y)=(a,b)}+N_{A}(a)\times N_{B}(b).

Since
\displaystyle A\cap B=\emptyset
, then
\displaystyle a-b\neq 0
and

\partial(\left\|x-y\right\|)\mid_{(x,y)=(a,b)}=\left\{\left(\frac{a-b}{\left\|a-b\right\|},\frac{b-a}{\left\|b-a\right\|}\right)\right\}.

The inclusion
\displaystyle 0\in\partial f(a,b)
is equivalent to (
4
).
∎

Note that in nonconvex settings inclusion (
4
) is a necessary but not sufficient condition for
\displaystyle\left\|a-b\right\|=d(A,B)
.

The following definition was introduced in
[
2
]
. Our convergence results rely on a modification of this definition to the inconsistent setting.

Definition 4

(Intrinsic transversality)
.

[
2
, Definition 3.1]

Given two closed sets
\displaystyle A,B
of a Hilbert space
\displaystyle X
,
\displaystyle\bar{x}\in A\cap B
, we say that
\displaystyle\{A,B\}
is intrinsically transversal at
\displaystyle\bar{x}
with degree
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
if there is
\displaystyle\rho>0
such that for all
\displaystyle x\in(A\setminus B)\cap B_{\rho}(\bar{x}),y\in(B\setminus A)\cap B_{\rho}(\bar{x})
, we have

\max\left\{d\left(\frac{x-y}{\left\|x-y\right\|},N_{B}(y)\right),d\left(\frac{y-x}{\left\|x-y\right\|},N_{A}(x)\right)\right\}\geq\alpha.

(5)

The key result linking intrinsic transversality with linear convergence of the alternating projections method is restated from
[
2
]
below.

Theorem 5

(Linear convergence)
.

[
2
, Theorem 6.1]

If two closed sets
\displaystyle A,B
of an Euclidean space
\displaystyle X
are intrinsically transversal at a point
\displaystyle\bar{x}\in A\cap B
, with degree
\displaystyle\alpha>0
, then, for any constant
\displaystyle c
in the interval
\displaystyle(0,\alpha)
the method of alternating projections, initiated sufficiently near
\displaystyle\bar{x}
, converges to a point in the intersection
\displaystyle A\cap B
with linear rate
\displaystyle 1-c^{2}
.

3
Convergence results

We extend the definition of intrinsic transversality in Definition
4
to more general frameworks without the assumption
\displaystyle A\cap B\neq\emptyset
, removing the need for
\displaystyle\bar{x}
and its local neighbourhood
\displaystyle B_{\rho}(\bar{x})
.
Condition 1 below is a global condition that requires (
5
) to hold across the entire sets
\displaystyle A,B
.
Condition 1’ is a weaker condition that only requires (
5
) to hold in certain neighbourhoods around two points in
\displaystyle A,B
that are of minimum distance apart.

Condition 1.

Given two closed sets
\displaystyle A,B
of a Hilbert space
\displaystyle X
, and
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
, inequality (
5
) holds for all
\displaystyle x\in A\setminus B,y\in B
with
\displaystyle d(y,A)>d(A,B)
.

Condition 1’.

Given two closed sets
\displaystyle A,B
of a Hilbert space
\displaystyle X
,
\displaystyle a\in A
,
\displaystyle b\in B
such that
\displaystyle\left\|a-b\right\|=d(A,B)=d
, and
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists
\displaystyle\rho>0
such that inequality (
5
) holds for all
\displaystyle x\in(A\setminus B)\cap B_{2d+\rho}(b)
and
\displaystyle y\in(B\setminus A)\cap B_{2d+\rho}(a)
with
\displaystyle d(y,A)>d(A,B)
.

Remark 6
.

If
\displaystyle A\cap B\neq\emptyset
, then
\displaystyle d(A,B)=0
and Condition 1’ reduces to Definition
4
and Condition 1 reduces to the following condition.

Condition 1”.

Given two closed sets
\displaystyle A,B
of a Hilbert space
\displaystyle X
,
\displaystyle A\cap B\neq\emptyset
, and a constant
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
, inequality (

\displaystyle x\in A\setminus B,y\in B\setminus A

Condition 1” is an extension of Definition

to the global framework. Indeed, under Condition 1”, the pair
\displaystyle\{A,B\}
is intrinsically transversal at any
\displaystyle\bar{x}\in A\cap B
.

We will show later in this section that under Conditions 1 or 1’, when
\displaystyle A\cap B=\emptyset
, the method of alternating projections converges after a finite number of steps.
To do this, we need the following key result.

Lemma 7
.

Consider two closed subsets
\displaystyle A,B
of a Hilbert space
\displaystyle X
,
\displaystyle x\in A\setminus B
and
\displaystyle y\in P_{B}(x)
satisfying
\displaystyle d(y,A)>d(A,B)
, and
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\displaystyle\delta:=\alpha\left\|x-y\right\|
. Suppose that the following inequality holds for all
\displaystyle z\in A\cap B_{\delta}(x)\setminus B
:

\max\left\{d\left(\frac{z-y}{\left\|z-y\right\|},N_{B}(y)\right),d\left(\frac{y-z}{\left\|z-y\right\|},N_{A}(z)\right)\right\}\geq\alpha.

(6)

Then,

d(y,A)\leq(1-\alpha^{2})\left\|x-y\right\|.

(7)

Proof.

Take
\displaystyle x\in A\setminus B
and
\displaystyle y\in P_{B}(x)
such that
\displaystyle d(y,A)>d(A,B)
.
We have
\displaystyle x\neq y
.
Let
\displaystyle\rho:=\left\|x-y\right\|>0
and
\displaystyle\delta:=\alpha\left\|x-y\right\|>0
.
For any
\displaystyle z\in A\cap B_{\delta}(x)
, we have
\displaystyle z\neq y
and

\displaystyle\displaystyle d\left(\frac{z-y}{\left\|z-y\right\|},N_{B}(y)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq d\left(\frac{z-y}{\left\|z-y\right\|},\mathbb{R}_{+}(x-y)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=d\left(\frac{x-y}{\left\|x-y\right\|},\mathbb{R}_{+}(z-y)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{x-y}{\left\|x-y\right\|}-\frac{z-y}{\left\|x-y\right\|}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{\left\|z-x\right\|}{\left\|x-y\right\|}<\alpha.

Therefore, by inequality (
6
),

\inf\left\{d\left(\frac{y-z}{\left\|y-z\right\|},N_{A}(z)\right):z\in A\cap B_{\delta}(x)\setminus B\right\}\geq\alpha.

(8)

Furthermore, for any
\displaystyle z\in A\cap B_{\delta}(x)
, by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\displaystyle d(z,B)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq d(x,B)-\left\|x-z\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\|x-y\right\|-\left\|x-z\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\left\|x-y\right\|-\delta=(1-\alpha)\left\|x-y\right\|>0,

which implies
\displaystyle z\in A\setminus B
. Thus,
\displaystyle A\cap B_{\delta}(x)\cap B_{\rho}(y)\setminus B=A\cap B_{\delta}(x)\cap B_{\rho}(y)
, and from (
8
),

\inf\left\{d\left(\frac{y-z}{\left\|y-z\right\|},N_{A}(z)\right):z\in A\cap B_{\delta}(x)\cap B_{\rho}(y)\right\}\geq\alpha.

Then, applying Theorem
2
, we obtain

d(y,A)\leq\left\|x-y\right\|-\alpha\delta=(1-\alpha^{2})\left\|x-y\right\|,

as required.∎

Note that Conditions 1 and 1” meet the conditions required for Lemma
7
. Now, consider three consecutive alternating projections:

x_{2n}\in A,\quad x_{2n+1}\in P_{B}(x_{2n}),\quad x_{2n+2}\in P_{A}(x_{2n+1}).

Under Condition 1, if
\displaystyle d(x_{2n+1},A)>d(A,B)
(i.e., convergence has not been achieved after
\displaystyle 2n+1

Theorem 8
.

Consider two closed sets
\displaystyle A,B
of a Hilbert space
\displaystyle X
and suppose Condition 1 holds for some
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
. Consider a sequence of alternating projections
\displaystyle(x_{n})
where
\displaystyle x_{2n}\in A
and
\displaystyle x_{2n+1}\in B
(
\displaystyle n\geq 0
).

(i)

If
\displaystyle d(A,B)>0
, then the sequence
\displaystyle(x_{n})
attains the minimum distance in at most
\displaystyle 2N+1
steps, where

N:=\left\lfloor\log_{1-\alpha^{2}}\left(\frac{d(A,B)}{d(x_{0},B)}\right)\right\rfloor.

(9)

(ii)

If
\displaystyle d(A,B)=0
, then the sequence
\displaystyle(x_{n})
converges linearly to a point in the intersection
\displaystyle A\cap B
with rate
\displaystyle(1-\alpha^{2})
, i.e.,

\left\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|\leq(1-\alpha^{2})^{n}\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|.

(10)

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle d(A,B)>0
.
If convergence has not occurred after
\displaystyle 2n+1
steps (

d(A,B)<d\left(x_{2n+1},A\right)\leq(1-\alpha^{2})^{n+1}\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|.

The next theorem establishes linear and finite convergence for the local setting defined by Condition 1’.

Theorem 13
.

Consider two closed subsets
\displaystyle A,B
of a Hilbert space
\displaystyle X
,
\displaystyle a\in A,b\in B
such that
\displaystyle\left\|a-b\right\|=d(A,B)=d
and suppose Condition 1’ holds for some
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle\rho>0
. Consider a sequence of alternating projections
\displaystyle(x_{n})
where
\displaystyle x_{2n}\in A
and
\displaystyle x_{2n+1}\in B
(
\displaystyle n\geq 0
), initiated sufficiently close to
\displaystyle a
.

(i)

If
\displaystyle d(A,B)>0
, then
\displaystyle(x_{n})
attains the minimum distance in one step.

(ii)

If
\displaystyle d(A,B)=0
, then
\displaystyle(x_{n})
converges linearly with rate
\displaystyle 1-\alpha^{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose that Condition 1’ holds with
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle\rho>0
.

Consider the case
\displaystyle d(A,B)>0
. Take
\displaystyle\kappa:=\min\{\alpha^{2}d(A,B),\rho/2\}
and
\displaystyle x_{0}\in A\cap B_{\kappa}(a)
,
\displaystyle x_{1}\in P_{B}(x_{0})
. Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|x_{1}-a\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|+\left\|x_{0}-a\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left\|b-x_{0}\right\|+\left\|x_{0}-a\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left\|b-a\right\|+2\left\|x_{0}-a\right\|<2d(A,B)+2\kappa,

and so
\displaystyle x_{1}\in B_{2d+\rho}(a)
. Let
\displaystyle\delta:=\alpha\left\|x_{0}-x_{1}\right\|
.
For any
\displaystyle z\in A\cap B_{\delta}(x_{0})
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|z-b\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left\|z-x_{0}\right\|+\left\|x_{0}-b\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\alpha\left\|x_{0}-x_{1}\right\|+\left\|x_{0}-b\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq(1+\alpha)\left\|x_{0}-b\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle<2d(A,B)+2\kappa,

and hence
\displaystyle z\in A\cap B_{2d+\rho}(b)
. Thus, since
\displaystyle x_{1}\in B_{2d+\rho}(a)
and
\displaystyle z\in A\cap B_{\delta}(x_{0})
implies
\displaystyle z\in A\cap B_{2d+\rho}(b)
, Condition 1’ ensures that Lemma
7
can be applied to
\displaystyle x_{0}
and
\displaystyle x_{1}
when
\displaystyle d(x_{1},A)>d(A,B)
.
This gives

\displaystyle\displaystyle d(x_{1},A)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|-\alpha^{2}\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left\|x_{0}-b\right\|-\alpha^{2}d(A,B)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left\|x_{0}-b\right\|-\kappa

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left\|x_{0}-a\right\|+\left\|a-b\right\|-\kappa<d(A,B),

which contradicts the assumption
\displaystyle d(x_{1},A)>d
. This implies
\displaystyle d(x_{1},A)=d(A,B)
, and hence convergence occurs after one step when the sequence is initiated in
\displaystyle A\cap B_{\kappa}(a)
.

Now consider the case
\displaystyle d(A,B)=0
. In this case, we have
\displaystyle a\equiv b\equiv\bar{x}\in A\cap B
.
Set
\displaystyle\kappa:=\alpha^{2}\rho/(2\alpha^{2}+2)
.
Take
\displaystyle x_{0}\in A\cap B_{\kappa}(\bar{x})
. We now prove by induction that the following holds for all
\displaystyle n\geq 0
:

x_{n}\in B_{\rho/2}(\bar{x}),\quad\left\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|\leq(1-\alpha^{2})^{n}\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|.

(13)

This holds immediately for
\displaystyle n=0
. Assume now that it holds for
\displaystyle n=0,\ldots,k
. Then,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|x_{k+1}-\bar{x}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{k}\left\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|+\left\|x_{0}-\bar{x}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{k}(1-\alpha^{2})^{n}\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|+\left\|x_{0}-\bar{x}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1-(1-\alpha^{2})^{k+1}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|+\left\|x_{0}-\bar{x}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|}{\alpha^{2}}+\left\|x_{0}-\bar{x}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}+1\right)\left\|x_{0}-\bar{x}\right\|<\tfrac{1}{2}\rho,

and hence
\displaystyle x_{k+1}\in B_{\rho/2}(\bar{x})
. If
\displaystyle x_{k+1}\in A\cap B
, then convergence has been achieved and the inequality in (
13
) holds trivially for
\displaystyle n=k+1
. Otherwise,
\displaystyle d(x_{n+1},A)>0
if
\displaystyle x_{k}\in A
and
\displaystyle d(x_{k+1},B)>0
if
\displaystyle x_{k}\in B
. For any
\displaystyle z\in B_{\delta}(x_{k})
, with
\displaystyle\delta:=\alpha\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|z-\bar{x}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left\|z-x_{k}\right\|+\left\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle<\delta+\left\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|+\left\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\right\|<\rho,

and hence
\displaystyle z\in B_{\rho}(\bar{x})
. Hence, by Condition 1’, if
\displaystyle x_{k+1}\notin A\cap B
, then when
\displaystyle x_{k}\in A
, we can apply Lemma
7
to derive
\displaystyle d(x_{k+1},A)\leq(1-\alpha^{2})\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|
, and when
\displaystyle x_{k}\in B
we can apply Lemma
7
to derive
\displaystyle d(x_{k+1},B)\leq(1-\alpha^{2})\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|
. This gives

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|x_{k+2}-x_{k+1}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha^{2})\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha^{2})^{n+1}\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|,

which shows that (
13
) holds for
\displaystyle n=k+1
.
∎

Remark 14
.

(i)

Since Condition 1’ covers intrinsic transversality, Theorem
13
covers Theorem
5
.

(ii)

Under Condition 1’ when
\displaystyle d(A,B)>0
, it follows from Theorem
13
(i) that if the alternating projections converge to
\displaystyle a
and
\displaystyle b
, then they must converge in a finite number of steps, because eventually the sequence will enter the ball
\displaystyle A\cap B_{\kappa}(a)
, after which only one more projection is needed. However, estimating the number of steps is difficult because Condition 1’ is only a local condition and it may not be satisfied at every iteration. In Theorem
8
, we could quantify the number of steps because Condition 1 applies globally, unlike Condition 1’.

We now define an alternative to Conditions 1 and 1’.

Condition 2.

Given two closed subsets
\displaystyle A,B
of a Hilbert space
\displaystyle X
, and
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\displaystyle\beta\in[0,1]
, the following inequality holds for all
\displaystyle x\in A\setminus B
and
\displaystyle y\in P_{B}(x)
such that
\displaystyle d(y,A)>d(A,B)
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\inf\bigg\{d\left(\frac{y-z}{\left\|y-z\right\|},N_{A}(z)\right):\;z\in A\cap B_{\rho}(y)\cap B_{\delta}(x),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\rho=\left\|x-y\right\|,\,\delta=\alpha(\left\|x-y\right\|-\beta d(A,B))\bigg\}\geq\alpha.

Unlike Condition 1, which considers all vectors
\displaystyle y\in B\setminus A
, Condition 2 only considers the projections of
\displaystyle x\in A
onto
\displaystyle B
.
Condition 2 also provides the flexibility to choose the neighbourhood of
\displaystyle x
in
\displaystyle A
by adjusting the new parameter
\displaystyle\beta
. The next theorem shows that the speed of convergence depends on the neighbourhood’s radius.

Theorem 15
.

Consider two closed subsets
\displaystyle A,B
of a Hilbert space
\displaystyle X
with
\displaystyle d(A,B)=d\geq 0
and suppose Condition 2 holds for some
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle\beta\in[0,1]
. Consider a sequence of alternating projections
\displaystyle(x_{n})
where
\displaystyle x_{2n}\in A
and
\displaystyle x_{2n+1}\in B
(
\displaystyle n\geq 0
).

(i)

If
\displaystyle\beta<1
and
\displaystyle d>0
, then
\displaystyle(x_{n})
attains the minimum distance in at most
\displaystyle 2N+1
steps, where

N:=\left\lfloor\log_{1-\alpha^{2}}\left(\frac{d(1-\beta)}{\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|-\beta d}\right)\right\rfloor.

(14)

(ii)

If
\displaystyle d=0
or
\displaystyle\beta=1
, then the sequence
\displaystyle(x_{n})
converges linearly with rate
\displaystyle\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}
.

Proof.

Assume that Condition 2 holds for
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle\beta\in[0,1]
.
If
\displaystyle d(x_{2n+1},A)>d(A,B)
, then Condition 2 ensures that we can apply Theorem
2
with
\displaystyle x_{2n}\in A
and
\displaystyle x_{2n+1}\in B
to yield

\displaystyle\displaystyle d<d(x_{2n+1},A)\leq(1-\alpha^{2})\left\|x_{2n+1}-x_{2n}\right\|+\alpha^{2}\beta d.

(15)

We now prove by induction that, whenever
\displaystyle d(x_{2n+1},A)>d(A,B)
(
\displaystyle n\geq 0
),

d<d(x_{2n+1},A)\leq(1-\alpha^{2})^{n+1}\left(\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|-\beta d\right)+\beta d.

(16)

This is easily proved in the base step by substituting
\displaystyle n=0
into (
15
).
For the inductive step, we assume that (
16
) holds for
\displaystyle n=k\geq 0
, and then if
\displaystyle d(x_{2k+3},A)>d(A,B)
, using (
15
) gives

\displaystyle\displaystyle d(x_{2k+3},A)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha^{2})\left\|x_{2k+3}-x_{2k+2}\right\|+\alpha^{2}\beta d

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha^{2})\left\|x_{2k+2}-x_{2k+1}\right\|+\alpha^{2}\beta d

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(1-\alpha^{2})d(x_{2k+1},A)+\alpha^{2}\beta d

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha^{2})^{k+2}\left(\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|-\beta d\right)+(1-\alpha^{2})\beta d

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\alpha^{2}\beta d

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(1-\alpha^{2})^{k+2}\left(\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|-\beta d\right)+\beta d,

which proves (
16
) for
\displaystyle n=k+1
, and hence (
16
) holds for all
\displaystyle n\geq 0
. We now consider two cases:

1.

\displaystyle\beta<1
and
\displaystyle d>0
; and

2.

\displaystyle\beta=1
or
\displaystyle d=0
.

For case 1, if the alternating projections have not converged in
\displaystyle 2n+1
iterations (
\displaystyle d(x_{2n+1},A)>d
), then by (
16
),

\displaystyle\displaystyle n<\log_{1-\alpha^{2}}\left(\frac{d(1-\beta)}{\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|-\beta d}\right)-1<N.

Hence, convergence must have occurred after
\displaystyle 2N+1
iterations, proving part (i).

For case 2, we have from (
16
) that while convergence has not occurred, for odd integers
\displaystyle n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=d(x_{n},A)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left(\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}\right)^{n+1}(\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|-d)+d

\displaystyle\displaystyle<\left(\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}\right)^{n}(\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|-d)+d,

(17)

and for even integers
\displaystyle n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=d(x_{n},B)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\left\|x_{n}-x_{n-1}\right\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=d(x_{n-1},A)

\displaystyle\displaystyle<\left(\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}\right)^{n}(\left\|x_{1}-x_{0}\right\|-d)+d.

(18)

Inequalities (
17
) and (
18
) show that
\displaystyle\left\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\|
converges to
\displaystyle d
linearly with rate
\displaystyle\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}
.
∎

Remark 16
.

In part (i) of Theorem
15
, the neighbourhood radius
\displaystyle\delta
in Condition 2 is always larger than the constant
\displaystyle\alpha(1-\beta)d(A,B)>0
, and in this case finite convergence is guaranteed. In part (ii),
\displaystyle\delta\to 0
as
\displaystyle\left\|x-y\right\|\to d(A,B)
and hence the neighbourhoods become arbitrarily small, and the theorem only gives convergence in the limit. We suspect that it may be possible to derive stronger convergence rates by using different expressions for the radius
\displaystyle\delta
in Condition 2.

When
\displaystyle d(A,B)=0
, Theorem
15
provides a new sufficient condition for linear convergence in the consistent case. Condition 2 is weaker than intrinsic transversality as it only takes into account the normal cones for one of the sets. This shows that intrinsic transversality is not a necessary condition for linear convergence of alternating projections in general nonconvex settings.

4
Special case: Polyhedron and closed half space

We now apply the results in Section
3
to the special case where the two sets are a polyhedron and a closed half space.

Proposition 17
.

Consider two non-intersecting closed subsets
\displaystyle A,B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
\displaystyle n\geq 1
) defined by

A:=\{x:\left\langle c,x\right\rangle\leq M\},\and B:=\{x:\mathbb{A}x\leq b\},

where
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}
is a
\displaystyle m\times n
matrix with rows
\displaystyle a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
\displaystyle i=1,\ldots,m
),
\displaystyle b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\displaystyle c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
\displaystyle M\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then, the pair
\displaystyle\{A,B\}
satisfies Condition 2 with
\displaystyle\beta=0
and

\alpha:=\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{1,\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,m;\\
\left\langle a_{i},c\right\rangle>-\left\|a_{i}\right\|\left\|c\right\|\end{subarray}}d\left(\frac{a_{i}}{\left\|a_{i}\right\|},\mathbb{R}_{+}(-c)\right)\right\},

(19)

with the convention
\displaystyle\min\emptyset=+\infty
.

Proof.

Take
\displaystyle x\in A
and
\displaystyle y\in P_{B}(x)
such that
\displaystyle d(y,A)>d(A,B)
and let
\displaystyle x^{\prime}\in P_{A}(y)
.
Then,
\displaystyle x-y\in N_{B}(y)
and
\displaystyle y-x^{\prime}\in N_{A}(x^{\prime})=\mathbb{R}_{+}(c)
. Furthermore, since
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle B
are closed convex sets and
\displaystyle y-x^{\prime}\in N_{A}(x^{\prime})
, we must have
\displaystyle x^{\prime}-y\notin N_{B}(y)
, since otherwise by Proposition
3

\displaystyle d(y,A)=d(A,B)
, which is a contradiction.
Therefore,
\displaystyle-c\notin N_{B}(y)
, or equivalently,

\left\langle c,v\right\rangle>-\left\|c\right\|\cdot\left\|v\right\|,\quad\text{for all }v\in N_{B}(y)\setminus\{0\},

(20)

since for vectors
\displaystyle u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|=\left\|v\right\|=1
, we have
\displaystyle\left\langle u,v\right\rangle=-1
if and only if
\displaystyle u=-v
.
By
[
20
, Theorem 6.46]
, we have

N_{B}(y)={\rm cone}\,\{a_{i}:\;i\in I(y)\},

where
\displaystyle I(y):=\{i:\,\left\langle a_{i},y\right\rangle=b_{i}\}
. Note that if
\displaystyle i\in I(y)
, then (
20
) implies
\displaystyle\left\langle a_{i},c\right\rangle>-\left\|a_{i}\right\|\cdot\left\|c\right\|
.
Hence,

x-y\in N_{B}(y)\subset{\rm cone}\,\{a_{i}:\;\left\langle a_{i},c\right\rangle>-\left\|a_{i}\right\|\cdot\left\|c\right\|\}=:S,

and from (
19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle d\left(\frac{y-x}{\left\|x-y\right\|},\mathbb{R}_{+}(c)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=d\left(\frac{x-y}{\left\|x-y\right\|},\mathbb{R}_{+}(-c)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\inf\left\{d\left(\frac{v}{\left\|v\right\|},\mathbb{R}_{+}(-c)\right):v\in S\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,m;\\
\left\langle a_{i},c\right\rangle>-\left\|a_{i}\right\|\left\|c\right\|\end{subarray}}d\left(\frac{a_{i}}{\left\|a_{i}\right\|},\mathbb{R}_{+}(-c)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq 2\alpha.

Set
\displaystyle\delta:=\alpha\left\|x-y\right\|
,
\displaystyle\rho:=\left\|x-y\right\|
. Take
\displaystyle z\in A\cap B_{\delta}(x)\cap B_{\rho}(y)
.
Observe that either
\displaystyle z\in{\rm int}\,A
and
\displaystyle N_{A}(z)=\{0\}
or
\displaystyle z\in{\rm bd}\,A
and
\displaystyle N_{A}(z)=\mathbb{R}_{+}(c)
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\displaystyle d\left(\frac{y-z}{\left\|y-z\right\|},N_{A}(z)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left\|y-x\right\|}{\left\|y-z\right\|}d\left(\frac{y-z}{\left\|y-x\right\|},\mathbb{R}_{+}(c)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq d\left(\frac{y-x+x-z}{\left\|y-x\right\|},\mathbb{R}_{+}(c)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq d\left(\frac{y-x}{\left\|y-x\right\|},\mathbb{R}_{+}(c)\right)-\frac{\left\|x-z\right\|}{\left\|y-x\right\|}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq 2\alpha-\alpha=\alpha.

Hence, the pair
\displaystyle\{A,B\}
satisfies Condition 2 with
\displaystyle\beta=0
and
\displaystyle\alpha
defined by (
19
).

Note that the above derivations assume
\displaystyle S\neq\emptyset
, since otherwise no such
\displaystyle x\in A
and
\displaystyle y\in P_{A}(x)
with
\displaystyle d(y,A)>d(A,B)
exist, and Condition 2 is redundant.
∎

Corollary 18
.

Let
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle B
be as defined in Proposition
17
with
\displaystyle d(A,B)>0
and let
\displaystyle\alpha
be defined by (
19
). Then, the method of alternating projections, initiated at
\displaystyle x_{0}\in A
, attains the minimum distance in at most
\displaystyle 2N+1
steps, where

N:=\left\lfloor\log_{1-\alpha^{2}}\left(\frac{d(A,B)}{d(x_{0},B)}\right)\right\rfloor.

Furthermore, if
\displaystyle d(x_{0},B)<\frac{d(A,B)}{1-\alpha^{2}}
, then the minimum distance is attained after one step.

Proof.

Observe that the constant
\displaystyle\alpha
given in (
19
) is always in
\displaystyle(0,1)
, since
\displaystyle a_{i}\notin\mathbb{R}_{+}(-c)
for
\displaystyle a_{i}\in S
, and hence

0<d(a_{i}/\left\|a_{i}\right\|,\mathbb{R}_{+}(-c))\leq 1,\quad\text{ for each }a_{i}\in S,

where
\displaystyle S
is as defined in the proof of Proposition
17
.

By Proposition
17
and Theorem
15
, we conclude that the alternating projections converge after
\displaystyle 2N+1
steps with

N=\left\lfloor\log_{1-\alpha^{2}}\left(\frac{d(A,B)}{d(x_{0},B)}\right)\right\rfloor.

When
\displaystyle d(x_{0},B)<\frac{d(A,B)}{1-\alpha^{2}}
, we have
\displaystyle N=0
and the method converges after one step.
∎

Remark 19
.

(i)

Consider two closed convex sets
\displaystyle A
,
\displaystyle B
and let
\displaystyle a\in A
,
\displaystyle b\in B
such that
\displaystyle\left\|a-b\right\|=d(A,B)>0
. Then by the definition of normal cone,
\displaystyle b-a\in N_{A}(a)
and
\displaystyle a-b\in N_{B}(b)
. If we shift
\displaystyle A
by a vector
\displaystyle v:=\lambda(b-a)
with
\displaystyle\lambda>0
, then
\displaystyle b-(a-v)\in N_{A}(a)=N_{A-v}(a-v)
and
\displaystyle a-v-b\in N_{B}(b)
. Hence, by Proposition
3
, we have
\displaystyle\left\|(a-v)-b\right\|=d(A-v,B)
. This result is used in the next remark to determine by how much the closed half space needs to be shifted to ensure one-step convergence.

(ii)

Consider two sets
\displaystyle A,B
as defined in Proposition
17
, and
\displaystyle x_{0}\in A
. Let
\displaystyle a\in A
and
\displaystyle b\in B
with
\displaystyle\left\|a-b\right\|=d(A,B)
, and then
\displaystyle c=\lambda(b-a)
for some
\displaystyle\lambda>0
, since
\displaystyle b-a\in N_{A}(a)=\mathbb{R}_{+}(c)
. To ensure one-step convergence, we can shift
\displaystyle A
by a vector
\displaystyle v:=\mu c
, where
\displaystyle\mu>0
and
\displaystyle\mu>\tfrac{1}{\alpha^{2}\left\|c\right\|}\left((1-\alpha^{2})d(x_{0},B)-d(A,B)\right)
. Then by part (i) above,
\displaystyle d(A-v,B)=d(A,B)+\left\|v\right\|=d(A,B)+\mu\left\|c\right\|
. From the choice of
\displaystyle\mu
, we have

\mu\left\|c\right\|+d(x_{0},B)<\frac{d(A,B)+\mu\left\|c\right\|}{1-\alpha^{2}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle d(x_{0}-v,B)\leq d(x_{0},B)+\left\|v\right\|=d(x_{0},B)+\mu\left\|c\right\|<\frac{d(A,B)+\mu\left\|c\right\|}{1-\alpha^{2}}=\frac{d(A-v,B)}{1-\alpha^{2}}
. By Corollary
18
, the alternating projections for
\displaystyle A-v
and
\displaystyle B
, starting from
\displaystyle x_{0}-v
, converge after one step.

We now propose a projection method for solving linear programming problems of the form (LP):
\displaystyle\min_{x:\,\mathbb{A}x\leq b}\left\langle c,x\right\rangle
,
where
\displaystyle c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}
is a matrix. We assume that LP is bounded with
\displaystyle M
as a lower bound that is strictly less than the optimal value. Set

\displaystyle\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left\langle c,x\right\rangle\leq M\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathbb{A}x\leq b\}.

A solution of LP is obtained by applying iteratively the alternating projections
\displaystyle P_{B}(x_{2n})
,
\displaystyle P_{A}(x_{2n+1})
until the minimum distance is attained. By Remark
19
(ii),
the projection of
\displaystyle x_{0}-\mu c
with
\displaystyle\mu>\frac{(1-\alpha^{2})}{\alpha^{2}\left\|c\right\|}d(x_{0},B)
onto
\displaystyle B
is a solution of LP.

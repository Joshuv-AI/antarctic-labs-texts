Linear maps preserve the covariance sets
Mohammad Hossein Alizadeh

Abstract




Abstract.
Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are
C^{{\huge\ast}}
-algebras
.
A

linear map
\phi:\mathcal{A\rightarrow B}
is

C^{\ast}
-Jordan homomorphism if it is a Jordan homomorphism which preserves
the adjoint operation. In this note we show that
C^{\ast}
-Jordan
homomorphisms -under mild assumptions- preserving covariance set and
covariance coset in
C^{\ast}
-algebras.

Keywords:
Moore-Penrose inverse; covariance set; Jordan homomorphism.

Mathematics Subject Classification :
47A05; 15A09; 46H05.

1
Introduction and preliminaries

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
is a
C^{\ast}
-algebra with identity
1
. An
element
a\in\mathcal{A}
is called
regular
if it has a generalized
inverse in
\mathcal{A}
, i.e. there exists
b\in\mathcal{A}
such that

aba=a.

We say that an element
a\in\mathcal{A}
is Moore-Penrose invertible
if there exists
b\in\mathcal{A}
such that

aba=a,\ \ \ bab=b,\ \ \ \left(ab\right)^{\ast}=ab\ \ \ \text{and
\ \ }\left(ba\right)^{\ast}=ba.

It is well known that the Moore-Penrose inverse (briefly, MP–inverse) is
unique if it exists. We reserve the notation
a^{{\dagger}}
for the MP–inverse of

a.
In what follows, we will denote by
\mathcal{A}^{-1}
the subset of
invertible elements of
\mathcal{A}
and by
\mathcal{A}^{{\dagger}}
, the set of
all MP–invertible elements of
\mathcal{A}
. The
commutator
of a
pair of elements
x
and
y
in
\mathcal{A}
is given by

\left[x,y\right]=xy-yx.

Note that
\left[x,y\right]=0
if and only if
x
and
y
commute.

In the next section we need the following definition of covariance set which
was studied in
[
2
]

Definition 1

[
2
]
For a given element
a\in\mathcal{A}^{{\dagger}}
with MP–inverse

a^{{\dagger}}
we will denote the covariance set by
\mathfrak{C}(a)

and define;

\mathfrak{C}(a)=\{b\in\mathcal{A}^{-1}:(bab^{-1})^{{\dagger}}=ba^{{\dagger}}b^{-1}\}\text{.}

(1)

Also the notion of covariance coset was introduced and studied in

[
1
]
. In fact, this set is defined by reversing the roles of
a

and
b
in
\mathfrak{C}(a)
and is denoted by
\mathfrak{B}\left(b\right)
. i.e.,

\mathfrak{B}\left(b\right)=\left\{a\in\mathfrak{A}^{{\dagger}}:(bab^{-1})^{{\dagger}}=ba^{{\dagger}}b^{-1}\right\}.

(2)

The propose of this work is to show that under weak assumptions,
C^{\ast}
-Jordan homomorphisms preserving covariance set and covariance coset in

C^{\ast}
-algebras.

2
Main results

We recall the following definitions and theorems which will be needed to prove
some of our results.

Definition 2

[
3
]
We say that a
C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
is of real rank
zero if the set formed by all the real linear combinations of (orthogonal)
projections is dense in the set of self-adjoint elements of
\mathcal{A}
.

Remark 3

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are
C^{\ast}
-algebras. It is well known that (see
[
3
]
) the property of the
above definition is satisfied by every von Neumann algebra, and in particular
by the
C^{\ast}
-algebra
B(H)
of all bounded linear operators on a Hilbert
space
H
, and by the Calkin algebra
C(H)=\frac{B(H)}{K(H)}
.

Definition 4

We say that a linear map
\phi:\mathcal{A\rightarrow B}
is
C^{\ast}
-Jordan
homomorphism if it is a, Jordan homomorphism which preserves the adjoint
operation, i.e.

\phi\left(x^{\ast}\right)=\left(\phi\left(x\right)\right)^{\ast}\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{A}\text{.}

The
C^{\ast}
-homomorphism and
C^{\ast}
-anti-homomorphism are analogously defined.

In 2012, Boudi and Mbekhta
[
3
]
proved the following theorem.

Theorem 5

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
C^{\ast}
-algebra of real rank zero and

\mathcal{B}
a prime
C^{\ast}
-algebra. Let
\phi:\mathcal{A\rightarrow B}

be a surjective, unital linear map. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

1)
\phi(x^{{\dagger}})=\left(\phi(x)\right)^{{\dagger}}
for all
x\in A^{{\dagger}};

2)
\phi
is either a
C^{\ast}
-homomorphism or a
C^{\ast}
-anti-homomorphism.

Proof.
See
[
3
, Theorem 3.3]
.

The next proposition describes a relation between the covariance set

\mathfrak{C}(a),
and commutators. It was proved in
[
2
]
.

Proposition 6

Let
a

\in

\mathcal{A}^{{\dagger}}
with MP–inverse
a^{{\dagger}}.

Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)
b\in\mathfrak{C}(a);

(ii)
[b^{\ast}b,aa^{{\dagger}}]=0
and
[b^{\ast}b,a^{{\dagger}}a]=0
.

A similar result also is true for covariance coset:

Proposition 7

[
1
]
Assume
b\in\mathfrak{A}^{-1}
. Then the
following statements are equivalent:

(i)
a\in\mathfrak{B}\left(b\right);

(ii)
\left[a^{{\dagger}}a,b^{\ast}b\right]=0
and
\left[aa^{{\dagger}},b^{\ast}b\right]=0.

Now we are going to prove the main result.

Theorem 8

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
C^{\ast}
-algebra of real rank zero and

\mathcal{B}
a prime
C^{\ast}
-algebra. Let
\phi:\mathcal{A\rightarrow B}

be a surjective, unital linear map. If
\phi(x^{{\dagger}})=\left(\phi(x)\right)^{{\dagger}}
for all
x\in A^{{\dagger}},
then
\phi\left(\mathfrak{C}(a)\right)=

\mathfrak{C}(\phi\left(a\right))
and
\phi\left(\mathfrak{B}(a)\right)=

\mathfrak{B}(\phi\left(a\right)).

Proof.
By Theorem
5
,
\phi
is either a
C^{\ast}
-homomorphism or a

C^{\ast}
-anti-homomorphism. First we assume that
\phi
is a
C^{\ast}
-homomorphism. Let
b\in\mathfrak{C}(a)
. By Proposition
6

b^{\ast}baa^{{\dagger}}=aa^{{\dagger}}b^{\ast}b,\qquad b^{\ast}ba^{{\dagger}}a=a^{{\dagger}}ab^{\ast}b

(3)

Since
\phi
is a
C^{\ast}
-homomorphism, from (
3
) we get

\displaystyle\phi\left(b\right)^{\ast}\phi\left(b\right)\phi\left(a\right)\phi\left(a\right)^{{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=\phi\left(a\right)\phi\left(a\right)^{{\dagger}}\phi\left(b\right)^{\ast}\phi\left(b\right),

\displaystyle\phi\left(b\right)^{\ast}\phi\left(b\right)\phi\left(a\right)^{{\dagger}}\phi\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=\phi\left(a\right)^{{\dagger}}\phi\left(a\right)\phi\left(b\right)^{\ast}\phi\left(b\right)

which means that
\phi\left(b\right)\in\phi\left(\mathfrak{C}(a)\right)
i.e.
\phi\left(\mathfrak{C}(a)\right)\subset\mathfrak{C}(\phi\left(a\right))
. Since
\phi
is surjective we get
\phi\left(\mathfrak{C}(a)\right)=\mathfrak{C}(\phi\left(a\right)).

Now we suppose that
\phi
is a
C^{\ast}
-anti-homomorphism. Let

b\in\mathfrak{C}(a)
. Again by Proposition
6
we have (
3
).
Applying
\phi
on (
3
) we get

\phi\left(aa^{{\dagger}}\right)\phi\left(b^{\ast}b\right)=\phi\left(b^{\ast}b\right)\phi\left(aa^{{\dagger}}\right),\quad\phi\left(a^{{\dagger}}a\right)\phi\left(b^{\ast}b\right)=\phi\left(b^{\ast}b\right)\phi\left(a^{{\dagger}}a\right).

(4)

Since
\phi
is a
C^{\ast}
-anti-homomorphism and
\phi(x^{{\dagger}})=\left(\phi(x)\right)^{{\dagger}}
from (
4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\phi\left(a)^{{\dagger}}\phi(a\right)\phi\left(b)\phi(b^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=\phi\left(b)\phi(b^{\ast}\right)\phi\left(a)^{{\dagger}}\phi(a\right),

\displaystyle\phi\left(a\right)\phi(a)^{{\dagger}}\phi\left(b)\phi(b^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=\phi\left(b)\phi(b^{\ast}\right)\phi\left(a\right)\phi(a)^{{\dagger}}

Now by using Proposition
6
we conclude that
\phi\left(b\right)\in\phi\left(\mathfrak{C}(a)\right)
i.e.
\phi\left(\mathfrak{C}(a)\right)=\mathfrak{C}(\phi\left(a\right))
.

Applying Proposition
7
, a similar argument shows that

\phi\left(\mathfrak{B}(a)\right)=\mathfrak{B}(\phi\left(a\right))
.

By Theorem
8
and Remark
3
, we deduce the following results.

Corollary 9

Assume that
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are
C^{\ast}
-algebras and also
von Neumann algebras. Let
\phi:\mathcal{A\rightarrow B}
be a surjective,
unital linear map. If
\phi(x^{{\dagger}})=\left(\phi(x)\right)^{{\dagger}}
for all

x\in A^{{\dagger}},
then
\phi\left(\mathfrak{C}(a)\right)=

\mathfrak{C}(\phi\left(a\right))
and
\phi\left(\mathfrak{B}(a)\right)=

\mathfrak{B}(\phi\left(a\right)).

Corollary 10

Suppose that
H
and
K
are Hilbert spaces. Let
\phi:B\left(H\right)\mathcal{\rightarrow}B\left(K\right)
be a surjective linear map. If

\phi(T^{{\dagger}})=\left(\phi(T)\right)^{{\dagger}}
for all
T\in B\left(H\right)^{{\dagger}},
then
\phi\left(\mathfrak{C}(T)\right)=

\mathfrak{C}(\phi\left(T\right))
and
\phi\left(\mathfrak{B}(T)\right)=

\mathfrak{B}(\phi\left(T\right)).

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that a linear map
\phi:\mathcal{A\rightarrow B}
is
n
-
C^{\ast}
-Jordan homomorphism if it is a,
n
-Jordan homomorphism (for more detail see

[
4
]
) which preserves the adjoint operation.

Question
: For which
n\in\mathbb{N},
the above results are true for
n
-
C^{\ast}
-Jordan homomorphism?

In connection with Theorem
8
, we conclude the paper by the following conjecture:

Conjecture 11

Assume that
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are
C^{\ast}
-algebras. Let

\phi:\mathcal{A\rightarrow B}
be a surjective, unital linear map. If

\phi(x^{{\dagger}})=\left(\phi(x)\right)^{{\dagger}}
for all
x\in A^{{\dagger}},
then

\phi\left(\mathfrak{C}(a)\right)=

\mathfrak{C}(\phi\left(a\right))

and
\phi\left(\mathfrak{B}(a)\right)=

\mathfrak{B}(\phi\left(a\right)).

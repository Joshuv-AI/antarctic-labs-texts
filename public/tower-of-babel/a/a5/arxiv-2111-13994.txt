Bressoud’s identities for even moduli. New companions and related positivity results.
Alexander Berkovich

Abstract
. We revisit Bressoud’s generalised Borwein conjecture. Making use of certain positivity-preserving transformations for q q -binomial coefficients, we establish the truth of infinitely many new cases of the Bressoud conjecture. In addition, we prove new doubly-bounded refinement of the Foda-Quano identities. Finally, we discuss new companions to the Bressoud even moduli identities. In particular, all 10 mod 20 10\mod 20 identities are derived.

Introduction and Background

In 1980, Bressoud
[
6
]
discovered even moduli identities:

(1.1)

\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,,n_{v-1}\geq 0}\frac{q^{N_{1}^{2}+\cdots+N^{2}_{v-1}+N_{i}+\cdots+N_{v-1}}}{(q)_{n_{1}}(q)_{n_{2}}\cdots(q)_{n_{v-2}}(q^{2};q^{2})_{n_{v-1}}}=\frac{(q^{2v},q^{i},q^{2v-i};q^{2v})_{\infty}}{(q)_{\infty}}

with
v\geq 2
,
1\leq i\leq v
,
|q|<1
and
N_{i}=\sum_{j=i}^{v-1}n_{j}
.
In (
1.1
) we use the standard notations

(a;q)_{n}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{j}),

(a;q)_{-n}=(\frac{a}{q};\frac{1}{q})_{n}^{-1},

(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{j\geq 0}(1-aq^{j}),

(q)_{n}=(q;q)_{n},

(q)_{\infty}=(q;q)_{\infty},

(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k};q)_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(a_{i};q)_{n},

where
n\geq 0
.

We remark that case
i=v
was not considered in
[
6
]
. The identities in (
1.1
) are closely related to
the celebrated Andrews-Gordon identities
[
1
]
:

\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,,n_{v-1}\geq 0}\frac{q^{N_{1}^{2}+\cdots+N^{2}_{v-1}+N_{i}+\cdots+N_{v-1}}}{(q)_{n_{1}}(q)_{n_{2}}\cdots(q)_{n_{v-1}}}=\frac{(q^{2v+1},q^{i},q^{2v+1-i};q^{2v+1})_{\infty}}{(q)_{\infty}},

with
v\geq 2
,
1\leq i\leq v
.

In 1995, O. Foda, Y.H. Quano
[
10
]
found polynomial refinement of (
1.1
)

(1.2)

\begin{split}&\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,,n_{v-1}\geq 0}q^{N_{1}^{2}+\cdots+N^{2}_{v-1}+N_{i}+\cdots+N_{v-1}}\\
&{L-\sum_{t=1}^{v-2}N_{t}\brack n_{v-1}}_{q^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{v-2}{n_{j}+2L-2\sum_{t=1}^{j}N_{t}+\min(v-i,v-1-j)\brack n_{j}}_{q}=\\
&\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{j(vj+v-i)}{2L+v-i\brack L-vj}_{q},\end{split}

with
1\leq i\leq v
. For
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
the
q
-binomial coefficient is defined as

{n+m\brack n}_{q}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{(q)_{n+m}}{(q)_{n}(q)_{m}},&\text{ if }m,n\geq 0\\
0,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

It is well known
[
2
]
that

(1.3)

{n+m\brack n}_{q}\geq 0.

Here and everywhere

P(q)\geq 0,

means that a polynomial in
q
,
P(q)
, has non-negative coefficients.

Obviously, (
1.2
), (
1.3
) imply that for
v\geq 1
and
i=1,\ldots,v

(1.4)

G(L,L+v-i,2-\frac{i}{v},\frac{i}{v},v,q)\geq 0,

where

G(N,M,\alpha,\beta,K,q)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{Kj\frac{(\alpha+\beta)j+(\alpha-\beta)}{2}}{N+M\brack N-Kj}_{q}.

Note that (
1.4
) is consistent with the Bressoud positivity conjecture
[
8
]
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

(Bressoud)
Let
K\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
N,M,\alpha K,\beta K\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(1.5)

1\leq\alpha+\beta\leq 2K-1,

(1.6)

\beta-K\leq N-M\leq K-\alpha,

(strict inequality when
K=2
),

then

(1.7)

G(N,M,\alpha,\beta,K,q)\geq 0.

We remark that when
\alpha,\beta
are integers, (
1.7
) becomes a theorem in
[
3
]
.
Many cases of Conjecture
1.1
were settled in the literature
[

,
5
,
7
,

,
14
,
15
,

]

In Section 2, we will show that (

) implies

.

,

Hence, (

For

As

2.
Positivity-preserving Transformations. Proof of Theorem
1.2

We start with the following summation formula
[
4
]

Theorem 2.1
.

For
L\in\mathbb{N}
,
a\in\mathbb{Z}

(2.1)

\sum_{k\geq 0}C_{L,k}(q){k\brack\lfloor\frac{k-a}{2}\rfloor}_{q}=q^{T(a)}{2L+1\brack L-a}_{q},

where

T(j):=\frac{(j+1)j}{2}

and

(2.2)

C_{L,k}(q)=\sum_{m=0}^{L}q^{T(m)+T(m+k)}{L\choose m,k}_{q}.

Observe that
C_{L,k}(q)\geq 0
.
Using transformation (
2.1
) it is easy to check that identity of the form

(2.3)

F_{c}(L,q)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha(j){L\brack\lfloor\frac{L-j}{2}\rfloor}_{q},

implies that

(2.4)

\sum_{k\geq 0}C_{L,k}(q)F_{c}(k,q)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha(j)q^{T(j)}{2L+1\brack L-j}_{q}.

Hence, if
F_{c}(L,q)\geq 0
then

(2.5)

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha(j)q^{T(j)}{2L+1\brack L-j}_{q}\geq 0.

For that reason, we say that (
2.1
) is positivity-preserving.

Letting
L\rightarrow\infty
in (
2.4
) we derive

(2.6)

\sum_{m,k\geq 0}\frac{q^{T(m)+T(m+k)}}{(q)_{m}(q)_{k}}F_{c}(k,q)=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha(j)q^{T(j)}.

Theorem
2.1
is closely related to the Warnaar transformation (Corollary 2.6 in
[
16
]
).

Theorem 2.2
.

For
L\in\mathbb{N}
,
a\in\mathbb{Z}

(2.7)

\sum_{k\geq 0}W_{L,k}(q){2k\brack k-a}_{q}=q^{2a^{2}}{2L\brack L-2a}_{q},

where

(2.8)

W_{L,k}(q)=\sum_{m=0}^{L}q^{(m+k)^{2}+k^{2}}{L\choose m,2k}_{q}\geq 0.

Using transformation (
2.7
) it is easy to check that identity of the form

(2.9)

F_{w}(L,q)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha(j){2L\brack L-j}_{q},

implies

(2.10)

\sum_{k\geq 0}W_{L,k}(q)F_{w}(k,q)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha(j)q^{2j^{2}}{2L\brack L-2j}_{q}.

Hence, if
F_{w}(L,q)\geq 0
, then

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha(j)q^{2j^{2}}{2L\brack L-2j}_{q}\geq 0.

Letting
L\rightarrow\infty
in (
2.10
), we derive

(2.11)

\sum_{m,k\geq 0}\frac{q^{(m+k)^{2}+k^{2}}}{(q)_{m}(q)_{2k}}F_{w}(k,q)=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha(j)q^{2j^{2}}.

Observe that unlike (
2.7
), transformation (
2.1
) can not be iterated. Interestingly enough, there exists an
odd

companion to Theorem
2.2
, discussed in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
L\in\mathbb{N}
,
a\in\mathbb{Z}

(2.12)

\sum_{k\geq 0}O_{L,k}(q){2k+1\brack k-a}_{q}=q^{4T(a)}{2L\brack L-2a-1}_{q},

where

(2.13)

O_{L,k}(q)=\sum_{m=0}^{L}q^{2T(m+k)+2T(k)}{L\choose m,2k+1}_{q}\geq 0.

Using (
2.12
) it is easy to check that identity of the form

F_{o}(L,q)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha(j){2L+1\brack L-j}_{q}

implies that

(2.14)

\sum_{k\geq 0}O_{L,k}(q)F_{o}(k,q)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha(j)q^{2j^{2}+2j}{2L\brack L-2j-1}_{q}.

Hence, if
F_{o}(L,q)\geq 0
, then

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha(j)q^{2j^{2}+2j}{2L\brack L-2j-1}_{q}\geq 0.

Letting
L\rightarrow\infty
in (
2.14
), we obtain

(2.15)

\sum_{m,k\geq 0}\frac{q^{2T(m+k)+2T(k)}}{(q)_{m}(q)_{2k+1}}F_{o}(k,q)=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\alpha(j)q^{2j^{2}+2j}.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we replace

in (

)
where

. We then apply Theorem

.
After simplification we derive (

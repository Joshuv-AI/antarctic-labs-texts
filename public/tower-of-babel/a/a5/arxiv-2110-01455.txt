Symmetries and conformal bridge in Schwarschild-(A)dS black hole mechanics
Jibril Ben Achour, Etera R. Livine

Abstract
We show that the Schwarzschild-(A)dS black hole mechanics possesses a hidden symmetry under the three-dimensional Poincaré group. This symmetry shows up after having gauge-fixed the diffeomorphism invariance in the symmetry-reduced homogeneous Einstein- Λ \Lambda model and stands as a physical symmetry of the system. It dictates the geometry both in the black hole interior and exterior regions, as well as beyond the cosmological horizon in the Schwarzschild-dS case. It follows that one can associate a set of non-trivial conserved charges to the Schwarzschild-(A)dS black hole which act in each causally disconnected regions. In T-regions, they act on fields living on spacelike hypersurface of constant time, while in the R-region, they act on time-like hypersurface of constant radius. We find that while the expression of the charges depend explicitly on the location of the hypersurface, the charge algebra remains the same at any radius in R-regions (or time in T-regions). Finally, the analysis of the Casimirs of the charge algebra reveals a new solution-generating map. The 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) {\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R}) Casimir is shown to generate a one-parameter family of deformation of the black hole geometry labelled by the cosmological constant. This gives rise to a new conformal bridge allowing one to continuously deform the Schwarzschild-AdS geometry to the Schwarzschild and the Schwarzschild-dS solutions.



Introduction

Symmetries play a fundamental role in our description of physical systems. For Lagrangian field theories, Noether’s theorems provide a one-to-one relation between conservation laws and symmetries of the action. At the classical level, the algebra of charges generating these symmetries constrains the structure of the solution space while at the quantum level, its irreducible representations organize the quantum theory. The conserved charges thereby identified provide a rich information on the IR sector of the theory through their algebra.

Nevertheless, identifying non-trivial charges is a subtle subject in gravity. Indeed, it is well known that any diffeomorphism stands as a pure gauge transformation associated to a trivial charge which vanishes identically in the absence of boundary. In order to associate an algebra of non-trivial charges to a gravitational field, one has to introduce a notion of boundary, being at finite distance or asymptotic. The presence of such boundary breaks the diffeomorphism gauge invariance, giving rise to non-trivial symmetries associated, via Noether’s second theorem, to non-trivial surface charges

In the context of symmetry reduced GR, the case of homogeneous models such as cosmological spacetime reveals some surprising features. For such models, the restriction to homogeneity effectively reduces the gravitational field to a mechanical system and the diffeomorphism gauge invariance of the full theory collapses to a simple time-reparametrization gauge invariance. It was noticed that, even once this gauge is fixed, the system exhibits new symmetries associated to non-trivial charges which fully encode the gravitational dynamics

In such mechanical set-up, the presence of a boundary translates into suitable cut-off scales fixing the size of the system under consideration, but its presence does not seem to play any role in the emergence of these non-trivial symmetries. Therefore, the status of these mechanical symmetries remain puzzling and begs for further exploration. The goal of this work is to discuss the realization of such hidden symmetries for the Schwarzschild-(A)dS black hole mechanics. In order to appreciate the present results, it will be useful to first review previous works on this subject.

Finally, we investigate the action of the symmetry at the level of the solution space. We also present the explicit action of the conformal bridge connecting the Schwarzschild black hole geometry and its (A)dS extension. Schwarzschild-(A)dS mechanics

In this section, we introduce the set of geometries we shall investigate throughout this work. We present the symmetry reduced action describing (A)dS mechanics and present the hidden symmetries of the gauge-fixed action.

The model

Consider a spherically symmetric geometry with ADM line element

Symmetries of the gauge-fixed action

In this section, we present the hidden symmetries of Schwarzschild-(A)dS mechanics. We introduce for convenience the dimensionless quantity
\kappa=L_{0}L^{2}_{s}/L^{3}_{P}
which encodes the ratio between the effective size of the system, i.e the IR cut-off, and the Planck volume, i.e the UV cut-off our our system.

Now, consider the symmetry reduced action (
2.6
) and let us fix the gauge by reabsorbing the lapse field through the
\tau
-coordinate defined by
\textrm{d}\tau=\mathcal{N}\textrm{d}\eta
.
The gauge-fixed action reads

\displaystyle S_{\epsilon}[A,B]=\frac{\kappa}{\epsilon}\int\textrm{d}\tau\left[\epsilon\left(\frac{1}{L^{2}_{s}}-\frac{B^{2}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\right)-A^{2}\dot{B}^{2}-2AB\dot{B}\dot{A}\right]

(2.13)

Let us make two important remarks at this stage.
First, we started from an action principle
S[\eta,\mathcal{N}(\eta),A(\eta),B(\eta)]
, gauge invariant under time reparametrizations, to a gauge-fixed action
S[\tau,A(\tau),B(\tau)]
where the lapse
\mathcal{N}
has been re-absorbed in the
\tau
-coordinate. The equation of motion of the original gauge-invariant action with respect to the variation of the lapse
\mathcal{N}
imply that its Hamiltonian is of the form
H_{\eta}[\mathcal{N},A,B]=\mathcal{N}{\cal H}[A,B]
and therefore vanishes on-shell, i.e. that we have a Hamiltonian constraint
{\cal H}=0
. If one starts directly from the gauge-fixed action
S[\tau,A(\tau),B(\tau)]
, without remembering where it comes from, one computes a Hamiltonian
H_{\tau}[A,B]={\cal H}[A,B]
, which does not necessarily vanishes. One should nevertheless remember that there is an implicit dependence on the lapse field
\mathcal{N}
hidden in the
\tau
-coordinate, through its definition
\textrm{d}\tau=\mathcal{N}\textrm{d}\eta
, and that this leads to a non-trivial equation of motion with respect to lapse variations
\delta\mathcal{N}
. In simpler words, the classical solutions for the gauge-fixed action
S[\tau,A(\tau),B(\tau)]
are classical solutions for the gauge-invariant action principle
S[\eta,\mathcal{N}(\eta),A(\eta),B(\eta)]
when and only when the Hamiltonian vanishes
H_{\tau}[A,B]={\cal H}[A,B]=0
.

The second point concerns the status of the constant term
\varepsilon L_{s}^{-2}
in the gauge-fixed action
S[\tau,A(\tau),B(\tau)]
. Constant terms are irrelevant for the equations of motion, so we could simply remove it from the Lagrangian. However, one should keep in mind that it is actually a term
\varepsilon L_{s}^{-2}\,\textrm{d}\tau
, with some implicit dependence on the lapse. Indeed it actually comes from a term
\mathcal{N}\varepsilon L_{s}^{-2}\,\textrm{d}\eta
in the original gauge-invariant action. Such a term is clearly not constant and affects the equation of motion derived from lapse variations. In fact, it shifts the Hamiltonian constraint, from
H_{\eta}[\mathcal{N},A,B]=\mathcal{N}{\cal H}[A,B]
to
H_{\eta}[\mathcal{N},A,B]=\mathcal{N}\,({\cal H}[A,B]-\varepsilon L_{s})
. Thus, the role of the apparently constant term
\varepsilon L_{s}^{-2}
is essential: it changes the value of the Hamiltonian
H_{\tau}
of the gauge-fixed system.

(2.26)

Therefore, for
h(\tau):=\delta+\gamma\tau+\beta\tau^{2}
where
(\delta,\gamma,\beta)
are real constants, this variation reduces to a total derivative and one obtains a second symmetry of the action. Moreover, choosing instead the function
h(\tau)
such that
\dddot{h}
is a constant, the above transformation is not a symmetry but instead generates a new term in the gauge-fixed action which shifts the value of the cosmological constant via the second term in (
2.26
). This reveals the existence of a solution-generating map. We shall discuss this point in detail at the end of Section
3.5
. Coming back the the symmetry transformation with
\dddot{h}=0
, the Noether charges generating the infinitesimal version of this symmetry are easily computed and read

\displaystyle T_{+}^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\kappa L_{P}\dot{B}^{2}

(2.27)

\displaystyle T_{0}^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\kappa L_{P}\left\{\tau\dot{B}^{2}-B\dot{B}\right\}

(2.28)

\displaystyle T_{-}^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\kappa L_{P}\left\{\tau^{2}\dot{B}^{2}-2\tau B\dot{B}+B^{2}\right\}

(2.29)

They are not affected by the presence of the cosmological constant and therefore coincide with the ones found in
[
28
]
for the Schwarzschild black hole. However, with our new parametrization, the above expressions for the translational charges corresponds both to the interior and the exterior regions of the black hole. The conservation of these charges is easily obtained. See (
B.24
) in Appendix
B
. This second set of charges generate the translation sector of the symmetry group. It follows from this section that the Schwarzschild-(A)dS black mechanics possesses a set of hidden symmetries under the three dimensional Poincaré group SL
(2,\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Poincaré structure of Schwarzschild-(A)dS mechanics

In this section, we show that the symmetry identified above translates at the phase space level in the so called extended CVH algebra. We further show that the Noether charges computed in Section
2.2
form as expected an
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{3}
algebra which upgrades the extended CVH algebra to a
\tau
-dependent structure. We conclude this section by the analysis of the Casimirs of the system and identify the
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})
Casimir as generating a one-parameter family of deformation of the dynamics labelled by the cosmological constant.

Hamiltonian formulation and physical observables

Consider therefore the reduced action (
2.6
).
The conjugate momentum are given by

\displaystyle P_{A}=-2\epsilon\kappa L_{P}\frac{AB\dot{B}}{\mathcal{N}}\;,\qquad P_{B}=-2\epsilon\kappa L_{P}\frac{A}{\mathcal{N}}\left(A\dot{B}+B\dot{A}\right)\;,

(3.1)

such that
[P_{A}]=[P_{B}]=[A]=[B]=1
and
\{P_{A},A\}=\{P_{B},B\}=1
.
Then the reduced action takes the form

S_{\epsilon}=\int\textrm{d}\eta\left[P_{A}\dot{A}+P_{B}\dot{B}-\mathcal{N}H\right]

(3.2)

where the physical hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon}{2\kappa L_{P}}\left[\frac{P^{2}_{A}}{2B^{2}}-\frac{P_{A}P_{B}}{AB}\right]+\kappa L_{P}\left[\frac{B^{2}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}-\frac{1}{L^{2}_{s}}\right]

(3.3)

such that
[H]=L^{-1}
. The first term contains the kinetic contribution, the second term contains the contribution of the curvature induced by the cosmological constant while the last term encodes the contribution of the curvature induced by the 2-sphere.
Let us now introduce a new set of canonical variables given by

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=B^{2}\;,\;\;\;\;\qquad P_{1}=\frac{1}{2B}\left(P_{B}-\frac{AP_{A}}{B}\right)\;,

(3.4)

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{2}B^{2}}{2},\qquad P_{2}=\frac{P_{A}}{AB^{2}}

(3.5)

We have that
[P_{1}]=[P_{2}]=[V_{1}]=[V_{2}]=1
and
\{V_{1},P_{1}\}=\{V_{2},P_{2}\}=1
while all other brackets vanish. With this new set of canonical variables, the hamiltonian takes the form

\displaystyle H=-\frac{\epsilon}{\kappa L_{P}}\left[V_{1}P_{1}P_{2}+\frac{1}{2}V_{2}P^{2}_{2}\right]+\kappa L_{P}\left[\frac{V_{1}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}-\frac{1}{L^{2}_{s}}\right]

(3.6)

which corresponds to the notation introduced in
[
28
]
.
Let us now discuss the physical observables of the system. A straightforward computation shows that the Dirac observables of the vacuum (A)dS system are given by the two phase space functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{1}

\displaystyle:=V_{1}P_{1}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{\kappa^{2}L^{2}_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\epsilon\frac{V_{1}}{P_{2}}\;,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{V_{1}P^{2}_{2}}{2}

(3.8)

which satisfy
\{\mathcal{O}_{1},H\}=0=\{\mathcal{O}_{2},H\}
. Notice that
[\mathcal{O}_{2}]=[\mathcal{O}_{1}]=1
. Computing these observables for the Schwarzschild-(A)dS solution using the metric functions (
2.8
) with the associated value for
\epsilon
, one can show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\kappa L_{P}\mathcal{C}_{1}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{0})=\frac{L_{0}L_{M}}{2L^{2}_{P}}\;,\qquad\mathcal{O}_{2}=2\kappa^{2}L^{2}_{P}\mathcal{C}^{2}_{2}=\frac{2L^{2}_{0}L^{2}_{s}}{L^{2}_{P}}\mathcal{C}^{2}_{2}

(3.9)

Therefore,
\mathcal{O}_{1}
encodes the mass of the system given by the length scale
L_{M}=\tau_{1}-\tau_{0}
. On the other hand,
\mathcal{O}_{2}
encodes the effective size of the system. One can thus fix
\mathcal{C}_{2}=L^{-1}_{P}
without loss of generality such that
\mathcal{O}_{2}
coincides with the squared central charge
\kappa
. Now, at the phase space level, the symmetry of the system becomes manifest in the existence of the so called CVH algebra which we now present.

The extended CVH algebra

Consider now the shifted hamiltonian given by

\tilde{H}=H+\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{s}}=-\frac{\epsilon}{\kappa L_{P}}\left[V_{1}P_{1}P_{2}+\frac{1}{2}V_{2}P^{2}_{2}\right]+\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}V_{1}

(3.10)

and let us introduce the phase space function
C
defined by

C:=\kappa L_{P}\{V_{2},\tilde{H}\}=-\epsilon\left(V_{1}P_{1}+V_{2}P_{2}\right)

(3.11)

With the phase space variables
(V_{1},V_{2})
, they satisfy the following brackets

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \{C,V_{2}\}&=+\epsilon V_{2}\;,\qquad\{C,\tilde{H}\}=-\epsilon\tilde{H}+\epsilon\frac{2\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}V_{1}\;,\qquad\{V_{2},\tilde{H}\}=\frac{C}{\kappa L_{P}}\end{aligned}

(3.12)

where the parameter
\epsilon=\pm 1
enters explicitly. We see that when
L_{\Lambda}\rightarrow+\infty
, the algebra closes which corresponds to the results of
[
28
]
. This algebra is known as the CVH algebra and was initially found and investigated in cosmological models
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
. Here, we see that this CVH algebra is no longer closed for the Schwarzschild-(A)dS system as it inherits a term linear in
V_{1}
which stands out of the triplet
(C,V_{2},\tilde{H})
. Therefore, one has to look for an extended version of it. To that end, let us define the quantity

D:=\kappa L_{P}\{V_{1},\tilde{H}\}=-\epsilon V_{1}P_{2}

(3.13)

Together with the other elements, it satisfies the following commutation relations

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \{D,V_{1}\}=\{D,C\}&=0\;,\qquad\{D,V_{2}\}=+\epsilon V_{1}\;,\;\;\;\;\{C,V_{1}\}=+\epsilon V_{1}\;,\;\;\;\;\{D,\tilde{H}\}=\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\kappa L_{P}}\;,\end{aligned}

(3.14)

where
\mathcal{O}_{2}=V_{1}P^{2}_{2}/2
is the Dirac observable introduced previously in (
3.8
). The remaining brackets are given by

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{O}_{2},C\}=+\epsilon\mathcal{O}_{2}\;,\qquad\{\mathcal{O}_{2},V_{2}\}=\epsilon D\;,\qquad\{O_{2},V_{1}\}=\{\mathcal{O}_{2},D\}=0

(3.15)

which allows one to close the algebra. This structure is therefore generated by six generators
(C,V_{2},\tilde{H},V_{1},D,\mathcal{O}_{2})
. Notice that except
\tilde{H}
, none of the generators depend on the cosmological constant
L_{\Lambda}
. Therefore, their expressions are the same for the Schwarzschild case.

Algebra of Noether charges

Using these phase space functions, one can rewrite the
\text{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
Noether charges derived in Section
2.2
as

\displaystyle Q^{\epsilon}_{+}

\displaystyle=\left(\tilde{H}-\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}V_{1}\right)+\frac{1}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\left(\tau D-\tau^{2}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{2\kappa L_{P}}\right)

(3.16)

\displaystyle Q^{\epsilon}_{0}\;

\displaystyle=\tau\left(\tilde{H}-\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}V_{1}\right)+\epsilon C+\frac{1}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\left(\tau^{2}D-\tau\kappa L_{P}V_{1}-\frac{1}{3}\tau^{3}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\kappa L_{P}}\right)

(3.17)

\displaystyle Q^{\epsilon}_{-}

\displaystyle=\tau^{2}\left(\tilde{H}-\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}V_{1}\right)+2\epsilon\tau C-2\epsilon\kappa L_{P}V_{2}+\frac{1}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\left(\frac{2}{3}\tau^{3}D-\tau^{2}\kappa L_{P}V_{1}-\frac{\tau^{4}}{6}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\kappa L_{P}}\right)

(3.18)

while the translation Noether charges can be recast as

\displaystyle T_{+}^{\epsilon}=\frac{\epsilon}{2\kappa L_{P}}\mathcal{O}_{2}\;,\qquad T_{0}^{\epsilon}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\left\{\tau\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\kappa L_{P}}-D\right\}\;,\qquad T_{-}^{\epsilon}=\epsilon\left\{\tau^{2}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{2\kappa L_{P}}-\tau D+\kappa L_{P}V_{1}\right\}

(3.19)

The conservation and the algebra satisfied by these charges are easily checked. The
\text{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
sector is given by

\{Q^{\epsilon}_{+},Q^{\epsilon}_{-}\}=2Q^{\epsilon}_{0}\;,\qquad\{Q^{\epsilon}_{0},Q^{\epsilon}_{-}\}=Q^{\epsilon}_{-}\;,\qquad\{Q^{\epsilon}_{0},Q^{\epsilon}_{+}\}=-Q^{\epsilon}_{+}

(3.20)

while the charges generating the translations commute
as expected. The cross brackets are given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\{Q^{\epsilon}_{-},T_{+}^{\epsilon}\}=2T_{0}^{\epsilon}\;,\qquad\{Q^{\epsilon}_{+},T_{+}^{\epsilon}\}=0\;,\qquad\;\;\;\{Q^{\epsilon}_{0},T_{+}^{\epsilon}\}=-T_{+}^{\epsilon}\;,\\
&\{Q^{\epsilon}_{-},T_{-}^{\epsilon}\}=0\;,\qquad\;\;\;\;\{Q^{\epsilon}_{+},T_{-}^{\epsilon}\}=2T^{\epsilon}_{0}\;,\qquad\{Q^{\epsilon}_{0},T_{-}^{\epsilon}\}=T_{-}^{\epsilon}\;,\\
&\{Q^{\epsilon}_{-},T_{0}^{\epsilon}\}=-T^{\epsilon}_{-}\;,\qquad\{Q^{\epsilon}_{+},T_{-}^{\epsilon}\}=T^{\epsilon}_{+}\;,\qquad\{Q^{\epsilon}_{0},T_{0}^{\epsilon}\}=0\;,\end{aligned}

(3.21)

It is interesting to note that while the expression of the conserved charges depend explicitly on the parameter
\epsilon
as well as on the
\tau
-coordinate, the brackets of the charge algebra do not. Therefore, the charge algebra is the same in both
T
-region (
\epsilon=+1
) and
R
-region
(\epsilon=-1)
and keep the same form on any hypersurface and encodes the geometry at any radial position, close to the horizon, deep inside the trapped region, or far away from the black hole horizon. In the dS case, it also holds beyond the cosmological horizon.

2+1
Poincaré algebra

Now, we would like to show how the above extended CVH algebra can be organized into a
2+1
Poincaré algebra. Let us first focus on the
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})
sector. The phase space functions
(C,V_{2},V_{1},\tilde{H})
can be combined to form the following boosts and rotation generators

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} K_{y}&=C\;,\\
K_{x}&=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{2}}\left[\frac{\epsilon V_{2}}{\sqrt{\sigma L_{P}}}+\sqrt{\sigma L_{P}}\left(\tilde{H}-\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}V_{1}\right)\right]\;,\\
J_{z}&=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{2}}\left[\frac{V_{2}}{\sqrt{\sigma L_{P}}}-\epsilon\sqrt{\sigma L_{P}}\left(\tilde{H}-\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}V_{1}\right)\right]\end{aligned}

(3.22)

where the parameter
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
is free.
Notice that the generators
K_{x}
and
J_{z}
both depend on the parameter
\epsilon
. The triplet
(K_{x},K_{y},J_{z})
statisfy the standard
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})
commutation relations given by

\displaystyle\{J_{z},K_{x}\}=K_{y}\;,\qquad\{J_{z},K_{y}\}=-K_{x}\;,\qquad\{K_{x},K_{y}\}=-J_{z}

(3.23)

The
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})
Casimir generator is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{T}_{0}&=J^{2}_{z}-K^{2}_{x}-K^{2}_{y}\\
&=2\epsilon\kappa L_{P}V_{2}\left(\tilde{H}-\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}V_{1}\right)-C^{2}=-V_{1}^{2}P^{2}_{1}<0\end{aligned}

(3.24)

Therefore, the
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})
Casimir is independent of
\epsilon
and always negative. From the quantization point of view, it selects the continuous serie of the irreducible representation of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. When
L_{\Lambda}\rightarrow+\infty
, this Casimir coincides with the observable
\mathcal{O}^{2}_{1}
and thus with the squared Schwarzschild mass, which implies that the mass spectrum is continous. When
L_{\Lambda}
is finite, the Casimir is no longer a Dirac observable. Its role will be discussed in the next section.

Now let us focus on the translation sector. The phase space functions
(D,V_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2})
can be organized as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} P_{x}&=-D\;,\\
P_{y}&=\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{2}}\left[\frac{V_{1}}{\sqrt{\sigma L_{P}}}-\sqrt{\sigma L_{P}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\kappa L_{p}}\right]\;,\\
P_{z}&=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{2}}\left[\frac{V_{1}}{\sqrt{\sigma L_{P}}}+\sqrt{\sigma L_{P}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\kappa L_{p}}\right]\;,\end{aligned}

(3.25)

where the parameter
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
is free and
\mathcal{O}_{2}
is the Dirac observable introduced in (
3.8
). Notice that
P_{y}
depends on the parameter
\epsilon
.
It is straightforward to check that the generators
(P_{x},P_{y},P_{z})
commute as expected
and that the Casimir operator associated to this sector vanishes

\mathcal{T}_{1}=P^{2}_{z}-P^{2}_{x}-P^{2}_{y}=0

(3.26)

It describes the translation sector. We can now look at the brackets between the
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})
generators and the translation generators. Step by step, the remaining brackets are given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\{K_{x},P_{x}\}=P_{z}\;,\qquad\{K_{x},P_{y}\}=0\qquad\;\;\;\;\;\{K_{x},P_{z}\}=P_{x}\;\\
&\{K_{y},P_{x}\}=0\;,\qquad\;\;\{K_{y},P_{y}\}=P_{z}\qquad\;\;\;\;\{K_{y},P_{z}\}=P_{y}\;,\\
&\{J_{z},P_{x}\}=P_{y}\;,\qquad\;\;\{J_{z},P_{y}\}=-P_{x}\;,\qquad\{J_{z},P_{z}\}=0\;,\end{aligned}

(3.27)

Before closing this section, let us finally compute the last Casimir of this Poincaré algebra. It is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}=J_{z}P_{z}+K_{y}P_{x}-K_{x}P_{y}=0

(3.28)

Again, notice that the brackets of this 2+1 Poincare algebra do not depend on the parameter
\epsilon
.
This concludes the presentation of the algebraic structure of the Schwarzschild-(A)dS mechanics. It follows that the Schwarzschild-(A)dS black hole can be characterized by three invariant numbers which label its classical states and correspond to the allowed values of the three Casimirs
(\mathcal{T}_{0},\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2})
, the only non-vanishing one being
\mathcal{T}_{0}
.

Role of the
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})
Casimir

In this section, we discuss the role of the non-vanishing Casimir
\mathcal{T}_{0}
for the Schwarzschild and Schwarzschild-(A)dS black holes.

Schwarzschild mass spectrum

We first focus on the Schwarzschild system with
L_{\Lambda}\rightarrow+\infty
. Consider the Casimir generator

\mathcal{T}_{0}=-V_{1}^{2}P^{2}_{1}=-\frac{1}{4}\left(BP_{B}-AP_{A}\right)^{2}

(3.29)

which depends explicitly on the parameter
\epsilon
. Using the hamiltonian (
3.3
) or (
3.6
) for
L_{\Lambda}\rightarrow+\infty
, it is direct to check that
\{\mathcal{T}_{0},H\}=0
, showing that
\mathcal{T}_{0}
is a strong Dirac observable of the system. Computing its value for the Schwarzschild solution, one obtains

\mathcal{T}_{0}=-\frac{L^{2}_{0}L^{2}_{M}}{4L^{4}_{P}}

(3.30)

which corresponds to the (rescaled) squared mass of the Schwarzschild black hole. The Schwarzschild mass spectrum is therefore given by the eigenvalues of the
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})
Casimir. Since it is negative, the Casimir selects either the continuous serie or the complementary serie of the unitary irreducible representation of
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})
. In the first case, the mass inherits a continuous spectrum given by

\displaystyle\frac{L^{2}_{0}L^{2}_{M}}{4L^{4}_{P}}=s^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\;,\qquad\text{with}\qquad s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}

(3.31)

This spectrum holds both for the exterior and interior regions. It is worth pointing that the continuity of this mass spectrum derived from purely symmetry-based arguments is in conflict with several other investigations on the quantization of the Schwarzschild black hole where the mass spectrum was postulated to be discrete
[
33
]
. Let us now investigate the role of
\mathcal{T}_{0}
when
L_{\Lambda}
remains finite.

Generating shift of the cosmological constant

Consider now the general Schwarzschild-(A)dS system where
L_{\Lambda}
remains finite. The Casimir
\mathcal{T}_{0}
is no longer a Dirac observable since

\{\mathcal{T}_{0},H\}=\frac{2\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}V^{2}_{1}P_{1}

(3.32)

and it does not coincide anymore with the mass of the Schwarzschild-(A)dS spacetime given by (
3.7
). It is instructive to consider its square root , i.e.
S=\sqrt{-\mathcal{T}_{0}}=V_{1}P_{1}
, and compute its equations of motion which read

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{S}&=-\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}V_{1}\;,\qquad\ddot{S}=-\frac{D}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\;,\qquad\dddot{S}=-\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\kappa L_{P}L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\;,\qquad\ddddot{S}=0\end{aligned}

(3.33)

such that
\dddot{S}
coincides with the (rescaled) Dirac observable
\mathcal{O}_{2}
. Integrating the above equations, we obtain

S(\tau)=\delta+\gamma\tau+\frac{\beta}{2}\tau^{2}-\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{6\kappa L_{P}L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\tau^{3}

(3.34)

where
(\delta,\gamma,\beta)
are integration constants. In order to fix them, one can compare to the Schwarzschild-(A)dS solution which gives

\beta=\gamma=0\;,\qquad\delta=\frac{L_{0}L_{M}}{2L^{2}_{P}}

(3.35)

We can now investigate the action of the generator
S=V_{1}P_{1}
on the observables of the system and the flows it generates on the phase space. First, the bracket (
3.33
) provides the infinitesimal action of S on the hamiltonian, namely

\delta_{\lambda}H=\lambda\{S,H\}=-\lambda\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}V_{1}

(3.36)

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
parametrizes the flow. It follows that the generator
S=V_{1}P_{1}
induces a one-parameter family of deformation of the dynamics labeled by the cosmological constant.
It allows one to modify the dynamics by shifting, and thus possibly removing, the interaction term associated to the cosmological constant. Now, let us compute how the two Dirac observables
\mathcal{O}_{1}
and
\mathcal{O}_{2}
introduced in (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) evolve along the flow generated by
S
. Their evolution along that flow read

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{O}_{1}

\displaystyle=\lambda\{S,\mathcal{O}_{1}\}=\lambda\epsilon\frac{2}{3}\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\frac{V_{1}}{P_{2}}

(3.37)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{O}_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda\{S,\mathcal{O}_{2}\}=-\lambda\mathcal{O}_{2}

(3.38)

Therefore, the observable
\mathcal{O}_{2}
is rescaled while
\mathcal{O}_{1}
is corrected by a term which shifts its contribution involving the cosmological constant. Exponentiating the generator
S
, we obtain therefore the generator of finite shifts along the cosmological constant. At the phase space level, the flow generated by
S=V_{1}P_{1}
reflects the transformation of the action under the generalized translation (
2.25
) which shifts the cosmological constant, i.e. when
\dddot{h}
is a given constant.

We conclude that when
L_{\Lambda}
is finite, the
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})
Casimir plays the role of shifting the cosmological constant. This provides a new solution-generating technique which allows one to flow between the three different sectors of solutions of the Schwarzschild-(A)dS system. In the next section, we shall apply this solution-generating method and show how the Schwarzschild-(A)dS solution can be obtained from the pure Schwarzschild one through such finite transformation.

Möbius covariance, trajectories mapping and solution-generating flow

In this last section, we would like to understand how the Möbius symmetry (
2.14
) acts at the level of the solutions. Being a physical symmetry, it should transforms gauge-inequivalent solutions to the field equations onto each other. More concretely, we expect that this symmetry connects Schwarzschild-(A)dS blakc hole with different parameters
(L_{M},L_{\Lambda})
. Moreover, the Schwarzschild-(A)dS geometry should be covariant under the newly found
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{3}
transformations. In this section, we demonstrate this covariance property explicitly and give the mapping between the trajectory parameters. Moreover, we show that one can use the translational sector to exhibit finite transformations which are not symmetry but stands as solution-generating transformation for the cosmological constant, allowing one to freely shift the value of this key parameter.

Möbius covariance of the Schwarzschild solution

Let us first fccus on the Schwarzschild solution. Considering the Möbius reparametrization of the
\tau
-coordinate,
the two coordinates before and after the transformation are related through

\tilde{\tau}=\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\;,\qquad\tau=-\frac{d\tilde{\tau}-b}{c\tilde{\tau}-a}\,,

(4.1)

Now, consider the classical solutions for the
A
-field and
B
-field given by (
2.8
) expressed in the time
\tilde{\tau}
and with the constants
(\tilde{\tau}_{0},\tilde{\tau}_{1},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2})
. Explicitly, the
B
-field transforms as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{B}(\tilde{\tau})&=\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2}(\tilde{\tau}-\tilde{\tau}_{0})\\
&=\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}-\tilde{\tau}_{0}\right)\\
&=\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2}(a-c\tilde{\tau}_{0})}{c\tau+d}\left(\tau+\frac{d\tilde{\tau}_{0}-b}{c\tilde{\tau}_{0}-a}\right)=\dot{f}(\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathcal{C}_{2}(\tau-\tau_{0})=\dot{f}(\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,B(\tau)\,,\end{aligned}

(4.2)

which corresponds to (
2.14
) as expected. This transformation shows that the solution for the
B
-field is covariant and that the involved constants
(\tilde{\tau}_{0},\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2})
transform as follows

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2}=\dot{f}(\tau_{0})^{-1/2}\mathcal{C}_{2}\\
\tilde{\tau}_{0}=f(\tau_{0})\end{array}\right.

Let us now look at the transformation law of the
A
-field, which should fix the mapping for the other two constants of motion
(\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1})
. It can easily been shown that

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{2}(\tilde{\tau})

\displaystyle=A^{2}(\tau)

(4.5)

which also fits with (
2.14
). The transformations of the constants
(\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1})
are given

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1}&=\dot{f}^{-1/2}(\tau_{0})\dot{f}^{-1/2}(\tau_{1})\mathcal{C}_{1}\\
\tilde{\tau}_{1}&=f(\tau_{1})\,.\end{array}\right.

The above transformation shows that the space of trajectories is indeed invariant under the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
symmetry and that the Schwarzschild solution is covariant under such Möbius reparametrization. As expected, this physical symmetry maps the Schwarzschild solution with mass
\tilde{L}_{M}
, parametrized by the coordinate
\tilde{\tau}
, into another Schwarzschild solution with mass
L_{M}
, parametrized by the coordinate
\tau
. Under this mapping, the mass parameter changes as

\tilde{L}_{M}=\tilde{\tau}_{1}-\tilde{\tau}_{0}\;,\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad L_{M}=\frac{ad-bc}{(c\tilde{\tau}_{1}-a)(c\tilde{\tau}_{0}-a)}(\tilde{\tau}_{1}-\tilde{\tau}_{0})

(4.8)

It shows that our symmetry transformation is not a diffeomorphism but connects the Schwarzschild geometries at different masses.
It follows that there is an equivalence class of Schwarzschild geometries related by this
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
symmetry transformation. This provides the first result of this section.

Before going further, it is interesting to discuss the fate of the boundary under our symmetry transformation. In our set-up, the boundary appears through the presence of the cut-off scales
(L_{0},L_{s})
which are equivalent to
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
. These scales play the role of IR cut-off which fix the size of the region of interest in our symmetry-reduced model. Although these constants do not enter explicitly in the physical solution, and can be removed by a rescaling of the coordinates, they actually transform non trivially under our transformation (as (
4.1
) and (
4.1
)). Therefore, the symmetry also acts on these scales, and thus on the implicit boundary that we have fixed by hand from the beginning.

Now, in order to generalize this result to the Schwarzschild-(A)dS solution, we will have to consider more general maps acting on the space of trajectories and study their composition. Discussion

In this work, we have shown that the Schwarzschild-(A)dS black hole mechanics enjoys a hidden Noether symmetry under the group SL
(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This symmetry is realized only after the gauge invariance of the symmetry-reduced action (
2.13
) is fixed. Being a physical symmetry, it maps gauge-inequivalent Schwarzschild-(A)dS solutions with different mass and cosmological constant onto each other. A direct consequence of this hidden symmetry is that one can associate a set of non-trivial charges with the Schwarzschild-(A)dS black hole whose algebra fully dictates the underlying geometry on both side of the horizon(s). This result generalizes the symmetry structure found for the Schwarzschild black hole interior mechanics in
[
28
]
and in isotropic cosmological systems in
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
.

This generalization has been obtained by considering the Kantowski-Sachs symmetry-reduced homogeneous model of GR with a cosmological constant which admits the family of Schwarzschild-(A)dS black hole as solutions. The results obtained in this work are the following:

•

By considering the metric (
2.1
) with parameter
\epsilon=\pm 1
, we have shown that one can treat at once both the
T
-region (
\epsilon=+1
) and
R
-region (
\epsilon=-1
) of the underlying Schwarzschild-(A)dS geometry. This set-up allows one to foliate the
T
-region with spacelike hypersurface and
R
-region with timelike ones, and switch between each other by flipping the sign of the parameter
\epsilon
. The SL
(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{3}
symmetry uncovered in this work acts therefore on fields living on two distinct hypersurfaces depending on the region of interest. It follows that in the exterior region, one can associate a set of non-trivial charges to any time-like hypersurface at a given radius
\tau
. While the expression of the charge is
\tau
-dependent, the charge algebra is the same at any value of the radial coordinate, such that the symmetry is realized in the same way near the horizon as well as at large radius. The same is true in the interior region where the
\tau
-coordinate is now a time coordinate labelling spacelike hypersurface. This extension of the result found in
[
28
]
for the Schwarzschild interior mechanics provides the first main result of this work.

•

Moreover, we have shown that when turning on the cosmological constant, the SL
(2,\mathbb{R})
sector of the symmetry has to be modified. The new symmetry transformation are given by (
2.14
-
) where the corrective term affects only the
A
-field through (
B.4
). These new transformations find an elegant interpretation in term of the conformal bridge we have identified. Indeed, by noticing that one can freely change the value of the cosmological constant with suitable translations (which are not symmetries of the action), one can start from the Schwarzschild-(A)dS reduced action, acts with the conformal bridge to remove the cosmological constant, perform the Möbius transformation identified in
[
28
]
for the pure Schwarzschild case, and finally acts once more with the conformal bridge map to set back the cosmological constant to its original value. This process consists in a composition of a Möbius transformation with suitable translations which can be compactly written as (
C.5
). From that perspective, the conformal bridge identified in this work plays a key role in the realization of the SL
(2,\mathbb{R})
symmetry of the Schwarzschild-(A)dS black hole mechanics. The conserved charges generating this hidden symmetry are given in (
2.22
-
2.24
) and (
2.27
-
2.29
)

•

At the hamiltonian level, this symmetry is reflected in an extended version of the CVH algebra first identified in cosmological models in
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
. Just as for the Schwarzschild case, this algebra can be shown to be isomorphic to the three dimensional Poincaré algebra
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{3}
and can be used to rewrite the conserved charges in an intuitive form given by (
4.1
-
). An interesting outcome of our analysis is that, although the charges depend explicitly on the parameter
\epsilon
which distinguishes between the
T
-region and
R
-region, the charge algebra does not dependent on this parameter. It follows that the same algebraic structure is at play in each region, although acting on fields living on different hypersurfaces. Physically, these conserved charges can be used to reconstruct the Schwarzschild-(A)dS solution and their algebra fully dictates the geometry on both side of the horizons (the black hole or/and the cosmological ones). An interesting question is whether one can view the matching of the charges at the horizon as an algebraic realization of the standard junction condition in GR ? We leave this intriguing question for future work.

•

Another interesting outcome of this investigation concerns the role played by the invariant Casimirs of the
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{3}
Poincaré algebra. Among the three Casimirs, only the one of the
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})
algebra is non-vanishing and given by (

). In the pure Schwarzschild case, it coincides with the squared mass, therefore labelling the equilibrium thermodynamical states of the black hole. When the cosmological constant is turned on, the
{\mathfrak{sl}}(2,\mathbb{R})
Casimir does not match with the mass of the black hole given by (
3.7
), but play instead a new surprising role. As shown in Section
3.5.2
, it generates a one-parameter family of deformation whose parameter is nothing else than the cosmological constant. Therefore, the existence of such generator reveals a new map connecting the Schwarzschild, Schwarzschild-AdS and Schwarzschild-dS black hole solutions. Setting the mass to zero, one obtains a simple map between the Minkowski and the vacuum AdS or dS spacetimes. This give rise to a new conformal bridge which generalizes the one identified in cosmology which relates the flat FLRW model to its (A)dS extensions or to the
k=\pm 1
universes
[
18
,
19
]
. This provides the last key result of this work. The fact that one can map different black holes solutions with radically different asymptotic behavior is quite remarquable and begs for further investigations. Whether this conformal bridge or some extension of it can be of any use to connect results in AdS holography to its flat or dS version remain to be explored.

From a more general perspective, the present work provides one more example illustrating the emergence of hidden symmetries in homogeneous symmetry-reduced gravity
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
. Nevertheless, the status of such hidden symmetry remains puzzling for several reasons. First, because of homogeneity, the boundary seems to not play any role in the emergence of this symmetry and the information on the boundary carried by the non-trivial conserved charges shows up only through the cut-off scales fixing the size of the fiducial cell, namely the scales
(L_{0},L_{s})
(or equivalently
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
) introduced in Section
2
. This specific feature of homogeneous models, where the dynamics of fields on the boundary matches exactly the one in the bulk, contrast with the inhomogeneous case, in which the boundary play a central role in the construction of the conserved charges. Second, the symmetry transformations that we consider cannot be represented as diffeomorphism of the metric, whose components transform as primary fields with a suitable (possibly different) conformal weight. Despite these key differences, the transformations exhibited in this work stand as well defined physical symmetries of the Schwarzschild-(A)dS black hole mechanics. We stress that our analysis holds for both the anti-de Sitter and de Sitter case.

At this stage, several questions remain to be addressed. First of all, what is the maximal symmetry group of the gauge-fixed system ? The three-dimensional Poincaré structure uncovered here and in
[
28
]
might well be only the corner of a larger structure. Previous results obtained in cosmological systems have revealed that the CVH algebra belongs actually to a larger
{\mathfrak{so}}(3,2)
conformal algebra of observables
[
21
]
. Whether a similar extension exists for the black hole phase space remains to be investigated. Finally, can we use this symmetry structure to unravel physical properties of the Schwarzschild black hole ? Does this symmetry play a role in black hole perturbation theory, for example, by giving rise to new invariance for test fields propagating on it ? We expect to address these fascinating questions in the near future.

Appendix B
Symmetry transformations and Noether charges computation

In this appendix, we present the detailed computation of variation of the action under the hidden symmetries identified in Section
2.2
. We introduce the dimensionless quantity

\kappa=\frac{L_{0}L^{2}_{s}}{L^{3}_{P}}

(B.1)

for simplicity. It encodes the ratio between the effective size of the system, i.e the IR cut-off, and the Planck volume, i.e the UV cut-off our our system.
The gauge fixed action reads

\displaystyle S_{\epsilon}[A,B]=\frac{L_{0}L^{2}_{s}}{\epsilon L^{2}_{P}}\int\textrm{d}\tau\left[\epsilon\left(\frac{1}{L^{2}_{s}}-\frac{B^{2}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\right)-A^{2}\dot{B}^{2}-2AB\dot{B}\dot{A}\right]

(B.2)

Consider now the following transformations

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tau\rightarrow\tilde{\tau}&=f(\tau)\\
B(\tau)\rightarrow\tilde{B}(\tilde{\tau})&=\dot{f}^{1/2}B(\tau)\\
A^{2}(\tau)\rightarrow\tilde{A}^{2}(\tilde{\tau})&=A^{2}(\tau)+\chi(\tau)\end{aligned}

(B.3)

where
f(\tau)
is an arbitrary function and where

\displaystyle\chi(\tau)

\displaystyle:=2h\frac{\dot{B}}{B}-\dot{h}-\frac{2h\circ f}{\dot{f}}\left[\frac{\dot{B}}{B}+\frac{\ddot{f}}{2\dot{f}}\right]+\dot{h}\circ f\;,

(B.4)

\displaystyle h(\tau)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\epsilon}{3L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\tau^{3}\;,

(B.5)

Under these transformations, the reduced action varies as

\displaystyle\Delta S_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\kappa L_{P}\int\textrm{d}\tau B^{2}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\text{Sch}[f]\left[A^{2}+\chi-4\dot{h}\circ f\right]+\frac{h\circ f}{\dot{f}}\;\frac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}\tau}\text{Sch}[f]\right\}+\epsilon\kappa L_{P}\int\textrm{d}\tau\frac{\textrm{d}F}{\textrm{d}\tau}

(B.6)

defined as

\text{Sch}[f]=\frac{\dddot{f}}{\dot{f}}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\ddot{f}}{\dot{f}}\right)^{2}

(B.7)

The second term is the total derivative term given by

F=\left\{\frac{\epsilon}{L^{2}_{s}}(f-\tau)+\left[\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}}{\textrm{d}\tau^{2}}\left(h-\frac{h\circ f}{\dot{f}}\right)-\frac{\ddot{f}}{\dot{f}}(A^{2}+\chi)\right]\frac{B^{2}}{2}-\left(h-\frac{h\circ f}{\dot{f}}\right)\dot{B}^{2}\right\}

(B.8)

Now, by definition of the Schwarzian, the anomalous contribution vanishes provided we restrict the reparametrization function

to a Möbius reparametrization, i.e.

f(\tau)=\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\;,\qquad ad-bc\neq 0

B.5
) and (
B.9

which shows that the Schwarzschild-(A)dS mechanics enjoys indeed a Noether symmetry under the conformal reparametrization introduced above. Since this result is independent of the value of
\epsilon
, it implies that the symmetry acts both in the exterior and interior of the Schwarzschild-(A)dS black hole. We now derive the Noether charges generating this symmetry.

Consider thus an infinitesimal transformation of the form
f(\tau)=\tau+\xi(\tau)
. For an infinitesimal Möbius transformation, the function
\xi(\tau)
corresponds to

\displaystyle\xi(\tau)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma&\mbox{for translation }\\
\sigma\tau&\mbox{for dilatation}\\
\sigma\tau^{2}&\mbox{for special conformal transformation}\end{array}\right.

which implies that

is at most quadratic in

\dddot{\xi}=0

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \delta\tau&=\tilde{\tau}-\tau=\xi\\
\delta B&=\tilde{B}(\tau)-B(\tau)=-\xi\dot{B}+\frac{1}{2}\dot{\xi}B\\
\delta(A^{2})&=\tilde{A}^{2}(\tau)-A^{2}(\tau)=-\xi\frac{\textrm{d}A^{2}}{\textrm{d}\tau}+\chi\end{aligned}

(B.13)

where the last term

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=\frac{2\epsilon}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\left[\tau\xi-\frac{\tau^{3}}{6}\ddot{\xi}+\left(\frac{\tau^{3}}{3}\dot{\xi}-\tau^{2}\xi\right)\frac{\dot{B}}{B}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{2})

(B.14)

Using these infinitesimal variations of the fields, the infinitesimal variation of the action can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \delta S_{\epsilon}&=\epsilon\kappa L_{P}\int\textrm{d}\tau\frac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}\tau}\left[\xi\left(\frac{\epsilon}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}B^{2}+A^{2}\dot{B}^{2}+2A\dot{A}B\dot{B}\right)-\ddot{\xi}\frac{A^{2}B^{2}}{2}\right.\\

where the first term is a total derivative while the second term vanishes since

. Then following the Noether theorem, it is straightforward to compute the Noether charge generating the above symmetry. It can be compactly written as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} Q^{\epsilon}&=\epsilon\kappa L_{P}\left\{\dot{\xi}\left(A\dot{A}B^{2}+B\dot{B}A^{2}\right)-\ddot{\xi}\frac{A^{2}B^{2}}{2}-\xi\left(A^{2}\dot{B}^{2}+2A\dot{A}B\dot{B}\right)\right\}\\
&\;\;+\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\left\{\left(\frac{\tau^{3}}{3}\dot{\xi}-\tau^{2}\xi\right)\dot{B}^{2}+\left(\tau\xi-\frac{\tau^{3}}{6}\ddot{\xi}\right)2B\dot{B}-\left(\tau\dot{\xi}-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\ddot{\xi}\right)B^{2}\right\}\end{aligned}

(B.16)

The first line corresponds to the Schwarzschild geometry while the second line encodes the corrections induced by the presence of the cosmological constant. Its time evolution can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{Q}^{\epsilon}&=\epsilon\kappa L_{P}\left\{\dot{\xi}B\mathcal{E}_{B}-2\xi\left[\dot{B}\mathcal{E}_{B}+\dot{A}\mathcal{E}_{A}\right]-\left[\frac{A^{2}B^{2}}{2}-\frac{\epsilon}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\left(\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}B^{2}-\frac{\tau^{3}}{3}B\dot{B}\right)\right]\dddot{\xi}\right\}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{\kappa L_{P}}{L^{2}_{\Lambda}}\left\{\frac{\tau^{3}}{3}\dot{\xi}-\tau^{2}\xi+\tau\xi-\frac{\tau^{3}}{6}\ddot{\xi}\right\}\frac{2\dot{B}}{AB}\mathcal{E}_{A}\simeq 0\end{aligned}

(B.17)

where
\simeq
refers to the on-shell value. Indeed,
\mathcal{E}_{B}=\mathcal{E}_{A}\simeq 0
are the e.o.m for the
A
-field and
B
-field and one has
\dddot{\xi}=0
. The above evolution equation has to be understood as an off shell identity which is the first consequence of the Noether theorem. Imposing the equation of motion then leads to on shell conservation of the quantity
Q_{\epsilon}
which stands as first integral of the system, i.e a conserved charge. Using the allowed form of the time-dependent function
\xi(\tau)
given by (
B
), the three charges generating this
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
symmetry are given by (
2.22
-
2.24
).
They are not all independent since they are related by a Casimir condition. In the end, only two of them are needed to solve for the dynamics and therefore to
label the equilibrium states of the black hole.

Now, we consider a second set of symmetry which corresponds to the translation sector. Consider the transformation

\displaystyle\tau\rightarrow\tilde{\tau}

\displaystyle=\tau

(B.18)

\displaystyle A\rightarrow\tilde{A}^{2}(\tilde{\tau})

\displaystyle=A^{2}(\tau)+2h\frac{\dot{B}}{B}-\dot{h}

(B.19)

\displaystyle B\rightarrow\tilde{B}(\tilde{\tau})

\displaystyle=B(\tau)

(B.20)

Under this transformation, the action becomes

\displaystyle\Delta S

\displaystyle=\epsilon\kappa L_{P}\int\textrm{d}\tau\left[\frac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}\tau}\left(\ddot{h}B^{2}-h\dot{B}^{2}\right)-\dddot{h}\frac{B^{2}}{2}\right]

(B.21)

which shows that the action enjoys indeed a Noether symmetry when

(B.22)

where

This second symmetry was first found in

Its time evolution vanishes on shell, i.e.

Appendix C
Möbius covariance of the Schwarzschild-(A)dS solution

Consider the Schwarzschild-(A)dS solution at some value
\Lambda\neq 0
. We first perform a translation
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, where
h
is carefully chosen so that
h\triangleright\Lambda=0
. To that end, one can choose the translation functional parameter
2
2

2

We could also have chosen the function
h_{\Lambda}(\tau)=\tfrac{\Lambda}{3}\tau^{3}
, which does not depend on the singularity proper time
\tau_{0}
, or any more general function. Here we made the choice which simplifies as much as possible
as

h_{\Lambda,\tau_{0}}(\tau)\equiv\frac{\Lambda}{3}(\tau-\tau_{0})^{3}\,,\qquad\dddot{h}(\tau)=2\Lambda\,,\qquad h(\tau_{0})=\dot{h}(\tau_{0})=0\,.

(C.1)

Using the notation introduced above, the transformation law of the corresponding translation reads:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{h_{\Lambda,\tau_{0}}}\Big[A[\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},\Lambda]\Big]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A[h_{\Lambda,\tau_{0}}\triangleright(\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},\Lambda)]^{2}=A[\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},0]^{2}\,,

(C.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{h_{\Lambda,\tau_{0}}}\Big[B[\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},\Lambda]\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B[h_{\Lambda,\tau_{0}}\triangleright(\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},\Lambda)]=B[\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},0]\,,

with the translation by
h_{\Lambda,\tau_{0}}
acting simply on the trajectory’s parameter multiplet
(\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},\Lambda)

h_{\Lambda,\tau_{0}}\triangleright(\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},\Lambda)=(\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},0)\,.

(C.3)

Now we apply a Möbius transformation by an arbitrary function
f

\displaystyle A[\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},0](\tau)

\displaystyle\underset{\mathcal{D}_{f}}{\longmapsto}

\displaystyle A[f\triangleright(\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},0)](f(\tau))=A[\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},0](\tau)\,,\qquad

\displaystyle B[\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},0](\tau)

\displaystyle\underset{\mathcal{D}_{f}}{\longmapsto}

\displaystyle B[f\triangleright(\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},0)](f(\tau))=\dot{f}(\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,B[\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2},0](\tau)\,,

f

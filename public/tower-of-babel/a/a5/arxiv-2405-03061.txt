Saturation in Random Hypergraphs
Sahar Diskin, Ilay Hoshen, Dániel Korándi, Benny Sudakov, Maksim Zhukovskii

Abstract
Let K n r K^{r}_{n} be the complete r r -uniform hypergraph on n n vertices, that is, the hypergraph whose vertex set is [ n ] ≔ { 1 , 2 , … , n } [n]\coloneqq\{1,2,\ldots,n\} and whose edge set is ( [ n ] r ) \binom{[n]}{r} . We form G r ​ ( n , p ) G^{r}(n,p) by retaining each edge of K n r K^{r}_{n} independently with probability p p . An r r -uniform hypergraph H ⊆ G H\subseteq G is F F -saturated if H H does not contain any copy of F F , but any missing edge of H H in G G creates a copy of F F . Furthermore, we say that H H is weakly F F -saturated in G G if H H does not contain any copy of F F , but the missing edges of H H in G G can be added back one-by-one, in some order, such that every edge creates a new copy of F F . The smallest number of edges in an F F -saturated hypergraph in G G is denoted by sat ​ ( G , F ) \textit{sat}(G,F) , and in a weakly F F -saturated hypergraph in G G by w - 𝑠𝑎𝑡 ⁡ ( G , F ) \mathop{\mbox{$w$-$\mathit{sat}$}}(G,F) . In 2017, Korándi and Sudakov initiated the study of saturation in random graphs, showing that for constant p p , with high probability sat ​ ( G ⁡ ( n , p ) , K s ) = ( 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ n ​ log 1 1 − p ​ n \textit{sat}(G(n,p),K_{s})=(1+o(1))n\log_{\frac{1}{1-p}}n , and w - 𝑠𝑎𝑡 ⁡ ( G ⁡ ( n , p ) , K s ) = w - 𝑠𝑎𝑡 ⁡ ( K n , K s ) \mathop{\mbox{$w$-$\mathit{sat}$}}(G(n,p),K_{s})=\mathop{\mbox{$w$-$\mathit{sat}$}}(K_{n},K_{s}) . Generalising their results, in this paper, we solve the saturation problem for random hypergraphs G r ​ ( n , p ) G^{r}(n,p) for cliques K s r K_{s}^{r} , for every 2 ≤ r < s 2\leq r<s and constant p p .

The proof of the upper bound follows from a delicate construction. For the sake of clarity of presentation, and since it already illustrates all the main issues one needs to overcome, we consider the case when

The natural first step for finding a weakly
K_{s}^{r}

to be the graph whose edges are of the form

The proof is divided into four main stages. In the first stage, we define appropriate cores for all relevant subsets of vertices

of size at most two.
The second stage describes the construction of a weakly

-saturated subhypergraph

-saturated subhypergraph

3
Weak saturation

Let
r\geq 3
and
s\geq r+1
be integers. Let
p\in(0,1]
be a constant. Recall that
G\coloneqq G^{r}(n,p)
. Set
\ell\coloneqq s-r
.

We start we the lower bound, which is the easiest part of the paper, and follows from the fact that
whp

G
is weakly saturated in
K_{n}

Claim 3.1
.

Whp

\mathop{\mbox{$w$-$\mathit{sat}$}}\left(G,K_{s}^{r}\right)\geq\mathop{\mbox{$w$-$\mathit{sat}$}}(K_{n}^{r},K_{s}^{r})
.

Proof.

Let us first show that
whp

G
is weakly saturated in
K^{r}_{n}
. To that end, fix some edge
e\in K^{r}_{n}\setminus G
. The probability that for a fixed set
S
of size
\ell
we have that
e\cup G[S\cup e]\not\cong K_{s}^{r}
is
1-p^{\binom{s}{r}-1}
. This event is independent for every two disjoint sets of size
\ell
, and thus the probability that
e
does not form a copy of
K^{r}_{s}
in
G\cup e
is at most
\left(1-p^{\binom{s}{r}-1}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{n}{\ell}\rfloor}=\text{exp}\left(-\Theta(n)\right)
. The union bound over
\binom{n}{r}\leq n^{r}
edges
e\in K^{r}_{n}
shows that
whp
every edge
e\in K^{r}_{n}
belongs to a copy of
K^{r}_{s}
in
G\cup e
, and therefore
whp

G
is weakly saturated in
K^{r}_{n}
.

Now, if
H
is weakly saturated in
G
, then we can add to
H
edges one-by-one until we obtain
G
, and then keep adding edges until we reach
K^{r}_{n}
. Thus,
whp
any such

is weakly saturated in
K_{n}^{r}
as well. But then,
\mathop{\mbox{$w$-$\mathit{sat}$}}\left(G,K_{s}^{r}\right)\geq\mathop{\mbox{$w$-$\mathit{sat}$}}(K_{n}^{r},K_{s}^{r})
.
∎

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of the upper bound. In Section
3.1
, we lay the groundwork for our proof: define the cores, construct the weakly saturated subhypergraph

, and count its edges. In Section
3.2

that are not in

.

3.1

Strong Saturation

We begin with the proof of the lower bound of Theorem
1
2
, which sheds some light as to why typically
\textit{sat}(G^{r}(n,p),K^{r}_{s})=(1+o(1))\binom{n}{r-1}\log_{\frac{1}{1-p^{r-1}}}(n)
.

Proof of the lower bound of Theorem
1
2
.

Let us show that if
H
is
K_{s}^{r}
-saturated in
G
, then
e(H)\geq(1+o(1))\binom{n}{r-1}\log_{\frac{1}{1-p^{r-1}}}n
. Note that if
H
is
K_{s}^{r}
-saturated in
G
, then adding any

creates a new copy of
K_{s}^{r}
, and in particular, a new copy of

. Let
\alpha=\frac{1}{1-p^{r-1}}
.

Given an
(r-1)
-subset

, let

. Let

\displaystyle A\coloneqq\{S\subseteq V(G)\colon|S|=r-1\text{ and }|N_{H}(S)|\geq\log_{\alpha}^{2}n\},

and set

|E(H)|=\Omega\left(n^{r-1}\log_{\alpha}^{2}n\right)

Let

. We claim that there are at least
(1+o(1))\log_{\alpha}n

, such that

is the only
(r-1)
-subset of

which is in
B
. This will imply that the number of edges in

is at least
|B|\log_{\alpha}n=(1+o(1))\binom{n}{r-1}\log_{\alpha}n
, as required. Let

be such that

. Then
\{u\}\cup S
closes a copy of
K_{r+1}^{r}
together with

, in particular, there is some

such that

. Note that for all but at most
\log_{\alpha}^{4}n
choices of

, we have that the only
(r-1)
-subset of
S\cup\{w\}
that is in
B
is
S
. Indeed,
S\in B
, so there are at most
\log_{\alpha}^{2}n
choices of

such that

. Then, if at least one other
(r-1)
-subset
S\neq S^{\prime}\subseteq S\cup\{w\}
is such that

, then we have at most

choices for this

as well, since

.

Thus, we have a set
U
of at least
|N_{G}(S)|-\log_{\alpha}^{4}-O(1)
vertices

such that there exists

forming an edge with
S
and with every

, where

,
|S^{\prime}|=r-2
, and such that

is the only
(r-1)

. Let us prove that

. This will immediately imply the desired upper bound.

To that end, it suffices to show that
whp
for any
(r-1)
-set
S
, and

of size at most
\log_{\alpha}n-5\log_{\alpha}\log_{\alpha}n

such that

, and for every

there is some

with

, such that

and

. These events are independent for different

at least

•

Lemma 4.2

Let

, for every two vertices

Proof.

Fix two vertices

which contain

\mathbb{P}\left(X>2\binom{n}{t-2}p^{\binom{t}{r}}\right)\ll n^{-2}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X\mid H\subset G]\leq\binom{n}{t-2}\rho^{\binom{t}{r}}+\sum_{j=r}^{t}e(H)^{\binom{j}{r}}n^{t-j}\rho^{\binom{t}{r}-\binom{j}{r}}.

\frac{\binom{t}{r}(t+1-r)}{\left(\binom{t+1}{r}-1\right)(t-1)}<1-\frac{r-1}{t}

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{t}{r}(t+1-r)}{\left(\binom{t+1}{r}-1\right)(t-1)}

\displaystyle<\frac{\binom{t}{r}}{\binom{t+1}{r}-1}=\frac{\binom{t}{r}}{\binom{t}{r}+\binom{t}{r-1}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\binom{t}{r}}{\binom{t}{r}+\binom{t}{r}\cdot\frac{r}{t-r+1}-1}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{r}{t-r+1}-\frac{1}{\binom{t}{r}}}

\displaystyle\leq 1-\frac{r}{t-r+1}+\frac{1}{\binom{t}{r}}\leq 1-\frac{r}{t-r+1}+\frac{1}{t-r+1}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{r-1}{t-r+1}<1-\frac{r-1}{t}.

∎

By Claim

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{j}{r}(t+1-r)}{\binom{t+1}{r}-1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r\geq 3}}{{\leq}}j-2,

as needed.

where the last asymptotical inequality is true if

Let us first state (several versions of) Janson’s inequality, which we will use later on. In some cases, we will need to bound the probability of the existence of many edge-disjoint copies of some graph in

Let

\Omega

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[X]=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb{P}(X_{i}=1)\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i\sim j}\mathbb{P}(X_{i}=X_{j}=1),

.

Theorem A.1

(Janson’s inequality
[
11
]
)
.

\mathbb{P}(X=0)\leq e^{-\mu^{2}/2\Delta}
.

We also use versions of Janson’s inequality (see, for example,
[
2
]
) which bound the probability of the event that we do not have many disjoint copies of

’s in

Lemma A.2
.

For every integer

\mathbb{P}(D_{s})\leq\frac{\mu^{s}}{s!}.

For a probability bound on the size of a maximal disjoint family, we need another notation. Define

\displaystyle\nu=\max_{j\in I}\sum_{i\sim j}\mathbb{P}(X_{i}=1).

Lemma A.3
.

For every integer

\mathbb{P}(M_{s})\leq\frac{\mu^{s}}{s!}e^{-\mu+s\nu+\Delta/2}.

Let us derive from Lemma

Corollary A.4

If

\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{s\leq(1-\epsilon)\mu}\ M_{s}\right)\leq e^{-\epsilon^{2}\mu/3}.

Proof.

By the union bound,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{s\leq(1-\epsilon)\mu}\ M_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=1}^{(1-\epsilon)\mu}\mathbb{P}(M_{s})\leq\sum_{s=1}^{(1-\epsilon)\mu}\frac{\mu^{s}}{s!}e^{-\mu}e^{s\nu+\Delta/2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\epsilon^{3}\mu}\sum_{s=1}^{(1-\epsilon)\mu}\frac{\mu^{s}}{s!}e^{-\mu},

where the second inequality is true by Lemma

and the last inequality is true since, by the assumptions of the corollary,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{s\leq(1-\epsilon)\mu}\ M_{s}\right)\leq e^{\epsilon^{2}\mu/6}e^{-\epsilon^{2}\mu/2}=e^{-\epsilon^{2}\mu/3}.

∎

We also make use of the following bounds (see, for example,

. Below, we consider only those

that contain

and

(in particular, sum up over such sets in the computation of the

-th moment of

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X^{L}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1},\ldots,T_{L}\subset V(G)\\
|T_{1}|=\ldots=|T_{L}|=t\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}[Y_{T_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot Y_{T_{L}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1},\ldots,T_{L-1}\subset V(G)\\
|T_{1}|=\ldots=|T_{L-1}|=t\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}[Y_{T_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot Y_{T_{L-1}}]\sum_{T_{L}\subset V(G),|T_{L}|=t}\mathbb{E}[Y_{T_{L}}\mid Y_{T_{1}}=\ldots=Y_{T_{L-1}}=1]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{1},\ldots,T_{L-1}\subset V(G)\\
|T_{1}|=\ldots=|T_{L-1}|=t\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}[Y_{T_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot Y_{T_{L-1}}]\cdot\mathbb{E}[X\mid Y_{T_{1}}=\ldots=Y_{T_{L-1}}=1].

(6)

Let
H
be a hypergraph with
e(H)\leq L\cdot t
. By FKG inequality,
\mathbb{E}[X\mid H\subset G]\geq\mathbb{E}X
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X\mid H\subset G]\leq\binom{n}{t-2}p^{\binom{t}{r}}+\sum_{j=r}^{t}e(H)^{\binom{j}{r}}n^{t-j}p^{\binom{t}{r}-\binom{j}{r}}.

The first summand in the right-hand side above is an upper bound to the expectation of the number of copies of
K_{t}^{r}
which does not share any edges with
H
while the second summand is an upper bound to the expectation of the number of copies of
K_{t}^{r}
which does share at least one edge with
H
. The index

indicates how many vertices in the copy of
K_{t}^{r}
belongs to
H
. So we have at most

edges which are already in
H
. The term
e(H)^{\binom{j}{r}}
is an upper bound to the number of choices of the common

is an upper bound to the number of choices of the remaining
t-j
vertices and
p^{\binom{t}{r}-\binom{j}{r}}
is an upper bound to the probability that this copy appears in
G
conditioned on
H\subseteq G
. We will show that the second summand on the right-hand side of the inequality is asymptotically smaller than

\mathbb{E}[X]=(1+o(1))\binom{n}{t-2}p^{\binom{t}{r}},

(7)

implying
\mathbb{E}[X\mid H\subset G]\leq(1+o(1))\mathbb{E}[X]
. Note that, for every
j=r,r+1,\ldots,t
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{n^{t-j}p^{\binom{t}{r}-\binom{j}{r}}}{n^{t-2}p^{\binom{t}{r}}}=n^{2-j}p^{-\binom{j}{r}}=\Theta\left(n^{2-j+\frac{\binom{j}{r}(t+1-r)}{\binom{t+1}{r}-1}}\right).

Thus, it is sufficient to show that the last expression tends to 0 as

, which is true if

\frac{\binom{j}{r}(t+1-r)}{\binom{t+1}{r}-1}<j-2.

To show the latter, we will use the following claim.

Claim A.7
.

\frac{\binom{t}{r}(t+1-r)}{\left(\binom{t+1}{r}-1\right)(t-1)}<1-\frac{r-1}{t}
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{t}{r}(t+1-r)}{\left(\binom{t+1}{r}-1\right)(t-1)}

\displaystyle<\frac{\binom{t}{r}}{\binom{t+1}{r}-1}=\frac{\binom{t}{r}}{\binom{t}{r}+\binom{t}{r-1}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\binom{t}{r}}{\binom{t}{r}+\binom{t}{r}\cdot\frac{r}{t-r+1}-1}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{r}{t-r+1}-\frac{1}{\binom{t}{r}}}

\displaystyle\leq 1-\frac{r}{t-r+1}+\frac{1}{\binom{t}{r}}\leq 1-\frac{r}{t-r+1}+\frac{1}{t-r+1}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{r-1}{t-r+1}<1-\frac{r-1}{t}.

∎

By Claim
A.7
,

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{j}{r}(t+1-r)}{\binom{t+1}{r}-1}

\displaystyle<\frac{\binom{j}{r}}{\binom{t}{r}}\left(1-\frac{r-1}{t}\right)(t-1)\leq\frac{\binom{j}{r}}{\binom{t}{r}}(t-2)=\frac{j(j-1)\ldots(j-r+1)}{t(t-1)\ldots(t-r+1)}(t-2)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r\geq 3}}{{\leq}}j-2,

as needed.

Thus, we conclude that
\mathbb{E}[X\mid H\subset G]=(1+o(1))\mathbb{E}[X]
uniformly over all
H
with
e(H)\leq L\cdot t
.
By induction, from (
6
), we have

\mathbb{E}[X^{L}]=\biggl((1+o(1))\mathbb{E}[X]\biggr)^{L}.

From (

), by Markov’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(X>2\binom{n}{t-2}p^{\binom{t}{r}}\right)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[X^{L}]}{((2+o(1))\mathbb{E}[X])^{L}}=\left(\frac{(1+o(1))\mathbb{E}[X]}{2\mathbb{E}[X]}\right)^{L}=(1/2+o(1))^{L}\ll n^{-2},

where the last asymptotical inequality is true if
K
is sufficiently large.
∎

Linking disjoint segments into a simple polygon is hard
Rain Jiang, Kai Jiang, Minghui Jiang

Abstract
Deciding whether a family of disjoint line segments in the plane can be linked into a simple polygon (or a simple polygonal chain) by adding segments between their endpoints is NP-hard.



1
Introduction

Given a family
\mathcal{S}
of
n
closed line segments in the plane,

Simple Circuit
(respectively,
Simple Path
)
is the problem of deciding whether
these segments can be linked into
a simple polygon (respectively, a simple polygonal chain)
by adding segments between their endpoints.

Rappaport
[
4
]
proved that
Simple Circuit
is NP-hard
if the segments in
\mathcal{S}
are allowed to intersect at their common endpoints,
and asked whether the problem remains NP-hard when the segments are disjoint.
Later, Bose, Houle, and Toussaint
[
3
]
asked
whether the related problem
Simple Path
is NP-hard
when the segments in
\mathcal{S}
are disjoint.
More recently, Tóth
[
6
]
asked about the complexity of
Simple Path
again,
with a special interest in the case
when the segments in
\mathcal{S}
are both disjoint and axis-parallel.

In this note,
we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

Simple Circuit
and
Simple Path
are both NP-hard
even if the segments in
\mathcal{S}
are disjoint and have only four distinct orientations.

We prove the theorem in two steps.
First, we modify the construction in Rappaport’s proof
of NP-hardness of
Simple Circuit
on not necessarily disjoint segments
[
4
]

to show that the problem remains NP-hard on disjoint segments.
Next, we modify the construction further
to prove that
Simple Path
is also NP-hard on disjoint segments.
In the following, we briefly review Rappaport’s proof,
which is based on a polynomial reduction from the NP-hard problem

Hamiltonian Path
in planar cubic graphs.

For any family
\mathcal{S}
of closed segments in the plane,
denote by
V(\mathcal{S})
the set of endpoints of the segments in
\mathcal{S}
.
For any two endpoints
p
and
q
in
V(\mathcal{S})
,
we call the open segment
pq
a
visibility edge

if it does not intersect any closed segment in
\mathcal{S}
.

Given a planar cubic graph
G
with
n\geq 4
vertices,
the reduction
[
4
]
first obtains a rectilinear planar layout of the planar graph
using an algorithm of Rosenstiehl and Tarjan
[
5
]
,
then constructs a family
\mathcal{S}
of
O(n)
segments
following the rectilinear planar layout,
such that
G
admits a Hamiltonian path
if and only if
\mathcal{S}
can be linked into a simple polygon
by adding visibility edges between points in
V(\mathcal{S})
.
The segments in
\mathcal{S}
are axis-parallel and interior-disjoint,
but may intersect at common endpoints.
Each endpoint in
V(\mathcal{S})
is incident to at most one horizontal segment
and at most one vertical segment in
\mathcal{S}
.
Since the rectilinear planar layout
is of width at most
2n-4
and of height at most
n

[
5
]
,
all coordinates of endpoints in
V(\mathcal{S})
are integers of magnitude

O(n^{2})
;
indeed a closer look at the construction
[
4
, Figure 8]

shows that

V(\mathcal{S})\subseteq[1,22n^{2}]\times[1,11n]
.
The reduction is hence strongly polynomial.
Consequently,
Simple Circuit
is strongly NP-hard,
on not necessarily disjoint segments.

2
Modification for
Simple Circuit

To show that
Simple Circuit
remains strongly NP-hard on disjoint segments,
we will transform the family
\mathcal{S}
of interior-disjoint axis-parallel segments,
which Rappaport constructed,
into a family
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
of disjoint segments with four distinct orientations,
in polynomial time,
such that
\mathcal{S}
can be linked into a simple polygon
if and only if
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
can be linked into a simple polygon.
This transformation can be viewed as a reduction from
Simple Circuit
on one type
of input to the same problem on another type of input.

The following lemma shows that our local transformation preserves linkability:

Lemma 2
.

\mathcal{S}
can be linked into a simple polygon
if and only if
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
can be linked into a simple polygon.

Proof.

We first prove the direct implication.
Suppose that
\mathcal{S}
can be linked into a simple polygon.
For each visibility edge of this polygon between two endpoints in
V(\mathcal{S})
,
we add a visibility edge between the corresponding endpoints in
V(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
.
For each endpoint
o
incident to two segments
oa
and
ob
in
\mathcal{S}
,
we link the segments in the corresponding gadget in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

following the alternating path as illustrated in Figure
3
right.
Then
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is also linked into a simple polygon.

We next prove the reverse implication.
Suppose that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
can be linked into a simple polygon.
Recall Lemma
1
.
In any gadget,

B_{1}
can only see
A^{\prime}_{1}
and
a^{\prime}b^{\prime}
,
and
B_{2}
can only see
A_{2}
and
a^{\prime}b^{\prime}
.
Thus
a^{\prime}b^{\prime}
must be linked to both
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
,
which are linked to
A^{\prime}_{1}
and
A_{2}
, respectively.
This chain can extend further only to
o^{\prime}a
and
o^{\prime\prime}b
, respectively,
since
A_{2}
cannot see
o^{\prime}a
.
Thus the seven segments in each gadget must be linked consecutively
in the simple polygon along the sequence

o^{\prime}a,A^{\prime}_{1},B_{1},a^{\prime}b^{\prime},B_{2},A_{2},o^{\prime\prime}b
.
Then, following the other visibility edges of the simple polygon through
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,
which are outside and between the gadgets,

\mathcal{S}
can be linked into a simple polygon too.
∎

Recall that the coordinates of endpoints in
V(\mathcal{S})

are integers of magnitude
O(n^{2})
.
After the transformation,
all endpoints in
V(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
except
a^{\prime}_{1}

have integer coordinates too.
We can scale all coordinates by another factor of
1/\delta=800n^{2}
,
so that all endpoints in
V(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
including
a^{\prime}_{1}

have integer coordinates of magnitude
O(n^{4})
,
and the reduction remains strongly polynomial.
Thus
Simple Circuit
is strongly NP-hard,
even if the input segments are disjoint and have only four distinct orientations.

Modification for
Simple Path

To prove that
Simple Path
is also NP-hard,
we use almost the same transformation from
\mathcal{S}
to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
as before,
except two changes:

1.

Increase the initial scaling factor from
40
to
80
,
and correspondingly decrease the distance
\delta
between
a_{1}
and
a^{\prime}_{1}

from
\frac{1}{800n^{2}}
to
\frac{1}{1600n^{2}}

when constructing the gadgets.

2.

Select an arbitrary gadget as illustrated in Figure
3
,
and replace it by an extended gadget as illustrated in Figure
5

The purpose of the first change is to make room for additional segments
in the extended gadget.
Updating the scaling factor and the distance
\delta
together
ensures that (
1

and hence Lemma
1
remains valid.
Clearly, the reduction remains strongly polynomial.

Lemma 3
.

\mathcal{S}
can be linked into a simple polygon
if and only if
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
can be linked into a simple polygonal chain.

Proof.

We first prove the direct implication.
Suppose that
\mathcal{S}
can be linked into a simple polygon.
We link the segments in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
as before, except that in the extended gadget
we link the segments as illustrated in Figure
5
right.
Then
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is linked into a simple polygonal chain starting and ending in the extended gadget.

We next prove the reverse implication.
Suppose that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
can be linked into a simple polygonal chain.
In the extended gadget,

B_{1}
can only see
A^{\prime}_{1}
and
a^{\prime}b^{\prime}
,

B_{2}
can only see
A_{2}
and
a^{\prime}b^{\prime}
,
and moreover for
i\in\{3,4,5,6\}
,

B_{i}
can only see
A_{i}
,
a^{\prime}b^{\prime}
, and
a^{\prime\prime}b^{\prime\prime}
.
Since
a^{\prime}b^{\prime}
and
a^{\prime\prime}b^{\prime\prime}
together can be linked to at most four other segments
in the polygonal chain,
and since the polygonal chain has only two vertices of degree one,
it follows that among the six segments
B_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq 6
,
each of
a^{\prime}b^{\prime}
and
a^{\prime\prime}b^{\prime\prime}
must be linked to two distinct segments,
and the polygonal chain must start and end at the other
two segments.
Thus the
12
segments
A^{\prime}_{1},B_{1},A_{2},B_{2},A_{3},B_{3},A_{4},B_{4},A_{5},B_{5},A_{6},B_{6}
,
and the two segments
a^{\prime}b^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}b^{\prime\prime}
,
are all linked locally to each other in the extended gadget,
and they are then linked to
o^{\prime}a
and
o^{\prime\prime}b

o^{\prime}
and
o^{\prime\prime}
.
As before,
in each gadget other than this extended gadget,

along the sequence

o^{\prime}a,A^{\prime}_{1},B_{1},a^{\prime}b^{\prime},B_{2},A_{2},o^{\prime\prime}b
.
Then, following the other visibility edges of the simple polygonal chain through
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,
which are outside and between the gadgets,

\mathcal{S}
can be linked into a simple polygon.

Thus

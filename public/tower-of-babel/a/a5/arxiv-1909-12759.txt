Device-independent certification of tensor products of quantum states using single-copy self-testing protocols
Ivan Šupić, Daniel Cavalcanti, Joseph Bowles

Abstract
Self-testing protocols are methods to determine the presence of shared entangled states in a device independent scenario, where no assumptions on the measurements involved in the protocol are made. A particular type of self-testing protocol, called parallel self-testing, can certify the presence of copies of a state, however such protocols typically suffer from the problem of requiring a number of measurements that increases with respect to the number of copies one aims to certify. Here we propose a procedure to transform single-copy self-testing protocols into a procedure that certifies the tensor product of an arbitrary number of (not necessarily equal) quantum states, without increasing the number of parties or measurement choices. Moreover, we prove that self-testing protocols that certify a state and rank-one measurements can always be parallelized to certify many copies of the state. Our results suggest a method to achieve device-independent unbounded randomness expansion with high-dimensional quantum states.



Introduction

— Bell nonlocality describes measurement correlations which are rigidly incompatible with the notion of local determinism
[
Bel64
,
BCP+14
]
. Namely, all local deterministic theories satisfy bounds—called Bell inequalities—which limit the strength of the correlations between measurement outcomes of two spatially distant and non-communicating systems. Interestingly, it is possible to violate such Bell inequalities in quantum experiments
[
HBD+15
,
SMSC+15
,
GVW+15
]
. Such violating correlations, called nonlocal, are closely related to quantum resources such as entanglement and measurement incompatibility,
essential for the development of modern day quantum technologies.

Bell nonlocality also plays a crucial role in so-called device-independent protocols. It turns out that the violation of a Bell inequality is a function of the observed correlations alone, regardless of the underlying physical realization. Thus, the sole observation of a Bell inequality violation witnesses the presence of both entanglement and incompatibility without having knowledge or making any assumptions about the underlying experimental implementation. Such an assumption-free verification is named
device-independent
and has a special significance in cryptographic scenarios
[
ABG+07
,
PAM+10
]
.

The maximal violation of some Bell inequalities can even imply the precise form of the underlying state and measurements. This can be seen as a device-independent certification, and has received the name of self-testing
[
vB20
,
MY04
]
. The simplest example of such phenomenon is the maximal violation of the Clauser-Horn-Shimony-Holt (Bell) inequality
[
CHSH69
]
, which can be used to self-test the maximally entangled pair of qubits and mutually unbiased local measurements
[
PR92
,
Tsi93
,
SW87
]
.

There exist nonlocal correlations that self-test several copies of a quantum state, a process called parallel self-testing. These self-testing protocols have immediate applications in situations where high amounts of entanglement is needed, such as randomness expansion
[
CY14
]
, parallel quantum key distribution
[
JMS20
,
Vid17
]
, delegated quantum computing
[
RUV13
,
CGJV19
]
, and universal entanglement certification
[
BŠCA18a
]
. One drawback of the first parallel self-testing protocols is that they require a number of local measurements that increases exponentially with the number of copies one wants to certify
[
WBMS16
,
Col17
,
CN16
,
McK17
,
BŠCA18b
]
. This fact increases the time-cost and the randomness consumption of the protocol (relevant for cryptographic applications). More recently, techniques to reduce the number of local measurements to poly(log(n)) and log(n) were found
[
NV17
,
NV18
,
CRSV18
,
OV16
,
BŠCA18b
]
.
There exist protocols to self-test entangled states of arbitrary dimension with a constant number of three or four inputs per party
[
YN13
,
CGS17
]
. These protocols, when applied to copies of quantum states require making joint measurements between the local subsystems of each copy, making them challenging from an experimental perspective.

In this manuscript, we show a procedure to combine different self-testing protocols into a protocol that self tests tensor products of quantum states, without increasing the number of required measurements. The combined protocol has the advantage of not requiring joint measurements among the copies. As a key application, we show a way of self-testing
n
copies of the two-qubit maximally entangled state using only two measurements per party (see Figure
1

Main idea of the method
– For simplicity, we explain how our method can transform the self-testing based on the Clauser-Horn-Shimony-Holt (CHSH) game into a self-test for
n
copies of a two-qubit maximally entangled state. The scenario consists of two space-like separated parties, Alice and Bob, making local measurements on a shared quantum system. Alice and Bob apply one of two measurements each, labeled
x=0,1
and
y=0,1
respectively, and their goal is to obtain outputs
a=0,1
and
b=0,1
such that
a\oplus b=x\cdot y
where
\oplus
is addition modulo 2. Their score is defined as
\omega=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{x,y}P(a\oplus b=x\cdot y|x,y)
,
i.e.
the probability of satisfying the winning condition averaged over a uniform choice of
x,y
. Alice and Bob can use quantum resources to increase their score. In that case the inputs denote the measurement choices, while the outputs are simply measurement results. When they share quantum state
\rho_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}
, Alice performs measurements
\{\mathsf{M}_{a|x}\}_{a,x}
, and Bob
\{\mathsf{N}_{b|y}\}_{b,y}
the probabilities
p(a,b|x,y)
satisfy the Born rule
p(a,b|x,y)=\operatorname{tr}\left[\mathsf{M}_{a|x}\otimes\mathsf{N}_{b|y}\rho_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}\right]
. The measurement observables are defined as
\mathrm{A}_{x}=\mathsf{M}_{0|x}-\mathsf{M}_{1|x}
and
\mathrm{B}_{y}=\mathsf{N}_{0|y}-\mathsf{N}_{1|y}
. If Alice and Bob make use of classical resources (or separable states), the best score they can achieve is
\omega=\frac{3}{4}
, which is equivalent to satisfying the CHSH Bell inequality

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{A}_{0}\mathrm{B}_{0}\rangle+\langle\mathrm{A}_{0}\mathrm{B}_{1}\rangle+\langle\mathrm{A}_{1}\mathrm{B}_{0}\rangle-\langle\mathrm{A}_{1}\mathrm{B}_{1}\rangle\leq 2,

(1)

where
\langle\mathrm{A}_{x}\mathrm{B}_{y}\rangle=\sum_{ab}(-1)^{a+b}P(a,b|x,y)
.
On the other hand, if they share a maximally entangled pair of qubits
{\lvert\phi^{+}\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[{\lvert 00\rangle}+{\lvert 11\rangle}]
, Alice’s measurements correspond to the Pauli observables
\mathrm{A}_{0}=\sigma_{\mathsf{Z}}
and
\mathrm{A}_{1}=\sigma_{\mathsf{X}}
and Bob’s measurements to
\mathrm{B}_{0}=(\sigma_{\mathsf{Z}}+\sigma_{\mathsf{X}})/\sqrt{2}\equiv\sigma_{+}
and
\mathrm{B}_{1}=(\sigma_{\mathsf{Z}}-\sigma_{\mathsf{X}})/\sqrt{2}\equiv\sigma_{-}
, they can achieve the score
\omega_{q}\approx 0.8536
. This strategy violates the CHSH Bell inequality to a value of
2\sqrt{2}
, and is the largest violation possible in quantum theory. This maximum value is also known to self-test the state
{\lvert\phi^{+}\rangle}
. Thus, up to a possible local change of basis and extra unused degrees of freedom, the state
{\lvert\phi^{+}\rangle}
is the only state that achieves this value.

The CHSH inequality can also be used to self-test
n
copies of
{\lvert\phi^{+}\rangle}^{\otimes n}
via parallel self-testing
[
Col17
,
McK17
]
. For that purpose Alice and Bob in each round receive
n
-bit inputs
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})
and
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})
, and return
n
-bit outputs
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n})
and
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{n})
. To self-test the state, Alice measures
\sigma_{\mathsf{Z}}
or
\sigma_{\mathsf{X}}
on her local qubit
i
depending on
x_{i}=0,1
, and Bob does similarly, measuring
\sigma_{+}
or
\sigma_{-}
depending on
y_{i}=0,1
and returning outcome
b_{i}
(see Fig.
1
b). The resulting correlations maximally violate
n
CHSH inequalities in parallel and imply Alice and Bob share a tensor product of
n
maximally entangled pairs of qubits.

We consider, in turn, a scenario in which each party has only two choices of measurements
x=0,1
and
y=0,1
(Fig.
1
c). If
x=0
Alice measures all of her qubits in the
\sigma_{\mathsf{Z}}
-basis, whereas if
x=1
she measures all of them in the
\sigma_{\mathsf{X}}
-basis. In both cases she returns an output consisting of
n
bits
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n})
corresponding to the outcomes of each of the
n
measurements. Bob proceeds similarly, measuring all his qubits in
\sigma_{+}
and
\sigma_{-}
bases and returning
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{n})
. For any pair
(a_{i},b_{i})
, the condition
P(a_{i}\oplus b_{i}=x\cdot y)=\omega_{q}
is satisfied, and one might naively think that this information alone is enough to conclude that the state is
{\lvert\phi^{+}\rangle}^{\otimes n}
. This would be a mistake however, as the following counter-example shows. Suppose Alice and Bob measure a single copy of
{\lvert\phi^{+}\rangle}
, obtaining outputs
a
,
b
such that
P(a\oplus b=x\cdot y)=\omega_{q}
. Then, they set
a_{i}=a
and
b_{i}=b
for all
i
, leading to
P(a_{i}\oplus b_{i}=x\cdot y)=\omega_{q}
for all
i
. Thus, one can achieve
P(a_{i}\oplus b_{i}=x\cdot y)=\omega_{q}
for all
i
with only a single copy of
{\lvert\phi^{+}\rangle}
.

One thus needs to consider more information than just the CHSH score of each copy. One possibility is to consider the marginal statistics, since in the parallel
n
-copy strategy one has
p(\mathbf{a}|x)=1/2^{n}
whereas in the single-copy strategy the local output bits
a_{i}
are always perfectly correlated (with a similar situation for Bob). However this will not work, since by using
n
bits of pre-shared randomness
(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}
) and post-processing their outputs according to
a_{i}=a+\lambda_{i}
and
b_{i}=b+\lambda_{i}
, the local output bits of the single-copy strategy can be decorrelated without affecting the CHSH scores. Notice however, that there is still a crucial difference between the two strategies that remains: in each round of the single-copy strategy, if the first pair satisfies
a_{1}+b_{1}=x\cdot y
, then all other pairs also satisfy
a_{i}+b_{i}=x\cdot y
. This is not the case for the
n
-copy strategy, where each pair has a probability
\omega_{q}
to satisfy the condition in each round, independent of the other pairs. It is precisely this difference that we use as an inspiration to design our self-testing protocol.

The main result
– Before stating our main result, let us define self-testing in a precise way. Consider a general bipartite Bell scenario, where
x,y\in\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}
and
{a},{b}\in\{0,1,\cdots,o-1\}
. Alice and Bob share the state
\rho_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}=\operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{P}}\left[{\lvert\psi\rangle}{\langle\psi\rvert}_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\right]
, with
{\lvert\psi\rangle}_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}
being any purification of
\rho_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}
.

The probabilities of obtaining outputs
a
and
b
, when the inputs are
x
and
y
, respectively, are given by

\displaystyle p(a,b|x,y)={\langle\psi\rvert}\mathsf{M}_{{a}|x}\otimes\mathsf{N}_{{b}|y}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathrm{P}}{\lvert\psi\rangle},

(2)

where
\{\mathsf{M}_{{a}|x}\}
and
\{\mathsf{N}_{{b}|y}\}
are Alice’s and Bob’s local measurement operators. We say that the probabilities
\{p(a,b|x,y)\}
self-test the reference experiment
\{{\lvert\psi^{\prime}\rangle}_{\mathrm{A}^{\prime}\mathrm{B}^{\prime}},\mathsf{M}^{\prime}_{{a}|x},\mathsf{N}^{\prime}_{{b}|y}\}
if observing them in an experiment implies the existence of a local unitary transformation
U=U_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{A}^{\prime}}\otimes V_{\mathrm{B}\mathrm{B}^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathrm{P}}
such that

\displaystyle U\left[{\lvert\psi\rangle}_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\otimes{\lvert 00\rangle}_{\mathrm{A}^{\prime}\mathrm{B}^{\prime}}\right]={\lvert\xi\rangle}_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\otimes{\lvert\psi^{\prime}\rangle}_{\mathrm{A}^{\prime}\mathrm{B}^{\prime}};

\displaystyle U\left[{\mathsf{M}}_{{a}|x}\otimes{\mathsf{N}}_{{b}|y}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathrm{P}}{\lvert\psi\rangle}_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\otimes{\lvert 00\rangle}_{\mathrm{A}^{\prime}\mathrm{B}^{\prime}}\right]=

(3)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad={\lvert\xi\rangle}_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\otimes\left({\mathsf{M}}^{\prime}_{{a}|x}\otimes{\mathsf{N}^{\prime}}_{{b}|y}{\lvert\psi^{\prime}\rangle}_{\mathrm{A}^{\prime}\mathrm{B}^{\prime}}\right).

These equations state that, up to ancillary degrees of freedom and local basis transformations, the state
{\lvert\psi\rangle}
is equivalent to
{\lvert\psi^{\prime}\rangle}
, and the measurements
\mathsf{M}_{{a}|x}
and
\mathsf{N}_{{b}|y}
act on
{\lvert\psi\rangle}
in the same way as
\mathsf{M}^{\prime}_{{a}|x}
and
\mathsf{N}^{\prime}_{{b}|y}
act on
{\lvert\psi^{\prime}\rangle}
. The state
{\lvert\xi\rangle}
is usually called the junk state and it can be seen as the state of additional degrees of freedom. Finally, let us define a Bell expression
\mathcal{I}
, as a linear combination of experimental probabilities
\mathcal{I}=\sum_{abxy}b_{a,b}^{x,y}p(a,b|x,y)
, where
b_{a,b}^{x,y}\in\mathbb{R}
.

.

Extensions
–
Theorem
1
is defined for bipartite Bell inequalities that feature an equal number of inputs per party. Here we present some more general extensions to this result that either do not require the knowledge of a specific Bell inequality, or allow one to self-test tensor products of different bipartite states.

Parallel self-testing protocols from full statistics
—While Theorem
1
refers to self-tests based on the maximal violation of some Bell inequality, it is worth noting that similar claims can be made for self-testing protocols based on the observation of a particular set of correlations. More precisely, we have the following result.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that the correlations
\{p(a,b|x,y)\}
self-test the reference state
{\lvert\psi^{\prime}\rangle}
, such that
p(a,b|x,y)>0
for all
a,b,x,y
. Then the correlations
\{p(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}|x,y)\}
obtained by performing the reference experiment
n
times in the parallel scheme of Fig.
1
c self-test the corresponding tensor product
{\lvert\psi^{\prime}\rangle}^{\otimes n}
.

In Appendix
B
we give an example of this technique that self-tests tensor products of the maximally entangled two-qubit state based on the correlations presented in
[
WWS16
]
.

Combining self-testing protocols
—The previous results show how it is possible to self-test a tensor product
{\lvert\psi^{\prime}\rangle}^{\otimes n}
of the same state
{\lvert\psi^{\prime}\rangle}
, starting from a given protocol to self-test the state
{\lvert\psi^{\prime}\rangle}
. In fact, the following describes how our construction can also be used to generate self-testing protocols for a tensor product of different states
\bigotimes_{i}{\lvert\psi_{i}^{\prime}\rangle}
.

Theorem 3
.

Consider a set of states
\{{\lvert\psi_{i}^{\prime}\rangle}\}
, each of which is self-tested through the maximal value of a corresponding Bell expression
\mathcal{I}_{i}
, where each
\mathcal{I}_{i}
features the same number of inputs for each party. Then the correlations
\{p(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}|x,y)\}
obtained by performing the reference experiments
n
times in the parallel scheme of Fig.
1
c self-test the state
\bigotimes_{i}{\lvert\psi_{i}^{\prime}\rangle}
.

The proof of this is almost identical to the proof of Theorem
1
, instead now in (
4
) the Bell expressions
\mathcal{I}_{i}
are different for each
i
; see Appendix
C
for more details. One implication of this is the possibility to self-test a tensor product of different partially entangled pairs of qubits with two inputs for each party, which can be achieved through the use of different tilted CHSH inequalities
[
BP15
]
.

All self-testing protocols can be paralellized
— Here we discuss conditions for parallel self-testing without aiming to keep the number of inputs constant. As mentioned in the introduction, a number of schemes to parallel self-test certain classes of states are known (see
[
Col17
,
McK17
,
CN16
,
KM18
,
BKM19
]
), where the number of inputs scales exponentially with the number of copies to be self-tested. Given these examples, one interesting question is thus whether any self-testing protocol for a state
{\lvert\psi\rangle}
can be ‘parallelized’ to self-test the tensor product
{\lvert\psi\rangle}^{\otimes n}
, at the expense of having exponentially many inputs. We give a positive answer to this question, answering an even a more general question of self-testing a tensor product
\bigotimes_{i}{\lvert\psi_{i}\rangle}
of different states, given that there are self-tests for the individual states
{\lvert\psi_{i}\rangle}
. Note that unlike the previous results, no restrictions are needed on the reference correlations (i.e. some probabilities can be zero).

Theorem 4
.

Consider a set of
n
bipartite Bell expressions
\{\mathcal{I}_{i}\}
characterised by
m_{i}
inputs and
o_{i}
outputs, respectively, such that the value
\mathcal{I}_{i}(\{p(a_{i},b_{i}|x_{i},y_{i})\})=\beta_{i}
self-tests the reference experiment
R_{i}=\{{\lvert\psi_{i}^{\prime}\rangle},\mathsf{M}^{\prime}_{a_{i}|x_{i}},\mathsf{N}^{\prime}_{b_{i}|y_{i}}\}
, where
\mathsf{M}^{\prime}_{a_{i}|x_{i}},\mathsf{N}^{\prime}_{b_{i}|y_{i}}
are rank-one projectors for each
i
. Then, the correlations obtained by performing the
R_{i}
’s in parallel as in Fig. 1b self-test the reference experiment
R_{n}=\{\bigotimes_{i=1}{\lvert\psi_{i}^{\prime}\rangle},\bigotimes_{i}\mathsf{M}^{\prime}_{a_{i}|x},\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{N}^{\prime}_{b_{i}|y}\}
.

The result is proven by constructing a single Bell expression whose maximal value self-tests
R_{n}
. For each pair
i
define the Bell value conditioned on particular choice of other inputs
x_{j},y_{j}
,
j\neq i
:

\mathcal{I}^{i}_{\mathbf{x}_{(i)}\mathbf{y}_{(i)}}=\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{(i)},\mathbf{b}_{(i)}}\mathcal{I}_{i}(p(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}|\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}))

(7)

where
\mathbf{a}_{(i)}=\{a_{1},\cdots a_{i-1},a_{i+1},\cdots,a_{n}\}
and similarly for
\mathbf{b}_{(i)}
,
\mathbf{x}_{(i)}
and
\mathbf{y}_{(i)}
. The Bell expression
\mathcal{I}_{i}
averaged over all other inputs
\mathbf{x}_{(i)}
and
\mathbf{y}_{(i)}

\mathcal{J}^{i}=\frac{1}{\prod_{j\neq i}m_{j}^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{(i)}\mathbf{y}_{(i)}}\mathcal{I}^{i}_{\mathbf{x}_{(i)}\mathbf{y}_{(i)}}.

(8)

If

achieves its maximal value, it means that for for every
\mathbf{x}_{i}
and
\mathbf{y}_{i}

\mathcal{I}^{i}_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{y}_{i}}
also achieves the maximal value, which is enough to prove self-testing of the reference experiment
R_{n}
. The proof of the theorem is given in Appendix
D

Application: unbounded randomness expansion.
– Self-testing is intrinsically related to device-independent randomness expansion
[
CY14
,
CK11
,
LLR+21
]
. This is because, once we certify that the system is in the state
{\lvert\psi\rangle}
, we can also conclude that any external system is uncorrelated to it. Thus, an external observer can not predict the outcomes of the measurements applied to the system of interest,
i.e.
the outcomes are random. In particular, if the state is maximally entangled of local dimension
d
, and the measurements applied to it are rank-one projective, the amount of random bits obtained per round is
\log_{2}(d)
. Notice, however, that in a Bell test some initial amount of randomness must be consumed in the choice of inputs. Thus, the efficiency of the randomness generation protocol is typically given by the the trade-off between the initial randomness consumed and the final randomness obtained. Our Theorem
1
applied to the CHSH inequality and self-testing
n
copies of a maximally entangled state shows that the best trade-off can be achieved from the noiseless correlations, where only one bit of randomness is used to generate
\log_{2}(d)
bits or randomness per round. We thus suspect that our results could improve extraction rates of practical device-independent randomness expansion protocols, although this would require a closer look at issues of noise robustness.

Discussion
–In this manuscript we introduced a new procedure useful in parallel self-testing. It allows to self-test highly entangled quantum states with a constant number of measurements. Such schemes are important in protocols for randomness expansion: a small amount of randomness can be expanded to a string of unbounded length. At the heart of our construction lies an interesting insight: independent Bell violations can be used to ensure independence of sources even when the measurements are perfectly correlated. There are several directions for future research on the topic. It would be interesting to explore noise robustness of our scheme. Furthermore, the condition for self-testing can be seen as the maximal violation of a non-linear Bell inequality. One might try to understand if this can be achieved using a single linear Bell inequality.

\displaystyle={\lvert\xi_{1}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\otimes\left({{\mathsf{M}}^{\prime}}_{a|x}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}}\otimes{{\mathsf{N}}^{\prime}}_{b|y}^{\mathrm{B}_{1}}\right){\lvert\psi^{\prime}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}\mathrm{B}_{1}}

(A5)

\displaystyle U_{i}\mathsf{M}_{\mathbf{a}_{i}|x}\otimes\mathsf{N}_{\mathbf{b}_{i}|y}{\lvert\psi\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\otimes{\lvert 00\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}\mathrm{B}_{1}}\cdots\otimes{\lvert 00\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{i}\mathrm{B}_{i}}

Appendix B
Example: parallel self-testing beyond Bell inequalities

Appendix C
Combining self-testing protocols to test a tensor product of different quantum states

Let us introduce the notion of compatible self-testing protocols, as those using the same number of inputs to self-tests the corresponding reference states. Here we outline how one can build a self-testing protocol certifying a tensor product of
n
different states, which can be independently self-tested by using compatible self-testing protocols.

Namely, the aim is to self-test a state of the form
\bigotimes_{i}{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{i}\rangle}
, knowing that every
{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{i}\rangle}
can be self-tested through observing the maximal violation
\beta_{i}
of the inequality

\mathcal{I}_{i}\equiv\sum_{a_{i},b_{i}=0}^{o_{i}-1}\sum_{x,y=0}^{m-1}b_{i,a_{i}b_{i}}^{xy}p_{i}(a_{i}b_{i}|xy)\leq\beta_{i}

(C62)

Let us introduce the generalized conditional Bell value:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{b}_{i}}^{i+1}=\sum_{a_{i+1}b_{i+1}xy}\frac{b_{i+1,a_{i+1}b_{i+1}}^{xy}}{p(\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{b}_{i}|xy)}\operatorname{tr}\left[\left({\mathsf{M}}_{\mathbf{a}_{i},a_{i+1}|x}\otimes{\mathsf{N}}_{\mathbf{b}_{i},b_{i+1}|y}\right)\rho_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}\otimes{\lvert 00\rangle\!\langle 00\rvert}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}\mathrm{B}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{A}_{i}\mathrm{B}_{i}}\right].

(C63)

The conditions for self-testing
\bigotimes_{i}{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{i}\rangle}
by using only
m
different inputs per party are the following

•

\mathcal{I}_{1}=\beta_{1}
,

•

\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{b}_{i}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{a}_{i}\mathbf{b}_{i}}^{i+1}=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i}m_{j}\right)^{2}\beta_{i+1}
.

The self-testing proof goes along the same lines as the proof for Theorem 1. The only difference is that for every
i
the second condition corresponds to the maximal violation of
\mathcal{I}_{i}
by the junk state appearing in the self-testing statement for
i-1
.

Appendix D
Proof of Theorem
4

In this section we give a proof of Theorem
4
which deals with combining two or more incompatible self-tests,
i.e.
self-tests corresponding to the scenarios with different inputs and/or output size. The general scenario is as follows: the protocol
\mathcal{S}_{i}
can be used to self-test the state
{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{i}\rangle}
in the scenario where each party has
m_{i}
inputs denoted with
x_{i},y_{i}
and
o_{i}
outputs denoted as
a_{i},b_{i}
. If the correlations
p_{i}(a_{i}b_{i}|x_{i}y_{i})
satisfy the self-testing conditions given by the protocol
\mathcal{S}_{i}
for each
i
then there is isometry mapping the physical state to the
\bigotimes_{i}{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{i}\rangle}
. This can be seen as a generalization of parallel self-testing, where usually the reference state is an
n
-fold tensor product of some state.

According to the theorem conditions the state
{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{i}\rangle}
, for
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
, is self-tested through achieving the maximal violation of the Bell inequality
\mathcal{I}_{i}
, evaluated as

\mathcal{I}_{i}\equiv\sum_{a_{i}b_{i}x_{i}y_{i}}b_{i,a_{i}b_{i}}^{x_{i}y_{i}}p_{i}(a_{i}b_{i}|x_{i}y_{i})=\beta_{i}.

(D64)

The self-testing scenario is as follows: in every round Alice and Bob receive
n
classical inputs each, denoted with
\mathbf{x}=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\},\mathbf{y}=\{y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\}
, where
x_{i},y_{i}\in\{0,1,\cdots,m_{i}-1\}
. They return strings
\mathbf{a}=\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\},\mathbf{b}=\{b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}\}
, where
a_{i},b_{i}\in\{0,1,\cdots,o_{i}-1\}
. The measurement operators are denoted by
\mathsf{M}_{\mathbf{a}|\mathbf{x}}
for Alice and
\mathsf{N}_{\mathbf{b}|\mathbf{y}}
for Bob. Let us introduce the following notation

\displaystyle{\mathsf{M}}_{a_{i}|x_{i}}=\frac{1}{\prod_{j|j\neq i}m_{i}}\sum_{\mathbf{a}_{(i)},\mathbf{x}_{(i)}}\mathsf{M}_{\mathbf{a}|\mathbf{x}},\qquad{\mathsf{N}}_{b_{i}|y_{i}}=\frac{1}{\prod_{j|j\neq i}m_{i}}\sum_{\mathbf{b}_{(i)},\mathbf{y}_{(i)}}\mathsf{N}_{\mathbf{b}|\mathbf{y}}

(D65)

These operators are valid measurement operators, as a sum of positive operators they are positive and they satisfy completeness relations
\sum_{a_{i}}{\mathsf{M}}_{a_{i}|x_{i}}=\mathbbm{1}
and
\sum_{b_{j}}{\mathsf{N}}_{b_{j}|y_{j}}=\mathbbm{1}
, for all
x_{i}
and
y_{j}
. Let us define Bell-like expressions

\mathcal{I}^{i}_{\mathbf{x}_{(i)}\mathbf{y}_{(i)}}=\sum_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},x_{i},y_{i}}b_{i,a_{i}b_{i}}^{x_{i}y_{i}}p_{i}(\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}|\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})

(D66)

The Bell violation for the
i
-th inequality is a normalized sum of values given in
D66
:

\mathcal{J}^{i}=\frac{1}{\prod_{j|j\neq i}m_{j}^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{(i)},\mathbf{y}_{(i)}}\mathcal{I}^{i}_{\mathbf{x}_{(i)},\mathbf{y}_{(i)}},

(D67)

Let us set
i=1
. The Bell violation
\mathcal{J}^{1}
can be modelled as

\mathcal{J}^{1}=\sum_{a_{1},b_{1},x_{1},y_{1}}b_{1,a_{1}b_{1}}^{x_{1}y_{1}}{\langle\psi\rvert}{\mathsf{M}}_{a_{1}|x_{1}}\otimes{\mathsf{N}}_{b_{1}|y_{1}}{\lvert\psi\rangle}

(D68)

If the inequality
\mathcal{J}^{1}
is maximally violated there exist a local unitary
U_{1}=U_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{A}_{1}}\otimes U_{\mathrm{B}\mathrm{B}_{1}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathrm{P}}
such that

\displaystyle U_{1}\left[{\lvert\psi\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\otimes{\lvert 00\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}\mathrm{B}_{1}}\right]

\displaystyle={\lvert\xi_{1}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\otimes{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{1}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}\mathrm{B}_{1}}

(D69)

\displaystyle U_{1}\left[\left({\mathsf{M}}_{a_{1}|x_{1}}\otimes{\mathsf{N}}_{b_{1}|y_{1}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathrm{P}}{\lvert\psi\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\right)\otimes{\lvert 00\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}\mathrm{B}_{1}}\right]

\displaystyle={\lvert\xi_{1}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\otimes\left(\mathsf{M}^{\prime}_{a_{1}|x_{1}}\otimes\mathsf{N}^{\prime}_{b_{1}|y_{1}}{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{1}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}\mathrm{B}_{1}}\right).

(D70)

This implies the following relations

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{A}_{1}}\left[{\mathsf{M}}_{a_{1}|x_{1}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}}\right]U_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{A}_{1}}^{\dagger}=\mathbbm{1}^{\mathrm{A}}\otimes{\mathsf{M}^{\prime}}_{a_{1}|x_{1}}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}},

\displaystyle\quad U_{\mathrm{B}\mathrm{B}_{1}}\left[{\mathsf{N}}_{b_{1}|y_{1}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}^{\mathrm{B}_{1}}\right]U_{\mathrm{B}\mathrm{B}_{1}}^{\dagger}=\mathbbm{1}^{\mathrm{B}}\otimes{\mathsf{N}^{\prime}}_{b_{1}|y_{1}}^{\mathrm{B}_{1}},

(D71)

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{A}_{1}}\left[{\mathsf{M}}_{a_{1},a_{2}|x_{1},x_{2}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}}\right]U_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{A}_{1}}^{\dagger}={\mathsf{K}}_{a_{1},a_{2}|x_{1},x_{2}}^{\mathrm{A}}\otimes{\mathsf{M}^{\prime}}_{a_{1}|x_{1}}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}},

\displaystyle\quad U_{\mathrm{B}\mathrm{B}_{1}}\left[{\mathsf{N}}_{b_{1},b_{2}|y_{1},y_{2}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}^{\mathrm{B}_{1}}\right]U_{\mathrm{B}\mathrm{B}_{1}}^{\dagger}={\mathsf{L}}_{b_{1},b_{2}|y_{1},y_{2}}^{\mathrm{B}}\otimes{\mathsf{N}^{\prime}}_{b_{1}|y_{1}}^{\mathrm{B}_{1}}

(D72)

where

{\mathsf{M}}_{a_{1},a_{2}|x_{1},x_{2}}=\frac{1}{m_{3}m_{4}\cdots m_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{3},a_{4},\cdots,a_{n}\\
x_{3},x_{4},\cdots,x_{n}\end{subarray}}\mathsf{M}_{\mathbf{a}|\mathbf{x}}\qquad{\mathsf{N}}_{b_{1},b_{2}|y_{1},y_{2}}=\frac{1}{m_{3}m_{4}\cdots m_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{3},b_{4},\cdots,b_{n}\\
y_{3},y_{3},\cdots,y_{n}\end{subarray}}\mathsf{N}_{\mathbf{b}|\mathbf{y}}

(D73)

and
{\mathsf{K}}_{a_{1},a_{2}|x_{1},x_{2}}
and
{\mathsf{L}}_{b_{1},b_{2}|y_{1},y_{2}}
are positive operators satisfying

\sum_{a_{2}}{\mathsf{K}}_{a_{1},a_{2}|x_{1},x_{2}}=\mathbbm{1},\quad\sum_{b_{2}}{\mathsf{L}}_{b_{1},b_{2}|y_{1},y_{2}}=\mathbbm{1},\qquad\textrm{for}\;\textrm{all}\quad x_{2},y_{2},x_{1},y_{1},a_{1},b_{1}

(D74)

In the second step the maximal violation of the inequality
\mathcal{J}^{2}
can be modelled as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{1}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}\\
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\end{subarray}}b_{2,a_{2}b_{2}}^{x_{2}y_{2}}{\langle\psi\rvert}{\mathsf{M}}_{a_{1},a_{2}|x_{1},x_{2}}\otimes{\mathsf{N}}_{b_{1},b_{2}|y_{1},y_{2}}{\lvert\psi\rangle}

(D75)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{1}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}\\
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\end{subarray}}b_{2,a_{2}b_{2}}^{x_{2}y_{2}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rvert}{\mathsf{K}}_{a_{1},a_{2}|x_{1},x_{2}}\otimes{\mathsf{L}}_{b_{1},b_{2}|y_{1},y_{2}}{\lvert\xi_{1}\rangle}{\langle\psi^{\prime}_{1}\rvert}{\mathsf{M}^{\prime}}_{a_{1}|x_{1}}\otimes{\mathsf{N}^{\prime}}_{b_{1}|y_{1}}{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{1}\rangle}

(D76)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{1}^{2}}\sum_{a_{1},b_{1},x_{1},y_{1}}{\langle\psi^{\prime}_{1}\rvert}{\mathsf{M}^{\prime}}_{a_{1}|x_{1}}\otimes{\mathsf{N}^{\prime}}_{b_{1}|y_{1}}{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{1}\rangle}\sum_{a_{2},b_{2},x_{2},y_{2}}b_{2,a_{2}b_{2}}^{x_{2}y_{2}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rvert}{\mathsf{K}}_{a_{1},a_{2}|x_{1},x_{2}}\otimes{\mathsf{L}}_{b_{1},b_{2}|y_{1},y_{2}}{\lvert\xi_{1}\rangle}

(D77)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{1},b_{1},x_{1},y_{1}}\frac{p_{1}(a_{1},b_{1}|x_{1},y_{1})}{m_{1}^{2}}\sum_{a_{2},b_{2},x_{2},y_{2}}b_{2,a_{2}b_{2}}^{x_{2}y_{2}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rvert}{\mathsf{K}}_{a_{1},a_{2}|x_{1},x_{2}}\otimes{\mathsf{L}}_{b_{1},b_{2}|y_{1},y_{2}}{\lvert\xi_{1}\rangle}

(D78)

Since numbers
p_{1}(a_{1}b_{1}|x_{1}y_{1})/m_{1}^{2}
are positive and sum to one, and all
{\mathsf{K}}_{a_{1},a_{2}|x_{1},x_{2}}
and
{\mathsf{L}}_{b_{1},b_{2}|y_{1},y_{2}}
are valid measurement operators the observation
\mathcal{J}^{2}=\beta_{2}
implies that

\sum_{a_{2},b_{2},x_{2},y_{2}}b_{2,a_{2}b_{2}}^{x_{2}y_{2}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rvert}{\mathsf{K}}_{a_{1},a_{2}|x_{1},x_{2}}\otimes{\mathsf{L}}_{b_{1},b_{2}|y_{1},y_{2}}{\lvert\xi_{1}\rangle}=\beta_{2}

(D79)

for all
a_{1},b_{1},x_{1},y_{1}
. Let us define operators

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{a_{2}|x_{2}}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{m_{1}}\sum_{a_{1},x_{1}}{\mathsf{K}}_{a_{1},a_{2}|x_{1},x_{2}},\qquad{\mathsf{L}}_{b_{2}|y_{2}}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{m_{1}}\sum_{b_{1},y_{1}}{\mathsf{L}}_{b_{1},b_{2}|y_{1},y_{2}}

(D80)

These operators are valid measurement operators, and they satisfy

\sum_{a_{2},b_{2},x_{2},y_{2}}b_{2,a_{2}b_{2}}^{x_{2}y_{2}}{\langle\xi_{1}\rvert}{\mathsf{K}}_{a_{2}|x_{2}}^{(2)}\otimes{\mathsf{L}}_{b_{2}|y_{2}}^{(2)}{\lvert\xi_{1}\rangle}=\beta_{2}.

(D81)

The maximal violation
\mathcal{J}^{2}
implies that there exists a local unitary
U_{2}=U_{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{A}_{2}}\otimes U_{\mathrm{B}\mathrm{B}_{2}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathrm{P}}
such that

\displaystyle U_{2}\left[{\lvert\xi_{1}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}\otimes{\lvert 00\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle={\lvert\xi_{2}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}\otimes{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{2}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{2}}

(D82)

\displaystyle U_{2}\left[({\mathsf{K}}_{a_{2}|x_{2}}^{(2)}\otimes{\mathsf{L}}_{b_{2}|y_{2}}^{(2)}{\lvert\xi_{1}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}})\otimes{\lvert 00\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle={\lvert\xi_{2}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}\otimes(\mathsf{M}^{\prime}_{a_{2}|x_{2}}\otimes\mathsf{N}^{\prime}_{b_{2}|y_{2}}{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{2}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{2}}).

(D83)

Combining (
D69
) and (
D82
) we get a self-testing statement for a tensor product of two different states

U_{2}\circ U_{1}\left[{\lvert\psi\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\otimes{\lvert 0000\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}\mathrm{A}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{1}\mathrm{B}_{2}}\right]={\lvert\xi_{2}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\otimes{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{1}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}\mathrm{B}_{1}}\otimes{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{2}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{2}\mathrm{B}_{2}}.

(D84)

The process can be further repeated for
i=3
to
i=n
, reaching the final statement

U_{n}\circ\cdots\circ U_{1}\left[{\lvert\psi\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\otimes{\lvert 0000\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{A}_{n}\mathrm{B}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{B}_{n}}\right]={\lvert\xi_{n}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{P}}\otimes{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{1}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{1}\mathrm{B}_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\lvert\psi^{\prime}_{n}\rangle}^{\mathrm{A}_{n}\mathrm{B}_{n}}.

(D85)

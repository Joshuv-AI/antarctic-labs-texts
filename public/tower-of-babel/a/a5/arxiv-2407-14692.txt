Some three-dimensional non-nilpotent Leibniz algebras: automorphism groups
L. A. Kurdachenko, O. O. Pypka

Abstract
The article presents the structure of the automorphism groups of two types of non-nilpotent Leibniz algebras with a dimension of 3.



Key Words:

Leibniz algebra, automorphism group.

2020 MSC:

17A32, 17A36.

1
Introduction.

A
Leibniz algebra
is defined as a vector space
L
over a field
F
with a bilinear binary operation
[,]
that satisfies the following condition:

[x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]+[y,[x,z]]

for all
x,y,z\in L

[
2
]
. The term “Leibniz algebra” first appeared in
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
. We will not provide all the definitions related to Leibniz algebras here. They can be found, for example, in the monographs
[
1
]
or
[
10
]
.

Despite the many analogies between Leibniz algebras and Lie algebras, these types of algebras differ significantly from each other (see, for example, survey articles
[
3
,
4
,
12
,
17
]
).

A bijective linear transformation
f
of a Leibniz algebra
L
is called an
automorphism
of the algebra
L
if it satisfies the following condition:

f([x,y])=[f(x),f(y)]

for all
x,y\in L
. It is easy to prove that the set
Aut_{[,]}(L)
of all automorphisms of
L
is a group by a multiplication (see, for example,
[
9
]
).

Describing the automorphism groups of algebraic structures is a fundamental and essential problem in algebra. Leibniz algebras are no exception. It is prudent to begin by exploring the automorphism groups of those Leibniz algebras whose structures are already well understood.

As of today, the structure of the automorphism groups of one-generated Leibniz algebras
[
9
,
13
]
, two-dimensional Leibniz algebras
[
11
]
, and some three-dimensional Leibniz algebras
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
11
,
16
]
is known.

This article extends our research to two other types of three-dimensional non-nilpotent Leibniz algebras. It should be noted that the most comprehensive description of three-dimensional Leibniz algebras over arbitrary fields can be found in
[
8
]
.

2
The automorphism group of
L_{1}
.

Let us consider the following type of non-nilpotent Leibniz algebras:

\displaystyle L_{1}=Fe_{1}\oplus Fe_{2}\oplus Fe_{3}\ \mbox{where }[e_{1},e_{1}]=[e_{1},e_{3}]=e_{3},\ [e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{2},

\displaystyle[e_{2},e_{1}]=[e_{2},e_{2}]=[e_{2},e_{3}]=[e_{3},e_{1}]=[e_{3},e_{2}]=[e_{3},e_{3}]=0.

In other words,
L_{1}
is the sum of the ideal
A_{2}=Fe_{2}
and the non-nilpotent one-generated Leibniz algebra
A_{1}=Fe_{1}\oplus Fe_{3}
, which has dimension 2,
[A_{1},A_{2}]=A_{2}
,
Leib(L_{1})=Fe_{2}\oplus Fe_{3}=[L_{1},L_{1}]=\zeta^{\mathrm{left}}(L_{1})
,
\zeta^{\mathrm{right}}(L_{1})=\zeta(L_{1})=\langle 0\rangle
.

The following theorem illustrates the structure of the automorphism group for these Leibniz algebras.

Denote by
\Xi
the canonical monomorphism of
Aut_{[,]}(L)
in
M_{3}(F)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
G
be an automorphism group of the Leibniz algebra
L_{1}
. Then
G
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
GL_{3}(F)
that consists of matrices of the form:

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
\alpha_{2}&\beta_{2}&\alpha_{2}\\
\alpha_{3}&\beta_{3}&1+\alpha_{3}\end{array}\right),

\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\in F
. Furthermore,
G
is isomorphic to the group
GL_{2}(F)
. Moreover, it is a product of two subgroups
C_{1}=C_{G}(e_{1})
,
C_{2}=C_{G}(e_{2})
, and
\Xi(C_{1})
is the set of matrices of the form

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&\beta_{2}&0\\
0&\beta_{3}&1\end{array}\right),

\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\in F
, and
\Xi(C_{2})
is the set of matrices of the form

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
\alpha_{2}&1&\alpha_{2}\\
\alpha_{3}&0&1+\alpha_{3}\end{array}\right),

\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in F
. A subgroup
C_{1}
is a semidirect product of a normal subgroup
C_{3}
, which is isomorphic to the additive group of a field
F
, and a subgroup
C_{4}
, which is isomorphic to the multiplicative group of a field
F
; a subgroup
C_{2}
is a semidirect product of a normal subgroup, which is isomorphic to the additive group of a field
F
, and a subgroup that is isomorphic to the multiplicative group of a field
F
.

Proof
. Let
L=L_{1}
and let
f\in Aut_{[,]}(L)
. By
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
,
f([L,L])=[L,L]
, so that

\displaystyle f(e_{1})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}e_{1}+\alpha_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{3}e_{3},

\displaystyle f(e_{2})

\displaystyle=\beta_{2}e_{2}+\beta_{3}e_{3},

\displaystyle f(e_{3})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{2}e_{2}+\gamma_{3}e_{3}.

Then

\displaystyle f(e_{3})

\displaystyle=f([e_{1},e_{1}])=[f(e_{1}),f(e_{1})]

\displaystyle=[\alpha_{1}e_{1}+\alpha_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{3}e_{3},\alpha_{1}e_{1}+\alpha_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{3}e_{3}]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}^{2}[e_{1},e_{1}]+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}[e_{1},e_{2}]+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}[e_{1},e_{3}]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}^{2}e_{3}+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}e_{3}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}e_{2}+(\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3})e_{3},

\displaystyle f(e_{3})

\displaystyle=f([e_{1},e_{3}])=[f(e_{1}),f(e_{3})]

\displaystyle=[\alpha_{1}e_{1}+\alpha_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{3}e_{3},\gamma_{2}e_{2}+\gamma_{3}e_{3}]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\gamma_{2}[e_{1},e_{2}]+\alpha_{1}\gamma_{3}[e_{1},e_{3}]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\gamma_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{1}\gamma_{3}e_{3},

\displaystyle f(e_{2})

\displaystyle=f([e_{1},e_{2}])=[f(e_{1}),f(e_{2})]

\displaystyle=[\alpha_{1}e_{1}+\alpha_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{3}e_{3},\beta_{2}e_{2}+\beta_{3}e_{3}]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\beta_{2}[e_{1},e_{2}]+\alpha_{1}\beta_{3}[e_{1},e_{3}]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\beta_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{1}\beta_{3}e_{3}.

Thus, we can see that

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}e_{2}+(\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3})e_{3}=\alpha_{1}\gamma_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{1}\gamma_{3}e_{3}=\gamma_{2}e_{2}+\gamma_{3}e_{3},

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\beta_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{1}\beta_{3}e_{3}=\beta_{2}e_{2}+\beta_{3}e_{3}.

It follows that

\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{1}\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{2},\ \alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}=\alpha_{1}\gamma_{3}=\gamma_{3},\ \alpha_{1}\beta_{2}=\beta_{2},\ \alpha_{1}\beta_{3}=\beta_{3}.

If we suppose that
\alpha_{1}=0
, then
f(e_{1})\in Fe_{2}\oplus Fe_{3}
. Since
f(e_{2}),f(e_{3})\in Fe_{2}\oplus Fe_{3}
, we obtain that
f(L)\leq Fe_{2}\oplus Fe_{3}
– in particular,
f(L)\neq L
– and we obtain a contradiction with the fact that
f
is an automorphism of
L
. Hence,
\alpha_{1}\neq 0
. It follows that
\alpha_{2}=\gamma_{2}
.

If we suppose that both
\beta_{2}=\gamma_{2}=0
, then
f(e_{2})\in Fe_{3}
and
f(e_{3})\in Fe_{3}
, so that
f([L,L])\leq Fe_{3}
. On the other hand,
dim_{F}([L,L])=2
, and we, again, obtain a contradiction with the fact that
f
is an automorphism of
L
. Hence,
(\beta_{2},\gamma_{2})\neq(0,0)
. It follows that
\alpha_{1}=1
and then
1+\alpha_{3}=\gamma_{3}
.

Denote by
\Xi
the canonical monomorphism of
Aut_{[,]}(L)
in
M_{3}(F)
. Then
\Xi(f)
is the following matrix:

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
\alpha_{2}&\beta_{2}&\alpha_{2}\\
\alpha_{3}&\beta_{3}&1+\alpha_{3}\end{array}\right),

\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\in F
.

Let
f
be a linear transformation of
L
, having in a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
the matrix above. Let
x,y
be the arbitrary elements of
L
,
x=\xi_{1}e_{1}+\xi_{2}e_{2}+\xi_{3}e_{3}
,
y=\eta_{1}e_{1}+\eta_{2}e_{2}+\eta_{3}e_{3}
, where
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3}\in F
. Then

\displaystyle[x,y]

\displaystyle=[\xi_{1}e_{1}+\xi_{2}e_{2}+\xi_{3}e_{3},\eta_{1}e_{1}+\eta_{2}e_{2}+\eta_{3}e_{3}]

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}\eta_{1}[e_{1},e_{1}]+\xi_{1}\eta_{2}[e_{1},e_{2}]+\xi_{1}\eta_{3}[e_{1},e_{3}]

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}\eta_{1}e_{3}+\xi_{1}\eta_{2}e_{2}+\xi_{1}\eta_{3}e_{3}

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}\eta_{2}e_{2}+(\xi_{1}\eta_{1}+\xi_{1}\eta_{3})e_{3}=\xi_{1}\eta_{2}e_{2}+\xi_{1}(\eta_{1}+\eta_{3})e_{3},

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=f(\xi_{1}e_{1}+\xi_{2}e_{2}+\xi_{3}e_{3})=\xi_{1}f(e_{1})+\xi_{2}f(e_{2})+\xi_{3}f(e_{3})

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}(e_{1}+\alpha_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{3}e_{3})+\xi_{2}(\beta_{2}e_{2}+\beta_{3}e_{3})+\xi_{3}(\alpha_{2}e_{2}+(1+\alpha_{3})e_{3})

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}e_{1}+(\xi_{1}\alpha_{2}+\xi_{2}\beta_{2}+\xi_{3}\alpha_{2})e_{2}+(\xi_{1}\alpha_{3}+\xi_{2}\beta_{3}+\xi_{3}+\xi_{3}\alpha_{3})e_{3},

\displaystyle f(y)

\displaystyle=\eta_{1}e_{1}+(\eta_{1}\alpha_{2}+\eta_{2}\beta_{2}+\eta_{3}\alpha_{2})e_{2}+(\eta_{1}\alpha_{3}+\eta_{2}\beta_{3}+\eta_{3}+\eta_{3}\alpha_{3})e_{3},

\displaystyle f([x,y])

\displaystyle=f(\xi_{1}\eta_{2}e_{2}+\xi_{1}(\eta_{1}+\eta_{3})e_{3})=\xi_{1}\eta_{2}f(e_{2})+\xi_{1}(\eta_{1}+\eta_{3})f(e_{3})

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}\eta_{2}(\beta_{2}e_{2}+\beta_{3}e_{3})+\xi_{1}(\eta_{1}+\eta_{3})(\alpha_{2}e_{2}+(1+\alpha_{3})e_{3})

\displaystyle=(\xi_{1}\eta_{2}\beta_{2}+\xi_{1}(\eta_{1}+\eta_{3})\alpha_{2})e_{2}+(\xi_{1}\eta_{2}\beta_{3}+\xi_{1}(\eta_{1}+\eta_{3})(1+\alpha_{3}))e_{3},

\displaystyle[f(x),f(y)]

\displaystyle=[\xi_{1}e_{1}+(\xi_{1}\alpha_{2}+\xi_{2}\beta_{2}+\xi_{3}\alpha_{2})e_{2}+(\xi_{1}\alpha_{3}+\xi_{2}\beta_{3}+\xi_{3}+\xi_{3}\alpha_{3})e_{3},

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \eta_{1}e_{1}+(\eta_{1}\alpha_{2}+\eta_{2}\beta_{2}+\eta_{3}\alpha_{2})e_{2}+(\eta_{1}\alpha_{3}+\eta_{2}\beta_{3}+\eta_{3}+\eta_{3}\alpha_{3})e_{3}]

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}\eta_{1}[e_{1},e_{1}]+\xi_{1}(\eta_{1}\alpha_{2}+\eta_{2}\beta_{2}+\eta_{3}\alpha_{2})[e_{1},e_{2}]

\displaystyle+\xi_{1}(\eta_{1}\alpha_{3}+\eta_{2}\beta_{3}+\eta_{3}+\eta_{3}\alpha_{3})[e_{1},e_{3}]

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}\eta_{1}e_{3}+\xi_{1}(\eta_{1}\alpha_{2}+\eta_{2}\beta_{2}+\eta_{3}\alpha_{2})e_{2}+\xi_{1}(\eta_{1}\alpha_{3}+\eta_{2}\beta_{3}+\eta_{3}+\eta_{3}\alpha_{3})e_{3}

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}(\eta_{1}\alpha_{2}+\eta_{2}\beta_{2}+\eta_{3}\alpha_{2})e_{2}+\xi_{1}(\eta_{1}+\eta_{1}\alpha_{3}+\eta_{2}\beta_{3}+\eta_{3}+\eta_{3}\alpha_{3})e_{3}.

We can see that
f([x,y])=[f(x),f(y)]
. It follows that a linear transformation
f
having in a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
the above matrix is an automorphism of a Leibniz algebra.

\Phi
the mapping of
\Xi(G)
in
GL_{2}(F)
defined by the rule:

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
\alpha_{2}&\beta_{2}&\alpha_{2}\\
\alpha_{3}&\beta_{3}&1+\alpha_{3}\end{array}\right)\rightarrow\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\beta_{2}&\alpha_{2}\\
\beta_{3}&1+\alpha_{3}\end{array}\right).

We have:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
\alpha_{2}&\beta_{2}&\alpha_{2}\\
\alpha_{3}&\beta_{3}&1+\alpha_{3}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
\gamma_{2}&\kappa_{2}&\gamma_{2}\\
\gamma_{3}&\kappa_{3}&1+\gamma_{3}\end{array}\right)=

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}\gamma_{2}+\alpha_{2}\gamma_{3}&\beta_{2}\kappa_{2}+\alpha_{2}\kappa_{3}&\beta_{2}\gamma_{2}+\alpha_{2}(1+\gamma_{3})\\
\alpha_{3}+\beta_{3}\gamma_{2}+(1+\alpha_{3})\gamma_{3}&\beta_{3}\kappa_{2}+(1+\alpha_{3})\kappa_{3}&\beta_{3}\gamma_{2}+(1+\alpha_{3})(1+\gamma_{3})\end{array}\right)

and

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\beta_{2}&\alpha_{2}\\
\beta_{3}&1+\alpha_{3}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\kappa_{2}&\gamma_{2}\\
\kappa_{3}&1+\gamma_{3}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\beta_{2}\kappa_{2}+\alpha_{2}\kappa_{3}&\beta_{2}\gamma_{2}+\alpha_{2}(1+\gamma_{3})\\
\beta_{3}\kappa_{2}+(1+\alpha_{3})\kappa_{3}&\beta_{3}\gamma_{2}+(1+\alpha_{3})(1+\gamma_{3})\end{array}\right)

These equalities show that a mapping
\Phi
is a homomorphism. Clearly,
Ker(\Phi)
consists only of the identity matrix. Finally, let

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sigma_{11}&\sigma_{12}\\
\sigma_{21}&\sigma_{22}\end{array}\right)

be the arbitrary element of
GL_{2}(F)
. Consider the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
\sigma_{12}&\sigma_{11}&\sigma_{12}\\
\sigma_{22}-1&\sigma_{21}&\sigma_{22}\end{array}\right).

This matrix is a preimage of the matrix above by the mapping
\Phi
. Moreover, both these matrices have the same determinants. It follows that the last matrix belongs to
\Xi(G)
, so that a mapping
\Phi
is an isomorphism.

Let
C_{1}=C_{G}(e_{1})
,
C_{2}=C_{G}(e_{2})
. Then
\Xi(C_{1})
is the set of matrices of the form

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&\beta_{2}&0\\
0&\beta_{3}&1\end{array}\right),

\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\in F
, and
\Xi(C_{2})
is the set of matrices of the form

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
\alpha_{2}&1&\alpha_{2}\\
\alpha_{3}&0&1+\alpha_{3}\end{array}\right),

\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in F
.
We have:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
\sigma_{2}&1&\sigma_{2}\\
\sigma_{3}&0&1+\sigma_{3}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\

\alpha_{2}&\beta_{2}&\alpha_{2}\\

is an arbitrary matrix of

,

is a product of the subgroups

and

.

, and a subgroup

. Moreover,

C_{3}
and

.

3
The automorphism group of
L_{2}
.

Let us consider the second type of non-nilpotent Leibniz algebras:

\displaystyle L_{2}=Fe_{1}\oplus Fe_{2}\oplus Fe_{3}\ \mbox{where }[e_{1},e_{1}]=[e_{1},e_{3}]=e_{3},

\displaystyle[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{2}+\lambda e_{3},\ 0\neq\lambda\in F,

\displaystyle[e_{2},e_{1}]=[e_{2},e_{2}]=[e_{2},e_{3}]=[e_{3},e_{1}]=[e_{3},e_{2}]=[e_{3},e_{3}]=0.

In other words,
L_{2}
is the sum of the ideal
Leib(L_{2})=Fe_{2}\oplus Fe_{3}
and the non-nilpotent one-generated Leibniz algebra
A_{1}=Fe_{1}\oplus Fe_{3}
of dimension 2,
[A_{1},Leib(L_{2})]=Leib(L_{2})
,
Leib(L_{2})=[L_{2},L_{2}]=\zeta^{\mathrm{left}}(L_{2})
,
\zeta^{\mathrm{right}}(L_{2})=\zeta(L_{2})=\langle 0\rangle
.

The following theorem illustrates the structure of the automorphism group for these Leibniz algebras.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be an automorphism group of the Leibniz algebra
L_{2}
. Then
G
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
GL_{3}(F)
that consists of matrices of the form

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&\beta_{1}&0\\
\beta_{1}-1&\beta_{2}&\beta_{1}\end{array}\right),

\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in F
. Furthermore,
G
is abelian,
G=C\times A
such that
\Xi(C)
is the set of matrices of the form

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&\sigma&1\end{array}\right),

\sigma\in F
, and
\Xi(A)
is the set of matrices of the form

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&\beta&0\\
\beta-1&0&\beta\end{array}\right),

\beta\in F
. A subgroup
C
is isomorphic to the additive group of a field
F
, and a subgroup
A
is isomorphic to the multiplicative group of a field
F
.

Proof
. Let
L=L_{2}
and let
f\in Aut_{[,]}(L)
. By
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
,
f([L,L])=[L,L]
, so that

\displaystyle f(e_{1})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}e_{1}+\alpha_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{3}e_{3},

\displaystyle f(e_{2})

\displaystyle=\beta_{2}e_{2}+\beta_{3}e_{3},

\displaystyle f(e_{3})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{2}e_{2}+\gamma_{3}e_{3}.

Then

\displaystyle f(e_{3})

\displaystyle=f([e_{1},e_{1}])=[f(e_{1}),f(e_{1})]

\displaystyle=[\alpha_{1}e_{1}+\alpha_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{3}e_{3},\alpha_{1}e_{1}+\alpha_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{3}e_{3}]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}^{2}[e_{1},e_{1}]+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}[e_{1},e_{2}]+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}[e_{1},e_{3}]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}^{2}e_{3}+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}(e_{2}+\lambda e_{3})+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}e_{3}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}e_{2}+(\alpha_{1}^{2}+\lambda\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3})e_{3},

\displaystyle f(e_{3})

\displaystyle=f([e_{1},e_{3}])=[f(e_{1}),f(e_{3})]

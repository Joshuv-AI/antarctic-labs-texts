Small subsets with large sumset: Beyond the Cauchy–Davenport bound
Jacob Fox, Sammy Luo, Huy Tuan Pham, Yunkun Zhou

Abstract

We show that a subset A ′ ⊆ A A^{\prime}\subseteq A of size at most s s can be found so that | A + A ′ | = Ω ⁡ ( min ⁡ ( κ 1 / 3 , s ) ​ | A | ) |A+A^{\prime}|=\Omega(\min(\kappa^{1/3},s)|A|) . Thus a sumset significantly larger than the Cauchy–Davenport bound can be guaranteed by a bounded size subset assuming that the doubling κ \kappa is large. Introduction

For finite subsets
A,B
of an abelian group, their sumset is
A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\}
. Estimating the sizes of sumsets is a classical topic extensively studied in additive combinatorics, and has motivated the development of a wide variety of influential tools and techniques. One of the most classical results in this area is the Cauchy–Davenport bound
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
, which says that for nonempty subsets
A,B
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
|A+B|\geq\min(p,|A|+|B|-1)
. This strengthens the simple observation that for nonempty subsets
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
,
|A+B|\geq|A|+|B|-1
. The equality cases of the Cauchy–Davenport bound were later characterized by Vosper
[
29
]
, who showed that either
A,B
must be arithmetic progressions of the same common difference, or
\min(|A|,|B|)=1
, or
|A|+|B|\geq p-1
.

Recently, in a sequence of papers, Bollobás, Leader and Tiba
[
5
,
6
]
studied several intriguing strengthenings of classical sumset estimates, including the Cauchy–Davenport bound. In particular, they showed that for nonempty subsets
A,B
of integers with
|A|\geq|B|
, it is enough to use three elements from
B
to achieve the sumset bound, that is, there exists a subset
B_{(3)}
of
B
of size at most three such that
|A+B_{(3)}|\geq|A|+|B|-1
. Similarly, they showed that for subsets
A,B
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
|A|\geq|B|
, there exists a subset
B_{(C)}
of
B
of constant size
C
for which
|A+B_{(C)}|\geq\min(p,|A|+|B|-1)
. (Here and throughout the paper, we use the notation
A_{(s)}
and
B_{(s)}
to denote subsets of
A
and
B
of size at most
s
.) The results were also later shown in the “medium-sized” regime in
[
6
]
: for subsets
A,B
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
|A|=|B|=n
, there exist subsets
A^{\prime}
of
A
and
B^{\prime}
of
B
each of size at most
C\sqrt{n}
for which
|A^{\prime}|+|B^{\prime}|\geq\min(p,|A|+|B|-1)
for some absolute constant
C
. Bollobás, Leader and Tiba
[
5
,
6
]
pose several interesting conjectures motivated from their work.

Sumset estimates beyond the Cauchy–Davenport bound have been extensively studied, where the behavior gets significantly more interesting. For example, while sets achieving the Cauchy–Davenport bound have particularly simple structure, for sets
A,B
with
|A|=|B|=n
and
|A+B|\leq\lambda n
for a constant
\lambda>2
, the structure gets considerably more complex and there is no exact characterization of
A
and
B
. Nevertheless, we know from Freiman’s Theorem
[
14
,
15
]
that
A
and
B
are dense subsets of generalized arithmetic progressions of bounded dimension. This central result has been very influential in the further development of additive combinatorics and related areas, see for example
[
24
,
28
]
. Motivated by this theme, in this paper, we study sumset bounds beyond the Cauchy–Davenport bound that can be achieved using small subsets. We prove several positive results and present constructions which give information about the fundamental limits.

Lower bounds beyond the Cauchy–Davenport bound.
Our first main result in the paper shows that we can indeed achieve bounds much better than the Cauchy–Davenport bound using a bounded number of elements, where the size of the sumset we can guarantee using the small subset grows with the size of the sumset
A+A
.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists
c>0
such that the following holds. Let
G
be an abelian group. Let
A\subseteq G
be nonempty and let
\kappa=\kappa(A):=|A+A|/|A|
. Then for each
s\geq 1
, there exists
A_{(s)}\subseteq A
of size at most
s
such that

|A+A_{(s)}|\geq c\min(\kappa^{1/3},s)|A|.

It is clear that
|A+A_{(s)}|\leq s|A|
and
|A+A_{(s)}|\leq\kappa|A|
. In fact, we give a construction (see Proposition
2.4
) showing that a bound better than
c\kappa^{1/1.29}|A|
cannot hold, so the bound in Theorem
1.1
is tight up to possibly replacing
\kappa^{1/3}
by
\kappa^{C}
for some
C\in[1/3,1/1.29]
.

We also prove the following asymmetric version.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists
c>0
such that the following holds. Let
G
be an abelian group. Let
A,B\subseteq G
be nonempty such that
|A|=|B|
and let
\kappa=\kappa(A,B):=|A+B|/|A|
. Then for each
s\geq 1
, there exist
A_{(s)}\subseteq A
and
B_{(s)}\subseteq B
of size at most
s
such that

\max(|A+B_{(s)}|,|B+A_{(s)}|)\geq c\min(\kappa^{1/3},s)|A|.

Unlike the result of
[
5
]
achieving the Cauchy–Davenport bound, in our case, it is necessary to consider
\max(|A+B_{(s)}|,|B+A_{(s)}|)
(see Proposition
2.5
). In Proposition
2.3
, we also derive an analog of Theorem
1.1
for difference sets, in which the power
1/3
can be replaced by
1/2
. We further extend these results to the medium-sized regime; however, in this setting, we can only expect to improve over the Cauchy–Davenport bound in a lower order term (see Theorem
2.7
and Proposition
2.8
).

To illustrate the idea, we sketch our proof of Theorem
1.1
. We first run a greedy procedure to grow
A+A_{(s)}
, and terminate when adding any additional element does not increase
|A+A_{(s)}|
by more than
c|A|
. When this happens, we show that for each element
a\in A\setminus A_{(s)}
, all but a
c
fraction of sums
a+a^{\prime},a^{\prime}\in A
have to lie in
A+A_{(s)}
. Using a path counting argument similar to the one employed in the proof of the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem
[
1
,
19
]
, we can then upper bound
|A+A|
by
O_{c}(|A+A_{(s)}|^{3}/n^{2})
, which yields the desired bound. The full proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are contained in Section
2
.

Application: The Cauchy–Davenport bound over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
using three elements.
The path counting argument to bound
|A+A|
from the sum of
A
and a set of bounded size is useful for other applications. In Section
3
, we use this idea to resolve a conjecture of Bollobás, Leader and Tiba (Conjecture 1 of
[
5
]
), showing that over

, it suffices to use three elements to achieve the Cauchy–Davenport bound.

Theorem 1.3
.

There exists
\alpha>0
such that the following holds. Let
A,B
be nonempty subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
of size
n\leq\alpha p
. Then there exists a subset
B_{(3)}
of
B
of size at most three such that
|A+B_{(3)}|\geq 2n-1
.

The proof involves applying the path counting argument as in Section
2
in several iterations to bound the size of
A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}
for appropriate subsets
A^{\prime}
of
A
and
B^{\prime}
of
B
. When dealing with sets that have bounded sumset, one can apply appropriate rectification results to connect the problem over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with a problem over
\mathbb{Z}
. However, the main difficulty is that sumsets are not stable under addition or removal of a small number of elements, and even controlling sums of subsets of
A
and
B
that contain all but at most an
\epsilon

A
and
B
. (We will later revisit this phenomenon when discussing Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.8
.) We then need a careful argument that works directly with appropriate rectifications of subsets of
A
and
B
and adapts the argument inspired by the argument of
[
5
]
over
\mathbb{Z}
appropriately.

High dimensional versions.
Another setting where one can expect to go beyond the Cauchy–Davenport bound is the high dimension setting, for example where we have a set
A
in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
which is not close to a lower dimensional set in an appropriate sense. In particular, using the Freiman–Bilu Theorem
[
3
,
17
]
, we can show that for every
d
and
\epsilon
, there exists
t>0
such that if
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
A
cannot be covered by a union of
t
hyperplanes, then
|A+A|\geq(2^{d}-\epsilon)|A|
. In Section
4
, we consider the analog of the results of
[
5
]
over the high dimension setting, and show that the high-dimension analog of the Cauchy–Davenport bound can be achieved using only a bounded number of elements.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and let
d
be a positive integer. Assume
t
is sufficiently large in
d
and
\epsilon
. Let
A
be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
A
is not contained in the union of any
t
parallel hyperplanes. Then there is a subset
A^{\prime}
of
A
of size
O_{\epsilon,d,t}(1)
for which
|A+A^{\prime}|\geq(2^{d}-\epsilon)|A|
.

In
[
6
, Question 3]
, it was asked if a medium-sized version holds: for a subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
that is not contained in the union of any
t
parallel hyperplanes, does there exist
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
|A^{\prime}|\leq C\sqrt{|A|}
and
|A^{\prime}+A^{\prime}|\geq(2^{d}-\epsilon)|A|
? It turns out that the answer to this question is negative, as shown in Proposition

. Nevertheless, the bounded size version of this, Theorem

, holds.

Saturating the entire sumset from a subset.
In the following parts, we will work with subsets of general abelian groups. As discussed earlier, it is not possible to achieve a fixed positive fraction of
|A+A|
using the sum of
A
and a subset
A_{C}
of
A
of size
C
depending only on the doubling constant
\kappa=|A+A|/|A|
. Nevertheless, it is interesting to determine how large a subset
A^{\prime}
of
A
is needed in order to guarantee
|A+A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\epsilon)|A+A|
. It turns out that if
A
has bounded doubling,
\kappa\leq\lambda=O(1)
, one can always guarantee the existence of a subset
A^{\prime}
of
A
of size
o(|A|)
with
A^{\prime}+A=A+A
. Furthermore, the asymmetric version holds: for any
A
and
B
with
|A|=|B|
and
|A+B|\leq\lambda|A|
, one can find subsets
A^{\prime}
of
A
and
B^{\prime}
of
B
each of size
o(|A|)
such that
A+B=(A^{\prime}+B)\cup(A+B^{\prime})
. In the case where
A,B
are dense subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, this was earlier shown by Ellenberg
[
11
]
with efficient bounds building on the cap-set breakthrough. In Section
5
, we show that a much more general phenomenon holds for subsets of groups with bounded doubling which are not necessarily dense.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
G
be an abelian group, and let
S,T\subseteq G
be nonempty subsets of equal size such that
|S+T|=O(|S|)
. Then there exist
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
and
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
with
S+T=(S^{\prime}+T)\cup(S+T^{\prime})
and
|S^{\prime}|+|T^{\prime}|=o(|S|)
.

Our proof also gives rise to the appropriate generalization over general groups. For subsets
A,B
of a group which is not necessarily abelian, we write
AB=\{ab:a\in A,b\in B\}
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
G
be a group, and let
S,T\subseteq G
be nonempty subsets of equal size such that
|S^{2}T|=O(|S|)
. Then there exist
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
and
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
with
ST=(S^{\prime}T)\cup(ST^{\prime})
and
|S^{\prime}|+|T^{\prime}|=o(|S|)
.

Both corollaries follow from a more general result in Section
5
, which is proved using the graph theoretic triangle removal lemma. A reduction from an arithmetic removal lemma to a graph removal lemma goes back to work of Král’, Serra and Vena
[
23
]
, and our main technical result builds on and extends their argument. As applications of the graph removal lemma, our proofs of Corollaries
1.5
and
1.6
do not achieve strong quantitative bounds over general groups. By using the bounds for the arithmetic removal lemma over
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
due to Fox and Lovász
[
13
]
, we can achieve a polynomial saving, replacing
o(|S|)
by an appropriate power
|S|^{1-c_{m}}
in the case where
G
is an abelian group with bounded exponent
m
. Nevertheless, in the case
G=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, the argument of Ellenberg
[
11
]
gives a better (in fact sharp) exponent for Corollary
1.5
. In Section
5
, we also give constructions in the cases
G=\mathbb{Z}
and
G=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
suggesting fundamental limits on quantitative bounds to the corollaries.

A construction of a non-saturating set.
While it is not possible to achieve almost all of
A+A
using a bounded number of elements, one of the key results in
[
5
]
says that this is possible upon replacing
A
with a subset
A^{\prime}
consisting of almost all elements of
A
.

Theorem 1.7

(
[
5
]
)
.

For
\lambda,\epsilon>0
, there exists
C>0
such that the following holds. Let
A,B
be nonempty subsets of an abelian group
G
with
|A|=|B|
. Then there exist
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
,
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
with
|A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\epsilon)|A|
and
|B^{\prime}|\geq(1-\epsilon)|B|

In particular, if

, then there exists a subset

of

Indeed, much of the subtlety in obtaining sumset bounds growing with

is that, unlike the Cauchy–Davenport bound which is stable under passing

to a subset
A^{\prime}
that contains almost all elements of

can be as large as

, and Theorem

suggests that this is the only obstruction.

The example of a progression together with few additional elements mentioned above suggests that it is impossible to replace
|A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}|
with

in Theorem
1.7
. However, it is unclear if we can replace only one of two sets with a subset. Considering that we are allowed to make our selection of elements from

, it could be the case that we only need to replace

by a subset

with better additive structure. Bollobás, Leader and Tiba (Question 5,
[
5
]
) ask this explicitly: Given constants
\epsilon>0
and
\lambda>0
, is there a constant

such that the following assertion holds? If
A,B
are subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
|A|=|B|=n
and
|A+B|\leq\lambda n
, then there are sets
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
and
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
such that
|A^{\prime}|\geq(1-\epsilon)n
and
|B^{\prime}|\leq c
and
|A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}|\geq(1-\epsilon)|A^{\prime}+B|
.

It turns out that the answer to this question is negative. In Section
6
, we give a construction showing this in a strong sense: It is not possible to achieve a fixed positive fraction of the sumset
A^{\prime}+A
using a bounded number of elements from
A^{\prime}
even when we are allowed to choose

which is a subset of

of size at least

.

Theorem 1.8
.

For any
\nu>0
, there exist
\lambda>1
and sufficiently small

sufficiently large in

\mathbb{Z}_{p}

In fact, in our proof, we can even let

General lower bounds on sumsets from small subsets

In this section, we will prove our results guaranteeing sumsets with size significantly larger than the Cauchy–Davenport bound using a small number of elements, assuming that
|A+B|
significantly exceeds the size of the sets
A
and
B
. In particular, we will prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, as well as their “medium-sized” analogs.

Throughout this and the following sections, we use a convenient path counting argument, which we record here. A similar argument is used in Gowers’ proof of the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem (see
[
19
,
28
]
). Given a subset
C\subseteq A+B
, let
\Gamma_{C}
be the bipartite graph on vertex sets
A,B
such that
a\in A
,
b\in B
are adjacent if and only if
a+b\in C
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k
be an odd positive integer. Let
A,B
be subsets of an abelian group and
C\subseteq A+B
. Suppose that for every pair
(a,b)\in A\times B
there are at least
w
walks of length
k
in
\Gamma_{C}
going from
a
to
b
. Then
|A+B|\leq|C|^{k}/w
.

Proof.

For each walk
(a_{0},b_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k-1},b_{k})
of length
k
with
a_{0}=a
and
b_{k}=b
, we have
a+b=(a+b_{1})-(b_{1}+a_{2})+\dots+(a_{k-1}+b)
. Thus, for each
x\in A+B
, fixing
(a,b)\in A\times B
such that
x=a+b
, we have at least
w
different representations
x=c_{1}-c_{2}+\cdots+c_{k}
where
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\in C
. Since there are at most
|C|^{k}
choices of
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}
, and each choice uniquely determines
x\in A+B
and the walk, we have that
|A+B|\cdot w\leq|C|^{k}
, which rearranges to the desired inequality.
∎

We also have the following version of Lemma
2.1
for even values of
k
, which follows from essentially the same proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
k
be an even positive integer. Let
A,B
be subsets of an abelian group and
C\subseteq A+B
. Suppose that for every pair
(b,b^{\prime})\in B\times B
there are at least
w
walks of length

in
\Gamma_{C}
going from
b
to
b^{\prime}
. Then
|B-B|\leq|C|^{k}/w
.

|(B+a)\cap(B+A_{(s-1)})|>(1-2c)n
for all
a\in A\setminus A_{(s-1)}
. The same inequalities are clearly true when
a\in A_{(s-1)}
or

, so we in fact have

|(B+a)\cap(B+A_{(s-1)})|>(1-2c)n\,\,\forall a\in A,\qquad|(A+b)\cap(A+B_{(s-1)})|>(1-2c)n\,\,\forall b\in B.

(1)

Let
C=(B+A_{(s-1)})\cup(A+B_{(s-1)})
, and consider the graph
\Gamma_{C}
defined as above. From (
1
) we know that every vertex in
A
is adjacent to all but
2cn
vertices of
B
, and vice versa. In particular, given any pair
(x,y)\in A\times B
, there are at least
(1-2c)n
choices of
x^{\prime}
and
(1-2c)n
choices of
y^{\prime}
such that
(x,y^{\prime}),(y,x^{\prime})\in\Gamma_{C}
. For each such choice of
x^{\prime}
, all but at most
2cn
of the choices of
y^{\prime}
satisfy
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\Gamma_{C}
. Thus, for every choice of
(x,y)\in A\times B
, there are at least
(1-2c)n(1-4c)n\geq(1-6c)n^{2}
paths of length three from
x
to
y
in
\Gamma_{C}
. Applying Lemma
2.1
with
k=3
,
w=(1-6c)n^{2}
yields

|A+B|(1-6c)n^{2}\leq|(B+A_{(s-1)})\cup(A+B_{(s-1)})|^{3}<8c^{3}\kappa n^{3}.

Note that
|A+B|=\kappa n
. Simplifying the inequality above gives
\frac{8c^{3}}{1-6c}>1
, which contradicts with
c=\frac{1}{14}
. Thus (
1
) is false. This means that there exist
A_{(s)}
and

with

∎

Theorem

follows immediately by taking

. We remark that in the case

Proposition 2.3
.

There exists

such that the following holds. Let

On the other hand, the following construction gives an upper bound on how large of a sumset we can guarantee.

Proposition 2.4
.

There exists
c>0
for arbitrarily large

Proof.

Fix a small constant
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. For a positive integer

, a perfect square

, and a real number
\delta\in(2\epsilon^{-1}m^{-1/2},1-m^{-1/2})

, where

is a set of size

. In particular,

. It is easy to check that for

Hence,
\kappa\geq(2/(\delta+2m^{-1/2}))^{d}\geq(2/((1+\epsilon)\delta))^{d}
.

On the other hand, for any elements
a,b\in A
, we have

unless there is some

for which both

have their

. Therefore, for any

|(A+a)\setminus[(1+\delta)m]^{d}|\leq d|B||A|^{(d-1)/d}=2d\sqrt{m}|A|^{(d-1)/d}.

Thus, for any

and any subset

of

of size

|(A+A_{(s)})\setminus[(1+\delta)m]^{d}|\leq\frac{2sd\sqrt{m}}{\delta m+\sqrt{m}}\cdot|A|\leq\frac{2s}{\sqrt{m}}\left(\frac{1+\delta}{\delta}\right)^{d}|A|

Thus, for

n^{c/\log\kappa}\leq\exp\left(\frac{cd\log(\delta m+2\sqrt{m})}{d\log(2/((1+\epsilon)\delta))}\right)\leq(\delta m)^{2c/\log(2/\delta)}\leq\epsilon\sqrt{m}/2,

assuming that

.
∎

2.2
Large sumsets from medium-sized subsets

We now consider sumsets of medium-sized subsets, where we take subsets of both
A
and
B
while keeping the product of their sizes small. Bollobás, Leader, and Tiba
[
6
]
proved the following result.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
6
]
, Theorem 1)
.

For all
\alpha,\beta>0
there exists
c>0
such that the following holds. Let
A
and
B
be non-empty subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
\alpha|B|\leq|A|\leq\alpha^{-1}|B|
and
|A|+|B|\leq(1-\beta)p
. Then, for any integers
1\leq c_{1}\leq|A|
and
1\leq c_{2}\leq|B|
such that
c_{1}c_{2}\geq c\max(|A|,|B|)
, there exist subsets
A^{\prime}\subset A
and
B^{\prime}\subset B
of sizes
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that
|A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}|\geq|A|+|B|-1
.

In particular, when
|A|=|B|=n
, and we take
c_{1}=c_{2}\geq\sqrt{cn}
, this result again yields the Cauchy–Davenport lower bound of
2n-1
using only medium-sized subsets of
A
and
B
. We are interested in going beyond this bound. However, in this case, unlike in the case of a constant-sized subset of
B
plus all of
A
, it turns out that only a lower-order improvement over the Cauchy–Davenport bound is possible in general.

Theorem 2.7
.

For all
\beta
there exists
c^{\prime}>0
such that the following holds. Let
A,B
be nonempty subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
|A|=|B|=n\leq\frac{1-\beta}{2}p
and
|A+B|=\kappa n
. Then for all
c\in(0,\sqrt{n}]
there exist subsets
A_{(s)}\subset A
and
B_{(s)}\subset B
of size
s\leq(c^{\prime}+c)\sqrt{n}
such that

|A_{(s)}+B_{(s)}|\geq 2n-1+c\min\left(\frac{1}{4}n,\left({\kappa^{1/3}}/{2}-2\right)\sqrt{n}\right).

Proof.

Let
c_{2}=\frac{1}{4}
. By Theorem
2.6
, for some
c_{1}>0
, there exist subsets
A^{\prime}\subset A
and
B^{\prime}\subset B
with
|A^{\prime}|=|B^{\prime}|=c_{1}\sqrt{n}
such that
|A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}|\geq 2n-1
. Fixing such a pair of subsets and letting
S_{0}:=A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}
, we iteratively perform the following procedure. Given
S_{i-1}
for
1\leq i\leq\sqrt{n}
, we form
S_{i}\subseteq A+B
by either taking an element
a\in A
such that
|(a+B)\setminus S_{i-1}|\geq c_{2}n
and letting
S_{i}=S_{i-1}\cup(a+B)
, or taking an element
b\in B
such that
|(b+A)\setminus S_{i-1}|\geq c_{2}n
and letting
S_{i}=S_{i-1}\cup(b+A)
. The procedure terminates when neither action is possible, or once
S_{i}
is formed for some
i\geq\sqrt{n}
. Suppose the process terminates upon the formation of
S_{\ell}
. Let
A^{*}
and
B^{*}
be the sets of elements chosen from
A
and
B
, respectively, in the course of the procedure, so
|A^{*}|,|B^{*}|\leq\ell<\sqrt{n}+1
and
S_{\ell}=S_{0}\cup(A^{*}+B)\cup(B^{*}+A)
.

If
\ell\geq\sqrt{n}
, then we have
|S_{\ell}|\geq|S_{0}|+c_{2}\ell n\geq|S_{0}|+c_{2}n^{3/2}
. Otherwise, assume
\ell<\sqrt{n}
. Then by the termination condition, for all
a\in A
and
b\in B
we have
|(b+A)\setminus S_{\ell}|,|(a+B)\setminus S_{\ell}|<c_{2}n
. Then in the graph
\Gamma_{S_{\ell}}
, every vertex has degree at least
(1-c_{2})n
, which as before means that between every pair
(x,y)\in A\times B
there are at least
(1-3c_{2})n^{2}
paths of length three. Applying Lemma
2.1
with
k=3
and
w=(1-3c_{2})n^{2}
then yields
|A+B|\leq\frac{|S_{\ell}|^{3}}{(1-3c_{2})n^{2}}
, which means that
|S_{\ell}|\geq((1-3c_{2})\kappa)^{1/3}n
in this case.

Choose subsets
\tilde{A}\subset A
and
\tilde{B}\subset B
of size
c\sqrt{n}
uniformly at random. By construction, each element of
A^{*}+B
appears in
A^{*}+\tilde{B}
with probability at least
\frac{|\tilde{B}|}{B}=cn^{-1/2}
, and likewise each element of
A+B^{*}
appears in
\tilde{A}+B^{*}
with probability at least
cn^{-1/2}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{A},\tilde{B}}[|(A^{\prime}\cup A^{*}\cup\tilde{A})+(B^{\prime}\cup B^{*}\cup\tilde{B})|]

\displaystyle\geq|A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}|+\mathbb{E}[|((A^{*}\cup\tilde{A})+(B^{*}\cup\tilde{B}))\setminus(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})|]

\displaystyle\geq|S_{0}|+\sum_{s\in((A^{*}+B)\cup(A+B^{*}))\setminus S_{0}}\Pr[s\in(A^{*}\cup\tilde{A})+(B^{*}\cup\tilde{B})]

\displaystyle\geq|S_{0}|+cn^{-1/2}|S_{\ell}\setminus S_{0}|

\displaystyle\geq(1-cn^{-1/2})|S_{0}|+cn^{-1/2}\min(|S_{0}|+c_{2}n^{3/2},(1-3c_{2})^{1/3}\kappa^{1/3}n)

\displaystyle\geq 2n-1+c\min(c_{2}n,((1-3c_{2})^{1/3}\kappa^{1/3}-2)n^{1/2}).

Fix choices of
\tilde{A},\tilde{B}
such that the size of this sumset is at least its expectation, and let
A_{(s)}=A^{\prime}\cup A^{*}\cup\tilde{A}
and
B_{(s)}=B^{\prime}\cup B^{*}\cup\tilde{B}
. Note that
A_{(s)},B_{(s)}
each has size at most
s=(c^{\prime}+c)\sqrt{n}
, where
c^{\prime}=c_{1}+1
. We then have
|A_{(s)}+B_{(s)}|\geq 2n-1+c\min(\frac{1}{4}n,(c^{\prime\prime}\kappa^{1/3}-2)\sqrt{n})
, where
c^{\prime\prime}=(1-3c_{2})^{1/3}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, as desired.
∎

The matching upper bound showing that Theorem
2.7
is best possible up to the power of
\kappa
comes from taking
s=C\sqrt{n}
in the following construction.

Proposition 2.8
.

For all positive integers
n,k
with
k\leq\sqrt{n}
, there exist a real number
\kappa\in(k,k+1]
and a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
such that
|A|=n
,
|A+A|=\kappa n
, and for all subsets
A_{(s)},B_{(s)}\subset A
of size

, we have

|A_{(s)}+B_{(s)}|\leq 2n+2\kappa s.

Proof.

Let
n_{0}=n-k+1
, and let
A=[n_{0}]\cup\{2n_{0},3n_{0},\dots,kn_{0}\}
. Then
|A|=n
and
|A+A|=|[(k+1)n_{0}]\cup\{(k+2)n_{0},\dots,2kn_{0}\}|=(k+1)(n-k+1)+k-1
, so indeed
(k+1)n\geq|A+A|\geq(k+1)(n-k+1)>(k+1)n-k^{2}\geq kn
.

Fix arbitrary subsets
A_{(s)},B_{(s)}\subset A
with
|A_{(s)}|=|B_{(s)}|=s
. Partition
A_{(s)}
as
A_{0}\sqcup A_{1}
where
A_{0}=A_{(s)}\cap[n_{0}]
, and partition

similarly. We have
A_{0}+B_{0}\subseteq[2n_{0}]
while

, and
|A_{1}|,|B_{1}|\leq|\{2n_{0},3n_{0},\dots,kn_{0}\}|=k-1
. Then

\displaystyle|A_{(s)}+B_{(s)}|

\displaystyle\leq|A_{0}+B_{0}|+|A_{0}+B_{1}|+|A_{1}+B_{0}|+|A_{1}+B_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq 2n_{0}+|A_{0}||B_{1}|+|A_{1}||B_{0}|+(2k-1)

\displaystyle\leq 2n+1+2(k-1)s\leq 2n+2\kappa s,

as desired.
∎

3
The Cauchy–Davenport bound in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
from three elements

In this section, we prove Theorem

, restated below, which resolves Conjecture 1 of

]
.

See
1.3

Our argument is based on an extension of the argument in Section
2
, used to show that appropriate subsets of
A
and

have bounded doubling. We then use Freiman’s rectification principle to relate appropriate subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and subsets of

We will make use of the analog of the above theorem in the integer setting that was already shown in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
5
]
, Theorem 1)
.

Let
A,B
be subsets of
\mathbb{Z}
of size
n
. Then there exists a subset
B_{(3)}
of
B
of size at most three such that
|A+B_{(3)}|\geq 2n-1
.

We will also make use of Freiman’s rectification principle
[
4
,
14
]
. Recall that a Freiman
s
-homomorphism
\phi:A\to B
is a map such that
\sum_{i=1}^{s}\phi(a_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\phi(a_{i}^{\prime})
whenever
\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}^{\prime}
, and it is called an
s
-isomorphism when
\phi^{-1}
is also a Freiman
s
-homomorphism.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
4
,
14
]
)
.

Let
A
be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. For
\lambda>0
and
s>0
, there exists
c>0
such that if
|A|<cp
and
|A+A|\leq\lambda|A|
, then there is a Freiman
s
-isomorphism between
A
and a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
.

We use the following version of the Plünnecke-Ruzsa Inequality with different summands
[
27
]
.

Lemma 3.3

(The Plünnecke-Ruzsa Inequality,
[
27
]
)
.

Let
X,Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}
be subsets of an abelian group
G
. Assume that
|X+Y_{i}|\leq\alpha_{i}|X|
for each

. Then
|Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{k}|\leq\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{k}|X|
.

Using the Theorem
3.2
and Lemma

Corollary 3.4
.

For
\lambda,s,t>0
, there exists
c>0
such that the following holds. Let

be subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
|A|=|B|<cp
. If

, then there is a Freiman

-isomorphism between

and a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
applied with

, we have
|2B|\ll_{\lambda}|A|
. By another application of Lemma

with

, we can obtain
|kB+\ell A|\ll_{k,\ell,\lambda}|A|
and hence
|k(A\cup B)|\ll_{k,\lambda}|A|
. In particular we have

implies that

is Freiman

-isomorphic to a subset

. ∎

The following simple lemma is a warm-up to the proof of Theorem

Lemma 3.5
.

For every
\lambda>0
, there is
\alpha>0
such that the following holds. Let

be subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of size

. Assume that

. Then there exists a subset

of

Proof.

By Corollary

, for

-isomorphism
\psi
from

to a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
.
By Theorem

, there exists a subset

of

so that

. Noting that since

\psi(a)+\psi(b)=\psi(a^{\prime})+\psi(b^{\prime})
if and only if

∎

The full proof of Theorem

is much more involved, due to the fact that we cannot reduce to the case
|A+B|\ll|A|
, but can only find suitable dense subsets
\tilde{A}
of
A
with
|\tilde{A}+B|\ll|A|
. Making the choice of elements

greedily, we can find a subset

of

of size at least

and a set

with

such that for each

, at least a positive fraction of

satisfies

. From this we obtain the bound
|A^{\prime}+B|\ll|A|
. We then bootstrap this subset to a slightly larger subset
A^{\prime\prime}
consisting of all
a^{\prime\prime}\in A
with
a^{\prime\prime}+b\in(A^{\prime}+B)\cup A\cup(A+x)
for a positive fraction of

. At this point, we need to employ another argument (inspired by the argument in the integer case) in order to handle the elements of
A
that are not contained in

. To proceed with the proof in full detail, we first state two useful auxiliary results.

Lemma 3.6

(Theorem 27,
[
5
]
)
.

There is
c>0
such that the following holds. Let
A,B
be subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of size

, where

is contained in an interval of length at most

. Then there exists a subset
B_{(3)}
of

of size at most three such that
|A+B_{(3)}|\geq 2n-1
.

Lemma 3.7

(
[
20
]
)
.

There is
c>0
such that the following holds. Let
B
be a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
|B-B|<(2+c)|B|<4cp
. Then B is contained in an interval of length at most
(1+c)|B|
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By Lemma
3.6
, in the remaining part of the proof, we can assume that
B
is not contained in an interval of length at most
(1+c)n
, and hence, by Lemma

,
|B-B|\geq(2+c)n
.

Step 1.
We first show that we can assume that there exists
B_{(2)}\subseteq B
of size at most two where
|A+B_{(2)}|\geq(3/2+c_{1})n
for an appropriate constant
c_{1}>0
.

If not, then for any two elements

|(A+b_{2}-b_{1})\cap A|>(1/2-c_{1})n
.
By our assumption, we have that for any

in at least

is

, we conclude that
|B-B|\leq n^{2}/(n(1/2-c_{1}))
. For

sufficiently small in

, we then have
|B-B|<(2+c)n
, a contradiction.

Step 2.

Assume that for all
B_{(3)}\subseteq B
of size at most three,
|A+B_{(3)}|\leq(2+c_{1}/2)n
. From Step 1, there exists

of size at most two with
|A+B_{(2)}|\geq(3/2+c_{1})n
. Then for all

, we have
|(A+b)\setminus(A+B_{(2)})|\leq(1/2+c_{1}/2-c_{1})n=(1/2-c_{1}/2)n
.

Recall the construction from Section
2
of a bipartite graph
\Gamma_{C}
on vertex sets

where
a\in A
and
b\in B
are connected if and only if

. Let

. Then each

is adjacent in

to at least
(1/2+c_{1}/2)n
vertices

. Thus, every
b,b^{\prime}\in B
are connected by at least
c_{1}n
walks of length two in the graph. By Lemma

5
Saturating the entire sumset from a subset

In this section, we study the following question. Given a finite set
A
contained in an abelian group with
|A+A|=O(|A|)
, how large do we need
A^{\prime}
to be so that
A+A^{\prime}=A+A
? We also consider the asymmetric version: Given finite sets
A,B
contained in an abelian group with
|A|=|B|
and
|A+B|=O(|A|)
, how large do we need
A^{\prime}\subseteq A,B^{\prime}\subseteq B
to be so that
(B+A^{\prime})\cup(A+B^{\prime})=A+B
? We begin first with general upper bounds that hold for arbitrary groups.

5.1
Upper bound

The following theorem will be our main technical result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G
be a group, let
S,T,X
be subsets of
G
, and let
Z=XST
. Then there exist subsets
S^{\prime}
of
S
and
T^{\prime}
of
T
with
|S^{\prime}|,|T^{\prime}|=o(|Z|^{2}/|X|)
, and
(S^{\prime}T)\cup(ST^{\prime})=ST
.

We will use the graph-theoretic triangle removal lemma in the proof of Theorem
5.1
. The idea of using the triangle removal lemma to prove an arithmetic removal lemma in general groups goes back to work of Král’, Serra and Vena
[
23
]
. Here we build on and generalize their proof idea to prove a result that is potentially meaningful for sparse subsets of a group. We have stated below the triangle removal lemma, which follows from Szemerédi’s regularity lemma. The best quantitative bound known for the triangle removal lemma is by Fox
[
12
]
, where
1/\epsilon(\delta)
can be taken as the inverse function of an exponential tower of height
O(\log\delta^{-1})
.

Lemma 5.2

(Triangle Removal Lemma
[
12
]
)
.

There exists
\epsilon:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\epsilon(\delta)\to 0
when
\delta\to 0
and the following holds. If
\Gamma
is a graph with at most
\delta|V(\Gamma)|^{3}
triangles, then one can remove at most
\epsilon(\delta)|V(\Gamma)|^{2}
edges from
\Gamma
and obtain a triangle free graph.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Let
\tau>0
be a threshold to be chosen later. We consider a greedy procedure to define a subset

of
S
. Start with
S_{*}=\emptyset
. Whenever there exists

such that

, we update
S_{*}
to be

. The procedure terminates with a set

satisfying the condition that for all
s\in S\setminus S_{*}
,
|(sT)\setminus(S_{*}T)|\leq\tau
. Note that

|S_{*}|\leq|ST|/\tau\leq|XST|/\tau.

Let
Y=XS
and recall that
Z=XST
. Consider a tripartite graph
\Gamma
with vertex sets
X,Y,Z
where
x\in X
,

are connected if

for
s\in S\setminus S_{*}
;
y\in Y
,

are connected if

for

; and
z\in Z

are connected if

for

. Each triangle in
\Gamma
corresponds to a triple
(x,xs,xst)
where
s\in S\setminus S_{*}
,
t\in T
and

. For each

and
s\in S\setminus S_{*}
, the number of

forming such a triple is at most
\tau
by our choice of

. Hence,
\Gamma
has at most
\tau|X||S|\leq(\tau/|Z|)|V(\Gamma)|^{3}

\epsilon(\tau/|Z|)|V(\Gamma)|^{2}

\Gamma
and ensure that no triangle remains. Let
\hat{S}
be the set of elements

for which at least
|X|/3
edges

of
\Gamma
are removed. Let
\hat{T}
be the set of elements

for which at least
|X|/3
edges

of
\Gamma
are removed. Let
\hat{U}
be the set of elements

|X|/3

are removed. For each

.

showed an arithmetic removal lemma with polynomial dependency over
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, stated below. Let
c_{p}
be a positive constant given by
p^{1-c_{p}}=\inf_{0<x<1}x^{-(p-1)/3}(1+x+\dots+x^{p-1})
, and let
C_{p}=1+1/c_{p}
.

Theorem 5.4

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
X,Y,Z\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
be such that there are at most
\delta p^{2n}
solutions to
x+y=z
for
x\in X,y\in Y,z\in Z
. Then we can remove
\epsilon p^{n}
elements from
X,Y,Z
and remove all solutions to
x+y=z
, where
\epsilon=\delta^{1/C_{p}-o(1)}
.

In fact, the dependency of
\delta
on
\epsilon
in Theorem
5.4
is tight up to the
o(1)
term in the exponent.

Note that in the proof of Theorem
5.1
in the case
G=X=Y=Z
, denoting by
\epsilon_{G}(\delta)
the bound we have for the arithmetic removal lemma over
G
, we obtain a bound

|S^{\prime}|+|T^{\prime}|\leq\inf_{\tau\geq 1}\left(|G|/\tau+27\epsilon(\tau/|G|)|G|\right).

If
\epsilon(\delta)
depends polynomially in
\delta
, so that
\epsilon(\delta)\leq\delta^{c}
for some
c>0
, then we can optimize
\tau
to obtain
|S^{\prime}|+|T^{\prime}|\leq|G|^{1-c^{\prime}}
for some
c^{\prime}>0
depending on
c
. In particular, in the case
G=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, we can use the arithmetic removal lemma of Fox and Lovász to obtain

|S^{\prime}|+|T^{\prime}|\leq|G|^{1-1/(1+C_{p})+o(1)}=|G|^{1-1/(2+1/c_{p})+o(1)}.

A similar result also holds more generally over abelian groups with bounded exponent.

For
S,T\subseteq G
of equal size with
|S+T|\leq\lambda|S|
, by a result of Green and Ruzsa
[
16
]
, we can guarantee that
S
and
T
are subsets of a coset progression of size
O_{\lambda}(|S|)
. When
G=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, one can then guarantee that
S,T
are subsets of subgroups of
G
of size
O_{\lambda,p}(|S|)
. Hence, we also obtain the same result where we can guarantee
|S^{\prime}|+|T^{\prime}|\leq O_{\lambda,p}(|S|^{1-1/(2+1/c_{p})+o(1)})
. Furthermore, a power-saving bound holds in the same setting over abelian groups with bounded exponent.

In
[
11
]
, by adapting directly the linear-algebraic method behind the cap-set result, Ellenberg obtained a power-saving bound with tight exponent for the special case
G=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
. Combining this result with the above argument, one obtains the following corollary.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
G=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
. For
S,T\subseteq G
of equal size with
|S+T|=O(|S|)
, there exist
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
and
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
with
S+T=(S^{\prime}+T)\cup(S+T^{\prime})
and
|S^{\prime}|+|T^{\prime}|=O(|S|^{1-c_{p}})
.

5.2
Lower bound construction

In this section, we study constructions of sets
S,T
for which
|S+T|\ll|S|=|T|
, and any choice of
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
,
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
such that
S+T=(S^{\prime}+T)\cup(S+T^{\prime})
must necessarily have
|S^{\prime}|+|T^{\prime}|
large. In fact, we will only need to focus on the symmetric case where
S=T=A
.

First, we will show that one cannot expect a power-saving upper bound for
|S^{\prime}|+|T^{\prime}|
over
\mathbb{Z}
. We will make use of a construction of Behrend for progression-free sets
[
2
,
10
,
18
]
.

Proposition 5.6
.

For any positive integer
t
, the set
X_{r,n}(t):=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):1\leq x_{i}\leq r\;\forall\;1\leq i\leq n,\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{2}=t\}
is progression-free in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{d}
when
m\geq 2r
.

Proof.

Suppose that three distinct elements
x,y,z\in X_{r,n}(t)
form a 3-term progression in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
.

For each
i
, we know that
2y_{i}\equiv x_{i}+z_{i}\pmod{m}
. Moreover, because
1\leq x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}\leq r
, we know that
2\leq 2y_{i}\leq 2r\leq m
and
2\leq x_{i}+z_{i}\leq 2r\leq m
. Therefore,
2y_{i}=x_{i}+z_{i}
, and hence we have

0=2t+2t-4t=\sum_{i=1}^{n}2x_{i}^{2}+2z_{i}^{2}-4y_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}2x_{i}^{2}+2z_{i}^{2}-(x_{i}+z_{i})^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-z_{i})^{2}.

This is only possible when
x=z
. Since
2y=x+z
, we have
y=(x+z)/2=x
, which contradicts the assumption of distinctness. Therefore we conclude that
X_{r,n}(t)
is progression-free in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
.
∎

Proposition 5.7

(Construction in
\mathbb{Z}
)
.

For any sufficiently large positive integer
N
, there exists a finite set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that
|A|\leq N
,
|A+A|\leq 6|A|
, and for any subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
A^{\prime}+A=A+A
, we have
|A^{\prime}|\geq Ne^{-c\sqrt{\log N}}
for some absolute constant
c>0
.

Proof.

Let
r,n
be determined later.
We would like to use Proposition
5.6
. By the Pigeonhole Principle, since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}
is in
[n,nr^{2}]
when
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is taken from
\{1,\dots,r\}^{n}
, there exists
t
such that
X_{r,n}(t)
contains at least
\frac{r^{n}}{nr^{2}}
elements. Fix such a
t
. Let
\phi:\mathbb{Z}_{2r}^{n}\to\{0,\dots,(2r)^{n}-1\}
be a bijective map that induces a Freiman
2
-isomorphism from
\{1,\dots,r\}^{n}
to its image. Since
X_{r,n}(t)
is a progression-free set in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{2r}
, the set
A_{0}:=\phi(X_{r,n}(t))
is a progression-free set in
\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, if
2a=a_{1}+a_{2}
for
a,a_{1},a_{2}\in A_{0}
, then
a_{1}=a_{2}=a
.

For simplicity we denote
T=(2r)^{n}
. Let
A_{1}=[-2T,-T-1]\cap\mathbb{Z}
and let
A=A_{0}\cup A_{1}
. Clearly
T=|A_{1}|\leq|A|=|A_{0}|+|A_{1}|\leq 2T
. On the other hand, we have
A+A\subseteq[-4T,2T)
, so
|A+A|\leq 6|A|
. Suppose
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
satisfies that
A^{\prime}+A=A+A
. For any
a\in A_{0}
, we know that
2a\in A+A
. If
2a=a_{1}+a_{2}
for some
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
, then since
a_{1}+a_{2}=2a>0
, neither
a_{1}
nor
a_{2}
is in
A_{1}
, implying that
a_{1},a_{2}\in A_{0}
. From our construction of
A_{0}
, we must have
a_{1}=a_{2}=a
. Hence if
2a\in A^{\prime}+A
for some subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
, then we must have
a\in A^{\prime}
. Hence,
A+A^{\prime}=A+A
implies that
A_{0}\subseteq A^{\prime}
. Now if we optimize by choosing
N=2T
,
n=\sqrt{\log N}
and
r=e^{\sqrt{\log N}}/4
, then
|A|\leq N
and
|A^{\prime}|\geq|A_{0}|\geq\frac{r^{n}}{nr^{2}}\geq Ne^{-c\sqrt{\log N}}
for some positive constant
c
.
∎

As we saw in the previous subsection, the behavior over
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
is different, and we can show a power-saving upper bound on
|S^{\prime}|+|T^{\prime}|
. For the construction over
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, we will use the result of Kleinberg, Sawin and Speyer
[

]
(combined with a conjecture later resolved by Pebody
[

]
and Norin
[

]
) on tricolored sum-free sets.

Theorem 5.8

(
[
22
,
25
,
26
]
)
.

Given a collection of ordered triples
\{(x_{i},y_{i},z_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
such that

x_{i}+y_{j}+z_{k}=0
holds if and only if
i=j=k
, the size of the collection satisfies the
bound

m\leq p^{(1-c_{p})n}
.
Furthermore, there exists such a collection with
m\geq p^{(1-c_{p})n-o(n)}
.

Proposition 5.9

(Construction in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
)
.

For positive integers
n
, there exists
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
such that
|A+A|\leq 6|A|
, and for any subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
A^{\prime}+A=A+A
, we have
|A^{\prime}|\geq p^{(1-c_{p})n-o(n)}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.8
, there exist
m\geq p^{(1-c_{p})n-o(n)}
and
\{(x_{i},y_{i},z_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}
, where
x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n-2}
for each
1\leq i\leq m
, such that
x_{i}+y_{j}+z_{k}=0
if and only if
i=j=k
. By this condition, if
x_{i}=x_{j}
, then as
x_{j}+y_{i}+z_{i}=0
, we have
i=j
. Thus the
x_{i}
’s are distinct, so
X=\{x_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
is of size
m
. Similarly we have that
Y=\{y_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
is of size
m
.

For simplicity, for
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n-2}
, we write
(a,b,x)=(a,b,x_{1},\dots,x_{n-2})
to denote an element in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
. Then we shall take
A=(\{(0,0)\}\times X)\cup(\{(0,1)\}\times Y)\cup(\{(1,0)\}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n-2})
. Clearly
A\supset\{(1,0)\}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n-2}
while
A+A\subseteq\{(0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(1,0),(1,1),(2,0)\}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n-2}
, so
|A+A|\leq 6|A|
.

We now show that for
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
, if
A^{\prime}+A=A+A
, then
|A^{\prime}|\geq m
. Note that
(0,1,-z_{i})=(0,0,x_{i})+(0,1,y_{i})\in A+A
. We show that
(0,1,-z_{i})
can only be represented in this way as a sum of two elements in
A
. Suppose that
u+v=(0,1,-z_{i})
. If both of
u,v
are in
\{(1,0)\}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n-2}
, then the second coordinate is
0
, which is different from
1
in
(0,1,-z_{i})
. If exactly one of
u,v
is in
\{(1,0)\}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n-2}
, then
u+v
must have the first coordinate
1
, which is different from
0
in
(0,1,-z_{i})
. Finally, the only possibility remaining is that
u,v
are both from
(\{(0,0)\}\times X)\cup(\{(0,1)\}\times Y)
. By considering the second coordinate in
u+v=(0,1,-z_{i})
, the only possible combination is that one of
u,v
is from
\{(0,0)\}\times X
and the other one from
\{(0,1)\}\times Y
. Without loss of generality, say that
u=(0,0,x_{j})
and
v=(0,1,y_{k})
. Then this implies that
x_{j}+y_{k}=-z_{i}
, which is only possible when
i=j=k
. Hence
(0,1,-z_{i})=(0,0,x_{i})+(0,1,y_{i})
is the only representation (up to permutation of the summands). Therefore, for each
1\leq i\leq m
, either
(0,0,x_{i})
or
(0,1,y_{i})
is in
A^{\prime}
if
A^{\prime}+A\ni(0,1,-z_{i})
. Hence
|A^{\prime}|\geq m
as desired.
∎

Another interesting question is to determine the optimal exponent on the doubling constant in Theorem
1.1
.

Question 7.2
.

What is the largest
C
such that the following holds? For some constant
c>0
, if
A
is a nonempty subset of an abelian group
G
and
\kappa=|A+A|/|A|
, then for each
s\leq\kappa^{C-o(1)}
, there exists
A_{(s)}\subseteq A
of size at most
s

|A+A_{(s)}|\geq cs|A|
.

As discussed previously, from Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
2.4

C\in[\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{1.29}]
, while for the setting of difference sets
A-A
we have the better lower bound
C\geq\frac{1}{2}

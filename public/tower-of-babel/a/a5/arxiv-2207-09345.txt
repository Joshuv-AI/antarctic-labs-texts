Local to global principles for homomorphisms of abelian schemes
by Wojciech Gajda, Sebastian Petersen

Abstract




Let
A
and
B
be abelian varieties defined over the function field
k(S)
of a smooth algebraic variety
S/k.
We establish criteria,
in terms of restriction maps to subvarieties of
S,
for existence of various important classes of
k(S)
-homomorphisms from
A
to
B,
e.g.,
for existence of
k(S)
-isogenies. Our main tools consist of Hilbertianity methods, Tate conjecture as proven by Tate, Zarhin and Faltings, and of the

minuscule weights conjecture
of Zarhin in the case, when the base field is finite.

.

(ii)

For every

-isogeny

\kappa

\overline{k(S)}

\overline{k(T)}

Structure of the paper.
In Sections 2 and 3 we gathered material which is needed in the sequel including basic facts on: twists of abelian varieties, Galois representations and abelian
schemes. Section 4 is a central part of the paper. It contains proofs of main results by Hilbertianity methods in the case when
k
is an infinite field or
m\geq 1.
In the final section we discuss the remaining case of
k
finite,
m{=}0
and work under extra assumptions, either the minuscule weights conjecture or trivial endomorphisms for generic fibres.

Notation

For a field
K
we denote by
\overline{K}
a separable closure of
K.

If
E/K
is a Galois extension, we denote by
\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)
its Galois group and define

\mathrm{Gal}(K):=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
. A
K
-variety is a separated algebraic
K
-scheme which is reduced and irreducible. A
K
-curve is a
K
-variety of dimension
1
. For a scheme
S
and
s\in S

we denote by
k(s)
the residue field of
s
. Let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then, as usual, we denote by
S[n^{-1}]
the
open subscheme of
S
with underlying set
\{s\in S:n\in k(s)^{\times}\}
(where
n
is viewed as an element
of
k(s)
via the ring homomorphism
{\mathbb{Z}}\to k(s)
). We let
{\mathbb{L}}
be the set of all rational primes and define
{\mathbb{L}}(S):=\{\ell\in{\mathbb{L}}:S[\ell^{-1}]\neq\emptyset\}
.
If
S
is reduced and irreducible we denote, following EGA, by
R(S)
the
function field of
S
. If
S
is a
K
-variety, we sometimes write
K(S)
instead of
R(S)
.
If
T
is a finite free
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}
-module,
V=T\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
and
\Gamma
is a subgroup
\mathrm{GL}_{T}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell})
, then we denote by
\Gamma^{\mathrm{Zar}}

the Zariski closure of
\Gamma
inside the algebraic group
\mathrm{GL}_{V}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
so that
\Gamma^{\mathrm{Zar}}
is an algebraic group of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
. If
\underline{G}
is an algebraic group over

\underline{G}^{\circ}
the connected component of the identity element of
\underline{G}
.

Twists of abelian varieties.

Let
K
be a field and
A
and
B
abelian varieties over
K
. Let
E/K
be a Galois extension.
We call
B
an
E/K
-twist of
A
if there is an
E
-isomorphism
A_{E}\to B_{E}
. In subsequent sections we are mainly interested in the case

E=\overline{K}
.
We denote by

\mathrm{Twist}_{E/K}(A)
the set of all isomorphism classes of
E/K
-twists of
A
and define
\mathrm{Twist}(A):=\mathrm{Twist}_{\overline{K}/K}(A)
.
There are natural operations
\rho_{A}:\mathrm{Gal}(K)\to\mathrm{Aut}_{K}(A_{E})
and
\rho_{B}:\mathrm{Gal}(K)\to\mathrm{Aut}_{K}(B_{E})
and an operation

\mathrm{Gal}(K)\times\mathrm{Hom}_{E}(A_{E},B_{E})\to\mathrm{Hom}_{E}(A_{E},B_{E}),\ {}^{\sigma}f:=\rho_{B}(\sigma)\circ f\circ\rho_{A}(\sigma){{}^{-1}}.

Now assume that
B/K
is an
E/K
-twist of
A
and choose an
E
-isomorphism
f:A_{E}\to B_{E}
. Then

\xi:\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)\to\mathrm{Aut}_{E}(A_{E}),\ \xi(\sigma)=f^{-1}\circ{}^{\sigma}f

is a
1
-cocycle (i.e.
\xi(\sigma\eta)=\xi(\sigma)\circ{}^{\sigma}\xi(\eta)
) whose cohomology class does not depend on the choice of
f
.
Let

\tilde{\rho}_{B}:\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)\to\mathrm{Aut}_{K}(A_{E}),\ \tilde{\rho}_{B}(\sigma)=f^{-1}\circ\rho_{B}(\sigma)\circ f

be the operation on
A
derived from
\rho_{B}
via transport of structure via
f
. Then an easy calculation shows that

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{B}(\sigma)=\xi(\sigma)\rho_{A}(\sigma)\quad\forall\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)

(1)

is simply the action
\rho_{A}
twisted by the
1
-cocycle
\xi
.
It is well-known that this sets up a
bijective map

\Xi_{E/K}:\mathrm{Twist}_{E/K}(A)\to H^{1}(\mathrm{Gal}(E/K),\mathrm{Aut}_{E}(A_{E})).

There is a natural map

j:H^{1}(\mathrm{Gal}(E/K),\{\pm Id_{A}\})\to H^{1}(\mathrm{Gal}(E/K),\mathrm{Aut}_{E}(A_{E})).

For a quadratic character
\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{Gal}(E/K),\{\pm Id_{A}\})
we denote by
A_{\chi}
an
E/K
-twist of
A

corresponding to
j(\chi)
under
\Xi_{E/K}
. We call
B

a quadratic isogeny twist of
A,
if there exists a quadratic character
\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K),\{\pm Id_{A}\})

and a
K
-isogeny
B\to A_{\chi}
. Sometimes we tacitly identify
\{\pm Id_{A}\}
with
\mu_{2}({\mathbb{Q}})=\{\pm 1\}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a finite field
K
. Then there is a unique non-trivial quadratic character
\mathrm{Gal}(K)\to\{\pm Id_{A}\}
and accordingly
a unique non-trivial quadratic twist of
A
. This is clear since
\mathrm{Gal}(K)\cong\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

Galois representations.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p\geq 0
and
A/K
an abelian variety. For a rational prime
\ell\neq p
we denote by

\rho_{A,\ell}:\mathrm{Gal}(K)\to\mathrm{GL}_{V_{\ell}(A)}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})

the corresponding
\ell
-adic Galois representation, where
T_{\ell}(A)
is the
\ell
-adic Tate
module of
A
and
V_{\ell}(A)=T_{\ell}(A)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
.
Then the Zariski closure
\rho_{A,\ell}(\mathrm{Gal}(K))^{\mathrm{Zar}}
of
\rho_{A,\ell}(\mathrm{Gal}(K))
in
\mathrm{GL}_{V_{\ell}(A)}
and its identity component

(\rho_{A,\ell}(\mathrm{Gal}(K))^{\mathrm{Zar}})^{\circ}

are algebraic groups over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
.

If
K
is finite, then we define the
L
-series of
A
by

L(A/K,T):=\det(Id_{V_{\ell}(A)}-\rho_{A,\ell}(\mathrm{Fr})T)

where
\mathrm{Fr}\in\mathrm{Gal}(K)
is the Frobenius element. This characteristic polynomial
L(A/K,T)
has integer coefficients and does not
depend on the rational prime
\ell\neq p
by the Weil conjectures. We have the following elementary but useful fact.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p\geq 0
and
\ell
a prime different from
p
. Let
A/K
and
B/K
be abelian varieties. Let
f:A\to B
be a homomorphism
and let
V_{\ell}(f):V_{\ell}(A)\to V_{\ell}(B)
be the homomorphism of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-vector spaces induced by
f
. Then:

a)

\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(\mathrm{im}(V_{\ell}(f)))=2\dim(\mathrm{im}(f))
.

b)

\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(\ker(V_{\ell}(f)))=2\dim(\ker(f))
.

c)

f:A\to B
is an isogeny if and only if the map
V_{\ell}(f)
is bijective. In particular, we have
\rho_{A,\ell}\cong\rho_{B,\ell}
provided
B
is
K
-isogenous to
A
.

Proof.
We note that
T_{\ell}(A)=\mathrm{Hom}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell},A(\overline{K}))
. For the purpose of that proof we put

T_{\ell}(M):=\mathrm{Hom}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell},M)

for an arbitrariy abelian group
M
.
Let
C=\ker(f)
Let
I=\mathrm{im}(f)
. Then
C^{\circ}
and
I
are abelian varieties.
From the exact sequence

0\to C^{\circ}(\overline{K})\to C(\overline{K})\to C(\overline{K})/C^{\circ}(\overline{K})\to 0

we derive an exact sequence

0\to T_{\ell}(C^{\circ}(\overline{K}))\to T_{\ell}(C(\overline{K}))\to T_{\ell}(C(\overline{K})/C^{\circ}(\overline{K}))

because the functor
\mathrm{Hom}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell},-)
is left exact. But
T_{\ell}(C(\overline{K})/C^{\circ}(\overline{K}))=0
because the group
C(\overline{K})/C^{\circ}(\overline{K})
is finite. Thus

T_{\ell}(C^{\circ}(\overline{K}))=T_{\ell}(C(\overline{K}))
. From the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to C(\overline{K})\to A(\overline{K})\to I(\overline{K})\to 0

(2)

we obtain an exact sequence
0\to T_{\ell}(C(\overline{K}))\to T_{\ell}(A(\overline{K}))\to T_{\ell}(I(\overline{K}))
, again because the functor
\mathrm{Hom}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell},-)
is left exact. Using

T_{\ell}(C^{\circ}(\overline{K}))=T_{\ell}(C(\overline{K}))
and tensoring with
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
we see that we have an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to V_{\ell}(C^{\circ})\to V_{\ell}(A)\to V_{\ell}(I).

(3)

Let
c=\dim(C)
,
a=\dim(A)
and
i=\dim(I)
. Then
a=i+c
by (
2
). Also
\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(V_{\ell}(C))=2c
,

\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(V_{\ell}(A))=2a
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(V_{\ell}(I))=2i
(cf.
[
15
, Remark 8.4]
). The image of the right hand map of the exact sequence
(
3
) has dimension
2a-2c=2i
, too, hence
V_{\ell}(A)\to V_{\ell}(I)
is surjective. By the left exactness of
\mathrm{Hom}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell},-)
we also see that

V_{\ell}(I)\to V_{\ell}(B)
is injective. Thus
V_{\ell}(I)=\mathrm{im}(V_{\ell}(f))
and
V_{\ell}(C^{\circ})=\ker(V_{\ell}(f))
. It follows that

\displaystyle\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(\ker(V_{\ell}(f))){=}\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(V_{\ell}(C^{\circ})){=}2\dim(C^{\circ}){=2}\dim(C){=}2\dim(\ker(f)),

\displaystyle\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(\mathrm{im}(V_{\ell}(f))){=}\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(V_{\ell}(I)){=}2\dim(I){=}2\dim(\mathrm{im}(f)),

as desired. This finishes the proof of a) and b). For c) put
b:=\dim(B)
and note that:

f
is an isogeny

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mbox{$f$ is surjective with a finite kernel}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\dim(\ker(f)){=}0\,\,{\rm and}\,\dim(\mathrm{im}(f)){=}b

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(\ker(V_{\ell}(f))){=}0\,\,{\rm and}\,\dim_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}(\mathrm{im}(V_{\ell}(f))){=}2b

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mbox{$V_{\ell}(f)$ is bijective.}

\Box

(b)

Remark 2.5

Let

(b)

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
K
be a finitely generated field of characteristic
p\geq 0
.
Let
A,B
be abelian varieties over
K
and
\ell\neq p
a rational prime. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(a)

B
is a quadratic isogeny twist of
A
.

(b)

There exists a quadratic character
\chi:\mathrm{Gal}(K)\to\{\pm Id_{A}\}
such that
\rho_{B,\ell}\cong\chi\otimes\rho_{A,\ell}
.

If
K
is finite, then the equivalent conditions (a) and (b) are also equivalent to

(c)

L(B/K,T)=L(A/K,T)
or
L(B/K,T)=L(A/K,-T)
.

Proof.
We prove the implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(b): Assume
B
is a quadratic isogeny twist of
A
. Then there exists a quadratic character

\chi:\mathrm{Gal}(K)\to\{\pm Id_{A}\}
and a
K
-isogeny
B\to A_{\chi}
. From Lemma
2.2
and Remark
2.5
we conclude that

\rho_{B,\ell}\cong\rho_{A_{\chi},\ell}\cong\chi\otimes\rho_{A,\ell}.

For the proof of the implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(a) assume that there
exists a quadratic character
\chi:\mathrm{Gal}(K)\to\{\pm Id_{A}\}
such that
\rho_{B,\ell}\cong\chi\otimes\rho_{A,\ell}
. From Remark
2.5
we conclude
that
\rho_{B,\ell}\cong\rho_{A_{\chi},\ell}
and then Corollary
2.4
implies that there exists an isogeny
B\to A_{\chi}
. It follows that
B
is a quadratic
isogeny twist of
A
.

From now on assume that
K
is finite. Then
\mathrm{Gal}(K)=\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and thus there exist exactly two quadratic characters thereof.

We prove the implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(c): If
\chi
is the trivial character then
\rho_{B,\ell}\cong\rho_{A,\ell}
and hence
L(A/K,T)=L(B/K,T)
.
If
\chi
is non-trivial, then
\rho_{B,\ell}\cong\rho_{A_{\chi},\ell}
, hence

L(B/K,T)=L(A_{\chi},T)=L(A/K,-T)

by Corollary
2.4
and Remark
2.5
.

We prove the implication (c)

\mathrm{Hom}_{k(S)}(A,B)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\overline{k(S)}}(A_{\overline{k(S)}},B_{\overline{k(S)}}).

The implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(b) is immediate from Lemma

3.6
and Remark
3.7
.
We prove the other
implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(a): Let
K=R(S)
and let
\ell\neq\mathrm{char}(K)
be a rational prime.
By Corollary
4.2
there exists a
T\in\mathrm{Sm}_{1}(S/k)
of
S
such that
T
is
\rho_{A\times B,\ell}
-generic. Then
T
is automatically

\rho_{A,\ell}
-generic and
\rho_{B,\ell}
-generic. In particular,
\rho_{A,\ell}(\mathrm{Gal}(K))^{\mathrm{Zar}}=\rho_{A_{T},\ell}(\mathrm{Gal}(k(T)))^{\mathrm{Zar}}
.
In particular,
A_{T}/k(T)
satisfies
MWC(A_{T})
. Similarly
B_{T}/k(T)
satisfies
MWC(B_{T})
. By (b), for

. By

.

.

We can upgrade Theorem
5.5
a bit, to treat not only non-zero homomorphisms but also other important classes of homomorphisms, much in the spirit of Corollary
B
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
S
be a smooth variety over a finite
field
k
. Let
\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}
be abelian schemes over
S
with
generic fibers
A
and
B
respectively.
Assume that
A
satisfies

MWC(A)
and
B
satisfies
MWC(B)
.
The following are equivalent:

(a)

There exists a surjective
\overline{k(S)}
-homomorphism (resp.
\overline{k(S)}
-isogeny)
A_{\overline{k(S)}}\to B_{\overline{k(S)}}
.

(b)

For every closed point
s\in S
there exists a surjective
\overline{k(s)}
-homomorphism (resp.
\overline{k(s)}
-isogeny)

A_{s,\overline{k(s)}}\to B_{s,\overline{k(s)}}
.

Proof.
The implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(b) is immediate from Lemma
3.6
.

We prove the implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(a) in case “surjective”.
After replacing
S
by one of its connected finite étale covers we can assume that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{k(S)}(A,B)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\overline{k(S)}}(A_{\overline{k(S)}},B_{\overline{k(S)}}),

(13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{End}_{k(S)}(A)=\mathrm{End}_{\overline{k(S)}}(A_{\overline{k(S)}}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{End}_{k(S)}(B)=\mathrm{End}_{\overline{k(S)}}(B_{\overline{k(S)}}).

By the Poincaré reducibility theorem (cf.
[
15
, Prop. 12.1]
and the passage below)
A
(resp.
B
) is
k(S)
-isogenous to
\prod_{i\in I}A_{i}^{n_{i}}

(resp.
\prod_{j\in J}B_{j}^{m_{i}}
) where
I
and
J
are finite sets,
the
A_{i}
(
i\in I
) are mutually not
k(S)
-isogenous
simple abelian varieties over
k(S)
and the
B_{j}
(
j\in J
)) are mutually not
k(S)
-isogenous
simple abelian varieties over
k(S)
.
We consider the commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{End}^{0}_{\overline{k(S)}}(A_{\overline{k(S)}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\prod_{i,j\in I}\mathrm{Hom}^{0}_{\overline{k(S)}}(A_{i,\overline{k(S)}},A_{j,\overline{k(S)}})^{n_{i}\times n_{j}}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{End}^{0}_{k(S)}(A_{k(S)})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\prod_{i,j\in I}\mathrm{Hom}^{0}_{k(S)}(A_{i},A_{j})^{n_{i}\times n_{j}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\prod_{i,j\in I}s_{ij}^{n_{i}\times n_{j}}}

where the

s_{ij}:\mathrm{Hom}^{0}_{k(S)}(A_{i},A_{j})\to\mathrm{Hom}^{0}_{\overline{k(S)}}(A_{i,\overline{k(S)}},A_{j,\overline{k(S)}})

are the canonical maps.
The horizontal maps in the diagram are bijective. The left hand vertical map is bijective by
(
13
). Thus the
s_{ij}
are bijective, too.
For
i\neq j
we have
A_{i}\not\simeq A_{j}
, hence

\mathrm{Hom}^{0}_{\overline{k(S)}}(A_{i,\overline{k(S)}},A_{j,\overline{k(S)}})=\mathrm{Hom}^{0}_{k(S)}(A_{i},A_{j})=0,

and thus the

A_{i,\overline{k(S)}}
are mutually non-isogenous over
\overline{k(S)}
. As
A_{i}
is simple it follows that

\mathrm{End}^{0}_{\overline{k(S)}}(A_{i,\overline{k(S)}})=\mathrm{End}^{0}_{k(S)}(A_{i})
is a division algebra over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and thus the

A_{i,\overline{k(S)}}
are simple.
Likewise the

B_{j,\overline{k(S)}}
are
simple and mutually non-isogenous over
\overline{k(S)}
. Note that
MWC(A_{i})
and
MWC(B_{j})
holds true (cf. Remark
5.2
).

After replacing
S
by one of its dense open subschemes each
A_{i}
(resp.
B_{j}
) extends to an abelian scheme
\mathscr{A}_{i}
(resp.
\mathscr{B}_{j}
) over
S
. It is then clear that for every closed point

s\in S
the fibre
A_{s,\overline{k(s)}}
is
\overline{k(s)}
-isogenous to
\prod_{i\in I}A_{i,s,\overline{k(s)}}^{n_{i}}
and

B_{s,\overline{k(s)}}
is
\overline{k(s)}
-isogenous to
\prod_{j\in J}B_{j,s,\overline{k(s)}}^{m_{j}}
. In what follows we can thus asssume, without loss of generality, that
A=\prod_{i\in I}A_{i}^{n_{i}}
and that
B=\prod_{j\in J}B_{j}^{m_{j}}.
From
(b) we know that for every closed point
s\in S
there exists a surjective
\overline{k(s)}
-homomorphism

A_{s,\overline{k(s)}}\to B_{s,\overline{k(s)}}
and thus for every
j\in J
a surjective homomorphism

A_{s,\overline{k(s)}}\to B_{j,s,\overline{k(s)}}
. By Theorem
5.5
there exists for every
j\in J
a non-zero homomorphism

A_{\overline{k(S)}}\to B_{j,\overline{k(S)}}
. Thus, for every
j\in J
, there must be a unique
\nu(j)\in I
, such that there exists a non-zero
\overline{k(S)}
-homomorphism

h_{j}:A_{\nu(j),\overline{k(S)}}\to B_{j,\overline{k(S)}}
and
h_{j}
must be a
\overline{k(S)}
-isogeny, because

A_{\nu(j),\overline{k(S)}}
and
B_{j,\overline{k(S)}}
are both simple.

We now show that
n_{\nu(j)}\geq m_{j},
for all
j\in J
.
Let
j\in J
be arbitrary. Let
A^{\prime}_{j}=\prod_{i\in I\setminus\{\nu(j)\}}A_{i}^{n_{i}}
. Then for every
j\in J
we have
A=A_{\nu(j)}^{n_{\nu(j)}}\times A_{j}^{\prime}
. There is no
non-zero

\overline{k(S)}
-homomorphism
A_{j,\overline{k(S)}}^{\prime}\to B_{j,\overline{k(S)}}
, because the
A_{i,\overline{k(S)}}
are mutually
non-isogenous over
\overline{k(S)}
and
A_{\nu(j),\overline{k(S)}}\simeq B_{j,\overline{k(S)}}
.
The contraposition of Theorem
5.5
implies that
there exists a closed point
s\in S
such that there is no non-zero
\overline{k(s)}
-homomorphism

A_{j,s,\overline{k(s)}}^{\prime}\to B_{j,s,\overline{k(s)}}
. Furthermore, by (b), there is a surjective

\overline{k(s)}
-homomorphism

A_{s,\overline{k(s)}}=A_{\nu(j),s,\overline{k(s)}}^{n_{\nu(j)}}\times A_{j,s,\overline{k(s)}}^{\prime}\to B_{j,s,\overline{k(s)}}^{m_{j}}

which restricts to a surjective
\overline{k(s)}
-homomorphism

A_{\nu(j),s,\overline{k(s)}}^{n_{\nu(j)}}\times\{0\}\to B_{j,s,\overline{k(s)}}^{m_{j}}.

As
\dim(A_{\nu(j),s,\overline{k(s)}})=\dim(B_{j,s,\overline{k(s)}})
,
this finally shows that
n_{\nu(j)}\geq m_{j}
as claimed above.

This together with the existence of the isogeny
h_{j}
implies the existence of a surjective
\overline{k(S)}
-homomorphism
f_{j}:A_{\nu(j),\overline{k(S)}}^{n_{\nu(j)}}\to B_{j,\overline{k(S)}}^{m_{j}},
and these in turn induce
a surjective
\overline{k(S)}
-homomorphism

\displaystyle\prod_{j\in J}f_{j}:\prod_{j\in J}A_{\nu(j),\overline{k(S)}}^{n_{\nu(j)}}\to\prod_{j\in J}B_{j,\overline{k(S)}}^{m_{j}}=B_{\overline{k(S)}}.

(14)

Composing the surjective
\overline{k(S)}
-homomorphism
(
14
)
with the
projection
A_{\overline{k(S)}}\to\prod_{j\in J}A_{\nu(j),\overline{k(S)}}^{n_{\nu(j)}}
we obtain a

\overline{k(S)}
-epimorphism
A_{\overline{k(S)}}\to B_{\overline{k(S)}}
as desired.

We next prove the implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(a) in case “isogeny”. In that case we know by (b) that
for every closed point
s\in S
there exists a
\overline{k(s)}
-isogeny

g_{s}:A_{s,\overline{k(s)}}\to B_{s,\overline{k(s)}}
. The above arguments imply the
existence of a surjective
\overline{k(S)}
-homomorphism
g:A_{\overline{k(S)}}\to B_{\overline{k(S)}}
. Let
s\in S
be
a closed point. Then, by the existence of the isogeny
g_{s}
,

\dim(A)=\dim(A_{s,\overline{k(s)}})=\dim(B_{s,\overline{k(s)}})=\dim(B),

and thus

g
must automatically be an isogeny.
\Box

We shall now establish a global function field analogue of
[
9
, Cor. 2.7]
, following proof in
loc. cit.
quite closely. For this we need the following theorem
of Rajan
[
17
]
. Let
K
be a global field and denote by
\Sigma_{K}
the set of all non-archimedian discrete valuations of
K
. Let
\ell\neq\mathrm{char}(K)
be a rational prime and let
\rho_{1}:\mathrm{Gal}(K)\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
and
\rho_{2}:\mathrm{Gal}(K)\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
be continuous semisimple representations which are unramified outside a finite set
S\subset\Sigma_{K}
. Define

SM(\rho_{1},\rho_{2}):=\{v\in\Sigma_{K}:Tr(\rho_{1}(Fr_{v}))=Tr(\rho_{2}(Fr_{v}))\}.

Theorem 5.7
.

(Rajan,
[
17
, Thm. 2]
) Suppose that the Zariski closure
H_{1}
of
\rho_{1}(\mathrm{Gal}(K))
is connected and that the upper Dirichlet density of
SM(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
is
positive. Then the following holds true.

(a)

There is a finite Galois extension
L/K
such that
\rho_{1}|_{\mathrm{Gal}(L)}{\cong}\rho_{2}|_{\mathrm{Gal}(L)}
and the connected component of the Zariski closure of
\rho_{2}(\mathrm{Gal}(K))
is
conjugate to
H_{1}
.

(b)

If
\rho_{1}
is absolutely irreducible, then there is a Dirichlet character
\chi:\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)\to{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
of finite order such that
\rho_{2}\cong\chi\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}\rho_{1}
.

We have the following global function field analogue of
[
9
, Cor. 2.7]
.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
S
be a smooth curve over a finite
field
k
. Let
\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}
be abelian schemes over
S
with
generic fibers
A
and
B,
respectively. Let
\ell\neq\mathrm{char}(k)
be a rational prime and let
K=k(S)
.
Let
U
be a dense open subscheme of
S
.
Assume that
\mathrm{End}_{\overline{k(S)}}(A)=\mathrm{End}_{\overline{k(S)}}(B)={\mathbb{Z}}
and that the Zariski closures of
\rho_{A,\ell}(\mathrm{Gal}(K))
and of

\rho_{B,\ell}(\mathrm{Gal}(K))
are connected.
If the set of all closed points
u\in U
such that

A_{u}
is a quadratic isogeny twist of
B_{u}
has Dirichlet density
1
, then
A
is a quadratic isogeny twist of
B
.

Proof.
Let
\Gamma
be the set of all
u\in U
such that

A_{u}
is a quadratic isogeny twist of
B_{u}
. Assume that
\Gamma
has Dirichlet density
1
. We claim that
SM(\rho_{\ell,A},\rho_{\ell,B})
has positive upper
Dirichlet density. Consider the two sets

\Gamma_{\pm}:=\{u\in\Gamma:Tr(\rho_{A,\ell}(Fr_{u}))=\pm Tr(\rho_{B,\ell}(Fr_{u}))\}.

For every
u\in\Gamma
we have
Tr(\rho_{A,\ell}(Fr_{u}))\in\{\pm Tr(\rho_{B,\ell}(Fr_{u}))\}
by Lemma
2.6
(implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(c) applied over
k(u)
). Thus
\Gamma=\Gamma_{+}\cup\Gamma_{-}
. Moreover
\Gamma_{+}\subset SM(\rho_{\ell,A},\rho_{\ell,B})
. If the inclusion were wrong, then it would follow that
\Gamma_{+}
has upper Dirichlet density zero and that
\Gamma_{-}
has Dirichlet density
1
. But then the Chebotarev density theorem would imply that
Tr(\rho_{A,\ell}(g))=-Tr(\rho_{B,\ell}(g))
for all
g\in\mathrm{Gal}(K)
, which is obviously false for
g=Id
. Thus the inclusion
\Gamma_{+}\subset SM(\rho_{\ell,A},\rho_{\ell,B})
holds true.

Our assumption
\mathrm{End}_{\overline{k(S)}}(A)=\mathrm{End}_{\overline{k(S)}}(B)={\mathbb{Z}}
implies that
\rho_{A,\ell}
and
\rho_{B,\ell}
are absolutely irreducible.

Now, because of the claim, Theorem
5.7
implies that there is a finite Galois extension
L/K
and a character
\chi:\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)\to{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
of finite order such that
\rho_{A,\ell}=\chi\otimes\rho_{B,\ell}
. But now, by Theorem
2.3
, we have an isomorphism of
\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
-modules

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{L}(A_{L},B_{L})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\cong V_{\ell}(A)^{*}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}V_{\ell}(B)\cong

\displaystyle\cong V_{\ell}(A)^{*}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}V_{\ell}(A)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}(\chi)\cong

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{End}_{L}(A_{L})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}(\chi)\cong{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}(\chi).

Thus
\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
acts on
\mathrm{Hom}_{L}(A_{L},B_{L})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
via
\chi
and this implies that
\chi
is a
quadratic
character. Lemma
2.6
(implication
(b)
\Rightarrow
(a) applied over
K
) now implies that
B
is a quadratic isogeny twist of
A
.
\Box

Again one can eliminate the assumption
\dim(S)=1
by the Hilbertianity approach of the previous section.

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
S
be a smooth variety over a finite
field
k
. Let
\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}
be abelian schemes over
S
with
generic fibers
A
and
B
respectively. Let
U
be a dense open subscheme of
S
.
Assume that
End_{\overline{k(S)}}(A)=End_{\overline{k(S)}}(B)={\mathbb{Z}}
.
The following are equivalent:

(a)

A
is a quadratic isogeny twist of
B
.

(b)

For every closed point
u
of
U
the abelian variety

A_{u}
is a quadratic isogeny twist of
B_{u}
.

Proof.
The implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(b) is known from Theorem
4.7
. We prove the other implication.
Assume that for every closed point
u
of
U
the abelian variety

A_{u}
is a quadratic isogeny twist of
B_{u}
. Then, for every smooth connected curve
T
on
U
the abelian variety
A_{T}
is a quadratic isogeny twist of
B_{T}
by Proposition
5.8
. Thus Theorem
4.7
implies (a).
\Box

Remark 5.10
.

It is easy to show that if
A
and
B
in Corollary
5.9
are elliptic curves with nontrivial endomorphisms, then the claim still holds. It is so, since then j-invariants
j(A)
and
j(B)
belong to
\overline{k},
hence the curves are isotrivial cf.
[
21
, V.3.1 and Exerc.V.5.8]
, so a twist between fibres at
u\in U
extends to a twist between the curves. It is an interesting question, if Corollary
5.9
holds true for nonsimple abelian varieties, e.g., for products of mutually nonisogenous elliptic curves over
k(S).

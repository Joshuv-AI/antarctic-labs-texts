Isoparametric Virtual Element Methods
Andrea Cangiani, Andreas Dedner, Matthew Hubbard, Harry Wells

Abstract
We present two approaches to constructing isoparametric Virtual Element Methods of arbitrary order for linear elliptic partial differential equations on general two-dimensional domains. The first method approximates the variational problem transformed onto a computational reference domain. The second method computes a virtual domain and uses bespoke polynomial approximation operators to construct a computable method. Both methods are shown to converge optimally, a behaviour confirmed in practice for the solution of problems posed on curved domains.



keywords
Isoparametric Methods; Virtual Element Methods, Curved Edge Polygons, Second-order Problems, DUNE.

1
Introduction

In recent years there has been an increasing interest in exploiting the mesh flexibility allowed by the Virtual Element Method (VEM)
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2013)
for the efficient discretisation of problems posed on general transformed and time-dependent domains
Lipnikov and
Morgan (2019)
;
Lipnikov and
Morgan (2020)
;
Mazzia
et al. (2020)
;
Wells et al. (2024)
, including a broader interest on combining the VEM with other numerical methods
(e.g. discontinuous Galerkin)
Antonietti
et al. (2019)
;
Gaburro et al. (2020)
;
Gaburro (2021)
;
Huang and
Wang (2020)
.
While these studies have already demonstrated the effective utilisation of mesh flexibility permitted by the VEM through compelling numerical experiments, a rigorous theoretical foundation to support the VEM’s applicability in this context is still lacking.

Motivated by the need to fill such a theoretical gap, we analyse the effect of domain discretisation errors in the solution of
steady
problems posed on general domains, as a starting point to develop the required theory to analyse the aforementioned approaches on time-dependent domains. In particular, we propose and analyse
Isoparametric Virtual Element Methods
(IsoVEMs) for the solution of linear elliptic problems posed on general curved domains in two dimensions given as the image of a smooth transformation of a
reference polygonal domain
.

Under the classical definition
Ciarlet (2002)
, isoparametric FEMs are characterised by the use of the same finite element space for the solution as that used to approximate the domain geometry.
Typically, the geometric elements are constructed from a single reference element via finite element transformations of a given degree. Then, in order to obtain balanced domain and solution approximations, the problem is discretised using a finite element space of the same degree.
In the case of the VEM, we have no obvious concept of a reference element as virtual element discretisations are constructed directly on the
physical
domain to allow for heterogeneous elements. Thus, we consider, instead, the concept of a
reference polygonal domain
from which a computational domain and mesh approximating the physical domain can be constructed. For instance, the reference polygonal domain may simply be obtained by a fixed linear interpolation of the physical domain’s boundary. We note that this idea naturally extends to time-dependent problems whereby the reference polygonal domain may be constructed on the reference (initial) configuration.
In principle, once the reference polygonal domain is given, the isoparametric FEM’s idea extends to the VEM as we could use a standard virtual element space to define a continuous transformation of the reference domain on which a same order VEM may be used to solve the problem given on the transformed domain. However, the virtual nature of this transformation makes this naive approach impractical in general, as the evaluation of virtual element functions on the resulting isoparametric elements may not be available.
Instead, we introduce two approaches to construct a computable isoparametric VEM, herein referred to as
reference
and
physical
IsoVEM. These methods are designed in such a way that they emulate the classical isoparametric finite element method
Ciarlet and
Raviart (1972)
;
Lenoir (1986)
extended to polygonal meshes.

To motivate our consideration of two isoparametric methods, we emphasize the role of PDE data in the context of a given problem. Often, the PDE data may be constrained to a specific computational reference frame, as exemplified in Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian schemes for computational fluid dynamics (CFD) applications
Donea et al. (2004)
;
Richter (2017)
. In other scenarios, the PDE data might need to be computed or approximated within the physical reference frame and then extended to the PDE domain. These varying requirements of PDE data handling motivates the consideration of separate IsoVEMs, accommodating the framework of different computational and physical contexts.

In the reference IsoVEM, we consider the problem mapped back to the reference polygonal domain by a discrete VEM mapping. A VEM is then used to approximate the terms resulting from this transformation such as the Jacobian operator, its determinant, and inverse Jacobian operator. Using these approximations results in a computable VEM on the reference polygonal domain. This framework closely aligns with the approach taken in
Lipnikov and
Morgan (2019)
;
Lipnikov and
Morgan (2020)
in which Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian schemes are constructed on a computational reference domain using approximations of the transformed weak formulations. We note, however, that in
Lipnikov and
Morgan (2019)
;
Lipnikov and
Morgan (2020)
the solution itself is computed by a discontinuous Galerkin method and no analysis is presented to support the proposed method.

For the physical IsoVEM, we consider the problem on the domain generated by the VEM approximation of the true reference-to-physical transformation. As such, the computational mesh is made of curved polygons with polynomial edges. Here we take advantage of the curved virtual element space presented in
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
for the solution of the constant coefficients Poisson problem and generalised here to a broader class of linear elliptic problems. To ensure the method is computatable, we introduce bespoke polynomial approximation operators such that the consistency and stability terms can be easily computed to a sufficient degree of accuracy.
Note that we could have instead considered directly the curved VEM of
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
defined on the true physical domain, hence avoiding the introduction of a geometrical error.
Our, alternative, approach is motivated by computability considerations.
While in curved VEM the mapping used to generate the curved elements is generally unknown and integration over curved elements must be approximated, the polynomial approximation employed in our isoparametric approach is available and uses the minimum amount of quadrature point evaluations whilst still maintaining the same degree of accuracy. Moreover, our approach becomes the only one available for three-dimensional problems, as in this case mesh faces are themselves virtual.
Our approach is also relevant to time-dependent problems, when only the discrete domain is available as it is itself obtained as the numerical solution of a problem determining the evolution of the domain such as in fluid-structure interaction.
On the other hand, our approach requires a more involved analysis, accounting for the domain approximation error.

To support the proposed methods, we present
H^{1}
-norm
a priori
error estimates which show that the same degree of accuracy can be achieved as the classical isoparametric finite element method.
For brevity, we limit the scope of the analysis to two-dimensional problems but emphasise that the analysis can be extended to three-dimensional problems with additional work.

In addition, we implement both methods using the DUNE-VEM module
Dedner and Hodson (2024)
which is part of the Distributed and Unified Numerics Environment
Bastian
et al. (2021)
;
Dedner
et al. (2012)
. Numerical experiments within DUNE validate the
H^{1}
-norm estimates and obtain optimal rates of convergence in the
H^{1}
-norm and
L^{2}
-norm error.

Although the motivation of this paper is the transformation of two-dimensional domains, the framework relates to the field of surface PDEs. Indeed there has been recent publications in developing VEMs for surface problems such as the Laplace-Beltrami equation and bulk-surface PDEs
Frittelli
et al. (2021)
;
Frittelli and
Sgura (2018)
, elliptic
Bachini
et al. (2021)
and diffusion-reaction
Li et al. (2022)
equations on surfaces. In particular, the methods presented in
Bachini
et al. (2021)
and
Li et al. (2022)
closely relate to the proposed methods in this paper. The former relating to our first proposed discretization and the latter being equivalent to a two-dimensional, linear case of our second method. This paper can be viewed as an extension on these ideas, providing a foundation to develop higher-order methods and derive error estimates.

The layout of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
we define the PDE and domain transformation considered in the analysis and present the weak formulation of this problem. In Section
3
we recall the fundamentals of the VEM and present the curved VEM from
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
for general elliptic problems. We then formulate the reference and physical IsoVEMs in Section
6
and detail their a priori analysis in Section
7
. Numerical results are presented in Section
8
for a set of transformations and PDEs. Concluding remarks are made in Section
9
.

2
Preliminaries

Let
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n=1,2
, denote an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
m
a non-negative integer and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we denote by
W_{m}^{p}(\omega)
the respective Sobolev space endowed with norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{m,p,\omega}
and semi-norm
\left|\cdot\right|_{m,p,\omega}
. In the case of
p=2
we denote the corresponding Hilbert space by
H^{m}(\omega)
with norm and semi-norm denoted by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{m,\omega}
and
\left|\cdot\right|_{m,\omega}
, respectively. Finally, with
\mathbb{P}_{k}(\omega)
we denote the space of polynomials of degree
k\in\mathbb{N}
over
\omega
and with
\mathbb{P}_{k}
the polynomial space over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

We use the relation
x\lesssim y
to indicate the existence of an hidden constant
C>0
such that
x\leq Cy
. Unless stated explicitly otherwise, it is assumed that this hidden constant is independent of the solution and the discretisation parameter
h
.

2.1
Model problem

On
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
a bounded Lipschitz domain, we consider the boundary value problem

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(a\nabla u)+\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u+cu

\displaystyle=f\ \ \ \text{in }\Omega,

(2.1)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\ \ \text{on }\partial\Omega,

(2.2)

with given coefficients
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\mathbf{b}\in\left[L^{\infty}(\Omega)\right]^{2}
,
c\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and forcing function
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. We assume the existence of two constants
a_{0}>0
and
\mu_{0}\geq 0
and
\mu:=c-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{b}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

a_{0}\leq a\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{0}\leq\mu\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega.

From this, we consider the weak formulation with convective term in skew-symmetric form: find
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\mathcal{A}(u,v):=A(u,v)+B(u,v)+C(u,v)=l(v)\ \ \ \forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

(2.3)

where, for all
u,v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle A(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}a\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\ d\mathbf{x},\quad B(u,v):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{b}\cdot(v\nabla u-u\nabla v)\ d\mathbf{x},

(2.4)

\displaystyle C(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\mu\ uv\ d\mathbf{x},\qquad\,\,\,\,l(v):=\int_{\Omega}fv\ d\mathbf{x}.

(2.5)

It is well known that this weak formulation has a unique solution in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
by the Lax-Milgram Theorem.

Remark 1
.

It is by all means possible to slightly relax the above assumptions on the coefficients and to consider the weak form obtained without writing the convective term in skew-symmetric form by following e.g.
Beirão Da Veiga
et al. (2016)
. We refrain from doing so here to avoid inessential technicalities.

2.2
Reference domain

We assume we are given a polygonal reference domain
\hat{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that there exists a bijective reference-to-physical transformation
\mathbf{F}:\hat{\Omega}\rightarrow\Omega
.
We further suppose that
\mathbf{F}
is bi-Lipschitz, in line with assumptions made within ALE analysis
Bonito
et al. (2013a)
;
Bonito
et al. (2013b)
;
Formaggia and
Nobile (2004)
;
Gastaldi (2001)
and that
\mathbf{F}\in\left[W^{m+1}_{\infty}(\hat{\Omega})\right]^{2}
for some integer
m\in\mathbb{N}
to be specified later. The bi-Lipschitz condition allows for the analysis to be conducted independently of regularity conditions on
\mathbf{F}^{-1}
and its corresponding VEM approximation. This contrasts with the classical isoparametric FEM work in which an inverse mapping between isoparametric finite elements is easily computable
Ciarlet and
Raviart (1972)
;
Lenoir (1986)
.

As a consequence, the Jacobian matrix

\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}:=\nabla\mathbf{F},

is invertible
Bonito
et al. (2013a)
;
Bonito
et al. (2013b)
. Moreover, the determinant of
\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}
is uniformly bounded and strictly positive; namely, there exists a
j_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

j_{0}\leq j:=\text{det}\left(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}\right)\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\Omega}).

The domain
\hat{\Omega}
is the basis of the IsoVEM approach. The polygonal meshes defined over
\hat{\Omega}
will serve as collections of polygonal reference elements. Corresponding virtual maps constructed over the reference elements will provide the computational meshes approximating
\Omega
.

Remark 2
.

For instance,
\hat{\Omega}
could just be defined as a polygon whose boundary interpolates
\partial\Omega
. In this case, the IsoVEM introduced below provides a direct generalisation of the classical isoparametric finite elements on general meshes.

To account for the discrepancy between the problem domain and the computational domain, we require the availability of extension operators, which is guaranteed by the well-known Stein’s Extension Theorem
Stein (1971)
;
Adams and Fournier (2003)
.

Theorem 1

(The Stein Extension Theorem)
.

Let
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be bounded and Lipschitz. For all
p\in[1,\infty]
and integers
s\geq 0
there exists an extension operator
\mathfrak{E}:W_{p}^{s}(\omega)\rightarrow W_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that, for all
v\in W^{s}_{p}(\omega)
,

\mathfrak{E}v=v
a.e. in
\omega
and
\left\|\mathfrak{E}v\right\|_{s,p,\mathbb{R}^{2}}\lesssim\left\|v\right\|_{s,p,\omega}
,
where the hidden constant depends only on
s
and
p
.

In this paper, extensions will be required for the PDE data and exact solutions; for short we shall denote these by
\tilde{v}:=\mathfrak{E}v
and so on.

3
C^{0}
-conforming VEM

In this section we present an enhanced version of the
C^{0}
-conforming VEM on two-dimensional polygonal meshes with curved edges from
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
. We present these spaces in a general form and independent of the IsoVEM frames of reference for ease of reading, noting that the standard VEM spaces on polygonal meshes can be considered as a special case of this presentation
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2013)
;
Ahmad
et al. (2013)
;
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2014)
. The implementation details for VEMs are well documented in the literature
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2014)
;
Sutton (2017)
;
Dedner and Hodson (2024)
and thus are omitted.

A mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is a partitioning of a general domain
\Omega
into simple, non-overlapping polygons

E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
with curved edges of class
C^{m+1}
. The mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is characterised by the global mesh size
h:=\max_{E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{E}
with
h_{E}
denoting the diameter of
E
. We further denote by
e
a generic (possibly curved) edge belonging to
\partial E
.

For
k\geq 0
integer we denote by
\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)
,
E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the space of polynomials of degree up to
k
and by
\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
the corresponding space of piecewise polynomials with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

The mesh is required to satisfy the following shape regularity assumptions which are standard in the VEM literature
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2013)
;
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2014)
;
Ahmad
et al. (2013)
.

Assumption 1

(Shape regularity)
.

Every
E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is a star-shaped domain or a finite union of star shaped domains with respect to a ball of radius greater than
\alpha h_{E}
for some uniform
\alpha>0
. Additionally, the length of any edge
e\in\partial E
is greater than
\alpha h_{E}
.

The above assumption readily ensures the availability of polynomial best approximation results, see e.g. Theorem 1.45 in
Di Pietro and Droniou (2020)
.

Theorem 2

(
L^{2}
-projection accuracy)
.

Let a polynomial degree
k\geq 0
, an integer
s\in\{0,...,k+1\}
, and a real number
p\in[1,\infty]
be given. Then, the piecewise
L^{2}
-projection operator

\Pi^{0}_{k}:L^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

satisfies, on each element
E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

\left|v-\Pi^{0}_{k}v\right|_{l,p,E}\lesssim h_{E}^{s-l}\left|v\right|_{s,p,E},

for all
v\in W_{p}^{s}(E)
and
l\in\{0,...,s\}
.

3.1
Enhanced curved VEM spaces

Let
k\geq 1
be fixed. We introduce the enhanced version of the curved-edged polygonal mesh VEM of order
k
from
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
.

Given
E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define for each edge
e\subset\partial E
its corresponding reference interval
I_{e}=[0,l_{e}]
where
l_{e}=\left|e\right|
. We denote the (invertible) arc length parameterisation of the edge
e
by
\gamma_{e}:I_{e}\rightarrow e
and introduce over
e
the mapped polynomial space of degree
k\geq 1
as

\mathbb{P}^{\gamma}_{k}(e)=\{q\in L^{2}(e)\ :\ q=\hat{q}\circ\gamma_{e}^{-1},\ \hat{q}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{e})\}.

Note that if
e
is straight then
\gamma_{e}
is affine and it follows that
\mathbb{P}^{\gamma}_{k}(e)\equiv\mathbb{P}_{k}(e)
. Otherwise,
\mathbb{P}^{\gamma}_{k}(e)
is not made of polynomials in general.
In line with the construction of polygonal enhanced virtual element spaces
Ahmad
et al. (2013)
, we first introduce the enlarged local space

W_{k}(E):=\{v_{h}\in H^{1}(E)\cap C^{0}(\overline{E})\ :\ \Delta v_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(E),\ v_{h}|_{e}\in\mathbb{P}^{\gamma}_{k}(e)\ \forall e\subset\partial E\}.

Remark 3
.

We note that the parametrisation of each curved edge by
I=[0,l_{e}]
is a choice we make here to simplify the presentation. Alternatives include parametrising on the unit interval
[0,1]
or a reference edge
\hat{e}
from a computational reference polygon
\hat{E}
as in the original presentation
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
.

We define a set of linear forms which will be used as degrees of freedom for the curved VEM space defined below in (
3.1
).

Definition 1

(Virtual element
DoF
)
.

For a regular enough function
v
of
E
, we introduce the following degrees of freedom:

•

the point value of
v
at all vertices of
E
;

•

for all
e\subset\partial E
, the edge moments

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{e}}\int_{I_{e}}(v_{h}|_{e}\circ\gamma_{e})q\ dx
, for all
q\in\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(I_{e})
;

•

the internal moments
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|E\right|}\int_{E}vq\ dx
for all
q\in\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(E)
.

Here,
\mathcal{M}_{m}(\omega)
, for
\omega=I_{e}
or
E
and
m
an integer, represents a set of
scaled
monomials which constitute a basis for
\mathbb{P}_{m}(\omega)
with the convention that
\mathcal{M}_{m}(\omega)=\emptyset
if
m<0
. The scaled monomials have the important property that
\left\|q\right\|_{0,\infty,E}\sim 1
for all
q\in\mathcal{M}_{m}(\omega)
and will be frequently used in our analysis; see e.g.
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2013)
;
Ahmad
et al. (2013)
;
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2014)
for more details.

The forms of Definition
1
constitute also an (incomplete) set of
DoF
for the space
W_{k}(E)
.
We let
\Pi^{*}_{k}:W_{k}(E)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{l}(E)
denote any
L^{2}
-stable
auxiliary
projection operator that is computable by only accessing such
DoF
.
The existence of auxiliary projection operators is a consequence of the fact that the above set of
DoF
is sufficient to determine the elements of
\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)
.

Remark 4
.

In
Ahmad
et al. (2013)
it is shown that the
H^{1}
-seminorm projection known in the VEM literature as
\Pi^{\nabla}
can serve as an auxiliary projection operator. This approach is extended in
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
to the curved polygonal elements discussed below, where
\Pi^{*}
is chosen as an approximation of
\Pi^{\nabla}
by using a sufficiently accurate quadrature scheme. However, this choice is not essential. For instance, a complete family of auxiliary projection operators is showcased in
Cangiani et al. (2016)
for the straight VEM case. As mentioned in the main text, in our implementation we make use of the least square fitting technique from
Dedner and
Hodson (2022)
.

We are now ready to introduce the enhanced local virtual element space as

\displaystyle V_{k}(E):=\Big\{v_{h}\in W_{k}(E)\ :\

\displaystyle\int_{E}(\Pi^{*}_{k}v_{h}-v_{h})q\ d\mathbf{x}=0\ \forall q\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)\backslash\mathbb{P}_{k-2}(E)\Big\}.

(3.1)

Following
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
;
Ahmad
et al. (2013)
it can be shown that the
DoF
of Definition
1
represent a set of unisolvent degrees of freedom for
V_{k}(E)
.
Moreover, such
DoF
allow for the evaluation of

\Pi^{0}_{k}v_{h}
and
\Pi^{1}_{k-1}v_{h}:=\Pi^{0}_{k-1}\nabla v_{h}

as well as of
\Pi^{*}_{k}v_{h}
, which is computable by construction for all
v_{h}\in V_{k}(E)
.

A global
C^{0}
-conforming virtual element space is then defined by gluing together the local spaces in (
3.1
); thus, we introduce the global (curved) VEM space

V_{h}^{k}:=\{v_{h}\in H^{1}(\Omega)\ :\ v_{h}|_{E}\in V_{k}(E)\ \ \ \forall E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

(3.2)

together with the space
V_{h,0}^{k}:=V_{h}^{k}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
of virtual functions with zero trace on
\partial\Omega
.

It is easy to show that if
E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is straight-edged, the polynomial space
\mathbb{P}_{k}(E)
is a subspace of
V_{k}(E)
. However, this is not the case if curved edges are present. Still, optimal approximation results can be shown in all cases. The following best approximation result
can be easily derived by combining
Theorem 3.7 from
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
, dealing with the original curved VEM space, and Theorem 11 in
Cangiani et al. (2016)
, which proves similar results for the enhanced VEM space with straight edges,
cf. also
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2020)
.

Theorem 3

(VEM interpolation error estimate)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
satisfying Assumption
1
and
k\geq 1
be given. For all
v\in H^{s}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
s\in\{2,...,k+1\}
,
there exists a
v_{I}\in V_{h,0}^{k}
such that,
for any element
E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, it holds that

\left\|v-v_{I}\right\|_{0,E}+h_{E}\left|v-v_{I}\right|_{1,E}\lesssim h_{E}^{s}\left|v\right|_{s,E},

where the hidden constant depends on the shape-regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and on
k
.

Remark 5
.

We stress once again that, whenever the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is made of polygonal elements, the virtual element space (
3.2
) reduces to the standard
C^{0}
-conforming space of
Ahmad
et al. (2013)
. Further, in the definition of the IsoVEM below, physical elements will be made at most of edges given as polynomial curves, thus simplifying implementation considerably, as we shall see. Nevertheless, here we have introduced the overly general curved VEM to exploit relevant estimates which are readily available from
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
.

3.2
Curved VEM

The original presentation of the curved VEM in
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
was limited to the Poisson problem with constant coefficients. For completeness, we report here its generalisation to the elliptic problem (
2.3
) which appears to be new. The results shown are easily obtained by combining
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
with the framework in
Cangiani et al. (2016)
.

On each
{E}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define the local virtual element forms by

\displaystyle A_{h}^{{E}}({u},{v})

\displaystyle:=\int_{{E}}{a}\ \Pi^{1}_{k-1}{u}_{h}\cdot\Pi^{1}_{k-1}{v}_{h}\ d\mathbf{x},\quad\qquad\qquad C_{h}^{{E}}({u},{v}):=\int_{{E}}{\mu}\ \Pi^{0}_{k}{u}_{h}\ \Pi^{0}_{k}{v}_{h}\ d\mathbf{x},

\displaystyle B_{h}^{{E}}({u},{v})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{E}}{\mathbf{b}}\cdot(\Pi^{0}_{k}{v}_{h}\ \Pi^{1}_{k-1}{u}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}{u}_{h}\ \Pi^{1}_{k-1}{v}_{h})\ d\mathbf{x},\quad l_{h}^{{E}}({v}):=\int_{{E}}{f}\ \Pi^{0}_{k}\ {v}_{h}\ d\mathbf{x},

for all
{u},{v}\in V_{k}({E})
.
Further, as customary in the definition of VEMs, we also require a VEM stabilisation form. That is, any bilinear form
S^{{E}}(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfying the following.

Assumption 2
.

For each
{E}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, a VEM stabilisation form
S^{{E}}:V_{k}({E})\times V_{k}({E})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is available which is computable from the degrees of freedom of Definition
1
and is such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{{E}}({v},{v})\lesssim S^{{E}}({v},{v})\lesssim\mathcal{A}^{{E}}({v},{v})\qquad\forall{v}\in V_{k}({E})\,\text{ such that }\,\Pi^{0}_{k}{v}=0.

Here,
\mathcal{A}^{{E}}
denotes the restriction to
{E}
of the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}
from (
2.3
).

We then define the discrete analogue of the global forms in (
2.3
) as follows. For
{u},{v}\in{V}^{k}_{h}
, we let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}({u},{v})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{{E}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big[A_{h}^{{E}}({u},{v})+B_{h}^{{E}}({u},{v})+C_{h}^{{E}}({u},{v})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+S^{{E}}({u}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}{u}_{h},{v}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}{v}_{h})\Big],

(3.3)

\displaystyle l_{h}({v})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{{E}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}l_{h}^{{E}}({v}).

(3.4)

The curved VEM now reads: find
{u}_{h}\in{V}^{k}_{h,0}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}({u}_{h},{v}_{h})=l_{h}({v}_{h})\qquad\forall{v}_{h}\in{V}^{k}_{h,0}.

(3.5)

The implementation of the above curved VEM requires the ability to compute integrals of polynomials over curved domains. We refer to
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
for more details.

Theorem 4

(Strang-type bound)
.

Under Assumption
2
the curved VEM (
3.5
) is well-posed. Moreover, letting
{u}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be the solution of (
2.3
) and
{u}_{h}\in{V}_{h,0}^{k}
the curved VEM solution of (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|{u}-{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{{v}_{h}\in{V}_{h,0}^{k}}\left\|{u}-{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega}+\inf_{{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\left\|{u}-{p}\right\|_{1,\Omega}

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38092pt+\sup_{{w}_{h}\in{V}_{h,0}^{k}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left|l_{h}({w}_{h})-l({w}_{h})\right|}{\left\|{w}_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega}}

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38092pt+\inf_{{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\sup_{{w}_{h}\in{V}_{h,0}^{k}\backslash\{0\}}\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{{E}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left|\mathcal{A}^{{E}}_{h}({p},{w}_{h})-\mathcal{A}^{{E}}({p},{w}_{h})\right|}{\left\|{w}_{h}\right\|_{1,{\Omega}}}.

(3.6)

The hidden constant only depends on the continuity and coercivity of the bilinear form (
3.3
).

The following error bound for the curved VEM is easily proven following the steps detailed in
Cangiani et al. (2016)
to bound each term in the Strang-type bound (
3.6
) exploiting also
Theorem
2
and Theorem
3
.

Theorem 5

(Curved VEM error estimate)
.

Let Assumptions
1
,
2
hold true. Suppose also that, for some
s\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, the solution
{u}
of (
2.3
) satisfies
{u}\in H^{s+1}(\Omega)
with data
{a},{\mathbf{b}},{\mu}\in W^{s+1}_{\infty}(\hat{\Omega})
and
f\in H^{s-1}(\Omega)
. Then, for
k\leq m
, the curved VEM solution of (
3.5
) satisfies

\left\|{u}-{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega}+\left\|\nabla{u}-\Pi^{1}_{k-1}{u}_{h}\right\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim h^{s}\left(\left\|{u}\right\|_{s+1,\Omega}+\left\|{f}\right\|_{s-1,\Omega}\right).

(3.7)

The hidden constant depends on the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, on
k
, on
a_{0}
and
\mu_{0}
, and the
W^{s+1}_{\infty}(\Omega)
-norms of
{a}
,
{\mathbf{b}}
, and
{\mu}
.

4
Virtual element mapping

The construction of the IsoVEMs depends on a (equal order) virtual element approximation of the reference-to-physical transformation
\mathbf{F}
rather than on the transformation itself, the knowledge of which will not be required in practice.
The idea of using the VEM to construct conforming approximations of the computational domain was already used in
Lipnikov and
Morgan (2019)
;
Lipnikov and
Morgan (2020)
.

Firstly, we require a virtual element space on the polygonal reference domain
\hat{\Omega}
. Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a polygonal mesh of
\hat{\Omega}
, containing polygonal elements
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
satisfying Assumption
1
, and
\hat{V}_{h}^{l}
the corresponding enhanced virtual element space of degree
l\geq 1
. By design,
\hat{V}_{h}^{l}
is the standard
enhanced
virtual element space on straight-edged meshes
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2013)
;
Ahmad
et al. (2013)
;
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2014)
, cf. Remark
5
above.

A
virtual computational domain
approximating the physical domain
\Omega
may be defined as the image of the reference domain
\hat{\Omega}
through a vectorial virtual element approximation of
\mathbf{F}
with components in the virtual element space
\hat{V}_{h}^{l}
. That is, we let
\mathbf{F}_{h}\in[\hat{V}_{h}^{l}]^{2}
and for each
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
we define the corresponding physical element
E_{h}=\mathbf{F}_{h}(\hat{E})
. We denote the virtual domain and mesh so obtained by

\Omega_{h}:=\mathbf{F}_{h}(\hat{\Omega}),\quad\mathcal{T}_{h}:=\{E_{h}:=\mathbf{F}_{h}(\hat{E})\}.

(4.1)

The analysis below requires that
\Omega_{h}
is a sufficiently accurate approximation of the physical domain
\Omega
.

Assumption 3

(Virtual element mapping accuracy)
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a polygonal mesh of
\hat{\Omega}
and let
m,l\in\mathbb{N}
with
l\leq m
. The reference-to-physical transformation
\mathbf{F}\in\left[W^{\infty}_{m+1}(\hat{\Omega})\right]^{2}
has a VEM approximation
\mathbf{F}_{h}\in\left[\hat{V}_{h}^{l}\right]^{2}
of degree
l\geq 1
such that for all
s\in\{0,1,...,l\}

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{F}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{\Omega}}+h\left|\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{F}_{h}\right|_{1,\hat{\Omega}}\lesssim h^{s+1}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{s+1,\hat{\Omega}},

where the hidden constant depends only on the mesh regularity of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and on
l
.

For instance, if
\mathbf{F}_{h}
is a VEM interpolant of
\mathbf{F}
, then the approximation properties required by Assumption
3
are guaranteed by Theorem
3
. This case is depicted in Figure
1
, showing a physical element
E=\mathbf{F}(\hat{E})
and its VEM interpolant
E_{h}=\mathbf{F}_{h}(\hat{E})
of degree
l=1,2,3
. However, we prefer to keep the above as an assumption to cater for other cases, such as
\mathbf{F}_{h}
being a sufficiently accurate VEM solution to a separate problem.

As a consequence of the bi-Lipschitz continuity condition on
\mathbf{F}
, it can be easily shown that a reference element
\hat{E}
is shape regular if and only if its corresponding physical element
E=\mathbf{F}(\hat{E})
is shape regular under the conditions of Assumption
1
. The ratio between the regularity parameters is bounded above and below dependent on the bi-Lipschitz constants. It can be shown using Assumption
3
and a continuity argument that
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is a virtual domain of shape regular elements; see
Weißer (2019)
;
Antonietti
et al. (2019)
for a framework to estimate perturbed regularity parameters on transformed elements.

Figure 1
:
From left to right, a physical element
E
and its virtual interpolating polygons
E_{h}
obtained with virtual map of order
l=1
,
l=2
, and
l=3
.

5
Formulation of the isoparametric VEMs

Both methods presented below are based on the virtual element map of Section
4
.
Letting
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a mesh of
\hat{\Omega}

satisfying the shape-regularity Assumption
1
,
we consider the corresponding reference virtual element space
\hat{V}_{h}^{l}
for some discrete map order
l\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathbf{F}_{h}\in[\hat{V}_{h}^{l}]^{2}
be an approximation of
\mathbf{F}
satisfying Assumption
3
.
We generally construct, over the virtual computational domain
\mathbf{F}_{h}
, VEMs of order
k\geq 1
with the understanding that an isoparametric method is obtained by setting
l=k
.

5.1
Reference IsoVEM formulation

The reference-frame isoparametric approach is to consider a discretization of the problem transformed back onto the reference domain.

We operate under the assumption that the transformation of the problem’s data from the physical to the reference frame is readily available or can be estimated with sufficient accuracy. Namely, we assume we are given
\hat{a}
,
\hat{\mathbf{b}}
,
\hat{c}
, and
\hat{f}
defined over
\hat{\Omega}
and satisfying the same properties imposed on the original data.

The problem written in the reference frame reads: find
\hat{u}\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})
such that

\hat{\mathcal{A}}(\hat{u},\hat{v}):=\hat{A}(\hat{u},\hat{v})+\hat{B}(\hat{u},\hat{v})+\hat{C}(\hat{u},\hat{v})=\hat{l}(\hat{v})\ \ \ \forall\hat{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega}),

(5.1)

where, for all
\hat{u},\hat{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\hat{\Omega})
,

\displaystyle\hat{A}(\hat{u},\hat{v})

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\hat{a}\ \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}^{-T}\hat{\nabla}\hat{u}\cdot\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}^{-T}\hat{\nabla}\hat{v}\ j\ d\boldsymbol{\xi},\qquad\qquad\hat{C}(\hat{u},\hat{v})=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\hat{\mu}\ \hat{u}\ \hat{v}\ j\ d\boldsymbol{\xi},

\displaystyle\hat{B}(\hat{u},\hat{v})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\hat{\mathbf{b}}\cdot(\hat{v}\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}^{-T}\hat{\nabla}\hat{u}-\hat{u}\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}^{-T}\hat{\nabla}\hat{v})\ j\ d\boldsymbol{\xi},\quad\hat{l}(\hat{v})=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\hat{f}\ \hat{v}\ j\ d\boldsymbol{\xi}.

We exploit the virtual element map
\mathbf{F}_{h}
to approximate the components of the transformed weak formulation, such as the Jacobian and its determinant.
In particular, we produce a computable approximation of the Jacobian
\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}
via
L^{2}
-projection:

\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}:=\Pi^{1}_{l-1}\mathbf{F}_{h}.

(5.2)

From this, an approximate determinant,

j_{h}:=\text{det}\left(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}\right),

is also available as a polynomial of degree
2(l-1)
.

Remark 6
.

The idea of using virtual element maps to transfer the solution and the Jacobian across meshes was first introduced in
Lipnikov and
Morgan (2019)
where the Jacobian was approximated, instead of with the
L^{2}
-projector, with the
H^{1}
-semi norm or gradient projection
\Pi^{\nabla}
, cf. Remark
4
. Here, we take the idea one step further by using virtual elements to solve the problem at hand, rather than simply as a solution transfer tool.

We are now ready to introduce the reference IsoVEM of order
k\geq 1
.
The solution will be sought in the reference virtual element space
\hat{V}_{h,0}^{k}
. On each
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
we define the local forms by

\displaystyle A_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u},\hat{v})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\hat{E}}\hat{a}\ \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-T}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{u}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-T}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h}\ j_{h}\ d\boldsymbol{\xi},

\displaystyle B_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u},\hat{v})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\hat{E}}\hat{\mathbf{b}}\cdot(\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h}\ \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-T}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{u}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{u}_{h}\ \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-T}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h})\ j_{h}\ d\boldsymbol{\xi},

\displaystyle C_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u},\hat{v})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\hat{E}}\hat{\mu}\ \Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{u}_{h}\ \Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h}\ j_{h}\ d\boldsymbol{\xi},

\displaystyle l_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{v})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\hat{E}}\hat{f}\ \Pi^{0}_{k}\ \hat{v}_{h}\ j_{h}\ d\boldsymbol{\xi},

for all
\hat{u},\hat{v}\in V_{k}(\hat{E})
.
Our analysis is valid for any stabilisation choice satisfying Assumption
2
.
In the numerical experiments of Section
8
, we use the appropriately weighted dofi-dofi stabilisation
Cangiani et al. (2016)
:

S^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u},\hat{v})=(\bar{a}+h_{\hat{E}}^{2}\bar{\mu})\sum_{i}\text{dof}_{i}(\hat{u}_{h})\cdot\text{dof}_{i}(\hat{v}_{h}),

(5.3)

where
\bar{\cdot}
denotes the nodal average over
\hat{E}
and
\{\text{dof}_{i}\}_{i}
is the set of
DoF
operators associated with the
DoF
of Definition
1
. A proof that this choice satisfies the above assumption can be found in
Cangiani et al. (2016)
.

We then define the discrete analogue of the global forms in (
5.1
) for
\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h}\in\hat{V}^{k}_{h}
by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\Big[A_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})+B_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})+C_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+S^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h})\Big],

(5.4)

\displaystyle\hat{l}_{h}(\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}l_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{v}_{h}),

(5.5)

yielding the reference IsoVEM as: find
\hat{u}_{h}\in\hat{V}^{k}_{h,0}
such that

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})=\hat{l}_{h}(\hat{v}_{h})\qquad\forall\hat{v}_{h}\in\hat{V}^{k}_{h,0}.

(5.6)

5.2
Physical IsoVEM formulation

The physical IsoVEM is constructed over the physical approximate domain
\Omega_{h}
, cf. (
4.1
). Given that the mapped elements
E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
are curved in general, the curved virtual element space
V_{h}^{k}
relative to
\mathcal{T}_{h}
given by (
3.2
) can be used as the solution space. With this, a VEM may be directly constructed as in
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
.
However, our approach is not a direct application of the curved VEM. Instead, we
exploit the virtual nature of the curved polygonal elements
by accessing their geometrical features only through their
L^{2}
-projection.
As such, our approach only requires the availability of the degrees of freedom of
\mathbf{F}_{h}

and hence it
does not
rely on full knowledge of the mapping
\mathbf{F}
(and, in fact,
\mathbf{F}_{h}
). Moreover, by using the projection of the element domains, we have a computable geometry on which we can more easily integrate polynomials exactly.
It was already argued in
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2019)
that a sufficient amount of quadrature points must be used to compute integrals over curved edges and curvilinear polygons. These quadratures are required, for instance, to compute
approximately
the projection operators which are core to the VEM.
In our approach, we introduce two altogether new computable operators that serve as approximate projectors. The benefit to this is that we can compute these new operators using only the projected elements and the DoFs with minimal quadrature rules required.

In keeping with the VEM philosophy, we decompose
\mathbf{F}_{h}
into a polynomial and non-polynomial component, namely

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{h}=\Pi^{0}_{l}\mathbf{F}_{h}+(\mathbf{F}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{l}\mathbf{F}_{h}).

The physical virtual element space
V_{h}^{k}
is constructed over the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}=\{E_{h}:=\mathbf{F}_{h}(\hat{E})\}_{h}
, cf. (
4.1
), using (
3.2
).
The projected element
E_{h}^{\pi}:=\Pi^{0}_{l}\mathbf{F}_{h}(\hat{E})
is used to construct the necessary projection operators as follows.

We start with the approximate local
L^{2}
-projection. In the forthcoming definitions, for
v_{h}\in V_{k}(E_{h})
, we let
\{\text{dof}_{\alpha}(v_{h})\}_{\alpha}
denote the set of internal moment
DoF
of Definition
1
. Without loss of generality, these are assumed to be ordered so that
\text{dof}_{\alpha}(v_{h})
corresponds to the internal moment with respect to the
\alpha^{{\rm th}}
scaled monomial in
\mathcal{M}_{k}(E_{h})
, cf. Definition
1
. In the physical IsoVEM, we always consider the scaled monomials to be extended to
\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
by evaluating the polynomial expansion of a given
m_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(E_{h})
at any given point in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Definition 2
.

The operator
\Pi^{0}_{h,k}:V_{h}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
satisfies, on each
E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

\displaystyle\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\ m_{\alpha}\ d\mathbf{x}=\begin{cases}\left|E_{h}^{\pi}\right|\text{dof}_{\alpha}(v_{h})\qquad&\forall m_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(E_{h}),\\
\displaystyle\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\Pi^{*}_{k}v_{h}\ m_{\alpha}\ d\mathbf{x}\qquad&\forall m_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(E_{h})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(E_{h}),\end{cases}

for all
v_{h}\in V_{k}(E_{h})
.

Noting that, by definition
\int_{E_{h}}\Pi^{*}_{k}v_{h}q\ d\mathbf{x}=\int_{E_{h}}v_{h}q\ d\mathbf{x}
, cf. (
3.1
), we observe that

\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\ m_{\alpha}\ d\mathbf{x}=\frac{\left|E_{h}^{\pi}\right|}{\left|E_{h}\right|}\int_{E_{h}}v_{h}\ m_{\alpha}\ d\mathbf{x}\qquad\forall m_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(E_{h}).

(5.7)

Next we construct an approximation of the gradient projection as follows.

Definition 3
.

The operator
\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}:V_{h}^{k}\rightarrow[\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})]^{2}
satisfies, on each
E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

\displaystyle\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{m}_{\alpha}\ d\mathbf{x}=

\displaystyle-\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\Pi^{0}_{h,k}v_{h}\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf{m}_{\alpha}\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial E_{h}}v_{h}\mathbf{m}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{n}\ dS\qquad\forall\mathbf{m}_{\alpha}\in[\mathcal{M}_{k-1}(E_{h})]^{2},

for all
v_{h}\in V_{k}(E_{h})
.

Remark 7
.

In Definition
3
, we exploit the full knowledge of the boundary of
E_{h}
to compute edge integrals over
\partial E_{h}
instead of
\partial E_{h}^{\pi}
. The alternative approach of computing
\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}
using only
\partial E_{h}^{\pi}
is also viable, at the expense of a more involved analysis, cf.
Wells (2023)
. Note that the latter approach would be the only option available in higher-dimensions as the elemental interfaces are virtual in this case.

Remark 8
.

We shall refer to these new operators as projections but note that they are
not
exact projections as they do not reproduce polynomials on
E_{h}
. The error estimates of Theorems
15
and
16
in Section
7
show that this inconsistency introduces errors which are appropriately controlled.

We are now ready to introduce the physical IsoVEM of order
k\in\mathbb{N}
over the physical curved polygonal mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
On each
E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
we define the local forms

\displaystyle A_{h}^{E_{h}}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\tilde{a}\ \Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}u_{h}\cdot\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}v_{h}\ d\mathbf{x},

\displaystyle\ B_{h}^{E_{h}}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\tilde{\mathbf{b}}\cdot(\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}u_{h}-\Pi_{h,k}^{0}u_{h}\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}v_{h})\ d\mathbf{x},

\displaystyle C_{h}^{E_{h}}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\tilde{\mu}\ \Pi_{h,k}^{0}u_{h}\ \Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\ d\mathbf{x},

\displaystyle l_{h}^{E_{h}}(v_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\tilde{f}\ \Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}.

Due to the fact that
E_{h}^{\pi}
may fall outside of
\Omega
, the local forms rely on extensions of the PDE data of the problem to be available.

We thus introduce the following forms by summing the local contributions over
\mathcal{T}_{h}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big[A_{h}^{E_{h}}(u_{h},v_{h})+B_{h}^{E_{h}}(u_{h},v_{h})+C_{h}^{E_{h}}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+S^{E_{h}}(u_{h}-\Pi_{h,k}^{0}u_{h},v_{h}-\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h})\Big],

(5.8)

\displaystyle l_{h}(v_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}l_{h}^{E_{h}}(v_{h}).

(5.9)

Here, as before,
S^{E_{h}}
represents any VEM stabilisation form satisfying Assumption
2
with
\Pi_{h,k}^{0}
in place of
\Pi_{k}^{0}
. In Section
8
we again choose a dofi-dofi form (cf. Equation (
5.3
))

S^{E_{h}}(u,v)=(\bar{a}+h_{E_{h}}^{2}\bar{\mu})\sum_{i}\text{dof}_{i}(u)\cdot\text{dof}_{i}(v).

(5.10)

The physical IsoVEM is then defined, recalling the definition of
V_{h}^{k}
and
V_{h,0}^{k}
in Equation (
3.2
), as follows: find
u_{h}\in V_{h,0}^{k}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=l_{h}(v_{h})\qquad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h,0}^{k}.

(5.11)

Remark 9
.

An alternative formulation is possible defining the method directly over the physical isoparametric elements
E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. However, while exact integration of polynomials is directly available over the projected element through accurate enough quadrature rules defined over the reference elements, integration over the true physical elements would require an isoparametric subtriangulation of each
E_{h}
.
Moreover, the physical element based formulation would not be computable in three space dimensions, as the faces of three-dimensional elements are virtual.
For these reasons we prefer to construct the physical IsoVEM formulation over the projected elements and show in Section
7
that the extra source of error so introduced does not reduce the order of accuracy of the method

6
Reference IsoVEM analysis

For simplicity, we constrain the regularity of the domain transformation prior to presenting our analysis. In what follows,
m\in\mathbb{N}
is a fixed integer such that
\mathbf{F}\in\left[H^{m+1}(\hat{\Omega})\right]^{2}
. In all subsequent results, applicable to both the reference and physical IsoVEM, we exclusively consider VEM discretizations with degrees
k,l\leq m
.
We should note that here,
k
denotes the degree of the solution space, while
l
pertains to the mapping space. While an isoparametric method requires that
k=l
, our analysis encompasses the general case, illustrating the impact of mixing discretization degrees.

We record here some straightforward bounds which will be used repeatedly in the forthcoming analysis. These can be proved in any space dimension. However, in keeping with the rest of the paper, we present them here explicitly for the two-dimensional case only.

For a matrix
\mathbf{A}
with entries
A_{i,j}\in W^{s}_{\infty}(\omega)
,
i,j=1,2
, and a vector
\mathbf{v}\in\left[H^{s}(\omega)\right]^{2}
for some
s\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,\omega}\lesssim\left\|\mathbf{A}\right\|_{s,\infty,\omega}\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,\omega},

(6.1)

with the hidden constant only depending on
s
. Further, if
\text{det}\left(\mathbf{A}\right)>a_{0}>0
a.e. in
\omega
then it holds

\left\|\text{det}\left(\mathbf{A}\right)\right\|_{0,\infty,\omega}\leq 2\left\|\mathbf{A}\right\|_{0,\infty,\omega}^{2},\quad\left\|\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right\|_{0,\infty,\omega}\leq\frac{1}{a_{0}}\left\|\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right\|_{0,\infty,\omega}\leq\frac{1}{a_{0}}\left\|\mathbf{A}\right\|_{0,\infty,\omega}.

(6.2)

Here, we expressed the inverse of a matrix
\mathbf{A}
in the form

\mathbf{A}^{-1}=\frac{1}{\text{det}\left(\mathbf{A}\right)}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{A}}^{T}
,
where
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{A}}
is the matrix of cofactors of
\mathbf{A}
.

In what follows, the matrix of cofactors of
\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}
and
\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}
are denoted by
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F}}
and
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}
, respectively.

6.1
Jacobian error & stability estimates

We introduce and analyse the error terms related to the approximation of the Jacobian and its cofactors, the determinant, and the inverse Jacobian of the reference to physical map. Namely,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{J}}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}-\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}},\ \mathcal{E}_{\text{cof}}:=\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}-\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F}},\ \mathcal{E}_{\text{det}}:=j_{h}-j,\ \mathcal{E}_{\text{inv}}:=\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-1}-\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}^{-1}.

(6.3)

Lemma 6

(Jacobian estimates)
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
1
and
\mathbf{F}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
3
. For the projected Jacobian (
5.2
) and related errors in (
6.3
) there hold

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s}_{\hat{E}}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}},

(6.4)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{1,\infty,\hat{E}},

(6.5)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\text{cof}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s}_{\hat{E}}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}},

(6.6)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{1,\infty,\hat{E}},

(6.7)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}^{2},

(6.8)

\displaystyle\left\|j_{h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\mathbf{F}\right|^{2}_{1,\infty,\hat{E}},

(6.9)

for all
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and
s\in\{0,\dots,l\}
. The hidden constants depend only on the shape regularity of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and on
l
.

Proof.

By definition of the approximate Jacobian (
5.2
) and discrete mapping
\mathbf{F}_{h}
and using the triangle inequality it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\leq\left\|\Pi^{1}_{l-1}\mathbf{F}_{h}-\Pi^{1}_{l-1}\mathbf{F}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}+\left\|\Pi^{1}_{l-1}\mathbf{F}-\hat{\nabla}\mathbf{F}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}.

(6.10)

We apply the inverse estimate found in
Di Pietro and Droniou (2020)
(Lemma 1.25) to get

\left\|\Pi^{1}_{l-1}\mathbf{F}_{h}-\Pi^{1}_{l-1}\mathbf{F}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{-1}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{l-1}\mathbf{F}_{h}-\Pi^{1}_{l-1}\mathbf{F}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s-1}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{s+1,\hat{E}}

thanks to the stability of
\Pi^{1}
and Assumption
3
.
Inserting this bound into Equation (
6.10
) and directly applying Theorem
2
provides

\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}+h_{\hat{E}}^{s-1}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{s+1,\hat{E}}\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}},

proving (
6.4
), from which the stability bound (
6.5
) follows by taking
s=0
and applying the triangle inequality.

The cofactor stability bound (
6.7
) is a direct consequence of (
6.2
) and of (
6.5
) while the error estimate (
6.6
) is trivial, given the definition of minors of
\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}
.

To show stability of the determinant of the Jacobian (
6.9
), we denote the terms of the discrete Jacobian matrix by
J_{h,i,j}=(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h})_{i,j}
,
i,j=1,2
. Then, noting that
j_{h}
is a linear combination of products of terms from
\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}
,
applying the triangle inequality, and bounding
L^{\infty}
norms leads to

\displaystyle\left\|j_{h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle=\left\|J_{h,0,0}J_{h,1,1}-J_{h,1,0}J_{h,0,1}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\leq 2\left\|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}^{2},

and thus (
6.9
) follows applying (
6.5
). The error estimate (
6.8
) is proven with a similar argument as follows. By considering the entries of
\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}
, we have the error term of

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}}=J_{h,0,0}J_{h,1,1}-J_{0,0}J_{1,1}+J_{1,0}J_{0,1}-J_{h,1,0}J_{h,0,1}.

Considering the first difference term, we can rewrite this as

\displaystyle J_{h,0,0}J_{h,1,1}-J_{0,0}J_{1,1}=J_{h,0,0}(J_{h,1,1}-J_{1,1})+J_{1,1}(J_{h,0,0}-J_{0,0}).

Bounding this term using the triangle inequality and (
6.4
) gives

\displaystyle\left\|J_{h,0,0}J_{h,1,1}-J_{0,0}J_{1,1}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}+\left\|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{1,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{1,\infty,\hat{E}}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}.

By repeating this argument for the remaining terms in
\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}}
and applying the triangle inequality we get

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{1,\infty,\hat{E}}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}.

Bounding the semi-norms by the
W^{\infty}_{s+1}
norm completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 7

(Inverse Jacobian estimates)
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
1
and
\mathbf{F}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
3
with
h
sufficiently small. For the inverse of the projected Jacobian (
5.2
) and related errors in (
6.3
), there hold

\displaystyle j_{h}|_{\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim j_{0},

(6.11)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-1}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{1,\infty,\hat{E}},

(6.12)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\text{inv}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s}_{\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}^{3},

(6.13)

for all
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and
s\in\{0,\dots,l\}
. The hidden constants only depend on
j_{0}
, the shape regularity of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, and on
l
.

Proof.

From the estimate (
6.8
), there exists a
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|(j-j_{h})(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|\leq Ch_{\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{2,\infty,\hat{E}}^{2},

observing that
j
is continuous. Then, we define

\displaystyle h^{*}=\frac{j_{0}}{2C\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{2,\infty,\hat{E}}^{2}},

such that for
h_{E}\leq h^{*}
we have
\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\leq j_{0}/2
. Equation (
6.11
) is then deduced by using a continuity argument.

Equation (
6.12
) is proven by using (
6.11
) and (
6.2
)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-1}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{j_{h}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{\top}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\leq\frac{1}{j_{0}}\left\|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}.

The bound is then concluded by applying Lemma
6
.

Using the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{J}}
and substitution of
\mathbf{I}=\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}^{-1}
results in

\mathcal{E}_{\text{inv}}=-\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-1}\ \mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\ \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}^{-1}
.
Expanding and bounding norms gives

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\text{inv}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\leq\left\|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-1}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F}}^{-1}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}.

Using (
6.2
), Lemma
6
, and (
6.12
) provides

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\text{inv}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{1,\infty,\hat{E}}h^{s}_{\hat{E}}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}\frac{1}{j_{0}}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{1,\infty,\hat{E}}\lesssim\frac{1}{j_{0}}h^{s}_{\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}^{3}.

∎

6.2
Well-posedness

The above estimates on the Jacobian error allow us to establish the well-posedness of the reference IsoVEM formulation (
5.6
) resorting on the Lax-Milgram lemma.

Theorem 8

(Reference IsoVEM well-posedness)
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
1
,
\mathbf{F}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
3
, and
h
be small enough.
Then, the reference IsoVEM formulation (
5.6
) with stabilisation (
5.3
) obeying Assumption
2
possesses a unique solution.

Proof.

We start by establishing the continuity of the bilinear form
\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
given in (
5.4
).
Let
\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h}\in\hat{V}_{h,0}^{k}
.
Through applying the Hölder and Cauchy-Schwarz
inequalities and the operator norm inequality (
6.1
) we have, on any
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,

\displaystyle A_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\hat{a}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-T}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\Pi_{k-1}^{1}\hat{u}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}},

\displaystyle B_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\Big(\left\|\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{u}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}\left\|\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\left\|\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{u}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}\Big),

\displaystyle C_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\hat{\mu}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|j_{h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{u}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}\left\|\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}.

Applying the stability of the projection operators and bounding these norms by the
H^{1}
norm leads to

\displaystyle A_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\hat{a}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-T}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}},

\displaystyle B_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}},

\displaystyle C_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\hat{\mu}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|j_{h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}.

Next we apply Lemmas
6
and
7
to get, respectively,

\displaystyle A_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\hat{a}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{1,\infty,\hat{E}}^{2}\left\|\hat{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}},

\displaystyle B_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{1,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}},

\displaystyle C_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\hat{\mu}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left|\mathbf{F}\right|^{2}_{1,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}.

For the stabilization term, we employ the stability Assumption
2
and the continuity of
\hat{\mathcal{A}}(\cdot,\cdot)
to get

\displaystyle S^{\hat{E}}(\hat{u}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h})\lesssim\left\|\hat{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}.

Summing these bounds over
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
results in

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(\hat{u}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})\lesssim\left(\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{1,\infty,\hat{\Omega}}+\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{1,\infty,\hat{\Omega}}^{2}+1\right)\left\|\hat{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{\Omega}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{\Omega}}\lesssim\left\|\hat{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{\Omega}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{\Omega}},

(6.14)

thus establishing the continuity of
\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
. The (hidden) continuity constant depends on shape regularity of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, on
l
, on appropriate norms of the data and the
W^{1}_{\infty}
semi-norm of
\mathbf{F}
, and on the stability constant in Assumption
2
.
The continuity of the linear form
\hat{l}_{h}(\cdot)
given in (
5.5
) follows easily by the same argument used for the reaction term
C_{h}^{\hat{E}}
.

It remains to show the coercivity of the bilinear form
\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
. Considering the
L^{2}
norm of the
\Pi^{1}_{k-1}
projection on an element
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and assuming a sufficiently small
h
such that
j_{0}\leq j_{h}
(see Lemma
6
) gives, for all
\hat{v}_{h}\in\hat{V}_{h,0}^{k}
,

\displaystyle a_{0}j_{0}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\hat{E}}\hat{a}\left|\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h}\right|^{2}j_{h}\ d\boldsymbol{\xi}\lesssim\int_{\hat{E}}\hat{a}\left|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{T}\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-T}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h}\right|^{2}j_{h}\ d\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{T}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}^{2}\int_{\hat{E}}\hat{a}\left|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-T}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h}\right|^{2}j_{h}\ d\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{T}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}^{2}A_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{v}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})\lesssim A_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{v}_{h},\hat{v}_{h}),

by definition of
A_{h}^{\hat{E}}
and Lemma
6
.
Arguing similarly for the reaction term
C_{h}^{\hat{E}}
and noting that
B_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{v}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})=0
we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{v}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\mu_{0}j_{0}\left\|\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}^{2}+a_{0}j_{0}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}^{2}+S^{\hat{E}}(\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h},\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h}).

(6.15)

Following standard VEM analysis
Beirão Da Veiga
et al. (2016)
;
Cangiani et al. (2016)
, we apply Assumption
2
to get

\displaystyle S^{\hat{E}}(\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h},\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h})\gtrsim a_{0}\left\|\hat{\nabla}\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}^{2}+\mu_{0}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k}\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}^{2},

from which substitution into Equation (
6.15
) obtains

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{v}_{h},\hat{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\gtrsim j_{0}\left(\mu_{0}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}^{2}+a_{0}\left\|\hat{\nabla}\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}^{2}\right)\gtrsim\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}^{2}.

The coercivity of
\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
now follows summing over elements and bounding norms yielding, for all
\hat{v}_{h}\in\hat{V}_{h,0}^{k}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{\Omega}}^{2}\lesssim\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(\hat{v}_{h},\hat{v}_{h}).

(6.16)

The hidden constant depends on the shape regularity of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, on
l
,
j_{0}
,
a_{0}
, and
\mu_{0}
, the stability constant of Assumption
2
and the
W^{1}_{\infty}
-seminorm of
\mathbf{F}
.
The well-posedness of the reference IsoVEM formulation is thus given by the Lax-Milgram lemma.
∎

6.3
Reference IsoVEM error estimate

We now prove an
H^{1}
-norm error estimate for the reference IsoVEM. It is straightforward to check that the Strang-type bound of Theorem
4
applies to the IsoVEM formulation (
5.6
).

The difficulty lays in analysing the inconsistency terms in (
3.6
).
These include new inconsistencies produced by the virtual map and hence we cannot simply resort on the analysis in
Cangiani et al. (2016)
;
Beirão Da Veiga
et al. (2016)
. Instead, the analysis relies on the bounds of Section
6.1
as follows.

Lemma 9

(Reference IsoVEM consistency error)
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
1
,
\mathbf{F}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
3
, and
h
be small enough.
Assume that
\hat{a},\hat{\mu},\hat{\mathbf{b}}\in W^{s+1}_{\infty}(\hat{\Omega})
and
\hat{f}\in H^{s-1}(\hat{\Omega})
, for some
s\in\{1,\dots,\min\{k,l\}\}
. For all
\hat{v}_{h}\in\hat{V}^{k}_{h}
and
\hat{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h})
, there hold

\displaystyle\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left|\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{\hat{E}}_{h}(\hat{p},\hat{v}_{h})-\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{p},\hat{v}_{h})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s}\left\|\hat{p}\right\|_{s+1,\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{\Omega}},

(6.17)

\displaystyle\left|\hat{l}_{h}(\hat{v}_{h})-\hat{l}(\hat{v}_{h})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s}\left\|\hat{f}\right\|_{s-1,\hat{\Omega}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{\Omega}}.

(6.18)

The hidden constants depend on the shape regularity of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, on
s
and
j_{0}
, and on the
W^{s+1}_{\infty}
-norm of
\mathbf{F}
. The first constant depends also on the
W^{s+1}_{\infty}(\hat{\Omega})
-norm of
\hat{a}
,
\hat{\mathbf{b}}
, and
\hat{\mu}
.

Proof.

Let
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
.
For brevity, we only detail the estimation of the diffusion term
A_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{p},\hat{v}_{h})-A^{\hat{E}}(\hat{p},\hat{v}_{h})
; all other terms can be treated similarly as in
Cangiani et al. (2016)
. Adding and subtracting terms gives

\displaystyle A^{\hat{E}}_{h}(\hat{p},\hat{v}_{h})-A^{\hat{E}}(\hat{p},\hat{v}_{h})=

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{E}}\hat{a}\mathcal{E}_{\text{cof}}\hat{\nabla}\hat{p}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{-T}_{\mathbf{F}}\hat{\nabla}\hat{v}_{h}\ d\boldsymbol{\xi}+\int_{\hat{E}}\hat{a}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}\hat{\nabla}\hat{p}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{\text{inv}}^{T}\hat{\nabla}\hat{v}_{h}\ d\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle+\int_{\hat{E}}\hat{a}\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-T}\hat{\nabla}\hat{p}\cdot\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}(\hat{\nabla}-\Pi^{1}_{k-1})\hat{v}_{h}\ d\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\;T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}.

Combining Hölder and Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities with (
6.2
) we have

\displaystyle|T_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\hat{a}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\text{cof}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{J}^{-T}_{\mathbf{F}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left|\hat{p}\right|_{1,\hat{E}}\left|\hat{v}_{h}\right|_{1,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\hat{a}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\text{cof}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\frac{1}{j_{0}}\left\|\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left|\hat{p}\right|_{1,\hat{E}}\left|\hat{v}_{h}\right|_{1,\hat{E}},

from which, applying Lemma
6
, yields

\displaystyle|T_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\hat{a}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\frac{1}{j_{0}}h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}^{2}\left|\hat{p}\right|_{1,\hat{E}}\left|\hat{v}_{h}\right|_{1,\hat{E}}.

A similar argument using, instead, Lemma
7
provides

\displaystyle|T_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\left\|\hat{a}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\frac{1}{j_{0}}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}^{4}\right\}h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left|\hat{p}\right|_{1,\hat{E}}\left|\hat{v}_{h}\right|_{1,\hat{E}}

Combining these bounds and hiding constants leads to

\displaystyle|T_{1}|+|T_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|\hat{p}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}.

To bound
T_{3}
, we emulate the steps taken in
Cangiani et al. (2016)
, writing

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{E}}\hat{a}\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-T}\hat{\nabla}\hat{p}\cdot\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}(\hat{\nabla}\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h})\ d\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{E}}\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot(\hat{\nabla}\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h})\ d\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{E}}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}-\Pi^{0}_{k-1}\boldsymbol{\gamma})\cdot(\hat{\nabla}\hat{v}_{h}-\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{v}_{h})\ d\boldsymbol{\xi}.

noting that
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}
has polynomial entries of degree
(l-1)
, introducing
\boldsymbol{\gamma}:=(\hat{a}\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-T}\hat{\nabla}\hat{p})^{T}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}
.
Then, applying Cauchy-Schwarz and Theorem
2
, gives

\displaystyle|T_{3}|\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}-\Pi^{0}_{k-1}\boldsymbol{\gamma}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}\left\|(\hat{\nabla}-\Pi^{1}_{k-1})\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{0,\hat{E}}\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}\right\|_{s,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}.

(6.19)

Note that
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
can be written as

\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\hat{a}\frac{1}{j_{h}}(\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}\hat{\nabla}\hat{p})^{\top}C_{\mathbf{F},h}
,
and thus a bound of norms of
{1}/{j_{h}}
is required. To this end, we use the chain rule and quotient rule yielding, for any
|\alpha|
multi-index with
|\alpha|\leq s
and noting that
j_{0}\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\left|D^{\alpha}\frac{1}{j_{h}}\right|=\left|(-1)^{\left|\alpha\right|}\frac{\alpha!}{j_{h}^{\left|\alpha\right|+1}}D^{\alpha}j_{h}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{s!}{\left|j_{h}\right|^{\left|\alpha\right|+1}}\left|D^{\alpha}j_{h}\right|\lesssim s!\left(\frac{1}{j_{0}}\right)^{s+1}\left\|j_{h}\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}},

and we conclude that

\left\|{1}/{j_{h}}\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}\lesssim\left\|j_{h}\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}

holds with the hidden constant depending on
s
and
j_{0}
.
Hence, by repeated applications of (
6.1
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}\right\|_{s,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\hat{a}\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|j_{h}\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}^{2}\left\|\hat{\nabla}\hat{p}\right\|_{s,\hat{E}}.

(6.20)

We further bound
\left\|j_{h}\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}
adding and subtracting
\Pi^{0}_{2l-2}j
and applying the triangle inequality, yielding

\displaystyle\left\|j_{h}\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|j_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{2l-2}j\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}+\left\|\Pi^{0}_{2l-2}j\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\Pi^{0}_{2l-2}\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}}\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}+\left\|\Pi^{0}_{2l-2}j\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}},

from which, an inverse inequality

Di Pietro and Droniou (2020)
(Corollary 1.29) gives

\displaystyle\left\|j_{h}\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{-s}\left\|\Pi^{0}_{2l-2}\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}+\left\|\Pi^{0}_{2l-2}j\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{-s}\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}+\left\|j\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}.

Lemma
6
is then applied to the first term and the second term is bounded using (
6.1
), giving

\displaystyle\left\|j_{h}\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}\lesssim\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}^{2}.

A similar argument yields

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{F},h}\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}\lesssim\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}},

and, inserting these bounds in (
6.20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}\right\|_{s,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\hat{a}\right\|_{s,\infty,\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}^{4}\left\|\hat{\nabla}\hat{p}\right\|_{s,\hat{E}}\lesssim\left\|\hat{p}\right\|_{s+1,\hat{E}}.

Using this in (
6.19
) completes the bound of
T_{3}
which, together with the bound of
T_{1}+T_{2}
gives

\displaystyle\left|A^{\hat{E}}(\hat{p},\hat{v}_{h})-A_{h}^{\hat{E}}(\hat{p},\hat{v}_{h})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|\hat{p}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}+h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|\boldsymbol{\gamma}\right\|_{s,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|\hat{p}\right\|_{s+1,\hat{E}}\left\|\hat{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\hat{E}}

A similar line of reasoning provides bounds for the lower order terms, while we note that the stabilization term is zero in this case.
Summing up the contributions from all elements already gives (
6.17
). The proof of (
6.18
) is similar and omitted for brevity.
∎

3.6
) the results from Theorem
2
, Theorem

; see eg.
Cangiani et al. (2016)

7
Physical IsoVEM analysis

The main difficulty in the analysis of the physical IsoVEM is in assessing the error due to the use of the projected elements in both the definition of the projection operators and the method itself. We thus proceed with a series of results aimed at assessing these sources of errors. One important step is given in Lemma
12
below, showing that the mesh sizes of the physical elements scale like the respective reference and projected elements, and thus can be used interchangeably.

We recall from Section
5.2
that the physical IsoVEM (
5.11
) is defined over the virtual domain
\Omega_{h}
by fixing the physical mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and corresponding virtual element space
V_{h}^{k}
via a reference mesh
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and corresponding virtual map
\mathbf{F}_{h}
.

7.1
Projected elements

We quantify the effects of using the projected elements for integration in the following technical results.

Lemma 11

(Projected element area approximation)
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
1
,
\mathbf{F}_{h}\in\left[\hat{V}_{h}^{l}\right]^{2}
satisfy Assumption
3
, and
h
be small enough.
For each reference element
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
with corresponding physical and projected elements
E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
E_{h}^{\pi}
, respectively, there holds

\displaystyle|\left|E_{h}\right|-\left|E_{h}^{\pi}\right||

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s+2}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left|E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}\right|=\left|E_{h}\backslash E_{h}^{\pi}\right|+\left|E_{h}^{\pi}\backslash E_{h}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s+2}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|^{3}_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}},

\displaystyle\left|E_{h}\triangle E\right|=\left|E_{h}\backslash E\right|+\left|E\backslash E_{h}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s+2}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|^{3}_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}},

with
s\in\{0,1,...,l\}
.
The symbol
\triangle
is used to denote the symmetric difference between the two sets. So, for instance,
E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}=(E_{h}\backslash E_{h}^{\pi})\cup(E_{h}^{\pi}\backslash E_{h})
.
The hidden constants depend on the shape regularity of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and on
l
.

Proof.

By similar arguments as in Lemma
7
, for
h
small enough, the determinants

\text{det}\left(\hat{\nabla}\mathbf{F}_{h}\right)
and
\text{det}\left(\hat{\nabla}\Pi^{0}_{l}\mathbf{F}_{h}\right)

are strictly positive. Hence, Hölder’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\left|\left|E_{h}\right|-\left|E_{h}^{\pi}\right|\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\hat{E}}\left(\text{det}\left(\hat{\nabla}\mathbf{F}_{h}\right)-\text{det}\left(\hat{\nabla}\Pi^{0}_{l}\mathbf{F}_{h}\right)\right)\ d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right|

\displaystyle\leq h^{2}_{\hat{E}}\left\|\text{det}\left(\hat{\nabla}\mathbf{F}_{h}\right)-\text{det}\left(\hat{\nabla}\Pi^{0}_{l}\mathbf{F}_{h}\right)\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}.

(7.1)

Adding and subtracting the determinants of
{\hat{\nabla}\mathbf{F}}
and
{\hat{\nabla}\Pi^{0}_{l}\mathbf{F}}
as in the proof of Lemma
6
we easily get

\displaystyle\left\|\text{det}\left(\hat{\nabla}\mathbf{F}_{h}\right)-\text{det}\left(\hat{\nabla}\Pi^{0}_{l}\mathbf{F}_{h}\right)\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}^{2},

and substitution of this into Equation (
7.1
) completes the first estimate.

In view of bounding the set difference
\left|E_{h}^{\pi}\backslash E_{h}\right|
, we reason that this set must be contained within a band around
E_{h}^{\pi}\backslash E_{h}
of thickness
r=\left\|\mathbf{F}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{l}\mathbf{F}_{h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}
. In turn, this can be estimated from above considering the bands constructed over each edge of
E_{h}
plus the area of circles of the same radius
r
centred on each vertex. Thus,

\displaystyle\left|E_{h}^{\pi}\backslash E_{h}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{e_{h}\subset\partial E_{h}}\left(\left\|\mathbf{F}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{l}\mathbf{F}_{h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left|e_{h}\right|+\frac{\pi}{4}\left\|\mathbf{F}_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{l}\mathbf{F}_{h}\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}^{2}\right).

From this, using once more the arguments of Lemma
6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|E_{h}^{\pi}\backslash E_{h}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s+1}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}\left(h_{\hat{E}}\left|\mathbf{F}_{h}\right|^{2}_{1,\infty,\hat{E}}+\frac{\pi}{4}h_{\hat{E}}^{s+1}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}\right)\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}^{3}.

A similar argument follows to provide bounds for
\left|E_{h}\backslash E_{h}^{\pi}\right|
. The bounds of
\left|E\backslash E_{h}\right|
and
\left|E_{h}\backslash E\right|
also follow similarly making use of Assumption
3
.
∎

When quantifying the error of the physical IsoVEM, we have to consider the scaling of the mesh size terms
h_{\omega}
for a choice of
\omega=\hat{E},E_{h},E_{h}^{\pi}
. A first consequence of the above lemma is that reference, physical, and projected elements mesh sizes can, in fact, be used interchangeably.

Lemma 12

(equivalence of mesh sizes)
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
1
,
\mathbf{F}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
3
, and
h
be small enough. For each
\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle h_{\hat{E}}\sim h_{E_{h}}\sim h_{E_{h}^{\pi}}

(7.2)

where the hidden constants depend on
j_{0}
and the shape regularity of
\hat{E}
. The second hidden constant depends also on the
W^{1}_{\infty}(\hat{E})
-norm of
\mathbf{F}
.

Proof.

We first show that
h_{\hat{E}}\sim h_{E_{h}}
.
Using
the shape regularity of
\hat{E}
and the arguments of Lemma
7
we have

\displaystyle j_{0}h_{\hat{E}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim j_{0}\left|\hat{E}\right|\lesssim\left|E_{h}\right|\lesssim h_{E_{h}}^{2}.

(7.3)

Taking square roots completes the lower bound. The upper bound is given using the shape regularity of
E_{h}
, computing the area of
E_{h}
using
\hat{E}
, applying Hölder’s inequality and the stability of the VEM interpolation

\displaystyle h_{E_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\left|E_{h}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\text{det}\left(\hat{\nabla}\mathbf{F}_{h}\right)\right\|_{0,\infty,\hat{E}}\left|\hat{E}\right|\leq 2\left|\mathbf{F}_{h}\right|^{2}_{1,\infty,\hat{E}}\left|\hat{E}\right|\lesssim\left|\mathbf{F}\right|_{1,\infty,\hat{E}}h_{\hat{E}}^{2}.

(7.4)

The second equivalence relation, namely
h_{E_{h}}\sim h_{E_{h}^{\pi}}
, follows easily from the first and Lemma
11
.
∎

Remark 10
.

We note that if the computational reference domain
\hat{\Omega}
is defined as being
close to

\Omega
, then the hidden constants in Lemma
12
are close to
1
.

7.2
Extension of polynomials

For polynomials
p\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(E_{h})
,
E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we consider their natural extensions to the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, still denoted by
p
, in view of their use over the projected element
E_{h}^{\pi}
.
We emphasise that this extension does not satisfy Stein’s extension Theorem
1
. Instead we have the following local polynomial extension bound.

Lemma 13

(Polynomial extension)
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
1
,
\mathbf{F}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
3
, and
h
be small enough.
For each physical element
E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and corresponding projected element
E_{h}^{\pi}
, there holds

\displaystyle\left\|p\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}^{\pi}}\lesssim\left\|p\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}},

for all
p\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(E_{h})
.
The hidden constant depends on the shape regularity of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, on
k
, and on the
W^{1}_{\infty}(\hat{E})
-norm of
\mathbf{F}
.

Proof.

We proceed by bounding the ratio between the two norms
R:={\left\|p\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}^{\pi}}}/{\left\|p\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}}
.
Applying an inverse estimate
(
Di Pietro and Droniou, 2020
, Lemma 1.25)
to the numerator and a Sobolev inequality
Adams and Fournier (2003)
to the denominator of
R
gives

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{h_{E_{h}^{\pi}}^{-1}\left\|p\right\|_{0,E_{h}^{\pi}}}{h_{E_{h}}^{-1}\left\|p\right\|_{0,E_{h}}}\lesssim\frac{\left\|p\right\|_{0,E_{h}^{\pi}}}{\left\|p\right\|_{0,E_{h}}},

by the equivalence
h_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\sim h_{E_{h}}
from Lemma
12
.
Passing to squares and expanding the numerator gives

\displaystyle R^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\left\|p\right\|^{2}_{0,E_{h}^{\pi}}}{\left\|p\right\|^{2}_{0,E_{h}}}=\left(\frac{\left\|p\right\|^{2}_{0,E_{h}}-\left\|p\right\|^{2}_{0,E_{h}\backslash E_{h}^{\pi}}+\left\|p\right\|^{2}_{0,E_{h}^{\pi}\backslash E_{h}}}{\left\|p\right\|^{2}_{0,E_{h}}}\right)\lesssim\left(1+\frac{\left\|p\right\|^{2}_{0,E_{h}^{\pi}\backslash E_{h}}}{\left\|p\right\|^{2}_{0,E_{h}}}\right).

(7.5)

The ratio of norms in the bound above can be further bounded by applying Hölder’s inequality on the numerator and, once again, an inverse estimate on the denominator to give

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|p\right\|^{2}_{0,E_{h}^{\pi}\backslash E_{h}}}{\left\|p\right\|^{2}_{0,E_{h}}}\lesssim\frac{\left|E_{h}^{\pi}\backslash E_{h}\right|}{\left|E_{h}\right|}R^{2}\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}R^{2},

having used Lemmas
11
and
12
to obtain the last inequality.
Substitution into (
7.5
) implies that there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle R^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}+C_{2}R^{2}h_{\hat{E}}^{s},

therefore assuming
h_{\hat{E}}
is such that
h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\leq C_{2}^{-1}/\penalty 2

results in
R^{2}\leq 2C_{1}
, implying that the ratio between norms
R
is uniformly bounded for
h
small enough, as required.
∎

7.3
Quadrature error

Herein we analyse the error due to the use of projected elements within the physical IsoVEM.

Lemma 14

(Projected element integration error)
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
1
,
\mathbf{F}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
3
, and
h
be small enough.
For all
E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
g\in L^{\infty}(E_{h}\cup E_{h}^{\pi})
, and
p\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(E_{h})
, there holds

\displaystyle\left|\int_{E_{h}}{g}p\ d\mathbf{x}-\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}{g}p\ d\mathbf{x}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s+1}_{\hat{E}}\left\|{g}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}}\left\|p\right\|_{0,E_{h}},

(7.6)

with
s\in\{0,1,...,l\}
.
The hidden constant depends on the shape regularity of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, on
k,l
, on the
W^{s+1}_{\infty}(\hat{E})
-norm
\mathbf{F}
, and on
j_{0}
.

Proof.

We decompose the integral difference into

\displaystyle\left|\int_{E_{h}}{g}p\ d\mathbf{x}-\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}{g}p\ d\mathbf{x}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{E_{h}\backslash E_{h}^{\pi}}{g}p\ d\mathbf{x}-\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}\backslash E_{h}}{g}p\ d\mathbf{x}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}\right|\left\|{g}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}}\left\|p\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}}.

(7.7)

Next, we bound the
L^{\infty}
-norm of
p
using Lemma
13
, yielding

\displaystyle\left\|p\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|p\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}\backslash E_{h}^{\pi}}+\left\|p\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}^{\pi}\backslash E_{h}}\leq\left\|p\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}+\left\|p\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}^{\pi}}\lesssim\left\|p\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}.

Substituting this bound into Equation (
7.7
), applying Lemma
11
to bound the measure of
E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}
, hiding norms of
\mathbf{F}
within the hidden constant, and using once again an inverse estimate, results in

\displaystyle\left|\int_{E_{h}}{g}p\ d\mathbf{x}-\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\tilde{g}p\ d\mathbf{x}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s+2}\left\|{g}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}}\left\|p\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|E_{h}\right|^{-1/2}h_{\hat{E}}^{s+2}\left\|{g}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}}\left\|p\right\|_{0,E_{h}},

from which applying Lemma
12
concludes the proof.
∎

7.4
Projection error estimates

We are now in a position to assess the accuracy with respect to the true
L^{2}
-projection of the approximate projection operators
\Pi^{0}_{h}
and
\Pi^{1}_{h}
given by Definitions
2
and
3
, respectively.

Theorem 15
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
1
,
\mathbf{F}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
3
, and
h
be small enough.
The operator
\Pi_{h,k}^{0}
given by Definition
2

satisfies, for each
E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
v_{h}\in V_{k}(E_{h})
,

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi^{0}_{k}v_{h}-\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}},

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}},

for
s\in\{0,1,...,l\}
.
The hidden constants depend on the shape regularity of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, on
k
,
l
,
j_{0}
, and on the
W^{s+1}_{\infty}(\hat{E})
-norm of
\mathbf{F}
.

Proof.

In this proof we adopt the notation used in
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2014)
in defining the matrix equations to compute the
L^{2}
projection. We consider the error for some
v_{h}\in V_{h}
as a vector-matrix product

\left\|\Pi^{0}_{k}v_{h}-\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}^{2}=\left(\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{t}_{h}\right)^{T}\mathbf{M}\left(\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{t}_{h}\right),

where we define the mass matrix
\mathbf{M}
on
E_{h}
by

\displaystyle M_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{h}}m_{\alpha}m_{\beta}\ d\mathbf{x},

for
m_{\alpha},m_{\beta}\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(E_{h})
(see Definition
1
) and the vectors of coefficients of
\Pi^{0}
and
\Pi_{h}^{0}
are given by
\mathbf{t}
and
\mathbf{t}_{h}
respectively. By using the
l_{2}
norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}
and the matrix norm induced by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}
and analysing individual matrix terms we bound the error noting that the mass matrix terms scale like
\left|E_{h}\right|

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi^{0}_{k}v_{h}-\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathbf{M}\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{t}_{h}\right\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim h_{E_{h}}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{t}_{h}\right\|_{2}^{2},

(7.8)

and it remains to bound the error in the projection coefficients. The
\Pi^{0}_{k}
projection is determined by
\mathbf{t}=\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{c}
where

c_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\left|E_{h}\right|\text{dof}_{\alpha}(v_{h})\qquad&m_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(E_{h}),\\
\int_{E_{h}}\Pi^{*}_{k}\ v_{h}\ m_{\alpha}\ d\mathbf{x}\qquad&m_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(E_{h})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(E_{h}),\end{cases}

while the
\Pi_{h}^{0}
projection is determined by
\mathbf{t}_{h}=\mathbf{M}_{h}^{-1}\mathbf{c}_{h}
where

c_{\alpha,h}=\begin{cases}\left|E_{h}^{\pi}\right|\text{dof}_{\alpha}(v_{h})\qquad&m_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(E_{h}),\\
\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\Pi^{*}_{k}v_{h}\ m_{\alpha}\ d\mathbf{x}\qquad&m_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(E_{h})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(E_{h}).\end{cases}

We then consider the difference of these coefficients, noting that

\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{t}_{h}=\left(\mathbf{M}^{-1}-\mathbf{M}_{h}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{c}\ +\ \mathbf{M}_{h}^{-1}\left(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{c}_{h}\right).

Passing to norms and applying the triangle and matrix norm inequalities results in

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{t}_{h}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|\mathbf{M}^{-1}-\mathbf{M}_{h}^{-1}\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathbf{c}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\mathbf{M}_{h}^{-1}\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{c}_{h}\right\|_{2}.

(7.9)

The first term can be expanded by

\mathbf{M}^{-1}-\mathbf{M}_{h}^{-1}=\mathbf{M}_{h}^{-1}\left(\mathbf{M}_{h}-\mathbf{M}\right)\mathbf{M}^{-1},

and hence, noting that the matrix inverses norms
\left\|\mathbf{M}^{-1}\right\|_{2}
and
\left\|\mathbf{M}_{h}^{-1}\right\|_{2}
scale like
h_{E_{h}}^{-2}
and applying Lemma
14
with
g\equiv 1
and
p=m_{\alpha}m_{\beta}
, and inverse estimates we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{M}^{-1}-\mathbf{M}_{h}^{-1}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathbf{M}_{h}^{-1}\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathbf{M}_{h}-\mathbf{M}\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathbf{M}^{-1}\right\|_{2}\lesssim h_{E_{h}}^{-4}\left\|\mathbf{M}_{h}-\mathbf{M}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{E_{h}}^{-4}\max_{\alpha,\beta}\left|\int_{E_{h}}m_{\alpha}m_{\beta}\ d\mathbf{x}-\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}m_{\alpha}m_{\beta}\ d\mathbf{x}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{E_{h}}^{-4}h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|m_{\alpha}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|m_{\beta}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\lesssim h_{E_{h}}^{-2}h^{s}_{\hat{E}},

given that
\left\|m_{\alpha}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\sim h_{E_{h}}
for all
m_{\alpha}
. Substituting this back in (
7.9
) gives

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{t}_{h}\right\|_{2}\lesssim h_{E_{h}}^{-2}h^{s}_{\hat{E}}\left\|\mathbf{c}\right\|_{2}+h_{E_{h}}^{-2}\left\|\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{c}_{h}\right\|_{2}.

(7.10)

The difference of
\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{c}_{h}
requires a case-by-case analysis.

Case I:
m_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(E_{h})
.

Taking the difference and applying Lemma
11
gives

\displaystyle\left|c_{\alpha}-c_{\alpha,h}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\text{dof}_{\alpha}(v_{h})\right|\left|\left|E_{h}\right|-\left|E_{h}^{\pi}\right|\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\text{dof}_{\alpha}(v_{h})\right|h_{\hat{E}}^{s+2}\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|^{2}_{s+1,\infty,\hat{E}}\lesssim h_{E_{h}}h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}},

(7.11)

having used in the last bound the fact that the scaled monomials scale like one, that is

\text{dof}_{\alpha}(v_{h})\sim h_{E_{h}}^{-1}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}
, cf. Definition
1
and
Beirão da Veiga
et al. (2014)
.

Case II:
m_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(E_{h})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{k-2}(E_{h})
.

Using the definition of the VEM space (
3.1
), and then applying Lemma
14
with
g\equiv 1
and
p=\Pi^{*}_{k}v_{h}\,m_{\alpha}
, inverse estimates, and Lemma
12
, we get

\displaystyle\left|c_{\alpha}-c_{\alpha,h}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|\Pi^{*}_{k}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|m_{\alpha}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\lesssim h_{E_{h}}h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}.

Having bounded both cases, we can now bound
\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{c}_{h}
via

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{c}_{h}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s+1}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}.

The norm of
\mathbf{c}
can also be bounded by a similar argument, yielding

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{c}\right\|_{2}\lesssim h_{E_{h}}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}},

and, using these bounds and the scaling of
\mathbf{M}_{h}^{-1}
in (
7.10
), we arrive to

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{t}_{h}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}h_{E_{h}}^{-1}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}.

To conclude the proof, we substitute this bound back into the original error, Equation (
7.8
), to get

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi^{0}_{k}v_{h}-\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{E_{h}}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{t}_{h}\right\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim h_{E_{h}}^{2}\left(h_{\hat{E}}^{s}h_{E_{h}}^{-1}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\right)^{2}\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{2s}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}^{2}.

(7.12)

Taking the square root completes the proof. The stability bound is a consequence of setting
s=0
and applying the triangle inequality.
∎

Theorem 16
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
1
,
\mathbf{F}_{h}

, and

The operator

The proof of the estimate follows the same arguments as Theorem

. We have,

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi^{1}_{k-1}v_{h}-\Pi_{h,k-1}^{1}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathbf{M}\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{t}_{h}\right\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim h_{E_{h}}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{t}_{h}\right\|_{2}^{2},

(7.13)

where
\mathbf{M}

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}=-\int_{E_{h}}v_{h}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{m}_{\alpha}\ d\mathbf{x}+\int_{\partial E_{h}}v_{h}\mathbf{m}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{n}\ dS,\qquad\mathbf{m}_{\alpha}\in\left[\mathcal{M}_{k-1}(E_{h})\right]^{2}.

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{t}_{h}\right\|_{2}\lesssim h_{E_{h}}^{-2}h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|\mathbf{c}\right\|_{2}+h_{E_{h}}^{-2}\left\|\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{c}_{h}\right\|_{2}.

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}-c_{\alpha,h}=\int_{E_{h}}v_{h}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{m}_{\alpha}\ d\mathbf{x}-\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\Pi^{0}_{h,k}v_{h}\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf{m}_{\alpha}\ d\mathbf{x}.

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}v_{h}-\Pi^{1}_{k-1}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}^{2}

(7.18)

Taking the square root completes the proof. The stability bound is a consequence of setting

7.5
Well-posedness

The use of
overlapping
projected elements in the assembly of the physical IsoVEM introduces a new form of inconsistency. As a consequence, the proof of coercivity of the physical IsoVEM bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{h}
given in (
5.8
) is not-trivial and requires a careful analysis.
To this end, we introduce the discrete bilinear form

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}:V_{h}^{k}\times V_{h}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by summing up the elemental contributions

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{E_{h}}(u,v)=

\displaystyle\int_{E_{h}}\Big(\tilde{a}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}u\cdot\Pi^{1}_{k-1}v+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathbf{b}}\cdot(\Pi^{0}_{k}v\Pi^{1}_{k-1}u-\Pi^{0}_{k}u\Pi^{1}_{k-1}v)+\tilde{\mu}\Pi^{0}_{k}u\ \Pi^{0}_{k}v\Big)\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle S^{E_{h}}(u-\Pi^{0}_{k}u,v-\Pi^{0}_{k}v),

(7.19)

where the stabilisation term is assumed to be identical to the choice of stabilisation in Section
5.2
.

Lemma 17
.

Let
s=\min\{k,l\}
. Then, for all
u_{h},v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}
, it holds that

\sum_{E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{E_{h}}(u_{h},v_{h})-\mathcal{A}_{h}^{E_{h}}(u_{h},v_{h})\right|\lesssim h^{s}\left\|u_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}},

where the hidden constant depends on the shape regularity of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, on
k
,
l
,
j_{0}
, the
W^{s+1}_{\infty}(\hat{\Omega})
-norm of
\mathbf{F}
, and the
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
-norm of
a
,
\mathbf{b}
, and
\mu
.

Proof.

Noting that there is no contribution to
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{h}
from the stabilisation term, we split the difference via

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{E_{h}}(u_{h},v_{h})-\mathcal{A}_{h}^{E_{h}}(u_{h},v_{h})=(T_{q}^{E_{h}}+T_{a}^{E_{h}}+T_{b}^{E_{h}}+T_{c}^{E_{h}}),

where through adding and subtracting terms we have,

\displaystyle T_{q}^{E_{h}}

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{h}}\tilde{a}\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}u_{h}\cdot\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}v_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}-\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\tilde{a}\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}u_{h}\cdot\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}v_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(\int_{E_{h}}\tilde{\mathbf{b}}\cdot(\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}u_{h}-\Pi_{h,k}^{0}u_{h}\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}v_{h})\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\tilde{\mathbf{b}}\cdot(\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}u_{h}-\Pi_{h,k}^{0}u_{h}\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}v_{h})\ d\mathbf{x}\Bigg)

\displaystyle+\int_{E_{h}}\tilde{\mu}\Pi_{h,k}^{0}u_{h}\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}-\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\tilde{\mu}\Pi_{h,k}^{0}u_{h}\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\ d\mathbf{x},

\displaystyle T_{a}^{E_{h}}

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{h}}\tilde{a}(\Pi^{1}_{k-1}-\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1})u_{h}\cdot\Pi^{1}_{k-1}v_{h}+\tilde{a}\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}u_{h}\cdot(\Pi^{1}_{k-1}-\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1})v_{h}\ d\mathbf{x},

\displaystyle T_{b}^{E_{h}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{E_{h}}\tilde{\mathbf{b}}\cdot\big(\Pi_{k}^{0}v_{h}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}u_{h}-\Pi_{k}^{0}u_{h}\Pi^{1}_{k-1}v_{h}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}u_{h}-\Pi_{h,k}^{0}u_{h}\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}v_{h}\big)d\mathbf{x},

\displaystyle T_{c}^{E_{h}}

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{h}}\left(\tilde{\mu}(\Pi^{0}_{k}-\Pi_{h,k}^{0})u_{h}\Pi^{0}_{k}v_{h}+\tilde{\mu}\Pi^{0}_{h,k}u_{h}(\Pi^{0}_{k}-\Pi_{h,k}^{0})v_{h}\ \right)d\mathbf{x}.

The local quadrature error term
T_{q}^{E_{h}}
is bounded by applying Lemma
14
to give

\displaystyle\left|T_{q}^{E_{h}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s}_{\hat{E}}\Big(\left\|\tilde{a}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}u_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{\tilde{b}}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}u_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{\tilde{b}}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi_{h,k}^{0}u_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\left\|\tilde{\mu}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}}\left\|\Pi_{h,k}^{0}u_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\Big).

Then, using the stability bounds in Theorems
15
and
16
, provides

\displaystyle\left|T_{q}^{E_{h}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s}_{\hat{E}}\max\left(\left\|\tilde{a}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}},\left\|\mathbf{\tilde{b}}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}},\left\|\tilde{\mu}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}\triangle E_{h}^{\pi}}\right)\left\|u_{h}\right\|_{1,E_{h}}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{1,E_{h}}

Considering
T_{a}^{E_{h}}
,
we use Theorem
16
to get

\displaystyle\left|T_{a}^{E_{h}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\tilde{a}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}\left(\left\|\Pi^{1}_{k-1}u_{h}-\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}u_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{k-1}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left.\left\|\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}u_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{k-1}v_{h}-\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|\tilde{a}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}\left\|u_{h}\right\|_{1,E_{h}}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{1,E_{h}}.

The terms
T_{b}^{E_{h}}
and
T_{c}^{E_{h}}
can be similarly bounded.
We conclude the proof by summing up the above bounds over all elements in the mesh and applying Stein’s extension Theorem
1
to bound the infinity norms of
\tilde{a}
,
\mathbf{\tilde{b}}
, and
\tilde{\mu}
from
\Omega_{h}
back to
\Omega
.
∎

Theorem 18

(Physical IsoVEM well-posedness)
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
1
,
\mathbf{F}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
3
, and
h
be small enough. Then, the physical IsoVEM formulation (
5.11
) with stabilisation (
5.10
) obeying Assumption
2
possesses a unique solution.

Proof.

We verify once more the assumptions of the Lax-Milgram lemma, starting with the continuity of
\mathcal{A}_{h}
. Application of the triangle, Hölder and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{A}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big(\left\|\tilde{a}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}u_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\tilde{\mathbf{b}}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}u_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\tilde{\mathbf{b}}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi_{h,k}^{0}u_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\left\|\tilde{\mu}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi_{h,k}^{0}u_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi_{h,k}^{0}v_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}+S^{E_{h}}(u_{h},v_{h})\Big).

Then we apply the stability estimates from Theorems
15
and
16
, Assumption
2
on the VEM stabilisation term, and finally Cauchy-Schwarz to get

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{A}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\left\{\left\|\tilde{a}\right\|_{0,\infty,\Omega_{h}},\left\|\tilde{\mathbf{b}}\right\|_{0,\infty,\Omega_{h}},\left\|\tilde{\mu}\right\|_{0,\infty,\Omega_{h}}\right\}\left\|u_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}},

which can be further bounded in terms of norms of the data over
\Omega
thanks to Stein’s extension Theorem
1
, thus completing the continuity proof.
The continuity of
l_{h}
can be similarly proven.

To prove the coercivity of
\mathcal{A}_{h}
, we consider the bilinear form
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{E_{h}}
defined in Equation (
7.19
) and consider the coercivity constant
C_{1}>0
given in
Cangiani et al. (2016)
and the continuity constant
C_{2}
of Lemma
17
to get

\displaystyle C_{1}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})\leq\mathcal{A}_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})+\sum_{E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left|\mathcal{A}_{h}^{E_{h}}(v_{h},v_{h})-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{E_{h}}(v_{h},v_{h})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{A}_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})+C_{2}h^{s}\left\|v_{h}\right\|^{2}_{1,\Omega_{h}}.

Coercivity now follows for sufficiently small
h
such that
C_{1}-C_{2}h^{s}>0
.
∎

7.6
Error estimate

Before embarking on the error analysis for the physical IsoVEM (
5.11
), we note that the discrete solution
u_{h}\in V_{h,0}^{k}
is defined over the virtual domain
\Omega_{h}
while the exact solution
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is defined over the true physical domain
\Omega
, hence they are
not
directly comparable. Instead, we shall compare
u_{h}
with the restriction on
\Omega_{h}
of the extension
\tilde{u}
given by Stein’s Theorem
1
.
Similarly, we introduce a new bilinear form
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
to replace the form
\mathcal{A}
used in the continuum problem (
2.3
). This is defined over
H^{1}(\Omega_{h})
by replacing
\Omega,{a},{\mathbf{b}},
and
{\mu}
with, respectively,
\Omega_{h},\tilde{a},\tilde{\mathbf{b}},
and
\tilde{\mu}
in (
2.4
)-(
2.5
). Finally, following Ciarlet
Ciarlet (2002)
, we introduce the extension
f_{*}
of the forcing function
f
given a.e. in
\Omega_{h}
by

f_{*}:=-\nabla\cdot(\tilde{a}\nabla\tilde{u})+\tilde{\mathbf{b}}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}+\tilde{c}\tilde{u},

(7.20)

assuming that
f_{*}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{h})
. We thus notice that the extended solution
\tilde{u}
satisfies

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\tilde{u},v)=l_{*}(v)\qquad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{h}),

(7.21)

where
the linear operator
l_{*}
is given by

\displaystyle l_{*}(v)=\int_{\Omega_{h}}f_{*}v\ d\mathbf{x}\qquad\forall v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{h}).

These extensions allow derivation of the Strang-type bound analogue to that of Theorem
4
, cf. also
Ciarlet (2002)
, and thus prove the following result.

Theorem 19

(Physical IsoVEM Strang-type bound)
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
1
,
\mathbf{F}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
3
, and
h
be small enough.
For the solution
u_{h}\in V_{h,0}^{k}
of the physical IsoVEM (
5.11
) it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{u}-u_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h,0}^{k}}\left\|\tilde{u}-v_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}+\inf_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\left\|\tilde{u}-p\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle+\sup_{w_{h}\in V_{h,0}^{k}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left|l_{h}(w_{h})-l_{*}(w_{h})\right|}{\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}}

\displaystyle+\inf_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\sup_{w_{h}\in V_{h,0}^{k}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\sum_{E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{E_{h}}(p,w_{h})-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{E_{h}}(p,w_{h})\right|}{\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}}

\displaystyle+\inf_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\sup_{w_{h}\in V_{h,0}^{k}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\sum_{E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{E_{h}}(p,w_{h})-\mathcal{A}_{h}^{E_{h}}(p,w_{h})\right|}{\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}},

(7.22)

where the hidden constant depends on the shape regularity of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, on
k
and
l
, on the lower bounds
a_{0}
,
\mu_{0}
,
j_{0}
and the
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
-norms of
a
,
\mathbf{b}
, and
\mu
, and on the
W^{s+1}_{\infty}(\hat{\Omega})
norm of
\mathbf{F}
.

Proof.

We have once again following
Cangiani et al. (2016)
the basic Strang-type bound analogue to (
4
), which in the present setting reads

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{u}-u_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h,0}^{k}}\left\|\tilde{u}-v_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}+\inf_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\left\|\tilde{u}-p\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38092pt+\sup_{w_{h}\in V_{h,0}^{k}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left|l_{h}(w_{h})-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}(\tilde{u},w_{h})\right|}{\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}}

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38092pt+\inf_{p\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\sup_{w_{h}\in V_{k}(E_{h})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\sum_{E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{E_{h}}(p,w_{h})-\mathcal{A}_{h}^{E_{h}}(p,w_{h})\right|}{\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}}.

Inserting (
7.21
) in the third term and adding and subtracting
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{E_{h}}(p,w_{h})
in the fourth we conclude.
∎

We assess the consistency error terms in the above Strang-type bound. The fifth term in (
7.22
), measuring the bilinear form inconsistency due to projection, was already treated in Lemma
17
. The fourth term, measuring the consistency between the exact and virtual bilinear forms defined on
\Omega_{h}
is standard and can be bounded by applying verbatim the results of
Cangiani et al. (2016)
. We record this bound in the following lemma.

Lemma 20
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
19
, if, moreover,
a,\mathbf{b},\mu\in W_{\infty}^{s+1}(\Omega)
and
u\in H^{s+1}(\Omega)
, for some
s\in\{1,\dots,\min\{k,l\}\}
, there holds

\sup_{w_{h}\in V_{h,0}^{k}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\sum_{E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left|{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{E_{h}}}(p,w_{h})-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}^{E_{h}}(p,w_{h})\right|}{\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}}\lesssim h^{s}\left\|\tilde{u}\right\|_{s+1,\Omega_{h}},

for all
p\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
. The hidden constant depends on the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, on
k
and
l
, and on the
W^{s+1}_{\infty}(\Omega)
-norms of
{a}
,
{\mathbf{b}}
, and
{\mu}
.

We proceed with bounding the remaining term three in (
7.22
) relating to the linear functional consistency.

Lemma 21
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
19
, if, moreover,
a\in W_{\infty}^{s}(\Omega)
,
\mathbf{b},\mu\in W_{\infty}^{s-1}(\Omega)
,
f\in H^{s-1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
u\in H^{s+1}(\Omega)\cap W_{\infty}^{2}(\Omega)
for some
s\in\{1,\dots,\min\{k,l\}\}
, then

\frac{\left|l_{h}(w_{h})-l_{*}(w_{h})\right|}{\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}}\lesssim h^{s}\left(\left\|\tilde{f}\right\|_{s-1,\Omega_{h}}+\left\|\tilde{u}\right\|_{s+1,\Omega_{h}}+\left\|\tilde{f}\right\|_{0,\infty,\Omega_{h}}+\left\|\tilde{u}\right\|_{2,\infty,\Omega_{h}}\right),

holds true with hidden constant depending on the shape regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, on
k
,
l
, and
j_{0}
, and on the
W^{s+1}_{\infty}(\hat{\Omega})
-norm of
\mathbf{F}
.

Proof.

Adding and subtracting
\Pi^{0}_{k-2}\tilde{f}
and using the orthogonality of
\Pi^{0}
we rewrite the difference term as

\displaystyle l_{h}(w_{h})-l_{*}(w_{h})=

\displaystyle\sum_{E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left[\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\tilde{f}\ \Pi_{h,k}^{0}w_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}-\int_{E_{h}}f_{*}w_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Bigg[\left(\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}\Pi^{0}_{k-2}\tilde{f}\ \Pi^{0}_{h,k}w_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}-\int_{E_{h}}\Pi^{0}_{k-2}\tilde{f}\ w_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}(\tilde{f}-\Pi^{0}_{k-2}\tilde{f})\Pi^{0}_{h,k}w_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{E_{h}}(\Pi^{0}_{k-2}f_{*}-f_{*})(w_{h}-\Pi^{0}_{k-2}w_{h})\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{E_{h}}(\tilde{f}-f_{*})\Pi^{0}_{k-2}w_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(T_{1}^{E_{h}}+T_{2}^{E_{h}}+T_{3}^{E_{h}}+T_{4}^{E_{h}}).

(7.23)

The term
T_{1}^{E_{h}}
can be rewritten using (
5.7
) as

T_{1}^{E_{h}}=\frac{\left|E_{h}^{\pi}\right|-\left|E_{h}\right|}{\left|E_{h}\right|}\int_{E_{h}}\Pi^{0}_{k-2}\tilde{f}\ w_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the stability of
\Pi^{0}
, and Lemmas
11
and
12
results in

\displaystyle\left|T_{1}^{E_{h}}\right|\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|\tilde{f}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}.

To bound
T_{2}^{E_{h}}
, we introduce the
L^{2}
-projection onto
\mathbb{P}_{k-2}(E_{h}^{\pi})
, denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{k-2}
, and apply Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
13
, and inverse estimates over
E_{h}
, yielding

\displaystyle\left|\int_{E_{h}^{\pi}}(\mathcal{P}_{k-2}\tilde{f}-\Pi^{0}_{k-2}\tilde{f})\Pi^{0}_{h,k}w_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|E_{h}^{\pi}\right|\left\|\mathcal{P}_{k-2}\tilde{f}-\Pi^{0}_{k-2}\tilde{f}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}^{\pi}}\left\|\Pi^{0}_{h,k}w_{h}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}^{\pi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|E_{h}^{\pi}\right|\left\|\mathcal{P}_{k-2}\tilde{f}-\Pi^{0}_{k-2}\tilde{f}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi^{0}_{h,k}w_{h}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mathcal{P}_{k-2}\tilde{f}-\Pi^{0}_{k-2}\tilde{f}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi^{0}_{h,k}w_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}.

Following the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
15
results in

\displaystyle\left|T_{2}^{E_{h}}\right|\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|\tilde{f}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}.

T_{3}^{E_{h}}
is bounded via Cauchy-Schwarz and Theorem
2
, resulting in

\displaystyle\left|T_{3}^{E_{h}}\right|\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left\|f_{*}\right\|_{s-1,E_{h}}\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{1,E_{h}}.

We rewrite
T_{4}^{E_{h}}
by splitting the integral into
E_{h}\backslash E
and
E_{h}\cap E
and noting that
f_{*}=\tilde{f}
a.e. on
E_{h}\cap E

\displaystyle\int_{E_{h}}(\tilde{f}-f_{*})\Pi^{0}_{k-2}w_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{h}\setminus E}(\tilde{f}-f_{*})\ \Pi^{0}_{k-2}w_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}.

T_{4}^{E_{h}}
is bounded by applying Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
11
, inverse estimates over
E_{h}
, and the stability of
\Pi^{0}

\displaystyle\left|\int_{E_{h}\backslash E}(\tilde{f}-f_{*})\ \Pi^{0}_{k-2}w_{h}\ d\mathbf{x}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|E_{h}\backslash E\right|\left\|\tilde{f}-f_{*}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi^{0}_{k-2}w_{h}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s+1}\left\|\tilde{f}-f_{*}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}\left\|\Pi^{0}_{k-2}w_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{\hat{E}}^{s+1}\left\|\tilde{f}-f_{*}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{0,E_{h}}.

Substituting the bound of each term back into (
7.23
) and bounding norms gives

\displaystyle\left|l_{h}(w_{h})-l_{*}(w_{h})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{E_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{\hat{E}}^{s}\left(\left\|\tilde{f}\right\|_{s-1,E_{h}}+\left\|f_{*}\right\|_{s-1,E_{h}}+\left\|\tilde{f}-f_{*}\right\|_{0,\infty,E_{h}}\right)\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{1,E_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s}\left(\left\|\tilde{f}\right\|_{s-1,\Omega_{h}}+\left\|f_{*}\right\|_{s-1,\Omega_{h}}+\left\|\tilde{f}-f_{*}\right\|_{0,\infty,\Omega_{h}}\right)\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}.

Bounding norms of
f_{*}
with norms of
\tilde{u}
using (
7.20
)
and dividing by
\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}
the required estimate readily follows.
∎

Remark 11
.

Assuming, as in the classical analysis of isoparametric FEM by Ciarlet
Ciarlet (2002)
, that
\tilde{f}\equiv f_{*}
, we obtain an
O(h^{s})
estimate without requiring the additional
L^{\infty}
regularity on
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{f}
, cf.
Wells (2023)
.
Also, for
\tilde{f}\neq f_{*}
as in the above lemma, following a slightly different line of proof, we can obtain an estimate of the relevant terms of
O(h^{3s/2-1})
, without the additional
L^{\infty}
regularity. Thus, the additional regularity assumption can be dropped from the statement of Lemma
21
when
k,l\geq 2
.

We are finally in a position to conclude the a priori analysis of the physical IsoVEM with the following error bound.

Theorem 22

(Physical IsoVEM error estimate)
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
1
,
\mathbf{F}_{h}
satisfy Assumption
3
, and
h
be small enough. Suppose additionally that the solution to (
2.3
) satisfies
u\in H^{s+1}(\Omega)\cap W^{2}_{\infty}(\Omega)
with coefficients
a,\mathbf{b},\mu\in W_{\infty}^{s+1}(\Omega)
, and forcing term
f\in H^{s-1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, for some
s\in\{1,\dots,\min\{k,l\}\}
. Then, for the solution
u_{h}\in V_{h,0}^{k}
of the physical IsoVEM (
5.11
), there holds

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{u}-u_{h}\right\|_{1,\Omega_{h}}+\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}-\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}{u}_{h}\right\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}\lesssim h^{s}\Big(

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{s+1,\Omega}+\left\|f\right\|_{s-1,\Omega}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt+\left\|f\right\|_{0,\infty,\Omega}+\left\|u\right\|_{2,\infty,\Omega}\Big).

The hidden constant is dependent on the shape regularity of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, on
s
,
k
,
l
, and
j_{0}
, on the
W^{s+1}_{\infty}(\hat{\Omega})
-norm of
\mathbf{F}
, and on the
W^{s+1}_{\infty}(\Omega)
-norms of
a
,
\mathbf{b}
, and
\mu
.

Proof.

The proof of the first term’s bound is standard, cf. Section
3
and
Cangiani et al. (2016)
. It follows by bounding each term in the Strang-type bound (
7.22
). The first two terms are bounded by considering
v_{h}=\tilde{u}_{I}
and
p=\Pi^{0}_{k}\tilde{u}
and then using the polynomial projection and virtual interpolation error bounds from Theorem
2
and Theorem
3
. The remaining, consistency error, terms are bounded using the results from Lemmata
17
,
20
, and
21
. The proof is concluded by bounding norms of the extensions
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{f}
back to
\Omega
using Stein’s extension Theorem
1
.

To bound the second term we add and subtract the
\Pi^{1}_{k-1}
projection of both
\tilde{u}
and
u_{h}
to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}-\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}{u}_{h}\right\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}-\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\tilde{u}\right\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}+\left\|\Pi^{1}_{k-1}(\tilde{u}-{u}_{h})\right\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle+\left\|\Pi^{1}_{k-1}{u}_{h}-\Pi^{1}_{h,k-1}{u}_{h}\right\|_{0,\Omega_{h}}.

The result easily follows from Theorems
2
and
16
and the bound of
\tilde{u}-u_{h}
already derived.
∎

\displaystyle\left|u-u_{h}\right|_{h,1}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\hat{E}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\hat{E}}\left|\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{F},h}^{-\top}\ \left(\hat{\nabla}\hat{u}-\Pi^{1}_{k-1}\hat{u}_{h}\right)\right|^{2}\ j_{h}\ d\boldsymbol{\xi},

\displaystyle\left|u-u_{h}\right|_{h,1}^{2}

9
Conclusion

We presented two isoparametric VEM: a method that approximates the problem on a reference computational domain and a method that constructs a discretization on a physical
virtual
domain. In both cases, optimal
H^{1}
-norm error estimates are proven and numerical results are shown supporting the theoretical results. In addition, we have verified experimentally that the methods achieve optimal orders of convergence in the
L^{2}
-norm as well.Extensions of the analysis to
L^{2}
-norm estimates and three-dimensional problems will be the subject of future work.

In contrast to other works in curved virtual element methods, this paper considers the mapping between domains to be subject to an approximation, not known explicitly
a priori
and only computable through a set of degrees of freedom. This perspective naturally allows for the consideration of more complex problems upon which the mapping is the subject of further numerical approximation. As such, our analysis provides the basis for the development of accurate VEMs for time-dependent problems requiring discretization of both the solution and domain evolution, such as fluid-structure interaction problems.

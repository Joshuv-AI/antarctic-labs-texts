Path-homotopy is equivalent to ℝ \mathbb{R} -tree reduction Thanks: The third author gratefully acknowledges the generous support of University Primorska, Koper, Slovenia, May 2018
Jeremy Brazas, Gregory R. Conner, Paul Fabel, Curtis Kent

Abstract
. Suppose a path α \alpha factors through an ℝ \mathbb{R} -tree T T as α = q ∘ p \alpha=q\circ p . Let r r parameterize the unique geodesic in T T joining the endpoints of p p . Then we say that the path β = q ∘ r \beta=q\circ r is obtained from α \alpha by geodesic ℝ \mathbb{R} -tree reduction . Essentially, β \beta is obtained from α \alpha by deleting one-dimensional back-tracking. In this paper, we show that any two homotopic paths are geodesic ℝ \mathbb{R} -tree reductions of some single common path. Hence, the equivalence relation on paths generated by geodesic ℝ \mathbb{R} -tree reduction is precisely path-homotopy. The common path is explicitly constructed and is necessarily space-filling in the image of a given path-homotopy.

Introduction

Suppose the non-constant path
\alpha:[0,1]\to X
in a metric space factors through an
\mathbb{R}
-tree
T
as
\alpha=q\circ p
for a path
p:[0,1]\to T
and a map

. Let

parameterize the unique geodesic in

from

to

. Then we say that the path
\beta=q\circ r
is obtained from
\alpha
by

\mathbb{R}
-tree reduction
. Certainly, if
\beta
is a geodesic
\mathbb{R}
-tree reduction of
\alpha
, then
\alpha
and
\beta
are homotopic. In this paper, we show that any two homotopic paths are both
\mathbb{R}
-tree reductions of a single common path. Hence, one may delete “one-dimensional backtracking” from this common path to obtain either of the two homotopic paths.

Main Theorem
.

If
X
is a topological space and
\alpha,\beta:[0,1]\to X
are homotopic paths, then there exists a path
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
such that
\alpha
and
\beta
are both geodesic
\mathbb{R}
-tree reductions of
\gamma
.

Typically, the path
\gamma
will be very complicated, as it (1) will be space-filling in the image of some chosen homotopy between
\alpha
and
\beta
and (2) must pass through all of the points of both
\alpha
and
\beta
in the same order that each of these paths does. Despite its complexity, the construction of
\gamma
from the pair
\alpha,\beta
is entirely explicit. Our Main Theorem implies that the equivalence relation on paths generated by geodesic
\mathbb{R}
-tree reduction coincides precisely with path-homotopy. This theorem also implies that the one-dimensional analogue of the path-homotopy relation (two paths are equivalent if they form a loop that factors through an
\mathbb{R}
-tree) is, in fact,
not
an equivalence relation on paths in the plane because it fails to be transitive.

\mathbb{R}
-tree factorization of paths and loops

\alpha

\beta
in a space

\alpha\beta

\overline{\alpha}

[0,1]
to

is complete, then so is its path space

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha:[a,b]\to X

\beta:[c,d]\to X

(1)

We say
\alpha
is

\beta

(2)

We say

.

Both

\simeq
. See
[
7
]
for a proof of the transitivity of
\approx
\approx
. Recall that an
\mathbb{R}
-tree
is a uniquely arcwise-connected, and locally arcwise connected geodesic metric space
[
4
,
11
]
. A compact
\mathbb{R}
-tree is called a

[
12
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\alpha,\beta:[0,1]\to X
be paths in a topological space
X
.

(1)

We say that
\beta
is a

\mathbb{R}
-tree reduction of

\alpha
, and we write
\alpha\geq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta
, if there is an
\mathbb{R}
-tree
T
, a path
p:[0,1]\to T
, an injective path

with
p(i)=r(i)
for
i\in\{0,1\}
, and a map

such that
f\circ p=\alpha
and
f\circ r=\beta
.

(2)

We say that
\alpha
and
\beta
are
\mathbb{R}
-tree homotopic
, and we write
\alpha\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta
, if
\alpha\overline{\beta}
is a loop that factors through an
\mathbb{R}
-tree, that is, if
\alpha(i)=\beta(i)
for

and if there exists an
\mathbb{R}
-tree
T
, a loop
\ell:[0,1]\to T

.

Note that

\alpha\equiv\beta\text{ }\Rightarrow\text{ }\alpha\geq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta\text{ }\Rightarrow\text{ }\alpha\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta\text{ }\Rightarrow\text{ }\alpha\simeq\beta

where the last implication holds since
\mathbb{R}
-trees are contractible. Also, if
\alpha\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta
where
\beta
is injective, then
\alpha\geq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta
. Certainly,
\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}
is reflexive and symmetric. However, the transitivity of
\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}
is less clear. In a given space, transitivity of
\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}
is equivalent to whether or not each path admits a unique (up to
\equiv
) “maximally reduced” geodesic
\mathbb{R}
-tree reduction. One must be wary of this temptation as our main result implies that there exist some paths in the plane which fail to have unique maximally reduced representatives. We note some key properties of
\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\geq_{\mathbb{R}}
that
do
hold.

Remark 2.3
.

If
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are
\mathbb{R}
-trees,
f_{1}:[0,1]\to T_{1}
is an injective path, and
f_{2}:[0,1]\to T_{2}
is any path, then the pushout of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
is an
\mathbb{R}
-tree. Indeed, this pushout is obtained by attaching the closure of each connected component of
T_{1}\backslash f_{1}([0,1])
to
T_{2}
at a point along the image of
f_{2}
. This fact can be used to show the following.

(1)

\geq_{\mathbb{R}}
is transitive and is antisymmetric up to equivalence, that is,
\alpha\geq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta
and
\beta\geq_{\mathbb{R}}\alpha

\Rightarrow

\alpha\equiv\beta
. Hence,
\geq_{\mathbb{R}}
induces a partial order on path-equivalence classes.

(2)

\alpha\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta
if and only if
\alpha
and
\beta
share a common geodesic
\mathbb{R}
-tree reduction, that is, if and only if there exists
\delta
with
\alpha\geq_{\mathbb{R}}\delta
and
\beta\geq_{\mathbb{R}}\delta
.

Lemma 2.4
.

The following properties of
\mathbb{R}
-tree homotopy hold.

(1)

If
\alpha,\beta:[0,1]\to X
are paths such that
\alpha\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta
(resp.
\alpha\geq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta
) and
f:X\to Y
is a map, then
f\circ\alpha\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}f\circ\beta
(resp.
f\circ\alpha\geq_{\mathbb{R}}f\circ\beta
).

(2)

If
\alpha_{1}\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta_{2}
, and
\alpha_{1}(1)=\alpha_{2}(0)
, then
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta_{1}\beta_{2}
.

(3)

If
\alpha,\beta:[0,1]\to X
are paths and

for non-decreasing continuous surjections

.

(4)

If
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}:[0,1]\to X
are paths such that
\alpha_{1}\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\alpha_{2}

\beta_{1},\beta_{2}:[0,1]\to X

\alpha_{1}\,\mbox{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\raisebox{2.31895pt}{$\approx$}}$\approx$}\,\beta_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}\,\mbox{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\raisebox{2.31895pt}{$\approx$}}$\approx$}\,\beta_{2}

Proof.

(1) is clear. (2) holds since the one-point union of two
\mathbb{R}
-trees is an
\mathbb{R}
-tree. (3) Since
\alpha\circ g_{1}\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta\circ g_{2}
, there exists an
\mathbb{R}
-tree

, map
F:T\to X
, and loop
\ell:[0,1]\to T

. We may replace

with the dendrite
\ell([0,1])
and assume
\ell
is surjective. Since

is a compact metric space
[

and we may replace

. Define a non-decreasing surjection

by

g(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}g_{1}(2t),&\text{ if }t\in[0,\frac{1}{2}],\\
1-\frac{1}{2}g_{2}(2-2t),&\text{ if }t\in[\frac{1}{2},1]\end{cases}

so that
F\circ\ell=(\alpha\overline{\beta})\circ g
. Applying the Monotone-Light Factorization Theorem
[

for a monotone map
\pi:T\to T^{\prime}
and a light map

. Since

is a monotone quotient map on a dendrite,

is a dendrite
[

. Since
g
is monotone,
F^{\prime}
is light, and
F^{\prime}\circ\pi\circ\ell=(\alpha\overline{\beta})\circ g
, the loop
\pi\circ\ell:[0,1]\to T^{\prime}
is constant on the fibers of

. Thus, there is a unique loop
\mu:[0,1]\to T^{\prime}
such that
\pi\circ\ell=\mu\circ g
.

Since

(4) Write

3.
Cantor paths and their staggering

Definition 3.1
.

An open set
U\subseteq(0,1)
is a
Cantor complement
if
[0,1]\backslash U
is homeomorphic to a Cantor set.

Note that an open set
U\subseteq(0,1)
is a Cantor complement if and only if
U
is dense in
[0,1]
and the set of connected components of
U
have the order type of
\mathbb{Q}
. It is necessarily the case that the connected components of a Cantor complement have pairwise-disjoint closures. Given a path
\alpha:[0,1]\to X
, let
\text{lc}(\alpha)
denote the set of connected components of the open set
\mathcal{O}(\alpha)=\{t\in[0,1]\mid\alpha\text{ is locally constant at }t\}
. Note that distinct elements of
\text{lc}(\alpha)
have pairwise-disjoint closures and that
\alpha
is a light map if and only if
\mathcal{O}(\alpha)=\emptyset
.

Definition 3.2
.

A non-constant path
\alpha:[0,1]\to X
is a
Cantor path
if
\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
is a Cantor complement.

The standard ternary Cantor map
\tau:[0,1]\to[0,1]
collapses the closure of each component of the complement of the ternary Cantor set to a point. Thus
\tau
is a non-decreasing, surjective Cantor path. If
\alpha
is a light path, then
\alpha\circ\tau
is a Cantor path. A key concept in the proof of our main theorem is the following.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
U_{1},U_{2}
be proper open subsets of
(0,1)
such that for each
i\in\{1,2\}
,
\inf(U_{i})=0
,
\sup(U_{i})=1
, and such that the connected components of
U_{i}
have pairwise disjoint closures. We say the sets
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are
staggered
if
U_{1}\cup U_{2}=(0,1)
, or equivalently, if
\partial U_{1}\cap\partial U_{2}=\{0,1\}
.

The next lemma, which has a straightforward proof, allows us to select connected components from staggered Cantor complements
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
so that the resulting collections have the order type of
\mathbb{Z}
while still having staggered unions.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
be staggered Cantor complements. Then for
i\in\{1,2\}
there exists a set of connected components
\mathscr{U}_{i}
of
U_{i}
such that,
\mathscr{U}_{i}
has the order type of
\mathbb{Z}
,
\inf(\bigcup\mathscr{U}_{i})=0
,
\sup(\bigcup\mathscr{U}_{i})=1
, and such that
\bigcup\mathscr{U}_{1}
and
\bigcup\mathscr{U}_{2}
are staggered.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
U
be a Cantor complement. For every
\epsilon>0
, there exists an increasing homeomorphism
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that
\rho(f,id_{[0,1]})<\epsilon
and such that
U
and
f(U)
are staggered.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
\mathscr{U}
be the set of connected components of
U
with the induced ordering. Since
U
is a Cantor complement,
\mathscr{U}
is densely ordered and we may find a bi-infinite sequence
I_{n}=(a_{n},b_{n})\in\mathscr{U}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\inf(\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I_{n})=0
,
\sup(\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I_{n})=1
, and such that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the segment
[b_{n},a_{n+1}]
has diameter less than
\frac{\epsilon}{3}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, find
J_{n}=(c_{n},d_{n})\in\mathscr{U}
with
J\subseteq(b_{n},a_{n+1})
. We will define
f
by first setting its value on the closures of the intervals in the bi-infinite sequence

\cdots<I_{-2}<J_{-2}<I_{-1}<J_{-1}<I_{0}<J_{0}<I_{1}<J_{1}<I_{2}<J_{2}<\cdots.

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, choose a subdivision of
[a_{n},b_{n}]
as follows: find
a_{n}<D_{n-1}<A_{n}<B_{n}<C_{n}<b_{n}
where the outer four segments
[a_{n},D_{n-1}]
,
[D_{n-1},A_{n}]
,
[B_{n},C_{n}]
, and
[C_{n},b_{n}]
all have diameter less that
\frac{\epsilon}{3}
. Note that if
K_{n}=(A_{n},B_{n})
and
L_{n}=(C_{n},D_{n})
, then the bi-infinite sequence

\cdots<K_{-2}<L_{-2}<K_{-1}<L_{-1}<K_{0}<L_{0}<K_{1}<L_{1}<K_{2}<L_{2}<\cdots

limits to
0
on the left and
1
on the right. Moreover,
K_{n}\subseteq I_{n}
and
J_{n}\subseteq L_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We define
f
so that it maps
[a_{n},b_{n}]
to
[A_{n},B_{n}]
and
[c_{n},d_{n}]
to
[C_{n},D_{n}]
respectively by increasing linear maps. Moreover, since the sets
I_{n},J_{n},K_{n},L_{n}
limit to
0
as
n\to-\infty
and to
1
as
n\to\infty
, this definition extends uniquely to an increasing homeomorphism
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
, which is piecewise-linear on
[a_{-n},b_{n}]
for all
n\geq 1
(see Figure
1
).

Our choices of the sizes of the intervals
[b_{n},a_{n+1}]
and subdivisions ensures that
\rho(f,id_{[0,1]})<\epsilon
. Additionally, if
t\in(0,1)\backslash U
, then
t\in[b_{n},c_{n}]\cup[d_{n},a_{n+1}]
for some

. Since
[b_{n},c_{n}]\cup[d_{n},a_{n+1}]\subseteq L_{n}=f(J_{n})

. We conclude that

Definition 3.6
.

We say two Cantor paths
\alpha,\beta:[0,1]\to X
are
staggered
if
\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
and
\mathcal{O}(\beta)
are staggered.

Our final lemma of the section allows us to take two non-staggered Cantor paths and perturb one of them so that the resulting pair is staggered.

Lemma 3.7
.

Given any two Cantor paths
\alpha,\beta:[0,1]\to X
and
\epsilon,\delta>0
, there exist an increasing homeomorphism
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that
\rho(f,id_{[0,1]})<\delta
,
\rho(\alpha,\alpha\circ f)<\epsilon
, and such that
\alpha\circ f
and
\beta
are staggered.

Proof.

Let
U_{1}=\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
and
U_{2}=\mathcal{O}(\beta)
and note that
V=U_{1}\cap U_{2}
is also a Cantor complement. Find
0<\delta^{\prime}<\delta
such that
|s-t|<\delta^{\prime}
implies
|\alpha(s)-\alpha(t)|<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
. By Lemma
3.5
, we may find an increasing homeomorphism
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that
|f(t)-t|<\delta^{\prime}
for all
t\in[0,1]
and
V\cup f(V)=(0,1)
. Hence,
|\alpha(f(t))-\alpha(t)|<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
for all
t\in[0,1]
, which gives
\rho(f,id_{[0,1]})\leq\delta^{\prime}<\delta
and
\rho(\alpha,\alpha\circ f)<\epsilon
. Also, note that
V\cup f^{-1}(V)=(0,1)
and
\mathcal{O}(\alpha\circ f)=f^{-1}(U_{1})
. Thus

(0,1)=V\cup f^{-1}(V)=(U_{1}\cap U_{2})\cup(f^{-1}(U_{1})\cap f^{-1}(U_{2}))\subseteq U_{2}\cup\mathcal{O}(\alpha\circ f),

showing that
\alpha\circ f
and
\beta
are staggered.
∎

4.
Inserting inverse pairs into Cantor paths

If
\mathscr{U}
is a collection of open intervals in
[0,1]
with disjoint closures, then a
\mathscr{U}
-collapsing map
is a non-decreasing, continuous surjection
\mu:[0,1]\to[0,1]
, which is constant on the closure of each
J\in\mathscr{U}
and which is bijective on
[0,1]\backslash\bigcup_{j\in\mathscr{U}}\overline{J}
. Such maps may be constructed canonically using dyadic rational outputs and by enumerating
\mathscr{U}
by non-increasing length and the ordering in
[0,1]
(lexicographically). If a path
\alpha:[0,1]\to X
is not light and
k_{\alpha}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
is a
\text{lc}(\alpha)
-collapsing map, then there is a unique light path
\alpha^{\lambda}:[0,1]\to X
such that
\alpha^{\lambda}\circ k_{\alpha}=\alpha
.

Definition 4.1
.

We call a given loop
\ell:[0,1]\to X
an
inverse-pair loop
if there exists a path
\alpha:[0,1]\to X
such that
\ell\equiv\alpha\overline{\alpha}
. More specifically,

(1)

if
\alpha
is a Cantor path or has the form
\alpha\equiv\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}
for Cantor path
\alpha_{1}
and constant path
\alpha_{2}
, then, we call

If

Definition 4.3
.

Let

Remark 4.4
.

If
\alpha\preceq_{\ast}\beta
, then
\mathcal{O}(\beta)\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
. In particular, if
J\in\text{lc}(\beta)
, then either
J\in\text{lc}(\alpha)\backslash\mathscr{U}
or

for some

. Moreover, the loop
\alpha\overline{\beta}
factors through a dendrite in a highly structured way. In particular, there is a dendrite
D(\beta,\alpha)
constructed by starting with a “base arc”

and attaching an arc to
B
for each element of
\mathscr{U}
. We have
F\circ(a\overline{b})=\alpha\overline{\beta}
where

is a monotone map onto

and where
b:[0,1]\to D(\beta,\alpha)
maps
[0,1]\backslash\bigcup\mathscr{U}
into
B
and the closure of each element of
\mathscr{U}
onto the corresponding attached arc in

by an inverse-pair loop. While

holds, the relation
\alpha\leq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta
only holds in the trivial case where
\mathscr{U}=\emptyset
(see the Appendix for more details).

Remark 4.5
.

The relation
\preceq_{\ast}
on the set of paths in a space
X
is certainly reflexive and it is straightforward to check that
\preceq_{\ast}
antisymmetric. However,
\preceq_{\ast}
is
not
transitive. Rather,
\preceq_{\ast}
is a very fine relation that generates a partial order relation, which is strictly finer than
\leq_{\mathbb{R}}
.

If we have a sequence
\gamma_{1}\preceq_{\ast}\gamma_{2}\preceq_{\ast}\gamma_{3}\preceq_{\ast}\cdots
in a space
X
where, as one proceeds through the sequence, the added out-and-back loops have very quickly shrinking diameters, then
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
should converge uniformly to a path
\gamma
. Moreover,
\gamma_{1}\overline{\gamma_{2}}
factors through a dendrite, call it
D_{1}
, as described in Remark
4.4
. Since
\gamma_{n+1}
agrees with
\gamma_{n}
except on portions on which
\gamma_{n+1}
is a CIP-loop, we may recursively construct a dendrite
D_{n}
by attaching arcs to
D_{n-1}
so that
\gamma_{j}\overline{\gamma_{n+1}}
factors through
D_{n}
for each
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. In the limit, we find that there is a uniquely determined limit dendrite
D_{\infty}=\varprojlim_{n}D_{n}
that
\gamma_{n}\overline{\gamma}
factors through for all
n
. Hence,
\gamma\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The next lemma establishes this conclusion assuming the existence of the limit
\gamma
. As one can see from the above proof sketch, this result is fairly intuitive. However, a detailed proof requires careful bookkeeping of parameterizations of paths in inverse limits. We omit the details here and include them in the Appendix.

Lemma 4.6
.

If
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of Cantor paths in a metric space
(X,d)
such that
\gamma_{n}\preceq_{\ast}\gamma_{n+1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and such that
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\to\gamma
uniformly, then
\gamma_{n}\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the next section, we will be forming alternating sequences
\alpha_{1}\preceq_{\ast}\alpha_{1}^{\prime}\equiv\alpha_{2}\preceq_{\ast}\alpha_{2}^{\prime}\equiv\cdots
where the equivalences are given by small perturbations. The next lemma, which is proved using elementary real analysis, allows us to manage all of these perturbations simultaneously.

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of increasing homeomorphisms
f_{n}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that
\rho(f_{n},id_{[0,1]})\leq ar^{n}
for some
a>0
and
|r|<1
. If
g_{n}=f_{n}^{-1}\circ f_{n-1}^{-1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{2}^{-1}\circ f_{1}^{-1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges uniformly to a continuous, non-decreasing surjection
g_{\infty}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
.

Next, we combine the previous two lemmas.

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are sequences of Cantor paths in a metric space
(X,d)
,
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
is a path, and
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of increasing homeomorphisms
f_{n}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that the following hold:

(1)

\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\to\gamma
uniformly,

(2)

\alpha_{n}\preceq_{\ast}\alpha_{n}^{\prime}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3)

\alpha_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\circ f_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(4)

there exists
a>0
and
|r|<1
such that
\rho(f_{n},id_{[0,1]})\leq ar^{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then
\alpha_{n}\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
g_{0}=id_{[0,1]}
and
g_{n}=f_{n}^{-1}\circ f_{n-1}^{-1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{2}^{-1}\circ f_{1}^{-1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that
f_{n}\circ g_{n}=g_{n-1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
4.7
,
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges uniformly to a continuous, non-decreasing surjection
g_{\infty}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
.

Let
\gamma_{1}=\alpha_{1}
and for
n\geq 2
, set
\gamma_{n}=\alpha_{n}\circ g_{n-1}
. For the moment, fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\alpha_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\circ f_{n}
, we have

\gamma_{n+1}=\alpha_{n+1}\circ g_{n}=\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\circ f_{n}\circ g_{n}=\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\circ g_{n-1}.

By assumption,
\alpha_{n}\preceq_{\ast}\alpha_{n}^{\prime}
. Composing
\alpha_{n}
and
\alpha_{n}^{\prime}
with the homeomorphism
g_{n-1}
gives
\gamma_{n}\preceq_{\ast}\gamma_{n+1}
. Additionally, note that since
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\to\gamma
and
\{g_{n-1}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\to g_{\infty}
uniformly, we have
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=\{\alpha_{n}\circ g_{n-1}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\to\gamma\circ g_{\infty}
. It now follows from Lemma
4.6
that
\gamma_{n}\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma\circ g_{\infty}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus
\alpha_{n}\circ g_{n-1}\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma\circ g_{\infty}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since we have Fréchet equivalences
\alpha_{n}\,\mbox{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\raisebox{2.31895pt}{$\approx$}}$\approx$}\,\alpha_{n}\circ g_{n-1}
and
\gamma\,\mbox{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\raisebox{2.31895pt}{$\approx$}}$\approx$}\,\gamma\circ g_{\infty}
it follows from Lemma
2.4
(4) that
\alpha_{n}\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

\alpha^{\prime}
is a Cantor path. Additionally, since

is a Cantor path satisfying

The construction given in the proof of Lemma
5.3
results in two Cantor paths
\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}
, which are not staggered. Hence, to iterate this construction, we must perturb one of these two paths so that they become staggered.

Lemma 5.4
.

For given staggered Cantor paths
\alpha,\beta:[0,1]\to\mathbb{D}^{2}
with
\alpha(i)=\beta(i)
for
i\in\{0,1\}
and
\delta=\rho(\alpha,\beta)
, there exists sequences of Cantor paths
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
,
\{\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\}_{n\geq 0}
,
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, such that
\alpha_{0}=\alpha
,
\beta_{0}=\beta
and such that for all
n\geq 0
,

(1)

\alpha_{n}\preceq_{\ast}\alpha_{n}^{\prime}
and
\beta_{n}\preceq_{\ast}\beta_{n+1}
,

(2)

\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
are staggered,

(3)

\alpha_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\circ f_{n}
for some increasing homeomorphism
f_{n}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
with
\rho(f_{n},id_{[0,1]})<\frac{1}{2^{n}}
,

(4)

\max\{\rho(\alpha_{n}^{\prime},\alpha_{n+1}),\rho(\alpha_{n},\alpha_{n+1}),\rho(\beta_{n},\beta_{n+1}),\rho(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})\}\leq\delta\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{0}=\alpha
,
\beta_{0}=\beta
, and
\delta=\rho(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
. Suppose
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
are given staggered Cantor paths that satisfy
\rho(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})\leq\delta\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n}
. Applying Lemma
5.3
, find Cantor paths
\alpha_{n}^{\prime},\beta_{n}^{\prime}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{D}^{2}
such that
\alpha_{n}\preceq_{\ast}\alpha_{n}^{\prime}
,
\beta_{n}\preceq_{\ast}\beta_{n}^{\prime}
,
\max\{\rho(\alpha_{n},\alpha_{n}^{\prime}),\rho(\beta_{n},\beta_{n}^{\prime})\}\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
, and
\rho(\alpha_{n}^{\prime},\beta_{n}^{\prime})\leq\frac{2\delta}{3}
. By Lemma
3.7
, there exists an increasing homeomorphism
f_{n}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that
\rho(f_{n},id_{[0,1]})<\frac{1}{2^{n}}
,
\rho(\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\circ f_{n},\alpha_{n}^{\prime})<\frac{1}{12}\rho(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
, and such that
\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\circ f_{n}
and
\beta_{n}^{\prime}
are staggered. Set
\alpha_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\circ f_{n}
and
\beta_{n+1}=\beta_{n}^{\prime}
. Then
\alpha_{n+1}
and
\beta_{n+1}
are staggered Cantor paths and satisfy the following inequalities:

\rho(\alpha_{n}^{\prime},\alpha_{n+1})<\frac{1}{12}\rho(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})\leq\frac{\delta}{12}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n}\leq\delta\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n+1}

and

\displaystyle\rho(\alpha_{n},\alpha_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho(\alpha_{n},\alpha_{n}^{\prime})+\rho(\alpha_{n}^{\prime},\alpha_{n+1})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\rho(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})+\frac{1}{12}\rho(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\rho(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})\,\leq\,\delta\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n+1}

and

\rho(\beta_{n},\beta_{n+1})\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})\leq\frac{3}{4}\rho(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})\leq\delta\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n+1}

and

\displaystyle\rho(\alpha_{n+1},\beta_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho(\alpha_{n+1},\alpha_{n}^{\prime})+\rho(\alpha_{n}^{\prime},\beta_{n}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}\rho(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})+\frac{2}{3}\rho(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\rho(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})\,\leq\,\delta\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n+1}.

This completes the inductive construction of the desired sequences.
∎

In the next two statements, we assume
\alpha,\beta
are fixed staggered Cantor paths as given in the hypothesis of Lemma

Proposition 5.5
.

The sequences
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
constructed in the proof of Lemma

both converge uniformly to a single path
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{D}^{2}

Proof.

Recall that
\delta=\rho(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
is fixed. Since
\rho(\alpha_{n},\alpha_{n+1})\leq\delta\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{n}
for all
n\geq 0
,
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is Cauchy in the
\sup
-metric and, therefore, converges uniformly to some path
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{D}^{2}
. Since the sequence
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the same inequality,
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
also converges uniformly to some path. Additionally, since
\rho(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})\leq\delta(\frac{3}{4})^{n}
for all

,
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
must both converge uniformly to
\gamma
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

If
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{D}^{2}
is the uniform limit of the sequences

and
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
as given in the conclusion of Proposition

\alpha\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma
and
\beta\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma
.

Proof.

For given paths
\alpha
and
\beta
, Lemma
5.4
gives sequences

,
\{\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\}_{n\geq 0}
,
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, and
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfying a variety of relations and inequalities. Proposition
5.5
ensures
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
converge uniformly to a path

. The sequences
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
,

. Similarly, we may apply Lemma
4.8
to the sequence

in the case where

and

\beta=\beta_{0}\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma
.
∎

Finally, we complete the proof of our main result.

Proof of Main Theorem.

First, we prove a single case in
\mathbb{D}^{2}
. Respectively, let

and

be the injective paths in
\mathbb{D}^{2}
from

Corollary 5.7
.

The equivalence relation on the set of paths in a given topological space generated by
\geq_{\mathbb{R}}
(and

If

A Solution to Dydak’s Problem

We conclude with a proof of Corollary
1.1
. First, we note the following lemma, which is proved using standard techniques from covering space theory.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
p:(E,e_{0})\to(X,x_{0})
be a based map with unique lifting of paths rel. starting point and suppose
T
is an
\mathbb{R}
-tree. If
f:(T,t_{0})\to(X,x_{0})
is a based map, then there exists a unique based map
\widetilde{f}:(T,t_{0})\to(E,e_{0})
such that
p\circ\widetilde{f}=f
.

Proof of Corollary
1.1
.

Let
p:E\to X
be a map where
X
is first countable, locally path connected and simply connected and such that every path in
X
has a unique lift in
E
rel. starting point. Since
X
is path connected,
p
is surjective. Suppose that
p(e_{1})=p(e_{2})=x
for
e_{1},e_{2}\in E
. Let
\widetilde{\beta}:[0,1]\to E
be a path from
e_{1}
to
e_{2}
. Then
\beta=p\circ\widetilde{\beta}
is a loop based at
x
. Let
\alpha:[0,1]\to X
be the constant path at
x
. Since
X
is simply connected,
\alpha\simeq\beta
and thus, by our Main Theorem, there exists a path
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
such that
\gamma\geq_{\mathbb{R}}\alpha
and
\gamma\geq_{\mathbb{R}}\beta
. Let
\widetilde{\alpha},\widetilde{\gamma}:([0,1],0)\to(E,e_{1})
be the lifts of
\alpha
and
\gamma
respectively and note that
\widetilde{\alpha}
is constant at
e_{1}
. Since
\alpha\overline{\gamma}
factors through an
\mathbb{R}
-tree, it follows from Lemma

that
\widetilde{\alpha}(1)=\widetilde{\gamma}(1)
in
E
. Similarly, we have
\widetilde{\beta}(1)=\widetilde{\gamma}(1)
. Thus
e_{1}=\widetilde{\gamma}(1)=e_{2}
, proving that
p
is injective. Since
X
is first countable, it suffices to show

preserves convergent sequences. If

, the hypotheses on

allow us to find a path
\alpha:[0,1]\to X
with

t\in[0,1]
. Since
\{Q_{\infty}(t_{n_{m}})\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}=\{x_{n_{m}}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\to x
and
\{Q_{\infty}(t_{n_{m}})\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\to Q_{\infty}(t)
, it follows that
Q_{\infty}(t)=x
. Thus
Q_{\infty}
is surjective.

Knowing that
Q_{\infty}\circ k_{\infty}
is surjective, we now check that
\gamma
is constant on each fiber of
Q_{\infty}\circ k_{\infty}
. Suppose
a,b\in[0,1]
such that
Q_{\infty}\circ k_{\infty}(a)=Q_{\infty}\circ k_{\infty}(b)
. Then
Q_{n}\circ k_{n}(a)=Q_{n}\circ k_{n}(b)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Applying
H_{n}
gives

\gamma_{n}(a)=H_{n}\circ Q_{n}\circ k_{n}(a)=H_{n}\circ Q_{n}\circ k_{n}(b)=\gamma_{n}(b)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\{\gamma_{n}\}\to\gamma
uniformly, we have
\{\gamma_{n}(a)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\to\gamma(a)
and
\{\gamma_{n}(b)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\to\gamma(b)
but since these sequences in
X
are equal, it follows that
\gamma(a)=\gamma(b)
. This completes the check and so we conclude that the desired map
H_{\infty}
exists.

With the existence of
H_{\infty}
confirmed, we fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
and check that the equality
H_{m}=H_{\infty}\circ i_{m}
holds. Since
\{H_{n}\circ R_{n}\circ Q_{\infty}\circ k_{\infty}\}=\{\gamma_{n}\}\to\gamma=H_{\infty}\circ Q_{\infty}\circ k_{\infty}
uniformly where
Q_{\infty}\circ k_{\infty}
, we have that
\{H_{n}\circ R_{n}\}\to H_{\infty}
uniformly. Recalling that
m
is fixed, we have
\{H_{n}\circ R_{n}\circ i_{m}\}_{n>m}\to H_{\infty}\circ i_{m}
uniformly. However,
\{H_{n}\circ R_{n}\circ i_{m}\}_{n>m}=\{H_{n}\circ i_{m,n}\}_{n>m}=\{H_{m}\}_{n>m}
is the constant sequence at
H_{m}
. Thus
H_{m}=H_{\infty}\circ i_{m}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

For
t\in\{0,1\}
, set
x_{n,t}=Q_{n}\circ k_{\gamma_{n}}(t)
. Since
R_{n+1,n}(x_{n+1,t})=x_{n,t}
, we have
Q_{\infty}\circ K_{\infty}(t)=(x_{n,t})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. However, recall that our inductive construction ensures that
i_{n,m}(x_{n,t})=x_{m,t}
whenever
m>n
. Thus in the limit, we also have
i_{m}(x_{m,t})=(x_{n,t})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. We conclude that
Q_{\infty}\circ k_{\infty}(t)=i_{n}\circ Q_{n}\circ k_{n}(t)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in\{0,1\}
. Therefore, if we set
g_{n,1}=Q_{\infty}\circ K_{\infty}
and
g_{n,2}=i_{n}\circ Q_{n}\circ k_{n}
, the concatenation
g_{n,1}\overline{g_{n,2}}
is a well-defined loop in
D_{\infty}
. Moreover,
H_{\infty}\circ g_{n,1}=H_{\infty}\circ Q_{\infty}\circ K_{\infty}=\gamma
and
H_{\infty}\circ g_{n,2}=H_{\infty}\circ i_{n}\circ Q_{n}\circ k_{n}=H_{n}\circ Q_{n}\circ k_{n}=\gamma_{n}
. Thus
H_{\infty}\circ(g_{n,1}\overline{g_{n,2}})=\gamma\overline{\gamma_{n}}
, proving
\gamma\simeq_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma_{n}
.
∎

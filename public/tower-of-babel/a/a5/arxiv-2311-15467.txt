Characterization of Lipschitz Normally Embedded complex curves
André Costa, Vincent Grandjean, Maria Michalska

Abstract
. The main result of the paper states that a connected complex affine algebraic curve is Lipschitz normally embedded (shortened to LNE afterwards) in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} if and only if its germ at any singular point is a finite union of non-singular complex curve germs which are pairwise transverse, and its projective closure is in general position with the hyperplane at infinity. To this aim, we completely characterize complex analytic curves of a compact complex manifold which are LNE (regardless of the given Riemannian structure), and therefore we can relate when a projective algebraic curve is LNE in ℂ ​ ℙ n \mathbb{CP}^{n} with the property of its general affine traces being LNE in ℂ n {\mathbb{C}^{n}} . This allows us to deduce that Lipschitz classification of LNE curves is topological. We describe a complete invariant for the (outer and inner) Lipschitz equivalence of affine LNE curves and show that most such curves cannot be bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic to a plane one.

1.
Introduction

A Riemannian manifold
(M,g_{M})

carries a natural metric space structure
(M,d_{M})
.
Any subset
S
inherits the
outer metric space
structure
(S,d_{S})
where the distance
d_{S}
between points of
S
is
their distance in
(M,d_{M})
. When
S
is path-connected,
the infimum of the lengths of all the rectifiable curves in
S
connecting
two given points of
S
is called the inner distance between these
points, yielding the inner metric space structure
(S,d_{\rm inn}^{S})
over
S
. While investigating
C^{\infty}
functions over closed subsets of
the Euclidean space, Whitney observed in
[
23
,
24
]

that the metric spaces
(S,d_{S})
and
(S,d_{\rm inn}^{S})
need not be
metrically equivalent, thus introduced the property: there exists a positive
constant
L
such that

(P)\hfill{\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime}\in S\;\Longrightarrow\;d_{\rm inn}^{S}({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime})\;\leq\;L\cdot d_{S}({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime}),\hfill

which guarantees that
the metric spaces
(S,d_{S})
and

(S,d_{\rm inn}^{S})
are equivalent, since
d_{S}\leq d_{\rm inn}^{S}
.

The equivalence of inner and outer distances
relates strongly with Lipschitz
Stratifications, L-regular decompositions and Pancakes of sub-analytic sets
of Euclidean spaces
[
15
,
17
,
12
,
18
]
.
Property
(P)
is called quasi-convexity in the fields of Lipschitz
Analysis and Metric Geometry, while to Singularity Theorists, it
is known as being Lipschitz Normally Embedded (shortened below to LNE) since
[
1
]
.

The last decade has shown growing interest in investigating analytic subset
germs
(X,{\bf 0})
admitting LNE representatives.
Still, outside the case of complex curve germs and isolated complex surface
singularity germs,
the higher dimensional situation is a terra incognita yet to be
explored:
the recent short survey
[
6
]
and the paper
[
13
]
present a
rather exhaustive list of what is known on this topic. Similarly, asking
when an algebraic set is LNE nearby infinity has been addressed in a single
paper, under very restrictive hypotheses
[
8
]
, which recently gave
rise to a generalization to necessary condition on tangent cones in

[
4
]
.
Moreover, very little
is known on how to recognize which affine algebraic subsets of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
are
LNE, both when
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
.
The only non-trivial example we are aware of is from
[
11
]
: it has
non-isolated singularities, but is a
\mathbb{K}
-cone, thus essentially a
projective result. Let us mention that the new article
[
21
]
describes
fully
the Lipschitz outer geometry of complex plane algebraic curves
by a complete finite combinatorial invariant. Last,
[
9
]
gives a
complete
description of quasi-convex subsets of the real plane, definable in some
o-minimal structure. Still, neither of these two works characterizes LNE
curves.

The goal of this paper is to start the investigation of the LNE property
for subsets of the Euclidean space or of a given
compact Riemannian manifold. We treat the case of complex curves in
both settings: we fully characterize which complex algebraic curves of

{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
are LNE and which complex analytic curves of a compact complex
manifold are LNE (regardless of the Riemannian metric used).

The main result of this paper is the following:

Theorem
8.1
.
Let
X
be a complex algebraic curve of

{\mathbb{C}^{n}}

of positive degree
d
. It is LNE if and only if the following conditions
are satisfied:
(0)
X
is connected; (i) At each singular point
{\bf x}_{0}
of
X
, the germ

(X,{\bf x}_{0})
consists
of finitely many pairwise transverse non-singular complex
algebraic curve germs;
(ii) The intersection of the projective
closure of
X

in
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
with the hyperplane at infinity consists exactly of

d
points.

Prior to establishing
the affine result, and as a consequence of only local considerations,
we obtain the following result in complex compact manifolds:

Proposition
4.6
.
Let
X
be a complex analytic
curve of a complex compact manifold. It is LNE if and only if
the following conditions are satisfied:
(0)
X
is connected; (i) At each singular point
{\bf x}_{0}
of
X
, the
germ
(X,{\bf x}_{0})

consists of finitely many pairwise transverse non-singular complex analytic
curve germs.

Both above results require the same condition at singular points, but the
fundamental difference between the two lies of course in what happens at
infinity, situation whose treatment will occupy half of the paper.
These results leads to the very close interplay between some
metric properties of a complex projective algebraic curve with those of its
general affine traces.

Theorem
9.4
.

Let
X
be an algebraic curve of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
. The following statements
are equivalent:

(1)
X
is LNE w.r.t. to the Fubini-Study metric.

(2) There exists a hyperplane
H
of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
intersecting
X

only at non-singular points of
X
at which
X
and
H
are transverse
and such that the affine trace
X\setminus H
of
X
in
\mathbb{C}_{H}^{n}:=\mathbb{CP}^{n}\setminus H
is LNE in
\mathbb{C}_{H}^{n}
.

(3)
For any hyperplane
H
of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
intersecting
X

only at non-singular points of
X
at which
X
and
H
are transverse, the
affine trace
X\setminus H
is LNE in
\mathbb{C}_{H}^{n}
.

This latter result allows us to describe a complete invariant of (outer and inner) Lipschitz equivalence which simply encodes degree, number of singular
points, genus and multiplicities at singular points. From this, we deduce the following result:

Theorem
10.2
.

Let
X_{1}
be an irreducible LNE complex algebraic curve of

\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}}
and
X_{2}
be an irreducible LNE complex algebraic curve of
\mathbb{C}^{n_{2}}
. Then

X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic if and only if they are
homeomorphic.

Lipschitz equivalence has been extensively studied for germs of analytic
curves. In dimension
n_{1}=n_{2}=2
, two curves are outer Lipschitz
equivalent if and only if there
exists a homeomorphism germ
(\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}},{\bf 0})\to(\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}},{\bf 0})
mapping

(X_{1},{\bf 0})
to
(X_{2},{\bf 0})
, see
[
19
]
. Therefore, a complete
outer Lipschitz equivalence invariant of plane curves was introduced
recently in
[
21
]
. As shown in
[
7
]
, the topological and outer Lipschitz classification are no longer the same for
space curve germs, i.e.
n_{1}=n_{2}\geq 3
. Furthermore, by the result

[
22
]
, a complex space curve germ is outer Lipschitz equivalent to
the germ of one of its generic linear plane projections.
Corollary
10.5
establishes that this is no longer true for
global curves.

,
at the end of Section

. The next three sections develop the

in Section

: Sections

2.
Geometric Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{K}
be
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. The space
{\mathbb{K}^{n}}
is equipped with the Euclidean
distance denoted by
|-|
.
Let
eucl
be the Euclidean/Hermitian metric tensor over
{\mathbb{K}^{n}}
.

Let
{\bf x}
be a point of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
. Let
r
be a positive number.
The open ball of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
centred at
{\bf x}
with radius
r
is

B_{r}^{n_{\mathbb{K}}}({\bf x})
,
for
n_{\mathbb{K}}:=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{K}^{n}}
.
Its closure is
{\bf B}_{r}^{n_{\mathbb{K}}}({\bf x})
. The Euclidean sphere of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
centred
at
{\bf x}
and of radius
r
is
{\bf S}_{r}^{n_{\mathbb{K}}-1}({\bf x})
. When
{\bf x}
is the origin

{\bf 0}
of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
, we will simply write
B_{r}^{n_{\mathbb{K}}}
,
{\bf B}_{r}^{n_{\mathbb{K}}}
and

{\bf S}_{r}^{n_{\mathbb{K}}-1}
.

Convention 1
.

A Riemannian manifold
(M,g_{M})
consists of a
C^{k}

or
\mathbb{K}
-analytic manifold
M
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\cup\infty
,
of positive
\mathbb{K}
-dimension
m
, and of a continuous Riemannian metric

g_{M}
on
M
.

Let
(M,g_{M})
be a Riemannian manifold. Thus
g_{M}
induces the
distance
d_{M}
on
M
defined as the infimum of the lengths of rectifiable
curves connecting any given pair of points.

Any subset
S
of
M
admits two natural metric space structures on
S

inherited from
(M,g_{M})
:

Definition 2.1
.

1) The outer distance on
S
is the distance function
d_{S}
on

S\times S
obtained by restricting
d_{M}
to
S\times S
.

2) The inner distance on
S
is the function

d_{\rm inn}^{S}:S\times S\to[0,+\infty]

such that given
{\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime}\in S
, the number
d_{\rm inn}^{S}({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime})
is the infimum of the lengths
of the rectifiable paths lying in
S
joining
{\bf s}
and
{\bf s}^{\prime}
. If there is no such a path, then
d_{\rm inn}^{S}({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime})=\infty
.

Observe that
d_{S}\leq d_{\rm inn}^{S}
. We recall the following notion.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X,d_{i})
, where
i=1,2
, be two metric space structures over the
space
X
. The metric space structures
(X,d_{1})
and
(X,d_{2})
are equivalent if
there exists a constant
L>1
such that

\displaystyle{{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}\in X\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{1}{L}\cdot d_{1}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\;\leq\;d_{2}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\;\leq\;L\cdot d_{1}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}).}

An elementary question arising from presenting these two definitions is
when are the metric spaces
(S,d_{S})
and
(S,d_{\rm inn}^{S})
equivalent?
This motivates the next

Definition 2.3
.

A subset
S
of the Riemannian manifold
(M,g_{M})
is Lipschitz normally
embedded (shortened to LNE) if the identity mapping
(S,d_{S})\to(S,d_{\rm inn}^{S})
is bi-Lipschitz: there exists a positive constant
L
such
that

{\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime}\;\in\;S\;\Longrightarrow\;d_{\rm inn}^{S}({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime})\;\leq\;L\cdot d_{S}({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime}).

Any constant
L
satisfying the previous inequality is called a LNE
constant of
S
.

We will simply use the expression LNE, but it must never be forgotten
that it is with respect to the metric structure given by the Riemannian
metric
g_{M}
on
M
.

Remark 2.4
.

(i) The metric space structure
(M,d_{M})
associated with the Riemannian
manifold
(M,g_{M})
is LNE, with LNE constant
1
. Moreover each chart is
locally bi-Lipschitz.

(ii) Since any two continuous Riemannian metrics on a
C^{1}
compact
Riemannian manifold
M
yield equivalent metric space structures,
the property of being LNE depends only on the
C^{1}
differentiable structure.
Moreover, following Sullivan’s result

[
20
]
, a compact topological manifold of dimension
different from
4
has
a unique metric space structure, up to equivalence.
Therefore the property of being LNE in a compact
Riemannian manifold of dimension which is not 4 depends only on the ambient topology.

Finding a local characterisation of a property is usually a first step to
understand it globally, but the property being LNE does not pass to germs: the open ball
B_{r}^{2}
centred at
{\bf 0}

of positive radius
r
is a
LNE representative of the germ
(\mathbb{R}^{2},{\bf 0})
, while
\{(x,y):y^{2}-(x-r)^{3}>0\}\cap B_{2r}^{2}
is a non-LNE one.

We introduce
the following notion (see also
[
11
]
),
that will be central to the paper.

Definition 2.5
.

1) A subset
S
of the Riemannian manifold
(M,g_{M})
is
locally LNE at
the point
{\bf s}
of
{\rm clos}(S)
,
the closure of
S
in
M
, if there
exists an open neighbourhood
\mathcal{U}
of
{\bf s}
in
M
such
that
S\cap\mathcal{U}
is LNE.

2) The subset
S
is locally LNE if it is locally LNE at each
point of
{\rm clos}(S)
.

The next Lemma
generalizes in our setting the Euclidean result
[
11
, Proposition
2.4]
. It
presents a simple criterion for a compact subset of
(M,g_{M})
to be LNE.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
S
be a compact connected subset of the Riemannian manifold
(M,g_{M})
.
It is LNE if and only if it is locally LNE.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
11
]
.
When
S
is LNE, taking
\mathcal{U}=M
yields that
S
is locally LNE.

For each point
{\bf s}
of
S
, let
\mathcal{U}_{\bf s}
be a neighbourhood of
{\bf s}
in

M
such that
U_{\bf s}:=S\cap\mathcal{U}_{\bf s}
is LNE. Thus
S
is covered by

U_{1},\ldots,U_{N},
where
U_{i}:=U_{{\bf s}_{i}}
for some points

{\bf s}_{1},\ldots,{\bf s}_{N}
of
S
.
Let
L_{0}
be a LNE constant of
U_{1},\ldots,U_{N}
.
Consider the following function

f:S\times S\setminus\Delta\to\mathbb{R},\;\;({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime})\mapsto\frac{d_{\rm inn}^{S}({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime})}{d_{S}({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime})},\;\;{\rm where}\;\;\Delta:=\{({\bf s},{\bf s})\in S\times S\}.

Note that
f
is bounded below by
1
. Let
V:=\cup_{i=1}^{N}U_{i}\times U_{i}

which is an open neighbourhood of
\Delta
in
S\times S
. Observe that it is
connected.
Since
f
is continuous and
S\times S\setminus V
is compact it admits a
maximum
L_{1}
. Let
({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime})
in
V\setminus\Delta
, thus there exists
i

such that

({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime})\in U_{i}\times U_{i}
. Therefore
f({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime})\leq L_{0}
.
Thus
S
is LNE with LNE constant
\max(L_{0},L_{1})
.
∎

We recall that a subset
S
of a
C^{k}
manifold
M
,
k\geq 1
, is a
C^{1}
sub-manifold (possibly with boundary) of
M
if: i)
S
is a
C^{1}

manifold (possibly with boundary); ii) the manifold topology coincides
with the subspace topology; iii) the inclusion mapping

S\hookrightarrow M
is a
C^{1}
injective immersion.
As a corollary of the previous criterion we obtain the following result that
we will use extensively in the paper.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
N
be a
C^{1}
compact connected sub-manifold of the Riemannian manifold

(M,g_{M})
possibly with
C^{1}
boundary. Then
N
is LNE.

Proof.

Since
N
is connected we can assume that
M
is also connected. Let
m,n

be the respective dimensions of
M,N
. By Lemma
2.6
, it suffices to show that
N
is locally LNE.

\bullet

Assume that
(M,g_{M})=(\mathbb{R}^{m},eucl)
and
n=m
. For each
{\bf x}
in
N\setminus{\partial}N
, there exists a positive radius
r

such that
{\bf B}_{r}^{m}({\bf x})
is contained in
N
. Since the ball is convex, it is
LNE, thus
N
is locally LNE at
{\bf x}
. Let
{\bf x}\in{\partial}N
. There exists
a positive radius
r
and a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism

\phi_{\bf x}^{\partial}:N\cap B_{r}^{m}({\bf x})\to B_{2r}^{m-1}\times[0,2),

mapping
{\partial}N\cap B_{r}^{m}({\bf x})
onto
B_{2}^{m-1}\times 0
.
Up to shrinking

we can assume that
\phi_{\bf x}^{\partial}
is bi-Lipschitz: there
exists a positive constant
A
such that

{\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime}\in N\cap B_{r}^{m}({\bf x})\;\Longrightarrow\;|{\bf y}-{\bf y}^{\prime}|\leq A\cdot|\phi_{\bf x}^{\partial}({\bf y})-\phi_{\bf x}^{\partial}({\bf y}^{\prime})|\;\leq\;A^{2}\cdot|{\bf y}-{\bf y}^{\prime}|.

Since
B_{2r}^{m-1}\times[0,2)
is convex in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
it is LNE, thus

N\cap B_{r}^{m}({\bf x})
is also LNE. Therefore,

is locally LNE at each of its points.

\bullet
Assume that
(M,g_{M})=(\mathbb{R}^{m},eucl)
and
n<m
. For each
{\bf x}
in
N
, let
T_{\bf x}
be the tangent space of

at
{\bf x}
and
let
\pi_{\bf x}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to T_{\bf x}
be the orthogonal projection onto

.
There exists a positive radius
r
such that
p_{\bf x}
, the restriction of

\pi_{\bf x}
to

is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism onto its image, and
further we find

{\bf y},{\bf y}^{\prime}\in N\cap B_{r}^{m}({\bf x})\;\Longrightarrow\;|{\bf y}-{\bf y}^{\prime}|\geq|p_{\bf x}({\bf y})-p_{\bf x}({\bf y}^{\prime})|\;\geq\;\frac{1}{2}|{\bf y}-{\bf y}^{\prime}|.

Let
Z:=\pi_{\bf x}(N)
. Therefore
(Z,{\bf x})
is a
C^{1}
sub-manifold possibly with boundary
of
T_{\bf x}
and thus
Z
is locally LNE at
{\bf x}
w.r.t. the Euclidean metric
of
T_{\bf x}
by the same argument as in the previous case. Since
T_{\bf x}
is still an euclidean space
for the restriction of the Euclidean metric of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, there exists a
positive radius
r^{\prime}
such that
Z\cap B_{r^{\prime}}^{m}({\bf x})
is LNE since

T_{\bf x}\cap B_{r^{\prime}}^{m}({\bf x}^{\prime})
is an Euclidean ball of

of radius

.
Since
p_{\bf x}
is bi-Lipschitz we deduce that
p_{\bf x}^{-1}(Z\cap B_{r^{\prime}}^{m}({\bf x}))
is LNE whenever

.

We conclude that any sub-manifold of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is locally LNE at each of its
points.

\bullet
General Case. For each
{\bf x}
in
N
, let
\Phi_{\bf x}:\mathcal{V}_{\bf x}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}

In the Euclidean context, the following result complements
Corollary
2.7

Lemma 2.8

Let
N

C^{1}
sub-manifold with

We will treat the case

Let

Let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s},
be the connected components of
{\partial}E

{\delta}:=\min_{1\leq i<j\leq s}{\rm dist}(B_{i},B_{j}),

{\delta}=\infty
if
{\partial}E
is connected.

Let
{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}
be two distinct points of
E
, and let
I
be the segment of

{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
with end points
{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}
. The segment
I
is oriented from
{\bf x}
to
{\bf x}^{\prime}
, thus
is totally ordered. When
I
intersects with
{\partial}E
, we can assume without loss of
generality that
{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}
are both in
{\partial}E
.
Since
{\delta}
is positive, there exists finitely many
open intervals
J^{k}=({\bf x}_{k}^{1},{\bf x}_{k+1}^{0})
of
I
,
for

, with the following properties:

{\bf x}={\bf x}_{1}^{0}\leq{\bf x}_{1}^{1}<{\bf x}_{2}^{0}\leq\ldots<{\bf x}_{p}^{0}\leq{\bf x}_{p}^{1}={\bf x}^{\prime}
;

ii)

For each

{\bf x}_{k}^{0},{\bf x}_{k}^{1}\in B_{i_{k}}
;

iii)

For each

;

iv)

for each

Observe that each connected component of
the compact set

is contained in one of the intervals
[{\bf x}_{k}^{0},{\bf x}_{k}^{1}]
,
where
k=1,\ldots,p
.

Since each connected component of
{\partial}E
is LNE by Corollary

2.7
, let
A
be a common LNE constant. For each

k=1,\ldots,p
, we find

d_{\rm inn}^{E}({\bf x}_{k}^{0},{\bf x}_{k}^{1})\leq d_{\rm inn}^{B_{i_{k}}}({\bf x}_{k}^{0},{\bf x}_{k}^{1})\leq A\,|{\bf x}_{k}^{0}-{\bf x}_{k}^{1}|.

Since the segment
J^{k}
is contained in
E
, we find

d_{\rm inn}^{E}({\bf x}_{k}^{1},{\bf x}_{k+1}^{0})=|{\bf x}_{k}^{1}-{\bf x}_{k+1}^{0}|\leq A\,|{\bf x}_{k}^{1}-{\bf x}_{k+1}^{0}|.

We deduce

d_{\rm inn}^{E}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{p}d_{\rm inn}^{E}({\bf x}_{k}^{0},{\bf x}_{k}^{1})+\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}d_{\rm inn}^{E}({\bf x}_{k}^{1},{\bf x}_{k+1}^{0})\leq A|{\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}|,

and the lemma is proved.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

The analogue of Lemma
2.8
is also true when the ambient
metric space is a connected Riemannian manifold
(M,g_{M})
.
The proof of this more general case goes
through similarly, using estimates of curves of length close enough to
the outer/inner distance.
Since we only use such a result in the Euclidean context, we
presented the statement and proof in this simpler setting.

Let
(M,g_{M})
and
(P,g_{P})
be Riemannian manifolds.
The manifold
M\times P
is thus equipped
with the Riemannian product metric
g_{M\times P}:=g_{M}\otimes g_{P}
,
whose associated distance function is
d_{M}+d_{P}
.
Given a subset
S
of
(M,g_{M})
, we can speak of a Lipschitz mapping

F:(S,d_{S})\to(P,d_{P})
with Lipschitz constant
K_{F}
:

{\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime}\;\in\;S\;\Longrightarrow\;d_{P}(F({\bf s}),F({\bf s}^{\prime}))\;\leq\;K_{F}\cdot d_{S}({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime})=K_{F}\cdot d_{M}({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime}).

The space
S\times P
comes naturally equipped with the product distance

d_{S\times P}:=d_{S}+d_{P}
. We conclude this section with the following very
useful, though simple, result.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
S
be a LNE subset of
(M,g_{M})
. If
F:(S,d_{S})\to(P,d_{P})
is a Lipschitz mapping, then its graph is LNE in
(M\times P,d_{M\times P})
.

Proof.

Since
F
is Lipschitz there exists a positive constant
K_{F}
such that

{\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime}\;\in\;S\;\Longrightarrow\;d_{P}(F({\bf s}),F({\bf s}^{\prime}))\;\leq\;K_{F}\cdot d_{S}({\bf s},{\bf s}^{\prime}).

Let
{\gamma}:[0,1]\to S
be a rectifiable curve and let
{\gamma}_{F}:[0,1]\to S\times P
be the lift of
{\gamma}
to
G_{F}
, the graph of
F
. The result follows from
the following estimate

{\rm length}_{M\times P}({\gamma}_{F})\;\leq(1+K_{F})\cdot{\rm length}_{M}({\gamma})

where
{\rm length}_{Q}
is the length taken w.r.t. to the Riemannian metric

g_{Q}
, for
Q=M,\,M\times P
.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

When
M=\mathbb{R}^{m},P={\mathbb{R}^{p}}
, their product distance
d_{M}+d_{P}

is not the Euclidean distance of
\mathbb{R}^{m+p}
, but it is equivalent to the
Euclidean distance of
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
, and this is how we will use Proposition

2.10
in the complex affine context.

3.
A criterion for isolated singularity germs

Let
{\bf 0}
be the origin of the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. If
S
is any subset
of
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
containing the origin, let
S^{*}
be
S\setminus{\bf 0}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(N_{1},{\bf 0}),\ldots,(N_{s},{\bf 0}),
be germs at
{\bf 0}
of
C^{1}
sub-manifolds
of
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
of positive dimensions and codimensions. Assume that
(Y,{\bf 0}):=\cup_{j=1}^{s}(N_{j},{\bf 0})
has an isolated singularity at
{\bf 0}
.

The germ
(Y,{\bf 0})
is locally LNE at
{\bf 0}
if and only if
T_{\bf 0}N_{j}\cap T_{\bf 0}N_{k}=\{{\bf 0}\}
for each
1\leq j<k\leq s
.

Proof.

For each
j=1,\ldots,s,
let
T_{j}:=T_{\bf 0}N_{j}
and let
T_{j}^{\perp}
be the
orthogonal supplement of
T_{j}
. Since each
(N_{j},{\bf 0})
is a
C^{1}

sub-manifold, there exists a positive radius
r_{Y}>0
, and a
C^{1}
-mapping

G_{j}:T_{j}\cap B_{2r_{Y}}^{n}\to T_{j}^{\perp}

such that a representative of
(N_{j},{\bf 0})
is the graph
\Gamma_{j}
of
G_{j}
.
Since
D_{\bf 0}G_{j}={\bf 0}
and
G
is
C^{1}
, up to shrinking
r_{Y}
the mapping
G_{j}
is
Lipschitz over
{\bf B}_{r}^{n}
for any radius
0<r\leq r_{Y}
, with Lipschitz
constant tending to
0
as
r
goes to
0
. For
j=1,\ldots,s,
we define

N_{j}^{{\leq r}}:={\Gamma}_{j}\cap{\bf B}_{r}^{n}.

Whenever
r_{Y}
is small enough, we can further assume that
r_{Y}
is such
that
N_{j}^{{\leq r}}
is LNE for any
r\leq r_{Y}
, since they are connected compact

C^{1}
sub-manifolds with boundaries

{\partial}N_{j}^{{\leq r}}={\Gamma}_{j}\cap{\bf S}_{r}^{n-1}.

Each
{\partial}N_{j}^{{\leq r}}
is diffeomorphic to
{\bf S}^{\dim T_{j}-1}
.

Given a pair
1\leq j<k\leq s
, and
r\leq r_{Y}
, let
{\bf x}_{l}\in(N_{l}^{{\leq r}})^{*}
for
l=j,k
. Let
r_{l}>0
be defined as follows:

r_{l}:=|{\bf x}_{l}|.

Let
2{\alpha}\in[0,\pi]
be the (non-oriented) angle between
{\bf x}_{j}
and

{\bf x}_{k}
. From plane geometry classes we recall that

\left|\frac{{\bf x}_{j}}{r_{j}}-\frac{{\bf x}_{k}}{r_{k}}\right|=2\sin{\alpha}

and further remember that

(3.1)

|{\bf x}_{j}-{\bf x}_{k}|^{2}=(r_{j}-r_{k})^{2}\cos^{2}{\alpha}+(r_{j}+r_{k})^{2}\sin^{2}{\alpha}.

By definition of
{\alpha}
, we deduce that

(3.2)

(r_{j}+r_{k})\sin{\alpha}\;\leq\;|{\bf x}_{j}-{\bf x}_{k}|\;\leq\;r_{j}+r_{k}.

Finally let
Y_{{\leq r}}:=Y\cap{\bf B}_{r}^{2n}
,
d_{\rm inn}^{{\leq r}}:=d_{\rm inn}^{Y_{{\leq r}}}

and
d_{j}^{{\leq r}}:=d_{\rm inn}^{N_{j}^{{\leq r}}}
, for
1\leq j\leq s
.

\bullet

Assume that
T_{j}\cap T_{k}={\bf 0}
, for each pair
j\neq k
.

For
j=1,\ldots,s,
we define the link of
T_{j}
at
{\bf 0}

S_{j}:=T_{j}\cap{\bf S}_{1}^{n-1}.

By hypothesis, we get

(3.3)

{\delta}:=\min_{1\leq j<k\leq w}{\rm dist}(S_{j},S_{k})>0.

We can require that
r_{Y}
is
small enough such that for each
j=1,\ldots,s,
and each
{\bf x}_{j}\in(N_{j}^{\leq r_{Y}})^{*}
the followings estimates hold true

(3.4)

{\rm dist}\left(S_{j},\frac{{\bf x}_{j}}{r_{j}}\right)\;\leq\;\frac{{\delta}}{4}\cdot

For each
1\leq j<k\leq s
, each
{\bf x}_{j}\in N_{j}^{*}
,
{\bf x}_{k}\in N_{k}^{*}
,
we also find the following estimates

(3.5)

\left|\frac{{\bf x}_{j}}{r_{j}}-\frac{{\bf x}_{k}}{r_{k}}\right|\geq\frac{{\delta}}{2}\cdot

By choice of
r_{Y}
and
j,k
we have

\left|\frac{{\bf x}_{j}}{r_{j}}-\frac{{\bf x}_{k}}{r_{k}}\right|=2\sin({\alpha})\;\geq\;\frac{{\delta}}{2}.

From Estimate (
3.2
), we deduce

(3.6)

\frac{{\delta}}{4}\cdot(r_{k}+r_{j})\leq|{\bf x}_{j}-{\bf x}_{k}|.

as well as

(3.7)

|{\bf x}_{l}|=r_{l}\leq\;\frac{4}{{\delta}}\cdot|{\bf x}_{j}-{\bf x}_{k}|\;\;{\rm for}\;\;l=j,k.

Since
Y
has an isolated singularity at
{\bf 0}
, we obviously have

d_{\rm inn}^{{\leq r}}({\bf x}_{j},{\bf x}_{k})\leq d_{j}^{{\leq r}}({\bf x}_{j},{\bf 0})+d_{k}^{{\leq r}}({\bf 0},{\bf x}_{k}),

combining Estimate (
3.7
) with

N_{j}^{{\leq r}}
and
N_{k}^{{\leq r}}
being LNE, each with LNE constant
A
, yield an expected
inequality

d_{\rm inn}^{{\leq r}}({\bf x}_{j},{\bf x}_{k})\;\leq\;\frac{8A}{{\delta}}\cdot|{\bf x}_{j}-{\bf x}_{k}|.

Since each
N_{j}^{{\leq r}}
is LNE, we obtain that
Y^{{\leq r}}
is LNE.

\bullet
Assume that
(Y,{\bf 0})
is locally LNE at
{\bf 0}
.

Since
(Y,{\bf 0})
has an isolated singularity at
{\bf 0}
, we can assume
that
r_{Y}
is small enough so that the following identity
holds true

j<k\;\Longrightarrow\;(N_{j}^{\leq r_{Y}})^{*}\cap(N_{k}^{\leq r_{Y}})^{*}=\emptyset.

Given any pair
1\leq j<k\leq s
, observe that to connect
{\bf x}_{j}
to
{\bf x}_{k}

within
Y^{\leq r_{Y}}
it is necessary to go through
{\bf 0}
. Therefore

(3.8)

d_{\rm inn}^{Y}({\bf x}_{j},{\bf x}_{k})\geq r_{j}+r_{k}

Let
E:=\{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{t}\}
such that
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{t}\leq s,
with
t\geq 2
and
\dim\cap_{j=1}^{t}T_{i_{j}}\geq 1\}
.

Assume that
E
is not empty. Let
J
be an element of
E
and let

P_{J}:=\cap_{j\in J}T_{j}.

For any
{\bf p}\in P_{J}
and each
j\in J
, let
{\bf x}_{j}
be the point

({\bf p},G_{j}({\bf p}))
of
N_{j}
. Since
P_{J}
is tangent at
{\bf 0}
to each
N_{l}

and
D_{\bf 0}G_{l}
is null, for any
l\in J
, as
{\bf p}
goes to
{\bf 0}
in

P_{J}
we find that

|{\bf x}_{j}|,|{\bf x}_{k}|=|{\bf p}|+o(|{\bf p}|)\;\;{\rm and}\;\;|{\bf x}_{j}-{\bf x}_{k}|=o(|{\bf p}|)

for any pair
j,k
of
J
with
j\neq k
. Comparing the last estimate
with Estimate (
3.8
) shows that the germ
(Y,{\bf 0})
cannot
admit a LNE representative in any neighbourhood of
{\bf 0}
. Necessarily

is empty.
∎

4.
The local case

We work in the local complex analytic category.

We present here Proposition
4.2
, a known local criterion
for a complex analytic curve germ to be LNE (see for instance

[
16
,
3
]
), with a complete proof.
This result is used to obtain Proposition
4.6
which fully
characterizes complex curves which are LNE in a given compact complex
manifold (independently of any prescribed continuous Riemannian metric).

Convention 2
.

A curve germ is a complex analytic curve germ
at a prescribed point.

Let
(Y,{\bf 0})
be a curve germ of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
at
{\bf 0}\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
.
Let
m(Y,{\bf 0})
be the multiplicity of
Y
at
{\bf 0}
.
We recall that
Y
is non-singular at
{\bf y}_{0}
if and only if

m(Y,{\bf y}_{0})=1
.

Assume that
Y
is irreducible at
{\bf 0}
and write
m
for
m(Y,{\bf 0})
.
Let
L
be the tangent cone of
Y
at
{\bf 0}
, which is a
complex line through
{\bf 0}
. The restriction to
(Y,{\bf 0})
of the
(orthogonal) projection
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\to L
is a complex analytic finite
mapping
p_{L}:(Y,{\bf 0})\to(L,{\bf 0})
inducing a holomorphic
m
-sheeted
covering
(Y^{*},{\bf 0})\to(L^{*},{\bf 0})
.
After an orthonormal change of complex coordinates we can assume
that

L:=\{{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\,:\,x_{2}=\ldots=x_{n}=0\}=\mathbb{C}\times{\bf 0}.

From Puiseux Theorem, there exists a holomorphic map germ

F:(\mathbb{C},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n-1},{\bf 0})
such that

(4.1)

(Y,{\bf 0})=\{(s^{m},F(s))\,:\,s\in(\mathbb{C},0)\}.

More precisely, writing
F=(f_{2},\ldots,f_{n})
, for each
j=2,\ldots,n,

know that

f_{j}(s)=s^{a_{j}}\phi_{j}(s),\;\;{\rm with}\;\;\phi_{j}(0)\neq 0,\;\;{\rm and}\;\;a_{j}\geq m+1

with the convention

if and only if
f_{j}\equiv 0
.
Moreover there exists no holomorphic map germ
H:(\mathbb{C},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n-1},{\bf 0})
such that
F(s)=H(s^{N})
, for a positive integer

N\geq 2
dividing
m
.

Let
(X,{\bf 0})
be a curve germ of
({\mathbb{C}^{n}},{\bf 0})
. Let

(X_{1},{\bf 0}),\ldots,(X_{s},{\bf 0}),
be its local irreducible components.
For each
j=1,\ldots,s,
let
L_{j}
be the tangent cone of
X_{j}
at
{\bf 0}
,
thus a complex line through
{\bf 0}
.

For each
j=1,\ldots,s,
the (orthogonal) projection
\pi_{j}:(X_{j},{\bf 0})\to(L_{j},{\bf 0})
is a complex finite mapping and it induces the
germ of a holomorphic cover
(X_{j}^{*},{\bf 0})\to(L_{j}^{*},{\bf 0})
with

Remark 4.1
.

The length of any rectifiable curve of an Euclidean
space is taken w.r.t. the Euclidean metric.

The first principal result of this section is the following

Proposition 4.2
.

The germ of analytic curve
(X,{\bf 0})
of
({\mathbb{C}^{n}},{\bf 0})
admits a
representative which is locally LNE at
{\bf 0}
if and only if the
following conditions are satisfied: (i)

for each

, and (ii)

such that

(X,{\bf 0})
is LNE if and only if it is a union of non-singular curve germs intersecting pairwise transversally.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t},
be complex lines of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
through the origin.
We define the following “angle”

{\delta}((\lambda_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,t}):=\min_{1\leq j<k\leq t}\left\{|{\bf u}_{j}-{\bf u}_{k}|:{\bf u}_{j}\in\lambda_{j}\cap{\bf S}_{1}^{2n-1}\;{\rm and}\;{\bf u}_{k}\in\lambda_{k}\cap{\bf S}_{1}^{2n-1}\right\}.

Condition (ii) holds true if and only if
{\delta}((L_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,s})>0
.

\bullet

Assume that (i) and (ii) are satisfied.

Condition (i) means that each
(X_{j},{\bf 0})
is a non-singular germ.
Condition (ii) means that the lines
L_{j}
s are pairwise transverse.
This is the setting of Lemma
3.1
.

\bullet
Assume that
X
admits a representative locally LNE at

{\bf 0}
.

First we show the following property.

Claim 4.3
.

Let
(Y,{\bf 0})
be a curve germ whose tangent cone at

consists of

L
. If

For simplicity let

Definition 4.5
.

Let
M
be a complex manifold. A complex curve of
M
is a complex
analytic subset of
M
, thus closed, and which is of local (complex)
dimension
1
at each of its points.

When the complex manifold

of

is
compact. Therefore the set
X_{\rm sing}
of singular points of

is either empty
or consists of finitely many points.

Following point (ii) of Remark

, since any

Proposition 4.6
.

A complex curve
X
of a compact complex manifold

is LNE if and only if

X
is connected and is locally LNE at each point of its singular locus.

Proof.

If
X
is LNE, it is connected and it is locally LNE at each of its points

.

Let
m
be the complex dimension of
M
.
Assume that

X_{\rm sing}:=\{{\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{s}\}

For each
j=1,\ldots,s,
let

\psi_{j}:\mathcal{U}_{j}\to\mathcal{V}_{m}:=B_{2}^{2m}\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}

centred at
{\bf x}_{j}
such that
\psi_{j}({\bf x}_{j})={\bf 0}
. The image

is a complex analytic set of

of local complex dimension

at each of its points. There

such that for every positive radius

]

,

and for each positive

,
T_{j,r}
or

.

.
∎

Claim 5.1

For any positive radius

, the subsets

and

{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\setminus{\bf B}_{R}^{2n}
are LNE.

Let
{\bf P}^{n}:=\mathbb{CP}^{n}
be the complex projective space with projective
coordinates
[z_{1}:\ldots:z_{n+1}]
. Let
{\bf H}_{\infty}
be the projective
hyperplane

.

The affine space
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
embeds in
{\bf P}^{n}
as
{\bf x}\mapsto[{\bf x}:1]
, that
is
{\bf P}^{n}={\mathbb{C}^{n}}\sqcup{\bf H}_{\infty}
. The hyperplane
{\bf H}_{\infty}
is called
the hyperplane at infinity and any point of
{\bf H}_{\infty}
is called a

If
S
is any subset of
{\bf P}^{n}
, the affine part
S^{a}
of

is
defined as

S^{a}:=S\cap{\mathbb{C}^{n}}=S\setminus{\bf H}_{\infty}.

The subset

is affine if
S=S^{a}
.

When

is affine, the germ
(S,\infty):=(S,{\bf H}_{\infty})
is well defined
as a germ in
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
, and so is the germ
(S,\lambda)
for
\lambda\in{\bf H}_{\infty}
.

Let
\lambda
be a point of
{\bf H}_{\infty}
. If
(Y,\lambda)
is a (complex analytic) curve germ at
\lambda
, we will denote by

any representative of the germ
(Y,\lambda)
in
{\bf P}^{n}
.
We are only interested in representatives of
(Y,\lambda)
whose intersection
with
{\bf H}_{\infty}
is just
\lambda
, property which we will always implicitly
assume true.

Assume that
(Y,\lambda)
is irreducible at
\lambda
and is not contained in

{\bf H}_{\infty}
. Thus the tangent cone to
Y
at
\lambda
is a line

L
of the vector space
T_{\lambda}{\bf P}^{n}
. The line

is either transverse
to
{\bf H}_{\infty}
or contained in
T_{\lambda}{\bf H}^{\infty}
.
After a linear change of coordinates in
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
, we can assume that
\lambda:=[1:0:\ldots:0]
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{1}
be the affine chart of
{\bf P}^{n}
defined as

\{z_{1}\neq 0\}
. Let
[1:{\bf w}:z]
be affine coordinates in

\mathcal{A}_{1}
so that in
{\bf H}_{\infty}\cap\mathcal{A}_{1}=\{z=0\}
. We further write

{\bf w}=(w_{2},{\bf w}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-2}
.
We identify
T_{\lambda}{\bf P}^{n}
with
\mathcal{A}_{1}
as well.

Since
{\bf x}
are the affine coordinates of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
, in
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\cap\mathcal{A}_{1}

we write
[x_{1}:{\bf y}:1]=[{\bf x}:1]=[1:{\bf w}:z]
.

If
L
is contained in
\{z=0\}
, after a linear transformation of
\{z=0\}

in
\mathcal{A}_{1}
, corresponding to a linear transformation of
\{x_{1}=0\}
in

{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
, we can further require that

(5.1)

L=\{[1:w_{2}:0:\ldots:0]\,:\,w_{2}\in\mathbb{C}\}.

The line
L
is transverse to
{\bf H}_{\infty}
at
\lambda
if and only if there
exists
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
such that

L=\{[1:z{\bf a}:z]\,:\,z\in\mathbb{C}\}.

The affine translation
{\bf x}\to(x_{1},{\bf y}-{\bf a})
in
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
, corresponding
to the linear change of coordinates
({\bf w},z)\to({\bf w}^{\prime},z)=({\bf v},z)=({\bf w}-z{\bf a},z)
in
\mathcal{A}_{1}
, shows that the line
L
has the following
equation

(5.2)

L=\{[1:0:\ldots:0:z]\,:\,z\in\mathbb{C}\}.

Let
(s,{\bf w}^{\prime},t)
be either
(w_{2},{\bf w}^{\prime},z)
or
(z,{\bf w}^{\prime},w_{2})
for
w_{2},z,

as appearing in the simplified equations
(
5.1
) and (
5.2
)
of
L
, so that
L=\{t={\bf w}^{\prime}=0\}
.

The multiplicity
m:=m(Y,\lambda)
is preserved by this change of
coordinates to obtain the simpler equation of
L
. Let

Y^{*}:=Y\setminus\lambda\;\;{\rm and}\;\;L^{*}:=L\setminus\lambda.

The projection
p_{L}:(Y^{*},\lambda)\to(L^{*},\lambda)
is a holomorphic

m
-sheeted covering.

We introduce the following avatar at infinity of being locally LNE at a point
(see also
[
8
]
).

Definition 5.2
.

A subset
S
of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
is locally LNE at infinity (w.r.t. the
euclidean metric of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
) if there exists a compact subset
K
of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
such
that
S\setminus K
is LNE (w.r.t. the
euclidean metric of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
).

The second non-compact result about being LNE is the following

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume
L
is transverse to
{\bf H}_{\infty}
. The germ
(Y^{a},\lambda)
is locally LNE
at infinity if and only if
m=1
.

Proof.

In this case we have
(s,t)=(z,w_{2})
, and using parametrization
(
4.1
) we find

(Y,\lambda)=\left\{[1:F(z):z^{m}]=[1:O(z^{p}):z^{m}]\,:\,z\in(\mathbb{C},0)\right\}

for a positive integer
p\geq m+1
, maximal for this property.
In this case
F\equiv 0
would mean
p=\infty
, which corresponds to
the case
Y^{a}=L
.
Observe also that from the affine point of view we check that

(Y^{a},\lambda)=\left\{\left(x_{1}^{m},x_{1}^{m}F(x_{1}^{-1})\right)=\left(x_{1}^{m},O\left(x_{1}^{-(p-m)}\right)\right)\,:\,x_{1}\in(\mathbb{C},\infty)\right\}

so that if
(x_{1},{\bf y})\in(Y^{a},\infty)
, then
{\bf y}\to{\bf 0}
as
x_{1}\to\infty
. Let us consider the following holomorphic map germ
(\mathbb{C},\infty)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n-1},0)

x_{1}\to G(x_{1}):=x_{1}^{m}F(x_{1}^{-1})=O\left(x_{1}^{-(p-m)}\right).

\bullet
Assume
m=1
. The germ
(Y,\lambda)
is non-singular at
\lambda
,

{\bf w}=z^{p}{\alpha}(z)

where either
p\geq 2
and
{\alpha}(z)\in\mathbb{C}\{z\}^{n-1}
with
{\alpha}(0)\neq{\bf 0}
, or
(Y,\lambda)=(L,\lambda)
.
This presentation is equivalent to the following affine one

{\bf y}=G(x_{1}).

Since the components of
G
are power series in
x_{1}^{-1}
converging for

|x_{1}|\geq R_{1}>0
,
G
goes to
{\bf 0}

as
x_{1}
goes to infinity. Thus its derivative goes to
{\bf 0}

as
x_{1}
goes to infinity, therefore
it is a Lipschitz mapping
\mathbb{C}\setminus{\bf B}_{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
for any radius

R\geq R_{1}
. Proposition
2.10
and Claim

5.1

imply that the subset
\{(x_{1},G(x_{1})\,:\;|x_{1}|\geq R\}
is LNE
since it is a Lipschitz graph over a LNE subset. Therefore the germ

(Y^{a},\lambda)
is locally LNE at infinity.

\bullet
Assume
m\geq 2
.
Let
z\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and let
{\zeta}
be a
m
-th root of
z
. Let

{\bf x}_{0}:=[1:F({\zeta}):z]
and
{\bf x}_{1}:=[1:F(\omega{\zeta}):z]
for

\omega=e^{i\frac{2\pi}{m}}
.
By parametrization (
4.1
), we find
F({\zeta})\neq F(\omega{\zeta})
.
Writing

{\bf x}_{0}^{a}:=\left(\frac{1}{z},\frac{F({\zeta})}{z}\right)\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;{\bf x}_{1}^{a}:=\left(\frac{1}{z},\frac{F(\omega{\zeta})}{z}\right)\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}}

for
|z|
small enough we obtain

(5.3)

|{\bf x}_{1}^{a}-{\bf x}_{0}^{a}|=\left|\frac{F({\zeta})}{z}-\frac{F(\omega{\zeta})}{z}\right|\leq const.|z|^{\frac{p}{m}-1}=const.\frac{1}{|x_{1}|^{q}}\;\;{\rm for}\;\;q:=\frac{p-m}{m}>0.

We can take a representative of
Y^{a}
outside a large Euclidean
closed ball
{\bf B}^{2n}
centred at
{\bf 0}
such that the projection
mapping

\pi:Y^{a}\to\mathbb{C}\setminus{\bf B}^{2},\;\;[x_{1}:{\bf y}:1]\mapsto x_{1}

is a
m
-sheeted holomorphic covering, where
{\bf B}^{2}
is an Euclidean
ball of
\mathbb{C}
centred at
{\bf 0}
image of
{\bf B}^{2n}
under the projection
onto the
x_{1}
-axis.

Let
{\gamma}:[0,1]\to Y^{a}
be a rectifiable path from
{\bf x}_{0}:={\gamma}(0)

to
{\bf x}_{1}:={\gamma}(1)
. We write
{\gamma}(t)=(x_{1}(t),{\bf y}(t))
.
The path
\pi\circ{\gamma}:t\mapsto x_{1}(t)

is well defined over
[0,1]
, and is a loop since
\pi({\bf x}_{0})=\pi({\bf x}_{1})\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. Therefore it is not contractible in
\mathbb{C}\setminus{\bf B}^{2}
.
We obtain

{\rm length}(\pi\circ{\gamma}):=\int_{0}^{1}|x_{1}^{\prime}(t)|{\rm d}t\,\leq\,{\rm length}({\gamma}).

Since
\pi\circ{\gamma}
is not contractible in
\mathbb{C}\setminus{\bf B}

the following estimate is satisfied

|x_{1}(0)|\gg 1\;\Longrightarrow\;|x_{1}(0)|\;\leq\;{\rm length}(\pi\circ{\gamma})\;\leq\;{\rm length}({\gamma}).

Combining this last estimate with Estimate (
5.3
), which implies
that
|{\bf x}_{1}^{a}-{\bf x}_{0}^{a}|
goes to
0
as
|x_{1}(0)|
goes to
\infty
, shows that
no representative of the germ
(Y^{a},\lambda)
can be locally LNE at infinity.
∎

The next result presents the case of tangency to
{\bf H}_{\infty}
. Its proof will
follow from arguments similar to those used for the transverse case (see also

8
]
for a similar situation).

Lemma 5.4
.

If
L
is contained in
T_{\lambda}{\bf H}_{\infty}
, then the germ
(Y^{a},\lambda)
is
not locally LNE at infinity.

Proof.

In this case we have
(s,t)=(w_{2},z)
.
Using again parametrization (
4.1
) we find

(Y,\lambda)=\left\{[1:w_{2}^{m}:F(w_{2})]=[1:w_{2}^{m}:O(w_{2}^{m+1})]\,:\,w_{2}\in(\mathbb{C},0)\right\}.

Since the curve germ is not contained in
{\bf H}_{\infty}
, there exist a
positive integer
p\geq m+1
, a function
\varphi\in\mathbb{C}\{w_{2}\}

satisfying
\varphi(0)\neq 0
such that

z(w_{2})=w_{2}^{p}\cdot\varphi(w_{2}),

writing
F=({\bf w}^{\prime},z)=(w_{3},\ldots,w_{n},z)
. For each
j=3,\ldots,n,
there
exists an exponent
q_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq m+1}\cup\infty
and a function
\psi_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\{w_{2}\}

such that

w_{j}(w_{2})=w_{2}^{q_{j}}\cdot\psi_{j}(w_{2})

where
\psi_{j}(0)\neq 0
if the function
w_{j}
is not the null function,
equivalently if
q_{j}<\infty
.
Since
\varphi(0)\neq 0
, we can re-parametrize the affine part
(Y^{a},\lambda)
as

(Y^{a},\lambda)=\left\{[s^{p}:O(|s|^{p-m}):1]\,:\,s\in(\mathbb{C},\infty)\right\}.

From the affine point of view, since
p-m>p-q_{j}
for all
j
, we get

(x_{1},{\bf y})\in Y^{a}\;\Longrightarrow\;|{\bf y}|\leq const.|x_{1}|^{r}\;\;{\rm for}\;\;r:=\frac{p-m}{p}\in]0,1[

when
|x_{1}|
is large enough.

We can take a representative of
Y^{a}
outside a large Euclidean closed
ball
{\bf B}^{2n}
centred at
{\bf 0}
so that, by parametrization
(
4.1
), the projection mapping

\pi:Y^{a}\setminus{\bf B}^{2n}\to\mathbb{C}\setminus{\bf B}^{2},\;\;[x_{1}:{\bf y}:1]\mapsto x_{1}

is a
p
-sheeted holomorphic covering whenever
|x_{1}|
is large enough,
where
{\bf B}^{2}
is Euclidean ball of
\mathbb{C}
centred at
{\bf 0}
image of

{\bf B}^{2n}
under the projection onto the
x_{1}
-axis. Let
{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}
in

Y^{a}
such that

\pi({\bf x})=\pi({\bf x}^{\prime})=x_{1}.

Since
p\geq m+1\geq 2
, we assume that
{\bf x}\neq{\bf x}^{\prime}
.
If
|x_{1}|
is large enough, we find

|{\bf x}|,\,|{\bf x}^{\prime}|=|x_{1}|(1+o(1))

as well as the following estimate

|{\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}|=o(|x_{1}|)\ll|x_{1}|.

From here on, we conclude as in the proof of the case

of Lemma

5.3
.
∎

6.
Unbounded part of affine curves

This section continues what was initiated in Section
5
,
but working with explicit representatives of germs of infinity.

Convention 4
.

A curve in
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
is an affine algebraic curve.
A curve in
{\bf P}^{n}
is a projective algebraic curve.

Let
X
be a curve of
{\bf P}^{n}
and let
X^{\infty}
be the intersection

X\cap{\bf H}^{\infty}
. Let
\deg(X)
be the degree of
X
.

Remark 6.1
.

1) If
X
has no irreducible component contained in
{\bf H}_{\infty}
, then

\deg(X)=\deg(X^{a})
.

2) As a consequence of Bézout Theorem, if
card(X^{\infty})=\deg(X)
,
then for each
\lambda\in X^{\infty}
, the germ
(X,\lambda)
is non-singular at

\lambda
and is transverse to
{\bf H}_{\infty}
at
\lambda
.

We assume that
X^{\infty}
consists of finitely many points

\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{p},
with
p=card(X^{\infty})\leq\deg(X)
.

The germ of the affine part
X^{a}
at infinity is

(X^{a},\infty)=\sqcup_{j=1}^{p}(X^{a},\lambda_{j}).

Given any positive radius
R
, let

X_{R}:=X\cap{\bf S}_{R}^{2n-1}=X^{a}\cap{\bf S}_{R}^{2n-1}.

Using the Euclidean inversion
\iota_{2n}:{\bf x}\mapsto|{\bf x}|^{-2}\cdot{\bf x}
, the Euclidean closure of the image of
\iota_{2n}(X^{a}\setminus{\bf 0})
is a real algebraic
set of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\supset\iota_{2n}({\mathbb{C}^{n}})
, with an isolated singularity at

{\bf 0}
. The Local Conic Structure at
{\bf 0}
when combined with the
inversion yields a Conical Structure Theorem at infinity, which states
that there exists a positive radius
R_{X}
such that for
R,R^{\prime}\geq R_{X}

the links
X_{R}
and
X_{R^{\prime}}
are
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphic. Moreover
for
R\geq R_{X}
the sub-manifold with boundary

X_{{\geq R}}^{a}:=X^{a}\setminus B_{R}^{2n}

is
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphic to the cylinder
X_{R}\times[R,+\infty[
, mapping

X_{r}
onto
X_{R}\times r
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a connected component of
X_{{\geq R}}^{a}
.
Therefore the closure of
\mathcal{C}
in
{\bf P}^{n}
intersects the hyperplane at
infinity
{\bf H}_{\infty}
in a single point
\lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
. Two such connected
components

\mathcal{C}_{1}
,
\mathcal{C}_{2}
may accumulate at the same point at infinity:

\lambda_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}=\lambda_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}
.

For
R\geq R_{X}
, let
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{R},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{e}^{R},
be the connected components of

X_{{\geq R}}^{a}
. Thus
card(X^{\infty})\leq e\leq\deg(X)
.
A first key piece to the main result is the following

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
X^{a}
be an affine curve and let
X
be its projective closure. Assume
that
\deg(X)=card(X^{\infty})
. Then, there exists a positive radius
R_{X}^{\prime}

\geq R_{X}
such that for each
R\geq R_{X}^{\prime}
, the connected components

\mathcal{C}_{j}^{R}
are LNE,
j=1,\ldots,\deg(X)
.

Proof.

Let
d:=\deg(X)
.
Let
i(X,\lambda)
be the local intersection multiplicity of the germ
(X,\lambda)

with
({\bf H}_{\infty},\lambda)
(see
[
5
]
).
Since
{\bf H}_{\infty}
is non-singular, we deduce that

i(X,\lambda)\geq m(X,\lambda)
for any
\lambda\in X^{\infty}
. The hypothesis means
that

d=\sum_{\lambda\in X^{\infty}}i(X,\lambda),

and thus
1=i(X,\lambda)\geq m(X,\lambda)\geq 1
. Therefore the curve germ

(X,\lambda)
is non-singular and is in general position with
{\bf H}_{\infty}
at

\lambda
for each
\lambda\in X^{\infty}
, that is transverse to
{\bf H}_{\infty}
.
Thus we are in the hypotheses of Lemma
5.3
.

Let
X^{\infty}=\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}\}
.
Assume that
(X^{a},\lambda_{j})=(\mathcal{C}_{j}^{R},\lambda_{j})
for each
j=1,\ldots,d,

and each
R\geq R_{X}
.

Let
L_{j}
be the line tangent to
X
at
\lambda_{j}
. Since it is transverse
to
{\bf H}_{\infty}
we can consider
L_{j}
as an affine line of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
. Let

{\bf e}_{j}
be a unit vector of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
such that the point
\lambda_{j}\in{\bf H}_{\infty}

corresponds to the complex line
\mathbb{C}{\bf e}_{j}
.
Let
H_{j}
be the complex hyperplane orthogonal to
\mathbb{C}{\bf e}_{j}
.
Since
L_{j}
is transverse to
{\bf H}_{\infty}
there exists
{\bf a}_{j}\in H_{j}
such
that

L_{j}=\mathbb{C}{\bf e}_{j}+{\bf a}_{j}.

Claim 6.3
.

\mathcal{C}_{j}^{R}
is bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic to
L_{j}\setminus B_{R}^{2n}
with respect to the outer distance.

Proof of the claim.

Similarly to the proof of Lemma
5.3
, we deduce that

(\mathcal{C}_{j}^{R},\lambda_{j})
is a Lipschitz and holomorphic graph over

\mathbb{C}\setminus B_{r_{j}}^{2}=\mathbb{C}{\bf e}_{j}\setminus B_{r_{j}}^{2}
for each

j=1,\ldots,d
. Moreover the Lipschitz constant tends to
1
as
r_{j}
goes
to
\infty
. Let

r_{X}:=\max_{j=1,\ldots,d}r_{j}.

For each
R\geq r_{X}
, let

Y_{j}^{R}:=\{(s,G_{j}(s))=s{\bf e}_{j}+G_{j}(s)\in\mathbb{C}{\bf e}_{j}\times H_{j}:s\in\mathbb{C}\setminus B_{R}^{2}\}

where
G_{j}
is the holomorphic mapping taking values in
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}=H_{j}

we mentioned. We recall that

G_{j}(s)=g_{j}\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)\;\;{\rm where}\;\;g_{j}(t)\in\mathbb{C}\{t\}^{n-1}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;g_{j}(0)={\bf a}_{j}.

Therefore, the following estimate holds true

\lim_{|s|\to\infty}\;G_{j}^{\prime}(s)={\bf 0}.

Since
G_{j}
goes to
{\bf a}_{j}
and its derivative goes to
{\bf 0}
as
s
goes
to
\infty
, for every
positive
\varepsilon
, there exists
r_{\varepsilon}\geq\max(R_{X},r_{X})
such that

x_{1},x_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}{\bf e}_{j}\setminus B_{r_{\varepsilon}}^{2}\;\Longrightarrow\;|G_{j}(x_{1})-G_{j}(x_{1}^{\prime})|<\varepsilon|x_{1}-x_{1}^{\prime}|\;\;{\rm and}\;\;|G_{j}(x_{1})|^{2}<|{\bf a}_{j}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}.

Thus the mapping
{\Gamma}_{j}:s\mapsto s{\bf e}_{j}+G_{j}(s))
is holomorphic
over
\mathbb{C}\setminus B_{r_{\varepsilon}}^{2}
and is Lipschitz, with Lipschitz constant
smaller than
(1+\varepsilon)
.

Since
{\Gamma}_{j}
is bi-holomorphic, up to taking a
smaller
\varepsilon
we find that
p_{j}:={\Gamma}_{j}^{-1}
, that is the projection onto

\mathbb{C}{\bf e}_{j}
, is Lipschitz with constant larger than
(1-\varepsilon)
. Natural
embedding of
\mathbb{C}{\bf e}_{j}\setminus B_{r_{\varepsilon}}^{2}
into
\mathbb{C}^{n}
yields the
first part of the proof. Last, there is just to recall that the orthogonal
projection onto an affine space is Lipschitz.
∎

In the notation of the proof above, if
\varepsilon
and
r_{\varepsilon}
are given, we find that for
each
j=1,\ldots,d,
the following inclusions are satisfied

Y_{j}^{R_{j,\varepsilon}}\;\subset\;\mathcal{C}_{j}^{R_{j,\varepsilon}}\;\subset\;Y_{j}^{R},\;\;{\rm where}\;\;R_{j,\varepsilon}:=\sqrt{R^{2}+|{\bf a}_{j}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}

whenever
R\geq r_{\varepsilon}
. Under this choice of
\varepsilon
we get

{\partial}\mathcal{C}_{j}^{R_{j,\varepsilon}}\cap Y_{j}^{R_{j,\varepsilon}}={\partial}Y_{j}^{R}\cap\mathcal{C}_{j}^{R_{j,\varepsilon}}=\emptyset.

where
{\partial}\mathcal{C}_{j}^{R}=\mathcal{C}_{j}^{R}\cap{\bf S}_{R}^{2n-1}
and
{\partial}Y_{j}^{R}={\Gamma}_{j}(L_{j}\cap{\bf S}_{R}^{2n-1})
. We define the following smooth Jordan curve of

L_{j}

Z_{j}^{R}:=p_{j}({\partial}\mathcal{C}_{j}^{R}),

which is diffeomorphic to
{\bf S}^{1}
. Let
D_{j}^{R}
be the open disk it bounds. We
cannot expect
D_{j}^{R}
to be convex.

The following claim follows from Lemma
2.8
.

Claim 6.4
.

\mathbb{C}{\bf e}_{j}\setminus D_{j}^{R}
is LNE.

Since
{\bf B}_{R}^{2}\cap\mathbb{C}{\bf e}_{j}\subset D_{j}^{R_{j,\varepsilon}}
and the mapping
{\Gamma}_{j}

is Lipschitz over
\mathbb{C}{\bf e}_{j}\setminus B_{R}^{2}
, it is also Lipschitz over

\mathbb{C}{\bf e}_{j}\setminus D_{j}^{R}
. The result follows from Proposition

2.10
. This concludes the proof of Proposition

.
∎

There are two scenarii for which

. The first one

, when the germ

is irreducible and with tangent cone

at

contained

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
X
be a curve of
{\bf P}^{n}
. Let
\lambda
be an isolated point of
X^{\infty}
.
If the germ
(X,\lambda)
is not irreducible, then

is not LNE.

Proof.

We can assume that
\lambda=[1:0:\ldots:0]
.
Let
\left\{(X_{j},\lambda)\right\}_{j=1,\ldots,N}
be the irreducible components
of
(X,\lambda)
.
Fix

so that

is not connected.
Note that

(X^{a},\lambda)=(S,\lambda).

For each connected components
\mathcal{C}
of

accumulating at
\lambda
,
there exists a unique

such that

(\mathcal{C},\infty)=(X_{j}^{a},\lambda).

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}
,
\mathcal{C}_{2}
be two connected components of

Let
({\bf x}_{l})_{l}\subset\mathcal{C}_{1}
and

converging to
\infty
. We further require that

{\bf x}_{l}=(l,{\bf x}_{k})\;\;{\rm and}\;\;{\bf x}_{l}^{\prime}=(l,{\bf x}_{k}^{\prime}).

Thus we obtain the following estimate

|{\bf x}_{l}-{\bf x}_{l}^{\prime}|=o(l).

Let
{\gamma}_{l}
be a rectifiable path on
X^{a}
connecting
{\bf x}_{l}
and

{\bf x}_{l}^{\prime}
. Its length satisfies the following inequality

{\rm length}({\gamma}_{l})\;\geq\;{\rm dist}({\bf x}_{l},{\partial}S)+{\rm dist}({\bf x}_{l}^{\prime},{\partial}S),

where
{\partial}S
is the boundary of
S
, which is compact.
For
l
large enough we check that

{\rm dist}({\bf x}_{l},{\partial}S)\geq\frac{l}{2}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;{\rm dist}({\bf x}_{l}^{\prime},{\partial}S)\geq\frac{l}{2}.

Since for
l
large enough we have found that

{\rm length}({\gamma}_{l})\;\geq\;l,

the subset
X^{a}
cannot be LNE.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

The demonstration of Lemma
6.5
still holds whenever
a germ
(S,\infty)
of a closed connected subset
S
of
\mathbb{R}^{q}
has
two connected components at infinity,
whose respective accumulation loci at infinity contain a common point.

8.
Main Result: how to recognize an affine LNE curve

We have gathered in the previous sections all the ingredients to prove the
main result of the paper:

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
X
be a connected projective curve of
{\bf P}^{n}
of degree
\deg(X)

such that
X^{\infty}
is finite. The affine curve
X^{a}
is LNE if and only if the
following conditions are satisfied:
(0)

X^{a}
is connected;
(i)
X^{a}
is locally LNE at each of its singular points; (ii) The curve

X
intersects
{\bf H}_{\infty}
in exactly
\deg(X)
points.

Proof.

Assume
X^{a}
is LNE. It is connected and it is locally LNE at each of its
points,
thus we obtained
(0)
and
(i)
. Since Lemma
6.5
cannot
happen, by Lemma
5.4
we deduce that at each
\lambda\in X^{\infty}
the germ
(X,\lambda)
is non-singular.
Lemma
5.3
further implies that

(X,\lambda)
is transverse to
{\bf H}_{\infty}
at each
\lambda
of
X^{\infty}
.
Since
\deg(X)
is the intersection number of
X
with any projective
hyperplane
of
{\bf P}^{n}
, the transversality of
X
and
{\bf H}_{\infty}
at each of their
intersection points yields
card(X^{\infty})=\deg(X)
.

Assume that conditions
(0)
,
(i)
,
(ii)
are satisfied.

Thus there exists
R_{X}^{\prime}
such that
X_{{\leq R}}^{a}
is connected for each

R\geq R_{X}^{\prime}
. Since
X_{R}
is a smooth compact sub-manifold once
R\geq R_{X}^{\prime}
,
hypothesis
(i)
and Proposition
7.1
guarantee that

X_{{\leq R}}^{a}
is LNE for each
R\geq R_{X}^{\prime}
. Let

\mathcal{C}_{0}:=X_{{\leq R}}^{a}.

Let
d=\deg(X)
and let
X^{\infty}=\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1,\ldots,d}
.
Condition
(ii)
implies that Proposition
6.2
is
satisfied,
that is, each connected component of
X_{{\geq R}}^{a}
is LNE whenever
R\geq R_{X}^{\prime}
.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{d},
be the connected components of
X_{{\geq R}}^{a}
, indexed
such that
\lambda_{j}
is the accumulation point at infinity of
\mathcal{C}_{j}
.

For each
j=0,\ldots,d,
let
d_{j}:=d_{\rm inn}^{\mathcal{C}_{j}}
.

Since each
\mathcal{C}_{j}
is LNE, let
A
be a positive constant which is
a LNE constant for each
\mathcal{C}_{j}
:

{\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}\;\Longrightarrow\;d_{j}({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\;\leq\;A|{\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}|,\;\;j=0,\ldots,d.

Claim 8.2
.

There exists a positive constant
A^{\prime}
such that for any
0\leq j<k\leq d

the following estimate holds true

{\bf x}_{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{j},{\bf x}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}\;\Longrightarrow\;d_{\rm inn}^{X^{a}}({\bf x}_{j},{\bf x}_{k})\leq A^{\prime}|{\bf x}_{j}-{\bf x}_{k}|.

Proof of the claim.

For
j\geq 1
, let
\mathbb{C}_{j}
be the complex line of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
through the origin
corresponding to complex line direction

\lambda_{j}
. Let
S_{j}:=\mathbb{C}_{j}\cap{\bf S}_{1}^{2n-1}
be the unit circle of
\mathbb{C}_{j}

centred at the origin. Whenever
j\neq k
, the complex lines
\mathbb{C}_{j}
and
\mathbb{C}_{k}

only meet at
{\bf 0}
, therefore the intersection
S_{j}\cap S_{k}

is empty. For any
1\leq j<k\leq d
, let

{\delta}_{j,k}:={\rm dist}(S_{j},S_{k})>0,

the Euclidean distance between
S_{j}
and
S_{k}
, and let

{\delta}:=\min_{1\leq j<k\leq d}{\delta}_{j,k}>0.

Since
\mathcal{C}_{j}
accumulates at
\lambda_{j}
at infinity, we can assume that
R
is
large enough so that

\sup\left\{{\rm dist}\left(S_{j},\frac{{\bf x}}{|{\bf x}|}\right):{\bf x}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}\right\}\;\leq\;\frac{{\delta}}{4}

for each
j=1,\ldots,d
.

Let
{\bf x}_{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}
and
{\bf x}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}
, for a given pair of indices

0\leq j<k\leq d
. Let
{\bf u}_{l}\in{\bf S}^{2n-1}
(when defined) and

r_{l}\geq 0
, for
l=j,k,
be defined as follows:

{\bf u}_{l}:=\frac{{\bf x}_{l}}{|{\bf x}_{l}|},\;\;{\rm and}\;\;r_{l}:=|{\bf x}_{l}|.

Let
2{\alpha}\in[0,\pi]
be the non-oriented angle between
{\bf u}_{j}
and

{\bf u}_{k}
.

\bullet

Case 1:

1\leq j<k\leq d
.

Let
{\bf x}_{k}
be a point of
{\partial}\mathcal{C}_{l}\subset X_{R}^{a}
which realizes
the minimum of
d_{l}({\bf x}_{l},{\partial}\mathcal{C}_{l})
for
l=j,k
.

By choice of
R
and
j,k
we have

|{\bf u}_{j}-{\bf u}_{k}|=2\sin({\alpha})\;\geq\;\frac{{\delta}}{2},

Assume that
r_{j}\geq r_{k}
, we recall identity (
3.1
)

|{\bf x}_{j}-{\bf x}_{k}|^{2}=(r_{j}+r_{k})^{2}\sin^{2}{\alpha}+(r_{j}-r_{k})^{2}\cos^{2}{\alpha}

from which we deduce again Estimates (
3.6
)

\frac{{\delta}}{4}\cdot(r_{k}+r_{j})\leq|{\bf x}_{j}-{\bf x}_{k}|.

Since
r_{j},r_{k}\geq R
, Estimate (
3.2
) yields the
following estimate

(8.1)

|{\bf y}_{j}-{\bf y}_{k}|\;\leq\;2R\;\leq\;\frac{4}{{\delta}}\cdot|{\bf x}_{j}-{\bf x}_{k}|.

as well as

(8.2)

|{\bf x}_{l}-{\bf x}_{k}|\;\leq\;r_{l}+R\;\leq\;\frac{4}{{\delta}}\cdot|{\bf x}_{j}-{\bf x}_{k}|\;\;{\rm for}\;\;l=j,k.

Since, we obviously have

d_{\rm inn}^{X^{a}}({\bf x}_{j},{\bf x}_{k})\leq d_{j}({\bf x}_{j},{\bf y}_{j})+d_{0}({\bf y}_{j},{\bf y}_{k})+d_{k}({\bf y}_{k},{\bf x}_{k}),

combining Estimates (
8.1
) and (
8.2
) with
\mathcal{C}_{0},\mathcal{C}_{j},\mathcal{C}_{k}
being LNE with LNE constant
A
yield an expected inequality

d_{\rm inn}^{X^{a}}({\bf x}_{j},{\bf x}_{k})\;\leq\;\frac{12A}{{\delta}}\cdot|{\bf x}_{j}-{\bf x}_{k}|.

\bullet

Case 2:

j=0
and
1\leq k\leq d
.

We can assume
r_{0}<R<r_{k}
.

If
r_{k}\leq 2R
, we know that
X_{\leq 2R}^{a}
is LNE.

Let
{\bf y}
be a point of
{\partial}\mathcal{C}_{k}
.
When
r_{k}\geq 2R
, we find that

\frac{1}{2}r_{k}\;\leq\;|{\bf x}_{k}-{\bf x}_{0}|\;\leq\;\frac{3}{2}r_{k},\;\;{\rm and}\;\;\frac{1}{2}r_{k}\;\leq\;d_{k}({\bf x}_{k},{\bf y})\;\leq\;\frac{3A}{2}r_{k}.

For
r_{k}\geq 2R
, we therefore deduce the following estimate

d_{\rm inn}^{X^{a}}({\bf x}_{0},{\bf x}_{k})\;\leq\;d_{0}({\bf x}_{0},{\bf y})+d_{k}({\bf x}_{k},{\bf y})\;\leq\;2AR+\frac{3A}{2}r_{k}\;\leq\;5A|{\bf x}_{k}-{\bf x}_{0}|,

proving the statement in the second case.
∎

Claim
8.2
establishes the desired LNE properties between any
pair of points belonging to different subsets
\mathcal{C}_{j}
.
Since each of these subset is LNE, the result is proved.
∎

9.
Affinely LNE versus Projectively LNE

The space
{\bf P}^{n}
is naturally equipped with a Khäler metric, the
Fubini-Study metric. As mentioned in point (ii) of Remark

2.4
, any continuous Riemannian structure equipping

{\bf P}^{n}
, Hermitian or not, will yield a metric space structure equivalent to
the one induced from the Fubini-Study metric.
We will refer to any such continuous Riemannian metric on
{\bf P}^{n}
as the
projective metric
g_{{\rm proj}}
.

Let
H
be a hyperplane of
{\bf P}^{n}
, and let
\mathbb{C}_{H}^{n}
be the affine space

{\bf P}^{n}\setminus H
. We can embed
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
in
{\bf P}^{n}
as
\mathbb{C}_{H}^{n}
for any
a priori given hyperplane
H
of
{\bf P}^{n}
such that the embedding provides
a unitary isomorphism between
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
and
\mathbb{C}_{H}^{n}
.

As a subset of the projective space
{\bf P}^{n}
, the affine space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is
equipped with the restriction of the projective metric
g_{{\rm proj}}
,
whereas as a complex vector space it is equipped with its canonical Hermitian
structure
eucl
. The metric space structures respectively obtained
from
({\mathbb{C}^{n}},g_{{\rm proj}})
and
({\mathbb{C}^{n}},eucl)
are clearly not equivalent,
since
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is bounded for
g_{{\rm proj}}
while it is not bounded for
eucl
.
Yet the respective restrictions of
eucl
and
g_{{\rm proj}}
to any given compact
subset
of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
produce equivalent metric space structures over the compact subset,
for the outer metric and thus also for the inner metric.

In this section, Theorem
9.4
establishes the relation
between LNE property of affine curve w.r.t. the projective and
euclidean metrics. We present some conventions first.

Definition 9.1
.

Let
X
be a curve of
{\bf P}^{n}
and let
H
be a hyperplane of
{\bf P}^{n}
.

1) The curve
X\setminus H
in
\mathbb{C}_{H}^{n}
is the affine trace of
X
w.r.t.

H
.

2) The pair
X,H
is in general position if the intersection

X\cap H
consists only of non-singular points of
X
at each of which
X

and
H
are transverse.

Here we forget about Convention 3, introducing
in its stead the following

Definition 9.2
.

(i) A subset
S
of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
is LNE in
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
if it is LNE in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
w.r.t.

eucl
.

(ii) A subset
S
of
{\bf P}^{n}
is LNE in
{\bf P}^{n}
if it is LNE
in
{\bf P}^{n}
w.r.t.
g_{{\rm proj}}
.

Observe that when the curve
X
is LNE in
{\bf P}^{n}
, its affine trace

X\setminus H
is not necessarily LNE in
\mathbb{C}_{H}^{n}
for any hyperplane
H
,
as already suggested by Lemma
5.4
and Lemma

6.5
.

Example 9.3
.

Let
{\bf P}^{2}
be equipped with projective coordinates

{\bf z}=[x:y:z]
. Let
L
be the hyperplane
\{z=0\}
and
\mathbb{C}^{2}:=\mathbb{C}_{L}^{2}
.
Consider the non-singular quadrics

P:=\{{\bf z}:yz-x^{2}=0\}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;H:=\{{\bf z}:xy-z^{2}=0\}.

The parabola
P
and the hyperbola
H
are isomorphic via the unitary
linear change of coordinates
[x:y:z]\to[z:y:x]
.
Both curves are LNE in
{\bf P}^{2}
since they are non-singular and irreducible.
The affine traces w.r.t.
L
are

P\setminus L:=\{(x,y):y-x^{2}=0\}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;H\setminus L:=\{(x,y):xy-1=0\}.

By Theorem
8.1
, the hyperbola
H\setminus L
is LNE in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
while the parabola
P\setminus L
is not LNE in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
The difference being that
H,L
are in general position while

P,L
are not.

The next and penultimate result points at the very close interplay between
a projective curve
X
and its general affine traces while investigating
the LNE natures of
X
.

Theorem 9.4
.

Let
X
be a curve of
{\bf P}^{n}
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)
X
is LNE in
{\bf P}^{n}
.

(ii)
X
is connected and, at each singular point
\lambda
of
X
the germ
(
X,\lambda)
is a finite union of pairwise transverse non-singular curve germs.

(iii) For any hyperplane
H
of
{\bf P}^{n}
such that the pair
X,H
is in
general position, the variety
X\cup H
is LNE in
{\bf P}^{n}
.

(iv) For any hyperplane
H
of
{\bf P}^{n}
such that the pair
X,H
is in
general position, the affine curve
X\setminus H
is LNE in
\mathbb{C}_{H}^{n}
.

(v) There exists a hyperplane
H
of
{\bf P}^{n}
in general position with
X

such that the affine curve
X\setminus H
is LNE in
\mathbb{C}_{H}^{n}
.

Proof.

The equivalence
(i)
and
(ii)
is simply Proposition
4.6
.

Implication
(ii)\Longrightarrow(iii)
follows from Lemmas
3.1
and
2.6
.

To obtain
(iii)\Longrightarrow(i)
, note that if the union
X\cup H

is LNE in
{\bf P}^{n}
then it is locally LNE in
{\bf P}^{n}
at each singular point
of
X
. Thus Proposition
4.6
yields the implication.

To prove
(i)\Longrightarrow(iv)
, we observe that whenever
X
and
H

are in general position, the intersection
H\cap X_{\rm sing}
is empty, and
thus the affine trace
X\setminus H
is connected.
Therefore the affine trace
X\setminus H
is LNE in
\mathbb{C}_{H}^{n}

by point
(ii)
and Theorem
8.1
.

Implication
(iv)\Longrightarrow(v)
is obvious.

To end the proof, let us show
(v)\Longrightarrow(i)
.
Let
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}:=\mathbb{C}_{H}^{n}
and let
X^{a}:=X\setminus H
.
By hypothesis any point of
X\cap H
is a non-singular point of
X
,
therefore
X_{\rm sing}=(X^{a})_{\rm sing}
. Since
X^{a}
is LNE in
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
, it is
locally LNE in
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
at each of its singular points, and so the affine curve

X^{a}
contains a compact neighbourhood of each point of

which is

{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
. Thus

is locally LNE in
{\bf P}^{n}
at each of its singular
points. We conclude applying Proposition
4.6
.
∎

10.
Lipschitz classification of LNE curves

In this section we present a Lipschitz complete invariant of LNE affine curves.

Definition 10.1
.

Let
X_{1}^{a}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}}
and
X_{2}^{a}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n_{2}}
be two affine curves.

1) They are topologically equivalent if there exists a homeomorphism

X_{1}^{a}\to X_{2}^{a}
.

2) They are outer Lipschitz equivalent if there exist a
bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism

(X_{1}^{a},d_{X_{1}^{a}})\to(X_{2}^{a},d_{X_{2}^{a}}).

3) They are inner Lipschitz equivalent if there exist a
bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism

(X_{1}^{a},d_{\rm inn}^{X_{1}^{a}})\to(X_{2}^{a},d_{\rm inn}^{X_{2}^{a}}).

Obviously Lipschitz equivalence implies topological equivalence. Moreover,
for LNE curves outer Lipschitz equivalence is the same as inner
Lipschitz equivalence, thus in case of LNE curves we will speak simply of
Lipschitz equivalence. We will show that in fact for LNE curves all three
classifications coincide.

Let
X
be an irreducible curve of
{\bf P}^{n}
of degree
d\geq 1
.
For each
{\bf y}\in X
, let
c({\bf y})
be the number of
irreducible components of the curve germ
(X,{\bf y})
. The curve
X

is bi-rationally equivalent to a compact Riemann surface
S_{g}
, which is
a compact connected oriented surface of genus
g
.
Therefore the Euler Characteristic of
X^{a}\setminus X_{\rm sing}^{a}
satisfies
the following identity

(10.1)

\chi(X^{a}\setminus X_{\rm sing}^{a})=2-2g-\sum_{{\bf y}\in X_{\rm sing}^{a}}c({\bf y})-\sum_{\lambda\in X^{\infty}}c(\lambda).

Given the irreducible LNE curve
X^{a}
of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
with projective closure

X
, we define the following ordered object (encoding degree, number of
singular points, genus, and multiplicities at singular points)

DSGM(X):=\left(\deg(X);s(X);g(X);{({\bf y},c({\bf y}))}_{{\bf y}\in X_{\rm sing}}\right),

where
s(X):=\#X_{\rm sing}
and
g(X)
is the genus of the
surface
S_{g}
resolved from
X
. When
s(X)=0
,
we suppress the last entry, that is
DSGM(X)=(\deg(X),0,g(X))
. Two objects

DSGM(X_{i})=\left(d_{i};s_{i};g_{i};{({\bf y},c({\bf y}))}_{{\bf y}\in(X_{i})_{\rm sing}}\right),\;\;i=1,2,

are equivalent if
d_{1}=d_{2}
,
s_{1}=s_{2}=:s
,
g_{1}=g_{2}
, and there
exists a bijection
\varphi
of
\{1,\ldots,s\}
such that
c({\bf y}_{j}^{1})=c({\bf y}_{\varphi(j)}^{2})
for each
j=1,\ldots,s
, where
(X_{i})_{\rm sing}=\{{\bf y}_{1}^{i},\ldots,{\bf y}_{s}^{i}\}
for
i=1,2
.

Theorem 10.2
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

Two irreducible complex affine algebraic LNE curves are Lipschitz equivalent.

(b)

Two irreducible complex affine algebraic LNE curves are topologically equivalent.

(c)

The
DSGM
invariants of the two irreducible complex affine algebraic LNE curves are equivalent.

Proof.

Let
X_{i}^{a}
be an irreducible LNE curve of
\mathbb{C}^{n_{i}}
, for
i=1,2
. Let
X_{i}

be the projective closure of
X_{i}^{a}
in
{\bf P}^{n_{i}}
.
Denote

d_{i}:=\deg(X_{i}),\;\;Y_{i}:=(X_{i})_{\rm sing}=\{{\bf y}_{i}^{1},\ldots,{\bf y}_{i}^{s_{i}}\},\;\;{\rm and}\;\;X_{i}^{\infty}=\{\lambda_{i}^{1},\ldots,\lambda_{i}^{d_{i}}\},

since
X_{i}^{a}
is LNE by hypotheses implies that

\#X_{i}^{\infty}=d_{i}
.

For
{\bf y}_{i}\in Y_{i}
, let
T_{{\bf y}_{i}}X_{i}^{a}
be the tangent cone of

X_{i}^{a}
at
{\bf y}_{i}
.

Let
R\gg 1
be a large radius, as in the proof of Lemma

3.1
, such that for each

{\bf y}_{i}^{j}\in X_{i}^{a}
, with
i=1,2
and
j=1,\ldots,s_{i}
, the subset

\mathcal{C}_{i}^{j}:=X_{i}^{a}\cap B_{i}^{j}

is bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic to its truncated tangent cone

T_{{\bf y}_{i}^{j}}X_{i}^{a}\cap B_{i}^{j},

where
B_{i}^{j}
is either the open ball
B^{2n}({\bf y}_{i}^{j},R^{-1})
or its closure

{\bf B}^{2n}({\bf y}_{i}^{j},R^{-1})
.
In particular the boundary
{\partial}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{j}
is a finite union
of
c({\bf y}_{i}^{j})
embedded
{\bf S}^{1}
.
Similarly, for
i=1,2
and by Claim
6.3
,
once
R
is large enough, the subset

\mathcal{X}_{i}:=X_{i}^{a}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{i},

is bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic to the truncated cone

\widehat{X_{i}^{\infty}}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{i}=\left(\cup_{j=1}^{d_{i}}\mathbb{C}\lambda_{i}^{j}\right)\setminus\mathcal{B}_{i},

where
\mathcal{B}_{i}
is either the open ball
B_{R}^{2n}
or its closure

{\bf B}_{R}^{2n}
. The boundary
{\partial}\mathcal{X}_{i}
is a finite union
of
d_{i}
embedded
{\bf S}^{1}
.

Removing
N(\geq 1)
open (resp. closed) disks from
S_{g}
, whose
closures do not intersect, yields the compact (resp. open and bounded)
connected surface
S_{g,N}
with
N
boundary components.

For
i=1,2
, let

N_{i}^{a}:=d_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{s_{i}}c({\bf y}_{i}^{j})\;\;{\rm and}\;\;\mathcal{C}_{i}^{a}:=\mathcal{X}_{i}\cup\left(\cup_{j=1}^{s_{i}}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{j}\right).

Therefore the compact (resp. open and bounded) surface with
boundary
X_{i}^{a}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{i}^{a}
is smoothly diffeomorphic to
the compact (resp. open and bounded) surface with boundary

S_{g_{i},N_{i}^{a}}
.

Obviously, (a) implies (b). We will show that (b) implies (c) implies (a).

\bullet
Assume (b). Let
h:X_{1}^{a}\to X_{2}^{a}
be a homeomorphism.
Since
X_{i}^{a}
is LNE, a point
{\bf y}_{i}
of
X_{i}^{a}
is singular if and
only if
c({\bf y}_{i})\geq 2
. Since
h
maps each germ

(X_{1}^{a}\setminus{\bf y},{\bf y})\to(X_{2}^{a}\setminus h({\bf y}),h({\bf y}))

we deduce that

h(Y_{1})=Y_{2},\;\;{\rm and}\;\;c({\bf y})=c(h({\bf y}))\;\;\forall{\bf y}\in Y_{1}.

For
R
large enough, the number of connected components
X_{i}\setminus B_{R}^{2n}
are equal for
i=1,2
, that is
d_{1}=d_{2}
.
Thus we deduce that
g_{1}=g_{2}
by (
10.1
).
Condition (c) is then satisfied.

\bullet
Assume (c). Let
d:=d_{1}=d_{2}
and
g:=g_{1}=g_{2}
,
s:=s_{1}=s_{2}
.
For each
i=1,2
and each
j=1,\ldots,s
we can assume without
loss of generality that
c({\bf y}_{1}^{j})=c({\bf y}_{2}^{j})
.

We deduce
X_{1}^{a}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{1}^{a}

and
X_{2}^{a}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{2}^{a}
are both diffeomorphic to
S_{g,N^{a}}

for
N^{a}:=N_{1}^{a}=N_{2}^{a}
.

Since
X_{1}^{a}
and
X_{2}^{a}
are both LNE, we further deduce that

\mathcal{X}_{1}
and
\mathcal{X}_{2}
are bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic. So are
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{j}
and

\mathcal{C}_{2}^{j}
for each
j=1,\ldots,s
.

We can glue all these pieces together to
S_{g,N^{a}}=X_{i}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{i}^{a}

along the boundary
{\partial}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{a}
to produce a bi-Lipschitz
homeomorphism
(X_{1}^{a},d_{X_{1}^{a}})\to(X_{2}^{a},d_{X_{2}^{a}})
.
Thus (a) is verified.
∎

As a straightforward consequence of Theorem
10.2
we obtain the
full Lipschitz classification of LNE curves:

Corollary 10.3
.

Let
X_{1}
be a LNE complex algebraic curve of

\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}}
and
X_{2}
be a LNE complex algebraic curve of
\mathbb{C}^{n_{2}}
. Then

X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic if and only if they are
homeomorphic.

We obtain in our LNE setting the following

Corollary 10.4
.

Let
X^{a}
be a LNE curve of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
with
n\geq 3
. There exists a Zariski
dense open subset of linear projections
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
mapping
X^{a}
onto
a LNE curve of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Proof.

The linear projections
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
, identified with their kernels,
consist of the Grassmann manifold
{\bf G}(n-2,n)
of the
(n-2)
planes of

{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
. Consider the Gauss mapping of
X^{a}
:

\tau:X^{a}\setminus X_{\rm sing}\to{\bf G}(1,n),\;\;{\bf x}\to T_{\bf x}X.

The closure
G_{0}(X)
of

\tau(X^{a}\setminus X)
is either a curve of
{\bf G}(1,n)
or a
finite set of points. Let

G(X):=G_{0}(X)\cup\left(\cup_{\lambda\in X^{\infty}}\mathbb{C}\lambda\right)

Thus
G(X)
is a sub-variety of
{\bf G}(1,n)
of dimension
0
or
1
.
Consider the following sub-variety

V:=\{(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},P)\in G(X)\times G(X)\times{\bf G}(n-2,n):\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}\lambda+\mathbb{C}\lambda^{\prime}+P\leq n-1\}

We check that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V=2n-5
and that
W
, the Zariski closure of the
projection of
V
onto
{\bf G}(n-2,n)
, has also dimension
2n-5
.
Therefore

\Omega(X):={\bf G}(n-2,n)\setminus W

is Zariski open and dense. Let

\pi_{P}:{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}=P^{\perp}
be the orthogonal projection onto the
orthogonal
P^{\perp}
of
P\in{\bf G}(n-2,n)
. By definition of
\Omega(X)
, we
check that

X_{P}^{a}:=\pi_{P}(X^{a})

has exactly
d=deg(X^{a})
points at infinity at each of which
X_{P}^{a}

is transverse with the line at infinity. Therefore the degree of
X_{P}^{a}
is

d
. Let
{\bf z}
be a point of
X_{P,{\rm sing}}^{a}
and let

\pi_{P}^{-1}({\bf z})=\{{\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{q}\}
. For each
i=1,\ldots,q
, the
number of distinct lines in the tangent cone of
X
at
{\bf x}_{i}
is

c({\bf x}_{i})
.
The definition of
\Omega(X)
implies that the tangent cone of
X_{P}^{a}
at

{\bf z}
will consist of
\sum_{i=1}^{q}c({\bf x}_{i})
distinct lines. Since
X

is LNE and
P\in\Omega(X)
, the germ
(X_{P}^{a},{\bf z})
is a finite union of
pairwise transverse non-singular curve germs. Thus

X_{P}^{a}
is locally LNE at
{\bf z}
. Since
X^{a}
is connected so is
X_{P}^{a}
.
Theorem
8.1
shows that
X_{P}^{a}
is LNE.
∎

To end the section let us remark that the above classification allows more
types than represented by plane algebraic curves.

Corollary 10.5
.

There exist LNE curves of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
, with
n\geq 3
, which are not
Lipschitz equivalent to a plane curve.
In particular,
there are LNE curves of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
not Lipschitz equivalent
to any of their plane projections.

Proof.

Indeed, for each
n\geq 3
and each positive
integer
d
, there is a non-negative integer
p(d,n)
, polynomial in
d
,
such that for every
g
such that
0\leq g\leq p(d,n)
, there
is an irreducible non-singular (and non-degenerate) curve of
{\bf P}^{n}

with degree
d
and genus
g
(see
[
10
,
2
]
). Thus, there
exist non-singular space curves with
2g\neq(d-1)(d-2)
. The general
affine trace of such a projective curve is LNE by Theorem

9.4
. By Theorem
10.2
, such an affine trace
cannot be
Lipschitz equivalent to a non-singular plane curve, which must satisfy the
equality
2g=(d-1)(d-2)
. It can neither be equivalent to any of its
plane projections since the previous genus argument implies that these must
have singular points. The claim follows from Theorem
10.2
.
∎

Note that generic affine traces of non-singular irreducible projective
curves with genus zero and degree
d\geq 3
are the simplest examples of LNE
curves which are not Lipschitz equivalent to any plane curve, since
DSGM=(d,0,0)
cannot be realized by a plane curve, which is opposite to the
results of
[
22
,
7
]
in the local case.

A criterion for absolute continuity relative to the law of fractional Brownian motion
Xiyue Han, Alexander Schied

Abstract
Let X X be the sum of a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter H H and an absolutely continuous and adapted drift process. We establish a simple criterion that guarantees that the law of X X is absolutely continuous with respect to the law of the original fractional Brownian motion. For H < 1 / 2 H<1/2 , the trajectories of the derivative of the drift need to be bounded by an almost surely finite random variable; for H > 1 / 2 H>1/2 , they need to satisfy a Hölder condition with some exponent larger than 2 ​ H − 1 2H-1 . These are almost-sure conditions, and no expectation requirements are imposed. For the case in which X X arises as the solution of a nonlinear stochastic integral equation driven by fractional Brownian motion, we provide a simple criterion on the drift coefficient under which the law of X X is automatically equivalent to the one of fractional Brownian motion.



Keywords
: Absolute continuity of measures, fractional Brownian motion, fractional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, Kadota–Shepp Theorem

Introduction and statement of the main result

In recent decades, fractional Brownian motion has emerged as a fundamental mathematical tool for modeling scale-dependent random processes, exhibiting long-range dependence and self-similarity. Originating from the seminal work of Benoît Mandelbrot in the early 1960s, fractional Brownian motion has since captured the attention of researchers across numerous scientific disciplines due to its versatile applications in fields such as physics, finance, telecommunications, and image processing; see the monographs
[
2
,
8
,
12
,
14
,
16
]
and the references therein for a comprehensive account.

Fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
is a centered Gaussian stochastic process
W^{H}
with continuous sample paths and covariance function

{\mathbb{E}}[W^{H}_{s}W^{H}_{t}]=\frac{1}{2}\big(|s|^{2H}+|t|^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\big),\qquad s,t\geq 0,

on a given probability space
(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
. For
H=1/2
, we recover standard Brownian motion
W=W^{1/2}
as a special case.

A classical research question concerns the characterization of stochastic processes
X
whose law is absolutely continuous with respect to the one of
W^{H}
. Its solutions have numerous applications in the statistics of stochastic processes, the analysis of stochastic differential equations, and mathematical finance. A large portion of the two-volume monograph
[
10
,
11
]
by Liptser and Shiryaev is devoted to the study of this question for standard Brownian motion
W
. It applies to the setting in which
(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},({\mathscr{F}}_{t}),{\mathbb{P}})
is a probability space satisfying the usual conditions,
W
is a Brownian motion relative to the filtration
({\mathscr{F}}_{t})
, and
X
is an Itô process of the form

(1.1)

X_{t}=W_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\xi_{s}\,ds,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T,

for some progressively measurable process
\xi
with
\int_{0}^{T}\xi_{t}^{2}\,dt<\infty

{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. and some
T>0
. Then the traditional approach to absolute continuity uses Girsanov’s theorem to obtain a candidate for the density
\varphi
of the law of
X
with respect to the Wiener measure in the following exponential form,

(1.2)

\varphi=\exp\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\xi_{t}\,dW_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\xi_{t}^{2}\,dt\bigg).

The main difficulty with this approach is that it requires the verification of the condition
{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi]=1
, which can be quite hard in concrete applications. As long as one only needs absolute continuity of the law and not the specific form of the density, one can rely on the following remarkable result, which was first obtained by Kadota and Shepp
[
7
]
and later improved in
[
17
,
9
]
; it can also be found in
[
10
, Theorem 7.2]
. It is remarkable insofar as no integrability conditions with respect to the measure
{\mathbb{P}}
are imposed on the drift
\xi
.

Theorem 1.1

(Kadota and Shepp
[
7
]
)
.

If
X
is as in (
1.1
) and
\int_{0}^{T}\xi_{t}^{2}\,dt<\infty

{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s., then the law of
(X_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Wiener measure on
C[0,T]
.

Even more can be said in case
X
is a process satisfying the stochastic integral equation

(1.3)

X_{t}=W_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s,X)\,ds,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T,

where
\gamma
is progressively measurable with respect to the natural filtration of
W
. See
[
17
, Theorem 3]
or
[
10
, Theorem 7.7]
for a proof of the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(Yershov
[
17
]
)
.

If
X
as in (
1.3
) is adapted to
({\mathscr{F}}_{t})
, and
\int_{0}^{T}\gamma(t,X)^{2}\,dt
and
\int_{0}^{T}\gamma(t,W)^{2}\,dt
are both
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. finite, then the law of
(X_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
is equivalent to the one of
(W_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}

In the present paper, our goal is to obtain sufficient conditions for the absolute continuity of the stochastic process

(1.4)

X_{t}:=x_{0}+W^{H}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\xi_{s}\,ds,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T,

for the case in which the Hurst parameter
H
is not equal to
1/2
and
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Here, we assume that
\xi
is progressively measurable with respect to the filtration
({\mathscr{F}}_{t})
. If, in addition, the drift
\xi
is of the form
\xi_{t}=b(X_{t})
for some function
b:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, our aim is to establish sufficient conditions for the equivalence of the law of
X
to the one of
x_{0}+W^{H}
.
An important special case is the fractional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, which can be defined as the pathwise solution of the following integral equation

(1.5)

X_{t}=x_{0}+\rho\int_{0}^{t}(m-X_{s})\,ds+W^{H}_{t},\qquad t\geq 0,

where
\rho
and
m
are real parameters; see
[
4
]
.

There exists also a Girsanov-type theorem for fractional Brownian motion. It was developed by Norros, Valkeila, and Virtamo
[
13
]
and works by transforming
W^{H}
into a certain standard Brownian motion, to which one can then apply the traditional Girsanov theorem. However, in this context, verifying the condition
{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi]=1
for the corresponding stochastic exponential becomes even more involved, because the drift process also needs to be transformed in an intricate manner. For this reason, we believe that easily verifiable criteria for absolute continuity may come in handy in many applications.

Before stating our main results, however, we need to specify our requirements on the filtration
({\mathscr{F}}_{t})
, with respect to which both
W^{H}
and
\xi
are adapted. Recall that in the Brownian case,
H=1/2
, the condition required for
Theorem

1.1
is that
W
is a Brownian motion relative to
({\mathscr{F}}_{t})
. That is, the increment
W_{t}-W_{s}
is independent of
{\mathscr{F}}_{s}
for
0\leq s<t
. Since the increments of fractional Brownian motion
W^{H}
for
H\neq 1/2
are not independent, the preceding condition cannot be translated verbatim. Instead, we propose the following
Definition

1.3
, based on the process

(1.6)

\widetilde{M}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}s^{1/2-H}(t-s)^{1/2-H}\,dW^{H}_{s},\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.

It was shown in
[
13
]
that the stochastic integral on the right exists
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. in a pathwise sense and that
\widetilde{M}
is a Gaussian martingale and hence has independent increments. Clearly,
\widetilde{M}=W^{H}=W
for
H=1/2
.

Definition 1.3
.

We say that
W^{H}
is a
fractional Brownian motion relative to the filtration
({\mathscr{F}}_{t})
if
W^{H}
is adapted to
({\mathscr{F}}_{t})
and the increment
\widetilde{M}_{t}-\widetilde{M}_{s}
is independent of
{\mathscr{F}}_{s}
for
0\leq s<t
.

Throughout the remainder of the paper, we will always assume that
W^{H}
is a fractional Brownian motion relative to the given filtration
({\mathscr{F}}_{t})
. Since
it follows from
[
13
, Proposition 3.1]
that
W^{H}
is a fractional Brownian motion with respect to its natural filtration, the assumption includes the case in which
({\mathscr{F}}_{t})
is the natural filtration of
W^{H}
.
Now we can state our main result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be given by (
1.4
) and suppose the following assumption is satisfied.

•

If
H<1/2
, we assume that the function
t\mapsto\xi_{t}
is
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. bounded in the sense that there exists a finite random variable
C
such that
|\xi_{t}(\omega)|\leq C(\omega)
for a.e.
t
and
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.e.
\omega
.

•

If
H>1/2
, we assume that for
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s.
\omega
the function
t\mapsto\xi_{t}(\omega)
is Hölder continuous with some exponent
\alpha(\omega)>2H-1
.

Then the law of
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the law of
(x_{0}+W^{H}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
.

We emphasize that in
Theorem

1.4
no integrability conditions are imposed on
\xi
or the random variable
C
. Nevertheless, as illustrated by
Theorem

1.1
for
H=1/2
, the conditions in
Theorem

1.4
may not be optimal and can probably be improved. However, our goal was not to state and prove the most general theorem on absolute continuity, but to provide conditions for absolute continuity that can be verified with minimal effort and that apply in the most important applications. We believe this is the case for the conditions presented here, and we illustrate this point by means of the following example and the subsequent result.

Example 1.5
.

The motivation for this research originated in the context of
[
6
]
, where we prove the strong consistency of a statistical estimator that estimates the parameter
H
from discrete observations of the process

Y^{W^{H}}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}g(W^{H}_{s})\,ds,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T,

where
g\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
is strictly monotone. This problem arises from the desire to estimate the ‘roughness’ of a rough stochastic volatility model (see, e.g.,
[
5
]
) from its realized quadratic variation. Rough stochastic volatility models often involve an additional drift, i.e., they are based on
X
as in (
1.4
) or (
1.5
) instead of
W^{H}
and so one will observe the process

Y^{X}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}g(X_{s})\,ds,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.

If we know that the law of
X
is absolutely continuous with respect to the law of
W^{H}
for every given
H\in(0,1)
, then we can conclude immediately that the strong consistency of the estimator carries over from the process
Y^{W^{H}}
to the process
Y^{X}
. Clearly, analogous statements hold for other strongly consistent statistical estimators in the context of fractional Brownian motion; see, e.g.,
[
8
]
for examples.

The next theorem applies in particular to the fractional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process (
1.5
).
It is basically a corollary to
Theorem

1.2
and our proof of
Theorem

1.4
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that
X
solves the following integral equation,

(1.7)

X_{t}=x_{0}+W^{H}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s})\,ds,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T,

for some locally bounded and measurable function
b:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and that
X
is adapted to
({\mathscr{F}}_{t})
. For
H>1/2
we assume in addition that
b
is locally Hölder continuous with some exponent
\alpha>2-1/H
. Then the law of
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is equivalent to the law of
(x_{0}+W^{H}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
.

A criterion for the existence of the weak solution to the nonlinear integral equation (
1.7
) in the case
H\leq 1/2
is given in
[
1
]
.

Remark 1.7
.

As a matter of fact, in the context of
Theorem

1.6
it is even possible to provide a formula for the density between the two laws. This formula, however,
involves a number of quantities that will only be introduced in the proof of
Theorem

1.4
. For this reason, it is deferred to
Remark

Example 1.8
.

Cheridito
[
3
]
showed that a financial market model based on fractional Brownian motion with deterministic drift does not admit arbitrage if trading is restricted to a certain class of strategies with finitely many rebalancing times in
[0,T]
. Since the absence of arbitrage is preserved under an equivalent measure change,
Theorem

1.6
shows that this no-arbitrage result carries over to market models built on a process of the form (
1.7
).

2.1
Proof of
Theorem

1.4

In this section, we prove
Theorem

1.4
. We first note that we may clearly assume without loss of generality that
x_{0}=0
. Our approach to proving
Theorem

1.4
combines
Theorem

1.1
with
the transformation theory developed by Norros et al.
[
13
]
.
Drawing on the latter reference, we define the following constants,

c_{H}=\sqrt{\frac{2H\Gamma(3/2-H)}{\Gamma(H+1/2)\Gamma(2-2H)}},\quad c_{1}=\frac{1}{2H\Gamma(\frac{3}{2}-H)\Gamma(H+\frac{1}{2})},\quad c_{2}=\frac{c_{H}}{2H(2-2H)^{1/2}}.

We also let

\displaystyle w(t,s)

\displaystyle=c_{1}s^{1/2-H}(t-s)^{1/2-H},\qquad 0\leq s\leq t\leq T.

It is shown in
[
13
]
that the following integrals exist
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. in a pathwise sense,

(2.1)

Y_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}s^{1/2-H}\,dW^{H}_{s}\quad\text{and}\quad M_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}w(t,s)\,dW^{H}_{s}.

Note that
M=c_{1}\widetilde{M}
, where
\widetilde{M}
is as in (
1.6
).
Theorem 3.1 in
[
13
]
states that
M
is a continuous Gaussian martingale with quadratic variation
\langle M\rangle_{t}=c_{2}^{2}t^{2-2H}
. Since

\int_{0}^{t}(s^{H-1/2})^{2}\,d\langle M\rangle_{s}=c_{2}^{2}(2-2H)t=\frac{c_{H}^{2}t}{4H^{2}},

the process

(2.2)

B_{t}:=\frac{2H}{c_{H}}\int_{0}^{t}s^{H-1/2}\,dM_{s}

is a standard Brownian motion. Theorem 5.2 in
[
13
]
states that
W^{H}
can be recovered from
B
as follows,

(2.3)

W^{H}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\zeta(t,s)\,dB_{s},

where for
0\leq s<t\leq T
,

\zeta(t,s)=c_{H}\bigg[\Big(\frac{t}{s}\Big)^{H-1/2}(t-s)^{H-1/2}-(H-1/2)s^{1/2-H}\int_{s}^{t}u^{H-3/2}(u-s)^{H-1/2}\,du\bigg].

It follows that there is a one-to-one correspondence between
W^{H}
and
B
.
The proof of the identity (
2.3
) is based on the following two relations, which are derived in Theorem 3.2 and Remark 3.1 of
[
13
]
,

(2.4)

M_{t}=c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{1/2-H}\,dY_{s}\quad\text{and}\quad Y_{t}=2H\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{H-1/2}\,dM_{s}.

Our goal is to extend these arguments to the process
X
. We start with the following simple lemma, which extends
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
to the case of a constant,
{\mathscr{F}}_{0}
-measurable drift.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\xi_{0}
be a finite
{\mathscr{F}}_{0}
-measurable random variable and define

\varphi=\exp\Big(\xi_{0}M_{T}-\frac{\xi_{0}^{2}}{2}\langle M\rangle_{T}\Big).

Then
d{\mathbb{P}}_{\xi_{0}}:=\varphi\,d{\mathbb{P}}
defined a probability measure
{\mathbb{P}}_{\xi_{0}}
under which
W^{H}
is a fractional Brownian motion with constant drift
\xi_{0}
. That is, the law of
(W^{H}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
under
{\mathbb{P}}_{\xi_{0}}
is the same as that of
(W^{H}_{t}+\xi_{0}t)_{0\leq t\leq T}
under
{\mathbb{P}}
.

Proof.

Our assumption that
W^{H}
is a fractional Brownian motion with respect to
({\mathscr{F}}_{t})
implies that the increments of
M
, and hence
M
itself, are independent of
{\mathscr{F}}_{0}
. Thus,
\xi_{0}
and
M
are independent, which gives
{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi]={\mathbb{E}}[{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi|{\mathscr{F}}_{0}]]=1
. Thus,
{\mathbb{P}}_{\xi_{0}}
is indeed a probability measure.

Next, it follows from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) that
W^{H}
is independent of
{\mathscr{F}}_{0}
. Moreover, it was shown in
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
that the assertion holds if
\xi_{0}
is a constant. Thus, by putting these two facts together and conditioning on
{\mathscr{F}}_{0}
, one sees that the assertion also holds for
{\mathscr{F}}_{0}
-measurable
\xi_{0}
.
∎

The preceding lemma allows us to assume without loss of generality that
\xi_{0}=0

{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. To see why, we rewrite (
1.4
) as

(2.5)

X_{t}=\left(W^{H}_{t}+\xi_{0}t\right)+\int_{0}^{t}(\xi_{s}-\xi_{0})\,ds.

Lemma

2.1
implies that the law of
W^{H}
is equivalent to the one of
(W^{H}_{t}+\xi_{0}t)_{0\leq t\leq T}
. Since absolute continuity is a transitive relation, our claim follows.

In the sequel, we shall work in a strictly pathwise manner for a fixed sample path
\xi
that satisfies our assumptions. Under these assumptions, we may define in analogy to (
2.1
),

\eta_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}s^{1/2-H}\xi_{s}\,ds\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}w(t,s)\xi_{s}\,ds.

The function
\eta
is clearly absolutely continuous with progressively measurable derivative
\eta^{\prime}_{s}=s^{1/2-H}\xi_{s}
, and so we have

(2.6)

\mu_{t}=c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{1/2-H}s^{1/2-H}\xi_{s}\,ds=c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{1/2-H}\eta^{\prime}_{s}\,ds,

which is the analogue of the first relation in (
2.4
). An analogue of the second relation in (
2.4
) is proved in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem

1.4
and the additional assumption
\xi_{0}=0
, the function
t\mapsto\mu_{t}
is absolutely continuous with bounded and progressively measurable derivative
\mu^{\prime}_{t}
, and we have

(2.7)

\eta_{t}=2H\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{H-1/2}\mu^{\prime}_{s}\,ds.

Proof.

We start with the case
H<1/2
, in which the derivative
\eta^{\prime}
is bounded. Equation (
2.6
), our assumption
H<1/2
, and the boundedness of
\eta_{t}^{\prime}
imply that
t\mapsto\mu_{t}
is also absolutely continuous with bounded and progressively measurable derivative

\mu_{t}^{\prime}=c_{1}(1/2-H)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-H-1/2}\eta^{\prime}_{s}\,ds.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{H-1/2}\mu^{\prime}_{s}\,ds

\displaystyle=c_{1}(1/2-H)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{H-1/2}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-H-1/2}\eta^{\prime}_{r}\,dr\,ds

\displaystyle=c_{1}(1/2-H)\int_{0}^{t}\eta_{r}^{\prime}\int_{r}^{t}(t-s)^{H-1/2}(s-r)^{-H-1/2}\,ds\,dr.

After the change of variables
s=r+u(t-r)
, the inner integral becomes

(t-r)^{1+H-1/2-H-1/2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{H-1/2}u^{-H-1/2}\,du=\Gamma(1/2+H)\Gamma(1/2-H).

This gives (
2.7
)
in case
H<1/2
.

For
H>1/2
, recall that we assume that
\xi_{t}
is Hölder continuous with some exponent
\alpha>2H-1
.
Let
I_{0+}^{\beta}
denote the fractional Riemann–Liouville integral operator of order
\beta>0
, i.e.,

I_{0+}^{\beta}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f(s)}{(t-s)^{1-\beta}}\,ds,\qquad t\in(0,T],\ f\in L^{1}[0,T].

Furthermore, the fractional Riemann–Liouville derivative of order
\beta\in(0,1)
is given by

{\mathscr{D}}^{\beta}_{0+}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f(s)}{(t-s)^{\beta}}\,ds,\qquad 0<t<T,

provided the expression on the right makes sense.
When defining
\mu_{t}
as in (
2.6
) and letting
\widetilde{\xi}_{t}:=t^{1/2-H}\xi_{t}
, we can write

(2.8)

\mu_{t}=c_{1}\Gamma(3/2-H)I^{3/2-H}_{0+}\widetilde{\xi}(t).

By
Lemma

2.3
,
\widetilde{\xi}_{t}
is Hölder continuous with exponent
\lambda:=\alpha+1/2-H>H-1/2
. It follows from
[
15
, Lemma 13.2]
that
\widetilde{\xi}\in I_{0+}^{H-1/2}(L^{1}[0,T])
. In addition, (
2.8
) and
[
15
, Theorem 2.3]
yield that
\mu_{t}
is absolutely continuous. Its derivative is given by

(2.9)

\mu^{\prime}_{t}=c_{1}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\widetilde{\xi}_{s}}{(t-s)^{H-1/2}}\,ds=c_{1}\Gamma(3/2-H){\mathscr{D}}_{0+}^{H-1/2}\widetilde{\xi}(t).

To see that
\mu^{\prime}
is bounded, we use
[
15
, Lemma 13.1]
to conclude that there exists a function
\psi
that is Hölder continuous with exponent

such that

\mu^{\prime}_{t}=c_{1}\Gamma(3/2-H){\mathscr{D}}_{0+}^{H-1/2}\widetilde{\xi}(t)=\frac{c_{1}\widetilde{\xi}_{t}}{t^{H-1/2}}+\psi(t)=c_{1}\xi_{t}+\psi(t).

Hence
\mu^{\prime}_{t}
is Hölder continuous and in particular bounded. Moreover,
\mu^{\prime}_{t}
is adapted by (

) and, hence, progressively measurable due to its continuity.

By
[
15
, Theorem 2.4]
, we can recover

from

c_{1}\Gamma(3/2-H)\widetilde{\xi}_{t}=I^{H-1/2}_{0+}\mu^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(H-1/2)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{s}}{(t-s)^{3/2-H}}\,ds.

Integrating with respect to

and using Fubini’s theorem yields,

\displaystyle\eta_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{\xi}_{s}\,ds=\frac{1}{c_{1}\Gamma(3/2-H)\Gamma(H-1/2)}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\mu^{\prime}_{s}}{(r-s)^{3/2-H}}\,ds\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{1}\Gamma(3/2-H)\Gamma(H-1/2)}\cdot\frac{1}{H-1/2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{H-1/2}\mu^{\prime}_{s}\,ds.

This is (
2.7
) in our case

, because one checks that the constant in front of the integral is equal to
2H
.
∎

.

Next,
Lemma

2.2
implies that
\beta^{\prime}_{t}=t^{H-1/2}\mu^{\prime}_{t}
is progressively measurable and satisfies

(2.14)

\int_{0}^{T}(\beta^{\prime}_{t})^{2}\,dt=\frac{4H^{2}}{c_{H}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}t^{2H-1}(\mu^{\prime}_{t})^{2}\,dt<\infty,\quad\text{${\mathbb{P}}$-a.s.,}

because

The following elementary lemma was used in the proof.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
f:[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is Hölder continuous with exponent
\beta\in(0,1]
, satisfies
f(0)=0
, and
\alpha\in(0,\beta)
, then
\widetilde{f}(t):=t^{-\alpha}f(t)
is Hölder continuous with exponent
\beta-\alpha
.

Proof.

Let
c\geq 0
be such that
|f(t)-f(s)|\leq c|t-s|^{\beta}
for all
s,t\in[0,1]
. Let
0\leq s<t\leq T
be given. If
s=0
, then
\widetilde{f}(s)=0
and

\big|\widetilde{f}(t)-\widetilde{f}(s)\big|=t^{-\alpha}|f(t)|\leq ct^{\beta-\alpha}
.
Now suppose that
s>0
. Using the inequalities
|f(t)-f(s)|\leq c|t-s|^{\beta-\alpha}|t-s|^{\alpha}\leq c|t-s|^{\beta-\alpha}t^{\alpha}
and
|f(s)|=|f(s)-f(0)|\leq cs^{\beta}
yields

\displaystyle\big|\widetilde{f}(t)-\widetilde{f}(s)\big|

\displaystyle\leq t^{-\alpha}|f(t)-f(s)|+(t^{-\alpha}-s^{-\alpha})|f(s)|\leq c|t-s|^{\beta-\alpha}+c|t^{-\alpha}-s^{-\alpha}|s^{\beta}.

For the rightmost term, we have

\displaystyle|t^{-\alpha}-s^{-\alpha}|s^{\beta}=\alpha\int_{s}^{t}s^{\beta}r^{-\beta}r^{\beta-\alpha-1}\,dr\leq\alpha\int_{s}^{t}r^{\beta-\alpha-1}\,dr=\frac{\alpha}{\beta-\alpha}(t^{\beta-\alpha}-s^{\beta-\alpha})\leq\frac{\alpha}{\beta-\alpha}|t-s|^{\beta-\alpha}.

This completes the proof.∎

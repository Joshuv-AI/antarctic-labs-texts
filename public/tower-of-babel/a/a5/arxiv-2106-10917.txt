Multi-Lah numbers and multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind
Dae San KiM, Hye Kyung KiM, Taekyun Kim, Hyunseok Lee, SeongHo Park

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce multi-Lah numbers and multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind and recall multi-Bernoulli numbers, all of whose generating functions are given with the help of multiple logarithm. The aim of this paper is to study several relations among those three numbers. In more detail, we represent the multi-Bernoulli numbers in terms of the multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind and vice versa, and the multi-Lah numbers in terms of multi-Stirling numbers. In addition, we deduce a recurrence relation for multi-Lah numbers.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11B68; 11B73; 11B83

1.
Introduction

As is well known, the unsigned Stirling number
{n\brack r}
counts the number of permutations of a set with
n
elements which are products of
r
disjoint cycles. We generalize these numbers to the multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind
S_{1}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)
(see (
10
)) which reduce to the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind for
(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})=(1,1,\dots,1)
. Indeed,
S_{1}^{(1,1,\dots,1)}(n,r)={n\brack r}
.

It is also well known that the unsigned Lah numbers
L(n,k)
counts the number of ways of a set of
n
elements can be partitioned into
k
nonempty linearly ordered subsets. These numbers are generalized to the multi-Lah numbers
L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)
(see (
18
)) which reduce to the unsigned Lah numbers for
(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})=(1,1,\dots,1)
. In fact,
L^{(1,1,\dots,1)}(n,r)=L(n,r).

In addition, we need to recall the multi-Bernoulli numbers
B_{n}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}
(see (
9
)) which were introduced earlier under the different name the generalized Bernoulli numbers with order
r
in [7]. These numbers reduce to the Bernoulli numbers of order
r
up to some constants. Indeed, we see that
B_{m}^{(1,1,\dots,1)}=\frac{1}{r!}(-1)^{m}B_{m}^{(r)}
.

The common feature of those three kinds of numbers is that they are all defined with the help of the multiple logarithm
\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(z)
(see (
8
)), which reduce to the poly-logarithm
\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1}}(z)
, for
r=1
.

The aim of this paper is to study several relations among those three kinds of numbers. In more detail, we represent the multi-Bernoulli numbers in terms of the multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind and vice versa, and the multi-Lah numbers in terms of multi-Stirling numbers. Moreover, we deduce a recurrence relation for multi-Lah numbers. For the rest of this section, we recall the necessary facts that will be needed throughout this paper.

The unsigned Stirling numbers
L(n,k)
are defined by

(1)

\langle x\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,3,4,5,6]),

where
\langle x\rangle_{0}=1,\ \langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1),\ (n\geq 1),
and
(x)_{0}=1,\ (x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\ (n\geq 1)
.

The inverse formula of (
1
) is given by

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}L(n,k)\langle x\rangle_{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

From (
1
), we can derive the generating function of unsigned Lah numbers given by

(2)

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,3,8]).

Thus, we note that

(3)

L(n,k)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\frac{n!}{k!},\quad(n,k\geq 1),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,8]).

The Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(4)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5,8]),

and the Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(5)

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,3,8]).

From (
4
) and (
5
), we note that

(6)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{t}-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,4,6,8]),

and

(7)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8]).

For any
k_{i}\geq 1\ (1\leq i\leq r)
, and
|z|<1
, the multiple logarithm is defined by

(8)

\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(z)=\sum_{0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{r}}\frac{z^{m_{r}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,7]).

If
r=1
,
\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1}}(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{m}}{m^{k_{1}}}\displaystyle
is the poly-logarithm.

The multi-Bernoulli numbers, which are called the generalized Bernoulli numbers with order
r
in [7], are defined by

(9)

\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(z)}{z^{r}}\bigg|_{z=1-e^{-t}}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}}{m!}t^{m}.

From (
13
), we note that

B_{m}^{\overbrace{(1,1,\dots,1)}^{r-times}}=\frac{1}{r!}(-1)^{m}B_{m}^{(r)},\quad(m\geq 0,\ r\geq 1),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7]),

where
B_{n}^{(r)}
are the Bernoulli numbers of order
r
given by

\bigg(\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}\bigg)^{r}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(r)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,7,8]).

2.
Multi-Lah numbers and multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind

Now, we define the
multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind
by

(10)

\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(t)=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}S_{1}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
k_{i}\geq 1\ (1\leq i\leq r-1)
,
k_{r}\geq 2,\ and\ |t|<1
.

From (
8
), we note that

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(t)\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{d}{dt}\sum_{0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{r}}\frac{t^{m_{r}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{t}\sum_{0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{r}}\frac{t^{m_{r}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}m_{r}^{k_{r}-1}}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{t}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1},k_{r}-1}(t).

Let us take
k_{r}=1
in (
11
). Then we have

(12)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1},1}(t)\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{t}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1},0}(t)

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{t}\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}}\sum_{m_{r}=m_{r-1}+1}^{\infty}t^{m_{r}}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}}\frac{t^{m_{r-1}+1}}{1-t}\frac{1}{t}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{1-t}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1}}(t).

We claim that the following relations hold. For this, we only need to show the first equality which we prove by induction on
r
.

(13)

\mathrm{Li}_{\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-times}}(t)=\frac{1}{r!}\big(-\log(1-t)\big)^{r}=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-r}S_{1}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

If
r=1
, then
\mathrm{Li}_{1}(t)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{m}=-\log(1-t)
, as we wanted.
Assume that
r\geq 2
and that it holds for
r-1
. By (
12
) and induction hypothesis, we get

(14)

\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Li}_{\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-times}}(t)=\frac{1}{1-t}\mathrm{Li}_{\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{(r-1)-times}}(t)=\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\frac{1}{1-t}\big(-\log(1-t)\big)^{r-1}.

Now, by (
14
) we obtain

(15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-times}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{1-t}\big(-\log(1-t)\big)^{r-1}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\int_{0}^{-\log(1-t)}u^{r-1}du=\frac{1}{r!}\big(-\log(1-t)\big)^{r}.

Thus our proof is completed.
From (
10
), we note that

(16)

\mathrm{Li}_{\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-times}}(t)=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}S_{1}^{\overbrace{(1,1,\dots,1)}^{r-times}}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
13
) and (
16
), we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

For

n,r\geq 1
, we have

S_{1}^{\overbrace{(1,1,\dots,1)}^{r-times}}(n,r)=(-1)^{n-r}S_{1}(n,r)={n\brack r},

where
{n\brack r}
are the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind.

We observe that

(17)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(1-e^{-t})}{(1-e^{-t})^{r}}\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{(1-e^{-t})^{r}}\sum_{m=r}^{\infty}S_{1}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(m,r)\frac{1}{m!}\big(1-e^{-t}\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{(1-e^{-t})^{r}}\sum_{l=r}^{\infty}\sum_{m=r}^{l}(-1)^{m-l}S_{2}(l,m)S_{1}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(m,r)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{(-1)^{r}t^{r}}{(1-e^{-t})^{r}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=r}^{l+r}(-1)^{m-l}S_{2}(l+r,m)S_{1}^{(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})}(m,r)\frac{l!}{(l+r)!}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}B_{j}^{(r)}(-1)^{r-j}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=r}^{l+r}(-1)^{m-l}S_{2}(l+r,m)S_{1}^{(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})}(m,r)\frac{1}{r!\binom{l+r}{l}}\bigg)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sum_{m=r}^{l+r}\frac{\binom{n}{l}B_{n-l}^{(r)}(-1)^{n-r-m}}{r!\binom{l+r}{r}}S_{2}(l+r,m)S_{1}^{(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})}(m,r)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
9
) and (
17
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

For
k_{i}\geq 1\ (i=1,2,\dots,r),\ and\ n\geq 0
, we have

B_{n}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sum_{m=r}^{l+r}\frac{\binom{n}{l}B_{n-l}^{(r)}(-1)^{n-r-m}}{r!\binom{l+r}{r}}S_{2}(l+r,m)S_{1}^{(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})}(m,r).

For any integer
k_{i}\ (i=1,2,\dots,r)
, in the view of (
9
), we define
L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r),(n,r\geq 0)
, which are called
multi-Lah numbers
, as

(18)

\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(1-e^{-t})}{(1-t)^{r}}=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
13
), we note that

(19)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L^{\overbrace{(1,1,\dots,1)}^{r-times}}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{(1-t)^{r}}\mathrm{Li}_{\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-times}}\big(1-e^{-t}\big)

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{(1-t)^{r}}\frac{1}{r!}\Big(-\log\big(1-(1-e^{-t})\big)\Big)^{r}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{r!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)^{r}\ =\ \sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
19
), we get

L^{\overbrace{(1,1,\dots,1)}^{r-times}}(n,r)=L(n,r),\quad(n,r\geq 0).

For
n\geq 1
, from (
8
) and (
18
), we get

(20)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L^{(k_{1},\dots,k_{r-1},-k_{r})}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{r}}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r-1},-k_{r}}(1-e^{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{r}}\sum_{0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}}\sum_{m_{r}=m_{r-1}+1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-e^{-t})^{m_{r}}}{m_{r}^{-k_{r}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{r}}\sum_{0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}}\sum_{m_{r}=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-e^{-t})^{m_{r}+m_{r-1}}}{(m_{r}+m_{r-1})^{-k_{r}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{r}}\sum_{0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{(1-e^{t})^{m_{r-1}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}}\sum_{m_{r}=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m_{r}}m_{r}!}{(m_{r}+m_{r-1})^{-k_{r}}}\frac{1}{m_{r}!}\big(e^{-t}-1\big)^{m_{r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{r}}\sum_{0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{(1-e^{t})^{m_{r-1}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}}\sum_{m_{r}=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m_{r}}m_{r}!}{(m_{r}+m_{r-1})^{-k_{r}}}\sum_{l=m_{r}}^{\infty}S_{2}(l,m_{r})\frac{(-t)^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{r}}\sum_{0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{(1-e^{t})^{m_{r-1}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m_{r}=1}^{l}\frac{(-1)^{m_{r}+l}m_{r}!}{(m_{r}+m_{r-1})^{-k_{r}}}S_{2}(l,m_{r})\bigg)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-t}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m_{r}=1}^{l}(-1)^{m_{r}+l}m_{r}!S_{2}(l,m_{r})\sum_{j=0}^{k_{r}}\binom{k_{r}}{j}m_{r}^{k_{r}-j}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}\bigg)^{r-1}\sum_{0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{(1-e^{-t})^{m_{r-1}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}-j}}\bigg)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-t}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m_{r}=1}^{l}(-1)^{m_{r}+l}m_{r}!S_{2}(l,m_{r})\sum_{j=0}^{k_{r}}\binom{k_{r}}{j}m_{r}^{k_{r}-j}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}\bigg)^{r-1}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r-1}-j}(1-e^{-t})\bigg)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}.

From (
20
), we note that

(21)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1},-k_{r})}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{1-t}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m_{r}=1}^{l}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{r}}(-1)^{m_{r}+l}m_{r}!S_{2}(l,m_{r})\binom{k_{r}}{j}m_{r}^{k_{r}-j}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{m=r-1}^{\infty}L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-2},k_{r-1}-j)}(m,r-1)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\bigg)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{1}{1-t}\sum_{p=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=1}^{p+1-r}\binom{p}{l}\sum_{m_{r}=1}^{p}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{r}}(-1)^{m_{r}+l}m_{r}!m_{r}^{k_{r}-j}S_{2}(l,m_{r})\binom{k_{r}}{j}

\displaystyle\qquad\times L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-2},k_{r-1}-j)}(p-l,r-1)\bigg)\frac{t^{p}}{p!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{p=r}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{p+1-r}\binom{p}{l}\sum_{m_{r}=1}^{p}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{r}}(-1)^{m_{r}+l}m_{r}!m_{r}^{k_{r}-j}S_{2}(l,m_{r})\binom{k_{r}}{j}

\displaystyle\qquad\times L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-2},k_{r-1}-j)}(p-l,r-1)\frac{n!}{p!}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
21
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

For any
k_{i}\geq 1\ (i=1,2,\dots,r)
, and
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1},-k_{r})}(n,r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=r}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{p}\sum_{m_{r}=1}^{p}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{r}}(-1)^{m_{r}+l}m_{r}!m_{r}^{k_{r}-j}\binom{p}{l}\binom{k_{r}}{j}S_{2}(l,m_{r})L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-2},k_{r-1}-j)}(p-l,r-1)\frac{n!}{p!}.

Replacing
t
by
-\log(1-t)
in (
9
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{r}}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(t)\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(-1)^{m}\frac{1}{m!}\big(\log(1-t)\big)^{m}

(22)

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(-1)^{m}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,m)(-1)^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-m}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}S_{1}(n,m)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by (
10
), we get

(23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{r}}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(t)\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{t^{r}}\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}S_{1}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{1}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n+r,r)\frac{n!}{(n+r)!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{S_{1}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n+r,r)}{r!\binom{n+r}{n}}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
22
) and (
23
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For each
k_{i}\geq 1\ (i=1,2,\dots,r),\ n\geq 0,\,\,and\ r\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

S_{1}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n+r,r)=r!\binom{n+r}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}{n\brack m}.

Now, we observe that

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{r}}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(1-e^{-t})

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+j-1}{j}t^{j}\sum_{m=r}^{\infty}S_{1}^{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(m,r)\frac{1}{m!}\big(1-e^{-t}\big)^{m}

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+j-1}{j}t^{j}\sum_{l=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=r}^{l}S_{1}^{(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})}(m,r)(-1)^{m-l}S_{2}(l,m)\bigg)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=r}^{n}\sum_{m=r}^{l}S_{1}^{(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})}(m,r)(-1)^{m-l}S_{2}(l,m)\binom{r+n-l-1}{n-l}\frac{n!}{l!}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
24
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

For each
k_{i}\ (i=1,2,\dots,r),\,\,and\,\,n,r\in\mathbb{N}
, with
n\geq r
, we have

\displaystyle L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{l=r}^{n}\sum_{m=r}^{l}S_{1}^{(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})}(m,r)(-1)^{m-l}S_{2}(l,m)\binom{r+n-l-1}{n-l}\frac{n!}{l!}.

3.
Conclusion

There are various ways of studying special polynomials and numbers which include generating functions, combinatorial methods,
p
-adic analysis, umbral calculus, special functions, differential equations and probability theory. In this paper, using generating function method and by making use of the multiple logarithm, we studied three numbers, namely the multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind, the multi-Lah numbers and the multi-Bernoulli numbers which reduce respectively to the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind, the Lah numbers and the higher-order Bernoulli numbers up to constants when the index is specialized to
(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})=(1,1,\dots,1)
. We deduced several relations among those numbers. In more detail, we expressed the multi-Bernoulli numbers in terms of the multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind and vice versa, and the multi-Lah numbers in terms of multi-Stirling numbers. Further, we derived a recurrence relation for multi-Lah numbers.

It is our continuous interest to explore some special numbers and polynomials by using different tools like the ones mentioned in the above.

Studies of Z Z boson decay into double Υ \Upsilon mesons at the NLO QCD accuracy
Cong Li, Ying-Zhao Jiang, Zhan Sun

Abstract
In this paper, we employ the nonrelativistic QCD factorization to conduct a comprehensive examination of the Z Z boson decay into a pair of Υ \Upsilon mesons, achieving accuracy at the next-to-leading-order (NLO) in α s \alpha_{s} . Our calculations demonstrate that the QED diagrams are indispensable in comparison to the pure QCD diagrams, and the implementation of QCD corrections markedly enhance the QCD results, whereas it substantially diminish the QED results. To ensure consistency with the experimental methodology, we have taken into account the feed-down transitions originating from higher excited states, which exhibit significant relevance. Combining all the contributions, we arrive at the NLO prediction of ℬ Z → Υ ⁡ ( n ​ S ) + Υ ⁡ ( m ​ S ) ∼ 10 − 11 \mathcal{B}_{Z\to\Upsilon(nS)+\Upsilon(mS)}\sim 10^{-11} , which is notably lower than the upper limits set by CMS.

Calculation formalism

II.1
Theoretical Framework

As previously discussed, the CMS measurements have incorporated feed-down transitions; consequently, our calculations will assess the contributions stemming from higher excited states, including those of the
\Upsilon(mS)
and
\chi_{b}(nP)
mesons. Within the NRQCD framework
[
55
,
57
]
, the decay width of
Z\to H_{1}(b\bar{b})+H_{2}(b\bar{b})
can be factorized as

\displaystyle\Gamma=\hat{\Gamma}_{Z\to b\bar{b}[n_{1}]+b\bar{b}[n_{2}]}\langle\mathcal{O}^{H_{1}}(n_{1})\rangle\langle\mathcal{O}^{H_{2}}(n_{2})\rangle,

(2)

where
\hat{\Gamma}_{Z\to b\bar{b}[n_{1}]+b\bar{b}[n_{2}]}
represents the perturbative calculable short-distance coefficients (SDCs), denoting the production of the intermediate states consisting of
b\bar{b}[n_{1}]
and
b\bar{b}[n_{2}]
. With the restriction to color-singlet contributions, we have
n_{1}=^{3}S_{1}^{1}
and
n_{2}=^{3}S_{1}^{1}(\textrm{or}~^{3}P_{J}^{1})
(
J=0,1,2
).
1
1

1

When addressing the feed-down transitions, we exclude the evaluation of
Z\to\chi_{b}(nP)+\chi_{b}(n^{\prime}P)
. This process is anticipated to contribute
Z\to\Upsilon(mS)+\Upsilon(m^{\prime}S)
through a multi-step decay, which involves at least two decay stages.
The universal nonperturbative long distant matrix element (LDME)
\langle\mathcal{O}^{H_{1(2)}}(n_{1(2)})\rangle
stands for the probabilities of transitions from
b\bar{b}[n_{1(2)}]
into the
H_{1(2)}
meson states.

The
\hat{\Gamma}_{Z\to b\bar{b}[n_{1}]+b\bar{b}[n_{2}]}
can further be expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{Z\to b\bar{b}[n_{1}]+b\bar{b}[n_{2}]}=\frac{\kappa}{2m_{Z}}\frac{1}{N_{I}N_{s}}|\mathcal{M}|^{2},

(3)

where
|\mathcal{M}|^{2}
is the squared matrix elements,
1/N_{s}
is the spin average factor of the initial
Z
boson multiplied by the identity factor
N_{I}
of the two final
b\bar{b}
states. For
b\bar{b}[n_{1}]
and
b\bar{b}[n_{2}]
representing the same states,
N_{I}
is set to
1/2!
; however, if they are distinct states,
N_{I}
equals 1. The symbol
\kappa
denotes the factor originating from the standard two-body phase space.

Based on the framework used to handle
Z-J/\psi+J/\psi

[
40
]
, we include terms up to the
\alpha^{3}
order and achieve NLO accuracy in
\alpha_{s}
. The squared matrix elements specified in Eq. (
3
) can subsequently be expressed as follows

\displaystyle\bigg|\left(\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{s}}+\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha^{2}_{s}}\right)+\left(\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}\alpha_{s}}\right)\bigg|^{2}

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{s}}\big|^{2}+2\textrm{Re}\left(\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{s}}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha^{2}_{s}}\right)

\displaystyle+2\textrm{Re}\left(\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{s}}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)+2\textrm{Re}\left(\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{s}}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}\alpha_{s}}+\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha^{2}_{s}}\right)

\displaystyle+\big|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}}\big|^{2}+2\textrm{Re}\left(\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}\alpha_{s}}\right)+\cdots.

Accordingly, we decompose the SDCs into three distinct components,

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}=\hat{\Gamma}^{(0,1)}_{1}+\hat{\Gamma}^{(0,1)}_{2}+\hat{\Gamma}^{(0,1)}_{3},

(5)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}^{(0)}_{1}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{s}}\big|^{2},

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{1}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle 2\textrm{Re}\left(\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{s}}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha^{2}_{s}}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}^{(0)}_{2}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle 2\textrm{Re}\left(\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{s}}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{2}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle 2\textrm{Re}\left(\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{s}}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}\alpha_{s}}+\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha^{2}_{s}}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}^{(0)}_{3}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}}\big|^{2},

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{3}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle 2\textrm{Re}\left(\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}\alpha_{s}}\right).

(6)

The subscripts 1, 2, and 3 represent the order in
\alpha

0(1)
denotes the terms at the LO (or NLO) level in
\alpha_{s}
.

II.2.2
NLO

We utilize the dimensional regularization with
D=4-2\epsilon
to isolate the ultraviolet (UV) and infrared (IR) divergences. The on-mass-shell (OS) scheme is employed to set the renormalization constants for the
b
-quark mass (
Z_{m}

Z_{2}

\overline{MS}

Z_{g}

Z_{3}

\displaystyle\delta Z_{m}^{OS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-3C_{F}\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}N_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\textrm{UV}}}+\frac{4}{3}+2\textrm{ln}{2}\right],

\displaystyle\delta Z_{2}^{OS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-C_{F}\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}N_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\textrm{UV}}}+\frac{2}{\epsilon_{\textrm{IR}}}+4+6\textrm{ln}{2}\right],

\displaystyle\delta Z_{3}^{\overline{MS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}(\beta_{0}-2C_{A})N_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\textrm{UV}}}+\textrm{ln}\frac{4m_{b}^{2}}{\mu_{r}^{2}}\right],

\displaystyle\delta Z_{g}^{\overline{MS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta_{0}}{2}\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}N_{\epsilon}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\textrm{UV}}}+\textrm{ln}\frac{4m_{b}^{2}}{\mu_{r}^{2}}\right],

IV
Summary

In this paper, we carry out a comprehensive study of
Z\to\Upsilon(mS)+\Upsilon(nS)
utilizing the NRQCD framework at the QCD NLO accuracy. The LO results indicate that the contributions from QED diagrams significantly enhance those from the QCD processes. The QCD corrections substantially amplify the QCD results, while concurrently reducing the QED results. Additionally, we discover that the transitions involving higher excited states of bottomonium mesons exert an indispensable influence. Taking into account both the QCD and QED contributions, we estimate the branching ratio for
\mathcal{B}_{Z\to\Upsilon(mS)+\Upsilon(nS)}
to be on the order of
10^{-11}
. This value is significantly lower than the upper limit established by CMS and aligns with experimental observations.

Double Schubert polynomials do have saturated Newton polytopes
Federico Castillo, Yairon Cid-Ruiz, Fatemeh Mohammadi, Jonathan Montaño

Abstract
. We prove that double Schubert polynomials have the Saturated Newton Polytope property. This settles a conjecture by Monical, Tokcan and Yong. Our ideas are motivated by the theory of multidegrees. We introduce a notion of standardization of ideals that enables us to study non-standard multigradings. This allows us to show that the support of the multidegree polynomial of each Cohen-Macaulay prime ideal in a non-standard multigrading, and in particular, that of each Schubert determinantal ideal is a discrete polymatroid.

13H15, 14M15, 14C17, 52B40

1.
Introduction

Schubert polynomials are classical and important objects in algebraic combinatorics.
They were introduced by Lascoux and Schützenberger
[
18
]
to study the cohomology classes of Schubert varieties.
Since then, Schubert polynomials have played a fundamental role in algebraic combinatorics (see, e.g.,
[
9
,
20
,
1
,
3
,
14
,
15
,
17
]
and the references therein).

We first recall the definition of Schubert polynomials.
Let
\mathscr{S}_{p}
be the symmetric group on the set
[p]=\{1,\ldots,p\}
.
For every
i\in[p-1]=\{1,\ldots,p-1\}
we have the transposition
\sigma_{i}=(i,i+1)\in\mathscr{S}_{p}
. Recall that the set
\mathfrak{T}=\{\sigma_{i}\mid 1\leq i<p\}
generates
\mathscr{S}_{p}
.
The
length

\ell(\pi)
of a permutation
\pi
is the least amount of elements in
\mathfrak{T}
counting repetitions needed to obtain
\pi
from the identity permutation.
The permutation
\pi_{0}=(p,p-1,\ldots,2,1)
(in one-line notation) is the
longest permutation and has length
\frac{p(p-1)}{2}
.
We follow the notation of
[
15
]
and
[
19
, Chapter 15]
to present permutations and Schubert polynomials.

Definition 1.1
.

The
double Schubert polynomial

{\mathfrak{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})\in\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p},s_{1},\dots,s_{p}]
of a permutation
\pi\in\mathscr{S}_{p}
is defined recursively in the following way.
First we define
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\pi_{0}}=\prod_{i+j\leq p}(t_{i}-s_{j})
, and for any permutation
\pi
and transposition
\sigma_{i}
with
\ell(\sigma_{i}\pi)<\ell(\pi)
we set

{\mathfrak{S}}_{\sigma_{i}\pi}\,=\,\dfrac{{\mathfrak{S}}_{\pi}-\sigma_{i}{\mathfrak{S}}_{\pi}}{t_{i}-t_{i+1}},

where
\mathscr{S}_{p}
acts only on
\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
by permutation of variables.
The
(ordinary) Schubert polynomial

{\mathfrak{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{0})\in\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
is obtained from
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\pi}
by setting each variable
s_{j}
equal to
0
.

The monomial expansion of ordinary Schubert polynomials has been combinatorially analyzed using different objects such as compatible sequences
[
2
]
, reduced pipe dreams
[
10
,
1
]
, and Kohnert diagrams
[
16
]
.
The description using pipe dreams also works for the double Schubert polynomials
[
19
, Corollary 16.30]
.
We also have a formula for double Schubert polynomials using bumpless pipe dreams
[
17
]
.

Following
[
20
]
, we say that a polynomial
f=\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}}c_{{\mathbf{n}}}{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
has the
Saturated Newton Polytope property
(SNP property for short) if the support
{\text{supp}}(f)=\{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\mid c_{\mathbf{n}}\neq 0\}
of
f
is equal to
\text{Newton}(f)\cap\mathbb{N}^{n}
, where
\text{Newton}(f)=\text{ConvexHull}\{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\mid c_{\mathbf{n}}\neq 0\}
denotes the
Newton polytope
of
f
; in other words, if the support of
f
consists of the integer points of a polytope.

The main goal of this paper is to confirm the following challenging conjecture by Monical, Tokcan and Yong that appeared in
[
20
, Conjecture 5.2]
.

Conjecture 1.2

(
[
20
]
)
.

Double Schubert polynomials have the Saturated Newton Polytope property.

We confirm the conjecture by proving a stronger result that the support of each double Schubert polynomial is a discrete polymatroid.
A
discrete polymatroid

\mathcal{P}
on
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
is a collection of points in
\mathbb{N}^{n}
of the following form

\mathcal{P=}\Big\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\;\mid\;\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}x_{j}\leq r({\mathfrak{J}}),\;\forall{\mathfrak{J}}\subsetneq[n],\;\sum_{i\in[n]}x_{i}=r([n])\Big\}

with
r
being a rank function on
[n]
. A
rank function on

[n]

is a function
r:2^{[n]}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
satisfying the following three properties: (i)
r(\emptyset)=0
, (ii)
r({\mathfrak{J}}_{1})\leq r({\mathfrak{J}}_{2})
if
{\mathfrak{J}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathfrak{J}}_{2}\subseteq[n]
, and (iii)
r({\mathfrak{J}}_{1}\cap{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})+r({\mathfrak{J}}_{1}\cup{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})\leq r({\mathfrak{J}}_{1})+r({\mathfrak{J}}_{2})
if
{\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2}\subseteq[n]
.

The following is the main theorem of this article.

Theorem A
.

Let
\pi\in\mathscr{S}_{p}
be a permutation and
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})\in\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p},s_{1},\dots,s_{p}]
be the corresponding double Schubert polynomial.
Then, the support
{\text{supp}}({\mathfrak{S}}_{\pi})\subset\mathbb{N}^{2p}
of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\pi}
is a discrete polymatroid on
[2p]=\{1,\ldots,2p\}
.
In particular, the statement of
Section 1
holds.

Our approach to prove
Theorem A
can be summarized in the following quote by Miller and Sturmfels
[
19
, Introduction to Chapter 15]
:

“We consider the finest possible multigrading, which demands the refined toolkit
of a new generation of combinatorialists.”

More precisely, we utilize the result that double Schubert polynomials equal the multidegree polynomial of Schubert determinantal ideals with the aforementioned
“finest possible multigrading”
(see
[
19
, Theorem 15.40]
), and then we develop a method of
standardization of ideals
.
This process of standardization allows us to study multidegrees in certain non-standard multigradings by reducing the problem to a standard multigraded setting.
Our main tool is
Theorem 2.2
from
[
4
]
which shows that the support of the multidegree polynomial of any multihomogeneous prime ideal (with usual standard multigrading) is a discrete polymatroid.
Here we extend this theorem to the family of non-standard multigradings that we study.

Much interest has been paid to the important conjectures proposed in
[
20
]
and a number of them have already been confirmed (see
[
9
]
).
Therefore,
Theorem A
settles a hitherto remaining conjecture from
[
20
]
and gives further evidence to the ubiquity of the SNP property in many “combinatorially defined polynomials”.
Theorem A
also gives more evidence for the presence of the
Lorentzian
property in double Schubert polynomials as conjectured in
[
13
]
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. We review the notion of multidegrees in
Section 2
and recall the connection between double Schubert polynomials and Schubert determinantal ideals in
Section 3
.

Section 4
contains our main results, in particular the proof of
Theorem A
.

2.
A short recap on multidegrees

In this short section, we briefly recall the notion of multidegrees and some of its basic properties; for more details the reader is referred to
[
19
,
6
]
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring (for now, we do not assume the grading to be positive).
Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded module and
F_{\bullet}
be a
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded free
R
-resolution

F_{\bullet}:\;\cdots\rightarrow F_{i}\rightarrow F_{i-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}

of
M
.
Let
t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}
be variables over
\mathbb{Z}
and consider the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
, where the variable
t_{i}
corresponds with the
i
-th elementary vector
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
.
If we write
F_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}R(-\mathbf{b}_{i,j})
with
\mathbf{b}_{i,j}=(\mathbf{b}_{i,j,1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{i,j,p})\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
, then we define the Laurent polynomial

\left[F_{i}\right]_{\mathbf{t}}\,:=\,\sum_{j}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{b}_{i,j}}=\sum_{j}t_{1}^{\mathbf{b}_{i,j,1}}\cdots t_{p}^{\mathbf{b}_{i,j,p}}.

Then, the
K-polynomial
of
M
is defined by

\mathcal{K}(M;{\mathbf{t}})\,:=\,\sum_{i}{(-1)}^{i}\left[F_{i}\right]_{\mathbf{t}}.

It turns out that, even if the grading of
R
is non-positive and we do not have a well-defined notion of Hilbert series, the above definition of K-polynomial is an invariant of the module
M
and it does not depend on the chosen free
R
-resolution
F_{\bullet}
(see
[
19
, Theorem 8.34]
).

Definition 2.1
.

The
multidegree polynomial
of a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
R
-module
M
is the homogeneous polynomial
\mathcal{C}(M;{\mathbf{t}})\in\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]
given as the sum of all terms in

\mathcal{K}(M;\mathbf{1}-{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{K}(M;1-t_{1},\ldots,1-t_{p})

having total degree
{\text{codim}}(M)=n-\dim(M)
.

One case of particular interest is when
R
is a standard multigraded polynomial ring.
We say that
R
is
standard
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
if the total degree of each variable
x_{i}
is equal to one (i.e., for each
1\leq i\leq n
, we have
\deg(x_{i})={\mathbf{e}}_{k_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
with
1\leq k_{i}\leq p
).
The study of standard multigraded algebras is of utmost importance as they correspond with closed subschemes of a product of projective spaces (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
and the references therein).
Since the coefficients of the multidegree polynomial are non-negative in the standard multigraded case, it becomes natural to address the positivity of these coefficients.
For each subset
\mathfrak{J}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subseteq[p]=\{1,\ldots,p\}
denote by
R_{({\mathfrak{J}})}
the
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra given by

R_{({\mathfrak{J}})}:=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\geq 0,\ldots,i_{p}\geq 0\\
i_{j}=0\text{ if }j\not\in{\mathfrak{J}}\end{subarray}}{\left[R\right]}_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{p})},

and for any
R
-homogeneous ideal
I\subset R
we define
I_{({\mathfrak{J}})}
as the contraction
I_{({\mathfrak{J}})}:=I\cap R_{({\mathfrak{J}})}
. The following theorem completely characterizes the positivity of multidegrees and is our main tool to prove
Theorem A
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring.
Let
I\subset R
be an
R
-homogeneous prime ideal.
Write the multidegree polynomial of
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})
as

\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|{\mathbf{n}}|={\text{codim}}(I)\end{subarray}}c_{{\mathbf{n}}}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}\;\in\;\mathbb{N}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}].

Then, for all
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|={\text{codim}}(I)=n-\dim(R/I)
, we have that
c_{\mathbf{n}}>0
if and only if for each
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subseteq[p]
the inequality

n_{j_{1}}+\cdots+n_{j_{k}}\,\geq\,{\text{codim}}\left(I_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\right)

holds.
Furthermore, the support of
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})
is a discrete polymatroid.

Proof.

Consider the standard
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring
R^{\prime}=R[x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{n+p}]
with
\deg(x_{n+i})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
and notice that
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{C}(R^{\prime}/IR^{\prime};{\mathbf{t}})
.
Thus we assume that
I
is a relevant prime (i.e.,
I\not\supset\bigoplus_{i_{1}\geq 1,\ldots,i_{p}\geq 1}{\left[R\right]}_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{p})}
), and so
\text{MultiProj}(R/I)\neq\emptyset
.
We embed
X=\text{MultiProj}(R/I)
as a closed subscheme of a multiprojective space
{\mathbb{P}}:={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
From
[
4
, Remark 2.9]
we have that
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\text{supp}}(\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}}))
if and only if
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}}}(X)>0
where
{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}}=(m_{1}-n_{1},\ldots,m_{p}-n_{p})
.
Then,
[
4
, Theorem A]
implies that
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\text{supp}}(\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}}))
if and only if
|{\mathbf{n}}|={\text{codim}}(I)
and
\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}n_{j}\geq{\text{codim}}\left(I_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\right)
for each
{\mathfrak{J}}\subseteq[p]
.
Equivalently, we obtain that
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\text{supp}}(\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}}))
if and only if
|{\mathbf{n}}|={\text{codim}}(I)
and

\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}n_{j}\,\leq\,{\text{codim}}(I)-{\text{codim}}\left(I_{([p]\setminus{\mathfrak{J}})}\right)\,=\,\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}m_{j}+r([p]\setminus{\mathfrak{J}})-r([p])

for each
{\mathfrak{J}}\subseteq[p]
, where
r:2^{[p]}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is the rank function
r({\mathfrak{J}}):=\dim\big(\text{MultiProj}\big(R_{({\mathfrak{J}})}/I_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\big)\big)
(see
[
4
, Proposition 5.1]
).
Finally, we can check that
s:2^{[p]}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
with
s({\mathfrak{J}}):=\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}m_{j}+r([p]\setminus{\mathfrak{J}})-r([p])
is a rank function (see, e.g.,
[
21
, §44.6f]
), and so it follows that
{\text{supp}}(\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}}))
is a polymatroid.
∎

3.
Schubert determinantal ideals

Here we recall the connection between double Schubert polynomials and Schubert determinantal ideals (for more details, the reader is referred to
[
19
, Chapters 15, 16]
).

First, we define matrix Schubert varieties and Schubert determinantal ideals by following
[
19
, Chapter 15]
.
Let
\mathbb{k}
be an algebraically closed field and
M_{p}(\mathbb{k})
be the
\mathbb{k}
-vector space of
p\times p
matrices with entries in
\mathbb{k}
.
As an affine variety we define its coordinate ring as
\widetilde{R}=\mathbb{k}[x_{i,j}\mid(i,j)\in[p]\times[p]]
.
Furthermore, we consider a
(\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p})
-grading on
\widetilde{R}
by setting
\deg(x_{i,j})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\oplus-{\mathbf{e}}_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p}
, where
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
denotes the
i
-th elementary vector.

Definition 3.1

(see
[
19
, Chapter 15]
)
.

Let
\pi
be a permutation matrix.
The
matrix Schubert variety

\overline{X_{\pi}}\subset M_{p}(\mathbb{k})
is the subvariety given by

\overline{X_{\pi}}=\{Z\in M_{p}(\mathbb{k})\mid\text{rank}(Z_{m\times n})\leq\text{rank}(\pi_{m\times n})\;\text{ for all }\;m,n\},

where
Z_{m\times n}
is the restriction to the first
m
rows and
n
columns.
The
Schubert determinantal ideal

I_{\pi}\subset\widetilde{R}
is the
\widetilde{R}
-homogeneous ideal generated by all minors in
\mathbf{X}_{m\times n}
of size
1+\text{rank}(\pi_{m\times n})
for all
m
and
n
, where
\mathbf{X}=(x_{i,j})
is the
p\times p
matrix with the variables of
\widetilde{R}
.

The following theorem collects several results of fundamental importance to our approach.
In particular, it shows that double Schubert polynomials equal the multidegree polynomial of matrix Schubert varieties.
To define multidegrees over
\widetilde{R}
with its
(\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p})
-grading, we consider the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}}]=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p},s_{1},\ldots,s_{p}]
, where
t_{i}
has degree
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\oplus\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p}
and
s_{i}
has degree
\mathbf{0}\oplus{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\pi\in\mathscr{S}_{p}
be a permutation and denote also by
\pi
the corresponding permutation matrix.
Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

I_{\pi}
is a prime ideal, and so it coincides with the ideal
I(\overline{X_{\pi}})
of polynomials vanishing on the matrix Schubert variety
\overline{X_{\pi}}
.
(
[
11
]
,
[
19
, Corollary 16.29]
)

(ii)

\widetilde{R}/I_{\pi}
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring.
(
[
11
]
,
[
19
, Corollary 16.44]
)

(iii)

{\mathfrak{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{s})=\mathcal{C}(\widetilde{R}/I_{\pi};{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
.
(
[
8
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
19
, Theorem 15.40]
)

The next technical lemma will allow us to substitute the grading of
\widetilde{R}
which has negative components for the degrees of the variables.
Let
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{i,j}\mid(i,j)\in[p]\times[p]]
with induced
(\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p})
-grading by setting
\deg(x_{i,j})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\oplus{\mathbf{e}}_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p}
.
As for
\widetilde{R}
, define multidegrees over
R
in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}}]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
I\subset\widetilde{R}
be an
\widetilde{R}
-homogeneous ideal, and denote also by
I
the corresponding
R
-homogeneous ideal in
R
.
Then we have

\mathcal{C}(\widetilde{R}/I;t_{1},\ldots,t_{p},s_{1},\ldots,s_{p})=\mathcal{C}(R/I;t_{1},\ldots,t_{p},-s_{1},\ldots,-s_{p}).

Proof.

Notice that, if
\widetilde{F}_{\bullet}
is a
(\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p})
-graded free
\widetilde{R}
-resolution of
\widetilde{R}/I
with
\widetilde{F}_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}\widetilde{R}(-\mathbf{a}_{i,j},-\mathbf{b}_{i,j})
, then there is a corresponding
(\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p})
-graded free
R
-resolution
F_{\bullet}
of
R/I
with
F_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}R(-\mathbf{a}_{i,j},\mathbf{b}_{i,j})
.
By definition, this yields the equality of
K
-polynomials

\mathcal{K}(\widetilde{R}/I;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})=\mathcal{K}(\widetilde{R}/I;t_{1},\ldots,t_{p},s_{1},\ldots,s_{p})=\mathcal{K}(R/I;t_{1},\ldots,t_{p},s_{1}^{-1},\ldots,s_{p}^{-1})=\mathcal{K}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}}^{\mathbf{-1}}).

From
[
19
, Claim 8.54]
, we have
\mathcal{K}(R/I;\mathbf{1-t},\mathbf{1-s})=\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})+Q({\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
, where
Q({\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
is a polynomial with terms of degree at least
{\text{codim}}(I)+1
.
Equivalently, we get
\mathcal{K}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})=\mathcal{C}(R/I;\mathbf{1-t},\mathbf{1-s})+Q(\mathbf{1-t},\mathbf{1-s})
.
It then follows that

\mathcal{K}(\widetilde{R}/I;\mathbf{1-t},\mathbf{1-s})=\mathcal{C}(R/I;t_{1},\ldots,t_{p},1-\tfrac{1}{1-s_{1}},\ldots,1-\tfrac{1}{1-s_{p}})+Q(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p},1-\tfrac{1}{1-s_{1}},\ldots,1-\tfrac{1}{1-s_{p}}).

By expanding the right hand side of the above equality, the result of the lemma is obtained. ∎

4.
Standardization of ideals

In this section, we develop a process of standardization of ideals in a certain non-standard multigrading.
This process will allow us to show that the support of the multidegree polynomial of any Cohen-Macaulay prime ideal is a discrete polymatroid in the non-standard multigradings that we consider.
The following setup is used throughout this section.

Setup 4.1
.

Let
p\geq 1
be a positive integer and
\mathbb{k}
be a field.
Let
R
and
S
be the polynomial rings
R=\mathbb{k}[{\mathbf{x}}]
and
S=\mathbb{k}[\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}]
over the set of variables
{\mathbf{x}}=\{x_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq p}
,
\mathbf{w}=\{w_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq p}
and
\mathbf{z}=\{z_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq p}
.
We consider
R
and
S
as
(\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p})
-graded rings by setting that

\deg(x_{i,j})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\oplus{\mathbf{e}}_{j},\quad\deg(w_{i,j})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\oplus\mathbf{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\deg(z_{i,j})=\mathbf{0}\oplus{\mathbf{e}}_{j},

where
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
denotes the
i
-th elementary vector and
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
denotes the zero vector.
We define the
\mathbb{k}
-algebra homomorphism

\phi:R=\mathbb{k}[{\mathbf{x}}]\longrightarrow S=\mathbb{k}[\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}],\quad\phi(x_{i,j})=w_{i,j}z_{i,j}.

For an
R
-homogeneous ideal
I\subset R
, we say that the extension
\phi(I)S
is the
standardization
of
I
, as
\phi(I)S
is an
S
-homogeneous ideal in the standard multigraded polynomial ring
S
.
Let
{\mathbf{t}}=\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}\}
and
{\mathbf{s}}=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{p}\}
be variables indexing the
(\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p})
-grading, where
t_{i}
corresponds with
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\oplus\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p}
and
s_{i}
corresponds with
\mathbf{0}\oplus{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p}
.
Given a finitely generated graded
R
-module
M
and a finitely generated graded
S
-module
N
, by a slight abuse of notation, we consider both multidegrees
\mathcal{C}(M;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
and
\mathcal{C}(N;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
as elements of the same polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}}]=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p},s_{1},\ldots,s_{p}]
.

First, we show some basic properties of the process of standardization.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume
Section 4
.
Let
I\subset R
be an
R
-homogeneous ideal and
J=\phi(I)S
be its standardization.
Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

{\text{codim}}(I)={\text{codim}}(J)
.

(ii)

\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})=\mathcal{C}(S/J;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
.

(iii)

If
R/I
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring, then
S/J
also is.

(iv)

Let
>
be a monomial order on
R
and
>^{\prime}
be a monomial order on
S
which is compatible with
\phi
(i.e., if
f,g\in R
with
f>g
, then
\phi(f)>^{\prime}\phi(g)
).
Then
{\text{in}}_{>^{\prime}}(J)=\phi({\text{in}}_{>}(I))S
.

Proof.

Let
T
be the polynomial ring
T=\mathbb{k}[{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}]\cong R\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}S
with its natural
(\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p})
-grading induced from the ones of
R
and
S
.
We now think of
R
and
S
as subrings of
T
.
Consider the quotient ring
T/IT
and notice that
\{x_{i,j}-w_{i,j}z_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq p}
is a regular sequence of homogeneous elements over
T/IT
.
We also have the following natural isomorphism

\frac{T}{IT+\left(\{x_{i,j}-w_{i,j}z_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq p}\right)}\;\cong\;S/J.

As the natural inclusion
R\hookrightarrow T
is a polynomial extension, we have that
\dim(T/IT)=\dim(R/I)+\dim(S)=\dim(R)+\dim(S)-{\text{codim}}(I)
and that
T/IT
is Cohen-Macaulay when
R/I
is.
So, by cutting out with the regular sequence described above, we obtain that

\dim(S/J)=\dim(T/IT)-\dim(R)=\dim(S)-{\text{codim}}(I)

and that
S/J
is Cohen-Macaulay when
T/IT
is.
This completes the proofs of parts (i) and (iii).

Let
F_{\bullet}:\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{2}}F_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}F_{0}
be a graded free
R
-resolution of
R/I
.
Since
\{x_{i,j}-w_{i,j}z_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq p}
is a regular sequence on both
T
and
T/IT
, it follows that
{\text{Tor}}_{k}^{T}\left(T/IT,T/(\{x_{i,j}-w_{i,j}z_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq p})\right)=0
for all
k>0
, and so
G_{\bullet}=F_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}T/(\{x_{i,j}-w_{i,j}z_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq p})
provides (up to isomorphism) a graded free
S
-resolution of
S/J
.
The identification of
G_{\bullet}
as a resolution of
S
-modules is the same as
\phi(F_{\bullet})
(more precisely,
G_{\bullet}
has the same shiftings as
F_{\bullet}
in the
(\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p})
-grading and the
i
-th differential matrix of
G_{\bullet}
is given by the substitution
\phi(f_{i})
of
f_{i}
).
Therefore, by definition, we obtain the equality
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})=\mathcal{C}(S/J;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
that shows part (ii).

To show part (iv) we can use Buchberger’s algorithm (see, e.g.,
[
7
, Chapter 15]
).
Indeed, we can perform essentially the same steps of the algorithm in a set of generators of
I
and the corresponding set of generators for
J
.
∎

The following theorem provides the main result of this section.
It shows that the support of the multidegree polynomial is a discrete polymatroid for Cohen-Macaulay prime ideals in
R
.
The proof is carried out by performing a standardization process that allows us to invoke
Theorem 2.2
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume
Section 4
.
Let
I\subset R
be an
R
-homogeneous Cohen-Macaulay prime ideal.
Then, the support of the multidegree polynomial
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
is a discrete polymatroid.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\{(i,j)\mid x_{i,j}\in I\}
be the set of indices such that the corresponding variable belongs to
I
.
We consider the polynomial rings

R^{\prime}=\mathbb{k}[x_{i,j}\mid(i,j)\not\in\mathcal{L}]\subset R

and

S^{\prime}=\mathbb{k}[w_{i,j},z_{i,j}\mid(i,j)\not\in\mathcal{L}]\subset S.

Let
I^{\prime}\subset R^{\prime}
be the (unique) ideal that satisfies the condition
I=I^{\prime}R+\left(x_{i,j}\mid(i,j)\in\mathcal{L}\right)
.
By construction we have that
x_{i,j}\not\in I^{\prime}
for all
x_{i,j}\in R^{\prime}
.
Since
R/I\cong R^{\prime}/I^{\prime}
, it follows that
I^{\prime}
is also a Cohen-Macaulay prime ideal.

Let
J^{\prime}=\phi(I^{\prime}R)S\cap S^{\prime}
.
For any
w_{i,j}z_{i,j}\in S^{\prime}
,
Section 4
(i) and the fact that the corresponding
x_{i,j}
does not belong to the prime
I^{\prime}
imply that

{\text{codim}}(J^{\prime}S+w_{i,j}z_{i,j}S)={\text{codim}}(I^{\prime}R+x_{i,j}R)={\text{codim}}(I^{\prime}R)+1={\text{codim}}(J^{\prime}S)+1.

By
Section 4
(iii),
S/J^{\prime}S
is Cohen-Macaulay, and so it necessarily follows that
w_{i,j}z_{i,j}
is a non-zero-divisor over
S/J^{\prime}S
for all
w_{i,j}z_{i,j}\in S^{\prime}
.
Consequently, we obtain that
S^{\prime}/J^{\prime}
is a domain if and only if
\left(S^{\prime}/J^{\prime}\right)_{\prod w_{i,j}z_{i,j}}
is a domain.
Let
B=\mathbb{k}[w_{i,j},z_{i,j},z_{i,j}^{-1}\mid(i,j)\not\in\mathcal{L}]
and consider the automorphism given by

\psi:B\rightarrow B,\quad w_{i,j}\mapsto\frac{w_{i,j}}{z_{i,j}},\quad z_{i,j}\mapsto{z_{i,j}}.

The ideal
\psi(J^{\prime}B)
coincides with the extension of
I^{\prime}
in
B
under the ring homomorphism
R^{\prime}\rightarrow B,x_{i,j}\mapsto w_{i,j}
, and so it follows that
\psi(J^{\prime}B)
and, consequently,
J^{\prime}B
are prime ideals.
We then conclude that
J^{\prime}
is a prime ideal.

Since the variables
x_{i,j}
with indices in
\mathcal{L}
form a regular sequence over
R/I^{\prime}R
, we obtain the equation

(1)

\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})=\prod_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{L}}(t_{i}+s_{j})\cdot\mathcal{C}(R/I^{\prime}R;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})

(see, e.g.,
[
19
, Exercise 8.12]
).
To conclude the proof, it is now sufficient to show that the support of
\mathcal{C}(R/I^{\prime}R;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
is a discrete polymatroid; indeed, we would obtain that the support of
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
is a Minkowski sum of a finite number of discrete polymatroids which in turn is also a discrete polymatroid by
[
21
, Corollary 46.2c]
.
Finally, this condition follows by applying
Theorem 2.2
to the prime ideal
J^{\prime}S
and exploiting the equality
\mathcal{C}(R/I^{\prime}R;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})=\mathcal{C}(S/J^{\prime}S;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
from
Section 4
(ii).
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this paper.

Proof of
Theorem A
.

As we already have all the necessary ingredients, the proof follows straightforwardly by combining
Theorem 3.2
,
Section 3
and
Theorem 4.3
.
∎

Furthermore, we determine the defining inequalities of the discrete polymatroids in
Theorem 4.3
and, accordingly, in
Theorem A
.
Similarly to
Section 2
, for any two subsets
{\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2}\subseteq[p]
, we denote by
R_{({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})}\subseteq R
and
S_{({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})}\subseteq S
the
(\mathbb{Z}^{|{\mathfrak{J}}_{1}|}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{|{\mathfrak{J}}_{2}|})
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebras obtained by restricting to the positions in
{\mathfrak{J}}_{1}
for the first part
\mathbb{Z}^{p}\oplus\mathbf{0}
of the grading, and to the ones in
{\mathfrak{J}}_{2}
for the second part of the grading
\mathbf{0}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{p}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume
Section 4
.
Let
I\subset R
be an
R
-homogeneous Cohen-Macaulay prime ideal.
Then, we have that the coefficient of
\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{r}}\mathbf{s}^{\mathbf{c}}=t_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{r_{p}}s_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots s_{p}^{c_{p}}
is nonzero in
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
if and only if

(i)

\sum_{j\in[p]}r_{j}+\sum_{j\in[p]}c_{j}={\text{codim}}(I)

(ii)

For every
\mathfrak{J}_{1},\mathfrak{J}_{2}\subseteq[p]
, we have that
\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{J}_{1}}r_{j}+\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{J}_{2}}c_{j}\geq{\text{codim}}\big(I_{({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})}\big)
, where
I_{({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})}
is the contracted ideal
I_{({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})}=I\cap R_{({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})}
.

Proof.

By
Theorem 4.3
, we know that the Newton polytope of
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
is a base polymatroid polytope, and so, under the condition
\sum_{j\in[p]}r_{j}+\sum_{j\in[p]}c_{j}={\text{codim}}(I)
, all its defining inequalities are of the form

(2)

\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{J}_{1}}r_{j}+\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{J}_{2}}c_{j}\geq C({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2}),

for some constant
C({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})
that depends on the subsets
{\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2}\subseteq[p]
.
We now determine
C({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})
.

We keep the same notation of the proof of
Theorem 4.3
, in particular,
I=I^{\prime}R+\left(x_{i,j}\mid(i,j)\in\mathcal{L}\right)
.
Equation
(1)
decomposes the Newton polytope of
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
as the Minkowski sum of the Newton polytopes of
\prod_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{L}}(t_{i}+s_{j})
and
\mathcal{C}(S/J^{\prime}S;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
, both of which are also base polymatroid polytopes.
So, we analyze the minimum of the sum in Equation
(2)
with the two contributions.

(a)

\textrm{Newton}(\prod_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{L}}(t_{i}+s_{j}))
is determined by the equality
\sum_{j\in[p]}r_{j}+\sum_{j\in[p]}c_{j}=|\mathcal{L}|
and the inequalities
\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{J}_{1}}r_{j}+\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{J}_{2}}c_{j}\geq\big|\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{L}\mid i\in{\mathfrak{J}}_{1}\text{ and }j\in{\mathfrak{J}}_{2}\}\big|
.

(b)

Due to
Theorem 2.2
,
\textrm{Newton}(\mathcal{C}(S/J^{\prime}S;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}}))
is determined by the equality
\sum_{j\in[p]}r_{j}+\sum_{j\in[p]}c_{j}={\text{codim}}(J^{\prime}S)
and the inequalities
\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{J}_{1}}r_{j}+\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{J}_{2}}c_{j}\geq{\text{codim}}\big({(J^{\prime}S)}_{({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})}\big)
, where
{(J^{\prime}S)}_{({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})}
is the contracted ideal
{(J^{\prime}S)}_{({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})}=J^{\prime}S\cap S_{({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})}
.

Notice that
Section 4
(i) yields the equality

{\text{codim}}\big(I_{({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})}\big)={\text{codim}}\big(J_{({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})}\big)={\text{codim}}\big({(J^{\prime}S)}_{({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})}\big)+\big|\{(i,j)\in\mathcal{L}\mid i\in{\mathfrak{J}}_{1}\textrm{ and }j\in{\mathfrak{J}}_{2}\}\big|.

Therefore, since we can split the value of
C({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})
in terms of the sum of the defining inequalities of the Newton polytopes of
\prod_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{L}}(t_{i}+s_{j})
and
\mathcal{C}(S/J^{\prime}S;{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
, it follows that
C({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})={\text{codim}}\big(I_{({\mathfrak{J}}_{1},{\mathfrak{J}}_{2})}\big)
.
This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

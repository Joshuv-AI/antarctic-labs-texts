Embeddings and Integrable Charges for Extended Corner Symmetry
Luca Ciambelli, Robert G. Leigh, Pin-Chun Pai

Abstract
We revisit the problem of extending the phase space of diffeomorphism-invariant theories to account for embeddings associated with the boundary of sub-regions. We do so by emphasizing the importance of a careful treatment of embeddings in all aspects of the covariant phase space formalism. In so doing we introduce a new notion of the extension of field space associated with the embeddings which has the important feature that the Noether charges associated with all extended corner symmetries are in fact integrable, but not necessarily conserved. We give an intuitive understanding of this description. We then show that the charges give a representation of the extended corner symmetry via the Poisson bracket, without central extension.

Classical gravitational theories are gauge theories for which the principal symmetry is diffeomorphism invariance.
The study of symmetry in gauge theories has a long history going back to the seminal work of Noether

In this letter, we will show how to extend the field space of diffeomorphism-invariant theories such that the entire extended corner symmetry is realized by integrable charges. This is achieved by introducing the same careful treatment of embeddings into the covariant phase space methodology, which leads to a natural extension of the symplectic structure of the theory which differs from that introduced in
Donnelly and Freidel 2016

We stress that integrability is achieved on the entire field space (on-shell) without a need for first setting some flux to zero. The existing literature contains many examples where integrability is achieved only in this sense.
Here we propose a universal resolution and the consequences are far-reaching: we find that the algebra of charges associated with the full extended corner symmetry (and thus any of its subalgebras appropriate to any particular physical situation) is represented in terms of Poisson brackets on the extended phase space, without central extension.
As a further consequence, the flux is accounted for fully by the field-space contraction of the Hamiltonian vector field associated to the symmetry and the extended pre-symplectic potential.

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\Sigma}\phi^{*}_{2}\Big(i_{\underline{\eta}}\theta+i_{\underline{\chi}}I_{V_{\underline{\eta}}}\theta+\tfrac{1}{2}i_{\underline{\eta}}i_{\underline{\chi}}L-\tfrac{1}{2}i_{\underline{\chi}}i_{\underline{\eta}}L\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\Sigma}\phi^{*}_{2}\Big(i_{\underline{\eta}}\theta+i_{\underline{\chi}}(dQ_{\underline{\eta}}+\tfrac{1}{2}i_{\underline{\eta}}L)+\tfrac{1}{2}i_{\underline{\eta}}i_{\underline{\chi}}L\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\Sigma}\phi^{*}_{2}\Big(i_{\underline{\eta}}\theta+i_{\underline{\chi}}dQ_{\underline{\eta}}\Big)

and so

\displaystyle I_{V_{\underline{\eta}}}\Omega^{ext.}_{\Sigma}\hat{=}-\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{*}_{1}\Big(\delta dQ_{\underline{\eta}}+{\cal L}_{\underline{\chi}}dQ_{\underline{\eta}}\Big)=-\delta\int_{\partial\Sigma}\phi^{*}_{2}\Big(Q_{\underline{\eta}}\Big)

where we made use of eq. (
12
) in reverse. Thus the charge is integrable, and we write

\displaystyle I_{V_{\underline{\eta}}}\Omega^{ext.}_{\Sigma}=-\delta\int_{\partial\Sigma}\phi^{*}_{2}(Q_{\underline{\eta}})=-\delta H_{\underline{\eta}}

(22)

Thus
V_{\underline{\eta}}
is the Hamiltonian vector field on field space corresponding to the Hamiltonian function
H_{\underline{\eta}}
, the latter coinciding precisely with that appearing in (
4
). Continuing in the same vein, it can be shown that

\displaystyle I_{V_{\underline{\xi}}}I_{V_{\underline{\eta}}}\Omega^{ext.}_{\Sigma}=-I_{V_{\underline{\xi}}}\delta\int_{\partial\Sigma}\phi^{*}_{2}(Q_{\underline{\eta}})=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\phi^{*}_{2}(Q_{[\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta}]})=H_{[\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta}]}

which coincides with the result of
Ciambelli and Leigh 2021
.
Thus we have the Poisson bracket

\displaystyle\Big\{H_{\underline{\xi}},H_{\underline{\eta}}\Big\}=\delta_{\underline{\xi}}H_{\underline{\eta}}=-H_{[\underline{\xi},\underline{\eta}]}.

(23)

This proves that the charges are canonically represented, given the choice (
17
). This is achieved without any restriction on the field space, without boundary conditions being imposed and without involvement of ambiguities that would alter the definition of the Noether charge.

Finally we note that
\Theta^{ext.}_{\Sigma}
correctly accounts for flux. Consider for example a ‘pillbox’ with spacelike boundaries
\Sigma_{1,2}
which have boundaries
S_{1,2}
and a timelike boundary
B
(whose boundary is
S_{1}\cup S_{2}
). Then

\displaystyle H_{\underline{\xi}}\Big|_{S_{2}}-H_{\underline{\xi}}\Big|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B}\phi^{*}_{1}(dQ_{\underline{\xi}})

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B}\phi^{*}_{1}\Big(I_{V_{\underline{\xi}}}\theta-i_{\underline{\xi}}L\Big)

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{V_{\underline{\xi}}}\int_{B}\phi^{*}_{1}\Big(\theta+i_{\underline{\chi}}L\Big)

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{V_{\underline{\xi}}}\Theta_{B}^{ext.}

(27)

where we used again our definition (
17
) applied to
B
. Thus the flux of the extended symplectic form precisely accounts for possible non-conservation of the charges. This applies to all vector fields
\underline{\xi}
, whether it be tangential or orthogonal.

III.1
Comparison to Previous Treatments

In the usual treatment, where embeddings are not considered at all, one has
\Theta_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}\theta
and
I_{V_{\underline{\eta}}}\Omega_{\Sigma}=-\int_{\partial\Sigma}\Big(\delta Q_{\underline{\eta}}-i_{\underline{\eta}}\theta\Big)
as above. Thus, one says that the charge is integrable if
i_{\underline{\eta}}\theta
is either zero, or is set to zero by imposing some condition. Note that it is automatically zero if
\underline{\eta}
is tangent to
\partial\Sigma
, but not if
\underline{\eta}
is normal.
In fact, this is precisely the problem that we have resolved by including the embedding – the charges are then integrable for all
\underline{\eta}
.

Finally we consider the relationship of our treatment to that of
Donnelly and Freidel 2016
;
Speranza 2018
. In fact, they chose a different extension by making use of an ambiguity in the pre-symplectic form

\displaystyle\Theta^{DF}_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta^{ext.}_{\Sigma}+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\phi_{2}^{*}\Big(Q_{\underline{\chi}}\Big)

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\phi_{1}^{*}\Big(\theta+i_{\underline{\chi}}L+dQ_{\underline{\chi}}\Big).

(29)

This has the property that
I_{V_{\underline{\eta}}}\Theta^{DF}_{\Sigma}=0
, and thus all diffeomorphism charges have been made to vanish. However, as we have seen, it is the pre-symplectic structure
\Theta^{ext.}_{\Sigma}
that gives rise to the actual Noether charges that generate the extended corner symmetry.

IV
Conclusions

In this letter, we have emphasized the need for an interpretation in which physical symmetries are associated with corners and we have found the description of field space that gives rise to that interpretation. We plan to follow up this work with a more extensive account in which we study many of the examples of current interest and extend the construction to field-dependent vector fields and the corresponding modified brackets. For example, one may anticipate that our formalism has important implications when applied to the case of black hole horizons as well as asymptotic structures. The latter case in particular requires modifications to account for renormalization, which is currently under investigation.

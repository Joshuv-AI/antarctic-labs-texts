Generalized Hukuhara Weak Subdifferential and its Application on Identifying Optimality Conditions for Nonsmooth Interval-valued Functions
Suprova Ghosh, Debdas Ghosh

Abstract
In this article, we introduce the idea of g ​ H gH -weak subdifferential for interval-valued functions (IVFs) and show how to calculate g ​ H gH -weak subgradients. It is observed that a nonempty g ​ H gH -weak subdifferential set is closed and convex. In characterizing the class of functions for which the g ​ H gH -weak subdifferential set is nonempty, it is identified that this class is the collection of g ​ H gH -lower Lipschitz IVFs. In checking the validity of sum rule of g ​ H gH -weak subdifferential for a pair of IVFs, a counterexample is obtained, which reflects that the sum rule does not hold. However, under a mild restriction on one of the IVFs, one-sided inclusion for the sum rule holds. Next, as applications, we employ g ​ H gH -weak subdifferential to provide a few optimality conditions for nonsmooth IVFs. Further, a necessary optimality condition for interval optimization problems with difference of two nonsmooth IVFs as objective is established. Lastly, a necessary and sufficient condition via augmented normal cone and g ​ H gH -weak subdifferential of IVFs for finding weak efficient point is presented.

Introduction

Interval arithmetic of Moore
Moore1966
is the foundation stone in interval analysis. Realistic applicability of Moore’s method is relevant till today. We can currently discover several papers in the community of interval-valued optimization problems (IOPs) where Moore’s interval analysis is applied extensively. To find optimality conditions for IOPs, ideas of derivatives for interval-valued function (IVF) have been proposed
chalco2013
;
markov
;
Osuna
;
stefanini2009
;
Ghosh2019derivative
. In
markov
, the concept of
gH
-differentiability for IVFs was introduced.
Chalco-Cano
chalco2019
addressed the algebraic property of
gH
-differentiable interval-valued functions. Ghosh et al.
Ghosh2019derivative
proved the existence of
gH
-directional derivative for convex IVFs and presented optimality conditions for IOPs.

It is a familiar fact that in nonsmooth optimization, the classical gradient algorithm fails: even in finding the optimum point, as there is no derivative, the conventional optimality condition
\nabla f(x)=0
does not hold. More crucially, it is observed that optima of an almost everywhere differentiable function categorically arise at nondifferentiable points—for instance, take the minimization of
f(x)=|x|
. The notion of subdifferential, defined by Rockafellar
rockafellar
, is a crucial factor in the body of optimization theory that perfectly replaces the role of the gradient to identify optima for convex functions. However, subdifferential is inadequate in developing optimality conditions for nonconvex optimization problems. Due to this insufficiency, the idea of subdifferential has been generalized. Most common of such generalizations is weak subdifferential
azimov
. Based on this notion, a strong duality theorem for nonconvex inequality constrained problem has been found by defining a weak conjugate function
Yalcin
. A substantial application of this notion in duality theory with the help of a weak subdifferentiable perturbation function is given in
gasimov
.

In the context of nonsmooth calculus for nondifferentiable convex IVFs, Ghosh et al.
Ghosh2020lasso
has recently proposed the idea of
gH
-subgradient and
gH
-subdifferential. The same article
Ghosh2020lasso
found that
gH
-directional derivative is the maximum of all the products of the direction and
gH
-subgradients. Afterward, Anshika et al.
anshika
characterized weak efficiency for nonconvex composite optimization problems with the subdifferential sets of convex interval-valued functions. In
anshika
, by formulating supremum and infimum of an IVF, a Fermat-type, a Fritz-John-type, and a KKT-type weak efficiency condition for nonsmooth IOPs have been derived. Anshika et al.
anshika2
introduced
gH
-subdifferential of interval-valued value function. Furthermore, Chauhan et al.
chauhan2021generalized
derived the notion of
gH
-Clarke derivative for IVFs and IOPs. Under the Clarke subdifferentiablility assumption, Chen and Li
chen
provided KKT conditions for efficient solutions. Additionally, Karaman et al.
karaman
presented two subdifferentials for interval-valued functions and some optimality criteria, which were obtained by using subdifferentials.

From the available literature on nonsmooth IOPs, it is found that the study of
gH
-weak subdifferential notion has not yet been addressed. However, the notion of
gH
-weak subdifferential might be effective to characterize and capture the efficient solutions of IOPs with nonconvex and nonsmooth IVFs. By using subgradient, one may face difficulties to solve problem which does not satisfy convexity assumption because subgradient refers to the slope of a supporting hyperplane to the graph of convex functions in convex analysis. Thus, in this study, we introduce the notion of weak subgradient, which does not need any kind of convexity.

In this article, we attempt to show various properties of weak-subdifferential and its use in nonsmooth nonconvex IOPs. As an application of the proposed
gH
-weak subdifferential, we propose a necessary and sufficient optimality condition for finding weak efficient points of difference of two IVFs.

The rest of article is presented as follows. Section
2
is devoted to the conventional properties of intervals, followed by calculus of IVFs. Section
3
introduces the notion of
gH
-weak subdifferential for IVFs and discusses their properties such as convexity, closedness and nonemptiness. Additionally, the role of
gH
-weak subdifferential to derive the necessary condition for weak efficiency for
gH
-weak subdifferentiable IVFs is shown in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we analyze the necessary condition for obtaining efficient solution of difference of two IVFs. Finally, we draw conclusion with future directions to extend the present study.

Example 1

Definition 15

(

{\bf{\Phi}}

at

.

Theorem 3.2

\left[-\frac{1}{2}y-y\right]&~\text{if}~~y\in[-1,0]\end{cases}~\text{ and }~{\bf{\Phi}}_{2}(y)=[y^{2},-y+3],

respectively.
For these two functions, the

-weak subdifferential at

\displaystyle\partial^{w}{\bf{\Phi}}_{1}(0)\oplus\partial^{w}{\bf{\Phi}}_{2}(0)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\{({\textbf{H}^{w}},c)\in I(\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}_{+}:\left[-2,-\tfrac{1}{2}\right]\preceq{\textbf{H}^{w}}\oplus c,~{\textbf{H}^{w}}\ominus_{gH}c\preceq\left[-2,\tfrac{1}{2}\right]~\forall~y\in[-1,1]\}.

(25)

{\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)\in\partial^{-}_{\mathscr{F}}{\bf{\Psi}}(u)
and this clearly yields

\displaystyle\textbf{0}={\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)^{\top}\odot(y-u)\preceq{{\bf{\Psi}}}(y)\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Psi}}(u)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle{\bf{\Psi}}(u)\preceq{{\bf{\Psi}}}(y)~\text{by (ii) of Lemma 2.1}~\text{in \cite[cite]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{Ghosh2019derivative}{\@@citephrase{(}}{\@@citephrase{)}}}},

and this means that
u
is weak efficient point of
T
.

Theorem 3.9

Let
\emptyset\neq\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
{\bf{\Psi}}
is
gH
-Fréchet differentiable at
u
, then
{\bf{\Psi}}
is
gH
-weak subdifferentiable at
u
if and only if
{\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)
is
gH
-weak subdifferentiable at
0\in\mathcal{Y}
, and

\partial^{w}({\bf{\Psi}}(u))=\partial^{w}({\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)(0)).

Proof

By the
gH
-Fréchet differentiability of
{\bf{\Psi}}
at
u
, we have

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lVert h\rVert\to 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\lVert h\rVert}\odot\{({\bf{\Psi}}(u+h)\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Psi}}(u))\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)^{\top}\odot h\}=\textbf{0}.

Inserting
h=\lambda\odot(y-u)
, by
gH
-weak subdifferentiability of
{\bf{\Psi}}
at
u
, there exists (
\widehat{\textbf{G}^{w}},c)\in\partial^{w}{\bf{\Psi}}(u)
such that for any
y\in\mathcal{Y}
,

\widehat{\textbf{G}^{w}}^{\top}\odot(y-u)\ominus_{gH}c\lVert y-u\rVert\preceq{{\bf{\Psi}}}(y)\ominus_{gH}\textbf{T}(u).

Hence,

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\to 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\lVert\lambda(y-u)\rVert}\odot\{({\bf{\Psi}}(u+\lambda(y-u))\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Psi}}(u))\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)^{\top}\odot\lambda(y-u)\}=\textbf{0}

and by
gH
-weak subdifferentiability of
{\bf{\Psi}}
at
u
, we get, for any
y\in\mathcal{Y}
that

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\to 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\lVert\lambda(y-u)\rVert}\odot\bigg\{(\widehat{\textbf{G}^{w}}^{\top}\odot\lambda(y-u)\ominus_{gH}\lambda c\lVert y-u\rVert)\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)^{\top}\odot\lambda(y-u)\bigg\}\preceq\textbf{0},~

\displaystyle(\text{by~(\ref{cv}) of Lemma \ref{yure}})

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lVert(y-u)\rVert}\odot\{(\widehat{\textbf{G}^{w}}^{\top}\odot(y-u)\ominus_{gH}c\lVert y-u\rVert)\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)^{\top}\odot(y-u)\}\preceq\textbf{0}.

Therefore,

\widehat{\textbf{G}^{w}}^{\top}\odot(y-u)\ominus_{gH}c\lVert y-u\rVert\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)^{\top}\odot(y-u)\preceq\textbf{0}~~\forall~y\in\mathcal{Y}

and so by letting
z=y-u
, we obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{\textbf{G}^{w}}^{\top}\odot z\ominus_{gH}c\lVert z\rVert\preceq{\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)^{\top}\odot z~\forall~z\in\mathcal{Y}.

(32)

Note that the
gH
-Fréchet derivative
{\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)
is also
gH
-Gáteaux derivative as in (see Theorem 5.2 of
Ghosh2019derivative
). Hence, it is a linear IVF as in Definition 4.1 of
Ghosh2019derivative
. By this fact, we have
{\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)^{\top}\odot(0)=\textbf{0}
. Then, the inequality (
32
) implies that
(\widehat{\textbf{G}^{w}},c)\in\partial^{w}({\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)(0))
.

Conversely, let
(\widehat{\textbf{G}^{w}},c)\in\partial^{w}({\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)(0))
. Then, we can write

\displaystyle\widehat{\textbf{G}^{w}}^{\top}\odot y\ominus_{gH}c\lVert y\rVert\preceq{\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)^{\top}\odot y~\forall~y\in\mathcal{Y}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\widehat{\textbf{G}^{w}}^{\top}\odot(y-u)\ominus_{gH}c\lVert y-u\rVert\preceq{\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)^{\top}\odot(y-u)~\forall~y\in\mathcal{Y}.

Since
{\bf{\Psi}}
has
gH
-Fréchet derivative
{\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(u)
and it is also a

-subgradient, it follows that

{\bf{\Psi}}_{\mathscr{F}}(y)^{\top}\odot(y-u)\preceq{{\bf{\Psi}}}(y)\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Psi}}(u)~\forall~y\in\mathcal{Y}.

Then,
\widehat{\textbf{G}^{w}}^{\top}\odot(y-u)\ominus_{gH}c\lVert y-u\rVert\preceq{{\bf{\Psi}}}(y)\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Psi}}(u)
. Hence the proof is complete.

Theorem 3.10

Let
\emptyset\neq\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
{\bf{\Phi}}
is
gH
-Fréchet differentiable at

. If

First, we show that

4
Optimality for the difference of two IVFs

In this section,
we consider the constrained IOP as below:

\displaystyle\min_{y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}~\{{\bf{\Phi}}_{2}(y)\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Phi}}_{1}(y)\},

(33)

where
\emptyset\neq\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

are two IVFs. We are going to study of weak efficiency condition for the IOP (

Theorem 4.1

Let

\partial^{w}{\bf{\Phi}}_{1}(u)\subset\partial^{w}{\bf{\Phi}}_{2}(u).

Proof

The
gH
-weak subdifferentiability of
{\bf{\Phi}}_{1}
at
u
implies that
\partial^{w}{\bf{\Phi}}_{1}(u)
is nonempty. Hence, there exists
(\widehat{\textbf{U}^{w}},c)\in I(\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\widehat{\textbf{U}^{w}}^{\top}\odot(y-u)\ominus_{gH}c\lVert y-u\rVert\preceq{\bf{\Phi}}_{1}(y)\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Phi}}_{1}(u)~\text{for all}~y\in\mathcal{Y}.

(34)

Since
{\bf{\Phi}}_{2}\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Phi}}_{1}
gets the weak efficiency value
0
at
u
, for any
y\in\mathcal{Y}
, we have

\displaystyle\textbf{0}\preceq({\bf{\Phi}}_{2}\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Phi}}_{1})(y)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\textbf{0}\preceq{\bf{\Phi}}_{2}(y)\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Phi}}_{1}(y)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle{\bf{\Phi}}_{1}(y)\preceq{\bf{\Phi}}_{2}(y)~\text{by Lemma 2.1(ii) of \cite[cite]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{Ghosh2019derivative}{\@@citephrase{(}}{\@@citephrase{)}}}}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle{\bf{\Phi}}_{1}(y)\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Phi}}_{1}(u)\preceq{\bf{\Phi}}_{2}(y)\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Phi}}_{2}(u)~\text{by Note 2 of \cite[cite]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{anshika}{\@@citephrase{(}}{\@@citephrase{)}}}}.

(35)

Consequently, the inequality (
35
) implies that

\widehat{\textbf{U}^{w}}^{\top}\odot(y-u)\ominus_{gH}c\lVert y-u\rVert\preceq{\bf{\Phi}}_{2}(y)\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Phi}}_{2}(u).

This means
(\widehat{\textbf{U}^{w}},c)\in\partial^{w}{\bf{\Phi}}_{2}(y)
. Hence, the result follows.

Theorem 4.2

Let

Theorem 4.3

(Optimality condition via augmented normal cone)
.
An IVF

Since

is a weak efficient point of
{\bf{\Psi}}
on
\mathcal{Y}
,

\displaystyle{\bf{\Psi}}(u)\preceq{\bf{\Psi}}(y)~\forall~y\in\mathcal{Y}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\textbf{0}\preceq{{\bf{\Psi}}}(y)\ominus_{gH}{\bf{\Psi}}(u)~\forall~y\in\mathcal{Y}~~\text{by Lemma 2.1(ii)~ of~\cite[cite]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{Ghosh2019derivative}{\@@citephrase{(}}{\@@citephrase{)}}}}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle(\textbf{0},0)\in\partial^{w}{\bf{\Psi}}(u).

Let

be an indicator function, defined by

\infty,&\text{for }~y\notin\mathcal{Y}\end{cases}
.
Since

\end{cases}

(\textbf{0},0)\in\partial^{w}{\bf{\Psi}}(u)=\partial^{w}({\bf{\Psi}}\oplus\delta_{\mathcal{Y}})(u)

It needs to show that

let

Conclusion

In this paper, the concepts of
gH
-weak subdifferentials and
gH
-weak subgradients (Definition
15
) for IVFs with illustrative examples have been provided. The
gH
-weak subdifferential set of an IVF has been found to be convex (Theorem
3.1
) and closed (Theorem
3.2
). We have further introduced a necessary and sufficient condition (Theorem
3.4
) for the set of
gH
-weak subgradients to be nonempty. We have derived the necessary optimality condition (Theorem
3.10
) involving
gH
- Fréchet differential and
gH
-weak subdifferential for IVFs. We have derived a necessary optimality criterion for difference of two IVFs (Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.3
). Towards the end of the paper, we have provided a necessary and sufficient condition for weak efficient solution in terms of two notions of augmented normal cone and
gH
-weak subdifferential.

Continuing the present study, in the forthcoming work we will attempt to solve the following three problems.

\bullet

Introducing a
gH
-weak subgradient algorithm which characterizes efficient solutions for nonsmooth nonconvex interval optimization problems.

\bullet

In future, we will take up the practical optimization problems to be solved by
gH
-weak subgradient algorithm.

\bullet

Analogous to the notion of weak-stability for conventional optimization problems
gasimov
, in future, one may attempt to extend the notion for the following IOP (P):

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle~~{{\bf{\Phi}}}(y)

subject to

\displaystyle~~g_{j}(y)\leq 0,~j=1,2,\ldots,p

\displaystyle~~y\in\mathcal{Y},

where
{\bf{\Phi}}:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow I(\mathbb{R})\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}
is an IVF and
g_{j}:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a real-valued constraint,
j=1,2,\ldots,p
, and the feasible set
C
is

C=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:y\in\mathcal{Y},~g_{j}(y)\leq 0,j=1,2,\ldots,p\}.

To establish an interrelation between strong duality and weak-stability for (P), one may define the augmented Lagrange interval-valued function for (P) as follows. Let
J
be an arbitrary index set, for which define

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{(J)}_{\lambda}:=\{e\in\mathbb{R}^{(J)}:\lvert e_{j}\rvert\leq 1,j\in J(\lambda)\}

and

\displaystyle\Lambda:=\left\{(\lambda,k)\in\mathbb{R}^{(J)}:\exists~e\in\mathbb{R}^{(J)}_{\lambda},ke-\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{(J)}_{+}\right\},

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{(J)}:=\{\lambda=(\lambda_{j})_{j\in J}:\lambda_{j}=0~\text{for all}~j\in J~\text{but only finitely many}~\lambda_{j}\neq 0\},

\displaystyle J(\lambda):=\{j\in J:\lambda_{j}\neq 0\},~\text{is a finite subset of}~J~

and

\displaystyle~~\mathbb{R}^{J}_{+}:=\{\lambda=(\lambda_{j})_{j\in J}\in\mathbb{R}^{(J)}:\lambda_{j}\geq 0,j\in J\}.

For each
j\in J
, the augmented Lagrange interval-valued function for (P) can be defined by

\displaystyle\textbf{L}(y,\Lambda,k)=\textbf{T}(y)\ominus_{gH}\langle\lambda,(g_{j}(y))_{j}\rangle\oplus\boldsymbol{\beta}((g_{j}(y)),\lambda,k),

where
\boldsymbol{\beta}(u,\lambda,k):\mathbb{R}^{J}\times\mathbb{R}^{(J)}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is such that

\boldsymbol{\beta}(y,\lambda,k)=\begin{cases}\sup_{e\in\mathbb{R}^{(J)}_{\lambda}}\left\{\langle ke,u\rangle:ke-\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{(J)}_{+}\right\}~&\text{if}~J(\lambda)\neq\emptyset,\\
0&\text{if}~J(\lambda)=\emptyset.\end{cases}

The dual of (P) can be found as

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle~~~\inf~\textbf{L}(x,\lambda,k)

subject to

\displaystyle~~~(\lambda,k)\in\Lambda.

We will make an effort to reduce the duality gap by weak-stability property of the following perturbation function
{\bf{\Psi}}:\mathcal{Y}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to I(\mathbb{R})\cup\{+\infty\}
associated to the IOP (P):

\displaystyle{\bf{\Psi}}(y,u)=\begin{cases}{{\bf{\Phi}}}(y)~&\text{if}~y\in\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}~\text{and}~~g_{j}(y)\leq u_{j},~\forall~j=1,2,3,\ldots,p\\
+\infty~&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
u=(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n})
is called the perturbation vector.

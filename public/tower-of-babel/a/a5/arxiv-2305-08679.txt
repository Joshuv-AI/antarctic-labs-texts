Abstract:
In this paper, we study sharp bound on higher-dimensional Lebesgue product space for Hardy operator on Heisenberg group, the constants of sharp bounds are obtained. In addition, we also give the boundedness for weighted Hardy operator and weighted Ces
\mathrm{\grave{a}}

1
Introduction

The most fundamental averaging operator is Hardy operator defined by

\mathcal{H}f(x):=\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}f(t)dt,

where the function
f
is a nonnegative integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{+}=(0,\infty)
and
x>0
. A celebrated integral inequality, due to Hardy
[
7
]
, states that

\|\mathcal{H}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}

holds for
1<p<\infty
, and the constant
\frac{p}{p-1}
is the best.

For the higher-dimensional case
n\geq 2
, generally speaking, there exist two different definitions. One is the rectangle averaging operator defined by

\mathfrak{H}(f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\frac{1}{x_{1}\cdots x_{n}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{x_{n}}f(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})dt_{1}\cdots dt_{n},

where the function
f
is a nonnegative measurable function on
G=(0,\infty)^{n}
and
x_{i}>0
, for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
, the norm of
\|\mathfrak{H}\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}
is
(\frac{p}{p-1})^{n}
and obviously depends on the dimension of the space.

Another definition is the spherical averaging operator, which was introduced by Christ and Grafakos in
[
1
]
as follows

\mathcal{H}(f)(x)=\frac{1}{|B(0,|x|)|}\int_{|y|<|x|}f(y)dy,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\},

where
f
is a nonnegative measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The norm of
\mathcal{H}
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is still
\frac{p}{p-1}
, and doesn’t depend on the dimension of the space.

In
[
11
]
, Lu et al. defined the Hardy operator on higher-dimensional product spaces. Let us recall their definition.

Definition 1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq m
and
f
be a nonnegative measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}}
. The Hardy operator on higher-dimensional product spaces is defined by

\mathcal{H}_{m}(f)(x)=\left(\prod^{m}_{i=1}\frac{1}{|B(0,|x_{i}|)|}\right)\int_{|y_{1}|<|x_{1}|}\cdots\int_{|y_{m}|<|x_{m}|}f(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m},

(1)

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}}
with
\prod^{m}_{i=1}|x_{i}|\neq 0
.

Next, we will study the sharp bounds for Hardy type operators in product spaces on Heisenberg group. Allow us to introduce some basic knowledge about the Heisenberg group which will be used in the following.

The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is non-commutative nilpotent Lie group, with the underlying manifold
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
and the group law.

Let

x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n},x_{2n+1}),y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{2n},y_{2n+1}),

then we have

x\circ y=\left(x_{1}+y_{1},\ldots,x_{2n}+y_{2n},x_{2n+1}+y_{2n+1}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}(y_{j}x_{n+j}-x_{j}y_{n+j})\right).

By definition, we can see that the identity element on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is
0\in\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
, while the element
x^{-1}
inverse to
x
is
-x
. The corresponding Lie algebra is generated by the left-invariant vector fields

\begin{gathered}X_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+2x_{n+j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2n+1}},\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n,\\
X_{n+j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n+j}}-2x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2n+1}},\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n,\\
X_{2n+1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2n+1}}.\end{gathered}

The only non-trivial commutator relation is

\left[X_{j},X_{n+j}\right]=-4X_{2n+1},\quad j=1,2\ldots,n.

Note that Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a homogeneous group with dilations

\delta_{r}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n},x_{2n+1})=(rx_{1},rx_{2},\ldots,rx_{2n},r^{2}x_{2n+1}),\quad r>0.

The Haar measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
coincides with the usual Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
. Denoting any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
by
|E|
, then we obtain

|\delta_{r}(E)|=r^{Q}|E|,d(\delta_{r}x)=r_{Q}dx,

where
Q=2n+2
is called the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

The Heisenberg distance derived from the norm

|x|_{h}=\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2n}x_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}+x_{2n+1}^{2}\right]^{1/4},

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n},x_{2n+1})
is given by

d(p,q)=d(q^{-1}p,0)=|q^{-1}p|_{h}.

This distance
d
is left-invariant in the sense that
d(p,q)
remains unchanged when
p
and
q
are both left-translated by some fixed vector on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Besides,
d
satisfies the triangular inequality defined by
[
8
]

d(p,q)\leq d(p,x)+d(x,q),\quad p,x,q\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

For
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the ball and sphere with center
x
and radius
r
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
are given by

B(x,r)=\{y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:d(x,y)<r\}

and

S(x,r)=\{y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:d(x,y)=r\}.

Then we have

|B(x,r)|=|B(0,r)|=\Omega_{Q}r^{Q},

where

\Omega_{Q}=\frac{2\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(n/2)}{(n+1)\Gamma(n)\Gamma((n+1)/2)}

denote the volume of the unit ball
B(0,1)
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
that is
\omega_{Q}=Q\Omega_{Q}
(see
[
3
]
). For more details about Heisenberg group can be refer to
[
4
]
and
[
12
]
.

In recent years, many operators in harmonic analysis have been proved to be bounded on Heisenberg group. For instance, Wu and Fu
[
13
]
studied the sharp bound for
n
-dimensional Hardy operator in Lebesgue space on Heisenberg group. Guo et al.
[
5
]
studied the Hausdorff operator on Heisenberg group. Hang et al. have also conducted many related studies(see
[
6
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
9
]
).

Now, we provide the definition of Hardy-type operator in higher-dimensional spaces on Heisenberg group.

Definition 2
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{H}^{n_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq m
and
f
be a nonnegative measurable function on
\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{m}}
. The Hardy-type operator on Heisenberg group is defined by

\mathcal{P}_{m}(f)(x)=\left(\prod^{m}_{i=1}\frac{1}{|B(0,|x_{i}|_{h})|}\right)\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}<|x_{1}|_{h}}\cdots\int_{|y_{m}|_{h}<|x_{m}|_{h}}f(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m},

(2)

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{m}}
with
\prod^{m}_{i=1}|x_{i}|_{h}\neq 0
.

In
[
2
]
, Chu et al. defined the
n
-dimensional weighted Hardy operator on Heisenberg group
\mathcal{H}_{hw}
and
n
-dimensional weighted Ces
\mathrm{\grave{a}}
ro operator on Heisenberg group
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{hw}
. Let us recall their definition.

Definition 3
.

Let
w:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a function, for a measurable function
f
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. The
n
-dimensional weighted Hardy operator on Heisenberg group
\mathcal{H}_{hw}
is defined by

\mathcal{H}_{hw}:=\int_{0}^{1}f(\delta_{t}x)w(t)dt,\quad x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

(3)

Definition 4
.

For a nonnegative function
w:[0,1]\rightarrow(0,\infty)
. For a measurable complex-valued function
f
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the
n
-dimensional weighted Ces
\grave{a}
ro operator is defined by

\mathcal{H}^{*}_{hw}:=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(\delta_{1/t}x)}{t^{Q}}w(t)dt,\quad x\in\mathbb{H}^{n},

(4)

which satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(x)(\mathcal{H}_{hw}g)(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}g(x)(\mathcal{H}^{*}_{hw})(x)dx,

where
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
,
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
,
1<p<\infty
,
q=p/(p-1)
,
\mathcal{H}_{hw}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, and
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{hw}
is bounded on
L^{q}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
.

Based on the above definitions, we will provide the definition of weighted Hardy operator and weighted Ces
\mathrm{\grave{a}}
ro operator in product spaces on Heisenberg group.

Definition 5
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{H}^{n_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq m
and
f
be a nonnegative measurable function on
\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{m}}
.The weighted Hardy operator in product space on Heisenberg group is defined by

\mathcal{P}_{\varphi,m}(f)(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}f(\delta_{t_{1}}x_{1},\ldots,\delta_{t_{m}}x_{m})\varphi(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m},

(5)

where
\varphi
is a nonnegative measurable function on
\overbrace{[0,1]\times\cdots\times[0,1]}^{m}
and
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{m}}
.

Definition 6
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{H}^{n_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq m
and
f
be a nonnegative measurable function on
\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{m}}
.The weighted Ces
\grave{a}
ro operator in product space on Heisenberg group is defined by

\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\varphi,m}(f)(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(\delta_{t_{1}^{-1}}x_{1},\ldots,\delta_{t_{m}^{-1}}x_{m})\varphi(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})}{|t_{1}|_{h}^{Q}\cdots|t_{m}|_{h}^{Q}}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m},

(6)

where
\varphi
is a nonnegative measurable function on
\overbrace{[0,1]\times\cdots\times[0,1]}^{m}
and
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{m}}
.

Next, we will give our main results.

2
Sharp bound for the Hardy-type operator

In this section, we will give the sharp bound for Hardy type operator in higher-dimensional Lebesgue space on Heisenberg group.

Theorem 1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{H}^{n_{i}}
, 1
\leq i\leq m
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{m}})
. Then the Hardy-type operator
\mathcal{P}_{m}
defined in (
2
) is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{m}})
and the norm of
\mathcal{P}_{m}
can be obtained as follows

\|\mathcal{P}_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{m}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{m}})}=\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{m}.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We merely give the proof with the case
m=2
for the sake of clarity in writing, the same is true for the general case
m\geq 2
. We set

g_{f}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{\omega_{Q_{1}}}\frac{1}{\omega_{Q_{2}}}\int_{|\xi_{1}|_{h}=1}\int_{|\xi_{2}|_{h}=1}f(\delta_{|x_{1}|_{h}}\xi_{1},\delta_{|x_{2}|_{h}}\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2},\quad x\in\mathbb{H}^{n_{i}},

then
g
is a nonnegative radial function with respect to the variables
x_{1},x_{2}
respectively. By change of variables, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{2}(g_{f})(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B(0,|x_{1}|_{h})|}\frac{1}{|B(0,|x_{2}|_{h})|}\int_{B(0,|x_{1}|_{h})}\int_{B(0,|x_{2}|_{h})}\frac{1}{\omega_{Q_{1}}}\frac{1}{\omega_{Q_{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{|\xi_{1}|_{h}=1}\int_{|\xi_{2}|_{h}=1}f(\delta_{|y_{1}|_{h}}\xi_{1},\delta_{|y_{2}|_{h}}\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{Q_{1}}}\frac{1}{\omega_{Q_{2}}}\int_{|\xi_{1}|_{h}=1}\int_{|\xi_{2}|_{h}=1}\frac{1}{|B(0,|x_{1}|_{h})|}\frac{1}{|B(0,|x_{2}|_{h})|}

\displaystyle\times\int_{B(0,|x_{1}|_{h})}\int_{B(0,|x_{2}|_{h})}f(\delta_{|y_{1}|_{h}}\xi_{1},\delta_{|y_{2}|_{h}}\xi_{2})dy_{1}dy_{2}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{Q_{1}}}\frac{1}{\omega_{Q_{2}}}\int_{|\xi_{1}|_{h}=1}\int_{|\xi_{2}|_{h}=1}\frac{1}{|B(0,|x_{1}|_{h})|}\frac{1}{|B(0,|x_{2}|_{h})|}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{|x_{1}|_{h}}\int_{0}^{|x_{2}|_{h}}\int_{|y_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}|_{h}=1}\int_{|y_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}|_{h}=1}f(\delta_{r_{1}}\xi_{1},\delta_{r_{2}}\xi_{2})

\displaystyle\times r_{1}^{Q_{1}-1}r_{2}^{Q_{2}-1}dy_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}dy_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}dr_{1}dr_{2}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi_{1}|_{h}=1}\int_{|\xi_{2}|_{h}=1}\frac{1}{|B(0,|x_{1}|_{h})|}\frac{1}{|B(0,|x_{2}|_{h})|}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{|x_{1}|_{h}}\int_{0}^{|x_{2}|_{h}}f(\delta_{r_{1}}\xi_{1},\delta_{r_{2}}\xi_{2})r_{1}^{Q_{1}-1}r_{2}^{Q_{2}-1}dr_{1}dr_{2}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{2}(f)(x_{1},x_{2}).

Using H
\ddot{o}
lder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|g_{f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}}\left|\frac{1}{\omega_{Q_{1}}}\frac{1}{\omega_{Q_{2}}}\int_{|\xi_{1}|_{h}=1}\int_{|\xi_{2}|_{h}=1}f_{(}\delta_{|x_{1}|_{h}}\xi_{1},\delta_{|x_{2}|_{h}}\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}\right|^{p}dx_{1}dx_{2}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{Q_{1}}}\frac{1}{\omega_{Q_{2}}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}}\left(\int_{|\xi_{1}|_{h}=1}\int_{|\xi_{2}|_{h}=1}|f(\delta_{|x_{1}|_{h}}\xi_{1},\delta_{|x_{2}|_{h}}\xi_{2})|^{p}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\times\left.\left(\int_{|\xi_{1}|_{h}=1}\int_{|\xi_{2}|_{h}=1}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}\right)^{p/p^{{}^{\prime}}}dx_{1}dx_{2}\right\}^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega^{1/p}_{Q_{1}}}\frac{1}{\omega^{1/p}_{Q_{2}}}\left\{\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{|x^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}|_{h}}\int_{|x^{{}^{\prime}}_{2}|_{h}}\left(\int_{|\xi_{1}|_{h}=1}\int_{|\xi_{2}|_{h}=1}|f(\delta_{r_{1}}\xi_{1},\delta_{r_{2}}\xi_{2})|^{p}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\times\left.r_{1}^{Q_{1}-1}r_{2}^{Q_{2}-1}dx_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}dx_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}dr_{1}dr_{2}\right\}^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}.

Thus, we have

\frac{\|\mathcal{P}_{2}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}}{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}}\leq\frac{\|\mathcal{P}_{2}(g_{f})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}}{\|g_{f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}}.

This implies the operator
\mathcal{P}_{2}
and its restriction to radial function have same norm in
L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
f
is a radial function in the rest of the proof.

By changing variables, we have that

\mathcal{P}_{2}(f)=\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q_{1}}}\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q_{2}}}\int_{|z_{1}|_{h}<1}\int_{|z_{2}|_{h}<1}f(\delta_{|x_{1}|_{h}}z_{1},\delta_{|x_{2}|_{h}}z_{2})dz_{1}dz_{2}.

Using Minkowski’s inequality, we can get

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{2}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}}\left|\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q_{1}}}\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q_{2}}}\int_{|z_{1}|_{h}<1}\int_{|z_{2}|_{h}<1}f(\delta_{|x_{1}|_{h}}z_{1},\delta_{|x_{2}|_{h}}z_{2})dz_{1}dz_{2}\right|^{p}dx_{1}dx_{2}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q_{1}}}\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q_{2}}}\int_{|z_{1}|_{h}<1}\int_{|z_{2}|_{h}<1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}}|f(\delta_{|z_{1}|_{h}}x_{1},\delta_{|z_{2}|_{h}}x_{2})|^{p}dx_{1}dx_{2}\right)^{1/p}dz_{1}dz_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q_{1}}}\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q_{2}}}\int_{|z_{1}|_{h}<1}\int_{|z_{2}|_{h}<1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}}|f(x_{1},x_{2})|^{p}dx_{1}dx_{2}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\times|z_{1}|_{h}^{-Q_{1}/p}|z_{2}|_{h}^{-Q_{2}/p}dz_{1}dz_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q_{1}}}\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q_{2}}}\int_{|z_{1}|_{h}<1}\int_{|z_{2}|_{h}<1}|z_{1}|_{h}^{-Q_{1}/p}|z_{2}|_{h}^{-Q_{2}/p}dz_{1}dz_{2}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{2}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times{H}^{n_{2}})}.

Therefore, it implies that

\|\mathcal{P}_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}\leq\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{2}.

On the other hand, for

0<\varepsilon<\min\left\{1,\frac{(p-1)Q_{1}}{p},\frac{(p-1)Q_{2}}{p}\right\},

taking

f_{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2})=|x_{1}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{1}}{p}+\varepsilon}|x_{2}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{2}}{p}+\varepsilon}\chi_{\{|x_{1}|_{h}<1,|x_{2}|_{h}<1\}}(x_{1},x_{2}),

then we have

\|f_{\varepsilon}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}=\frac{\omega_{Q_{1}}\omega_{Q_{2}}}{\varepsilon^{2}p^{2}}.

Let us rewrite
\mathcal{P}_{2}(f_{\varepsilon})
as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{2}(f_{\varepsilon})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B(0,|x_{1}|_{h})||B(0,|x_{2}|_{h})|}\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}<|x_{1}|_{h}}\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}<|x_{2}|_{h}}f_{\varepsilon}(y_{1},y_{2})dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B(0,1)||B(0,1)|}\int_{|z_{1}|_{h}<1}\int_{|z_{2}|_{h}<1}f_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{|x_{1}|_{h}}z_{1},\delta_{|x_{2}|_{h}}z_{2})dz_{1}dz_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|x_{1}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{1}}{p}+\varepsilon}|x_{2}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{2}}{p}+\varepsilon}}{|B(0,1)||B(0,1)|}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\{|z_{1}|_{h}<1,|z_{1}|_{h}<|x_{1}|_{h}^{-1}\}}\int_{\{|z_{2}|_{h}<1,|z_{2}|_{h}<|x_{2}|_{h}^{-1}\}}|z_{1}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{1}}{p}+\varepsilon}|z_{2}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{2}}{p}+\varepsilon}dz_{1}dz_{2}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{2}(f_{\varepsilon})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\Omega_{Q_{1}}\Omega_{Q_{2}})^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}}\left|\int_{\{|z_{1}|_{h}<1,|z_{1}|_{h}<|x_{1}|_{h}^{-1}\}}\int_{\{|z_{2}|_{h}<1,|z_{2}|_{h}<|x_{2}|_{h}^{-1}\}}|z_{1}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{1}}{p}+\varepsilon}\right.

\displaystyle\times\left.|z_{2}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{2}}{p}+\varepsilon}dz_{1}dz_{2}\right|^{p}|x_{1}|_{h}^{p\varepsilon-Q_{1}}|x_{2}|_{h}^{p\varepsilon-Q_{2}}dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\Omega_{Q_{1}}\Omega_{Q_{2}})^{p}}\int_{|x_{1}|_{h}<1}\int_{|x_{2}|_{h}<1}\left|\int_{|z_{1}|_{h}<1}\int_{|z_{2}|_{h}<1}|z_{1}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{1}}{p}+\varepsilon}\right.

\displaystyle\times\left.|z_{2}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{2}}{p}+\varepsilon}dz_{1}dz_{2}\right|^{p}|x_{1}|_{h}^{p\varepsilon-Q_{1}}|x_{2}|_{h}^{p\varepsilon-Q_{2}}dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{Q_{1}}\omega_{Q_{2}}}{\varepsilon^{2}p^{2}}\frac{1}{(\Omega_{Q_{1}}\Omega_{Q_{2}})^{p}}\left(\int_{|z_{1}|_{h}<1}\int_{|z_{2}|_{h}<1}|z_{1}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{1}}{p}+\varepsilon}|z_{2}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{2}}{p}+\varepsilon}dz_{1}dz_{2}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{p-1+{r\varepsilon/Q_{1}}}\frac{p}{p-1+{r\varepsilon/Q_{2}}}\right)^{p}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}.

Thus, we have

\|\mathcal{P}_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}\geq\frac{p}{p-1+{r\varepsilon/Q_{1}}}\frac{p}{p-1+{r\varepsilon/Q_{2}}}.

Consequently, using the definition of the norm of an operator and letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we conclude that

\|\mathcal{P}_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}\geq\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{2}.

This finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

3
Boundedness for the weighted Hardy operator and weighted Ces
\grave{a}
ro operator

In this section, we will study the boundedness of weighted Hardy-type operator and give the necessary and sufficient conditions for the boundedness.

Theorem 2
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{H}^{n_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq m
,
\varphi
is a nonnegative measurable function on
\overbrace{[0,1]\times\cdots\times[0,1]}^{m}
. If
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{m}})
, then the weighted Hardy operator in product space on Heisenberg group
\mathcal{P}_{\varphi,m}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{m}})
if and only if

\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}|t_{1}|_{h}^{-Q_{1}/p}\cdots|t_{m}|_{h}^{-Q_{m}/p}\varphi(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m}<\infty.

Theorem 3
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N},n_{i}\in\mathbb{N},x_{i}\in\mathbb{H}^{n_{i}},1\leq i\leq m
,
\varphi
is a nonnegative measurable function on
\overbrace{[0,1]\times\cdots\times[0,1]}^{m}
. If
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{m}})
, then the weighted Ces
\grave{a}
ro operator in product space on Heisenberg group
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\varphi,m}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{m}})
if and only if

\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}|t_{1}|_{h}^{-Q_{1}(1-1/p)}\cdots|t_{m}|_{h}^{-Q_{m}(1-1/p)}\varphi(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m}<\infty.

The proof methods for Theorem
2
and Theorem
3
are the same. We only give the proof of Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

We merely give the proof with the case
m=2
for the sake of clarity in writing, the same is true for the general case
m\geq 2
.

Since the case
p=\infty
is trivial, it suffices to consider the case
1\leq p<\infty
.

Using Minkowski’s inequality and the change of variables, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{\varphi,2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}f(\delta_{t_{1}}x_{1},\delta_{t_{2}}x_{2})\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2}\right|^{p}dx_{1}dx_{2}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}}|f(\delta_{t_{1}}x_{1},\delta_{t_{2}}x_{2})|^{p}dx_{1}dx_{2}\right)^{1/p}\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}}}|f(x_{1},x_{2})|^{p}dx_{1}dx_{2}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\times|t_{1}|_{h}^{-Q_{1}/p}|t_{2}|_{h}^{-Q_{2}/p}\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|t_{1}|_{h}^{-Q_{1}/p}|t_{2}|_{h}^{-Q_{2}/p}\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}.

Therefore, we have

\|\mathcal{P}_{\varphi,2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|t_{1}|_{h}^{-Q_{1}/p}|t_{2}|_{h}^{-Q_{2}/p}\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2}.

(7)

Next, taking

C=\|\mathcal{P}_{\varphi,2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}<\infty

and for
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})
, we have

\|\mathcal{P}_{\varphi,2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}.

For
\epsilon>0
, taking

f_{\epsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&|x|_{h}\leq 1,\\
|x|_{h}^{-\left(\frac{Q}{p}+\epsilon\right)}&|x|_{h}>1\end{cases},

we obtain

\|f_{\epsilon}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}=\frac{\omega_{Q_{1}}}{\epsilon p}\frac{\omega_{Q_{2}}}{\epsilon p}

and

\mathcal{P}_{\varphi,2}(f_{\epsilon})(x)=\begin{cases}0,&|x|_{h}\leq 1,\\
|x_{1}|_{h}^{-{\frac{Q_{1}}{p}}-\epsilon}|x_{2}|_{h}^{-{\frac{Q_{2}}{p}}-\epsilon}\int_{|x_{1}|_{h}^{-1}}^{1}\int_{|x_{2}|_{h}^{-1}}^{1}|t_{1}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{1}}{p}-\epsilon}|t_{1}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{2}}{p}-\epsilon}\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2},&|x|_{h}>1\end{cases}.

So, we have

\displaystyle C^{p}\|f_{\epsilon}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}\geq

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{\varphi,2}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|x_{1}|_{h}>1}\int_{|x_{2}|_{h}>1}\left(|x_{1}|_{h}^{-Q_{1}/p-\epsilon}|x_{2}|_{h}^{-Q_{2}/p-\epsilon}\right.

\displaystyle\times\left.\int_{|x_{1}|_{h}^{-1}}^{1}\int_{|x_{2}|_{h}^{-1}}^{1}|t_{1}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{1}}{p}-\epsilon}|t_{2}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{2}}{p}-\epsilon}\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2}\right)^{p}dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{|x_{1}|_{h}>\epsilon^{-1}}\int_{|x_{2}|_{h}>\epsilon^{-1}}\left(|x_{1}|_{h}^{-Q_{1}/p-\epsilon}|x_{2}|_{h}^{-Q_{2}/p-\epsilon}\right.

\displaystyle\times\left.\int_{\epsilon}^{1}\int_{\epsilon}^{1}|t_{1}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{1}}{p}-\epsilon}|t_{2}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{2}}{p}-\epsilon}\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2}\right)^{p}dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|x_{1}|_{h}>\epsilon^{-1}}\int_{|x_{2}|_{h}>\epsilon^{-1}}|x_{1}|_{h}^{-Q_{1}-\epsilon p}|x_{2}|_{h}^{-Q_{2}-\epsilon p}dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{\epsilon}^{1}\int_{\epsilon}^{1}|t_{1}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{1}}{p}-\epsilon}|t_{2}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{2}}{p}-\epsilon}\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\epsilon}^{1}\int_{\epsilon}^{1}|t_{1}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{1}}{p}-\epsilon}|t_{2}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{2}}{p}-\epsilon}\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2}\right)^{p}\|f_{\epsilon}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}.

This implies that

\|\mathcal{P}_{\varphi,2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}\geq\int_{\epsilon}^{1}\int_{\epsilon}^{1}|t_{1}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{1}}{p}-\epsilon}|t_{2}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{2}}{p}-\epsilon}\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2}.

Since
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we can obtain that

\|\mathcal{P}_{\varphi,2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{H}^{n_{2}})}\geq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|t_{1}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{1}}{p}}|t_{2}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q_{2}}{p}}\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})dt_{1}dt_{2}.

(8)

Combining (
7
) and (
8
), we have finished the proof.
∎

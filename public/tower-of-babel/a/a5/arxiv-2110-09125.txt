On generalization of a theorem of Harish-Chandra
Taiwang DENG

Abstract
. In this paper under some conditions we generalize a theorem of Harish-Chandra concerning representability of Fourier transforms of orbital integrals.



1.
Introduction

In this short paper we consider the question of representability of Fourier transform of orbital integrals for general representations of a reductive groups
\displaystyle G
.
In the case
\displaystyle\rho:G\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathfrak{g})
is the adjoint representation, let

\widehat{\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{G}}(X,f):=\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{G}(X,\widehat{f}),f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}),X\in\mathfrak{g},

where
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{G}(X,f)
is the orbital integral of
\displaystyle f
along the
\displaystyle G
-orbit of
\displaystyle X
, then Harish-Chandra
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
shows that the distribution
is representable by a kernel function
\displaystyle\kappa(X,\cdot)
. He approaches the problem by local trace formula and a finiteness theorem due to Howe.
In
[
15
, §4]
, Zhang raises the question whether analogues theorem holds for the general representations of
\displaystyle G
. Note
that Howe’s finiteness theorem is known to fail in general (see loc.cit. for counter examples). In this paper, we answer this question
affirmatively under some mild conditions. Our main result is Theorem
1.10
. After we finish the proof,
we notice that a particular case when
\displaystyle G=\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}
and
\displaystyle V=\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}\nolimits_{2}
the adjoint representation is studied in
[
7
]
, where the author applies the same method to obtain almost the same result.

Let
\displaystyle F
be a finite extension of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{F}
be the ring of integers of
\displaystyle F
and
\displaystyle\pi
be its uniformizer. Let
\displaystyle\mathrm{k}_{F}
be its residue field.
We fix a valuation
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}
on
\displaystyle F
with
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(\pi)=1
.
Let us also fix an additive character
\displaystyle\psi
of
\displaystyle F
satisfying

\psi|_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}=1,\quad\sum_{a\in\pi^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathcal{O}_{F}}\psi(a)=0.

Assume that
\displaystyle G
is a quasi-split reductive group defined over
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{F}
.
In general,
\displaystyle G\otimes F
may have different models over

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{F}
, and the reductive models depend on a choice of a special vertex on the Bruhat-Tits building of
\displaystyle G\otimes F
. Note that
it follows from our assumption that the group
\displaystyle G\otimes\mathrm{k}_{F}
is also reductive.

Let us fix the notations. Assume that
\displaystyle(\rho,V)
is an algebraic representation of a reductive group
\displaystyle G
over

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{F}
. We identify
\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{m}
as the center of
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(V)
.

We assume that
\displaystyle G
is a reductive subgroup such that
\displaystyle G\cap\mathbb{G}_{m}
is a finite subgroup
and denote
\displaystyle H=G\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
Finally we denote by
\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}
the Lie algebra of
\displaystyle H
and
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}
the Lie algebra of
\displaystyle G
.

According to
[
11
, §3 and §7]
, there exists an involution
\displaystyle\theta
of
\displaystyle G

defined over
\displaystyle F
(called Chevalley involution) which interchanges an irreducible and its contragredient.

The existence of the Chevalley involution allows us to construct a non-degenerate symmetric pairing on
\displaystyle V_{F}=V\otimes F

(without confusion we identify
\displaystyle V_{F}
with its
\displaystyle F
-rational points
\displaystyle V(F)
)

\langle,\rangle:V_{F}\otimes V_{F}\rightarrow F

satisfying

\langle gX,\theta(g)Y\rangle=\langle X,Y\rangle,\quad\forall X,Y\in V(F),g\in G(F).

We also note that
\displaystyle\theta
can be extended to an involution on
\displaystyle H
.

Definition 1.1
.

We fix a basis
\displaystyle\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{d}\}
of
\displaystyle V(\mathcal{O}_{F})
. Define a valuation
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}
on
\displaystyle V
by letting

\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(\sum_{i}x_{i}e_{i})=\min\{\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(x_{i}):i=1,\cdots,d\}.

We also define the norm
\displaystyle|Y|=|\mathrm{k}_{F}|^{-\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)}
.
For any subspace
\displaystyle V_{1}
of
\displaystyle V(F)
, define

d_{\langle,\rangle}(V_{1})=\min\{\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)|Y\in V_{1},\langle Z,Y\rangle\in\mathcal{O}_{F},\forall Z\in V(\mathcal{O}_{F})\}.

Finally, define the depth of
\displaystyle\langle,\rangle
by

d_{\langle,\rangle}:=\max\{d_{\langle,\rangle}(V_{1})|V_{1}\subseteq V\}.

It follows immediately that

Lemma 1.2
.

The valuation
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}
is independent of the choice of the basis of
\displaystyle V(\mathcal{O}_{F})
.

Lemma 1.3
.

For any
\displaystyle Y\in V(F)
with
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)\geq d_{\langle,\rangle}
, we have

\langle Z,Y\rangle\in\mathcal{O}_{F},\forall Z\in V(\mathcal{O}_{F}).

Proof.

In fact, let
\displaystyle Y\in V(F)
with
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)\geq d_{\langle,\rangle}
and
\displaystyle Z\in V(\mathcal{O}_{F})
such that

\langle Z,Y\rangle\notin\mathcal{O}_{F}.

Then take
\displaystyle V_{1}
to be the subspace generated by
\displaystyle Y
. Then
\displaystyle d_{\langle,\rangle}(V_{1})\geq\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)\geq d_{\langle,\rangle}
,
which is absurd.
∎

Assumption 1.4
.

We assume that our choice of
\displaystyle\langle,\rangle
is of depth zero.

Remark 1.5
.

In general if both
\displaystyle G
and
\displaystyle V
are defined over
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
, then for fixed
\displaystyle\langle-,-\rangle
, after excluding finitely many primes the above assumption
is always satisfied. In case
\displaystyle G=\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}
and
\displaystyle V=\mathop{\mathrm{sl}}\nolimits_{2}
is the adjoint representation, we can take

\langle U,X\rangle:=2ux+vz+wy,\quad X=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\
z&-x\end{pmatrix},U=\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\
w&-u\end{pmatrix}.

In this case, only
\displaystyle p=2
violates the above assumption.

Let
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(V)
be the space of locally constant functions on
\displaystyle V(F)
with compact support. For
\displaystyle Y\in V(F)
and
\displaystyle f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(V)
, we define the
Fourier transform of
\displaystyle f
by

\widehat{f}(Y)=\int_{V(F)}f(X)\psi(\langle X,Y\rangle)dX.

We consider the orbital integrals of
\displaystyle f
on
\displaystyle V(F)
to be a linear functional on
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(V)
:

(1.1)

\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{G}(X,f):=\int_{{\mathscr{O}}_{G}(X)}f(Y)dY,

where
\displaystyle{\mathscr{O}}_{G}(X)
denote the
\displaystyle G
-orbit of
\displaystyle X
in
\displaystyle V(F)
.
We denote by
\displaystyle{\mathscr{S}}(V)
the linear functional on
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(V)
generated by the orbital integrals.
We are interested in the question of representability of the orbital integral
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{G}(X,\widehat{f})
, cf.
[
15
, §3]
.

In general, it is convenient to introduce a variant of the orbital integrals, especially when we deal with the orbital integrals of
\displaystyle H
.
We assume that there exists a non-constant H-quasi-invariant
\displaystyle P(X)
in the polynomial ring
\displaystyle F[V]

such that the set

V_{P}:=\{X\in V(F)|P(X)\neq 0\}

is open and dense in
\displaystyle V(F)
( in the
\displaystyle p
-adic topology). By assumption, we have

(1.2)

(g.P)(X)=P(g^{-1}X)=\nu^{-1}(g)P(X),\forall g\in H,

for some algebraic character
\displaystyle\nu:H\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Let
\displaystyle\nu_{s}(g)=|\nu(g)|^{s}
.

Let
\displaystyle\chi:F^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a (unitary) character. The orbit
\displaystyle{\mathscr{O}}_{H}(X)
of
\displaystyle X\in V(F)
admits an action of
\displaystyle F^{\times}
identifying with the center of
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(V)
, which commutes with
the action of
\displaystyle G
.
We modify our definition of orbital integral to be

(1.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{G}(X,f,\nu_{s}):

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathscr{O}}_{G}(X)}f(Y)|P(Y)|^{s}dY,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{H}(X,f,\chi\nu_{s}):

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{F^{\times}}\chi(t)\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{G}(tX,f,\nu_{s})d^{\times}t,

where
\displaystyle dY
is a quasi-invariant algebraic measure on
\displaystyle{\mathscr{O}}_{G}(X)
coming from the algebraic volume form such that

d(tY)=\delta(t)dY,t\in F^{\times}

with
\displaystyle\delta
a (quasi) character of
\displaystyle F^{\times}
.
Observe that when
\displaystyle\chi=1
, the term
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{H}(X,f,\chi\nu_{s})
can be rewritten as

(1.5)

\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{H}(X,f,\nu_{s})=\int_{{\mathscr{O}}_{H}(X)}f(Y)|P(Y)|^{s}dY,

which is compatible with (
1.3
).

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{H}^{\operatorname{Zar}}
be a geometric orbit of
\displaystyle H
in
\displaystyle V
which is defined over
\displaystyle F
. It is an affine variety since
\displaystyle H
is reductive.

We impose some assumptions on the orbit
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{G}(X)
.

Assumption 1.6
.

We assume that
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{G}(X)\subseteq V_{P}
is closed.

Note that we do not ask the orbit
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{G}(X)
to be closed in
\displaystyle V
since it will rule out our example of prehomogeneous space (in which we have
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{G}(X)=V_{P}
).
This assumption is crucial for certain compactness result (cf. Lemma
2.3
).

Put
\displaystyle q=|\mathrm{k}_{F}|
. For
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{Z}
, set
\displaystyle W_{n}(X)=\pi^{-n}V(\mathcal{O}_{F})\cap{\mathscr{O}}_{G}(X)
.

Assumption 1.7
.

We assume that for any
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
and
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\int_{\mathcal{O}_{H}(X)}1_{\{Y\in W_{n}(X):|P(Y)|>\epsilon\}}(Y)dY<\infty.

Note that this assumption excludes the case of taking
\displaystyle P(Y)
to be the constant function in general.
This leads to the following lemma

Lemma 1.8
.

Under the assumption
1.7

the integral (
1.3
) converges for
\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}{s}\gg 0
. Moreover, there exists a meromorphic function

M(s)=\frac{Q(q^{-s},q^{s})}{(1-q^{-a_{1}-b_{1}s})^{n_{1}}(1-q^{-a_{2}-b_{2}s})^{n_{2}}\cdots(1-q^{-a_{r}-b_{r}s})^{n_{r}}}

on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
and

•

\displaystyle r\in\mathbb{N}
,
\displaystyle(a_{1},b_{1}),(a_{2},b_{2}),\cdots,(a_{r},b_{r})
are pairwise distinct integers such that
\displaystyle b_{i}\geq 0(i=1,\cdots,r)
and
\displaystyle b_{i}>0
if
\displaystyle a_{i}<0
;

•

the
\displaystyle b_{i}
’s depend only on
\displaystyle P(X)
;

•

\displaystyle n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}\in\{1,2,\cdots,\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits(\mathcal{O}_{G}(X))\}
;

•

\displaystyle Q
is a two variable polynomial with coefficients in
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
,

such that if the integral (
1.3
) is absolutely convergent at
\displaystyle s=s_{0}
, then
\displaystyle M(s)
is holomorphic at
\displaystyle s=s_{0}
and

\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{G}(X,f,\nu_{s_{0}})=M(s_{0}).

A version of this lemma is the main result of
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
obtaining by
\displaystyle p
-adic cell decomposition. Later a new proof is given in
[
4
, Corollary 15]
using
\displaystyle p
-adic
integration theory which rely on a partition of semi-algebraic sets into simple pieces(cf.
[
4
, Theorem 7]
). The above version we stated is a special case of
[
8
, Theorem 5.13]
(with comparison to
[
4
, Corollary 15]
and
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
).

As for the rationality of
\displaystyle p
-adic orbital integrals,
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
proves a general version of it for arbitrary linear algebraic group under some complicated conditions.
In special case of
\displaystyle G=\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits_{n}
with adjoint representation, Yun
[
14
, Corollary 4.6]
deduce the rationality of
\displaystyle p
-adic orbital integrals by connecting it to counting lattices, which is quite amenable for generalizations. We hope to come back to this point in future work.

Remark 1.9
.

The assumption is satisfied for the case of prehomogeneous space
[
10
, Lemma 2]
as well as the case of
\displaystyle(\rho,V)=(\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits,\mathfrak{g})
the adjoint representation.
Here the second case follows from the first as is explained in
[
1
, §3]
.

Our main result in this paper is the following

Theorem 1.10
.

Assume the hypothesis
1.4
and
1.7
.
There exists a locally constant function
\displaystyle\kappa_{G}(\nu_{s},X,\cdot)

on
\displaystyle V(F)
given by the principal value integral

(1.6)

\int_{{\mathscr{O}}_{G}(X)}|P(Z)|^{s}\psi(\langle Z,Y\rangle)dZ,\quad\operatorname{Re}(s)\gg 0.

It
is a rational function in
\displaystyle q^{-s}
with order of poles bounded by constant depending only on
\displaystyle P(X)
and
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits(\mathcal{O}_{G}(X))
.
When
\displaystyle s=s_{0}
is not its pole, then the function
\displaystyle\kappa_{G}(\nu_{s_{0}},X,\cdot)
is locally integrable.
If furthermore
\displaystyle s=s_{0}
is also not the pole of
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{G}(X,f,\nu_{s})
, then
we have for
\displaystyle f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(V)
,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{G}(X,\widehat{f},\nu_{s_{0}})=\int_{V(F)}f(Y)\kappa_{G}(\nu_{s_{0}},X,Y)dY.

Remark 1.11
.

When
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{G}(X,f,\nu_{s})
has no poles at
\displaystyle s=0
,
letting
\displaystyle s=0
we recover the usual definition (
1.1
) of orbital integrals for
\displaystyle G
.

In fact, we can also derive from our main results a twisted generalization for
\displaystyle H
, which is the following:

Theorem 1.12
.

Assume the hypothesis
1.4
and
1.7
. There exists a locally constant function
\displaystyle\kappa_{H}(\chi\nu_{s},X,\cdot)
well defined outside a measure zero subset
on
\displaystyle V(F)
given by the integral

\int_{F^{\times}}\chi(t)\kappa_{G}(\nu_{s},tX,\cdot)d^{\times}t

For
\displaystyle f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(V)
,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{H}(X,\widehat{f},\chi\nu_{s})=\int_{V(F)}f(Y)\kappa_{H}(\chi\nu_{s},X,Y)dY.

Our theorem
1.10
implies Harish-Chandra’s original theorem, which corresponds to the case
when
\displaystyle G
is semi-simple and
\displaystyle(\rho,V)
is the adjoint representation. See the discussion after the proof of the theorem.

Remark 1.13
.

When
\displaystyle V_{F}
contains only finitely many open
\displaystyle H
-orbits
\displaystyle\{{\mathscr{O}}_{1},\cdots,{\mathscr{O}}_{r}\}
, then the above theorem gives

\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{H}({\mathscr{O}}_{i},\widehat{f},\nu_{s})=\sum_{j}\Gamma_{ij}(\nu_{s})\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{H}({\mathscr{O}}_{j},f,\theta(\delta^{-1}\nu_{s}))

.

2.
Proof of the Main Results

Proof of Lemma
1.8
.

Consider

W_{n}(X)=\pi^{-n}V(\mathcal{O}_{F})\cap{\mathscr{O}}_{G}(X)

which is an semi-algebraic set which is open in the semi-algebraic space
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{G}(X)
(This is even a Nash manifold, cf.
[
8
, §6.5]
).
We endow it with the
\displaystyle\delta
-invariant measure
\displaystyle dY
(or the one from the volume form). When
\displaystyle X\in V_{P}
,
we observe that the restriction of
\displaystyle P(X)
to
\displaystyle W_{n}(X)
is a nowhere vanishing and bounded semi-algebraic function, hence
we can apply the
[
8
, Theorem 5.13]
. Note that our choice of measure is of order
\displaystyle\leq 0
as indicated by
[
8
, Theorem 6.15]
.
This explains the special form of
\displaystyle M(s)
in our lemma (Compare also with
[
4
, Corollary]
).
∎

Remark:
The exponents
\displaystyle b_{i}
’s are missed in
[
8
, Theorem 5.13]
, which is clear according to the proof appearing after
[
8
, Lemma 5.17]
.

Let us consider the proof of Theorem
1.10
.
We need some preparations.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
\displaystyle Z_{0}\in V(F)\backslash\{0\}
, let
\displaystyle V_{0}
be the
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{F}
-module generated by
\displaystyle H(\mathcal{O}_{F}).Z_{0}
. Let

d_{V_{0}}=\min\{\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)|Y\in V(F),\langle Z,Y\rangle\in\mathcal{O}_{F},\forall Z\in V_{0}\}.

Let

U_{Z_{0}}=\{Y\in V(F)|\psi(\frac{\langle\cdot,\pi^{d_{V_{0}}-\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Z_{0})-\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)}Y\rangle}{\pi}):V_{0}\otimes\mathrm{k}_{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}\text{ is nontrivial}\}.

Then
\displaystyle U_{Z_{0}}
is an non-empty open subset of
\displaystyle V(F)
whose complement is closed of measure zero.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

The non-emptiness of
\displaystyle U_{Z_{0}}
follows from the definition of
\displaystyle d_{V_{0}}
.
By definition, if
\displaystyle Y\notin U_{Z_{0}}
, then
\displaystyle\langle Z,\pi^{d_{V_{0}}-\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)-\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Z)}Y\rangle\in\pi\mathcal{O}_{F}
for all
\displaystyle Z\in V_{0}
.
Let
\displaystyle U^{c}
be the complement of
\displaystyle U_{Z_{0}}
in
\displaystyle V(F)
and
\displaystyle U^{c}_{n}=U^{c}\cap\pi^{n}V(\mathcal{O}_{F})
.
It suffices to show that for any
\displaystyle n>0
, the image of
\displaystyle U_{n}^{c}
in
\displaystyle\pi^{n}V(\mathcal{O}_{F})/\pi^{n+1}V(\mathcal{O}_{F})
is not surjective.
In fact, let
\displaystyle Y\in U_{n}^{c}\backslash U_{n+1}^{c}
, then
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)=n
, but by definition
\displaystyle Y_{0}=\pi^{-n}Y\in U_{0}^{c}
, hence
we only need to show that the image of
\displaystyle U_{0}^{c}
in
\displaystyle V(\mathrm{k}_{F})
is not surjective. But it readily follows from the definition
there exists
\displaystyle Y\in V(F),Z\in V_{0}
such that
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)=0
and
\displaystyle\langle Z,\pi^{d_{V_{0}}-\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Z_{0})}Y\rangle\in\mathcal{O}_{F}\backslash\pi\mathcal{O}_{F}
.
Hence the projection of
\displaystyle U_{0}^{c}
is not surjective to
\displaystyle V(\mathrm{k}_{F})
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

We keep the notations in Lemma
2.1
. Then for
\displaystyle Y\in U_{Z_{0}}
, we have

\sum_{Z\in V_{0}\otimes\mathrm{k}_{F}}\psi(\frac{\langle Z,\pi^{d_{V_{0}}-\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Z_{0})-\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)}Y\rangle}{\pi})=0.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Note that
\displaystyle\psi(\frac{\langle Z,\pi^{d_{V_{0}}-\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Z_{0})-\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)}Y\rangle}{\pi})
is a non-trivial character on
\displaystyle V_{0}\otimes\mathrm{k}_{F}
.
But for any nontrivial character
\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}:V_{0}\otimes\mathrm{k}_{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, assume that
\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}(Z)\neq 1
and
\displaystyle V_{0}\otimes\mathrm{k}_{F}=V^{\prime}\oplus\mathrm{k}_{F}Z
, then

\sum_{X\in V_{0}\otimes\mathrm{k}_{F}}\chi^{\prime}(X)=\sum_{Z^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}}\chi^{\prime}(Z^{\prime})\sum_{a\in\mathrm{k}_{F}}\chi^{\prime}(Z)^{a}=0.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\displaystyle W_{n}(X)=\pi^{-n}V(\mathcal{O}_{F})\cap{\mathscr{O}}_{G}(X)
, then

\displaystyle W_{n}(X)\backslash W_{n-1}(X)
is a compact subset of
\displaystyle{\mathscr{O}}_{G}(X)
.

Proof.

We regard
\displaystyle V(F)
as a metric space with respect to the norm in definition
1.1
, i.e.,

|Y|:=q^{\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)}.

It follows from the completeness of
\displaystyle F
that
\displaystyle V(F)
with respect to this metric is complete. Note that by our assumption
\displaystyle G
has finite center, hence
the algebraic character in (
1.2
) restricting to
\displaystyle G
is trivial, hence for any
\displaystyle g\in G
,

P(gY)=\nu^{-1}(g)P(Y)=P(Y).

Now assume that
\displaystyle g_{i}\in G(i=1,2,\cdots)
with
\displaystyle g_{i}(X)\in W_{n}(X)\backslash W_{n-1}(X)
such that

\displaystyle Y_{0}=\lim_{i}g_{i}(X)
in
\displaystyle V(F)
. In particular, we have

P(Y_{0})=\lim_{i}P(g_{i}(X))=P(X)\neq 0.

Therefore we have
\displaystyle Y_{0}\in V_{P}
. By assumption
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{G}(X)
is closed in
\displaystyle V_{P}
hence we must have
\displaystyle Y_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{G}(X)
.
It is also clear that
\displaystyle Y_{0}\in\pi^{-n}V(\mathcal{O}_{F})\backslash\pi^{-n+1}V(\mathcal{O}_{F})
, which implies that
\displaystyle Y_{0}\in W_{n}(X)\backslash W_{n-1}(X)
.
It follows then
\displaystyle W_{n}(X)\backslash W_{n-1}(X)
is actually closed in
\displaystyle V
.
Since it is also bounded, it is compact.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

We consider the sequence of functions

f_{n}:=1_{\pi^{-n}V(\mathcal{O}_{F})},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

And denote for
\displaystyle\rm{Re}(s)>0
,

I_{n}(Y):=\int_{{\mathscr{O}}_{G}(X)}f_{n}(Z)|P(Z)|^{s}\psi(\langle Z,Y\rangle)dZ.

Then there exists an open dense subset
\displaystyle U
of
\displaystyle V(F)
such that
\displaystyle V(F)\backslash U
is of measure zero and for any
\displaystyle Y\in U
,
the sequence
\displaystyle\{I_{n}(Y)|n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
stabilizes as
\displaystyle n\geq\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)+3
. Consequently,
the sequence
\displaystyle\{I_{n}(Y)|n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
convereges to a locally constant function
\displaystyle I_{\infty}(Y)
on
\displaystyle U
.
Furthermore, the function
\displaystyle I_{\infty}(Y)

is a rational function in
\displaystyle q^{-s}
with order of poles bounded by constant depending only on
\displaystyle P(X)
and
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits(\mathcal{O}_{G}(X))
.

Remark:
The example below with
\displaystyle H=\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits_{n}
shows that the restriction to
\displaystyle U
is necessary.

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

Let

\displaystyle G(F)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{O}}_{G}(X)

Note that for

\displaystyle\nu

where

\displaystyle G^{i}
is the stabilizer of
\displaystyle Z_{i}
in
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{O}_{F})

\displaystyle c_{i}\neq 0
for some
\displaystyle i
otherwise we are done.
Let
\displaystyle Y_{1}=\pi^{-\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)}Y
. Now for each such
\displaystyle i

\sum_{h\in G(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{F})}\psi(\langle hZ_{i},Y\rangle)=\sum_{h\in G(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{F})}\psi(\pi^{-\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)}\langle hZ_{i},Y_{1}\rangle).

\sum_{h\in G(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{F})}\psi(\langle hZ_{i},Y\rangle)=0,\quad\forall n\geq\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)+3.

Upon replacing
\displaystyle Y
by
\displaystyle Y_{1}
above, we only need to show that for
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{v}}\nolimits_{\pi}(Y)=0

\sum_{h\in G(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{F})}\psi(\langle hZ_{i},Y\rangle)=0,\quad\forall n\geq 3.

\displaystyle\langle Z,Y\rangle\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
holds for any
\displaystyle Z\in V(\mathcal{O}_{F})

Let
\displaystyle G_{n}=\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\nolimits(G(\mathcal{O}_{F})\rightarrow G(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{F}))

\displaystyle V(F)

Remark 2.6

3.
Application of the Main Results

Let us explain how to derive the theorem of Harish-Chandra from our main results.
Let
\displaystyle G
be a semi-simple group and
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}
its Lie algebra. We consider the adjoint representation

\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits:G\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathfrak{g}).

In particular, we have
\displaystyle H=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}\nolimits(G)\mathbb{G}_{m}
, where
\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{m}
is regarded as the center of
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathfrak{g})
.
We also fix an
\displaystyle G
-invariant symmetric bilinear form
\displaystyle\langle,\rangle
on
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}
.
When
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}
is a classical Lie algebra of type
\displaystyle A,B,C,D
, which can be realized in a standard way as
a sub Lie algebra of the Lie algebra
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{gl}}\nolimits_{n}
(cf.
[
9
, Chapter 2]
). Then
\displaystyle\langle,\rangle
is simply the restriction of
the trace pairing

(X,Y)\mapsto\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits(XY).

Furthermore, following
[
5
, Page 2]
, we consider the invariant polynomial
\displaystyle P(X)
as the coefficient of
\displaystyle t^{\ell}
in

\det(t-\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits(X)),\quad\ell=\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits(\mathfrak{g}).

In general the algebra of invariants
\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
is identified by Chevalley with
\displaystyle S(\mathfrak{g})^{W}
with
\displaystyle W
the Weyl group.
Note that by a theorem of Deligne and Rao
[
12
, Theorem 1]
, the integral

\mathop{\mathrm{Orb}}\nolimits_{G}(Y,f)=\int_{{\mathscr{O}}_{G}(Y)}f(X)dX

is well defined for any
\displaystyle f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\displaystyle X\in\{X\in\mathfrak{g}:P(X)\neq 0\}
. As a consequence, we can take
\displaystyle s=0
in the
equation (
1.7
) and recover the theorem of Harish-Chandra.

Remark 3.1
.

Similar considerations can be applied to the case of representability for the Jacquet–Rallis orbital integral in
[
15
]
once the analogue
of theorem of Deligne and Rao is established, which is an interesting question.

Note on a theorem of Professor X.
Levent Alpöge

Abstract
. Between his arrival in Frankfurt in 1922 1922 and and his proof of his famous finiteness theorem for integral points in 1929 1929 , Siegel had no publications. He did, however, write a letter to Mordell in 1926 1926 in which he explained a proof of the finiteness of integral points on hyperelliptic curves. Recognizing the importance of this argument (and Siegel’s views on publication), Mordell sent the relevant extract to be published under the pseudonym "X". The purpose of this note is to explain how to optimize Siegel’s 1926 1926 technique to obtain the following bound. Let K K be a number field, S S a finite set of places of K K , and f ∈ 𝔬 K , S ​ [ t ] f\in\mathfrak{o}_{K,S}[t] monic of degree d ≥ 5 d\geq 5 with discriminant Δ f ∈ 𝔬 K , S × \Delta_{f}\in\mathfrak{o}_{K,S}^{\times} . Then: # | { ( x , y ) : x , y ∈ 𝔬 K , S , y 2 = f ( x ) } | ≤ 2 rank Jac ​ ( C f ) ​ ( K ) ⋅ O ( 1 ) d 3 ⋅ ( [ K : ℚ ] + # | S | ) . \#|\{(x,y):x,y\in\mathfrak{o}_{K,S},y^{2}=f(x)\}|\leq 2^{\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}{\mathrm{Jac}(C_{f})(K)}}\cdot O(1)^{d^{3}\cdot([K:\mathbb{Q}]+\#|S|)}. This improves bounds of Evertse-Silverman and Bombieri-Gubler from 1986 1986 and 2006 2006 , respectively. The main point underlying our improvement is that, informally speaking, we insist on "executing the descents in the presence of only one root (and not three) until the last possible moment".

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a number field. Let
S
be a finite set of places of
K
. Let
f\in\mathfrak{o}_{K,S}[t]
be monic of degree
d\geq 5
with discriminant
\Delta_{f}\in\mathfrak{o}_{K,S}^{\times}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{f}^{\mathrm{aff.}}:y^{2}=f(x)
be the Weierstrass model of the hyperelliptic curve
C_{f}
corresponding to
f
. Then:

\#|\mathcal{C}_{f}^{\mathrm{aff.}}(\mathfrak{o}_{K,S})|\leq 2^{\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}{\mathrm{Jac}(C_{f})(K)}}\cdot O(1)^{d^{3}\cdot([K:\mathbb{Q}]+\#|S|)}.

Also, for all
K
-irreducible
g_{i}\in\mathfrak{o}_{K,S}[t]
with
\sum_{i}\deg{g_{i}}\geq 3
and
\prod_{i}g_{i}\big|f
,

\#|\mathcal{C}_{f}^{\mathrm{aff.}}(\mathfrak{o}_{K,S})|\leq\left(\prod_{i}\#|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S})[2]|\right)\cdot O(1)^{d^{3}\cdot([K:\mathbb{Q}]+\#|S|)},

where
K_{g_{i}}:=K[t]/(g_{i})
and
\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S}:=\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}}}\otimes_{\mathfrak{o}_{K}}\mathfrak{o}_{K,S}
.

We include the second statement in case e.g. the product of the linear and quadratic irreducible factors of
f
is of degree at least
3
.

In the elliptic curve case an even stronger bound is available because one can execute the whole descent over
K
(following Mordell)

Let us explain how the argument goes. First, the
2
-descent. À la Fermat, given
y^{2}=f(x)
, adjoin a root
\rho
and conclude that
x-\rho
is almost a square, say
x-\rho=\alpha\cdot\beta^{2}
with
\alpha,\beta\in K[\rho]:=K[t]/(f)
.

Now adjoin three roots. Let
L/K
be an extension containing three roots, say
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3}
. Then, for all
K

\sigma:K[\rho]\to L
, we find
x-\sigma(\rho)=\sigma(\alpha)\cdot\sigma(\beta)^{2}
. Thus (obvious notation)
\rho_{j}-\rho_{i}=\alpha_{i}\cdot\beta_{i}^{2}-\alpha_{j}\cdot\beta_{j}^{2}
.

Now pass to
L^{\prime}:=L(\sqrt{\alpha_{1}},\sqrt{\alpha_{2}},\sqrt{\alpha_{3}})
. We obtain the six elements
\gamma_{ij,\pm}:=\beta_{i}\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}\pm\beta_{j}\sqrt{\alpha_{j}}
, all divisors of
\rho_{j}-\rho_{i}
. By hypothesis they are therefore all
S^{\prime}
-units, with
S^{\prime}
the set of places of
L^{\prime}
above a place in
S
.

Now for the
3
-descent. We may then write
\gamma_{ij,\pm}=\delta_{ij,\pm}\cdot\varepsilon_{ij,\pm}^{3}
with
\delta_{ij,\pm}
representatives for
\mathfrak{o}_{L^{\prime},S^{\prime}}^{\times}/3
. The relation
\gamma_{12,+}-\gamma_{23,+}=\gamma_{13,-}
becomes the cubic Thue equation
\frac{\delta_{12,+}}{\delta_{13,-}}\cdot\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{12,+}}{\varepsilon_{13,-}}\right)^{3}-\frac{\delta_{23,+}}{\delta_{13,-}}\cdot\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{23,+}}{\varepsilon_{13,-}}\right)^{3}=1
.

We conclude with a bound of Evertse.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
g\in\mathfrak{o}_{K,S}[t]
with
g|f
be a monic divisor of
f
with
\deg{g}\geq 3
(for the first part of the theorem we will take
g=f
). Write
f=:gh
with
h\in\mathfrak{o}_{K,S}[t]
. Let
L/K
be an extension of minimal degree containing at least three roots of
g
.

Factorize
g=:\prod_{i}g_{i}
into
K
-irreducible factors
g_{i}\in\mathfrak{o}_{K,S}[t]
. Note that
(g_{i},g_{j})=(1)
for each
i\neq j
because
\Delta_{f}\in\mathfrak{o}_{K,S}^{\times}
. Thus
K_{g}\simeq\bigoplus_{i}K_{g_{i}}
, with
K_{g_{i}}:=K[t]/(g_{i})
and
K_{g}:=K[t]/(g)
. We will repeatedly write
\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S}
etc. for the evident localizations, e.g.
\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S}:=\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}}}\otimes_{\mathfrak{o}_{K}}\mathfrak{o}_{K,S}
. Write
\rho^{(i)}
for the image of
t
in
K_{g_{i}}=K[t]/(g_{i})
.

By the Chebotarev density theorem (and its explicit error term) applied to the Hilbert class field of
K_{g_{i}}
, each ideal class of
K_{g_{i}}
contains a prime of norm
\ll|\Delta_{K_{g_{i}}}|^{O(1)}
. (We will only use existence.) Let then
P^{(i)}
be a minimal set of prime representatives of
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S})[2]
in
K_{g_{i}}
of norm
\ll|\Delta_{K_{g_{i}}}|^{O(1)}
.

Now let us begin the argument. For
(x,y)\in\mathcal{C}_{f}^{\mathrm{aff.}}(\mathfrak{o}_{K,S})
,
(y)^{2}=(x-\rho^{(i)})\cdot\left(\frac{g_{i}(x)}{x-\rho^{(i)}}\right)\cdot\prod_{j\neq i}(g_{j}(x))\cdot(h(x))
as ideals in
\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S}
. Without loss of generality
f(x)\neq 0
. Since
\Delta_{f}\in\mathfrak{o}_{K,S}^{\times}
, it follows that there is an ideal
\mathfrak{a}_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S}
with
(x-\rho^{(i)})=\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{2}
. Thus there is a
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in P^{(i)}
with
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\equiv\mathfrak{a}_{i}
modulo principal ideals of
K_{g_{i}}
. Hence
\mathfrak{a}_{i}
is principal in
\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S\cup\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}}
(obvious meaning).

Let
\alpha_{i}\in\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S\cup\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}}
be such that
\mathfrak{a}_{i}=(\alpha_{i})
as ideals of
\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S\cup\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}}
. Thus
x-\rho^{(i)}=\alpha_{i}^{2}\cdot(\in\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S\cup\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}}^{\times})
, where
(\in\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S\cup\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}}^{\times})
denotes an element of the
(S\cup\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\})
-units of
K_{g_{i}}
.

Let
U^{(i)}
be a minimal set of representatives of
\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S\cup\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}}^{\times}/2
(that is, modulo squares). It follows that there are
\gamma_{i}\in U^{(i)}
and
u^{(i)}\in\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S\cup\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}}^{\times}
such that
x-\rho^{(i)}=\gamma_{i}\cdot(\alpha_{i}\cdot u^{(i)})^{2}
. Let
\eta_{i}:=\alpha_{i}\cdot u^{(i)}
. Thus
x-\rho^{(i)}=\gamma_{i}\cdot\eta_{i}^{2}
.

Therefore we find that, for each
K
-embedding
\sigma:K_{g_{i}}\hookrightarrow L
, we have
x-\sigma(\rho^{(i)})=\sigma(\gamma_{i})\cdot\sigma(\eta_{i})^{2}
.

By definition of
L
, for each
1\leq k\leq 3
there is an
i_{k}
and a
K
-embedding
\tau_{k}:K_{g_{i_{k}}}\hookrightarrow L
such that the corresponding roots
\tau_{k}(\rho^{(i_{k})})
are pairwise distinct. Let then:

\displaystyle\kappa_{k}

\displaystyle:=\tau_{k}(\rho^{(i_{k})}),

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle:=\tau_{k}(\gamma_{i_{k}}),

\displaystyle\mu_{k}

\displaystyle:=\tau_{k}(\eta_{i_{k}}).

Hence
x-\kappa_{k}=\lambda_{k}\cdot\mu_{k}^{2}
as elements of
L
. Hence for
k\neq\ell
we find that
\kappa_{\ell}-\kappa_{k}=\lambda_{k}\cdot\mu_{k}^{2}-\lambda_{\ell}\cdot\mu_{\ell}^{2}
.

We are done with the
2
-descent on
C_{f}
. Now it is time for the
3
-descent on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Let
L_{k\ell}:=L\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{k}},\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}}\right)
. Thus
(1)=(\kappa_{\ell}-\kappa_{k})=\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\mu_{k}-\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}}\mu_{\ell}\right)\cdot\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\mu_{k}+\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}}\mu_{\ell}\right)
as ideals of
\mathfrak{o}_{L_{k\ell},S\cup\{\mathfrak{p}_{k},\mathfrak{p}_{\ell}\}}
(obvious meaning), the first equality following from
\Delta_{f}\in\mathfrak{o}_{K,S}^{\times}
.

Let
V_{k\ell}
be a minimal set of representatives of
\mathfrak{o}_{L_{k\ell},S\cup\{\mathfrak{p}_{k},\mathfrak{p}_{\ell}\}}^{\times}/3
(that is, modulo cubes). It follows that there are
v_{k\ell,\pm}\in V_{k\ell},\zeta_{k\ell,\pm}\in\mathfrak{o}_{L_{k\ell},S\cup\{\mathfrak{p}_{k},\mathfrak{p}_{\ell}\}}^{\times}
for which
\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\mu_{k}\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}}\mu_{\ell}=v_{k\ell,\pm}\cdot\zeta_{k\ell,\pm}^{3}
. Hence:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\mu_{1}\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}\mu_{2})\mp(\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}\mu_{2}\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{3}}\mu_{3})-(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\mu_{1}-\sqrt{\lambda_{3}}\mu_{3})

\displaystyle=v_{12,\pm}\cdot\zeta_{12,\pm}^{3}\mp v_{23,\pm}\cdot\zeta_{23,\pm}^{3}-v_{13,-}\cdot\zeta_{13,-}^{3}.

We may of course rearrange this as
\frac{v_{12,\pm}}{v_{13,-}}\cdot\left(\frac{\zeta_{12,\pm}}{\zeta_{13,-}}\right)^{3}\mp\frac{v_{23,\pm}}{v_{13,-}}\cdot\left(\frac{\zeta_{23,\pm}}{\zeta_{13,-}}\right)^{3}=1
.

Now for Evertse’s bound. Let
F_{\pm}(X,Y):=\frac{v_{12,\pm}}{v_{13,-}}\cdot X^{3}\mp\frac{v_{23,\pm}}{v_{13,-}}\cdot Y^{3}\in\mathfrak{o}_{L_{123}}[X,Y]
, with
L_{123}:=L(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}},\sqrt{\lambda_{2}},\sqrt{\lambda_{3}})
. Then
F_{\pm}
is a cubic form with nonzero discriminant. Moreover if
F_{\pm}(X,Y)=F_{\pm}(tX,tY)=1
then
t^{3}=1
. Therefore by
[
5
]
the number of solutions of
F_{\pm}(X,Y)=1
with
(X,Y)\in\mathfrak{o}_{L_{123},S\cup\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2},\mathfrak{p}_{3}\}}
is
\ll O(1)^{[L:\mathbb{Q}]+[L:K]\cdot\#|S|}
.

We are done with the argument. Now let us examine the tally. Let us first show that our original point
(x,y)
can be recovered from the pairs
\left(\frac{\zeta_{12,\pm}}{\zeta_{13,-}},\frac{\zeta_{23,\pm}}{\zeta_{13,-}}\right)
up to
O(1)
many possibilities. To see this first multiply the two
X
-coordinates together to form
\frac{\zeta_{12,+}\cdot\zeta_{12,-}}{\zeta_{13,-}^{2}}
. Note that:

\displaystyle v_{12,+}\cdot v_{12,-}\cdot\zeta_{12,+}^{3}\cdot\zeta_{12,-}^{3}

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\mu_{1}+\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}\mu_{2})\cdot(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\mu_{1}-\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}\mu_{2})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}\mu_{1}^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mu_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{2}-\kappa_{1}.

Thus it follows that
\zeta_{12,+}^{3}\cdot\zeta_{12,-}^{3}=\frac{\kappa_{2}-\kappa_{1}}{v_{12,+}\cdot v_{12,-}}
. Hence the cube of the product of the two
X
-coordinates is
\zeta_{13,-}^{-6}\cdot\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}-\kappa_{1}}{v_{12,+}\cdot v_{12,-}}\right)
. We note that the term in parentheses is fixed (in terms of our choices up til now). Thus we may recover
\zeta_{13,-}^{6}
, and hence
\zeta_{13,-}
up to at most six choices. Having done so we return to the
X
-coordinates of both solutions and recover
\zeta_{12,+}
and
\zeta_{12,-}
. Then the equality

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\mu_{1}+\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}\mu_{2})+(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\mu_{1}-\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}\mu_{2})

\displaystyle=(v_{12,+}\cdot\zeta_{12,+}^{3})+(v_{12,-}\cdot\zeta_{12,-}^{3})

implies that we can recover
2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\mu_{1}
. Squaring this we find that we can recover
4\lambda_{1}\mu_{1}^{2}=4(x-\kappa_{1})
. Since
\kappa_{1}
is fixed we can recover
x
, and then there are at most two choices for
y
given
x
, so we can indeed recover the point up to
O(1)
many choices.

So we see that a point
(x,y)\in\mathcal{C}_{f}^{\mathrm{aff.}}(\mathfrak{o}_{K,S})
is determined up to
O(d^{3})
(arising from the choice of three roots in
L
) many choices by the data

(\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2},\mathfrak{p}_{3},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3},v_{12,+},v_{12,-},v_{23,+},v_{23,-},v_{31,-}).

The number of choices for each
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is
\#|P^{(i)}|=\#|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S})[2]|
. The number of choices for each
\gamma_{i}\in U^{(i)}
is
\#|U^{(i)}|\ll 2^{\deg{g_{i}}\cdot(\#|S|+[K:\mathbb{Q}])}
by Dirichlet. Similarly the number of choices for each
v_{k\ell,\pm}\in V_{k\ell}
is
\#|V_{k\ell}|\ll 3^{4\cdot[L:\mathbb{Q}]+4\cdot[L:K]\cdot\#|S|}
. Of course
[L:K]\leq d^{3}
.

Therefore the total number of tuples
(\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2},\mathfrak{p}_{3},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3},v_{12,+},v_{12,-},v_{23,+},v_{23,-},v_{31,-})
is

\ll\left(\prod_{i}\#|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S})[2]|\right)\cdot O(1)^{d^{3}\cdot([K:\mathbb{Q}]+\#|S|)}.

We are done with the second part of the theorem.

So take
g=f
, and let us count the ideal classes of
\bigoplus_{i}\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S}\simeq\mathfrak{o}_{K_{f},S}
that could possibly arise in the
2
-descent step (we have bounded said count by
\leq\prod_{i}\#|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{o}_{K_{g_{i}},S})[2]|
and we claim it is also
\ll 2^{\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}{\mathrm{Jac}\,{(C_{f})}(K)}}\cdot O(1)^{d}
).

Let
J_{f}:=\mathrm{Jac}\,{C_{f}}
. Let
W\subseteq C_{f}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
be the set of Weierstrass points of
C_{f}
. Let
\infty\in W
be a point at infinity. We embed
C_{f}\hookrightarrow J_{f}=\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C_{f})
via
P\mapsto P-\infty
. Thus as
\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/K)
-modules
J_{f}[2]\simeq\mathbb{F}_{2}[W-\{\infty\}]/\mathbb{F}_{2}\cdot\left(\sum_{P\in W-\{\infty\}}P\right)
. In other words,
\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{K_{f}}\mathbb{F}_{2}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{2}\oplus J_{f}[2]
, where we have written
\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{K_{f}}(\bullet):=\bigoplus_{i}\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{K_{g_{i}}}(\bullet)
. Thus
H^{1}(K_{f},\mathbb{F}_{2})\simeq H^{1}(K,\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{K_{f}}\mathbb{F}_{2})\simeq H^{1}(K,\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{1}(K,J_{f}[2])
, where the first isomorphism follows by Shapiro’s lemma (and
H^{1}(K_{f},\bullet):=\bigoplus_{i}H^{1}(K_{g_{i}},\bullet)
). By Kummer it follows that
H^{1}(K,J_{f}[2])\simeq(K_{f}^{\times}/2)_{\mathrm{Nm}=\square}
.

Thus by taking invariants of
0\to J_{f}[2]\to J_{f}\to J_{f}\to 0
we obtain
J_{f}(K)/2\hookrightarrow H^{1}(K,J_{f}[2])\simeq(K_{f}^{\times}/2)_{\mathrm{Nm}=\square}
. Write
G\subseteq H^{1}(K,J_{f}[2])
for the image of this map. Note that the restriction of this map to
C_{f}(K)-W
is simply
(x,y)\mapsto x-\rho
, so similarly write
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
for the image of
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{aff.}}_{f}(\mathfrak{o}_{K,S})-W
.

We have already seen that each
g\in G^{\prime}\subseteq G\hookrightarrow(K_{f}^{\times}/2)_{\mathrm{Nm}=\square}
gives rise to a class represented by an
\alpha\in K_{f}^{\times}
for which
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)
is even for all primes
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{o}_{K_{f},S}
(because
\Delta_{f}\in\mathfrak{o}_{K,S}^{\times}
). From such a class we may produce an element of
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{o}_{K_{f},S})[2]
via
\alpha\mapsto\mathfrak{a}
such that
(\alpha)=\mathfrak{a}^{2}
as ideals of
\mathfrak{o}_{K_{f},S}
.

The corresponding map
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{aff.}}_{f}(\mathfrak{o}_{K,S})-W\to\mathrm{Cl}(\mathfrak{o}_{K_{f},S})[2]
is precisely (up to our choice of representatives) our map
(x,y)\mapsto(\mathfrak{p}_{i})_{i}
. It therefore suffices to show that
\#|G^{\prime}|\ll 2^{\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}{J_{f}(K)}}\cdot O(1)^{d}
. However
\#|G|=\#|(J_{f}[2])(K)|\cdot 2^{\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}{J_{f}(K)}}
and
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
.
∎

1.
Introduction and main results

This short note establishes a Hopf’s boundary point lemma and strong minimum principle for the fractional nonlocal equation

(1.1)

\mathcal{L}u=c(x)g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}\,\,\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\,\Omega,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open set and for
0<s<1
the nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}u
is given by

(1.2)

\mathcal{L}u(x):=\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\frac{K(x,y)}{|x-y|^{s}}dy.

The function
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is continuous, strictly increasing and
g
satisfies
g(0)=0,\displaystyle{\lim_{t\to\infty}}g(t)=\infty.
Furthermore,
g
satisfies the following condition:

(1.3)

1<p_{g}\leq\frac{tg^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\leq q_{g}<\infty,\,\,\,\text{for some}\,\,\,1<p_{g}\leq q_{g}.

Also, we set the following assumptions on the kernel
K(x,y).

K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(0,\infty]
satisfies

•

Symmetry :
K(x,y)=K(y,x)

for all

x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

•

Translation invariance:
K(x+z,y+z)=K(x,y)

for all

x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

•

Growth condition:
\frac{\lambda}{|x-y|^{n}}\leq K(x,y)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x-y|^{n}}

for all

x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and for some

0<\lambda\leq\Lambda.

•

Continuity:
K(x,y)
is continuous in both of its variable.

Note that when
K(x,y)=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},
the operator becomes the so called fractional
g
-Laplacian and it is denoted by
(-\Delta)^{s}_{g}.
The well known fractional Laplacian can be obtained by simply taking
g:=Id.
In recent years, these operators have received a lot of attention due to their applicability in various fields, for example, optimization, finance, phase transitions, thin obstacle problem, optimal transport, image reconstruction, Lev́y processes, game theory, conservation laws, minimal surfaces, materials science, water waves, as well as diffusion problems ( see
[
6
,
19
,
45
]
and the references therein ). The structure of
(-\Delta)_{g}^{s}
naturally suggests to consider
fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
introduced by Bonder and Salort in
[
12
]
. Interested readers can see
[
2
,
3
,
5
]
and the references therein, where some structural properties of fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces have been studied recently. Coming back to the operator
(-\Delta)_{g}^{s},
several topics from the point of view of PDE, such as eigenvalue related problems
[
41
,
42
,
43
]
, Liouville type theorem and symmetry results
[
44
]
, regularity results
[
10
,
15
]
have been addressed very recently.

In this note, we are interested to establish a Hopf lemma and strong minimum principle for the equation (
1.1
) involving a general operator (
1.2
). Hopf boundary point lemma is a classical and one of the most useful results in the theory of elliptic partial differential equations. This result was first proved by E. Hopf in his seminal paper
[
31
]
for second order elliptic PDE without the term
c(x)u.
After this influential work of Hopf, there are various improvements and applications came into the literature, for instance, it is observed that Hopf lemma is useful to validate strong maximum principle for second order uniformly elliptic operators, see
[
36
,
37
]
for a comprehensive overview. Moreover, it has applications to the symmetry results and overdetermined problems for local and nonlocal elliptic equations
[
8
,
26
,
29
,
30
]
. In
[
9
]
, Hopf lemma, maximum principle and certain symmetry results for a general class of nonlocal operators have been recently established.

There is a large class of functions
g
that satisfies (
1.3
). To cite some of them we mention,
g(t)=Ct^{p-1},p>1
and some constant
C>\frac{1}{p-1},

g(t)=t^{\alpha}\log(\beta+\gamma t)
where
\alpha>1,\beta\geq e^{M_{0}}
for some
M_{0}>0
and
\gamma\geq 0.
Furthermore, if
g_{1},g_{2}
are two functions satisfying (
1.3
), then their linear combination
\alpha g_{1}+\beta g_{2}
with
\alpha,\beta\geq 0,
product
g_{1}\cdot g_{2}
and composition
g_{1}\circ g_{2}
also satisfy (
1.3
). When
g(t)=Ct^{p-1}
with
p>1,
the operator (
1.2
) becomes the fractional
p
-Laplacian. Harnack inequality and other regularity results were established for fractional
p
-Laplace equations and nonlocal equations with general growth in
[
20
,
21
,
27
]
. Del Pezzo and Quaas
[
39
]
provided a Hopf lemma and strong minimum principle for the equation of type (
1.1
) when
\mathcal{L}
is fractional
p
-Laplacian. Hopf lemma and strong maximum principles are obtained for other fractional
p
-Laplace equations and their generalizations in
[
22
,
34
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
28
]
. Furthermore, very recently similar kind of study has been carried out for regional fractional Laplacian
[
1
]
and for fractional
(p,q)
-Laplacian operators
[

]
.

1.1.
Main results

In this section we state our main results. The first result is the strong minimum principle.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
c\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
(resp.
c\in C(\overline{\Omega})
) with
c(x)\leq 0
in
\Omega
and
u\in W^{s,G}_{loc}(\Omega)\cap L_{s}^{g}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(resp.
u\in W^{s,G}_{loc}(\Omega)\cap L_{s}^{g}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
) be a weak supersolution to

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u(x)=c(x)g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega.

Then the following conclusions hold:

(1)

For a bounded domain
\Omega,
if
u
satisfies the condition that
u\geq 0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\Omega^{c}
then either
u>0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\Omega
(resp.
u>0
in
\Omega
) or
u=0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2)

If
u\geq 0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\mathbb{R}^{n},
we have either
u>0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\Omega
(resp.
u>0
in
\Omega
) or
u=0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Now we state our Hopf’s boundary point lemma.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\Omega
satisfies an interior ball condition at a point
y\in\partial\Omega,

c\in C(\overline{\Omega})
and
u\in W^{s,G}_{loc}(\Omega)\cap L_{s}^{g}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
be a weak supersolution to

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u(x)=c(x)g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega.

Then the following conclusions hold:

(1)

Let
\Omega
be bounded,
c(x)\leq 0
in
\Omega
and let
u
satisfy the condition that
u\geq 0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\Omega^{c}.
Also, denote

as a ball of radius

from the interior and

Then either

(1.4)

\displaystyle\liminf_{B_{r}(x_{0})\ni x\to y}\frac{u(x)}{\delta^{s}(x)}>0

in

Whenever

2.
Preliminaries and Auxiliary lemmas

This section collects all the necessary definitions, notions of solutions, and lemmas. We start with the
Young function.

2.1.
Young functions

A
C^{2}
-function
G:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called a
Young function
if it is increasing, convex,
G(0)=0,

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{G(t)}{t}=0,\,\,\,\,\,\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{G(t)}{t}=\infty

and
G
satisfies an integral representation

G(t):=\int_{0}^{t}g(\theta)d\theta

for some
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
satisfying the property (
1.3
). We extend
g
in
\mathbb{R}
by defining
g(t)=-g(-t)
for
t<0.
Simply by integrating (
1.3
) and using the fundamental theorem of calculus, we obtain

(2.1)

\displaystyle 2<p\leq\frac{tg(t)}{G(t)}\leq q<\infty\,\,\,\,\textnormal{where}\,\,\,p=p_{g}+1,\,\,q=q_{g}+1.

For a Young function
G
we define its conjugate
G^{*}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
by

(2.2)

\displaystyle G^{*}(t):=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{st-G(s)\},\,\,\,t\geq 0.

Moreover
G
satisfies the following
\Delta_{2}
condition
:

G(2t)\leq 2^{q}G(t)
and using (
2.1
), this certainly implies
g(2t)\leq 2^{q_{g}}g(t).
Further, (
1.3
) implies

\displaystyle a^{q}G(t)\leq G(at)\leq a^{p}G(t)\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,0<a<1\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,a^{p}G(t)\leq G(at)\leq a^{q}G(t)\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,a>1.

More generally, for any
a,t\geq 0,
we have (see
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
)

\displaystyle\min\left\{a^{p_{g}},a^{q_{g}}\right\}g(t)\leq g(at)\leq\max\left\{a^{p_{g}},a^{q_{g}}\right\}g(t)

and

\displaystyle\min\left\{a^{p},a^{q}\right\}G(t)\leq G(at)\leq\max\left\{a^{p},a^{q}\right\}G(t).

Furthermore, we impose the following condition on
g
:

g
is a convex function on
[0,\infty).

This condition corresponds to the degenerate case
p\geq 2
for fractional
p
-Laplacian. Notice that fine boundary regularity estimates are only available for the case
p\geq 2
for fractional
p
-Laplace equation
[
33
]
.

2.2.
Fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

For any open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},
we consider the class of all real valued measurable functions on
\Omega,
denoted by
\mathcal{M}(\Omega).
Given a Young function
G
satisfying
g=G^{\prime}
and
\Delta_{2}
condition, we define the
Orlicz spaces
by

L^{G}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)\,\,\Big|\,\,\int_{\Omega}G\left(|u(x)|\right)dx<\infty\right\}.

This forms a Banach space with the Luxemburg norm:

||u||_{L^{G}(\Omega)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\,\,\Big|\int_{\Omega}G\left(\frac{\left|u(x)\right|}{\lambda}\right)dx\leq 1\right\}.

For
s\in(0,1),
we define the
fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space
as

W^{s,G}:=\left\{u\in L^{G}(\Omega)\,\,\Big|\,\,\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}G\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n}}<\infty\right\}.

This also forms a Banach space with the Luxemburg type norm

||u||_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}:=||u||_{L^{G}(\Omega)}+[u]_{s,G,\Omega}\,\,,

where
[u]_{s,G,\Omega}
is the Gagliardo semi-norm defined as

[u]_{s,G,\Omega}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\,\,\Big|\,\,\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}G\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{\lambda|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n}}\leq 1\right\}.

Further we define the spaces

\displaystyle W^{s,G}_{loc}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{G}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,\,\Big|\,\,u\in W^{s,G}(U)\right\},

where
U
be any open set such that
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset U
for any bounded
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega,
and

W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,\,\Big|\,\,u=0\,\,\,\text{in }\,\,\,\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\right\},

W^{-s,G^{*}}(\Omega):=\left(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{*},

where
G^{*}
denotes the conjugate of
G
defined in (
2.2
). Next, we define

\displaystyle L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,\,\Big|\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{s}}\right)\frac{dx}{(1+|x|)^{n+s}}<\infty\right\},

and the nonlocal tail of
u\in L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for the ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
is define by

\displaystyle\text{Tail}(u,x_{0},R):=\int_{B^{c}_{R}(x_{0})}g\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{|x-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{dx}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+s}}.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that
u\in L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
\text{Tail}(u,x_{0},R)<\infty
for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R>0.
Also if
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then clearly
u\in L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

2.3.
Notion of weak and viscosity solutions

In this section, we recall the notion of weak and viscosity solutions. Let us start with the weak solution.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain. A function
u\in W^{s,G}(\Omega)\cap L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be a weak supersolution (subsolution) of
\mathcal{L}u=f
if

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\left(\eta(x)-\eta(y)\right)K(x,y)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{s}}\geq(\leq)\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\eta(x)dx

holds for all non-negative
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),
where
f\in W^{-s,G^{*}}(\Omega).

We say
u
be a weak solution of
\mathcal{L}u=f
if it is both sub and supersolution.

If
\Omega
is unbounded, then
u\in W^{s,G}_{loc}(\Omega)\cap L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a weak supersolution (subsolution) if (
2.3
) holds for any bounded
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega.

Next, we recall the definition of viscosity solution from
[
13
, Definition 2.1]
. For any open bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\beta\geq 2,
we denote

C^{2}_{\beta}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in C^{2}(\Omega):\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left(\frac{\min\{\delta_{u}(x),1\}^{\beta-1}}{\left|Du(x)\right|}+\frac{\left|D^{2}u(x)\right|}{\delta^{\beta-2}_{u}(x)}\right)<\infty\right\},

where
\delta_{u}(x):=dist(x,N_{u})
and
N_{u}:=\{x\in\Omega:Du(x)=0\}
denotes the set of all critical points of
u.
Further, denote
u_{+}:=\max\{u,0\}
and
u_{-}:=\max\{-u,0\}.
Now we introduce our definition below.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[-\infty,\infty]
is a viscosity supersolution (subsolution) of (
1.1
) in
\Omega
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)
u<+\infty
(
u>-\infty
)
a.e.
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u>-\infty
(
u<\infty
)
a.e.
in
\Omega.

(2)
u
is lower (upper) semicontinuous in
\Omega.

(3) If
\psi\in C^{2}(B_{r}(x_{0}))\cap L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
such that
\psi(x_{0})=u(x_{0})
and
\psi\leq u
(
\psi\geq u
) in
B_{r}(x_{0})
and one of the following holds:

(i)

p>\frac{2}{2-s}
or
D\psi(x_{0})\neq 0,

(ii)

1<p\leq\frac{2}{2-s},D\psi(x_{0})=0
such that
x_{0}
is an isolated point of
\psi
and
\psi\in C^{2}_{\beta}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
for some
\beta>\frac{sp}{p-1},

where
p
is defined in (
2.1
),
then

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\psi_{r}(x_{0})\geq(\leq)\,\,c(x_{0})g(|u(x_{0})|)\frac{u(x_{0})}{|u(x_{0})|},

where

\displaystyle\psi_{r}(x)=\begin{cases}\psi(x)\,\,\,\,\textnormal{in}\,\,\,B_{r}(x_{0})\\
u(x)\,\,\,\,\textnormal{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(4)
u_{-}(u_{+})\in L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

A function
u
is said to be a viscosity solution to (
1.1
) if it is both viscosity sub and supersolution.

Remark 2.2
.

One basic difference between the definitions given in
[
13
]
and
[
32
]
is following:
[
13
]
needed a larger set of test functions
\psi
such that
\psi\leq u
in
\mathbb{R}^{n},
since the non-homogeneous term depends on

D^{s}_{g}u(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)K(x,y)dy

as well. As pointed out in
[
7
, Remark 2.3]
, if the non-homogeneous term depends only on
x
and
u
then one can allow
\psi_{r}
as a test function where
\psi\leq u
only in
B_{r}(x_{0}).
In our case using
\psi_{r}
in (
2.4
) is not restrictive.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\frac{(\eta(x)-\eta(y))K(x,y)}{|x-y|^{s}}dxdy\leq\int_{\Omega}f(x)\eta(x)dx

Multiplying the above inequality (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\frac{\eta(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}K(x,y)\frac{dydx}{|x-y|^{s}}\leq\int_{\Omega}f(x)\eta(x)dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\frac{\eta(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}K(x,y)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{s}}\leq\int_{\Omega}f(y)\eta(y)dy.

Adding the inequalities (

Proposition 2.3

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{3r}{2}}}c(x)g(u(x))\frac{(u(x)+\alpha)\phi^{q}(x)}{G(\frac{(u(x)+\alpha)}{r^{s}})}dx

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
c(x)
be a continuous function in
\overline{\Omega}.
If

is a viscosity supersolution of (

).

Proof.

By the assumptions on

Definition

2.2
are satisfied. To check 3, we proceed by contradiction. On the contrary, suppose there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
\psi\in C^{2}(B_{r}(x_{0}))\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(a)\,\,u(x_{0})=\psi(x_{0}),

(b)
either
(i)
or
(ii)
in 3 holds, and
(c)

\mathcal{L}\psi(x_{0})<c(x_{0})g(|u(x_{0})|)\frac{u(x_{0})}{|u(x_{0})|}.

Then by the continuity of

(cf.

2.3
), there exists a
\kappa\in(0,r)
such that for all

2.2
we conclude that there exist
\tilde{\theta}>0,\rho\in(0,\frac{\kappa}{2})

Consequently, we have

\sup_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\mathcal{L}\psi_{\theta}(x)\right|<c(x)g(|u(x)|)\frac{u(x)}{|u(x)|}.

Moreover, we have
\psi_{\theta}\leq u
in

2.4

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|\psi_{\theta}(x)-\psi_{\theta}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{\psi_{\theta}(x)-\psi_{\theta}(y)}{|\psi_{\theta}(x)-\psi_{\theta}(y)|}\frac{(\eta(x)-\eta(y))K(x,y)}{|x-y|^{s}}dxdy

3.
Strong minimum principle

The Aim of this section is to prove
Theorem

1.1
. We start with some preparatory lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
c\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega),

c(x)\leq 0
and
u
be a weak supersolution of (
1.1
). Then we have the following conclusions:

(1)

If
u\geq 0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
and
u=0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\Omega,
then
u=0\,\,\,a.e.
in all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2)

If
u\geq 0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
for a bounded domain
\Omega,
then
u\geq 0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\Omega.

Proof.

Take any non negative test function
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).
Since
u=0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\Omega
and
\eta=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,
from the definition of weak solution, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\frac{(\eta(x)-\eta(y))K(x,y)}{|x-y|^{s}}dxdy

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}g\left(\frac{\left|u(x)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}\right)\frac{u(x)}{\left|u(x)\right|}\frac{\eta(y)}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}K(x,y)dxdy\geq 0.

Using the fact that
u\geq 0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
we get
u=0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.
Thus combining
u=0\,\,\,a.e.
in
\Omega,
we have
u=0\,\,\,a.e
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This proves
(1).

Note that, since
u\in W^{s,G}_{loc}(\Omega)
and
u\geq 0\,\,\,a.e
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,
we get
u_{-}\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega).
Then using
u_{-}
as a test function by definition of supersolution, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\frac{(u_{-}(x)-u_{-}(y))K(x,y)}{|x-y|^{s}}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}c(x)g(|u|)\frac{u}{|u|}u_{-}(x)dx\geq 0.

In the last inequality, we used
c(x)\leq 0
and
\frac{u(x)u_{-}(x)}{|u(x)|}
is always non-positive for any
x\in\Omega
. On the other hand, noting that the term
(u(x)-u(y))(u_{-}(x)-u_{-}(y))\leq 0
for any
x,y\in\Omega
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\frac{(u_{-}(x)-u_{-}(y))K(x,y)}{|x-y|^{s}}dxdy\leq 0.

Since
u_{-}
is zero outside of
\Omega
and the above inequalities show that
u_{-}
is constant inside
\Omega,
using
[
38
, Proposition 2.9]
we conclude that
u_{-}
is identically zero in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
This proves the second part of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
c\in C(\overline{B_{R}(x_{0})})
be non-positive and

) in

Our aim is to show that if

First, using

Now for any

Therefore by the definition of viscosity supersolution,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\psi^{\epsilon}_{r}(\tilde{x})\geq c(\tilde{x})g(|u(\tilde{x})|)\frac{u(\tilde{x})}{|u(\tilde{x})|}=0,

from the interior ball condition we deduce for any

there exists a ball
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
such that
y\in\partial B_{r}
and the proof follows.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

First we make an observation about non-positivity of
c(x).
In
Theorem

1.1
, when
c,u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
and
c(x)\leq 0,
for a bounded domain
\Omega
with
u\geq 0
in
\Omega^{c}
we conclude that either
u>0
in
\Omega
or
u=0\,\,a.e.
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
The same conclusion holds if
u\geq 0\,\,a.e.
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
In the second part of
Theorem

1.2
no non-positivity assumption is made on
c(x).
If
u\geq 0\,\,a.e
in
\mathbb{R}^{n},
since
c(x)\geq-c_{-}(x),

u
is a weak supersolution of

\mathcal{L}u=-c_{-}(x)g(u)\,\,\,\,\textnormal{in}\,\,\,\,\Omega.

Hence by
Theorem

1.1
(
Case 2
) either
u>0
in
\Omega,
in particular
\Omega=B_{r}(x_{0})
or
u=0,\,\,a.e.
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Suppose
u\not\equiv 0
in
B_{r}(x_{0}),
and we need to show (
1.4
) holds for all
y\in\partial B_{r}.
Using
Lemma

2.1
and
Proposition

2.2
, for any compact set
K\subset B_{r}\setminus B_{\rho},
and
d>0
we obtain that
\delta^{s}+d\chi_{K}
is a weak solution of

\mathcal{L}(\delta^{s}+d\chi_{K})=f+h_{d}\,\,\,\,\textnormal{in}\,\,\,\,B_{\rho},

where

\displaystyle h_{d}(x):=2\int_{K}\Big[

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{\left|\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)-d\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}\right)\frac{\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)-d}{\left|\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)-d\right|}

\displaystyle-g\left(\frac{\left|\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}\right)\frac{\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)}{\left|\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)\right|}\Big]K(x,y)\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}

\displaystyle=2\int_{K}\Big[

\displaystyle\frac{g\left(\frac{\left|\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)-d\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}\right)}{\frac{\left|\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)-d\right|}{|x-y|^{s}}}\cdot\frac{\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)-d}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}

\displaystyle-g\left(\frac{\left|\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}\right)\frac{\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)}{\left|\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)\right|}\Big]K(x,y)\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{K}\Big[

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{g}}g^{\prime}\left(\frac{\left|\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)-d\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}\right)\frac{\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)-d}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}

\displaystyle-g\left(\frac{\left|\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}\right)\frac{\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)}{\left|\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)\right|}\Big]K(x,y)\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}.

Note that in the last inequality we used (
1.3
) and
\frac{\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)-d}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}\leq 0.
Since
g
and
g^{\prime}
are continuous and
K
is compact

\displaystyle h_{d}(x)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(||g^{\prime}||_{L^{\infty}(K)}+||g||_{L^{\infty}(K)}\right)\int_{K}\Big[\frac{\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)-d}{q_{g}\left|x-y\right|^{s}}+\frac{\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)}{\left|\delta^{s}(x)-\delta^{s}(y)\right|}\Big]K(x,y)\frac{dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}

\displaystyle\to-\infty\,\,\,\,\textnormal{in}\,\,\,B_{\rho}\,\,\,\,\textnormal{as}\,\,\,\,d\to\infty.

In the previous conclusion we also make use of
\delta^{s}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
dist\left(K,B_{\rho}\right)>0.
Now we can choose a large
d
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{\rho}}f(x)+h_{d}(x)\leq\inf_{B_{\rho}}c(x)g(u(x)).

Furthermore, for a small
\kappa>0,
we have
\kappa(r^{s}+d)\leq\displaystyle{\inf_{B_{r}\setminus B_{\rho}}}u(x)
and thus we find that
\kappa(\delta^{s}+d\chi_{K})\leq u
in
B^{c}_{\rho}.
Now using (
4.1
) and the definition of weak supersolution, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}(\eta(x)-\eta(y))K(x,y)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{s}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|\kappa v(x)-\kappa v(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|v(x)-v(y)|}(\eta(x)-\eta(y))K(x,y)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{s}}

where
v=\delta^{s}+d\chi_{K}.
Therefore by using comparison principle (cf.
Proposition

2.1
) we obtain
\kappa v\leq u
in
B_{\rho}.
Hence

\kappa\leq\frac{u(x)}{\delta^{s}(x)}\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,x\in B_{\rho}.

Thus we find

\liminf_{B_{r}(x_{0})\ni x\to y}\frac{u(x)}{\delta^{s}(x)}>0,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,y\in\partial B_{r}.

This completes the proof.

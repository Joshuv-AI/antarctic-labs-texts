On Exponential Convergence of Random Variables
Dawid Tarłowski

Abstract
Given the discrete-time sequence of nonnegative random variables, general dependencies between the exponential convergence of the expectations, exponential convergence of the trajectories and the logarithmic growth of the corresponding expected hitting times are analysed. The applications are presented: the general results are applied to the areas of optimization, stochastic control and estimation.

2
Exponential convergence of random variables

In the whole paper we assume that
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mathbb{P})
is a probability space and that
X_{t}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
, is a sequence of random variables, where
\mathbb{R}^{+}=[0,+\infty)
. We start with the following definition.

Definition 1
.

Given the sequence
x_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
, define:

\overline{R}(x_{t})=\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{x_{t}}.

We are mostly interested in the convergence to
0
and thus we will usually refer to
\overline{R}(x_{t})
as convergence rate. If
x_{t}\to 0
then
\overline{R}(x_{t})\in[0,1]
. Condition
\overline{R}(x_{t})\in(0,1)
determines the exponential convergence of
x_{t}
to zero. We will sometimes slightly abuse the nomenclature and we will refer to
\overline{R}(x_{t})
as convergence rate regardless of whether the
x_{t}
converges or not. Condition
\overline{R}(x_{t})=1
excludes the exponential convergence and does not determine the convergence of
x_{t}
. Condition
\overline{R}(x_{t})>1
implies that some subsequence of
x_{t}
diverges exponentially fast. The following observation presents general characterisation of exponential convergence and is rather easy to prove.

Observation 1
.

Given
C\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and a sequence
x_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the following conditions are equivalent:

1.

C\geq\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{x_{t}}
,

2.

\log C\geq\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log(x_{t})
, where
\log(\cdot)=\exp^{-1}(\cdot)
and
\log(0):=-\infty
,

3.

for any
R>C
,
\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{x_{t}}{R^{t}}<+\infty
.

4.

for any
R>C
,
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{x_{t}}{R^{t}}=0
,

5.

for any
R>C
,
\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{x_{t}}{R^{t}}<\infty
,

6.

for any
R>C
,
\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\sum\limits_{i=t}^{\infty}\frac{x_{i}}{R^{i}}<\infty
.

If additionally
C\in(0,1)
and
\tau_{\varepsilon}:=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{N}\colon x_{t}<\varepsilon\},\ \varepsilon>0,
then any of the above conditions implies that

\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\tau_{\varepsilon}}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}\leq\frac{1}{|\log(C)|}.

(5)

Observation
1
implies that the value of the limit
\overline{R}(x_{t})=\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{x_{t}}
is the infimum of constants
C\geq 0
such that for any
R>C
:

x_{t}\leq M_{R}\cdot R^{t},\ t\in\mathbb{N},\mbox{ where }M_{R}:=\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{x_{t}}{R^{t}}<\infty.

(6)

Equation (
6
) is satisfied for any
R>\overline{R}(x_{t})
.

Given any measurable sequence
X_{t}\geq 0
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
, we may consider the random variable
C\colon\Omega\to[0,+\infty]
given by

C:=\overline{R}(X_{t})=\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{X_{t}}.

(7)

Below, if
E[X_{t}]=\infty
then we put
\sqrt[t]{\infty}:=\infty
. Theorem
2
does not assume that
X_{t}\to 0
and may describe the divergence of
X_{t}
to infinity.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X_{t}\geq 0
. The convergence rate of the trajectories is bounded from above by the convergence rate of the expectations in sense

\mathbb{P}[\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{X_{t}}\leq\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{E[X_{t}]}]=1.

Proof.

For the proof it is enough to assume that
A:=\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{E[X_{t}]}<\infty
and it is enough to show that for any
\varepsilon>0

\mathbb{P}[\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{X_{t}}>A+\varepsilon]=0.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and note that
\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{X_{t}}=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\sup\limits_{i\geq t}\sqrt[i]{X_{i}}
. We thus have

\mathbb{P}[\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{X_{t}}>A+\varepsilon]=\mathbb{P}[\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\sup\limits_{i\geq t}\sqrt[i]{X_{i}}>A+\varepsilon].

By the monotonicity
\sup\limits_{i\geq t+1}\sqrt[i]{X_{i}}\leq\sup\limits_{i\geq t}\sqrt[i]{X_{i}}
, we have

\{\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\sup\limits_{i\geq t}\sqrt[i]{X_{i}}>A+\varepsilon\}\subset\bigcap\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\{\sup\limits_{i\geq t}\sqrt[i]{X_{i}}>A+\varepsilon\}

and

\mathbb{P}[\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\sup\limits_{i\geq t}\sqrt[i]{X_{i}}>A+\varepsilon]\leq\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[\sup\limits_{i\geq t}\sqrt[i]{X_{i}}>A+\varepsilon]=

=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[\bigcup\limits_{i=t}^{+\infty}\{X_{i}>(A+\varepsilon)^{i}\}]\leq\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=t}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[X_{i}>(A+\varepsilon)^{i}].

By Chebyshev inequality

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=t}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[X_{i}>(A+\varepsilon)^{i}]\leq\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=t}^{\infty}\frac{E[X_{i}]}{(A+\varepsilon)^{i}},

and by point 5. of Observation
1
(with
x_{t}:=E[X_{t}]
and
R=A+\varepsilon
),

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=t}^{\infty}\frac{E[X_{i}]}{(A+\varepsilon)^{i}}=0.

∎

Conclusion 3
.

The convergence rate of the expectations
E[X_{t}]
is bounded from below by the essential suppremum of
\overline{R}(X_{t})
, i.e.

\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{E[X_{t}]}\geq\inf\{\hat{C}\in\mathbb{R}\colon\mathbb{P}[\overline{R}(X_{t})\leq\hat{C}]=1\}.

Now we will focus on the convergence of the expectations of the following hitting times

\tau_{\epsilon}=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{N}\colon X_{t}<\varepsilon\},\ \varepsilon>0.

Assume from now on that
X_{t}\to 0
a.s. so
\overline{R}(X_{t})=\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{X_{t}}\leq 1
a.s. Let

C:=ess\sup\overline{R}(X_{t})

denote the essential supremum of
\overline{R}(X_{t})
. By point 3. of Observation
1
for any
R>C
there is some random variable
M_{R}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with

X_{t}\leq M_{R}\cdot R^{t}\ a.s.

If
C\in(0,1)
then again by Observation
1
,

\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\tau_{\varepsilon}}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}\leq\frac{-1}{\log(C)}\ a.s.

At the same time the expectation of
\tau_{\epsilon}
may be infinite, see a simple example below.

Example 1
.

Let
\Omega=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
,
\Sigma=\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
be the family of all subsets of
\Omega
and let
p_{n}:=\mathbb{P}[n],n\in\Omega
. Assume that
X_{t}
is the characteristic function of the complement of the set
\mathbb{N}_{t}:=\{0,1,\dots,t\}
, i.e.

X_{t}(n)=1_{\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}_{t}}(n),\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Clearly we have
X_{t}(n)=0\Leftrightarrow n\leq t
. Hence,
\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{X_{t}}=0
. At the same time, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
we have

\tau_{\varepsilon}(n)={n},\ n\in\Omega,

and

E[\tau_{\varepsilon}]=\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}[\tau_{\varepsilon}>t]=\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}[X_{t}=1]=\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}_{t}]=\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{n>t}p_{n}.

The above sum may be infinite depending on how the probabilities
p_{n}
are chosen. At the same time
\overline{R}(X_{t})=0
and
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\tau_{\varepsilon}}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}=0
a.s.

If the expectations
E[X_{t}]
decrease exponentially fast then
E[\tau_{\varepsilon}]
is finite and Theorem
4
shows that the convergence rate of the expectations
\overline{R}(E[X_{t}])
controls the convergence of
E[\tau_{\varepsilon}]
with
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

Theorem 4
.

If
A:=\overline{R}(E[X_{t}])=\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{E[X_{t}]}<1
then

\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{E[\tau_{\varepsilon}]}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}\leq\frac{-1}{\log(A)}.

Above, if
A=0
then
\frac{-1}{\log(A)}:=0
. The proof of the above theorem is based on the following lemma which follows from Theorem
2
and Observation
1
.

Lemma 5
.

Assume that
A=\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{E[X_{t}]}<1
. For any
R>A

the following random variable

H_{R}:=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{N}\colon\sup\limits_{i\geq t}\frac{X_{i}}{R^{i}}\ \leq\ 1\}

has finite expectation.

Proof.

Fix
R>A
. By Theorem
2
and by point 4. of Observation
1
we have
\mathbb{P}[H_{R}<\infty]=1
. We need to show
E[H_{R}]<\infty
. We have:

E[H_{R}]=\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}[H_{R}>t].

By definition of
H_{R}
and by Chebyszev inequality,

\mathbb{P}[H_{R}>t]=\mathbb{P}[\bigcup\limits_{i=t}^{\infty}\{X_{i}>R^{i}\}]\leq\sum\limits_{i=t}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[X_{i}>R^{i}]\leq\sum\limits_{i=t}^{\infty}\frac{E[X_{i}]}{R^{i}}.

We have thus shown that

E[H_{R}]\leq\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\sum\limits_{i=t}^{\infty}\frac{E[X_{i}]}{R^{i}}

and the above series is finite by point 6. of Observarion
1
applied to the sequence
x_{t}=E[X_{t}]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4
. Fix
C\in(A,1)
. Under notation of Lemma
5
, for any
t\in\mathbb{N}
on set
\{t\geq H_{C}\}
we have
X_{t+i}\leq C^{t+i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. In other words, for any
t\geq H_{C}
we have
X_{t}\leq C^{t}
. At the same time, as
C<1
, note that for

\hat{C}:=\frac{-1}{log(C)}

the ceiling
t=\lceil\hat{C}\cdot|\log(\varepsilon)|\rceil
is the smallest natural number with
C^{t}\leq\varepsilon
. This implies that if
t\geq\max\{H_{C},\lceil\hat{C}\cdot|\log(\varepsilon)|\rceil\}

then
X_{t}\leq\varepsilon
and
X_{t+1}<\varepsilon
. Hence,

\tau_{\varepsilon}\leq\max\{H_{C},\lceil\hat{C}\cdot|\log(\varepsilon)|\rceil\}+1

and

\frac{\tau_{\varepsilon}}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}\leq\frac{1}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}(\max\{H_{C},\lceil\hat{C}\cdot|\log(\varepsilon)|\rceil\}+1).

(8)

Thus

E[\frac{\tau_{\varepsilon}}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}]\leq\frac{1}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}E[\max\{H_{C},\lceil\hat{C}\cdot|\log(\varepsilon)|\rceil\}]+\frac{1}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}.

(9)

Lemma
5
allows us to use the Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem:

\frac{1}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}E[\max\{H_{C},\lceil\hat{C}\cdot|\log(\varepsilon)|\rceil\}]=E[\max\{\frac{H_{C}}{|\log(\varepsilon)|},\hat{C}\}]\to\hat{C}\mbox{ with }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

Equation (
9
) and the above imply that
\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{E[\tau]}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}\leq\hat{C}\mbox{ for any }C\in(A,1)
and thus

\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{E[\tau_{\varepsilon}]}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}\leq\hat{A}=\frac{-1}{\log(A)}.

\Box

3
Applications.

The results of the previous sections will be applied to some problems of optimization, control and estimation.

3.1
Optimization

Let
(K,d)
be some metric space and
f\colon K\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a Borel-measurable function which attains its global minimum
f_{\min}
. Assume that
X_{t}\in K
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
, represents an optimization process. The hitting time of the
\varepsilon
-optimal sublevel set
\{x\in A\colon f(x)<f_{\min}+\varepsilon\}
is defined by

\tau_{\varepsilon}=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{N}\colon f(X_{t})<f_{\min}+\epsilon\}.

Theorems
2
and
4
give us the relations between convergence rate of the trajectories
f(X_{t})
, the convergence rate of the expectations
E[|f(X_{t})-f_{\min}|]
and the convergence behaviour of
E[\tau_{\varepsilon}]
under general assumptions on the sequence
f(X_{t})
. In particular, if the following constant

A=\overline{R}(E[f(X_{t})-f_{\min}],t\in\mathbb{N})=\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{E[f(X_{t})-f_{\min}]}

satisfies
A<1
then by Theorem
4
we have the following upper bound
\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{E[\tau_{\varepsilon}]}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}\leq\frac{-1}{\log(A)}
and the following control of the asymptotic behaviour of
E[\tau_{\varepsilon}]
with
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
:

E[\tau_{\varepsilon}]\leq C(\varepsilon)\cdot|\log(\varepsilon)|,

where
C\colon(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfies
\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}C(\varepsilon)\leq\hat{A}=\frac{-1}{\log(A)}
. This strengthens Theorem 7 from
[
17
]
. We also have, by (
2
) and (
4
),

\mathbb{P}[\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{f(X_{t})-f_{min}}\leq A]=1\mbox{ and }\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\sqrt[t]{\mathbb{P}[f(X_{t})\geq f_{\min}+\varepsilon]}\leq A.

3.2
Risk sensitive control

Let
W_{t}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
, represent the wealth process of an investor, see
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
13
]
for details. Let
\gamma\neq 0
represent the risk-averse parameter of an investor so we are interested in the limit of the long-run risk-sensitive criterion of the following log wealth growth:

C=\liminf\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\frac{1}{\gamma}\log(E[(W_{t})^{\gamma}]).

(10)

We will assume that
\gamma<0
which is a common investment criterion in the context of long run optimization. This section shows how the value of the risk-sensitive criterion determines the exponential growth of the trajectories of the wealth process
W_{t}
and the logarithmic growth of the expected barrier-hitting time
T_{b}
with
b\nearrow\infty
. This type of convergence behaviour is natural under suitable ergodicity-type assumptions on the log-wealth process, see for instance the asymptotic optimality principle,
[
2
]
. This section brings to attention that the log growth rate of the trajectories of
W_{t}
and the logarithmic growth of the corresponding expected hitting times are determined by the limit
(
10
)
under general assumptions on
W_{t}
(no ergodicity involved) which follows from the results of Section
2
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\gamma<0
and let
W_{t}\colon\Omega\to(0,\infty)
satisfy
E[(W_{t})^{\gamma}]<\infty
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
. The constant

C=\liminf\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\frac{1}{\gamma}\log(E[(W_{t})^{\gamma}])

determines the log-growth of
W_{t}
according to

\liminf\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log W_{t}\geq C\ a.s.

Additionally, if
C>0
then the following hitting times
T_{b}=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{N}\colon W_{t}>b\},\ b\in\mathbb{R}^{+},
are integrable and satisfy

\limsup\limits_{b\to+\infty}\frac{E[T_{b}]}{\log(b)}\leq\frac{1}{C}.

Let
X_{t}:=\frac{1}{W_{t}}
. Recall that

E[(X_{t})^{p_{1}}]^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\leq E[(X_{t})^{p_{2}}]^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}}\mbox{ for }0<p_{1}<p_{2}<\infty,

(11)

assuming that the above expectations exist. For
\gamma<0
let

C_{\gamma}=\liminf\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\frac{1}{\gamma}\log(E[(W_{t})^{\gamma}]).

By elementary calculations and by (
11
) one may show that

C_{\gamma_{1}}\leq C_{\gamma_{2}}\mbox{ for }\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}<0.

(12)

See also Lemma 2.1 in
[
10
]
for the above. Theorem
6
and Equation (
12
) immediately lead to the following.

Conclusion 7
.

Assume that
W_{t}\colon\Omega\to(0,\infty)
satisfy
E[(W_{t})^{\gamma}]<\infty
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
, for any
\gamma<0
close to zero. The following limit

C=\lim\limits_{\gamma\to 0^{-}}\liminf\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\frac{1}{\gamma}\log(E[(W_{t})^{\gamma}])

bounds the log-growth of
W_{t}
according to
\liminf\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log W_{t}\geq C\ a.s.

Additionally, if
C>0
then the hitting times
T_{b}=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{N}\colon W_{t}>b\}
are integrable and satisfy

\limsup\limits_{b\to+\infty}\frac{E[T_{b}]}{\log(b)}\leq\frac{1}{C}.

Proof of Theorem
6
. As
\gamma<0
, equation (
10
) implies that

C\cdot\gamma=\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log(E[(W_{t})^{\gamma}]).

Hence,

\exp(C\gamma)=\limsup\limits\limits_{t\to\infty}(E[(W_{t})^{\gamma}])^{\frac{1}{t}}.

(13)

By Theorem
2
,

\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}((W_{t})^{\gamma})^{\frac{1}{t}}\leq\exp(C\cdot\gamma)\ a.s.

As
\gamma<0
, the above determines the growth of
W_{t}
according to

\liminf\limits_{t\to\infty}((W_{t})^{|\gamma|})^{\frac{1}{t}}\geq\exp(C\cdot|\gamma|)\ a.s.

Hence, we have

\liminf\limits_{t\to\infty}(W_{t})^{\frac{1}{t}}\geq\exp(C)\ a.s.

(14)

and

\liminf\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log W_{t}\geq C\ a.s.

which proves the first part of the theorem.

Now we assume that
C>0
and we will discuss the hitting times
T_{b}=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{N}\colon W_{t}>b\}.

Let

\tau_{\varepsilon}=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{N}\colon(W_{t})^{\gamma}<\varepsilon\}=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{N}\colon W_{t}>(\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon})^{|\gamma|^{-1}}\}.

Condition
C>0
implies
\exp(\gamma\cdot C)<1
. By (
13
) and Theorem
4
, we have

\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{E[\tau_{\varepsilon}]}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}\leq\frac{1}{C\cdot|\gamma|}.

(15)

Let

T(\varepsilon)=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{N}\colon W_{t}>\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\}

so we have
\tau_{\varepsilon}=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{N}\colon W_{t}>\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{|\gamma|^{-1}}}\}=T(\varepsilon^{|\gamma|^{-1}})
and

\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{E[\tau_{\varepsilon}]}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}=\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{E[T(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{|\gamma|}})]}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}=\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{E[T(\varepsilon)]}{|\log(\varepsilon^{|\gamma|})|}=\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{E[T(\varepsilon)]}{|\gamma|\cdot|\log(\varepsilon)|}.

Hence, by (
15
),
\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{E[T(\varepsilon)]}{|\gamma|\cdot|\log(\varepsilon)|}\leq\frac{1}{|\gamma|\cdot C}
, and thus

\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{E[T(\varepsilon)]}{|\log(\varepsilon)|}\leq\frac{1}{C}.

For
b=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
we have
T_{b}=T(\varepsilon)
and
|\log(\varepsilon)|=\log(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})=\log(b)
. We thus have

\limsup\limits_{b\to+\infty}\frac{E[T_{b}]}{\log(b)}\leq\frac{1}{C}.

3.3
Estimation

Assume that a sequence
Z_{t}\in\mathbb{R}
approaches the unknown parameter
\theta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}
and that the mean squared error
E|Z_{t}-\theta|^{2}
satisfies

C=\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}(E|Z_{t}-\theta|^{2})^{\frac{1}{t}}.

The results of Section
2
can be applied to the sequence
X_{t}=|Z_{t}-\theta|^{2}
. In particular, by Theorem
2
, we bound the convergence rate of the trajectories

\mathbb{P}[\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}(|Z_{t}-\theta|^{2})^{\frac{1}{t}}\leq C]=1\mbox{ and hence }\mathbb{P}[\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}(|Z_{t}-\theta|)^{\frac{1}{t}}\leq\sqrt{C}]=1.

More generally, if
(K,d)
is a metric space,
\theta\in K
and
Z_{t}\in K
converges in mean to
\theta
, i.e.
E[d(Z_{t},\theta)]\to 0
, then the results of Section
2
may be applied to the sequence
X_{t}:=d(Z_{t},\theta)
.

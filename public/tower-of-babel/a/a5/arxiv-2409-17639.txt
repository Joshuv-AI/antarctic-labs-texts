Electrification of a non-rotating black hole
Ken-ichi Nakao 1,2,3, Kenta Matsuo 1, Hirotaka Yoshino 1,2,3, Hideki Ishihara 2

Abstract
Zajaček et al made an interesting theoretical prediction on the electrification of a non-rotating black hole; if a non-rotating black hole is surrounded by plasma composed of protons and electrons, it will acquire electric charge due to the large difference of the inertial mass of a proton and that of an electron. Furthermore they revealed the effects of the electric charge of the black hole on the surrounding plasma. Since their results mainly rely on non-relativistic analyses, we study the same subject through relativistic analyses in this paper. By investigating a test particle in the Schwarzschild spacetime, we find that if initial velocities of protons and electrons far from a black hole follow the Maxwell distribution, the black hole can acquire electric charge whose value depends on the ratio of temperature of the proton and that of the electron. We also show that if the black hole acquires the electric charge, the radii of the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) and the specific energy of a charged particle on ISCO can be very different from those of a neutral particle. In contrast to the result obtained by Zajaček et al , we find that the ISCO radii of a proton and an electron are necessarily larger than that of a neutral test particle as long as the black hole acquires the charge. The large ISCO radius might lead to a larger angular diameter of a black hole shadow and a different estimate of the released energy due to the accretion of plasma from the estimate based on the assumption of electric neutrality of the central black hole.

I
Introduction

The black hole is a central subject in the study of strong gravity. It is defined as a complement of the causal past
of the future null infinity
[
1
]
, and so not observable by its definition. However, nontrivial
physical phenomena will occur due to the strong gravitational field around it; various active astrophysical phenomena are considered to
come from it. For example, energies of relativistic jets from active galactic nuclei (see, for example, Ref.
[
2
]
)
are considered to be supplied by the gravitational energy of matter accreting to the black hole
[
3
,
4
,
5
]

or the extraction of the rotational energy stored in the ergosphere of a rotating black hole
[
6
]
.

In the study of astrophysical black holes, they have usually been assumed to be electrically neutral. However, there are several
studies on the significance of electrically charged black hole in the context of the astrophysics. The most attracted electrification mechanism of a black hole
was given by Wald
[
7
]
: if a rotating black hole with the mass
M
and the Kerr parameter
a
, i.e., the angular momentum divided by
Mc

is immersed in the external homogeneous magnetic field
B
, it will be electrified until the acquired charge reaches the the value

8\pi\varepsilon_{0}GMaB/c
, where
\varepsilon_{0}
,
c
and
G
are the permittivity of vacuum, the speed of light and Newton’s gravitational constant.
Then, effects of the electric field due to the electric charge of a black hole
have been studied in various astrophysical contexts
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
.

Zajaček et al. studied the electrification of a non-rotating black hole
[
8
]
, based on mainly the non-relativistic analyses.
Although they studied this subject also in the framework of general relativity,
we believe that more detailed relativistic analyses of this topic are needed.
Thus, in this paper, we explore the possibility of the electrification of a non-rotating black hole in the framework of general relativity
by investigating the motion of a proton and an electron as test particles in the Schwarzschild spacetime, and estimate
the charge acquired by a non-rotating black hole in plasma. We also study the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO)
of the proton and the electron.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec. II, we reconsider the motion of a test particle in the Schwarzschild spacetime,
and show that if initial velocities of protons and electrons far from a black hole follow the Maxwell distribution, the probability for the proton to enter the black hole
will be different from that of the electron, and as a result, the black hole will acquire the electric charge.
In Sec. III, we estimate the electric charge acquired by the black hole along the discussion on the probability made in Sec. II.
In Sec. IV, the ISCO of a proton and an electron in the spacetime with the electrified black hole are shown.
Section V is devoted to a summary.

In this paper, we adopt the abstract index notation in which the Latin indices, except for
t
and
r
which are
assigned to the time and radial coordinates, represent a type of a tensor,
whereas the Greek indices, except for
\theta
and
\varphi
which are assigned to the coordinates on a sphere,
represent the components of a tensor with respect to the coordinate bases,
and follow the sign convention of the metric tensor in Ref.
[
12
]
and the SI unit.

II
Motion of a proton and an electron in the Schwarzschild spacetime

In this section, we reconsider the motion of a test particle in the spacetime with a non-rotating black hole, i.e.,
the Schwarzschild spacetime. The infinitesimal world interval of the Schwarzschild spacetime is given as

ds^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=-f(r)c^{2}dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2}\right),

(1)

where, with
r_{\rm g}
as a positive constant,

f(r)=1-\frac{r_{\rm g}}{r}.

(2)

The constant
r_{\rm g}
is called the gravitational radius, or the Schwarzschild radius, which is related to the gravitational mass of the system
M
through

r_{\rm g}=\dfrac{2GM}{c^{2}},

(3)

and is the radius of the black hole which corresponds to the domain of
r\leq r_{\rm g}
.

The world line of a massive test particle is a timelike geodesic whose unit tangent is called the four-velocity of the particle and denoted
by
u^{\mu}=\dfrac{dx^{\mu}}{d\tau}
, where
x^{\mu}
and
\tau
represent the coordinates and the proper time of the particle, respectively.
The four-velocity is determined by the geodesic equation

u^{a}\nabla_{a}u_{b}=0,

(4)

where
\nabla_{a}
is the covariant derivative.

Since the time and azimuthal coordinate bases are Killing vectors, the time and azimuthal components of Eq. (
4
) are

\frac{du_{t}}{d\tau}=0~~~~{\rm and}~~~~\frac{du_{\varphi}}{d\tau}=0,

(5)

respectively. Thus, with
E
and
L
as constants, we have

cf\frac{dt}{d\tau}=E~~~{\rm and}~~~r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\frac{d\varphi}{d\tau}=L_{z}.

(6)

Substituting these results into the
\theta
-component of the geodesic equations, we have

\frac{d}{d\tau}\left[\left(r^{2}\frac{d\theta}{d\tau}\right)^{2}+\frac{L_{z}^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}\right]=0.

(7)

An integration of this equation leads to

r^{2}\left(\frac{d\theta}{d\tau}\right)^{2}+\frac{L_{z}^{2}}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}=\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}},

(8)

where
L
is a constant of integration.
The integration constants
E
,
L_{z}
and
L
correspond to the specific energy divided by the speed of light
c
,

z
-component of the specific angular momentum and the total specific angular momentum, respectively.

Here, for notational simplicity, we introduce following dimensionless quantities.

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle:=\frac{r}{r_{\rm g}},

(9)

\displaystyle{\cal T}

\displaystyle:=\frac{c\tau}{r_{\rm g}},

(10)

\displaystyle{\cal E}

\displaystyle:=\frac{E}{c},

(11)

\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle:=\frac{L}{cr_{\rm g}}.

(12)

Then the normalization condition of the
four-velocity
g_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}=-c^{2}
leads to

\left(\frac{dR}{d{\cal T}}\right)^{2}+V(R;{\cal E},{\cal L}^{2})=0,

(13)

where

\displaystyle V(R;{\cal E},{\cal L}^{2})

\displaystyle=-{\cal E}^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{R}\right)\left(1+\frac{{\cal L}^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=1-{\cal E}^{2}-\frac{1}{R}+\frac{{\cal L}^{2}}{R^{2}}-\frac{{\cal L}^{2}}{R^{3}}

(14)

is the effective potential. The allowed domain for the motion of the test particle is determined by an inequality
V(R;{\cal E},{\cal L}^{2})=-(dR/d\tau)^{2}\leq 0
.

In this section, we consider the situation in which the test particle falls from infinity.
Thus,
{\cal E}
has to be larger than or equal to one. Such motion is called the unbound or marginally bound one.
Here, note that
{\cal E}
has to be positive so that
t
is an increasing function of the proper time
\tau

in the domain outside the black hole,
R>1
.
The extremum of the effective potential is determined by
\partial_{R}V(R;{\cal E},{\cal L})=0
which is equivalent to

R^{2}-2{\cal L}^{2}R+3{\cal L}^{2}=0.

(15)

The roots of this equation are given as

R=R_{\pm}:={\cal L}^{2}\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-\frac{3}{{\cal L}^{2}}}\right).

(16)

We can easily see that the derivative of the effective potential with respect to
R
is positive for both
R\rightarrow 0
and
R\rightarrow\infty
.
In the case of
{\cal L}^{2}\leq 3
, since
R_{\pm}
are complex, there is no extremum, and hence, the test particle enters the black hole.
On the other hand, if
{\cal L}^{2}>3
holds,
V(R;{\cal E},{\cal L})
takes the maximum and the minimum at
R=R_{-}
and
R=R_{+}
, respectively.
In this case, the test particle enters the black hole if and only if
V(R_{-};{\cal E},{\cal L})<0
holds,
or equivalently,

{\cal E}^{2}-1>\lambda({\cal L}^{2}),

(17)

where

\displaystyle\lambda({\cal L}^{2})

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{R_{-}}+\frac{{\cal L}^{2}}{R_{-}^{2}}-\frac{{\cal L}^{2}}{R_{-}^{3}}=\frac{{\cal L}^{2}}{27}\left[2-\frac{9}{{\cal L}^{2}}+2\left(1-\frac{3}{{\cal L}^{2}}\right)^{3\over 2}\right].

(18)

We consider the situation in which the velocity of the test particle is much less than the speed of light at
r\gg r_{\rm g}
(
R\gg 1
)
and follows the Maxwell distribution with the temperature
T
;

f(\bm{v})=\left(\frac{m}{2\pi k_{\rm B}T}\right)^{3\over 2}\exp\left(-\frac{m}{2k_{\rm B}T}\bm{v}^{2}\right),

(19)

where
m
is the mass of the test particle, and the three-velocity
\bm{v}
is defined as the spatial components of the four-velocity
u^{a}
with respect to
the orthonormal frame of the static observer (see Appendix
A
for more details);

\displaystyle v_{(r)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{f(r)}}\frac{dr}{d\tau},

(20)

\displaystyle v_{(\theta)}

\displaystyle=r\frac{d\theta}{d\tau},

(21)

\displaystyle v_{(\varphi)}

\displaystyle=r\sin\theta\frac{d\varphi}{d\tau}.

(22)

We have

\displaystyle\bm{v}^{2}

\displaystyle=v_{(r)}^{2}+v_{(\theta)}^{2}+v_{(\varphi)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{f(r)}\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^{2}+r^{2}\left(\frac{d\theta}{d\tau}\right)^{2}+r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\left(\frac{d\varphi}{d\tau}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{f(r)}\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^{2}+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{2}}{f(r)}\left[{\cal E}^{2}-f(r)\right]

(23)

and so

{\cal E}^{2}-1=f(r)\dfrac{\bm{v}^{2}}{c^{2}}-\dfrac{r_{\rm g}}{r}.

(24)

Then, Eq. (
17
) leads to

\frac{v_{(r)}^{2}}{c^{2}}>\left(1-\frac{1}{R}\right)^{-1}\left[\lambda\left({\cal L}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{R}\right]-\frac{{\cal L}^{2}}{R^{2}}.

(25)

For
R\gg 1
, we have

\frac{v_{(r)}^{2}}{c^{2}}>\lambda\left({\cal L}^{2}\right)+{\cal O}\left(R^{-1}\right),

(26)

or equivalently,

{\cal L}^{2}<\lambda^{-1}\left(\frac{v_{(r)}^{2}}{c^{2}}\right)+{\cal O}\left(R^{-1}\right),

(27)

where
x=\lambda^{-1}(y)
is the inverse function of
y=\lambda(x)
.

We introduce
{\cal L}_{\rm crit}^{2}
as a positive root of the following equation,

\lambda\left({\cal L}_{\rm crit}^{2}\right)=0,

(28)

We have the root of this equation as

{\cal L}_{\rm crit}^{2}=4

(29)

In the case of
3\leq{\cal L}^{2}\leq{\cal L}_{\rm crit}^{2}
, since
\lambda({\cal L}^{2})\leq 0
holds, Eq. (
26
) is trivial.
Since the allowed domain for the motion of the charged test particle with
{\cal L}^{2}<3
should be
0<R<\infty
,
the test particle enters the black hole if the initial radial velocity is less than or equal to zero in the case of
0\leq{\cal L}^{2}\leq{\cal L}_{\rm crit}^{2}
.
Only in the case of
{\cal L}^{2}>{\cal L}_{\rm crit}^{2}
, Eq. (
26
) gives a non-trivial condition.

Here, we estimate the probability
P
for the particle to enter the black hole. Instead of
v_{(\theta)}
and
v_{(\varphi)}
,
we adopt the variables defined as

\displaystyle v_{(\theta)}

\displaystyle=v_{\Omega}\cos\Theta,

(30)

\displaystyle v_{(\varphi)}

\displaystyle=v_{\Omega}\sin\Theta,

(31)

where
0\leq v_{\Omega}<\infty
and
0\leq\Theta<2\pi
. Note that

\frac{v_{\Omega}^{2}}{c^{2}}=\frac{{\cal L}^{2}}{R^{2}}.

(32)

The infinitesimal volume element in the velocity space is given as

dv_{(r)}dv_{(\theta)}dv_{(\varphi)}=dv_{(r)}v_{\Omega}dv_{\Omega}d\Theta=\frac{c^{2}}{2R^{2}}dv_{(r)}d{\cal L}^{2}d\Theta,

(33)

where Eq. (
32
) has been used in the last equality.
The square of the dimensionless angular momentum
{\cal L}^{2}
should satisfy Eq. (
27
) and
v_{(r)}
should be
less than or equal to zero so that the test particle enters the black hole. Then, the probability for the test particle to enter the black hole is given as

\displaystyle P=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\Theta\int_{-\infty}^{0}dv_{(r)}\left(\frac{m}{2\pi k_{\rm B}T}\right)^{1\over 2}\exp\left(-\frac{m}{2k_{\rm B}T}v_{(r)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times\int_{0}^{\lambda^{-1}\left(v_{(r)}^{2}/c^{2}\right)}d{\cal L}^{2}\frac{mc^{2}}{4\pi k_{\rm B}TR^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{mc^{2}}{2k_{\rm B}TR^{2}}{\cal L}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}dv_{(r)}\left(\frac{m}{2\pi k_{\rm B}T}\right)^{1\over 2}\exp\left(-\frac{m}{2k_{\rm B}T}v_{(r)}^{2}\right)\left\{1-\exp\left[-\frac{mc^{2}}{2k_{\rm B}TR^{2}}\lambda^{-1}\left(\frac{v_{(r)}^{2}}{c^{2}}\right)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dv_{(r)}\left(\frac{m}{2\pi k_{\rm B}T}\right)^{1\over 2}\exp\left(-\frac{m}{2k_{\rm B}T}v_{(r)}^{2}\right)\left[\frac{mc^{2}}{2k_{\rm B}TR^{2}}\lambda^{-1}\left(\frac{v_{(r)}^{2}}{c^{2}}\right)+{\cal O}\left(R^{-4}\right)\right],

(34)

where, in the final equality,
|v_{(r)}/c|
has been assumed to be much less than unity, since
we assume
mc^{2}/k_{\rm B}T\gg 1
.

By solving Eq. (
18
) with respect to
{\cal L}^{2}
, we have

\lambda^{-1}(x)=\frac{1}{8x}\left(27x^{2}+18x-1+\sqrt{\left(27x^{2}+18x-1\right)^{2}+64x}\right).

(35)

Hence, we have

\lambda^{-1}\left(\frac{v_{(r)}^{2}}{c^{2}}\right)=4+\frac{8v_{(r)}^{2}}{c^{2}}+{\cal O}\left(\frac{v_{(r)}^{4}}{c^{4}}\right)

(36)

and

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\frac{2mc^{2}}{k_{\rm B}TR^{2}}\times\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[1+\frac{4k_{\rm B}T}{mc^{2}}x^{2}+{\cal O}\left(\left(\frac{k_{\rm B}T}{mc^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\right]e^{-x^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{mc^{2}}{k_{\rm B}TR^{2}}\left[1+{\cal O}\left(\frac{k_{\rm B}T}{mc^{2}}\right)\right].

(37)

Hereafter the test particle is assumed to be a proton or an electron.
Any quantity of a proton is denoted by the character with a subscript “p”, whereas
that of an electron is denoted by the character with a subscript “e”.
Thus the excess of the probability of the proton to enter the black hole is given as

\Delta P=P_{\rm p}-P_{\rm e}\simeq\frac{1}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{m_{\rm p}c^{2}}{k_{\rm B}T_{\rm p}}-\frac{m_{\rm e}c^{2}}{k_{\rm B}T_{\rm e}}\right).

(38)

If
m_{\rm p}/T_{\rm p}>m_{\rm e}/T_{\rm e}
holds,
\Delta P
is positive and the black hole will be positively electrified.
By contrast, if
m_{\rm p}/T_{\rm p}<m_{\rm e}/T_{\rm e}
holds,
\Delta P
is negative and the black hole will be negatively electrified.
If
m_{\rm p}/T_{\rm p}=m_{\rm e}/T_{\rm e}
holds, no electrification of the black hole will occur.
If the temperature of the protons and that of the electrons are identical to each other, the black hole will be positively electrified
because of
m_{\rm p}\gg m_{\rm e}
.

Let us consider the Coulomb force between protons and electrons, which is ignored in our consideration.
The mean separation between protons and electrons is denoted by
l
.
Then, the relative acceleration
a_{\rm C}
between a proton and a neighboring electron due to Coulomb force is estimated as

a_{\rm C}=\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}l^{2}}\frac{m_{\rm e}+m_{\rm p}}{m_{\rm e}m_{\rm p}}\simeq\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}m_{\rm e}l^{2}},

(39)

whereas, for the proton and the neighboring electron on a sphere of
r=L
, the relative acceleration
a_{\rm T}
due to the tidal force is given as

a_{\rm T}\simeq\frac{2GMl}{L^{3}},

(40)

where we have assumed
l\ll L
.
Our present consideration is justified only if
a_{\rm C}<a_{\rm T}
, or equivalently,

l>\left(\frac{\alpha\hbar r_{\rm g}^{2}}{m_{\rm e}c}\right)^{1\over 3}\frac{L}{r_{\rm g}}=7.3\times 10^{2}\left(\frac{L}{10r_{\rm g}}\right)\left(\frac{M}{4\times 10^{6}M_{\odot}}\right)^{2\over 3}{\rm m}

(41)

holds, where
\hbar
and
\alpha
are the Dirac constant and the fine structure constant
e^{2}/4\pi\varepsilon_{0}\hbar c\simeq 1/137
, respectively.

The spatial curvature of our Universe is almost equal to zero

]
, and thus, from the observed baryon to photon ratio,
the average density of baryonic matter is estimated at

\Omega_{\rm b}=0.0224h^{-2}
times the critical density
3H_{0}^{2}/8\pi G
, where
H_{0}=100h
(km/s/Mpc)

III
Maximal charge acquired by non-rotating black hole

In this section, we estimate the charge acquired by the black hole in the situation assumed in the previous section.
The selective accretion of protons or electrons to the black hole will cease by the electromagnetic repulsion after the black hole
acquires sufficient electric charge.

The non-rotating charged black hole is described by the Reissner-Nordström solution
whose infinitesimal world interval is also given by Eq. (
1
) but with

f(r)=1-\frac{r_{\rm g}}{r}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r^{2}},

(42)

where
Q
is the charge parameter which is related to the electric charge
q_{\rm BH}
of the black hole through

Q^{2}=\frac{Gq_{\rm BH}^{2}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}c^{4}}.

(43)

As will be shown later, the maximal charge acquired by the black hole is so small that

r_{\rm g}\gg\dfrac{Q^{2}}{r}
holds for
r>r_{\rm g}
. Hence, we need not use Eq. (
42
)
but Eq. (
2
) in the following analyses.
By contrast, the electric field produced by
q_{\rm BH}
can strongly affect the motion of a proton and an electron.
The four-vector potential
A_{a}
is given as

A_{a}=-\frac{q_{\rm BH}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}cr}\delta^{t}_{a},

(44)

where
\delta_{a}^{b}
is the Kronecker delta.

The world line of a charged test particle is not a geodesic in the situation we consider. The equation of motion is

mu^{a}\nabla_{a}u_{b}=qF_{bc}u^{c},

(45)

where
m
and
q
are the mass and the electric charge of the test particle, and,
with
\partial_{b}
as the ordinary partial derivative,
F_{bc}=\partial_{b}A_{c}-\partial_{c}A_{b}
is
the electromagnetic field strength tensor.
Since the time and the azimuthal coordinate bases are Killing vectors,
the time and azimuthal components of Eq. (
45
) are

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(u_{t}+\frac{q}{m}A_{t}\right)=0~~~~~{\rm and}~~~~~\frac{du_{\varphi}}{d\tau}=0.

(46)

Thus, as in the previous section, we have, with
E
and
L_{z}
as constants of integration, we have

-u_{t}-\frac{q}{m}A_{t}=E~~~{\rm and}~~~u_{\varphi}=L_{z}.

(47)

Substituting these results into the
\theta
-component of Eq. (
45
), we have

\frac{d}{d\tau}\left[\left(r^{2}\frac{d\theta}{d\tau}\right)^{2}+\frac{L_{z}^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}\right]=0.

(48)

An integration of this equation leads to

r^{2}\left(\frac{d\theta}{d\tau}\right)^{2}+\frac{L_{z}^{2}}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}=\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}},

(49)

where
L
is a constant of integration.
The constants of integration,
E
,
L_{z}
and
L
, are the specific energy devided by the speed of light
c
,

z
-component of the specific angular momentum and the total specific angular momentum, respectively.

As in the previous section, we adopt the dimensionless quantities defined as Eqs. (
9
)–(
12
).
We introduce one more dimensionless quantity related to
the electric charges of the black hole and the test particle, which is defined as

{\cal Q}:=\frac{qq_{\rm BH}}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}mc^{2}r_{\rm g}}.

(50)

Since we have already studied the case of
{\cal Q}=0
, we will consider the case of
{\cal Q}\neq 0
in this section.

Then, the normalization condition of the four-velocity
g_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}=-c^{2}
leads to the similar equation to Eq. (
13
) as

\left(\frac{dR}{d{\cal T}}\right)^{2}+U(R;{\cal E},{\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q})=0,

(51)

where

\displaystyle U(R;{\cal E},{\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q})

\displaystyle=-\left({\cal E}-\frac{{\cal Q}}{R}\right)^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{R}\right)\left(1+\frac{{\cal L}^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)

(52)

is the effective potential for the charged particle.
The allowed domain for the motion of the charged test particle is determined by an inequality
U(R;{\cal E},{\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q})=-(dR/d{\cal T})^{2}\leq 0
.

Here note that since
u_{t}=-f(r)d(ct)/d\tau
should be negative outside the black hole,
{\cal E}-{\cal Q}/R
should be positive in the allowed
domain for the motion of the test particle. This fact implies that
R>{\cal Q}/{\cal E}
must hold in the allowed domain.
In order that the infinity
R\rightarrow\infty
is included in the allowed domain, both
{\cal E}
and
{\cal E}^{2}-1

must be positive. Thus,
{\cal E}\geq 1
should hold in the situation of our interest.

We rewrite the effective potential
U
in the form,

U(R;{\cal E},{\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q})=\Bigl(G_{-}(R;{\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q})+{\cal E}\Bigr)\Bigl(G_{+}(R;{\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q})-{\cal E}\Bigr),

(53)

where

G_{\pm}(R;{\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q})=\sqrt{\left(1-\frac{1}{R}\right)\left(1+\frac{{\cal L}^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)}~\pm\frac{{\cal Q}}{R}.

(54)

Note that
G_{\pm}
is defined in the domain
R\geq 1
. Since, as mentioned,
{\cal E}-{\cal Q}/R
should be positive,

G_{-}+{\cal E}
is positive. Hence the necessary condition
U(R;{\cal E},{\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q})\leq 0

for the domain allowed for the motion of the charged test particle is equivalent to

G_{+}(R;{\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q})\leq{\cal E}.

(55)

By investigating numerically and analytically the behavior of
G_{+}
, we find the following facts.

1.

In the case of
{\cal Q}\leq\dfrac{1}{2}
,
G_{+}
has a maximum as a function of
R

if and only if
{\cal L}^{2}
is larger than a threshold
{\cal L}_{\rm th}^{2}
. On the other hand, if
{\cal L}^{2}
is less than or equal to
{\cal L}_{\rm th}^{2}
,

G_{+}
is a non-decreasing function of
R
.

2.

In the case of
{\cal Q}=\dfrac{1}{2}
, the threshold
{\cal L}_{\rm th}^{2}
is equal to
\dfrac{1}{4}
.

3.

In the case of
{\cal Q}>\dfrac{1}{2}
,
G_{+}
has a maximum as a function of
R
for any
{\cal L}^{2}
. The maximum value of
G_{+}
is positive.

If
G_{+}
has a maximum, the dimensionless radius
R
at which
G_{+}
takes
the maximum value is denoted by
R_{\rm max}({\cal L}^{2};{\cal Q})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G_{+}\bigl(R_{\rm max}\left({\cal L}^{2};{\cal Q}\right);{\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q}\bigr)}{\partial{\cal L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left.\left(\frac{\partial R_{\rm max}\left({\cal L}^{2};{\cal Q}\right)}{\partial{\cal L}^{2}}\frac{\partial G_{+}\left(R;{\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q}\right)}{\partial R}+\frac{\partial G_{+}\left(R;{\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q}\right)}{\partial{\cal L}^{2}}\right)\right|_{R=R_{\rm max}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2R_{\rm max}^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{R_{\rm max}(R_{\rm max}-1)}{R_{\rm max}^{2}+{\cal L}^{2}}}>0,

(56)

where we have used
\partial G_{+}/\partial R|_{R=R_{\rm max}}=0
in the second equality.
Thus, when there is a maximum of
G_{+}
as a function of
R
, the maximum value of
G_{+}
is an increasing function of
{\cal L}^{2}
.

We define a function
\Lambda({\cal L}^{2};{\cal Q})
as

\Lambda({\cal L}^{2};{\cal Q}):=G_{+}^{2}\Bigl(R_{\rm max}({\cal L}^{2};{\cal Q});{\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q}\Bigr)-1.

(57)

We can easily see from Eq. (
56

\Lambda({\cal L};{\cal Q})
is an increasing function of
{\cal L}^{2}

if and only if the maximum value of
G_{+}
is non-negative. In Appendix
B
, we show

\lim_{{\cal L}^{2}\rightarrow\infty}R_{\rm max}({\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q})=\frac{3}{2}

(58)

and

\Lambda({\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q})\longrightarrow\frac{4}{27}{\cal L}^{2}~~~{\rm for}~~~{\cal L}^{2}\gg 1.

(59)

Note that
\Lambda\left({\cal L}^{2};{\cal Q}\right)
is a corresponding quantity to
\lambda\left({\cal L}^{2}\right)
introduced in the case of the
electrically neutral test particle. If
G_{+}
has a positive maximum,
the necessary and sufficient condition for the charged test particle to enter the black hole is

\Lambda({\cal L}^{2};{\cal Q})<{\cal E}^{2}-1.

(60)

By using the three-velocity defined as Eqs. (
20
)–(
22

{\cal E}-\frac{Q}{R}=\sqrt{\left(1-\frac{1}{R}\right)\left(1+\frac{{\cal L}^{2}}{R^{2}}+\frac{v_{(r)}^{2}}{c^{2}}\right)},

(61)

where we have used the fact that
{\cal E}-Q/R
should be positive. Hence, Eq. (
60

\Lambda\left({\cal L}^{2};Q\right)<\left[\sqrt{\left(1-\frac{1}{R}\right)\left(1+\frac{{\cal L}^{2}}{R^{2}}+\frac{v_{(r)}^{2}}{c^{2}}\right)}+\frac{Q}{R}\right]^{2}-1.

(62)

Since we focus on the case of
R\gg 1

59

\frac{v_{(r)}^{2}}{c^{2}}>\Lambda\left({\cal L}^{2};Q\right)+{\cal O}\left(R^{-1}\right),

(63)

{\cal L}^{2}<\Lambda^{-1}\left(\frac{v_{(r)}^{2}}{c^{2}};{\cal Q}\right)+{\cal O}\left(R^{-1}\right),

(64)

where
x=\Lambda^{-1}(y;{\cal Q})
is the inverse function of

III.1
The case of
{\cal Q}\leq 1/2

We introduce a quantity
{\cal L}_{\rm crit}({\cal Q})

G_{+}\Bigl(R_{\rm max}({\cal L}_{\rm crit}^{2};{\cal Q});{\cal L}_{\rm crit}^{2},{\cal Q}\Bigr)-1=0,

(65)

or equivalently,

\sqrt{\left(1-\frac{1}{R_{\rm max}}\right)\left(1+\frac{{\cal L}^{2}}{R_{\rm max}^{2}}\right)}=1-\frac{{\cal Q}}{R_{\rm max}}.

(66)

Taking a square of both sides of this equation, we obtain a quadratic equation for
R_{\rm max}

(1-2{\cal Q})R_{\rm max}^{2}-\left({\cal L}_{\rm crit}^{2}-{\cal Q}^{2}\right)R_{\rm max}+{\cal L}_{\rm crit}^{2}=0.

(67)

Since

should be a degenerate root of this quadratic equation, the discriminant should vanish;

({\cal L}_{\rm crit}^{2}-{\cal Q}^{2})^{2}-4(1-2{\cal Q}){\cal L}_{\rm crit}^{2}=0.

(68)

The roots of this equation for
{\cal L}^{2}

{\cal L}_{\rm crit}^{2}={\cal L}_{\pm}^{2}:={\cal Q}^{2}-4{\cal Q}+2\pm 2\sqrt{(1-{\cal Q})^{2}(1-2{\cal Q})}.

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{-}^{2}-{\cal Q}^{2}

\displaystyle=2-4{\cal Q}-2\sqrt{(1-{\cal Q})^{2}(1-2{\cal Q})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2{\cal Q}^{2}(1-2{\cal Q})}{1-2{\cal Q}+\sqrt{(1-{\cal Q})^{2}(1-2{\cal Q})}}

\displaystyle\leq 0

(70)

{\cal L}_{+}^{2}-{\cal Q}^{2}>0

The degenerate root of Eq. (

\left({\cal L}_{+}^{2}-{\cal Q}^{2}\right)/2(1-2{\cal Q})

By Eq. (

\displaystyle\Lambda\left({\cal L}_{\rm crit}^{2};{\cal Q}\right)

\displaystyle=0

(73)

holds.

as long as

By contrast, Eq. (

for any

\Lambda(0;{\cal Q})=\left({\cal Q}-\frac{1}{4{\cal Q}}\right)^{2}>0.

IV
Proton and electron on ISCO

The ISCO is important to estimate the angular diameter of a black hole shadow and luminosity accompanied
by the accretion process of matter to a black hole. We study the ISCO of a proton and an electron around
a non-rotating charged black hole.

Note that
{\cal E}
cannot be larger than or equal to one for the stable circular orbit.
We rewrite the effective potential defined as Eq. (
52
) in the form

U(R;{\cal E},{\cal L}^{2},{\cal Q})=1-{\cal E}^{2}-\frac{1-2{\cal Q}{\cal E}}{R}+\frac{{\cal L}^{2}-{\cal Q}^{2}}{R^{2}}-\frac{{\cal L}^{2}}{R^{3}}.

(106)

The radial coordinate of the stable circular orbit is a local minimum and zero point of the effective potential;

U=0,~~~~\frac{\partial U}{\partial R}=0~~~~{\rm and}~~~~\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial R^{2}}>0.

(107)

The second equation of Eq. (
107
) is equivalent to a quadratic equation with respect to
R
as

\left(1-2{\cal Q}{\cal E}\right)R^{2}-2\left({\cal L}^{2}-{\cal Q}^{2}\right)R+3{\cal L}^{2}=0.

(108)

Since
\partial U/\partial R\rightarrow+\infty
for
R\rightarrow 0
, the stable circular orbit
exists only if there are two extrema in the domain of
R>0
; if there is only one extremum of
U
in the domain
R>0
,
except for the double root case,
it is necessarily a maximum which should correspond to the unstable circular orbit.
Hence the stable circular orbit exists only if Eq. (
108
) has two positive roots,
or in other words, only if

1-2{\cal Q}{\cal E}>0~~~~{\rm and}~~~~{\cal L}^{2}-{\cal Q}^{2}>0

(109)

hold.

The ISCO is a circular orbit whose radius is a double root of Eq. (
108
), i.e., a marginally stable circular orbit.
The dimensionless specific energy
{\cal E}
and the dimensionless specific angular momentum
{\cal L}
of the ISCO
should lead to the vanishing discriminant of Eq. (
108
);

\left({\cal L}^{2}-{\cal Q}^{2}\right)^{2}-3{\cal L}^{2}(1-2{\cal Q}{\cal E})=0,

(110)

or equivalently,

1-2{\cal Q}{\cal E}=\frac{\left({\cal L}^{2}-{\cal Q}^{2}\right)^{2}}{3{\cal L}^{2}}.

(111)

Then, solving Eq. (
108
), the ISCO radius is given by

\displaystyle R:=\frac{{\cal L}^{2}-{\cal Q}^{2}}{1-2{\cal Q}{\cal E}}=\frac{3{\cal L}^{2}}{{\cal L}^{2}-{\cal Q}^{2}}=3\left(1+\frac{{\cal Q}^{2}}{{\cal L}^{2}-{\cal Q}^{2}}\right)>3,

(112)

where we have used Eq. (
111
) in the second equality. Note that the positivity of the ISCO radius leads to
{\cal L}^{2}-{\cal Q}^{2}>0
and
so the final inequality is derived. This result implies that the ISCO radius of a charged test particle is necessarily larger than that of a neutral test
particle,
R=3
, or equivalently,
r=3r_{\rm g}

V
Summary

We studied the electrification of a non-rotating black hole by the accretion of protons and electrons with thermal
velocity distributions through the analysis of their motion by assuming them to be charged test particles
in the Schwarzschild spacetime and investigated the effects of the charge acquired by the black hole on the motion of
a proton and an electron through the investigation of their ISCO.

We showed that, assuming that the initial velocities of the protons and the electrons follow the Maxwell distribution,
the difference appears between the probability of a proton to enter a non-rotating black hole and that of an electron
due to the difference of mass between a proton and an electron and due to the temperature difference.
This result will lead to the electrification of a non-rotating black hole if the black hole is surrounded by plasma so diffuse that the Coulomb
force between protons and electrons is smaller than the tidal force between them due to the black hole.
We also estimated the maximal electric charge acquired by the black hole as Eq. (
86
).
We saw, by an order estimate, that the Coulomb force between protons and electrons should be taken into account in the case of
supermassive black holes like as Srg A*, whereas it might be negligible in the case of a stellar mass black hole.

Although, exactly speaking, a non-rotating charged black hole is described by the Reissner-Nordström solution, the charge parameter
of the Reissner-Nordström black hole electrified in the situation considered in this paper is so small that the spacetime geometry
is well described by the Schwarzschild solution. Thus, the motion of a neutral test particle is not affected by the electrification of the
non-rotating black hole. By contrast, the motion of a charged test particle can be largely affected.
We studied the ISCO of a proton and an electron in the spacetime with a non-rotating charged black hole
and showed that there can be large differences between the proton, the electron and a neutral particle if the black hole acquires electric charge
very close to the maximal value given by (
86
). This result implies that the efficiency of the energy extraction
due to the accretion of plasma to the charged black hole might be much less than that of neutral matter.
However, the Coulomb force between protons and electrons should be taken into account in the accretion process.
This subject is out of scope of this paper and will be discussed elsewhere in future.

Orthonormal frame associated with a static observer

In this paper, we adopt an orthonormal frame associated with a static observer, which is defined as

\displaystyle e_{(0)\mu}

\displaystyle=\left(-\sqrt{f(r)},0,0,0\right),

(127)

\displaystyle e_{(1)\mu}

\displaystyle=\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{f(r)}},0,0\right),

\displaystyle e_{(2)\mu}

\displaystyle=\left(0,0,r,0\right),

(129)

\displaystyle e_{(3)\mu}

\displaystyle=\left(0,0,0,r\sin\theta\right)

(130)

127

\left(v_{(r)},v_{(\theta)},v_{(\varphi)}\right):=\left(u_{(1)},u_{(2)},u_{(3)}\right),

where

.
Then we obtain Eqs. (
20
)–(
22

The momentum distribution function

Extrema of the function

\frac{\partial G_{+}(R;{\cal L},{\cal Q})}{\partial R}=0,

(134)

where introducing the following functions

\displaystyle D_{1}(R;{\cal L})

\displaystyle=R^{2}-2{\cal L}^{2}R+3{\cal L}^{2},

\displaystyle D_{2}(R;{\cal L},{\cal Q})

G_{+}

\frac{\partial G_{+}(R;{\cal L},{\cal Q})}{\partial R}=\frac{D_{1}(R;{\cal L})+D_{2}(R;{\cal L},{\cal Q})}{2R^{2}\sqrt{R(R-1)\left(R^{2}+{\cal L}^{2}\right)}}.

(139)

R_{\rm max}=\frac{3}{2}+{\cal O}\left(|{\cal L}|^{-1}\right).

(140)

Appendix C
End of the electrification

We consider the accretion of plasma composed of protons and electrons to a non-rotating
black hole in the situation studied in Sec.
III.1
.
In the case that
N
plasma particles fall into the black hole, the number of falling protons is equal to
NP_{\rm p}/(P_{\rm p}+P_{\rm e})
, whereas that
of falling electrons is equal to
NP_{\rm e}/(P_{\rm p}+P_{\rm e})
. The increment of the electric charge of the black hole,
\Delta q_{\rm BH}
, is given as

\Delta q_{\rm BH}=\frac{Ne}{P_{\rm p}+P_{\rm e}}\left(P_{\rm p}-P_{\rm e}\right),

(142)

whereas the increment of the mass of the black hole,
\Delta M_{\rm BH}
, is given as

\Delta M_{\rm BH}=\frac{Nm_{\rm p}}{P_{\rm p}+P_{\rm e}}\left(P_{\rm p}+\epsilon P_{\rm e}\right).

(143)

Note that
\Delta M_{\rm BH}
should be non-negative.
Hence, the increment of the charge parameter
{\cal Q}_{\rm p}
of a proton, which is denoted by
\Delta{\cal Q}_{\rm p}
, is given as

\displaystyle\Delta{\cal Q}_{\rm p}

\displaystyle={\cal Q}_{\rm p}\left[\left(1+\frac{\Delta q_{\rm BH}}{q_{\rm BH}}\right)\left(1+\frac{\Delta M_{\rm BH}}{M_{\rm BH}}\right)^{-1}-1\right]

\displaystyle={\cal Q}_{\rm p}\left(\frac{\Delta q_{\rm BH}}{q_{\rm BH}}-\frac{\Delta M_{\rm BH}}{M_{\rm BH}}\right)\left(1+\frac{\Delta M_{\rm BH}}{M_{\rm BH}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{Ne{\cal Q}_{\rm p}}{q_{\rm BH}}\frac{P_{\rm p}+\epsilon P_{\rm e}}{P_{\rm p}+P_{\rm e}}\left[\frac{P_{\rm p}-P_{\rm e}}{P_{\rm p}+\epsilon P_{\rm e}}-\frac{q_{\rm BH}}{M_{\rm BH}}\left(\frac{e}{m_{\rm p}}\right)^{-1}\right]\left(1+\frac{\Delta M_{\rm BH}}{M_{\rm BH}}\right)^{-1},

(144)

where
M_{\rm BH}
is the mass of the black hole.

The end of the electrification of the black hole corresponds to

\Delta{\cal Q}_{\rm p}=0
which leads to

\frac{P_{\rm p}-P_{\rm e}}{P_{\rm p}+\epsilon P_{\rm e}}=\frac{q_{\rm BH}}{M_{\rm BH}}\left(\frac{e}{m_{\rm p}}\right)^{-1}=\frac{2Q_{\rm BH}}{r_{\rm g}}\left(\frac{m_{\rm pl}}{m_{\rm p}}\sqrt{\alpha}\right)^{-1},

(145)

where
Q_{\rm BH}
is the charge parameter of the Reissner-Nordström black hole with the electric charge
q_{\rm BH}
, and

m_{\rm pl}
is the Planck mass (
\simeq 1.3\times 10^{19}m_{\rm p}
).
Since
\left|Q_{\rm BH}\right|/r_{\rm g}<1
must hold in the case of our interest, the following inequality

\frac{\left|P_{\rm p}-P_{\rm e}\right|}{P_{\rm p}}\ll 1

(146)

should hold, if
\Delta{\cal Q}_{\rm p}=0
holds. Conversely, if
P_{\rm p}-P_{\rm e}=0
holds, we can see from Eq. (
144
) that

\left|\Delta{\cal Q}_{\rm p}/{\cal Q}_{\rm p}\right|\ll 1
holds, as long as
Ne/q_{\rm BH}\ll m_{\rm pl}/m_{\rm p}
is satisfied. Hence, Eq. (
82
) is a very good approximation
of the necessary and sufficient condition for the end of the electrification of the black hole.

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to prove Kolmogorov-Riesz type theorems via Bessel and Laguerre translations, and Pego-type theorems by the corresponding transformations.

1.
Introduction

The populous family of compactness theorems is established by Arzelá and Ascoli. Our starting point, the Kolmogorov-Riesz theorem characterizes precompact (totally bounded) sets of
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, see e.g.
[
1
]
. Besides, such a characterization is interesting in itself, it has several applications to differential and integral equations. Compactness criteria were studied in particular non-standard function spaces, e.g. in Sobolev spaces
[
17
]
, variable Lebesgue spaces
[
13
]
, or weighted variable exponent amalgam and Sobolev spaces
[
5
]
, and also in more general circumstances, see e.g.
[
10
]
,
[
25
]
and
[
14
]
. Sudakov-type improvements of the classical theorem are derived in
[
18
]
(see
[
26
]
as well).

The useful criterion of compactness given by Pego via Fourier transformation (see
[
23
]
) has also great influence, see e.g.
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
. In the inspirating paper
[
19
]
, compactness criterion is given by Laplace transformation.

Below we offer a new aspect of characterization of precompact sets in certain Banach spaces. Instead of deriving similar theorems by related, for instance Mellin or cosine transformations, we investigate and extend the notion of equicontinuity. Motivated by the effect of translation,
\tau_{y}f(x)=f(x-y)
, on exponential functions, different translation operators were introduced by orthogonal systems
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n}
as
T_{y}\varphi_{n}(x)=\varphi_{n}(x)\varphi_{n}(y)
. Hereinafter we deal with Laguerre and Bessel translations.

In the next section a Riesz-Kolmogorov type theorem is derived by Laguerre translation to weighted
L^{p}
spaces on the half-line. The corresponding transformation, the discrete Laguerre-Fourier transformation implies a remarkable simplification, since the structure of the corresponding
l^{p^{\prime}}
spaces are simpler than the original
L^{p}
ones. In this section we also introduce and study Laguerre translation on sequences.

In the third section the method is presented by Bessel translation. The corresponding transformation is the Hankel (Bessel-Fourier) transformation which establishes a Pego-type theorem.

2.
Laguerre translation method

Laguerre translation is developed by product formulae, see e.g.
[
28
]
and by investigation of the related Cauchy problem, see e.g.
[
7
]
. We mention that it is a natural idea to handle Bessel and Laguerre translations in parallel, since the translated functions in both cases are the solutions to very similar Cauchy problems,
u_{xx}-u_{tt}+\frac{2\alpha+1}{x}u_{x}-\frac{2\alpha+1}{t}u_{t}-ru=0
, where
r(x,t)=0
in Bessel case and
r(x,t)=x^{2}-t^{2}
in Laguerre case. The derived convolution structure is examined e.g. in
[
15
]
. The norm of the translation operator ensures a maximum principle to the corresponding hyperbolic problem, and it implies Nikol’skii-type norm-estimations on the half-line, see
[
2
]
. Convolution is applicable to study of best approximation in certain spaces, see
[
16
]
. In this section compactness is investigated by Laguerre translation. The main result of the section is the characterization of precompact sets in
L^{2}_{\alpha}
.

2.1.
Laguerre translation and precompact sets in
L^{p}_{\alpha}

We introduce weighted
L^{p}
-spaces on the half-line similar to the ones given in the previous section.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
\alpha>-1
.

f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}\in L^{p}_{\alpha}
if

\|f\|_{p,\alpha}:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|f(x)e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\right|^{p}x^{\alpha}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\tilde{f}|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

\|f\|_{\infty,\alpha}=\|\tilde{f}\|_{\infty}

independently of
\alpha
.

The translation operator acting on the space given above can be defined as follows, see e.g.
[
15
]
.

Let
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}

(1)

T_{t}^{\alpha}(f,x)

=c_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\pi}f(x+t+2\sqrt{xt}\cos\vartheta)e^{-\sqrt{xt}\cos\vartheta}j_{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}(\sqrt{xt}\sin\vartheta)\sin^{2\alpha}\vartheta d\vartheta,

where
c_{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}
and
j_{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}
is the entire Bessel function, cf. (
30
).

By symmetry of the definition in
t,x\geq 0
,

T_{t}^{\alpha}(f,x)=T_{x}^{\alpha}(f,t),

and again by definition

T_{t}^{\alpha}(f,0)=f(t),\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0ptT_{0}^{\alpha}(f,x)=f(x).

Denoting by
L_{n}^{\alpha}(x)
the
n^{th}
Laguerre polynomial orthogonal on
(0,\infty)
with respect to the Laguerre weight, that is

\int_{0}^{\infty}L_{n}^{\alpha}(x)L_{k}^{\alpha}(x)e^{-x}x^{\alpha}dx=\Gamma(\alpha+1)\binom{n+\alpha}{n}\delta_{n,k},

and taking into consideration that

L_{n}^{\alpha}(0)=\binom{n+\alpha}{n}=w(\alpha,n)=:w(n),

we denote by

R_{n}^{\alpha}(x)=\frac{L_{n}^{\alpha}(x)}{\binom{n+\alpha}{n}}.

Thus
R_{n}^{\alpha}(0)=1
and

(2)

\left\|\sqrt{\frac{w(n)}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}}R_{n}^{\alpha}\right\|_{2,\alpha}=1,\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\|\tilde{R}_{n}^{\alpha}\|_{\infty}=\tilde{R}_{n}^{\alpha}(0)=1.

With this notation we have that

(3)

T_{t}^{\alpha}(R_{n}^{\alpha},x)=R_{n}^{\alpha}(x)R_{n}^{\alpha}(t),

see e.g.
[
15
]
.

According to
[
15
, Theorem 1]
for all
\alpha\geq 0
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, considering the translated function as a function of the variable
x
,

(4)

\|e^{-\frac{t}{2}}T_{t}^{\alpha}(f,x)\|_{p,\alpha}\leq\|f\|_{p,\alpha}.

The corresponding convolution is

(5)

(f*g)(t):=\int_{0}^{\infty}T_{t}^{\alpha}(f,x)g(x)e^{-x}x^{\alpha}dx.

Again by
[
15
, Theorem 1]
, for all
\alpha\geq 0
and
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
with
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-1
; if
f\in L^{p}_{\alpha}
and
g\in L^{q}_{\alpha}
, then

(6)

\|f*g\|_{r,\alpha}\leq\|f\|_{p,\alpha}\|g\|_{q,\alpha}.

The first theorem of this section is a Kolmogorov -Riesz type theorem, where the standard equicontinuity property is replaced by the one based on Laguerre translation.

Theorem 1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\alpha\geq 0
. A bounded set
K\subset L^{p}_{\alpha}
is precompact if and only if the properties below are fulfilled.

{\bf P_{a}}.
For all
\varepsilon>0
there is an
R>0
such that for all
f\in K

(7)

\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}|\tilde{f}|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\varepsilon.

{\bf P_{b}.}
For all
\varepsilon
and
M_{0}
positive numbers there is a
\delta>0
such that for all
0\leq t\leq M_{0}
,
0\leq h\leq\delta
and
f\in K

(8)

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\left(T_{t+h}^{\alpha}f(x)-T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)\right)e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\right|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\varepsilon.

Proof.

First we assume that
K
is precompact. Then for an
\varepsilon>0
there is a finite
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
-net,
H_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{j}\}
in
K

(j=j(\varepsilon))
. Since
C_{0}
- the continuous functions with compact support - are dense in
L_{p,\alpha}
, there is an
S_{\varepsilon}\subset C_{0}
, such that
S_{\varepsilon}=\{\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{j}\}
and
\|u_{l}-\Phi_{l}\|_{p,(\alpha)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for all
u_{l}\in H_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}
. That is there is an
R=R_{\varepsilon}>0
such that the closed ball
\overline{B(0,R)}
contains the support of each
\Phi\in S_{\varepsilon}
wich ensures
{\bf P_{a}}
.
To prove
{\bf P_{b}}
take an
f\in L^{p}_{\alpha}
,
t\in[0,M_{0}]
with some
M_{0}
, and let
\Phi
be the element of
S_{\varepsilon}
closest to
f
with
\mathrm{supp}\Phi\in(0,R)
. In view of (
1
)

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\left(T_{t+h}^{\alpha}\Phi(x)-T_{t}^{\alpha}\Phi(x)\right)e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\right|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|c_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left(\Phi(x+t+h+2\sqrt{x(t+h)}\cos\vartheta)e^{-\sqrt{x(t+h)}\cos\vartheta}j_{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}(\sqrt{x(t+h)}\sin\vartheta)\right.\right.\right.

\left.\left.\left.-\Phi(x+t+2\sqrt{xt}\cos\vartheta)e^{-\sqrt{xt}\cos\vartheta}j_{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}(\sqrt{xt}\sin\vartheta)\right)\sin^{2\alpha}\vartheta d\vartheta e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\right|^{p}x^{\alpha}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=(*).

For sake of simplicity let us denote by

\Psi(x,t,\vartheta):=\Phi(x+t+2\sqrt{xt}\cos\vartheta)e^{-\sqrt{xt}\cos\vartheta}j_{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}(\sqrt{xt}\sin\vartheta).

Recalling that
\mathrm{supp}\Phi\subset(0,R)
and
t\in[0,M_{0}]
, if
R<|\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{t}|
then
\Psi(x,t,\vartheta)=0
, that is if
\sqrt{x}>\sqrt{M_{0}}+R
then
\Psi(x,t,\vartheta)=0
. Considering that difference of the arguments of the three functions above are at most
2\sqrt{xh}\leq c(R,M_{0})\sqrt{h}
and
\Psi(x,t,\vartheta)
is compactly supported and continuous, there is a
\delta>0
such that if
0\leq h\leq\delta

\left|\Psi(x,t+h,\vartheta)-\Psi(x,t,\vartheta)\right|\leq\varepsilon

and so

(*)\leq\varepsilon\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-p\frac{x}{2}}x^{\alpha}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then, because the set of
\Phi
-s in question is finite we can choose
R
and
\delta
uniformly. Since the norm of translation is bounded on
[0,M_{0}]
, cf. (
4
) (and it is a linear operator), we can finish this part with triangle inequality.

Vice versa assuming
{\bf P_{a}}
and
{\bf P_{b}}
let

V_{a}f(x):=\frac{1}{A}\int_{0}^{a}T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)e^{-\frac{t}{2}}t^{\alpha}dt

if
0\leq x\leq R
with some finite
R
, and we define
V_{a}f(x)=0
if
x>R
.
where
A=\int_{0}^{a}e^{-\frac{t}{2}}t^{\alpha}dt
. Then, applying Hölder’s inequality and the symmetry of translation

(9)

|V_{a}f(x+u)-V_{a}f(x)|\leq\frac{1}{A}\int_{0}^{a}|T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x+u)-T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)|e^{-\frac{t}{2}}t^{\alpha}dt

\leq\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{p}}}e^{\frac{a}{2p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|T_{x+u}^{\alpha}f(t)-T_{x}^{\alpha}f(t)|^{p}e^{-\frac{pt}{2}}t^{\alpha}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and similarly

(10)

|V_{a}f(x)|\leq\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{p}}}e^{\frac{a}{2p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|T_{x}^{\alpha}f(t)|^{p}e^{-\frac{pt}{2}}t^{\alpha}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Let

F_{a,R}:=\left\{V_{a}f(x):f\in K,\hskip 4.0ptx\leq R\right\}.

By (
10
) and (
4
)

|e^{-\frac{x}{2}}V_{a}f(x)|\leq\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{p}}}e^{\frac{a}{2p^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{p,\alpha}.

Thus with fixed
a
and
R
,
F_{a,R}
is bounded (by
M\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{p}}}e^{\frac{a}{2p^{\prime}}+\frac{R}{2}}
if
K
is bounded by
M
) and (choosing
M_{0}=R
) by (
9
) and assumption
{\bf P_{b}}
it is equicontinuous. Thus for an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
there is an
\varepsilon
-net,
V_{a}f_{1},\dots,V_{a}f_{n}
, in
F_{a,R}
such that
f_{i}\in K
,
i=1,\dots,n
.

Let
f\in K
be arbitrary, and for an
\varepsilon>0
choose
R
according to property
{\bf P_{a}}
. Then again by Hölder and Fubini theorems

\|V_{a}f-f\|_{p,\alpha}

\leq\left(\int_{0}^{R}\left|e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\int_{0}^{a}\frac{1}{A}\left(T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)-f(x)\right)e^{-\frac{t}{2}}t^{\alpha}dt\right|^{p}x^{\alpha}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}\left|e^{-\frac{x}{2}}f(x)\right|^{p}x^{\alpha}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\leq\varepsilon+e^{\frac{a}{2p^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{1}{A}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{px}{2}}\int_{0}^{a}\left|T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)-f(x)\right|^{p}e^{-\frac{t}{2}}t^{\alpha}dtx^{\alpha}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\leq\varepsilon+e^{\frac{a}{2p^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{1}{A}\int_{0}^{a}e^{-\frac{t}{2}}t^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{px}{2}}\left|T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)-f(x)\right|^{p}x^{\alpha}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\leq\varepsilon+e^{\frac{a}{2p^{\prime}}}\sup_{0\leq t\leq a}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{px}{2}}\left|T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)-f(x)\right|^{p}x^{\alpha}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Thus choosing
a
according to property
{\bf P_{b}}
small enough (assume that
a<1
),
\|V_{a}f-f\|_{p,\alpha}<2\varepsilon
.

With these chosen
a
and
R
construct
F_{a,R}
and for the previous
\varepsilon
and
f
, from the
\varepsilon
-net in question choose
V_{a}f_{i}
such that
|V_{a}f(x)-V_{a}f_{i}(x)|<\varepsilon
on
[0,R]
. Then we have

\|V_{a}f-V_{a}f_{i}\|_{p,\alpha}=\left(\int_{0}^{R}\left|e^{-\frac{x}{2}}(V_{a}f(x)-V_{a}f_{i}(x))\right|^{p}x^{\alpha}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\leq\varepsilon\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{px}{2}}x^{\alpha}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\varepsilon c(\alpha,p).

That is by the triangle inequality
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a
(4+c(\alpha,p))\varepsilon
-net in
K
.

2.2.
Laguerre translation on sequences

Laguerre translation on sequences, to the best of my knowledge, have not appeared explicitly just as convolution of sequences, see e.g.
[
4
]
. The corresponding algebras are investigated in
[
20
]
. Below we derive the translation from convolution and investigate its properties.

Let
\alpha>-1
. Recalling the notation above we introduce the discrete weights

w(k)=w(\alpha,k)=\binom{k+\alpha}{k},

and the space of real sequences,
a=\{a(k)\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
. For
1\leq p<\infty

a\in l^{p}_{\alpha}
if

\|a\|_{p,\alpha}:=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a(k)|^{p}w(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

\|a\|_{\infty,\alpha}=\|a\|_{\infty}

independently of
\alpha
.

If
a\in l^{p}_{\alpha}
and
b\in l^{p^{\prime}}_{\alpha}
we denote by

\langle a,b\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a(k)b(k)w(k).

Remark.

Certainly, in discrete case the criterion of precompactness is simpler, cf.
[
17
, Theorem 4]
. It is as follows.

For any
1\leq p<\infty
and
\alpha\geq 0
a set
K\subset l^{p}_{\alpha}
is precompact if and only if it is pointwise bounded (i.e. for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is an
M(n)
such that for all
a\in K

a(n)\leq M(n)
), and the next property fulfils.

{\bf P_{as}.}
For all
\varepsilon>0
there is an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
a\in K

(11)

\left(\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}|a(k)|^{p}w(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\varepsilon.

Indeed, suppose, that
K
is precompact. Then it is obviously bounded in
l^{p}_{\alpha}
. Since
w(k)\geq 1
, it is pointwise bounded as well. For
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
it has a finite
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
-net,
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
, say. Since the finite sequences are dense in
l^{p}_{\alpha}
, there are
s_{i}
finite sequences, such that
\|p_{i}-s_{i}\|_{p,\alpha}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
,
i=1,\dots,n
. Thus the maximal length of
s_{i}
-s is an appropriate choice of
N
.

Assume now, that
K
is pointwise bounded and fulfils
{\bf P_{as}}
. Choose an
N
for an arbitrary
\varepsilon
ensured by
{\bf P_{as}}
. Let
S_{N}:=\{a^{N}:=a(0),\dots,a(N):a\in K\}
be the set of the
N+1
-long initial parts of the sequences in
K
. Then the distance of
K
and
S_{N}
is at most
\varepsilon
. Because
S_{N}
is finite dimensional it is bounded in
l^{p}_{\alpha}
(
S_{N}
is bounded by
cM_{N}N^{\alpha+1}
, where
M_{N}:=\max_{n=1}^{N}M(n)
) and (again by being finite dimensional)
S_{N}
contains a finite
\varepsilon
-net, and the corresponding sequences form a
2\varepsilon
-net in
K
.

To define translation on the spaces of sequences, our starting point is the convolution defined in
[
4
]
.

Let
\alpha>\alpha_{0}=\frac{-5+\sqrt{17}}{2}
. Then by
[
4
, Theorem 1]

(12)

\gamma(n,m,k):=\gamma(n,m,k,\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}R_{n}^{\alpha}(x)R_{m}^{\alpha}(x)R_{k}^{\alpha}(x)e^{-2x}x^{\alpha}dx>0,

for all
k,m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. In view of
[
4
, (4.2)]

(13)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma(n,m,k)w(k)=1

for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
a\in l^{p}_{\alpha}
,
b\in l^{q}_{\alpha}
, where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\geq 1
. In
[
4
, (4.5)]
the next convolution is defined.

(14)

(a*b)(k):=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(m)b(n)\gamma(n,m,k)w(n)w(m).

Similarly to the
L^{p}
-cases if
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
and
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-1
,

(15)

\|a*b\|_{r,\alpha}\leq\|a\|_{p,\alpha}\|b\|_{q,\alpha},

see
[
4
, (4.6)]
.

Thus, fixing
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
, we can define the corresponding translation as

(16)

T_{k}(a)(n):=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a(m)w(m)\gamma(n,m,k).

By symmetry we immediately have that

(17)

T_{k}(a)(n)=T_{n}(a)(k).

So (
14
) can be written as

(18)

(a*b)(k)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b(n)T_{k}(a)(n)w(n)=\langle b,T_{k}(a)\rangle=\langle a,T_{k}(b)\rangle.

As on functions, Laguerre translation on sequences is also a bounded operator.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
Then for all
k

\|T_{k}(a)\|_{p,\alpha}\leq\|a\|_{p,\alpha}.

Proof.

Let
a\in l^{\infty}
. In view of (
16
), (
12
) and (
13
)

|T_{k}(a)(n)|=|\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a(m)w(m)\gamma(n,m,k)|\leq\|a\|_{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\gamma(n,m,k)w(m)=\|a\|_{\infty}.

If
a\in l^{1}_{\alpha}

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|T_{k}(a)(n)|w(m)=\sup_{b,\|b\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b(n)T_{k}(a)(n)w(n)

=\sup_{b,\|b\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(m)b(n)\gamma(n,m,k)w(n)w(m)=\sup_{b,\|b\|_{\infty}\leq 1}(a*b)(k).

According to (
15
)

\|a*b\|_{\infty}\leq\|a\|_{1,\alpha},

which implies the statement in
l^{1}_{\alpha}
. Finally interpolation ensures the result.

To prepare the next subsection, we mention the standard connection between the corresponding spaces of functions and sequences.

For a function
f
in some
L^{p}_{\alpha}
let us denote the corresponding sequence by
\hat{f}=a_{f}
, where

(19)

\hat{f}(n):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{f}\tilde{R}_{n}^{\alpha}d\mu_{\alpha},

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
a
be a sequence. The corresponding function can be defined as
\check{a}=f_{a}(x)
, where

\tilde{f}_{a}\sim\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a(m)w(m)\tilde{R}_{m}^{\alpha}

if the series is convergent in some sense.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
. Then, as usual, if
f\in L^{p}_{\alpha}
then
\hat{f}\in l^{p^{\prime}}_{\alpha}
, and if
a\in l^{p}_{\alpha}
then
\check{a}\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{\alpha}
and the operators map
L^{p}_{\alpha}
to
l^{p^{\prime}}_{\alpha}
and
l^{p}_{\alpha}
to
L^{p^{\prime}}_{\alpha}
are bounded.

Indeed, by interpolation it is a consequence of Parseval’s formula and the next inequalities:

|\hat{f}(k)|=\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{f}\tilde{R}^{\alpha}_{k}d\mu_{\alpha}\right|\leq\|f\|_{1,\alpha},

and

\|\tilde{f}_{a}\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|a(m)|w(m)\|\tilde{R}_{m}^{\alpha}\|_{\infty}=\|a\|_{1,\alpha},

where (
2
) is considered.

Let
a
and
b
are in
l^{1}_{\alpha}
, say, then the series
\tilde{f}_{a}
and
\tilde{g}_{b}
are uniformly convergent. Thus by (
14
)

(20)

\tilde{f}_{a}(x)\tilde{g}_{b}(x)e^{-\frac{x}{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(a*b)(k)w(k)\tilde{R}_{k}^{\alpha},

that is

(21)

\check{a}\check{b}\sim a*b,

see
[
4
]
.

Similarly (
14
) ensures that if
\hat{f}=a_{f}
then

(22)

\widehat{\tilde{f}\tilde{R}_{n}^{\alpha}}(k)=T_{n}(a_{f})(k).

Moreover (
3
) implies that

(23)

\widehat{f*g}(n)=\hat{f}(n)\hat{g}(n),\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\widehat{T_{t}^{\alpha}(f)}(n)=\hat{f}(n)R_{n}^{\alpha}(t),

provided that
\alpha\geq 0
,
f\in L^{p}_{\alpha}
and
g\in L^{q}_{\alpha}
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\geq 1
.

2.3.
Pego-type theorem with Laguerre transformation

At first we introduce the notion of equicontinuity in mean with respect to sequences.

{\bf P_{bs}}.
A set
K\subset l^{p}_{\alpha}
is equicontinuous in mean if for all
\varepsilon>0
there is an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
j>N
and
a\in K

(24)

\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|T_{j}(a)(k)-a(k)|^{p}w(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\varepsilon.

Subsequently we need the the next extra property with respect to functions.

{\bf P_{a0}}.
A set
K\subset L^{p}_{\alpha}
is equivanishing at zero if for all
\varepsilon>0
there is a
\delta>0
such that for all
f\in K

(25)

\left(\int_{0}^{\delta}|\tilde{f}|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\varepsilon.

After this preparation we are in position to state a Pego-type theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
and
\alpha\geq 0
.

(\mathrm{a})
If
K\subset l^{p}_{\alpha}
is bounded and fulfils
{\bf P_{as}}
, then
\check{K}\subset L^{p^{\prime}}_{\alpha}
fulfils
{\bf P_{b}}
.

(\mathrm{b})
If
K\subset L^{p}_{\alpha}
fulfils
{\bf P_{b}}
, then
\hat{K}\subset l^{p^{\prime}}_{\alpha}
fulfils
{\bf P_{as}}
.

(\mathrm{c})
If
K\subset l^{p}_{\alpha}
fulfils
{\bf P_{bs}}
, then
\check{K}\subset L^{p^{\prime}}_{\alpha}
fulfils
{\bf P_{a}}
.

(\mathrm{d})
If in addition
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
,
K\subset L^{p}_{\alpha}
is bounded and fulfils
{\bf P_{a}}
and
{\bf P_{a0}}
, then
\hat{K}\subset l^{p^{\prime}}_{\alpha}
fulfils
{\bf P_{bs}}
.

Proof.

(\mathrm{a})
: Let
f=f_{a}\in\check{K}\subset L^{p^{\prime}}_{\alpha}
. Then by (
23
) and (
2
)

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\left(T_{t+h}^{\alpha}f(x)-T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)\right|e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\right)^{p^{\prime}}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\leq c\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|a(k)\left(R_{k}^{\alpha}(t+h)-R_{k}^{\alpha}(t)\right)\right|^{p}w(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\leq c\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N}\left|a(k)\left(R_{k}^{\alpha}(t+h)-R_{k}^{\alpha}(t)\right)\right|^{p}w(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+2ce^{\frac{t}{2}}\left(\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}|a(k)|^{p}w(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

=S_{1}+S_{2}.

Since
\left\|\left(R_{k}^{\alpha}\right)^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\leq ck\left\|R_{k-1}^{(\alpha+1)}\right\|_{\infty}
, see
[
27
, (5.1.14)]
,

S_{1}\leq cNMe^{\frac{t}{2}}h,

where
K
is bounded by
M
. Thus, recalling that
t\in[0,M_{0}]
by
{\bf P_{as}}
if
N
is large enough,

S_{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, and we can choose
\delta
so small such that
S_{1}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2},
if
0\leq h\leq\delta
.

(\mathrm{b})
: Let
f\in K
. Take a function
g\in C_{0}
such that

g\geq 0
,
\mathrm{supp}g\subset[0,\delta]
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}g(x)e^{-x}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=1
. As
\hat{g}(k)\to 0
, we can choose
N
so large that
|\hat{g}(k)|<\frac{1}{2}
if
k\geq N
. Thus applying (
23
), (
5
), Minkowski inequality and the symmetry of translation we have

\left(\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}|\hat{f}(k)|^{p^{\prime}}w(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq 2\left(\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}|\hat{f}(k)(1-\hat{g}(k))|^{p^{\prime}}w(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\leq c\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|(f(t)-f*g(t))e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\right|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

=c\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\left(f(t)-\int_{0}^{\infty}T_{t}^{\alpha}f(x)g(x)e^{-x}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\right|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\leq c\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(|f(t)-T_{x}^{\alpha}f(t)|e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\right)^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}g(x)e^{-x}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\leq c\sup_{0\leq x\leq\delta}\|f-T_{x}^{\alpha}f\|_{p,\alpha}.

(\mathrm{c})
: Let
N
be the appropriate index for
\varepsilon
in
{\bf P_{bs}}
, and let us define a finite sequence
b=(b(N),\dots,b(N+n))
such that
b_{k}\geq 0
,
k=N,\dots,N+n
and
\sum_{N}^{N+n}b(k)w(k)=1
. Let us denote by
\check{b}=g
and for an
a\in K

\check{a}=f
. For
\varepsilon>0
we can take an apropriate initial part of
a
,
a_{L}:=(a(0),a(1),\dots,a(L))
, and
\check{a}_{L}=p_{L}
such that
\|f-p_{L}\|_{p^{\prime},\alpha}\leq\varepsilon
.

Since
\lim_{x\to\infty}g(x)e^{-x}=0
, there is an
R
such that
|g(x)e^{-x}|\leq\frac{1}{2}
if
x>R
. Considering that

on
x\geq 0

21
), as above

\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}|\tilde{f}(x)|^{p^{\prime}}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq\varepsilon+\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}|\tilde{p_{L}}(x)|^{p^{\prime}}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\leq\varepsilon+2\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}|\tilde{p_{L}}(x)(1-g(x)e^{-x})|^{p^{\prime}}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

=\varepsilon+\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}|\tilde{p_{L}}(x)-\tilde{p_{L}}(x)g(x)e^{-x}|^{p^{\prime}}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq\varepsilon+c\|a_{L}-a_{L}*b\|_{p,\alpha}

=\varepsilon+c\left(\sum_{k=0}^{L}\left|a(k)-\sum_{j=N}^{N+n}b(j)T_{k}(a)(j)w(j)\right|^{p}w(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\leq\varepsilon+c\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{j=N}^{N+n}\left[a(k)-T_{j}(a)(k)\right]b(j)w(j)\right|^{p}w(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\varepsilon+\leq c\sup_{j\geq N}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a(k)-T_{j}(a)(k)|^{p}w(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq(1+c)\varepsilon.

(\mathrm{d})
: According to (
22
) and (
2
)

\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a(k)-T_{j}(a)(k)|^{p^{\prime}}w(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|\hat{f}(k)-\widehat{\tilde{R}_{j}^{\alpha}\tilde{f}}(k)\right|^{p^{\prime}}w(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\leq c\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|f(x)\left(1-R_{j}^{\alpha}(x)e^{-x}\right)e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\right|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}(x))\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 2c\left(\int_{0}^{\delta}\left|f(x)e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\right|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}(x))\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

+c\left(\int_{\delta}^{R}\left|f(x)\left(1-R_{j}^{\alpha}(x)e^{-x}\right)e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\right|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}(x))\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

+2c\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}\left|f(x)e^{-\frac{x}{2}}\right|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}(x))\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=I+II+III.

By the assumption
I+III\leq 4c\varepsilon
. To estimate

we consider that

\left(R_{j}(x)e^{-x}\right)^{\prime}=e^{-x}\frac{-L_{j-1}^{(\alpha+1)}(x)-L_{j}^{\alpha}(x)}{\binom{j+\alpha}{j}}=\frac{-L_{j}^{(\alpha+1)}(x)e^{-x}}{\binom{j+\alpha}{j}},

see
[
27
, (5.1.13)]
. In view of
[
22
, (2.8)]

(26)

\tilde{R}_{n}^{\alpha}(x)x^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq\frac{C}{n^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(x\nu){\frac{\alpha}{2}},\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt0\leq x\leq\frac{1}{\nu}\\
(x\nu)^{-\frac{1}{4}},\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\frac{1}{\nu}<x\leq\frac{\nu}{2}\\
\left(\nu\left(\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}+|x-\nu|\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}},\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\frac{\nu}{2}<x\leq\frac{3\nu}{2}\\
e^{-\gamma x},\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\frac{3\nu}{2}<x,\end{array}\right.

where

. Thus

(27)

\frac{\left|L_{j}^{(\alpha+1)}(x)\right|e^{-x}}{\binom{j+\alpha}{j}}\leq c\frac{j^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\delta^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}},\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0ptx\in(\delta,R).

Thus, denoting the bound of
K
with
M

II\leq c(\delta)Rj^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|f\|_{p,\alpha}\leq c(\delta,R,M)j^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\alpha}{2}},

which is small if

is large enough.

The computation in proof of (d) ensures the regular behavior of translation on sequences.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
,
1\leq p<\infty
. If
K\subset l^{p}_{\alpha}
is precompact, then
K
fulfils
{\bf P_{bs}}
.

Proof.

By property
{\bf P_{as}}
it is enough to prove the statement for finite sets of finite sequences.
Let
S:=s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
and
s_{i}=\{a_{ik}\}_{k=0}^{n_{i}}
and
p_{i}=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{i}}a_{ik}R_{k}^{\alpha}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
there are
\delta_{i}
and
R_{i}
such that
\left(\int_{0}^{\delta_{i}}|\tilde{p}_{i}|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\varepsilon
and
\left(\int_{R_{i}}^{\infty}|\tilde{p}_{i}|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\varepsilon
,
i=1,\dots,n
. Let
R:=R(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon)=\max_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}
,
N:=N(\varepsilon_{1})=\max_{i=1}^{n}n_{i}
,
\delta:=\delta(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon)=\min_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{i}
,
M:=M(\varepsilon_{1})=\max_{i=1}^{n}\|p_{i}\|_{2,\alpha}
. With the abbreviation
a_{ik}=\hat{p}_{i}(k)
, as above we have

\left|\hat{p}_{i}(k)-T_{j}(\hat{p}_{i})(k)\right|=\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(p_{i}(x)-R_{j}^{\alpha}(x)p_{i}(x)e^{-x}\right)R_{k}(x)e^{-x}x^{\alpha}dx\right|

\leq\|R_{k}^{\alpha}\|_{2,\alpha}\left(2\left(\int_{0}^{\delta}|\tilde{p}_{i}|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|p_{i}\|_{2,\alpha}\sup_{[\delta,R]}\left|1-R_{j}(x)e^{-x}\right|+2\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}|\tilde{p}_{i}|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

By (
27
) and (
2
)

\left|\hat{p}_{i}(k)-T_{j}(\hat{p}_{i})(k)\right|\leq ck^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left(4\varepsilon+MR\frac{j^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\delta^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}\right)\leq 5c\varepsilon k^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}

if
j
is large enough. Thus

\|\hat{p}_{i}-T_{j}(\hat{p}_{i})\|_{p,\alpha}\leq 5c\varepsilon\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N}k^{-p\frac{\alpha}{2}}w(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 5c\varepsilon N^{\frac{\alpha+1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Since
N
is independent of
\varepsilon
, it is arbitrary small if
j
is large enough. According to Proposition
1
the proof can be finished with a triangle inequality.

The main theorem of this section os the next corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
. A set
K\subset L^{2}_{\alpha}
is precompact if and only if it satisfies
{\bf P_{b}}
, and the corresponding set
\hat{K}
is pointwise bounded.

Proof.

If
K
is precompact in
L^{2}_{\alpha}
, Theorem
1
ensures equicontinuity in mean. Moreover
K
is bounded in
L^{2}_{\alpha}
, so
\hat{K}
is bonded in
l^{2}_{\alpha}
and as above, pointwise too.

If
K
is equicontinuous in mean, by Theorem
2

\hat{K}
is equivanishing in
l^{2}_{\alpha}
, and the pointwise boundedness ensures precompactness of
\hat{K}
and of
K
too.

3.
Bessel translation method

Bessel translation and Hankel transformation are widely examined by several authors. We mention here only a few examples. One of our main sources is an early paper,
[
21
]
. By Bessel translation modulus of smoothness and the related best approximation can be investigated, see
[
24
]
, and Nikol’skii inequalities for entire functions can be proved, see
[
3
]
. For Fourier-Bessel transformation, similarly to the standard Fourier transformation, uncertainty results are derived, see
[
8
]
, which are somehow in concordance with Pego-type theorems. Hereinafter we concentrate to compactness criteria.

Let
L_{p,\alpha}
be the space of measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{p,(\alpha)}=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(x)|^{p}x^{2\alpha+1}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{2},\hskip 4.0pt1\leq p<\infty,\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\|f\|_{\infty,(\alpha)}=\|f\|_{\infty,\mathbb{R}_{+}}.

The dual space of
L_{p,\alpha}
is denoted by
L_{p^{\prime},\alpha}
, where

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1.

First we introduce the Bessel translation of an integrable function, see
[
21
, (5.19)]
:

(28)

T_{t,\alpha}f(s)=\int_{0}^{\pi}f(\sqrt{t^{2}+s^{2}-2st\cos\varphi})d\mu(\varphi),

where
d\mu(\varphi)=c_{\alpha}\sin^{2\alpha}\varphi d\varphi
, and
c_{\alpha}=\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{2\alpha}\varphi d\varphi\right)^{-1}
.
The symmetry of the definition implies

(29)

T_{t,\alpha}f(s)=T_{s,\alpha}f(t).

We need the entire Bessel functions which are

(30)

j_{\alpha}(z)=\Gamma(\alpha+1)\left(\frac{2}{z}\right)^{\alpha}J_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(k+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{2k}.

Subsequently the next properties will be necessary. The norm of an entire Bessel function on the half-line is attained at zero:

(31)

\|j_{\alpha}\|_{\infty,\mathbb{R}_{+}}=j_{\alpha}(0)=1.

The derivative of
j_{\alpha}
can be expressed as

(32)

j_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z)=-\frac{1}{2(\alpha+1)}zj_{\alpha+1}(z),

see
[
6
]
.

As it is mentioned in the introduction, similarly to the exponential case, the Bessel translation fulfils

T_{t,\alpha}j_{\alpha}(\lambda x)=j_{\alpha}(\lambda t)j_{\alpha}(\lambda x),

see
[
21
]
.

The operator norm of Bessel translation is one, see
[
24
, (2.24)]

(33)

\|T_{t}f\|_{p,(\alpha)}\leq\|f\|_{p,(\alpha)},\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt1\leq p\leq\infty.

The Kolmogorov-Riesz type theorem of this section is as follows.

Theorem 3
.

1\leq p<\infty

K\subset L_{p,\alpha}
is bounded.
K
is precompact if and only if the two conditions below are fulfiled.

{\bf P_{A}.}

K
is equivanishing, i.e.

(34)

\forall\varepsilon>0\hskip 4.0pt\exists R>0,\hskip 4.0pt\forall f\in K\hskip 4.0pt\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}|f(x)|^{p}x^{2\alpha+1}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\varepsilon.

{\bf P_{B}.}

K
is
L_{p,\alpha}
-equicontinuous, i.e.
\forall\varepsilon>0
and
\forall

M_{0}>0
there is a
\delta>0
such that
\forall

0\leq h\leq\delta
,
\forall

f\in K
,
\forall

t\in[0,M_{0}]

(35)

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|T_{t+h,\alpha}f(s)-T_{t,\alpha}f(s)|^{p}s^{2\alpha+1}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\varepsilon.

Proof.

To prove property
{\bf P_{A}}
we can proceed just as in the proof of Theorem
1
.

To prove
{\bf P_{B}}
, with the notation of Theorem
1
we take an
f\in L_{p,\alpha}
and a
\Phi
in
S_{\frac{\varepsilon}{3}}
closest to

.

Denoting by
(s,t,\varphi):=(\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}-2st\cos\varphi})

28
)

\|T_{t+h,\alpha}\Phi-T_{t,\alpha}\Phi\|_{p,(\alpha)}

=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{\pi}\left(\Phi(s,t+h,\varphi)-\Phi(s,t,\varphi)\right)d\mu(\varphi)\right|^{p}s^{2\alpha+1}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\leq\int_{0}^{\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\Phi(s,t+h,\varphi)-\Phi(s,t,\varphi)\right|^{p}s^{2\alpha+1}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}d\mu(\varphi).

Since
||s-t|-h|\leq(s,t,\varphi),(s,t+h,\varphi)\leq s+t+h
, recalling that
\Phi\in S_{\frac{\varepsilon}{3}}
and

\|T_{t+h,\alpha}\Phi-T_{t,\alpha}\Phi\|_{p,(\alpha)}\leq\int_{0}^{\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{R}\left|\Phi(s,t+h,\varphi)-\Phi(s,t,\varphi)\right|^{p}s^{2\alpha+1}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}d\mu(\varphi),

where

|\sqrt{s^{2}+(t+h)^{2}-2s(t+h)\cos\varphi}-\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}-2st\cos\varphi}|\leq h
(cf.
[
24
, (2.27)]
). Recall that there are finitely many uniformly continuous
\Phi
in

. Since they are equicontinuous (in standard sense) we can choose an appropriate
\delta
to
\frac{\varepsilon}{3B}
, where

such that

, thus considering that

\|T_{t+h,\alpha}\Phi-T_{t,\alpha}\Phi\|_{p,(\alpha)}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
. Considering the norm of the linear operator, (

On the other hand supposing
{\bf P_{A}}
and
{\bf P_{B}}
, we show that

is precompact. To this we define

(36)

M_{a}f(s):=\frac{1}{A}\int_{0}^{a}T_{t,\alpha}f(s)t^{2\alpha+1}dt,

where

.
By Hölder’s inequality, then applying the symmetry of translation, cf. (
29
)

(37)

|M_{a}f(s+u)-M_{a}f(s)|\leq\frac{1}{A}\int_{0}^{a}|T_{t,\alpha}f(s+u)-T_{t,\alpha}f(s)|t^{2\alpha+1}dt

\leq\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|T_{t,\alpha}f(s+u)-T_{t,\alpha}f(s)|^{p}t^{2\alpha+1}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

=\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|T_{s+u,\alpha}f(t)-T_{s,\alpha}f(t)|^{p}t^{2\alpha+1}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Similarly, and recalling (
33
)

(38)

|M_{a}f(s)|\leq\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|T_{s,\alpha}f(t)|^{p}t^{2\alpha+1}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{1}{A^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|f\|_{p,(\alpha)}.

a

Let us denote the elements of

,

Applying again Hölder’s inequality and then Fubini’s theorem

\|M_{a}f-f\|_{p,(\alpha)}^{p}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{a}\frac{1}{A}|T_{t,\alpha}f(s)-f(s)|t^{2\alpha+1}dt\right)^{p}s^{2\alpha+1}ds

\|M_{a}f-f\|_{p,(\alpha)}^{p}\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq a}\int_{0}^{\infty}|T_{t,\alpha}f(s)-f(s)|^{p}s^{2\alpha+1}ds.

Similarly

\|M_{a}f-M_{a}f_{j}\|_{p,(\alpha)}^{p}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{R}|M_{a}f(s)-M_{a}f_{j}(s)|^{p}s^{2\alpha+1}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

I\leq\sup_{0\leq s\leq R}|M_{a}f(s)-M_{a}f_{i}(s)|B(R),

. Thus

(40)

\|M_{a}f-M_{a}f_{j}\|_{p,(\alpha)}^{p}\leq B(R)\sup_{0\leq s\leq R}|M_{a}f(s)-M_{a}f_{i}(s)|

+\sup_{g\in K}\left(\|M_{a}g-g\|_{p,(\alpha)}^{p}+\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}|g(s)|^{p}s^{2\alpha+1}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right).

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary and let us choose by (

)

so large that
\forall f\in K

\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}|f(x)|^{p}x^{2\alpha+1}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{5}
and by (

) let us choose
a
so small such that for all
0\leq h\leq a
and for all
f\in K
,
t\in[0,R]\hskip 4.0pt\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|T_{t+h,\alpha}f(s)-T_{t,\alpha}f(s)|^{p}s^{2\alpha+1}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{5}
. Now let us take an
\frac{\varepsilon}{5B(R)}
-net,
N:=N_{\frac{\varepsilon}{5B(R)}}\subset F_{a,R}
. By this choice for all
g\in K

\|g-M_{a}g\|_{p,(\alpha)}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{5},

cf. (
39
). Moreover for an arbitrary
f\in K
there is a suitable
f_{j}
with
M_{a}f_{j}\in N
, such that

\|M_{a}f-M_{a}f_{j}\|_{p,(\alpha)}\leq B(R)\frac{\varepsilon}{5B(R)}+\frac{\varepsilon}{5}+\frac{\varepsilon}{5},

cf. (
40
). Finally we finish the proof with

\|f-f_{j}\|_{p,(\alpha)}\leq\|f-M_{a}f\|_{p,(\alpha)}+\|M_{a}f-M_{a}f_{j}\|_{p,(\alpha)}+\|M_{a}f_{j}-f_{j}\|_{p,(\alpha)}\leq\varepsilon.

Now we define the Bessel-Fourier or Hankel transformation to facilitate formulating a Pego-type theorem. Let us denote the Hankel trasform of a function
f
by

H_{\alpha}(f)(y)=\hat{f}(y):=c_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)j_{\alpha}(xy)x^{2\alpha+1}dx.

The inverse transformation is

H_{\alpha}^{[-1]}(\hat{f})(x)=c_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(y)j_{\alpha}(xy)x^{2\alpha+1}dy,

if exists.
If
K
is a set of certain functions, we denote by
\hat{K}
the set of the Hankel transforms of the functions in
K
.

Below we need that Hankel transformation fulfils the Hausdorff-Young inequality, that is

(41)

\|H_{\alpha}(f)\|_{p^{\prime},(\alpha)}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{p,(\alpha)},\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt1\leq p\leq 2,

see
[
8
]
. We also use the next property of the Hankel transform.

(42)

H_{\alpha}(T_{t,\alpha}f)(y)=j_{\alpha}(ty)\hat{f}(y);\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0ptT_{t,\alpha}\hat{f}(y)=H_{\alpha}(j_{\alpha}(t\cdot)f(\cdot))(y),

see
[
8
]
. Furthermore

(43)

\lim_{y\to\infty}\hat{f}(y)=0,

see
[
24
]
. Defining the convolution by

(f*g)(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}T_{x,\alpha}f(t)g(t)t^{2\alpha+1}dt,

we have

(44)

H_{\alpha}(f*g)=\hat{f}\hat{g},

see
[
8
]
.

Theorem 4
.

1\leq p\leq 2
. Let
K\subset L_{p,\alpha}
. If
K
is bounded and satisfies
{\bf P_{A}}
in
L_{p,\alpha}
,
\hat{K}
satisfies
{\bf P_{B}}
in
L_{p^{\prime},\alpha}
, and if
K
satisfies
{\bf P_{B}}
in
L_{p,\alpha}
,
\hat{K}
satisfies
{\bf P_{A}}
in
L_{p^{\prime},\alpha}
.

Proof.

Let
K
be bounded by
M
and
f\in K
. Then by (
42
) and then (
41
)

\|T_{t+h,\alpha}\hat{f}-T_{t,\alpha}\hat{f}\|_{p^{\prime},(\alpha)}=\|H_{\alpha}\left((j_{\alpha}((t+h)(\cdot))-j_{\alpha}(t(\cdot)))f(\cdot)\right)\|_{p^{\prime},(\alpha)}

\leq C_{p}\|(j_{\alpha}((t+h)s)-j_{\alpha}(ts))f(s)\|_{p,(\alpha)}\leq C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{R}\left|(j_{\alpha}((t+h)s)-j_{\alpha}(ts))f(s)\right|^{p}s^{2\alpha+1}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

+C_{p}\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}\left|(j_{\alpha}((t+h)s)-j_{\alpha}(ts))f(s)\right|^{p}s^{2\alpha+1}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=C_{p}(I+II).

Let
\varepsilon
be arbitrary and we choose
R=R(\varepsilon)
to
\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
according to (
34
). By (
31
)

II\leq 2\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}\left|f(s)\right|^{p}s^{2\alpha+1}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

To estimate
I
, let
M_{0}>0
be arbitrary and assume that
0<t<M_{0}
. Let us choose
\delta=\delta(R(\varepsilon),M_{0},\varepsilon)<1
such that

\delta\leq\frac{(\alpha+1)\varepsilon}{(M_{0}+1)R^{2}M}.

Then in view of (
32
) and (
31
) for all
0\leq h\leq\delta
, by choice of
\delta

I\leq(M_{0}+1)R\frac{1}{2(\alpha+1)}\|j_{\alpha+1}\|_{\infty}\delta R\|f\|_{p,(\alpha)}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

To prove the second statement, for an arbitrary fixed
\delta>0
we choose nonnegative functions
g_{\delta}
supported on
(0,\delta)
such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}g_{\delta}(x)x^{2\alpha+1}dx=1.

Then in view of (
43
) we choose
R=R(\delta)
such that
|H_{\alpha}(g_{\delta})(y)|\leq\frac{1}{2}
if
y>R
. Thus, by (
44
) and (
41
)

\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}|\hat{f}(y)|^{p^{\prime}}y^{2\alpha+1}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq 2\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}|H_{\alpha}(f)(y)(1-H_{\alpha}(g_{\delta})(y))|^{p^{\prime}}y^{2\alpha+1}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\leq C(p)\|f-f*g_{\delta}\|_{p,(\alpha)}=C(p)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}(f(x)-T_{x,\alpha}f(t))g_{\delta}(t)t^{2\alpha+1}dt\right|^{p}x^{2\alpha+1}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\leq C(p)\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(x)-T_{t,\alpha}f(x)|^{p}x^{2\alpha+1}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}g_{\delta}(t)t^{2\alpha+1}dt

\leq C(p)\sup_{0\leq t\leq\delta}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(x)-T_{t,\alpha}f(x)|^{p}x^{2\alpha+1}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where in the last but one (Minkowski) inequality the symmetry of translation is considered. Thus the proof can be finished by choosing an appropriate
\delta
to the arbitrary
\varepsilon
in view of (
35
), and
g_{\delta}
and
R(\delta)
to
\delta
as above.

Corollary 2
.

\mathrm{(I)}
A bounded subset
K\subset L_{2,\alpha}
is precompact if and only if for all
M_{0}>0
,
t\in[0,M_{0}]

\|T_{t+h,\alpha}f-T_{t,\alpha}f\|_{2,(\alpha)}\to 0
, and
\|T_{t+h,\alpha}\hat{f}-T_{t,\alpha}\hat{f}\|_{2,(\alpha)}\to 0
uniformly in
K
.

\mathrm{(II)}
A bounded subset
K\subset L_{2,\alpha}
is precompact if and only if

\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}|f(x)|^{2}x^{2\alpha+1}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\to 0
, and
\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}|\hat{f}(y)|^{2}y^{2\alpha+1}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\to 0
uniformly in
K
.

Corollary 3
.

Let
u,v:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
,
u,v\in L_{\infty}
such that
\lim_{x\to\infty}u(x),v(x)=0
, and assume that
u
is continuous. Then the operator
L:=uH_{\alpha}v
,
f\mapsto uH_{\alpha}(vf)
is compact on
L_{2,(\alpha)}
.

Proof.

Let
K\subset L_{2,\alpha}
be a subset bounded by
M
. By the assumption, for an
\varepsilon>0
there is an
R>0
such that
|v(x)|<\varepsilon
if
x>R
. Thus
\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}|(vf)(x)|^{2}x^{2\alpha+1}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\varepsilon M
, that is
vK
is bounded and equivanishing. So by Theorem
4

H_{\alpha}(vK)
is
L_{2,\alpha}
-equicontinuous, and by assumption
LK=uH_{\alpha}(vK)
is also
L_{2,\alpha}
-equicontinuous and equivanishing.

Centrality and Partition of Idempotents
Vineeth Chintala

Abstract
. We show that an idempotent lies in the center if it commutes with the other idempotents in the ring. Furthermore, we introduce a partition of the set of idempotents and show that the automorphisms of the ring act transitively on each equivalence class. (Mathematics Subject Classification : 05C25, 16B99)



1.
Introduction

Idempotents play a critical role in the structure of rings. We first show that an idempotent lies in the center if it commutes with the other idempotents. Furthermore, we partition the set of idempotents into equivalence classes and show that

i.

If
e_{1},e_{2}
are two idempotents in the same equivalence class, then there is an automorphism taking
e_{1}
to
e_{2}
. (Theorem
3.6
).

ii.

For finite rings over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we prove that the number of idempotents in an equivalence class is
q^{k}
for some
k
(Theorem
3.7
).

1.1.
Notation

R
can be any associative ring with identity. An element
x
is called an idempotent if
x^{2}=x
.
An element
c
is said to be in the center of the ring if
cx=xc
for all elements
x\in R
.

2.
Lying in the center

The results in this section are presumably known, though probably not in the version stated here.
The importance of the following theorem will become clear when we introduce a partition of a graph of idempotents.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
e
be an idempotent in
R
. If
e
does not lie in the center, then
R
contains another idempotent
e^{\prime}
such that

(ee^{\prime}=e,{\hskip 5.69054pt}e^{\prime}e=e^{\prime})\text{\hskip 14.22636ptor \hskip 14.22636pt}(ee^{\prime}=e^{\prime},{\hskip 5.69054pt}e^{\prime}e=e).

Proof.

Since
e
does not lie in the center, there is an element
b\in R
such that

be-ebe\neq eb-ebe.

Let
u=be-ebe
and
v=eb-ebe
. Then
u^{2}=v^{2}=0
.

The idempotents
e_{l}=e+u
and
e_{r}=e+v
satisfy the required properties

ee_{r}=e,\hskip 5.69054pte_{r}e=e_{r}\hskip 14.22636pt|\hskip 14.22636ptee_{l}=e_{l},\hskip 5.69054pte_{l}e=e.

Clearly
e_{r},e_{l}
are distinct from
e
whenever
u,v
are non-zero.
Since
u\neq v
, both
u,v
can’t be simultaneously zero.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
e
be an idempotent in
R
. If
e
commutes with all the idempotents of
R
, then
e
lies in the center.

3.
Equivalence classes of Idempotents

Let
\gamma(R)
be a directed graph, where the vertices are idempotents of
R
, and there is an edge
e\longrightarrow e^{\prime}
if and only if
ee^{\prime}=0
. Then
e^{\prime}
is said to be an out-neighbour of
e
and
e
is said to be an in-neighbour of
e^{\prime}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Zero-divisor graphs (for commutative rings) were introduced by I. Beck in
[
3
]
and later extended to the noncommutative setting (see
[
2
]
,
[
5
]
). Departing from the literature, we focus here on the idempotents (instead of all the zero-divisors) and prove results that hold for arbitrary noncommutative rings.

Definition 3.2
.

•

Let
e\sim_{o}e^{\prime}
if the idempotents
e,e^{\prime}
have the same out-neighbours in
\gamma(R)
. Clearly
\sim_{o}
is an equivalence relation.

•

Let
\mathcal{O}_{e}:=\{x\in R:ex=e,xe=x\}
.

Observe that all elements of
\mathcal{O}_{e}
are idempotents. Indeed if
a\in\mathcal{O}_{e}
, then
a^{2}=(ae)a=a(ea)=ae=a
.

Theorem 3.3
.

e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{e}
if and only if
e^{\prime}\sim_{o}e
.

Proof.

Let
e^{\prime}\sim_{o}e
. Then
e^{\prime}(1-e)=0
. Therefore
e^{\prime}=e^{\prime}e
. Similarly
e(1-e^{\prime})=0
and so
e=ee^{\prime}
.
For the converse, suppose
er=0
. Then
e^{\prime}r=(e^{\prime}e)r=0
.
∎

Similarly one can define an equivalence relation
\sim_{i}
corresponding to the in-neighbours in
\gamma(R)
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{e}=\{x\in R:ex=x,xe=e\}
.

It follows from the definitions that
\mathcal{I}_{e}\cap\mathcal{O}_{e}=\{e\}
. In other words, no two idempotents have the same (in and out) neighbours in
\gamma(R)
.

3.5.
Automorphisms of
\gamma(R)

Any automorphism of the ring translates to an automorphism of the graph.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
e_{1}\sim_{o}e_{2}
. Then there is an inner automorphism of
R
which sends
e_{1}
to
e_{2}
.

Proof.

Take
u=e_{2}-e_{1}
. Then
u^{2}=0
and
(1+u)^{-1}=1-u
.
Consider the inner automorphism of
R
where

x\rightarrow(1+u)x(1-u).

This automorphism takes
e_{1}
to
e_{2}
.
∎

Therefore there are at least
|\mathcal{O}_{e}|
distinct automorphisms stabilizing
\mathcal{O}_{e}
. Similarly if
e_{1}\sim_{i}e_{2}
, then there is an automorphism which sends
e_{1}
to
e_{2}
.

Theorem
2.1
tells us that at least one of the sets
\mathcal{I}_{e},\mathcal{O}_{e}
is nontrivial whenever
e
is not in the center. In fact we can use the equivalence relations to say more.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
R
be a finite algebra over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Suppose
e
is a non-central idempotent, and
\mathcal{X}_{e}\in\{\mathcal{I}_{e},\mathcal{O}_{e}\}
. Then
|\mathcal{X}_{e}|=q^{k}
for some integer
k
.

Proof.

Let
V
be the vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
generated by the elements of
\mathcal{X}_{e}
. Let
v=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}e_{i}
, where
e_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{e}
and
c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then

v^{2}=\big(\small{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}}c_{i}\big)v.

We can choose a basis of
V
consisting of idempotents
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{e}
. Any element of
\mathcal{X}_{e}
is of the form
e=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}e_{i}
, where
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}=1
. There are
q^{n-1}
such elements.
∎

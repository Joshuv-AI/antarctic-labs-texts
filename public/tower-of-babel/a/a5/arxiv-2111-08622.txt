Introduction

Let
X
be a minimal surface of general type with maximal Albanese dimension (recall that a surface has maximal Albanese dimension if its Albanese morphism is generically finite). We denote by
K_{X}
the canonical divisor, by
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
the Euler characteristic of the structure sheaf, by
q
the dimension of its Albanese variety and by
q^{\prime}=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
the irregularity (recall that over fields of positive characteristic we have
q^{\prime}\geq q
, while over the complex numbers
q=q^{\prime}
holds).

In this paper we are interested in characterizing surfaces which lie on or close to the Severi lines, i.e. surfaces for which the quantity

K_{X}^{2}-4\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})-4(q-2)

(1.1)

vanishes or is "small" provided that
K_{X}^{2}<\frac{9}{2}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
over algebraically closed fields of positive characteristic. This value is strictly related to the so called Severi inequality, which states that a surface of general type with maximal Albanese dimension satisfies

K_{X}^{2}\geq 4\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})

(1.2)

and was proved over the complex numbers by Pardini in
[
35
]
and over fields of any characteristic by Yuan and Zhang in
[
40
]
for fields of positive characteristic.
Barja, Pardini and Stoppino have given a characterization of surfaces over the complex numbers for which the inequality
1.2
is indeed an equality in
[
2
]
, namely these are surfaces whose canonical model is a double cover of its Albanese variety branched over an ample divisor with at most negligible singularities (in particular
q=2
). An analogous characterization is obtained in
[
16
]
by Gu, Sun and Zhou over fields of any characteristic. There are many generalizations of the Severi inequality; in particular Lu and Zuo have proved in
[
29
]
a similar inequality involving also the irregularity
q=q^{\prime}
over the complex numbers: a surface of general type and maximal Albanese dimension satisfies

K_{X}^{2}\geq\min\Bigl\{\frac{9}{2}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}),4\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})+4(q-2)\Bigr\}

(1.3)

or, equivalently, if
K_{X}^{2}<\frac{9}{2}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
then
K_{X}^{2}\geq 4\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})+4(q-2)
.
They also give conditions for a surface to satisfy the equality

\frac{9}{2}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})>K_{X}^{2}=4\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})+4(q-2).

(1.4)

The condition
K_{X}^{2}<\frac{9}{2}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
is necessary to prove that there exists an involution
i
for which the Albanese morphism of
X
is composed with
i
(cf.
[
29
]
Theorem 3.1) which is central in their argument.
There is a single step,
[
29
]
Lemma 4.4(2), where the condition
K_{X}^{2}<\frac{9}{2}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
is really needed in their proof and it is not enough to require that the Albanese morphism of
X
is composed with an involution.

Finally the author have given a complete classification of surfaces lying close to the Severi lines over the complex numbers in
[
7
]
. Namely, it is showed that a surface of general type with maximal Albanese dimension satisfies
K_{X}^{2}=4\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})+4(q-2)
in case
q\geq 3
and
K_{X}^{2}<\frac{9}{2}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
if and only if its canonical model
X
is a double cover of a product elliptic surface
C\times E
over a divisor
R
with at most negligible singularities. Moreover it is proved that, under the same hypotheses, surfaces for which
K_{X}^{2}\neq 4\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})+4(q-2)
satisfy
K_{X}^{2}\geq 4\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})+8(q-2)
. It is also given a characterization of surfaces for which equality holds when
q\geq 3
: these are surfaces whose canonical model
X
is isomorphic to a double cover of a smooth isotrivial elliptic surface
Y
over a curve
C
of genus
q-1
, branched over a divisor
R
with at worst negligible singularities for which
K_{Y}.R=8(q-2)
.

In the present article we extend the results of
[
29
]
and
[
7
]
over fields of positive characteristic.

from which we easily derive

Example 3.8

(Multiplicative and additive rational vector fields on an elliptic curve with only two zeroes and poles)
.

Over a field of characteristic
2
an elliptic curve
E
can be seen as the projectivization inside
\mathds{P}^{2}
of

y^{2}+\alpha xy+y+x^{3}

(3.12)

where
\alpha\in k
is such that
\alpha^{3}\neq 1
: this is the so called Deuring normal form (cf.
[
38
]
Proposition A.1.3).
In particular the
j
-invariant of
E
is
j(E)=\frac{\alpha^{12}}{\alpha^{3}-1}
and
E
is supersingular (i.e. it has no non-trivial
2
-torsion points) if and only if
\alpha=0
(ibidem).

Notice that every rational vector field on
E
is uniquely determined by its value on the function
x
: indeed we can determine its value on
y
(hence on all the field
k(E)
of rational functions on
E
) via the condition that it has to vanish on
y^{2}+\alpha xy+y+x^{3}
.
Now let
\partial_{x}
be the rational vector field on
E
which correspond to the derivation by
x
: from the condition
\partial_{x}(y^{2}+\alpha xy+y+x^{3})=0
we get

(1+\alpha x)\partial_{x}(y)=x^{2}+\alpha y:

(3.13)

we claim that
\delta=(1+\alpha x)\partial_{x}
is a never-vanishing vector field on
E
.

Indeed
\delta(x)=1+\alpha x=0
implies
x=\frac{1}{\alpha}
while
\delta(y)=x^{2}+\alpha y=0
implies that
y=\frac{1}{\alpha^{3}}
, but
(\frac{1}{\alpha},\frac{1}{\alpha^{3}})
is not a point of
E
: in particular, because the tangent sheaf of an elliptic curve is trivial, we can extend this to a regular never-vanishing vector field on
E
.

If we consider the rational vector field
\delta_{E}^{\prime}=x\delta
: by the previous discussion we easily see that it has a single zero at
(0,0)
and at
(0,1)
and a double pole at
\infty
.
We notice that
\delta_{E}^{\prime}
is a multiplicative rational vector field, indeed

\delta_{E}^{\prime 2}(x)=\delta_{E}^{\prime}(x+\alpha x^{2})=x+\alpha x^{2}=\delta_{E}^{\prime}(x)

(3.14)

and

\delta_{E}^{\prime 2}(y)=\delta_{E}^{\prime}(x^{3}+\alpha xy)=\delta_{E}^{\prime}(y^{2}+y)=\delta_{E}^{\prime}(y).

(3.15)

On the other hand, if we assume
\alpha\neq 0
, a similar calculation shows that
\delta_{E}=(1+\alpha x)\delta
is an additive rational vector field with a double zero at
(\frac{1}{\alpha},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha^{3}}})
and a double pole at
\infty
.
If
\alpha=0
, i.e. the elliptic curve is supersingular, we believe it is not possible to find an additive rational vector field with only two (possibly equal) zeroes.

Let
m(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x+\gamma_{i})
,
n(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x+\epsilon_{i})
,
\beta(x)=\frac{m^{2}(x)}{\alpha n^{2}(x)}
and
\overline{\beta}(x)=\frac{m^{2}(x)+xn^{2}(x)}{n^{2}(x)}
: we can always choose
m(x)
and
n(x)
such that
\gamma_{i}\neq\frac{1}{\alpha},0\neq\epsilon_{k}
for all
i
and
k
and that all the zeroes
\mu_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots 2d+1
of
m^{2}(x)+xn^{2}(x)
are non-multiple and mutually different from
\frac{1}{\alpha},0
and
\epsilon_{i}
(
i=1,\ldots,d
).
A similar computation as above, shows that
\overline{\delta}_{E}=\beta(x)\delta_{E}
and
\overline{\delta}_{E}^{\prime}=\overline{\beta}(x)\partial_{x}
are, respectively, an additive and a multiplicative rational vector field on
E
.
Moreover their corresponding divisors are, respectively,

\begin{split}2P_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}-2P_{\infty}+2G_{1}+2&G_{1}^{\prime}+\ldots+2G_{d}+\\
+2G_{d}^{\prime}-2E_{1}-2E_{1}^{\prime}-&\ldots-2E_{d}-2E_{d}^{\prime}\end{split}

(3.16)

and

\begin{split}P_{\infty}-2P_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+M_{1}+M_{1}^{\prime}+\ldots+&M_{2d+1}+M_{2d+1}^{\prime}-\\
-2E_{1}-2E_{1}^{\prime}-\ldots-&2E_{d}-2E_{d}^{\prime}\end{split}

(3.17)

where
G_{i},G_{i}^{\prime}
,
E_{i},E_{i}^{\prime}
and
M_{i},M_{i}^{\prime}
are the points where
x+\gamma_{i}
,
x+\epsilon_{i}
and
x+\mu_{i}
vanish,
P_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}=(\frac{1}{\alpha},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha^{3}}})
and
P_{\infty}
is the point at infinity (notice that, if
\alpha=0
, then
P_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}=P_{\infty}
).

Example 3.9

(Multiplicative and additive rational vector fields on a hyperelliptic curve)
.

Over a field of characteristic
2
, a smooth hyperelliptic curve
C
of genus

can be described as the projectivization inside the total space of the line bundle

of

where

is a polynomial of degree

and

is a polynomial of degree

]

.
In order to have

as tame as possible, i.e.

has the maximum number of branch points, we require

to have no multiple zeroes.

Then we can rewrite the equation of

as

where
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}
for

and
\beta_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}
for all

.
Denote by

for

the ramification points of

and by

,

the two points of

lying over

and by

.

Consider, as in Example

, the rational vector field

on

: the equation defining

tells us that

We then easily derive that the divisor corresponding to

is
2P_{\alpha}+2P_{\alpha}^{\prime}-2P_{0}-\ldots-2P_{g}
where
\alpha\neq\alpha_{i}
for all

and
P_{\alpha}
and
P_{\alpha}^{\prime}
are the two points of

lying over
\alpha\in\mathds{P}^{1}
: indeed

vanishes of order

on every

,

vanishes of order two on

and
P_{\alpha}^{\prime}
and the degree of the tangent line bundle of

is

.
In particular, because the divisor associated with

is

, we have that the divisor associated with
\delta
is
2P_{\infty}+2P_{\infty}^{\prime}-2P_{0}-\ldots-2P_{g}
.

Let
m(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x+\gamma_{i})
,
n(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x+\epsilon_{i})
,
\beta(x)=\frac{m^{2}(x)}{n^{2}(x)}
and
\overline{\beta}(x)=\frac{m^{2}(x)+xn^{2}(x)}{n^{2}(x)}
: we can always choose
m(x)
and
n(x)
such that
\gamma_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}\neq\epsilon_{k}
for all
i
,
j
and
k
and that all the zeroes
\mu_{i}
for

of

are non-multiple and mutually different from
\alpha_{i}
(

) and
\epsilon_{i}
(

).
A similar computation to the one in Example

, shows that

and
\delta_{C}^{\prime}=\overline{\beta}(x)\delta
are, respectively, an additive and a multiplicative rational vector field on

.
Moreover their corresponding divisors are, respectively,

\begin{split}2P_{\infty}+2P_{\infty}^{\prime}+2G_{1}+2&G_{1}^{\prime}+\ldots+2G_{d}+\\
+2G_{d}^{\prime}-2E_{1}-2E_{1}^{\prime}-\ldots-2E_{d}&-2E_{d}^{\prime}-2P_{0}-\ldots-2P_{g}\end{split}

(3.21)

and

\begin{split}P_{\infty}+P_{\infty}^{\prime}+M_{1}+M_{1}^{\prime}+\ldots+&M_{2d+1}+M_{2d+1}^{\prime}-\\
-2E_{1}-2E_{1}^{\prime}-\ldots-2E_{d}-2E_{d}^{\prime}-&2P_{0}-\ldots-2P_{g}\end{split}

(3.22)

where
G_{i},G_{i}^{\prime}
,
E_{i},E_{i}^{\prime}
and

are the points where

,

and

vanish.

Proposition 3.10
.

be a product surface where

,

and

be two rational additive (respectively multiplicative) vector fields on

and

both of which have at least one zero.
Then the rational vector field

naturally induced on

If we consider the induced inseparable double cover

we have that the corresponding 1-foliation

is isomorphic, as a line bundle, to

\delta_{A_{\infty}}
(respectively
\delta_{B_{\infty}}
) is the divisor of poles of
\delta_{A}
(respectively
\delta_{B}
) and
\pi_{A}
,
\pi_{B}
are the projection to

and

.

lie in the image of the points

where

and

are both zeroes or both poles of

and
\delta_{B}

Proof.

and

).

Before considering non-hyperlliptic surface fibrations we recall some facts about Harder-Narasimhan filtration of vector bundles over curves and (strongly) semi-stability.

Definition 4.1
.

A vector bundle
E
on a curve
C
is said to be (strongly) semi-stable if, for every sub-vector bundle
F\subseteq E

\mu(F)\leq\mu(E)
where
\mu(E)
is the slope of
E
and is defined as
\frac{\deg(E)}{\rk(E)}
(the same holds true for
f^{*}E
where
f\colon C^{\prime}\to C
is a finite morphism of smooth curves).

Remark 4.2
.

It is known (
[
32
]
Proposition 3.2) that, given a finite separable morphism
f\colon C^{\prime}\to C
, a vector bundle

is semi-stable if and only if
f^{*}E
is.
The "if" part holds true even for inseparable morphism, while there are many counterexample for the "only if" part (cf.
[
24
]

That is why the notion of strongly semi-stability has been introduced.
It is also known that strongly semi-stability coincide with semi-stability over curves with globally generated tangent bundle (cf.
[
30
]
Theorem 2.1 and Remark 2.2), e.g. over rational and elliptic curves.

Recall that for a vector bundle
E
there exists a unique Harder-Narasimhan filtration

0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\ldots\subset E_{n}=E,

(4.1)

such that the subsequent quotients
E_{i}/E_{i-1}
are semi-stable vector bundles with slopes
\mu_{i}=\mu(E_{i}/E_{i-1})
satisfying

(4.2)

which can be constructed inductively from the bottom (

is the subbundle with lowest slope) or from the top (

is the subbundle for which the quotient has maximal slope).
An easy calculation shows that

\deg(E)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1}),

(4.3)

where

Proof of Theorem

.

We use the same notation as in Section

If

If

and Theorem

If

is an elliptic surface, by Equation

\begin{split}K_{X}^{2}-4\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})=4(q-2)F&.L_{0}-2\deg(\mathcal{L}_{0})F.L_{0}+\\
+2\sum_{j=1}^{m}(a_{j}-1)F_{j}.L_{0}+&2\sum_{i=1}^{t}(m_{i}-1)+n.\end{split}

for all

for all

and

If we want to increase slightly

First we discuss

(-2)

3

(ibidem). This means that in this case

Now suppose that there exists an
i

has a singular point

we get

and part 1 is proven.

Now we study the case when

.
If

is a surface of general type and satisfies

.

we have that in all other cases

as above, if

8
Partial results in characteristic
2

In this section we are going to see what can be said about our results when the characteristic of the ground field is
2
.
As we have already mentioned, the results presented on Sections
6
and
7
are based on the theory of double covers of surfaces and that’s why things get wilder when the characteristic of the ground field is exactly
2
.
In Section
2
we have shown the theory of double covers, with a particular attention to the case of characteristic
2
: even though this case is more complicated we have some similarities.

First of all we noticed that for every double cover
f\colon X\to Y
ther is naturally associated a line bundle
L
via the following short exact sequence:

0\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\to L^{-1}\to 0.

(8.1)

If the characteristic is different from two, we have seen that this exact sequence splits and the divisor associated with
L^{2}
is the ramification divisor of

: in particular we have that
L
is an effective divisor up to a multiple.
We have used this effectiveness result for

to show that
K_{Y}.L\geq 0
when
Y
is a minimal non-ruled surface, and this is the real result we would like to extend in characteristic two.

As we have already noticed in Remark
2.14
, the line bundle

is effective up to a multiple in most cases even when the characteristic of the ground field is
2
.
There is just one single case where nothing assures
L
to be effective up to a multiple, i.e. for non-splittable inseparable double covers.

Now suppose that

is an inseparable double cover such that

is a factor of the Albanese morphism
\alb_{X}\colon X\to\Alb(X)
.
For simplicity we also assume that both

and

are smooth.
As we have seen, it is canonically defined another double cover

.

As we have seen in Remark
2.23
, the line bundle
M^{-1}
, which is the inverse of the line bundle associated with
g^{(1)}\colon Y^{(1)}\to X
, has many sections.
At first glance, we thought it were possible to prove that
L
has to be effective up to a multiple using this and the relations of Lemma
2.21
between
L
,
M
and the associated foliations on
Y^{(1)}
and
X
, but in the end we were not able to do so.

Without this, we can only collect here partial results concerning Severi type inequalities when the characteristic of the ground field is
2
.

Let

be a surface of general type with maximal Albanese dimension over a field of characteristic

satisfying

: by Theorem

we know that there exists a morphism of degree two

Suppose that

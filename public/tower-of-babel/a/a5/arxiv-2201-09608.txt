Path integral analysis of the axial anomaly in Very Special Relativity
R. Bufalo, M. Ghasemkhani

Abstract
In this note, we revise the problem of the axial anomaly in the framework of very special relativity following Fujikawa’s path integral approach. We show nonperturbatively that no VSR contribution is present in the path integral measure in ( 3 + 1 ) (3+1) -dimensional spacetime. Furthermore, we extend our results to ( 1 + 1 ) (1+1) dimensions, as well as to the two-dimesional curved spacetime.

The study of anomalies in gauge theories is extremely important in demonstrating its consistency and physical properties, such as unitarity and renormalizability, as they are expected to cancel.
In order to verify whether a symmetry is preserved at the quantum level, one must analyze the respective Ward identity (or Slavnov-Taylor identity in the non-Abelian case).

Remarkably, in some special cases, the full structure of the anomaly can be calculated exactly at the one-loop order, and in the case of the chiral anomaly, it is given by the famous Adler-Bell-
Jackiw (ABJ) anomaly, justifying the

2
Fujikawa’s approach to the axial anomaly

In order to investigate the axial anomaly in the context of path integral approach, it is necessary to establish the path integral measure accurately
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
. We start with the Euclidean VSR path integral

Z\left[A\right]=\int D\overline{\psi}D\psi\exp\left[\int d^{4}x~\bar{\psi}\left(i\not{\nabla}-m_{e}\right)\psi\right],

(2.1)

where
\nabla_{\mu}=D_{\mu}-\frac{m^{2}n_{\mu}}{2\left(n.D\right)}
is defined as the VSR covariant derivative with
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-ieA_{\mu}
, and
m
is the VSR parameter.

1
1

1

Although a gauge fixing is allowed in this gauge-invariant approach, we shall show that contrary to
[
30
]
, where the light-cone gauge
n.A=0
was used in order to simplify the operator
\nabla
to
\nabla_{\mu}=D_{\mu}-\frac{m^{2}n_{\mu}}{2\left(n.\partial\right)}
(removing all nonlocal couplings), we can perform the whole analysis in path integral without any gauge fixing.

We would like to analyze the path integral measure behavior under local chiral transformations

\psi\to e^{i\beta\left(x\right)\gamma_{5}}\psi,\quad\overline{\psi}\to\overline{\psi}e^{i\beta\left(x\right)\gamma_{5}},

(2.2)

and then determine whether VSR effects can modify the well-known axial anomaly
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

In this sense, we decompose the spinors into eigenfunctions of the
Dirac operator as follows

\psi\left(x\right)=\sum_{n}a_{n}\varphi_{n}\left(x\right),\quad\bar{\psi}\left(x\right)=\sum_{m}\varphi_{m}^{\dagger}\left(x\right)\overline{b}_{m},

(2.3)

where the coefficients
a_{n}
and
\overline{b}_{m}
are independent
Grassmann variables. The hermitian Dirac operator
\nabla
satisfies

\not{\nabla}\varphi_{n}\left(x\right)=\lambda_{n}\varphi_{n}\left(x\right),

(2.4)

where
\lambda_{n}
’s are real eigenvalues of
\nabla
, the set of
eigenfunctions is orthonormal and complete

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x~\varphi_{m}^{\dagger}\left(x\right)\varphi_{n}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\delta_{mn},

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\varphi_{n}\left(x\right)\varphi_{n}^{\dagger}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=\delta^{(4)}\left(x-y\right),

(2.5)

The first point to emphasize is that this basis set allows us to diagonalize

\bar{\psi}\left(i\not{\nabla}-m_{e}\right)\psi=\sum_{n}\left(i\lambda_{n}-m_{e}\right)\overline{b}_{n}a_{n}.

(2.6)

Hence, we realize that under the decomposition (
2.3
) the fermionic path integral measure can be expressed as
D\overline{\psi}D\psi=\prod_{n}d\overline{b}_{n}da_{n}
.
In the next step, we shall consider how the fermionic fields behave under the local chiral transformations (
2.2
).
In particular, we are interested in analyzing how the fermionic path
integral measure behaves under these infinitesimal transformations.
Applying these considerations,we find the following transformation law for the coefficients of (
2.3
)

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{m}^{\prime}\\
\overline{b}_{m}^{\prime}\end{array}\right)=\sum_{k}C_{mk}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{k}\\
\overline{b}_{k}\end{array}\right),

(2.7)

with the transformations matrix

C_{mk}=\delta_{mk}+i\int d^{4}x~\varphi_{m}^{\dagger}\left(x\right)\beta\left(x\right)\gamma_{5}\varphi_{k}\left(x\right).

(2.8)

Finally, from these results, we can write the transformation for the
path integral measure as

\displaystyle D\overline{\psi}^{\prime}D\psi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n}d\overline{b}_{n}^{\prime}da_{n}^{\prime}\equiv\pazocal{J}\left[\beta\right]D\overline{\psi}d\psi,

(2.9)

where we have identified the Jacobian
\pazocal{J}\left[\beta\right]=\left(\det C\right)^{-2}
.
This functional can also be cast as

\displaystyle\pazocal{J}\left[\beta\right]=\exp\Bigg(-2i\int d^{4}x~\beta\left(x\right)\sum_{m}\varphi_{m}^{\dagger}\left(x\right)\gamma_{5}\varphi_{m}\left(x\right)\Bigg),

(2.10)

which form is valid for infinitesimal
\beta\left(x\right)
.
However, this sum is not well defined, actually

\sum_{m}\varphi_{m}^{\dagger}\left(x\right)\gamma_{5}\varphi_{m}\left(x\right)=\text{tr}\left(\gamma_{5}\right)\delta\left(0\right),

(2.11)

where we have used the completeness relation of the eigenfunctions (
2.5
).
Hence, it is necessary to regularize the sum in (
2.10
), and we follow Fujikawa’s prescription
[
5
]
.
As we will show below, the correct use of the Fujikawa’s regularization approach leads to an unambiguous analysis of the axial anomaly in the VSR setting.

In the regularization procedure, we shall consider an arbitrary function that is smooth and decreasing sufficiently rapidly at infinity

f\Big(\frac{\lambda_{m}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\Big),

(2.12)

with
\Lambda\to\infty
and

\displaystyle f\left(\infty\right)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}\left(\infty\right)=f^{\prime\prime}\left(\infty\right)=\cdots=0,

\displaystyle f\left(0\right)

\displaystyle=1.

(2.13)

Hence, since the Dirac operator is now regularized, we can express the eigenfunctions in Fourier components, i.e. plane waves, and thus we can compute the Jacobian (
2.10
) explicitly.

First, we should consider

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\varphi_{m}^{\dagger}\left(x\right)\gamma_{5}\varphi_{m}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{\left(2\pi\right)^{4}}\text{tr}\Big[e^{-iq.x}\gamma_{5}f\Big(\frac{\not{\nabla}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\Big)e^{iq.x}\Big]

(2.14)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{\left(2\pi\right)^{4}}\text{tr}\Big[e^{-iq.x}\gamma_{5}f\Big(\frac{\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}}{\Lambda^{2}}+\frac{1}{4\Lambda^{2}}\left[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\right]\left[\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}\right]\Big)e^{iq.x}\Big],

(2.15)

where we have decomposed the Dirac operator as usual.
Furthermore, the effect of moving the plane waves is a shift in the differential operators

e^{-iq.x}f\left(\partial_{\mu}\right)e^{iq.x}=f\left(\partial_{\mu}+iq_{\mu}\right).

(2.16)

Hence, we observe that under this change we have
D\to D+iq
. Thus, the VSR covariant derivative is written as

\nabla_{\mu}^{\prime}=D_{\mu}+iq_{\mu}-\frac{m^{2}n_{\mu}}{2\left(n.D+in.q\right)}.

(2.17)

This implies that the argument of the function
f(z)
in (
2.15
) takes the form

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=-q_{\mu}q^{\mu}+\frac{2iq_{\mu}\left[D^{\mu}-\frac{m^{2}n^{\mu}}{2\left(n.D+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\right]}{\Lambda}+\frac{\left(D_{\mu}-\frac{m^{2}n_{\mu}}{2\left(n.D+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\right)\left(D^{\mu}-\frac{m^{2}n^{\mu}}{2\left(n.D+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\right)}{\Lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\Lambda^{2}}\left[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\right]\left(\left[D_{\mu},D_{\nu}\right]-\frac{m^{2}n_{\nu}}{2}\left[D_{\mu},\frac{1}{\left(n.D+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\right]-\frac{m^{2}n_{\mu}}{2}\left[\frac{1}{\left(n.D+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)},D_{\nu}\right]\right).

(2.18)

Here, it is worth to formulate some comments as follows about this expression:

1.

We have performed the rescaling in the momentum
q\to\Lambda q
, which allows gathering the relevant contributing terms of eq.(
2.15
);

2.

The
m^{4}
term vanishes, since it is proportional to the product of a symmetric tensor
n_{\mu}n_{\nu}
with
\left[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\right]
;

3.

Since
q
is a fixed/constant 4-vector, its commutator with the derivative
D_{\mu}
is zero, i.e.
\left[D_{\mu},q_{\nu}\right]=0
;

4.

In regard to the commutator
\left[D_{\mu},\frac{1}{\left(n.D+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\right]
, it vanishes identically because in the limit
\Lambda\to\infty
the nonlocal factor goes as
\frac{1}{\left(n.D+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\sim\frac{1}{\left(i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}
,
i.e.
\left[D_{\mu},\frac{1}{\left(n.D+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\right]\sim\left[D_{\mu},\frac{1}{\left(i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\right]=0
.

Now, we expand the content of the function
f\left(z\right)
around the value
y=-q_{\mu}q^{\mu}=q^{2}
.
Furthermore, only the terms proportional to
1/\Lambda^{4}
or larger survive in the limit

\Lambda\to\infty
. Also, looking at the trace of the
\gamma
-matrices
with
\gamma_{5}
, only terms with four or more
\gamma
-matrices
have nonvanishing traces. Hence, we find

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\varphi_{m}^{\dagger}\left(x\right)\gamma_{5}\varphi_{m}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\Lambda^{4}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{\left(2\pi\right)^{4}}\text{tr}\left[\gamma_{5}\Big(f\left(y\right)+wf^{\prime}\left(y\right)+\frac{w^{2}}{2!}f^{\prime\prime}\left(y\right)+\cdots\Big)\right],

(2.19)

where
w
is defined as

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=\frac{2iq_{\mu}\left[D^{\mu}-\frac{m^{2}n^{\mu}}{2\left(n.D+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\right]}{\Lambda}+\frac{\left(D_{\mu}-\frac{m^{2}n_{\mu}}{2\left(n.D+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\right)\left(D^{\mu}-\frac{m^{2}n^{\mu}}{2\left(n.D+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\right)}{\Lambda^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\Lambda^{2}}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\left(-ie\right)F_{\mu\nu},

(2.20)

where
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}=-\frac{1}{ie}\left[D_{\mu},D_{\nu}\right]
.
Although we have some terms of
w
that survive in the limit
\Lambda\to\infty
in the Taylor’s expansion (
2.19
) but they all vanish due to trace identities:
\text{tr}(\gamma_{5})=\text{tr}(\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu})=0
and
\text{tr}(\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma^{\beta})=-4\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
.
The only remaining term that survives after the regulator limit and taking the trace is

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\varphi_{m}^{\dagger}\left(x\right)\gamma_{5}\varphi_{m}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\Lambda^{4}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{\left(2\pi\right)^{4}}\text{tr}\left[\gamma_{5}\frac{1}{2!}\left(\frac{1}{2\Lambda^{2}}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\left(-ie\right)F_{\mu\nu}\right)^{2}f^{\prime\prime}\left(y\right)\right]

(2.21)

\displaystyle=-\frac{e^{2}}{8}\text{tr}\left[\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma^{\beta}\right]F_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{\left(2\pi\right)^{4}}f^{\prime\prime}\left(y\right),

(2.22)

which corresponds exactly to the usual singlet axial anomaly expression

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\varphi_{m}^{\dagger}\left(x\right)\gamma_{5}\varphi_{m}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta},

(2.23)

where we have made use of (obtained through integration by parts)

\int\frac{d^{4}q}{\left(2\pi\right)^{4}}f^{\prime\prime}\left(q^{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\int dq^{2}f^{\prime}\left(q^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}.

(2.24)

Accordingly, the Jacobian of the path integral measure (
2.10
) in
(3+1)
dimensions is given by

\pazocal{J}\left[\beta\right]=\exp\left(-\frac{ie^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\int d^{4}x~\beta\left(x\right)\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}\right).

(2.25)

Hence, we have shown that under the correct use of the Fujikawa’s regularization prescription, no VSR corrections are found for the axial anomaly in the path integral approach.

3
Axial anomaly in curved spacetime

As an additional analysis of the axial anomaly in VSR, we can extend our discussion to the case of chiral anomalies associated with the (background) gravitational field.
In regard to the formal development presented above to determine the functional Jacobian (
2.10
), the main change in the case of curved spacetime is the spacetime measure
d^{\omega}x\to\sqrt{-g}~d^{\omega}x

[
5
]
.

Now, about VSR in curved spacetime, there are some formal developments and applications found in the literature
[
31
,
32
]
, where the Lorentz violating effects are studied in terms of (spinor) auxiliary fields
(\lambda,\chi)

\pazocal{L}=i\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\pazocal{D}_{\mu}\psi+i\bar{\chi}(n.\pazocal{D})\lambda+i\bar{\lambda}(n.\pazocal{D})\chi+im\bar{\chi}(\gamma.n)\psi+im\bar{\lambda}\psi+h.c.

(3.1)

to avoid the presence of explicit non-local factors.
Furthermore, in this formulation
n^{\mu}
is a recurrent null vector field satisfying
\pazocal{D}_{\mu}n^{\nu}=0
.
However, the construction of VSR invariant in curved spacetime is not a closed matter, we shall consider as a matter of discussion the results of
[
31
]
and assume that the functional form of the fermionic minimal coupling is
i\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\psi
, with the VSR covariant derivative defined as
\nabla_{\mu}=\pazocal{D}_{\mu}-\frac{m^{2}n_{\mu}}{2\left(n.\pazocal{D}\right)}
, but now with new couplings
\pazocal{D}_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-ieA_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\mu}^{ab}\sigma_{ab}\equiv D_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\Omega_{\mu}^{ab}\sigma_{ab}
, where
\omega_{\mu}^{ab}
is the usual spin connection
[
31
]
.
Furthermore,
\sigma_{ab}
stands for the representation appropriate for the field that the derivative acts on, e.g. in the case of the usual components
\omega_{\mu}^{ab}
acting on a fermionic field, we have that
\sigma_{ab}=\frac{1}{4}[\gamma_{a},\gamma_{b}]
.

By simplicity, we shall consider a two-dimensional curved spacetime to develop our analysis.
Once again, the matrix structure of the regularized sum is very similar to (
2.26
). Thus, we shall focus only on the evaluation of the commutator
\left[\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}\right]
.
In this case, we have that

\displaystyle e^{-iq.x}\left[\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}\right]e^{iq.x}

\displaystyle=\left[\pazocal{D}_{\mu},\pazocal{D}_{\nu}\right]

(3.2)

\displaystyle-\frac{m^{2}n_{\nu}}{2}\left[\pazocal{D}_{\mu},\frac{1}{\left(n.\pazocal{D}+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\right]-\frac{m^{2}n_{\mu}}{2}\left[\frac{1}{\left(n.\pazocal{D}+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)},\pazocal{D}_{\nu}\right],

(3.3)

where the rescaling in the momentum
q\to\Lambda q
was performed.
We can make use of the comments 3. and 4. presented after eq. (
2
) to show that the last two terms of (
3.2
) vanish identically.
Because in the limit
\Lambda\to\infty
the nonlocal factor goes as
\lim\limits_{\Lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{\left(n.\pazocal{D}+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\sim\lim\limits_{\Lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{\left(i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}
,
i.e.

\lim\limits_{\Lambda\to\infty}\left[\pazocal{D}_{\mu},\frac{1}{\left(n.\pazocal{D}+i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\right]\sim\lim\limits_{\Lambda\to\infty}\left[\pazocal{D}_{\mu},\frac{1}{\left(i(n.q)\Lambda\right)}\right]=0.

(3.4)

We then find that

e^{-iq.x}\left[\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}\right]e^{iq.x}=\left[\pazocal{D}_{\mu},\pazocal{D}_{\nu}\right],

(3.5)

showing that, once again, no VSR contribution is present in the axial anomaly in the two-dimensional QED, even in a curved spacetime.
As a matter of fact, the chiral anomaly of the two-dimensional QED in curved spacetime is the same as in the flat spacetime
[
33
]
.

4
Final remarks

In this paper, we have analyzed the four-dimensional chiral anomaly for Dirac fermions in the context of the very special relativity using the path integral approach, where we have found no presence of VSR effects in the Jacobian functional.
Although some points were dealt in ref.
[
30
]
, we believe that the treatment in
[
30
]
is somehow limited
because it makes use of unnecessary conditions such as “light-cone gauge” (gauge-fixing), “fixed A” and “large q”.
Moreover, the main issue is the improper procedure in the regularization of the ill-defined sum in eq.(
2.10
).

On the other hand, our analysis is as general as it can be since
it holds nonperturbatively.
As we have shown above, the correct application of Fujikawa’s sum regularization procedure (in terms of the limit
\Lambda\to\infty
) is the key element to obtain the axial anomaly in the path integral formalism.
Mainly because it allows us to collect naturally the contributing terms in the Taylor’s series (
2.19
).

In addition, we have extended our discussion to the case of the two-dimensional chiral anomaly associated with the (background) gravitational field, showing again that no VSR effect is present.
In summary, as long as the VSR effects have the “usual” nonlocal form
\nabla_{\mu}=\pazocal{D}_{\mu}-\frac{m^{2}n_{\mu}}{2\left(n.\pazocal{D}\right)}
, independently of the functional form of the gauge connection (electromagnetic and gravitational part) of the covariant derivative
\pazocal{D}_{\mu}
, it can always be removed in the evaluation of the path integral Jacobian.

Since there is a close relation among the ABJ anomaly with the
\pi_{0}\to\gamma+\gamma
decay
[
2
,
3
]
, it is a natural extension to examine the VSR effects upon this type of anomalous processes and find how the VSR nonlocal effects contribute to its decay width.
This is currently under development and will be reported elsewhere.

Reduced Tate–Shafarevich group
Hayato Morimura

Abstract
. We prove a sort of reconstruction theorem for generic elliptic Calabi–Yau 3 3 -folds in the sense of Căldăraru. From our argument it follows that two generic elliptic Calabi–Yau 3 3 -folds are derived-equivalent linear over the base if and only if their generic fibers are derived-equivalent. As an application, we give affirmative answers to the conjectures raised in [ KSS ] . Namely, for each pair of elliptic Calabi–Yau 3 3 -folds in [ KSS , Table 19] we prove that they share the relative Jacobian and are ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} -linear derived-equivalent.



1.
Introduction

Among Calabi–Yau manifolds,
there are two classes of considerable interest for both
algebraic geometers
and
string theorists.
One consists of

Fourier–Mukai partners
,
pairs of nonbirational derived-equivalent Calabi–Yau
3
-folds.
The other consists of

elliptic Calabi–Yau manifolds
,
those which admit elliptic fibrations.
In this paper,
we provide new examples of derived-equivalent elliptic Calabi–Yau
3
-folds.
Our method can be applied to other suitable settings to find more examples.
In fact,
the construction

[
KSS
]

due to Knapp–Scheidegger–Schimannek is
a special case of orbital degeneracy loci

[
BFMT20
]

and
closed to that of determinantal varieties

[
JKLMR12
]
.
We will demonstrate how to apply our method to Inoue varieties in Section

7
.

First,
we prove the following reconstruction theorem for generic elliptic Calabi–Yau
3
-folds in the sense of Căldăraru.

Theorem 1.1

(Corollary
3.20
)
.

Let

f\colon X\to S,g\colon Y\to S

be generic elliptic Calabi–Yau
3
-folds.
If there exists an
S
-linear exact equivalence

\Phi\colon D^{b}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}(Y)
,
then

g

is an almost coprime twisted power of

f

in the sense of Definition

3.3
.

The opposite direction is

[
Căl02
, Theorem 6.1]
.
Nontrivial part is the identification of the relative Jacobians of

f,g
.
If one knew that

f,g

share the same relative Jacobian,
then the result would obtained
either by

[
AKW17
, Theorem 1.5]

or
careful reading of

[
Căl00
]
.
Different respectively from

[
AKW17
, Theorem 1.5]

and

[
Căl00
]
,
here

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X=3

and
the Calabi–Yau condition
are crucial to identify the relative Jacobians of

f,g
.

Presumably,
this is the best possible reconstruction result for generic elliptic Calabi–Yau
3
-folds.
Indeed,
if the smooth parts

f^{\circ}\colon X^{\circ}\to S^{\circ},g^{\circ}\colon Y^{\circ}\to S^{\circ}

are represented by

\alpha^{\circ},\beta^{\circ}\in\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J^{\circ})
,
for any analytic small resolution

\rho\colon\bar{J}\to J

we have the
S
-linear exact equivalences

\displaystyle D^{b}(X)\simeq D^{b}(\bar{J},\bar{\alpha}),\ D^{b}(Y)\simeq D^{b}(\bar{J},\bar{\beta})

from

[
Căl02
, Theorem 5.1]
,
where

\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta}\in\operatorname{Br}_{\text{an}}(\bar{J})

denote the Brauer classes canonically determined by

\alpha^{\circ},\beta^{\circ}
.
Moreover,
each

\rho_{i}\colon\bar{J}_{i}\to J_{i}

over

U_{i}

with only one node can be either of the two possible resolutions,
which is the source of the differences between

X,Y

and

\bar{J}
.
Hence the right hand sides should not recover

X\setminus X^{\circ},Y\setminus Y^{\circ}
.

As an application of
Theorem
1.1
,
in Section

4
,
we developed a deformation method to construct families of pairs of (derived-equivalent) elliptic Calabi–Yau
3
-folds
which are mutually almost coprime twisted powers.
Due to

[
Wil94
,
Wil98
]

and

[
Mor23
, Theorem 1.1]
,
the morphisms

f,g

in the above statement deform to families of derived-equivalent elliptic Calabi–Yau
3
-folds

\mathbf{f}\colon\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{S},\mathbf{g}\colon\mathbf{Y}\to\mathbf{S}

over a smooth affine
\mathbb{C}
-variety

\operatorname{Spec}B
.
We check that
the deformed Fourier–Mukai kernel is supported on

\mathbf{X}\times_{\mathbf{S}}\mathbf{Y}
.

Recently,
Knapp–Scheidegger–Schimannek constructed

12

pairs of Calabi–Yau
3
-folds
admitting elliptic fibrations over

with

-section

Although not smooth,

The idea was to consider fiberwise homological projective duality

]

for Grassmannian

of

\operatorname{Gr}(2,V^{\vee}_{5})

Let

(

By construction it is natural to ask

(
[
KSS
]
)
.

Type

Theorem 1.6
.

Let

f\colon X\to S,g\colon Y\to S

be generic elliptic Calabi–Yau
3
-folds.
Then there exists an
S
-linear exact equivalence

\Phi\colon D^{b}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}(Y)

if and only if

Combination with

Let

3

Then their generic fibers are derived-equivalent

3.3
.

Toward
Theorem
1.5

one needs to prove that

A_{i,\eta},B_{i,\eta}

of

share the Jacobian

J_{i,\eta}
.
In our setting,
this follows from

[
AKW17
, Lemma 2.4]
,
as the fiberwise homological projective duality induces the derived equivalence

\displaystyle D^{b}(A_{i,\eta})\simeq D^{b}(B_{i,\eta}).

Similarly,

,
which implies their being isomorphic

[
LT17
, Theorem 7.4]
.
Then one might seek to invoke instead

[
DG94
, Lemma 5.5]
.
Alternatively,
one would try to apply

[

,

However,

The former proof ended showing

to be twists of the geometric generic fiber

In general,

, Proposition 5.3]
.
Namely,
the

-isomorphism

The latter proof ended showing the relative Jacobians to be isomorphic to minimal Weierstrass models

which become isomorphic over the base

See also Remark

Realizing KSS varieties as two different geometric phases of non-abelian gauged linear sigma models,

(
[

]
)

For

]

(
[

]
)
.

The Tate–Shafarevich group

to prove the conjecture,

Notations and conventions
.

Throughout the paper,
all
\mathbb{C}
-varieties are integral separated scheme of finite type over

\mathbb{C}
.
Via Serre’s GAGA theorem
we sometimes go from the
algebraic
to
analytic
categories for proper
\mathbb{C}
-varieties.
For any morphism

f\colon X\to S

of smooth
\mathbb{C}
-varieties,
we consider the canonical
S
-linear structure on

D^{b}(X)\simeq\operatorname{perf}(X)

given by the action

\displaystyle\operatorname{perf}(X)\times\operatorname{perf}(S)\to\operatorname{perf}(X),\ (F,G)\mapsto F\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}f^{*}G.

Then for morphisms

f\colon X\to S,g\colon Y\to S

of smooth
\mathbb{C}
-varieties,
an exact functor

\Phi\colon D^{b}(X)\to D^{b}(Y)

is

-linear
if

\Phi

respects the
S
-linear structures,
i.e.,
we have functorial isomorphisms

\displaystyle\Phi(F\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}f^{*}G)\cong\Phi(F)\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{Y}}g^{*}G,\ F\in\operatorname{perf}(X),G\in\operatorname{perf}(S).

2.1.
The Weil–Châtelet group

Let

E

be an elliptic curve over a filed

K

of characteristic

0
.
The Weil–Châtelet group is a pointed set equipped with a group structure
which classifies torsors for

E
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
Sil08
, Section X.2]
)
.

The

isomorphism group

\operatorname{Isom}(E)

of

E

is the group of
\bar{K}
-isomorphism from

E

to

E
.
The

automorphism group

\operatorname{Aut}(E)

of

E

is the
G_{\bar{K}/K}
-invariant subgroup of

\operatorname{Isom}(E)

whose elements preserve the origin

O\in E
.
We use the same symbol

E

to denote the elliptic curve
and
its

translation group
,
the
G_{\bar{K}/K}
-invariant subgroup of

\operatorname{Isom}(E)

of translations

\tau_{p}

for points

p\in E
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
Sil08
, Proposition X5.1]
)
.

There is a bijection of pointed sets

\displaystyle E\times\operatorname{Aut}(E)\to\operatorname{Isom}(E),\ (p,\alpha)\mapsto\tau_{p}\circ\alpha,

identifying

\operatorname{Isom}(E)

with the product

E\times\operatorname{Aut}(E)

twisted by the natural action of

\operatorname{Aut}(E)

on

E
.

Definition 2.3

(
[
Sil08
, Section X.2]
)
.

A

twist

of

E

is a smooth
K
-curve

C

which is
\bar{K}
-isomorphic to

E
.
Two twists

C,C^{\prime}

are

equivalent

if they are
K
-isomorphic.
We denote by

\operatorname{Twist}(E/K)

the set of equivalence classes of twists of

E
.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
Sil08
, Theorem X2.2]
)
.

There is a canonical bijection of pointed sets

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(G_{\bar{K}/K},\operatorname{Isom}(E))\to\operatorname{Twist}(E/K).

Definition 2.5

(
[
Sil08
, Section X.3]
)
.

A

torsor

for

E

is a smooth
K
-curve

C

equipped with a simply transitive algebraic group
E
-action defined over

K
.
It is

trivial

if

C(K)\neq\emptyset
.
Two torsors

C,C^{\prime}

are

equivalent

if there is an
E
-equivariant
K
-isomorphism

\theta\colon C\to C^{\prime}
.
The

Weil–Châtelet group

\operatorname{WC}(E/K)

is the set of equivalence classes of torsors for

E
.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
Sil08
, Theorem X3.6]
)
.

There is a canonical bijection of pointed sets

\displaystyle\operatorname{WC}(E/K)\to H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(G_{\bar{K}/K},E).

In particular,
the image of

H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(G_{\bar{K}/K},E)

under the inclusion induced by

E\subset\operatorname{Isom}(E)

gives a natural group structure to

\operatorname{WC}(E/K)\subset\operatorname{Twist}(E/K)
.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
Sil08
, Proposition X5.3]
)
.

The inclusion

\operatorname{Aut}(E)\subset\operatorname{Isom}(E)

induces

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(G_{\bar{K}/K},\operatorname{Aut}(E))\subset H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(G_{\bar{K}/K},\operatorname{Isom}(E)).

Let

\operatorname{Twist}((E,O)/K)

be the image of

H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(G_{\bar{K}/K},\operatorname{Aut}(E))

regarded as a subset of

\operatorname{Twist}(E/K)
.
If

C\in\operatorname{Twist}((E,O)/K)

then

C(K)\neq\emptyset
.
Conversely,
if

E^{\prime}

is an elliptic curve over

K

which is
\bar{K}
-isomorphic to

E
,
then

E^{\prime}

represents an element of

\operatorname{Twist}((E,O)/K)
.

Remark 2.8
.

In general,

C\in\operatorname{Twist}((E,O)/K)

is not
K
-isomorphic to

E
.
By

[
Sil08
, Proposition 5.4]

the group

\operatorname{Twist}((E,O)/K)

is canonically isomorphic to

K^{*}/(K^{*})^{n}

where

n

becomes equal to

2,4

or

6

depending on the
j
-invariant of

E
.
Hence the elements of

K^{*}/(K^{*})^{n}

correspond to the twists of

E

which do not come from torsors.

2.2.
The Tate–Shafarevich group

Let

S

be a normal integral excellent scheme.
We denote by

\eta=\operatorname{Spec}k(S)

its generic point.
Let

E

be an elliptic curve over

k(S)
.
Assume that
the characteristic of

k(S)

is

0
.
In good cases,
the Tate–Shafarevich group
can be described as a certain quotient of Brauer groups
and
classifies smooth elliptic fibrations.

Definition 2.9

(
[
DG94
, Section 1]
)
.

The

Tate–Shafarevich group

\Sha_{S}(E)

associated with

S

and

E

is the subset

\bigcap_{s\in S}\operatorname{Ker}(loc_{\bar{s}})\subset\operatorname{WC}(J_{\eta}/k(S))

for natural specialization maps

\displaystyle loc_{\bar{s}}\colon\operatorname{WC}(E/k(S))\to\operatorname{WC}(E(\bar{s})/k(\bar{s})),\ C\mapsto C(\bar{s})=C\times_{k(s)}k(\bar{s}).

There is a standard cohomological interpretation of

\Sha_{S}(E)
.
Consider the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\iota_{*}E)\to H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\eta,E)\to H^{0}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,R^{1}\iota_{*}E)\to H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\iota_{*}E)\to H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\eta,E)

where

\iota\colon\operatorname{Spec}k(S)\to S

denotes the canonical morphism.
For any

s\in S

we have

(R^{1}\iota_{*}E)_{\bar{s}}\cong H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\eta_{\bar{s}},E(\bar{s}))

and
the natural homomorphism

\displaystyle H^{0}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,R^{1}\iota_{*}E)\to\prod_{s\in S}(R^{1}\iota_{*}E)_{\bar{s}}

is injective.
Since the composition

H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\eta,E)\to H^{0}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,R^{1}\iota_{*}E)\to(R^{1}\iota_{*}E)_{\bar{s}}

coincides with

loc_{\bar{s}}
,
one obtains an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\iota_{*}E)\to\operatorname{WC}(E/k(S))\to\prod_{s\in S}\operatorname{WC}(E(\bar{s})/k(\bar{s}))

and

\Sha_{S}(E)=H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\iota_{*}E)
.

Remark 2.10
.

An element

C\in\operatorname{WC}(E/k(S))

maps to

0

in

\operatorname{WC}(E(\bar{s})/k(\bar{s}))

if and only if
there exists an irreducible étale neighborhood

U\to S

of

s

such that

C\times_{k(S)}k(U)

has a rational point over

k(U)
.
Indeed,
if the image of

C

in

(R^{1}\iota_{*}E)_{\bar{s}}

is

0
,
then there exists an irreducible étale neighborhood

U\to S

of

s

such that
the image of

C

in

H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(k(U),E\times_{k(S)}k(U))

is

0
.
Hence

\Sha_{S}(E)

consists of equivalence classes of étale locally trivial torsors.

In good cases,
there is another cohomological interpretation of

\Sha_{S}(E)
.
Let

X

be a scheme.

Definition 2.11

(
[
Căl00
, Definition 1.1.7]
)
.

The

cohomological Brauer group

\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(X)

of

X

is defined as

H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,\mathscr{O}^{*}_{X})_{tors}
.
The

Brauer group

\operatorname{Br}(X)

of

X

is the group of isomorphism classes of Azumaya algebras on

X

modulo equivalence relation.
Here,
the group structure is given by tensor products.
Two Azumaya algebras

\mathscr{A},\mathscr{A}^{\prime}

on

X

are

equivalent

if there exists a locally free sheaves

\mathscr{E},\mathscr{E}^{\prime}

it is easy to reconstruct generic elliptic
3
-folds in a suitable sense.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

f\colon X\to S,g\colon Y\to S

be flat locally projective morphisms of
\mathbb{C}
-varieties.
Then any
S
-linear exact equivalence

\Phi\colon D^{b}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}(Y)

induces a
k(S)
-linear exact equivalence

\Phi_{k(S)}\colon D^{b}(X_{\eta})\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}(Y_{\eta})

on the generic fibers

X_{\eta},Y_{\eta}
.

Proof.

By

[
HLS09
, Proposition 2.15]

we may assume that

f,g

are smooth over

S=\operatorname{Spec}R
.
Then one can apply

[
Mora
, Theorem 1.1]

to obtain
k(S)
-linear exact equivalences

\displaystyle D^{b}(X_{\eta})\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}(X)/D^{b}_{0}(X),\ D^{b}(Y_{\eta})\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}(Y)/D^{b}_{0}(Y).

to the Verdier quotients by the full
S
-linear triangulated subcategories spanned by complexes with coherent
R
-torsion cohomology.
Since

\Phi

is
S
-linear,
universality of Verdier quotients induces the desired equivalence.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let

f\colon X\to S,g\colon Y\to S

be generic elliptic
3
-folds.
If there exists an
S
-linear exact equivalence

\Phi\colon D^{b}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}(Y)
,
then their generic fibers

X_{\eta},Y_{\eta}

share the Jacobian

J_{\eta}
.

Proof.

The generic fibers

X_{\eta},Y_{\eta}

are derived-equivalent by
Lemma
3.7
.
Now,
the claim follows from

[
AKW17
, Lemma 2.4]
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let

f\colon X\to S,g\colon Y\to S

be generic elliptic
3
-folds.
Assume that

S

is proper over

\mathbb{C}
.
If there exists an
S
-linear exact equivalence

\Phi\colon D^{b}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}(Y)
,
then over any closed point

s\in S

the fibers

X_{s},Y_{s}

are isomorphic.
In particular,
the discriminant loci

\Delta_{f},\Delta_{g}

coincide.

Proof.

Since

X,Y

are smooth proper over

\mathbb{C}
,
the equivalence

\Phi

is of Fourier–Mukai type by

[
Ola
]
.
Then
its
S
-linearity
and

[
HLS09
, Proposition 2.15]

implies the derived equivalence of

X_{s},Y_{s}
.
Now,
the claim follows from
Lemma
3.2

and

[
LT17
, Theorem 7.4(2)]
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let

f\colon X\to S,g\colon Y\to S

be smooth elliptic
3
-folds with relative Jacobian

\pi\colon J\to S
.
If there exists an
S
-linear exact equivalence

\Phi\colon D^{b}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}(Y)
,
then

g

is a coprime twisted power of

f
.

Proof.

Let

\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J)

be representatives of

\Sha_{S}(J_{\eta})\cong\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J)/\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(S)

for

f,g
.
There is an étale cover

\{U_{i}\}

of

S

such that
to each

U_{i}

the base changes

f_{i},g_{i}

admit sections

\displaystyle\sigma_{X,i}\colon U_{i}\to X_{i}=X\times_{S}U_{i},\ \sigma_{Y,i}\colon U_{i}\to Y_{i}=Y\times_{S}U_{i}

and

\alpha,\beta

give the gluing data for

\{J_{i}\}

to yield

X,Y

respectively.
The injection

\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J)\to\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J_{\eta})

induced by the base change

J_{\eta}\to J

to

k(S)

sends

\alpha,\beta

to

\alpha_{\eta},\beta_{\eta}\in\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J_{\eta})
.
Then the étale cover

\{U_{i}\times_{S}k(S)\}

of

\operatorname{Spec}k(S)

represents both

\alpha_{\eta}

and

\beta_{\eta}
.

Since by
assumption
and
Lemma
3.7

the generic fibers

X_{\eta},Y_{\eta}

are derived-equivalent,
one can apply

[
AKW17
, Lemma 2.4, Theorem 2.5]

to see that

Y_{\eta}

is isomorphic to the moduli space of degree

k

line bundles on

X_{\eta}

for some

k\in\mathbb{Z}

coprime to the order

\operatorname{ord}([\alpha])
.
Hence both

\alpha^{k}_{\eta}

and

\beta_{\eta}

yield

g_{\eta}

via gluing the étale cover

\{J_{i}\times_{U_{i}}k(U_{i})\}

of

J_{\eta}
.
Since the map

\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J)\to\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J_{\eta})

is injective,
both

\alpha^{k}

and

\beta

yield

g

via gluing the étale cover

\{J_{i}\}

of

J
.
By
Theorem
2.19

the Brauer class

\alpha^{k}

represents

f^{k}
.
Hence

g

is isomorphic to

f^{k}
.
∎

Lemma 3.12
.

Let

be generic elliptic
3
-folds with relative Jacobian

.
If there exists an

-linear exact equivalence

\Phi\colon D^{b}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}(Y)
,
then

is an almost coprime twisted power of

Let

be representatives of

for the smooth parts

f^{\circ},g^{\circ}

there exist unique extensions

\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Br}(J)

of

\alpha^{\circ},\beta^{\circ}

\operatorname{Br}(J)\to\operatorname{Br}(\bar{J})

given by the pullback along any analytic small resolution

of singularities.

\{U_{i}\}

of

\bar{\alpha}

and

\bar{\beta}

are isomorphic as an analytic space over

3.4
.

[

, Proposition 2.15]

we have the induced
S^{\circ}
-linear exact equivalence

.
One can apply
Lemma
3.10

to find some

k\in\mathbb{Z}

coprime to

\operatorname{ord}([\alpha^{\circ}])

such that

is isomorphic to

f^{\circ k}
.
As explained in

[
Căl00
, Section 6.6]
,
the base changes

X^{k}_{i}

is
U_{i}
-isomorphic to

X_{i}

as an analytic space.

3.3.
Calabi–Yau case

The triviality of the canonical bundle allows us to detect common relative Jacobians via derived equivalences linear over the base.

Remark 3.14
.

If

X

is Calabi–Yau,
then

S

must be either a
rational
or
Enriques
surface by

[
Gro94
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let

f\colon X\to S

be a generic elliptic Calabi–Yau
3
-fold.
Then the restriction

f^{\circ}_{*}\omega_{X^{\circ}/S^{\circ}}=f_{*}\omega_{X/S}|_{S^{\circ}}

of

f_{*}\omega_{X/S}

to

S^{\circ}=S\setminus\Delta_{f}

is nontrivial.

Proof.

Otherwise,
by
Lemma
2.26

the restriction

\pi^{\circ}\colon J^{\circ}\to S^{\circ}

must coincide with the Weierstrass fibration associated with

\mathscr{O}_{S^{\circ}}
.
Then we would obtain

J^{\circ}=\mathbb{P}^{2}\times E

for some elliptic curve over

\mathbb{C}
.
By Künneth formula
we have

\displaystyle H^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\times E,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}\times E})=0,\ H^{3}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\times E,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

Since

\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J^{\circ})

is torsion,
we would obtain

\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J^{\circ})=0
.
By the standard purity theorem for the cohomological Brauer group,
we would obtain

\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J)=0
.
Let

J^{st}

be the stable part of

J
,
the complement of the singularities,
which by definition is isomorphic to

\bar{J}^{st}=\rho^{-1}(J^{st})
.
Let

X^{st}

be the complement in

X

of the type

I_{2}

fibers.
Then

[
Căl00
, Theorem 3.3.4]

and
the proof of

[
Căl00
, Theorem 6.4.3]

imply that
there would exist
a universal sheaf

\mathscr{U}^{st}

on

X^{st}\times_{S}X^{st}

and
an isomorphism

X^{st}\to J^{st}
.
Recall that
the section

\sigma_{J}\colon S\to J

of

\pi

factorizes through

J^{st}
.
Hence

f

would admit a section,
which contradicts to condition

(3)

in Definition

3.1
.
∎

Lemma 3.16
.

Let

f\colon X\to S

be a generic elliptic Calabi–Yau
3
-fold.
Then there exists an affine open cover

\{U_{i}\}^{3}_{i=1}

of

such that
the restrictions

\mathscr{V}|_{U_{i}}

are trivial.

Proof.

Recall from Remark

3.14

that

S

must be either a
rational
or
Enriques

When

is rational,

[
CSSV23

there exists an affine open cover

\{U_{i}\}^{3}_{i=1}

of

Lemma 3.17
.

Let

f\colon X\to S

be a generic elliptic Calabi–Yau
3
-fold.
Then the relative dualizing sheaf

\omega_{X/S}

is an invertible

Proof.

Since

is flat

by

[

the relative dualizing sheaf

is an invertible
\mathscr{O}_{X}

For any point

Theorem 3.18
.

Let

f\colon X\to S,g\colon Y\to S

be generic elliptic Calabi–Yau
3
-folds.
If there exists an
S
-linear exact equivalence

\Phi\colon D^{b}(X)\to D^{b}(Y)
,
then

f,g

share the relative Jacobian

\pi\colon J\to S
.

Proof.

Let

\pi_{X}\colon J_{X}\to S,\pi_{Y}\colon J_{Y}\to S

be the relative Jacobians of

f,g
.
Take any analytic small resolutions of their singularities

\rho_{X}\colon\bar{J}_{X}\to J_{X},\rho_{Y}\colon\bar{J}_{Y}\to J_{Y}
.
Recall that
the only singularities of

J_{X},J_{Y}

are isolated ordinary double points.
Their complements

J^{st}_{X},J^{st}_{Y}

are called the stable parts of

J_{X},J_{Y}

and
we have

\displaystyle\bar{J}^{st}_{X}=\rho^{-1}_{X}(J^{st}_{X})\cong J^{st}_{X},\ \bar{J}^{st}_{Y}=\rho^{-1}_{Y}(J^{st}_{Y})\cong J^{st}_{Y}.

Since the sections

\sigma_{J_{X}}\colon S\to J_{X},\sigma_{J_{Y}}\colon S\to J_{Y}

of

\pi_{X},\pi_{Y}

factorizes through

J^{st}_{X},J^{st}_{Y}
,
they canonically extend to that

\displaystyle\bar{\sigma}_{X}\colon S\to\bar{J}_{X},\ \bar{\sigma}_{Y}\colon S\to\bar{J}_{Y}

of

\bar{\pi}_{X}=\pi_{X}\circ\rho_{X},\bar{\pi}_{Y}=\pi_{Y}\circ\rho_{Y}
.

Since by Remark

3.22

below we have

\omega_{\bar{J}_{X}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{\bar{J}_{X}},\omega_{\bar{J}_{Y}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{\bar{J}_{Y}}
,
the compositions

\bar{\pi}_{X},\bar{\pi}_{Y}

are relatively minimal in the sense of Definition

2.29
.
Applying
Lemma
2.26
,
we obtain birational morphisms

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{X}\to W(\mathscr{L},a,b),\ \bar{J}_{Y}\to W(\mathscr{M},c,d)

to some Weierstrass fibrations,
which are the contractions of all components of fibers not intersecting

\bar{\sigma}_{X}(S),\bar{\sigma}_{Y}(S)
.
From the description of

\rho_{X},\rho_{Y}

it follows

\displaystyle W(\mathscr{L},a,b)=J_{X},\ W(\mathscr{M},c,d)=J_{Y}.

As we have

\displaystyle\bar{\pi}_{X*}\omega_{\bar{J}_{X}/S}|_{S^{\circ}}\cong f^{\circ}_{*}\omega_{X^{\circ}/S^{\circ}}\cong\omega_{S^{\circ}}^{-1}\cong g^{\circ}_{*}\omega_{Y^{\circ}/S^{\circ}}\cong\bar{\pi}_{Y*}\omega_{\bar{J}_{Y}/S}|_{S^{\circ}},

the same argument as the proof of
Lemma
3.17

shows

\displaystyle\bar{\pi}_{X*}\omega_{\bar{J}_{X}/S}\cong\omega_{S}^{-1}\cong\bar{\pi}_{Y*}\omega_{\bar{J}_{Y}/S}.

Then by
Lemma
2.26

we obtain

\displaystyle J_{X}=W(\omega_{S}^{-1},a,b),\ J_{Y}=W(\omega_{S}^{-1},c,d).

One can apply
Lemma
2.30

to see that

\displaystyle\pi_{X}\colon W(\omega_{S}^{-1},a,b)\to S,\ \pi_{Y}\colon W(\omega_{S}^{-1},c,d)\to S

are minimal in the sense of Definition

2.27
.
From

[
DG94
, Proposition 2.7]

we obtain an
S
-isomorphism

\displaystyle J_{X}=W(\omega_{S}^{-1},a,b)\cong W(\omega_{S}^{-1},c,d)=J_{Y},

as by Corollary

3.8

the generic fibers

J_{X,\eta},J_{Y,\eta}

of

\pi_{X},\pi_{Y}

are isomorphic.
Indeed,
by Remark

2.24

and

[
DG94
, Proposition 2.7]

the restrictions

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}W(\omega_{S}^{-1},a,b)|_{\pi_{X}(J^{st}_{X})\cup\pi_{Y}(J^{st}_{Y})}\cong W(\omega_{S}^{-1}|_{\pi_{X}(J^{st}_{X})\cup\pi_{Y}(J^{st}_{Y})},a|_{\pi_{X}(J^{st}_{X})\cup\pi_{Y}(J^{st}_{Y})},b|_{\pi_{X}(J^{st}_{X})\cup\pi_{Y}(J^{st}_{Y})}),\\
W(\omega_{S}^{-1},c,d)|_{\pi_{X}(J^{st}_{X})\cup\pi_{Y}(J^{st}_{Y})}\cong W(\omega_{S}^{-1}|_{\pi_{X}(J^{st}_{X})\cup\pi_{Y}(J^{st}_{Y})},c|_{\pi_{X}(J^{st}_{X})\cup\pi_{Y}(J^{st}_{Y})},d|_{\pi_{X}(J^{st}_{X})\cup\pi_{Y}(J^{st}_{Y})})\end{gathered}

Lemma 5.4
.

\displaystyle\operatorname{tot}(F^{\vee}_{i})_{x}\cong V_{5},\ \operatorname{tot}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{G}_{i}/\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1))_{x}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\mathbf{G}_{i}}\operatorname{tot}(E^{\prime\vee}_{i})^{\perp}_{x}\cong\operatorname{tot}(E^{\prime\vee}_{i})^{\perp}_{x},

as in

[

, Section 2.3]

that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gr}(2,V^{\vee}_{5})\times_{\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{2}V^{\vee}_{5})}\mathbb{P}(\operatorname{tot}(E^{\prime\vee}_{i})_{x}).

5.4.
Common relative Jacobian

Here is a sufficient condition
which works in our setting.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let

f\colon X\to S,g\colon Y\to S

be flat elliptic
3
-folds over smooth
\mathbb{C}
-variety

S

without multiple fibers.
Assume that
all reducible fibers of

f

are of type

I_{2}
.
Assume further that the following conditions hold:

(1)

The generic fibers of

f,g

share the Jacobian

J_{\eta}
.

(2)

There exist possibly analytic small resolutions of singularities

\displaystyle\rho_{X}\colon\bar{J}_{X}\to J_{X},\ \rho_{Y}\colon\bar{J}_{Y}\to J_{Y}

such that

\bar{\pi}_{X}=\pi_{X}\circ\rho_{X},\bar{\pi}_{Y}=\pi_{Y}\circ\rho_{Y}

give relatively minimal elliptic fibrations
and

\bar{\pi}_{X*}\omega_{\bar{J}_{X}/S},\bar{\pi}_{Y*}\omega_{\bar{J}_{Y}/S}

are isomorphic invertible sheaves.

Then

f,g

share the relative Jacobian

\pi\colon J\to S
.

Proof.

We run the same argument as the proof of
Theorem
3.18
.
The relatively minimal elliptic fibrations

\bar{\pi}_{X},\bar{\pi}_{Y}

of smooth
\mathbb{C}
-varieties admit sections,
as the sections

\sigma_{J_{X}},\sigma_{J_{Y}}

of

\pi_{X},\pi_{Y}

factorize through the complements

J^{st}_{X},J^{st}_{Y}

in

J_{X},J_{Y}

of the singular loci.
Since

\rho_{X},\rho_{Y}

are small resolutions,
the singular loci consist of finitely many points
whose fibers are unions of curves.
By
Lemma
2.26
,
Lemma
2.30

and
condition
(2)
there exist birational morphisms

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{X}\to W(\bar{\pi}_{X*}\omega_{\bar{J}_{X}/S},a,b),\ \bar{J}_{Y}\to W(\bar{\pi}_{Y*}\omega_{\bar{J}_{Y}/S},c,d)

to minimal Weierstrass fibrations,
which are the contractions of all components of fibers not intersecting

\bar{\sigma}_{X}(S),\bar{\sigma}_{Y}(S)
.
Note that
by assumption such component over each singular point is a copy of

\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Hence we obtain

\displaystyle J_{X}=W(\bar{\pi}_{X*}\omega_{\bar{J}_{X}/S},a,b),\ J_{Y}=W(\bar{\pi}_{Y*}\omega_{\bar{J}_{Y}/S},c,d).

Using the same extension technique as in the proof of
Theorem
3.18
,
from conditions
(1), (2)
and

[
DG94
, Proposition 2.7]

we obtain an
S
-isomorphism

J_{X}\cong J_{Y}
.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

When

\rho_{X},\rho_{Y}

contract all components of fibers not intersecting

.

Remark 5.9
.

In earlier version of this paper,

(1)
required only very general fibers to be isomorphic.
However,

cannot be true.
Consider any elliptic fibration

.
Suppose that

admits a nontrivial double covering

.
Let

be the quotient of

by

,
where the action is given by

and

The generic fiber of

so called

is not isomorphic to that of

On the other hand,

.

The elliptic fibrations

share the relative Jacobian

∎

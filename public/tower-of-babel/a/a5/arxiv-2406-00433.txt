Periodic Waves for the Regularized Camassa-Holm Equation: Existence and Spectral Stability
Fábio Natali

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the existence and spectral stability of periodic traveling wave solutions for the regularized Camassa-Holm equation. To establish the existence of periodic waves, we employ tools from bifurcation theory to construct solutions with the zero-mean property. We also prove that such waves may not exist for the well-known Camassa-Holm equation. Regarding spectral stability, we analyze the difference between the number of negative eigenvalues of the second variation of the Lyapunov functional at the wave, restricted to the space of zero-mean periodic functions, and the number of negative eigenvalues of the matrix formed from the tangent space associated with the low-order conserved quantities of the evolution model. Finally, we address the problem of orbital stability as a consequence of the spectral stability.

Lemma 2.4

Let

{\raisebox{1.02228pt}{$H$}\left/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{$K$}\right.}\cong K^{\bot},

Proof.

Let us define

Lemma 3.1

∎

The proofs rely on Theorem 3.1 in

Let

Proposition 3.4

Proposition 3.5
.

Let

be fixed. Consider the linearized operator

\langle\mathcal{L}_{\Pi}\phi,\phi\rangle=-c\int_{0}^{2\pi}\phi^{\prime 2}dx-c\int_{0}^{2\pi}\phi^{2}dx+\omega\int_{0}^{2\pi}\phi^{2}dx.

(3.9)

By applying the Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality to the first term on the right-hand side of equality

\langle\mathcal{L}_{\Pi}\phi,\phi\rangle\leq(-2c+\omega)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\phi^{2}dx.

Next, since

\int_{0}^{2\pi}hdx=\frac{2}{c-\phi(0)}\left[-\phi(0)\int_{0}^{2\pi}y_{1}dx+\int_{0}^{2\pi}y_{1}\phi dx\right].

(3.14)

The next step is to obtain convenient expressions for the integrals
\int_{0}^{2\pi}y_{1}(x)dx
and
\int_{0}^{2\pi}y_{1}(x)\phi(x)dx
. Indeed, multiplying the equation
\mathcal{L}h=1
by
y_{1}
, integrating over the interval
[0,2\pi]
, performing two integration by parts, and using the fact that
h
is even and periodic, we deduce from
(\ref{varpar1})
that

\int_{0}^{2\pi}y_{1}dx=(c-\phi(0))h(0)y_{1}^{\prime}(2\pi)=0.

(3.15)

Again, since
\mathcal{L}:[\phi^{\prime}]^{\bot}\rightarrow[\phi^{\prime}]^{\bot}
and
\phi
is periodic and even, it follows that there exists
\chi\in H_{\rm per,e}^{2}
, such that
\mathcal{L}\chi=\phi
. By equation
(\ref{CHode})
, we have

\mathcal{L}\phi=c(\phi^{\prime\prime}-\phi)+\omega\phi-2A.

(3.16)

Using that
\mathcal{L}h=1
and

\mathcal{L}1=(c-\omega)-3\phi+\phi^{\prime\prime},

(3.17)

we conclude that
\chi
can be expressed by

\chi(x)=-\frac{c}{2c+\omega}+\frac{1}{2c+\omega}\phi(x)+\frac{c(c-\omega)+2A}{2c+\omega}h(x).

(3.18)

Thus, multiplying the equation
\mathcal{L}\chi=\phi
by
y_{1}
, integrating over the interval
[0,2\pi]
, performing two integration by parts, and using the fact that
\chi
is even and periodic, we deduce from
(\ref{varpar1})
and
(\ref{chi})
that

\int_{0}^{2\pi}y_{1}\phi dx=(c-\phi(0))\chi(0)y_{1}^{\prime}(2\pi)=-\frac{(c-\phi(0))^{2}}{2c+\omega}y_{1}^{\prime}(2\pi).

(3.19)

Combining the information from
(\ref{varpar2})
,
(\ref{varpar3})
, and
(\ref{varpar4})
, and using the fact that
c-\phi(0)>0
, we conclude that

\int_{0}^{2\pi}hdx=-2y_{1}^{\prime}(2\pi)\frac{c-\phi(0)}{2c+\omega}.

(3.20)

We need to calculate
y_{1}^{\prime}(2\pi)
. In fact, first we need to use
(\ref{rel-varphi})
and the fact that
\varphi_{1}(x)=\left(\frac{c-\phi(0)}{c-\phi(x)}\right)^{-1/2}y_{1}(x)
, where
\varphi_{1}
is not periodic. Together with the
2\pi-
periodic function
\varphi_{2}(x)=\left(\frac{c-\phi(0)}{c-\phi(x)}\right)^{-1/2}\phi^{\prime}(x)
, we have the fundamental set
\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\}
of solutions of the equation
\mathcal{M}_{\tau}w=0
. Thus, we obtain by
(\ref{relpq1})
and the explicit expressions of
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
that

y_{1}^{\prime}(2\pi)=\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(2\pi)=\theta\varphi_{2}^{\prime}(0)=\theta\phi^{\prime\prime}(0).

(3.21)

Since
\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)<0
and
\theta<0
, we obtain from
(\ref{dery1})
, that
y_{1}^{\prime}(2\pi)>0
. From
(\ref{varpar5})
and the fact that
c-\phi(0)>0
, it follows that
\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}1,1\rangle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}hdx<0
. By Remark
2.2
, this implies that

n(\mathcal{L}_{\Pi})=n(\mathcal{L})-n_{0}-z_{0}=2-1-0=1,

(3.22)

and

(3.23)

The proposition is thus proved.

4.
Spectral stability of periodic waves - Proof of Theorem
\ref{theorem-stability}-(iii)

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
c>\frac{\omega}{2}
be fixed. If
\frac{d}{dc}E(\phi)>0
, then the periodic wave
\phi
is spectrally stable in the sense of Definition
1.2
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\frac{d}{dc}E(\phi)>0
. By Proposition
3.5
, we have
n(\mathcal{L}_{\Pi})=z(\mathcal{L}_{\Pi})=1
. Hence, since
\mathcal{L}_{\Pi}=\mathcal{L}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\langle\phi^{\prime},\partial_{x}\cdot\rangle+\frac{3}{2\pi}\langle\phi,\cdot\rangle,
we obtain by applying
[
1
, Proposition 3.8]
with
Q(u)=E(u)
and
Q^{\prime}(u)=u-u^{\prime\prime}
, that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\langle\mathcal{L}v,v\rangle=\left\langle\mathcal{L}v+\frac{1}{2\pi}\langle\phi^{\prime},\partial_{x}v\rangle+\frac{3}{2\pi}\langle\phi,v\rangle,v\right\rangle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{\Pi}v,v\rangle\geq C||v||_{L_{{\rm per}}^{2}}^{2},

(4.1)

for all
v\in H_{\rm per,m}^{2}
such that
\langle v,\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}\rangle=0
and
\langle v,\phi^{\prime}\rangle=0
. Therefore, one has, since
z(\mathcal{L}_{\Pi})=1
, that

\langle\mathcal{L}v,v\rangle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{\Pi}v,v\rangle\geq 0,

(4.2)

for all
v\in H_{\rm per,m}^{2}
such that
\langle v,\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}\rangle=0
. The inequality in
(\ref{coerc2})
establishes that
\mathcal{L}_{|_{\{{1,\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}}\}^{\bot}}}\geq 0
, as desired, and the first part of Theorem
1.5
-(iii) is therefore proved for all possible cases of
d_{c}
, that is,
d_{c}\neq 0
and
d_{c}=0
.

∎

A sufficient condition for ensuring that
\frac{d}{dc}E(\phi)>0
is established in the next result.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
c>\frac{\omega}{2}
be fixed. If
d_{c}\neq 0
and
\frac{dA}{dc}>0
, then the wave
\phi
is spectrally stable in the sense of Definition
1.2
. In particular, we have that
\frac{d}{dc}E(\phi)>0
.

Proof.

Since
d_{c}\neq 0
, we have that
\ker(\mathcal{L})=[\phi^{\prime}]
. The symmetric matrix
\mathcal{A}(0)
given by (
2.1
) is given by

\mathcal{A}(0)=\left[\begin{array}[]{llll}\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}),\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}\rangle&\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}),1\rangle\\
\\
\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}),1\rangle&\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}1,1\rangle\end{array}\right].

(4.3)

To clarify for the reader, in Proposition
2.1
we have, in this context,
z_{0}=\dim(\ker(\mathcal{A}(0)))
and
n_{0}=n(\mathcal{A}(0))
. We prove our result for the case
d_{c}\neq 0
. Again, since
\mathcal{L}:[\phi^{\prime}]^{\bot}\rightarrow[\phi^{\prime}]^{\bot}
, there exists
\Phi\in H_{\rm per,e}^{2}
, such that
\mathcal{L}\Phi=-\phi^{\prime\prime}
. Since
\mathcal{L}h=1
, we see by
(\ref{6L})
that
\int_{0}^{2\pi}hdx=\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}1,1\rangle=\frac{2\pi\omega}{d_{c}}\neq 0
. Thus, by
(\ref{2L})
and
(\ref{3L})
, we obtain that
\Phi
is given by

\Phi(x)=\frac{c-\omega}{2c+\omega}-\frac{3\phi}{2c+\omega}-\frac{(c-\omega)^{2}+6A}{2c+\omega}h.

(4.4)

Thus, we obtain by
(\ref{chi})
and
(\ref{Phi})

\chi+\Phi=\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime})=-\frac{\omega}{2c+\omega}-\frac{2\phi}{2c+\omega}+\frac{c\omega-\omega^{2}-4A}{2c+\omega}\mathcal{L}^{-1}1,

(4.5)

so that

\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}),1\rangle=-\frac{\omega}{2c+\omega}+\frac{c\omega-\omega^{2}-4A}{2c+\omega}\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}1,1\rangle.

(4.6)

Gathering the results in
(\ref{Lphiphi2})
and
(\ref{Lphi1})
, we obtain from
(\ref{matrixD})

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\det\mathcal{A}(0)&=&\displaystyle-\frac{2}{2c+\omega}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\phi^{2}+\phi^{\prime 2})dx\int_{0}^{2\pi}hdx\\
\\
&+&\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\omega}{2c+\omega}\left[-\frac{2\pi\omega}{2c+\omega}+\frac{c\omega-\omega^{2}-4A}{2c+\omega}\int_{0}^{2\pi}hdx\right].\end{array}

(4.7)

We need to determine the sign of the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.7
), namely the sign of

\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\chi+\Phi)dx=-\frac{2\pi\omega}{2c+\omega}+\frac{c\omega-\omega^{2}-4A}{2c+\omega}\int_{0}^{2\pi}hdx.

(4.8)

By Remark
3.6
, the mapping
c\in\left(\frac{\omega}{2},+\infty\right)\mapsto\phi\in H_{\rm per,m,e}^{\infty},
is smooth. Consequently, by
(\ref{1L})
and
(\ref{Lphiphi2})
, it follows that

\mathcal{L}\left(\chi+\Phi+\frac{d\phi}{dc}\right)=-\frac{dA}{dc}.

(4.9)

From
(\ref{chiphiderA1})
and using the fact that
\frac{dA}{dc}>0
, we then obtain

\chi+\Phi+\frac{d\phi}{dc}=-\frac{dA}{dc}\mathcal{L}^{-1}1.

(4.10)

Integrating the result in
(\ref{chiphiderA})
over the interval
[0,2\pi]
, and using the facts that
\frac{dA}{dc}>0
and
\int_{0}^{2\pi}hdx\neq 0
, we obtain that

\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\chi+\Phi)dx=-\frac{dA}{dc}\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}1,1\rangle=-\frac{dA}{dc}\int_{0}^{2\pi}hdx\neq 0.

(4.11)

On the other hand, since we have
\frac{dA}{dc}>0
for all
c>\frac{\omega}{2}
, it follows from
(\ref{detD})
and by assuming
d_{c}<0
, that
\det\mathcal{A}(0)>0
. Again, since
\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}1,1\rangle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}hdx<0
, we have
z_{0}=0
and
n_{0}=2
. By Proposition
2.1
, we obtain

n\left(\mathcal{L}_{|_{\{1,\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}\}^{\bot}}}\right)=n(\mathcal{L})-z_{0}-n_{0}=2-0-2=0.

Now, if
d_{c}>0
, we conclude
\int_{0}^{2\pi}hdx>0
. Since
\frac{dA}{dc}>0
, it follows from
(\ref{intchiphiderA})
that
\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\chi+\Phi)dx<0
. Therefore, we obtain by
(\ref{detD})
that
\det\mathcal{A}(0)<0
. By Proposition
2.1
, we obtain

n\left(\mathcal{L}_{|_{\{1,\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}\}^{\bot}}}\right)=n(\mathcal{L})-z_{0}-n_{0}=1-0-1=0.

The spectral stability is now complete for the case
d_{c}\neq 0
and
\frac{dA}{dc}>0
.

To complete the proof of the proposition, we need to highlight some additional facts. In fact, by Remark
3.6
, we have that the mapping
c\in\left(\frac{\omega}{2},+\infty\right)\mapsto\phi\in H_{\rm per,m,e}^{\infty}
is smooth. Therefore, since
d_{c}\neq 0
, we obtain that
z(\mathcal{L})=1
and that
\mathcal{L}:[\phi^{\prime}]^{\bot}\to[\phi^{\prime}]^{\bot}
is invertible. Thus, by
(\ref{1L})
, we obtain that

\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}),\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dc}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\phi^{2}+\phi^{\prime 2})dx-\frac{dA}{dc}\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}),1\rangle.

(4.12)

Similarly, again by
(\ref{1L})
, we deduce from the fact that
\frac{d\phi}{dc}
has the zero-mean property that

\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}),1\rangle=-\frac{dA}{dc}\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}1,1\rangle.

(4.13)

Thus, by
(\ref{1L1})
and
(\ref{2L2})
, we obtain

\det\mathcal{A}(0)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d}{dc}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\phi^{2}+\phi^{\prime 2})dx\right)\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}1,1\rangle=-\frac{d}{dc}E(\phi)\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}1,1\rangle.

(4.14)

As proved below
(\ref{intchiphiderA})
, if
d_{c}<0
then

\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}1,1\rangle<0
and
\frac{dA}{dc}>0
. Therefore,
(\ref{deta0})
implies the well-known Vakhitov-Kolokolov stability criterion:

\langle\mathcal{L}_{\Pi}^{-1}(\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}),\phi-\phi^{\prime\prime}\rangle=-\frac{d}{dc}E(\phi)<0.

(4.15)

Similarly, if
d_{c}>0
, we obtain
\langle\mathcal{L}^{-1}1,1\rangle=\frac{2\pi\omega}{d_{c}}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}hdx>0
and
\det\mathcal{A}(0)<0
. Thus, the same condition in
(\ref{LPiphiphi2})
is also satisfied, and we conclude that if
d_{c}\neq 0
and
\frac{dA}{dc}>0
, then it follows that
\frac{d}{dc}E(\phi)>0
.
∎

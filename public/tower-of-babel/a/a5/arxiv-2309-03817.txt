Abstract
. For each primitive Dirichlet character χ \chi , a hypothesis 𝙶𝚁𝙷 † ​ [ χ ] {\tt GRH}^{\dagger}[\chi] is formulated in terms of zeros of the associated L L -function L ⁡ ( s , χ ) L(s,\chi) . It is shown that for any such character, 𝙶𝚁𝙷 † ​ [ χ ] {\tt GRH}^{\dagger}[\chi] is equivalent to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis.

1.
Introduction

An old result of Sprindžuk
[
12
,
13
]

(which he obtained by developing ideas of Linnik
[
9
]
)
states that, under the Riemann Hypothesis (RH), the Generalized Riemann
Hypothesis (GRH) holds for
all
Dirichlet
L
-functions provided some
suitable conditions on the vertical distribution of the zeros
of
\zeta(s)
are met. More precisely, the
Linnik–Sprindžuk theorem

asserts that GRH is equivalent to the validity of both RH and the hypothesis that,
for any rational number
\xi\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=h/k
with
0<|h|\leqslant k/2
and
(h,k)=1
,
and any real
\varepsilon>0
, the bound

\sum_{\rho=\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma}|\gamma|^{i\gamma}\mathrm{e}^{-i\gamma-\pi|\gamma|/2}(x+2\pi i\xi)^{-\rho}+\frac{\mu(k)}{\phi(k)}\frac{1}{x\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi,\varepsilon}\,x^{-1/2-\varepsilon}

holds for
x\to 0^{+}
. Similar results
have been attained by Fujii
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
,
Suzuki
[
14
]
, Kaczorowski and Perelli
[
8
]
,
and the author
[
1
,
2
]
.

In the present paper, we establish an analogous result in which

\zeta(s)
is replaced by the Dirichlet
L
-function
L(s,\chi)

attached to an arbitrary primitive character
\chi
.
To formulate the theorem, we introduce some notation.
In what follows,
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
denotes the space of smooth
functions
\mathcal{B}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
As usual, we write
\mathbf{e}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi iu}
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\chi
be a primitive character modulo
q
, and put

(1.1)

\kappa_{\chi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{cases}0&\quad\hbox{if $\chi(-1)=+1$,}\\
1&\quad\hbox{if $\chi(-1)=-1$,}\\
\end{cases}\quad\tau(\chi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{a\bmod q}\chi(a)\mathbf{e}(a/q),\quad\epsilon_{\chi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{\tau(\chi)}{i^{\kappa_{\chi}}\sqrt{q}}.

We recall the asymmetric form of the functional equation

L(s,\chi)=\mathcal{X}_{\chi}(s)L(1-s,{\overline{\chi}}),

where

(1.2)

\mathcal{X}_{\chi}(s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\epsilon_{\chi}2^{s}\pi^{s-1}q^{1/2-s}\Gamma(1-s)\sin\tfrac{\pi}{2}(s+\kappa_{\chi}).

In particular, if
\mathbb{1}
is
the trivial character given by
\mathbb{1}(n)=1

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, then the functional equation

\zeta(s)=\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{1}}(s)\zeta(1-s)
holds with

\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{1}}(s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=2^{s}\pi^{s-1}\Gamma(1-s)\sin\tfrac{\pi s}{2}.

Finally, for a fixed primitive character
\chi
modulo
q
, consider
the following two hypotheses concerning the zeros of
L(s,\chi)
.
The first hypothesis is

Hypothesis
{\tt GRH}[\chi]
:

If
L(\beta+i\gamma,\chi)=0
and
\beta>0
, then
\beta=\tfrac{1}{2}
.

Note that GRH is equivalent to the assertion
that
{\tt GRH}[\chi]
holds for all primitive
characters
\chi
. The second (stronger) hypothesis is

Hypothesis
{\tt GRH}^{\dagger}[\chi]
:

Hypothesis
{\tt GRH}[\chi]
is true, and for any rational number

\xi\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=h/k
with
h,k>0

and
(h,k)=1
, any
\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and any
\varepsilon>0
,
the bound

(1.3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\chi}}(1-\rho)\mathcal{B}\Big(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi\xi X}\Big)+C_{\mathcal{B}}\widetilde{C}_{\chi,\xi}X\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\chi,\xi,\mathcal{B},\varepsilon}\,X^{1/2+\varepsilon}

holds for
X\to\infty
, where

(1.4)

C_{\mathcal{B}}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}(u)\,du,\qquad\widetilde{C}_{\chi,\xi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{{\overline{\chi}}(h)\chi(k)\mu(k)\,q}{\phi(qk)}}&\quad\hbox{if $(h,q)=1$},\\
0&\quad\hbox{otherwise},\\
\end{cases}

the sum in (
1.3
) runs over the complex zeros

\rho=\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma
of
L(s,\chi)

(
counted with multiplicity
)
,
and the implied constant in (
1.3
) depends only on

\chi
,
\xi
,
\mathcal{B}
, and
\varepsilon
.

Our aim in this note is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

For each primitive
character
\chi
, the hypotheses
{\tt GRH}^{\dagger}[\chi]

and
{\rm GRH}
are equivalent.

Theorem
1.1
generalizes the main result of
[
2
]
,
in which the author showed that
{\tt GRH}^{\dagger}[\mathbb{1}]

and
{\rm GRH}
are equivalent.

Corollary 1.2
.

If
{\tt GRH}^{\dagger}[\chi]
holds for one primitive
character
\chi
, then it holds for all primitive
characters.

We emphasize that
{\tt GRH}^{\dagger}[\chi]
is formulated
entirely in terms of the zeros of a single
L
-function

L(s,\chi)
.
If
\chi
and
\psi
are different primitive characters,
it is reasonable to predict that the zeros of
L(s,\chi)
and
L(s,\psi)

are unrelated, and that there is no reason

a priori
that the hypotheses
{\tt GRH}^{\dagger}[\chi]
and

{\tt GRH}^{\dagger}[\psi]
should be connected. Nevertheless,

{\tt GRH}^{\dagger}[\chi]
and
{\tt GRH}^{\dagger}[\psi]

are actually equivalent in view of Corollary
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

We continue to use the notation introduced in §
1
.
Below,
\chi
always denotes
an arbitrary (but fixed) primitive Dirichlet
character of modulus
q\geqslant 1
.

Throughout the paper, implied constants in the
symbols
\ll
,
O
, etc., may depend on various parameters
as indicated by the notation (see, e.g., (
1.3
)), but

2.1.
The function
\mathcal{X}_{\chi}(s)

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a bounded interval in
\mathbb{R}
. Uniformly for
c\in\mathcal{I}
and
t\geqslant 1
,
we have

(2.1)

\mathcal{X}_{\chi}(1-c-it)=\tau(\chi)q^{c-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi i/4}\exp\Big(it\log\Big(\frac{qt}{2\pi\mathrm{e}}\Big)\Big)\Big(\frac{t}{2\pi}\Big)^{c-1/2}\big\{1+O_{\mathcal{I}}(t^{-1})\big\}.

Proof.

Replacing
s
by
1-s
in (
1.2
) gives

\mathcal{X}_{\chi}(1-s)=\epsilon_{\chi}2^{1-s}\pi^{-s}q^{s-1/2}\Gamma(s)\sin\tfrac{\pi}{2}(1-s+\kappa_{\chi}).

Let
s=c+it
,
t\geqslant 1
. Using Stirling’s formula for the gamma function

\Gamma(s)=\sqrt{2\pi}\,s^{s-1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-s}\{1+O(t^{-1})\}

(see, e.g., Montgomery and Vaughan
[
10
, Theorem C.1]
)
along with the estimates

(s-\tfrac{1}{2})\log s=(c-\tfrac{1}{2})\log t+c+(t\log t-\tfrac{\pi}{4})i+\tfrac{\pi is}{2}+O(t^{-1})

and

\sin\tfrac{\pi}{2}(1-s+\kappa_{\chi})=\tfrac{1}{2}i^{\kappa_{\chi}}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi is/2}\{1+O(\mathrm{e}^{-\pi t})\},

and recalling (
1.1
), a straightforward computation
leads to (
2.1
).
∎

The following lemma is due to Gonek
[
7
, Lemma 2]
;
the proof is based on the stationary phase method.

Lemma 2.2
.

Uniformly for
c\in[\frac{1}{10},2]

and
a<b\leqslant 2a
, we have

\intop\nolimits_{a}^{b}\exp\Big(it\log\Big(\frac{t}{u\mathrm{e}}\Big)\Big)\Big(\frac{t}{2\pi}\Big)^{c-1/2}dt=(2\pi)^{1-c}u^{c}\mathrm{e}^{-iu+\pi i/4}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{a,b}(u)+\widetilde{E}(a,b,u),

where

\mathbf{1}_{a,b}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{cases}1&\quad\hbox{if $u\in(a,b]$},\\
0&\quad\hbox{otherwise},\\
\end{cases}

and

\widetilde{E}(a,b,u)\ll a^{c-1/2}+\frac{a^{c+1/2}}{|a-u|+a^{1/2}}+\frac{b^{c+1/2}}{|b-u|+b^{1/2}}.

The next lemma is a variant of
Conrey, Ghosh, and Gonek
[
3
, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Uniformly for
v>0
and
c\in[\frac{1}{10},2]
, we have

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\intop\nolimits_{c+i}^{c+iT}v^{-s}\mathcal{X}_{\chi}(1-s)\,ds=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{\tau(\chi)}{q}\,\mathbf{e}(-v/q)+E(q,T,v)}&\quad\hbox{if $\frac{q}{2\pi}<v\leqslant\frac{qT}{2\pi}$},\\
E(q,T,v)&\quad\hbox{otherwise},\\
\end{cases}

where

E(q,T,v)\ll\frac{q^{c-1/2}}{v^{c}}\bigg(T^{c-1/2}+\frac{T^{c+1/2}}{|T-2\pi v/q|+T^{1/2}}\bigg).

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\intop\nolimits_{c+i}^{c+iT}v^{-s}\mathcal{X}_{\chi}(1-s)\,ds=\frac{1}{2\pi}\intop\nolimits_{1}^{T}v^{-c-it}\mathcal{X}_{\chi}(1-c-it)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau(\chi)q^{c-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi i/4}}{2\pi v^{c}}\bigg(\intop\nolimits_{1}^{T}\exp\Big(it\log\Big(\frac{qt}{2\pi v\mathrm{e}}\Big)\Big)\Big(\frac{t}{2\pi}\Big)^{c-1/2}\,dt+O(T^{c-1/2})\bigg),

and the result follows by applying Lemma
2.2
with
u\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=2\pi v/q
.
∎

2.2.
Essential bound

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
t\geqslant 2
, there is a real number
t_{*}\in[t,t+1]
such that

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma\pm it_{*},\chi)\ll(\log qt)^{2}\qquad(-1\leqslant\sigma\leqslant 2).

Proof.

See
[
10
, Lemmas 12.2 and 12.7]
.
∎

2.3.
Conditional results

Lemma 2.5
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

GRH
is true;

(ii)

RH
is true, and
for any primitive character
\psi\neq\mathbb{1}
,
any
\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

(2.2)

\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\psi(n)\mathcal{B}(n/X)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\psi,\mathcal{B},\varepsilon}\,X^{1/2+\varepsilon};

(iii)

RH
is true, and
for any nonprincipal character
\psi
,
any
\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and any
\varepsilon>0
,
the bound (
2.2
) holds.

Proof.

The equivalence
(i)\Longleftrightarrow(ii)

is the content of
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
, and the
implication
(iii)\Longrightarrow(ii)
is obvious.
Using the simple bound

(2.3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant N\\
(n,M)\neq 1\end{subarray}}\Lambda(n)\ll\log M\cdot\log N\qquad(M,N\geqslant 1),

the implication
(ii)\Longrightarrow(iii)
is immediate.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Under
GRH
, for any
\xi\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
,

\mathcal{B}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and
\varepsilon>0
, we have

(2.4)

\frac{\tau({\overline{\chi}})}{q}\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\mathbf{e}(-n\xi/q)\mathcal{B}(n/qX)=C_{\mathcal{B}}\widetilde{C}_{\chi,\xi}X+O_{\chi,\xi,\mathcal{B},\varepsilon}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}),

where
C_{\mathcal{B}}
and
\widetilde{C}_{\chi,\xi}
are defined
by (
1.4
).

Proof.

Let
\xi\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=h/k
with
h,k>0
and
(h,k)=1
.
Using (
2.3
), we see that
the sum in (
2.4
) is equal to

\sum_{(n,qk)=1}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\mathbf{e}(-nh/qk)\mathcal{B}(n/qX)+O_{\chi,\xi,\mathcal{B}}(1).

and the latter sum can be expressed as

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\bmod qk\\
(a,qk)=1\end{subarray}}\mathbf{e}(-ah/qk)\chi(a)\sum_{n\equiv a\bmod qk}\Lambda(n)\mathcal{B}(n/qX)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\phi(qk)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\bmod qk\\
(a,qk)=1\end{subarray}}\mathbf{e}(-ah/qk)\chi(a)\sum_{\psi\bmod qk}{\overline{\psi}}(a)\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\psi(n)\mathcal{B}(n/qX),

where the middle sum runs over all characters
\psi
modulo
qk
.
By Lemma
2.5

(iii)

the contribution from all nonprincipal characters
\psi

is
O_{\chi,\xi,\mathcal{B},\varepsilon}(X^{1/2+\varepsilon})
.
On the other hand, for the principal character
\psi_{0}
,
the contribution is

\frac{C}{\phi(qk)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,qk)=1\end{subarray}}\Lambda(n)\mathcal{B}(n/qX)=\frac{C}{\phi(qk)}\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\mathcal{B}(n/qX)+O_{\chi,\xi,\mathcal{B}}(1),

where we used (
2.3
) again, and

C\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\bmod qk\\
(a,qk)=1\end{subarray}}\mathbf{e}(-ah/qk)\chi(a).

By
[
10
, Theorem 9.12]
,

C=\begin{cases}{\overline{\chi}}(-h)\chi(k)\mu(k)\tau(\chi)&\quad\hbox{if $(h,q)=1$},\\
0&\quad\hbox{otherwise},\\
\end{cases}

and by
[
2
, Lemma 2.3]
,

\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\mathcal{B}(n/qX)=C_{\mathcal{B}}\cdot qX+O_{\chi,\mathcal{B}}(X^{1/2}\log^{2}X).

To finish the proof, observe that

\frac{\tau({\overline{\chi}})}{q}\cdot\frac{C}{\phi(qk)}\cdot q=\widetilde{C}_{\chi,\xi},

which follows from the well known relation

(2.5)

\tau(\chi)\tau({\overline{\chi}})=\chi(-1)q

for the Gauss sums defined in (
1.1
).
∎

3.
Twisting the von Mangoldt function

The results of this section are
unconditional
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
T\geqslant 2q^{2}
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
0<\gamma\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\chi}}(1-\rho)+\frac{\tau({\overline{\chi}})}{q}\sum_{n\leqslant qT/2\pi\xi}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\mathbf{e}(-n\xi/q)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,(qT)^{1/2}\log^{2}T.

where the first sum runs over complex zeros

\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of
L(s,\chi)

(
counted with multiplicity
)
.

Proof.

For any
u>0
, let

\Sigma_{1}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
0<\gamma\leqslant u\end{subarray}}\xi^{-\rho}\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\chi}}(1-\rho),\qquad\Sigma_{2}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{\tau({\overline{\chi}})}{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant qu/2\pi\xi\end{subarray}}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\mathbf{e}(-n\xi/q).

Our goal is to show that

(3.1)

\Sigma_{1}(T)+\Sigma_{2}(T)\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,(qT)^{1/2}\log^{2}T.

According to Lemma
2.4
, there is a number

t_{\circ}\in[2,3]
such that

(3.2)

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma\pm it_{\circ},\chi)\ll\log^{2}2q\qquad(-1\leqslant\sigma\leqslant 2).

Let
t_{\circ}
be fixed in what follows. Note that

(3.3)

\Sigma_{1}(t_{\circ})\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,q^{3/2}\log 2q,

since
|\xi^{-\rho}\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\chi}}(1-\rho)|\ll_{\xi}q^{1/2}
for all zeros

\rho=\beta+i\gamma
with
0<\gamma\leqslant t_{\circ}

(see Lemma
2.1
),
and there are only
O(q\log 2q)
such zeros
(see Siegel
[
11
, Theorem III]
). By the Chebyshev
bound, we also have

(3.4)

\Sigma_{2}(t_{\circ})\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,q^{1/2}.

Using Lemma
2.4
again,
for any
T_{*}\geqslant 2q^{2}
, there is a number
T\in[T_{*},T_{*}+1]
for which

(3.5)

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma\pm iT,\chi)\ll\log^{2}qT\ll\log^{2}T\qquad(-1\leqslant\sigma\leqslant 2).

To prove (
3.1
) in general, one can assume without
loss of generality that
T
has the property (
3.5
).
Indeed, let
T_{*}\geqslant 2q^{2}
be
arbitrary, and suppose
T\in[T_{*},T_{*}+1]
satisfies both (
3.1
)
and (
3.5
). By Lemma
2.1
, the bound

\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\chi}}(1-\rho)\ll(q\gamma)^{1/2}

holds uniformly for all complex zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of
L(s,\chi)

with
\gamma\geqslant 1
; therefore,

\big|\Sigma_{1}(T_{*})-\Sigma_{1}(T)\big|\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
T_{*}<\gamma\leqslant T_{*}+1\end{subarray}}\big|\xi^{-\rho}\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\chi}}(1-\rho)\big|\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,(qT_{*})^{1/2}\log T_{*}

since the number of zeros with
T_{*}<\gamma\leqslant T_{*}+1

is at most
O(\log T_{*})
. Moreover,

\big|\Sigma_{2}(T_{*})-\Sigma_{2}(T)\big|\leqslant\frac{|\tau({\overline{\chi}})|}{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}qT_{*}/2\pi\xi<n\leqslant q(T_{*}+1)/2\pi\xi\end{subarray}}\big|\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\mathbf{e}(-n\xi/q)\big|\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,q^{1/2}\log T_{*}.

Combining the preceding two bounds and (
3.1
), we deduce that

\Sigma_{1}(T_{*})+\Sigma_{2}(T_{*})\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,(qT_{*})^{1/2}\log^{2}T_{*},

which shows that (
3.1
) holds with
T_{*}
in place of
T
.

From now on, we can assume that
T
satisfies (
3.5
).
Put
c\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1+\frac{1}{\log qT}
,
and let
\mathcal{C}
be the following rectangle in
\mathbb{C}
:

c+it_{\circ}~~\longrightarrow~~c+iT~~\longrightarrow~~-1+iT~~\longrightarrow~~-1+it_{\circ}~~\longrightarrow~~c+it_{\circ}.

Note that, by (
3.2
) and (
3.5
),
neither
t_{\circ}
nor
T
is the ordinate of a zero of
L(s,\chi)
.
By Cauchy’s theorem,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}(T)-\Sigma_{1}(t_{\circ})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)\,\xi^{-s}\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\chi}}(1-s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\bigg(\intop\nolimits_{c+it_{\circ}}^{c+iT}+\intop\nolimits_{c+iT}^{-1+iT}+\intop\nolimits_{-1+iT}^{-1+it_{\circ}}+\intop\nolimits_{-1+it_{\circ}}^{c+it_{\circ}}\bigg)\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)\,\xi^{-s}\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\chi}}(1-s)\,ds

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}\quad\text{(say)},

and so by (
3.3
), we have

(3.6)

\Sigma_{1}(T)=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+O_{\xi}(q^{3/2}\log 2q).

We estimate the four integrals
I_{j}
separately.

Noting that

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)=-\sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}}\qquad(\sigma>1),

and applying Lemma
2.3
, we get that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\intop\nolimits_{c+it_{\circ}}^{c+iT}(n\xi)^{-s}\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\chi}}(1-s)\,ds

\displaystyle=-\frac{\tau({\overline{\chi}})}{q}\sum_{qt_{\circ}/2\pi\xi<n\leqslant qT/2\pi\xi}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\mathbf{e}(-n\xi/q)-\sum_{n}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)E(q,T,n\xi)

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{2}(t_{\circ})-\Sigma_{2}(T)+O\bigg(\frac{(qT)^{c-1/2}}{\xi^{c}}(E_{1}+E_{2})\bigg),

where

E_{1}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad E_{2}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}\frac{T}{|T-2\pi n\xi/q|+T^{1/2}}.

Note that
(qT)^{c-1/2}\asymp(qT)^{1/2}
, and recall that

\Sigma_{2}(t_{\circ})
satisfies (
3.4
).
Also,

(3.7)

E_{1}=-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(c)\ll\frac{1}{c-1}=\log qT\ll\log T.

To bound
E_{2}
, we split the integers
n\geqslant 2
into
three disjoint sets:

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{n\geqslant 2:|T-2\pi n\xi/q|>\tfrac{1}{2}T\},

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{n\geqslant 2:|T-2\pi n\xi/q|\leqslant T^{1/2}\},

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{n\geqslant 2:T^{1/2}<|T-2\pi n\xi/q|\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}T\}.

Using (
3.7
), we have

\sum_{n\in S_{1}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}\frac{T}{|T-2\pi n\xi/q|+T^{1/2}}\ll\log T,

which is acceptable. For each
n\in S_{2}
, we have

n\,\mathop{\asymp}\limits_{\xi}\,qT,\qquad\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}\,\mathop{\ll}\limits_{\xi}\,\frac{\log qT}{(qT)^{c}}\ll\frac{\log T}{qT},\qquad\frac{T}{|T-2\pi n\xi/q|+T^{1/2}}\asymp T^{1/2}.

Since
|S_{2}|\ll_{\xi}qT^{1/2}

Taking

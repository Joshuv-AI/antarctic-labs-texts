Deciding dependence in logic and algebra This research was supported by Swiss National Science Foundation grant 200021 _ \_ 165850.

Abstract
We introduce a universal algebraic generalization of de Jongh’s notion of dependence for formulas of intuitionistic propositional logic, relating it to a notion of dependence defined by Marczewski for elements of an algebraic structure. Following ideas of de Jongh and Chagrova, we show how constructive proofs of (weak forms of) uniform interpolation can be used to decide dependence for varieties of abelian ℓ \ell -groups, MV-algebras, semigroups, and modal algebras. We also consider minimal provability results for dependence, obtaining in particular a complete description and decidability of dependence for the variety of lattices.

Introduction

In
[
8
]
de Jongh and Chagrova introduced an intriguing notion of dependence of formulas for intuitionistic propositional logic
{\sf IPC}
as an analogue of the usual notion of dependence of vectors in linear algebra.

1

This notion was previously defined by de Jongh in
[
9
]
using different terminology and appears also in Lemmon’s textbook
Beginning Logic

[
21
]
in the context of comparing two formulas of classical propositional logic. For a detailed history and comparison of this and other notions of dependence in logic, we refer to
[
16
]
. Details of the seemingly unrelated but fascinating field of
dependence logic
may be found in
[
38
,
44
]
.
Formulas
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}
are said to be
{\sf IPC}
-dependent
if there exists a formula
\psi(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
such that
\vdash_{{\sf IPC}}\psi(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n})
, but
\not\vdash_{{\sf IPC}}\psi(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
; otherwise,
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}
are said to be
{\sf IPC}
-independent
. In
[
9
]
it is shown that a single formula
\varphi
is
{\sf IPC}
-dependent if, and only if, either
\vdash_{{\sf IPC}}\lnot\lnot\varphi\to\varphi
or
\vdash_{{\sf IPC}}\lnot\lnot\varphi
, and
[
8
]
provides a “reasonably simple” sequence of formulas
(\psi_{i}(p_{1},p_{2}))_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that any two formulas
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are dependent if, and only if,
\vdash_{{\sf IPC}}\psi_{i}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Notably, it is also proved in
[
8
]
that checking the
{\sf IPC}
-dependence of any finite number of formulas is decidable, making ingenious use of Pitts’ constructive proof of uniform interpolation for
{\sf IPC}

[
32
]
.

In this paper, we extend the ideas of
[
8
]
to a general universal algebraic setting (recalled in Section
2
), defining the
\mathcal{V}
-dependence of terms
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
for an arbitrary variety (equational class)
\mathcal{V}
. In Section
3
, we show that
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are
\mathcal{V}
-independent if, and only if, a certain homomorphism between finitely generated
\mathcal{V}
-free algebras is an embedding, or, equivalently, a certain finitely generated subalgebra of a
\mathcal{V}
-free algebra is
\mathcal{V}
-free over
n
generators. It then follows that
\mathcal{V}
-dependence is a special case of Marczewski’s notion of dependence for elements of an algebraic structure
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
, and a converse is obtained by relativizing
\mathcal{V}
-dependence to a given set of equations. Marczewski-dependence has been studied extensively in universal algebra (see
[
15
]
*Chapter 5), leading in particular to the introduction of
v^{*}
-algebras
[
30
]
, later rediscovered by semigroup theorists in the guise of independence algebras
[
14
]
.

In Section
4
, we turn our attention to the problem of deciding
\mathcal{V}
-dependence. Generalizing the proof strategy of
[
8
]
, we show that
\mathcal{V}
-dependence is decidable (even relative to a finite set of equations) if there is a constructive proof that
\mathcal{V}
is
coherent
, i.e., that every finitely generated subalgebra of a finitely presented algebra in
\mathcal{V}
is finitely presented. The property of coherence originated in sheaf theory and has been studied widely in algebra (see, e.g.,
[
36
,
34
,
13
]
), and from a more general model-theoretic perspective in
[
41
,
42
]
. In
[
20
]
it was proved that coherence and deductive interpolation are jointly equivalent to the property of right uniform deductive interpolation considered in
[
39
]
.

Constructive proofs of coherence are implicit in uniform interpolation proofs for, e.g.,
{\sf IPC}

[
32
]
, the modal logics
{\sf GL}
and
{\sf S4Grz}

[
3
]
, and the varieties of abelian
\ell
-groups and MV-algebras
[
27
,
39
]
. However, coherence is a quite rare property for non-locally finite varieties, at least those corresponding to modal, substructural, and other non-classical logics
[
20
,
19
]
. We therefore also consider a weaker condition for deciding
\mathcal{V}
-dependence that requires only a constructive proof that finitely generated
\mathcal{V}
-free algebras are coherent, and is satisfied, for example, by the non-coherent variety of groups. Even when such a constructive proof is not available, however, there may exist other methods for deciding
\mathcal{V}
-dependence. Notably, the dependence problem for the variety of semigroups corresponds precisely to checking whether a finite set of words is a code, solved by the famous Sardinas-Patterson algorithm
[
33
]
, and the dependence problem for the variety of modal algebras can be decided using bisimulation-based methods described in
[
22
]
for calculating uniform interpolants (when they exist) for the description logic
{\sf ALC}
.

In Section
5
, following again ideas of
[
8
]
, we consider minimal sets of equations for checking
\mathcal{V}
-dependence for some variety
\mathcal{V}
. In particular, we provide finite minimal sets of equations for dependence in both the locally finite variety of distributive lattices and the non-locally finite variety of lattices, obtaining a first proof that the dependence problem for the variety of lattices is decidable. Finally, in Section
6
, we conclude the paper with a short list of open problems.

2
Equational consequence and free algebras

Let us begin by recalling some elementary material on universal algebra, referring to
[
4
]
for further details and references. We assume that
\mathcal{L}
is an algebraic language and that an
\mathcal{L}
-algebra

{\bf{A}}
is a (first-order) structure for this language with universe
A
and fundamental operations
f^{\bf{A}}
for each function symbol
f
of
\mathcal{L}
. For any set of variables
\overline{x}
, we denote by
\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
the set of
\mathcal{L}
-terms over
\overline{x}
, and by
\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{x}):=\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})\times\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
, the set of
\mathcal{L}
-equations over
\overline{x}
. For
\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})\neq\emptyset
(i.e., when
\overline{x}\neq\emptyset
or
\mathcal{L}
contains a constant), we denote by
{\bf{Tm}}(\overline{x})
the
\mathcal{L}
-term

algebra over
\overline{x}
. We also write
t(\overline{x})
,
\varepsilon(\overline{x})
, or
\Sigma(\overline{x})
to mean that the variables occurring in an
\mathcal{L}
-term
t
, an
\mathcal{L}
-equation
\varepsilon
, or a set of
\mathcal{L}
-equations
\Sigma
, are included in
\overline{x}
, and assume that
\overline{x}
,
\overline{y}
, etc. are disjoint sets, writing
\overline{x},\overline{y}
to denote their disjoint union. Given an
\mathcal{L}
-term
t(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and an
\mathcal{L}
-algebra
{\bf{A}}
, we denote by
t^{\bf{A}}
the induced
term-function
from
A^{n}
to
A
.

For any homomorphism
h\colon{\bf{A}}\to{\bf{B}}
between
\mathcal{L}
-algebras
{\bf{A}}
and
{\bf{B}}
, its
kernel

\ker(h):=\{\langle a,b\rangle\in A\times A\mid h(a)=h(b)\}
forms a
congruence
of
{\bf{A}}
: that is, an equivalence relation that is preserved by the fundamental operations of
{\bf{A}}
. Moreover, the converse is also true; every congruence of
{\bf{A}}
is the kernel of some homomorphism with domain
{\bf{A}}
. For any
\mathcal{L}
-algebra
{\bf{A}}
, the set
\textup{Con}({{\bf{A}}})
of congruences of
{\bf{A}}
forms a complete lattice ordered by inclusion
\subseteq
with greatest element
\nabla_{A}:=A\times A
and least element
\Delta_{A}:=\{\langle a,a\rangle\mid a\in A\}
. Given
S\subseteq A\times A
, the congruence
{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{A}}}}(S)
of
{\bf{A}}

generated by
S
is the smallest congruence of
{\bf{A}}
containing
S
. A congruence
\Theta
of
{\bf{A}}
is
finitely generated
if
\Theta={\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{A}}}}(S)
for some finite
S\subseteq A\times A
.

Let
\mathbb{H}
,
\mathbb{I}
,
\mathbb{S}
, and
\mathbb{P}
denote the class operators of taking homomorphic images, isomorphic images, subalgebras, and products, respectively. A class of
\mathcal{L}
-algebras
\mathcal{K}
is called a
variety
if it is closed under
\mathbb{H}
,
\mathbb{S}
, and
\mathbb{P}
. By theorems of Birkhoff and Tarski, respectively,
\mathcal{K}
is a variety if, and only if, it is an equational class, and
\mathbb{HSP}(\mathcal{K})
is the variety generated by
\mathcal{K}
.

Example 2.1
.

A
Heyting algebra
is an algebra
\langle H,\land,\lor,\to,0,1\rangle
such that
\langle H,\land,\lor,0,1\rangle
is a bounded distributive lattice (with
a\leq b:\Leftrightarrow a\land b=a
) and
\to
is the residual of
\land
; that is,
a\leq b\to c
if, and only if,
a\land b\leq c
for all
a,b,c\in H
. Heyting algebras form a variety
\mathcal{HA}
that provides the algebraic semantics for intuitionistic propositional logic.

Equational consequence for a class
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{L}
-algebras may be defined as follows. For a set of
\mathcal{L}
-equations
\Sigma\cup\{\varepsilon\}
containing exactly the variables in a set
\overline{x}
,

\begin{array}[]{rcc}\Sigma\models_{\mathcal{K}}\varepsilon&:\Longleftrightarrow&\text{for every ${\bf{A}}\in\mathcal{K}$ and homomorphism $e\colon{\bf{Tm}}(\overline{x})\to{\bf{A}}$,}\\[3.61371pt]
&&\Sigma\subseteq\ker(e)\kern 5.0pt\Longrightarrow\kern 5.0pt\varepsilon\in\ker(e).\end{array}

For a set of
\mathcal{L}
-equations
\Sigma\cup\Delta
, we write
\Sigma\models_{\mathcal{K}}\Delta
if
\Sigma\models_{\mathcal{K}}\varepsilon
for all
\varepsilon\in\Delta
.

In the case where
\mathcal{K}
is a variety, we may reformulate equational consequence in terms of congruences of
\mathcal{K}
-free algebras. Let us first recall the construction of
\mathcal{K}
-free algebras for an arbitrary class
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{L}
-algebras over a set
\overline{x}
, assuming that either
\mathcal{L}
contains a constant or
\overline{x}
is non-empty. Let
\Theta_{\mathcal{K}}(\overline{x})
be the smallest congruence of
{\bf{Tm}}(\overline{x})
such that the quotient by this congruence embeds into a member of
\mathcal{K}
, i.e.,

\Theta_{\mathcal{K}}(\overline{x}):=\bigcap\{\Theta\in\textup{Con}({{\bf{Tm}}(\overline{x})})\mid{\bf{Tm}}(\overline{x})/\Theta\in\mathbb{IS}(\mathcal{K})\}.

The
\mathcal{K}
-free algebra over
\overline{x}
may then be defined as

{\bf{F}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\overline{x}):={\bf{Tm}}(\overline{x})/\Theta_{\mathcal{K}}(\overline{x}).

It follows that for any
s,t\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
,

\langle s,t\rangle\in\Theta_{\mathcal{K}}(X)\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pt\>\models_{\mathcal{K}}s\approx t\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pt\>\models_{{\bf{F}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\overline{x})}s\approx t.

Where appropriate, we deliberately confuse
\mathcal{L}
-terms,
\mathcal{L}
-equations, and sets of
\mathcal{L}
-equations with the corresponding elements, pairs of elements, and sets of pairs of elements from
{\bf{F}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\overline{x})
. Hence for
s,t\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
,

\models_{\mathcal{K}}s\approx t\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pts=t\>\text{ in ${\bf{F}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\overline{x})$.}

Also, when the class of algebras
\mathcal{K}
is clear from the context, we drop the subscript and write simply
{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})
.

The following lemma expresses the crucial connection between equational consequence in a variety and congruences on the free algebras of that variety.

Lemma 2.2

(c.f.
[
27
]
*Lemma 2)
.

For any variety
\mathcal{V}
and
\Sigma\cup\Delta\subseteq\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{x})
,

\Sigma\models_{\mathcal{V}}\Delta\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pt{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})}}(\Delta)\subseteq{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})}}(\Sigma).

In what follows, we will omit mention of the language
\mathcal{L}
, assuming throughout that a class of algebras
\mathcal{K}
is a class of
\mathcal{L}
-algebras, and that terms, equations, and sets of equations are defined over this language. We will also adopt the useful notation
[n]
to denote the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

3
An algebraic theory of dependence

We now have the tools available to formulate and study the de Jongh notion of dependence in a more general algebraic setting. Let
\mathcal{V}
be any variety. We call terms
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})

\mathcal{V}
-
dependent
if for some equation
\varepsilon(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
,

\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon;

otherwise, we call
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}

\mathcal{V}
-
independent
.

Example 3.1
.

If
\mathcal{V}
is the variety of vector spaces over some fixed field
{\bf{K}}
, this notion of independence coincides with the usual notion in linear algebra. Just observe that terms
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
in the language with the usual group operations and scalar multiplication for each
\lambda\in K
are
\mathcal{V}
-independent if, and only if, (without loss of generality) for any equation
\varepsilon(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
of the form
\lambda_{1}y_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}y_{n}\approx 0
with
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in K
,

\displaystyle\models_{\mathcal{V}}\lambda_{1}t_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}t_{n}\approx 0

\displaystyle\kern 5.0pt\Longrightarrow\kern 5.0pt\>\models_{\mathcal{V}}\lambda_{1}y_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}y_{n}\approx 0,

and that
\models_{\mathcal{V}}\lambda_{1}y_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}y_{n}\approx 0
if, and only if,
\lambda_{1}=\dots=\lambda_{n}=0
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}
be the variety of lattices and
\mathcal{DL}\mathit{at}
the variety of distributive lattices, and consider the lattice terms

t_{1}:=x_{1}\land(x_{2}\lor x_{3})\quad\text{and}\quad t_{2}:=x_{2}\lor(x_{1}\land x_{3}).

Defining
\varepsilon(y_{1},y_{2}):=y_{1}\leq y_{2}
(where
s\leq t
denotes
s\land t\approx s
), we have
\models_{\mathcal{DL}\mathit{at}}\varepsilon(t_{1},t_{2})
and
\not\models_{\mathcal{DL}\mathit{at}}\varepsilon
, so
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
are
\mathcal{DL}\mathit{at}
-dependent. However, no such equation exists in the case of lattices, so
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
are
\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}
-independent.

The next result provides equivalent characterizations of
\mathcal{V}
-independence.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a variety. For any terms
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
and variables
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are
\mathcal{V}
-independent.

(2)

The homomorphism
h\colon{\bf{F}}(\overline{y})\to{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})
defined by mapping
y_{i}
to
t_{i}
for each
i\in[n]
is injective.

(3)

{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})}}(\langle y_{1},t_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{n},t_{n}\rangle)\cap F(\overline{y})^{2}=\Delta_{F(\overline{y})}
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)  Suppose that
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are
\mathcal{V}
-independent and that
h(u)=h(v)
in
{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})
for some
u,v\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{y})
. That is,
u(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})=v(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
in
{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})
, and hence
\models_{\mathcal{V}}u(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\approx v(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
. But
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are
\mathcal{V}
-independent, so
\models_{\mathcal{V}}u\approx v
and
u=v
in
{\bf{F}}(\overline{y})
. Hence
h
is injective.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3)  Suppose that
h
is injective. Let
f\colon{\bf{F}}(\overline{y})\to{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})
be the natural inclusion homomorphism and let
g\colon{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})\to{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})
be the homomorphism mapping
x\in\overline{x}
to
x
and
y_{i}
to
t_{i}
for each
i\in[n]
. Clearly,
h=g\circ f
and
{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})}}(\langle y_{1},t_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{n},t_{n}\rangle)\subseteq\ker(g)
, and hence, since
h
is injective,

{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})}}(\langle y_{1},t_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{n},t_{n}\rangle)\cap F(\overline{y})^{2}\subseteq\ker(h)=\Delta_{F(\overline{y})}.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1)
Suppose that
{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})}}(\langle y_{1},t_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{n},t_{n}\rangle)\cap F(\overline{y})^{2}=\Delta_{F(\overline{y})}
and consider
\varepsilon\in\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{y})
with
\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
. Then
\{y_{1}\approx t_{1},\dots,y_{n}\approx t_{n}\}\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon
and an application of Lemma
2.2
yields

\varepsilon\in{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})}}(\langle y_{1},t_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{n},t_{n}\rangle)\cap F(\overline{y})^{2}=\Delta_{F(\overline{y})}.

Hence, by Lemma
2.2
, also
\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon
. So
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are
\mathcal{V}
-independent.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Condition (3) of Proposition
3.3
can also be understood as a property of equational consequence. Given terms
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
and variables
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
, define

\Gamma:=\{y_{1}\approx t_{1},\dots,y_{n}\approx t_{n}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\Pi:=\{\varepsilon\in\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{y})\mid\Gamma\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon\}.

Then for any
\varepsilon\in\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{y})
,

\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pt\Gamma\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pt\Pi\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon,

and, corresponding to condition (3),

t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\text{ are $\mathcal{V}$-independent}\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pt\>\models_{\mathcal{V}}\Pi.

Let us now consider the relationship between this notion of dependence and the general algebraic notion introduced by Marczewski in
[
23
]
. We say that elements
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
of an algebra
{\bf{A}}
are
Marczewski-dependent
in
{\bf{A}}
if there exist terms
u(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}),v(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
satisfying

u^{\bf{A}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=v^{\bf{A}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad u^{\bf{A}}\neq v^{\bf{A}};

otherwise, we call
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}

Marczewski-independent
in
{\bf{A}}
.

Remark 3.5
.

Equivalently,
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in A
are Marczewski-independent in
{\bf{A}}
if, and only if, they are distinct and generate a subalgebra of
{\bf{A}}
that is
\mathbb{HSP}({\bf{A}})
-free over the set of generators
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}

[
24
]
.

It is not hard to see that
\mathcal{V}
-dependence for a variety
\mathcal{V}
corresponds to the Marczewski-dependence of elements of finitely generated
\mathcal{V}
-free algebras.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a variety. Terms
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
are
\mathcal{V}
-dependent if, and only if,
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are Marczewski-dependent in
{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})
.

Proof.

It suffices to observe that
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
are Marczewski-dependent in
{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})
if, and only if, there exist terms
u(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}),v(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
satisfying

u^{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})=v^{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad u^{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})}\neq v^{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})},

or, equivalently, there exist terms
u(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}),v(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
satisfying

\models_{\mathcal{V}}u(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\approx v(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\models_{\mathcal{V}}u\not\approx v,

which holds if, and only if,
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are
\mathcal{V}
-dependent.
∎

Let us now consider a more general version of dependence defined for a variety
\mathcal{V}
relative to a fixed set of equations. We call
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})

\mathcal{V}
-
dependent over
\Sigma\subseteq\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{x})
if for some equation
\varepsilon(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
,

\Sigma\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon;

otherwise, we call
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}

\mathcal{V}
-
independent over
\Sigma
.

To obtain a reformulation of this property analogous to Proposition
3.3
, we consider for
\Sigma\subseteq\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{x})
, the quotient algebra
{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})/{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})}}(\Sigma)
, denoting its elements by
[t]_{\Sigma}
for each
t\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a variety. For any
\Sigma\subseteq\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{x})
,
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
, and
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are
\mathcal{V}
-independent over
\Sigma
.

(2)

The homomorphism
h\colon{\bf{F}}(\overline{y})\to{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})/{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})}}(\Sigma)
mapping
y_{i}
to
[t_{i}]_{\Sigma}
for each
i\in[n]
is injective.

(3)

{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})}}(\Sigma\cup\{\langle y_{1},t_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{n},t_{n}\rangle\})\cap F(\overline{y})^{2}=\Delta_{F(\overline{y})}
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)  Suppose that
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are
\mathcal{V}
-independent over
\Sigma
and
h(u)=h(v)
for some
u,v\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{y})
. It follows that
[u(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})]_{\Sigma}=[v(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})]_{\Sigma}
in
{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})/{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})}}(\Sigma)
and, by Lemma
2.2
, that
\Sigma\models_{\mathcal{V}}u(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\approx v(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
. Since, by assumption,
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are
\mathcal{V}
-independent over
\Sigma
, we have
\models_{\mathcal{V}}u\approx v
. So
u=v
in
{\bf{F}}(\overline{y})
. That is,
h
is injective.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3)  Suppose that
h
is injective. Let
f\colon{\bf{F}}(\overline{y})\to{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})
be the natural inclusion homomorphism and let
g\colon{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})\to{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})/{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})}}(\Sigma)
be the homomorphism mapping
x\in\overline{x}
to
[x]_{\Sigma}
and
y_{i}
to
[t_{i}]_{\Sigma}
for each
i\in[n]
. Clearly,
h=g\circ f
and
{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})}}(\Sigma\cup\{\langle y_{1},t_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{n},t_{n}\rangle\})\subseteq\ker(g)
, and hence, since
h
is injective,

{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})}}(\Sigma\cup\{\langle y_{1},t_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{n},t_{n}\rangle\})\cap F(\overline{y})^{2}\subseteq\ker(h)=\Delta_{F(\overline{y})}.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1)
Suppose that
{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})}}(\Sigma\cup\{\langle y_{1},t_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{n},t_{n}\rangle\})\cap F(\overline{y})^{2}=\Delta_{F(\overline{y})}
and
\Sigma\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
for some
\varepsilon\in\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{y})
. Then
\Sigma\cup\{y_{1}\approx t_{1},\dots,y_{n}\approx t_{n}\}\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon
and, by Lemma
2.2
,

\varepsilon\in{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})}}(\Sigma\cup\{\langle y_{1},t_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{n},t_{n}\rangle\})\cap F(\overline{y})^{2}=\Delta_{F(\overline{y})}.

Hence, by Lemma
2.2
, also
\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon
. So
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are
\mathcal{V}
-independent over
\Sigma
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

\mathcal{V}
-dependence over
\Sigma
can again be understood as a property of equational consequence. For
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
and
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
, let

\Gamma:=\Sigma\cup\{y_{1}\approx t_{1},\dots,y_{n}\approx t_{n}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\Pi:=\{\varepsilon\in\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{y})\mid\Gamma\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon\}.

Then for any
\varepsilon\in\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{y})
,

\Sigma\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pt\Gamma\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pt\Pi\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon,

and, corresponding to condition (3),

t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\text{ are $\mathcal{V}$-independent over $\Sigma$}\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pt\>\models_{\mathcal{V}}\Pi.

A natural question to ask at this point is whether this more general notion is related to Marczewski-dependence. Below we show that this is indeed the case, although the relationship is unlikely to be of any practical value.

Recall that the
positive diagram

{\rm Diag}^{+}({\bf{A}})
of an algebra
{\bf{A}}
can be identified with the set of equations
s(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\approx t(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathrm{Eq}(A)
such that
s^{\bf{A}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=t^{\bf{A}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
{\bf{A}}
be any algebra. Then
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in A
are Marczewski-dependent in
{\bf{A}}
if, and only if,
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(A)
are
\mathbb{HSP}({\bf{A}})
-dependent over
{\rm Diag}^{+}({\bf{A}})
.

Proof.

It suffices to observe that for any set of variables
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
, equation
u\approx v\in\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{y})
, and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in A
,

(i)

{\rm Diag}^{+}({\bf{A}})\models_{\mathbb{HSP}({\bf{A}})}u(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\approx v(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle u(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\approx v(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in{\rm Diag}^{+}({\bf{A}})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle u^{\bf{A}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=v^{\bf{A}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})

(ii)

\models_{\mathbb{HSP}({\bf{A}})}u\approx v\iff{\bf{A}}\models u\approx v\iff u^{\bf{A}}=v^{\bf{A}}
. ∎

4
Deciding dependence

In
[
8
]
de Jongh and Chagrova established the decidability of
{\sf IPC}
-dependence for finitely many formulas (equivalently, the
\mathcal{HA}
-dependence of finitely many terms) using Pitts’ constructive proof of uniform interpolation for
{\sf IPC}

[
32
]
. More concretely, they proved that formulas
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}
are
{\sf IPC}
-independent if, and only if, the right uniform interpolant of
(p_{1}\leftrightarrow\varphi_{1})\land\dots\land(p_{n}\leftrightarrow\varphi_{n})
with respect to a new set of variables
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\}
is a theorem of
{\sf IPC}
. In this section, we generalize their approach to an arbitrary variety
\mathcal{V}
and finite set of equations
\Sigma
, showing that to check the
\mathcal{V}
-dependence of terms
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
over
\Sigma
, a constructive proof of the weaker property of
coherence
in
\mathcal{V}
— or, when
\Sigma=\emptyset
, just coherence for finitely generated
\mathcal{V}
-free algebras — suffices.

A finitely presented algebra
{\bf{A}}\in\mathcal{V}
is said to be
coherent
if every finitely generated subalgebra of
{\bf{A}}
is finitely presented, and a variety
\mathcal{V}
is called coherent if all of its finitely presented members are coherent. The following result from
[
20
]
relates this notion to finitely generated congruences on finitely generated
\mathcal{V}
-free algebras and equational consequence.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
20
]
*Theorem 2.3)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a variety. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{V}
is coherent.

(2)

For any finite sets
\overline{x},\overline{y}
and finitely generated congruence
\Theta
of
{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})
, the congruence
\Theta\cap F(\overline{y})^{2}
on
{\bf{F}}(\overline{y})
is finitely generated.

(3)

For any finite sets
\overline{x},\overline{y}
and finite set of equations
\Gamma(\overline{x},\overline{y})
, there exists a finite set of equations
\Pi(\overline{y})
such that for any equation
\varepsilon(\overline{y})
,

\Gamma\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pt\Pi\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon.

Remark 4.2
.

Condition (3) is closely related to the property of
right uniform deductive interpolation
, which is obtained by replacing “any equation
\varepsilon(\overline{y})
” with “any equation
\varepsilon(\overline{y},\overline{z})
”. Indeed, a variety has right uniform deductive interpolation if, and only if, it is coherent and has deductive interpolation. Let us note also that deductive interpolation (obtained from right uniform deductive interpolation by dropping the requirement that
\Pi(\overline{y})
be finite) is equivalent to the amalgamation property in the presence of the congruence extension property. We refer to
[
27
,
39
,
20
]
for further details and discussion of these relationships.

Let us call the problem of finding for any finite sets
\overline{x},\overline{y}
and finite set of equations
\Gamma(\overline{x},\overline{y})
, a finite set of equations
\Pi(\overline{y})
satisfying the equivalence in condition (3) of Theorem
4.1
, the
coherence problem
for
\mathcal{V}
. Note that this is equivalent to the problem of finding a finite presentation for a finitely generated subalgebra of a finitely presented algebra of
\mathcal{V}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a variety and let
\Sigma
be any finite set of equations. If the coherence problem for
\mathcal{V}
and the equational theory of
\mathcal{V}
are both decidable, then
\mathcal{V}
-dependence over
\Sigma
is decidable.

Proof.

Given
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
, define
\Gamma:=\Sigma\cup\{y_{1}\approx t_{1},\dots,y_{n}\approx t_{n}\}
. It follows from Remark
3.8
that
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are
\mathcal{V}
-independent over
\Sigma
if, and only if, for any equation
\varepsilon(\overline{y})
,

\Gamma\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pt\>\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon.

However, by assumption, a finite set of equations
\Pi(\overline{y})
can be constructed such that for any equation
\varepsilon(\overline{y})
,

\Gamma\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pt\Pi\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon.

It follows that
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
are
\mathcal{V}
-independent over
\Sigma
if, and only if,
\models_{\mathcal{V}}\Pi
, which, by assumption, is decidable.
∎

The coherence problem is clearly decidable for any locally finite variety. Also, as mentioned above, Pitts’ constructive proof of uniform interpolation for
{\sf IPC}
provides an algorithm that decides the coherence problem for the variety
\mathcal{HA}
of Heyting algebras
[
32
]
and hence also an algorithm for deciding
\mathcal{HA}
-dependence over any finite set of equations.

Pitts-style proofs of uniform interpolation have been obtained for various intermediate, modal, and substructural logics (see in particular
[
40
,
35
,
3
,
1
]
), but typically establish an implication-based uniform interpolation property that does not imply coherence. However, for the modal logics
{\sf GL}
and
{\sf S4Grz}
, the proof-theoretic proofs of uniform interpolation by Bilková
[
3
]
(originally proved semantically by Shavrukov
[
35
]
and Visser
[
40
]
, respectively) provide constructive proofs of coherence for the associated varieties and hence also decidability of dependence over finite sets of equations.

Example 4.4

(Abelian
\ell
-groups)
.

An
abelian
\ell
-group
is an algebraic structure
\langle L,\land,\lor,+,-,0\rangle
such that
\langle L,\land,\lor\rangle
is a lattice with order
a\leq b:\Leftrightarrow a\land b=a
,
\langle L,+,-,0\rangle
is an abelian group, and
a\leq b
implies
a+c\leq b+c
for all
a,b,c\in L
. They form a variety
\mathcal{LA}
that is generated as a quasivariety by
{\bf{R}}=\langle\mathbb{R},\land,\lor,+,-,0\rangle
(cf.
[
2
]
*Lemma 6.2) and has a decidable equational theory. In
[
29
]
it is proved that checking whether the subalgebra generated by
n
elements of a finitely generated free abelian
\ell
-group is isomorphic to the
n
-generated free abelian
\ell
-group is decidable, and hence, although this is not explicitly stated, that
\mathcal{LA}
-dependence is decidable. However, a stronger version of this result, the decidability of
\mathcal{LA}
-dependence over a finite set of equations, follows already from a (quite easy) constructive proof of coherence given implicitly in
[
27
]
. Note first that it suffices to show that for any finite set
\overline{y}
and finite set of equations
\Gamma(x,\overline{y})
, there exists a finite set of equations
\Pi(\overline{y})
such that for any equation
\varepsilon(\overline{y})
,

\Gamma\models_{{\bf{R}}}\varepsilon\iff\Pi\models_{{\bf{R}}}\varepsilon.

Moreover, we may assume (with a little work, omitted here) that
\Gamma
consists of inequations
0\leq s_{i}+nx
(
i\in I
),
0\leq t_{j}-nx
(
j\in J
), and
0\leq u_{k}
(
k\in K
) for some
n\geq 1
, finite sets
I,J,K
, and terms
s_{i}(\overline{y}),t_{j}(\overline{y}),u_{k}(\overline{y})
. The desired set
\Pi(\overline{y})
is then
\{0\leq s_{i}+t_{j}\mid i\in I;j\in J\}\cup\{0\leq u_{k}\mid k\in K\}
.

Example 4.5

(MV-algebras)
.

The variety
\mathcal{MV}
of
MV-algebras
consists of algebraic structures
\langle M,\oplus,\lnot,0\rangle
satisfying the equations

\begin{array}[]{rlrl}{\rm(M1)}&x\oplus(y\oplus z)\approx(x\oplus y)\oplus z&{\rm(M4)}&\lnot\lnot x\approx x\\
{\rm(M2)}&x\oplus y\approx y\oplus x&{\rm(M5)}&x\oplus\lnot 0\approx\lnot 0\\
{\rm(M3)}&x\oplus 0\approx x&{\rm(M6)}&\lnot(\lnot x\oplus y)\oplus y\approx\lnot(\lnot y\oplus x)\oplus x.\end{array}

It is generated as a quasivariety by
{\bf{[0,1]}}=\langle[0,1],\oplus,\lnot,0\rangle
, where
a\oplus b=\min(1,a+b)
and
\lnot a=1-a
, with defined operations
1:=\lnot 0
,
a\odot b:=\lnot(\lnot a\oplus\lnot b)
,
a\lor b:=\lnot(\lnot a\oplus b)\oplus b
, and
a\land b:=\lnot(\lnot a\lor\lnot b)

[
10
]
. Let
{\bf{R}}^{u}
be the unital abelian
\ell
-group consisting of
{\bf{R}}
with an additional constant
1
. It follows from McNaughton’s representation theorem (or see
[
27
]
*Sec. 6 for a direct proof) that (i) the interpretation of any term
s
in
{\bf{[0,1]}}
is equivalent on
[0,1]
to the interpretation of some term
(t\land 0)\lor 1
in
{\bf{R}}^{u}
, and, conversely, (ii) the interpretation of any term
(t\land 0)\lor 1
in
{\bf{R}}^{u}
is equivalent on
[0,1]
to the interpretation of some term
s
in
{\bf{[0,1]}}
. Coherence for
\mathcal{MV}
may then be established constructively as in the case of abelian
\ell
-groups described in the previous example. Since
\mathcal{MV}
also has a decidable equational theory,
\mathcal{MV}
-dependence over a finite set of equations is decidable.

As shown in
[
20
,
19
]
, coherence for a non-locally finite variety is a rather exceptional property. In particular, the varieties of lattices, semigroups, and groups, as well as broad families of varieties of modal algebras and residuated lattices are not coherent. However, for checking
\mathcal{V}
-dependence (i.e., over the empty set of equations), it is not necessary to have an algorithm that decides the full coherence problem; it suffices to consider the coherence of finitely generated
\mathcal{V}
-free algebras.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a variety. The following are equivalent for any finite set
\overline{x}
:

(1)

{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})
is coherent.

(2)

For any
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
and
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
, the congruence
{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})}}(\langle y_{1},t_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{n},t_{n}\rangle)\cap F(\overline{y})^{2}
is finitely generated.

(3)

For any
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
and
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
, there exists a finite set of equations
\Pi(\overline{y})
such that for any equation
\varepsilon(\overline{y})
,

\{y_{1}\approx t_{1},\dots,y_{n}\approx t_{n}\}\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pt\Pi\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon.

Proof.

For the equivalence of (1) and (2), consider any
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
and
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
. Let
h\colon{\bf{F}}(\overline{y})\to{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})
be the homomorphism mapping
y_{i}
to
t_{i}
for each
i\in[n]
. Then
\ker(h)={\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})}}(\langle y_{1},t_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{n},t_{n}\rangle)\cap F(\overline{y})^{2}
and, by the homomorphism theorem (see
[
4
]
), the quotient
{\bf{F}}(\overline{y})/\ker(h)
is isomorphic to the subalgebra
{\bf{A}}
of
{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})
generated by
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\}
. We now recall the following:

Fact

(
[
20
]
*Lemma 2.2)
.

If
{\bf{B}}\in\mathcal{V}
is finitely presented and isomorphic to a quotient
{\bf{F}}(\overline{z})/\Theta
for some finite set
\overline{z}
, then
\Theta
is finitely generated.

Hence, if
{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})
is coherent,
{\bf{A}}
is finitely presented and, by the fact stated above,
{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})}}(\langle y_{1},t_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{n},t_{n}\rangle)\cap F(\overline{y})^{2}
is finitely generated. Conversely, assuming (2), if
{\bf{A}}
is a subalgebra of
{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})
generated by
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\}
, then the congruence
{\rm Cg}_{{}_{{\bf{F}}(\overline{x},\overline{y})}}(\langle y_{1},t_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{n},t_{n}\rangle)\cap F(\overline{y})^{2}
is finitely generated and
{\bf{A}}
is finitely presented, so
{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})
is coherent. The equivalence of (2) and (3) follows directly from Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Let us therefore call the problem of finding for any
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
and
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
, a finite set of equations
\Pi(\overline{y})
satisfying the equivalence in condition (3), the
free coherence problem
for
\mathcal{V}
. (Note that this is equivalent to finding a finite presentation for a finitely generated subalgebra of a finitely generated
\mathcal{V}
-free algebra.) It follows directly from the proof of Proposition
4.3
that if the free coherence problem for
\mathcal{V}
and the equational theory of
\mathcal{V}
are decidable, then
\mathcal{V}
-dependence is decidable.

Example 4.7

(Groups)
.

The variety
\mathcal{G}\mathit{rp}
of
groups
is not coherent; e.g., the wreath product
\mathbb{Z}\,{\rm wr}\,\mathbb{Z}
is a finitely generated subgroup of a finitely presented group that is not finitely presented
[
7
]
. However, by the Nielsen-Schreier theorem, every finitely generated subgroup of a finitely generated free group is again a finitely generated free group, so finitely generated free groups are coherent. Moreover, Nielsen’s proof in
[
31
]
also determines the rank of a finitely generated subgroup of a free group, so the free coherence problem for
\mathcal{G}\mathit{rp}
is decidable. Hence also
\mathcal{G}\mathit{rp}
-dependence is decidable.

It may not be the case that all finitely generated free algebras of a variety
\mathcal{V}
are coherent. However, assuming that the equational theory of
\mathcal{V}
is decidable,
\mathcal{V}
-dependence is decidable (again considering the proof of Proposition
4.3
) if there exists an algorithm to check for
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
and
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
whether for any equation
\varepsilon(\overline{y})
,

\{y_{1}\approx t_{1},\dots,y_{n}\approx t_{n}\}\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon\kern 5.0pt\iff\kern 5.0pt\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon.

Example 4.8

(Semigroups)
.

The variety
\mathcal{SG}
of
semigroups
is not coherent. Indeed, even the
n
-generated free semigroups are not coherent for
n\geq 3
; e.g., the subsemigroup of the free semigroup
{\bf{F}}(x,y,z)
generated by
yx
,
yx^{2}
,
x^{3}
,
xz
, and
x^{2}z
is not finitely presented
[
6
]
. However, the
\mathcal{SG}
-dependence problem corresponds precisely to the problem of checking whether a finite subset
X
of a finitely generated free semigroup
{\bf{F}}(\overline{x})
is a
code
, solved by the famous Sardinas-Patterson algorithm
[
33
]
. For finite subsets
Y,Z\subseteq F(\overline{x})
, let
Y^{-1}Z:=\{t\in F(\overline{x})\mid st\in Z;\,s\in Y\}
. The algorithm starts with
U_{0}:=X
and continues iteratively with
U_{n+1}:=X^{-1}U_{n}\cup U^{-1}_{n}X
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It can be proved that
X
is
not
a code if, and only if,
X\cap U_{n}\neq\emptyset
for some
n\geq 1
, and, since there can only be finitely many different
U_{n}
’s, the algorithm is terminating.

Example 4.9

(Modal algebras)
.

Modal algebras
— Boolean algebras with an additional unary operation
\Box
satisfying
\Box(x\land y)\approx\Box x\land\Box y
and
\Box 1=~1
— form a variety
\mathcal{MA}
that provides algebraic semantics for the modal logic
{\sf K}
. This variety is not coherent
[
20
]
, and the coherence of finitely generated free modal algebras seems to be an open problem. Nevertheless,
\mathcal{MA}
-dependence can be decided using a bisimulation-based method given in
[
22
]
for calculating existing right uniform deductive interpolants for the description logic
{\sf ALC}
. It is well known that
{\sf ALC}
restricted to a single role may be viewed as a syntactic variant of the logic
{\sf K}
. Hence to decide
\mathcal{MA}
-dependence, it suffices to observe that modal formulas
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}
(corresponding to terms) are
\mathcal{MA}
-independent if, and only if, the formula
(p_{1}\leftrightarrow\varphi_{1})\land\dots\land(p_{n}\leftrightarrow\varphi_{n})
has a right uniform deductive interpolant with respect to the new variables
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
that is a theorem of
{\sf K}
. This latter claim can be checked using the algorithm described in
[
22
]
.

5
Dependence and minimal provability

In
[
9
]
it is shown that a single formula
\varphi
is
{\sf IPC}
-dependent if, and only if, either
\vdash_{{\sf IPC}}\lnot\lnot\varphi\to\varphi
or
\vdash_{{\sf IPC}}\lnot\lnot\varphi
, and in
[
8
]
a family
(\psi_{i}(p_{1},p_{2}))_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of formulas is given such that two formulas
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are
{\sf IPC}
-dependent if, and only if,
\vdash_{{\sf IPC}}\psi_{i}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
. In both cases, no proper subset of the given set of formulas suffices for checking
{\sf IPC}
-dependence. In this section, we provide a general framework for describing such “minimal provability” results, obtaining a complete description and decidability of dependence for the variety of lattices.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a variety. Given any sets of equations
\Gamma,\Delta
with variables in a set
\overline{y}
, we write
\Gamma\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta
to denote that for any substitution (i.e., homomorphism)
\sigma\colon{\bf{Tm}}(\overline{y})\to{\bf{Tm}}(\omega)
extended to
\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{y})
by
\sigma(s\approx t)=\sigma(s)\approx\sigma(t)
,

\models_{\mathcal{V}}\sigma[\Gamma]\kern 5.0pt\Longrightarrow\quad\models_{\mathcal{V}}\sigma(\delta)\,\text{ for some }\delta\in\Delta.

It is not hard to check (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
) that
\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}
is a (finitary) multiple-conclusion consequence relation over
\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{y})
; that is, for any equation
\varepsilon
and finite sets of equations
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime},\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
with variables in
\overline{y}
,

(i)

\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\{\varepsilon\}

(ii)

if
\Gamma\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta
, then
\Gamma\cup\Gamma^{\prime}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta\cup\Delta^{\prime}

(iii)

if
\Gamma\cup\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta
and
\Gamma^{\prime}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\{\varepsilon\}\cup\Delta^{\prime}
, then
\Gamma\cup\Gamma^{\prime}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta\cup\Delta^{\prime}

(iv)

if
\Gamma\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta
, then
\sigma[\Gamma]\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\sigma[\Delta]
for any substitution
\sigma\colon{\bf{Tm}}(\overline{y})\to{\bf{Tm}}(\overline{y})
.

It is also easy to see that for any set of equations
\Gamma\cup\{\varepsilon\}
with variables in
\overline{y}
,

\Gamma\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon\kern 5.0pt\Longrightarrow\kern 5.0pt\Gamma\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\{\varepsilon\}.

Remark 5.1
.

The relation
\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}
describes the
admissibility
of universal formulas (or multiple-conclusion rules) in the variety
\mathcal{V}
. Indeed, for finite sets of equations
\Gamma,\Delta
, it is the case that
\Gamma\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta
is equivalent to the validity of the implication from the conjunction of the equations in
\Gamma
to the disjunction of the equations in
\Delta
in the free algebra
{\bf{F}}(\omega)
(see, e.g.,
[
5
]
).

Let us call
\Delta\subseteq\mathrm{Eq}(\overline{y})

\mathcal{V}
-refuting
for a set
\overline{y}
if for any equation
\varepsilon(\overline{y})
,

\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon\>\iff\>\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta,

and
minimal
if, additionally, no proper subset of
\Delta
is
\mathcal{V}
-refuting for
\overline{y}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a variety and let
\Delta(\overline{y})
be a
\mathcal{V}
-refuting set of equations for
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
. Then
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
are
\mathcal{V}
-dependent if, and only if,
\models_{\mathcal{V}}\delta(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
for some
\delta\in\Delta
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are
\mathcal{V}
-dependent. Then
\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
and
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon
for some equation
\varepsilon(\overline{y})
. Since
\Delta
is a
\mathcal{V}
-refuting set of equations for
\overline{y}
, also
\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta
. Hence
\models_{\mathcal{V}}\delta(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
for some
\delta\in\Delta
. For the converse, suppose that
\models_{\mathcal{V}}\delta(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
for some
\delta\in\Delta
. Clearly
\{\delta\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta
and hence
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}\delta
, so
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
are
\mathcal{V}
-dependent.
∎

If
\mathcal{V}
has a decidable equational theory and a finite
\mathcal{V}
-refuting set of equations can be found for
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\mathcal{V}
-dependence is clearly decidable. In the case where
\mathcal{V}
is locally finite, a finite
\mathcal{V}
-refuting set of equations
\Delta_{n}
can be obtained for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
by considering all pairs of distinct elements
s,t
from the finite free algebra
{\bf{F}}(\overline{y})
. A minimal
\mathcal{V}
-refuting set for
\overline{y}
can then be obtained by iteratively removing any
\delta\in\Delta_{n}
such that
\{\delta\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}\mathop{\setminus}\{\delta\}
.

Example 5.3
.

Let us illustrate this idea with the simple case of the (locally finite) variety
\mathcal{DL}\mathit{at}
of distributive lattices. It is straightforward to show that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\Delta_{n}:=\big\{\bigwedge_{i\in I}y_{i}\,\leq\!\!\!\bigvee_{j\in[n]{\setminus}I}\!\!\!\!y_{j}\mid\emptyset\neq I\subsetneq[n]\big\}

is a minimal
\mathcal{DL}\mathit{at}
-refuting set of equations for
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
. We first observe that, using distributivity, the set of equations of the form
s\leq t
, where
s
is a join of meets of variables, and
t
is a meet of joins of variables, is
\mathcal{DL}\mathit{at}
-refuting for
\overline{y}
. We then obtain the minimal
\mathcal{DL}\mathit{at}
-refuting set
\Delta_{n}
using the fact that for
i\in\{1,2\}
,

\{s_{1}\lor s_{2}\leq t\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{DL}\mathit{at}}}}\{s_{i}\leq t\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{s\leq t_{1}\land t_{2}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{DL}\mathit{at}}}}\{s\leq t_{i}\}.

It follows that
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
are
\mathcal{DL}\mathit{at}
-dependent if, and only if, for some
\emptyset\neq I\subsetneq[n]
,

\models_{\mathcal{DL}\mathit{at}}\bigwedge_{i\in I}t_{i}\leq\!\!\!\bigvee_{j\in[n]{\setminus}I}\!\!\!\!t_{j}.

We therefore obtain further confirmation that dependence in the variety of distributive lattices is decidable.

(i)

\{x_{1}\leq y,x_{2}\leq y\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}}}\{x_{1}\lor x_{2}\leq y\}

\{x\leq y_{1}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}}}\{x\leq y_{1}\lor y_{2}\}

\{x\leq y_{2}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}}}\{x\leq y_{1}\lor y_{2}\}

(iv)

\{x\leq y_{1},x\leq y_{2}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}}}\{x\leq y_{1}\land y_{2}\}

(v)

\{x\leq y_{1}\land y_{2}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}}}\{x\leq y_{1}\}

\{x\leq y_{1}\land y_{2}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}}}\{x\leq y_{2}\}

\{x_{1}\leq y\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}}}\{x_{1}\land x_{2}\leq y\}

\{x_{2}\leq y\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}}}\{x_{1}\land x_{2}\leq y\}
.

(vii)

\models_{\mathcal{V}}y\leq t_{1}\lor t_{2}\kern 5.0pt\Longrightarrow\>\kern 5.0pt\models_{\mathcal{V}}y\leq t_{1}\,\text{ or }\models_{\mathcal{V}}y\leq t_{2}

\{x_{1}\land x_{2}\leq y_{1}\lor y_{2}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}}}\{x_{1}\leq y_{1}\lor y_{2},\,x_{2}\leq y_{1}\lor y_{2},\,x_{1}\land x_{2}\leq y_{1},\,x_{1}\land x_{2}\leq y_{2}\}.

Theorem 5.4
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the following is a minimal
\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}
-refuting set of equations for
\overline{y}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
:

\Delta_{n}:=\big\{y_{i}\leq\!\!\!\!\!\bigvee_{j\in[n]\setminus\{i\}}\!\!\!\!\!\!y_{j}\mid i\in[n]\big\}\>\cup\>\big\{\!\!\!\!\!\bigwedge_{j\in[n]\setminus\{i\}}\!\!\!\!\!\!y_{j}\leq y_{i}\mid i\in[n]\big\}.

Proof.

Note first that clearly
\not\models_{\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}\delta
for each
\delta\in\Delta_{n}
. Also
\{\delta\}\not\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}\mathop{\setminus}\{\delta\}
for each
\delta\in\Delta_{n}
. E.g., if
\delta
is
y_{1}\leq y_{2}\lor\cdots\lor y_{n}
, let
\sigma
be the substitution mapping
y_{1}
to
(y_{2}\land z)\lor\cdots\lor(y_{n}\land z)
and
y_{i}
to
y_{i}
for
i\in\{2,\dots,n\}
. Then
\models_{\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}\sigma(\delta)
, but
\not\models_{\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}\sigma(\varepsilon)
for each
\varepsilon\in\Delta_{n}
.

Hence it suffices now without loss of generality (since an equation
s\approx t
can always be replaced by inequations
s\leq t
and
t\leq s
) to prove that for any inequation
\varepsilon(\overline{y})
,

\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon\kern 5.0pt\Longrightarrow\kern 5.0pt\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n},

proceeding by induction on the number of symbols in
\varepsilon
. For the base case, observe that if
\varepsilon
is an inequation with one variable on the left and a join of variables on the right, or one variable on the right and a meet of variables on the left, the claim follows directly from the definition of
\Delta_{n}
. For the induction step, we consider the following cases:

(a)

Suppose that
\varepsilon
is
s_{1}\lor s_{2}\leq t
and
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon
. Then, by property (i),
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}s_{1}\leq t
or
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}s_{2}\leq t
, and, by the induction hypothesis,
\{s_{1}\leq t\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
or
\{s_{2}\leq t\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
. By property (ii),
\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\{s_{1}\leq t\}
and
\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\{s_{2}\leq t\}
, so also
\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
.

(b)

Suppose that
\varepsilon
is
s\leq t_{1}\land t_{2}
and
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon
. Then, by property (iv),
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}s\leq t_{1}
or
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}s\leq t_{2}
, and, by the induction hypothesis,
\{s\leq t_{1}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
or
\{s\leq t_{2}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
. By property (v),
\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}s\leq t_{1}
and
\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}s\leq t_{2}
, so also
\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
.

(c)

Suppose that
\varepsilon
is
s_{1}\land s_{2}\leq t_{1}\lor t_{2}
and
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon
. Then, by properties (iii) and (vi),
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}s_{1}\leq t_{1}\lor t_{2}
,
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}s_{2}\leq t_{1}\lor t_{2}
,
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}s_{1}\land s_{2}\leq t_{1}
, and
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}s_{1}\land s_{2}\leq t_{2}
. So, by the induction hypothesis,
\{s_{1}\leq t_{1}\lor t_{2}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
,
\{s_{2}\leq t_{1}\lor t_{2}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
,
\{s_{1}\land s_{2}\leq t_{1}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
, and
\{s_{1}\land s_{2}\leq t_{2}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
. But also
\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\{s_{1}\leq t_{1}\lor t_{2},s_{2}\leq t_{1}\lor t_{2},s_{1}\land s_{2}\leq t_{1},s_{1}\land s_{2}\leq t_{2}\}
, by Whitman’s condition, so
\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
.

(d)

Suppose that
\varepsilon
is (up to a permutation of meets)
(s_{1}\lor s_{2})\land s_{3}\leq y_{i}
and
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon
. Then, by properties (vi) and (i),
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}s_{3}\leq y_{i}
and either
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}s_{1}\leq y_{i}
or
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}s_{2}\leq y_{i}
. Hence, by property (viii), either
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}s_{1}\land s_{3}\leq y_{i}
or
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}s_{2}\land s_{3}\leq y_{i}
, and, by the induction hypothesis, either
\{s_{1}\land s_{3}\leq y_{i}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
or
\{s_{2}\land s_{3}\leq y_{i}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
. By the monotonicity of the lattice operations,
\{(s_{1}\lor s_{2})\land s_{3}\leq y_{i}\}\models_{\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}s_{j}\land s_{3}\leq y_{i}
for
j\in\{1,2\}
, so also
\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\{s_{j}\land s_{3}\leq y_{i}\}
for
j\in\{1,2\}
. Hence
\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
.

(e)

Suppose that
\varepsilon
is (up to a permutation of joins)
y_{i}\leq(t_{1}\land t_{2})\lor t_{3}
and
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon
. Then, by properties (iii) and (v),
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}y_{i}\leq t_{3}
and either
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}y_{i}\leq t_{1}
or
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}y_{i}\leq t_{2}
. Hence
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}y_{i}\leq t_{1}\lor t_{3}
or
\not\models_{\mathcal{V}}y_{i}\leq t_{2}\lor t_{3}
, and, by the induction hypothesis, either
\{y_{i}\leq t_{1}\lor t_{3}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
or
\{y_{i}\leq t_{2}\lor t_{3}\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
. By the monotonicity of the lattice operations,
\{y_{i}\leq(t_{1}\land t_{2})\lor t_{3}\}\models_{\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}y_{i}\leq t_{j}\lor t_{3}
for
j\in\{1,2\}
, so also
\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\{y_{i}\leq t_{j}\lor t_{3}\}
for
j\in\{1,2\}
. Hence
\{\varepsilon\}\mathrel{\makebox{\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\scriptsize$\mid$}\raisebox{1.20554pt}{\footnotesize$\!\sim$}}_{{\sf\mathcal{V}}}}\Delta_{n}
. ∎

Corollary 5.5
.

The terms
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
are
\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}
-dependent if, and only if, for some
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

\models_{\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}t_{i}\leq\!\!\!\!\!\!\bigvee_{j\in[n]{\setminus}\{i\}}\!\!\!\!\!t_{j}\quad\text{or}\quad\models_{\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\bigwedge_{j\in[n]{\setminus}\{i\}}\!\!\!\!\!\!t_{j}\leq t_{i}.

Hence dependence in the variety of lattices is decidable.

6
Open Problems

We conclude with a short list of open problems:

(1)

Proofs of uniform interpolation can be quite intricate. In particular, Pitts’ constructive proof of this property for
{\sf IPC}
involves a complicated definition of left and right interpolants that is checked by induction on derivations in a terminating sequent calculus. It therefore makes sense to seek a simpler proof that
{\sf IPC}
-dependence is decidable. Such a proof might perhaps use the fact that subalgebras of finitely generated free Heyting algebras are projective
[
12
]
and hence that finitely generated subalgebras of finitely generated free Heyting algebras are finitely presented. The challenge would then be to provide a simple algorithm for producing finite presentations of these subalgebras. Similarly, we expect that there should be a more direct proof of decidability for dependence in the variety of modal algebras that does not rely on the bisimulation-based method of
[
22
]
for calculating uniform interpolants.

(2)

It follows from Theorem
5.4
and Whitman’s condition that the minimal
\mathcal{DL}\mathit{at}
-refuting sets of equations described in Example
5.3
also serve as (non-minimal)
\mathcal{L}\mathit{at}
-refuting sets of equations. This raises the question of whether it is the case for
any
variety of lattices
\mathcal{V}
that
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathrm{Tm}(\overline{x})
are
\mathcal{V}
-dependent if, and only if, for some
\emptyset\neq I\subsetneq[n]
,

\models_{\mathcal{V}}\bigwedge_{i\in I}t_{i}\leq\!\!\!\bigvee_{j\in[n]{\setminus}I}\!\!\!\!t_{j}.

Note that it is easily checked (since the free lattice on two generators is finite) that this is true for
n=2
; that is, in any variety
\mathcal{V}
of lattices,
t_{1},t_{2}
are
\mathcal{V}
-dependent if, and only if,
\models_{\mathcal{V}}t_{1}\leq t_{2}
or
\models_{\mathcal{V}}t_{2}\leq t_{1}
.

(3)

For all the varieties considered in this paper, the dependence problem is decidable. It would therefore be interesting to know of an example (if one exists) of a variety with a decidable equational theory for which dependence is undecidable.

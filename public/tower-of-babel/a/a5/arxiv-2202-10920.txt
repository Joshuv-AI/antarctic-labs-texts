Abstract
. We show that the strong cohomological rigidity conjecture for Bott manifolds is true. Namely, any graded cohomology ring isomorphism between two Bott manifolds is induced by a diffeomorphism.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
57S12, (57R19, 57R50)

1.
Introduction

A
Bott tower
of height
n
is a sequence of projective bundles

(1.1)

B_{n}\xrightarrow{\pi_{n}}B_{n-1}\xrightarrow{\pi_{n-1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\pi_{3}}B_{2}\xrightarrow{\pi_{2}}B_{1}\xrightarrow{\pi_{1}}B_{0}=\{\text{a point}\}.

Each
B_{i}
is the projectivization
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus\gamma^{\alpha_{i}})
over
B_{i-1}
, where
\gamma^{\alpha_{i}}
is the complex line bundle over
B_{i-1}
whose first Chern class is
\alpha_{i}
. We call the total space
B_{n}
an
n
-stage
Bott manifold
.

When Grossberg and Karshon
[
8
]
introduced the notion of a Bott tower, they described a Bott manifold as a special member in a one-parameter family of complex manifolds arising from a Bott–Samelson variety
[
1
]
. Bott manifolds carry holomorphic actions of the algebraic torus of maximal dimension and form an important family of compact smooth toric varieties, which we call toric manifolds for short.

One remarkable result in toric topology is that the classification of toric manifolds as varieties is closely related to the equivariant cohomology
[
15
]
.
However, not much is known about the smooth classification.
It was shown by Hirzebruch
[
11
]
that two-stage Bott manifolds, known as
Hirzebruch surfaces
, are diffeomorphic if and only if their cohomology rings are isomorphic as graded rings.
In 2008, Masuda and Panov
[
14
]
showed that if the cohomology of
B_{n}
is isomorphic to that of
(\mathbb{C}P^{1})^{n}
, then
B_{n}
is diffeomorphic to
(\mathbb{C}P^{1})^{n}
.
Motivated by this, the following smooth classification problem was posed in
[
14
,
16
]
(see also
[
2
, Problem 7.8.29]
) for the class of Bott manifolds, and more generally for the class of toric manifolds.

Cohomological rigidity problem for toric manifolds.

Are toric manifolds diffeomorphic if their cohomology rings are isomorphic as graded rings?

There is no evidence so far that the problem gets easier when we instead consider the topological classification.
Although the cohomology ring as an invariant is too weak to determine the diffeomorphism type in general, no counterexamples are known at the time of writing.

By restricting the problem to the class of Bott manifolds, many partial answers were given; see
[
6
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
for example.
Interestingly, some results suggested that an even stronger proposition, called the
strong cohomological rigidity conjecture
, should hold: any cohomology ring isomorphism between two Bott manifolds can be induced by a diffeomorphism.
The conjecture was confirmed to be true for
n\leq 4
so far, whereas it does not hold for general toric manifolds (see
[
7
, Theorem 10]
).

The purpose of the paper is to show that the strong cohomological rigidity conjecture is true for all Bott manifolds of arbitrary dimension
n
. Throughout the paper, we let
H^{\ast}(X)
denote the integral cohomology ring of a topological space
X
.

Theorem 1.1

(Strong cohomological rigidity for Bott manifolds)
.

Let
B_{n}
and
B_{n}^{\prime}
be Bott manifolds.
Given any graded ring isomorphism
\phi\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n})\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}^{\prime})
, there exists a diffeomorphism
\Phi\colon B_{n}^{\prime}\to B_{n}
such that
\Phi^{\ast}=\phi
.

One of the key ingredients for the proof of Theorem
1.1
is Ishida’s theorem (Theorem
2.5
) on the construction of the diffeomorphism. To apply his theorem, one needs to assume that
\phi
is
k
-stable (see Definition
2.2
) for
k=n-2
or
k=n-1
. What we prove in this paper is that, by changing the tower structures of
B_{n}
and
B^{\prime}_{n}
through diffeomorphisms, such assumption can always be made; see Theorem
4.2
for the statement.

Note that an
n
-stage Bott manifold is a toric manifold over an
n
-cube
I^{n}
.
Hasui et al.
[
9
]
studied the cohomological rigidity for toric manifolds over an
n
-cube with one vertex cut, say
vc(I^{n})
, assuming the cohomological rigidity for Bott manifolds.
By Remark in
[
9
, p.4892]
, we have the following non-trivial corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be toric manifolds over
vc(I^{n})
.
If
H^{\ast}(X)\cong H^{\ast}(Y)
as graded rings, then
X
and
Y
are diffeomorphic.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we fix notation that will be used for the rest of the paper and collect some results on the realization of an isomorphism between cohomology rings of Bott manifolds by a diffeomorphism.

2.1.
Notation

Let
B_{n}
be a Bott manifold defined in (
1.1
). By Leray–Hirsch theorem, we have the following presentation of the cohomology ring

(2.1)

H^{\ast}(B_{n})=\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/(x_{i}^{2}-\alpha_{i}x_{i})_{i=1}^{n},

where

(2.2)

\alpha_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}a_{ij}x_{j},\quad a_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}.

Once this presentation is fixed, or equivalently the strictly lower triangular integer matrix
A=(a_{ij})_{n\times n}
is given, we let
B_{n}(A)
denote the Bott tower (
1.1
). By slight abuse of the notation, the symbol
B_{n}(A)
will also denote the Bott manifold
B_{n}
. Define the filtration

(2.3)

F_{k}(A):=\mathrm{span}\{x_{1},\dots x_{k}\}\subset H^{2}(B_{n}(A))

with the convention
F_{0}(A):=\{0\}
. For given
\eta\in H^{2}(B_{n}(A))
, the
height
of
\eta
is defined as

(2.4)

\mathrm{ht}(\eta):=\min\{k\mid\eta\in F_{k}(A)\}.

Remark 2.1
.

The expression (
2.1
) shows that
\{x_{i}x_{j}\}_{i<j}
forms an additive basis for
H^{4}(B_{n}(A))
. In particular, for
\eta_{i}\in H^{2}(B_{n}(A))
with
\mathrm{ht}(\eta_{i})<i
,

(2.5)

\sum_{i}\eta_{i}x_{i}=0\implies\eta_{i}=0\quad\text{for all }i.

Throughout the paper, we will use
A=(a_{ij})
for the source and
B=(b_{ij})
for the target. To avoid confusion, we will use the symbols
y_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
in place of
x_{i}
and
\alpha_{i}
for the matrix
B
.

Definition 2.2
.

A graded ring isomorphism
\phi\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(A))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B))
is called
k
-stable
if
\phi(F_{k}(A))\subset F_{k}(B)
for some
k
.

2.2.
Realizable isomorphisms

We say that an isomorphism between two cohomology rings is
realizable
if it is induced from a diffeomorphism.

Let
B=(b_{ij})
be an
n\times n
matrix defining a Bott tower. In Section
4
, using the following two matrix operations, we will produce a new matrix
B^{\prime}=(b^{\prime}_{ij})
that is better suited for the proof of Theorem
4.2
. We provide the proofs for the reader’s convenience. Following the expression (
2.2
), we will use
\beta_{i}
to express the
i
th row of
B
.

Lemma 2.3

(Switching)
.

Suppose
b_{j+1,j}=0
for some
j
. Let
B^{\prime}
be the matrix obtained from
B
by exchanging the
j
th and the
(j+1)
st rows and columns. Then the isomorphism
g\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B^{\prime}))
defined by

g(y_{j})=y^{\prime}_{j+1},\quad g(y_{j+1})=y^{\prime}_{j},\quad\text{and}\quad g(y_{i})=y^{\prime}_{i}\text{ for }i\neq j,j+1

is realizable.

Proof.

Since
b_{j+1,j}=0
, the line bundle
\gamma^{\beta_{j+1}}
can be regarded as a bundle over
B_{j-1}
. Then the space
B_{j+1}
is the fiber product of
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus\gamma^{\beta_{j}})\to B_{j-1}
and
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus\gamma^{\beta_{j+1}})\to B_{j-1}
. The diffeomorphism is given by switching the two factors of the fiber product.
∎

Lemma 2.4

(Twisting)
.

Suppose there exists
v\in F_{j-1}(B)
satisfying the equation
v(\beta_{j}-v)=0
. Then there exists
B^{\prime}
such that

\beta^{\prime}_{i}=\beta_{i}\text{ for }i<j\quad\text{and}\quad\beta^{\prime}_{j}=\beta_{j}-2v

and a realizable graded ring isomorphism
g\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B^{\prime}))
which is the identity when restricted to
F_{j-1}(B)
.

Proof.

Ishida
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
showed that rank
2
decomposable vector bundles over a Bott manifold are isomorphic if and only if they have the same Chern classes. Since a projective bundle is unchanged under tensoring by a line bundle, we have the following isomorphisms of bundles:

\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus\gamma^{\beta_{j}-2v})\cong\mathbb{P}((\mathbb{C}\oplus\gamma^{\beta_{j}-2v})\otimes\gamma^{v})\cong\mathbb{P}(\gamma^{v}\oplus\gamma^{\beta_{j}-v})\cong\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus\gamma^{\beta_{j}}).

By pulling the bundle
B_{n}(B)\to\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus\gamma^{\beta_{j}})
back by the above diffeomorphism, we obtain a new bundle
B_{n}(B^{\prime})\to\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}\oplus\gamma^{\beta_{j}-2v})
. The desired isomorphism
g
is induced from the bundle isomorphism.
∎

Finally, we introduce the following theorem by Ishida that will be combined with Theorem
4.2
to prove the strong cohomological rigidity.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
12
]
for
k=n-1
and
[
13
]
for
k=n-2
)
.

Suppose that a graded ring isomorphism
\phi\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(A))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B))
is
k
-stable and realizable. Then there exists a diffeomorphism
\Phi\colon B_{n}(B)\to B_{n}(A)
such that
\Phi^{\ast}=\phi
.

3.
\mathbb{Q}
-trivial decomposition

A Bott manifold is called
\mathbb{Q}
-trivial
if its rational cohomology ring is isomorphic to that of the product of the complex projective lines. Such Bott manifolds were introduced and classified in
[
4
]
. In this section, we decompose general Bott towers into
\mathbb{Q}
-trivial ones and study how the decomposition plays a role in the cohomology ring.

3.1.
\mathbb{Q}
-trivial Bott manifolds

We begin with the lemma that classifies the square zero elements of degree
2
in the cohomology ring. Such elements generate the cohomology ring when the Bott manifold is
\mathbb{Q}
-trivial.

Lemma 3.1
.

Any element
z\in H^{2}(B_{n}(A))
satisfying
z^{2}=0
is of the form

z=\lambda(2x_{i}-\alpha_{i})

with
\alpha_{i}^{2}=0
for some
i
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

For any
z\neq 0
, write
z=\lambda(2x_{i}+u)
for some nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
,
i>0
and
u\in H^{2}(B_{n}(A))
with
\mathrm{ht}(u)<i
. Since
\mathrm{ht}(x_{i})=i
, it follows from Remark
2.1
and the expression (
2.1
) that
u=-\alpha_{i}
and
u^{2}=0
.
∎

Given an integer
j\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{H}_{j}
denote the Bott tower determined by the following
j\times j
matrix

(3.1)

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\dots&0\\
1&0&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&0&\dots&0\end{pmatrix}.

By the classification result of
\mathbb{Q}
-trivial Bott manifolds
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
,

(3.2)

B_{n}(A)\cong\prod_{i=1}^{t}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{i}}

for some partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{t})
of
n
when
B_{n}(A)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-trivial.

3.2.
Towers of
\mathbb{Q}
-trivial manifolds

Given a matrix
A
defining a Bott tower, one can construct a new one by taking a submatrix of A.
For any integer
1\leq k<n
, let
\hat{A}
and
\bar{A}
denote the
k\times k
upper left matrix and the
(n-k)\times(n-k)
lower right matrix, respectively.
Explicitly,

(3.3)

\hat{A}_{ij}:=a_{ij}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{A}_{ij}:=a_{i+k,j+k}.

The Bott manifold
B_{n}(A)
can be geometrically interpreted as a fiber bundle over
B_{k}(\hat{A})
with fiber
B_{n-k}(\bar{A})
.
The definition of
\hat{A}
and
\bar{A}
depends on the choice of
k
, but we will omit it from the notation when it is clear from the context.

By applying the switching operations (Lemma
2.3
) if necessary, we may, hence will always, assume that a Bott tower
B_{n}(A)
is well-ordered (see
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
).
That is,

\alpha_{j}^{2}=0\implies\alpha_{i}^{2}=0\quad\text{for }i<j.

Let
k:=\max\{i\mid\alpha_{i}^{2}=0\}
. Then the space
B_{n}(A)
has a fibration structure

(3.4)

B_{n-k}(\bar{A})\to B_{n}(A)\to B_{k}(\hat{A}).

Since
2x_{i}-\alpha_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,k
generates
H^{\ast}(B_{k}(\hat{A}),\mathbb{Q})
, the base space
B_{k}(\hat{A})
is
\mathbb{Q}
-trivial.
By repeating this process for
B_{n-k}(\bar{A})
, we obtain a tower

(3.5)

Q_{m}\xrightarrow{p_{m}}Q_{m-1}\xrightarrow{p_{m-1}}\dots\xrightarrow{p_{2}}Q_{1}=B_{k}(\hat{A})\xrightarrow{p_{1}}Q_{0}=\{\text{a point}\}

whose fibers are
\mathbb{Q}
-trivial Bott manifolds. Since each
p_{i}
induces an injection on the cohomology ring, we will regard
H^{2}(Q_{i})
as a subgroup of
H^{2}(Q_{m})
.

Definition 3.2
.

The
level
of
\eta\in H^{2}(B_{n}(A))
is defined to be

(3.6)

\mathrm{lev}(\eta):=\min\{j\mid\eta\in H^{2}(Q_{j})\}.

By the above construction of
\mathbb{Q}
-trivial towers, we have

(3.7)

\mathrm{ht}(\eta)\leq\mathrm{ht}(\xi)\implies\mathrm{lev}(\eta)\leq\mathrm{lev}(\xi).

We will always assume this throughout the paper. Since a graded ring isomorphism preserves the set of square zero elements, the following lemma follows from induction (cf.
[
5
, Proposition 4.1]
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\phi\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(A))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B))
be a graded ring isomorphism. Then there exists a permutation
\sigma\colon[n]\to[n]
such that

(3.8)

\phi(2x_{i}-\alpha_{i})=\epsilon_{i}(2y_{\sigma(i)}-\beta_{\sigma(i)})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{lev}(x_{i})=\mathrm{lev}(y_{\sigma(i)})

for some
\epsilon_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
and
i=1,\dots,n
. In particular, the isomorphism
\phi
is
k
-stable if
k=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Q_{j}
for some
j=0,\dots,m
.

3.3.
Blocks

Let
B_{n}(A)
be a Bott tower well-ordered in the sense of (
3.7
). Pick
i
and
j
so that
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
have the same level, say
\ell
. The quotient
H^{\ast}(Q_{\ell})/H^{\ast}(Q_{\ell-1})
is isomorphic to the cohomology ring of a
\mathbb{Q}
-trivial Bott manifold. Fix a graded ring isomorphism

(3.9)

\psi\colon H^{\ast}(Q_{\ell})/H^{\ast}(Q_{\ell-1})\to\prod_{i}H^{\ast}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{i}}).

Definition 3.4
.

Two numbers
i
and
j
are said to be
in the same block for
A
if
\mathrm{lev}(x_{i})=\mathrm{lev}(x_{j})
and there exists
k
such that both
\psi(2x_{i}-\alpha_{i})
and
\psi(2x_{j}-\alpha_{j})
are elements of
H^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{k})
.

We remark that this definition is independent of the choice of
\psi
because any graded automorphism of
\prod_{i}H^{\ast}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{i}})
must send
H^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{j}})
isomorphically onto
H^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda_{j^{\prime}}})
for some
j^{\prime}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\phi\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(A))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B))
be a graded ring isomorphism. Let
\sigma\colon[n]\to[n]
be the permutation defined by the equation (
3.8
). Then
i
and
j
are in the same block for
A
if and only if
\sigma(i)
and
\sigma(j)
are in the same block for
B
.

Next, we characterize the condition when
i
and
j
are in the same block for
A
in terms of the entries of the matrix. Let
k=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Q_{\ell-1}
for some
\ell\geq 1
. For each
r>k
, let
z_{r}
denote the image of
2x_{r}-\alpha_{r}
under the projection

(3.10)

H^{2}(B_{n}(A))\to H^{2}(B_{n-k}(\bar{A})),

divided by
2
when it is not primitive. That is,
z_{r}
is primitive and
z_{r}^{2}=0
when
\mathrm{lev}(x_{r})=\ell
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Pick
i,j>k
so that
\mathrm{lev}(x_{i})=\mathrm{lev}(x_{j})=\ell
. Then
i
and
j
are in the same block for
A
if and only if
z_{i}\equiv z_{j}\mod 2
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, expression (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), the image of a primitive square zero element under (
3.9
) must be of the form
\pm x_{r}
or
\pm(2x_{s}-x_{r})
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
A
be a matrix defining a Bott tower with
a_{j+1,j}
odd.

(1)

If
\mathrm{lev}(x_{j})=\mathrm{lev}(x_{j+1})
, then
j
and
j+1
are in the same block.

(2)

i
and
j+1
are not in the same block for any
i<j
.

Proof.

Let
\ell=\mathrm{lev}(x_{j})
. Let
\bar{x}_{r}
and
\bar{\alpha}_{r}
denote the image of
x_{r}
and
\alpha_{r}
under (
3.10
). By Lemma
3.1
, we have
\bar{\alpha}_{j+1}=a_{j+1,j}(\bar{x}_{j}-\bar{\alpha}_{j}/2)
. This implies that
\bar{\alpha}_{j}
is even. Since
z_{j}=\bar{x}_{j}-\bar{\alpha}_{j}/2
and
z_{j+1}=2\bar{x}_{j+1}-\bar{\alpha}_{j+1}
, the first statement follows from Lemma
3.6
. The second statement also follows from Lemma
3.6
.
∎

4.
Proof

The following is the key lemma to prove our main theorem.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\phi\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(A))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B))
be a
k
-stable isomorphism. Let
\ell>k+1
be the smallest integer satisfying the condition
\phi(x_{k+1})\in F_{\ell}(B)
. Set
p:=b_{\ell,\ell-1}
. Depending on the parity of
p
, we can find an
n\times n
matrix
B^{\prime}
and a
k
-stable realizable graded isomorphism
g\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B^{\prime}))
satisfying the following properties.

(1)

If
p
is even, then
(g\circ\phi)(x_{k+1})\in F_{\ell-1}(B^{\prime})
. The
i
th row of
B^{\prime}
coincides with that of
B
for
i<\ell-1
.

(2)

If
p
is odd, then
(g\circ\phi)(x_{k+1})\in F_{\ell-1}(B^{\prime})
provided that
\ell>k+2
. The
i
th row of
B^{\prime}
coincides with that of
B
for
i<\ell-2
.

Proof.

We will derive properties that hold regardless of the parity of
p
and divide the cases later when needed. For each
i>k
, let

(4.1)

\bar{\beta}_{i}:=\sum_{j=k+1}^{i-1}b_{ij}y_{j}\in H^{2}(B_{n}(B))

denote the class obtained from
\beta_{i}
by truncating the terms in
F_{k}(B)
. Since
\phi
is
k
-stable, the image of
\alpha_{k}
under
\phi
is contained in
F_{k}(B)
. Using Lemma
3.3
and the assumption that
\ell
is the smallest integer satisfying the condition
\phi(x_{k+1})\in F_{\ell}(B)
, we may write

(4.2)

\phi(x_{k+1})=\epsilon(2y_{\ell}-\bar{\beta}_{\ell})+w

for some nonzero
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Q}
and
w\in F_{k}(B)
. If
p=0
, the proof is complete by applying Lemma
2.3
for
j=\ell-1
.

We assume
p\neq 0
from now on. If follows from (
2.1
) and (
4.2
) that

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\phi\left(x_{k+1}(x_{k+1}-\alpha_{k+1})\right)=(2\epsilon y_{\ell}-\epsilon\bar{\beta}_{\ell}+w)(2\epsilon y_{\ell}-\epsilon\bar{\beta}_{\ell}+w-\phi(\alpha_{k+1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\epsilon y_{\ell}(2\epsilon y_{\ell}-2\epsilon\bar{\beta}_{\ell}+2w-\phi(\alpha_{k+1}))

\displaystyle+\epsilon\bar{\beta}_{\ell}(\epsilon\bar{\beta}_{\ell}-2w+\phi(\alpha_{k+1}))

\displaystyle+w(w-\phi(\alpha_{k+1})).

We can remove all the square terms using the relation
y_{i}^{2}=\beta_{i}y_{i}
. From the expression (
4.1
) and the condition that
w,\phi(\alpha_{k+1})\in F_{k}(B)
, all the three terms in the last expression are zero by Remark
2.1
. By setting

(4.3)

u:=\frac{-2w+\phi(\alpha_{k+1})}{\epsilon},

the first two terms lead to the relations

(4.4)

\beta_{\ell}=\bar{\beta}_{\ell}+\frac{-2w+\phi(\alpha_{k+1})}{2\epsilon}=\bar{\beta}_{\ell}+\frac{u}{2}

and

(4.5)

\bar{\beta}_{\ell}\left(\bar{\beta}_{\ell}+u\right)=\bar{\beta}_{\ell}\left(\bar{\beta}_{\ell}+\frac{-2w+\phi(\alpha_{k+1})}{\epsilon}\right)=0.

Since
x_{k+1}^{2}
is contained in the ideal generated by
F_{k}(A)
in
H^{\ast}(B_{n}(A))
, using the equation (
4.2
) and the assumption that
\phi
is
k
-stable, we see that
(2y_{\ell}-\bar{\beta}_{\ell})^{2}
is contained in the ideal generated by
F_{k}(B)
. By the classification of the square zero elements (Lemma
3.1
), we see that

(4.6)

\bar{\beta}_{\ell}=p\left(y_{\ell-1}-\frac{\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}}{2}\right).

We divide the cases by the parity of
p
.

Case 1
:
p
is even.

By (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we have

(4.7)

\bar{\beta}_{\ell}(2\beta_{\ell}-\bar{\beta}_{\ell})=0.

Substituting (
4.6
) into (
4.7
), it follows that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left(y_{\ell-1}-\frac{\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}}{2}\right)\left(2\beta_{\ell}-py_{\ell-1}+\frac{p}{2}\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}\right)

\displaystyle=y_{\ell-1}(2\beta_{\ell}-py_{\ell-1})-\frac{\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}}{2}\left(2\beta_{\ell}-2py_{\ell-1}+\frac{p}{2}\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}\right)

\displaystyle=2y_{\ell-1}\left(\beta_{\ell}-\frac{p}{2}y_{\ell-1}\right)-\frac{\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}}{2}\left(u-\frac{p}{2}\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}\right),

where the last equality follows from (
4.4
) and (
4.6
). Since
\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}
has no
y_{\ell-1}
term and
u\in F_{k}(B)
, both terms in the last expression vanish. Hence,

\frac{p}{2}y_{\ell-1}\left(\beta_{\ell}-\frac{p}{2}y_{\ell-1}\right)=0.

By applying Lemma
2.4
for
j=\ell
and
v=py_{\ell-1}/2
, we can find a new matrix
B^{\prime}=(b^{\prime}_{ij})
with
b^{\prime}_{\ell,\ell-1}=0
. This reduces the proof to the case
p=0
.

Case 2
:
p
is odd.

Substituting (
4.6
) into (
4.5
), we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left(y_{\ell-1}-\frac{\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}}{2}\right)\left(y_{\ell-1}-\frac{\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}}{2}+\frac{u}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=y_{\ell-1}\left(y_{\ell-1}-\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}+\frac{u}{p}\right)+\frac{\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}}{2}\left(\frac{\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}}{2}-\frac{u}{p}\right).

By the similar reasoning as before, it follows that

(4.8)

\beta_{\ell-1}=\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}-\frac{u}{p}

and

(4.9)

\frac{\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}}{2}\left(\frac{\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}}{2}-\frac{u}{p}\right)=0.

Since
p
is odd, the class
\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}
is divisible by
2
by (
4.6
). By taking
v:=\bar{\beta}_{\ell-1}/2
, we obtain
v(\beta_{\ell-1}-v)=0
from (
4.8
) and (
4.9
). By Lemma
2.4
, we obtain a new matrix
B^{\prime}=(b^{\prime}_{ij})
with

(4.10)

b^{\prime}_{\ell-1,k+1}=b^{\prime}_{\ell-1,k+2}=\dots=b^{\prime}_{\ell-1,\ell-2}=0.

The assumption that
\ell>k+2
implies that (
4.10
) is not vacuous. Note that
b^{\prime}_{\ell,\ell-2}
must be zero since
(\bar{\beta}^{\prime}_{\ell})^{2}
is contained in the ideal generated by
F_{k}(B^{\prime})
.
Applying Lemma
2.3
for
j=\ell-2
, we obtain a new matrix
B^{\prime\prime}=(b^{\prime\prime}_{ij})
satisfying

(4.11)

b^{\prime\prime}_{\ell,\ell-1}=b^{\prime}_{\ell,\ell-2}=0.

This reduces the proof to the case
p=0
and the proof is complete. Note that we used Lemma
2.3
for
j=\ell-2
and
\ell-1
, and Lemma
2.4
for
j=\ell-1
, so the
i
th row for
i<\ell-2
is unchanged.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
k<n
be the largest integer such that the graded ring isomorphism
\phi\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(A))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B))
is
k
-stable. Then there exist
n\times n
matrices
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
, and realizable graded ring isomorphisms

f\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(A^{\prime}))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(A))\quad\text{and}\quad g\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B^{\prime}))

such that the composition
\phi^{\prime}:=g\circ\phi\circ f
is either
(k+1)
or
(k+2)
-stable. In particular, if
\phi
is realizable, so is
\phi^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Pick smallest integer
\ell
such that
\phi(x_{k+1})\in F_{\ell}(B)
. If
\ell=k+1
, there is nothing to prove.

For
\ell>k+1
, by applying Lemma
4.1
repeatedly, we can find
B^{\prime}
and
g\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B^{\prime}))
satisfying

(4.12)

(g\circ\phi)(x_{k+1})\in F_{k+2}(B^{\prime}).

If
b^{\prime}_{k+2,k+1}
is even, the proof is complete by Lemma
4.1
.

Now assume that
b^{\prime}_{k+2,k+1}
is odd. From the choice of
k
and Lemma
3.3
, it follows that
\mathrm{lev}(y^{\prime}_{k+1})=\mathrm{lev}(y^{\prime}_{k+2})
. By Corollary
3.7
, we see that
k+1
and
k+2
are in the same block for
B^{\prime}
.

Next, we apply Lemma
4.1
using

(g\circ\phi)^{-1}\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B^{\prime}))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(A))

while keeping the
(k+1)
st row of
A
unchanged. Then we find
A^{\prime}
and
f^{-1}\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(A))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(A^{\prime}))
such that

(4.13)

(g\circ\phi\circ f)^{-1}(y^{\prime}_{k+1})\in F_{k+3}(A^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad(g\circ\phi\circ f)^{-1}(y^{\prime}_{k+2})\in F_{k+1}(A^{\prime}).

If the image of
y^{\prime}_{k+1}
has height
k+2
, the proof is complete. So we assume the height is
k+3
. Since
k+1
and
k+2
are in the same block for
B^{\prime}
, Lemma
3.5
shows that
k+1
and
k+3
are in the same block for
A^{\prime}
. By Corollary
3.7
, the entry
a^{\prime}_{k+3,k+2}
must be even. By applying Lemma
4.1
once again, we are done.
∎

Theorem 4.3

(Strong cohomological rigidity for Bott manifolds)
.

Given any graded ring isomorphism
\phi\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(A))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B))
, there exists a diffeomorphism
\Phi\colon B_{n}(B)\to B_{n}(A)
such that
\Phi^{\ast}=\phi
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
n
. Assume that the statement holds for all Bott towers of height less than
n
. Let
\phi\colon H^{\ast}(B_{n}(A))\to H^{\ast}(B_{n}(B))
be a graded ring isomorphism. Take the largest
k<n
such that
\phi
is
k
-stable and let
\phi_{k}
denote the restriction of
\phi
. By applying Theorem
4.2
repeatedly and replacing
A
,
B
,
\phi
with
A^{\prime}
,
B^{\prime}
,
\phi^{\prime}
, we may assume that
k=n-2
or
k=n-1
. Now the proof is complete by Theorem
2.5
.
∎

Abstract
. We present two types of the hydrodynamic limit of the nonlinear Schrödinger-Chern-Simons (SCS) system. We consider two different scalings of the SCS system and show that each SCS system asymptotically converges towards the compressible and incompressible Euler system, coupled with the Chern-Simons equations and Poisson equation respectively, as the scaled Planck constant converges to 0. Our method is based on the modulated energy estimate. In the case of compressible limit, we observe that the classical theory of relative entropy method can be applied to show the hydrodynamic limit, with the additional quantum correction term. On the other hand, for the incompressible limit, we directly estimate the modulated energy to derive the desired asymptotic convergence.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the hydrodynamic limit of the Schrödinger-Chern-Simons system with the general potential:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\textup{i}\hbar D_{0}\psi+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}(D_{1}D_{1}\psi+D_{2}D_{2}\psi)-V^{\prime}(|\psi|^{2})\psi=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega,\\
&\partial_{0}A_{1}-\partial_{1}A_{0}=-\hbar\textup{Im}(\overline{\psi}D_{2}\psi),\quad\partial_{0}A_{2}-\partial_{2}A_{0}=\hbar\textup{Im}(\overline{\psi}D_{1}\psi),\\
&\partial_{1}A_{2}-\partial_{2}A_{1}=-m|\psi|^{2},\end{aligned}

subject to initial data

(\psi,A_{\mu})|_{t=0}=(\psi_{\textup{in}},A_{\mu,in}),

where
\textup{i}:=\sqrt{-1}
,
\partial_{0}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
,
\partial_{i}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
,
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
is a complex scalar field,
m
is the mass of the particle,
A_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is the gauge field and
V
is the potential energy density of the fields. Here, the spatial domain is either the whole domain
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}
or the periodic domain
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Also,
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+\frac{\textup{i}}{\hbar}A_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative for
\mu=0,1,2
. The SCS system (
1.1
) proposed in
[
5
,
10
,
11
]
consists of the Schrödinger equation augmented by the gauge field
A_{\mu}
to study vortex solitons in a nonrelativistic (2+1)-dimensional Chern-Simons gauge theory. It is well-known that the SCS system (
1.1
) is invariant under the following gauge transformation:

\psi\to\psi e^{\textup{i}\chi},\quad A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}-\hbar\partial_{\mu}\chi.

Therefore, to determine the gauge field, one need to give an extra condition on the gauge field. In many cases, the following Coulomb gauge condition is assumed:

\nabla\cdot A=\partial_{1}A_{1}+\partial_{2}A_{2}=0,\quad A:=(A_{1},A_{2}).

Under the Coulomb gauge condition, the Cauchy problem for the SCS system (
1.1
) is equivalent to the following system:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\textup{i}\hbar\partial_{t}\psi-A_{0}\psi+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\left(\Delta\psi+\frac{2\textup{i}}{\hbar}A\cdot\nabla\psi-\frac{1}{\hbar^{2}}|A|^{2}\psi\right)-V^{\prime}(|\psi|^{2})\psi=0,\\
&\Delta A_{0}=\hbar\textup{Im}(Q_{12}(\overline{\psi},\psi))+\partial_{1}(A_{2}|\psi|^{2})-\partial_{2}(A_{1}|\psi|^{2}),\\
&\Delta A_{1}=m\partial_{2}|\psi|^{2},\quad\Delta A_{2}=-m\partial_{1}|\psi|^{2},\end{aligned}

where
Q_{12}(\overline{\psi},\psi):=\partial_{1}\overline{\psi}\partial_{2}\psi-\partial_{2}\overline{\psi}\partial_{1}\psi
, when the initial data
(\psi_{\textup{in}},A_{\textup{in}})
satisfy the following compatibility conditions

(1.3)

\partial_{1}A_{2,\textup{in}}-\partial_{2}A_{1,\textup{in}}=-m|\psi_{\textup{in}}|^{2},\quad\nabla\cdot A_{\textup{in}}=0.

We choose
m=1
and
\hbar=\varepsilon
in (
1.2
) to obtain the following scaled SCS equation:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\textup{i}\varepsilon\partial_{t}\psi^{\varepsilon}-A^{\varepsilon}_{0}\psi^{\varepsilon}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\Delta\psi^{\varepsilon}+\textup{i}\varepsilon A^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}|A^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\psi^{\varepsilon}-V^{\prime}(|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2})\psi^{\varepsilon}=0,\\
&\Delta A^{\varepsilon}_{0}=\varepsilon\textup{Im}(Q_{12}(\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}},\psi^{\varepsilon}))+\partial_{1}(A^{\varepsilon}_{2}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2})-\partial_{2}(A^{\varepsilon}_{1}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}),\\
&\Delta A^{\varepsilon}_{1}=\partial_{2}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2},\quad\Delta A^{\varepsilon}_{2}=-\partial_{1}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2},\end{aligned}

subject to the initial data
(\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\textup{in}},A^{\varepsilon}_{\textup{in}})
satisfying (
1.3
). We remind that, the compatibility condition (
1.3
) propagates along the system
\eqref{Chern-Simons-Schrodinger}_{1,2}
:

(1.5)

\nabla\times A^{\varepsilon}=\partial_{1}A^{\varepsilon}_{2}-\partial_{2}A^{\varepsilon}_{1}=-|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2},\quad\nabla\cdot A^{\varepsilon}=0,\quad t\geq 0,

and the additional relations correspond to
\eqref{Chern-Simons-Schrodinger}_{2}
also hold:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}A^{\varepsilon}_{1}-\partial_{1}A^{\varepsilon}_{0}=-\varepsilon\textup{Im}(\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}D^{\varepsilon}_{2}\psi^{\varepsilon}),\quad\partial_{t}A^{\varepsilon}_{2}-\partial_{2}A^{\varepsilon}_{0}=\varepsilon\textup{Im}(\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}D^{\varepsilon}_{1}\psi^{\varepsilon}).\end{aligned}

The main concern of the paper is the hydrodynamic limit of (
1.4
) in the semiclassical regime, i.e., when
\hbar=\varepsilon\to 0
. We consider two different scaling regimes, each of which leads to the compressible and incompressible Euler-type equations as an asymptotic system. We first focus on the hydrodynamic limit towards the compressible Euler equations. In this case, we consider the special family of the potential
V(\rho):=\frac{1}{\gamma}\rho^{\gamma}
with
\gamma\geq 1
. This choice of the potential covers the standard quadratic potential
V(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2}
. To identify the asymptotic hydrodynamic system, we consider the following Madelung transformation
[
23
]
:

\psi^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)}\exp\left(\frac{\textup{i}}{\varepsilon}S^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\right).

Then, the hydrodynamic quantities
\rho^{\varepsilon}=|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}
and
u^{\varepsilon}:=\nabla S^{\varepsilon}+A^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\textup{i}\varepsilon}{2|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}(\psi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}-\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon})+A^{\varepsilon}
satisfy the following compressible Euler-type equations coupled with the Chern-Simons equations:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho^{\varepsilon}+\nabla\cdot(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})+\nabla\cdot(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}\otimes u^{\varepsilon})+\nabla p(\rho^{\varepsilon})=\rho^{\varepsilon}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\nabla\left(\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}{\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}\right),\\
&\Delta A^{\varepsilon}_{0}=\nabla\times(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}),\quad\Delta A^{\varepsilon}=-(\nabla\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\perp},\end{aligned}

subject to initial data
(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{\textup{in}},A^{\varepsilon}_{\textup{in}})
satisfying the compatibility condition

\displaystyle\nabla\times A^{\varepsilon}_{\textup{in}}=-\rho^{\varepsilon}_{\textup{in}},\quad\nabla\cdot A^{\varepsilon}_{\textup{in}}=0.

Here,
p(\rho):=\rho V^{\prime}(\rho)-V(\rho)=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\rho^{\gamma}
is a pressure and
u^{\perp}:=(-u_{2},u_{1})
. The density-dependent source term in the momentum equation (
1.7
)
2
is so-called the Bohm potential, which is exactly the same as
\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\nabla\cdot(\rho^{\varepsilon}\nabla^{2}\log\rho^{\varepsilon})

and this term is also understood as a quantum pressure tensor. We refer Section
2.2.1
for the detailed procedure to derive (
1.7
). Heuristically, the hydrodynamic equations (
1.7
) converges to the following compressible Euler-Chern-Simons (ECS) system as
\varepsilon\to 0
:

(1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\nabla\cdot(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla p(\rho)=0,\\
&\Delta A_{0}=\nabla\times(\rho u),\quad\Delta A=-(\nabla\rho)^{\perp},\end{aligned}

subject to initial data
(\rho_{\textup{in}},A_{\textup{in}})
satisfying the compatibility condition

(1.9)

\nabla\times A_{\textup{in}}=-\rho_{\textup{in}},\quad\nabla\cdot A_{\textup{in}}=0.

The first goal of the paper is to obtain the rigorous convergence from (
1.7
) to (
1.8
).

On the other hand, for the case of incompressible limit, we further scale the equation (
1.4
) by taking
t\to\varepsilon^{\alpha}t
,
A_{0}^{\varepsilon}\to\varepsilon^{\alpha}A_{0}^{\varepsilon}
for
0<\alpha<1
and
A^{\varepsilon}\to\varepsilon^{-\alpha}A^{\varepsilon}
and consider another special family of potential
V(\rho)=\frac{1}{\gamma}(\rho^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}-1)^{2}
with
\gamma\geq 2
. Then, the rescaled SCS system becomes:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\textup{i}\varepsilon^{\alpha+1}\partial_{t}\psi^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{\alpha}A_{0}^{\varepsilon}\psi^{\varepsilon}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\Delta\psi^{\varepsilon}+\textup{i}\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}A^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}-\frac{\varepsilon^{-2\alpha}}{2}|A^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\psi^{\varepsilon}-(|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{\gamma}-1)|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{\gamma-2}\psi^{\varepsilon}=0,\\
&\Delta A_{0}^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}\textup{Im}(Q_{12}(\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}},\psi^{\varepsilon}))+\varepsilon^{-2\alpha}\partial_{1}(A_{2}^{\varepsilon}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2})-\varepsilon^{-2\alpha}\partial_{2}(A_{1}^{\varepsilon}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}),\\
&\Delta A^{\varepsilon}_{1}=\varepsilon^{\alpha}\partial_{2}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2},\quad\Delta A^{\varepsilon}_{2}=-\varepsilon^{\alpha}\partial_{1}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2},\end{aligned}

Again, considering the same Madelung transformation and introduce the hydrodynamic quantities
\rho^{\varepsilon}:=|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}
and
u^{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon^{-\alpha}\nabla S^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{-2\alpha}A^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\textup{i}\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}{2|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}(\psi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}-\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon^{-2\alpha}A^{\varepsilon}
, one can derive the similar Euler-type equations coupled with Chern-Simons equations:

(1.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho^{\varepsilon}+\nabla\cdot(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})+\nabla\cdot(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}\otimes u^{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon^{-2\alpha}\nabla p(\rho^{\varepsilon})=\rho^{\varepsilon}\frac{\varepsilon^{2-2\alpha}}{2}\nabla\left(\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}{\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}\right),\\
&\Delta A^{\varepsilon}_{0}=\nabla\times(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}),\quad\Delta A^{\varepsilon}=-\varepsilon^{\alpha}(\nabla\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\perp},\end{aligned}

where the pressure is now
p(\rho)=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\rho^{\gamma}-\frac{\gamma-2}{\gamma}\rho^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}
. Heuristically, it follows from (
1.11
)
2
that
\nabla p(\rho^{\varepsilon})=\nabla\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}-\frac{\gamma-2}{\gamma}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\right)
is dominating term, and therefore, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we may guess
\rho^{\varepsilon}
converges to constant. Consequently, an appropriate limit system for (
1.11
) would be the following incompressible Euler equations coupled with the Poisson equation:

(1.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla\pi=0,\quad\nabla\cdot u=0,\\
&\Delta A_{0}=\nabla\times u.\end{aligned}

The second goal of the paper is the rigorous derivation of (
1.12
) from (
1.11
).

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present the preliminaries for the Schrödinger-Chern-Simons equations and its hydrodynamic formulation. We also state the main theorem of the paper in this section. Section
3
provides the relation between two concepts, the relative entropy used in the kinetic theory and the modulated energy used in the quantum theory. Indeed, we show that they are almost equivalent concepts, except for the quantum correction term. Then, we provide the complete proofs of Theorem
2.1
in Section 4, using the classical relative entropy method and the relation between modulated energy and the relative entropy. In Section 5, we provide the proof of Theorem
2.2
by directly estimating modulated energy. Finally, Section
6
is devoted to a summary and future perspectives.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present the basic conservation laws for SCS equations (
1.4
) and (
1.10
), and identify the asymptotic hydrodynamic equations. We also provide the main theorem of the paper at the end of the section.

2.1.
Conservation laws

One of the most important properties of the SCS system (
1.1
) is the conservation of charge and energy.
One can obtain the conservation laws for SCS equations (
1.4
) and (
1.10
), respectively, using the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(\psi,A_{0},A)
be a smooth solution to the SCS system (
1.1
) subject to the initial data
(\psi_{\textup{in}},A_{\textup{in}})
. Then, the total charge and the total energy are conserved:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Omega}|\psi(t,x)|^{2}\,\textup{d}x=0,\quad\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}|D_{j}\psi(t,x)|^{2}+V(|\psi(t,x)|^{2})\,\textup{d}x=0.\end{aligned}

Proof.

We first note that the covariant derivative satisfies the following calculation rules:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}(\bar{\phi}\psi)=\bar{\phi}D_{\mu}\psi+\overline{D_{\mu}\phi}\psi,\quad D_{\mu}D_{\nu}\psi=D_{\nu}D_{\mu}\psi+\frac{i}{\hbar}(\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu})\psi.

\bullet
(Estimate for charge): We multiply (
1.1
) by
\bar{\psi}
and take its imaginary part to derive

(2.2)

\displaystyle\hbar\partial_{t}|\psi|^{2}+\hbar^{2}\sum_{j=1}\partial_{j}\mbox{Im}(\bar{\psi}D_{j}\psi)=0.

By integrating (
2.2
) over
\Omega
, we obtain the desired result.

\bullet
(Estimate for energy): Similarly, we multiply (
1.1
) by
\overline{D_{0}\psi}
and consider its real part to observe

(2.3)

\displaystyle\hbar^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\partial_{j}\mbox{Re}(\overline{D_{0}\psi}D_{j}\psi)-\hbar^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\mbox{Re}(\overline{D_{j}D_{0}\psi}D_{j}\psi)-\partial_{t}\big(V(|\psi|^{2})\big)=0.

We use (
2.1
) and
\eqref{Chern-Simons-Schrodinger}_{2}
to estimate the second term as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{j=1}^{2}\mbox{Re}(\overline{D_{j}D_{0}\psi}D_{j}\psi)=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\Big(\mbox{Re}(\overline{D_{0}D_{j}\psi}D_{j}\psi)+\frac{1}{\hbar}(\partial_{0}A_{j}-\partial_{j}A_{0})\mbox{Im}(\bar{\psi}D_{j}\psi)\Big)=\partial_{t}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{1}{2}|D_{j}\psi|^{2}.\end{aligned}

Inserting the above identity to (
2.3
) and integrating it over
\Omega
, we conclude the desired estimate.
∎

We define natural energy functions for the scaled SCS system (
1.4
) and (
1.10
), respectively, as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}|D_{j}^{\varepsilon}\psi^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}+V(|\psi^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}):=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|D^{\varepsilon}\psi^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2\gamma},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\varepsilon^{2-2\alpha}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}|\widetilde{D}^{\varepsilon}_{j}\psi^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}+\varepsilon^{-2\alpha}\widetilde{V}(|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}):=\frac{\varepsilon^{2-2\alpha}}{2}|\widetilde{D}^{\varepsilon}\psi^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{-2\alpha}}{\gamma}(|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{\gamma}-1)^{2},

where
D^{\varepsilon}_{j}:=\partial_{j}+\frac{\textup{i}}{\varepsilon}A^{\varepsilon}_{j}
and
\widetilde{D}_{j}^{\varepsilon}:=\partial_{j}+\frac{\textup{i}}{\varepsilon^{1+\alpha}}A_{j}^{\varepsilon}
. By directly applying Lemma
2.1
to each scaled SCS system (
1.4
) and (
1.10
) respectively, one can obtain the following conservation laws, written in terms of the corresponding total charge and energy. We omit the detailed proof.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(\psi^{\varepsilon},A^{\varepsilon}_{0},A^{\varepsilon})
be a global solution to the SCS equations (
1.4
) subject to the initial data
(\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\textup{in}},A^{\varepsilon}_{\textup{in}})
. Then, the total charge and the total energy are conserved:

\int_{\Omega}|\psi^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\,\textup{d}x=\int_{\Omega}|\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\textup{in}}(x)|^{2}\,\textup{d}x,\quad\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\textup{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(0,x)\,\textup{d}x,\quad t\geq 0.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(\psi^{\varepsilon},A^{\varepsilon}_{0},A^{\varepsilon})
be a global solution to the SCS equations (
1.10
) subject to the initial data
(\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\textup{in}},A^{\varepsilon}_{\textup{in}})
. Then, the total charge and the total energy are conserved:

\int_{\Omega}|\psi^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\,\textup{d}x=\int_{\Omega}|\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\textup{in}}(x)|^{2}\,\textup{d}x,\quad\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\textup{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\varepsilon}(0,x)\,\textup{d}x,\quad t\geq 0.

2.2.
Identifying the hydrodynamic equations

We now present the detailed derivation of the hydrodynamic system for the SCS equations.

2.2.1.
Derivation of (
1.7
)

As we discussed in the introduction, we consider the well-known Madelung transformation for
\psi^{\varepsilon}
:

(2.4)

\psi^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)}\exp\left(\frac{\textup{i}}{\varepsilon}S^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\right)

and then substitute the representation (
2.4
) of
\psi^{\varepsilon}
into (
1.4
). Then, the imaginary part of the Schrödinger equation (
1.4
)
1
becomes

\partial_{t}\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}+\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\cdot\nabla S^{\varepsilon}+\frac{\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}{2}\Delta S^{\varepsilon}+A^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}=0,

which implies the continuity equation for the density
\rho^{\varepsilon}
:

\partial_{t}\rho^{\varepsilon}+\nabla\cdot(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})=0,\quad u^{\varepsilon}:=\nabla S^{\varepsilon}+A^{\varepsilon}.

We note that the definition of
u^{\varepsilon}
implies

(2.5)

\partial_{i}u^{\varepsilon}_{j}-\partial_{j}u^{\varepsilon}_{i}=\partial_{i}(\partial_{j}S^{\varepsilon}+A^{\varepsilon}_{j})-\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}S^{\varepsilon}+A^{\varepsilon}_{i})=\partial_{i}A^{\varepsilon}_{j}-\partial_{j}A^{\varepsilon}_{i},\quad\mbox{i.e.},\quad\nabla\times u^{\varepsilon}=\nabla\times A^{\varepsilon}.

On the other hand, we note that the right-hand sides of (
1.6
) become

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\varepsilon\textup{Im}\left(\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}D^{\varepsilon}_{2}\psi^{\varepsilon}\right)=-\varepsilon\textup{Im}\left(\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\left(\partial_{2}\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}+\frac{\textup{i}}{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\partial_{2}S^{\varepsilon}+\frac{\textup{i}}{\varepsilon}A_{2}^{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right)\right)=-\rho^{\varepsilon}\partial_{2}S^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}A_{2}^{\varepsilon}=-\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}_{2},

\displaystyle\varepsilon\textup{Im}\left(\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}D^{\varepsilon}_{1}\psi^{\varepsilon}\right)=\rho^{\varepsilon}\partial_{1}S^{\varepsilon}+\rho^{\varepsilon}A_{1}^{\varepsilon}=\rho^{\varepsilon}u_{1}^{\varepsilon},

and the right-hand side of (
1.4
)
2
becomes

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle\textup{Im}(Q_{12}(\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}},\psi^{\varepsilon}))+\partial_{1}(A_{2}^{\varepsilon}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2})-\partial_{2}(A_{1}^{\varepsilon}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\textup{Im}\left(\partial_{1}\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}\partial_{2}\psi^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{2}\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}\partial_{1}\psi^{\varepsilon}\right)+\partial_{1}(A_{2}^{\varepsilon}\rho^{\varepsilon})-\partial_{2}(A_{1}^{\varepsilon}\rho^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{1}\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\partial_{2}S^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{2}\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\partial_{1}S^{\varepsilon})+(\partial_{1}A_{2}^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{2}A_{1}^{\varepsilon})\rho^{\varepsilon}+A_{2}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{1}\rho^{\varepsilon}-A_{1}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{2}\rho^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}\rho^{\varepsilon}(\partial_{2}S^{\varepsilon}+A_{2}^{\varepsilon})-\partial_{2}\rho^{\varepsilon}(\partial_{1}S^{\varepsilon}+A_{1}^{\varepsilon})+(\nabla\times A^{\varepsilon})\rho^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{1}\rho^{\varepsilon})u_{2}^{\varepsilon}-(\partial_{2}\rho^{\varepsilon})u_{1}^{\varepsilon}+(\nabla\times A^{\varepsilon})\rho^{\varepsilon}=\nabla\rho^{\varepsilon}\times u^{\varepsilon}+(\nabla\times u^{\varepsilon})\rho^{\varepsilon},

where we used (
2.5
) in the last identity. Therefore, the Chern-Simons parts (
1.4
)
2,3
become

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Delta A^{\varepsilon}_{0}=\nabla\times(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}),\quad\Delta A^{\varepsilon}=-(\nabla\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\perp},\end{aligned}

where
(u^{\varepsilon})^{\perp}:=(-u_{2}^{\varepsilon},u_{1}^{\varepsilon})
.
We also note that the additional relation (
1.6
) can be written as

(2.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}A^{\varepsilon}=\nabla A_{0}^{\varepsilon}+\rho^{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon})^{\perp}.

Finally, to identify the momentum equation, we consider the real part of the Schrödinger equation (
1.4
)
1
:

(2.7)

\partial_{t}S^{\varepsilon}+A^{\varepsilon}_{0}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}{\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla S^{\varepsilon}+A^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+V^{\prime}(\rho^{\varepsilon})=0,

and then take a gradient to (
2.7
) to obtain

(2.8)

\partial_{t}(\nabla S^{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\nabla|\nabla S^{\varepsilon}+A^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\nabla A^{\varepsilon}_{0}+V^{\prime\prime}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\nabla\rho^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\nabla\left(\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}{\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}\right).

We note that for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the following identity holds:

\frac{1}{2}(\nabla|u|^{2})_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{2}u_{j}\partial_{i}u_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{2}u_{j}\partial_{j}u_{i}+u_{j}(\partial_{i}u_{j}-\partial_{j}u_{i})=(u\cdot\nabla)u_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{2}u_{j}(\partial_{i}u_{j}-\partial_{j}u_{i}).

Therefore, we modify the second term in (
2.8
) as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\nabla|\nabla S^{\varepsilon}+A^{\varepsilon}|^{2})_{i}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2})_{i}=(u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla)u^{\varepsilon}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{2}u^{\varepsilon}_{j}(\partial_{i}u^{\varepsilon}_{j}-\partial_{j}u^{\varepsilon}_{i}),

and again use (
2.5
) and (
1.5
) to obtain

u^{\varepsilon}_{2}(\partial_{1}u^{\varepsilon}_{2}-\partial_{2}u^{\varepsilon}_{1})=u^{\varepsilon}_{2}(\partial_{1}A^{\varepsilon}_{2}-\partial_{2}A^{\varepsilon}_{1})=-\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}_{2},\quad u^{\varepsilon}_{1}(\partial_{2}u^{\varepsilon}_{1}-\partial_{1}u^{\varepsilon}_{2})=u^{\varepsilon}_{1}(\partial_{2}A^{\varepsilon}_{1}-\partial_{1}A^{\varepsilon}_{2})=\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}_{1}.

Hence, the equation (
2.8
) becomes

(2.9)

\partial_{t}(\nabla S^{\varepsilon})+(u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla)u^{\varepsilon}+\rho^{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon})^{\perp}+\nabla A^{\varepsilon}_{0}+\frac{\nabla p(\rho^{\varepsilon})}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\nabla\left(\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}{\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}\right),

where
p(\rho):=\rho V^{\prime}(\rho)-V(\rho)
is a pressure. Thus, combining the estimates (
2.9
) for
\nabla S^{\varepsilon}
and (
2.6
) for
A^{\varepsilon}_{0}
, we finally derive the governing equation for
u^{\varepsilon}
:

\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}+(u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla)u^{\varepsilon}+\frac{\nabla p(\rho^{\varepsilon})}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\nabla\left(\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}{\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}\right).

In conclusion, the quantities
(\rho^{\varepsilon},\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon},A_{0}^{\varepsilon},A^{\varepsilon})
satisfy (
1.7
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{\varepsilon}+\nabla\cdot(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})+\nabla\cdot(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}\otimes u^{\varepsilon})+\nabla p(\rho^{\varepsilon})=\rho^{\varepsilon}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\nabla\left(\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}{\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}\right),

\displaystyle\Delta A^{\varepsilon}_{0}=\nabla\times(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}),\quad\Delta A^{\varepsilon}=-(\nabla\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\perp}.

Remark 2.1
.

Since the total charge
\int_{\Omega}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x
is conserved by Proposition
2.1
, we also obtain that the total density
\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\varepsilon}\,\textup{d}x

Remark 2.2

3.
Relative entropy and the modulated energy

In this section, we introduce the main technical tools for the hydrodynamic limit of the SCS system towards the compressible ECS system. Precisely, we first review the relative entropy method, which has been used to study the hydrodynamic limit of particle
[
7
,
14
,
30
]
and kinetic systems
[
1
,
3
,
6
,
8
,
12
,
26
,
28
,
29
]
. The main idea of the relative entropy method is to estimate the functional called
relative entropy
, or sometimes called the
modulated energy
, which measures the difference between the solution to the scaled system and the solution to the asymptotic system. This quantity is shown to vanish as the scaling parameter
\varepsilon
tends to 0, guaranteeing the desired convergence of the solution toward the solution to the target equation. On the other hand, the modulated energy estimate was also used to obtaining the hydrodynamic limit for the Schrödinger-type equations
[
16
,
17
,
21
,
22
,
27
]
. Therefore, we first review the relative entropy method for the classical conservation laws and provide the close relation between relative entropy of the compressible Euler equations and the modulated energy of the SCS system, which will be crucially used in the proof of the main theorem.

3.1.
Review on the relative entropy method

We consider the following general system of conservation laws:

(3.1)

\partial_{t}U_{i}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\partial_{k}\bm{A}_{ik}(U)=0,

where
U\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the state and
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
is the flux. Indeed, the compressible Euler equations (
1.8
)
1,2
can be written in the form of (
3.1
) with
m=3
,
d=2
,
U=(\rho,P):=(\rho,\rho u)
and

\bm{A}(U):=\frac{1}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}\rho P_{1}&\rho P_{2}\\
P_{1}^{2}+\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\rho^{\gamma+1}&P_{1}P_{2}\\
P_{2}P_{1}&P_{2}^{2}+\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\rho^{\gamma+1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho u^{\top}\\
\rho u\otimes u+\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\rho^{\gamma}I_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

For the compressible Euler equation, one usually consider the following natural (mathematical) entropy
\eta(U)
defined as

\eta(U):=\frac{|P|^{2}}{2\rho}+\frac{\rho^{\gamma}}{\gamma}=\frac{\rho|u|^{2}}{2}+\frac{\rho^{\gamma}}{\gamma}.

For any function
V=(n,nv)
, we define the relative entropy
\eta(V|U)
and the relative flux
\bm{A}(V|U)
as follows:

\displaystyle\eta(V|U):=\eta(V)-\eta(U)-D\eta(U)\cdot(V-U),

\displaystyle\bm{A}(V|U):=\bm{A}(V)-\bm{A}(U)-D\bm{A}(U)\cdot(V-U),

where
D\bm{A}(U)\cdot(V-U)
is a
3\times 2
matrix defined as

[D\bm{A}(U)\cdot(V-U)]_{ij}:=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\partial_{U_{k}}\bm{A}_{ij}(U)(V_{k}-U_{k}).

By direct computation, one can easily compute
D\eta
as

D\eta=\begin{pmatrix}D_{\rho}\eta\\
D_{P}\eta\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{|P|^{2}}{2\rho^{2}}+\rho^{\gamma-1}\\
\frac{P}{\rho}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}+\rho^{\gamma-1}\\
u\end{pmatrix}

and therefore, the relative entropy becomes

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \eta(V|U)&=\frac{n|v|^{2}}{2}+\frac{n^{\gamma}}{\gamma}-\frac{\rho|u|^{2}}{2}-\frac{\rho^{\gamma}}{\gamma}-\left(-\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}+\rho^{\gamma-1}\right)(n-\rho)-u\cdot(nv-\rho u)\\
&=\frac{n|u-v|^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma}(n^{\gamma}-\rho^{\gamma}-\gamma\rho^{\gamma-1}(n-\rho))=\frac{n|u-v|^{2}}{2}+\frac{p(n|\rho)}{\gamma-1}.\end{aligned}

Moreover, using the definition of
D\bm{A}(U)\cdot(V-U)
, it is straightforward to observe that

D\bm{A}(U)\cdot(V-U)=\begin{pmatrix}(nv-\rho u)^{\top}\\
-(n-\rho)(u\otimes u)+u\otimes(nv-\rho u)+(nv-\rho u)\otimes u+(\gamma-1)\rho^{\gamma-1}(n-\rho)I_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore, the relative flux
\bm{A}(V|U)
is computed as

\displaystyle\bm{A}(V|U)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
n(v-u)\otimes(v-u)+p(n|\rho)I_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

The hydrodynamic limit towards the Euler-type system is based on the following proposition, which is the cornerstone of the relative entropy method.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
4
]

Let
U
be a smooth solution to the conservation law (
3.1
), and let
V
be any function. Then, the following estimate on the relative entropy holds:

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta(V|U)\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta(V)\,\textup{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}(D\eta(U)):\bm{A}(V|U)\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}D\eta(U)\cdot\left(\partial_{t}V+\nabla_{x}\cdot\bm{A}(V)\right)\,\textup{d}x.

3.2.
Relation between relative entropy and the modulated energy

In this part, we first show that the energy function
\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}
can be represented in terms of the hydrodynamic variables
(\rho^{\varepsilon},\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})
. Then, we introduce the modulated energy, which is used to show the hydrodynamic limit of the Schrödinger-type equations, and show that it can be understood as a perturbation of the relative entropy (
3.2
) of the compressible Euler equation. First, the energy function can be expanded as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}|\varepsilon D^{\varepsilon}\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+V(|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2})=\frac{1}{2}(\varepsilon\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}+\textup{i}A^{\varepsilon}\psi^{\varepsilon})\cdot(\varepsilon\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}-\textup{i}A^{\varepsilon}\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}})+V(\rho^{\varepsilon}).

We also note that

\displaystyle(\varepsilon\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}+\textup{i}A^{\varepsilon}\psi^{\varepsilon})\cdot(\varepsilon\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}-\textup{i}A^{\varepsilon}\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}})=\varepsilon^{2}\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}+\textup{i}\varepsilon A^{\varepsilon}\cdot(\psi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}-\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}\nabla{\psi^{\varepsilon}})+|A^{\varepsilon}|^{2}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

On the other hand, it follows from the definition
u^{\varepsilon}=\nabla S^{\varepsilon}+A^{\varepsilon}
that

\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}=\rho^{\varepsilon}\left|\frac{\textup{i}\varepsilon}{2\rho^{\varepsilon}}(\psi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}-\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon})+A^{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4\rho^{\varepsilon}}|\psi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}-\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\textup{i}\varepsilon(\psi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}-\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon})\cdot A^{\varepsilon}+\rho^{\varepsilon}|A^{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

Therefore, one has

\displaystyle(\varepsilon\nabla

\displaystyle\psi^{\varepsilon}+\textup{i}A^{\varepsilon}\psi^{\varepsilon})\cdot(\varepsilon\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}-\textup{i}A^{\varepsilon}\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}})

\displaystyle=\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4\rho^{\varepsilon}}|\psi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}-\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4\rho^{\varepsilon}}|\psi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}+\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}=\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4\rho^{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\rho^{\varepsilon}|^{2}=\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}.

We substitute the above estimate to the energy function to write the energy function in terms of the hydrodynamic quantities:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}&=\frac{1}{2}\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+V(\rho^{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}}{\gamma}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}\\
&=\eta(U^{\varepsilon})+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2},\end{aligned}

where
U^{\varepsilon}:=(\rho^{\varepsilon},\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})
. Therefore, one can observe that the energy function of the SCS equations can be understood as a modified entropy with the additional term
\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}
. We interpret this additional term as a quantum correction.

On the other hand, considering the modulated energy as in the Schrödinger-type equations
[
17
,
21
,
22
]
, the natural modulated energy for the SCS equations is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|(\varepsilon D^{\varepsilon}-\textup{i}u)\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{p(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)}{\gamma-1}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|(\varepsilon D^{\varepsilon}-\textup{i}u)\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}-\rho^{\gamma}-\gamma\rho^{\gamma-1}(\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho)}{\gamma}\,\textup{d}x.

In what follows, we show that the modulated energy can also be represented in terms of the hydrodynamic variables. We first expand the modulated energy as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|D^{\varepsilon}\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{p(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)}{\gamma-1}\,\textup{d}x+\frac{\varepsilon\textup{i}}{2}\int_{\Omega}(D^{\varepsilon}\psi^{\varepsilon}\cdot u\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}-\overline{D^{\varepsilon}\psi^{\varepsilon}}\cdot u\psi^{\varepsilon})\textup{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\gamma-1}\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\rho-\rho^{\varepsilon}\right)\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\varepsilon\textup{i}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}+\frac{\textup{i}}{\varepsilon}A^{\varepsilon}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\psi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}+\frac{\textup{i}}{\varepsilon}A^{\varepsilon}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)\cdot u+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}\,\textup{d}x.

However, since

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon\textup{i}}{2}\left(\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}+\frac{\textup{i}}{\varepsilon}A^{\varepsilon}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\psi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}+\frac{\textup{i}}{\varepsilon}A^{\varepsilon}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)=\frac{\varepsilon\textup{i}}{2}\left(\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}-\psi^{\varepsilon}\nabla\overline{\psi^{\varepsilon}}\right)-A^{\varepsilon}|\psi^{\varepsilon}|^{2}=-\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon},

the modulated energy becomes

\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\gamma-1}\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\rho-\rho^{\varepsilon}\right)\,\textup{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}\cdot u\,\textup{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\varepsilon}|u|^{2}\,\textup{d}x.

Considering the hydrodynamic formulation (
3.3
) for
\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}
, the modulated energy is related to classical relative entropy with the quantum correction term:

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}&=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}-u|^{2}}{2}+\frac{p(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)}{\gamma-1}\,\textup{d}x+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\eta(U^{\varepsilon}|U)\,\textup{d}x+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x.\end{aligned}

Therefore, the relation (
3.4
) reveals that the theory of relative entropy method developed in the classical hydrodynamic limit problem can also be applied to the hydrodynamic limit problem of the Schrödinger-type equations. In the following section, we present the estimate of the modulated energy
\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}
, with the help of the classical relative entropy estimate.

4.
Hydrodynamic limit to the compressible Euler-Chern-Simons system

In this section, we provide the proof of Theorem

. We first estimate the modulated energy function to show that it disappears as

converges to 0. Then, using the decay estimate of the modulated energy, we show that the desired convergences are attained.

4.1.
Modulated energy estimate

We start by deriving the estimate of the modulated energy. The main idea is to use the relation between the modulated energy and the relative entropy, and the well-known classical estimate on the relative entropy in Proposition

Proposition 4.1

Let

)
1,2

(4.1)

\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}(t)\leq C\varepsilon^{\min\{\lambda,2\}},\quad 0\leq t<T_{*}.

Proof.

We use the relation (

and the relative entropy

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}}{\textup{d}t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\eta(U^{\varepsilon}|U)\,\textup{d}x+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\textup{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}(D\eta(U)):\bm{A}(U^{\varepsilon}|U)\,\textup{d}x-\int_{\Omega}D\eta(U)\cdot(\partial_{t}U^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\cdot\bm{A}(U^{\varepsilon}))\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}(D\eta(U)):\bm{A}(U^{\varepsilon}|U)\,\textup{d}x-\int_{\Omega}D\eta(U)\cdot(\partial_{t}U^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\cdot\bm{A}(U^{\varepsilon}))\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{I}_{11}+\mathcal{I}_{12},

where we used the conservation of the total energy. In the following, we estimate each term

separately.

\bullet
(Estimate of
\mathcal{I}_{11}
): We first recall that the derivative of the entropy

\nabla_{x}(D\eta(U))=\nabla_{x}\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}+\rho^{\gamma-1}\\
u\end{pmatrix},\quad\bm{A}(U^{\varepsilon}|U)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\rho^{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon}-u)\otimes(u^{\varepsilon}-u)+p(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)I_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Since

is bounded. Therefore, we estimate
\mathcal{I}_{11}

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{11}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}(D\eta(U)):\bm{A}(U^{\varepsilon}|U)\,\textup{d}x\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}-u|^{2}\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}p(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)\,\textup{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}\eta(U^{\varepsilon}|U)\,\textup{d}x.

\bullet
(Estimate of
\mathcal{I}_{12}

\partial_{t}U^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\cdot\bm{A}(U^{\varepsilon})=\begin{pmatrix}0\\

Hence, the integrand of

can be computed as

D\eta(U)\cdot(\partial_{t}U^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\cdot\bm{A}(U^{\varepsilon}))=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\rho^{\varepsilon}u\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}{\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}\right).

Therefore, we use the integration-by-part to estimate
\mathcal{I}_{12}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{12}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}D\eta(U)\cdot(\partial_{t}U^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\cdot\bm{A}(U^{\varepsilon}))\,\textup{d}x=-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\varepsilon}u\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}{\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}\right)\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot(\rho^{\varepsilon}u)\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}{\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}\,\textup{d}x=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\cdot u)\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Delta\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\,\textup{d}x+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\nabla\rho^{\varepsilon}\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}{\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}}\,\textup{d}x

\diamond
(Estimate of
\mathcal{I}_{121}
): We use integrate-by-parts once more and use the boundedness of
\nabla\cdot u
and
\nabla\nabla\cdot u

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{121}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left(-\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\nabla\cdot u)\cdot\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\,\textup{d}x-\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\cdot u)|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x\right)

where we use the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and the conservation of the mass

\diamond
(Estimate of
\mathcal{I}_{122}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Delta\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\partial_{i}\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\partial_{j}^{2}\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}u_{i}\partial_{i}\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\partial_{j}\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\,\textup{d}x-\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\partial_{j}\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\cdot\nabla u\cdot\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\,\textup{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\nabla(|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2})\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\cdot\nabla u\cdot\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\,\textup{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\cdot u)|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x.

Therefore, we conclude that
\mathcal{I}_{122}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x
.

We now combine the estimates of
\mathcal{I}_{121}
and
\mathcal{I}_{122}
to estimate

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{12}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x+C\varepsilon^{2}.

Therefore, combining the estimates of
\mathcal{I}_{11}
and

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}}{\textup{d}t}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}\eta(U^{\varepsilon}|U)+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x\right)+C\varepsilon^{2}\leq C\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}+C\varepsilon^{2}.

Then, Grönwall inequality implies

\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}(t)\leq C(\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}(0)+\varepsilon^{2})\leq C\varepsilon^{\min\{\lambda,2\}},

where the last inequality comes from the well-prepared initial data condition (

∎

Proof of Theorem

We now present the asymptotic convergence of the SCS equations using the modulated energy estimates (

4.1
. Although the convergence of
(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})
can be obtained in the same way as in
[

, we provide the full proof for the completeness of the paper. First, it is well-known (e.g.
[
18

) that the
L^{\gamma}
-norm of
\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho
for can be controlled by the relative pressure
p(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)
as follows.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\gamma>1
be a constant.

(1)

If
\gamma\geq 2
,

|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho|^{\gamma}\leq(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}-\rho^{\gamma}-\gamma\rho^{\gamma-1}(\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho)=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}p(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho).

(2)

If
1<\gamma<2
,

\begin{cases}|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho|^{2}\leq C\rho^{2-\gamma}p(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho),\quad&\mbox{if}\quad\rho^{\varepsilon}\leq 2\rho,\\
|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho|^{\gamma}\leq Cp(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho)\quad&\mbox{if}\quad\rho^{\varepsilon}\geq 2\rho.\end{cases}

Proof.

Although the estimates are well-known, we present the detailed proof for the completeness of the paper.

(1) It suffice to show that for
x>0
,

x^{\gamma}-1-\gamma(x-1)\geq|x-1|^{\gamma}.

Once we obtain the above estimate, the desired estimate directly obtained by taking
x=\frac{\rho^{\varepsilon}}{\rho}
. We take
f(x):=x^{\gamma}-1-\gamma(x-1)-|x-1|^{\gamma}
. Then,
f(1)=0
and

f^{\prime}(x)=\begin{cases}\gamma(x^{\gamma-1}-1-(x-1)^{\gamma-1})>0,\quad&\mbox{if}\quad x>1,\\
\gamma(x^{\gamma-1}-1+(1-x)^{\gamma-1})<0,\quad&\mbox{if}\quad 0<x<1.\end{cases}

Therefore,
f(x)
has the minimum
f(1)=0
, which implies the desired estimate.

(2) We first show that

|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho|^{2}\leq C\rho^{2-\gamma}p(\rho^{\varepsilon}|\rho),\quad\mbox{if}\quad\rho^{\varepsilon}\leq 2\rho.

By dividing
\rho^{2}
on both sides, it is equivalent to show that

(x-1)^{2}\leq C\left(x^{\gamma}-1-\gamma\left(x-1\right)\right),\quad\mbox{if}\quad x\leq 2.

We define
g(x):=x^{\gamma}-1-\gamma(x-1)
. Then, it follows from the Taylor’s theorem that

g(x)=g(1)+g^{\prime}(1)(x-1)+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(\xi)}{2}(x-1)^{2}=\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(\xi)}{2}(x-1)^{2},\quad\mbox{for some $\xi$ between 1 and $x$}.

Since,
x\leq 2
, we also have
\xi\leq 2
, and therefore,

\frac{g(x)}{(x-1)^{2}}=\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(\xi)}{2}=\gamma(\gamma-1)\xi^{\gamma-2}\geq\gamma(\gamma-1)2^{\gamma-2}.

Thus, the desired estimate holds with
C=\frac{2^{2-\gamma}}{\gamma(\gamma-1)}
. The second assertion is equivalent to

(x-1)^{\gamma}\leq C(x^{\gamma}-1-\gamma(x-1)),\quad x\geq 2.

Similarly, we have

\frac{g(x)}{(x-1)^{\gamma}}=\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(\xi)}{2}(x-1)^{2-\gamma}=\frac{\gamma(\gamma-1)}{2}\xi^{\gamma-2}(x-1)^{2-\gamma}\geq\frac{\gamma(\gamma-1)}{2}\left(\frac{x-1}{x}\right)^{2-\gamma}\geq\frac{\gamma(\gamma-1)}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2-\gamma}.

Therefore, we still have
(x-1)^{\gamma}\leq C(x^{\gamma}-1-\gamma(x-1))
with the same
C
.
∎

We now show the desired convergence, according to the spatial domain.

\bullet
(Case 1:
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}
) In this case, we assume that
\gamma\geq 2
. It follows from Lemma

For the convergence regarding

to obtain

\displaystyle\|\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}-\rho u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho^{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon}-u)\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}}+\|(\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho)u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2\gamma}}\|\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|u^{\varepsilon}-u|\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{\gamma}}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|u^{\varepsilon}-u|\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{\gamma}}\leq C\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}\to 0.

Furthermore, since

, we deduce

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\varepsilon}(0)+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)-1)\pi(t,x)\,\textup{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\rho^{\varepsilon}(0,x)-1)\pi(0,x)\,\textup{d}x+C\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\varepsilon}(s)\,\textup{d}s+C\varepsilon^{\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\varepsilon}(0)+\|\rho^{\varepsilon}(t)-1\|_{L^{\gamma}}\|\pi(t)\|_{L^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}+\|\rho^{\varepsilon}(0)-1\|_{L^{\gamma}}\|\pi(0)\|_{L^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\varepsilon}(s)\,\textup{d}s+C\varepsilon^{\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\varepsilon}(0)+C\varepsilon^{\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\varepsilon}(s)\,\textup{d}s,

where we used
\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-1\|_{L^{\gamma}}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}}
. Then, Gröwnall inequality implies the desired estimate:

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\varepsilon}(t)\leq C\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\varepsilon}(0)+\varepsilon^{\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}}\right)\leq C\varepsilon^{\min\left\{\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma},\lambda\right\}},\quad 0\leq t<T_{*}.

∎

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

We now provide the desired convergence estimate and complete the proof of Theorem
2.2
. We already observe in Lemma
5.1
that

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-1\|_{L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}}\to 0,\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

Moreover, since both
\rho^{\varepsilon}-1
and
1
are in
L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,
\rho^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and
\|\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}}
is uniformly bounded. Therefore, we estimate the difference
\|\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}-u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}}
as

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}-u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}}&\leq\|\rho^{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon}-u)\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}}+\|(\rho^{\varepsilon}-1)u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}}\\
&\leq\|\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2\gamma}}\|\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|u^{\varepsilon}-u|\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-1\|_{L^{\gamma}}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}\\
&\leq C\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\varepsilon}(t)+C\varepsilon^{\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}}\to 0,\end{aligned}

as well as the difference
\|\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}u^{\varepsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}}
as

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}u^{\varepsilon}-u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|u^{\varepsilon}-u|\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}-1)|u|\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|u^{\varepsilon}-u|\|_{L^{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}\|\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}-1\|_{L^{2\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq C\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\varepsilon}+C\|\rho^{\varepsilon}-1\|_{L^{\gamma}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow 0.

For the convergence of the Chern-Simons part, we first note that

∎

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied the hydrodynamic limit problem for the Schrödinger-Chern-Simons equations. We adopt the Madelung transformation and different scalings to derive the compressible and incompressible Euler equations coupled with the Chern-Simons equations and Poisson equation respectively. To obtain the rigorous convergence, we focus on the modulated energy functional. For the case of compressible Euler limit, we relate the modulated energy with the standard relative entropy in the kinetic theory and use the well-known estimates on the relative entropy functional to provide the simple estimate of the modulated energy functional. On the other hand, for the case of the incompressible Euler limit, we directly estimate the modified energy functional to obtain the desired convergence. Beyond the Schrödinger-Chern-Simons equations, other interesting systems, such as Schrödinger-Maxwell, Chern-Simons-Higgs, or Maxwell-Klein-Gordon equations, are also actively studied from the various points of view. Therefore, investigating the hydrodynamic limits of those systems would be left as interesting future works.

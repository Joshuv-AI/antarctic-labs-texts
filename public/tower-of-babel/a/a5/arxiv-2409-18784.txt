Stationary Soap Film Bridge Subjected to an Electrostatic Force: The Balanced Case
Lina Sophie Schmitz

Abstract
. We analytically study a stationary free boundary problem describing a soap film bridge subjected to an electrostatic force. Starting from a cylinder that the soap film forms if its surface tension and the electrostatic force are perfectly balanced, we construct a local branch of stationary solutions. Then, we prove that there is a sharp threshold value for a characteristic model parameter at which the stability behaviour of this branch switches from stable to unstable. Finally, we show that the soap film deflects monotonically outwards if the strength of the electrostatic force is increased. Besides rigorous results, we also discuss a possible balancing effect of the electrostatic force.

1.
Introduction

A classic example for the relation between physical and mathematical objects is given by that of soap films and minimal surfaces
[
6
]
. If a soap film is spanned between two parallel metal rings with a small gap, its surface tension forces it to take the shape of a catenoid, which is a rotationally symmetric minimal surface. In contrast, for a large gap between the rings, such a surface does not exist, which manifests as a pinch-off (i.e. breaking) of the soap film bridge, see
[
7
,
14
]
. Applying an additional force to the soap film may influence the occurrence of a pinch-off and also changes the shape of the film. Since soap responds to electrostatics
[
24
]
, the choice of an additional electrostatic force is possible, which is also particularly interesting due to its technological relevance
[
30
]
. A mathematical model for a soap film bridge subjected to an additional electrostatic force which counteracts the surface tension has been introduced in
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
and consists of a singular ordinary differential equation (ODE).
In the present paper, we analytically study a stationary version of a new mathematical model
[
32
]
describing the same physical set-up but derived under more general modelling assumptions. In particular, we deal with a free boundary problem instead of a singular ODE. In our investigation, we focus on the case where surface tension and electrostatics are nearly balanced and study how small changes of the electrostatic force affect the soap film bridge.

1.1.
The Mathematical Model

The precise set-up is given by a tiny soap film spanned between two metal rings and placed inside an outer metal cylinder, see Figure
1.1
. Applying a voltage between this metal cylinder and the rings induces an electrostatic force pulling the film outwards. Throughout, we assume rotational symmetry, which allows us to consider the cross section, see Figure
1.2

u=u(z):(-1,1)\rightarrow(-1,1)
with

such that

gives the profile of the soap film bridge, and by the electrostatic potential

\Omega(u)=\big\{(z,r)\in(-1,1)\times(0,2)\,|\,u(z)+1<r<2\,\big\}.

In the stationary case,

solves the elliptic equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\qquad-\sigma\,\partial_{z}\mathrm{arctan}(\sigma\partial_{z}u)&=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{u+1}+\lambda\,(1+\sigma^{2}(\partial_{z}u)^{2})^{3/2}|\partial_{r}\psi(z,u+1)|^{2}\,,\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad u(\pm 1)&=0\,,\qquad-1<u<1\,,\end{cases}

and the electrostatic potential
\psi
is a solution to

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\left(r\partial_{r}\psi\right)+\sigma^{2}\partial^{2}_{z}\psi&=0\quad\ \text{in}\quad\Omega(u)\,,\\
\ \,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\psi&=h_{u}\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega(u)\end{cases}

with

(1.3)

\displaystyle h_{u}(z,r)=\frac{\ln\Big(\displaystyle\frac{r}{u(z)+1}\Big)}{\ln\Big(\displaystyle\frac{2}{u(z)+1}\Big)}\,.

1.4.
Main Results and Strategies

We present results concerning existence, stability and direction of deflection for stationary solutions close to the cylinder in dependence on varying voltages
\lambda
. In all statements, the so-called eigencurve profile
[s\mapsto\mu(s)]
will occur, which has a unique zero and can be written as

\displaystyle\mu(s)=-s+3+2\,\partial_{r}h_{s}(1)\,,\qquad s\in(0,\infty)

where
h_{s}\in W^{2}_{2,D}(-1,1)
solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\big(r\,\partial_{r}h_{s}\big)+s\,h_{s}=\frac{-2}{r^{3}}+s\,\frac{2-r}{r}\,,\\
&h_{s}(1)=h_{s}(2)=0\,,\end{cases}

see Section
4
. With
[s\mapsto\mu(s)]
at hand, we can state the first result which focuses on existence of stationary solutions for
\lambda
close to
\lambda_{cyl}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Existence)

Let
q\in(2,\infty)
, and
s_{0}>0
be the unique zero of the eigencurve profile
[s\mapsto\mu(s)]
.
Then, for each
\sigma>0
with

\sigma^{2}\neq\frac{4\,s_{0}}{\pi^{2}\,(j+1)^{2}}\,,\qquad j\in\mathbb{N}\,,

there exists
\delta=\delta(\sigma)>0
and an analytic function

\displaystyle[\lambda\mapsto u^{\lambda}_{cyl}]

\displaystyle:(\lambda_{cyl}-\delta,\lambda_{cyl}+\delta)\rightarrow W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)\,,\ \qquad u_{cyl}^{\lambda_{cyl}}=0

such that
u^{\lambda}_{cyl}
is a solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) for each
\lambda\in(\lambda_{cyl}-\delta,\lambda_{cyl}+\delta)
. Moreover,
u^{\lambda}_{cyl}
as well as the corresponding electrostatic
potential
\psi_{u^{\lambda}_{cyl}}\in W^{2}_{2}\big(\Omega(u^{\lambda}_{cyl})\big)
are symmetric with respect to the
r
-axis.

Note that functions in
W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)
satisfy Dirichlet boundary conditions. To prove Theorem
1.1
, we rewrite (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) as a non-local elliptic equation for
u
only

(1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&F(u)+\lambda\,g(u)=0\,,\\
&u(\pm 1)=0\,,\quad-1<u<1\end{cases}

with non-local electrostatic force

(1.9)

\displaystyle g(u):=(1+\sigma^{2}(\partial_{z}u)^{2})^{3/2}\,\big|\partial_{r}\psi_{u}(z,u+1)\big|^{2}

and

(1.10)

\displaystyle F(u):=\sigma\partial_{z}\mathrm{arctan}(\sigma\partial_{z}u)-\displaystyle\frac{1}{u+1}\,.

In (
1.9
), the notion
\psi_{u}
highlights that we consider (
1.2
) in dependence on
u
.
Theorem
1.1
follows from an application of the implicit function theorem which requires the linearization of problem (
1.8
) around
(\lambda,u)=(\lambda_{cyl},0)
. The non-local character of
g
makes the exact computation of the spectrum of the linearized operator
DF(0)+\lambda_{cyl}Dg(0)
difficult. Instead, we derive qualitative properties of it using a Fourier series ansatz
[
1
,
12
,
13
,
21
]
. For our specific problem, this yields to the investigation of the eigencurve profile
[s\mapsto\mu(s)]
.

Second, we rigorously study stability of stationary solutions near the cylinder under rotationally symmetric perturbations. We find a sharp threshold value
\sigma_{cyl}>0
such that the stationary solution
u^{\lambda}_{cyl}
from Theorem
1.1
is unstable for
\sigma<\sigma_{cyl}
and stable for
\sigma>\sigma_{cyl}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Stability)

Let
q\in(2,\infty)
and
\sigma^{2}\neq\displaystyle\frac{4s_{0}}{\pi^{2}(j+1)^{2}}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
s_{0}
being the unique zero of the eigencurve profile
[s\mapsto\mu(s)]
. Define

(1.11)

\displaystyle\sigma_{cyl}:=\sqrt{\frac{4s_{0}}{\pi^{2}}}\,,

Then, there exists
\delta>0
such that for each
\lambda\in(\lambda_{cyl}-\delta,\lambda_{cyl}+\delta)
the stationary solution
u_{cyl}^{\lambda}
satisfies:

(i)
If
\sigma<\sigma_{cyl}
, then
u_{cyl}^{\lambda}
is unstable in
W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)
.

(ii)
If
\sigma>\sigma_{cyl}
, then
u_{cyl}^{\lambda}
is exponentially asymptotically stable in
W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)
. More precisely,
there exist numbers
\omega_{0},m,M>0
such that for each initial value

with
\|u_{0}-u_{cyl}^{\lambda}\|_{W^{2}_{q,D}}<m
, the solution
u
to the dynamical version of (
1.1
)-(
1.3
), see (
1.5
), exists globally in time and the estimate

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{cyl}^{\lambda}\|_{W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)}

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L_{q}(-1,1)}\leq M\,e^{-\omega_{0}t}\|u_{0}-u_{cyl}^{\lambda}\|_{W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)}

holds for

.

Here, we use the notion of unstable from
[
23
]
. The proof of Theorem
1.2
is based on the principle of linearized stability. We point out that part (ii) contains an alternative argument for existence of solutions compared to
[

, at least for
u_{0}
close to

in the

-norm. Concerning the first two results, we note that the implicit function theorem and principle of linearized stability have been applied in

As a third result, for the stable range

Theorem 1.3

(Direction of Deflection)

For
\sigma>\sigma_{cyl}
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle u^{\overline{\lambda}}_{cyl}(z)<u^{\lambda}_{cyl}(z)\,,\qquad\lambda_{cyl}-\delta<\overline{\lambda}<\lambda<\lambda_{cyl}+\delta\,,\quad z\in(-1,1)\,.

This reflects the physically expected behaviour that increasing the electrostatic force, which is scaled by

, pulls the film farther outwards. Theorem
1.3
is in accordance with the behaviour of the simpler ODE model in
[
25
]
where formal asymptotic analysis has been used to establish a similar result.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is based on the linear approximation

\displaystyle u^{\lambda}_{cyl}

\displaystyle=u^{\lambda_{cyl}}_{cyl}+(\lambda-\lambda_{cyl})\,\partial_{\lambda}u^{\lambda_{cyl}}_{cyl}+o\big(\lambda-\lambda_{cyl}\big)

(1.12)

\displaystyle=(\lambda-\lambda_{cyl})\,\partial_{\lambda}u^{\lambda_{cyl}}_{cyl}+o(\lambda-\lambda_{cyl})\,,\qquad\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{cyl}

with

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}u_{cyl}^{\lambda_{cyl}}

\displaystyle=-\big[DF(0)+\lambda_{cyl}Dg(0)\big]^{-1}g(0)

(1.13)

\displaystyle=-\,\frac{1}{\ln(2)^{2}}\,\big[DF(0)+\lambda_{cyl}Dg(0)\big]^{-1}\,\mathbbm{1}

in
W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)
. Here, we inserted

, which easily follows from (
1.9
) combined with (
1.7
), and used the fact that
[\lambda\mapsto u^{\lambda}_{cyl}]

was constructed via the implicit function theorem. We now ask for positivity of (

) includes the non-local part
+\lambda_{cyl}Dg(0)
,
which most likely precludes the use of any standard method such as applying the maximum principle. Instead, we expand
\partial_{\lambda}u^{\lambda_{cyl}}_{cyl}
in a Fourier series and show positivity of this series by hand.
Essential ingredients for the positivity proof are estimates on the eigencurve profile
[s\mapsto\mu(s)]
.

-A
generates an analytic semigroup on

with domain

[

]

If

are Banach spaces, we let

be the Banach space of bounded linear operators from

to

, and
\mathcal{L}_{is}(E,F)
is the subspace of isomorphisms. Moreover, we write

if
E
is continuously embedded in
F
, and
E\overset{c}{\hookrightarrow}F
if the embedding is also compact.

For
\Omega=\big\{(z,r)\in(-1,1)\times(1,2)\big\}
, we let

\displaystyle-\Delta_{cyl,D}:\ W^{2}_{2,D}(\Omega)\rightarrow L_{2}(\Omega)\,,\quad f\mapsto-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\big(r\partial_{r}f\big)-\sigma^{2}\partial_{z}^{2}f

and, analogously,

with weighted scalar product

The operator

splits into two parts: We recall that the spectrum of the one-dimensional Dirichlet-Laplacian

4.

To further analyse the spectrum of the linearized operator

Proposition 4.1

The eigencurve profile

is strictly decreasing on

and there exists

with

The proof of Proposition

Lemma 4.2

The eigencurve profile

may equivalently be written as

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mu(s)=-s+3+2\,\partial_{r}h_{s}(1)\,,\qquad s\in(0,\infty)

where

solves

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\big(r\,\partial_{r}h_{s}\big)+s\,h_{s}=\frac{-2}{r^{3}}+s\,\frac{2-r}{r}\,,\\
&h_{s}(1)=h_{s}(2)=0\,.\end{cases}

This representation holds even for
s>-\xi_{0}
with
\xi_{0}>0
from (
2.2
). In particular,
\mu\in C^{\infty}\big((-\xi_{0},\infty),\mathbb{R}\big)
with

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(s)=-1+2\,\partial_{r}p_{s}(1)\,,

where
p_{s}\in W^{2}_{2,D}(-1,1)
solves

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\big(r\,\partial_{r}p_{s}\big)+s\,p_{s}=\frac{2-r}{r}-h_{s}\,,\\
&p_{s}(1)=p_{s}(2)=0\,.\end{cases}

Proof
.

(i)
We derive the alternative formula (
4.1
) for
\mu
: For
s\in(0,\infty)
, we find
\sigma\in(0,\infty)
such that
s=\sigma^{2}\nu_{0}
. Let us note that the solution

to

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{cyl,D})f_{s}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{r^{3}}\varphi_{0}-\sigma^{2}\frac{2-r}{r}\partial_{z}^{2}\varphi_{0}

\displaystyle=\Big(-\frac{2}{r^{3}}+s\,\frac{2-r}{r}\Big)\,\varphi_{0}

with

denoting the first eigenfunction of the one-dimensional Dirichlet-Laplacian can be written in the form

(4.5)

\displaystyle f_{s}(z,r)=h_{s}(r)\,\varphi_{0}(z)

with

solving

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\big(r\,\partial_{r}h_{s}\big)+s\,h_{s}=-\frac{2}{r^{3}}+s\,\frac{2-r}{r}\,,\quad r\in(1,2)\,,\\
&h_{s}(1)=h_{s}(2)=0\,.\end{cases}

We now derive from the relation

, (
3.7
) and (

) that

\displaystyle\big(DF(0)+\lambda_{cyl}Dg(0)\big)\varphi_{0}

\displaystyle=(-s+3)\varphi_{0}+2\,\partial_{r}f_{s}(\,\cdot\,,1)

\displaystyle=\big(-s+3+2\,\partial_{r}h_{s}(1)\big)\varphi_{0}\,.

Combining this with Lemma
3.7
yields

\mu(s)=-s+3+2\,\partial_{r}h_{s}(1)\,,\qquad s\in(0,\infty)\,,

which is formula (

).

(ii)
Note that the operator

is invertible for each

.
Because the right-hand side of (

. Moreover, its derivative is given by

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(s)=-1+2\,\partial_{r}p_{s}(1)\,,

where

. Finally, we note that taking the derivative of both sides of (

results in (

). This shows the remaining formula (

) for

.
∎

Remark 4.3

Note that the solution

to (

For the special case

and

Lemma 4.4

The values
\mu(0)
and
\mu^{\prime}(0)
are given by

\displaystyle\mu(0)=-1+\frac{2}{\ln(2)}>0

and

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(0)=-2+\frac{3}{2\ln(2)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\ln(2)}<-\frac{3}{10}\,.

Proof
.

(i)
For
\mu(0)
, we note that (

) with

reads

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\big(r\,\partial_{r}h_{0}\big)=\frac{-2}{r^{3}}\,,\\
&h_{0}(1)=h_{0}(2)=0\,.\end{cases}

This equation is solved by

(4.6)

\displaystyle h_{0}(r)=\frac{2-r}{r}+\frac{\ln(r)}{\ln(2)}-1

with derivative

(4.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{r}h_{0}(1)=-2+\frac{1}{\ln(2)}\,.

Hence, equation (

\displaystyle\mu(0)=-1+\frac{2}{\ln(2)}>0\,.

(ii)
For
\mu^{\prime}(0)
, we first recall from (

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(0)=-1+2\,\partial_{r}p_{0}(1)\,.

The function

solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\big(r\,\partial_{r}p_{0}\big)=1-\frac{\ln(r)}{\ln(2)}\,,\\

which is (

) with

and the inserted expression for

from (

). This equation has the explicit solution

\displaystyle p_{0}(r)=\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{3-\ln(2)}{4\ln(2)^{2}}\bigg)\ln(r)+\frac{1+\ln(2)}{4\ln(2)}+\frac{r^{2}\,\ln(r/2)-r^{2}}{4\ln(2)}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{3}{4\ln(2)^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\ln(2)}-\frac{1}{2}\,.

Plugging

into the formula for

∎

Based on this preparation, we provide a proof that
[s\mapsto\mu(s)]
is strictly decreasing on
[0,\infty)
and has exactly one zero.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

As
\mu
is smooth with
\mu(0)>0
as well as
\mu^{\prime}(0)<0
by Lemma

and Lemma

, it is enough to show that
[s\mapsto\mu^{\prime}(s)]
is decreasing for

. We will achieve that by applying the weak maximum principle several times.

(i)
First, we apply it to
[s\mapsto h_{s}]
, where we recall from (
4.2
) that
h_{s}
solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\big(r\,\partial_{r}h_{s}\big)+s\,h_{s}=\frac{-2}{r^{3}}+s\,\frac{2-r}{r}\,,\\
&h_{s}(1)=h_{s}(2)=0\,.\end{cases}

Because
\frac{2-r}{r}
solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\big(r\,\partial_{r}f(r)\big)=-\frac{2}{r^{3}}\,,\\
&f(1)=1\,,\quad f(2)=0\,,\end{cases}

it follows that
\frac{2-r}{r}-h_{s}
is a solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&-\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\big(r\,\partial_{r}f(r)\big)+s\,f(r)=0\,,\\
&f(1)=1\,,\quad f(2)=0\,.\end{cases}

An application of the weak maximum principle yields

(4.8)

\displaystyle h_{s}\leq\frac{2-r}{r}\,,\qquad s\geq 0\,.

For
s>\tilde{s}\geq 0
, the difference
h_{s}-h_{\tilde{s}}
solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\big(r\partial_{r}f(r)\big)+sf(r)=(s-\tilde{s})\Big(\frac{2-r}{r}-h_{\tilde{s}}(r)\Big)\,,\\
&f(1)=f(2)=0\,,\end{cases}

where the right-hand side is non-negative thanks to (
4.8
). Consequently, the weak maximum principle yields

(4.9)

\displaystyle h_{s}\geq h_{\tilde{s}}\,,\qquad s>\tilde{s}\geq 0\,.

(ii)
Now, we apply the weak maximum principle for
s\geq 0
to the solution
p_{s}=\partial_{s}h_{s}
to (

), i.e. to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\big(r\,\partial_{r}p_{s}\big)+s\,p_{s}=\frac{2-r}{r}-h_{s}\,,\\
&p_{s}(1)=p_{s}(2)=0\,.\end{cases}

Due to (
4.8
), the right-hand side of this equation is non-negative and the weak maximum principle yields
p_{s}\geq 0
. For
s>\tilde{s}\geq 0
, we then find that
p_{s}-p_{\tilde{s}}
solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\big(r\,\partial_{r}f\big)+s\,f=(h_{\tilde{s}}-h_{s})+(\tilde{s}-s)p_{\tilde{s}}\,,\\
&f(1)=f(2)=0\end{cases}

with the non-positive right-hand side thanks to
p_{\tilde{s}}\geq 0
and (
4.9
). Applying the weak maxiumum principle once more, we see that
p_{s}-p_{\tilde{s}}
attains its maximum at

and hence

\displaystyle\partial_{r}p_{s}(1)\leq\partial_{r}p_{\tilde{s}}(1)\,,\qquad s>\tilde{s}\geq 0\,,

which shows
\mu^{\prime}(s)\leq\mu^{\prime}(\tilde{s})
for
s>\tilde{s}\geq 0
as claimed.
∎

Remark 4.5

Since

are ordered

∎

6.
Discussion on Parameter Relations

Finally, we compare the values for
\sigma_{crit}
and
\sigma_{cyl}
. The outcome points towards a balancing effect of the electrostatic force confirming observations from the simplified model in
[
25
]
. Recall that, in general,
\sigma
gives the ratio between the radius of the metal rings between which the soap film is spanned divided by half of their distance. The first characteristic value of
\sigma
is the critical ratio
\sigma_{crit}\approx 1.5
from (
1.4
) below which no stationary solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) with
\lambda=0
exist. From
[
7
]
and the parabolic comparison principle, it even follows that for
\sigma<\sigma_{crit}
there exists no global solution for the film’s dynamics at all. The second characteristic value of
\sigma
is
\sigma_{cyl}
from (
1.11
) at which the stability of the cylinder and also that of
u^{\lambda}_{cyl}
switches. The value is given approximately by

\displaystyle\sigma_{cyl}=\frac{2\sqrt{s_{0}}}{\pi}\leq\frac{2\,\sqrt{4.2}}{\pi}\approx 1.3\,.

Herein, the inequality is formally justified by a numerical plot, see Figure
6.1
, which shows that the unique zero
s_{0}
of the eigencurve profile
[s\mapsto\mu(s)]
satisfies
s_{0}\leq 4.2
. In summary, we have
\sigma_{cyl}<\sigma_{crit}
. Hence, for
\sigma\in(\sigma_{cyl},\sigma_{crit})
and
suitably scaled electrostatic force, we observe stability of the cylinder by Theorem
1.2
– in particular, many solutions to the dynamical version of (
1.1
)-(
1.3
),see (
1.5
), with
\lambda
close to
\lambda_{cyl}
do exist globally in time –
while in absence of the electrostatic force, i.e. for
\lambda=0
in the dynamical version of (
1.1
)-(
1.3
), see (
1.5
), all solutions cease to exist after a finite time.
In other words, this is a first indication that the electrostatic force might be used to prevent the soap film bridge from pinching off.

\displaystyle\quad\ \ +\left[-\sigma^{2}(2-r)\Big(\partial_{z}^{2}v+\frac{2(\partial_{z}v)^{2}}{1-v}\Big)+\frac{1}{2v+(1-v)r}\right]\partial_{r}w\,.

In divergence form, this operator reads

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2v+(1-v)r}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(i)

\displaystyle DF(0)v=\sigma^{2}\partial_{z}^{2}v+v\,.

A.2.
Odd Cosine Sums

We present a sufficient condition for an odd cosine sum to be positive on
(-1,1)
. In the following, a cosine sum is odd if each cosine is scaled by an odd multiple of
\pi/2
.

As preparation, we reduce odd cosine sums to even ones by applying trigonometric identities, see
[
4
, Lemma 5]
.

Lemma A.1

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{0},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
. Consider the sum of odd cosines

\displaystyle f(z):=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}\cos\bigg(\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{2}\,z\bigg)\,,\qquad z\in(-1,1)\,.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{f(z)}{\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}z)}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}b_{j}\cos(j\pi z)\,,\qquad z\in(-1,1)\,,

with coefficients

\displaystyle b_{0}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}a_{k}\,,\qquad b_{j}:=2\sum_{k=j}^{n}(-1)^{k-j}a_{k}\,,\qquad j=1,\dots,n\,.

Proof
.
This follows by induction. For the step from
n-1
to
n
, we apply the trigonometric identity

\displaystyle\cos\bigg(\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2}z\bigg)+\cos\bigg(\frac{(2n-1)\pi}{2}z\bigg)=2\cos(n\pi z)\cos\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}z\Big)

to rewrite
f
as

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}a_{j}\cos\bigg(\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{2}\,z\bigg)+(a_{n-1}-a_{n})\cos\bigg(\frac{(2(n-1)+1)\pi}{2}\,z\bigg)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +2a_{n}\cos(n\pi z)\cos\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}z\Big)\,.

Consequently, the coefficients
b_{0}
to
b_{n-1}
of
\frac{f(z)}{\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}z)}
are given by
1
1

1

For
b_{n-1}
, we use the convention
\sum_{k=n-1}^{n-2}(-1)^{k-(n-1)}a_{k}=0
.

\displaystyle b_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{k}a_{k}+(-1)^{n-1}(a_{n-1}-a_{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}a_{k}\,,

\displaystyle b_{j}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=j}^{n-2}(-1)^{k-j}a_{k}+2(-1)^{n-1-j}(a_{n-1}-a_{n})=2\sum_{k=j}^{n}(-1)^{k-j}a_{k}\,,\qquad j=1,\dots,n-1,

while

\displaystyle\vskip-19.91692ptb_{n}=2a_{n}=2\sum_{k=n}^{n}(-1)^{k-n}a_{k}

is also fulfilled.
∎

Now, the condition for positivity reads:

Lemma A.2

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{0},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, let
C>0
with

\displaystyle a_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(2j+1)\,|a_{j}|\geq C>0\,.

Then, the sum of odd cosines

\displaystyle f(z):=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}\cos\bigg(\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{2}\,z\bigg)\,,\qquad z\in(-1,1)

satisfies

f(z)\geq C\,\cos\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}z\Big)\,,\qquad z\in(-1,1)\,.

In particular,
f(z)>0
for each
z\in(-1,1)
and
f_{z}(\pm 1)\neq 0
.

Proof
.
We prove the equivalent statement

\displaystyle\frac{f(z)}{\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}z)}\geq C\,,\qquad z\in(-1,1).

Lemma
A.1
yields

\displaystyle\frac{f(z)}{\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}z)}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}b_{j}\cos(j\pi z)

with coefficients

\displaystyle b_{0}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}a_{k}\,,\quad b_{j}=2\sum_{k=j}^{n}(-1)^{k-j}a_{k}\,,\qquad j=1,\dots,n\,,

so that

\displaystyle\frac{f(z)}{\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}z)}

\displaystyle\geq b_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}|b_{j}|\geq\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}a_{k}-2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=j}^{n}|a_{k}|

\displaystyle\geq a_{0}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{k}|-2\sum_{j=1}^{n}j\,|a_{j}|=a_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(2j+1)|a_{j}|\geq C\,.

∎

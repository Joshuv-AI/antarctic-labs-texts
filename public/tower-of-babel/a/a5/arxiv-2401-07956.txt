Taylor Daniels

Abstract
. We demonstrate some 10-periodic properties of the coefficients of two q q -series related to partition numbers signed by the Legendre symbol ( a 5 ) (\frac{a}{5}) and to the Rogers-Ramanujan continued fraction R ⁡ ( q ) R(q) .

1.
Introduction

The
partitions
of a given
n\in\mathbb{N}
are the tuples
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
of positive integers such that
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}=n
and
a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{k}
. In this paper we consider two
q
-series arising naturally from the notion of partitions “signed” by the Legendre symbols
\chi_{p}(n):=(\frac{n}{p})
associated to odd primes
p
; namely
\chi_{p}(n)
is
1
(or
-1
) when
n
is (or is not) a quadratic residue modulo
p
, and
\chi_{p}(n)
is
0
when
p\mid n
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any partition
\pi=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
of
n
, let

\displaystyle\chi_{p}(\pi)

\displaystyle:=\chi_{p}(a_{1})\chi_{p}(a_{2})\cdots\chi_{p}(a_{k})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\chi_{p}^{\dagger}(\pi):=(-1)^{k}\chi_{p}(\pi).

With this notation we define the integers
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})
and
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p}^{\dagger})
via

(1.1)

\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi[n]}\chi_{p}(\pi)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p}^{\dagger})=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi[n]}\chi_{p}^{\dagger}(\pi),

where
\Pi[n]
is the set of all partitions of
n
.

The quantities
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})
are the
Legendre-signed partition numbers
(associated to
5
) as introduced and studied in
[
2
]
. In
[
2
]
*Thm. 1.7, it is shown that as
n\to\infty
, one has

(1.2)

\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})=\big(\tfrac{3+\sqrt{5}}{960}\big)^{\frac{1}{4}}n^{-\frac{3}{4}}\exp\!\Big(\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{8}{15}n}\,\Big)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathfrak{S}(n)+O(n^{-\frac{1}{5}})}}\right],

where

\mathfrak{S}(n):=1+(-1)^{n}\Big(\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}\Big)+\sqrt{2(5-\sqrt{5})}\cos\!\Big(\frac{2\pi n}{5}-\frac{\pi}{10}\Big).

Because
\mathfrak{S}(n)=0
for all
n\equiv 2\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,10)
and
\mathfrak{S}(n)\neq 0
for all other
n
, formula (
1.2
) suggests that
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})=0
for all sufficiently large
n\equiv 2\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,10)
. Confirming both this periodic vanishing of
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})
and a similar periodic vanishing of
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5}^{\dagger})
is the primary objective of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

One has
\mathfrak{p}(10j+2,\chi_{5})=0
and
\mathfrak{p}(10j+6,\chi_{5}^{\dagger})=0
for all
j\geq 0
.

Considering Theorem
1.1
, we say that
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})

vanishes on the arithmetic progression

(10j+2)_{j\geq 0}
.
This vanishing is surprising in light of the following result from
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
2
]
*Thm. 1.10)
.

If
p
is an odd prime such that
p\neq 5
and
p\not\equiv 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8)
, then the sequence
(\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p}))_{\mathbb{N}}
does not vanish on any arithmetic progression.

We remark that Theorem
1.2
is established by showing that the asymptotic formulae for
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})
(analogous to formula (
1.2
)) are dominated by a single positive exponential term, and thus
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})\to\infty
for the
p
in the theorem.
In addition to the vanishings of Theorem
1.1
, we establish two further relations between
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})
and
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5}^{\dagger})
.

Theorem 1.3
.

One has
\mathfrak{p}(10j,\chi_{5}^{\dagger})=\mathfrak{p}(10j,\chi_{5})
and
\mathfrak{p}(10j+8,\chi_{5}^{\dagger})=-\mathfrak{p}(10j+8,\chi_{5})
for all
j\geq 0
.

We consider
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})
and
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5}^{\dagger})
primarily through the lens of
q
-
series
, that is, series of the form
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{n}
defined for
|q|<1
. Using the notations

(z;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-zq^{n})\qquad\text{and}\qquad(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m};q)_{\infty}=(z_{1};q)_{\infty}\cdots(z_{m};q)_{\infty},

where
|q|<1
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we define

(1.3)

X(q):=(q,-q^{2},-q^{3},q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}\qquad\text{and}\qquad Y(q):=(-q,q^{2},q^{3},-q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}

so that

(1.4)

\frac{1}{X(q)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})q^{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{1}{Y(q)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5}^{\dagger})q^{n}.

The key to our analyses of the series in (
1.4
) is the recognition that, since

X(q)Y(q)=(q^{2},q^{4},q^{6},q^{8};q^{10})_{\infty}=\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{10};q^{10})_{\infty}},

we have the useful pair of relations

(1.5)

\frac{1}{X(q)}=\frac{Y(q)(q^{10};q^{10})_{\infty}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{1}{Y(q)}=\frac{X(q)(q^{10};q^{10})_{\infty}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}.

In particular, relations (
1.5
) allow us to leverage results on the well-studied function
(q;q)_{\infty}^{-1}
into results on
X(q)^{-1}
and
Y(q)^{-1}
.

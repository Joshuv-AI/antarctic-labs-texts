Asymptotic formula for the M 2 M_{2} -ranks of overpartitions


Abstract
Let N ¯ 2 ​ ( a , c , n ) {\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n) be the number of overpartitions of n n whose the M 2 M_{2} -rank is congruent to a a modulo c c . In this paper, we obtain the asymptotic formula of N ¯ 2 ​ ( a , c , n ) {\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n) utilizing the Ingham Tauberian Theorem. As applications, we derive inequalities concerning with N ¯ 2 ​ ( a , c , n ) {\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n) including its strict concavity and log-concavity. Introduction

The objective of this paper is to give an asymptotic formula for the
M_{2}
-rank of overpartition.
Based on this formula,
we derived several inequalities on the
M_{2}
-rank of overpartition such as concavity and log-concavity.

Hardy and Ramanujan
[
15
]
initially introduced the celebrated Circle Method (now known as the Hardy-Ramanujan circle method) to
obtain the asymptotic formula for integer partitions originated from finding an exact formula for it.
Recall that a partition of a positive integer
n
is a sequence of non-increasing positive integers whose sum equals
n
and
p(n)
is defined as the number of partitions of
n
.
Hardy and Ramanujan
[
15
]
proved that

\displaystyle p(n)\sim\frac{1}{4n\sqrt{3}}e^{\pi\sqrt{\frac{2n}{3}}},~\text{for}~n\rightarrow\infty.

Asymptotic formulas have been proven as powerful tools to gain inequalities of the partition function.
For example,
DeSalvo and Pak
[
10
]
established strong log-concavity of the
p(n)
, that is, for all
n>m>1
,

p(n)^{2}>p(n-m)p(n+m)

from the asymptotic formula above.
See
[
2
,
8
]
for further applications.

Other statistics involving partitions have been introduced since.
To interpret Ramanujan’s congruences for
p(n)
combinatorially,
Dyson
[
12
]
introduced the rank of a partition.
This is defined as the largest part of the partition minus the number of parts.
Bringmann obtained the asymptotic formula of
N(a,c,n)
in
[
3
]
by the Hardy-Ramanujan circle method. Moreover, she confirmed the conjecture which was given by Andrews and Lewis
[
1
]
,

\displaystyle N(0,3,n)<N(1,3,n)

\displaystyle~\text{ if }n\equiv 0\text{ or }2\quad(\bmod 3),

\displaystyle N(0,3,n)>N(1,3,n)

\displaystyle~\text{ if }n\equiv 1\quad(\bmod 3).

With the help of the Ingham Tauberian Theorem, Males
[
20
]
stated another asymptotic formula of
N(a,c,n)
, that is, for fixed
0\leq r<t
and
t\geq 2
,

N(r,t,n)\sim\frac{1}{4tn\sqrt{3}}e^{2\pi\sqrt{\frac{n}{6}}},~\text{as}~n\rightarrow\infty.

The asymptotic formula yields that when
0\leq r<t
and
t\geq 2
,

N(r,t,a)N(r,t,b)>N(r,t,a+b)

for sufficiently large
a
and
b
.
Several other asymptotic formulas and inequalities for rank of a partition have also been obtained, for example
[
5
,
16
,
11
,
13
]
.

Bringmann and Males illustrated two different approaches to studying the asymptotic behavior of the coefficients of a series:
One can either use the Hardy-Ramanujan circle method
[
6
,
15
]

or apply the Ingham Tauberian Theorem
[
17
,
18
]
.
In this paper,
we study an asymptotic formula for the
M_{2}
-rank of overpartition
in the spirit of The Ingham Tauberian Theorem.

The
M_{2}
-rank for overpartitions was introduced by Lovejoy
[
19
]
.
Recall that an overpartition
[
9
]
of a nonnegative integer
n
is a partition of
n
where the first occurrence of each distinct part may be overlined. Denote
{\overline{p}}(n)
by the number of overpartitions of
n
.
Let
l(\lambda)
be the largest part of
\lambda
,
n(\lambda)
be the number of parts of
\lambda
and
n(\lambda_{o})
be the number of odd non-overlined parts of
\lambda
.
Then the
M_{2}
-rank of an overpartition
\lambda
is defined as

M_{2}\text{-}\mathrm{rank}(\lambda)=\left\lceil\frac{l(\lambda)}{2}\right\rceil-n(\lambda)-n(\lambda_{o})-\chi(\lambda),

where
\chi(\lambda)=1
if
l(\lambda)
is odd and non-overlined and
\chi(\lambda)=0
otherwise.
Denote
{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)
by the number of overpartitions of
n
whose the
M_{2}
-rank is congruent to
a
modulo
c
.
By investigating the asymptotic behavior of the Appell function associated with the generating function of
{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)
,
we confirm that the
M_{2}
-rank of overpartition fulfills the conditions in the Ingham Tauberian Theorem.
Hence we obtain the following asymptotic formula for the
M_{2}
-rank of overpartition.

Theorem 1.1
.

If the odd integer
c\geq 2
, then for any
0\leq a<c
we have

{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)\sim\frac{1}{c}{\overline{p}}(n)\sim\frac{1}{8cn}e^{\pi\sqrt{n}}

(1.1)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

We deduce strict concavity of
{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)
by applying Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If the odd integer
c\geq 2
, then for any
0\leq a<c
we have

{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{1}){\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{2})>{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{1}+n_{2})

for sufficiently large
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
.

The following inequality also serves as an application of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
c\geq 2
be an odd integer and
0\leq a<c
.
Then for sufficiently large
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
with
n_{1}<n_{2}+1
,
we have

\displaystyle{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{1}){\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{2})>{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{1}-1){\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{2}+1).

Setting
n_{1}=n_{2}
in Theorem
1.3
,
we show that
{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)
is asymptotically log-concave.

Corollary 1.4
.

If the odd integer
c\geq 2
, then for any
0\leq a<c
, we have

{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)^{2}>{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n-1){\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n+1)

for sufficiently large
n
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we introduce the Ingham Tauberian Theorem
[
4
,
17
]
and the monotonicity of rank of overpartition obtained by Xiong and Zang
[
21
]
.
In Section
3
, we obtain the asymptotic behavior of the Appell function associated with the generating function of
{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)
.
In Section
4

1.1
and provide its applications in deriving inequalities (Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
).

2
Preliminary

In this section, we recall some facts about the Appell function without proof.
Let the Appell function
[
22
]

\mu(u,z;\tau):=\frac{e^{\pi iu}}{\vartheta(z;\tau)}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{n}e^{\pi i(n^{2}+n)\tau}e^{2\pi inz}}{1-e^{2\pi in\tau}e^{2\pi iu}},

(2.1)

where Jacobi theta function

\vartheta(z;\tau):=\sum_{n\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}}e^{\pi in^{2}\tau+2\pi in\left(z+\frac{1}{2}\right)},

(2.2)

with
z\in\mathbb{C}
. And
\vartheta
satisfies

\vartheta(z;\tau)=\frac{i}{\sqrt{-i\tau}}e^{\frac{-\pi iz^{2}}{\tau}}\vartheta\left(\frac{z}{\tau};-\frac{1}{\tau}\right).

(2.3)

What’s more, Zwegers showed that

\mu(u,v;\tau)=\frac{-1}{\sqrt{-i\tau}}e^{\frac{\pi i(u-v)^{2}}{\tau}}\mu\left(\frac{u}{\tau},\frac{v}{\tau};-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)+\frac{1}{2i}h(u-v;\tau),

(2.4)

where
h
is the Mordell integral

h(z;\tau):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{\pi i\tau x^{2}-2\pi zx}}{\cosh(\pi x)}dx,

(2.5)

which satisfied the following transformation

h(z;\tau)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-i\tau}}e^{\frac{\pi iz^{2}}{\tau}}h\left(\frac{z}{\tau};-\frac{1}{\tau}\right).

(2.6)

Recently, Zwegers
[
23
]
introduced the Appell function of higher level

A_{\ell}(u,v;\tau):=e^{\pi i\ell u}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{\ell n}q^{\frac{\ell n(n+1)}{2}}e^{2\pi inv}}{1-e^{2\pi iu}q^{n}},

(2.7)

which satisfies the following transformation formulae

\displaystyle A_{\ell}(u,v;\tau)=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}e^{2\pi iuk}\vartheta\left(v+k\tau+\frac{\ell-1}{2};\ell\tau\right)\mu\left(\ell u,v+k\tau+\frac{\ell-1}{2};\ell\tau\right).

(2.8)

In this paper, we shall use the following theorem

[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
due to Ingham
[
17
]
to obtain the asymptotic formula of
{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f(q):=\sum_{n\geq 0}a(n)q^{n}
be a power series with weakly increasing nonnegative coefficients and radius of convergence equal to one. If there exist constants

A>0,\lambda,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that

f(e^{-t})\sim\lambda t^{\alpha}e^{\frac{A}{t}}~~~~\text{as}~t\rightarrow 0^{+}

and

f(e^{-\varepsilon})\ll\left|\varepsilon\right|^{\alpha}e^{\frac{A}{\left|\varepsilon\right|}}~~~~\text{as}~\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

(2.9)

with
\varepsilon=x+iy~(x,~y\in\mathbb{R},x>0)
in each region of the form
|y|\leq\Delta x
for
\Delta>0
,

then

a(n)\sim\frac{\lambda}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{A^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}{n^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{3}{4}}}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Remark 2.2
.

Bringmann, Jennings-Schaffer and Mahlburg
[
4
, Remark of Theorem 1.1]
noted that if in each region
|y|\leq\Delta x

f(e^{-\varepsilon})\sim\lambda\varepsilon^{\alpha}e^{\frac{A}{\varepsilon}},

then the second bound in (
2.9
) is automatically satisfied.

Let
{\overline{N}}_{2}(m,n)
denote the number of overpartitions of
n
with the
M_{2}
-rank
m
.
Xiong and Zang
[
21
]
proved the following inequality on
{\overline{N}}_{2}(m,n)
conjectured by Chan and Mao
[
7
]
.
We highlight this result to confirm the monotonicity of
{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)
in Theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For all
m\geq 0
and
n>0
, we have

{\overline{N}}_{2}(m,n)\geq{\overline{N}}_{2}(m,n-1).

3
Asymptotic behavior of
A_{1}(\pm z,\tau;2\tau)

In this section, we investigate the asymptotic behavior of the Appell function
A_{1}(\pm z,\tau;2\tau)
associated with the generating function of
{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)
.
This confirms the asymptotic condition in the Ingham Tauberian Theorem in our context.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
0<z<\frac{1}{2}
, and
\tau=\frac{i\varepsilon}{2\pi}
with
\varepsilon=x+iy
in each region of the form
|y|\leq\Delta x
for
\Delta>0
then

A_{1}(\pm z,\tau;2\tau)\rightarrow 0

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Proof.

Using (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle A_{1}(\pm z,\tau;2\tau)=\vartheta(\tau;2\tau)\mu(\pm z,\tau;2\tau).

Then combining (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we find that

\displaystyle A_{1}(\pm z,\tau;2\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\tau}e^{\frac{\pi i(z^{2}\mp 2z\tau)}{2\tau}}\vartheta\left(\frac{1}{2};-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right)\mu\left(\frac{\pm z}{2\tau},\frac{1}{2};-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\sqrt{-8i\tau}}e^{\frac{-\pi i{\tau}}{2}}\vartheta\left(\frac{1}{2};-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right)h(\pm z-\tau;2\tau).

(3.1)

Let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
denote the summations on the right-hand side of (
3
), respectively.
We first investigate the terms from
S_{1}
. By (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\vartheta\left(\frac{1}{2};-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right)\mu\left(\frac{\pm z}{2\tau},\frac{1}{2};-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\pm\pi iz}{2\tau}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{n}e^{\frac{-\pi i(n^{2}+n)}{2\tau}}e^{\pi in}}{1-e^{\frac{-\pi in}{\tau}}e^{\frac{\pm\pi iz}{\tau}}}

\displaystyle=q_{0}^{\mp\frac{z}{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{q_{0}^{\frac{n^{2}+n}{2}}}{1-q_{0}^{n\mp z}},

(3.2)

where
q_{0}:=e^{-\frac{\pi i}{\tau}}=e^{-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\varepsilon}}
.

In view of (
3
),
S_{1}
can be rewritten as

S_{1}=\frac{1}{2\tau}q_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}(z^{2}\pm z)}e^{\mp\pi iz}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{q_{0}^{\frac{n^{2}+n}{2}}}{1-q_{0}^{n\mp z}}.

Noting that

\left|e^{-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\right|=e^{-\frac{x}{x^{2}+y^{2}}}\leq e^{-\frac{1}{(1+\Delta^{2})x}}\leq e^{-\frac{1}{(1+\Delta^{2})|\varepsilon|}},

so if the lowest power of
q_{0}
is positive,
S_{1}
tends to
0
in our asymptotic limit.

We first consider the plus sign, that is

S_{1}^{+}:=\frac{1}{2\tau}q_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}(z^{2}+z)}e^{-\pi iz}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{q_{0}^{\frac{n^{2}+n}{2}}}{1-q_{0}^{n-z}}.

Considering only the inner sum without the prefactor, the
n=0
term is

\frac{1}{1-q_{0}^{-z}}=\frac{-q_{0}^{z}}{1-q_{0}^{z}}=-q_{0}^{z}-q_{0}^{2z}-\cdots,

where
z\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
.
For
n\geq 1
, it is clear that the lowest power of
q_{0}
in the inner sum is
1
.
When
n<0
, we have
n-z<0
and hence the term

\frac{q_{0}^{\frac{n^{2}+n}{2}}}{1-q_{0}^{n-z}}=-q_{0}^{\frac{n^{2}-n+2z}{2}}\sum_{j\geq 0}q_{0}^{(z-n)j},

with the lowest order term
-q_{0}^{\frac{n^{2}-n+2z}{2}}
. Obviously,
\frac{n^{2}-n+2z}{2}\geq 1+z
.
Therefore, for
z\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, the lowest power of
q_{0}
is given by the
n=0
term, which is

-\frac{1}{2\tau}q_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}(z^{2}+z)}e^{-\pi iz}q_{0}^{z}=-\frac{1}{2\tau}q_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}(z^{2}-z)}e^{-\pi iz}.

Using the facts that
0<z<\frac{1}{2}
,
-\frac{1}{2}(z^{2}-z)>0
, we infer that
S_{1}^{+}\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

We next deal with the minus sign

S_{1}^{-}:=\frac{1}{2\tau}q_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}(z^{2}-z)}e^{\pi iz}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{q_{0}^{\frac{n^{2}+n}{2}}}{1-q_{0}^{n+z}}.

Similar to the plus sign
S_{1}^{+}
, we consider the three cases about
n
. Then we find that the lowest power of
q_{0}
is also given by the
n=0
term, that is

\frac{1}{2\tau}q_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}(z^{2}-z)}e^{\pi iz}.

Since
-\frac{1}{2}(z^{2}-z)>0
, we have
S_{1}^{-}\rightarrow 0
.

We now verify the remaining term
S_{2}\rightarrow 0
as well.
Applying (
2.6
), we find that

\displaystyle S_{2}=\frac{i}{4\tau}e^{\frac{\pi i(z^{2}\mp 2z\tau)}{2\tau}}\vartheta\left(\frac{1}{2};-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right)h\left(\frac{\pm z-\tau}{2\tau};-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right).

On the one hand, by (
2.2
), we have

\vartheta\left(\frac{1}{2};-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right)=\sum_{n\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\frac{\pi in^{2}}{2\tau}+2\pi in}=\sum_{n\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}}q_{0}^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}}e^{2\pi in}.

It is obvious that the smallest power of
q_{0}
appearing in
e^{\frac{\pi i(z^{2}\mp 2z\tau)}{2\tau}}\vartheta\left(\frac{1}{2};-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right)
is given by (
n=\pm\frac{1}{2}
)

q_{0}^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{8}}e^{\mp\pi iz}\left(e^{\pi i}+e^{-\pi i}\right).

Then the power of
q_{0}
in
e^{\frac{\pi i(z^{2}\mp 2z\tau)}{2\tau}}\vartheta\left(\frac{1}{2};-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right)
is positive due to
0<z<\frac{1}{2}
. Hence these terms give no contribution in the limiting situation.

On the other hand, with the definition of
h(z;\tau)
in (
2.5
), we find that

\displaystyle h\left(\frac{\pm z-\tau}{2\tau};-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{-\frac{\pi ix^{2}}{2\tau}-2\pi\left(\frac{\pm z}{2\tau}-\frac{1}{2}\right)x}}{\cosh(\pi x)}dx

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{-\pi iz^{2}}{2\tau}\pm\pi iz}\int_{\mathbb{R}\pm iz}\frac{e^{-\frac{\pi iw^{2}}{2\tau}+\pi w}}{\cosh\left(\pi(w\mp iz)\right)}dw,

here we let
w=x\pm iz
.
Since
0<z<\frac{1}{2}
, the integrand has no poles. So we can shift the path of integration back to
\mathbb{R}
,

h\left(\frac{\pm z-\tau}{2\tau};-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right)=e^{\frac{-\pi iz^{2}}{2\tau}\pm\pi iz}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{-\frac{\pi iw^{2}}{2\tau}+\pi w}}{\cosh\left(\pi(w\mp iz)\right)}dw.

Using the trivial bound

\frac{1}{\left|\cosh\left(\pi(w\mp iz)\right)\right|}=\frac{2}{\left|e^{\pi(w\mp iz)}+e^{-\pi(w\mp iz)}\right|}\leq\frac{2}{\left|e^{-\pi iz}+e^{\pi iz}\right|}=\left|\sec(\pi z)\right|,

and the fact
\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-t^{2}}dt=\sqrt{\pi}
,
we find that

\left|h\left(\frac{\pm z-\tau}{2\tau};-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right)\right|\leq\left|\sec(\pi z)\right|\exp\left(-\frac{\pi^{2}z^{2}}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\tau=\frac{i\varepsilon}{2\pi}
.
Hence, we have

\left|\frac{i}{4\tau}h\left(\frac{\pm z-\tau}{2\tau};-\frac{1}{2\tau}\right)\right|\leq\left|\sec(\pi z)\right|\exp\left(-\frac{\pi^{2}z^{2}}{\varepsilon}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)\frac{\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

It is easy to see that as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
this contribution vanishes. This completes the proof.

4
Asymptotic formula and inequalities

In this section, we employ the Ingham Tauberian Theorem on the generating function of
{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)
to deduce its asymptotic formula.
Several inequalities concerning on
{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)
including its strict concavity and log-concavity are obtained based on this formula.

We basically handle with the generating function of
{\overline{N}}_{2}(m,n)
.
Lovejoy
[
19
]
found that

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{2}(\zeta,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}{\overline{N}}_{2}(m,n)\zeta^{m}q^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1;q)_{2n}q^{n}}{(\zeta q^{2};q^{2})_{n}(\zeta^{-1}q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(1-\zeta)(1-\zeta^{-1})(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}+2n}}{(1-\zeta q^{2n})(1-\zeta^{-1}q^{2n})},

(4.1)

where

(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{n})~~\text{and}~~(a;q)_{n}=\frac{(a;q)_{\infty}}{(aq^{n};q)_{\infty}}.

In particular,

\overline{R}_{2}(1,q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\overline{p}}(n)q^{n}.

The following lemmas play central roles in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For integer
0\leq a<c
and odd integer
c
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{c}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\overline{p}}(n)q^{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{c-1}{2}}\left(\zeta_{c}^{-aj}+\zeta_{c}^{aj}\right)\overline{R}_{2}(\zeta_{c}^{j},q)\right],

(4.2)

where
\zeta_{c}=e^{{2\pi i}/c}
and
q=e^{2\pi i\tau},\tau\in\mathbb{H}
.

To verify (
4.2
), we need to show that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\overline{p}}(n)q^{n}+\frac{1}{c}\sum_{j=1}^{c-1}\zeta_{c}^{-aj}\overline{R}_{2}(\zeta_{c}^{j},q),

(4.3)

where
\zeta
is the root of unity.

By (
4
), we notice that the right hand side of (
4.3
) can be expanded as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\overline{p}}(n)q^{n}+\frac{1}{c}\sum_{j=1}^{c-1}\zeta_{c}^{-aj}\overline{R}_{2}(\zeta_{c}^{j},q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{j~~(\text{mod}~c)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}{\overline{N}}_{2}(m,n)\zeta_{c}^{mj}\zeta_{c}^{-aj}q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}{\overline{N}}_{2}(m,n)\left(\sum_{j~~(\text{mod}~c)}\zeta_{c}^{mj-aj}\right)q^{n}.

By the orthogonality of the roots of unity

\sum_{j\pmod{c}}\zeta_{c}^{rj}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&r\not\equiv 0\pmod{c},\\
c,&r\equiv 0\pmod{c},\end{array}\right.

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{p}(n)q^{n}+\frac{1}{c}\sum_{j=1}^{c-1}\zeta^{-aj}\overline{R}_{2}\left(\zeta_{c}^{j};q\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\infty\atop m\equiv a\pmod{c}}^{\infty}{\overline{N}}_{2}(m,n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)q^{n},

which gives (
4.3
). Since
\overline{R}_{2}\left(\zeta;q\right)=\overline{R}_{2}\left(\zeta^{-1};q\right)
, then applying (
4.3
), we can get (
4.2
).

Then we prove an identity on the
M_{2}
-rank generating function
\overline{R}_{2}(\zeta,q)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{H},z\in\mathbb{R},q:=e^{2\pi i\tau}
and
\zeta:=e^{2\pi iz}
. We have

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{2}(\zeta,q)=\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\frac{(1-\zeta)}{1+\zeta}\left\{\zeta^{\frac{1}{2}}A_{1}(z,\tau;2\tau)-\zeta^{-\frac{1}{2}}A_{1}(-z,\tau;2\tau)\right\}.

(4.4)

Proof.

One can easily derive that

\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta q^{2n}}-\frac{\zeta^{-1}}{1-\zeta^{-1}q^{2n}}=\frac{\zeta-\zeta^{-1}}{\left(1-\zeta q^{2n}\right)\left(1-\zeta^{-1}q^{2n}\right)}.

(4.5)

Substituting (
4.5
) into (
4
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{2}(\zeta,q)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}+2n}\cdot\left(\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta q^{2n}}-\frac{\zeta^{-1}}{1-\zeta^{-1}q^{2n}}\right)\frac{(1-\zeta)(1-\zeta^{-1})}{\zeta-\zeta^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\frac{1-\zeta}{1+\zeta}\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}+2n}\zeta}{1-\zeta q^{2n}}-\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}+2n}\zeta^{-1}}{1-\zeta^{-1}q^{2n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\frac{(1-\zeta)}{1+\zeta}\left\{\zeta^{\frac{1}{2}}A_{1}(z,\tau;2\tau)-\zeta^{-\frac{1}{2}}A_{1}(-z,\tau;2\tau)\right\}.

This completes the proof.

Now we are in a position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We can rewrite
{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)
as

{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\overline{N}}_{2}(a+kc,n),

which is a finite sum, since for
|a+kc|>n
we have
{\overline{N}}_{2}(a+kc,n)=0
.

By Lemma
2.3
, we derive that
for all non-negative integers
m
and positive integers
n
,

{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)\geq{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n-1).

We are therefore in the situation where we may apply Theorem
2.1
.
By Remark
2.2
, we only need to investigate the asymptotic behavior

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)e^{-\varepsilon n}.

Combining (
4.2
) and (
4.4
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)q^{n}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\overline{p}}(n)q^{n}\Bigg(1+\sum_{j=1}^{\frac{c-1}{2}}\left(\zeta_{c}^{-aj}+\zeta_{c}^{aj}\right)\frac{(1-\zeta_{c}^{j})}{1+\zeta_{c}^{j}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\zeta_{2c}^{j}A_{1}(\frac{j}{c},\tau;2\tau)-\zeta_{2c}^{-j}A_{1}(-\frac{j}{c},\tau;2\tau)\right)\Bigg).

Let
\tau=\frac{i\varepsilon}{2\pi}
with
\varepsilon=x+iy
in each region
|y|\leq\Delta x
for
\Delta>0
and consider
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Since
0<\frac{j}{c}<\frac{1}{2}
, we can know that the term in the biggest brackets is asymptotically equal to
1
in this limit by Theorem
3.1
. Hence we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)e^{-\varepsilon n}\sim\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2c\sqrt{\pi}}e^{\frac{\pi^{2}}{4\varepsilon}}

in these regions of restricted angle.
Then applying Theorem
2.1
we see that for
n\rightarrow\infty

{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n)\sim\frac{1}{8cn}e^{\pi\sqrt{n}}.

By the following well-known fact (see, e.g.,
[
14
]
)

{\overline{p}}(n)\sim\frac{1}{8n}e^{\pi\sqrt{n}},~\text{for}~n\rightarrow\infty,

(4.6)

we arrive at (
1.1
), which completes the proof.

We prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
as applications of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Consider the ratio

\frac{{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{1}){\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{2})}{{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{1}+n_{2})}

as
n_{1},n_{2}\rightarrow\infty
. By Theorem
1.1
we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{1}){\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{2})}{{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{1}+n_{2})}\sim\frac{n_{1}+n_{2}}{8cn_{1}n_{2}}\cdot\frac{e^{\left(\pi\sqrt{n_{1}}+\pi\sqrt{n_{2}}\right)}}{e^{\pi\sqrt{n_{1}+n_{2}}}}>1

as
n_{1},n_{2}\rightarrow\infty
, which completes the proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we have to consider the ratio

\frac{{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{1}){\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{2})}{{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{1}-1){\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{2}+1)}.

For
n_{1}<n_{2}+1
, by Theorem
1.1
, as
n_{1},n_{2}\rightarrow\infty
we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{1}){\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{2})}{{\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{1}-1){\overline{N}}_{2}(a,c,n_{2}+1)}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{(n_{1}-1)(n_{2}+1)}{n_{1}n_{2}}\cdot\frac{e^{\left(\pi\sqrt{n_{1}}+\pi\sqrt{n_{2}}\right)}}{e^{\left(\pi\sqrt{n_{1}-1}+\pi\sqrt{n_{2}+1}\right)}}>1.

This completes the proof.

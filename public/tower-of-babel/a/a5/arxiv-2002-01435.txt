Superfluid flow of polaron polaritons above Landau’s critical velocity
K. Knakkergaard Nielsen, A. Camacho-Guardian, G. M. Bruun, T. Pohl

Abstract
We develop a theory for the interaction of light with superfluid optical media, describing the motion of quantum impurities that are created and dragged through the liquid by propagating photons. It is well known that a mobile impurity suffers dissipation due to phonon emission as soon as it moves faster than the speed of sound in the superfluid – Landau’s critical velocity. Surprisingly we find that in the present hybrid light-matter setting, polaritonic impurities can be protected against environmental decoherence and be allowed to propagate well above the Landau velocity without jeopardizing the superfluid response of the medium.



When an object moves through a superfluid it can do so without friction as long as it is slower than a certain critical velocity. In his seminal work
Landau 1941
, Landau obtained this bound by arguing that a moving impurity can generate excitations only when it exceeds the speed of sound in the superfluid. In this case, the object emits Cherenkov radiation which decelerates its motion. Being a hallmark of superfluidity this effect and the associated Landau velocity have since been investigated in diverse systems, from liquid helium
Allum et al. 1977
;
Brauer et al. 2013
;
Bradley et al. 2016
and exciton-polariton fluids in semiconductor microcavities
Amo et al. 2009
, to ultracold atomic quantum gases
Raman et al. 1999
.

Here, we address this issue by developing a theory for the non-equilibrium dynamics of polaritons in a quantum many-body system under the formation of Fröhlich polarons [see Fig.
1
(a)]. Considering the three-level scheme illustrated in Fig.
1
(b), we demonstrate the emergence of polaron-polariton quasiparticles that can vastly exceed the traditional Landau critical velocity of the medium without suffering phonon-induced decoherence [see Fig.
1
(c)]. This effect, in turn, permits to stabilize and protect an otherwise decaying polaron against phonon-induced decoherence via a vanishingly small photon-component of the formed polariton [see Fig.
1
(d)]. The discovery of such unusual behavior sheds new light on the optical properties of quantum many-body systems and may open up new routes for controlling and mitigating phonon-induced decoherence in light-matter interfaces.

More specifically, we consider a superfluid medium consisting of a weakly interacting atomic Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC), whereby an incident photon may transfer an atom to a different internal quantum state, which then acts as an impurity. Its interaction with the surrounding superfluid generates phonons, which screen the impurity to form a polaronic quasiparticle. To avoid dissipation from radiative decay of the excited state
\ket{e}
, one can apply an additional control field and couple two stable atomic states, the state
\ket{b}
comprising the BEC and the state
\ket{c}
being the impurity state, via a two-photon transition as shown in Fig.
1
(b). On two-photon resonance, the depicted three-level scheme realizes electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT), which affords strong light-matter coupling at virtually vanishing photon losses
Fleischhauer and Lukin 2002
due to the formation of so-called dark-state polaritons
Fleischhauer and Lukin 2000
that propagate with a greatly reduced group velocity,
v_{\rm g}
, as low as a few m/s
Hau et al. 1999
. At such low group velocities, the dark-state polariton is primarily composed of the impurity excitation with a very low photon fraction less than
10^{-6}
Fleischhauer and Lukin 2000
.

Taken separately, these scenarios thus yield two stable quasiparticles: a photon-dressed impurity and a phonon-dressed impurity, which remains stable as long as its velocity is below the Landau velocity, i.e. the speed of sound in the superfluid. Consequently, one would expect that the combined quasiparticle destroys superfluidity
Grusdt and Fleischhauer 2016
as soon as
v_{\rm g}
exceeds Landau’s critical velocity. Surprisingly, this is not the case. First, it turns out that it is not the group velocity which determines the viscosity of its environment, but the total recoil momentum exerted on the impurity state by the two applied light fields. The resulting impurity velocity
v
, is widely tunable via the angle between the two laser fields and can differ vastly from
v_{\rm g}
. Second, we show that
both
of these velocities of the moving impurity can greatly exceed Landau’s critical velocity without destroying the superfluid response of the quantum liquid [see Fig.
1
(c)].

The demonstrated ability to stabilize mobile polaritons in a dissipative environment thus provides an intriguing outlook for realizing coherent optical interfaces and makes it possible to explore and control the combined formation of polaritonic and polaronic quasiparticle states at greatly reduced losses and decoherence. Not only does this combination yield an attractive platform for exploring impurity physics
Grusdt and Fleischhauer 2016
, and suggest novel optical probes of quantum many-body dynamics
Camacho-Guardian et al. 2020
, but also promises new functionalities for light-matter interfaces and optical devices
Sidler et al. 2016
;
Tan et al. 2020
. In the present context, ensuing applications include the generation of few-photon nonlinearities via induced polaron interactions in atomic superfluids
Camacho-Guardian et al. 2018
;
Camacho-Guardian and Bruun 2018
, which may even be controlled and enhanced via resonant phonon-exchange processes. Moreover, as outlined above, the underlying interaction Hamiltonian (
3

Alexandrov and Devreese 2010
, which often presents a limitation to the coherence of light-matter interactions in such systems
Toyozawa 1958
. The EIT-enabled stabilization against phonon-induced dissipation, described in this work, therefore suggests a promising approach to alleviating this obstacle. These combined perspectives motivate future investigations into the strong-coupling regime as well as a wider range of environmental interactions and photon interfaces for exploiting correlated quantum dynamics and exploring quantum nonlinear optics in strongly interacting many-body systems.

Appendix SII
The Hamiltonian

The quantum light field is described by the Hamiltonian

\hat{H}_{\rm l}=\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}ck\cdot\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}\lambda}\hat{\alpha}_{{\bf k}\lambda},

(S1)

where
ck
is the energy of a photon at momentum
{\bf k}
and polarization
\lambda
created by
\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}\lambda}
. The atom-light coupling consists of a classical control field and a quantum field. For the former, we use
\mathbf{E}=\bm{\epsilon}\cdot E_{0}\cos({\bf k}_{\rm cl}\cdot{\bf r}-ck_{\rm cl}t)
, with a (classical) wave vector
{\bf k}_{\rm cl}
and polarization vector
\bm{\epsilon}
. In the dipole approximation the Hamiltonian in first quantization is
-e{\bf r}_{e}\cdot\mathbf{E}
, with
{\bf r}_{e}
the position vector of the electron relative to the atomic nucleus. With the Rabi frequency
\Omega=-eE_{0}\bra{e}{\bf r}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}\ket{c}/2
we can then write the classical control field in second quantization,

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm al}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=2\Omega\int{\rm d}^{3}r\left[\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\rm c}({\bf r})\hat{\psi}_{\rm e}({\bf r})\cos({\bf k}_{\rm cl}\cdot{\bf r}-ck_{\rm cl}t)+{\rm h.c.}\right]\simeq\Omega\int{\rm d}^{3}r\left[\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\rm c}({\bf r})\hat{\psi}_{\rm e}({\bf r}){\rm e}^{-i({\bf k}_{\rm cl}\cdot{\bf r}-ck_{\rm cl}t)}+{\rm h.c.}\right]

\displaystyle=\Omega\sum_{{\bf k}}\left[\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}\hat{e}_{\bf k}{\rm e}^{ick_{\rm cl}t}+{\rm h.c.}\right]=\Omega\sum_{{\bf k}}\left[\hat{\tilde{c}}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}(t)\hat{\tilde{e}}_{\bf k}(t)+{\rm h.c.}\right],

(S2)

where
\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\rm a}({\bf r})
creates an atom in state
\ket{a}
at position
{\bf r}
. In the second equality we make the usual rotating wave approximation. In the second line we first transform to momentum space using
\hat{\psi}_{\rm a}({\bf r})=\sum_{{\bf k}}{\rm e}^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}\hat{a}_{{\bf k}}/\sqrt{V}
for
a=e,c
, with
V
the volume of the gas. Hence,
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bf k}
creates an atom in state
\ket{a}
at momentum
{\bf k}
. We finally describe the Hamiltonian in the frame rotating with the light fields, using
\hat{\tilde{e}}_{{\bf k}}=\hat{e}_{\bf k}\cdot{\rm e}^{icp_{0}t}
and
\hat{\tilde{c}}_{\bf k}=\hat{c}_{\bf k}\cdot{\rm e}^{ic(k_{\rm cl}-p_{0})t}
. Here
cp_{0}
is the carrier frequency of the quantum light field, which we now turn to. We describe the coupling to the quantum light field in terms of a quantized electric field

\hat{\mathbf{E}}({\bf r})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}\sqrt{\frac{ck}{2\epsilon_{0}}}\left[\bm{\epsilon}_{{\bf k}\lambda}\hat{\alpha}_{{\bf k}\lambda}{\rm e}^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}+{\rm h.c.}\right],

(S3)

with
\epsilon_{0}
the vacuum permittivity. The field is transverse:
{\bf k}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}_{{\bf k}\lambda}=0
. We then get

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm al}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}^{3}r\left[\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\rm b}({\bf r})\hat{\psi}_{\rm e}({\bf r})\mathbf{d}_{\rm be}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{E}}({\bf r})+{\rm h.c.}\right]\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}\sum_{{\bf k},{\bf q},\lambda}g_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}\lambda}\left[\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}}\hat{\tilde{e}}_{{\bf k}}(t)\hat{\tilde{\alpha}}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}\lambda}(t)+{\rm h.c.}\right],

(S4)

with the electric dipole moment
\mathbf{d}_{\rm be}=-e\bra{b}{\bf r}\ket{e}
. In turn
g_{{\bf k}\lambda}=\sqrt{ck/2\epsilon_{0}}\;\bm{\epsilon}_{{\bf k}\lambda}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{\rm be}
. We again make the rotating wave approximation and write the fields in the rotating frame, with
\hat{\tilde{\alpha}}_{{\bf k}\lambda}=\hat{\alpha}_{{\bf k}\lambda}{\rm e}^{icp_{0}t}
a temporally slowly varying field when
k\simeq p_{0}
. We can describe the Hamiltonian in terms of time-independent fields, if we further adjust the energies of the photons and the atomic excited and impurity states. Hence, we write

\hat{H}_{0}=\sum_{{\bf k}}\left[\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{{\bf k}}\sum_{\lambda}\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}\lambda}\hat{\alpha}_{{\bf k}\lambda}+\left(\xi_{\bf k}+\Delta\right)\hat{e}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}}\hat{e}_{{\bf k}}+\left(\xi_{\bf k}+\tilde{\delta}\right)\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}}\hat{c}_{{\bf k}}+\omega_{{\bf k}}\hat{\beta}_{{\bf k}}^{\dagger}\hat{\beta}_{{\bf k}}\right].

(S5)

The first term describes the photons, where we shift the energy by
cp_{0}
in the rotating frame, letting
\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{{\bf k}}=c(k-p_{0})
. The second term describes the excited state
\ket{e}
with the one-photon detuning
\Delta=\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{0}-cp_{0}
,
\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{0}
being the bare energy of the state. Also,
\xi_{\bf k}=k^{2}/2m
is the kinetic energy. The third term describes the impurity state with the two-photon detuning
\tilde{\delta}=\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{0}+c(p_{0}-k_{\rm cl})
,
\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{0}
being the bare energy of the state. We here dropped the
\sim
’s for simplicity. Finally, the fourth term is the usual expression for the BEC Hamiltonian with
\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}_{\bf k}=u_{\bf k}\hat{b}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}+v_{\bf k}\hat{b}_{-{\bf k}}
creating a Bogoliubov mode at momentum
{\bf k}
and energy
\omega_{\bf k}=[\xi_{\bf k}(\xi_{\bf k}+2n{\mathcal{T}_{\rm B}})]^{1/2}
.
u_{\bf k},v_{\bf k}=((\xi_{\bf k}+n{\mathcal{T}_{\rm B}})/\omega_{\bf k}\pm 1)^{1/2}/\sqrt{2}
are the BEC coherence factors,
n
is the density of the condensate, and
{\mathcal{T}_{\rm B}}=4\pi a_{\rm B}/m
the zero energy scattering matrix for the
\ket{b}
atoms. With the rotating frame in place, we may write for the atom-light coupling

\hat{H}_{\rm al}=\Omega\sum_{{\bf k}}\left[\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}\hat{e}_{\bf k}+{\rm h.c.}\right]+\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}\sum_{{\bf k},{\bf q},\lambda}g_{{\bf k}\lambda}\left[\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}}\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}\lambda}\hat{e}_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}}+{\rm h.c.}\right].

(S6)

Further, the impurity state,
c
, interacts with the ground state atoms, which at weak interactions can be described by the Fröhlich interaction

\hat{H}_{\rm int}=n{\mathcal{T}}\sum_{{\bf k}}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\bf k}\hat{c}_{\bf k}+\frac{\sqrt{n}{\mathcal{T}}}{\sqrt{V}}\sum_{{\bf k},{\bf q}}(u_{\bf k}-v_{\bf k})\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}-{\bf k}}\hat{c}_{{\bf q}}\left(\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}}+\hat{\beta}_{-{\bf k}}\right),

(S7)

where
{\mathcal{T}}=4\pi a/m
is the zero energy scattering matrix for the
b
-
c
interaction. The atomic
\ket{c}
-
\ket{c}
,
\ket{c}
-
\ket{e}
, and
\ket{e}
-
\ket{e}
interactions are absent under the assumption that only a single quantum of light is propagating, i.e. at most a single atom is excited. Further, we will not consider any interaction between the ground and excited state,
\ket{b}
-
\ket{e}
. The elementary Hamiltonian of the system is thus
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{\rm al}+\hat{H}_{\rm int}
.

We wish to simplify the Hamiltonian description to the incoming modes
{\bf p}
along the
z
-axis. This is accomplished by integrating out the photonic vacuum, i.e. all the photonic modes for
{\bf k}\neq{\bf p}
. The Feynman diagram associated with this is shown in Fig.
S1
, leading to the decay rate

\displaystyle\gamma_{\bf q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}u_{{\bf q}-{\bf k}}^{2}g_{{\bf k}\lambda}^{2}\delta(\xi_{\bf q}+\Delta-\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{\bf k}-\omega_{{\bf q}-{\bf k}})\simeq\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}u_{{\bf q}-{\bf k}}^{2}g_{{\bf k}\lambda}^{2}\delta(\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{0}-ck)

\displaystyle=\gamma+\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}v_{{\bf q}-{\bf k}}^{2}g_{{\bf k}\lambda}^{2}\delta(\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{0}-ck).

(S8)

In principle, we should omit the modes
{\bf p}
in this integration. However, because they lie along a single line the result is unaffected. Due to the huge slope of the photonic dispersion, the speed of light
c
, the atomic energies
\xi_{\bf q},\omega_{{\bf q}-{\bf k}}
are completely negligible. In the second line we use
u_{\bf k}^{2}-v_{\bf k}^{2}=1
. There is thus in principle a small correction to the bare (Wigner-Weisskopf) decay rate
\gamma=\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}g_{{\bf k}\lambda}^{2}\delta(\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{0}-ck)
, scaling with the number of non-condensate
\ket{b}
-atoms. However, this has no bearing on our studies, and we will simply ignore it.

Figure S1:
Feynman diagram for excited state to photonic vacuum coupling. This leads to a Lamb shift, incorporated in the energy
\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{0}
, and decay rate
\gamma
, see Eq. (
S8
).

For concreteness, we assume that the incoming photons are linearly polarized and that the direction of the electric dipole moment is fixed orthogonal to the propagation of the incoming photons. We can then set one of the polarizations,
\lambda_{1}
, to be parallel to the dipole moment. I.e.
\bm{\epsilon}_{{\bf p}\lambda_{1}}\cdot{\bf d}_{\rm be}=|{\bf d}_{\rm be}|
and
\bm{\epsilon}_{{\bf p}\lambda_{2}}\cdot{\bf d}_{\rm be}=0
. This thus picks out a particular polarization, and defining
g=g_{{\bf p}\lambda_{1}}=\sqrt{cp/2\epsilon_{0}}\;|\mathbf{d}_{\rm be}|
we may write an effective Hamiltonian describing only the incoming photonic modes,
{\bf p}
,

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bf p}\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{{\bf p}}\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{\bf p}\hat{\alpha}_{\bf p}+\sum_{{\bf k}}\left[\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{\bf k}\hat{e}^{\dagger}_{\bf k}\hat{e}_{\bf k}+\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{\bf k}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\bf k}\hat{c}_{\bf k}+\omega_{\bf k}\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}_{\bf k}\hat{\beta}_{\bf k}\right],

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm al}

\displaystyle=\Omega\sum_{{\bf k}}\left[\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}\hat{e}_{\bf k}+{\rm h.c.}\right]+\sum_{\bf p}\sqrt{n}g\left(\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{\bf p}\hat{e}_{\bf p}+{\rm h.c.}\right)+\frac{g}{\sqrt{V}}\sum_{{\bf k},{\bf p}}\left[(u_{\bf k}\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}_{\bf k}-v_{\bf k}\hat{\beta}_{-{\bf k}})\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{{\bf p}}\hat{e}_{{\bf p}+{\bf k}}+{\rm h.c.}\right],

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm int}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{n}{\mathcal{T}}}{\sqrt{V}}\sum_{{\bf k},{\bf q}}(u_{\bf k}-v_{\bf k})\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{{\bf q}-{\bf k}}\hat{c}_{{\bf q}}\left(\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}}+\hat{\beta}_{-{\bf k}}\right).

(S9)

Here we drop the now redundant polarization index
\lambda_{1}
on
\alpha_{{\bf p}\lambda_{1}}
. Also,
\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{\bf k}=\xi_{\bf k}+\Delta-i\gamma
includes the decay rate of the excited state, and
\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{\bf k}=\xi_{\bf k}+\delta
includes the mean field energy shift
n{\mathcal{T}}
due to the impurity-boson interaction in the two-photon detuning
\delta=\tilde{\delta}+n{\mathcal{T}}
.

Appendix SIII
Deriving the equations of motion in the physical basis

To accommodate for the atomic interactions and the quantum fluctuations in the BEC we use the state ansatz

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{\bf p}}(t)=\left[A^{(0)}_{\bf p}(t)\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{{\bf p}}+E^{(0)}_{\bf p}(t)\hat{e}^{\dagger}_{{\bf p}}+C^{(0)}_{\bf p}(t)\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}\right]\ket{{\rm BEC}}+\sum_{{\bf k}}\!\left[E^{(1)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}(t)\hat{e}^{\dagger}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}+C^{(1)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}(t)\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}-{\bf k}}\right]\!\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}}\ket{{\rm BEC}},

(S10)

also given in the main text. Here the term describing an impurity plus a single phonon,
C^{(1)}
, is generated by the impurity-boson interaction
\hat{H}_{\rm int}
. This in turn is coupled to the
E^{(1)}
term through the classical light field
\propto\Omega
. It is finally coupled to the photonic mode
A^{(0)}
via terms in
\hat{H}_{\rm al}
present due to quantum fluctuations
\propto v_{\bf k}g
. The underlying assumption of this ansatz is that when the impurity scatters on the condensate atoms, it breaks apart the dark state and decouples the photonic mode from the atomic states. This is accurate when the typical scattering momentum is much larger than the largest change in momentum the dark state can suffer without breaking apart,
\Delta p_{\rm cr}
. In the weak coupling limit investigated here we have
k_{\rm scat}\simeq 1/\xi
with
\xi=1/\sqrt{8\pi n{a_{\text{B}}}}
the BEC coherence length. On the other hand
\Delta p_{\rm cr}
is determined by equating the energy at the edge of the EIT window
\Omega^{2}/\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+\gamma^{2}}
with the dark state energy
v_{\rm g}\Delta p_{\rm cr}
, resulting in
\Delta p_{\rm cr}=ng^{2}/(c\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+\gamma^{2}})
. Thus, for the wave function ansatz to accurately describe the scattering, we need

\frac{\Delta p_{\rm cr}}{k_{\rm scat}}=\frac{3\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{n}{{a_{\text{B}}}(1+(\Delta/\gamma)^{2})}}\ll 1,

(S11)

where we use
g^{2}=3\pi c\gamma/p^{2}
. When this inequality is fulfilled the dark state breaks apart during an atomic scattering event as shown in Fig. 2 of the main text. The only caveat is when the scattering preserves the magnitude of the dark state momentum, i.e.
|{\bf p}-{\bf k}|\simeq p
. These events are however extremely rare and negligible as discussed at the end of this Supplemental Material. For atomic densities of
n=10^{13}-10^{15}{\rm cm}^{-3}
, optical transitions
\lambda=2\pi/p=400-800{\rm nm}
, and typical atomic interactions in the condensate of
{a_{\text{B}}}=100a_{0}
,
\Delta p_{\rm cr}/k_{\rm scat}
is of order unity on the single photon resonance
\Delta=0
. Therefore, the theory is restricted in validity to detunings much larger than the decay rate,
|\Delta|\gg\gamma
. Finally, terms with more than one phonon present will be higher order in the impurity-boson interaction, or macroscopically suppressed. E.g. there is in principle a term
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bf k}A^{(2)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}(t)\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{\bf p}\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}_{\bf k}\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}_{-{\bf k}}\ket{{\rm BEC}}
coupling to the
E^{(1)}
term through
gu_{\bf k}
. However, this coupling turns out to be zero in the thermodynamic limit, where
N\to\infty,V\to\infty
, and we thus neglect it completely. We first solve the equations for the one phonon amplitudes
E^{(1)}
and
C^{(1)}
in terms of zero phonon amplitudes
A^{(0)},E^{(0)}
and
C^{(0)}
, and then plug these solutions back into the equations of motion for the zero phonon amplitudes. For convenience, we let
{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(1)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}=[E^{(1)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}},\;C^{(1)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}]
and
{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(0)}_{{\bf p}}=[A^{(0)}_{{\bf p}},\;E^{(0)}_{{\bf p}},\;C^{(0)}_{{\bf p}}]
. Using the Schrödinger equation we then get

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(1)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}(t)

\displaystyle=\left[\omega_{\bf k}+\mathcal{H}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}\right]{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(1)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}(t)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}G_{\bf k}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(0)}_{\bf p}(t),

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(0)}_{{\bf p}}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{{\bf p}}^{(0)}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(0)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}(t)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}\sum_{\bf k}G_{\bf k}^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(1)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}(t).

(S12)

Here,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{{\bf q}}=\begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{{\bf q}}&\Omega\\
\Omega&\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{{\bf q}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}\end{bmatrix},\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{H}^{(0)}_{\bf p}=\begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{{\bf p}}&\sqrt{n}g&0\\
\sqrt{n}g&\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{{\bf p}}&\Omega\\
0&\Omega&\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}\end{bmatrix},\hskip 14.22636ptG_{\bf k}=\begin{bmatrix}-gv_{\bf k}&0&0\\
0&0&\sqrt{n}{\mathcal{T}}(u_{\bf k}-v_{\bf k})\end{bmatrix}.

(S13)

The first two describe the effective Hamiltonian of the scattered and unscattered states respectively, while
G_{\bf k}
describes the coupling matrix to the scattered states. The equation for these,
{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(1)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}
, in Eq. (
S12
) is formally solved to yield

{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(1)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}(t)=-\frac{i}{\sqrt{V}}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau\;{\rm e}^{-i\omega_{\bf k}(t-\tau)}{\rm e}^{-i\mathcal{H}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}(t-\tau)}G_{\bf k}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(0)}_{{\bf p}}(\tau),

(S14)

using the initial condition
{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(1)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}(0)={\bf 0}
, i.e. that there are no phonons initially. Reinserting this in the equation for
{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(0)}_{{\bf p}}
in Eq. (
S12
) we get

i\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(0)}_{\bf p}(t)=\mathcal{H}^{(0)}_{\bf p}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(0)}_{{\bf p}}(t)-\frac{i}{V}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau\sum_{{\bf k}}{\rm e}^{-i\omega_{\bf k}(t-\tau)}G_{\bf k}^{\dagger}{\rm e}^{-i\mathcal{H}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}(t-\tau)}G_{\bf k}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(0)}_{{\bf p}}(\tau).

(S15)

We write out the explicit solution by finding eigenvectors and -values to
\mathcal{H}_{\bf q}
. The eigenvalues are
\varepsilon^{(\pm)}_{\bf q}=\frac{1}{2}[\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{{\bf q}}+\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{{\bf q}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}\pm(4\Omega^{2}+(\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{{\bf q}}-\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{{\bf q}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}})^{2})^{1/2}]
describing hybridized modes
\ket{\pm}
of the
\ket{e}
- and
\ket{c}
-states. The corresponding eigenvectors are

\displaystyle{\bf u}^{(+)}_{{\bf q}}=\begin{bmatrix}w^{\rm ec}_{{\bf q}}\\
u^{\rm ec}_{{\bf q}}\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\varepsilon^{(+)}_{{\bf q}}-\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{{\bf q}})^{2}+\Omega^{2}}}\begin{bmatrix}\Omega\\
\varepsilon^{(+)}_{{\bf q}}-\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{{\bf q}}\end{bmatrix},\hskip 14.22636pt{\bf u}^{(-)}_{{\bf q}}=\begin{bmatrix}u^{\rm ec}_{{\bf q}}\\
-w^{\rm ec}_{{\bf q}}\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\varepsilon^{(+)}_{{\bf q}}-\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{{\bf q}})^{2}+\Omega^{2}}}\begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon^{(+)}_{{\bf q}}-\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{{\bf q}}\\
-\Omega\end{bmatrix}.

(S16)

The eigenmatrix
U_{{\bf q}}=[{\bf u}^{(+)}_{{\bf q}}\;{\bf u}^{(-)}_{{\bf q}}]
is its own inverse, and so we get
{\rm e}^{-i\mathcal{H}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}(t-\tau)}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(0)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}(\tau)={\rm e}^{-i\mathcal{H}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}(t-\tau)}(U_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}})^{2}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(0)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}(\tau)=[{\bf u}^{(+)}_{{\bf q}}{\rm e}^{-i\varepsilon^{(+)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}(t-\tau)}\;{\bf u}^{(-)}_{{\bf q}}{\rm e}^{-i\varepsilon^{(-)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}(t-\tau)}]U_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{(0)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}(\tau)
. Performing the matrix multiplication, we get

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\begin{bmatrix}A^{(0)}_{\bf p}\\[2.84544pt]
E^{(0)}_{\bf p}\\[2.84544pt]
C^{(0)}_{\bf p}\end{bmatrix}=\mathcal{H}^{0}_{{\bf p}}\begin{bmatrix}A^{(0)}_{\bf p}(t)\\[2.84544pt]
E^{(0)}_{\bf p}(t)\\[2.84544pt]
C^{(0)}_{\bf p}(t)\end{bmatrix}+i\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau\;\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}(t-\tau)\begin{bmatrix}A^{(0)}_{\bf p}(\tau)\\[2.84544pt]
E^{(0)}_{\bf p}(\tau)\\[2.84544pt]
C^{(0)}_{\bf p}(\tau)\end{bmatrix}.

(S17)

Here,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}(t)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}^{\alpha\alpha}(t)&0&\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}^{\rm\alpha c}(t)\\
0&0&0\\
\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}^{\rm\alpha c}(t)&0&\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}^{\rm cc}(t)\end{bmatrix},\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}^{\alpha\alpha}(t)=-g^{2}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{{\bf k}}v_{\bf k}^{2}\cdot{\rm e}^{-i\omega_{\bf k}t}\left[(w^{\rm ec}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}})^{2}{\rm e}^{-i\varepsilon^{(+)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}t}+(u^{\rm ec}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}})^{2}{\rm e}^{-i\varepsilon^{(-)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}t}\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}^{\rm\alpha c}(t)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n}g{\mathcal{T}}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{{\bf k}}v_{\bf k}(u_{\bf k}-v_{\bf k})u^{\rm ec}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}w^{\rm ec}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}\cdot{\rm e}^{-i\omega_{\bf k}t}\left[{\rm e}^{-i\varepsilon^{(+)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}t}-{\rm e}^{-i\varepsilon^{(-)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}t}\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}^{\rm cc}(t)

\displaystyle=-n{\mathcal{T}}^{2}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{{\bf k}}(u_{\bf k}-v_{\bf k})^{2}\cdot{\rm e}^{-i\omega_{\bf k}t}\left[(u^{\rm ec}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}})^{2}{\rm e}^{-i\varepsilon^{(+)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}t}+(w^{\rm ec}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}})^{2}{\rm e}^{-i\varepsilon^{(-)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}t}\right].

(S18)

We are now ready to transform to the polariton basis and make the equations of motion local in time.

Appendix SIV
Dark state equation of motion

The polaritons are the eigenstates of the Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(00)}_{{\bf p}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\sqrt{n}g&0\\
\sqrt{n}g&0&\Omega\\
0&\Omega&0\end{bmatrix},\hskip 14.22636pt\ket{D_{\bf p}}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta\\
0\\
-\sin\theta\end{bmatrix},\hskip 14.22636pt\ket{B^{(\pm)}_{\bf p}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}\sin\theta\\
\pm 1\\
\cos\theta\end{bmatrix},

(S19)

with the eigenvectors given on the right, using
\tan\theta=\Omega/\sqrt{n}g
. The eigenvalues of these are
0
for the dark state
\ket{D_{\bf p}}
and
\pm\sqrt{ng^{2}+\Omega^{2}}
for the two bright states
\ket{B^{(\pm)}_{\bf p}}
. We thus define
W_{{\bf p}}=\left[\ket{B^{(+)}_{{\bf p}}}\;\ket{B^{(-)}_{{\bf p}}}\;\ket{D_{{\bf p}}}\right]
and let

\begin{bmatrix}A^{(0)}_{\bf p}\\[2.84544pt]
E^{(0)}_{\bf p}\\[2.84544pt]
C^{(0)}_{\bf p}\end{bmatrix}=W_{{\bf p}}\begin{bmatrix}B^{(+)}_{\bf p}\\[2.84544pt]
B^{(-)}_{\bf p}\\[2.84544pt]
D_{\bf p}\end{bmatrix},

(S20)

defining the polariton amplitudes
B^{(\pm)}_{\bf p}
and
D_{\bf p}
. The equations of motion in Eq. (
S17
) transformed to the polariton basis is thus

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\begin{bmatrix}B^{(+)}_{{\bf p}}(t)\\[2.84544pt]
B^{(-)}_{{\bf p}}(t)\\[2.84544pt]
D_{{\bf p}}(t)\end{bmatrix}=\bar{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{\bf p}\begin{bmatrix}B^{(+)}_{{\bf p}}(t)\\[2.84544pt]
B^{(-)}_{{\bf p}}(t)\\[2.84544pt]
D_{{\bf p}}(t)\end{bmatrix}+i\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau\;\bar{\mathcal{K}}_{{\bf p}}(t-\tau)\begin{bmatrix}B^{(+)}_{{\bf p}}(\tau)\\[2.84544pt]
B^{(-)}_{{\bf p}}(\tau)\\[2.84544pt]
D_{{\bf p}}(\tau)\end{bmatrix},

(S21)

with

\displaystyle\!\!\bar{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)}_{\bf p}=W^{\dagger}_{\bf p}\mathcal{H}^{(0)}_{\bf p}W_{\bf p}=\begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon^{\rm B(+)}_{\bf p}&\frac{\sin^{2}\theta\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{{\bf p}}-\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{{\bf p}}+\cos^{2}\theta\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}}{2}&\frac{\cos\theta\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}}(\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{\bf p}-\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}})\\
\frac{\sin^{2}\theta\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{{\bf p}}-\varepsilon^{\rm e}_{{\bf p}}+\cos^{2}\theta\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}}{2}&\varepsilon^{\rm B(-)}_{\bf p}&\frac{\cos\theta\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}}(\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{\bf p}-\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}})\\
\frac{\cos\theta\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}}(\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{\bf p}-\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}})&\frac{\cos\theta\sin\theta}{\sqrt{2}}(\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{\bf p}-\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}})&\varepsilon_{\bf p}\end{bmatrix},

(S22)

defining the energies

\varepsilon_{\bf p}=\cos^{2}\theta\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{\bf p}\!+\!\sin^{2}\theta\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{K}}_{\bf p}(t)

\displaystyle=W^{\dagger}_{\bf p}\mathcal{K}_{\bf p}(t)W_{\bf p}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{K}^{\rm B}_{{\bf p}}(t)&\mathcal{K}^{\rm B}_{{\bf p}}(t)&\mathcal{K}^{\rm BD}_{{\bf p}}(t)\\[2.84544pt]

(S23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\rm B}_{{\bf p}}(t)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\rm BD}_{{\bf p}}(t)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\rm D}_{{\bf p}}(t)

(S24)

We could keep all terms and propagate all three amplitudes,
B^{(+)}_{\bf p},B^{(-)}_{\bf p}
and
D_{\bf p}
. However, because the bright states are so far removed in energy, by
\pm(ng^{2}+\Omega^{2})^{1/2}

\mathcal{K}^{\rm D}

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\rm D}_{{\bf p}}(t)

\displaystyle=\sin^{2}\theta\left[\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}^{\rm cc}(t)-\frac{2}{\tan\theta}\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}^{\rm\alpha c}(t)+\frac{1}{\tan^{2}\theta}\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}^{\alpha\alpha}(t)\right]=\sin^{2}\theta\left[\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}^{\rm cc}(t)-2\frac{\Omega}{\sqrt{n}g}\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}^{\rm\alpha c}(t)+\left(\frac{\Omega}{\sqrt{n}g}\right)^{2}\mathcal{K}_{{\bf p}}^{\alpha\alpha}(t)\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{V}\sum_{{\bf k}}{\rm e}^{-i\omega_{\bf k}t}\left[\left(g^{(+)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}\right)^{2}{\rm e}^{-i\varepsilon^{(+)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}t}+\left(g^{(-)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}\right)^{2}{\rm e}^{-i\varepsilon^{(-)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}t}\right],

(S25)

with the effective couplings

\displaystyle g^{(+)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}\!=\sin\theta\left[u^{\rm ec}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}\sqrt{n}{\mathcal{T}}(u_{\bf k}-v_{\bf k})+w^{\rm ec}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}\frac{v_{\bf k}\Omega}{\sqrt{n}}\right],\hskip 14.22636ptg^{(-)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}\!=\sin\theta\left[w^{\rm ec}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}\sqrt{n}{\mathcal{T}}(u_{\bf k}-v_{\bf k})-u^{\rm ec}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}\frac{v_{\bf k}\Omega}{\sqrt{n}}\right].

(S26)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}D_{\bf p}=\left(\varepsilon_{\bf p}+K_{\bf p}(t)\right)D_{\bf p}(t),

(S27)

with

\sin^{2}\theta\cdot n{\mathcal{T}}^{2}/V\cdot\sum_{{\bf k}}m/k^{2}

{\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\bf p}=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\frac{\left(g^{(+)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}\right)^{2}}{\varepsilon_{{\bf p}}-\varepsilon^{(+)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}-\omega_{\bf k}}+\frac{\left(g^{(-)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}\right)^{2}}{\varepsilon_{{\bf p}}-\varepsilon^{(-)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}-\omega_{\bf k}}+\sin^{2}\theta\cdot n{\mathcal{T}}^{2}\frac{m}{k^{2}}\right],

also given in Eq. (6) of the main text and the time-dependent contribution

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}_{\bf p}(t)=\int

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\left(g^{(+)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}\right)^{2}\frac{{\rm e}^{i(\varepsilon_{{\bf p}}-\varepsilon^{(+)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}-\omega_{\bf k})t}}{\varepsilon_{{\bf p}}-\varepsilon^{(+)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}-\omega_{\bf k}}+\left(g^{(-)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}\right)^{2}\frac{{\rm e}^{i(\varepsilon_{{\bf p}}-\varepsilon^{(-)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}-\omega_{\bf k})t}}{\varepsilon_{{\bf p}}-\varepsilon^{(-)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}-\omega_{\bf k}}\right].

(S29)

We here replace the sum over momentum modes with integrals:
\sum_{\bf k}/V\to\int{\rm d}^{3}k/(2\pi)^{3}

\ket{e}

\ket{c}

\ket{\pm}

To clarify the interaction scalings we put
\tilde{\Sigma}
on unitless form. We let the
z

{\bf p}

g^{(\pm)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}

\displaystyle{t_{\text{B}}}\cdot\tilde{\Sigma}_{\bf p}(t)=-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\pi^{2}}\frac{{a_{\text{B}}}}{\xi}\sin^{2}\theta\int_{0}^{2\pi}{\rm d}\varphi\int_{-1}^{+1}{\rm d}\cos\theta\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}{\tilde{k}}\,{\tilde{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle\left[\left(u^{\rm ec}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}(u_{\bf k}-v_{\bf k})\frac{a}{2{a_{\text{B}}}}+w^{\rm ec}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}v_{{\bf k}}\tilde{\Omega}\right)^{2}\frac{{\rm e}^{-i\Phi^{(+)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}\tilde{t}}}{\Phi^{(+)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\!\!+\left(w^{\rm ec}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}(u_{\bf k}-v_{\bf k})\frac{a}{2{a_{\text{B}}}}-u^{\rm ec}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}v_{{\bf k}}\tilde{\Omega}\right)^{2}\frac{{\rm e}^{-i\Phi^{(-)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}\tilde{t}}}{\Phi^{(-)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}}\right],

(S30)

\Phi^{(\pm)}_{{\bf p},{\bf k}}=-(\varepsilon_{{\bf p}}-\varepsilon^{(\pm)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}-\omega_{\bf k}){t_{\text{B}}}

Appendix SV
Critical velocity and Rabi frequency

In this section we compute the critical velocity and Rabi frequency in the limit of
\Omega/|\Delta|\ll 1
relevant for Figs. 1(c) and 1(d) of the main text.

In this limit the coupling to the
\ket{-}
state vanishes,
g^{(-)}\to 0
. The critical behaviour is thus kinematically set by when the dark state can scatter into the
\ket{+}
state in an energy conserving way, i.e.
\varepsilon_{{\bf p}}={\rm Re}[\varepsilon^{(+)}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}+\omega_{{\bf k}}]
for some phonon momentum
{\bf k}
, as evident from the self-energy
\Sigma_{{\bf p}}
(Eq. (
S28
)). Expanding this equation to leading order in
\Omega/\Delta
, using
\varepsilon_{\bf p}\simeq\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}
for
p=p_{0}
, and
\varepsilon^{\rm c}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}=\xi_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}+\delta
we must check when

\xi_{\bf q}=\xi_{{\bf q}-{\bf k}}-\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\Delta}+\omega_{\bf k}

(S31)

can be solved as a function of
{\bf k}
, with
{\bf q}={\bf p}-{\bf k}_{\rm cl}
. This equation also follows from a simple second order argument: to 2nd order in
\Omega
the scattered impurity experiences a light shift
-\Omega^{2}/\Delta
, and thus alters the energy balance. If the scattered phonon is emitted in the forward direction of the impurity, the impurity kinetic energy
\xi_{{\bf q}-{\bf k}}=({\bf q}-{\bf k})^{2}/2m
is lowered most significantly, and this is thus where we first get a solution. So we focus on
{\bf k}\parallel{\bf q}
. Writing Eq. (
S31
) in units of
{t_{\text{B}}}
we must then solve

0=({\tilde{q}}-{\tilde{k}})^{2}+{\tilde{k}}\sqrt{1+{\tilde{k}}^{2}}-{\tilde{q}}^{2}-\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\Delta}{t_{\text{B}}}=f({\tilde{q}},{\tilde{k}})-\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\Delta}{t_{\text{B}}}.

(S32)

For
\Delta>0
there always exists a solution to this equation, and therefore the critical velocity is 0 for positive detunings. For
\Delta<0
the light shift is always positive. Therefore,
f({\tilde{q}},{\tilde{k}})
must be negative for some interval of
{\tilde{k}}
for a solution to exist. This only happens when
{\tilde{q}}=q\xi/\sqrt{2}>1/2
or equivalently when
v=q/m=|{\bf p}-{\bf k}_{\rm cl}|/m>c_{\rm s}
. This shows that the speed still needs to be larger than the speed of sound, as one might expect. However, because of the light shift, there may still not be a solution to Eq. (
S32
), leading to an increased critical velocity
v_{\rm c}
. We can compute this by finding the minimum of
f({\tilde{p}},{\tilde{k}})
and equating it to the light shift. While the general solution is rather involved, we can find a simple approximate solution for
v\gtrsim 2c_{\rm s}
. Here we can approximate
{\tilde{k}}\sqrt{1+{\tilde{k}}^{2}}\simeq{\tilde{k}}^{2}
at the minimum. Taking the derivative,
\partial_{{\tilde{k}}}f({\tilde{q}},{\tilde{k}})\simeq 4({\tilde{k}}-{\tilde{q}}/2)
, then yields a minimum at
{\tilde{k}}\simeq{\tilde{q}}/2
, and thus
\min_{{\tilde{k}}}f({\tilde{q}},{\tilde{k}})\simeq f({\tilde{q}},{\tilde{q}}/2)\simeq-{\tilde{q}}^{2}/2
. The critical momentum is thus
{\tilde{q}}_{\rm c}\simeq\sqrt{-2\Omega^{2}{t_{\text{B}}}/\Delta}
, yielding a critical velocity

v_{\rm c}=\frac{q_{\rm c}}{m}\simeq\frac{2\Omega}{\sqrt{-m\Delta}},

(S33)

using
{\tilde{q}}=q\xi/\sqrt{2}
and
{t_{\text{B}}}=m\xi^{2}
.
Hence, only if the impurity moves with a speed faster than
v_{\rm c}
does it experience decay, as shown in Fig. 1(c). This gives an accurate result for
v_{\rm c}\gtrsim 2c_{\rm s}
. Reversely, for a fixed velocity
v
as in Fig. 1(d), one can increase the critical velocity by increasing
\Omega
. This thus exhibits a critical behaviour at

\Omega_{\rm c}\simeq\frac{\sqrt{-m\Delta}v}{2},

(S34)

below
which the dark state experiences decay, while above the decay rate becomes vanishingly small, as shown in Fig. 1(d). Again this is accurate for
v\gtrsim 2c_{\rm s}
. The underlying reason for the vanishing decay rate is thus that the additional energy cost from the light field becomes too large at
\Omega_{\rm c}
for the scattering to be allowed kinematically.

Appendix SVI
Propagation of the dark state

We derive an expression for the propagation of the dark state in real space. First however, we need an expression for the group speed. We thus define

v_{\rm g}(t)-i\kappa(t)=\partial_{p}\left.\left(\varepsilon_{{\bf p}}+\Sigma_{{\bf p}}-\tilde{\Sigma}_{{\bf p}}(t)\right)\right|_{{\bf p}={\bf p}_{0}}.

(S35)

Since the right hand side is in general a complex number there is both a contribution to the group speed
v_{\rm g}
and a damping coefficient
\kappa
, and due to the presence of the time-dependent rate coefficient
\tilde{\Sigma}
these depend on time as well. Keeping only the dominant terms proportional to the group speed in the absence of atomic interactions
v_{\rm g}^{0}=\Omega^{2}/(\Omega^{2}+ng^{2})\cdot c
, with
c
the speed of light, we obtain

v_{\rm g}(t)-i\kappa(t)=v_{\rm g}^{0}\left[1-it\cdot\tilde{\Sigma}_{{\bf p}_{0}}(t)+i\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau\;\tilde{\Sigma}_{{\bf p}_{0}}(\tau)\right].

(S36)

Let us now turn to the propagation. Suppose that we prepare a non-interacting dark state pulse
D(z,t=0)
at time
t=0
. The evolution of this state can be studied by expanding in plane waves (along the propagation axis
z
)

D(z,t)=\int\frac{{\rm d}p}{2\pi}{\rm e}^{ipz}D_{{\bf p}}(t)=\int\frac{{\rm d}p}{2\pi}{\rm e}^{ipz}D_{{\bf p}}(0){\rm e}^{-iE_{{\bf p}}t-\Gamma_{{\bf p}}t}{\rm e}^{i\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau\tilde{\Sigma}_{\bf p}(\tau)},

(S37)

where we in the second equality use that
D_{{\bf p}}(t)=D_{{\bf p}}(0){\rm e}^{-iE_{{\bf p}}\,t-\Gamma_{{\bf p}}\,t}{\rm e}^{i\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau\tilde{\Sigma}_{\bf p}(\tau)}
as described in the main text, with
E_{{\bf p}}=\varepsilon_{{\bf p}}+{\rm Re}\Sigma_{{\bf p}}
the dark state energy and
\Gamma_{{\bf p}}=-{\rm Im}\Sigma_{\bf p}
the dark state decay rate. This simple solution is accurate, provided the pulse fits within the EIT window, i.e.
v_{\rm g}\sigma_{p}<\Omega^{2}/\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+\gamma^{2}}
, with
\sigma_{p}
the momentum standard deviation. Using the definition of the group speed and damping coefficient in Eq. (
S35
) we get

\displaystyle D(z,t)

\displaystyle\simeq{\rm e}^{ip_{0}z}{\rm e}^{-iE_{{\bf p}_{0}}t}{\rm e}^{-\Gamma_{{\bf p}_{0}}t}{\rm e}^{i\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau\tilde{\Sigma}_{{\bf p}_{0}}(\tau)}\int\frac{{\rm d}p}{2\pi}{\rm e}^{i(p-p_{0})\left(z-\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau[v_{\rm g}(\tau)-i\kappa(\tau)]\right)}D_{{\bf p}}(0)

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{ip_{0}(z-z^{\prime}(t))}{\rm e}^{-iE_{{\bf p}_{0}}t}{\rm e}^{-\Gamma_{{\bf p}_{0}}t}{\rm e}^{i\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau\tilde{\Sigma}_{{\bf p}_{0}}(\tau)}\int\frac{{\rm d}p}{2\pi}{\rm e}^{ipz^{\prime}(t)}D_{{\bf p}}(0)

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{ip_{0}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau[v_{\rm g}(\tau)-i\kappa(\tau)]}{\rm e}^{-iE_{{\bf p}_{0}}t}{\rm e}^{-\Gamma_{{\bf p}_{0}}t}{\rm e}^{i\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau\tilde{\Sigma}_{{\bf p}_{0}}(\tau)}\cdot D(z^{\prime}(t),0),

with
z^{\prime}(t)=z-\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau[v_{\rm g}(\tau)-i\kappa(\tau)]
. The probability distribution consequently becomes

|D(z,t)|^{2}={\rm e}^{+2p_{0}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau\kappa(\tau)}{\rm e}^{-2\Gamma_{{\bf p}_{0}}t}{\rm e}^{-2\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}\tau{\rm Im}\tilde{\Sigma}_{{\bf p}_{0}}(\tau)}|D(z^{\prime}(t),0)|^{2}.

(S38)

This concludes the present derivation, and describes motion of the pulse at a time-dependent group velocity
v_{\rm g}(t)
. In Fig.
S2
we plot
v_{\rm g}
and
\kappa
as a function of time for the same parameters considered in Fig. 3 of the main text. The atomic interactions eventually leads to a slight lowering of the group speed, while the damping coefficient
\kappa
remains vanishingly small. An analysis of the asymptotic dynamics shows that the oscillation frequency is exactly the light shift
\Omega^{2}/|\Delta|
, while the dominant non-equilibrium contribution to
v_{\rm g}
at long times vanishes as
{\rm e}^{-\Omega^{2}/\Delta^{2}\cdot\gamma t}/t
. For
\Delta<0
,
\Omega/|\Delta|\ll 1
, and below the critical velocity the dark state decay rate
\Gamma_{{\bf p}}
becomes vanishingly small stabilizing the pulse as evident in Fig. 3, and the overall pulse is only reduced by the square of the dark state residue:
Z={\rm e}^{-\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}\tau{\rm Im}\tilde{\Sigma}_{{\bf p}_{0}}(\tau)}
.

Appendix SVII
Impurity damping rate at large speeds

In Fig. 3 we make a comparison of the dark state propagation with an impurity shot through the condensate at the group speed
v_{\rm g}
. Since the group speed is several orders of magnitude larger than the speed of sound,
v_{\rm g}\gg c_{\rm s}
, the perturbative result for the damping rate would here give a dramatic overestimate. Instead we use the ladder approximation as described in Ref. [7]. At zero temperature the ladder approximation yields the self-energy
\Sigma({\bf p},\omega)=n{\mathcal{T}}({\bf p},\omega)
with the scattering matrix

{\mathcal{T}}({\bf p},\omega)=\frac{{\mathcal{T}}}{1-{\mathcal{T}}\cdot\Pi({\bf p},\omega)},

(S39)

written in terms of the zero-energy impurity-boson scattering matrix
{\mathcal{T}}=4\pi a/m
and the pair propagator

\Pi({\bf p},\omega)=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\frac{u_{\bf k}^{2}}{\omega-\xi_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}-\omega_{\bf k}+i{\rm e}a}+\frac{m}{k^{2}}\right]\simeq-i\frac{m^{3/2}}{4\pi}\sqrt{\omega-\frac{p^{2}}{4m}},

(S40)

with
{\rm e}a=0^{+}
a positive infinitesimal. In the second equality we use that at the very high energy we are interested in,
\omega=\xi_{\bf p}=p^{2}/2m=mv_{\rm g}^{2}/2
, only the large momenta contribute. Hence, we can safely approximate
u_{\bf k}\simeq 1
and
\omega_{\bf k}\simeq\xi_{\bf k}
. This makes the integral analytically solvable, yielding the vacuum pair propagator. The impurity damping rate then becomes

\Gamma_{\rm imp}=-{\rm Im}\left[\Sigma\left({\bf p},\frac{p^{2}}{2m}\right)\right]\simeq-{\rm Im}\left[n{\mathcal{T}}({\bf p},\omega)\right]=n\frac{2\pi pa^{2}}{m}\frac{1}{1+(pa/2)^{2}}\simeq\frac{8\pi n}{mp}.

(S41)

In the last equality we use that
pa\simeq 25\gg 1
for the parameters used in Fig. 3 of the main text. In this figure we thus plot the
retrieval
probability distribution of the impurity, using that the damping
\Gamma_{\rm imp}
gives the scattering rate out of the
{\bf p}
momentum state.

Appendix SVIII
Additional damping rate around
|{\bf p}-{\bf k}|\simeq p

In the wave function ansatz (
S10
), equivalent to Eq. (4) of the main text, we have assumed that the dark state breaks apart in any atomic scattering event. However, there is a small probability for the dark state at momentum
{\bf p}
to scatter to other dark states. This happens when only the direction of the photonic momentum changes – not the magnitude. I.e. when scattering a phonon with momentum
{\bf k}
, the dark states survives when
|{\bf p}-{\bf k}|\simeq p
, defining a sphere of possible dark states. This leads to an additional damping rate
\Gamma_{\rm damp}
of the dark state on top of the decay rate
\Gamma
investigated in our present work. We calculate the damping rate from Fermi’s golden rule,

\Gamma_{\rm damp}=\sum_{{\bf k},\lambda}\left|\bra{f_{{\bf p},{\bf k},\lambda}}\hat{H}_{\rm int}\ket{i_{{\bf p},\lambda_{i}}}\right|^{2}\pi\delta(\varepsilon_{{\bf p}}-\varepsilon_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}}-\omega_{{\bf k}}),

(S42)

with the initial state
\ket{i_{{\bf p},\lambda_{i}}}=\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{{\bf p},\lambda_{i}}\ket{{\rm BEC}}
and the final states
\ket{f_{{\bf p},{\bf k},\lambda}}=\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}\lambda}\hat{\beta}^{\dagger}_{\bf k}\ket{{\rm BEC}}
. Here the dark state operator is defined as
\hat{d}^{\dagger}_{{\bf p}\lambda}=\cos\theta_{{\bf p}\lambda}\hat{\alpha}^{\dagger}_{{\bf p}\lambda}-\sin\theta_{{\bf p}\lambda}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}{\rm cl}}
, with
\tan\theta_{{\bf p}\lambda}=\sqrt{n}g_{{\bf p}\lambda}/\Omega
. Using Eq. (
S9
) we then get

\Gamma_{\rm damp}=\pi\,\frac{n{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}{V}\sum_{{\bf k},\lambda}(u_{\bf k}-v_{\bf k})^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}\lambda}\cdot\delta(\varepsilon_{\bf p}-\varepsilon_{{\bf p}-{\bf k}} -\omega_{{\bf k}}),

using that
\sin\theta_{{\bf p}\lambda_{i}}\simeq 1
. We let
{\bf q}={\bf p}-{\bf k}
and let the dipole moment define the
z
-direction,
\hat{d}_{\rm be}={\bf d}_{\rm be}/|{\bf d}_{\rm be}|=\hat{z}
. Then
g_{{\bf q}\lambda}=g\,{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bf q}\lambda}\cdot\hat{d}_{\rm be}
, with
{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bf q}\lambda}
the polarization vector. Since the polarizations have to be perpendicular to
{\bf q}
we can choose them as the spherical angle unit vectors:
{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bf q}\lambda_{1}}=\hat{\theta}
, and
{\bm{\epsilon}}_{{\bf q}\lambda_{2}}=\hat{\phi}
. Here
\theta,\phi
are the polar and azimuthal angles respectively. Then
g_{{\bf q}\lambda_{1}}=g\,\hat{\theta}\cdot\hat{z}=-g\sin\theta
, and
g_{{\bf q}\lambda_{2}}=g\,\hat{\phi}\cdot\hat{z}=0
. This in turn yields
\sin^{2}\theta_{{\bf q}\lambda}=ng_{{\bf q}\lambda}^{2}/(\Omega^{2}+ng_{{\bf q}\lambda}^{2})=\delta_{\lambda,\lambda_{1}}ng^{2}\sin^{2}\theta/(\Omega^{2}+ng^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)
. We then get for the damping rate

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm damp}

\displaystyle=\pi\,n{\mathcal{T}}^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}(u_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}} -v_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}})^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{{\bf q}\lambda}\delta(\varepsilon_{\bf p}-\varepsilon_{{\bf q}} -\omega_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}})

\displaystyle=\pi\,n{\mathcal{T}}^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\xi_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}}{\omega_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}}\frac{ng^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\Omega^{2}+ng^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\delta(\varepsilon_{\bf p}-\varepsilon_{{\bf q}} -\omega_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}),

(S43)

using that
(u_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}-v_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}})^{2}=\xi_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}/\omega_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}
. In the energy difference of the
\delta
-function, we may approximate
\varepsilon_{\bf p}-\varepsilon_{{\bf q}} -\omega_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}\simeq-v_{\rm g}(\theta)(q-p_{0})
, evaluating the expression at the carrier momentum
{\bf p}={\bf p}_{0}
. Inserting this in Eq. (
S44
) we then get

\Gamma_{\rm damp}=\pi\,n{\mathcal{T}}^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\xi_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}}{\omega_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}}\frac{ng^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\Omega^{2}c}\delta(q-p)\simeq\pi\,\frac{n{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}v_{\rm g}}p^{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}{\rm d}\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}{\rm d}\theta\frac{\xi_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}}{\omega_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}}\sin^{3}\theta,

(S44)

using that the group velocity for the dark state corresponding to the incoming photons is
v_{\rm g}=\Omega^{2}/(\Omega^{2}+ng^{2})\cdot c\simeq\Omega^{2}/ng^{2}\cdot c
. Although this expression is rather difficult to evaluate analytically, we can come with a simple upper bound by approximating
\xi_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}/\omega_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}=1
– it yields an upper bound since
\omega_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}>\xi_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}
. The integrals are then readily evaluated to
8\pi/3
, and writing the damping rate in units of
{t_{\text{B}}}
we finally get

\Gamma_{\rm damp}\simeq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\frac{a^{2}}{{a_{\text{B}}}\xi}\frac{c_{\rm s}}{v_{\rm g}}\left(\frac{p}{p_{\rm c}}\right)^{2},

(S45)

with
p_{\rm c}=mc_{\rm s}
the critical momentum of the BEC. This additional damping rate is thus significantly suppressed by
c_{\rm s}/v_{\rm g}\ll 1
. The factor of
\xi_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}/\omega_{{\bf p}-{\bf q}}
in the integrand can easily be included in a numerical calculation and leads to a further suppression of
\Gamma_{\rm damp}
by about
10\%
for the parameters used in Fig. 1(d), and by about
20\%
for the parameters in Fig. 3. For completeness we show Fig. 1(d) of the main text corrected with this additional damping rate in Fig.
S3
(a). Importantly, we see that
\Gamma_{\rm damp}
is completely negligible for
\Omega>\gamma
preserving the critical behaviour of the total scattering rate
\Gamma+\Gamma_{\rm damp}
. Further, in Fig.
S3
(b) we plot the pulse propagation as in Fig. 3 of the main text. The dark state propagation including the additional damping rate calculated here is shown in green, and only shows a very small correction.

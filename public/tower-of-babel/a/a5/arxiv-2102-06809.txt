From perspective maps to epigraphical projections
Michael P. Friedlander, Ariel Goodwin, Tim Hoheisel

Abstract
The projection onto the epigraph or a level set of a closed proper convex function can be achieved by finding a root of a scalar equation that involves the proximal operator as a function of the proximal parameter. This paper develops the variational analysis of this scalar equation. The approach is based on a study of the variational-analytic properties of general convex optimization problems that are (partial) infimal projections of the the sum of the function in question and the perspective map of a convex kernel. When the kernel is the Euclidean norm squared, the solution map corresponds to the proximal map, and thus the variational properties derived for the general case apply to the proximal case. Properties of the value function and the corresponding solution map—including local Lipschitz continuity, directional differentiability, and semismoothness—are derived. An SC 1 optimization framework for computing epigraphical and level-set projections is thus established. Numerical experiments on 1-norm projection illustrate the effectiveness of the approach as compared with specialized algorithms.

Introduction

The Moreau proximal map of a closed proper convex function
f
that maps a finite-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{E}_{f}
to
\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is given by the minimizing set

P_{\lambda}f(x)=\mathop{{\rm argmin}}_{u\in\mathbb{E}_{f}}\left\{f(u)+(1/2\lambda)\|x-u\|^{2}\right\}\quad(\lambda>0).

The proximal map is a central operation of algorithms for nonsmooth optimization, including first-order methods such as proximal gradient and operator splitting
[
35
,
3
]
. Geometrically, the proximal map corresponds to the Euclidean projection
\proj_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}
onto the epigraph
\mathrm{epi}\,f
; see
Fig.

1
. Indeed, for all positive
\lambda
and
x_{\lambda}:=\prox_{\lambda}f(x)
,

\big(x_{\lambda},\,f(x_{\lambda})\big)=P_{\mathrm{epi}\,f}(x,\,f(x_{\lambda})-\lambda).

(1)

Thus, the projection of an arbitrary point
(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{E}_{f}\times\mathbb{R}\not\in\mathrm{epi}\,f
corresponds to the proximal map of the base point
x
using the parameter
\lambda
that is the unique positive root of the function

0<\lambda\mapsto f(x_{\lambda})-\lambda-\alpha.

(2)

This connection between epigraphical projection and the proximal map—described by Beck
[
4
]
, Bauschke and Combettes
[
3
, Section 29.5]
, Chierchia et al.
[
11
, Proposition 1]
, and Meng et al.
[
31
,
32
]
—is a defining feature of a class of
epigraphical first-order methods
for structured convex optimization over
\mathbb{E}_{f}
that operate through a sequence of projections onto the epigraphs of the underlying functions. In effect, these methods operate on an equivalent optimization problem over
\mathbb{E}_{f}\times\mathbb{R}

[
11
,
45
,
43
,
44
]
.

Figure 1
:
The proximal map
x_{\lambda}:=P_{\lambda}f(x)
corresponds to the projection of the pair
(x,\,f(x_{\lambda})-\lambda))
onto the epigraph of
f
; see (
1
).

This paper develops a general analysis that provides, among other things, the variational properties of the maps

\displaystyle(x,\,\lambda)\mapsto x_{\lambda}:=P_{\lambda}f(x)\quad\ \mbox{ and }\ \quad(x,\,\lambda)\mapsto f(x_{\lambda}),

defined on
\mathbb{E}_{f}\times\mathbb{R}
. This analysis and its supporting calculus allows us to determine the sensitivity of the epigraphical projection with respect to the simultaneous variation of the base point
x
and the scaling parameter
\lambda
. Although the resulting mathematical statements are key for our deeper understanding of epigraphical first-order methods, the overall analysis applies much more generally.

The approach we take is based on the variational analysis of the optimal value function

p_{L,\omega,f}:(x,\,\lambda)\in\mathbb{E}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}\mapsto\inf_{u\in\mathbb{E}_{f}}f(u)+\omega^{\pi}(L(u,x),\,\lambda)

(3)

and its corresponding solution map. Here,
L
is a linear map, and the perspective transform
\omega^{\pi}
of a closed proper convex function
\omega
is defined by
\mathrm{epi}\,\omega^{\pi}=\mathrm{cl}\,\mathbb{R}_{+}(\mathrm{epi}\,\omega\times\{1\})
. When the linear map
L
is defined as
(u,\,x)\mapsto x-u
, the value function (
3
) is the infimal convolution of the functions
f
and
\omega^{\pi}(\cdot,\,\lambda)
. For this reason, we refer to this value function as the
generalized convolution
of these two functions.

The convex calculus we establish in
Section

3
for the analysis of the generalized convolution (
3
) provides a key tool for understanding several important cases. These include the variational properties of infimal convolution (
Section

3.3
); parametric constrained optimization (
Section

3.4
); the Moreau envelope of a convex function and the corresponding proximal map (
Section

4
); and epigraphical and level-set projections, including an
SC
1
optimization

[
20
,
36
]
method for numerically evaluating these projections (
Section

7
).

Contributions and related work

.

[

]
. These conditions hold, for instance, when

[

,

]
and Milzarek
[
33
]
.

1.1.2
Parametric constrained optimization

A general form of parametric constrained optimization occurs when we specialize the convolution kernel
\omega
in (
3
) to be the indicator function to a closed convex set.
Section

3.4
focuses the variational analysis of the generalized convolution operation to obtain formulas for the sensitivity of the optimal value of parametric optimization problems with relaxed linear constraints. This analysis includes perturbations to the relaxation parameter and to the right-hand side.

1.1.3
Moreau envelope and proximal map

In
Section

4
we further focus our analysis of infimal convolution on the
proximal case
, which occurs when
\omega=\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2}
. Here we develop the variational properties of the Moreau envelope and the associated proximal map as a function of the base point
x
and the proximal parameter
\lambda
, simultaneously. We also establish conditions under which the proximal map is semismooth*. Special attention is given to the limiting properties as
\lambda\downarrow 0
(
Propositions

4.1
and
4.5
) and to continuity and smoothness properties of the proximal map (
Corollaries

4.9
,
4.12
and
4.15
). Milzarek’s dissertation
[
33
]
includes a related analysis that generalizes the proximal parameter
\lambda
to a positive-definite matrix, but makes no statements regarding the limiting case where
\lambda
(or its matrix counterpart) vanishes, as we do in our general analysis. See also Attouch’s seminal monograph
[
2
]
.

1.1.4
Proximal value map

In
Section

5
we describe the main continuity properties of the
proximal value function

0<\lambda\mapsto f(P_{\lambda}f(\bar{x})),

(4)

where
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{E}
is held fixed.
Corollary

5.4
establishes its Lipschitzian properties and
Corollary

5.7
characterizes it as the derivative of the map

.
Proposition

5.9
describes sufficient conditions under which the proximal value function is semismooth.

1.1.5
Post compositions, and epigraphical and level-set projection

We use our analysis of the proximal value function (
4
) to establish, via
Proposition

6.1
, novel variational formulas for the Moreau envelope and proximal map of
post-compositions
, i.e., functions of the form
g\circ\psi
, where the scalar function
g
is increasing and convex, and
\psi
is closed proper convex. As a consequence,
Corollary

7.3
provides a refined version of the epigraphical projection conditions in (
1
), including analogous results for the projection onto the level set of
f
(
Corollary

7.1
). This analysis does not require the function to be finite-valued, and extends existing results
[
3
,
4
]
. Importantly,
Corollary

7.3
shows that the root of the aligning equation (
2
) coincides with the unique minimizer of a strongly convex scalar optimization problem. It follows from
Proposition

5.9
that the objective for this problem is continuously differentiable with a locally Lipschitz derivative. We use this latter property to derive a novel SC
1
optimization method to find the root of the function (
2
) and its analog in the level-set case. Numerical experiments in
Section

7.2
show that for projection onto the 1-norm unit ball, the resulting SC
1
method is competitive with two specialized state-of-the-art methods: CONDAT
[
16
]
and IBIS
[
30
]
.

1.2
Notation

Let
\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
denote the set of functions
f:\mathbb{E}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
that are proper closed convex, i.e., the epigraph
\mathrm{epi}\,f=\left\{(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{R}\,\left|\;f(x)\leq\alpha\right.\right\}
contains no vertical lines and is closed convex. Its
level sets
are given by
\mathrm{lev}_{\alpha}f:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{E}\,\left|\;f(x)\leq\alpha\right.\right\}
. The Fenchel conjugate of any function
f:\mathbb{E}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is
f^{*}(y)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{E}}\{\left\langle y,\,x\right\rangle-f(x)\}
. The Jacobian of a differentiable map
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
F^{\prime}(x)
.
We denote the Euclidean projection of
\bar{x}
onto
C
by
P_{C}(\bar{x})
. Throughout, fractions such as
(1/(2\lambda))
are abbreviated as
(1/2\lambda)
.

For a set
C\subset\mathbb{E}
, its
indicator function
is
\delta_{C}:\mathbb{E}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
given by
\delta_{C}(x):=0
if
x\in C
and
\delta_{C}(x)=+\infty
otherwise. The subdifferential of
\delta_{C}
is the
normal cone
of
C
, i.e.,
N_{C}(\bar{x}):=\partial\delta_{C}(\bar{x}):=\left\{v\in\mathbb{E}\,\left|\;\left\langle v,\,x-\bar{x}\right\rangle\leq 0\ (x\in C)\right.\right\}
, which is empty if
\bar{x}\not\in C
. The relative interior of
C
is the set
\mathrm{ri}\,C

[
38
, Section 6]
, and the
horizon cone
is
C^{\infty}:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{E}\,\left|\;\exists\{\lambda_{k}\}\downarrow 0,\ \{x_{k}\in C\}:\lambda_{k}x_{k}\to v\right.\right\}
. The
horizon function
of
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
is the closed proper convex and positively homogeneous function
f^{\infty}:\mathbb{E}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
defined via
\mathrm{epi}\,f^{\infty}=(\mathrm{epi}\,f)^{\infty}
.

Let
f_{k}:\mathbb{E}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. Then we say that the sequence
\{f_{k}\}

epi-converges
to a function
f:\mathbb{E}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
if

\forall x\in\mathbb{E}:\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\forall\{x_{k}\}\to x:&\liminf_{k\to\infty}f_{k}(x_{k})\geq f(x),\\
\exists\{x_{k}\}\to x:&\limsup_{k\to\infty}f_{k}(x_{k})\leq f(x),\end{array}\right.

and we write
f_{k}\overset{e}{\to}f
.
The sequence
\{f_{k}\}
is said to converge
continuously
to
f
if

\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{k}(x_{k})=f(x)\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{E}\ \mbox{and}\ \{x_{k}\}\to x,

and we write
f_{k}\overset{c}{\to}f
.
Furthermore,
\{f_{k}\}
is said to converge
pointwise
to
f
if

\lim_{k\to\infty}f_{k}(x)=f(x)\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{E},

and we write
f_{k}\overset{p}{\to}f
.
We extend these notions to families of functions

\{f_{\lambda}\}_{\{\lambda\downarrow 0\}}
via

f_{\lambda}\overset{\xi}{\to}f\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad\forall\{\lambda_{k}\}\downarrow 0:\;f_{\lambda_{k}}\overset{\xi}{\to}f\quad(\xi\in\{p,e,c\}).

2
Properties of the perspective map

The perspective map
\omega^{\pi}
that appears in the generalized infimal convolution
Eq.

3
provides a mechanism for controlling, through the parameter
\lambda
, the degree to which the functions
f
and
\omega
are combined. Beck and Teboulle
[
5
]
and Burke and Hoheisel
[
8
]
promoted this technique for generating smooth approximations to nonsmooth functions.

We work with the following definition of the perspective map of
\omega
, which appears in Rockafellar
[
38
, Corollary 13.5.1]
:

\omega^{\pi}:(z,\lambda)\in\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\times\mathbb{R}\mapsto\begin{cases}\lambda\omega\left(z/\lambda\right)&\mbox{if $\lambda>0$,}\\
\omega^{\infty}(z)&\mbox{if $\lambda=0$,}\\
+\infty&\mbox{if $\lambda<0$.}\end{cases}

(5)

For positive values of the parameter
\lambda
, the perspective map corresponds to
epi-multiplication
:

(\lambda\star\omega)(x):=\lambda\omega\left(x/\lambda\right).

The following result confirms the consistency of the perspective map (
5
) as the parameter
\lambda
decreases towards zero.

Lemma 2.1

(Variational convergence of epi-multiplication)

Let
\phi\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
. Then as
\lambda\downarrow 0
,
(\lambda\star\phi)(x)\to\phi^{\infty}(x)
for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,\phi
, and
\lambda\star\phi\overset{e}{\to}\phi^{\infty}
.

Proof 2.2

Proof.
The pointwise convergence of
(\lambda\star\phi)
over
\mathrm{dom}\,\phi
follows from
[
38
, Corollary 8.5.2]
. To prove epi-convergence, observe that, for all
\lambda>0
and
x\in\mathbb{E}
,

(\lambda\star\phi)(x)=\phi^{\pi}(x,\lambda).

Hence,

\liminf_{\overset{x\to\bar{x}}{\lambda\downarrow 0}}\,(\lambda\star\phi)(x)=\liminf_{\overset{x\to\bar{x}}{\lambda\downarrow 0}}\phi^{\pi}(x,\lambda)\geq\phi^{\pi}(\bar{x},0)=\phi^{\infty}(\bar{x})\quad\forall\bar{x}\in\mathbb{E},

where the inequality follows because
\omega^{\pi}
is a support function
[
38
, Corollary 13.5.1]
and thus closed
[
25
, Proposition 2.1.2]
.

Fix any sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}\downarrow 0
and take
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,\phi
. Then
(\lambda_{k}\star\phi)(\bar{x})\to\phi^{\infty}(\bar{x})
. Hence, in particular, with
x_{k}:=\bar{x}\;(k\in\mathbb{N})
,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\,(\lambda_{k}\star\phi)(x_{k})\leq\phi^{\infty}(\bar{x})

(6)

for all
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,\phi
. Now let
\bar{x}\notin\mathrm{dom}\,\phi
, take
\hat{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,\phi
and define
x_{k}:=\lambda_{k}\hat{x}+(1-\lambda_{k})\bar{x}\to\bar{x}
. Then

\phi^{\infty}(\bar{x})=\sup_{t>0}\frac{\phi(\hat{x}+t\bar{x})-\phi(\hat{x})}{t}\geq\frac{\phi\left(\hat{x}+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}-1\right)\bar{x}\right)-\phi(\hat{x})}{\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}-1}=\lambda_{k}\cdot\frac{\phi\left(\hat{x}+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}-1\right)\bar{x}\right)-\phi(\hat{x})}{1-\lambda_{k}}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large. Hence for such
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(\lambda_{k}\star\phi)(x_{k})=\lambda_{k}\phi\left(\frac{\lambda_{k}\hat{x}+(1-\lambda_{k})\bar{x}}{\lambda_{k}}\right)\leq(1-\lambda_{k})\phi^{\infty}(\bar{x})+\lambda_{k}\phi(\hat{x}).

Take the limit superior to obtain (
6
) here.
This establishes epi-convergence.

\square

The following result summarizes key properties of the perspective map. It also provides a support-function representation, which means that it can be written as the support function
\sigma_{\mathcal{D}}(y)\equiv\delta^{*}_{\mathcal{D}}(y)=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\left\langle x,\,y\right\rangle

for some set
\mathcal{D}
.

Proposition 2.3

(Properties of perspective map)

For
\omega\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{\omega})
, the following hold:

1.

\omega^{\pi}(z,\lambda)=\sigma_{\mathrm{epi}\,\omega^{*}}(z,-\lambda)
, hence
\omega^{\pi}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\times\mathbb{R})
is sublinear with
\mathrm{dom}\,\omega^{\pi}=\mathbb{R}_{+}(\mathrm{dom}\,\omega\times\{1\})
;

2.

(\omega^{\pi})^{*}(y,\beta)=\delta_{\mathrm{epi}\,\omega^{*}}(y,-\beta)
;

3.

for all
(z,\lambda)\in\mathrm{dom}\,\omega^{\pi}
,

\partial\omega^{\pi}(z,\lambda)=\begin{cases}\left\{(y,-\beta)\,\left|\;y\in\partial\omega(z/\lambda),\ \beta=\omega^{*}(y)\right.\right\}&\mbox{if $\lambda>0$,}\\[6.0pt]
\left\{(y,-\beta)\,\left|\;y\in\partial\omega^{\infty}(z),\ (y,\beta)\in\mathrm{epi}\,\omega^{*}\right.\right\}&\mbox{if $\lambda=0$.}\end{cases}

(7)

Proof 2.4

Proof. For Parts (a) and (b) see
[
38
, Corollary 13.5.1]
. Part (c) follows from
[
13
, Proposition 2.3]
or
[
1
, Lemma 3.8]
.

\square

The expression for the subdifferential
Eq.

7
, evaluated at the origin, reduces to
\partial\omega^{\pi}(0,0)=\{(y,-\beta)\in\mathrm{epi}\,\omega^{*}\}
, which is just the epigraph of
\omega^{*}
under the reflection
(z,\lambda)\mapsto(z,-\lambda)
. This follows because the subdifferential formula
\partial\omega^{\infty}(0)=\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{dom}\,\omega^{*}}(0)=\mathrm{dom}\,\omega^{*}
; cf.
[
39
, Corollary 8.25]
. Combettes
[
13
, Corollary 2.5]
provides a simplified characterization of
Proposition

2.3
under the additional assumption that
\omega
is supercoercive
[
3
, Definition 11.11]
.

3
Partial infimal projection with perspective maps

Our main objective in this section is to deduce the variational properties of the generalized infimal convolution
p_{L,\omega,f}
defined by
Eq.

3
. Throughout this section, we make the assumptions that
L
is a linear map from
\mathbb{E}_{f}\times\mathbb{E}_{x}
to
\mathbb{E}_{\omega}
for Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{E}_{i}
,
i\in\{f,x,\omega\}
, that
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{f})
and
\omega\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{\omega})
, and that
\range L\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}\mathrm{dom}\,\omega
. Under these standing assumptions, it follows from
Theorem

3.1
below that
p_{L,\omega,f}
is convex.

3.1
Infimal projection

We lead with a general result on infimal projections.

Theorem 3.1

(Conjugate and subdifferentials of infimal projection)

For a function
\psi\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{1}\times\mathbb{E}_{2})
, the infimal projection

p:x\in\mathbb{E}_{1}\mapsto\inf_{u}\,\psi(x,u)

(8)

is convex and

1.

p^{*}=\psi^{*}(\cdot,0)
, which is closed and convex;

2.

\partial p(x)=\left\{v\,\left|\;(v,0)\in\partial\psi(x,\bar{u})\right.\right\}
for all
\bar{u}\in\mathop{{\rm argmin}}\psi(x,\cdot)
;

3.

p^{*}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
if and only if

\mathrm{dom}\,\psi^{*}(\cdot,0)\neq\emptyset
;

4.

p\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
if
\mathrm{dom}\,\psi^{*}(\cdot,0)\neq\emptyset
, and hence the infimum in
Eq.

8
is attained when finite.

Proof 3.2

Proof. For convexity of
p
and Parts (a,b,d,e), see, e.g.,
[
26
, Theorem 3.101]
. Part (c) follows from Part (b) via Rockafellar
[
38
, Theorem 23.5]
.

\square

3.2
Generalized infimal convolution

The following auxiliary result is used in this section to derive conjugate and a subdifferential formulas for the value function
p_{L,\omega,f}
.

Lemma 3.3

(Domain and conjugate of linear-perspective composition)

The function

\eta:(u,x,\lambda)\in\mathbb{E}_{f}\times\mathbb{E}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}\mapsto\omega^{\pi}(L(u,x),\lambda)

is closed proper convex, i.e.,
\eta\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{f}\times\mathbb{E}_{x}\times\mathbb{R})
. The nonempty domain and its (possibly empty) relative interior are given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,\eta\phantom{)}

\displaystyle=\left\{(u,x,\lambda)\,\left|\;\lambda\geq 0,\;L(u,x)\in\lambda\cdot\mathrm{dom}\,\omega\right.\right\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,\eta)

\displaystyle=\left\{(u,x,\lambda)\,\left|\;\lambda>0,\;L(u,x)\in\lambda\cdot\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,\omega)\right.\right\}.

If
\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,\eta)
is nonempty, then
\eta^{*}
is the indicator to the set

C=\left\{(w,z,\mu)\,\left|\;\exists y\mid(y,-\mu)\in\mathrm{epi}\,\omega^{*},\;L^{*}(y)=(w,z)\right.\right\}.

(9)

Proof 3.4

Proof.

Proposition

2.3
(a) asserts that
\eta\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{f}\times\mathbb{E}_{x}\times\mathbb{R})
, and also yields the expression for its domain. Now assume that
\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,\eta)
is nonempty, and that there exists an element
(u,x)
such that
L(u,x)\in\lambda\cdot\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,\omega)
for some
\lambda>0
. Define the linear map
\tilde{L}:(u,x,\lambda)\mapsto(L(u,x),\lambda)
. Then,

\displaystyle\emptyset

\displaystyle\neq

\displaystyle\left\{(u,x,t)\,\left|\;t>0,\;L(u,x)\in t\cdot\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,\omega)\right.\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(u,x,\lambda)\,\left|\;\exists t>0:\;L(u,x)\in t\cdot\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,\omega),\ \lambda=t\right.\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{L}^{-1}\mathbb{R}_{++}(\mathrm{dom}\,\omega\times\{1\})

\displaystyle\overset{\rm(i)}{=}

\displaystyle\tilde{L}^{-1}\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathbb{R}_{+}(\mathrm{dom}\,\omega\times\{1\}))

\displaystyle\overset{\rm(ii)}{=}

\displaystyle\mathrm{ri}\,(\tilde{L}^{-1}\mathbb{R}_{+}(\mathrm{dom}\,\omega\times\{1\}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ri}\,(\tilde{L}^{-1}\mathrm{dom}\,\omega^{\pi})=\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,\eta),

where (i) uses
[
38
, Corollary 6.8.1]
and (ii) uses
[
38
, Theorem 6.7]
and the fact that
L^{-1}\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathbb{R}_{+}(\mathrm{dom}\,\omega\times\{1\}))\neq\emptyset
.

3.3.1

Thus far, our analysis has focused exclusively on the variational properties of the optimal value function

[

,

Let
S:\mathbb{E}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{E}_{2}
be a set-valued map between spaces
\mathbb{E}_{1}
and
\mathbb{E}_{2}
. The domain and graph of
S
, respectively, are the sets
\mathrm{dom}\,S:=\left\{x\,\left|\;S(x)\neq\emptyset\right.\right\}
and
\mathrm{gph}\,S:=\left\{(x,u)\in\mathbb{E}_{1}\times\mathbb{E}_{2}\,\left|\;u\in S(x)\right.\right\}
. The
outer limit
of
S
at
\bar{x}
is

\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\bar{x}}S(x):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{E}_{x}\,\left|\;\exists\{x_{k}\}\to\bar{x},\{y_{k}\in S(x_{k})\}\to y\right.\right\}.

Now let
A\subset\mathbb{E}
. The
tangent cone
of
A
at
\bar{x}\in A
is

T_{A}(\bar{x}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{t\downarrow 0}(A-\bar{x})/t.

The
regular normal cone
of
A
at
\bar{x}\in A
is the polar of the tangent cone, i.e.,

\hat{N}_{A}(\bar{x}):=\left\{v\,\left|\;\left\langle v,\,y\right\rangle\leq 0\;\;\forall y\in T_{A}(\bar{x})\right.\right\}.

The
limiting normal cone
of
A
at
\bar{x}\in A
is

N_{A}(\bar{x}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\to\bar{x}}\hat{N}_{A}(x).

The
coderivative
of
S
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,S
is the map
D^{*}S(\bar{x}\mid\bar{y}):\mathbb{E}_{2}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{E}_{1}
defined via

v\in D^{*}S(\bar{x}\mid\bar{y})(y)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(v,-y)\in N_{\mathrm{gph}\,S}(\bar{x},\bar{y}).

The
graphical derivative
of
S
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is the map
DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{y}):\mathbb{E}_{f}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{E}_{x}
given by

v\in DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{y})(u)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(u,v)\in T_{\mathrm{gph}\,S}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),

or, equivalently,
DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{y})(u)=DS(\bar{x}|\bar{y})(u)=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{\overset{t\downarrow 0,}{u^{\prime}\to u}}\frac{S(\bar{x}+tu^{\prime})-\bar{y}}{t}

[
39
, Eq. 8(14)]
.
The
strict graphical derivative
of
S
at

D_{*}S(\bar{x}\mid\bar{y}):\mathbb{E}_{f}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{E}_{x}

\displaystyle D_{*}S(\bar{x}\mid\bar{y})(w)=\left\{z\,\left|\;\exists\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 2.84544pt\{t_{k}\}\downarrow 0,\,\{w_{k}\}\to w,\,\{z_{k}\}\to z,\\
\{(x_{k},y_{k})\in\mathrm{gph}\,S\}\to(\bar{x},\bar{y})\end{array}\right\}:z_{k}\in\frac{S(x_{k}+t_{k}w_{k})-y_{k}}{t_{k}}\right.\right\}.

We adopt the convention to set

if

is a singleton, and proceed analogously for the graphical derivatives.

The above generalized derivatives possess the following definiteness properties when applied to a maximally monotone operator

, which (by definition) satisfies the inequality

\left\langle v-w,\,x-y\right\rangle\geq 0\quad\forall(v,w)\in T(x)\times T(y),

Lemma 3.9

Let

Proof 3.10

\bar{u}\in\mathbb{E}

N(\bar{x};\bar{u})=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{u\to\bar{u},\;t\downarrow 0}\hat{N}_{A}(\bar{x}+tu).

Note that
N(\bar{x};\bar{u})=\emptyset
if
\bar{u}\notin T_{A}(\bar{x})
and that
N(\bar{x};\bar{u})\subset N_{A}(\bar{x})
for all
u\in\mathbb{E}
. Given a set-valued map
S:\mathbb{E}_{f}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{E}_{x}
, based on the directional normal cone, we define the
directional coderivative

[
21
]

D^{*}S((\bar{x},\bar{u});(u,v)):\mathbb{E}_{x}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{E}_{f}
of
S
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,S
in the direction
(u,v)
via

\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}S((\bar{x},\bar{u});(u,v))(v^{*})=\left\{u^{*}\in\mathbb{E}_{f}\,\left|\;(u^{*},-v^{*})\in N_{\mathrm{gph}\,S}((\bar{x},\bar{y});(u,v))\right.\right\}.

As
N(\bar{x};\bar{u})=\emptyset
if
\bar{u}\notin T_{A}(\bar{x})
, we also have

\mathrm{dom}\,D^{*}S((\bar{x},\bar{u});(u,v))=\emptyset\quad\forall(u,v)\notin DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{u}).

(16)

Definition 3.17

(Semismothness*)

The set
A\subset\mathbb{E}
is semismooth* at
\bar{x}\subset A
if

\left\langle x^{*},\,u\right\rangle=0\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{E},\;x^{*}\in N_{A}(\bar{x};u).

The map
S:\mathbb{E}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{E}_{2}
is semismooth* at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,S
if
\mathrm{gph}\,S
is semismooth* at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, i.e.,

\left\langle u,\,u^{*}\right\rangle=\left\langle v,\,v^{*}\right\rangle\quad\forall(u,v)\in\mathbb{E}_{1}\times\mathbb{E}_{2},\;(v^{*},u^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}S((\bar{x},\bar{u});(u,v)).

The notion of
metric (sub)regularity
is used only in the next two results, and hence we refer the reader to the abundant literature for a definition, e.g.,
[
18
]
.

Proposition 3.18

(Metric regularity and semismoothness*)

Let
F:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
be continuously differentiable at
\bar{x}
, let
Q\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be semismooth* (as a set) at
F(\bar{x})
and let
S:\mathbb{E}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{E}_{2},\;S(x):=F(x)-Q
be metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},0)
. Then
F^{-1}(Q)
is semismooth* at
\bar{x}
(as a set).

Proof 3.19

Proof. By
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
, for any
h\in\mathbb{E}_{1}
,

N_{F^{-1}(Q)}(\bar{x};h)\subset F^{\prime}(\bar{x})^{*}N_{Q}(F(\bar{x});F^{\prime}(\bar{x})h),

(17)

see also
[
6
, Remark 2.1]
. Since
Q
is semismooth* at
F(\bar{x})
,

\left\langle v,\,z\right\rangle=0\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{E}_{2},\ v\in N_{Q}(F(\bar{x});z).

Therefore

\left\langle v,\,F^{\prime}(\bar{x})h\right\rangle=0\quad\forall h\in\mathbb{E}_{1},\ v\in N_{Q}(F(\bar{x});F^{\prime}(\bar{x})h),

and hence

\left\langle u,\,h\right\rangle=0\quad\forall h\in\mathbb{E}_{1},\ u\in F^{\prime}(\bar{x})^{*}N_{Q}(F(\bar{x});F^{\prime}(\bar{x})h).

By (
17
) this implies that

\left\langle u,\,h\right\rangle=0\quad\forall h\in\mathbb{E}_{1},\ u\in N_{F^{-1}(Q)}(\bar{x};h),

i.e.,
F^{-1}(Q)
is semismooth* at
\bar{x}
.

\square

Corollary 3.20

(Semismoothness* of the infimal convolution solution map)

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
, let
(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})\in\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}
and let
\omega
be strongly convex and twice continuously differentiable. Then the map
P_{\omega,f}
from (
13
) is semismooth* at
((\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}),P_{\omega,f}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}))
if
\partial f
is semismooth* at
\left(P_{\omega,f}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}),\nabla\omega(\frac{1}{\bar{\lambda}}[\bar{x}-P_{\omega,f}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})])\right)
.

Proof 3.21

Proof. Without loss of generality, assume
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,

F(x,\lambda,z):=(z,\nabla\omega([x-z]/\lambda).

Then for all
x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\lambda>0
, setting
V:=\nabla^{2}\omega([x-z]/\lambda)\succ 0
we have

F^{\prime}(x,\lambda,z)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&I\\
\frac{1}{\lambda}V&-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}V(x-z)&-\frac{1}{\lambda}V\end{pmatrix}.

Hence,
\ker F^{\prime}(x,\lambda,z)^{*}=\{0\}
for all
x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\lambda>0
. Thus,
(x,\lambda,z)\mapsto F(x,\lambda,z)-\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
is metrically regular. As
\mathrm{gph}\,P_{\omega,f}=F^{-1}(\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f)
, if
\partial f
is semismooth* at
\left(P_{\omega,f}(\bar{x},\bar{x}),\nabla\omega\left(\frac{\bar{x}-P_{\omega,f}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda})}{\bar{\lambda}}\right)\right)=F(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda},P_{\omega,f}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}))
, by
Proposition

3.18
,
P_{\omega,f}
is semismooth* at
((\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}),P_{\omega,f}(\bar{x},\bar{\lambda}))
.

\square

Corollary

3.20
provides a sufficient criterion for establishing semismoothness* of the solution map
P
on the interior of its domain. It will be a topic of future research to exploit this on a broad scale, but we can immediately state the following result for a function
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
which is either twice continuously differentiable or
piecewise linear-quadratic
(PLQ) in the sense of Rockafellar and Wets
[
39
, Definition 10.20]
.

Proposition 3.22

(Semismoothness* of the subdifferential)

For
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
, the subgradient
\partial f
is semismooth* at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
under one of the following conditions:

1.

f
is twice continuously differentiable at
\bar{x}
;

2.

f
is piecewise linear-quadratic (in which case
\partial f
is semismooth* on
\mathbb{E}
).

Proof 3.23

Proof.

Assume condition (a) holds. If
f
is twice continuously differentiable, then
D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid\bar{y})=\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})=D^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid\bar{y})
, see
[
39
, Example 8.43]
. Now let
(u,v)\in T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, i.e.,
v\in D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid\bar{y})(u)=\{\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})u\}
, and let
(x^{*},y^{*})\in N_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}((\bar{x},\bar{y});(u,v))\subset N_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, hence
x^{*}\in D^{*}(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid\bar{y})(-y^{*})=\{-\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})y^{*}\}
. Thus, we have

\left\langle(x^{*},y^{*}),\,(u,v)\right\rangle=\left\langle y^{*},\,\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})u\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})y^{*},\,u\right\rangle=0.

Now assume condition (b) holds. It follows from
[
39
, Proposition 12.30]
that
\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
is a finite union of polyhedra. Then
[
23
, Proposition 3.4/3.5]
yields that
\mathrm{gph}\,\partial f
is semismooth*, which gives the desired statement.

\square

3.4
Constrained optimization

We now consider an application of
Theorem

3.1
to derive the variational properties of the optimal value of the constrained optimization problem

v:(x,\lambda)\in\mathbb{E}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}\mapsto\inf_{u\in\mathbb{E}_{f}}\left\{f(u)\,\left|\;L(u,x)\in\lambda S\right.\right\},

(18)

where
S\subset\mathbb{E}_{\omega}
is a closed convex set. This function can be viewed as a special case of (
3
), where
\omega=\delta_{S}
for some closed convex set
S\subset\mathbb{E}_{\omega}
. To see this, it is sufficient to note that

\delta^{\pi}_{S}(z,t)=\begin{cases}\delta_{\lambda S}(z)&\mbox{if $\lambda>0$,}\\
\delta_{S^{\infty}}(z)&\mbox{if $\lambda=0$,}\\
+\infty&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

and thus
L(u,x)\in\lambda S
if and only if
\delta_{S}^{\pi}(L(u,x),\lambda)
vanishes.
Let
S^{\circ}:=\left\{v\,\left|\;\left\langle v,\,s\right\rangle\leq 1\;\forall x\in S\right.\right\}
be the polar to the set
S
.

The following result is an immediate consequence of the general study in
Theorem

3.5
.

Corollary 3.24

(Conjugate and subdifferential of the constrained value function)

Let
v
be given by (
18
) with
S\subset\mathbb{E}_{\omega}
closed and convex, and assume that

\exists u\in\mathrm{ri}\,\mathrm{dom}\,f,\ x\in\mathbb{E}_{x}\;:\;L(u,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{++}(\mathrm{ri}\,S).

Then the following hold.

1.

We have

v^{*}(y,\mu)=\inf_{w}\left\{f^{*}(w)\,\left|\;\exists a\in-\mu S^{\circ}\;:\;L^{*}(a)=(-w,y)\right.\right\}.

If
S
is a cone then

v^{*}(y,\mu)=\inf_{w}\left\{f^{*}(w)+\delta_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}(\mu)\,\left|\;(-w,y)\in L^{*}(S^{\circ})\right.\right\}.

2.

For any
(x,\lambda)\in\mathrm{dom}\,v
and
\bar{u}\in\mathop{{\rm argmin}}_{u}\left\{f(u)\,\left|\;L(u,x)\in\lambda S\right.\right\}
,

\displaystyle\partial v(x,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left\{(v,-\sigma_{S}(y))\,\left|\;y\in N_{S}(L(\bar{u},x)/\lambda),\;(0,v)\in\mathcal{D}(\bar{u},y)\right.\right\}&\mbox{if $\lambda>0$,}\\
\left\{(v,-\beta)\,\left|\;\exists y\in N_{S^{\infty}}(L(\bar{u},y))\cap(\beta S^{\circ})\;:\;(0,v)\in\mathcal{D}(\bar{u},y)\right.\right\}&\mbox{if $\lambda=0$,}\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{D}(u,y):=\partial f(u)\times\{0\}+L^{*}(y)
.
If
S
is bounded (hence compact), then

\displaystyle\partial v(x,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left\{(v,-\sigma_{S}(y))\,\left|\;y\in N_{S}\left(L(\bar{u},x)/\lambda\right),\;(0,v)\in\mathcal{D}(\bar{u},y)\right.\right\}&\mbox{if $\lambda>0$,}\\
\left\{(v,-\beta)\,\left|\;\exists y\in\beta S^{\circ}\;:\;(0,v)\in\mathcal{D}(\bar{u},y)\right.\right\}&\mbox{if $\lambda=0$.}\end{cases}

3.

We have
v^{*}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{x}\times\mathbb{R})
if and only if there exist
y\in\mathbb{E}_{x}
,
w\in\mathrm{dom}\,f^{*}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that
(-w,y)\in-\beta L^{*}(S^{\circ})
. In this case, also
v\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{x}\times\mathbb{R})
and the infimum is attained when finite.

Proof 3.25

Proof.
Part (a) follows from
Theorem

3.5
(a) with
w^{*}=\sigma_{S}
. If
S
is a cone then
w^{*}=\delta_{S^{\circ}}
.
Part (b) follows from
Theorem

3.5
(b), observing that
\omega^{\infty}=\delta_{S^{\infty}}
and that
S^{\infty}=\{0\}
if
S
is bounded, in which case
N_{S^{\infty}}=\mathbb{E}_{\omega}
.
Part (c) follows from (a) and
Theorem

3.5
(c).

\square

Proof 3.27

Part (b) follows from

3.24
(b) with the foregoing observations.
d) For

4
Moreau envelope and proximal map

In this section we outline existing and new results regarding the variational properties of the Moreau envelope and the proximal map of a closed proper convex function.

4.1
The Moreau envelope

The
Moreau envelope
of
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
is defined by

e_{\lambda}f(x):=\min_{u\in\mathbb{E}}\left\{f(u)+(1/2\lambda)\|x-u\|^{2}\right\}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{E},\ \lambda>0,

which has a Lipschitz gradient given by
\nabla e_{\lambda}f(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(x-P_{\lambda}f(x))
.

The following result summarizes limiting properties of the Moreau envelope as
\lambda\downarrow 0
.

Proposition 4.1

(Convergence of the Moreau envelope)

For
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
, the following hold as
\lambda\downarrow 0
:

1.

e_{\lambda}f\overset{e}{\to}f
and
e_{\lambda}f\overset{p}{\to}f
(in fact
e_{\lambda}f(x)\uparrow f(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{E}
);

2.

\lambda f\overset{e}{\to}\delta_{\mathrm{cl}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}
;

3.

\lambda e_{\lambda}f(x)\to\frac{1}{2}d^{2}_{\mathrm{cl}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}(\bar{x})
as
x\to\bar{x}
;

4.

\lambda\partial f
converges to
N_{\mathrm{cl}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}
graphically in the sense of
[
39
, Definition 5.32]
;

5.

for
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,\partial f
we have
\nabla e_{\lambda}f(x)\to\mathop{{\rm argmin}}_{g\in\partial f(x)}\|g\|
.

Proof 4.2

Proof. Part (a). See, e.g.,
[
39
, Theorem 1.25, Proposition 7.4]
.

Part (b). By
Lemma

2.1
(b) and
[
38
, Theorem 13.3]
,

\lambda\star f^{*}\overset{e}{\to}(f^{*})^{\infty}=\sigma_{\mathrm{dom}\,f}.

Wijsman’s theorem
[
39
, Theorem 11.34]
then yields

\lambda f=(\lambda\star f^{*})^{*}\overset{e}{\to}\delta_{\mathrm{cl}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}.

Part (c). By Part (b),
\lambda f\overset{e}{\to}\delta_{\mathrm{cl}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}
. Hence, by
[
39
, Theorem 7.37]
,

\lambda e_{\lambda}f=e_{1}(\lambda f)\overset{c}{\to}e_{1}\delta_{\mathrm{cl}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}=\tfrac{1}{2}d^{2}_{\mathrm{cl}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}.

Part (d). Follows from Part (b) and Attouch
[
39
, Theorem 12.35]
.

Part (e). See
[
2
, Remark 3.32]
.

\square

Note that
Proposition

4.1
(e) implies that there exists
K>0
such that

\forall\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,\partial f\;\exists K>0\;\forall\lambda>0:\;\|P_{\lambda}f(\bar{x})-\bar{x}\|\leq K\lambda.

(21)

Proposition

4.1
(a) suggests the following extension of the Moreau envelope at
\lambda=0
:

p_{f}:(x,\lambda)\in\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{R}\mapsto\begin{cases}e_{\lambda}f(x)&\mbox{if $\lambda>0$,}\\
f(x)&\mbox{if $\lambda=0$,}\\
+\infty&\mbox{if $\lambda<0$.}\end{cases}

This is exactly the value function
p_{\omega,f}
from (
11
) with
\omega=\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
. Hence, we may rely on our general study on infimal convolution from
Section

3.3
to understand the properties of this extension of the Moreau envelope.

Corollary 4.3

(Conjugate and subdifferential of the Moreau envelope)

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
. Then
p_{f}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{R})
and

1.

p_{f}^{*}(y,\mu)=f^{*}(y)+\delta_{\mathrm{epi}\,\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}}(y,-\mu)
and
p_{f}^{*}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{R})
;

2.

for all
(x,\lambda)\in\mathrm{dom}\,p_{f}
,

\partial p_{f}(x,\lambda)=\begin{cases}\vskip 2.84544pt\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}[x-P_{\lambda}f(x)],\ -\frac{1}{2}\|\frac{1}{\lambda}[x-P_{\lambda}f(x)]\|^{2}\right)&\mbox{if $\lambda>0$},\\
\left\{(v,\beta)\,\left|\;-v\in\partial f(x),\ \frac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}\leq\beta\right.\right\}&\mbox{if $\lambda=0$}.\end{cases}

Proof 4.4

Proof. We are in the situation of
Corollary

3.7
with
\omega=\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
. In particular, the qualification condition (

) is trivially satisfied.

\square

4.2
Properties of the proximal map

We now turn our attention to the proximal map. It is straightforward to show
P_{\lambda}f(x)\to x
as
\lambda\downarrow 0
for any
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
. The following proposition, which generalizes this statement, can be derived from monotone operator theory
[
39
, Theorem 12.37]
. The proof that we provide here instead relies on epigraphical convergence.

Proposition 4.5

(Convergence of the proximal map)

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{E}
. Then

\lim_{\overset{\lambda\downarrow 0,}{x\to\bar{x}}}P_{\lambda}f(x)=P_{\mathrm{cl}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}(\bar{x}).

Proof 4.6

Proof.
Let
\{\lambda_{k}\}\downarrow 0
,
\{x_{k}\}\to\bar{x}
, and
\phi_{k}(u):=\lambda_{k}f(u)+\frac{1}{2}\|u-x_{k}\|^{2}
. Use
Proposition

4.1
(b) to deduce
\lambda_{k}f\overset{e}{\to}\delta_{\mathrm{cl}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}
. Then because
\frac{1}{2}\|(\cdot)-x_{k}\|^{2}\overset{c}{\to}\frac{1}{2}\|(\cdot)-\bar{x}\|^{2}
, we obtain

\phi_{k}\overset{e}{\to}\phi:=\delta_{\mathrm{cl}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}+\frac{1}{2}\|(\cdot)-\bar{x}\|^{2};

see
[
39
, Theorem 7.46 b)]
. Now observe that

P_{\lambda_{k}}f(x_{k})=\mathop{{\rm argmin}}\phi_{k}
and
P_{\mathrm{cl}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}(\bar{x})=\mathop{{\rm argmin}}\phi
. Since all functions
\phi_{k}
are convex and
\phi
is level-bounded (in fact, strongly convex), the sequence
\{\phi_{k}\}
is, by
[
39
, Exercise 7.32 c)]
, eventually level-bounded (in the sense of
[
39
, p. 266]
). Therefore, we can apply
[
39
, Theorem 7.33]
, with
\varepsilon_{k}=0\;(k\in\mathbb{N})
, to deduce

P_{\lambda_{k}}f(x_{k})\to P_{\mathrm{cl}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}(\bar{x}).

\square

We record the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 4.7

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
and fix positive scalars
\lambda
and
\mu
. Then for all
x\in\mathbb{E}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mu}\big(\|P_{\mu}f(x)

\displaystyle-x\|^{2}-\|P_{\lambda}f(x)-x\|^{2}+\|P_{\mu}f(x)-P_{\lambda}f(x)\|^{2}\big)

(22)

\displaystyle\leq f(P_{\lambda}f(x))-f(P_{\mu}f(x))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left(\|P_{\mu}f(x)-x\|^{2}-\|P_{\lambda}f(x)-x\|^{2}-\|P_{\mu}f(x)-P_{\lambda}f(x)\|^{2}\right),

and

\|P_{\lambda}f(x)-P_{\mu}f(x)\|^{2}\leq\frac{\mu-\lambda}{\lambda+\mu}\left(\|P_{\mu}f(x)-x\|^{2}-\|P_{\lambda}f(x)-x\|^{2}\right).

(23)

Proof 4.8

Proof. Set
P(\tau):=P_{\tau}f(\bar{x})
for all
\tau>0
. To obtain the bounds in (
22
), use
[
39
, Eq. 7(34)]
to infer

f(x)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}-f(P(\tau))-\frac{1}{2\tau}\|P(\tau)-\bar{x}\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2\tau}\|x-P(\tau)\|^{2}\quad\forall\tau>0,\ \forall x\in\mathbb{E}.

For
\tau=\lambda
and
x=P(\mu)
, we hence obtain

f(P(\mu))+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|P(\mu)-\bar{x}\|^{2}-f(P(\lambda))-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|P(\lambda)-\bar{x}\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|P(\mu)-P(\lambda)\|^{2}.

Analogously, for
\tau=\mu
and

Example 5.1

(Nonconvexity of the proximal value)

The next result describes the monotonicity and continuity of the map (

Corollary 5.2

(Monotonicity and continuity in

Proof 5.3

Given functions

We are now equipped to answer the initial question about computing epigraphical and level-set projections via proximal mappings. Our approach is based on the Moreau envelopes of the indicator functions to the epigraph and level set of a function

provides the required tools.

7.2.3
Discussion

The numerical examples we presented extend easily to other useful cases involving matrices, such as the nuclear norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and the barrier function
-\log\det
on the space of symmetric matrices, using variational formulas that depend on matrix spectra
[
28
,
29
]
. In these cases, the main computational effort involves computing singular value and eigenvalue decompositions, respectively, of the matrix iterates.

The cases where
\theta^{\prime}_{\xi}
does not satisfy either Assumption
7.1.1
or the domain condition
P_{\mathrm{cl}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}(\bar{x})\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
lies outside the theoretical guarantees presented in this section, though the algorithms we present may still work in practice. In the case where
\mathrm{dom}\,f\subsetneq\mathbb{E}
is open, the formula provided by Chierchia et al.
[
11
, Proposition 1]
is a viable option.

8
Final remarks

Our analysis on the variational properties of epigraphical projections and infimal convolution is motivated by the authors’ larger research interests on variations of first-order methods that operate in a lifted space. The promising work by Chierchia et al.
[
11
]
on epigraphical-projection methods for minimizing convex functions over
p
-norm constraints shows promise for this algorithmic approach, and we aim to develop methods for more general problem classes. We are also motivated by statistical M-estimation approaches that include as an additional unknown a particular parameter that characterizes data distribution
[
15
]
. The variational calculus that we derive is a useful tool for developing algorithmic approaches for solving these lifted M-estimation problems.

There are at least two avenues of future research that extend our analysis in this paper.

\bm{K}
-epigraphical projections.

A significant generalization of the post-composition operation defined in
Section

6
occurs when we allow compositions of the form
f=g\circ H:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, where

•

K\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}
a closed convex cone;

•

H:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}

K
-convex, i.e., the
K
-epigraph

\left\{(X,Y)\,\left|\;Y-H(x)\in K\right.\right\}
is convex;

•

g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{2})

K
-increasing
, i.e.,
g\leq g((\cdot)+v)
for all
v\in K
.

This
convex convex-composite
setting was studied by Burke et al.
[
10
]
, and the required subdifferential formulas for the analysis are readily available. This may lead to a proximal calculus and ultimately to formulas and algorithms for projecting onto
K
-epigraphs, thus encompassing the study in
Section

6
.

Semismoothness* of subdifferential operators.

The notion of semismooth* sets and maps is recent and still in development. One of the critical conditions in our study is the semismoothness* of the subdifferential operator
\partial f
, which also occurs in a recent report by Khanh et al.
[
27
]
. This suggests an important avenue of research that relaxes the overarching convexity assumption and, in particular, establishes verifiable sufficient conditions.

An integrated semigroup approach for age structured equations with diffusion and non-homogeneous boundary conditions
Arnaud Ducrot, Pierre Magal, Alexandre Thorel

Abstract
In this work, we consider a linear age-structured problem with diffusion and non-homogeneous boundary conditions both for the age and the space variables. We handle this linear problem by re-writing it as a non-densely defined abstract Cauchy problem. To that aim we develop a new result on the closedness of a commutative sum of two non-densely defined operators by using the theory of integrated semigroups. As an application of this abstract result, we are able to associate a suitable integrated semigroup to some age-structured problem with spatial diffusion and equipped with non-homogeneous boundary conditions. This integrated semigroup is characterized by the description of its infinitesimal generator. Further applications of our abstract result are also given to the commutative sum of two almost sectorial operators, for which we derive a closedness results. Key words . Age-structured problem with diffusion, non densely-defined operators, almost sectorial operators, integrated semigroups, commutative sum of linear operators. 2010 Mathematical Subject Classification . 47A10, 47D06, 47D62, 47F05, 47D03.

In this work we study the following linear age-structured problem with diffusion and non-homogeneous boundary conditions

\begin{cases}\left(\partial_{t}+\partial_{a}-\Delta_{x}\right)u(t,a,x)=f(t,a,x),&t>0,\;a>0,\;x\in\Omega,\\
u(t,0,x)=g(t,x),&t>0,\;x\in\Omega,\\
\nabla u(t,a,x)\cdot\nu(x)=h(t,a,x),&t>0,\;a>0,\;x\in\partial\Omega,\\

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\left(\partial_{t}+\partial_{a}-\Delta_{x}\right)u_{2}(t,a,x)=0,&t>0,\;a>0,\;x\in\Omega,\\
u_{2}(t,0,x)=g(t,x),&t>0,\;x\in\Omega,\\
\nabla u_{2}(t,a,x)\cdot\nu(x)=0,&t>0,\;a>0,\;x\in\partial\Omega,\\
u_{2}(0,a,x)=0,&a>0,\;x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.3)

Each of these sub-problems can be re-written as abstract Cauchy problems when considered in suitable Lebesgue spaces. The first one can be handled by the commutative sum of two densely defined operators (see

In the literature the properties of the sum of two resolvent commuting linear operators has been extensively studied, starting from the pioneer work of Da Prato and Grisvard

Rather our arguments and our analysis are based on the integrated semigroups theory, in the spirit of the works of Thieme

Definition 2.1

Let
T:D(T)\subset X\to X
be a linear operator. We denote by

{\rm Graph}\,(T):=\left\{(x,Tx),\;x\in D(T)\right\}\subset X\times X,

its graph. We say that the linear operator
T
is closable if it admits a closed extension, that is there exists a closed linear operator
T^{\prime}:D(T^{\prime})\subset X\to X
such that

\overline{{\rm Graph}\,(T)}\subset{\rm Graph}\,(T^{\prime}).

In that case, we define the closure of
T
, denoted by
\overline{T}:D\left(\overline{T}\right)\subset X\to X
, as the smallest closed extension of
T
, namely

D(\overline{T})=\{x\in X:\;\exists!y\in X\;(x,y)\in\overline{{\rm Graph}\,(T)}\}\text{ and }\overline{T}x=y,\quad\forall x\in D(\overline{T}).

In other words, one has
x\in D(\overline{T})
and
y=\overline{T}x
if and only if there exists a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\geq 0}\subset D(T)
such that
\|x_{n}-x\|\to 0
and
\|Tx_{n}-y\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Throughout the rest of this paper, if
T:D(T)\subset X\to X
is a linear operator, we use the notation
{\rm R}(T)
to denote the range of
T
, that is
{\rm R}(T)=T\left(D(T)\right)\subset X
.

\left(S_{B}\diamond f\right)(t)=\left(T_{B}\ast f\right)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\lambda-B_{0}\right)^{\beta}T_{B_{0}}(t-s)\left(\lambda-B\right)^{-\beta}f(s){\rm d}s,

as well as the following estimate

\left\|\left(S_{B}\diamond f\right)(t)\right\|\leq M_{\beta}\left\|\left(\lambda-B\right)^{-\beta}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\beta}e^{\omega(t-s)}\left\|f(s)\right\|{\rm d}s,

wherein
M_{\beta}
denotes some positive constant and
\omega>\omega_{0}(B_{0})
.

Remark 2.8

We may note that the above result ensures that the linear operator
B
satisfies Assumption
2.3
with any
\omega>\omega_{0}(B_{0})
and for any
p>p^{\ast}
.

2.2
Assumptions and main results

In this subsection, we formulate the main assumptions that will be used in this note and state our main results as well. Let
A:D(A)\subset X\to X
and
B:D(B)\subset X\to X
be two closed and resolvent commuting linear operators. Here by resolvent commuting we mean that

(\lambda-A)^{-1}(\mu-B)^{-1}=(\mu-B)^{-1}(\lambda-A)^{-1},\quad\forall\lambda,\mu\in\rho(A)\cap\rho(B),

(2.11)

wherein
\rho(A)
and
\rho(B)
denote the resolvent set of
A
and
B
, respectively.

As far as operator
A:D(A)\subset X\to X
is concerned, we assume that it satisfies the following set of properties.

Assumption 2.9
(Assumption on the linear operator
A
)

The closed linear operator
A
satisfies Assumptions
2.2
with parameters
\omega_{A}\in\mathbb{R}
,
M_{A}\geq 1
and Assumption
2.3
with some parameter
p\in[1,\infty)
and, without loss of generality,
M=M_{A}
and
\omega=\omega_{A}
, that reads as for all
f\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}\left([0,\infty);X\right)
one has

\left\|(S_{A}\diamond f)(t)\right\|\leq M_{A}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{p\omega_{A}(t-s)}\|f(s)\|^{p}{\rm d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

About the linear operator
B:D(B)\subset X\to X
, we assume that it satisfies the following set of assumptions.

Assumption 2.10
(Assumption on the linear operator
B
)

The closed linear operator
B:D(B)\subset X\to X
satisfies Assumption
2.6
with parameters
\omega_{B}\in\mathbb{R}
and
p^{*}\in[1,\infty)
.

In order to state our main result, we need an additional assumption coupling the properties of the linear operators
A
and
B
, that reads as follows.

Assumption 2.11
(Assumption coupling
A
and
B
)

In addition to the two above assumptions, namely Assumption
2.9
and
2.10
, and the commutativity property (
2.11
), we assume that the parameters
p
and
p^{*}
satisfy

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{*}}>1.

Setting
q^{*}\in(1,\infty]
, the conjugate exponent of
p^{*}
defined by
\frac{1}{p^{*}}+\frac{1}{q^{*}}=1
, the above inequality rewrites as

\frac{1}{q^{*}}<\frac{1}{p}.

As recalled in the previous subsection, Assumption
2.9
allows us to introduce the strongly continuous semigroup
\{T_{A_{0}}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}\left(\overline{D(A)}\right)
generated by
A_{0}
, the part of
A
in
\overline{D(A)}
, while Assumption
2.10
allows us to consider
\{S_{B}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
the integrated semigroup generated by
B
,
T_{B}(t)=\frac{{\rm d}S_{B}(t)}{{\rm d}t}
as well as
\{T_{B_{0}}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}\left(\overline{D(B)}\right)
, the analytic and strongly continuous semigroup generated by
B_{0}
, the part of
B
in
\overline{D(B)}
. Let us observe that from the commutativity property (
2.11
) one has, for all
t\geq 0
,

T_{A_{0}}(t)\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}\subset\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)},\quad T_{B_{0}}(t)\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}\subset\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)},

and

T_{A_{0}}(t)T_{B_{0}}(t)x=T_{B_{0}}(t)T_{A_{0}}(t)x,\quad\forall x\in\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)},\;\forall t\geq 0.

Using the above notations, the main result of this note reads as the follows.

Theorem 2.12

Let Assumption
2.11
be satisfied. Consider the linear operator
A+B:D(A+B)\subset X\to X
given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &D(A+B)=D(A)\cap D(B),\\
&(A+B)x=Ax+Bx,\quad\forall x\in D(A+B).\end{aligned}\right.

Then, the following hold

(a)

The linear operator
(A+B)
is closable and there exists
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, we have

\overline{\overline{D(B)}+\overline{D(A)}}\subset\overline{{\rm R}\left(\lambda-\left(A+B\right)\right)}.

(2.12)

Moreover,
A+B
admits a closed extension, denoted by
\widehat{A+B}:D\left(\widehat{A+B}\right)\subset X\to X
such that

\rho\left(\widehat{A+B}\right)
is not empty and contains some interval of the form
\left[\widehat{\lambda},\infty\right)
, for some
\widehat{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}
.

(b)

If moreover we assume that there exists
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that, for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, one has

{\rm R}\left(\lambda-\left(A+B\right)\right)\text{ is dense in }X,

(2.13)

then the closure
\overline{A+B}:D\left(\overline{A+B}\right)\subset X\to X
satisfies Assumption
2.2
and the following properties hold true:

(i)

\overline{D\left(\overline{A+B}\right)}=\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}
.

(ii)

The strongly continuous semigroup generated by
\left(\overline{A+B}\right)_{0}
, the part of
\overline{A+B}
in
\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}
and denoted by

is given by

T_{\left(\overline{A+B}\right)_{0}}(t)=T_{A_{0}}(t)_{|\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}}\circ T_{B_{0}}(t)_{|\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

(iii)

\overline{A+B}
on

(iv)

satisfies the following regularity property: for any

with

\frac{1}{r}<\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{*}}-1,

and any
\omega>\omega_{0}(B_{0})
there exists a continuous function

with
\delta(0)=0
such that for any function

, the map
t\longmapsto\left(S_{\overline{A+B}}\ast f\right)(t)
is continuously differentiable from
[0,\infty)
into
X
and the following estimate holds

\|\left(S_{\overline{A+B}}\diamond f\right)(t)\|\leq\delta(t)\|f\|_{L^{r}(0,t;X)},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

3
Proof of Theorem
2.12

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.12
. To that aim we closely follow the construction provided by Ducrot and Magal in
[
13
]
to handle the closability of the commutative sum of two linear operators, one satisfying Assumption
2.9
while the other is the generator of a strongly continuous semigroup.
Here we extend this methodology to take care of the additional difficulty coming from the fact that the linear operator
B
is, as
A
, also not densely-defined and that the derivative semigroup
T_{B}(t)=\frac{{\rm d}S_{B}}{{\rm d}t}(t)
admits a singularity at
t=0
. Here we shall crucially make use the fact that such a singularity is of polynomial type and thus belongs to a suitable Lebesgue space.

To perform our analysis and prove Theorem
2.12
, we shall make use of exponentially weighted Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces. More specifically, for any
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
we define the Banach space
L^{p}_{\eta}(0,\infty;X)
by

L^{p}_{\eta}(0,\infty;X)=\left\{\psi\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}([0,\infty);X):\;t\mapsto e^{\eta t}\psi(t)\in L^{p}(0,\infty;X)\right\},

endowed with the weighted norm, denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{q}_{\eta}(0,\infty;X)}
, and given by

\|\psi\|_{L^{p}_{\eta}(0,\infty;X)}=\|e^{\eta\cdot}\psi\|_{L^{p}(0,\infty;X)},\quad\forall\psi\in L^{p}_{\eta}(0,\infty;X).

We also introduce the associated weighted Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}_{\eta}(0,\infty;X)
given by

W^{1,p}_{\eta}(0,\infty;X)=\left\{\psi\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}([0,\infty);X):\;\left(\psi,\psi^{\prime}\right)\in L^{p}_{\eta}\hskip-1.42271pt\left(0,\infty;X\right)^{2}\hskip-1.42271pt\right\}\hskip-1.42271pt.

Before proving Theorem
2.12
we need to prove several preliminary lemmas and results.
Our first lemma reads as follows.

Lemma 3.1

Let Assumption
2.9
be satisfied. Recalling that
\omega_{A}
,
M_{A}
and
p
are defined in Assumption
2.9
, let
\eta>\max\left(0,\omega_{A}\right)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(S_{A}\diamond f(t-\cdot)\right)(t).

\mathbb{K}_{A}:L_{\eta}^{p}\left(0,\infty;X\right)\to\overline{D(A)}

\mathbb{K}_{A}(f)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(S_{A}\diamond f(t-\cdot)\right)(t),

\left\|\mathbb{K}_{A}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}\left(L_{\eta}^{p}\left(0,\infty;X\right),\overline{D(A)}\right)}\leq\dfrac{M_{A}^{1+\frac{1}{p}}}{1-e^{\left(\omega_{A}-\eta\right)}}=:\widetilde{M}_{A}\left(\eta\right).

\eta>\max(0,\omega_{A})
and

t\mapsto\left(S_{A}\diamond f(t-\cdot)\right)(t)
satisfies the Cauchy criterion as
t\to\infty
.
To that aim, let us first observe that due to the constant variation formula (
2.8
), for all
0\leq s\leq t
, one has

(S_{A}\diamond f)(t)=T_{A_{0}}(t-s)(S_{A}\diamond f)(s)+(S_{A}\diamond f(s+\cdot))(t-s).

Hence one gets, for all
t>0
and
h>0

\begin{split}(S_{A}\diamond f(t+h-.))(t+h)\;=\;&T_{A_{0}}(t+h-h)(S_{A}\diamond f(t+h-\cdot))(h)\\
&+(S_{A}\diamond f(t+h-h-.))(t+h-h),\end{split}

so that, for any

and
h>0

\begin{split}(S_{A}\diamond f(t+h-.))(t+h)-(S_{A}\diamond f(t&-\cdot))(t)\\

(3.14)

Now, for any
t>0
and

\|T_{A_{0}}(t)(S_{A}\diamond f(t+h-\cdot))(h)\|\leq\|T_{A_{0}}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\overline{D(A)})}\|(S_{A}\diamond f(t+h-\cdot))(h)\|,

Next, since the strongly continuous semigroup
\{T_{A_{0}}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}

\|T_{A_{0}}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\overline{D(A)})}\leq M_{A}e^{\omega_{A}t},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

\begin{split}&\|T_{A_{0}}(t)(S_{A}\diamond f(t+h-\cdot))(h)\|\\
&\leq M_{A}e^{\omega_{A}t}\left(M_{A}\int_{0}^{h}e^{p\omega_{A}(h-l)}\|f(t+h-l)\|^{p}{\rm d}l\right)^{1/p}\\
&\leq M_{A}^{1+\frac{1}{p}}e^{\omega_{A}t}\left(\int_{0}^{h}e^{p\omega_{A}(h-l)}e^{-p\eta(t+h-l)}e^{p\eta(t+h-l)}\|f(t+h-l)\|^{p}{\rm d}l\right)^{1/p},\end{split}

\begin{split}&\|T_{A_{0}}(t)(S_{A}\diamond f(t+h-\cdot))(h)\|\\

Since

Now we derive the estimate for
\mathbb{K}_{A}
.
To do, note that since
\omega_{A}-\eta<0

(S_{A}\diamond f(0-\cdot))(0)=0

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\|(S_{A}\diamond f(t-\cdot))(t)\right\|&\leq\left\|(S_{A}\diamond f(t-\cdot))(t)-(S_{A}\diamond f(k-\cdot))(k)\right\|\\

(3.15)

3.1
together with (

f\in L^{p}_{\eta-\delta}(0,\infty;X)\;\Longleftrightarrow\;e^{-\delta\cdot}f\in L^{p}_{\eta}(0,\infty;X),

Lemma 3.3

Let
\eta>\max\left(0,\omega_{A}\right)

\mathbb{K}_{A}(f)\in D(A).

\mathbb{K}_{A}(f^{\prime})+A\mathbb{K}_{A}(f)+f(0)=0,

Proof.

2.9

\left(S_{A}\diamond f\right)(t)=S_{A}(t)x,

Now let

S_{A}(t)f(t)\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty.

(3.17)

On the one hand, let us observe that, since

, for all

, one has

\left(S_{A}\diamond f\right)(t)=S_{A}(t)f(0)+\left(S_{A}\ast f^{\prime}\right)(t)=S_{A}(t)f(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\left(S_{A}\diamond f^{\prime}\right)(s)ds.

Thus, this yields

\displaystyle\left(S_{A}\diamond f(t-.)\right)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{A}(t)f(t)-\left(S_{A}\ast f^{\prime}(t-.)\right)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{A}(t)f(t)-\int_{0}^{t}S_{A}(t-s)f^{\prime}(t-s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{A}(t)f(t)-\int_{0}^{t}\left(S_{A}\diamond f^{\prime}(t-.)\right)(s)ds.

The above computation coupled together with (
3.17
) and the definition of
\mathbb{K}_{A}(f)
in Lemma
3.1
yields

\mathbb{K}_{A}(f)=-\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\left(S_{A}\diamond f^{\prime}(t-.)\right)(s)ds.

On the other hand, note that the map
t\rightarrow\left(S_{A}\diamond f^{\prime}(t-.)\right)(t)
satisfies, for any
t\geq 0
, the following equation

\displaystyle\left(S_{A}\diamond f^{\prime}(t-.)\right)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\int_{0}^{t}\left(S_{A}\diamond f^{\prime}(t-.)\right)(s){\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}f^{\prime}(t-s){\rm d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\int_{0}^{t}\left(S_{A}\diamond f^{\prime}(t-.)\right)(s){\rm d}s+f(t)-f(0).

Finally, since
A
is closed and
f^{\prime}\in L^{p}_{\eta}(0,\infty;X)
, letting
t\to\infty
and using Lemma
3.1
(with
f
replaced by
f^{\prime}
) yields
\mathbb{K}_{A}(f)\in D(A)
and the following identity

\mathbb{K}_{A}(f^{\prime})=-A\mathbb{K}_{A}(f)-f(0).

This completes the proof of the result.

Lemma 3.4

Fix
\eta>\max(0,\omega_{A})
. Then, for each
f\in L^{p}_{\eta}\left(0,\infty;D(B)\right)
one has

\mathbb{K}_{A}(f)\in D(B)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{K}_{A}(Bf)=B\mathbb{K}_{A}(f).

Here the notation
f\in L^{p}_{\eta}\left(0,\infty;D(B)\right)
is used to mean
f\in L^{p}_{\eta}\left(0,\infty;X\right)
,
f(t)\in D(B)
for almost any
t\geq 0
and
t\to Bf(t)
belongs to
L^{p}_{\eta}\left(0,\infty;X\right)
.

The proof of this lemma is similar to the one of Lemma 4.2 in
[
13
]
and thus omitted.

B
.
According to Assumption
2.10

B_{0}

\{T_{B}(t)\}_{t>0}\subset\mathcal{L}(X)
the semigroup obtained by differentiating

with respect to time, for

Recall also that for each
\omega>\omega_{0}(B_{0})
and each
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{q^{*}},1\right)
, there exists some constant

such that

\|T_{B}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq\frac{M}{t^{\alpha}}e^{\omega t},\quad\forall t>0.

(3.18)

Now fix

and recalling Assumption
2.11
, let us fix

With such a choice of the parameter

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda,A,B\right)x=&\lim_{t\to\infty}\left[S_{A}\diamond\left(e^{-\lambda(t-\cdot)}T_{B}(t-\cdot)x\right)\right](t),\quad\forall x\in X\\
&=\mathbb{K}_{A}\left(e^{-\lambda\cdot}T_{B}(\cdot)x\right).\end{split}

Let us observe that, using Lemma
3.2
, the following identities hold

\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda,A,B\right)=\mathcal{R}\left(0,A,B-\lambda\right)=\mathcal{R}\left(0,A-\lambda,B\right),\quad\forall\lambda>\eta+\omega_{0}(B_{0}).

Next the following estimate holds true

Lemma 3.5

For any
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{q^{*}},\frac{1}{p}\right)
, for each
\omega>\omega_{0}(B_{0})
, there exists some constant
M>0
such that, for any
\lambda>\eta+\omega
, one has

\left\|\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda,A,B\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,\overline{D(A)})}\leq\frac{M}{\left(\lambda-(\eta+\omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-\alpha}}.

In particular one has

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\left\|\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda,A,B\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,\overline{D(A)})}=0.

Proof.
Fix
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{q^{*}},\frac{1}{p}\right)
and
\omega>\omega_{0}(B_{0})
and recall that there exists
M=M_{\alpha,\omega}
such that (
3.18
) holds true.
Next using Lemma
3.1
, one has for any

\left\|\mathcal{R}(\lambda,A,B)x\right\|\leq\widetilde{M}_{A}\left(\eta\right)\left\|e^{-\lambda\cdot}T_{B}(\cdot)x\right\|_{L^{p}_{\eta}(0,\infty;X)}.

We infer from (

\left\|\mathcal{R}(\lambda,A,B)x\right\|\leq\widetilde{M}_{A}\left(\eta\right)M\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-p\left(\lambda-(\eta+\omega)\right)t}\frac{1}{t^{\alpha p}}{\rm d}t\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\|x\|.

The change of variable

ensures that estimate (
3.19
) holds true and this completes the proof of the lemma.

Our next preliminary result reads as follows.

Lemma 3.6

For all
\lambda>\eta+\omega_{0}\left(B_{0}\right)
one has

\mathcal{R}(\lambda,A,B)D(B)\subset D(A)\cap D(B),

and

\left(\lambda-(A+B)\right)R\left(\lambda,A,B\right)x=x,\quad\forall x\in D(B).

Proof.
To prove the above lemma, let us fix
\lambda>\eta+\omega_{0}(B_{0})
.
Next fix

and let us check that the map

belongs both to
W^{1,p}_{\eta}(0,\infty;X)
and to
L^{p}_{\eta}\left(0,\infty;D(B)\right)
.
Note that the first statement follows from (
2.10
). The second statement directly follows from the first one coupled with the following property of the semigroup
T_{B_{0}}(t)
, that reads as

T_{B_{0}}(t)x\in D(B_{0}),\quad\forall t>0\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{{\rm d}T_{B_{0}}(t)x}{{\rm d}t}=B_{0}T_{B}(t)x,\quad\forall t>0.

Hence (see (
2.10
))
t\mapsto e^{-\lambda t}T_{B_{0}}(t)x
belongs to

.
As a consequence of Lemma
3.3
and
3.4
, one obtains that

\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda,A,B\right)D(B)\subset D(A)\cap D(B),\quad\forall\lambda>\eta+\omega_{0}(B_{0}).

The second statement of the result follows from the same arguments as the ones developed in
[
13
]
.

Proposition 3.7

Let Assumption
2.11
be satisfied. Then there exists
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that, for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, one has

\overline{\overline{D(B)}+\overline{D(A)}}\subset\overline{{\rm R}(\lambda-(A+B))}.

Proof.
To prove the above proposition, let us define the part of
B
in
\overline{D(A)}
, that is linear operator
B_{1}:D(B_{1})\subset\overline{D(A)}\rightarrow\overline{D(A)}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}D(B_{1})=\{x\in D(B)\cap\overline{D(A)}:Bx\in\overline{D(A)}\},\\

Let us also recall that the linear operator
A_{0}:D(A_{0})\subset\overline{D(A)}\rightarrow\overline{D(A)}

2.5

A
in
\overline{D(A)}

2.2

.

Moreover, thanks to the commutativity property (
2.11
), for all

(\lambda-B)^{-1}\overline{D(A)}\subset\overline{D(A)}.

Then, we deduce that

\rho(B_{1})=\rho(B)\quad\text{and}\quad(\lambda-B_{1})^{-1}=(\lambda-B)^{-1}_{|\overline{D(A)}},\quad\forall\lambda\in\rho(B_{1})=\rho(B).

2.6

\|(\lambda-B_{1})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\overline{D(A)})}\leq\|(\lambda-B)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{p*}}}\right)\text{ as }\lambda\to\infty.

.

Moreover, from Assumption
2.3

2.8

2.9

Next using Theorem 4.12 in

(3.20)

Furthermore Lemma
3.6
ensures that

D(B)\subset{\rm R}\left(\lambda-(A+B)\right),\quad\forall\lambda>\eta+\omega_{0}(B_{0}).

(3.21)

Finally, (
3.20
) and (
3.21

.

(ii)

We are now able to prove the closability of

Proposition 3.10

Let Assumption
2.11
be satisfied. Then the linear operator
A+B:D(A)\cap D(B)\subset X\to X
is closable and its closure, denoted by
\overline{A+B}:D\left(\overline{A+B}\right)\subset X\to X
satisfies for all
\widehat{\lambda}\geq\lambda_{0}

\left\{\begin{aligned} &D(A)\cap D(B)\subset D\left(\overline{A+B}\right)\subset\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)},\quad D\left(\overline{A+B}\right)\subset D\left(L_{\widehat{\lambda}}\right),\\
&\overline{A+B}\,x=\left(L_{\widehat{\lambda}}+\widehat{\lambda}\right)x,\quad\forall x\in D\left(\overline{A+B}\right).\end{aligned}\right.

Let us furthermore assume that there exists
\widehat{\lambda}\geq\lambda_{0}
such that

{\rm R}\left(\widehat{\lambda}-(A+B)\right)\quad\text{is dense in }X.

Then, the closure
\overline{A+B}
coincides with
L_{\widehat{\lambda}}+\widehat{\lambda}
, namely
D\left(L_{\widehat{\lambda}}\right)=D\left(\overline{A+B}\right)
. Moreover one also has
\widehat{\lambda}\in\rho\left(\overline{A+B}\right)
and

\left(\widehat{\lambda}-\left(\overline{A+B}\right)\right)^{-1}=\mathcal{R}(\widehat{\lambda},A,B).

Proof.
We first prove the first part of the proposition, namely the closability part.
To that aim, fix
\widehat{\lambda}\geq\lambda_{0}

and consider
G_{0}={\rm Graph}\,\left(A+B-\widehat{\lambda}\right)
. Then, from
(i1)
in Lemma
3.9
and recalling that
L_{\widehat{\lambda}}
is a closed linear operator one gets

G_{0}\subset G\;\Longrightarrow\;\overline{G_{0}}\subset{\rm Graph}\,\left(L_{\widehat{\lambda}}\right).

This means that
L_{\widehat{\lambda}}:D\left(L_{\widehat{\lambda}}\right)\subset X\to X
is an invertible closed extension of
(A+B-\widehat{\lambda})
and this completes the proof of the first part of the result.

To prove the second part, assume now there exists
\widehat{\lambda}\geq\lambda_{0}
such that the range
\left(\widehat{\lambda}-(A+B)\right)\left[D(A)\cap D(B)\right]
is dense in
X
.

Fix
(x,y)\in{\rm Graph}\,\left(L_{\widehat{\lambda}}\right)
, that is
x\in D\left(L_{\widehat{\lambda}}\right)
and
y=L_{\widehat{\lambda}}x
, namely
x=-\mathcal{R}\left(\widehat{\lambda},A,B\right)y
. Then, there exists a sequence
\{(x_{n},y_{n})\}_{n\geq 0}\subset\left(D(A)\cap D(B)\right)\times X
such that

y_{n}\to y\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\lambda}x_{n}-\left(A+B\right)x_{n}=-y_{n},\quad\forall n\geq 0.

Next, according to Lemma
3.9

(i2)
, one has

x_{n}=-\mathcal{R}\left(\widehat{\lambda},A,B\right)y_{n},\quad\forall n\geq 0,

so that the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\geq 0}
converges in
X
toward
x_{\infty}:=-\mathcal{R}\left(\widehat{\lambda},A,B\right)y\in X
. This ensures that
x=x_{\infty}\in D\left(L_{\widehat{\lambda}}\right)
. Since
\overline{A+B}
is closed, one also deduces that

x_{\infty}\in D\left(\overline{A+B}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\lambda}x_{\infty}-\left(\overline{A+B}\right)x_{\infty}=-y.

Therefore
x\in D\left(\overline{A+B}\right)
and
(x,y)\in{\rm Graph}\left(\left(\overline{A+B}\right)-\widehat{\lambda}\right)
.
Hence
D\left(L_{\widehat{\lambda}}\right)=D\left(\overline{A+B}\right)
and
L_{\widehat{\lambda}}=\overline{A+B}-\widehat{\lambda}
. Since
0\in\rho\left(L_{\widehat{\lambda}}\right)
we obtain that
\widehat{\lambda}\in\rho\left(\overline{A+B}\right)
and
\left(\widehat{\lambda}-\overline{A+B}\right)^{-1}=\mathcal{R}\left(\widehat{\lambda},A,B\right)
.
This completes the proof of the proposition.

We are now in position to complete this section by proving Theorem
2.12
.

Proof of Theorem
2.12
.

Note that the above proposition already proves the first part of the theorem on the closability of
A+B
.
It also show that when the range of
\lambda-(A+B)
is dense, for all
\lambda
large enough, then
\overline{D(\overline{A+B})}=\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}
and there exists
\tilde{\lambda}
large enough such that
[\tilde{\lambda},\infty)\subset\rho\left(\overline{A+B}\right)
and that

\left(\lambda-\overline{A+B}\right)^{-1}=\mathcal{R}(\lambda,A,B),\quad\forall\lambda\geq\tilde{\lambda}.

We will now prove
{\rm(ii)}
. To that aim observe that
\{T_{A_{0}}(t)T_{B_{0}}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}
. Next, for any
x\in\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}
and any
\lambda\geq\tilde{\lambda}
, one has

\begin{split}\left(\lambda-\left(\overline{A+B}\right)_{0}\right)^{-1}x&=\mathcal{R}(\lambda,A,B)x\\
&=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(S_{A}\diamond\left(e^{-\lambda(t-\cdot)}T_{B}(t-\cdot)x\right)\right)(t).\end{split}

Since
x\in\overline{D(B)}
, one has
T_{B}(s)x=T_{B_{0}}(s)x
, for all
s\geq 0
while since
x\in\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}
, one has
T_{B_{0}}(s)x\in\overline{D(A)}
for all
s\geq 0
. As a consequence for any
t\geq 0
we have

\begin{split}\left(S_{A}\diamond(e^{-\lambda(t-\cdot)}T_{B}(t-\cdot)x)\right)(t)&=\int_{0}^{t}T_{A_{0}}(t-s)e^{-\lambda(t-s)}T_{B_{0}}(s)x{\rm d}s\\
&=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}T_{A_{0}}(s)T_{B_{0}}(s)x{\rm d}s.\end{split}

Hence we get
x\in\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}
and for any
\lambda\geq\tilde{\lambda}
we have

\left(\lambda-\left(\overline{A+B}\right)_{0}\right)^{-1}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda s}T_{A_{0}}(s)T_{B_{0}}(s)x{\rm d}s,

that ensures that
\left(\overline{A+B}\right)_{0}
, the part of
\overline{A+B}
in
\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}
, is the infinitesimal generator of the strongly continuous semigroup
\left\{T_{A_{0}}(t)T_{B_{0}}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
(see
[
15
]
). This completes the proof of
{\rm(ii)}
.

We now turn to the proof of
{\rm(iii)}
. To that aim, let us first observe that due to Lemma
3.5
, for all
x\in X
, one has

\left(\lambda-\overline{A+B}\right)^{-1}x\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad\lambda\to\infty,

with respect to the operator norm topology in
\mathcal{L}(X)
. Hence, due to
{\rm(ii)}
,
\overline{A+B}
satisfies Assumption
2.2
and thus
\overline{A+B}
is the infinitesimal generator of a non-degenerate and exponentially bounded integrated semigroup
\{S_{\overline{A+B}}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and for all
\lambda>0
large enough, one has

\left(\lambda-\overline{A+B}\right)^{-1}x=\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda l}S_{\overline{A+B}}(l)x{\rm d}l,\quad\forall x\in X.

On the other hand, let us fix
\mu>\omega_{A}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, we infer from Lemma
2.5
that for each
t\geq 0
one has

(\mu-A_{0})^{-1}\left(S_{A}\diamond\left(e^{-\lambda(t-\cdot)}T_{B}(t-\cdot)\right)\right)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}T_{A_{0}}(s)(\mu-A)^{-1}T_{B}(s){\rm d}s.

Hence, using integration by parts we obtain, for all
x\in X
and
t\geq 0
,

\begin{split}\left(S_{A}\diamond\left(e^{-\lambda(t-\cdot)}T_{B}(t-\cdot)x\right)\right)(t)=\,\,&e^{-\lambda t}\left(S_{A}\diamond T_{B}(t-\cdot)x\right)(t)\\
&+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}\left(S_{A}\diamond T_{B}(s-\cdot)x\right)(s){\rm d}s.\end{split}

Now, choose
\lambda>0
large enough such that
\lambda>\eta+\max\left(0,\omega_{0}(B)\right)
. Then, for all
x\in X
, we have

e^{-\lambda t}\left(S_{A}\diamond T_{B}(t-\cdot)x\right)(t)\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty,

and for all
x\in X
and all
\lambda>0
large enough,

\begin{split}\left(\lambda-\overline{A+B}\right)^{-1}x&=\mathcal{R}(\lambda,A,B)x\\
&=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(S_{A}\diamond\left(e^{-\lambda(t-\cdot)}T_{B}(t-\cdot)x\right)\right)(t)\\
&=\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda s}\left(S_{A}\diamond T_{B}(s-\cdot)x\right)(s){\rm d}s.\end{split}

This implies that

S_{\overline{A+B}}(t)x=\left(S_{A}\diamond T_{B}(t-\cdot)x\right)(t),\quad\forall t\geq 0,\;\forall x\in X,

and this completes the proof of
{\rm(iii)}
.

Finally, let us complete the proof of
{\rm(iv)}
. To do so, let us fix
\tau>0
and let
f\in C^{1}\left([0,\tau];X\right)
be given. Next observe that one has

S_{\overline{A+B}}\ast f(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\left(S_{A}\diamond T_{B}(l-\cdot)f(t-l)\right)(l){\rm d}l,\;\forall t\in[0,\tau].

Next, fix
\mu>\omega_{A}
and note that for any
t\in[0,\tau]
one has

\begin{split}(\mu-A_{0})^{-1}S_{\overline{A+B}}\ast f(t)&=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{l}T_{A_{0}}(s)(\mu-A)^{-1}T_{B}(s)f(t-l){\rm d}s{\rm d}l\\
&=(\mu-A_{0})\int_{0}^{t}T_{A_{0}}(s)(\mu-A)^{-1}T_{B}(s)\hskip-1.42271pt\left[\int_{s}^{t}f(t-l){\rm d}l\right]\hskip-1.42271pt{\rm d}s.\end{split}

Setting
\sigma=t-l
yields

(\mu-A_{0})^{-1}S_{\overline{A+B}}\ast f(t)=\int_{0}^{t}T_{A_{0}}(s)(\mu-A)^{-1}T_{B}(s)\left[\int_{0}^{t-s}f(\sigma){\rm d}\sigma\right]{\rm d}s,

so that, for each
t\in[0,\tau]
, we get

(\mu-A_{0})^{-1}\left(S_{\overline{A+B}}\diamond f\right)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}T_{A_{0}}(s)(\mu-A)^{-1}T_{B}(s)f(t-s){\rm d}s.

This ensures that

\left(S_{\overline{A+B}}\diamond f\right)(t)=\Bigl(S_{A}\diamond\bigl(T_{B}(t-\cdot)f(\cdot)\bigr)\Bigr)(t),\quad\forall t\geq 0.

Now, since for each
t\in(0,\tau]
the map
s\mapsto T_{B}(t-s)f(s)\in L^{p}(0,t;X)
, Assumption
2.9
for the linear operator
A
ensures that, for all
t\in[0,\tau]
,

\|\left(S_{\overline{A+B}}\diamond f\right)(t)\|\leq M_{A}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{p\omega_{A}(t-s)}\|T_{B}(t-s)f(s)\|^{p}{\rm d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

Hence, due to estimate (
3.18
), for each
\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{q^{*}},\frac{1}{p}\right)
and each
\omega>\omega_{0}(B_{0})
, there exists some constant
M=M_{\alpha,\omega}
(independent of
f
) such that for any
t\in[0,\tau]
and any
1<r<\frac{1}{p\alpha}
it holds that

\begin{split}\|\left(S_{\overline{A+B}}\diamond f\right)(t)\|^{p}&\leq M_{A}M\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{p\alpha}}\,e^{p(\omega_{A}-\omega)(t-s)}\|f(s)\|^{p}{\rm d}s\\
&\leq M_{A}M\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e^{rp(\omega_{A}-\omega)(t-s)}}{(t-s)^{rp\alpha}}{\rm d}s\right)^{1/r}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)\|^{r^{\prime}p}{\rm d}s\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}.\end{split}

This prove
{\rm(iv)}
for any
f
of class
C^{1}
and the result follows from Theorem 3.6 in
[
28
]
.

4
Applications

In this section, we study problem (
1.1
), that we recall below

\begin{cases}\left(\partial_{t}+\partial_{a}-\Delta_{x}\right)u(t,a,x)=f(t,a,x),&t>0,\;a>0,\;x\in\Omega,\\
u(t,0,x)=g(t,x),&t>0,\;x\in\Omega,\\
\nabla u(t,a,x)\cdot\nu(x)=h(t,a,x),&t>0,\;a>0,\;x\in\partial\Omega,\\
u(0,a,x)=u_{0}(a,x),&a>0,\;x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

(4.23)

Herein
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
denotes a bounded domain with a smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
.

To handle the above problem, fix
q\in(1,\infty)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
those specific properties will be discussed below (see condition (
2.12
)). Our aim in this section is to consider (
4.23
) using the results of Theorem
2.12
on the commutative sum of linear operators and to reformulate this partial differential equation as an abstract Cauchy problem involving a non-densely defined operator that generated a suitable integrated semigroup with a well described generator.
Before going to the main result of this section, we need to introduce various spaces and linear operators.

Firstly, we consider the Banach space

Y:=W^{1-\frac{1}{q},q}\left(\partial\Omega\right)\times L^{q}(\Omega),

(4.24)

as well as the linear operator
B^{\prime}:D(B^{\prime})\subset Y\to Y
defined by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &D(B^{\prime})=\{0\}\times W^{2,q}(\Omega),\\
&B^{\prime}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\nabla\psi\cdot\nu\\
\Delta\psi\end{pmatrix},\quad\forall~\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}\in D(B^{\prime}).\end{aligned}\right.

Here
W^{1-\frac{1}{q},q}\left(\partial\Omega\right)
denotes the trace space on
\partial\Omega
associated to functions in
W^{1,q}(\Omega)
.

Define also the linear operator
B_{0}^{\prime}:D(B_{0}^{\prime})\subset\overline{D(B^{\prime})}\to\overline{D(B^{\prime})}
as the part of
B^{\prime}
in
\overline{D(B^{\prime})}
, that is the linear operator given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &D(B^{\prime}_{0})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}\in\{0\}\times W^{2,q}(\Omega):\nabla\psi\cdot\nu=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\right\},\\
&B^{\prime}_{0}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\Delta\psi\end{pmatrix},\quad\forall~\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}\in D(B^{\prime}_{0}).\end{aligned}\right.

It is well known (see for instance Henry
[
18
]
and Yagi
[
40
]
) that
B^{\prime}_{0}
is a sectorial operator on
\overline{D(B^{\prime})}=\{0\}\times L^{q}(\Omega)
.

Next, recalling the definition of the space
Y
in (
4.24
), let us define the Banach space

X=\overline{D(B^{\prime})}\times L^{p}(0,\infty;Y)=\left(\{0\}\times L^{q}(\Omega)\right)\times L^{p}(0,\infty;Y).

We define the linear operator
B:D(B)\subset X\to X
by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &D(B)=D(B_{0}^{\prime})\times L^{p}(0,\infty,D(B^{\prime})),\\
&B\begin{pmatrix}y\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}B_{0}^{\prime}y\\
a\mapsto B^{\prime}\psi(a,\cdot)\end{pmatrix},\quad\forall~\begin{pmatrix}y\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}\in D(B),\end{aligned}\right.

as well as
A:D(A)\subset X\to X
the linear operator given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &D(A)=\{0_{\overline{D(B^{\prime})}}\}\times\left\{\varphi\in W^{1,p}(0,\infty;Y):\;\varphi(0)\in\overline{D(B^{\prime})}\right\},\\
&A\begin{pmatrix}0_{\overline{D(B^{\prime})}}\\
\varphi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\varphi(0,\cdot)\\
-\partial_{a}\varphi\end{pmatrix},\quad\forall~\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\varphi\end{pmatrix}\in D(A).\end{aligned}\right.

Next we now consider the linear operator
L:D(L)\subset X\to X
defined as the sum of these two linear operators, that is

D(L)=D(A)\cap D(B)\quad\text{and}\quad Lx=Ax+Bx,\quad\forall x\in D(A)\cap D(B).

In a more concrete form, the operator
L
expresses as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &D(L)=D(A)\cap D(B)=\{0_{\overline{D(B^{\prime})}}\}\times\left(W^{1,p}(0,\infty;Y)\cap L^{p}(0,\infty,D(B^{\prime}))\right),\\
&L\begin{pmatrix}0_{\overline{D(B^{\prime})}}\\
\varphi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\varphi(0)\\
a\mapsto-\partial_{a}\varphi(a,\cdot)+B^{\prime}\varphi(a,\cdot)\end{pmatrix},\quad\forall~\begin{pmatrix}0_{\overline{D(B^{\prime})}}\\
\varphi\end{pmatrix}\in D(L).\end{aligned}\right.

The main result of this section is concerned with the closability of the linear operator
L
and with property of the integrated semigroup generated by
L
.
Our result reads as follows.

Theorem 4.1

Assume that the parameters
p>1
and
q>1
satisfy the following condition

1<\frac{1+q}{2q}+\frac{1}{p}.

(4.25)

Then the linear operator
L:D(L)\subset X\to X
is closable. Its closure
\overline{L}:D\left(\overline{L}\right)\subset X\to X
enjoys the following properties

(i)

D(A)\cap D(B)\subset D(L)
and
\overline{D\left(\overline{L}\right)}=\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}
,

(ii)

The linear operator
\overline{L}:D(\overline{L})\subset X\to X
satisfies Assumption
2.9
with parameter
p
replaced by any
r\in(1,\infty)
such that

1+\frac{1}{r}<\frac{1+q}{2q}+\frac{1}{p}.

To prove this result we apply Theorem
2.12
with the linear operators
A
and
B
defined above. To that aim, let us first recall some properties satisfied by these operators.

We start with the description of the linear operator
A:D(A)\subset X\to X
. It enjoys the following properties.

Lemma 4.2

Let
p>1
be given. The linear operator
A:D(A)\subset X\to X
satisfies Assumption
2.9
.
More precisely, one has

(i)

(0,\infty)\subset\rho(A)
and for all
\lambda>0
one has

(\lambda-A)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}y\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
e^{-\lambda\cdot}y+\int_{0}^{\cdot}e^{-\lambda(\cdot-s)}\psi(s){\rm d}s\end{pmatrix},\quad\forall\begin{pmatrix}y\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}\in X,

and

\left\|(\lambda-A)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}=\mathcal{O}\left(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p}}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad\lambda\to\infty.

(ii)

The part of
A
in
\overline{D(A)}
, denoted by
A_{0}:D(A_{0})\subset\overline{D(A)}\to\overline{D(A)}
is the infinitesimal generator of a strongly continuous semigroup, denoted by
\left\{T_{A_{0}}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
, on
\overline{D(A)}
.

(iii)

The integrated semigroup
\left\{S_{A}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(X)
generated by
A:D(A)\subset X\to X
is given, for each
\begin{pmatrix}g\\
h\end{pmatrix}\in L_{\rm loc}^{p}\left([0,\infty);X\right)
, by

\left(S_{A}\diamond\begin{pmatrix}g\\
h\end{pmatrix}\right)(t)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
g(t-\cdot)\chi_{(0,t)}(\cdot)\end{pmatrix}+\int_{0}^{t}T_{A_{0}}(t-s)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
h(s)\end{pmatrix}{\rm d}s,

wherein we have used
\chi
to denote the characteristic function of a set.

The proof of this lemma can be found in
[
25
,
28
]
.

We are now concerned with the linear operator
B^{\prime}:D(B^{\prime})\subset Y\to Y
that enjoys the following properties.

Lemma 4.3

Let
q>1
be given. Then, the linear operator
B^{\prime}:D(B^{\prime})\subset Y\to Y
satisfies Assumption
2.10
with
p^{*}=\frac{2q}{1+q}
. One furthermore has
(0,\infty)\subset\rho\left(B^{\prime}\right)=\rho\left(B_{0}^{\prime}\right)
.

The proof of this lemma follows from the elliptic estimates derived by Agranovich et al.
[
1
]
.

Using this property we are now able to discuss some properties satisfied by the linear operator
B:D(B)\subset X\to X
.

Lemma 4.4

Let
q>1
be given.
Then the linear operator
B:D(B)\subset X\to X
satisfies Assumption
2.10
with
p^{*}=\frac{2q}{1+q}
. Moreover one has

(0,\infty)\subset\rho(B)=\rho(B^{\prime})=\rho(B^{\prime}_{0}),

and for all
\lambda\in\rho(B)
,

(\lambda-B)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}y\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}(\lambda-B_{0}^{\prime})^{-1}(y)\\
a\mapsto(\lambda-B^{\prime})^{-1}\psi(a,\cdot)\end{pmatrix}.

The proof of this lemma directly follows from the properties of the linear operator
B^{\prime}
stated in Lemma
4.3
above.

Using the above properties, we are able to prove Theorem
4.1
. As already mentioned above, its proof is a direct application of Theorem
2.12
.

Proof.
First note that due to Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.4
, to apply Theorem
2.12
, it is sufficient to check that
A
and
B
are resolvent commuting and that
{\rm R}\left(\lambda-\left(A+B\right)\right)
is dense in
X
for all
\lambda
large enough.

Resolvent commutativity:
This property follows from the resolvent formula described in Lemma
4.2
and
4.4
.
Indeed, let
\lambda\in\rho(A)
and
\mu\in\rho(B)
be given. Then, for each
(y,\psi)^{T}\in X
one has

\begin{cases}(\mu-B)^{-1}(\lambda-A)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}y\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}=(\mu-B)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
e^{-\lambda\cdot}y+\int_{0}^{\cdot}e^{-\lambda(\cdot-s)}\psi(s){\rm d}s\end{pmatrix},\quad\forall\begin{pmatrix}y\\
\psi\end{pmatrix},\\
\begin{pmatrix}0\\
e^{-\lambda\cdot}(\mu-B_{0}^{\prime})^{-1}y+\int_{0}^{\cdot}e^{-\lambda(\cdot-s)}(\mu-B^{\prime})^{-1}\psi(s){\rm d}s\end{pmatrix},\quad\forall\begin{pmatrix}y\\
\psi\end{pmatrix},\end{cases}

while

\begin{cases}(\lambda-A)^{-1}(\mu-B)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}y\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}=(\lambda-A)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}(\mu-B_{0}^{\prime})^{-1}(y)\\
a\mapsto(\mu-B^{\prime})^{-1}\psi(a,\cdot)\end{pmatrix},\\
=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
e^{-\lambda\cdot}(\mu-B_{0}^{\prime})^{-1}y+\int_{0}^{\cdot}e^{-\lambda(\cdot-s)}(\mu-B^{\prime})^{-1}\psi(s){\rm d}s\end{pmatrix}.\end{cases}

Hence
A
and
B
are resolvent commuting.

Density of the range:
To prove that
{\rm Ran\,(\lambda-A-B)}
is dense for all
\lambda
large enough, let us notice that one has

\begin{split}&\overline{D(A)}=\left\{0_{\overline{D(B^{\prime})}}\right\}\times L^{p}\left(0,\infty;Y\right),\\
&\overline{D(B)}=\overline{D(B^{\prime})}\times L^{p}\left(0,\infty;\overline{D(B^{\prime})}\right),\end{split}

Hence
\overline{D(A)}+\overline{D(B)}=X
. Hence Property (
2.12
) in Theorem
2.12
applies and ensures that the range of
\lambda-A-B
is dense in
X
for all
\lambda
large enough. Hence Condition (
2.13
) in Theorem
2.12
is satisfied.

As a conclusion, since
p^{*}=\frac{2q}{1+q}
, Condition (
4.25
) ensures that Assumption
2.11
is satisfied. Applying Theorem
2.12
with the linear operators
A
and
B
completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Now let us come back to (
4.23
). Let
p>1
and
q>1
be given satisfying (
4.25
). Let
r>1
such that

1+\frac{1}{r}<\frac{1+q}{2q}+\frac{1}{p}.

Now let
T>0
be given and fixed. Consider
u_{0}=u_{0}(a,x)\in L^{p}\left(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega)\right)
,
f=f(t,a,x)\in L^{r}\left(0,T;L^{p}\left(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega)\right)\right)
,
g=g(t,x)\in L^{r}\left(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega)\right)
and
h=h(t,a,x)\in L^{r}\left(0,T;L^{p}\left(0,\infty;W^{1-\frac{1}{q},q}(\partial\Omega)\right)\right)
.
Next set
F\in L^{r}(0,T;X)
the function defined by

F(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
g(t,\cdot)\end{pmatrix}\\
a\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}h(t,a,\cdot)\\
f(t,a,\cdot)\end{pmatrix}\end{pmatrix}\in L^{r}\Bigl(0,T;\bigl(\overline{D(B^{\prime})}\times L^{p}(0,\infty;Y)\bigr)\Bigr).

Then problem (
4.23
) formally rewrites as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{{\rm d}v(t)}{{\rm d}t}=Lv(t)+F(t),\quad t\in(0,T),\\
&v(0)=v_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

wherein we have identified
v(t)\in\overline{D(L)}=\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}
together with the map

t\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0_{\overline{D(B^{\prime})}}\\
a\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0_{W^{1-\frac{1}{q},q}\left(\partial\Omega\right)}\\
u(t,a,\cdot)\end{pmatrix}\end{pmatrix}\in\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}\subset X,

while
v(0)=v_{0}
is defined similarly with
u(t,a,\cdot)
replaced by
u_{0}(a,\cdot)
.

According to Theorem
4.1
,
L
is closable, so that we consider the closure equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{{\rm d}v(t)}{{\rm d}t}=\overline{L}v(t)+F(t),\quad t\in(0,T),\\
&v(0)=v_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.26)

Herein
\overline{L}
denotes the closure of
L
. Next using the properties of the linear operator
\overline{L}
, the above equation has a unique mild solution
u\in C\left([0,T];\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}\right)
given by

v(t)=T_{\overline{L}_{0}}(t)v_{0}+\left(S_{\overline{L}}\diamond F\right)(t),\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

wherein
\left\{T_{\overline{L}_{0}}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}\left(\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}\right)
denotes the strongly continuous semigroup generated by
\overline{L}_{0}
, the part of
\overline{L}
in
\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)}
while
\left\{S_{\overline{L}}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(X;\overline{D(A)\cap D(B)})
denotes the integrated semigroup on
X
generated by
\overline{L}
.

Now using the formula for
T_{\overline{L}_{0}}
and
S_{\overline{L}}
expressed in our general result Theorem
2.12
in term of
T_{A_{0}}
and
T_{B_{0}}
, that respectively denote the strongly continuous semigroups generated by
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
, the parts of
A
and
B
in
\overline{D(A)}
and
\overline{D(B)}
; and
S_{A}
,
S_{B}
the integrated semigroups generated by
A
and
B
respectively, one may obtain the formula below for the mild solution
u=u(t,a,x)
of (
4.23
).

\begin{split}v(t)=&T_{\overline{L}_{0}}(t)v_{0}+\left(S_{\overline{L}}\diamond F\right)(t)\\
=&T_{A_{0}}(t)T_{B_{0}}(t)v_{0}+\left(S_{A}\diamond\left(T_{B}(t-\cdot)F(\cdot)\right)\right)(t).\end{split}

Recalling that
\left\{S_{B^{\prime}}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(Y)
denotes the integrated semigroup generated by
B^{\prime}
and
\left\{T_{B_{0}^{\prime}}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}\left(\overline{D(B^{\prime})}\right)
is the strongly continuous semigroup generated by
B_{0}^{\prime}
, the part of
B^{\prime}
in
\overline{D(B^{\prime})}
then one obtains the following explicit formula for the function
u=u(t,a,x)\in C\left([0,T];L^{p}\left(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega)\right)\right)
, the mild solution of (
4.23
).

Lemma 4.5
(Mild solution)

The mild solution of the abstract Cauchy problem (
4.26
) (which corresponds to (
4.23
)) is given – in
Y
– for
t\in[0,T]
and almost every
a>0
by

\begin{pmatrix}0_{W^{1-\frac{1}{q},q}(\partial\Omega)}\\
u(t,a,\cdot)\end{pmatrix}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}T_{B_{0}^{\prime}}(t)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
u_{0}(a-t,\cdot)\end{pmatrix}+\left(S_{B^{\prime}}\diamond\begin{pmatrix}h(\cdot,\cdot+a-t)\\
f(\cdot,\cdot+a-t)\end{pmatrix}\right)(t),&\text{if }t<a,\\
T_{B_{0}^{\prime}}(a)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
g(t-a,\cdot)\end{pmatrix}\,\,+\left(S_{B^{\prime}}\diamond\begin{pmatrix}h(\cdot,\cdot+t-a)\\
f(\cdot,\cdot+t-a)\end{pmatrix}\right)(a),&\text{if }t>a.\end{array}\right.

The above formula for the weak solution of the abstract Cauchy problem (
4.26
) corresponds to the formal expression of the solution of (
4.23
) that can be obtained using integration along the characteristics and also to the decomposition (
1.2
)-(
1.3
)-(
1.4
) proposed in the introduction.

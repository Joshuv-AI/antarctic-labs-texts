On spaces of minimal higher topological complexity.
Yuli B. Rudyak

Abstract
. Let TC n ⁡ ( X ) \operatorname{TC}_{n}(X) denote the n n -th topological complexity of a topological space X. It is known that TC n ⁡ ( X ) \operatorname{TC}_{n}(X) does not exceed n − 1 n-1 for non-contractible X X , and so it makes sense to describe spaces X X with TC n ⁡ ( X ) = n − 1 \operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)=n-1 . Grant–Lupton–Oprea proved the following: If X is a nilpotent space with TC n ⁡ ( X ) = n − 1 \operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)=n-1 then X X is homotopy equivalent to an odd-dimensional sphere. Here we made an attempt to get rid of nilpotency condition and prove the following: If TC n ⁡ ( X ) = n − 1 \operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)=n-1 then either X X is homotopy equivalent to a sphere of odd dimension or is a homology circle with the infinite cyclic fundamental group.

1.
Introduction

The
n
th topological complexity
\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)
of a topological space
X
is the sectional category of a fibrational substitute of the diagonal map
\Delta:X\to X^{n}
. This concept appeared in
[
R2010
]
, see
[
BGRT2014
]
or
[
GLO2013
, Section 2]
for greater details. Note that the inequality
\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)\geq n-1
holds for all (non-contractible)
X
and
n
, and so it seems reasonable to describe the spaces
X
with
\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)=n-1
. It is easy to see
\operatorname{TC}_{n}(S^{2k+1})=n-1
for
n\geq 2
and
k\geq 0
,
[
BGRT2014
]
. Thus it is quite natural to conjecture that the converse is also true: If
\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)=n-1
then
X
is homotopy equivalent to an odd-dimensional sphere. Currently we have two theorems below.

1.1 Theorem
.

[
GLO2013
, Theorem 3.4]

Let X be a connected CW space of finite type. If
X
is simply connected then
X
is homotopy equivalent to some sphere
S^{2r+1}
of odd dimension,
r\geq 1
. If
X
is not simply connected then
X
is either acyclic space or homology sphere. Additionally, if
X
is a nilpotent space or a co-H-space then
X
is a homotopy circle.

Note that the proof in
[
GLO2013
, Lemma 3.3]
has a small error that is corrected in
[
A2019
]
. However, the statement of Theorem
1.1
is correct.

We have the following slight improvement of Theorem
1.1
.

1.2 Theorem

(Theorem
5.6
)
.

Let X be a connected CW space of finite type.
If
\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)=n-1,n\geq 2
, then
X
is either homotopy equivalent to some sphere
S^{2r+1}
of odd dimension,
r\geq 0
or a homology circle with
\pi_{1}(X)={\mathbb{Z}}
.

We emphasize again: It is an open question whether the space
X
with
\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)=n-1,n\geq 3
and
\pi_{1}(X)={\mathbb{Z}}
is a homotopy circle.

We note the following known fact.

1.3 Proposition
.

If the CW space
X
is not contractible then
\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)\geq n-1
.

Proof.

We know that
\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)\geq\operatorname{cat}(X^{n-1})
,
[
BGRT2014
, §3]
. Furthermore, if
X
is not acyclic then cup-length of
X
is at least 1, and so cup-length of
X^{n-1}
is at least
n-1
, and hence
\operatorname{cat}(X^{n-1})\geq n-1
by
[
CLOT2003
, Proposition 1.5]
.
If
X
is acyclic but non-contractible then
\pi_{1}(X)
contains a non-trivial cyclic subgroup
G
. Take a covering map
\widetilde{X}
of
X
with
\pi_{1}(\widetilde{X})=G
. Now,

\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)\geq\operatorname{cat}(X^{n-1})\geq\operatorname{cat}(\widetilde{X^{n-1}})=\operatorname{cat}((\widetilde{X})^{n-1})\geq n-1

because
\operatorname{cat}(Y)\geq\operatorname{cat}(\widetilde{Y})
for all
Y
and all its coverings. To prove this known fact, note the following: If
p:\widetilde{Y}\to Y
is a covering map and a subspace
U\subset Y
is contractible in
Y
then
p^{-1}(U)
is contractible in
\widetilde{Y}
, because of the covering homotopy property.
∎

All spaces are assumed to be connected CW spaces of finite type.

We use the sign
\simeq
for isomorphism of groups and homotopy equivalences of spaces.

2.
Zero Divisors Cup Length

2.1 Definition
.

Fix a natural number
n\geq 2
. Let
X
be a connected CW space and
d=d_{n}:X\to X^{n}
the diagonal
map,
d(x)=(x,\cdots,x)
. The
n
-fold zero divisor ideal for
X
is the kernel of the homomorphism

d^{*}:H^{*}(X^{n})\to H^{*}(X)

induced by the diagonal.
A non-zero element
x\in X^{n}
is called a
n
-fold zero divisor element
if
d^{*}(x)=0
.
A
n
-fold zero divisor cup length
of
X
denoted by

\operatorname{zcl}_{n}(X)
is a maximal number
k
such that there are classes

u_{i}\in H^{*}(X^{n};A_{i}),i=1,\ldots,k,\,\dim u_{i}>0

where the
A_{i}
’s are the local coefficient systems, with the following property:

\displaystyle d^{*}u_{i}

\displaystyle=0\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,k,\text{ and }

\displaystyle 0\neq

\displaystyle u_{1}\smile\cdots\smile u_{k}\in H^{*}(X^{n};A_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes A_{k}).

2.2 Theorem
.

\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)\geq\operatorname{zcl}_{n}(X)
for all local coefficient system.

Proof.

This is a special case of
[
Sva1962
, Theorem 4]
.
∎

3.
Berstein–Schwarz Class

Consider a pointed connected
CW
space
(X,x_{0})
. Put
\pi=\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})
,
{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi]
the group ring of
\pi
, and

I(\pi)
the augmentation ideal of
{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi]
, i.e. the kernel of the augmentation map
\varepsilon:{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi]\to{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The cohomological dimension of the group
\pi
is denoted by
\operatorname{cd}(\pi)
.

Let
p:\widetilde{X}\to X
be the universal covering map for
X
and
\widetilde{X}_{0}=p^{-1}(x_{0})
. The exactness of the sequence

0\to H_{1}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{X}_{0})\to H_{0}(\widetilde{X}_{0})\to H_{0}(\widetilde{X})

yields an isomorphism
H_{1}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{X}_{0})\cong I(\pi)
. So, we get
isomorphisms

H^{1}(X,x_{0};I(\pi))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi]}(H_{1}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{X}_{0}),I(\pi))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi]}(I(\pi),I(\pi)),

see
[
CLOT2003
, Text before Theorem 2.51]
or
[
Ber1976
, (3.7)]
.

We denote by
\overline{\mathfrak{b}}\in H^{1}(X,x_{0};I(\pi))
the element that
corresponds to the identity
1_{I(\pi)}
in the right-hand side of the last isomorphism. (Here we use local coefficient system for the group
I(\pi)
.)
Finally, we set

(3.1)

\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}_{X}=j^{*}\overline{\mathfrak{b}}\in H^{1}(X;I(\pi))

where
j:X=(X,\emptyset)\to(X,x_{0})
is the inclusion of pairs. We call
\mathfrak{b}_{X}
the
Berstein-Schwarz class
of
X
.

3.1 Remark
.

To justify the name ”Berstein–Schwarz class”, note that the class appeared in
[
Sva1962
, p.99]
(implicitly) and
[
Ber1976
, (3.7)]
.

Let
B\pi
denote the classifying space for
\pi
and put
\mathfrak{b}_{\pi}:=\mathfrak{b}_{B\pi}
. Consider its cup power

(3.2)

\mathfrak{b}_{\pi}^{m}=\mathfrak{b}_{\pi}\smile\cdots\smile\mathfrak{b}_{\pi}\in H^{m}(B{\pi};I(\pi)^{\otimes m})

where
I(\pi)^{\otimes m}=I(\pi){\otimes}\cdots\otimes I(\pi)
(
m
factors) and
\otimes
denotes the tensor product over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

3.2 Theorem
.

If
\operatorname{cd}(\pi)=m\leq\infty
then
\mathfrak{b}^{n}_{\pi}\neq 0
for all
n\leq m
.

Proof.

See e.g.
[
DR2009
, Theorem 3.4]
. Cf.
[
Sva1962
, Prop. 34]
.
∎

4.
Künneth Theorem in the Present of Local Coefficients

Let me cite the following fact,
[
G2006
, Theorem 1.7]
.

4.1 Theorem
.

Let
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
be path connected CW complexes of finite type and
K_{3}=K_{1}\times K_{2}
. Let
G_{i}
be left modules over
\pi_{1}(K_{i}),i=1,2
where

G_{3}=G_{1}\otimes G_{2}
and the action of
\pi_{1}(K_{3})
on
G_{3}
is the ”product action”. If
G_{1}
or
G_{2}

is a free abelian group, then there exists a natural exact sequence

(4.1)

\begin{CD}\begin{aligned} 0&@>{}>{}>H^{*}(K_{1};G_{1})\otimes H^{*}(K_{2};G_{2})@>{\mu}>{}>H^{*}(K_{3};G_{3})\\
&\ @>{}>{}>\operatorname{Tor}[H^{*}(K_{1};G_{1}),H^{*}(K_{2};G_{2})]@>{}>{}>0\end{aligned}\end{CD}

where all cohomology is with local coefficients and the maps are of degree 0 and 1.

4.2 Corollary
.

Let
X
be a path connected CW space of finite type,
\pi=\pi_{1}(X)
, and
I(\pi)
the augmentation ideal of
\pi
. Then we have a monomorphism

\mu\colon(H^{*}(X;I(\pi))^{\otimes n}\to H^{*}(X^{n};(I(\pi)^{\otimes n}).

Proof.

Note that
I(\pi)
is a free abelian group. Now, the corollary follows from Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Note also the following fact. Given two spaces
X,Y
with
\pi_{1}(X)=\pi=\pi_{1}(Y)
, consider the projections
p:X\times Y\to X
and
q:X\times Y\to Y
, and
x\in H^{*}(X;A
and
y\in H^{*}(Y;B)
.

4.3 Proposition
.

\mu(x\otimes y)=p^{*}(x)\smile q^{*}(y)\in H^{*}(X\times Y;A\otimes B)
.

Proof.

Use
[
D1972
, VII.7.6 and VII.8.15]
.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem 1.2

Given a connected CW space
X
of finite type and a particular natural
n
such that
\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)=n-1
. The goal is to prove that
X
is either homotopy equivalent to some sphere
S^{2r+1}
of odd dimension,
r\geq 0
or a homology circle with
\pi_{1}(X)={\mathbb{Z}}
. Since the simply connected case is treated in
[
GLO2013
]
we concentrate the exposition for non-simply connected case. As before, we put
\pi=\pi_{1}(X)
and denote by
I(\pi)
the augmentation for
\pi
.

Recall the Berstein-Schwarz class
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}_{X}\in H^{1}(X;I(\pi))
in (
3.1
).

Let
p_{i}:X^{n}\to X
be the projection on
i
th factor. Consider the induced map

p^{*}_{i}:H^{*}(X;-)\to H^{*}(X^{n};-).

Define

\mathfrak{b}_{i}:=p_{i}^{*}(\mathfrak{b})\in H^{*}(X^{n};I(\pi)).

Similarly to (
3.2
), we put
\mathfrak{b}^{2}=\mathfrak{b}\smile\mathfrak{b}
,
\mathfrak{b}_{i}^{2}=\mathfrak{b}_{i}\smile\mathfrak{b}_{i}
, etc.

5.1 Lemma
.

If
\mathfrak{b}^{2}\neq 0
in
H^{2}(X;I(\pi)^{\otimes 2})
then the cup product

\mathfrak{b}_{1}\smile\mathfrak{b}_{2}\smile\cdots\smile\mathfrak{b}_{n-2}\smile(\mathfrak{b}_{n-1})^{2}\in H^{n}(X^{n};I(p)^{\otimes n})

is non-zero.

Proof.

By the definition of tensor product,

0\neq\mathfrak{b}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{b}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{b}_{n-2}\otimes\mathfrak{b}_{n-1}\otimes\mathfrak{b}_{n-1}\in(H^{1}(X;I(p))^{\otimes n}.

Hence, because of monomorphicity of
\mu
and by Proposition
4.3
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\neq\mu(\mathfrak{b}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{b}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{b}_{n-2}\otimes\mathfrak{b}_{n-1}\otimes\mathfrak{b}_{n-1}

\displaystyle=p_{1}^{*}(\mathfrak{b})\smile\cdots\smile p_{i}^{*}(\mathfrak{b})\smile\cdots\smile p_{n-2}^{*}(\mathfrak{b})\smile p_{n-1}^{*}(\mathfrak{b})\smile p_{n-1}^{*}(\mathfrak{b})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{b}_{1}\smile\cdots\smile\mathfrak{b}_{i}\smile\cdots\smile\mathfrak{b}_{n-2}\smile\mathfrak{b}_{n-1}\smile\mathfrak{b}_{n-1}.

∎

Take a point
*\in X
and define

\varphi:X^{n}\to X^{n},\,(*,x_{n-1},\ldots,x_{1})\mapsto(x_{n},\ldots,x_{1}).

Consider the induced homomorphism

\varphi^{*}:H^{*}(X^{n};I(\pi)^{\otimes n})\to H^{*}(X^{n};I(\pi)^{\otimes n}).

Put
\mathfrak{a}_{i}=\mathfrak{b}_{i}-\mathfrak{b}_{n}
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
. Note that all
\mathfrak{b}_{i}
’s belong to the same group
H^{*}(X^{n};I(\pi))
, and so the difference
\mathfrak{b}_{i}-\mathfrak{b}_{n}
is well-defined.

5.2 Lemma
.

\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{b}_{n})=0
and
\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{a}_{i})=\mathfrak{b}_{i}
for
i<n
.

Proof.

Prove that
\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{b}_{n})=0
. Indeed,
p_{n}\varphi
maps
X^{n}
to the point, and so
\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{b}_{n})=\varphi^{*}p_{n}^{*}(\mathfrak{b})=0
.

Prove that
\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{b}_{i})=\mathfrak{b}_{i}
for
i<n
. Indeed,
p_{i}\varphi=p_{i}
for
i<n
, and so

\mathfrak{b}_{i}=p_{i}^{*}(\mathfrak{b})=\varphi^{*}p_{i}^{*}(\mathfrak{b})=\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{b}_{i}).

Finally,
\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{a}_{i})=\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{b}_{i}-\mathfrak{b}_{n})=\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{b}_{i})=\mathfrak{b}_{i}
because
\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{b}_{n})=0
.
∎

5.3 Lemma
.

If
\mathfrak{b}^{2}\neq 0
in
H^{2}(X;I(\pi)^{\otimes 2})
then

(5.1)

\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathfrak{a}_{i}\right)\smile\mathfrak{a}_{n-1}\neq 0

in
H^{n}(X^{n};I(\pi)^{\otimes n})
.

Here
\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}v_{i}
denotes
v_{1}\smile\cdots\smile v_{n-1}
where
v_{i}\in H^{n}(X^{n};I(\pi))
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
we have

\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathfrak{b}_{i}\right)\smile\mathfrak{b}_{n-1}=\mathfrak{b}_{1}\smile\mathfrak{b}_{2}\smile\cdots\smile\mathfrak{b}_{n-2}\smile(\mathfrak{b}_{n-1})^{\smile 2}\neq 0

in
H^{n}(X^{n};I(p)^{\otimes n})
.

By Lemma
5.2
,
\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{b}_{n})=0
and
\varphi^{*}(\mathfrak{a}_{i})=\mathfrak{b}_{i}
for
i<n
. Hence

\varphi^{*}\left(\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathfrak{a}_{i}\right)\smile\mathfrak{a}_{n-1}\right)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathfrak{b}_{i}\right)\smile\mathfrak{b}_{n-1}\neq 0

by Lemma
5.1
. Thus

\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathfrak{a}_{i}\right)\smile\mathfrak{a}_{n-1}\neq 0.

∎

5.4 Corollary
.

If
\mathfrak{b}^{2}\neq 0
then
\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)\geq n
.

Proof.

Let
d:X\to X^{n}
be the diagonal. Clearly
d^{*}(\mathfrak{b}_{i})=\mathfrak{b}
for all
i
, and so
d^{*}(\mathfrak{a})=0
for all
i
. The non-zero product

\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathfrak{a}_{i}\right)\smile\mathfrak{a}_{n-1}

in Lemma
5.3
contains
n
non-zero factors each of which is annihilated by the diagonal
d^{*}
. Hence
\operatorname{zcl}_{n}(X)\geq n
. Now the result follows from Theorem
2.2
.
∎

5.5 Proposition
.

If
\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)=n-1
then
\operatorname{cd}(\pi)\leq 1
.

Proof.

Because of Corollary
5.4
we have the following implication: If
\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)=n-1
then
\mathfrak{b}^{2}=0
in
H^{2}(X;I(\pi)^{\otimes 2})
. We can assume that the classifying space
B\pi
can be obtained from
X
by adding cells of dimensional
\geq 3
. Hence,

H^{2}(B\pi;I(\pi)^{\otimes 2})=0
, and so
\mathfrak{b}_{\pi}^{2}=0
, and the result follows from Theorem
3.2
.
∎

5.6 Theorem
.

If
\operatorname{TC}_{n}(X)=n-1
then either
X
is homotopy equivalent to an odd-dimensional sphere
S^{2n+1},n\geq 1
or an integral homology circle with
\pi_{1}(X)={\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.5
we know that either
\operatorname{cd}(\pi)=0
or
\operatorname{cd}(\pi)=1
. If
\operatorname{cd}(\pi)=0
then
\pi
is trivial group, and so
X
is simply connected. In this case
X
is homotopy equivalent to an odd-dimensional sphere
S^{2n+1},n\geq 1
by
[
GLO2013
, Theorem 1.3]
.

If
\operatorname{cd}(\pi)=1
then
\pi
is a nontrivial free group by
[
Sta1968
,
Swan1969
]
. Because of
[
GLO2013
, Theorem 3.4]
we know that is either
X
is acyclic, or
X
is an integral homology sphere. First,
H_{1}(X)
is a free nontrivial abelian group and hence
X
cannot be acyclic. Furthermore, since
H_{1}(X)
is a free abelian group and
X
is an integral homology sphere, we conclude that
X
is an integral homology circle. Finally,
\pi=\pi_{1}(X)={\mathbb{Z}}
since
\pi
is a free group.
∎

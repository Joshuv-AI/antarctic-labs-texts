Martin D. Buhmann, Feng Dai

Abstract
. We consider quasi-interpolation with a main application in radial basis function approximations and compression in this article. Constructing and using these quasi-interpolants, we consider wavelet and compression-type approximations from their linear spaces and provide convergence estimates. The results include an error estimate for nonlinear approximation by quasi-interpolation, results about compression in the space of continuous functions and a pointwise convergence estimate for approximands of low smoothness.

Introduction

We wish to study multivariate approximation schemes in this artice
which are useful approximation methods called quasi-interpolation methods. A principal
useful context – but not the only one – we have in mind is the related to
the so-called
radial basis functions and the linear spaces spanned by their
translates because they in particular provide excellent means for multivariate approximations in

d
-dimensional spaces. They are known in a variety of types for at
least thirty years
[
17
]
. Among their attractive,
interesting properties are
(i)
their approximation orders
[
6
,
28
]

and
(ii)
their ability to adapt to many different applications
in several dimensions. Thus
they are similar to other successful approximation methods
such as multivariate splines or finite elements (both as the
aforementoned piecewise polynomials) in low dimensional ambient spaces

[
4
]
,
[
3
]
. The most often used forms of
approximation are either interpolation at the same points by which
they are translated
[
6
,
2
]
, or quasi-interpolation (as detailed below), or
wavelets
[
14
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
11
]
.

All the different approaches to multivariate approximation
have very positive aspects; in this article we
choose among them the mentioned quasi-interpolation
[
8
]
. To
this end, let
A
be a set of quasi-uniformly distributed points in

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, let a function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a at a minimum continuous
given and to be approximated (the “approximand”) based on knowing
its values on
A
, and let
\psi_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in A
, be decaying
continuous functions. The we define the quasi-interpolant in the
simplest case as

Qf(x)=\sum_{\alpha\in A}f(\alpha)\psi_{\alpha}(x),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

requiring that the
\psi_{\alpha}
decay sufficiently fast for large
argument, so that the
above sum is well-defined, that is

|\psi_{\alpha}(x)|=O((1+\|x-\alpha|)^{-d-m_{2}})

for a positive
m_{2}
and agrees with
f
in case

is a
polynomial of some maximum degree

to be specified below.

We wish to
find some pointwise convergence
results of such approximations, and we will study compression using them too.
Our theorems include an error estimate for nonlinear
approximation by quasi-interpolation using radial basis functions and otherwise,
positive and negative results about compression in the space of
continuous functions using such approximations, and a pointwise
convergence estimate for approximands of low smoothness and quasi-interpolation.

First, still keeping in mind the key context in which we wish to
create and use quasi-interpolation, we mention a few advantages of the radial basis function
approach: first they are available in any dimension of
the ambient space, so that it is now possible – in contrast to many of the polynomial
spline approaches – to approximate target functions in arbitrary
dimensions. This is needed often in practical scientific applications.
From time to time
they are actually the only feasible approach to function
approximation.

In this paper, we have some pointwise convergence results for very general sets of
approximands and compression methods. After repeating some preliminary
results partly from
[
9
]
, we analyse approximants with quasi-interpolation and find
that both for compression and for deriving the convergence properties of the approximants and the
spaces where they are from, quasi-interpolation is a suitable approach. Let us initially describe the form of
the approximants more precisely, including some examples for radial basis functions.

Since they will be our main application, let us briefly summarise that
radial basis function approximants typically have the form of sums
multiples of translates

\varphi(\|\cdot-\alpha\|),

where the norm is Euclidean,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
are
from a fixed, discrete set
A

[

\phi(r)=1-(r^{\beta}/(1+r^{\beta}))^{\gamma}

\beta

\varphi(r)=\exp(-c^{2}r^{2})

\varphi(r)=\exp(-cr)

parameter

There are many ways to form decaying functions for quasi-interpolation
as (finite) linear

[

-norms such as presented in the third

[

about errors of interpolation by

We use a wavelet approach

We begin our article by summarising suitable conditions on the aforementioned

The idea of compression, i.e. approximations employed where the sizes

2.
Quasi-interpolation: General assumptions and results

In this section, we summarise results from
[
10
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
8
]

on quasi-interpolation and using the central example of radial basis functions which will play crucial roles in our discussions in the later sections of this article.
We will also at this stage set the general definitions and assumptions on the radial
basis functions
\varphi
in use which enable us to go from plain shifts of
radial basis functions to quasi-interpolants. Indeed, we no longer
require the functions to be radially symmetric as in the introduction
but deal with general,
d
-variate, real-valued continuous functions
\varphi
.

We start with a brief description of some necessary notation. The
Fourier transform is specified by

\widehat{f}(\xi):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(y)e^{-i\xi\cdot y}\,dy,\ \ \ \xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\ \ f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

thus the inverse Fourier transform will be scaled in this way:

{1\over(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{i\xi\cdot y}\,d\xi,\ \ \ y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\ \ \widehat{f}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

We provide these definitions also because we need to state which
normalisation we take for the transforms.
The convolution of two functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is defined by

f\ast g(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x-y)g(y)\,dy,\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where
f\in\bigcup_{1\leq p\leq\infty}L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

g\in L^{1}
, or vice-versa, or
f
and
g
are both nonnegative.
We denote by
B_{r}(x):=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\|x-y\|\leq r\}
the ball centred at
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with radius
r
.
For
f\in C^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we use the notation
T_{x}^{k}f
to denote the Taylor polynomial of
f
of degree
k
at the
point
x
; that is,

T_{x}^{k}f(y)=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq k}\frac{D^{\gamma}f(x)}{\gamma!}(y-x)^{\gamma},\ \ y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Here, with respect to
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{d}
we use the standard multi-index
notation; in particular,
|\gamma|=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{d}
and
D^{\gamma}=\Bigl(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\Bigr)^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\Bigl(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{d}}\Bigr)^{\gamma_{d}}
.
Furthermore, we denote by
\Pi_{n}^{d}
the set of all algebraic polynomials of degree at most
n
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\mathcal{P}_{j}^{d}
being the homogeneous ones of degree
j
.

The set of centres by which the quasi-interpolating basis functions are translated
is of course relevant in any approach using quasi-interpolation. In our work here, we
allow infinite sets of such centres which need not, however, be placed
on a lattice.
A countable subset
A
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called
quasi-uniformly distributed
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
if there exist positive constants

c_{0}
and
C_{1}
such that
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}=\cup_{\alpha\in A}B_{c_{0}}(\alpha)
, and

\sum_{\alpha\in A}\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{B_{c_{0}}(\alpha)}\leq C_{1}.

We denote by
\mathcal{A}
the collection of all quasi-uniformly distributed subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with the same constants
c_{0}
and
C_{1}
.
For convenience, we call the constants
c_{0}
and
C_{1}
here the geometric constants of the set
A
.
Throughout this paper, the letter
A
will always denote a
quasi-uniformly distributed subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with the same and fixed
geometric constants
c_{0}
and
C_{1}
.
All the general constants
C
in this paper may depend on the
geometric constants
c_{0}
and
C_{1}
but will be independent of the
set
A
itself.

The following lemma, proved in
[
10
, Lemma 3]
, and used also in

[
9
]
, plays an important general role in the definition of
quasi-interpolation:

Lemma 2.1
.

Given a quasi-uniformly distributed
subset
A
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and a positive integer
k
, there exists a sequence of compactly supported functions
\{N_{\alpha}\}_{{\alpha}\in A}\subset C_{c}^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with the properties that each
N_{\alpha}
is supported in the ball
B_{M/2}({\alpha})
for a positive constant
M
;
\sup_{{\alpha}\in A}\|D^{\gamma}N_{{\alpha}}\|_{\infty}\leq C
for all
\gamma\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{d}
with
|\gamma|\leq k
; and

q=\sum_{{\alpha}\in A}q({\alpha})N_{{\alpha}},\ \ \ \forall q\in\Pi_{k}^{d}.

Here the constants
C
and
M
depend only on
k
,
d
.

According to the proof in
[
10
, Lemma 3]
,
each function
N_{\alpha}
in the above lemma may be a compactly supported multivariate spline
depending only on
d
,
k
and the points in the set
A\cap B_{M/2}({\alpha})
. In particular, if
A\cap B_{M/2}({\alpha})={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\cap B_{M/2}({\alpha})
for some
{\alpha}\in A
, then
we write
N_{\alpha}(x)=N(x-{\alpha})
for a compactly supported multivariate spline
N\in C_{c}^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
independent of the set
A
.

Let now
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous function. We require this
\varphi
to be of at most polynomial growth so that it can be viewed as a tempered
distribution or so-called generalised function. Therefore it has a distributional Fourier
transform
\widehat{\varphi}
which we specifically require to be of the form

\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)=(F(\xi)+bG_{0}(\xi)\log\|\xi\|)/G(\xi)

for
\xi
near the origin, say for
\xi\in B_{1}(0)
.
Here
b
is a constant,
G_{0}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
n_{0}\geq 0
. Furthermore

F
and
G
are real-valued functions on the ball
B_{1}(0)

satisfying the following conditions:

(2A)

G
is a homogeneous polynomial of positive even degree
2m_{1}
and
G(\xi)\neq 0
for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
.

(2B)

F\in C^{m_{0}}(B_{1}(0))\cap C^{m_{0}+d+1}(B_{1}(0)\setminus\{0\})
for some nonnegative integer
m_{0}
,
F(0)\neq 0
, and there exists
{\theta}\in(0,1]
such that for all
\gamma\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{d}
with
|{\gamma}|\leq m_{0}+d+1
,

(2.1)

\Bigl|D^{\gamma}\bigl[F(\xi)-\tau_{m_{0}}(\xi)\bigr]\Bigr|\leq C\|\xi\|^{m_{0}+{\theta}-|\gamma|},\ \ \text{as $\|\xi\|\to 0_{+}$},

where
\tau_{m_{0}}
denotes the Taylor polynomial of
F
of degree
m_{0}
about
\xi=0
.

In addition to the conditions (2A) and (2B), we also assume the following condition on derivatives of
\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)
for large
\|\xi\|
:

(2C)

The Fourier transform
\widehat{\varphi}
of
\varphi
is
(m_{0}+d+1)
-times continuously differentiable on the domain
\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\ \|\xi\|>\frac{1}{4}\}
, and

(2.2)

\max_{|{\gamma}|\leq m_{0}+d+1}\int_{\|\xi\|\geq\frac{1}{4}}|D^{\gamma}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)|\,d\xi\leq C<\infty.

As in
[
10
, p. 245]
, we introduce the following definition:

Definition 2.2
.

Let
T_{m_{0}}
denote the Taylor polynomial of the function
1/F
of degree
m_{0}
at the origin, and let
P(x)=T_{m_{0}}(x)G(x)
.
For each
{\alpha},{\beta}\in A
, define

(2.3)

\mu_{{\alpha}{\beta}}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}N_{\alpha}(x)P(iD)N_{\beta}(x)\,dx,

where
\{N_{\alpha}\}_{{\alpha}\in A}
is the sequence of functions given in Lemma
2.1
with
k=2m_{1}+m_{0}+1
.

From the above definition and Lemma
2.1
, it is clear that

\mu_{{\alpha}{\beta}}=0
if
\|{\alpha}-{\beta}\|\geq M
,
\mu_{{\alpha}{\beta}}=\overline{\mu_{{\beta}{\alpha}}}
for all
{\alpha},{\beta}\in A
, where
M
is the positive constant given in Lemma
2.1
with
k=2m_{1}+m_{0}+1
.
More importantly, the sequence
\{\mu_{{\alpha}{\beta}}\}
has the following important property, as was shown in
[
10
, Theorem 7]
:

(2.4)

\sum_{{\beta}\in A}\mu_{{\alpha}{\beta}}q({\beta})=0,\ \ \forall{\alpha}\in A,\ \ \forall q\in\text{ker}\,G(D)\cap\Pi_{2m_{1}+m_{0}+1}^{d}.

Furthermore, in the case when

A={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,

(2.5)

\mu_{{\alpha}{\beta}}=\mu_{{\beta}-{\alpha}}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}N(y-{\beta}+{\alpha})P(iD)N(y)\,dy,\ \ \ {\alpha},{\beta}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},

and
the sequence satisfies (
[
8
,
10
]
)

(2.6)

\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\mu_{j}q(j)=P(iD)q(0),\ \ \ \forall q\in\Pi_{2m_{1}+m_{0}+1}^{d}.

The expression on the left-hand side of (
2.4
) or (
2.6
) is in fact a local difference of the polynomial
q

at the point
{\alpha}\in A
which is closely related to the derivative
P(iD)q({\alpha})
.

A remarkable fact is that the above defined sequence
admits the mentioned quasi-interpolation, central and useful for our application.
More precisely, under the above assumptions on the Fourier transform
\widehat{\varphi}
, the following result
[
9
]
holds:

Theorem A.

Under the above assumptions, the functions
\psi_{\alpha}
,
{\alpha}\in A
, defined by

(2.7)

\psi_{\alpha}(x):=\sum_{{\beta}\in A}\mu_{{\alpha}{\beta}}\varphi(x-{\beta}),\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

have the decaying property

(2.8)

|\psi_{\alpha}(x)|\leq C(1+\|x-{\alpha}\|)^{-d-m_{2}},\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\ \ {\alpha}\in A,

where

(2.9)

m_{2}:=\begin{cases}m_{0}+{\theta},&\ \ \text{if $b=0$};\\
\min\{m_{0}+{\theta},n_{0}\},&\ \ \text{if $b\neq 0$};\end{cases}

and furthermore,
the operator defined by (“quasi-interpolation”)

(2.10)

Qf:=\sum_{{\alpha}\in A}f({\alpha})\psi_{\alpha}

reproduces all polynomials of total degree
at most
\ell_{0}:=\min\{m_{0},2m_{1}-1\}
; that is,
Qp=p
for all
p\in\Pi_{\ell_{0}}^{d}
.

Here and elsewhere in the paper, the letter
C
denotes a constant that may depend on
m_{1},m_{0}
,
n_{0}
, and
d
.

Remark 2.1
.

(i)

We would like to remark that, in fact,
in the case of
b=0
,
Theorem A
with slightly weaker decaying estimates of the functions
\psi_{\alpha}
was proved in
[
10
]
, where (
2.8
) with
m_{0}
in place of
m_{2}
was shown.
Infinite differentiability on

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and slightly stronger pointwise estimates of the derivatives

for large

were required in
[
10
]
, but by looking carefully at the proofs there, the
assumptions can
be slightly reduced as we did above.
Concretely, the aforementioned differentiability assumptions are used
in that paper
[
10
]
to obtain the correct asymptotic behaviour of
the inverse Fourier transform of the remainder term
\hat{r}_{\ell+1+\theta}(\omega)
. For this, no infinite differentiability is
required as indeed the said inverse Fourier transform need not decay
of arbitrary high negative order but only at the same rate as the
rest of the expression.

(ii)

Here an additional term
b\log\|\xi\|
has been added in the Fourier transform of
\varphi
in order to include the “shifted” versions of some important radial basis functions, where a direct application of the general results of
[
10
, Theorem 8]
normally
would not yield the optimal decaying estimate (
2.8
) .
In fact, Theorem A of
[
9
]
for
b\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
A={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and
G(x)=\|x\|^{2m}

was proved in
[

]
.

The combination of localisation of the operator (by the decaying
properties of
\psi_{\alpha}
) and the polynomial reproduction admits
the derivation of approximation orders to sufficiently smooth
approximands. This requires, of course, a scaling of the given centres.

Indeed, given
h>0
, the following quasi-interpolation operator with scaling was introduced and studied in
[
10
]
:

(2.11)

Q_{h}f(x):=\sum_{{\alpha}\in A}f(h{\alpha})\psi_{\alpha}(h^{-1}x),\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

For the rest of the paper, we will set
\ell:=\min\{m_{0}-1,2m_{1}-1\}
. Observe that condition (
2.10
) implies that we have the
desirable polynomial reproduction property

Q_{h}q=q,\ \ \forall q\in\Pi_{\ell}^{d}.

The following estimates were proved in
[
10
]
for the case of
b=0
: If
f\in C^{\ell+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
has bounded partial derivatives of total orders
\ell
and
\ell+1
, then for any
h\in(0,1)
,

(2.12)

\|f-Q_{h}f\|_{\infty}\leq C_{f}h^{\ell+1}|\log h|,

where

C_{f}:=\|\nabla^{\ell}f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\|\nabla^{\ell+1}f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Remark 2.2
.

These estimates are already reformulated slightly to suit the
analysis in this paper below, and in particular, here and in what
follows, the notation
\nabla^{k}f
denotes the vector with
components
D^{\alpha}f
,
|{\alpha}|=k
, for a given positive integer

k
. For convenience, we also set
\nabla^{0}f=f
.

Remark 2.3
.

Due to the slightly faster decay (
2.8
), we can easily prove, whenever
b=0
, that neither the log term in (
2.12
), nor the term
\|\nabla^{\ell}f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
in
C_{f}
is needed.

Using (
2.8
), and a straightforward
calculation, we can deduce the following useful estimates which
shall be employed in the next section:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
A
be a quasi-uniformly distributed subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and let
h\in(0,1)
. Then for
0\leq a<m_{2}
,

(2.13)

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sum_{{\alpha}\in A}|\psi_{\alpha}(x/h)|(\|x-{\alpha}h\|+h)^{a}\leq C_{a}h^{a},

and for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sum_{{\alpha}\in A}|\psi_{\alpha}(x/h)|(\|x-{\alpha}h\|+h)^{m_{2}}(1+\|x-{\alpha}h\|)^{-\varepsilon}\leq C_{\varepsilon}h^{m_{2}}|\log h|.

3.
Pointwise approximation by quasi-interpolation for functions of lower smoothness in
L^{p}

Error estimates where the approximands are rougher, i.e. from larger
function spaces with less smoothness required, than the approximating
(radial) basis functions or quasi-interpolants are essential in practical applications
because we cannot in general guarantee high order smoothness of the
possibly unknown functions which are to be approximated (i.e., they
are only known e.g. at a finite number of points).
On the other hand, however, functions of lower smoothness in
L^{p}
(say, for instance,
f\in W_{p}^{r}
with
r<d/p
) are defined only almost everywhere on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
and pointwise evaluation functionals make no sense for such
functions. Thus, the quasi-interpolation operators
Q_{h}
considered
in the last two sections are no longer applicable to functions of
lower smoothness in
L^{p}
. In this section, we will modify
the definition of
Q_{h}f
so that it is defined for every locally integrable function. It turns out that
such modified operators, although not as convenient for
practical implementation, is easier to handle. In fact, we will
establish stronger pointwise error estimates in this section, which
will play crucial roles in Section 4 when we consider compression in approximation by radial basis functions.

We start with the definition of the modified quasi-interpolation.
First, consider the linear functional
S_{0}:\Pi_{\ell}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by
S_{0}P=P(0)
.
Since the space
\Pi_{\ell}^{d}
is finite dimensional, it follows that

|S_{0}P|\leq\max_{y\in B_{1}(0)}|P(y)|\leq C_{\ell,d}\int_{B_{1}(0)}|P(y)|\,dy,\ \ \forall P\in\Pi_{\ell}^{d}.

By the Hahn-Banach theorem, this linear functional can be extended to
a bounded linear functional on the space
L^{1}(B_{1}(0))
such that
S_{0}P=P(0)
for all
P\in\Pi_{\ell}^{d}
, and

(3.1)

|S_{0}f|\leq C\int_{B_{1}(0)}|f(y)|\,dy,\ \ \forall f\in L^{1}(B_{1}(0)).

For each
{\alpha}\in A
, we define

S_{\alpha}f:=S_{0}\Bigl(f(\cdot+{\alpha})\Bigr),\ \ f\in L^{1}(B_{1}({\alpha})).

Hence,
S_{\alpha}P=P({\alpha})
for all
P\in\Pi_{\ell}^{d}
, and by (
3.1
),

(3.2)

|S_{\alpha}f|\leq C\int_{B_{1}({\alpha})}|f(y)|\,dy,\ \ \forall f\in L^{1}(B_{1}({\alpha})),\ \ \forall{\alpha}\in A.

Now for
h>0
and every locally integrable function
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we define the modified quasi-interpolation
\widetilde{Q}_{h}f
by

(3.3)

\widetilde{Q}_{h}f(x):=\sum_{{\alpha}\in A}f^{h}({\alpha})\psi_{\alpha}(x/h),\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where
f^{h}({\alpha}):=S_{{\alpha}}({\sigma}_{h}f)
and
\sigma_{h}f(\cdot)=f(h\cdot)
.

There are many smoothing techniques to construct the coefficients
f^{h}({\alpha})
in (
3.3
) explicitly via integrals.
Next, we give several examples of these techniques.

Example 3.1
.

Let
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\text{dim}\,\Pi_{\ell}^{d}}
be an orthonormal basis of
\Pi_{\ell}^{d}
with respect to the inner product of
L^{2}(B_{1}(0))
, and let

\Phi_{\ell}(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{\text{dim}\,\Pi_{\ell}^{d}}\phi_{j}(y)\phi_{j}(0)
. Define

S_{0}f:=\int_{B_{1}(0)}f(y)\Phi_{\ell}(y)\,dy
for
f\in L^{1}(B_{1}(0)).

Accordingly, we have

f^{h}({\alpha}):=h^{-d}\int_{B_{h}({\alpha}h)}f(y)\Phi_{\ell}(h^{-1}y-{\alpha})\,dy,\ \ \ h>0,\ \ \ {\alpha}\in A.

Example 3.2
.

Let
N\in C^{2\ell+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be a compactly supported multivariate spline with
\text{supp}\,N\subset B_{c_{1}}(0)
for some
c_{1}>0
such that

\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}p(j)N(x-j)=p(x),\ \ \forall p\in\Pi_{\ell}^{d}.

The Strang-Fix condition on
N
then implies that (see, for instance,
[
10
]
)

p(0)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}p(x)N(x)\,dx,\ \ \forall p\in\Pi_{\ell}^{d}.

Thus, we can define

S_{0}(f):=\int_{B_{1}(0)}f(x)N_{c^{\prime}}(x)\,dx,\ \ \forall f\in L^{1}(B_{1}(0)),

where
c^{\prime}=c_{1}^{-1}
and
N_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)=\varepsilon^{-d}N(\varepsilon^{-1}\cdot)
for
\varepsilon>0
.
Accordingly,

f^{h}({\alpha}):=h^{-d}\int_{B_{h}({\alpha}h)}f(y)N_{c^{\prime}}(h^{-1}y-{\alpha})\,dy,\ \ \ h>0,\ \ \ {\alpha}\in A.

The next example is due to Jia and Lei
[
20
]
, who also obtained the order of
L^{p}
-approximation by the associated quasi-interpolation
with
A={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be such that
\text{supp}\ \eta\subset B_{1}(0)
and
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\eta(x)\,dx=1
.
Define

S_{0}f:=\int_{B_{1}(0)}(f(0)-{\triangle}_{u}^{\ell+1}f(0))\eta(u)\,du,

where
{\triangle}_{u}^{k}
denotes the
k
-th order difference operator with step
u
. Since
{\triangle}_{u}^{\ell+1}P=0
for all
P\in\Pi_{\ell}^{d}
,
S_{0}
clearly satisfies the conditions stated above.
In this case, the
f^{h}
can be expressed explicitly as in
(5.1) in
[
9
]
with
k=\ell+1
: viz.

(3.4)

f^{h}(x):=h^{-d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(f(x)-{\triangle}_{u}^{k}f(x))\eta(u/h)\,du,\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where
{\triangle}_{u}^{k}
denotes the
k
-th order difference operator given by

{\triangle}_{u}^{k}f(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}f(x-ju),\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

The main goal in this section is to prove the following pointwise error estimate:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let

f\in C^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
1\leq r\leq\ell+1
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

(i)

If
r<m_{2}
, then for all
h>0
,

(3.5)

|f(x)-\widetilde{Q}_{h}f(x)|\leq Ch^{r}\bigl(\|\nabla^{r}f\|\ast\Psi_{h}\bigr)(x)

where

\Psi(z):=\|z\|^{-d+1}(1+\|z\|)^{-1-m_{2}+r}

and
\Psi_{h}(\cdot)=h^{-d}\Psi(h^{-1}\cdot)
.

(ii)

If
r=m_{2}
, then for
h\in(0,1)
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle|f(x)-\widetilde{Q}_{h}f(x)|\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{r}\Bigl[(\|\nabla^{r}f\|\ast\Phi^{h})(x)+\|\nabla^{r-1}f(x)\|\Bigr]

where

\Phi^{h}(z):=\|z\|^{-d+1}(\|z\|+h)^{-1}(1+\|z\|)^{-1}.

A straightforward calculation shows that the convolutions on the
right hand sides of both (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) can be
dominated by the Hardy-Littlewood maximal functions of some
derivatives of
f
. Thus, from Theorem
3.4
, we deduce
the following useful corollary, which will play a crucial role in
Section 4 when we consider compressions in radial basis function
approximation. The operator
M
used here is the Hardy–Littlewood
maximal function

Mf(x):=\sup_{\delta>0}{1\over|B_{\delta}(x)|}\int_{B_{\delta}(x)}|f(z)|\,dz.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
1\leq r\leq\ell+1
,
f\in C^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
If
r<m_{2}
, then

(3.7)

\displaystyle\ \ \ |f(x)-\widetilde{Q}_{h}f(x)|\leq Ch^{r}M(\|\nabla^{r}f\|)(x),\ \ \ h>0,

whereas if
r=m_{2}
, then for
h\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\ \ \ |f(x)-\widetilde{Q}_{h}f(x)|\leq Ch^{r}\max\{1,|\log h|\}\Bigl(M(\|\nabla^{r}f\|)(x)+\|\nabla^{r-1}f(x)\|\Bigr).

Corollary
3.5
together with the usual density argument also implies the following rate of pointwise convergence of the modified quasi-interpolation.

Corollary 3.6
.

If
1\leq p<\infty
,
w\in A_{p}
,
f\in W_{p}^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w)
and
1\leq r\leq\ell
, then for almost every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\lim_{h\to 0}h^{-r}(f(x)-\widetilde{Q}_{h}f(x))=0.

Another result that can be deduced directly from Theorem
3.4
via straightforward calculations
is the following.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
w\in A_{p}
,
1\leq r\leq\ell+1
, and
f\in W_{p}^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w)
.
Then for
h\in(0,1)
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|f-\widetilde{Q}_{h}f\|_{p,w}\leq Ch^{r}\|f\|_{W_{p}^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w)},

with
h^{r}|\log h|
in place of
h^{r}
in the case when
r=m_{2}
.

The rest of the section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
3.4
.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
3.4

For simplicity, we will prove the result for the case of
1\leq r<m_{2}
only. The proof below with slight modifications works equally well for
the case of
r=m_{2}
.
By the property of polynomial reproduction, we may write

(3.9)

\displaystyle|f(x)-\widetilde{Q}_{h}f(x)|\leq\sum_{{\alpha}\in A}|S_{\alpha}(\sigma_{h}f)-T_{x}^{r-1}f({\alpha}h)||\psi_{\alpha}(x/h)|.

Since
T_{x}^{r-1}f({\alpha}h)=T_{x/h}^{r-1}(\sigma_{h}f)({\alpha})
and
T_{x/h}^{r-1}(\sigma_{h}f)\in\Pi_{\ell}^{d}
, it follows by (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle|S_{\alpha}(\sigma_{h}f)-T_{x}^{r-1}f({\alpha}h)|

\displaystyle=\Bigl|S_{\alpha}\Bigl[\sigma_{h}f-T_{x/h}^{r-1}(\sigma_{h}f)\Bigr]\Bigr|

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{|B_{h}({\alpha}h)|}\int_{B_{h}({\alpha}h)}|f(y)-T_{x}^{r-1}f(y)|\,dy,

which, using Theorem 3.1 of
[
9
]
with
r=m
, is bounded above by

(3.11)

\displaystyle Ch^{-d}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{h}({\alpha}h)}\|x-y\|^{r-1}\max_{u=x,y}\Bigl[\int_{B_{2\|x-y\|}(x)}\|\nabla^{r}f(z)\|\,\|z-u\|^{1-d}\,dz\Bigr]\,dy.

Since
B_{2\|x-y\|}(x)\subset B_{2(\|x-{\alpha}h\|+h)}(x)
whenever
y\in B_{h}({\alpha}h)
, the term in (
3.11
) is bounded above by a constant multiple of

(h+\|x-{\alpha}h\|)^{r-1}L_{x,{\alpha},h}(x)+h^{-d}\int_{B_{h}({\alpha}h)}\|x-y\|^{r-1}L_{x,{\alpha},h}(y)\,dy,

where

(3.12)

L_{x,{\alpha},h}(u):=\int_{B_{2(\|x-{\alpha}h\|+h)}(x)}\|\nabla^{r}f(z)\|\|z-u\|^{-d+1}\,dz.

Thus,
the term on the right hand side of (
3.9
) is dominated by a constant multiple of
\Sigma_{1}(x)+\Sigma_{2}(x)
, where

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}(x):=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\alpha}\in A}\bigl(h+\|x-{\alpha}h\|\bigr)^{r-1}|\psi_{\alpha}(x/h)|L_{x,{\alpha},h}(x),

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}(x):=

\displaystyle h^{-d}\sum_{{\alpha}\in A}|\psi_{\alpha}(x/h)|\int_{B_{h}({\alpha}h)}\|x-y\|^{r-1}L_{x,{\alpha},h}(y)\,dy.

By the proof of Lemma 4.4 in
[
9
]
with
m=r
, it is easily seen that

(3.13)

\Sigma_{1}(x)\leq Ch^{r}\bigl(\|\nabla^{r}f\|\ast\Psi_{h}\bigr)(x).

To estimate the sum
\Sigma_{2}(x)
, we decompose
A
as
A:=\cup_{j=0}^{\infty}A_{j,h}(x)
, where

A_{0,h}(x):=\{{\alpha}\in A:\ \ \|{\alpha}-h^{-1}x\|\leq 2\}
and
A_{j,h}(x):=\{{\alpha}\in A:\ \ 2^{j}<\|{\alpha}-h^{-1}x\|\leq 2^{j+1}\}
for
j\geq 1
.
Using (
3.12
), for each
{\alpha}\in A_{j,h}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{h}({\alpha}h)}\|x-y\|^{r-1}L_{x,{\alpha},h}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C(2^{j}h)^{r-1}\int_{B_{2^{j+3}h}(x)}\|\nabla^{r}f(z)\|\Bigl[\int_{B_{h}({\alpha}h)}\|z-y\|^{-d+1}\,dy\Bigr]\,dz.

Since

\cup_{{\alpha}\in A_{j,h}}B_{h}({\alpha}h)\subset B_{2^{j+2}h}(x)
and by quasi-uniformity,

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sup_{h\in(0,1)}\sum_{{\alpha}\in A}\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{B_{h}({\alpha}h)}(x)\leq C<\infty,

it follows that

\displaystyle h^{-d}\sum_{{\alpha}\in A_{j,h}(x)}|\psi_{\alpha}(x/h)|\int_{B_{h}({\alpha}h)}\|x-y\|^{r-1}L_{x,{\alpha},h}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-jm_{2}}(2^{j}h)^{r-1-d}\int_{B_{2^{j+3}h}(x)}\|\nabla^{r}f(z)\|\Bigl[\int_{B_{2^{j+2}h}(x)}\|y-z\|^{-d+1}\,dy\Bigr]\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-jm_{2}}(2^{j}h)^{r-d}\int_{B_{2^{j+3}h}(x)}\|\nabla^{r}f(z)\|dz,

where the second step uses the fact that
B_{2^{j+3}h}(x)\subset B_{2^{j+4}h}(z)
whenever
z\in B_{2^{j+3}h}(x)
.
Hence, by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}(x)\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-jm_{2}}(2^{j}h)^{r-d}\int_{B_{2^{j+3}h}(x)}\|\nabla^{r}f(z)\|dz

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{r-d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|\nabla^{r}f(z)\|\Bigl[\sum_{2^{j}\geq c^{\prime}(1+h^{-1}\|x-z\|)}2^{-j(m_{2}+d-r)}\Bigr]\,dz

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{r}(\|\nabla^{r}f\|\ast\Psi_{h})(x).

Thus, combining (
3.13
) with (
3.14
), we obtain the desired estimate, and hence complete the proof.

4.
Compression in the space
C({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

One view of using compression for our approximation purposes is to
limit the number of terms in the expansions with radial basis
functions. Compression with radial basis functions in the very
special way of using Gauß-kernels turns up also in the paper

[
18
]
.

Indeed,
let
\varphi
be a continuous function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and let
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be quasi-uniformly distributed.
For a positive integer
n
, we define

\Sigma_{n,\varphi,h}:=\Bigl\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\varphi(h^{-1}\cdot-{\alpha}_{j}):\ \ {\alpha}_{j}\in A,\ x_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\ \ c_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}\Bigr\},\ \ h\in(0,1).

Our main purpose in this section
is to study the following
n
-term approximation of a given continuous function
f
:

\hat{\sigma}_{n,h}(f):=\inf_{g\in\Sigma_{n,\varphi,h}}\|f-g\|_{\infty}.

We start with a negative result for compression in the space
C({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, which asserts that given any
h>0
,
the quantity
\hat{\sigma}_{n,h}(f)
may not tend
to zero as
n\to\infty
for general
continuous functions
f
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be a sequence of continuous functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with

\lim_{\|x\|\to\infty}|\psi_{j}(x)|=0,\ \ \forall j\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Then for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\sup_{\|f\|_{W_{\infty}^{r}}\leq 1}\inf_{g\in\Sigma_{n}}\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\geq c_{r}>0,

where

\Sigma_{n}:=\Bigl\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\psi_{j}(h_{j}^{-1}\cdot):\ c_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}},\ \ h_{j}>0\Bigr\}.

Proof.

Since for
any
g\in\Sigma_{n}
,

\lim_{\|x\|\to\infty}g(x)=0,

it follows that

\|f-g\|_{\infty}

remains positive for any constant function
f
or infinite sum
f
of shifts
of a suitable compactly supported test-function. ∎

The above proposition shows that in order to study the
n
-term approximation problem in
C({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, one has to assume some decaying condition of the function at infinity. For example, we have a positive result if we assume that
f\in W_{p}^{r}
with
r>\frac{d}{p}
for some
1\leq p<\infty
.
In fact, a greedy algorithm can be constructed in this case. This is the theme of our next theorem.
We refer to the book
[
27
]
for recent development
of greedy approximation.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that
1\leq p<\infty
,
r>\frac{d}{p}
and

f\in W_{p}^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Let
\beta:=\min\{r-\frac{d}{p},\ell+1\}
. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
define

\mathcal{G}_{n}f(x)=\sum_{{\alpha}\in\Lambda}f(h{\alpha})\psi_{\alpha}(h^{-1}x)\ \ \ \text{with $h\sim n^{-\frac{1}{p{\beta}+d}}$},

where
\Lambda\subset A
satisfies that
\#\Lambda=n
, and

\min_{{\alpha}\in\Lambda}|f(h{\alpha})|\geq\max_{{\alpha}\in A\setminus\Lambda}|f(h{\alpha})|
. Then

(4.1)

\|f-\mathcal{G}_{n}f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq Cn^{-\frac{{\beta}}{p{\beta}+d}}\log n\|f\|_{W_{p}^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

The proof of Theorem
4.2
relies on the following straightforward lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Given any
(a_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

\Biggl\|\sum_{{\alpha}\in A}a_{\alpha}\psi_{\alpha}\Biggr\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C\Bigl(\sum_{{\alpha}\in A}|a_{\alpha}|^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}},\ \ 1\leq p\leq\infty,

with the usual change when
p=\infty
.

Proof.

This lemma follows directly from the Hölder
inequality and the first estimate in Proposition 2.3 in

[
9
]
(i.e., (
2.13
)) with
i=0
.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
4.2
.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Using Lemma 6.4 of
[
9
]
, we have

\sum_{{\alpha}\in A}|f(h{\alpha})|^{p}\leq C^{p}h^{-d}\|f\|^{p}_{W_{p}^{r}}.

This, in particular, implies that there are at most
n
elements in the set
A
such that

|f(h{\alpha})|\geq Ch^{-d/p}n^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{W_{p}^{r}}.

Thus, for the set
\Lambda
defined in Theorem
4.2
, we have

|f(h{\alpha})|\leq Ch^{-d/p}n^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{W_{p}^{r}},\ \ \forall{\alpha}\in A\setminus\Lambda.

By Lemma
4.3
, this in turn implies that

|Q_{h}f(x)-\mathcal{G}_{n}f(x)|\leq\sum_{{\alpha}\in A\setminus\Lambda}|f({\alpha}h)||\psi_{\alpha}(x/h)|\leq Ch^{-d/p}n^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{W_{p}^{r}}.

On the other hand, using (5.7) in
[
9
]
,

\|f-Q_{h}f\|_{\infty}\leq Ch^{\beta}|\log h|\|f\|_{W_{p}^{r}}.

Therefore, choosing
h\sim n^{-\frac{1}{{\beta}p+d}}
so that

h^{\beta}\sim n^{-\frac{1}{p}}h^{-d/p},

we have that

\|f-\mathcal{G}_{n}f\|_{\infty}\leq Cn^{-\frac{{\beta}}{{\beta}p+d}}\log n\|f\|_{W_{p}^{r}}.

\Box

In general, we have the following result:

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume that
1\leq r\leq\ell+1
, and
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
has bounded, continuous partial derivatives up to the order
r
. Assume, in addition, that
f
satisfies the decaying condition

(4.2)

\sup_{M\geq 1}M^{a}\Bigl(\int_{\|x\|\geq M}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C_{1}<\infty

for some
a\geq 0
, and
1\leq p<\infty
. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
h\in(0,1)
be such that

h\sim n^{-\frac{1}{d}\frac{ap+d}{d+(a+r)p}},

and define

\Lambda:=\Bigl\{{\alpha}\in A:\ \|{\alpha}\|\leq n^{1/d}\ \text{\rm or}\ \ |S_{\alpha}(\sigma_{h}f)|\geq h^{r}\Bigr\}.

Then
\#\Lambda\leq cn
, and the function

\mathcal{G}_{n}f(x):=\sum_{{\alpha}\in\Lambda}(S_{\alpha}(\sigma_{h}f))\psi_{\alpha}(x/h)

satisfies

(4.3)

\|f-\mathcal{G}_{n}f\|_{\infty}\leq Cn^{-\frac{r}{d}\frac{a+d}{a+d+r}}\Bigl[C_{1}+\|\nabla^{r}f\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla^{\ell}f\|_{\infty}\Bigr].

We note that for compactly supported functions
f
, (
4.2
) is true for any positive number
a
, with the constant
C_{1}
depending on the diameter of the support of
f
, and (
4.3
) then provides the optimal approximation order.

Proof.

Using (
3.1
), we have that

\displaystyle h^{d}\sum_{\|{\alpha}\|\geq n^{1/d}}|S_{\alpha}(\sigma_{h}f)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\|{\alpha}\|\geq n^{1/d}}\int_{B_{h}({\alpha}h)}|f(y)|^{p}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\|y\|\geq n^{1/d}h/2}|f(y)|^{p}dy\leq CC_{1}^{p}(n^{1/d}h)^{-ap}.

This means that there exist at most
cn
points
{\alpha}
in the set
A
such that
\|{\alpha}\|\geq n^{1/d}
and

|S_{\alpha}(\sigma_{h}f)|\geq C^{1/p}C_{1}n^{-\frac{a}{d}-\frac{1}{p}}h^{-a-\frac{d}{p}}.

Since

\#\Bigl\{{\alpha}\in A:\ \|{\alpha}\|\leq n^{1/d}\Bigr\}\leq cn,

it follows that
\#\Lambda\leq c^{\prime}n
, and that

(4.4)

|S_{\alpha}(\sigma_{h}f)|\leq C^{1/p}C_{1}n^{-\frac{a}{d}-\frac{1}{p}}h^{-a-\frac{d}{p}}\leq CC_{1}h^{r},\ \ \forall{\alpha}\in A\setminus\Lambda.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|f-\mathcal{G}_{n}f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-Q_{h}f\|_{\infty}+\|Q_{h}f-\mathcal{G}_{n}f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C_{f}h^{r}|\log h|+C\sup_{{\alpha}\in A\setminus\Lambda}|S_{\alpha}(\sigma_{h}f)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{f}h^{r}|\log h|+CC_{1}h^{r}\leq C_{f}^{\prime}n^{-\frac{r}{d}\frac{a+d}{a+d+r}}.

∎

5.
Compression in
L^{p}
with
1<p<\infty

The main purpose in this section is to study compression with radial basis functions for smooth functions in
L^{p}
.
We will follow closely the argument of DeVore and Ron
[
15
]
. The pointwise error estimates (i.e., Corollary 4.2 in
[
9
]
and Corollary
3.5
) will play crucial roles in our proof.

We begin with studying decompositions of locally integrable functions by wavelets (see the aforementioned book by Daubechies). Later-on we shall use these remarks for studying radial basis functions and quasi-interpolants.

5.1.
Wavelet decomposition and the Triebel-Lizorkin spaces

Most materials in this subsection can be found in
[
23
]
and
[
15
]
.
Let
\mathcal{D}_{j}
denote the set of dyadic cubes of side length
2^{-j}
, and let
\mathcal{D}=\bigcup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathcal{D}_{j}
. Thus, each
I\in\mathcal{D}_{j}
can be written in the form

I=2^{-j}(k+[0,1)^{d})\ \ \text{for some $k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}$}.

Write
\ell(I)=2^{-j}
for each
I\in\mathcal{D}_{j}
.
Let

E:=\{1,2,\ldots,2^{d}-1\},\ \ \mathcal{V}=\mathcal{D}\times E.

For each
v=(I_{v},e_{v})\in\mathcal{V}
with
I_{v}\in\mathcal{D}
and
e_{v}\in E
, we denote

\ell(v):=\ell(I_{v}),\ \ |v|=\ell(v)^{d}=|I_{v}|.

Let
\{w_{v}\}_{v\in\mathcal{V}}
be an orthogonal wavelet basis for
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with the following properties:

(i)

Each wavelet
w_{v}
with
I_{v}:=2^{j}(k+[0,1]^{d})\in\mathcal{D}
, is supported in the cube

\bar{I}_{v}=2^{j}(k+A_{0}[0,1]^{d}),

with
A_{0}
being some fixed absolute constant.

(ii)

The wavelets
w_{v}
are
m_{2}
-times differentiable, and have
m_{2}
vanishing moments for a sufficiently large positive integer
m_{2}
. Moreover,

(5.1)

|D^{\alpha}w_{v}(x)|\leq C\ell(I_{v})^{-|{\alpha}|}\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{\bar{I}_{v}}(x),\ \ |{\alpha}|\leq m_{2},\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

and

\|w_{v}\|_{p}\sim|I_{v}|^{\frac{1}{p}},\ \ \ \forall v\in\mathcal{V},\ \ 1\leq p\leq\infty.

(iii)

Each locally integrable function
f
defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
has a wavelet decomposition

f=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}f_{v}w_{v},\ \ f_{v}:=|I_{v}|^{-1}{\langle}f,w_{v}{\rangle},

and many classical function spaces on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
can be defined or characterized equivalently in terms of the coefficients of these wavelet decompositions. For example, given
s>0
and
0<p,q<\infty
, the Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
F_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
can be equivalently defined to be the space of all functions
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with

|f|_{F_{p,q}^{s}}:=\|\Delta_{s,q}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\infty,

where

\Delta_{s,q}f(x):=\Bigl(\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\ell(v)^{-qs}|f_{v}|^{q}\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{\bar{I}_{v}}(x)\Bigr)^{1/q},

The quasi-norm in
F_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is defined by

\|f\|_{F_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+|f|_{F_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

The definition of the Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
F_{p,q}^{s}
extends naturally to the
q=\infty
case, with
\Delta_{s,\infty}f
defined by

\Delta_{s,\infty}f(x):=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\ell(v)^{-s}|f_{v}|\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{\bar{I}_{v}}(x).

5.2.
Nonlinear approximation

Let
\Sigma_{N}=\Sigma_{N}(\varphi)
denote the set of all functions
S
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
for which there exists a subset
\Lambda\subset A
of cardinality
\#\Lambda=N
such that

S=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}a_{\alpha}\psi_{\alpha}(h^{-1}\cdot)\ \ \ \text{for some $h>0$ and $a_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{R}}$},

with the quasi-interpolants as above.
Define

\sigma_{N}(f)_{p}:=\inf_{S\in\Sigma_{N}}\|f-S\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

For the sake of simplicity, we will assume that

\ell+1<m_{2}
in this section so that there is no
log term in (
3.7
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
be given, and let
f\in F_{\tau,q}^{s}
, with
0<s\leq\ell+1
,
\tau:=(1/p+s/d)^{-1}
and
q=(1+s/d)^{-1}
. Then

\hat{\sigma}_{N}(f)_{p}\leq CN^{-s/d}\|f\|_{F_{\tau,q}^{s}}.

The proof of Theorem
5.1
follows along the same line as
that of Theorem 6.2 of
[
15
]
. It relies on a series of lemmas
with which we will begin.

Lemma 5.2
.

For each
v\in\mathcal{V}
and
N_{v}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, there exists a positive number
h\sim\ell(I_{v})(N_{v}+1)^{-1/d}
such that

S_{v,N_{v}}:=\widetilde{Q}_{h}w_{v}\in\Sigma_{N_{v}},

and

(5.2)

\displaystyle R_{v}(x):

\displaystyle=|w_{v}(x)-S_{v,N_{v}}(x)|

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leq C(N_{v}+1)^{-(\ell+1)/d}\Bigl[M(\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{\bar{I_{v}}})(x)\Bigr]^{1+\frac{m_{2}}{d}},\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Proof.

By (
5.1
), the estimate holds trivially if
N_{v}=0
. For the rest of the proof, we assume that
N_{v}\geq 1
.
Let
h
be a positive number to be specified in a moment, and let

\Lambda:=\Bigl\{{\alpha}\in A:\ \ \|{\alpha}h-c_{v}\|\leq CA_{0}\ell(I_{v})\Bigr\},

where
c_{v}
denotes the centre of the dyadic cube
I_{v}
, and
C
is a large positive constant depending only on
d
. Then

\#\Lambda\leq C\Bigl(1+\frac{\ell(I_{v})}{h}\Bigr)^{d}.

Thus, we may choose
h\sim\ell(I_{v})N_{v}^{-1/d}
so that

\#\Lambda\leq N_{v}
and

\bigcup_{x\in\bar{I_{v}}}B_{4h}(x)\subset B_{CA_{0}\ell(I_{v})}(c_{v}).

Since
w_{v}
is supported in
\bar{I}_{v}
, it follows that
w_{v}
is identically zero on
\bigcup_{{\alpha}\in A\setminus\Lambda}B_{2h}({\alpha}h)
.
This in turn implies that

\ell_{{\alpha}}[(w_{v})_{h}]=S_{0}[w_{v}(h\cdot+h{\alpha})]=0,\ \ \ \ \forall{\alpha}\in A\setminus\Lambda,

and hence

Q_{h}w_{v}=\sum_{{\alpha}\in\Lambda}\ell_{{\alpha}}[(w_{v})_{h}]\psi_{\alpha}(h^{-1}\cdot)\in\Sigma_{N_{v}}.

Therefore, on one hand, using (
3.7
) and (
5.1
),

\displaystyle|w_{v}(x)-\widetilde{Q}_{h}w_{v}(x)|\leq Ch^{\ell+1}M(\|\nabla^{\ell+1}w_{v}\|)(x)

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leq C\Biggl(\frac{h}{\ell(I_{v})}\Biggr)^{\ell+1}\leq CN_{v}^{-(\ell+1)/d},\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

On the other hand, if
\|x-c_{v}\|\geq 2CA_{0}\ell(I_{v})
, then
w_{v}(x)=0
, and

\displaystyle|w_{v}(x)-\widetilde{Q}_{h}w_{v}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{{\alpha}\in\Lambda}|\ell_{{\alpha}}[(w_{v})_{h}]|\Bigl(1+h^{-1}\|x-{\alpha}h\|\Bigr)^{-m_{2}-d}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{{\alpha}\in\Lambda}\Bigl(h^{-1}\|x-c_{v}\|\Bigr)^{-m_{2}-d}\frac{1}{|B_{h}({\alpha}h)|}\int_{B_{h}({\alpha}h)}|w_{v}(z)|\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C\Biggl(\frac{\|x-c_{v}\|}{\ell(I_{v})}\Biggr)^{-m_{2}-d}\Bigl(\frac{\ell(I_{v})}{h}\Bigr)^{-m_{2}-d}\#\Lambda

(5.5)

\displaystyle\leq C\Biggl(1+\frac{\|x-c_{v}\|}{\ell(I_{v})}\Biggr)^{-m_{2}-d}N_{v}^{-m_{2}/d}.

Therefore, combining (
5.4
) with (
5.5
), and in view of the fact that
m_{2}\geq\ell+1
, we deduce that

(5.6)

|w_{v}(x)-\widetilde{Q}_{h}w_{v}(x)|\leq CN_{v}^{-\frac{\ell+1}{d}}\Bigl(1+\frac{\|x-c_{v}\|}{\ell(I_{v})}\Bigr)^{-m_{2}-d},\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Finally, to complete the proof, we just need to observe that

M(\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{I_{v}})(x)\geq C_{0}\Biggl(1+\frac{\|x-c_{v}\|}{\ell(I_{v})}\Biggr)^{-d},\ \ \forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
R_{v}(x)
be as defined in (
5.2
). Then for
1<p<\infty
,

\Bigl\|\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}|f_{v}|R_{v}\Bigr\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C\Bigl\|\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}(1+N_{v})^{-(\ell+1)/d}|f_{v}|\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{\bar{I_{v}}}\Bigr\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Proof.

Using (
5.3
), and setting
q=1+\frac{m_{2}}{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}|f_{v}|R_{v}\Bigr\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigl\|\Bigl(\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}|f_{v}|(N_{v}+1)^{-\frac{\ell+1}{d}}\Bigl[M\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{\bar{I_{v}}}(x)\Bigr]^{q}\Bigr)^{1/q}\Bigr\|^{q}_{L^{qp}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

which, using the Fefferman-Stein inequality (see
[
26
, p. 55, 1.3.1]
, is estimated by

\displaystyle C\Bigl\|\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}|f_{v}|(N_{v}+1)^{-\frac{\ell+1}{d}}\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{\bar{I_{v}}}\Bigr\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

∎

Lemma 5.4
.

[
15
, Lemma 6.3]
Let
\{z_{j}\}_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}
be a sequence of nonnegative numbers with
Z:=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}z_{j}<\infty
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}z_{j}\Biggl(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{j}z_{k}\Biggr)^{\varepsilon-1}\leq C_{\varepsilon}Z^{\varepsilon},

where it is understood that
0\cdot\infty=0
.

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
5.1
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.
The proof of Theorem
5.1
follows along the same line as that of Theorem 6.2 of
[
15
]
.
Fix a nonzero function
f\in F_{\tau,q}^{s}
, and a positive integer
N
. Given
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, let
\mathcal{V}_{x}
denote the set of all
v\in\mathcal{V}
such that
\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{I_{v}}(x)\neq 0
. Define for each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and each
v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
,

M_{q,v^{\prime}}(x):=\Biggl(\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{x}:v\geq v^{\prime}}|I_{v}|^{-qs/d}|f_{v}|^{q}\Biggr)^{1/q},

where we say
v\geq v^{\prime}
if either
|I_{v}|>|I_{v^{\prime}}|
or
|I_{v}|=|I_{v^{\prime}}|
and
e_{v}\geq e_{v^{\prime}}
.
Notice that if
x\in I_{v^{\prime}}
, then

(5.7)

\displaystyle M_{q,v^{\prime}}(x)^{q}=\Biggl(\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}:I_{v}\supsetneqq I_{v^{\prime}}}+\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}:I_{v}=I_{v^{\prime}},e_{v}\geq e_{v^{\prime}}}\Biggr)|I_{v}|^{-qs/d}|f_{v}|^{q}.

Thus,
M_{q,v^{\prime}}(x)
is actually a constant on
I_{v^{\prime}}
, which we denote by
M_{q,v^{\prime}}
. Thus,

M_{q,v^{\prime}}:=M_{q,v^{\prime}}(x)\leq\Delta_{s,q}(f)(x),\ \ x\in I_{v^{\prime}}.

Let

(5.8)

a_{v}=a|I_{v}|^{q}|f_{v}|^{q}M_{q,v}^{\tau-q},\ \ v\in\mathcal{V},

where
a
will be specified in a moment. Since
\tau-q>0
and
q=1-qs/d
, it follows that

\displaystyle a_{v}=\Bigl\|a|I_{v}|^{-qs/d}|f_{v}|^{q}M_{q,v}^{\tau-q}\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{I_{v}}\Bigr\|_{1}\leq\Bigl\|a|I_{v}|^{-qs/d}|f_{v}|^{q}(\Delta_{s,q}f)^{\tau-q}\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{I_{v}}\Bigr\|_{1}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}a_{v}

\displaystyle\leq a\Bigl\|\Delta_{s,q}(f)^{\tau-q}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}|I_{v}|^{-qs/d}|f_{v}|^{q}\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{I_{v}}\Bigr\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq a\|\Delta_{s,q}(f)^{\tau}\|_{1}=a\|\Delta_{s,q}(f)\|_{\tau}^{\tau}=a\|f\|_{F_{\tau,q}^{s}}^{\tau}.

Now choose
a
so that
a\|f\|_{F_{\tau,q}^{s}}^{\tau}=N
,
and let
N_{v}
be equal to
\lceil a_{v}\rceil
, the greatest integer
\leq a_{v}
. Then
\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}N_{v}\leq N
.
Now for each
v\in\mathcal{V}
, let
S_{v,N_{v}}\in\Sigma_{N_{v}}
be as in Lemma
5.2
, and let

S:=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}S_{v,N_{v}}f_{v}.

Then
S\in\Sigma_{N}
, and using Lemma
5.3
, we have that

\displaystyle\|f-S\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}f_{v}w_{v}-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}f_{v}s_{v,N_{v}}\Bigr\|_{p}\leq\Bigl\|\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}|f_{v}||w_{v}-S_{v,N_{v}}|\Bigr\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigl\|\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}(1+a_{v})^{-(\ell+1)/d}|f_{v}|\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{\bar{I_{v}}}\Bigr\|_{p}\leq C\Bigl\|\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}(1+a_{v})^{-s/d}|f_{v}|\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{\bar{I_{v}}}\Bigr\|_{p}

\displaystyle=:C\|\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}E_{v}\|_{p},

where

E_{v}(x):=(1+a_{v})^{-s/d}|f_{v}|\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{\bar{I_{v}}}(x).

If
a_{v}>0
, then using (
5.8
), for
x\in\bar{I_{v}}
,

\displaystyle E_{v}(x)

\displaystyle\leq a_{v}^{-s/d}|f_{v}|\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{\bar{I_{v}}}(x)=a^{-s/d}|I_{v}|^{-qs/d}|f_{v}|^{1-qs/d}M_{q,v}^{\frac{\tau}{p}-q}

(5.9)

\displaystyle=a^{-s/d}|I_{v}|^{-qs/d}|f_{v}|^{q}\Biggl(\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}:v^{\prime}\geq v}|I_{v^{\prime}}|^{-qs/d}|f_{v^{\prime}}|^{q}\Biggr)^{\frac{\tau}{pq}-1}.

On the other hand, if
a_{v}=0
, then by (
5.7
) and (
5.8
), we have that
f_{v}=0
,
E_{v}(x)=0
, and (
5.9
) remains true under the agreement that
0\cdot\infty=0
. Thus,
using Lemma
5.4
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}E_{v}(x)

\displaystyle\leq Ca^{-s/d}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}|I_{v}|^{-qs/d}|f_{v}|^{q}\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{\bar{I_{v}}}(x)\Bigl(\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}:v^{\prime}\geq v}|I_{v^{\prime}}|^{-qs/d}|f_{v^{\prime}}|^{q}\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{\bar{I_{v^{\prime}}}}(x)\Bigr)^{\frac{\tau}{pq}-1}

\displaystyle\leq Ca^{-s/d}(\Delta_{s,q}(f)(x))^{\tau/p}.

Recalling that
a=N\|f\|_{F_{\tau,q}^{s}}^{-\tau}
and
\frac{\tau}{p}+\frac{\tau s}{d}=1
, we conclude that

\Bigl\|\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}E_{v}\Bigr\|_{p}\leq Ca^{-s/d}\|\Delta_{s,q}(f)\|_{\tau}^{\tau/p}=CN^{-s/d}\|f\|_{F_{\tau,q}^{s}}.

This completes the proof.

\Box

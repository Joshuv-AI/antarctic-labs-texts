Neighborhood selection with application to social networks
Nana Wang, Wolfgang Polonik

Abstract
The topic of this paper is modeling and analyzing dependence in stochastic social networks. We propose a latent variable block model that allows the analysis of dependence between blocks via the analysis of a latent graphical model. Our approach is based on the idea underlying the neighborhood selection scheme put forward by Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2006 . However, because of the latent nature of our model, estimates have to be used in lieu of the unobserved variables. This leads to a novel analysis of graphical models under uncertainty, in the spirit of Rosenbaum et al. 2010 , or Belloni, Rosenbaum and Tsybakov (2016). Lasso-based selectors, and a class of Dantzig-type selectors are studied.



1
Introduction

The study of random networks has been a topic of great interest in recent years, e.g. see
Kolaczyk 2009
and
Newman 2010
. A network is defined as a structure composed of nodes and edges connecting nodes in various relationships.
The observed network can be represented by an
N\times N
adjacency matrix
\bm{Y}=(Y_{ij})_{i,j=1,\ldots,N}
, where
N
is the total number of nodes within the network. For a binary relation network, as considered here,
Y_{ij}=1
if there is an edge from node
i
to node
j,
and
0
otherwise. In the following we identify an adjacency matrix
\bm{Y}
with the network itself.

Most relational phenomena are dependent phenomena, and dependence is often of substantive interest.
Frank and Strauss 1986
and
Wasserman and Pattison 1996
introduced exponential random graph models which allow the modelling of a wide range of dependences of substantive interest, including transitive closure. For such models,
Y_{ij}\in\{0,1\}
and the distribution of
\bm{Y}
is assumed to follow the exponential family form

P_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{Y}=\bm{y})=\exp\left(\bm{\theta}\cdot\bm{T}(\bm{y})-\phi(\bm{\theta})\right),\bm{y}\in\mathcal{Y},

where

\phi(\bm{\theta})=-\log\left(\sum_{\bm{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}\exp(\bm{\theta}\cdot\bm{T}(\bm{y}))\right)
and
\bm{T}(\bm{y}):\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{q},
are the sufficient statistics, e. g. the total number of edges.
However, as mentioned in
Schweinberger and Handcock 2014
, exponential random graph models are lacking neighborhood structure, and that makes modelling dependencies challenging for such networks. Neighborhoods (or communities, blocks) are in general defined as a group of individuals (nodes), such that individuals within a group interact with each other more frequently than with those outside the group. Recently,
Schweinberger and Handcock 2014
proposed the concept of local dependence in stochastic networks. This concept allows for dependence within neighborhoods, while different neighborhoods are independent.

In contrast to that, our work is considering dependence between blocks, while the connections within blocks are assumed independent. We also assume the blocks to be known. We then propose to analyze dependencies between blocks by means of graphical models. To this end, we assume an undirected network so that

\displaystyle Y_{ij}|(\bm{P},\bm{z})\sim{\rm Bernoulli}\left(p_{\bm{z}[i],\bm{z}[j]}\right),

(1.1)

where
\bm{z}[i]\in\widebar{K}:=\{1,\cdots,K\},i=1,\ldots,N
indicate block memberships in one of
K
blocks;
p_{k,\ell},k,\ell\in\widebar{K}
govern the intensities of the connectivities within and between blocks,
0<p_{k,l}<1
; and
\bm{P}=(p_{k,l})_{k,l\in\widebar{K}}
is a
K\times K
symmetric matrix. We then put a Gaussian logistic model on the
p_{k,\ell}
. More precisely, for the diagonal elements
(p_{k,k})_{1\leq k\leq K}
, assume that

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{p_{k,k}}{1-p_{k,k}}\right)=\bm{x}_{k}^{T}\bm{\beta}+\epsilon_{k},\;1\leq k\leq K,

(1.2)

where
\bm{x}_{k}
is a
(L\times 1)
vector of given co-variables corresponding to block
k
, and
\bm{\beta}
is the
(L\times 1)
parameter vector. Furthermore,
\bm{\epsilon}=(\epsilon_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{K})^{T}
with

\displaystyle\bm{\epsilon}\sim N(\bm{0},\bm{\Sigma}),

(1.3)

where
\bm{\Sigma}=(\sigma_{kl})_{1\leq k,l\leq K}
is an nonsingular covariance matrix.
Each off-diagonal element
p_{k,l}
(
k\neq l
) is assumed to be independent with all the other elements of
\bm{P}
. The latter assumption is made to simplify the exposition. A similar model can be found in
Xu and Hero 2014
.

The dependence between the
p_{k,k}
induces dependence between blocks. We can thus analyze this induced dependence in our network model, by using methods from Gaussian graphical models, via selecting the zeros in the precision matrix
\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}
. Adding dependencies between the
p_{k,\ell}
with
k\neq\ell
would increase the dimension of
\bm{\Sigma}
, and induce ‘second order dependencies’ to the network structure, namely, dependencies of block connections between different pairs of blocks.

It is crucial to observe that this Gaussian graphical model is defined in terms of the
p_{k,k}
(or, more precisely, in terms of their log-adds ratios), and that these quantities obviously are not observed. Thus, they need to be estimated from our network data, and, to this end, we here assume the availability of iid observations of the network. This estimation, in turn, induces
additional randomness
to our analysis of the graphical model. We are therefore facing similar challenges as in the analysis of Gaussian graphical models
under uncertainty
. However, our situation is more complex, as will become clear below.

The methods for neighborhood selection considered here, are based on the column-wise methodology of
Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2006
. We apply this methodology (under uncertainty) to some known selection methods from the literature, thereby, adjusting these methods for the additional uncertainty. The selection methods considered here are (i) the graphical Lasso of
Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2006
, (ii) a class of Dantzig-type selectors, that includes the Dantzig selector of
Candes and Tao 2007
, and (iii) the matrix uncertainty selector of
Rosenbaum et al. 2010
. This will lead to ‘graphical’ versions of the respective procedures. The graphical Dantzig selector already has been studied in
Yuan 2010
, but without the additional uncertainty we are facing here. This leads to novel selection methodologies for which we derive statistical guarantees. We also present numerical studies to illustrate their finite sample performance.

More details on our latent variable block model is discussed in Section
2
. Thereby we also introduce some basic notation. Section
3
introduces our neighborhood selection method-ologies, and presents results on their large sample performance. Tuning parameter selection is also discussed there. Numerical studies are presented in Section
4
, and the proofs of our main results are in Appendix
5
..

2
Some important preliminary facts

Let
\bm{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{K})^{T}
with
\eta_{k}=\log(p_{kk}/(1-p_{kk}))
be the vector of log odds of the within-block connection probabilities, and let
\bm{X}_{K\times L}=(\bm{x}_{1},\cdots,\bm{x}_{K})^{T}
be the design matrix. Our latent variable block model (
1.1
) - (
1.3
) says that
\bm{\eta}\sim N(\bm{X}\bm{\beta},\bm{\Sigma})
. The dependence among the
\eta_{k},
encoded in
\bm{\Sigma},
is propagated to the
p_{kk}
. Let
\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}=\bm{D}=(d_{kl})_{1\leq k,l\leq K}
, then the following fact holds.

Fact 2.1
.

Under (
1.1
) - (
1.3
), we have

d_{kl}=0,
if and only if,
p_{k,k}
is independent of
p_{l,l}
given the other variables
\bm{p}_{-(k,l)}=\{p_{i,j}:(i,j)\in\widebar{K}\times\widebar{K}\backslash\{(k,k),(l,l)\},i\leq j\}
, or just given
\{p_{i,i}:i\in\widebar{K}\backslash\{k,l\}\}
.

In other words, if

\displaystyle E=\{(k,l):d_{kl}\neq 0,k\neq l\}

denotes the edge set of the graph corresponding to
\bm{\eta}
, then, under our latent variable block model,
(k,l)\notin E
if and only if
p_{k,k}
is conditionally independent with
p_{l,l}
given the other variables
\{p_{ii}:1\leq i\leq K,i\notin\{k,l\}\}
. Identifying nonzero elements in
\bm{D}
thus will reveal the conditional dependence structure of the blocks in our underlying network.

We will use the relative number of edges within each block, as estimates for the unobserved values
p_{kk},k=1,\ldots,K
. Let
S_{k}=\sum_{\bm{z}[i]=\bm{z}[j]=k}Y_{ij},\,k=1,\cdots,K,
denote the total number of edges in the
K
blocks.

Fact 2.2
.

Under (
1.1
) - (
1.3
), we have

{\rm sign}(\sigma_{kl})={\rm sign}\left({\rm Cov}(S_{k},S_{l})\right).

For proofs of the two facts see
Oliveira 2012
(page 13, Theorem 1.35) and
Liu et al. 2009
(Section 3, Lemma 2), respectively.

3
Neighborhood selection

Here we discuss the identification of the nonzero elements in
\bm{D}
. We first assume that (
1.1
) - (
1.3
) holds with a known
\bm{\beta}
, and we write
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{K})^{T}=\bm{X}\bm{\beta}
. We also assume that
0<p_{i,j}<1
for all
i,j\in\widebar{K}
. Let
\bm{Y}^{(t)},t=1,\ldots,n
denote
n
iid observed networks with corresponding independent unobserved random vectors
\bm{p}^{(t)},t=1,\ldots,n
following our model. Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{A}_{K}
denote the
K
blocks of the networks
\bm{Y}^{(t)}
and
\mathcal{V}=\{1,\cdots,N\}
be the node set.
Assume
\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
\mathcal{A}_{l}
are mutually exclusive for
k\neq l
so that
\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{A}_{k}=\mathcal{V}
. The number of possible edges within each block is
m_{k}=|\mathcal{A}_{k}|(|\mathcal{A}_{k}|-1)/2
for
k=1,\cdots,K
, and the number of possible edges between block
k
and block
l
is then
|\mathcal{A}_{k}||\mathcal{A}_{l}|
for
1\leq k\neq l\leq K
. We would like to point out again that the block membership variable
\bm{z}
is assumed to be known.

3.1
Controlling the estimation error

Given a network
\bm{Y}^{(t)}
, let
S^{(t)}_{k}=\sum_{\bm{z}[i]=\bm{z}[j]=k}Y_{ij}^{(t)},k=1,\ldots,K,t=1,\ldots,n
denote the number of edges within block
k
in network
t
. Natural estimates of
p_{kk}^{(t)}
and
\eta_{k}^{(t)}
are

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}=\frac{S_{k}^{(t)}}{m_{k}}\quad\text{ and}\quad\widetilde{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}=\log\Bigg(\frac{\widetilde{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}}{1-\widetilde{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}}\Bigg)

(3.1)

respectively.

Let
\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}^{(t)}=(\widetilde{\eta}_{1}^{(t)},\cdots,\widetilde{\eta}_{K}^{(t)})^{T}
,
and let
m_{\min}=\min_{1\leq k\leq K}m_{k}
be the minimum number of possible edges within a block, which of course measures the minimum blocksize.

Fact 3.1
.

Assume that
K
is fixed. Then, under (
1.1
) - (
1.3
), we have for each
t=1,\cdots,n
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}^{(t)}\rightarrow N(\bm{X}\bm{\beta},\bm{\Sigma})\quad\text{in distribution as }\,m_{\min}\rightarrow\infty.

This result tells us that, if we base our edge selection on
\widetilde{\bm{\eta}}^{(t)}
, then, for
m_{\min}
large, we are
close
to a Gaussian model, and thus we can hope that our analysis is similar to that of a Gaussian graphical model. However, the approximation error has to be examined carefully. In order to do that, we first truncate the
\widetilde{p}_{kk}^{(t)}
’s, or, equivalently, the
\widetilde{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}
. For
T>0,
let

\widehat{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}=\begin{cases}-T&\mbox{if $\widetilde{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}<-T$}\\
\widetilde{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}&\mbox{if $|\widetilde{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}|\leq T$}\\
T&\mbox{if $\widetilde{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}>T$}.\end{cases}

This truncation corresponds to

\widehat{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}=\begin{cases}(1+e^{T})^{-1}&\mbox{if $\widetilde{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}<(1+e^{T})^{-1}$ }\\
\widetilde{p}_{k}^{(t)}&\mbox{if $(1+e^{T})^{-1}\leq\widetilde{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}\leq(1+e^{-T})^{-1}$ }\\
(1+e^{-T})^{-1}&\mbox{if $\widetilde{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}>(1+e^{-T})^{-1}$}.\end{cases}

In what follows, we work with these truncated versions. Note that the dependence on
T
is not indicated explicitly in this notation.

The magnitude of
m_{\min}
is important, as it reflects the accuracy of our estimates. This estimation error will crucially enter the performance of the graphical model based inverse covariance estimator. Under the latent variable block model, we have the following concentration result:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\sigma^{2}=\max_{k\in\widebar{K}}\sigma_{kk}
and
\mu_{B}=\max_{k\in\widebar{K}}|\mu_{k}|
. Then, under (
1.1
) - (
1.3
), we have, for
\min(L,T)>\mu_{B}
, and
m_{\min}\geq 16M^{2}\log(nK)e^{2L}
, that

P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K\atop 1\leq t\leq n}|\widehat{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}-\eta_{k}^{(t)}|<8Me^{L}\sqrt{\frac{\log(nK)}{m_{\min}}}\Big)\\
\geq 1-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{nK\sigma}{\min\{L,T\}-\mu_{B}}\exp\Big(-\frac{(\min\{L,T\}-\mu_{B})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\Big)-\Big(\frac{1}{nK}\Big)^{2M^{2}-1}.

(3.2)

Remark
.

Note that the larger
\mu_{B}
, the larger we need to choose both
T
and
L
. A large
T
will cause problems, because the
\widehat{p}_{k}^{(t)}
then might be too close to zero or one, causing challenges by definition of
\widehat{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}
. A large
L
makes our approximation less tight. Therefore we will have to control the size of
\mu_{B}
(even if
\mu_{B}
is known); see assumption A1.6 and B1.5.

To better understand the bound in (
3.2
), suppose that the number of blocks,
K
, grows with
n
such that
K(n)=O(n^{\gamma}),
for some
\gamma>0.
While
K
is allowed to grow with
n
, we assume that
\sigma^{2}
is bounded. If we further choose
0<\min\{L,T\}-\mu_{B}=\gamma\log n,
for some
\gamma>0
, then, there exists
c>0
, such that as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{nK\sigma}{\min\{L,T\}-\mu_{B}}\exp\left(-\frac{(\min\{L,T\}-\mu_{B})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)=O\left(\exp\left(-c(\log n)^{2}\right)\right).

The last term on the right-hand side of (
3.2
) can be controlled similarly, by choosing
M=\sqrt{\left(1+(c\log n)/(\gamma+1)\right)/2}
.
With these choices, we obtain an approximation error of
\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}|\widehat{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}-\eta_{k}^{(t)}|=O(n^{-p})
by choosing the minimum blocksize large enough

\displaystyle m_{\min}^{-1}=O\left(n^{-2p}(\log n)^{-2}e^{-2L}\right).

3.2
Edge selection under uncertainty

In order to identify the nonzero elements in
\bm{D}
, we consider the graphical model in terms of the distribution of
\bm{\eta}
. Recall that
\bm{\eta}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{K}
, where each component of
\bm{\eta}
belongs to one of the
K
blocks, thus
\widebar{K}=\{1,\cdots,K\}
are not only the block labels, but also the node set in the underlying graph corresponding to the joint distribution of the
\bm{\eta}
. Using Gaussianity of
\bm{\eta}
, the set
{\rm ne}_{a}=\{b\in\widebar{K}:d_{ab}\neq 0\}
is the neighborhood of node
a\in\widebar{K}
of the associated graph. We follow the idea of
Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2006
to convert the problem into a series of linear regression problems: for each
a\in\widebar{K}
,

\displaystyle\eta_{a}-\mu_{a}=\sum_{b\in\widebar{K}\backslash\{a\}}\theta_{b}^{a}(\eta_{b}-\mu_{b})+v_{a}

with the residual
v_{a}
independent of
\{\eta_{b}:1\leq b\neq a\leq K\}
. Let
\bm{\theta}^{a}=(\theta^{a}_{1},\cdots,\theta^{a}_{K})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
with
\theta^{a}_{a}=0
, then the neighborhood can also be written as
{\rm ne}_{a}=\{b\in\widebar{K}:\theta_{b}^{a}\neq 0\}
.

Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2006
consider the case of
n
i.i.d. observations of
\bm{\eta}
.
However, under the assumption of our model, we only have observations of
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}=(\widehat{\eta}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\eta}_{K})^{T}
. Under our assumptions, we have available
n
independent realizations
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{(1)},\cdots,\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{(n)}
.
Let
\widehat{\bm{H}}=(\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{(1)},\cdots,\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{(n)})^{T}
be the
n\times K
-matrix with columns
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{a}=(\widehat{\eta}^{(1)}_{a},\ldots,\widehat{\eta}_{a}^{(n)})^{T}
,
a\in\widebar{K}
.
Similarly denote by
\bm{H}=(\bm{\eta}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{\eta}^{(n)})^{T}
the
n\times K
-matrix whose rows are
n
independent copies of
\bm{\eta}
. Its column
\bm{\eta}_{a},a\in\widebar{K}
are vectors of
n
independent observations of
\eta_{a}
. That is, we can also write
\widehat{\bm{H}}=(\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{K})
and
\bm{H}=(\bm{\eta}_{1},\cdots,\bm{\eta}_{K})
. With this notation, for all
a\in\widebar{K}
,

\displaystyle\bm{\eta_{a}}-\mu_{a}\bm{1}_{n}=\sum_{b\in\widebar{K}}\theta_{b}^{a}(\bm{\eta_{b}}-\mu_{b}\bm{1}_{n})+\bm{v}_{a}.

(3.3)

Let
\bm{R}=\widehat{\bm{H}}-\bm{H}
. The new matrix model can be written as

\displaystyle(\widehat{\bm{H}}-\bm{1}_{n}{\bm{\mu}}^{T})=(\bm{H}-\bm{1}_{n}{\bm{\mu}}^{T})+\bm{R}

(3.4)

\displaystyle(\bm{\eta}^{(t)}-\bm{\mu})\sim N(\bm{0},\bm{\Sigma})\quad\text{i.i.d. for }t=1,\cdots,n.

(3.5)

Moreover, for each
a\in\widebar{K}
, let
\bm{H}_{-a}=\{\bm{\eta}_{b}:b\in\widebar{K}\backslash\{a\}\}
,
\widehat{\bm{H}}_{-a}=\{\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{b}:b\in\widebar{K}\backslash\{a\}\}
and
\bm{\theta}^{a}_{-a}=(\theta^{a}_{1},\cdots,\theta^{a}_{a-1},\theta^{a}_{a+1},\cdots,\theta^{a}_{K})^{T}
,
\bm{\mu}_{-a}=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{a-1},\mu_{a+1},\cdots,\mu_{K})^{T}
. We can write the above model as

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{a}-\mu_{a}\bm{1}_{n}=(\bm{H}_{-a}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu}_{-a}^{T})\bm{\theta}^{a}_{-a}+\bm{\xi}_{a}

(3.6)

\displaystyle(\widehat{\bm{H}}_{-a}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu}_{-a}^{T})=(\bm{H}_{-a}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu}_{-a}^{T})+\bm{R}_{-a},

where
\bm{\xi}_{a}=\bm{v}_{a}+(\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{a}-\bm{\eta}_{a})
and
\bm{R}_{-a}=\widehat{\bm{H}}_{-a}-\bm{H}_{-a}
.
Note that (
3.6
) has a similar structure as the model considered by
Rosenbaum et al. 2010
. The important difference is that in our situation, we do not have independence of
\bm{\xi}_{a}
and
\bm{R}_{-a}
.

3.3
Edge selection under uncertainty using the Lasso

As in
Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2006
, we define our Lasso estimate
\widehat{\bm{\theta}}^{a,\lambda,lasso}
of
\bm{\theta}^{a}
as

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\theta}}^{a,\lambda,lasso}=\mathop{\arg\min}\limits_{\bm{\theta}:\theta_{a}=0}\big(n^{-1}\|(\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{a}-\mu_{a}\bm{1}_{n})-(\widehat{\bm{H}}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu}^{T})\bm{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1}\big).

(3.7)

The corresponding neighborhood estimate is

\displaystyle\widehat{{\rm ne}}_{a}^{\lambda,lasso}=\big\{b\in\widebar{K}:\widehat{\theta}^{a,\lambda,lasso}_{b}\neq 0\big\};

and the full edge set can be estimated by

\displaystyle\widehat{E}^{\lambda,\wedge,lasso}=\big\{(a,b):a\in\widehat{{\rm ne}}_{b}^{\lambda,lasso}\text{ and }b\in\widehat{{\rm ne}}_{a}^{\lambda,lasso}\big\}

or

\displaystyle\widehat{E}^{\lambda,\vee,lasso}=\big\{(a,b):a\in\widehat{{\rm ne}}_{b}^{\lambda,lasso}\text{ or }b\in\widehat{{\rm ne}}_{a}^{\lambda,lasso}\big\}.

In order to formulate statistical guarantees for the behavior of these estimates, we need the following assumptions. On top of the assumptions from
Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2006
, which are assumptions A1.1 - A1.5, we need further assumption on the underlying network.

A1

Assumptions on the underlying Gaussian graph

1.

High-dimensionality
: There exists some
\gamma>0
so that

K(n)=O(n^{\gamma})
for
n\rightarrow\infty
.

2.

Nonsingularity
: For all
a\in\widebar{K}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
{\rm Var}(\eta_{a})=1
and there exists
\upsilon^{2}>0
so that

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\eta_{a}|\bm{\eta}_{\widebar{K}\backslash\{a\}})\geq\upsilon^{2}.

3.

Sparsity

(a)

There exists some

so that
\max_{a\in\widebar{K}}\left|{{\rm ne}}_{a}\right|=O(n^{\kappa})
for

.

(b)

6.

Asymptotic upper bound on the mean:

\mu_{B}(n)=o(\log n)
for

.

A2

Block size of networks
:
There exists constants
c>0
and

such that

\displaystyle m_{\min}(n)\geq c\cdot n^{\nu}\quad\text{for }n\geq n_{0},

where

The following theorem shows that, for proper choice of
\lambda=\lambda_{n}
, our selection procedure finds the correct neighborhoods with high probability, provided

is large enough.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let assumptions A1 and A2 hold, and assume
\bm{\beta}
to be known. Let
\epsilon
be such that

\displaystyle 0<\max\big\{\kappa,\tfrac{4-\xi-\nu}{3},\tfrac{2+2\kappa-\nu}{2}\big\}<\epsilon<\xi.

If, for some
d_{T},d_{\lambda}>0,
we have
T_{n}\sim d_{T}\log n
and
\lambda_{n}\sim d_{\lambda}n^{-(1-\epsilon)/2}

1
1

1

For two sequence
\{a_{n}\}
,
\{b_{n}\}
of real numbers, we write
a_{n}\sim b_{n}
for
\frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}\rightarrow c
for some
0<c<\infty
.
,
respectively, then there exists a constant
c>0,
such that

\displaystyle P(\widehat{E}^{\lambda,lasso}=E)=1-O\left(\exp\left(-c(\log n)^{2}\right)\right)\quad\text{as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Remark
.

Assumption A2 says that the rate of increase of the minimum block size, which behaves like
\sqrt{m_{\min}},
depends on the neighborhood size in our graphical model, and on the magnitude of the partial correlations in the graphical model. Roughly speaking, large neighborhoods (large
\kappa
), and small partial correlations (small

), both require a large minimum block size (large
\nu
), which appears reasonable. The choice of a proper penalty parameter

also depends on these two parameters.

3.4
Edge selection with a class of Dantzig-type selectors under uncertainty

In this section, we propose a novel class of Dantzig-type selectors that are iterated over all
a\in\widebar{K}
. For a linear model as in (
3.3
), i.e. for fixed
a
,
Candes and Tao 2007
introduced the Dantzig selector as a solution to the convex problem

\quad\min\Big\{\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1}:\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{K(n)},\theta_{a}=0\text{ and }\\
\Big|\frac{1}{n}\big(\bm{H}_{-a}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu}_{-a}^{T}\big)^{T}\big((\bm{\eta}_{a}-\mu_{a}\bm{1}_{n})-(\bm{H}-\bm{1}_{n}{\bm{\mu}}^{T})\bm{\theta}\big)\Big|_{\infty}\leq\lambda\Big\},\quad

where
\lambda\geq 0
is a tuning parameter, and for a matrix
A=(a_{ij})
,
|\cdot|_{\infty}=\max_{ij}|a_{ij}|.
Under our model, we define the Dantzig selector as a solution of the minimization problem

\quad\min\Big\{\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1}:\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{K(n)},\theta_{a}=0\text{ and }\\
\Big|\frac{1}{n}\big(\widehat{\bm{H}}_{-a}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu}_{-a}^{T}\big)^{T}\big((\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{a}-\mu_{a}\bm{1}_{n})-(\widehat{\bm{H}}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu}^{T})\bm{\theta}\big)\Big|_{\infty}\leq\lambda\Big\}\quad

(3.8)

with
\lambda\geq 0
. Moreover, when considering (
3.6
), the idea of matrix uncertainty selector (MU-selector) comes into our mind. In our setting, we define an MU-selector, a generalization of the Dantzig selector under matrix uncertainty, as a solution of the minimization problem

\quad\min\Big\{\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1}:\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{K(n)},\theta_{a}=0\text{ and}\\
\Big|\frac{1}{n}\big(\widehat{\bm{H}}_{-a}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu}_{-a}^{T}\big)^{T}\big((\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{a}-\mu_{a}\bm{1}_{n})-(\widehat{\bm{H}}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu}^{T})\bm{\theta}\big)\Big|_{\infty}\leq\mu\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1}+\lambda\Big\}

(3.9)

with tuning parameters
\mu\geq 0
and
\lambda\geq 0
. Note that our MU-selector deals with matrix uncertainty directly, rather than replacing
\bm{H}
by
\widehat{\bm{H}}
in the optimization equations like the Lasso or the Dantzig selector. What we mean by this is that our MU-selector is based on the structural equation (
3.6
), while both Lasso-based estimator and Dantzig selector are based on the linear model (
3.3
) with the unknown
\bm{\eta}
’s simply replaced by their estimators.

Now we consider a class of Dantzig-type selectors, which can be considered as generalizations of the Dantzig selector and the MU-selector. For each

, let the Dantzig-type selector
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{a,\lambda,ds}
be a solution of the optimization problem

\quad\min\Big\{\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1}:\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{K(n)}:\theta_{a}=0\text{ and }\\
\Big|\frac{1}{n}(\widehat{\bm{H}}_{-a}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu}_{-a}^{T})^{T}\big((\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{a}-\mu_{a}\bm{1}_{n})-(\widehat{\bm{H}}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu}^{T})\bm{\theta}\big)\Big|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{a,n}(\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1})\Big\},

(3.10)

where for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{\lambda_{a,n}(\cdot):a\in\widebar{K}\}
is a set of functions such that

•

For each

and
a\in\widebar{K}

is an increasing function.

•

For all

,

\lambda_{n}\geq 0
, i.e, for all

so that

\displaystyle\min_{a\in\widebar{K}}\min_{\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}:\theta_{a}=0}\lambda_{a,n}(\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1})\geq\lambda_{n}.

•

\max_{a\in\widebar{K}}\lambda_{a,n}(\|\bm{\theta}^{a}\|_{1})=o(n^{-\frac{1-\xi}{2}}(\log n)^{-1})
, i.e, there exist

\displaystyle\lambda_{a,n}(\|\bm{\theta}^{a}\|_{1})\leq u_{n}n^{-\frac{1-\xi}{2}}(\log n)^{-1},\text{ for all }a\in\widebar{K}.

The Dantzig-type selector

always exists, because the LSE

\Big|\frac{1}{n}\big(\widehat{\bm{H}}_{-a}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu}_{-a}^{T}\big)^{T}\big((\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{a}-\mu_{a}\bm{1}_{n})-(\widehat{\bm{H}}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu}^{T})\bm{\theta}\big)\Big|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{a,n}(\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1})\Big\}

. It may not be unique, however. We will show that, similar to

Let

3.5
Extension

Here we consider the case of an unknown coefficient vector
\bm{\beta},
or unknown mean
\bm{\mu}=\bm{X}\bm{\beta}
.
Recall that
\bm{\eta}^{(t)}\sim N(\bm{\mu},\bm{\Sigma}),

t=1,\cdots,n
are i.i.d. Given
\{\bm{\eta}^{(t)}:t=1,\cdots,n\}
, a natural way to estimate
\bm{\mu}
is via the MLE
\widebar{\bm{\eta}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\bm{\eta}^{(t)}
. Recall, however, that we only have estimates
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{(t)},t=1,\cdots,n
, available. Using the estimates
\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{(t)}

\bm{\mu}
by
\widebar{\widehat{\bm{\eta}}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{(t)}

\bm{\beta}
via

, where
\bm{X}^{+}
is the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of

(when

,
\bm{X}^{+}=(\bm{X}^{T}\bm{X})^{-1}\bm{X}^{T}
). In order to derive consistency properties for

, assumptions on the design matrix are needed.
Theorem
3.3
below states asymptotic properties of the estimators.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let assumptions A1.1 (or B1.1) and A1.6 (or B1.5) hold. If
m^{-1}_{\min}=O(n^{-\nu})
for some
\nu>0

If, moreover, the design matrix is of full rank and the singular value of
\bm{X}
is asymptotically upper bounded, that is,
{\rm rank}(\bm{X})=L
and
\sigma_{\max}(\bm{X})=O(1)

c>0,
so that

\displaystyle P\big(n^{\tfrac{b-\gamma}{2}}\|\widehat{\bm{\beta}}-\bm{\beta}\|_{2}>\delta\big)=O\left(\exp\left(-c(\log n)^{2}\right)\right)\quad\text{as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Next we consider the estimation of the edge set

based on

3.6
Selection of penalty parameters in finite samples.

The results above only show that consistent edge selection is possible with the Lasso and the Dantzig-type selector in a high-dimensional setting. However, we still have not given a concrete way to choose the penalty parameter for a given data set. In this section, we discuss the choice of tuning parameter for finite
n
for the following estimation methods:

•

The Lasso

•

The Dantzig-type selectors:

–

the Dantzig selector:
\lambda(\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1})=\lambda

–

the MU-selector:
\lambda(\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1})=\lambda\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1}+\lambda

Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2006
discussed a data-driven choice of the penalty parameter of the Lasso for Gaussian random vectors. Our data are not Gaussian, however. Moreover, according to our numerical studies,
the choice suggested by Meinshausen and Bühlmann tends to result in a very sparse graph, which goes along with a very small type I error. Another natural idea is choosing the penalty parameter via cross-validation. However,
Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2006
already state that the choice
\lambda_{\rm oracle}
gives an inconsistent estimator, and
\lambda_{\rm cv}
is an estimate of
\lambda_{\rm oracle}
. So the cross-validation approach is also not recommended. Instead we here consider the following two-stage procedure: for each
a\in\widebar{K}
, let

\widehat{\theta}_{b}^{a,\lambda,\tau}=\begin{cases}\widetilde{\theta}_{b}^{a,\lambda}I\big(|\widetilde{\theta}_{b}^{a,\lambda}|/\max_{k\in\widebar{K}\backslash\{a\}}|\widetilde{\theta}_{b}^{a,\lambda}|>\tau\big)&\mbox{if $\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{a,\lambda}\neq\bm{0}$ }\\
0&\mbox{if $\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{a,\lambda}=\bm{0}$ },\end{cases}

(3.12)

where
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{a,\lambda}
is obtained by solving either (
3.7
), (
3.8
) or (
3.9
) with
\mu=\lambda
. Such procedures have also been used in
Rosenbaum et al. 2010
and
Zhou et al. 2011
. However, the use of
\max_{k\in\widebar{K}\backslash\{a\}}|\widehat{\theta}_{b}^{a,\lambda}|
in the truncation is novel. By using
\max_{k\in\widebar{K}\backslash\{a\}}|\widehat{\theta}_{b}^{a,\lambda}|
, we have
\tau\in[0,1]
, making the tuning parameter
\tau
more standardized.
Note that when
\widehat{\theta}_{b}^{a,\lambda}
is a Dantzig-type selector, then, under the assumptions in Section
3
, and for large
n
, (
3.12
) is equivalent to (
3.11
).

For the choice of
\lambda
and
\tau
, we follow a similar idea as in
Zhou et al. 2011
, but with some modification: for each
a\in\widebar{K}
, we select
\lambda_{a}
via cross-validation to minimize the squared error prediction loss for
a
-th regression. After all
\lambda_{a}
,
a\in\widebar{K}
, are chosen, we select
\tau

\displaystyle{BIC}(\bm{D})=-2l_{n}(\bm{D})+\log(n)\dim(\bm{D}),

where

Recall the notation introduced in Section
3.2
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{H}}=\bm{H}+\bm{R}

\displaystyle\bm{\eta}^{(t)}=\bm{X}\bm{\beta}+\bm{\epsilon}^{(t)}\;\text{ for }t=1,\cdots,n

\displaystyle\bm{\epsilon}^{(t)}\sim N(\bm{0},\bm{\Sigma})\;\text{ i.i.d. for }t=1,\cdots,n.

In the proofs we denote by “
c
” a positive constant that can be different in each formula.

5.1
Proof of Lemma
3.1

We first consider the case
\bm{\beta}=\bm{0}
and show the following result:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\sigma=\max_{k\in\widebar{K}}\sqrt{\sigma_{kk}}
. Under the latent block model with
\bm{\beta}=\bm{0}
, for
L>0
, if
m_{\min}\geq 16M^{2}\log(nK)e^{2L}
,

P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}|\widehat{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}-\eta_{k}^{(t)}|<8Me^{L}\sqrt{\log(nK)/m_{k}}\Big)\\
\geq 1-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{nK\sigma}{\min\{L,T\}}\exp\Big(-\frac{(\min\{L,T\})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\Big)-\Big(\frac{1}{nK}\Big)^{2M^{2}-1}.

Proof.

From Hoeffding’s inequality we have, for any
M>0
, that

\displaystyle P\big(\sqrt{m_{k}}|\widetilde{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}-p_{k,k}^{(t)}|>M\,\big|\,p_{k,k}^{(t)}\big)

\displaystyle=P\Big(\big|\sum\nolimits_{z[i]=k,z[j]=k}(Y^{(t)}_{ij}-p_{k,k}^{(t)})/m_{k}\big|>{M}/{\sqrt{m_{k}}}\,\Big|\,p_{k,k}^{(t)}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\big(-2m_{k}\big({M}/{\sqrt{m_{k}}}\big)^{2}\big)=2\exp\big(-2M^{2}\big).

Thus, using the fact that, given
\bm{p}=(p_{k,k},\ldots,p_{K,K})^{T}
, all the
Y_{ij}
are independent, we obtain

\displaystyle P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}\sqrt{m_{k}}

\displaystyle|\widetilde{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}-p_{k,k}^{(t)}|>M\sqrt{\log(nK)}\,\big|\,\big\{\bm{p}^{(t)}:1\leq t\leq n\big\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq nK\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}P\left(\sqrt{m_{k}}|\widetilde{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}-p_{k,k}^{(t)}|>M\sqrt{\log(nK)}\,\Big|\,p_{k,k}^{(t)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq nK\exp\left(-2M^{2}\log(nK)\right)=(nK)^{1-2M^{2}},

By integrating over
\{\bm{p}^{(t)}:1\leq t\leq n\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}\sqrt{m_{k}}

\displaystyle|\widetilde{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}-p_{k,k}^{(t)}|>M\sqrt{\log(nK)}\Big)\leq(nK)^{1-2M^{2}}.

(5.1)

Note that if
|\eta_{k}^{(t)}|<T
, we have

|\widehat{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}-\eta_{k}^{(t)}|\leq|\widetilde{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}-\eta_{k}^{(t)}|
, and we can write

\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}-\eta_{k}^{(t)}=\log\tfrac{\widetilde{p}^{(t)}_{k,k}}{1-\widetilde{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}}-\log\tfrac{p_{k,k}^{(t)}}{1-p_{k,k}^{(t)}}=\tfrac{1}{\xi_{k}^{(t)}(1-\xi_{k}^{(t)})}(\widetilde{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}-p_{k,k}^{(t)}),

where
\xi_{k}^{(t)}
lies between
\widetilde{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}
and
p_{k,k}^{(t)}
. Since
|\xi_{k}^{(t)}-p_{k,k}^{(t)}|\leq|\widetilde{p}_{k,k}^{(t)}-p_{k,k}^{(t)}|
, inequality (
5.1
) also applies to
|\xi_{k}^{(t)}-p_{k,k}^{(t)}|
, so that

\displaystyle P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}\sqrt{m_{k}}|\xi_{k}^{(t)}-p_{k,k}^{(t)}|>M\sqrt{\log(nK)}\Big)\leq(nK)^{1-2M^{2}}.

It follows that

P\left(\xi^{(t)}_{k}<\epsilon\text{ or }\;\xi^{(t)}_{k}>1-\epsilon,\text{ for all }1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n\right)\\
\leq P\Big(p_{k,k}^{(t)}<\epsilon+\tfrac{M\sqrt{\log(nK)}}{\sqrt{m_{k}}}\text{ or }\;p_{k,k}^{(t)}\geq 1-\epsilon-\tfrac{M\sqrt{\log(nK)}}{\sqrt{m_{k}}},\text{ for all }1\leq k\leq K,\;1\leq t\leq n\Big)\\
+P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}\sqrt{m_{k}}|\xi_{k}^{(t)}-p_{k,k}^{(t)}|>M\sqrt{\log(nK)}\Big).

(5.2)

As for
\eta_{k}
, since
\eta_{k}^{(t)}-\mu_{k}\sim N(0,\sigma_{kk})
and

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}|\eta_{k}^{(t)}|\leq\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}|\eta_{k}^{(t)}-\mu_{k}|+\max_{1\leq k\leq K}|\mu_{k}|,

we have, for
C>1
, that

\displaystyle P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}|\eta_{k}^{(t)}|>\sqrt{2\log C}+\mu_{B}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}\big|(\eta_{k}^{(t)}-\mu_{k})/\sqrt{\sigma_{kk}}\big|>\sqrt{2\log C}/\sigma\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2nK\widetilde{\Phi}\big({\sqrt{2\log C}}/\sigma\big),

where
\widetilde{\Phi}=1-\Phi
(
\Phi
is the c.d.f. of
N(0,1)
). Note that

P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}|\eta_{k}^{(t)}|>\sqrt{2\log B}\Big)\\
=P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}p_{k,k}^{(t)}>\frac{1}{1+e^{-\sqrt{2\log B}}}\Big)+P\Big(\min_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}p_{k,k}^{(t)}<\frac{1}{1+e^{\sqrt{2\log B}}}\Big).\quad

Now we are using the following:

Fact 5.1
.

For each
c_{0}>0,
we can find
x_{0}=e^{1/c_{0}^{2}}>1
such that, for
x\geq x_{0}>1,
we have
e^{-\sqrt{\log x}}\geq x^{-c_{0}}
.

Using this fact, it follows that, for any
L>0,
we have that
\frac{1}{1+B^{-2/L}}\geq\frac{1}{1+e^{-\sqrt{2\log B\,}}}
for
B\geq e^{L^{2}/2}
. W.l.o.g. assume that
B>1.
Then,

\displaystyle P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}p_{k,k}^{(t)}>\frac{1}{1+e^{-\sqrt{2\log B\,}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}p_{k,k}^{(t)}>\frac{1}{1+B^{-2/L}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}p_{k,k}^{(t)}>1-2^{-1}B^{-\frac{2}{L}}\Big).

Further, using that
\frac{e^{x}}{1+e^{x}}\geq\frac{1}{2}
for
x>0
, we obtain (by using the above fact again) that for any
L>0
and
B\geq e^{L^{2}/2}
,

\displaystyle P\Big(\min_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}p_{k,k}^{(t)}<\frac{1}{1+e^{\sqrt{2\log B}}}\Big)

\displaystyle=P\Big(\min_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}p_{k,k}^{(t)}<e^{-\sqrt{2\log B}}\frac{e^{\sqrt{2\log B\,}}}{1+e^{\sqrt{2\log B\,}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq P\Big(\min_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}p_{k,k}^{(t)}<\tfrac{1}{2}B^{-\frac{2}{L}}\Big).

This means that in (
5.2
) we can choose
\epsilon=\epsilon(L)=\frac{1}{4}B^{-\frac{2}{L}}
. It follows that with this choice of
\epsilon
(for arbitrarily large
L
) and assuming that

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq K}\tfrac{M\sqrt{\log(nK)}}{\sqrt{m_{k}}}\leq\epsilon,

(5.3)

we have

\displaystyle P\big(\xi^{(t)}_{k}<\epsilon\text{ or }\xi^{(t)}_{k}>1-\epsilon,\;1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n\big)\leq 2nK\widetilde{\Phi}\big({\sqrt{2\log B}}/{\sigma}\big)+(nK)^{1-2M^{2}}.

Finally, this leads to: let
\epsilon=\epsilon(L)=\frac{1}{4}B^{-\frac{2}{L}}
. If (
5.3
) holds, then for
B\geq e^{L^{2}/2}

\displaystyle P\Big(\tfrac{1}{\xi^{(t)}_{k}(1-\xi^{(t)}_{k})}\geq{2}/{\epsilon},\;1\leq t\leq n,1\leq k\leq K\Big)\leq 2nK\widetilde{\Phi}\big({\sqrt{2\log B}}/{\sigma}\big)+(nK)^{1-2M^{2}}.

Then

\quad P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}\sqrt{m_{k}}|\widehat{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}-\eta_{k}^{(t)}|<2M\epsilon^{-1}\sqrt{\log(nK)}\Big)\\
\geq 1-2nK\widetilde{\Phi}\big(\sigma^{-1}\min\big\{\sqrt{2\log B},T\big\}\big)-({nK})^{1-2M^{2}},\quad

i.e. for
L>0
, if
m_{\min}\geq 16M^{2}\log(nK)B^{4/L}
and
B\geq e^{L^{2}/2}
,

\quad P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}\sqrt{m_{k}}|\widehat{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}-\eta_{k}^{(t)}|<8MB^{\frac{2}{L}}\sqrt{\log(nK)}\Big)\\
\geq 1-2nK\widetilde{\Phi}\big(\sigma^{-1}\min\big\{\sqrt{2\log B},T\big\}\big)-({nK})^{1-2M^{2}}.\quad

Choosing
B=e^{L^{2}/2}
, then for
L>0
, if
m_{\min}\geq 16M^{2}\log(nK)e^{2L}
,

\displaystyle P\Big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}|\widehat{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}-\eta_{k}^{(t)}|<8Me^{L}\sqrt{{\log(nK)}/{m_{\min}}}\,\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 62.59596pt\geq 1-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{nK\sigma}{\min\{L,T\}}\exp\Big(-\frac{(\min\{L,T\})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\Big)-\Big(\frac{1}{nK}\Big)^{2M^{2}-1}.

∎

The proof of Lemma
3.1
with
\bm{\beta}\neq\bm{0}
is similar, but it uses

\displaystyle P\big(\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}|\eta_{k}^{(t)}|>\sqrt{2\log C}+\mu_{B}\big)\leq nKC^{-1/\sigma^{2}}/{\sqrt{\pi\log C^{1/\sigma^{2}}}},

which comes from
\eta_{k}^{(t)}-\mu_{k}\sim N(0,\sigma_{kk})
and

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}|\eta_{k}^{(t)}|\leq\max_{1\leq k\leq K,1\leq t\leq n}|\eta_{k}^{(t)}-\mu_{k}|+\max_{1\leq k\leq K}|\mu_{k}|.

5.2
Proof of Theorem
3.1

We first introduce assumption

C0

|\bm{R}|_{\infty}<\delta
for fixed
\delta>0
.

This assumption makes the proofs more transparent. Intermediate results are using this assumptions. When applying these intermediate results to prove the main results (that are not using assumption C0 explicitly), we will show that C0 holds with sufficiently large probability. The following fact immediately follows from the definition of
\bm{R}
(see Section
3.2
):

Fact 5.2
.

Under assumption C0,
\|\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{k}-\bm{\eta}_{k}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{n}\delta
.

We prove a series of results: Theorem
5.1
and
5.2
, Corollary
5.1
, which will then imply Theorem
3.1
. The assertion of Theorem
3.1
then follows from Corollary
5.1
together with Lemma
3.1
. The proof is an adaptation of
Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2006
(proof of Theorem 1), to our more complex situation. Both of the proofs are mainly established with the property of chi-square distribution and the Lasso. For any
\mathcal{A}\subset\widebar{K}
, let the Lasso estimate
\widehat{\bm{\theta}}^{a,\mathcal{A},\lambda,lasso}
of
\bm{\theta}^{a,\mathcal{A}}
be defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\theta}}^{a,\mathcal{A},\lambda,lasso}=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\bm{\theta}:\theta_{k}=0,\forall k\notin\mathcal{A}}\Big(n^{-1}\|(\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{a}-\mu_{a}\bm{1}_{n})-(\widehat{\bm{H}}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu})\bm{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1}\Big).

(5.4)

Claim 5.1
.

For problem (
5.4
), under assumption C0, for any
q>1
,

\displaystyle P\Big(\|\widehat{\bm{\theta}}^{a,\mathcal{A},\lambda,lasso}\|_{1}\leq(q+\delta)^{2}\lambda^{-1}\Big)\geq 1-\exp\Big(-\tfrac{q^{2}-\sqrt{2q^{2}-1}}{2}n\Big).

Proof.

The claim follows directly from the tail bounds of the
\chi^{2}
-distribution (see
Laurent and Massart 2000
) and the inequality

\displaystyle n\lambda\|\widehat{\bm{\theta}}^{a,\mathcal{A},\lambda,lasso}\|_{1}\leq\|\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{a}-\mu_{a}\bm{1}_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left(\|\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{a}-\bm{\eta}_{a}\|_{2}+\|\bm{\eta}_{a}-\mu_{a}\bm{1}_{n}\|_{2}\right)^{2}.

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Given
\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
, let
G(\bm{\theta})
be a
K
-dimensional vector with elements

\displaystyle G_{b}(\bm{\theta})=-2n^{-1}\langle(\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{a}-\mu_{a}\bm{1}_{n})-(\widehat{\bm{H}}-\bm{1}_{n}\bm{\mu}^{T})\bm{\theta},\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{b}-\mu_{b}\bm{1}_{n}\rangle.

A vector
\widehat{\bm{\theta}}
with
\widehat{\theta}_{k}=0,\forall k\in\widebar{K}\backslash\mathcal{A}
is a solution to (
5.4
) iff for all
b\in\mathcal{A}
,
G_{b}(\widehat{\bm{\theta}})=-{\rm sign}(\widehat{\theta}_{b})\lambda
in case
\widehat{\theta}_{b}\neq 0
, and
|G_{b}(\widehat{\bm{\theta}})|\leq\lambda
in case
\widehat{\theta}_{b}=0
. Moreover, if the solution is not unique, and
|G_{b}(\widehat{\bm{\theta}})|<\lambda
for some solution
\widehat{\bm{\theta}}
, then
\widehat{\theta}_{b}=0
for all solutions of (
5.4
).

This Lemma is almost the same as Lemma A.1 in
Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2006
but without normality assumption of
\widehat{\bm{H}}
. Since the Gaussian assumption is not needed, the proof is a straightforward adaptation of the proof of Lemma A.1 in
Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2006
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For every
a\in\widebar{K}
, let
\widehat{\bm{\theta}}^{a,{\rm ne}_{a},\lambda,lasso}
be defined as in (
5.4
). Let the penalty parameter satisfy
\lambda_{n}\sim dn^{-(1-\epsilon)/2}
with some
d>0
and
\kappa<\epsilon<\xi
. Suppose that assumptions A1 and C0 hold with
\delta=o(n^{-(4-\xi-3\epsilon)/2})
. Then there exists
c>0
so that, for all
a\in\widebar{K}
,

\displaystyle P\big({\rm sign}(\widehat{\theta}_{b}^{a,{\rm ne}_{a},\lambda,lasso})={\rm sign}(\theta_{b}^{a}),\;\forall b\in{\rm ne}_{a}\big)=1-O\big(\exp(-cn^{\epsilon})\big)\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

Using similar notation as in
Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2006
, we set

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\theta}}^{a,{\rm ne}_{a},\lambda,lasso}=\mathop{\arg\min}\limits_{\bm{\theta}:\theta_{k}=0,\forall k\notin{\rm ne}_{a}}\Big(n^{-1}\|(\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{a}-\mu_{a}\bm{1}_{n})-(\widehat{\bm{H}}-\bm{1}_{n}{\bm{\mu}}^{T})\bm{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1}\Big),

(5.5)

and for all
a,b\in\widebar{K}
with
b\in{\rm ne}_{a}
, we let

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{a,b,\lambda}(\omega)=\mathop{\arg\min}\limits_{\bm{\theta}\in\Theta_{a,b}(\omega)}\Big(n^{-1}\|(\widehat{\bm{\eta}}_{a}-\mu_{a}\bm{1}_{n})-(\widehat{\bm{H}}-\bm{1}_{n}{\bm{\mu}}^{T})\bm{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1}\Big),

(5.6)

where

\displaystyle\Theta_{a,b}(\omega)=\big\{\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{K(n)}:\theta_{b}=\omega;\theta_{k}=0,\forall k\notin{\rm ne}_{a}\big\}.

Setting
\omega=\widehat{\theta}_{b}^{a,{\rm ne}_{a},\lambda,lasso}
, then
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{a,b,\lambda}(\omega)=\widehat{\bm{\theta}}^{a,{\rm ne}_{a},\lambda,lasso}
, and by Claim
5.1
with
q=2
,

\displaystyle P\Big(|\widehat{\theta}_{b}^{a,{\rm ne}_{a},\lambda,lasso}|\leq(2+\delta)^{2}\lambda^{-1}\Big)\geq 1-\exp\left(-2^{-1}n\right).

Thus, if
{\rm sign}(\widehat{\theta}_{b}^{a,{\rm ne}_{a},\lambda,lasso})\neq{\rm sign}(\theta_{b}^{a})
, with probability at least
1-\exp(2^{-1}n)
, there would exist some
\omega
with
|\omega|\leq(2+\delta)^{2}\lambda^{-1}
so that
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{a,b,\lambda}(\omega)
is a solution to (
5.5
) but
{\rm sign}(\omega){\rm sign}(\theta_{b}^{a})\leq 0
.
Without loss of generality, we assume
\theta_{b}^{a}>0
since
{\rm sign}(\theta_{b}^{a})\neq 0
for all
b\in{\rm ne}_{a}
. Note that by Lemma
5.2
,
\widetilde{\bm{\theta}}^{a,b,\lambda}(\omega)
can be a solution to (

\widehat{\bm{H}}-\bm{1}_{n}\widebar{\widehat{\bm{\eta}}}^{T}
in (
3.7
). For our model (cf.
3.4
), this means that we replace
\bm{R}
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{R}}=(\widehat{\bm{H}}-\bm{1}_{n}\widebar{\widehat{\bm{\eta}}}^{T})-(\bm{H}-\bm{1}_{n}{\bm{\mu}}^{T})=\left((\widehat{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}-\widebar{\widehat{\eta}}_{k})-(\eta_{k}^{(t)}-\mu_{k})\right)_{1\leq t\leq n\atop 1\leq k\leq K}.

(5.25)

Note that under assumption C0, for all
k\in\widebar{K}
,

\displaystyle\big|(\widehat{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}-\widebar{\widehat{\eta}}_{k})-(\eta_{k}^{(t)}-\mu_{k})\big|\leq\big|\widehat{\eta}_{k}^{(t)}-\eta_{k}^{(t)}\big|+\big|\widebar{\widehat{\eta}}_{k}-\mu_{k}\big|\leq 2\delta+\big|\widebar{\eta}_{k}-\mu_{k}\big|,

which means

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\bm{R}}|_{\infty}\leq 2\delta+\max_{k\in\widebar{K}}|\widebar{\eta}_{k}-\mu_{k}|.

(5.26)

Together with (
5.24
), we have

\displaystyle P\big(|\widetilde{\bm{R}}|_{\infty}\leq 2\delta+\delta_{\mu}\big)\geq 1-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{K\sigma}{\sqrt{n}\delta_{\mu}}\exp\left(-\frac{n\delta_{\mu}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right).

(5.27)

Using (
5.27
), Corollary
5.1
(or Theorem
5.3
) and Lemma
3.1
, we have Corollary
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
.

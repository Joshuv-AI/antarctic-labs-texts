Symbolic powers of generalized star configurations of hypersurfaces Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification . 13A15, 13A50, 13D02, 14N20, 52C35. Thanks: Keyword: Betti numbers, linear quotients, star configuration, symbolic power, containment problem, stable Harbourne–Huneke conjecture.
Kuei-Nuan Lin and Yi-Huang Shen

Abstract
. We introduce the class of sparse symmetric shifted monomial ideals. These ideals have linear quotients and their Betti numbers are computed. Using this, we prove that the symbolic powers of the generalized star configuration ideal are sequentially Cohen–Macaulay under some mild genericness assumption. With respect to these symbolic powers, we also consider the Harbourne–Huneke containment problem and establish the Demailly-like bound.



1.
Introduction

In the simplest case, a
star configuration
of codimension
c
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}

is a certain union of linear subspaces
V_{1},\dots,V_{p}
each of codimension
c
.
The study of various aspects of generalizations of star configurations has
attracted considerable attention recently; see for example,
[
6
]
,

[
7
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
17
]
,

[
18
]
and
[
19
]
, to name just a few. As a sequel
to our previous work
[
17
]
along this line, we continue to
investigate the symbolic powers of the generalized star configuration of
hypersurfaces.

Let
R={\mathbb{K}}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over an
infinite
{\mathbb{K}}
and denote its graded maximal ideal by
\mathfrak{m}
. Suppose that

s\geq n+1
and let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\}
be a set of forms in
R

satisfying some genericness condition. For a fixed choice of positive integers

a
and
b
such that
a\leq bs
, the
generalized star configuration
ideal
that we shall study here is the
uniform
a
-fold product
ideal

I_{a}(\mathcal{F}^{b})\coloneqq I_{a}(f_{1}^{b}\cdots f_{s}^{b})
.

In general, the
a
-fold product ideal

I_{a}(f_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots f_{s}^{b_{s}})

is generated by the
a
-fold products of the forms
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}
with
multiplicities
b_{1},\dots,b_{s}
respectively:

\Braket{f_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots f_{s}^{n_{s}}:0\leq n_{i}\leq b_{i}\text{ for each $i$ such that $\sum\nolimits_{i}n_{i}=a$}}.

It has also been studied extensively recently; see for example
[
2
]
and
[
10
]
. This notion was originally introduced as a nice tool for determining the minimum distance of linear codes in the coding theory. And it also emerges naturally when dealing with the higher order Orlik–Terao algebra of hyperplane arrangements.

The
a
-fold product ideal
I_{a}(f_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots f_{s}^{b_{s}})
is
uniform
when the multiplicities
b_{1},\dots,b_{s}
are all identical, say, to some positive integer
b
. Whence, we will write it as
I_{a}(\mathcal{F}^{b})
for simplicity. It is treated as the
generalized star configuration ideal
, since when
b=1
, we will get back the star configuration ideal of hypersurfaces

I_{c,\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\bigcap_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{c}\leq s}\braket{f_{i_{1}},\dots,f_{i_{c}}}

for
c=s-a+1
, which were studied for instance in
[
11
]
and
[
18
]
.
In their work, the set
\mathcal{F}
is often assumed to be
c
-generic
, namely any subset of size at most
c+1
will form a regular sequence.

Our current work will focus on the study of the symbolic powers of the generalized star configuration ideal
I_{a}(\mathcal{F}^{b})
.
Recall that give an ideal
I
in a ring
R
, the
m
-th symbolic power
of
I
is defined by

I^{(m)}\coloneqq\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(R/I)}I^{m}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap R.

For instance, when
I
is the defining ideal of a reduced affine scheme over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, Zariski and Nagata showed that
I^{(m)}
is generated by the polynomials whose partial derivatives of orders up to
m-1
vanish on this scheme.
And in the previous work
[
17
]
, we have already studied the resurgence and symbolic defect of
I_{a}(\mathcal{F}^{b})
, as well as the Betti table and the primary decomposition of its symbolic powers.

In
[
12
]
and
[
11
]
, Geramita, Harbourne, Migliore, and Nagel showed that the symbolic powers of the ordinary star configuration ideal define arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay subschemes. It is then natural to ask if the same property still holds in the generalized case.
Notice that the ideal
I_{a}(\mathcal{F}^{b})
is no longer unmixed for
b\geq 2
. Thus, the correct question can be put as: whether the symbolic powers of this ideal are all sequentially Cohen–Macaulay?
We provide a positive answer under some mild genericness assumption.

In this work, we also study the Harbourne–Huneke containment problem, which was originally raised in
[
14
]
. After
[
4
]
, a generalized version can be stated as: given a homogeneous ideal
I
of big height
h
in a standard graded ring with the maximal homogeneous ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, does the inequality

I^{(\ell(h+m-1))}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\ell(h-1)}(I^{(m)})^{\ell}

hold for all
m,\ell\geq 1
?
The comparison between ordinary powers of an ideal and its symbolic ideals is wildly open in general; see, for example,
[
5
]
and
[
16
]
. We provide a positive answer for Harbourne–Huneke containment problem in the case
I=I_{a}(\mathcal{F}^{b})

under some mild genericness assumption. As a by-product, we establish the Demailly-like bound
in this case as well.

As usual, for a homogeneous ideal
J
, we will write
\alpha(J)
for the least degree of nonzero forms in this ideal. Then, our main results can be summarized in the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
a
and
b
be positive integers such that
(b-1)s+1<a\leq bs
. We assume further that
n\geq bs-a+1
and
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\}
is a set of
(bs-a+1)
-generic
d
-forms in
R={\mathbb{K}}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
. Then the following properties hold for the generalized star configuration ideal
I=I_{a}(\mathcal{F}^{b})
whose big height is known to be
h=bs-a+1
.

(1)

The symbolic powers of
I
are all sequentially Cohen–Macaulay.

(2)

The ideal
I
satisfies

I^{(\ell(h+m-1)-h+k)}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{d((\ell-1)(h-1)+k-1)(bh-(bs-a))}(I^{(m)})^{\ell}

for all positive integers
k,\ell
, and
m
.

(3)

The inequality

\frac{\alpha(I^{(\ell)})}{\ell}\geq\frac{\alpha(I^{(m)})+h-1}{m+h-1}

holds for all
\ell,m\geq 1
.

2.
Sparse symmetric shifted ideals

To show the sequentially Cohen–Macaulayness of the symbolic powers of the generalized star configuration ideal
I_{a}(\mathcal{F}^{b})
, we have to introduce first the sparse symmetric shifted ideals. These squarefree monomial ideals can be viewed as variations of the symmetric shifted ideals introduced in
[
3
]
. Likewise, the new ideals have linear quotients (
2.6
) and the Betti tables can be obtained combinatorially with ease (
2.18
).

2.1.
Definition and the linear quotient property

The ground ring that we shall consider is the polynomial ring
Z={\mathbb{K}}[z_{i,j}:1\leq i\leq s,1\leq j\leq b]
over an arbitrary field
{\mathbb{K}}
.
For each tuple
{\bm{\mu}}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{s})\in\{0,1,2,\dots,b\}^{s}
,
a monomial of the form
{\bm{z}}^{{\bm{\mu}}}\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{s}z_{i,\mu_{i}}
will be called a
sparse squarefree monomial
. Here, when
\mu_{i}=0
,
z_{i,\mu_{i}}
is understood to be the identity element
1\in Z
.

Recall that a tuple
{\bm{\lambda}}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{s}
is called a
partition
of length
s
, if
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{s}
. For the sparse squarefree monomial
f={\bm{z}}^{{\bm{\mu}}}
above, we may suppose that the multi-set

\{\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{s}\}=\{\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{s}\}.

Now, we write

{\bm{\lambda}}(f)\coloneqq(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{s},

and call it the
partition
associated to
f
.
If
{\bm{\lambda}}(f)=(0,\dots,0,e_{1},\dots,e_{d})
where
e_{1}\geq 1
, then
the
support degree
of both
f
and
{\bm{\lambda}}={\bm{\lambda}}(f)
are

\operatorname{suppdeg}(f)=\operatorname{suppdeg}({\bm{\lambda}})\coloneqq d,

and the
weight
of both
f
and
{\bm{\lambda}}
are

w(f)=|{\bm{\lambda}}|\coloneqq e_{1}+\cdots+e_{d}=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{s}=\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{s}.

Related, a sparse squarefree monomial in
Z
whose support degree is
s
, is called a
rainbow monomial
in
[
1
, Subsection 1.3]
.

Let
{\bm{\mu}}^{\prime}=(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\mu_{s}^{\prime})\in\{0,1,2,\dots,b\}^{s}
be another tuple. We will write
{\bm{\mu}}^{\prime}\leq_{0}{\bm{\mu}}
if

•

\mu_{i}^{\prime}\leq\mu_{i}
for each
i
, and

•

\mu_{i}^{\prime}=0
if and only if
\mu_{i}=0
.

Notice that the permutation group
\mathfrak{S}_{s}
has an action on
Z
, such that for each permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{s}
and a variable
z_{i,j}
in
Z
, we have
\sigma(z_{i,j})=z_{\sigma(i),j}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A squarefree monomial proper ideal
J
of
Z
will be called
sparse symmetric shifted
if it satisfies the following requirements.

(S-1)

The ideal
J
is generated by some sparse squarefree monomials.

(S-2)

For each minimal monomial generator
f={\bm{z}}^{{\bm{\mu}}}\in G(J)
with
{\bm{\mu}}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{s})
, if
f^{\prime}={\bm{z}}^{{\bm{\mu}}^{\prime}}
with
{\bm{\mu}}^{\prime}=(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\mu_{s}^{\prime})
and
{\bm{\mu}}^{\prime}\leq_{0}{\bm{\mu}}
, then
f^{\prime}\in J
.

(S-3)

The ideal
J
is invariant under the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{s}
.

Remark 2.2
.

We may imagine the variables in
Z
displayed in the canonical matrix
{\bm{Z}}=(z_{i,j})_{s\times b}
. Then, roughly speaking, the sparse symmetry shifted ideal above has sparseness, vertical (upward) shiftiness, and horizontal symmetry. And these properties correspond to the (S-1), (S-2), and (S-3) respectively. Meanwhile, the symmetric shifted ideal, introduced in
[
3
]
, has both horizontal shiftiness and horizontal symmetry, since it lacks the sparse structure here.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
J
be a sparse symmetric shifted ideal in
Z
. Suppose that there is some sparse squarefree monomial
f=\prod_{i}z_{\sigma(i),\mu_{i}}\in J
for some
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{s}
. If
f^{\prime}=\prod_{i}z_{\sigma^{\prime}(i),\mu_{i}^{\prime}}
for some
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{S}_{s}
with
(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\mu_{s}^{\prime})\leq_{0}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{s})
, then
f^{\prime}\in J
as well.

Proof.

By the symmetry condition (S-3), it suffices to consider the case when
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}=\operatorname{id}\in\mathfrak{S}_{s}
. Since
f\in J
, there exists some minimal monomial generator
g=\prod_{i}z_{i,\tau_{i}}\in G(J)
such that
\tau_{i}=\mu_{i}
or
0
for each
i
. Define
\tau_{i}^{\prime}=\min(\tau_{i},\mu_{i}^{\prime})
for each
i
. Since
(\tau_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\tau_{s}^{\prime})\leq_{0}(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{s})
, the monomial
g^{\prime}\coloneqq\prod_{i}z_{i,\tau_{i}^{\prime}}\in J
by the shiftiness condition (S-2). As
g^{\prime}
divides
f^{\prime}
, this implies that
f^{\prime}\in J
as well.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
J
be a sparse symmetric shifted ideal in
Z
. Suppose that
f=\prod_{i}z_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{i}}\in J
where
{\bm{\lambda}}(f)=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s})
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{s}
. If
\lambda_{i_{1}}=0<k\leq\lambda_{i_{2}}
for some
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
, then
f^{\prime}\coloneqq fz_{\sigma(i_{1}),k}/z_{\sigma(i_{2}),\lambda_{i_{2}}}\in J
.

Remark 2.5
.

Suppose that the ideal
J\subset Z
is generated by some sparse squarefree monomials. If
f
is a monomial in
Z
, then the colon ideal
J:_{Z}f
is also generated by some sparse squarefree monomials that are coprime to
f
. To see this, it suffices to notice that when
g
is a sparse squarefree monomial, then
\braket{g}:f
is generated by the sparse squarefree monomial
g/\gcd(f,g)
.

Here is the first main result of this section.

Theorem 2.6
.

If
J
is a sparse symmetric shifted ideal in
Z
, then it has linear quotients.

Proof.

Inspired by the treatment in
[
3
]
, we first consider the lexicographic ordering
>_{{\operatorname{lex}}}
on
Z
where the variables
z_{i,j}>z_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
if and only if

•

j<j^{\prime}
, or

•

j=j^{\prime}
and
i<i^{\prime}
.

Thus, among those squarefree monomials of degree
s
,
z_{1,1}z_{2,1}\cdots z_{s,1}
is the initial (biggest) one with respect to
>_{{\operatorname{lex}}}
.

Next, we take a total order
\prec
on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{s}
defined by

{\bm{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{s})\prec{\bm{b}}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{s})

if and only if

•

\operatorname{suppdeg}({\bm{a}})<\operatorname{suppdeg}({\bm{b}})
, or

•

\operatorname{suppdeg}({\bm{a}})=\operatorname{suppdeg}({\bm{b}})
and
|{\bm{a}}|<|{\bm{b}}|
, or

•

\operatorname{suppdeg}({\bm{a}})=\operatorname{suppdeg}({\bm{b}})
and
|{\bm{a}}|=|{\bm{b}}|
, while the leftmost non-zero entry of
(a_{1}-b_{1},\dots,a_{s}-b_{s})
is negative.

Now, we are ready to introduce a total order on the minimal generating set
G(J)
, which, by abuse of notation, will still be denoted by
\prec
. For
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
in
G(J)
, we define
f_{1}\prec f_{2}
if and only if

•

{\bm{\lambda}}(f_{1})\prec{\bm{\lambda}}(f_{2})
, or

•

{\bm{\lambda}}(f_{1})={\bm{\lambda}}(f_{2})
and
f_{1}>_{{\operatorname{lex}}}f_{2}
.

Then, we sort the squarefree monomials in
G(J)
with respect to
\prec
. To establish the linear quotient property, we take arbitrary minimal monomial generator
f\in G(J)
and let

J_{f}\coloneqq\braket{g\in G(J):g\prec f}.

The remaining task is to show that the colon ideal
J_{f}:_{Z}f
is linear for each such
f
.
We can accomplish this task with the following two steps.

(1)

First of all, we describe explicitly the set
\mathcal{G}_{f}
of variables that belong to this colon ideal.

(2)

Next, we prove that the set
\mathcal{G}_{f}
actually generates the colon ideal
J_{f}:f
in general. Thus,
J_{f}:f
is linear, as expected.

They are established in the coming
2.7
and
2.8

respectively. And consequently, the ideal
J
has linear quotients.
∎

In the remaining of this subsection, we fix a monomial
f
in
G(J)
. The first task is to describe the set
\mathcal{G}_{f}
which collects the variables in the colon ideal
J_{f}:f
.
Suppose that the partition
{\bm{\lambda}}(f)=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s})
. Now, there is some permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{s}
such that
f=\prod_{i=1}^{s}z_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{i}}
. Recall that for
i
with
\lambda_{i}=0
,
z_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{i}}
is understood to be the identity element
1\in Z
.
And by abuse of notation, we define
\max(f)\coloneqq\max\Set{\sigma(k):\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{s}}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The set
\mathcal{G}_{f}
is the disjoint union of the following three types of sets:

•

\{z_{\sigma(i),j}:1\leq j<\lambda_{i}\text{ and }\lambda_{i}>0\}
,

•

\{z_{\sigma(i),j}:1\leq j<\lambda_{s}\text{ and }\lambda_{i}=0\}
, and

•

\{z_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{s}}:\sigma(i)<\max(f)\text{ and }\lambda_{i}=0\}
.

A quick example illustrating the linear generating set
\mathcal{G}_{f}
above is given in
3.4
.

Proof.

To confirm the assertion, roughly speaking, we have two cases.

(1)

Firstly, we consider the case when
\lambda_{i}>0
. We will show that
z_{\sigma(i),j}\in\mathcal{G}_{f}
if and only if
1\leq j<\lambda_{i}
.

(a)

When
1\leq j<\lambda_{i}
, we look at the squarefree monomial
f^{\prime}\coloneqq fz_{\sigma(i),j}/z_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{i}}
. By (S-2), we have
f^{\prime}\in J
. Thus, we can find suitable
f^{\prime\prime}\in G(J)
that divides
f^{\prime}
. Obviously, we have
\operatorname{suppdeg}(f^{\prime\prime})\leq\operatorname{suppdeg}(f^{\prime})=\operatorname{suppdeg}(f)
and
w(f^{\prime\prime})\leq w(f^{\prime})<w(f)
. It follows that
f^{\prime\prime}\prec f
. Now,
z_{\sigma(i),j}f\in\braket{f^{\prime}}\subseteq\braket{f^{\prime\prime}}\subseteq J_{f}
. Consequently,
z_{\sigma(i),j}\in J_{f}:f
, as expected.

(b)

Suppose that
j=\lambda_{i}
. It follows from
2.5
that
z_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{i}}\notin\mathcal{G}_{f}
.

(c)

When
j>\lambda_{i}
, we suppose for contradiction that
z_{\sigma(i),j}\in\mathcal{G}_{f}
. Since now
fz_{\sigma(i),j}\in J_{f}
, there exists some
f^{\prime\prime}\in G(J_{f})
such that
f^{\prime\prime}
divides
fz_{\sigma(i),j}
. If
z_{\sigma(i),j}
does not divide this
f^{\prime\prime}
, then
f^{\prime\prime}
divides
f
directly, contradicting the minimality of
f
. Thus,
z_{\sigma(i),j}
divides
f^{\prime\prime}
. Since
f^{\prime\prime}
is a sparse monomial,
z_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{i}}
does not divide
f^{\prime\prime}
. Consequently,
f^{\prime\prime}
divides the squarefree monomial
f^{\prime}\coloneqq fz_{\sigma(i),j}/z_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{i}}
. Now,
f^{\prime}\in J
such that
\operatorname{suppdeg}(f^{\prime})=\operatorname{suppdeg}(f)
and
|{\bm{\lambda}}(f^{\prime})|>|{\bm{\lambda}}(f)|
. Hence, we don’t have
f^{\prime}\prec f
. Consequently,
f^{\prime\prime}\neq f^{\prime}
and
\operatorname{suppdeg}(f^{\prime\prime})<\operatorname{suppdeg}(f^{\prime})=\operatorname{suppdeg}(f)
. Next, we look at
f^{\prime\prime\prime}\coloneqq f^{\prime\prime}z_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{i}}/z_{\sigma(i),j}
, which belongs to
J
by (S-2). Notice that
f^{\prime\prime\prime}
properly divides
f
, as
f^{\prime\prime}
properly divides
f^{\prime}
. This contradicts the minimality of
f
. Therefore,
z_{\sigma(i),j}\notin J_{f}:f
, as expected.

(2)

Next, we consider the case when
\lambda_{i}=0
. We will show that
z_{\sigma(i),j}\in\mathcal{G}_{f}
if and only if
1\leq j<\lambda_{s}
, or
j=\lambda_{s}
and
\sigma(i)<\max(f)
.

(a)

When
1\leq j<\lambda_{s}
, we look at the squarefree monomial
f^{\prime}\coloneqq fz_{\sigma(i),j}/z_{\sigma(s),\lambda_{s}}
, which belongs to
J
by
2.4
. Then, we can argue as in the item
1a
of
1
to see that
z_{\sigma(i),j}\in J_{f}:f
.

(b)

If
j>\lambda_{s}
,
we suppose for contradiction that
z_{\sigma(i),j}\in\mathcal{G}_{f}
.
As in the item
1c
of
1
, there exists some
f^{\prime\prime}\in G(J_{f})
such that
f^{\prime\prime}
divides
fz_{\sigma(i),j}
, and
z_{\sigma(i),j}
divides
f^{\prime\prime}
by the minimality of
f
. Furthermore, since
f^{\prime\prime}\prec f
, we have
\operatorname{suppdeg}(f^{\prime\prime})\leq\operatorname{suppdeg}(f)
and
f^{\prime\prime}
properly divides
fz_{\sigma(i),j}
. We have two subcases to consider here.

(i)

If
\operatorname{suppdeg}(f^{\prime\prime})=\operatorname{suppdeg}(f)
, there is some
i^{\prime}\neq i
such that
\lambda_{i^{\prime}}>0
and
f^{\prime\prime}=fz_{\sigma(i),j}/z_{\sigma(i^{\prime}),\lambda_{i^{\prime}}}
. Since
\operatorname{suppdeg}(f^{\prime\prime})=\operatorname{suppdeg}(f)
while
w(f^{\prime\prime})>w(f)
, this contradicts the condition
f^{\prime\prime}\prec f
.

(ii)

If
\operatorname{suppdeg}(f^{\prime\prime})<\operatorname{suppdeg}(f)
, there exists some subset

\mathcal{I}\subseteq\Set{k:1\leq k\leq s,\lambda_{k}>0},

such that
|\mathcal{I}|\geq 2
,
i\notin\mathcal{I}
and
f^{\prime\prime}=fz_{\sigma(i),j}\big/\prod_{k\in\mathcal{I}}z_{\sigma(k),\lambda_{k}}
. We can fix an arbitrary
k_{0}\in\mathcal{I}
and look at

f^{\prime\prime\prime}\coloneqq f^{\prime\prime}z_{\sigma(k_{0}),\lambda_{k_{0}}}/z_{\sigma(i),j}=f\bigg/\prod_{k\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\{k_{0}\}}z_{\sigma(k),\lambda_{k}}.

Since
j>\lambda_{k_{0}}
, it follows from
2.3
that
f^{\prime\prime\prime}\in J
by considering the pair
(f^{\prime\prime},f^{\prime\prime\prime})
. However,
f^{\prime\prime\prime}
divides
f
properly. This contradicts the minimality of
f
.

Since we get contradiction in both subcases,
z_{\sigma(i),j}\notin\mathcal{G}_{f}
, as expected.

(c)

If
\sigma(i)<\max(f)
, we look at the squarefree monomial
f^{\prime}\coloneqq fz_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{s}}/z_{\max(f),\lambda_{s}}
, which belongs to
J
by (S-3). Notice that although
{\bm{\lambda}}(f^{\prime})={\bm{\lambda}}(f)
, we have
f^{\prime}>_{{\operatorname{lex}}}f
. Thus,
f^{\prime}\prec f
, which in turn implies
z_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{s}}\in\mathcal{G}_{f}
.

(d)

It remains to consider the situation when
\sigma(i)\geq\max(f)
. Since
\sigma(i)=0<\lambda_{s}
, this amounts to saying that
\sigma(i)>\max(f)
. Now, we suppose for contradiction that
z_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{s}}\in\mathcal{G}_{f}
.
Like in the case
2b
, we can find some
f^{\prime\prime}\in G(J_{f})
such that
z_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{s}}
divides
f^{\prime\prime}
and
f^{\prime\prime}
properly divides
fz_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{s}}
.

(i)

If
\operatorname{suppdeg}(f^{\prime\prime})=\operatorname{suppdeg}(f)
, there is some
i^{\prime}\neq i
such that
\lambda_{i^{\prime}}>0
and
f^{\prime\prime}=fz_{\sigma(i),\lambda_{s}}/z_{\sigma(i^{\prime}),\lambda_{i^{\prime}}}
. We always have
\lambda_{i^{\prime}}\leq\lambda_{s}
. If
\lambda_{i^{\prime}}<\lambda_{s}
, then
w(f^{\prime\prime})>w(f)
, contradicting the assumption that
f^{\prime\prime}\prec f
. On the other hand, if
\lambda_{i^{\prime}}=\lambda_{s}
, then
{\bm{\lambda}}(f^{\prime\prime})={\bm{\lambda}}(f)
. As
\sigma(i)>\max(f)\geq\sigma(i^{\prime})
, we have
f>_{{\operatorname{lex}}}f^{\prime\prime}

Lemma 2.8
.

We have
\braket{\mathcal{G}_{f}}=J_{f}:f
in general.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that we only have

. Then, we can find suitable

and

\lambda_{j}^{\prime}>\lambda_{j}
;

(2)

\lambda_{j}=0
, then either
\lambda_{j}^{\prime}>\lambda_{s}
, or
\lambda_{j}^{\prime}=\lambda_{s}
and
\sigma(j)>\max(f)
.

As

while

Furthermore, as

(1)

The first case is when

Here,

(2)

The second case is when

is a subset of the disjoint union

w(f^{\prime})\leq w(f)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{J}\\
\lambda_{j}>0\end{subarray}}\lambda_{j}^{\prime}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}\lambda_{j}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}}\lambda_{\varphi(j)}^{\prime}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{J}\\

(b)

for each

Consequently,
{\bm{\lambda}}(f^{\prime})={\bm{\lambda}}(f)

•

for each

\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}
or
\mathcal{J}

•

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+d}(N_{A,m}^{{\bm{\lambda}}})

\displaystyle=\beta_{i}(Z_{m}^{\vartriangle}/\mathfrak{m}_{Z_{m}^{\vartriangle}}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}Z_{m}^{\triangledown}\otimes_{{\mathbb{K}}}V_{A^{\complement},{\bm{\lambda}}}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}Z_{A^{\complement},\lambda_{s}+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k+\ell=i}\beta_{\ell}(Z_{m}^{\vartriangle}/\mathfrak{m}_{Z_{m}^{\vartriangle}})\beta_{k}(V_{A^{\complement},{\bm{\lambda}}}^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k+\ell=i}\binom{p^{\vartriangle}({\bm{\lambda}})}{\ell}\binom{s-p({\bm{\lambda}})}{r({\bm{\lambda}})+k}\binom{r({\bm{\lambda}})+k-1}{k};

see also
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Meanwhile, we will define the term
\operatorname{type}(\pi_{{\bm{\lambda}}})\coloneqq(t_{1},\dots,t_{\lambda_{s-1}})
where
t_{k}=\left|\{j:\lambda_{j}=k\}\right|
for
1\leq k\leq\lambda_{s-1}
. And it is clear that
\left|\mathfrak{S}_{A}{\bm{z}}_{A}^{\pi_{{\bm{\lambda}}}}\right|=\frac{p({\bm{\lambda}})!}{\operatorname{type}(\pi_{{\bm{\lambda}}})!}
, where
\operatorname{type}(\pi_{{\bm{\lambda}}})!\coloneqq t_{1}!t_{2}!\cdots t_{\lambda_{s-1}}!
. Consequently, we have the following closed formula for Betti numbers.

Corollary 2.18
.

If
J
is a sparse symmetric shifted ideal, then
\beta_{i,i+d}(J)
is given by

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bm{\lambda}}\in\Lambda(J)\\
\operatorname{suppdeg}({\bm{\lambda}})=d\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{k+\ell=i}\frac{p({\bm{\lambda}})!}{\operatorname{type}(\pi_{{\bm{\lambda}}})!}\binom{p^{\vartriangle}({\bm{\lambda}})}{\ell}\binom{s}{p({\bm{\lambda}})}\binom{s-p({\bm{\lambda}})}{r({\bm{\lambda}})+k}\binom{r({\bm{\lambda}})+k-1}{k}\right).

A quick example using this formula is given in
3.4
.

.

(1)

\operatorname{dim}(R)-\operatorname{dim}(\overline{K_{j+1}}/\overline{K_{j}})=h-j
.

(2)

Similarly, if
\overline{K_{j+1}({\bm{z}})}\neq\overline{K_{j}({\bm{z}})}

In the first case,

\displaystyle\overline{K_{j+1}}/\overline{K_{j}}

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

for each

. Suppose that

K_{j}=\varphi(K_{j}({\bm{z}}))R

The argument will be similar to the proof of
[

We prove by induction on

When

be a new variable over

. For each

be a general

-form in the ideal

. Now,
consider the new homomorphism

-generic forms in

{\bm{F}}_{\bullet}\otimes_{T}R[y]

Theorem 3.8
.

Assuming

, the symbolic powers of

Proof.

We will fix a positive integer
m
and look at the symbolic power

. After applying the notations
K_{j}
and

introduced before

4.
Harbourne–Huneke containment problem for the uniform
a
-fold product ideal

Recall that for any homogeneous ideal
I
in a standard graded ring,

\alpha(I)
stands for the least degree of a homogeneous polynomial in this
ideal. This final section is mainly concerned with the following two questions
involving this invariant, which were raised for
radical
homogeneous
ideals in
[
4
, Section 3]
.

Question 4.1
.

[Demailly-like bound]
Let
R
be a polynomial ring over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
and let
I
be a
homogeneous ideal of big height
h
in
R
. Does the inequality

\frac{\alpha(I^{(n)})}{n}\geq\frac{\alpha(I^{(m)})+h-1}{m+h-1}

hold for all
n,m\geq 1
?

Notice that the
Waldschmidt constant

\widehat{\alpha}(I)\coloneqq\lim_{n}\frac{\alpha(I^{(n)})}{n}

actually agrees with
\inf_{n}\frac{\alpha(I^{n})}{n}
. Thus, the above inequality
is simply

\widehat{\alpha}(I)\geq\frac{\alpha(I^{(m)})+h-1}{m+h-1}.

Question 4.2
.

[General version of the Harbourne–Huneke containment for radical homogeneous ideal]
Let
R
be a polynomial ring over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
with the maximal
homogeneous ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. If
I
is a homogeneous ideal of big height
h

in
R
, does the inequality

I^{(\ell(h+m-1))}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\ell(h-1)}(I^{(m)})^{\ell}

holds for all
\ell,m\geq 1
?

We will drop the radical requirement and study these questions for

I=I_{a}(\mathcal{F}^{b})
under the assumptions in
3.5
. It is
not difficult to see that a positive answer to
4.2
would imply an
affirmative answer to
4.1
. Thus, we will focus on
4.2
.
Actually, like
[
4
, Theorem 3.6]
, a stronger result can be
achieved. Notice that the big height of the ideal
I_{a}(\mathcal{F}^{b})
is
h=bs-a+1

while the height is the positive integer
c_{0}
by
[
17
, Remark
4.5]
. Furthermore, we have seen in the proof of

[
17
, Proposition 4.7]
that
c_{0}=h
precisely when
b=1
or

a=bs
. The
b=1
case is just the standard star configuration case studied in

[
4
]
. Meanwhile, the
a=bs
case is the trivial one when the
ideal is actually principal. Thus, in the following, we may assume that

c_{0}<h
. Consequently,
b>1
,
a<bs
and
h-1>0
.

We present two technical lemmas first.

Lemma 4.3
.

We have

(h-1)(\mu_{a}^{0}+b(c-c_{0}))\geq c

for each
c
with
c_{0}\leq c\leq h
.

Proof.

Notice that the coefficients of
c
are
(h-1)b
and
1
on the two sides. Since
(h-1)b\geq 1
, it suffices to show the inequality for
c=c_{0}
, namely, to show

(7)

(h-1)\mu_{a}^{0}\geq c_{0}.

After putting back the definitions of
h
,
\mu_{a}^{0}
and
c_{0}
, this inequality is actually

(bs-a)\left(a-b\left\lfloor\frac{a-1}{b}\right\rfloor\right)\geq s-\left\lfloor\frac{a-1}{b}\right\rfloor.

And it can be simplified into

(bs-a)a-s\geq\left((bs-a)b-1\right)\left\lfloor\frac{a-1}{b}\right\rfloor.

Since
bs>a
and
b>1
, we have
(bs-a)b-1>0
. Thus, it suffices to show that

(bs-a)a-s\geq\left((bs-a)b-1\right)\cdot\frac{a-1}{b},

which is equivalent to saying that

(b-1)(bs-a)\geq 1.

However, the last inequality is clear. Thus, we have successfully shown the inequality (
7
). And this completes our proof for
4.3
.
∎

In the following, for each positive integer
c
such that
c_{0}\leq c\leq h
, we write
\widehat{\mu}_{c}
for the positive integer
\mu_{a}^{0}+b(c-c_{0})
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
k,\ell
, and
m
be positive integers.
Then, we have

(\ell(h+m-1)-h+k)\widehat{\mu}_{c}-c(\ell-1)\geq\ell m\widehat{\mu}_{c}

for each
c
with
c_{0}\leq c\leq h
.

Proof.

This artificially irregular inequality can be quickly modified into

(8)

\ell\left((h-1)\widehat{\mu}_{c}-c\right)\geq(h-k)\widehat{\mu}_{c}-c.

Since
(h-1)\widehat{\mu}_{c}\geq c
by
4.3
, we only need to show (
8
) for
\ell=1
, i.e., to show that

(h-1)\widehat{\mu}_{c}-c\geq(h-k)\widehat{\mu}_{c}-c.

However, this holds automatically, since both
k
and
\widehat{\mu}_{c}
are positive integers.
∎

Now, we are ready for the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.5
.

Under the assumptions in
3.5
, the ideal

I=I_{a}(\mathcal{F}^{b})
satisfies

(9)

I^{(\ell(h+m-1)-h+k)}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{d((\ell-1)(h-1)+k-1)\widehat{\mu}_{h}}(I^{(m)})^{\ell}

for all positive integers
k,\ell
and
m
.

Note that
\widehat{\mu}_{h}
above is simply
\mu_{a}^{0}+b(h-c_{0})=bh-(bs-a)
.

Proof.

Firstly, we consider the standard monomial case when

I=I_{a}(\mathcal{F}_{{\bm{z}}}^{b})
for
\mathcal{F}_{\bm{z}}=\{z_{1},\dots,z_{s}\}
in

T={\mathbb{K}}[z_{1},\dots,z_{s}]
. Within this ring, by abuse of notation, we will
write
{\bm{z}}^{\bm{\mu}}\coloneqq z_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots z_{s}^{\mu_{s}}
for any tuple

{\bm{\mu}}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{s}
. As usual,
|{\bm{\mu}}|
is simply

\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{s}
, and
{\bm{\mu}}_{\leq c}
is short for the sub-tuple

(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{c})
. Since the permutation group
\mathfrak{S}_{s}
has a
natural action on
T
, for any monomial ideal
K
that is invariant under this
action, we will introduce

\mathcal{P}(K)\coloneqq\Set{{\bm{\lambda}}\text{ is a partition of length $s$}:{\bm{z}}^{{\bm{\lambda}}}\in K}

and, by abuse of notation,

\Lambda(K)\coloneqq\Set{{\bm{\lambda}}:{\bm{z}}^{{\bm{\lambda}}}\text{ is a minimal monomial generator of $K$}}.

Take an arbitrary partition
{\bm{\lambda}}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s})\in\mathcal{P}(I_{a}(\mathcal{F}_{{\bm{z}}}^{b})^{(m^{\prime})})
where
m^{\prime}\coloneqq\ell(h+m-1)-h+k
.
Due to the decomposition in (
6
),
this amounts to saying that

{\bm{\lambda}}\in\mathcal{P}(I_{c,\mathcal{F}_{{\bm{z}}}}^{(m^{\prime}\widehat{\mu}_{c})})\qquad\text{for each $c$ with $c_{0}\leq c\leq h$,}

namely,

|{\bm{\lambda}}_{\leq c}|\geq m^{\prime}\widehat{\mu}_{c}\qquad\text{for each $c$ with $c_{0}\leq c\leq h$}

by
[
3
, Proposition 4.1]
.

In the following, for each
j\leq h
, we write
\lambda_{j}^{\prime}\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\ell}\right\rfloor
and
\widehat{\lambda}_{j}\coloneqq\lambda_{j}-\ell\lambda_{j}^{\prime}
. Since
\widehat{\lambda}_{j}\leq\ell-1
for such

j
, we have
\sum_{j\leq c}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}\leq c(\ell-1)
. Thus,

\sum_{j\leq c}\ell\lambda_{j}^{\prime}=\sum_{j\leq c}(\lambda_{j}-\widehat{\lambda}_{j})=\sum_{j\leq c}\lambda_{j}-\sum_{j\leq c}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}\\
\geq m^{\prime}\widehat{\mu}_{c}-c(\ell-1).

As a quick consequence of
4.4
, we have

\sum_{j\leq c}\lambda_{j}^{\prime}\geq m\widehat{\mu}_{c}

for each such
c
. It follows again from
[
3
, Proposition 4.1]
and the decomposition in (
6
)
that

{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}\coloneqq(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime},\dots,\lambda_{h}^{\prime},\lambda_{h}^{\prime},\dots,\lambda_{h}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{P}(I_{a}(\mathcal{F}_{{\bm{z}}}^{b})^{(m)}).

Therefore, we can find suitable partition

{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime\prime}=(\lambda_{1}^{\prime\prime},\dots,\lambda_{s}^{\prime\prime})\in\Lambda(I_{a}(\mathcal{F}_{{\bm{z}}}^{b})^{(m)})
such that
{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime\prime}\leq{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}

componentwise. In the part (b) of the proof of
[
17
, Proposition
4.7]
, we have shown that
|{\bm{\lambda}}_{\leq h}^{\prime\prime}|=m\widehat{\mu}_{h}
. Furthermore,
\lambda_{j}^{\prime\prime}=\lambda_{h}^{\prime\prime}

whenever
h\leq j\leq s
. Now,
\ell{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime\prime}\leq{\bm{\lambda}}
componentwise.
And it remains to estimate
|{\bm{\lambda}}|-\ell|{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime\prime}|
. It is clear
that

\displaystyle|{\bm{\lambda}}|-\ell|{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime\prime}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{s}({\bm{\lambda}}_{j}-\ell{\bm{\lambda}}_{j}^{\prime\prime})\geq\sum_{j=1}^{h}({\bm{\lambda}}_{j}-\ell{\bm{\lambda}}_{j}^{\prime\prime})=|{\bm{\lambda}}_{\leq h}|-\ell|{\bm{\lambda}}_{\leq h}^{\prime\prime}|

\displaystyle\geq m^{\prime}\widehat{\mu}_{h}-\ell m\widehat{\mu}_{h}=(\ell(h+m-1)-h+k-\ell m)\widehat{\mu}_{h}

\displaystyle=\left((\ell-1)(h-1)+k-1\right)\widehat{\mu}_{h}.

Consequently, for
I=I_{a}(\mathcal{F}_{{\bm{z}}}^{b})
, we have

(10)

I^{(\ell(h+m-1)-h+k)}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\left((\ell-1)(h-1)+k-1\right)\widehat{\mu}_{h}}(I^{(m)})^{\ell},

establishing the containment (
9
) in this standard monomial case.

In the final step, we consider the general uniform star configuration case,
namely when
I=I_{a}(\mathcal{F}^{b})
. Notice that both
I^{(\ell(h+m-1)-h+k)}
and

I^{(m)}
are generated by monomials in the forms in
\mathcal{F}
;
cf.
[
17
, Proposition 4.11]
. Thus, it follows directly
from (
10
) that

I^{(\ell(h+m-1)-h+k)}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{d\left((\ell-1)(h-1)+k-1\right)\widehat{\mu}_{h}}(I^{(m)})^{\ell},

where
d
is the common degree of the forms in
\mathcal{F}
.
∎

The following result generalizes
[
4
, Corollary 3.7]
.

Corollary 4.6
.

The Harbourne–Huneke type containment
(
4.2
)
and the Demailly-like bound
(
4.1
)
hold for the ideal
I=I_{a}(\mathcal{F}^{b})
under the assumptions in
3.5
.

Proof.

The Harbourne–Huneke type containment follows from (
9
), since both
d
and
\widehat{\mu}_{h}
are positive integers, while
k
can be specially taken to be
h
. By applying the identical arguments in the proof of
[
4
, Corollary 3.7]
, we see that the Demailly-like bound also holds.
∎

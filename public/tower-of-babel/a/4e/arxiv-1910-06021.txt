Introduction & Main Results

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the family of functions
f
that are analytic in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z:\,|z|<1\}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}_{1}
is the subset of
\mathcal{A}
with the normalization
f(0)=1
.
A single valued function
f\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
is said to be univalent in a
domain
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{C}
if
f
is one-to-one in
\Delta
.
The class of all univalent functions
with the normalization
f(0)=0=f^{\prime}(0)-1
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}
.
Let
\Omega
be the family of functions
\omega
, regular in
\mathbb{D}
and
satisfying the conditions
\omega(0)=0
and
|\omega(z)|<1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
For
f,g\in\mathcal{A}
, the function
f
is said to be subordinate to
g
, denoted by
f\prec g

if and only if there exists an analytic function
\omega\in\Omega

such that
f=g\circ\omega
.
In particular, if
g
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
then
f(0)=g(0)

and
f(\mathbb{D})\subseteq g(\mathbb{D})
hold.

The function
zf(z)\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
is
starlike of order
\lambda
if
{\rm Re}\left(\dfrac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)>\lambda

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
0\leq\lambda<1
.
The class of all starlike functions, denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}(\lambda)
is a subclass of
\mathcal{S}
.
The
n^{th}
partial sum
s_{n}(f(z))
of
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
is given by

s_{n}(f(z))=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}z^{k},n=0,1,2,\ldots
.
For more details about the univalent functions, its subclasses and subordination properties, we
refer
[
2
,
3
,
5
]
.

The concept of stable functions was first introduced by Ruscheweyh and Salinas

[
6
]
, while discussing the class of starlike functions
of order
\lambda
, where
1/2\leq\lambda<1
. However, the class of starlike functions
of order
\lambda\in[1/2,1)
is comparatively a much narrow class but it has many
interesting properties too. Ruscheweyh and Salinas
[
6
]

proved the following result.

Theorem 1
.

[
6
]

Let
\lambda\in[1/2,1)
and
zf\in\mathcal{S}^{\ast}(\lambda)
, then

\displaystyle\frac{s_{n}(f,z)}{f(z)}\prec(1-z)^{\lambda},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},z\in\mathbb{D}.

Theorem
1
has several applications in Gegenbauer
polynomial sums and motivated by Theorem
1
,
Ruscheweyh and Salinas
[
6
]
introduced the concept
of Stable functions which is stated as follows.
For some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a function
F
is said to be
n
-stable function
with respect to
G
if

\displaystyle\frac{s_{n}(F(z))}{F(z)}\prec\frac{1}{G(z)},\quad\mbox{for $F,G\in\mathcal{A}_{1}$ and $z\in\mathbb{D}$}.

Moreover, the function
F
is said to be stable with respect to
G
, if
F
is

n
-stable with respect to
G
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Particularly, if the function
F
is
n
-stable with respect to itself. Then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

F
is stable. In the present context, for
-1\leq B<A\leq 1
, we define a function

\displaystyle v_{\lambda}(A,B,z):=\left(\frac{1+Az}{1+Bz}\right)^{\lambda}\quad\mbox{ for $z\in\mathbb{D}$ and $\lambda\in(0,1]$}.

For
\lambda=1/2
, Ruscheweyh and Salinas
[
7
]

proved that
v_{1/2}(1,-1,z)
is stable function with respect to itself.
The stability of
v_{1/2}(1,-1,z)
is equivalent to the simultaneous
non-negativity of general class of sine and cosine sums given by Vietoris
[
11
]
,
the most celebrated theorem of positivity of trigonometric sums.
Ruscheweyh and Salinas
[
7
]

conjectured that
v_{\lambda}(1,-1,z)
is stable for
0<\lambda<1/2
.
Using computer algebra, for
\lambda=1/4
it was shown in
[
7
]

that
v_{1/4}(1,-1,z)
is
n
-stable for
n=1,2,3,\cdots,5000
. In the limiting case,
the validation of stability of
v_{\lambda}(1,-1,z)
for
0<\lambda<1/2
interpreted in terms of
positivity of trigonometric polynomials.

Further extensions of Vietoris Theorem and stable functions to Cesàro stable functions
and Generalized Cesàro stable functions have been
studied in
[
4
]
and
[
9
]
respectively.
In this direction, conjectures are also proposed in
[
9
]
that
linked Generalized Cesàro stable functions with the positivity of trigonometric sums.
Chakraborty and Vasudevarao
[
1
]
considered
A=1-2\alpha
,
B=-1

and proved the following result.

Theorem 2
.

[
1
]

For
0<\lambda\leq 1
and
1/2\leq\alpha<1
,

v_{\lambda}(1-2\alpha,-1,z)=\left(\dfrac{1+(1-2\alpha)z}{1-z}\right)^{\lambda}

is stable with respect to
v_{\lambda}(0,-1,z)=\dfrac{1}{(1-z)^{\lambda}}
.

Chakraborty and Vasudevarao
[
1
]
also proved that

v_{\lambda}(1-2\alpha,-1,z)
is not stable with repsect to itself when

1/2<\alpha<1
and
0<\lambda\leq 1
. For
\lambda=1
, the function

v_{1}(A,B,z)=\dfrac{1+Az}{1+Bz}
have been studied widely by many researchers.
The analytic functions of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
subordinate
to
\dfrac{1+Az}{1+Bz}
have been studied by Janowski
[
3
]
and
the class of such functions is denoted by
\mathcal{P}(A,B)
. The functions of

\mathcal{P}(A,B)
are called Janowski functions. Moreover, the set of functions

zf\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
, for which
\dfrac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\dfrac{1+Az}{1+Bz}
holds, called Janowski
starlike functions and the class of such functions is denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}(A,B)
. It can be easily seen that

\mathcal{S}^{\ast}(1,-1)\equiv\mathcal{S}^{\ast}
.

In this paper, we show that
v_{\lambda}(A,B,z)
is stable with respect to

v_{\lambda}(0,B,z)=1/(1+Bz)^{\lambda}
for
0<\lambda\leq 1

and
-1\leq B<A\leq 0
. Further,
v_{\lambda}(A,B,z)
is not stable with respect
to itself, when
0<\lambda\leq 1
and
-1\leq B<A<0
.
We can write
v_{\lambda}(A,B,z)
as,

\displaystyle v_{\lambda}(A,B,z)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1+Az}{1+Bz}\right)^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\left({1+Az}\right)^{\lambda}\left({1+Bz}\right)^{-\lambda}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{[\lambda]_{k}}{k!}A^{k}z^{k}\right)\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda)_{k}}{k!}(-B)^{k}z^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{[\lambda]_{k}}{k!}\frac{(\lambda)_{n-k}}{(n-k)!}A^{k}(-B)^{n-k}\right)z^{n},

(1)

where
[\lambda]_{k}
and
(\lambda)_{k}
denote the factorial polynomials given as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[\lambda]_{k}=&\lambda(\lambda-1)(\lambda-2)(\lambda-3)\cdots(\lambda-k+1),\quad\mbox{ and }\\
(\lambda)_{k}=&\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+k)}{\Gamma(\lambda)}=\lambda(\lambda+1)\cdots(\lambda+k-1),\quad\mbox{ Where $\Gamma$ is well-known gamma function, }\end{array}\right.

for
k=1,2,\cdots
respectively with
[\lambda]_{0}=1=(\lambda)_{0}
. So
v_{\lambda}(A,B,z)

can be written as

\displaystyle v_{\lambda}(A,B,z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(A,B,\lambda)z^{n},

where

\displaystyle a_{n}:=a_{n}(A,B,\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{[\lambda]_{k}}{k!}\frac{(\lambda)_{n-k}}{(n-k)!}A^{k}(-B)^{n-k}.

Now, we state two lemmas which will helpful to prove our main results.

Lemma 1
.

For
0<\lambda\leq 1
and
-1\leq B<A\leq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{[\lambda]_{k}}{k!}\frac{(\lambda)_{n-k}}{(n-k)!}A^{k}(-B)^{n-k}>0.

Lemma 2
.

Let
v_{\lambda}(A,B,z)
be defined by (
1
). Then for
\lambda\in(0,1]
and
-1\leq B<A\leq 0
,

\displaystyle(m+1)(n+1)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\frac{[\lambda]_{k}}{k!}\frac{(\lambda)_{n+1-k}}{(n+1-k)!}A^{k}B^{n+1-k}\right)

\displaystyle-mn\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{[\lambda]_{k}}{k!}\frac{(\lambda)_{n-k}}{(n-k)!}A^{k}B^{n-k}\right)\geq 0

(2)

holds for all
m,\ n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now, we state main results of this paper which are about the stability of
v_{\lambda}(A,B,z)

with repsect to
v_{\lambda}(0,B,z)

\displaystyle\frac{s_{n}(v_{\mu}(0,B,z))}{v_{\lambda}(0,B,z)}\prec\frac{1}{v_{\lambda}(0,B,z)},\qquad\mbox{ for $z\in\mathbb{D}$}.

Proof of Main Results

Proof of Lemma
1
.

Consider,

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=(1-z)^{\lambda}(1-z)^{-\lambda}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{[\lambda]_{k}}{k!}\frac{(\lambda)_{n-k}}{(n-k)!}(-1)^{k}\right)z^{n}

Comparing the coefficients of
z^{n}
on both the sides we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{[\lambda]_{k}}{k!}\frac{(\lambda)_{n-k}}{(n-k)!}(-1)^{k}=0,

which can be expanded as

\displaystyle\frac{(\lambda)(\lambda+1)\cdots(\lambda+n-1)}{(n)!}+\frac{(\lambda)(\lambda+1)\cdots(\lambda+n-2)}{(n-1)!}\left(\frac{\lambda}{1!}\right)(-1)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{(\lambda)(\lambda+1)\cdots(\lambda+n-3)}{(n-2)!}\frac{\lambda(\lambda-1)}{2!}(-1)^{2}+\cdots+\frac{\lambda}{1!}\frac{(\lambda)(\lambda-1)\cdots(\lambda-n+2)}{(n-1)!}(-1)^{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{(\lambda)(\lambda-1)\cdots(\lambda-n+1)}{n!}(-1)^{n}=0.

Since
0\leq\lambda<1
, so only first term in the above equation is positive. By multiplying

2^{nd},3^{rd},\cdots
,
(n+1)^{th}
terms by
\dfrac{\alpha}{\beta}
,
\dfrac{\alpha^{2}}{\beta^{2}}
,
\cdots
,

\dfrac{\alpha^{n}}{\beta^{n}}
respectively, we obtain for
0\leq\alpha<\beta
,

\displaystyle\frac{(\lambda)(\lambda+1)\cdots(\lambda+n-1)}{(n)!}+\frac{(\lambda)(\lambda+1)\cdots(\lambda+n-2)}{(n-1)!}\left(\frac{\lambda}{1!}\right)(-1)\frac{\alpha}{\beta}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(\lambda)(\lambda+1)\cdots(\lambda+n-3)}{(n-2)!}\frac{\lambda(\lambda-1)}{2!}\left(\frac{-\alpha}{\beta}\right)^{2}+\cdots+\frac{\lambda}{1!}\frac{(\lambda)(\lambda-1)\cdots(\lambda-n+2)}{(n-1)!}\left(\frac{-\alpha}{\beta}\right)^{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{(\lambda)(\lambda-1)\cdots(\lambda-n+1)}{n!}\left(\frac{-\alpha}{\beta}\right)^{n}\geq 0.

After multiplying by
\beta^{n}
we obtain

\displaystyle\beta^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{[\lambda]_{k}}{k!}\frac{(\lambda)_{n-k}}{(n-k)!}(-1)^{k}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right)^{k}\geq 0

(3)

By substituting
\alpha=-A
,
\beta=-B
in (
3
)
so that for
-1\leq B<A\leq 0
, the lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2
.

Let
v_{\lambda}(A,B,z)
be defined by (
1
). Then,

\displaystyle v_{\lambda}(A,B,z)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1+Az}{1+Bz}\right)^{\lambda}=1+a_{1}z+a_{2}z^{2}+a_{3}z^{3}+\cdots

\displaystyle v_{\lambda}^{\prime}(A,B,z)

\displaystyle=\lambda\left(\frac{1+Az}{1+Bz}\right)^{\lambda-1}\left(\frac{(1+Bz)A-(1+Az)B}{(1+Bz)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda(A-B)\frac{(1+Az)^{\lambda-1}}{(1+Bz)^{\lambda+1}}

\displaystyle(1+Bz)v_{\lambda}^{\prime}(A,B,z)

\displaystyle=\lambda(A-B)(1+Az)^{\lambda-1}(1+Bz)^{-\lambda}

(4)

Since
0>A>B
,
0<\lambda\leq 1
,
(1+Az)^{\lambda-1}=1+(\lambda-1)Az+\frac{(\lambda-1)(\lambda-2)}{2!}A^{2}z^{2}+\cdots
and

have positive Taylor series coefficients.
A simple computation yields that

\displaystyle(1+Bz)v_{\lambda}^{\prime}(A,B,z)

\displaystyle=(a_{1}+2a_{2}z+3a_{3}z^{2}+\cdots)(1+Bz)

\displaystyle=a_{1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}((n+1)a_{n+1}+Bna_{n})z^{n}.

(5)

Since right hand side of (

\displaystyle(n+1)a_{n+1}+Bna_{n}>0,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(6)

The left hand side of the expression given in (

\displaystyle(m+1)(n+1)a_{n+1}+mnBa_{n}.

(7)

Equivalently, (

\displaystyle m((n+1)a_{n+1}+Bna_{n})+(n+1)a_{n+1}.

Using (

Proof of Theorem

.

To show that

is stable with respect to
v_{\lambda}(0,B,z)

it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\frac{s_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)}{v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z}\prec\frac{1}{v_{\lambda}(0,B,z)},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}

for all

, i.e., to prove that

\displaystyle\frac{(1+Bz)^{\lambda}s_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)}{(1+Az)^{\lambda}}\prec(1+Bz)^{\lambda},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

which can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle\frac{(1+Bz)s_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}}{(1+Az)}\prec(1+Bz).

To show that , it is enough to prove that

\displaystyle\left|\displaystyle\frac{(1+Bz)s_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}}{(1+Az)}-1\right|\leq|B|\leq 1,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

For fixed
n
and
\lambda
, we consider the following function

\displaystyle h(z)=1-\displaystyle\frac{(1+Bz)s_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}}{(1+Az)},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle v^{\prime}_{\lambda}(A,B,z)=\lambda(A-B)\frac{(1+Az)^{\lambda-1}}{(1+Bz)^{\lambda+1}}=\lambda(A-B)\frac{v_{\lambda}(A,B,z)}{(1+Bz)(1+Az)},

which can be rewritten in the following form

\displaystyle v_{\lambda}(A,B,z)-\frac{(1+(A+B)z+ABz^{2})}{\lambda(A-B)}v^{\prime}_{\lambda}(A,B,z)=0\quad\mbox{for $\ z\in\mathbb{D}$}.

(9)

A simple calculations gives that

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{A-B}{(1+Az)^{2}}s_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)^{1/\lambda}-\frac{(1+Bz)}{(1+Az)\lambda}s_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}s^{\prime}_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)

\displaystyle=\frac{(A-B)s_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}}{(1+Az)^{2}}\left(s_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)-\frac{(1+Az)(1+Bz)}{(A-B)\lambda}s^{\prime}_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)\right)

(10)

Using relations (
8
) in (
10
), we get

\begin{split}h^{\prime}(z)=&\frac{(A-B)s_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}}{(1+Az)^{2}}\bigg[s_{n}\left(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z)-\frac{(1+Az)(1+Bz)}{(A-B)\lambda}v^{\prime}_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{(n+1)}{\lambda(A-B)}\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\frac{[\lambda]_{k}}{k!}\frac{(\lambda)_{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}A^{k}(-B)^{n-k+1}z^{n}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\frac{nAB}{\lambda(A-B)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{[\lambda]_{k}}{k!}\frac{(\lambda)_{n-k}}{(n-k)!}A^{k}(-B)^{n-k}z^{n+1}\bigg].\end{split}

(11)

Substituting (
9
) in (
11
) and using
definition of
a_{n}
, the following form of
h^{\prime}(z)
can be obtained.

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{z^{n}s_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}}{\lambda(1+Az)^{2}}\left((n+1)a_{n+1}-ABzna_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{z^{n}s_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}}{\lambda}\left((n+1)a_{n+1}-ABzna_{n}\right)(1+Az)^{-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{z^{n}s_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}}{\lambda}\left((n+1)a_{n+1}-ABzna_{n}\right)(1-2Az+3A^{2}z^{2}-4A^{3}z^{3}+\cdots)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{z^{n}s_{n}(v_{\lambda}(A,B,z),z)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}}{\lambda}\left((n+1)a_{n+1}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(m+1)(n+1)a_{n+1}+mnBa_{n}(-A)^{m}z^{m}\right).

Since

, we have
-A\geq 0
. Therefore in view of Lemma

,
we obtain

for all

. Further, from Lemma

\displaystyle(n+1)a_{n+1}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}[(m+1)(n+1)a_{n+1}+Bmna_{n}](-A)^{m}z^{m}

Hence,

\displaystyle|h^{\prime}(z)|\leq h^{\prime}(|z|),\quad\mbox{ for all $z\in\mathbb{D}$ holds.}

(12)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{(1+Bz)(s_{n}(v_{(}A,B,z),z))^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}}{(1+Az)}-1\right|<1,\quad\ z\in\mathbb{D}.

which implies that

Introduction

A partition of a positive integer
n
is a non-increasing sequence of positive integers whose sum is n. If
p(n)
denote the number of partition of n, then the generating function of n is given by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}

where, as customary, for any complex number a and
|q|<1
,

(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-aq^{n-1}).

Andrews
[
1
]
introduced the function
\mathscr{EO}^{*}(n)
, which counts the number of integer partitions of
n
with all even parts smaller than all odd parts, where the only part appearing an odd number of times is the largest even part. He also studied its many interesting properties including the generating function, simply,
\frac{1}{2}(\nu(q)+\nu(-q))
, where

\nu(q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}+n}}{(-q;q^{2})_{n+1}}

is of Watson’s third order mock theta function.
Chern
[
5
]
, provided a combinatorial proof of the generating function for
\mathscr{EO}^{*}(n)
and studied several further properties in
[
6
]
.

Burson and Eichhorn in
[
3
]
generalized
\mathscr{EO}^{*}(n)
by introducing new partition-theoretic objects called copartitions, which reveal an inherent symmetry in partitions counted by
\mathscr{EO}^{*}(n)
that was not previously obvious. Copartitions are counted by the function
cp_{a,b,m}(n)
, where
cp_{1,1,2}(n)=\mathscr{EO}^{*}(2n)
.

Definition 1.1
.

An
(a,b,m)
-copartition is a triple of partition
(\gamma,\rho,\sigma)
, where each of the parts of
\gamma
is at least
a
and congruent to
a\,\,(mod\,m)
, each of the parts of
\gamma
is at least
b
and congruent to
b\,\,(mod\,m)
, and
\rho
has the same number of parts
\sigma
, each of which have size equal to
m
times the number of parts
\gamma
.

Example 1.1
.

The
(1,3,4)
-copartitions of size
12
are

(\{9,1^{3}\},{\O},{\O}),(\{5^{2},1^{2}\},{\O},{\O}),(\{5,1^{7}\},{\O},{\O}),(\{1^{12}\},{\O},{\O})

(\{5\},\{4\},\{3\}),(\{1\},\{4\},\{7\}),and\,\,({\O},{\O},\{3^{4}\})

Thus
cp_{1,3,4}(12)=7
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
3
, Theorem 3.5]

cp_{1,1,2}(n)=\mathscr{EO}^{*}(2n)
.

Now the generating function for the copartitions can be given as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

[
3
, Theorem 3.8]

Define
cp_{a,b,m}(w,s,n)
to be the number of
(a,b,m)
-copartitions of size
n
that have
w
ground parts and
s
sky parts. Then,

cp_{a,b,m}(x,y,q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{w=0}^{\infty}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}cp_{a,b,m}x^{s}y^{w}q^{n}=\frac{(xyq^{a+b};q^{m})_{\infty}}{(xq^{b};q^{m})_{\infty}(yq^{a};q^{m})_{\infty}}

(1.1)

The following special cases has been discussed.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
3
, Theorem 4.5]

cp_{1,1,1}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}p(k).

(1.2)

Theorem 1.4
.

[
3
, Theorem 4.10]

cp_{0,1,1}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p(k)d(n-k).

(1.3)

where
d(n)
is the number of divisions of
n
.

Theorem 1.5
.

[
3
, Theorem 4.16]

cp_{0,0,1}(n)=-p(n)+2\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}p(k)d(n-k).

(1.4)

where
d(n)
is the number of divisions of
n
.

Preliminary Results

In this section, we list few dissection formulas which are useful in proving our main results. As customary, we use

f_{k}=f(-q^{k})=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{nk})=(q^{k};q^{k})_{\infty},\,\,for\,\,k\,\,\geq 1.

.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
7
, Theorem 2.2]

For any prime
p\geq 5
,

f(-q)\,=\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-\frac{p-1}{2}\\
k\neq\frac{\pm p-1}{6}\end{subarray}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(-1)^{k}q^{\frac{3k^{2}+k}{2}}f(-q^{\frac{3p^{2}-(6k+1)p}{2}},-q^{\frac{3p^{2}+(6k+1)p}{2}})+(-1)^{\frac{\pm p-1}{6}}q^{\frac{p^{2}-1}{24}}f(-q^{p^{2}}).

(2.1)

where
\pm
depends on the conditions that
(\pm p-1)/6
should be an integer. Moreover, note that
(3k^{2}+k)/2\ncong(p^{2}-1)/24(\mod p)
as k runs through the range of the summation.

Lemma 2.2
.

For positive integers
k
and
m
, we have

\displaystyle f_{2k}^{m}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle f_{k}^{2m}(mod\,\,2)

(2.2)

\displaystyle f_{3k}^{m}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle f_{k}^{3m}(mod\,\,3)

(2.3)

3
Main Results

In this section, we state and prove our main results.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any prime
p\geq 5
and
\alpha,n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}cp_{3,1,4}\left(p^{2\alpha}n+5\frac{p^{2\alpha}-1}{24}\right)q^{n}\cong f^{5}(-q)\,\,(mod\,\,2).

(3.1)

Proof.

Setting
x=y=1
,
a=3
,
b=1
and
m=4
in (
1.1
), we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}cp_{3,1,4}q^{n}=\frac{(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{4})_{\infty}(q^{3};q^{4})_{\infty}}=\frac{(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}}{(q:q^{2})_{\infty}}=\frac{f_{4}f_{2}}{f_{1}}.

(3.2)

Applying (
2.2
) in (
3.2
), we obtain

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}cp_{3,1,4}(n)q^{n}\cong\,\,f_{1}^{5}\,\,(mod\,\,2).

(3.3)

Applying (
2.1
)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}cp_{3,1,4}\left(p^{2\alpha}n+5\frac{p^{2\alpha}-1}{24}\right)q^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-\frac{p-1}{2}\\
k\neq\pm\frac{p-1}{6}\end{subarray}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(-1)^{k}q^{\frac{3k^{2}+k}{2}}f(-q^{\frac{3p^{2}-(6k+1)p}{2}},-q^{\frac{3p^{2}+(6k+1)p}{2}})+(-1)^{\pm\frac{p-1}{6}}q^{\frac{p^{2}-1}{24}}f(-q^{p^{2}})\right)^{5}(mod\,\,2)

(3.4)

Extracting the term containing
q^{pn+5\frac{p^{2}-1}{24}}
from both sides of (
3.4
), and replacing
q^{p}
by
q
, we obtain

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}cp_{3,1,4}\left(p^{2\alpha+1}n+5\frac{p^{2\alpha+1}-1}{24}\right)q^{n}\cong\,\,f^{5}(-q^{p})\,\,(mod\,\,2).

(3.5)

Again extracting the term containing
q^{pn}
from both sides of (
3.5
) and replacing
q^{p}
by
q
, we arrive at

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}cp_{3,1,4}\left(p^{2\alpha+2}n+5\frac{p^{2\alpha+2}-1}{24}\right)q^{n}\cong\,\,f^{5}(-q)\,\,(mod\,\,2).

(3.6)

which is the
\alpha+1
term of (
3.1
).
∎

,

(3.7)

where
j=1,2,\cdots p-1
.

Proof.

Comparing the coefficients of
q^{pn+j}
,
1\leq j\leq p-1
in (
3.5
), we easily obtain (
3.7
).
∎

Corollary 3.2

,
\alpha\geq 0

for

According to (

For the primes

Concluding Remarks

On the odderon mechanism for transverse single spin asymmetry in the Wandzura-Wilczek approximation Preprint: ZTF-EP-22-05
Sanjin Benić, Davor Horvatić, Abhiram Kaushik, Eric Andreas Vivoda

Abstract
We compute the transverse single spin asymmetry in forward p ↑ ​ p → h ​ X p^{\uparrow}p\to hX and p ↑ ​ A → h ​ X p^{\uparrow}A\to hX collisions from the odderon mechanism originally suggested by Kovchegov and Sievert Kovchegov:2012ga . Working in the hybrid approach of the Color Glass Condensate effective theory we firstly identify the relevant collinear parton distribution function (PDF) of the transversely polarized proton p ↑ p^{\uparrow} as the intrinsic twist-3 g T ​ ( x ) g_{T}(x) distribution. We further argue that the complete polarized cross section also contains contributions from the kinematical and the dynamical twist-3 PDFs, in addition to the intrinsic twist-3 PDF. By restricting to the Wandzura-Wilczek approximation, where the dynamical twist-3 PDFs are dropped, we find that the odderon contribution to the polarized cross section for inclusive hadron production is exactly zero at the next-to-leading order in the strong coupling.

III.2
Proof that the real contribution in the
q\to q
channel vanishes

We now argue that in fact (
35
) is exactly zero. Before performing an explicit computation we can appreciate it in an intuitive way as follows. In general for the polarized cross section to be non-zero we need two vectors: the transverse momentum of the final state and the spin so that we can form the familiar cross product
\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}\times\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}
. In case of (
35
), we have
\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}
, while we can think of
\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}
as a proxy for the spin, thanks to the derivative
S_{\perp}^{\lambda}\partial/\partial k_{1\perp}^{\lambda}
. However, owing to the particular form of the hard factor (
36
), the two vectors
\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}
and
\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}
enter the cross section only through the linear combination
\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}\equiv\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}-\bar{z}\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}
(the soft part of the cross section is independent of
\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}
). Thus the final result depends only on a single vector,
\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}
, and therefore must be zero.

To see the above statement explicitly we start by switching to the coordinates

\begin{split}&\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}=\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}\,,\qquad\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}=\frac{\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}}{2}\,,\\
&\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}\,,\qquad\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}^{\prime}=\frac{\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}}{2}\,,\end{split}

(39)

to obtain

\begin{split}{\rm tr}\left[\gamma_{5}\not{k}_{1}S^{(0)}(k_{1})\right]&=\mathrm{i}g^{2}N_{c}\frac{q^{+}}{P_{p}^{+}}\int_{\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}}\int_{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{\prime}}\\
&\times\left[\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}^{\prime})-\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}^{\prime})\right]\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp})\,.\end{split}

(40)

40

\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}-\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{\prime})\,.

(41)

This is an important point because
\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},-\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})=-\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})
and so an integral over
\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}
would superficially vanish simply via
\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}\to-\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}
. Next, in order to de-convolve the transverse integrals in (
40

(42)

and similarly for
\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})

40
) becomes

\begin{split}{\rm tr}[\gamma_{5}\not{k}_{1}S^{(0)}(k_{1})]&=\mathrm{i}g^{2}N_{c}\frac{q^{+}}{P_{p}^{+}}\int_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}}\left[\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}^{\prime})-\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}^{\prime})\right]\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp})\,,\end{split}

(43)

\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}=\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}

\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}=\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}

\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}^{\prime}=-\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}

\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})\propto(\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})

\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\sim(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})

;
Dong:2018wsp
;
Boussarie:2019vmk

(44)

Introducing
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1\perp}\equiv\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}-\bar{z}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}/2

\boldsymbol{\delta}_{2\perp}\equiv\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}-\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}/2

\boldsymbol{v}_{1\perp}\times\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1\perp}\times\boldsymbol{\delta}_{2\perp}-\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1\perp}\times\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}-\bar{z}\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}^{\prime}\times\boldsymbol{\delta}_{2\perp}+\bar{z}\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}\times\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}^{\prime}\,,

(45)

\begin{split}&\boldsymbol{v}_{1\perp}^{2}=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1\perp}^{2}+\bar{z}^{2}\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}^{\prime 2}-2\bar{z}\kappa^{\prime}_{\perp}\delta_{1\perp}\cos(\phi_{\kappa^{\prime}}-\phi_{\delta_{1}})\,,\\
&\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}^{2}=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{2\perp}^{2}+\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}^{2}-2\kappa_{\perp}\delta_{2\perp}\cos(\phi_{\kappa}-\phi_{\delta_{2}})\,,\end{split}

(46)

Now we compute the integrals over
\phi_{\kappa}
and
\phi_{\kappa^{\prime}}
. From the first term in (
45
) we obtain

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{\kappa}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{\kappa^{\prime}}}{2\pi}(\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}^{\prime})\frac{(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1\perp}\times\boldsymbol{\delta}_{2\perp})}{\boldsymbol{v}_{1\perp}^{2}\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}^{2}}=-\frac{1}{4}\frac{z}{\bar{z}}(\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})\frac{(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}\times\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})}{|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{2\perp}^{2}-\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}^{2}|}\left(1-\frac{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1\perp}^{2}+\bar{z}^{2}\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}^{\prime 2}}{|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1\perp}^{2}-\bar{z}^{2}\boldsymbol{\kappa}_{\perp}^{\prime 2}|}\right)\,.

(47)

where we used
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1\perp}\times\boldsymbol{\delta}_{2\perp}=-z(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}\times\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})/2
. Inserting now the definition of
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{1\perp}
we will in general have an expression of the type

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi_{\Delta}}{2\pi}\sin(\phi_{q_{1}}-\phi_{\Delta})f\left(\cos(\phi_{q_{1}}-\phi_{\Delta})\right)=\int_{\phi_{q_{1}}}^{\phi_{q_{1}}-2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi}{2\pi}\sin\phi f(\cos\phi)\,,

(48)

but this is simply zero as

\int_{\phi_{1}}^{\phi_{1}-2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\sin\phi f(\cos\phi)=-\int_{\phi_{1}}^{\phi_{1}-2\pi}\mathrm{d}(\cos\phi)f(\cos\phi)=F(\cos\phi)|_{\phi_{1}}^{\phi_{1}-2\pi}=0\,,

(49)

where
F(\cos\phi)
is a primitive function of
f(\cos\phi)
.
By a completely analogous computation we can show that each of the remaining three pieces in (
45
) is also zero and thus conclude that the complete real contribution in the
q\to q
channel vanishes.

III.3
q\to q
: virtual contribution

For the virtual correction to the
q\to q
channel we have the following amplitude

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}&=g^{2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}k_{g}^{+}}{(2\pi)}\int_{\boldsymbol{k}_{g\perp}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}}\int_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\cdot\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}}\\
&\times\Big[t^{a}t^{a}V(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})\mathcal{T}_{q,1}+V(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})t^{a}t^{a}\mathcal{T}_{q,2}+t^{a}V(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})t^{b}U^{ab}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})\mathcal{T}_{qg}\Big],\end{split}

(50)

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{q,1}&=\mathrm{i}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}k_{g}^{-}}{(2\pi)}\gamma^{\mu}\frac{\not{q}-\not{k}_{g}}{(q-k_{g})^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}\gamma^{\nu}\frac{\not{q}}{q^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}\gamma^{+}\frac{d_{\mu\nu}(k_{g})}{k_{g}^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}\,,\\
\mathcal{T}_{q,2}&=\mathrm{i}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}k_{g}^{-}}{(2\pi)}\gamma^{+}\frac{\not{k}_{1}}{k_{1}^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}\gamma^{\mu}\frac{\not{k}_{1}-\not{k}_{g}}{(k_{1}-k_{g})^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}\gamma^{\nu}\frac{d_{\mu\nu}(k_{g})}{k_{g}^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}\,\\
\mathcal{T}_{qg}&=\mathrm{i}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}k_{g}^{-}}{(2\pi)}\gamma^{\mu}\frac{\not{q}-\not{k}_{g}}{(q-k_{g})^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}\gamma^{+}\frac{\not{q}-\not{k}-\not{k}_{g}}{(q-k-k_{g})^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}\gamma_{\rho}d^{\rho\nu}(k+k_{1}+k_{g}-q)\frac{d_{\mu\nu}(k_{g})}{k_{g}^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}\,.\end{split}

(51)

Above, in the first line of
\mathcal{T}_{qg}
we have evaluated the
k^{-}
integration in favor of the singularity at
(k_{1}+k+k_{g}-q)^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon=0
so that

(q-k-k_{g})^{2}=-\frac{1}{k_{1}^{+}k_{g}^{+}}\left[k_{1}^{+}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}+\boldsymbol{k}_{g\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp})-k_{g}^{+}\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}\right]^{2}\,.

(52)

To get the NLO virtual contribution to
S^{(0)}(k_{1})
we combine the virtual amplitude (

\begin{split}S^{(0)}(k_{1})&=(2\pi)\delta(k_{1}^{+}-q^{+})C_{F}g^{2}\frac{1}{2P_{p}^{+}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}k_{g}^{+}}{(2\pi)}\int_{\boldsymbol{k}_{g\perp}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime}}\int_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\cdot\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})\cdot\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}^{\prime}}\\

&+\mathcal{S}_{qqg}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})\gamma^{+}\not{q}\mathcal{T}_{qg}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})+\mathcal{S}_{qqg}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}^{\prime})\bar{\mathcal{T}}_{qg}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})\not{q}\gamma^{+}\Big]\,,\end{split}

(53)

where now
\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}\equiv\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}
and
\mathcal{T}_{q}\equiv\mathcal{T}_{q,1}+\mathcal{T}_{q,2}
.
Analogous to the case of real production, the terms in (

) that are proportional to the dipole operator will not contribute as a consequence of
C
-parity. This includes the entirety of the second line and the dipole pieces of the third line according to (
32
). Repeating further the steps of the calculation used for real production we find

\begin{split}{\rm tr}\left[\gamma_{5}\not{k}_{1}S^{(0)}(k_{1})\right]&=\mathrm{i}(2\pi)\delta(k_{1}^{+}-q^{+})N_{c}g^{2}\frac{q^{+}}{P_{p}^{+}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}k_{g}^{+}}{(2\pi)}\int_{\boldsymbol{k}_{g\perp}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}}\int_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\cdot(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}\cdot(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})}\\
&\times\left[\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})-\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})\right]\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp})\,.\end{split}

(54)

See Fig.
2
for the diagram corresponding to this remaining contribution. Here now
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp})
is

\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp})\equiv\frac{1}{2q^{+}}{\rm tr}\left[\gamma_{5}\not{k}_{1}\gamma^{+}\not{q}\mathcal{T}_{qg}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\right]=-4\mathrm{i}(\bar{y}+1)\frac{\boldsymbol{v}_{1\perp}\times\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}}{\boldsymbol{v}_{1\perp}^{2}\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}^{2}}\,,

(55)

where we have introduced
y\equiv k_{g}^{+}/k_{1}^{+}=k_{g}^{+}/q^{+}=z/\bar{z}
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{1\perp}
(
\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}
) are associated with final (initial) state collinear configurations explicitly given as
\boldsymbol{v}_{1\perp}\equiv y\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{g\perp}
,
\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}\equiv\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}+\bar{y}\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}+\boldsymbol{k}_{g\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}
. To compute (
55
) we have evaluated the
k_{g}^{-}
integral in
\mathcal{T}_{qg}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})
in favor of the singularity

.
Proceeding with the
\boldsymbol{k}_{g\perp}

\boldsymbol{k}_{g\perp}
to
\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}
and write

\int_{\boldsymbol{k}_{g\perp}}\frac{\boldsymbol{v}_{1\perp}\times\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}}{\boldsymbol{v}_{1\perp}^{2}\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}^{2}}=-\int_{\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}}\frac{\boldsymbol{v}_{\perp}\times\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}}{(\boldsymbol{v}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp})^{2}\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}^{2}}\,,

(56)

where
\boldsymbol{v}_{\perp}\equiv\bar{y}\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}-y\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}
. But this contains an angular integral that is precisely of the form (
49
) and therefore vanishes.

III.4
q\to g

In this case we only have the real diagram with gluon fragmenting into a final state hadron. The expression for
S^{(0)}(k_{1})
takes the same form as (
22
), with the only difference being that now we are integrating over
\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}
(the momenta of the untagged quark) instead of over
\boldsymbol{k}_{g\perp}
,

\begin{split}S^{(0)}(k_{1})&=\frac{k_{g}^{+}}{P_{p}^{+}}\frac{g^{2}C_{F}}{(2q^{+})(2k_{g}^{+})}\int_{\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime}}\int_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\cdot\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}}\\
&\times\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})\cdot\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}^{\prime}}d_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(k_{g})\Big[\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime})\bar{T}_{q}^{\mu^{\prime}}\not{q}T^{\mu}_{q}+\mathcal{S}_{qqg}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}^{\prime})\bar{T}^{\mu^{\prime}}_{qg}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})\not{q}T^{\mu}_{q}\\
&+\mathcal{S}_{qqg}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})\bar{T}_{q}^{\mu^{\prime}}\not{q}T^{\mu}_{qg}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})+\mathcal{S}_{qgqg}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})\bar{T}^{\mu^{\prime}}_{qg}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})\not{q}T^{\mu}_{qg}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\Big]\,.\end{split}

(57)

The first term again vanishes due to
C
-parity. To show that last term vanishes, we first need to make following replacements:
\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}\to\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}
and
\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}\to\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime}
for the
\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}
and
\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime}
integrals which makes
T_{gq}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})
independent of
\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}
. Additionally, since
\not{q}
is sandwiched between two
\gamma^{+}
matrices it does not give a
\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}
contribution. Therefore, the respective hard factor does not depend on
\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}
, and the only
\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}
dependence in the last term appears in the exponential. From here we take the analogous steps as in the
q\to q
channel. First performing the
\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}
integration

\int_{\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime})}=\delta^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime})\,,

(58)

S_{qgqg}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})
collapses to an adjoint dipole, see (
23
) when
\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}
which is in addition independent of
\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}
. This allows us to perform the
\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}
integral to conclude that
\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}
. With this we can utilize
C
-parity to find the hard factor from the last term vanishes. For the remaining interference terms the trace is given in (
36
). With the vectors
\boldsymbol{v}_{1\perp}
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}
suitably re-written in the form
\boldsymbol{v}_{1\perp}=(z\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{g\perp})+\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}=(z\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{g\perp})+\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}
, where we see that a unique combination
z\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{g\perp}
appears. Thus the steps to show that the cross section also vanishes in the
q\to g
channel are from this point completely analogous to those for the
q\to q
channel, see Sec.
III.2
.
Together with the result from Sec.
III.2
and Sec.
III.3
this completes the statement that in the
q\to qg
channel the the odderon mechanism in the WW approximation does not contribute to SSA at NLO.

IV
The
g\to q\bar{q}
channel

In this channel we label the momenta as
g(k_{1})\to q(q)\bar{q}(p)
. The NLO amplitude can be written as

\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}=(+g)\int_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}}\int_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\cdot\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}}\left[T_{g}^{\alpha}t^{b}U^{ba}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})+T_{q\bar{q}}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})V(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})t^{a}V^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})\right]\,,

(59)

where

(60)

and

T_{q\bar{q}}^{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\equiv\frac{1}{2p^{+}}\gamma^{+}\frac{\not{q}-\not{k}}{(q-k)^{2}}\gamma^{\alpha}(\not{q}-\not{k}-\not{k}_{1})\gamma^{+}\,.

(61)

In the above expressions, similar to the discussion in Sec.
III.1
,
k^{+}
is obtained by picking up the pole from the condition

. The above results can be shown to agree with the corresponding amplitude in Eq. (38) in
Blaizot:2004wv
used for unpolarized
pA
collisions after taking the collinear limit for the gluon from the proton. Using (
59
) we calculate
S^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta}(k_{1})
as

\begin{split}S^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta}(k_{1})&=\frac{1}{2P_{p}^{+}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{p}}\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}-1}{\rm Tr}\langle\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{\alpha}\not{q}\mathcal{M}_{\beta}\not{p}\rangle\\
&=\frac{q^{+}}{P_{p}^{+}}\frac{g^{2}T_{R}}{(2q^{+})(2p^{+})}\int_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime}}\int_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\cdot\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})\cdot\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}^{\prime}}\\
&\times\Big\{\mathcal{S}_{A}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}){\rm tr}\left[\bar{T}^{\prime}_{g,\alpha}\not{q}T_{g,\beta}\not{p}\right]+\mathcal{S}_{qqg}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}){\rm tr}\left[\bar{T}^{\prime}_{q\bar{q},\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})\not{q}T_{g,\beta}\not{p}\right]\\
&+\mathcal{S}_{qqg}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime}){\rm tr}\left[\bar{T}^{\prime}_{g,\alpha}\not{q}T_{q\bar{q},\beta}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\not{p}\right]+\mathcal{S}_{qqqq}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}){\rm tr}\left[\bar{T}^{\prime}_{q\bar{q},\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})\not{q}T_{q\bar{q},\beta}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\not{p}\right]\Big\}\,,\end{split}

(62)

where
T_{R}=1/2
. Here
\mathcal{S}_{A}
and
\mathcal{S}_{qqg}
are defined in (
15
) and the first line of (
23
) respectively, while

\mathcal{S}_{qqqq}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})\equiv\frac{1}{C_{F}N_{c}}{\rm tr}\left\langle V^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})V(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})t^{a}V^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})V(\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}^{\prime})t^{a}\right\rangle\,,

(63)

is an additional gluon distribution of the target.

Similar to the findings in Sec.
III.1
, the first and the fourth term in (

) vanish. For the first term this can be argued from
C
-parity on the Dirac trace (another way is simply from the fact that the adjoint dipole
\mathcal{S}_{A}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}^{\prime})
is real). For the fourth term the key point is that the hard factor does not depend on
\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}
(
\not{p}
is sandwiched between
\gamma^{+}
and so the
\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}
dependence drops out). Then, the fourth term does not contribute by the same steps used in Sec.
III.1
. This leaves the interference term in (

. According to the WW truncation of (

) these require the evaluation of the following hard factors

\begin{split}&\mathcal{H}^{(\mathcal{G}_{3T})}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\equiv\frac{1}{4q^{+}p^{+}}\frac{1}{p_{1}^{+}}\epsilon^{+\alpha\beta S_{\perp}}\omega_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha}\omega_{\beta^{\prime}\beta}{\rm tr}\left[\bar{T}^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{g}\not{q}T^{\beta^{\prime}}_{q\bar{q}}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\not{p}\right]\,,\\

(64)

We find

\mathcal{H}^{(\mathcal{G}_{3T})}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})=4z\bar{z}(z-\bar{z})\frac{\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{1\perp}\times\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}}{\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{1\perp}^{2}\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{2\perp}^{2}}\,,

(65)

&+z\bar{z}(z-\bar{z})\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{1\perp}\times\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}\right)k_{1\perp}^{\lambda}\Big\}\,,\end{split}

(66)

where now
\boldsymbol{v}_{1\perp}\equiv z\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}-\bar{z}\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}=\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}-\bar{z}\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}-\bar{z}\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}
,
\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}\equiv\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}-\bar{z}\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}
, with

the momentum fraction of the recoiling antiquark. By
\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{1\perp}
(
\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{2\perp}
) in (

\boldsymbol{v}_{1\perp}
(
\boldsymbol{v}_{2\perp}
) at
\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}=0
. According to the WW truncation of the polarized cross section we also need to take a derivative of
\mathcal{H}^{(\tilde{g}),\lambda}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp})

k_{1\lambda}
(and sum over
\lambda
), c.f., 2nd line in (

). After this, we could proceed with the angular integrals as in Sec.

, but this time the expressions would involve
\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}
. A simpler way to proceed is to first combine the 1st and the 2nd line in (
7
) leading to

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{(\mathcal{G}_{3T})}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})&-\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{1}^{\lambda}}\mathcal{H}^{(\tilde{g}),\lambda}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp})\right]_{k_{1}=p_{1}}=-\frac{4\bar{z}(z^{2}+\bar{z}^{2})}{\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{1\perp}^{4}\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{2\perp}^{4}}\\

(67)

where

through

(69)

where
q
is the quark loop momentum and

\mathcal{T}_{q\bar{q}}^{\mu\beta}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\equiv\frac{1}{2q^{+}}\int_{q^{-}}{\rm tr}\left[\frac{\not{q}-\not{k}_{g}}{(q-k_{g})^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}\gamma^{\mu}\frac{\not{q}}{q^{2}+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}\gamma^{+}(\not{q}-\not{k}_{g}+\not{k}_{1}+\not{k})\gamma^{\beta}\frac{\not{q}-\not{k}_{g}+\not{k}}{(q-k_{g}+k)^{2}}\gamma^{+}\right]\,.

(70)

VI
Conclusions and outline

We have revisited the odderon mechanism for SSA in
p^{\uparrow}A
originally suggested in
Kovchegov:2012ga
at the quark level. At the hadron level this mechanism would involve the
g_{T}(x)
distribution. We have considered the WW truncation of the full twist-3 polarized cross section and argued that in addition to
g_{T}(x)
we also need to take into account the
g_{1T}^{(1)}(x)
for a consistent computation. Our main finding is that under this truncation the polarized cross section vanishes exactly up to NLO for all possible partonic channels.

Figure 5:
A sample interference diagram appearing at NNLO. The crosses denote cut propagators that determine the imaginary part of the loop.

It is natural to consider whether any of the above assumptions can be relaxed so that a non-zero contribution to SSA from the odderon mechanism may be found after all. One option is to go beyond the WW approximation, namely including the ETQS pieces in (
6
). Note the difference from the more conventional pole calculus – here one needs to pick up the principal value of internal propagators so that the general functional forms of the ETQS functions would be required. Alternatively, one can consider the twist-3 FF mechanism, where we pick up the real part of the twist-3 FFs with the phase provided by the odderon. Once more, this in contrast to the conventional computations where the phase is supplied by the imaginary part of twist-3 FFs. Given that the current global fits constrain only the imaginary part of the twist-3 FFs
Cammarota:2020qcw
;
Gamberg:2022kdb
, the phenomenological implications of this alternative would be worth exploring.

Another possibility would be to retain the WW approximation but compute the hard factor up to NNLO. While of course only an explicit computation can reveal whether the odderon appears at NNLO, we mention here a competing mechanism that is already known to appear at NNLO. The basic premise is very simple: at higher orders it is an imaginary part of the loop amplitude that can supply the phase. A specific NNLO contribution illustrating this is given in Fig.
5
, where the crosses denote cut propagators. Physically, the initial
q\to qg
splitting occurs inside the target nucleus in the amplitude. The
qg
system subsequently rescatters with a
t
-channel quark into the final state providing a phase with respect to the amplitude on the opposite side of the final state cut. Such final state rescattering is sometimes referred to as the lensing mechanism and was considered in
Kovchegov:2020kxg
. In fact, this idea
Brodsky:2002cx
is closely related to the very first estimate of SSA in perturbative QCD
Kane:1978nd
. The computation in
Kovchegov:2020kxg
was in the quark-diquark model. As a future work it would be important to consider this in the hybrid approach.

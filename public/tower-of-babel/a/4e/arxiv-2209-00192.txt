Uniform W 1 , p W^{1,p} Estimates and Large-Scale Regularity for Dirichlet Problems in Perforated Domains
Zhongwei Shen, Jamison Wallace

Abstract
In this paper we study the Dirichlet problem for Laplace’s equation in a domain ω ε , η \omega_{\varepsilon,\eta} perforated periodically with small holes in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , where ε \varepsilon represents the scale of the minimal distances between holes and η \eta the ratio between the scale of sizes of holes and ε \varepsilon . We establish W 1 , p W^{1,p} estimates for solutions with bounding constants depending explicitly on ε \varepsilon and η \eta . The proof relies on a large-scale Lipschitz estimate for harmonic functions in perforated domains. The results are optimal for d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . Keywords : Uniform Estimates; Large-scale Regularity; Perforated Domain; Homogenization. MR (2020) Subject Classification : 35Q35; 35B27; 76D07.

Introduction

This paper continues the study of the Dirichlet problem for Laplace’s equation,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=F+\text{\rm div}(f)&\quad&\text{ in }\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta},\\
u&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

in a domain
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
perforated with small holes,
where
\varepsilon
represents the scale of the minimal distances between holes and
\eta
the ratio between the sizes of holes and
\varepsilon
.
We are interested in the
W^{1,p}
estimates,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq A_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})},

(1.2)

and

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq C_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+D_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})},

(1.3)

for
1<p<\infty
,
with bounding constants
A_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
,
B_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
,
C_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)
and
D_{p}(\varepsilon,\eta)

depending explicitly on the small parameters
\varepsilon,\eta\in(0,1]
.

To state our main results, we let
Y=[-1/2,1/2]^{d}
be a closed unit cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
T

the closure of an open subset of
Y
.
Throughout the paper we shall assume that

Y\setminus{T}
is connected and that

B(0,c_{0})\subset T\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{\rm dist}(\partial T,\partial Y)\geq c_{0}>0

(1.4)

for some
c_{0}>0
.
Define

\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varepsilon(k+\eta{T}),

(1.5)

where
0<\varepsilon,\eta\leq 1
.
Roughly speaking, the periodically perforated domain
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is obtained from

\mathbb{R}^{d}
by removing a hole
\varepsilon(k+\eta{T})
of size
\varepsilon\eta
from each cube
\varepsilon(k+Y)
of size
\varepsilon
.
The distances between holes are bounded below by
c_{0}\varepsilon
.

The following are the main results in this paper.
The first theorem deals with the case
d\geq 3
, while the second treats the case
d=2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
d\geq 3
and
1<p<\infty
.
Let
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be given by (
1.5
), where
T
is the closure of an open subset of
Y
with
C^{1}
boundary.
For any
f\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
F\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
, the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
)
has a unique solution
u
in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
.
Moreover, the solution satisfies the estimate,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\eta^{-d|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+C\varepsilon\eta^{1-\frac{d}{2}}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\quad&\text{ for }1<p<2,\\
&C\eta^{-d|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+C\varepsilon\eta^{1-d+\frac{d}{p}}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\quad&\text{ for }2\leq p<\infty,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

and

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\varepsilon\eta^{1-\frac{d}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+C\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\quad&\text{ for }1<p<2,\\
&C\varepsilon\eta^{1-\frac{d}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+C\varepsilon^{2}\eta^{2-d}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\quad&\text{ for }2\leq p<\infty,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.7)

where
C
depends on
d
,
p
and
T
.
Furthermore, the estimates (
1.6
)-(
1.7
) are sharp.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
d=2
and
1<p<\infty
.
Let
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
be given by (
1.5
), where
T
is the closure of an open subset of
Y
with

boundary.
For any
f\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta};\mathbb{R}^{2})

F\in L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})

1.1
)
has a unique solution
u
in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})
.
Moreover, the solution satisfies the estimate,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\eta^{-2|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|}|\ln(\eta/2)|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+C\varepsilon|\ln(\eta/2)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\quad&\text{ for }1<p<2,\\

(1.8)

and

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\varepsilon\eta^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}+C\varepsilon^{2}|\ln(\eta/2)|\|F\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta})}&\quad&\text{ for }1<p<2,\\

\end{aligned}\right.

(1.9)

where
C
depends on

and

.
Furthermore, the estimates (

We now describe our approach to Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
By rescaling we assume
\varepsilon=1
.
Our starting point is the estimates (
1.7
) and (
1.9
) for
u

in the case
2\leq p<\infty
.
The estimates were proved in a general non-periodic setting in
[
10
]
, using a classical method of test functions and a Poincaré
inequality for
W^{1,2}(Q_{1})
functions that vanish on
Q_{1}\setminus\eta T
, where
Q_{R}=(-R/2,R/2)^{d}
.
To establish the estimates (
1.6
) and (
1.8
) for
\nabla u
,
we consider the Dirichlet problem in a weighted Sobolev space for Laplace’s equation
-\Delta u=F+\text{\rm div}(f)
in
an exterior domain
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T
.
By localization and rescaling, this allows us to control the
L^{p}
norm of
\nabla u
in each cell

by the
L^{p}
norm of
u
in a slightly larger cell
k+(1+c_{0})Q_{1}
.

Next, to bound the localization error,
we construct a corrector
\psi_{\eta}\in W^{1,2}(Q_{1})
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta\psi_{\eta}&=F_{\eta}+\text{\rm div}(f_{\eta})&\quad&\text{ in }Q_{1}\setminus\eta T,\\
\psi_{\eta}&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\eta T,\\
\psi_{\eta}&=1&\quad&\text{ in }Q_{1}\setminus B(0,1/3),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.13)

where
F_{\eta}
and

satisfy the condition
\|f_{\eta}\|_{\infty}+\|F_{\eta}\|_{\infty}\leq C\eta^{d-2}
for

.
The construction of
\psi_{\eta}
, which is motivated by the correctors used in
[
1
]
, uses a solution to the exterior problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\phi_{*}&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T,\\
\phi_{*}&=0&\quad&\text{ in }T,\\
\phi_{*}&\to 1&\quad&\text{ as }|x|\to\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.14)

See (
6.5
) for

) for

We apply a localization argument to the solution
u-\alpha\psi_{\eta}
in
(1+c_{0})Q_{1}\setminus\eta T

\alpha=\fint_{(1+c_{0})Q_{1}\setminus B(0,1/3)}u.

With sharp estimates for

and

this reduces the

estimate of

estimate for the operator

Note that

.
By a real-variable argument in
[

to establish the

boundedness of the operator for

Finally, we note that the desired reverse Hölder inequality in

2
Large-scale
L^{\infty}
estimates

Throughout this section we assume that
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is given by (
1.5
), where

T
is the closure of an open subset of
Y
with Lipschitz boundary.
Let
Q_{R}=(-R/2,R/2)^{d}
.
Our goal is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}(Q_{R})
for some
R\geq\varepsilon
.
Suppose that

\Delta u=0\quad\text{ in }\quad Q_{R}\cap\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}\quad\text{ and }\quad u=0\quad\text{ in }Q_{R}\setminus\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}.

(2.1)

Then, for
\varepsilon\leq r\leq R
,

\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(2.2)

where
C
depends on
d
.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
, as well as the proof of Theorem
3.1
in the next section,
is based on an approach found in
[
4
]
and
relies on a Caccioppoli inequality for solutions of (
2.1
).

For
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, define

\hat{u}(z)=\int_{z+Q_{1}}u(x)\,dx.

(2.3)

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}(Q_{r+2})
, where
r\geq 1
.
Then

\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\max_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap{Q_{r+2}}}|\hat{u}(z)|+C\left(\fint_{Q_{r+2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(2.4)

where
C
depends only on
d
.

Proof.

Let
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap Q_{r}
. By Poincaré’s inequality,

\int_{z+Q_{1}}|u|^{2}\,dx\leq C|\hat{u}(z)|^{2}+C\int_{z+Q_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx,

(2.5)

where
C
depends only on
d
.
Let
\ell\geq 1
be an odd integer.
By summing (
2.5
) over
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap Q_{\ell}
, we obtain

\left(\fint_{Q_{\ell}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\max_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap{Q_{\ell}}}|\hat{u}(z)|+C\left(\fint_{Q_{\ell}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(2.6)

Finally, for any
r\geq 1
, choose an odd integer
\ell
such that
r\leq\ell\leq r+2
.
It is not hard to see that (
2.4
) follows from (
2.6
).
∎

For a function
g
defined in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
let

\Delta_{j}g(x)=g(x+e_{j})-g(x)

(2.7)

for
1\leq j\leq d
, where
e_{j}=(0,\dots,1,\dots,0)
with
1
in the
j
th position.
For a multi-index
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{d})
,
we use the notation
\Delta^{\gamma}g=g
if
\gamma=0
, and

\Delta^{\gamma}g=\Delta_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}\Delta_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\cdots\Delta_{d}^{\gamma_{d}}g

if
|\gamma|\geq 1
.
Let
\partial^{\ell}g=(\Delta^{\gamma}g)_{|\gamma|=\ell}
and

|\partial^{\ell}g|=\left(\sum_{|\gamma|=\ell}|\Delta^{\gamma}g|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(2.8)

for an integer
\ell\geq 0
.
It is not hard to see that

|\partial^{\ell+1}\hat{u}(z)|\leq\left(\int_{z+3Q_{1}}|\nabla\partial^{\ell}u|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}

(2.9)

for any
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

The next lemma provides a discrete Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
g
be a function on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Then, for
R\geq 3d
,

\max_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap{Q}_{R}}|g(z)|\leq C\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}R^{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{R^{d}}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap{Q}_{2R}}|\partial^{\ell}g(z)|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(2.10)

where
N=[d/2]+1
and
C
depends only on
d
,

Proof.

This follows from
[
11
, Lemma 2.6]
.
∎

The following lemma gives the Caccioppoli inequality for solutions of

-\Delta u=F
in
Q_{r}\cap\omega_{1,\eta}
with
u=0
on
Q_{r}\setminus\omega_{1,\eta}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}(Q_{r})
for some
r\geq 1
.
Suppose that
-\Delta u=F
in
Q_{r}\cap\omega_{1,\eta}
and
u=0
in
Q_{r}\setminus\omega_{1,\eta}
.
Then

\left(\fint_{Q_{sr}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\frac{C}{(t-s)r}\left(\fint_{Q_{tr}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C(t-s)r\left(\fint_{Q_{tr}}|F|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(2.11)

for
(1/2)\leq s<t\leq 1
, where
C
depends only on
d
.

Proof.

The proof is exactly the same as that for the usual Caccioppoli inequality.
Choose a cut-off function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q_{tr})
such that

\varphi=1
in
Q_{sr}
and
|\nabla\varphi|\leq C(t-s)^{-1}r^{-1}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{tr}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(u\varphi^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{tr}\cap\omega_{1,\eta}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(u\varphi^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{tr}\cap\omega_{1,\eta}}F(u\varphi^{2})\,dx=\int_{Q_{tr}}F(u\varphi^{2})\,dx.

Hence,

\int_{Q_{tr}}|\nabla u|^{2}\varphi^{2}\,dx=-2\int_{Q_{tr}}\varphi(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi)u\,dx+\int_{Q_{tr}}F(u\varphi^{2})\,dx,

which, by the Cauchy inequality, yields (
2.11
).
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

By rescaling we may assume
\varepsilon=1
.
Let
u\in W^{1,2}(Q_{R})
for some

.
Suppose that
\Delta u=0

By applying the discrete Sobolev inequality (

\displaystyle\max_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap Q_{r+2}}|\hat{u}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap Q_{2\delta R}}|\hat{u}(z)|

(2.12)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}R^{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{R^{d}}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap Q_{4\delta R}}|\partial^{\ell}\hat{u}(z)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{5\delta R}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}R^{\ell}\left(\fint_{Q_{5\delta R}}|\nabla\partial^{\ell-1}u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where

2.9
) for the last inequality.

Next, let
v=\Delta^{\gamma}u
, where
\gamma
is a multi-index with
1\leq|\gamma|=\ell\leq d

Observe that
\Delta v=0
in
Q_{R-3\ell}\cap\omega_{1,\eta}

By applying Lemma

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{\rho}}|\nabla\partial^{\ell}u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\rho}\left(\fint_{Q_{2\rho}}|\partial^{\ell}u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\rho}\left(\fint_{Q_{2\rho+3}}|\nabla\partial^{\ell-1}u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

, where, for the last step, we have used the inequality

\left(\int_{y+Q_{1}}|\Delta_{j}u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(\int_{y+3Q_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(2.13)

By induction it follows that

\left(\fint_{Q_{\rho}}|\nabla\partial^{\ell}u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\frac{C}{\rho^{\ell}}\left(\fint_{Q_{C\rho}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(2.14)

where
0\leq\ell\leq d
and

depends only on

.
This, together with Lemma
2.2

2.12
), shows that
for any
1\leq r\leq\delta R
,

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+CR\left(\fint_{Q_{R/2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C\left(\fint_{Q_{3r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{C}{r}\left(\fint_{Q_{4r}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

It follows that

\sup_{s\leq r\leq R}\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{C}{s}\sup_{s\leq r\leq R}\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

for any
1<s\leq R
, where
C
depends only on
d
.
By choosing
s=s(d)>1
sufficiently large we obtain

\sup_{s\leq r\leq R}\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(2.15)

Finally, we note that

\sup_{1\leq r\leq s}\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C_{s}\left(\fint_{Q_{s}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where we have used (
2.15
) for the last step.
This, together with (
2.15
), gives (
2.2
).
∎

3
Large-scale Lipschitz estimates

In this section we establish a large-scale Lipschitz estimate.
Recall that
Q_{R}=(-R/2,R/2)^{d}
.
As in the last section, we assume
\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is given by (
1.5
), where
T
is the closure of an open subset of
Y

with Lipschitz boundary.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}(Q_{R})
for some
R\geq\varepsilon
.
Suppose that

\Delta u=0\quad\text{ in }\quad Q_{R}\cap\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}\quad\text{ and }\quad u=0\quad\text{ in }Q_{R}\setminus\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}.

(3.1)

Then, for
\varepsilon\leq r\leq R

\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(3.2)

where
C
depends on
d
and
c_{0}
in (
1.4
).

We begin with a Poincaré inequality.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
u\in W^{1,2}(Q_{R})
and
u=0
on
Q_{R}\setminus\omega_{1,\eta}
, where
R\geq 1
is an odd integer.
Then

\int_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}\,dx\leq C\eta^{2-d}\int_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

(3.3)

for
d\geq 3
, and

\int_{Q_{R}}|u|^{2}\,dx\leq C|\ln(\eta/2)|\int_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

(3.4)

for
d=2
, where
C
depends only on
d
and
c_{0}
.

Proof.

The case
R=1
is well known. See e.g.
[
2
, p.270]
or
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
for a proof.
The general case follows by covering
Q_{R}
with unit cubes
\{k+Q_{1}:k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap Q_{R}\}
.
∎

Following
[
6
]
, we introduce a
Y
-periodic function
\chi_{\eta}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that satisfies

-\Delta\chi_{\eta}=\eta^{d-2}\quad\text{ in }\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}\quad\text{ and }\quad\chi_{\eta}=0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\omega_{\varepsilon,\eta}.

(3.5)

Let

denote the closure in

of the set of smooth

The existence and uniqueness of
\chi_{\eta}
may be proved by using the Lax-Milgram Theorem on a Hilbert space

H
, given by the closure of
\{u\in H_{per}^{1}(Q_{1}):u=0\text{ on }\eta T\}
in
H^{1}_{per}(Q_{1})
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\chi_{\eta}
be given by (
3.5
).
Then

\displaystyle C_{1}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{1}}|\nabla\chi_{\eta}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C_{2}\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}},

(3.6)

\displaystyle C_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\fint_{Q_{1}}\chi_{\eta}\leq C_{2},

for
d\geq 3
, and

\displaystyle C_{1}|\ln(\eta/2)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{1}}|\nabla\chi_{\eta}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C_{2}|\ln(\eta/2)|^{\frac{1}{2}},

(3.7)

\displaystyle C_{1}|\ln(\eta/2)|\leq

\displaystyle\fint_{Q_{1}}\chi_{\eta}\leq C_{2}|\ln(\eta/2)|,

for

, where

depend only on
d
and
c_{0}
.

Proof.

See
[
6
]
or
[

, Lemma 4.4]
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}(Q_{R})
for some
R\geq 100d
.
Then, for any
r\in[1,R/100]
,

\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|u-\hat{u}(0)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq Cr\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}R^{\ell}\left(\fint_{Q_{R/2}}|\nabla\partial^{\ell}u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C\left(\fint_{Q_{3r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(3.8)

where
N=[d/2]+1
and
C
depends only on
d
.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
2.2
it suffices to show that if

, then

|\hat{u}(z)-\hat{u}(0)|
is bounded by the first term in the right-hand side of (

).
To this end we use the observation,

\max_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap Q_{3r}}|\hat{u}(z)-\hat{u}(0)|\leq Cr\max_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap Q_{3r}}|\partial\hat{u}(z)|.

By applying the discrete Sobolev inequality (
2.10
) with
g(z)=\partial\hat{u}(z)

we obtain

\displaystyle\max_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap Q_{3r}}|\hat{u}(z)-\hat{u}(0)|

\displaystyle\leq Cr\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}R^{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{R^{d}}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap{Q}_{R/4}}|\partial^{\ell+1}\hat{u}(z)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq Cr\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}R^{\ell}\left(\fint_{Q_{R/2}}|\nabla\partial^{\ell}u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where we have used (
2.9
) for the last inequality.
∎

We are now in a position to give the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem

.

By rescaling we may assume
\varepsilon=1

Let

&C|\ln(\eta/2)|^{-1}|\hat{u}(0)|&\quad&\text{ for }d=2,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.9)

where
C
depends only on
d
and
c_{0}
.
Let

.
By applying Lemma

\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|w|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq Cr\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}R^{\ell}\left(\fint_{Q_{100\delta R}}|\nabla\partial^{\ell}w|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C\left(\fint_{Q_{3r}}|\nabla w|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Next, we note that
-\Delta w=-\alpha\eta^{d-2}
in

and

for

Also, observe that if

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{\rho}}|\nabla\partial^{\ell}w|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

for

and
1\leq\rho\leq\delta R
, where

depends only on
d
.
It follows from (
3.11
) and (

) that for
1\leq r\leq\delta R/2
,

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|\nabla w|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}R^{\ell}\left(\fint_{Q_{100\delta R}}|\nabla\partial^{\ell}w|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{C}{r}\left(\fint_{Q_{6r}}|\nabla w|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C|\alpha|\eta^{d-2}r

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|\nabla w|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{C}{r}\left(\fint_{Q_{6r}}|\nabla w|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C|\alpha|\eta^{d-2}R,

where
C
depends only on
d
and we have used (
3.12
) for the last inequality. Since

,
this yields,

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{C}{r}\left(\fint_{Q_{6r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(3.13)

\displaystyle+C|\alpha|\left(\fint_{Q_{1}}|\nabla\chi_{\eta}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C|\alpha|\eta^{d-2}R,

for any

is also used for (

Suppose

. We consider two cases.

,

) and (

\displaystyle|\alpha|\left(\fint_{Q_{1}}|\nabla\chi_{\eta}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+|\alpha|\eta^{d-2}R

where we have used the large-scale

estimate (
2.2
) for the third inequality
and the Poincaré inequality (
3.3
) for the last step.
The assumption that

is an odd integer is also used here.
This, together with (

\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(\fint_{Q_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{C}{r}\left(\fint_{Q_{6r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

for any

and

)
with

and

. Let

.
We use the Caccioppoli inequality (

) to obtain

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r}\left(\fint_{Q_{2r}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\eta^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\left(\fint_{Q_{2r}}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

) for the third inequality

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq r\leq R}\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{1\leq r\leq R_{\eta}}\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\sup_{R_{\eta}<r\leq R}\left(\fint_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

for the second

\displaystyle|\alpha|\left(\fint_{Q_{1}}|\nabla\chi_{\eta}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+|\alpha|\eta^{d-2}R

where we have used the large-scale
L^{\infty}
estimate (
2.2
) for the third inequality and
the Poincaré inequality (
3.4
) for the last step.
As in the case
d\geq 3
, this gives (

), which leads to (

).
If

where we have used the

)

Using Lemma

Proof.

5
Estimates in an exterior domain

In this section we establish
W^{1,p}
estimates for solutions with compact support of Laplace’s equation in the
exterior domain
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T
, where
T
is the closure of a bounded
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with connected
boundary. We assume that
B(0,c_{0})\subset T
.

We begin with
W^{1,p}
estimates for Laplace’s equation in a bounded Lipschitz or
C^{1}
domain.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. There exists
\delta>0
, depending on
d
and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
, such that
if

\Big|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\Big|<\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{6}+\delta&\quad&\text{ for }d\geq 3,\\
&\frac{1}{4}+\delta&\quad&\text{ for }d=2,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.1)

the Dirichlet problem,

-\Delta u=F
in
\Omega
and
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
, has a unique solution in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)

for any
F\in W^{-1,p}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, the solution satisfies the estimate,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}\|F\|_{W^{-1,p}(\Omega)},

(5.2)

where
C_{p}
depends on
d
,
p
and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.
Furthermore, if
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{1}
domain, the results above hold for
1<p<\infty
.

Proof.

The estimate (
5.2
) for
1<p<\infty
is well known if
\Omega
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
domain.
For Lipchitz and
C^{1}
domains, the theorem was proved in
[
5
]
.
∎

The next theorem is on the solvability of the Dirichlet problem in a weighted Sobolev space in the exterior domain
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T
,

-\Delta u=F\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T\quad\text{ and }\quad u=0\quad\text{ on }\partial T.

(5.3)

We first introduce some notations.
For
1<p<\infty
and
p\neq d
, let

\displaystyle X^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)=\Big\{u\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T):

\displaystyle(1+|x|)^{-1}u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)

(5.4)

\displaystyle\text{ and }\nabla u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)\Big\},

with its natural norm,

\|u\|_{X^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}=\|(1+|x|)^{-1}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}.

(5.5)

If
p=d
, let

\displaystyle X^{1,d}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)=\Big\{u\in W^{1,d}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T):

\displaystyle((1+|x|)\ln(2+|x|))^{-1}u\in L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)

(5.6)

\displaystyle\text{ and }\nabla u\in L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)\Big\},

and

\|u\|_{X^{1,d}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}=\|((1+|x|)\ln(2+|x|))^{-1}u\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}.

(5.7)

It follows from
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
that for
u\in X^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}

\displaystyle\text{ if }1<p<d,

(5.8)

\displaystyle\inf_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}\|u-\alpha\|_{X^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}

\displaystyle\text{ if }d\leq p<\infty.

Let

X_{0}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)=\left\{u\in X^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T):\ u=0\text{ on }\partial T\right\},

(5.9)

and
X^{-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)
be the dual of
X^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)
, where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
.
It is known that
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
X^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)
and

C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)
is dense in
X_{0}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)

[
3
]
.

Let

V^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)=\left\{w\in X^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T):\ \Delta w=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T\right\}.

(5.10)

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
2\leq p<\infty
.
Let
T
be the closure of a bounded
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with connected boundary.
Then, for any
F\in X^{-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)
, the Dirichlet problem (
5.3
)
has a unique solution in
X_{0}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)/V_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)
.
Moreover, the solution satisfies

\inf_{w\in V^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\|u-w\|_{X^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\leq C\|F\|_{X^{-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)},

(5.11)

where
C
depends on
d
,
p
and
T
.

Proof.

This was proved in
[
3
, Theorem 2.10]
under the assumption that
\partial T
is
C^{1,1}
.
With the
W^{1,p}
estimates in Theorem
5.1
for bounded
C^{1}
domains, an inspection of the proof shows that
Theorem 2.10 in
[
3
]
continues to hold under the condition that
\partial T
is
C^{1}
.
∎

A few remarks are in order.

Remark 5.3
.

If
d\geq 3
and
2\leq p<d
, or
d=p=2
, then

V_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)=\{0\}.

(5.12)

As a result, the solution of (
5.3
) is unique in
X_{0}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)
and satisfies

\|u\|_{X^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\leq C\|F\|_{X^{-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}.

(5.13)

Remark 5.4
.

Suppose
d\geq 3
and
p\geq d
, Then

V_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)=\left\{\alpha\phi_{*}:\ \alpha\in\mathbb{R}\right\},

(5.14)

where
\phi_{*}
is the unique solution of the exterior problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\phi_{*}&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus{T},\\
\phi_{*}&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial T,\\
\phi_{*}(x)&\to 1&\quad&\text{ as }|x|\to\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.15)

Moreover, the solution is given by

\phi_{*}(x)=1-\int_{\partial T}\frac{g_{*}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\,d\sigma(y)

for some
g_{*}\in L^{2}(\partial T)

[
12
,
3
]
. It follows that if
0\in T
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \phi_{*}(x)&=1-c_{*}|x|^{2-d}+O(|x|^{1-d}),\\
\nabla\phi_{*}(x)&=-c_{*}\nabla(|x|^{2-d})+O(|x|^{-d}),\\
\nabla^{2}\phi_{*}(x)&=O(|x|^{-d}),\end{aligned}\right.

(5.16)

as
|x|\to\infty
,
where

c_{*}=\int_{\partial T}g_{*}(y)\,d\sigma(y)\neq 0.

Remark 5.5
.

If
d=2
and
p>2
, then

V_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)=\left\{\alpha\phi_{*}:\ \alpha\in\mathbb{R}\right\},

(5.17)

where
\phi_{*}
is a harmonic function in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus{T}
with the properties that
\phi_{*}=0
on
\partial T
and

\left\{\begin{aligned} \phi_{*}(x)&=-c_{*}\ln|x|+O(|x|^{-1}),\\
\nabla\phi_{*}(x)&=-c_{*}\nabla(\ln|x|)+O(|x|^{-2}),\\
\nabla^{2}\phi_{*}(x)&=O(|x|^{-2}),\end{aligned}\right.

(5.18)

as
|x|\to\infty

[
12
,
3
]
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
2<p<\infty
.
Let
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)
be a solution of

-\Delta u=F+\text{\rm div}(f)\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T\quad\text{ and }\quad u=0\quad\text{ on }\partial T.

(5.19)

Suppose that
T\subset B(0,R)
and
supp
(u)
, supp
(F)
, supp
(f)

\subset B(0,R)
for some
R\geq 2
.
Then

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\leq C\Phi_{p}(R)\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+R\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\right\},

(5.20)

where

\Phi_{p}(R)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1&\quad&\text{ if }d\geq 3\text{ and }2<p<d,\\
&(\ln R)^{1-\frac{1}{d}}&\quad&\text{ if }d\geq 3\text{ and }p=d,\\
&R^{1-\frac{d}{p}}&\quad&\text{ if }d\geq 3\text{ and }d<p<\infty,\\
&R^{1-\frac{2}{p}}(\ln R)^{-1}&\quad&\text{ if }d=2\text{ and }2<p<\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.21)

and
C
depends only on
d
,
p
and
T
.

Proof.

Note that
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)\subset X^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)
, and that for any
\psi\in X^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)
,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T}F\psi\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|_{L^{p}(B(0,R))}\|\psi\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B(0,R))}

\displaystyle\leq CR\|F\|_{L^{p}(B(0,R))}\|\psi\|_{X^{1,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)},

where we have used the facts that supp
(F)\subset B(0,R)
and
p^{\prime}\neq d
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\|F+\text{\rm div}(f)\|_{X^{-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\leq C\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+R\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\right\}.

(5.22)

This allows us to apply Theorem
5.2
to obtain

\inf_{w\in V_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\|u-w\|_{X^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\leq C\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+R\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\right\}.

(5.23)

Suppose
d\geq 3
and
2<p<d
.
Then
V_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)=\{0\}
.
It follows from (
5.23
) that

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\leq C\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+R\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\right\}.

(5.24)

Let
d\geq 3
and
d<p<\infty
. Then, by Remark
5.4
,

V_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)=\{\alpha\phi_{*}:\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\}
,
where the harmonic function
\phi_{*}
satisfies (
5.16
).
Let

\inf_{w\in V_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\|u-w\|_{X^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}=\|u-\alpha_{0}\phi_{*}\|_{X^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}

(5.25)

for some
\alpha_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,R)
, it follows by (
5.23
) that

|\alpha_{0}|\||x|^{-1}\phi_{*}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,R))}\leq C\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+R\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\right\}.

Since
\phi_{*}\sim 1
for
|x|
large, this yields

|\alpha_{0}|\leq CR^{1-\frac{d}{p}}\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+R\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\right\}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(0,R)\setminus T)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla(u-\alpha_{0}\phi_{*})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+|\alpha_{0}|\|\nabla\phi_{*}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}

(5.26)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+R\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\right\}+C|\alpha_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq CR^{1-\frac{d}{p}}\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+R\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\right\}.

If
d\geq 3
and
p=d
, a similar argument shows that

|\alpha_{0}|\leq C(\ln R)^{1-\frac{1}{d}}\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+R\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\right\},

and

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(0,R)\setminus T)}\leq C(\ln R)^{1-\frac{1}{d}}\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+R\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\right\}.

(5.27)

The argument above works equally well for
d=2
and
2<p<\infty
.
In this case, using (
5.18
), we obtain

|\alpha_{0}|\leq CR^{1-\frac{2}{p}}(\ln R)^{-1}\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+R\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\right\},

and

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(0,R)\setminus T)}\leq CR^{1-\frac{2}{p}}(\ln R)^{-1}\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}+R\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}\right\}.

(5.28)

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
2<p<\infty
.
Let
u
be a solution of
-\Delta u=F+\text{\rm div}(f)
in
R\widetilde{Y}\setminus T
with

u=0
on
\partial T
, where
\widetilde{Y}=(1+c_{0})Q_{1}
.
Then, for
R\geq 3
,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(Q_{R}\setminus T)}\leq C\Phi_{p}(R)\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(R\widetilde{Y}\setminus T)}+R\|F\|_{L^{p}(R\widetilde{Y}\setminus T)}+R^{\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{2}-1}\|u\|_{L^{2}(R\widetilde{Y}\setminus B(0,R/3))}\right\},

(5.29)

where
\Phi_{p}(R)
is given by (
5.21
) and
C
depends only on
d
,
p
and
T
.

Proof.

Choose a cut-off function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((1+c_{0}/3)Q_{R})
such that
\varphi=1
in
Q_{R}
and

|\nabla\varphi|\leq CR^{-1}
,
|\nabla^{2}\varphi|\leq CR^{-2}
.
Note that
u\varphi=0
on
\partial T
and

-\Delta(u\varphi)=F\varphi+\text{\rm div}(f\varphi)-f\cdot\nabla\varphi-2\text{\rm div}(u\nabla\varphi)+u\Delta\varphi

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T
.
It follows by Theorem
5.6
that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(Q_{R}\setminus T)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla(u\varphi)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus T)}

(5.30)

\displaystyle\leq C\Phi_{p}(R)\left\{\|f\|_{L^{p}(R\widetilde{Y}\setminus T)}+R\|F\|_{L^{p}(R\widetilde{Y}\setminus T)}+R^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{p}((1+c_{0}/3)Q_{R}\setminus Q_{R})}\right\},

where
\Phi_{p}(R)
is given by (
5.21
).
Using interior estimates for Laplace’s equation, one may show that

\|u\|_{L^{p}((1+c_{0}/3)Q_{R}\setminus Q_{R})}\leq CR^{\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(R\widetilde{Y}\setminus B(0,R/3))}+CR\|f\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{Y}\setminus T)}+CR^{2}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{Y}\setminus T)},

which, together with (
5.30
), yields (
5.29
).
∎

6

In this section we establish

estimates for solutions of

(6.1)

where
\widetilde{Y}=(1+c_{0})Q_{1}

\eta\in(0,(4d)^{-1})
.
Throughout the section, unless indicated otherwise, we assume that

and satisfies (

).
Let

\displaystyle+C\Phi_{p}(\eta^{-1})\left(\int_{\widetilde{Y}\setminus B(0,1/3)}|u-\alpha|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2},

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(Y\setminus\eta T)}

\displaystyle\leq C|\alpha|\eta^{\frac{2}{p}-1}|\ln\eta|^{-1}+C\eta^{\frac{2}{p}-1}|\ln\eta|^{-1}\left(\int_{\widetilde{Y}\setminus\eta T}\left(|f|^{p}+|F|^{p}\right)\,dx\right)^{1/p}

(6.3)

\displaystyle+C\eta^{\frac{2}{p}-1}|\ln\eta|^{-1}\left(\int_{\widetilde{Y}\setminus B(0,1/3)}|u-\alpha|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2},

where

depends only on
d
,
p
and
T
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
2<p<\infty
.
Let
u
be the same as in Theorem

. Then

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(Y\setminus\eta T)}\leq C\Phi_{p}(\eta^{-1})\left\{\|u\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{Y}\setminus B(0,1/3))}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{Y}\setminus\eta T)}+\|F\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{Y}\setminus\eta T)}\right\},

(6.4)

where
\Phi_{p}
is give by (
5.21
) and
C
depends only on
d
,
p
and
T
.

Proof.

This follows readily from Corollary
5.7
by a simple rescaling argument.
Indeed, suppose
-\Delta u=F+\text{\rm div}(f)
in
\widetilde{Y}\setminus\eta T
.
Let
v(x)=u(\eta x)
. Then

-\Delta v=G+\text{\rm div}(g)
in
R\widetilde{Y}\setminus T
, where
R=\eta^{-1}
,

G(x)=\eta^{2}F(\eta x)
and
g(x)=\eta f(\eta x)
.
∎

Note that if
u
is a solution of (
6.1
) and
\alpha\neq 0
, then
u-\alpha
is not a solution of (
6.1
) since it
does not satisfy the boundary condition on
\partial(\eta T)
.
To prove Theorem
6.1
,
we need to construct a corrector
\psi_{\eta}
such that
\psi_{\eta}=0
on
\partial T
and

\psi_{\eta}=1
on
(1+c_{0})Y\setminus B(0,1/3)
.

Let
d\geq 3
.
Let
\phi_{*}
be defined by (
5.15
).
For each
\eta\in(0,1/(4d))
, we introduce a function
\psi_{\eta}
in

, defined by

\psi_{\eta}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1&\quad&\text{ if }x\in Y\setminus B(0,1/3),\\
&\phi_{*}(x/\eta)&\quad&\text{ if }x\in B(0,1/4)\setminus\eta T,\\
&0&\quad&\text{ if }x\in\eta T,\end{aligned}\right.

(6.5)

and
\psi_{\eta}
is the harmonic function in
B(0,1/3)\setminus\overline{B(0,1/4)}
such that
\psi_{\eta}=1
on
\partial B(0,1/3)
and

\psi_{\eta}(x)=\phi_{*}(x/\eta)
on
\partial B(0,1/4)

In the case

\psi_{\eta}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1&\quad&\text{ if }x\in Y\setminus B(0,1/3),\\
&\frac{\ln|x|-\ln(d\eta)}{\ln(1/3)-\ln(d\eta)}&\quad&\text{ if }x\in B(0,1/3)\setminus B(0,d\eta),\\
&0&\quad&\text{ if }x\in B(0,d\eta).\end{aligned}\right.

(6.6)

Since
\psi_{\eta}=1
on
\partial Y
, we may extend
\psi_{\eta}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
periodically.
Thus,
\psi_{\eta}
is
Y

\psi_{\eta}(x+k)=\psi_{\eta}(x)

Note that

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\psi_{\eta}
be defined by (
6.5
)-(
6.6
).
If

,

\left(\int_{Y}|\nabla\psi_{\eta}|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\approx\left\{\begin{aligned} &\eta^{\frac{d}{p}-1}&\quad&\text{ if }d^{\prime}<p<\infty,\\
&\eta^{d-2}|\ln\eta|^{\frac{1}{p}}&\quad&\text{ if }p=d^{\prime},\\
&\eta^{d-2}&\quad&\text{ if }1<p<d^{\prime},\end{aligned}\right.

(6.7)

where

.
If

, we have
\|\nabla\psi_{\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Y)}\approx\eta^{\frac{2}{p}-1}|\ln\eta|^{-1}
for

,

\|\nabla\psi_{\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Y)}\approx|\ln\eta|^{-1/2}
for

,
and
\|\nabla\psi_{\eta}\|_{L^{p}(Y)}\approx|\ln\eta|^{-1}
for

Proof.

The case
d=2
follows by a direct calculation.
Consider the case
d\geq 3
.
Since
\psi_{\eta}(x)=\phi_{*}(x/\eta)
in

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,1/4)\setminus\eta T}|\nabla\psi_{\eta}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\eta^{d-p}\int_{B(0,(4\eta)^{-1})\setminus T}|\nabla\phi_{*}|^{p}\,dx

(6.8)

\displaystyle\approx\left\{\begin{aligned} &\eta^{d-p}&\quad&\text{ if }p>d^{\prime},\\
&\eta^{d-p}|\ln\eta|&\quad&\text{ if }p=d^{\prime},\\
&\eta^{(d-2)p}&\quad&\text{ if }1<p<d^{\prime},\end{aligned}\right.

where we have used (
5.16
). We also used the fact that

|\nabla\phi_{*}|\in L^{p}(2T\setminus T)
for any
1<p<\infty
, under the assumption that
\partial T
is
C^{1}
.

To bound
\nabla\psi_{\eta}
on

w=\psi_{\eta}-1
is harmonic in
B(0,1/3)\setminus B(0,1/4)

w=\phi_{*}(x/\eta)-1
on
\partial B(0,1/4)
.
By (
5.16
) and regularity estimates for harmonic functions,
we obtain
|\nabla\psi_{\eta}|=|\nabla w|\leq C\eta^{d-2}
in
B(0,1/3)\setminus B(0,1/4)
.
This, together with (
6.8
), gives (

).
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\psi_{\eta}
be defined by (
6.5
)-(
6.6
) and extended periodically to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta\psi_{\eta}&=F_{\eta}+\text{\rm div}(f_{\eta})&\quad&\text{ in }\omega_{1,\eta},\\
\psi_{\eta}&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\omega_{1,\eta},\end{aligned}\right.

(6.9)

where
F_{\eta}
and

are

-periodic functions satisfying

\begin{aligned} |F_{\eta}|+|f_{\eta}|&\leq C\eta^{d-2}\end{aligned}\qquad\text{ in }Y\setminus\eta T

for

,
and

|F_{\eta}|+|f_{\eta}|\leq C|\ln\eta|^{-1}\qquad\text{ in }Y\setminus\eta T

(6.11)

for
d=2
. The constant
C
depends only on
d
and
T
.

Proof.

We first consider the case
d\geq 3
.
Let
\varphi
be a
Y
-periodic

\mathbb{R}^{d}

such that
\varphi=0

\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\omega_{1,\eta}

We need to show that

and
\eta_{\eta}
satisfying (
6.10
).
To this end,
observe that

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\nabla\psi_{\eta}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,1/3)\setminus B(0,1/4)}\nabla\psi_{\eta}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx+\int_{B(0,1/4)\setminus\eta T}\nabla\psi_{\eta}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
I_{1}
, recall that

|\nabla\psi_{\eta}(x)|\leq C\eta^{d-2}\qquad\text{ for }x\in B(0,1/3)\setminus B(0,1/4).

(6.12)

To handle

, using
\varphi=0
on

, we may write

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B(0,1/4)}\frac{\partial\psi_{\eta}}{\partial n}\varphi\,d\sigma,

(6.13)

where we also used the fact that
\psi_{\eta}
is harmonic in
B(0,1/4)\setminus{\eta T}
.
Let

on

.
By (

),
|g|+|\nabla g|\leq C\eta^{d-2}
.
Hence, there exists

such that

and

It follows that

\int_{\partial B(0,r)}g\varphi\,d\sigma=\frac{1}{r}\int_{B(0,r)}\{dG+x\cdot\nabla G\}\varphi\,dx+\frac{1}{r}\int_{B(0,r)}G(x\cdot\nabla\varphi)\,dx,

where

.
This, together with (
6.12
), yields (
6.9
) and (
6.10
).

The proof for the case

is similar. Indeed, note that

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\nabla\psi_{\eta}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,1/3)\setminus B(0,d\eta)}\nabla\psi_{\eta}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B(0,1/3)}\frac{\partial\psi_{\eta}}{\partial n}\varphi\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\ln(1/3)-\ln(d\eta)}\int_{\partial B(0,1/3)}\varphi\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{9}{\ln(1/3)-\ln(d\eta)}\int_{B(0,1/3)}\left(2\varphi+x\cdot\nabla\varphi\right)dx,

which yields the estimate (
6.11
).
∎

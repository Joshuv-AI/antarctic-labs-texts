When Locality Meets Preservation
Aliaume Lopez

Abstract
. This paper investigates the expressiveness of a fragment of first-order sentences in Gaifman normal form, namely the positive Boolean combinations of basic local sentences. We show that they match exactly the first-order sentences preserved under local elementary embeddings, thus providing a new general preservation theorem and extending the Łós-Tarski Theorem. This full preservation result fails as usual in the finite, and we show furthermore that the naturally related decision problems are undecidable. In the more restricted case of preservation under extensions, it nevertheless yields new well-behaved classes of finite structures: we show that preservation under extensions holds if and only if it holds locally.

Introduction

Preservation theorems.

In classical model
theory, preservation theorems characterise
first-order definable sets enjoying some semantic property as those
definable in a suitable syntactic fragment

(e.g.,
Chang and Keisler 1990
, Section 5.2)
.
A well-known instance of a preservation theorem is
the Łoś-Tarski Theorem
Tarski 1954
;
Łoś 1955
: a first-order
sentence
\varphi
is preserved under extensions over all structures—i.e.,

A\models\varphi
and
A
is an induced substructure of
B
imply

B\models\varphi
—if and only if it is equivalent to an existential
sentence.
Similarly, the Lyndon Positivity Theorem applied to a unary predicate
X

Lyndon 1959

connects surjective homomorphisms that are
strong

in every predicate except
X

to sentences that are positive in
X
.
These two preservation theorems can be seen as
bridges between syntactic and semantic fragments in
Figure

1
.

As most of classical model theory,
preservation theorems typically rely on compactness,
which is known to fail in the finite case.
Consequently,
preservation theorems generally do
not relativise to classes of structures, and in particular to the
class
\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)
of all finite structures [see the discussions
in
23
, Section 2 and
14
,
Section 3.4]. For example, Lyndon’s Positivity Theorem fails even
over the class of finite words
Kuperberg 2021
.
Nevertheless,
there are a few known instances of classes of finite structures where
some preservation theorems
hold
Atserias
et al. 2006
;
Rossman 2008
;
Rossman 2016
;
Harwath
et al. 2015
, and this type of question is still actively
investigated
(
Chen and Flum 2021
;
Kuperberg 2021
;
Dawar and
Sankaran 2021
, e.g.)
.

In the case of ‘order-based’ preservation theorems like
the Łoś-Tarski Theorem, that hinge on the interplay between the
properties of the ordering and those of first-order logic, one can
typically focus on either of these two aspects.

Focusing on the
ordering, one can ask for the class
\mathcal{C}
of
considered structures to
be
well-quasi-ordered
with respect to
the induced substructure ordering (hereafter written

\subseteq_{i}
).
In order-theoretic terms,
the set
\left\llbracket\varphi\right\rrbracket_{\mathcal{C}}

of models
of a sentence
\varphi
in
\mathcal{C}

is
upwards-closed
whenever

\varphi
is preserved under extensions.
The assumption that
\mathcal{C}
is well-quasi-ordered implies that

\left\llbracket\varphi\right\rrbracket_{\mathcal{C}}
has finitely many minimal elements
for
\subseteq_{i}
and this is well known to imply preservation under extensions. Practical
instances where
\subseteq_{i}
gives rise to a well-quasi-order are
scarce; it is the case for graphs of bounded tree-depth
(
Ding 1992
)
, but
to our knowledge the characterisation
by
Daligault
et al. 2010
of which classes of bounded clique-width are
well-quasi-ordered yields the broadest known such class
(see the left column in
Figure

3
).

Focusing on first-order logic, one can leverage finite model theory results over classes of
structures provided they are somewhat locally well-behaved; this can be ensured
for instance by some flavour of
sparsity
.
Two instances of
this are classes of bounded degree and the class of all graphs of
treewidth less than
k

Atserias
et al. 2008
. This is the
direction we take in this paper.

Locality.

Given a structure
A
over a finite relational signature
\sigma
,
its
Gaifman
graph
has the elements of
A
as vertices and an edge
(a,b)

whenever both
a
and
b
are in relation in
A
. The distance
d_{A}(a,b)
between two elements of
A
is their distance in the Gaifman graph
of
A
. For a tuple
\mathbf{a}\in A
, and a radius
r\in\mathbb{N}

one can consider the
r
-neighborhood around
\mathbf{a}
in
A
defined
as
\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)\triangleq\left\{a^{\prime}\in A\mid\exists a\in\mathbf{a},d_{A}(a,a^{\prime})\leq r\right\}=\bigcup_{a\in\mathbf{a}}\mathcal{N}_{A}(a,r)
; we emphasise that this union is not required to be disjoint.
Slightly abusing notations, we identify the set

\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)\subseteq_{i}A
with the corresponding induced
substructure of
A
.

A first-order formula
\varphi(\mathbf{x})
is said to
be
r
-local
if its evaluation over a structure
A
and a tuple

\mathbf{a}
from
A
only depends on the
r
-neighborhood of
\mathbf{a}

in
A
, i.e.
A,\mathbf{a}\models\varphi
if and only if

\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r),\mathbf{a}\models\varphi
. In the particular
case of
r=0
, local sentences are equivalent to quantifier free
sentences. Since
\upsigma
is a finite relational signature, every
formula
\varphi
can be
relativised
to a
r
-local formula,
hereafter denoted by
\varphi_{\leq r}
, which coincides with

\varphi
over neighborhoods of size
r
, although
\varphi_{\leq r}

might have a higher quantifier rank.

It is more delicate to craft a notion of locality
for first order sentences
since they have no free variables.
The classical approach is to consider

basic local sentences
of the form

\exists\mathbf{x}.\bigwedge_{i\neq j}d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\wedge\bigwedge_{i}\psi_{\leq r}(x_{i})

where
\psi_{\leq r}

is a formula relativised to the
r
neighborhood
of its single free variable.
Simply
put, the evaluation of a local
r
-basic sentence
is determined solely by the evaluation of

\psi_{\leq r}
over
disjoint

neighborhoods of radius
r
.
Note that the predicates
d(x,y)\leq r

and

y\in\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},r)

are definable for each fixed
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

The Gaifman Locality Theorem
Gaifman 1982

states
that every first-order sentence is equivalent to a Boolean combination of
basic local sentences.
This can be thought of as

\mathsf{FO}[\upsigma]

being limited to describing the

local behaviour
of structures.
In the study of preservation theorems
such as the Łoś-Tarski Theorem over finite structures,
a first step is often to use Gaifman’s normal form
and rely on the structural properties of
models
as a substitute for compactness
Atserias
et al. 2008
;
Atserias
et al. 2006
;
Harwath
et al. 2015
;
Dawar and
Sankaran 2021
.

In
Figure

1
several variants
of the Gaifman normal form are listed
along with their correspondence to
syntactic fragments of first-order logic.
For instance, in order to provide a faster
model checking algorithm,

Grohe and
Wöhrle 2004

crafted an
existential Gaifman normal form

as follows:
every existential first order sentence is equivalent to a
positive

Boolean combination of

existential
basic local sentences
Grohe and
Wöhrle 2004
,
where an existential
basic local sentence is of the form

\exists\mathbf{x}.\bigwedge_{i\neq j}d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\wedge\bigwedge_{i}\psi_{\leq r}(x_{i})

and
\psi_{\leq r}

is an
existential
formula relativised to the
r
-neighborhood
of its one free variable.
Note that this not an equivalence:

\exists x.\exists y.d(x,y)>2
is in existential Gaifman normal form
but not preserved under extensions nor equivalent
to an existential sentence.
Another variant
of the Gaifman normal form,
tailored to the study of Lyndon’s Positivity Theorem
over one unary predicate
X

is
defined by
Dawar
et al. 2006
.
It allows the authors to provide approximation schemes
for evaluating sentences positive in one unary predicate.

Contributions

Our first main contribution is a new line of equivalent characterisations
through local normal forms, syntactic restrictions, and preservation under
some ordering, as embodied in

This generalises the
correspondences between

defined by

1.1.1.
Existential Local Sentences and Positive Gaifman Normal Forms

This paper studies a positive variant
of locality through the prism of
existential closures of
r
-local formulas,
abbreviated here as
existential local sentences
. Those
are of the form

\exists\mathbf{x}.\tau(\mathbf{x})

where
\tau
is an
r
-local formula.

As opposed to basic local sentences,

existential local sentences

allow
interaction between the existentially quantified variables,
which increases their expressiveness.
Existential local sentences
also generalise existential sentences,
as quantifier-free formulas are
0
-local
(conversely,
0
-local formulas
can be rewritten as quantifier-free formulas).
Thus, by allowing formulas with a non-zero
locality radius, we provide a middle ground
between existential sentences and arbitrary sentences.

We prove that existential local sentences
and
positive
Boolean combinations of basic local sentences
are equally expressive,
regardless of the class of structures considered.
This theorem, proven in
Section

3
,
is related to the existential Gaifman normal form
of
Grohe and
Wöhrle 2004

and relies, in part, on similar combinatorial arguments.

Theorem 1.1

(Positive Locality)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\operatorname{Struct}(\upsigma)

be a class of structures
and

\varphi\in\mathsf{FO}[\upsigma]
be a first-order sentence.
The sentence
\varphi
is equivalent over
\mathcal{C}

to an existential local sentence if and only if
it is equivalent over
\mathcal{C}
to a positive Boolean
combination of basic local sentences.

1.1.2.
A Semantic Characterisation of Existential Local Sentences

Recall that a map
h\colon A\to B

is an
elementary embedding
whenever
for every first order sentence
\varphi(\mathbf{x})

and every
\mathbf{a}\in A^{k}

A,\mathbf{a}\models\varphi
if and only if

A,h(\mathbf{a})\models\varphi
.
A localised notion of elementary embeddings is obtained
as follows:

h\colon A\to B

is a
local elementary embedding

when for every
k\geq 1
,
r\geq 0
,
\mathbf{a}\in A^{k}
,
and
r
-local formula
\varphi

A,\mathbf{a}\models\varphi

if and only if
B,h(\mathbf{a})\models\varphi
.
This definition is a strengthening of the
induced substructure

ordering,
defined by
A\subseteq_{i}B
whenever there exists
an injective morphism

such that for all relations

and elements
\mathbf{a}\in A^{k}

A,\mathbf{a}\models R(\mathbf{x})

if and only if

B,h(\mathbf{a})\models R(\mathbf{x})
.

We define another natural
ordering on structures by writing

A\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}B

whenever,
for all
\varphi=\exists x_{1},\dots x_{k}.\tau(\mathbf{x})

where
\tau
is an
r
-local formula of quantifier rank at most
q

A\models\varphi
implies
B\models\varphi
.
An existential local sentence
is naturally preserved under
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}

for some
r,q\geq 0
and

.
We define the limit of those preorders as

(1)

\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{\infty},{\infty}}_{\infty}}\triangleq\bigcap_{r\geq 0}\bigcap_{q\geq 0}\bigcap_{k\geq 1}\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}\,.

Theorem 1.2

(Local preservation)
.

Let
\varphi
be a sentence in
\mathsf{FO}[\upsigma]
.
The following properties are equivalent
over the class of all structures
\operatorname{Struct}(\upsigma)
.

(a)

The sentence
\varphi
is equivalent to an existential local

(b)

There exist

such
that
\varphi
is preserved under
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}
.

(c)

The sentence
\varphi
is
preserved under
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{\infty},{\infty}}_{\infty}}
.

(d)

The sentence
\varphi

1.1.3.
Non-relativisation in the Finite

The proof of
Theorem

1.2
does not relativise
to classes of finite structures, except for the equivalence

(a)\Leftrightarrow(b)
. Our second main contribution
in
Section

5
is to show that
Theorem

1.2

fails over
\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)
the class of finite
structures, and to characterise for which parameters

preservation under

leads to an

.

The picture is even bleaker when applying the
methodology of

for the Łoś-Tarski Theorem

Namely, we show in

that

1.1.4.
Application to Preservation Under Extensions

We use our understanding of existential local sentences

(✁)

2.
Locality preorders

As most of this paper is centered
around preorders of the form
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}
,
we start by illustrating them
over several examples and
by relating them to well-known preorders
for specific values of
r,q
and
k
.

Given a structure
A\in\operatorname{Struct}(\upsigma)

and a tuple
\mathbf{a}
of elements from
A
,
let us write
\operatorname{tp}_{A}^{q}\left(\mathbf{a},r\right)
for
the
(q,r)
-local type of
\mathbf{a}
,
i.e.,
the set of all formulas
of quantifier rank at most
q
with
|\mathbf{a}|
free variables
that are
r
-local such that
(A,\mathbf{a})\models\varphi(\mathbf{x})
.
Note that there are only finitely many possible local types
for a given pair
(q,r)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
and a given number of variables
|\mathbf{a}|
.
Those local types can be collected to fully describe the local
behaviour of
A
in

(3)

\operatorname{Types}^{q,k}_{r}(A)\triangleq\left\{\operatorname{tp}_{A}^{q}\left(\mathbf{a},r\right)\mid\mathbf{a}\in A^{\leq k}\right\}\;.

We shall often use this collection of types to
reason with our preorders.

Fact 2.1

(Type collection)
.

Let
r,q,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 1
.
For all
A,B\in\operatorname{Struct}(\upsigma)
,

A\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}B

if and only if

\operatorname{Types}^{q,k}_{r}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{Types}^{q,k}_{r}(B)
.

Proof.

Assume that
A\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}B
. Let us write
T
for the
finite set (up to logical equivalence) of possible
r
-local
formulas at radius
r
, quantifier rank
q
and
k
variables.
Given a vector
\mathbf{a}\in A^{k}
, define

T_{+}\triangleq\operatorname{tp}_{A}^{q}\left(\mathbf{a},r\right)
and
T_{-}\triangleq T\setminus T_{+}
. Those are finite collections of
r
-local
formulas of quantifier rank at most
q
with
k
free variables

x_{1},\dots x_{k}
. Let us write

\psi(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\bigwedge_{t\in T_{+}}t(\mathbf{x})\wedge\bigwedge_{t\in T_{-}}\neg t(\mathbf{x})
, which is

r
-local and of quantifier rank at most
q
. The structure
A

satisfies
\varphi\triangleq\exists\mathbf{x}.\psi(\mathbf{x})
through
the choice of the vector
\mathbf{a}
. Because
A\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}B
,

B\models\varphi
and this provides a vector
\mathbf{b}\in B^{k}

such that
B,\mathbf{b}\models\psi(\mathbf{x})
. In turn, this proves that
the sentences of quantifier rank at most
q
and locality radius
r

that hold over

are exactly those in

. Finally,

\operatorname{tp}_{B}^{q}\left(\mathbf{b},r\right)=\operatorname{tp}_{A}^{q}\left(\mathbf{a},r\right)
.

Conversely, assume that

\operatorname{Types}^{q,k}_{r}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{Types}^{q,k}_{r}(B)
. Let

\varphi
be a sentence of the shape

\exists\mathbf{x}.\tau(\mathbf{x})
, where

and

\tau(\mathbf{x})
is an

-local formula of quantifier rank at most

, that is true in

. There exists a vector
\mathbf{a}\in A^{k}

such that

, in particular

\tau(\mathbf{x})\in\operatorname{tp}_{A}^{q}\left(\mathbf{a},r\right)

\operatorname{Types}^{q,k}_{r}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{Types}^{q,k}_{r}(B)
, there

\mathbf{b}\in B^{k}
such that
\operatorname{tp}_{A}^{q}\left(\mathbf{a},r\right)=\operatorname{tp}_{B}^{q}\left(\mathbf{b},r\right)

and therefore

Lemma 2.4

Let

.
By construction, the tuple
h(\mathbf{a})\in B^{k}

satisfies the same local
\mathsf{FO}
formulas,
and in particular,
\operatorname{tp}_{A}^{q}\left(\mathbf{a},r\right)=\operatorname{tp}_{B}^{q}\left(h(\mathbf{a}),r\right)
, thus
A\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}B
.
∎

Lemma 2.5

(Preorders in the finite)
.

Let
A,B
be two finite structures in
\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)
. The
following statements are equivalent.

(1)

There exists
C
such that
A\uplus C=B
.

(2)

A\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{\infty},{\infty}}_{\infty}}B
.

(3)

A\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{\infty}}_{q}}B
for some
r,q\geq 1
.

(4)

There exists a local elementary embedding from
A
to
B
.

Proof of
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
.

Given a vector
\mathbf{a}\in A^{k}
, a radius
r
, and
quantifier rank
q
,
one notices that

\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)=\mathcal{N}_{A\uplus C}(\mathbf{a},r)
, hence

\operatorname{tp}_{A}^{q}\left(\mathbf{a},r\right)=\operatorname{tp}_{B}^{q}\left(\mathbf{a},r\right)
.
We have proven that
A\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{\infty},{\infty}}_{\infty}}B
.
∎

Proof of
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
.

By definition.
∎

Proof of
(3)\Rightarrow(4)
.

Let us consider
\mathbf{a}\in A^{|A|}

a vector containing all the points of
A

exactly once.
There exists a vector
\mathbf{b}\in B^{|A|}

such that

\operatorname{tp}_{A}^{1}\left(\mathbf{a},1\right)=\operatorname{tp}_{B}^{1}\left(\mathbf{b},1\right)
.
As a consequence,
\mathcal{N}_{B}(\mathbf{b},1)=\mathcal{N}_{B}(\mathbf{b},0)

since this equation holds for
\mathbf{a}
and is expressible
using one universal quantifier.
The mapping
h\colon\mathbf{a}\mapsto\mathbf{b}
is
a local elementary embedding. Indeed,

r
-neighborhoods around
\mathbf{a}
(resp.
\mathbf{b}
)
are subsets of
\mathbf{a}
(resp.
\mathbf{b}
). This
proves that
r
-local formulas around
\mathbf{a}
(resp.
\mathbf{b}
)
can be rewritten as
0
-local, and we conclude using the equality
of their
(1,1)
-local types.
∎

Proof of
(4)\Rightarrow(1)
.

Let
h\colon A\to B
be a local elementary embedding.
Let
D
be the substructure induced by
h(A)
in
B
; since

h
is a local elementary embedding,
A
and
D
are isomorphic.
Let us consider a point

.
Assume by contradiction that
there exists a relation containing both
c

and an element of
d\in D
. Let
\varphi(\mathbf{x})
be
a
1
-local formula of quantifier rank
1

with
|A|
free variables
stating that there exists a point not in
\mathbf{x}

connected to some element of
\mathbf{x}
.
Since

is a local elementary embedding and

,

B,h(A)\models\varphi(\mathbf{x})

and

. This is absurd,
hence
B=D\uplus(B\setminus D)=A\uplus(B\setminus D)
.
∎

However, the existence of a

\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{\infty},{\infty}}_{\infty}}
, as shown next.

Example 2.6

(Preorder difference)
.

Over the signature of graphs, let

and

. There exists no local elementary

to

.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that there exists an elementary embedding
h
from

to

. There exist two points

in

that are at an infinite distance,
and their images

are at a finite distance

in

.
The sentence
\psi(x,y)
stating that

and

are at distance

is

and

, hence
is satisfied for

and

since given

,

Over finite structures, when the parameter values of

or

are

.

Fact 2.7

(Trivial orders)
.

Over finite structures, the following preorders are trivial, i.e. every pair of structures is related:
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{0}}_{q}}
whenever

r,q\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, and
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{\infty},{1}}_{0}}
.

Lemma 2.8

(Extension preorder)
.

Over finite structures and for all

q,r\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
,

{\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{0},{\infty}}_{q}}}={\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{\infty}}_{0}}}={\subseteq_{i}}
.

Proof.

We first notice that
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{0},{\infty}}_{q}}

and
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{\infty}}_{0}}
cannot quantify
over the neighborhoods of a vector
\mathbf{a}

since they either contain only
\mathbf{a}

or cannot quantify at all. This explains the first equality.

(1)

Assume
A\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{0},{\infty}}_{q}}B
for some
q\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, consider

k\triangleq|A|

and let
\mathbf{a}\in A^{k}
be the list of all elements in
A
.
Assume without loss of generality that
q
is finite.
The inequality provides a vector
\mathbf{b}\in B^{k}

such that
\operatorname{tp}_{A}^{q}\left(\mathbf{a},0\right)=\operatorname{tp}_{B}^{q}\left(\mathbf{b},0\right)
.
In particular,
\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},0)=A
and

\mathcal{N}_{B}(\mathbf{b},0)=\mathbf{b}
.
Since it is possible to express using quantifier free formulas

A
,
the substructure of
B
induced by
\mathbf{b}

is isomorphic to
A
.
In particular,
A\subseteq_{i}B
.

(2)

Assume that
A\subseteq_{i}B
though a map
h
,
let
k\in\mathbb{N}

and
\mathbf{a}\in A^{k}
.
Let us define
\mathbf{b}\triangleq h(\mathbf{a})
;
in particular,
\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},0)=A
and

\mathcal{N}_{B}(\mathbf{b},0)=h(\mathbf{a})
.
By definition of the map

,

induces a substructure of
B

that is isomorphic to the substructure induced by
\mathbf{a}
in

.
This entails that the two structures are elementary equivalent,
hence that

\operatorname{tp}_{A}^{q}\left(\mathbf{a},0\right)=\operatorname{tp}_{B}^{q}\left(\mathbf{b},0\right)

for all

.
We have proven that

for all

.
∎

3.
Positive Gaifman Normal Form

The aim of this section is to provide
a connection between the positive variant of Gaifman normal forms
and existential local sentences.
The theorem and its proof are heavily inspired by the combinatorics
behind
Grohe and
Wöhrle 2004
’s proof
of the existential Gaifman normal form. In particular,
the main issue arises from
finding points with disjoint neighborhoods.

As basic local sentences are existential local,
the only difficulty in
Theorem

1.1

is converting an existential local sentence
into a positive Boolean combination of basic local sentences.
We split this transformation into intermediate syntactic steps

Existential local:

\exists\mathbf{x}.\psi(\mathbf{x})

where
\psi
is an
r
-local formula.

Almost basic local:

\exists\mathbf{x}.\bigwedge_{i\neq j}d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\wedge\psi(\mathbf{x})

where
\psi
is an
r
-local formula.

Asymmetric basic local:

\exists\mathbf{x}.\bigwedge_{i\neq j}d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\wedge\bigwedge_{i}\psi_{i}(x_{i})

where
(\psi_{i})_{i}
is a family
of
r
-local formulas with exactly one free variable.

Basic local:

\exists\mathbf{x}.\bigwedge_{i\neq j}d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\wedge\bigwedge_{i}\psi(x_{i})

where
\psi
is an

r
-local formula with exactly one free variable.

Recall that a formula
\varphi\left(\mathbf{x}\right)
can be made

r
-local around
\mathbf{x}
by relativising quantifications
to
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},r)
. As a consequence, the classes
above can equivalently be defined syntactically.

To provide some intuition about the behaviour of
theses syntactic restrictions interpolating between
existential local sentences and basic local sentences,
let us use examples in plain English using two colours “green”
and “blue”, writing increasingly complex sentences.
A basic local sentence can ask “whether there exists at least

2
disjoint neighbourhoods containing only green points”,
which can be rewritten as

\exists x_{1},x_{2}.d(x_{1},x_{2})>2r\wedge\text{green}_{r}(x_{1})\wedge\text{green}_{r}(x_{2})
.
An asymmetric basic local sentence
can ask “whether there exists
2
disjoint neighbourhoods,
one green and one blue”,
which can be rewritten as

\exists x_{1},x_{2}.d(x_{1},x_{2})>2r\wedge\text{green}_{r}(x_{1})\wedge\text{blue}_{r}(x_{2})
.
The asymmetry comes from the ability to select a different
property for each disjoint neighbourhood.
An almost basic local sentence
can ask “whether there exists
2
disjoint neighbourhoods
that have the same colour”,
which can be rewritten as

\exists x_{1},x_{2}.d(x_{1},x_{2})>2r\wedge(\text{green}_{r}(x_{1})\wedge\text{green}_{r}(x_{2}))\vee(\text{blue}_{r}(x_{1})\wedge\text{blue}_{r}(x_{2}))
.
An existential local sentence
can ask “whether there exists
2
points such that
every green point connected to one is connected to the other”,
which can be rewritten as

\exists x_{1},x_{2}.\forall y\in\mathcal{N}(x_{1}x_{2},r).(\text{green}(y)\wedge E(x_{1},y))\iff(\text{green}(y)\wedge E(x_{2},y))
.

Asymmetric basic local sentences already appear as an intermediate
steps towards basic local sentences in the constructions
of
Grohe and
Wöhrle 2004
and
Dawar
et al. 2006
. Most of the
transformations will rely on the description of the ‘spatial’
repartition of elements in a given structure
A
. We handle this
description through the following lemma.
The intended purpose of
Lemma

3.1

is to build, from a neighbourhood
\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)
, a
disjoint union of neighbourhoods containing the former one
using a radius and a number of points controlled independently of the structure
A
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
k,r\geq 0
,
for every structure
A\in\operatorname{Struct}(\upsigma)

and vector
\mathbf{a}\in A^{\leq k}

there exists
a vector
\mathbf{b}\in A^{\leq k}

and a radius
r\leq R\leq 4^{k}r

such that

\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{b},R)

and
\forall b\neq b^{\prime}\in\mathbf{b},\mathcal{N}_{A}(b,3R)\cap\mathcal{N}_{A}(b^{\prime},3R)=\emptyset
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction over
k
.

•

When
k\leq 1
it suffices to take

R\triangleq r
and for every vector
\mathbf{a}\in A^{\leq k}

build

\mathbf{b}\triangleq\mathbf{a}

and notice that

\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)=\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{b},R)
.

•

When
k\geq 2
, we proceed by a simple case analysis

(1)

Either the balls
\mathcal{N}_{A}(a,3r)
are pairwise disjoint
when
a
ranges over
\mathbf{a}
;
in which case it suffices to consider
\mathbf{b}\triangleq\mathbf{a}
and
R\triangleq r
to conclude.

(2)

Or at least two of the balls
\mathcal{N}_{A}(a,3r)

intersect
when
a
ranges over
\mathbf{a}
,
and we can assume without loss of generality
that the neighborhoods of
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
at
radius
3r
intersect.

Let us consider
c\in\mathcal{N}_{A}(a_{1},3r)\cap\mathcal{N}_{A}(a_{2},3r)
.
Define
\mathbf{c}\triangleq(c,a_{3},\ldots,a_{k})
,
this vector is of size
1
when

k=2
.

Because
d(a_{1},c)\leq 3r
and
d(a_{2},c)\leq 3r
,

\mathcal{N}_{A}(a_{1}a_{2},r)\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{A}(c,4r)
.

By induction hypothesis,
there exists a radius
4r\leq R\leq 4^{k-1}(4r)

and a vector
\mathbf{b}\in A^{\leq k-1}

such that

\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{c},4r)\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{b},R)

and

\forall b\neq b^{\prime}\in\mathbf{b},\mathcal{N}_{A}(b,3R)\cap\mathcal{N}_{A}(b^{\prime},3R)=\emptyset
.

Since

\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{c},4r)

and
r\leq 4r\leq R\leq 4^{k}r

we concluded.
∎

3.1.
From Existential Local Sentences to Asymmetric Basic Local Sentences

Using
Lemma

3.1

it is already possible
to transform an existential local sentence
into a positive Boolean combination
of
almost
basic local sentences.

Lemma 3.2

(From Existential local to Almost basic local)
.

Let
\varphi(\mathbf{x})
be an
r
-local formula.
There exist
1\leq n\leq 4^{|\mathbf{x}|}r
and

\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}

almost basic local sentences
such that

\exists\mathbf{x}.\varphi(\mathbf{x})

is equivalent to

\bigvee_{1\leq i\leq k}\psi_{i}

over
\operatorname{Struct}(\upsigma)
.

Proof.

Let us define
\Delta\triangleq\left\{(k,R)\mid 0\leq k\leq|\mathbf{x}|\wedge r\leq R\leq 4^{|\mathbf{x}|}r\right\}

and

(4)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\psi_{(k,R)}&\triangleq&\exists b_{1},\dots,b_{k}.\\
&&\bigwedge_{1\leq i<j\leq k}d(b_{i},b_{j})>2R\\
&&\wedge\>\exists\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{b},R).\varphi(\mathbf{x})\end{array}

To conclude it suffices to prove that

\exists\mathbf{x}.\varphi(\mathbf{x})

is equivalent to

\bigvee_{(k,R)\in\Delta}\psi_{(k,R)}\triangleq\Psi
.

•

Assume that
A\in\operatorname{Struct}(\upsigma)

satisfies
\exists\mathbf{x}.\varphi(\mathbf{x})
.
Then there exists
\mathbf{a}\in A^{|\mathbf{x}|}

such that
\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)\models\exists\mathbf{x}.\varphi(\mathbf{x})
since
\varphi
is
r
-local around
\mathbf{x}
.
Using
Lemma

3.1
,
there exists a size
0\leq k\leq|\mathbf{a}|

a radius

r\leq R\leq 4^{|\mathbf{a}|}r
,
and a vector
\mathbf{b}\in A^{k}
such that

\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{b},R)

and the balls of radius
3R
around the
points of
\mathbf{b}
do not intersect.

In particular,

\biguplus_{b\in\mathbf{b}}\mathcal{N}_{A}(b,R)\models\exists\mathbf{x}.\varphi(\mathbf{x})
since witnesses in

\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)
can still be found
and
\varphi
is
r
-local.
This proves that
A\models\psi_{(k,R)}

hence that
A\models\Psi
.

•

Assume that
A\in\operatorname{Struct}(\upsigma)

satisfies
\Psi
.
Then there exists
(k,R)
such that

A\models\psi_{k,R}

thus proving that
there exists
\mathbf{b}\in A^{k}

such that

\biguplus_{b\in\mathbf{b}}\mathcal{N}_{A}(b,R)\models\exists\mathbf{x}.\varphi(\mathbf{x})
.
Since
r\leq R
and
\varphi
is
r
-local
this proves

A\models\exists\mathbf{x}.\varphi(\mathbf{x})
.
∎

An application of
the Feferman-Vaught technique
Makowsky 2004
;
Feferman and
Vaught 1959

allows to transform
almost
basic local sentences
into the
asymmetric
ones introduced
by
Grohe and
Wöhrle 2004
, i.e. to sentences of the form

\exists\mathbf{x}.\bigwedge_{i\neq j}d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\wedge\bigwedge_{i}\psi_{i}(x_{i})

where each
\psi_{i}
is a
r
-local formula
when
i
ranges from
1
to
|\mathbf{x}|
.
The combination of this transformation
with
Lemma

3.2
generalises a similar statement over
existential sentences
(
Grohe and
Wöhrle 2004
, Theorem 6)

to existential local sentences.

Lemma 3.3

(From Almost basic local to Asymmetric Basic local)
.

Every almost basic local sentence
is equivalent to a disjunction of
asymmetric basic local sentences.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\triangleq\exists\mathbf{x}.\bigwedge_{i\neq j}d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\wedge\psi(\mathbf{x})

where
\psi
is a
r
-local formula around
\mathbf{x}
.
Following a syntactical proof of Feferman-Vaught
given by Thomas Colcombet,
we introduce
types

T_{1},\dots,T_{|\mathbf{x}|}

and define
typed formulas inductively as follows

\displaystyle\tau:=\,

\displaystyle x:T\mid R(x_{1}:T,\dots,x_{n}:T)\quad\text{ when }R\in\upsigma

\displaystyle\tau\wedge\tau\mid\tau\vee\tau\mid\neg\tau\mid

\displaystyle\exists x:T.\tau\mid\forall x:T.\tau

In this new language, a relation can only be checked using variables
of the same type
T
.
The evaluation of a formula
\tau
is defined as the
evaluation of the formula
\tau^{\prime}
obtained by removing type annotations.

An
environment

\rho
is a mapping from finitely many variables to
types. Given an environment
\rho
, we write
\rho[x\mapsto T]

to denote the environment
\rho^{\prime}
obtained by
\rho^{\prime}(y)\triangleq\rho(y)

if
x\neq y
and
\rho^{\prime}(x)=T
.
We first translate
\psi
into a typed formula inductively
given an environment
\rho
from the free variables of
\psi

to types:

•

f(\exists x.\psi,\rho)=\bigvee_{1\leq i\leq|\mathbf{x}|}\exists x:T_{i}.f(\psi,\rho[x\mapsto T_{i}])
,

•

f(\psi_{1}\vee\psi_{2},\rho)=f(\psi_{1},\rho)\vee f(\psi_{2},\rho)
,

•

f(\neg\psi,\rho)=\neg f(\psi,\rho)
,

•

f(R(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}),\rho)=R(y_{1}:\rho(y_{1}),\dots,y_{n}:\rho(y_{n}))
if all the variables have the same type under
\rho
,
and

f(R(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}),\rho)=\bot
otherwise.

By a straightforward induction on the formula
\psi
,
one can show that for every structure
A
,
every valuation
\nu
from variables to elements of
A
,
and environment
\rho
mapping free variables of
\psi

to types, such that
d_{A}(\nu(x),\nu(y))\leq 1

implies
\rho(x)=\rho(y)

the following holds:

A,\nu\models\psi(\mathbf{x})
if and only
if
A,\nu\models f(\psi(\mathbf{x}),\rho)
.
The only non trivial case is the translation of relations,
which is handled of through the hypothesis on
\nu
and
\rho
.

As a consequence, for every structure
A
,

A\models\exists\mathbf{x}.\bigwedge_{i\neq j}d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\wedge\psi(\mathbf{x})

if and only if

A\models\exists\mathbf{x}.\bigwedge_{i\neq j}d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\wedge f(\psi(\mathbf{x}),x_{i}\mapsto T_{i})
.

A second induction allows us to prove that
typed formulas are equivalent to positive Boolean combinations
of
monotyped
formulas, i.e. formulas where only one type
T

appears.
The only non-trivial case is the existential quantification,
handled through the equivalence between

\exists x:T.(\psi_{1}\wedge\psi_{2})

and
(\exists x:T.\psi_{1})\wedge\psi_{2}

whenever
\psi_{2}
contains no variable of type
T
.

Since the environment
\rho\colon x_{i}\mapsto T_{i}

assigns a different type to every free variable of
\psi(\mathbf{x})
,
the positive Boolean combination obtained by transforming
f(\psi(\mathbf{x}),\rho)

is composed of formulas with exactly one free variable. Let us write

\bigvee_{n}\bigwedge_{1 \leq m\leq k}\tau_{n,m}(x_{m}:T_{m})

for this positive Boolean combination.
For every structure
A
,

A
models
\exists\mathbf{x}.\bigwedge_{i\neq j}d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\wedge\psi(\mathbf{x})

if and only if

A
models
\exists\mathbf{x}.\bigwedge_{i\neq j}d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\wedge\bigvee_{n}\bigwedge_{1\leq m\leq k}\tau_{n,m}(x_{m}:T_{m})
.
By removing type annotations on the formulas
\tau_{n,m}

and considering their relativisation to the
r
-neighborhood
of their single free variable, one obtains
\tau_{n,m}^{r}(x)
.
Let us prove that this relativisation preserves the expected equivalence
with
\varphi
:

\displaystyle A\models\varphi

\displaystyle\iff\exists\mathbf{a}\in A^{k},\bigwedge_{1\leq i\neq j\leq k}d_{A}(a_{i},a_{j})>2r\wedge A,\mathbf{a}\models\psi(\mathbf{x})

\psi
is
r
-local

\displaystyle\iff\exists\mathbf{a}\in A^{k},\bigwedge_{1\leq i\neq j\leq k}d_{A}(a_{i},a_{j})>2r\wedge\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r),\mathbf{a}\models\psi(\mathbf{x})

by definition

\displaystyle\iff\exists\mathbf{a}\in A^{k},\bigwedge_{1\leq i\neq j\leq k}d_{A}(a_{i},a_{j})>2r

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\wedge\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r),\mathbf{a}\models\bigvee_{n}\bigwedge_{1\leq m\leq k}\tau_{n,m}(x_{m}:T_{m})

disjunctions

\displaystyle\iff\exists n.\exists\mathbf{a}\in A^{k},\bigwedge_{1\leq i\neq j\leq k}d_{A}(a_{i},a_{j})>2r

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\wedge\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r),\mathbf{a}\models\bigwedge_{1\leq m\leq k}\tau_{n,m}(x_{m}:T_{m})

relativisation

\displaystyle\iff\exists n.\exists\mathbf{a}\in A^{k},\bigwedge_{1\leq i\neq j\leq k}d_{A}(a_{i},a_{j})>2r\wedge A,\mathbf{a}\models\bigwedge_{1\leq m\leq k}\tau_{n,m}^{r}(x_{m})

\exists
and
\wedge

\displaystyle\iff\exists n.A\models\exists\mathbf{x}.\bigwedge_{1\leq i\neq j\leq k}d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\wedge\bigwedge_{1\leq m\leq k}\tau_{n,m}^{r}(x_{m})

We have expressed
\varphi
as a disjunction of asymmetric basic local
sentences

\varphi\equiv\bigvee_{n}\exists x_{1},\dots,x_{k}.\bigwedge_{1\leq i\neq j\leq k}d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\wedge\bigwedge_{1\leq m\leq k}\tau_{n,m}^{r}(x_{m})\,.\qed

3.2.
From Asymmetric Basic Local to Basic Local Sentences

We are now ready to build the final transformation
between asymmetric basic local sentences
and basic local sentences,
reusing some of the combinatorics of
Grohe and
Wöhrle 2004
.

As a convenience, let us write

\phi/i
for the sentence
\varphi
where the variable
x_{i}

and local sentence
\psi_{i}
are ‘removed.’ For instance,
if
\phi\triangleq\exists x_{1},x_{2}.d(x_{1},x_{2})>2r\wedge\psi_{1}(x_{1})\wedge\psi_{2}(x_{2})
then

\phi/1=\exists x_{2}.\psi_{2}(x_{2})
and

\phi/2=\exists x_{1}.\psi_{1}(x_{1})

but not equivalent to an existential local
sentence over
\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)
.

5.2.
Undecidability

We have proven that deciding whether a sentence preserved under
local elementary embeddings is equivalent to an
existential local sentence is a non-trivial problem in
\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)
.
We strengthen this by proving said problem is actually undecidable.
This work was initially
inspired by the work of
Kuperberg 2021

proving that Lyndon’s Positivity Theorem fails for finite words
and providing undecidability of the associated decision problem.
Moreover, the statements and theorems can be interpreted
as variations of those from

Chen and Flum 2021
,
who considers preservation under induced substructures.

Given a Universal Turing Machine
U

over an alphabet
\Sigma
and with control states

Q
,
we extend the signature of
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
with
unary predicates
q/1
for
q\in Q
,

P_{a}/1
for
a\in\Sigma
,

P_{\$}/1
and
P_{\square}/1

to encode configurations of
U
in
\leq
-components.
Without loss of generality, we assume that this Universal
Turing Machine accepts only on a specific state
q_{f}^{a}\in Q

and rejects only on a specific state
q_{f}^{r}\in Q
;
those two states being the only ones with no possible forward transitions.
In a structure
A
, we call
C(a)
the
\leq
-component of
a\in A
.

Fact 5.8
.

There exists a
1
-local formula
\theta_{C}(x)

such that for all structure
A\in\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
, element
a\in A
,

A,a\models\theta_{C}(x)
if and only if

C(a)
represents a valid configuration of
U
.

The only difficulty in representing
runs of the machine
U
is
to map positions from one
\leq
-component
to its successor.
To that end, we exploit the
first-order definability of the function

f\colon a\mapsto\max_{\leq}\left\{d\mid E(a,d)\right\}

that links
\leq
-components

Lemma 5.9

(Transitions are definable)
.

There exists a
1
-local formula
\theta_{T}(x,y)

such that for every structure

A\in\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
and points
a,b\in A

the following two properties are equivalent:

(i)

The
\leq
-components of
a
and
b

are connected through
S
and
represent valid configurations
C
and
C^{\prime}

satisfying
C\to_{U}C^{\prime}
,

(ii)

A,ab\models\theta_{T}(x,y)
.

Proof.

This follows the standard encoding of transitions.
We check that the sentence is
1
-local since
the ball of radius
1
around
a
(or
b
) contains
both
\leq
-components entirely.

The only technical issue is relating
positions in the configuration
C(a)
to positions
in the configuration
C(b)
, which
is done through the use of the function

f\colon a\mapsto\max_{\leq}\left\{d\mid E(a,d)\right\}

which is first-order definable and
1
-local.
Let us spell out the definition of
f
as a formula:

\phi_{f}(x,y)\triangleq E(x,y)\wedge\forall z.y<z\implies\neg E(x,z)

For instance, to assert
that every letter except those near the current position
in the tape are left unchanged, one can first write a formula
stating that the position of the head of the Turing Machine
in the
\leq
-component of
x
is not close to
x
:

\phi_{Q}(x)\triangleq\forall z.(z\leq x\vee x\leq z)\wedge(S(x,z)\vee S(z,x)\vee x=z)\implies\bigwedge_{q\in Q}\neg q(x)

As a shorthand, let us write
z\in C(x)
instead of
z\leq x\vee x\leq z
.
Using
\phi_{f}
and
\phi_{Q}
it is easy to write a formula stating
that letters far from the head of the Turing Machine are unchanged
in a transition:

\displaystyle\phi(x,y)\triangleq

\displaystyle\forall z.z\in C(x)\wedge\phi_{Q}(z)

\displaystyle\implies\exists z^{\prime}.z^{\prime}\in C(y)\wedge\phi_{f}(z,z^{\prime})\wedge\bigwedge_{a\in\Sigma\cup\{\$,\square\}}P_{a}(z)\Leftrightarrow P_{a}(z^{\prime})

We can assert that some specific transition has been
taken similarly, by first checking the movement of the head,
change of letters around the head, and the evolution of the state.
∎

Theorem 5.13

(Undecidability)
.

It is in general not possible
to decide whether a sentence
\varphi

closed under disjoint unions
over
\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)

has an existential local equivalent
form.

Proof.

Without loss of generality
thanks to
5.2
, we only work over

\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
, and
we reduce from the halting problem.

Let
M
be a Turing Machine
and
\langle M\rangle
its code in the alphabet of the
Universal Turing Machine
U
.
Let
\varphi_{M}=\exists x.\theta_{I}^{\langle M\rangle}(x)\wedge\forall x,y.S(x,y)\wedge\neg(x\leq y)\implies\theta_{T}(x,y)
.
We consider the sentence
\varphi\triangleq\varphi_{M}\vee\varphi_{CC}

that is closed under disjoint unions over
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
. This sentence
is computable from the data
\langle M\rangle
.

Assume that
M
halts in at most
k^{\prime}
steps,
there exists a bound
k
for the run of the universal
Turing Machine
U
.
Given a size
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\varphi_{M}^{n}
to be the existential local sentence

\exists x_{1},\dots,x_{n}.\theta_{I}^{\langle M\rangle}(x_{1})\wedge\theta_{T}(x_{1},x_{2})\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{T}(x_{n-1},x_{n})\wedge\theta_{N}(x_{n})
.
It is a routine check that
\varphi
is equivalent to

\varphi_{CC}\vee\bigvee_{1\leq k\leq n}\varphi_{M}^{n}
over
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
.

Assume that
M
does not halt.
The universal Turing Machine
U
does not halt
on the word
\langle M\rangle
.
Assume by contradiction that
\varphi
is equivalent to a sentence

\psi\triangleq\exists x_{1},\dots,x_{k}.\theta

where
\theta
is
r
-local.
Find a run of size greater than
k\cdot(2r+1)

and evaluate
\psi
. It cannot look at all the configurations
simultaneously; change the state of the one not seen: it still satisfies

\psi
but this is no longer a run of
U
, which is absurd.
∎

Theorem 5.14

(Undecidable semantic property)
.

It not possible, given a sentence
\varphi
,
to decide whether or not it is closed under disjoint unions
over
\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we only work over

\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
, and
we reduce from the halting problem.
Consider the sentence

\varphi_{M}\triangleq\exists x.\theta_{I}^{\langle M\rangle}(x)\wedge\exists x.\theta_{F}(x)\wedge\forall x,y.S(x,y)\wedge\neg(x\leq y)\Rightarrow\theta_{T}(x,y)
,
and let
\varphi=\varphi_{M}\wedge\neg\varphi_{CC}
.

Assume that
M
halts. There
exists exactly one model of
\varphi
, the unique
run of the universal Turing machine
U
,
and this contradicts closure under disjoint unions.

Assume that
M
does not halt.
The sentence
\varphi
has no finite model and
in particular is closed under disjoint unions.

Hence, the sentence
\varphi
is
closed under disjoint unions
if and only if
M
does not halt.
∎

Theorem 5.15

(Uncomputable equivalence)
.

There is no algorithm,
which given a sentence
\varphi

that is equivalent to an existential local
sentence over
\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)

computes such a sentence.

Proof.

Without loss of generality
thanks to
5.2
, we only work over

\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
, and
we reduce from the halting problem.
Let
M
be a Turing Machine,

\varphi_{M}\triangleq\exists x.\theta_{I}^{\langle M\rangle}(x)\wedge\exists x.\theta_{F}(x)\wedge\forall x,y.S(x,y)\wedge\neg(x\leq y)\implies\theta_{T}(x,y)
,
and
\varphi\triangleq\varphi_{M}\vee\varphi_{CC}
.

Assume that
M
halts. Then
\varphi

is equivalent to an existential local sentence

\varphi^{\prime}\vee\varphi_{CC}
,
as noticed in the proof of
Theorem

5.13
.
Assume that
M
does not halt.
Then
\varphi
is equivalent to
\varphi_{CC}
,
which is existential local.
We have proven that
\varphi
is equivalent to an existential
local sentence in all cases.

Assume by contradiction that there exists
an algorithm
computing an existential local sentence
\mu

that is equivalent to
\varphi_{M}\vee\varphi_{CC}
over
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
.
One can use
\mu
to decide whether
M
halts.
Let us write
k

for the number of existential quantifiers of
\mu

and
r
for the locality radius of its inner formula.
If the sentence
\mu
accepts the coding of a run of size greater
than
2k\cdot(2r+1)
, then it accepts
structures that are not coding runs of
U
,
and
\mu
cannot be equivalent to
\varphi
.
As a consequence, if
M
terminates, it must
terminate in
at most
2k\cdot(2r+1)
steps, which is decidable.
∎

5.3.
Generalisation to weaker preorders

We characterise in this section the set of parameters
(r,q,k)
for which
a sentence preserved under
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}
over

\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)
is equivalent to an existential local sentence
through a finer analysis of

\mathcal{A}_{\textnormal{ord}}
.

Lemma 5.16

(Failure at arbitrary radius)
.

There exists a sentence
\varphi_{B}

preserved under
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{\infty},{k}}_{q}}
over
\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)

for
k\geq 2
and
q\geq 1

that is not equivalent to an existential local
sentence over
\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)
.

Proof.

Using
2.2
it suffices to consider
the case where
k=2
and
q=1
to conclude.

A first check is that
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}

can alternatively be defined by
sentences that have at most
2
universal quantifiers
over variables
x_{1}
and
x_{2}

and then one variable
y
at distance at most 1
from the tuple
x_{1}x_{2}
.
This proves that
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
is definable
and downwards closed for
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{1},{2}}_{1}}
.
In particular, sentences preserved under
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{\infty},{2}}_{1}}

over
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
are preserved under
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{\infty},{2}}_{1}}

over
\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)
, which is a strengthening of

5.2
.

Using the same syntactical analysis,
\varphi_{CC}
is preserved
under
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{\infty},{2}}_{1}}
over
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
.
As a consequence it is an easy check that

\varphi_{B}\triangleq\forall x.\neg B(x)\vee\varphi_{CC}

is preserved under
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{\infty},{2}}_{1}}

over
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
.
Moreover, it was stated in
Lemma

5.4

that
\varphi_{B}
cannot be defined as an existential local sentence
over
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
.
∎

Lemma 5.17

(Failure at arbitrary quantifier rank)
.

For every
r\geq 1,k\geq 2
,
there exists a sentence
\varphi
preserved under

\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{\infty}}

over
\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)

but not equivalent to an existential local sentence
over
\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)
.

Proof.

Using
2.2
it suffices
to consider the case
r=1
and
k=2
.
The proof follows the same pattern as
Lemma

5.16
,
we enumerate the axioms from
\mathcal{A}_{\textnormal{ord}}

and notice that they are of the form
\forall x.\theta(x)

where
\theta(x)
is a
1
-local formula.
As a consequence,
sentences preserved under
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{1},{2}}_{\infty}}

over
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
are preserved under
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{1},{2}}_{\infty}}

over
\operatorname{Fin}(\upsigma)
, which is a strengthening of

5.2
.

Moreover,
\varphi_{CC}
preserved under
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{1},{2}}_{\infty}}

using a simple syntactical analysis.
We now check that
\varphi_{B}
is preserved under
\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{1},{2}}_{\infty}}

over
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
.
Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
such that
A\models\varphi_{B}

and
A\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{1},{1}}_{\infty}}B
.

•

Let us first examine the case
where
A
has a single connected component.

Let
a\in A
; since
\mathcal{N}_{A}(a,1)
is finite,
there exists a
1
-local formula
\psi_{a}(x)

of quantifier rank less than
|\mathcal{N}_{A}(a,1)|+1

such that
B,b\models\psi_{a}(x)
if and only
if
\mathcal{N}_{A}(a,1)
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{N}_{B}(b,1)
.
In particular, if
C
is a

\leq
-component of
A
, it is of radius less than
1

and there exits
C^{\prime}
a
\leq
-component of
B

isomorphic to
C
.
Moreover, the
1
-neighborhood of a
\leq
-component
contains the previous and next
\leq
-component for
S
.

If
B
has a single connected component, then
two distinct
\leq
-components in
B
must have distinct sizes.
Using the fact that the components of
A
are all found in
B

and that their relative position is preserved,
this proves that
B
contains exactly the same
\leq
-components as
A
,
which
only happens if
A=B
.

If
B
has at least two connected components,
then it satisfies
\varphi_{CC}
which implies
\varphi_{B}
.

•

In the case where
A
has two connected components,

A\models\varphi_{CC}
but then

B\models\varphi_{CC}
and
B\models\varphi_{B}
.

Moreover, we know from
Lemma

5.4

that
\varphi_{B}
cannot be defined as an existential local sentence
over
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{ord}}
.
∎

Example 5.18

(Disjoint unions)
.

Let
A,B
be finite structures.
For all
0\leq r,q\leq\infty

and
1\leq k<\infty
,

A\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}A\uplus B

and
\biguplus_{i=1}^{k+n}A\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}\biguplus_{i=1}^{k}A
.

Proof.

Let us prove that

A\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}A\uplus B
.
Consider a vector
\mathbf{a}\in A^{k}
,
it is clear that this vector appears as-is in
A\uplus B

and since the union is disjoint,

\mathcal{N}_{A\uplus B}(\mathbf{a},r)=\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)
.
In particular,
\operatorname{tp}_{A}^{q}\left(\mathbf{a},r\right)=\operatorname{tp}_{A\uplus B}^{q}\left(\mathbf{a},r\right)
for all
q\geq 0
.

Let us write
A^{\uplus k}\triangleq\biguplus_{i=1}^{k}A

and
A^{\uplus k+n}\triangleq\biguplus_{i=1}^{k+n}A
.
Remark that the previous statement shows

A^{\uplus k}\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}A^{\uplus k+n}
.
Let us prove now
A^{\uplus k+n}\mathbin{{\Rrightarrow}^{{r},{k}}_{q}}A^{\uplus k}
.
Consider a vector
\mathbf{a}\in(A^{\uplus k+n})^{k}
,
this vector has elements in at most
k
copies of
A
,
hence one can select one copy of
A
in
A^{\uplus k}
for each
of those and consider the exact same elements in those copies.
As the unions are disjoint, the obtained neighborhoods are
isomorphic to those in
A^{\uplus k+n}
and in particular share
the same local types.
∎

Lemma 5.19

(Success at

)
.

For every

,

Proof.

Without loss of generality thanks to

Localising the Łoś-Tarski Theorem

Let us recall our proof scheme to handle preservation under extensions

in two distinct steps:

(✁)

\mathcal{C}
is

are equivalent to existential local sentences
\mathcal{C}
,

(✻)

\mathcal{C}
satisfies

, i.e.

over
\mathcal{C}

are equivalent to existential sentences over
\mathcal{C}
.

We prove in
Section

that step (✁) can be done on every hereditary class of finite

of preservation under extensions over structures of bounded degree
of
Atserias
et al. 2008
.
Moreover, we show in
Section

6.2
that (✻) can be done
under very mild assumptions on the class. These two results
are represented as a path from sentences preserved under extensions to existential
sentences in

, which

,

6.1.
Localisable Classes (✁)

Given a sentence
\varphi
preserved under extensions,
our goal is to prove that it has an existential local normal form.
As a typical first step in preservation theorems, we will
focus on the
\subseteq_{i}
-minimal
models of
\varphi
. For this study to make sense
we restrict our attention to classes
\mathcal{C}
that are
preserved under induced substructures, also known as hereditary
classes.

Lemma 6.1

(Minimal models)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a hereditary class of finite structures.
A sentence
\varphi
preserved under extensions over

\mathcal{C}
has an equivalent existential local normal form
if and only if there exists
k\geq 1,r\geq 0

such that
its
\subseteq_{i}
-minimal models are all found in
\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)
.

Proof of
\Rightarrow
.

If
\varphi\equiv\exists\mathbf{x}.\tau(\mathbf{x})
over

\mathcal{C}
where
\tau(x)
is a
r
-local formula, then a
minimal model
A\in\mathcal{C}
of
\varphi
necessarily contains
a vector
\mathbf{a}
such that

\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r),\mathbf{a}\models\tau
. This shows
that
\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)\models\varphi

where
\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)\subseteq_{i}A
by definition.
Since
\mathcal{C}
is hereditary,

\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)\in\mathcal{C}
, thus
A=\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)
by minimality and

A\in\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,|\mathbf{x}|)
.
∎

Proof of
\Leftarrow
.

Assume that the minimal models
of
\varphi
are all found in
\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)
.
Let
q
be the quantifier rank of
\varphi
.
We are going to show that

6.3.
Preservation under Extensions on Locally Well-Behaved Classes

We can now combine our study of step (✁)
in
Section

6.1
and step (✻)
in
Section

6.2

to harvest new classes enjoying preservation under extensions,
by characterising classes satisfying
preservation under extensions as those
locally satisfying preservation under extensions.
Quite surprisingly given the
non-relativisation properties of preservation theorems, we are able to
state an equivalence between preservation under extensions over a set

\mathcal{C}
and preservation under extensions on its local
neighbourhoods.

Theorem 6.12

(Local preservation under extensions)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a hereditary class of finite structures
stable under disjoint unions.
Preservation under extensions holds over
\mathcal{C}

if and only if
preservation under extension holds over
\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)

for all
r\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Assume that the preservation under extensions holds over

\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)
for
r,k\geq 0
. Let
\varphi

be a sentence preserved under extensions over
\mathcal{C}
.
Because
\mathcal{C}
is hereditary and closed under disjoint unions,
we can apply

Theorem

6.8
, and

\mathcal{C}
satisfies (✁). Hence,
\varphi
is equivalent
to an existential local sentence
\psi
over
\mathcal{C}
.
Since
\mathcal{C}
is hereditary and locally satisfies
preservation under extensions, we can apply

Lemma

6.11
, and
\mathcal{C}
satisfies (✻).
Therefore,
\psi
is equivalent over
\mathcal{C}
to an existential sentence.
We have proven that
\mathcal{C}
satisfies preservation under extensions.

Conversely, assume that the preservation under extensions holds over
\mathcal{C}
.
Let us fix
r,k\in\mathbb{N}^{2}

and consider
a sentence
\varphi
preserved under
extensions over
\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)
.

Let
\psi\triangleq\exists x_{1},\dots,x_{k}.\varphi_{|\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},r)}
,
i.e.
the relativisation of
\varphi
to a neighborhood
of size
r
around some elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
.
It is left as an exercice to prove that
\psi
and
\varphi

are equivalent on
\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)
.

Assume that
A,B\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
are such that

A\models\psi
and
A\subseteq_{i}B
.
This amounts to the existence of a strong injective
morphism
h\colon A\to B
.
Since
A\models\psi
, there exists
\mathbf{a}\in A^{k}

(possibly with repetition)
such that
\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)\models\varphi
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{N}_{A}(\mathbf{a},r)\subseteq_{i}\mathcal{N}_{B}(h(\mathbf{a}),r)
through the morphism
h
.
As both neighborhoods are built using radius
r
and
tuples of
k
elements, they belong to
\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)
.
The assumption on
\varphi
implies
that
\mathcal{N}_{B}(h(\mathbf{a}),r)\models\varphi

and in turn
this proves

B\models\psi
.

Using the preservation under extensions over
\mathcal{C}
on
\psi

gives an existential sentence
\theta

equivalent to
\psi
over
\mathcal{C}
.
In particular,
\theta
is equivalent to
\psi
over

\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)
since it is a subset of
\mathcal{C}
,
thus
\theta
is equivalent to
\psi
on
\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)
.

We have proven that preservation under extensions holds over
\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)
.
∎

The spaces
\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)
appear naturally in the study of

structures

through the notions of

and

which were already at play in recent proofs

et al. 2008

Indeed, they rely crucially on the existence of

Recall that a class

Fact 6.13

(
Nešetřil and Ossona de Mendez 2011
)
.

For a hereditary class
\mathcal{C}

to be

or for

for all

.

Proof.

Notice that whenever

\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)
with

.
Conversely, if

(r,m)
-scattered set,

Assume that
\mathcal{C}
is wide, the set

\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)

Recall here that the

\operatorname{td}(G)

of partial orders such

(

extends as usual to structures by considering

there is a bound on the tree-depth of the structures in

Note that working with
\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)

respect to disjoint unions, the localisation using a
single ball or several ones will coincide; examples of such properties
are wideness, exclusion of a minor, or bounded clique-width. The
following proposition illustrates this point in the case of bounded

Lemma 6.14

(Locally bounded tree-depth)
.

A class
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\operatorname{Struct}(\upsigma)
has locally bounded
tree-depth
if and only if

\exists\rho\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N},\forall A\in\mathcal{C},\forall a\in A,\forall r\geq 1,\operatorname{td}(\mathcal{N}_{A}(a,r))\leq\rho(r)
.

Proof.

Assuming that for all
k
there is a bound
on the tree-depth of
elements of
\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)
, we define

\rho(r)
to be
the maximum of
\operatorname{td}(A)

for
A
in
\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,1)
.

Conversely, assume that there exists
an increasing function
\rho
such that

\operatorname{td}(A)\leq\rho(r)
for every
A\in\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,1)

a simple induction on
A
shows that

\operatorname{td}(A)\leq\rho(r\times(2k+1))
for every
A\in\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)
.

•

If
A\in\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)
can be written
A_{1}\uplus A_{2}

then
\operatorname{td}(A)=\max(\operatorname{td}(A_{1}),\operatorname{td}(A_{2}))
and
we conclude by induction hypothesis since
\rho
is increasing.

•

If
A
is totally connected and in
\operatorname{Balls}(\mathcal{C},r,k)

then it is included in a ball of radius
r\times(2k+1)

hence the tree-depth is bounded by

\rho(r\times(2k+1))
.
∎

Consider the class

Concluding Remarks

We investigate
the notion of positive locality
through three fragments of first-order logic: existential local sentences,
a positive variant of the Gaifman normal form, and
sentences preserved under local elementary embeddings.
We prove that those three fragments are equally expressive in
the case of arbitrary structures, but that this fails in the finite.
Following the line of undecidability results for preservation
theorems
Kuperberg 2021
;
Chen and Flum 2021
,
we prove that most of the associated decision problems are undecidable
in the case of finite structures.

Maybe surprisingly,
the study of this seemingly arbitrary notion of positive locality
has a direct application in the study of preservation under extensions
over classes of finite structures.
In the case of finite structures,
our notion of local elementary embeddings
describes exactly disjoint unions,
and might explain why they
featured so prominently in the study of
preservation under
extensions
(
Atserias
et al. 2006
;
Atserias
et al. 2008
;
Rossman 2008
;
Harwath
et al. 2015
)
.

We prove that under mild assumptions on the class
\mathcal{C}

of structures considered, sentences preserved under extensions
can be rewritten as existential local sentences.
We leverage this to craft a locality principle
relating preservation under extensions over the neighborhoods of a class
and preservation under extensions over the whole class.

This allows us to build new classes of structures
where preservation under extensions holds
by localising known properties.
This proof scheme does not always yield
new classes:
for instance,
nowhere dense
classes

(see
Nešetřil and Ossona de Mendez 2012
, Chapter 5)

are locally nowhere dense and vice-versa.

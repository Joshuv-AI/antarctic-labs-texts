1.2.
Earlier attempts.

1.3.
Why dimension matters.

Here we describe two key differences between the
d\geq 3
and
d=2
cases.

As mentioned in §
1.1
, to prove their result (
1.2
) for
d\geq 3
, Pierce and Yung
[
21
]
used a
TT^{*}
method. In particular, they wrote the kernel of the resulting
TT^{*}
operator as an oscillatory integral and then carried out sophisticated oscillatory integral estimates.

To be more precise, let
\varphi_{0}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function supported on the interval
[1,2]
.
Pierce and Yung
[
21
]
first linearized the maximal operator (
1.1
) and aimed to show that there exists
\varepsilon>0
, depending only on the dimension
d
and the kernel
K
, such that

(1.6)

\big\|\mathcal{C}_{d,j}f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim_{d,K}2^{-\varepsilon j}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

holds for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
d\geq 3
,
where

\mathcal{C}_{d,j}f(x,y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}f(x-t,y-|t|^{2})e^{iv(x,y)|t|^{3}}\varphi_{0}(2^{-j}|t|)K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

for
v(x,y)
a measurable function taking values in the interval
[1,2]
. Once the
L^{2}
estimate (
1.6
) is proved, one may interpolate with trivial
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
estimates (without any decaying factor
2^{-\varepsilon j}
) to obtain the full range
p\in(1,\infty)
.

The decaying factor
2^{-\varepsilon j}
is natural to expect: by Minkowski’s inequality, we trivially have

\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}f(x-t,y-|t|^{2})\varphi_{0}(2^{-j}|t|)K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim_{d,K}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

uniformly in
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Adding the extra oscillatory term
e^{iv(x,y)|t|^{3}}
, it is very natural to expect a decay estimate of the form (
1.6
). However, proving the decay estimate (
1.6
) is highly non-trivial as
v
does not have any regularity and can be an arbitrary measurable function. Moreover, our estimates must also be uniform over all such choices of
v
.

The strategy of Pierce and Yung
[
21
]
is to regularise the operator
\mathcal{C}_{d,j}
by considering
\mathcal{C}_{d,j}\mathcal{C}_{d,j}^{*}
, the composition of the adjoint operator
\mathcal{C}_{d,j}^{*}
with
\mathcal{C}_{d,j}
. The operator
\mathcal{C}_{d,j}\mathcal{C}_{d,j}^{*}
may be better behaved than the operator
\mathcal{C}_{d,j}
as it involves an integral in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, rather than
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
itself. As the function
f
depends on
d
variables, when expressing
\mathcal{C}_{d,j}\mathcal{C}_{d,j}^{*}
as a convolution operator, the kernel can be written as an integral in

(1.7)

(d-1)+(d-1)-d=d-2

variables. From (
1.7
), one immediately sees how the constraint
d\geq 3
in Pierce and Yung
[
21
]
appears: when
d=2
the convolution kernel is not given by an integral formula, and is just a very rough measure.

There is a second place where
d\geq 3
plays a key role in
[
21
]
. This can be explained by the (vague) principle that integrating along the paraboloid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\geq 3
is less ‘singular’ than integrating along the parabola in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Indeed, the larger
d
gets, the less ‘singular’ it is to integrate along the paraboloid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Following the above principle, Stein
[
29
]
proved that the spherical maximal operator in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is bounded whenever
d\geq 3
. Some time later, the case
d=2
was resolved in Bourgain’s breakthrough work
[
5
]
. Sogge
[
26
]
and Mockenhaupt, Seeger and Sogge
[
20
,
19
]
further developed and significantly expanded upon Bourgain’s ideas, leading to the discovery of the local smoothing phenomenon for linear wave equations.

The difference between Stein’s approach to the spherical maximal function
[
29
]
and Mockenhaupt, Seeger and Sogge’s approach to the circular maximal function
[
19
]
is very similar to the difference between the approach of Pierce and Yung
[
21
]
and the approach in the current paper. Neither Stein
[
29
]
nor Pierce and Yung
[
21
]
required any local smoothing estimates in their work. By contrast, local smoothing estimates are crucial in Mockenhaupt, Seeger and Sogge’s work
[
19
]
and in our proof of Theorem

. It is perhaps helpful to remark here that Mockenhaupt, Seeger and Sogge
[
19
]
used local smoothing estimates for linear wave operators; in the current paper, what we encounter is a local smoothing estimate for a variable-coefficient Schrödinger operator (see Theorem
1.4
below). These local smoothing estimates follow from the recent work
[
10
]

[

]
regarding differences between the geometry of Kakeya and Nikodym sets of curves.

1.4.
An
L^{2}
bound and its surprising proof.

In this subsection we discuss the proof strategy for Theorem
1.1
and further remark on the variable-coefficient local smoothing estimates for Schödinger operators that feature in the argument.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we still prove a decay estimate of the form (
1.6
). More precisely, we prove that there exists a universal constant
\varepsilon>0
such that

(1.8)

\big\|\mathcal{C}_{j}f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon j}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

holds for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
where

\mathcal{C}_{j}f(x,y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x-t,y-t^{2})e^{iv(x,y)t^{3}}\varphi_{0}(2^{-j}|t|)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}

for
v(x,y)
a measurable function taking values in the interval
[1,2]
. What is surprising to us is that we do not know how to prove (
1.8
) directly, and instead we show (see Theorem
2.3
below) that there exist
p_{\circ}>2
and
\varepsilon_{\circ}>0
such that

(1.9)

\big\|\mathcal{C}_{j}f\big\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon_{\circ}j}\|f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. This, together with the trivial bound

\big\|\mathcal{C}_{j}f\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

implies (
1.8
). In the literature related to (singular) Radon transforms in harmonic analysis, we are not aware of another instance where an
L^{2}
decay estimate is proven via interpolation; usually it is natural to work directly in
L^{2}
because of, for instance, self-adjoint properties and Plancherel’s theorem.

The estimate (
1.9
) (or, more generally,
Theorem
2.3
below) follows in part from a theorem for variable coefficient Schrödinger-type propagators recently proved in
[
10
]
. The general result in
[
10
]
can be thought of as a local smoothing variant of the celebrated work of Bourgain
[
6
]
on oscillatory integral operators, and is partly inspired by important observations of Wisewell
[
32
]
concerning Kakeya and Nikodym sets of curves. To apply the theory from
[
10
]
, one must check that the relevant phase functions satisfy certain conditions in addition to those of Hörmander
[
16
]
, called the
Nikodym non-compression hypothesis
(see Definition
1.3
below). This hypothesis ensures certain families of curves associated to the operator
\mathcal{C}_{j}
cannot compress into small sets (what Bourgain
[
6
]
called the Kakeya compression phenomenon): we refer the reader to
[
10
]
for further details.

We emphasise that (
1.9
) is not an off-the-shelf application of the aforementioned local smoothing results from
[
10
]
. An array of classical oscillatory integral and
L^{2}
-based techniques are applied to reduce the problem to analysing the most singular part of the operator where the local smoothing result applies. These arguments are partly inspired by methods developed by Pramanik–Seeger
[
22
]
to prove local smoothing-type results in the presence of fold singularities. Furthermore, it transpires that, even after the above reductions, the Nikodym non-compression hypothesis is not globally valid, and an exceptional piece of the operator must be analysed via alternative means. However, this piece is amenable to (variable coefficient) decoupling theory, which is a standard tool in the study of local smoothing estimates
[
33
,
7
,
3
,
4
]
.

1.5.
Hörmander-type phase conditions and the Nikodym non-compression hypothesis

In this subsection we review a key result from
[
10
]
that we shall use later.
We begin by reviewing some of the standard definitions in the oscillatory integral operator literature (see, for example,
[
10
,
15
]
), in the special case of translation-invariant phases. For
n\geq 2
and
\rho>0
, let

\mathbb{D}_{\rho}^{n}:=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{1}\times\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1},

where
\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{d}
denotes the open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of radius
\rho

Definition 1.2
.

We say
\Phi\colon\mathbb{D}_{\rho}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
translation-invariant phase
if there exists some smooth function
\phi\colon\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{1}\times\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(1.10)

\Phi(\mathbf{x},w;\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\mathbf{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}+\phi(w;\boldsymbol{\xi})\qquad\textrm{for all $(\mathbf{x},w;\boldsymbol{\xi})\in\mathbb{D}_{\rho}^{n}$.}

Furthermore, if
\Phi
is of the form (
1.10
), then we say that
\Phi
is a
translation-invariant, Hörmander-type phase
if

(1.11)

\det\partial_{w}\partial^{2}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}}\phi(w;\boldsymbol{\xi})\neq 0\quad\textrm{for all $(w;\boldsymbol{\xi})\in\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{1}\times\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{2}$}.

Often we pair the phase with a smooth cutoff function
a\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{1}\times\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1})
, in which case we refer to the pairing
[\Phi;a]
as a
phase-amplitude pair
.

Given
[\Phi;a]
a phase-amplitude pair and
\lambda\geq 1
, we consider the rescaled data

\Phi^{\lambda}(\mathbf{x},w;\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\lambda\Phi(\mathbf{x}/\lambda,w/\lambda;\boldsymbol{\xi})\quad\textrm{and}\quad a^{\lambda}(w;\boldsymbol{\xi}):=a(w/\lambda;\boldsymbol{\xi}).

The
variable Schrödinger propagator

U^{\lambda}=U^{\lambda}[\Phi;a]
associated to
[\Phi;a]
is the operator

(1.12)

U^{\lambda}f(\mathbf{x},w):=\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{i\Phi^{\lambda}(\mathbf{x},w;\boldsymbol{\xi})}a^{\lambda}(w;\boldsymbol{\xi})\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\xi},

initially defined as acting on
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Any variable Schrödinger propagator associated to a Hörmander-type phase satisfies a range of
universal
estimates, given by Stein’s oscillatory integral theorem
[
28
]
. Our approach requires estimates for specific operators (related to the
\mathcal{C}_{j}
above) which go beyond this universal range. Examples due to Bourgain
[
6
]
and Wisewell
[
32
]
demonstrate Stein’s theorem
[
28
]
is sharp over the class of general translation-invariant, Hörmander-type phases. Thus, to proceed we must impose additional conditions on our class of phase functions.

Definition 1.3
.

For
0<\rho<1
, let
\Phi\colon\mathbb{D}^{2}_{\rho}\to\mathbb{R}
be a real analytic translation-invariant phase of the form (

). For

if :

a)

b)

For all
(w;\boldsymbol{\xi})\in\mathbb{B}^{1}_{\rho}\times\mathbb{B}^{2}_{\rho}
, we have

\left|\det\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{w}\partial^{2}_{\xi_{1}\xi_{1}}\phi(w;\boldsymbol{\xi}),&\partial_{w}\partial^{2}_{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}\phi(w;\boldsymbol{\xi}),&\partial_{w}\partial^{2}_{\xi_{2}\xi_{2}}\phi(w;\boldsymbol{\xi})\\
\partial^{2}_{w}\partial^{2}_{\xi_{1}\xi_{1}}\phi(w;\boldsymbol{\xi}),&\partial^{2}_{w}\partial^{2}_{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}\phi(w;\boldsymbol{\xi}),&\partial^{2}_{w}\partial^{2}_{\xi_{2}\xi_{2}}\phi(w;\boldsymbol{\xi})\\

1.6.
The full planar Pierce–Yung operator.

It may be possible to generalise the argument in the current paper to prove that

\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x-t,y-t^{2})e^{ivt^{m}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big|

is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for every
p\in(1,\infty)
and every integer
m\geq 4
. However, it remains an interesting question whether the full Pierce–Yung operator

\sup_{v_{3},\dots,v_{m}\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x-t,y-t^{2})e^{i(v_{3}t^{3}+\dots+v_{m}t^{m})}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big|

is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for every integer
m\geq 4
and
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Notational conventions

We let
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
denote the
frequency domain
, which is the Pontryagin dual group of
\mathbb{R}
understood here as simply a copy of
\mathbb{R}
. Given
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

we define the Fourier transform and inverse Fourier transform by

\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\langle\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}\rangle}f(\mathbf{x})\,\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\check{g}(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{i\langle\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\xi}\rangle}g(\boldsymbol{\xi})\,\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\xi},

respectively. Given
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
we write
a\wedge b:=\min\{a,b\}
and
a\vee b:=\max\{a,b\}
.

\beta(s):=\beta_{0}(s)-\beta_{0}(2s)
so that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\beta(2^{-j}s)=1\quad\textrm{and}\quad\beta_{0}(s)=\sum_{j\leq 0}\beta(2^{-j}s)\quad\textrm{for all $s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$.}

For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, define

\displaystyle H^{v}_{\leq m}f(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x-t,y-t^{2})e^{ivt^{3}}\beta_{0}(2^{-m}t)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t},

\displaystyle H^{v}_{m}f(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x-t,y-t^{2})e^{ivt^{3}}\beta(2^{-m}t)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

We then decompose

(2.1)

\mathcal{C}f(x,y)\leq\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}f(x,y)+\sum_{\ell>0}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}f(x,y)

where

\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}f(x,y):=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{v\in V_{j}}|H^{v}_{\leq-j}f(x,y)|,\qquad\mathcal{C}_{\ell}f(x,y):=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{v\in V_{j}}|H^{v}_{-j+\ell}f(x,y)|

for
V_{j}:=[2^{3j},2^{3(j+1)}]
.

2.2.
Non-oscillatory analysis

The term
\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
has no oscillation and can be controlled by the maximal function and the Hilbert transform along the parabola. Recall that these are the operators

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{par}}f(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{2r}\int_{-r}^{r}|f(x-t,y-t^{2})|\,\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{par}}f(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x-t,y-t^{2})\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}

which are both well-known to be bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
1<p<\infty
(see, for instance,
[
11
, Chapter 8, §7]
). Moreover, it was shown in
[
21
, Theorem 5.1]
that
L^{p}
-boundedness also holds for the maximal truncations

(2.2)

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}|H_{\mathrm{par},\varepsilon}f(x,y)|,\qquad\text{ where }\quad H_{\mathrm{par},\varepsilon}f(x,y):=\int_{|y|\geq\varepsilon}f(x-t,y-t^{2})\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}.

In view of these known results, we quickly deduce the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
1<p<\infty
, we have

\|\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Proof.

Write

H_{\leq-j}^{v}=\big(H_{\leq-j}^{v}-H_{\leq-j}^{0}\big)+H_{\leq-j}^{0}=:M_{-j}^{v}+H_{\leq-j}^{0}.

Then

\displaystyle\sup_{v\in V_{j}}|M_{-j}^{v}f(x,y)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{v\in V_{j}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x-t,y-t^{2})\big(e^{ivt^{3}}-1\big)\beta_{0}(2^{j}t)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x-t,y-t^{2})|\beta_{0}(2^{j}t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{-j+2}}\int_{|t|\leq 2^{-j+1}}|f(x-t,y-t^{2})|\,\mathrm{d}t.

Thus,

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{v\in V_{j}}|M_{-j}^{v}f(x,y)|\lesssim M_{\mathrm{par}}f(x,y)

and the
L^{p}
-boundedness for this term follows.

For the second term, since
|t|\leq 2^{-j+1}
for all
t\in\mathrm{supp}\,\beta_{0}(2^{j}\,\cdot\,)
, we note that

\begin{split}H^{0}_{\leq-j}f(x,y)&=H_{\mathrm{par}}f(x,y)-H_{\mathrm{par},2^{-j+1}}f(x,y)\\
&+\int_{|t|\leq 2^{-j+1}}f(x-t,y-t^{2})\big(\beta_{0}(2^{j}t)-1\big)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t},\end{split}

where
H_{\mathrm{par},2^{-j+1}}f
is a truncation as defined in (
2.2
). Since
\beta_{0}(s)=1
for
s\in[-1,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{|t|\leq 2^{-j+1}}f(x-t,y-t^{2})\big(\beta_{0}(2^{j}t)-1\big)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{-j}}\int_{2^{-j}\leq|t|\leq 2^{-j+1}}|f(x-t,y-t^{2})|\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim M_{\mathrm{par}}f(x,y).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{v\in V_{j}}|H_{\leq-j}^{0}f(x,y)|\leq|H_{\mathrm{par}}f(x,y)|+\sup_{\varepsilon>0}|H_{\varepsilon}f(x,y)|+M_{\mathrm{par}}f(x,y)

and the
L^{p}
-boundedness follows.
∎

Regarding the terms
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
, we have the following uniform bound.

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
1<p\leq\infty
, we have

\|\mathcal{C}_{\ell}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Proof.

We trivially bound

\displaystyle|H_{-j+\ell}^{v}f(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x-t,y-t^{2})|\beta(2^{j-\ell}t)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{|t|}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{-j+\ell+2}}\int_{|t|\leq 2^{-j+\ell+1}}|f(x-t,y-t^{2})|\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim M_{\mathrm{par}}f(x,y)

uniformly in
v
and
j
, so the result follows from the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
-boundedness of
M_{\mathrm{par}}
.
∎

2.3.
Our goal

Lemma
2.2
falls short of proving Theorem
1.1
since the estimates are not summable in
\ell
. The key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the following improvement.

Theorem 2.3
.

There exists some
p_{\circ}>2
and
\varepsilon_{\circ}>0
such that

(2.3)

\|\mathcal{C}_{\ell}f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim 2^{-\ell\varepsilon_{\circ}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

holds for all
\ell\geq 1
.

Once this is established, interpolation with Lemma
2.2
immediately upgrades (
2.3
) to all
1<p<\infty
. Theorem
1.1
is then an immediate consequence of this upgrade and Lemma
2.1
, by virtue of the decomposition (
2.1
) and the triangle inequality.

3.
Reduction to key estimates

3.1.
Frequency decomposition

Let
\beta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be as in §
2
. For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
i=1,2
, let
P_{m}^{(i)}
denote the
i
th coordinate Littlewood–Paley projection associated with
\beta(2^{-m}\,\cdot\,)
: that is, for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we define

(P_{m}^{(1)}f)\;\widehat{}\;(\xi,\eta):=\beta(2^{-m}\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi,\eta),\qquad(P_{m}^{(2)}f)\;\widehat{}\;(\xi,\eta):=\beta(2^{-m}\eta)\widehat{f}(\xi,\eta).

Note that

f=\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{m_{1}}^{(1)}P_{m_{2}}^{(2)}f.

It is also convenient to define the operators

P_{\leq m}^{(i)}:=\sum_{n\leq m}P_{n}^{(i)},

which, for
i=1
,
2
, are associated to the multipliers
\beta_{0}(2^{-m}\xi)
and
\beta_{0}(2^{-m}\eta)
, respectively. We shall also make use of Littlewood–Paley projections
\widetilde{P}_{m}^{(i)}
satisfying

P_{m}^{(i)}=P_{m}^{(i)}\widetilde{P}_{m}^{(i)}

and associated to a cut-off function
\widetilde{\beta}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
supported in
[-4,-1/4]\cup[1/4,4]
and that equals
1
on
\mathrm{supp}\,\beta\subseteq[-2,-1/2]\cup[1/2,2]
.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
v\in V_{j}
, we decompose

H_{-j+\ell}^{v}f=\sum_{k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{-j+\ell}^{v}P_{k_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{k_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}f.

Moreover, we group the low frequencies and write, for
C_{1},C_{2}\geq 10^{6}
satisfying
C_{1}=2C_{2}+12
fixed (absolute) constants,

\displaystyle H_{-j+\ell}^{v}f

\displaystyle=H_{-j+\ell}^{v}P_{\leq-C_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{\leq-C_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}f

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k_{1}>-C_{1}}H_{-j+\ell}^{v}P_{k_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{\leq-C_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}f

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k_{2}>-C_{2}}H_{-j+\ell}^{v}P_{\leq-C_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{k_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}f

(3.1)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k_{1}>-C_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}>-C_{2}}H_{-j+\ell}^{v}P_{k_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{k_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}f.

Note that the Fourier multiplier associated to
H_{-j+\ell}^{v}P_{k_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{k_{2}+\ell+j}^{(2)}
is given by

(3.2)

\beta(2^{-k_{1}-2\ell-j}\xi)\beta(2^{-k_{2}-\ell-2j}\eta)\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i(t\xi+t^{2}\eta-t^{3}v)}\beta(2^{-\ell+j}t)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t},

and similar formulas hold for the other frequency projections.

The low frequency part of the operator is easily dealt with.

Lemma 3.1

(Low frequency part)
.

For
2\leq p\leq\infty
, the inequality

\|\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{v\in V_{j}}|H^{v}_{-j+\ell}P_{\leq-C_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{\leq-C_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}f|\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim_{p,N,C_{1},C_{2}}2^{-\ell N}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

holds for all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.

The proof of Lemma
3.1
is based on pointwise comparison with the strong maximal function, which is achieved by estimating the kernel using elementary integration-by-parts arguments. We postpone the details until §
4.3
.

For the high frequencies, we introduce a rescaled variant
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{w}
of
H_{-j+\ell}^{v}
for which we prove the key estimates and then relate back to
H_{-j+\ell}^{v}
. Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
w>0
, define

\mathcal{H}_{n}^{w}f(x,y):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i2^{n}(t\xi+t^{2}\eta-t^{3}w)}\beta(t)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}\widehat{f}(\xi,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\eta

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Furthermore, for
(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
we define the frequency localised operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}:=\mathcal{H}_{n}^{w}P_{k_{1}}^{(1)}P_{k_{2}}^{(2)},\quad\mathcal{H}_{n,\leq k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}:=\mathcal{H}_{n}^{w}P_{\leq k_{1}}^{(1)}P_{k_{2}}^{(2)},\quad\mathcal{H}_{n,k_{1},\leq k_{2}}^{w}:=\mathcal{H}_{n}^{w}P_{k_{1}}^{(1)}P_{\leq k_{2}}^{(2)}.

With this notation, our goal is to prove the following.

Theorem 3.2
.

There exist
p_{\circ}>2
and
\varepsilon_{\circ}>0
,
\delta_{\circ}>0
such that the inequalities

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{1}^{8}\|\mathcal{H}^{w}_{n,k_{1},\leq-C_{2}}f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p_{\circ}}\,\mathrm{d}w\Big)^{1/p_{\circ}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-n/p_{\circ}-n\varepsilon_{\circ}-k_{1}\delta_{\circ}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{1}^{8}\|\mathcal{H}^{w}_{n,\leq-C_{1},k_{2}}f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p_{\circ}}\,\mathrm{d}w\Big)^{1/p_{\circ}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-n/p_{\circ}-n\varepsilon_{\circ}-k_{2}\delta_{\circ}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{1}^{8}\|\mathcal{H}_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p_{\circ}}\,\mathrm{d}w\Big)^{1/p_{\circ}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-n/p_{\circ}-n\varepsilon_{\circ}-(k_{1}+k_{2})\delta_{\circ}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

hold for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
k_{1}>-C_{1}
and
k_{2}>-C_{2}
.

Once the above theorem is established, Theorem
2.3
follows from standard Littlewood–Paley, Sobolev embedding and scaling arguments.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
(assuming Lemma
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
).

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Performing the change of variables

(t;\xi,\eta)\mapsto(2^{\ell-j}t;2^{2\ell+j}\xi,2^{\ell+2j}\eta)

in (
3.2
), we obtain

H_{-j+\ell}^{v}P_{k_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{k_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}f(x,y)=\mathcal{H}_{3\ell}^{2^{-3j}v}P_{k_{1}}^{(1)}P_{k_{2}}^{(2)}f_{\ell,j}(2^{2\ell+j}x,2^{\ell+2j}y)

where

f_{\ell,j}(x,y):=f(2^{-2\ell-j}x,2^{-\ell-2j}y).

Recall that
V_{j}:=[2^{3j},2^{3(j+1)}]
. By the above scaling, for any fixed
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,
p\geq 1
and
M>0
, the norm inequality

\|\sup_{v\in V_{j}}|H_{-j+\ell}^{v}P_{k_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{k_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}f|\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq M\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

holds for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if the norm inequality

\|\sup_{1\leq w\leq 8}|\mathcal{H}^{w}_{3\ell,k_{1},k_{2}}f|\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq M\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

holds for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, with the same constant
M
. A similar statement holds if either
P_{k_{1}}^{(1)}
or
P_{k_{2}}^{(2)}
is replaced with
P_{\leq k_{1}}^{(1)}
or
P_{\leq k_{2}}^{(2)}
.

For
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and Hölder’s inequality,

\sup_{1\leq w\leq 8}|\mathcal{H}_{3\ell}^{w}g|^{p}\leq|\mathcal{H}^{1}_{3\ell}g|^{p}+p\Big(\int_{1}^{8}|\mathcal{H}^{w}_{3\ell}g|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}w\Big)^{1-1/p}\Big(\int_{1}^{8}|\partial_{w}\mathcal{H}_{3\ell}^{w}g|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}w\Big)^{1/p}.

Furthermore,

\partial_{w}\mathcal{H}^{w}_{3\ell}g=3i2^{3\ell}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{w}_{3\ell}g,

where the operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{w}_{3\ell}
is defined by the Fourier multiplier

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i2^{3\ell}(-t\xi-t^{2}\eta+t^{3}w)}\beta(t)t^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t,

which is essentially identical to the multiplier of
\mathcal{H}^{w}_{3\ell}
. Combining the above observations and letting
g
be suitable Littlewood–Paley projections of
f
, provided
C_{1}
,
C_{2}\geq 1
are chosen sufficiently large, Theorem
3.2
implies

\displaystyle\|\sup_{v\in V_{j}}|H^{v}_{-j+\ell}P_{k_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{\leq-C_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}f|\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-3\ell\varepsilon_{\circ}-k_{1}\delta_{\circ}}\|\widetilde{P}_{k_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

\displaystyle\|\sup_{v\in V_{j}}|H^{v}_{-j+\ell}P_{\leq-C_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{k_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}f|\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-3\ell\varepsilon_{\circ}-k_{2}\delta_{\circ}}\|\widetilde{P}^{(2)}_{k_{2}+\ell+2j}f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

\displaystyle\|\sup_{v\in V_{j}}|H^{v}_{-j+\ell}P_{k_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{k_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-3\ell\varepsilon_{\circ}-(k_{1}+k_{2})\delta_{\circ}}\|\widetilde{P}^{(1)}_{k_{1}+2\ell+j}f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where we have used that
P_{m}^{(i)}=P_{m}^{(i)}\widetilde{P}_{m}^{(i)}
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
i=1
,
2
.

By (one-dimensional) Littlewood–Paley theory and Fubini’s theorem, we have that

\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\widetilde{P}^{(i)}_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\qquad\textrm{for all $2\leq p<\infty$.}

Using that
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|a_{j}|^{p}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|a_{j}|^{p}
, the above estimates therefore upgrade to

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}>-C_{1}}\|\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{v\in V_{j}}|H^{v}_{-j+\ell}P_{k_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{\leq-C_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}f|\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-3\ell\varepsilon_{\circ}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{2}>-C_{2}}\|\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{v\in V_{j}}|H^{v}_{-j+\ell}P_{\leq-C_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{k_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}f|\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-3\ell\varepsilon_{\circ}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}>-C_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}>-C_{2}}\|\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{v\in V_{j}}|H^{v}_{-j+\ell}P_{k_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{k_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}f|\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-3\ell\varepsilon_{\circ}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{\circ}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

In view of the decomposition (
3.1
), theses estimates, together with Lemma
3.1
and an application of the triangle inequality, imply (
2.3
).
∎

3.2.
Isolating the
critical points

Let
m_{n}^{w}
denote the multiplier associated to the operator
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{w}
, so that

(3.6)

m_{n}^{w}(\xi,\eta):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i2^{n}\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w}(t)}a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

where

(3.7)

\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w}(t):=-t\xi-t^{2}\eta+t^{3}w\quad\textrm{and}\quad a(t):=\frac{\beta(t)}{t}.

For
(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the multipliers of the frequency localised operators
\mathcal{H}_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}
,
\mathcal{H}_{n,\leq k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}
,
\mathcal{H}_{n,k_{1},\leq k_{2}}^{w}
are then given by

\displaystyle m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle:=\beta(2^{-k_{1}}\xi)\beta(2^{-k_{2}}\eta)m_{n}^{w}(\xi,\eta),

\displaystyle m_{n,\leq k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle:=\beta_{0}(2^{-k_{1}}\xi)\beta(2^{-k_{2}}\eta)m_{n}^{w}(\xi,\eta),

\displaystyle m_{n,k_{1},\leq k_{2}}^{w}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle:=\beta(2^{-k_{1}}\xi)\beta_{0}(2^{-k_{2}}\eta)m_{n}^{w}(\xi,\eta),

respectively.

We analyse the above multipliers using the method of stationary phase. This relies on understanding the critical points of
\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w}
, which are the zeros of the function

(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime}(t)=-\xi-2t\eta+3t^{2}w.

These are given by

(3.8)

t_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta):=\frac{\eta\pm\sqrt{\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)}}{3w}\quad\textrm{where}\quad\Delta(w;\xi,\eta):=\eta^{2}+3w\xi,

provided
\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)\geq 0
.
We partition the range of
(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
according to whether the critical points
t_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)
exist for either
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}
,
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,m_{n,\leq k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}
or
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,m_{n,k_{1},\leq k_{2}}^{w}
, for some choice of
1\leq w\leq 8
. To this end, define the sets of indices

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\{(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap(-C_{1},\infty)\times(-C_{2},\infty):k_{1}>(k_{2}\vee 0)+8\}

\displaystyle=\{(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:k_{1}>8\textrm{ and }k_{1}-8>k_{2}>-C_{2}\}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\{(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap(-C_{1},\infty)\times(-C_{2},\infty):k_{2}>(k_{1}\vee 0)+8\}

\displaystyle=\{(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:k_{2}>8\textrm{ and }k_{2}-8>k_{1}>-C_{1}\}.

We shall show below, in the proof of Lemma
5.2
, that for
(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathcal{G}_{1}
or
(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
there are no critical points when
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,m_{n,k_{1},\leq k_{2}}^{w}
or
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,m_{n,\leq k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}
, respectively. This leads to very favourable bounds for the corresponding multipliers: see Proposition
3.3
.

It is easy to check that
\big(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap(-C_{1},\infty)\times(-C_{2},\infty)\big)\setminus(\mathcal{G}_{1}\cup\mathcal{G}_{2})\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{diag}}
where

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{diag}}:=\{(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}:|k_{1}-k_{2}|\leq(C_{1}\vee C_{2})+16\text{ and }k_{1}>-C_{1},k_{2}>-C_{2}-8\}.

These ‘diagonal’ exponents index pieces of the multiplier admitting critical points.

3.3.
Non-stationary case

The following proposition guarantees very strong estimates when the the exponents
(k_{1},k_{2})
lie in
\mathcal{G}_{1}
or
\mathcal{G}_{2}
.

Proposition 3.3

(Non-stationary estimates)
.

Let
2\leq p<\infty
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

i)

If
(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathcal{G}_{1}
, then

\|\mathcal{H}^{w}_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|\mathcal{H}^{w}_{n,k_{1},\leq k_{2}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim_{p,N}2^{-k_{1}N-nN}

holds for all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, uniformly in
1\leq w\leq 8
.

ii)

If
(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
, then

\|\mathcal{H}^{w}_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|\mathcal{H}^{w}_{n,\leq k_{1},k_{2}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim_{p,N}2^{-k_{2}N-nN}

holds for all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, uniformly in
1\leq w\leq 8
.

Proposition
3.3
is a consequence of elementary
L^{2}
-based arguments. We postpone the proof until §
5
.

3.4.
Stationary case

The analysis of the remaining pieces of the operator requires much deeper tools. The key estimates are as follows.

Proposition 3.4

In this section we apply elementary integration-by-parts arguments to bound the kernels of our localised operators. This will imply

4.1.
The operators
\mathcal{H}_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}

We begin with the operators
\mathcal{H}_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}
introduced in §
3.1
(together with their low frequency variants), whose kernels are given by the inverse Fourier transforms of the multipliers
m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}
from §
3.2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq w\leq 8
and
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}]\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim 1,

and the same holds if
m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}
is replaced by
m_{n,\leq k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}
,
m_{n,k_{1},\leq k_{2}}^{w}
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}](x,y)|

\displaystyle\sim\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+2^{n}\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w}(t))}\beta(2^{-k_{1}}\xi)\beta(2^{-k_{2}}\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\eta\,a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{2^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}}}{(1+2^{k_{1}}|x-2^{n}t|+2^{k_{2}}|y-2^{n}t^{2}|)^{N}}\,a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

by repeated integration-by-parts. Integrating in
(x,y)
and using Fubini’s theorem, we obtain the result.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
4.1
, we have the basic
L^{p}
estimate from Lemma
3.7
.

Proof of Lemma
3.7
.

Since, for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have

\mathcal{H}^{w}_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}f=K_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}\ast f\qquad\textrm{where}\qquad K_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}],

the desired bound follows from Lemma
4.1
and Young’s inequality.
∎

and

We now turn to the kernels of the operators

and

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq w\leq 8
and

Proof.

The proof of i) follows from a minor adaptation of that of ii), and so we only provide the latter.

It suffices to show the pointwise bound

|\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell}](x,y)|\lesssim_{N}\int_{1/2}^{2}\frac{2^{-2\ell}}{(1+2^{-2\ell}|x-2^{n}t|+2^{-2\ell}|y-2^{n}t^{2}|)^{N}}\,\mathrm{d}t

holds for all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, uniformly in

. Write
m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell}=a_{k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell}\cdot m_{n}^{w}
where

a_{k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell}(\xi,\eta):=\beta(2^{-2k_{2}+2\ell}\Delta(w;\xi,\eta))\beta(2^{-k_{1}}\xi)\beta(2^{-k_{2}}\eta)

and

is as defined in (

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell}](x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i2^{n}wt^{3}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}[a_{k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell}](x-2^{n}t,y-2^{n}t^{2})a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

In light of the above, it suffices to show that

|\mathcal{F}^{-1}[a_{k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell}](x,y)|\lesssim_{N}\frac{2^{-2\ell}}{(1+2^{-2\ell}|x|+2^{-2\ell}|y|)^{N}}

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. However, this follows from routine integration-by-parts and the fact that, for fixed
\eta
, the

lies in a fixed interval of length

and that

.

4.3.
Proof of Lemma

Here we provide the proof of Lemma

. The key ingredient is the following kernel estimate, which shares similarities with the above kernel estimates.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
v\in V_{j}
, let
K_{\ell,j}^{v}
denote the kernel of the operator
H_{-j+\ell}^{v}P_{\leq-C_{1}+2\ell+j}^{(1)}P_{\leq-C_{2}+\ell+2j}^{(2)}

|K_{\ell,j}^{v}(x,y)|\lesssim_{N}2^{-\ell N}2^{3(j+\ell)}(1+2^{2\ell+j}|x|+2^{\ell+2j}|y|)^{-N}\qquad\textrm{for all $N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$.}

Proof.

By the rescaling argument from §
3.1
, it follows that

K_{\ell,j}^{v}(x,y)=2^{3(j+\ell)}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{i(2^{2\ell+j}x\xi+2^{\ell+2j}y\eta)}\mathfrak{m}_{3\ell}^{2^{-3j}v}(\xi,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\eta

where, using the definitions from (
3.7
), we have

\mathfrak{m}_{n}^{w}(\xi,\eta):=\beta_{0}(2^{C_{1}}\xi)\beta_{0}(2^{C_{2}}\eta)\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i2^{n}\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w}(t)}a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Since
v\in V_{j}=[2^{3j},2^{3(j+1)}]
, repeated integration-by-parts shows that

(4.1)

|K_{\ell,j}^{v}(x,y)|\lesssim_{N}\sup_{1\leq w\leq 8}\|\mathfrak{m}_{3\ell}^{w}\|_{C^{N}(\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}2^{3(j+\ell)}\big(1+2^{2\ell+j}|x|+2^{\ell+2j}|y|\big)^{-N}

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Differentiating
\mathfrak{m}_{n}^{w}
, the problem reduces to showing

(4.2)

\sup_{1\leq w\leq 8}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\xi|\leq 2^{-C_{1}+1}\\
|\eta|\leq 2^{-C_{2}+1}\end{subarray}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i2^{n}\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w}(t)}t^{m}a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|\lesssim_{m,M}2^{-nM}\qquad\textrm{for all $m$, $M\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$.}

Indeed, the desired bounds then follow by taking
M:=2N
, so the gain in
2^{-2Nn}
from (
4.2
) compensates for the loss of
2^{Nn}
in (
4.1
).

Let
1\leq w\leq 8
,
(\xi,\eta)\in\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
satisfy
|\xi|\leq 2^{-C_{1}+1}
and
|\eta|\leq 2^{-C_{2}+1}
and
t\in\mathrm{supp}\,a
, so that
1/2\leq|t|\leq 2
. Since
(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime}(t)=-\xi-2\eta t+3wt^{2}
, we have

|(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime}(t)|\geq 3/4-|\xi|-4|\eta|\geq 1/8,

provided
C_{1}
,
C_{2}\geq 4
. Moreover,
|(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime\prime}(t)|\leq 2|\eta|+6|t|w\leq 2^{6}
and
|(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)|\leq 6w\leq 2^{6}
. The desired estimate (

) now follows from an application of the non-stationary phase Lemma
5.1
below.
∎

Lemma
3.1
easily follows from the above kernel estimate.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Using a standard argument (see, for instance,

Lemma 5.2

We now turn to the proofs of Lemma

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

,
(\xi,\eta)\in\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
1\leq w\leq 8
. If
|\eta|\geq 2^{7}
, then

|m_{n}^{w}(\xi,\eta)|\lesssim 2^{-n/2}|\eta|^{-1/2}.

Lemma

and Lemma

easily combine to give Lemma

.

Proof of Lemma
3.6
.

For
k_{2}>8
we have
|\eta|\geq 2^{k_{2}-1}>2^{7}
on the support of
m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}
. Thus, we can apply Lemma
5.3
and Plancherel’s theorem to deduce the desired bound for

It remains to prove Lemma
3.9
. The key ingredient is the following multiplier estimate, which is again based on van der Corput’s lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{diag}}
and
(\xi,\eta)\in\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
1\leq w\leq 8
.

i)

If
k_{2}>8
and
k_{1}\leq\kappa n
, then

\Big(\int_{1}^{8}|m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\leq\kappa}(\xi,\eta)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}w\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim 2^{-n/2-\kappa n/2-k_{2}/2}.

ii)

If
k_{2}\leq 8
and
\lfloor\kappa n\rfloor\leq\ell\leq\lfloor n/3\rfloor
, then

\Big(\int_{1}^{8}|m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell}(\xi,\eta)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}w\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim 2^{-(n+\ell)/2}.

Proof.

For both i) and ii) we shall exploit the fact that the
\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)
localisation corresponds to localising the
w
variable to a small interval. In particular,

(5.4)

\Big|w+\frac{\eta^{2}}{3\xi}\Big|=\frac{1}{3|\xi|}|\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)|\leq 2^{-k_{1}}|\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)|

for
2^{k_{1}-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{k_{1}+1}
.

i)
On the support of
m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\leq\kappa}
we have
|\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)|\leq 2^{2k_{2}-2\kappa n+1}
. This combines with (
5.4
) to give

\Big|w+\frac{\eta^{2}}{3\xi}\Big|\leq 2^{2k_{2}-k_{1}-2\kappa n+1}.

Since
(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{diag}}
and
k_{1}\leq\kappa n
, it follows that
k_{2}\leq k_{1}+C\leq\kappa n+C
for
C:=(C_{1}\vee C_{2})+16
. Thus, for fixed
(\xi,\eta)\in\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we conclude that
m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\leq\kappa}(\xi,\eta)
is non-zero only if
w
belongs to a fixed interval of length
O(2^{-\kappa n})
centred at
-\eta^{2}/(3\xi)
.

On the other hand, since
k_{2}>8
we have
|\eta|\geq 2^{k_{2}-1}>2^{7}
and Lemma
5.3
implies that

|m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\leq\kappa}(\xi,\eta)|\lesssim 2^{-n/2-k_{2}/2}

holds uniformly in
1\leq w\leq 8
. Combining the above observations, the desired result immediately follows.

ii)
On the support of
m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell}
we have
|\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)|\leq 2^{2k_{2}-2\ell+1}
. This combines with (
5.4
) to give

\Big|w+\frac{\eta^{2}}{3\xi}\Big|\leq 2^{2k_{2}-k_{1}-2\ell+1}.

Since
k_{2}\leq 8
, it follows that
2k_{2}-k_{1}\leq 32+C_{1}
. Thus, for fixed
(\xi,\eta)\in\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we conclude that
m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell}(\xi,\eta)
is non-zero only if
w
belongs to a fixed interval of length
O(2^{-2\ell})
centred at
-\eta^{2}/(3\xi)
.

On the other hand, we claim that

(5.5)

|m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell}(\xi,\eta)|\lesssim 2^{-(n-\ell)/2}

holds uniformly in
1\leq w\leq 8
. Once, this is established and combined with our earlier observation, the desired result immediately follows.

The case
\ell=\lfloor n/3\rfloor
of (
5.5
) follows from van der Corput’s lemma with third order derivatives, since
|(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)|=6w\geq 1
. For the case
\lfloor\kappa n\rfloor\leq\ell<\lfloor n/3\rfloor
of (
5.5
), we first write
\beta=\beta^{+}+\beta^{-}
where
\beta^{+}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
is supported on
[1/2,2]
and
\beta^{-}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
is supported on
[-2,-1/2]
. We decompose

m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell}=m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell,+}+m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell,-}

where

m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell,\pm}(\xi,\eta):=\beta^{\pm}(2^{-2k_{2}+2\ell}\Delta(w;\xi,\eta))m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w}(\xi,\eta).

Since
\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)<0
on the support of
m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell,-}
, the phase function
\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w}
has no (real) critical points (see (
3.8
)). Furthermore,

(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime}(t)\geq(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime}\Big(\frac{\eta}{3w}\Big)=-\xi-\frac{\eta^{2}}{3w}\geq 2^{2k_{2}-2\ell-6}

for all
t\in\mathrm{supp}\,a
and
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell,-}
, since
t\mapsto(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime}(t)
has a minimum at

. Consequently, by van der Corput’s lemma with first order derivatives,

(5.6)

|m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell,-}(\xi,\eta)|\lesssim 2^{-(n-2\ell)}\qquad\text{for $(\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell,-}$}.

On the support of
m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell,+}
, we have
\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)>0
and the phase function
\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w}
admits the critical points
t_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)
, which are the roots of the quadratic polynomial
(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime}(t)
with leading coefficient
3w
. Thus,

(5.7)

\displaystyle(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=3w(t-t_{+}(w;\xi,\eta))(t-t_{-}(w;\xi,\eta)),

(5.8)

\displaystyle(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=6w\Big(t-\frac{t_{+}(w;\xi,\eta)+t_{-}(w;\xi,\eta)}{2}\Big).

From the quadratic formula (
3.8
), we have

\frac{t_{+}(w;\xi,\eta)-t_{-}(w;\xi,\eta)}{2}=\frac{\sqrt{\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)}}{3w}.

Since
\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)\geq 2^{2k_{2}-2\ell-1}
on the support of
m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell,+}
, we conclude that there exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that
|t_{+}(w;\xi,\eta)-t_{-}(w;\xi,\eta)|\geq 4c2^{-\ell}
on the support of
m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell,+}
.

We now write
m_{n}^{w}=\mathfrak{m}_{n,1}^{w}+\mathfrak{m}_{n,2}^{w}
where

\mathfrak{m}_{n,i}^{w}(\xi,\eta):=\int_{T_{i}}e^{i2^{n}\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w}(t)}a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad i=1,\,2,

for

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle:=\big\{t\in\mathbb{R}:1/2\leq|t|\leq 2\textrm{ and }\min_{\pm}|t-t_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)|\geq c2^{-\ell}\big\},

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle:=\big\{t\in\mathbb{R}:1/2\leq|t|\leq 2\big\}\setminus T_{1}.

Note that each of the sets
T_{i}
consists of a union of a bounded number of intervals. It follows from (
5.7
) that
|(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime}(t)|\geq 3c^{2}2^{-2\ell}
for all
t\in T_{1}
whenver
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell,+}
. Thus, van der Corput’s lemma with first order derivatives gives

(5.9)

|\mathfrak{m}_{n,1}^{w}(\xi,\eta)|\lesssim 2^{-(n-2\ell)}\qquad\textrm{for $(\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell,+}$.}

On the other hand, it follows from (
5.8
) that
|(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime\prime}(t)|\geq 6c2^{-\ell}
for all
t\in T_{2}
whenever
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell,+}
. Thus, van der Corput’s lemma with second order derivatives gives

(5.10)

|\mathfrak{m}_{n,2}^{w}(\xi,\eta)|\lesssim 2^{-(n-\ell)/2}\qquad\textrm{for $(\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,m_{n,k_{1},k_{2}}^{w,\ell,+}$.}

Since
\ell\leq n/3
, the estimates (
5.6
) and (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) combine to give (
5.5
).
∎

Using the preceding oscillatory integral estimates, we conclude this section with the proof of Lemma
3.9
.

6.1.

Recall the multiplier

N_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta):=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{w}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)&\partial_{w}\partial_{\xi\eta}^{2}\phi_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)&\partial_{w}\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\phi_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)\\

\partial_{w}^{3}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)&\partial_{w}^{3}\partial_{\xi\eta}^{2}\phi_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)&\partial_{w}^{3}\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\phi_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)\\
\end{bmatrix}

satisfies

(6.10)

|\det N_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)|\gtrsim\sigma\qquad\textrm{for all $(w;\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mathfrak{h}_{\pm}$},

where the implied constants can be taken to be absolute.

Proof.

Direct computation (
c.f.
§
B.3
) reveals that
\det\partial^{2}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}}\phi_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)\equiv 0
as a function of
(w;\xi,\eta)
and so we only need to verify (
6.10
). A further, and somewhat involved(!), computation (
c.f.
§
B.3
) shows that

(6.11)

\det N_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)=-\frac{3^{7}}{2^{4}}\cdot\frac{\xi\cdot t_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)^{8}}{\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)^{5}}.

The desired bound therefore follows immediately from the support conditions in Definition
6.1
and Definition
6.3
i).
∎

6.3.
Local smoothing via Nikodym non-compression

We combine the observations of the preceding section with Theorem
1.4
to deduce the following.

Proposition 6.5
.

There exists some
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds. For all
2<p<4
there exists
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{n}}(p)>0
such that

\big\|U^{\lambda}[\Phi_{\pm};\mathfrak{h}_{\pm}]f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{n}}(p)}(\sigma\tau)^{-M}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

for all
\lambda\geq 1
, whenever
\mathfrak{h}_{\pm}\in\mathfrak{N}_{\pm}(\sigma,\tau)
for some
0<\sigma,\tau<1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.2
i), we know that
\Phi_{\pm}
is translation-invariant, real analytic and satisfies H1) and H2) on an open neighbourhood of
\mathrm{supp}\,\mathfrak{h}_{\pm}
. Furthermore, by Lemma
6.4
, there exists some
\gamma\sim\sigma
such that
\Phi_{\pm}
satisfies the Nikodym non-compression hypothesis with parameter
\gamma
on an open neighbourhood of
\mathrm{supp}\,\mathfrak{h}_{\pm}
. The hypothesis (
6.7
) and Lemma
6.2
ii) guarantee that the derivative bounds (
1.14
) hold for
W=O((\sigma\tau)^{-M_{0}})
for some absolute exponent
M_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. We may therefore directly apply Theorem
1.4
to obtain the desired result.
∎

6.4.
Local smoothing for the exceptional piece via decoupling

To complement Proposition
6.5
, we prove a local smoothing result for operators of the form
U^{\lambda}[\Phi_{\pm};\mathfrak{e}_{\pm}]
with
\mathfrak{e}_{\pm}\in\mathfrak{E}_{\pm}(\sigma)
.

Proposition 6.6
.

For all
2\leq p\leq 4
and
\delta>0
, given
\lambda\geq 1
and
\lambda^{-1/2}\leq\sigma\leq 1
, we have

(6.12)

\big\|U^{\lambda}[\Phi_{\pm};\mathfrak{e}_{\pm}]f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim_{\delta}\lambda^{1/2+\delta}\sigma^{1/2-1/p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

whenever
\mathfrak{e}_{\pm}\in\mathfrak{E}_{\pm}(\sigma)
.

The mechanism behind Proposition
6.6
is very different from that of Proposition
6.5
and, in particular, relies on
\ell^{p}
decoupling theory. This is a standard tool for proving
L^{p}
-local smoothing estimates for wave-type propagators
[
33
]
; it is typically not as relevant in the Schrödinger context. However, here decoupling allows for the (crucial) gain of
\sigma^{1/2-1/p}
, which arises from the frequency localisation near the line
\mathcal{L}:=\{(0,\eta):\eta\in\mathbb{R}\}
in the amplitude class
\mathfrak{E}_{\pm}(\sigma)
. The proof is complicated by the fact that our operator is
hyperbolic
: the eigenvalues of the matrix (
6.3
) have opposite signs. This rules out application of the more powerful
\ell^{2}
decoupling theory, and instead we rely on a two-step decoupling process which combines a lower dimensional ‘small cap’ decoupling inequality, exploiting curvature properties of
\Phi_{\pm}
restricted to
\mathcal{L}
, with a variable coefficient extension of the
\ell^{p}
decoupling theory from
[
8
]
. We postpone the details until §
6.7
below.

6.5.
Applying the local smoothing estimates

We now combine Proposition
6.5
and Proposition
6.6
with stationary phase arguments to prove a general
L^{p}
smoothing estimate from which Proposition
3.4
and Proposition
3.10
follows.

Definition 6.7
.

For
0<\tau\leq 1
, let
\mathfrak{B}(\tau)
denote the class of
b\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\times\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
such that:

i)

|\xi|
,
|\eta|\leq C_{\star}
,
\tau\leq w\leq C_{\star}
and
|\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)|\geq\tau
for all
(w;\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,b
.

ii)

If
\tau<C_{\star}^{-1}
, then
|\xi|>C_{\star}^{-1}
for all
\xi\in\mathrm{supp}_{\xi}\,b
.

iii)

|\partial_{w}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{1}}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta_{2}}b(w;\xi,\eta)|\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}\tau^{-\alpha-\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}}
for all
\alpha
,
\beta_{1}
,
\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Given
b\in\mathfrak{B}(\tau)
, for each
w\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{w}[b]
denote the operator with Fourier multiplier

m_{n}^{w}[b](\xi,\eta):=b(w;\xi,\eta)m_{n}^{w}(\xi,\eta)=b(w;\xi,\eta)\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i2^{n}\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w}(t)}a(t)\,\,\mathrm{d}t.

With these definitions, our general smoothing estimate reads thus.

Proposition 6.8
.

There exists some
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds. For all
2<p<4
, there exists some
\varepsilon(p)>0
such that

(6.13)

\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\|\mathcal{H}_{n}^{w}[b]f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}\,\,\mathrm{d}w\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim_{p}2^{-n/p-\varepsilon(p)n}\tau^{-M}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

holds whenever
b\in\mathfrak{B}(\tau)
for some
2^{-n/4}\leq\tau\leq 1
.

Proof.

We break the proof into steps.

Step 1: Initial decomposition.
Write
m_{n}^{w}[b]=\sum_{\pm}m_{n,\pm}^{w}[b]+m_{n,\mathrm{error}}^{w}[b]
where

m_{n,\pm}^{w}[b](\xi,\eta):=b_{\mathrm{main}}(w;\xi,\eta)\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i2^{n}\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w}(t)}a_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta;t)\,\,\mathrm{d}t

and, for
C_{\star}\geq 1
as above,

\displaystyle b_{\mathrm{main}}(w;\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle:=b(w;\xi,\eta)\chi_{(0,\infty)}\big(\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)\big),

\displaystyle a_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta;t)

\displaystyle:=a(t)\beta_{0}\big(C_{\star}^{2}\tau^{-1/2}(t-t_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta))\big).

The hypotheses on the support of
b
ensures the function
b_{\mathrm{main}}
is smooth. Furthermore, for
(w;\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,b_{\mathrm{main}}
, the critical points
t_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)
are real valued and so the
a_{\pm}
are well-defined. Recall that the
t_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)
satisfy

\frac{t_{+}(w;\xi,\eta)-t_{-}(w;\xi,\eta)}{2}=\frac{\sqrt{\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)}}{3w}\geq(3C_{\star})^{-1}\tau^{1/2}.

It therefore follows that for each fixed
(w;\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,b_{\mathrm{main}}
, the
t
-supports of the
a_{\pm}
are disjoint.

Step 2: Non-stationary term.
Here we argue as in the proof of Lemma
5.4
ii).

Let
b_{\mathrm{error}}:=b-b_{\mathrm{main}}
. For
(w;\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,b_{\mathrm{error}}
we have
\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)<0
and

(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime}(t)\geq(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime}\Big(\frac{\eta}{3w}\Big)=-\frac{\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)}{3w}\geq(3C_{\star})^{-1}\tau>0\quad\text{for all $t\in\mathrm{supp}\,a$,}

where in the first inequality we have used that
t\mapsto(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime}(t)
has a minimum at
t=\eta/3w
.
Thus, by non-stationary phase (Lemma
5.1
), we see that

(6.14)

|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{1}}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta_{2}}m_{n}^{w}[b_{\mathrm{error}}](\xi,\eta)|\lesssim_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},N}2^{n(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})}2^{-Nn}\tau^{-N},\quad\textrm{$\beta_{1}$, $\beta_{2}$, $N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$.}

On the other hand, let
a_{\mathrm{error}}:=a-a_{+}-a_{-}
. It follows from (
5.7
) that, if
(w;\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,b_{\mathrm{main}}
, then

|(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime}(t)|\geq 3C_{\star}^{-4}\tau^{2}
for all
t\in\mathrm{supp}\,a_{\mathrm{error}}(w;\xi,\eta;\,\cdot\,)
.
Since

(m_{n}^{w}-m_{n,\pm}^{w})[b_{\mathrm{main}}](\xi,\eta)=b_{\mathrm{main}}(w;\xi,\eta)\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i2^{n}\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w}(t)}a_{\mathrm{error}}(w;\xi,\eta;t)\,\mathrm{d}t,

another application of non-stationary phase yields

(6.15)

|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{1}}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta_{2}}(m_{n}^{w}-m_{n,\pm}^{w})[b_{\mathrm{main}}](\xi,\eta)|\lesssim_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},N}2^{n(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})}2^{-Nn}\tau^{-2N}

for all
\beta_{1}
,
\beta_{2}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Combining (
6.14
) and (
6.15
) we have, by repeated integration-by-parts,

|\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m_{n,\mathrm{error}}^{w}[b]](x,y)|\lesssim_{N}\frac{2^{-Nn}\tau^{-2N}}{(1+2^{-n}|x|+2^{-n}|y|)^{10}}

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
w\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus, by Young’s convolution inequality, (
6.13
) holds for the piece of the operator associated to
m_{n,\mathrm{error}}^{w}[b]
.

Step 3: Passing to variable propagators.
In light of the above observations, it suffices to prove that (
6.13
) holds with the operator
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{w}[b]
replaced with either of the operators
\mathcal{H}_{n,\pm}^{w}[b]
associated to the Fourier multipliers
m_{n,\pm}^{w}[b]
. By (
5.8
), we have

(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime\prime}(t_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta))=\pm 6w\Big(\frac{t_{+}(w;\xi,\eta)-t_{-}(w;\xi,\eta)}{2}\Big)=\pm 2\sqrt{\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)}

and so the critical points of the phase are non-degenerate, in the sense that

|(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime\prime}(t_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta))|\geq 2\tau^{1/2}>0.

Provided
C_{\star}\geq 1
is chosen sufficiently large, we may therefore apply standard asymptotic expansions for oscillatory integrals (see, for instance,
[
27
, Chapter 1]
) to deduce that

(6.16)

m_{n,\pm}^{w}[b](\xi,\eta)=\tau^{-1/4}2^{-n/2}e^{i2^{n}\phi_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)}\mathfrak{a}_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)

where each
\mathfrak{a}_{\pm}
is a smooth function satisfying
\mathrm{supp}\,\mathfrak{a}_{\pm}\subseteq b_{\mathrm{main}}
and

(6.17)

|\partial^{\alpha}_{w}\partial^{\beta_{1}}_{\xi}\partial^{\beta_{2}}_{\eta}\mathfrak{a}_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)|\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}\tau^{-\alpha-\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}}\qquad\textrm{for all $\alpha$, $\beta_{1}$, $\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$.}

In view of (
6.16
), for
\Phi_{\pm}
as in (
6.1
), we have
\mathcal{H}_{n,\pm}^{2^{-n}w}=\tau^{-1/4}2^{-n/2}U^{2^{n}}[\Phi_{\pm};\mathfrak{a}_{\pm}]
. In particular, applying the rescaling
w\mapsto 2^{-n}w
, in order to prove (
6.13
) it suffices to show that for all
2<p<4
there exists some
\varepsilon(p)>0
such that

(6.18)

\big\|U^{\lambda}[\Phi_{\pm};\mathfrak{a}_{\pm}]f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2-\varepsilon(p)}\tau^{-M}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

holds uniformly over all
\lambda\geq 1
, for some universal exponent
M\in\mathbb{N}
.

Step 4: Applying
L^{p}
local smoothing.

We shall prove (
6.18
) by combining Proposition
6.5
and Proposition
6.6
. First of all, we verify that the amplitudes
\mathfrak{a}_{\pm}
lie in the class
\mathfrak{A}_{\pm}(\tau)
from Definition
6.1
. The conditions
|\xi|
,
|\eta|\leq C_{\star}
,
\tau\leq w\leq C_{\star}
and
|\Delta(w;\xi,\eta)|\geq\tau
for
(w;\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mathfrak{a}_{\pm}
are inherited from the support of
b
. It remains to show
1/4\leq t_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)\leq 4
for
(w;\xi,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mathfrak{a}_{\pm}
. Indeed, if the above condition on
t_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)
fails and
C_{\star}\geq 4
, then it follows that

|t-t_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)|>2C_{\star}^{-2}\geq 2C_{\star}^{-2}\tau^{1/2}\qquad\textrm{for all $t\in[1/2,2]$.}

Thus, we see from the definitions that
a_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta;\,\cdot\,)\equiv 0
, which forces
\mathfrak{a}_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)=0
. These conclusions hold in a neighbourhood of
(w;\xi,\eta)
and so
(w;\xi,\eta)\notin\mathrm{supp}\,\mathfrak{a}_{\pm}
.

Now fix
2<p<4
and let
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{n}}(p)>0
be the quantities guaranteed by Proposition
6.5
. Defining
\delta_{\mathrm{n}}(p):=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{n}}(p)/(2M)\leq 1/4
, we write

\mathfrak{a}_{\pm}=\mathfrak{h}_{\pm}+\mathfrak{e}_{\pm}\quad\textrm{where}\quad\mathfrak{e}_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta):=\mathfrak{a}_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)\beta_{0}\big(\lambda^{\delta_{\mathrm{n}}(p)}\xi\big).

We may assume
\lambda^{\delta_{\mathrm{n}}(p)}\geq 4C_{\star}
, since otherwise the desired bound is trivial. Thus, by property ii) from Definition
6.7
, if
\tau<C_{\star}^{-1}
, then
|\xi|>C_{\star}^{-1}
for
\xi\in\mathrm{supp}_{\xi}\,\mathfrak{a}_{\pm}
, so
\mathfrak{e}_{\pm}\equiv 0
. Using this observation and (
6.17
), we see that

\mathfrak{h}_{\pm}\in\mathfrak{N}_{\pm}(\lambda^{-\delta_{\mathrm{n}}(p)},\tau)\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\mathfrak{e}_{\pm}\in\mathfrak{E}_{\pm}(\lambda^{-\delta_{\mathrm{n}}(p)}).

Thus, Proposition
6.5
implies that

\big\|U^{\lambda}[\Phi_{\pm};\mathfrak{h}_{\pm}]f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim_{p}\lambda^{1/2-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{n}}(p)/2}\tau^{-M}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

On the other hand, Proposition
6.6
(with, say,
\delta:=1/4-1/(2p)>0
) implies that

\big\|U^{\lambda}[\Phi_{\pm};\mathfrak{e}_{\pm}]f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim_{p}\lambda^{1/2-\varepsilon(p)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

where
\varepsilon(p):=(1/4-1/(2p))\delta_{\mathrm{n}}(p)
satisfies
0<\varepsilon(p)<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{n}}(p)/2
. Combining these two estimates, we deduce the desired bound (
6.18
).
∎

6.7.
The decoupling argument

It remains to prove Proposition
6.6
. We begin with some preliminary definitions and lemmas.

For
0<\sigma\leq 1
, let
\mathcal{I}(\sigma)
be a finitely-overlapping cover of
[-2C_{\star},2C_{\star}]
intervals of length
2\sigma
and
(\zeta_{I})_{I\in\mathcal{I}(\sigma)}
be a smooth partition of unity subordinate to
\mathcal{I}(\sigma)
, satisfying
|\partial_{s}^{N}\zeta_{I}(s)|\lesssim_{N}\sigma^{-N}
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Let
P_{I}
be the Fourier projection operator acting on functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with multiplier
(\xi,\eta)\mapsto\zeta_{I}(\eta)

\mathfrak{e}_{\pm}\in\mathfrak{E}_{\pm}(\sigma)

\mathfrak{e}_{\pm,I}(w;\xi,\eta):=\mathfrak{e}_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)\zeta_{I}(\eta)

\mathfrak{e}_{\pm,I}\in\mathfrak{E}_{\pm}(\sigma)

Lemma 6.9

(Coarse scale decoupling)
.

For
2\leq p\leq 4
and
\delta>0
, given
\lambda\geq 1
and
\lambda^{-1/2}\leq\sigma\leq 1

(6.20)

\big\|U^{\lambda}[\Phi_{\pm};\mathfrak{e}_{\pm}]f\big\|_{L^{p}(B_{\lambda})}\lesssim_{\delta}\sigma^{-(1/2-1/p)-\delta}\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}(\sigma)}\big\|U^{\lambda}[\Phi_{\pm};\mathfrak{e}_{\pm,I}]f\big\|_{L^{p}(w_{B_{\lambda}})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

up to a rapidly decreasing
L^{p}

is a
\lambda

\mathfrak{e}_{\pm}\in\mathfrak{E}_{\pm}(\sigma)
.

Here and below, we say that an inequality
A(f;\lambda)\lesssim_{L}B(f;\lambda)
holds
up to a rapidly decreasing

if
A(f;\lambda)\lesssim_{L,N}B(f;\lambda)+\lambda^{-N}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
holds for all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Furthermore, given a ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for

,
3
, we let

\lambda
-ball
B_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

By combining Lemma
6.9
and Lemma
6.12
, using the rapid decay of
w_{B_{\lambda}}
, we deduce that

\big\|U^{\lambda}[\Phi_{\pm};\mathfrak{e}_{\pm}]f\big\|_{L^{p}(B_{\lambda})}\lesssim_{\delta}\lambda^{\delta}(\lambda\sigma)^{1/2-1/p}\Big(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta(\lambda^{-1/2})}\big\|U^{\lambda}[\Phi_{\pm};\mathfrak{e}_{\pm,\theta}]f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

holds up to the inclusion of a rapidly decreasing
L^{p}
error. It remains to show

(6.41)

\Big(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta(\lambda^{-1/2})}\big\|U^{\lambda}[\Phi_{\pm};\mathfrak{e}_{\pm,\theta}]f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\lambda^{1/p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

By Plancherel’s theorem (since our phases are translation-invariant: more generally one may appeal to Hörmander’s oscillatory integral theorem
[
16
]
),

(6.42)

\big\|U^{\lambda}[\Phi_{\pm};\mathfrak{e}_{\pm,\theta}]f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
f_{\theta}
is defined by
\widehat{f}_{\theta}:=\tilde{\psi}_{\theta}\cdot\widehat{f}
for
\tilde{\psi}_{\theta}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
satisfying
\tilde{\psi}_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=1
for
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in 2\cdot\theta
and
\mathrm{supp}\,\tilde{\psi}_{\theta}\subseteq 4\cdot\theta
. By taking the
\ell^{2}
sum of (
6.42
) over all
\theta\in\Theta(\lambda^{-1/2})
and summing the resulting right-hand side using Plancherel’s theorem, we obtain the
p=2
case of (
6.41
).

On the other hand, the kernel estimate from Lemma
6.13
readily implies that

\|U^{\lambda}[\Phi_{\pm};\mathfrak{e}_{\pm,\theta}]f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

for all
\theta\in\Theta(\lambda^{-1/2})
. This is precisely the
p=\infty
case of (
6.41
). Interpolating this with the above
L^{2}
yields (
6.41
) for all
2\leq p\leq\infty
.
∎

Appendix B
Calculus computations

For the reader’s convenience, we present the steps of some of the more involved calculus computations appearing in §
6
.

B.1.
Derivative dictionary

Applying the chain rule to the definition of
\phi_{\pm}
, together with the fact that the
t_{\pm}(w;\xi,\eta)
are roots of
(\phi_{\xi,\eta}^{w})^{\prime}
, we see that

\partial_{\xi}\phi_{\pm}=-t_{\pm},\qquad\partial_{\eta}\phi_{\pm}=-t_{\pm}^{2},\qquad\partial_{w}\phi_{\pm}=t_{\pm}^{3}

and so

\phi_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}=-\partial_{\xi}t_{\pm},\qquad\phi_{\xi\eta}^{2}\phi_{\pm}=-\partial_{\eta}t_{\pm},\qquad\phi_{\eta\eta}^{2}\phi_{\pm}=-2(\partial_{\eta}t_{\pm})t_{\pm}.

By (simple) direct computation,

(B.1)

\partial_{\xi}t_{\pm}=\pm\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\Delta^{1/2}},\qquad\partial_{\eta}t_{\pm}=\pm\frac{t_{\pm}}{\Delta^{1/2}},\qquad\partial_{w}t_{\pm}=\mp\frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{t_{\pm}^{2}}{\Delta^{1/2}}

and, by combining the above displays, we see that

(B.2)

\partial_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{2}\phi_{\pm}=\mp\frac{1}{2\Delta^{1/2}}\begin{bmatrix}1&2t_{\pm}\\
2t_{\pm}&4t_{\pm}^{2}\end{bmatrix}.

We use these identities to verify the various differential conditions on
\phi_{\pm}
.

B.2.
Hörmander condition

Here we verify (
6.6
). From (
B.2
), we see that
\partial_{\xi\eta}^{2}\phi_{\pm}=2t_{\pm}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}
and
\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\phi_{\pm}=4t_{\pm}^{2}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}
. Applying the product rule to these identities,

\partial_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{2}\partial_{w}\phi_{\pm}=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{w}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}&2(\partial_{w}t_{\pm})\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}+2t_{\pm}\partial_{w}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}\\
2(\partial_{w}t_{\pm})\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}+2t_{\pm}\partial_{w}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}&8(\partial_{w}t_{\pm})t_{\pm}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}+4t_{\pm}^{2}\partial_{w}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}\end{bmatrix}.

Taking the determinant, we may simplify the matrix by performing the operations

\textrm{Row 2}\to\textrm{Row 2}-2t_{\pm}\times\textrm{Row 1}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\textrm{Col 2}\to\textrm{Col 2}-2t_{\pm}\times\textrm{Col 1}

to obtain

\det\partial_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{2}\partial_{w}\phi_{\pm}=\det\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{w}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}&2(\partial_{w}t_{\pm})\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}\\
2(\partial_{w}t_{\pm})\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}&0\end{bmatrix}=-4(\partial_{w}t_{\pm})^{2}(\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\phi_{\pm})^{2}.

Applying the formula for
\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\phi_{\pm}
from (
B.2
) and for
\partial_{w}t_{\pm}
from (
B.1
), the desired result follows.

B.3.
Nikodym non-compression

By computing the determinant in (
B.2
), we see that
\det\partial_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{2}\phi_{\pm}\equiv 0
.

We now verify (
6.11
). Again note that, by (
B.2
), we have
\partial_{\xi\eta}^{2}\phi_{\pm}=2t_{\pm}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}
and
\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\phi_{\pm}=4t_{\pm}^{2}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}
. Thus, by applying the chain rule and exploiting obvious linear dependence relations,

(B.3)

\det N_{\pm}=2^{4}\det\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{w}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}&(\partial_{w}t_{\pm})(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm})&0\\
\partial_{w}^{2}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}&A&(\partial_{w}t_{\pm})^{2}(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm})\\
\partial_{w}^{3}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}&B&C\end{bmatrix}

where

(B.4-i)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=(\partial_{w}^{2}t_{\pm})(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm})+2(\partial_{w}t_{\pm})(\partial_{w}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}),

(B.4-ii)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=(\partial_{w}^{3}t_{\pm})(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm})+3(\partial_{w}^{2}t_{\pm})(\partial_{w}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm})+3(\partial_{w}t_{\pm})(\partial_{w}^{2}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}),

(B.4-iii)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle:=3(\partial_{w}^{2}t_{\pm})(\partial_{w}t_{\pm})(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm})+3(\partial_{w}t_{\pm})^{2}(\partial_{w}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}).

By differentiating the right-hand formula in (
B.1
), we have

\partial_{w}^{2}t_{\pm}=\frac{3^{2}}{2}\Big(\frac{t_{\pm}^{3}}{\Delta}\pm\frac{\xi t_{\pm}^{2}}{2\Delta^{3/2}}\Big),\qquad\partial_{w}^{3}t_{\pm}=\mp\frac{3^{4}}{2^{2}}\Big(\frac{t_{\pm}^{4}}{\Delta^{3/2}}\pm\frac{\xi t_{\pm}^{3}}{\Delta^{2}}+\frac{\xi^{2}t_{\pm}^{2}}{2\Delta^{5/2}}\Big).

Whilst differentiating the top-left entry of the Hessian in (
B.2

\partial_{w}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}=-\frac{3\xi}{2\Delta}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm},\quad\partial_{w}^{2}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}=\frac{3^{3}\xi^{2}}{2^{2}\Delta^{2}}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm},\quad\partial_{w}^{3}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}=-\frac{3^{4}5\xi^{3}}{2^{3}\Delta^{3}}\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}.

Substituting these identities into (
B.4-i
), (
B.4-ii
) and (
B.4-iii
), we obtain

(B.5-i)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\frac{3^{2}t_{\pm}^{2}}{2\Delta}\Big(t_{\pm}\pm\frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{\xi}{\Delta^{1/2}}\Big)(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}),

(B.5-ii)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{3^{4}t_{\pm}^{2}}{2\Delta^{3/2}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\cdot t_{\pm}^{2}\pm\frac{\xi t_{\pm}}{\Delta^{1/2}}+\frac{5}{2^{2}}\cdot\frac{\xi^{2}}{\Delta}\Big)(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}),

(B.5-iii)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{3^{4}t_{\pm}^{4}}{2^{2}\Delta^{3/2}}\Big(t_{\pm}\pm\frac{\xi}{\Delta^{1/2}}\Big)(\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi_{\pm}).

Substituting (

\det N_{\pm}=\pm\frac{3^{4}\xi t_{\pm}^{6}}{\Delta^{5/2}}\Big(\mp\frac{1}{2\Delta^{1/2}}\Big)^{3}\det\begin{bmatrix}1&1&0\\
-\frac{3^{2}\xi}{2\Delta}&\mp\frac{3t_{\pm}}{\Delta^{1/2}}-\frac{3^{2}\xi}{2\Delta}&1\\

6.11

B.4.
Curvature condition

Here we verify (
6.37
). Note that

\Delta(w;0,\eta)=\eta^{2}\quad\textrm{and}\quad t_{\pm}(w;0,\eta)=\frac{\eta\pm|\eta|}{3w}.

In particular, there is always at most one non-zero root which is given by
t_{\mathrm{sgn}\,\eta}(w;0,\eta):=2\eta/(3w)
. Arguing as in the previous sections

\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\partial_{w}\phi_{\pm}=\frac{6t_{\pm}^{2}}{\Delta}\Big(t_{\pm}\pm\frac{\xi}{2\Delta^{1/2}}\Big)

and substituting the above values gives

\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\partial_{w}\phi_{\mathrm{sgn}\,\eta}(w;0,\eta)=6\cdot\frac{t_{\mathrm{sgn}\,\eta}(w;0,\eta)^{3}}{\eta^{2}}=\frac{2^{4}}{3^{2}}\cdot\frac{\eta}{w^{3}},

as required.

Abstract




Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed nonempty set such that for some

d\in[0,n]
and
\varepsilon>0
the
d
-Hausdorff content
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(S\cap Q(x,r))\geq\varepsilon r^{d}

for all cubes
Q(x,r)
centered in
x\in S
with side length
2r\in(0,2]
. For every
p\in(1,\infty)
, denote by
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

the classical Sobolev space on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We give an intrinsic characterization of the restriction
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
of the space
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the set
S
provided that
p>\max\{1,n-d\}
. Furthermore, we prove the existence of a bounded linear operator
\operatorname{Ext}:W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}\to W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\operatorname{Ext}
is right inverse for the usual trace operator. In particular, for
p>n-1
we characterize the trace space of the Sobolev space
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the closure
\overline{\Omega}
of an arbitrary open path-connected set
\Omega
.
Our results extend those available for
p\in(1,n]
with much more stringent restrictions on
S
.

1.1
Main results

Henceforth
D^{\alpha}f
, where
\alpha
is a multi-index, stands for the distributional (Sobolev) partial derivative of
a function
f\in L^{\text{\rm loc}}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In what follows we set
D^{0}f:=f
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For an open set
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, denote by

W_{p}^{1}(G)
the Sobolev space of all equivalence classes of real-valued functions
F\in L_{p}(G)

whose distributional partial derivatives on
G
belong to
L_{p}(G)
. Equip this space with the norm

\|F|W^{1}_{p}(G)\|:=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leq 1}\|D^{\alpha}F|L_{p}(G)\|.

(1.1)

Similarly, given an open set
G
for every
p\in[1,\infty]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we can define the homogeneous Sobolev space
L^{1}_{p}(G)
of all equivalence classes of real-valued functions
F\in L^{\operatorname{loc}}_{1}(G)
, with the seminorm

\|F|L^{1}_{p}(G)\|:=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=1}\|D^{\alpha}F|L_{p}(G)\|.

(1.2)

By
B(x,r)
(
Q(x,r)
) we denote the closed ball (cube) centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of radius
r>0
(with side length
2r
).
We say that
F\in W_{p}^{1,\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(
F\in L_{p}^{1,\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) if and only if
F\in W_{p}^{1}(\operatorname{int}B(0,r))
(
F\in L_{p}^{1}(\operatorname{int}B(0,r))
)
for every
r\in(0,\infty)
.

In order to formulate our main results we briefly recall (for the reader convenience) the basic notions of geometric measure theory.
For a detailed exposition, see Ch. 5, Section 1 of
[
1
]
or Ch. 1, Ch. 2 of
[
7
]
. We present it here in a slightly different form (see Remark
2.1
for explanations).

Let
0\leq d\leq n
,
S
be a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\delta\in(0,+\infty]
.
Consider the set function

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(S)=\inf\sum\limits_{j}r^{d}_{j}

where the infimum is taken over all countable coverings of
S
by balls
B(x_{j},r_{j})
with arbitrary centers

x_{j}
and radii
r_{j}<\delta
. The
d
-
Hausdorff content
of a set
S
is defined as
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(S)
.
The
d
-
Hausdorff measure
of a set
S
is defined as

\mathcal{H}^{d}(S):=\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(S).

The limit exists due to monotonicity of
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(S)
with respect to
\delta
.
It can be shown that for every
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exists a number
d_{0}(S)\in[0,n]
such that

d_{0}(S)=\sup\{d:\mathcal{H}^{d}(S)=+\infty\}=\inf\{d^{\prime}:\mathcal{H}^{d^{\prime}}(S)=0\}

This number is called the
Hausdorff dimension
of
S

and denoted by
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S
.

Let
d\in[0,n]
. We say that a set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
d
-thick
if and only if there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(B(x,r)\cap S)\geq\varepsilon r^{d}
for every
x\in S
and every
r\in(0,1]
.

Fix a number
d\in[0,n]
and
p\in(\max\{1,n-d\},\infty]
. Recall (see Theorem
2.2
below for details) that for every function
F\in W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
there exist a set
E_{F}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a representative (in the class of equivalent functions)
\widehat{F}
such that
\mathcal{H}^{d}(E)=0
and every point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E_{F}
is a Lebesgue point of the function
\widehat{F}
. Thus, in what follows we identify each element
F\in W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with an arbitrary chosen such representative. As a result, for every set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d
we can define
the
d
-trace of a given element

F\in W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the set
S
as the
pointwise restriction of

\widehat{F}
to
S
. In what follows, given
d
and
p
as above and an element
F\in W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by the symbol
F|_{S}
we will denote the
d
-trace of
F
to
S
. Clearly, the
d
-trace
F|_{S}
is uniquely defined
\mathcal{H}^{d}
-a.e. on
S
. Hence, given a function
f:S\to\mathbb{R}
we will write
F|_{S}=f
if and only if
F|_{S}(x)=f(x)
for
\mathcal{H}^{d}
-almost every
x\in S
.

Using the facts mentioned above, given
p
and
d
as above, we can consider
the
d
-trace space
of the space
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
. More precisely,

W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}:=\widetilde{W}_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}/\mathring{W}_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S},

where

\begin{split}&\widetilde{W}_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}:=\{f:S\to\mathbb{R}|\text{ there exists }F\in W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ such that }F|_{S}=f\},\\
&\mathring{W}^{1}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}:=\{f:S\to\mathbb{R}|\text{ there exists }F\in W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ such that }F|_{S}=0\}.\end{split}

Equip this space with the usual
quotient-space norm.

Define the
trace operator

\operatorname{Tr}|_{S}:W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
which acts as follows

\operatorname{Tr}|_{S}[F]:=F|_{S}.

Clearly, this operator is
linear and bounded.

Our main goal in this paper is a study of the following

Problem A
.
Fix parameters
d\in[0,n]
,
p\in(\max\{1,n-d\},\infty]
and a closed set

S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d
.
Given a function
f:S\to\mathbb{R}
, how can we decide whether there exists a function
F\in W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that the
d
-trace
F|_{S}=f
? Consider the
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-norm of all functions
F\in W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
F|_{S}=f
on
S
. How small can these norms be?

Furthermore, in this article we consider closely related

Problem B
.
Fix parameters
d\in[0,n]
,
p\in(\max\{1,n-d\},\infty]
and a closed set

S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d
. Does there exist a bounded linear operator
\operatorname{Ext}:W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}\to W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

such that
\operatorname{Tr}|_{S}\circ\operatorname{Ext}=\operatorname{Id}
on
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
?

Remark 1.1.
In the case where
p=\infty
, the Sobolev space
W_{\infty}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

can be identified with the space
\operatorname{LIP}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of Lipschitz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and it is known that the restriction
\operatorname{LIP}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
coincides with the space
\operatorname{LIP}(S)
of Lipschitz functions on
S
and that, furthermore, the
classical Whitney extension operator linearly and continuously maps the space
\operatorname{LIP}(S)

into the space
\operatorname{LIP}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see e.g.,
[
40
]
, Chapter 6). Hence in the sequel
we will deal only with the case

1<p<\infty
.

Let
d\in[0,n]
. Assume that
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d
. Let
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a family of positive Borel measures with
\operatorname{supp}\mu_{k}\subset S
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
We say that
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a
d
-
regular system of measures on

S
if and only if for some universal constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
C_{3}
the following properties hold for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
:

(1)
\mu_{k}(B(x,r))\leq C_{1}r^{d}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
r\in(0,2^{-k}]
;

(2)
\mu_{k}(B(x,2^{-k}))\geq C_{2}2^{-k}
for every
x\in S
;

(3)
2^{d-n}\mu_{k}(G)\leq\mu_{k-1}(G)\leq\mu_{k}(G)
for every Borel set
G\subset S
.

Remark 1.2.

We will show in Corollary
3.1
below that for every closed
d
-thick set
S
one can construct a
d
-regular system of measures on
S
.

Assume that
p\in[1,\infty]
. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be an arbitrary Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Given a Borel function
f
, we say that
f\in L^{\text{\rm loc}}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathfrak{m})

if and only if
f\in L_{p}(B(x,r),\mathfrak{m})
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
.

Given a function
f\in L^{\text{\rm loc}}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathfrak{m})
, we set for every Borel set
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

\fint\limits_{G}f(x)\,d\mathfrak{m}(x):=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{m}(G)}\int\limits_{G}f(x)\,d\mathfrak{m}(x).

Let
S
be a closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d
for some
d\in[0,n]
. Assume that there exists a
d
-regular system of measures
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
on
S
. Let
f\in L^{\text{loc}}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu_{k})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Consider the following
Calderon-type maximal function
. Given a number
t\in[0,1)
, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we set

f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,t):=\sup\limits_{r\in(t,1)}\frac{1}{r}\fint\limits_{Q(x,r)}\Bigl|f(y)-\fint\limits_{Q(x,r)}f(z)\,d\mu_{k(r)}(z)\Bigr|\,d\mu_{k(r)}(y),

where
k(r)

is the unique integer number
for which
r\in[2^{-k(r)},2^{-k(r)+1})
.
In what follows we set
f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}:=f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(\cdot,0)
for brevity.

Fix a closed set
S
and a parameter
\lambda\in(0,1)
. For every
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
define the
maximal
2^{-j}
-porous subset
of
S
as follows. We set for every
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

S_{j}(\lambda):=\{x\in S|\hbox{ there exists }y\in Q(x,2^{-j})\hbox{ for which }Q(y,\lambda 2^{-j})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S\}.

If there exists a number
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that
S_{j}(\lambda)=S
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we say that the set
S
is
porous
.

Now we are ready to formulate the main result of this paper which gives the solution of Problems
A
and
B
.

In what follows by
\mathcal{L}_{n}
we denote the classical
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Recall Remark 1.1.

Theorem 1.1.

Let
d\in[0,n]
and
p\in(\max\{1,n-d\},\infty)
. Let
S
be a closed
d
-thick set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a
d
-regular system of measures on
S
. Then
f
belongs to the
d
-trace space
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
if and only if there exists a set
S^{\prime}\subset S
with
\mathcal{H}^{d}(S\setminus S^{\prime})=0
such that

\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\fint\limits_{Q(x,r)\cap S}|f(x)-f(z)|\,d\mu_{k(r)}(z)=0,\quad\forall x\in S^{\prime},

(1.3)

and for some
\lambda\in(0,1)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{N}_{S,p,\lambda}[f]:=\left(\int\limits_{S}|f(x)|^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int\limits_{S}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x)\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&+\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{S_{k}(\lambda)}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,2^{-k})\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.\end{split}

Furthermore

\|f|W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}\|\sim\mathcal{N}_{S,p,\lambda}[f],

(1.4)

and there exists a bounded linear operator
\operatorname{Ext}:W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}\to W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\operatorname{Tr}|_{S}\circ\operatorname{Ext}=\operatorname{Id}
on

W^{1}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
.

Remark 1.3.

Assume that
S=\overline{\Omega}
for some open path-connected set
\Omega
. It is not difficult to show (see Appendix below) the existence of a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
such that
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\infty}(Q(x,r)\cap S)\geq\varepsilon r
for every
x\in S
and

. In other words,
\overline{\Omega}
is
1
-thick. Recall Remark
1.1
.
This implies that Theorem
1.1
provides for every

a description of the trace space of the Sobolev space
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the closure of
\Omega
.

1.2
Simplifications for sets with porous boundary

The results of Theorem
1.1
can be simplified if
S
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S

possesses certain "plumpness" properties. More precisely, in this section we restrict ourselves to the case in which the set
S
has the
porous boundary

\partial S
.

Let
d\in[0,n]
. Let
S
be a nonempty closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d
. Assume that there exists a
d
-regular system of measures
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
on
S
. Assume that
f\in L_{1}^{\text{loc}}(S,\mu_{k})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Define for every
x\in S
and
r\in(0,1]

the normalized with respect to the measure
\mu_{k(r)}
best approximation of
f
by constants on
Q(x,r)

\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k(r)}}(f,Q(x,r)):=\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\fint\limits_{Q(x,r)\cap S}|f(y)-c|\,d\mu_{k(r)}(y).

Remark 1.4.

It is easy to see that

\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k(r)}}(f,Q(x,r))\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu_{k(r)}}(f,Q(x,r))\leq 2\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k(r)}}(f,Q(x,r)),

where

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu_{k(r)}}(f,Q(x,r)):=\fint\limits_{Q(x,r)\cap S}\Bigl|f(y)-\fint\limits_{Q(x,r)\cap S}f(z)\,d\mu_{k(r)}(z)\Bigr|\,d\mu_{k(r)}(y).

The exact value of
\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k(r)}}(f,Q(x,r))
will not be important for us in the sequel. Hence, we can use

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu_{k(r)}}(f,Q(x,r))
instead of
\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k(r)}}(f,Q(x,r))
which is easier to compute.

Now we present criterion which is simpler to verify in practice. Namely, instead of Calderon-type maximal functions our simplified criterion uses the normalized best approximations.

Given a closed nonempty set
S
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
consider the set

\Sigma_{k}:=\Sigma_{k}(S):=\{x\in S|\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial S)\leq 2^{-k}\}.

Recall Remark 1.1 and definition of porous sets from the previous subsection.

Theorem 1.2.

Let
d\in[0,n]
and
p\in(\max\{1,n-d\},\infty)
. Let
S
be a closed
d
-thick set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a
d
-regular system of measures on
S
. Assume that
\partial S
is porous. Then
f
belongs to the
d
-trace space
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
if and only if there exists a set
S^{\prime}\subset S
with
\mathcal{H}^{d}(S\setminus S^{\prime})=0
such that

\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\fint\limits_{Q(x,r)\cap S}|f(x)-f(z)|\,d\mu_{k(r)}(z)=0,\quad\forall x\in S^{\prime},

and

\begin{split}&\mathcal{R}_{S,p}[f]:=\left(\int\limits_{S}|f(x)|^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int\limits_{S}\bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x)\bigr)^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&+\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{kp(1-\frac{n-d}{p})}\int\limits_{\Sigma_{k}}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k}}(f,Q(x,2^{-k}))\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.\end{split}

Furthermore

\|f|W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}\|\sim\mathcal{R}_{S,p}[f],

(1.5)

and there exists a bounded linear operator
\operatorname{Ext}:W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}\to W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\operatorname{Tr}|_{S}\circ\operatorname{Ext}=\operatorname{Id}
on

W^{1}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
.

Remark 1.5.

In the case in which the set
S
is
Ahlfors

d
-
regular
our result coincides with that obtained in
[
36
]
(
d=n
) and
[
22
]
(
d\in[0,n)
). We will present the details in Section 4.

2.1
Geometric measure theory

In this section we briefly recall basic facts from geometric measure theory.

Let
d\in[0,\infty)
,
\delta\in(0,\infty]
and
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Define

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(S):=\inf\Bigl\{\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha(d)\Bigl(\frac{\operatorname{diam}G_{j}}{2}\Bigr)^{d}|S\subset\bigcup\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}G_{j},\operatorname{diam}G_{j}\leq\delta\Bigr\},

(2.1)

where

\alpha(d)=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)}.

Here
\Gamma(d)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}x^{d-1}e^{-x}\,dx
, (
0<d<\infty
), is the usual gamma function.

Remark 2.1.

Using the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(S)
and the fact that every bounded set
G
is contained in a ball of diameter
2\operatorname{diam}G
we see that

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(S)\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{d}_{\delta}(S)\leq 2^{d}\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(S),

where

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{d}_{\delta}(S):=\inf\Bigl\{\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha(d)r_{j}^{d}|S\subset\bigcup\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}B(x_{j},r_{j}),2r_{j}\leq\delta\Bigr\}.

Exact value of
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(S)
will not play any essential role in the sequel.
Hence, we will not distinguish between
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{d}_{\delta}(S)
and
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(S)
. This fact was used in the first section of this article.

It is easy to see that given a set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
the function
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(S)

decreases
when
\delta

increases.
This fact allows to introduce the following

Definition 2.1.

For
S
and
d
as above we call
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(S)

d
-Hausdorf content
of the set
S
. We also define

\mathcal{H}^{d}(S):=\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(S)=\sup\limits_{\delta>0}\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\delta}(S)

(2.2)

and call
\mathcal{H}^{d}(S)

d
-Hausdorf measure
of the set
S
.

Definition 2.2.

Let
d\in[0,n]
. We say that a set
S

is Ahlfors
d
-regular

if there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{2}r^{d}\leq\mathcal{H}^{d}(Q(x,r)\cap S)\leq C_{1}r^{d}

for every cube

with

and

.

The following definition is a natural generalization (see

for explanations) of the previous and is taken from
[

]
.

Definition 2.3.

Let

It is not difficult to show (see Section 5 of

Lemma 2.7.

Let

Lemma 2.8.

Let

and

is finite and bounded above by a constant

depending only on

and

Proof.
Assume that

conclude that

Let

(3)

\begin{split}&\frac{\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q(y,t)\cap S)}{\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q(x,r))}\leq\widetilde{C}(C_{1},C_{2})\frac{\mu_{k(r)}(Q(y,t)\cap S)}{\mu_{k(r)}(Q(x,r)\cap S)}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q(y,t)\cap S)}{\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q(y,t))}\\

Using

\mathcal{L}_{n}(G)=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{L}_{n}(U_{j}),\quad\mu_{k(r)}(G)=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\mu_{k(r)}(U_{j}),

(3.13)

where

For every

let

Combining (

3.2
Calderon-type maximal functions

Now we introduce one of the main tool for this paper. In the sequel we will often use the following notation. Given

the unique
integer number for which

Definition 3.2.

Let

. Let
S
be an arbitrary closed nonempty set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d

d

\begin{split}&f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,t):=\sup\limits_{r\in(t,1)}\frac{1}{r}\fint\limits_{Q(x,r)}\Bigl|f(y)-\fint\limits_{Q(x,r)}f(z)\,d\mu_{k(r)}(z)\Bigr|\,d\mu_{k(r)}(y)\\

In the case

for every

our function

coincide with that introduced in
[

(it was denoted there by

we obtain
the classical Calderon-type maximal function

is not unique. Furthermore, the choice of metric projections
\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa}
is also not unique.

4.3
Poincare-type inequalities

The aim of this subsection is to prove Poincare-type inequalities with a
d
-regular system of measures. This inequality will be the cornerstone in proving "direct" trace theorem.

Recall the classical Poincare-type inequlity.

Lemma 4.1.

Assume that
F\in W_{1}^{1,\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then for every cube
Q=Q(x,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
r>0

\fint\limits_{Q}\Bigl|F(y)-\fint\limits_{Q}F(z)\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(z)\Bigr|\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\leq C(n)r\fint\limits_{Q}|\nabla F(y)|\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y).

(4.4)

Proof.
Using the density of smooth functions in the space
W^{1}_{1}(\operatorname{int}Q(x,r))
for every
r>0
(see section 1.4 of
[
33
]
) it is sufficient to prove (
4.4
)
only for smooth functions. But the latter is a well known fact, see section 8.1 in
[
21
]
.

The following lemma is standard. We present the proof to make our exposition complete.

Lemma 4.2.

Let
d\in[0,n]
. Let
F\in W^{1}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
p\in(1,\infty)
,
p>n-d
. Then there exists a representative
\widehat{F}
of
the element
F
such that for
\mathcal{H}^{d}
-a.e. point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for every cube
Q(y,r)\ni x

\Bigl|\widehat{F}(x)-\fint\limits_{Q(y,r)}F(z)\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(z)\Bigr|\leq C\int\limits_{Q(y,r)}\frac{|\nabla F(z)|}{|x-z|^{n-1}}d\mathcal{L}_{n}(z),

(4.5)

where the constant
C>0
does not depend on
F
,
x
and
r
.

Proof.
We give only a sketch of the proof because all steps are routine. For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
we write the cube
Q=Q(y,r)
as a union of
2^{jn}
equal cubes with disjoint interiors and choose an
arbitrary such cube
Q_{j}\ni x
with side length
\frac{r}{2^{j}}
. We set
Q_{0}=Q(y,r)
.

Using Theorem
2.2
it is easy to show that there exists a representative
\widehat{F}
such that

\widehat{F}(x)=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\fint\limits_{Q_{j}}F(z)\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(z).

Using this and triangle inequality we clearly have

\begin{split}\Bigl|\widehat{F}(x)-\fint\limits_{Q_{0}}F(z)\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(z)\Bigr|\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl|\fint\limits_{Q_{j-1}}F(z)\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(z)-\fint\limits_{Q_{j}}F(z)\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(z)\Bigr|.\end{split}

(4.6)

It is clear that
|x-y|\leq\frac{r}{2^{j}}
for every
y\in Q_{j}
. Hence, using (
4.6
) and Lemma
4.3
, we get for
\mathcal{H}^{d}
-a.e. point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the estimate

\begin{split}&\Bigl|\widehat{F}(x)-\fint\limits_{Q(y,r)}F(z)\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(z)\Bigr|\leq C\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{r}{2^{j}}\fint\limits_{Q_{j}}|\nabla F(z)|\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(z)\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{i=0}^{j}\Bigl(\frac{2^{i}}{r}\Bigr)^{n-1}\int\limits_{Q_{j}\setminus Q_{j+1}}|\nabla F(z)|\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(z)\leq C\int\limits_{Q(y,r)}\frac{|\nabla F(z)|}{|x-z|^{n-1}}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(z).\end{split}

(4.7)

The lemma is proved.

Let
\alpha\in[0,n)
. Given a function
g\in L^{\operatorname{loc}}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we set

\operatorname{I}_{\alpha}[g](x):=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{g(y)}{\|x-y\|^{\alpha}}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y).

Now we formulate a particular case of the result obtained in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 4.2.

Let
\alpha\in(0,n)
and
q\in(1,\infty)
, and let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The the following statements are equivalent:

(1)
the inequality

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\operatorname{I}_{\alpha}[g](x)\,d\mu(x)\leq C\|g|L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\|

(4.8)

holds for every
g\in L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the constant
C>0
independent on
g
;

(2)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}\left[\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{n-\alpha q}}\right]^{q^{\prime}-1}\frac{dr}{r}\,d\mu(x)<+\infty.

Moreover, the least possible constant
C
in (
4.8
) satisfies the inequality

a\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}\left[\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{n-\alpha q}}\right]^{q^{\prime}-1}\frac{dr}{r}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\leq C\leq b\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}\left[\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{n-\alpha q}}\right]^{q^{\prime}-1}\frac{dr}{r}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}},

(4.9)

where the constants
a,b>0
do not depend on
\mu
.

Now we are ready to formulate the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 4.3.

Let
d\in[0,n]
. Let
S
be a closed set with
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d
. Assume that there exists a
d
-regular system of measures
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
on
S
.
Let
q\in(\max\{1,n-d\},\infty)
. Take
F\in W_{q}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then for every cube
Q=Q(x,r)
with
x\in S
and
r\in(0,1]

\fint\limits_{Q\cap S}\Bigl|F|_{S}(y)-\fint\limits_{Q}F(z)\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(z)\Bigr|\,d\mu_{k(r)}(y)\leq Cr\left(\fint\limits_{Q}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=1}|D^{\alpha}F(t)|^{q}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

(4.10)

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
F
.

Proof.
Let us fix a cube
Q=Q(x,r_{0})
. We set
g:=\chi_{Q}|\nabla F|
. We can rewrite (
4.5
) as follows. For
\mathcal{H}^{d}
-a.e.
y\in Q\cap S

\Bigl|\widehat{F}(y)-\fint\limits_{Q(x,r_{0})}F(z)\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(z)\Bigr|\leq C\operatorname{I}_{n-1}[g](y).

(4.11)

Now we consider the measure
\mu_{Q}:=\mu_{k(r)}\lfloor{Q\cap S}
. Apply Theorem
4.3
with the measure
\mu_{Q}
(instead of
\mu
) and with
\alpha=1
. It is clear that
Q\cap S\subset Q(x,2r_{0})
for every
x\in Q\cap S
. Hence, using (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) we can easily estimate the least possible constant
C
in (
4.8
) from above.
Direct computations give

\begin{split}&\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{\mu_{Q}(B(y,r))}{r^{n-q}}\right]^{q^{\prime}-1}\frac{dr}{r}\,d\mu_{Q}(y)\\
&\leq C_{1}(r_{0})^{d}\left(C_{2}\int\limits_{0}^{2r_{0}}r^{(q+d-n)(q^{\prime}-1)-1}\,dr+C_{3}(r_{0})^{d(q^{\prime}-1)}\int\limits_{2r_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{(n-q)(q^{\prime}-1)+1}}\right)\leq C_{4}(r_{0})^{1+d-\frac{n}{q}}.\end{split}

(4.12)

The constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}>0
in (
4.12
) do not depend on
x,r_{0}
.

Combining (
4.8
), (
4.9
), (
4.11
), (
4.12
) and using (
3.5
), we obtain (
4.10
).

The theorem is proved.

Corollary 4.1.

Let
d\in[0,n]
. Let
S
be a
d
-thick closed set. Assume that
q\in(\max\{1,n-d\},\infty)
and take
F\in W_{q}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Set
f:=F|_{S}
. Then
for every
r\in[0,1)
and every
x\in S

f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,r)\leq C\Bigl(\operatorname{M}^{<1}_{>r}[|\nabla F|^{q}](x)\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(4.13)

Proof.
Using Theorem
4.3
, we have for every
x\in S

\begin{split}&\sup\limits_{t\in(r,1)}\frac{1}{t}\fint\limits_{Q(x,t)\cap S}\Bigl|f(y)-\fint\limits_{Q(x,t)\cap S}f(z)\,d\mu_{k(t)}(z)\Bigr|\,d\mu_{k(t)}(y)\\
&\leq\sup\limits_{t\in(r,1)}\frac{2}{t}\fint\limits_{Q(x,t)\cap S}\Bigl|f(y)-\fint\limits_{Q(x,t)}f(z)\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\Bigr|\,d\mu_{k(t)}(y)\\
&\leq C\sup\limits_{t\in(r,1)}\left(\fint\limits_{Q(x,t)}|\nabla F(y)|^{q}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\Bigl(\operatorname{M}^{<1}_{>r}[|\nabla F|^{q}](x)\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{q}}.\end{split}

(4.14)

This proves the claim.

4.4
Pointwise estimates of the extension

In this section we prove the lemma which will be the cornerstone in proving "inverse" trace theorem. Recall that given
r>0
we denoted by
k(r)
the unique integer number for which

r\in[2^{-k(r)},2^{-k(r)+1})
. For every
\varkappa\in I
we set
k(\varkappa):=k(r_{\varkappa})
.

Lemma 4.3.

Let
d\in[0,n]
. Let
S
be a closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d
. Assume that there exists a
d
-regular system of measures
\{\mu_{k}\}=\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
on
S
.
Let
f\in L_{1}^{\operatorname{loc}}(S,\mu_{k})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Suppose that

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\fint\limits_{Q(x,2^{-k})}|f(x)-f(y)|\,d\mu_{k}(y)=0

(4.15)

for
\mathcal{L}_{n}
-a.e. point
x\in S
.
Then there exist numbers
\delta\in(0,1)
and
C>0
such that the function

F:=\operatorname{Ext}[f]:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
defined in (
4.3
) satisfies

|F(x)-F(y)|\leq C\|x-y\|\Bigl(g(x)+g(y)\Bigr)

(4.16)

for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|x-y\|<\delta
, where

\begin{split}&g(x)=\chi_{S}(x)f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x)\\
&+\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\chi_{Q_{\varkappa}}(x)\sum\limits_{\varkappa^{\prime}\in b(x)}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa^{\prime}},r_{\kappa^{\prime}})+\fint\limits_{\widetilde{Q}_{\varkappa^{\prime}}\cap S}|f(z)|\,d\mu_{k(\varkappa^{\prime})}(z)\Bigr),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{split}

(4.17)

Proof
. Let us verify that (
4.17
) holds for all
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{50})
.
It is obvious that we should consider five cases:

(1)

x,y\in S
with
\|x-y\|<\delta
;

(2)

x\in S
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S
with

\|x-y\|<\delta
;

(3)

y\in S
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S

with
\|x-y\|<\delta
;

(4)

x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S

with
\|x-y\|<\delta
and

x,y\in U_{\frac{1}{25}}(S)
;

(5)

x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S

with
\|x-y\|<\delta
and either

x\notin U_{\frac{1}{25}}(S)
or
y\notin U_{\frac{1}{25}}(S)
.

Clearly, in the last case
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus U_{\frac{1}{50}}S
because

\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{50})
. In addition, by the symmetry of the left-hand side of (
4.16
)
with respect to
x
and
y
, we can combine cases 2 and 3.

Case 1.
Take
f:S\to\mathbb{R}
. Assume that
x,y\in S
and
\|x-y\|<\delta
with
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{50})
. Let
k
be the unique natural number such that
\|x-y\|\in[2^{-k},2^{-k+1})
.
We have

\begin{split}&|F(x)-F(y)|=|f(x)-f(y)|\\
&\leq\Bigl|f(x)-\fint\limits_{Q(x,\|x-y\|)}f(z)d\mu_{k}(z)\Bigr|+\Bigl|f(y)-\fint\limits_{Q(x,\|x-y\|)}f(z)d\mu_{k}(z)\Bigr|.\end{split}

(4.18)

It is clear that
Q(x,\|x-y\|)\subset Q(y,2\|x-y\|)
. Hence, using (
4.15
), Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.2
, for
\mathcal{L}_{n}
-a.e.
y\in S
we obtain

\begin{split}&\Bigl|f(y)-\fint\limits_{Q(x,\|x-y\|)}f(z)d\mu_{k}(z)\Bigr|\leq\Bigl|\fint\limits_{Q(y,2\|x-y\|)}f(z^{\prime})d\mu_{k}(z^{\prime})-\fint\limits_{Q(x,\|x-y\|)}f(z)d\mu_{k}(z)\Bigr|\\
&+\Bigl|f(y)-\fint\limits_{Q(y,2\|x-y\|)}f(z^{\prime})d\mu_{k}(z^{\prime})\Bigr|\leq C\|x-y\|f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(y).\end{split}

(4.19)

Similar arguments for
\mathcal{L}_{n}
-almost every
x\in S
yield

\begin{split}&\Bigl|f(x)-\fint\limits_{Q(x,\|x-y\|)}f(z)d\mu_{k}(z)\Bigr|\leq C\|x-y\|f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x).\end{split}

(4.20)

As a result, from (
4.18
), (
4.19
), (
4.20
) for
\mathcal{L}_{n}
-a.e.
x\in S
and
\mathcal{L}_{n}
-a.e.
y\in S
we obtain

\begin{split}|F(x)-F(y)|=|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C\|x-y\|\bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x)+f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(y)\bigr).\end{split}

(4.21)

Case 2.
Consider the case that
x\in S
and
y\in U_{\delta}(S)\setminus S
.
As we noted at the begining of the proof, the case when
y\in S
and
x\in U_{\delta}(S)\setminus S
is similar.
Assume also that
\|x-y\|\leq\delta
with
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{50})
.

If
\delta\in(0,1)
then by (
2.6
) each point
y\in U_{\delta}(S)

lies in some cube
Q_{\varkappa}
with

. Hence,
\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}
.
If
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
then by (

) we may assume in addition that

\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\varphi_{\varkappa}(y)=1,\quad\text{ for every }y\in U_{\delta}(S).

(4.22)

Using claim 2 of Lemma
2.3
, observe that
b(y):=\{\varkappa\in I:\varphi_{\varkappa}(y)\neq 0\}
.
Therefore, (
4.3
) and (
4.22
) yield

|F(x)-F(y)|=|f(x)-F(y)|\leq\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in b(y)}\varphi_{\varkappa}(y)\Bigl|f(x)-\fint\limits_{\widetilde{Q}_{\varkappa}\cap S}f(z)\,d\mu_{k(\varkappa)}(z)\Bigr|.

(4.23)

Fix
\varkappa\in b(y)
and consider the cube
Q=Q(x,r)
with

Note that
\widetilde{Q}_{\varkappa}:=Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\varkappa})\subset Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},\frac{r}{4})\subset Q(x,r)
and

for

Observe also that (
2.6

\begin{split}&\|x-y\|\geq\operatorname{dist}(x,Q_{\varkappa})-\frac{1}{16}\operatorname{diam}Q_{\varkappa}\geq\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{diam}\widetilde{Q}_{\varkappa},\\
&\|x-\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa}\|\leq\|x-y\|+\|y-x_{\varkappa}\|+\|x_{\varkappa}-\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa}\|\leq\|x-y\|+5\operatorname{diam}\widetilde{Q}_{\varkappa}.\end{split}

(4.24)

From (
4.24

r<C\|x-y\|

(4.25)

with the constant
C>0
independent of
x
and

Hence, using (
4.15

3.2
,
3.2

\varkappa\in b(y)
and
for
\mathcal{L}_{n}
-a.e.

\begin{split}&\Bigl|f(x)-\fint\limits_{\widetilde{Q}_{\varkappa}\cap S}f(z)\,d\mu_{k(\varkappa)}(z)\Bigr|\leq\Bigl|f(x)-\fint\limits_{Q(x,r)\cap S}f(y)\,d\mu_{k(r)}(y)\Bigr|\\

As a result,

\begin{split}&|F(x)-F(y)|\leq C\|x-y\|\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x)+\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in b(y)}f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\varkappa})\Bigr)\\

(4.27)

Case 3.
Fix
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{50})

.

From (

4.32
) we conclude that

\begin{split}&|F(x)-F(y)|\leq C\|x-y\|\Bigl(\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\chi_{Q_{\varkappa}}(x)f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\kappa})+\sum\limits_{\varkappa^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}}\chi_{Q_{\varkappa^{\prime}}}(y)f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa^{\prime}},r_{\kappa^{\prime}})\Bigr)\\
&\leq C\|x-y\|\bigl(g(x)+g(y)\bigr).\end{split}

(4.33)

Consider now the second subcase. Since
F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S)
,
the mean value inequality applies. By claim 4 of Lemma
2.3
,

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\|x-y\|}|F(x)-F(y)|\leq C(n)\max\limits_{t\in[0,1]}|\nabla F(x+t(y-x))|\\
&\leq\frac{C}{r(Q_{\varkappa_{0}})}\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in b(\varkappa_{0})}\Bigl|\fint\limits_{\widetilde{Q}_{\varkappa_{0}}\cap S}f(z^{\prime})\,d\mu_{k(\varkappa_{0})}(z^{\prime})-\fint\limits_{\widetilde{Q}_{\varkappa}\cap S}f(z)\,d\mu_{k(\varkappa)}(z)\Bigr|.\end{split}

(4.34)

Using (
2.7
) and Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{C}{r(Q_{\varkappa_{0}})}\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in b(\varkappa_{0})}\Bigl|\fint\limits_{\widetilde{Q}_{\varkappa_{0}}}f(z^{\prime})\,d\mu_{k(\varkappa_{0})}(z^{\prime})-\fint\limits_{\widetilde{Q}_{\varkappa}}f(z)\,d\mu_{k(\varkappa)}(z)\Bigr|\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in b(\varkappa_{0})}f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\varkappa})\leq C\bigl(g(x)+g(y)\bigr).\end{split}

(4.35)

Combining (
4.33
), (
4.34
), and (
4.35
), we handle case 3.

Case 4.
Fix
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{50})
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|x-y\|<\delta
and at least one of this points
does not lie in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus U_{\frac{1}{25}}(S)
. Then
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus U_{\frac{1}{50}}(S)
.
By (
2.6
), this implies that for every
Q_{\varkappa_{0}}\ni x
,
Q_{\varkappa_{1}}\ni y

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{50}\leq\operatorname{dist}(x,S)\leq\operatorname{diam}Q_{\varkappa_{0}}+\operatorname{dist}(Q_{\varkappa_{0}},S)\leq 5\operatorname{diam}Q_{\varkappa_{0}},\\
&\frac{1}{50}\leq\operatorname{dist}(y,S)\leq\operatorname{diam}Q_{\varkappa_{1}}+\operatorname{dist}(Q_{\varkappa_{1}},S)\leq 5\operatorname{diam}Q_{\varkappa_{1}}.\end{split}

(4.36)

Consider two subcases by analogy with case 3.

In the first subcase there exist disjoint cubes
Q_{\varkappa_{0}}\ni x
and
Q_{\varkappa_{1}}\ni y
.
Then (
4.36
) yields

\|x-y\|\geq\min\{\operatorname{diam}Q_{\varkappa_{0}},\operatorname{diam}Q_{\varkappa_{1}}\}\geq\frac{1}{250}.

(4.37)

By (
4.3
) and (
4.17
) this implies that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\|x-y\|}|F(x)-F(y)|\\
&\leq C\Bigl(\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\chi_{Q_{\varkappa}}(x)\int\limits_{\widetilde{Q}_{\varkappa}}|f(z)|\,\mu_{k(\varkappa)}(z)+\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\chi_{Q_{\varkappa^{\prime}}}(y)\fint\limits_{\widetilde{Q}_{\varkappa^{\prime}}}|f(z^{\prime})|\,d\mu_{k(\varkappa^{\prime})}(z^{\prime}))\Bigr)\\
&\leq C\bigl(g(x)+g(y)\bigr).\end{split}

(4.38)

In the second subcase every cube
Q_{\varkappa_{0}}\ni x
has nonempty intersection with every cube

Q_{\varkappa_{1}}\ni y
meet. By claim 4 of Lemma
2.3
together with (
4.3
) and (
4.17
),
we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\|x-y\|}|F(x)-F(y)|\leq\max\limits_{t\in[0,1]}|\nabla F(x+t(y-x))|\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in b(\varkappa_{0})\cup b(\varkappa_{1})}\fint\limits_{\widetilde{Q}_{\varkappa}\bigcap S}|f(z)|\,d\mu_{k(\varkappa)}(z)\leq C\bigl(g(x)+g(y)\bigr).\end{split}

(4.39)

Combining (
4.38
) and (
4.39
), we handle case 4.

The proof of Lemma
4.4
is complete.

4.5
Trace norm

In this section, given a closed nonempty set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we introduce the functional
\mathcal{N}_{S,p,\varkappa}
and show that this functional is bounded on the trace space
W^{1}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
.

Recall Defintions
3.1
and
3.3
.

Definition 4.2.

Let
d\in[0,n]
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Let
S
be a closed nonempty set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d
. Assume that there exists a
d
-regular system of measures
\{\mu_{k}\}=\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
on
S
. Assume that
f\in L^{\operatorname{loc}}_{1}(S,\mu_{k})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. For every
p\in(1,\infty)
we set

\begin{split}&\mathcal{SN}_{S,p}[f]:=\left(\int\limits_{S}\bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x)\bigr)^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}};\\
&\mathcal{BN}_{S,p,\lambda}[f]:=\left(\int\limits_{S}|f(x)|^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\int\limits_{S_{k}(\lambda)}\bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,2^{-k})\bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}};\\
&\mathcal{N}_{S,p,\lambda}[f]:=\mathcal{SN}_{S,p}[f]+\mathcal{BN}_{S,p,\lambda}[f].\end{split}

(4.40)

Remark 4.2.

The functionals
\mathcal{BN}_{S,p,\lambda}
,
\mathcal{SN}_{S,p}
, and
\mathcal{N}_{S,p,\lambda}
have values in
[0,+\infty]
. Lemma
3.1
implies that the values of functionals
\mathcal{SN}_{S,p}[f]
,
\mathcal{BN}_{S,p,\lambda}[f]
,
\mathcal{N}_{S,p,\lambda}[f]
will remain the same after changing of the function
f
on set of
\mathcal{H}^{d}
-measure zero.
Below we establish that these functionals are bounded on
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
.

Remark 4.3.

Our notation
\mathcal{SN}_{S,p}
and
\mathcal{BN}_{S,p,\lambda}
is not picked at random. Informally speaking,
the functional
\mathcal{SN}_{p}
is the "Sobolev part" of the norm of the function
f

on the set
S
, while we may regard the functional
\mathcal{BN}_{S,p,\lambda}
as the "Besov part" of the norm of
f
on
S
. We clarify this in Example 6.1 and Example 6.2 respectively.

Lemma 4.4.

Let
d\in[0,n]
,
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
S
be a closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d
. Assume that there exists a
d
-regular system of measures
\{\mu_{k}\}=\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
on
S
. Assume that
f\in L^{\operatorname{loc}}_{1}(S,\mu_{k})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Let
\{Q_{\varkappa}\}_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}=\{Q(x_{\varkappa},r_{\varkappa})\}_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}
be the family of all Whitney cubes with
r_{\varkappa}\leq 1
,
\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}
. Then for every
\lambda\in(0,1)

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{k(d-n)}\int\limits_{S_{k}(\lambda)}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,2^{-k})\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\\
&\leq C_{1}\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\kappa})\Bigr)^{p}+C_{1}\int\limits_{S}|f(y)|^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(y),\end{split}

(4.41)

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\kappa})\Bigr)^{p}\\
&\leq C_{2}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{k(d-n)}\int\limits_{S_{k}(\lambda)}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,2^{-k})\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)+C_{2}\int\limits_{S}|f(x)|^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(x).\end{split}

(4.42)

The constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
do not depend on
f
.

Proof.
Firstly we prove the estimate (
4.41
). Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Let
\{x_{k,j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}
be a maximal
2^{-k}
separated subset of
S_{k}(\lambda)
. Using item (1) of Lemma
2.4
, we have

\int\limits_{S_{k}(\lambda)}\bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,2^{-k})\bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\leq\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\int\limits_{S_{k}(\lambda)\cap Q(x_{k,j},2^{-k})}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,2^{-k})\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x).

(4.43)

Now we use Lemma
3.3
. Choose arbitrary point
y(x_{k,j})
and index
\varkappa(k,j)\in\mathcal{I}
such that
Q_{\varkappa(k,j)}\ni y(x_{k,j})
and (
3.25
) holds. Define the function

\Theta(k,j):=\varkappa(k,j),\quad\text{ for every }k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\text{ and }j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}.

(4.44)

It follows from (
3.25
) that if
Q_{\varkappa}\cap Q(x_{k,j},2^{-k})\neq\emptyset
then
Q_{\varkappa}\supset 3Q(x_{k,j},2^{-k})
. Using this, item (2) of Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.4
with
c=6
, it is easy to see that there exists a constant
C(n)>0
such that

\operatorname{card}\{\Theta^{-1}(\varkappa)\}\leq C(n),\quad\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}.

Hence, using this and (
3.25
), we obtain for every
\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}

\sum\limits_{(k,j)\in\Theta^{-1}(\varkappa)}2^{-kn}\leq C\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa}).

(4.45)

If
\varkappa=\Theta(k,j)
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}
, then from (
2.6
) and (
3.25
) it follows that

\operatorname{dist}(x_{k,j},\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa})\leq 2^{-k}+\operatorname{dist}(Q_{\varkappa},S)+\operatorname{diam}(Q_{\varkappa})\leq 2^{-k}+5\operatorname{diam}(Q_{\varkappa})\leq\frac{11}{2^{k}}.

This gives inclusion

Q\bigl(\widetilde{x}_{\Theta(k,j)},\frac{25}{2^{k}}\bigr)\supset Q(x,2^{-k}),\quad x\in Q(x_{k,j},2^{-k}).

(4.46)

Using (
3.4
), (
4.46
) and Lemma
3.2
with
c=25
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\int\limits_{S_{k}(\lambda)\cap Q(x_{k,j},2^{-k})}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,2^{-k})\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\\
&\leq C2^{-kd}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}\bigl(\widetilde{x}_{\Theta(k,j)},2^{-k}\bigr)+\fint\limits_{Q(\widetilde{x}_{\Theta(k,j)},25)}|f(y)|\,d\mu_{0}(y)\Bigr)^{p}.\end{split}

(4.47)

From (
3.25
) it is clear that
2^{-k}\geq r_{\varkappa}\geq\frac{\lambda}{5}2^{-k}
for
\varkappa=\Theta(k,j)
. Hence, using this, monotonicity of
f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,t)
with respect to
t
, and, combining the estimates (
4.43
), (
4.45
), (
4.47
), we derive

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{k(d-n)}\int\limits_{S_{k}(\lambda)}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,2^{-k})\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}2^{-kn}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}\bigl(\widetilde{x}_{\Theta(k,j)},2^{-k}\bigr)+\fint\limits_{Q(\widetilde{x}_{\Theta(k,j)},25)}|f(y)|\,d\mu_{0}(y)\Bigr)^{p}\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\sum\limits_{(k,j)\in\Theta^{-1}(\varkappa)}2^{-kn}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}\bigl(\widetilde{x}_{\Theta(k,j)},2^{-k}\bigr)+\fint\limits_{Q(\widetilde{x}_{\Theta(k,j)},25)}|f(y)|\,d\mu_{0}(y)\Bigr)^{p}\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}\bigl(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\varkappa}\bigr)\Bigr)^{p}+C\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\Bigl(\fint\limits_{Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},25)}|f(y)|\,d\mu_{0}(y)\Bigr)^{p}.\end{split}

(4.48)

Using Hölder inequality, (
3.5
) and Lemma
2.3
with
d\mathfrak{m}(x)=|f(x)|^{p}d\mu_{0}(x)
and
c=25
, we have

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\Bigl(\fint\limits_{Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},25)}|f(y)|\,d\mu_{0}(y)\Bigr)^{p}\leq\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\int\limits_{Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},25)}|f(y)|^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(y)\\
&\leq C\int\limits_{S}|f(y)|^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(y).\end{split}

(4.49)

Combining (
4.48
) and (
4.49
) we obtain (
4.41
).

Now we prove (
4.42
). Using (
3.5
), Lemma
3.2
, and simple inclusions
Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},3r)\supset Q(x,2r)\supset Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r)
which hold for every
x\in Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\varkappa})
and for every
r\in[r_{\varkappa},1]
, we obtain

\begin{split}&f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\kappa})\leq C\inf\limits_{x\in Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\varkappa})\cap S}f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,r_{\varkappa})+C\inf\limits_{x\in Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\varkappa})\cap S}\fint\limits_{Q(x,2)}|f(y)|\,d\mu_{0}(y)\\
&\leq C\inf\limits_{x\in Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\varkappa})\cap S}f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,r_{\varkappa})+C\int\limits_{Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},3)}|f(y)|\,d\mu_{0}(y).\end{split}

(4.50)

Using (
3.7
) with
\frac{1}{c}=1-\lambda
, (
3.5
) and Hölder inequality, we derive from (
4.50
) (we use also identity
\mu_{k(\varkappa)}(Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},(1-\lambda)r_{\varkappa})\cap S)=\mu_{k(\varkappa)}(Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},(1-\lambda)r_{\varkappa}))
because
\operatorname{supp}\mu_{k}\subset S
)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\kappa})\Bigr)^{p}=\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\frac{\mu_{k(\varkappa)}(Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},(1-\lambda)r_{\varkappa}))}{\mu_{k(\varkappa)}(Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},(1-\lambda)r_{\varkappa}))}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\kappa})\Bigr)^{p}\\
&\leq C(r_{\varkappa})^{n-d}\int\limits_{Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},(1-\lambda)r_{\varkappa})\cap S}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,r_{\varkappa})\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k(\varkappa)}(x)\\
&+C\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\int\limits_{Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},3)}|f(y)|^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(y).\end{split}

(4.51)

From Lemma
2.3
it follows that the multiplicity of overlapping of the cubes
Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\varkappa})
with the same side length is finite
and bounded above by a constant
C=C(n)
. Hence, it is easy to see that for every
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

the multiplicity of overlapping of the sets
Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},(1-\lambda)r_{\varkappa})\cap S
with
r_{\varkappa}=2^{-j}
is bounded above by the same constant
C(n)
.
Furthermore, from Lemma
3.3
it follows that
Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},(1-\lambda)r_{\varkappa})\cap S\subset S_{j}(\lambda)
for
r_{\varkappa}=2^{-j}
.
We combine these facts and apply for every fixed
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
Lemma
2.4
with
d\mathfrak{m}(x)=\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,r_{\varkappa})\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k(\varkappa)}(x)
.
This gives

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{r_{\varkappa}=2^{-j}}(r_{\varkappa})^{n-d}\int\limits_{Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},(1-\lambda)r_{\varkappa})\cap S}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,r_{\varkappa})\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k(\varkappa)}(x)\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}2^{j(d-n)}\int\limits_{S_{j}(\lambda)}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,2^{-j})\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{j}(x).\end{split}

Hence, we derive

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}(r_{\varkappa})^{n-d}\int\limits_{Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},(1-\lambda)r_{\varkappa})\cap S}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,r_{\varkappa})\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k(\varkappa)}(x)\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}2^{j(d-n)}\int\limits_{S_{j}(\lambda)}\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x,2^{-j})\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{j}(x).\end{split}

(4.52)

Using Lemma
2.3
with
d\mathfrak{m}(x)=|f(x)|^{p}d\mu_{0}(x)
and
c=3
, we obtain

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\int\limits_{Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},3)}|f(y)|^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(y)\leq C\int\limits_{S}|f(x)|^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(x).\end{split}

(4.53)

Combining (
4.51
), (
4.52
), (
4.53
), we conclude.

Now we are ready to prove the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 4.4.

Let
d\in[0,n]
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Let
S
be a closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d
. Assume that there exists a
d
-regular system of measures
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
on
S
. Then the functional
\mathcal{N}_{S,p,\lambda}
is bounded on the space
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
.

Proof.
Recall Definition
2.2
. It is sufficient to verify that there exists a (universal) constant
C>0

such that the inequality

\mathcal{N}_{S,p,\lambda}[f]\leq C\|F|W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\|

(4.54)

holds for every
F\in W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
F|_{S}=f
.

Step 1.
First of all we estimate
\mathcal{SN}_{S,p}[f]
from above. Fix some
q\in(\max\{1,n-d\},p)
and apply Corollary
4.3
. Using (2.3), the fact that
p>q
and Theorem
2.2
with the exponent
\frac{p}{q}
instead of
p
, we obtain

\mathcal{SN}_{S,p}[f]\leq C(p,q,d,n)\|F|L_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\|.

(4.55)

Step 2.
Now we are going to estimate the second term in the right-hand side of (
4.41
). Fix some
q\in(\max\{1,n-d\},p)
and apply Corollary
4.3
.
We also use Remark 2.3, Theorem
2.2
with the exponent
\frac{p}{q}
instead of
p
and the fact that interiors of Whitney cubes are mutually disjoint.
This gives the estimate

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\Bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},r_{\kappa})\Bigr)^{p}\leq C\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\Bigl(\operatorname{M}_{>r_{\varkappa}}[|\nabla F|^{q}](\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa})\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{q}}\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\inf\limits_{x\in Q_{\varkappa}}\Bigl(\operatorname{M}_{>r_{\varkappa}}[|\nabla F|^{q}](x)\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq C\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Bigl(\operatorname{M}_{>r_{\varkappa}}[|\nabla F|^{q}](x)\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(x)\\
&\leq C\|F|L_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\|^{p}.\end{split}

(4.56)

Step 3.
It remains to estimate
\|f|L_{p}(S,\mu_{0})\|
from above. Let
\{x_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
be a maximal
1
-separated subset of
S
. Consider the family of cubes
\{Q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}:=\{Q(x_{j},1)\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
. Using item (1) of Lemma
2.4
, we derive the estimate

\begin{split}&\int\limits_{S}|f(x)|^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(x)\\
&\leq\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\int\limits_{Q_{j}}\Bigl|f(x)-\fint\limits_{Q_{j}}F(y)\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\Bigr|^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(x)+\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\int\limits_{Q_{j}}\Bigl(\fint\limits_{Q_{j}}|F(y)|\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(x).\end{split}

(4.57)

Using Hölder inequality, estimate (
3.4
), item (3) of Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.4
with
d\mathfrak{m}(x)=|F(x)|^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(x)
, we easily obtain

\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\int\limits_{Q_{j}}\Bigl(\fint\limits_{Q_{j}}|F(y)|\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(x)\leq C\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\int\limits_{Q_{j}}|F(y)|^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\leq C\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|F(y)|^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y).

(4.58)

Fix
q\in(\max\{1,n-d\},p)
and
Q_{j}=Q(x_{j},1)
. It is clear that
Q_{j}\subset Q(x,3)
for every
x\in S\cap Q_{j}
. Recall Theorem
2.2
and Lemma
3.1
. This gives for
\mu_{0}
-a.e.
x\in S\cap Q_{j}
and for every sufficiently small
\delta\in(0,1)

\begin{split}&\Bigl|f(x)-\fint\limits_{Q_{j}}F(y)\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\Bigr|\leq\Bigl|\fint\limits_{Q(x,1)}F(y)\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)-\fint\limits_{Q_{j}}F(y^{\prime})\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y^{\prime})\Bigr|\\

Presently the authors do not know whether it is possible to obtain (

) from the condition
\mathcal{N}_{S,p,\lambda}[f]<\infty
.
This question is not as simple as it may seem at first. Indeed, from the proof of Theorem

it is clear that given a function

. Then, we obtain from Theorem

that for

-a.e. point

But is not obvious why

for
\mathcal{H}^{d}
-almost every

?

Remark 4.5.

As we noted above, while constructing the extension operator, we make a choice of a

-regular system of measures.
It is remarkable, however, that both the statement and proof of Theorem

5
Simplified criterion for sets with porous boundary

In this section we are going to prove Theorem
1.2
which is a simplified version of Theorem
1.1
in the case of sets with porous boundary.
Recall Definition
3.3
.

Let
S
be a closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with porous boundary. Given
\lambda>0
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
consider the sets

\begin{split}&\partial S^{+}_{k}(\lambda):=\{x\in\partial S|\operatorname{there}\operatorname{exists}y\in Q(x,2^{-k})\operatorname{for}\operatorname{which}\quad Q(y,\lambda 2^{-k})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S\}\\
&\partial S^{-}_{k}(\lambda):=\{x\in\partial S|\operatorname{there}\operatorname{exists}y^{\prime}\in Q(x,2^{-k})\operatorname{for}\operatorname{which}\quad Q(y^{\prime},\lambda 2^{-k})\subset S\setminus\partial S\}.\end{split}

(5.1)

From Definition
3.3
it is clear that if
\partial S
is porous then there exists a number
\lambda>0
such that

\partial S=\partial S^{+}_{k}(\lambda)\cup\partial S^{-}_{k}(\lambda)

(5.2)

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Definition 5.1.

Let
S
be a closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be an arbitrary Radon measure with
\operatorname{supp}\mathfrak{m}=S
. Let
Q=Q(x,r)
be a cube with
x\in S
and
r>0
. Given a function
f\in L_{1}^{\operatorname{loc}}(S,\mathfrak{m})
define

the normalized with respect to the measure
\mathfrak{m}
best approximation of
f
by constants on
Q

\mathcal{E}_{\mathfrak{m}}(f,Q):=\inf\limits_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\fint\limits_{Q\cap S}|f(y)-c|\,d\mathfrak{m}(y).

(5.3)

Remark 5.1.

It is easy to see that

\mathcal{E}_{\mathfrak{m}}(f,Q)\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{m}}(f,Q)\leq 2\mathcal{E}_{\mathfrak{m}}(f,Q),

(5.4)

where

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{m}}(f,Q):=\fint\limits_{Q\cap S}\Bigl|f(y)-\fint\limits_{Q\cap S}f(z)\,d\mathfrak{m}(z)\Bigr|\,d\mathfrak{m}(y)

The exact value of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathfrak{m}}(f,Q)
will not be important for us in the sequel. Hence, in practice we can work without loss of generality with

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathfrak{m}}(f,Q)
instead of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathfrak{m}}(f,Q)
which is easier to compute.

Definition 5.2.

Let
S
be an arbitrary closed nonempty subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
consider the set

\Sigma_{k}:=\Sigma_{k}(S):=\{x\in S|\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial S)\leq 2^{-k}\}.

(5.5)

Remark 5.2.

It is clear that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the set
\Sigma_{k}
is closed.

Lemma 5.1.

Let
d\in[0,n]
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
p>n-d
. Let
S
be a closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d
. Assume that there exists a
d
-regular system of measures
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
on
S
. Assume that
\partial S
is porous. Then for every
F\in W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{kp(1-\frac{n-d}{p})}\int\limits_{\Sigma_{k}}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k}}(F|_{S},Q(x,2^{-k}))\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\leq C\|F|W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\|.

(5.6)

The constant
C>0
in (
5.6
) does not depend on
F
.

Proof.
Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and consider an arbitrary maximal
2^{-k}
separated subset
\{x_{k,j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}
of
\Sigma_{k}
. We set
Q_{k,j}:=Q(x_{k,j},2^{-k})
for every
j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}
.

It is clear from the construction that for every
x\in\Sigma_{k}
there exists index
j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}
such that
3Q_{k,j}\supset Q(x,2^{-k})
. Using this and (
3.5
), (
3.6
), we get

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu_{k}}(F|_{S},Q(x,2^{-k}))\leq C\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu_{k}}(F|_{S},3Q_{k,j})

(5.7)

Using item (1) of Lemma
2.4
, (
3.4
), (
5.7
) and Remark
5
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\int\limits_{S}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k}}(F|_{S},Q(x,2^{-k}))\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\leq\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\int\limits_{Q_{k,j}}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k}}(F|_{S},Q(x,2^{-k}))\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\\
&\leq C2^{-kd}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k}}(F|_{S},3Q_{k,j}\Bigr)^{p}.\end{split}

(5.8)

Fix some
q\in(\max\{1,n-d\},p)
. Using (
4.10
) and (
5.8
), we derive the following estimate

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{kp(1-\frac{n-d}{p})}\int\limits_{S}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k}}(F|_{S},Q(x,2^{-k}))\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{kp(1-\frac{n}{p})}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k}}(F|_{S},3Q_{k,j})\Bigr)^{p}\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{k,j})\Bigl(\fint\limits_{3Q_{k,j}}|\nabla F(y)|^{q}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{q}}.\end{split}

(5.9)

Fix some
\lambda>0
such that (
5.2
) holds. Let
\mathcal{J}^{1}_{k}
be the set of all
j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}
for each of which
Q_{k,j}\cap\partial S^{+}_{k}(\lambda)\neq\emptyset
. Let
\mathcal{J}^{2}_{k}
be the set of all
j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}
for each of which
Q_{k,j}\cap\partial S^{-}_{k}(\lambda)\neq\emptyset
. It is clear that
\mathcal{J}_{k}=\mathcal{J}^{1}_{k}\cup\mathcal{J}^{2}_{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Fix a Whitney decomposition
W^{1}
of the set
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S
and a Whitney decomposition
W^{2}
of the set
\operatorname{int}S
. Let
\mathcal{I}^{1}
and
\mathcal{I}^{2}
be the sets of all indices corresponding to cubes with side length
\leq 1
from the family
W^{1}
and
W^{2}
respectively.

It is clear that for every
j\in\mathcal{J}^{1}_{k}
there is a point
x^{\prime}_{k,j}\in Q_{k,j}\cap\partial S^{+}_{k}(\lambda)
. Similarly for every
j\in\mathcal{J}^{2}_{k}
there is a point
x^{\prime}_{k,j}\in Q_{k,j}\cap\partial S^{-}_{k}(\lambda)
. Using Lemma
3.3
, we find for every
j\in\mathcal{J}^{1}_{k}
a point
y(x^{\prime}_{k,j})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S
such that for every cube
Q_{\varkappa}\ni y(x^{\prime}_{k,j})
,
\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}^{1}

\frac{\lambda}{5}2^{-k}\leq 2r_{\varkappa}\leq 2^{-k}.

(5.10)

Similarly, for every
j\in\mathcal{J}^{2}_{k}
we find a point
z(x^{\prime}_{k,j})\in\operatorname{int}S
such that for every cube
Q_{\varkappa^{\prime}}\ni z(x^{\prime}_{k,j})
,
\varkappa^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}^{2}

\frac{\lambda}{5}2^{-k}\leq 2r_{\varkappa^{\prime}}\leq 2^{-k}.

(5.11)

Consider the map
\Theta^{1}
which takes a pair
(k,j)
with
j\in\mathcal{J}^{1}_{k}
and gives back an arbitrary chosen
\varkappa=\Theta^{1}(k,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{1}

such that (
5.10
) holds. Similarly, we built the map
\Theta^{2}
which takes a pair
(k,j)
with
j\in\mathcal{J}^{2}_{k}
and gives back an arbitrary chosen
\varkappa=\Theta^{2}(k,j)\in\mathcal{I}^{2}
such that (
5.11
) holds.

Using item (3) of Lemma
2.4
, it is
easy to conclude that for every
\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}^{1}
Whitney cube
Q_{\varkappa}
has nonempty intersection with at most
C(n)
cubes from the family

\{Q_{k,j}\}_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}
. The similar fact clearly holds for every
\varkappa^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}^{2}
. Then, there exists a constant
C(n)
such that

\operatorname{card}\{(\Theta^{1})^{-1}(\varkappa)\}\leq C(n),\quad\operatorname{card}\{(\Theta^{2})^{-1}(\varkappa)\}\leq C(n).

(5.12)

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
j\in\mathcal{J}^{1}_{k}
. Let
\varkappa=\Theta^{1}(k,j)
. Then from (
5.10
) it follows that
7Q_{k,j}\supset Q_{\varkappa}
. Hence,
from (
2.4
) we derive

\begin{split}&\fint\limits_{3Q_{k,j}}|\nabla F(y)|\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\leq C\fint\limits_{7Q_{k,j}}|\nabla F(y)|\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\\
&\leq C\inf\limits_{x\in Q_{\varkappa}}\operatorname{M}_{>\frac{7}{2^{k}}}[|\nabla F|](x)\leq C\inf\limits_{x\in Q_{\varkappa}}\operatorname{M}_{>2^{-k}}[|\nabla F|](x).\end{split}

(5.13)

Similarly, if
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
j\in\mathcal{J}^{2}_{k}
and
\varkappa^{\prime}=\Theta^{2}(k,j)
, then

\fint\limits_{3Q_{k,j}}|\nabla F(y)|\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\leq C\inf\limits_{x\in Q_{\varkappa^{\prime}}}\operatorname{M}_{>2^{-k}}[|\nabla F|](x).

(5.14)

Combining (
5.10
), (
5.12
) and (
5.13
), we have

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}^{1}_{k}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{k,j})\Bigl(\fint\limits_{3Q_{k,j}}|\nabla F(y)|^{q}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{q}}\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}^{1}}\sum\limits_{(k,j)\in(\Theta^{1})^{-1}(\varkappa)}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{k,j})\inf\limits_{x\in Q_{\varkappa}}\Bigl(\operatorname{M}_{>2^{-k}}[|\nabla F|^{q}](x)\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{q}}\\
&\leq C\sum\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}^{1}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\inf\limits_{x\in Q_{\varkappa}}\Bigl(\operatorname{M}_{>2^{-k}}[|\nabla F|^{q}](x)\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq C\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus S}\Bigl(\operatorname{M}_{>2^{-k}}[|\nabla F|^{q}](x)\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(x).\end{split}

(5.15)

Similarly, from (
5.11
), (
5.12
) and (
5.14
) we obtain

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}^{2}_{k}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{k,j})\Bigl(\fint\limits_{3Q_{k,j}}|\nabla F(y)|^{q}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq C\int\limits_{\operatorname{int}S}\Bigl(\operatorname{M}_{>2^{-k}}[|\nabla F|^{q}](x)\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(x).\end{split}

(5.16)

Using Theorem
2.2
, and combining estimates (
5.9
), (
5.15
), (
5.16
), we derive

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{kp(1-\frac{n-d}{p})}\int\limits_{S}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k}}(F|_{S},Q(x,2^{-k}))\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\leq C\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Bigl(\operatorname{M}[|\nabla F|^{q}](x)\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(x)\\
&\leq C\|F|L^{1}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\|^{p}.\end{split}

(5.17)

The lemma is proved.

Lemma 5.2.

Let
d\in[0,n]
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
p>n-d
. Let
S
be a closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d
. Assume that there exists a

-regular system of measures

for every

. Then for every

,
k\geq 4

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}\\
r_{\varkappa}\leq 2^{-k}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{L}_{n}(Q_{\varkappa})\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k}}(f,Q(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},2^{-k}))\right)^{p}\\

(5.18)

\mathcal{I}_{k}:=\{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}|r_{\varkappa}\leq 2^{-k}\}

and Remark

, we find an index set
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{k}\subset\mathcal{I}_{k}

\bigcup\limits_{\varkappa\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}Q\bigl(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},\frac{1}{2^{k}}\bigr)\subset\bigcup\limits_{\varkappa\in\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{k}}Q\bigl(\widetilde{x}_{\varkappa},\frac{5}{2^{k}}\bigr).

(5.19)

Note that if

,
\varkappa\in\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{k}

5

Theorem 5.1.

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
d\in[0,n]
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
p>n-d
. Let
S
be a closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\operatorname{dim}_{H}S\geq d
. Assume that there exists a
d
-regular system of measures
\{\mu_{k}\}=\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
on
S
. Assume that
\partial S
is porous. Then
f
belongs to the
d
-trace space
W_{p}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{S}
if and only if there exists a set
S^{\prime}\subset S
with
\mathcal{H}^{d}(S\setminus S^{\prime})=0
such that

\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\fint\limits_{Q(x,r)\cap S}|f(x)-f(z)|\,d\mu_{k(r)}(z)=0,\quad\forall x\in S^{\prime},

and

\begin{split}&\mathcal{R}_{S,p}[f]:=\left(\int\limits_{S}|f(x)|^{p}\,d\mu_{0}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int\limits_{S}\bigl(f^{\sharp}_{\{\mu_{k}\}}(x)\bigr)^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&+\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{kp(1-\frac{n-d}{p})}\int\limits_{\Sigma_{k}}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{\mu_{k}}(f,Q(x,2^{-k}))\Bigr)^{p}\,d\mu_{k}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.\end{split}

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\infty}(Q(x,r)\cap G)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j}(r_{j})^{n-1}.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
B_{j}\cap Q(x,r)\cap G\neq\emptyset
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
there is a point
y_{j}\in B_{j}\cap G
. Then, it is clear that
B(y_{j},2r_{j})\supset B_{j}
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. This gives

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\infty}(Q(x,r)\cap G)\geq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum\limits_{j}(2r_{j})^{n-1}.

(7.7)

Let
\widehat{Q}
be the projection of the cube
Q
to the space
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|x_{n}=0\}
. For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
let
\widehat{x}_{j}
be the projection of the center of the ball
B_{j}
to the space
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Clearly
\widehat{Q}\cap\overline{\Omega}\subset\cup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}B(\widehat{x}_{j},2r_{j})
.

From the previous item we know that
\overline{\Omega}
is Ahlfors
(n-1)
-regular. Hence, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

Cr^{n-1}\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(Q(x,r)\cap G)\leq\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(B(\widehat{x}_{j},2r_{j})\cap\overline{\Omega})\leq\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(2r_{j})^{n-1}.

(7.8)

Combining (
7.7
) and (
7.8
) we conclude.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
. We follow the classical scheme of the proof of Theorem
2.2
(see section 1.5 of
[
40
]
). Fix
t>0
,
f\in L_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and define

f_{1}(x):=\begin{cases}&f(x),\quad|f(x)|\geq\frac{t}{2\min\{1,s^{\alpha}\}},\\
&0,\quad|f(x)|<\frac{t}{2\min\{1,s^{\alpha}\}}.\end{cases}

We put
f_{2}(x):=f(x)-f_{1}(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

It is clear that

|f(x)|\leq|f_{1}(x)|+\frac{t}{2\min\{1,s^{\alpha}\}}.

Hence, we derive

\operatorname{M}^{<s}[f,\alpha](x)\leq\operatorname{M}^{<s}[f_{1},\alpha](x)+\frac{t}{2}.

(7.9)

Consider the set

E_{t}(f):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\operatorname{M}^{<s}[f,\alpha](x)>t\}.

Using inequality (
7.9
) we get the inclusion

E_{t}(f)\subset E_{\frac{t}{2}}(f_{1}).

(7.10)

In order to estimate
\mathfrak{m}(E_{t}(f))
we should use more delicate arguments than that of
[
40
]
, because our measure
\mathfrak{m}
is not assumed to be doubling.
We are going to use Theorem
2.4
.

It is clear that for every
x\in E_{\frac{t}{2}}(f_{1})
there is a Euclidean ball
B_{x}=B(x,r_{x})
with radius
r_{x}<s
such that

r^{\alpha}\fint\limits_{B(x,r)}|f_{1}(y)|\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)>\frac{t}{2}.

Hence we have a covering
\mathcal{F}:=\{B_{x}\}
of the set
E_{\frac{t}{2}}(f_{1})
with radii bounded above by
s
. Using Besicovitch’s covering Theorem, we
obtain finite number of subfamilies of balls
\mathcal{G}_{1},...,\mathcal{G}_{N(n)}\subset\mathcal{F}
such that each
\mathcal{G}_{i}
(
i=1,...,N(n)
) is a countable collection of disjoint
balls in
\mathcal{F}
and

E_{\frac{t}{2}}(f_{1})\subset\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{N(n)}\bigcup\limits_{B\in\mathcal{G}_{i}}B.

Note that, using (
2.5
) and the condition
d\geq n-\alpha
, for every ball
B\in\mathcal{F}
we have the estimate

\mathfrak{m}(B)\leq Cr^{-\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{n}(B)\leq\frac{2C}{t}\int\limits_{B}|f_{1}(y)|\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y).

(7.11)

Using (
7.10
), (
7.11
) and the fact that balls in every family
\mathcal{G}_{i}
are disjoint, we have the key weak-type estimate

\begin{split}&\mathfrak{m}(E_{t}(f))\leq\mathfrak{m}(E_{\frac{t}{2}}(f_{1}))\leq\frac{2C}{t}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N(n)}\sum\limits_{B\in\mathcal{G}_{i}}\int\limits_{B}|f_{1}(y)|\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y)\\
&\leq N(n)\frac{2C}{t}\int\limits_{|f|\geq\frac{t}{2\min\{1,s^{\alpha}\}}}|f(y)|\,d\mathcal{L}_{n}(y).\end{split}

(7.12)

From (
7.12
) using standard arguments (see the end of the proof of the corresponding theorem in section 1.5 of the book
[
40
]
) we obtain

\|\operatorname{M}^{<s}[f,\alpha]|L_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathfrak{m})\|^{\gamma}=\gamma\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}t^{\gamma-1}\mathfrak{m}(E_{t}(f))\leq C(\gamma,n,d,\alpha)\|f|L_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathcal{L}_{n})\|^{\gamma}.

(7.13)

The theorem is proved.

Remark 7.1.
We would like to note that in the proof above we essentially used the fact that
s<+\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
. Idea of proof repeat that of Theorem 5.1.12 of
[
1
]
. Recall the definition of the mesh of diadic cubes
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
(see the beginning of section 2). Fix a nonnegative integer
k
and let
\mu^{k}
be a measure such that
\mu^{k}
has constant density and has mass equal to
2^{-kd}
on each
Q_{k,m}
that intersects
S
.

We now modify
\mu^{k}
in the following way. If
\mu^{k}(Q_{k-1,m})>2^{-(k-1)d}
for some
Q_{k-1,m}\in\mathcal{Q}_{k-1}
we reduce its mass uniformly on
Q_{k-1,m}

until it equals
2^{-(k-1)d}
. If on the other hand
\mu^{k}(Q_{k-1,m})\leq 2^{-(k-1)d}
, we leave
\mu^{k}
unchanged on
Q_{k-1,m}
. This way we obtain a new measure
\mu^{k,1}
.
Using the fact that every cube
Q_{k-1,m}
which has nonempty intersection with
S
contains
\leq 2^{n}
cubes
Q_{k,m^{\prime}}
with the property
Q_{k,m^{\prime}}\cap S\neq\emptyset
, we have

\mu^{k,0}(Q_{k,m})\leq\mu^{k,1}(Q_{k,m})\leq 2^{n-d}\mu^{k,0}(Q_{k,m})

We repeat this procedure with
\mu^{k,1}
, obtaining
\mu^{k,2}
, and after
k
such steps we have obtained
\mu^{k,k}
. It follows from the construction that

\mu^{k,k-j}(Q_{i,m})\leq h(2^{-id}).

(7.14)

for every
j=0,1,...,k
and every dyadic cube
Q_{i,m}\in\mathcal{Q}_{i}
with
i=j,...,k
. Furthermore, it is clear that

\mu^{k,j}(Q_{k,m})\leq\mu^{k,j+1}(Q_{k,m})\leq 2^{n-d}\mu^{k,j}(Q_{k,m}),\quad j=0,1,...,k-1.

(7.15)

Using (
7.14
), it is easy to see that the sequence
\{\mu^{k,k}(E)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is bounded for every compact set
E\subset S
. Then
\{\mu^{k,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
has a subsequence that converges weakly to
\mu_{0}
(see Theorem 2 in section 1.9 of
[
7
]
), and clearly
\operatorname{supp}\mu\subset S
.

Similarly for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
we consider the sequence
\{\mu^{k,k-j}\}_{k\geq j}
. This sequence has a subsequence
that converges weakly to
\mu_{j}
.

Fix an arbitrary
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Fix also an arbitrary Borel set
G\subset S
. Compare
\mu_{j}(G)
and
\mu_{j-1}(G)
. Firstly note, that according to our construction for every dyadic cube
Q_{k,m}
we have

\mu^{k,k-j+1}(Q_{k,m})\leq\mu^{k,k-j}(Q_{k,m})\leq 2^{n-d}\mu^{k,k-j+1}(Q_{k,m}),\quad k\geq j.

This gives for every
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,d\mu^{k,k-j+1}(x)\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,d\mu^{k,k-j}(x)\leq 2^{n-d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,d\mu^{k-j+1}(x),\quad k\geq j.

(7.16)

Fix an arbitrary
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Choosing, if required, an appropriate subsequence, and passing to the limit in (
7.16
), we obtain

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,d\mu_{j-1}(x)\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,d\mu_{j}(x)\leq 2^{n-d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,d\mu_{j-1}(x),\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

(7.17)

Using Borel regularity of measures
\mu_{j}
and taking into account estimate (
7.17
), we obtain (
3.3
).

Now we show that
\mu_{j}(Q_{i,m})\leq 3^{n}2^{-id}
for every
i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
i\geq j
and every dyadic cube
Q_{i,m}\in\mathcal{Q}_{i}
. Indeed, if
f_{i,m}
is a continuous function with
\operatorname{supp}f_{i,m}\subset Q_{i,m}
such that
\chi_{Q_{i,m}}\leq f_{i,m}\leq\chi_{3Q_{i,m}}
, then (
7.14
) gives

\mu_{j}(Q_{i,m})\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i,m}(x)\,d\mu_{j}(x)=\lim\limits_{l\to\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i,m}(x)\,d\mu^{k_{l},k_{l}-j}(x)\leq 3^{n}2^{-id}.

Hence, using the fact that every closed ball
B(x,r)
with
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
r\in(0,2^{-k}]
has nonempty intersection with
\leq 5^{n}
dyadic cubes
Q_{k(r),m}
(where
k(r)
is chosen such that
r\in[2^{-i(r)},2^{-i(r)+1})
), we obtain (
3.1
).

It remains to prove (
3.2
). Fix an arbitrary nonempty index set
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Consider the set
V=\cup_{m\in\mathcal{A}}Q_{k,m}
. Fix an arbitrary
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
l\geq k
and note that every
x\in V\cap S
belongs to some (or several)
Q^{(j)}\in\mathcal{Q}_{n_{j}}
,
k\leq n_{j}\leq l
such that

\mu^{l,l-k}(Q_{j,m})=2^{-jd}
. We obtain a disjoint covering,
S\cap V\subset\cup_{j}Q^{(j)}
, such that

\mu^{l,l-k}(V\cap S)=\sum\limits_{j}\mu^{l,l-k}(Q^{(j)})=\sum\limits_{j}2^{-n_{j}d}\geq\inf\sum\limits_{i}2^{-n_{i}d},

where the infimum is taken over all finite or denumerable coverings of
V\cap S
with
Q^{(i)}\in\cup_{l\geq k}\mathcal{Q}_{l}
. The right hand side is independent
of
l
, and letting
l\to\infty
it follows that

\mu_{k}(V\cap S)\geq\inf\sum\limits_{i}2^{-n_{i}d}.

(7.18)

To finish the proof it remains to replace cubes by balls. Suppose that
S\cap V\subset\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}Q^{(j)}
, where
Q^{(j)}\in\mathcal{Q}_{n_{j}}
and
n_{j}\geq k
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there is a constant
A_{n}
such that each
Q^{(j)}
is contained in the union of
\leq A_{n}
balls with radius
2^{-n_{j}}
. Thus
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(V\cap S)\leq A_{n}\inf\sum\limits_{j}2^{-n_{j}d}
, where the infimum is taken as above, and thus

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\infty}(V\cap S)\leq A_{n}\mu_{k}(V\cap S).

The proof of Theorem
3.1
is completed.

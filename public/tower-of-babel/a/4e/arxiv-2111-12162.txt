A Chern-Simons transgression formula for supersymmetric path integrals on spin manifolds
Sebastian Boldt, Sergio Luigi Cacciatori, and Batu Güneysu

Abstract
Earlier results show that the N = 1 / 2 N=1/2 supersymmetric path integral 𝔍 g \mathfrak{J}^{g} on a closed even dimensional Riemannian spin manifold ( X ​ ; ​ g ) (X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}g) can be constructed in a mathematically rigorous way via Chen differential forms and techniques from noncommutative geometry, if one considers 𝔍 g \mathfrak{J}^{g} as a current on the loop space L ​ X LX , that is, as a linear form on differential forms on L ​ X LX . This construction admits a Duistermaat-Heckman localization formula. In this note, fixing a topological spin structure on X X , we prove that any smooth family g ∙ = ( g t ) t ∈ [ 0 ​ ; ​ 1 ] g_{\bullet}=(g_{t})_{t\in[0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1]} of Riemannian metrics on X X canonically induces a Chern-Simons current ℭ g ∙ \mathfrak{C}^{g_{\bullet}} which fits into a transgression formula for the supersymmetric path integral. In particular, this result entails that the supersymmetric path integral induces a differential topological invariant on X X , which essentially stems from the A ^ \widehat{A} -genus of X X .

4.3
) is equal to

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}g^{\prime}_{t}\left(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{ds}Z\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\{\dot{\gamma}g^{\prime}_{t}(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Z)-\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{ds}(g^{\prime}_{t}(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\cdot\,))(Z)\right\}\\
=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}g^{\prime}_{t}\left(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{ds}Z\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{ds}(g^{\prime}_{t}(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\cdot\,))(Z)\\
=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\{g_{t}^{\prime}\left(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{ds}Z\right)-g^{\prime}_{t}\left(Z\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{ds}Y\right)\right\}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\nabla(t)_{\dot{\gamma}}g^{\prime}_{t})(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Z)\,.

For the third term on the right-hand side of (
4.3
), we use the well-known formula (see, e.g.,
[
16
, Proposition 2.3.1]
) for the time derivative of the Levi-Civita connection,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}g_{t}\left(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\tfrac{\nabla(t)^{\prime}}{ds}Z\right)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\{(\nabla(t)_{Z}g^{\prime}(t))(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\dot{\gamma})+(\nabla(t)_{\dot{\gamma}}g^{\prime}_{t})(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Z)-(\nabla(t)_{Y}g^{\prime}_{t})(Z\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\dot{\gamma})\right\}\,.

Putting the above together, we obtain

(d/dt)\left(-E^{g_{t}}_{\gamma}-\omega^{g_{t}}_{\gamma}\right)(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Z)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}g^{\prime}_{t}(\dot{\gamma}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\dot{\gamma})-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\{g^{\prime}_{t}\left(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{ds}Z\right)-g^{\prime}_{t}\left(Z\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{ds}Y\right)\right\}\\
+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\{(\nabla(t)_{Y}g^{\prime}_{t})(\dot{\gamma}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Z)-(\nabla(t)_{Z}g^{\prime}_{t})(\dot{\gamma}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Y)\right\}\,.

(4.4)

On the other hand, defining the 1-form
\beta^{g_{\bullet}}_{t}
on
LX
by

\displaystyle(\beta^{g_{\bullet}}_{t})_{\gamma}(Y)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}g^{\prime}_{t}(\dot{\gamma}\mathchar 59\relax Y)\,\mathchar 59\relax

its exterior derivative
d\beta^{g_{\bullet}}_{t}
is defined by the Cartan formula
[
12
, 33.12]
,

\displaystyle d(\beta^{g_{\bullet}}_{t})_{\gamma}(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Z)=Y\beta^{g_{\bullet}}_{t}(\widetilde{Z})-Z\beta^{g_{\bullet}}_{t}(\widetilde{Y})-\beta^{g_{\bullet}}_{t}([\widetilde{Y}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\widetilde{Z}])\,\mathchar 59\relax

(4.5)

where
\widetilde{Y}
and
\widetilde{Z}
are local extensions of
Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Z
, i.e., vector fields defined on a neighborhood of
\gamma\in LX
with
\widetilde{Y}_{\gamma}=Y
and
\widetilde{Z}_{\gamma}=Z
(this definition is independent of the extensions
\widetilde{Y}\mathchar 59\relax\widetilde{Z}
), and where we have used the usual identification of tangent vectors with the derivations they induce on the algebra of smooth functions on
LX
.

To compute the right hand side of (
4.5
), fix
\gamma\in LX
and let
\eta\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\xi:(-\varepsilon\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\varepsilon)\to LX
be smooth with
\eta(0)=\xi(0)=\gamma
and
\dot{\eta}(0)=Y
,
\dot{\xi}(0)=Z
. Then

Y\beta^{g_{\bullet}}_{t}(\widetilde{Z})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\tau}_{|\tau=0}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(g^{\prime}_{t})_{\eta(\tau)(s)}\left(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\eta(\tau)(s)\mathchar 59\relax\widetilde{Z}_{\eta(\tau)}(s)\right)ds\\
=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\{(\nabla(t)_{Y(s)}g_{t}^{\prime})(\dot{\gamma}(s)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Z(s))+g_{t}^{\prime}\left(\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{\partial\tau}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\eta(\tau)(s)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Z(s)\right)+g_{t}^{\prime}\left(\dot{\gamma}(s)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{d\tau}\widetilde{Z}_{\eta(\tau)}(s)\right)\right\}_{|\tau=0}\mkern-10.0muds\\
=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\{(\nabla(t)_{Y(s)}g_{t}^{\prime})(\dot{\gamma}(s)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Z(s))+g_{t}^{\prime}\left(\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{ds}Y(s)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Z(s)\right)+g_{t}^{\prime}\left(\dot{\gamma}(s)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{d\tau}\widetilde{Z}_{\eta(\tau)}(s)\right)\right\}_{|\tau=0}\mkern-10.0muds\,\mathchar 59\relax

where the last equality comes from the well-known identity

\displaystyle\frac{\nabla(t)}{\partial\tau}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\eta(\tau)(s)=\frac{\nabla(t)}{\partial s}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\eta(\tau)(s)\,.

Analogously, we have

Z\beta^{g_{\bullet}}\beta_{t}(\widetilde{Y})=\\
=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\{(\nabla(t)_{Z(s)}g_{t}^{\prime})(\dot{\gamma}(s)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Y(s))+g_{t}^{\prime}\left(\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{ds}Z(s)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Y(s)\right)+g_{t}^{\prime}\left(\dot{\gamma}(s)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{d\tau}\widetilde{Y}_{\xi(\tau)}(s)\right)\right\}_{|\tau=0}\mkern-10.0muds\,.

To calculate
[\widetilde{Y}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\widetilde{Z}]_{\gamma}(s)
, we use that the space of smooth vector fields on
LX
forms a Lie subalgebra of the space of bounded derivations
[
12
, Theorem 32.8]
. To this end, fix
s\in\mathbb{T}
, let
f\in C^{\infty}(X)
, denote by
\mathrm{ev}_{s}:LX\to X
the smooth evaluation map
\gamma\mapsto\gamma(s)
, and define
\widetilde{f}:=f\circ\mathrm{ev}_{s}\in C^{\infty}(LX)
. Then

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}_{\gamma}\widetilde{f}=d\widetilde{f}_{\gamma}\widetilde{Z}_{\gamma}=df_{\gamma(s)}d(\mathrm{ev}_{s})_{\gamma}\widetilde{Z}_{\gamma}=df_{\gamma(s)}\widetilde{Z}_{\gamma}(s)\,\mathchar 59\relax

so that

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}_{\gamma}(\widetilde{Z}\widetilde{f})=\frac{d}{d\tau}_{|\tau=0}df_{\eta(\tau)(s)}\widetilde{Z}_{\eta(\tau)}(s)=(\nabla(t)_{Y(s)}df)(Z(s))+df\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{d\tau}\widetilde{Z}_{\eta(\tau)}(s)\,\mathchar 59\relax

showing

\displaystyle[\widetilde{Y}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\widetilde{Z}]_{\gamma}(s)f=[\widetilde{Y}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\widetilde{Z}]_{\gamma}\widetilde{f}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hess}f(Y(s)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Z(s))+df\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{d\tau}\widetilde{Z}_{\eta(\tau)}(s)_{|\tau=0}

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Hess}f(Z(s)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Y(s))-df\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{d\tau}\widetilde{Y}_{\xi(\tau)}(s)_{|\tau=0}

\displaystyle=\left(\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{d\tau}\widetilde{Z}_{\eta(\tau)}(s)_{|\tau=0}-\tfrac{\nabla(t)}{d\tau}\widetilde{Y}_{\xi(\tau)}(s)_{|\tau=0}\right)f\,.

We have proved

\displaystyle d(\beta^{g_{\bullet}}_{t})_{\gamma}(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Z)=(d/dt)\left(-E^{g_{t}}_{\gamma}-\omega^{g_{t}}_{\gamma}\right)(Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}Z)+\iota\beta^{g_{\bullet}}_{t}\,.

Hence, for any differential form
\sigma
on
LX
we have

\displaystyle(d/dt)\mathfrak{I}^{g_{t}}[\sigma]=\int_{LX}e^{-E^{g_{t}}-\omega^{g_{t}}}\wedge(d-\iota)\beta^{g_{\bullet}}_{t}\wedge\sigma=\int_{LX}e^{-E^{g_{t}}-\omega^{g_{t}}}\wedge\beta^{g_{\bullet}}_{t}\wedge(d-\iota)\sigma\,\mathchar 59\relax

where the last equality follows from the fact that by definition one has

(d-\iota)\mathfrak{I}^{g_{t}}[\sigma]=\mathfrak{I}^{g_{t}}[(d-\iota)\sigma]=0.

Defining

\mathfrak{C}^{g_{\bullet}}_{t}(\sigma):=\int_{LX}\!e^{-E^{g_{t}}-\omega^{g_{t}}}\wedge\beta^{g_{\bullet}}_{t}\wedge\sigma\,\mathchar 59\relax

we end up with

(d/dt)\mathfrak{I}^{g_{t}}=(d-\iota)\mathfrak{C}^{g_{\bullet}}_{t}\mathchar 59\relax

formally proving (
1.5
).

A Class of few-Lee weight ℤ 2 ​ [ u ] \mathbb{Z}_{2}[u] -linear codes using simplicial complexes and minimal codes via Gray map
Pramod Kumar Kewat 1 and Nilay Kumar Mondal 1,∗

Abstract
. Recently some mixed alphabet rings are involved in constructing few-Lee weight additive codes with optimal or minimal Gray images using suitable defining sets or down-sets. Inspired by these works, we choose the mixed alphabet ring ℤ 2 ​ ℤ 2 ​ [ u ] \mathbb{Z}_{2}\mathbb{Z}_{2}[u] to construct a special class of linear code C L C_{L} over ℤ 2 ​ [ u ] \mathbb{Z}_{2}[u] with u 2 = 0 u^{2}=0 by employing simplicial complexes generated by a single maximal element. We show that C L C_{L} has few-Lee weights by determining the Lee weight distribution of C L C_{L} . Theoretically, this shows that we may employ simplicial complexes to obatin few-weight codes even in the case of mixed alphabet rings. We show that the Gray image of C L C_{L} is self-orthogonal and we have an infinite family of minimal codes over ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} via Gray map, which can be used to secret sharing schemes.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Rings and simplicial complexes

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
be the ring of integers modulo
2
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[u]=\mathbb{Z}_{2}+u\mathbb{Z}_{2},~u^{2}=0
. We define
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\mathbb{Z}_{2}[u]=\{(x,y+uz)|x,y,z\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\}
and
\mathcal{R}^{m}=\{(p,q+ur)|p,q,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}\}
, where
m
is any positive integer strictly greater than 1. Since
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is a subring of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[u]
, it is easy to observe that
\mathcal{R}^{m}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[u]
-module under component-wise addition and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[u]
-scalar multiplication defined as:
(p_{1},q_{1}+ur_{1})+(p_{2},q_{2}+ur_{2})=((p_{1}+p_{2}),(q_{1}+q_{2})+u(r_{1}+r_{2}))
and
(y+uz)(p,q+ur)=(yp,yq+u(yr+zq))
.

For any vector
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
, the support of
w
denoted by
supp(w)
is defined as the set of all nonzero coordinate positions of
w
. Clearly,
w\mapsto supp(w)
is a bijection between
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
and the power set of
[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}
i.e.,
2^{[m]}
. Let
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
such that if
w_{1}\in\Delta
and
supp(w_{2})\subseteq supp(w_{1})
imply
w_{2}\in\Delta
, then
\Delta
is called a simplicial complex. For a vector
w\in\Delta
, if there does not exist any vector
v\in\Delta
such that
supp(w)\subseteq supp(v)
, then we call
w
is a maximal element of
\Delta
. Clearly a simplical complex
\Delta
may admit more than one maximal elements. Here we consider the simplicial complexes with a single maximal element. The simplicial complex
\Delta_{supp(w)}
generated by a single maximal element
w
is defined to be the set of all subsets of
supp(w)\subseteq[m]
and is of size
2^{|supp(w)|}
, where
|\cdot|
denotes the size of a set.

2.2.
Lee weight, inner product and code

A Gray map
\phi:\mathbb{Z}_{2}[u]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
is defined as
y+uz\mapsto(z,y+z)
, where
y,z\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. We can naturally extend the map
\phi
to
\Phi:\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}+u\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2m}
as
\Phi(q+ur)=(r,q+r)
, where
q,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
. For a vector
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
, the Hamming weight of
w
denoted by
wt_{H}(w)
is defined as
wt_{H}(w)=|supp(w)|
. The Lee weight of a vector
w^{\prime}=(q+ur)\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}+u\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
denoted by
wt_{L}(a)
is defined as its Hamming weight under the Gray map
\Phi
, i.e.
wt_{L}(w^{\prime})=wt_{L}(q+ur)=wt_{H}(r)+wt_{H}(q+r)
. The Lee distance of
w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}+u\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
is defined as
wt_{L}(w^{\prime}_{1}-w^{\prime}_{2})
. It is quite easy to check that
\Phi
is an isometry between
(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}+u\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m},d_{L})
and
(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2m},d_{H})
.

A linear code
C
of length
m
over a finite commutative ring
R
is an
R
-submodule of
R^{m}
. The Euclidean inner product of vectors
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}),b=(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m})\in R^{m}
is
\langle a,b\rangle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}b_{i}\in R
. Let
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
and
w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}+u\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
. Now we define the inner product of two vectors
(w_{1},w^{\prime}_{1})
and
(w_{2},w^{\prime}_{2})
in
\mathcal{R}^{m}
as
((w_{1},w^{\prime}_{1})\cdot(w_{2},w^{\prime}_{2}))=u\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle+\langle w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{2}\rangle
.

Let
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
be three simplicial complexes generated by a single maximal elements having support
D,E,F\subseteq[m]
respectively and they are not equal to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}
. From now on we denote
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3}
as
\Delta_{D},\Delta_{E},\Delta_{F}
throughout this paper. We set
L=\{l=(t_{1},t_{2}+ut_{3})|t_{1}\in\Delta_{D}^{c},t_{2}\in\Delta_{E}^{c},t_{3}\in\Delta_{F}^{c}\}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{m}
, where
\Delta^{c}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{m}\setminus\Delta
. We define
C_{L}=\{c_{a}=((a\cdot l))_{l\in L}|a\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\}
. It is easy to check that
C_{L}
is a code over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[u]
of length
|L|
. The code
C_{L}
is said to be a
t
-Lee weight code if all of its codewords have only
t
different nonzero Lee weights.

4.
Gray images and Minimal codes

First of all we observe that: Since
\Phi
is a linear map and
C_{L}
is a linear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[u]
,
\Phi(C_{L})
is linear over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Next we will use some characterizations of binary linear codes regarding orthogonality and minimality.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
C_{L}
be the linear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[u]
as in Theorem
3.2
. Then the binary linear code
\phi(C_{L})
is self-orthogonal with respect to the Euclidean inner product if
|D|,|E|,|F|\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
0<|D|,|E|,|F|\leq m-1
. We know that if all the codewords of a binary linear codes is of weight
4k
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then the linear code is self-orthogonal (see (ii) of
[
10
, Theorem 1.4.8]
). Now,
(2^{m}-2^{|D|})\geq 2^{|D|}\geq 2
(as
m>1
and
0<|D|\leq m-1
). So, from Table
1
, it is easy to observe that 4 divides all the Lee weights. Hence
\phi(C_{L})
is self-orthogonal.
∎

Example 4.3
.

Let
|D|=|E|=|F|=n<m-1
. Then
\phi(C_{L})
has parameters
[2(2^{m}-2^{n})^{3},3m,(2^{m}-2^{n})(2^{m})(2^{m}-2^{n+1})]
. Moreover, if
n\neq 0
,
\phi(C_{L})
is self orthogonal.

We call a linear code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is minimal if all of its nonzero codewords are minimal. Further a codeword
c\in C
is said to be minimal if
supp(c^{\prime})\subseteq supp(c)
if and only if
c^{\prime}=ac
, where
a(\neq 0)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. In general, it is hard to determine whether a linear code is minimal or not but the famous Ashikhmin-Barg condition (
[
2
]
) is a sufficient condition for a linear code to be minimal and it is relatively easy to check.

Lemma 4.4
.

(Ashikhmin-Barg condition for p=2)
[
2
]

Let
C
be a linear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Denote
w_{0}
as the minimum(nonzero) and
w_{\infty}
as the maximum Hamming weights. If
\frac{w_{0}}{w_{\infty}}>\frac{1}{2}
, then
C
is minimal.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
C_{L}
be the linear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[u]
as in Theorem
3.2
with
|D|=|E|=|F|=n(<m)
. Then the code
\Phi(C_{L})
is minimal if
n\leq m-2
.

Proof.

Suppose
|D|=|E|=|F|=n(<m)
. Then
|L|=(2^{m}-2^{n})^{3}
and
(2^{m}-2^{n})\geq 2^{n},\forall m,n
. Now we divide this proof in two cases.

Case I
: Let
n<m-1
. Then we observe that
(2^{m}-2^{n})>2^{n}
. Now with these assumptions and using Table
1
, we have
w_{0}=|L|-[(2^{m}-2^{n})(2^{n})^{2}]=(2^{m}-2^{n})(2^{m})(2^{m}-2^{n+1})
and
w_{\infty}=|L|+[(2^{m}-2^{n})^{2}(2^{n})]=(2^{m}-2^{n})^{2}(2^{m})
. So,
\frac{w_{0}}{w_{\infty}}=\frac{2^{m}-2^{n+1}}{2^{m}-2^{n}}
(as
m\neq 0
and
n\neq m
). Now to satisfy Lemma
4.4
, it must be
2^{m+1}-2^{n+2}>2^{m}-2^{n}
i.e.
2^{m}>2^{n+2}-2^{n}
and this will be true if and only if
n+2\leq m
, which is also our assumption in this case.

Case II
: Let
n=m-1
. Then we observe that
|L|=(2^{n})^{3}
and
(2^{m}-2^{n})=2^{n}
. Now with these assumptions and using Table
2
, we have
w_{0}=|L|-\frac{1}{2}(2^{n})^{3}=\frac{1}{2}|L|
and
w_{\infty}=|L|+(2^{n})^{3}=2|L|
. So,
\frac{w_{0}}{w_{\infty}}=\frac{1}{4}
(as
n\neq 0
). Since Lemma
4.4
is only a sufficient condition, we cannot conclude anything in this case. Hence the theorem.
∎

5.
Conclusion

In
[
19
]
, the authors constructed few-Lee weight additive codes over the mixed alphabet
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[u],u^{2}=0
with optimal Gray images by using a suitable defining set. In
[
20
]
, the authors used the mixed alphabet ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[u]
with
u^{2}=u
and
p>2
to study some few-Lee weight additive codes by employing down-sets. In this paper, we use the mixed alphabet ring
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\mathbb{Z}_{2}[u]
to construct a class of few-Lee weight linear codes using simplicial complexes generated by a single element and also have an infinite family of self-orthogonal binary minimal codes over as Gray images. This shows that like in the case of single alphabet rings, we may employ all three popular means of constructing few-Lee weight codes including simplicial complexes when mixed alphabet rings are involved and hence fully answer the question raised in
[
20
]
about the use of simplicial complexes or down-sets in the case of mixed alphabet rings.

Abstract
. Let ℱ \mathcal{F} be a Riemann surface foliation on M ∖ E M\setminus E , where M M is a complex manifold and E ⊂ M E\subset M is a closed set. Assume that ℱ \cal{F} is hyperbolic, i.e., all leaves of the foliation ℱ \cal{F} are hyperbolic Riemann surface. Fix a hermitian metric g g on M M . We will consider the Verjovsky’s modulus of uniformization map η \eta , which measures the largest possible derivative in the class of holomorphic maps from the unit disk into the leaves of ℱ \cal{F} . Various results are known to ensure the continuity of the map η \eta along the transverse directions, with suitable conditions on M M , ℱ \cal{F} and E E . For a domain U ⊂ M U\subset M , let ℱ 𝒰 \cal{F}_{U} be the holomorphic foliation given by the restriction of ℱ \cal{F} to the domain U U , i.e., ℱ | 𝒰 \cal{F}|_{U} . We will consider the modulus of uniformization map η U \eta_{U} corresponding to the foliation ℱ 𝒰 \cal{F}_{U} , and study its variation when the corresponding domain U U varies in the Caratheodory kernel sense, motivated by the work of Lins Neto–Martins.

1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a complex manifold of dimension
N
, and
E\subset M
a closed subset of codimension atleast 2. Recall that a singular holomorphic foliation by Riemann surfaces
\cal{F}
with singular set
E
is defined by an atlas of flow-boxes
(U_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha})
where
\phi_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}^{N-1}
(
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
is the open unit disc) and the transition maps

\phi_{\alpha\beta}
are holomorphic in
(x,y)\in\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}^{N-1}
and are of the form

\phi_{\alpha\beta}=\phi_{\alpha\beta}(x,y)=\phi_{\alpha}\circ\phi^{-1}_{\beta}(x,y)=(P(x,y),Q(y)).

The leaves of
\mathcal{F}
are Riemann surfaces, which are locally given by
\phi^{-1}_{\alpha}(\{y=\mbox{constant}\})
. A leaf passing through a given
p\in M\setminus E
will be denoted by
L_{p}
. A hyperbolic Riemann surface foliation
(M,E,\mathcal{F})
is a singular holomorphic foliation in which every leaf
L_{p}
of
\cal{F}
is a hyperbolic Riemann surface.
Fix a hermitian metric
g
on
M
and denote the length of a tangent vector
v
by
|v|_{g}
. Consider the space
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},M\setminus E)
, which is the set of all holomorphic maps from the unit disc
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
into the complex manifold
M\setminus E
. Now consider the subset
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},\cal{F})\subset\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},M\setminus E)
, the set of holomorphic maps
f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},M\setminus E)
with values in a leaf of the foliation
\cal{F}
, that is
f(\mathbb{D})\subset L
, for some leaf
L
of
\cal{F}
. Consider the map
\eta:M\setminus E\rightarrow(0,\infty)
given by

\eta(z):=\sup\left\{|f^{\prime}(0)|_{g}:f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F}),f(0)=z\right\}.

One can check that
\eta>0
, and that it achieves its extremal value with the map
\pi_{z}
, where
\pi_{z}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow L_{z}
is the universal covering map chosen such that
\pi_{z}(0)=z
. Thus,
\eta(z)=|\pi^{\prime}_{z}(0)|_{g}
. For a leaf
L\subset\cal{F}
and a vector
v
tangent to
L
at
z
, let
\lambda_{L}
denote the Poincaré metric on
L
and
|v|_{L}
denote the Poincaré length of
v
. The extremal property of the Kobayashi metric shows that
|v|_{g}=\eta(z)|v|_{L}
. Symbolically, this will be written as
g=\eta\cdot\lambda_{L}
, and it can be understood that the restriction of
g/\eta
to a leaf
L
induces the corresponding Poincaré metric
\lambda_{L}
on it.

Since the Poincaré metric
\lambda_{L}
for a leaf
L
is a positive real-analytic function on
L
and
g
is smooth on
M
, therefore
\eta
is also smooth along leaves. However, the regularity of
\eta
along transverse directions is not a priori clear. In fact, the regularity of
\eta
is related to the variation of
\lambda_{L}
along such directions. This question of the regularity of
\eta
corresponding to a hyperbolic singular foliation
\cal{F}
has been studied under various hypotheses on
M,E
and

\mathcal{F}
. One can have a look at Verjovsky
[
18
]
, Lins Neto
[
13
,
14
]
, Candel
[
4
]
in which the continuity of
\eta
on
M\setminus E
is established. Recently, Dinh–Nguyên–Sibony
[
7
,
8
]
gave a stronger estimate on the modulus of continuity
of
\eta
under suitable hypotheses on
M,E
and
\mathcal{F}
. Related work on this theme can be found in
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
. To see few ramifications of studying the transverse regularity of the leafwise Poincaré metric, one can look at
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
, where Nguyen highlights its utility in studying the ergodic properties of Riemann surface laminations.

In
[
15
]
, the variation of
\eta
with respect to its domain of definition was studied. To describe it, let
U\subset M
be an open subset, denote the restriction of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
to
U
, that is
\cal{F}|_{U}
by
\mathcal{F}_{U}
. Let
\eta_{U}:U\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be the positive function corresponding to the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{U}
. Note that
\eta_{U}
is defined using holomorphic maps from
\mathbb{D}
into
L_{p,U}
as
p
varies in
U
. The following facts were established in
[
15
]
by Lins Neto–Martins. First, that
\eta_{U}
is a monotone function of
U
, i.e., for domains
U_{1}\subset U_{2}
,
\eta_{U_{1}}(p)\leq\eta_{U_{2}}(p)
for all
p\in U_{1}
. Also, if
\{W_{n}\}
is an increasing exhaustion of
W
and the singular set of foliation
\cal{F}
is discrete, then
\eta_{W_{n}}\rightarrow\eta_{W}
pointwise on
W\setminus E
. The convergence is uniform on compact subsets of
W\setminus E
if the functions
\eta_{W_{n}}
and

\eta_{W}
are continuous. Similar observations about the pointwise convergence of
\eta_{W_{n}}
were made by the author (with co-author Kaushal Verma) in
[
11
]
, where the singular set
E
is assumed to be discrete,
W
be a bounded taut domain and
W_{n}
converges to
W
in a Hausdorff sense. The uniform convergence of
\eta_{W_{n}}
to
\eta_{W}
on the compacts of
W\setminus E
was also established in
[
11
]
, with the additional hypotheses that
\eta_{W}
is continuous on
W\setminus E
and that there exist another bounded taut domain
V\subset M
such that
\overline{W}\subset V
. In
[
12
]
, these observations were proved to be true for the case when
E
is not discrete. It was also proved that if the foliation
\cal{F}
is transversal type (see
[
12
]
for the definition), then the uniform convergence will be on the compacts of
W
instead of
W\setminus E
.

The aim of this note is to study the variation of the function
\eta_{W_{n}}
of the foliation
\cal{F}_{W_{n}}
when the domains
W_{n}
converges to the domain
W
in the kernel sense (see below for definitions). We will observe that outside a
defective
set, the convergence results still holds true. We will also see that these results are a generalization to the similar observations made in
[
11
,
12
]
for the case when domain
U
varies in Hausdorff sense, and in
[
15
]
where
U
varies in monotonic increasing fashion. To keep things simple, unless otherwise mentioned we will fix
M=U\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
, where
U
is a connected open subset with the metric
g
to be the Euclidean metric throughout the article. Consider
W_{n},W\subset U\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be connected open subsets with
0\in W_{n},W
for all
n
. Let
\cal{F}
a hyperbolic singular holomorphic foliation on
U
with singular set
E\subset U
. Throughout the article, we will denote the foliation
\cal{F}_{W_{n}}
by
\cal{F}_{n}
, the function
\eta_{W_{n}}
by
\eta_{n}
, and the leaf
L
of
\cal{F}
on
U
passing through
p\in U
by
L_{p}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
W_{n}\subset U
be a sequence of connected open subsets of
U
such that
0\in W_{n}
for all
n
. Let
\tilde{V}_{n}:=\cap_{k\geq n}W_{k}
and
V_{n}
be the connected component of the interior of
\tilde{V}_{n}
containing
0\in U
, that is
V_{n}=(\text{int}(\tilde{V}_{n}))_{0}
. The
kernel
of the sequence
\{W_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, denoted by
W
is defined to be the
\cup_{n\geq 1}V_{n}
if it is non-empty, otherwise we say it is
\{0\}
.

We will say that
W_{n}
converges to
W
, if every subsequence of
\{W_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has the same kernel
W
.

Now we can define the kernel convergence of a sequence of domains.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
W_{n}\subset U\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a sequence of connected open subsets of
U
such that
0\in W_{n}
for all
n
, and
W\subset U
be another connected open set with
0\in W
. We say that
W_{n}
converges to
W
in the kernel sense if
W
is the kernel of the sequence
\{W_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
W_{n}
converges to its kernel.

Note:
We can check that if
W_{n}\to W
in the kernel sense, then for any compact set
K\subset W
, there exist
N_{K}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
K\subset W_{n}
for all
n\geq N_{K}
.

The following example shows that even the pointwise convergence of
\eta_{n}
to
\eta_{W}
is not guaranteed on the whole set
W\setminus E
, when
W_{n}\to W
in the kernel sense.

Example 1.3
.

Let
U=P(0,(5,5))\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
be the polydisc of polyradius
(r_{1},r_{2})=(5,5)
and centre
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Take
W_{n}=P(0,(1,1))\cup P(0,(2,\frac{1}{n}))
and
W=P(0,(1,1))
. Now consider the vector field
X(z)=z
on
U
and let
\cal{F},\cal{F}_{n},\cal{F}_{W}
be the foliation induced by
X
on the set
U,W_{n},W
respectively. Here, the singular set of the foliation is the singleton set
E=\{0\}
. Since
U
is bounded, the foliations
\cal{F},\cal{F}_{n},\cal{F}_{W}
are all hyperbolic.

Observe that
P(0,(2,\frac{1}{n}))\searrow D(0,2)\times\{0\}
as
n\to+\infty
, that is

\cap_{k\geq n}P(0,(2,\frac{1}{k}))=D(0,2)\times\{0\}.

This tells us that the set
\tilde{V}_{n}=\cap_{k\geq n}W_{n}
satisfies
\text{int}(\tilde{V}_{n})=P(0,(1,1))=W
. Therefore, the kernel of the sequence
W_{n}
is
W
. One can easily check that this is true for all subsequences of
\{W_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Thus,
W_{n}\to W
in the kernel sense.

Now take
p=(\frac{1}{2},0)\in U
, and observe that
p\in W_{n},W
for all
n\geq 1
. Denote the leaf of foliation
\cal{F}
(or
\cal{F}_{n},\cal{F}_{W}
) passing through point
p
by
L_{p}
(or
L_{p,n},L_{p,W}
respectively). One can easily check that
L_{p,n}=D(0,2)\setminus\{0\}\times\{0\}\cong D(0,2)\setminus\{0\}
, and
L_{p,W}=D(0,1)\setminus\{0\}\times\{0\}\cong\mathbb{D}^{*}
. Since the Poincaré metric of the punctured disc
\mathbb{D}^{*}
is given by

ds^{2}=\frac{4}{|q|^{2}(\log{|q|^{2})^{2}}}|dq|^{2},

and on
D(0,2)\setminus\{0\}
by

ds^{2}=\frac{4}{|q|^{2}(\log{\frac{|q|^{2}}{4})^{2}}}|dq|^{2}.

Therefore

\eta_{n}^{2}(p)=\frac{(\frac{1}{2})^{2}(\log{16})^{2}}{4},\;\eta_{W}^{2}(p)=\frac{(\frac{1}{2})^{2}(\log{4})^{2}}{4}.

Thus,
\eta_{n}(p)
doesn’t converge to
\eta_{W}(p)
, although
W_{n}\to W
in the kernel sense.

But all is not lost though. We will see that the pointwise convergence will be true away from a subset which we will call as
defective set
.

Definition 1.4
.

Consider
(W_{n},W,U,\cal{F})
be as in the above discussion. Now for a subsequence
\sigma=\{n_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of the sequence
\{n\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, consider

F_{\sigma}:=\{p\in U\setminus\overline{W}:\exists\{p_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset U\ \text{such that}\ p_{n_{k}}\in W_{n_{k}}\setminus\overline{W}\ \forall\ k,\ \text{and}\ p_{n_{k}}\to p\}.

Let
F:=\cup_{\sigma}F_{\sigma}
, where
\sigma
varies over all possible subsequence. The set
S:=\cup_{q\in F}(L_{q}\cap W)\subset W
will be called as the
defective set
corresponding to the foliation
\cal{F}
.

Note that
S\subset W
is a
\cal{F}_{W}-
invariant set since it is union of leaves in
W
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(W_{n},W,U,\cal{F})
be as above with the additional hypothesis that
W
is a bounded taut domain. If
W_{n}\to W
in the kernel sense, then
\eta_{n}(p)\to\eta_{W}(p)
for all
p\in W\setminus(S\cup E)
.

The following proposition will be used in proving the above theorem.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
(W_{n},W,U,\cal{F})
be as above. If
W
is the kernel of the sequence
\{W_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, then

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}{\eta_{n}(p)}\geq\eta_{W}(p),

for all
p\in W\setminus E
.

Proof.

Let
p\in W\setminus E
, and
\alpha:\mathbb{D}\to L_{p,W}
be the uniformization of the leaf
L_{p,W}
of the foliation
\cal{F}_{W}
. For
\epsilon>0
, consider the map
\psi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
given by
\psi(z)=(1-\epsilon)z
. Now consider the map
\alpha\circ\psi:\mathbb{D}\to L_{p,W}
and observe that
\alpha\circ\psi(\mathbb{D})
is relatively compact in
W
. From the definition of kernel, we know
W=\cup_{n\geq 1}V_{n}
, where
V_{n}
satisfies
V_{k}\subset V_{k+1}
for all
k
. This tells us that there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha\circ\psi(\mathbb{D})\subset V_{N}
, which in turn gives us that
\alpha\circ\psi(\mathbb{D})\subset W_{k}
for all
k\geq N
. Thus
\alpha\circ\psi(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{p}\cap W_{k}
and
p\in\alpha\circ\psi(\mathbb{D})
. Since
L_{p,k}
is the connected component of
L_{p}\cap W_{k}
containing
p
, therefore
\alpha\circ\psi(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{p,k}
for all
k\geq N
. By definition of the map
\eta_{k}
, we get for
k\geq N

\eta_{k}(p)\geq|(\alpha\circ\psi)^{\prime}(0)|=(1-\epsilon)\eta_{W}(p).

This gives us that
\liminf_{n\to+\infty}{\eta_{n}(p)}\geq\eta_{W}(p)
for all
p\in W\setminus E
.
∎

Now we shift our focus on the uniform convergence of
\eta_{n}
. First note that we can extend the map
\eta
on the singular set
E
by defining
\eta(p)=0
for all
p\in E
. Before we state our next result, lets quickly recall what we mean by a transversal type foliation. For a singular point
p\in E
of a holomorphic foliation
\cal{F}
on a complex manifold
M
, we consider the cone
C_{p}(\cal{F})\subset T_{p}{M}
which is the collection of all limit points of the vectors coming from the tangent line bundle of the foliation
T\cal{F}=\cup_{q\in M\setminus E}T_{q}{\cal{F}}
on
M\setminus E
. Also, since
E\subset M
is an analytic subset, we will denote the Whitney’s
C_{4}
-tangent cone (see
[
5
]
) at
p\in E
by
C_{p}E
. We say that
\cal{F}
is transversal type at
p\in E
, if for some neighourhood
U_{p}\subset M
of
p
,
\overline{C_{q}{\cal{F}}}\cap C_{q}{E}=\{0\}
holds for all
q\in E\cap U_{p}
. The foliation
\cal{F}
is transversal type if it is transversal type at each
p\in E
. For a detailed definition, look at
[
12
]
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(W_{n},W,U,\cal{F})
be as before with the additional hypothesis that
W,U
are bounded taut domains. If
W_{n}\to W
in the kernel sense and
\eta_{W}
is continuous on
W\setminus E
, then
\eta_{n}\to\eta_{W}
uniformly on the compacts of
W\setminus(S\cup E)
. If in addition,
\cal{F}
is transversal type, then
\eta_{n}\rightarrow\eta_{W}
uniformly on the compacts of
W\setminus S
.

It should be noted that not all points in the defective set
S
of a foliation
\cal{F}
are
`
bad’. Given
(W_{n},W,U,\cal{F})
, we will say that a point
p\in S
is a
removable defective point
if
\eta_{n}(p)\to\eta_{W}(p)
. We will denote the set of all such points by
\hat{S}
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Suppose
W_{n},W\subset U
be bounded connected open sets such that
0\in W_{n},W
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
W
is taut, and
W_{n}\to W
in the kernel sense. Let
\cal{F}
be a singular hyperbolic Riemann surface foliation on
U
, and
S\subset W
be its defective set. If
p\in F
satisfies

\text{int}(L_{p}\cap F)=\emptyset,

where
\text{int}(L_{p}\cap F)
denotes the interior of the set
L_{p}\cap F
when considered as a subset of the Riemann surface
L_{p}
, then

(1)

\eta_{n}(q)\to\eta_{W}(q)
for all
q\in L_{p}\cap W
, that is
L_{p}\cap W\subset\hat{S}
,

(2)

given additional hypothesis of
U
being taut, and
\eta_{W}
being continuous on
W\setminus E
, the uniform convergence
\eta_{n}\to\eta_{W}
will be true on the compacts of
(W\setminus(S\cup E))\cup(L_{p}\cap W)
.

What is left now is to show that the results proved in
[
11
]
(Theorem 1.1) and
[
12
]
(Theorem 2.1) are special cases of Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.7
. To do that lets first recall the type of domain convergence considered in
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
. Let
(M,g)
be a complex hermitian manifold and let
d
be the distance on
M
induced by
g
. For

S\subset M
and
\epsilon>0
, let
S_{\epsilon}
be the
\epsilon
-thickening of
S
with distances being measured using
d
. Recall that the Hausdorff distance
\mathcal{H}(A,B)
between compact sets
A,B\subset M
is the infimum of all
\epsilon>0
such that
A\subset B_{\epsilon}
and
B\subset A_{\epsilon}
. For bounded domains
U,V\subset M
, the prescription
\rho(U,V)=\mathcal{H}(\overline{U},\overline{V})+\mathcal{H}(\partial U,\partial V)
defines a metric (see
[
1
]
) on the collection of all bounded open subsets of
X
with the property that if
\rho(U,U_{n})\rightarrow 0
, then every compact subset of
U
is eventually contained in
U_{n}
, and every neighbourhood of
\overline{U}
contains all the
U_{n}
’s eventually. We will say that
U_{n}\rightarrow U
in the Hausdorff sense if
\rho(U_{n},U)\to 0
.

In the following result
M=\mathbb{C}^{N}
, and
g
is the Euclidean metric.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
W_{n},W
be bounded connected domains in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
such that
0\in W_{n},W
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
W_{n}\to W
in the Hausdorff sense, then
W=\text{ker}(W_{n})
and
W_{n}\to W
in the kernel sense with
F=\emptyset
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

Having Proposition 1.5 at our disposal, it suffices to prove that for
p\in W\setminus(S\cup E)
and for some convergent subsequence
\{\eta_{n_{k}}(p)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, it satisfies

\lim_{k\to+\infty}{\eta_{n_{k}}(p)}\leq\eta_{W}(p).

Fix
p\in W\setminus(S\cup E)
. Since
\eta_{n}(p)\leq\eta(p)<+\infty
(here
\eta
corresponds to the set
U
), therefore we can choose a subsequence
\{\eta_{n_{k}}(p)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
which converges to
M
. Consider the corresponding uniformizers
\alpha_{n_{k}}:\mathbb{D}\to L_{p,n_{k}}
with
\alpha_{n_{k}}(0)=p
. Since
\alpha_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{p,n_{k}}\subset L_{p}
,
\alpha_{n_{k}}(0)=p
and
L_{p}
being hyperbolic, upto a further subsequence we can suppose that
\alpha_{n_{k}}
converges to some holomorphic map
\alpha:\mathbb{D}\to L_{p}
, with
\alpha(0)=p
.

Now we just need to show that
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{p,W}
. Suppose there exist a point
q\in\alpha(\mathbb{D})
such that
q\in U\setminus\overline{W}
. Let
\alpha(z_{0})=q
. Choose a neighourhood
U_{q}\subset U
of
q
such that
\overline{U}_{q}\cap\overline{W}=\emptyset
. Take
q_{n_{k}}:=\alpha_{n_{k}}(z_{0})
, since
\alpha_{n_{k}}\to\alpha
, we have
q_{n_{k}}\to q
. Therefore there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
q_{n_{k}}\in U_{q}
for all
k\geq N
.
Also
q_{n_{k}}\in\alpha_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{D})=L_{p,n_{k}}\subset W_{n_{k}}
for all
k
. Therefore for
k\geq N

q_{n_{k}}\in W_{n_{k}}\setminus\overline{W},

and
q_{n_{k}}\to q
. Thus
q\in F_{\sigma}\subset F
, and since
q\in L_{p}
, therefore
p\in S
, which is a contradiction since
p\in W\setminus(S\cup E)
.

Hence
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset\overline{W}
, but
W
is taut so
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset W
. Since
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{p}
is connected, therefore
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{p,W}
. Now
|\alpha^{\prime}(0)|=\lim_{k\to+\infty}{|\alpha_{n_{k}}^{\prime}(0)|}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}{\eta_{n_{k}}(p)}
, and by definition of
\eta_{W}
,

\eta_{W}(p)\geq|\alpha^{\prime}(0)|.

So we have our result.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.7

Suppose that there is a compact set
K\subset W\setminus(S\cup E)
and a sequence
\{p_{n}\}\in K
such that

|\eta_{n}(p_{n})-\eta_{W}(p_{n})|>\epsilon

for some
\epsilon>0
.
Assume that
p_{n}\rightarrow p\in W\setminus(S\cup E)
after passing to a subsequence.

By the continuity of

|\eta_{W}(p_{n})-\eta_{W}(p)|<\epsilon/2

(3.1)

|\eta_{n}(p_{n})-\eta_{W}(p)|>\epsilon/2

for large

. Let
\alpha_{n}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow L_{p_{n},W_{n}}\subset W_{n}
be a uniformization map with
\alpha_{n}(0)=p_{n}
. Since

for all

and

is taut, hence the family
\{\alpha_{n}\}
is normal. By passing to a subsequence, let
\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\overline{U}
be a holomorphic limit with
\tilde{\alpha}(0)=p

\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow L_{p,W}
is a uniformization. Indeed, if this were the case, then
\eta_{W}(p)=|\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}(0)|
which would then imply that

\eta_{n}(p_{n})=|\alpha^{\prime}_{n}(0)|\rightarrow|\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}(0)|=\eta_{W}(p)

and this contradicts (
3.1

\tilde{\alpha}
uniformizes the leaf

(a)
\tilde{\alpha}
satisfies
\tilde{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\subset W
.

(b)
\tilde{\alpha}
satisfies
\tilde{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{p,W}\cup E

(c) The map
\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{D}\setminus\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)\rightarrow L_{p,W}

(d)

\tilde{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{p,W}

(a) Since

\tilde{\alpha}(0)=p\in U

. Now suppose that

, therefore

which is a contradiction to the fact that

\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\overline{W}

\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow W
.

(b) To see that
\tilde{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{p,W}\cup E
, we will use the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a hyperbolic SHFC on a complex manifold
M
, with singular set

. If
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},\cal{F})

\mathbb{D}
to some

, then
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset L\cup E
, where either
L=\emptyset
or
L
is a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
. In particular, if
L=\emptyset
,
q\in\alpha(\mathbb{D})\cap E
, and
\mathcal{F}
is transversal type at
q\in E
, and then
\alpha\equiv q\in E
.

Proof.

If
\alpha
is constant or
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset E
, then we are done.

Now suppose
\alpha
is non-constant, and
p\in\alpha(\mathbb{D})\cap(M\setminus E)

N:=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\alpha(z)\notin E\}\neq\emptyset
. Define for

, the set
N_{w_{0}}=\{z\in N:L_{z}=L_{w_{0}}\}
. To see that
N_{w_{0}}
is open in
\mathbb{D}
, pick
w_{1}\in N_{w_{0}}
and a chart
\phi=(x,y):\tilde{W}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-1}
around
\tilde{\alpha}(w_{1})=q
such that
(x,y)(q)=0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and the leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{\tilde{W}}
are described by
y=\mbox{constant}
. Let
V
be the connected component of
\alpha^{-1}(\tilde{W})\subset\mathbb{D}
containing
w_{1}
. Now, the claim is that
y(\alpha(V))=0
. If this is not the case, then
y(\alpha(V))\not=\{0\}
. Since
y(\alpha(w_{1}))=0

y\circ\alpha:V\rightarrow\mathbb{D}^{n-1}
is a non-constant map which implies that its derivative

for some
w_{2}\in V
near
w_{1}
. Since
\alpha_{n}
converges to
\alpha
,
d(y\circ\alpha_{n})(w_{2})\not=0
for
n
large. This means that
\alpha_{n}(\mathbb{D})
is not contained in a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
. This contradiction shows that
y(\alpha(V))=0
. Hence every
w\in V
has the property that the leaf of
\mathcal{F}_{\tilde{W}}
containing
\alpha(w)
coincides with
y=0
which is also the leaf passing through
w_{1}
. This shows that
N_{w_{0}}
is open.

Now let
F=\alpha^{-1}(E)
. It suffices to show that if
F
is non-empty, then
F
is a discrete set. Suppose
F
is not discrete, and
z\in D
is a limit point of
F
. Since
F
is closed,
z\in F
. Consider the point
\alpha(z)=p\in E

E
is an analytic subset of
M
, let
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{m}
be the local defining functions for
E
near the point
p
, i.e., there exist a neighourhood
V\subset M
of
p
such that
V\cap E=\{f_{1}=f_{2}=\ldots=f_{m}=0\}
. Let
r>0
be such that
\alpha(D(z,r))\subset V
. Consider the holomorphic functions
f_{1}\circ\alpha|_{D(z,r)},f_{2}\circ\alpha|_{D(z,r)},\ldots,f_{m}\circ\alpha|_{D(z,r)}
defined on the disc
D(z,r)
. Since
z
is an accumulation point of the zero set
\mathcal{Z}(f_{i}\circ\alpha|_{D(z,r)})
in
D(z,r)
, for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Therefore by Identity principle,
f_{i}\circ\alpha|_{D(z,r)}\equiv 0
on
D(z,r)
for all

, which in turn gives us that
D(z,r)\subset F
. Now if
F\neq\mathbb{D}
, then there exist a point

which is an accumulation point of
F
(since
F
is not discrete). But by above argument
\tilde{z}
belongs to the interior of
F
, which is a contradiction. Thus

, that is
\alpha(\mathbb{D})\subset E
which is a contradiction to the assumption that
p\in\alpha(\mathbb{D})\cap(M\setminus E)
. Therefore

is a discrete set.

Now if
L=\emptyset
, and consider

such that
\mathcal{F}
is transversal type at
q
. We need to show that
\alpha
is a constant map. Suppose not. Let
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
,

\alpha(z_{0})=q

\alpha(D(z_{0},r))\subset U_{q}

\alpha^{\prime}(z_{1})\neq 0

\alpha_{n}^{\prime}(z_{1})\rightarrow\alpha^{\prime}(z_{1})

\alpha^{\prime}(z_{1})\in C_{\alpha(z_{0})}\mathcal{F}

in the neighourhood

\alpha\equiv q\in E
.
∎

Since each
\alpha_{n}\in\cal{O}(\mathbb{D},\cal{F})

\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{D}\to L_{p}\cup E

(a)

\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{D}\to L_{p,W}\cup E

Now if
\mathcal{F}

of the leaf

to a map

is constant and

Thus,

Proof of Theorem

We will prove this in 3 steps.

(1)
The first step is to show that
W=\text{ker}(W_{n})
. Let
W_{0}:=\text{ker}(W_{n})
and let

. Consider a connected neighourhood

of

in

and

(Example 1.17), using the fact that
\Sigma_{1}=\{x=0\},\Sigma_{2}=\{y=0\}\;\&\;\Sigma_{3}=\{z=0\}
are
\cal{F}-
invariant to conclude that
\eta_{W}
is continuous on
W\setminus E
. It has also been proved that
\cal{F}
is transversal type. Therefore,
\eta_{W}
can be extended continuously on whole of
W
.

Since
W,U
are bounded taut domains in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
, therefore Theorem
1.7

\eta_{n}\to\eta_{W}
uniformly on the compacts of
W

Example 6.2
.

Let
\cal{F}
be the hyperbolic singular holomorphic foliation induced by the vector field

X=xy\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+zy\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+zx\frac{\partial}{\partial z}

on the open polydisc of radius
r=(5,5,5)
denoted by
U=P(0,r)\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}
. The singular set of
\cal{F}
is given by
E=\{(x,y,z)\in U:xy=yz=zx=0\}=\{x-\text{axis}\}\cup\{y-\text{axis}\}\cup\{z-\text{axis}\}
(here dim
(E)=1
). Let
W_{n}=P(0,(1,1,1))\cup P(0,(\frac{1}{n},2,\frac{1}{n}))
and
W=P(0,(1,1,1))
. Here
W_{n}\to W
in the kernel sense with the set
F=\{(0,y,0)\in U:y\in D(0,2)\setminus\overline{D(0,1)}\}
. Note that
F\subset E
, therefore the defective set corresponding to the foliation
\cal{F}
is empty, that is
S=\emptyset
.

As shown in
[
12
]
(Example 1.18), using the fact that
\Sigma_{1}=\{x=0\},\Sigma_{2}=\{y=0\}\;\&\;\Sigma_{3}=\{z=0\}
are
\cal{F}-
invariant to conclude that
\eta_{W}
is continuous on
W\setminus E
.

Take
p=(x,0,0)\in E
for
x\neq 0
. Observe that
C_{p}E=\langle e_{1}\rangle
, and therefore
(1,0,0)=e_{1}\in C_{p}E
. Consider the sequence
p_{n}=(x,\frac{1}{n},0)\in W\setminus E
and note that
e_{1}\in T_{p_{n}}\cal{F}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore
e_{1}\in\overline{C_{p}\cal{F}}
. Thus
\cal{F}
is not transversal type at
p
. Since
p
is arbitrary,
\cal{F}
is not transversal type at any point of
\{(x,0,0):x\in\mathbb{D}^{\ast}\}\subset E
.

Now on
\Sigma_{3}=\{z=0\}
, the foliation
\cal{F}_{W}
is given by
X=xy\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
. One can check that the leaf of
\cal{F}_{W}
passing through
p_{n}
is given by
L_{p_{n},W}=\{(\xi,\frac{1}{n},0):\xi\in\mathbb{D}^{\ast}\}\cong\mathbb{D}^{\ast}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore
\eta_{W}(p_{n})=\eta_{W}(p_{m})\neq 0
for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
.

On
\Sigma_{2}=\{y=0\}
, the foliation
\cal{F}_{W}
is given by
X=xz\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
. Here the leaf of
\cal{F}_{W}
passing through
\tilde{p}=(x,0,z)
is given by
L_{\tilde{p},W}=\{(x,0,\xi):\xi\in D(0,r_{3})^{\ast}\}\cong\mathbb{D}^{\ast}
. Note that
L_{\tilde{p},W}\cup\{p\}\cong\mathbb{D}
, i.e.,
L_{\tilde{p},W}
is a separatrix through
p
. Take any sequence
\{q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L_{\tilde{p},W}
such that
q_{n}\to p
, we have
\eta_{W}(q_{n})\to 0
.

Therefore, we get two sequences
p_{n},q_{n}\to p
, but
\eta_{W}(p_{n})\to k\neq 0
, and
\eta_{W}(q_{n})\to 0
. Thus,
\eta_{W}
cannot be extended continuously to
p
.

We use similar arguments to check that for every
p\in E\setminus\{0\}=:\tilde{E}
,
\cal{F}
is not transversal type at
p
(in fact
\cal{F}
is not transversal type for any
p\in E
). Once again, by looking at the structure of leaves in the invariant hyperplanes
\Sigma_{i}
’s, we can conclude that
\eta_{W}
can not be extended continuously on any point
p\in\tilde{E}=E\setminus\{0\}
.

Since
W,U
are bounded taut domains in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
, therefore Theorem
1.7
tells us that
\eta_{n}\to\eta_{W}
uniformly on the compacts of
W\setminus E
.

Example 6.3
.

Let
\cal{F}
be the hyperbolic singular holomorphic foliation induced by the vector field

X=x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+2y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

on the open polydisc of radius
r=(5,5)
denoted by
U=P(0,r)\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
. The singular set of the foliation
\cal{F}
is
E=\{(0,0)\}
.

Consider the complex line segment
R=\{(x,y)\in P(0,(3,3))\mid y=x\}
. Let
V_{n}\subset U
be a sequence of decreasing open connected neighourhoods of the segment
R
such that
\cap_{n\geq 1}V_{n}=R
. Now let
W_{n}=P(0,(1,1))\cup V_{n}
, and
W=P(0,(1,1))
. Note that
W_{n}\to W
in the kernel sense with the set
F=\{(x,y)\in P(0,(3,3))\setminus\overline{P(0,(1,1))}\mid y=x\}
.

Let
p=(x_{0},y_{0})\in U\setminus E
, the leaf
L_{p}
of
\cal{F}
is given by the solution of the differential equation

\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{2y}{x},

with the initial condition
y(x_{0})=y_{0}
. One can check that

L_{p}=\{(x,y)\in U\setminus E\mid x_{0}^{2}y=y_{0}x^{2}\}.

Now if
q=(x,x)\in R\cap L_{p}
, then
x_{0}^{2}x=y_{0}x^{2}
. Since
(0,0)\notin L_{p}
, therefore
R\cap L_{p}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
x_{0}y_{0}\neq 0
. If
p\in U\setminus E
is such that
x_{0}y_{0}\neq 0
, then
q=(\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{y_{0}},\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{y_{0}})
, that is

R\cap L_{p}=\{(\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{y_{0}},\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{y_{0}})\}.

So,
F\cap L_{p}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
|y_{0}|<|x_{0}|^{2}
. Consider the set

T=\{(x,y)\in W\setminus E:|y|<|x|^{2}\}\subset W.

Note that if
p\in T
, then we can see from above that
L_{p,W}\subset S
, where
S
is the defective set in
W
corresponding to the foliation
\cal{F}
. Since
p\in T
is arbitrary, therefore
T\subset S
. Now one can easily check from the description of leaves of
\cal{F}
that for
p\in T
, the leaf
L_{p,W}
is contained in
T
, that is
L_{p,W}\subset T
. Therefore, we get
S\subset T
. Thus, the defective set
S=T
is an open set in
W
.

But as we already observed that for every
p\in S
, the intersection
L_{p}\cap F
is a singleton set. Therefore, for every
p\in S
,

\text{int}(L_{p}\cap F)=\emptyset,

where
\text{int}(L_{p}\cap F)
denotes the interior of
L_{p}\cap F
as a subset of the leaf
L_{p}
. Thus, every
p\in S
is a removable defective point, that is
\hat{S}=S
.

Since
W,U
are bounded taut domains in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
[
12
]
gives us the continuity of
\eta_{W}
on
W\setminus E
. Furthermore,
\cal{F}
is transversal type at
0\in E
, which gives us that
\eta_{W}
is continuous on whole of
W
.

Theorem
1.8
now tells us that
\eta_{n}\to\eta_{W}
uniformly on the compacts of
W
.

We will end the section with a few questions.

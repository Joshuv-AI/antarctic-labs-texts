The Dirichlet problem for Monge-Ampère equation for ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -PSH functions on Hermitian manifolds
Rirong Yuan

Abstract
We solve the Dirichlet problem for Monge-Ampère equation for ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -PSH functions possibly with degenerate right-hand side, through deriving a quantitative version of boundary estimate under the assumption of ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -PSH subsolutions. In addition, we confirm the subsolution assumption on a product of a closed balanced manifold with a compact Riemann surface with boundary.



1
Introduction

A basic work in Kähler geometry is Yau’s
[
31
]
proof of Calabi’s conjecture.
In
[
8
]
Gauduchon showed that
every closed Hermitian manifold
(M,J,\omega)
of complex dimension
n\geq 2

admits a unique (up to rescaling) Gauduchon metric
in the conformal class
[\omega]
, and he furthermore
conjectured in
[
9
]
that the Calabi-Yau theorem is also true for Gauduchon metric.
On complex surfaces and astheno-Kähler manifolds introduced by Jost-Yau
[
19
]
,
the Gauduchon conjecture was solved by Cherrier
[
5
]
and Tosatti-Weinkove
[
29
]
, respectively.
The Gauduchon conjecture in higher dimensions
amounts to solving a Monge-Ampère type equation

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\chi}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(\Delta u\omega-\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u\right)+Z\right)^{n}=\phi\omega^{n}

(1.1)

where

\tilde{\chi}=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}*\omega_{0}^{n-1},\quad Z=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}*\mathfrak{Re}\left(\sqrt{-1}\partial u\wedge\overline{\partial}(\omega^{n-2})\right),

see
[
24
,
29
]
. Here
*
is the Hodge star operator with respect to
\omega
and
\omega_{0}
is a Gauduchon metric.
Following
[
16
]
, also
[
28
]
, we call
u
an
(n-1)
-plurisubharmonic (
(n-1)
-PSH in short) function if

\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(\Delta u\omega-\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u\right)+Z\geq 0\mbox{ in }M.

Furthermore, we call
u
a strictly
(n-1)
-PSH function if

\tilde{\chi}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(\Delta u\omega-\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u\right)+Z>0\mbox{ in }M.

Geometrically, the strictly
(n-1)
-PSH solution of
(
1.1
) allows one to construct
a Gauduchon metric, say
\Omega_{u}
,
with prescribed volume form

\displaystyle\Omega_{u}^{n}=\tilde{\phi}\omega^{n}

(1.2)

and
\Omega_{u}^{n-1}=\omega_{0}^{n-1}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u\wedge\omega^{n-2}+\mathfrak{Re}(\sqrt{-1}\partial u\wedge\overline{\partial}\omega^{n-2}),

where and hereafter

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}=\phi^{{1}/{(n-1)}}.

(1.3)

Recently, Székelyhidi-Tosatti-Weinkove
[
27
]
proved Gauduchon’s conjecture for
n\geq 3
, thereby extending the Calabi-Yau theorem to Gauduchon metric.

This paper is devoted to extending Székelyhidi-Tosatti-Weinkove’s results to complex manifolds with boundary.
To this end, we assume that
(M,J,\omega)
is a compact Hermitian manifold of complex dimension
n\geq 2
with boundary
\partial M
,
\bar{M}:=M\cup\partial M
,
where
J
is the complex structure,
\omega=\sqrt{-1}g_{i\bar{j}}dz^{i}\wedge d\bar{z}^{j}
denotes the Kähler form. We consider the Dirichlet problem with prescribed boundary value condition

\displaystyle u=\varphi\mbox{ }\mbox{ on }{\partial M}.

(1.4)

Before stating our results, we summarize some notion.

Definition 1.1
.

A strictly
(n-1)
-PSH function
\underline{u}\in C^{2}(\bar{M})
is called a
subsolution
of the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) and (
1.4
), if

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\chi}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(\Delta\underline{u}\omega-\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\underline{u}\right)+\underline{Z}\right)^{n}\geq

\displaystyle\phi\omega^{n}\,

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }M,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\underline{u}=

\displaystyle\varphi\,

\displaystyle\mbox{ on }\partial M.

We say a strictly
(n-1)
-PSH function
\underline{u}\in C^{2}(\bar{M})
is a
strict subsolution
of the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) and (
1.4
), if

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\chi}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(\Delta\underline{u}\omega-\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\underline{u}\right)+\underline{Z}\right)^{n}\geq

\displaystyle(\phi+\delta)\omega^{n}\,

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }M

(1.6)

\displaystyle\underline{u}=

\displaystyle\varphi\,

\displaystyle\mbox{ on }\partial M

for a positive constant
\delta
.
Here
\underline{Z}=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}*\mathfrak{Re}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\underline{u}\wedge\overline{\partial}(\omega^{n-2}))
.

Our main results are stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,J,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian manifold with smooth boundary.
Assume the given data
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial M)
and

0<\phi\in C^{\infty}(\bar{M})
support an
(n-1)
-PSH subsolution
\underline{u}\in C^{2,1}(\bar{M})
subject to (
1.5
).
Then the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) and (
1.4
)
is uniquely solvable in the class of smooth strictly
(n-1)
-PSH functions.

We say the boundary is

if

\displaystyle-(\kappa_{1}+\cdots+\kappa_{n-1})\geq 0\quad\mbox{ on }\partial M

(1.7)

where
\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n-1}
are the eigenvalues of Levi form
L_{\partial M}

with respect to

\omega^{\prime}=\omega|_{T_{\partial M}\cap JT_{\partial M}}
.
When the boundary is mean pseudoconcave we can solve the equation
with degenerate right-hand side

.

Let

be a compact Hermitian manifold with smooth mean pseudoconcave boundary. Let
\varphi\in C^{2,1}(\partial M)
and

-smooth

-PSH strict subsolution satisfying (

As shown in the above theorem, we obtain a

-smooth

-PSH weak solution for (

with right-hand side of the form

However, it is fairly restrictive even in the case

.
In some problems arising from complex geometry and analysis,

\Omega_{u}^{n}=\tilde{\phi}\omega^{n}

\partial\overline{\partial}\Omega_{u}^{n-1}=0

-smooth

.

We make progress on this subject under the assumption that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{\phi}}{\partial\nu}=0\mbox{ }\mbox{ at }\left\{p\in\partial M:\tilde{\phi}(p)=0\right\}

•

(M,\omega)
is a complex submanifold of an open Hermitian manifold
(M^{\prime},\omega)
of complex dimension
n

\bar{M}\subsetneq M^{\prime}

•

\tilde{\phi}

C^{1,1}
-smoothly extended to a nonnegative function on

.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,J,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian manifold with smooth mean pseudoconcave boundary.
Let
\varphi\in C^{2,1}(\partial M)
and
\tilde{\phi}=\phi^{1/(n-1)}
be as in (

) for
\phi\geq 0
.
Assume the data

) and suppose a

-PSH strict subsolution subject to (

\Omega_{u}^{n}=\tilde{\phi}\omega^{n}

possesses an
(n-1)
-PSH weak solution

with
\forall 0<\alpha<1
,
u|_{\partial M}=\varphi
,

.

The existence of
subsolution is imposed as an important ingredient to study the Dirichlet problem, subsequent to the works of
[
13
,
10
]

concerning Mong-Ampère equations on domains. It would be worthwhile to note that the subsolution assumption is a necessary condition for the solvability of Dirichlet problem.

Let
(X,J_{X},\omega_{X})
be a closed balanced manifold (introduced by Michelsohn
[
22
]
), let
(S,J_{S},\omega_{S})
be a compact Riemann surface with boundary

.
We confirm the subsolution assumption

\displaystyle(M,J,\omega)=(X\times S,J,\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{X}+\pi_{2}^{*}\omega_{S}),

when the boundary data

can be extended to a

Here

\displaystyle\pi_{2}:X\times S\rightarrow S.

The construction starts with the solution of

\displaystyle h=0\mbox{ }\mbox{ on }\partial S.

According to Lemma

We can verify

The
(n-1)
-PSH subsolution is given by

Let

Finally, we make some remarks:

\mathrm{{(1)}}
Our results
are valid when replacing
\tilde{\chi}=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}*\omega_{0}^{n-1}
by more general real
(1,1)
-forms
\tilde{\chi}
.

\mathrm{{(2)}}

It is still open to derive gradient estimate directly for Monge-Ampère equation for
(n-1)
-PSH functions.
Our strategy is to derive a quantitative version of boundary estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{\partial M}\Delta u\leq C\left(1+\sup_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\right).

This is precisely the estimate we prove in Theorem
2.2
. In the case of
Z=0
such a boundary estimate was derived by the author in

[
33
]
.
It would be worthwhile to note that when
Z\neq 0
the proof of quantitative boundary estimate
is much more complicated and fairly difficult, due to the gradient terms from
Z
. It requires some new ideas and insights.

\mathrm{{(3)}}
We prove in Theorem
1.4
the existence of
C^{1,\alpha}
-smooth solution to

\Omega_{u}^{n}=\tilde{\phi}\omega^{n}
with the assumption (
1.8
) but without restriction to the dimension.
That is different from
the works of Guan
[
14
]
and Guan-Li
[
15
]
on certain degenerate real Monge-Ampère type equations, in which the assumptions were only completely confirmed in dimensions
2
and
3
.

\mathrm{{(4)}}
In Theorems
1.5
-
1.6
we find a significant phenomenon on weakening regularity assumptions on boundary. For the Dirichlet problem with homogeneous boundary data
on
M=X\times S
,
the regularity assumption on boundary can be weakened to
C^{2,\beta}
; while
such
C^{2,\beta}
regularity assumption is impossible even for Dirichlet problem of
nondegenerate real Monge-Ampère equation on certain bounded domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
as shown by Wang
[
30
]
, the optimal regularity assumptions on the boundary and boundary data are both
C^{3}
-smooth.
More results with less regularity assumption are established in Section
6
.

\mathrm{{(5)}}
When
\omega_{0}
is a balanced metric and
\omega
is astheno-Kähler, one obtains a
\mathrm{d}
-closed
(n-1,n-1)
-form

\omega_{0}^{n-1}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u\wedge\omega^{n-2}+2\mathfrak{Re}(\sqrt{-1}\partial u\wedge\overline{\partial}\omega^{n-2}),

the Form-type Calabi-Yau equation introduced in
[
7
]
thus falls into a Monge-Ampère type equations analogous to (
1.1
). By the same argument, we obtain a balanced metric
\Psi_{u}
with prescribed volume form

\Psi_{u}^{n}=\tilde{\phi}\omega^{n}

with the boundary value (
1.4
); similarly,
we can construct the subsolutions on the products as we constructed in (
1.9
). It was shown by Latorre-Ugarte

[
20
]
that there are closed complex manifolds
X
admitting a non-Kähler balanced metric

\omega_{X,0}
and a non-Kähler metirc
\omega_{X}
simultaneously being astheno-Kähler and Gauduchon.
On that product
X\times S
one obtains a balanced metric

\omega_{0}=\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{X,0}+\pi_{2}^{*}\omega_{S}
and an astheno-Kähler metric

\omega=\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{X}+\pi_{2}^{*}\omega_{S}
.
As a result, we can solve the Dirichlet problem for Form-type Calabi-Yau equation on such products.

) directly, as done by Hanani
[

]
, Błocki
[

]

[

In

\displaystyle\sup_{M}\Delta u\leq C\left(1+\sup_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)

[

]
.
Such a blow-up argument using (

[

]
, complemented by
[
2
,
23
]
,
for Dirichlet problem of complex
Monge-Ampère equation, and by Dinew-Kołodziej
[
6
]
based on Hou-Ma-Wu’s second estimate

[
18
]
for complex
k
-Hessian equations on
closed Kähler manifolds.
An extensive extension was obtained by Szèkelyhidi
[
26
]
on closed manifolds, and by the author
[
33
]

for the Dirichlet problem.

When
\partial M\neq\emptyset
,
Székelyhidi-Tosatti-Weinkove’s estimate
yields the following:

Theorem 2.1

(
[
27
]
)
.

Let
u\in C^{4}(M)\cap C^{2}(\bar{M})
be a
(n-1)
-PSH solution to (
1.1
), then

\displaystyle\sup_{M}\Delta u\leq C\left(1+\sup_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}+\sup_{\partial M}|\Delta u|\right)

(2.2)

where
C
depends on
|\phi^{1/n}|_{C^{2}(\bar{M})}

and other known data but not on
(\inf_{M}\phi)^{-1}
.

It only requires to prove quantitative boundary estimate.

Theorem 2.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
, any strictly
(n-1)
-PSH solution
u\in C^{3}(M)\cap C^{2}(\bar{M})
to the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) and (
1.4
) satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{\partial M}\Delta u\leq C\left(1+\sup_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\right),

(2.3)

where

depends on
|\phi^{{1}/{n}}|_{C^{1}(\bar{M})}
,
|\varphi|_{C^{3}(\bar{M})}
,

|\underline{u}|_{C^{2}(\bar{M})}
,
\partial M
up to third derivatives and other known data.

Furthermore,

is independent of
(\inf_{M}\phi)^{-1}
if.
\partial M
is mean pseudoconcave.

With Theorems
2.2
and
2.1
at hand, we derive (

) and then establish gradient estimate via the blow-up argument.
A somewhat remarkable fact to us is that we can prove the quantitative boundary estimate under a weaker assumption

3
Preliminaries and lemmas

3.1
Background of the equation

The equation (
1.1
) can be reformulated in the following form

\displaystyle\log P_{n-1}(\lambda(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}[u]))=\psi\mbox{ and }\lambda(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}[u])\in\mathcal{P}_{n-1}.

(3.1)

Here

\displaystyle P_{n-1}(\lambda)=\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n},\quad\mu_{i}=\sum_{j\neq i}\lambda_{j}.

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n-1}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mu_{i}>0,\mbox{ }\forall 1\leq i\leq n\},

(3.3)

and
\psi=\log\phi+n\log(n-1)
,

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{i\bar{j}}=u_{i\bar{j}}+\check{\chi}_{i\bar{j}}+W_{i\bar{j}},

(3.4)

where
\check{\chi}_{i\bar{j}}=(\mathrm{tr}_{\omega}\tilde{\chi})g_{i\bar{j}}-(n-1)\tilde{\chi}_{i\bar{j}}
,

W_{i\bar{j}}=(\mathrm{tr}_{\omega}Z)g_{i\bar{j}}-(n-1)Z_{i\bar{j}}
,

\displaystyle Z_{i\bar{j}}=

\displaystyle\frac{g^{p\bar{q}}\bar{T}^{l}_{ql}g_{i\bar{j}}u_{p}+g^{p\bar{q}}T^{k}_{pk}g_{i\bar{j}}u_{\bar{q}}-g^{k\bar{l}}g_{i\bar{q}}\bar{T}^{q}_{lj}u_{k}-g^{k\bar{l}}g_{q\bar{j}}T^{q}_{ki}u_{\bar{l}}-\bar{T}^{l}_{jl}u_{i}-T^{k}_{ik}u_{\bar{j}}}{2(n-1)},

(3.5)

where
T^{k}_{ij}=g^{k\bar{l}}(\frac{\partial g_{j\bar{l}}}{\partial z_{i}}-\frac{\partial g_{i\bar{l}}}{\partial z_{j}}),

see also
[
27
]
.

For simplicity we denote

\displaystyle f(\lambda)=\log P_{n-1}(\lambda),

(3.6)

For any
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{n-1}
we have the following simple properties:

\displaystyle f_{i}(\lambda)=\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)=(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}},

(3.7)

\displaystyle f_{i}(\lambda)\geq\frac{\min_{k}{\mu_{k}}}{n\max_{k}{\mu_{k}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\lambda),\quad\forall i,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)\lambda_{i}=n,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)\underline{\lambda}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\underline{\mu}_{i}}{\mu_{i}}.

(3.9)

Moreover

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)\geq n(n-1)e^{-\sigma/n},\mbox{ for }f(\lambda)=\sigma,

(3.10)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\underline{\lambda}\in\mathcal{P}_{n-1}
and
\varepsilon=\inf_{M}\min_{i}\underline{\mu}_{i}
(then
\varepsilon>0
).
Suppose that

\displaystyle\min_{j}\mu_{j}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2n}.

(3.11)

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}}\geq\frac{2n}{\varepsilon},

(3.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)(\underline{\lambda}_{i}-\lambda_{i})\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2(n-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda).

(3.13)

Remark 3.2
.

It only requires to consider the case when (
3.11
) holds. Otherwise for

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\mu_{i}=\sigma
, we have

\displaystyle\max_{i}\mu_{i}\leq(2n)^{n-1}e^{\sigma}/\varepsilon^{n-1},

(3.14)

and

\frac{\min_{i}\mu_{i}}{\max_{i}\mu_{i}}\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{n}}{(2n)^{n}e^{\sigma}}.

(3.15)

Throughout this paper we denote

\mathfrak{\tilde{g}}=\mathfrak{\tilde{g}}[u],\mbox{ }\mathfrak{\underline{\tilde{g}}}=\mathfrak{\tilde{g}}[\underline{u}],\mbox{ }\underline{Z}=Z[\underline{u}],\mbox{ }Z=Z[u],\mbox{ }W=W[u],\mbox{ }\underline{W}=W[\underline{u}]

for solution

and subsolution
\underline{u}
. In Sections
4
and
5
we denote

\displaystyle\lambda=\lambda(\mathfrak{\tilde{g}}),\,

\displaystyle\underline{\lambda}=\lambda(\mathfrak{\underline{\tilde{g}}}).

(3.16)

3.2
Some computation and notation

Fix

we can choose a local holomorphic coordinate system

\displaystyle(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}),\mbox{ }z_{i}=x_{i}+\sqrt{-1}y_{i},

(3.17)

centered at

,
such that

,

\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{\alpha}}
for

.
We denote

the distance function from

\partial M

\omega
.
Also we denote

\displaystyle\Omega_{\delta}:=\{z\in M:|z|<\delta\},\,

\displaystyle M_{\delta}:=\{z\in M:\sigma(z)<\delta\}.

(3.18)

In the computation we use derivatives with respect to Chern connection
\nabla

\omega
,
and write

\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}
,

\overline{\partial}_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}_{i}}

\nabla_{i}=\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}}
,

\nabla_{\bar{i}}=\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}_{i}}}

For a smooth function

\psi=\log\phi+n\log(n-1)
.
Generalized Newton-Maclaurin inequality gives

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}}\geq n^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}}{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n}}\right)^{1/(n-1)}.

(5.21)

By straightforward computations we get

\displaystyle(\log\phi)_{i\bar{j}}=\frac{\phi_{i\bar{j}}}{\phi}-\frac{\phi_{i}\phi_{\bar{j}}}{\phi^{2}},

\displaystyle(\phi^{1/(n-1)})_{i\bar{j}}=\frac{1}{n-1}\phi^{1/(n-1)}\left(\frac{\phi_{i\bar{j}}}{\phi}-\frac{n-2}{n-1}\frac{\phi_{i}\phi_{\bar{j}}}{\phi^{2}}\right).

Combining with (
5.21
) and concavity of
f
,
for

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\mu_{i}=\log\phi+n\log(n-1),

we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\geq n^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\phi^{-\frac{1}{n-1}},

(5.22)

\displaystyle|\psi_{k}|=|(\log\phi)_{k}|=(n-1)\phi^{-1/(n-1)}|(\phi^{1/(n-1)})_{k}|,

(5.23)

\displaystyle\psi_{k\bar{k}}=(\log\phi)_{k\bar{k}}=(n-1)\phi^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}(\phi^{{1}/{(n-1)}})_{k\bar{k}}-\frac{1}{n-1}\frac{|\phi_{k}|^{2}}{\phi^{2}}.

(5.24)

By (
5.19
) again,

\displaystyle\frac{|\phi_{k}|^{2}}{\phi^{2}}\leq\frac{|\nabla\phi|^{2}}{\phi^{2}}=(n-1)^{2}\frac{|\nabla\phi^{{1}/{(n-1)}}|^{2}}{\phi^{\frac{2}{n-1}}}\leq C\phi^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}.

(5.25)

Thus

\displaystyle|\psi_{k}|,-\psi_{k\bar{k}}\leq C\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\right)^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}.

With those conditions at hand,
one can prove the second order
estimates (
2.2
), following the original proof of Szèkelyhidi-Tosatti-Weinkove almost words by words.

∎

Remark 5.6

5.19

Further discussion on more general equations

Our method works for more general equations generated by smooth symmetric functions
f
defined on
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, dating to the work of Caffarelli-Nirenberg-Spruck
[
3
]
. We consider the Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle f(\lambda(*\Phi[u]))=\psi\mbox{ in }M,\,

\displaystyle u=\varphi\mbox{ on }\partial M

(7.1)

where
*\Phi[u]=\chi+\Delta u\omega-\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u+\varrho Z[u],

and
\varrho
is a smooth function, i.e.

\Phi[u]=*\chi+\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u\wedge\omega^{n-2}+\frac{\varrho}{(n-1)!}\mathfrak{Re}(\sqrt{-1}\partial u\wedge\overline{\partial}\omega^{n-2}).

Here
\Gamma
is an open symmetric convex cone containing positive cone

\Gamma_{n}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mbox{ each component }\lambda_{i}>0\}\subseteq\Gamma

with vertex at the origin and with boundary
\partial\Gamma\neq\emptyset
.
In addition we assume

\displaystyle f_{i}(\lambda):=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(\lambda)>0\mbox{ in }\Gamma,\,

\displaystyle\forall 1\leq i\leq n,

(7.2)

\displaystyle f\mbox{ is concave in }\Gamma,

(7.3)

\displaystyle\mbox{For any }\lambda\in\Gamma,\mbox{ }\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}f(t\lambda)>-\infty,

(7.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}f(\lambda_{1}+t,\cdots,\lambda_{n-1}+t,\lambda_{n})=\sup_{\Gamma}f,\,

\displaystyle\forall\lambda\in\Gamma.

(7.5)

The equation (
7.1
) is nondegenerate when the right-hand side satisfies

\displaystyle\inf_{M}\psi>\sup_{\partial\Gamma}f.

(7.6)

Also it is called a degenerate equation if
\inf_{M}\psi=\sup_{\partial\Gamma}f
and
f\in C^{\infty}(\infty)\cap C(\Gamma)
, where

\sup_{\partial\Gamma}f:=\sup_{\lambda_{0}\in\partial\Gamma}\limsup_{\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}}f(\lambda).

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(M,J,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian manifold with smooth boundary.
Suppose, in addition to

)-(

(7.7)

Then Dirichlet problem (

) admits a unique smooth solution with

in
\bar{M}
.

denotes the summation of all the algebraic multiplicities of distinct eigenvalues which
lie in
J_{k}
).
This function measures the number of the eigenvalues which lie in
J_{k}
.

The crucial ingredient is that Lemma
A.2
yields the continuity of
\mathrm{{\bf\widetilde{Card}}}_{i}(\mathrm{{\bf a}})
for
\mathrm{{\bf a}}\geq P_{0}^{\prime}
. More explicitly,
by using Lemma
A.2
and

\lambda_{n}\geq{\bf a}\geq P_{0}^{\prime}>\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|d_{i}|+\frac{\epsilon}{2n-3}

we conclude that
if
\mathrm{{\bf a}}
satisfies the quadratic growth condition (
A.2
) then

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}\overline{I_{k}^{\prime}})=\mathbb{R}\setminus(\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\overline{J_{i}}),

(A.14)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha}\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1}I_{i}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}J_{i}\mbox{ for }1\leq\alpha\leq n-1.

Hence,
\mathrm{{\bf\widetilde{Card}}}_{i}(\mathrm{{\bf a}})
is a continuous function
in the variable
\mathrm{{\bf a}}
. So it is a constant.
Together with the line of the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 1.2]

we see
that
\mathrm{{\bf\widetilde{Card}}}_{i}(\mathrm{{\bf a}})=j_{i}-j_{i-1}
for sufficiently large
\mathrm{{\bf a}}
.
Here we denote
j_{0}=0
and
j_{m}=n-1
.
The constant of
\mathrm{{\bf\widetilde{Card}}}_{i}
therefore follows that

\mathrm{{\bf\widetilde{Card}}}_{i}(\mathrm{{\bf a}})=j_{i}-j_{i-1}.

We thus know that the
(j_{i}-j_{i-1})
eigenvalues

\lambda_{j_{i-1}+1},\lambda_{j_{i-1}+2},\cdots,\lambda_{j_{i}}

lie in the connected component
J_{i}
.
Thus, for any
j_{i-1}+1\leq\gamma\leq j_{i}
, we have
I_{\gamma}^{\prime}\subset J_{i}
and
\lambda_{\gamma}

lies in the connected component
J_{i}
.
Therefore,

|\lambda_{\gamma}-d_{\gamma}|<\frac{(2(j_{i}-j_{i-1})-1)\epsilon}{2n-3}\leq\epsilon.

Here we also use the fact that
d_{\gamma}
is midpoint of
I_{\gamma}^{\prime}
and
every
J_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open subset.

To be brief, if for fixed index
1\leq i\leq n-1
the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}(P_{0}^{\prime})
lies in
J_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha
,
then Lemma
A.2
implies that, for any
{\bf a}>P_{0}^{\prime}
, the corresponding eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}({\bf a})
lies in the same interval
J_{\alpha}
.
The computation of
\mathrm{{\bf\widetilde{Card}}}_{k}
can be done by letting
\mathrm{\bf a}\rightarrow+\infty
.

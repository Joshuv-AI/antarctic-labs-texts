Xia Yin, Nanying Yang

Abstract
In this paper, we prove the existence and conjugacy of injectors of a generalized π \pi -soluble groups for the Hartley class defined by a invariable Hartley function, and give a description of the structure of the injectors.

Throughout this paper, all groups are finite and

\pi

be the set of all primes, then let

. We use
G_{\pi}
denote a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of

Recall that a class
\mathfrak{F}
of groups is called a Fitting class if
\mathfrak{F}
is closed under taking normal subgroups and products of normal
\mathfrak{F}
-subgroups.
As usual, we denote by
\mathfrak{E},\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{N}
the classes of all groups, all soluble groups, all nilpotent groups, respectively;
\mathfrak{E}_{\pi},\mathfrak{S}_{\pi},\mathfrak{N}_{\pi}
denote the classes of all
\pi
-groups, all soluble
\pi
-groups, all nilpotent
\pi
-groups, respectively; and

to denote the classes of all
\pi
-soluble groups and all
\pi
-nilpotent groups, respectively. It is well known that all the above classes are Fitting classes.

From the definition of Fitting class, we see that for each non-empty Fitting class
\mathfrak{F}
, every group
G
has a unique maximal
normal
\mathfrak{F}
-subgroup, which is called the
\mathfrak{F}
-radical of
G
and denoted by

.

For any non-empty class
\mathfrak{F}
of groups, a subgroup

\mathfrak{F}
-maximal if
V\in\mathfrak{F}
and

V\leq U\leq G
and

.
A subgroup

of a group

is said to be an
\mathfrak{F}
-injector of

if

is an

\mathfrak{F}
-maximal subgroup of

of

. Clearly, every
\mathfrak{F}
-injector of
G
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-maximal subgroup of
G

Fitting classes play an important role in the theory of groups. The importance of the theory of Fitting classes can firstly be seen in the following theorem proved by Fischer, Gasch
\ddot{u}
tz and Harley [1], which is in fact a generalization of the classical Sylow theorem and Hall theorem.

Theorem 1.1
(see
[
1
]
or
[
2
, Theorem VIII,2.8]
. Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a non-empty Fitting class. Then a soluble group possesses exactly one conjugacy class of
\mathfrak{F}
-injectors.

For a Fitting class
\mathfrak{F}
, we let
\pi(\mathfrak{F})=\bigcup_{G\in\mathfrak{F}}\sigma(G)
. Note that if
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{N}_{p}
is the Fitting class of all
p
-groups, then the
\mathfrak{F}
-injectors of a group
G
are Sylow
p
-subgroups of
G
; If
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{E}_{\pi}
and

has a Hall
\pi
-soluble group, then the
\mathfrak{F}
-injectors of

are Hall
\pi
-subgroups of
G
(see
[
3
, p.68, Ex.1]
or
[
4
, p.238]
).

About the existence of
\mathfrak{X}
-injector of
G
, Shemetkov posed the following problem.

Problem 1.2
(Shemetkov
[
5
, Problem 11.117]
). Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a Fitting class of soluble groups. Is it true that every finite non-soluble group possesses an
\mathfrak{X}
-injector ?

This problem has been resolved in
[
6

7
]
for the Fitting classes
\mathfrak{X}\in\{\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{S}_{\pi},\mathfrak{N}\}
.

In connection with this, a interesting problem is: to find the conjugate class of injectors in any
\pi
-soluble groups. The first result in this direction is the following famous Chuchin’s theorem
[
8
]
: A
\pi
-soluble

possesses a Hall
\pi
-subgroup (that is,
\mathfrak{E}_{\pi}
-injectors) and any two Hall
\pi
-subgroups are conjugate.

As a development of Chuchin’s theorem, Shemetkov and Guo proved the following

Theorem 1.3
(
[
9
, Theorem 2.2]
and
[
10
]
). Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a Fitting class,
\pi=\pi(\mathfrak{F})
. If
G/G_{\mathfrak{F}}
is
\pi
-soluble, then
G
has an
\mathfrak{F}
-injector and any two
\mathfrak{F}
-injector of
G
are conjugate in
G
.

The product
\mathfrak{FH}
of two Fitting classes
\mathfrak{F}
and

\mathfrak{H}
is the class
(G\mid G/G_{\mathfrak{F}}\in\mathfrak{H}).
It is
well known that the product of any two Fitting classes is also a
Fitting class and the multiplication of Fitting classes satisfies
associative law (see [2, Theorem IX, 1.12(a)(c)]).

Following
[
11
,
12
]
, a function
f
:
\mathbb{P}\longrightarrow\{\rm{nonempty\ Fitting\ classes}\}
is called a Hartley function (or in brevity, H-function). A Fitting class
\mathfrak{F}
is local if

\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{E}_{\pi(\mathfrak{F})}\bigcap(\bigcap_{p\in\pi(\mathfrak{F})}f(p)\mathfrak{N}_{p}\mathfrak{E}_{p^{\prime}})

for some
H-function
f
.

For a H-function
h
, let
\pi=Supp(h):=\{p\in\mathbb{P}:h(p)\neq\emptyset\}
, which is called the
support of the H-function

LH(h)=\bigcap_{p\in\pi}h(p)\mathfrak{E}_{p^{\prime}}\mathfrak{N}_{p}.
Then, a Fitting class
\mathfrak{F}
is called Hartley class
if there exists a H-function
h
such that
\mathfrak{H}=LH(h)
. In this case,
\mathfrak{H}
is said to be defined by the H-function
h
or
h
is an H-function of
\mathfrak{H}
.

It is clear that
\mathfrak{N}\subseteq LH(h)
. Hence, if
\mathfrak{F}=LH(h),
then
\pi(\mathfrak{F})=\mathbb{P}.
Any Hartley class is a local Fitting class (see
[
13
, p.31]
). But the converse is not true in general (see
[
11
, p.207, 4.2]
).
For two class functions

for all

.

Concerning Fitting classes and injectors, the authors in

Let

-injectors of a soluble group?

Problem 1.4.
For a local Fitting class
\mathfrak{F}
and a non-soluble group

-soluble group

possesses an
\mathfrak{F}
-injector and any two

-injectors are conjugate ?

In this paper, by developing local method offered by Hartley

(in particular, for
\pi
-soluble groups) and the Hartley class defined by a invariable Hartley function. In fact, we will prove the following

Support that

be a Hartley class defined by a invariable Hartley function

h(p)=\mathfrak{X}
for all prime

(1)

-injector and any two

(2) Every
\mathfrak{H}
-injector

-injector of

-subgroup of

From Theorem 1.5, a series of new classical conjugate classes of injectors in any

-soluble group are obtained and the structure of injectors of some groups are described. For example, the following results directly follow from Theorem 1.5.

Corollary 1.5.1.
Every
\pi
-soluble group possesses exactly one conjugacy class of
\mathfrak{X}\mathfrak{N}^{\pi}
-injectors.

Corollary 1.5.2.
Every

-soluble group has a injector with limited

If

Note that even if

Proof of Theorem 1.5

The proof of Theorem 1.5 consists of a large number of steps. The following 5 lemmas are the main steps of it.

Lemma 3.1.
Every
\pi
-soluble group
G
possesses exactly one conjugacy class of
\pi
-nilpotent injectors, and each
\pi
-nilpotent injector of
G
is the product of the
\pi^{\prime}
-radical of
G
and an
\mathfrak{N}_{\pi}
-injector of some Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
G
.

Proof.
We prove the lemma by induction on
|G|
. Let
M
be any maximal normal subgroup of
G
. We consider the following two possible cases.

Case 1. The
\pi^{\prime}
-radical
G_{\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}}
of
G
is a identity group, that is,
G_{\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}}=1
.

Then
M_{\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}}=1
. By induction,

possesses exactly one conjugacy class of
\pi
-nilpotent injectors and every
\pi
-nilpotent injector of

is an
\mathfrak{N}_{\pi}
-injector of some Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
M
.

Let
F_{1}
be an
\mathfrak{N}_{\pi}
-injector of some Hall
\pi
-subgroup
M_{\pi}
of
M
. Since every Hall
\pi
-subgroup
G_{\pi}
of

is soluble,
G_{\pi}
has an
\mathfrak{N}_{\pi}
-injector
V
and any two
\mathfrak{N}_{\pi}
-injectors of
G_{\pi}
are conjugate in
G_{\pi}
.
Since

M_{\pi}=M\cap G_{\pi}\lhd G_{\pi},

we have that

is an
\mathfrak{N}_{\pi}
-injector of
M
by Lemma 2.2(1). In view of the conjugacy of
\mathfrak{N}_{\pi}
-injectors of

F_{1}=V\cap M_{\pi}.
Since a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of any
\pi
-nilpotent group is a nilpotent
\pi
-subgroup, every
\pi
-nilpotent injector of
G_{\pi}
is an
\mathfrak{N}_{\pi}
-injector of
G_{\pi}
. Hence, if we can prove that

\pi
-nilpotent injector of

is an
\mathfrak{N}_{\pi}
-injector of

Support that

where

(V_{1})_{\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}}=1.
This means that

and so

is a maximal
\pi
-nilpotent subgroup of

. This shows that the statement of the lemma holds in case 1.

Case 2.
G_{\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}}\neq 1.

Let
G_{1}=G/G_{\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}}.
By Lemma 2.3(2),
(G_{1})_{\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}}=G_{\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}}G_{\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}}/G_{\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}}=1.
Hence by case 1, we have that

are coincide with the set of

Lemma 3.3.

Proof.

H

\psi(p)=\{G\ |\ G\simeq H^{\mathfrak{E}_{p^{\prime}}},{\rm for\ some}\ H\in h_{1}(p)\},

\psi(p)\mathfrak{E}_{p^{\prime}}=h_{1}(p)\mathfrak{E}_{p^{\prime}}.

[formula]

\mathfrak{H}

Lemma 3.4.
Let
\mathfrak{H}=LH(h)
for a full integrated
H
-function

, and

is
\mathfrak{N}^{\pi}

containing

-nilpotent.

Proof.
Assume that
V\in\mathfrak{H}
and
G_{\mathfrak{H}}\subseteq V
. Then
V_{h(p)}\cap G_{\mathfrak{H}}=(G_{\mathfrak{H}})_{h(p)}=G_{h(p)},
and so
[V_{h(p)},G_{\mathfrak{H}}]\subseteq G_{h(p)}.

This implies that
V_{h(p)}\subseteq C_{G}(G_{\mathfrak{H}}/G_{h(p)}),
for all
p\in\pi
.

We first prove that
G_{\mathfrak{H}}/G_{h}=F_{\pi}(G/G_{h}).
Let
F_{\pi}(G/G_{h})=L/G_{h}.
Since
G_{\mathfrak{H}}\in\mathfrak{H}=\bigcap_{p\in\pi}h(p)\mathfrak{E}_{p^{\prime}}\mathfrak{N}_{p}.
and
(G_{\mathfrak{H}})_{h(p)}=G_{h(p)},
we have that
G_{\mathfrak{H}}/G_{h(p)}
is
p
-nilpotent for all
p\in\pi.
Hence
G_{\mathfrak{H}}/G_{h}
is
\pi
-nilpotent. Consequently,
G_{\mathfrak{H}}/G_{h}\leq L/G_{h}
and so
G_{\mathfrak{H}}\leq L.
On the other hand, since
L/G_{h}
is
\pi
-nilpotent, by the isomorphism

L/L_{h(p)}G_{h}\simeq(L/G_{h})/(L_{h(p)}G_{h}/G_{h}),

we have that
L/L_{h(p)}G_{h}\in\mathfrak{E}_{p^{\prime}}\mathfrak{N}_{p}
for all

It follows that
(L/G_{h(p)}/(L_{h(p)}G_{h}/L_{h(p)})\in\mathfrak{E}_{p^{\prime}}\mathfrak{N}_{p}
,
for all
p\in\pi.

In order to prove that
L\leq G_{\mathfrak{H}}
, we only need to prove that
L\in\mathfrak{H}
. But it only need to prove that
L_{h(p)}G_{h}/L_{h(p)}
is a
p^{\prime}
-group for all
p\in\pi
.
Since
G_{h}\unlhd L
, by Lemma 2.3,

G_{h(p)}=(G_{h})_{h(p)}=G_{h}\cap L_{h(p)}\leq L_{h(p)}.

Let
q
be an arbitrary prime in
\pi
and
q\neq p
. Since

G_{h(p)}G_{h(q)}/G_{h(p)}\simeq G_{h(q)}/G_{h(q)}\cap G_{h(p)},

G_{h(p)}G_{h(q)}/G_{h(p)}\simeq G_{h(q)}/(G_{h(q)})_{h(p)}.

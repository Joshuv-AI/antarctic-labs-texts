Thermodynamic phase structure of complex Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev model and charged black hole in deformed JT gravity
Sizheng Cao, Yi-Cheng Rui, Xian-Hui Ge

Abstract
The van der Waals-Maxwell phase transition theory is among the oldest and the most simplest theories describing thermodynamic phase transitions in physics. Studying the complex Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (cSYK) model numerically and deriving a new black hole solution in Jackiw-Telteiboim (JT)gravity analytically, we investigate how these models behave like the van der Waals-Maxwell liquid-gas model. The cSYK model exhibits first order phase transition terminating at the critical point, while the charged black hole in JT gravity shows a clear van der Waals-Maxwell like phase structure. In the absence of charge, the corresponding neutral black hole still has the Hawking-Page phase transition. The black hole solution also provides the first example in JT gravity yielding the van der Waals-Maxwell like phase structure.



Introduction.—

As a concrete microscopic model with low energy holographic dual, the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model, describing
N
Majorana fermions with random
q
-body interactions, has been the focus of much recent attention in both the quantum gravity and the condensed matter literature. The emergence of conformal symmetry, the
AdS_{2}
factor, the absence of quasi-particle excitations and the maximal chaos in the low temperature limit of the SYK model strongly indicates that the SYK model at finite temperature is dual to a black hole solution in two-dimensional JT gravity
SY93
;
kitaev15
;
stanford
;
Jensen2016
. Recently, Maldacena and Qi (MQ) studied two statistically correlated copies of the SYK model with a bilinear coupling term
MQ18
. This coupled SYK model (MQ model) has an interesting phase diagram at finite temperature, which demonstrates the Hawking-Page phase transition between the thermal AdS phase at low temperature and two black holes phase at high temperature. Another interesting aspect of this coupled SYK model is that a new phase emerges in the large
q
limit with a negative specific heat. Notably, the similar first-order phase transition was also observed in melonic quantum mechanics with
U^{2}(n)
symmetry
aze17
;
Ferrari
and Yukawa-SYK model
ysyk1
;
ysyk21
. The complex version of the MQ model was further studied in
2006sahoo
;
tgzhou
;
zhou
and same Hawking-Page phase transition observed there, while no phase transition was observed in mass deformed single-sided SYK model
naso1912
.

The well-known Hawking-Page phase transition discovered in 1983 describes the phase transition between AdS thermal gas (unstable small black hole) to finite temperature neutral black hole (large black hole) in AdS spacetime
HP1983
. Concerned the SYK/JT duality, it is significant to explore the Hawking-Page phase transition in the deformations of JT gravity.
Very recently, Witten considered an arbitrary dilaton potential such that there are black hole solutions asymptotic at infinity to the nearly
AdS_{2}
solutions of the standard JT gravity and further showed that if there is a black hole solution at temperature
T
with negative specific heat, there will be another black hole solution at the same temperature with a positive specific heat and lower free energy
witten
(see also
lala2005
;
Sachdev
). There can be first-order phase transitions similar to the
Hawking-Page transition between them. Nevertheless, there are richer thermodynamic phase structures for a class of charged black holes in AdS spacetime, including structures isomorphic to the van der Waals-Maxwell liquid-gas system
andrew9902
;
rob9904
. Compared with the Hawking-Page phase transition, the appearance of a stable small black hole solution is a new feature when one adds small fixed charge to the Schwarzschild-AdS black hole.

As long as the SYK model has a neutral black hole dual, one has strong motivations to investigate how the cSYK model behaves like a charged black hole in AdS spacetime

footnote1
. This is not only because both of them have globally conserved charge, but also because they may share the same thermodynamic structure. Moreover, the microscopic structure of black holes can be better understood through obtaining an exact charged black hole solution in JT gravity which is dual to the cSYK model.
The aim of this letter is to explore thermodynamic phase transition of cSYK model, present a new charged black hole solution in JT gravity yielding the Van der Waals-Maxwell-like thermodynamical phase structure, and compare these two models.

The model and thermodynamics.—
The cSYK model of fermions is the charge conserving variant of the Majorana SYK model yielding the Hamiltonian

H=\sum^{N}_{i,j,k,l=1}J_{ijkl}c^{\dagger}_{i}c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{k}c_{l}-\mu\sum^{N}_{i}c^{\dagger}_{i}c_{i},

(1)

where
c_{i}
,
c^{\dagger}_{i}
are fermion operators satisfying
\{c_{i},c^{\dagger}_{j}\}=\delta_{ij}
. In the natural units, the model depends on the chemical potential
\mu
and the temperature
T
.
The couplings
J_{ijkl}
are independent random complex Gaussian variables with zero mean and the following variance

\overline{J_{ijkl}}=0,~~~\overline{|J_{ijkl}|^{2}}=\frac{J^{2}}{8N^{3}},

We can define the global conserved
U(1)
charge by

Q=\sum^{N}_{i}(c^{\dagger}_{i}c_{i}-\frac{1}{2}),

which is related to the ultraviolet (UV) asymmetry of the Green function

G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\langle Tc_{i}(\tau)c^{\dagger}_{i}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle,~G(0^{-})=-\frac{1}{2}-\mathcal{Q},~G(0^{+})=\frac{1}{2}-\mathcal{Q},~~~\mathcal{Q}=\frac{\langle Q\rangle}{N}
.

\displaystyle-S_{\rm effective}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(\tau^{\prime},\tau)+\frac{J^{2}}{4}G^{2}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})G^{2}(\tau^{\prime},\tau)\bigg]

Charged black hole solution—

Conclusions and discussions.—
In summary, we studied the thermodynamic phase structures of the microscopic complex SYK model and its possible gravity dual–the charged black holes in JT gravity. On the cSYK side, the free energy-temperature diagram shows a hysteresis structure, demonstrating a first-order phase transition ending at the critical point, analogous to the water-vapor transition.
To compare with such a complex SYK model on the
0+1
-dimensional field theory side, we provide a 2D charged black hole solution in deformed JT gravity, yielding the familiar van der Waals-Maxwell-like phase structure. First-order phase transition is the common feature of both sides, but cSYK model provides microscopic description with non-mean field critical exponents to the water-vapor phase transition diagram, while the black hole model offers the mean field theory picture.

Unlike the
\mu-\mathcal{Q}
diagram of the cSYK model, the isothermal curves for the
\mu-\mathcal{Q}
diagram on the black hole side exhibit inverse dependence of the charge density. That is to say, as the charge density decreases below the critical value, phase transition occurs for black holes. For special case with
b_{0}=\mathcal{Q}^{2}=0
, the black hole thermodynamics phase diagram reduces to the Hawking-Page phase transition.

Interestingly, such a black hole solution can reproduce almost all the physical picture of global RN-AdS black holes with similar thermodynamic phase structure. This may help us understand the microscopic structure of black holes since it has a cSYK dual. Considered the related studies on the competition between chaotic and
nonchaotic phases
KAM
;
garcia17
;
zhangpf
, it would be interesting to investigate the Lyapunov exponent for this charged black hole solution and compare the result with that of cSYK.

I.1
A. Solving Schwinger-Dyson equations numerically

The effective action is given by

\displaystyle-\frac{S_{\rm effective}}{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln{\rm Det}\bigg[(\partial_{\tau}-\mu)\delta-\Sigma\bigg]+\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\bigg[\Sigma(\tau,\tau^{\prime})G(\tau^{\prime},\tau)+\frac{J^{2}}{4}G^{2}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})G^{2}(\tau^{\prime},\tau)\bigg].

(S1)

The time translation invariance of Euclidean correlators
G(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=G(\tau-\tau^{\prime})
allow us recast the action as

\displaystyle-\frac{S_{\rm eff}}{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln{\rm Det}\bigg[(\partial_{\tau}-\mu)\delta-\Sigma\bigg]+\beta\int d\tau\bigg[\Sigma(\tau)G(-\tau)+\frac{J^{2}}{4}G^{2}(\tau)G^{2}(-\tau)\bigg].

(S2)

Utilizing the Fourier transformation

\displaystyle f(\tau)=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\omega_{n}}e^{-i\omega_{n}\tau}f(i\omega_{n}),\quad f(i\omega_{n})=\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau e^{i\omega_{n}}f(\tau),

(S3)

we obtain the action

\displaystyle-\frac{S_{\rm eff}}{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln 2+\sum_{\omega_{n}}\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{-i\omega_{n}-\mu-\Sigma(i\omega_{n})}{-i\omega_{n}}\right)^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\Sigma(i\omega_{n})G(i\omega_{n}).

(S4)

Considering
stanford16
, we renormalized determinant term by the fact
\sum\limits_{\omega_{n}}\ln(i\omega_{n})=\ln 2
to converge the series, which can correctly reproduce the partition function of N noninteracting complex fermion. The equations of motion from
S_{\rm eff}
satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S_{\rm eff}(G,\Sigma)}{\delta\Sigma(i\omega_{n})}=0,\quad\frac{\delta S_{\rm eff}(G,\Sigma)}{\delta G(\tau)}=0,

(S5)

from which we can derive the corresponding saddle point equation, i.e. the Schwinger-Dyson equation

\displaystyle G(i\omega_{n})=\frac{1}{-i\omega_{n}-\mu-\Sigma(i\omega_{n})},~~~\Sigma(\tau)=-J^{2}G^{2}(\tau)G(-\tau).

(S6)

To find the solution for the Schwinger-Dyson equation above, one needs to solve the fixed-point equation as the form
G(i\omega_{n})={f}\left(G(i\omega_{n})\right)
, in this paper we are using the algorithm introduced in
stanford16
and numerically calculate the solution for SD equation. Starting with an assumption for correlator and after a standard numerical procedure, the algorithm can always iterative out another solution by

\displaystyle G^{\rm new}(i\omega_{n})=(1-x)G^{\rm prev}(i\omega_{n})+x\frac{1}{-i\omega_{n}-\mu-\Sigma(i\omega_{n})},

(S7)

where we choose
x=0.05
as the update parameter. At the same time, to initial the iteration we assume the noninteracting solution
G(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}{\rm sgn}(\tau)
to be the solution for green function in the first round iterative procedure, and the coupling strength
J
set to be one. For numerical needs, we discrete the Euclidean time and the Matsubara frequency as

\displaystyle\tau_{m}=\frac{\beta m}{2\Lambda},\ (m=0,1,\dots,2\Lambda-1)\quad{\rm and}\quad\omega_{n}=\frac{2\pi}{\beta}(\omega_{n}+\frac{1}{2}),\ (n=-\Lambda,-\Lambda+1,\dots,\Lambda-1),

(S8)

which is equivalent to introduce a UV cutoff, and for accuracy, most of the time we choose
\Lambda=2^{16}
. After the final iteration is completed, one should evaluate the difference

\displaystyle\Delta G=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\left|G^{\rm new}(i\omega_{n})-G^{\rm prev}(i\omega_{n})\right|.

(S9)

When the difference is below a tolerence limits
\Delta G<\epsilon=10^{-14}
, the algorithm will terminate the iteration. We thus can argue the numerical solution for Green function at this moment has converged. The numerical solution of the Green function
G(\tau)
at different temperatures and chemical potentials is shown in figure S

Note that even in the zero temperature limit, the first-order phase transition exists as shown in figure S

Figure S

shows the inverse temperature as a function of the energy, which can be compared with the
\beta-r_{\textrm{\tiny H}}
diagram of charged black holes.

I.2
B. Thermodynamic quantities of cSYK model

Once we obtain the numerical solution for Euclidean Green function, the energy can be expressed as a functional of Green function. Here we follow the way in
MQ
to obtain the expression of energy. First note that

\displaystyle N\partial_{\tau_{1}}G(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\big|_{\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}=0^{+}}=N\partial_{\tau_{1}}G(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\big|_{\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}=0^{+}}=\sum_{i}\partial_{\tau}\big<c_{i}(\tau)c_{i}^{\dagger}\big>=\sum_{m}\big<[H,c_{m}]c_{m}^{\dagger}\big>,

(S10)

where the Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H=H_{\rm SYK}+H_{0}=\sum_{ij;kl}J_{ij;kl}c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{k}c_{l}-\mu\sum_{i}c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i}.

(S11)

After calculating the commutator
[H,c_{m}]
and intert the result into (
S10
), we have

\displaystyle N\partial_{\tau}G(\tau)\big|_{\tau=0^{+}}=\big<qH_{\rm SYK}+H_{0}\big>,

(S12)

with some additional terms, we can obtain the relation bewteen Green functions and energy

\displaystyle N\partial_{\tau}G(\tau)\big|_{\tau=0^{+}}-\mu(q-1)\big<Q+\frac{N}{2}\big>=\big<qH_{\rm SYK}+H_{0}+(q-1)H_{0}\big>=q\big<H\big>,

(S13)

which means the average energy is

\displaystyle\frac{E}{N}=\frac{1}{q}\big[\partial_{\tau}G(\tau)\big|_{\tau=0^{+}}-\mu(q-1)(\mathcal{Q}+\frac{1}{2})\big],

(S14)

where
\mathcal{Q}
is the
U(1)
conserved charge density satisfying the relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}\equiv\frac{Q}{N}=-\frac{1}{2}[G(0^{+})+G(0^{-})].

(S15)

For the purpose of numerical calculations, we need to evaluate the first term in (
S14
).First, the saddle-point equation in our model is

\displaystyle(\partial_{\tau}-\mu)G(\tau)-\int_{0}^{\beta}\Sigma(\tau-\tilde{\tau})G(\tilde{\tau})d\tilde{\tau}=-\delta_{\beta}(\tau),

(S16)

which means

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}G(\tau)=\mu G(\tau)+\int_{0}^{\beta}\Sigma(\tau-\tilde{\tau})G(\tilde{\tau})d\tilde{\tau}+\delta_{\beta}(\tau).

(S17)

Taking the limit
\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}
and the using the expression for
\mathcal{Q}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}G(\tau)\big|_{\tau=0^{+}}=\mu(\frac{1}{2}-\mathcal{Q})+\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\Sigma(i\omega_{n})G(i\omega_{n}).

(S18)

Finally we obtain the numerical computable formula for the energy

\displaystyle\frac{E}{N}=\frac{1}{q}\big[4\mu T\sum_{\omega_{n}}{\rm Re}[G(i\omega_{n})]-\mu+T\sum_{\omega_{n}}\Sigma(i\omega_{n})G(i\omega_{n})\big],

(S19)

where we used the Dirichlet trick to ensure that the series converges

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=-\frac{1}{2}[G(0^{+})+G(0^{-})]=-T\sum_{\omega_{n}}Re[G(i\omega_{n})].

(S20)

After substituting the saddle point solution in (
S4
) we obtain the free energy

\displaystyle\frac{F}{N}=-T\left[\ln 2+\sum_{\omega_{n}}\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{-i\omega_{n}-\mu-\Sigma(i\omega_{n})}{-i\omega_{n}}\right)^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\sum_{\omega_{n}}\Sigma(i\omega_{n})G(i\omega_{n})\right].

(S21)

D. Black hole thermodynamics in deformed JT gravity

In this section, we briefly review the black hole thermodynamics for the deformed JT gravity. Related discussions can also be found in
witten20
;
hesong18
.
We start with the bulk action in Euclidean signature

\displaystyle S^{E}_{JT}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{E}_{\rm top}+S^{E}_{\rm bulk}+S^{E}_{\rm GHY}+S^{E}_{\rm ct}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\phi_{0}}{2}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{2}x\sqrt{g}R+2\int_{\partial{M}}\sqrt{h}Kd\tau\bigg)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\bigg(\phi R+\frac{V(\phi)}{l^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}Z(\phi)\mathcal{F}^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{\partial{M}}\sqrt{h}\phi Kd\tau+\int_{\partial{M}}\sqrt{h}\mathcal{L}_{\rm ct}d\tau,

where
h=f(r_{\infty})
is the induced metric and
K
is the extrinsic curvature of the boundary, explicitly
K=\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{\infty})}{2\sqrt{f(r_{\infty})}}
. The
\phi_{0}
terms in the action (
I.4
) are generated from the dimension reduction of the RN black hole and
\phi_{0}=Q^{2}_{\rm rn}/2
with
Q_{\rm rn}
the charge of RN black hole.
Considered the fact that

\lim_{\phi\rightarrow\infty}\int^{\phi}(\frac{V(\phi^{\prime})}{l^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}Z(\phi)F^{2})d\phi^{\prime}\rightarrow\infty~,

(S29)

the boundary counter term
\mathcal{L}_{\rm ct}
should be of the form

\mathcal{L}_{\rm ct}(\phi)=\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}-\mathcal{Q}^{2}r}+\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}r}{\sqrt{|f(\phi)|}}.

(S30)

The bulk on-shell action can be evaluated as

\displaystyle S^{E}_{\rm bulk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\bigg(\phi R+\frac{V(\phi)}{l^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}Z(\phi)\mathcal{F}^{2}\bigg)

(S31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\bigg(-\frac{\phi V^{\prime}(\phi)}{2l^{2}}+\frac{V(\phi)}{l^{2}}+\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{2Z(\phi)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta\phi V(\phi)}{2l^{2}}\bigg|^{\phi_{\infty}}_{\phi_{H}}-\beta J(\phi)\bigg|^{\phi_{\infty}}_{\phi_{H}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\beta\mathcal{Q}^{2}\phi}{Z(\phi)}\bigg|^{\phi_{\infty}}_{\phi_{H}},

where
J(\phi)=\int\frac{V(\phi)}{l^{2}}d\phi
. The Gibbons-Hawking-York term can be evaluated as

\displaystyle S^{E}_{\rm GHY}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\sqrt{h}\phi Kd\tau=-\frac{\beta}{2}\phi(r_{\infty})\bigg(\frac{V(r_{\infty})}{l^{2}}-\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{Z(\phi_{\infty})}\bigg)

(S32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta\phi_{\infty}V(\phi_{\infty})}{2l^{2}}+\frac{\beta\phi_{\infty}\mathcal{Q}^{2}}{2Z(\phi_{\infty})}.

The counter term reads

S^{E}_{\rm ct}=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\sqrt{h}\mathcal{L}_{\rm ct}d\tau=-\beta M+\beta J(\phi_{\infty}),

(S33)

where the mass
M
is defined as
M=J(\phi_{H})/2
.
The total on-shell action is then given by

\displaystyle S^{E}_{\rm on-shell}=S^{E}_{\rm bulk}+S^{E}_{\rm GHY}+S^{E}_{\rm ct}=-\beta M+\beta J(\phi_{H})-\frac{\beta\phi_{H}V(\phi_{H})}{2l^{2}}+\frac{\beta\mathcal{Q}^{2}\phi_{H}}{2Z(\phi_{H})}.

(S34)

The free energy can be read off from the on-shell action

(S35)

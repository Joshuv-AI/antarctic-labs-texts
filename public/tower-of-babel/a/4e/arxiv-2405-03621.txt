Uniform bounds for fields of definition in projective spaces
Giulio Bresciani

Abstract
. We give a positive answer to a question of J. Doyle and J. Silverman about fields of definition of dynamical systems on ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} . We prove that, for fixed n n , there exists a constant C n C_{n} such that every dynamical system ℙ n → ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n}\to\mathbb{P}^{n} is defined over an extension of degree ≤ C n \leq C_{n} of the field of moduli. More generally, the same bound works for any kind of “algebraic structure” defined over ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} , such as embedded curves, hypersurfaces, algebraic cycles. As a consequence we prove that, if x ∈ X ⁡ ( k ) x\in X(k) is a rational point of an n n -dimensional variety with quotient singularities, there exists a field extension k ′ / k k^{\prime}/k of degree ≤ C n − 1 \leq C_{n-1} such that x x lifts to a k ′ k^{\prime} -rational point of any resolution of singularities.



We work over a field
k
of characteristic
0
with algebraic closure

Consider a variety

over

,

,

,

,

,

,

In the case of a dynamical system

Theorem 1

Fix

C_{n}
such that, for every algebraic structure

The constant

In our joint paper with A. Vistoli
[

The

]

2.
Group theoretic bounds

We are going to need some group-theoretic bounds. We start with a simple bound about abelian groups.

Lemma 3
.

Let
n,d
be integers, and let
a_{d}
be the number of abelian groups of degree
\leq d
up to isomorphism. For every finite abelian group
A
of rank at most
n
, there are at most
d^{n}a_{d}
subgroups
B\subset A
with
|A|\leq d|B|
, i.e. of index at most
d
.

Proof.

For every abelian group
H
of degree
\leq d
there are at most
d^{n}
homomorphisms
A\to H
. The statement follows.
∎

Next, we need the following theorem of C. Jordan.

Theorem 4

(C. Jordan,
[
Jor78
]

[
CR62
, Theorem 36.13]
)
.

Fix
n>0
a positive integer. There exists a constant
b_{n}
such that every finite subgroup
G
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
has an abelian, normal subgroup
A\subset G
of index at most
b_{n}
.

We say that a subgroup
G\subset\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(K)
is diagonalizable if its inverse image in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
is diagonalizable. Equivalently,
G
is diagonalizable if it is the image of a diagonalizable subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K)
.

As a consequence of Jordan’s theorem, we prove the following.

Corollary 5
.

Fix
n>0
a positive integer. There exists a constant
c_{n}
such that every finite subgroup
G
of
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
has a diagonalizable, characteristic subgroup of index at most
c_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
G\subset\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a finite subgroup,
G^{\prime}\subset\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
its inverse image in
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Let
A^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
be the normal, abelian subgroup given by Jordan’s theorem
4
, it is diagonalizable since this is true for every finite, abelian subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Denote by
A\subset G
the image of
A^{\prime}
; by construction,
A\subset G
is a normal, diagonalizable subgroup of index
d\leq c_{n}
. Let us show that we can extract from
A
a characteristic subgroup of
G
of bounded index.

Consider the set
S=\{H\subset G\mid H=\phi(A),~\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\}
of subgroups of
G
of the form
\phi(A)
for some automorphism
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
, we want to bound the size of
S
.

Given an element
H\in S
, notice that the index of
H\cap A
in
A
is equal to the index of
H\cap A
in
H
, which in turn is at most
d
since
H\cap A
is the kernel of
H\to G/A
. Furthermore, notice that
H
is normal as well, so that
H\cap A
is normal in
G
.

Let
S_{1}
be the set of images of homomorphisms
A\to G/A
, and
S_{2}
the set of subgroups of
A
of index
\leq d
which are normal in
G
, we have a natural map
S\to S_{1}\times S_{2}
given by
H\mapsto(H/(H\cap A),H\cap A)
. Bounding the size of
S
reduces to bounding the sizes of
S_{1},S_{2}
and of the fibers of
S\to S_{1}\times S_{2}
.

First,
|S_{1}|
is bounded by the number of homomorphisms
A\to G/A
, which in turn is bounded by
d^{n}
since
A
has rank
\leq n
. Second,
|S_{2}|\leq d^{n}a_{d}
by Lemma
3
. Finally, the fibers of
S\to S_{1}\times S_{2}
have at most
d^{n}
elements. In fact, let
G^{\prime}/A\in S_{1}
be the image of an homomorphism
A\to G/A
(where
G^{\prime}
is the inverse image in
G
of the subgroup of
G/A
), and
A^{\prime}\in S_{2}
be a subgroup of
A
of index at most
d
which is normal in
G
. An element
H\in S
is in the fiber of
(G^{\prime}/A,A^{\prime})\in S_{1}\times S_{2}
if it maps surjectively on
G^{\prime}/A
and satisfies
H\cap A=A^{\prime}
. Such a subgroup is identified by the induced section

G^{\prime}/A\simeq H/A^{\prime}\to G^{\prime}/A^{\prime}

of
G^{\prime}/A^{\prime}\to G^{\prime}/A
, and there are at most
d^{n}
such sections because
G^{\prime}/A
is an abelian group of rank
\leq n
(it’s a quotient of
A
) and the kernel
A/A^{\prime}
has degree at most
d
. It follows that
|S|\leq d^{3n}a_{d}
.

Define

A_{c}\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}\bigcap_{\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)}\phi(A)=\bigcap_{H\in S}H=\ker(G\to\prod_{H\in S}G/H)

as the intersection of all the subgroups in
S
. By construction,
A_{c}
is a characteristic subgroup. Since
|S|\leq d^{3n}\cdot a_{d}
, then
\prod_{H\in S}G/H
has at most
d^{d^{3n}\cdot a_{d}}
elements, hence the index of
A_{c}
in
G
is at most
d^{d^{3n}\cdot a_{d}}
. We can then choose

c_{n}\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}b_{n}^{b_{n}^{3n}\cdot a_{b_{n}}}.

∎

3.
Proof of Theorems
1
and
2

We now want to prove Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
. We will prove them at the same time by induction on

(n-1)
-th step of Theorem
1
is used to prove the
n
-th step of Theorem

, which in turn is used to prove the
n
-th step of Theorem

, and so forth. The cases

. Assume

.

First, let us recall a few definitions from
[

]
.

Definition 6

(
[

, Definition 6.6]
)
.

A rational point

of one (and hence all, by Lang-Nishimura

) resolution of singularities

.

Definition 7

(
[

, §6.4, Proposition 6.5]
)

An extension

has at least one

Proposition 8
.

Assume that Theorem
1
holds in dimension

. Then Theorem
2
holds in dimension

.

Proof.

The base change of the singularity
(X,x)
to

is equivalent to
(\mathbb{A}^{n}/G,[0])
, where

is the local fundamental group of the singularity
[
BV24
, Corollary 6.4]
. Let
\hat{X}\to X
be the minimal stack of

[
BV24
, §6.1]
, the reduced fiber over

of the singularity
[
BV24
, Definition 6.2]
. The base change of
\mathcal{G}
to
K
is the classifying stack
\mathcal{B}_{K}G
, and

is liftable if and only if
\mathcal{G}(k)\neq\emptyset

[
BV24
, Proposition 6.5]
. Denote by
\mathcal{N}
the normal bundle of
\mathcal{G}
in
\hat{X}
, it is a vector bundle over
\mathcal{G}
of rank

whose base change to

.

Consider the inertia stack

of

, it is a relative group sheaf with an induced action on
\mathcal{N}
. Denote by
\Delta\subset\mathrm{I}
the subgroup sheaf of elements acting by scalar multiplication on the vector bundle
\mathcal{N}
, and let
\bar{G}=\mathcal{G}/\Delta
the rigidification of
\mathcal{G}
by

[

, Appendix C]
. By construction, the rigidification

is a gerbe over

whose base change to

is the classifying stack
\mathcal{B}_{K}\bar{G}
, where

is the image of
G
in
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(K)
.

Let
E
be the associated variety of

[
BV24
, Definition 6.8]
, i.e. the coarse moduli space of the projectivization
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N})
; it is equipped with a non-canonical rational map
E\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}

[
BV24
, Corollary 6.9]
. Consider the composition
E\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}\to\bar{\mathcal{G}}
; by construction, its base change to

is the rational map
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}_{K}/\bar{G}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{B}_{K}\bar{G}
associated with the projection
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}_{K}\to\mathbb{P}^{n-1}_{K}/\bar{G}
. By
[
Bre23
, Theorem 2]
, this defines an algebraic structure
\xi
on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}_{K}
with automorphism group
\bar{G}
, field of moduli

and residual gerbe
\bar{G}
.

Since we are assuming that Theorem
1
holds for
n-1
, there exists a finite extension

of degree
\leq C_{n-1}
such that the algebraic structure

is defined over

. By
[
Bre23
, Theorem 2]
again, the fact that

descends to

implies that
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}_{K}\to\mathbb{P}^{n-1}_{K}/\bar{G}
descends to a ramified covering

, where

is the Brauer–Severi variety to which

descends. However, Theorem

does not only guarantee that

descends to

, it also guarantees that

descends to
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}_{k^{\prime}}
. This means that we may choose

, and hence we get that the

-rational points of

are dense. Since we have a rational map

, this implies that

is liftable.
∎

Proposition 9
.

Assume that Theorem
1
holds in dimension
\leq n-1
. Then Theorem
1
holds in dimension
n
.

Proof.

By Proposition
8
, we know that Theorem
2
holds in dimension
\leq n
. Up to replacing the constants
C_{1},\dots,C_{n-1}
, we may assume
C_{1}\leq C_{2}\leq\dots\leq C_{n-1}
. Furthermore, up to replacing
k
with
k_{\xi}
, we may assume that
k
is the field of moduli.

Let
G=\operatorname{Aut}(\xi)\subset\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}(K)
be the automorphism group of
\xi
, by assumption it is finite. Let
\mathcal{G}
over
k
be the residual gerbe c.f.
[
BV24
, §3.1]
, it is a finite gerbe classifying twisted forms of
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K},\xi)
, and its base change to
K
is the classifying stack
\mathcal{B}_{K}G
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{G}
the universal family c.f.
[
BV24
, §5.1]
, it is an
n
-dimensional projective bundle with the property that, if
(P,\eta)
is a twisted form over
k
of
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K},\xi)
associated with a morphism
s:\operatorname{Spec}k\to\mathcal{G}
, then
P
identifies with the fibered product
\mathcal{P}\times_{\mathcal{G}}\operatorname{Spec}k
. In general,
P
will be a Brauer–Severi variety over
k
, and
P\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
if and only if
s:\operatorname{Spec}k\to\mathcal{G}
lifts to
\mathcal{P}
. It follows that
\xi
descends to an algebraic structure on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
if and only if
\mathcal{P}(k)\neq\emptyset
.

Denote by
\mathbf{P}
the compression of
\xi
c.f.
[
BV24
, Definition 5.3]
, it is the coarse moduli space of
\mathcal{P}
and it is a twisted form of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}/G
over the field of moduli

. The base change to
K
of the structural map
\mathcal{P}\to\mathbf{P}
is the projection
[\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}/G]\to\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}/G
; since the action of
G
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
is faithful, this map is birational, and we get a birational inverse
\mathbf{P}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{P}
. By
[
BV23
, Theorem 4.1]
, this implies that
\mathcal{P}(k)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\mathbf{P}
has a liftable
k
-rational point.

Because of this, to prove the statement it is enough to find liftable
k^{\prime}
-points on
\mathbf{P}_{k^{\prime}}
, with
k^{\prime}/k
of bounded degree.

Let
A\subset G
be the diagonalizable subgroup of index at most
c_{n+1}
given by Corollary
5
. There are three cases: either
G
is trivial, or
G
is non-trivial and
A
is trivial, or
A
is non-trivial.

Case 1:
G
is trivial.

If
G
is trivial, then
\mathbf{P}
is a Brauer–Severi variety over
k
of dimension
n
, i.e. a twisted form of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
. Such a variety is always split by a field extension of degree at most
n+1

[
GS17
, Theorem 2.4.3, Proposition 4.5.4, Theorem 5.2.1]
. Hence, choosing
C_{n}\geq n+1
is sufficient for the case in which
G
is trivial.

Case 2:
G
is non-trivial,
A
is trivial.

If
G
is non-trivial but
A
is trivial, then
G
has cardinality at most
c_{n+1}
. Choose
g\in G
any non-trivial element and
g_{0}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(K)
a lifting of
g
. Notice that
g^{k}=\lambda\operatorname{Id}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda\in K
since
G
is finite, hence
g_{0}
is diagonalizable. The eigenspaces of
g_{0}
only depend on
g
(and not on the choice of
g_{0}
), hence the projective eigenspaces of

in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
are well defined. Since

is non-trivial,
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
is not a projective eigenspace.

Choose
1\leq m\leq n-1
an integer such that

has
m
-dimensional projective eigenspaces, and denote by

the union of all

-dimensional eigenspaces of the elements of

of the form

for some

; the irreducible components of

are

-dimensional projective subspaces, and their number is at most

.

Clearly,
Z
is distinguished in the sense of
[
Bre23
, §7]
, hence

descends to a closed subset
\mathbf{Z}\subset\mathbf{P}
. Notice that

has at most
(n+1)\cdot c_{n+1}
irreducible components as well. Choose
W\subset Z
an
m
-dimensional eigenspace of
g
, and consider
\mathbf{W}_{0}\subset\mathbf{Z}
its image in
\mathbf{Z}
. By construction,
\mathbf{W}_{0,K}
has at most
n\cdot c_{n+1}
irreducible components, hence there exists a field extension

of degree at most
(n+1)\cdot c_{n+1}
such that the image
\mathbf{W}\subset\mathbf{P}_{k^{\prime}}
of
W
in
\mathbf{P}_{k^{\prime}}
is geometrically irreducible.

Let
G_{W}
be the subquotient of elements of

mapping

. By
[

,

is the compression of an algebraic structure on

with automorphism group

. By inductive hypothesis, there exists an extension

of degree at most

such that
\mathbf{W}_{k^{\prime\prime}}
has a liftable

-rational point

. By Proposition

, there exists a finite extension

of degree at most

such that

is liftable
as a point of
\mathbf{P}_{k^{\prime\prime\prime}}
(and not only as a point of
\mathbf{W}_{k^{\prime\prime\prime}}
). Since

[k^{\prime\prime\prime}:k]=[k^{\prime\prime\prime}:k^{\prime\prime}]\cdot[k^{\prime\prime}:k^{\prime}]\cdot[k^{\prime}:k]\leq C_{n-1}\cdot C_{n-1}\cdot(n+1)\cdot c_{n+1},

it is sufficient to choose
C_{n}\geq(n+1)c_{n+1}C_{n-1}^{2}
for the case in which
G
is non-trivial but
A
is trivial.

Case 3:
A
is non-trivial.

Since
A
is diagonalizable, its projective eigenspaces are well defined. Furthermore, since
A
is non-trivial,
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
is not a projective eigenspace. Choose
1\leq m\leq n-1
an integer such that
A
has
m
-dimensional projective eigenspaces, and denote by
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}
the union of all
m
-dimensional eigenspaces of
A
. Since
A
is characteristic, then
Z
is distinguished in the sense of
[
Bre23
, §7]
. Notice that
Z
has at most
n+1
irreducible components. The rest of the proof of this case is identical to the previous one, with the exception that the constant
(n+1)\cdot c_{n+1}
is replaced by
n+1
. Hence, choosing
C_{n}\geq(n+1)\cdot C_{n-1}^{2}
is sufficient for the case in which
A
is non-trivial

To sum up, since clearly
(n+1)\cdot c_{n+1}\cdot C_{n-1}^{2}\geq(n+1)\cdot C_{n-1}^{2}\geq n+1
, the statement holds for

C_{n}\mathrel{\smash{\overset{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle def}}{=}}}(n+1)\cdot c_{n+1}\cdot C_{n-1}^{2}.

∎

Remark 10
.

It is possible repeat the arguments above more explicitly and obtain an explicit upper bound for
C_{n}
, using estimates for the constants
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
of Lemma
3
and Theorem
4
such as
[
ES34
]
and
[
Col07
]
. However, this upper bound is going to be wildly large, something similar to

(n+1)!^{(n+1)!^{(n+1)!}}.

We do not claim that this is actually an upper bound, we just want to give an idea of the result of such a computation.

We think that trying to formalize this upper bound is mostly pointless, for two reasons. The first reason is that the proofs above do not try in any way to be optimal, and frequently use larger but simpler bounds instead of smaller and more complex ones. More importantly, we think that any attempt to obtain a reasonable estimate should change strategy completely, rather than trying to optimize the one given above. Let us explain this.

In the proof of Theorem
1
, we used the constants
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
to take care of the most complex finite subgroups of
\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
. However, there is a common, counterintuitive phenomenon in the study of fields of moduli: for a variety with a structure
(X,\xi)
, the more
\operatorname{Aut}(X,\xi)
is complex, the more likely it is that
(X,\xi)
is defined over the field of moduli, see e.g.
[
Hug07
]
,
[
Bre24b
]
,
[
Brea
]
. We think that any attempt to obtain a reasonable estimate should try to leverage this phenomenon in order to take care of the most complex automorphism groups (instead of simply blowing up the bounds, as we do), and obtain sharp estimates for the less complex ones, e.g. the abelian ones.

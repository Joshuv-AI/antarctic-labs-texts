Pointed vortex loops in ideal 2D fluids
Ioana Ciuclea, Cornelia Vizman

Abstract
We study a special kind of singular vorticities in ideal 2D fluids that combine features of point vortices and vortex sheets, namely pointed vortex loops. We focus on the coadjoint orbits of the area-preserving diffeomorphism group of ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} determined by them. We show that a polarization subgroup consists of diffeomorphisms that preserve the loop as a set, thus the configuration space is the space of loops that enclose a fixed area, without information on vorticity distribution and attached points.

Introduction

Euler’s equations
\dot{\bf v}+\nabla_{\bf v}{\bf v}=-\nabla p
,
\operatorname{div}{\bf v}=0
, for
{\bf v}
the fluid velocity and
p
the pressure,
are geodesic equations on the volume preserving diffeomorphism group
[
1
,
4
]
.
In particular the vorticity
\operatorname{curl}{\bf v}
is confined to a coadjoint orbit of this group.

Among singular vorticities for ideal fluids in 2D, the point vortices
have been much studied (see
[
2
]
for a survey).
In
[
16
]
they are set in the framework of momentum maps:
point vortices evolve in coadjoint orbits of the area preserving diffeomorphism group.
These consist of
k
-tuples of distinct points
in the plane, and the Kostant-Kirillov-Souriau (KKS) symplectic form
is a linear combination of the area forms weighted with the vorticities (circulations)
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{k}
.
If some of the
\Gamma_{i}
’s coincide, then the quotient by a permutation subgroup of
k
elements has to be taken
(Proposition
2.1
).

Another kind of singular vorticity is the vortex sheet
[
3
]
,
e.g. the vortex loop: a pair
(C,\beta)
with
C
a closed curve and
\beta
its vorticity density (strength), assumed to be nowhere zero
[
9
]
.
The corresponding coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\textsf{w}}
of the area preserving diffeomorphism group
consists of curves that enclose a fixed area
a
, endowed with vorticity densities of fixed total vorticity
\textsf{w}=\int_{C}\beta
(Theorem
2.2
).
One can view these spaces as isodrastic leaves in the space of weighted Lagrangian submanifolds, showed in
[
20
]

to be coadjoint orbits of the Hamiltonian group.
Another way to get the same coadjoint orbits is by using results from
[
7
]
on coadjoint orbits of the volume preserving diffeomorphism group consisting of vortex sheets,
results that provide also the prequantization condition
a\textsf{w}\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
of the KKS symplectic form (see also
[
8
]
).
Here we obtain the same coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\textsf{w}}
by symplectic reduction on one of the legs of the ideal fluid dual pair
due to Marsden and Weinstein
[
16
]
.

In this article we study a different type of singular vorticities, that combine features of point vortices and vortex loops, called
pointed vortex loops.
A complete set of invariants for the natural action of the area preserving diffeomorphism group
on pointed vortex loops are the enclosed area
a
and the
k
partial vorticities

\textsf{w}_{i}=\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{i+1}}\beta
along the loop, with total vorticity
\textsf{w}_{1}+\dots\textsf{w}_{k}=\textsf{w}
.
The corresponding coadjoint orbit can be identified with the space of embeddings
that enclose a fixed area, possibly factorized by a permutation subgroup of
k
elements, depending on the rotational symmetry of the data,

(\Gamma_{i})
and
(\textsf{w}_{i})
(Theorem
3.8
).
The KKS symplectic form is exact.
At the pointed vortex loop
(C,\beta,(x_{i}))
it can be written in canonical form, with a well chosen non-degenerate pairing between

C^{\infty}(C)
and its subspace
C_{0}^{\infty}(C)
of zero integral functions (with respect to the volume form on
C
induced by the Euclidean metric).

In
[
9
]
is shown that, within the framework of the 2D Euler equations, point vortices cannot be consistently quantized,
while vortex loops admit natural polarizations.
The resulting configuration space is the space of loops enclosing a fixed area, without information about the vorticity distribution.
In the same article the authors attach vortex dipoles to the point vortices,
so that the additional degrees of freedom allow for a polarization.
We show that pointed vortex loops also have natural polarizations.
A polarization subgroup consists of diffeomorphisms that preserve the loop as a set,
so the configuration space is the space of loops that enclose a fixed area,
without information about the vorticity distribution and the attached points.

2
Singular vorticities

In this section we describe coadjoint orbits for two types of singular vorticities for ideal fluids in 2D: point vortices and vortex loops.

We denote by
\omega
the canonical area form on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(a symplectic form) and by
G
the group of compactly supported area preserving diffeomorphisms, which coincides with
\operatorname{Ham}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, the compactly supported Hamiltonian diffeomorphism group.
Its Lie algebra, denoted by
\mathfrak{g}
, consists of compactly supported divergence free vector fields
(the same as compactly supported Hamiltonian vector fields in this case).
It can be identified with the Lie algebra
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and its
dual
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with the space of distributions.

Euler’s equations in the plane,
\dot{\bf v}+\nabla_{\bf v}{\bf v}=-\nabla p
,
\operatorname{div}{\bf v}=0
, for
{\bf v}
the fluid velocity and
p
the pressure,
are the geodesic equations on the group
G
endowed with the right invariant
L^{2}
metric
[
1
]
.
Here
{\bf v}
lives in
\mathfrak{g}
, while the vorticity
\operatorname{curl}{\bf v}

is confined to a coadjoint orbit in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
Beside the smooth (regular) vorticities, non-smooth (singular) vorticities were widely considered.
These are confined to coadjoint orbits too (see for instance
[
13
]
).
In 2D one has point vortices, with zero dimensional support, and vortex loops, with 1-dimensional support (i.e. vortex sheets).

Point vortices.

The configuration space of
k
ordered points in the plane is

{\operatorname{Conf}_{k}}=(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{k}\setminus\Delta_{k},

(1)

where
\Delta_{k}=\{(x_{1},...,x_{k})\in(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{k}:x_{i}=x_{j}\text{ for some }i\neq j\}
is the fat diagonal.
The non-zero vorticities (circulations)
\Gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
for
i=1,\dots k
, define a symplectic form on
\operatorname{Conf}_{k}

\omega^{\Gamma}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}p_{i}^{*}\omega,\quad p_{i}:\operatorname{Conf}_{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(2)

The natural action of
\operatorname{Ham}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

is transitive and Hamiltonian with equivariant momentum map
[
16
]

J:\operatorname{Conf}_{k}\to C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{*},\quad J(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}.

(3)

In the generic case, when all vorticities are distinct, the map
J
is one-to-one onto a coadjoint orbit and the KKS symplectic form on the coadjoint orbit satisfies

J^{*}\omega_{KKS}=\omega^{\Gamma}
.
If some of the vorticities coincide, let’s say
\Gamma_{i_{1}}=\dots=\Gamma_{i_{k^{\prime}}}
, then the permutation group
\mathcal{S}_{k^{\prime}}
of the set of indices
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k^{\prime}}\}
acts on the configuration space while preserving the symplectic form
\omega^{\Gamma}
,
and we have to quotient out this action to ensure the injectivity of
J
.
In the most symmetric case, when all vorticities are equal, factorization by the whole permutation group
\mathcal{S}_{k}
is needed,
and the coadjoint orbit is the configuration space of
k
unordered points in the plane.
The general case is described in the following proposition:

Proposition 2.1

Let
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{k}
be fixed vorticities,
let
K
be the partition of
\{1,\dots,k\}
that corresponds to equal vorticities,
and let

\mathcal{S}_{K}:=\mathcal{S}_{k_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathcal{S}_{k_{m}}

be the subgroup of
\mathcal{S}_{k}
that consists of all permutations that preserve the partition
K
.
Then the action of
\mathcal{S}_{K}
on the configuration space
\operatorname{Conf}_{k}
preserves the symplectic form
\omega^{\Gamma}
in (
2
)
and the quotient space
\operatorname{Conf}_{k}/\mathcal{S}_{K}
inherits a symplectic form
{\bar{\omega}}^{\Gamma}
.
The momentum map for the Hamiltonian action of
\operatorname{Ham}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\bar{J}:\operatorname{Conf}_{k}/\mathcal{S}_{K}\to C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{*},\quad\bar{J}([x_{1},\dots,x_{k}])=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}\delta_{x_{i}},

is one-to-one onto a coadjoint orbit and the KKS symplectic form on the coadjoint orbit satisfies

{\bar{J}}^{*}\omega_{KKS}={\bar{\omega}}^{\Gamma}
.

Vortex loops.

We call a vortex loop any weighted curve
(C,\beta)
, with
C
a closed curve in the plane endowed with a nowhere zero vorticity density
\beta\in\Omega^{1}(C)
,
assumed to be a volume form.
We consider the orientation of the curve induced by
\beta
, so the total vorticity
\textsf{w}=\int_{C}\beta
is positive.
This a special type of vortex sheet in two dimensions
[
3
]
. A formulation within the groupoid framework can be found in
[
12
]
.

Each such pair
(C,\beta)
corresponds to a unique non-smooth element of the dual
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
,

\langle(C,\beta),X_{h}\rangle=\int_{C}h\beta,\quad h\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

(4)

where the compactly supported Hamiltonian vector field
X_{h}\in\mathfrak{g}
is identified with its unique compactly supported hamiltonian function
h
.
The enclosed area and the total vorticity are a complete set of invariants of the coadjoint action

\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{\varphi}(C,\beta)=(\varphi(C),\varphi_{*}\beta)
for all
\varphi\in G
(see Theorem
2.2
below).

The ideal fluid dual pair, due to Marsden and Weinstein
[
16
]
(and further studied in
[
5
]
), has a low dimensional version
that captures vortex loops, which we present below. The manifold
\operatorname{Emb}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})

carries a symplectic form naturally defined with the volume form
\mu=\frac{\textsf{w}}{2\pi}dt

on
S^{1}
and the symplectic form
\omega=d\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

\Omega_{f}(u_{f},v_{f})=\int_{S^{1}}\omega(u_{f},v_{f})\mu,\quad u_{f},v_{f}:S^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(5)

The commuting Hamiltonian actions of
\operatorname{Ham}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
from the left and
\operatorname{Rot}(S^{1})
from the right
admit equivariant momentum maps:

J_{L}(f)=f_{*}\mu\text{ and }J_{R}(f)=\int_{S^{1}}f^{*}\nu
the area enclosed by the image of
f
.
They form a symplectic dual pair

\mathfrak{g}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle J_{L}}}{{\longleftarrow}}(\operatorname{Emb}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2}),\Omega)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle J_{R}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{R},

which means that the distributions
\ker TJ_{L}
and
\ker TJ_{R}

are symplectic orthogonal complements of one another
[
19
]
.
A slightly stronger fact holds here: each of the two groups acts transitively on level sets of the other group’s momentum map.
Thus the orbits are symplectic complements of one another (this type of actions are called mutually completely orthogonal in
[
15
]
).

For such dual pairs of momentum maps, a general principle says that symplectic reduction on one leg yields coadjoint orbits for the other group.
Symplectic reduction at zero in the ideal fluid dual pair has been already used in
[
6
]
to obtain
coadjoint orbits of the Hamiltonian group, orbits that consist of weighted isotropic submanifolds.
The symplectically reduced manifold for the right action at non-zero
a\in\mathbb{R}
, which is

(\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})/\operatorname{Rot}(S^{1}),\Omega_{a}^{\textsf{w}})

(6)

can be realized via
J_{L}
as a coadjoint orbit of
G
.
It is in one-to-one correspondence with the space

\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\textsf{w}}
of all vortex loops
(C,\beta)
with total vorticity
\int_{C}\beta=\textsf{w}
and the enclosed area
\int_{C}\nu=a
.
More precisely,

\Psi:\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})/\operatorname{Rot}(S^{1})\to\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\textsf{w}},\quad\Psi([f])=(f(S^{1}),f_{*}\mu)

(7)

is a bijection that intertwines the natural
G
action
\varphi\cdot[f]=[\varphi\circ f]
with the
\operatorname{Ad}^{*}
action.

The same result can be deduced by adapting to the case of curves in the plane the results from

[
20
]
(see also
[
14
]
), where isodrastic leaves in the space of weighted Lagrangian submanifolds are shown to be
coadjoint orbits of the Hamiltonian group,
The name isodrast refers to the same action:
the action integral around loops in Lagrangian submanifolds is preserved under isodrastic deformations.
In this 2D setting,
the isodrasts of the space of weighted 1-dimensional Lagrangian submanifolds of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with total weight
w
are the coadjoint orbits
\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\textsf{w}}

with
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

A third way to arrive to these coadjoint orbits is by adapting to dimension two the results from
[
7
]
on vortex sheets, i.e. singular vorticities of codimension one (see also
[
8
]
).
One also gets which of the coadjoint orbits
(\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\textsf{w}},\Omega_{a}^{\textsf{w}})
in (
6
) are prequantizable:
those that satisfy the Onsager-Feynman prequantization condition
a\textsf{w}\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
(see also
[
9
]
).

We summarize these facts in the following theorem:

Theorem 2.2

[
20
,
14
,
7
]

The space
\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\textsf{w}}
of vortex loops with enclosed area
a
and total weight
w
, identified with
\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})/\operatorname{Rot}(S^{1})

and endowed with reduced symplectic form
\Omega_{a}^{\textsf{w}}
, has a natural Hamiltonian
G
action.
The momentum map

J:\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})/\operatorname{Rot}(S^{1})\to\mathfrak{g}^{*},\quad\langle J([f]),X_{h}\rangle=\frac{\textsf{w}}{2\pi}\int_{S^{1}}h(f(t))dt

(8)

is one-to-one onto a coadjoint orbit and the KKS symplectic form on the coadjoint orbit satisfies

J^{*}\omega_{KKS}=\Omega_{a}^{\textsf{w}}
. The coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\textsf{w}}
is prequantizable if and only if
aw\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
.

3
Pointed vortex loops

We call pointed vortex loop a triple
(C,\beta,(x_{i}))
that consists of a vortex loop
(C,\beta)
to which we attach
k
point vortices

x_{i}\in C
.
In this section we study singular vorticities in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}=C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{*}
induced by pointed vortex loops:

X_{h}\mapsto\int_{C}h\beta+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}h(x_{i}).

(9)

The invariants of the
G
action.

Beside the total vorticity
\textsf{w}=\int_{C}\beta>0
, the point vortices introduce
k
additional partial vorticities along the loop:

\textsf{w}_{i}=\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{i}+1}\beta\quad\text{ with }\quad\textsf{w}=\textsf{w}_{1}+...+\textsf{w}_{k}.

We only consider consecutive points on the curve, while respecting the orientation induced by
\beta
, which means that all
\textsf{w}_{i}>0
.
The natural action of
G=\operatorname{Ham}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\varphi\cdot((C,\beta,(x_{i}))=(\varphi(C),\varphi_{*}\beta,(\varphi(x_{i})),

(10)

leaves invariant the area
a
enclosed by the curve, as well as all partial vorticities
\textsf{w}_{i}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\bar{\textsf{w}}}
the space of all pointed vortex loops
(C,\beta,(x_{i}))
with

a=\int_{C}\nu
and
\textsf{w}_{i}=\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{i}+1}\beta
,
i=1,\dots,k
.

Lemma 3.1

Consider the manifold
\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
of embeddings
that enclose a fixed area
a
, and fix a collection of positive numbers
\bar{\textsf{w}}=(\textsf{w}_{i})
with
\textsf{w}=\textsf{w}_{1}+\dots+\textsf{w}_{k}
.
Then the map

\Phi:\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\bar{\textsf{w}}},\quad\Phi(f)=(f(S^{1}),f_{*}\mu,(f(t_{i})))

(11)

is a bijection,
where
\mu:=\frac{\textsf{w}}{2\pi}dt
and, for
i=1,\dots,k
,

t_{i}:=\frac{\textsf{w}_{1}+\dots+\textsf{w}_{i-1}}{\textsf{w}}2\pi.

(12)

Proof.
The map
\Phi
is well defined since

\int_{f(S^{1})}f_{*}\mu=\frac{\textsf{w}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}dt=\textsf{w},\quad\int_{f(t_{i})}^{f(t_{i+1})}f_{*}\mu=\frac{\textsf{w}}{2\pi}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}dt=\textsf{w}_{i}.

It is injective because
\Phi(f_{1})=\Phi(f_{2})
implies that the two parametrizations differ by a rigid rotation:

f_{2}=f_{1}\circ R_{\tau}
. From
f_{1}(0)=f_{2}(0)
(since
t_{1}=0
) we infer that the rotation
R_{\tau}
must be the identity map, so
f_{1}=f_{2}
.

For the surjectivity we consider an arbitrary pointed vortex loop
(C,\beta,(x_{i}))\in\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\bar{\textsf{w}}}
.
There is a parametrization
f\in\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
C=f(S^{1})
and
\beta=f_{*}\mu
.
We use the freedom to compose the embedding
f
with a rigid rotation to get
x_{1}=f(0)=f(t_{1})
. It remains to be shown that
x_{i}=f(t_{i})
for
i=2,\dots,k
. This follows from

\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{i}}\beta=\textsf{w}_{1}+\dots\textsf{w}_{i-1}=\frac{\textsf{w}}{2\pi}t_{i}=\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\mu=\int_{x_{1}}^{f(t_{i})}\beta.

Thus
\Phi(f)=(C,\beta,(x_{i}))
.

The bijection
\Phi
in (
11
) intertwines the
G
action
\varphi\cdot f=\varphi\circ f
with the natural action (
10
).
It is well known that the action of
\operatorname{Ham}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
on
\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
is transitive (see
[
7
]
or Proposition

B.2
from the Appendix).
With the Lemma
3.1
we obtain that:

Proposition 3.2

The space of pointed vortex loops
\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\bar{\textsf{w}}}
is a
\operatorname{Ham}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
orbit,
thus the enclosed area
a
and the partial vorticities
\bar{\textsf{w}}
are a complete set of invariants.

Remark 3.3

Another expression of the tangent space to the orbit of pointed vortex loops
\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\bar{\textsf{w}}}
,
in terms of triples (inspired by the vortex sheet approach in
[
7
]
)
uses the orthogonal decomposition of vector fields along the curve (with respect to the ambient Euclidean metric).
The infinitesimal generator to
X_{h}\in\mathfrak{g}
, which is the vector field
f\mapsto X_{h}\circ f
on the space of embeddings,
becomes

\zeta_{X_{h}}(C,\beta,(x_{i}))=(X_{h}|_{C}^{\perp},di_{X_{h}|_{C}^{\top}}\beta,(X_{h}(x_{i}))).

Here the tangent space at
(C,\beta,(x_{i}))
to its
G
orbit
\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\bar{\textsf{w}}}
is identified with

\displaystyle\{(u_{C},d\lambda,(v_{i}))\in\Gamma(TC^{\perp})\times dC^{\infty}(C)\times\mathbb{R}^{2k}:i_{u_{C}}\omega|_{TC}\in dC^{\infty}(C),

\displaystyle\ v_{i}^{\perp}=u_{C}(x_{i}),

\displaystyle\beta(v_{i}^{\top})-\lambda(x_{i})={\rm ct.}\}.

(13)

The first condition comes from the constant enclosed area.
The second condition doesn’t let the points
x_{i}
to leave the curve.
The requirement that the local vorticity
\textsf{w}_{i}=\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{i+1}}\beta
is constant,
implies the third condition, that
\beta(v_{i}^{\top})-\lambda(x_{i})
is constant for all
i=1,\dots,k
.
This reflects the fact that the move of one of the points on the curve predicts the movement of all the other
k-1
points.

The symplectic form.

Our plan is to show that (
9
) is a momentum map for a well chosen symplectic form on the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\bar{\textsf{w}}}
.
Let
\operatorname{ev}_{t_{i}}:\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
denote the evaluation map at the points
t_{i}\in S^{1}
given by (
12
),
so the collection of maps
(\operatorname{ev}_{t_{i}})

takes values in the configuration space
\operatorname{Conf}_{k}
.
On
\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
we consider the 2-form

\Omega^{\Gamma\bar{\textsf{w}}}=\Omega+(\operatorname{ev}_{t_{i}})^{*}\omega^{\Gamma}=\Omega+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}\operatorname{ev}_{t_{i}}^{*}\omega,

(14)

where
\Omega
is the 2-form (
5
) restricted to embeddings that enclose a fixed area
a
,
and
\omega^{\Gamma}
is the symplectic form (
2
) on the configuration space
\operatorname{Conf}_{k}
.
More precisely,

\displaystyle(\Omega^{\Gamma\bar{\textsf{w}}})_{f}(u_{f},v_{f})

\displaystyle=\frac{\textsf{w}}{2\pi}\int_{S^{1}}\omega(u_{f}(t),v_{f}(t))dt+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}\omega(u_{f}(t_{i}),v_{f}(t_{i})).

(15)

Given the fact that
\omega=d\nu
is an exact form,
\Omega^{\Gamma\bar{\textsf{w}}}
is also exact, hence closed
(the hat calculus from
[
18
]
can be useful here).

To prove the non-degeneracy of
\Omega^{\Gamma\bar{\textsf{w}}}
, we will use the Euclidean orthogonal decomposition of vector fields along the curve
C=f(S^{1})
into their normal and tangent parts, together with the non-degenerate pairing from the Appendix.
Let
\{{\bf t},{\bf n}\}
be the positively oriented orthonormal frame along the curve, where the tangent vector
{\bf t}
respects the orientation induced by
the volume form
\beta
on the curve. In particular
\omega({\bf t},{\bf n})=1
.
We decompose
u_{f}\in T_{f}\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
into its normal and tangential components

u_{f}=(\rho{\bf n}+\lambda{\bf t})\circ f.

(16)

Because of the fixed enclosed area, the normal variation
\rho
is performed with zero integral functions in
C_{0}^{\infty}(C):=\{\rho\in C^{\infty}(C):\int_{C}\rho\mu_{C}=0\}
,
where
\mu_{C}=i_{\bf n}\omega
denotes the volume form on
C
induced by the Euclidean metric,
so the tangent space decomposes as

T_{f}\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})\cong C_{0}^{\infty}(C)\times C^{\infty}(C).

(17)

The symplectic form (
15
) becomes

(\Omega^{\Gamma\bar{\textsf{w}}})_{f}((\rho_{1},\lambda_{1}),(\rho_{2},\lambda_{2}))=\frac{\textsf{w}}{2\pi}\int_{C}(\rho_{2}\lambda_{1}-\rho_{1}\lambda_{2})\beta+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}(\rho_{2}\lambda_{1}-\rho_{1}\lambda_{2})(x_{i}),

where
x_{i}=f(t_{i})
are
k
points on the curve
C=f(S^{1})
.
We define the pairing

\langle\langle\ ,\ \rangle\rangle:C_{0}^{\infty}(C)\times C^{\infty}(C)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad\langle\langle\rho,\lambda\rangle\rangle:=\frac{\textsf{w}}{2\pi}\int_{C}\rho\lambda\beta+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}(\rho\lambda)(x_{i}),

(18)

which is non-degenerate by the Lemma
A.1
.
It allows us to rewrite the 2-form
\Omega^{\Gamma\bar{\textsf{w}}}_{a}
in (
15
) at
f
as

(\Omega^{\Gamma\bar{\textsf{w}}})_{f}((\rho_{1},\lambda_{1}),(\rho_{2},\lambda_{2}))=\langle\langle\rho_{2},\lambda_{1}\rangle\rangle-\langle\langle\rho_{1},\lambda_{2}\rangle\rangle

(19)

and we conclude that:

Proposition 3.4

The 2-form
\Omega^{\Gamma\bar{\textsf{w}}}
on
\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
given in (
15
) is symplectic.

Remark 3.5

Let us denote by the same symbol
\Omega^{\Gamma\bar{\textsf{w}}}
the symplectic form on
\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\bar{\textsf{w}}}
transported
with the bijection
\Phi
, and let us use the decomposition (
17
) also for the tangent space
T_{(C,\beta,(x_{i}))}\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\Gamma\bar{\textsf{w}}}
. The infinitesimal generator
f\mapsto f^{\prime}
for the
\operatorname{Rot}(S^{1})

Coadjoint orbit.

It is easy to see that the natural

on the symplectic manifold

Under the identification

The injectivity of

(\Gamma_{i})
and
(\textsf{w}_{i})

Let

\bar{J}:\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})/\mathbb{Z}_{m}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*},\quad\langle\bar{J}([f]),h\rangle=\frac{\textsf{w}}{2\pi}\int_{S^{1}}h(f(t))dt+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}h(f(t_{i})).

The two extreme cases are:

Lemma 3.7

The equivariant momentum map
\bar{J}
is injective.

Proof.
Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\bar{J}([f_{1}])=\bar{J}([f_{2}])
, which means that
J(f_{1})=J(f_{2})
.
Thus for all
h\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\frac{\textsf{w}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}h(f_{1}(t))dt+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}h(f_{1}(t_{i}))=\frac{\textsf{w}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}h(f_{2}(t))dt+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}h(f_{2}(t_{i})),

(23)

with
t_{i}=\tfrac{2\pi}{\textsf{w}}(\textsf{w}_{1}+\dots+\textsf{w}_{i-1})
.
Assume by contradiction that the curves
C_{1}=f_{1}(S^{1})
and
C_{2}=f_{2}(S^{1})
do not coincide.
There exists a point
x
contained in the second, but not in the first, which means that exists a whole neighborhood of
x
contained in
C_{2}
but not in
C_{1}
.
Shrinking this neighborhood, we get a subset
U\subset C_{2}\setminus C_{1}
such that
f_{2}(t_{i})\notin U
for all

.
Now let
h\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a non-negative function supported in

. In particular,
h
vanishes on
C_{1}
.
Under these circumstances

\displaystyle J(f_{1})=J(f_{2})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{\textsf{w}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}h(f_{2}(t))dt=0,

which contradicts the choice of
h
. The assumption has been false, so the embeddings
f_{1},f_{2}
have the same image.

The existence of a diffeomorphism
\gamma\in\operatorname{Diff}(S^{1})
such that
f_{2}=f_{1}\circ\gamma
follows. We show that
\gamma^{\prime}=1
, i.e.
\gamma
is a rotation.
We assume by contradiction that there exists
t_{0}\in S^{1}
such that
\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})\neq 1
. Then there is a whole neighborhood of
t_{0}
in
S^{1}
on which

\gamma^{\prime}-1
doesn’t change sign.
Shrinking it, we get a subset

of
S^{1}
such that
t_{i}\notin V
and
\gamma(t_{i})\notin V
for all
i
.
Any non-negative function

such that

is supported in

\int_{0}^{2\pi}(h\circ f_{1})(t))dt=\int_{0}^{2\pi}(h\circ f_{2})(\gamma^{-1}(t))dt=\int_{0}^{2\pi}(h\circ f_{1})(t){\gamma}^{\prime}(t)dt\neq\int_{0}^{2\pi}(h\circ f_{2})(t)dt.

This contradicts the identity
\int_{0}^{2\pi}(h\circ f_{1})(t))dt=\int_{0}^{2\pi}(h\circ f_{2})(t)dt
that follows from (
23
).
Thus

\gamma^{\prime}=1\Rightarrow\gamma\in\text{Rot}(S^{1})\Rightarrow f_{2}=f_{1}\circ R_{\tau}.

It remains to prove that
\tau
is a multiple of
2\pi/m
.
With

, the identity (
23
) becomes

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}\delta_{f_{1}(t_{i})}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{i}\delta_{f_{1}(t_{i}+\tau)}.

Since

is an embedding, the rotation

has to make a rotational permutation of the points

on the circle,
while preserving the corresponding vorticities. More precisely, there exists a natural number

such that for all

t_{i+\ell(\tau)}=t_{i}+\tau,\quad\Gamma_{i+\ell(\tau)}=\Gamma_{i}.

It follows that
t_{i+\ell(\tau)+1}-t_{i+\ell(\tau)}=t_{i+1}-t_{i}
, so
\textsf{w}_{i+\ell(\tau)}=\textsf{w}_{i}
for all

Now the symmetry conditions (

which implies that
\ell(\tau)
is a multiple of
\ell
.
Writing

for

, we get

\tau=t_{\ell(\tau)+1}-t_{1}=\frac{2\pi}{\textsf{w}}(\textsf{w}_{1}+\dots+\textsf{w}_{\ell(\tau)})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{sym}}}{{=}}\frac{2\pi}{\textsf{w}}j(\textsf{w}_{1}+\dots+\textsf{w}_{\ell})=\frac{2\pi}{m}j,

as desired. Hence

This ensures the injectivity of

Polarization.

In
[
9
]
is shown that, within the framework of the 2D Euler equations, point vortices cannot be consistently quantized,
while vortex loops admit natural polarizations. They notice that group
G_{C}
of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms that preserve the curve
C
is a polarization subgroup of
G
for the coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\textsf{w}}
of the vortex loop
(C,\beta)\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, as noticed in
[
9
]
.
The resulting configuration space is the space of loops enclosing a fixed area, without information about the vorticity distribution.

Here we show that pointed vortex loops also have natural polarizations.
More precisely, the same polarization subgroup
G_{C}
, that consists of diffeomorphisms that preserve the loop as a set,
works for the coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}_{a}^{\bar{\textsf{w}}}
of the pointed vortex loop

(C,\beta,(x_{i}))
from (
9
).
Thus the configuration space is the space of loops that enclose a fixed area,
without information about the vorticity distribution and the attached points.

Proposition 3.15

The group
G_{C}
of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms that preserve the curve
C
is a polarization subgroup for the coadjoint orbit
of the pointed vortex loop
(C,\beta,(x_{i}))\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Proof.
The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{C}
of
G_{C}
consists of compactly supported Hamiltonian vector fields that are tangent to the curve
C
.

for all

,
because along
C
the two Hamiltonian vector fields are linear dependent and
x_{i}\in C
.
We also use the fact that
\omega(X_{h_{1}},X_{h_{2}})
is a Hamiltonian function for
[X_{h_{1}},X_{h_{2}}]
.

Let us assume by contradiction that there exists a Lie subalgebra

. This means we find

(so

and

.

\lambda_{0}\in C^{\infty}(S^{1})
such that
\langle\langle\rho,\lambda_{0}\rangle\rangle=0
,
\forall\rho\in C_{0}^{\infty}(S^{1})
,
which means that

c\int_{S^{1}}\rho(t)\lambda_{0}(t)dt+\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\rho(t_{i})\lambda_{0}(t_{i})=0.

(29)

Assume by contradiction that
\lambda_{0}
is not constant on
S^{1}\setminus\{t_{1},...,t_{k}\}
,
hence there exist
t^{\prime}\neq t^{\prime\prime}
distinct from
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}
, such that
\lambda_{0}(t^{\prime})\neq\lambda_{0}(t^{\prime\prime})
.
Choose a ”double bump” zero integral function
\rho\in C_{0}^{\infty}(S^{1})
supported in the union of small neighborhoods of
t^{\prime}
and
t^{\prime\prime}
that don’t contain any of the
t_{i}
’s.
By shrinking the support of
\rho
around the points
t^{\prime}
and
t^{\prime\prime}
, we can achieve

\int_{S^{1}}\rho(t)\lambda_{0}(t)dt\rightarrow\lambda_{0}(t^{\prime})-\lambda_{0}(t^{\prime\prime})\neq 0,

which contradicts relation
(
29
)
.
We conclude that
\lambda_{0}
is constant on
S^{1}\setminus\{t_{1},...,t_{k}\}
. Since
\lambda_{0}
is a continuous function, this means
\lambda_{0}
is constant on
S^{1}
.

Going back to equation
(
29
)
, we get

\lambda_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\rho(t_{i})=0
for all zero integral functions
\rho
.
Pick
\rho\in C_{0}^{\infty}(S^{1})
with
\rho(t_{1})\neq 0
and
\rho(t_{i})=0
for all the other
t_{i}
.
From
\lambda_{0}c_{1}\rho(t_{1})=0
and
c_{1}\neq 0
we conclude that
\lambda_{0}=0
.

Appendix B
A transitivity result

We prove transitivity of the
\operatorname{Ham}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
action on
\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})

by applying the following lemma (see the appendix in
[
11
]
):

Lemma B.1

Let
G
be a regular Lie group acting on a smooth manifold
M
with infinitesimal action
\zeta:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{X}(M)
.
Suppose that every point
x_{0}
in
M
admits an open neighborhood
U
and a smooth map
\sigma:TM|_{U}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
such that

\zeta_{\sigma(X)}(x)=X

(30)

for all
x\in U
and
X\in T_{x}M
. Then the
G
action on
M
admits local smooth sections.

Proposition B.2

The action of the group
\operatorname{Ham}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
on
\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
, is transitive.

Proof.
Firstly, we prove that the action is inifinitesimally transitive, which means proving that given any
u_{f}
in

T_{f}\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})=\left\{u_{f}:S^{1}\rightarrow T\mathbb{R}^{2}:\int_{S^{1}}f^{*}i_{u_{f}}\omega=0\right\},

there is
h\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, such that
u_{f}=\zeta_{X_{h}}(f)=X_{h}\circ f
,.
Let
\lambda\in C^{\infty}(S^{1})
with the property
f^{*}i_{{u_{f}}}\omega=d\lambda
.
We extend it to a function
h_{1}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, which means that
\lambda=h_{1}\circ f
.

We define a 1-form along the curve
f(S^{1})\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
by

\gamma=dh_{1}\circ f-i_{u_{f}}\omega\in\Gamma(f^{*}T^{*}M).

(31)

It vanishes on vectors tangent to the curve, hence it can be seen as a function on the normal bundle
Tf(S^{1})^{\bot}
, with differential along the zero section
f(S^{1})
equal to the 1-form itself. Thus, on a tubular neighborhood of
f(S^{1})
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, cutting
\gamma
with a suitable bump function,
we get
h_{2}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\gamma=dh_{2}\circ f
. It follows by (
31
) that

dh_{2}\circ f=dh_{1}\circ f-i_{u_{f}}\omega
. This implies
i_{u_{f}}\omega=i_{{X_{h}}\circ f}\omega

for
h=h_{1}-h_{2}
.
We conclude that
u_{f}=X_{h}\circ f=\zeta_{X_{h}}(f)
is the infinitesimal generator at
f
for the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{h}
.

Using tubular neighborhoods constructed with the help of a Riemannian metric, we see that the function
h
above
may be chosen to depend smoothly on
f
and
u_{f}
, for
f
in a sufficiently small open neighborhood of a fixed embedding
f_{0}\in\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Thus
\sigma(u_{f})=X_{h}
defines a smooth map that satisfies (
30
).
With Lemma
(
B.1
)
we obtain that the action of
\text{Ham}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
on
\operatorname{Emb}_{a}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
admits local smooth sections,
hence it is transitive.

Abstract
. Let f f be a C 1 + bv C^{1+\text{bv}} circle diffeomorphism with irrational rotation number. As established by Douady and Yoccoz in the eighties, for any given s > 0 s>0 there exists a unique automorphic measure of exponent s s for f f . In the present paper we prove that the same holds for multicritical circle maps , and we provide two applications of this result. The first one, is to prove that the space of invariant distributions of order 1 1 of any given multicritical circle map is one-dimensional, spanned by the unique invariant measure. The second one, is an improvement over the Denjoy-Koksma inequality for multicritical circle maps and absolutely continuous observables.

1.
Introduction

Smooth one-dimensional dynamical systems can be studied from various viewpoints, such as their topological classification, their smooth rigidity properties, the behaviour of their individual orbits or their measure-theoretic and ergodic properties. A specific class of such systems that has received a great deal of attention in recent years is the class of
multicritical circle maps
.

A
multicritical circle map
is a
C^{3}
circle homeomorphism
f:S^{1}\to S^{1}
having
N\geq 1
critical points (all of which are
non-flat
, see §
2
). We are only interested in maps of this type having no periodic points, in other words, only in those maps that have irrational rotation number.
The classification os such maps up to topological conjugacy goes back to Yoccoz
[
37
]
, who proved that they are always minimal, hence topologically equivalent to a rotation of the circle (see Theorem
2.4
below). The smooth rigidity of such maps – including the preliminary step known as
quasi-symmetric rigidity
– has been the object of intense research in recent decades; it is by now fairly well-understood, at least in the unicritical case, thanks to the combined efforts of several mathematicians, see
[
3
,
6
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
22
,
23
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
]
, or the book
[
8
]
and references therein (we note
en passant
that, quite recently, some rigidity results for maps with
more than one
critical point have been established, see
[
4
,
5
,
36
]
and the recent preprint
[
14
]
). The geometric behaviour of individual orbits of such maps was examined in the recent paper
[
10
]
.

From the measure-theoretic viewpoint, multicritical circle maps have also been studied in detail. Having irrational rotation number, they are uniquely ergodic. Their unique invariant probability measure was shown to be purely singular with respect to Lebesgue (Haar) measure by Khanin
[
21
]
(see also
[
15
]
), and later it was shown to have zero Lyapunov exponent in
[
7
]
. In
[
9
]
, the authors went a bit further and showed that such maps do not admit even
\sigma
-finite absolutely continuous invariant measures.

In the present paper, we are interested in further ergodic-theoretic properties of multicritical circle maps. In particular, we are interested in the question: “Does a (minimal) multicritical circle map admit other invariant
distributions
besides its unique invariant probability measure?”. The analogous question in a more general setting seems to have been first asked by Katok (for a general reference, see
[
20
]
). However, our main source of inspiration is the remarkable paper by Avila and Kocsard
[
1
]
in which they give a fairly complete answer to the corresponding question for smooth circle
diffeomorphisms
.

Here, we give a (partial) answer to the above question by relating it (following the paper
[
27
]
, by Navas and Triestino) to the question of existence and uniqueness of so-called
automorphic measures
for multicritical circle maps – a question to which we give here a full answer.

Given
s\in\mathbb{R}
, an
automorphic measure
of exponent
s
for
f
is a Borel probability measure
\nu
on
S^{1}
whose pullback
f^{\ast}\nu
is equivalent to
\nu
, with Radon-Nikodym derivative given by
(Df)^{s}
. This concept is the analogue, for real one-dimensional maps, of the concept of
conformal measure
introduced by Sullivan in
[
30
]
in the context of rational maps – which in turn is inspired by a similar notion introduced by Patterson
[
28
]
and Sullivan himself
[
29
]
in the context of Fuchsian and Kleinian groups.

The precise definition of automorphic measure is given in §
3
(def.
3.1
). In the eighties, it was proved by Douady and Yoccoz (but only published some years later, in
[
2
]
– see also
[
24
]
) that, for every minimal
C^{1+\text{bv}}
circle diffeomorphism and every real number
s
, there exists a unique automorphic measure of exponent
s
. In the present paper we prove the following result.

Theorem A

(Existence and uniqueness of automorphic measures)
.

Let
f
be a multicritical circle map. For any given
s\geq 0
there exists a unique automorphic measure of exponent
s
for
f
. This measure has no atoms, is supported on the whole circle and it is ergodic under
f
.

1.1.
Applications

In Section
7
of the present paper we provide two applications of Theorem
A
, that we now describe.

As usual, a
1
-distribution
is a continuous linear functional defined on the space of
C^{1}
real-valued functions of the circle (see §
7.1
for precise definitions). As a consequence of Theorem
A
, we have the following result.

Theorem B

(No invariant distributions)
.

Let
f
be a multicritical circle map with irrational rotation number and unique invariant measure
\mu
. Then the space
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{1}\left(f\right)
of
f
-invariant distributions of order at most
1
is spanned by
\mu
, that is,

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{1}\left(f\right)=\mathbb{R}\mu\,.

In other words,
f
admits no invariant distributions of order at most
1
different from (a scalar multiple of) its unique invariant measure. The proof of Theorem
B
will be given in §
7.1
, and will follow the approach of Navas and Triestino developed in
[
27
]
for
C^{1+\text{bv}}
-diffeomorphisms. We would like to remark that this approach deals with distributions of order at most
1
. Since multicritical circle maps are assumed to be
C^{3}
smooth, it would be desirable to also rule out invariant distributions of order up to
3
. Unfortunately, we do not know how to do this. Moreover, if we consider
C^{\infty}
, or
C^{\omega}
, multicritical circle maps, we do not know how to deal with higher order distributions. Let us be more precise: a
C^{\infty}
dynamical system is
distributionally uniquely ergodic
if it admits a single invariant distribution (up to multiplication by a constant). In
[
1
]
, Avila and Kocsard proved that every
C^{\infty}
circle diffeomorphism with irrational rotation number is distributionally uniquely ergodic. We believe that
C^{\infty}
multicritical circle maps are distributionally uniquely ergodic too but, as already mentioned, we do not know how to prove that. Nevertheless, to the best of our knowledge, Theorem
B
provides the first examples of dynamics with no invariant distributions of order at most
1
outside the realm of flows and diffeomorphisms. Finally, we would like to remark that the non-flatness condition on each critical point of
f
(see §
2
below) is crucial in order to prove Theorem
B
. Indeed, the following holds.

Theorem C
.

For any given irrational number
\rho\in(0,1)
there exists a
C^{\infty}
homeomorphism
f\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}
, with rotation number
\rho(f)=\rho
, having invariant distributions of order
1
(different from a scalar multiple of its unique invariant measure).

The examples of Theorem
C
are those constructed by Hall in
[
18
]
, see §
7.2
for the details.

As it turns out, Theorem
A
implies the following improvement over the Denjoy-Koksma inequality for absolutely continuous observables.

Theorem D

(Improved Denjoy-Koksma)
.

Let
f
be a multicritical circle map with irrational rotation number
\rho
and unique invariant measure
\mu
, and let
\phi\colon S^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be absolutely continuous. If
\{q_{n}\}
is the sequence of denominators for the rational approximations of
\rho
, we have that

q_{n}\left\|\frac{1}{q_{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\,\phi\circ f^{i}-\int_{S^{1}}\phi\,d\mu\right\|_{C^{0}(S^{1})}\longrightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as $n$ goes to $\infty$.}

The proof of Theorem
D
will be given in §
7.3
, following the very same lines as that of Avila and Kocsard
[
1
, Section 3]
and Navas
[
26
, Section 2]
for circle diffeomorphisms.

The paper is organized as follows: in §
2
, we present a recap of some topics from the theory of critical circle maps. In §
3
, we define automorphic measures of exponent
s\geq 0
for
f
, and explore some of their elementary properties; we remark that, at that point in the paper, it is still not clear whether
f
-automorphic measures actually exist. This is done in §
4
, where we show that
f
-automorphic measures indeed exist for all
s>0
. In §
5
, we obtain fundamental bounds on
f
-automorphic measures, which may themselves be of interest in future works. In §
6
, we use said bounds to show that any
f
-automorphic measure is ergodic with respect to
f
, and as a consequence, easily derive the uniqueness part of Theorem
A
. Finally, in §
7
, we prove that Theorem
A
implies both Theorem
B
and Theorem
D
, and we briefly explain the proof of Theorem
C
.

2.
Multicritical circle maps

Let us now define the maps which are the main object of study in the present paper. We start with the notion of
non-flat critical point
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a critical point
c
of a one-dimensional
C^{3}
map
f
is
non-flat
of degree
d>1
if there exists a neighborhood
W_{c}
of the critical point and a
C^{3}
diffeomorphism

\phi_{c}\colon W_{c}\to\phi_{c}(W_{c})\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi_{c}(c)=0
and, for all
x\in W_{c}
,

f(x)=f(c)+\phi_{c}(x)\,\left\lvert\phi_{c}(x)\right\rvert^{d-1}\,.

This local form easily implies the following estimate (see
[
8
, ch. 5]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
c
be a non-flat critical point of degree
d
of a one-dimensional
C^{3}
map
f
. There exists an interval
U=U_{c}\subset W_{c}
that contains
c
such that, for any non-empty interval
J\subset U
and
x\in J
,

(2.1)

Df(x)\leq 3d\,\frac{\left\lvert f(J)\right\rvert}{\left\lvert J\right\rvert}\,,

where
|J|
denotes the Euclidean length of an interval
J
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
multicritical circle map
is an orientation-preserving
C^{3}
circle homeomorphism having finitely many critical points, all of which are non-flat.

Being an orientation-preserving circle homeomorphism, a multicritical circle map
f
has a well defined rotation number. We will focus on the case that
f
has no periodic orbits (
i.e.
,
\rho(f)\notin\mathbb{Q}
). As it turns out, these maps have no wandering intervals. More precisely, we have the following fundamental result.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
f
be a multicritical circle map with irrational rotation number
\rho
. Then
f
is topologically conjugate to the rigid rotation
R_{\rho}
, i.e., there exists a homeomorphism
h\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}
such that
h\circ f=R_{\rho}\circ h.

Theorem
2.4
was proved by Yoccoz in
[
37
]
, see also
[
8
, ch. 6]
.

2.1.
The Koebe distortion principle

Given two circle intervals
M\subset T\subset S^{1}
with
M
compactly contained in the interior of
T
(written
M\Subset T
), we denote by
L
and
R
the two connected components of
T\setminus M
. The
space of
M
inside
T
is defined to be the number

(2.2)

\tau=\min\left\{\frac{\left\lvert L\right\rvert}{\left\lvert M\right\rvert},\frac{\left\lvert R\right\rvert}{\left\lvert M\right\rvert}\right\}\,.

Given circle intervals
M,T
with
M\Subset T
and
k\geq 1
such that
f^{k}\colon T\to f^{k}(T)
is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism onto its image, one can bound the distortion of
f^{k}
inside
M
independently of
k
as long as the intermediate images
T,f(T),\cdots,f^{k-1}(T)
satisfy a mild summability condition and the space of
f^{k}(M)
inside
f^{k}(T)
is bounded from below independently of
k
. This is the content of the Koebe distortion principle, and, as one can expect, it is of fundamental importance in controlling the geometric behavior of large iterates of the map
f
.

Lemma 2.5

(Koebe distortion principle)
.

For each
\ell,\tau>0
and each multicritical circle map
f\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}
there exists a constant
K=K(\ell,\tau,f)>1
with the following property. If
k\geq 1
,
M\subset T\subset S^{1}
are intervals, with
M
compactly contained in the interior of
T
, are such that the intervals
T,f(T),\dots,f^{k-1}(T)
contain no critical point of
f
,

(2.3)

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\,\left\lvert f^{j}(T)\right\rvert\leq\ell

and the space of
f^{k}(M)
inside
f^{k}(T)
is at least
\tau
, then

(2.4)

K^{-1}\leq\frac{Df^{k}(x)}{Df^{k}(y)}\leq K\quad\text{for all }x,y\in M\,.

A proof of the Koebe distortion principle can be found in
[
25
, p. 295]
.

Remark 2.6
.

Given a family of intervals
\mathcal{F}
on
S^{1}
and a positive integer
m
, we say that
\mathcal{F}
has
multiplicity of intersection
at most
m
if each
x\in S^{1}
belongs to at most
m
elements of
\mathcal{F}
. For our purposes, the following (elementary) observation relating the hypotheses of the Koebe distortion principle to multiplicity of intersection shall be crucial: if the family
T,f(T),\dots,f^{k-1}(T)
has multiplicity of intersection at most
m
, then (
2.3
) holds with
\ell=m
. This observation also holds in the context of arbitrary finite measures on
S^{1}
: if
\nu
is a finite measure on the circle,
m\geq 1
and
\mathcal{F}
is a family of circle intervals with intersection multiplicity at most
m
, then

\sum_{I\in\mathcal{F}}\,\nu(I)\leq m\,\nu(S^{1})\,.

2.2.
Combinatorics and real bounds

Throughout this paper,
f\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}
will be a
C^{3}
multicritical circle map with irrational rotation number. Furthermore,
N\geq 1
shall be the number of critical points of
f
,
\operatorname{Crit}\left(f\right)=\left\{c_{1},\dots,c_{N}\right\}
shall be the set of critical points of
f
, and
d_{1},\dots,d_{N}
their corresponding criticalities.

Let
\rho
be the rotation number of
f
. As we know, it has a infinite continued fraction expansion, say

\rho(f)=[a_{0},a_{1},\cdots]=\cfrac{1}{a_{0}+\cfrac{1}{a_{1}+\cfrac{1}{\ddots}}}\ .

Truncating the expansion at level
n-1
, we obtain a sequence
of fractions
p_{n}/q_{n}
which are called the
convergents
of the irrational
\rho
.

\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\;=\;[a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{n-1}]\;=\;\dfrac{1}{a_{0}+\dfrac{1}{a_{1}+\dfrac{1}{\ddots\dfrac{1}{a_{n-1}}}}}\ .

Since each
p_{n}/q_{n}
is the best possible approximation to
\rho
by fractions with denominator at most
q_{n}
,
we have

\text{If}\hskip 11.38092pt0<q<q_{n}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{then}\hskip 8.5359pt\left|\rho-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|<\left|\rho-\frac{p}{q}\right|,\hskip 8.5359pt\text{ for any $p\in\mathbb{Z}$.}

The sequence of numerators satisfies

p_{0}=0,\hskip 11.38092ptp_{1}=1,\hskip 11.38092ptp_{n+1}=a_{n}p_{n}+p_{n-1}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{for $n\geq 1$}.

Analogously, the sequence of the denominators, which we call the
return times
, satisfies

q_{0}=1,\hskip 11.38092ptq_{1}=a_{0},\hskip 11.38092ptq_{n+1}=a_{n}q_{n}+q_{n-1}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{for $n\geq 1$}.

For each point
x\in S^{1}
and each non-negative integer
n
, let
I_{n}(x)
be the closed interval with endpoints

x
and
f^{q_{n}}(x)
containing
f^{q_{n+2}}(x)
(note that
I_{n}(x)
contains no other iterate
f^{j}(x)
with
1\leq j\leq q_{n}-1
). We write
I_{n}^{j}(x)=f^{j}(I_{n}(x))
for all
j
and
n
.

Lemma 2.7
.

For each
n\geq 0
and each
x\in S^{1}
, the collection of intervals

\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)\;=\;\left\{f^{i}(I_{n}(x)):\;0\leq i\leq q_{n+1}-1\right\}\;\bigcup\;\left\{f^{j}(I_{n+1}(x)):\;0\leq j\leq q_{n}-1\right\}

is a partition of the unit circle (modulo endpoints), called the
n
-th dynamical partition associated to the point
x
.

For a proof of this lemma, see
[
8
, ch. 6]
. Note that, for each
n
, the partition
\mathcal{P}_{n+1}(x)
is a (non-strict) refinement of
\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)
, while the partition

\mathcal{P}_{n+2}(x)
is a strict refinement of
\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)
.

Theorem 2.8

(Real Bounds)
.

There exists a constant
C=C(f)>1
, depending only on
f
, such that the following holds for every critical point
c
of
f
. For all
n\geq 0
and for each pair of adjacent atoms
I,J\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(c)
we have

(2.5)

C^{-1}\left\lvert J\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert I\right\rvert\leq C\left\lvert J\right\rvert\,.

Note that for a rigid rotation we have
|I_{n}|=a_{n+1}|I_{n+1}|+|I_{n+2}|
. If
a_{n+1}
is large, then
I_{n}
is much larger than
I_{n+1}
. Thus, even for rigid rotations, real bounds do not hold in general.

Theorem
2.8
was obtained by Herman
[
19
]
, based on estimates by Świa̧tek
[
31
]
. A detailed proof can be found in
[
8
, ch. 6]
.

Theorem
2.8
has the following consequence (see
[
8
, ch. 8]
).

Lemma 2.9
.

There exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(f)>0
such that, for each
x\in S^{1}
and all
n\geq 0
, we have
Df^{q_{n}}(x)\leq C_{1}
.

Yet another consequence of the real bounds that will be useful in the present paper (see §
4
below) is the following.

Lemma 2.10

(Zero Lyapunov Exponent)
.

Let
f
be a multicritical circle map with irrational rotation number and unique invariant measure
\mu
. Then
\log Df\in L^{1}(\mu)
and

\int_{S^{1}}\log Df\,d\mu=0\,.

A proof of Lemma
2.10
can be found in
[
7
]
(see also
[
8
, section 8.3]
).

2.2.1.
On the notions of domination and comparability

To simplify both the understanding of and future calculations involving the real bounds, we introduce the notions of domination and comparability
modulo

f
.

Given two circle intervals
I,J
, we shall say that
I
dominates
J
modulo
f
, and write
I\geq J
, if there exists a constant
K>1
depending only on
f
such that
\left\lvert J\right\rvert\leq K\left\lvert I\right\rvert
. If both
I\geq J
and
J\geq I
(i.e. if there is
K=K(f)>1
such that
K^{-1}\left\lvert I\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert J\right\rvert\leq K\left\lvert I\right\rvert
), we shall say that
I
and
J
are comparable modulo
f
(and write
I\asymp J
).

Thus, Theorem
2.8
states precisely that adjacent atoms of a dynamical partition are always comparable.

Observe that neither domination or comparability are transitive relations: if we are given a domination chain
I_{1}\geq I_{2}\geq\cdots\geq I_{k}
, we can only say that
I_{1}\geq I_{k}
if the length
k
of the chain is bounded by a constant that depends only on
f
(and similarly for comparability).

3.
Automorphic measures

In this section we define automorphic measures of non-negative exponent for multicritical circle maps. We further prove that they have full support on the circle (Proposition
3.3
) and are non-atomic (Lemma
3.4
).

Definition 3.1

(Automorphic measures)
.

Let
s\geq 0
.
An
automorphic measure of exponent
s
for
f
(or
f
-automorphic measure of exponent
s
) is a Radon probability measure
\nu
on
S^{1}
such that, for all continuous functions
\phi\in C^{0}(S^{1})
,

(3.1)

\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d\nu=\int_{S^{1}}\,(\phi\circ f)\,\left(Df\right)^{s}\,d\nu\,.

We denote the set of
f
-automorphic measures of exponent
s
by
\mathcal{A}_{s}
.

Equivalently (see Proposition
3.2
below), a Radon probability measure
\nu
on
S^{1}
is
f
-automorphic of exponent
s
if, and only if, the pullback measure
f^{\ast}\nu
is equivalent to
\nu
, with Radon-Nikodym derivative

\frac{df^{\ast}\nu}{d\nu}=(Df)^{s}\,.

Observe that we leave out the possibility of negative exponents,
i.e.
,
s<0
. Though automorphic measures of negative exponent make perfect sense and indeed always exist in the case of diffeomorphisms
1
1

1

As it happens, the case
s=-1
is suitable to understand both the variation of the rotation number along generic
1
-parameter families of circle diffeomorphisms
[
24
]
, as well as to build the tangent space of the set of
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms with a given irrational rotation number
[
2
, Théorème 2]
.
, they are significantly more difficult to work with in the critical case. Indeed, if
s<0
, then
(Df)^{s}
blows up at the critical points of
f
, so we cannot take for granted that
(\phi\circ f)\,\left(Df\right)^{s}
will be
\nu
-integrable for any
\phi\in C^{0}(S^{1})
and Radon probability measure
\nu
on
S^{1}
.

We further remark that the notion of automorphic measures makes perfect sense on any dimension, provided the one-dimensional derivative
Df(x)
is replaced by the Jacobian of
f
at
x
,
i.e.
, the absolute value of the determinant of the matrix
Df(x)
. As we mentioned in the introduction, for complex one-dimensional systems this is exactly the same as the notion of
conformal measure
introduced by Sullivan in
[
30
]
. In the present paper, however, we will of course only treat the real one-dimensional case.

Finally, observe that, in the case
s=0
, an
f
-automorphic measure of exponent
0
is simply an
f
-invariant probability measure. Therefore, the case
s=0
is well understood, and Theorem
A
in this case is precisely the statement that
f
(just like any circle homeomorphism with irrational rotation number) is uniquely ergodic. Therefore, for the rest of this paper, we will focus on the case
s>0
. Thus, let
s>0
and
\nu\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
be fixed.

Proposition 3.2
.

For all
\phi\in L^{1}(\nu)
and
n\geq 1
,
(\phi\circ f^{n})(Df^{n})^{s}\in L^{1}(\nu)
and

(3.2)

\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d\nu=\int_{S^{1}}\,(\phi\circ f^{n})(Df^{n})^{s}\,d\nu\,.

Proof.

Observe that (
3.2
) holds trivially if
\phi
is continuous, by applying (
3.1
) inductively. The extension to
L^{1}
functions
\phi
now follows from a standard argument from the theory or Radon measures, with the main difficulty being to show that
(\phi\circ f^{n})(Df^{n})^{s}\in L^{1}(\mu)
for all
\phi\in L^{1}(\mu)
.
∎

Note in particular that, under forward iteration, the
\nu
-measure of a Borel set
A\subset S^{1}
behaves according to the following rule:

(3.3)

\nu\big(f^{n}(A)\big)=\int_{A}\,(Df^{n})^{s}\,d\nu\,,\quad\mbox{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Proposition 3.3
.

The measure
\nu
is supported on the entire circle.

Proof.

Since the pullback measure
f^{\ast}\nu
is equivalent to
\nu
,
\nu
-null sets are mapped under
f
into
\nu
-null sets (see (
3.3
) above). But, since
f
is topologically conjugate to an irrational rotation, the positive orbit of an open interval eventually covers the whole circle, and then this interval must have positive
\nu
-measure.
∎

We denote by

\operatorname{Crit}^{\pm}\left(f\right)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}\,\mathcal{O}_{f}(c_{j})

the union of the critical orbits of
f

by
\Lambda

is
f
-invariant and its complement is countable (but dense!).

Lemma 3.4
.

The measure
\nu
has no atoms. In particular,
\Lambda
has full
\nu
-measure on the circle.

Proof.

Arguing by contradiction, suppose there is some
x_{0}\in S^{1}
such that
\nu(\left\{x_{0}\right\})=\delta>0
, and note that (
3.3
) implies

\nu(\left\{x_{0}\right\})=\nu\big(\left\{f^{-n}(x_{0})\right\}\big)\,\big(Df^{n}(f^{-n}(x_{0}))\big)^{s}\quad\mbox{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

In particular,
x_{0}
cannot be in the forward orbit of any critical point of
f
. Moreover, since
\nu
is a probability measure and
f
has no periodic orbits,

1\geq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,\nu(\left\{f^{-n}(x_{0})\right\})=\delta\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(Df^{n}(f^{-n}(x_{0}))\right)^{s}}\geq\delta\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(Df^{q_{n}}(f^{-q_{n}}(x_{0}))\right)^{s}}\,.

However, by Lemma
2.9
, we have
Df^{q_{n}}(f^{-q_{n}}(x_{0}))\leq C_{1}
for all

. Thus we obtain

1\geq\delta\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,C_{1}^{-s}=\infty,

which is the desired contradiction.
∎

4.
Existence

In this section we show that, for all
s>0
,
\mathcal{A}_{s}
is non-empty (the existence part of Theorem
A
). For the entire section,
s
shall be a fixed positive number.

Let
\Sigma\colon S^{1}\to[0,\infty]
be defined by

(4.1)

\Sigma(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,\left(Df^{n}(x)\right)^{s}.

Observe that if
f^{k}(x)\in\operatorname{Crit}\left(f\right)
for some
k\geq 0
, then
\Sigma(x)<\infty
, since it is just a finite sum. Therefore, there is a dense subset of
S^{1}
(the union of the backward orbits of the critical points) on which
\Sigma
is finite. However, as the following lemma shows, there are points on the circle where
\Sigma
diverges.

Lemma 4.1
.

\Sigma=\infty

\mu
-almost everywhere.

Proof.

We show that the set

(4.2)

A\coloneqq\left\{\,x\in\Lambda\;\middle|\;\Sigma(x)=\infty\,\right\}

has full
\mu
-measure. To do this, first observe that, for all
x\in S^{1}
,

\Sigma(x)=1+(Df(x))^{s}\,\Sigma(f(x))\,.

It follows that
A
is
f
-invariant, so, by the ergodicity of
\mu
, it suffices to show that
\mu(A)>0
.

For each
n\geq 0
,
\left(Df^{n}\right)^{s}\in C^{2}(S^{1})
, so we may apply Jensen’s inequality to obtain

\log\left(\int_{S^{1}}\,\left(Df^{n}\right)^{s}\,d\mu\right)\geq\int_{S^{1}}\,\log\left(Df^{n}\right)^{s}\,d\mu=s\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\,\int_{S^{1}}\,\log Df\circ f^{i}\,d\mu\,.

Since
\mu
is
f
-invariant,

(4.3)

\log\left(\int_{S^{1}}\,\left(Df^{n}\right)^{s}\,d\mu\right)\geq s\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{S^{1}}\,\log Df\,d\mu=0\,,

where we have used Lemma
2.10
. Thus,

\int_{S^{1}}\,\left(Df^{n}\right)^{s}\,d\mu\geq 1

for all
n\geq 0
, which implies in particular that

(4.4)

\int_{S^{1}}\,\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,(Df^{q_{k}})^{s}\,d\mu\geq n

for all
n\geq 1
.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose
\mu(A)=0
; then
\Sigma
must be finite
\mu
-almost everywhere. Now, for each
m\geq 1
, let

(4.5)

X_{m}\coloneqq\left\{\,x\in S^{1}\;\middle|\;\Sigma(x)\leq m\,\right\}\,.

Since we are assuming that
\Sigma
is finite
\mu
-almost everywhere,

(4.6)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\,\mu(X_{m})=1.

Let
0<\epsilon<C_{1}^{-s}
, where
C_{1}=C_{1}(f)
is the constant of Lemma
2.9
. From (
4.6
), there exists
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
m\geq m_{0}
,
\mu(S^{1}\setminus X_{m})\leq\epsilon
.

But then, from (
4.4
), (
4.5
) and Lemma
2.9
, we have that, for all
n\geq 1
and
m\geq m_{0}
,

(4.7)

\begin{split}n&\leq\int_{S^{1}}\,\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,(Df^{q_{k}})^{s}\,d\mu=\int_{X_{m}}\,\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,(Df^{q_{k}})^{s}\,d\mu+\int_{S^{1}\setminus X_{m}}\,\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,(Df^{q_{k}})^{s}\,d\mu\\
&\leq\int_{X_{m}}\,\Sigma\,d\mu+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{S^{1}\setminus X_{m}}\,(Df^{q_{k}})^{s}\,d\mu\leq m\,\mu(X_{m})+n\,C_{1}^{s}\,\mu(S^{1}\setminus X_{m})\\
&\leq m+n\,C_{1}^{s}\,\epsilon\,,\end{split}

which implies that
m\geq n\,(1-C_{1}^{s}\,\epsilon)
. Since
1-C_{1}^{s}\,\epsilon>0
, the contradiction arises by letting
n\to\infty
while keeping
m
fixed.
∎

As it turns out, the only thing we will need in the proof below from the set
A
is the fact that it is non-empty, which certainly follows from Lemma
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
A
, existence part.

Consider the function
\Sigma
and the set
A
from Lemma
4.1
. Fix

x\in A
and, for each
n\geq 1
, let

S_{n}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\,(Df^{i}(x))^{s}\,.

Consider

\mu_{s,x,n}\coloneqq\frac{1}{S_{n}(x)}\,\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\,(Df^{i}(x))^{s}\,\delta_{f^{i}(x)}\,,

which is an atomic probability measure. By compactness, there is a monotone sequence
(n_{k})\subset\mathbb{N}
and
\mu_{s,x}\in\mathcal{P}(S^{1})
such that, for all
\phi\in C^{0}(S^{1})
,

\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d\mu_{s,x,n_{k}}\longrightarrow\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d\mu_{s,x}\,.

In particular, since
(\phi\circ f)(Df)^{s}\in C^{0}(S^{1})
,

\int_{S^{1}}\,(\phi\circ f)(Df)^{s}\,d\mu_{s,x,n_{k}}\longrightarrow\int_{S^{1}}\,(\phi\circ f)(Df)^{s}\,d\mu_{s,x}

for all
\phi\in C^{0}(S^{1})
. We claim that
\mu_{s,x}
is
s
-automorphic under
f
. Indeed, for all
k\geq 1
and
\phi\in C^{0}(S^{1})
, we have

\begin{split}&\quad\left\lvert\int_{S^{1}}\,[\phi-(\phi\circ f)(Df)^{s}]\,d\mu_{s,x,n_{k}}\right\rvert\\
&=\frac{1}{S_{n_{k}}(x)}\,\left\lvert\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n_{k}}-1}\,(Df^{i}(x))^{s}\,[\phi(f^{i}(x))-\phi(f^{i+1}(x))(Df(f^{i}(x)))^{s}]\right\rvert\\
&=\frac{1}{S_{n_{k}}(x)}\,\left\lvert\phi(x)-\phi(f^{q_{n_{k}}}(x))\,(Df^{q_{n_{k}}}(x))^{s}\right\rvert\\
&\leq\left\|\phi\right\|_{C^{0}}\,[1+(Df^{q_{n_{k}}}(x))^{s}]\,\frac{1}{S_{n_{k}}(x)}\leq\left\|\phi\right\|_{C^{0}}(1+C_{1}^{s})\,\frac{1}{S_{n_{k}}(x)}\,,\end{split}

where we have used Lemma
2.9
. Consequently,

\begin{split}&\quad\left\lvert\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d\mu_{s,x}-\int_{S^{1}}\,(\phi\circ f)(Df)^{s}\,d\mu_{s,x}\right\rvert=\lim_{k\to\infty}\,\left\lvert\int_{S^{1}}\,[\phi-(\phi\circ f)(Df)^{s}]\,d\mu_{s,x,n_{k}}\right\rvert\\
&\leq\left\|\phi\right\|_{C^{0}}(1+C_{1}^{s})\,\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{S_{n_{k}}(x)}=0\,,\end{split}

since
x\in A
. Thus,
\mu_{s,x}
is
f
-automorphic of exponent
s
, which concludes the proof. ∎

Remark 4.2
.

In
[
2
]
, Douady and Yoccoz prove the existence part of Theorem
A
in the context of diffeomorphisms through a different approach. First, they define a continuous operator
U_{s,f}\colon\mathcal{M}(S^{1})\to\mathcal{M}(S^{1})
on the space
\mathcal{M}(S^{1})
of signed finite Radon measures on the circle (equipped with the weak-
\ast
topology) by

\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d(U_{s,f}\nu)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\int_{S^{1}}\,(Df)^{s}\,d\nu}\,\int_{S^{1}}\,(\phi\circ f)(Df)^{s}\,d\nu\,.

Clearly, the operator
U_{s,f}
leaves invariant the convex compact set
\mathcal{P}(S^{1})
of Radon probability measures on the circle. The authors then use the Schauder-Tychonoff fixed point theorem to conclude that
U_{s,f}
has a fixed point
\mu_{s}\in\mathcal{P}(S^{1})
, and through some estimates, they conclude that this fixed point
\mu_{s}
must be
f
-automorphic of exponent
s
.

In the critical case, this approach fails. Indeed, if
\nu=\delta_{c}
is a point mass on a critical point
c
of
f
, then
U_{s,f}\nu
is ill-defined, since

\int_{S^{1}}\,(Df)^{s}\,d\nu=(Df(c))^{s}=0\,.

Furthermore, if we remove from
\mathcal{P}(S^{1})
the point masses at the critical points of
f
, then we lose compactness, which is essential to apply the Schauder-Tychonoff fixed point theorem.

6.
Ergodicity and uniqueness

In this section, we prove that automorphic measures for multicritical circle maps with irrational rotation number are ergodic (Theorem
6.8
below). As a consequence, we will obtain the uniqueness part of Theorem
A
(we would like to remark that the non-flatness condition on each critical point of
f
is crucial in order to have uniqueness, see §
7.2
below). In particular, Lebesgue is the unique
f
-automorphic measure of exponent
1
(Corollary
6.9
).

We further show that this uniqueness remains true (up to a scalar multiple) in the context of continuous linear functionals on
C^{0}(S^{1})
(Corollary
6.10
). As we will see in §
7
, Corollary
6.10
, applied to Lebesgue measure (

), is the core step towards proving Theorem

.

6.1.
The
\Gamma

We first introduce a bit of notation. Fix some
s>0
and
\nu\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
. For an interval

and a Borel
f
-invariant set

, we shall denote by
\Gamma(I;B)
the ratio

(6.1)

\Gamma(I;B)\coloneqq\frac{\nu(I\cap B)}{\nu(I)}.

Observe that
\Gamma(I;B)
can be expressed as the quotient of two
\omega
-ratios with respect to
I
and different invariant sets: in the numerator, we take
B
as the
f
-invariant set, and in the denominator, we take
S^{1}
as the invariant set.

By direct analogy with Lemma
5.8
, we thus obtain the following result:

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
n\geq 0
and
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
. Then, for any interval
I\subset\Delta^{\ast}
and
0\leq j<k\leq q_{n+1}
such that the intervals

do not contain any critical point of

, the following holds for all Borel

-invariant sets

:

(6.2)

B_{1}^{-2s}\,\Gamma(I^{j};B)\leq\Gamma(I^{k};B)\leq B_{1}^{2s}\,\Gamma(I^{j};B).

Definition 6.2
.

Let
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
,
0\leq k<q_{n+1}
. We shall say that
k
is a

for

if
f^{k}(\widehat{\Delta})\cap\operatorname{Crit}\left(f\right)\neq\varnothing
.

Since
f
has
N

c_{1},\dots,c_{N}
and the collection
\left\{f^{k}(\widehat{\Delta})\right\}_{k=0}^{q_{n+1}-1}
has intersection multiplicity at most

(Lemma
5.2
), it follows that, for any
n\geq 0
and
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
, there are at most
8N
critical times of type 2 for
\Delta
.

Remark 6.3
.

By a direct analogy with Remark
5.7
, there is some level
n_{1}=n_{1}(f)\geq n_{0}(f)\in\mathbb{N}
, depending only on
f
, such that, for all
n\geq n_{1}
,

and
0\leq k<q_{n+1}
a critical time of type 2 for
\Delta
, we have that: (i)
f^{k}(\widehat{\Delta})
contains a
single
critical point of
f
; and (ii)
f^{k}(\widehat{\Delta})\subset U
, where

is the interval about the critical point of

in

from Proposition

.

With this terminology in mind, the following corollary is now an immediate consequence of Lemma

:

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
n\geq 0
,
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
, and let

be the critical times of type 2 for

. Then, for any interval

, the following holds for all Borel
f
-invariant sets

:

(a)

For any

,

(6.3)

B_{1}^{-2s}\,\Gamma(I^{j};B)\leq\Gamma(I^{\ell};B)\leq B_{1}^{2s}\,\Gamma(I^{j};B)\,;

,

;

(c)

(

.

As we did in Lemma
5.10
, we now turn to the problem of understanding the behavior of

that compose

We are now ready to prove the ergodicity of

Theorem 6.8

(Ergodicity)
.

Let
s>0
and let
\nu\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
be an automorphic measure of exponent

for

. Then
\nu
is ergodic with respect to

Proof.

Let
B\subset S^{1}
be a Borel
f
-invariant set such that
\nu(B)<1
. Our aim is to show that, in fact,
\nu(B)=0
. For
x\in S^{1}
, consider the sequence

\mathcal{V}(x)=\left\{\,\Delta^{\ast}\;\middle|\;n\geq 0,\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{n},x\in\Delta\,\right\}

of triples of adjacent atoms from the dynamical partitions
\mathcal{P}_{n}
such that
x
is contained in the central atom of the triple. As
n
increases, the triples of level
n
in this family shrink to
x
while mantaining definite space on both sides (by the Real Bounds). If
x\in\Lambda
, then there is a unique atom
\Delta_{n}(x)\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
that contains
x
, and
x
is contained in its interior. Therefore, for
x\in\Lambda
,
\mathcal{V}(x)
contains precisely one triple (
\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x)
) of each level
n\geq 0
.

Since
\Lambda\setminus B
has positive
\nu
-measure, we claim that for any given
\epsilon>0
there exist
x\in\Lambda\setminus B
and
n_{2}\geq n_{1}(f)\geq n_{0}(f)
such that, for all
n\geq n_{2}
,

(6.11)

\Gamma(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x);B)=\frac{\nu(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x)\cap B)}{\nu(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x))}<\epsilon\,.

Indeed, note that since
\nu
has no atoms and is supported on the whole circle (recall §
3
), the map
h\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}
given by
h(x)=\int_{[0,x]}\,d\nu
is a circle homeomorphism, which identifies the measure
\nu
with Lebesgue measure on
S^{1}
. Thus, the existence of a point
x
satisfying (
6.11
) follows from the standard Lebesgue Density Theorem.

Now, some necessary distinctions depending on the combinatorics of
f
must be made. If
\rho
is the rotation number of
f
,
\rho=[a_{0},a_{1},\cdots]
, then either we have
a_{n}=1
for every sufficiently large
n
, or
a_{n}\geq 2
occurs infinitely often. In any case, we can choose
n\geq n_{2}
such that either
a_{n+1}=a_{n+2}=1
(in the first case) or
a_{n+1}\geq 2
(in the latter case). We thus fix some level
n\geq n_{2}
that satisfies one of these conditions, so that both (
6.11
) and Corollary
6.7
hold simultaneously.

Observe that the collection
\left\{f^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x))\right\}_{i=0}^{q_{n+1}}
covers the circle and has intersection multiplicity at most
4
(see Lemma
5.2
), so

(6.12)

\begin{split}\nu(B)&=\nu\left(\bigcup_{i=0}^{q_{n+1}}\,f^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x))\cap B\right)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n+1}}\,\nu(f^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x))\cap B)\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n+1}}\,\nu(f^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x)))\,\Gamma(f^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x));B)\end{split}

and furthermore,

(6.13)

\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n+1}}\,\nu(f^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x)))\leq 4

(see Remark
2.6
).

From Corollary
6.7
,

(6.14)

\Gamma(f^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x));B)\leq B_{4}\,\Gamma(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x));B)

so, plugging (
6.14
), (
6.13
) and (
6.11
) into (
6.12
):

(6.15)

\begin{split}\nu(B)&\leq\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n+1}}\,\nu(f^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x)))\,\Gamma(f^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x));B)\leq B_{4}\,\Gamma(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x);B)\,\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n+1}}\,\nu(f^{i}(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x)))\\
&\leq 4\,B_{4}\,\frac{\nu(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x)\cap B)}{\nu(\Delta_{n}^{\ast}(x))}<4\,B_{4}\,\epsilon.\end{split}

By letting
\epsilon\to 0
, we get
\nu(B)=0
.

Thus, there is no Borel
f
-invariant set
B\subset S^{1}
such that
0<\nu(B)<1
, which proves that
\nu
is ergodic.
∎

6.3.
Uniqueness

We now show that ergodicity of
f
-automorphic measures of positive exponent implies that there is a unique
f
-automorphic measure of exponent
s
for each
s>0
. We further extend this uniqueness statement to finite signed measures (
i.e.
, continuous linear functionals on
C^{0}(S^{1})
).

Proof of Theorem
A
, uniqueness part.

Arguing by contradiction, suppose there is some
s>0
such that
\mathcal{A}_{s}
contains two distinct measures,
\mu_{s},\nu
. First, suppose
\nu\ll\mu_{s}
, and let
\psi\in L^{1}(\nu)
be the Radon-Nikodym derivative

\psi=\frac{d\nu}{d\mu_{s}}\,.

Then, as a simple calculation shows,
\psi\circ f=\psi

\mu_{s}
-almost everywhere, i.e.
\psi
is
f
-invariant
\mu_{s}
-almost everywhere. But, from Theorem
6.8
,
\mu_{s}
is ergodic for
f
, so
\psi
must be constant
\mu_{s}
-a.e. Since
\int_{S^{1}}\,\psi\,d\mu_{s}=1
, we conclude that
\psi=1

\mu_{s}
-almost everywhere, and thus,
\mu_{s}=\nu
, contradicting our assumption that
\mu_{s},\nu
are distinct.

Finally, if
\nu
is not absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu_{s}
, let
\rho=\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{s}+\nu)
. Then
\rho\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
(since
\mathcal{A}_{s}
can be easily verified to be convex) and
\mu_{s}\ll\rho
,
\nu\ll\rho
. Thus, from the previous case, we must have
\mu_{s}=\rho=\nu
,
once more in contradiction to our assumption that
\mu_{s},\nu
are distinct.
∎

Thus, we have now given a complete proof of Theorem
A
. Correspondingly, for
s>0
, we shall denote the unique
f
-automorphic measure of exponent
s
by
\mu_{s}
.

Since Lebesgue measure is always
f
-automorphic of exponent
1
, we have the following immediate consequence of Theorem
A
:

Corollary 6.9
.

Lebesgue measure is the unique automorphic measure of exponent
1
for
f
.

We now show that the uniqueness statement of Theorem
A
(and in particular, Corollary
6.9
) remains true (up to a scalar multiple) in the context of continuous linear functionals on
C^{0}(S^{1})
:

Corollary 6.10
.

Let
s>0
. If
L\in C^{0}(S^{1})^{\ast}
is such that

(6.16)

\left\langle L,\phi\right\rangle=\left\langle L,(\phi\circ f)(Df)^{s}\right\rangle

for all
\phi\in C^{0}(S^{1})
, then

(6.17)

L=\left\langle L,1\right\rangle\,\mu_{s}\,.

Proof.

The proof is reproduced almost verbatim from
[
2
]
. From the Riesz Representation Theorem, there is a unique signed finite Radon measure
\nu
on
S^{1}
such that

\left\langle L,\phi\right\rangle=\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d\nu

for all
\phi\in C^{0}(S^{1})
. Therefore, it suffices to show that

(6.18)

\nu=\nu(S^{1})\,\mu_{s}\,.

First, suppose
\nu
is positive. Then
\tilde{\nu}\coloneqq\frac{\nu}{\nu(S^{1})}\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
, so

\nu=\nu(S^{1})\,\tilde{\nu}=\nu(S^{1})\,\mu_{s}\,.

Now, for the general case, let
\nu=\nu_{+}-\nu_{-}
be the Jordan decomposition of
\nu
. We wish to show that

(6.19)

\begin{gathered}\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d\nu_{+}=\int_{S^{1}}\,(\phi\circ f)(Df)^{s}\,d\nu_{+}\,,\\
\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d\nu_{-}=\int_{S^{1}}\,(\phi\circ f)(Df)^{s}\,d\nu_{-}\end{gathered}

for all
\phi\in C^{0}(S^{1})
. Since the linear operator
U_{s}\colon C^{0}(S^{1})\to C^{0}(S^{1})
given by

U_{s}(\psi)=(\psi\circ f)(Df)^{s}

is positive, for all
\phi\in C^{0}(S^{1})
with
\phi\geq 0
, we have

(6.20)

\begin{split}\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d\nu_{+}&=\sup_{0\leq\psi\leq\phi}\,\int_{S^{1}}\,\psi\,d\nu=\sup_{0\leq\psi\leq\phi}\,\int_{S^{1}}\,(\psi\circ f)(Df)^{s}\,d\nu\\
&=\sup_{0\leq\psi\leq U_{s}(\phi)}\,\int_{S^{1}}\,\psi\,d\nu=\int_{S^{1}}\,U_{s}(\phi)\,d\nu_{+}\end{split}

and similarly for
\nu_{-}
. It follows that (
6.19
) holds for all continuous
\phi\geq 0
; by linearity, it must hold for all
\phi\in C^{0}(S^{1})
. Thus, applying the first case to
\nu_{+},\nu_{-}
, we have

\nu=\nu_{+}-\nu_{-}=\nu_{+}(S^{1})\,\mu_{s}-\nu_{-}(S^{1})\,\mu_{s}=\nu(S^{1})\,\mu_{s}

which finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.11
.

It follows from (
6.17
) that all linear functionals
L\in C^{0}(S^{1})^{\ast}
that satisfy (
6.16
) have a
definite sign
: if
L=L_{+}-L_{-}
is its Jordan decomposition, then
L_{+}=0
or
L_{-}=0
, depending on whether
\left\langle L,1\right\rangle
is negative, positive or zero (and in this last case,
L=0
).

7.
Applications

In this final section we prove Theorem
B
(§
7.1
), Theorem
C
(§
7.2
) and Theorem
D
(§
7.3
). With these purposes, we first review some basic results regarding invariant distributions for dynamical systems on compact manifolds.

7.1.
Cohomological equations and invariant distributions

Let
M
be a compact smooth manifold. For integer
0\leq r\leq\infty
, let
C^{r}(M)
be the space of
C^{r}
functions
u\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
, equipped with its
C^{r}
topology. Recall that the
C^{r}
topology turns
C^{r}(M)
into a Banach space for finite
r
and
C^{\infty}(M)
into a Fréchet space, and that a distribution on
M
is simply an element of the continuous dual space
C^{\infty}(M)^{\ast}
; we shall denote the space of distributions on
M
by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(M\right)
, and the value of a distribution
T\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(M\right)
acting on a function
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
by
\left\langle T,u\right\rangle
.

Suppose
T\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(M\right)
and
0\leq k<\infty
are such that there exists
C>0
with

\left\lvert\left\langle T,u\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{k}\quad\forall\;u\in C^{\infty}(M).

In this case,
T
has a unique continuous extension
\tilde{T}\in C^{k}(M)^{\ast}
; we say that
T
has
order at most
k
. In fact, every
T\in C^{k}(M)^{\ast}
is the unique continuous extension of a distribution on
M
. Denoting
C^{k}(M)^{\ast}
by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{k}\left(M\right)
, we have the following chain of inclusions
modulo
unique extensions:

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{0}\left(M\right)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{1}\left(M\right)\hookrightarrow\cdots\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(M\right).

Observe that the Riesz Representation Theorem naturally identifies
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{0}\left(M\right)
with the space
\mathcal{M}(M)
of signed finite Radon measures on
M
.

If
T\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(M\right)
belongs to
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{k}\left(M\right)
for some finite
k
, we say that
T
has finite order, and we define its order as the least such
k
. A noteworthy consequence of the compactness of
M
is that all distributions on
M
have finite order, i.e.

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(M\right)=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{k}\left(M\right)\,.

Now, let
f\colon M\to M
be a
C^{r}
endomorphism of
M
,
0\leq r\leq\infty
.

Definition 7.1

(
C^{\ell}
-coboundary)
.

Let
0\leq\ell\leq\infty
and
\phi\in C^{\ell}(M)
. We say that
\phi
is a
C^{\ell}
-coboundary
for
f
if the
cohomological equation

(7.1)

u\circ f-u=\phi

has a solution
u\in C^{\ell}(M)
.

For integer
0\leq\ell\leq\infty
, the set of
C^{\ell}
-coboundaries for
f
forms a vector subspace of
C^{\ell}(M)
, which we shall denote by
\operatorname{B}\left(f,C^{\ell}(M)\right)
.

Definition 7.2

(Invariant distribution)
.

We say that
T\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{r}\left(M\right)
is
f
-invariant
if

(7.2)

\left\langle T,u\circ f\right\rangle=\left\langle T,u\right\rangle

for all
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

Remark 7.3
.

Let the manifold
M
be the unit circle
S^{1}
. An
f
-automorphic measure
\nu
of exponent
1
naturally induces an
f
-invariant distribution
T\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{1}\left(S^{1}\right)
by letting

\left\langle T,u\right\rangle=\int_{S^{1}}u^{\prime}\,d\nu

for all
u\in C^{1}(S^{1})
. Indeed, note that

\left\langle T,u\circ f\right\rangle=\int_{S^{1}}(u\circ f)^{\prime}\,d\nu=\int_{S^{1}}(u^{\prime}\circ f)\,Df\,d\nu=\int_{S^{1}}u^{\prime}\,d\nu=\left\langle T,u\right\rangle.

Of course, Lebesgue measure (which is automorphic of exponent
1
for any
C^{1}
circle homeomorphism) induces the null distribution
\left\langle T,u\right\rangle=0
. However, automorphic measures of exponent
1
different from Lebesgue provide non-trivial invariant distributions, see §
7.2
below.

For all integer
0\leq k\leq r
, the set
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{k}\left(f\right)
of
f
-invariant distributions of order at most
k
forms a vector subspace of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{k}\left(M\right)
. In fact, equations (
7.1
) and (
7.2
) (by unique extension to
C^{k}
functions) identify
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{k}\left(f\right)
with the (continuous) annihilator of
\operatorname{B}\left(f,C^{k}(M)\right)
.

Thus, by the Hahn-Banach separation theorem,

(7.3)

\operatorname{cl}_{k}\,\operatorname{B}\left(f,C^{k}(M)\right)=\bigcap_{T\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{k}\left(f\right)}\ker T

where
\operatorname{cl}_{k}\,
denotes closure in the
C^{k}
topology.

Furthermore, we have the chain of inclusions

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{0}\left(f\right)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{1}\left(f\right)\hookrightarrow\cdots\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{r}\left(f\right)

and also

(7.4)

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{r}\left(f\right)=\bigcup_{k=0}^{r}\,\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{k}\left(f\right)\,.

The following proposition is a simple but crucial consequence of (
7.3
).

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
f\colon M\to M
be a
C^{r}
endomorphism of a compact smooth manifold
M
,
0\leq r\leq\infty
, and let
\mu
be an
f
-invariant Radon probability measure on
M
. Let
0\leq k\leq r
be an integer. Then

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{k}\left(f\right)=\mathbb{R}\mu

if, and only if, the following holds. For any
\phi\in C^{k}(M)
with
\int_{M}\,\phi\,d\mu=0
, there is a sequence
\left\{\phi_{n}=u_{n}\circ f-u_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\operatorname{B}\left(f,C^{k}(M)\right)
of
C^{k}
-coboundaries for
f
converging to
\phi
in the
C^{k}
topology.

With the above criterion at hand, we are ready to prove that Theorem
A
implies Theorem
B
. The proof given below is taken almost verbatim from
[
27
]
. We reproduce it here for the sake of completeness as well as to indicate the points of the proof in which estimates depending on the bounded variation of
\log Df
for
C^{1+\text{bv}}
-diffeomorphisms must be replaced by estimates suitable for multicritical circle maps and where results from
[
2
]
must be replaced by consequences of Theorem
A
. From Proposition
7.4
, it suffices to show that Theorem
A
implies the following lemma.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
u\in C^{1}(S^{1})
have zero
\mu
-mean, that is,
\int_{S^{1}}\,u\,d\mu=0
. Then there is a sequence
v_{n}
of
C^{1}
functions
S^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(7.5)

v_{n}\circ f-v_{n}\longrightarrow u

and

(7.6)

(v_{n}^{\prime}\circ f)Df-v_{n}^{\prime}\longrightarrow u^{\prime}

uniformly.

Let
u\in C^{1}(S^{1})
,
\int_{S^{1}}\,u\,d\mu=0
, be fixed. The construction of the sequence
v_{n}
from Lemma
7.5
shall be derived as a consequence of the following fact.

Proposition 7.6
.

There exists a sequence
\left\{w_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}\subset C^{0}(S^{1})
such that

(7.7)

(w_{n}\circ f)Df-w_{n}\longrightarrow u^{\prime}

uniformly and, for all
n\geq 1
,
\int_{S^{1}}\,w_{n}\,d\operatorname{Leb}=0
.

Indeed, assume that Proposition
7.6
is true, and for each
n\geq 1
, let
v_{n}\colon S^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

v_{n}(x)=\int_{[0,x]}\,w_{n}(y)\,dy\,,

where
[0,x]
is the positively oriented closed circle interval with endpoints
0,x
.

Observe that, since
\int_{S^{1}}\,w_{n}\,d\operatorname{Leb}=0
,
v_{n}
is well-defined as a
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic function from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
(
i.e.
,
v_{n}\in C^{0}(S^{1})
). Furthermore,
v_{n}\in C^{1}(S^{1})
and
v_{n}^{\prime}=w_{n}
, so

(v_{n}^{\prime}\circ f)Df-v_{n}^{\prime}=(w_{n}\circ f)Df-w_{n}\,.

Thus, Proposition
7.6
implies that the sequence
v_{n}
we have just defined satisfies (
7.6
). It remains to show that the
v_{n}\circ f-v_{n}
also converge uniformly to
u
.

Well, for any
x\in S^{1}
, we have

(7.8)

\begin{split}v_{n}(f(x))-v_{n}(x)-u(x)&=\int_{[0,f(x)]}\,w_{n}(y)\,dy-\int_{[0,x]}\,w_{n}(y)\,dy\\
&\quad-\left(u(0)+\int_{[0,x]}\,u^{\prime}(y)\,dy\right)\\
&=\int_{[0,x]}\,\left[\left(w_{n}(f(y))Df(y)-w_{n}(y)\right)-u^{\prime}(y)\right]\,dy-c_{n}\end{split}

where
c_{n}\coloneqq u(0)-\int_{[0,f(0)]}\,w_{n}(y)\,dy
. Thus,

(7.9)

\left\|(v_{n}\circ f-v_{n}+c_{n})-u\right\|_{C^{0}}\leq\left\|[(w_{n}\circ f)Df-w_{n}]-u^{\prime}\right\|_{C^{0}}\,.

Now, from Proposition
7.6
, the right-hand side in (
7.9
) converges to
0
, so the sequence
\left\{v_{n}\circ f-v_{n}+c_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}
converges uniformly to
u
. Consequently, since
\mu
is
f
-invariant,

(7.10)

c_{n}=\int_{S^{1}}\,(v_{n}\circ f-v_{n}+c_{n})\,d\mu\longrightarrow\int_{S^{1}}\,u\,d\mu=0

so we conclude that (
7.5
) holds for the sequence
\left\{v_{n}\right\}
as well. This finishes the proof of Lemma
7.5
, assuming Proposition
7.6
.

With the knowledge that Proposition
7.6
implies Lemma
7.5
(which in turn implies Theorem
B
), we now show that Theorem
A
implies this proposition. First, a technical lemma, which consists of Proposition
7.6
in the special case
u=f-\operatorname{Id}
.

Lemma 7.7
.

There exists a sequence
\left\{\hat{w}_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1}\subset C^{0}(S^{1})
, with
(\hat{w}_{k}\circ f)Df-\hat{w}_{k}
converging uniformly to
Df-1
, such that, for all
k\geq 1
,
\int_{S^{1}}\,\hat{w}_{k}\,d\operatorname{Leb}=0
.

Proof.

For
k\geq 1
, let
\hat{w}_{k}\in C^{0}(S^{1})
be defined by

(7.11)

\hat{w}_{k}=1-\frac{1}{q_{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{q_{k}-1}Df^{i}\,.

Observe that

\int_{S^{1}}\,Df^{i}\,d\operatorname{Leb}=1\,,

so

(7.12)

\int_{S^{1}}\,\hat{w}_{k}\,d\operatorname{Leb}=1-\frac{1}{q_{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{q_{k}-1}\,\int_{S^{1}}\,Df^{i}\,d\operatorname{Leb}=1-1=0.

Furthermore,

(7.13)

\begin{split}&\quad\left\|\left[(\hat{w}_{k}\circ f)Df-\hat{w}_{k}\right]-(Df-1)\right\|_{C^{0}}\\
&=\left\|\left[\left(Df-\frac{1}{q_{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{q_{k}-1}(Df^{i}\circ f)Df\right)-1+\frac{1}{q_{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{q_{k}-1}Df^{i}\right]-Df+1\right\|_{C^{0}}\\
&=\frac{1}{q_{k}}\left\|1-Df^{q_{k}}\right\|_{C^{0}}\leq\frac{1+\left\|Df^{q_{k}}\right\|_{C^{0}}}{q_{k}}\,.\end{split}

Now, from Lemma
2.9
,

(7.14)

\left\|\left[(\hat{w}_{k}\circ f)Df-\hat{w}_{k}\right]-(Df-1)\right\|_{C^{0}}\leq\frac{1+\left\|Df^{q_{k}}\right\|_{C^{0}}}{q_{k}}\leq\frac{1+C_{1}}{q_{k}}\longrightarrow 0

since
q_{k}\longrightarrow\infty
. Equations (
7.14
) and (
7.12
) prove the lemma.
∎

Remark 7.8
.

The main difference between the proofs of Lemma
7.7
above (in the critical case) and the corresponding lemma in
[
27
, p. 317]
(in the diffeomorphism case) is the use of Lemma
2.9
to bound
\left\|Df^{q_{n}}\right\|_{C^{0}}
, instead of the standard Denjoy inequality (see
[
8
, Section 3.2]
). Furthermore, in our case the
\hat{w}_{k}
are not only continuous, but in fact
C^{2}
, since we require
f
to be at least
C^{3}
.

The proof of Proposition
7.6
given below depends essentially on the crucial fact that, if
L\in C^{0}(S^{1})^{\ast}
satisfies

(7.15)

\left\langle L,(\phi\circ f)Df-\phi\right\rangle=0

for all
\phi\in C^{0}(S^{1})
, then
L
is a scalar multiple of Lebesgue measure. In the diffeomorphism case, this fact is a consequence of
[
2
, Théorème 1]
, while in the critical case, it follows from Theorem
A
(recall Corollary
6.10
).

Proof of Proposition
7.6
.

The proof will result from two claims.

Claim #1:
There is a sequence
\left\{\bar{w}_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}\subset C^{0}(S^{1})
such that

(7.16)

(\bar{w}_{n}\circ f)Df-\bar{w}_{n}\longrightarrow u^{\prime}

uniformly.

Indeed, consider the continuous linear operator
U_{1}\colon C^{0}(S^{1})\to C^{0}(S^{1})
given by

(7.17)

U_{1}\,w=(w\circ f)Df-w

and let
M
be the image of
U_{1}
. If no sequence
\bar{w}_{n}
satisfying (
7.16
) exists, then
u^{\prime}\notin\operatorname{cl}_{0}\,M
, so the Hahn-Banach separation theorem implies the existence of a linear functional
L\in C^{0}(S^{1})^{\ast}
such that
L
is identically null on
M
and
\left\langle L,u^{\prime}\right\rangle=1
. But the fact that
L
is null on
M
is easily seen to be equivalent to (
7.15
), so
L
must be a multiple of Lebesgue measure; this contradicts the fact that
\int_{S^{1}}\,u^{\prime}\,d\operatorname{Leb}=0
(since
u
is
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic). Thus, a sequence
\left\{\bar{w}_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfying (
7.16
) must exist. For each
n\geq 1
, let

c_{n}\coloneqq\int_{S^{1}}\,\bar{w}_{n}\,d\operatorname{Leb}\,,\quad\quad\tilde{w}_{n}\coloneqq\bar{w}_{n}-c_{n}\,.

and choose
k_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left\lvert c_{n}\right\rvert\left\|\left[(\hat{w}_{k_{n}}\circ f)Df-\hat{w}_{k_{n}}\right]-(Df-1)\right\|_{C^{0}}\leq 2^{-n}

(Lemma
7.7
guarantees that this is possible). Finally, define

(7.18)

w_{n}\coloneqq\tilde{w}_{n}+c_{n}\hat{w}_{k_{n}}=\bar{w}_{n}+c_{n}(\hat{w}_{k_{n}}-1)\,.

Observe that
\int_{S^{1}}\,w_{n}\,d\operatorname{Leb}=0
.

Claim # 2:

(7.19)

(w_{n}\circ f)Df-w_{n}\longrightarrow u^{\prime}

uniformly. Observe that proving claim # 2 will finish the proof.

To prove the claim, let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary, and choose
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{-n_{0}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
and

\left\|(\bar{w}_{n}\circ f)Df-\bar{w}_{n}-u^{\prime}\right\|_{C^{0}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}

for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

Thus, for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

(7.20)

\begin{split}&\quad\left\|(w_{n}\circ f)Df-w_{n}-u^{\prime}\right\|_{C^{0}}\\
&\leq\left\|(\bar{w}_{n}\circ f)Df-\bar{w}_{n}-u^{\prime}\right\|_{C^{0}}+\left\lvert c_{n}\right\rvert\left\|\left[(\hat{w}_{k_{n}}-1)\circ f\right]Df-(\hat{w}_{k_{n}}-1)\right\|_{C^{0}}\\
&<\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\left\lvert c_{n}\right\rvert\left\|\left[(\hat{w}_{k_{n}}\circ f)Df-\hat{w}_{k_{n}}\right]-(Df-1)\right\|_{C^{0}}\\
&\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}+2^{-n}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}=\epsilon\end{split}

which proves the claim.
∎

We have thus proved Theorem
B
.

7.2.
Wandering intervals and invariant distributions

Before entering the proof of Theorem
D
, let us briefly explain why Theorem
C
holds.

For any given irrational number
\rho\in(0,1)
, Hall was able to construct in
[
18
]
a
C^{\infty}
homeomorphism
f\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}
, with rotation number
\rho(f)=\rho
, having a wandering interval
I
(
i.e.
,
I
is an open interval such that
f^{n}(I)
is disjoint from
f^{m}(I)
whenever
n\neq m
in
\mathbb{Z}
). These examples, so-called
Hall’s examples
, present a single critical point
c
which is
flat
: the successive derivatives of
f
(of all orders) vanish at
c
. Note that this critical point necessarily belongs to the invariant Cantor set of
f
(otherwise, a
C^{\infty}
perturbation supported on the wandering interval containing
c
would produce a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism with irrational rotation number and wandering intervals). In particular,
f^{n}\colon I\to f^{n}(I)
is a diffeomorphism for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

To these Hall’s examples, we will apply the following general remark.

Lemma 7.9
.

Let
f\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}
be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism with a wandering interval
I\subset S^{1}
such that
f^{n}\colon I\to f^{n}(I)
is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then, the series

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}Df^{n}(x)

is finite for Lebesgue almost every
x\in I
.

Proof.

Fix some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, and consider the set

A_{\ell}=\big\{x\in I:\,\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}Df^{n}(x)\geq\ell\big\}\,.

We claim that
\operatorname{Leb}(A_{\ell})\leq 1/\ell
, where
\operatorname{Leb}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
S^{1}
. Indeed, by the Monotone Convergence Theorem (recall here that
Df
is non-negative on the whole circle),

\int_{A_{\ell}}\,\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}Df^{n}\,\,d\operatorname{Leb}\leq\int_{I}\,\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}Df^{n}\,\,d\operatorname{Leb}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\int_{I}\,Df^{n}\,\,d\operatorname{Leb}\,.

But

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\int_{I}\,Df^{n}\,\,d\operatorname{Leb}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\big|f^{n}(I)\big|\leq 1,

since
I
is a wandering interval and
f^{n}\colon I\to f^{n}(I)
is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, we have that

\int_{A_{\ell}}\,\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}Df^{n}\,\,d\operatorname{Leb}\leq 1\,,

and then
\operatorname{Leb}(A_{\ell})\leq 1/\ell
for all
\ell\geq 1
, as claimed. The claim clearly implies the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
C
.

Let
f\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}
be a Hall’s example as above, having a wandering interval
I\subset S^{1}
. Pick some
x\in I
such that
S=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}Df^{n}(x)
is finite (recall that, by Lemma
7.9
, this series is finite for Lebesgue almost every
x
outside the
f
-invariant Cantor set). Following
[
2
, Section 3.1]
, we consider the probability measure

\nu=\frac{1}{S}\,\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}Df^{n}(x)\,\delta_{f^{n}(x)}\,.

We see at once that
\nu
is
f
-automorphic of exponent
1
(note, in particular, that the
uniqueness
part of Theorem
A
also breaks down if we remove the non-flatness condition on the critical points of
f
). Now we consider
T\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{1}\left(S^{1}\right)
given by

\left\langle T,u\right\rangle=\int_{S^{1}}u^{\prime}\,d\nu.

As explained in Remark
7.3
above, the distribution
T
is
f
-invariant. Finally, to prove that
T
is
not
a scalar multiple of the unique
f
-invariant probability measure
\mu
is straightforward (compare
[
27
, Section 3]
). Indeed, let
u\colon S^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be of class
C^{1}
, supported on the wandering interval
I
, and such that
x
is not a critical point of
u
. Then, on one hand, we have

\left\langle T,u\right\rangle=\int_{S^{1}}u^{\prime}\,d\nu=\frac{1}{S}\,\,u^{\prime}(x)\neq 0.

On the other hand, since the support of
u
is disjoint from the non-wandering set of
f
, we certainly have
\int_{S^{1}}u\,d\mu=0
. This finishes the proof of Theorem
C
.
∎

7.3.
Denjoy-Koksma inequality improved

We finish this paper by proving that Theorem
A
implies Theorem
D
. In fact, we will present two different proofs of Theorem
D
. The first proof works only when the observable
\phi
is of class
C^{1}
, whereas the second works in the general case,
i.e.
, when
\phi
is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue. The former follows
[
1
, pages 513–514]
, and relies on the absence of invariant distributions of order
1
(obtained in Theorem
B
), while the latter follows
[
26
, pages 379–381]
, and it only uses the ergodicity of the Lebesgue measure under a multicritical circle map (as established in Theorem
6.8
).

Proof of Theorem
D
for
C^{1}
observables.

For any given
\phi\in C^{1}(S^{1})
note that
\phi-\int_{S^{1}}\phi\,d\mu
belongs to
\ker\mu
. Combining Theorem
B
with Proposition
7.4
we have that, for any given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
u\in C^{1}(S^{1})
such that

\left\|\big(u\circ f-u\big)-\big(\phi-\int_{S^{1}}\phi\,d\mu\big)\right\|_{C^{1}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,.

Let
\tilde{\phi}\in C^{1}(S^{1})
be given by
\tilde{\phi}=u\circ f-u+\int_{S^{1}}\phi\,d\mu
, so that
\big\|\tilde{\phi}-\phi\big\|_{C^{1}}\leq\varepsilon/2
. Since
\mu
is
f
-invariant, we have
\int_{S^{1}}\tilde{\phi}\,d\mu=\int_{S^{1}}\phi\,d\mu
. Now for any given
x\in S^{1}
,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\phi\big(f^{i}(x)\big)-q_{n}\int_{S^{1}}\phi\,d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\big(\phi-\tilde{\phi}\big)\big(f^{i}(x)\big)-q_{n}\int_{S^{1}}\big(\phi-\tilde{\phi}\big)\,d\mu\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\tilde{\phi}\big(f^{i}(x)\big)-q_{n}\int_{S^{1}}\tilde{\phi}\,d\mu\right|.

Let us estimate both terms at the right side of this inequality. On one hand, by the standard Denjoy-Koksma inequality,

\left|\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\big(\phi-\tilde{\phi}\big)\big(f^{i}(x)\big)-q_{n}\int_{S^{1}}\big(\phi-\tilde{\phi}\big)\,d\mu\right|\leq\big\|\tilde{\phi}-\phi\big\|_{C^{1}}\leq\varepsilon/2\,.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\tilde{\phi}\big(f^{i}(x)\big)-q_{n}\int_{S^{1}}\tilde{\phi}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\left[u\big(f^{i+1}(x)\big)-u\big(f^{i}(x)\big)+\int_{S^{1}}\phi\,d\mu\right]-q_{n}\int_{S^{1}}\tilde{\phi}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=u\big(f^{q_{n}}(x)\big)-u(x)+q_{n}\left(\int_{S^{1}}\phi\,d\mu-\int_{S^{1}}\tilde{\phi}\,d\mu\right)

\displaystyle=u\big(f^{q_{n}}(x)\big)-u(x)\,.

In particular,

\left|\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\tilde{\phi}\big(f^{i}(x)\big)-q_{n}\int_{S^{1}}\tilde{\phi}\,d\mu\right|\leq\|u\|_{C^{1}}\,\|f^{q_{n}}-\operatorname{Id}\|_{C^{0}}\,.

By minimality of
f
, we can choose
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|u\|_{C^{1}}\,\|f^{q_{n}}-\operatorname{Id}\|_{C^{0}}<\varepsilon/2
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Therefore,
\left|\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\phi\big(f^{i}(x)\big)-q_{n}\int_{S^{1}}\phi\,d\mu\right|<\varepsilon
for all
x\in S^{1}
and
n\geq n_{0}
. Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, this finishes the proof.
∎

Let us now give a proof of Theorem
D
that works in general, following
[
26
, Section 2]
. With this purpose, we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 7.10
.

If
v\in L^{1}(\operatorname{Leb})
is a Lebesgue-integrable function on the circle such that
\int_{S^{1}}\,v\,d\operatorname{Leb}=0
, then there exists a sequence
v_{n}
of Lebesgue-integrable functions on the circle such that
\int_{S^{1}}\,v_{n}\,d\operatorname{Leb}=0
for all
n
and

(v_{n}\circ f)Df-v_{n}\longrightarrow v

in the
L^{1}
sense.

Proof.

Consider the continuous linear operator
U\colon L^{1}(\operatorname{Leb})\to L^{1}(\operatorname{Leb})
given by

Uw=(w\circ f)Df-w
, and let
M
be the image of
U
. First, assume that
v\notin\operatorname{cl}\,M
; then, by the Hahn-Banach theorem, there exists
L\in L^{1}(\operatorname{Leb})^{\ast}
such that
L
is identically null on
M
and
\left\langle L,v\right\rangle=1
. By identification of
L^{1}(\operatorname{Leb})^{\ast}
with
L^{\infty}(\operatorname{Leb})
, there exists an
L^{\infty}
function
\phi
such that

\left\langle L,w\right\rangle=\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,w\,d\operatorname{Leb}

for all
w\in L^{1}(\operatorname{Leb})
. But then, for all
\psi=Uw\in M
,

\begin{split}0&=\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,\left[(w\circ f)Df-w\right]\,d\operatorname{Leb}=\int_{S^{1}}\,(\phi\circ f^{-1})\,w\,d\operatorname{Leb}-\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,w\,d\operatorname{Leb}\,,\end{split}

where we have used the fact that Lebesgue measure is
f
-automorphic of exponent
1
.

Since the previous equality holds for all Lebesgue-integrable
w
, we conclude that
\phi
is
f
-invariant
\operatorname{Leb}
-almost everywhere. As proved in Section
6.2
, any multicritical circle map with irrational rotation number is ergodic with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Therefore,
\phi
must be almost everywhere constant,
i.e.
, there exists some constant
\beta
such that
\phi=\beta

\operatorname{Leb}
-almost everywhere. But then

1=\left\langle L,v\right\rangle=\int_{S^{1}}\,v\,\phi\,d\operatorname{Leb}=\beta\,\int_{S^{1}}\,v\,d\operatorname{Leb}\,,

contradicting the fact that
v\in\ker\operatorname{Leb}
. Thus,
v\in\operatorname{cl}\,M
, and there is some sequence
\bar{w}_{n}
of
L^{1}
functions for which

(\bar{w}_{n}\circ f)Df-\bar{w}_{n}\longrightarrow v

in the
L^{1}
sense.

Now, recall the functions
\hat{w}_{k}
from Lemma
7.7
: we have that

(\hat{w}_{k}\circ f)Df-\hat{w}_{k}\longrightarrow Df-1

uniformly. If we define
c_{n}\coloneqq\int_{S^{1}}\,\bar{w}_{n}\,d\operatorname{Leb}
, then the desired sequence
w_{n}
is given by

w_{n}\coloneqq\bar{w}_{n}-c_{n}+c_{n}\,\hat{w}_{k_{n}}\,,

where the
k_{n}
are chosen as in the proof of Proposition
7.6
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
D
.

For any given
\phi\in AC(S^{1})
, we have that its derivative
v\coloneqq D\phi
exists
\operatorname{Leb}
-almost everywhere, and furthermore,
\int_{S^{1}}\,v\,d\operatorname{Leb}=0
(since
\phi
is
\mathbb{Z}
-periodic). Let
\epsilon>0
be fixed. By Lemma
7.10
, there exists
w\in L^{1}(\operatorname{Leb})
,
w\in\ker\operatorname{Leb}
, such that

(7.21)

u\coloneqq v-\left[(w\circ f)Df-w\right]

satisfies

(7.22)

\int_{S^{1}}\,\left\lvert u\right\rvert\,d\operatorname{Leb}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Now, let
\psi,\xi\in AC(S^{1})
be given by

\xi(x)=\int_{[0,x]}\,u\,d\operatorname{Leb}-\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d\mu,\quad\psi(x)=\int_{[0,x]}\,w\,d\operatorname{Leb}\,,

so that
\int_{S^{1}}\,\xi\,d\mu=0
and

(7.23)

\xi=\phi-(\psi\circ f)+\psi-\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d\mu\,,

or equivalently,

(7.24)

\phi=\xi+(\psi\circ f)-\psi+\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d\mu.

By a telescoping sum, we have

(7.25)

\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\,\phi\circ f^{i}-q_{n}\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d\mu=\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\,\xi\circ f^{i}+\psi\circ f^{q_{n}}-\psi.

We now proceed to estimate both terms on the right hand side of the above equation. On the one hand, by the standard Denjoy-Koksma inequality,

(7.26)

\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\,\xi\circ f^{i}\right\|_{C^{0}}\leq\operatorname{var}(\xi)\leq\left\|D\xi\right\|_{L^{1}(\operatorname{Leb})}=\left\|u\right\|_{L^{1}(\operatorname{Leb})}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

On the other hand, by the minimality of
f
,

(7.27)

\left\|\psi\circ f^{q_{n}}-\psi\right\|_{C^{0}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}

for sufficiently large
n
. By the triangle inequality, equations (
7.25
)-(
7.27
) together show that, for sufficiently large
n
,

(7.28)

\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{q_{n}-1}\,\phi\circ f^{i}-q_{n}\int_{S^{1}}\,\phi\,d\mu\right\|_{C^{0}}\leq\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, this concludes the proof of Theorem
D
.
∎

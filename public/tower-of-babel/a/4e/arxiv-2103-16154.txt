Abstract
In this paper, we develop a symmetric accelerated stochastic Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (SAS-ADMM) for solving separable convex optimization problems with linear constraints. The objective function is the sum of a possibly nonsmooth convex function and an average function of many smooth convex functions. Our proposed algorithm combines both ideas of ADMM and the techniques of accelerated stochastic gradient methods possibly with variance reduction to solve the smooth subproblem. One main feature of SAS-ADMM is that its dual variable is symmetrically updated after each update of the separated primal variable, which would allow a more flexible and larger convergence region of the dual variable compared with that of standard deterministic or stochastic ADMM. This new stochastic optimization algorithm is shown to have ergodic converge in expectation with 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / T ) {\cal{O}}(1/T) convergence rate, where T T is the number of outer iterations. Our preliminary experiments indicate the proposed algorithm is very effective for solving separable optimization problems from big-data applications. Finally, 3-block extensions of the algorithm and its variant of an accelerated stochastic augmented Lagrangian method are discussed in the appendix.

Introduction

We consider the following structured composite convex optimization problem with
linear equality constraints:

\min\{f({\bm{x}})+g({\bm{y}})\mid{\bm{x}}\in{\cal{X}},\;{\bm{y}}\in{\cal{Y}},\;A{\bm{x}}+B{\bm{y}}={\bm{b}}\},

(1)

where
{\cal{X}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}},{\cal{Y}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
are closed convex subsets,

A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n_{1}}
,
B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n_{2}}
,
{\bm{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

are given,
g:{\cal{Y}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a convex
but possibly nonsmooth function, and
f
is an average of
N
real-valued convex functions:

f({\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}f_{j}({\bm{x}}).

We assume that each
f_{j}
defined on an open set
containing
{\cal{X}}
is Lipschitz continuously differentiable on
\mathcal{X}
.
Problem (
1
) is also referred as regularized empirical risk minimization
in big-data applications
[
24
,
33
]
, including classification and regression models
in machine learning, where
N
denotes the sample size and
f_{j}
corresponds to the empirical loss.
A major difficulty for solving (
1
) is that the sample size
N
can be very large
such that it is often computationally prohibitive to evaluate either the full function value or
the gradient of
f
at each iteration of an algorithm.
Hence, it is essential for an effective algorithm, e.g., a stochastic gradient method,
to explore the summation structure of
f
in the objective function.

The augmented Lagrangian function of (
1
) is

{\cal{L}}_{\beta}\left({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\right)={\cal{L}}\left({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\right)+\frac{\beta}{2}\left\|A{\bm{x}}+B{\bm{y}}-{\bm{b}}\right\|^{2},

(2)

where
\beta>0
is a penalty parameter,
\lambda
is the Lagrange multiplier and
the Lagrangian of (
1
) is defined as

{\cal{L}}\left({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\right)=f({\bm{x}})+g({\bm{y}})-{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{\sf T}\left(A{\bm{x}}+B{\bm{y}}-{\bm{b}}\right).

(3)

Although the Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM) can be applied to solve (
1
),
it does not take advantage of the separable structure of (
1
).
As a splitting version of ALM, the standard
Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM,
[
10
,
11
]
)
exploits the separable structure of the objective function and performs the following iterations:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\bm{x}}^{k+1}\in\arg\min\limits_{{\bm{x}}\in{\cal{X}}}\;{\cal{L}}_{\beta}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}^{k},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k}),\\
{\bm{y}}^{k+1}\in\arg\min\limits_{{\bm{y}}\in{\cal{Y}}}\;{\cal{L}}_{\beta}({\bm{x}}^{k+1},{\bm{y}},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k}),\\
{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k+1}={\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k}-s\beta\left(A{\bm{x}}^{k+1}+B{\bm{y}}^{k+1}-{\bm{b}}\right),\end{array}\right.

where
s\in(0,\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2})
is the stepsize for updating the dual variable
\lambda
.

If the Peaceman-Rachford Splitting
Method (PRSM,
[
25
]
) is applied to the dual of (
1
), then we obtain a variation of ADMM,
whose iteration reads

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}{\bm{x}}^{k+1}&\in&\arg\min\limits_{{\bm{x}}\in{\cal{X}}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}^{k},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k}),\\
{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}&=&{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k}-\beta\left(A{\bm{x}}^{k+1}+B{\bm{y}}^{k}-{\bm{b}}\right){,}\\
{\bm{y}}^{k+1}&\in&\arg\min\limits_{{\bm{y}}\in{\cal{Y}}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}({\bm{x}}^{k+1},{\bm{y}},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}),\\
{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k+1}&=&{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}-\beta\left(A{\bm{x}}^{k+1}+B{\bm{y}}^{k+1}-{\bm{b}}\right).\end{array}\right.

PRSM is also called Symmetric ADMM (S-ADMM) since the Lagrange multipliers are symmetrically updated twice in each loop. Note that both updates of dual variable in PRSM use the same constant stepsize 1.
Recently, Luo-Yang
[
23
]
proposed a fast S-ADMM for solving (
1
) with only equality constraints, where the Nesterov’s acceleration technique was applied for an additional update of

{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k+1}
and the
{\bm{y}}
variable was updated again by solving

\min\limits_{{\bm{y}}}g({\bm{y}})-(\widehat{{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}}^{k+1})^{\sf T}B{\bm{y}}~~\textrm{ with }~~\widehat{{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}}^{k+1}={\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k+1}+\frac{\theta^{k+1}(1-\theta^{k})}{\theta^{k}}({\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k+1}-{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k})

and
\theta^{k+1}={2}/(k+1)
.

Motivated from the ideas of enlarging the dual stepsize in
[
16
]
,
Gu, et al.
[
13
]
proposed a symmetric proximal ADMM whose dual variable is updated twice with different stepsizes.
Meanwhile, the following extension of S-ADMM was developed by He, et al.
[
17
]
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}{\bm{x}}^{k+1}&\in&\arg\min\limits_{{\bm{x}}\in{\cal{X}}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}^{k},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k}),\\
{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}&=&{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k}-\tau\beta\left(A{\bm{x}}^{k+1}+B{\bm{y}}^{k}-{\bm{b}}\right){,}\\
{\bm{y}}^{k+1}&\in&\arg\min\limits_{{\bm{y}}\in{\cal{Y}}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}({\bm{x}}^{k+1},{\bm{y}},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}),\\
{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k+1}&=&{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}-s\beta\left(A{\bm{x}}^{k+1}+B{\bm{y}}^{k+1}-{\bm{b}}\right),\end{array}\right.

(4)

where, for convergence, the stepsize pair
(\tau,s)
is required to belong to the following region:

\Delta_{0}=\left\{(\tau,s)\mid~s\in(0,(1+\sqrt{5})/2),~\tau+s>0,~\tau\in(-1,1),~\mid\tau\mid<1+s-s^{2}\right\}.

Bai, et al.
[
1
]
further designed a Generalized Symmetric ADMM (GS-ADMM) for solving
a multi-block separable convex optimization and enlarged the above region

to

defined in (

Numerical experiments show that symmetrically updating the dual variable in a more
flexible way often improves the algorithm performance
[
1
,
13
,
17
]
.
The sublinear convergence rate of GS-ADMM in the nonergodic sense and its linear convergence rate
were shown in
[
2
]
.
To our knowledge,
\Delta
is currently the largest convergence
region of the dual stepsizes for symmetric ADMM-type algorithms and has been used in the logarithmic-quadratic proximal based ADMM for solving the 2-block problems
[

]
and the grouped multi-block problems

Another line of developing ADMM is to apply relaxation techniques such as using

{\bm{\chi}}^{k+1}=\alpha A{\bm{x}}^{k+1}+(1-\alpha)({\bm{b}}-B{\bm{y}}^{k})

to replace
A{\bm{x}}^{k+1}
during the update of
{\bm{y}}^{k+1}
and
{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k+1}
, where
\alpha\in(0,2)
is a relaxation factor. This leads to the classical Generalized ADMM (G-ADMM,
[

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}{\bm{x}}^{k+1}&\in&\arg\min\limits_{{\bm{x}}\in{\cal{X}}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}^{k},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k}),\\
{\bm{y}}^{k+1}&\in&\arg\min\limits_{{\bm{y}}\in{\cal{Y}}}g({\bm{y}})-({\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k})^{\sf T}B{\bm{y}}+\frac{\beta}{2}\left\|{\bm{\chi}}^{k+1}+B{\bm{y}}-{\bm{b}}\right\|^{2},\\

Clearly, G-ADMM with

ADMM using some additional proximal terms in their subproblems is also called G-ADMM.
For instance, the
{\bm{x}}
-subproblem in
[
8
]
was proposed as

\min\limits_{{\bm{x}}\in{\cal{X}}}{\cal{L}}_{\beta}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}^{k},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{k})+\frac{1}{2}\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{k}\|_{G}^{2}
,
where

is a symmetric positive definite matrix.
For more recent G-ADMMs using possibly indefinite proximal terms,
one may refer to the references
[
19
,
28
]
.

For convergence rate of ADMM, it is well-known that most of deterministic ADMM algorithms

Assuming that an error bound condition holds, the dual stepsize

Zhang et al.

More recently, Yuan-Zeng-Zhang

2.2
Variational characterization of (
1

Denote

It’s well-known in convex optimization that any saddle-point of
the Lagrangian (

1

A point

is called a saddle-point of
{\cal{L}}\left({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\right)
if it satisfies

{\cal{L}}\left({\bm{x}}^{*},{\bm{y}}^{*},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}\right)\leq{\cal{L}}\left({\bm{x}}^{*},{\bm{y}}^{*},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{*}\right)\leq{\cal{L}}\left({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{*}\right),\quad\forall{\bm{w}}\in\Omega,

(8)

which is equivalent to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}f({\bm{x}})-f({\bm{x}}^{*})&+&({\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{*})^{\sf T}\left(-A^{\sf T}{\mbox{\boldmath$\lambda$}}^{*}\right)&\geq&0,\\

&&A{\bm{x}}^{*}+B{\bm{y}}^{*}-{\bm{b}}&=&{\bm{0}}.\end{array}\right.

Rewriting these inequalities as a more compact form, it gives

\quad F({\bm{w}})-F({\bm{w}}^{*})+({\bm{w}}-{\bm{w}}^{*})^{\sf T}{\cal{J}}({\bm{w}}^{*})\geq 0,\quad\forall{\bm{w}}\in\Omega.

(9)

Notice that the affine mapping
{\cal{J}}(\cdot)
is skew-symmetric. So, we have

\displaystyle\left({\bm{w}}-\overline{{\bm{w}}}\right)^{\sf T}\left[{\cal{J}}({\bm{w}})-{\cal{J}}(\overline{{\bm{w}}})\right]\equiv 0,\quad\forall{\bm{w}},\overline{{\bm{w}}}\in\Omega.

(10)

Hence, (
9

\quad F({\bm{w}})-F({\bm{w}}^{*})+({\bm{w}}-{\bm{w}}^{*})^{\sf T}{\cal{J}}({\bm{w}})\geq 0,\quad\forall{\bm{w}}\in\Omega.

(11)

The above discussion shows that the saddle-point
{\bm{w}}^{*}
can be
also characterized by the variational inequality (
11

6
Conclusion

We proposed a symmetric accelerated stochastic
alternating direction method of multipliers, called SAS-ADMM, whose dual variables are symmetrically
updated. We gave the specific dual stepsizes region
ensuring the global convergence, which is larger than those in the literature.
Under proper choice of the algorithm parameters, we proved the convergence of SAS-ADMM in expectation with the worst-case
O(1/T)
convergence rate, where
T
represents the number of iterations.
Our preliminary experiments showed that by symmetrically updating the dual variables
using a more flexible region, SAS-ADMM could outperform AS-ADMM, which only updates the
dual variable once, for solving some
structured optimization problems arising in machine learning.

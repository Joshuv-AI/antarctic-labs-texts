Turning non-smooth points into rational points
Cesar Hilario

Abstract
. In a recent paper, the author and Stöhr established a bound on the number of iterated Frobenius pullbacks needed to transform a non-smooth non-decomposed point on a regular geometrically integral curve into a rational point. In this note we improve this result, by establishing a new bound that is sharp in every characteristic p > 0 p>0 .

14G05, 14G17, 14H05, 14H45

1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a regular curve over an imperfect field
K
of characteristic
p>0
, and let
\mathfrak{p}
be a non-smooth point on
C
that is non-decomposed.
By
non-decomposed
we mean that
\mathfrak{p}
has a unique pre-image under the natural morphism
C\otimes_{K}\overline{K}\to C
.
In
[
HiSt22
]
the authors established a bound for the number of iterated Frobenius pullbacks needed to transform
\mathfrak{p}
into a rational point.
To state this precisely, let
C^{(p^{n})}
denote the
n
th iterated Frobenius pullback of
C
, so that we have an infinite chain of relative Frobenius morphisms

C^{(p^{0})}=C\to C^{(p^{1})}\to C^{(p^{2})}\to C^{(p^{3})}\to\cdots.

By taking normalization we obtain a sequence of regular curves over
K

C_{0}=C\to C_{1}\to C_{2}\to C_{3}\to\cdots.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
HiSt22
]
)
.

Let
C|K
be a regular curve and let
\mathfrak{p}\in C
be a non-decomposed non-smooth point of geometric
\delta
-invariant
\delta(\mathfrak{p})>0
.
Then the image
\mathfrak{p}_{n}\in C_{n}
of
\mathfrak{p}
is a
K
-rational point for all
n\geq\log_{p}(2\delta(\mathfrak{p})+1)
. If in addition the integer
\frac{2}{p-1}\delta(\mathfrak{p})
is not a sum of consecutive
p
-powers then
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is a
K
-rational point for all
n\geq\log_{p}(2\delta(\mathfrak{p})+1)-1
.

Here, by a
curve
we mean a proper geometrically integral
K
-scheme of dimension 1.
Recall that the
geometric
\delta
-invariant

\delta(\mathfrak{p})
of a point
\mathfrak{p}\in C
is defined as

\delta(\mathfrak{p})=\sum_{\mathfrak{q}}\delta(\mathfrak{q}),\quad\delta(\mathfrak{q})=\dim_{\overline{K}}\big(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C}_{\overline{K}}}/\mathcal{O}_{C_{\overline{K}}}\big)_{\mathfrak{q}},

where the sum runs over the points
\mathfrak{q}
on
C_{\overline{K}}=C\otimes_{K}\overline{K}
above
\mathfrak{p}

and
\pi:\widetilde{C}_{\overline{K}}\to C_{\overline{K}}
denotes the normalization morphism.
Note that
\frac{2}{p-1}\delta(\mathfrak{p})
is indeed an integer (each
\delta(\mathfrak{q})
is a multiple of
\frac{p-1}{2}

[
HiSt22
, Remark 2.18]
), which vanishes if and only if
\mathfrak{p}
is smooth.

In characteristic
p=2
the above bound is sharp
[
HiSt22
, Proposition 2.22]
.
In other words, if for a given integer
d>0
we write
n=\lceil\log_{2}(2d+1)\rceil
or
n=\lceil\log_{2}(2d+1)\rceil-1
for the bound in the theorem, then there exist a regular curve
C|K
and a non-decomposed point
\mathfrak{p}\in C
with
\delta(\mathfrak{p})=d
whose image
\mathfrak{p}_{n-1}
is not a
K
-rational point.

In characteristic
p>2
there is less flexibility, because the integers
\delta(\mathfrak{p})
are all multiples of
\frac{p-1}{2}
but not every such multiple can occur as the geometric
\delta
-invariant
\delta(\mathfrak{p})
of a non-decomposed point
\mathfrak{p}
.
In fact one can show (see Proposition
3.1
) that a positive integer
d
is
admissible
, i.e., there exist a regular curve
C|K
and a non-decomposed point
\mathfrak{p}\in C
with
\delta(\mathfrak{p})=\frac{p-1}{2}{\cdot}d
, if and only if the congruence relation
d\not\equiv-1\pmod{p}
is satisfied.
The goal of this paper is to determine a sharp bound for every admissible
d>0
.

Let
\lambda_{p}(d)
denote the smallest integer
m
satisfying the following property: for any regular curve
C|K
and any non-decomposed point
\mathfrak{p}\in C
with
\delta(\mathfrak{p})=\frac{p-1}{2}d
the image
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is a
K
-rational point for all
n\geq m
.
Note that Theorem
1.1
reformulates as

\lambda_{p}(d)\leq\tau_{p}(d)\quad\text{ for every admissible $d>0$},

where

(1)

\displaystyle\tau_{p}(d)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\lceil\log_{p}((p-1)d+1)\rceil&\text{if $d$ is a sum of consecutive $p$-powers,}\\
\lceil\log_{p}((p-1)d+1)\rceil-1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Our main result (see Theorem
3.2
) provides the exact value of
\lambda_{p}(d)
.

To prove our theorem we construct a class of regular curves
C|K
with non-decomposed points
\mathfrak{p}\in C
whose images
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
are not
K
-rational for suitable
n
.
These curves let us bound
\lambda_{p}(d)
from below, and show that the difference
\tau_{p}(d)-\lambda_{p}(d)
is at most
1
.
Through a careful analysis we then identify in which cases the bound
\tau_{p}(d)
is sharp, i.e.,
\lambda_{p}(d)=\tau_{p}(d)
, or fails to be sharp, i.e.,
\lambda_{p}(d)=\tau_{p}(d)-1
.
This involves studying certain partitions of the integers
\delta(\mathfrak{p})
, which were introduced in
[
HiSt22
]
.

A by-product of our construction is a subclass of curves
C|K
with non-decomposed points
\mathfrak{p}\in C
that realize the sharp bounds
\lambda_{p}(d)
.
While explicit descriptions of each point
\mathfrak{p}
and its images
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
are obtained, we have no a-priori control over the remaining points on the curves
C
, hence it is difficult to describe their global geometry, e.g., compute their genus
g=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C})=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C_{\overline{K}}})
.
These curves, however, share a common feature: they are geometrically rational, or in other words, each base change
C_{\overline{K}}
has geometric genus
\overline{g}=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C}_{\overline{K}}})=0
.
Though it should be possible (but not obvious) to build examples with
\overline{g}>0
,
it is not clear whether the same sharp bound
\lambda_{p}(d)
works if further restrictions on the regular curves
C|K
considered are imposed, for instance by allowing only curves of positive genus
\overline{g}
, or by requesting that
\mathfrak{p}
be the only non-smooth point on
C
.

We remark that the distinction between a regular curve and a smooth curve
is relevant only over base fields that are imperfect, in particular only in positive characteristic.
The interest in this phenomenon is related to the arithmetic of algebraic surfaces in characteristics
p=2,3
, where the so-called
quasi-elliptic curves

[
Queen71
]
, i.e., regular but non-smooth curves of genus
g=1
,
play a central role.
The existence of such curves, which occur as generic fibres of quasi-elliptic fibrations, particularly on K3 and Enriques surfaces
[
BM76
,
CDL23
,
KKM20
]
, is indeed the reason why the classification of algebraic surfaces is more subtle in these characteristics than in other characteristics.
Recently, there has been an interest in investigating higher genus curves
[
HiSc23
]
and higher dimensional varieties
[
FaSc20
,
Tan21
]
.

We note that an arbitrary regular curve
C|K
can be studied by looking at the base change
C_{L}
, where
L=K^{sep}
,
since the Galois group
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L|K)
acts on
C_{L}
with quotient isomorphic to
C
.
In other words, to understand the regular curve
C|K
it suffices to understand the
L
-scheme
C_{L}|L
, which is a regular curve whose points are all non-decomposed,
together with the action of
G
; see Section
2
for details.
This is reminiscent of the situation in characteristic zero, where one can interpret a regular (actually smooth) curve
C|K
as the quotient of the smooth curve
C_{\overline{K}}|\overline{K}
by the action of the absolute Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{K}|K)
.
In turn, non-decomposed points play a role that in positive characteristic is to a certain extent analogous to the role played by geometric points in characteristic zero.

Furthermore, a (possibly decomposed) point
\mathfrak{p}
on a regular curve
C|K
is
separable
, i.e., the residue field extension
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})|K
is separable, if and only if each (non-decomposed) point
\mathfrak{q}
on
C_{L}
above
\mathfrak{p}
is
L
-rational.
This connects the sharp bounds
\lambda_{p}(d)
with the separability of the images
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
of
\mathfrak{p}
,
since above each
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
there lie the images
\mathfrak{q}_{n}
of the points
\mathfrak{q}
, which have
\delta(\mathfrak{q})=\delta(\mathfrak{p})/[\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):K]_{s}
,
where
[\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):K]_{s}
is the separable degree of
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})|K
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
C|K
be a regular curve and let
\mathfrak{p}
be a non-smooth point on
C
.
Then the image point
\mathfrak{p}_{n}\in C_{n}
is separable for all
n\geq\lambda_{p}\big(\frac{2}{p-1}\frac{\delta(\mathfrak{p})}{[\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):K]_{s}}\big)
.

This improves
[
HiSt22
, Theorem 2.21]
.
Observe that
\frac{2}{p-1}\frac{\delta(\mathfrak{p})}{[\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):K]_{s}}
is indeed an integer.

A key ingredient in our approach is the duality between regular curves and function fields (see
[
EGA2
, Section 7.4]
), which we recall in Section
2
for the convenience of the reader, and also to set the terminology we use throughout.
We remark that the same approach was employed in
[
HiSt22
]
.
As in
[
HiSt22
]
, the algorithm developed in
[
BedSt87
]
will be of fundamental importance here, as it allows to compute the geometric
\delta
-invariants
\delta(\mathfrak{p})
of every non-decomposed point
\mathfrak{p}
in any regular curve
C|K
.

2.
Generalities on regular curves

2.1.
Regular curves

By a
curve
over a field
K
we mean a proper geometrically integral
K
-scheme of dimension
1
.
A curve
C|K
is
regular
(or
normal
) if its local ring
\mathcal{O}_{C,x}
at every point
x\in C
is a regular local ring.
A curve
C|K
is
smooth
if the structure morphism
C\to\Spec(K)
is smooth, or equivalently, if
C
is geometrically regular, i.e.,

C
is regular and the base change
C_{\overline{K}}=C\otimes_{K}\overline{K}
remains regular.
Every smooth curve is regular.
The converse holds over perfect fields
K
, but may fail when
K
is imperfect.

Let
C
be a regular curve over an imperfect field
K
of characteristic
p>0
.
Let
\widetilde{C}_{\overline{K}}
denote the normalization of the base change
C_{\overline{K}}
. Its genus
\overline{g}=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C}_{\overline{K}}})
is at most the genus
g=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C})
of
C
, and
C
is non-smooth if and only if
\overline{g}<g
.
The difference
g-\overline{g}
actually admits the representation

g-\overline{g}=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in C}\delta(\mathfrak{p}),

where
\mathfrak{p}
runs through the closed points on
C
and
\delta(\mathfrak{p})
is the
geometric
\delta
-invariant
of
C
at
\mathfrak{p}
. The integer
\delta(\mathfrak{p})
, which is a multiple of
\frac{p-1}{2}
, is positive if
\mathfrak{p}
is non-smooth, and zero otherwise.
Recall that
\mathfrak{p}
is smooth (resp. non-smooth) if its pre-images under the natural map
C_{\overline{K}}\to C
are all non-singular (resp. singular) points on the curve
C_{\overline{K}}
.

The
Frobenius pullback

C^{(p)}
of
C
is the pullback of
C
by the absolute Frobenius morphism
F_{K}:\Spec(K)\to\Spec(K)
of
K
, which comes from the Frobenius map
K\to K
,
c\mapsto c^{p}
.
This curve is related to
C
via the
relative Frobenius morphism

C\to C^{(p)}
, which is induced by the absolute Frobenius morphism
F_{C}:C\to C
of
C
.

Repeated applications of this yield iterated Frobenius pullbacks
C^{(p^{n})}
together with iterated relative Frobenius morphisms
C\to C^{(p^{n})}
,
and in turn a sequence

C^{(p^{0})}=C\to C^{(p^{1})}\to C^{(p^{2})}\to C^{(p^{3})}\to\cdots.

As the map
K^{1/p}\to K
,
c\mapsto c^{p}
is an isomorphism of rings, we can identify the Frobenius pullback
C^{(p)}|K
with the base change
C_{K^{1/p}}|K^{1/p}
, and similarly each
C^{(p^{n})}|K
with
C_{K^{1/p^{n}}}|K^{1/p^{n}}
.
Thus extending the base from
K
to
K^{1/p}
is equivalent to passing from
C|K
to
C^{(p)}|K
. In particular, the unique pre-image of a point
\mathfrak{p}\in C
under the projection
C_{K^{1/p}}\to C
can be identified with its image under the map
C\to C^{(p)}
.

The curves
C^{(p^{n})}
and
C_{K^{1/p^{n}}}
are not necessarily normal (this actually happens if and only if
C
is non-smooth), hence in order to get regular curves we pass to their normalizations
C_{n}:=\widetilde{C}^{(p^{n})}
and
\widetilde{C}_{K^{1/p^{n}}}
, which we can also identify.
The above sequence then yields a sequence of normal curves over
K

(2)

C_{0}=C\to C_{1}\to C_{2}\to C_{3}\to\cdots.

As before, the only point
\mathfrak{p}^{(n)}\in\widetilde{C}_{K^{1/p^{n}}}
lying over a point
\mathfrak{p}\in C
can be identified with its image
\mathfrak{p}_{n}\in C_{n}
.
Thus studying the points above
\mathfrak{p}
on the normalized base changes
\widetilde{C}_{K^{1/p^{n}}}
is equivalent to studying the images of
\mathfrak{p}
in the normalized Frobenius pullbacks
C_{n}
.

A closed point
\mathfrak{p}\in C
is
non-decomposed
(in
C_{\overline{K}}
) if it has a unique pre-image under the map
C_{\overline{K}}\to C
, or equivalently, if the residue field extension
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})|K
is purely inseparable.
Indeed,
the number of
\overline{K}
-points of
C
above
\mathfrak{p}
is equal to the number of embeddings of
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
into
\overline{K}
, and therefore coincides with the separable degree
[\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):K]_{s}
.

A closed point
\mathfrak{p}\in C
is
separable
if the extension
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})|K
is separable.
Every separable point is smooth
[
SGA1
, Exp. II, Cor. 5.3]
.
A closed point
\mathfrak{p}
is a
K
-rational point if and only if it is both separable and non-decomposed.
Note that after sufficiently many purely inseparable base extensions the inseparable part of
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})|K
vanishes, i.e., for
n
large enough the point
\mathfrak{p}^{(n)}
, hence also the point
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
, becomes separable. For
\mathfrak{p}
non-decomposed this means that
\mathfrak{p}^{(n)}\in\widetilde{C}_{K^{1/p^{n}}}(K^{1/p^{n}})
, or
\mathfrak{p}_{n}\in C_{n}(K)
, for
n
sufficiently large.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a closed point on
C
.
By
[
HiSt22
, Proposition 2.10]
,
there exists a separable extension
K\subset L
such that every point above
\mathfrak{p}
on the regular curve
C_{L}
is non-decomposed, and moreover there are exactly
d_{s}:=[\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):K]_{s}
such points, say
\mathfrak{q}_{i}\in C_{L}
(
1\leq i\leq d_{s}
), which have
\delta(\mathfrak{q}_{i})=\delta(\mathfrak{p})/d_{s}
and
\deg(\mathfrak{q}_{i})=[\kappa(\mathfrak{q}_{i}):L]=[\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):K]/d_{s}
.
Here
L
can be the separable closure
K^{sep}
of
K
in
\overline{K}
, or just (the normal closure of) the separable closure of
K
in
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
.
In particular, the point
\mathfrak{p}
is separable if and only if each
\mathfrak{q}_{i}
is an
L
-rational point on
C_{L}
.
It follows that for fixed
n
the image
\mathfrak{p}_{n}\in C_{n}
of
\mathfrak{p}
is separable if and only if some (and every) point
(\mathfrak{q}_{i})_{n}\in(C_{L})_{n}=(C_{n})_{L}
lying above
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is an
L
-rational point.
Furthermore, if the extension
L|K
is Galois then the Galois group
G=\mathrm{Gal}(L|K)
acts transitively on the set of points
\mathfrak{q}_{i}
above
\mathfrak{p}
, and thus if
L=K^{sep}
then we can regard
C
as the quotient of the regular curve
C_{L}
by the Galois action of
G
.

As should be clear from the preceding paragraph, many properties of a point
\mathfrak{p}
can be read off from the corresponding properties of the non-decomposed points
\mathfrak{q}_{i}\in C_{L}
above
\mathfrak{p}
, e.g., the
\delta
-invariant
\delta(\mathfrak{p})
or the degree
\deg(\mathfrak{p})
.
The analysis of an arbitrary point can therefore be restricted to the case of a non-decomposed point, several of whose invariants can be computed via the algorithm developed in
[
BedSt87
]
.
For instance, Theorem
2.1
below provides a method to compute the
\delta
-invariant of any non-decomposed point (see Section
4
for explicit applications).

2.2.
Function fields

A
function field
(in one variable) is a field extension
F|K
which satisfies the following two properties:
(i)
F
is finitely generated, separable, and of transcendence degree
1
over
K
;
(ii)
K
is algebraically closed in
F
.
The first condition (i) means that there is a transcendental element
z\in F
over
K
such that the extension
F|K(z)
is finite and separable. Such a function
z
is called a
separating variable
of
F|K
.

The assignment
C|K\mapsto F|K=K(C)|K
establishes a bijective contravariant correspondence between the regular curves
C
defined over a field
K
and the function fields
F|K
with base
K
.
If
C|K
is a regular curve and
K\subset K^{\prime}
is an algebraic extension then
F^{\prime}|K^{\prime}=K(C)\otimes_{K}K^{\prime}|K^{\prime}
becomes a function field, actually the function field of the integral curve
C_{K^{\prime}}|K^{\prime}
, and
F^{\prime}
is equal to the compositum
K(C)\cdot K^{\prime}
.

We remark that the second condition (ii) and the separability assumption in (i) are sometimes disregarded in the literature (see e.g.
[
Che51
]
). In this more general setting (which we shall not consider), function fields correspond, under the above assignment, to proper normal
K
-schemes of dimension
1
that are not necessarily geometrically integral.

The
primes

\mathfrak{p}
of a function field
F|K
are the (regular) closed points of the corresponding regular curve
C|K
, and their local rings
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
are the (discrete) valuation rings of
F|K
.
The
geometric singularity degree
of a prime
\mathfrak{p}
,
defined as the
\overline{K}
-codimension of the semilocal domain
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{K}\overline{K}\subset F\otimes_{K}\overline{K}
in its integral closure,
agrees with the geometric
\delta
-invariant
\delta(\mathfrak{p})
of
\mathfrak{p}
as a point on
C
.
A prime
\mathfrak{p}
is called
singular
if it is non-smooth as a point on
C
, i.e.,
\delta(\mathfrak{p})>0
.
It is called
rational
if
\mathfrak{p}\in C(K)
, or
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})=K
.
Given a function
f
in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
we write
f(\mathfrak{p})
for the
value
of
f
at
\mathfrak{p}
, which is the residue class of
f
in
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
.

Recall that the
n
-th iterated Frobenius pullback
C^{(p^{n})}|K
of
C|K
may not be normal.
The function field of its normalization
C_{n}|K
agrees with the
n
th
Frobenius pullback

F_{n}|K:=F^{p^{n}}{\cdot}K|K
of
F|K
, which is the only intermediate field of
F|K
such that the extension
F|F_{n}
is purely inseparable of degree
p^{n}
.
The sequence of iterated Frobenius morphisms (
2
) corresponds to the sequence of purely inseparable degree
p
field extensions

F_{0}=F\supset F_{1}\supset F_{2}\supset F_{3}\supset\cdots\supset K.

The images
\mathfrak{p}_{n}\in C_{n}
of a point
\mathfrak{p}\in C
correspond to the
restrictions
of the prime
\mathfrak{p}
to the Frobenius pullbacks
F_{n}|K
, since each valuation
v_{\mathfrak{p}_{n}}
of
F_{n}|K
is obtained by restricting the valuation
v_{\mathfrak{p}}
of
F|K
to
F_{n}
.
The sequence of geometric singularity degrees
\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n})
is non-increasing and converges to zero. The partial differences
\Delta_{n}:=\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n})-\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n+1})\geq 0
are divisible by
\frac{p-1}{2}
and satisfy
\Delta_{n+1}\leq p^{-1}\Delta_{n}
, and furthermore
\delta(\mathfrak{p})=\Delta_{0}+\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+\cdots
(see
[
HiSt22
, pp. 5-6]
).

A function
z\in F
is a separating variable of
F|K
if and only if
z\notin F_{1}
, i.e.,
F=F_{1}(z)
.
For each
n>0
, this is equivalent to
z^{p^{n}}\notin F_{n+1}
, i.e.,
F=F_{n+1}(z)
, that is, the power
z^{p^{n}}\in F_{n}
is a separating variable of
F_{n}|K
.

By the Fundamental Equality for function fields, the product between the ramification and inertia indices
e_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
and
f_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
of the extension
\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{p}_{1}
equals
[F:F_{1}]=p
.
A prime
\mathfrak{p}
is
unramified
(or
inertial
) over
F_{1}
if
e_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{p}_{1}}=1
, i.e., the purely inseparable extension
\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{1})\subset\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
has degree
p
;
the prime
\mathfrak{p}
is
ramified
over
F_{1}
if
e_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{p}_{1}}=p
, i.e.,
\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
.

A prime
\mathfrak{p}
is separable if and only if it is non-singular and unramified over
F_{1}

[
HiSt22
, Proposition 2.7]
.
A prime
\mathfrak{p}
is non-decomposed if and only if there is an integer
m
such that the restricted prime
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is rational for
n=m
(hence for all
n\geq m
)
[
HiSt22
, Corollary 2.15]
.
A prime
\mathfrak{p}
is non-decomposed if and only if some (and every) restricted prime
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is non-decomposed.

The following result will be used repeatedly to compute singularity degrees.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
BedSt87
, Theorem 2.3]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a non-decomposed prime in a function field
F|K
.
Choose
n>0
such that the restricted prime
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
of
F_{n}|K

is rational.
If
z\in F
is a function such that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}[z]

then

\delta(\mathfrak{p})=p\,\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{1})+\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot v_{\mathfrak{p}_{n}}(dz^{p^{n}}).

Here
v_{\mathfrak{p}_{n}}(dz^{p^{n}})
is the order of the differential
dz^{p^{n}}
of
F_{n}|K
at the prime
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
.
Note that
z^{p^{n}}\in F_{n}
is a separating variable of
F_{n}|K
, since
z\notin F_{1}
.
If
\mathfrak{p}
is ramified over
F_{1}
one can take
z
to be a local parameter at
\mathfrak{p}
;
if
\mathfrak{p}
is inertial over
F_{1}
one can select a function
z\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
whose value
z(\mathfrak{p})
generates the degree
p
purely inseparable extension
\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{1})\subset\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
.

Recall that an exact differential
dz
of a function field
F|K
does not vanish if and only if the function
z\in F
is a separating variable.

3.
The sharp bound

We say that a positive integer
d
is
admissible
if there exist a function field
F|K
and a non-decomposed prime
\mathfrak{p}
in
F|K
of geometric singularity degree
\delta(\mathfrak{p})=\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot d
.

Proposition 3.1
.

A positive integer
d
is admissible if and only if
d\not\equiv-1\pmod{p}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
d\not\equiv-1\pmod{p}
.
The function field
F|K=K(x,z)|K
given by

z^{p}=a+x^{d+1},\quad\text{where $a\in K\setminus K^{p}$}

has Frobenius pullback
F_{1}|K=K(x)|K
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}
be the zero of
x
, i.e., let
\mathfrak{p}
be the only prime such that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)>0
.
Its restriction
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
to
F_{1}|K
is rational and has local parameter
x
, hence
\mathfrak{p}
is non-decomposed.
As
\mathfrak{p}
is inertial over
F_{1}
with residue field
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})=K(z(\mathfrak{p}))=K(a^{1/p})
and as the differential
dz^{p}=(d+1)x^{d}dx
of
F_{1}|K
has order
d
at
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
, we conclude from Theorem
2.1
that

\delta(\mathfrak{p})=p\,\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{1})+\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot v_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}(dz^{p})=\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot d.

The converse follows from
[
BedSt87
, Corollary 2.4]
, which states that every
\delta(\mathfrak{p})
must fulfill the congruence relation
2\delta(\mathfrak{p})\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
.
∎

Let
d
be a positive integer that is admissible, i.e., such that
d\not\equiv-1\pmod{p}
.
In this section we ask for the smallest integer
m
satisfying the following property: for any function field
F|K
and any non-decomposed prime
\mathfrak{p}
in
F|K
with
\delta(\mathfrak{p})=\frac{p-1}{2}{\cdot}d
we have that its restriction
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is rational for all
n\geq m
.
By Theorem
1.1
, this integer
\lambda_{p}(d)
satisfies

1\leq\lambda_{p}(d)\leq\tau_{p}(d),

where
\tau_{p}(d)
is given as in (
1
),
or equivalently

\displaystyle\tau_{p}(d)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}i+1&\text{if $d=P^{i}_{j}$ for some $j\leq i$},\\
i&\text{if $P_{0}^{i-1}<d<P_{0}^{i}$ and $d\neq P_{j}^{i}$ for all $j\leq i$},\end{cases}

where for every pair of integers
i\geq j\geq 0
the symbol
P_{j}^{i}
denotes

P_{j}^{i}=p^{j}+\dots+p^{i}=\sum_{k=j}^{i}p^{k}=\frac{p^{i+1}-p^{j}}{p-1}.

For
i<j
we follow the convention that
P_{j}^{i}=0
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
d
be a positive integer such that
d\not\equiv-1\pmod{p}
.
Choose
0\leq r<p-1
with
d\equiv r\pmod{p}
.
Then
\lambda_{p}(d)<\tau_{p}(d)
if and only if
there is an integer
i>1
such that
P_{0}^{i-1}<d<(r+1)P_{0}^{i-1}
and

d\neq rP_{0}^{i-1}+P_{j}^{i-1}\quad\text{for all $j$ with $0<j\leq i$}.

Moreover, for any such
d
we have

\lambda_{p}(d)=\tau_{p}(d)-1.

As an immediate consequence of the theorem we recover
[
HiSt22
, Proposition 2.22]
.

Corollary 3.3
.

If a positive integer
d
satisfies
d\equiv 0\pmod{p}
then the bound
\tau_{p}(d)
is sharp for
d
, i.e.,
\lambda_{p}(d)=\tau_{p}(d)
.
In particular, in characteristic
p=2

the bound
\tau_{p}(d)
is always sharp.

We make some preparations towards the proof of the proposition.
Our first objective is to bound
\lambda_{p}(d)
from below.
This requires concrete examples of function fields
F|K
, non-decomposed primes
\mathfrak{p}
with
\delta(\mathfrak{p})=(\frac{p-1}{2})d
, and integers
n
such that the restricted primes
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
are non-rational.
We postpone the discussion of such examples to the next section, and use them here to prove the proposition.

In Example
4.1
we construct for every
i\geq j>0
,
\ell\geq 0
and
r\in\{0,\dots,p-2\}
a function field
F|K
and a non-decomposed prime
\mathfrak{p}
in
F|K
such that

\delta(\mathfrak{p})=\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot(rP_{0}^{i}+\ell P_{j}^{i}),\quad\text{$\mathfrak{p}_{i}$ is non-rational},\quad\text{$\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}$ is rational.}

Similarly, in Example
4.2
we build for each
i>j>0
,
\ell\geq 0
and
r,r^{\prime}\in\{0,\dots,p-2\}
a function field
F|K
and a non-decomposed prime
\mathfrak{p}
in
F|K
satisfying

\delta(\mathfrak{p})=\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot(rP_{0}^{i}+P_{j}^{i}+r^{\prime}p^{j}+\ell p^{j+1}),\quad\text{$\mathfrak{p}_{i}$ is non-rational},\quad\text{$\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}$ is rational.}

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
d
be a positive integer such that
d\equiv r\pmod{p}
, where
0\leq r<p-1
.
If for some integer
i>0
one has

rP_{0}^{i}+P_{1}^{i}-2p<d

then
i<\lambda_{p}(d)
.

Proof.

In view of the two examples,
it is enough to show that
d
can be written as

(3)

d=rP_{0}^{i}+\ell p^{i}\quad\text{for some $\ell\geq 0$,}

or as

(4)

d=rP_{0}^{i}+P_{j}^{i}+r^{\prime}p^{j}+\ell p^{j+1}

for some
\ell\geq 0
,
r^{\prime}\in\{0,\dots,p-2\}
and
j
with
0<j<i
.
Note first that
-p^{i}<d-rP_{0}^{i}
, and that

-p^{j+1}<d-rP_{0}^{i}-P_{j}^{i}-r^{\prime}p^{j}

whenever
r^{\prime}\in\{0,\dots,p-2\}
and
0<j<i
.
Now, if
d
does not admit
a representation as in (
4
), then

d-rP_{0}^{i}\not\equiv(r^{\prime}+1)p^{j}\pmod{p^{j+1}}\quad\text{for each $j=0,\dots,i-1$ and $r^{\prime}=0,\dots,p-2$,}

which means
d-rP_{0}^{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{i}}
, that is,

d
admits a representation as in (
3
).
∎

Given a prime
\mathfrak{p}
, recall that the integers
\Delta_{n}=\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n})-\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n+1})\geq 0
satisfy
\Delta_{n+1}\leq p^{-1}\Delta_{n}
and
\delta(\mathfrak{p})=\Delta_{0}+\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+\cdots
.
Recall also that each
\Delta_{n}
is a multiple of
\frac{p-1}{2}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a singular non-decomposed prime in a function field
F|K
. Let
n>0
be such that
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is rational and assume that

\Delta_{i-1}=rp^{n-i}\cdot\frac{p-1}{2}\quad\text{for every $i=1,\dots,n$},

where
0<r<p-1
.
Then the prime
\mathfrak{p}
is unramified over
F_{n}
.
In particular, it has degree
\deg(\mathfrak{p})=p^{n}
.

Proof.

The prime
\mathfrak{p}_{n-1}
is unramified over
F_{n}

because
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is rational and
\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n-1})=r\cdot\tfrac{p-1}{2}>0
.
Suppose we have proved that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is unramified over
F_{n}
for some
i<n
, and let us show that
\mathfrak{p}_{i-1}
is unramified over
F_{i}
.
Assume this is not the case.
Since
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is not rational, and therefore not separable, it follows from
[
HiSt23
, Lemma 3.7]
that
\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{i-1})-p\,\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{i})\geq\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot\deg(\mathfrak{p}_{i})
, i.e.,
r\geq p^{n-i}
, a contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

According to
[
HiSt22
]
the integer
\tau_{p}(d)
admits the combinatorial description

\tau_{p}(d)=\max\{s+\min\{v_{p}(d_{1}),\dots,v_{p}(d_{s})\}\},

where
v_{p}
is the
p
-adic valuation of
\mathbb{Q}
and the maximum is taken over all the partitions

d=d_{1}+\dots+d_{s}

of
d
such that

d_{i+1}\leq p^{-1}d_{i}\,\,\text{ for each $i=1,\dots,s-1$}.

By the proof of
[
HiSt22
, Proposition 2.19]
, the bound
n=\tau_{p}(d)
in Theorem
1.1
is sharp for
d
, i.e.,
\lambda_{p}(d)=\tau_{p}(d)
, if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied

(1)

there is a partition

d=d_{1}+\dots+d_{s}

of
d
realizing the above maximum,

(2)

there exist a function field
F|K
and a non-decomposed prime
\mathfrak{p}
in
F|K
such that

\delta(\mathfrak{p})=\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot d\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta_{i-1}=\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot d_{i}\text{ for all $i=1,\dots,s$},

whose restriction
\mathfrak{p}_{n-1}
is non-rational.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
3.2
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

If
d=P_{j}^{i}
for some
i\geq j\geq 0
then the bound
\tau_{p}(d)=i+1
is sharp for
d
, i.e.,
\lambda_{p}(d)=\tau_{p}(d)
, as follows from Example
4.1
.
Thus we may assume that

P_{0}^{i-1}<d<P_{0}^{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\text{$d\neq P_{j}^{i}$ for all $j\leq i$},

where
i>0
,
and we wish to see when the bound
\tau_{p}(d)=i
is not sharp for
d
.
As the bound is sharp when
\tau_{p}(d)=1
we can further assume
i>1
.
In addition, since the bound is sharp when
(r+1)P_{0}^{i-1}\leq d<P_{0}^{i}
, as
this implies by Lemma
3.4
that
i-1<\lambda_{p}(d)
,
we may suppose that

P_{0}^{i-1}<d<(r+1)P_{0}^{i-1},

and in turn that
r>0
,
p>2
.
Therefore, we must prove that in this situation the bound
\tau_{p}(d)=i
is sharp if and only if
d
can be written as
d=rP_{0}^{i-1}+P_{j}^{i-1}
for some
j
with
0<j\leq i
.
As the if part follows from Example
4.1
we just need to show the only if part.

For any partition

d=d_{1}+\dots+d_{s}

of
d

we have
s+\min\{v_{p}(d_{1}),\dots,v_{p}(d_{s})\}=s
,
hence in light of Remark
3.6

the condition that the bound
\tau_{p}(d)=i
is sharp means that there exist a partition

d=d_{1}+\dots+d_{i}

of length
i
, a function field
F|K
and a non-decomposed prime
\mathfrak{p}
such that

\text{$\mathfrak{p}_{i}$ is rational}\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta_{k-1}=\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot d_{k}\text{ for all $k=1,\dots,i$}.

It is clear that

p^{i-k}\leq d_{k}<p\cdot p^{i-k}\quad\text{for all $k=1,\dots,i$},

since
d_{k+1}\leq p^{-1}d_{k}
for each
k
and
d_{1}<p^{i}
as
p^{i}=(p-1)P_{0}^{i-1}+1>(r+1)P_{0}^{i-1}
.
As
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is rational and
\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{i-1})=\Delta_{i-1}>0
we deduce that
\mathfrak{p}_{i-1}
has degree
p
and hence that
p
divides
d_{k}
for every
k<i
(see
[
HiSt22
, Proposition 2.6]
).
Therefore
d_{i}=r
, so in particular

rp^{i-k}\leq d_{k}<p\cdot p^{i-k}\quad\text{for all $k=1,\dots,i$.}

Choose a positive integer
j\leq i
that is maximal with respect to the following property

d_{k}=rp^{i-k}\quad\text{for all $k>i-j$.}

By Lemma
3.5
, the prime
\mathfrak{p}_{i-j}
has degree
p^{j}
,
which implies that
p^{j}
divides
d_{k}
for each
k\leq i-j
(see
[
HiSt22
, Proposition 2.6]
). Now
(r+1)p^{j}\leq d_{i-j}
, and hence
(r+1)p^{i-k}\leq d_{k}
for each
k\leq i-j
, so we conclude that the integer

d-(rP_{0}^{i-1}+P_{j}^{i-1})=\sum_{1\leq k\leq i-j}\big(d_{k}-(r+1)p^{i-k}\big)

is a non-negative multiple of
p^{j}
. In view of
d<(r+1)P_{0}^{i-1}
, this means

d=rP_{0}^{i-1}+P_{j}^{i-1}
.

It remains to show that for any
d
satisfying the conditions in the theorem we have

\lambda_{p}(d)=\tau_{p}(d)-1
.
Since
\lambda_{p}(d)\geq 1
we may assume
\tau_{p}(d)>2
, i.e.,
i>2
.
As
P^{i-1}_{0}<d
we conclude from Lemma
3.4
that
i-2<\lambda_{p}(d)
, i.e.,
\tau_{p}(d)-2<\lambda_{p}(d)
.
∎

4.
Examples

This section discusses the examples of function fields and non-decomposed primes that were needed in the previous section to bound
\lambda_{p}(d)
from below.

Example 4.1
.

Let
i\geq j>0
,
r\in\{0,\dots,p-2\}
and
\ell\geq 0
.
We construct a function field
F|K
and a non-decomposed prime
\mathfrak{p}
in
F|K
of geometric singularity degree

\delta(\mathfrak{p})=\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot\big(rP_{0}^{i}+\ell P_{j}^{i}\big)

with the property that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}
are non-rational and rational respectively.
Let
K
be a field containing elements

a\in K\setminus K^{p}\quad\text{and}\quad b\in K\setminus K^{p}(a^{1/p^{j}}),

e.g.,
K
can be the function field of the projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}(k)
over an algebraically closed ground field
k
.
Consider the function field
F|K=K(x,y)|K
defined by the equation

y^{p^{i+1}}=b^{p^{j}}+x^{\ell\cdot p^{j}}(a+x^{r+1}).

Set
z:=x^{-\ell}(b+y^{p^{i-j+1}})
, so that
z^{p^{j}}=a+x^{r+1}
and
y^{p^{i-j+1}}=b+x^{\ell}z
.
The Frobenius pullbacks of
F|K
are then given by

\displaystyle F_{n}|K

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}K(x,z,y^{p^{n}})|K&\text{if $0\leq n\leq i-j$},\\
K(x,z^{p^{n-i+j-1}})|K&\text{if $i-j<n<i+1$},\\
K(x)|K&\text{if $n=i+1$}.\end{cases}

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be the zero of the function
x
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{p}
is the only prime of
F|K
such that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)>0
.
Note that its restriction
\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}
to the rational function field
F_{i+1}|K=K(x)|K
is rational and has local parameter
x
.
In particular,
\mathfrak{p}
is non-decomposed.

We compute the singularity degree
\delta(\mathfrak{p})
, and on the way check that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is non-rational.
As is clear from
z(\mathfrak{p})^{p^{j}}=a\notin K^{p}=\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{i+1})^{p}
, for every
i-j<n<i+1
the prime
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is unramified over
F_{n}
, or more precisely, the residue field extension
\kappa({\mathfrak{p}_{n}})|\kappa({\mathfrak{p}_{n+1}})
is purely inseparable of degree
p
, generated by the value
z(\mathfrak{p})^{p^{n-i+j-1}}
of the function
z^{p^{n-i+j-1}}\in F_{n}
.
As the differential
dz^{p^{j}}=(r+1)x^{r}dx
of
F_{i+1}|K=K(x)|K
has order
r
at
\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}
, from Theorem
2.1
we infer

\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n})=p\,\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n+1})+\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot v_{\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}}(dz^{p^{j}})=p\,\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n+1})+\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot r,\quad(i-j<n<i+1).

Similarly, since

y(\mathfrak{p})^{p^{i-j+1}}=\begin{cases}b&\text{if $\ell>0$},\\
b+a^{1/p^{j}}&\text{if $\ell=0$},\end{cases}

does not lie in
\kappa({\mathfrak{p}_{i-j+1}})^{p}=K^{p}(a^{1/p^{j-1}})
, for every
n\leq i-j
the prime
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is unramified over
F_{n+1}
and

\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n})=p\,\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n+1})+\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot v_{\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}}(dy^{p^{i+1}})=p\,\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n+1})+\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot(r+\ell p^{j}),\quad(0\leq n\leq i-j),

where the last equality is due to the fact that the differential
dy^{p^{i+1}}=x^{\ell p^{j}}dz^{p^{j}}
of
F_{i+1}|K
has order
\ell p^{j}+r
at
\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}
.
This shows that
\delta(\mathfrak{p})=\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot(rP_{0}^{i}+\ell P_{j}^{i})
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
i>j>0
,
r,r^{\prime}\in\{0,\dots,p-2\}
and
\ell\geq 0
.
We construct a function field
F|K
and a non-decomposed prime
\mathfrak{p}
of geometric singularity degree

\delta(\mathfrak{p})=\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot\big(rP_{0}^{i}+P_{j}^{i}+r^{\prime}p^{j}+\ell p^{j+1}\big)

with the property that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}
are non-rational and rational respectively.
Take an imperfect field
K
and consider the function field
F|K=K(y,u)|K

given by the relation

z^{p^{j}+r+1}=az^{r+1}+y^{p^{i-j}(r+1)},

where
z:=u^{p}-y^{p\ell+r^{\prime}+1}
and
a\in K\setminus K^{p}
.
Let
x:=y^{p^{i-j}}/z
, so that

z^{p^{j}}=a+x^{r+1},\quad y^{p^{i-j}}=xz,\quad u^{p}=z+y^{p\ell+r^{\prime}+1}.

Then the Frobenius pullbacks take the form

\displaystyle F_{n}|K

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}K(x,z,y,u)|K&\text{if $n=0$},\\
K(x,z,y^{p^{n-1}})|K&\text{if $1\leq n\leq i-j$},\\
K(x,z^{p^{n-i+j-1}})|K&\text{if $i-j<n<i+1$},\\
K(x)|K&\text{if $n=i+1$}.\end{cases}

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be the (non-decomposed) zero of the function
x
, whose restriction
\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}
to the function field
F_{i+1}|K=K(x)|K
is a rational prime with local parameter
x
.
As follows from
z(\mathfrak{p})^{p^{j}}=a\notin K^{p}=\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{i+1})^{p}
, the prime
\mathfrak{p}_{i-j+1}
is unramified over
F_{i+1}
and

\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n})=p\,\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n+1})+\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot v_{\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}}(dz^{p^{j}})=p\,\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n+1})+\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot r,\quad(i-j<n<i+1).

In particular, the prime
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is non-rational.
Since
y^{p^{i-j}}=xz\in F_{i-j+1}
is a local parameter at
\mathfrak{p}_{i-j+1}
the prime
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
is totally ramified over
F_{i-j+1}
, that is, for every
1\leq n\leq i-j
the prime
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is ramified over
F_{n+1}
with local parameter
y^{p^{n-1}}
.
Using Theorem
2.1
we deduce

\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n})=p\,\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n+1})+\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot v_{\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}}(dy^{p^{i}})=p\,\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{n+1})+\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot(p^{j}+r),\quad(1\leq n\leq i-j).

Now, because
u(\mathfrak{p})=z(\mathfrak{p})^{1/p}
does not lie in
\kappa({\mathfrak{p}_{1}})=\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{i-j+1})=K(z(\mathfrak{p}))
the prime
\mathfrak{p}
is unramified over
F_{1}
and

\delta(\mathfrak{p})=p\,\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{1})+\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot v_{\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}}(du^{p^{i+1}})=p\,\delta(\mathfrak{p}_{1})+\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot\big((r^{\prime}+1)p^{j}+r+\ell p^{j+1}\big).

Putting the above together we conclude

\delta(\mathfrak{p})=\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot\big(rP_{0}^{i}+P_{j}^{i}+r^{\prime}p^{j}+\ell p^{j+1}\big)
.

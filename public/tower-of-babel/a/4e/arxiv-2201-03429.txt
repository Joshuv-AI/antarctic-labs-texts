Large Deviations for Ablowitz-Ladik lattice, and the Schur flow
G. Mazzuca, R. Memin

Abstract
We consider the Generalized Gibbs ensemble of the Ablowitz-Ladik lattice, and the Schur flow. We derive large deviations principles for the distribution of the empirical measures of the equilibrium measures for these ensembles. As a consequence, we deduce their almost sure convergence. Moreover, we are able to characterize their limit in terms of the equilibrium measure of the Circular, and the Jacobi beta ensemble respectively.

Introduction

The defocusing Ablowitz-Ladik (AL) lattice is the system of ODEs

i\dot{\alpha}_{j}=-(\alpha_{j+1}+\alpha_{j-1}-2\alpha_{j})+|\alpha_{j}|^{2}(\alpha_{j-1}+\alpha_{j+1})\,,

(1.1)

that describe the evolution of the complex functions
\alpha_{j}(t)
,
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, here
\dot{\alpha}_{j}=\dfrac{d\alpha_{j}}{dt}
. We assume
N
-periodic boundary conditions
\alpha_{j+N}=\alpha_{j}
, for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. For simplicity, we consider the case
N
even, and, when not mentioned, the limits as
N\to\infty
is taken along
N
even. This system was introduced by Ablowitz and Ladik
[
2
,
3
]
as a spatial discretization of the defocusing Nonlinear Schrödinger Equation (NLS)

i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)=-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{x}\psi(x,t)+\lvert\psi(x,t)\rvert^{2}\psi(x,t).

(1.2)

The NLS is a well-known integrable model
[
36
]
, and the Ablowitz-Ladik lattice is one of the several discretizations that preserve integrability
[
27
]
.

It is straightforward to verify that the two quantities

K^{(0)}:=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\right),\quad K^{(1)}:=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}\overline{\alpha}_{j+1},

(1.3)

are constants of motion for the AL lattice. Since
K^{(0)}
is conserved along the flow, it implies that if
|\alpha_{j}(0)|<1
for all
j=1,\ldots,N
, then
|\alpha_{j}(t)|<1
for all times. Thus, we can consider
{\mathbb{D}}^{N}
as our phase space, where
{\mathbb{D}}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,|z|<1\}
.

On this phase space we consider the symplectic form
\omega

[
15
,
18
]

\omega=i\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\rho_{j}^{2}}{\rm d}\alpha_{j}\wedge{\rm d}{\overline{\alpha}}_{j}\,,\quad\rho_{j}=\sqrt{1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}\,.

(1.4)

The corresponding Poisson bracket is defined for functions
f,g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}}^{N})
as

\begin{split}\{f,g\}&=i\sum_{j=1}^{N}\rho_{j}^{2}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial{\overline{\alpha}}_{j}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\alpha_{j}}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial\alpha_{j}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial{\overline{\alpha}}_{j}}\right)\,.\end{split}

(1.5)

Using this Poisson bracket, it is possible to rewrite the equations of motion (
1.1
) of the AL lattice in Hamiltonian form as

\dot{\alpha}_{j}=\{{\alpha}_{j},H_{AL}\},\quad H_{AL}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})=-2\ln(K^{(0)})+K^{(1)}+\overline{K^{(1)}}\,,

(1.6)

here
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N})
.

Conserved quantities.

As we already mentioned, the AL lattice is an integrable model: this was proved by Ablowitz and Ladik
[
1
,
2
]
. Specifically, they were able to obtain a Lax pair for the Ablowitz-Ladik lattice by discretizing the
2\times 2
Zakharov-Shabat Lax pair of the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation.

Nenciu and Simon in
[
26
,
28
]
constructed a new Lax pair for the Ablowitz-Ladik lattice, exploiting the connection of this system to the orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle. This link is the analogue of the well-known link between the Toda lattice and orthogonal polynomials on
the real line (see e.g.
[
8
]
). This connection was also generalized to the non-commutative case
[
6
]
.

Following
[
26
,
28
]
,
we construct the Lax matrix as follows.
Consider the
2\times 2
unitary matrices

\Xi(\alpha_{j})=\Xi_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}{\overline{\alpha}}_{j}&\rho_{j}\\
\rho_{j}&-\alpha_{j}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\rho_{j}=\sqrt{1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}},\quad j=1,\dots,N\,,

(1.7)

and the
N\times N
matrices

{\mathcal{M}}=\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha_{N}&&&&&\rho_{N}\\
&\Xi_{2}\\
&&\Xi_{4}\\
&&&\ddots\\
&&&&\Xi_{N-2}\\
\rho_{N}&&&&&{\overline{\alpha}}_{N}\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad{\mathcal{L}}=\begin{pmatrix}\Xi_{1}\\
&\Xi_{3}\\
&&\ddots\\
&&&\Xi_{N-1}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(1.8)

Now let us define the Lax matrix

{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{L}}{\mathcal{M}}\,,

(1.9)

which has the following structure

{\mathcal{E}}=\begin{pmatrix}*&*&*&&&&&&&*\\
*&*&*&&&&&&&*\\
&*&*&*&*&&&&&\\
&*&*&*&*&&&&&\\
&&&&&\ddots&\ddots&&&\\
&&&&&&*&*&*&*\\
&&&&&&*&*&*&*\\
*&&&&&&&*&*&*\\
*&&&&&&&*&*&*\\
\end{pmatrix}\,.

The matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
is a periodic CMV matrix
(after Cantero, Moral and Velazquez
[
7
]
).
It is straightforward to verify that the equations of motions (
1.1
) are equivalent to the following Lax equation for the matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
:

\dot{\mathcal{E}}=i\left[{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{E}}^{+}+({\mathcal{E}}^{+})^{\dagger}\right]\,,

(1.10)

where
†
stands for hermitian conjugate, and

{\mathcal{E}}^{+}_{j,k}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{E}}_{j,j}\quad j=k\\
{\mathcal{E}}_{j,k}\quad k=j+1\,{\,{\rm mod}\,}\,N\,\mbox{or}\,k=j+2\,{\,{\rm mod}\,}\,N\\
0\quad\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(1.11)

Remark 1.1
.

We notice that since all the
\Xi_{j}
are unitary, then also
{\mathcal{E}}
is unitary, this implies that all the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
lie on the unit circle, and they can be written in terms of their argument, namely for all
j=1,\ldots,N
there exists a
\theta_{j}\in\mathbb{T}:=[-\pi,\pi)
such that

\lambda_{j}=e^{i\theta_{j}}\,.

In view of this identification, and in order to simplify the notations, for any function
f(z)\;:\;\partial{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we write
f(\theta)
in place of
f(e^{i\theta})
when it is convenient. Further, we will write indifferently
\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(\theta){\rm d}\mu(\theta)
or
\int_{\partial{\mathbb{D}}}f(z){\rm d}\mu(z)
for any probability measure
\mu
having support on the circle.

Remark 1.2
.

We notice that
({\mathcal{E}}^{+})^{\dagger}+({\mathcal{E}}^{\dagger})^{+}={\mathcal{E}}^{\dagger}
and
[{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{E}}^{\dagger}]=0
since
{\mathcal{E}}
is unitary. Therefore, the Lax pair (
1.10
) can be rewritten
in the equivalent form

\dot{\mathcal{E}}=i\left[{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{E}}^{+}-({\mathcal{E}}^{\dagger})^{+}\right]\,.

(1.12)

The formulation (
1.10
) implies that the quantities

K^{(\ell)}=\mbox{Tr}\left({\mathcal{E}}^{\ell}\right),\quad\ell=1,\dots,N-1,

(1.13)

are constants of motion for the defocusing AL system (
1.1
).

As in
[
29
,
25
]
, we introduce the Generalized Gibbs ensemble for the Ablowitz-Ladik lattice, namely the following probability measure on the phase space
{\mathbb{D}}^{N}

{\rm d}\mathbb{P}^{V,\beta}_{AL,N}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N})=\frac{1}{Z_{N}^{AL}(V,\beta)}\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2})^{\beta-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{\alpha_{j}\in{\mathbb{D}}\}}\exp(-\text{Tr}(V(\mathcal{E}))){\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},\quad\,

(1.14)

where
V(e^{i\theta})\,:\,\mathbb{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a continuous function,
\mathbf{1}_{A}
is the indicator function of the set
A
, and
Z_{N}^{AL}(V,\beta)
is the partition function of the system

Z_{N}^{AL}(V,\beta)=\int_{{\mathbb{D}}^{N}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2})^{\beta-1}\exp(-\text{Tr}(V(\mathcal{E}))){\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}.

Furthermore,
we consider the empirical measure
\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})
of the eigenvalues
e^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,e^{i\theta_{N}}
of the matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
(
1.9
), namely

\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{e^{i\theta_{j}({\mathcal{E}})}}\,,

(1.15)

here
\delta_{x}
is the delta function centred at
x
, furthermore, we notice that we can just consider the arguments
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N}
of the eigenvalues since the matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
is unitary, see Remark
1.1
.

Our main result is a large deviations principle (LDP) with good rate function for the sequence
(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}))
under the law
\mathbb{P}_{AL,N}^{V,\beta}
(
1.14
). Namely, denoting by
{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})
the set of probability measures on the Torus
\mathbb{T}
endowed with the topology of weak convergence, there exists a function
J_{\beta}^{V}:{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})\to[0,+\infty]
such that:

1.

it is
lower semicontinuous/good
, namely for any
a\geq 0
,
\{\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})\ |\ J_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)\leq a\}\subset{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})
is compact,

2.

it satisfies a
large deviations lower bound
, namely for all
O\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
open,

-\inf_{O}J\leq\liminf_{N\text{ even}}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})\in O),

(1.16)

3.

it satisfies a
large deviations upper bound
, namely for all
F\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
closed,

\limsup_{N\text{ even}}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})\in F)\leq-\inf_{F}J.

(1.17)

We refer to
[
10
]
for a general introduction to large deviations.

Remark 1.3
.

We notice that, by compactness of
{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})
, it is sufficient to prove a
weak
large deviations principle, see
[
10
, Section 1.2]
, which is equivalent to a full large deviations principle, except that the large deviation upper bound (point 3) holds only for
compact
subsets of
{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})
.

From this large deviations principle we are able to deduce that
\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})
converges almost surely as
N
goes to infinity.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\beta>0
. For any continuous function
V\;:\;\mathbb{T}\;\to{\mathbb{R}}
the following holds.

1.

the sequence
\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})
under the law
\mathbb{P}_{AL,N}^{V,\beta}
satisfies a large deviations principle at speed
N
with a good rate function
J_{\beta}^{V}
,

2.

J_{\beta}^{V}
achieves its minimum at a unique probability measure
\nu_{\beta}^{V}
,

3.

\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})
converges almost surely and in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
towards
\nu_{\beta}^{V}
.

Moreover, following
[
25
,
29
,
20
]
, we are able to characterize the measure
\nu_{\beta}^{V}
in terms of the equilibrium measure of the Circular beta ensemble at high temperature
[
22
,
21
]
. More precisely, consider the functional
\mu\mapsto f_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)
given, for any
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})
absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure and with density
\frac{{\rm d}\mu}{{\rm d}\theta}
, by

f_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)=-\beta\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}\log\left(|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\varphi}|\right)\mu({\rm d}\theta)\mu({\rm d}\varphi)+\beta\log(2)+\int_{\mathbb{T}}V(\theta)\mu({\rm d}\theta)+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\left({\frac{{\rm d}\mu}{{\rm d}\theta}(\theta)}\right)\mu({\rm d}\theta)+\log(2\pi)\,.

(1.18)

It is shown in
[
21
]
, that the previous functional reaches its minimum for a unique absolutely continuous probability measure
\mu_{\beta}^{V}
. Moreover in
[
25
]
it is proved that this measure is almost surely differentiable with respect to
\beta
. Exploiting this result, and Theorem
1.4
we are able to show that

Theorem 1.5
.

For any continuous
V,f\,:\mathbb{T}\,\to{\mathbb{R}}

\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(\theta){\rm d}\nu_{\beta}^{V}(\theta)=\partial_{\beta}\left(\beta\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(\theta){\rm d}\mu_{\beta}^{V}(\theta)\right)\,.

(1.19)

Thus, we obtain a unique characterization of the measure
\nu_{\beta}^{V}
.

In
[

,
25
]
, the authors considered the GGE (

) for this particular class of potentials using a transfer operator technique. In this sense, we generalize their result, extending it to the class of continuous potentials.

In the last part of the manuscript, we consider another integrable model related to the Ablowitz-Ladik lattice, namely the so-called Schur flow
[

. Also for this system, the Lax matrix is

(

and Theorem

for the Schur flow. The main difference is that in place of the Circular beta ensemble, we have the Jacobi one.

2
Existence of a Large deviations principle for the empirical measure of the Ablowitz-Ladik lattice

The aim of this section is to prove the first point of Theorem
1.4
, namely to show that, for
{\mathbb{N}}\ni N\geq 2
and even, the sequence of empirical measures
\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{e^{i\theta_{j}({\mathcal{E}})}}
satisfies a large deviations principle. The strategy of proof is the following. First, we show that if
\mathcal{E}
is distributed according to
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{AL,N}:=\mathbb{P}^{0,\beta}_{AL,N}
defined in (
1.14
), then the sequence of random probability measures
(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}))_{N\text{ even}}
satisfies a large deviations principle in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
, the space of probability measures on
\mathbb{T}
, endowed with the topology of weak convergence. Since according to this toppology
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
is compact, it suffices to show that the sequence
(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}))_{N\text{ even}}
satisfies a
weak
large deviations principle, see Remark
1.3
. Then, applying Varadhan’s Lemma
[
14
, Theorem 1.2.1]
, we obtain the existence of a large deviations principle for arbitrary continuous
V
,
i.e.
the first point of Theorem
1.4
.

We also notice that when
V=0
in (
1.14
) the
\alpha_{i}
’s are independent and identically distributed (
i.i.d
) with distribution
\Theta_{2\beta+1}
, where
\Theta_{\nu}
is defined for
\nu>1
as the random variable such that for
f:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
bounded and measurable

{\mathbb{E}}[f(X)]=\frac{\nu-1}{2\pi}\int_{\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}}f(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{\nu-3}{2}}{\rm d}^{2}z.

(2.1)

Remark 2.1
.

We recall that for integer
\nu\geq 2
, such measure has the following geometrical interpretation: if
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\nu+1})
is chosen at random according to the surface measure on the unit sphere
S^{\nu}
in
\mathbb{R}^{\nu+1}
, then
u_{1}+iu_{2}
is
\Theta_{\nu}
distributed
[
23
]
.

To show that the sequence
(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}))_{N\text{ even}}
satisfies a weak large deviations principle according to the law
\mathbb{P}_{AL,N}^{\beta}
, we only need the
\alpha_{i}
’s to be
i.i.d
according to some law
\sigma
with
\rm{supp}(\sigma)\subseteq\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
. Thus, we just assume the latter hypothesis, and we prove the result in more generality.

2.1
Large Deviations Principle for periodic CMV matrix

Let
d
be the distance on
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
defined by

d(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{\|f\|_{\text{Lip}}\leq 1,\|f\|_{\text{BV}\leq 1}}\left\{\left|\int fd\mu-\int fd\nu\right|\right\}.

(2.2)

Where the Lipschitz and the bounded variation norms are defined on the space of functions
f:\mathbb{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\textrm{Lip}}=\sup_{{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathbb{T}}\atop\theta_{1}\neq\theta_{2}}\frac{|f\left(e^{i\theta_{1}}\right)-f\left(e^{i\theta_{2}}\right)|}{|e^{i\theta_{1}}-e^{i\theta_{2}}|}\,,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\textrm{BV}}=\sup_{n\geq 1,0=\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<\ldots<\theta_{n}=2\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left|f\left(e^{i\theta_{k+1}}\right)-f\left(e^{i\theta_{k}}\right)\right|\,.

(2.4)

The distance
d
is compatible with the weak convergence of probability measures
[
20
]
. We recall that for a
N\times N
matrix
A
, its empirical measure of eigenvalues is defined by

\mu(A)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{\lambda_{j}(A)},

where
\lambda_{j}(A)
,
j=1,\ldots,N
, are the eigenvalues of
A
.
The following Lemma, whose proof can be found in Appendix
A
, gives an upper bound on the distance of the empirical measures of two unitary matrices.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
A
,
B
unitary matrices of size
N\times N
,

•

For
f
with bounded variation,

\left|\int fd\mu(A)-\int fd\mu(B)\right|\leq\|f\|_{\textrm{BV}}\frac{rank(A-B)}{N},

•

For
f
Lipschitz,

\left|\int fd\mu(A)-\int fd\mu(B)\right|\leq\|f\|_{\textrm{Lip}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}|(A-B)_{i,j}|.

As a consequence,

d(\mu(A),\mu(B))\leq\min\left\{\frac{rank(A-B)}{N},\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}|(A-B)_{i,j}|\right\}.

(2.5)

We are now in position to prove that the sequence
\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})
with
(\alpha_{i})_{i\geq 1}

i.i.d
with law
\sigma
, such that
\text{supp}(\sigma)\subseteq\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
, satisfies a large deviations principle. The proof of the following Lemma follows the same line as the corresponding one in
[
20
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(\alpha_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be an
i.i.d
sequence of law
\sigma
with
\text{supp}(\sigma)\subseteq\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
, and
\mathcal{E}
be the associated matrix defined in (
1.9
). Then the sequence of empirical measures
(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}))_{N\text{ even}}
satisfies a large deviations principle in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
endowed with the topology of weak convergence.

Proof.

We use a subadditivity argument to show that for any fixed
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
the following holds

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\liminf_{N\text{ even}}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\bigg)=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{N\text{ even}}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\bigg),

(2.6)

where
B_{\mu}(\delta):=\left\{\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})\;|\;d(\mu,\nu)<\delta\right\}
. Then, applying
[
9
, Theorem 4.1.11]
, along with the fact that in our setting a weak LDP is equivalent to a full LDP, due to the compactness of
{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})
, see remark
1.3
, we conclude.

The first step to prove the result is to approximate the matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
(whose law we denote by
{\mathcal{E}}^{(N)}
) by a diagonal block matrix of independent blocks. To this end,
fix
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
even such that
q\leq N
, write the euclidean division of
N
by
q
,
N=kq+r
with
0\leq r<q
.
We consider
{\mathcal{M}}
given by (
1.8
),

{\mathcal{M}}=\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha_{N}&&&&&\rho_{N}\\
&\Xi_{2}\\
&&\Xi_{4}\\
&&&\ddots\\
&&&&\Xi_{N-2}\\
\rho_{N}&&&&&{\overline{\alpha}}_{N}\end{pmatrix},

and approximate it the following way.

Let
\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}=\text{diag}({\mathcal{M}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{M}}_{k},R)
, where
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
is the block diagonal matrix given by

{\mathcal{M}}_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}-\widetilde{\alpha}_{(i-1)q}&&&&&\widetilde{\rho}_{(i-1)q}\\
&\Xi_{(i-1)q+2}\\
&&\Xi_{(i-1)q+4}\\
&&&\ddots\\
&&&&\Xi_{iq-2}\\
\widetilde{\rho}_{(i-1)q}&&&&&\overline{\widetilde{\alpha}}_{(i-1)q}\end{pmatrix},

where
(\widetilde{\alpha}_{(i-1)q})_{1\leq i\leq k}
are
i.i.d
of law
\sigma
, independent of the
\alpha_{i}
’s,
\widetilde{\rho}_{i}=\sqrt{1-|\widetilde{\alpha}_{i}|^{2}}
,
and the remaining block (of size
r\times r
)
R
is defined similarly:

R=\begin{pmatrix}-\widetilde{\alpha}_{kq}&&&&&\widetilde{\rho}_{k}\\
&\Xi_{kq+2}\\
&&\Xi_{kq+4}\\
&&&\ddots\\
&&&&\Xi_{N-2}\\
\widetilde{\rho}_{kq+1}&&&&&\overline{\widetilde{\alpha}_{kq}}\end{pmatrix}.

Following the same decomposition of
N=kq+r
we write
{\mathcal{L}}=\text{diag}({\mathcal{L}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{L}}_{k},{\mathcal{L}}_{k+1})
, with
{\mathcal{L}}_{i}
of size
q
for
1\leq i\leq k
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{k+1}
of size
r
.

Notice that by construction, we have

\text{rank}({\mathcal{M}}-\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}})\leq 2(k+1).

(2.7)

Now, defining
\widetilde{{\mathcal{E}}}={\mathcal{L}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}
,
\widetilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
is a block diagonal matrix
\text{diag}({\mathcal{E}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{E}}_{k},{\mathcal{E}}_{k+1})
.Then, the blocks
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k+1
are independent, each
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
,
i=i,\ldots,k
, has law
{\mathcal{E}}^{(q)}
, and
{\mathcal{E}}_{k+1}
has law
{\mathcal{E}}^{(r)}
.

Furthermore, using that
\text{rank}(AB)\leq\min\left\{\text{rank}(A);\text{rank}(B)\right\}
for
A,B
two square matrices, and (
2.7
) we get

\text{rank}({\mathcal{E}}-\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}})=\text{rank}({{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{M}}-\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}})})\leq 4(k+1).

By the first point of Lemma
2.2
we deduce

d(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}),\mu_{N}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{E}}}))\leq\frac{4(k+1)}{N}\leq\frac{8}{q}.

Moreover, we can rewrite
\mu_{N}({\widetilde{{\mathcal{E}}}})
as

\mu_{N}({\widetilde{{\mathcal{E}}}})=\frac{q}{N}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\mu_{q}({\mathcal{E}}_{\ell})+\frac{r}{N}\mu_{r}({\mathcal{E}}_{k+1})\,.

(2.8)

Using the independence of the blocks of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(\mu_{q}({\mathcal{E}}_{1})\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\bigg)^{k}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\mu_{r}({\mathcal{E}}_{k+1})\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\bigg(\mu_{q}({\mathcal{E}}_{1}),\ldots,\mu_{q}({\mathcal{E}}_{k}),\mu_{r}({\mathcal{E}}_{k+1})\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg({\frac{q}{N}}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\mu_{q}({\mathcal{E}}_{l})+{\frac{r}{N}}\mu_{r}({\mathcal{E}}_{k+1})\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\bigg(\mu_{N}(\tilde{{\mathcal{E}}})\in B_{\mu}(\delta)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})\in B_{\mu}\left(\delta+\frac{8}{q}\right)\bigg),

Where we used the convexity of balls in the first inequality.

This implies that, setting

u_{N}(\delta)=-\ln\left(\mathbb{P}(\mu_{N}\in B_{\mu}(\delta))\right)\,,

(2.9)

we have

u_{N}\left(\delta+\frac{8}{q}\right)\leq ku_{q}(\delta)+u_{r}(\delta).

(2.10)

We now conclude as in
[
20
, Lemma 2.3]
.
Let
\delta>0
and choose
q
in such a way that
\frac{8}{q}\leq\delta
, so we deduce that

\frac{u_{N}(2\delta)}{N}\leq\frac{u_{N}\left(\delta+\frac{8}{q}\right)}{N}\leq\frac{u_{q}(\delta)}{q}+\frac{u_{r}(\delta)}{N}\,,

(2.11)

since
\frac{u_{r}(\delta)}{N}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
, we deduce that

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{u_{N}(2\delta)}{N}\leq\frac{u_{q}(\delta)}{q}\,.

(2.12)

The previous inequality holds true for all
q
big enough, so we conclude that

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{u_{N}(2\delta)}{N}\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{u_{N}(\delta)}{N}\,.

(2.13)

From this last inequality we deduce that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{u_{N}(\delta)}{N}\leq\lim_{\delta\to 0}\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{u_{N}(\delta)}{N}\,,

(2.14)

thus we obtain (
2.6
), and the conclusion follows applying
[
9
, Theorem 4.1.11]
.
∎

Since
(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T}),d)
is compact, Lemma
2.3
automatically implies the existence of a
strong
large deviations principle. Furthermore, the corresponding rate function
J
,which depends on the distribution
\sigma
of the entries of
{\mathcal{L}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}
, can be seen to be convex. We collect these results in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4
.

In the same hypothesis and notations as in Lemma
2.3
, the sequence of empirical measures
(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}))_{N\text{ even}}
satisfies a large deviations principle with good, convex rate function
J:\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})\to[0,+\infty]
, i.e.

•

The function
J
is convex and its level sets
J^{-1}([0,a])
,
a\geq 0
, are compact,

•

For all
O\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
open,

-\inf_{O}J\leq\liminf_{N\text{ even}}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})\in O),

•

For all
F\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
closed,

\limsup_{N\text{ even}}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})\in F)\leq-\inf_{F}J.

Proof.

We already established all the claims except the fact that the function
J
is convex and that the level sets
J^{-1}([0,a])
are compact. The latter comes from the fact that these sets are closed, see
[
9
, Theorem 4.1.11]
. To prove the convexity of
J
, we follow the same argument as
[
20
, Theorem 2.4]
.

Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})
. Since
\mu_{2N}({\mathcal{E}})
can be approximated by the sum of two independent
\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})
’s up to a mistake smaller than
\frac{4}{N}
by the first point of Lemma
2.2
, for
\delta>0
the following holds

\mathbb{P}\left(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})\in B_{\mu_{1}}(\delta)\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})\in B_{\mu_{2}}(\delta)\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mu_{2N}({\mathcal{E}})\in B_{\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}{2}}\left(\delta+\frac{4}{N}\right)\right)\,,

(2.15)

taking minus the logarithm of both sides, dividing by
2N
, taking the limit for
N
going to infinity and then for
\delta
to zero, we deduce that:

J\left(\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}{2}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(J(\mu_{1})+J(\mu_{2})\right)\,,

(2.16)

which, together with the lower semi-continuity of
J
, implies the convexity of
J
, see
[
10
, Lemma 4.1.21]
.
∎

2.2
Large deviations principle for the Ablowitz-Ladik lattice

Taking
\sigma=\Theta_{2\beta+1}
given by equation (
2.1
), Proposition
2.4
applies to
(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}))_{N\text{ even}}
, where
{\mathcal{E}}
follows
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{AL,N}
defined in (

). Thus,
(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}))_{N\text{ even}}
with law

satisfies a large deviations principle, with a good convex rate function, that we denote by

.

We can now state the existence of a large deviations principle for

under

for

continuous.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\beta>0
, and
V:\mathbb{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be continuous. Under
\mathbb{P}^{V,\beta}_{AL,N}
the sequence
(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}))_{N\text{ even}}
fulfils a large deviations principle with good, convex rate function
J^{V}_{\beta}(\mu)=g_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)-\inf_{\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})}g_{\beta}^{V}(\nu)
, where
g_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)
is given for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
by

{g^{V}_{\beta}(\mu)=J_{\beta}(\mu)+\int_{\mathbb{T}}V{\rm d}\mu\,.}

(2.17)

Proof.

Let us write

{\rm d}\mathbb{P}^{V,\beta}_{AL,N}=\frac{Z_{N}^{AL}(0,\beta)}{Z_{N}^{AL}(V,\beta)}e^{-N\int_{\mathbb{T}}V{\rm d}\mu_{N}}{\rm d}\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{AL,N}=\frac{1}{{\mathcal{Z}}^{AL,V}_{N}}e^{-N\int_{\mathbb{T}}V{\rm d}\mu_{N}}{\rm d}\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{AL,N}.

The function
\mu\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{T}}V{\rm d}\mu
being bounded continuous, by the large deviations principle under
\mathbb{P}^{\beta}_{AL,N}
and Varadhan’s Lemma,
[
14
, Theorem 1.2.1]
, we see that for any bounded continuous

we have

\lim_{N}\frac{1}{N}\ln\int_{{\mathbb{D}}^{N}}e^{Nf(\mu_{N}}){\rm d}\mathbb{P}^{V,\beta}_{AL,N}=\sup_{\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})}\left\{f(\mu)-\left(J_{\beta}(\mu)+\int V{\rm d}\mu-{\inf_{\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})}\left\{J_{\beta}(\nu)+\int V{\rm d}\nu\right\}}\right)\right\},

which ensures by
[
14
, Theorem 1.2.3]
that
(\mu_{N})
satisfies a large deviations principle under
\mathbb{P}^{V,\beta}_{AL,N}
with the announced rate function. Since the function
J^{V}_{\beta}
is an affine perturbation of
J_{\beta}
, which is convex,
J^{V}_{\beta}
is also convex.
∎

The first point of Theorem
1.4
is proven.

Circular
\beta

In this section, we consider the Circular

3.1
Large deviations principle for Circular
\beta
ensemble

Coulomb gas on the torus
\mathbb{T}=[-\pi,\pi)
at temperature
{\widetilde{\beta}}^{-1}
are described by

{\rm d}\mathbb{P}^{V,{\widetilde{\beta}}}_{C,N}=\frac{1}{Z_{N}^{C}(V,{\widetilde{\beta}})}\prod_{j\leq\ell\leq N}|e^{i\theta_{j}}-e^{i\theta_{\ell}}|^{{\widetilde{\beta}}}e^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}V(\theta_{j})}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\theta}\,,

(3.1)

here
V\,:\,\mathbb{T}\;\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a continuous potential, and
\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N})
. When
V=0
, Killip and Nenciu showed that
{\rm d}\mathbb{P}^{0,{\widetilde{\beta}}}_{C,N}
is the law of the eigenvalues of a CMV matrix
[
22
]
, see Theorem
3.4
. In this manuscript, we are interested in the so-called
high-temperature regime
of this ensemble, namely the limit of number of particles
N
going to infinity with the constraint that
{\widetilde{\beta}}N\to 2\beta>0
. This regime was considered by Hardy and Lambert in
[
21
]
, who proved the following large deviations principle for the measure
\mu_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{e^{i\theta_{j}}}
, where the
\theta_{j}
are distributed according to (
3.1
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\widetilde{\beta}}=\frac{2\beta}{N}
,
\beta>0
and assume
V\;:\;\mathbb{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
to be continuous. Define for any
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})
absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure the functional

f_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)=-\beta\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}\log\left(|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\varphi}|\right)\mu({\rm d}\theta)\mu({\rm d}\varphi)+\beta\log(2)+\int_{\mathbb{T}}V(\theta)\mu({\rm d}\theta)+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\left({\frac{{\rm d}\mu}{{\rm d}\theta}(\theta)}\right)\mu({\rm d}\theta)+\log(2\pi)\,,

(3.2)

then

i.

the functional
f_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)
is strictly convex and achieves its minimal value at the unique probability measure
\mu_{\beta}^{V}
absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure;

ii.

the sequence
(\mu_{N})
satisfies a large deviations principle in
{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})
equipped with the weak topology at speed
\beta N
with rate function defined for absolutely continuous
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})
with respect to Lebesgue measure by
I^{V}_{\beta}(\mu)=f_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)-f_{\beta}^{V}(\mu_{\beta}^{V})
, and
I^{V}_{\beta}(\mu)=+\infty
otherwise. In particular

\mu_{N}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}\mu_{\beta}^{V}\,.

(3.3)

Exploiting this result, in
[
25
]
the authors deduced several useful properties of the minimizer
\mu_{\beta}^{V}
, specifically they proved the following.

Lemma 3.2

(cf.
[
25
]
Lemma 3.5 )
.

Let
\beta>0
, consider a continuous potential
V\,:\,\mathbb{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, then the following holds

i.

The map
\beta\to\inf\left(f^{V}_{\beta}(\mu)\right)
is Lipschitz;

ii.

Let
D
be the distance on
{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{T})
given by

\begin{split}D(\mu,\mu^{\prime})&=\left(-\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}\ln\left|\sin\left(\frac{\theta-\phi}{2}\right)\right|(\mu-\mu^{\prime})({\rm d}\theta)(\mu-\mu^{\prime})({\rm d}\phi)\right)^{1/2}\\
&=\sqrt{\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}\left|{\widehat{\mu}}_{k}-{\widehat{\mu}}^{\prime}_{k}\right|^{2}}\,,\end{split}

(3.4)

where
{\widehat{\mu}}_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{ik\theta}\mu({\rm d}\theta)
.
Then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a finite constant
C_{\varepsilon}
such that for all
\beta,\beta^{\prime}>\varepsilon

D(\mu_{\beta}^{V},\mu_{\beta^{\prime}}^{V})\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left|\beta-\beta^{\prime}\right|\,.

(3.5)

Remark 3.3
.

We observe that if
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
with derivative in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, we can set
||f||_{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{\sum_{k\geq 1}k|{\widehat{f}}_{k}|^{2}}
. So, for any measure
\nu
with zero mass we obtain the following

\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(\theta)\nu({\rm d}\theta)=\sum_{k\neq 0}{\widehat{f}}_{k}\overline{{\widehat{\nu}}_{k}}=\sum_{k\neq 0}k{\widehat{f}}_{k}\frac{\overline{{\widehat{\nu}}_{k}}}{k}\,.

(3.6)

Then, by Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we deduce the following inequality

\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(\theta)\nu({\rm d}\theta)\right|^{2}\leq\left|\sum_{k\neq 0}k{\widehat{f}}_{k}\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{\overline{{\widehat{\nu}}_{k}}}{k}\right|\leq 4||f||_{\frac{1}{2}}^{2}D(\nu,0)^{2}.

(3.7)

Combining (
3.5
) and (

with finite

norm, the map
\beta\to\int_{\mathbb{T}}f{\rm d}\mu_{\beta}^{V}(\theta)
is Lipschitz for
\beta>0
.

3.2

In the case

Theorem 3.4

(cf.
[
22

Theorem 1.2)

Consider the block diagonal

To simplify the notation, we will denote by

To achive our goal, we need several technical results regarding the distribution

(

(

First, in the next Lemma, we give a reprensentation of

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\nu>1
. Let
X_{1},X_{2},Y_{\nu}
be independent,
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
standard Gaussian variables and
Y_{\nu}
be
\chi_{\nu-1}
distributed, i.e. with density

\chi_{\nu-1}(x)=\frac{2^{\frac{3-\nu}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{\nu-1}{2})}x^{\nu-2}e^{-x^{2}/2}\mathbf{1}_{x>0}\,,

here

is the classical Gamma function
[
11

\Gamma(x)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{x-1}e^{-t}dt\,,

(3.11)

Then,
Z:=\frac{X_{1}+iX_{2}}{(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu}^{2})^{1/2}}
follows the law
\Theta_{\nu}
.

Remark 3.6
.

If
\nu\geq 2
is an integer,
Y_{\nu}^{2}
has the law of
\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}N_{i}^{2}
where the
N_{i}

Z
is equal in distribution to
\frac{X_{1}+iX_{2}}{(X_{1}^{2}+\dots+X_{\nu+1}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}
, which follows the law
\Theta_{\nu}
by Remark
2.1
.

Proof.

We identify
{\mathbb{C}}
with
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and check that for any
f:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
bounded and measurable,

{\mathbb{E}}[f(Z)]=\frac{\nu-1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{D}}}f(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{\nu-3}{2}}{\rm d}^{2}z,

i.e. that for some constant
c
,

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{*}}f\left(\frac{x_{1}}{(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+y^{2})^{1/2}},\frac{x_{2}}{(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+y^{2})^{1/2}}\right)&e^{-\frac{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}{2}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}y^{\nu-2}{\rm d}x_{1}{\rm d}x_{2}{\rm d}y\\
&=c\int_{{\mathbb{D}}}f(u,v)(1-(u^{2}+v^{2}))^{\frac{\nu-3}{2}}{\rm d}u{\rm d}v\,.\end{split}

(3.12)

For fixed

(u,v)=\frac{1}{(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+y^{2})^{1/2}}(x_{1},x_{2})

(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{y}{(1-(u^{2}+v^{2}))^{1/2}}(u,v)
has Jacobian equal to

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{f(u,v)}{(1-(u^{2}+v^{2}))^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{*}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(1-(u^{2}+v^{2}))}}y^{\nu}{\rm d}y{\rm d}u{\rm d}v=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{f(u,v)}{(1-(u^{2}+v^{2}))^{3/2}}{\mathbb{E}}[|X_{u,v}|^{\nu}]{\rm d}u{\rm d}v\,,

(3.13)

where

35

{\mathbb{E}}[|X_{u,v}|^{\nu}]=c_{\nu}(1-(u^{2}+v^{2}))^{\frac{\nu}{2}}

∎

5
The Schur Flow

In this section, we consider another integrable model, namely the Schur flow. Our goal is to show that is possible to obtain a similar result to the one that we presented for the Ablowitz-ladik lattice. Namely, we prove the existence of a large deviations principle for the Schur flow, and we relate its density of state to the one of the Jacobi beta ensemble in the high temperature regime.

5.1
Generalized Gibbs Ensemble

The Schur flow is the system of ODEs
[
19
]

\dot{\alpha_{j}}=\rho_{j}^{2}(\alpha_{j+1}-\alpha_{j-1})\,,\quad\rho_{j}=\sqrt{1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}

(5.1)

and, as before, we consider periodic boundary conditions, namely
\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{j+N}
for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

In
[
3
]
, it is argued that the continuum limit of (
5.1
) is the modified Korteweg-de Vries equation:

\partial_{t}u=\partial_{x}^{3}u-6u^{2}\partial_{x}u\,.

(5.2)

We notice that, if one chooses an initial data such that
\alpha_{j}(0)\in{\mathbb{R}}
for all
j=1,\ldots,N
, then
\alpha_{j}(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}
for all times. Moreover, it is straightforeward to verify that
K_{0}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\right)
is conserved along the Schur flow. This implies that we can choose as phase space for the Schur flow the
N
-cube
\mathbb{I}^{N}
, where
{\mathbb{I}}:=(-1,1)
.

On this phase space, we consider the Poisson braket (
1.5
), so we can rewrite the Schur flow (
5.1
) in Hamiltonian form as

\dot{\alpha}_{j}=\{\alpha_{j},H_{S}\},\quad H_{S}=-i\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\alpha_{j}{\overline{\alpha}}_{j+1}-{\overline{\alpha}}_{j}\alpha_{j+1}\right)\,.

(5.3)

It is well known that the Schur flow admits as Lax matrix the same one as the AL
[
19
]
, namely
{\mathcal{E}}
(
1.9
) is the Lax matrix of the Schur flow. This implies that the Ablowitz-Ladik’s constants of motion are conserved also along the Schur flow (
5.1
).

Following the same construction made for the Ablowitz-Ladik lattice, on
{\mathbb{I}}^{N}
we define the finite volume limit GGE as

{\rm d}\mathbb{P}^{V,\beta}_{S,N}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N})=\frac{1}{Z_{N}^{S}(V,\beta)}\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1-\alpha_{j}^{2})^{\beta-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{\alpha_{j}\in{\mathbb{I}}\}}\exp(-\text{Tr}(V(\mathcal{E}))){\rm d}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},

(5.4)

where
Z_{N}^{S}(V,\beta)
is the partition function of the system

Z_{N}^{S}(V,\beta)=\int_{{\mathbb{I}}^{N}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1-\alpha_{j}^{2})^{\beta-1}\exp(-\text{Tr}(V(\mathcal{E}))){\rm d}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}.

Since according to the measure (
5.4
) the matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
is real, its eigenvalues come in pairs
[
28
]
, meaning that if
e^{i\theta_{j}}
is an eigenvalue, then its conjugate
e^{-i\theta_{j}}
is also an eigenvalue. This implies that for a system of size
N
even, there are just
n=N/2
independent eigenvalues. Following the same idea as in
[
22
]
, it is more convenient to restrict the argument of the eigenvalues in
[0,\pi)
and then consider
x_{j}=\cos(\theta_{j})
,
j=1,\ldots,n
. In these variables, the empirical spectral measure
\mu_{n}({\mathcal{E}})
reads:

\mu_{n}({\mathcal{E}})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{x_{j}}\,,\quad x_{j}\in{\mathbb{I}}\,.

(5.5)

As a corollary of Lemma
2.3
and Proposition
2.4
, we obtain the existence of a large deviations principle for the sequence
(\mu_{n}({\mathcal{E}}))
, namely:

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
V\;:\;{\mathbb{I}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be continuous. Under
\mathbb{P}^{V,\beta}_{S,n}
the sequence
(\mu_{n}({\mathcal{E}}))
fulfils a large deviations principle with good, convex rate function
S_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)=h_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)-\inf_{\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{I}})}h_{\beta}^{V}(\nu)
, where

h_{\beta}^{V}(\nu)=K_{\beta}(\nu)+\int_{\mathbb{I}}V{\rm d}\nu\,,

(5.6)

where
K_{\beta}(\nu)
is the rate function of
\mu_{n}
under the law
\mathbb{P}^{0,\beta}_{S,n}
.

5.2
Jacobi beta ensemble in the high temperature regime

The Jacobi beta ensemble refers to the distribution of charges constrained to the segment
{\mathbb{I}}
, and subjected to an external potential
W(x)=-a\ln(1-x)-b\ln(1+x)+V(x)
, here
a,b>-1
and
W(x)\in C^{0}({\mathbb{I}})
. Specifically the joint distribution of these particles is

{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{J,n}^{(V,{\widetilde{\beta}})}=\frac{1}{Z_{N}^{J}(V,{\widetilde{\beta}})}\prod_{i<j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{{\widetilde{\beta}}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-x_{j})^{a}(1+x_{j})^{b}e^{-V(x_{j})}{\rm d}x_{j}\,.

(5.7)

In
[
22
]
, Killip and Nenciu were able to show that the distribution (
5.7
) can be realized as the eigenvalues distribution of a particular CMV matrix, specifically they proved the following

Theorem 5.2

(cf.
[
22
]
Proposition 5.3)
.

Let
N=2n
, consider the CMV matrix
E
in (
3.10
) with parameters
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{2n-1}\in{\mathbb{I}}
distributed according to

{\rm d}\mathbb{B}_{n}^{(V,{\widetilde{\beta}})}=\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{Z}}_{n}(V,{\widetilde{\beta}})}\prod_{j=1}^{2n-1}(1-\alpha_{j}^{2})^{{\widetilde{\beta}}(2n-j)/4-1}\prod_{j=1}^{2n-1}(1-\alpha_{j})^{a+1-{\widetilde{\beta}}/4}(1+(-1)^{j+1}\alpha_{j})^{b+1-{\widetilde{\beta}}/4}e^{\text{Tr}{V(E)}}{\rm d}\alpha_{j}\,,

(5.8)

and
\alpha_{2n}=-1
, here
{\mathfrak{Z}}_{N}(V,\beta)
is the normalization constant. Then all the eigenvalues of
E
come in pairs, meaning that if
e^{i\theta_{j}}
is an eigenvalue, then also
e^{-i\theta_{j}}
is one. Moreover, under the change of variables
\cos(\theta_{j})=x_{j}
, the
x_{j}
s are distributed according to (
5.7
).

Remark 5.3
.

We notice that the previous proposition is not stated in this way in
[
22
]
, but this equivalent formulation is more useful for our purpose.

Also in this case, we are interested in the high temperature regime for this ensemble. Specifically we consider the situation
{\widetilde{\beta}}=\frac{4\beta}{N}=\frac{2\beta}{n}
, and
a=b=-1+\frac{{\widetilde{\beta}}}{4}
, in this regime
{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{J,n}^{\left(V,\frac{\beta}{n}\right)}
reads

{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{J,n}^{\left(V,\frac{2\beta}{n}\right)}=\frac{1}{Z_{N}^{J}\left(V,\frac{\beta}{n}\right)}\prod_{i<j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{\frac{2\beta}{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-x_{j})^{-1+\frac{\beta}{2n}}(1+x_{j})^{-1+\frac{\beta}{2n}}e^{-V(x_{j})}{\rm d}x_{j}\,,

(5.9)

and
{\rm d}\mathbb{B}_{n}^{\left(V,\frac{\beta}{n}\right)}
becomes

{\rm d}\mathbb{B}_{n}^{\left(V,\frac{\beta}{n}\right)}=\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{Z}}_{n}\left(V,\frac{\beta}{n}\right)}\prod_{j=1}^{2n-1}(1-\alpha_{j}^{2})^{\beta\left(1-\frac{j}{2n}\right)-1}\prod_{j=1}^{2n-1}e^{\text{Tr}{V(E)}}{\rm d}\alpha_{j}\,.

(5.10)

We mention that this particular regime was considered in
[
16
,
34
]
. In these papers the authors computed the density of states for this ensemble in the case
V=0
.

We can apply
[
17
, Corollary 1.3]
to (
5.9
) to obtain a large deviations principle for the empirical measure
\mu_{n}(E)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{x_{j}}
. Specifically, we deduce that

Proposition 5.4
.

For any continuous
V\;:\;{\mathbb{I}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. The law of the empirical measures
\mu_{n}(E)
under
{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{J,n}^{\left(V,\frac{2\beta}{n}\right)}
satisfies a large deviations principle at speed
n
in the space
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{I}})
, with a good rate function
\mu\mapsto Q_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)
given for
\mu
absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure, and with density
\frac{{\rm d}\mu}{{\rm d}x}
, by
Q_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)=q_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)-\inf_{\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{I}})}q_{\beta}^{V}(\nu)
, where

q_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)=\int_{\mathbb{I}}(V(x)+\ln(1+x)+\ln(1-x)){\rm d}\mu(x)-2\beta\int_{{\mathbb{I}}\times{\mathbb{I}}}\ln(|x-y|){\rm d}\mu(x){\rm d}\mu(y)+\int_{\mathbb{I}}\ln\left(\frac{{\rm d}\mu}{{\rm d}x}(x)\right){\rm d}\mu(x)\,,

(5.11)

and
Q_{\beta}^{V}(\mu)=+\infty
otherwise.

We notice that the arguments in Section
3
and
4
can be applied also in this context with
{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{J,n}^{\left(V,\frac{2\beta}{n}\right)}
in place of
{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{C,N}^{\left(V,\frac{2\beta}{N}\right)}
, and
{\rm d}\mathbb{P}^{V,\beta}_{S,N}
in place of
{\rm d}\mathbb{P}^{V,\beta}_{AL,N}
. Hence, we deduce the following result

Theorem 5.5
.

Consider the sequence of measures
\mu_{n}({\mathcal{E}})
(
5.5
) under the law
{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{S,2n}^{V,\beta}
(
5.4
), then

\mu_{n}({\mathcal{E}})\xrightarrow{\textrm{a.s.}}\nu_{\beta}^{V}\,.

(5.12)

Moreover,
\nu_{\beta}^{V}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, and it reads

\nu_{\beta}^{V}=\partial_{\beta}(\beta\mu_{\beta}^{V})\,,

(5.13)

where
\mu_{\beta}^{V}
is the unique minimizer of the functional
q_{\beta}^{V}
(
5.11
).

Finally, it is worth to mention that in the case
V(x)=0
, it is possible to compute explicitly the densities of states for both the Jacobi beta ensemble at high temperature and for the Schur flow
[
24
,
16
]
.

Appendix A
Technical Results

In this appendix we collect the proof of all the technical results that we exploit along the proof of the main theorem. For reader convenience, we report here the statement of Lemmas.

Proof of Lemma
2.2

Lemma A.1
.

For any
A
,
B
unitary matrices of size
N\times N
, we have

•

For
f
with bounded variation,

\left|\int fd\mu-\int fd\nu\right|\leq\|f\|_{\text{BV}}\frac{rank(A-B)}{N},

•

For
f
Lipschitz,

\left|\int fd\mu-\int fd\nu\right|\leq\|f\|_{\text{Lip}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j}|(A-B)_{i,j}|.

As a consequence,

d(\mu(A),\mu(B))\leq\min\left\{\frac{rank(A-B)}{N},\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j}|(A-B)_{i,j}|\right\}.

(A.1)

Proof.

The first point is a consequence of the fact that the eigenvalues of
A
and
B
interlace on the unit circle.

First, we order the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(A),\ldots,\lambda_{N}(A),\lambda_{1}(B),\ldots,\lambda_{N}(B)
of
A,B
in such a way that

-\pi\leq\arg(\lambda_{1}(A))\leq\ldots\leq\arg(\lambda_{N}(A))<\pi\,,

(A.2)

and analogously for
B
.

Write
B=(I_{N}+(B-A)A^{-1})A
and set
U:=I_{N}+(B-A)A^{-1}
. One checks that
U
is unitary,
B=UA
, and that
\text{rank}(U-I)=\text{rank}(B-A)=:r
. By
[
5
, section 6, equation (85)]
, we deduce that for
1\leq j\leq N

\arg(\lambda_{j-r}(A))\leq\arg(\lambda_{j}(B))\leq\arg(\lambda_{j+r}(A))\,.

(A.3)

This means that
\lambda_{j}(B)
lies on the anticlockwise arc
(\arg(\lambda_{j-r}(A)),\arg(\lambda_{j+r}(A)))
of the circle. If
j-r\leq 0
we identify
\lambda_{j-r}
with
\lambda_{j-r+N}
, and analogously for the case
j+r>N
.

It is a classical result (see
[
4
]
) to deduce from (
A.3
) that

\left|\int fd\mu_{N}(A)-\int fd\mu_{N}(B)\right|\leq\|f\|_{\text{BV}}\frac{r}{N}=\frac{r}{N}\,,

for any
f:\mathbb{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
||f||_{BV}\leq 1
. As a consequence, we obtain the first point.

The proof of the second point is the same as in the symmetric case, see
[
20
, (16)]
. Indeed, we only use the fact that a normal matrix is unitarily diagonalizable.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.7

Lemma A.2
.

Let
N=2k
be even and
A
be a
N\times N
matrix. Then,

•

\sum_{i,j}|({\mathcal{L}}A)_{i,j}|\leq 2\sum_{i,j}|A_{i,j}|
,

•

\sum_{i,j}|(A{\mathcal{M}})_{i,j}|\leq 2\sum_{i,j}|A_{i,j}|,

where
{\mathcal{M}}
, and
{\mathcal{L}}
are defined in (
1.8
).

Proof.

We will just prove the first point, since the proof of the second one follows the same lines.

For
0\leq l\leq k-1
and
1\leq j\leq N
, consider

({\mathcal{L}}A)_{2l+1,j}=\overline{\alpha}_{2l+1}A_{2l+1,j}+\rho_{2l+1}A_{2l+2,j}\quad\text{and}\quad({\mathcal{L}}A)_{2l+2,j}=\rho_{2l+1}A_{2l+1,j}-\alpha_{2l+2}A_{2l+2,j}.

Summing over
i,j
,

\sum_{i,j}\left|({\mathcal{L}}A)_{i,j}\right|=\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left|({\mathcal{L}}A)_{2l+1,j}\right|+\left|({\mathcal{L}}A)_{2l+2,j}\right|\leq 2\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|A_{2l+1,j}|+|A_{2l+2,j}|=2\sum_{i,j}|A_{i,j}|,

where we used that
|\alpha_{i}|,\rho_{i}\leq 1
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.8

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\alpha_{\nu}
and
\alpha_{\nu+h}
defined by equation (
3.14
). Define
\rho_{\nu}=\sqrt{1-|\alpha_{\nu}|^{2}}
, and
\rho_{\nu+h}=\sqrt{1-|\alpha_{\nu+h}|^{2}}
, then the following hold

1.

\begin{split}&|\alpha_{\nu}-\alpha_{\nu+h}|\leq\frac{Y_{h}}{(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{h}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,,\,\text{almost surely}\,,\\
&|\rho_{\nu}-\rho_{\nu+h}|\leq\frac{Y_{h}}{(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{h}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,,\,\text{almost surely}\,,\end{split}

(A.4)

where
X_{1},X_{2}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
,
Y_{h}\sim\chi_{h}
are all independent.

2.

define
Z_{h}=\frac{Y_{h}}{(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{h}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}
, and
a(h)=-\frac{1}{2}\ln(h)+1
, then there exists a constant
K
independent of
h
such that

\sup_{0<h<1}{\bf E}\left[\exp(a(h)Z_{h})\right]\leq K\,.

(A.5)

Proof.

First, we focus on claim
i.
.
We recall that
\alpha_{\nu},\alpha_{\nu+h}
are defined by

\alpha_{\nu}{=}\frac{X_{1}+iX_{2}}{(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu-1}^{2})}\,,\quad\alpha_{\nu+h}{=}\frac{X_{1}+iX_{2}}{(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu-1}^{2}+Y_{h}^{2})}\,.

(A.6)

From the previous equation, we deduce that

\displaystyle|\alpha_{\nu}-\alpha_{\nu+h}|

\displaystyle=\frac{|X_{1}+iX_{2}|}{(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu-1}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(1-\left(\frac{X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu-1}^{2}}{X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu-1}^{2}+Y_{h}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|X_{1}+iX_{2}|}{(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu-1}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(1-\left(1-\frac{Y_{h}^{2}}{X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu-1}^{2}+Y_{h}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}}{X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu-1}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{Y_{h}}{(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu-1}^{2}+Y_{h}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}},

where we used in the previous line that for
0\leq a\leq b
we have

\sqrt{b}\leq\sqrt{b-a}+\sqrt{a}

(A.7)

and we took
a=\frac{Y_{h}^{2}}{X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu-1}^{2}+Y_{h}^{2}}
,
b=1
. The last term is bounded by the announced bound.

One can proceed analogously for
|\rho_{\nu}-\rho_{\nu+h}|
obtaining that

\begin{split}|\rho(\alpha_{\nu+h})-\rho(\alpha_{\nu})|&=\sqrt{1-|\alpha_{\nu+h}|^{2}}-\sqrt{1-|\alpha_{\nu}|^{2}}\leq\sqrt{|\alpha_{\nu}|^{2}-|\alpha_{\nu+h}|^{2}}\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}}{X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu-1}^{2}}}\sqrt{1-\frac{X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu-1}^{2}}{X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu-1}^{2}+Y_{h}^{2}}}\leq\frac{Y_{h}}{(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+Y_{\nu-1}^{2}+Y_{h}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\end{split}

(A.8)

where we used again equation (
A.7
) with
a=1-|\alpha_{\nu}|^{2}
and
b=1-|\alpha_{\nu+h}|^{2}
. Thus, point
i.
is proved.

To prove point
ii.
, we find explicitly the law of
Z_{h}
. Thus, we consider a continuous function
f:\;(0,1)\;\to{\mathbb{R}}
, and we compute:

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}f\left(\frac{y}{\left(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+y^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)e^{-\frac{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+y^{2}}{2}}y^{h-1}{\rm d}x_{1}{\rm d}x_{2}{\rm d}y\,.

(A.9)

Performing the change of coordinates
(u,v)=\frac{1}{(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+y^{2})^{1/2}}(x_{1},x_{2})
, which is the same one that we performed in Lemma
3.5
, we obtain that

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}f\left(\frac{y}{\left(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+y^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)e^{-\frac{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+y^{2}}{2}}y^{h-1}{\rm d}x_{1}{\rm d}x_{2}{\rm d}y\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{D}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\frac{f\left(\sqrt{1-u^{2}-v^{2}}\right)}{(1-u^{2}-v^{2})^{2}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(1-u^{2}-v^{2})}}y^{h+1}{\rm d}u{\rm d}v{\rm d}y\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sqrt{1-u^{2}-v^{2}}t=y}}{{=}}\int_{{\mathbb{D}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}}f\left(\sqrt{1-u^{2}-v^{2}}\right)\left(1-u^{2}-v^{2}\right)^{\frac{h}{2}-1}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}t^{h+1}{\rm d}u{\rm d}v{\rm d}t\,.\end{split}

(A.10)

We can now explicitly compute the integral in
t
. Moreover, we can express the remaining part of the integral in polar coordinates; namely, we apply the change of variables
u=\rho\cos(\theta),v=\rho\sin(\theta)
, obtaining that:

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{D}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}}f\left(\sqrt{1-u^{2}-v^{2}}\right)\left(1-u^{2}-v^{2}\right)^{\frac{h}{2}-1}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}t^{h+1}{\rm d}u{\rm d}v{\rm d}t\\
&=2\pi 2^{\frac{h}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{h}{2}+1\right)\int_{0}^{1}\rho f(\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}})\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)^{\frac{h}{2}-1}{\rm d}\rho\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}={w}}}{{=}}2\pi 2^{\frac{h}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{h}{2}+1\right)\int_{0}^{1}f(w)w^{h-1}{{\rm d}w}\,,\end{split}

(A.11)

here
\Gamma(x)
is the gamma function (
3.11
). Thus, in order to obtain the estimate (
A.5
), we have to deduce an upper bound for

\sup_{0<h<1}\frac{\int_{0}^{1}e^{a(h)w}w^{h-1}{\rm d}w}{\int_{0}^{1}w^{h-1}{\rm d}w}\,.

(A.12)

For any
0<h<1
, we can explicitly compute the denominator as

\int_{0}^{1}w^{h-1}{\rm d}w=\frac{1}{h}\,.

(A.13)

Moreover, we can give an upper bound on the numerator as

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}e^{a(h)w}w^{h-1}{\rm d}w&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a(h)w=r}}{{=}}\frac{1}{a(h)^{h}}\int_{0}^{a(h)}e^{r}r^{h-1}{\rm d}r=\frac{1}{a(h)^{h}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}e^{r}r^{h-1}{\rm d}r+\int_{1}^{a(h)}e^{r}r^{h-1}{\rm d}r\right)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{a(h)^{h}}\left(e\int_{0}^{1}r^{h-1}{\rm d}r+\int_{1}^{a(h)}e^{r}{\rm d}r\right)\leq\frac{{e}}{a(h)^{h}h}+\frac{e^{a(h)}}{a(h)^{h}}\,.\end{split}

(A.14)

Combining (
A.13
)-(
A.14
), with our choice of
a(h)=-\frac{1}{2}\ln(h)+1
, we deduce that there exists a constant
K
independent of
h
such that (
A.5
) holds.

∎

Proof of Lemma
4.3

Lemma A.4
.

Let
M_{\beta}^{V}=(J_{\beta}^{V})^{-1}(\{0\})
be the set of minimizers of
J_{\beta}^{V}
. Then,
\beta\mapsto M_{\beta}^{V}
is continuous in the sense that for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
0\leq h\leq\delta
,
M_{\beta+h}^{V}\subset(M_{\beta}^{V})^{\varepsilon}
, where for
A\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
we denote
A^{\varepsilon}=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})\ |\ d(\mu,A)\leq\varepsilon\}.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. We are going to show that for
h>0
small enough, we have

-\inf_{\left[(M_{\beta}^{V})^{\varepsilon}\right]^{c}}J^{V}_{\beta+h}<0,

which will ensure that
J^{V}_{\beta+h}>0
on
\left[(M_{\beta}^{V})^{\varepsilon}\right]^{c}
, thus
\left[(M_{\beta}^{V})^{\varepsilon}\right]^{c}\subset\left[(M_{\beta+h}^{V})\right]^{c},
and hence the conclusion.

By the large deviations principle for
(\mu_{N})_{N\text{ even}}
under
\mathbb{P}^{V,\beta}_{AL,N}
, Corollary
2.5
, since
\left[(M_{\beta}^{V})^{\varepsilon}\right]^{c}
is open, we have

\displaystyle-\inf_{\left[(M_{\beta}^{V})^{\varepsilon}\right]^{c}}J^{V}_{\beta+h}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{N\text{ even}}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}^{V,\beta+h}_{AL,N}\left(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}})\in\left[(M_{\beta}^{V})^{\varepsilon}\right]^{c}\right)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{N\text{ even}}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}^{V,\beta+h}_{AL,N}\left(d(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}),M_{\beta}^{V})>\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{N\text{ even}}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathbb{P}^{V,\beta+h}_{AL,N}\left(d(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}),M_{\beta}^{V})\geq\varepsilon\right)\,.

Since for any positive
h
and
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in{\mathbb{D}}^{N}

\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2})^{h}\leq 1
,we deduce that for any
A\subseteq{\mathbb{D}}^{N}

\frac{1}{N}\ln\left(\mathbb{P}^{V,\beta+h}_{AL,N}\left(A\right)\right)\leq\frac{1}{N}\left(\ln\left(\frac{Z_{N}^{AL}(V,\beta)}{Z_{N}^{AL}(V,\beta+h)}\right)+\ln\left(\mathbb{P}_{AL,N}^{V,\beta}(A)\right)\right)\,,

(A.15)

we recall that
\mathbb{P}_{AL,N}^{V,\beta}
is defined in (
1.14
).

Applying the previous inequality in the case
A=\{d(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}),M_{\beta}^{V})\geq\varepsilon\}
, we conclude that

-\inf_{\left[(M_{\beta}^{V})^{\varepsilon}\right]^{c}}J^{V}_{\beta+h}\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\left(\ln\left(\frac{Z_{N}^{AL}(V,\beta)}{Z_{N}^{AL}(V,\beta+h)}\right)+\ln\left(\mathbb{P}_{AL,N}^{V,\beta}(d(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}),M_{\beta}^{V})\geq\varepsilon)\right)\right)\,.

(A.16)

From Corollary
2.5
, we deduce that there exists a positive constant
c
, independent of
h
, such that

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{P}_{AL,N}^{V,\beta}(d(\mu_{N}({\mathcal{E}}),M_{\beta}^{V})\geq\varepsilon))\leq-\inf_{{\overline{\left[(M_{\beta}^{V})^{\varepsilon}\right]^{c}}}}J^{V}_{\beta}<-c\,.

(A.17)

Thus, to conclude we have just to prove that the function
g(\beta)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln\left(Z_{N}^{AL}(V,\beta)\right)
is continuous in
\beta
. Actually, we prove that this function is convex in
\beta
. Let
1/p+1/q=1
, and
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
then

\begin{split}Z_{N}^{AL}\left(V,\frac{\beta_{1}}{p}+\frac{\beta_{2}}{q}\right)&=\int_{{\mathbb{D}}^{N}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2})^{\frac{\beta_{1}}{p}+\frac{\beta_{2}}{q}-1}\exp(-\text{Tr}(V(\mathcal{E}))){\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{D}}^{N}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2})^{\frac{\beta_{1}-1}{p}+\frac{\beta_{2}-1}{q}}\exp\left(-\left(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\right)\text{Tr}(V({\mathcal{E}}))\right){\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\\
&\leq Z_{N}^{AL}\left(V,\beta_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}Z_{N}^{AL}\left(V,\beta_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,,\end{split}

(A.18)

where in the last inequality we used Hölder inequality. This implies that

g\left(\frac{\beta_{1}}{p}+\frac{\beta_{2}}{q}\right)\leq\frac{1}{p}g(\beta_{1})+\frac{1}{q}g(\beta_{2})\,,

(A.19)

thus
g(\beta)
is convex, and so continuous, for
\beta>0
. We can now choose
h
is such a way that

\Bigg|\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln\left(\frac{Z_{N}^{AL}(V,\beta)}{Z_{N}^{AL}(V,\beta+h)}\right)\Bigg|<c\,,

so we obtain that

\inf_{\left[(M_{\beta}^{V})^{\varepsilon}\right]^{c}}J^{V}_{\beta+h}>0\,.

(A.20)

∎

Bulk Gauge Fields and Holographic RG from Exact RG
Pavan Dharanipragada, Semanti Dutta, B. Sathiapalan

Abstract
Recently, a method was described for deriving Holographic RG equation in A ​ d ​ S D + 1 AdS_{D+1} space starting from an Exact RG equation of a D D -dimensional boundary CFT [ 22 ] . The evolution operator corresponding to the Exact RG equation was rewritten as a functional integral of a D + 1 D+1 dimensional field theory in A ​ d ​ S D + 1 AdS_{D+1} space. This method has since been applied to elementary scalars and composite scalars in the O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) model [ 34 ] . In this paper, we apply this technique to the conserved vector current and the energy momentum tensor of a boundary CFT, the O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) model at a fixed point. These composite spin one and spin two operators are represented by auxiliary fields and extend into the bulk as gauge fields and metric perturbations. We obtain, at the free level, the (gauge fixed) Maxwell and Einstein actions. While the steps involved are motivated by the AdS/CFT correspondence, none of the steps logically require the AdS/CFT conjecture for their justification.

One of the remarkable features of the AdS/CFT correspondence is that the bulk theory has gravity—the metric field being dual to the energy momentum tensor operator of the boundary theory. It also has gauge fields dual to conserved currents generating global symmetries of the boundary theory. In this paper, this is the main object of study. We derive ERG equations for the action that describes correlators of the energy momentum tensor and O(N) currents of the boundary theory. Once again, auxiliary fields are defined for the composite operators, and ERG equations are written for the action for these auxiliary fields. The term “auxiliary field” is used in this paper to stand for any dynamical field that does not have a tree level kinetic term, i.e., its classical equation of motion is algebraic. The word dynamical signifies then that, in the functional integral, this field is integrated over. This is in contrast with external fields that are non-dynamical, i.e., not integrated over.

\chi
in order to obtain the anomalous dimension. So, for simplicity, we choose
\chi_{0}=0
.

We change variables:
\chi+J=\eta
. Then let
\eta=\sqrt{\bar{\lambda}}\eta^{\prime}
.
We get

Z[J]=\int{\cal D}\eta^{\prime}e^{-\frac{1}{2}NTr\ln[1+2\frac{\Delta J}{\sqrt{N}}-2\frac{\sqrt{\bar{\lambda}}\Delta\eta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{N}}]-\int\frac{\eta^{\prime 2}}{2}}.

(2.3.28)

In the expnasion of the logarithm, the following two terms (cf. Figure
2
) give the anomalous dimension

2{\bar{\lambda}}\int{\cal D}\eta^{\prime}\{Tr(\Delta J\Delta\eta^{\prime})Tr(\Delta J\Delta\eta^{\prime})+\frac{4{\bar{\lambda}}}{N}Tr[(\Delta J\Delta\eta^{\prime}\Delta J\Delta\eta^{\prime})]\}e^{-\int\frac{\eta^{\prime 2}}{2}}.

(2.3.29)

Since the
\eta^{\prime}
propagator just gives a delta function in position space, the momentum loop integrals in both terms give the same value. The final answer thus has a factor

2\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{N}(N+2)
. This is the correct
N
-dependence. Details are given in Appendix
A.2
.

3
Introducing a Regulator: Auxiliary Fields and ERG

In this section, we derive an Exact RG equation for the auxiliary field action. First, we show that the Wilson action for auxiliary field
\sigma
is the natural object to find the correlators. We then set up the ERG equation. We argue that it is necessary to perform an expansion in powers of
1/N
in order to make contact with Holographic renormalization. Hence, at the end, we transfer all the results to N-scalar field theory. The ERG equation we derive has a linear term, and cubic and higher order terms at subleading order, in addition to the usual quadratic term present in Polchinski’s equation. The linear term can be cancelled by redefinition of the auxiliary field. The cubic term is of the form derived in
[
34
]
.

3.1
Regulated Theory

We have been fairly cavalier about defining composite fields in the last section because it is a free theory. As a preparation for the interacting theory let us rectify this by introducing a regulator so that there are no short distance singularities. We will then be able to define an ERG equation.

We start with

Z[J]=\int{\cal D}\phi~e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int\phi\Delta^{-1}\phi-S_{B,I}[\phi,J]}.

(3.1.1)

We have included the dependence on the source
J
as part of
S_{B,I}
to be completely general. For the free theory, if we are interested in the composite
\phi^{2}
we take

S_{B,I}[\phi,J]=-i\int J\phi^{2}=-i\int J\sigma,

(3.1.2)

where we have anticipated that an auxiliary field
\sigma
will be introduced to stand for
\phi^{2}
.

We write
\phi=\phi_{l}+\phi_{h}
and
\Delta=\Delta_{l}+\Delta_{h}
as usual

5

5

See
[

We take
\Delta=\frac{K_{0}}{p^{2}}
and
\Delta_{l}=\frac{K}{p^{2}}

\Lambda_{0}

\Lambda

\Delta_{l}

\Lambda_{0}

\Delta_{h}

\Lambda

\Lambda_{0}

\phi

\chi,\sigma

Nevertheless, since the only interactions these fields have are of the form

\phi_{h}

We define first the interacting part of the Wilson action

\displaystyle e^{-S_{\Lambda,I}[\phi_{l},J]}

\displaystyle=\int{\cal D}\phi_{h}~e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int\phi_{h}\Delta^{-1}_{h}\phi_{h}-S_{B,I}[\phi_{l}+\phi_{h},J]}

(3.1.4)

For the case at hand,

(3.1.5)

and then the full Wilson action is

S_{\Lambda}[\phi_{l},J]=\frac{1}{2}\int\phi_{l}\Delta^{-1}_{l}\phi_{l}+S_{\Lambda,I}[\phi_{l},J],

(3.1.6)

and also

S_{\Lambda}[\phi_{l},\sigma]=\frac{1}{2}\int\phi_{l}\Delta^{-1}_{l}\phi_{l}+S_{\Lambda,I}[\phi_{l},\sigma],

Note:

In ERG literature, the composite operator

(3.1.10)

The physical interpretation is that these are correlations in a theory with an IR cutoff at
\Lambda

\lim_{\Lambda\to 0}\langle\sigma(x_{1})...\sigma(x_{n})\rangle_{\Lambda}=\langle\sigma(x_{1})...\sigma(x_{n})\rangle_{B}=\langle\phi^{2}(x_{1})...\phi^{2}(x_{n})\rangle_{B}.

(3.1.11)

We use the notation:

S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]=S_{\Lambda}[0,\sigma],~~~S_{\Lambda}[J]=S_{\Lambda}[0,J].

From (

Z_{\Lambda}[\phi_{l},J]=e^{-S_{\Lambda}[\phi_{l},J]}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int\phi_{l}\Delta_{l}^{-1}\phi_{l}}\int{\cal D}\phi_{h}~e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int\phi_{h}\Delta^{-1}_{h}\phi_{h}+i\int J(\phi_{l}+\phi_{h})^{2}}.

(3.1.13)

This is the generating functional of a theory with IR cutoff

The full generating functional (independent of

Z[J]=\int{\cal D}\phi_{l}~e^{-S_{\Lambda}[\phi_{l},J]}.

(3.1.14)

It is also true that

\lim_{\Lambda\to 0}Z_{\Lambda}[0,J]=Z[J].

(3.1.15)

Thus, if we could determine

We are thus interested in obtaining an equation for
S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]

3.2
ERG equation of
S_{\Lambda,I}[\phi_{l},J]

S_{\Lambda,I}[\phi_{l},J]

using (

Polchinski’s ERG Equation is

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}e^{-S_{\Lambda,I}[\phi_{l},J]}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\int_{y}\dot{\Delta}_{hxy}\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta\phi_{l}(x)\delta\phi_{l}(y)}e^{-S_{\Lambda,I}[\phi_{l},J]}.

(3.2.16)

S_{\Lambda,I}[\phi_{l},J]
is given by doing the Gaussian integral in (

S_{\Lambda,I}[\phi_{l},J]=-\int_{x}iJ(x)\phi_{l}^{2}(x)+2\int_{x}\int_{y}J\phi_{l}(x)(\frac{\Delta_{h}}{1-2i\Delta_{h}J})_{xy}J\phi_{l}(y)+\frac{1}{2}Tr\ln[1-2i\Delta_{h}J].

(3.2.17)

Simplifying:

S_{\Lambda,I}[\phi_{l},J]=-\int_{x}\int_{y}J\phi_{l}(x)\Big(\frac{1}{1-2i\Delta_{h}J}\Big)_{xy}\phi_{l}(y)+\frac{1}{2}Tr\ln[1-2i\Delta_{h}J].

(3.2.18)

In the limit

B.1

\boxed{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}e^{-S_{\Lambda,I}[\phi_{l},J]}|_{\phi_{l}=0}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\int_{y}\left(\frac{2i\dot{\Delta}_{h}J}{1-2i\Delta_{h}J}\right)_{xy}}.

(3.2.19)

It can also be shown that it is a fixed point. (See Appendix
B.1
).

3.3
ERG equation for

We go back to (

\displaystyle e^{-S_{\Lambda,I}[\phi_{l},\sigma]}

\displaystyle=\int{\cal D}\chi~e^{i\int\chi\sigma-i\int\chi\phi_{l}^{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}Tr\ln[\frac{1}{\Delta_{h}}+2i\chi]}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\int_{y}(2\chi\phi_{l}(x))([\frac{1}{\Delta_{h}}+2i\chi]^{-1})_{xy}(2\chi\phi_{l}(y))}

Let us work out the ERG equation obeyed by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}e^{-S_{\Lambda,I}[0,\sigma]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\int_{y}\dot{\Delta}_{hxy}\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta\phi_{l}(x)\delta\phi_{l}(y)}e^{-S_{\Lambda,I}[\phi_{l},\sigma]}|_{\phi_{l}=0}

(3.3.22)

This a functional differential equation where only the quadratic term is of the Polchinski form. The linear term can be set to zero by a choice of counterterm linear in
\sigma

\sigma

The cubic and higher terms were dealt with in

Order by order in

These also become potential terms in the AdS action when one performs the mapping to AdS as described in
[
22
]
. This was worked out in
[
34
]
for the
O(N)

The conclusion is then that a small parameter

\phi^{I}
, and a normalization for the kinetic term that ensures that the propagator for

\displaystyle e^{-S_{\Lambda,I}[\phi_{l},\sigma]}

\displaystyle=\int{\cal D}\chi~e^{i\int\chi\sigma-i\int\chi\phi_{l}^{2}}e^{-\frac{N}{2}Tr\ln[\frac{\sqrt{N}}{\Delta_{h}}+2i\chi]^{-1}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\int_{y}(2\chi\phi_{l}^{I}(x)([\frac{\sqrt{N}}{\Delta_{h}}+2i\chi]^{-1})_{xy}2\chi\phi_{l}^{I}(y)}

4
Vector Auxiliary Fields

We consider the simplest conserved current in the boundary CFT—the current associated with the global O(N) symmetry. As before, we define an auxiliary vector field to stand in for the current. This field is defined as being dual to an external
O(N)
gauge field. This ensures that current conservation is obeyed all along the RG trajectory as long as gauge invariance is. We show that the evolution operator corresponding to the ERG equation of the auxiliary field gives the kinetic term for an action of a theory of gauge boson in D+1 dimensions. As we set out to show the result only to leading order, we take the current to be effectively abelian in this paper. This section deals with defining the auxiliary field and its action. After we have defined a suitable action for the auxiliary field, the ERG equation is worked out in the next section.

4.1
What action should we work with?

A generalization of the auxiliary field technique that was used for scalar composites would lead us to the following action:

S_{B}=\int_{x}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{N}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{I}\partial^{\mu}\phi^{I}+\frac{\bar{u}}{2}\sigma^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{N}r\sigma+\chi(\sigma-\phi.\phi)-\chi_{\mu}^{AB}(\phi_{A}\overleftrightarrow{\partial^{\mu}}\phi_{B}-\sigma^{\mu}_{AB})\Big].

(4.1.1)

Here,
\chi_{\mu}^{AB}
is a Lagrange multiplier that imposes

\sigma^{\mu}_{AB}=\phi_{A}\overleftrightarrow{\partial^{\mu}}\phi_{B}.

We need to improve on this because we want
\sigma_{\mu}^{AB}
to always be the effective current—which may not be just
\phi^{A}\overleftrightarrow{\partial_{\mu}}\phi^{B}
. So, we start by introducing an external gauge field
A^{\mu}_{IJ}
to gauge the
O(N)
symmetry. Thus we start with:

S_{B}[\phi^{I},\chi,\sigma,A_{\mu J}^{I}]=\int_{x}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{N}D_{\mu}\phi^{I}D^{\mu}\phi^{I}+\frac{\bar{u}}{2}\sigma^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{N}r\sigma+\chi(\sigma-\phi.\phi)\Big],

(4.1.2)

where

D_{\mu}\phi^{I}=\partial_{\mu}\phi^{I}-A_{\mu J}^{I}\phi^{J}.

For the
O(N)
group, we don’t have to distinguish between upstairs and downstairs indices, so,
A_{\mu IJ}=A_{\mu}^{IJ}=A_{\mu J}^{I}
. The order from left to right is important;
A_{\mu J}^{I}=-A_{\mu I}^{J}
.

4.2
S[\sigma_{\mu},A^{\mu}]
from

Z[A_{\mu}^{IJ}]=\int{\cal D}\phi^{I}e^{-S_{B}[\phi,A_{\mu}^{IJ}]}=\int{\cal D}\phi^{I}\int{\cal D}\sigma\int{\cal D}\chi~e^{-S_{B}[\phi,\sigma,\chi,A_{\mu}^{IJ}]}.

(4.2.3)

After doing the

Z[A_{\mu}^{IJ}]=\int{\cal D}\phi^{I}\int{\cal D}\chi e^{-S_{B}[\phi^{I},\chi,A_{\mu}^{IJ}]},

(4.2.4)

\displaystyle S_{B}[\phi^{I},\chi,A_{\mu}^{IJ}]=

\displaystyle\int_{x}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{N}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{I}\partial^{\mu}\phi^{I}+\sqrt{N}A_{\mu}^{IJ}(\phi^{I}\overleftrightarrow{\partial}_{\mu}\phi^{J})+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{N}A_{\mu}^{IJ}A_{\mu}^{IK}\phi^{J}\phi^{K}

\displaystyle+\frac{(\chi+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{N}r)^{2}}{2\bar{u}}-\chi\phi.\phi\Big].

(4.2.5)

The
O(N)

j_{\mu}^{IJ}=\frac{\delta}{\delta A^{\mu}_{IJ}}.

(4.2.6)

Thus,

\displaystyle\langle j_{\mu}^{IJ}(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{\delta A^{\mu}_{IJ}(x)}Z[A],

(4.2.7)

\displaystyle\langle j_{\mu}^{IJ}(x)j_{\nu}^{KL}(y)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta A^{\mu}_{IJ}(x)\delta A^{\nu}_{KL}(y)}Z[A].

(4.2.8)

Let us introduce an auxiliary field
\sigma_{\mu}
to stand for the current
J_{\mu}

by means of a delta function:

Z[A]=\int{\cal D}\sigma_{\mu}^{IJ}\delta\Big(\sigma_{\mu}^{IJ}-\frac{\delta}{\delta A^{\mu}_{IJ}}\Big)Z[A],

(4.2.9)

and rewrite using Lagrange multiplier
\chi^{\mu}_{IJ}

\displaystyle Z[A]

\displaystyle=\int{\cal D}\sigma_{\mu}^{IJ}\int{\cal D}\chi^{\mu}_{IJ}e^{i\int_{x}\chi^{\mu}_{IJ}(x)\big(\sigma_{\mu}^{IJ}(x)-\frac{\delta}{\delta A^{\mu}_{IJ}(x)}\big)}\int{\cal D}\phi^{I}\int{\cal D}\chi e^{-S_{B}[\phi^{I},\chi,A_{\mu}^{IJ}]}

\displaystyle=\int{\cal D}\sigma_{\mu}^{IJ}\int{\cal D}\chi^{\mu}_{IJ}e^{i\int_{x}\chi^{\mu}_{IJ}(x)\sigma_{\mu}^{IJ}(x)}\int{\cal D}\phi^{I}\int{\cal D}\chi e^{-S_{B}[\phi^{I},\chi,(A^{\mu}_{IJ}-i\chi^{\mu}_{IJ})]}.

(4.2.10)

Note that
\chi^{\mu}_{AB}=-\chi^{\mu}_{BA}
and has the same interaction as the external gauge field
A_{\mu}
. Thus the action has gauge invariance where we can transform
\chi^{\mu}
instead of
A^{\mu}
, along with
\phi^{I}

\sigma_{\mu}^{IJ}

\partial^{\mu}\sigma_{\mu}^{IJ}=0

Shifting

=\int{\cal D}\sigma_{\mu}^{IJ}e^{\int\sigma_{\mu}^{IJ}A^{\mu}_{IJ}}\int{\cal D}\chi^{\mu}_{IJ}e^{i\int_{x}\chi^{\mu}_{IJ}(x)\sigma_{\mu}^{IJ}(x)}\int{\cal D}\phi^{I}\int{\cal D}\chi e^{-S_{B}[\phi^{I},\chi,(-i\chi^{\mu}_{IJ})]}.

(4.2.11)

This can be used to define the action for

(4.2.12)

One can evaluate

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{\mu}^{AB}(x_{1})\sigma_{\nu}^{CD}(x_{2})\rangle_{B}

Using (

The strategy is to define the bulk action in terms of the transverse degrees of freedom. This can then be interpreted as a gauge fixed version of a gauge invariant bulk action in AdS space.

We work out only the quadratic part of the bulk action, (kinetic term), in this paper. This is intended as a proof of the principle that a dynamical graviton emerges out of Exact RG without invoking an AdS/CFT conjecture.

As in the case of the vector current, in the boundary CFT, we keep only the physical degrees of freedom.
The procedure for obtaining an AdS action is again the same as for a scalar field decribed in

The interactions of the bulk gravitational field can in principle be worked out, but we do not attempt this in this paper. Many of the interactions should be determined by requiring manifest general coordinate or diffeomorphism invariance. Understanding these issues is work for the future.

Furthermore, for the CFT at the boundary, we consider the simplest case of a free scalar theory.
The form of the

We start with

Z[h_{\mu\nu}]=\int{\cal D}\phi^{I}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{I}\partial^{\mu}\phi^{I}+\int_{x}h_{\mu\nu}\Theta^{\mu\nu}}.

(7.0.1)

Here,

\Theta_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\phi^{I}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{I}-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\alpha}\phi^{I}\partial^{\alpha}\phi^{I}-\frac{D-2}{4(D-1)}s_{\mu\nu}[\phi^{2}],

(7.0.2)

where

s_{\mu\nu}[\phi^{2}]\equiv(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}-\delta_{\mu\nu}\Box)\phi^{2}

(7.0.3)

is a transverse piece that has been added to make the EM Tensor traceless.

\Theta_{\mu\nu}

\Theta_{\mu\nu}=\frac{D}{4(D-1)}t_{\mu\nu}[\phi^{2}]-\phi t_{\mu\nu}\phi+\frac{D-2}{2D}\delta_{\mu\nu}\phi\Box\phi,

(7.0.4)

t_{\mu\nu}[\phi^{2}]\equiv(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}-\frac{\delta_{\mu\nu}}{D}\Box)\phi^{2}.

(7.0.5)

The last term in
\Theta_{\mu\nu}
is not traceless but vanishes on-shell.

\Theta_{\mu\nu}

\partial^{\mu}\Theta_{\mu\nu}=0.

Therefore, there is an invariance under

\delta h_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}\xi_{\mu},

which allows us to set a gauge condition

\partial^{\mu}h_{\mu\nu}=0.

Thus we can define

(7.0.6)

P_{\mu\nu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}}{\Box}.

It is also traceless because it is a CFT:

\Theta^{\mu}_{\mu}=0.

So, we can choose
h^{\mu}_{\mu}=0

To check this, let us consider the linearized equations for

\displaystyle\Box h-\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\nu}h^{\mu}_{\nu}+\frac{1-D}{z}\partial_{z}h

The first two equations are constraints.

We would like to show that the conditions (

Define a transverse tensor:

(7.3.29)

So,

Acting with

On the boundary,

Thus, one can say that dynamical bulk gravity in AdS space emerges from an ERG equation, (in the large N limit), of a boundary CFT using a well defined mathematical map. Correctness of the result is built into it because it follows from (correctness of) the ERG equation—it does not require an invocation of the AdS/CFT conjecture. This method can be applied to interactions as well, and correctness of the boundary correlations derived from the bulk action is once again guaranteed by the same logic. The details of interactions and their locality properties have to be worked out and this is work for the future. A conceptual issue that needs to be understood is diffeomorphism invariance of the bulk from the point of view of the ERG in the boundary—this is also work for the future.

8
Summary and Conclusions

In the AdS/CFT correspondence, a massless gauge field in the bulk is dual to a conserved vector current in the boundary theory. Simlarly, the metric perturbation is dual to the energy momentum tensor in the boundary theory.
Thus, it is expected that Maxwell equations should describe the RG evolution of the boundary theory when perturbed by a currrent operator, and Einstein equations for metric perturbation describe the RG evolution of the boundary theory when perturbed by the energy momentum tensor.

In this paper, we have derived this using the techniques developed in
[
22
]
—at the linearized level—starting from the Exact RG equation of the
D
dimensional boundary theory, which for us is the O(N) scalar model in three dimensions. This is a three step process. The first step is to write the Polchinksi ERG equation for the Wilson action of the relevant field. The second step is to write down the evolution operator for this equation in the form of a functional integral of a
D+1
dimensional field theory—the extra dimension being the RG scale. The third step is to perform a field redefinition to map this action to AdS space.

On performing these steps one finds
that if
a_{\mu}(z,x)
is the bulk gauge field, and
J_{\mu}(x)
is the boundary current, then, near the boundary
z=0
,

a_{\mu}(z,x)\approx z^{D-2}J_{\mu}(x),

and for the bulk metric perturbation
h_{\mu\nu}(x)
and boundary energy momentum tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
,

h_{\mu\nu}(z,x)\approx z^{D-2}T_{\mu\nu}(x).

Furthermore, the AdS bulk field theory functional integral is the Exact RG evolution operator of the boundary (Wilson) action for the auxiliary fields that stand for the current and energy momentum tensor respectively.

In order to perform these steps, it was found convenient to introduce auxiliary fields for the composite operators, following the idea introduced in
[
34
]
for scalar composites. Thus, a spin one field
\sigma_{\mu}
was introduced for the vector current, and a spin two field
\sigma_{\mu\nu}
was introduced for the energy momentum tensor. A brief introduction to computations using auxiliary fields, (standing for scalar composite operators), and ERG equations for their Wilson actions, has also been given in Section 2 and Section 3.
These techniques should be useful in dealing with composite operators in field theory.

Following these same techniques, Wilson actions were defined for these spin one and spin two auxiliary fields, and ERG equations derived. The ERG equation for auxiliary (composite) field actions has a Polchinski equation form but with additional terms. These additional terms map to interaction terms in the bulk theory. We discussed this for the scalar composite in the free theory. In the case of the conserved currents, the leading term maps, after performing the three steps mentioned above, to a kinetic term—describing linearized metric perturbations for spin
2
and Maxwell equations for spin
1
. The entire computation was done after making a gauge choice. Doing all this while maintaining manifest gauge invariance is an open problem that needs to be addressed.

It is important to point out that these steps follow logically from the ERG of the boundary theory, and do not require the AdS/CFT conjecture for their justification at any point. Thus, one can say that at least at the
linearized
level, one can
derive
actions for gauge fields and gravity in a bulk AdS theory starting from ERG equations of a boundary CFT. This provides some additional insight into the workings of holography.

Interactions terms in the bulk action for scalar fields were discussed in
[
34
]
. This needs to be worked out for gauge fields and metric perturbations. It should be pointed out that because all these follow from the ERG equations, they are guaranteed to give the right boundary correlations. This should be true to any order, without invoking the AdS/CFT conjecture. The real issue that needs to be understood then are the locality properties of the bulk interactions obtained this way. This is work for the future.

Fixed Point Condition

To see the fixed point nature, one has to write the action in terms of dimensionless variables. This changes the time derivative:

\frac{d}{dt}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bigg|_{dimensionless}-G_{dil},

where
G_{dil}
is the contribution to the time derivative due to the powers of
\Lambda
that are there simply for dimensional reasons, i.e., due to the engineering dimensions of the variables, the fields and momenta.

G_{dil}=-N_{J}[J]-N_{\phi}[\phi]-N_{p}.

[J]
is the dimension of the field
J
,
N_{J}
is the number of fields in any term.
N_{p}
is the number of factors of momenta in the coefficient functions in any term. Formally,

N_{p}=\sum_{i}p_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}.

At a fixed point, one expects

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bigg|_{dimensionless}=0.

(B.1.6)

The physical meaning is that, at the fixed point, when the action is written in terms of dimensionless variables with
\Lambda
setting the scale, all
\Lambda
dependence disappears, i.e., the dimensionless couplings do not “run”.

Consider a typical term:

\int_{p_{1}}...\int_{p_{n}}\delta(p_{1}+...+p_{n})\int_{k}\Delta_{h}(p_{1}+k)\Delta_{h}(p_{1}+p_{2}+k)+...+\Delta_{h}(p_{1}+...+p_{n}+k)J(p_{1})...J(p_{n})

\equiv\int_{p_{1}}...\int_{p_{n}}\delta(p_{1}+...+p_{n}){\cal F}_{n}(p_{1},...,p_{n})J(p_{1})...J(p_{n}).

N_{p}
gives
-D
acting on the momentum conserving delta function.
Using

p\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\Delta_{h}(p)=-2\Delta_{h}(p)-2K^{\prime}(p^{2}),

one finds

\sum_{i}p_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}{\cal F}_{n}(p_{1},...,p_{n})=(D-2n){\cal F}_{n}(p_{1},...,p_{n})+\frac{d}{dt}{\cal F}_{n}(p_{1},...,p_{n}).

(B.1.7)

We have used

\dot{\Delta}_{h}=-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{K(p^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}{p^{2}}=+2\Lambda^{2}\frac{d}{d\Lambda^{2}}\frac{K(p^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}{p^{2}}=-2\frac{d}{d\bar{p}^{2}}K(\bar{p}^{2}).

The
D
in (
B.1.7
) cancels the
-D
of momentum delta function.
n=N_{J}
and
[J]=2
.
Thus,

G_{dil}=-N_{J}([J]-2)-\frac{d}{dt}=-\frac{d}{dt}

for the term containing only
J
. The other terms have an extra factor of
\int_{x}J(x)\phi^{2}
which is also dimensionless and do not contribute to
G_{dil}
. Thus, the entire time derivative comes from
G_{dil}
, and thus,
(
B.1.6
) is satisfied, and we have a fixed point action. Of course, since the underlying theory is free, this is expected.

Let us expand this:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}e^{-S_{\Lambda,I}[0,\sigma]}=

\displaystyle-\Big(\frac{1}{i}\frac{N}{2}\int_{x}\frac{2\dot{\Delta}_{h}(0)}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\delta}{\delta\sigma(x)}+\frac{N}{2}\int_{x}\int_{y}\frac{2\dot{\Delta}_{h}(x-y)}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{2\Delta_{h}(x-y)}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta\sigma(x)\delta\sigma(y)}

Linear Term:

The linear term is unpleasant. Its origin is in the tadpole diagram or self-contraction of
the
\phi

\phi^{2}(x)=:\phi^{2}(x):+\frac{1}{i}\sqrt{N}\Delta_{h}(0).

The

stands for normal ordering. Below, we show that by redefining

). Let us write

(B.2.13)

Since
\sigma
has no

\frac{d\sigma^{\prime}}{dt}=-\frac{1}{i}\sqrt{N}\dot{\Delta}_{h}(0).

(B.2.14)

We have also defined earlier

(B.2.15)

We see that, in addition to the explicit dependence on

\Lambda
dependence inside
\sigma^{\prime}
.
Thus,

\frac{dS_{\Lambda}[\sigma]}{dt}=\frac{dS_{\Lambda}^{\prime}[\sigma^{\prime}]}{dt}=\frac{\partial S_{\Lambda}^{\prime}[\sigma^{\prime}]}{\partial t}-\frac{1}{i}\sqrt{N}\dot{\Delta}_{h}(0)\int_{x}\frac{\delta S^{\prime}[\sigma^{\prime}]}{\delta\sigma^{\prime}(x)}.

Substituting (

), we see that the linear derivative term cancels out, and we are left with a Polchinski type ERG equation.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}e^{-S_{\Lambda,I}[\sigma]}=

\displaystyle\Big(-\int_{x}\int_{y}\frac{d(\Delta_{h}(x-y))^{2}}{dt}\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta\sigma(x)\delta\sigma(y)}

(B.2.17)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{i}\frac{4}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{x,y,z}\dot{\Delta}_{h}(x-y)\Delta_{h}(x-z)\Delta_{h}(z-y)\frac{\delta^{3}}{\delta\sigma(x)\delta\sigma(y)\delta\sigma(z)}+....\Big)e^{-S_{\Lambda,I}[\sigma]}.

Hereafter, we work with
\sigma^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime}[\sigma^{\prime}]
, drop the linear term in (
B.2.11
), and also drop the primes for notational simplicity.

Quadratic Term:

The second term is of the type in Polchinski ERG equation. Its coefficient is the time derivative of the
\sigma
field propagator and is of
O(1)
thanks to our choice of normalization for the
\phi
kinetic term. The propagator for
\sigma
is the
\phi^{2}
two point correlation, which is proportional to
\Delta_{h}(x-y)^{2}
. If
\sigma
had been an elementary field (like
\phi
) the equation would have ended there. But,
\sigma
stands for a composite field and one expects Polchinski equation to be modified. The higher order correction terms are proportional to powers of
1/\sqrt{N}
.

Cubic Term:

The cubic term is also unpleasant. However, one can evaluate this to leading order in
1/N
by acting on the
O(1)
of
S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]
,
which can be obtained as follows.
The quadratic
\chi
term in
S_{\Lambda,I}[0,\chi]
is the
O(1)
kinetic term

-\int_{x}\int_{y}\chi(x)\Delta_{h}(x-y)^{2}\chi(y).

Doing the Gaussian
\chi
integral gives for the quadratic
\sigma
the
O(1)
term

-\frac{1}{4}\int_{x}\int_{y}\sigma(x)\Delta_{h}^{-2}(x-y)\sigma(y).

(B.2.18)

Acting with the cubic in derivatives term, we obtain

-\frac{4}{\sqrt{N}}.\frac{1}{8}\int_{x,y,z}\int_{u,v,w}\dot{\Delta}_{h}(u-v)\Delta_{h}(u-w)\Delta_{h}(w-v)\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}(u-x)}\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}(v-y)}\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}(w-z)}\sigma(x)\sigma(y)\sigma(z)

(B.2.19)

where

f(x,y,z)\equiv\int_{u,v,w}\dot{\Delta}_{h}(u-v)\Delta_{h}(u-w)\Delta_{h}(w-v)\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}(u-x)}\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}(v-y)}\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}(w-z)}.

(B.2.20)

This is a modification of the ERG equation—if the leading term of Polchinski’s ERG equation is like a free particle Hamiltonian, this one is like a potential term in a Hamiltonian.

\int_{x}\chi(x)\phi_{h}^{I}(x)\phi_{h}^{I}(x)=\int_{x}\int_{y}\phi_{h}^{I}(x)\chi(x)\delta(x-y)\phi_{h}^{I}(y);

\displaystyle\sqrt{N}\int_{x}\chi^{\mu}_{IJ}(x)\phi_{h}^{I}(x)\overleftrightarrow{\partial_{\mu}}\phi_{h}^{J}(x)

Appendix D
Constraints on
f
and the analyticity of the boundary term

In this appendix, we verify that the field redefinition we have done to obtain the AdS kinetic term is not illegal, i.e., the function
1/f
involved in the redefinition has to be analytic everywhere. Since the function is expressed in terms of Bessel functions, we need only check for analyticity at the boundary, as
z\to 0
. In (
6.0.1
), we are left with a boundary term after redefining the field. We show here that the integrand of the boundary term in the action is analytic. As long as the term is analytic in
p
, we can remove it from the action by adding counter-terms.

As written in Section
6
and
[
22
]
,

1/f=A(p)z^{\frac{D}{2}}K_{\nu}(pz)+B(p)z^{\frac{D}{2}}I_{\nu}(pz),

(D.0.1)

with
\nu^{2}=m^{2}+\frac{D^{2}}{4}
, and
A(p)
and
B(p)
being arbitrary functions that we can tune to make
1/f
analytic at
z\to 0
, and to get the desired boundary conditions for the Green function. In
[
34
]
, these were chosen to be
A(p)=p^{\nu}
and
B(p)=-\frac{1}{\gamma}p^{\nu}
for a positive
\nu
. To ensure that Green function boundary behaviour is as desired, we just need to have
A(p)B(p)\sim p^{-2|\nu|}
.
The modified Bessel functions
I_{\nu}(x)
and
K_{\nu}(x)
have the following expansions in
x
.

\displaystyle I_{\nu}(x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\Gamma(k+\nu+1)k!}\Big(\frac{x}{2}\Big)^{2k+\nu},

(D.0.2)

\displaystyle K_{\nu}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big[\Gamma(\nu)\Big(\frac{x}{2}\Big)^{-\nu}\Big(1+\frac{x^{2}}{4(1-\nu)}+\ldots\Big)+\Gamma(-\nu)\Big(\frac{x}{2}\Big)^{\nu}\Big(1+\frac{x^{2}}{4(1+\nu)}+\ldots\Big)\Big],~\nu\notin\mathbb{Z},

(D.0.3)

\displaystyle K_{0}(x)=

\displaystyle\Big(-\gamma+\frac{1}{4}(1-\gamma)x^{2}+\frac{1}{128}(3-2\gamma)x^{4}+\ldots\Big)-\log\Big(\frac{x}{2}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{x^{2}}{4}+\frac{x^{4}}{64}+\ldots\Big),

(D.0.4)

\displaystyle K_{1}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}+\frac{x}{4}\Big(2\gamma-\frac{1}{8}\Big(2\gamma-\frac{5}{2}\Big)x^{2}+\ldots\Big)+\frac{x}{2}\log\Big(\frac{x}{2}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{x^{2}}{8}+\ldots\Big),

(D.0.5)

\displaystyle K_{n}(x)=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}\log\Big(\frac{x}{2}\Big)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x/2)^{2k}}{k!(k+n)!}+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{x}{2}\Big)^{-n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}(n-k-1)!}{k!}\Big(\frac{x}{2}\Big)^{2k}

\displaystyle+\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2}\Big(\frac{x}{2}\Big)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\psi(k+1)+\psi(k+n+1)}{k!(k+n)!}\Big(\frac{x}{2}\Big)^{2k},~n\in\mathbb{N},\text{ with }

(D.0.6)

\displaystyle K_{n}(x)=

\displaystyle K_{-n}(x).

(D.0.7)

Using these, we can write the expansion for
1/f
.

•

For
\nu\notin\mathbb{Z}
and
\nu<-1

\displaystyle 1/f=

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\Gamma(-\nu)A(p)+\frac{B(p)}{\Gamma(1+\nu)}\bigg)2^{-\nu}z^{D/2+\nu}p^{\nu}

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma(-\nu)A(p)}{4(1+\nu)}+\frac{B(p)}{\Gamma(2+\nu)}\bigg)2^{2+\nu}z^{D/2+2+\nu}p^{2+\nu}+O(p^{\min{\{-2\nu,4\}}}),

where the order of the rest of the terms is expressed assuming
A(p),B(p)\sim p^{-\nu}
, for which choice, the divergent terms from
1/f
are removed.

•

For
\nu=-1
,

1/f=\bigg(A(p)+\frac{2B(p)}{\Gamma(0)}\bigg)z^{D/2-1}\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}A(p)z^{D/2+1}p\log{p}+O(p^{2}),

taking
A(p),B(p)\sim p
. The
\log

•

For
-1<\nu<0

1/f=\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\Gamma(-\nu)A(p)+\frac{B(p)}{\Gamma(1+\nu)}\bigg)2^{-\nu}z^{D/2+\nu}p^{\nu}+2^{-1+\nu}z^{D/2-\nu}\Gamma{(\nu)}A(p)p^{-\nu}+O(p^{2}),

again taking
A(p),B(p)\sim p^{-\nu}

•

For
\nu=0

1/f=-z^{D/2}A(p)\log p-z^{D/2}(A(p)(\gamma+\log{(z/2)})+B(p))+O(p^{2}\log p),

where we must take
A(p),B(p)\sim p^{0}
. This case is impossibly nonanalytic.

•

For
0<\nu<1
,

1/f=\Gamma(\nu)A(p)2^{\nu-1}z^{D/2-\nu}p^{-\nu}+\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\Gamma(-\nu)A(p)+\frac{B(p)}{\Gamma(1+\nu)}\bigg)2^{-\nu}z^{D/2+\nu}p^{\nu}+O(p^{2}),

with
A(p),B(p)\sim p^{\nu}
.

•

For
\nu=1
,

1/f=A(p)z^{D/2-1}\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}A(p)z^{D/2+1}p\log{p}+O(p^{2}),

and we take
A(p)\sim p
to make the first term analytic.

•

For
\nu\notin\mathbb{Z}
and
\nu>1
,

1/f=\Gamma(\nu)A(p)2^{\nu-1}z^{D/2-\nu}p^{-\nu}+\frac{\Gamma(\nu)A(p)}{1+\nu}2^{\nu-1}z^{D/2+2-\nu}p^{2-\nu}+O(p^{\min{\{2\nu,4\}}}),

with
A(p),B(p)\sim p^{\nu}
.

Thus, we will not be able to do field redefinitions for any integer values of
\nu
, (which is the case for
D=4
). But since there are powers of
p^{2\nu}
for non-integer values of
\nu
, for half-integer
\nu
,
1/f
is analytic. This is the case in
D=3
for the scalar, vector and tensor, where
\nu=\pm 1/2,\pm 1/2,\pm 3/2
, respectively. Other non-integer values of
\nu
correspond to non-integer dimensions, where we anyway do not expect the Green function behaviour to be analytic.

Now, let us look at the boundary term resulting from redefining the field. From (
6.0.1
), (see also sec 2.4.3 of
[
22
]
), the integrand in the boundary term of the action has the following dependence on
f
.

\frac{\partial}{\partial z}{\log f}=-f\frac{\partial}{\partial z}{\frac{1}{f}}.

(D.0.8)

The derivatives of the Bessel functions are

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}I_{\nu}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(I_{\nu-1}(x)+I_{\nu+1}(x)),

(D.0.9)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}K_{\nu}(x)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(K_{\nu-1}(x)+K_{\nu+1}(x)).

(D.0.10)

Thus,

\displaystyle f\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\frac{1}{f}

\displaystyle=\frac{D}{2z}-\frac{pA(p)(K_{\nu-1}(pz)+K_{\nu+1}(pz))-pB(p)(I_{\nu-1}(pz)+I_{\nu+1}(pz)}{2(A(p)K_{\nu}(pz)+B(p)I_{\nu}(pz))}.

(D.0.11)

The behaviour of this quantity as
z\to 0
needs to be examined case by case.

•

\boldsymbol{\nu>1,\nu\notin\mathbb{Z}:}

\lim_{z\to 0}f\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\frac{1}{f}=\frac{D-2\nu}{2z}-\Big(\frac{1}{2^{2-\nu}(\nu-1)}-\frac{1}{8}\Big)zp^{2}+O(p^{2\nu}).

While the leading and subleading terms are analytic, there are powers of the form
p^{2\nu}
. Therefore, the boundary term is analytic for half-integer
\nu
.

•

\boldsymbol{\nu=1:}

\lim_{z\to 0}f\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\frac{1}{f}=\frac{D-2}{2z}+zp^{2}\log p+O(p^{2}).

With the log terms, this is nonanalytic. We cannot redefine fields for which
\nu=1
.

•

\boldsymbol{0<\nu<1:}

\lim_{z\to 0}f\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\frac{1}{f}=\frac{D-2\nu}{2z}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{2}{z}\bigg)^{1-2\nu}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\nu}+\frac{2B(p)}{\Gamma(\nu)^{2}A(p)}\bigg)p^{2\nu}+O(p^{2}).

This is analytic for
\nu=1/2
.

•

\boldsymbol{\nu=0:}

\lim_{z\to 0}f\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\frac{1}{f}=\frac{D}{2z}-\frac{1}{z}\bigg(1-\frac{B(p)}{A(p)\Gamma(0)}\bigg)\frac{1}{\log p}+O(p^{2}),

which is not analytic.

•

\boldsymbol{-1<\nu<0:}

\lim_{z\to 0}f\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\frac{1}{f}=\frac{D+2\nu}{2z}-\frac{A(p)\Gamma(1+\nu)}{2A(p)\Gamma(-\nu)+4B(p)/\Gamma(1+\nu)}\bigg(\frac{2}{z}\bigg)^{1+2\nu}p^{-2\nu}+O(p^{2}).

This is analytic for
p=-1/2
.

•

\boldsymbol{\nu=-1:}

\lim_{z\to 0}f\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\frac{1}{f}=\frac{D-2}{2z}+\frac{1}{A(p)+2B(p)/\Gamma(0)}zp^{2}\log p+O(p^{2}).

With the log terms, this is nonanalytic. We cannot redefine fields for which
\nu=-1
.

•

\boldsymbol{\nu<-1,\nu\notin\mathbb{Z}:}

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to 0}f\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\frac{1}{f}=

\displaystyle\frac{D+2\nu}{2z}-\frac{z}{4}\frac{A(p)\Big(\frac{\Gamma(-\nu+1)}{4\nu}+\Gamma(-\nu-1)\Big)-2B(p)\Big(\frac{1}{\Gamma(2+\nu)}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\nu)}\Big)}{A(p)\Gamma(-\nu)+2B(p)/\Gamma(1+\nu)}p^{2}

\displaystyle+O(p^{-2\nu}).

Again, this is analytic for half-integer
\nu
.

As described in section
7.5
, the values of
\nu
we need for redefining the scalar composite, the vector, and the tensor are
\pm(-D/2+2),\pm(-D/2+1),\pm D/2
respectively.
\therefore
, for
D=3
, we can redefine all three fields without a problem, but for
D=4
, the operators requires
\nu=0,\pm 1
, and we will not be able to redefine it. But, as the quartic theory itself is not defined in
D=4
, we need not worry about that case.

Appendix E
ERG equation for Tensor composite

The details of the derivation of the ERG equation in the tensor case dealt with in Section
7
are given below.

We start with:

e^{-S_{\Lambda}[\sigma_{\mu\nu},\phi_{l}^{I}]}=\int{\cal D}\chi_{\mu\nu}e^{i\int_{x}\chi_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}}\int{\cal D}\phi_{h}^{I}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\phi_{h}^{I}(p)\Delta_{h}^{-1}(p^{2})\phi_{h}^{I}(-p)-i\int_{p}\chi_{\mu\nu}(p)\Theta^{\mu\nu}[\phi_{l}^{I}+\phi_{h}^{I}](-p)}.

(E.0.1)

Note that with
\chi_{\mu\nu}
being transverse and traceless, the terms
\int_{x}\chi_{\mu\nu}t^{\mu\nu}[\phi^{2}]
and
\int_{x}\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\delta^{\mu\nu}\phi\Box\phi(x)
drop out. Therefore, we obtain:

\int_{x}\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\Theta^{\mu\nu}[\phi_{l}^{I}+\phi_{h}^{I}]=-\int_{x}\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\phi_{l}^{I}\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\phi_{l}^{I}-\int_{x}\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\phi_{h}^{I}\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\phi_{h}^{I}-\int_{x}\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)(2\phi_{h}^{I}\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\phi_{l}^{I}).

(E.0.2)

Quadratic in
\phi_{h}

Let us collect the terms in the action that are quadratic in
\phi_{h}
:

\int_{x}\int_{y}\{\phi_{h}^{I}(x)\frac{1}{2}\underbrace{[\Delta_{h}^{-1}(x,y)-2i\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\delta(x-y)]}_{O^{-1}(x,y)}\phi_{h}^{I}(y).

(E.0.3)

Linear in
\phi_{h}

-2i\int_{x}\int_{y}\phi_{l}^{I}(x)\chi_{\mu\nu}\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\delta(x-y)\phi_{h}^{I}(y)\equiv-i\int_{y}j^{I}(y)\phi_{h}^{I}(y),

(E.0.4)

where

j_{l}(y)=2\int_{x}\phi_{l}(x)\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\delta(x-y)=2\chi_{\mu\nu}(y)\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\phi_{l}(y).

(E.0.5)

We have used the transverality of
\chi_{\mu\nu}
, (
\partial^{\mu}\chi_{\mu\nu}=0
), in the last equation.

Thus,

\Psi\equiv e^{-S_{\Lambda}[\sigma_{\mu\nu},\phi_{l}^{I}]}=\int{\cal D}\chi_{\mu\nu}e^{i\int\chi_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}+i\int\chi_{\mu\nu}\phi_{l}^{I}\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\phi_{l}^{I}}\int{\cal D}\phi_{h}^{I}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\int_{y}\phi_{h}^{I}O^{-1}(x,y)\phi_{h}^{I}+i\int j^{I}\phi_{h}^{I}}

(E.0.6)

=\int{\cal D}\chi_{\mu\nu}e^{i\int\chi_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}+i\int\chi_{\mu\nu}\phi_{l}^{I}\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\phi_{l}^{I}}Det^{-\frac{N}{2}}[O(x,y)]e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\int_{y}j^{I}(x)O(x,y)j^{I}(y)}.

(E.0.7)

\Psi
obeys Polchisnki’s equation. This is easy to see.
Before integrating over
\chi_{\mu\nu},\sigma^{\mu\nu}
, one can think of these fields as fixed background fields interacting with the
\phi^{I}
fields.
Thus, consider

\bar{\Psi}=\int{\cal D}\phi_{h}^{I}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\phi_{h}^{I}(p)\Delta_{h}^{-1}(p^{2})\phi_{h}^{I}(-p)-i\int_{p}\chi_{\mu\nu}(p)\Theta^{\mu\nu}[\phi_{l}^{I}+\phi_{h}^{I}](-p)}.

\bar{\Psi}
obeys

\frac{\partial\bar{\Psi}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x,y}\dot{\Delta}_{h}(x-y)\frac{\delta^{2}\bar{\Psi}}{\delta\phi_{l}^{I}(x)\delta\phi_{l}^{I}(y)}.

Multiply both sides by
e^{\int\chi_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}}
, and then integrate over
\chi_{\mu\nu}
. One obtains

\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x,y}\dot{\Delta}_{h}(x-y)\frac{\delta^{2}\Psi}{\delta\phi_{l}^{I}(x)\delta\phi_{l}^{I}(y)}.

(E.0.8)

We evaluate the functional derivatives, and obtain the time evolution of
S_{\Lambda}[\sigma^{\mu\nu},\phi_{l}^{I}]
. In particular, we can set
\phi_{l}^{I}=0
, and obtain the evolution of
S_{\Lambda}[\sigma^{\mu\nu},\phi_{l}^{I}=0]\equiv S_{\Lambda}[\sigma^{\mu\nu}]
. This is a theory with an IR cutoff
\Lambda
. The infinite volume physics is recovered when
\Lambda\to 0
. In practice, we obtain a functional differential equation for
S_{\Lambda}[\sigma^{\mu\nu}]
that can be solved in perturbation theory.

Let us separate out the terms in
\Psi
that depend on
\phi_{l}^{I}
.

\Psi^{\prime}=e^{i\int_{u}\chi_{\mu\nu}(u)\phi_{l}^{I}(u)\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\phi_{l}^{I}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{u}\int_{v}j^{I}(u)O(u,v)j^{I}(v)}.

\frac{\delta\Psi^{\prime}}{\delta\phi_{l}^{I}(x)}=[2i\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\phi_{l}^{I}(x)-2\int_{u}\int_{v}\chi_{\mu\nu}(u)\partial_{u}^{\mu}\partial_{u}^{\nu}\delta(u-x)O(u,v)j^{I}(v)]\Psi^{\prime};

\frac{\delta^{2}\Psi^{\prime}}{\delta\phi_{l}^{I}(x)\delta\phi_{l}^{I}(y)}\bigg|_{\phi_{l}=0}=[2i\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\delta(x-y)-4\int_{u}\int_{v}\chi_{\mu\nu}(u)\partial_{u}^{\mu}\partial_{u}^{\nu}\delta(u-x)O(u,v)\chi_{\rho\sigma}(v)\partial_{v}^{\rho}\partial_{v}^{\sigma}\delta(v-y)]\Psi^{\prime}.

We have dropped terms that vanish when
\phi_{l}^{I}=0
.

\frac{\delta^{2}\Psi^{\prime}}{\delta\phi_{l}^{I}(x)\delta\phi_{l}^{I}(y)}=[2i\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\delta(x-y)-4\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\partial_{y}^{\rho}\partial_{y}^{\sigma}O(x,v)\chi_{\rho\sigma}(y)]\Psi^{\prime},

(E.0.9)

from which we obtain

\int_{x}\int_{y}\frac{1}{2}\dot{\Delta}_{h}(x-y)\frac{\delta^{2}\Psi}{\delta\phi_{l}^{I}(x)\delta\phi_{l}^{I}(y)}|_{\phi_{l}=0}=\int{\cal D}\chi_{\mu\nu}e^{i\int\chi_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}}\times

\int_{x}\int_{y}\frac{1}{2}\dot{\Delta}_{h}(x-y)[2i\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\delta(x-y)-4\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\partial_{y}^{\rho}\partial_{y}^{\sigma}O(x,v)\chi_{\rho\sigma}(y)]Det^{-\frac{N}{2}}[O(x,y)]\bigg|_{\phi_{l}=0}.

(E.0.10)

The first term in the RH above is of the form

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\dot{\Delta}_{h}(x-y)|_{x=y}\int_{x}\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\approx\delta^{\mu\nu}\int_{x}\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\approx 0

because
\chi^{\mu}_{\mu}=0
.

In the second term,
O(x,y)=\Delta_{h}(x-y)+O(\chi_{\mu\nu})
.
The leading term is thus

\int{\cal D}\chi_{\mu\nu}e^{i\int\chi_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}}\int_{x}\int_{y}2\dot{\Delta}_{h}(x-y)\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\partial_{y}^{\rho}\partial_{y}^{\sigma}\Delta_{h}(x-y)\chi_{\rho\sigma}(y)Det^{-\frac{N}{2}}[O(x,y)]

=-\int_{x}\int_{y}2\dot{\Delta}_{h}(x-y)\chi_{\mu\nu}(x)\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\partial_{y}^{\rho}\partial_{y}^{\sigma}\Delta_{h}(x-y)\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta\sigma^{\mu\nu}(x)\delta\sigma^{\rho\sigma}(y)}\int{\cal D}\chi_{\mu\nu}e^{i\int\chi_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}}Det^{-\frac{N}{2}}[O(x,y)].

At lowest order, we get

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}e^{-S_{\Lambda}[\sigma_{\mu\nu}]}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\int_{y}4\dot{\Delta}_{h}(x-y)\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{x}^{\nu}\partial_{y}^{\rho}\partial_{y}^{\sigma}\Delta_{h}(x-y)\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta\sigma^{\mu\nu}(x)\delta\sigma^{\rho\sigma}(y)}e^{-S_{\Lambda}[\sigma^{\mu\nu}]},

(E.0.11)

from which we get (
7.2.14
).

Appendix F
\Theta_{\mu\nu}
Two Point Function

We give the two point function of the energy momentum tensor in free field theory.

F.1
Position Space

The improved traceless, (traceless on-shell), EM tensor is

\Theta_{\mu\nu}=\frac{D}{4(D-1)}t_{\mu\nu}[\phi^{2}]-\phi t_{\mu\nu}\phi+\frac{D-2}{2D}\delta_{\mu\nu}\phi\Box\phi.

(F.1.1)

Define

\langle\phi(x)\phi(y)\rangle=G(x-y);

(F.1.2)

G(X)=\frac{c}{(X^{2})^{\frac{D}{2}-1}},

(F.1.3)

where
X^{\mu}=(x-y)^{\mu}
, and
c=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{D}{2})}{2\pi^{\frac{D}{2}}(D-2)}=\frac{1}{S^{D-1}(D-2)}
.
S^{n}
denotes the volume of the unit
n
-sphere.

Note that
\Box G(X)=\delta^{D}(X)
.

The result is:

\displaystyle\langle\Theta_{\mu\nu}(x)\Theta_{\rho\sigma}(y)\rangle=

\displaystyle c^{2}\frac{(D-2)^{2}}{4(D-1)}\bigg\{-2\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{\rho\sigma}+D[\delta_{\mu\sigma}\delta_{\nu\rho}+\delta_{\mu\rho}\delta_{\nu\sigma}]

\displaystyle-2D\frac{[\delta_{\mu\rho}X_{\nu}X_{\sigma}+\delta_{\mu\sigma}X_{\nu}X_{\rho}+\delta_{\nu\rho}X_{\mu}X_{\sigma}+\delta_{\nu\sigma}X_{\mu}X_{\rho}]}{X^{2}}

\displaystyle+8\frac{X_{\mu}X_{\nu}X_{\rho}X_{\sigma}}{(X^{2})^{2}}\bigg\}\times(X^{2})^{-D}.

(F.1.4)

It can be checked that it is traceless (on shell) in
\mu,\nu
and
\rho,\sigma
, and also transverse in each index.

F.2
Momentum Space

\displaystyle\langle\Theta_{\mu\nu}(q)~~\Theta_{\rho\sigma}(-q)\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{D(D-1)}\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{\rho\sigma}(q^{2})^{\frac{D}{2}}-\frac{1}{D}[\delta_{\mu\rho}\delta_{\nu\sigma}+\delta_{\mu\sigma}\delta_{\nu\rho}](q^{2})^{\frac{D}{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{2(D-2)}{D(D-1)}q_{\mu}q_{\nu}q_{\rho}q_{\sigma}(q^{2})^{\frac{D}{2}-2}-\frac{2}{D(D-1)}[q_{\mu}q_{\nu}\delta_{\rho\sigma}+q_{\rho}q_{\sigma}\delta_{\mu\nu}](q^{2})^{\frac{D}{2}-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{D}[\delta_{\mu\rho}q_{\nu}q_{\sigma}+3~perm](q^{2})^{\frac{D}{2}-1}\times\gamma,

(F.2.5)

where

\gamma
is

\gamma=\frac{\Gamma(1-\frac{D}{2})}{(4\pi)^{\frac{D}{2}}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{D}{2}+1)^{2}}{\Gamma(D+2)}.

We have already seen that the expression is traceless. One can also check explicitly that it is transverse on each index. It is also possible to write it in a manifestly transverse form:

\displaystyle\langle\Theta_{\mu\nu}(q)~~\Theta_{\rho\sigma}(-q)\rangle=

\displaystyle\gamma\Big\{\frac{2}{D(D-1)}[(\delta_{\mu\nu}q^{2}-q_{\mu}q_{\nu})(\delta_{\rho\sigma}q^{2}-q_{\rho}q_{\sigma})]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{D}[(\delta_{\mu\rho}q^{2}-q_{\mu}q_{\rho})(\delta_{\nu\sigma}q^{2}-q_{\nu}q_{\sigma})]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{D}[(\delta_{\mu\sigma}q^{2}-q_{\mu}q_{\sigma})(\delta_{\nu\rho}q^{2}-q_{\nu}q_{\rho})]\Big\}.

(F.2.6)

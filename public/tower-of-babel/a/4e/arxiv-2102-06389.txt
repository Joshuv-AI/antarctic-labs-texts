Infrared spectroscopy of endohedral H 2 ​ O \mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O} in C 60 \mathrm{C}_{60} .
A. Shugai, U. Nagel, Y. Murata, Yongjun Li, S. Mamone, A. Krachmalnicoff, S. Alom, R. J. Whitby, M. H. Levitt, T. Rõõm

Abstract
Infrared absorption spectroscopy study of endohedral water molecule in a solid mixture of H 2 ​ O ​ @ ​ C 60 \mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}@\mathrm{C}_{60} and C 60 \mathrm{C}_{60} was carried out at liquid helium temperature. From the evolution of the spectra during the ortho - para conversion process, the spectral lines were identified as para - and ortho - H 2 ​ O \mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O} transitions. Eight vibrational transitions with rotational side peaks were observed in the mid-infrared: ω 1 \omega_{1} , ω 2 \omega_{2} , ω 3 \omega_{3} , 2 ​ ω 1 2\omega_{1} , 2 ​ ω 2 2\omega_{2} , ω 1 + ω 3 \omega_{1}+\omega_{3} , ω 2 + ω 3 \omega_{2}+\omega_{3} , and 2 ​ ω 2 + ω 3 2\omega_{2}+\omega_{3} . The vibrational frequencies ω 2 \omega_{2} and 2 ω 2 \omega_{2} are lower by 1.6% and the rest by 2.4%, as compared to free H 2 ​ O \mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O} . A model consisting of a rovibrational Hamiltonian with the dipole and quadrupole moments of H 2 ​ O \mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O} interacting with the crystal field was used to fit the infrared absorption spectra. The electric quadrupole interaction with the crystal field lifts the degeneracy of the rotational levels. The finite amplitudes of the pure v 1 v_{1} and v 2 v_{2} vibrational transitions are consistent with the interaction of the water molecule dipole moment with a lattice-induced electric field. The permanent dipole moment of encapsulated H 2 ​ O \mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O} is found to be 0.5 ± 0.1 0.5\pm 0.1 D as determined from the far-infrared rotational line intensities. The translational mode of the quantized center of mass motion of H 2 ​ O \mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O} in the molecular cage of C 60 \mathrm{C}_{60} was observed at 110 cm − 1 \,\mathrm{cm}^{-1} (13.6 meV).

Introduction

Endohedral fullerenes consist of atoms or molecules fully encapsulated in closed carbon cages.
The remarkable synthetic route known as “molecular surgery" has led to the synthesis of atomic endofullerenes He@C
60

60

60

;
Ge et al. 2011a
;
Krachmalnicoff, Levitt, and Whitby 2014

It is well established that these endohedral molecules do not form chemical bonds with the carbon cage and rotate freely

;
Mamone et al. 2009
;
Beduz et al. 2012
.
The rotation is further facilitated by the nearly spherical symmetry of the
\mathrm{C}_{60}
cage.
The thermal and chemical stability of A@C
60
opens up the unique possibility of studying the dynamics and the interactions of a small molecule with carbon nano-surfaces.
The trapping potential of dihydrogen H
2
has been described with high accuracy using infrared (IR) spectroscopy
Ge et al. 2011b
;
Ge et al. 2011a
, inelastic neutron scattering (INS)
Horsewill et al. 2012
and theoretical calculations
Xu et al. 2009
.
The non-spherical shape of the small molecule and the quantized translational motion of its center of mass leads to coupled rotational and translational dynamics

;
Mamone et al. 2009
;
Felker and Bačić 2016
.

The water-endofullerene
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}@\mathrm{C}_{60}
is of particular interest. The encapsulated water molecule possesses rich spatial quantum dynamics. It is an asymmetric-top rotor, supports three vibrational modes, displays nuclear spin isomerism (
para
- and
ortho
-water) and has an electric dipole and quadrupole moment.

Low-temperature dielectric measurements

\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}@\mathrm{C}_{60}
show that the electric dipole moment of the encapsulated water is reduced to
0.51\pm 0.05
D from the free water value 1.85 D.
The
\mathrm{C}_{60}
carbon cage responds to the endohedral water molecule with a counteracting induced dipole, resulting in the lower total dipole moment

.

The dynamics of isolated or encapsulated single water molecules have been studied before in other environments, such as noble gas matrices, solid hydrogen and liquid helium droplets. Although the trapping sites in these matrices have high symmetry and allow water rotation, these systems exist only at low temperature

Fajardo, Tam, and DeRose 2004

Kuyanov, Slipchenko, and Vilesov 2006

Water has also been studied in crystalline environments with nano-size cavities.

;
Zhukova et al. 2014
;
Kolesnikov et al. 2016
;
Belyanchikov et al. 2020
.

Several spectroscopic techniques have been used to study
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}@\mathrm{C}_{60}

;
Mamone et al. 2011
;
Meier et al. 2018
;
Kurotobi and Murata 2011

The low-lying rotational states of the encapsulated molecule are found to be very similar to those of an isolated water molecule, with the notable exception of a 0.6 meV splitting in the

This indicates that the local environment of the water molecule in

The splitting has been attributed to the interaction between the electric quadrupole moment of

The merohedral disorder present in solid

There is also evidence from dielectric measurements that merohedral disorder leads to electric dipolar activity in solid

Confined molecules exhibit quantization of their translational motion (“particle in a box"), in addition to their quantized rotational and vibrational modes.
Quantized translational modes have been observed at 60 to 70

However, comparatively little is known about the centre-of-mass translational mode of

The fundamental frequency of the water translation mode in

The energy level separation between the ground

The full thermal equilibration of

Cohen-Tannoudji, Diu, and Laloë 2005

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=-2}^{2}(-1)^{m}V^{(2)}_{-m}Q^{C}_{m},

and
\{Q^{C}_{m}\}

The experimental value of the

\displaystyle\{Q_{m}^{M}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\frac{1}{2}(Q_{xx}-Q_{yy}),0,\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}Q_{zz},0,\frac{1}{2}(Q_{xx}-Q_{yy})\}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle Q_{zz}\{\frac{1}{2},0,\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}},0,\frac{1}{2}\},\quad m=-2,\ldots,+2.

The site symmetry of the

The spherical tensor component in the frame

After the transformation of the quadrupole moment (

\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{Q}}Q_{zz}\left[\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\left[D^{2}_{00}({\Omega_{C\rightarrow M}})\right]^{*}\right.

(14)

\displaystyle+\left.\frac{1}{2}\left[D^{2}_{0,-2}({\Omega_{C\rightarrow M}})+D^{2}_{02}({\Omega_{C\rightarrow M}})\right]^{*}\right].

Dipolar interaction.

Dielectric measurements of solid
\mathrm{C}_{60}
have provided evidence for the existence of electric dipoles in solid
\mathrm{C}_{60}

We assume these electric dipoles can be source of an electric field in the

Consider a crystal electric field
\mathbf{\mathcal{E}}
with spherical coordinates
\{\mathcal{E},\phi_{E},\theta_{E}\}

The interaction of the electric dipole moment with the electric field is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ed}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{m=-1}^{1}(-1)^{m}\mathcal{E}^{E}_{-m}\mu^{E}_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{m^{\prime}=-1}^{1}\mathcal{E}\left[D^{1}_{0m^{\prime}}({\Omega_{E\rightarrow C}})\right]^{*}

where the dipole moment in the molecule-fixed frame

, and

III.1.4
Confined water translations: spherical oscillator

The translational motion is the center of mass motion and is quantized for a confined molecule.
The high icosahedral symmetry of the
\mathrm{C}_{60}
cavity is close to spherical symmetry and
therefore the translational motion of trapped molecule can be described by the three-dimensional isotropic spherical oscillator model
Shaffer 1944
.
For simplicity we write the potential in the harmonic approximation
Cohen-Tannoudji, Diu, and Laloë 2005
:

V(R)=V_{2}R^{2},

(17)

where
R
is the displacement of
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
center of mass from the
\mathrm{C}_{60}
cage center.
The
\mathrm{C}_{60}
cage is assumed rigid and its center of mass is fixed.

The frequency of the spherical harmonic oscillator is:

\omega^{0}_{\mathrm{t}}=\sqrt{2V_{2}/m},

(18)

where
m
is the mass of a molecule moving in the potential and
[\omega^{0}_{\mathrm{t}}]=\mathrm{rad}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}
.
The energy of spherical harmonic oscillator is quantized,

E_{N}=\hbar\omega^{0}_{\mathrm{t}}(N+\frac{3}{2}),

(19)

where
N
is the translational quantum number,
N\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
.
The orbital quantum number
L
takes values
L=N,N-2,\ldots 1(0)
for
N
odd (even).
Energy of the harmonic spherical oscillator does not depend on
L
and in isotropic approximation there is an additional degeneracy of each
E_{N}
level in quantum number
M_{L}
, taking
2L+1
values,
M_{L}\in\{-L,-L+1,\ldots L\}
.

III.1.5
Nuclear spin isomers:
para
and
ortho
water

The Pauli principle requires that the total quantum state is antisymmetric with respect to exchange of the two protons in water, which as spin-1/2 particles are fermions.
This constraint leads to the existence of two nuclear spin isomers, with total nuclear spin
I=0
(
para
-
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
) and
I=1
(
ortho
-
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
), and different sets of rovibrational states.
The antisymmetric nature of the quantum state has consequences on the IR spectra: only
para
to
para
and
ortho
to
ortho
transitions are allowed.

The
ortho
-
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
states have odd values of
K_{a}+K_{c}
while the
para
-
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
states have even values of
K_{a}+K_{c}
in the ground vibrational state
\left|000\right>
, see Fig.
1
(c).
The allowed rotational transitions are depicted in the inset to Fig.
2
(a).
The same rule applies to the excited vibrational states
\left|100\right>
and
\left|010\right>
, Fig.
1
(c), upper left part.
However, the rules are inverted for the states
\left|001\right>
,
\left|101\right>
and
\left|011\right>
, which involve one-quantum excitation of the asymmetric stretch mode
v_{3}
.
In these cases
Bunker and Jensen 1998
,
para
-
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
has odd values of
K_{a}+K_{c}
, while
ortho
-
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
has even values of
K_{a}+K_{c}
, Fig.
1
(c), upper right part.

The energy difference between the lowest
para
rotational state
\left|0_{00}\right>
and the lowest
ortho
rotational state
\left|1_{01}\right>
is 2.6 meV (28 K)
Beduz et al. 2012
.
Above 30 K the ratio of
ortho
and
para
molecules is
n_{\mathrm{o}}/n_{\mathrm{p}}\approx 3
.
Hence, if the sample is cooled rapidly to 4 K, the number of
para
molecules slowly grows in the subsequent time interval, while the number of the
ortho
molecules slowly decreases to the thermal equilibrium value
n_{\mathrm{o}}\approx 0
.
The full conversion takes several hours
Beduz et al. 2012
;
Mamone et al. 2014
;
Meier et al. 2015
;
Zhukov et al. 2020
.

III.2
Absorption cross-section of
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}@\mathrm{C}_{60}

The strengths of the transitions between the rotational states of a polar molecule are determined by the permanent electric dipole moment of the molecule and by the electric field of the infrared radiation, corrected by the polarizability of the medium.
In principle, the polarizability
\chi
of the solid depends on the fraction
f
of
\mathrm{C}_{60}
cages which contain a water molecule,
\chi(f)=\chi_{\mathrm{C}_{60}}+f\chi_{\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}@\mathrm{C}_{60}}
.
However, we found that
within the studied range of filling factors,
f=0.1
to 0.8, the absorption cross-section of
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}@\mathrm{C}_{60}
was independent of
f
.
Hence, only the polarizability of solid
\mathrm{C}_{60}
is relevant,
\chi\approx\chi_{\mathrm{C}_{60}}
,
and the problem is similar to the optical absorption of an isolated impurity atom in a crystal
Dexter 1956
.

Following Ref.
Dexter 1956
, the electric field at the molecule embedded into medium with an index of refraction
\eta
is
{\cal E}_{\mathrm{eff}}={\cal E}(\eta^{2}+2)/3
, where
{\cal E}
is the electric field of radiation in the vacuum.
The refractive index of solid
\mathrm{C}_{60}
is
\eta=2

Homes et al. 1994
, and hence
{\cal E}_{\mathrm{eff}}=2{\cal E}
.

In the following discussion, we use the index
k\in\{\mathrm{o},\mathrm{p}\}
to indicate the
ortho
or
para
nuclear spin isomers.
The absorption cross-section
Bunker and Jensen 1998
for a given nuclear spin isomer

\displaystyle\sigma_{ji}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{k}^{-1}\int_{\mathrm{Line}}\alpha_{ji}^{(k)}(\omega)\mathrm{d}\omega

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{2}}{h\epsilon_{0}c_{0}\eta}\left({\frac{\eta^{2}+2}{3}}\right)^{2}\omega_{ji}^{(k)}\left(p_{i}^{(k)}-p_{j}^{(k)}\right)S_{ji}^{(k)},

c_{0}
is the speed of light in vacuum and

SI units are used and the frequency

The

\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}

From our fit the permanent dipole moment

of the encapsulated water is given by the absorption cross-section of the IR rotational transitions 3, 4, and 5 in the ground vibrational state.

in hand and by using the intensities of transitions 1 and 2 we were able to determine the internal static electric field in solid
\mathrm{C}_{60}
.
The interaction of
\mu^{x}

For example, in case of
ortho
water the components of state
\left|1_{10}\right>

\left|1_{01}\right>
, Table
5
.

3
, the fitted value of the electric field at the
\mathrm{C}_{60}
cage centres is
(110\pm 5)10^{6}
Vm
-1
.

\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}

ortho

\left|1_{01}\right>

1

The electric field

This electric field lifts the degeneracy of

V.3
Rotations of
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}

There are two possibilities why the rotational constants of water change when it is encapsulated.
First is that the bond length and angles of
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
change.
The second is that
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
, because of confinement and being non-centrosymmetric, is forced to rotate about the “center of interaction” which does not coincide with its nuclear center of mass
Friedmann and Kimel 1965
.

The rotational constant relates to the moment of inertia
I_{aa}
as
A=h(8\pi^{2}c_{0}I_{aa})^{-1}
where
c_{0}
is the speed of light.
Similarly,
B=h(8\pi^{2}c_{0}I_{bb})^{-1}
for the
b
-axis and
C=h(8\pi^{2}c_{0}I_{cc})^{-1}
for the
c
-axis rotation (
[A]=
m
-1
in SI units and
0.01[A]=
\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}
).
The moments of inertia are
I_{\alpha\alpha}=\sum_{i}m_{i}(\beta_{i}^{2}+\gamma_{i}^{2})
, where
\{\alpha_{i},\beta_{i},\gamma_{i}\}
are the Cartesian coordinates of the
i

m_{i}
with the origin at the nuclear center of mass.

For non-centrosymmetric molecules, the translation-rotation coupling shifts the rotational energy levels.
In quantum mechanical terms, the shift of rotational states is caused by the mixing of rotational and translational states by translation-rotation coupling, example is HD@C
60

Xu et al. 2008b
;
Ge et al. 2011a
.
Translation-rotation coupling was not included in our quantum mechanical model of
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}@\mathrm{C}_{60}

The rotational constants of the model were free parameters to capture the effect of translation-rotation coupling and the change of the
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}

\mathrm{C}_{60}

The rotational constants of the free water are

\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}

The rotational constants of endohedral water have relative shifts

and

In the following discussion, we use classical arguments to assess whether the shift of rotational levels is caused by the change of water molecule geometry or by the translation-rotation coupling.

From the symmetry of the
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
molecule, the nuclear center of mass is on the
b

The shift of
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}

b

A

If

of caged water with respect to free water

With these parameters the relative change of rotational constants

-14\%,0.65\%
and
-5.2\%
.
This relative change of
A_{000}
and
C_{000}
is not very different from the values derived from the IR spectra while the relative change of

is within the error limits, Table

Thus, it is likely that the dominant contribution to the observed changes of rotational constants in the ground translational state comes not from the change of
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
molecule bond lengths and bond angle but from the shifting its center of rotation away from the nuclear center of mass of
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}

V.5
Effect of solid
\mathrm{C}_{60}
crystal field

The electric dipolar and quadrupolar interactions of the
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}@\mathrm{C}_{60}
molecule with the electrostatic field in solid
\mathrm{C}_{60}
explains the oscillator strength of the pure
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
vibrational transitions and the splitting of the rotational states with
J>0
.

Theoretical work of Felker et al.
Felker et al. 2017
shows that the source of the quadrupole crystal field is the orientation of electron-rich double bonds of 12 nearest-neighbour
\mathrm{C}_{60}
relative to the central
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}@\mathrm{C}_{60}
.
When the solid
\mathrm{C}_{60}
is cooled below 90 K a merohedral disorder is frozen in where approximately 85% of
\mathrm{C}_{60}
have electron-rich 6:6 bond (bond between the two hexagonal rings) facing pentagonal ring of a neighbouring cage, the P-orientation
Heiney 1992
.
The rest are H-oriented where the 6:6 bond faces neighbouring cages hexagonal ring.
The calculated quadrupolar interaction for the P-oriented molecules is ten times bigger than for the H-oriented molecules
Felker et al. 2017
.
The electric field gradient couples to the quadrupole moment of water and splits the
\left|1_{01}\right>
rotational state by
4.2
\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}
, where the
m=\pm 1
doublet is above the
m=0
level
Felker et al. 2017
.
This theoretically predicted splitting of
\left|1_{01}\right>
state for the P-oriented molecules is remarkably close to the observed experimental value, seen as a 4
\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}
splitting of line 7, transition starting from the
ortho
ground state, Fig.
4
.
It is not possible to determine the crystal electric field gradient tensor and the encapsulated water quadrupole moment separately from our IR spectra.

Further splitting is possible if the symmetry is lower than
S_{6}
, but the maximum number of components for
J=1
remains three.
However, we see that line 7 consists of four components instead of three, Fig.
4
.
This suggests that there are two sites with different local electrostatic fields.
Anomalous splitting of triply degenerate phonons into quartets has been seen in solid
\mathrm{C}_{60}
by IR spectroscopy
Homes et al. 1994
and was attributed to the merohedral disorder.
Thus, our work and IR study of phonons
Homes et al. 1994
clearly show that there are two different quadrupolar interactions in solid
\mathrm{C}_{60}
.
As was proposed by Felker et al.
Felker et al. 2017
, the crystal field has a different magnitude for P-oriented sites and for H-oriented sites. The small population of H-oriented sites (about 15%) justifies our spectral fitting with a single quadrupolar interaction.

We assumed that there is an internal electric field in
\mathrm{C}_{60}
and this field is a possible reason why the pure vibrational transitions 1 and 2, see Fig.
1
(c), become visible in the IR spectrum.
It is also plausible that 1 and 2 gain intensity through the translation-rotation coupling from the induced dipole moment of translational motion.
However, there is evidence that local electric fields exist in solid
\mathrm{C}_{60}
as a result of merohedral disorder
\mathrm{C}_{60}
Alers et al. 1992
.
The estimate of un-balanced charge by Alers et al.
Alers et al. 1992
was
q=6\times 10^{-3}e
assuming a dipole moment
\mu=qd_{0}
, where
e
is the electron charge and
d_{0}=0.7
nm is the diameter of a
\mathrm{C}_{60}
molecule.
Our estimate is that the electric field
\mathcal{E}=1.1\times 10^{8}
Vm
-1
at the center of
\mathrm{C}_{60}
cage is created by the dipole moment with charge
q=4.7\times 10^{-3}e
.
These two estimates are very close.

\mathrm{C}_{60}
has six nearest-neighbor equatorial cages and three axial cages above and three axial cages below the equatorial plane, following the notation of Ref.
Felker et al. 2017
.
The
z^{\prime}
axis of the crystal field coordinate frame is normal to the equatorial plane.
As our fit shows, the electric field is rotated away from the
z^{\prime}
axis by
\theta_{E}\approx 80^{\circ}
, Table
3

It is possible that one of the 6 nearest-neighbours in the equatorial plane does not have P-orientation and this mis-oriented cage is the source of the electric field.

\theta_{E}
has a large relative error consistent with the probability to have the mis-oriented cage in the equatorial or in the axial position.

VI
Summary

The infrared absorption spectra of solid
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}@\mathrm{C}_{60}
samples were measured close to liquid He temperature and rotational, vibrational, rovibrational, overtone, and combination rovibrational transitions of
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
were seen.
The spectral lines were identified as
para
and
ortho
water transitions by following the
para
-
ortho
conversion process over the timescale of hours.
The vibrational frequencies are shifted by -2.4% relative to free water, except bending mode frequency
\omega_{2}
and its overtone
2\omega_{2}
, where the shift is -1.6%.
An absorption mode due to the quantized center of mass motion of
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
in the molecular cage of
\mathrm{C}_{60}
, was observed at 110
\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}
.
The dipole moment of encapsulated water is
0.50\pm 0.05
D, approximately 4 times less than for free water and agrees with previous estimates
Meier et al. 2015
.

The rotational and rovibrational spectra were fitted with a quantum mechanical model of a vibrating rotor in electrostatic field with dipolar and quadrupolar interactions.
The quadrupolar interaction splits the
J\geq 1
rotational states of
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
.
The source of quadrupolar interaction is the relative orientation of electron-rich chemical bonds relative to pentagonal and hexagonal motifs of
\mathrm{C}_{60}
and its 12 nearest neighbours
Felker et al. 2017
.
Further IR study by using pressure to change the ratio of P- and H-oriented motifs
Sundqvist 1999
would provide more information on the quadrupolar crystal fields of these motifs.
The finite oscillator strength of the fundamental vibrational transitions is attributed to a finite electric field at the centres of the
\mathrm{C}_{60}
cages due to merohedral disorder, as has been postulated in different contexts
Alers et al. 1992
.
Our results are consistent with an internal electric field of
10^{8}
Vm
-1
.
However, it is also plausible that the fundamental vibrational transitions gain intensity through the translation-rotation coupling from the dipole moment induced by the translational motion, something that can be addressed in further theoretical studies.

To conclude,
\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}
in the molecular cavity of
\mathrm{C}_{60}
behaves as a vibrating asymmetric top, its dipole moment is reduced, and the translational motion is quantized.
The splitting of rotational levels is caused by the quadrupolar interaction with the crystal field of solid
\mathrm{C}_{60}
. Evidence is found for the existence of a finite electric field at the centres of the
\mathrm{C}_{60}
cages in the water-endofullerene, due to merohedral disorder.

Two out of three components necessary for a rigorous, comprehensive description of the water translations, rotations and vibrations inside
\mathrm{C}_{60}
molecular cage are now in place: firstly, the infrared spectroscopy data reported here and secondly, the 9-dimensional quantum bound-state methodology
Felker and Bačić 2020
plus the theory of symmetry breaking in solid
\mathrm{C}_{60}

Felker et al. 2017
;
Bačić et al. 2018
.
What is missing is a high-quality ab initio 9-dimensional potential energy surface for this system.

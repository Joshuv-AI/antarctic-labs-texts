Tightening Discretization-based MILP Models for the Pooling Problem using Upper Bounds on Bilinear Terms
Yifu Chen, Christos T. Maravelias, Xiaomin Zhang

Abstract
Discretization-based methods have been proposed for solving nonconvex optimization problems with bilinear terms such as the pooling problem. These methods convert the original nonconvex optimization problems into mixed-integer linear programs (MILPs). In this paper we study tightening methods for these MILP models for the pooling problem, and derive valid constraints using upper bounds on bilinear terms. Computational results demonstrate the effectiveness of our methods in terms of reducing solution time.



1
Introduction

The pooling problem is a nonconvex optimization problem that has been studied extensively. First studied by Harvey
[
21
]
, it continues to be an active research topic. In the pooling problem, we are given a set of streams to be blended in a set of pools to produce certain products. Streams, pools, and products are connected through pipelines. Streams possess multiple properties and products have specifications on those properties. We aim to optimize the flows through the pipelines to maximize profit, subject to product specifications and other constraints, such as pool and pipeline capacity.

Various formulations for the pooling problem have been proposed (
[
21
,
5
,
31
,
1
,
6
]
), and a number of extensions of the pooling problem have been studied. Comprehensive reviews on these topics can be found in a survey by Misener and Floudas
[
27
]
and a thesis by Gupte
[
17
]
.

Solving the pooling problem is computationally challenging
[
20
]
, and many solution methods for the pooling problem have been proposed. These solution methods include, for example, methods based on piecewise linear relaxations (
[
16
,
7
]
) and domain partitioning (
[
28
,
29
]
), as well as methods that exploit the product specifications constraints (
[
30
,
9
]
). Nonlinear relaxations of the pooling problem have also been studied. For example, Kimizuka et al. studied the second order cone relaxation of the pooling problem
[
22
]
, Luedtke et al. proposed a strong convex nonlinear relaxation derived from extended formulation
[
25
]
, and Dey et al. studied the convexifiation methods for rank-one-based substructures arising in the pooling problem
[
13
]
.

Discretization-based methods for solving the pooling problem have received much attention over the years. Since the only nonlinear component in the pooling problem are bilinear terms that contain two continuous variables, by discretizing one variable in each bilinear term, one can convert the original nonconvex optimization problem into a mixed-integer linear program (MILP). Kolodziej et al. proposed global optimization algorithms based on solving MILPs resulted from discretization
[
23
]
. Dey and Gupte analyzed MILP techniques to address bilinear terms
[
12
]
, Gupte et al. studied relaxations and discretizations for the pooling problem
[
19
]
. Ceccon and Misener
[
8
]
implemented a discretization technique into an extensible solver for the pooling problem.

In this paper, we study tightening constraints for the discretization-based MILP models for the pooling problem, with the focus on using the upper bounds on bilinear terms to tighten the MILP models. Specifically, we consider the following set, which, as we will show later, arises in the models for the pooling problem:

\textbf{S}_{1}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:x\leq x^{\text{U}},y\leq 1,xy\leq w^{\text{U}}\}

(1)

Note that setting the upper bound on
y
to one is not overly restrictive since one can re-scale variables
x
and
y
accordingly. The upper bound on the bilinear term,
w^{\text{U}}
, is said to be nontrivial if
w^{\text{U}}<x^{\text{U}}
. In this paper, we assume such an upper bound is nontrivial.

We start with discretizing
y
. Specifically, we restrict the value of
y
to be in a discrete set
\textbf{O}=\{0,2^{1-n},2^{2-n},...,1-2^{1-n},1\}
, whose elements form an arithmetic sequence, where

\textbf{S}_{1}^{*}={\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:x\leq x^{\text{U}},y\in\textbf{O},xy\leq w^{\text{U}}\}}

We note that
\textbf{S}_{1}^{*}
can be represented with linear constraints. First, to model

, we consider the binary expansion of

:

y=\sum_{p\in[\![n]\!]}2^{1-p}Z_{p}

(3)

where

and
[\![n]\!]=\{1,2,...,n\}
. By introducing binary variable
Z_{p}
, (
3
), and a continuous variable
w
, we have the following set:

\textbf{S}_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}={\{(w,x,y,Z)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}\times\{0,1\}^{n}:w\leq w^{\text{U}},x\leq x^{\text{U}},y\leq 1,\eqref{Binary_expansion},w=xy\}}

(4)

We next re-write

w=\sum_{p}2^{1-p}xZ_{p}

(5)

By defining
v_{p}=xZ_{p}
for each
p\in[\![n]\!]
, we can linearize (
5
) exactly using:

w=\sum_{p}2^{1-p}v_{p}

(6)

v_{p}\leq x^{\text{U}}Z_{p},\quad\forall p

(7)

v_{p}\leq x,\quad\forall p

(8)

v_{p}\geq x+x^{\text{U}}Z_{p}-x^{\text{U}},\quad\forall p

(9)

We define the set
\textbf{S}_{2}
as:

\textbf{S}_{2}=\{(v,w,x,y,Z)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}\times\{0,1\}^{n}:\\
w\leq w^{\text{U}},x\leq x^{\text{U}},y\leq 1,\eqref{Binary_expansion},\eqref{Re-Def_w_v}-\eqref{LB_v_p_1}\}

(10)

which is a linear representation of

We note one related work from Gupte et al.
[
18
]
, in which the authors studied a set equivalent to

\textbf{S}_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}=\{(v,w,x,y,Z)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}\times\{0,1\}^{n}:x\leq x^{\text{U}},y\leq 1,\eqref{Binary_expansion},\eqref{Re-Def_w_v}-\eqref{LB_v_p_1}\}

which is a relaxation of

[

\textbf{S}_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}

Different from
[
18
]
, in this paper we study valid constraints for
\textbf{S}_{2}
derived from nontrivial upper bounds on bilinear terms. Specifically, we give the full description of the convex hull of
\textbf{S}_{1}^{*}
; in addition, we tighten (
7
) using tightened upper bounds on
x
. We also note that while our methods are presented in the context of the pooling problem, they can be extended to address other nonconvex optimization problems with nontrivial upper bounds on bilinear terms.

We note that methods that exploit the nontrivial bounds of bilinear terms have been proposed; most notably, Anstreicher et al. derived convex hull representations for bounded products of continuous variables

[
2
]
. In this work we focus on the discretized version of the problem, and propose a different set of tightening constraints.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we present background material, including the problem statement, a nonlinear model for the pooling problem, and an MILP model obtained from discretization. In Section
3

2.2
A nonlinear model for the pooling problem

Various models have been proposed for the pooling problem. In this paper, we first study a model that contains variable for split fraction, which is similar to the one presented in Boland et al.
[
6
]
and the source-based model studied by Lotero et al.
[
24
]
. We define the following nonnegative continuous variables:

F_{ij}
:

Flow of stream
i
to pool
j

\hat{F}_{ijk}
:

Flow of stream
i
from pool
j
to product
k

R_{jk}
:

Split fraction for total inlet flows from pool
j
to product
k
(
R_{jk}\leq 1
)

We have the following constraints:

Pool capacity:

\sum_{i}F_{ij}\leq\gamma_{j},\quad\forall j

(11)

Product demand:

\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\hat{F}_{ijk}\leq\omega_{k},\quad\forall k

(12)

Product specifications:

\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\pi_{il}\hat{F}_{ijk}\leq\psi_{kl}\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\hat{F}_{ijk},\quad\forall k,l

(13)

Stream splitting:

\hat{F}_{ijk}=F_{ij}R_{jk},\quad\forall i,j,k

(14)

Note that (
14
) is an equality constraint with a bilinear term.

For split fraction
R_{jk}
we have:

\sum_{k}R_{jk}=1,\quad\forall j

(15)

(
14
) and (
15
) enforce that there is no flow accumulation in pools.

We also have upper bound on the flows from pools to products due to pipeline capacity:

\sum_{i}\hat{F}_{ijk}\leq\upsilon_{jk},\quad\forall j,k

(16)

We note that the left hand side (LHS) of (
16
) is also upper bounded by
\gamma_{j}
. However,
\upsilon_{jk}
is a tighter upper bound since in practice pipeline capacity is typically much smaller than pool capacity. In this work we assume
\upsilon_{jk}<\gamma_{j}
. In other words,
\upsilon_{jk}
is a nontrivial upper bound on the bilinear term
\sum_{i}\hat{F}_{ijk}=\sum_{i}F_{ij}R_{jk}
.

Reformulation–Linearization Technique (RLT) constraints can be added to tighten the formulation. Summing over index
k
on both sides of (
14
), we have:

\sum_{k}\hat{F}_{ijk}=F_{ij}\sum_{k}R_{jk},\quad\forall i,j

which, combined with (
15
), leads to:

\sum_{k}\hat{F}_{ijk}=F_{ij},\quad\forall i,j

(17)

Another RLT constraint can be obtained by multiplying both sides of (
11
) with
R_{jk}
(a nonnegative variable):

\sum_{i}F_{ij}R_{jk}\leq\gamma_{j}R_{jk},\quad\forall j,k

which, combined with (
14
), leads to:

\sum_{i}\hat{F}_{ijk}\leq\gamma_{j}R_{jk},\quad\forall j,k

(18)

The objective function is profit maximization:

\text{max}\sum_{i}\sum_{j}(\sum_{k}\beta_{k}\hat{F}_{ijk}-\alpha_{i}{F}_{ij})

(19)

(
11
) - (
19
) comprise a nonlinear model for the pooling problem. We next present a discretization-based model derived from it, and show how we exploit the structure of the above nonlinear model to tighten the discretization-based model.

2.3
A discretization-based model for the pooling problem

We consider a discretization-based model for the above nonlinear model by restricting
R_{jk}\in\textbf{O}
, with the following binary expansion of
R_{jk}
:

R_{jk}=\sum_{p\in[\![n]\!]}2^{1-p}Z_{jkp},\quad\forall j,k

(20)

Combining (
14
) and (
20
) we have:

\hat{F}_{ijk}=\sum_{p}2^{1-p}{F}_{ij}Z_{jkp},\quad\forall i,j,k

(21)

We introduce a nonnegative continuous variable
V_{ijkp}
to represent
{F}_{ij}Z_{jkp}
:

V_{ijkp}={F}_{ij}Z_{jkp},\quad\forall i,j,k,p

(22)

Since
{F}_{ij}
is upper bounded by
\gamma_{j}
, we linearize (
21
) using constraints similar to (
6
) - (
9
):

\hat{F}_{ijk}=\sum_{p}2^{1-p}V_{ijkp},\quad\forall i,j,k

(23)

V_{ijkp}\leq\gamma_{j}Z_{jkp},\quad\forall i,j,k,p

(24)

V_{ijkp}\leq{F}_{ij},\quad\forall i,j,k,p

(25)

V_{ijkp}\geq{F}_{ij}-\gamma_{j}(1-Z_{jkp}),\quad\forall i,j,k,p

(26)

Inequalities (
24
) and (
26
) can be tightened. To simplify notation, we define
\tilde{F}_{j}=\sum_{i}F_{ij}
, and
\bar{V}_{jkp}=\sum_{i}{V}_{ijkp}
. By summing over index

for (

) we have:

\bar{V}_{jkp}=\tilde{F}_{j}Z_{jkp},\quad\forall j,k,p

(27)

From (
11
) recall that

is also the upper bound on
\tilde{F}_{j}
. Hence, we have the following two tightening constraints:

\bar{V}_{jkp}\leq\gamma_{j}Z_{jkp},\quad\forall j,k,p

(28)

\bar{V}_{jkp}\geq\tilde{F}_{j}-\gamma_{j}(1-Z_{jkp}),\quad\forall j,k,p

(29)

(
11
) - (
13
), (
15
) - (
20
), (

) - (
26
), and (
28
) - (
29
) comprise a discretization-based model for the pooling problem, henceforth referred to as

. Solving

leads to a feasible solution to the original nonlinear model of the pooling problem, and the optimal objective function value of

is a lower bound on the optimal objective function value of the nonlinear problem. In practice, discretization-based models provide pretty good solutions within a reasonable amount of time; this observation is also noted by Dey and Gupte after extensive computational comparisons

), (

) - (

) are similar to the constraints involved in

We note that many other formulations for the pooling problem have been proposed (

3
Valid Constraints

We first provide a high level description of the structure in
\text{M}^{\text{SB}}
that we exploit to generate valid constraints. Let
\bar{F}_{jk}=\sum_{i}\hat{F}_{ijk}
and consider the bilinear term
\bar{F}_{jk}=\tilde{F}_{j}R_{jk}
. For a given
(j,k)
pair, we define the following set:

\textbf{S}_{jk}=\{(R_{jk},\tilde{F}_{j},\bar{F}_{jk})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}:\tilde{F}_{j}\leq\gamma_{j},\bar{F}_{jk}\leq\upsilon_{jk},R_{jk}\leq 1,\bar{F}_{jk}=\tilde{F}_{j}R_{jk}\}

(30)

and after restricting
R_{jk}\in\textbf{O}
, we have the following set:

\textbf{S}_{jk}^{*}=\{(R_{jk},\tilde{F}_{j})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:\tilde{F}_{j}\leq\gamma_{j},\tilde{F}_{j}R_{jk}\leq\upsilon_{jk},R_{jk}\in\textbf{O}\}

(31)

In Section
3.1
, we present a set of valid constraints derived from
\textbf{S}_{jk}^{*}
. Specifically, we analyze the feasible space defined by it and provide a closed-form representation of its convex hull.

Similar to
\textbf{S}_{1}^{*}
,
\textbf{S}_{jk}^{*}
can be represented by linear constraints. Such a linear representation requires linearizing
\tilde{F}_{j}Z_{jkp}
, which utilizes the upper bound on
\tilde{F}_{j}
. We note that while
\gamma_{j}
is a valid upper bound, a tighter upper bound can be obtained in certain situations. Details are presented in Section
3.2
.

For the remaining parts of this section, we focus on a
(j,k)
pair, and for simplicity we drop these two indices.

3.1
Rounding cuts for flows into pools

We first analyze the feasible space defined by
\textbf{S}^{*}
:

\textbf{S}^{*}=\{(R,\tilde{F})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:\tilde{F}\leq\gamma,\tilde{F}R\leq\upsilon,R\in\textbf{O}\}

For a fixed
R

\tilde{F}\in[0,\text{min}(\gamma,\upsilon/R)]

) space. Since

can only take discrete values in the finite set

\textbf{S}^{*}
is the union of those vertical line segments. One can thus conclude that the convex hull of

Valid constraints and polyhedral results

We next formalize the valid constraints for
\textbf{S}^{*}
. Let
\mu^{-}
denote the maximum value of

\tilde{F}
can still take value
\gamma

\mu^{+}=2^{1-n}(\left\lfloor\upsilon/(2^{1-n}\gamma)\right\rfloor+1)=\mu^{-}+2^{1-n}

\delta=(\gamma-\upsilon)/(\mu^{-}-1)

We have the following constraints:

\tilde{F}\leq\delta(R-1)+\upsilon,\quad\delta\geq\epsilon

\tilde{F}\leq\epsilon(R-\mu^{-})+\gamma,\quad\delta<\epsilon

(33)

\tilde{F}\leq\frac{\upsilon/\mu^{+}-\upsilon}{\mu^{+}-1}(R-1)+\upsilon,\quad\delta<\epsilon

We define the polyhedron

Q_{1}=\{(R,\tilde{F})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:\tilde{F}\leq\gamma,R\leq 1,\eqref{Rounded Cut 1}\}

3.2
p-dependent upper bounds on flows into pools

In this section we focus on the linear representation of the set
\textbf{S}^{*}
, which involves linearizing (
27
). Specifically, we note that when certain
Z_{p}
takes the value 1, the upper bound on
\tilde{F}
can be tightened, which leads to constraints tighter than (
28
).

We first consider the case where for a given
(j,k)
pair,
{Z}_{1}=1
. In this case, from (
20
) we have
{R}=1
; in other words, all inlet flows to pool
j
go to product
k
. Thus, we have
\tilde{F}\leq\upsilon
. We can tighten (
28
) using:

\bar{V}_{1}\leq\upsilon Z_{1}

(35)

Now, consider the case where for a given
(j,k)
pair and
p=2
,
{Z}_{2}=1
. In this case, from (
20
) we know that
{R}\geq 1/2
, in other words, no less than half the inlet flows to pool
j
go to product
k
. Thus, we have
\tilde{F}\leq 2\upsilon
. We can tighten (
28
) using:

\bar{V}_{2}\leq 2\upsilon Z_{2}

(36)

Following the same approach we can derive constraints for
p=3,4...
, and the constraints obtained from this approach will be tighter than (
28
) when
2^{p-1}\upsilon<\gamma
, which holds for
p<1+log_{2}(\gamma/\upsilon)
. Thus, (
28
) can be tightened using the following valid constraint:

\bar{V}_{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\upsilon Z_{p},\quad p:p<1+log_{2}(\gamma/\upsilon)

In this section, we present computational results for models with the proposed constraints. Computational experiments are conducted on a Windows 10 machine with Intel Core i5 at 2.70 GHz and 8 GB of RAM. Models are coded in GAMS 33.2 and solved using CPLEX 12.10 with default settings. Instances are modified from the 90 randomly generated instances in D’Ambrosio et al.

4.4
Comparison with lifted tangent inequalities

Valid constraints that exploit nontrivial bounds on bilinear terms with two continuous variables have been proposed
[
3
,
4
,
2
,
10
]
. One class of these constraints is called lifted tangent inequalities (LTI), which is involved in the convex hull of bilinear terms with nontrivial upper bounds. Here, for
\text{M}^{\text{SB}}
and for each
(j,k)
pair we consider the LTI in the following form given in
[
2
]
:

\frac{\upsilon}{\rho}R+\rho\tilde{F}-2\bar{F}\leq 0

(43)

where
\rho
is a parameter. Inequality (
43
) is tangent to the curve
\tilde{F}R=\upsilon
at point
(\rho,\frac{\upsilon}{\rho})
when
\bar{F}=\upsilon
. To add (
43
) into the discretized model, we set the values of
\rho
is such a way that the resulting points of tangency are feasible in
\text{M}^{\text{SB}}
. Specifically, we have:

\frac{\upsilon}{2^{1-p}}R+2^{1-p}\tilde{F}-2\bar{F}\leq 0,\quad\forall p\in[\![n]\!]

(44)

As its name suggested, LTI can be derived from lifting the tangent line to
\tilde{F}R=\upsilon
to include the origin. It can be strengthened for
\text{M}^{\text{SB}}
. Since only certain points on the curve are feasible in
\text{M}^{\text{SB}}
, we derive a constraint by lifting the secant line passing through two adjacent feasible points on the curve. We have:

\frac{\upsilon}{2^{2-p}+2^{1-n}}R+\frac{2^{1-p}(2^{1-p}+2^{1-n})}{2^{2-p}+2^{1-n}}\tilde{F}-\bar{F}\leq 0,\quad\forall p\in[\![n-1]\!]

(45)

Figure 5:
Performance profiles for variants of
\text{M}^{\text{SB}}
with LTI, strengthened LTI, and constraints proposed in this work.

We test (
44
) and (
45
) on
\text{M}^{\text{SB}}
and compare them with the constraints proposed in this work. Fig.
5
shows the performance profiles for models with different constraints on the same set of instances tested in Section
4.1
with
n=4
, where the blue profile corresponds to the model with constraints proposed in this work, and green and red profiles correspond to models with (
44
) and (
45
), respectively. We note that for the discretized model the constraints proposed in this work bring more improvement.

5
Conclusion

In this paper we studied tightening methods for discretization-based MILP models for the pooling problem, and derived valid constraints using upper bounds on bilinear terms. The proposed constraints are tested on different formulations as well as different discretization methods. Computational results demonstrate the effectiveness of our methods in terms of reducing solution time.

Appendix B. Proof of Proposition 1

Proposition 1.
conv(
\textbf{S}^{*}
)
=\{(R,\tilde{F})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:\tilde{F}\leq\gamma,R\leq 1,\eqref{Rounded Cut 1}-\eqref{Rounded Cut 3}\}
.

Proof

In this proof we consider the two cases,
\delta\geq\epsilon
and
\delta<\epsilon
, separately. For each case, we consider the following definition of convex hull: The convex hull of a set is the set of all convex combinations of points in it.

Case 1:

\delta\geq\epsilon

We define the polyhedron

Q_{1}=\{(R,\tilde{F})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:\tilde{F}\leq\gamma,R\leq 1,\eqref{Rounded Cut 1}\}

We next show that (1)
Q_{1}
contains all convex combinations of points in
\textbf{S}^{*}
, and (2) all points in
Q_{1}
are convex combinations of points in
\textbf{S}^{*}
.

To show (1), we first note that since all points in
\textbf{S}^{*}
are in (finitely many) line segments, all convex combinations of those points can be represented by convex combinations of the end points for those line segments. Since all end points are in the form of (
R,0
) and (
R,\text{min}(\gamma,\upsilon/R)
), they can be partitioned into three groups: (a) (
R,0
), (b) (
R,\gamma)
with
R\leq\mu^{-}
, and (c) (
R,\upsilon/R)
with
R\geq\mu^{+}
. It is clear that points in group (a) and (b) are in
Q_{1}
. We show that points in group (c) are also in
Q_{1}
.

For points in group (c), the inequality
R\leq 1
is valid; To show these points also satisfy
\tilde{F}\leq\gamma
, we note that when
R=\mu^{+}
, the
\tilde{F}
-coordinate values for these points satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{\upsilon}{R}=\frac{\upsilon}{2^{1-n}(\left\lfloor\upsilon/(2^{1-n}\gamma)\right\rfloor+1)}\leq\frac{\upsilon}{2^{1-n}\upsilon/(2^{1-n}\gamma)}=\gamma

and
\upsilon/R
is monotonic decreasing w.r.t.
R
, thus points in group (c) satisfy
\tilde{F}\leq\gamma
.

It remains to be shown that points in group (c) also satisfy (
32
). We note that these points are on the curve defined by the convex function
\tilde{F}=\upsilon/R
, and linear constraint (
32
) is upper bounding
\tilde{F}
. To show points in group (c) satisfy (
32
), it suffices to show that the RHS of (
32
) is greater than or equal to
\upsilon/R
when
R=\mu^{+}
and when
R=1
(since for points in group (c) we have
R\in[\mu^{+},1]
).

When
R=\mu^{+}
, for the RHS of (
32
) we have:

\displaystyle\delta(\mu^{+}-1)+\upsilon

\displaystyle=\frac{(\gamma-\upsilon)(\mu^{+}-1)}{\mu^{-}-1}+\upsilon

\displaystyle=\frac{(\gamma-\upsilon)(\mu^{-}+2^{1-n}-1)}{\mu^{-}-1}+\upsilon

\displaystyle=(\gamma-\upsilon)(1+\frac{2^{1-n}}{\mu^{-}-1})+\upsilon

\displaystyle=\gamma+\frac{2^{1-n}(\gamma-\upsilon)}{\mu^{-}-1}

(46)

Since
\delta\geq\epsilon
, we have:

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma-\upsilon}{\mu^{-}-1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\gamma-\upsilon/\mu^{+}}{\mu^{-}-\mu^{+}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma-\upsilon/\mu^{+}}{2^{1-n}}

(47)

Combining (
46
) and (
47
) we have

\displaystyle\delta(\mu^{+}-1)+\upsilon

\displaystyle=\gamma+\frac{2^{1-n}(\gamma-\upsilon)}{\mu^{-}-1}

\displaystyle\geq\gamma-(\gamma-\upsilon/\mu^{+})=\upsilon/\mu^{+}

Thus, when
R=\mu^{+}
, the RHS of (
32
) is greater than or equal to
\upsilon/R
.

When
R=1
, the RHS of (
32
) becomes
\upsilon=\upsilon/R
. Thus, we conclude that the RHS of (
32
) is greater or equal to
\upsilon/R
when
R\in[\mu^{+},1]
, and thus points in group (c) are in
Q_{1}
.

We thus conclude that all end points for line segments in
\textbf{S}^{*}
are in
Q_{1}
.

To show (2): all points in
Q_{1}
are convex combinations of points in
\textbf{S}^{*}
, one can verify that the five extreme points of
Q_{1}
are:
P_{1}=(0,0),P_{2}=(0,\gamma),P_{3}=(\mu^{-},\gamma),P_{4}=(1,\upsilon),P_{5}=(1,0)
, and all of them are in
\textbf{S}^{*}
. Since all points in
Q_{1}
are convex combinations of these extreme points, we conclude that all points in
Q_{1}
are convex combinations of points in
\textbf{S}^{*}
.

We thus conclude that when
\delta\geq\epsilon

\textbf{S}^{*}
) =
Q_{1}
.

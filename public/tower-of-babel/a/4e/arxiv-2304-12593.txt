Averaging algebras of any nonzero weight
Apurba Das, Ramkrishna Mandal

Abstract
. In this paper, we first introduce the notion of a (relative) averaging operator of any nonzero weight λ \lambda . We show that such operators are intimately related to triassociative algebras introduced by Loday and Ronco. Next, we construct a differential graded Lie algebra and a L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are respectively relative averaging operators of weight λ \lambda and relative averaging algebras of weight λ \lambda . Subsequently, we define cohomology theories for relative averaging operators and relative averaging algebras of any nonzero weight. Our cohomology is useful to study infinitesimal deformations of relative averaging algebras. Finally, we introduce and study homotopy triassociative algebras and homotopy relative averaging operators of any nonzero weight λ \lambda .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Averaging algebras

The notion of averaging operator was first implicitly considered by O. Reynolds in 1895
[
33
]
. His description was given in terms of idempotent Reynolds operators that appeared in the turbulence theory of fluid dynamics. In 1930, Kampé de Fériet gave the explicit description of an averaging operator in the context of turbulence theory and functional analysis
[
17
]
. An averaging operator generalizes the time average operator of real-valued functions defined in time space. Let
A
be an associative algebra. A linear map
P:A\rightarrow A
is said to be an averaging operator on the algebra
A
if

\displaystyle P(a)P(b)=P(P(a)b)=P(aP(b)),\text{ for }a,b\in A.

An associative algebra
A
equipped with a distinguished averaging operator on it is called an averaging algebra. In the last century, averaging operators and averaging algebras were mostly studied in the algebra of functions on a space
[
5
,
19
,
12
,
27
,
28
]
. In the algebraic contexts, B. Brainerd
[
7
]
first studied the conditions for which an averaging operator can be realized as a generalization of the integral operator on the ring of real-valued measurable functions. In his PhD thesis, W. Cao
[
8
]
considered averaging operators from algebraic and combinatorial points of view. Among others, he described the Lie algebra and the Leibniz algebra induced from an averaging operator and construct the free commutative averaging algebra. Further algebraic study of averaging operators on any binary operad and their relations with bisuccessors, duplicators and Rota-Baxter operators are explicitly given in
[
2
,
30
,
31
]
. Recently, J. Pei and L. Guo
[
32
]
constructed the free (associative) averaging algebra using a class of bracketed words, called averaging words. It is worth mentioning that averaging operators also appeared in the context of Lie algebras with the name of embedding tensors. They have close connections with Leibniz algebras, tensor hierarchies and higher gauge theories
[
6
,
22
,
36
,
34
]
.

1.2.
Diassociative algebras and triassociative algebras

In his study of Leibniz algebras, J.-L. Loday introduced the notion of a diassociative algebra (also called an associative dialgebra or simply a dialgebra)
[
23
]
. A diassociative algebra is a vector space
D
equipped with two linear maps
\dashv,\vdash:D\otimes D\rightarrow D
satisfying

\displaystyle(x\dashv y)\dashv z=~

\displaystyle x\dashv(y\dashv z),

\displaystyle(x\dashv y)\dashv z=~

\displaystyle x\dashv(y\vdash z),

\displaystyle(x\vdash y)\dashv z=~

\displaystyle x\vdash(y\dashv z),

\displaystyle(x\dashv y)\vdash z=~

\displaystyle x\vdash(y\vdash z),

\displaystyle(x\vdash y)\vdash z=~

\displaystyle x\vdash(y\vdash z),

for
x,y,z\in D.
It turns out that both the operations
\dashv
and
\vdash
are associative. See
[
11
,
26
,
25
,
16
]
for some interesting studies on diassociative algebras. In
[
1
]
M. Aguiar first observes that an averaging operator
P:A\rightarrow A
on an associative algebra
A
induces a diassociative algebra structure
a\dashv b:=aP(b)
and
a\vdash b:=P(a)b

on the underlying vector space
A
. However, the converse need not be true. In
[
9
]
the author considered a notion of relative averaging algebra (a generalization of an averaging algebra) and showed that any diassociative algebra is always induced by a relative averaging algebra.

In
[
24
]
J.-L. Loday and M. Ronco introduced the notion of a triassociative algebra that consist of three associative operations satisfying additional compatibilities (cf. Theorem
2.1
). It turns out that any triassociative algebra is intrinsically a diassociative algebra. In the same article, Loday and Ronco introduced tridendriform algebras and showed that triassociative algebras are Koszul dual to tridendriform algebras.

1.3.
Averaging operators and averaging algebras of nonzero weight

Our first aim in the present paper is to introduce the concept of an averaging operator of any nonzero weight. Let
A
be an associative algebra. We define an averaging operator of any nonzero weight
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
as a linear map
P:A\rightarrow A
that satisfies

\displaystyle P(a)P(b)=P(P(a)b)=P(aP(b))=\lambda P(ab),\text{ for }a,b\in A.

It turns out that any averaging operator of weight
\lambda\neq 0
is intrinsically an averaging operator. However, the converse need not be true (see Remark
3.3
). An associative algebra equipped with an averaging operator of nonzero weight
\lambda

\lambda
. We also introduce a generalization, called relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
. We show that any relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
induces a triassociative algebra structure. We also consider the notion of a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda

1.4.
Cohomology and deformations of averaging algebras of any nonzero weight

Cohomology is invariant for a given algebraic structure. The first instance of cohomology appeared in the work of Hochschild for associative algebras and later found important applications in deformation theory developed by Gerstenhaber
[
14
]
. Subsequently, their study was generalized to other algebraic structures (e.g. Lie algebras, Leibniz algebras)
[
29
,
4
]
. See also
[
25
,
39
,
23
,
24
]
for cohomology and deformations of diassociative algebras and triassociative algebras.

In the present paper, we first construct a differential graded Lie algebra that characterizes relative averaging operators of nonzero weight
\lambda
as its Maurer-Cartan elements. Subsequently, we define the cohomology of a relative averaging operator of weight
\lambda
. When one wants to define the cohomology of a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
, the theory becomes much more delicate. To overcome this situation, we first use Voronov’s derived bracket to construct a
L_{\infty}
-algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely relative averaging algebras of any fixed weight
\lambda
. Then by a suitable twisting, we find the controlling
L_{\infty}
-algebra of a given relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
. Next, we define the cohomology of a given relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
and as an application, we study infinitesimal deformations.

1.5.
Homotopy algebras

Algebraic structures often appeared with their homotopy version. The concept of a strongly homotopy associative algebra (also called
A_{\infty}
-algebra) appeared in the work of J. Stasheff in the study of topological loop spaces
[
35
]
. Subsequently, various other homotopy algebraic structures were studied in the literature. Recently, some authors have considered homotopy averaging operators
[
9
,
34
]
. In the present paper, we first explicitly define the notion of a
Triass_{\infty}
-algebra (strongly homotopy triassociative algebra) and show that any
Triass_{\infty}
-algebra is intrinsically a strongly homotopy diassociative algebra. Next, we define the notion of a homotopy relative averaging operator of weight
\lambda
and show that such a homotopy operator induces a
Triass_{\infty}
-algebra structure.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some necessary background on triassociative algebras. In particular, we recall the graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements correspond to triassociative algebra structures on a given vector space. In Section
3
, we introduce averaging operators and averaging algebras of any nonzero weight and construct the free averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
. In Section
4
, we consider relative averaging operators of weight
\lambda
, their Maurer-Cartan characterization and define their cohomology theory. In Section
5
, we study relative averaging algebras of any nonzero weight. In particular, we find out their relations with triassociative algebras. We also construct the
L_{\infty}
-algebra that characterizes relative averaging algebras of weight
\lambda
as its Maurer-Cartan elements. In Sections
6
and
7
, we respectively study cohomology and deformations of a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
. Finally, in Section
8
, we introduce homotopy relative averaging operators of weight
\lambda
and show that they induce homotopy triassociative algebra structures.

All (graded) vector spaces, linear maps and tensor products are over a field
\mathbb{K}

0

2.
Triassociative algebras

Triassociative algebras are given by three associative multiplications satisfying some compatibilities. They were introduced by Loday and Ronco in the study of algebraic structures associated with planar trees. Here we recall the graded Lie algebra that plays an important role to study triassociative algebras.

A

\displaystyle x\vdash(y\dashv z),

\displaystyle x\vdash(y\vdash z),

(12)

\displaystyle x\vdash(y\vdash z),

(13)

\displaystyle x\dashv(y\perp z),

3.
Averaging operators and averaging algebras of any nonzero weight

In this section, we first introduce the notion of an averaging operator of any nonzero weight
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
.
We also construct free averaging algebra of any nonzero weight
\lambda
over any nonempty set
X.

3.1 Definition
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra. A linear map
P:A\rightarrow A
is said to be an
averaging operator of weight
\lambda

(\neq 0)
if

\displaystyle P(a)P(b)=P(P(a)b)=P(aP(b))=\lambda P(ab),\text{ for all }a,b\in A.

(23)

It follows from the above definition that an averaging operator of nonzero weight
\lambda
is automatically an averaging operator on
A
. The above definition can be equivalently stated as follows: A linear map
P:A\rightarrow A
is said to be an averaging operator of weight
\lambda~(\neq 0)
if and only if
(1/\lambda)P
is an averaging operator and an algebra morphism. This also suggests that we can concentrate only on averaging operators of weight
1
.

3.2 Remark
.

The above definition of an averaging operator of nonzero weight
\lambda
doesn’t get back to the usual averaging operator if we substitute
\lambda=0
. However, the identities in (
23
) can be equivalently rephrased as

\displaystyle P(a)P(b)=P(aP(b))=P(P(a)b)~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\lambda P(a)P(b)=\lambda^{2}P(ab),\text{ for }a,b\in A.

Thus, an averaging operator of weight
\lambda
is an averaging operator (in the usual sense) satisfying additionally
\lambda P(a)P(b)=\lambda^{2}P(ab)
, for all
a,b\in A
. With this consideration, an averaging operator on
A
is simply an averaging operator of weight
0
.

3.3 Remark
.

It follows from the above definition that any averaging operator of nonzero weight
\lambda
is intrinsically an averaging operator. However, the converse need not be true. Let
A
be an associative algebra and
e\in\mathrm{Cent}(A)
be any element from the centre of
A
. Then the linear map
P_{e}:A\rightarrow A,~P_{e}(a)=ea
is an averaging operator but in general not an averaging operator of any nonzero weight.

3.4 Example
.

Let A be an associative algebra. Then the identity map
P=\mathrm{id}_{A}:A\rightarrow A
is an averaging operator of weight 1.

3.5 Example
.

Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be two commuting averaging operators of weight
1

3.7 Example

3.8 Example

Let

3.10 Proposition
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
P
be an averaging operator of weight 1 on A. Then for every

, the operator
P^{\phi}={\phi}^{-1}P\phi
is also an averaging operator of weight 1.

Proof.

For all
a,b\in A
, we have

\displaystyle P^{\phi}(aP^{\phi}(b))=P^{\phi}\big(a({\phi}^{-1}P\phi)(b)\big)

\displaystyle={\phi}^{-1}P\phi\big(a({\phi}^{-1}P\phi)(b)\big)

\displaystyle={\phi}^{-1}P\big(\phi(a)P\phi(b)\big)

\displaystyle={\phi}^{-1}\big(P\phi(a)P\phi(b)\big)

\displaystyle=({\phi}^{-1}P\phi)(a)({\phi}^{-1}P\phi)(b)

\displaystyle=P^{\phi}(a)P^{\phi}(b).

Similarly, we can show that
P^{\phi}(P^{\phi}(a)b)=P^{\phi}(a)P^{\phi}(b)

and
P^{\phi}(ab)=P^{\phi}(a)P^{\phi}(b).
This completes the proof.
∎

3.1.
Free averaging algebra of nonzero weight
\lambda

An
averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
is a pair
(A,P)
consisting of an associative algebra
A
equipped with an averaging operator
P:A\rightarrow A
of weight
\lambda
. Let
(A,P)
and
(A^{\prime},P^{\prime})
be two averaging algebras of same weight
\lambda
. A morphism of averaging algebras of weight
\lambda
from
(A,P)
to
(A^{\prime},P^{\prime})
is given by an algebra homomorphism
\varphi:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
that satisfies
P^{\prime}\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ P
. In this subsection, we define the free averaging algebra of any nonzero weight
\lambda
over any nonempty set
X
. This construction is similar to the free averaging algebra given in
[
32
]
.

3.12 Definition
.

Let
X
be a nonempty set. The
free averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
over the set
X
is an averaging algebra
(\mathcal{A}(X),\mathcal{P})
of weight
\lambda
with an inclusion
i:X\rightarrow\mathcal{A}(X)
that satisfies the following universal condition: for any averaging algebra
(A,P)
of weight
\lambda
and a set map
f:X\rightarrow A
, there exists a unique morphism of averaging algebras
\widetilde{f}:(\mathcal{A}(X),\mathcal{P})\rightsquigarrow(A,P)
such that
\widetilde{f}\circ i=f.

Let
X
be a nonempty set. Let
\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)=\mathcal{M}(X)
be the free monoid on
X
and we define
\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)=\mathcal{M}(X\sqcup\lfloor\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)\rfloor)
. For any set
S
, here
\lfloor S\rfloor=\{\lfloor s\rfloor|s\in S\}
is a copy of
S
and the notation
\sqcup
stands for the disjoint union. The inclusion map
X\hookrightarrow X\sqcup\lfloor\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)\rfloor
induces a monomorphism
i_{0}:\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
. Through this map, we can identify
\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)
with its image in
\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
. For each
n\geq 1
, we will now construct a monoid
\mathcal{M}_{n}(X)
and an embedding
i_{n-1}:\mathcal{M}_{n-1}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{n}(X)
as follows. Suppose the monoid
\mathcal{M}_{n-1}(X)
has been constructed with the embedding
i_{n-2}:\mathcal{M}_{n-2}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{n-1}(X)
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}(X)=\mathcal{M}(X\sqcup\lfloor\mathcal{M}_{n-1}(X)\rfloor).

Since
\mathcal{M}_{n-1}(X)=\mathcal{M}(X\sqcup\lfloor\mathcal{M}_{n-2}(X)\rfloor)
is the free monoid on the set
X\sqcup\lfloor\mathcal{M}_{n-2}(X)\rfloor
, the injection map
X\sqcup\lfloor\mathcal{M}_{n-2}(X)\rfloor\hookrightarrow X\sqcup\lfloor\mathcal{M}_{n-1}(X)\rfloor
induces an embedding

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n-1}(X)=\mathcal{M}(X\sqcup\lfloor\mathcal{M}_{n-2}(X)\rfloor)\xrightarrow{i_{n}}\mathcal{M}(X\sqcup\lfloor\mathcal{M}_{n-1}(X)\rfloor)=\mathcal{M}_{n}(X).

We define the monoid
\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}
as the direct limit of the monoids
\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)\subset\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}(X)\subset\cdots.
That is,
\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{M}_{n}(X)=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{n}(X)
. The elements of
\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}
are often called bracketed words or bracketed monomials on
X
. Let
\mathbb{K}[\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}]
be the free vector space over the set
\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}
. Note that the monoid product (concatenation product) on
\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}
can be extended to a multiplication on
\mathbb{K}[\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}]
that makes
\mathbb{K}[\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}]
into an associative algebra. On the other hand, the map
\lfloor~\rfloor:\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}
,
x\mapsto\lfloor x\rfloor
, for
x\in\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}
can be extended to a linear map (which we denote by the same notation)
\lfloor~\rfloor:\mathbb{K}[\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}]\rightarrow\mathbb{K}[\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}]
. Define a subset
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{K}[\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}]
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\big\{\lfloor x\rfloor\lfloor y\rfloor-\lfloor\lfloor x\rfloor y\rfloor,~\lfloor x\rfloor\lfloor y\rfloor-\lfloor x\lfloor y\rfloor\rfloor,~\lfloor x\rfloor\lfloor y\rfloor-\lambda\lfloor xy\rfloor~|~x,y\in\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}\big\}.

Let
I
be the smallest operated ideal of
\mathbb{K}[\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}]
containing the set
\mathcal{S}
. Define
\mathcal{A}(X)=\mathbb{K}[\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}]/I
and a linear map
\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{A}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}(X)
induced by the map
\lfloor~\rfloor
. Then it turns out that
(\mathcal{A}(X),\mathcal{P})
is an averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
.

Let
i:X\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}(X)
be the composition of the maps

\displaystyle X\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)\subset\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}(X)\subset\cdots\subset\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{n}(X)=\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}\subset\mathbb{K}[\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}]\xrightarrow{q}\mathcal{A}(X).

Let
(A,P)
be any relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
and
f:X\rightarrow A
be a set map. We define a map
\overline{f}:\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}\rightarrow A
as follows. For
w=x_{1}\cdots x_{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}
, where
x_{i}\in X~(1\leq i\leq m)
, we define

\overline{f}(w)=f(x_{1})\cdots f(x_{m})
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
is the free monoid on the set
X\sqcup\lfloor\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)\rfloor
, we can define
\overline{f}
on the elements of
\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
. Suppose we are able to define
\overline{f}
on the elements of
\mathcal{M}_{n-1}(X)
. As
\mathcal{M}_{n}(X)
is the free monoid on the set
X\sqcup\lfloor\mathcal{M}_{n-1}(X)\rfloor
, we can define
\overline{f}
on the elements of
\mathcal{M}_{n}(X)
. It is easy to see that the map
\overline{f}:\overline{\mathcal{M}(X)}\rightarrow A
induces a map
\widetilde{f}:\mathcal{A}(X)\rightarrow A
. The map
\widetilde{f}
is obviously an algebra morphism that satisfies
\widetilde{f}\circ i=f
and
P\circ\widetilde{f}=\widetilde{f}\circ\mathcal{P}
. This shows that
(\mathcal{A}(X),\mathcal{P})
is a free averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
over the set
X
.

4.
Relative averaging operators of nonzero weight

In this section, we consider a notion of the relative averaging operator of weight
\lambda
as a generalization of the averaging operator of weight
\lambda
. We give some characterizations of such operators in terms of their graphs. Moreover, given some suitable structures, we construct a differential graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely relative averaging operators of nonzero weight
\lambda
. Using this characterization, we also define the cohomology associated with a relative averaging operator of weight
\lambda
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra. An associative
A
-bimodule is an associative algebra
B_{\nu}
(i.e.,
\nu:B\otimes B\rightarrow B,(x,y)\mapsto xy
is a linear map satisfying the associativity) equipped with two linear maps
l:A\otimes B\rightarrow B
,
(a,x)\mapsto a\cdot x
and
r:B\otimes A\mapsto B
,
(x,a)\mapsto x\cdot a
, satisfying for
a,b\in A
and
x,y\in A
,

\displaystyle(ab)\cdot x=~

\displaystyle a\cdot(b\cdot x),\qquad(a\cdot x)\cdot b=a\cdot(x\cdot b),\qquad(x\cdot a)\cdot b=x\cdot(ab),

(25)

\displaystyle(xy)\cdot a=~

\displaystyle x(y\cdot a),\qquad(x\cdot a)y=x(a\cdot y),\qquad(a\cdot x)y=a\cdot(xy).

(26)

In this case, we often say that the associative algebra
A
acts on the algebra
B_{\nu}
and
l,r
being the left and right
A
-actions on
B_{\nu}
. We denote an associative
A
-bimodule as above by
B_{\nu}^{l,r}
or simply by
B
when the associative multiplication
\nu
of
B
and the left and right
A
-actions on
B_{\nu}
are clear from the context. It follows from the above definition that any associative algebra
A
can be realized as an associative
A
-bimodule called the adjoint
A
-bimodule.

4.1 Definition
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
B
be an associative
A
-bimodule. A linear map
P:B\rightarrow A
is said to be a
relative averaging operator of nonzero weight

\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
if

\displaystyle P(x)P(y)=P(P(x)\cdot y)=P(x\cdot P(y))=\lambda P(xy),\text{ for all }x,y\in B.

It follows that any averaging operator of weight
\lambda
on an associative algebra
A
can be realized as a relative averaging operator of weight
\lambda
. Thus, a relative averaging operator of weight
\lambda
is a generalization of an averaging operator of weight
\lambda
.

4.8 Proposition
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
B
be an associative
A
-bimodule. Then the direct sum
A\oplus B
inherits a triassociative algebra structure with the operations

\displaystyle(a,x)\dashv(b,y):=(ab,x\cdot b),\quad(a,x)\vdash(b,y):=(ab,a\cdot y)~~\text{ and }~~(a,x)\perp(b,y):=(ab,\lambda xy),

for
(a,x),(b,y)\in A\oplus B.
We denote this triassociative algebra by
A\oplus_{\lambda}B
.

Proof.

For any
(a,x),(b,y),(c,z)\in A\oplus B
, we have

\displaystyle((a,x)\dashv(b,y))\dashv(c,z)=~

\displaystyle(ab,x\cdot b)\dashv(c,z)=((ab)c,(x\cdot b)\cdot c),

\displaystyle(a,x)\dashv((b,y)\dashv(c,z))=~

\displaystyle(a,x)\dashv(bc,y\cdot c)=(a(bc),x\cdot(bc)),

\displaystyle(a,x)\dashv((b,y)\vdash(c,z))=~

\displaystyle(a,x)\dashv(bc,b\cdot z)=(a(bc),x\cdot(bc)),

\displaystyle(a,x)\dashv((b,y)\perp(c,z))=~

\displaystyle(a,x)\dashv(bc,\lambda yz)=(a(bc),x\cdot(bc)).

Since
A
is associative algebra and
B
is an associative
A
bimodule, we have

\displaystyle((a,x)\dashv(b,y))\dashv(c,z)=(a,x)\dashv((b,y)\dashv(c,z))=(a,x)\dashv((b,y)\vdash(c,z))=(a,x)\dashv((b,y)\perp(c,z)).

Similarly, we can show that

\displaystyle(a,x)\vdash((b,y)\vdash(c,z))=((a,x)\vdash(b,y))\vdash(c,z))=((a,x)\dashv(b,y))\vdash(c,z)=((a,x)\perp(b,y))\vdash(c,z).

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle((a,x)\vdash(b,y))\dashv(c,z)=((ab)c,(a\cdot y)\cdot c)=(a(bc),a\cdot(y\cdot c))=(a,x)\vdash((b,y)\dashv(c,z)),

\displaystyle((a,x)\perp(b,y))\dashv(c,z)=((ab)c,\lambda(xy)\cdot c)=(a(bc),\lambda x(y\cdot c))=(a,x)\perp((b,y)\dashv(c,z)).

Similarly, one can show that

\displaystyle((a,x)\dashv(b,y))\perp(c,z)=~

\displaystyle(a,x)\perp((b,y)\vdash(c,z)),

\displaystyle((a,x)\vdash(b,y))\perp(c,z)=~

\displaystyle(a,x)\vdash((b,y)\perp(c,z)),

\displaystyle((a,x)\perp(b,y))\perp(c,z)=~

\displaystyle(a,x)\perp((b,y)\perp(c,z)).

Hence the result follows.
∎

4.9 Remark
.

The converse of the above proposition also holds true. More precisely, suppose
A
and
B
are two vector spaces equipped with the linear maps

\displaystyle\mu:A\otimes A\rightarrow A,\quad\nu:B\otimes B\rightarrow B,\quad l:A\otimes B\rightarrow B~~~\text{ and }~~~~r:B\otimes A\rightarrow A.

Then we may define three maps
\dashv,\vdash,\perp:(A\oplus B)\otimes(A\oplus B)\rightarrow(A\oplus B)
by

\displaystyle(a,x)\dashv(b,y)=(\mu(a,b),r(x,b)),~~~~(a,x)\vdash(b,y)=(\mu(a,b),l(a,y))~~\text{ and }~~(a,x)\perp(b,y)=(\mu(a,b),\lambda\nu(x,y)),

for
(a,x),(b,y)\in A\oplus B
.
With these notations, the maps
\dashv,\vdash,\perp
make
A\oplus B
into a triassociative algebra if and only if
A_{\mu}
is an associative algebra and
B^{l,r}_{\nu}
is an associative
A_{\mu}
-bimodule.

4.10 Proposition
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
B
be an associative
A
-bimodule. Then a linear map

is a relative averaging operator of weight
\lambda
if and only if the graph
Gr(P)=\{(P(x),x)|~x\in B\}

is a relative averaging operator of weight
\lambda
.

We often denote an averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
as above by the notation
(A_{\mu},B_{\nu}^{l,r},P)
when the underlying structures are required to mention.

5.2 Remark
.

Let
(A,P)
be an averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
. Then
(A,A,P)
is a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
, where
A
is equipped with the adjoint
A
-bimodule structure.

5.3 Definition
.

Let
(A,B,P)
and
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime},P^{\prime})
be two relative averaging algebras of weight
\lambda
. A
morphism
of relative averaging algebras of weight
\lambda
from
(A,B,P)
to
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime},P^{\prime})
is given by a pair
(\varphi,\psi)
of algebra homomorphisms
\varphi:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
and
\psi:B\rightarrow B^{\prime}
satisfying additionally

\displaystyle\psi(a\cdot x)=\varphi(a)\cdot^{\prime}\psi(x),\quad\psi(x\cdot a)=\psi(x)\cdot^{\prime}\varphi(a)~~~\text{ and }~~~\varphi\circ P=P^{\prime}\circ\psi,\text{ for }a\in A,x\in B.

Here
\cdot^{\prime}
denotes both the left and right
A^{\prime}
-actions on
B^{\prime}
.

The collection of all relative averaging algebras of weight
\lambda
and morphisms between them forms a category, denoted by
{\bf rAvg_{\lambda}}
. If
(A,B,P)
is a relative averaging algebra of any nonzero weight
\lambda
then
(A,B,\frac{1}{\lambda}P)
is a relative averaging algebra of weight
1
and vice-versa. Therefore, for any
\lambda\neq 0
, the category
{\bf rAvg_{\lambda}}
is equivalent to the category
{\bf rAvg_{1}}
of relative averaging algebras of weight
1
.

5.4 Proposition
.

(i) Let
(A,B,P)
be a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
. Then the vector space
B
with the operations

\displaystyle x\dashv_{P}y:=x\cdot P(y),\quad x\vdash_{P}y:=P(x)\cdot y~~~\text{ and }~~~x\perp_{P}y:=\lambda xy,\text{ for }x,y\in B,

(30)

is a triassociative algebra. The triassociative algebra
(B,\dashv_{P},\vdash_{P},\perp_{P})
is said to be induced from the given relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
.

(ii) Let
(A,B,P)
and
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime},P^{\prime})
be two relative averaging algebras of weight
\lambda
, and
(\varphi,\psi)
be a morphism between them. Then the linear map
\psi:B\rightarrow B^{\prime}
is a morphism of induced triassociative algebras from
(B,\dashv_{P},\vdash_{P},\perp_{P})
to
(B^{\prime},\dashv_{P^{\prime}},\vdash_{P^{\prime}},\perp_{P^{\prime}})
.

Proof.

(i) Since
P
is a relative averaging operator of weight
\lambda
, it follows that
Gr(P)
is a subalgebra of the triassociative algebra
A\oplus_{\lambda}B
(cf. Proposition
4.10
). As
Gr(P)
is isomorphic to
B
, the space
B
inherits a triassociative algebra structure. The triassociative structure is precisely given by (
30
).

(ii) For any
x,y\in B
, we have

\displaystyle\psi(x\dashv_{P}y)=\psi(x\cdot P(y))=\psi(x)\cdot^{\prime}\varphi P(y)=\psi(x)\cdot^{\prime}P^{\prime}\psi(y)=\psi(x)\dashv_{P^{\prime}}\psi(y),

\displaystyle\psi(x\vdash_{P}y)=\psi(P(x)\cdot y)=\varphi P(x)\cdot^{\prime}\psi(y)=P^{\prime}\psi(x)\cdot^{\prime}\psi(y)=\psi(x)\vdash_{P^{\prime}}\psi(y),

\displaystyle\psi(x\perp_{P}y)=\psi(\lambda xy)=\lambda\psi(x)\psi(y)=\psi(x)\perp_{P^{\prime}}\psi(y).

This completes the proof.
∎

The above proposition shows that there is a functor
\mathcal{F}
from the category
{\bf rAvg_{\lambda}}
(in particular from the category
{\bf rAvg_{\lambda}}
) to the category
{\bf Triass}
of triassociative algebras. In the following, we construct a functor from the category
\bf{Triass}
to the category
\bf{rAvg_{1}}
of relative averaging algebras of weight 1.

We start with a triassociative algebra
(D,\dashv,\vdash,\perp)
. Let
I
be the ideal of
D
generated by the set
\big\{x\dashv y-x\vdash y~\big|~x,y\in D\big\}
. Then the quotient space
D/I
has the obvious associative algebra structure with the multiplication

\displaystyle[x][y]:=[x\dashv y]=[x\vdash y],\text{ for }[x],[y]\in D/I.

We denote this associative algebra simply by
D_{ass}
. On the other hand, the vector space
D
has the associative product
\perp
. Denote this associative algebra
D_{\perp}
. Moreover, the associative algebra
D_{\perp}
can be given an associative
D_{ass}
-bimodule structure with the left and right
D_{ass}
-actions given by

[x]\cdot y=x\vdash y
and
y\cdot[x]=y\dashv x
, for
[x]\in D_{ass},~y\in D_{\perp}
.

With these notations, the quotient map
q:D_{\perp}\rightarrow D_{ass}
,
q(x)=[x]
, for
x\in D_{\perp}
, is a relative averaging operator of weight 1. In other words,
(D_{ass},D_{\perp},q)
is a relative averaging algebra of weight
1
. Moreover, the induced triassociative algebra structure on
D
coincides with the given one.

Let
(D,\dashv,\vdash,\perp)
and
(D^{\prime},{\dashv}^{\prime},{\vdash}^{\prime},{\perp}^{\prime})
be two triassociative algebras and
\psi:D\rightarrow D^{\prime}
be a morphism between them. Then it can check that the pair

\displaystyle(\varphi^{\psi},\psi):(D_{ass},D_{\perp},q)\rightarrow(D^{\prime}_{ass},D^{\prime}_{\perp^{\prime}},q^{\prime})

is a morphism of relative averaging algebras of weight
1
, where
\varphi^{\psi}([x])=[\psi(x)]
for
[x]\in D_{ass}
. These constructions yield a functor
\mathcal{G}
from the category
{\bf Triass}
to the category
{\bf rAvg_{1}}
. Moreover, we have the following result whose proof is straightforward.

5.5 Proposition
.

The functor
\mathcal{G}
is left adjoint to the functor
\mathcal{F}
. More precisely, for any triassociative algebra
(D,\dashv,\vdash,\perp)
and an averaging algebra
(A,B,P)
of weight
1
, there is a bijection

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\bf Triass}\big((D,\dashv,\vdash,\perp),(B,\dashv_{P},\vdash_{P},\perp_{P})\big)~\cong~\mathrm{Hom}_{\bf rAvg_{1}}\big((D_{ass},D_{\perp},q),(A,B,P)\big).

5.6 Remark
.

In
[
3
]
the authors have considered the relative Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\lambda
as a generalization of a Rota-Baxter algebra. Let
A
be an associative algebra and
B
an associative
A
-bimodule. A linear map
R:B\rightarrow A
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
if it satisfies

\displaystyle R(x)R(y)=R(R(x)\cdot y+x\cdot R(y)+\lambda xy),\text{ for }x,y\in B.

(31)

In this case, the triple
(A,B,R)
is called a relative Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\lambda
. In
[

Ebrahimi-Fard showed that a relative Rota-Baxter algebra of weight

induces a tridendriform algebra structure on

\displaystyle x\prec y=x\cdot R(y),\quad x\succ y=R(x)\cdot y~~~~\text{ and }~~~~x\curlyvee y=\lambda xy,\text{ for }x,y\in B.

(See
[
24

for the definition of a tridendriform algebra). Further, it follows from (

\lambda
is a relative Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
-\lambda
. Next, suppose we start with a triassociative algebra
(D,\dashv,\vdash,\perp)
. Then we have seen earlier that
(D_{ass},D_{\perp},q)
is a relative averaging algebra of weight

(D_{ass},D_{\perp},q)
is a relative Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
-1

carries a tridendriform algebra structure with the operations

\displaystyle x\prec y:=x\dashv y,\quad x\succ y:=x\vdash y~~~\text{ and }~~~x\curlyvee y:=-x\perp y,\text{ for }x,y\in D.

With this tridendriform algebra (induced from a triassociative algebra), the diagram given in (
1.3

5.1.
Some background on homotopy Lie algebras and Maurer-Cartan characterization of relative averaging algebras of nonzero weight

In this subsection, we mainly recall some definitions regarding
L_{\infty}
-algebras (strongly homotopy Lie algebras). Throughout the present paper, we follow the definition of a
L_{\infty}
-algebra given in
[
21
]
.

5.7 Definition
.

A
L_{\infty}
-algebra
is a pair
(\mathcal{L},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
consisting of a graded vector space
\mathcal{L}=\displaystyle\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{L}_{i}
equipped with a collection
\{l_{k}:{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes k}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of degree
1
graded symmetric linear maps satisfying the following higher Jacobi identities:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1}~\sum_{\sigma\in Sh(i,n-i)}l_{j}\big(l_{i}(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(i)}),x_{\sigma(i+1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)}\big)=0,

(32)

for all
n\geq 1
and homogeneous elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{L}
.

Any (differential) graded Lie algebra can be realized as a
L_{\infty}
-algebra by a degree shift. More precisely, let
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],d)
be a differential graded Lie algebra. Then
(s^{-1}\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}))
is a
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where

\displaystyle l_{1}(s^{-1}x)=s^{-1}(dx),~~~l_{2}(s^{-1}x,s^{-1}y)=(-1)^{\lvert x\rvert}s^{-1}[x,y]~~\text{ and }~~l_{k}=0\text{ for }k\geq 3.

A
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{L},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
is said to be weakly filtered if there exists a descending filtration
\mathcal{L}={\mathcal{F}}_{1}\mathcal{L}\supset{\mathcal{F}}_{2}\mathcal{L}\supset\cdots\supset{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\mathcal{L}\supset\cdots
and a natural number
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\lim{\mathcal{L}/{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\mathcal{L}}~~\text{ and }~~l_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\mathcal{L},\text{ for all }k\geq N\text{ and }x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{L}.

Weakly filtered
L_{\infty}
-algebras are useful to make sense of certain infinite sums in
\mathcal{L}

[
15
]
. Throughout our paper, we assume that all
L_{\infty}
-algebras are weakly filtered.

5.8 Definition
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be a
L_{\infty}
-algebra. An element
\alpha\in\mathcal{L}_{0}
is said to be a
Maurer-Cartan element
of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra if

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{k!}~l_{k}(\alpha,\ldots,\alpha)=0.

If
\alpha
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{L},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
then one can construct a new
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{L},\{{l^{\alpha}_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
on the same graded vector space
\mathcal{L}
. The structure maps
\{{l^{\alpha}_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
for this new
L_{\infty}
-algebra are given by

\displaystyle{l^{\alpha}_{k}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{n!}~l_{n+k}(\alpha,\ldots,\alpha,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}),\text{ for }x_{1},\ldots x_{k}\in\mathcal{L}.

The
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{L},\{{l^{\alpha}_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
is said to be obtained from
(\mathcal{L},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
twisted by the Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha
. For any
\alpha^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
, the sum
\alpha+{\alpha}^{\prime}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{L},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
if and only if
\alpha^{\prime}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathcal{L},\{{l^{\alpha}_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
.

Next, we recall Voronov’s construction of a
L_{\infty}
-algebra
[
37
]
. Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a},p,\Delta)
be a quadruple consisting of a graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(with the bracket [ , ]), an abelian Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, a projection map
p:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathrm{ker}(p)\subset\mathfrak{g}
is a graded Lie subalgebra and
\mathrm{im}(p)=\mathfrak{a}
, and an element
\Delta\in\mathrm{ker}{(p)}_{1}
that satisfies
[\Delta,\Delta]=0
. Such a quadruple is called a
V
-data
.

5.9 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a},p,\Delta)
be a
V
-data.

(i) Then the graded vector space
\mathfrak{a}
carries a
L_{\infty}
-algebra with the structure maps
\{l_{k}:{\mathfrak{a}}^{\otimes k}\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
given by

\displaystyle l_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=p[\cdots[[\Delta,a_{1}],a_{2}],\ldots,a_{k}],~~for~~k\geq 1.

(ii) Let
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be a graded Lie subalgebra that satisfies
[\Delta,\mathfrak{h}]\subset\mathfrak{h}
. Then the graded vector space
s^{-1}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
carries a

-algebra with the structure maps

\displaystyle l_{1}\big((s^{-1}x,a)\big)=~

\displaystyle\big(-s^{-1}[\Delta,x],~p(x+[\Delta,a])\big),

\displaystyle l_{2}\big((s^{-1}x,0),(s^{-1}y,0)\big)=~

\displaystyle\big((-1)^{\lvert x\rvert}s^{-1}[x,y],0\big),

\displaystyle l_{k}\big((s^{-1}x,0),(0,a_{1}),\ldots,(0,a_{k-1})\big)=~

\displaystyle\big(0,p~[\cdots[[x,a_{1}],a_{2}],\ldots,a_{k-1}]\big),~~for~k\geq 2,

\displaystyle l_{k}\big((0,a_{1}),\ldots,(0,a_{k})\big)=~

\displaystyle\big(0,p~[\cdots[[\Delta,a_{1}],a_{2}],\ldots,a_{k}]\big),~~for~k\geq 2.

A

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\big(\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}C^{n+1}(A\oplus B,A\oplus B)=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{K}[T_{n+1}]\otimes(A\oplus B)^{\otimes n+1},A\oplus B),[~,~]\big)

if and only if
(s^{-1}{\pi_{\lambda}},P)
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(s^{-1}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
constructed above. In other words,

\displaystyle(A_{\mu},B_{\nu}^{l,r},P)\text{ is a relative averaging algebra of weight }\lambda~

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow~(s^{-1}{\pi_{\lambda}},P)\text{ is a Maurer-Cartan element of }(s^{-1}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}).

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle l_{2}\big((s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P),(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P)\big)=\big(-s^{-1}[\pi_{\lambda},\pi_{\lambda}],2l_{2}((s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},0),(0,P))\big)=(-s^{-1}[\pi_{\lambda},\pi_{\lambda}],2[\pi_{\lambda},P]).

On the other hand, it is straightforward to observe that

[[[\pi_{\lambda},P],P],P]=0
. As a consequence,

\displaystyle l_{k}\big((s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P),\ldots,(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P)\big)=0,\text{ for }k\geq 4.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}l_{k}\big((s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P),\ldots,(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}l_{2}\big((s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P),(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P)\big)+\frac{1}{6}l_{3}\big((s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P),(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P),(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(-s^{-1}[\pi_{\lambda},\pi_{\lambda}],2[\pi_{\lambda},P]\big)+\frac{1}{6}\big(0,3[[\pi_{\lambda},P],P]\big)

\displaystyle=\big(-\frac{1}{2}s^{-1}[\pi_{\lambda},\pi_{\lambda}],[\pi_{\lambda},P]+\frac{1}{2}[[\pi_{\lambda},P],P]\big).

This shows that
(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P)\in(s^{-1}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a})_{0}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra if and only if
[\pi_{\lambda},\pi_{\lambda}]=0
(i.e.
A_{\mu}
is an associative algebra and
B_{\nu}^{l,r}
is an associative
A_{\mu}
-bimodule) and

\displaystyle[\pi_{\lambda},P]+\frac{1}{2}[[\pi_{\lambda},P],P]=0

(i.e.
P
is a relative averaging operator of weight
\lambda
(cf. Theorem
4.13
)). This completes the proof.
∎

Let
(A_{\mu},B_{\nu}^{l,r},{P})
be a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
with
\alpha=(s^{-1}{\pi_{\lambda}},P)
being the corresponding Maurer-Cartan element of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(s^{-1}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
. Hence one can construct the new
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(s^{-1}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}^{(s^{-1}{\pi_{\lambda}},P)}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
twisted by the Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha=(s^{-1}{\pi_{\lambda}},P)
.

5.12 Theorem
.

Let
(A_{\mu},B_{\nu}^{l,r},{P})
be a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
. Then for any linear maps

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}:A\otimes A\rightarrow A,\quad\nu^{\prime}:B\otimes B\rightarrow B,\quad l^{\prime}:A\otimes B\rightarrow B,\quad r^{\prime}:B\otimes A\rightarrow B~~~~\text{ and }~~~~P^{\prime}:B\rightarrow A,

the structure
(A_{\mu+\mu^{\prime}},B_{\nu+\nu^{\prime}}^{l+l^{\prime},r+r^{\prime}},{P+P^{\prime}})
is a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
if and only if
(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda}^{\prime},P^{\prime})\in(s^{-1}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a})_{0}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the twisted
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(s^{-1}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}^{(s^{-1}{\pi_{\lambda}},P)}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
.

The above proposition says that the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(s^{-1}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}^{(s^{-1}{\pi_{\lambda}},P)}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
controlls the Maurer-Cartan deformations of the given relative averaging algebra
(A_{\mu},B_{\nu}^{l,r},{P})
of weight
\lambda
. For this reason, we call the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(s^{-1}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}^{(s^{-1}{\pi_{\lambda}},P)}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
as the controlling algebra for the deformations of
(A_{\mu},B_{\nu}^{l,r},P)
.

6.
Cohomology of (relative) averaging algebras of nonzero weight

In this section, we define the cohomology of a relative averaging algebra of nonzero weight
\lambda
. In particular, we describe the cohomology of an averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
. Finally, given a relative averaging algebra
(A,B,{P})
of weight
\lambda
, we find a long exact sequence connecting the cohomology groups of the operator
P
and the cohomology groups of the full relative averaging algebra
(A,B,{P})
of weight
\lambda
.

Let
(A,B,{P})
be a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
with the corresponding Maurer-Cartan element
(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P)
. For each
n\geq 0
, we define the space
C^{n}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)
of
n
-cochains by
C^{0}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)=0
,
C^{1}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)=\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(B,B)
and

\displaystyle C^{n\geq 2}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)=\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(B^{\otimes n},B)\oplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}k+l=n+1\\
k,l\geq 1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}^{k,l},B)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{K}[T_{n-1}]\otimes B^{\otimes n-1},A).

6.1 Remark
.

(i) Note that an element
(f,g)\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)
corresponds to the element
(s^{-1}(f+g),0)\in(s^{-1}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a})_{-1}
.

(ii) An element
(f,g,h,\gamma)\in C^{n\geq 2}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)
gives rise to an element
(s^{-1}(\pi_{f,g,h}),\gamma)\in(s^{-1}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a})_{n-2}
, where
\pi_{f,g,h}\in\mathfrak{h}_{n-1}
is given by

\displaystyle(\pi_{f,g,h})(T;(a_{1},x_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n},x_{n}))=\big(f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}),\lambda^{k-2}(g+h)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i_{1}-1},x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{k-1}},\ldots,a_{n})\big),

when
T=T^{i_{1}-1}\vee\cdots\vee T^{i_{k}-1}
for some unique
(i_{1}-1)
-tree
T^{i_{1}-1}\in T_{i_{1}-1},\ldots,(i_{k}-1)
-tree
T^{i_{k}-1}\in T_{i_{k}-1}
. This is in fact an embedding of the space
C^{n\geq 2}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)
inside
(s^{-1}\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{a})_{n-2}.

Using the above embeddings, we define a map
\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)
by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(f,g)=~

\displaystyle-l_{1}^{(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P)}(s^{-1}({f}+{g}),0),\text{ for }(f,g)\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P),

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(f,g,h,\gamma)=~

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-2}~l_{1}^{(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P)}(s^{-1}(\pi_{f,g,h}),\gamma),\text{ for }(f,g,h,\gamma)\in C^{n\geq 2}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P).

6.2 Proposition
.

With the above notations, we have
(\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}})^{2}=0
. That is,
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P),\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}\}
is a cochain complex.

Proof.

Since the controlling algebra
(s^{-1}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}^{(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P)}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
is a
L_{\infty}
-algebra, it follows that
(l_{1}^{(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P)})^{2}=0
.
∎

Let
(A,B,P)
be a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
. Then the cohomology of the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P),\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}\}
is called the
cohomology
of
(A,B,P)
. We denote the corresponding
n
-th cohomology group by
H^{n}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)
.

Note that the map
\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}
can be described as

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(f,g,h,\gamma)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-2}~l_{1}^{(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P)}\big(s^{-1}(\pi_{f,g,h}),\gamma\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}l_{k+1}\big(\underbrace{(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P),\ldots,(s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},P)}_{k~\mathrm{many}},(s^{-1}(\pi_{f,g,h}),\gamma)\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-2}\bigg\{l_{2}\big((s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},0),(s^{-1}(\pi_{f,g,h}),0)\big)+l_{2}\big((s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},0),(0,\gamma)\big)+l_{3}\big((s^{-1}\pi_{\lambda},0),(0,P),(0,\gamma)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{n!}l_{n+1}\big((s^{-1}(\pi_{f,g,h}),0),(0,P),\ldots,(0,P)\big)\bigg\}\quad(\text{all other terms are zero})

\displaystyle=\big((-1)^{n-1}s^{-1}[\pi_{\lambda},\pi_{f,g,h}],~\underbrace{(-1)^{n}[\pi_{\lambda},\gamma]+(-1)^{n}[[\pi_{\lambda},P],\gamma]}_{=(-1)^{n-1}d_{P}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}[\cdots[[\pi_{f,g,h},P],P],\ldots,P]\big).

Thus, using the above identifications, the coboundary map
\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}
can be explicitly given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(f,g,h,\gamma)=\big(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{A}(f),\delta^{B}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(g),\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{f,g}(h),(-1)^{n-1}d_{P}(\gamma)+\theta_{P}(f,g,h)\big).

Here
\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{A}
(resp.
\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{B}
) is the Hochschild coboundary map of the associative algebra
A
(resp.
B
). For any
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)
and
g\in\mathrm{Hom}(B^{\otimes n},B)
, the map

\displaystyle\delta^{f,g}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}:\oplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}k+l=n\\
k,l\geq 1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}^{k,l},B)\rightarrow\oplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}k+l=n+1\\
k,l\geq 1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}^{k,l},B)~\text{ is given by }

\displaystyle\big(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{f,g}(h)\big)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})=~

\displaystyle(\nu+l+r)(a_{1},(f+g+h)(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}(h+\nu)\big(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},(\mu+\nu+l+r)(a_{i},a_{i+1}),\ldots,a_{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}(\nu+l+r)((f+g+h)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}),a_{n+1}),

for
a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1}\in\mathcal{A}^{k,l}
with
k+l=n+1
and
k,l\geq 1
. The map
d_{P}
is the coboundary operator of the relative averaging operator
P
of weight
\lambda
. Finally, for any
T\in T_{n}
(say
T=T^{i_{1}-1}\vee\cdots\vee T^{i_{k}-1}
for some unique
(i_{1}-1)
-tree
T^{i_{1}-1}\in T_{i_{1}-1},\ldots,(i_{k}-1)
-tree
T^{i_{k}-1}\in T_{i_{k}-1}
), the map
\theta_{P}(f,g,h)\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{K}[T_{n}]\otimes B^{n},A)
is given by

\displaystyle(\theta_{P}(f,g,h))(T;x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=(-1)^{n}\big\{f\big(P(x_{1}),\ldots,P(x_{n})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad-\lambda^{k-2}P(g+h)\big(P(x_{1}),\ldots,P(x_{i_{1}-1}),x_{i_{1}},P(x_{i_{1}+1}),\ldots,x_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{k-1}},\ldots,P(x_{n})\big)\big\}.

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
B
be an associative
A
-bimodule. Then one may consider the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{AssAct}}(A,B),\delta_{\mathrm{AssAct}}\}
, where
C^{0}_{\mathrm{AssAct}}(A,B)=0
,
C^{1}_{\mathrm{AssAct}}(A,B)=\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(B,B)
and

\displaystyle C^{n\geq 2}_{\mathrm{AssAct}}(A,B)=\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(B^{\otimes n},B)\oplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}k+l=n+1\\
k,l\geq 1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}^{k,l},B).

The coboundary map
\delta_{\mathrm{AssAct}}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{AssAct}}(A,B)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{AssAct}}(A,B)
is given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{AssAct}}(f,g,h)=(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{A}(f),\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{B}(g),\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{A,B}(h)).

We denote the
n
-th cohomology of the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{AssAct}}(A,B),\delta_{\mathrm{AssAct}}\}
by
H^{n}_{\mathrm{AssAct}}(A,B)
.

6.3 Theorem
.

Let
(A,B,P)
be a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
. Then there is a long exact sequence

\displaystyle\cdots\longrightarrow H^{n-1}_{P}(B,A)\longrightarrow H^{n}_{\mathrm{rAvg_{\lambda}}}(A,B,P)\longrightarrow H^{n}_{\mathrm{AssAct}}(A,B)\longrightarrow H^{n}_{P}(B,A)\longrightarrow\cdots.

(33)

Proof.

There is an obvious short exact sequence of cochain complexes

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\{C^{\bullet-1}(B,A),d_{P}\}\longrightarrow\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{rAvg_{\lambda}}}(A,B,P),\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg_{\lambda}}}\}\longrightarrow\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{AssAct}}(A,B),\delta_{\mathrm{AssAct}}\}\longrightarrow 0.

This yields the long exact sequence (
33
) on the level of cohomology.
∎

6.1.
Cohomology of an averaging algebra of any nonzero weight

In this subsection, we introduce the cohomology of an averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
using the general theory of relative averaging algebras. Subsequently, we also find a long exact sequence connecting cohomology groups.

Let
(A,P)
be an averaging algebra of nonzero weight
\lambda
. We define
C^{0}_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}(A,P)=0
,
C^{1}_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}(A,P)=\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)
and

\displaystyle C^{n\geq 2}_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}(A,P)=\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{K}[T_{n-1}]\otimes A^{\otimes n-1},A).

Note that the averaging algebra
(A,P)
of weight
\lambda
can be seen as a relative averaging algebra
(A,A,{P})
of weight
\lambda
(see Remark
5.2
). Then for each
n\geq 0
, there is an embedding

\displaystyle i:C^{n}_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}(A,P)\hookrightarrow C^{n}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,A,P),~(f,\gamma)\mapsto(f,f,f,\gamma).

Further, it can be easily checked that
\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(\mathrm{im}(i))\subset\mathrm{im}(i)
. This implies that the coboundary map
\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,A,P)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,A,P)
induces a coboundary map (which we denote by
\delta_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}
)

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}(A,P)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}(A,P)\text{ for }n\geq 0.

The map
\delta_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}
is explicitly given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}(f,\gamma)=(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{A}(f),(-1)^{n-1}d_{P}(\gamma)+\theta_{P}(f)),\text{ for }(f,\gamma)\in C^{n}_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}(A,P).

The cohomology of the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}(A,P),\delta_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}\}
is called the cohomology of the relative averaging algebra
(A,P)
of weight
\lambda
. We denote the corresponding
n
-th cohomology group by
H^{n}_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}(A,P)
.

6.4 Theorem
.

Let
(A,P)
be a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
. Then there is a long exact sequence

\displaystyle\cdots\longrightarrow H^{n-1}_{P}(A,A)\longrightarrow H^{n}_{\mathrm{Avg}_{\lambda}}(A,P)\longrightarrow H^{n}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(A)\rightarrow H^{n}_{P}(A,A)\longrightarrow\cdots.

Here
{H^{n}_{P}(A,A)}
is the
n
-th cohomology group of the averaging operator
P
of weight
\lambda
and
H^{n}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(A)
is the
n
-th Hochschild cohomology group of the associative algebra
A
.

7.
Infinitesimal deformations of averaging algebras

In this section, we study infinitesimal deformations of a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
. In particular, we show that the set of all equivalence classes of infinitesimal deformations of a relative averaging algebra
(A,B,P)
of weight
\lambda
has a bijection with the second cohomology group
H^{2}_{\mathrm{rAvg_{\lambda}}}(A,B,P)
.

Let
(A_{\mu},B_{\nu}^{l,r},P)
be a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
. Consider the space
A[[t]]/(t^{2})
(resp.
B[[t]]/(t^{2})
) whose elements are of the form
a+tb
, for
a,b\in A
(resp.
x+ty
, for
x,y\in B
). Then
A[[t]]/(t^{2})
and
B[[t]]/(t^{2})
are both
\mathbb{K}[[t]]/(t^{2})
-modules.

7.1 Definition
.

An
infinitesimal deformation
of a relative averaging algebra
(A_{\mu},B_{\nu}^{l,r},P)
of weight
\lambda
is given by a quintuple
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t},l_{t},r_{t},P_{t})
in which

\displaystyle\mu_{t}=\mu+t\mu_{1},\quad\nu_{t}=\nu+t\nu_{1},\quad l_{t}=l+tl_{1},\quad r_{t}=r+tr_{1}\text{ and }P_{t}=P+tP_{1}

such that
\big(A[[t]]/(t^{2})\big)_{\mu_{t}}
is an associative algebra,
\big(B[[t]]/(t^{2})\big)_{\nu_{t}}^{l_{t},r_{t}}
is an associative
\big(A[[t]]/(t^{2})\big)_{\mu_{t}}
-bimodule over the ring
\mathbb{K}[[t]]/(t^{2})
and the
\mathbb{K}[[t]]/(t^{2})
-linear map
P_{t}:B[[t]]/(t^{2})\rightarrow A[[t]]/(t^{2})
is a relative averaging operator of weight
\lambda
. In other words,
\big(\big(A[[t]]/(t^{2})\big)_{\mu_{t}},\big(B[[t]]/(t^{2})\big)_{\nu_{t}}^{l_{t},r_{t}},P_{t}\big)
is a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
over the ring
\mathbb{K}[[t]]/(t^{2})
.

Let
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t},l_{t},r_{t},P_{t})
be an infinitesimal deformation of a relative averaging algebra
(A,B,P)
of weight
\lambda
. Then it follows from Theorem
5.12
that
(\mu_{1},\nu_{1},l_{1}+r_{1},P_{1})\in Z^{2}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)
is a
2
-cocycle.

7.2 Definition
.

Let
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t},l_{t},r_{t},P_{t})
and
(\mu^{\prime}_{t},\nu^{\prime}_{t},l^{\prime}_{t},r^{\prime}_{t},P^{\prime}_{t})
be two infinitesimal deformations of a relative averaging algebra
(A,B,P)
of weight
\lambda
. These two infinitesimal deformations are said to be
equivalent
if there exist linear maps
\varphi_{1}:A\rightarrow A
and
\psi_{1}:B\rightarrow B
such that the pair
(\varphi_{t}=\mathrm{id}_{A}+t\varphi_{1},\psi_{t}=\mathrm{id}_{B}+t\psi_{1})
defines a morphism of relative averaging algebras of weight
\lambda
from

\displaystyle\big(\big(A[[t]]/(t^{2})\big)_{\mu_{t}},\big(B[[t]]/(t^{2})\big)_{\nu_{t}}^{l_{t},r_{t}},P_{t}\big)~\text{ to }~\big(\big(A[[t]]/(t^{2})\big)_{\mu^{\prime}_{t}},\big(B[[t]]/(t^{2})\big)_{\nu^{\prime}_{t}}^{l^{\prime}_{t},r^{\prime}_{t}},P^{\prime}_{t}\big).

.

It follows from the above definition that
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t},l_{t},r_{t},P_{t})
and
(\mu_{t}^{\prime},\nu_{t}^{\prime},l_{t}^{\prime},r_{t}^{\prime},P_{t}^{\prime})
are equivalent if the following conditions are hold modulo
t^{2}
:

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}(\mu_{t}(a,b))=~

\displaystyle\mu_{t}^{\prime}(\varphi_{t}(a),\varphi_{t}(b)),

\displaystyle\psi_{t}(\nu_{t}(x,y))=~

\displaystyle\nu_{t}^{\prime}(\psi_{t}(x),\psi_{t}(y)),

\displaystyle\psi_{t}(l_{t}(a,x))=~

\displaystyle l_{t}^{\prime}(\varphi_{t}(a),\psi_{t}(x)),

\displaystyle\psi_{t}(r_{t}(x,a))=~

\displaystyle r_{t}^{\prime}(\psi_{t}(x),\varphi_{t}(a)),

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}\circ P_{t}=~

\displaystyle P_{t}^{\prime}\circ\psi_{t},

for all
a,b\in A
and
x,y\in B
. Comparing the coefficients of
t
on both sides of the above identities, we get

\displaystyle\mu_{1}-\mu_{1}^{\prime}=\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{A}(\varphi_{1}),\quad

\displaystyle\nu_{1}-\nu_{1}^{\prime}=\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{B}(\psi_{1}),\quad(l_{1}+r_{1})-(l_{1}^{\prime}+r_{1}^{\prime})=\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}^{\varphi_{1},\psi_{1}}(0),

\displaystyle P_{1}-P_{1}^{\prime}=\theta_{P}(\varphi_{1},\psi_{1},0).

Thus, we obtain that

\displaystyle(\mu_{1},\nu_{1},l_{1}+r_{1},P_{1})-(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\nu_{1}^{\prime},l_{1}^{\prime}+r_{1}^{\prime},P_{1}^{\prime})=\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(\varphi_{1},\psi_{1},0).

This shows that the
2
-cocycles
(\mu_{1},\nu_{1},l_{1}+r_{1},P_{1})
and
(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\nu_{1}^{\prime},l_{1}^{\prime}+r_{1}^{\prime},P_{1}^{\prime})
are cohomologous. Hence they induce same element in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)
. Therefore, we obtain a map

\displaystyle\Lambda:(\text{infinitesimal deformations of }(A,B,P))/\sim~\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P).

In the following result, we will show that the map
\Lambda
is a bijection.

7.3 Theorem
.

Let
(A,B,P)
be a relative averaging algebra of weight
\lambda
. Then there is a bijection between the set of all equivalence classes of infinitesimal deformations of
(A,B,P)
and the second cohomology groups
H^{2}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)
.

Proof.

Here we will construct the inverse map of
\Lambda
. Let
(\mu_{1},\nu_{1},\beta_{1},\gamma_{1})\in Z^{2}_{\mathrm{aAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)
be a
2
-cocycle. We define maps
l_{1}:A\otimes B\rightarrow B
and
r_{1}:B\otimes A\rightarrow A
by

\displaystyle l_{1}(a,x)=\beta_{1}(a,x)~~~~\text{ and }~~~~r_{1}(x,a)=\beta_{1}(x,a),\text{ for }a\in A,x\in B.

Then it is easy to verify that the quintuple
(\mu_{1},\nu_{t},l_{t},r_{t},P_{t})
is an infinitesimal deformation, where

\displaystyle\mu_{t}=\mu+t\mu_{1},\quad\nu_{t}=\nu+t\nu_{1},\quad l_{t}=l+tl_{1},\quad r_{t}=r+tr_{1}\text{ and }P_{t}=P+t\gamma_{1}.

Further, if
(\mu_{1},\nu_{1},\beta_{1},\gamma_{1})
and
(\mu^{\prime}_{1},\nu^{\prime}_{1},\beta^{\prime}_{1},\gamma^{\prime}_{1})
are two cohomologous
2
-cocycles, say

\displaystyle(\mu_{1},\nu_{1},\beta_{1},\gamma_{1})-(\mu_{1},\nu_{1},\beta_{1},\gamma_{1})=\delta_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(\varphi_{1},\psi_{1},0),

then the corresponding infinitesimal deformations
(\mu_{t},\nu_{t},l_{t},r_{t},P_{t})
and
(\mu^{\prime}_{t},\nu^{\prime}_{t},l^{\prime}_{t},r^{\prime}_{t},P^{\prime}_{t})
are equivalent by the pair of maps
(\varphi_{t}=\mathrm{id}_{A}+t\varphi_{1},\psi_{t}=\mathrm{id}_{B}+t\psi_{1})
. Thus, we obtain a map

\displaystyle\Upsilon:H^{2}_{\mathrm{rAvg}_{\lambda}}(A,B,P)\rightarrow(\text{infinitesimal deformations of }(A,B,P))/\sim.

Finally, the maps
\Lambda
and
\Upsilon
are inverses to each other. This completes the proof.
∎

8.
Homotopy triassociative algebras and homotopy relative averaging operators of nonzero weight

In this section, we first introduce the notion of
Triass_{\infty}
-algebras (strongly homotopy triassociative algebras) and show that any
Triass_{\infty}
-algebra naturally gives three
A_{\infty}
-algebra structures. Next, given an
A_{\infty}
-algebra and an
A_{\infty}
-representation, we define the notion of a homotopy relative averaging operator of any nonzero weight
\lambda
. Finally, we show that such a homotopy operator naturally induces a
Triass_{\infty}
-algebra structure (which generalizes Proposition
5.4
in the homotopy context).

The notion of homotopy algebras (
A_{\infty}
-algebras) first appeared in the work of J. Stasheff in the study of topological loop spaces
[
35
]
. See also
[
18
,
21
]
for some details.

8.1 Definition
.

An
A_{\infty}
-algebra
is a pair
(A=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i},\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
consisting of a graded vector space
A=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}
equipped with a collection
\{\mu_{k}:A^{\otimes k}\rightarrow A\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of degree
1
graded linear maps satisfying the following set of identities:

\displaystyle\sum_{k+l=n+1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{i-1}|}~\mu_{k}\big(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},\mu_{l}(a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+l-1}),a_{i+l},\ldots,a_{n}\big)=0,

(34)

for all
n\geq 1
and homogeneous elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in A.

8.2 Definition
.

Let
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be an
A_{\infty}
-algebra. An
A_{\infty}
-representation
of
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
is an
A_{\infty}
-algebra
(B,\{\nu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
together with a collection

\displaystyle\{\eta_{k}:\mathcal{A}^{k-1,1}\oplus\mathcal{A}^{k-2,2}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{A}^{1,k-1}\rightarrow B\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

of degree
1
graded linear maps satisfying the identities (
34
) with some (but atmost
n-1
many) of the elements among
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
is from
B
and the corresponding operation
\mu_{k}
(resp.
\mu_{l}
) is replaced by
\nu_{k}
or
\eta_{k}
(resp.
\nu_{l}
or
\eta_{l}
). We often denote an
A_{\infty}
-representation as above by
(B,\{\nu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty},\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
. Like nonhomotopic case, here
\mathcal{A}^{k,l}
is the direct sum of all possible
(k+l)
tensor powers of
A
and
B
in which
A
appears
k
times and
B
appears
l
times.

It follows from the above definition that any
A_{\infty}
-algebra
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
is obviously an
A_{\infty}
-representation of itself, where
\eta_{k}=\mu_{k}
for
k\geq 1
. This is called the adjoint
A_{\infty}
-representation.

8.3 Definition
.

A
Triass_{\infty}
-algebra
is a graded vector space
D=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}D_{i}
equipped with a collection
\{\pi_{k}:\mathbb{K}[T_{k}]\otimes D^{\otimes k}\rightarrow D\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of degree
1
graded linear maps satisfying the following set of identities:

\displaystyle\sum_{k+l=n+1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{|x_{1}|+\cdots+|x_{k-1}|}~\pi_{k}\big(R^{k;i,l}_{0}(T);x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},\pi_{l}\big(R^{k;i,l}_{i}(T);x_{1},\ldots,x_{i+l-1}),x_{i+l},\ldots,x_{n}\big)=0,

(35)

for all
n\geq 1
, any
T\in T_{n}
and homogeneous elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in D.

8.4 Remark
.

(i) Let
(D,\{\pi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be a
Triass_{\infty}
-algebra. Since the set
Y_{n}
of all planar binary
n
-trees is contained in the set
T_{n}
of all
n
-trees, the identities in (
35
) are naturally held when we consider the trees of
Y_{n}
. Thus the identities of
Diass_{\infty}
-algebra (strongly homotopy dissociative algebra) follow. In other words, any
Triass_{\infty}
-algebra is intrinsically a
Diass_{\infty}
-algebra. This generalizes the similar result from the nonhomotopic case.

(ii) For each
n\geq 1
, the set
T_{n}
has three distinguished trees, namely,

(call it
T^{\dashv}_{n}
)
(call it
T^{\vdash}_{n}
)
(call it
T^{\perp}_{n}
).

If we apply the maps
d_{i}:T_{n}\rightarrow T_{n-1}
(for any
0\leq i\leq n
) to the above distinguished trees, one get
d_{i}(T_{n}^{\dashv})=T_{n-1}^{\dashv}
,
d_{i}(T_{n}^{\vdash})=T^{\vdash}_{n-1}
and
d_{i}(T^{\perp}_{n})=T^{\perp}_{n-1}
. As a result, we have

\displaystyle R_{0}^{k;i,l}(T_{n}^{\dashv})=T_{k}^{\dashv},~R_{0}^{k;i,l}(T_{n}^{\vdash})=T_{k}^{\vdash},~R_{0}^{k;i,l}(T_{n}^{\perp})=T_{k}^{\perp},

\displaystyle R_{i}^{k;i,l}(T_{n}^{\dashv})=T_{l}^{\dashv},~R_{i}^{k;i,l}(T_{n}^{\vdash})=T_{l}^{\vdash},~R_{i}^{k;i,l}(T_{n}^{\perp})=T_{l}^{\perp}.

Let
(D,\{\pi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be a
Triass_{\infty}
-algebra. Then it follows that
(D,\{\pi_{k}^{\dashv}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
,
(D,\{\pi_{k}^{\vdash}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
and
(D,\{\pi_{k}^{\perp}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
are all
A_{\infty}
-algebras, where

\displaystyle\pi_{k}^{\dashv}=\pi_{k}|_{T_{k}^{\dashv}\otimes D^{\otimes k}},~~\pi_{k}^{\vdash}=\pi_{k}|_{T_{k}^{\vdash}\otimes D^{\otimes k}}~~\text{ and }~~\pi_{k}^{\perp}=\pi_{k}|_{T_{k}^{\perp}\otimes D^{\otimes k}},\text{ for }k\geq 1.

Let
D=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}D_{i}
be a graded vector space. For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\mathrm{Hom}_{n}(\mathbb{K}[T_{\bullet}]\otimes\overline{T}(D),D)
be the space of degree
n
graded linear maps from the graded space

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}[T_{\bullet}]\otimes\overline{T}(D)=(\mathbb{K}[T_{1}]\otimes D)\oplus(\mathbb{K}[T_{2}]\otimes D^{\otimes 2})\oplus\cdots\oplus(\mathbb{K}[T_{n}]\otimes D^{\otimes n})\oplus\cdots

to the space
D
. Thus, an element
\pi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{n}(\mathbb{K}[T_{\bullet}]\otimes\overline{T}(D),D)
is of the form
\pi=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\pi_{k}
, where
\pi_{k}:\mathbb{K}[T_{k}]\otimes D^{\otimes k}\rightarrow D
(for
k\geq 1
) is a degree
n
graded linear map. For any

\displaystyle\pi=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\pi_{k}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{m}(\mathbb{K}[T_{\bullet}]\otimes\overline{T}(D),D)~\text{ and }~\varpi=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\varpi_{l}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{n}(\mathbb{K}[T_{\bullet}]\otimes\overline{T}(D),D),

we define an element
\{\![\pi,\varpi]\!\}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{m+n}(\mathbb{K}[T_{\bullet}]\otimes\overline{T}(D),D)
by

\displaystyle\{\![\pi,\varpi]\!\}=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k+l=r+1}(\pi_{k}\diamond\varpi_{l}-(-1)^{mn}\varpi_{l}\diamond\pi_{k}),

where

\displaystyle(\pi_{k}\diamond\varpi_{l})

\displaystyle(T;x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{|x_{1}|+\cdots+|x_{i-1}|}~\pi_{k}\big(R^{k;i,l}_{0}(T);x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},\varpi_{l}\big(R^{k;i,l}_{i}(T);x_{i},\ldots,x_{i+l-1}\big),x_{i+l},\ldots,x_{r}\big),

for
T\in T_{r}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in D
. Then it turns out that
\big(\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Hom}_{n}(\mathbb{K}[T_{\bullet}]\otimes\overline{T}(D),D),\{\![~,~]\!\}\big)
is a graded Lie algebra. Note that an element
\pi=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\pi_{k}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{1}(\mathbb{K}[T_{\bullet}]\otimes\overline{T}(D),D)
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the above graded Lie algebra if and only if the collection
\{\pi_{k}:\mathbb{K}[T_{k}]\otimes D^{\otimes k}\rightarrow D\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
defines a
Triass_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
D
.

Let
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be an
A_{\infty}
-algebra and
(B,\{\nu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty},\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be an
A_{\infty}
-representation. Then for any nonzero
\lambda
, the graded vector space
A\oplus B
can be equipped with a
Triass_{\infty}
-algebra structure with the operations
\{\pi_{k}:\mathbb{K}[T_{k}]\otimes(A\oplus B)^{\otimes k}\rightarrow A\oplus B\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
given by

\displaystyle\pi_{k}\big(T;(a_{1},x_{1}),\ldots,(a_{k},x_{k})\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\mu_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}),\lambda^{l-2}~(\eta_{k}+\nu_{k})\big(P(x_{1}),\ldots,P(x_{i_{1}-1}),x_{i_{1}},P(x_{i_{1}+1}),\ldots,x_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{l-1}},\ldots,P(x_{k})\big)\big),

when the tree
T\in T_{k}
can be uniquely writtens as
T=T^{1}\vee\cdots\vee T^{l}
, for some unique
(i_{1}-1)
-tree
T^{1}\in T_{i_{1}-1},\ldots,(i_{l}-1)
-tree
T^{l}\in T_{i_{l}-1}
.

It follows that the above
Triass_{\infty}
-algebra
(A\oplus B,\{\pi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
gives rise to a Maurer-Cartan element
\pi=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\pi_{k}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{1}(\mathbb{K}[T_{\bullet}]\otimes\overline{T}(A\oplus B),A\oplus B)
in the graded Lie algebra

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\big(\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Hom}_{n}(\mathbb{K}[T_{\bullet}]\otimes\overline{T}(A\oplus B),A\oplus B),\{\![~,~]\!\}\big).

We define a graded subspace
\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Hom}_{n}(\mathbb{K}[T_{\bullet}]\otimes\overline{T}(B),A)
. Then
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{g}
is a graded Lie subalgebra. Let
P:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be the projection map on the subspace
\mathfrak{a}
. Then
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a},p,\pi)
is a
V
-data. As a consequence of Theorem
5.9
, we get the following result.

8.5 Theorem
.

Let
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be an
A_{\infty}
-algebra and
(B,\{\nu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty},\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be an
A_{\infty}
-representation. Then the graded vector space
\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Hom}_{n}(\mathbb{K}[T_{\bullet}]\otimes\overline{T}(B),A)
inherits a
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure with the operations
\{l_{k}:\mathfrak{a}^{\otimes k}\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
are given by

\displaystyle l_{k}(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k})=p\{\![\cdots\{\![\{\![\pi,\gamma_{1}]\!\},\gamma_{2}]\!\},\ldots,\gamma_{k}]\!\},

for
k\geq 1
and homogeneous elements
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}\in\mathfrak{a}
.

8.6 Definition
.

Let
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be an
A_{\infty}
-algebra and
(B,\{\nu_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty},\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
be an
A_{\infty}
-representation. A homotopy relative averaging operator of weight
\lambda
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty})
.

It follows that a homotopy relative averaging operator of weight
\lambda
is an element
P=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}P_{k}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{0}(\mathbb{K}[T_{\bullet}]\otimes\overline{T}(B),A)
that satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}l_{k}(P,\ldots,P)=0\quad\text{ equivalently}\quad p\big(e^{\{\![-,P]\!\}}\pi\big)=0.

In Proposition
5.4
, we have seen that a relative averaging operator of any nonzero weight
\lambda
induces a triassociative algebra structure. Here we will generalize this result in the homotopy context.

8.7 Proposition
.

Let
\Pi
be a homotopy relative averaging operator of weight
\lambda
. Then the graded vector space
B
inherits a
Triass_{\infty}
-algebra structure with the operations
\{\pi_{k}^{P}:\mathbb{K}[T_{k}]\otimes B^{\otimes k}\rightarrow B\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
are given by

\displaystyle\pi_{k}^{P}(T;x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=\big(e^{\{\![-,P]\!\}}\pi\big)(T;x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}),

for all
k\geq 1
,
T\in T_{k}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in B
.

Proof.

Since
\{\![\pi,\pi]\!\}=0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\{\![e^{\{\![-,P]\!\}}\pi,e^{\{\![-,P]\!\}}\pi]\!\}=e^{\{\![-,P]\!\}}\{\![\pi,\pi]\!\}=0.

Therefore,
e^{\{\![~,P]\!\}}\pi
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. As a consequence, the collection
\{\pi_{k}^{P}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
defines a
Triass_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
B
, where
\pi_{k}^{P}=(e^{\{\![-,P]\!\}}\pi)|_{\mathbb{K}[T_{k}]\otimes B^{\otimes k}}
for
k\geq 1
.
∎

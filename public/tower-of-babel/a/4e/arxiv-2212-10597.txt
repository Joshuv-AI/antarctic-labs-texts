On the representation-free formalism in quantum theory
V.D. Efros

Abstract
The widespread bra-ket formalism provides efficient means to carry out representation-free considerations in quantum theory. At the same time, drawbacks are inherent in it. We discuss these drawbacks in detail, and then, construct a new representation-free scheme. The present scheme provides efficient means to facilitate representation-free considerations, similar to all those offered by the bra-ket scheme but has none of the drawbacks of the bra-ket scheme. And, in contrast to the bra-ket formalism, the present scheme allows both the one-space and dual-space interpretations, which is a helpful feature. The constructed scheme is advantageous for performing representation-free practical calculations.

Mathematicians usually employ

II.3
Features of the bra-ket scheme

Efficient working tools provided by the bra-ket formalism

a special designation
|\,\,\rangle
for marking state vectors,
an ’explicit’ form of the projection-type operators, such

|u\rangle\langle v|

\langle n_{1}^{\prime},n_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,n_{k}^{\prime}|O|n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{k}\rangle

(In our

corrected version of the scheme described above.

III
The present scheme

First, we would like to justify the necessity for a new version of the
representation-free approach concisely.
The mathematically oriented formulation of this approach mentioned above does not
contain tools like Dirac’s to perform practical calculations, while the bra-ket formalism
contains drawbacks and potential ambiguity.
Gieres
gie
suggested that the representation-free approach itself is not that
useful because the bra-ket
formalism does not treat domains of definitions of operators in a due way. He argued that
the coordinate representation is to be applied to a higher degree since those domains of
definitions are determined naturally in this representation.

But,
because of the great use of the representation-free approach for carrying
out general considerations, a much better way out would be to
construct a more appropriate version of the representation-free approach than the
bra-ket formalism. While,
to determine the domains
of definitions of operators involved, one may employ suitable representations
before a representation-free description.

An appropriate realization of the representation-free approach should contain all the
tools like Dirac’s that facilitate practical calculations. It should employ the one-space
picture. Simultaneously, it should allow the dual-space interpretation.
The latter is related to the fact that one needs the dual-space
description for some noteworthy purposes, in particular for
rigged Hilbert space studies
bo
;
gel
;
be
. The representation-free
scheme constructed here meets all these conditions.

like

For the quantum mechanical scalar product, we introduce
the notation like

/u/\mathbin{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\centerdot}}{{}}}/v/

IV
Conclusion

To carry out representation-free considerations in quantum theory,
the bra-ket
formalism
is generally applied. It provides
helpful tools for this purpose, but,
at the same time, drawbacks are inherent in it.
Some of them originate from the fact that the bra-ket scheme
is not suitable for handling the domains of definitions of
operators involved. Another drawback is that
the bra-ket formalism, being a dual-space formalism, excludes the
natural one-space point of view. Sec. 2 addresses those drawbacks.

The scheme of the present work is free of the
drawbacks of the bra-ket formalism.
Herewith, the present scheme provides
efficient means to conduct representation-free
considerations, similar to all such means
offered by
the bra-ket scheme. In contrast to the bra-ket scheme,
the present scheme
allows both the needed one-space and
dual-space interpretations. We believe that
the present scheme would be very advantageous for performing representation-free practical
calculations.

It is easy to switch from
the bra-ket scheme to the present scheme.
And an easy opportunity
to learn the widespread bra-ket formalism would be
to proceed from the present scheme.

Over-The-Air Federated Learning Over Scalable Cell-free Massive MIMO
Houssem Sifaou, Geoffrey Ye Li

Abstract
Cell-free massive MIMO is emerging as a promising technology for future wireless communication systems, which is expected to offer uniform coverage and high spectral efficiency compared to classical cellular systems. We study in this paper how cell-free massive MIMO can support federated edge learning. Taking advantage of the additive nature of the wireless multiple access channel, over-the-air computation is exploited, where the clients send their local updates simultaneously over the same communication resource. Such an approach, known as over-the-air federated learning (OTA-FL), is proven to alleviate the communication overhead of federated learning over wireless networks. Considering channel correlation and only imperfect channel state information available at the central server, we propose a practical implementation of OTA-FL over cell-free massive MIMO. The convergence of the proposed implementation is studied analytically and experimentally, confirming the benefits of cell-free massive MIMO for OTA-FL.



I
Introduction

With the exponential growth in data collection from distributed devices and remarkable advancements in AI applications, there is a growing interest in distributed learning, which leverages the computing capabilities of these devices. Distributed learning offers two primary benefits. Firstly, it eliminates the need to transfer massive volumes of high-dimensional data from the collecting devices to central servers (CSs) for further processing. Secondly, it inherently ensures privacy since local data is not shared. This work specifically focuses on federated learning (FL), a popular technique in distributed learning, where a CS coordinates global model training by communicating with multiple distributed clients
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Instead of sharing local data, the parameters of the machine learning (ML) model are transferred. The FL process involves multiple training rounds, during which the global model is shared with the clients. The clients then perform several local iterations using their respective local data and send their model updates back to the CS. These model updates are subsequently aggregated to obtain the new global model.

In this work, we focus specifically on over-the-air FL (OTA-FL), which has been recently proposed to alleviate the communication overhead of the FL process
[
4
,
5
]
. By exploiting the additive nature of the multiple access channel, OTA-FL allows the clients to send their local updates simultaneously and the CS can obtain the sum directly from the received signal. Such an approach has been extensively studied in literature and has been proven to enhance the communication efficiency of FL over wireless networks. The main advantage of OTA-FL is that the communication resources needed to reach convergence do not scale with the number of clients. In contrast, the communication resources of digital transmission based FL scale with the number of clients
[
6
]
.

In this paper, we consider implementing OTA-FL over cell-free massive MIMO. The latter is a promising technique for future wireless communication systems, which is expected to bring several benefits such as ultra-high reliability, high energy efficiency, ultra-low latency, and ubiquitous and uniform coverage
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
. The main idea of cell-free massive MIMO is deploying a large number of distributed APs, connected to a central processing unit (CPU), that serve all users in a wide coverage area
[
9
]
. Specifically, we consider in this work the scalable cell-free architecture recently proposed in
[
10
]
. It is a user-centric architecture where a cluster composed of a finite number of APs is formed around each user terminal (UT). The latter will only communicate with the APs in its cluster. This is the main difference compared to the early architectures proposed for cell-free massive MIMO, where each UT is served by all the APs in the network
[
11
,
12
]
. The scalable architecture
[
10
]
allows the number of UTs and APs to be very large while keeping all the signal processing algorithms and the fronthaul signaling practically implementable. The main objective of our work is to explore the benefits that cell-free massive MIMO can bring for OTA-FL.

To the best of our knowledge, our work in this paper is the first effort to study the performance of OTA-FL in the context of cell-free massive MIMO.

OTA-FL has been studied extensively in both point-to-point and MIMO settings. However, these studies cannot be directly applied to cell-free massive MIMO scenarios. In Section III, we thoroughly demonstrate the vanishing interference terms and establish the necessary conditions specific to cell-free massive MIMO systems. These conditions differ significantly from those studied in the context of cellular massive MIMO, as outlined in

Particularly, while the conditions for cellular massive MIMO focus on a large number of antennas and a finite number of clients, our findings highlight the importance of both a large number of clients and a large number of antennas for cell-free massive MIMO. Moreover, proving the interference vanishing necessitates some practical assumptions specific to this setting. These assumptions, detailed in (
7

Our proposed analysis also addresses a critical challenge often overlooked in existing literature, which is the scalability issue. Previous studies on OTA-FL in cellular MIMO predominantly focus on single-cell scenarios with a limited number of clients, neglecting inter-cell interference. In contrast, our analysis considers the entire cell-free network with a substantial number of clients, presenting a more scalable approach. By investigating OTA-FL within this comprehensive network framework, we contribute to the understanding of its applicability and performance in large-scale deployments. Finally, a strong motivation for this work is to study the advantages of implementing OTA-FL in terms of energy efficiency compared with cellular MIMO. This is an important research question and our work is a first attempt to answer it. Our experiments show clearly the advantage of cell-free massive MIMO systems in terms of energy efficiency, which is an essential factor in FL taking into account the limited power at the clients’ devices.

The contributions of our work can be summarized as follows:

•

We study, for the first time, the performance of OTA-FL over cell-free massive MIMO and investigate the benefits that the latter can bring for OTA-FL in terms of energy efficiency.

•

We propose a practical implementation of OTA-FL over cell-free massive MIMO under reasonable assumptions and a practical channel model. Specifically, we propose a receiver based on maximum ratio combining (MRC) that is shown to mitigate the effect of the fading channel as well as the interference.

•

We provide the convergence rate of the proposed implementation and test its performance via extensive experiments. Numerical results show that OTA-FL over cell-free massive MIMO requires lower power constraints at the clients to reach convergence compared to cellular massive MIMO.

II
System Model

In this section, we give a brief overview of OTA-FL and cell-free massive MIMO. Particularly, we introduce the scalable user-centric architecture of cell-free massive MIMO that will be considered in our proposed implementation.

II-A

Federated learning

We consider a FL system, where
N
clients, each with a local dataset
\mathcal{D}_{n}
of size
D_{n}=|\mathcal{D}_{n}|
, communicate with a CS to collaboratively train a global model. The result of the FL process is the optimal parameter vector,
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, that minimizes a global loss function
F({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
given by

F({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{D_{n}}{D}F_{n}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}),

(1)

where
D=\sum_{n=1}^{N}D_{n}
and
F_{n}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
is the local empirical loss function at client
k
given by

F_{n}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=\frac{1}{D_{n}}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}}\mathcal{L}({\boldsymbol{\theta}};{\boldsymbol{\xi}}),

(2)

where
\mathcal{L}({\boldsymbol{\theta}};{\boldsymbol{\xi}})
denotes the loss function at data sample
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
, which depends on the learning task.
One of the main challenges of FL is the communication overhead. In fact, the local updates are sent at every global training round to the CS usually over limited bandwidth wireless channels. To reduce the communication overhead, over-the-air communication has been proposed as a promising solution, where the model updates are sent simultaneously over the multiple access channel
[
4
,
20
]
. This approach, referred to as OTA-FL, will be introduced hereafter.

OTA-FL consists of multiple global training rounds where the CS sends the model parameter vector,
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{t}
, to the clients at each round
t
. Due to the high power available at the CS, the downlink communication is usually assumed to be perfect and the clients receive the global model without distortion
[
20
]
. Then, client
n
sets its local model as
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{0}^{n}(t)={\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)
and runs its local SGD for
\tau
iterations based on its local dataset

{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i+1}^{n}(t)={\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}^{n}(t)-\eta_{t}F_{n,\xi_{n,i}^{t}}^{\prime}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}^{n}(t)),\ \ {\rm for}\ \ i=0,1,\cdots,\tau-1,

(3)

where
\eta_{t}
is the SGD step size at round
t
and
F_{n,\xi_{n,i}^{t}}^{\prime}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}^{n}(t))
denotes the stochastic gradient computed using a local mini-batch sample
\xi_{n,i}^{t}
chosen uniformly at random from the local dataset of client
n
.
The clients then send their model updates,

\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{n}(t)={\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{\tau}^{n}(t)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}({t}),

(4)

simultaneously to the CS via analog OTA. The CS recovers the new global model from the received signals. Further details will be given in Section
III
.

II-B

Cell-free massive MIMO

One of the promising technologies for 6G is cell-free massive MIMO, where distributed APs are deployed over the coverage area
[
38
,
39
]
. Each AP can serve multiple UTs and a UT can communicate with several APs, which is different from classical cellular massive MIMO where each UT is served by a single BS. Cell-free massive MIMO is shown to provide better spectral efficiency than classical cellular massive MIMO
[
10
]
.

We adopt in this work the scalable cell-free massive MIMO network proposed in
[
10
]
. Such a network can support a large number of UTs while the signal processing algorithms and the fronthaul signaling remain practically implementable. The network is composed of
N
single antenna UTs and
L
APs, each equipped with
M
antennas. Each subset of APs is connected to a CPU via high-capacity backhaul links. A user-centric network architecture is assumed where each UT is served by a subset of the APs providing the best channel conditions as illustrated in Fig.
1
. We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
the subset of UTs served by AP
\ell
.

Fig. 1:
Scalable cell-free massive MIMO network.

Since the model updates of a client will be received by multiple APs, we think that implementing OTA-FL over cell-free massive MIMO will be more robust to poor channel conditions and CSI errors. The objective of this work is to propose a practical implementation of OTA-FL over cell-free massive MIMO, investigate its advantages, and study its convergence.

15
) is the sum of
N|\mathcal{K}_{n}||\mathcal{S}_{\ell}\cup\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}|
terms of order
\mathcal{O}(1)
normalized by
N^{2}
, and since
|\mathcal{K}_{n}||\mathcal{S}_{\ell}\cup\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}|
is finite compared to
N
, this first term is
o(1)
when
N\to\infty
. For the second term, it is a sum of
N|\mathcal{K}_{n}|(N-|\mathcal{S}_{\ell}\cup\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}|)
terms of order
o(1)
normalized by
N^{2}
, it is thus
o(1)
. Based on the above analysis, the variance of the first interference term satisfies

\displaystyle\var(\text{interf 1})=o(1),\ \ \ \text{as}\ \ N\to\infty.

(16)

Similarly, the expectation of the second interference term is zero and its variance with respect to the randomness of the channel can be obtained as

\displaystyle\var(\text{interf 2})=\var\frac{\alpha_{t}}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{\tilde{\bf h}^{k,s}_{n}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}^{k,s}_{n^{\prime}}(t)}{c_{n}}x_{n^{\prime}}^{k,s}(t)

(17)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\alpha_{t}^{2}}{N^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{\tr({\bf R}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf C}_{n}{\bf D}_{n})}{c_{n}^{2}}|x_{n^{\prime}}^{k,s}(t)|^{2}.

(18)

Using similar arguments as in the analysis of the first interference term, one can easily show

\displaystyle\var(\text{interf 2})=o(1),\ \ \ \text{as}\ \ N\to\infty.

(19)

The above analysis justifies the estimation of the global model update entries as

\displaystyle\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{(2k-1)+2(s-1)K}(t)=\frac{\text{Re}\left\{y^{k,s}(t)\right\}}{\alpha_{t}}

(20)

\displaystyle\Delta\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{2k+2(s-1)K}(t)=\frac{\text{Im}\left\{y^{k,s}(t)\right\}}{\alpha_{t}}.

(21)

Remark 1
.

We have shown in this section that the interference terms vanish and determined the required conditions for that. These conditions turn out to be completely different than the case of cellular massive MIMO studied in
[
15
]
. Specifically, both the number of clients and the total number of antennas should be large while the conditions in the case of a cellular massive MIMO are such that the number of antennas is large while the number of clients is finite.

IV
Convergence analysis

In this section, we study the convergence of the proposed FEEL scheme. We assume without loss of generality that
d=2S
, which implies
K=1
and thus we drop the dependency on
k
of all variables for ease of the notations.

IV-A

Preliminaries

Let
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}
be the optimal parameter vector that minimizes
F({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
and define

F^{*}=F({\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*})
. We define also
\Gamma=F^{*}-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{D_{n}}{D}F_{n}^{*}
, which measures the amount of bias in the data distribution. Larger
\Gamma
indicates higher data heterogeneity among clients. In the sequel, we consider the following assumptions, which are classical when it comes to studying the convergence of FL
[
15
,
21
]
.

Assumption 1
.

We assume the following regularities for the loss functions:

(i)

The loss functions
F_{1},\cdots,F_{N}
are
\mu
-strongly convex, that is, for all
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

F_{n}({\bf x})-F_{n}({\bf y})\geq\langle F_{n}^{\prime}({\bf y}),{\bf x}-{\bf y}\rangle+\frac{\mu}{2}\|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\|^{2}.

(ii)

The loss functions
F_{1},\cdots,F_{N}
are all
\mathcal{L}
-smooth, that is, for all
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

F_{n}({\bf x})-F_{n}({\bf y})\leq\langle F_{n}^{\prime}({\bf y}),{\bf x}-{\bf y}\rangle+\frac{\mathcal{L}}{2}\|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\|^{2}.

(iii)

The stochastic gradient
F^{\prime}_{n,xi_{n,i}^{t}}({\bf x})
satisfies

\mathbb{E}\|F_{n,\xi_{n,i}^{t}}^{\prime}({\bf x})\|^{2}\leq G^{2},\ {\rm for\ all}\ {\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

IV-B

Convergence results

In this section, we establish the convergence rate of the proposed OTA-FL scheme. The proof is provided in Appendix A.

Theorem 1
.

Under Assumption
1
, when the step size
\eta_{t}
verifies
0<\eta_{t}\leq\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{\mu\tau}\right\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(T)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}\right\|^{2}\leq\left(\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}A(t)\right)\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(0)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}\right\|^{2}+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}B(t)\prod_{i=t+1}^{T-1}A(i),

(22)

where

\displaystyle A(t)

\displaystyle=(1-\mu\eta_{t}(\tau-\eta_{t}(\tau-1))),

(23)

\displaystyle B(t)

\displaystyle=2\eta_{t}(\tau-1)\Gamma+(1+\mu(1-\eta_{t}))\eta_{t}^{2}G^{2}\frac{\tau(\tau-1)(2\tau-1)}{6}+\eta_{t}^{2}(\tau^{2}+\tau-1)G^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{2}{N}+\frac{(\tilde{\gamma}+\gamma)}{2N}-\frac{\gamma}{2N^{2}}\right)\eta_{t}^{2}\tau^{2}G^{2}+\frac{d}{2N\alpha_{t}^{2}}(\kappa+\tilde{\kappa})\sigma_{z}^{2},

(24)

with

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\max_{n,n^{\prime}}\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}\tr({\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf D}_{n})

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\max_{n,n^{\prime}}\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}\tr({\bf C}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf D}_{n})

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{c_{n}}

\displaystyle\tilde{\kappa}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}\tr({\bf C}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}).

The term
\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}A(t)
in the bound in (
22
) measures the decay of the distance to the optimal solution after
T
rounds with respect to the initial distance. On the other hand, the term
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}B(t)\prod_{i=t+1}^{T-1}A(i)
measures the residual distance to the optimal solution at round
T
with respect to that of the initial model to the optimal solution. Reducing
A(t)
may lead to faster convergence but it may result in increasing the value of
B(t)
and thus higher residual distance. For instance, increasing
\tau
will result in a lower value of
A(t)
and a higher value of
B(t)
, thus higher values of
\tau
provide faster convergence but increased residual error. Moreover, the effect of the wireless channel appears in the last two terms of
B(t)
via the noise variance
\sigma_{z}^{2}
and the channel statistics captured by the parameters
\kappa
,
\tilde{\kappa}
,
\gamma
, and
\tilde{\gamma}
.

We highlight that the effect of the channel estimation error appears in the parameters
\tilde{\gamma}
and
\tilde{\kappa}
. Specifically, these parameters vanish when perfect CSI is available at the APs since
{\bf C}_{n}={\boldsymbol{0}}
in this case. Moreover, the parameters
\gamma
and
\kappa
are directly related to the quality of the wireless channels between the clients and their corresponding APs. For instance,
\kappa
which can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\tr({\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n})}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\tr({\bf R}_{n,\ell})},

(25)

increases when the quality of channel conditions of the clients decreases. We note also that the effect of the AP-UT association scheme is reflected in the parameters
\gamma
,
\tilde{\gamma}
,
\kappa
and
\tilde{\kappa}
through the association matrices
{\bf D}_{n}
.

Corollary 1
.

•

When the step size
\eta_{t}
verifies
0<\eta_{t}\leq\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{\mu\tau}\right\}
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}\left[F({\boldsymbol{\theta}}(T))\right]-F({\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*})\leq\frac{\mathcal{L}}{2}\left(\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}A(t)\right)\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(0)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\mathcal{L}}{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}B(t)\prod_{i=t+1}^{T-1}A(i).

•

When
\eta_{t}=\eta,\ \forall t
and
\tau=1
, we have for
0<\eta\leq\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{\mu\tau}\right\}
,

{\mathbb{E}}\left[F({\boldsymbol{\theta}}(T))\right]-F({\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*})\leq\frac{\mathcal{L}}{2}\left(1-\mu\eta\right)^{T}\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(0)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}\right\|^{2}-A_{1}\right)+\frac{\mathcal{L}}{2}A_{1},

where

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu\eta}\left(\eta^{2}G^{2}+\left(\frac{2}{N}+\frac{(\tilde{\gamma}+\gamma)}{2N}-\frac{\gamma}{2N^{2}}\right)\eta^{2}G^{2}+\frac{d(\kappa+\tilde{\kappa})}{2N\alpha_{0}^{2}}\sigma_{z}^{2}\right).

Corollary
1
states that the proposed implementation converges at a linear rate towards a neighborhood of the optimal solution with an asymptotic learning error,
A_{1}
, that depends on the noise variance, the UTs channel conditions, and the AP-UT association scheme. The latter is reflected in the parameters
\gamma
,
\tilde{\gamma}
,
\kappa
and
\tilde{\kappa}
. The above result can be leveraged to reduce the learning error of the FL process, also known as the optimality gap, by optimizing the AP-UT association scheme. Reducing the values of
\gamma+\tilde{\gamma}
and
\kappa+\tilde{\kappa}
will result in a lower learning error.

Remark 2
.

Various techniques, including user scheduling
[
44
]
, partial device participation
[
45
]
, and gradient sparsification or compression
[
46
]
, among others, can be employed in the context of cell-free massive MIMO for optimizing the FL process. The convergence analysis can be adapted depending on the specific technique but this is out of the scope of this work.

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied the performance of OTA-FL over scalable cell-free massive MIMO. Specifically, we have proposed a practical implementation that is shown to be convergent. The performance of the proposed implementation is studied analytically and experimentally, showing the advantages that cell-free massive MIMO can bring for OTA-FL. We have also compared the proposed scheme with cellular massive MIMO-based OTA-FL, where we have shown that the proposed implementation requires significantly lower energy to reach convergence. Future related research directions include studying different receiving techniques, such as the MMSE receiver, and investigating the convergence of second-order FL techniques, such as ADMM FL, in this context. Fine-tuning the proposed OTA-FL over cell-free massive MIMO in terms of client scheduling and uplink power optimization is also an interesting future direction.

Proof of Theorem 1

As mentioned in Section
IV
, we assume without loss of generality that
d=2S
, which implies
K=1
and thus we drop the dependency on
k
for ease of notations. The received signal in (
10
) can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle y^{s}(t)=\sum_{p=1}^{4}y^{s}_{p}(t),

(26)

where

\displaystyle y^{s}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{t}}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{{\bf h}^{s}_{n}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}^{s}_{n}(t)}{c_{n}}\left(\Delta\theta_{n,s}(t)+j\Delta\theta_{n,s+\frac{d}{2}}(t)\ \right)

(27a)

\displaystyle y^{s}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{t}}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\\
n^{\prime}\neq n\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{{\bf h}^{s}_{n}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}^{s}_{n^{\prime}}(t)}{c_{n}}\left(\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},s}(t)+j\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},s+\frac{d}{2}}(t)\ \right)

(27b)

\displaystyle y^{s}_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{t}}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{\tilde{\bf h}^{s}_{n}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n^{\prime}}^{s}(t)}{c_{n}}\left(\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},s}(t)+j\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},s+\frac{d}{2}}(t)\ \right)

(27c)

\displaystyle y^{s}_{4}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\left({\bf h}^{s}_{n}(t)+\tilde{\bf h}^{s}_{n}(t)\right)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf z}^{s}(t)}{c_{n}}.

(27d)

Accordingly, we define for
p\in[4]

\displaystyle\Delta{\widehat{\theta}}_{p,i}(t)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{Re(y^{i}_{p}(t))}{\alpha_{t}},\quad{\rm if}\quad 1\leq i\leq d/2,\\
\frac{Im(y^{i-d/2}_{p}(t))}{\alpha_{t}},\quad{\rm if}\quad d/2+1\leq i\leq{d},\end{cases}

(28)

and the estimate of the
i
-the entry of the global model update can be written as

\displaystyle\Delta{\widehat{\theta}}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=1}^{4}\Delta{\widehat{\theta}}_{p,i}(t).

Define
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t)
as

{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t+1)={\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)+\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t),

where

\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}(t).

Recall that

{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t+1)={\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)+\Delta\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t).

The objective is to bound
{\mathbb{E}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}\right\|^{2}
, where the expectation is taken over all randomness, namely the channels, the channel estimation error, the noise, and the stochastic gradients. We start by rewriting
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}\right\|^{2}
as

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t+1)+{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t+1)\right\|^{2}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}\right\|^{2}

(29)

\displaystyle\quad+2\left\langle{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t+1),{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}\right\rangle.

It takes no effort to verify that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t+1),{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left\langle\Delta\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)-\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t),{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)+\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}\right\rangle=0,

where the last result is obtained by noting that the local model updates at global round
t
are independent of the channels and noise during the same global round, and the expectation over
{\bf h}_{n}^{s}(t)
,
\tilde{\bf h}_{n}^{s}(t)
, and
{\bf z}^{s}(t)
of
\Delta\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)
is
{\mathbb{E}}\Delta\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)=\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)
.

It remains to bound the first two terms of the RHS of (
29
), which is the objective of the following two lemmas.

Lemma 2
.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t+1)\right\|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{2}{N}+\frac{(\gamma+\tilde{\gamma})}{2N}-\frac{\gamma}{2N^{2}}\right)\eta_{t}^{2}\tau^{2}G^{2}+\frac{d}{2N\alpha_{t}^{2}}(\kappa+\tilde{\kappa})\sigma_{z}^{2}.

(30)

Proof.

See Appendix B.
∎

Lemma 3
.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}\right\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle(1-\mu\eta_{t}(\tau-\eta_{t}(\tau-1))){\mathbb{E}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{*}\right\|^{2}+2\eta_{t}(\tau-1)\Gamma

(31)

\displaystyle+(1+\mu(1-\eta_{t}))\eta_{t}^{2}G^{2}\frac{\tau(\tau-1)(2\tau-1)}{6}+\eta_{t}^{2}(\tau^{2}+\tau-1)G^{2}.

(32)

Proof.

The proof is classical in the convergence analysis of FL since the terms involved do not depend on the transmission scheme and can be found in several papers in literature such as
[
15
,
30
]
.

Proof of Lemma
2

First, we have

{\mathbb{E}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t+1)\right\|^{2}={\mathbb{E}}\left\|\Delta\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)-\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)\right\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i}(t)-\Delta\theta_{i}(t)\right)^{2},

where
\Delta\theta_{i}(t)
is the
i
-th entry of
\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)
. Note that

\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i}(t)=\sum_{p=1}^{4}\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,p}(t),

where
\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,p}(t),\ p\in[4],
are defined in (
27
).
Given that the channels and the noise are independent of the model updates at the global round
t
, it can be easily seen that

{\mathbb{E}}\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i}(t)-\Delta\theta_{i}(t)\right)^{2}={\mathbb{E}}\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,1}(t)-\Delta\theta_{i}(t)\right)^{2}+\sum_{p=2}^{4}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,p}(t)\right)^{2}.

Lemma 4
.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,1}(t)-\Delta\theta_{i}(t)\right)^{2}\leq\frac{\gamma}{N^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}(t)\|^{2},

(33)

with
\gamma=\max_{n,n^{\prime}}\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}\tr({\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf D}_{n})
.

Proof.

First, substituting
\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,1}(t)
by its expression, we have for
1\leq i\leq d/2

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,1}(t)-\Delta\theta_{i}(t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)-1\right)\Delta\theta_{n,i}(t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)-1\right)^{2}\left(\Delta\theta_{n,i}(t)\ \right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\tr\left({\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}\right)}{c_{n}^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\Delta\theta_{n,i}(t)\ \right)^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\leq}\frac{\gamma}{N^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\Delta\theta_{n,i}(t)\right)^{2},

(34)

where
(a)
follows from the independence of channel vectors
{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)
from each other and from the model updates at global round
t
, and by noting that
\frac{1}{c_{n}}{\mathbb{E}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)=1,\ \forall n
.
(b)
is obtained by computing the expectations over the channels as follows

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)-1\right)^{2}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)\right)^{2}-2{\mathbb{E}}\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)\right)+1

\displaystyle=\frac{\tr({\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n})+\left(\tr({\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n})\right)^{2}}{c_{n}^{2}}-\frac{2\tr({\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n})}{c_{n}}+1

\displaystyle=\frac{\tr({\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n})}{c_{n}^{2}}.

And
(c)
follows from the definition of
\gamma
.
Similarly, we obtain for
d/2+1\leq i\leq d
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,1}(t)-\Delta\theta_{i}(t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i-d/2}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n}^{i-d/2}(t)-1\right)\Delta\theta_{n,i}(t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle{\leq}\frac{\gamma}{N^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\Delta\theta_{n,i}(t)\right)^{2}.

(35)

Lemma
4
follows readily from (
34
) and (
35
).
∎

Lemma 5
.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,2}(t)\right)^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\gamma(N-1)}{N^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}(t)\|^{2}

(36)

with
\gamma=\max_{n,n^{\prime}}\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}\tr({\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf D}_{n})
.

Proof.

For
1\leq i\leq d/2
, replacing
\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,2}(t)
by its expression given in (
28
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,2}(t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\!{\mathbb{E}}\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\\
n^{\prime}\neq n\end{subarray}}^{N}Re\left\{\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n^{\prime}}^{i}(t)\right)\left(\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i}(t)+j\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i+d/2}(t)\right)\right\}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\!{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\frac{1}{N^{2}}\!\sum_{n=1}^{N}\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\\
n^{\prime}\neq n\end{subarray}}^{N}\!\left(\!Re\left\{\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n^{\prime}}^{i}(t)\!\right)\left(\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i}(t)+j\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i+d/2}(t)\right)\right\}\!\right)^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+Re\left\{\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n^{\prime}}^{i}(t)\right)\left(\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i}(t)+j\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i+d/2}(t)\right)\right\}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad Re\left\{\left(\frac{1}{c_{n^{\prime}}}{\bf h}_{n^{\prime}}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)\right)\left(\Delta\theta_{n,i}(t)+j\Delta\theta_{n,i+d/2}(t)\right)\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\\
n^{\prime}\neq n\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}\tr({\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf D}_{n})\left(\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i}(t)^{2}+\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i+d/2}(t)^{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\frac{1}{c_{n}c_{n^{\prime}}}\tr({\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf D}_{n^{\prime}})\left(\Delta\theta_{n,i}(t)\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i}(t)-\Delta\theta_{n,i+d/2}(t)\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i+d/2}(t)\right)\right]

where
(a)
follows from the independence of the channels
{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t),\ \forall n,i
, and
(b)
is obtained by taking the expectation over the channels.
Similarly, for
d/2+1\leq i\leq d
, following the same steps, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,2}(t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\!{\mathbb{E}}\!\left(\!\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\\
n^{\prime}\neq n\end{subarray}}^{N}Im\left\{\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n^{\prime}}^{i}(t)\right)\left(\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i-d/2}(t)+j\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i}(t)\right)\right\}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\!\frac{1}{N^{2}}\!\sum_{n=1}^{N}\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\\
n^{\prime}\neq n\end{subarray}}^{N}\!\left(Im\left\{\left(\!\frac{1}{c_{n}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n^{\prime}}^{i}(t)\!\right)\left(\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i-d/2}(t)+j\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i}(t)\right)\right\}\right)^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+Im\left\{\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n^{\prime}}^{i}(t)\right)\left(\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i-d/2}(t)+j\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i}(t)\right)\right\}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad Im\left\{\left(\frac{1}{c_{n^{\prime}}}{\bf h}_{n^{\prime}}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)\right)\left(\Delta\theta_{n,i-d/2}(t)+j\Delta\theta_{n,i}(t)\right)\right\}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\\
n^{\prime}\neq n\end{subarray}}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}\tr({\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf D}_{n})\left(\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i-d/2}(t)^{2}+\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i}(t)^{2}\right)\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\quad+\frac{1}{c_{n}c_{n^{\prime}}}\tr({\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf D}_{n^{\prime}})\!\left(\Delta\theta_{n,i}(t)\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i}(t)-\Delta\theta_{n,i-d/2}(t)\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i-d/2}(t)\right)\right)\!\right].

Summing over all
i\in[d]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,2}(t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\\
n^{\prime}\neq n\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}\tr({\bf R}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}){\mathbb{E}}\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma(N-1)}{N^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}(t)\|^{2},

where the last inequality follows from the definition
\gamma
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,3}(t)\right)^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\tilde{\gamma}}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}(t)\|^{2},

(37)

where
\tilde{\gamma}=\max_{n,n^{\prime}}\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}\tr({\bf C}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf D}_{n})
.

Proof.

Applying the same techniques as in the proof of the previous lemma, we have for
1\leq i\leq d/2
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,3}(t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\frac{\alpha_{t}}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\end{subarray}}^{N}Re\left\{\frac{\tilde{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n^{\prime}}^{i}(t)}{c_{n}}\left(\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i}(t)+j\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i+\frac{d}{2}}(t)\right)\right\}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{\alpha_{t}}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\end{subarray}}^{N}\left(Re\left\{\frac{\tilde{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf h}_{n^{\prime}}^{i}(t)}{c_{n}}\left(\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i}(t)+j\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i+\frac{d}{2}}(t)\right)\right\}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}\tr({\bf C}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf D}_{n})\left(\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i}(t)^{2}+\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i+d/2}(t)^{2}\right)\right].

Similarly, for
d/2+1\leq i\leq d
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,3}(t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}\tr({\bf C}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf D}_{n})\left(\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i-d/2}(t)^{2}+\Delta\theta_{n^{\prime},i}(t)^{2}\right)\right].

Summing over
i\in[d]

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,3}(t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}=1\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}\tr({\bf C}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf R}_{n^{\prime}}{\bf D}_{n}){\mathbb{E}}\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tilde{\gamma}}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}(t)\|^{2}.

∎

Lemma 7
.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,4}(t)\right)^{2}\right]\leq\frac{d}{2N\alpha_{t}^{2}}(\kappa+\tilde{\kappa})\sigma_{z}^{2},

(38)

with
\kappa=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{c_{n}}
and
\tilde{\kappa}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\tr({\bf C}_{n}{\bf D}_{n})}{c_{n}^{2}}
.

Proof.

For
1\leq i\leq d/2
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,4}(t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left(\frac{1}{N\alpha_{t}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}Re\left\{\frac{\left({\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)+\tilde{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)\ \right)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf z}^{i}(t)}{c_{n}}\right\}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}{\mathbb{E}}\frac{1}{N^{2}\alpha_{t}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}\left(Re\left\{{{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf z}^{i}(t)}{}\right\}\right)^{2}+\left(Re\left\{{\tilde{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf z}^{i}(t)}\right\}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}\frac{1}{2N\alpha_{t}^{2}}(\kappa+\tilde{\kappa})\sigma_{z}^{2},

where
(a)
follows from the independence of the channels
{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)
, the channels error
\tilde{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)
and the noise, and
(b)
follows from

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\left(Re\left\{{{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf z}^{i}(t)}\right\}\right)^{2}=\frac{\sigma_{z}^{2}}{2c_{n}^{2}}\tr({\bf R}_{n}{\bf D}_{n})=\frac{\sigma_{z}^{2}}{2c_{n}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{n}^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\left(Re\left\{{\tilde{\bf h}_{n}^{i}(t)^{H}{\bf D}_{n}{\bf z}^{i}(t)}{}\right\}\right)^{2}=\frac{\sigma_{z}^{2}}{2c_{n}^{2}}\tr({\bf C}_{n}{\bf D}_{n}).

Similar analysis yields for for

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\Delta\widehat{\theta}_{i,4}(t)\right)^{2}\right]=\frac{1}{2N\alpha_{t}^{2}}(\kappa+\tilde{\kappa})\sigma_{z}^{2}.

∎

Combining the results of Lemmas
4
-
7
, it follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t+1)-{\boldsymbol{\nu}}(t+1)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma+\tilde{\gamma}}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{d}{2N\alpha_{t}^{2}}(\kappa+\tilde{\kappa})\sigma_{z}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma+\tilde{\gamma}}{N}\eta_{t}^{2}\tau^{2}G^{2}+\frac{d}{2N\alpha_{t}^{2}}(\kappa+\tilde{\kappa})\sigma_{z}^{2},

(39)

where the last result follows from

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}(t)\|^{2}={\mathbb{E}}\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{\tau-1}\eta_{t}F_{n,\xi_{n,i}^{t}}^{\prime}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}^{n}(t))\right\|^{2}\leq\eta_{t}^{2}\tau\sum_{i=0}^{\tau-1}\left\|F_{n,\xi_{n,i}^{t}}^{\prime}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{i}^{n}(t))\right\|^{2}\leq\eta_{t}^{2}\tau^{2}G^{2}.

(40)

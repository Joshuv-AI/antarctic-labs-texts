Abstract

This article presents a tutorial overview of the
\mathbb{HR}
-calculus. The article presents the basic formulation of gradient for quaternion-valued functions, useful derivative rules, and their application in formulation of adaptive information processing techniques. Moreover, advantages of using quaternion-valued processing techniques, as compared to their real and complex-valued counterparts, are demonstrated in a number applications ranging from power system monitoring to quantum computing.

Overview of Quaternions

Concerned with representing rotations in three-dimensional space, in a manner analogous to that achieved via complex-valued numbers for rotations in two-dimensional spaces, Sir William Rowan Hamilton formulated the basis of quaternion algebra. A quaternion number
q\in\mathbb{H}
, consists of a real part,
\Re\{q\}

\Im\{q\}

\Im_{\imath}\{q\}

\Im_{\jmath}\{q\}

\Im_{\kappa}\{q\}

[

(1)

where
\{q_{r},q_{\imath},q_{\jmath},q_{\kappa}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
, with
\{\imath,\jmath,\kappa\}
admitting the product rules
[

]

\imath\jmath=\kappa,\hskip 3.41418pt\jmath\kappa=\imath,\hskip 3.41418pt\kappa\imath=\jmath,\hskip 3.41418pt\imath^{2}=\jmath^{2}=\kappa^{2}=\imath\jmath\kappa=-1.

(2)

The product of

can be found through component-wise multiplication of their real-valued constituents using the product rules in (
2

I-C

Main Advantages of Quaternions

Up to this point, focus of the information processing community has mainly been on the use of real-valued vector algebras. In principle, these techniques can handle data of any dimensions. Thus, the validity of deriving information processing techniques in the quaternion domain is sometimes questioned. The answer to these questions can be classified as follows:

•

Dealing with Rotations
: Representing rotations via quaternions, as compared to Euler angles and real-valued rotation matrix algebras, holds a number of advantages. First, real-valued rotation matrices lose a degree of freedom as one of the rotation angles reaches
\frac{\pi}{2}

[
8
]
, whereas this is not the case for quaternions.
2
2

2

This is a phenomena referred to as algebraic gimbal-lock
[
8
,
10
]
.
Second, real-valued rotation matrices must have a unit determinant. This is hard to guarantee after many rotations have been computed due to the finite accuracy of computer operations. This problem does not arise in the quaternion representation
[
1
,
8
]
. This vantage point is used in aerospace
[
10
,
8
]
, body motion tracking
[
11
]
, and modelling of quantum computing gates
[
4
]
.

•

Solving Differential Equations
: In engineering, behaviour of most physical phenomena is explainable via a set of real-valued differential equations. Finding, even numerical solutions to these equations in the real domain is not a straightforward affair. However, using transformations such as the Fourier and Z-transforms allow transition to a new complex-valued domain, where solutions are attainable with relative ease. The quaternion domain accommodates for similar approaches in cases where the underlying signal is two or three-dimensional, e.g. multi-channel sound processing
[
12
,
13
]
, colour image processing
[
14
]
, and communication techniques that adopt space-time-polarisation multiplexing
[
15
,
16

Moreover, in a number of applications, such as power system modelling, both of the raised issues come into play
[

,
17

III-A

Quaternion-Valued Least Mean Square (QLMS)

Given the most elementary learning task, the aim is to learn weight vector
\mathbf{w}_{\left[n\right]}
so that for input sequence
\{\mathbf{z}_{[n]}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
,
\hat{y}_{n}=\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{T}}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{z}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}
tracks observation sequence
\{y_{\left[n\right]}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
in a manner that minimises the cost function

J_{\left[n\right]}=\|\epsilon_{\left[n\right]}\|^{2}\hskip 7.39755pt\text{with}\hskip 7.39755pt\epsilon_{\left[n\right]}=y_{\left[n\right]}-\hat{y}_{\left[n\right]}.

(27)

This can be achieved via iterative updates of the weight vector as

\mathbf{w}_{\left[n+1\right]}=\mathbf{w}_{\left[n\right]}-\gamma\nabla_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}J_{\left[n\right]}

(28)

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is an adaptation gain. Now, considering that
\|\epsilon_{\left[n\right]}\|\in\mathbb{R}
and using the chain derivative rule of the
\mathbb{HR}
-calculus given in (
26
), we have

\nabla_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}J_{\left[n\right]}=\frac{\partial\|\epsilon_{\left[n\right]}\|^{2}}{\partial\|\epsilon_{\left[n\right]}\|}\nabla_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\|\epsilon_{\left[n\right]}\|=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{z}^{a*}_{\left[n\right]}.

(29)

Thus, the weight update term becomes

\mathbf{w}_{\left[n+1\right]}=\mathbf{w}_{\left[n\right]}+\gamma\epsilon_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{z}^{a*}_{\left[n\right]}

(30)

with the
\frac{1}{2}
factor absorbed into the adaptation gain. Alternatively, using the multiplication rule in (
21
), we can also have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}J_{\left[n\right]}=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\|\epsilon_{\left[n\right]}\|^{2}=\nabla_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\left(\epsilon^{*}_{\left[n\right]}\epsilon_{\left[n\right]}\right)=\epsilon^{*}_{\left[n\right]}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\epsilon_{\left[n\right]}\right)+\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{w}^{\epsilon_{\left[n\right]*}}}\epsilon^{*}_{\left[n\right]}\right)\epsilon_{\left[n\right]}

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{*}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{z}^{a*}_{\left[n\right]}-\mathbf{z}^{a*}_{\left[n\right]}\Re\left\{\epsilon_{\left[n\right]}\right\}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{z}^{a*}_{\left[n\right]}.

There are different derivations available, e.g. see
[
26
,
1
,
23
]
. Although these derivations might seem different from those in (
30
) and (
31
), they operate in the same manner.

Finally, if we assume that
\mathbf{w}^{\text{opt}}
exists so that
y_{\left[n\right]}=\mathbf{w}^{\text{opt}^{\mathsf{T}}}\mathbf{z}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}\hskip 7.39755pt\text{and}\hskip 7.39755pt\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\left[n\right]}=\mathbf{w}^{\text{opt}}-\mathbf{w}_{\left[n\right]}
. Then, from (
30
) it follows that
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n+1\right]}=\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}-\gamma\mathbf{z}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{z}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}
which in turn yields the quaternion-valued Lyapunov function

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{a}_{\left[n+1\right]}}=\left(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}\right)\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{\left[n\right]}}\left(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}\right)^{\mathsf{H}}

(32)

where
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}}=\mathsf{E}\left\{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\right\}
,
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{z}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}}=\mathsf{E}\left\{\mathbf{z}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{z}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\right\}
, and
\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}=\text{diag}\left\{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{z}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\imath}_{\mathbf{z}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\jmath}_{\mathbf{z}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{\kappa}_{\mathbf{z}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}}\right\}
. Thus, if
\gamma<\mathsf{p}\left\{\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}\right\}
and
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{z}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}}
is full rank; then, as
n\rightarrow\infty
one has
\mathsf{p}\left\{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{z}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}}\right\}\rightarrow 0
, resulting in a convergent filtering operation.

III-B

The Quaternion Kalman Filter

The aim is to track the state vector sequence
\{\mathbf{x}_{\left[n\right]}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
representing the sate of a physical system and formulated as

\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[n+1\right]}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}+\mathbf{v}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}

(35)

where
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{v}_{\left[n\right]}
are the evolution function and a noise vector at time instant
n
. Informative observations regarding the state vector are assumed available through observations

\mathbf{y}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}+\mathbf{w}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}

(36)

where
\mathbf{H}
denotes the observation function, while
\mathbf{y}_{\left[n\right]}
and
\mathbf{w}_{\left[n\right]}
are the observation and observation noise at time instant
n
. The aim is to formulate adaptation gain
\mathbf{G}_{[n]}
so that the estimate

\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{a}_{\left[n+1\right]}=\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}+\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}\left(\mathbf{y}_{\left[n\right]}-\mathbf{H}\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}\right)

(37)

\left\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\left[n+1\right]}\right\|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\mathsf{tr}\left\{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{a}_{\left[n+1\right]}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n+1\right]}\right\}
where

\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{a}_{\left[n+1\right]}=\mathbf{x}^{a}_{[n]}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{a}_{[n]}=\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{H}\right)\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}+\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{H}\right)\mathbf{v}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}+\mathbf{G}_{n}\mathbf{w}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}.

(38)

From (
38
), we have

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{a}_{\left[n+1\right]}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n+1\right]}=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{H}\right)\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{H}}\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{H}\right)^{\mathsf{H}}

(39)

\displaystyle+\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{H}\right)\mathbf{v}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{v}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{H}\right)^{\mathsf{H}}+\mathbf{G}_{n}\mathbf{w}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{w}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{G}^{\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}.

Taking the statistical expectation of (
39
) and solving for
\partial\mathsf{E}\left\{\left\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\left[n\right]}\right\|^{2}\right\}/\partial\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}=0
yields

\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}=\mathbf{M}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{H}}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{w}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}}^{-1}

(40)

where
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{w}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}}=\mathsf{E}\left\{\mathbf{w}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{w}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\right\}
and
\mathbf{M}_{[n]}=\mathsf{E}\left\{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\right\}
are positive semi-definite matrices. Furthermore, replacing (
40
) into (
39
) gives the Riccati recursions

\mathbf{M}_{\left[n+1\right]}=\left(\left(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{M}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{H}}+\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{v}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}}\right)^{-1}+\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{H}}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}_{\mathbf{w}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}}\mathbf{H}\right)^{-1}.

(41)

III-C

Linear Dynamic Programming and Initial Elements of Reinforcement Learning

Consider the optimisation problem formulated as:

\displaystyle\text{minimise}:

\displaystyle\hskip 4.55254ptJ=\mathbf{x}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[N\right]}\mathbf{T}^{a}\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[N\right]}+\sum^{N-1}_{n=1}\left(\mathbf{x}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}+\mathbf{u}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{u}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}\right)

(48)

so that:

\displaystyle\hskip 4.55254pt\mathbf{u}_{\left[n\right]}=\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{x}^{a}_{n}\hskip 7.39755pt\text{and}\hskip 7.39755pt\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[n+1\right]}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}

(49)

where
\{\mathbf{T},\mathbf{R},\mathbf{Q}\}
are Hermitian transpose symmetric and positive semi-definite, that is,
\forall\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{H}^{M}\hskip 3.41418pt\&\hskip 3.41418pt\mathbf{M}\in\{\mathbf{T},\mathbf{R},\mathbf{Q}\}:\hskip 4.55254pt\mathbf{z}^{a\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{z}^{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
. The aim is to find inputs,
\left\{\mathbf{u}_{\left[1\right]},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{\left[N\right]}\right\}
, to dynamic system,
\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[n+1\right]}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}
, so that cost function in (
48
) is minimised. In this context,
\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}
is the feedback gain matrix, referred to as optimal policy in reinforcement learning problems
[
27
]
.

The solution requires calculation of the optimal feedback gain matrix at each time instant. To this end, the cost function in (
48
) is sectioned into time specific cost functions given by

J_{\left[n\right]}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{x}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[N\right]}\mathbf{T}\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[N\right]}&\text{if }n=N,\\
\mathbf{x}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}+\mathbf{u}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{u}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(50)

Now, upon inserting (
49
) into (
50
) we have

J_{\left[n\right]}=\mathbf{x}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}+\mathbf{u}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{u}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}=\mathbf{x}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}

(51)

with
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\left[n\right]}=\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{G}^{\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}
. The expressions in (
50
) and (
51
) allow the time accumulative cost function to be defined as

{J}_{\left[n\right]}=\sum^{N}_{m=n}J_{\left[m\right]}.

(52)

This indicates that there exists a positive definite Hermitian symmetric matrix
\mathbf{P}_{\left[n\right]}
, the existence of which is proven in SM-6, such that

{J}_{\left[n\right]}=\mathbf{x}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{P}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}.

(53)

This extends the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman recursions (see
[
20
]
) for solving quaternion-valued optimisation problems.

Moving backwards in time with the implicit assumption that inputs for future time instances have been obtained, the task becomes that of calculating the input at the previous time instant resulting in

{J}_{\left[n\right]}=\underset{\mathbf{u}_{\left[n\right]}}{\text{min}}\left\{\mathbf{x}^{a\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}+\mathbf{u}^{\mathsf{H}}_{\left[n\right]}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{u}_{\left[n\right]}+{J}_{\left[n\right]}\right\}.

(54)

The minimum of (
54

\mathbf{u}_{\left[n\right]}=-\left(\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{P}_{\left[n+1\right]}\mathbf{B}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{P}_{\left[n+1\right]}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{a}_{\left[n\right]}.

(55)

Thus, from (
55

49

\mathbf{G}_{\left[n\right]}=-\left(\mathbf{R}+\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{P}_{\left[n+1\right]}\mathbf{B}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{P}_{\left[n+1\right]}\mathbf{A}.

(56)

An example for the use of quaternion linear dynamic programming for aircraft control is presented in SM-7. However, next we will delve into a more recent development, that is the use of quaternions in quantum computing.

The fundamental unit of information in quantum computing is referred to as a quantum bit or qubit. In contrast to a classical bit that can only exist in a state of one or zero, a qubit can exist in both states simultaneously. This results from the principles of quantum superposition. Denoting state of zero (
cf
. one) with
|0\rangle
(
cf
.
|1\rangle

\forall\{\alpha,\beta\}\subset\mathbb{C}\wedge\|\alpha\|^{2}+\|\beta\|^{2}=1:\hskip 7.39755pt|\psi\rangle=\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle

(57)

where
|\psi\rangle
represents state of the qubit while
\|\alpha\|^{2}
and
\|\beta\|^{2}
correspond to the probability of finding the qubit in state
|0\rangle
or
|1\rangle
.

8

Note that qubit states arise from quantum characteristics of a particle, e.g. the up or down spin of an electron.
Although use of two complex-valued variables

and
\beta

can be described via a pure imaginary quaternion

and

. This representation is referred to as Bloch sphere. In general, an operation of a quantum computer corresponds to manipulations of the quantum state, or groups of quantum states. These operations are effectively modelled in the quaternion domain using the quaternion rotations as formulated in (

\mathbb{HR}
-calculus tools. For example, let us consider the quantum compiler problem.

The input and output of quantum computers are unit pure imaginary quaternions, the setting derived in this article allows all quantum computing operations to be modelled effectively using the division algebra of quaternions, e.g. quaternion-valued involution and rotation. To this end, consider the general quantum computing operation represented by the mapping

\mathbf{q}_{\text{out}}\leftarrow\mathrm{M}\left(\mathbf{q}_{\text{in}}\right)

(59)

where

\hat{\mathbf{q}}_{\text{out}}\leftarrow\hat{\mathrm{M}}\left(\mathbf{q}_{\text{in}},\mathbf{W}\right)

denoting a parameter matrix determining the characteristics of

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{W}}

\displaystyle\left\{\|\mathrm{f}\left(\mathbf{q}_{\text{in}}\right)-\hat{\mathrm{f}}\left(\mathbf{q}_{\text{in}},\mathbf{W}\right)\|^{2}+\mathrm{g}\left(\mathbf{W}\right)\right\}

(61)

IV
Conclusion

In this article, an overview of key concepts in the
\mathbb{HR}
-calculus that play an integral role in derivation of quaternion-valued information processing techniques have been presented. Moreover, these concepts have been used to formulate a number of adaptive processing techniques, such as the LMS and Kalman filters, in the quaternion domain and demonstrate their advantages. The main vantage point of this approach is that the derived techniques are inherently quaternion-valued, preserving the notion of phase and the division algebra of quaternions. Finally, note that in this article only the concepts of derivatives and gradients have been considered, while formulation of integrals in this framework remains an open issue.

SM-1: Quaternion Rotations and Algebraic Lyapunov/Riccati Recursions

In order to demonstrate application of sensor fusion in the quaternion domain, let us now consider a classical bearings-only-tracking problem. In this class of problems, the goal is to track the trajectory of a manoeuvring target using sensors that can only provide bearing measurements. This problem is often encountered in passive radar, sonar, or infrared tracking applications. As the distance to the target is not known the location of the target can only be resolved from combination of multiple sensor measurements and use of triangulation. However, the natural presentation of phase in quaternions allows for a much more elegant and effective approach to be implemented.

The target location at time instant
n

, in the manner given in Section

\displaystyle q_{\left[n+1\right]}=

\displaystyle q_{\left[n\right]}+v_{\left[n\right]}\Delta T+u_{\left[n\right]}\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta T\right)^{2}

(119)

\displaystyle v_{\left[n+1\right]}=

\displaystyle v_{\left[n\right]}+u_{\left[n\right]}\Delta T

where
v_{\left[n\right]}
and
u_{\left[n\right]}
represent the speed and acceleration (normally modelled as zero-mean Gaussian noise) of the target, with
\Delta T
representing the sampling interval. Moreover, sensor
l

Painlevé/CFT correspondence on a torus
Harini Desiraju

Abstract
This note details the relationship between the isomonodromic tau-function and conformal blocks, on a torus with one simple pole. It is based on the author’s talk at ICMP 2021.



The seminal paper
[
GIL12
]
by Gamayun, Iorgov, Lisovyy introduced the relation between the Painlevé VI equation and conformal field theory (CFT) on a sphere with four punctures. Since then, such a correspondence has been extended to include other Painlevé equations
[
GIL13
,
Bon+17
]
. A rigorous approach to the Painlevé/CFT correspondence comes through the formulation of isomonodromic tau-functions as Fredholm determinants
[
GL18
,
CGL19
,
GL18a
]
. In a recent paper
[
DDG20
]
we rigorously proved the Painlevé/CFT correspondence on the torus with any number of simple poles, using Fredholm determinants. This note provides a detailed treatment of the Painlevé/CFT correspondence for the case of a one-point torus.

1
A quick overview of the story on a Riemann sphere

Painlevé equations were first formulated in the 1900s by Fuchs
[
Fuc05
]
, Gambier
[
Gam10
]
, Painlevé
[
Pai06
]
, and Picard
[
Pic89
]
in their quest to find second order differential equations whose solutions are such that their only movable singularities are poles. There are 6 such equations and they take the form

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}u}{dt^{2}}=F\left(u,\frac{du}{dt},t\right)

(1.1)

with
F
rational in
u,\frac{du}{dt}
, analytic in
t
. The solutions of these equations are transcendental in nature and can be viewed as nonlinear analogues to special functions. A century later, these equations found a renewed purpose in mathematical physics due to the discovery
[
FN80
,
Gar12
,
JMU81
]
that the Painlevé equations are integrable, i.e they have an associated Lax pair

\displaystyle\frac{\partial Y(z,t)}{\partial z}=Y(z,t)L_{1}(z,t),\qquad\frac{\partial Y(z,t)}{\partial t}=Y(z,t)L_{2}(z,t),

(1.2)

where the matrices
L_{1},L_{2}
are meromorphic matrix valued functions on an
n
-puntured Riemann sphere
C_{0,n}
depending on
z,t,u(t),du(t)/dt
, and the consistency condition

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}Y(z,t)}{\partial t\partial z}=\frac{\partial^{2}Y(z,t)}{\partial t\partial z}\Rightarrow\frac{\partial L_{1}}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial L_{2}}{\partial z}-[L_{1},L_{2}]=0

(1.3)

gives back the Painlevé equation (
1.1
). Moreover, the solution
Y(z,t)
is multivalued with the monodromy data (Stokes data included) dictated by the order of the singularities on the Riemann sphere
C_{0,n}
. The Painlevé equation therefore dictates the monodromy preserving deformations of the system (
1.2
), and in this sense, the variable
t
is called the
isomonodromic time
variable.

Painlevé equations also have an associated Hamiltonian structure
[
JMU81
,
Oka86
,
FW01
]

A central object in the study of integrable systems is the so-called tau-function, which not only generates the Hamiltonians governing its dynamics, but also contains important information, in the present case, about the Painlevé transcendent such as the singular locus, and the asymptotic data. Refer to
[
HB21
]
for a thorough treatment of the subject.

Definition 1
.

Isomonodromic tau-function can be formulated as a generator of the Hamiltonian (
1.4
) as
[
JMU81
,
IP18
]

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\log{\mathcal{T}}:=H.

(1.5)

The solutions of Painlevé equations

, known as the Painlevé transcendents, are then typically described by the logarithmic derivatives of the isomonodromic tau-function. For example,
for Painlevé II,

\displaystyle u_{PII}(t)^{2}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\log{\mathcal{T}},

(1.6)

and for the other Painlevé equations, the analogue of the above relations may involve some factors of

.
Due to the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence, which provides a map between the initial data of the Painlevé equation and the associated monodromy data obtained through the solution
Y(z,t)
of the system (
2.2
), the tau-function can be formulated in terms of the monodromy data
[
Mal83
,
Ber17
]
. The zeros of such tau-function determine where the Riemann-Hilbert map ceases to be bijective. Furthermore, as a consequence of the relations such as (
1.6
), the Painlevé transcendents can be expressed as a function of the monodromy data and isomonodromic time. Such an expression is key to understanding the relation to conformal blocks as will be shown in this note for the genus 1 case.

In late 1970s Jimbo, Miwa, Sato (dubbed the Kyoto school) formulated the tau-function as a 2-point correlation function of what they called holonomic fields in a series of papers starting from
[
SMJ77
]
, in a first step towards giving a field theoretic interpretation of Painlevé equations. This program came to full fruition for the Painlevé equations III, V, VI, only in mid 2010s with the papers of Gamayun, Iorgov, Lisovyy
[
GIL12
,
GIL13
]
that established a connection between some Painlevé equations and 2 dimensional CFTs, and was more rigorously proven in
[
CGL19
,
GL18
]
. The crucial technique in
[
GL18
,
CGL19
]
was that the tau-function of three (III, V, VI) of the six (I-VI) Painlevé equations could be written as a Fredholm determinant of some operator
[
GL18
,
CGL19
]
, and its minor expansion is a combinatorial expression in terms of some Virasoro conformal blocks
\mathcal{B}
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\pi in\eta}\mathcal{B}(\sigma+n,t).

(1.7)

For example, the above relation states that the tau-function of the Painlevé VI equation can be expressed in terms of Virasoro conformal blocks
\mathcal{B}
of a CFT with central charge
c=1
that are explicitly determined by hypergeometric functions. The expression (
1.7
) can equivalently, due to the Alday-Gaiotto-Tachikawa (AGT) correspondence
[
AGT10
]
, be written in terms of Nekrasov-Okounkov partition functions of a supersymmetric gauge theory with so called self-dual (
\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}=0
) omega background, which are combinatorial sums over random partitions. The conformal block expansions of the Painlevé equations I, II, IV are conjectured in
[
Bon+17
]
The Fredholm determinant representation for Painlevé II equation was formulated by the author in
[
Des19
,
Des21
]
.
A few comments are in order:

1.

the expression of tau-functions as Fredholm determinants and as conformal blocks is remarkable due to the transcendental nature of the solutions,

2.

the ratio of Painlevé transcendents at the critical points, known as the connection constant is related to the fusion kernel of the associated conformal blocks,

3.

the zero locus of the Fredholm determinant uniquely determines the poles of the transcendent, also known as the Malgrange divisor.

With all of the above discussion in mind, we detail the extension of the relationship between Painlevé type equations and CFTs on the torus with one Fuchsian singularity
C_{1,1}
. It will be shown that the isomonodromic tau-function of a special case of the elliptic Painlevé VI equation can be written, first as a Fredholm determinant, and then as a Fourier series of conformal blocks. The discussion in the following section holds in general for a torus with any number of Fuchsian singularities and the corresponding Lax matrices that are
N\times N
, as shown in the paper of the author along with P. Gavrylenko, F. Del Monte
[
DDG20
]
.

2
Painlevé/CFT correspondence on the one-point torus

Our goal in the paper
[
DDG20
]
was two fold:

1.

to explicitly formulate isomonodromic tau-functions on a torus and thereby find a way to understand the solutions of isomonodromic equations on a torus explicitly,

2.

to make express the isomonodromic tau-functions in terms of Nekrasov-Okounkov functions or equivalently conformal blocks.

We now demonstrate the above points using the simplest example of a torus with one simple pole: consider a special case of the elliptic Painlevé VI equation: the equation of motion of the 2-particle non-autonomous Calogero-Moser system (in the center of mass reference frame),

\displaystyle(2\pi i)^{2}\frac{d^{2}Q(\tau)}{d\tau^{2}}=m^{2}\wp^{\prime}(2Q(\tau)|\tau).

(2.1)

The modular parameter
\tau
assumes the role of the isomonodromic time and brings in an additional factor of
2\pi i
w.r.t to the sphere case in the introduction.
The above equation (

\begin{split}\partial_{z}\mathcal{Y}_{CM}(z,\tau)=\mathcal{Y}_{CM}(z,\tau)L_{1}(z,\tau);\quad 2\pi i\partial_{\tau}\mathcal{Y}_{CM}(z,\tau)=\mathcal{Y}_{CM}(z,\tau)L_{2}(z,\tau),\end{split}

(2.2)

where

\displaystyle L_{1}(z,\tau)=2\pi i\frac{dQ(\tau)}{d\tau}\sigma_{3}+{m}\frac{\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0|\tau)}{\theta_{1}(z|\tau)}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\frac{\theta_{1}(z+2Q(\tau)|\tau)}{\theta_{1}(-2Q(\tau)|\tau)}\\
\frac{\theta_{1}(z-2Q(\tau)|\tau)}{\theta_{1}(2Q(\tau)|\tau)}&0\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle L_{2}(z,\tau)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&my(-2Q(\tau),z)\\
my(2Q(\tau),z)&0\end{array}\right),

and the zero curvature equation (
1.3
)
gives back (

). The notation for the solution
\mathcal{Y}_{CM}
as opposed to

will be addressed in definition

The toric analogue to the Hamiltonian (

(2.7)

where

The tau-function is therefore

\displaystyle 2\pi i\partial_{\tau}\log\mathcal{T}_{CM}(\tau):=H(\tau).

(2.8)

In the form above, the tau-function depends on the initial data of the equation (
2.1

2.1
Monodromy problem

The Lax matrix (
2

z=0
and is multivalued:

\displaystyle L_{1}(z+1,\tau)=L_{1}(z,\tau),

\displaystyle L_{1}(z+\tau,\tau)=e^{-2\pi iQ(\tau)\sigma_{3}}L_{1}(z,\tau)e^{2\pi iQ(\tau)\sigma_{3}}.

(2.9)

In turn, the monodromies around the A, B-cycles, and the puncture at

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{Y}(z+1,\tau)=M_{A}\mathcal{Y}(z,\tau),\qquad\mathcal{Y}(z+\tau,\tau)=M_{B}\mathcal{Y}(z,\tau)e^{2\pi iQ(\tau)\sigma_{3}},\\
\\
\mathcal{Y}(e^{2\pi i}z,\tau)=M_{0}\mathcal{Y}(z,\tau),\end{array}

and the monodromy matrices

M_{0}=M_{A}^{-1}M_{B}^{-1}M_{A}M_{B}.

(2.13)

Explicitly, the monodromies around the A-cycle, and the puncture

\displaystyle M_{A}=e^{2\pi i{a}\sigma_{3}},\qquad M_{0}\sim e^{2\pi i{m}\sigma_{3}},

The parameters

Connection constant

An immediate consequence of the Fredholm determinant representation for the tau-function is the computation of the connection constant, which is equivalent to studying the modular transformation of the toric conformal block. On the torus, the connection constant corresponds to the choice of pants decomposition along the A-cycle vs the B-cycle. In turn, this corresponds to the ratio of the tau function in the limits
\tau\rightarrow i\infty
and
\tau\rightarrow 0
. In an upcoming paper
[

we evaluate this constant.

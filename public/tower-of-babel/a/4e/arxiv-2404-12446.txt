Ground States of Class ​ 𝒮 {\text{Class}\;\mathcal{S}} Theory on ADE Singularities and dual Chern-Simons theory
Emil Albrychiewicz, Andrés Franco Valiente, Ori J. Ganor, Chao Ju

Abstract
In radial quantization, the ground states of a gauge theory on ADE singularities ℝ 4 / Γ \mathbb{R}^{4}/\Gamma are characterized by flat connections that are maps from Γ \Gamma to the gauge group. We study Class ​ 𝒮 {\text{Class}\;\mathcal{S}} theory of type 𝔞 1 = 𝔰 ​ 𝔲 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{a}_{1}=\mathfrak{su}(2) on a Riemann surface of genus g > 1 g>1 , without punctures. The fundamental building block of Class ​ 𝒮 {\text{Class}\;\mathcal{S}} theory is the trifundamental Trinion theory - a low energy limit of two M5 branes compactified on the three-punctured Riemann sphere. We show, through the superconformal index, that the supersymmetric Casimir energy of the trifundamental theory imposes a constraint on the set of allowed flat connections, which agrees with the prediction of a duality relating the ground state Hilbert space of Class ​ 𝒮 {\text{Class}\;\mathcal{S}} on ADE singularities to the Hilbert space of a certain dual Chern-Simons theory whose gauge group is given by the McKay correspondence. The conjecture is shown to hold for Γ = ℤ k \Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{k} , agreeing with the previous results of Benini et al. and Alday et al. A non-abelian generalization of this duality is analyzed by considering the example of the dicyclic group Γ = Dic 2 \Gamma=\text{Dic}_{2} , corresponding to Chern-Simons gauge group SO ( 8 ) (8) .

Casimir energies and the superconformal index

Counting ground states on the SCFT side can be done through the superconformal index, although the latter obviously contains much more information.

3.2.4
A note about
\Delta<0
and unitarity

Let us explain why
\Delta<0
becomes possible when
\Gamma
is nonabelian (without manifestly contradicting unitarity).
In general, BPS states that contribute to the Macdonald limit of the index satisfy

\displaystyle\Delta+2j_{1}-2R-r=0,

(60)

in addition to the general BPS condition
\delta=0=\Delta-2j_{2}-2R+r
. Note that in (
60
) the sign of
r
is opposite to the one in
\delta
.
Since
\Gamma\subset\text{SU}(2)_{1}
, the SU
(2)_{2}
and SU
(2)_{R}
groups are symmetries of the system on
S^{3}/\Gamma
, and therefore states fall into multiplets of SU
(2)_{2}
and SU
(2)_{R}
but not of SU
(2)_{1}
.
The operators that contribute to the index are those components of the multiplet with highest
j_{2}
and
R
. (This is because the superconformal algebra requires
\delta\geq 0
for all states and if there was a state with higher
j_{2}
or
R
in the same multiplet as a state with
\delta=0
it would have negative
\delta
.) Since the highest
j_{2}
and
R
of an
\text{SU}(2)_{2}\times\text{SU}(2)_{R}
multiplet are nonnegative, we arrive at the bounds

\displaystyle\Delta+r\geq 0,\quad\Delta+2j_{1}-r\geq 0,

(61)

that have to be satisfied by any operator that contributes to the index in the Macdonald limit.

5
Generalization and conclusion

In this work, we put forward a conjecture about the action of the duality group on dimension
\Delta=0
operators of a
{\text{Class}\;\mathcal{S}}
theory formulated on
\mathbb{R}^{4}/\Gamma
, where the operators are at the singularity. The conjecture, motivated by Vafa and Witten’s results for
{\mathcal{N}}=4
SYM
[
1
]
is that it is equivalent to the action of the mapping class group of the Riemann surface
\Sigma
(underlying Gaiotto’s construction of the
{\text{Class}\;\mathcal{S}}
theory) on the Hilbert space of Chern-Simons theory on
\Sigma
with gauge group determined by
\Gamma
, through the McKay correspondence, and the level determined by the gauge group of the
{\text{Class}\;\mathcal{S}}
theory.
We provided some modest supporting evidence by matching the number of ground states of the relevant Chern-Simons theory with the number of
\Delta=0
operators at the singularity, for
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{k}
and
\Gamma=\text{Dic}_{2}
, corresponding to Chern-Simons gauge groups SU
(k)
and SO
(8)
. The counting for the abelian
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
proceeded in the same way as previously computed in the literature in the context of the 4d/2d and 3d/3d correspondence
[
8
,
9
,
12
]
, with the difference that our Chern-Simons theory parameters are connected by level-rank duality to a
compact
form of the noncompact Chern-Simons theory that appears in the 3d/3d correspondence, but the calculation is otherwise identical. It relies on the nontrivial Casimir energies that arise for some combinations of flat connections when the
{\text{Class}\;\mathcal{S}}
theory is formulated on
S^{3}/\Gamma
. (For
{\mathcal{N}}=4
SYM, such a Casimir energy does not produce constraints on holonomies, as demonstrated explicitly in
[
55
]
.) A novel aspect of our results, to the best of our knowledge, is the extension of the calculation to a nonabelian
\Gamma=\text{Dic}_{2}
where the
\Delta=0
operators again match with Chern-Simons states, thanks to restrictions due to nontrivial Casimir energies. For the nonabelian
\Gamma
, we also found a state with
\Delta<0
, which is another novelty.
It would be interesting to explore correlation functions of these operators, say, on
T^{4}/\text{Dic}_{2}
, where the action of
\text{Dic}_{2}
is defined so as to produce two singularities that look locally as
\mathbb{R}^{4}/\text{Dic}_{2}
, as well as additional singularities of type
A_{1},A_{3}
.
Extensions to other nonabelian
\Gamma
’s, i.e., corresponding to
E_{6},E_{7},E_{8}
, or
\text{Dic}_{k}
with
k>2
, will be explored elsewhere. Extensions to
{\text{Class}\;\mathcal{S}}
theories derived from D-type or E-type
(2,0)
-theory are also left for future exploration.

Appendix A
Illustration of Casimir Energy computation with index

We give an illustration of the Casimir energy formula (
23
) using the example of a free chiral NS fermion on
S^{1}
.
The modes are
\psi_{-n-\frac{1}{2}}
, with
n=0,1,2,\dots
, the states are

\psi_{-n_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{-n_{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\psi_{-n_{r}-\frac{1}{2}}\ket{0}.

Under the state-operator correspondence, this state corresponds to the operator

\partial^{n_{1}}\psi\partial^{n_{2}}\psi\cdots\partial^{n_{r}}\psi.

The partition function is

Z=e^{-\beta E_{0}}\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-(n+\frac{1}{2})\beta}\right),

where
E_{0}
is the Casimir energy.
We have

\left(1-e^{-(n+\frac{1}{2})\beta}\right)=\text{PE}\left[-e^{-(n+\frac{1}{2})\beta}\right],

and

Z=e^{-\beta E_{0}}\text{PE}\left[-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-(n+\frac{1}{2})\beta}\right],

the single particle index is

I=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-(n+\frac{1}{2})\beta},

and

-\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\partial I}{\partial\beta}\right]_{\beta\rightarrow 0}\longrightarrow-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+\frac{1}{2})

is the (divergent) Casimir energy.
To confirm, we calculate

I=-\frac{e^{-\beta/2}}{1-e^{-\beta}}=-\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{\beta}{24}+\cdots,\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\partial I}{\partial\beta}\right]=-\frac{1}{2\beta^{2}}-\frac{1}{48}+O(\beta^{2}).

We get the regularized Casimir energy of
-\frac{1}{48}
after dropping the divergent term
\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}
and taking the
\beta\to 0
limit.

\mu=\nu=1
, the first constraint implies

n_{1}-n_{2}\equiv 0\pmod{4}.

•

If
\mu=1,\nu=-1
, the first constraint implies

n_{1}-n_{2}\equiv 2\pmod{4}.

Noting that these are for the same constraints as the (155) case [equations (
92
) and (
93
)] except that (
92
) goes with
\nu=-1
this time and (
93
) goes with
\nu=1
, we immediately get

I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{355}=I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{155}=\frac{2(1+2t^{6}+t^{12})(t^{2}-t^{4})}{(1-t^{6})(1-t^{12})}.

(97)

By the same logic,

I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{455}=I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{155}=\frac{2(1+2t^{6}+t^{12})(t^{2}-t^{4})}{(1-t^{6})(1-t^{12})}.

(98)

In summary, for the nonabelian holonomies that appear twice in the triplet, we find

I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{155}=I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{255}=I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{355}=I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{455}=\frac{2(1+2t^{6}+t^{12})(t^{2}-t^{4})}{(1-t^{6})(1-t^{12})}.

(99)

Finally, we need to consider the last case, holonomy (555). A general operator is

\sum c^{\mu\nu\rho n_{1}n_{2}}\partial^{n_{1}}_{+\dot{+}}\partial^{n_{2}}_{-\dot{+}}\Phi_{\mu\nu\rho},

where now all three trifundamental SU
(2)
indices (
\mu,\nu,\rho=\pm 1
) are important. The constraints are

\displaystyle\sum e^{i\pi(n_{1}-n_{2})/2}c^{\mu\nu\rho n_{1}n_{2}}\partial^{n_{1}}_{+\dot{+}}\partial^{n_{2}}_{-\dot{+}}\Phi_{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum e^{i\pi(\mu+\nu+\rho)/2}c^{\mu\nu\rho n_{1}n_{2}}\partial^{n_{1}}_{+\dot{+}}\partial^{n_{2}}_{-\dot{+}}\Phi_{\mu\nu\rho},

\displaystyle\sum(-1)^{n_{2}}c^{\mu\nu\rho n_{1}n_{2}}\partial^{n_{2}}_{+\dot{+}}\partial^{n_{1}}_{-\dot{+}}\Phi_{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum(-1)^{(\mu+\nu+\rho-3)/2}c^{\mu\nu\rho n_{1}n_{2}}\partial^{n_{1}}_{+\dot{+}}\partial^{n_{2}}_{-\dot{+}}\Phi_{-\mu,-\nu,-\rho},

from which we derive

\displaystyle e^{i\pi(n_{1}-n_{2})/2}c^{\mu\nu\rho n_{1}n_{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{i\pi(\mu+\nu+\rho)/2}c^{\mu\nu\rho n_{1}n_{2}},

\displaystyle(-1)^{n_{1}}c^{-\mu,-\nu,-\rho n_{2}n_{1}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(\mu+\nu+\rho-3)/2}c^{\mu\nu\rho n_{1}n_{2}}.

•

For
\mu=1,\nu=1,\rho=1
, we have

n_{1}-n_{2}\equiv 3\pmod{4}.

•

For
\mu=1,\nu=1,\rho=-1
, we have

n_{1}-n_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.

•

For
\mu=1,\nu=-1,\rho=1
, we have

n_{1}-n_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{4}.

•

For
\mu=1,\nu=-1,\rho=-1
, we have

n_{1}-n_{2}\equiv 3\pmod{4}.

For each of the above four cases, the number of pairs
n_{1},n_{2}
that satisfy the mod 4 constraint, with given
n_{1}+n_{2}
, is the coefficient of
t^{3(n_{1}+n_{2})}
in the expansion of

\frac{t^{3}+t^{9}}{(1-t^{6})(1-t^{12})}.

Adding these up, and including the
t^{2}-t^{4}
contribution from the half-hypermultiplet base fields, we have

I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{555}=4\frac{(t^{3}+t^{9})(t^{2}-t^{4})}{(1-t^{6})(1-t^{12})}.

(100)

Now, we have all the components we need to compute the supersymmetric Casimir energy.

B.3
Supersymmetric Casimir Energy computation

When we sum over all the indices to compute the Casimir energy, we need to multiply the vector multiplet contribution by a factor of
1/2
, since each vector multiplet is shared between two Trinions.
We also set
t=\exp\beta
and expand near
\beta=0
. For example, for the triplet (111) we have

\displaystyle I_{111}

\displaystyle=I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{111}+\frac{3}{2}I^{\text{(vec)}}_{1}=-\frac{1}{18\beta}+\frac{9}{2}-\frac{35\beta}{54}+O(\beta^{2}),

which leads, using (
23
), to the supersymmetric Casimir energy contribution from the (111) vertex,

E_{111}=\frac{35}{108}.

(101)

It follows that

E_{111}=E_{122}=E_{133}=E_{144}=E_{234}=\frac{35}{108},

(102)

since these holonomies have the same index contributions as we computed in the previous subsection.

For the other holonomies that do not involve holonomy 5:

\displaystyle I_{112}

\displaystyle=I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{112}+\frac{1}{2}\left(2I^{\text{(vec)}}_{1}+I^{\text{(vec)}}_{1}\right)=-\frac{1}{18\beta}+\frac{9}{2}-\frac{251\beta}{54}+O(\beta^{2}),

(103)

\displaystyle I_{114}

\displaystyle=I_{113}=I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{113}+\frac{1}{2}\left(2I^{\text{(vec)}}_{1}+I^{\text{(vec)}}_{3}\right)=-\frac{1}{18\beta}+\frac{9}{2}-\frac{251\beta}{54}+O(\beta^{2}).

(104)

These show that

\displaystyle E_{112}

\displaystyle=E_{113}=E_{114}=E_{222}=E_{332}=E_{442}=E_{134}=\frac{251}{108},

(105)

\displaystyle E_{224}

\displaystyle=E_{334}=E_{444}=E_{223}=E_{333}=E_{443}=E_{123}=E_{124}=\frac{251}{108}.

(106)

For holonomies that involve a single nonabelian holonomy, we have

\displaystyle I_{115}

\displaystyle=I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{115}+I^{\text{(vec)}}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}I^{\text{(vec)}}_{5}=-\frac{1}{18\beta}+3-\frac{143\beta}{54}+O(\beta^{2}),

(107)

which shows that

E_{115}=E_{225}=E_{335}=E_{445}=\frac{143}{108}.

(108)

Similarly, one can show that
I_{125}=I_{135}=I_{145}=I_{115}
, so

E_{ab5}=E_{115}=\frac{143}{108},

(109)

where
a,b\in\{1,2,3,4\}
. Holonomy triplets that involve only one nonabelian holonomy have the same Casimir energy.

Now, consider holonomy triplets that contain two nonabelian holonomies.

\displaystyle I_{155}

\displaystyle=I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{155}+\frac{1}{2}I^{\text{(vec)}}_{1}+I^{\text{(vec)}}_{5}=-\frac{1}{18\beta}+\frac{3}{2}-\frac{35\beta}{54}+O(\beta^{2}).

(110)

What is surprising is that

E_{155}=\frac{35}{108}=E_{111}.

(111)

From the previous section, we see that

E_{255}=E_{355}=E_{455}=E_{155}=\frac{35}{108}.

(112)

The results we have so far, ranked from lowest energy to highest, is

•

E_{0}=35/108
: (111), (122), (133), (144), (234), (155), (255), (355), (455). These lead to 28 states, and they correspond to the states in the dual
SO(8)
Chern-Simons theory at level 2.

•

E_{1}=143/108=E_{0}+1
:
(ab5)
,
a,b\in\{1,2,3,4\}
. These lead to 48 states.

•

E_{2}=251/108=E_{0}+2
: (112), (113), (114), (332), (442), (222), (134), (224), (334), (223), (443), (333), (444), (123), (124). These lead to 48 states.

There is one more state, (555), that is left to consider.

\displaystyle I_{555}

\displaystyle=I^{\text{(trifund)}}_{555}+\frac{3}{2}I^{\text{(vec)}}_{5}=-\frac{1}{18\beta}+\frac{19}{54}\beta+O(\beta^{2}).

(113)

This case is special as it does not have a constant term in the single letter index, and the supersymmetric Casimir energy is negative:

E_{3}=E_{555}=-\frac{19}{108}=E_{0}-\frac{1}{2}.

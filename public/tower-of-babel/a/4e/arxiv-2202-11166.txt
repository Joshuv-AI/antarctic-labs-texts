Generalized Fubini transform with two variables
Madjid Sebaoui, Diffalah Laissaoui, Ghania Guettai, Mourad Rahmani

Abstract
. In the present paper, we define the generalized Kwang-Wu Chen matrix. Basic properties of this generalization, such as explicit formulas and generating functions are presented. Moreover, we focus on a new class of generalized Fubini polynomials. Then we discuss their relationship with other polynomials such as Fubini, Bell, Eulerian and Frobenius-Euler polynomials. We have also investigated some basic properties related to the degenerate generalized Fubini polynomials.

1.
Introduction

The n
th
Bernoulli numbers
B_{n}
are defined by the generating
function

(1.1)

\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}}B_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\ \ \left|t\right|<2\pi.

The rational numbers
B_{0}=1
,
B_{1}=-1/2
,
B_{2}=1/6
,
B_{3}=0
,
B_{4}=-1/30
and

B_{2n+1}=0
for
n>0
, have many beautiful properties. The most basic
recurrence relation is

(1.2)

{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}}\dbinom{n+1}{k}B_{k}=0.

In 2001, Kwang-Wu Chen
[
5
]
gave an algorithm for computing Bernoulli numbers, with

(1.3)

a_{0,m}=\frac{1}{m+1};\quad a_{n+1,m}=-\left(m+1\right)a_{n,m+1}+ma_{n,m}.

The primary purpose of this paper is to extend the Fubini transform for generalizing Fubini polynomials and studying its properties.
We first generalize (
1.3
). The idea is to construct an infinite matrix
\mathcal{M}:=\left(a_{n,m}\right)_{n,m\geq 0}
in which the first row
a_{0,m}:=\alpha_{m}
of the matrix is the initial sequence and the first column

a_{n,0}:=\beta_{n}
is the final sequence.
More precisely, for nonzero complex numbers
x
and
y
,
we propose to study the following three-term recurrence relation

(1.4)

a_{n+1,m}(x,y)=x\left(m+1\right)a_{n,m+1}(x,y)+yma_{n,m}(x,y).

By setting
x=-1
and
y=1
in (
1.4
), we get (
1.3
).
More directly, we propose to generalize the Fubini transformation.

The Fubini transform
of a sequence
\left(\alpha_{n}\right)_{n\geq 0}
is the sequence
\left(\beta_{n}\right)_{n\geq 0}

given by

\beta_{n}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}}k!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}t^{k}\alpha_{k}

and the inverse transform is

\alpha_{n}=\frac{1}{n!t^{n}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}}s\left(n,k\right)\beta_{k\text{ \ }}.

2.
Definitions and notation

In this section, we introduce some definitions and notations which are useful in the
rest of the paper. Following the usual notations
[
7
]
.

The falling factorial

x^{\underline{n}}

\left(x\in\mathbb{C}\right)
is defined by

x^{\underline{n}}=x\left(x-1\right)\cdots\left(x-n+1\right),x^{\underline{0}}=1

and the rising factorial denoted by
x^{\overline{n}}
, is defined by

x^{\overline{n}}=x\left(x+1\right)\cdots\left(x+n-1\right),x^{\overline{0}}=1.

The (signed) Stirling numbers of the first kind denoted
s\left(n,k\right)

are the coefficients in the expansion

(2.1)

x^{\underline{n}}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}}s\left(n,k\right)x^{k}.

The exponential generating function is

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\left(\ln\left(1+t\right)\right)^{k}

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq k}}s\left(n,k\right)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

and
s\left(n,k\right)
satisfy the following recurrence relation:

(2.3)

s\left(n+1,k\right)=s\left(n,k-1\right)-ns\left(n,k\right)

and that

s\left(n,0\right)=\delta_{n,0}

(n\in\mathbb{N}),

s\left(n,k\right)=0

(k>n
or
k<0),

where
\delta_{n,m}
denoted Kronecker symbol.

The Stirling numbers of the second kind denoted
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}
count
the number of ways to partition a set of
n
things into
k
nonempty subsets. Explicitly

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}
are the coefficients in the expansion

x^{n}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}x^{\underline{k}}.

The
r
-Stirling numbers
[
3
]
denotes
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}_{r}
, for any positive
r\in\mathds{N}
, the number of partitions of a set
of
n
objects into exactly
k
nonempty, disjoint subsets, such that the first
r
elements
are in distinct subsets. These numbers obey the recurrence relation

(2.4)

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}_{r}=0,

n<r,

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}_{r}=\delta_{k,r},

\text{ }n=r,

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}_{r}=k\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n-1}{k}_{r}+\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n-1}{k-1}_{r},

n>r

and

(2.5)

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}_{r}=\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}_{r-1}-(r-1)\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n-1}{k}_{r-1}.

The exponential generating function is given by

(2.6)

\frac{1}{k!}e^{rt}\left(e^{t}-1\right)^{k}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq k}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{n+r}{k+r}_{r}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

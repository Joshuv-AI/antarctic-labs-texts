Introduction

Since the classical works of
Aleksandrov
[
A
]
, Serrin
[
S
]
and
Gidas–Ni–Nirenberg
[
GNN
]
, an intensively studied topic
in the theory of partial differential equations and the calculus of variations focuses
on the radial symmetry of solutions under suitable assumptions
on the equation under consideration, on the boundary conditions and/or on the domain.

In this paper we consider the Finsler framework of a nonlinear anisotropic equation in divergence form.
The setting taken into account is quite general, since it includes nonlinear operators
of
p
-Laplace type, possibly with weights. The domain considered is obtained
from the intersection of a Wulff ball and a cone
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with vertex at its center.
The
results provided are of radial symmetry type, where, given
the possible anisotropy of the ambient space,
the term “radial” is understood in the sense that a function
u
is radial
if there exists a point
x
such that
u
is constant on the Wulff shapes
centered at
x
(see Section
2
for the detailed mathematical setting).

These results
will be obtained
under suitable
homogeneity or concavity assumptions on the weights
w
and suitable arithmetic
relations between the dimension of the ambient space, the homogeneous exponent of the weight and
the homogeneity of the nonlinear operator.
A pivotal step in our analysis consists in proving that the level sets of the solution
are isoperimetric (and additionally the anisotropic norm of the gradient of the solution is constant
along these level sets). This cornerstone result will be stated in detail in Theorem
1.1

below and then combined with a series of isoperimetric inequalities to establish
that the level sets are Wulff shapes: this, together with the
additional information
on the constancy of the anisotropic norm of the gradient of the solution, establishes
the radial symmetry of the solution. Depending on technical conditions on the (an)isotropy
of the ambient space and on the weights, these radial symmetry results
will be detailed in Theorems
1.4
,

1.5

and
1.6
.

The arguments that we use are inspired by those of J. Serra
in
[

]
, which in turn were inspired by the classical paper of P.-L. Lions
[

]
in which the symmetry was obtained in the Euclidean case with

weaken the smoothness assumptions on the source term
and on the solution with respect to the setting in
[

]

at the expense of restricting to positive nonlinearities and to dimension two only.
Interestingly, the method in

]

]

to the approach to radial symmetry inhaugurated by Weinberger in

Let

Lemma 3.1

(Maximum principle in cones)
.

Let
u
be a (weak) solution of

\begin{cases}-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\left\{w(x)\,H(\nabla u)^{p-1}\,\nabla_{\xi}H(\nabla u)\right\}=\widetilde{f}(x)\,w(x)\ &\mbox{ in }\ \Sigma\cap\Omega,\\

\langle\nabla_{\xi}H(\nabla u),\nu\rangle=0\ &\mbox{ on }\ \Gamma_{1}\setminus\left\{0\right\},\end{cases}

where the weight

is continuous in
\overline{\Sigma}
and positive and locally Lipschitz in
\Sigma
.
If
\widetilde{f}\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma\cap\Omega)

is nonnegative, then

is nonnegative. In particular, if
\widetilde{f}\equiv 0
, then

Proof.

We set
u^{-}:=\max\left\{0,-u\right\}
and we compute

\begin{split}0\leqslant\int_{\Sigma\cap\Omega}H^{p}(\nabla u^{-})\,w(x)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N}&=\int_{\left\{u<0\right\}}H^{p}(\nabla u)\,w(x)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N}\\
&=\int_{\left\{u<0\right\}}H(\nabla u)^{p-1}\,\langle\nabla_{\xi}H(\nabla u),\nabla u\rangle\,w(x)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N}\\
&=\int_{\left\{u<0\right\}}u\,\widetilde{f}\,w(x)\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N}\leqslant 0,\end{split}

where in the second equality we used (
2.3
) and in the third equality we used the boundary value problem.
Thus,
\nabla u^{-}=0
a.e. in
\Sigma\cap\Omega
.
Moreover, by Poincaré inequality (which holds true since
u^{-}=0
on
\Gamma_{0}
and

) we find that

, and hence

\Sigma\cap\Omega
.

(Pohozaev-type identity)

-a.e.

, and hence also for
{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}
-a.e.

Thus,

\begin{split}P_{K}(E)&=\int_{\partial^{*}E}\|\nu_{E}(x)\|_{K_{0}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\\
&=\int_{\Sigma\cap\partial^{*}E}\|\nu_{E}(x)\|_{K_{0}}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\\
&\leqslant\int_{\Sigma\cap\partial^{*}E}H(\nu_{E}(x))\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\\
&=P_{H}(E;\Sigma),\end{split}

where in the last identity we used (

P_{K}(E)\leqslant P_{H}(E;\Sigma)

) (with

.
Thus, we have that

P(K;\Sigma)=N{\mathcal{H}}^{N}(K)=P_{K}(E)=P_{H}(E;\Sigma),

where the first equality follows from (

) (with

By

E=K+a\quad\text{ with }\,a\in\overline{\Sigma}.

Notice that, for any

where

-a.e.

Since

\nu
denotes the exterior unit normal vector field to

\left\{u>t_{0}\right\}=B_{\rho(t_{0})}(x(t_{0}))
.
Moreover, since
\overline{\xi}\in B=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,H_{0}(x)=1\right\}
, by (
2.1
) it holds that

(5.18)

\langle\nu_{B}(\overline{\xi}),\overline{\xi}\rangle=H(\nu_{B}(\overline{\xi})).

We also observe that

(5.19)

\nu\left(P(t_{0})\right)=\nu_{B}(\overline{\xi})=-\nu_{B}(-\overline{\xi})=-\nu\left(Q(t_{0})\right).

To check this, we let
B_{r}=\left\{H_{0}(x)<r\right\}
be the Wulff ball of radius
r
centered at the origin, and set
B:=B_{1}
.
For any
x\in\partial B_{r}
it holds that

\nu_{B_{r}}(x)=\frac{\nabla_{x}H_{0}(x)}{|\nabla_{x}H_{0}(x)|}\quad\text{and}\quad\nu_{B_{r}}(-x)=\frac{\nabla_{x}H_{0}(-x)}{|\nabla_{x}H_{0}(-x)|}.

Also, by the homogeneity of
H_{0}
we have that

\nabla_{x}H_{0}(tx)=\mathrm{sgn}(t)\nabla_{x}H_{0}(x)\quad\text{for all }\,t\neq 0,\,x\neq 0

and consequently

(5.20)

\nu_{B_{r}}(r\,\xi)=\nu_{B}(\xi)=-\nu_{B}(-\xi)=\nu_{B_{r}}(-r\,\xi)\quad\text{ for any }\xi\in\partial B,\,r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Additionally, taking into account the translation invariance of the normals, we obtain

\nu_{B_{r}(x)}\left(x+r\,\xi\right)=\nu_{B_{r}}\left(r\,\xi\right)\quad\text{ for any }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,r\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\,\xi\in\partial B.

Combining this information and (
5.20
) we obtain (
5.19
), as desired.

Thus, from (
5.18
) and (
5.19
)
we deduce that

\langle\nu\left(P(t_{0})\right),\overline{\xi}\rangle=H\left(\nu\left(P(t_{0})\right)\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad\langle\nu\left(Q(t_{0})\right),\overline{\xi}\rangle=-H\left(\nu\left(Q(t_{0})\right)\right).

From this and (
5.17
), it follows that

(5.21)

\langle\nabla u\left(P(t_{0})\right),\overline{\xi}\rangle=-H\left(\nabla u\left(P(t_{0})\right)\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad\langle\nabla u\left(Q(t_{0})\right),\overline{\xi}\rangle=H\left(\nabla u\left(Q(t_{0})\right)\right).

By using (
5.16
) and (
5.21
), we compute

1=\frac{d}{dt}u\left(P(t)\right)|_{t=t_{0}}=\langle\nabla u\left(P(t_{0})\right),\left(H_{0}(x^{\prime}(t_{0}))+\rho^{\prime}(t_{0})\right)\overline{\xi}\rangle=-H\left(\nabla u\left(P(t_{0})\right)\right)\left(H_{0}(x^{\prime}(t_{0}))+\rho^{\prime}(t_{0})\right)

and

1=\frac{d}{dt}u\left(Q(t)\right)|_{t=t_{0}}=\langle\nabla u\left(Q(t_{0})\right),\left(H_{0}(x^{\prime}(t_{0}))-\rho^{\prime}(t_{0})\right)\overline{\xi}\rangle=H\left(\nabla u\left(Q(t_{0})\right)\right)\left(H_{0}(x^{\prime}(t_{0}))-\rho^{\prime}(t_{0})\right).

But by assumption (ii), we have that
H\left(\nabla u\left(P(t_{0})\right)\right)=H\left(\nabla u\left(Q(t_{0})\right)\right)
. This leads to
H_{0}(x^{\prime}(t_{0}))=0
, which is a contradiction.

Thus,
u
is radially symmetric, i.e.,
u=u(r)
, with
r=H_{0}(x)
. Since we showed that
\nabla u
does not vanish on
\left\{0<u<M\right\}
, we thus have
\partial_{r}u<0
in this open ring. The set
\left\{u=M\right\}
could be a point, but also a closed ball (as it happens, e.g., in the example described in
[
Se
, pag. 1895]
).
This proves the result when
k=N
.

By direct inspection and recalling that
u
is
C^{1}
up to
\Gamma_{1}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, it is easy to check that the same proof remains valid when
1\leqslant k\leqslant N-1
. Notice that in this case we have that
\overline{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\left\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{N-k}}\right\}
.

The case
k=0
is trivial.
∎

We have now all the ingredients to complete the

Proof of Theorems
1.4
,
1.5
and
1.6
.

Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
easily follow by putting together Theorems
1.1
,
4.2
, and Lemma
5.2
.

Theorem
1.6
easily follows by putting together Theorems
1.1
,
4.3
, and Lemma
5.2
.
∎

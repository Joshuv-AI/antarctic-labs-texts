Lower bounds for moments of the derivative of the Riemann zeta function
Peng Gao

Abstract
. We establish in this paper sharp lower bounds for the 2 ​ k 2k -th moment of the derivative of the Riemann zeta function on the critical line for all real k ≥ 0 k\geq 0 .

Keywords

1.
Introduction

It is an important subject in analytical number theory to investigate moments of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
on the critical line as they can be applied to study the maximum size of the zeta function as well as primes in short intervals via zero density estimates. We denote the
2k
-th moment of
\zeta(s)
on the critical line by

\displaystyle M_{k}(T)=\int\limits^{2T}_{T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2k}\mathrm{d}t.

The study on
M_{k}(T)
dates back to the work of G.H. Hardy and J. E. Littlewood
[
13
]
, who established an asymptotic formula for
M_{1}(T)
. In
[
18
]
, A. E. Ingham established an asymptotic formula for
M_{2}(T)
. No other asymptotic formulas are known for
M_{k}(T)
except for the trivial case
k=0
. Despite of this, J. P. Keating and N. C. Snaith
[
21
]
made precise conjectured formulas for
M_{k}(T)
for all real
k\geq 0
by drawing analogues with the random matrix theory.
Using multiple Dirichlet series, A. Diaconu, D. Goldfeld and J. Hoffstein
[
10
]
also obtained the same conjectured formulas. More precise asymptotic formulas with lower order terms were conjectured by J. B. Conrey, D. W. Farmer, J. P. Keating, M. O. Rubinstein and N. C. Snaith in
[
4
]
.

Owing much to the work in
[
29
,
28
,
30
,
17
,
27
,
2
,
32
,
26
,
35
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
6
,
34
,
1
,
5
,
7
]
, we now have sharp upper and lower bounds for
M_{k}(T)
of the conjectured order of magnitude for all
k\geq 0
with some of them being valid under the truth of the Riemann hypothesis (RH).

Among the many methods applied in the above work, we point out notably a simple and powerful method developed by Z. Rudnick and K. Soundararajan
[
32
,
31
]
towards establishing sharp lower bounds for moments of families of
L
-functions, a method of K. Soundararajan
[
35
]
and its refinement by A. J. Harper
[
14
]
to derive sharp upper bounds for moments of families of
L
-functions under the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH). We note also an upper bounds principle developed by M. Radziwiłł and K. Soundararajan in
[
26
]
for establishing upper bounds for moments of families of
L
-functions as well as its dual lower bounds principle developed by W. Heap and K. Soundararajan in
[
16
]
.

Similar to
M_{k}(T)
, it is also interesting to study moments of the derivatives of
\zeta(s)
on the critical line. For integers
l\geq 1
, let

\displaystyle I_{k,l}(T)=\int\limits^{T}_{1}|\zeta^{(l)}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2k}\mathrm{d}t.

An asymptotic formula for
I_{1,l}(T)
is also given in the above mentioned work of A. E. Ingham
[
18
]
. In
[
9
]
, J. B. Conrey obtained an asymptotic formula for
I_{2,l}(T)
. Also, in connection with the random matric theory, J.B. Conrey, M.O. Rubinstein and N.C. Snaith
[
8
, Conjecture 1]
conjectured that

\displaystyle I_{k,1}(T)\sim a_{k}b_{k}T(\log T)^{k^{2}+2k},

for some explicit constants
a_{k},b_{k}
.

Under RH, M. B. Milinovich
[
24
]
established essentially upper bounds of the correct order of magnitude for
I_{k,l}(T)
for positive integers
k,l
. His result was further improved to yield optimal upper bounds by A. Ivić
[
19
]
for
I_{k,2}(T)
for positive integers
k
. The methods employed in
[
24
]
and
[
19
]
allow one to deduce upper bounds for
I_{k,l}(T)
from the corresponding ones for
M_{k}(T)
. As sharp upper bounds for
M_{k}(T)
are known for all
k\geq 0
under RH from the work of K. Soundararajan
[
35
]
and for all
0\leq k\leq 2
unconditionally from the work of W. Heap, M. Radziwiłł and K. Soundararajan
[
15
]
, we may apply the methods in
[
24
]
and
[
19
]
to derive that unconditionally for
1/2\leq k\leq 2
and under RH for
k\geq 2
, we have for all integers
l\geq 1
,

\displaystyle I_{k,l}(T)\ll_{k,l}T(\log T)^{k^{2}+2kl}.

On the other hand, K. Sono
[
33
]
, T. Christ and J. Kalpokas
[
3
]
studied lower bounds for
I_{k,l}(T)
. It follows from
[
3
, Corollary 1.1]
that we have for any rational
k\geq 1
and any positive integer
l
,

\displaystyle I_{k,l}(T)\gg_{k,l}T(\log T)^{k^{2}+2kl}.

The aim of this paper is to obtain sharp lower bounds for
I_{k,l}(T)
for all real
k\geq 0
. For simplicity, we shall focus on
I_{k,1}(T)
throughout, although our methods carry over to treat
I_{k,l}(T)
for other
l
as well. Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For large
T
and any
k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle I_{k,1}(T)\gg_{k}T(\log T)^{k^{2}+2k}.

Combining Theorem
1.1
and our discussions above, we immediately obtain the following result concerning the order of magnitude of
I_{k,1}(T)
.

Corollary 1.2
.

For large
T
and any
1/2\leq k\leq 2
, we have

\displaystyle I_{k,1}(T)\asymp T(\log T)^{k^{2}+2k}.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on the above mentioned lower bounds principle of W. Heap and K. Soundararajan
[
16
]
, together with the approach taken in
[
3
, Section 5]
. We note that a similar approach to the one used in
[
3
, Section 5]
has already been employed by
M. B. Milinovich and N. Ng
[
22
]
in their study on lower bounds for the discrete moments of the derivative of

\zeta(s)
at nontrivial zeros. Since these discrete moments can be regarded as analogues to
I_{k,1}(T)
and are studied by the author in
[
11
]
, the proof of Theorem
1.1
also makes use of some approaches there as well.

2.
Preliminaries

We reserve the letter
p
for a prime number in this paper and we recall the following well-known results on sums of primes (see
[
25
, Theorem 2.7]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x\geq 2
. We have, for some constant
b
,

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p}=\log\log x+b+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log x}\Big),

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\log p}{p}=\log x+O(1).

We note the following mean value theorem given in
[
23
, Lemma 4.1]
concerning integrals over Dirichlet polynomials.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}
and
\{b_{n}\}
be sequences of complex numbers. Let
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
be positive real numbers and
g(t)
be a real-valued function that is continuously differentiable on the interval
[T_{1},T_{2}]
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int^{T_{2}}_{T_{1}}g(t)\left(\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}a_{n}n^{-it}\right)\left(\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}b_{n}n^{it}\right)dt\\
=&\int^{T_{2}}_{T_{1}}g(t)dt\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}a_{n}b_{n}+O\left(\left(|g(T_{1})|+|g(T_{2})|+\int^{T_{2}}_{T_{1}}|g^{\prime}(t)|dt\right)\left(\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}n|a_{n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}n|b_{n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right).\end{split}

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

3.1.
The lower bound principle

We may assume that
k>0
and
T
is a large number throughout the proof.
We also point out that the explicit constants involved in estimations using
\ll
or the big-
O
notations in the proof depend on
k
only and are uniform with
respect to
p
and
T
.

We follow the ideas of A. J. Harper in
[
14
]
to define for a large number
M
depending on
k
only,

\alpha_{0}=0,\;\;\;\;\;\alpha_{j}=\frac{20^{j-1}}{(\log\log T)^{2}}\;\;\;\forall\;j\geq 1,\quad\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{k,T}=\max\{j:\alpha_{j}\leq 10^{-M}\}.

We denote
\ell_{j}:=\lceil e^{2}k\alpha^{-3/4}_{j}\rceil
for
1\leq j\leq\mathcal{J}
and divide the interval
(0,T^{\alpha_{\mathcal{J}}}]
into disjoint subintervals
I_{j}=(T^{\alpha_{j-1}},T^{\alpha_{j}}],1\leq j\leq\mathcal{J}
. We define for any real number
\ell
and any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle E_{\ell}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil\ell\rceil}\frac{x^{j}}{j!}.

We also define for any real number
\alpha
and any
1\leq j\leq\mathcal{J}
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(s)=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in I_{j}}\frac{1}{p^{s}},\quad{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(s,\alpha)=E_{\ell_{j}}\Big(\alpha{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(s)\Big),\quad{\mathcal{N}}(s,\alpha)=\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(s,\alpha).

We deduce from
[
11
, (3.1)]
and Lemma
2.1
that for any large number
N
, we can take
T
large enough so that

(3.1)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(1)\leq\frac{1}{N}\ell_{1},\quad{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(1)\leq\min(10,\frac{1}{N}\ell_{j}),\quad 2\leq j\leq\mathcal{J}.

We denote
\Omega(n)
for the number of prime powers dividing
n
and
g(n)
for the multiplicative function given on prime powers by
g(p^{r})=1/r!
and define functions
b_{j}(n),1\leq j\leq{\mathcal{J}}
such that
b_{j}(n)=0
or
1
and that
b_{j}(n)=1
only when
\Omega(n)\leq\ell_{j}
and all the prime factors of
n
are from the interval
I_{j}
. We then have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(s,\alpha)=\sum_{n_{j}}\frac{\alpha^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j})\frac{1}{n^{s}_{j}},\quad 1\leq j\leq{\mathcal{J}}.

It follows from
[
11
, Section 3.1]
that each
{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(s,\alpha)
is a short Dirichlet polynomial of length at most
T^{\alpha_{j}\lceil e^{2}k\alpha^{-3/4}_{j}\rceil}
and that
{\mathcal{N}}(s,\alpha)
is also a short Dirichlet polynomial of length at most
T^{40e^{2}k10^{-M/4}}
.

We now write for simplicity that

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(s,\alpha)=\sum_{n}\frac{a_{\alpha}(n)}{n^{s}}.

We note that
a_{\alpha}(n)\neq 0
only when
n=\prod_{1\leq j\leq\mathcal{J}}n_{j}
such that
b_{j}(n_{j})=1
, in which case we have

\displaystyle a_{\alpha}(n)=\prod_{n_{j}}\frac{\alpha^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j}).

We combine
[
11
, (2.1), (3.3)]
to see that for all
n\geq 3
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle a_{\alpha}(n)\leq e^{\frac{|\alpha|\log n}{\log\log n}(1+O(\frac{1}{\log\log n}))}\text{ and}\quad a_{k}(n)=0\text{ when }n>T^{40e^{2}k10^{-M/4}}.

Moreover, we note that
[
11
, (3.4)]
implies that for
\Re(s)\geq-1/\log T
and
T
large enough,

(3.4)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{N}}(s,\alpha)|\ll e^{|\alpha|\frac{\log T}{\log\log T}(1+O(\frac{1}{\log\log T}))}T^{40e^{2}k10^{-M/4}(1+1/\log T)}.

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we need the following lower bounds principle of W. Heap and K. Soundararajan

[
16
]
for our case.

Lemma 3.2
.

With notations as above, we have for
0<k\leq 1/2
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int^{T}_{1}-\zeta^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\mathcal{N}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k-1)\mathcal{N}(\tfrac{1}{2}-it,k)dt\ll&\Big(\int^{T}_{1}|\zeta^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2k}dt\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int^{T}_{1}|\zeta^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}|\mathcal{N}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k-1)|^{2}dt\Big)^{(1-k)/2}\\
&\times\Big(\int^{T}_{1}\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\big(|{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k)|^{2}+|{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k)|^{2r_{k}}\big)dt\Big)^{k/2}.\end{split}

Also, we have for
k>1/2
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int^{T}_{1}-\zeta^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\mathcal{N}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k-1)\mathcal{N}(\tfrac{1}{2}-it,k)dt\\
\leq&\Big(\int^{T}_{1}|\zeta^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2k}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2k}}\Big(\int^{T}_{1}\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\big(|{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k)|^{2}+|{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k)|^{2r_{k}}\big)dt\Big)^{\frac{2k-1}{2k}}.\end{split}

Here the implied constants in (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) depend on
k
only, and we define

{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}(s,k)=\Big(\frac{64\max(2,k+3/2){\mathcal{P}}_{j}(s)}{\ell_{j}}\Big)^{\ell_{j}},

with
r_{k}=2+\lceil 1/k\rceil
for
0<k\leq 1/2
and
r_{k}=1+\lceil 2k/(2k-1)\rceil
for
k>1/2
.

We skip the proof of the above lemma as it can be established similar to those of
[
11
, Lemma 3.2-3.3]
. We deduce from the above lemma that in order to establish Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to prove the following three propositions.

Proposition 3.3
.

With notations as above, we have for
k>0
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{1}-\zeta^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\mathcal{N}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k-1)\mathcal{N}(\tfrac{1}{2}-it,k)dt\gg T(\log T)^{k^{2}+1}.

Proposition 3.4
.

With notations as above, we have for
0<k\leq 1/2
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{1}|\zeta^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}|\mathcal{N}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k-1)|^{2}dt\ll T(\log T)^{k^{2}+2}.

Proposition 3.5
.

With notations as above, we have for
k>0
,

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{1}\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{i=1}\big(|{\mathcal{N}}_{i}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k)|^{2}+|{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k)|^{2r_{k}}\big)dt\ll

\displaystyle T(\log T)^{k^{2}}.

We shall omit the proof of Proposition
3.5
as it is similar to that of
[
11
, Proposition 3.5]
, upon making use of Lemma
2.2
. In the rest of the paper, we shall prove the remaining two propositions.

3.6.
Proof of Proposition
3.3

The proof is based on the approaches used in Section 5 of
[
3
]
and Section 5 of
[
23
]
. We denote the left side expression in (
3.7
) by
S_{1}
and apply Cauchy’s residue theorem to deduce that

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}-\zeta^{\prime}(s)\mathcal{N}(s,k-1)\mathcal{N}(1-s,k)\,ds,

where
\mathcal{C}
consists of line segments from
\tfrac{1}{2}+i
to
\kappa+i
, then from
\kappa+i
to
\kappa+iT
and lastly from
\kappa+iT
to
\tfrac{1}{2}+iT
, where
\kappa=1+(\log T)^{-1}
.

We apply (
3.4
) and the estimation (see
[
12
, (20)]
)

\begin{split}\zeta^{\prime}(s)\ll\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}(1+|t|)^{(1-\Re(s))/2+\epsilon},\quad 0\leq\Re(s)\leq 1,\\
(1+|t|)^{\epsilon},\quad\Re(s)\geq 1,\end{array}\right.\end{split}

to see that the integral is bounded by
O(T^{1-\varepsilon})
on the horizontal edges of the contour. We thus conclude that

(3.9)

\displaystyle S_{1}=S_{1,R}+O(T^{1-\varepsilon}),

where

(3.10)

\displaystyle S_{1,R}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\kappa+i}^{\kappa+iT}-\zeta^{\prime}(s)\mathcal{N}(s,k-1)\mathcal{N}(1-s,k)\,ds+O(T^{1-\varepsilon}).

To evaluate
S_{1,R}
, we define the Dirichlet convolution
f*g
for two arithmetic functions
f(k),g(k)
by

\displaystyle f*g(k)=\sum_{mn=k}f(m)g(n).

Using this notation and that given in (
3.2
), we apply Lemma
2.2
to evaluate
S_{1,R}
in (
3.10
) to see that

\displaystyle S_{1,R}=

\displaystyle\frac{T-1}{2\pi}\sum_{n}\frac{(\log*a_{k-1})(n)\cdot a_{k}(n)}{n}+O\left(\left(\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{(\log*a_{k-1})(n)^{2}}{n^{2\kappa-1}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\left(\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{a_{k}(n)^{2}}{n^{1-2\kappa}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\right).

We apply the estimations given in (
3.3
) to see that for
T
large enough,

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{a_{k}(n)^{2}}{n^{1-2\kappa}}\ll e^{4k\log T/\log\log T}\sum_{n\leq T^{40e^{2}k10^{-M/4}}}\frac{1}{n^{1-2\kappa}}\ll T^{1-\varepsilon}.

Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\log*a_{k-1})(n)\leq\log n\sum_{n\leq T^{40e^{2}k10^{-M/4}}}|a_{k-1}(n)|\leq T^{1/2-\varepsilon}\log n.\end{split}

It follows from the above that

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{(\log*a_{k-1})(n)^{2}}{n^{2\kappa-1}}\ll T^{1-2\varepsilon}\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{\log^{2}n}{n^{2\kappa-1}}\ll T^{1-\varepsilon},

where the last estimation above follows from the bound that (see
[
23
, (16)]
) uniformly for
\sigma>1
and any integer
i\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{\log^{i}n}{n^{\sigma}}\ll\frac{1}{(\sigma-1)^{i+1}}.

We then conclude from the above discussions that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{1,R}=&\frac{T-1}{2\pi}\sum_{n,m}\frac{a_{k-1}(m)a_{k}(mn)(\log n)}{mn}+O(T^{1-\varepsilon})\\
=&\frac{T-1}{2\pi}\sum_{n}\frac{\log n}{n}\sum_{m}\frac{a_{k-1}(m)a_{k}(mn)}{m}+O(T^{1-\varepsilon}).\end{split}

It remains to estimate the last expression above. To do so, we may assume that
n=\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}n_{j}
with
b_{j}(n_{j})=1
for
1\leq j\leq\mathcal{J}
. Then the inner sum of the last expression above becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{m}\frac{a_{k-1}(m)a_{k}(mn)}{m}=&\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\Big(\sum_{m_{j}}\frac{1}{m_{j}}\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j}m_{j})}(k-1)^{\Omega(m_{j})}}{g(n_{j}m_{j})g(m_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j}m_{j})b_{j}(m_{j})\Big)\\
=&\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\Big(\sum_{m_{j}}\frac{1}{m_{j}}\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j}m_{j})}(k-1)^{\Omega(m_{j})}}{g(n_{j}m_{j})g(m_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j}m_{j})\Big),\end{split}

where the last equality above follows by noting that
b_{j}(n_{j}m_{j})=1
implies that
b_{j}(m_{j})=1
for all
1\leq j\leq\mathcal{J}
.

Note that the factor
b_{j}(n_{j}m_{j})
restricts
m_{j}
to have all prime factors in
I_{j}
such that
\Omega(n_{j}m_{j})\leq\ell_{j}
. If we remove this restrictions on
\Omega
, then the sum over
m_{j}
becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{m_{j}}\frac{1}{m_{j}}\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j}m_{j})}(k-1)^{\Omega(m_{j})}}{g(n_{j}m_{j})g(m_{j})}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
(p,n_{j})=1\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{k(k-1)}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{i,j}\in I_{j}\\
p^{l_{i,j}}_{i,j}\|n_{j}\\
l_{i,j}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{k^{l_{i,j}}}{l_{i,j}!}+\frac{k^{l_{i,j}+1}(k-1)}{(l_{i,j}+1)!p_{i,j}}+\frac{k^{l_{i,j}+2}(k-1)^{2}}{(l_{i,j}+2)!2!p^{2}_{i,j}}+\cdots\Big).\end{split}

We recast the last product above as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{i,j}\in I_{j}\\
p^{l_{i,j}}_{i,j}\|n_{j}\\
l_{i,j}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{k^{l_{i,j}}}{l_{i,j}!}+\frac{k^{l_{i,j}+1}(k-1)}{(l_{i,j}+1)!p_{i,j}}+\frac{k^{l_{i,j}+2}(k-1)^{2}}{(l_{i,j}+2)!2!p^{2}_{i,j}}+\cdots\Big)=\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big),\end{split}

and we note that each factor in the last product above is positive.

Using Rankin’s trick by noticing that
2^{\Omega(n_{j}m_{j})-\ell_{j}}\geq 1
if
\Omega(n_{j}m_{j})>\ell_{j}
, we see that the error
introduced this way does not exceed

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{m_{j}}\frac{1}{m_{j}}\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j}m_{j})}|1-k|^{\Omega(m_{j})}}{g(n_{j}m_{j})g(m_{j})}2^{\Omega(n_{j}m_{j})-\ell_{j}}\\
=&2^{\Omega(n_{j})-\ell_{j}}\sum_{m_{j}}\frac{1}{m_{j}}\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j}m_{j})}2^{\Omega(m_{j})}|1-k|^{\Omega(m_{j})}}{g(n_{j}m_{j})g(m_{j})}\\
\leq&2^{\Omega(n_{j})-\ell_{j}/2}\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
(p,n_{j})=1\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{k(k-1)}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big),\end{split}

where the last estimation above follows from (
3.1
).

It follows that we may write

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{m}\frac{a_{k-1}(m)a_{k}(mn)}{m}=&\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\bigcup^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}I_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{k(k-1)}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)\\
&\times\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\Big(1+f_{j}(n_{j})\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)\Big(1+\frac{k(k-1)}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)^{-1}\\
=&\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\bigcup^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}I_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{k(k-1)}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\Big(1+f_{j}(n_{j})\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big),\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}|f_{j}(n_{j})|\leq 2^{\Omega(n_{j})-\ell_{j}/2}.\end{split}

We apply the above estimation to see that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{n}\frac{\log n}{n}\sum_{m}\frac{a_{k-1}(m)a_{k}(mn)}{m}\\
=&\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\bigcup^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}I_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{k(k-1)}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=\prod_{j}n_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log n}{n}\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\Big(1+f_{j}(n_{j})\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big).\end{split}

Note that we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=\prod_{j}n_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log n}{n}\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\Big(1+f_{j}(n_{j})\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)\\
=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=\prod_{j}n_{j}\end{subarray}}\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\frac{1}{n_{j}}\Big(1+f_{j}(n_{j})\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)\Big(\sum_{j}\log n_{j}\Big)\\
=&\sum^{\mathcal{J}}_{j^{\prime}=1}\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq j^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n_{j}}\Big(1+f_{j}(n_{j})\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)\Big)\\
&\times\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log n_{j^{\prime}}}{n_{j^{\prime}}}\Big(1+f_{j^{\prime}}(n_{j^{\prime}})\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j^{\prime}})}}{g(n_{j^{\prime}})}b_{j^{\prime}}(n_{j^{\prime}})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j^{\prime}}\\
p|n_{j^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)\Big).\end{split}

We denote
\mathcal{N}_{j},1\leq j\leq\mathcal{J}
for the set of integers
n_{j}
such that
n_{j}
is divisible only by primes
p\in I_{j}
. We estimate the last sum of the last expression above by observing that
1-2^{\Omega(n_{j})-\ell_{j}/2}\leq 0
when
\Omega(n_{j})\geq\ell_{j}/2
, so that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log n_{j}}{n_{j}}\Big(1+f_{j}(n_{j})\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log n_{j}}{n_{j}}\Big(1-2^{\Omega(n_{j})-\ell_{j}/2}\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big).

We further observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log n_{j}}{n_{j}}\Big(1-2^{\Omega(n_{j})-\ell_{j}/2}\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{ds}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n^{1+s}_{j}}\Big(1-2^{\Omega(n_{j})-\ell_{j}/2}\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)\Big)\Big|_{s=0}.

Upon writing

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n^{1+s}_{j}}\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{k}{p^{1+s}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)+\frac{k^{2}}{2!p^{2(1+s)}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)+\cdots\Big),

we deduce that

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{ds}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n^{1+s}_{j}}\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)\Big)\Big|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{k}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{k\log p}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{k}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{k\log p}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big).

Note also that we have

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{ds}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{\Omega(n_{j})-\ell_{j}/2}}{n^{1+s}_{j}}\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)\Big)\Big|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-\ell_{j}/2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{2k}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{2k\log p}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{-\ell_{j}/4}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{k}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{k\log p}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big).

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log n_{j}}{n_{j}}\Big(1-2^{\Omega(n_{j})-\ell_{j}/2}\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-2^{-\ell_{j}/4})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{k}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{k\log p}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big).

We apply similar arguments as above to see that we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n_{j}}\Big(1+f_{j}(n_{j})\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n_{j}}\Big(1-2^{\Omega(n_{j})-\ell_{j}/2}\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)\geq(1-2^{-\ell_{j}/4})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{k}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big).

We then conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=\prod_{j}n_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log n}{n}\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\Big(1-2^{\Omega(n_{j})-\ell_{j}/2}\Big)\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in I_{j}\\
p|n_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{p})\Big)\\
\geq&\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\end{subarray}}\Big(1-2^{-\ell_{j}/4}\Big)\prod_{p\in\bigcup^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}I_{j}}\Big(1+\frac{k}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big)\Big(\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}I_{j}}\frac{k\log p}{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\Big).\end{split}

We apply the above estimation into (
3.12
) and apply (
3.9
), (
3.11
) together with Lemma
2.1
to conclude that

\displaystyle S_{1}\gg

\displaystyle T(\log T)^{k^{2}+1}.

This completes the proof of the proposition.

3.7.
Proof of Proposition
3.4

We denote the left side expression in (
3.8
) by
S_{2}
and apply Cauchy’s integral formula for derivatives to see that

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\int^{T}_{1}|\zeta^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}|\mathcal{N}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k-1)|^{2}dt=\int^{T}_{1}\Big|\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}\frac{\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+\alpha+it)}{\alpha^{2}}d\alpha\Big|^{2}|\mathcal{N}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k-1)|^{2}dt,

where
\mathcal{C}_{1}
denotes the positively oriented circle in the complex plane centered at
0
of radius
R=(\log T)^{-1}
. We then apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the integral over
\alpha
above to deduce that

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{2}\leq&(\frac{1}{2\pi})^{2}\int^{T}_{1}\Big|\int_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{4}}d\alpha\Big|\Big|\int_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+\alpha+it)|^{2}d\alpha\Big|\Big|\mathcal{N}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k-1)\Big|^{2}dt\\
\leq&(\frac{1}{2\pi})^{2}R^{-2}\max_{|\alpha|=R}\int^{T}_{1}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+\alpha+it)|^{2}|\mathcal{N}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,k-1)|^{2}dt.\end{split}

We denote the last integral above by
I
and we fix an
\alpha=\beta+i\gamma
with
\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|\alpha|=R
to estimate it. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\beta\leq 0
and we apply Cauchy’s residue theorem to deduce that

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}|\zeta(s)|^{2}|\mathcal{N}(s-\alpha,k-1)|^{2}\,ds,

where
\mathcal{C}_{2}
consists of line segments from
\tfrac{1}{2}+\beta+(1+\gamma)i
to
\tfrac{1}{2}+(1+\gamma)i
, then from
\tfrac{1}{2}+(1+\gamma)i
to
\tfrac{1}{2}+(T+\gamma)i
and lastly from
\tfrac{1}{2}+(T+\gamma)i
to
\tfrac{1}{2}+\beta+(T+\gamma)i
.

The integration on the on the horizontal edges of the contour can be estimated to be
O(T^{1-\varepsilon})
using (
3.4
) and the convexity bound for
\zeta(s)
(see
[
20
, Exercise 3, p.
100]
) that asserts

\displaystyle\begin{split}\zeta(s)\ll&\left(1+|s|\right)^{\frac{1-\Re(s)}{2}+\varepsilon},\quad 0\leq\Re(s)\leq 1,\end{split}

We then deduce that

\displaystyle I=I_{R}+O(T^{1-\varepsilon}),

where

\displaystyle I_{R}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1+\gamma}^{T+\gamma}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}|\mathcal{N}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it-\alpha,k-1)|^{2}dt.

We now apply arguments similar to the proof of
[
16
, Proposition 2]
to deduce that for
T
large enough,

\displaystyle I_{R}\ll T(\log T)^{k^{2}}.

We apply the above estimation in (
3.13
) to conclude that

\displaystyle S_{2}\ll

\displaystyle T(\log T)^{k^{2}+2}.

This completes the proof of the proposition.

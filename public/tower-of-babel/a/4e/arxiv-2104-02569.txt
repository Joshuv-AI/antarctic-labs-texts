Rational points on non-linear horocycles and pigeonhole statistics for the fractional parts of n \sqrt{n}
Sam Pattison

Abstract
In this paper we investigate pigeonhole statistics for the fractional parts of the sequence n \sqrt{n} . Namely, we partition the unit circle 𝕋 = ℝ / ℤ \mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z} into N N intervals and show that the proportion of intervals containing exactly j j points of the sequence ( n + ℤ ) n = 1 N (\sqrt{n}+\mathbb{Z})_{n=1}^{N} converges in the limit as N → ∞ N\to\infty . More generally, we investigate how the limiting distribution of the first s ​ N sN points of the sequence varies with the parameter s ≥ 0 s\geq 0 . A natural way to examine this is via point processes - random measures on [ 0 , ∞ ) [0,\infty) which represent the arrival times of the points of our sequence to a random interval from our partition. We show that the sequence of point processes we obtain converges in distribution and give an explicit description of the limiting process in terms of random affine unimodular lattices. Our work uses ergodic theory in the space of affine unimodular lattices, building upon work of Elkies and McMullen. We prove a generalisation of equidistribution of rational points on expanding horocycles in the modular surface, working instead on non-linear horocycle sections.

Remark 1.4
.

The pigeonhole statistics we consider were previously studied by Weiss and Peres for the fractional parts of the sequence

(as well as higher dimensional generalisations). In this case the analogous processes converge to a Poisson point process

Similarly to what is observed in

]
, even though our limiting processes isn’t Poissonian, its second moment is nearly Poissonian with an error resulting from

of the points

Corollary 1.1

\mathbb{E}[|Y^{N}_{s}|^{2}]\to\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}j^{2}E_{j}(s)^{2}+s=\int_{X}|Y_{s}|^{2}\;dm_{X}+s=s^{2}+2s

.

If we desire the (more satisfactory) convergence of the variance of the random variables

1.1
Ergodic Theory

Let
G=\text{ASL}(2,\mathbb{R})=\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the affine special linear group of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with multiplication law defined by

(M,x)(M^{\prime},x^{\prime})=(MM^{\prime},xM^{\prime}+x^{\prime}),

where elements of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
are viewed as row vectors. Let
\Gamma=\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be the discrete subgroup of
G
consisting of elements with integer entries. As is discussed in §
2
,
\Gamma
is a lattice in
G
, meaning we have a fundamental domain
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
with finite volume (and hence, up to normalization, volume 1) under the Haar measure
m_{G}
on
G
. By restricting
m_{G}
to
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
and projecting to
X
, we have a right invariant probability measure
m_{X}
on the space
X:=\Gamma\backslash G
which we call the Haar measure on
X
(
[
EW11
, Proposition 9.20]
). Let

\Phi(t):=\Bigg(\begin{pmatrix}e^{-\frac{t}{2}}&0\\
0&e^{\frac{t}{2}}\end{pmatrix},(0,0)\Bigg)

and

a(N):=\Phi(\log(N)).

As in (
[
EM04
]
), we shall be concerned with the equidistribution of points on certain
horocycle sections
in the space
X
. Here, a horocycle section is a function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to G
of the form

\sigma(t):=\bigg(\begin{pmatrix}1&2t\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},(x(t),y(t))\bigg),

where
x(t)
and
y(t)
are smooth functions. We call
\sigma(t)
a horocycle section of period
p\in\mathbb{N}
if there exists some
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma_{0}\sigma(t+p)=\gamma_{0}\sigma(t)
for all

. Moreover, such a horocycle section is

if there exists some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
such that the set
\{t\in[0,p]:y(t)=\alpha t+\beta\}
has zero Lebesgue measure. For such horocycle sections, the following equidistribution result is known.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
EM04
]
Theorem 2.2,
[
Mar07
]
Theorem 4.2)

Let
\sigma
be a non-linear horocycle section with period

\frac{1}{p}\int_{0}^{p}f(\Gamma\sigma(x_{0})\Phi(t))\;dx_{0}\to\int_{X}f\;dm_{X}

as

.

Applying this to the non-linear period 1 horocycle section

n(t):=\bigg(\begin{pmatrix}1&2t\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},(t,t^{2})\bigg),

one can determine the distribution of

among the intervals

is uniformly distributed on

. In our setting, we restrict

to lying in the set

for each

1.3
, applies more generally to functions

which are piecewise continuous: functions

.

Let
\sigma
be a non-linear horocycle section with period

\frac{1}{pN}\sum_{k=0}^{pN-1}f(\Gamma\sigma(\tfrac{k}{N})a(M))\to\int_{X}f\;dm_{X}

(1.10)

as
N\to\infty
and
\tfrac{1}{C}N\leq M\leq CN

As we show concretely in §
5

we can approximate

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By the by the Banach-Alaoglu Theorem, any subsequence of the measures
(\nu_{N})
defined in (
3.1
) have a further subsequence which converges weak-star to some limiting measure
\nu
. By Proposition
3.2
,
\nu=m_{X}
. This shows the sequence of measures
(\nu_{N})
indeed converges weak-star to

.

Notice that for any constant function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
, it is immediate that
\int_{X}f\;d\nu_{N}\to\int f\;dm_{X}
as
N\to\infty
. This convergence therefore also holds for continuous functions which are constant outside of a compact set, being the sum of a constant function and a function in
C_{c}(X)
. Now, let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded continuous function and let
\epsilon>0
. Then we can find continuous functions

, which are constant outside some compact set, with
f_{-}\leq f\leq f_{+}
and for which

\int_{X}f_{+}-f_{-}\;dm_{X}<\epsilon.

Then we have

\displaystyle\int f\;dm_{X}-\epsilon\leq\int f_{-}\;dm_{X}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\nu_{N}(f_{-})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\nu_{N}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\nu_{N}(f)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\nu_{N}(f_{+})=\int f_{+}\;dm_{X}\leq\int f\;dm_{X}+\epsilon

meaning

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\nu_{N}(f)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\nu_{N}(f)=\int fdm_{X}

as our choice of
\epsilon>0
was general. Thus,
(\nu_{N})
converges weakly to
m_{X}
and so
\nu_{N}(f)\to\int f\;dm_{X}
as

for all piecewise continuous

by the continuous mapping theorem.

and

contributes to the sum (

can be replaced by

giving us

whenever

. The case

can largely be ignored as the random variable

, which is uniformly distributed on the set

, has probability

of taking this value and we are interested in the limit as

Therefore, the counting function can be bounded above and below using the following family of functions depending parameters

0&\text{ if }x_{0}=0\end{cases}.

(5.4)

Note that (

implies we have

S_{N,-\epsilon,\delta}(x_{0},s)\leq S_{N}(x_{0},s)\leq S_{N,\epsilon,\delta}(x_{0},s)

for

according to

In terms of understanding the distribution of the points

among our partition intervals, the lack of independent increments in the limiting point process tells us, when

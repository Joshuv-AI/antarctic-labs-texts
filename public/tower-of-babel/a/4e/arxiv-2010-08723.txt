Rectifiability of the free boundary for varifolds
Luigi De Masi

Abstract
. We establish a partial rectifiability result for the free boundary of a k k -varifold V V . Namely, we first refine a theorem of Grüter and Jost by showing that the first variation of a general varifold with free boundary is a Radon measure. Next we show that if the mean curvature H H of V V is in L p L^{p} for some p ∈ [ 1 , k ] p\in[1,k] , then the set of points where the k k -density of V V does not exist or is infinite has Hausdorff dimension at most k − p k-p . We use this result to prove, under suitable assumptions, that the part of the first variation of V V with positive and finite ( k − 1 ) (k-1) -density is ( k − 1 ) (k-1) -rectifiable.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivations

The main goal of this paper is to study the rectifiability of the free boundary for a
k
-varifold
V
in a compact domain
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}
.

We say that
V
has free boundary at
\partial\mathcal{M}
if the following first variation formula holds for every vector field
X
that is tangent to
\partial\mathcal{M}
(see next section for more detailed definitions):

(1.1)

\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X,H\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert,

where
H\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
.

If
V
is the varifold induced by a smooth
k
-surface
\Sigma
with smooth boundary
\partial\Sigma
,
H
is the mean curvature vector of
\Sigma
and (
1.1
) implies that

\partial\Sigma\subset\partial\mathcal{M}
and that
\Sigma
meets
\partial\mathcal{M}
orthogonally: that is the unit conormal
\eta
to
\partial\Sigma
coincides with the exterior unit normal vector
N
to
\partial\mathcal{M}
. So, varifolds with free boundary generalize in a weak sense the idea of surfaces that meet
\partial\mathcal{M}
orthogonally.

If
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{M}
is a smooth
k
-surface with smooth boundary
\partial\Sigma
and meets
\partial\mathcal{M}
orthogonally, we can test the first variation formula

(1.2)

\int_{\Sigma}\dive_{T_{x}\Sigma}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{k}(x)=-\int_{\Sigma}\langle X,H\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{k}+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle X,N\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{k-1}

(where
\mathcal{H}^{s}
is the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure) with a smooth vector field
X
such that
X(x)=N(x)
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
, obtaining the estimate

(1.3)

\mathcal{H}^{k-1}(\partial\Sigma)\leq c\left(\frac{\mathcal{H}^{k}(\Sigma)}{R(\mathcal{M})}+\int_{\Sigma}|H|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{k}\right)

where
c=c(k,\mathcal{M})
and
R(\mathcal{M})
is the minimum radius of curvature of
\partial\mathcal{M}
. This bound can be easily localized to any ball
B_{r}(x)
where
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
. (In particular, if
\mathcal{M}=B_{1}
is the unit ball with center 0 and
\Sigma\subset B_{1}
is a minimal
k
-surface that meets
\partial B_{1}
orthogonally, choosing
X=x
we obtain the nice identity

\mathcal{H}^{k-1}(\partial\Sigma)=k\mathcal{H}^{k}(\Sigma).

)

The simple proofs of these a-priori bounds strongly rely on the fact that
\Sigma
and
\partial\Sigma
are assumed to be smooth (that is the first variation of
\Sigma
is assumed to be bounded) and on the assumption that the conormal
\eta
of
\partial\Sigma
points outside
\mathcal{M}
. It is natural to ask if similar estimates hold also for a general varifold with free boundary
V
: that is if a varifold
V
with free boundary at
\partial\mathcal{M}
has bounded first variation and if its unit conormal on
\partial\mathcal{M}
is orthogonal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
and points outside
\mathcal{M}
.

We answer these questions refining a result stated by Grüter and Jost in
[
9
]
and by Edelen in
[
6
]
: we prove that if
V
satisfies (
1.1
), then it has bounded first variation: namely there exists a positive Radon measure
\sigma_{V}
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
and a
\lVert V\rVert
-measurable vector field
\tilde{H}
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
such that, for every smooth vector field
X
on
\mathcal{M}
we have

(1.4)

\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X,H+\tilde{H}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X,N\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V},

where
N
is the exterior unit normal vector to
\partial\mathcal{M}
and
\tilde{H}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
is orthogonal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
. Moreover we prove bounds on
\sigma_{V}
similar to (
1.3
). The measure
\tilde{H}\lVert V\rVert+N\sigma_{V}
is the orthogonal part of the first variation of
V
:
\tilde{H}\lVert V\rVert
takes into account the absolutely continuous part (with respect to
\lVert V\rVert
), that is the areas where
V
“lean” on
\partial\mathcal{M}
tangentially, while
\sigma_{V}
takes into account the boundary part of the first variation, that is where
V
meets
\partial\mathcal{M}
transversally.

Indeed, (
1.4
) is clearly analogous to (
1.2
): by comparison we have that if
V
is induced by a smooth surface
\Sigma
with smooth boundary
\partial\Sigma
, then
\sigma_{V}=\mathcal{H}^{k-1}\llcorner\partial\Sigma
.
It is then natural to ask also for a general
k
-varifold with free boundary
V
, if
\sigma_{V}
is singular with respect to
\lVert V\rVert
or, more precisely, if
\sigma_{V}
is
(k-1)
-rectifiable. As far as we know, this question has not been investigated. Under suitable assumptions, we are able to show a rectifiability result for
\sigma_{V}
.

To prove it, we analyze tangent cones to
V
at points on
\partial\mathcal{M}
to get informations about the tangent measures of
\sigma_{V}
; tangent varifolds to
V
exist if the upper
k
-density of
V
is finite. When the mean curvature
H
of
V
is in
L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
for some
p>k
, it is well-known that the density of
V
exists and is finite for every point; whereas if
p\leq k
, the finiteness of the upper density is guaranteed just
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e. by monotonicity formulae and differentiation theorems, which is not enough to prove any rectifiability result on
\sigma_{V}
(since
\partial\mathcal{M}
may have
\lVert V\rVert
-measure
0
).

In order to deal with this case, we prove an estimate of the size of the set where the
k
-density of the varifold does not exists or is infinite, in terms of Hausdorff measures: if
H\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
for some
p\in[1,k]
, then this set has Hausdorff dimension at most
k-p
.

1.2.
Background and main results

Allard studied the first variation of a varifold in the two seminal papers
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
. In the former he considered the interior case, while in the latter he studied the behavior of a
k
-varifold
V
assuming it has, as a boundary, a smooth
(k-1)
-dimensional submanifold
\Gamma
, i.e. he assumes that
V
has generalized mean curvature with respect to vector fields that vanish on
\Gamma
. An extension of the boundary result of Allard can be found in
[
3
]
.

An
\varepsilon
-regularity theorem similar to the ones by Allard is proved by Grüter and Jost in
[
9
]
for varifolds with free boundaries.
Moreover they prove in
[
9
, 4.11(ii)]
that a varifold with free boundary with
\lVert V\rVert(\partial\mathcal{M})=0
has bounded first variation
\delta V
;
this is also proved by Edelen in
[
6
, Proposition 3.2]
removing the hypothesis that
\lVert V\rVert(\partial\mathcal{M})=0
, but assuming that
V
is rectifiable.

We refine these boundedness results, extending them to general varifolds and removing the assumption
\lVert V\rVert(\partial\mathcal{M})=0
.
We state it in a slightly more general setting: if
V
has generalized mean curvature with respect to vector fields that vanish on
\partial\mathcal{M}
,
then it has bounded first variation with respect to vector fields that are orthogonal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
(see next section for precise definitions):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathcal{M})
be a
k
-varifold with generalized mean curvature
H
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
, with
H\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
. Then there exists a positive Radon measure
\sigma_{V}
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
and a
\lVert V\rVert
-measurable vector field
\tilde{H}
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
such that, for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})
, it holds

(1.5)

\begin{split}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X,H+\tilde{H}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X,N\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\end{split}

where

\tilde{H}
is orthogonal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
,
\tilde{H}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
and
\lVert\tilde{H}\rVert_{\infty}
depends only on the second fundamental form of
\partial\mathcal{M}
.
In particular,
V
has bounded first variation with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})
. Moreover, the following global and local estimates hold:

(1.6)

\sigma_{V}(\partial\mathcal{M})\leq c\lVert V\rVert(\mathcal{M})+\int_{\mathcal{M}}|H|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert;

(1.7)

\sigma_{V}\big(B_{r/2}(x_{0})\big)\leq\frac{c}{r}\lVert V\rVert\big(B_{r}(x_{0})\big)+\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|H|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\qquad\forall x_{0}\in\partial\mathcal{M},\,\forall r\leq R(\mathcal{M}),

where the constant
c=c(\mathcal{M})
depends only on the second fundamental form of
\partial\mathcal{M}
and
R(\mathcal{M})
is such that the signed distance function from
\partial\mathcal{M}
is of class
C^{2}
in
U_{R}(\partial\mathcal{M})
.

In other words, this theorem states that the component of the first variation of
V
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
that is orthogonal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
is the sum of two terms:

•

An absolutely continuous part with respect to
\lVert V\rVert
given by
\tilde{H}\lVert V\rVert
, which takes into account the fact that
V
can “lean” on
\partial\mathcal{M}
: indeed,
if
V
is induced by a smooth surface
\Sigma
with constant multiplicity, then
\tilde{H}
is the mean curvature of
\Sigma
where
\Sigma
lean on
\partial\mathcal{M}
. This is orthogonal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
and depends on
T_{x}\Sigma
and of the second fundamental form of
\partial\mathcal{M}
, see (
3.12
).

•

A part given by
N\sigma_{V}
, which “points outward
\mathcal{M}
” and is bounded.
Roughly speaking, we expect that this is the “transversal boundary” of
V
at
\partial\mathcal{M}
. On the other hand,
V
can have unbounded first variation on
\partial\mathcal{M}
only where “
V
meets
\partial\mathcal{M}
tangentially”.

Looking at the case of varifolds with free boundary, since the tangent part to
\partial\mathcal{M}
of the first variation of such a varifold is controlled by definition, Theorem
1.1
easily implies that varifolds with free boundary have bounded first variation (Corollary
4.6
).

As we said before, Corollary
4.6
was already proved by Grüter and Jost when
\lVert V\rVert(\partial\mathcal{M})=0
, and Edelen extended the result to
\lVert V\rVert(\partial\mathcal{M})>0
but assuming that
V
is rectifiable. In Edelen’s proof, the rectifiability is used to show that for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
the only planes charged by
V
are those included in
T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M}
.
We are able to remove the rectifiability assumption by the use of Lemma
3.1
, which is a form of the Constancy Theorem (see
[
12
, Theorem 41.1]
) and asserts that on
\partial\mathcal{M}
, even in the non-rectifiable case,
V
charges only planes that are included in
T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M}
.

We have already stated above that, if the varifold is induced by a smooth surface with free boundary at
\partial\mathcal{M}
, then
\sigma_{V}=\mathcal{H}^{k-1}\llcorner\partial\Sigma
.
It is then natural to ask if
\sigma_{V}
is
(k-1)
-rectifiable in a more general case as well.

In order to state precisely our main result concerning
\sigma_{V}
, we define the
(k-1)
-dimensional part
of
\sigma_{V}
, written
\sigma_{V}^{*}
, as the restriction of
\sigma_{V}
to those points with strictly positive lower
(k-1)
-density and finite upper
(k-1)
-density :

(1.8)

\sigma_{V}^{*}={\sigma_{V}}\llcorner E,\qquad E=\{x\mid 0<\Theta_{*}^{k-1}(\sigma_{V},x)\leq\Theta^{*(k-1)}(\sigma_{V},x)<+\infty\}.

Our main result on
\sigma_{V}^{*}
is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathcal{M})
be a rectifiable
k
-varifold with free boundary at
\partial\mathcal{M}
such that
H\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
for some
p>1
and
\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,{x})\geq 1
for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\mathcal{M}
. Then
\sigma_{V}^{*}
is
(k-1)
-rectifiable.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we make an analysis of the blow-ups of
V
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
similar to the one performed in
[
5
]
. The study of blow-ups of
V
allows us to deduce, for
\sigma_{V}^{*}
-a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
, that every
(k-1)
-blow-up of
\sigma_{V}^{*}
at
x
is of the form
\beta\mathcal{H}^{k-1}\llcorner S
for some
(k-1)
-dimensional plane
S
and
\beta>0
. The Marstrand-Mattila Rectifiability Criterion (Theorem
5.1
), then implies that
\sigma_{V}^{*}
is
(k-1)
-rectifiable.

As we show at the beginning of section
5
, if
H\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
for some
p>k
, then the condition
\Theta^{*(k-1)}(\sigma_{V},x)<+\infty
is not restrictive, since it holds for every point
x
, basically by (
1.7
) and by the monotonicity formula for
\lVert V\rVert
that we prove in Corollary
4.8
.
Thus in this case, Theorem
1.2
reads as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathcal{M})
be a rectifiable
k
-varifold with free boundary at
\partial\mathcal{M}
such that
H\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
for some
p>k
and
\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,{x})\geq 1
for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\mathcal{M}
. Then the restriction
\sigma_{V}\llcorner\{x\mid\Theta_{*}^{k-1}(\sigma_{V},x)>0\}
is
(k-1)
-rectifiable.

To perform the analysis of the blow-ups of
V
in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we have to distinguish two cases:

•

If
H\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
for some
p>k
, then the monotonicity formula (Corollary
4.8
) assures the existence of the
k
-density
\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,{\cdot})<+\infty
and of tangent cones to
V
at
every
point in
\partial\mathcal{M}
.

•

The situation is more delicate when
p\leq k
, because the Lebesgue-Besicovitch differentation Theorem applied to the classical monotonicity identity for varifolds with bounded first variation guarantees (see e.g.
[
12
, Lemma 40.5]
) the existence and finiteness of the
k
-density and of tangent cones just
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.. Since
\lVert V\rVert(\partial\mathcal{M})=0
may hold true, it was in principle possible that
\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,{x})=+\infty
for every point in
\partial\mathcal{M}\cap\supp\lVert V\rVert
, and this would stop our analysis.

To overcome this difficulty, in subsection
4.6
we study more carefully
the set of points where the
k
-density of
V
exists and is finite also when
p\leq k
.

The behavior of the density for points in
{\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}}
was studied by Menne
[
11
, 2.9-2.11]
: in that paper the author was interested in a lower bound for the lower density of the varifold with respect to Hausdorff measures; in particular he shows that, if the density has a lower bound
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e. and
H\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
, then at
\mathcal{H}^{k-p}
-a.e. point, either the lower density still satisfies the lower bound, or it is equal to
0
.

Although the existence and finiteness of the density with respect to lower dimensional Hausdorff measures seems to be well-known at least for points in
{\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}}
, it does not appear in the literature; thus we report the result with its proof, both for points in
{\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}}
and on
\partial\mathcal{M}
, since it can be useful for a future reference too.

More precisely, we define the
density set
of
V
(Definition
4.1
), denoted by
\reg(V)
,
and we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathcal{M})
be a varifold with free boundary at
\partial\mathcal{M}
such that
H\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
for some
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then

(1.9)

\mathcal{H}^{s}\big(\mathcal{M}\setminus\reg(V)\big)=0\qquad\forall s>k-p

and, for every
x_{0}\in\reg(V)
there exists an increasing function
\varphi_{x_{0}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

such that

(1.10)

\frac{\lVert V\rVert\big(B_{r}(x_{0})\big)}{r^{k}}\leq\frac{\lVert V\rVert\big(B_{t}(x_{0})\big)}{t^{k}}+\varphi_{x_{0}}(t)\quad\forall\,0<r<t,\qquad\quad\lim_{t\to 0}\varphi_{x_{0}}(t)=0.

In particular,
the
k
-density
\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,x_{0})
exists and is finite for every
x_{0}\in\reg(V)
; moreover, the restrictions of
\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,{\cdot})
to
\reg(V)\cap{\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}}
and to
\reg(V)\cap\partial\mathcal{M}
are upper semi-continuous.

Thus the set of points where
\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,{\cdot})
is infinte or does not exist has Hausdorff dimension at most
k-p
.

\reg(V)
is the “good set” where we are able to study the blow-ups of
\lVert V\rVert
and
\sigma_{V}^{*}
, to conclude the proof of Theorem
1.2
. We remark that, if
p>k
, then the theorem is the well-known existence of the density at
every
point and is a straightforward consequence of Corollary
4.8
.

Besides its application in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, Theorem
1.4
is also interesting in itself, since it is a natural counterpart of the similar result for the set of Lebesgue points of a Sobolev function proved by Federer and Ziemer in
[
8
]
: if
f\in W^{1,p}(\mathcal{M})
for
p\in[1,n]
and if
\leb(f)
is the set of Lebesgue points of
f
, then
\mathcal{M}\setminus\leb(f)
has Hausdorff dimension at most
n-p
.

1.3.
Outline of the paper

In section
2
we recall the notations and the definitions used throughout the paper.

In section
3
we first prove Lemma
3.1
, which concerns the behavior of
V
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
; next we move on to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In section
4
we describe some consequences of Theorem
1.1
: we notice that, if
k=n-1
,
\tilde{H}
coincides with the mean curvature of
\partial\mathcal{M}
; next we adapt Theorem
1.1
to an other class of varifolds; we move on to varifolds with free boundary, proving that they have bounded first variation, establishing some monotonicity formulae and refining Lemma

, we prove Theorem

.

1.2
; this is obtained by the study of tangent varifolds to

and
T_{x}^{-}\Gamma
and we set
T_{x}^{+}\Gamma
to be the blow-up at
x
of the interior part of
\Gamma
.

We work on a compact domain
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
C^{2}
boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}
. We write
N(x)
for the exterior unit normal vector to
\partial\mathcal{M}
at
x
. In the following,
d
denotes the signed distance function from
\partial\mathcal{M}
such that
d>0
in
{\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}}
, that is

(2.3)

d(x)=\begin{cases}\inf\{|x-y|\mid y\in\partial\mathcal{M}\}&\mbox{if }x\in\mathcal{M}\\
-\inf\{|x-y|\mid y\in\partial\mathcal{M}\}&\mbox{if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathcal{M}.\end{cases}

Unless otherwise specified, we denote by
R=R(\mathcal{M})>0
a number such that
d
is
C^{2}
in
\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muU_{R}(\partial\mathcal{M})\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
. Thus
\nabla d
exists in
\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muU_{R}(\partial\mathcal{M})\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
and points inside
\mathcal{M}
.

We work with several classes of vector fields on
\mathcal{M}
, which we denote with the letter
\mathfrak{X}
with subscripts based on their behavior on
\partial\mathcal{M}
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})=C^{1}(\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mu\mathcal{M}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu,\mathbb{R}^{n+1}),\qquad\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})=\{X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\mid X(x)\in T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M},\,\,\forall x\in\partial\mathcal{M}\},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})=\{X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\mid X(x)\in(T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M})^{\perp},\,\,\forall x\in\partial\mathcal{M}\}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})=\{X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\mid X(x)=0,\,\,\forall x\in\partial\mathcal{M}\}\quad\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})=\{X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\mid\supp X\subset\subset{\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}}\}.

If
\Gamma
is a
C^{2}
submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by slight abuse of notation we write
\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\Gamma)
(respectively
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\Gamma)
) for the set of compactly supported
C^{1}
vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that are tangent to
\Gamma
(respectively that vanish on
\Gamma
).

2.2.
Measures, rectifiable sets

If
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathscr{M}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
is the space of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued Radon measures on
A
and
\mathscr{M}^{+}(A)
is the space of positive Radon measures on
A
. If
\mu\in\mathscr{M}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
we denote by
|\mu|
the total variation measure of
\mu
. If
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is Borel, we write
\mu\llcorner B
for the restriction of the measure
\mu
to
B
.
If
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
has non-empty interior, we endow
\mathscr{M}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
with the weak*-topology: i.e. we say that a sequence of Radon measures
\{\sigma_{j}\}_{j}
converges to
\mu
(
\sigma_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mu
) if

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{j}=\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\qquad\forall f\in C_{c}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m}).

If
\mu\in\mathscr{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the upper and the lower
k
-densities of
\mu
at
x
:

\Theta^{*k}(\mu,x)=\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}{r^{k}}\qquad\Theta_{*}^{k}(\mu,x)=\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}{r^{k}}.

If the above limits coincide, then we define the
k
-density of
\mu
at
x
as their common value, which we denote by
\Theta^{k}(\mu,x)
.
The
k
-singular set
\sing^{k}(\mu)
is defined as

\sing^{k}(\mu)=\{x\in\mathcal{M}\mid\Theta^{*k}(\mu,x)=+\infty\}.

If
\mu\in\mathscr{M}(A,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
f:A\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is proper, we define the push-forward
f_{\#}\mu
of
\mu
through
f
as the Radon measure in
\mathscr{M}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R}^{m})
defined by

f_{\#}\mu(B)=\mu\big(f^{-1}(B)\big)\qquad\forall B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\mbox{ Borel.}

If
\mu\in\mathscr{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we say that
\nu
is a
k
-blow-up of
\mu
at
x
or a
k
-tangent measure to
\mu
in
x
if there exists a sequence
r_{j}\downarrow 0
such that

(2.5)

\mu_{j}:=\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k}}\big(\tau_{{x,r_{j}}}\big)_{\#}\mu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\nu.

We denote by
\tang^{k}(\mu,x)
the (possibly empty) set of
k
-blow-ups of
\mu
at the point
x
. If
\Theta^{*k}(\mu,x)<\infty
then, by Banach-Alaoglu Theorem,
\tang^{k}(\mu,x)
is non-empty. If
\Theta_{*}^{k}(\mu,x)>0
, then every
k
-blow-up of
\mu
at
x
is non-trivial; indeed, if
\nu\in\tang^{k}(\mu,x)
and
\mu_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\nu
as in (
2.5
), then

(2.6)

\nu(B_{1})\geq\limsup_{j}\mu_{j}(B_{1})=\limsup_{k}\frac{\mu(B_{r_{j}}(x))}{r_{j}^{k}}\geq\Theta_{*}^{k}(\mu,x)>0.

For each
s>0
, we denote by
\mathcal{H}^{s}
the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure and, if
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\hdim(A)
denotes the Hausdorff dimension of
A
.
We say that a Borel set
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
k
-rectifiable if there exist
M_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathcal{H}^{k}(M_{0})=0
and a countable family of
C^{1}

k
-submanifolds
\{M_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
such that

M\subset\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}M_{j}.

We say that a measure
\mu\in\mathscr{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
k
-rectifiable if there exist a
k
-rectifiable set
M
and a positive function
\theta\in L_{\loc}^{1}(M,\mathcal{H}^{k})
such that
\mu=\theta\mathcal{H}^{k}\llcorner M
.

2.3.
Varifolds

If
1\leq k\leq n
we call
G(k,n)
the Grassmannian of the un-oriented
k
-dimensional linear subspaces (or
k
-planes) of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote by
G_{k}(A):=A\times G(k,n)
the trivial Grassmannian bundle over
A
.

A
k
-varifold
on
A
is a positive Radon measure on
G_{k}(A)
. We denote by
\mathcal{V}_{k}(A)
the set of all
k
-varifolds on
A
and we endow
\mathcal{V}_{k}(A)
with the topology of the weak*-convergence of Radon measures, i.e. we say that
V_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}V
if

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{G_{k}(A)}\varphi(x,S)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V_{j}(x,S)=\int_{G_{k}(A)}\varphi(x,S)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\qquad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}(G_{k}(A)).

A
k
-rectifiable measure
\mu=\theta\mathcal{H}^{k}\llcorner M
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
induces the
k
-varifold

V=\theta\mathcal{H}^{k}\llcorner M\otimes\delta_{T_{x}M},

where
T_{x}M
is the approximate tangent space of
M
at
x
. A varifold that is induced by a rectifiable set is called a
rectifiable varifold
. If the multiplicity function assumes only integer values, we say that the varifold is
integer rectifiable
.
If
V
is a
k
-varifold on
\Omega
, the
mass

\lVert V\rVert
(or
total variation
) of
V
is the positive Radon measure defined as

\lVert V\rVert(A)=V(G_{k}(A))\qquad\forall A\subset\Omega\mbox{ Borel}.

If
V
is the
k
-varifold induced by the rectifiable measure
\mu=\theta\mathcal{H}^{k}\llcorner M
, then

\lVert V\rVert(B)=\int_{B}\theta(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{k}(x).

By slight abuse of notation, we often denote
\supp\lVert V\rVert
by
\supp V
.
If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a domain,
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\Omega)
and if
\psi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a diffeomorphism, the
push forward

\psi_{\sharp}V
of
V
through
\psi
is the varifold in
\mathcal{V}_{k}\big(\psi(\Omega)\big)
such that,
\forall\varphi\in C_{c}\big(G_{k}(\psi(\Omega))\big)
,

(2.7)

\int_{G_{k}\left(\psi(\Omega)\right)}\varphi(y,T)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\psi_{\sharp}V(y,T)=\int_{G_{k}(\Omega)}J_{S}\psi(x)\varphi\big(\psi(x),\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\psi_{x}(S)\big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S),

where
J_{S}\psi(x)
is the Jacobian of
\psi
relative to the
k
-plane
S
, i.e.

J_{S}\psi(x)=\sqrt{\det\big((\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\psi_{x})_{|S}^{*}\circ\big((\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\psi_{x})_{|S}\big)}.

We notice that this
is not
the push forward of measures previous defined (which is denoted by the different symbol
f_{\#}\mu
). In fact, the push forward of varifolds is defined in this way in order to ensure the validity of the area formula: indeed if
V
is induced by a rectifiable set
M
, then
\psi_{\sharp}V
is induced by
\psi(M)
.
If
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we say that
C\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a
blow-up
of
V
at
x
or a
tangent varifold
to
V
at
x
if there exists a sequence of radii
r_{j}\downarrow 0
such that

(\tau_{{x,r_{j}}})_{\sharp}V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}C.

We write
\tang(V,x)
for the set of tangent varifold to
V
at the point
x
.

If
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathcal{M})
and if
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
the
first variation

\delta V(X)
of
V
with respect to
X
is

\delta V(X)=\left.\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\left\lVert(\psi_{t})_{\sharp}V\right\rVert(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Big)\right|_{t=0}

where
\psi_{t}
is the flow map of
X
at the time
t
.
The following
first variation formula
holds:

\delta V(X)=\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S).

We now define the class of varifolds with
bounded first variation
:

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a varifold
V
has
bounded first variation
in
\mathcal{M}
if

(2.8)

\sup\{|\delta V(X)|\mid X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\,,\max|X|\leq 1\}<+\infty.

If (
2.8
) holds with a proper subset of
\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
(e.g.
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})
,
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
…) in place of
\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
, we say that
V
has bounded first variation with respect to this subset.

Therefore
V
has bounded first variation if there exists
\delta V\in\mathscr{M}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that, for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
,

(2.9)

\delta V(X)=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X(x),\zeta(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|\delta V|(x),

where
\zeta
is the polar vector of
\delta V
with respect to
|\delta V|
.

If
V
has bounded first variation, then by Lebesgue decomposition there exist
|\delta^{s}{V}|\in\mathscr{M}^{+}(\mathcal{M})
, a
|\delta^{s}{V}|
-measurable function
\eta:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a
\lVert V\rVert
-measurable function
H:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\delta V(X)=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle H,X\rangle\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert+\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X,\eta\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|\delta^{s}{V}|\qquad\forall X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})

where
|\delta^{s}{V}|
is the singular part of
|\delta V|
with respect to
\lVert V\rVert
:

|\delta^{s}{V}|=|\delta V|\llcorner Z\qquad Z=\Big\{x\in\mathcal{M}\mid\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{|\delta V|(B_{r}(x))}{\lVert V\rVert(B_{r}(x))}=+\infty\Big\}.

Since the previous formula is similar to the corresponding one for smooth surfaces, we call
H
the
generalized mean curvature
of
V
,
|\delta^{s}{V}|
the
boundary measure
of
V
, the set
Z
is the
boundary
of
V
and
\eta
is the
unit co-normal
of
V
.

We now define the classes of varifolds with
generalized mean curvature
:

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathcal{M})
has
generalized mean curvature
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})
(respectively
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
) if there exists a
\lVert V\rVert
-measurable vector field
H\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
such that
H(x)=0
for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
and for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})
(respectively
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
) the following formula holds:

(2.10)

\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle H,X\rangle\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert.

Remark 2.1
.

The assumption that
H(x)=0
for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
is important: without this hypothesis, the generalized mean curvature is not uniquely defined, that is any combination
H+K
with
\supp K\subset\partial\mathcal{M}
satisfies (
2.10
). Without excluding this ambiguity, (
1.5
) is no longer true because of the extra term
\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle K,X\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert
on the right-hand side.
Similarly, if
V
has bounded variation with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})
or
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
, if not otherwise specified we assume that the polar vector of
\delta V
vanishes at
|\delta V|
-a.e. point on
\partial\mathcal{M}
.

Thus
V
has
generalized mean curvature
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})
(respectively
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
,
\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
) if it has bounded variation with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})
(respectively
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
,
\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
) and
\delta V
has no singular part with respect to
\lVert V\rVert
when we test with vector fields in
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})
(respectively
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
,
\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
).

Definition 2.3

(Varifold with free boundary)
.

We say that
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathcal{M})
has
free boundary
at
\partial\mathcal{M}
if there exists a
\lVert V\rVert
-measurable vector field
H\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
such that
H(x)
is tangent to
\partial\mathcal{M}
for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
and such that (
2.10
) holds for every
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
.

Remark 2.2
.

As in Remark
2.1
, the assumption that
H
is tangent to
\partial\mathcal{M}

\lVert V\rVert
-a.e. is important because otherwise (
4.7
) is no longer true: every sum
H+K
, with
K
orthogonal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
, satisfies (
2.10
) for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
, whereas when we test with non-tangent vector fields the presence of
K
is relevant.
As above, when we say that
V
has bounded variation with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
, if not otherwise specified we assume that the polar vector of
\delta V
is tangent to
\partial\mathcal{M}
at
|\delta V|
-a.e. point on
\partial\mathcal{M}
.

Remark 2.3
.

As we mentioned in the introduction, a varifold with free bondary meets
\partial\mathcal{M}
orthogonally in a weak sense: indeed, if
V
is the varifold induced by a surface
\Sigma
with smooth boundary
\partial\Sigma
, (
2.10
) implies that the conormal
\eta
to
\partial\Sigma
is orthogonal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

3.1.
Constancy Lemma

The proof of the Theorem
1.1
is based on the following lemma, which is a form of the Constancy Theorem
[
12
, Theorem 41.1]
with weaker hypotheses (and conclusion) and it is interesting in itself. The result is used in the proof of the Theorem
1.1
to deal with the case
\lVert V\rVert(\partial\mathcal{M})>0
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
C^{2}
-hypersurface without boundary, let
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
have bounded first variation
\delta_{0}V
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\Gamma)
(that is vector fields that vanish on
\Gamma
).
Then

V\big(\{(x,S)\in G_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mid x\in\Gamma,S\not\subset T_{x}\Gamma\}\big)=0.

Proof.

By a simple covering argument it is enough to prove the result locally: that is that, for each
x_{0}\in\Gamma
, there exists
r=r(x_{0})>0
and a ball
B_{r}(x_{0})
such that

V\big(\{(x,S)\in G_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mid x\in\Gamma\cap B_{r}(x_{0}),S\not\subset T_{x}\Gamma\}\big)=0.

We fix
x_{0}\in\Gamma
and without loss of generality we can assume that
x_{0}=0
.

Since
\Gamma
is locally-orientable, there exists
r^{\prime}>0
and a ball
B_{r^{\prime}}
such that
B_{r^{\prime}}\setminus\Gamma
is made of two connected components
D^{+}
and
D^{-}
separated by
\Gamma
.
Only in this proof,
d
denotes a fixed one of the two signed distance function from
\Gamma
in
B_{\rho}
, that is

d(x)=\begin{cases}\inf\{|x-y|\mid y\in\Gamma\}&\mbox{if }x\in D^{+}\\
-\inf\{|x-y|\mid y\in\Gamma\}&\mbox{if }x\in D^{-}.\end{cases}

Since
\Gamma
is of class
C^{2}
, there exists
r\leq r^{\prime}
such that
d\in C^{2}\big(\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muB_{r}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu\big)
. We set
r(x_{0})=r
.

We recall that
\gamma
is the cut-off function defined in section
2
.
Since
\nabla d(x)
is orthogonal to
\Gamma
for every
x\in\Gamma
, we have
|P_{S}\nabla d(x)|^{2}=0
if and only if
S\subset T_{x}\Gamma
. Therefore, to get the conclusion, it is enough to prove that

(3.1)

\int_{G_{k}(\Gamma)}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)|P_{S}\nabla d(x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=0.

To do so, we test (
2.10
) with a suitable vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
.
If
\rho<r
, we choose
X(x)=d(x)\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\nabla d(x)
. We clearly have
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
and

\begin{split}\dive_{S}X(x)=&\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)|P_{S}\nabla d(x)|^{2}+\frac{d(x)}{r}\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\langle\frac{x}{|x|},P_{S}\nabla d(x)\rangle\\
&+\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)|P_{S}\nabla d(x)|^{2}+d(x)\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\dive_{S}\nabla d(x).\end{split}

Since
V
has bounded first variation
\delta_{0}V=\zeta|\delta_{0}V|
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\Gamma)
(where
\zeta
is the polar vector of
\delta_{0}V
with respect its total variation
|\delta_{0}V|
), testing (
2.10
) with
X
we obtain

(3.2)

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{G_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)|P_{S}\nabla d(x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\right|\\
&\qquad\quad\leq\left|\int_{G_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\frac{d(x)}{r}\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\langle\frac{x}{|x|},P_{S}\nabla d(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\right|\\
&\qquad\qquad+\left|\int_{G_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)|P_{S}\nabla d(x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\right|\\
&\qquad\qquad+\left|\int_{G_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}d(x)\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\dive_{S}\nabla d(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\right|\\
&\qquad\qquad+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle X(x),\zeta(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|\delta_{0}V|(x)\right|\end{split}

For the left-hand side of the above inequality, by dominated convergence we have

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{G_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)|P_{S}\nabla d(x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=\int_{G_{k}(\Gamma)}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)|P_{S}\nabla d(x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S).

Therefore, to show (
3.1
), we have to prove that the terms on the right-hand side of (
3.2
) go to
0
as
\rho\to 0
:

(1)

Since
\gamma^{\prime}
is bounded and
d(x)\leq\rho
by cut-off, for the first term we have

\begin{split}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\left|\int_{G_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\frac{d(x)}{r}\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\langle\frac{x}{|x|},P_{S}\nabla d(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\right|\leq c\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\rho}{r}\lVert V\rVert\big(U_{\rho}(\Gamma)\cap B_{r}\big)=0.\end{split}

(2)

Since
\bigl|\frac{d}{\rho}\bigr|\leq 1
and
\gamma^{\prime}(s)\neq 0
only if
s\in(1/2,1)
, for the second term we have

\begin{split}&\lim_{\rho\to 0}\left|\int_{G_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)|P_{S}\nabla d(x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\right|\\
&\qquad\qquad\leq 3\lim_{\rho\to 0}\lVert V\rVert\Big(\big(U_{\rho}(\Gamma)\setminus U_{\rho/2}(\Gamma)\big)\cap B_{r}\Big)=0.\end{split}

(3)

By the choice of
r
we have that
|\dive_{S}\nabla d(x)|\leq c
in
B_{r}
; thus

\begin{split}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\left|\int_{G_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}d(x)\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\dive_{S}\nabla d(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\right|\leq c\lim_{\rho\to 0}\rho\lVert V\rVert\big(U_{\rho}(\Gamma)\cap B_{r}\big)=0.\end{split}

(4)

For the last term, since
\delta_{0}V
is a Radon measure, it holds

\begin{split}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle X(x),\zeta(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|\delta_{0}V|(x)\right|\leq\lim_{\rho\to 0}\rho|\delta_{0}V|\big(U_{\rho}(\Gamma)\cap B_{r}\big)=0.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We can now prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let us fix
R>0
(as in section
2
) so that the distance function
d
from
\partial\mathcal{M}
defined in (
2.3
) is of class
C^{2}
in
\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muU_{R}(\partial\mathcal{M})\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
.

In what follows we are going to repeatedly use the decomposition of a vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
we now present;
within
U_{R}(\partial\mathcal{M})
, we can decompose
X
in its normal and tangent component: there exists a scalar function
\chi(x)
such that
X=X^{\perp}+X^{T}
with
X^{\perp}(x)=\chi(x)\nabla d(x)
and
\langle X^{T}(x),\nabla d(x)\rangle=0
for all
x\in U_{R}(\partial\mathcal{M})
.

Step 1:

We begin by cut-offing a vector field in its “interior” and “boundary” part.
For every
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})
and
\rho<R
one has

X(x)=\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)X(x)+\bigg(1-\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\bigg)X(x).

Therefore

(3.3)

\begin{split}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=&\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}\left[\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)X(x)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
&+\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}\left[\left(1-\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\right)X(x)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S).\end{split}

Thus we have splitted
X
in the “interior” and the “boundary part” by cut-offing with
\gamma(d/\rho)
and the idea is to send
\rho\to 0
.

Step 2:

For the interior part,
since
\left(1-\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\right)X(x)\in\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})
(i.e. it is compactly supported in the interior of
\mathcal{M}
), by (
2.10
) and dominated convergence we have

(3.4)

\begin{split}&\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}\left[\left(1-\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\right)X(x)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
&\qquad=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(1-\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\right)\langle X(x),H(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\\
&\qquad=-\int_{{\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}}}\langle X(x),H(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\\
&\qquad=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X(x),H(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x),\end{split}

where the last equality follows by the fact that
H
is assumed to be equal to
0
on
\partial\mathcal{M}

(see Remark
2.1
for more details).

Step 3:

Since the limit in (
3.4
) exists, also for the “boundary part” of
X
the limit

(3.5)

\begin{split}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}\left[\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)X(x)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\end{split}

exists. We want now to compute (
3.5
) and to show the existence of
\tilde{H}
and
\sigma_{V}
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
. We begin by writing

(3.6)

\begin{split}\int\dive_{S}\left[\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)X(x)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=&\int\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
&+\int\frac{1}{\rho}\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\langle P_{S}\nabla d(x),X(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S).\end{split}

At this point, we want to study separately the limit as
\rho\to 0
of each term in the right-hand side of the above equation.

•

For the first term of the right-hand side in (
3.6
),
we expect that, as
\rho\to 0
, it give a sort of mean curvature of
\partial\mathcal{M}
. This expectation is justified by the fact that, on
\partial\mathcal{M}
,
V
charges only planes that are tangent to
\partial\mathcal{M}
by Lemma
3.1
and because
X
is orthogonal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
.
More precisely, we are going to prove that there exists a
\lVert V\rVert
-measurable vector field
\tilde{H}
such that

(3.7)

\begin{split}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=-\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle\tilde{H}(x),X(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x),\end{split}

which is orthogonal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
.
To this aim, we first observe that by dominated convergence we have

(3.8)

\begin{split}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=\int_{G_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\end{split}

We now have to compute the right-hand side of (
3.8
). To do this, we decompose
\dive_{S}X(x)=\dive_{S}X^{\perp}(x)+\dive_{S}X^{T}(x)
.
For the tangent part, we claim that

(3.9)

\int_{G_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X^{T}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=0.

In fact

\dive_{S}X^{T}(x)=0

for any

x\in\partial\mathcal{M},

and

\forall S\subset T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M}
. This is true because
X^{T}\equiv 0
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
, therefore
D_{\tau}\langle X(x),\tau\rangle=0
for any
\tau\in T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M}
. Thus (
3.9
) follows by definition of tangential divergence (
2.1
) and by Lemma
3.1
.

For the orthogonal component we get

\dive_{S}X^{\perp}(x)=\langle P_{S}\nabla\chi,\nabla d(x)\rangle+\chi(x)\dive_{S}\nabla d(x).

Since
\nabla d(x)
is orthogonal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
, by Lemma
3.1
again we have
\langle P_{S}\nabla\chi(x),\nabla d(x)\rangle=0
for
V
-a.e.
(x,S)\in G_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M})
. Hence

\int_{G_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\langle P_{S}\nabla\chi(x),\nabla d(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=0.

Since
\nabla d(x)=-N(x)
for every
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
, where
N(x)
is the unit normal vector to
\partial\mathcal{M}
at
x
, we obtain

(3.10)

\int_{G_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\chi(x)\dive_{S}\nabla d(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=\int_{G_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\langle X(x),N(x)\rangle\dive_{S}N(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)

Thus, combining (
3.9
)-(
3.10
), we obtain

(3.11)

\int_{G_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=\int_{G_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\langle X(x),N(x)\rangle\dive_{S}N(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S).

We are now going to define
\tilde{H}
and write the last integral in terms of it. To do so, by disintegration of
V
we can write

V=\lVert V\rVert\otimes\nu_{x}

where for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x
,
\nu_{x}
is a probability measure on
G(k,n)
. Hence the right-hand side of (
3.11
) can be written as

\begin{split}&\int_{G_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\langle X(x),N(x)\rangle\dive_{S}N(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
&\qquad=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X(x),N(x)\rangle\left(\int_{G(k,n)}\dive_{S}N(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{x}(S)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x).\end{split}

If we define

(3.12)

\tilde{H}(x):=-N(x)\int_{G(k,n)}\dive_{S}N(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{x}(S)\qquad\mbox{ for }\lVert V\rVert\mbox{-a.e. }x\in\partial\mathcal{M},

we can write (
3.11
) as

(3.13)

\begin{split}\int_{G_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=-\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle\tilde{H}(x),X(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x).\end{split}

Loosely speaking,
\tilde{H}(x)
can be interpreted as the “mean curvature of
\partial\mathcal{M}
weighted according to the planes charged by
V
at
x
”; in fact, in co-dimension 1,
\tilde{H}
turns out to be precisely the mean curvature of
\partial\mathcal{M}
(see Corollary
4.2
). By its definition, it is clear that
\tilde{H}
is orthogonal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
, that
\tilde{H}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
and that
\lVert\tilde{H}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)}
depends only on the second fundamental form of
\partial\mathcal{M}
.
Gathering (
3.8
) and (
3.13
) we finally get (
3.7
).

•

We now have to study the second term in the right-hand side of (
3.6
)
Roughly speaking, we can see it as “the mean orthogonal part to
\partial\mathcal{M}
of
V
” on the tubular neighborhood
U_{\rho}(\partial\mathcal{M})
. When
\rho\to 0
, we expect that this term takes into account the “transversal boundary” of
V
at
\partial\mathcal{M}
, that is the singular part of the first variation of
V
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
.

More precisely, we are going to show the existence of a positive Radon measure
\sigma_{V}
(as expressed in the statement of the theorem), such that

(3.14)

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{1}{\rho}\int\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\langle P_{S}\nabla d(x),X(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X(x),N(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}(x),

where
N(x)
is the exterior unit normal vector to
\partial\mathcal{M}
.
We first of all remark that the above limit exists by the existence of limits of the other two terms in (
3.6
).

To compute the limit in (
3.14
), we use again the decomposition
X=X^{T}+X^{\perp}=X^{T}+\chi\nabla d
.

As
\rho\to 0
, we expect that the contribution of
X^{T}
is zero. Indeed, since
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})
and
X
is of class
C^{1}
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that
|X^{T}(x)|\leq cd(x)
. Therefore, since
\gamma^{\prime}(s)\neq 0
only for
s\in(1/2,1)
, we obtain

(3.15)

\begin{split}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\,&\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\left|\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\langle P_{S}\nabla d(x),X^{T}(x)\rangle\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\lim_{\rho\to 0}3c\lVert V\rVert\big(U_{\rho}(\partial\mathcal{M})\setminus U_{\rho/2}(\partial\mathcal{M})\big)=0.\end{split}

This proves the existence of the limit for the orthogonal part

(3.16)

\begin{split}\lim_{\rho\to 0}&\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\langle P_{S}\nabla d(x),X^{\perp}(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
&\qquad=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\chi(x)\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)|P_{S}\nabla d(x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S).\end{split}

(
3.15
) and (
3.16
) yield

(3.17)

\begin{split}&\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int\frac{1}{\rho}\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\langle P_{S}\nabla d(x),X(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
&\qquad=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\chi(x)\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)|P_{S}\nabla d(x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S).\end{split}

We now observe that the map

T\colon X\in\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})\longmapsto\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\chi(x)\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)|P_{S}\nabla d(x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S).

is a well-defined distribution and, by its definition,
\supp T\subseteq\partial\mathcal{M}
.
Again by definition, if
\chi(x)\leq 0
for every
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
(i.e. if
X
point outward
\partial\mathcal{M}
), then
T(X)\geq 0
. Therefore
T
is a signed distribution and by Riesz Representation Theorem there exists a positive Radon measure
\sigma_{V}
such that

\langle T,X\rangle=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X,N\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\qquad\forall X\in\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M}).

This completes the proof of (
3.14
).

Step 4:

We now gather the previous computations to get (
1.5
).

By (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and (
3.14
), we can rewrite (
3.5
) as

(3.18)

\begin{split}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}\left[\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)X(x)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=-\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle\tilde{H}(x),X(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X,N\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}.\end{split}

Going back to (
3.3
), by (
3.4
) and (
3.18
) we finally get

\begin{split}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X(x),H(x)+\tilde{H}(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X,N\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}.\end{split}

which completes the proof of (
1.5
).

Step 5:

We are left with the proof of (
1.6
) and (
1.7
). We begin with (
1.7
). Let us fix
x_{0}\in\partial\mathcal{M}
and
r\leq R
.
Without loss of generality we can assume that
x_{0}=0
. To prove the estimate, we test (
1.5
) with
X(x)=-\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\nabla d(x)
which clearly belongs to
\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})
. We have

\dive_{S}X(x)=-\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\frac{1}{r}\langle P_{S}\frac{x}{|x|},\nabla d(x)\rangle-\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\dive_{S}\nabla d(x).

Therefore

(3.19)

\begin{split}\sigma_{V}\big(B_{r/2}(x)\big)\leq&\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\\
=&\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X,N\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\\
=&\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)+\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X,H+\tilde{H}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\\
=&\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\left[-\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\frac{1}{r}\langle P_{S}\frac{x}{|x|},\nabla d(x)\rangle-\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\dive_{S}\nabla d(x)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
&-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\langle\nabla d(x),H(x)+\tilde{H}(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\end{split}

We want to estimate the last member of the above inequality. To do so, we choose a constant
c=c(\partial\mathcal{M},R)
such that

|\dive_{S}\nabla d(x)|\leq c\qquad\forall x\in U_{R}(\partial\mathcal{M}).

By (
3.12
), the choice of
c
provides also
|\tilde{H}(x)|\leq c
for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
. Thus

\begin{split}-\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\dive_{S}\nabla d(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)-&\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\langle\nabla d(x),H(x)+\tilde{H}(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\\
\leq&\int_{B_{r}(x)}\big(c+|H(x)|\big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x).\end{split}

Substituting in (
3.19
) and since
\gamma
can be taken so that
\lVert\gamma^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty}\approx 2
, we get

\begin{split}\sigma_{V}\big(B_{r/2}(x)\big)\leq\frac{c}{r}\lVert V\rVert\big(B_{r}(x)\big)+\int_{B_{r}(x)}|H(x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x).\end{split}

The proof of (
1.6
) is similar to the previous one and is in fact easier: it is enough to take a vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
such that
X(x)=\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\nabla d(x)
for some
\rho
sufficiently small, so that
X=N
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
and use (
1.5
).

∎

4.1.
Varifolds with bounded variation with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})

The fact that the first variation of
V
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\lVert V\rVert
is not essential to prove Theorem
1.1
. In fact the following slight modification holds true.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathcal{M})
be a
k
-varifold with bounded first variation
\delta_{0}V
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
. Then there exists a positive Radon measure
\sigma_{V}
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
and a
\lVert V\rVert
-measurable vector field
\tilde{H}
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
such that, for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})
, it holds

(4.1)

\begin{split}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X,\zeta\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|\delta_{0}V|-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X,\tilde{H}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X,N\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\end{split}

where

\tilde{H}
is orthogonal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
,
\tilde{H}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
and
\lVert\tilde{H}\rVert_{\infty}
depends on the second fundamental form of
\partial\mathcal{M}
and
\zeta
is the polar vector of
\delta_{0}V
with respect to
|\delta_{0}V|
.
In particular,
V
has bounded first variation with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})
. Moreover, the following estimates hold:

(4.2)

\sigma_{V}(\partial\mathcal{M})\leq c\lVert V\rVert(\mathcal{M})+|\delta_{0}V|(\mathcal{M});

(4.3)

\sigma_{V}\big(B_{r/2}(x_{0})\big)\leq\frac{c}{r}\lVert V\rVert\big(B_{r}(x_{0})\big)+|\delta_{0}V|\big(B_{r}(x_{0})\big)\qquad\forall x_{0}\in\partial\mathcal{M},\,\forall r\leq R(\mathcal{M})

where
R(\mathcal{M})
is such that the distance function from
\partial\mathcal{M}
is of class
C^{2}
in
U_{R}(\partial\mathcal{M})
and the constant
c=c(\mathcal{M})
depends on the second fundamental form of
\partial\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

The proof follows the one of Theorem
1.1
. The only part of the proof of (
1.5
) where we use the hypothesis
\delta_{0}V\ll\lVert V\rVert
is in (
3.4
). If
V
has bounded first variation
\delta_{0}V
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
, then one easily obtain

(4.4)

\begin{split}&\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}\left[\left(1-\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\right)X(x)\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
&\qquad=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(1-\gamma\Big(\frac{d(x)}{\rho}\Big)\right)\langle X(x),\zeta(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|\delta_{0}V|(x)\\
&\qquad=-\int_{{\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}}}\langle X(x),\zeta(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|\delta_{0}V|(x)\\
&\qquad=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X(x),\zeta(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|\delta_{0}V|(x)\end{split}

where
\zeta
is the polar vector of
\delta_{0}V
with respect to
|\delta_{0}V|
and the last equality is due to the assumption
|\delta_{0}V|(\partial\mathcal{M})=0

(see Remark
2.1
).

The modifications to the proofs of (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) to obtain (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) are obvious.
∎

4.2.
The codimension 1 case:
k=n-1

If
k=n-1
, we can characterize
\tilde{H}
in a simpler way: if
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
, then
\tilde{H}(x)
is the mean curvature vector of
\partial\mathcal{M}
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{V}_{n-1}(\mathcal{M})
have generalized mean curvature
H
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
with
H\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
. Then there exists a positive Radon measure
\sigma_{V}
on
\partial\mathcal{M}

such that

\begin{split}\int_{G_{n-1}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X,H+\tilde{H}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X,N\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\qquad\forall X\in\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})\end{split}

where
\tilde{H}
is the mean curvature vector of
\partial\mathcal{M}
, that is
\tilde{H}(x):=-N(x)\big(\dive_{T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M}}N(x)\big)
for
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
.
Moreover, (
1.7
) holds true.

Proof.

If
S
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
S\subset T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M}
if and only if
S=T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M}
. Therefore, if
k=n-1
and
V\in\mathcal{V}_{n-1}(\mathcal{M})
with generalized mean curvature with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
, Lemma
3.1
yields

(4.5)

V\left(\{(x,S)\in G_{n-1}(\partial\mathcal{M})\mid S\neq T_{x}\Gamma\}\right)=0.

Hence, for
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})
,

\int_{G_{n-1}(\partial\mathcal{M})}X(x)\dive_{S}N(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}X(x)\dive_{T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M}}N(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert,

thus (
3.11
) becomes

\int_{G_{n-1}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle N(x),X(x)\rangle\dive_{T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M}}N(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x).

Thus, defining

\tilde{H}(x):=-N(x)\dive_{T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M}}N(x),

we get (
3.13
).
∎

If
k=n-1
and
V
has bounded first variation with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
instead of
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
, we can strengthen the conclusion of Lemma
3.1
:
V\llcorner G_{n-1}(\partial\mathcal{M})
is
(n-1)
-rectifiable.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{V}_{n-1}(\mathcal{M})
with bounded first variation with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
. Then
V\llcorner G_{n-1}(\partial\mathcal{M})
is an
(n-1)
-rectifiable varifold. More precisely, if
\varphi\in C_{c}\big(G_{n-1}(\mathcal{M})\big)
, then

\int_{G_{n-1}(\mathcal{M})}\varphi(x,S)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\varphi(x,T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M})\theta(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)+\int_{G_{n-1}({\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}})}\varphi(x,S)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S),

where
\theta(x)=(\omega_{n-1})^{-1}\Theta^{n-1}(\lVert V\rVert,x)
for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
and in particular by (
4.5
), for any
\varphi\in C_{c}\big(G_{n-1}(\mathcal{M})\big)
we have

\int_{G_{n-1}(\mathcal{M})}\varphi(x,S)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\varphi(x,T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)+\int_{G_{n-1}({\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}})}\varphi(x,S)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S).

By
[
12
, Lemma 40.5]
), for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
there exists the density
\Theta^{n-1}(\lVert V\rVert,x)<\infty
. Hence, since
\partial\mathcal{M}
is of class
C^{2}
, the quantity

\begin{split}\theta(x):=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\lVert V\rVert\big(B_{\rho}(x)\big)}{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner\partial\mathcal{M}\big(B_{\rho}(x)\big)}=\frac{\Theta^{n-1}(\lVert V\rVert,x)}{\omega_{n-1}}\end{split}

exists and is finite for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
.
By Radon-Nikodym Theorem
[
12
, Theorem 4.7]
, since the singular set

\{x\in\mathcal{M}\mid\theta(x)=+\infty\}
of
\lVert V\rVert\llcorner\partial\mathcal{M}
is
\lVert V\rVert
-negligible, we have
\lVert V\rVert\llcorner\partial\mathcal{M}\ll\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner\partial\mathcal{M}
and the conclusion follows.
∎

4.3.
Varifolds with mean curvature with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})

The analogous of Theorem
1.1
holds, adding the extra hypothesis
\lVert V\rVert(\partial\mathcal{M})=0
, even if
V
has generalized mean curvature with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})
, i.e. the vector fields with compact support in the interior of
\mathcal{M}
. In fact, if we analyze the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we can see that the only point where we used the existence of generalized mean curvature with respect
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
is to obtain (
3.11
), that is to obtain the identity

\int_{G_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=-\int_{G_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\langle X(x),N(x)\rangle\dive_{S}N(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)

by the use of Lemma
3.1
, whereas if
\lVert V\rVert(\partial\mathcal{M})=0
then obviously we have

\int_{G_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=0.

Since the remaining arguments remain valid also if
V
has mean curvature with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})
, we have proved the following corollary.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathcal{M})
with generalized mean curvature
H
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})
with
H\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
and
\lVert V\rVert(\partial\mathcal{M})=0
. Then there exists a positive Radon measure
\sigma_{V}
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
such that

(4.6)

\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X,H\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X,N\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\qquad\forall X\in\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})

In particular,
V
has bounded first variation with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})
and the estimates (
1.6
), (
1.7
) on
\sigma_{V}
hold true.

Remark 4.1
.

If we remove the hypothesis
\lVert V\rVert(\partial\mathcal{M})=0
nothing can be said about the behavior of
V
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
, because any vector field in
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})
has first derivatives compactly supported in the interior of
\mathcal{M}
. So we have a lack of test vector fields to establish any property of
V
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
: e.g. take a smooth surface in
{\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}}
and add any varifold
W\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\partial\mathcal{M})
with unbounded first variation with respect
\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})
.

Since
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})\subset\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})
, the following result follows.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathcal{M})
with generalized mean curvature
H
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(\mathcal{M})
, with
H\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
and
\lVert V\rVert(\partial\mathcal{M})=0
. Then
V
has generalized mean curvature
H
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
.

4.4.
Varifolds with free boundaries

As an immediate corollary of Theorem
1.1
, varifolds with free boundaries have bounded first variation.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathcal{M})
have free boundary at
\partial\mathcal{M}
with
H\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
. Then
V
has bounded first variation. More precisely, there exists a positive Radon measure
\sigma_{V}
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
and a
\lVert V\rVert
-measurable vector field
\tilde{H}
such that

(4.7)

\begin{split}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X,H+\tilde{H}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X,N\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\qquad\forall X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M}),\end{split}

where
\tilde{H}
is defined as in (
3.12
). In particular
\tilde{H}
is orthogonal to
\partial\mathcal{M}
,
\tilde{H}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
and
\lVert\tilde{H}\rVert_{\infty}
depends only on the second fundamental form of
\partial\mathcal{M}
.
Moreover, (
1.6
) (
1.7
) hold true.

Proof.

If
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
, there exist
X^{T},X^{\perp}
such that
X=X^{T}+X^{\perp}
, with
X^{T}\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
and
X^{\perp}\in\mathfrak{X}_{\perp}(\mathcal{M})
(see the decomposition at the beginning of the proof of Theorem
1.1
). We have

\dive_{S}X(x)=\dive_{S}X^{T}(x)+\dive_{S}X^{\perp}(x).

Since the mean curvature of
V
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
is given by
H\mathbf{1}_{{\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}}}
, by Theorem
1.1
there exists a positive Radon measure
\sigma_{V}
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
and a
\lVert V\rVert
-measurable vector field
\tilde{H}
such that

\begin{split}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X^{\perp}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=&-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X^{\perp},H\mathbf{1}_{{\kern 0.0pt\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{o}}}+\tilde{H}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X,N\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\\
=&-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X^{\perp},H+\tilde{H}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X,N\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V},\end{split}

where the last equality follows because
H
is assumed to be tangent to
\partial\mathcal{M}
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
(see Remark
2.2
).
Moreover we have the same estimates
(
1.6
) and (
1.7
) on
\sigma_{V}
. For what concerns the tangent part
X^{T}
, the definition of varifold with free boundary yields

\begin{split}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X^{T}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X^{T},H\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert.\end{split}

This shows the conclusion.
∎

4.5.
Monotonicity formulae

Grüter and Jost established in
[
9
]
several properties of varifolds with free boundaries: monotonicity formulae for
\lVert V\rVert
at the boundary
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]
, which imply the existence of
\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,x)
for every point
x
if the mean curvature
H\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
for some
p>k
.

The monotonicity results are obtained by reflecting the balls across
\partial\mathcal{M}
, i.e. they have monotonicity of the sum of the masses in the ball and in the reflected ball
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]
. Using Corollary
4.6
it is possible to obtain the monotonicity of the mass in
B_{r}(x)
, without reflecting the balls.

We begin with a monotonicity inequality which is used also in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Lemma 4.7

(Monotonicity inequality)
.

Suppose
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathcal{M})
has free boundary at
\partial\mathcal{M}
, with
H\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
for some
p\in[1,+\infty)
. Then there exists a constant
c>0
that depends only on
n,k,p
and on the second fundamental form of
\partial\mathcal{M}
such that, for all
x_{0}\in\partial\mathcal{M}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
the following inequality holds:

(4.8)

\begin{split}(1+c\rho)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq&-\rho^{-\frac{k-s}{p}}\Big(\frac{1}{p}+c\rho\Big)\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{s}}\int_{B_{\rho}}|H+\tilde{H}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&-c(1+\rho)\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\end{split}

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can suppose
x_{0}=0
. Since for large
\rho
the statement is obvious, we have to prove it only for
0<\rho<R(\mathcal{M})
, where
R(\mathcal{M})
is defined in section
2
. We want to bound from below the following derivative:

(4.9)

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\frac{1}{p}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{\frac{1-p}{p}}.

To do so, we want to bound from below the derivative in the right-hand side to get a differential inequality. We have

\begin{split}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\right)=-\frac{1}{\rho^{k+1}}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\left(k\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)+\frac{|x|}{\rho}\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S).\end{split}

Let us choose
X(x)=\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)x
. Then

\begin{split}\dive_{S}X(x)=k\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)+\frac{|x|}{\rho}\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\left|P_{S}\frac{x}{|x|}\right|^{2}.\end{split}

We use Corollary
4.6
: by testing (
4.7
) with
X
we get the following
monotonicity identity
:

(4.10)

\begin{split}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\right)=&-\frac{1}{\rho^{k+1}}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{\rho^{k+1}}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\frac{|x|}{\rho}\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\left|P_{S^{\perp}}\frac{x}{|x|}\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
=&\frac{1}{\rho^{k+1}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X,H+\tilde{H}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert-\frac{1}{\rho^{k+1}}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X,N\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{\rho^{k+1}}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\frac{|x|}{\rho}\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\left|P_{S^{\perp}}\frac{x}{|x|}\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\end{split}

We have to estimate from below the last member of the above identity.

Since
\gamma^{\prime}\leq 0
, we can neglect the last integral and, since
|x|\leq\rho
by cut-off, we obtain

(4.11)

\begin{split}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\right)\geq&-\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)|H+\tilde{H}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\\
&-\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\Bigl|\langle\frac{x}{|x|},N\rangle\Bigr|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}.\end{split}

We have now to bound the two terms in the right-hand side of (
4.11
).

•

For the first one, since
H\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
and
\tilde{H}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
, also
H+\tilde{H}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
. Therefore, by Hölder inequality we get

(4.12)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)|H+\tilde{H}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\leq&\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\left(\int_{B_{\rho}}|H+\tilde{H}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\\
\leq&\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{s}}\int_{B_{\rho}}|H+\tilde{H}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\rho^{-\frac{k-s}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\end{split}

•

We now move on the estimate of the second integral in the right-hand side of (
4.11
). Since
\partial\mathcal{M}
is of class
C^{2}
and since
0\in\partial\mathcal{M}
, there exists a constant
c
such that

(4.13)

|\langle\frac{x}{|x|},N(x)\rangle|\leq c|x|.

This yields

(4.14)

\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\big|\langle\frac{x}{|x|},N\rangle\big|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\leq\frac{c}{\rho^{k-1}}\int\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}.

We have to further estimate the righ-hand side of this inequality. This is done by testing (
4.7
) with
X(x)=-\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{r}\Big)\nabla d(x)
; as in (
3.19
) we get

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\rho^{k-1}}\int\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}=&-\frac{1}{\rho^{k-1}}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\frac{1}{\rho}\langle P_{S}\frac{x}{|x|},\nabla d(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
&-\frac{1}{\rho^{k-1}}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\dive_{S}\nabla d(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
&-\frac{1}{\rho^{k-1}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\langle\nabla d(x),H+\tilde{H}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x).\end{split}

Since
\gamma^{\prime}(s)=0
if
s\in(0,1/2)
, we have
\frac{|x|}{\rho}\geq\frac{1}{2}
; Moreover, using
|\dive_{S}\nabla d|\leq c
(because
\rho<R
and
d
is of class
C^{2}
in
\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muU_{R}(\partial\mathcal{M})\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
) and (
4.12
), we obtain

(4.15)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\rho^{k-1}}\int\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}\leq&-\frac{2}{\rho^{k-1}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma^{\prime}\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\frac{|x|}{\rho^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)+\frac{c}{\rho^{k-1}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\\
&+\rho^{1-\frac{k-s}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{s}}\int_{B_{\rho}}|H+\tilde{H}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\\
=&\frac{2}{\rho^{k-1}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\right)-\frac{2(k-1)}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\\
&+\frac{2(k-1)+c\rho}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\\
&+\rho^{1-\frac{k-s}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{s}}\int_{B_{\rho}}|H+\tilde{H}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\end{split}

Concerning the last member, we now observe that

\begin{split}\frac{2}{\rho^{k-1}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\right)-&\frac{2(k-1)}{\rho^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\\
=&2\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{k-1}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\right)\end{split}

Substituting in (
4.15
) we get

such that

(5.4)

\sigma_{j}:=\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\big(\tau_{{x_{0},r_{j}}}\big)_{\#}\sigma_{V}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\nu.

We are going to prove that

(5.5)

\sigma_{j}^{*}:=\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\big(\tau_{{x_{0},r_{j}}}\big)_{\#}\sigma_{V}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\nu.

Without loss of generality we can assume
x_{0}=0
. Let us consider
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We have

\begin{split}\int f(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(x)=&\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\int f\Big(\frac{x}{r_{j}}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}(x)\\
=&\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\left(\int_{E}f\Big(\frac{x}{r_{j}}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E}f\Big(\frac{x}{r_{j}}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}(x)\right).\end{split}

For the last term there exists
c>0
such that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E}\Big|f\Big(\frac{x}{r_{j}}\Big)\Big|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}(x)&\leq\frac{c}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\sigma_{V}\big(B_{cr_{j}}\setminus E\big)\\
&\leq c\frac{\sigma_{V}\big(B_{cr_{j}}\big)}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\frac{\sigma_{V}\big(B_{cr_{j}}\setminus E\big)}{\sigma_{V}\big(B_{cr_{j}}\big)}\xrightarrow[j\to\infty]{}0\end{split}

since
E
has density
1
with respect to
\sigma_{V}
and
\sigma_{V}(B_{cr_{j}})/r_{j}^{k-1}
remains bounded by
\sigma_{j}\wtoi\nu
. Hence

\begin{split}\int f(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(x)=&\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\int f\Big(\frac{x}{r_{j}}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}^{*}(x),\end{split}

thus

\sigma_{j}^{*}=\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\big(\tau_{{0,r_{j}}}\big)_{\#}\sigma_{V}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\nu.

This proves
\tang^{k-1}(\sigma_{V},x_{0})\subset\tang^{k-1}(\sigma_{V}^{*},x_{0})
; the reverse inclusion can be proved in a similar way.
∎

5.2.
Proof of the second condition of the Marstrand-Mattila Criterion

We begin by studying tangent varifolds to
V
in a point
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
V\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathcal{M})
has free boundary at
\partial\mathcal{M}
with
H\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\lVert V\rVert)
for some
p\geq 1
. Then,
for every
x_{0}\in\reg(V)\cap\partial\mathcal{M}

the following statements hold: let
r_{j}\downarrow 0
be fixed and let us use the notations for the scalings defined on p.
Notation
; then

(1)

every
V_{j}
has free boundary at
\partial\mathcal{M}_{j}
with
\tilde{H}_{j}(x)=r_{j}\tilde{H}(r_{j}x)
for every
x\in\partial\mathcal{M}_{j}
and

(5.6)

\sigma_{V_{j}}=\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}(\tau_{{0,r_{j}}})_{\#}\sigma_{V};

(2)

there exist a subsequence of
r_{j}
, not relabeled, and a
k
-varifold
C
with
\supp C\subset T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M}
such that

V_{j}\wtoi_{j\to\infty}C.

In particular,
\tang(V,x_{0})\neq\emptyset
.

(3)

C
is stationary with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{t}(T_{x_{0}}^{+}\mathcal{M})
;

(4)

if
\sigma_{C}
is the measure given by Corollary
4.6
relative to
C
, we have

(5.7)

\sigma_{j}:=\sigma_{V_{j}}\wtoi_{j\to\infty}\sigma_{C};

(5)

If in addition
\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,{x})\geq 1
for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\mathcal{M}
(that is if
V
satisfies Assumption
1
), then
C
is a rectifiable cone and
\sigma_{C}
is scaling invariant, that is

\frac{1}{r^{k-1}}(\tau_{{0,r}})_{\#}\sigma_{C}=\sigma_{C}\qquad\forall r>0.

Proof.

To simplify the notations, we assume without loss of generality that
x_{0}=0\in\partial\mathcal{M}\cap\reg(V)
and
T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M}=\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
.

Step 1:

We first prove that every
V_{j}
has free boundary at
\partial\mathcal{M}_{j}
. For every
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M}_{j})
the vector field
x\mapsto X(\frac{x}{r_{j}})
belongs to
\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M})
, thus we can apply (
1.1
) to
V
and get

\begin{split}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{j})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V_{j}(x,S)=&\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X\big(\frac{x}{r_{j}}\big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
=&-\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X\big(\frac{x}{r_{j}}\big),H(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x).\end{split}

Thus, if we define
H_{j}(x)=r_{j}H(r_{j}x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{j}
, by changing again variables we get

\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{j})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V_{j}(x,S)=-\int_{\mathcal{M}_{j}}\langle X(x),H_{j}(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V_{j}\rVert(x),

that is
V_{j}
has free boundary at
\partial\mathcal{M}_{j}
, with
H_{j}(x)=r_{j}H(r_{j}x)
. By Corollary
4.6
,
V_{j}
has bounded first variation and there exists
\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{V_{j}}
and
\tilde{H_{j}}
. By a similar argument and by definition of
\tilde{H}
it easily seen that

\tilde{H_{j}}(x)=r_{j}\tilde{H}(r_{j}x)\qquad\mbox{ for }\lVert V_{j}\rVert\mbox{-a.e. }x\in\partial\mathcal{M}_{j}.

If
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M}_{j})
, then

\begin{split}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{j})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V_{j}(x,S)=&\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X\big(\frac{x}{r_{j}}\big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
=&-\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\langle X\big(\frac{x}{r_{j}}\big),H(x)+\tilde{H}(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\\
&+\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X\big(\frac{x}{r_{j}}\big),N(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{V}(x)\\
=&-\int_{\mathcal{M}_{j}}\langle X,H_{j}+\tilde{H}_{j}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V_{j}\rVert+\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{j}}\langle X,N_{j}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\tau_{{0,r_{j}}})_{\#}\sigma_{V}\end{split}

If we compare this equality with (
4.7
) for
V_{j}
tested with
X
, we get

\sigma_{j}:=\sigma_{V_{j}}=\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}(\tau_{{0,r_{j}}})_{\#}\sigma_{V},

that is
\sigma_{j}
is obtained by scaling
\sigma_{V}
.

Step 2:

We now want to study the limit of the sequence
V_{j}
.
Since
0\in\reg(V)
, by Theorem
1.4
there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(5.8)

\lVert V_{j}\rVert(B_{1})=\lVert(\tau_{{0,r_{j}}})_{\sharp}V\rVert\big(B_{1})=\frac{\lVert V\rVert\big(B_{r_{j}}\big)}{r_{j}^{k}}\leq c\qquad\forall j\in\mathbb{N}.

Thus by compactness of Radon measures, there exist
C\in\mathcal{V}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and a subsequence of
r_{j}
, not relabeled, such that

(5.9)

V_{j}\wtoi_{j\to\infty}C.

Step 3:

Clearly
\supp C\subset T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M}
. To show that
C
is stationary with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{t}(T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M})
, we test with a vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}(T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M})
. We first need the following estimate on
H_{j}
(exactly the same relation holds for
\tilde{H}_{j}
):

(5.10)

\lVert H_{j}\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{1})}=\left(\int_{B_{1}}|H_{j}|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V_{j}\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-p}}\int_{B_{r_{j}}}|H|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\xrightarrow[j\to\infty]{}0.

where in the second equality we have used
H_{j}(x)=r_{j}H(r_{j}(x))
and the limit follows by
0\in\reg(V)
and
|H|\leq|H+\tilde{H}|
because
H(x)\in T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M}
(Remark
2.2
) while
\tilde{H}\in(T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M})^{\perp}
.

To prove that
C
is stationary, let us pick
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}(T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M})
with compact support; there exists a sequence of vector fields
X_{j}\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}(\mathcal{M}_{j})
with compact support such that
X_{j}\to X
in the
C^{1}
topology. Hence, using (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) we get

\begin{split}\int_{G_{k}(T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}C(x,S)=&\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{j})}\dive_{S}X_{j}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V_{j}(x,S)\\
=&-\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{j}}\langle X_{j}(x),H_{j}(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V_{j}\rVert(x)\\
=&\,0\end{split}

that is, by definition,
C
is stationary with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{t}(T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M})
.

Step 4:

Corollary
4.6
provides that
C
has bounded first variation and the existence of
\sigma_{C}
. We want to prove that

(5.11)

\sigma_{j}\wtoi_{j\to\infty}\sigma_{C}.

To this aim, we first remark that
\supp\sigma_{C}\subset T_{0}\partial\mathcal{M}
and, since

T_{0}\partial\mathcal{M}
is flat, we have that
\tilde{H}_{C}=0
.
We next need an uniform bound on
\sigma_{j}(B_{1})
and we use (
1.7
): since the second fundamental forms of
\partial\mathcal{M}_{j}
go to
0
as
r_{j}\to 0
, when we apply (
1.7
) to
V_{j}
in
B_{1}
, the constant
c
in (
1.7
) is bounded uniformly in
j
. This implies that there exists an uniform constant
c
such that for each
j\in\mathbb{N}

(5.12)

\begin{split}\sigma_{j}(B_{1})=&\frac{\sigma_{V}(B_{r_{j}})}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\\
\leq&\frac{c\lVert V\rVert(B_{2r_{j}})}{r_{j}^{k}}+\frac{1}{r_{j}^{k-1}}\int_{B_{2r_{j}}}|H|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert.\\
\leq&\frac{c\lVert V\rVert(B_{2r_{j}})}{r_{j}^{k}}+r_{j}^{1-\frac{k-s}{p}}\bigg(\frac{1}{r_{j}^{s}}\int_{B_{2r_{j}}}|H|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\frac{\lVert V\rVert(B_{2r_{j}})}{r_{j}^{k}}\bigg)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\end{split}

where
s\in(k-p,k]
is such that
0
satisfies the
s
-density condition in the definition of
\reg(V)
.
The last member of (
5.12
) is uniformly bounded in
j
since
0\in\reg(V)
, Theorem
1.4
and by
\frac{k-s}{p}\leq 1
. This proves the uniform bound on
\sigma_{j}(B_{1})
.

To complete the proof of (
5.11
), let
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a vector field with compact support. If
e_{n}
is the
n
-th coordinate unit vector (that is the interior unit normal vector to
\partial T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M}
), we have

(5.13)

\begin{split}-\int_{T_{0}\partial\mathcal{M}}\langle X,e_{n}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{C}=&\int_{G_{k}(T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}C(x,S)\\
=&\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{G_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{j})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V_{j}(x,S)\\
=&-\lim_{j\to\infty}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}_{j}}\langle X(x),H_{j}(x)+\tilde{H_{j}}(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V_{j}\rVert(x)+\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{j}}\langle X(x),N_{j}(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{j}(x)\right)\\
=&\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{j}}\langle X(x),N_{j}(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{j}(x),\end{split}

where the first identity follows by
H_{C}=\tilde{H}_{C}=0
and the last one follows by (
5.10
).
Thus
N_{j}\sigma_{j}\wtoi-e_{n}\sigma_{C}
is proved, since
\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by (
5.13
) and the uniform bound on
\sigma_{j}(B_{1})
(
5.12
).
To prove (
5.11
), it is enough to observe that, if
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
, then

\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{C}=\int\langle fe_{n},e_{n}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{C}=-\lim_{j\to\infty}\int\langle fe_{n},N_{j}\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{j}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{j},

by the uniform bound on
\sigma_{j}(B_{1})
and since
\partial\mathcal{M}_{j}\to\partial T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M}
in the
C^{1}
topology.

Step 5:

We are left to prove that, if
\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,{x})\geq 1
for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\mathcal{M}
, then
C
is a rectifiable cone. The scaling invariance of
\sigma_{C}
will be an easy consequence of this.
We first claim that
C
is rectifiable. In fact, we have:

•

\Theta^{k}(\lVert V_{j}\rVert,x)\geq 1
for
\lVert V_{j}\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\mathcal{M}_{j}
, since
\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,{x})\geq 1
for
\lVert V\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\mathcal{M}
;

•

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\lVert V_{j}\rVert(B_{1})<\infty
by (
5.8
);

•

the
V_{j}
’s have locally uniformly bounded first variations because
0\in\reg(V)
, by (
5.10
) and the uniform bound on (
5.12
).

Thus we can apply to the sequence
V_{j}
the compactnes theorem for rectifiable varifolds
[
12
, Theorem 42.7]
, which proves that
C
is rectifiable and that
\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,x)\geq 1
for
\lVert C\rVert
-a.e.
x\in\mathcal{M}
.
In particular, there exists a
k
-rectifiable set
\Gamma\subset T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M}
such that

C=\theta(x)\mathcal{H}^{k}\llcorner\Gamma

with
\theta(x)=\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,x)\geq 1
for
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-a.e.
x\in\Gamma
.

It remains to show that
C
is a cone.
The argument is exactly the same as
[
12
, Theorem 19.3]
, but we recall it for the reader convenience.
Since
C
is rectifiable, to prove

(5.14)

(\tau_{{0,\lambda}})_{\sharp}C=C\qquad\forall\lambda>0,

(that is the fact that
C
is a cone), it is enough to show that

(5.15)

\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,\lambda x)=\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,x)\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\forall\lambda>0,

that is
\theta
is homogeneous of degree
0
. This is clearly implied by

(5.16)

\lVert C\rVert(\lambda A)=\lambda^{k}\lVert C\rVert(A)\qquad\forall A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\mbox{ Borel, }\forall\lambda>0.

By approximation, it is enough to prove that, for every
0
-homogeneous function
h\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, one has

(5.17)

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\lambda}\Big)h(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\right)=0\qquad\forall\lambda>0,

where
\gamma
is the cut-off function defined in Section
2
.
To this aim, if
\lambda>0
is such that
\lVert C\rVert(\partial B_{\lambda})=0
, we observe that

\begin{split}\frac{\lVert C\rVert(B_{\lambda})}{\lambda^{k}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\lVert V_{j}\rVert(B_{\lambda})}{\lambda^{k}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\lVert V\rVert(B_{\lambda r_{j}})}{\lambda^{k}r_{j}^{k}}=\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,0).\end{split}

Since there exists at most a countable number of radii
\lambda_{j}
such that
\lVert C\rVert(\partial B_{\lambda_{j}})>0
, by approximation it follows that

(5.18)

\frac{\lVert C\rVert(B_{\lambda})}{\lambda^{k}}=\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,0)\qquad\forall\lambda>0.

If we write the monotonicity identity (
4.10
) for
C
, since
H_{C}=\tilde{H_{C}}=0
and
\partial(T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M})
is flat, integrating between
\sigma,\lambda
we obtain

(5.19)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\lambda^{k}}\int_{W}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\lambda}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert C\rVert(x)&-\frac{1}{\sigma^{k}}\int_{W}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\sigma}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert C\rVert(x)\\
&\qquad=\frac{1}{\lambda^{k}}\int_{G_{k}(W)}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\lambda}\Big)\bigl|P_{S^{\perp}}\nabla|x|\bigr|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}C(x,S)\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{\sigma^{k}}\int_{G_{k}(W)}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\sigma}\Big)\bigl|P_{S^{\perp}}\nabla|x|\bigr|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}C(x,S)\\
&\qquad+\int_{G_{k}(W)}\bigl|P_{S^{\perp}}\nabla|x|\bigr|^{2}\left(\int_{\sigma}^{\lambda}\frac{k}{\rho^{k+1}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\rho}\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}C(x,S)\end{split}

Letting
\gamma
increase to
\mathbf{1}_{[0,1)}
in (
5.19
), by dominated convergence and (
5.18
) we get

(5.20)

\begin{split}0=\frac{\lVert C\rVert(B_{\lambda})}{\lambda^{k}}-\frac{\lVert C\rVert(B_{\sigma})}{\sigma^{k}}=\int_{G_{k}(B_{\lambda}\setminus B_{\sigma})}\frac{\bigl|P_{S^{\perp}}\nabla|x|\bigr|^{2}}{|x|^{k}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}C(x,S).\end{split}

Since the last term is non-negative, we have

(5.21)

\bigl|P_{S^{\perp}}\nabla|x|\bigr|^{2}=\Bigl|P_{S^{\perp}}\frac{x}{|x|}\Bigr|^{2}=0\qquad\mbox{for }C\mbox{-a.e. }(x,S)\in G_{k}(T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M}),

that is

(5.22)

C\big((x,S)\mid P_{S}(x)\neq x\big)=C\big((x,S)\mid x\notin S\big)=0.

If
h
is
0
-homogeneous, then
\langle\nabla h(x),x\rangle=0
and (
5.22
) implies

(5.23)

\langle\nabla h(x),P_{S}\,x\rangle=\langle\nabla h(x),x\rangle=0\qquad\mbox{for }C\mbox{-a.e. }(x,S)\in G_{k}(T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M}).

Since
C
has free boundary at
\partial T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M}
, by testing (
2.10
) for
C
with
X(x)=h(x)\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\lambda}\Big)x\in\mathfrak{X}_{t}(T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M})
, one obtains

\begin{split}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{k}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\lambda}\Big)h(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lVert V\rVert(x)\right)=&-\frac{1}{\lambda^{k+1}}\int_{G_{k}(T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M})}\dive_{S}X(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
&+\frac{1}{\lambda^{k+1}}\int_{G_{k}(T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M})}\gamma\Big(\frac{|x|}{\lambda}\Big)\langle\nabla h(x),P_{S}\,x\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}V(x,S)\\
=&\,0,\end{split}

where the last equality follows by
H_{C}=0
and (
5.23
). This proves (
5.17
), thus
C
is a cone. The scaling invariance of
\sigma_{C}
is a trivial consequence of (
4.7
) applied to
C
and (
5.14
).

∎

Before of going on, we highlight

, Lemma

and Lemma

.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let

has free boundary at

with

for some

and let

,

It is well-known that, for a cone, points with maximal density form a linear subspace and that the cone is invariant by translation with respect these points (see e.g.
[
13
, Section 3.3]
and
[
14
, Theorem 3.1, Example (4) of Section 4]
). We report here the simple proof of this fact for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
V
satisfy Assumption
1
, let
x_{0}\in\reg(V)\cap\partial\mathcal{M}
be fixed and let
C
be a tangent cone to
V
at
x_{0}
. Then the set

D_{C}=\{y\in T_{x_{0}}\partial\mathcal{M}\mid\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,y)=\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,0)\}

is a linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover the translated cone
(L_{y})_{\sharp}C
coincides with
C
for all
y\in D_{C}
. In particular, if
\sigma_{C}
is the measure given by Corollary
4.6
relative to
C
, then
(L_{y})_{\#}\sigma_{C}=\sigma_{C}
.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
D_{C}
is the
invariant subspace
of the cone
C
.

Proof of Lemma
5.6
.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
x_{0}=0
. Let us call
\theta_{0}=\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,0)
. By Lemma
5.4

C
is rectifiable and it holds
\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,0)=\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,0)
. Since
C
is a cone, for
y\in T_{x}\partial\mathcal{M}
we have

(5.25)

\theta_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:constant_density_cone}}}{{=}}\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert C\rVert\big(B_{r}\big)}{r^{k}}\geq\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert C\rVert\big(B_{r-|y|}(y)\big)}{(r-|y|)^{k}}\frac{(r-|y|)^{k}}{r^{k}}=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert C\rVert\big(B_{r}(y)\big)}{r^{k}}\geq\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,y).

The last inequality is given by the monotonicity identity for
C
: in the last member of (
4.10
), the first two integrals disapper (since
H+\tilde{H}=0
and
\langle N(x),x\rangle=0
) and the last term is non-negative.
(
5.25
) shows that

(5.26)

\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,0)\geq\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,y)\qquad\forall y\in T_{0}\partial\mathcal{M}.

If
y\in D_{C}
, then (
5.25
) yields

\frac{\lVert C\rVert\big(B_{r}(y)\big)}{r^{k}}=\theta_{0}\qquad\forall r>0.

By the same arguments of Lemma
5.4
, it follows that
C
is a rectifiable cone also with respect to
y
.
By rectifiability of
C
, in order to show that
(L_{y})_{\sharp}C=C
, it is enough to prove that

(5.27)

\Theta^{k}(C,z)=\Theta^{k}(C,y+z)\qquad\forall z\in T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M}.

To this aim, let
z\in T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M}
be an arbitrary point.
Since
C
is a cone with respect to
y
, we have that

\Theta^{k}(C,z)=\Theta^{k}\Big(C,y+\frac{1}{2}(z-y)\Big)=\Theta^{k}\Big(C,\frac{1}{2}(y+z)\Big).

On the other hand, since
C
is a cone we have

\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,y+z)=\Theta^{k}\Big(C,\frac{1}{2}(y+z)\Big).

This shows (
5.27
) and hence
(L_{y})_{\sharp}C=C
.
The translation invariance of
\sigma_{C}
is a trivial consequence of (
4.7
) applied to
C
and of
(L_{y})_{\sharp}C=C
.

It remains to show that

is a linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
C
is a cone, if

, then

for each

. Since

is a cone also with respect to

, then

also if

. If

, it follows from the previous discussion that also

and this proves that

is a linear subspace.
∎

Before going on, we first recall the definition of
approximate continuity
:

Definition 5.2

(Approximate continuity)
.

If
\mu
is a positive Radon measure and
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Borel function, we say that

is approximate continuous at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to
\mu
if

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu\big(\left\{z\in B_{r}(x)\mid\left|f(z)-f(x)\right|>\varepsilon\right\}\big)}{\mu(B_{r}(x))}=0\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0.

Remark 5.1
.

It is well-known that, if
\mu
is a Radon measure, then every
\mu
-measurable function is approximate continuous at
\mu
-a.e. point (see e.g.
[
7
, Theorem 1.37]
where the proof is done for the Lebesgue measure, but the same arguments can be applied to any Radon measure).

The following lemma states that there exists a set
F
of full
\sigma_{V}
-measure with respect to
\reg(V)
such that for every

the invariant subspace

of any cone

coincides with
\supp\sigma_{C}
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let

satisfy Assumption
1
.
Then there exists a set

that satisfies

\sigma_{C}=0
or

or

of full
\sigma_{V}
-measure with respect to
\reg(V)
where

.
Next, we fix

,
r_{j}\to 0

with respect to
\sigma_{V}
.

Step 1:

We are going first to define the set
F
of full
\sigma_{V}
-measure with respect to
\reg(V)
and next we will prove that the conclusion of the theorem holds for every

We call
A
the set of points

that satisfy all the following conditions:

(1)

and is of density
1
for
\reg(V)
with respect to

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\sigma_{V}\big(\reg(V)\cap B_{\rho}(x)\big)}{\sigma_{V}\big(B_{\rho}(x)\big)}=1;

(2)

is a point of approximate continuity for

with respect to
\sigma_{V}
(which is a well-defined Borel function in

and can be extended, for instance, to be
0
outside
\reg(V)

in
\reg(V)

By Lebesgue differentiation theorem and by Remark
5.1
, we have

(5.29)

\sigma_{V}(\reg(V)\setminus A)=0.

In addition, by Theorem
1.4
, for every

the map

is monotone increasing and converge pointwise to

Thus, by Egoroff’s Theorem, for every

such that

\sigma_{V}(A\setminus F_{h})\leq 1/h,\qquad\varphi_{x}(\rho)\utoi_{\rho\to 0}0\mbox{ on }F_{h}.

Up to removing sets of
\sigma_{V}
-measure
0
, we can assume that every

is a point of density
1
with respect to
\sigma_{V}
, that is

(5.31)

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\sigma_{V}\big(F_{h}\cap B_{\rho}(x)\big)}{\sigma_{V}\big(B_{\rho}(x)\big)}=1\qquad\forall x\in F_{h}.

We now define

F=A\cap\Big(\bigcup_{h\in\mathbb{N}}F_{h}\Big).

By (
5.29
) and (
5.30
) it follows

\sigma_{V}^{*}(\reg(V)\setminus F)=0.

Let us fix
x_{0}\in F
and let us consider

such that

. Without loss of generality we can assume

.

Now let us fix

.
Using the notations for the scalings, since

, by Lemma
5.4
there exists a subsequence, not relabeled, such that
V_{j}\wtoi C
with

and

is a rectifiable cone with

for
\lVert C\rVert
-a.e.

.

We now need a technical remark that is useful in the rest of the proof:

More precisely, if we call
\varphi_{x}^{j}
the function for
V_{j}
defined in Theorem
1.4
, we get

(5.32)

\varphi_{x}^{j}(\rho)=\varphi_{r_{j}x}(r_{j}\rho).

Step 2:

We can now begin with the proof. If
\sigma_{C}=0
there is nothing to prove. Thus we can assume
\sigma_{C}\neq 0
and
\emptyset\neq\supp\sigma_{C}\subset\partial T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M}

C
is a cone,

\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,y)\leq\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,0)\qquad\forall y\in\partial T_{0}^{+}\mathcal{M}.

By contradiction, let us assume that there exists
y\in\supp\sigma_{C}
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,y)<\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,0)-\varepsilon.

Since
\sigma_{C}
is scaling invariant by Lemma
5.4
, without loss of generality we can assume that
y\in B_{1/2}
.
By the uniform convergence (
5.30
) and by definition of density, there exists
\rho\in(0,1/2)

(5.34)

\frac{\lVert C\rVert\big(B_{\rho}(y)\big)}{\rho^{k}}+\varphi_{z}(\rho)\leq\Theta^{k}(\lVert C\rVert,y)+\frac{\varepsilon}{8}\qquad\forall z\in F_{h}.

Since
V_{j}\wtoi C

where we used the fact that every

This shows that, for

We now want to estimate from below the measure of this set to get a contradiction with the approximate continuity of
\Theta^{k}(\lVert V\rVert,{\cdot})
in

By approximate continuity of the

&\geq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{\sigma_{V}\big(B_{rr_{j}}(r_{j}y)\cap F_{h}\big)}{\sigma_{V}(B_{r_{j}})}\\
&=\limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{\sigma_{V}\big(B_{rr_{j}}(r_{j}y)\big)}{\sigma_{V}(B_{r_{j}})}\\
&=\limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{\sigma_{j}\big(B_{r}(y)\big)}{\sigma_{j}(B_{1})},\end{split}

where the second identity is consequence of (

We want to estimate from below the last term to get a contradiction. To do so, let us notice that, by

and

, there exist two constants

which contradicts (

Lemma 5.8

Proof.

Let

Without loss of generality we can assume that

, where

\alpha_{0}=\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{\sigma_{V}\big(B_{r_{j}}\big)}{\omega_{k-1}r_{j}^{k-1}}.

•

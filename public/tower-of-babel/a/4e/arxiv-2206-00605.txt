Averaging and mixing for stochastic perturbations of linear conservative systems
Guan Huang, Sergei Kuksin

Abstract
. We study stochastic perturbations of linear systems of the form ( ∗ ) d ​ v ​ ( t ) + A ​ v ​ ( t ) ​ d ​ t = ε ​ P ​ ( v ⁡ ( t ) ) ​ d ​ t + ε ​ ℬ ​ ( v ⁡ ( t ) ) ​ d ​ W ​ ( t ) , v ∈ ℝ D , dv(t)+Av(t)\,dt={\varepsilon}P(v(t))dt+\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\,\mathcal{B}(v(t))dW(t),\quad v\in\mathbb{R}^{D}, where A A is a linear operator with non-zero imaginary spectrum. 1.
Introduction

1.1.
The setting and problems.

The goal of this paper is to present an averaging theory for perturbations of conservative linear differential equations
by locally Lipschitz nonlinearities and stochastic terms. Namely, we will examine stochastic equations

(1.1)

dv(t)+Av(t)\,dt={\varepsilon}P(v(t))dt+\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\,\mathcal{B}(v(t))dW(t),\qquad v\in\mathbb{R}^{D},

where
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
,

A
is a linear operator with non-zero pure imaginary eigenvalues
\{i\lambda_{j}\}

(so the dimension
D
is even),
P
is a locally Lipschitz vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{D}
,

W(t)
is the standard Wiener process in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\mathcal{B}(v)
is an
D\times N
-matrix. We wish
to study for small
\varepsilon
the behaviour of solutions for eq. (
1.1
) on time-intervals of order
\varepsilon^{-1}
, and under some
additional restriction on the equation examine the limiting as
\varepsilon\to 0
behaviour of
solutions uniformly in time.

1.2.
Our results and their deterministic analogies.

We tried to make our work “reader-friendly” and accessible to people with just a limited knowledge of
stochastic calculus. To achieve that in the main part of the paper we restrict ourselves to
the case of equations with additive noise
\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\mathcal{B}\,dW(t)
and exploit there a technical convenience:
we introduce in
\mathbb{R}^{D}
a complex structure, re-writing the phase-space

as
\mathbb{C}^{D/2}

General equations (

E.g. one may

Linear systems and their perturbations

In this section we give the setting of the problem and specify assumptions on the operator

P
and noise

\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\mathcal{B}(v)d{W}
in eq. (

To
simplify presentation and explain better the ideas, in the main part of the text we assume that the noise

2.1.
Assumptions on
A
and
{W}(t)

We assume that the unperturbed linear system

(2.1)

is such that all its trajectories
are bounded as

. Then
the eigenvalues of

are pure imaginary, go in pairs

has no Jordan cells. We also assume that

So

(2)

By these assumptions

\{\mathbf{e}_{1}^{+},\mathbf{e}_{1}^{-},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{n}^{+},\mathbf{e}_{n}^{-}\}

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c c}\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\lambda_{1}\\
\lambda_{1}&0\end{array}&&0\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\lambda_{n}\\
\lambda_{n}&0\end{array}\end{array}\right)

We denote by

the coordinates, corresponding to this base, and for

j=1,\dots,n

. Then
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
becomes the space of complex vectors
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})

\mathbb{R}^{2n}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{n}

reads

\langle z,z^{\prime}\rangle=\text{Re}\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}\bar{z}_{j}^{\prime},\quad z,\;z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

of the linear system (
2.1

Therefore in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}

(d/dt)v_{k}+i\lambda_{k}v_{k}=0,\quad\;\;1\leqslant k\leqslant n.

Below we examine the perturbed eq. (
1.1

We next discuss the random process

.

4.
Effective equation and the averaging theorem

In this section we show that the limiting measure

in (

is independent of the choice of the sequence

and represent

as the law of a solution of an auxiliary

. The drift in this equation is the averaged drift in eq. (

). Now we construct its dispersion.

The diffusion matrix for equation (
2.11
) is the
n\times n
complex matrix

\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}(\tau)=(\Phi_{\tau{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda}\Psi)\cdot(\Phi_{\tau{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda}\Psi)^{*}.

Denoting

(4.1)

\Phi_{\tau{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda}\Psi=\big(e^{i\tau{\varepsilon}^{-1}\lambda_{l}}\Psi_{lj}\big)=:\big(\psi^{\varepsilon}_{lj}(\tau)\big)=\psi^{\varepsilon}(\tau),

we have

\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}_{kj}(\tau)=\sum_{l=1}^{n_{1}}\psi^{\varepsilon}_{kl}(\tau)\bar{\psi^{\varepsilon}_{jl}}(\tau)=e^{i\tau{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})}\sum_{l=1}^{n_{1}}\Psi_{kl}\bar{\Psi}_{jl}.

So for any
\tau>0
,

\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}_{kj}(s)ds=\big(\sum_{l=1}^{n_{1}}\Psi_{kl}\bar{\Psi}_{jl}\big)\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{is{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})}ds,

and we immediately see that

(4.2)

\frac{1}{{\tau}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}_{kj}(\tau)d\tau\to A_{kj}\quad\text{as}\quad{\varepsilon}\to 0,

where

(4.3)

A_{kj}=\begin{cases}\sum_{l=1}^{n_{1}}\Psi_{kl}\bar{\Psi}_{jl},&\text{if }\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{j},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Clearly,
A_{kj}=\bar{A}_{jk}
, so
A
is a Hermitian matrix.
If
\lambda_{k}\neq\lambda_{j}
for
k\neq j
, then

(4.4)

A=\text{diag}\{b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\},\quad b_{k}=\sum_{l=1}^{n_{1}}|\Psi_{kl}|^{2}.

For any vector
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
we get from (

since obviously

for each
{\varepsilon}
.
Therefore
A
is a nonnegative Hermitian matrix, and there exists another nonnegative Hermitian matrix
B

(called the principal square root of
A
) such that
BB^{*}=B^{2}=A
. The matrix
B
is non-degenerate if
\Psi
is.

.

If
\Psi
is a diagonal matrix diag
\,\{\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}\}
,

In this case

and
B=|\Psi|=
diag
\,\{|\psi_{1}|,\dots,|\psi_{n}|\}
.

is square and Hermitian, and the argument below remains true if we take for

any

-matrix (with any

and Chebyshev’s inequality,

\mathbf{P}(\Omega\setminus\mathcal{E}^{1})\leqslant\mathcal{H}_{1}({M_{1}}^{-1};|v_{0}|).

Recalling that
\tilde{y}
was defined in (
4.9
), by Lemma
3.2
we have that

|\tilde{y}(a^{{\varepsilon}}(s),s{\varepsilon}^{-1})|\leqslant|Y(a^{{\varepsilon}}(s),s{\varepsilon}^{-1})|+|{\llangle P\rrangle}(a^{{\varepsilon}}(s))|\leqslant 2{\mathcal{C}}^{m_{0}}(P)|a^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{m_{0}}.

So, abbreviating
\tilde{y}(a^{{\varepsilon}}(s),s{\varepsilon}^{-1})
to
\tilde{y}(s)
, in view of (
2.7
) we have:

\begin{split}&{\mathbf{E}}_{\Omega\setminus{\mathcal{E}^{1}}}\max_{\tau\in[0,T]}|\int_{0}^{\tau}\tilde{y}(s)ds|\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}{\mathbf{E}}\big(\mathbf{1}_{\Omega\setminus{\mathcal{E}^{1}}}|\tilde{y}(s)|\big)ds\\
&\leqslant 2{\mathcal{C}}^{m_{0}}(P)\big(P(\Omega\setminus{\mathcal{E}^{1}})\big)^{1/2}(\int_{0}^{T}{\mathbf{E}}|a^{{\varepsilon}}(s)|^{2m_{0}}ds)^{1/2}\\
&\leqslant 2{\mathcal{C}}^{m_{0}}(P)(\mathcal{H}_{1}({M_{1}}^{-1};|v_{0}|))^{1/2}=:\mathcal{H}_{2}({M_{1}}^{-1};|v_{0}|).\end{split}

Now we should estimate

{\mathbf{E}}_{{\mathcal{E}^{1}}}\max_{\tau\in[0,T]}|\int_{0}^{\tau}\tilde{y}(s)ds|
. For an
M_{2}\geqslant 1

consider the event

\mathcal{E}^{2}=\mathcal{E}^{2\varepsilon}_{M_{2}}=\{\omega\in\Omega:\|a^{\varepsilon}\|_{1/3}\leqslant M_{2}\}

(see (
1.4
)).
Then by (
2.13
)

\mathbf{P}(\Omega\setminus{\mathcal{E}^{2}})\leqslant\mathcal{H}_{3}(M_{2}^{-1};|v_{0}|).

Therefore,

{\mathbf{E}}_{\Omega\setminus\mathcal{E}^{2}}\max_{\tau\in[0,T]}|\int_{0}^{\tau}\tilde{y}(s)ds|\leqslant\big(\mathbf{P}(\Omega\setminus{\mathcal{E}^{2}})\big)^{1/2}(C_{P}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathbf{E}}|a^{{\varepsilon}}(s)|^{2m_{0}}ds)^{1/2}\leqslant\mathcal{H}_{4}(M_{2}^{-1};|v_{0}|)\,.

It remains to bound

\ {\mathbf{E}}_{{\mathcal{E}^{1}}\cap{\mathcal{E}^{2}}}\max_{\tau\in[0,T]}\big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\tilde{y}(s)ds\big|.

We set

N=[T/\sqrt{\varepsilon}]+1,\qquad L=T/N.

Then

C^{-1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\leqslant L\leqslant C\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}

and

c^{-1}/\sqrt{\varepsilon}\leqslant N\leqslant c/\sqrt{\varepsilon}

for some constants
C
and
c
. We consider a partition of interval
[0,T]
by points
\tau_{l}=lL
,
l=0,\dots,N
, and denote

\eta_{l}=\int_{\tau_{l}}^{\tau_{l+1}}\tilde{y}(s)ds,\quad l=0,\dots,N-1.

For any
\tau\in[0,T]
let us find
l=l(\tau)
such that
\tau\in[\tau_{l},\tau_{l+1}]
. Then

|\int_{0}^{\tau}\tilde{y}(s)ds|\leqslant|\eta_{1}|+\dots+|\eta_{l}|+|\int_{\tau_{l}}^{\tau}\tilde{y}(s)ds|.

If
\omega\in\mathcal{E}^{1}
, then

|\int_{\tau_{l}}^{\tau}\tilde{y}(s)ds|\leqslant 2{\mathcal{C}}^{m_{0}}(P){M_{1}}^{m_{0}}L.

Therefore

(4.27)

{\mathbf{E}}_{{\mathcal{E}^{1}}\cap{\mathcal{E}^{2}}}\max_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}\big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\tilde{y}(s)ds\big|\leqslant 2{\mathcal{C}}^{m_{0}}(P){M_{1}}^{m_{0}}L+\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{E}^{1}\cap\mathcal{E}^{2}}|\eta_{l}|,

and it remains to estimate the integrals

{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{E}^{1}\cap\mathcal{E}^{2}}|\eta_{l}|
for
l=0,\dots,N-1
.
Observe that

|\eta_{l}|\leqslant\Big|\int_{\tau_{l}}^{\tau_{l+1}}[\tilde{y}(a^{\varepsilon}(s),s{\varepsilon}^{-1})-\tilde{y}(a^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{l}),s{\varepsilon}^{-1})]ds\Big|+\Big|\int_{\tau_{l}}^{\tau_{l+1}}\tilde{y}(a^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{l}),s{\varepsilon}^{-1})ds\Big|=:|U_{l}^{1}|+|U_{l}^{2}|.

Since
\tilde{y}(a^{\varepsilon},\tau{\varepsilon}^{-1})=(\Phi_{\tau{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda})_{*}P(a^{\varepsilon})-{\llangle P\rrangle}(a^{\varepsilon})
and
P,{\llangle P\rrangle}\in\text{Lip}_{m_{0}}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{n})
, then for
\omega\in{\mathcal{E}^{1}}\cap\mathcal{E}^{2}

the integrand in
U_{l}^{1}
is bounded by

2{\mathcal{C}}^{m_{0}}(P){M_{1}}^{m_{0}}\sup_{\tau_{l}\leqslant s\leqslant\tau_{l+1}}|a^{\varepsilon}(s)-a^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{l})|\leqslant 2{\mathcal{C}}^{m_{0}}(P){M_{1}}^{m_{0}}M_{2}L^{1/3}.

So

|U_{l}^{1}|\leqslant 2{\mathcal{C}}^{m_{0}}(P){M_{1}}^{m_{0}}M_{2}L^{4/3}.

Now consider integral
U_{l}^{2}
. By the definition of
\tilde{y}(a^{\varepsilon},\tau{\varepsilon}^{-1})
, we have

U_{l}^{2}=\int_{\tau_{l}}^{\tau_{l+1}}Y(a^{{\varepsilon}}(\tau_{l}),s{\varepsilon}^{-1})ds\,-\,L{\llangle P\rrangle}(a^{{\varepsilon}}(\tau_{l})\big)=:Z^{1}+Z^{2}.

For integral
Z^{1}
, making the change of variable
s=\tau_{l}+{\varepsilon}x
with
x\in[0,\frac{L}{{\varepsilon}}]
, we have

Z^{1}={\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{L/{\varepsilon}}Y(a^{{\varepsilon}}(\tau_{l}),\tau_{l}{\varepsilon}^{-1}+x)dx.

Since

Y(a^{{\varepsilon}}(\tau_{l}),\tau_{l}{\varepsilon}^{-1}+x)=\Phi_{\tau_{l}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda}\circ\Phi_{x\Lambda}P\big(\Phi_{-x\Lambda}(\Phi_{-\tau_{l}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda}a^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{l}))\big),

then

Z^{1}=L\Phi_{\tau_{l}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda}\Big(\frac{{\varepsilon}}{L}\!\int_{0}^{L/{\varepsilon}}\Phi_{x\Lambda}P\big(\Phi_{-x\Lambda}\Big(\Phi_{-\tau_{l}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda}a^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{l})\big)\big)dx\Big)=L\Phi_{\tau_{l}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda}\llangle P\rrangle^{L/{\varepsilon}}\big(\Phi_{-\tau_{l}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda}a^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{l})\big)

(see (
3.1
)).
As
L/{\varepsilon}\sim{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}\gg 1
and
\big|\Phi_{-\tau_{l}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda}a^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{l})\big|=|a^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{l})|\leqslant{M_{1}}
for

\omega\in{\mathcal{E}^{1}}\cap\mathcal{E}^{2}
, then by Lemma
3.2
the partial averaging

\llangle P\rrangle^{L/{\varepsilon}}

is close to the complete averaging

\llangle P\rrangle.

Thus

\begin{split}\big|Z^{1}-L\Phi_{\tau_{l}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda}&{\llangle P\rrangle}\big(\Phi_{-\tau_{l}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda}(a^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{l}))\big)\big|=\big|Z^{1}-L(\Phi_{\tau_{l}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda})_{*}{\llangle P\rrangle}(a^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{l}))\big|\\
=&\big|Z^{1}-L{\llangle P\rrangle}(a^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{l}))\big|\leq L\mathcal{H}_{5}(\sqrt{{\varepsilon}};{M_{1}},|v_{0}|),\end{split}

where we used Lemma
3.3
to get the second equality.
Since
Z^{2}=-L{\llangle P\rrangle}(a^{\varepsilon}(\tau_{l}))
, then

|U_{l}^{2}|=|Z^{1}+Z^{2}|\leq L\mathcal{H}_{5}(\sqrt{{\varepsilon}};{M_{1}},|v_{0}|).

We then have obtained that

{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{E}^{1}\cap{\mathcal{E}^{2}}}|\eta_{l}|\leqslant 2{\mathcal{C}}^{m_{0}}(P){M_{1}}^{m_{0}}M_{2}L^{4/3}+L\mathcal{H}_{5}(\sqrt{{\varepsilon}};{M_{1}},|v_{0}|).

Together with (
4.27
) it gives us that

\begin{split}{\mathbf{E}}_{{\mathcal{E}^{1}}\cap{\mathcal{E}^{2}}}\Big(\max_{0\leqslant\tau\leq T}\big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\tilde{y}(s)ds\big|\Big)&\leqslant 2{\mathcal{C}}^{m_{0}}(P){M_{1}}^{m_{0}}L+2{\mathcal{C}}^{m_{0}}(P){M_{1}}^{m_{0}}M_{2}L^{1/3}+\mathcal{H}_{5}(\sqrt{{\varepsilon}};{M_{1}},|v_{0}|).\end{split}

Therefore

\begin{split}{\mathbf{E}}\max_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}\big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\tilde{y}(s)ds\big|&\leqslant\mathcal{H}_{2}({M_{1}}^{-1};|v_{0}|)+\mathcal{H}_{4}(M_{2}^{-1};|v_{0}|)\\
&+2{\mathcal{C}}^{m_{0}}(P){M_{1}}^{m_{0}}{(M_{2}+1)}{\varepsilon}^{1/6}+\mathcal{H}_{5}(\sqrt{{\varepsilon}};{M_{1}},|v_{0}|).\end{split}

Now for any
\delta>0
, we perform the following procedure:

(1)

choose
{M_{1}}
so large that
\mathcal{H}_{2}({M_{1}}^{-1};|v_{0}|)\leqslant\delta
;

(2)

choose
M_{2}
so large that
\mathcal{H}_{4}(M_{2}^{-1};|v_{0}|)\leqslant\delta
;

(3)

finally, choose
{\varepsilon}_{\delta}>0
so small that

2{\mathcal{C}}^{m_{0}}(P){M_{1}}^{m_{0}}(M_{2}+1){\varepsilon}^{1/3}+\mathcal{H}_{5}(\sqrt{{\varepsilon}};{M_{1}},|v_{0}|)\leqslant\delta\quad\text{if}\;\;\;0<{\varepsilon}\leqslant{\varepsilon}_{\delta}.

We have seen that for any
\delta>0
,
{\mathbf{E}}\max_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}\big|\int_{0}^{\tau}\tilde{y}(a^{{\varepsilon}}(s),s{\varepsilon}^{-1})ds\big|\leq 3\delta

if
{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{\delta}
. So

{\mathbf{E}}\max_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant T}\big|\int_{0}^{\tau}[Y(a^{{\varepsilon}}(s),s{\varepsilon}^{-1})-{\llangle P\rrangle}(a^{\varepsilon}(s))]ds\big|\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad{\varepsilon}\to 0,

which completes the proof of Lemma
4.3
.

5.
Stationary solutions and mixing

In this section we study relation between stationary solutions of equation (
2.6
) and of the effective equation.
We recall that a solution
a(\tau)
,
\tau\geqslant 0
, of equation (
2.6
) (or of effective equation (
4.5
)) is stationary if
\mathcal{D}(a(\tau))\equiv\mu
for all
\tau\geqslant 0
and some measure

\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, called a
stationary measure
for the corresponding equation.

Through this section we assume that equation (
2.6
) satisfies the following strengthening of
Assumption
2.1
:

Assumption 5.1
.

(1)

The drift
P(v)
is a locally Lipschitz vector filed, belonging to

\text{Lip}_{m_{0}}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{n})
for some
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
.

(2)

For any
v_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
equation (
2.6
) has a unique strong solution
v^{\varepsilon}(\tau;v_{0})
,

\tau\geq 0
, equal
v_{0}
at
\tau=0
. There exists
m_{0}^{\prime}>(m_{0}\vee 1)
such that

(5.1)

{\mathbf{E}}\sup_{{T^{\prime}}\leqslant\tau\leqslant{T^{\prime}}+1}|v^{\varepsilon}(\tau;v_{0})|^{2m^{\prime}_{0}}\leqslant C_{m^{\prime}_{0}}(|v_{0}|)

for every
{T^{\prime}}\geqslant 0
and
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1]
, where
C_{m_{0}^{\prime}}(\cdot)
is a continuous non-decreasing function.

(3)

Equation (
2.6
) is
mixing
.
That is, it has a stationary solution

v^{\varepsilon}_{st}(\tau)
,
\mathcal{D}(v^{\varepsilon}_{st}(\tau))\equiv\mu^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, and

(5.2)

\mathcal{D}(v^{\varepsilon}(\tau;v_{0}))\rightharpoonup\mu^{\varepsilon}\text{ in }\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\;\;\text{ as }\tau\to+\infty,

for every
v_{0}
.

Under Assumption
5.1
equation (
2.6
) defines in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
a mixing
Markov process with the transition probability

\Sigma_{\tau}(v)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
,
\tau\geq 0
,
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

where

\Sigma_{\tau}(v)=\mathcal{D}v^{\varepsilon}(\tau;v);

e.g. see
[
14
, Section 5.4.C]
. Let us denote by
X
the complete separable metric space

X=C([0,\infty),\mathbb{C}^{n})

with the distance

(5.3)

\text{dist}(a_{1},a_{2})=\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}2^{-N}\frac{\|a_{1}-a_{2}\|_{C([0,N],\mathbb{C}^{n})}}{1+\|a_{1}-a_{2}\|_{C([0,N],\mathbb{C}^{n})}},\qquad a_{1},a_{2}\in X\,,

and consider on
X
continuous function

{g}(a)=\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|a(t)|^{2m^{\prime}_{0}}.

Denoting

\mu^{\tau}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{D}(v^{\varepsilon}(\tau;0))

we have by the Markov property that

(5.4)

{\mathbf{E}}\sup_{T^{\prime}\leqslant\tau\leqslant T^{\prime}+1}|v^{\varepsilon}(\tau;0)|^{m}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\mathbf{E}{g}(v^{\varepsilon}(\cdot;v_{0}))\mu^{T^{\prime}}_{\varepsilon}(dv_{0}),

and

(5.5)

{\mathbf{E}}\sup_{0\leqslant\tau\leqslant 1}|v^{\varepsilon}_{st}(\tau;0)|^{m}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\mathbf{E}{g}(v^{\varepsilon}(\cdot;v_{0}))\mu^{\varepsilon}(dv_{0}).

The l.h.s. of (
5.4
) is estimated in (
5.1
). To estimate (
5.5
) we will pass in the r.h.s. of (
5.4
) to the limit
as
{T^{\prime}}\to\infty
, using (
5.2
). To do that we start with a lemma

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
is a closed convex set which contains more than one point, and
F:\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
is
a Lipschitz mapping. Then
F
may be extended to a map
\tilde{F}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
in such a way that

a) Lip
\,\tilde{F}
= Lip
\,F
,

b)
\tilde{F}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}})=F(\mathcal{B})
.

Proof
.

Let
\Pi:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\to\mathcal{B}
be the projection, sending any point of
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
to a nearest point in
\mathcal{B}
.
Then Lip
\,\Pi
=1, see Appendix
D
,
and obviously
\tilde{F}=F\circ\Pi
is a required extension of
F
.
∎

Since
\mathcal{C}^{m_{0}}(P)=:C_{*}<\infty
, then for any
M\in\mathbb{N}
the restriction of
P
to
\bar{B}_{M}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
and its Lipschitz constant both
are bounded by
(1+M)^{m_{0}}C_{*}
. In view of the lemma above we may extend

P\!\mid_{\bar{B}_{M}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}

to a Lipschitz mapping
P^{M}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

\text{Lip}\,(P^{M})\leq(1+M)^{m_{0}}C_{*},\quad\sup|P^{M}(v)|\leq(1+M)^{m_{0}}C_{*}.

Considering for a solution
v(\tau)
of equation (
2.6
) the stopping time

\tau_{M}=\inf\{t\geq 0:|v(t)|\geq M\}
and denoting by
v^{\varepsilon M}
the stopped solution

v^{\varepsilon M}(\tau;v_{0})=v^{\varepsilon}(\tau\wedge\tau_{M};v_{0}),

we note that the process
v^{\varepsilon M}
will not change if in (
2.6
) we replace
P(v)
by
P^{M}(v)
. So

v^{\varepsilon M}(\tau;v_{0})
is a stopped solution of a stochastic equation with Lipschitz coefficients, and thus a.s. the
curve
v^{\varepsilon M\omega}(\cdot;v_{0})\in X
continuously depends on
v_{0}
, for each
M\in\mathbb{N}
. As

{g}(v^{\varepsilon M})\leq{g}(v^{\varepsilon})\quad\text{ a.s.,}

then in view of (
5.2
) and (
5.1
),
for every
M
and any
N>0
,

\int\mathbf{E}(N\wedge{g})(v^{\varepsilon M}(\cdot;v))\,\mu^{\varepsilon}(dv)=\lim_{{T^{\prime}}\to\infty}\int\mathbf{E}(N\wedge{g})(v^{\varepsilon M}(\cdot;v))\,\mu^{T^{\prime}}_{\varepsilon}(dv)\leq C_{m}(0)

(to get the last inequality from (
5.1
) we used the Markov property).
Passing in the l.h.s. to the limit as
N\to\infty
using the monotone convergence theorem we see that

(5.6)

\int\mathbf{E}{g}(v^{\varepsilon M}(\cdot;v))\,\mu^{\varepsilon}(dv)\leq C_{m}(0).

Since for every
v
a.s.

{g}(v^{\varepsilon M}(\cdot;v))\nearrow{g}(v^{\varepsilon}(\cdot;v))\leq\infty

as

M\to\infty,

then using again the latter theorem we derive from (
5.6
) that

\int\mathbf{E}{g}(v^{\varepsilon}(\cdot;v))\,\mu^{\varepsilon}(dv)\leq C_{m}(0).

Evoking (
5.5
) we get

Lemma 5.3
.

The stationary solution
v^{\varepsilon}_{st}(\tau)
satisfies estimate (
5.1
) with

C_{m_{0}^{\prime}}(|v_{0}|)
replaced by
C_{m_{0}^{\prime}}(0)
.

Let us consider the interaction representation for
v^{\varepsilon}_{st}
,

v^{\varepsilon}_{st}(\tau)=\Phi_{-\tau{\varepsilon}^{-1}\Lambda}a^{\varepsilon}(\tau)
(note that
a^{\varepsilon}
is not a stationary process!).
Then
a^{\varepsilon}(\tau)
satisfies equation (
2.11
), so for any
N\in\mathbb{N}

the collection of measures

\{\mathcal{D}(a^{\varepsilon}|_{[0,N]}),0<{\varepsilon}\leqslant 1\}
is tight in view of (
5.1
) (for the same reason as in Section
2.4
). We
choose a sequence
{\varepsilon}_{l}\to 0
(depending on
N
) such that

\mathcal{D}(a^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}|_{[0,N]})\rightharpoonup Q_{0}\text{ in }\mathcal{P}\big(C([0,N],\mathbb{C}^{n})\big).

Applying the diagonal process and replacing
\{{\varepsilon}_{l}\}
by a subsequence, which we still denote
\{{\varepsilon}_{l}\}
, we achieve that

\mathcal{D}a^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}\rightharpoonup Q_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad\mathcal{P}(X)

(see (
5.3
)).

Since
a^{\varepsilon}(0)=v_{st}^{\varepsilon}(0)
, then

\mu^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}\rightharpoonup\mu^{0}:=Q_{0}|_{\tau=0}.

Let
a^{0}(\tau)
be a process in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\mathcal{D}(a^{0})=Q_{0}.
Then

(5.7)

\mathcal{D}(a^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau))\rightharpoonup\mathcal{D}(a^{0}(\tau))\quad\forall\,0\leq\tau<\infty.

In particular,
\mathcal{D}(a^{0}(0))=\mu^{0}
.

Proposition 5.4
.

(1)

The limiting process
a^{0}
is a stationary weak solution of effective equation (
4.5
) and

\mathcal{D}(a^{0}(\tau))\equiv\mu^{0}
,
\tau\in[0,\infty)
. In particular,
the limiting points of the collection of stationary measures

\{\mu^{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon}\in(0,1]\}
as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
are stationary measures of the
effective equation.

(2)

Any limiting measure
\mu^{0}
is invariant under operators
\Phi_{\theta\Lambda}
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. So

\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{\theta\Lambda}a^{0}(\tau))=\mu^{0}
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\tau\in[0,\infty)
.

Proof
.

(1)
Using Lemma
5.3
and repeating the argument in the proof of Proposition
4.2
we
obtain that
a^{0}
is a weak solution of the effective equation. It remains to prove its stationarity.

Take any bounded Lipschitz function
f
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and consider

{\mathbf{E}}\int_{0}^{1}f(v_{st}^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau))d\tau={\mathbf{E}}\int_{0}^{1}f(\Phi_{-\tau{{\varepsilon}_{l}}^{-1}\Lambda}a^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau))d\tau.

Using the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
4.3
(but applied to averaging of functions rather than that of vector-fields),
we obtain that

(5.8)

\begin{split}{\mathbf{E}}\int_{0}^{1}&f\big(v_{st}^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau)\big)d\tau-{\mathbf{E}}\int_{0}^{1}\langle f\rangle\big(a^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau)\big)d\tau\\
&={\mathbf{E}}\int_{0}^{1}\big(f\big(\Phi_{-\tau{\varepsilon}_{l}^{-1}\Lambda}a^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau))-\langle f\rangle(a^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau)\big)\big)d\tau\to 0\quad\text{as}\;\;{\varepsilon}_{l}\to 0.\end{split}

By Lemma
3.6
,

\langle f\rangle\big(a^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau)\big)=\langle f\rangle\big(\Phi_{\tau{\varepsilon}_{l}^{-1}\Lambda}v_{st}^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau)\big)=\langle f\rangle\big(v_{st}^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau)\big)

for every
\tau
.
Since the process
v_{st}^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau)
is stationary, then

{\mathbf{E}}f(v_{st}^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau))=\text{Const}
and
{\mathbf{E}}\langle f\rangle\big(a^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau)\big)={\mathbf{E}}\langle f\rangle(v_{st}^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau))=\text{Const}^{\prime}
.

So we get from (
5.8
) that

(5.9)

{\mathbf{E}}f\big(v_{st}^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau)\big)-{\mathbf{E}}\langle f\rangle\big(a^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau)\big)\to 0\;\text{ as }{\varepsilon}_{l}\to 0\quad\forall\,\tau.

For any
\tau
let us consider
\tilde{f}_{\tau}=f\circ\Phi_{\tau{\varepsilon}_{l}^{-1}\Lambda}
.
Then
f(a^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau))=\tilde{f}_{\tau}(v^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}_{st}(\tau))
.
Since
\langle f\rangle=\langle\tilde{f}_{\tau}\rangle
by Lemma
3.6
.(4), then applying (
5.9
)
to
\tilde{f}_{\tau}
we get:

\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{l}\to 0}\mathbf{E}f(a^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau))=\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{l}\to 0}\mathbf{E}\tilde{f}_{\tau}(v_{st}^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau))=\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{l}\to 0}\mathbf{E}\langle\tilde{f}_{\tau}\rangle(a^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau))=\lim_{{\varepsilon}_{l}\to 0}\mathbf{E}\langle f\rangle(a^{{\varepsilon}_{l}}(\tau)).

From this relation, (
5.9
) and (
5.7
) we find that

\mathbf{E}f(a^{0}(\tau))=\int\!\!f(v)\,\mu^{0}(dv)

for each
\tau
and every

f
as above. This implies the first assertion of the lemma.

(2) Passing to the limit in (
5.9
) using (
5.7
) we have

\int f(v)\mu^{0}(dv)=\mathbf{E}\langle f\rangle(a^{0}(\tau))\quad\forall\,\tau.

Using this relation with
f:=f\circ\Phi_{\theta\Lambda}
and next with
f:=f
we get that

\int f\circ\Phi_{\theta\Lambda}(v)\mu^{0}(dv)=\mathbf{E}\langle f\circ\Phi_{\theta\Lambda}\rangle(a^{0}(\tau))=\mathbf{E}\langle f\rangle(a^{0}(\tau))=\int f(v)\mu^{0}(dv),

for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and any
\tau
, for every bounded Lipschitz function
f
. This implies the second assertion.
∎

If effective equation (
4.5
) is mixing, then it has a unique stationary measure. In this case the
measure
\mu^{0}
in Proposition
5.4
does not depend on a choice of the sequence
{\varepsilon}_{l}\to 0
,
and so
\mu^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\mu^{0}
as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
. Therefore, we have

Theorem 5.5
.

If in addition to Assumption
5.1
we assume
that the effective equation is mixing and
\mu^{0}
is its unique stationary measure, then

\mu^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\mu^{0}\text{ in }\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\quad\text{as}\quad{\varepsilon}\to 0.

Moreover, the measure
\mu^{0}
is invariant for all operators
\Phi_{\theta\Lambda}
,
and the law of the stationary solution of equation (
2.6
), written in the interaction presentation, converges to the law of the stationary solution of effective equation (
4.5
).

We recall that Theorem
4.7
and Corollary
4.13
only assure that on finite time-intervals
\tau\in[0,T]
the
actions of solutions for eq. (
2.6
), as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
converge in law to the actions of solutions for the effective equation with the same initial data. In difference, when

{\varepsilon}\to 0

all
of the stationary measure for eq. (
2.6
) converges
to that for the effective equation. This important fact was first observed
in
[
9
]
for a special class of equations (
2.6
).

Corollary 5.6
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
5.5
, for any
v_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

\partial^{2}f/\partial\bar{v}\partial\bar{v}
and
\partial^{2}f/\partial v\partial\bar{v}
, with the coefficients, quadratic in the matrices
M^{1}
and
M^{2}
.
So it may be written as

(C.3)

\Big[\operatorname{Trace}\big(Q^{1}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial v\partial v}\big)+\operatorname{Trace}\big(Q^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\bar{v}\partial\bar{v}}\big)+\operatorname{Trace}\big(Q^{3}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial v\partial\bar{v}}\big)\Big]dt,

where
Q^{j}
are complex
n\times n
-matrices, quadratic in
M^{1},M^{2}
. We should show that they
have the form, specified in
(
C.2
). To do that we note that since the processes
\beta^{c}_{j}
are independent and have the form (
2.3
), then for all
j,l
their
brackets have the following form:

(C.4)

\langle\beta^{c}_{j},\beta^{c}_{l}\rangle=\langle\bar{\beta}^{c}_{j},\bar{\beta}^{c}_{l}\rangle=0,\quad\langle\beta^{c}_{j},\bar{\beta}^{c}_{l}\rangle=\langle\bar{\beta}^{c}_{j},\beta^{c}_{l}\rangle=2\delta_{j,l}t.

Now let in (
C.1
)
g=0,v(0)=0
and
M^{1},M^{2}
are constant matrices. Then

v(t)=M^{1}B(t)+M^{2}\bar{B}(t).

Taking
f(v)=v_{i_{1}}v_{i_{2}}
and using (
C.4
) we see that

\begin{split}f(v(t))&=\Big(\sum_{j}M^{1}_{i_{1}j}B_{j}(t)+\sum_{j}M^{2}_{i_{1}j}\bar{B}_{j}(t)\Big)\cdot\Big(\sum_{j}M^{1}_{i_{2}j}B_{j}(t)+\sum_{j}M^{2}_{i_{2}j}\bar{B}_{j}(t)\Big)\\
&=\Big[\big(M^{1}(M^{2})^{t}\big)_{i_{1}i_{2}}+\big(M^{2}(M^{1})^{t}\big)_{i_{1}i_{2}}\Big]2\,t+\text{ a martingale}.\end{split}

Since due to (
C.3
) the linear in
t
part should equal
(Q_{i_{1}i_{2}}+Q_{i_{2}i_{1}})t
, then

Q^{1}=M^{1}(M^{2})^{t}+M^{2}(M^{1})^{t}.

Similar, considering
f(v)=\bar{v}_{i_{1}}\bar{v}_{i_{2}}
we find that

Q^{2}=\bar{M}^{1}(\bar{M}^{2})^{t}+\bar{M}^{2}(\bar{M}^{1})^{t},

while letting
f(v)=v_{i_{1}}\bar{v}_{i_{2}}
leads to

2\big(M^{1}(\bar{M}^{1})^{t}+M^{2}(\bar{M}^{2})^{t}\big)_{i_{1}i_{2}}=Q^{3}_{i_{1}i_{2}};

so

Q^{3}=2(M^{1}(\bar{M}^{1})^{t}+M^{2}(\bar{M}^{2})^{t}).

This completes the proof of (
C.2
). The second assertion of the theorem follows by a straightforward calculation.
∎

Appendix D
Projections to convex sets

Lemma D.1
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a closed convex subset of a Hilbert space
X
of finite or infinite dimension. Let
\mathcal{B}

contains at least two points and let
\Pi:X\to\mathcal{B}
be the projection,
sending any
point of
X
to a nearest point of
\mathcal{B}
. Then Lip
\,\Pi=1
.

Proof
.

Let
A,B\in X
and let
a=\Pi A\in\mathcal{B},b=\Pi B\in\mathcal{B}
. If
A,B\in\mathcal{B}
then
a=A
and
B=b
. So Lip
\,\Pi\geq 1
and
it remains to show that

\|a-b\|\leq\|A-B\|\qquad\forall\,A\neq B.

If
a=b
the assertion is trivial. Otherwise consider the vectors

\xi=b-a,

l^{a}=A-a
,
l^{b}=B-b
, and introduce in
X
an orthonormal basis
(e_{1},e_{2},\dots)
such that

e_{1}=\xi/|\xi|
. Then
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots)
, where
\xi_{1}=|\xi|
and
\xi_{j}=0
for
j\geq 2
. Since
a
is a point in

[a,b]\subset X
, the
closest to
A
,
then
l^{a}_{1}=l^{a}\cdot e_{1}\leq 0
. Similar
l^{b}_{1}\geq 0
. Thus

\|B-A\|=\|\xi+l^{b}-l^{a}\|\geq|\xi_{1}+l_{1}^{b}-l_{1}^{a}|\geq\xi_{1}=\|b-a\|,

and the assertion is proved.
∎

Note that an analogy of the lemma’s statement for a Banach space
X
in general is wrong.

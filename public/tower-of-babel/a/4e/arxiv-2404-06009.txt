Abstract
. We determine the maximal dimension of compact subvarieties of the moduli space 𝒜 g {\mathcal{A}}_{g} of complex principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension g g , and we characterize all compact subvarieties of maximal dimension. Moreover we determine the maximal dimension of a compact subvariety through a very general point of 𝒜 g {\mathcal{A}}_{g} . These results also allow us to draw some conclusions for compact subvarieties of the moduli space of complex curves of compact type.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Compact subvarieties of maximal dimension

Given a
complex
irreducible quasi-projective variety
\mathcal{V}
, it is natural to ask how far is
\mathcal{V}
from being affine or projective. Perhaps one of the most intuitive ways to measure this is the
maximal dimension of a compact subvariety of
\mathcal{V}
, which we denote
\operatorname{mdc}(\mathcal{V})
.
On the other hand, if one takes
\mathcal{V}
to be the blow-up
of an affine space at the origin,
such
\mathcal{V}
has a compact exceptional divisor, though this large compact subvariety is somehow “accidental”.
Thus, a perhaps more natural quantity, which in particular is invariant under
proper birational morphisms,
is the
maximal dimension of an
irreducible
compact subvariety of
\mathcal{V}
passing through a very general point of
\mathcal{V}
, which we denote by
\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}(\mathcal{V})
.

We observe that
0\leq\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}(\mathcal{V})\leq\operatorname{mdc}(\mathcal{V})\leq\dim\mathcal{V}
.
Clearly
\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}(\mathcal{V})=\operatorname{mdc}(\mathcal{V})=0
if
\mathcal{V}
is affine, while the converse is in general not true. On the other hand,
\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}(\mathcal{V})=\operatorname{mdc}(\mathcal{V})=\dim\mathcal{V}
if and only if
\mathcal{V}
is projective.

Note that
\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}(\mathcal{V})
can also be understood
as the maximum value of
d
for which there exists
a family of
d
-dimensional
irreducible
compact subvarieties of
\mathcal{V}
whose total space dominates
\mathcal{V}
.

Example 1.1
.

If we denote by
\operatorname{mdc}(\mathcal{V},p)
the maximal dimension
of an irreducible
compact subvariety of
\mathcal{V}
that contains the point
p\in\mathcal{V}
,
then the function
\mathcal{V}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
,
defined as
p\mapsto\operatorname{mdc}(\mathcal{V},p)
,
is in general neither upper nor lower semi-continuous.
As an example,
consider
\pi:\mathrm{Bl}_{\ell}({\mathbb{C}}^{3})\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
the blow-up
of the complex affine space
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
along a line
\ell\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
. Pick
q\in\ell
and let
\mathcal{V}\coloneqq\mathrm{Bl}_{\ell}({\mathbb{C}}^{3})\setminus\{\tilde{q}\}
, for some point
\tilde{q}\in\pi^{-1}(q)
.
We claim that
\operatorname{mdc}(\mathcal{V},p)
takes value
1

on
\pi^{-1}(\ell\setminus\{q\})
and
0
elsewhere.
Note that
\pi^{-1}(\ell\setminus\{q\})
is neither open nor closed (even in the classical topology).

Indeed, since the only irreducible compact subvarieties of
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}

are points, any irreducible compact subvariety of
\mathcal{V}

through the point
p
must be contained in
\pi^{-1}(\pi(p))\cap\mathcal{V}
.

Then
\operatorname{mdc}(\mathcal{V},p)=1
if

p
belongs to
\pi^{-1}(\ell\setminus\{q\})
, as one can take the whole fiber
\pi^{-1}(\pi(p))\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
as a compact subvariety. If
p\notin\pi^{-1}(\ell)
, then certainly
\operatorname{mdc}(\mathcal{V},p)=0
, as
\pi^{-1}(\pi(p))=\{p\}
.
Finally if
p\in\pi^{-1}(q)\setminus\{\tilde{q}\}
, then

\pi^{-1}(q)\cap\mathcal{V}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}
, and so an irreducible compact subvariety
through
p
must consist only of
\{p\}
,
which again implies
\operatorname{mdc}(\mathcal{V},p)=0
.

Remark 1.2
.

If
f:\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{V}
is a generically finite,
proper
map of
irreducible
varieties, then
\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}(\mathcal{V}^{\prime})=\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}(\mathcal{V})
; and furthermore, if
f
is finite surjective, then also
\operatorname{mdc}(\mathcal{V}^{\prime})=\operatorname{mdc}(\mathcal{V})
.

Remark 1.3
.

If
\overline{\mathcal{V}}\supsetneq\mathcal{V}
is a projective compactification of
\mathcal{V}
, such that the boundary
\partial\mathcal{V}\coloneqq\overline{\mathcal{V}}\setminus\mathcal{V}
has codimension
d
, then by embedding
\overline{\mathcal{V}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
and recursively choosing general hypersurfaces
H_{1},\dots,H_{\dim\mathcal{V}-d+1}\subsetneq{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
of sufficiently high degree such that
\dim\left(H_{1}\cap\dots\cap H_{i}\cap\partial\mathcal{V}\right)=(\dim\mathcal{V}-d)-i
for any
i
, it follows that
H_{1}\cap\dots\cap H_{\dim\mathcal{V}-d+1}\cap\overline{\mathcal{V}}
is a compact subvariety of
\mathcal{V}
, which moreover can be chosen to pass through any prescribed
finite collection of points of
\mathcal{V}
.
Thus the existence of such a compactification
\overline{\mathcal{V}}
implies
\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}(\mathcal{V})\geq d-1
. We note, however, that in general there is no implication going the other way, as is easily seen by considering
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\subsetneq{\mathbb{P}}^{m+n}
for various
m,n
.

In this paper we determine

\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})
and
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})
for the moduli space
{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

of complex principally polarized abelian varieties (ppav) of dimension
g
, and we draw some consequences for the moduli of complex curves of compact type, and for the locus of indecomposable abelian varieties.

Convention
.

Throughout this paper, varieties
are always assumed to be
reduced, but not necessarily irreducible.
We will abuse notation and we will use the same symbol
for a reduced algebraic scheme over
\mathrm{Spec}({\mathbb{C}})
and for the associated complex variety.

1.2.
Compact subvarieties through a very general point of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

We recall that the moduli space of principally polarized complex
abelian varieties of dimension
g

is a smooth Deligne-Mumford stack. Its underlying coarse moduli space

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

is an irreducible quasi-projective variety of dimension
\frac{g(g+1)}{2}
.
Its Satake compactification

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{*}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

is an irreducible projective variety
and its boundary

\partial{\mathcal{A}}^{*}_{g}={\mathcal{A}}^{*}_{g}\setminus{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
can be identified with
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}_{g-1}
,
as in fact
it can be naturally stratified as

\partial{\mathcal{A}}^{*}_{g}\cong{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}\sqcup{\mathcal{A}}_{g-2}\sqcup\dots\sqcup{\mathcal{A}}_{0}

(see
[
Sat56
]
).
Thus

\partial{\mathcal{A}}^{*}_{g}
has pure codimension
g
in
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}_{g}
,
which by
Remark

1.3
implies
\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})\geq g-1
.

Our first result is that this bound is sharp (as always, over
{\mathbb{C}}
).

Theorem A
.

The maximal dimension of a Hodge-generic compact subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

is
g-1
. In particular

\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})=g-1
.

The first claim in
Theorem

A
is a particular, and slightly easier, case of
Theorem

4.5
.
The second claim directly follows from the first one.
Indeed, as recalled in
Section

2.4
below, a subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

is
Hodge-generic
if it
is not contained in one of the countably many
special
subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
,
different
from
{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
itself.

1.3.
Compact subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

Turning to the question of maximal dimension of all compact subvarieties, the best known upper bound for

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

is the 20-year-old result of Keel and Sadun
[
KS03
]
, who proved that for any
g\geq 3

a compact subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

has codimension
strictly larger than
g
, namely

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})\leq\tfrac{g(g-1)}{2}-1
. This was conjectured by Oort, and underscored the difference between the moduli space
{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
of complex ppav (that we work with)
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{g,\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, as in finite characteristic there does exist a complete subvariety of
{\mathcal{A}}_{g,\mathbb{F}_{p}}
of codimension exactly
g
,
namely the locus of ppav
whose subscheme of
p
-torsion points is supported at zero.

We determine precisely the maximal dimension of compact subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
, for all genera.

Theorem B
.

The maximal dimension of a compact subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

is

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})=\max\left(g-1,\left\lfloor\frac{\lfloor g/2\rfloor^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor\right)=\begin{cases}\ \ g-1\ &\mbox{if $g<16$}\\[4.0pt]
\ \ \left\lfloor\tfrac{g^{2}}{16}\right\rfloor&\mbox{for even }g\geq 16\\[10.0pt]
\left\lfloor\tfrac{(g-1)^{2}}{16}\right\rfloor&\mbox{for odd }g\geq 17\,.\end{cases}

In
Table

1
we give our results and the bound of Keel-Sadun, for comparison, in some small genera, and also for
g=100
to underscore the difference of the growth rates.

\begin{array}[]{|lr||rrrr||rrrr||r|}\hline\cr&g=&3&4&5&6&15&16&17&18&100\\
\hline\cr\text{Theorem A:}&\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})=&2&3&4&5&14&15&16&17&99\\
\text{Theorem B:}&\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})=&2&3&4&5&14&16&16&20&625\\
\text{Keel-Sadun:}&\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})\leq&2&5&9&14&104&119&135&152&4949\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Maximal dimensions of compact subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
.

What we actually prove is much stronger: we show that any compact subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

is contained – up to Hecke translations – in the image of the product

S^{\prime}\times Z^{\prime\prime}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{g^{\prime}}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g^{\prime\prime}}
,
where

S^{\prime}
is a compact special subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g^{\prime}}

and
Z^{\prime\prime}
is compact Hodge-generic
in a non-compact special subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g^{\prime\prime}}
,
with dimension
\dim Z^{\prime\prime}\leq g^{\prime\prime}-1
, for some non-negative integers
g^{\prime}+g^{\prime\prime}=g

(see
Theorem

3.7
and
Theorem

4.5
).

We then determine the maximal dimension of compact special subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
.
In fact
our analysis allows us to describe all maximal-dimensional
irreducible compact subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
;
they are as follows:

(a)

Hodge-generic, for
g\leq 15
and for
g=17
, or

(b)

special subvarieties
S_{g}\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
of a specific type (see
Section

7.2.3
)
for
g=2
or
g\geq 16
even,
or

(c)

weakly special subvarieties
that are irreducible components of Hecke translates of the
image of
[E]\times S_{g-1}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}
under the standard morphism

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
,
with

S_{g-1}\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}
compact special as in subcase (b),
and with any fixed
[E]\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
, for
g\geq 17
odd.

The existence of two essentially different types of maximal compact subvarieties is reflected in
the two different growth regimes for
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})
:
linear in the Hodge-generic case (a),
versus quadratic in the (weakly) special
cases (b) and (c). We give a more precise statement in
Theorem

8.9
.

Remark 1.4
.

For any finite cover (e.g. any finite abelian level cover)

{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{g}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
,
we have

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})=\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{g})
and
\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})=\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{g})
by
Remark

1.2
.
It
also follows from that remark
that
\operatorname{mdc}
and
\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}
are preserved by finite (surjective) correspondences; as a consequence, our
Theorem

A
and
Theorem

B

apply to moduli of complex abelian varieties with any fixed polarization type and with any additional finite level structure. It also follows that, up to taking irreducible components,
the collection of compact subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

that achieve

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})
or
\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})
is stable under Hecke translations.

Remark 1.5
.

It is a natural interesting question to determine all maximal (as opposed to just maximal-dimensional) compact subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
,
that is to determine
all irreducible
compact subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

that are not strictly contained in another
irreducible
compact subvariety.
This investigation is related to understanding compact subvarieties of maximal dimension inside other non-compact Shimura varieties

1.4.
Compact subvarieties of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}

Recall that the Torelli map sending a smooth complex curve to its Jacobian is an injection

{\mathcal{J}}:{\mathcal{M}}_{g}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

of the coarse moduli spaces (and is 2:1 onto its image as a map of stacks, but this does not matter for discussing compact subvarieties).
Its image is disjoint from the
decomposable locus

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\mathrm{dec}}
,
namely the locus of ppav which are
isomorphic to the product of two smaller dimensional ppav,
which is naturally
the union
of the images of the standard morphisms

{\mathcal{A}}_{k}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-k}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

for
0<k<g
.
A very interesting problem is to determine

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\rm ind})
and
\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\rm ind})

of
the locus

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\rm ind}\coloneqq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}\setminus{\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\mathrm{dec}}

of indecomposable abelian varieties,
see
Section

9.1
.

Remark 1.6
.

The homology classes of the images of the standard maps

{\mathcal{A}}_{k}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-k}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

for
0<k<g
were recently studied in
[
CMOP24
,
COP24
,
IL25
]
. It would be interesting to investigate how our approach, described
in
Section

1.5
, relates to this study
(see also
Section

9.1
).

The Torelli morphism extends to a proper morphism

{\mathcal{J}}:{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}\to{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

from the moduli space of curves of compact type, that is from the moduli space of stable nodal curves such that each node is separating. As a corollary of their bound

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{3})\leq 2
and of a theorem of Diaz
[
Dia84
]
stating that
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g})\leq g-2
, Keel and Sadun
[
KS03
]
deduce the bound
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})\leq 2g-4
for any
g\geq 3
. Our results also have implications for
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}
.

Corollary C
.

The following equality and upper bound hold:

\displaystyle\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})

\displaystyle=\lfloor 3g/2\rfloor-2

\displaystyle\hbox{for any $2\leq g\leq 23$\,,}

\displaystyle\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}))

\displaystyle\leq g-1

\displaystyle\hbox{for any $2\leq g\leq 15$\,.}

Moreover
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}))\geq\lfloor\frac{2g}{3}\rfloor
for all
g\geq 2
.

The point of the difference between these two quantities is that
{\mathcal{J}}
is injective at a generic point of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}
,
but not along

\partial{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}={\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
,
as it sends a stable curve to the product of the Jacobians of its irreducible components, forgetting the locations of the nodes.

To obtain the first equality, in
Lemma

9.2
we construct for any
g\geq 3
a compact subvariety of

{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{g}

of dimension
\lfloor 3g/2\rfloor-2
, starting from a compact curve in

{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{\rm ct}

(which thus gives
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})\geq\lfloor 3g/2\rfloor-2
for any
g\geq 2
).

The second inequality
in the corollary
implies the bound on the maximal dimension of compact subvarieties of

{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}

that intersect

{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
,
and follows from
Theorem

B
(in fact
Theorem

B
also improves Keel and Sadun’s bound
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})\leq 2g-4
to
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})\leq\lfloor\lfloor g/2\rfloor^{2}/4\rfloor
for
g\leq 28
).

It is tempting to conjecture that in fact
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}))\leq g-1
for all
g\geq 2
, which would then imply
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})=\lfloor 3g/2\rfloor-2
for all
g\geq 2
. Notice that Krichever
[
Kri12
]
claimed exactly such a bound, but unfortunately his proof had a gap that was never fixed.

1.5.
Idea of the proof of
Theorem

A

We observed at the beginning of
Section

1.2
that

\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})\geq g-1
.
Thus it is enough to show that
\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})<g
.

The inspiration for the argument is the following.

Let

\iota:{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

be the natural product map

\iota([E],[A])=[E\times A]
, and let

\pi:{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{1}

be the projection onto the first factor.
Observe that
\iota
is proper and that
the fibers

\{[E]\}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}

of
\pi

have codimension
g
in

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
.

The essential but very simple remark is that,
if
Z\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
is a compact subvariety,
then the properness of
\iota
implies
that
\iota^{-1}(Z)
is compact
and so
\pi(\iota^{-1}(Z))
is a compact subset
of

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
,
which thus consists of finitely many points.

Assume now, for contradiction, that
Z\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
is a compact subvariety of dimension at least
g
.
If we could ensure that, for at least one
[E]\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
, the following property holds

(
\star
)
E

the intersection of
\pi^{-1}([E])=\{[E]\}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}
with
Z
is nonempty and has expected dimension
\dim Z-g\geq 0
,

then property (
\star
)
E
would hold for all
[E]
lying in a Zariski open subset of

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
.
This would mean that
\pi(\iota^{-1}(Z))
is a nonempty Zariski open
subset of

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
,
contradicting the above remark.

While (
\star
)
E
may not hold for any
[E]\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
as stated and without further assumptions on
Z
,
this idea essentially works for Hodge-generic subvarieties
Z
, once the loci of products
\{[E]\}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}
are replaced by their Hecke translates, i.e. by loci of ppav that are isogenous to such (i.e. by loci of abelian varieties that surject onto
E
).

An essential tool we will use for this is

Corollary

4.4
, in which we borrow an idea from

[
KU25
, Theorem 1.9(i)]
, and whose proof relies on the Ax-Schanuel conjecture, proven for

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

by
Pila and the fourth author
[
PT14
]
,
and proven for an arbitrary Shimura variety by Mok, Pila and the fourth author
[
MPT19
]
.
The details of the proof of
Theorem

A
can be found in
Section

4.2
.

1.6.
Structure of the proof of
Theorem

B

Here we describe the structure of the dimension
estimates needed to prove
Theorem

B
, which also exploits induction on
g
.

Let
Z\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
be an irreducible compact subvariety of largest dimension and let
S\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
be the smallest
special subvariety that contains
Z
(see
Theorem

2.14
).

We distinguish a few cases, according to the compactness
of
S
or its indecomposability (in the sense of
Definition

3.1
).

(o)

Suppose that
the tautological family of ppav over
S
is a product

of lower-dimensional ones.

Then
S
is the image
under the standard map

{\mathcal{A}}_{g^{\prime}}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-g^{\prime}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

of a special subvariety of some

{\mathcal{A}}_{g^{\prime}}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-g^{\prime}}
,
and so
\dim Z\leq\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g^{\prime}})+\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g-g^{\prime}})
can be bounded inductively on
g
.

(o’)

Suppose that
the tautological family of ppav over
S
is isogenous to a product

of lower-dimensional families.

Then, for some large
N
, the subvariety
S
is the image via a morphism

\Phi:{\mathcal{A}}_{g^{\prime}}(N)\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-g^{\prime}}(N)\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

of some special subvariety and the same estimates as in (o) apply.

(i)

Suppose that
S
is non-compact and
the universal family of ppav over
S
is not isogenous to a product.

Then we prove that
\dim Z\leq g-1

in
Theorem

4.5
(which uses Ax-Schanuel for
Shimura varieties associated to
non-compact special subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

).

Note that, if
S
is not compact,
then the above (o’) and (i)
imply that for some large
N
we have

Z=\Phi(S^{\prime}\times Z^{\prime\prime})
, where
S^{\prime}
is a compact special subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g^{\prime}}

and
Z^{\prime\prime}
is compact Hodge-generic
in a non-compact special subvariety
S^{\prime\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g-g^{\prime}}
.
Since
\dim Z^{\prime\prime}=g-g^{\prime}-1
by

Theorem

4.5
and by maximality, we can write an expression
for
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})
in terms
of the maximal dimension of
compact
special
subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g^{\prime}}

for all
g^{\prime}\leq g

(see
Section

4.3
).

Thus, in order to complete the proof,
we are only
left to determine the maximal dimension of a
compact special subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
,
assuming that this is at least
g-1
.
Note that cases (ii) and (iii) below are independent
of (o), (o’) and (i) above.

(ii)

Suppose that
S
is compact, and its associated
symplectic representation is decoupled

(in the sense of
Definition

6.1
). In this case
Z
must be equal to
S
.
In
Section

7
we determine those

S
of largest dimension (assuming that
this is at least
g-1
), and in particular we determine those
S

that achieve the optimal bound
\dim S=\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})
for
g=2
and
g\geq 16
.
Our analysis heavily relies on the fundamental works of Satake

[
Sat56
,
Sat67
]

recalled in
Section

6
.
Another important ingredient of our estimates
are the Hasse principles
recalled in
Section

5
.

(iii)

Suppose that
S
is compact and its associated
symplectic representation is not decoupled
.
In
Section

8
we show through
numerical estimates that the compact subvarieties
S
,
of dimension at least
g-1
, appearing in this case have strictly smaller dimension than
those that appear in the decoupled case (ii) above.

Finally, in
Theorem

8.9
we collect
the list of all maximal dimensional compact

2.1.
Algebraic groups

Let
\mathrm{G}
be an algebraic group over a number field
\mathbb{K}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
.
We will assume that
\mathrm{G}
is connected (in the Zariski topology),
which is equivalent to requiring that
\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{C}})
is connected in the classical topology.

Semisimple and simple groups.

The group
\mathrm{G}
is
semisimple
if every
connected solvable normal subgroup of
\mathrm{G}
is trivial; it is
simple
if it is semisimple and every connected normal subgroup of
\mathrm{G}
, different from
\mathrm{G}
itself, is trivial. Moreover,
\mathrm{G}
is
geometrically simple
if
\mathrm{G}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
is simple.

Reductive groups.

A group
\mathrm{G}
is
reductive
if
every connected unipotent normal subgroup of
G
is trivial.
The connected component of its center
\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{G})
that contains the identity is a torus,
and
\mathrm{G}
is semisimple if and only if
\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{G})
is finite.
The
adjoint group
is defined as
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}\coloneqq\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{G})

and the
derived subgroup

\mathrm{G}^{\operatorname{der}}
is the closed subgroup of
\mathrm{G}
generated by the commutators.
Then
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}
(resp.
\mathrm{G}^{\operatorname{der}}
) is semisimple
with trivial (resp. finite) center, and we say that a connected reductive group
\mathrm{G}
is
adjoint
if it is semisimple and
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}
.

Anisotropic groups.

A
split torus

\mathrm{T}
is a product of a number of copies of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. A reductive group
\mathrm{G}
is called
anisotropic
if

\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}
contains no positive-dimensional split tori.
It was shown in
[
BT65
, Corollary 8.5]
that a semisimple group over a number field
\mathbb{K}
is anisotropic if and only if it contains no nontrivial unipotent elements.
It follows that
\mathrm{G}
is anisotropic (as an algebraic
\mathbb{K}
-group) if and only if
\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbb{K}/{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathrm{G}
is (as an algebraic
{\mathbb{Q}}
-group).

Isogenies.

An
isogeny
is a surjective homomorphism of connected algebraic groups
\mathrm{G}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}
with finite kernel; the kernel is then necessarily contained in
\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{G}^{\prime})
.
An algebraic group
\mathrm{G}
is
simply connected
if every isogeny
\mathrm{G}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}
is an isomorphism.
Note that a reductive and simply connected algebraic group is necessarily semisimple.
For a reductive group
\mathrm{G}
, let
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}
denote the universal cover of
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}
, so that
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}
is an isogeny.

Simple factors.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be semisimple and let
\mathrm{G}_{(1)},\dots,\mathrm{G}_{(\ell)}
be all the simple closed normal
\mathbb{K}
-subgroups of
\mathrm{G}
. Then the product map
\mathrm{G}_{(1)}\times\dots\times\mathrm{G}_{(\ell)}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}
is an isogeny (see
[
Mil17a
, Theorem 22.121]
),
and we will call
\mathrm{G}_{(j)}
a
simple
\mathbb{K}
-factor
of
\mathrm{G}
.
If
\mathbb{K}={\mathbb{R}}
, then we will say that a real factor
\mathrm{G}_{(j)}
is
compact

if
\mathrm{G}_{(j)}({\mathbb{R}})
is.

Field of definition and splitting field.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be a semisimple group over
\mathbb{K}
.
The Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{C}}/\mathbb{K})
acts on
\mathrm{G}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
and permutes its simple
{\mathbb{C}}
-factors

\mathrm{G}^{\prime}_{i}
.
The
subgroup that sends
\mathrm{G}^{\prime}_{i}
to itself
can be identified with a finite extension
{\mathbb{L}}
of
\mathbb{K}
, called the
field of definition
of
\mathrm{G}^{\prime}_{i}
; indeed, there exists a
{\mathbb{L}}
-group
\mathrm{H}^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{G}^{\prime}_{i}=\mathrm{H}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
The
splitting field of
\mathrm{G}
is the subfield of
{\mathbb{C}}
generated by
the fields of definition of all the simple factors of
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Restriction of scalars.

Let
\mathrm{H}
is a geometrically simple group over a totally real number field
\mathbb{K}

and
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{H}_{\mathbb{K}/{\mathbb{Q}}}
is the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-group obtained from
\mathrm{H}
by
restriction of scalars
.
Then
\prod_{\sigma}\mathrm{H}_{\sigma,{\mathbb{R}}}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
is an
isomorphism,
where
\sigma
ranges over all embeddings
\mathbb{K}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathrm{H}_{\sigma,{\mathbb{R}}}\coloneqq\mathrm{H}\otimes_{\mathbb{K},\sigma}{\mathbb{R}}
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
\mathrm{G}
a real reductive group with
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}
having maximal compact subgroup
\mathrm{K}
, we define
d(\mathrm{G})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left(\dim\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}-\dim\mathrm{K}\right)
.

Identity component of real groups.

If
\mathrm{G}
is a reductive group, we denote by

\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}({\mathbb{R}})^{+}

the connected component (in the classical topology) of
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}({\mathbb{R}})
that contains the identity, and
by
\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{R}})^{+}
the preimage of
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}({\mathbb{R}})^{+}
under
\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{R}})\rightarrow\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{R}})^{\mathrm{ad}}
. Moreover,
we denote by
\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{Q}})^{+}
the intersection

\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{Q}})\cap\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{R}})^{+}
within
\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Galois conjugate representations.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be an algebraic group over
\mathbb{K}
and let

be a representation of
\mathrm{G}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
.
For every

Galois action and representations defined over

Let
\mathrm{G}

\mathbb{K}

.
Let also

2.2.
Shimura data

We follow
[
Mil17b
]
in our definitions of Shimura data and Shimura varieties.

We recall that the
Deligne torus
is the
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebraic group defined as

{\mathbb{S}}\coloneqq\operatorname{Res}_{{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{R}}}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,{\mathbb{C}}})
.
Note that
{\mathbb{S}}({\mathbb{R}})\cong{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}

and that

{\mathbb{S}}_{\mathbb{C}}\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m,{\mathbb{C}}}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m,{\mathbb{C}}}
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
Mil17b
, 4.4]
)
.

A
Shimura datum
is a pair
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})

where
\mathrm{G}
is a reductive algebraic group over
{\mathbb{Q}}
,
and
{\mathdutchcal{X}}
is a

\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{R}})
-conjugacy class
of homomorphisms of real algebraic groups
x:{\mathbb{S}}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}_{{\mathbb{R}}}

such that

(i)

the induced Hodge structure on the Lie algebra
has
weights

\{(-1,1),\ (0,0),\ (1,-1)\},

i.e. there is a direct sum decomposition
\mathfrak{g}_{{\mathbb{C}}}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{-}
of the Lie algebra of
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
, such that

\operatorname{Ad}_{x(z)}
acts trivially on
\mathfrak{s}
, as
\frac{z}{\bar{z}}
on
\mathfrak{t}^{+}

and as
\frac{\bar{z}}{z}
on
\mathfrak{t}^{-}
;

(ii)

the conjugation by
\theta\coloneqq u(i)
is a Cartan involution of
G^{\mathrm{ad}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
,
namely
\mathrm{G}^{\theta}\coloneqq\{g\in\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}({\mathbb{C}})\,|\,\theta\bar{g}\theta^{-1}=g\}
is compact;

(iii)

\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}
has no nontrivial simple
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factor
\mathrm{G}^{\prime}

with

\mathrm{G}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})

compact.

The Shimura datum
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
is said to be
adjoint
(resp.
semisimple
) if
\mathrm{G}
is.

A
morphism of Shimura data

(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})\rightarrow(\mathrm{G}^{\prime},{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\prime})

is a homomorphism
f:\mathrm{G}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}^{\prime}
of algebraic groups inducing a map
{\mathdutchcal{X}}\rightarrow{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\prime}
.
Such morphism is said to be an
embedding
if
f
is.

The
product
of two Shimura data
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}}),(\mathrm{G}^{\prime},{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\prime})

is the Shimura datum defined as
(\mathrm{G}\times\mathrm{G}^{\prime},{\mathdutchcal{X}}\times{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\prime})
, where
{\mathdutchcal{X}}\times{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\prime}
is naturally
identified with the

\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{R}})\times\mathrm{G}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
-conjugacy class
of
homomorphisms
(x,x^{\prime}):{\mathbb{S}}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathrm{G}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
with
x\in{\mathdutchcal{X}}
and
x^{\prime}\in{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\prime}
.

Remark 2.3
.

Given a Shimura datum
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
as in
Definition

2.2
,
one can define another Shimura datum
(\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}},{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\mathrm{ad}})
by composing the map
u:{\mathbb{S}}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
with the projection

\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathrm{ad}}
.
Then the natural map
{\mathdutchcal{X}}\rightarrow{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\mathrm{ad}}

is finite surjective, and
maps each component of
{\mathdutchcal{X}}
isomorphically onto a component of
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\mathrm{ad}}
.

If
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
is a Shimura datum, then

{\mathdutchcal{X}}
acquires the structure of
a disjoint union of finitely many Hermitian symmetric spaces,
that are pairwise conjugate to each other.

Definition 2.4
.

The
dimension
of a Shimura datum
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
is
\dim(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathdutchcal{X}}
.

Note that
\dim(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})=d(\mathrm{G})
, as introduced in
Definition

2.1
.

Shimura data and simple factors.

Let
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
be a Shimura datum and let
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}\subseteq{\mathdutchcal{X}}
be a connected component. Then
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}
is a connected Hermitian symmetric space,
and

\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}({\mathbb{R}})^{+}

acts via conjugation on
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}
transitively, isometrically, and holomorphically.
Pick a point
x\in{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}
, so that
the group

{\mathbb{S}}({\mathbb{R}})={\mathbb{C}}^{\times}

acts on
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}
via
\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathrm{G}}\circ x
, thus fixing
x
.
If

\theta=x(i)\in\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{R}})
,
then the compact subgroup

\mathrm{G}^{\theta}\cap\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}({\mathbb{R}})^{+}

is the identity component of the stabilizer of
x
inside

\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}({\mathbb{R}})^{+}
.

Recall that
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is the product of its simple closed normal subgroups.
Let

\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{c}
be the product of all compact simple factors of
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{\mathbb{R}}
, and
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{nc}=\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{1}\times\dots\times\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{k}
the product of all its non-compact
simple
factors. Note that we have natural factorizations

{\mathdutchcal{X}}={\mathdutchcal{X}}_{1}\times\dots\times{\mathdutchcal{X}}_{k}\hskip 34.56009pt\text{and}\hskip 34.56009pt{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}={\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}_{1}\times\dots\times{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}_{k}.

We will often use the following result, whose proof can also be found
in
[

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
be a Shimura datum, and
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{i}
an
{\mathbb{R}}
-simple

\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. Then
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{i}
is geometrically simple.
Hence the splitting field of
\mathrm{G}
is
a totally real number field.

Proof.

By contradiction,
suppose
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{i}
were not geometrically simple. Since
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{i,{\mathbb{C}}}
is adjoint and not simple, it can be written as a product of simple adjoint groups. Since
\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{R}})
acts transitively on the factors,
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{i,{\mathbb{C}}}\cong\mathrm{H}\times\overline{\mathrm{H}}=(\operatorname{Res}_{{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{R}}}\mathrm{H})_{\mathbb{C}}
, where
\mathrm{H}
is a complex simple group
and
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{i}\cong\operatorname{Res}_{{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{R}}}\mathrm{H}
.
Thus
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{i}
is non-compact. Moreover

\mathrm{G}_{i,{\mathbb{C}}}
has no Cartan involution by
Lemma

2.6
below.
This contradicts
Definition

2.2
(iii),
thus proving the first claim.
The second claim immediately follows.
∎

Lemma 2.6

(
[

Let
\mathrm{H}
be a complex group of positive dimension, and let
\mathrm{H}_{{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{R}}}
be the real group obtained from
\mathrm{H}
by the restriction of scalars.
Then there is no element
\gamma

in
\mathrm{H}_{{\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{R}}}({\mathbb{C}})

such that

\operatorname{Ad}_{\gamma}
is a Cartan involution.

The following well-known corollary will be essential for us:

.

Let
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})

with
\mathrm{G}

Then
\mathrm{G}\cong\mathrm{H}_{\mathbb{K}/{\mathbb{Q}}}

\mathbb{K}

Proof.

We can write
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{R}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{G}_{i}
, where each
\mathrm{G}_{i}

.
Let
\mathbb{K}
be the field of definition of

\mathrm{G}_{1}=\mathrm{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
for some
\mathbb{K}
-group
\mathrm{H}
.
It follows

that

Since

∎

and so
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{\mathbb{F}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{i,\mathbb{F}}
for any field
extension
\mathbb{F}/\mathbb{K}
.
Moreover, the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}/{\mathbb{Q}})
acts on
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{\mathbb{F}}
by permuting its simple factors,
meaning that
\varsigma(\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{i,\mathbb{F}})=\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{i^{\varsigma},\mathbb{F}}
.
Note that a permutation
i\mapsto i^{\varsigma}

is realized by an element
\varsigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}/{\mathbb{Q}})

if and only if the same permutation
is realized by some element of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{K}/{\mathbb{Q}})
.
In particular, observe that

(a)

condition (iii) in
Definition

2.2
implies that,
for each factor
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{i,\mathbb{F}}
,
there exists
\varsigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}/{\mathbb{Q}})

such that
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{i^{\varsigma},{\mathbb{R}}}
is non-compact;

(b)

if
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
is non-compact
and
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-simple, then all factors of
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{\mathbb{R}}
are conjugate to one another,
and there are no compact factors.

Analogous considerations hold replacing
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}
by its universal cover
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}
.

2.3.
Shimura varieties

We recall that the ring of
finite adèles

{\mathbb{A}}_{f}
is the subset of elements
a=(a_{\ell})
of
\prod_{\text{$\ell$ prime}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
such that
a_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}
for all but finitely many
\ell
’s. This can be equivalently defined as

{\mathbb{A}}_{f}=\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}
, where
\displaystyle\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\coloneqq\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longleftarrow\\
N\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{Z}}/N=\prod_{\text{$\ell$ prime}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
be a Shimura datum, and let
\mathrm{K}\subset\mathrm{G}_{{\mathbb{A}}_{f}}
be a compact open subgroup. There is a natural algebraic variety,
{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{K}({\mathdutchcal{X}},\mathrm{G})
,
defined over a number field
{\mathbb{E}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})\subset{\mathbb{C}}
,
whose complex points are

{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})\cong\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash{\mathdutchcal{X}}\times\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/\mathrm{K}.

We call
{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
a
Shimura variety
.
A
morphism of Shimura varieties

{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})\rightarrow{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{G}^{\prime},{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\prime})
is a morphism of Shimura data
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})\rightarrow(\mathrm{G}^{\prime},{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\prime})
that sends
\mathrm{K}\rightarrow\mathrm{K}^{\prime}
under the natural morphism
\mathrm{G}_{{\mathbb{A}}_{f}}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{A}}_{f}}
.

We will use the same symbol for a morphism of Shimura varieties
and for the induced morphism of complex varieties.

Remark 2.10
.

As in
[
Mil17b
, Lemma 5.13]
,
if
(r_{i})_{i\in I}
is a system of representatives in

\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})

of the finite quotient

\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/\mathrm{K}
,
and denoting
\Gamma_{i}\coloneqq\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{Q}})\cap(r_{i}\mathrm{K}r_{i}^{-1})
for each
i\in I
, then

{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})\cong\coprod_{i\in I}(\Gamma_{i}\backslash{\mathdutchcal{X}})\,.

Note that the Hermitian metric on
{\mathdutchcal{X}}
descends to

{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})
.
Moreover

\Gamma_{i}\subset\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{Q}})

is an
arithmetic subgroup
,
namely
\Gamma_{i}
is commensurable to

\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{Q}})\cap\operatorname{GL}_{N}({\mathbb{Z}})

for any
embedding
\mathrm{G}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{N}
.

As we are interested in compact Shimura varieties, we shall make use of the following group-theoretic characterization.
A proof can be found in
[
PRR94
, page 260, Theorem 5.5]
.

Lemma 2.11
.

The Shimura variety
{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})
is compact if and only if
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}
is anisotropic.

Motivated by this, we shall call a Shimura datum
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})

compact
if
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}
is anisotropic.

(

]

3.1.
Siegel modular variety

Here we review the quintessential Siegel modular variety from the Shimura perspective. See
[
Mil17b
, §6]
for more details on this section.

Let
V
be a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space of dimension
2g
, and let
\psi
be a symplectic form on
V
.
We denote
\operatorname{GSp}(\psi)
the general symplectic group,
whose set of
R
-points is

\operatorname{GSp}(\psi)(R)\coloneqq\left\{A\in\operatorname{GL}_{R}(V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}R)\ \Big|\begin{array}[]{l}\exists\chi(A)\in R^{\times}:\forall v,v^{\prime}\in V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}R\\
\psi(Av,Av^{\prime})=\chi(A)\cdot\psi(v,v^{\prime})\end{array}\right\}.

The map
A\mapsto\chi(A)
gives a
similitude character

\operatorname{GSp}(\psi)\rightarrow{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
, whose kernel is the symplectic group
\operatorname{Sp}(\psi)
, which is also the derived subgroup of
\operatorname{GSp}(\psi)
.

There is a natural Shimura datum
(\operatorname{GSp}(\psi),{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi))
defined as follows. For any
symplectic basis
{\mathcal{B}}_{\psi}=(e_{1},\dots,e_{g},f_{1},\dots,f_{g})
of
V
define
\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}}:{\mathbb{S}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}(\psi)_{\mathbb{R}}
so that

\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}}(a+bi)(e_{j})=ae_{j}+bf_{j},\quad\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}}(a+bi)(f_{j})=af_{j}-be_{j}.

The set of all such
\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}}
, as
{\mathcal{B}}
varies, constitutes
{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi)
.

Then
\mathscr{S}_{g}\coloneqq(\operatorname{GSp}(\psi),{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi))
is the
Siegel Shimura datum
of genus
g
. The name is justified by observing that
{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi)
can be
identified with
{\mathdutchcal{H}}^{+}_{g}\sqcup{\mathdutchcal{H}}^{-}_{g}
, where
{\mathdutchcal{H}}^{+}_{g}
is the usual Siegel upper half-space.

If
L\subset V
denotes an integral lattice that is self-dual with respect to
\psi
, and
\mathrm{K}\subset\mathrm{G}_{{\mathbb{A}}_{f}}
denotes the stabilizer of
L\otimes\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, then
{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\operatorname{GSp}(\psi),{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi)))

can be identified with the

moduli space
{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g}
of ppav
.
For any
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, if we denote
\mathrm{K}_{N}\subset\mathrm{K}
the subgroup of those elements that act trivially on
L/(N\cdot L)
,
then
{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}_{{}_{N}}}(\operatorname{GSp}(\psi),{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi))
can be identified with the

moduli space
{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g}(N)
of ppav with full level-
N
structure
.

Notation
.

In order to avoid too heavy notation, we use the symbol

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}\coloneqq{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g,{\mathbb{C}}}
to denote the complex variety associated to
{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g}
, namely
the
coarse moduli space of complex ppav of dimension
g
.
Similarly, we write
{\mathcal{A}}_{g}(N)\coloneqq{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Writing
(V,\psi)
as a direct sum
(V_{1},\psi_{1})\oplus(V_{2},\psi_{2})
of
{\mathbb{Q}}
-symplectic spaces of dimensions
2g_{1}
and
2g_{2}
, we obtain a group

\operatorname{GSp}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\subset\operatorname{GSp}(\psi_{1})\times\operatorname{GSp}(\psi_{2})

defined as the subgroup of pairs with the same similitude character. We endow
\operatorname{GSp}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
with a Shimura datum
{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
as follows.
Consider symplectic bases
{\mathcal{B}}_{k}=(e^{k}_{1},\dots,e^{k}_{g_{k}},f^{k}_{1},\dots,f^{k}_{g_{k}})
for
(V_{k},\psi_{k})

and let
{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
be the conjugacy class of the homomorphism

\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}_{1,2}}:{\mathbb{S}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})_{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}_{1,2}}(a+bi)(e^{k}_{j})=ae^{k}_{j}+bf^{k}_{j}
and
\mu_{{\mathcal{B}}_{1,2}}(a+bi)(f^{k}_{j})=af^{k}_{j}-be^{k}_{j}

for

.

Now there are two natural inclusions
\operatorname{GSp}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\subset\operatorname{GSp}(\psi_{1})\times\operatorname{GSp}(\psi_{2})

and
\operatorname{GSp}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\subset\operatorname{GSp}(\psi)
.
The former determines an embedding of the Shimura datum
\mathscr{S}_{g_{1},g_{2}}\coloneqq(\operatorname{GSp}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}),{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}))
into
\mathscr{S}_{g_{1}}\times\mathscr{S}_{g_{2}}
,
that identifies
{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
to two of the four components of
{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi_{1})\times{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi_{2})
,
and which descends to étale covers of Shimura varieties.
The latter determines an embedding
\operatorname{GSp}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GSp}(\psi)
.
If
L_{k}
is an integral
\psi_{k}
-self-dual lattice in
V_{k}
,
then
L=L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
is an integral
\psi
-self-dual lattice in
V

and the embedding
\operatorname{GSp}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GSp}(\psi)
induces
the standard map

\iota:{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g_{1}}\times{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g_{2}}\rightarrow{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g_{1}+g_{2}}
.

Definition 3.1
.

An embedding of Shimura data
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}_{g}
is called

if it does not factor through the standard morphism

\operatorname{GSp}
-representation

is
\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible (as defined in

From a geometric point of view,

The above considerations show that, for a decomposable special subvariety

there exist positive integers

\textstyle{{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

3.2.
Symplectic representations

Now we want to briefly discuss representations that are associated to morphisms of some Shimura datum to the Siegel Shimura datum.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be a connected reductive algebraic group over
\mathbb{K}
. An
\operatorname{Sp}
-representation

(V,\psi)
of
G

is a
\mathbb{K}
-representation
V
of
\mathrm{G}
equipped with a
non-degenerate symplectic pairing
\psi
preserved by the action of
\mathrm{G}
. An
\operatorname{Sp}
-subrepresentation
is a
\mathrm{G}
-invariant symplectic subspace.
An
\operatorname{Sp}
-subrepresentation is called
\operatorname{Sp}
-irreducible
if it has no nontrivial symplectic subrepresentations.

Likewise, a
\operatorname{GSp}
-representation
of
\mathrm{G}
is a representation
V
together with a non-degenerate symplectic pairing
\psi
that is preserved by
\mathrm{G}
up to scaling;
\operatorname{GSp}
-subrepresentations and
\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducibility are defined analogously.

Given a
\operatorname{GSp}
-representation
(V,\psi)
of
\mathrm{G}
, we obtain a
character
\chi
of
\mathrm{G}
such that
\psi(gv,gv^{\prime})=\chi(g)\psi(v,v^{\prime})

for all
v,w\in V
. The natural vector space isomorphism
V\rightarrow V^{\vee}
, induced by
\psi
, gives an isomorphism of
\mathrm{G}
-representations
V\rightarrow V^{\vee}\otimes\chi
. We call

V^{\vee}(\chi)\coloneqq V^{\vee}\otimes\chi
the
\operatorname{GSp}
-dual
representation.

The following lemma for
\operatorname{Sp}
-representations
was stated in
[
Mal43
, Theorem 3]
without proof,
and then
proven in
[
Sat65
, Section 2.3]
over
{\mathbb{R}}
.
For completeness, here we recall the argument which works over any field.

Lemma 3.3
.

Any
\operatorname{GSp}
-representation of
\mathrm{G}
is a direct sum of
\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible subrepresentations.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the dimension of the
\operatorname{GSp}
-representation
(V,\psi)
. The case
\dim V=0
is trivial.

Suppose
\dim V>0
.
It is enough to show that there exists a nontrivial

\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible subrepresentation
U
of
V
.
Indeed,
V
will then decompose as a direct sum
V=U\oplus U^{\perp}

of
\mathrm{G}
-invariant subspaces, and the conclusion follows
applying the inductive hypothesis to
U^{\perp}
.

Now, consider
an irreducible
\mathrm{G}
-invariant linear subspace
W
of
V
.
The pairing
\psi|_{W}
induces a
\mathrm{G}
-map
\beta:W\rightarrow W^{\vee}\otimes\chi
that must be either zero or an isomorphism, since
\mathrm{ker}(\beta)
is an invariant linear summand of
W
.

If
\beta
is an isomorphism, then
W
is a
\operatorname{GSp}
-subrepresentation,
hence
\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible, and so we are done.

Suppose now that
the restriction of
\psi

to every irreducible
\mathrm{G}
-invariant linear subspace of
V
is zero.
The pairing against
W
with
\psi
gives a map
\phi:V\rightarrow W^{\vee}(\chi)
.
Since
\psi
is non-degenerate, the map
\phi
is nonzero and thus surjective (by the irreducibility of
W^{\vee}
).
Since
\mathrm{G}
is reductive and
\ker(\phi)
is
\mathrm{G}
-invariant,
there exists an irreducible
\mathrm{G}
-invariant linear subspace

W^{\prime}
of
V
such that
V=\ker(\phi)\oplus W^{\prime}
.
Then the induced map
\psi^{\prime}:W^{\prime}\rightarrow W^{\vee}(\chi)
is
an isomorphism. Since
W
is isotropic for
\psi
, it follows that

W\cap W^{\prime}=\{0\}
and so
W\oplus W^{\prime}
is a
\operatorname{GSp}
-subrepresentation.
We conclude by observing that
W\oplus W^{\prime}

must be
\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible, as its only nontrivial
\mathrm{G}
-invariant subspaces are themselves irreducible, and by our assumption the restriction of
\psi
to them is zero.
∎

If
\rho
is an irreducible linear representation of
\mathrm{G}
,
the
isotypic component

V^{\rho}

is the subspace of
V
generated by all subrepresentations of
V

isomorphic to
\rho
. The decomposition
V=\bigoplus_{\rho}V^{\rho}

is canonical.

If
\rho
is symplectic, then
V^{\rho}
is a direct sum
of representations isomorphic to
\rho
by
Lemma

3.3
.
If
\rho
is not symplectic and
\rho^{\vee}\otimes\chi
is its
\operatorname{GSp}
-dual,
then
V^{\rho}\oplus V^{(\rho^{\vee}\otimes\chi)}
is symplectic
and so it is a direct sum of
\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible representations
isomorphic to
\rho\oplus(\rho^{\vee}\otimes\chi)
.
It follows that each
V^{\rho}
decomposes as a direct sum
of copies of
\rho
, though non-canonically.

The following simple observation
will be useful in

Section

7
and in
Section

8
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(V,\psi)
be
a
\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible
representation of a reductive group
G
.
Then all the irreducible subrepresentations of

V_{{\mathbb{C}}}

are either Galois conjugate to each other or to each other’s
\operatorname{GSp}
-duals. In particular, they all have the same dimension, and are all of the same type: orthogonal, or symplectic, or not self-dual (NSD).

Proof.

Let

U\subseteq V_{\mathbb{C}}
be an irreducible linear summand, and let
U^{\prime}\subseteq V_{\mathbb{C}}

denote the
subrepresentation generated by all
subspaces of
V_{\mathbb{C}}
that are isomorphic to a Galois conjugate of
U
or of its
\operatorname{GSp}
-dual
U^{\vee}(\chi)
. Then
U^{\prime}
is Galois stable, and thus

U^{\prime}=V^{\prime}_{\mathbb{C}}
for some rational subrepresentation
V^{\prime}
of
V
.
Moreover,

V
decomposes as
V^{\prime}\oplus V^{\prime\prime}
, with
V^{\prime\prime}
not having any of the irreducible linear summands that appear in
V^{\prime}

nor their
\operatorname{GSp}
-duals.
Thus
V^{\prime}
is symplectic. Since
V
is assumed to be
\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible, we must have
V=V^{\prime}
and the conclusion follows.
∎

We have seen that special subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

are associated
to embeddings of Shimura data
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
into
\mathscr{S}_{g}
.
These Shimura data, already studied in
[
Del79
]
, are called
of Hodge type
in

[
Mil90
, end of Section II.3]
.
This motivates the following definition.

Definition 3.5
.

A
symplectic representation
of a Shimura datum
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
is a
\operatorname{GSp}
-representation
(V,\psi)
of
\mathrm{G}

that induces a morphism
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})\rightarrow(\operatorname{GSp}(\psi),{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi))
of Shimura data. This morphism is called
irreducible
if
(V,\psi)
is
\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible as a representation of
\mathrm{G}
.

We will often invoke the following useful result
from
[
Del79
, Section 1.3.8]
(see also
[
Mil13
, Corollary 10.9]
),
which is essentially
a byproduct of the analysis carried out in
[
Sat56
, Section 2]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(V,\psi)
be a symplectic representation of a Shimura datum
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
, and let
U\subseteq V_{\mathbb{C}}
denote an irreducible complex-linear subrepresentation of
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{k}
denote all the simple non-compact
factors of
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then at most one of the
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{i,{\mathbb{C}}}
acts nontrivially on

.

3.3.
Products of Shimura data in
\mathscr{S}_{g}

Using the results recalled above, we now study embeddings into
\mathscr{S}_{g}

of Shimura data that geometrically factor as a product
of a non-compact Shimura datum and another Shimura datum. We show that these embeddings
factor in a suitable way.

We suspect that the following result might be already known, but we could not find
a reference in the literature. So we include a complete proof.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(\mathrm{G}_{(1)},{\mathdutchcal{X}}_{(1)})
be an adjoint Shimura datum with
\mathrm{G}_{(1)}
simple and such that
\mathrm{G}_{(1),{\mathbb{R}}}
has no compact factors, and let
(\mathrm{G}_{(2)},{\mathdutchcal{X}}_{(2)})
be any adjoint Shimura datum of positive dimension.
If
f:(\mathrm{H},{\mathdutchcal{X}}_{\mathrm{H}})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}_{g}
is an embedding of Shimura data, with
(\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{ad}},{\mathdutchcal{X}}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{ad}})\cong(\mathrm{G}_{(1)},{\mathdutchcal{X}}_{(1)})\times(\mathrm{G}_{(2)},{\mathdutchcal{X}}_{(2)})
, then
f
is decomposable.

Proof.

Let
(V,\psi)
be the
2g
-dimensional
\operatorname{GSp}
-representation of
\mathrm{H}

corresponding to the morphism
f
.
Consider the symplectic representation of

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1)}\times\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2)}
on
V
via

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1)}\times\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2)}\cong\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{\operatorname{der}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(V,\psi)
, and
canonically decompose
V
into a direct sum of isotypic components
for the action of
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}
.
We can then write

V=V_{\emptyset}\oplus V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{1,2}
, where

V_{\emptyset}
is the trivial isotypic component,

V_{1}
(resp.
V_{2}
) is the sum of isotypic components
on which
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1)}

(resp.
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2)}
)
acts nontrivially and
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2)}

(resp.
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1)}
)
acts trivially,
and
V_{1,2}
is the sum of isotypic components
on which both
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1)}
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2)}
act nontrivially.

Note that
the decomposition
V=V_{\emptyset}\oplus V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{1,2}
is orthogonal for
\psi

and
\mathrm{H}^{\operatorname{der}}
-invariant.
Since
\mathrm{H}^{\operatorname{der}}
and
\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{H})
generate
\mathrm{H}
,
the images of
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1)}
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2)}
in
\mathrm{H}
are both normal.
Hence both splittings
V=(V_{\emptyset}\oplus V_{2})\oplus(V_{1}\oplus V_{1,2})

and
V=(V_{\emptyset}\oplus V_{1})\oplus(V_{2}\oplus V_{1,2})
are preserved by
\mathrm{H}
.
It follows that each of
V_{\emptyset}
,
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
,
V_{1,2}
is a
\mathrm{H}
-subrepresentation.

We claim that
V_{1,2}=\{0\}
.

Assuming the claim,
we can write
V=V_{1}^{\prime}\oplus V_{2}
, where
V^{\prime}_{1}\coloneqq V_{\emptyset}\oplus V_{1}
. Setting
\psi_{1}\coloneqq\psi\mid_{V^{\prime}_{1}}
and
\psi_{2}\coloneqq\psi\mid_{V_{2}}
, we then obtain that
f
factors through
f^{\prime}:\mathrm{H}\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
, which gives the desired result.

In order to prove the claim, we proceed by contradiction and we consider
a nontrivial irreducible

(\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1),{\mathbb{C}}}\times\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2),{\mathbb{C}}})
-invariant subspace

U
of

(V_{1,2})_{{\mathbb{C}}}
.
Note that both
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1),{\mathbb{C}}}
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2),{\mathbb{C}}}

act nontrivially on
U
.
Hence
we can write
U\cong U_{1}\otimes U_{2}
,
where
U_{j}
is a nontrivial irreducible
representation of
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(j),{\mathbb{C}}}

for
j=1,2
.

Now let
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}_{2}
be a simple factor of
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2),{\mathbb{R}}}

such that
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}_{2,{\mathbb{C}}}
acts nontrivially on
U_{2}
.
By
Remark

2.8
(a), there exists
\varsigma\in\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
such that

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime\prime}_{2}\coloneqq\varsigma(\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}_{2})

is non-compact.
Let
\varsigma^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
be an element that sends

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}_{2,{\mathbb{C}}}
to
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime\prime}_{2,{\mathbb{C}}}
.
Up to replacing

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}_{2}
by
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime\prime}_{2}
and

U
by
\varsigma^{\prime}(U)
, we may and do assume that

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}_{2}
is non-compact.
Likewise, let
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}_{1}
be a simple
factor of

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1),{\mathbb{R}}}
such that

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}_{1,{\mathbb{C}}}
acts nontrivially on
U_{1}
.
By assumption

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}_{1}

is non-compact.

Thus,
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}_{1}
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}_{2}
are distinct, non-compact,
simple
{\mathbb{R}}
-factors of
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathbb{R}}
,
and both
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}_{1,{\mathbb{C}}}
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}^{\prime}_{2,{\mathbb{C}}}
act
nontrivially on
U
.
This contradicts
Lemma

3.6
, and so the claim is proven.
∎

4.
Ax-Schanuel for Shimura varieties,

and Hodge-generic compact subvarieties

In this section we recall the statement of the (weak) Ax-Schanuel conjecture for Shimura varieties, proven in
[
MPT19
]
.
As a consequence, we provide a compactness criterion for subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
,
borrowing an idea from

[
KU25
, Theorem 1.9(i)]
, and
use this criterion to prove a more general version of
Theorem

A
.
Finally, we will link the maximal dimension of a

to the

of compact special subvarieties
of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g^{\prime}}

for

.

4.1.
Ax-Schanuel for Shimura varieties, and a compactness criterion

Consider a Shimura variety
{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
and let
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}
be a connected component of
{\mathdutchcal{X}}
.
Denote by
\pi:{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}\rightarrow{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})
the natural projection,
and let
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}\subset\check{{\mathdutchcal{X}}}
be the embedding in the Hermitian symmetric space of compact type dual to
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}
, which is a projective variety (see, for instance,
[
Hel78
, Chapter VIII, Proposition 7.14]
).

Theorem 4.1

(Weak Ax-Schanuel
[
MPT19
]
)
.

Let
Y
be an algebraic subvariety of
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}
(namely, obtained by intersecting
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}
with an algebraic subvariety of
\check{{\mathdutchcal{X}}}
),
and let
Z\subseteq{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})
be an irreducible algebraic subvariety.
If
\pi^{-1}(Z)\cap Y
has an analytic irreducible component
C

of dimension larger than expected, then
\pi(C)
is contained in a weakly special subvariety of

{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})
,
of dimension smaller than

{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})

itself.

As a consequence of
Theorem

4.1
, one obtains
Theorem

4.2
below, which is essentially a simplified version of
[
KU25
, Theorem 1.9(i)]
, whose proof we include for completeness.

For a subvariety
Z^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})
, for any
\gamma\in\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{Q}})^{+}
the image
\pi(\gamma\cdot\pi^{-1}(Z^{\prime}))

is a subvariety of

{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})

called the
\gamma
-translate of
Y
.
We remark that, even if
Z^{\prime}
is irreducible, its
\gamma
-translate need not be.
However, an irreducible component of the
\gamma
-translate of a (weakly) special subvariety is again (weakly) special.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
Z\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
be an irreducible subvariety, such that the adjoint Mumford-Tate group
of its very general point
\mathrm{MT}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{Z}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-simple,
and let
S\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
be the smallest special subvariety containing
Z
.

If
S^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
is
a special subvariety such that

(a)

\dim Z+\dim S^{\prime}\geq\dim{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
, and

(b)

S^{\prime}\cap S\neq\emptyset
,

then there exists

\gamma\in\operatorname{GSp}(\psi)({\mathbb{Q}})^{+}
such that
the intersection of
Z
with the
\gamma
-translate
of
S^{\prime}
inside
{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
has a component
of the expected dimension.

Remark 4.3
.

A simple observation that is regularly used in the below arguments is the following. Let
M
and
N
be closed complex-analytic subvarieties of an ambient complex variety
X
. Then if
M\cap N
has a (nonempty) component
C
of the expected dimension, then
M\cap N_{t}
has a (nonempty) component of the expected dimension for any small perturbation
(N_{t})
of
N=N_{0}
inside
X
.
Indeed, we can first localize to a small ball
B\subset X
centered at a point
p
of
C
.
Then we can reduce to the case of
M,N
of complementary dimensions in
X
, by
intersecting both
M
and
N
by
\dim(C)
general hypersurfaces of
B
through
p
.
Namely, we can assume that
\dim(M)+\dim(N)=\dim(X)
, and that
B\cap(M\cap N)=\{p\}
.
Up to shrinking
B
a little, we can also assume that
\partial B\cap(M\cap N_{t})=\emptyset

for small
t
.
The above claim then follows from the upper semi-continuity of the dimension
t\mapsto\dim(B\cap M)\cap N_{t}
, and from the positivity and constancy of the local intersection number
t\mapsto\mathrm{deg}\,(B\cap M)\cdot N_{t}
.

Proof of
Theorem

4.2
.

Let
S
correspond to the
embedding
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})\hookrightarrow(\operatorname{GSp}(\psi),{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi))
of Shimura data.
By
Theorem

2.14
it follows that
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}\cong\mathrm{MT}_{Z}^{\mathrm{ad}}
and is therefore
{\mathbb{Q}}
-simple.

Now we obtain a natural map
\pi:{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}\rightarrow S\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
. Fix a point
z_{0}\in Z
that is
Hodge-generic in
S
, choose
\tilde{z}_{0}\in\pi^{-1}(z_{0})
, and let
\gamma_{0}\in\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{R}})^{+}
be such that
\tilde{z}_{0}\in\gamma_{0}\cdot\pi^{-1}(S\cap S^{\prime})
.

By (a), we have that

\dim Z+\dim(S^{\prime}\cap S)\geq\dim Z+\dim S^{\prime}+\dim S-\dim{\mathcal{A}}_{g}\geq\dim S.

Moreover, since
z_{0}
is Hodge-generic in
S
, by
Theorem

2.14
and
Theorem

2.13
there are no weakly special subvarieties of
S
containing
z_{0}
, other than
\{z_{0}\}
or
S
itself. Thus, it follows from
Theorem

4.1
that

\pi^{-1}(Z)\cap(\gamma_{0}\cdot\pi^{-1}(S\cap S^{\prime}))

has an analytic irreducible component of the expected dimension, containing
\tilde{z}_{0}
.

By
Remark

4.3
,
for any
\gamma\in\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{R}})^{+}
sufficiently close to
\gamma_{0}
, there will also exist an analytic irreducible component

C_{\gamma}

of
\pi^{-1}(Z)\cap(\gamma\cdot\pi^{-1}(S\cap S^{\prime}))
that still has the expected (non-negative) dimension.
In particular, since

\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{Q}})^{+}

is dense in

\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{R}})^{+}

for the classical topology,
this property holds for any element of

\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{Q}})^{+}

sufficiently close to
\gamma_{0}
.
We fix one such
\gamma
in

\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{Q}})^{+}
.
Regarding
\gamma
as an element of

\operatorname{GSp}(\psi)({\mathbb{Q}})
,
it is then enough to consider the
\gamma
-translate of
S^{\prime}
inside

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
,
since a component of its intersection
with
Z
is exactly
\pi(C_{\gamma})
.
∎

We would like to extend
Theorem

4.2
to the intersection of
Z
with a weakly special subvariety.
The following
Corollary

4.4
, which can also be understood as a non-compactness criterion for
Z
,
will play a key role in the proof of
Theorem

4.5
.

In order to explain the idea behind
Corollary

4.4
below with an example, consider the special subvariety
S^{\prime}\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
obtained as the image of the natural map

\iota:{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
.
The image
\iota([E]\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1})
is closed of codimension
g
for all
[E]\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
;
moreover, it limits to
\partial{\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{*}
as
[E]
approaches the boundary point of

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
.
Now, for the sake of contradiction, consider a compact subvariety
Z\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
of dimension at least
g
.
If the intersection of
Z
and
\iota([E]\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1})
is nonempty and
has a component of the expected dimension, such property is preserved under small perturbations of
[E]
(see
Remark

4.3
). Thus, by algebraicity,
Z
has nonempty intersection with
\iota([E]\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1})
for all but possibly finitely many
[E]
, which shows that
Z
is not compact. In order to achieve non-emptiness
and expected dimension of the intersection,
we use
\gamma
-translates and
Theorem

4.1
,
as in the proof of
Theorem

4.2
, and deal with a general finite map
\tau^{\prime}:S_{1}^{\prime}\times S_{2}^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}
instead of the standard
\iota
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
Z
and
S
be as in
Theorem

4.2
.
Assume that
S^{\prime}
is a special subvariety
corresponding to
(\mathrm{H},{\mathdutchcal{Y}})\hookrightarrow(\operatorname{GSp}(\psi),{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi))
and that
(\mathrm{H}^{\mathrm{ad}},{\mathdutchcal{Y}}^{\mathrm{ad}})\cong(\mathrm{H}_{1},{\mathdutchcal{Y}}_{1})\times(\mathrm{H}_{2},{\mathdutchcal{Y}}_{2})
, so that
the natural morphism
{\mathdutchcal{Y}}_{1}^{+}\times{\mathdutchcal{Y}}_{2}^{+}\rightarrow S^{\prime}
then factors through a finite map
\tau^{\prime}:S^{\prime}_{1}\times S^{\prime}_{2}\rightarrow S^{\prime}
.

Assume moreover that

(a)

\dim Z+\dim S^{\prime}_{2}\geq\dim{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
, and

(b)

\tau^{\prime}(\{s^{\prime}_{1}\}\times S^{\prime}_{2})\cap S\neq\emptyset
for a general

.

Then

(i)

there exists
\gamma\in\mathrm{G}({\mathbb{Q}})^{+}
such that
the
\gamma
-translate of

inside

intersects

(ii)

if

Proof.

(i) Fix any

works identically to show that there exists

-translate of

.

(ii) Let

∎

4.2.
Hodge-generic compact subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

Given an irreducible subvariety
Z\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
, by definition

is Hodge-generic within the smallest special subvariety
S

of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

containing

.
We recall that indecomposability for

was introduced in

Definition

.

In this section we prove the following.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
S\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
be an indecomposable, non-compact, special subvariety, and let
Z\subsetneq S
be an irreducible compact subvariety that is Hodge-generic within

. Then
\dim Z\leq g-1
.

The first main result of the present paper is an immediate consequence.

Proof of
Theorem

A
.

This is simply the
S={\mathcal{A}}_{g}
case of
Theorem

4.5
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

In fact, the proof of
Theorem

4.5
in the special case

is very quick, and does not require any of the below technical
Lemma

4.7
,
Corollary

4.8
or

4.9
. Indeed, it implements the idea discussed in
Section

1.5
, making use of
Corollary

4.4
(ii)
with
S^{\prime}_{1}={\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
S^{\prime}_{2}={\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}

and
\tau^{\prime}=\iota:{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}\rightarrow\iota({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1})\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
.

The idea of the proof of
Theorem

4.5
, for arbitrary non-compact

, is to first
produce a special subvariety of
{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
as the image of
a morphism
\Phi:S^{\prime}_{1}\times S^{\prime}_{2}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
with

S^{\prime}_{1}={\mathcal{A}}_{1}(N)
and
S^{\prime}_{2}={\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}(N)

for some

(where we recall that

denotes the coarse moduli of complex ppav with a full level

in such a way that

intersects

for

in a Zariski-dense open subset of

.
By

(ii), the compactness of

then forces

.

The construction of
\Phi
uses the following preliminary lemma, in which
we first exhibit a holomorphic map from a modular curve to a totally geodesic subvariety of
S
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})^{+}
be a connected component of the Shimura variety

{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
and suppose that
{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})
is non-compact.
Then there is a homomorphism of
{\mathbb{Q}}
-groups
\phi:\operatorname{SL}_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}
and a point
x\in{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
such that
x\mapsto\phi(g)\cdot x
induces a holomorphic map of the modular curve (with level)
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}(N)\rightarrow{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
, for some large enough

, whose image is totally geodesic.

Proof.

The adjoint action of
\mathrm{G}
on its Lie algebra
determines a canonical variation of Hodge structure on

{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})

(see
[
Mil13
, §5]
).
Since

{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})

is non-compact, the group
\mathrm{G}
contains nontrivial unipotent elements, and the variation associated to

{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})

degenerates. The conclusion now follows from
[
Sch73
, Cor 5.19]
. (Technically, Schmid works only for period domains corresponding to the full orthogonal/symplectic group, but the argument goes through unchanged in our context).
∎

We can now construct a morphism of Shimura varieties from a refined version of the lemma above:

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})^{+}
be a
connected component of the Shimura variety
{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
,
and suppose that

{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})

is non-compact.
Then there is an embedding of Shimura data
(\mathrm{H}^{\prime},{\mathdutchcal{Y}}^{\prime})\hookrightarrow(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})

and a connected component
({\mathdutchcal{Y}}^{\prime})^{+}
of
{\mathdutchcal{Y}}^{\prime}

such that

{\mathdutchcal{Sh}}_{\mathrm{K}}(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})({\mathbb{C}})^{+}

Proof.

By

We can now construct the desired morphism

Lemma 4.9

Let

be a non-compact special subvariety. Then there is a morphism of Shimura varieties
\Phi:{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{1}(N)\times{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g-1}(N)\rightarrow{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g}
, and an irreducible component

[E]\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1}(N)^{+}

Proof.

Let
\iota:(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}_{g}=(\operatorname{GSp}(\psi),{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi))
be the embedding of Shimura data, associated to a representation of
\mathrm{G}
on the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-symplectic space
(V,\psi)
, and let
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}
be a connected component
of
{\mathdutchcal{X}}
,
that induce
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}\twoheadrightarrow S\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
.
Denote by
({\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\mathrm{ad}})^{+}
the component of
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\mathrm{ad}}
which is the image of
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}
.

By
Corollary

4.8
there is an embedding of Shimura data
(\mathrm{H}^{\prime},{\mathdutchcal{Y}}^{\prime})\hookrightarrow(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})

and a component
({\mathdutchcal{Y}}^{\prime})^{+}
of
{\mathdutchcal{Y}}^{\prime}

so that

(\mathrm{H}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{ad}}\cong\operatorname{SL}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{2}

and
({\mathdutchcal{Y}}^{\prime})^{+}
maps to
{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{+}
. This gives a smaller non-compact special subvariety in

, and hence we may and do reduce to the case

(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})=(\mathrm{H}^{\prime},{\mathdutchcal{Y}}^{\prime})
and so
\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}\cong\operatorname{SL}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{2}

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
the smallest
closed normal
{\mathbb{Q}}
-subgroup of
\mathrm{G}

whose set of real points contains the image of each
x\in{\mathdutchcal{X}}
is
\mathrm{G}
itself (otherwise, we could replace

by such closed normal subgroup, which is also reductive).

Let
\mathrm{T}=\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{G})^{\circ}
be the connected
component of
\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{G})
that contains the identity.
We therefore have an isogeny
f:\operatorname{SL}_{2}\times\mathrm{T}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}
.

Let
j:\mathrm{U}_{1}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2,{\mathbb{R}}}
be the embedding given by the standard identification

\mathrm{U}_{1}\cong\operatorname{SO}_{2,{\mathbb{R}}}
, and note that the composition

\mathrm{U}_{1}\xrightarrow{j}\operatorname{SL}_{2,{\mathbb{R}}}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2,{\mathbb{R}}}\times\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{R}}\xrightarrow{f}\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2,{\mathbb{R}}}^{\mathrm{ad}}

agrees with the restriction of an element

x^{\mathrm{ad}}:{\mathbb{S}}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{\mathbb{R}}
of

({\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\mathrm{ad}})^{+}
.

From here on in the proof, we always consider
\mathrm{G}
as acting on
V_{\mathbb{C}}
via
\iota
and
\operatorname{SL}_{2,{\mathbb{R}}}\times\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{R}}
as
acting on
V_{\mathbb{C}}
via
\iota\circ f
.
In what follows we study the weights of certain actions of

on

.

Step 1:
\mathrm{U}_{1}
acts on
V_{\mathbb{C}}
via
\iota\circ f\circ j
with weights in
\{0,\pm 1\}
.

To prove this, consider any element

that projects to
x^{\mathrm{ad}}\in({\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\mathrm{ad}})^{+}
.
Let

be an integer such that

lifts to a map
\widehat{x\circ m_{d}}:{\mathbb{S}}\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2,{\mathbb{R}}}\times\mathrm{T}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
(where
m_{d}:\mathrm{U}_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{U}_{1}
is the
d
’th power map). Denote by

\nu:{\mathbb{S}}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{R}}

\widehat{x\circ m_{d}}
to the
second factor, and by

\xi_{d}=(j\circ m_{d},\nu_{1}):\mathrm{U}_{1}\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2,{\mathbb{R}}}\times\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{R}}
the restriction of
\widehat{x\circ m_{d}}
to
\mathrm{U}_{1}
. Note that
\nu
and
\nu_{1}
are independent of

We know, by inspecting a single element of
{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi)
,
that the restriction to
\mathrm{U}_{1}
of any element

{\mathbb{S}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}(\psi)

of
{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi)
acts on

with weights
\{\pm 1\}
.
Since the composition
\iota\circ f\circ\xi_{d}

is the restriction to
\mathrm{U}_{1}
of
the
d
’th power of an element in
{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi)
,
the action via
\xi_{d}
has weights
\{\pm d\}
.

Now, the action
\xi_{-d}=(j\circ m_{-d},\nu_{1})

on

is conjugate to

, and so it
also has weights
\{\pm d\}
.
(This follows from the fact that

\operatorname{Ad}_{J}\circ j=j\circ m_{-1}:\mathrm{U}_{1}\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2,{\mathbb{C}}}

where

\operatorname{Ad}_{\iota(f(J))(x|_{\mathrm{U}_{1}}}\circ\,m_{d})=x|_{\mathrm{U}_{1}}\circ m_{-d}

The homomorphisms

and

Thus the weights of

the sum of the weights of the action
\xi_{d}
and
\xi_{-d}
.
But now
\xi_{d}\cdot(\xi_{-d})^{-1}=(j\circ m_{2d},0)
,
and so
\mathrm{U}_{1}
acts via
\iota\circ f\circ j

with weights in
\{0,\pm 1\}
, as desired.

Step 2: Decomposing
V
as an
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
-module.

Denoting
V_{1}
the standard two-dimensional representation of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
, recall that all irreducible representations of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
are of the form
V_{\ell}\coloneqq\operatorname{Sym}^{\ell}V_{1}
for some integer
\ell\geq 0

\mathrm{U}_{1}
acting on

contains
\ell
, we conclude that, as a representation of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
acting via
\iota\circ f
,
an irreducible subrepresentation of

must be isomorphic either to
V_{0}
or to
V_{1}
.

We may therefore write
V=V^{\prime}\oplus V^{\prime\prime}
as a direct sum of
{\mathbb{Q}}
-representations, where

(non-canonically) decomposes as a
\psi
-orthogonal sum
V^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}V^{\prime}_{i}

with

\operatorname{SL}_{2}

and

is a trivial representation. Note that

\operatorname{SL}_{2}\xrightarrow{f}\mathrm{G}\xrightarrow{\iota}\operatorname{GSp}(\psi)

are both symplectic under
\psi
and

\psi
-orthogonal to each other.

Step 3:
\mathrm{T}
acts on
V^{\prime}
via scalars.

First, since
\mathrm{T}
commutes with
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
, it must preserve
V^{\prime}
as an
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
-isotypic space.
Next, in Step 2 we showed that the weights of

\xi_{d}
and of
\xi_{-d}
are precisely
\{\pm d\}
,
and that the weights of

are contained in
\{0,\pm d\}
.

Next, we want to show that
\nu_{1}
acts on

with weight
0
. Let
k
be a weight for the action of
\nu_{1}
on
V^{\prime}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Since
\nu_{1}
and
j\circ m_{d}
commute, we can
find
v\in V^{\prime}_{\mathbb{C}}
that is an eigenvector
for
j\circ m_{d}
of weight
d
,
and for
\nu_{1}
of weight
k
.
Then
\xi_{d}
and
\xi_{-d}
act on
{\mathbb{C}}v
with weights

and

acts trivially on

.

Moreover, by examining
{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi)
we see that
\widehat{x\circ m_{d}}({\mathbb{G}}_{m,{\mathbb{R}}})

and hence also on

Since we assumed that

does not factor through a

other than

itself, we conclude that

is

in

\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{R}}
acts on

,

Step 4: construction of the morphism

.

Finally, let
U=V^{\prime}_{1}
and
W=V^{\prime\prime}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i=2}^{m}V_{i}^{\prime}\right)
.
By Step 2 and Step 3 above,
\operatorname{SL}_{2}\times\mathrm{T}
acts on

preserving the mutually orthogonal subspaces

.
It follows that

\mathrm{G}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GSp}(\psi)
factors as

and hence

(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})\hookrightarrow(\operatorname{GSp}(\psi|_{U},\psi|_{W}),{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi|_{U},\psi|_{W}))\hookrightarrow(\operatorname{GSp}(\psi),{\mathdutchcal{X}}(\psi)).

This map of Shimura data yields a map of Shimura varieties

We are now ready to prove the theorem, implementing the original idea.

Proof of
Theorem

.

Assume, for contradiction, that

is a compact Hodge-generic subvariety of

, of dimension at least

.

Consider the morphism
\Phi:{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{1}(N)\times{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g-1}(N)\rightarrow{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g}

and the component

constructed in

4.3.
Compact special subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
determine
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})

Denote by
\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g)
the
maximal dimension of a compact special subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
.
Since

{\mathcal{A}}_{0}

is a point, we have
\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(0)=0
.
Moreover, if

S_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g_{1}}
and
S_{2}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g_{2}}

are compact

then
S_{1}\times S_{2}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g_{1}}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g_{2}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{g_{1}+g_{2}}

maps to a compact special subvariety of dimension

and so

\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g_{1}+g_{2})\geq\dim S_{1}+\dim S_{2}=\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g_{1})+\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g_{2}).

Combining
Theorem

3.7
and
Theorem

4.5

leads to the following exact expression for the maximal dimension of a compact subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
.

Proposition 4.10
.

The maximal dimension of a compact subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

is

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})=\max\left(\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g),\max_{0\leq g^{\prime}<g}\left(g-g^{\prime}-1+\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g^{\prime})\right)\right).

Proof.

For
g=0
we have

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})=0
.
Assume now
g\geq 1
.

For every
g^{\prime}\in[0,g-1]

let
S_{g^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g^{\prime}}
be a compact special subvariety of maximal dimension, that is
\dim S_{g^{\prime}}=\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g^{\prime})
,
and let
Z_{g-g^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g-g^{\prime}}
a compact subvariety of dimension
g-g^{\prime}-1
(for example, a complete intersection). Then
S_{g}
and

S_{g^{\prime}}\times Z_{g-g^{\prime}}
for
g^{\prime}=0,\dots,g-1

are compact subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
.
Thus
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})\geq\max\left(\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g),\max_{0\leq g^{\prime}<g}\left(g-g^{\prime}-1+\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g^{\prime})\right)\right)
.
We have now to show that equality holds.

Let
Z\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
be an irreducible compact subvariety and let
S\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
be the smallest special subvariety containing
Z
. Since

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})\geq\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})=g-1
,
it is enough to deal with the case
\dim Z\geq g
, in which case from
Theorem

4.5
it follows that
S\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
.

If
S
is compact, then
\dim Z\leq\dim S\leq\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g)
and the theorem holds.

So assume from now on that
S
is not compact and let
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}_{g}
be the embedding
of Shimura data associated to
S
.
It follows that
(\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}},{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\mathrm{ad}})
has a non-compact factor
(\mathrm{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{1},{\mathdutchcal{X}}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{1})
. Then we can apply
Theorem

3.7
to conclude that
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}_{g}
must factor through
\mathscr{S}_{g_{1},g_{2}}
for some positive integers

.
As a consequence,

is the image of a compact subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g_{1}}(N)\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g_{2}}(N)

via a morphism

{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g_{1}}(N)\times{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g_{2}}(N)\rightarrow{\mathdutchcal{A}}_{g}

for some

, which is a Hecke translate of the standard map.

By induction we see that

\displaystyle\dim Z

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g_{1}})+\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g_{2}})

\displaystyle\leq\max\!\Big(\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g_{1})+\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g_{2}),\max_{g_{1}^{\prime}<g_{1}}(g_{1}-g^{\prime}_{1}-1+\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g^{\prime}_{1})+\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g_{2})),

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\max\Big(}\max_{g_{2}^{\prime}<g_{2}}(g_{2}-g^{\prime}_{2}-1+\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g_{1})+\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g^{\prime}_{2})),

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\max\Big(}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}g^{\prime}_{1}<g_{1},\ g_{2}^{\prime}<g_{2}\end{subarray}}(g-g^{\prime}_{2}-g^{\prime}_{1}-2+\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g^{\prime}_{1})+\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g^{\prime}_{2})\ \Big)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left(\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g),\max_{0\leq g^{\prime}<g}(\mathrm{mdc_{sp}}(g^{\prime})+g-g^{\prime}-1)\right)\,,

as desired.
∎

5.
Compact factors in compact Shimura data

Let
\mathrm{G}=\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbb{K}/{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathrm{H}
be a simple adjoint
{\mathbb{Q}}
-group,
where
\mathrm{H}
is a geometrically simple group over a totally real field
\mathbb{K}
.

Note that the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-group
\mathrm{G}
is anisotropic if and only if the
\mathbb{K}
-group
\mathrm{H}
is anisotropic,
and recall that
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\prod_{\sigma}\mathrm{H}_{\sigma,{\mathbb{R}}}
, where
\sigma

ranges over all embeddings
\sigma:\mathbb{K}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
.

Fact 5.1
.

If
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
has a compact factor
\mathrm{H}_{\sigma,{\mathbb{R}}}
for some
\sigma
,
then
\mathrm{G}
is anisotropic.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{H}_{\sigma,{\mathbb{R}}}
is compact, it contains no nontrivial unipotent elements.
The same then holds for
\mathrm{H}
, which is thus anisotropic, and so is
\mathrm{G}
.
∎

In this section we study the following:

Question 5.2
.

Suppose that the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-group
\mathrm{G}
is anisotropic. Must
\mathrm{H}_{\sigma,{\mathbb{R}}}

be compact for some embedding
\sigma:\mathbb{K}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
?

We shall restrict our analysis to
the
\mathbb{K}
-groups

\mathrm{H}=\operatorname{Aut}(V,\eta)^{\mathrm{ad}}
, where
V
is a (finitely generated) left module for a division algebra
{\mathbb{D}}
with center
{\mathbb{L}}

endowed with an involution
*

(satisfying
(\alpha\beta)^{*}=\beta^{*}\alpha^{*}
),
such that
\mathbb{K}
is the fixed field in
{\mathbb{L}}
,
and
\eta
is a non-degenerate form on
V

that is
\epsilon
-Hermitian with respect to
*
(where
\epsilon=\pm 1
).

We say that
\eta
represents
0
(or, equivalently,
that
\eta
is isotropic
) if
there exists
v\in V\setminus\{0\}
such that
\eta(v,v)=0
.

Remark 5.3
.

As a motivation for
Question

5.2
, we anticipate that
in
Section

7
we will begin
to investigate the dimensions of compact special subvarieties
S
of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
.
Each of these
S
will be induced by a symplectic representation
of a reductive
{\mathbb{Q}}
-group. The basic, but most important case,
is when their adjoint groups are exactly those
\mathrm{G}

mentioned above.
To have a sharp estimate for the dimension of the special subvariety
S

we must know whether a factor
\mathrm{H}_{\sigma,{\mathbb{R}}}
must necessarily be compact.

We start with the following lemma which is certainly well-known, but we gather it for convenience of the reader:

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathrm{H}=\operatorname{Aut}(V,\eta)^{\mathrm{ad}}
be a
\mathbb{K}
-group as above,
and assume that
\mathrm{H}
is geometrically simple
(and in particular nontrivial). Then
\mathrm{H}
is isotropic if and only if
\eta
represents
0
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\mathrm{H}
is isotropic, so that there is a nontrivial unipotent element
h\in\mathrm{H}(\mathbb{K})
.
Since
h
is unipotent, there exist two linearly independent vectors
v,w\in V
such that
h(v)=v
and
h(w)=w+v
. But then

0=\eta(h(w),h(v))-\eta(w,v)=\eta(v,v)
, and so
\eta
represents
0
as desired.

Now suppose that
\eta
represents
0
. Then there exists a nonzero vector
v\in V
such that
\eta(v,v)=0
. Let
U={\mathbb{D}}v
, pick an element
w^{\prime}\not\in U^{\perp}
, and by scaling assume that
\eta(w^{\prime},v)=1
. Let
w=w^{\prime}+dv
with
d=-\eta(w^{\prime},w^{\prime})/2\in{\mathbb{D}}
, so that still
\eta(w,v)=1
.
Noting that
d=\epsilon d^{*}
, we have
\eta(w,w)=\eta(w^{\prime},w^{\prime})+d+\epsilon d^{*}=0
.
Then since
\eta
is non-degenerate on
W\coloneqq\mathrm{Span}(v,w)
,
it follows that
V\cong W\oplus W^{\perp}
is an orthogonal decomposition.

Now we obtain a split

\mathbb{K}
-torus

\mathrm{T}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(V,\eta)
by considering
the automorphisms of
V
that
rescale
w,v
by
t,t^{-1}
and leave
W^{\perp}
fixed.
If
\mathrm{T}
is not contained in the center of
\operatorname{Aut}(V,\eta)
, then

\mathrm{H}=\operatorname{Aut}(V,\eta)^{\mathrm{ad}}
contains a nontrivial split torus and so is isotropic.

Suppose now that
\mathrm{T}
is contained in the center of
\operatorname{Aut}(V,\eta)
.
Then every element of
\operatorname{Aut}(V,\eta)
must preserve
{\mathbb{D}}v
and
{\mathbb{D}}w
, and likewise any isotropic subspace.
We claim that
W^{\perp}=\{0\}
.
Indeed, for any
r\in W^{\perp}

and for
d=-\eta(r,r)/2\in{\mathbb{D}}

the vector
w+r+dv
is isotropic, and so each element of
\operatorname{Aut}(V,\eta)
must preserve
{\mathbb{D}}(w+r+dv)
. But our torus
\mathrm{T}
preserves
{\mathbb{D}}(w+r+dv)
only if
r=0
.

Hence
V\cong{\mathbb{D}}^{2}
, and our form is equivalent to
\eta((x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2}))=x_{1}\alpha y_{2}^{*}+\epsilon y_{1}\alpha^{*}x_{2}^{*}
for some
0\neq\alpha\in{\mathbb{D}}
. Now
(x,1)
is isotropic whenever
(x\alpha)=-\epsilon(x\alpha)^{*}
.
Since
{\mathbb{D}}(x,1)
is not preserved by our torus
\mathrm{T}
for any
x\neq 0
, the vector
(x,1)
cannot be isotropic, and so the equation
(x\alpha)=-\epsilon(x\alpha)^{*}
has no nonzero solutions.
This
means that
\epsilon=1
and
*
must be trivial. But in this case we have
{\mathbb{D}}=\mathbb{K}
and
\mathrm{H}\cong\mathrm{O}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{2,\mathbb{K}}
is the trivial group. The proof is finished.
∎

Definition 7.1

d_{\mathrm{max}}(g)\coloneqq\max\left(g-1,\left\lfloor\frac{\lfloor g/2\rfloor^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor\right)=\begin{cases}\ \ g-1&\mbox{if }g<16\,;\\
\ \ \left\lfloor\tfrac{g^{2}}{16}\right\rfloor&\mbox{for even }g\geq 16\,;\\
\left\lfloor\tfrac{(g-1)^{2}}{16}\right\rfloor&\mbox{for odd }g\geq 17\,.\end{cases}

First, we have some elementary numerical
property of the function
d_{\mathrm{max}}
.

Lemma 7.9
.

For all
1\leq g_{1}\leq g_{2}
we have

d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{1}+g_{2})\geq d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{1})+d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{2}).

Moreover, the above inequality is strict unless
g_{1}=1
and
g_{2}\geq 16
is even.

Proof.

Suppose first that
g_{1}=1
.
Then
d_{\mathrm{max}}(1+g_{2})\geq d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{2})
and the inequality is strict unless
g_{2}\geq 16
and is even.

Now assume
g_{1}\geq 2
.

Suppose that
g_{1}+g_{2}\leq 15
.
Then
d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{1}+g_{2})=g_{1}+g_{2}-1>(g_{1}-1)+(g_{2}-1)=d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{1})+d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{2})
.

Suppose now
g_{1}+g_{2}\geq 16
.

By direct computation,
d_{\mathrm{max}}(14)=13
,
d_{\mathrm{max}}(15)=14
,
d_{\mathrm{max}}(16)=d_{\mathrm{max}}(17)=16

and
d_{\mathrm{max}}(18)=d_{\mathrm{max}}(19)=20
.
It is then easy to check that, for every even
g^{\prime}\geq 2
smaller than
g_{1}
, we have
d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{1})-d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{1}-g^{\prime})\leq d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{2})-d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{2}-g^{\prime})
.
This implies that it is enough to verify the statement for
g_{1}=2
or
g_{1}=3
.

By inspection,
for
g_{1}=2
we have
d_{\mathrm{max}}(2+g_{2})\geq 2+d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{2})
and so
d_{\mathrm{max}}(2+g_{2})>d_{\mathrm{max}}(2)+d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{2})
.
Similarly, for
g_{1}=3
we have
d_{\mathrm{max}}(3+g_{2})\geq 3+d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{2})
and so
d_{\mathrm{max}}(3+g_{2})>d_{\mathrm{max}}(3)+d_{\mathrm{max}}(g_{2})
.
∎

As a consequence of the analysis done in
Section

7.1
and
Section

7.2
,
we obtain the following bound for the d-achievable pairs.

Proposition 7.10
.

For a d-achievable pair
(d,g)
we have
d\leq d_{\mathrm{max}}(g)
,
and equality holds for
g=2
, and for
g\geq 16
.
Moreover,
(d_{\mathrm{max}}(g),g)
is indecomposably d-achievable if and only if
g=2
, or if
g
is even and
g\geq 16
.

Proof.

Let us first check the indecomposably d-achieved pairs.
Here we invoke
Lemma

7.4
.
In particular,
we observe that by
Remark

7.5

we only have to consider only the
(I)_{n}
case with
n\geq 3
and
k\geq 2
, which gives the indecomposably d-achievable pairs

(d,g)=\left((k-1)\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil\cdot\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor,kn\right)\,.

By
Table

4
we see that, for
g=2
, the only indecomposably d-achievable pair is
(1,2)
, obtained in the (A
1
) case.
For
3\leq g\leq 15
in the (I
n
) case
we obtain the pairs

(2,6)
,
(4,8)
,

(6,10)
,
(9,12)
,
(12,14)
for
k=2
,
the pairs

(4,9)
,
(8,12)
,
(12,15)
for
k=3
,
and the pairs

(k-1,2k)
,
(2k-2,3k)
for
k\geq 4
.
All such pairs are negligible.

Assume from now on that
g\geq 16
.

Case
g=kn\geq 16
even.

We have
d=(k-1)\left\lceil\frac{g}{2k}\right\rceil\cdot\left\lfloor\frac{g}{2k}\right\rfloor
, and so for
k=2
we obtain
d=d_{\mathrm{max}}(g)
.

Since
\tfrac{g}{2k}=\tfrac{n}{2}
, the product
\left\lceil\frac{g}{2k}\right\rceil\cdot\left\lfloor\frac{g}{2k}\right\rfloor
is simply equal to
\tfrac{n^{2}}{4}
if
n
is even, and to
\tfrac{n^{2}-1}{4}
if
n
is odd, and thus

d\leq(k-1)\frac{n^{2}}{4}=(k-1)\frac{g^{2}}{4k^{2}}\,.

The derivative of this, as a function of
k
, is equal to
\tfrac{2-k}{k^{3}}g^{2}
, and thus it is a strictly decreasing function for
k\geq 2
. Thus for
k\geq 3
and
g\geq 16
we obtain the estimate

(k-1)\frac{g^{2}}{4k^{2}}\leq 2\frac{g^{2}}{4\cdot 3^{2}}=\frac{g^{2}}{18}<\frac{g^{2}-4}{16}\leq\left\lceil\frac{g}{4}\right\rceil\cdot\left\lfloor\frac{g}{4}\right\rfloor=d_{\mathrm{max}}(g).

Hence the achieved pairs are strictly dominated by the case
k=2
.

Case
g=kn\geq 17
odd.

Then

d=\frac{k-1}{4}\left(\frac{g^{2}}{k^{2}}-1\right),

which, as a function of
k
,
has derivative
-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{k-2}{4k^{3}}g^{2}
,
and so is again decreasing for
k\geq 2
.

Write
g=2\ell+1
with
\ell\geq 8
and
note that
k\geq 3
must be odd.
Then
d\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{g^{2}}{9}-1\right)=\frac{(2\ell+1)^{2}}{18}-\frac{1}{2}
.
Observe that
\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}-\frac{1}{4}\leq\lfloor\frac{(g-1)^{2}}{16}\rfloor

and
\frac{(2\ell+1)^{2}}{18}-\frac{1}{2}<\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}-\frac{1}{4}
for
\ell\geq 8
. Hence,
d<\lfloor\frac{(g-1)^{2}}{16}\rfloor=d_{\mathrm{max}}(g)
for
g\geq 17
odd.

We thus conclude that, for
g\geq 16
,
all indecomposably d-achievable pairs
(d,g)
satisfy

d\leq d_{\mathrm{max}}(g)
, and that
the pair

(d_{\mathrm{max}}(g),g)
is indecomposably
d-achievable only for
g=2n
even,
in the (I)
n
case, with
k=2
.

Let now
(d,g)
be an d-achievable pair,
which is not indecomposably d-achievable.
It corresponds to a compact special subvariety
of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

induced by an embedding

(G,X)\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}_{g}
of Shimura data that factors
through
\mathscr{S}_{g^{\prime},g-g^{\prime}}
with
g^{\prime}\leq g-g^{\prime}
. This implies
that
d\leq d_{\mathrm{max}}(g^{\prime})+d_{\mathrm{max}}(g-g^{\prime})
.
By
Lemma

7.9

we have
d_{\mathrm{max}}(g^{\prime})+d_{\mathrm{max}}(g-g^{\prime})\leq d_{\mathrm{max}}(g)
,
and equality

can be attained
only for
g\geq 17
odd and

. This is
indeed the case by

.
∎

The following corollary will be useful for the next section:

Corollary 7.11
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be a simple
{\mathbb{Q}}
-group with

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}({\mathbb{R}})

be a

{\mathbb{C}}
-irreducible decoupled representation of

.

\mathrm{G}
is of type
(I_{n})
with

.

Proof.

This is a byproduct of the analysis carried over
in

7.2
, when

Here we recall the relevant estimates,

and the exact same analysis as in

.

(A
1
)

We have

(D
4
)

We have
\dim U=8

.

(I
n
)

We have

(II

In view of

Let

.

,

,

∎

8.
Compact special subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

from non-decoupled representations

In this section we study the dimension of compact special subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

arising from non-decoupled representations.
We show that such special subvarieties always have smaller dimension than the already constructed special subvarieties,
and so
Proposition

7.12
indeed gives the maximal dimension of a compact subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
.

8.1.
Setting

We want to investigate indecomposable embeddings
of compact Shimura data
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
into a Siegel Shimura datum.
Hence we let
\mathrm{G}
be a reductive
algebraic group over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{i}
the geometrically simple factors of

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathbb{R}}
,
so that
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathbb{R}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{i}
.

Remark 8.1
.

Since the embedding of
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
in a Siegel Shimura
datum is indecomposable and compact (as defined
at the end of
Section

2.3
),
for every simple
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factor

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(j)}

of
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}
,
the real group
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(j)}({\mathbb{R}})

must have at least one
compact

factor by
Theorem

3.7
.

Let then
V
be a
\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible
{\mathbb{Q}}
-representation of
\mathrm{G}
associated to an indecomposable embedding of
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})
.
We recall that, as a
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathbb{C}}
representation,
V_{\mathbb{C}}

has a canonical decomposition into a direct sum of isotypic components.
Each isotypic component can be written
as a direct sum of a certain number of irreducible subspaces
V_{\alpha}
:
note that, though such direct sum into irreducibles is non-canonical,
the number of summands is.
Hence,
V_{\mathbb{C}}

is (non-canonically)
isomorphic to a direct sum
\bigoplus_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}
of
irreducible
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathbb{C}}
-representations
V_{\alpha}
.

Remark 8.2
.

Since
V
is
\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible,
by
Lemma

3.4
all
V_{\alpha}
are Galois conjugate to each other, or to each other’s
\operatorname{GSp}
-duals. Moreover all
V_{\alpha}
are self-dual or none of them is. Also, in the self-dual case, either all
V_{\alpha}
are symplectic or all
V_{\alpha}
are orthogonal.

Furthermore, each
V_{\alpha}

is isomorphic to

V_{\alpha,1}\otimes\dots\otimes V_{\alpha,k}
,
where
V_{\alpha,i}
is an irreducible linear representation of
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{i,{\mathbb{C}}}
: when this happens, we say that each
V_{\alpha,i}

occurs in
V
at the
i
-th position.

8.1.1.
An auxiliary decoupled representation

Given
\mathrm{G}
and
V
as above, we introduce
a
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathbb{C}}
-representation

V^{\prime}
, which will play an auxiliary role in
the proof of
Lemma

8.4
.

As discussed above, we can choose a splitting

V_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}

and isomorphisms
V_{\alpha}\cong V_{\alpha,1}\otimes\dots\otimes V_{\alpha,k}
for each
\alpha
.

Since each
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{i,{\mathbb{C}}}
is
semisimple,
its
1
-dimensional
representations are trivial. Hence,
for each
\alpha
, we define the
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathbb{C}}
-representation
V^{\prime}_{\alpha}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i\in I_{\alpha}}V_{\alpha,i}
,
where
I_{\alpha}=\{i\,|\,\dim(V_{\alpha,i})>1\}
.

Our auxiliary decoupled representation is

V^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\alpha}V^{\prime}_{\alpha}
.
Though the construction of
V^{\prime}
is not very natural,
its isomorphism class as a
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathbb{C}}
-representation is well-defined.

In order to compare the dimensions of
V
and
V^{\prime}
,
here we introduce some combinatorial quantities.

For a fixed
\alpha
consider the multiset of numbers

\{\dim V_{\alpha,1},\,\dots,\,\dim V_{\alpha,k}\}
, which is independent of
\alpha

since
V
is
\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible (the same phenomenon holds for an irreducible linear representation of
\mathrm{G}
), and let
N_{V}
be the sub-multiset
consisting of numbers strictly greater than one.
Denoting by
\operatorname{Prod}(N)
and
\operatorname{Sum}(N)
respectively the product and the sum of the elements of a multiset
N
, for any
\alpha
we have
\dim V_{\alpha}=\operatorname{Prod}(N_{V})
and
\dim(V^{\prime}_{\alpha})=\operatorname{Sum}(N_{V})
.
Clearly
\operatorname{Prod}(N_{V})\geq\operatorname{Sum}(N_{V})
.

For computational purposes in the next two subsections
it will be useful to
distinguish two cases according to a certain property of
N_{V}
, which we define here below.

Definition 8.3
.

A finite multiset
N
of integers strictly greater than one
is
inefficient

if
\operatorname{Prod}(N)\geq 2\cdot\operatorname{Sum}(N)
,
and
efficient
otherwise.
A representation
V
of
\mathrm{G}
, which is
irreducible or
\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible,
is

(in)efficient
if
N_{V}
is.

8.2.
Estimates for inefficient representations

Dimension estimates for inefficient representations can be easily reduced to the decoupled case, and the following lemma shows that inefficient representations are never associated to a compact special subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

of maximal dimension.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}_{g}
is an indecomposable embedding of Shimura data, with
associated
\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible representation
V
. Assume that either

(1)

V
is inefficient as a
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}
-representation, or

(2)

The irreducible subrepresentations of
V_{\mathbb{C}}
are not symplectic.

Then
d(\mathrm{G})<d_{\mathrm{max}}(g)
, where
2g=\dim V
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(j)}
be
the decomposition of
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}
into its
simple
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factors.
Let
k_{j}
denote the number of simple factors of
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(j),{\mathbb{R}}}
, so that
k=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}k_{j}
is the number
of simple factors of
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

For every
j
, let
U_{j}
be an irreducible nontrivial
{\mathbb{C}}
-representation
of a simple factor of
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(j),{\mathbb{C}}}
, which occurs in
V
as
defined in
Section

8.1
.

Claim 8.5
.

We have
g\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}k_{j}\cdot\dim U_{j}
.

We will prove the above claim below.
Assuming
Claim

8.5
, here we show how to conclude the proof.

By
Remark

8.1
each

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(j)}({\mathbb{R}})

must have at least one compact factor, and so

d_{\mathrm{max}}(k_{j}\dim U_{j})\geq d(\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(j)})

by
Corollary

7.11
.
Then, using
Lemma

7.9
and
Claim

8.5
we obtain

d_{\mathrm{max}}(g)\geq d_{\mathrm{max}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}k_{j}\dim U_{j}\Big)\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}d_{\mathrm{max}}(k_{j}\dim U_{j})\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}d(\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(j)})=d(\mathrm{G}).

Now, since
\dim U_{1}\geq 2
, for the second inequality to be an equality, by
Lemma

7.9
we must have
\ell=1
. By
Corollary

7.11
, for the third inequality to be an equality
we must have
\mathrm{G}
of type
(I
n
)
and
k=2
, meaning

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}({\mathbb{R}})

must have exactly one compact factor.

In this case, though, for
\mathrm{G}
of type (I
n
),
we have
2g=\dim V=mn^{2}
with
n=\dim U_{i}\geq 3

and
m\geq 2
even (since the (I
n
) type is not self-dual for
n\geq 3
).
It follows that
g\geq n^{2}\geq 2n+2
, and so

d_{\mathrm{max}}(g)>d_{\mathrm{max}}(2n)
by
Lemma

7.9
.
Finally, by
Lemma

7.4

we have

d(\mathrm{G})\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil\cdot\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor\leq d_{\mathrm{max}}(2n)<d_{\mathrm{max}}(g)
.
∎

Now we prove the above technical claim.

Proof of
Claim

8.5
.

Fix a decomposition
V_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}

and isomorphisms
V_{\alpha}\cong V_{\alpha,1}\otimes\dots\otimes V_{\alpha,k}
for all
\alpha
.

Consider first the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factor
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1)}
of
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}
,
so that
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1),{\mathbb{C}}}=\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{1,{\mathbb{C}}}\times\dots\times\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{k_{1},{\mathbb{C}}}
.
Up to relabelling the factors of
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1),{\mathbb{C}}}
, we can
assume that
U_{1}\cong V_{\gamma,1}
for some
\gamma
.
Let
\varsigma_{1},\dots,\varsigma_{k_{1}}\in\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb{C}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
such that

\varsigma_{i}(\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{1,{\mathbb{C}}})=\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{i,{\mathbb{C}}}
.
Observe that
\varsigma_{i}(V_{\gamma})
is a
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariant subspace of
V_{\mathbb{C}}
and so it belongs to the isotypic component of
V_{\beta(i)}

for some
\beta(i)
. It follows that

U_{(1),i}\coloneqq V_{\beta(i),i}
has the same dimension as
U_{1}
.

If the irreducible summands of
V_{\mathbb{C}}
are not symplectic,
then the
\operatorname{GSp}
-dual of
V_{\beta}
belongs to a different
isotypic component, say to the isotypic component of
V_{\beta(i)^{*}}
.
We then define
U_{(1),i}^{*}\coloneqq V_{\beta(i)^{*},i}
.

Put
U_{(1)}\coloneqq U_{(1),1}\oplus\dots\oplus U_{(1),k_{1}}

and note that
\dim U_{(1)}=k_{1}\cdot\dim U_{1}
.
If the
V_{\alpha}
are not symplectic,
put also
U^{*}_{(1)}\coloneqq U^{*}_{(1),1}\oplus\dots\oplus U^{*}_{(1),k_{1}}
.

For
j=2,\dots,\ell
define analogously
U_{(j)}

(and
U^{*}_{(j)}
, if the
V_{\alpha}
are not symplectic).

Assume first that
V
is inefficient.
By construction,
the auxiliary representation
V^{\prime}
introduced in

Section

8.1.1
contains

\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\ell}U_{(j)}
.
It follows
that

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\dim V=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\dim V_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\operatorname{Prod}(N_{V})\geq\sum_{\alpha}\operatorname{Sum}(N_{V})=

\displaystyle=\dim V^{\prime}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\dim U_{(j)}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}k_{j}\cdot\dim U_{j}.

Assume now that the summands
V_{\alpha}
are not symplectic.
Then
V^{\prime}
contains
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\ell}(U_{(j)}\oplus U^{*}_{(j)})
,
and so

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\dim V=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\operatorname{Prod}(N_{V})\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\operatorname{Sum}(N_{V})=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\dim V^{\prime}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\dim(U_{(j)}\oplus U^{*}_{(j)})=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}k_{j}\cdot\dim U_{j}.

Thus the claim is proven.
∎

8.3.
Special subvarieties from efficient representations

Now we have to deal with efficient representations. To that end we begin with the following numerical lemma.

Lemma 8.6
.

A finite collection
N
of integers larger than
1

is efficient if and only if it belongs to the following list:

(i)

\{b\}
;

(ii)

\{2,b\}
;

(iii)

\{3,b\}
with
3\leq b\leq 5
;

(iv)

\{2,2,b\}
with
2\leq b\leq 3
.

Proof.

First, if
|N|\geq 4
, then
N
is inefficient.
Indeed, for
N=\{2,\dots,2\}
we have
\operatorname{Prod}(N)=2^{|N|}\geq 4|N|=2\cdot\operatorname{Sum}(N)
, and increasing each number in
N
by one
results in increasing
2\cdot\operatorname{Sum}
by
2
and
\operatorname{Prod}
by at least
2^{|N|-1}
.

Next, if
|N|=3
, then
N=\{2,2,2\}
is efficient since
\operatorname{Prod}(N)=8<12=2\cdot\operatorname{Sum}(N)
.
Now, increasing a number in
N
by one results in increasing

2\cdot\operatorname{Sum}
by
2
and
\operatorname{Prod}
by at least
4
; so, in order to find efficient sets of three elements, we can only do it at most once. This shows that
\{2,2,2\},\{2,2,3\}
are the only efficient sets of 3 elements, since
\{2,3,3\},\{2,2,4\}
are inefficient by inspection.

Next, suppose that
N=\{a,b\}
, with
a\leq b
. Then
N
is efficient if and only if
ab<2a+2b
, which is equivalent to
(a-2)(b-2)<4
. So either
2=a\leq b
, or
3=a\leq b<6
.

Finally, if
|N|=1
, then it is certainly efficient.
∎

Now we show that efficient representations are never associated to compact special subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

of maximal dimension.

Lemma 8.7
.

Let
(\mathrm{G},{\mathdutchcal{X}})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}_{g}
is an indecomposable embedding of Shimura data, and assume that the associated

\operatorname{GSp}
-irreducible representation
V
is not decoupled.
If
N
is efficient, then
the pair
(d,g)
associated to the representation
V
of
\mathrm{G}
satisfies
d<d_{\mathrm{max}}(g)
.

In the proof, we will often use the following observation.

Remark 8.8
.

Let
U_{i}
be a complex irreducible linear representation
of the complex algebraic group
G^{\prime}_{i}
for
i=1,2
.
Then
U_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}U_{2}
is a self-dual
(G^{\prime}_{1}\times G^{\prime}_{2})
-representation if and only if
U_{i}
is a self-dual
G^{\prime}_{i}
-representation for
i=1,2
.
Moreover,
U_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}U_{2}
is symplectic if and only if exactly one of
\{U_{1},U_{2}\}
is symplectic, and the other is orthogonal.

Proof of
Lemma

8.7
.

Since
N=\{b\}
corresponds to a decoupled representation,
we proceed by separately analyzing each efficient case
with
N\neq\{b\}
.

Cases
N=\{2,2,2\}
and
N=\{2,2\}
.

This can only occur if all factors
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{i}
are of type A
1
.
Hence, the dimension
d=d(\mathrm{G})
of the Shimura datum is exactly the number of non-compact factors in

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}({\mathbb{R}})
.
On the other hand, each linear summand
V_{\alpha}
is nontrivially acted on
by at most one non-compact factor of

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}({\mathbb{R}})

by
Lemma

3.6
.
It follows that there must be at least
d
such linear summands, each one of dimension
2^{|N|}
.
Hence,
\dim V\geq 2^{|N|}d
and so
g\geq 2^{|N|-1}d\geq d+1
.

We will show now by contradiction that the case
d=g-1
does not occur, and so this case is negligible.

Indeed, if
d=g-1
, we must have
N=\{2,2\}
and
(d,g)=(1,2)
. So
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathbb{R}}
is a product of the unit quaternions
\mathrm{Nm}_{1}({\mathbb{H}}_{\mathbb{R}})
and
\operatorname{SL}_{2,{\mathbb{R}}}
. However the only nontrivial 4-dimensional real representation of
\mathrm{Nm}_{1}({\mathbb{H}}_{\mathbb{R}})
is on
{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathbb{R}}
by left-multiplication, and its centralizer is isomorphic to itself. Thus
\operatorname{SL}_{2,{\mathbb{R}}}
must act trivially, which is a contradiction.

Case
N=\{2,2,3\}
or
N=\{3,b\}
with
3\leq b\leq 5
.

In this case, we have
\dim V_{\alpha,i}=3
for some
\alpha,i
.
Inspecting
Table

2
, we see that

\mathrm{G}_{i}
must be isogenous to
\operatorname{SU}_{1,2}

and
V_{\alpha,i}
must be the standard representation or its dual.
In any case,

V_{\alpha,i}
is not self-dual, and so
V_{\alpha}
is not symplectic
by
Remark

8.8
.
Thus
d<d_{\mathrm{max}}(g)
by
Lemma

8.4
.

Case
N=\{2,b\}
with
b\geq 3
.

In this case
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}=\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1)}\times\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2)}
, where
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1)}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-simple of type A
1
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2)}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-simple of type different from A
1
.

If
V_{\alpha}
is not symplectic, we are done by
Lemma

8.4
.

So now we assume that
V_{\alpha}
is symplectic. In this case, since the factors of
V_{\alpha}
of dimension
2

are symplectic,
by
Remark

8.8

the factors of
V_{\alpha}
of dimension
b

must be orthogonal.

We denote by
\ell_{1}
the number of non-compact factors of

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(1)}({\mathbb{R}})

and by
\ell_{2}
the number of factors in
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2),{\mathbb{R}}}
. Then
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathbb{R}}
has at least
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\geq 2
factors, so we have
g\geq b(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})
and
d=\ell_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell_{2}}d(\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2),i})
, where we
recall that

d(\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2),i})=0
if

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2),i}({\mathbb{R}})

is compact.

We claim that
d(\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2),i})\leq d_{\mathrm{max}}(b)
for every non-compact

\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2),i}({\mathbb{R}})
.
As a consequence, by

Lemma

7.9
and the fact that

b\geq 3
, we obtain
d\leq\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\cdot d_{\mathrm{max}}(b)<d_{\mathrm{max}}(b\ell_{1}+b\ell_{2})=d_{\mathrm{max}}(g)
, thus concluding the proof of the lemma.

To prove the claim note that, by

Table

2
,
we always have
d(\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2),i})<b
, except
in the cases of
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2),i}
isogenous to
\operatorname{SO}^{*}_{2r}
with

r\geq 5
.

Finally, we separately analize the case of
\widetilde{\mathrm{G}}_{(2)}

of type (II)
r
.
We get that
d\leq\ell_{1}+\frac{r(r-1)}{2}\cdot\ell_{2}
and
g\geq 2r(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})
. If
(d,g)
is not negligible, we obtain

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{max}}(g)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\Big(r(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})\Big)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot r^{2}\cdot(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})-\frac{1}{4}=

\displaystyle=d+\frac{r\ell_{2}+(r^{2}-2)\ell_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}{2}>d+1

as desired.
∎

8.4.
Compact special subvarieties of maximal dimension

Now we can prove our second main result.

Proof of
Theorem

B
.

By
Lemma

8.4
and
Lemma

8.7

a compact subvariety of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

of dimension

must be
either a Hodge-generic subvariety, or a compact special subvariety associated to

The conclusion is then a consequence of
Proposition

7.12

and of
Remark

7.13
.
∎

The proofs of

7.4
and

7.10

and

in fact yield more information than

, allowing us to describe all maximal-dimensional compact subvarieties of

,

We collect such information in the following statement.

Theorem 8.9
.

All maximal-dimensional compact subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
,
in each genus

(o)

for

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}

(i)

9.
The indecomposable locus and the locus of Jacobians

In this section we discuss some consequences of our results, and some open problems concerning the locus of indecomposable ppav

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\rm ind}

and the locus of Jacobians, in particular proving
Corollary

C
.

We will shortly refer to the image of
the natural morphism

{\mathcal{A}}_{g^{\prime}}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-g^{\prime}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

as to the
locus
{\mathcal{A}}_{g^{\prime}}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-g^{\prime}}
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
, and similarly for the image
of
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}_{g^{\prime}}\times{\mathcal{A}}^{*}_{g-g^{\prime}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}^{*}_{g}
.
We will adopt the same convention for
similar decomposable loci in

{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}
,
such as
the image
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g^{\prime},1}^{\rm ct}\times{\mathcal{M}}_{g-g^{\prime},1}^{\rm ct}\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}
.

9.1.
The locus

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\rm ind}

of indecomposable ppav

As mentioned in
Section

1.4
, it is also very natural to investigate
the moduli space
{\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\rm ind}
of indecomposable ppav of dimension
g
, see
[
KS03
, §1]
for a further discussion of the motivation.

Thinking of the Satake compactification as a compactification of

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\rm ind}\subsetneq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{*}
,
we see that the boundary

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{*}\setminus{\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\rm ind}
has one irreducible component
of maximal dimension, namely
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}^{*}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}^{*}
, and it has codimension
g-1
.
Thus
g-2\leq\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\rm ind})
,
while
Theorem

A
of course implies the following.

Corollary 9.1
.

For
g\geq 2
the following holds:

g-2\leq\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\rm ind})\leq g-1.

It would be very interesting to know which value it in fact is. As we will now see,

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\rm ind})=\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{\rm ind})=g-2

for
g=2,3,4
, and it is natural to wonder if this is the case for all
g
.

Indeed, the cases of
g=2,3
are classical:

{\mathcal{A}}_{2}^{\rm ind}\cong{\mathcal{M}}_{2}

is affine,
while
{\mathcal{A}}_{3}^{\rm ind}\cong{\mathcal{M}}_{3}

does not contain a compact surface, for example by
[
Dia84
]
.

In general, recall that
\lambda_{i}
denotes the
i
’th Chern class of the Hodge rank
g
vector bundle on

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
,
and that the tautological ring

R^{*}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})\subseteq CH^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})

is the subring generated by the classes
\lambda_{i}
.

For
g=4
, from the results of
[
HT12
,
HT18
]
it follows that the classes of all algebraic subvarieties of

{\mathcal{A}}_{4}
lie in
R^{*}({\mathcal{A}}_{4})
,
and in fact the class of

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{3}

is a nonzero multiple of the class
\lambda_{3}
. Thus the main result of
[
KS03
]
shows that any compact subvariety
Z
of

{\mathcal{A}}_{4}\setminus({\mathcal{A}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{3})

must satisfy
\dim Z\leq 3\cdot 2/2-1=2
, since
\lambda_{3}|_{Z}=0
. This implies

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{4}^{\rm ind})=2
.

However, the situation in higher genus remains mysterious. By
[
vdG99
]
, for any
g
the homology class of
[E]\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}
is a multiple of the top Hodge class
\lambda_{g}
, which in fact vanishes on

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
.
As detailed in
[
CMOP24
]
, it is possible to define a projection map

CH^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})\to R^{*}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})
.
Then in
[
COP24
]
Canning, Oprea, Pandharipande show that, in general,
the projection of the class of

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{g-1}
to the tautological ring of
{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

is a nonzero multiple of
\lambda_{g-1}
, but also that for
g=6
the class of

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{5}

does not lie in
R^{*}({\mathcal{A}}_{6})
.
In
[
IL25
]
Iribar Lopez shows that such a locus of products is not tautological for
g=12
, or even
g\geq 16
, either. This makes the above argument for
g=4
fail in higher genus.

9.2.
The locus of Jacobians, and the moduli space of curves

In order to discuss

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})
,
we begin by recalling the construction of a compact subvariety contained in its boundary

\partial{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}={\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
,
already pointed out by the first author for
[
Kri12
]
.

Lemma 9.2
.

For
g\geq 2
there exists a compact subvariety of

\partial{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}

of dimension
\lfloor 3g/2\rfloor-2
, whose image in

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

under
{\mathcal{J}}
has dimension
\lfloor g/2\rfloor
.
Moreover there exists a compact subvariety of

{\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})

of dimension
\lfloor 2g/3\rfloor
.

Proof.

We first note that

{\mathcal{J}}:{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{\rm ct}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathcal{A}}_{2}

contains a compact curve, while
the Torelli map

{\mathcal{J}}:{\mathcal{M}}_{3}^{\rm ct}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{3}

is proper and surjective,
and thus

{\mathcal{M}}_{3}^{\rm ct}

contains a compact surface that is a preimage under
{\mathcal{J}}
of a compact surface in

{\mathcal{A}}_{3}
.

Since the forgetful map

{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{\rm ct}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}

is proper (the fiber over
[C]
being a blowup of the
n
’th Cartesian power
C^{\times n}
), taking preimages of a compact curve in

{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{\rm ct}

and of a compact surface in

{\mathcal{M}}_{3}^{\rm ct}
,
respectively, gives a compact surface in

{\mathcal{M}}_{2,1}^{\rm ct}
,
a compact threefold in

{\mathcal{M}}_{2,2}^{\rm ct}
,
and a compact threefold in

{\mathcal{M}}_{3,1}^{\rm ct}
.

Now, for even
g=2n\geq 4
, inside the boundary stratum

{\mathcal{M}}_{2,1}^{\rm ct}\times{\mathcal{M}}_{2,2}^{\rm ct}\times\dots\times{\mathcal{M}}_{2,2}^{\rm ct}\times{\mathcal{M}}_{2,1}^{\rm ct}

of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}
,
we consider the product of two compact surfaces in

{\mathcal{M}}_{2,1}^{\rm ct}
,
and
n-2
compact threefolds in

{\mathcal{M}}_{2,2}^{\rm ct}
,
giving altogether a product variety of dimension
2\cdot 2+3\cdot(n-2)=\tfrac{3g}{2}-2
. The image of this subvariety under
{\mathcal{J}}
is the product of
n
copies of the
{\mathcal{J}}
image in

{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
of the compact curve in
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{\rm ct}
,
and so has dimension
\frac{g}{2}
.

For
g=2n+1\geq 5
odd, we do the same except taking the last factor to be a compact threefold in

{\mathcal{M}}_{3,1}^{\rm ct}
.

As for the last claim, we argue in a very similar way, the cases
g=2,3
having already been treated above.
If
g=3n+1\geq 4
, then the locus

{\mathcal{M}}_{3,1}^{\rm ct}\times({\mathcal{M}}_{3,2}^{\rm ct})^{n-1}\times{\mathcal{M}}_{1,1}^{\rm ct}

inside
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}
is mapped by

{\mathcal{J}}
onto
({\mathcal{A}}_{3})^{n}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
,
which
contains a compact subvariety of dimension
2n
.
If
g=3n+2\geq 5
, then the locus

{\mathcal{M}}_{2,1}^{\rm ct}\times{\mathcal{M}}_{3,1}^{\rm ct}\times({\mathcal{M}}_{3,2}^{\rm ct})^{n-1}

of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}
maps onto
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}\times({\mathcal{A}}_{3})^{n}
,
which
contains a compact subvariety of dimension
1+2n
.
If
g=3n\geq 6
, then via
{\mathcal{J}}

the locus

({\mathcal{M}}_{3,1}^{\rm ct})^{2}\times({\mathcal{M}}_{3,2}^{\rm ct})^{n-2}

of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}
maps onto
({\mathcal{A}}_{3})^{n}
,
which contains
a compact subvariety of dimension
2n
.
∎

We note that all subvarieties constructed
to prove the last claim of
Lemma

9.2
are compact subvarieties of

{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}

of dimension
4g/3
plus a constant, mapping to loci in

J({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})

of dimension
2g/3
plus a constant.

We now prove the results on compact subvarieties of

{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}
and
{\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})
.

Proof of
Corollary

C
.

As already mentioned in the introduction, the upper bound
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}))\leq g-1
for
g\leq 15
simply follows from the inclusion

{\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

and the
g\leq 15
case of
Theorem

B
, giving

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})=\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})=g-1

for this range of
g
.

To determine

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})

for
2\leq g\leq 23
,
we first observe that

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})\geq\lfloor\frac{3g}{2}\rfloor-2
by
Lemma

9.2
.
Thus we only need to prove
that
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})\leq\lfloor\tfrac{3g}{2}\rfloor-2
for
2\leq g\leq 23
.

Since

{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{\rm ct}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{3}^{\rm ct}

contain a compact curve and a compact surface, respectively, the statement of the corollary is true for
g=2
and
g=3
.

For
4\leq g\leq 23
, we proceed by induction, assuming the result for all
g^{\prime}<g
. Indeed, by
Theorem

B
,
exactly for
g
in this range we have
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{A}}_{g})<\lfloor\tfrac{3g}{2}\rfloor-2

and so an irreducible compact subvariety
Z\subsetneq{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}

that satisfies
Z\cap{\mathcal{M}}_{g}\neq\emptyset
(and thus maps generically 1-to-1 to its image in

{\mathcal{A}}_{g}

under
{\mathcal{J}}
) has dimension strictly
smaller than
\lfloor\tfrac{3g}{2}\rfloor-2
.

Thus it is enough to deal with irreducible compact
Z\subsetneq\partial{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}
. Such

must then be contained in some irreducible component

{\mathcal{M}}_{g^{\prime},1}^{\rm ct}\times{\mathcal{M}}_{g-g^{\prime},1}^{\rm ct}
of the boundary
\partial{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}={\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
.
Since the forgetful map

{\mathcal{M}}_{k,1}^{\rm ct}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\rm ct}

has compact curve fibers, we obtain the equality

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{k,1}^{\rm ct})=1+\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\rm ct})

It then follows from the inductive assumption that

\dim Z\leq\left(\lfloor\tfrac{3g^{\prime}}{2}\rfloor-2\right)+1+\left(\lfloor\tfrac{3(g-g^{\prime})}{2}\rfloor-2\right)+1\leq\lfloor\tfrac{3g}{2}\rfloor-2\,,

which proves the upper bound
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})\leq\lfloor\tfrac{3g}{2}\rfloor-2

.

The last claim
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}))\geq\lfloor 2g/3\rfloor

.
∎

The compact subvariety constructed above

and we do not know any construction of a higher-dimensional compact

and

Known results for maximal dimensions of compact subvarieties of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}

Here we recall that Keel and Sadun proved
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})\leq 2g-4
for any
g\geq 3
. The lower bounds on

\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g})
and
\operatorname{mdc_{vg}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct})

simply follow from considering the Satake compactification

{\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{*}
, which is the closure of
{\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g})
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{g}^{*}
, so that
\operatorname{codim}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{*}\setminus{\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}))=2
and
\operatorname{codim}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{*}\setminus{\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g}^{\rm ct}))=3
.

The lower bounds for
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g})
are obtained by covering constructions starting
either from a compact curve in

{\mathcal{M}}_{3}

or
from a one-dimensional compact family of pairs of distinct points on a fixed curve of genus
2
.
The best known results are due to Zaal, following ideas
by González-Díez and Harvey in
[
GDH91
, Section 4]
, and are
described in detail in Zaal’s thesis, where in particular it is shown in
[
Zaa05
, Thm. 2.3]
that a compact
d
-fold exists in

{\mathcal{M}}_{g}

for any
g\geq 2^{d+1}
. Finally, the best known upper bound for
\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g})
is the famous 40 year old theorem of Diaz
[
Dia84
]
(which by now has multiple proofs):

\operatorname{mdc}({\mathcal{M}}_{g})\leq g-2
for any
g\geq 2
.

Critical properties of the prethermal Floquet Time Crystal
Muath Natsheh 1, Andrea Gambassi 2,3, Aditi Mitra 1

Abstract
The critical properties characterizing the formation of the Floquet time crystal in the prethermal phase are investigated analytically in the periodically driven O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) model. In particular, we focus on the critical line separating the trivial phase with period synchronized dynamics and absence of long-range spatial order from the non-trivial phase where long-range spatial order is accompanied by period-doubling dynamics. In the vicinity of the critical line, with a combination of dimensional expansion and exact solution for N → ∞ N\to\infty , we determine the exponent ν \nu that characterizes the divergence of the spatial correlation length of the equal-time correlation functions, the exponent β \beta characterizing the growth of the amplitude of the order-parameter, as well as the initial-slip exponent θ \theta of the aging dynamics when a quench is performed from deep in the trivial phase to the critical line. The exponents ν , β , θ \nu,\beta,\theta are found to be identical to those in the absence of the drive. In addition, the functional form of the aging is found to depend on whether the system is probed at times that are small or large compared to the drive period. The spatial structure of the two-point correlation functions, obtained as a linear response to a perturbing potential in the vicinity of the critical line, is found to show algebraic decays that are longer ranged than in the absence of a drive, and besides being period-doubled, are also found to oscillate in space at the wave-vector ω / ( 2 ​ v ) \omega/(2v) , v v being the velocity of the quasiparticles, and ω \omega being the drive frequency.



I
Introduction

Floquet time crystals (FTCs) are systems that show spontaneous breaking of discrete time-translation symmetry (TTS),
accompanied by the breaking of another internal symmetry of the system such as an
Ising symmetry
Sacha and Zakrzewski 2017
;
Else et al. 2020
;
Khemani et al. 2019
.
Simply broken Ising symmetry would imply the well known transition from a paramagnetic to a ferromagnetic Ising phase
Goldenfeld 1992
.
However, the appearance of broken TTS adds a new flavor to this problem.
Thus
natural questions that arise are: Is there any universality associated with the transition from a trivial phase where there is no long-range Ising order, and the dynamics is synchronized with the drive, to a FTC phase? And if yes, what are the critical exponents of the transition? Are they related to those
encountered at the Ising transition in the absence of drive, or are they different?
This question is particularly relevant due to the numerous microscopically different experimental platforms that have realized this phenomenon
Zhang et al. 2017
;
Choi et al. 2017
;
Rovny et al. 2018
;
Autti et al. 2018
;
Pal et al. 2018
;
Smits et al. 2018
.

Some of these questions have been addressed in spatial dimension
d=1
(1
d
)
Yao et al. 2017
;
Berdanier et al. 2018a
;
Yates et al. 2018
;
Yao et al. 2017
. In particular, in the presence of disorder, the system can become many-body-localized in 1
d
making the FTC, also known
as the discrete time crystal, stable even at long times
Khemani et al. 2019
. For a many-body-localized Floquet Ising chain, it was shown that the
critical behavior near the FTC critical point belongs to the infinite randomness universality class
Yao et al. 2017
;
Berdanier et al. 2018a
;
Berdanier et al. 2018b
. Thus, although driven, the underlying critical behavior could be related to an undriven problem, namely that of a static Ising model with disorder
Fisher 1992
;
Fisher 1994
;
Fisher 1995
;
Damle and Huse 2002
;
Motrunich et al. 2000
;
Vosk et al. 2015
.

In the absence of disorder, the critical point associated with the FTC of
free fermion Floquet chains, in which exactly
one band is occupied and the others empty, was shown to exhibit a central charge, which was extracted from the scaling of the entanglement entropy
Yates et al. 2018
;
Berdanier et al. 2018a
.
As in equilibrium, this central charge counts the number of Fermi points appearing
at the critical point. With driving, however, new Fermi-points can appear at the Floquet zone boundaries, and the central charge for Floquet chains was found to keep track of these additional Fermi points
Yates et al. 2018
.

The nature of the transition from a trivial phase to a FTC phase is also a very interesting question for periodically driven Hamiltonian systems coupled to a reservoir, where dissipation and noise make the dynamics effectively classical. For the particular case of the 1
d
driven-dissipative model studied in Ref.
Yao et al. 2020
, the transition was mapped to the locked-to-sliding transition of a d.c.-driven charge density wave.

The question of universality of the FTC critical point has not been addressed in
spatial dimension
d>1
, for driven but otherwise isolated quantum systems. For
d>1
, even with disorder, one is more likely
to encounter
a prethermal FTC as the fate of many-body-localization above
d=1
is unknown. The subject of the current paper is the universality
associated with prethermal FTC phases in
d>2
.
Since any Floquet system has a time-independent Floquet Hamiltonian associated with it, it is expected
Else et al. 2017
that its critical properties may have
some relation to those of an undriven model. Generically
the undriven model is not the same as the original one with
the driving switched off. This is because the Floquet driving affects non-trivially the parameters of the system, for example, by making the coupling constants more long ranged. In addition, Floquet driving can also change the underlying symmetries.

Non-perturbative approaches such as the large-
N
limit
have proven to be very useful in understanding
diverse systems ranging from impurity models
Read and Newns 1983
;
Ratiani and Mitra 2009
;
Mitra and Rosch 2011
, Kondo lattice models
Read et al. 1984
, and strange metals
Sachdev and Ye 1993
;
Kitaev 2015
.
In this paper, we address the question of universality in the context of the transition to a FTC by studying the periodically driven
O(N)
model. In the absence of the drive,
and depending on the value of
N
, this model is a textbook example for studying the transition to various broken symmetry phases both in equilibrium
Eyal et al. 1996
;
Moshe and Zinn-Justin 2003
, and out of equilibrium, due to, e.g., a quantum quench
Sotiriadis et al. 2009
;
Sotiriadis and Cardy 2010
;
Sciolla and Biroli 2011
;
Sciolla and Biroli 2013
;
Chandran et al. 2013
;
Gagel et al. 2014
;
Gagel et al. 2015
;
Chiocchetta et al. 2015
;
Maraga et al. 2015
;
Smacchia et al. 2015
;
Maraga et al. 2016
;
Chiocchetta et al. 2016
;
Lemonik and Mitra 2016
;
Chiocchetta et al. 2017
.
In addition, the model can be exactly solved in the limit
N\to\infty
, providing access to equilibrium and non-equilibrium collective behaviors and critical properties beyond perturbation theory
Moshe and Zinn-Justin 2003
;
Maraga et al. 2015
;
Lemonik and Mitra 2016
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

and

and

and to

and to

. In Section

.
Intermediate details of the calculations are reported in several Appendices.

\displaystyle H=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int d^{d}x\frac{1}{2}\bigl[

(2)

\displaystyle V=\frac{u}{4N}\int d^{d}x\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi_{i}^{2}\biggr)^{2}.

(3)

In Eq. (

For later convenience, we represent the fields

For generic values of

With drive, the emerging critical properties of Eq. (

III
Perturbative correction at one loop

In this section we determine the perturbative one-loop correction to
G_{R,K}
and discuss its effect on the phase diagram of the model and on its exponent
\nu
.

In particular, the predictions presented here are derived at the lowest order in the coupling constant

N
of the components of the field.
However, we will be eventually interested in the limit
N\to\infty

As the fixed-point value
u^{*}

\epsilon=4-d

\epsilon

u^{2}

The one-loop corrections
\delta G_{R,K}

can be written as

;
Chiocchetta et al. 2016

\displaystyle\delta G_{R}(k,t,t^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau\,G_{0R}(k,t,\tau)\;iT(\tau)\;G_{0R}(k,\tau,t^{\prime}),

(18a)

\displaystyle\delta iG_{K}(k,t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau\,G_{0R}(k,t,\tau)\;iT(\tau)\;iG_{0K}(k,\tau,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+(t\leftrightarrow t^{\prime}),

where

\displaystyle iT(t)

\displaystyle=u\langle\phi^{2}\rangle=\frac{u}{2}iG_{0K}(x=0,t,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{u}{2}\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,iG_{0K}(k,t,t)f_{c}\left(k/\Lambda\right),

In the expression above

III.1
Perturbative correction to the critical line

The Keldysh action of the model is given by Eq. (
II

S_{\rm int}\mapsto 0
, the part of it which is proportional to the fields

. In the presence of

\displaystyle\left[r-r_{1}\cos(\omega t)\right]\bm{\phi}_{q}\cdot\bm{\phi}_{c}\mapsto

\displaystyle\quad\left[r-r_{1}\cos(\omega t)+u\langle\phi^{2}\rangle\right]\bm{\phi}_{q}\cdot\bm{\phi}_{c}.

Using Eqs. (
19

\displaystyle\left[r-r_{1}\cos(\omega t)\right]\bm{\phi}_{q}\cdot\bm{\phi}_{c}\mapsto

\displaystyle\quad\left[r-r_{1}\cos(\omega t)+\widetilde{B}_{4}\cos^{2}(\omega t/2)\right]\bm{\phi}_{q}\cdot\bm{\phi}_{c}.

(24)

Writing
\cos^{2}(\omega t/2)=[1+\cos(\omega t)]/2
, it is clear that the effect of the long-time part of the tadpole is to shift

\displaystyle r\mapsto r+\frac{\widetilde{B}_{4}}{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad r_{1}\mapsto r_{1}-\frac{\widetilde{B}_{4}}{2},

(25)

with the resulting effective action, after these shifts, being still Gaussian at this order in perturbation theory.

Recall that the critical line for the Gaussian model is given by the condition
y=0
in Eq. (
7
), i.e., the critical value
r_{c}
of
r
for
q\ll 1
is determined by

\displaystyle r_{c}\approx\frac{\omega^{2}}{4}+\frac{r_{1}}{2}.

(26)

The shifts in Eq. (
25
) imply that
the critical value
r_{c}
of
r
is now determined by the condition above imposed on the shifted parameters, i.e.,

\displaystyle r_{c}+\frac{\widetilde{B}_{4}}{2}=\frac{\omega^{2}}{4}+\frac{r_{1}}{2}-\frac{\widetilde{B}_{4}}{4}.

(27)

Accordingly, in terms of the parameters
r
and
r_{1}
of the original model and of the associated dimensionless quantities
a
and
q
(see Eqs. (
4
) and (
6
)),
the critical line is shifted compared to that in the absence of interaction (i.e., with
\widetilde{B}_{4}\mapsto 0
) as

\displaystyle a_{1}(q)=\frac{4r_{c}}{\omega^{2}}=1+q-3\frac{\widetilde{B}_{4}}{\omega^{2}}.

(28)

The critical line is therefore shifted downwards, i.e., the stable green disordered region, due to the interaction, widens locally at the expense of the red unstable (ordered) region, as usually occurs also in equilibrium. This fact is shown schematically in Fig.
1
.

III.2
Perturbative correction to the exponent
\nu

We now discuss the perturbative correction to
the correlation length
\xi
which characterizes the spatial decay of the equal-time correlation function at long times.
This then allows us to determine perturbatively the associated critical exponent
\nu
to
O(u)
, which will eventually yield an estimate at the lowest order in the dimensional expansion
\epsilon=4-d
.
In Section
IV
we will then determine the exact dependence of
\nu
on the dimensionality
d
for
N\to\infty
beyond the perturbative result presented here.

The shifts in Eq. (
25
) caused by the long-time limit of the
tadpole imply a shift

y\mapsto y+3\widetilde{B}_{4}(y)/4

(29)

in the detuning from the critical line introduced in Eq. (
7
).
Based on the dependence of
G_{0R}
on
k
in Eq. (
13
) (which, is not altered by the contribution of the tadpole beyond the shifts in the parameters in Eq. (
25
)), one can easily identify the spatial correlation length
\xi
of the field as being determined by

y=\xi^{-2}
.
Accordingly, the correlation length accounting for the one loop correction is (see Eq. (
29
))

\displaystyle\xi^{-2}=y+\frac{3}{4}\widetilde{B}_{4}(y).

(30)

Let us denote by
y_{c}
the critical value of
y
, which is shifted by the interaction and is determined by the condition that, correspondingly,
\xi
diverges, i.e., from Eq. (
30
),

\displaystyle 0=y_{c}+\frac{3}{4}\widetilde{B}_{4}(y_{c}).

(31)

Subtracting Eq. (
31
)
from Eq. (
30
) one finds

\displaystyle\xi^{-2}

\displaystyle=y-y_{c}+\frac{3}{4}\left[\widetilde{B}_{4}(y)-\widetilde{B}_{4}(y_{c})\right]

\displaystyle=y-y_{c}+3\frac{u}{8}a_{4}q\sqrt{r_{0}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\,k^{3}\biggl[\frac{1}{k^{2}+y}-\frac{1}{k^{2}+y_{c}}\biggr]f_{c}\left(k/\Lambda\right),

(32)

where, in the last equality, we used Eq. (
21
).
Denoting by
\delta y=y-y_{c}
the distance from the actual critical line, the previous equation can be written as

\displaystyle\xi^{-2}

\displaystyle=\delta y\biggl[1-3\frac{u}{8}a_{4}q\sqrt{r_{0}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}dkk^{3}\frac{f_{c}\left(k/\Lambda\right)}{\left(k^{2}+\delta y+y_{c}\right)\left(k^{2}+y_{c}\right)}\biggr].

(33)

Note that, from Eq. (
31
), we expect
y_{c}
to vanish in perturbation theory as the interaction strength
u
vanishes
and therefore we may set
y_{c}=0
in the denominator of
the integrand in Eq. (
33
) as the prefactor of the integral is already of order
u
and the calculation is done at the lowest order in perturbation theory.
By choosing the exponential cut-off function introduced after Eq. (
21
) (see also Eq. (
79
))
and after
performing the momentum integral, one finds

\displaystyle\xi^{-2}

\displaystyle=\delta y\biggl[1-3\frac{u}{16}a_{4}q\sqrt{r_{0}}\ln\biggl|\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\delta y}\biggr|+O(u^{2})\biggr],

(34)

for
\delta y\ll\Lambda^{2}
.
At this order in the perturbative expansion, this logarithmic correction can be exponentiated in order to obtain

\displaystyle\xi^{-2}

\displaystyle=\delta y\biggl[1+quA\ln\biggl|\frac{\delta y}{\Lambda^{2}}\biggr|+O(u^{2})\biggr]

\displaystyle\approx\Lambda^{2}\biggl|\frac{\delta y}{\Lambda^{2}}\biggr|^{1+Aqu}+O(u^{2}),

(35)

where we introduced

\displaystyle A\equiv\frac{3}{16}a_{4}\sqrt{r_{0}}=\frac{3}{128\pi^{2}}\sqrt{r_{0}}.

(36)

This analysis reveals that the exponent
\nu
, defined by the algebraic singularity of
\xi\approx|\delta y|^{-\nu}
, is modified by the interaction compared to its Gaussian value
\nu=1/2
. The latter is recovered here by setting
u=0
.
Although in Eq. (
35
) the coupling constant
u
is determined by the microscopic value entering the model, in order to determine the leading scaling behavior close to the critical line,
u
is effectively replaced by its renormalization-group fixed-point value
u^{*}
; we later argue that in the limit
N\to\infty
this value can be determined and is given by, c.f., Eq. (
54
). Accordingly, from Eq. (
35
), we conclude that the resulting exponent
\nu
takes the value

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{1+Aqu^{*}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\frac{\epsilon}{2}+O(\epsilon^{2})\right],

(37)

where
\epsilon=4-d
.

So far we have considered the effects of introducing the interactions within a perturbative expansion at the lowest order in the coupling constant
u
, eventually leading to expressions which are perturbative in the actual expansion parameter
\epsilon
. While the expansion in
u
can be easily generalized to the case of finite
N
, and may also be used also to analyze the effects of the transient part of the tadpole, the fixed-point value
u^{*}
of
u
is easily determined only for
N\to\infty
, as shown below. This is the reason why we primarily focus on the limit
N\rightarrow\infty
.
In the next section, we determine the exact exponents
\nu
and
\beta
for
N\to\infty
as functions of the spatial dimensionality
d
. In particular, the expansion of the expression of
\nu
for
d=4-\epsilon
at the first order in
\epsilon
renders, as expected, Eq. (
37
).

IV
Exact solution for
\nu

In the
N\rightarrow\infty
limit, the evolution equation of
G_{K}
derived from the action in Eq. (
II
) becomes exact and can be solved self-consistently. Noting that
\langle\phi_{q}^{2}\rangle=0

Kamenev 2011
we obtain

\begin{split}\big[\partial_{t}^{2}+k^{2}+r-r_{1}\cos(\omega t)&+\frac{u}{2}iG_{K}(x=0,t,t)\big]\\
&\times iG_{K}(k,t,t^{\prime})=0.\end{split}

(38)

Motivated by Eqs. (
19
) and (
20
), which show that

iG_{0K}(x=0,t,t)\propto\cos^{2}(\omega t/2)
, we make the ansatz that in the equation above one has

\displaystyle r-r_{1}\cos(\omega t)+\frac{u}{2}iG_{K}(x=0,t,t)

\displaystyle=r+\delta r-(r_{1}+\delta r_{1})\cos(\omega t),

(39)

where
iG_{K}
is the Keldysh function of the Gaussian model (
u=0
)
but calculated with the renormalized parameters

r+\delta r
and
r_{1}+\delta r_{1}
instead of
r
and
r_{1}
, respectively.
These new parameters also imply that the effectively Gaussian detuning from the critical line, determined from Eq. (
7
), takes the form

\displaystyle y=r+\delta r-\frac{r_{1}+\delta r_{1}}{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{4}.

(40)

Substituting Eq. (
12
) in Eq. (
39
), and using the long-time limit of
the tadpole in Eq. (
20
), we find that the self-consistency condition requires

\delta r=\widetilde{B}_{d}/2\quad\mbox{and}\quad\delta r_{1}=-\widetilde{B}_{d}/2,

(41)

where
\widetilde{B}_{d}
is given in Eq. (
21
).
Accordingly, the detuning in Eq. (
40
) becomes

\displaystyle y=r-\frac{r_{1}}{2}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{4}+\frac{3}{4}\widetilde{B}_{d}(y).

(42)

This equation provides the implicit relationship between the Gaussian detuning
y
and the control parameters
r
and
r_{1}
.
We also note here that within this effective Gaussian model with renormalized parameters, the spatial correlation length
\xi
is still related to
y
as
y=\xi^{-2}
.
Since the critical line corresponds to
y=0
, the critical values
r_{c}
and
r_{1c}
of the parameters
r
and
r_{1}
, respectively, satisfy Eq. (
42
) with
y=0
(equivalently
\xi=+\infty
), i.e.,
0=r_{c}-r_{1c}/2-\omega^{2}/4+(3/4)\widetilde{B}_{d}(0)
.
Subtracting this condition from Eq. (
42
), and using Eq. (
21
), we find the self-consistency condition in terms of the correlation length
\xi
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\xi^{-2}=r-r_{c}-\frac{r_{1}-r_{1c}}{2}+g\Lambda^{d-2}\biggl[\omega_{d}\left(\Lambda^{-1}/\xi\right)-\omega_{d}(0)\biggr],

(43)

where we introduced

\displaystyle g=\frac{3}{8}qu\sqrt{r_{0}},

(44)

and the function
\omega_{d}(z)
which captures the scaling behavior of
\widetilde{B}_{d}(\xi^{-2})
as

\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{f_{c}\left(k/\Lambda\right)}{k^{2}+\xi^{-2}}=\Lambda^{d-2}\omega_{d}\left(\Lambda^{-1}/\xi\right).

(45)

We emphasize here that Eq. (
43
) turns out to be independent of the driving frequency
\omega
which influences, instead, the location of the critical line. In fact, Eq. (
43
) has the same structure as the equation which controls the correlation length
\xi
in the undriven model, both after the quench
Chiocchetta et al. 2016
and in equilibrium
Moshe and Zinn-Justin 2003
.
In the latter context it was shown in Ref.
Moshe and Zinn-Justin 2003
that

\displaystyle\omega_{d}(z)=\omega_{d}(0)-K_{d}z^{d-2}+c_{d}z^{2}+\mbox{h.o.},

(46)

where
c_{d}
is a constant that depends on the cut-off function
f_{c}
in Eq. (
45
),
while
K_{d}
is a universal constant given by

\displaystyle K_{d}=a_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\frac{z^{d-1}}{z^{2}(1+z^{2})}=-a_{d}\frac{\pi/2}{\sin(\pi d/2)},

(47)

with
K_{d}>0
for
2<d<4
.
When
d<4
, the contribution from
c_{d}z^{2}
is subleading compared to
K_{d}z^{d-2}
for
z\to 0
. Accordingly, for
\xi\gg\Lambda^{-1}
, the term in brackets on r.h.s. of Eq. (
43
) is more relevant than that on the l.h.s. and the equation implies that

\xi\propto\left(r-r_{c}-\frac{r_{1}-r_{1c}}{2}\right)^{-\nu},

(48)

with

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{1}{d-2}.

(49)

This exponent
\nu
controls the divergence of the spatial correlation length of the fluctuations within the system as
r-r_{1}/2\to r_{c}-r_{1c}/2
, i.e., as the critical line in Fig.
1
(b) is approached.
For
d>4
, instead, the term
\propto z^{2}
in Eq. (
46
) is the dominant one for
z\to 0
and therefore in Eq. (
43
) the contributions
\propto\xi^{-2}
on the l.h.s. and r.h.s. are the relevant ones. Accordingly, one finds the same expression as in Eq. (
48
) with

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{1}{2}.

(50)

The predictions presented above for the leading scaling behavior and for the exponent
\nu
are actually independent of the coupling constant
g
of the model which can take any (positive) value. However, among these values, there is a specific one
g^{*}
for which the leading corrections to the scaling behavior discussed above do vanish. In the renormalization-group framework,
g^{*}
correspond to the fixed-point value of
g
, see, e.g., Ref.
Moshe and Zinn-Justin 2003
.
In particular, for
2<d<4
, the corrections to the scaling in Eq. (
48
) come from the term
\propto z^{2}
in Eq. (
46
) and from the analogous one on the l.h.s. of Eq. (
43
). Taking them into account, the consistency condition in Eq. (
43
) can then be written as

\displaystyle\xi^{-2}\biggl[1-gc_{d}\Lambda^{d-4}\biggr]=r-r_{c}-\frac{r_{1}-r_{1c}}{2}-gK_{d}\xi^{2-d}.

(51)

The expression of
\xi
which can be derived from this equation matches the scaling behavior in Eq. (
48
), i.e., with no leading corrections to scaling, only if the l.h.s. of the equation vanishes, i.e., for
g=g^{*}
, where

g^{*}=\Lambda^{4-d}/c_{d}.

(52)

While
c_{d}
is a non-universal constant which depends on the cutoff function
f_{c}
(see Eqs. (
46
) and (
45
)), it turns out
Moshe and Zinn-Justin 2003
that its limit for
d\to 4
, i.e.,
\epsilon\to 0
, is universal and given by
c_{4-\epsilon}=1/(8\pi^{2}\epsilon)+O(\epsilon^{0})
,
which renders

\displaystyle g^{*}=8\pi^{2}\epsilon+O(\epsilon^{2}),

(53)

in agreement with what is expected from renormalization-group arguments.
This fixed-point value
g^{*}
of the coupling constant
g
in the dimensional expansion can now be used in the perturbative expansions discussed in Sec.
III
to set the fixed-point value of the coupling constant
u
which, using Eq. (
44
), is given by

\displaystyle u^{*}=\frac{(8\pi)^{2}}{3q\sqrt{r_{0}}}\epsilon+O(\epsilon^{2}).

(54)

In particular, as anticipated in Sec.
III.2
, the perturbative prediction for
\nu
can be recovered from the expression in Eq. (
35
) if we substitute
u
by its fixed point value
u^{*}
, and assume
\epsilon\ll 1
. This leads to Eq. (
37
), which indeed coincides with the dimensional expansion of Eq. (
49
) for
d=4-\epsilon
at the first order in
\epsilon
. This is also similar to what happens in equilibrium.

In Sec.
VI
the perturbative fixed-point value
u^{*}
of
u
determined here will be used to provide a prediction for the scaling exponents which emerge in perturbation theory when studying the short-time behavior of
G_{R,K}
.

V
Exact Solution for
\beta

In order to extract the exponent
\beta
that governs the behavior of the order parameter close to criticality, we assume that the symmetry of the model is (spontaneously) broken along one spatial direction, i.e., that
\langle\phi_{i}(t)\rangle=\sqrt{N}M(t)\delta_{i,1}
for
i=1,\ldots,N
.
Then for
N\rightarrow\infty
, the equations of motion for
M
and
G_{K}
are as follows
Chiocchetta et al. 2016
;
Chandran and Sondhi 2016
:

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{t}^{2}+r-r_{1}\cos(\omega t)+uM^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{u}{2}iG_{K}(x=0,t,t)\biggr]M(t)=0,

(55a)

\displaystyle\biggl[\partial_{t}^{2}+k^{2}+r-r_{1}\cos(\omega t)+uM^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{u}{2}iG_{K}(x=0,t,t)\biggr]iG_{K}(k,t,t^{\prime})=0.

(55b)

Assuming that the magnetization
M
shows period doubling, we make the ansatz
M(t)=M_{0}\cos(\omega t/2)
and find that the self-consistent equations written above imply that (see Appendix
B
for details)

|M_{0}|\propto\sqrt{r_{c}-r+r_{1c}/2-r_{1}/2},

(56)

and therefore

\displaystyle\beta=1/2,

(57)

as for the model in equilibrium
Moshe and Zinn-Justin 2003
.

VI
Aging following a quench

In this section we discuss how the correlation functions behave in the transient regime following a quantum quench. For this, we consider the transient part of the tadpole given in Eq. (
22
) and we substitute it into Eqs. (
18a
) and (
18
), which are derived at the lowest order in perturbation theory.
We recall that in Secs.
III.2
,
IV
, and
V
we focused on the long-time behavior by neglecting this transient.
A key quantity that turns out to characterize this early-time regime is the constant
\theta
, defined perturbatively on the basis of the transient behavior
iT^{\prime}(t)
of the tadpole in Eq. (
22
). As discussed below, this constant is eventually the exponent which appears in the scaling of
G_{R,K}
.
By using the perturbative fixed-point value
u^{*}
of the coupling
u
in Eq. (
54
),
\theta
in Eq. (
22
) takes the value

\displaystyle\theta^{*}=\frac{\epsilon}{4}+O(\epsilon^{2}).

(58)

This exponent
\theta^{*}
is identical to the one obtained for the undriven model
Chiocchetta et al. 2015
;
Maraga et al. 2015
;
Chiocchetta et al. 2016
, in which the higher-order corrections
O(\epsilon^{2})
turn out to vanish as
N\rightarrow\infty
for
2<d<4
, providing an exact exponent
Maraga et al. 2015
.
In this section we discuss how this
\theta
appears in the driven problem.

In the transient regime, for
k=0
and
t\gg t^{\prime}
, but with both times longer than the drive period
T=2\pi/\omega
, we find that
G_{R}=G_{0R}+\delta G_{R}
becomes (see Appendix
C
for details)

\displaystyle G_{R}(k=0,t\gg t^{\prime},\omega t\gg\omega t^{\prime}\gg 1)

\displaystyle\quad=-qt\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)\left[1-\theta\ln\bigl(t/t^{\prime}\bigr)\right]

\displaystyle\quad\approx-qt\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)\bigl(t^{\prime}/t\bigr)^{\theta}.

(59)

Using Eq. (
58
), this implies that at the fixed point, the universal
exponent
\theta^{*}
governs the aging dynamics, which manifests itself via the dependence of
G_{R}
on
(t^{\prime}/t)^{\theta^{*}}
.
On the other hand for
t\gg t^{\prime}
, but with one time being smaller than the drive period
T
, and the other longer, i.e., for
\omega t\gg 1\gg\omega t^{\prime}
,
one finds (see Appendix
C
for details)

\displaystyle G_{R}(k=0,t\gg t^{\prime},\omega t\gg 1\gg\omega t^{\prime})=-qt\cos(\omega t/2)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)\biggl[1-\theta\ln{(\omega t)}+\frac{8}{3}\theta\ln{(\omega t^{\prime})}\biggr]

\displaystyle\qquad\approx-qt\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)(\omega t^{\prime})^{8\theta/3}/(\omega t)^{\theta}.

(60)

Equations (
59
) and (
60
) show that, due to the appearance of two exponents,
\theta
and
8\theta/3
, the functional form of the observed aging, and the exponents which control it, are modified depending on whether the system is probed at longer or shorter times compared to the drive period.
The resummation of the leading logarithmic behavior done in the previous equation, leading to an algebraic dependence, is consistent within perturbation theory but it certainly needs to be put on a firmer ground by an analytic solution or a full-fledged renormalization-group analysis of the problem, as was done in the absence of driving
Maraga et al. 2015
;
Chiocchetta et al. 2016
;
Chiocchetta et al. 2015
.

In a similar manner, the
Keldysh Green’s function in perturbation theory at one-loop, when probed at times longer than the drive period, turns out to be (see Appendix
D
for details)

\displaystyle iG_{K}(k=0,\omega t\gg 1,\omega t^{\prime}\gg 1)

\displaystyle\quad=q^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)\;tt^{\prime}\biggl[1-\theta\ln\bigl(\bar{\Lambda}^{2}tt^{\prime}\bigr)\biggr]

\displaystyle\quad\propto q^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)\bigl(\bar{\Lambda}^{2}tt^{\prime}\bigr)^{1-\theta}.

(61)

On the other hand, for
\omega t\gg 1\gg\omega t^{\prime}
, one finds
(see Appendix
D
)

\displaystyle iG_{K}(k=0,\omega t\gg 1\gg\omega t^{\prime})=q^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}\cos(\omega t/2)

\displaystyle\times\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)tt^{\prime}\biggl[1-\theta\ln(\bar{\Lambda}t)-\frac{8}{3}\theta\ln(\bar{\Lambda}t^{\prime})\biggr]

\displaystyle\propto q^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)(\bar{\Lambda}t)^{1-\theta}(\bar{\Lambda}t^{\prime})^{1-8\theta/3}.

(62)

Thus here too — with the same proviso as the one spelled out after Eq. (
60
) on the resummation of the logarithmic dependence — the perturbative expressions indicate that the aging behavior changes depending on whether the time is small or large compared to the period of the drive.

Now we discuss the behavior of
iG_{K}(k,t,t)
for

. Within the Gaussian approximation in

12

upon increasing

In particular we have (see Appendix

\displaystyle iG_{K}(k,t,t)\approx q^{2}\cos^{2}(\omega t/2)\frac{\sqrt{r_{0}}}{\bar{k}^{2-2\theta}}\sin^{2}(\bar{k}t).

(63)

The expressions above indicate that, for a quantum quench to the FTC critical line, the correlators at long wavelengths

\displaystyle G_{K}(k,t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)\frac{1}{k^{2-2\theta}}{\cal G}_{K}(kt,kt^{\prime},\omega t^{\prime}),

(64)

\displaystyle G_{R}(k,t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)\frac{\bigl(t^{\prime}/t\bigr)^{\theta}}{k}{\cal G}_{R}(kt,kt^{\prime},\omega t^{\prime}).

In order for these expressions to render the behaviors discussed above, we assume

VII
Spatial structure due to resonances

We now discuss the effects on the dynamics of adding a perturbing potential of the form

\displaystyle V_{p}(t)=a_{p}\left[1-2\cos(\omega t)\right],

(66)

with
a_{p}/\omega^{2}\ll 1

does not change the value of

Accordingly, the Floquet quasi-modes for

However, here we address a different question: assuming that the

VIII
Conclusions

In this paper we have studied the physics of a
prethermal Floquet time crystal (FTC) in the vicinity of the critical line that separates it from a trivial phase. The results are derived for the periodically driven
O(N)
model with
N\to\infty
. Exact results for the exponents
\nu
and
\beta
are derived in the long-time (but still prethermal) regime. Results are also obtained in the transient regime following a quantum quench where the system shows aging dynamics controlled by an exponent
\theta
. The results in this transient regime are obtained to the leading order in
\epsilon=4-d
.

The critical exponents
\nu
(see Eq. (
49
)),
\beta
(see Eq. (
57
)), and
\theta
(see Eq. (
58
)) are found to be the same as those of the undriven problem, provided that the system is probed at times longer than the drive period
T
(see Eqs. (
59
), (
61
)). At times shorter than
T
, a perturbative treatment reveals that a different scaling behavior possibly emerges
(see Eqs. (
60
), (
62
)).

FTCs always arise when the drive is resonant with some microscopic scale of the undriven problem. We showed that this leads to certain peculiarities such as the linear response of the system to a time-dependent perturbing potential can lead to spatial oscillations controlled by the drive frequency and the quasiparticle velocity.

Our study leaves a number of open questions for further investigations. Solving numerically the dynamics of the system near the FTC critical line and extracting from these solutions the exponents would be a useful exercise. Carrying out a complete renormalization-group analysis which not only renders the exponents predicted here, but generalizes them to finite
N
is a direction worth pursuing together with an exact analysis of the short-time behavior for
N\to\infty
.

Finally, exploring other universality classes of FTCs, especially those where the exponents
\nu
,
\beta
, and
\theta
may be different from the undriven situation, is an exciting direction of research. Investigating critical properties even of undriven systems
Wilczek 2012
;
Shapere and Wilczek 2012
;
Li et al. 2012
, or non-Hermitian systems
Buca et al. 2019
, which can nevertheless show time-crystal behavior
Iemini et al. 2018
;
Prokof’ev and Svistunov 2020
;
Else et al. 2017
;
Else et al. 2020
;
Khemani et al. 2019
;
Kozin and Kyriienko 2019
;
Khemani et al. 2020
;
Kozin and Kyriienko 2020
;
Öhberg and Wright 2019
;
Syrwid et al. 2020
;
Öhberg and Wright 2020
despite no-go theorems
Bruno 2013a
;
Bruno 2013b
;
Bruno 2013c
;
Watanabe and Oshikawa 2015
, is also an important open question.

Appendix C

We now study the one-loop correction to the retarded Green’s function in Eq. (

\displaystyle\delta iG_{K}(k=0,t,t^{\prime})=-\frac{8\theta}{3}q^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\biggl[\frac{3}{8}+\frac{1}{2}\cos(\omega\tau)+\frac{1}{8}\cos(2\omega\tau)\biggr](tt^{\prime}-2t^{\prime}\tau)

\displaystyle\times\frac{4\bar{\Lambda}^{2}\tau}{1+(2\bar{\Lambda}\tau)^{2}}+\left(t\leftrightarrow t^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{8\theta}{3}q^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\biggl[\frac{\omega}{2}\sin(\omega\tau)+\frac{\omega}{4}\sin(2\omega\tau)\biggr](tt^{\prime}\tau-t^{\prime}\tau^{2})\frac{4\bar{\Lambda}^{2}\tau}{1+(2\bar{\Lambda}\tau)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left(t\leftrightarrow t^{\prime}\right).

(105)

At times which are large as compared to
\bar{\Lambda}^{-1}
, the dominant terms are those which give logarithmic corrections. These in particular arise from
the first and second lines above. Among them, it is the second term in the numerator
\propto 2t^{\prime}\tau,2t\tau
that dominates. Keeping only these dominant terms,
and approximating
\frac{4\bar{\Lambda}^{2}\tau}{1+(2\bar{\Lambda}\tau)^{2}}\approx 1/\tau^{2}
we obtain

\displaystyle\delta iG_{K}(k=0,t,t^{\prime})\approx-\frac{8\theta}{3}q^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}tt^{\prime}\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)

\displaystyle\times\int_{\bar{\Lambda}^{-1}}^{t}d\tau\biggl[\frac{3}{8}+\frac{1}{2}\cos(\omega\tau)+\frac{1}{8}\cos(2\omega\tau)\biggr]\frac{1}{\tau}+\left(t\leftrightarrow t^{\prime}\right).

(106)

Performing the integrals, one obtains

\displaystyle\delta iG_{K}(k=0,t,t^{\prime})\approx-\frac{8\theta}{3}q^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}tt^{\prime}\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)

\displaystyle\times\biggl\{\frac{3}{8}\ln\bigl(tt^{\prime}\bar{\Lambda}^{2}\bigr)+\frac{1}{2}\biggl[{\rm Ci}(\omega t)-{\rm Ci}(\omega/\bar{\Lambda})\biggr]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8}\biggl[{\rm Ci}(2\omega t)-{\rm Ci}(2\omega/\bar{\Lambda})\biggr]+\frac{1}{2}\biggl[{\rm Ci}(\omega t^{\prime})-{\rm Ci}(\omega/\bar{\Lambda})\biggr]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8}\biggl[{\rm Ci}(2\omega t^{\prime})-{\rm Ci}(2\omega/\bar{\Lambda})\biggr]\biggr\}.

(107)

For
\omega t\gg 1,\omega t^{\prime}\gg 1
the terms involving the cosine-integral
{\rm Ci}(x)
do not give any logarithmic corrections. Noting that the Green’s function at the Gaussian level is

\displaystyle iG_{0K}(k=0,t,t^{\prime})=q^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)\,\,tt^{\prime},

(108)

the leading logarithmic singularities and the correction discussed above modify the interacting Green’s function at one-loop as

\displaystyle\!\!iG_{K}(k=0,\omega t\gg 1,\omega t^{\prime}\gg 1)\!\!=q^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)

\displaystyle\times tt^{\prime}\biggl[1-\theta\ln\bigl(\bar{\Lambda}^{2}tt^{\prime}\bigr)\biggr]

(109)

\displaystyle\propto q^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)\bigl(\bar{\Lambda}^{2}tt^{\prime}\bigr)^{1-\theta}.

(110)

The above is Eq. (
61
) in the main text.

On the other hand if
\omega t\gg 1\gg\omega t^{\prime}
, then, some of the cosine-integrals do contribute with a logarithm singularity, giving

\displaystyle\delta iG_{K}(k=0,\omega t\gg 1\gg\omega t^{\prime})\approx-\frac{8\theta}{3}q^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}tt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\times\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)\biggl[\frac{3}{8}\ln(\bar{\Lambda}t)+\ln(\bar{\Lambda}t^{\prime})\biggr],

(111)

where we have used that
{\rm Ci}(\omega/\bar{\Lambda})\approx\ln(\omega/\bar{\Lambda})
for
\omega\ll\bar{\Lambda}
.
Thus

\displaystyle iG_{K}(k=0,\omega t\gg 1\gg\omega t^{\prime})=q^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)

\displaystyle\times tt^{\prime}\biggl[1-\theta\ln(\bar{\Lambda}t)-\frac{8\theta}{3}\ln(\bar{\Lambda}t^{\prime})\biggr]

(112)

\displaystyle\approx q^{2}\sqrt{r_{0}}\cos(\omega t/2)\cos(\omega t^{\prime}/2)(\bar{\Lambda}t)^{1-\theta}(\bar{\Lambda}t^{\prime})^{1-8\theta/3}.

(113)

The above is Eq. (
62
) in the main text.

Appendix E
Keldysh Green’s function: Long-time behavior

At the Gaussian level, the equal-time Keldysh Green’s function is

\displaystyle iG_{0K}(k,t,t)=q^{2}\cos^{2}(\omega t/2)\frac{\sqrt{r_{0}}}{\bar{k}^{2}}\sin^{2}(\bar{k}t).

(114)

We will now study the one-loop correction to this quantity in the limit of
\bar{k}t\gg 1
.
Using Eq. (
18
) and Eq. (
80
) along with the identity Eq. (
102
), we obtain

\displaystyle\delta iG_{K}(k,t,t)=\frac{16\theta}{3}q^{2}\frac{\sqrt{r_{0}}}{\bar{k}^{3}}\cos^{2}(\omega t/2)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\cos^{4}(\omega\tau/2)\sin(\bar{k}(t-\tau))\sin(\bar{k}t)\sin(\bar{k}\tau)

\displaystyle\times\frac{d}{d\tau}\biggl[\frac{4\bar{\Lambda}^{2}\tau}{1+(2\bar{\Lambda}\tau)^{2}}\biggr].

(115)

Above when we expand
\sin(\bar{k}(t-\tau))
we only keep the
\sin(\bar{k}t)\cos(\bar{k}\tau)
term
as this is the term that is proportional to the Gaussian Green’s function
G_{0K}
.
Thus

\displaystyle\delta iG_{K}(k,t,t)\approx\frac{8\theta}{3}q^{2}\frac{\sqrt{r_{0}}}{\bar{k}^{3}}\sin^{2}(\bar{k}t)\cos^{2}(\omega t/2)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\cos^{4}(\omega\tau/2)\sin(2\bar{k}\tau)\frac{d}{d\tau}\biggl[\frac{4\bar{\Lambda}^{2}\tau}{1+(2\bar{\Lambda}\tau)^{2}}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{8\theta}{3}q^{2}\frac{\sqrt{r_{0}}}{\bar{k}^{3}}\sin^{2}(\bar{k}t)\cos^{2}(\omega t/2)

\displaystyle\times\biggl[\cos^{4}(\omega t/2)\sin(2\bar{k}t)\frac{4\bar{\Lambda}^{2}t}{1+(2\bar{\Lambda}t)^{2}}

\displaystyle-2\bar{k}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\cos^{4}(\omega\tau/2)\cos(2\bar{k}\tau)\frac{4\bar{\Lambda}^{2}\tau}{1+(2\bar{\Lambda}\tau)^{2}}

\displaystyle+2\omega\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\cos^{3}(\omega\tau/2)\sin(\omega\tau/2)\cos(2\bar{k}\tau)\frac{4\bar{\Lambda}^{2}\tau}{1+(2\bar{\Lambda}\tau)^{2}}\biggr].

(116)

For
\bar{\Lambda}t\gg 1
, i.e., for times that are long as compared to the microscopic scale
\bar{\Lambda}^{-1}
, the first term falls off at long times as
1/t
, and therefore we drop it.
Collecting the remaining terms, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta iG_{K}(k,t,t)\approx\frac{8\theta}{3}q^{2}\frac{\sqrt{r_{0}}}{\bar{k}^{3}}\sin^{2}(\bar{k}t)\cos^{2}(\omega t/2)

\displaystyle\times\biggl\{-2\bar{k}\int_{{\bar{\Lambda}^{-1}}}^{t}d\tau\biggl[\frac{3}{8}+\frac{1}{2}\cos(\omega\tau)+\frac{1}{8}\cos(2\omega\tau)\biggr]\cos(2\bar{k}\tau)\frac{1}{\tau}

\displaystyle+\omega\int_{{\bar{\Lambda}^{-1}}}^{t}d\tau\biggl[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\cos(\omega\tau)\biggr]\sin(\omega\tau)\cos(2\bar{k}\tau)\frac{1}{\tau}\biggr\}.

(117)

Note that the second term does not give any logarithmic contributions.
We then define
T(\alpha)
for
\alpha\ll\bar{\Lambda}

\displaystyle T(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bar{\Lambda}^{-1}}}^{t}d\tau\frac{\cos(\alpha\tau)}{\tau}={\rm Ci}(\alpha t)-{\rm Ci}(\alpha/\bar{\Lambda})

\displaystyle\approx-\gamma-\ln(\alpha/\bar{\Lambda})+{\rm Ci}(\alpha t),

(118)

to write

\displaystyle\delta iG_{K}(k,t,t)\approx-\frac{16\theta}{3}q^{2}\frac{\sqrt{r_{0}}}{\bar{k}^{2}}\sin^{2}(\bar{k}t)\cos^{2}(\omega t/2)

\displaystyle\times\biggl[\frac{3}{8}T(2\bar{k})+\frac{1}{4}T(2\bar{k}+\omega)+\frac{1}{4}T(2\bar{k}-\omega)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{16}T(2\bar{k}+2\omega)+\frac{1}{16}T(2\bar{k}-2\omega)\biggr].

(119)

Now at times longer than
\alpha^{-1}
, where
\alpha=2\bar{k},\bigl|2\bar{k}\pm\omega\bigr|
, and
\bigl|2\bar{k}\pm 2\omega\bigr|
, the
{\rm Ci}(\alpha t)
terms can be dropped because they decrease as a power-law in
\alpha t
. Thus at long-times we obtain

\displaystyle\delta iG_{K}(k,t,t)\approx iG_{0K}(k,t,t)\frac{16\theta}{3}\biggl[\frac{3}{8}\ln(\bar{k}/\bar{\Lambda})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\ln((2\bar{k}+\omega)/\bar{\Lambda})+\frac{1}{4}\ln((2\bar{k}-\omega)/\bar{\Lambda})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{16}\ln((2\bar{k}+2\omega)/\bar{\Lambda})+\frac{1}{16}\ln((2\bar{k}-2\omega)/\bar{\Lambda})\biggr].

(120)

In the long wavelength limit

\bar{k}\ll\omega
, only the first logarithm dominates in the correction above. Therefore the resulting
G_{K}
can be written as

\displaystyle iG_{K}(k,t,t;\bar{k}\ll\omega)\approx q^{2}\frac{\sqrt{r_{0}}}{\bar{k}^{2-2\theta}}\sin^{2}(\bar{k}t)\cos^{2}(\omega t/2).

(121)

The above is Eq. (
63
) in the main text.

Appendix F
Derivation of Eq. (
69
)

In order to determine the asymptotic behavior of the Green’s functions discussed in Sec.
VII
, it is sufficient to study the terms with
m=\pm 1
in Eq. (
68
), as the behavior for
m=\pm 2
follows in a straightforward manner.
In addition, Eqs. (
68
) have terms that are resonant due to their denominators being proportional to
\bar{k}-m\omega/2
. In doing the Fourier transform in space for highlighting the structure of the spatial correlations, these resonant terms dominate over the non-resonant terms. Accordingly, the analysis below focuses only on the resonant contributions.

Using Eq. (
15
), the spatial Fourier transform in
d=4
requires computing

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{R,K}(x,t,t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dkk^{2}J_{1}(kx)I_{R,K}(k,t,t^{\prime}),

(122)

where
{\cal I}_{R,K}(x,t,t^{\prime})
contribute to the one loop corrections
G_{R,K}^{(1)}
and

\displaystyle I_{R}(k,t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=\pm 1}\cos(m\omega(t+t^{\prime})/2)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\sin((k-m\omega/2)(t-t^{\prime}))}{k^{2}(k-m\omega/2)},

(123)

\displaystyle I_{K}(k,t,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=\pm 1}\frac{1}{k^{3}(2k-m\omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl\{[1+\cos(m\omega t)][1-\cos(2kt)]

\displaystyle\quad-\sin(2kt)\sin(m\omega t)\biggr\}.

(124)

Above we have kept only the resonant terms in Eq. (
68
). For notational convenience, we have also set
v=1
, so that
\bar{k}=k
.

F.1
Evaluation of
{\cal I}_{R}

In order to evaluate
{\cal I}_{R}
, we perform a shift of variables
k-m\omega/2\rightarrow k
and obtain

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{R}(x,t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\cos(\omega(t+t^{\prime})/2)\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x}

\displaystyle\times\biggl[\int_{-\omega/2}^{\Lambda-\omega/2}dk\frac{\sin(k(t-t^{\prime}))}{k}J_{1}(kx+\omega x/2)

\displaystyle+\int_{\omega/2}^{\Lambda+\omega/2}dk\frac{\sin(k(t-t^{\prime}))}{k}J_{1}(kx-\omega x/2)\biggr]

\displaystyle\approx\cos(\omega(t+t^{\prime})/2)\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x}

\displaystyle\times\biggl\{\int_{-\omega/2}^{0}dk\frac{\sin(k(t-t^{\prime}))}{k}J_{1}(kx+\omega x/2)

\displaystyle+\int_{\omega/2}^{0}dk\frac{\sin(k(t-t^{\prime}))}{k}J_{1}(kx-\omega x/2)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dk\frac{\sin(k(t-t^{\prime}))}{k}\biggl[J_{1}(kx+\omega x/2)

\displaystyle+J_{1}(kx-\omega x/2)\biggr]\biggr\},

(125)

where in the last term we have used that
\Lambda\gg\omega
.
In the first term of Eq. (
125
), we perform the
transformation
k\rightarrow-k
and use that
J_{1}(-x)=-J_{1}(x)
to write

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{R}(x,t,t^{\prime})=\cos(\omega(t+t^{\prime})/2)\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x}

\displaystyle\times\biggl\{-2\int^{\omega/2}_{0}dk\frac{\sin(k(t-t^{\prime}))}{k}J_{1}(kx-\omega x/2)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dk\frac{\sin(k(t-t^{\prime}))}{k}\biggl[J_{1}(kx+\omega x/2)

\displaystyle+J_{1}(kx-\omega x/2)\biggr]\biggr\}.

(126)

Following this, we use that
J_{1}
may be replaced by its asymptotic value
J_{1}(kx+\omega x/2)=-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi(kx+\omega x/2)}}\cos(kx+\omega x/2+\pi/4)
for
\omega x\gg 1
.
This gives

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{R}(x,t,t^{\prime})\approx\cos(\omega(t+t^{\prime})/2)\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\times\biggl\{2\int^{\omega/2}_{0}dk\frac{\sin(k(t-t^{\prime}))}{k}\frac{\cos(kx-\omega x/2+\pi/4)}{\sqrt{k-\omega/2}}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dk\frac{\sin(k(t-t^{\prime}))}{k}\biggl[\frac{\cos(kx+\omega x/2+\pi/4)}{\sqrt{k+\omega/2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\cos(kx-\omega x/2+\pi/4)}{\sqrt{k-\omega/2}}\biggr]\biggr\}.

(127)

We now discuss the two non-trivial cases, i.e., the behavior on the light-cone and the behavior inside it.

On the light-cone, since
x=t-t^{\prime}
, (and
t>t^{\prime}
due to the causal structure of the retarded Green’s function)
we write

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{R}(x=t-t^{\prime})\approx\cos(\omega(t+t^{\prime})/2)\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\times\biggl\{2\int^{\omega/2}_{0}dk\frac{\sin(kx)}{k}\frac{\cos(kx-\omega x/2+\pi/4)}{\sqrt{k-\omega/2}}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dk\frac{\sin(kx)}{k}\biggl[\frac{\cos(kx+\omega x/2+\pi/4)}{\sqrt{k+\omega/2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\cos(kx-\omega x/2+\pi/4)}{\sqrt{k-\omega/2}}\biggr]\biggr\}

\displaystyle\approx\cos(\omega(t+t^{\prime})/2)\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\times\biggl\{2\sin(\omega x/2-\pi/4)\int^{\omega/2}_{0}dk\frac{\sin^{2}(kx)}{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k-\omega/2}}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dk\frac{\sin^{2}(kx)}{k}\biggl[-\frac{\sin(\omega x/2+\pi/4)}{\sqrt{k+\omega/2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sin(\omega x/2-\pi/4)}{\sqrt{k-\omega/2}}\biggr]\biggr\}.

(128)

In the last equality of the previous equation we have only kept terms that oscillate in phase.
Since this expression is dominated by
k
away from
\pm\omega/2
we may write

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{R}(x=t-t^{\prime})\approx\cos(\omega(t+t^{\prime})/2)\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{x^{1/2}}{4\pi^{2}x^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\times\biggl\{2\sin(\omega x/2-\pi/4)\int^{\omega x/2}_{0}dy\frac{\sin^{2}(y)}{y}\frac{1}{\sqrt{y}}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\Lambda x}dy\frac{\sin^{2}(y)}{y}\biggl[-\frac{\sin(\omega x/2+\pi/4)}{\sqrt{y}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sin(\omega x/2-\pi/4)}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr]\biggr\}.

(129)

Performing the
y
-integral we find

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{R}(x=t-t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\propto\cos(\omega(t+t^{\prime})/2)\sin(\omega x/2+\delta)\frac{1}{x^{3/2}},

(130)

where
\delta
stands for a constant phase shift that originates from the
\pi/4
phase in the asymptotic expansion of the Bessel function.

For studying the behavior of
{\cal I}_{R}
at points away from the light cone, let us introduce
y=k(t-t^{\prime})
. Then Eq. (
127
) becomes

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{R}(x,t,t^{\prime})=\cos(\omega(t+t^{\prime})/2)\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x}

\displaystyle\times\biggl\{-2\int^{\omega(t-t^{\prime})/2}_{0}dy\frac{\sin(y)}{y}J_{1}(yx/(t-t^{\prime})-\omega x/2)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\Lambda(t-t^{\prime})}dy\frac{\sin(y)}{y}\biggl[J_{1}(yx/(t-t^{\prime})+\omega x/2)

\displaystyle+J_{1}(yx/(t-t^{\prime})-\omega x/2)\biggr]\biggr\}.

(131)

When
x\ll t-t^{\prime}
, and since
\omega(t-t^{\prime}),\Lambda(t-t^{\prime})\gg 1
, the upper limits of integration in the expression above can be set to
\infty
. We can also approximate

J_{1}(yx/(t-t^{\prime})+\omega x/2)\approx J_{1}(\omega x/2)
.
Moreover, in the
second term above, the two Bessel functions cancel each other giving

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{R}(x\ll t-t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\cos(\omega(t+t^{\prime})/2)\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x}

\displaystyle\times J_{1}(\omega x/2)\biggl(2\int^{\infty}_{0}dy\frac{\sin y}{y}\biggr).

(132)

Accordingly, inside the light-cone, we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{R}(x\ll t-t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\cos(\omega(t+t^{\prime})/2)\frac{1}{4\pi x}J_{1}(\omega x/2)

\displaystyle\propto\cos(\omega(t+t^{\prime})/2)\sin(\omega x/2+\widetilde{\delta})\frac{1}{x^{3/2}},

(133)

where in the last line we have used the asymptotic form of
J_{1}(\omega x/2)
and
\widetilde{\delta}
denotes a constant phase, which as before, originates from the
\pi/4
phase coming from the asymptotic expansion of the Bessel function.
Thus, together with Eq. (
130
) and Eq. (
133
), we have recovered the asymptotic form
reported in Eq. (
69
) for the retarded Green’s function.

F.2
Evaluation of
{\cal I}_{K}

We now derive the asymptotic behavior of the equal-time Keldysh Green’s function at one loop.
Using Eq. (
F
) and assuming
\Lambda\gg\omega
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{K}(x,t,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x}\int_{-\omega/2}^{\Lambda}dk\frac{1}{2k}\biggl\{\sin(2kt)\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle+[1-\cos(2kt)][1+\cos(\omega t)]\biggr\}\frac{J_{1}(kx+\omega x/2)}{k+\omega/2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x}\int_{\omega/2}^{\Lambda}dk\frac{1}{2k}\biggl\{-\sin(2kt)\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle+[1-\cos(2kt)][1+\cos(\omega t)]\biggr\}\frac{J_{1}(kx-\omega x/2)}{k-\omega/2}.

(134)

As before, we split the integral as follows:

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{K}(x,t,t)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x}\int_{-\omega/2}^{0}dk\frac{1}{2k}\biggl\{\sin(2kt)\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle+[1-\cos(2kt)][1+\cos(\omega t)]\biggr\}\frac{J_{1}(kx+\omega x/2)}{k+\omega/2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x}\int_{0}^{\omega/2}dk\frac{1}{2k}\biggl\{\sin(2kt)\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle-[1-\cos(2kt)][1+\cos(\omega t)]\biggr\}\frac{J_{1}(kx-\omega x/2)}{k-\omega/2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dk\frac{1}{2k}\biggl\{\frac{J_{1}(kx+\omega x/2)}{k+\omega/2}\biggl[\sin(2kt)\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle+(1-\cos(2kt))(1+\cos(\omega t))\biggr]

\displaystyle+\frac{J_{1}(kx-\omega x/2)}{k-\omega/2}\biggl[-\sin(2kt)\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle+(1-\cos(2kt))(1+\cos(\omega t))\biggr]\biggr\}.

(135)

Performing the transformation
k\rightarrow-k
in the first term and noting that
J_{1}(-x)=-J_{1}(x)
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{K}(x,t,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}x}\int_{0}^{\omega/2}dk\frac{1}{2k}\biggl\{\sin(2kt)\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle-[1-\cos(2kt)][1+\cos(\omega t)]\biggr\}\frac{J_{1}(kx-\omega x/2)}{k-\omega/2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dk\frac{1}{2k}\biggl\{\frac{J_{1}(kx+\omega x/2)}{k+\omega/2}\biggl[\sin(2kt)\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle+(1-\cos(2kt))(1+\cos(\omega t))\biggr]

\displaystyle+\frac{J_{1}(kx-\omega x/2)}{k-\omega/2}\biggl[-\sin(2kt)\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle+(1-\cos(2kt))(1+\cos(\omega t))\biggr]\biggr\}.

(136)

We now separately discuss the behavior on the light-cone and inside the light-cone.
On the light-cone, since
x=2t
, we write Eq. (
136
) as

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{K}(x=2t)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\omega t}dy\frac{1}{2y}\biggl\{\sin y\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle-(1-\cos y)[1+\cos(\omega t)]\biggr\}\frac{J_{1}(y-\omega x/2)}{y-\omega x/2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\Lambda x}dy\frac{1}{2y}\biggl\{\frac{J_{1}(y+\omega x/2)}{y+\omega x/2}\biggl[\sin y\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle+(1-\cos y)(1+\cos(\omega t))\biggr]

\displaystyle+\frac{J_{1}(y-\omega x/2)}{y-\omega x/2}\biggl[-\sin y\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle+(1-\cos y)(1+\cos(\omega t))\biggr]\biggr\}.

(137)

Replacing
J_{1}
by its leading asymptotic form we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{K}(x=2t)=-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\omega t}dy\frac{1}{2y}\biggl\{\sin y\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle-(1-\cos y)[1+\cos(\omega t)]\biggr\}\frac{\cos(y-\omega x/2+\pi/4)}{(y-\omega x/2)^{3/2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\Lambda x}dy\frac{1}{2y}\biggl\{\frac{\cos(y+\omega x/2+\pi/4)}{(y+\omega x/2)^{3/2}}\biggl[\sin y\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle+(1-\cos y)(1+\cos(\omega t))\biggr]

\displaystyle+\frac{\cos(y-\omega x/2+\pi/4)}{(y-\omega x/2)^{3/2}}\biggl[-\sin y\sin(\omega t)

\displaystyle+(1-\cos y)(1+\cos(\omega t))\biggr]\biggr\}.

(138)

This equation implies

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{K}(x=2t)

\displaystyle\propto\frac{\cos(\omega x/2+\alpha^{\prime\prime})}{x^{3/2}},

(139)

where
\alpha^{\prime\prime}
is a constant phase-shift originating from the
\pi/4
phase in the asymptotic expansion of the Bessel function.

Now we discuss the behavior inside the light-cone, corresponding to having
2t\gg x
. Here we may replace
1-\cos(2kt)\approx 1
, obtaining

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{K}(2t\gg x)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{1}^{\Lambda x}dy\frac{1}{2y}\biggl[\frac{J_{1}(y+\omega x/2)}{y+\omega x/2}

\displaystyle+\frac{J_{1}(y-\omega x/2)}{y-\omega x/2}\biggr]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{1}^{\omega x/2}dy\frac{1}{2y}\frac{J_{1}(y-\omega x/2)}{y-\omega x/2}.

(140)

Let us assume
\omega x\gg 1
, so that we can set both the upper-limits of integration
\Lambda x=\omega x=\infty
. Then

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{K}(2t\gg x)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}dy\frac{1}{2y}\biggl[\frac{J_{1}(y+\omega x/2)}{y+\omega x/2}

\displaystyle-\frac{J_{1}(y-\omega x/2)}{y-\omega x/2}\biggr]

\displaystyle\propto\frac{\cos(\omega x/2+\alpha^{\prime\prime\prime})}{x^{3/2}}.

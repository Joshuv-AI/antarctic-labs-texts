Koszul duality for categories with a fixed object set
Hadrien Espic

Abstract
We define a notion of Koszul dual of a monoid object in a monoidal biclosed model category. Our construction generalizes the classic Yoneda algebra Ext A ​ ( 𝕜 , 𝕜 ) \mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\Bbbk,\Bbbk) . We apply this general construction to define the Koszul dual of a category enriched over spectra or chain complexes. This example relies on the classical observation that enriched categories are monoid objects in a category of enriched graphs. We observe that the category of enriched graphs is biclosed, meaning that it comes with both left and right internal hom objects. Given a category R R (which plays the role of the ground field 𝕜 \Bbbk in classical algebra), and an augmented R R -algebra C C , we define the Koszul dual of C C , denoted K ⁡ ( C ) K(C) , as the R R -algebra of derived endomorphisms of R R in the category of right C C -modules. We establish the expected adjunctions between the categories of modules over C C and modules over K ⁡ ( C ) K(C) . We investigate the question of when the map from C C to its double dual K ⁡ ( K ⁡ ( C ) ) K(K(C)) is an equivalence. We also show that Koszul duality of operads can be understood as a special case of Koszul duality of categories. In this way we incorporate Koszul duality of operads in a wider context, and possibly clarify some aspects of it.



Introduction

In this paper we develop and study a notion of Koszul duality for enriched categories with a fixed object set
S
, by way of a generalization of the Yoneda algebra construction to that setting. Let
C
be a spectrally enriched category. Assuming
C
is augmented, in a sense to be made precise below, we associate to
C
another spectrally enriched category
K(C)
, with the same object set as
C
. The category
K(C)
plays the role of the (derived) Koszul dual of
C
. We show that Koszul duality of operads (in spectra or in chain complexes) can be understood as a special case of our duality of categories. We believe that this can be applied to the Orthogonal Calculus of functors developed by Michael Weiss in
[
Wei95
]
, in a similar way that the Koszul duality of operads appears in Goodwillie Calculus (see
[
AC11
]
for example).

Our definition of the Koszul dual of an enriched category with object set
S
starts from the following standard idea. For
(\mathcal{V},\wedge,1)
a pointed cofibrantly generated symmetric monoidal model category, one can consider the category
\mathcal{V}^{S\times S}
of
\mathcal{V}
-graphs with vertex set
S
, which we will call precategories with object set
S
. This category of graphs has a monoidal structure given by the composition product

(X\circ Y)(i,j)=\bigvee_{k\in S}X(i,k)\wedge Y(k,j)

and the unit
I
defined by
I(i,j)=*
for
i\neq j
and
I(i,i)=1
. We explain in section 2 how this gives
\mathcal{V}^{S\times S}
a structure of cofibrantly generated monoidal model category, whose category of monoids is exactly that of
\mathcal{V}
-categories with object set
S
. This point of view on categories is known (dating back to MacLane
[
ML13
]
, II.7) and is discussed in section 6 of
[
SS03a
]
for example. However, it is usually not emphasized (to the author’s knowledge) that this category is biclosed, meaning the functors
X\circ-
and
-\circ X
have right adjoints
\hom^{l}(X,-)
and
\hom^{r}(X,-)
.

\displaystyle\hom^{r}(X,Y)(i,j)=\prod_{k\in S}\mathrm{map}(X(j,k),Y(i,k))

\displaystyle\hom^{l}(X,Y)(i,j)=\prod_{k\in S}\mathrm{map}(X(k,i),Y(k,j))

The existence of these left and right hom objects is what makes a generalization of the Yoneda algebra possible. Contrast this with the case of operads, where the monoidal category of symmetric sequences only has right hom objects, but no left hom objects.

This then fits in the more general framework discussed in section 1. If
R
is a monoid in an arbitrary cofibrantly generated monoidal biclosed model category
(\mathscr{C},\otimes,1)
and
C
is an
R
-algebra, we can define the Koszul dual of
C
by

K(C)=\mathbb{R}\hom_{C}(R,R)

This object comes equipped with a natural multiplicative structure given by the composition of right
C
-endomorphisms.

Another approach that is popular (especially in the setting of
\infty
-categories, see section 5.2 of
[
Lur12
]
) for tackling Koszul duality in a general setting is taking the dual of the bar construction
B(R,C,R)
, or equivalently just saying that the bar construction is the Koszul dual coalgebra of
C
. It relies on the existence of a comultiplicative structure on the bar construction (which in general seems to only be an
A_{\infty}
-coalgebra structure), and makes obtaining a map from
C
into its double dual a non-trivial problem. Our approach has the advantage that the
R
-algebra structure is straightforward and that an algebra map from
C
into its double dual is easily obtained. However, our
K(C)
has the drawback of neither being augmented nor being functorial, although it is both of these up to homotopy. Let us remark though that even though
K(C)
is not augmented, it has enough structure for us to provide a well-defined notion of double dual
KK(C)
. We give some basic examples in section 2.4 and also mention what we believe could be an interesting application of our construction to the category of finite sets and injections
FI
.

In section 3 we investigate the connection of Koszul duality of categories to Koszul duality of operads. Thus we consider the case where
R
is the symmetric groupoid, and
C
is the prop associated to a
\mathcal{V}
-operad. Here
\mathcal{V}
can be any symmetric monoidal stable model category, such as the category of chain complexes or spectra. Regarding spectra, for technical reasons we choose to work with the category of
\mathbb{S}
-modules from
[
EKMM97
]
, also known as EKMM spectra. But we believe that our results should hold with any of the highly structured model categories of spectra described, for example, in
[
MMSS01
]
. We obtain the following result, which compares our construction with Michael Ching’s coalgebra structure on
BP
from
[
Chi05
]
.

Theorem 1

(Proposition
3.19
)
.

If
P
is a reduced and levelwise finite operad in
\mathbb{S}
-modules, then there is a zigzag of weak equivalences of
\Sigma
-algebras

K({\mathrm{prop}\,}P)\sim{\mathrm{prop}\,}((BP)^{\sharp})^{\mathrm{op}}

Here
(BP)^{\sharp}
denotes the arity-wise Spanier-Whitehead dual of
BP
. The algebra structure on the target is induced by the cooperad structure on

and the fact that the functor
{\mathrm{prop}\,}
is monoidal (Proposition

). Let us mention as well that our proof of this result is constructive, building explicit
\Sigma
-algebra maps.

This result means that the approach to Koszul duality of categories we consider in this paper is compatible with the Koszul duality of operads in spectra developed by Michael Ching in
[
Chi05
]
.

Following that, we recall in section 4 the idea of prefunctor. This is similar in spirit to the idea of seeing categories with object set
S
as precategories with object set
S
with a monoid structure. Prefunctors are elements of
\mathcal{V}^{S}
, and one can see functors from
C
to
\mathcal{V}
as the prefunctors possessing a right
C
-module structure, with respect to an external tensor product with
\mathcal{V}^{S\times S}
. We then derive adjunctions and Quillen equivalences relating the functor categories
\mathcal{F}(C,\mathcal{V})
and
\mathcal{F}(C^{op},\mathcal{V})
with
\mathcal{F}(K(C),\mathcal{V})
. One of these equivalences, it turns out, is a special case of the work of Brooke Shipley and Stefan Schwede in
[
SS03b
]
.

Finally, in section 5, we remark that the algebra map from a finitely built square zero extension
R\vee M
(i.e.
M
is an
R
-bimodule and the algebra structure comes from this bimodule structure) to its double dual is a weak equivalence, and through a description of the bar construction
B(R,R\vee M,R\vee M)
(see Proposition
5.4
) prove the following result.

Theorem 2

(Corollary
5.7
)
.

Let
M
be an
R
-bimodule and
R\vee M
the corresponding square zero extension. Then there is a weak equivalence of algebras

\varphi:\mathbb{F}_{R}(\hom_{R}(\Sigma M,R))\xrightarrow{\sim}K(R\vee M)

from the free
R
-algebra generated by

to the Koszul dual of
R\vee M
.

1
A general Koszul dual construction

We explain in this section how one can extend the notion of Koszul dual of an augmented algebra in a general context. Throughout this section,
(\mathscr{C},\otimes,1)
denotes a monoidal biclosed model category, not necessarily symmetric. In fact in further sections,
\mathscr{C}
will not be symmetric. Besides we ask that the model structure on
\mathscr{C}
is cofibrantly generated, so that we can transfer its model structure to the various categories of modules and algebras that will be considered. As stated in the introduction, the idea of our definition is to generalize the classical Yoneda algebra
\mathrm{Ext}_{A}(\Bbbk,\Bbbk)
for
A
a
\Bbbk
-algebra where
\Bbbk
is a field. This first section does not really provide any new results. There is significant overlap with the first section of
[
BH18
]
(the arxiv version, even though it mostly deals with symmetric monoidal categories) and other similar discussions of model categories of algebras and modules in the literature. The role of this section is to lay out a very general context in which the phrase “the derived
C
-endomorphisms of
R
” makes sense for
R
a monoid and
C
an augmented
R
-algebra, and then define the Koszul dual of
C
to be just that.

1.1
Basic definitions

Let us start by recalling what we mean when we speak of a
monoidal biclosed model category
.

Definition 1.1
.

A monoidal category
(\mathscr{C},\otimes,1)
equipped with a model structure is said to be
biclosed
when for any object
A
of it, the two functors
A\otimes-
and
-\otimes A
have right adjoints.

Moreover, it is called a
monoidal model category
when the monoidal structure and the model structure satisfy the following compatibility condition, commonly referred to as SM7.

Axiom

(SM7)
.

Assume that
f:A\rightarrow B
and
g:X\rightarrow Y
are cofibrations in
\mathscr{C}
. Then the induced map

A\otimes Y\cup_{A\otimes X}B\otimes X\rightarrow A\otimes Y

is a cofibration in
\mathscr{C}
, and is trivial if either
f
or
j
is. In addition, denoting
f:1_{c}\xrightarrow{\sim}1
the cofibrant replacement of the unit, the map
f\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A}
is a weak equivalence for all cofibrant
A
in
\mathscr{C}
.

The two right adjoint functors to
A\otimes-
and
-\otimes A
will be respectively denoted
\hom^{l}(A,-)
and
\hom^{r}(A,-)
. We will often refer to the counits of the corresponding adjunctions as evaluation maps. Besides we will call

\hom^{l}(-,-),\hom^{r}(-,-):\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}

the left and right
hom bifunctors
.

This notion of monoidal model category comes from
[
SS00
]
, Definition 3.1. For more context on this axiom, the reader can have a look at part II.3 of
[
GJ09
]
, even though it deals with the case of a model category equipped with a hom object functor landing in the category of simplicial sets instead of
\mathscr{C}
itself.

Remark 1.2
.

We will simplify the notation by writing
\hom
for

, since it is the hom object we will be using the most. The two hom object bifunctors are adjoint to each other in the contravariant variable, that is, for

\mathrm{mor}_{\mathscr{C}}(\bullet,\hom(-,A))\cong\mathrm{mor}_{\mathscr{C}}(\bullet\otimes-,A)\cong\mathrm{mor}_{\mathscr{C}}(-,\hom^{l}(\bullet,A))

Remark 1.3
.

Let
(F,G)
be a pair of adjoint functors
\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}
, and assume we want to know whether this pair is
\mathscr{C}
-enriched, meaning, if the adjunction bijection actually comes from an isomorphism of hom objects. Given
M,N
in
\mathscr{C}
, there is a natural isomorphism
\mathrm{mor}(-,\hom(M,N))\cong\mathrm{mor}(-\otimes M,N)
of functors from
\mathscr{C}
to the category of sets and thus by the Yoneda lemma, an isomorphism
\hom(F(M),N)\cong\hom(M,G(N))
is the same as an isomorphism
\mathrm{mor}(-\otimes F(M),N)\cong\mathrm{mor}(-\otimes M,G(N))
. So in the particular case where

naturally for all

or if, equivalently,

naturally for all
X,N
, the adjoint pair is
\mathscr{C}
-enriched. In particular this applies to the case
F=-\otimes Y
,

. Note that in general one can only expect this condition

to hold in very particular cases, as this means the function

is determined by the image of the unit object, but it will come in handy later.

1.2
Transfer of model structures

Many people, going back to Kan, have discussed the conditions under which one can transfer a cofibrantly generated model structure along an adjoint pair of functors. The specific problem of transfer of a model structure from a monoidal model category to the category of its monoids and the category of modules over a given monoid is explored in
[
SS00
]
in the symmetric monoidal case, and in
[
Hov98
]
and
[
BB13
]
in a more general context. These last two sources are the ones we will be referring to here.

Given a cofibrantly generated monoidal biclosed category
\mathscr{C}
like in the previous section, there are two important assumptions we want a model structure on it to satisfy. First, that the
monoid axiom
holds in
\mathscr{C}
. This axiom is due to
[
SS00
]
(Definition 3.3) and states that any transfinite composition of pushouts of maps of the form
X\otimes f
or
f\otimes X
, where
f
is a trivial cofibration, is a weak equivalence. As stated in Lemma 3.5 of that same source, it is enough to check it for
f
a generating trivial cofibration.

Proposition 1.6

(Theorem 2.1 in
[
Hov98
]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a cofibrantly generated monoidal model category with generating cofibrations
I
and generating trivial cofibrations
J
. Let
A
be a monoid in
\mathscr{C}
and assume that the domains of
I
(resp.
J
) are small relative to
A\otimes I
-cell (resp.
A\otimes J
-cell). Suppose moreover that every map of

-cell is a weak equivalence in
\mathscr{C}
, which is a condition that can be seen as a monoid axiom specific to
A
(this holds trivially when
A
is cofibrant). Then, the category
{}_{A}{\mathsf{Mod}}
inherits a cofibrantly generated model structure through

.

In fact, corollary 2.2 in
[
Hov98
]
tells us that when
A
is cofibrant, not only does
{}_{A}{\mathsf{Mod}}
inherit a projective model structure for free, but the forgetful functor
U:_{A}{\mathsf{Mod}}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}
creates cofibrations as well. Besides, we will assume that the category
\mathscr{C}
is
compactly generated
as in Definition 2.4 of
[
BB13
]
recalled below (see section 2 of that paper for a more thorough discussion).

Definition 1.7
.

A class
K
of morphisms in
\mathscr{C}
is said to be
saturated
if it is closed under cobase change, transfinite composition and retracts. For

of weak equivalences in
\mathscr{C}
is

if

is closed under filtered colimits along morphisms in

.

A monoidal model category
\mathscr{C}
is said to be
K
-compactly generated
if

1.

it is cofibrantly generated,

2.

the class of weak equivalences of
\mathscr{C}
is
K
-perfect, and

3.

every object of
\mathscr{C}
is small relative to

.

Moreover
\mathscr{C}
is simply called

when it is

-compactly generated, where

is the monoidal saturation of

, that is, the closure of the class of cofibrations under tensoring with arbitrary objects of
\mathscr{C}
.

The category of compactly generated weak Hausdorff spaces, and suitable categories of simplicial sets or spectra are for example compactly generated and satisfy the monoid axiom; see section 2 for further discussion on this.

Proposition 1.8

(Theorem 4.1 in
[
BB13
]
)
.

If a compactly generated monoidal model category
\mathscr{C}
satisfies the monoid axiom, the category of monoids in
\mathscr{C}
inherits a transfered model structure through the adjunction given by the free monoid construction and the forgetful functor.

In addition, if
\EuScript D
is a small
\mathscr{C}
-enriched category, then we get a projective model structure on
[\EuScript D,\mathscr{C}]
. And if
\EuScript D
possesses a symmetric monoidal structure, the Day convolution product induces a compactly generated monoidal model category structure on
[\EuScript D,\mathscr{C}]
which satisfies the monoid axiom.

We can use the two previous results to get the model structures we want. By the first statement of

Proposition 1.9

Assuming

Proof.

We have explained above how this is a monoidal biclosed structure. As mentioned in Lemma 3.5 of

,

\mathscr{C}

is isomorphic to the pushout of
R\otimes f\otimes R\otimes X\otimes R
and
R\otimes A\otimes R\otimes g\otimes R
. Because
R
is cofibrant in
\mathscr{C}
, tensoring with
R
on one side or the other is left Quillen i.e. preserves cofibrations and trivial cofibrations, which means we can simply use the pushout-product axiom in
\mathscr{C}
with
R\otimes f\otimes R
and
g\otimes R
.

We also need to prove the extra condition about the unit, namely that for any
R
-bimodule
A
that is
R
-cofibrant (meaning, cofibrant as an
R
-bimodule) the map
R_{c}\otimes_{R}A\rightarrow R\otimes_{R}A
is a weak equivalence, where
R_{c}
is an
R
-cofibrant replacement of
R
. The transferred model structure on
{{}_{R}}{\mathsf{Mod}}_{R}
is such that
A
is
R
-cofibrant if and only if the map
*\rightarrow A
is a retract of transfinite compositions of pushouts of maps like
R\otimes f\otimes R
where
f
is a generating cofibration of
\mathscr{C}
. Therefore it is enough to consider
A=R\otimes A^{\prime}\otimes R
where
A^{\prime}
is a cofibrant object of
\mathscr{C}
. In addition, since
R
is cofibrant in
\mathscr{C}
, any
R
-cofibrant object is cofibrant in
\mathscr{C}
(this is because every map of the form
R\otimes f\otimes R
as above is a cofibration in
\mathscr{C}
); in particular this is the case for
R_{c}
. Therefore the map
R_{c}\rightarrow R
is a weak equivalence of cofibrant objects in
\mathscr{C}
, meaning that by Ken Brown’s lemma (Lemma 1.1.12 in
[
Hov07
]
), it is sent to a weak equivalence by the functor
-\otimes A^{\prime}\otimes R
, hence the map

R_{c}\otimes_{R}A\rightarrow R\otimes_{R}A

is one as well.
∎

It is then immediate that we can transfer this model structure on
R
-bimodules to categories of
R
-algebras and of
C
-modules for
C\in\mathsf{Alg}_{R}
. Indeed, assuming
C
is cofibrant as an
R
-bimodule, which as we will see later is a reasonable assumption to make, we can then use
1.6
to get a projective model structure on
{\mathsf{Mod}}_{C}
. Moreover, for any generating cofibration
R\otimes f\otimes R
of
{}_{R}{\mathsf{Mod}}_{R}
, there is a canonical isomorphism
R\otimes f\otimes R\otimes_{R}M\cong R\otimes f\otimes M
. Because
\otimes
distributes over colimits, in particular it distributes over transfinite composition of pushouts. Therefore, if the monoid axiom holds in
\mathscr{C}
it holds in
{}_{R}{\mathsf{Mod}}_{R}
as well, implying that we can transfer its model structure to the category
\mathsf{Alg}_{R}
of
R
-algebras.

The Koszul dual is naturally equipped with an

Let

2
Categories as algebras in a monoidal category

Let us fix a cofibrantly generated pointed symmetric monoidal model category
(\mathcal{V},\wedge,1)
, whose hom object bifunctor (recall that
\hom^{l}=\hom^{r}
in the symmetric case) will be written
\mathrm{map}(-,-)
. For example,
\mathcal{V}
can be the category
\EuScript{CG}_{*}
of pointed compactly generated weak Hausdorff spaces, or a suitable category of spectra like the category of
\mathbb{S}
-modules. In what follows we explain how one can see
\mathcal{V}
-categories with a fixed object set
S
as algebras in some monoidal biclosed category
\mathsf{PreCat}
of “
\mathcal{V}
-graphs”, an idea that has been used a number of times in the literature. This section has significant overlap with
[
SS03a
]
, section 6, although they do not mention the existence of hom objects. We are then able to use the framework discussed in section 1 to define the Koszul dual of an augmented
R
-algebra
C
, where
R
is a category with object set
S
.

2.1
Definitions

Let us start by fixing a set
S
, which will be the vertices/object set for all things considered in these notes. In the examples we discuss, we have
S=\mathbb{N}
, since we want to consider an environment in which props of operads naturally live, but there is no need to restrict ourselves to this case for the general definitions. One might for example be interested in setting
S=<\mathscr{C}>
, that is, the free commutative monoid generated by a given set of colors
\mathscr{C}
, in order to include props of
\mathscr{C}
-colored operads.

Definition 2.1
.

A
precategory
(with object set
S
) is the data, for all couples
(s,s^{\prime})\in S
, of an object
E(s,s^{\prime})
in
\mathcal{V}
. In other words, a precategory is simply a
\mathcal{V}
-enriched directed graph. Note that we allow self-arrows and parallel arrows. The category of precategories is the functor category
\mathcal{V}^{S\times S}
, which will be denoted by
\mathsf{PreCat}
.

The idea behind the name “precategory” is that a precategory is a category that is missing a multiplicative and unital structure. This idea can be made formal in the following way (
[
ML13
]
, II.7). Categories with object set
S
are exactly precategories equipped with a monoid structure for the composition product defined by

(C\circ D)(i,j)=\bigvee_{k\in S}C(i,k)\wedge D(k,j)

for all
i,j
in
S
. This gives us a (non-symmetric) monoidal category structure on
\mathsf{PreCat}
, with the unit object
I
defined by
I(s,s)=1
for all
s
and
I(s,s^{\prime})=*
for all
s\neq s^{\prime}
. We will usually not bother adding a “
\mathcal{V}
-” prefix in the rest of this paper, but the reader should keep in mind that all the precategories and categories we consider are
\mathcal{V}
-enriched. Similarly we will not write anything to specify the object set
S
.

This category has the following right and left hom-objects
\hom^{r}
and
\hom^{l}
, making
\mathsf{PreCat}
biclosed.

\displaystyle\hom^{r}(C,D)(i,j)=\prod_{k\in S}\mathrm{map}(C(j,k),D(i,k))

\displaystyle\hom^{l}(C,D)(i,j)=\prod_{k\in S}\mathrm{map}(C(k,i),D(k,j))

The notation is slightly unfortunate, but from now on, we will write
\mathsf{Cat}
for the category of categories with object set
S
, or
\mathsf{Cat}_{S}
when there is a risk for confusion. For
R
in
\mathsf{Cat}
, the monoid structure relative to
\circ
allows us to consider modules and algebras over
R
, and modules over an
R
-algebra
C
just like we did in the more general context of section 1.

Remark 2.2
.

Even though the category of precategories is not symmetric, it is equipped with an involution
(-)^{op}:\mathsf{PreCat}\rightarrow\mathsf{PreCat}
defined by
M^{op}(i,j)=M(j,i)
for all
i,j\in S
. This involution is anticommutative, that is,
(M\circ N)^{op}=N^{op}\circ M^{op}
. As a consequence, if
R
is a category with object set
S
, the maps
I=I^{op}\rightarrow R^{op}
and
R^{op}\circ R^{op}=(R\circ R)^{op}\rightarrow R^{op}
give
R^{op}
a structure of category with object set
S
as well. Moreover, a left
R
-module structure on
M
is the same as a right
R^{op}
-module structure on
M^{op}
. Another interesting property is that
\hom^{r}(A^{op},B^{op})^{op}=\hom^{l}(A,B)
.

We will write
{}_{R}{\mathsf{Mod}}_{R}
for the category of
R

Remark 2.3
.

The fact that the category of

Remark 2.5
.

Instead of

2.3
Model structures

We now apply the results from
[
Hov98
]
and
[
BB13
]
mentioned in section 1.2 to get model structures on
\mathsf{Cat}
,
{\mathsf{Mod}}_{R}
and the various categories of modules considered.

Given
i,j\in S
, consider the functor
(-)_{i,j}:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathsf{PreCat}
sending an object of
\mathcal{V}
to the precategory consisting in
\mathcal{V}
concentrated in level
(i,j)
, explicitly
V_{i,j}(k,l)
is
V
for

and
*
otherwise. Note that this functor has a right adjoint in the projection sending a precategory

to

. Since
\mathsf{PreCat}
is defined as
\mathcal{V}^{S\times S}
, it inherits a pointwise model structure from
\mathcal{V}
, which by Proposition 1.11.10 in
[

]
is cofibrantly generated with

defined as the union of image sets
\cup_{(i,j)\in S\times S}(I_{\mathcal{V}})_{i,j}
, and similarly for

.

Proposition 2.6
.

If
\mathcal{V}
satisfies the monoid axiom, so does
\mathsf{PreCat}
, and if
\mathcal{V}
is compactly generated, so is
\mathsf{PreCat}
.

Proof.

It is relatively obvious that the category of precategories satisfies the monoid axiom, given that transfinite compositions and pushouts are defined levelwise in
\mathsf{PreCat}
, which means it is enough to observe that for
X
any precategory,
J_{\mathsf{PreCat}}\circ X
is composed of maps whose levels are either
*\rightarrow*
or
\psi\wedge X(k,j)
for some
\psi
in
J_{\mathcal{V}}
. These are weak equivalences in
\mathcal{V}
by assumption.

Assume
\mathcal{V}
is compactly generated (see Definition
1.7
). We already know that
\mathsf{PreCat}
is cofibrantly generated, and since colimits and morphisms are defined levelwise, the condition of smallness relative
I_{\mathsf{PreCat}}
boils down to smallness relative
I_{\mathcal{V}}
. Finally, weak equivalences and colimits are determined pointwise and
I_{\mathsf{PreCat}}^{\circ}
is actually just
\Pi_{S\times S}I_{\mathcal{V}}^{\wedge}
, so the class of weak equivalences of
\mathsf{PreCat}
is
I_{\mathsf{PreCat}}^{\circ}
-perfect.
∎

This result coupled with Propositions
1.6
and
1.8
yield transfered projective model category structures on
{}_{R}{\mathsf{Mod}}
,
{\mathsf{Mod}}_{R}
,
{}_{R}{\mathsf{Mod}}_{R}
and
\mathsf{Cat}
. Most of the symmetric monoidal model categories one would want to consider for the choice of
\mathcal{V}
satisfy the monoid axiom.

Remark 2.7
.

We will mostly work with the assumption that the “ground category”
R
is cofibrant (this terminology being motivated by the analogy with the ground field in classical Koszul duality of algebras). This is reasonable, in part because of the applications we have in mind, in part because it yields desirable properties for the categories of
R
-modules and bimodules. For example, Theorem 2.4 in
[
Hov98
]
which says that under mild conditions, a weak equivalence of cofibrant monoids induces a Quillen equivalence between the corresponding categories of modules over the source and the target, which is not necessarily true if the monoids are not cofibrant (see Example 2.5 of the same source).

Corollary 2.8
.

If
\mathcal{V}
satisfies the monoid axiom and
R
is cofibrant, so does
{}_{R}{\mathsf{Mod}}_{R}
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of something that holds in the general context of section 1: if
\mathscr{C}
is a monoidal model category satisfying the monoid axiom, and
R
is a cofibrant monoid in it, then
{}_{R}{\mathsf{Mod}}_{R}
also satisfies it.

Let’s simply write
J_{R}
for the generating trivial cofibration set of
{}_{R}{\mathsf{Mod}}_{R}
, and let
X
be an
R
-bimodule. A generating trivial cofibration is of the form
R\circ f\circ R
where
f:A\rightarrow B
is a generating trivial cofibration of
\mathsf{PreCat}
. An element of the set
J_{R}\circ_{R}X
is therefore of the form
R\circ f\circ R\circ_{R}X=R\circ f\circ X
, and because
R
is cofibrant the functor
R\circ-
is left Quillen, which means that
R\circ f
is a trivial cofibration (using the pushout version of the monoidal model category axiom with
*\rightarrow R
and
A\rightarrow B
). Which means that
{}_{R}{\mathsf{Mod}}_{R}
satisfies the monoid axiom when
\mathsf{PreCat}
does.
∎

We can therefore use the general definition of the Koszul dual given in section 1.3 in this context of precategories and modules: for
C
an augmented
R
-algebra,

K(C)=\hom^{r}_{C}(B(R,C,C),B(R,C,C))

Note that the simplicial bar construction
B_{\bullet}(R,C,C)
consists of chains of composable arrows from
C
.

Remark 2.9
.

It is useful to clarify the situation of the geometric realization. In section 1.3 (Remark
1.17
and above), we defined the geometric realization of a Reedy cofibrant simplicial
R
-bimodule
X_{\bullet}
as the coend of a cofibrant approximation of
X_{\bullet}
when seen as a constant cosimplicial simplicial object

. However, in the cases where
\mathcal{V}
is a suitable category of spaces or spectra like
\EuScript{CG}
or the category of
\mathbb{S}
-modules, there is an external tensor product enabling us to write

(as mentioned in Remark

). To be precise, this holds because

is a cofibrant approximation of

in
\mathrm{cs}{{}_{R}{\mathsf{Mod}}_{R}}
.

2.4
A few examples

The main purpose of our approach is to get an extended definition of Koszul duality, thus we are taking a special interest in the case where
R
is the category
\Sigma
where
\Sigma(i,j)
is the set of bijections from
\{1,\ldots,i\}
to
\{1,\ldots,j\}
(with the appropriate modifications to make it an element of
\mathcal{V}
). In section 3, we will justify that our definition of Koszul dual extends the one for Koszul duality of operads in spectra due to Michael Ching, by taking
R=\Sigma
and
C
the prop associated to an operad
P
. But there are of course other notable examples.

Let us say the indexing set
S
has one element. In that case, precategories are just objects of
\mathcal{V}
and the definition of Koszul dual we study in this article corresponds to a notion of duality in
\mathcal{V}
, for example as in
[
DGI06
]
. Another natural example to consider is when both
R
and
C
only have endomorphisms, which implies that
B(R,C,C)(i,j)
is trivial for
i\neq j
so
B(R,C,C)
only has endomorphisms as well, and the same is true for the Koszul dual, with

K(C)(i,i)=\mathrm{map}_{C(i,i)}(B(R,C,C)(i,i),B(R,C,C)(i,i))=K(C(i,i))
.

Let us now consider the case
R=\Sigma
. Let
\mathcal{V}
be a suitable category of spaces, like the category
\EuScript{CG}_{+}
of compactly generated weak Hausdorff pointed spaces. To simplify the notation, whenever
m
is a natural number, we will simply denote by
m
the basic set with
m
elements
\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
(instead of using the traditional bold
m
or underlined
\underline{m}
). As mentioned above, the case where
C
is the prop of an operad is discussed in section 3, so let us consider the case
C=FI
, the skeleton of the category of finite sets and injections. That is,
FI(m,n)={inj}(m,n)_{+}
if
m\leq n
and
FI(m,n)=*
otherwise. Note that
FI
is a
\Sigma
-algebra. Although we cannot compute the Koszul dual of
FI
, there is a nice simple description of its bar construction levelwise.

Proposition 2.10
.

The bar construction
B(\Sigma,FI,\Sigma)
, which we will denote
B(FI)
for simplicity, is isomorphic in level
(m,n)
to

{inj}(m,n)_{+}\wedge S^{n-m}

Proof.

By definition, the
l
-th simplicial degree of the bar construction is

B_{l}(FI)(m,n)=(\Sigma\circ_{\Sigma}FI^{\circ_{\Sigma}^{l}}\circ_{\Sigma}\Sigma)(m,n)=FI^{\circ_{\Sigma}^{l}}(m,n)

so its non degenerate part is

\left(\coprod_{m<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{l-1}<n}{inj}(m,n_{1})\times_{\Sigma_{n_{1}}}\cdots\times_{\Sigma_{n_{l-1}}}{inj}(n_{l-1},n)\right)_{+}

A nontrivial element of this space is an equivalence class of composable chains of injections

m\xrightarrow{i_{1}}n_{1}\xrightarrow{i_{2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{i_{l-1}}n_{l-1}\xrightarrow{i_{l}}n

under the actions of the relevant symmetric groups. Such an equivalence class can be seen as the data of an injection
i:m\rightarrow n
together with a chain

i(m)\subset X_{1}\subset X_{2}\subset\cdots\subset X_{l-1}\subset n

of subsets of
n
.
A homeomorphism between these two spaces can be defined by setting
i=i_{l}\cdots i_{1}
and
X_{j}
as the image of
i_{l}\cdots i_{j+1}
. Looking at how the face and degeneracy maps of the bar construction translate to the latter description, we can deduce that

B(FI)(m,n)={inj}(m,n)_{+}\wedge|\mathbb{P}(n-m)|/(|\mathbb{P}_{\subsetneq}(n-m)|\cup|\mathbb{P}_{\supsetneq}(n-m)|)

where
\mathbb{P}(n-m)
denotes the poset of subsets of
n-m
, and
\mathbb{P}_{\subsetneq}(n-m)
(resp.
P_{\supsetneq}(n-m)
) denotes its subposet consisting of all chains whose first element is not the empty set (resp. whose last element is not the full set
n-m
). Using the model of
|\mathbb{P}(n-m)|
as a cube of dimension
n-m
we see that the right-hand quotient in the above formula corresponds to the quotient of the
(n-m)
-dimensional cube by its boundary, that is, to
S^{n-m}
.
∎

3
Connection to Koszul duality of operads

We now explore the case of
R=\Sigma
further, with the idea of relating our definition of the Koszul dual of a category to Koszul duality of operads. In this context, props are examples of
\Sigma
-algebras, and in particular props of operads.

Remark 3.1
.

This is a good time for a small interlude on what props are and how they fit in the setting of (pre)categories with a fixed object set
S=\langle\mathscr{C}\rangle
, meaning the set of words on a given set
\mathscr{C}
of colors. We can define
\mathscr{C}
-colored props to be strict symmetric monoidal model categories with object set
S
, and this coincides with, for example, definition 2.2.13 in
[
Yau08
]
. In the latter, props are symmetric bimodules equipped with compatible horizontal and vertical composition laws, one corresponding to the composition of morphisms i.e. the category structure, and the other to the monoidal structure. As before, we will focus on the case where
S=\mathbb{N}
, and thus on 1-colored props, but we believe our results could generalize to props with multiple colors.

3.1
The Prop functor is monoidal

In what follows we will assume that the base category
\mathcal{V}
is a suitable category of spectra, for example the category of EKMM spectra.

Definition 3.2
.

To every symmetric sequence
X
we can associate a
\Sigma
-bimodule

{\mathrm{prop}\,}X(m,n)=\coprod_{f:m\rightarrow n}X(m_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge X(m_{n})

where
m_{i}
denotes the number of elements in
f^{-1}(i)
(see Example 60 in
[
Mar08
]
). If
X
is an operad,
{\mathrm{prop}\,}X
is a prop (meaning that, admittedly, our notation can be a bit misleading if
X
is not).

If
X
is reduced, meaning
X(0)=*
and
X(1)=S^{0}
, then
{\mathrm{prop}\,}X(m,n)=*
for
m<n
(we say
{\mathrm{prop}\,}X
is
directed
) and
{\mathrm{prop}\,}X(n,n)=\Sigma_{n}
.

Remark 3.3
.

In the case where
\mathcal{V}
is, for example, the category of dg-modules, it can be interesting to note that directed precategories are exactly representations in
\mathcal{V}
of the infinite quiver
0\leftarrow 1\leftarrow\cdots
.

Just like in the context of operads, these properties are very useful for a precategory to have, one example among others is that they make the description of composition products of
{\mathrm{prop}\,}X
much nicer. Namely the indexing set of the coproduct in
({\mathrm{prop}\,}X\circ_{\Sigma}{\mathrm{prop}\,}X)(m,n)
becomes finite. We will therefore assume that
X
is reduced throughout this section, and in the more general context of section 5 we will also work with a directed
R
-category
C
.

We investigate how the Koszul dual
\Sigma
-algebra of the prop of an operad
P
relates to the prop of its Koszul dual. The main thing to look out for being that, whereas the Koszul dual construction from section 1 makes use of an endomorphism object and the multiplication is the composition of endomorphisms, we do not have access to hom objects in the context of operads, so the Koszul dual operad of
P
is defined as the dual of
B(1,P,1)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

The functor
{\mathrm{prop}\,}:\mathcal{V}^{\Sigma}\rightarrow{{}_{\Sigma}{\mathsf{Mod}}_{\Sigma}}
is monoidal.

Proof.

On one hand, we have

\displaystyle({\mathrm{prop}\,}(M)\circ_{\Sigma}{\mathrm{prop}\,}(N))(i,j)

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{k}{\mathrm{prop}\,}(M)(i,k)\wedge_{\Sigma_{k}}{\mathrm{prop}\,}(N)(k,j)

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{k}\big(\bigvee_{i\rightarrow k}M(i_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge M(i_{k})\big)\wedge_{\Sigma_{k}}\big(\bigvee_{k\rightarrow j}N(k_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge N(k_{j})\big)

Let us consider the groupoid
\mathscr{C}_{i,j}
whose objects are diagrams
i\rightarrow k\rightarrow j
and where
\mathscr{C}_{i,j}((g,f),(g^{\prime},f^{\prime}))
consists of the bijections
k\rightarrow k
making the natural diagram

\textstyle{i\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{k\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{\prime}}
\textstyle{k\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\scriptstyle{\sigma}
\textstyle{j}

commute. Notice that all morphisms can be written as
\sigma:(g,f)\rightarrow(\sigma g,f\sigma^{-1})
. Then the formula we obtained above can be seen as the colimit of the functor
F:\mathscr{C}_{i,j}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}
sending
i\rightarrow k\rightarrow j
to

M(i_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge M(i_{k})\wedge N(k_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge N(k_{j})

On the other hand,

{\mathrm{prop}\,}(M\circ_{\Sigma}N)(i,j)=\bigvee_{i\rightarrow j}(M\circ_{\Sigma}N)(i_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge(M\circ_{\Sigma}N)(i_{j})

with

(M\circ_{\Sigma}N)(i_{l})=\bigvee_{k_{l}}\bigvee_{i_{l}\rightarrow k_{l}}M(i_{l,1})\wedge\cdots\wedge M(i_{l,k_{l}})\wedge_{\Sigma_{k_{l}}}N(k_{l})

Consider now the full subcategory
\EuScript D_{i,j}
consisting of the objects
(g,f):i\rightarrow k\rightarrow j
of
\mathscr{C}_{i,j}
where the right-hand side morphism
f
is order-preserving. Notice that for
(g,f)
an object of
\EuScript D_{i,j}
, a morphism
\sigma:(g,f)\rightarrow(\sigma g,f\sigma^{-1})
lies in
\EuScript D_{i,j}
if and only if
\sigma
preserves
f^{-1}(x)
, for all
1\leq x\leq j
. From this we can deduce that taking the colimit of
F|_{\EuScript D_{i,j}}
gives the formula we have above for
{\mathrm{prop}\,}(M\circ_{\Sigma}N)(i,j)
. Since
\EuScript D_{i,j}
and
\mathscr{C}_{i,j}
are equivalent categories, we get the desired isomorphism
{\mathrm{prop}\,}(M\circ_{\Sigma}N)\cong{\mathrm{prop}\,}(M)\circ_{\Sigma}{\mathrm{prop}\,}(N)
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Coming back to Remark
2.3
, one can identify a symmetric sequence
M
to
{\mathrm{prop}\,}(M)(-,1)
, and using Proposition
3.4
, a map
M\circ_{\Sigma}N\rightarrow P
to a collection of maps

{\mathrm{prop}\,}(M)(i,k)\wedge_{\Sigma_{k}}{\mathrm{prop}\,}(N)(k,1)\rightarrow{\mathrm{prop}\,}(P)(i,1)

which is the same as a collection of maps
{\mathrm{prop}\,}(M)(i,k)\rightarrow\mathrm{map}(N(k),P(i))
. The obstacle to the existence of homomorphism objects for symmetric sequences is then that this does not translate to a map where the source is
M
. It is worth noting, in that regard, that while the functor
{\mathrm{prop}\,}
is monoidal it has no left or right adjoint.

3.2
An equivalence of
\Sigma
-bimodules

We will now use the fact that the functor
{\mathrm{prop}\,}
is monoidal to exhibit a zigzag of weak equivalences of
\Sigma
-bimodules between
K({\mathrm{prop}\,}P)^{\mathrm{op}}
and
{\mathrm{prop}\,}K(P)
. This is weaker than the result we obtain in section 3.3 (an actual zigzag of weak equivalences of
\Sigma
-algebras
), but it is worth presenting since its construction is much more natural.

Lemma 3.6
.

There is an equivalence of
\Sigma
-bimodules

K({\mathrm{prop}\,}P)\simeq\hom_{\Sigma}({\mathrm{prop}\,}(B(1,P,1)),\Sigma)

Proof.

Note that
{\mathrm{prop}\,}1=\Sigma
. By Proposition
1.22
it is enough to show that

B({\mathrm{prop}\,}1,{\mathrm{prop}\,}P,{\mathrm{prop}\,}1)={\mathrm{prop}\,}B(1,P,1)

which follows from the Proposition
3.4
and the fact that the symmetric monoidal product
\wedge
of the base category
\mathcal{V}
distributes over colimits.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Assume that
P(n)
is finite for all
n
, by which we mean it is a finite complex (i.e. dualizable). There is a zigzag of equivalences of
\Sigma
-bimodules

K({\mathrm{prop}\,}P)^{op}\simeq{\mathrm{prop}\,}(K(P))

Proof.

Because of Corollary
3.6
, it is sufficient to show that for a symmetric sequence
M
such that
M(n)
is finite for all
n
,
\hom_{\Sigma}({\mathrm{prop}\,}(M),\Sigma)(i,j)
is equivalent to
{\mathrm{prop}\,}(M^{\vee})(j,i)
. Here by
M^{\vee}
we mean the symmetric sequence defined by
M^{\vee}(n)=M(n)^{\vee}
, which is how we write the Spanier-Whitehead dual
F(M(n),\mathbb{S})
of
M(n)
(see part III in
[
GJMS+06
]
for a treatment of equivariant stable duality). First, note that
\hom_{\Sigma}({\mathrm{prop}\,}(M),\Sigma)(i,j)
is equivalent to

4
External modules

In this section, we recall a standard way of seeing functors as (external) modules over a category and discuss the link between the categories of external modules over
C
and
K(C)
. Just like we discussed in section 2 a notion of precategory as an object that when endowed with an algebra structure becomes a category, we now consider

become functors

. This approach of seeing functors as modules is classical, and is discussed in section 6 of
[
SS03b
]
for instance. There are several reasons for looking at these external modules instead of the ”internal” ones considered previously. First, this is the natural setting for defining enriched functors. In addition, a structure of external module over the prop of an operad is the same as a left module structure over that operad, as stated in Proposition
4.3
.

We will discuss Quillen equivalences between categories of
C
- and
K(C)
-modules, namely Corollary
4.8
and Proposition

[
SS03b
]
, while the latter is new.

Definition 4.1

using equivalences, homotopy cofiber sequences and retracts.

Definition 4.6
.

An
R
-equivalence
of (left or right)
C
-modules
U\rightarrow V
is a
C
-module map inducing an equivalence
\hom_{C}(R,U)\rightarrow\hom_{C}(R,V)
. If for an internal
R
-module
M
, the functor
\hom_{C}(M,-)
sends
R
-equivalences to equivalences,
M
is said to be
cellular
.

Definition 4.7
.

An external right
C
-module
M
is said to be
effectively constructible from
R
if the evaluation map

\hom_{C}(R,M)\otimes_{K(C)}R\rightarrow M

is an equivalence. Note that in the analogous definition for a left
C
-module
M
, the evaluation map has the order of the factors of the source swapped:

R\otimes_{K(C)}\hom_{C}(R,M)\rightarrow M

We will now use the results from section 3.9 of
[
SS03b
]
to derive an interesting corollary in our setting. First, looking at 3.9.1 of this same source, they define an endomorphism category construction
\EuScript{E}(\EuScript{G})
for a set
\EuScript{G}
of objects in a spectral category
\EuScript D
. In our case, with
\EuScript D
the category of
R
-cellular left
C
-modules, if we take
\EuScript{G}
to be the set of representable functors
R(-,i)
for
i\in S
, this definition coincides with the left-
C
-modules version of the Koszul dual
K(C)
, namely

\EuScript{E}(\EuScript{G})(R(-,i),R(-,j))={\mathrm{Nat}}_{C^{op}}(R(-,i),R(-,j))=K(C)(i,j)

Similarly it is also true that what they call
\mathrm{Hom}(\EuScript{G},Y)
, for
Y
an external left
C
-module, is the same as the definition of
\hom_{C}(R,Y)
that we gave above. Now we want to assume that
\EuScript{G}
is a set of compact generators for
\EuScript D
(where compact means small as in definition
4.5
). It is reasonable to make this assumption; think for example about the case
R=\Sigma
, or more generally the case where
R
consists of a tower of monoids, each of which is compact/small in the base category
\mathcal{V}
. In addition, observe that every element of
\EuScript{G}
is small if and only if the internal left
C
-module
R
is small. Note however that every element of
\EuScript{G}
being small does not imply that the
external
left
C
-module
\bigvee_{i\in S}R(-,i)=R\otimes S
is small.

Corollary 4.8
.

Assume that
R
is small as a
C
-module. Then the adjunction

R\otimes_{K(C)}-:{{}_{K(C)}}{\mathsf{Mod}}\leftrightarrows{{}_{C}}{\mathsf{Mod}}:\hom_{C}(R,-)

induces a Quillen equivalence between the category of left
K(C)
-modules and the category of
R
-cellular left
C
-modules.

Proof.

The only thing to argue is that a
C
-module is
R
-cellular if and only if it is built from
R
, that is, if and only if it belongs to the smallest localizing subcategory of
{{{}_{C}}{\mathsf{Mod}}}
that contains
R\otimes S
. This fact is mentioned in 4.1 of
[
DGI06
]
, which refers to Theorem 5.1.5 of Hirschorn
[
Hir09
]
for a proof. The reader should mind that, unfortunately, the terminology of these two sources differ. In
[
Hir09
]
, the term
cellular
actually corresponds to the notion of small/finitely built presented in
[
DGI06
]
that we have recalled above.
∎

4.3
The double dual of an external module

For any left
C
-module
X
there is a map

\phi_{X}:X\rightarrow\hom_{K(C)}(\hom_{C}(X,\bar{R}),\bar{R})

which is the unit of the adjunction

\hom_{C}(-,\bar{R}):{{{}_{C}}{\mathsf{Mod}}}\leftrightarrows{\mathsf{Mod}}_{{}_{K(C)}}^{op}:\hom_{K(C)}(-,\bar{R})

where
\bar{R}
denotes the bar construction
B(C,C,R)
. Let us emphasize that
\hom_{C}(X,\bar{R})
is a
right

K(C)
-module this time. Note that if we considered a right
C
-module instead, the source and target would look the same, although of course the hom-objects would be different (left hom instead of right hom and vice-versa).

We wish to generalize the observation made in the proof of Proposition 4.20 of
[
DGI06
]
, that for all
C
-modules finitely built from
R
this map is an equivalence. First, let us look at the case
X=\bar{R}\otimes S=\bigvee_{i\in S}\bar{R}(-,i)
. Then

\displaystyle\hom_{K(C)}(\hom_{C}(X,\bar{R}),\bar{R})

\displaystyle\cong\hom_{K(C)}(\Pi_{j}\hom_{C}(\bar{R}(-,j),\bar{R}),\bar{R})

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{i}{\mathrm{Nat}}_{K(C)^{op}}(\Pi_{j}{\mathrm{Nat}}_{C^{op}}(\bar{R}(-,j),\bar{R}(-,i)),\bar{R}(-,i))

\displaystyle\cong\bigvee_{i}\bar{R}(-,i)

and one can check that the
k
-th component of
\phi_{R\otimes S}
is simply the natural inclusion of the
k
-th summand, which means the map from
R\otimes S
to its double dual is, up to isomorphism, just
\mathrm{id}_{R\otimes S}
.

Proposition 4.9
.

The map
\phi_{X}
is a weak equivalence for all
C
-modules finitely built from
R\otimes S
. That is, for all
X
that belong to the smallest thick category (i.e. closed under equivalences, cofiber/fiber sequences and retracts) that contains
R\otimes S
.

Proof.

The category of left
C
-modules
X
such that
\phi_{X}
is a weak equivalence is thick. It is closed under weak equivalences because the hom objects are derived and because of the 2-out-of-3 axiom, closed under cofiber/fiber sequences because
{{{}_{C}}{\mathsf{Mod}}}
is stable, and closed under retracts because weak equivalences are closed under retracts.
∎

More concisely, this follows from the fact that the two functors
\hom_{C}(-,\bar{R})
and
\hom_{K(C)}(-,\bar{R})
preserve weak equivalences, cofiber/fiber sequences and retractions, which also yields the following result.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let us denote by
\mathrm{Thick}(X)
the smallest thick subcategory generated by an object
X
(be it a
C
-module or a
K(C)
-module). There is a Quillen equivalence

\hom_{C}(-,\bar{R}):\mathrm{Thick}(R\otimes S)\leftrightarrows\mathrm{Thick}(S\otimes K(C))^{op}:\hom_{K(C)}(-,\bar{R})

between the category of left
C
-modules finitely built from
R\otimes S
and the opposite of the category of right
K(C)
-modules finitely built from
S\otimes K(C)
.

5
Double duality of modules and categories

In this section, we focus on two basic types of
R
-algebras: free algebras and square zero extensions. We first explain why the natural algebra map from a square zero extension into its double dual is a weak equivalence. Then, we show that the Koszul dual of a square zero extension is weak equivalent, as an algebra, to a free algebra. In addition, any free algebra is equivalent to the Koszul dual of a square zero extension. As before, we tacitly assume that
R
is levelwise cofibrant and that all algebras considered are cofibrant as
R
-bimodules.

5.1
The double dual of a square zero extension

Definition 5.1
.

An
R
-algebra is said to be
finitely generated free
if it is of the form
\mathbb{F}(M)
where
\mathbb{F}
denotes the free
R
-algebra functor from section 2.2, and
M
is finitely built from
R
as an
R
-bimodule. The dual notion is that of a
trivial

R
-algebra, also called a
square zero extension
, consisting of a coproduct
R\vee M
, which is said to be finite if
M
is finitely built from
R
in
{}_{R}{\mathsf{Mod}}_{R}
. The unit map is given by the canonical inclusion map, and the multiplication map puts together the algebra structure on
R
, the left and right
R
-module structures on
M
, and the trivial map
M\circ M\rightarrow*\rightarrow R
. In classical algebra, it is analogous to taking the quotient of the algebra of polynomials
R[M]
by the ideal of elements of degree greater than or equal to
2
. Note that both of these kinds of algebra come naturally equipped with an augmentation map.

The discussion on the double dual of an external module from section 4 is also valid for internal modules, namely we get a result analogous to Proposition
4.10
.

Proposition 5.2
.

There is a Quillen equivalence

\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Hom}_{C}(-,\bar{R}):\mathrm{Thick}(R)\leftrightarrows\mathrm{Thick}(K(C))^{op}:\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Hom}_{K(C)}(-,\bar{R})

between the category of internal right
C
-modules finitely built from
R
and the opposite of the category of left
K(C)
-modules finitely built from
K(C)
.

The reason we are interested in this internal modules version of the statement is that we can apply it to
C
as a
C
-module in particular. Now, in general one cannot expect
C
to be finitely built from
R
as a
C
-module, as that is quite a strong condition and implies that the multiplicative structure on
C
is not very interesting. However, a finite square zero extension
R\vee M
is always finitely built from
R
as an
R\vee M
-module, because of the simple observation that it is the coproduct of
R
and
M
as
R\vee M
-modules.

Corollary 5.3
.

For
R\vee M
a finite square zero extension, the natural map

R\vee M\rightarrow K(K(R\vee M))

is a weak equivalence of
R
-algebras.

Proof.

Let
C=R\vee M
. Since
C
is cofibrant and finitely built from
R
as a right
C
-module, and since we are using the category of EKMM spectra (in which all objects are fibrant) as the ground category
\mathcal{V}
, the unit map

C\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Hom}_{K(C)}(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Hom}_{C}(C,\bar{R}),\bar{R})\cong\mathbb{R}\mathrm{Hom}_{K(C)}(\bar{R},\bar{R})

is a weak equivalence by Proposition
5.2
.
∎

We now want to show that this is true as well for finitely generated free
R
-algebras. As mentioned above, this is the natural dual notion, so one might expect that finite square zero extensions and finitely generated free algebras should be Koszul dual to one another. We will show this and use it to deduce that the natural map into the double dual is a weak equivalence of
R
-algebras in this case too.

5.2
The dual of a square zero extension is free

Throughout this discussion
C=R\vee M
is a square zero extension, with
M
an
R
-bimodule finitely built from
R
. We assume that
M
is strictly directed and levelwise finite, where by
strictly directed
we mean either
M(i,j)=*
for all
i\leq j
or
M(i,j)=*
for all
i\geq j
. We also assume that
R
is diagonal (
R(i,j)=*
for all
i\neq j
).

Proposition 5.4
.

The geometric realization
B(R,C,C)
of the simplicial bar construction associated to
(R,C,C)
is isomorphic to the coproduct

R\vee(I\wedge M)\vee(S^{1}\wedge I\wedge M^{\circ_{R}2})\vee\cdots\vee(({S^{1}})^{\wedge n-1}\wedge I\wedge M^{\circ_{R}n})\vee\cdots

where
I
denotes the interval
[0,1]
with
0
as base point.

Proof.

The first thing to notice is that since the simplices of
B(R,C,C)_{\bullet}
are chains of composable elements of
R
and
M
, we can introduce a weight grading, elements of
R
having weight
0
and elements of
M
having weight
1
. With this in mind, the weight
n

B_{(n)}(R,C,C)_{\bullet}
is defined as follows. For
k
a natural number (the simplicial index),
B_{(n)}(R,C,C)_{k}
is the coproduct of all
R\circ_{R}X_{1}\circ_{R}\cdots\circ_{R}X_{k+1}
, where among
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k+1}
there are
n
copies of
M
and the other
X_{i}

R
.
Because the faces and degeneracies of the bar construction preserve the weight grading (the basepoint being in every weight degree), this actually defines a ”sub-simplicial object” and we can write

B(R,C,C)_{\bullet}=\bigvee_{n\geq 0}B_{(n)}(R,C,C)_{\bullet}

It is then enough to show that the geometric realization
B_{(n)}(R,C,C)
of the
n
-th weight degree is isomorphic to
({S^{1}})^{\wedge n-1}\wedge I\wedge M^{\circ_{R}n}
.

First, recall the definition of the degeneracy maps of the simplicial bar construction: they consist in inserting a copy of
R
between the leftmost
R
and the rightmost
C
of
B(R,C,C)_{m}=R\circ_{R}C\circ_{R}\cdots\circ_{R}C
. This means that the only non-degenerate simplices of
B_{(n)}(R,C,C)
lie in the

component in simplicial degree
n
and the

. From this we can see that
B_{(n)}(R,C,C)
is isomorphic to the geometric realization of
X_{\bullet}\wedge M^{\circ_{R}n}
where

is the based simplicial set with one non-degenerate

-simplex
\beta_{n}
, one non-degenerate
n+1
-simplex
\alpha_{n}
, and face maps
d_{i}:X_{n+1}\rightarrow X_{n}
satisfying
d_{n+1}(\alpha_{n})=\beta_{n}
and
d_{i}(\alpha_{n})=*
for
i\neq n+1
.

We can write
X_{\bullet}
as the join of simplicial sets
S^{0}\star\cdots\star S^{0}\star\Delta^{1}
, where by join we mean that
Y\star Z
is the pushout

\textstyle{Y\wedge Z\wedge 1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y\wedge Z\wedge 0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y\wedge Z\wedge\Delta^{1}}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y\star Z}

This definition is a pointed version of the one presented in Section 4.2.1 of
[

]
that is due to Joyal. As mentioned in Proposition 4.2.1.2 of this source, it is equivalent to the more traditional definition of join of simplicial sets that involves the Day convolution product of augmented simplicial sets. By applying the geometric realization functor to this diagram, we see that the realization of the join is the (topological) join of the realizations. In particular, we get a natural homeomorphism
|S^{0}\star-|\cong S^{1}\wedge|-|
and therefore the geometric realization of

can be written as

∎

Under this description, the right action of
C=R\vee M
is given by the canonical right action of

on each weight component and an action of

seen as a trivial monoid without unit. Given a weight component

of the simplicial bar construction

(see the proof of Proposition

and the map

(S^{0})^{\star n-1}\star\Delta^{1}\rightarrow(S^{0})^{\star n}\rightarrow(S^{0})^{\star n}\star\Delta^{1}

obtained by composing the natural quotient and inclusion maps.

Proposition 5.5
.

The geometric realization
B(R,C,R)
of the simplicial bar construction associated to
(R,C,R)
can be described as a coproduct

\bigvee_{n\geq 0}(\Sigma M)^{n}

where
\Sigma M
is the levelwise suspension of

.

Proof.

This is very analogous to the proof of Proposition
5.4
, with the difference that in this case
all
face maps

send
\alpha_{n}
to the basepoint.
∎

We will build a weak equivalence of algebras
\varphi:\mathbb{F}(D(\Sigma M))\rightarrow K(C)
where

is the dual functor for right

that is a map of

D(\Sigma M)\circ_{R}R\rightarrow*\rightarrow B(R,C,C)

Then, in weight 1, we have

D(\Sigma M)\circ_{R}(I\wedge M)\rightarrow D(\Sigma M)\circ_{R}\Sigma M\rightarrow R

Finally, for weight

,

\textstyle{D(\Sigma M)\circ_{R}(({S^{1}})^{\wedge n-1}\wedge I\wedge M^{\circ_{R}n})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{D(\Sigma M)\circ_{R}\Sigma M\circ_{R}(({S^{1}})^{\wedge n-2}\wedge I\wedge M^{\circ_{R}n-1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{({S^{1}})^{\wedge n-2}\wedge I\wedge M^{\circ_{R}n-1}}

gives the desired evaluation map. Notice that this evaluation map preserves weight (considering that elements of
D(\Sigma M)
have weight -1).

We obtain a map of algebras
\varphi:\mathbb{F}(D(\Sigma M))\rightarrow K(C)
. We will show that the composite

\mathbb{F}(D(\Sigma M))\xrightarrow{\varphi}K(C)\xrightarrow{\psi}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(B(R,C,R),R)

\bigvee_{n\geq 0}D(\Sigma M)^{n}\rightarrow\prod_{n\geq 0}D((\Sigma M)^{n})

Let us look at the map

D(\Sigma M)^{n}\circ_{R}B(R,C,C)\rightarrow R

that is adjoint to

D(\Sigma M)^{n}\circ_{R}(S^{1})^{n-1}\wedge I\wedge M^{n}\rightarrow R

The adjoint map to

We will now show that
\varrho_{n,n}

Proposition 5.6
.

The map

.

Now let

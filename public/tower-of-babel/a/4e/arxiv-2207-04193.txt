A Bott periodicity theorem for ℓ p \ell^{p} -spaces and the coarse Novikov conjecture at infinity Supported in part by NSFC (No. 11831006, 12171156).
Liang Guo, Zheng Luo, Qin Wang, Yazhou Zhang

Abstract
We formulate and prove a Bott periodicity theorem for an ℓ p \ell^{p} -space ( 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty ). For a proper metric space X X with bounded geometry, we introduce a version of K K -homology at infinity, denoted by K ∗ ∞ ​ ( X ) K_{*}^{\infty}(X) , and the Roe algebra at infinity, denoted by C ∞ ∗ ​ ( X ) C^{*}_{\infty}(X) . Then the coarse assembly map descends to a map from lim d → ∞ K ∗ ∞ ​ ( P d ​ ( X ) ) \lim_{d\to\infty}K_{*}^{\infty}(P_{d}(X)) to K ∗ ​ ( C ∞ ∗ ​ ( X ) ) K_{*}(C^{*}_{\infty}(X)) , called the coarse assembly map at infinity. We show that to prove the coarse Novikov conjecture, it suffices to prove the coarse assembly map at infinity is an injection. As a result, we show that the coarse Novikov conjecture holds for any metric space with bounded geometry which admits a fibred coarse embedding into an ℓ p \ell^{p} -space. These include all box spaces of a residually finite hyperbolic group and a large class of warped cones of a compact space with an action by a hyperbolic group.

Introduction

The Novikov conjecture claims that the higher signatures of closed oriented smooth manifolds are invariant under orientation preserving homotopy equivalences. It is implied by the injectivity of the Baum-Connes assembly map
[
2
]
. The coarse Novikov conjecture, as a geometric analogue of the Novikov conjecture, is the injectivity part of the coarse Baum–Connes conjecture
[
12
,
18
]
. It offers a technique to determine when an elliptic operators higher index on a noncompact complete Riemannian manifold is nonzero. A detailed survey on the coarse Novikov conjecture can be found in
[
31
]
.

In
[
4
]
, X. Chen, Q. Wang and G. Yu generalized Gromov’s notion of coarse embedding into Hilbert space and introduced the notion of fibred coarse embedding (see Definition
2.4
) and showed that if a discrete metric space
X
with bounded geometry admits a fibred coarse embedding into Hilbert space, then the maximal coarse Baum–Connes conjecture holds for
X
. Later in
[
8
]
, M. Finn-Sell showed that the coarse Novikov conjecture also holds for
X
in such a case.

Our main theorem in this paper states as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

[Theorem
2.5
]

Let
X
be a metric space with bounded geometry. If
X
admits a fibred coarse embedding into an
\ell^{p}
-space for some
p\in[1,\infty)
, then the coarse Novikov conjecture holds for
X
, i.e. the coarse assembly map

\mu:\lim_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(P_{d}(X))\to K_{*}(C^{*}(X))

is injective.

Warped cones over actions of hyperbolic groups and box spaces of residually finite hyperbolic groups are typical examples to which our main result appies. A sequence of nested normal subgroups
\{\Gamma_{i}\}
of a residually finite group
\Gamma
is said to be a filtration if the sequence has trivial intersection. The box space of
\Gamma
associated to a filtration
\{\Gamma_{i}\}
, denoted by
{\bf Box}_{\{\Gamma_{i}\}}\Gamma
, is the coarse disjoint union
\bigsqcup\Gamma/\Gamma_{i}
, where each quotient is equipped with the word length metric. Warped cones are metric spaces introduced by J. Roe
[
20
]
from discrete group actions on compact metric spaces. Typically, let
\Gamma
be a finite generated group,
M
a compact Riemannian manifold. The warped cone for
(\Gamma,M)
, denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(M)
, is constructed from the action
\Gamma
on
M
by diffeomorphisms. It is a metric space whose underlying topological space is the open cone
\mathcal{O}M
on
M
, but whose coarse geometry produces large groups of translations. The second section will have a comprehensive introduction for warped cones.

In
[
22
]
, D. Sawicki and J. Wu found a relationship between fibred coarse embeddability of warped cones and existence of proper affine isometric actions of discrete groups on Banach spaces. They proved that if
(\Gamma,M)
is free and admits a linearization by unitary operators in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
\Gamma
admits a proper affine isometric action on
\mathcal{H}
(i.e.,
\Gamma
is a-T-menable), then
\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(M)
admits a fibred coarse embedding into Hilbert space. The result still holds if we replace the Hilbert space with an
\ell^{p}
-space. The same question is also considered by Q. Wang and Z. Wang in
[
24
]
. They provided a different proof for the case of
\ell^{p}
spaces and replace the condition
\Gamma\curvearrowright M
is free by that
M
is required to contain a dense and free orbit of
\Gamma
in their paper.

As corollaries, we have the following results:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite generated discrete group and let
M
be a compact manifold. Assume that the action of
\Gamma
on
M
is linearisable in an
\ell^{p}
-space and
\Gamma\curvearrowright M
is free (or
M
contains a dense free orbit). If
\Gamma
admits a proper affine isometric action on an
\ell^{p}
-space, then the coarse Novikov conjecture holds for the warped cone
\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(M)
.

Combining the result in
[
30
,
21
,
3
]
, we also have

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated residually finite hyperbolic group, and
G=\widehat{\Gamma}((\Gamma_{n}))
the corresponding profinite completion. Then the coarse Novikov conjecture hold for
\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(G)
and any box apace
{\bf Box}_{\{\Gamma_{n}\}}\Gamma
.

, Theorem 4.2]
. The second term is proved in
[
22
, Theorem 3.2]
for the case of
p=2
. Actually their prove also holds for any
p\geq 1
. One can also find another proof in
[
24
, Theorem 3.2]
for the case of
p\neq 2
.

In the last of this section, we introduce a typical example constructed by profinite completion:

Examples 2.9
.

Let
\Gamma
be a residually finite group and
\{\Gamma_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
a decreasing sequence of finite index normal subgroups with
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{n}=\{e\}
. We denote the quotient group by
G_{n}=\Gamma/\Gamma_{n}
. The identity map of
\Gamma
induces an group homomorphism
G_{n}\to G_{n-1}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have the following sequence:

\cdots\to G_{n}\to\cdots\to G_{2}\to G_{1}\to 1.

We denote
\widehat{\Gamma}((\Gamma_{n}))
the inverse limit of this sequence and call it the profinite completion of
\Gamma
with respect to
(\Gamma_{n})
. It is well-known that
\widehat{\Gamma}((\Gamma_{n}))
is a compact metrizable group containing
\Gamma
as a dense subgroup and
\Gamma
acts freely on
\widehat{\Gamma}((\Gamma_{n}))
by left multiplication and admit a linearization in
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R})
for any
1\leq p<\infty
(
[
22
, Lemma 3.19]
).

If
\Gamma
admits a proper affine isometric action on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R})
for some
p\geq 1
, then the warped cone
\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(\widehat{\Gamma}((\Gamma_{n})))
admits a fibred coarse embedding into
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R})
.

Combining with Theorem
2.5
, we have the following corollaries:

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group,
Y
be a compact metric space with a free
\Gamma
-action. If this action admits a linearization in the
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R})
and
\Gamma
admits a proper affine isometric action on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R})
for some
p\geq 1
, then the coarse Novikov conjecture holds for
\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}Y
.

G. Yu proved hyperbolic groups always admits a proper affine isometric action on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R})
for a sufficiently large
p\geq 1
in
[
30
, Theorem 1.1]
. In particular, we have the following:

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated residually finite hyperbolic group, and
G=\widehat{\Gamma}((\Gamma_{n}))
the corresponding profinite completion. Then the coarse Novikov conjecture holds for
\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(G)
.

3
A Bott periodicity theorem for
\ell^{p}
spaces

In this section, we will introduce a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}(B)
associated to a given real Banach space
B=\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R})
and calculate its
K
-theory.

In
[
14
]
, G. Kasparov and G. Yu introduced a
C^{*}
-algebra for Banach space with Property (H). They pointed out that its
K
-theory can be calculated if the base space is an
\ell^{p}
-space. We provide a detailed proof in this section. We would like to mention that our construction is slightly different from theirs. The
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}(X)
in
[
14
]
is generated by some certain functions on a dense subspace
V
of
B
. To build the twisted algebras in Section 6, for any affine isometry in Definition
2.4
, we shall need a
C^{*}
-isomorphism associated to the affine isometry to translate the support of a function in
\mathcal{A}(X)
. However, the affine isometries in Definition
2.4
may not preserve the dense subspace in
[
14
]
. Our construction
\mathcal{A}(B)
will circumvent this problem.

3.1
A Clifford algebras for a Hilbert space

In this subsection, we will recall the definition of Clifford algebras for Hilbert spaces.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an infinite dimensional separable real Hilbert space. For each
n\geq 0
, denote

\mathcal{H}^{\odot n}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\odot\underbrace{\mathcal{H}\odot\mathcal{H}\odot\cdots\odot\mathcal{H}}_{\text{n times}},

where
\odot
means the algebraic tensor product over
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 0}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}
. Then
\mathcal{H}^{\odot n}_{\mathbb{C}}
is equipped with a natural structure of complex vector space. We define the complex inner product on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\odot n}
by the formula:

\langle z_{1}\otimes v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n},z_{2}\otimes w_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes w_{n}\rangle=z_{1}\cdot\overline{z_{2}}\cdot\langle v_{1},w_{1}\rangle\cdot\ldots\cdot\langle v_{n},w_{n}\rangle,

and denote
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\otimes n}
the completion of
\mathcal{H}^{\odot n}_{\mathbb{C}}
under the inner product about. We denote
1\otimes v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}
simply by
v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}
.

Denote
K^{n}
the closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}_{\mathbb{C}}
which is spanned by

\left\{v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}-\text{sgn}(\sigma)v_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{\sigma(n)}\,\big|\,v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\in\mathcal{H},\text{$\sigma$ is a $k$-permutation}\right\}.

Define the
k
-th complex exterior power of
\mathcal{H}
to be the quotient Hilbert space

\wedge^{n}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}_{\mathbb{C}}/K^{n},

where the equivalence class in
\wedge^{n}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
is always denoted by
v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n}
. Let
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n},\cdots\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. Then the set
\{e_{i_{1}}\wedge e_{i_{2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{n}}\mid i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{n}\}
gives an orthonormal basis of
\wedge^{k}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Define the antisymmetric Fock space:

\wedge^{*}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\wedge^{k}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}.

For any
v\in\mathcal{H}
, we define the creation operator
C(v)
by

C(v)(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k})=v\wedge v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k}.

It is not hard to check that the adjoint operator defined by

C^{*}(v)(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}\langle v,v_{i}\rangle v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{v_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k}.

We define a self-adjoint operator

\hat{v}:=C(v)+C^{*}(v)\in\mathcal{B}(\wedge^{*}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}})

for each
v\in\mathcal{H}
, then we have that

\hat{w}\hat{v}+\hat{v}\hat{w}=2\langle w,v\rangle.

The complex Cliiford algebra
\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H})
to be subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(\wedge^{*}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
generated by
\{\hat{v}\mid v\in\mathcal{H}\}
.

If
V
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, then
\wedge^{*}V_{\mathbb{C}}
is clearly a subspace of
\wedge^{*}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Thus
\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(V)
can be naturally viewed as a subalgebra of
\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\{V_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence of finite-dimensional Hilbert space such that
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}V_{n}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{n})\cong\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}).

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can view
\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{n})
as a subalgebra of
\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H})
. It suffices to prove

\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{n})}=\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}),

which follows directly from the fact that
\|\hat{v}\|_{\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H})}=\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}V_{n}}=\mathcal{H}
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Actually, there is another way to construct the Clifford algebra for
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{\odot n}_{\mathbb{C}}
be tha complex algebraic tensor algebra of
\mathcal{H}
. Let
I
be the ideal of
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
which is generated by
\left\{v\otimes v-\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\cdot 1\mid v\in\mathcal{H}\right\}
. Denote
\mathbb{C}l(\mathcal{H})
the quotient algebra
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})/I
. As a linear space,
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
is also a dense subspace of the Hilbert space
\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Denote by
H(\mathbb{C}l(\mathcal{H}))
the completion of
\mathbb{C}l(\mathcal{H})
under the inner product induced by
\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
\mathbb{C}l(\mathcal{H})
has a canonical faithful representation by the left multiplication on
H(\mathbb{C}l(\mathcal{H}))
. Then the Clifford algebra

\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H})=\overline{\mathbb{C}l(\mathcal{H})^{\|\cdot\|}}

where the norm
\|\cdot\|
is given by the canonical representation on
H(\mathbb{C}l(\mathcal{H}))
.

3.2
A
C^{*}
-algebra associated with a real
\ell^{p}
space

Fix
1\leq p<\infty
and denote
B=\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R})
to be the real Banach space. Specially, we denote
\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R})
the real Hilbert space. We define the sign function
\text{sgn}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by the formula

\text{sgn}(a)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&&,a=0;\\
&a|a|^{-1}&&,a\neq 0.\end{aligned}\right.

Denote by
S(B)
the closed unit sphere of
B
. Recall the Mazur map
\psi:S(B)\to S(\mathcal{H})
, defined by

\psi(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n},\cdots)=(sgn(a_{1})|a_{1}|^{p/2},\cdots,\text{sgn}(a_{n})|a_{n}|^{p/2},\cdots),

is a uniform homeomorphism, i.e., the
r
-oscillation of
\psi
and
\psi^{-1}
tend to
0
as
r
tends to
0
, where the
r
-oscillation of
\psi
is defined to be

\omega_{r}(\psi)=\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in S(B),\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{p}\leq r}\|\psi(x)-\psi(x^{\prime})\|.

We consider the
p/2
-Hölder extension
of the Mazur map
\psi
, which is a map
\phi:B\to\mathcal{H}
defined by

\phi(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&&,x=0;\\
&\|x\|^{p/2}_{p}\psi\left(\|x\|_{p}^{-1}x\right)&&,x\neq 0.\end{aligned}\right.

Actually, we have the following uniform homeomorphism extension theorem due to E. Odell and T. Schlumprecht
[
15
, Proposition 2.9]
and Q. Cheng
[
6
, Theorem 2.4]
:

Proposition 3.3

(
[
15
,
6
]
)
.

For any
R>0
, the extended Mazur map restricted on the ball
\phi|_{Ball_{B}(0,R)}:Ball_{B}(0,R)\to Ball_{\mathcal{H}}(0,R^{p/2})
is a uniform homeomorphism, i.e.,

\omega_{r}\left(\phi|_{Ball_{B}(0,R)}\right)\to 0\text{ as }r\to 0.

As a corollary,
\phi
forms a homeomorphism between
B
and
\mathcal{H}
. When
p=2
, the extended Mazur map is the identity map on
\mathcal{H}
.

Denote by
\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,\infty)
. Let
C(B\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{R}))
be the algebra of all continuous functions from
B\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
to
\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{R})
. We view
\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H})
as a subalgebra of
\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{R})
in the following paper.

Definition 3.4
.

Define
C_{b,0}(B\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{R}))
to be the subalgebra of
C(B\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{R}))
consisting of all bounded functions
f
such that

f(x,0)\in\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H})\subseteq\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{R})

for all
x\in B
.

Definition 3.5
.

For any
x_{0}\in B
, we define the Clifford operator
C_{x_{0}}\in C(B\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{R}))
by

C_{x_{0}}(x,t)=(\phi(x-x_{0}),t)\in\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{R}\subseteq\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{R})

for any
x\in B
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

We denote
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})_{ev}
(or
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})_{odd}
, respectively) the subset of
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
of all even (or odd, respectively) functions. For any Hilbert space
H
, we next define the functional calculus of
\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(H)
: for any
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R})_{ev}
and
v\in\mathcal{H}\subset\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(H)
, we define
f(v)=f(\|v\|)\in\mathbb{C}\subset\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(H)
and for
g\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R})_{odd}
, we define the functional calculus by

g(v)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&&,v=0\\
&g(\|v\|)\frac{v}{\|v\|}&&,v\neq 0,\end{aligned}\right.

where
g(v)
is an element of
\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(H)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
x_{0}\in B
and
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, the Bott map
\beta_{x_{0}}
is defined by functional calculus such that

(\beta_{x_{0}}(f))(x,t)=f(C_{x_{0}}(x,t)),

for each
x\in B
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then
\beta_{x_{0}}
gives a graded homomorphism from
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
to
C_{b,0}(B\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{R}))
.

Proof.

Combining the definition of the functional calculus and the fact that
\phi
is continuous, it is obvious that
\beta_{x_{0}}(f)
is continuous.
∎

Definition 3.7
.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}(B)
is the
C^{*}
-subalgebra
C_{b,0}(B\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{R}))
generated by

\{\beta_{x}(f)\mid x\in B,f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\}.

Let
W
be a subspace of
B
, the algebra
\mathcal{A}(B,W)
is defined to be the
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}(B)
generated by

\{\beta_{x}(f)\mid x\in W\subseteq B,f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\}.

Lemma 3.8
.

Fix
f\in\mathcal{S}
.

(1)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\|\beta_{x}(f)-\beta_{y}(f)\|\leq\varepsilon

whenever
x,y\in B
satisfies
\|x-y\|_{p}\leq\delta
.

(2)

As a corollary, let
\{x_{n}\}\in B
be a sequence which converges to
x_{0}\in B
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\beta_{x_{n}}(f)-\beta_{x_{0}}(f)\|=0

for each
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Let
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
be the even part and odd part of
f
. There exists
R>0
such that
|f(t)|\leq\varepsilon/2
for all
|t|>R
.

Case 1. For the even part, by definition, for each
x,y,z\in B
, we have that

\|(\beta_{x}(f_{0})-\beta_{y}(f_{0}))(z,t)\|=|f_{0}(\|C_{x}(z,t)\|)-f_{0}(\|C_{y}(z,t)\|)|.

If
(z,t)
satisfies that both
\|C_{x}(z,t)\|
and
\|C_{y}(z,t)\|
are greater than
R
, then

|f_{0}(\|C_{x}(z,t)\|)-f_{0}(\|C_{y}(z,t)\|)|\leq|f_{0}(\|C_{x}(z,t)\|)|+|f_{0}(\|C_{y}(z,t)\|)|\leq\varepsilon.

Notice that
\|C_{x}(z,t)\|=(\|\phi(z-x)\|^{2}+t^{2})^{1/2}=(\|z-x\|^{p}_{p}+t^{2})^{1/2}
.
If one of
\|C_{x}(z,t)\|
or
\|C_{y}(z,t)\|
is less than
R
, without loss of generality, assume that
\|C_{x}(z,t)\|<R
. Then
\|z-x\|^{p}_{p}<R^{2}
. Notice that

\begin{split}\left|\|C_{x}(z,t)\|-\|C_{y}(z,t)\|\right|&=\left(\|z-x\|^{p}_{p}+t^{2}\right)^{1/2}-\left(\|z-y\|^{p}_{p}+t^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\left(\|z-x\|^{p}_{p}-\|z-y\|^{p}_{p}\right)^{1/2},\end{split}

and

\left|\|z-x\|_{p}-\|z-y\|_{p}\right|\leq\|x-y\|_{p}.

Set
\delta_{0}>0
satisfying that
|f_{0}(t_{1})-f_{0}(t_{2})|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for all
t_{1},t_{2}
with
|t_{1}-t_{2}|<\delta_{0}
.
As
y(t)=t^{p}
is uniform continuous in
[0,R^{p/2}+1]
, then there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that
|t_{1}^{p}-t_{2}^{p}|<\delta_{0}^{2}
for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,R^{p/2}+1]
with
|t_{1}-t_{2}|<\delta_{1}
. Set
\delta_{ev}=\min\{\delta_{1},1\}
. If
\|x-y\|_{p}\leq\delta_{ev}
, then both
\|z-x\|_{p}
and
\|z-y\|_{p}
are in
[0,R^{p/2}+1]
and we have that

|f_{0}(\|C_{x}(z,t)\|)-f_{0}(\|C_{y}(z,t)\|)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Case 2. For the odd part, assume that there exists
g\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R})_{ev}
such that
f_{1}(t)=tg(t)
. Such elements are dense in
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})_{odd}
. Then, by definition,

\begin{split}\|(\beta_{x}(f_{1})-\beta_{y}(f_{1}))(z,t)\|=&\left\|g(\|C_{x}(z,t)\|)C_{x}(z,t)-g(\|C_{y}(z,t)\|)C_{y}(z,t)\right\|\\
\leq&\left\|g(\|C_{x}(z,t)\|)-g(\|C_{y}(z,t)\|)C_{x}(z,t)\right\|\\
&+|g(C_{y}(z,t))|\cdot\|C_{x}(z,t)-C_{y}(z,t)\|.\end{split}

Similarly, if both
\|C_{x}(z,t)\|
and
\|C_{y}(z,t)\|
are greater than
R
, then

\left\|g(\|C_{x}(z,t)\|)C_{x}(z,t)-g(\|C_{y}(z,t)\|)C_{y}(z,t)\right\|\leq|f_{1}(\|C_{x}(z,t)\|)|+|f_{1}(\|C_{y}(z,t)\|)|\leq\varepsilon.

If one of
\|C_{x}(z,t)\|
or
\|C_{y}(z,t)\|
is less than
R
, with a similar argument as above, we can find a
\delta_{3}>0
such that
|g(\|C_{x}(z,t)\|)-g(\|C_{y}(z,t)\|)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4R}
. For the second term, we have that

\|C_{x}(z,t)-C_{y}(z,t)\|=\|\phi(z-x)-\phi(z-y)\|.

By Proposition
3.3
, there exists
0<\delta_{4}<1
associated with
\phi|_{Ball_{B}(0,R^{2/p}+1)}
such that if
\|x-y\|\leq\delta_{4}
, then
\|\phi(z-x)-\phi(z-y)\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4\|g\|}
. Set
\delta_{odd}=\min\{\delta_{3},\delta_{4}\}
, we conclude that if
\|x-y\|_{p}\leq\delta_{odd}
, then

\|(\beta_{x}(f_{1})-\beta_{y}(f_{1}))(z,t)\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

for all
(z,t)\in B\times\mathbb{R}
.

Set
\delta=\min\{\delta_{ev},\delta_{odd}\}
, then

\|\beta_{x}(f)-\beta_{y}(f)\|\leq\|\beta_{x}(f_{0})-\beta_{y}(f_{0})\|+\|\beta_{x}(f_{1})-\beta_{y}(f_{1})\|\leq\varepsilon.

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\{W_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence of finite dimensional linear subspaces of
B
with
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}W_{n}
dense in
B
. Then

(1)

\mathcal{A}(B,B)=\mathcal{A}(B)
and
\mathcal{A}(B,W_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{A}(B,W_{n+1})
;

(2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{A}(B,W_{n})=\mathcal{A}(B,\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}W_{n})=\mathcal{A}(B,\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}W_{n}})
.

Proof.

The first term and the first equality in (2) are immediate from the definition. The second equality in (2) comes from Lemma
3.8
.
∎

3.3
The
K
-theory of
\mathcal{A}(B)

In this subsection, we shall calculate the
K
-theory of
\mathcal{A}(B)
. We will show that the Bott map
(\beta_{x_{0}})_{*}:K_{*}(\mathcal{S})\to K_{*}(\mathcal{A}(B))
is an isomorphism for any
x_{0}\in B
. One can find a proof in
[
10
, Remark 7.7]
for the case when
p=2
. We remark that the Bott periodicity theorem holds for any Banach space which is spherical equivalent to
\ell^{2}
by using a similar argument.

Let
N_{n}=\{0,1,\cdots,n\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
. We denote
B_{n}=\ell^{p}(N_{n},\mathbb{R})
and
H_{n}=\ell^{2}(N_{n},\mathbb{R})
to be the finite dimensional subspaces of
B
and
\mathcal{H}
, respectively. It is clear that
\overline{\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}}=B
and
\overline{\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}}=\mathcal{H}
. Denote
\mathcal{S}=C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
equipped with the grading according to even and odd functions.

Define
C_{b,0}(B_{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(H_{n}\oplus\mathbb{R}))
to be the subalgebra of
C_{b}(B_{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(H_{n}\oplus\mathbb{R}))
consisting of all bounded function
f
such that

f(x,0)\in\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(H_{n})\subseteq\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(H_{n}\oplus\mathbb{R}).

Since the Mazur map restricts to a uniform homeomorphism
\psi_{n}=\psi|_{B_{n}}:S(B_{n})\to S(H_{n})
, for any
x_{0}\in B_{n}
, we can still define the Clifford operator
C^{n}_{x_{0}}\in C(B_{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(H_{n}\oplus\mathbb{R}))
on
B_{n}
by

C^{n}_{x_{0}}(x,t)=(\phi_{n}(x-x_{0}),t)\in\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(H_{n}\oplus\mathbb{R}),

where
\phi_{n}:B_{n}\to H_{n}
is the extended Mazur map of
\psi_{n}
and
x\in B_{n}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The Bott map
\beta_{x_{0}}^{n}:\mathcal{S}\to C_{b,0}(B_{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\textup{Cliff}_{\mathbb{C}}(H_{n}\oplus\mathbb{R}))
is defined to be

\left(\beta^{n}_{x_{0}}(f)\right)(x,t)=f(C^{n}_{x_{0}}(x,t)).

Definition 3.10
.

Define
\mathcal{A}(B_{n})
to be the subalgebra of

Theorem 4.17

Suppose

is an isomorphism for each

Proof.

By using an Eilenberg swindle argument as

5
Reduction to the coarse Novikov conjecture at infinity

In this section, we discuss the strategy to prove the main theorem. As general metric spaces (such as warped cones) are always not of the form of coarse disjoint unions of a sequence of bounded subsets, we can not use the techniques in
[
4
]
directly. We will show that there exists an assmbly map at infinity from the
K
-homology group at infinity to the
K
-theory group of the Roe algebra at infinity, and the coarse Novikov conjecture can always be reduced to verifying the assembly map at infinity to be injective whether the space is of the form of coarse disjoint union or not.

5.1
The case of coarse disjoint unions

In this section, we assume that

is a coarse disjoint union of a sequence of uniformly bounded geometry, finite metric spaces such that

as

[

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

be a coarse disjoint union of a sequence of uniformly bounded geometry, finite metric spaces such that

K_{*}^{\infty}(P_{d}(X))=\frac{\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}K_{*}(P_{d}(X_{n}))}{\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}K_{*}(P_{d}(X_{n}))}.

Proof.

For any
d\geq 0
, there exists

large enough such that

for

The next notion is introduced by M. Finn-Sell in

A bounded geometry proper metric space

Lemma 5.5

A bounded geometry proper metric space

has only finite coarse components if and only if it is a coarse disjoint union of a sequence of finite metric spaces.

Proof.

Let

has only finite coarse components.

is strongly Lipschitz homotopy equivalent to
W=(W_{g})_{g\in G}
, then

K_{*}(A^{*}_{L,0,\infty}(Y))\cong K_{*}(A^{*}_{L,0,\infty}(W)).

(2)

If
G_{R}=(B(g,R))_{g\in G}
, then

K_{*}(A^{*}_{L,0,\infty}(G_{R}))=0.

As a corollary, the evaluation map induces an isomorphism on the level of
K
-theory, i.e.

(ev_{A})_{*}:K_{*}(A^{*}_{L,\infty}(G_{R}))\to K_{*}(A^{*}_{\infty}(G_{R}))

is an isomorphism.∎

Proof of Proposition
6.13
.

By Proposition
6.16
, we have the following commuting diagram:

\textstyle{\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(C^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))_{O})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(ev^{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty})_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))_{O})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}\lim\limits_{R\to\infty}K_{*}(A^{*}_{L,\infty}(G_{R}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{(ev_{A})_{*}}
\textstyle{\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}\lim\limits_{R\to\infty}K_{*}(A^{*}_{\infty}(G_{R}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{\lim\limits_{R\to\infty}\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(A^{*}_{L,\infty}(G_{R}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(ev_{A})_{*}}
\textstyle{\lim\limits_{R\to\infty}\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(A^{*}_{\infty}(G_{R})).}

in which all vertical maps are isomorphisms. By Lemma
6.18
, we have that the bottom horizontal map
(ev_{A})_{*}
is an isomorphism. Thus,
(ev_{\infty}^{\mathcal{A}})_{*}
is also an isomorphism.
∎

Finally, we are able to prove Theorem
6.8
.

Proof of Theorem
6.8
.

For any
r>0
, we define
O_{x}^{(r)}
by

O_{x}^{(r)}=\bigcup_{y\in B(x,l_{d,m})}B(t_{x}(y)(s(y)),r)

for any
x\in K_{d,m+1}\cap U_{d,m}
. Then it is easy to check that
O^{(r)}=(O_{x}^{(r)})_{x\in X}
is a coherent collection of open subsets.

For any
d\geq 0
, by the definition of the twisted algebras, we have that

C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))=\lim_{r\to\infty}C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))_{O^{(r)}},

C^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))=\lim_{r\to\infty}C^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))_{O^{(r)}}.

Notice that the limits of
C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))_{O^{(r)}}
with limits order
\lim_{d\to\infty}\lim_{r\to\infty}
and
\lim_{d\to\infty}\lim_{r\to\infty}
are actually the same. Consequently, it suffices to prove that the
K
-theortic homomorphism

ev_{*}:\lim_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(C^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))_{O^{(r)}})\to\lim_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))_{O^{(r)}})

induced by the evaluation-at-zero map is an isomorphism for each
r>0
.

For any given
r>0
, as
X
has bounded geometry, there exists
N_{r}>0
such that

X=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{N_{r}}X_{k}

satisfying that for any
z\in Z_{d}\cap U_{d,m}
and
x,y\in X_{k}\cap B(z,l_{d,m})
, we have

\|t_{z}(x)(s(x))-t_{z}(y)(s(y))\|>2r.

We shall define

O^{(r)}_{x,k}=\bigcup_{y\in X_{k}\cap B(x,l_{d,m})}B(t_{x}(y)(s(y)),r)

for all
x\in X
and
k\in\{1,\cdots,N_{r}\}
. Then
O^{(r)}_{k}=(O^{(r)}_{x,k})_{x\in X}
is a coherent collection which satisfies the condition in Proposition
6.13
. Thus we have that

ev_{*}:\lim_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(C^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))_{O^{(r)}_{k}})\to\lim_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))_{O^{(r)}_{k}})

for all
k\in\{1,\cdots,N_{r}\}
. Then it follows from a Mayer-Vietories argument by using Lemma
6.12
to complete the proof.
∎

7
The geometric Bott map and proof of the main theorem

In this section, we shall define the Bott map
\beta
from the
K
-theory group of the Roe algebra to
K
-theory group of the twisted Roe algebra and its localization algebraic version
\beta_{L}
. The construction relies heavily on the Bott periodicity theorem we introduced in Section 5.

For each
t\geq 1
and
g\in\mathcal{S}
, denote
g_{t}\in\mathcal{S}
to be the function

g_{t}(r)=g(t^{-1}r).

For any
x\in X
, we define the Bott map
\beta(x):\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{A}(B)
to be

(\beta(x))(g)=\beta_{t_{x}(x)(s(x))}(g),

where
t_{x}
is the trivilazition on
B(x,l_{d,m})
defined as in Definition
2.4
,
t_{x}(x)(s(x))\in B
and
\beta_{t_{x}(x)(s(x))}
is the Bott map defined as in Section 5.

Definition 7.1
.

For each
d\geq 0
and
t\in[1,\infty)
, define a map

\beta_{t}:\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[P_{d}(X)]\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B)]

for each
g\in\mathcal{S}
,
[T]\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[P_{d}(X)]
by the formula

\beta_{t}(g\widehat{\otimes}[T])=[\beta_{t}(g\widehat{\otimes}T)],

where

(\beta_{t}(g\widehat{\otimes}T))(x,y)=(\beta(x))(g_{t})\widehat{\otimes}T(x,y).

Definition 7.2
.

For each
d\geq 0
and
t\in[1,\infty)
, define a map

(\beta_{L})_{t}:\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}\mathbb{C}_{L,\infty}[P_{d}(X)]\to\mathbb{C}_{L,\infty}[P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B)]

for each
g\in\mathcal{S}
,
f\in\mathbb{C}_{L,\infty}[P_{d}(X)]
by the formula

((\beta_{L})_{t}(g\widehat{\otimes}f))(r)=\beta_{t}(g\widehat{\otimes}f(r)),

for each
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

The following lemma is proved similarly with
[
29
, Proposition 7.6]
and
[
4
, Lemma 7.3]

Lemma 7.3
.

For each
d\geq 0
, the maps
\beta_{t}
and
(\beta_{L})_{t}
extend respectively to asymptotic morphisms

\beta:\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X))\leadsto C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X),A(B))

\beta_{L}:\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X))\leadsto C^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X),A(B)).

Proof.

We will only prove it for
\beta
and the case for
\beta_{L}
follows similarly.

First of all, we claim that

\left\|[\beta_{t}(gh\widehat{\otimes}ST)]-[\beta_{t}(g\widehat{\otimes}S)][\beta_{t}(h\widehat{\otimes}T)]\right\|\to 0

for any
[S],[T]\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[P_{d}(X)]
. By definition, we can calculate that the
(x,y)
-th entry of the above matrix coefficients of
\beta_{t}(gh\widehat{\otimes}ST)
and
\beta_{t}(g\widehat{\otimes}S)\beta_{t}(h\widehat{\otimes}T)
are

\sum_{z\in Z_{d}}\beta(x)(g_{t}h_{t})\widehat{\otimes}S(x,z)T(z,y)

and

\sum_{z\in Z_{d}}\beta(x)(g_{t})(t_{xy})_{*}(\beta(y)(h_{t}))\widehat{\otimes}S(x,z)T(z,y),

respectively. By using
[
9
, Lemma 3.4]
, it suffices to prove for any
\varepsilon>0
and
h\in\mathcal{S}
, there exists
T>0
such that

\|\beta(x)(h_{t})-(t_{xy})_{*}(\beta(y)(h_{t}))\|<\varepsilon

for all
t\geq T
. To simplify the notation, we denote
v_{x}=t_{x}(x)(s(x))
and
v_{y}=t_{x}(y)(s(y))
. Write
R
the propagation of
[ST]
. Assmue that
d(x,y)\leq R
and
d(x,x_{0})
is large enough such that
t_{y}(y)(s(y))=t_{xy}(v_{y})
, where
x_{0}
is the base point we choose. Combining Definition
6.1
, we have that

\begin{split}\|\beta(x)(h_{t})-(t_{xy})_{*}\beta(y)(h_{t})\|&=\sup_{(v,r)\in B\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\|h_{t}(C_{v_{x}}(v,r))-h_{t}(C_{v_{y}}(v,r))\|\\
&=\sup_{(v,r)\in B\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left\|h\left(t^{-1}\phi(v-v_{x}),t^{-1}r\right)-h\left(t^{-1}\phi(v-v_{y}),t^{-1}r\right)\right\|\\
&=\sup_{(v,r)\in B\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left\|h\left(\phi(t^{-2/p}(v-v_{x})),t^{-1}r\right)-h\left(\phi(t^{-2/p}(v-v_{y})),t^{-1}r\right)\right\|\\
&=\|\beta_{t^{-2/p}v_{x}}(h)-\beta_{t^{-2/p}v_{y}}(h)\|\end{split}

Notice that
\|v_{x}-v_{y}\|\leq\rho_{+}(d(x,y))\leq\rho_{+}(R)
, where
\rho_{+}
is the controlled function defined as in Definition
2.4
. By using the fact
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}t^{-2/p}=0
and Lemma
3.8
, we complete the proof of the claim.

The rest part of the proof is similar with
[
29
, Lemma 7.6]
. One can similarly prove that

\beta_{t}(g\widehat{\otimes}[T])\leq\|g\|\cdot\|[T]\|.

for all
g\in\mathcal{S}
and
[T]\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[P_{d}(X)]
. Hence
\beta_{t}
extends to an well-defined asymptotic morphism from
\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}_{max}C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X))
to
C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))
. Since
\mathcal{S}
is nuclear, we complete the proof.
∎

Note that the asymptotic morphisms

\beta_{t}:\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X))\leadsto C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))

(\beta_{L})_{t}:\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}C^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X))\leadsto C^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))

induce homomorphisms on
K
-theory

\beta_{*}:K_{*}(\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X)))\leadsto K_{*}(C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B)))

(\beta_{L})_{*}:K_{*}(\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}C^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X)))\leadsto K_{*}(C^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))).

Theorem 7.4
.

For any
d>0
, the Bott map

(\beta_{L})_{*}:K_{*}(\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}C^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X)))\leadsto K_{*}(C^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))).

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The
K
-theory of the localization algebra is invariant under the strong Lipschtiz homotopy equivalence (see
[
28
,
29
]
). By a Mayer-Vietoris sequence argument and induction on the dimension of the skeletons
[
13
,
28
]
, the general case can be reduced to the zero-dimensional case, i.e., if
\Delta^{(0)}\subseteq P_{d}(X)
is the
0
-skeleton of
P_{d}(X)
, then

(\beta_{L})_{*}:K_{*}\left(\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}C^{*}_{L,\infty}\left(\Delta^{(0)}\right)\right)\to K_{*}\left(C^{*}_{L,\infty}\left(\Delta^{(0)},\mathcal{A}(B)\right)\right)

is an isomorphism.

Notice that

K_{*}\left(\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}C^{*}_{L,\infty}\left(\Delta^{(0)}\right)\right)=\frac{\prod_{\gamma\in\Delta^{(0)}}K_{*}(\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}C^{*}_{L}(\{\gamma\}))}{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Delta^{(0)}}K_{*}(\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}C^{*}_{L}(\{\gamma\}))}

K_{*}\left(C^{*}_{L,\infty}\left(\Delta^{(0)},\mathcal{A}(B)\right)\right)=\frac{\prod_{\gamma\in\Delta^{(0)}}K_{*}(C^{*}_{L}(\{\gamma\},\mathcal{A}(B)))}{\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Delta^{(0)}}K_{*}(C^{*}_{L}(\{\gamma\},\mathcal{A}(B)))}.

Moreover, one can see that
K_{*}(\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}C^{*}_{L}(\{\gamma\}))\cong K_{*}(\mathcal{S}\widehat{\otimes}\mathcal{K})
and
K_{*}(C^{*}_{L}(\{\gamma\},\mathcal{A}(B)))\cong K_{*}(\mathcal{K}\widehat{\otimes}\mathcal{A}(B))
and the Bott map
(\beta_{L})_{*}
coincides with
\beta_{t_{\gamma}(\gamma)(s(\gamma))}
when
(\beta_{L})_{*}
restricts on
K_{*}(\{\gamma\})\cong K_{*}(\mathcal{S})
to
K_{*}(C^{*}_{L}(\{\gamma\},\mathcal{A}(B)))\cong K_{*}(\mathcal{A}(B))
. Then the theorem follows from Theorem
3.15
directly.
∎

Then we can finally prove the main result:

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

We have the following commuting diagram:

\textstyle{K_{*}(C^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X)))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(\beta_{L})_{*}\hskip 16.38895pt}
\scriptstyle{(ev_{\infty})_{*}}
\textstyle{K_{*}(C^{*}_{L,\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B)))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(ev^{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty})_{*}}
\textstyle{K_{*}(C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X)))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta_{*}\hskip 16.38895pt}
\textstyle{K_{*}(C^{*}_{\infty}(P_{d}(X),\mathcal{A}(B))).}

By Theorem
6.8
and Theorem
7.4
, we show that
ev_{\infty}^{\mathcal{A}}
is an isomorphism and
(\beta_{L})_{*}
is injective. It forces
ev_{\infty}
to be an injection. Combining Theorem
5.7
, we conclude that the coarse assembly map

\mu:\lim_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(P_{d}(X))\to K_{*}(C^{*}(X))

is injective.
∎

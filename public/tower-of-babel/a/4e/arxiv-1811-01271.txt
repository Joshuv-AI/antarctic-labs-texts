1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the class functions
f
which are analytic in the open unit disk
\Delta=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
.
Also, let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{H}
denote the class of functions
f
of the form

(1.1)

f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\quad(z\in\Delta),

which are normalized by the condition
f(0)=0=f^{\prime}(0)-1
in
\Delta
. The subclass of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of
all univalent functions
f(z)
in
\Delta
will be denoted by

\mathcal{S}
. We say that a function
f\in\mathcal{S}
is starlike of order
\alpha
, where
0\leq\alpha<1
if, and only if,

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}>\alpha\quad(z\in\Delta).

We denote by
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
the class of starlike functions of order
\alpha
. The class
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
was introduced by Robertson (see
[
19
]
).
Also, we say that a function
f\in\mathcal{S}
is strongly starlike of order
\beta
, where
0<\beta\leq 1
if, and only if,

\left|\arg\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}\right|<\frac{\pi\beta}{2}\quad(z\in\Delta).

The functions class of strongly starlike functions of order
\beta
is denoted by
\mathcal{SS}^{*}(\beta)
.
The class
\mathcal{SS}^{*}(\beta)
was introduced independently by
Stankiewicz (see
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
) and by Brannan and Kirvan (see
[
1
]
).
We remark that
\mathcal{SS}^{*}(1)\equiv\mathcal{S}^{*}(0)=\mathcal{S}^{*}
, where
\mathcal{S}^{*}
denotes the class of starlike functions.

Let
f(z)
and
g(z)
be two analytic functions in
\Delta
. Then the function

f(z)
is said to be subordinate to
g(z)
in
\Delta
, written by

f(z)\prec g(z)
or
f\prec g
, if there exists an analytic function
w(z)
in
\Delta
with
w(0)=0
and
|w(z)|<1
, and such that

f(z)=g(w(z))
for all
z\in\Delta
.

In the sequel, we consider the analytic function

G(z):=G(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},c)(z)
as follows

(1.2)

G(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},c)(z):=\left(\frac{1+cz}{1-z}\right)^{(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})/2}\quad(G(0)=1,z\in\Delta),

where
0<\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\leq 1
,
c=e^{\pi i\theta}
and

\theta=\frac{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1}}
. Also, we consider the set
\Omega_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}
as follows

(1.3)

\Omega_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}:=\left\{w\in\mathbb{C}:-\frac{\pi\alpha_{1}}{2}<\arg\{w\}<\frac{\pi\alpha_{2}}{2}\right\}.

We note that the function
G(z)
is convex univalent in
\Delta
and maps
\Delta
onto
\Omega_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}
(see

[
31
]
). Since

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1+cz}{1-z}\right)^{(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})/2}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{(1+c)z}{1-z}\right)^{(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})/2}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\binom{(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})/2}{k}(1+c)^{k}\left(\frac{z}{1-z}\right)^{k}\quad(z\in\Delta),

using the binomial formula, we obtain

(1.4)

G(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}z^{n}\quad(z\in\Delta),

where

(1.5)

\lambda_{n}:=\lambda_{n}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},c)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\binom{(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})/2}{k}(1+c)^{k}\quad(n\geq 1).

We note that
\lambda_{n}
may be conveniently written in the form

\lambda_{n}=\frac{(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})(1+c)}{2}{{}_{2}F_{1}}(1-n,1-(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})/2;2;1+c)\quad(n\geq 1),

where notation
{}_{2}F_{1}
stands for the Gauss hypergeometric function.

The main purpose of this paper is to study the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
which is provided below.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
belongs to the class

\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
, if
f
satisfies the following two–sided inequality

(1.6)

-\frac{\pi\alpha_{1}}{2}<\arg\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}<\frac{\pi\alpha_{2}}{2}\quad(z\in\Delta),

where
0<\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\leq 1
.

The class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
was introduced by Takahashi and Nunokawa (see

[
29
]
). We recall here the fact, that in
[
2
]
and

[

\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\subset\mathcal{S}^{*}
and that

\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
is a subclass of the class of
strongly starlike functions of order
\beta=\max\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}
, i.e.

\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\subset\mathcal{S}^{*}(\beta,\beta)\equiv\mathcal{SS}^{*}(\beta)
.

It is clear that defining a class by using the subordination makes it easy to investigate it’s geometric properties. Below, we present a necessary and sufficient condition for functions to be the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
. Actually, we present the definition of the class

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
f(z)\in\mathcal{A}
. Then

f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
if, and only if,

(1.7)

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec G(z)\quad(z\in\Delta),

where
G(z)
is defined in (
1.2
).

Proof.

Let
G(z)
be given by (
1.2
). By (
1.6

\left\{zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z)\right\}
lies in the domain
\Omega_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}
, where
\Omega_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}
in defined in (

G(\Delta)=\Omega_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}
. The function

is univalent in
\Delta

).
∎

For
f(z)=a_{0}+a_{1}z+a_{2}z^{2}+\cdots
and
g(z)=b_{0}+b_{1}z+b_{2}z^{2}+\cdots
,
their Hadamard product (or convolution) is defined by
(f\ast g)(z)=a_{0}b_{0}+a_{1}b_{1}z+a_{2}b_{2}z^{2}+\cdots

algebraic properties of ordinary multiplication. Many of convolution
problems were studied by St. Ruscheweyh in

Lemma 1.2

Let

f(z)*g(z)\prec F(z)*H(z)\quad(z\in\Delta).

Following, one have an another useful lemma (see

Lemma 1.3
.

Let

be analytic and
univalent in
\Delta

maps
\Delta

The structure of this paper is the following. Early, we find a lower bound and an upper bound for

2.
Some inequalities and subordination relations

We begin this section with the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
f\in\mathcal{A}
. If
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
, then

(2.1)

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}\geq\left(\frac{1-(2\cos\frac{\theta}{2}+1)r}{1-r}\right)^{(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})/2}\ \ \ 0\leq|z|=r\leq\frac{1}{2\cos\frac{\theta}{2}+1}

and

(2.2)

0<{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}\leq\left(\frac{1+(2\cos\frac{\theta}{2}-1)r}{1-r}\right)^{(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})/2}\ \ \ 0\leq|z|=r<1,

where
0<\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\leq 1
and

\theta=\frac{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1}}
.

Proof.

Let the function
f
be in the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
. Then by Lemma
1.1
and by the
definition of subordination, there exists a Schwarz function
w(z)
,
satisfying the following conditions

w(0)=0\quad{\rm and}\quad|w(z)|<1\quad(z\in\Delta)

and such that

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=\left(\frac{1+cw(z)}{1-w(z)}\right)^{(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})/2}\quad(z\in\Delta).

Define

(2.3)

F(z):=\frac{1+cw(z)}{1-w(z)}\quad(z\in\Delta).

It is clear that
{\rm Re}\{F(z)\}>0
in the unit disk. We
shall describe
{\rm Re}\{F(z)\}
more precisely. From (
2.3
) we have

\left|F(z)-1\right|=\left|\frac{(1+c)w(z)}{1-w(z)}\right|\leq\frac{2|w(z)|\cos\frac{\theta}{2}}{1-|w(z)|}.

For
|z|=r<1
, using the known fact that (see
[
6
]
)

|w(z)|\leq|z|,

this gives

\left|F(z)-1\right|\leq\frac{2r\cos\frac{\theta}{2}}{1-r}\quad(|z|=r<1).

Thus,
F(z)
for
|z|=r<1
lies in the disk which the center
C=1
and the radius
R
given by

R:=\frac{2r\cos\frac{\theta}{2}}{1-r}.

We note that the origin is outside of this disk for
|z|<1/(1+2\cos(\theta/2))
, and so we obtain (
2.1
) and (
2.2
).
This ends the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Putting
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\beta
in Theorem
2.1
, we have:

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
f
be a strongly starlike function of order
\beta
, where
0<\beta\leq 1
. Then

\left(\frac{1-3r}{1-r}\right)^{\beta}\leq{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}\leq\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)^{\beta}\qquad(|z|=r\leq 1/3).

In particular, if we let
r=1/3
, then

0<{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}<2^{\beta}.

As a corollary, by
[
7
]
or
[
15
, Theorem 3.2a]
, and by Theorem
2.1
, we obtain a sufficient condition for functions belonging the class
S^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
f
satisfies the following subordination

(2.4)

1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec G(z)\quad|z|\leq\frac{1}{1+2\cos\frac{\theta}{2}},

then
f
satisfies

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec G(z)\quad|z|\leq\frac{1}{1+2\cos\frac{\theta}{2}},

where
G(z)
is defined in (
1.2
).

Proof.

Denote

p(z)=\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\quad(z\in\Delta),

where
p
is analytic and
p(0)=1
.
A simple calculation implies that

p(z)+\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{p(z)}=1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}

and by (
2.4
) we get

p(z)+\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{p(z)}\prec G(z).

Since
{\rm Re}(p(z))>0
in
\Delta
and
G(z)
is convex, the desired result follows,
[
15
]
.
∎

In order to estimate of
f
(Growth Theorem), where
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
and the logarithmic coefficients of members of
S^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
, we need the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
, then

(2.5)

\log\left\{\frac{f(z)}{z}\right\}\prec\int_{0}^{z}\frac{G(t)-1}{t}{\rm d}t,

where the function
G
is convex univalent of the form
(
1.2
). Moreover,

(2.6)

\widetilde{G}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{G(t)-1}{t}{\rm d}t=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{n}z^{n},

is convex univalent too, where
\lambda_{n}
is defined in (
1.5
).

Proof.

The subordination relation (
1.7
) gives us

(2.7)

z\left(\log\left\{\frac{f(z)}{z}\right\}\right)^{\prime}\prec G(z)-1,

where
G(z)-1
is convex univalent. For
x\geq 0

the function

\tilde{h}(x;z)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1+x)z^{k}}{k+x}

is convex univalent in
\Delta
(see
[
21
]
). Since, for

\tilde{h}(0;z)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k},

we have by (
1.8
)

(2.8)

\left[g(z)\prec F(z)\right]\Rightarrow\left[g(z)*\tilde{h}(0;z)\prec F(z)*\tilde{h}(0;z)\right],

whenever
F(z)
is a convex univalent function. Because

(2.9)

g(z)*\tilde{h}(0;z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{g(t)}{t}{\rm d}t,

then from (
2.7
), (
2.8
) and from (
2.9
), we have

\int_{0}^{z}\log\left\{f(t)\right\}^{\prime}{\rm d}t\prec\int_{0}^{z}\frac{G(t)-1}{t}{\rm d}t,

this gives (
2.5
). Moreover,

\widetilde{G}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{G(t)-1}{t}{\rm d}t=\left\{G(z)-1\right\}*\tilde{h}(0;z),

where
G(z)-1
and
\tilde{h}(0;z)
are convex
univalent functions. Since the class of convex
univalent functions is preserved under the convolution (see
[
23
]
), therefore,
we conclude that the function
\widetilde{G}(z)
is convex univalent. This is the end of proof.
∎

Because
\widetilde{G}(z)
is univalent, we may
rewrite Theorem

If

\displaystyle\prec

\displaystyle=

and

) and (

Let

\displaystyle r\exp\widetilde{G}(-r)<\left|f(z)\right|<r\exp\widetilde{G}(r),

From (

), we have

\displaystyle\frac{f(z)}{z}\in\exp\widetilde{G}(|z|\leq r),

for each

3.
On Logarithmic Coefficients and Coefficients

The logarithmic coefficients
\gamma_{n}
of
f(z)
are defined by

(3.1)

\log\left\{\frac{f(z)}{z}\right\}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2\gamma_{n}z^{n}\quad(z\in\Delta),

which play an important role for various estimates in the theory of univalent functions. For example, if
f\in\mathcal{S}
, then we have

\gamma_{1}=\frac{a_{2}}{2}\quad{\rm and}\quad\gamma_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{3}-\frac{a_{2}^{2}}{2}\right)

and the sharp estimates

|\gamma_{1}|\leq 1\quad{\rm and}\quad|\gamma_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+2e^{-2})\approx 0.635\ldots,

hold. For
n\geq 3
, the estimate of
\gamma_{n}

is much harder and no significant upper bounds for
|\gamma_{n}|
when
f\in\mathcal{S}
and is still open for
n\geq 3
. The sharp upper bounds for modulus of logarithmic coefficients are known for functions in very few subclasses of
\mathcal{S}
. For functions in the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}
, it is easy to prove that
|\gamma_{n}|\leq 1/n
for
n\geq 1
and equality holds for the Koebe function. For another subclasses of
\mathcal{S}
, see also
[
8
,
16
,
17
,
30
]
.
Following, we estimate the logarithmic coefficients of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
0<\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\leq 1
,
c=e^{\pi i\theta}
and

\theta=\frac{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1}}
. Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
and the coefficients of
\log(f(z)/z)
be given by (
3.1
). Then

(3.2)

|\gamma_{n}|\leq\frac{(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}{2n}\cos\frac{1}{2}\theta.

The result is sharp.

Proof.

Let us
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
. With replacing (
1.4
) and (
3.1
) in (
2.7
), we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2n\gamma_{n}z^{n}\prec\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}z^{n},

Applying Lemma
1.3
gives

2n|\gamma_{n}|\leq|\lambda_{1}|,

where

\lambda_{1}=\frac{(1+c)(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}{2}.

Thus the desired inequality (
3.2
) follows.
The equality holds for the logarithmic coefficients of the function

z\mapsto z\exp\widetilde{G}(z),

where
\widetilde{G}
is defined in (
2.6
). This completes the proof.
∎

If we let
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\beta
in the above Theorem
3.1
, we get the following result which previously is obtained by Thomas, see
[
30
, Theorem 1]
.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
f
be a strongly starlike function of order
\beta
, where
0<\beta\leq 1
. Then the logarithmic coefficients of
f
satisfy the sharp inequality

|\gamma_{n}|\leq\frac{1}{n}\beta.

In particular, taking
\beta=1
gives us the estimate of logarithmic coefficients of starlike functions.

Next, we estimate the coefficients of the function
f
of the form (
1.1
) belonging to the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
. We remark that, generally our result is not sharp.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f
be of the form (
1.1
) belongs to the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{t}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
. Then

(3.3)

|a_{n}|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\cos\frac{1}{2}\theta&\qquad\hbox{$n=2$,}\\
\\
\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{n-1}\cos\frac{1}{2}\theta\prod_{k=2}^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{k-1}\cos\frac{1}{2}\theta\right)&\qquad\hbox{$n=3,4,\ldots$,}\end{array}\right.

where
c=e^{\pi i\theta}
,
\theta=(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1})/(\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1})
and
0<\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\leq 1
.

Proof.

Consider the function
q(z)
as follows

(3.4)

zf^{\prime}(z)=q(z)f(z)\quad(z\in\Delta).

Thus by Lemma
1.1
, we have

(3.5)

q(z)\prec G(z)\quad(z\in\Delta),

where
G(z)
is defined by (
1.2
). If we let

q(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n},

then by Lemma
1.3
, we see that the subordination relation
(
3.5
) implies that

(3.6)

|A_{n}|\leq(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\cos\frac{1}{2}\theta\quad(n=1,2,3,\ldots).

If we equate the coefficients of

in both sides of
(

) we obtain

na_{n}=A_{n-1}a_{1}+A_{n-2}a_{2}+\cdots+A_{1}a_{n-1}+A_{0}a_{n}\quad(n=2,3,\ldots),

where
A_{0}=a_{1}=1
. With a simple calculation and also by the inequality (

) we get

\displaystyle|a_{n}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-1}\times\left|A_{n-1}a_{1}+A_{n-2}a_{2}+\cdots+A_{1}a_{n-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{n-1}\cos\frac{1}{2}\theta(|a_{1}|+|a_{2}|+\cdots+|a_{n-1}|)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{n-1}\cos\frac{1}{2}\theta\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}|a_{k}|.

It is clear that
|a_{2}|\leq(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\cos\frac{1}{2}\theta
. To prove the remaining part of the
theorem, we need to show that

(3.7)

\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{n-1}\cos\frac{1}{2}\theta\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}|a_{k}|\leq\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{n-1}\cos\frac{1}{2}\theta\prod_{k=2}^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{k-1}\cos\frac{1}{2}\theta\right)\quad(n=3,4,5\ldots).

Using induction and simple calculation, we could to prove the
inequality (
3.7
). Hence, the desired estimate for

|a_{n}|\,(n=3,4,5,\ldots)
follows, as asserted in (
3.3
).
This completes the proof of theorem.

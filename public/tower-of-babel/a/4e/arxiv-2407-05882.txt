L p L^{p} estimates for the Laplacian via blow-up
Jan Lewenstein-Sanpera, Xavier Ros-Oton

Abstract
. In this note we provide a new proof of the W 2 , p W^{2,p} Calderón-Zygmund regularity estimates for the Laplacian, i.e., Δ ​ u = f \Delta u=f and its parabolic counterpart ∂ t u − Δ ​ u = f \partial_{t}u-\Delta u=f . Our proof is an adaptation of a contradiction and compactness argument that so far had been only used to prove estimates in Hölder spaces. This new approach is simpler than previous ones, and avoids the use of any interpolation theorem.

1.
Introduction

The main concern for this article is the following classical theorem:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
CZ52
]
)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and
u\in H^{1}(B_{1})
be any weak solution to:

\Delta u=f\quad in\quad B_{1},

with
f\in L^{p}(B_{1})
. Then u is in
W^{2,p}
inside
B_{1}
and the following estimate holds:

(1.1)

\int_{B_{1/2}}|D^{2}u|^{p}\leq C\left(\int_{B_{1}}|u|^{p}+\int_{B_{1}}|f|^{p}\right).

The constant
C
depends only on
n
and
p
.

The classical proof of this result, which can be found in
[
GT98
]
, is as follows:

(i)

Prove the result for
p=2
by an easy integration by parts argument.

(ii)

Using the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition, and the maximal function of Hardy-Littlewood, prove a
weak

L^{1}
estimate corresponding to the case
p=1
.

(iii)

Prove the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem, and use it to deduce the result for all
1<p<2
.

(iv)

By a duality argument, deduce the result for all
2<p<\infty
.

This result and its proof are a landmark in elliptic PDE, and connects this subject to harmonic analysis.

Up to date, some other approaches have been introduced to find new proofs of this result.
In particular, an alternative proof is presented in
[
Le13
]
, in which (ii)-(iii)-(iv) are replaced by:

(ii’)

Prove an
L^{\infty}
-to-BMO estimate, corresponding to the case
p=\infty
.

(iii’)

Prove the Stampacchia interpolation theorem, by using the one due to Marcinkiewicz as well as the sharp maximal function of Fefferman-Stein, and use it to deduce the result for all
2<p<\infty
.

(iv’)

By a duality argument, deduce the result for all
1<p<2
.

On the other hand, a completely different (and more geometric) approach was developed in
[
Ca89
]
to treat fully nonlinear equations for
p>n
, and later in
[
Wa13
]
for the Laplacian for all
1<p<\infty
.
The proof in
[
Wa13
]
uses only the maximal function, energy estimates, and Vitali covering lemma, to establish a decay estimate for the superlevel sets of
\mathcal{M}|D^{2}u|^{2}
in terms of those of
\mathcal{M}|f|^{2}
(where
\mathcal{M}
is the maximal function).

Our goal in this paper is to give a new proof of this result, which we believe is simpler and easier to follow than the one in
[
Wa13
,
Ca89
]
.
Our proof uses only the sharp maximal function, combined with a quite elementary contradiction and compactness argument in the spirit of those in
[
Si97
,
Se15
]
; see also
[
FR22
]
.

In the last decade there have been many regularity estimates that have been established by a contradiction and compactness estimate in the spirit of those in
[
Si97
,
Se15
]
.
However, this type of argument had been always used to prove estimates in Hölder spaces, and the present paper is the first one to establish a
W^{2,p}
estimate via a blow-up argument.
Notice that
L^{p}
regularity is quite different than
C^{0,\alpha}
, which is pointwise.
To solve this issue we use the sharp maximal function, and establish the pointwise inequality (
2.4
) below.
The idea to establish such a pointwise inequality comes from
[
LZ24
]
, where a simple proof of the
L^{\infty}
to BMO estimate is given, with a different argument.

Our proof is also somewhat related to the proof of
[
Le23
, Theorem 13.20]
, which uses the sharp maximal function and Caccioppoli’s inequality (but not a blow-up and compactness argument).
We refer also to the work
[
Kr07
]
and the survey
[
Do20
]
for another approach in which the sharp maximal function is used to prove
L^{p}
estimates for equations with VMO coefficients, in particular obtaining pointwise estimates for the sharp function of
D^{2}u
.

]

2.2.
Regularity estimates

We want to establish the estimate (
1.1
) for
p\neq 2
.
Indeed, recall that in the simplest case
p=2
, i.e.,

(2.3)

\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}\right),

it follows easily from the identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{2}v|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta v|^{2}\qquad\forall v\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n});

see e.g.
[
FR22
, Remark 2.13]
.

The estimate (
1.1
)
2<p<\infty
will follow from the following:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
u,f\in C^{\infty}(B_{1})
, with
\Delta u=f
in
B_{1}
, and
r_{\circ}=\frac{1}{8}
.
Then,

(2.4)

\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\#}D^{2}u(x)\leq C\big(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\#}f(x)\big),

for any
x\in B_{3/4}
, where
C
depends only on
n
.

To prove this result, we need the following elementary Lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{u_{k}\}_{k}\subset W^{2,2}(B_{r_{\circ}})
be a sequence of functions satisfying
\|D^{2}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r_{\circ}})}\leq C_{0}
and

\sup_{k}\sup_{r\in(0,r_{\circ})}\fint_{B_{r}}|D^{2}u_{k}(x)-\overline{D^{2}u_{k}}_{B_{r}}|^{2}=\infty.

Then, for any
0<\delta<1
there exists
r_{m}\to 0
and a subsequence
k_{m}
such that:

(1-\delta)\fint_{B_{\rho}}|D^{2}u_{k}(x)-\overline{D^{2}u_{k}}_{B_{\rho}}|^{2}\,\leq\fint_{B_{r_{m}}}|D^{2}u_{k_{m}}(x)-\overline{D^{2}u_{k_{m}}}_{B_{r_{m}}}|^{2}\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\ \rho\in[r_{m},r_{\circ}].

Proof.

For
r<r_{\circ}
, the quantity:

\Theta(r)=\sup_{k}\sup_{\rho\in[r,r_{\circ}]}\fint_{B_{\rho}}|D^{2}u_{k}(x)-\overline{D^{2}u_{k}}_{B_{\rho}}|^{2}

satisfies that
\Theta(r)\to\infty
as
r\to 0
. Hence, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
r_{\varepsilon}\geq\varepsilon
and
k_{\varepsilon}
such that:

(1-\delta)\Theta(\varepsilon)\leq\fint_{B_{r_{\varepsilon}}}|D^{2}u_{k_{\varepsilon}}(x)-\overline{D^{2}u_{k_{\varepsilon}}}_{B_{r_{\varepsilon}}}|^{2}.

Let now
\varepsilon\to 0
. Since
\|D^{2}u_{k_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r_{\circ}})}
is bounded, then for
\fint_{B_{r_{\varepsilon}}}|D^{2}u_{k_{\varepsilon}}(x)-\overline{D^{2}u_{k_{\varepsilon}}}_{B_{r_{\varepsilon}}}|^{2}\to\infty
we need
r_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Moreover, by monotonicity of
\Theta(r)
, we have that
\Theta(r_{\varepsilon})\leq\Theta(\varepsilon)
with

(1-\delta)\Theta(r_{\varepsilon})\leq\fint_{B_{r_{\varepsilon}}}|D^{2}u_{k_{\varepsilon}}(x)-\overline{D^{2}u_{k_{\varepsilon}}}_{B_{r_{\varepsilon}}}|^{2}.

The lemma follows by choosing any sequence
\varepsilon_{m}\to 0
.
∎

2.4
) does not hold.
Then, there exist functions
u_{k},f_{k}\in C^{\infty}(B_{1})

\Delta u_{k}=f_{k}

\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\#}D^{2}u_{k}(0)>k\big(\|u_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\|f_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\#}f_{k}(0)\big)

.
Moreover, dividing

\|u_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\|f_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\#}f_{k}(0)\;=1.

It then follows from the previous two inequalities that

\sup_{k}\sup_{r\in(0,r_{\circ})}\fint_{B_{r}}|D^{2}u_{k}(x)-\overline{D^{2}u_{k}}_{B_{r}}|^{2}=\infty.

Moreover, thanks to (

\|D^{2}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}\leq C

Let

\displaystyle v_{m}(x)

\fint_{B_{\rho}}\big|D^{2}u_{k}(x)-\overline{D^{2}u_{k}}_{B_{\rho}}\big|^{2}\leq 2\fint_{B_{r_{m}}}\big|D^{2}u_{k_{m}}(x)-\overline{D^{2}u_{k_{m}}}_{B_{r_{m}}}\big|^{2}=:\Theta(r_{m})\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\ \rho\geq r_{m}.

Notice also that it follows from the previous inequality that

Here,

\|g_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{R})}\to 0

\|D^{2}v_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1})}=\frac{\int_{B_{1}}|D^{2}u_{k_{m}}(r_{m}x)-\overline{D^{2}u_{k_{m}}}_{B_{r_{m}}}|^{2}}{\Theta(r_{m})}=\frac{1}{2},

\|v_{m}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}\geq c_{\circ}>0.

Finally, for any fixed

\fint_{B_{R}}|D^{2}v_{m}(x)-\overline{D^{2}v_{m}}_{B_{R}}|^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{\fint_{B_{R}}|D^{2}u_{k_{m}}(r_{m}x)-\overline{D^{2}u_{k_{m}}(r_{m}x)}_{B_{R}}|^{2}}{\Theta(r_{m})}\\

Using that

Finally, we provide the:

Proof of
Theorem 1.1
.

We split the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
We first prove the result for
2<p<\infty
.

Let
u
be a solution in
B_{1}
from
\Delta u=f
, with
f\in L^{p}(B_{1})
. Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1})
be any smooth function with
\eta\geq 0
and
\int_{B_{1}}\eta=1
and
\eta_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-n}\eta(x/{\varepsilon})
.

Then, the function
u_{\varepsilon}(x)=u*\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)
is
C^{\infty}
and satisfies

\Delta u_{\varepsilon}=f*\eta_{\varepsilon}=:f_{\varepsilon}\quad\textrm{in}\quad B_{1-\varepsilon}.

Since
u_{\varepsilon}
is
C^{\infty}
we can use the estimates (
2.2
), (
2.3
), and
2.1
(rescaled) to get:

\|D^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1/2})}\;\leq\;C\big(\|\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\#}D^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\|^{1/2}_{L^{p/2}(B_{1/2})}+\|D^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(B_{3/4})}\big)\\
\leq\;C\big(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1-\varepsilon})}+\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1-\varepsilon})}+\|\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\#}f_{\varepsilon}\|^{1/2}_{L^{p/2}(B_{1/2})}\big).\\
\leq\;C\big(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1-\varepsilon})}+\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1-\varepsilon})}\big).

Thanks to Young’s convolution inequality for
L^{p}
norms we have
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1-\varepsilon})}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}
and also
\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1-\varepsilon})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}
.
The result then follows by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
and using the lower semicontinuity of the
W^{2,p}
norm.

Step 2.
The result for
1<p<2
then follows from Step 1 and a standard duality argument, which we sketch next.
First, we prove the estimate

\|D^{2}\bar{u}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})},\qquad 1<p<2

for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1})
, where
\bar{u}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the solution of
\Delta\bar{u}=f
given by the fundamental solution of the Laplacian.

Recall that the
L^{p}
-norm of a function
f
can be characterized by

\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}=\sup_{g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1})}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}fg:\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{1})}=1\right\}.

Given
\bar{u}
as above, consider a test function
g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1})
and let
v\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the solution of
\Delta v=g
given by the fundamental solution of the Laplacian. Then, integrating by parts we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}D^{2}\bar{u}\,g=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\,D^{2}v=\int_{B_{1}}f\,D^{2}v\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\|D^{2}v\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{1})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{1})},

where the last inequality
\|D^{2}v\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{1})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{1})}
follows from Step 1, since
\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{2})}=\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{1})}
and
p^{\prime}>2
.

Taking the supremum one reaches
\|D^{2}\bar{u}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}
, as claimed.
Moreover, by the same approximation argument as in Step 1, the same inequality holds for any weak solutions of
\Delta\bar{u}=f
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
f\in L^{p}(B_{1})
.

Finally, if
\Delta u=f
in
B_{1}
, we consider the global solution
\bar{u}
of
\Delta\bar{u}=f\chi_{B_{1}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(given by the fundamental soluton), and then
u-\bar{u}
is harmonic in
B_{1}
. Combining interior regularity estimates for harmonic functions in
B_{1}
, with the fact that
\|D^{2}\bar{u}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}
, the result follows.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Once we have the
W^{2,p}
estimates for the Laplacian, one can prove by the method of “freezing coefficients” that the same result holds for general operators in non-divergence form with continuous coefficients, i.e.,

tr\big(A(x)D^{2}u(x)\big)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}(x)\partial_{ij}u(x)=f(x)\quad\textrm{in}\quad B_{1},

where
A(x)=(a_{ij}(x))_{i,j}
is uniformly elliptic and continuous; see e.g.
[
GT98
,
FR22
]
.

3.
Parabolic Equations

Our method can also be used to give a new proof for the following parabolic
W^{2,p}
estimate:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
u
be any weak solution of

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=f\;in\;Q_{1},

with
f\in L^{p}(Q_{1})
. Then
D^{2}u
and
u_{t}
are in
L^{p}
inside
Q_{1}
and the following estimate holds:

(3.1)

\int_{Q_{1/2}}|D^{2}u|^{p}+|\partial_{t}u|^{p}\leq C\left(\int_{Q_{1}}|u|^{p}+\int_{Q_{1}}|f|^{p}\right),

where
C
depends only on
n
.

Here,
Q_{r}(x_{\circ},t_{\circ})=B_{r}(x_{\circ})\times(t_{\circ}-\frac{r^{2}}{2},t_{\circ}+\frac{r^{2}}{2}]
represents the parabolic cube.
As before, the case
p=2
follows from an easy integration by parts argument
[
Ev10
]
, and we will give a new proof for
p\neq 2
.

Parabolic sharp maximal function

We define the parabolic 2-sharp maximal function for any
w\in L^{2}(D)
and any
Q_{r_{\circ}}(x,t)\subset D
as

\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{2,{\rm par}}w(x,t)=\sup_{r\in(0,r_{\circ})}\fint_{Q_{r}(x,t)}|w-\overline{w}_{Q_{r}(x,t)}|^{2},

for which the analogous bounds to (
2.2
) in
Q_{r}
hold; see
[
GM12
]
.
Here,
r_{\circ}>0
is a fixed constant.

Regularity estimates

In order to prove
Theorem 3.1
we follow the steps as in the elliptic case. First, we will show an equivalent result for the non-homogeneous heat equation:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
u,f\in C^{\infty}(Q_{1})
, with
u_{t}-\Delta u=f
, and
r_{\circ}=\frac{1}{8}
. Then,

(3.2)

\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{2,{\rm par}}\partial_{t}u(x,t)+\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{2,{\rm par}}D^{2}u(x,t)\leq C\big(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}+\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{2,{\rm par}}f(x,t)\big),

for any
(x,t)\in Q_{1/2}
, where
C
depends only on
n
.

Proof.

Following the same steps and calculations as in the elliptic case, we will see through contradiction that the result holds. Assume as before that
(x_{\circ},t_{\circ})=(0,0)
, and suppose that the estimate (
3.2
) does not hold. Then, there exist a set of functions
u_{k},f_{k}\in C^{\infty}(Q_{1})
, such that
\partial_{t}u_{k}-\Delta u_{k}=f_{k}
in
Q_{1}
and:

\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{2,{\rm par}}\partial_{t}u_{k}(0,0)+\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{2,{\rm par}}D^{2}u_{k}(x_{0},t_{0})>k(\|u_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\|f_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{2,{\rm par}}f_{k}(0,0)).

Moreover we can take
\|u_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\|f_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{2,{\rm par}}f(0,0)=1
.
Rewrite the inequality as:

\sup_{k}\sup_{r\in(0,r_{\circ})}\fint_{Q_{r}}\left[|D^{2}u_{k}(x)-\overline{D^{2}u_{k}}_{Q_{r}}|^{2}+|\partial_{t}u_{k}(x)-\overline{\partial_{t}u_{k}}_{Q_{r}}|^{2}\right]=\infty,

and exactly as in the elliptic case, there exist sequences
r_{m}\to 0
and
k_{m}
such that the new functions

\displaystyle v_{m}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{k_{m}}(r_{m}x,r_{m}^{2}t)-p_{m}(x,t)}{r_{m}^{2}\Theta^{1/2}(r_{m})},

\displaystyle g_{m}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{k_{m}}(r_{m}x,r_{m}^{2}t)-\overline{f_{k_{m}}}_{Q_{r_{m}}}}{\Theta^{1/2}(r_{m})}.

satisfy

\fint_{Q_{\rho}}\big|D^{2}u_{k}-\overline{D^{2}u_{k}}_{Q_{\rho}}\big|^{2}+\big|\partial_{t}u_{k}-\overline{\partial_{t}u_{k}}_{Q_{\rho}}\big|^{2}\leq 2\fint_{Q_{r_{m}}}\big|D^{2}u_{k_{m}}-\overline{D^{2}u_{k_{m}}}_{Q_{r_{m}}}\big|^{2}+\big|\partial_{t}u_{k_{m}}-\overline{\partial_{t}u_{k_{m}}}_{Q_{r_{m}}}\big|^{2}=:\Theta(r_{m})

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\rho\geq r_{m}
.

Here,
p_{m}(x,t)
denotes a quadratic polynomial in
x
and linear in
t
such that
\overline{v_{m}}_{Q_{1}}=\overline{\nabla v_{m}}_{Q_{1}}=\overline{D^{2}v_{m}}_{Q_{1}}=\overline{\partial_{t}v_{m}}_{Q_{1}}=0
.
Notice once more that
\partial_{t}v_{m}-\Delta v_{m}=g_{m}
in
Q_{1/r_{m}}
.

Through the same calculations as in the elliptic case, we find the following bounds:

\|D^{2}v_{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{R})}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}v_{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{R})}^{2}\;\leq\;CR^{2+n}(1+R^{2+n}),

while for our
g_{m}
we have

\|g_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{R})}\leq\frac{R^{2+n}(1+R^{2+n})\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{2,{\rm par}}f_{k_{m}}(0,0)}{\Theta(r_{m})}\longrightarrow 0,

for any fixed
R\in(1,1/r_{m})
. Note as well that

\|D^{2}v_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}+\|\partial_{t}v_{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}\geq\frac{1}{2},

which leads to
\frac{1}{2}\leq\|D^{2}v_{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}+\|\partial_{t}v_{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}\leq C(\|v_{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2})}+\|g_{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2})})
thanks to the case
p=2
.
For a large enough
m
, it means that
\|v_{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2})}\geq c_{\circ}>0
.
Finally, following the same steps, for any fixed
R\in(1,1/r_{m})
we have:

\fint_{Q_{R}}\left[|D^{2}v_{m}(x,t)-\overline{D^{2}v_{m}}_{Q_{R}}|^{2}+|\partial_{t}v_{m}(x,t)-\overline{\partial_{t}v_{m}}_{Q_{R}}|^{2}\right]\leq 1.

For any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, we have proved that the sequences
D^{2}v_{m}
and
\partial_{t}v_{m}
are bounded in
L^{2}(K)
–for
m
large enough so that
K\subset Q_{1/r_{m}}
.
Then, since
\overline{v_{m}}_{Q_{1}}=\overline{\nabla v_{m}}_{Q_{1}}=0
, it follows from the parabolic Poincaré inequality (see, e.g.
[
Li03
, Theorem 19]
) that
\|v_{m}\|_{L^{2}(K)}
and
\|\nabla v_{m}\|_{L^{2}(K)}
are bounded.
Hence, up to a subsequence, we have that
v_{m}\to v
strongly in
L^{2}(K)
, while
D^{2}v_{m}\to D^{2}v
,
\partial_{t}v_{m}\to\partial_{t}v
and
\nabla v_{m}\to\nabla v
weakly in
L^{2}(K)
.
Moreover, since
g_{m}\to 0
strongly in
L^{2}(K)
, then we can pass the equation
\partial_{t}v_{m}-\Delta v_{m}=g_{m}
in
K
(in its weak formulation) to the limit to deduce that
v
solves the heat equation in
K
.
Since this can be done for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, we find

\partial_{t}v-\Delta v=0\quad\textrm{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

Taking limits we find that

(3.3)

\overline{v}_{Q_{1}}=\overline{\nabla v}_{Q_{1}}=\overline{D^{2}v}_{Q_{1}}=\overline{\partial_{t}v}_{Q_{1}}=0,

(3.4)

0<\|v\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2})},

(3.5)

\|D^{2}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{R})}+\|\partial_{t}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{R})}\;\leq\;CR^{2+n}(1+R^{2+n})\text{ for any }R>1,

(3.6)

\fint_{Q_{R}}\left[|D^{2}v(x,t)-\overline{D^{2}v}_{Q_{R}}|^{2}+|\partial_{t}v(x,t)-\overline{\partial_{t}v}_{Q_{R}}|^{2}\right]\;\leq\;1\text{ for any }R>1.

By interior regularity for the heat equation, we can bound (see, e.g.
[
Ev10
, Section 2.3.3.c]
)

\max_{Q_{R/2}}\,|D^{k}_{x}D^{l}_{t}\,D^{2}v(x,t)|\leq\frac{C_{kl}}{R^{k+2l+n+2}}\|D^{2}v\|_{L^{1}(Q_{R})},

which with condition (
3.5
) means that
D^{2}v
and
\partial_{t}v
have to be polynomials of degree
N
at most. By
3.3
, this would mean that:

\fint_{Q_{R}}\left[|D^{2}v(x,t)-\overline{D^{2}v}_{Q_{R}}|^{2}+|\partial_{t}v(x,t)-\overline{\partial_{t}v}_{Q_{R}}|^{2}\right]\geq cR^{2N}>0\quad\text{ for any }R>1.

which fulfils condition (
3.6
) only if
N=0
. This means that
D^{2}v
and
\partial_{t}v
are constants and that
v
is a quadratic polynomial in space and linear in time. By condition (
3.3
) we reach that
v\equiv 0
which is a contradiction, and therefore the estimate (
3.1
) must hold.
∎

We used this elementary result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p(x,t)
be a polynomial of parabolic degree
N
.
Then, there exists
c>0
such that

\fint_{Q_{R}}|p-c_{R}|^{2}\geq cR^{2N}

for any large enough
R>1
and any
c_{R}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Write
p=p_{0}+...+p_{N}
, where each
p_{i}
is a parabolically homogeneous polynomial of degree
i=0,...,N
.
By triangle inequality,

\frac{1}{2}|p_{N}-c_{R}|^{2}-|p-p_{N}|^{2}\leq|p-c_{R}|^{2}\leq 2|p-p_{N}|^{2}+2|p_{N}-c_{R}|^{2}.

Notice that
p-p_{N}
is of degree
N-1
and therefore
\fint_{Q_{R}}|p-p_{N}|^{2}\leq CR^{2N-2}
. On the other hand, simple computations lead to

\fint_{Q_{R}}\left|p_{N}(x,t)-c_{R}\right|^{2}dxdt=\fint_{Q_{1}}\left|p_{N}(Ry,R^{2}s)-c_{R}\right|^{2}dyds=R^{2N}\fint_{Q_{1}}\left|p_{N}-\frac{c_{R}}{R^{N}}\right|^{2}\geq c_{*}R^{2N}>0

for any constant
c_{R}
, where

c_{*}:=\inf_{\kappa\in\mathbb{R}}\fint_{Q_{1}}\left|p_{N}-\kappa\right|^{2}.

Hence, for large enough
R>1

\fint_{Q_{R}}\left|p-c_{R}\right|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\fint_{Q_{R}}\left|p_{N}-c_{R}\right|^{2}-\fint_{Q_{R}}\left|p-p_{N}\right|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}c_{*}R^{2N}-CR^{2N-2}\geq cR^{2N},

and the Lemma follows.
∎

Finally, we sketch the:

Proof of
Theorem 3.1
.

The proof is essentially the same as in the elliptic case, the only nontrivial difference is in the duality argument.
Namely, once we have the result for
p>2
, we fix
1<p<2
and proceed as follows.

Consider
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,1)
and let
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,1])
be such that
\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=f
in
\Omega
, with
u(x,0)=0
.
For any
g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
let
v
be such that
\partial_{t}v-\Delta v=g
with
v(x,0)=0
.
Consider the same problem but backwards in time, i.e.,
\tilde{g}=g(x,1-t)
as well as
\tilde{v}=v(x,1-t)
which satisfies
-\partial_{t}\tilde{v}-\Delta\tilde{v}=\tilde{g}
and
\tilde{v}(x,1)=0
.

Then, integrating by parts we find

\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u\,\tilde{g}=-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u\partial_{t}\tilde{v}-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u\Delta\tilde{v}=-\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}u-\Delta u)\partial_{t}\tilde{v}+\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\Delta\tilde{v}\right]_{0}^{1}=-\int_{\Omega}f\partial_{t}\tilde{v}\leq\\
\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{t}\tilde{v}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}=C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}.

The last inequality
\|\partial_{t}\tilde{v}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}
results since
p^{\prime}>2
. Finally, take the supremum on the left hand side to reach the desired estimate for
\partial_{t}u
is in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. By definition of
f
, it means that
\Delta u
is bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega)
as well, and therefore that
D^{2}u
is in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. This yields the result for
1<p<2
in case of global solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,1)
.

Finally, if
\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=f
in
Q_{1}
, we consider the global solution
\bar{u}
of
\partial_{t}\bar{u}-\Delta\bar{u}=f\chi_{Q_{1}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,1)
, and then
u-\bar{u}
solves the heat equation in
Q_{1}
. Combining interior regularity estimates for caloric functions in
Q_{1}
, with the fact that
\|D^{2}\bar{u}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,1))}+\|\partial_{t}\bar{u}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,1))}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,1))}
, the result follows.
∎

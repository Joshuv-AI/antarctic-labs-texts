Abstract
Layered treewidth and row treewidth are recently introduced graph parameters that have been key ingredients in the solution of several well-known open problems. In particular, the layered treewidth of a graph G G is the minimum integer k k such that G G has a tree-decomposition and a layering such that each bag has at most k k vertices in each layer. The row treewidth of G G is the minimum integer k k such that G G is isomorphic to a subgraph of H ⊠ P H\boxtimes P for some graph H H of treewidth at most k k and for some path P P . It follows from the definitions that the layered treewidth of a graph is at most its row treewidth plus 1. Moreover, a minor-closed class has bounded layered treewidth if and only if it has bounded row treewidth. However, it has been open whether row treewidth is bounded by a function of layered treewidth. This paper answers this question in the negative. In particular, for every integer k k we describe a graph with layered treewidth 1 and row treewidth k k . We also prove an analogous result for layered pathwidth and row pathwidth.

with layered width at most
w
, there is a
K_{k+1}
minor in
H
, implying
\tw(H)\geqslant k
and
\rtw(G)\geqslant k
.

Say a
K_{k}
model
\{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}\}
in a graph
G

respects
an
H
-partition
(A_{x}:x\in V(H))
of
G
if for each
x\in V(H)
there is at most one value of
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
for which
V(Y_{i})\cap A_{x}\neq\emptyset
. For each
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, let
X_{i}
be the subgraph of
H
induced by those vertices
x\in V(H)
such that
V(Y_{i})\cap A_{x}\neq\emptyset
. Then
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{k}\}
is a
K_{k}
model in
H
. Thus the next lemma implies
8
.

Lemma 9
.

For all
w\in\mathbb{N}
and
k,s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, there exists a graph
G=G_{s,k,w}
such that
\tw(G)\leqslant k
, and for any
(\leqslant\!s)
-subdivision
G^{\prime}
of
G
, and for any graph
H
and any
H
-partition
(A_{x}:x\in V(H))
of
G^{\prime}
with layered width at most
w
, there is a model
\{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k+1}\}
of
K_{k+1}
in
G^{\prime}
that respects
(A_{x}:x\in V(H))
, and for each
i\in\{1,\dots,k+1\}
we have
|V(Y_{i})|\leqslant k(2s+1)+1
and
V(Y_{i})\cap V(G)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
k
. For the base case with
k=0
, let
G_{s,0,w}
be the graph with one vertex
v
and no edges. Any subdivision
G^{\prime}
contains a model
Y_{1}=G^{\prime}[\{v\}]
of
K_{1}
with
|Y_{1}|\leqslant 1=0(2s+1)+1
, and this model trivially respects any
H
-partition of
G^{\prime}
, for any graph
H
.

Now assume
k\geqslant 1
, and that the induction hypothesis holds for
k-1
. Let
Q=G_{s,k-1,w}
be the graph obtained by induction. Let
N:=k^{2}(2s+1)(4s+5)w+1
. Create the graph
G
by starting with
N
disjoint copies
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{N}
of
Q
. Next add a vertex
v
and, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and each
u\in V(Q_{i})
, add
N
internally disjoint paths of length
2
from
v
to
u
.

First we show that
\tw(G)\leqslant k
. If
k=1
then
G
is a subdivided star, which has treewidth 1. Now assume that
k\geqslant 2
. Begin with any tree-decomposition
(B_{x}:x\in V(T))
of
Q_{1}\cup\ldots\cup Q_{N}

with width at most
(k-1)
. Add
v
to every bag
B_{x}
. For all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and for each vertex
u\in V(Q_{i})
, choose a bag
B_{x}
that contains
u
, and attach a leaf node adjacent to
x
whose bag contains
v
,
u
, and the degree-2 vertex of the
j
-th length-2 path from
v
to
u
. Each bag of this decomposition has size at most
\max\{3,k+1\}\leqslant k+1
and therefore
\tw(G)\leqslant k
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be a
(\leqslant\!s)
-subdivision of
G
. Let
H
be a graph and let
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{L})
be a layered
H
-partition of
G^{\prime}
with width at most
w
, where
\mathcal{H}:=(A_{x}:x\in V(H))
. Since the radius of
G
is
2
, the radius of
G^{\prime}
is at most
2s+2
. Therefore
|\mathcal{L}|\leqslant 4s+5
, and
|A_{x}|\leqslant(4s+5)w
for each
x\in V(H)
.

Let
z
be the unique node of
H
such that
v\in A_{z}
, and let
Q^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,Q^{\prime}_{N}
be the (possibly subdivided) copies of
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{N}
that appear in
G^{\prime}
. So
A_{z}\cap V(Q^{\prime}_{i})\neq\emptyset
for at most
(4s+5)w-1
values of
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. Since
N\geqslant(4s+5)w
, we have
V(Q^{\prime}_{i})\cap A_{z}=\emptyset
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
.

Let
H_{i}
be the subgraph of
H
induced by the nodes
\tau\in V(H)
such that
A_{\tau}\cap V(Q^{\prime}_{i})\neq\emptyset
. So
(A_{\tau}\cap V(Q^{\prime}_{i}):\tau\in V(H_{i}))
defines a width-
w
layered
H_{i}
-partition of
Q^{\prime}_{i}
(with respect to layering
\mathcal{L}
). By induction, there is a
K_{k}
model
\{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}\}
in
G^{\prime}
that respects
(A_{\tau}\cap V(Q^{\prime}):\tau\in V(H_{i}))
, and for each
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
we have
|V(Y_{j})|\leqslant(k-1)(2s+1)+1\leqslant k(2s+1)
and
V(Y_{j})\cap V(G)\neq\emptyset
. Let
y_{j}
be a vertex in
V(Y_{j})\cap V(G)
. Note that
|V(Y_{1}\cup\dots\cup Y_{k})|\leqslant k^{2}(2s+1)
. Let
F:=\bigcup\{A_{x}:A_{x}\cap V(Y_{1}\cup\dots\cup Y_{k})\neq\emptyset\}
. So
|F|\leqslant k^{2}(2s+1)(4s+5)w
.

Since
V(Q^{\prime}_{i})\cap A_{z}=\emptyset
, we have
v\not\in F
. Since
N>|F|
, for each
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, for at least one of the
N
paths between
v
and
y_{j}
added in the construction of
G
, the corresponding path in
G^{\prime}
avoids
F
. Let
P_{j}
be this path, not including
y_{j}
. Let
Y_{k+1}:=\bigcup\{P_{j}:j\in\{1,\dots,k\}\}
. So
V(Y_{k+1})\cap F=\emptyset
. By construction, there is an edge from
y_{j}
to
Y_{k+1}
for each
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. So
\{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k+1}\}
is a
K_{k+1}
model in
G^{\prime}
. Since
\{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}\}
respects
(A_{\tau}\cap V(Q^{\prime}):\tau\in V(H_{i}))
and
V(Y_{k+1})\cap F=\emptyset
, it follows that
\{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k+1}\}
respects
(A_{x}:x\in V(H))
. By construction,
|V(Y_{k+1})|\leqslant k(2s+1)+1
and
v\in V(Y_{k+1})\cap V(G)
. By assumption,
for each
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
we have
|V(Y_{j})|\leqslant(k-1)(2s+1)+1
and
V(Y_{j})\cap V(G)\neq\emptyset
. This shows that
\{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k+1}\}
is the desired
K_{k+1}
model in
G^{\prime}
.
∎

We now prove the following strengthening of
1
.

Theorem 10
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there is a graph
G
with
\ltw(G)=1
and
\rtw(G)=\tw(G)=k
.

Proof.

By
6
with
s=k
, there is a graph
G
with
\tw(G)\leqslant k
such that
\rtw(G^{\prime})\geqslant k
for every
(\leqslant\!k)
-subdivision
G^{\prime}
of
G
.
By
4
, there exists a
(\leqslant\!k)
-subdivision
G^{\prime}
of
G
with
\ltw(G^{\prime})=1
. By definition,
\rtw(H)\leqslant\tw(H)
for every graph
H
. It is well known and easily proved that
\tw(H)=\tw(H^{\prime})
for every subdivision
H^{\prime}
of
H
. Thus
k\leqslant\rtw(G^{\prime})\leqslant\tw(G^{\prime})=\tw(G)\leqslant k
, implying
\rtw(G)=\tw(G^{\prime})=k
.
∎

See
[
22
,
9
]
for other examples where
O(\tw(G))
-subdivisions of graphs
G
are used to prove lower bounds.

4
Queue Layouts and Layered Treewidth

The
queue-number
\qn(G)
of a graph
G
is a well-studied graph parameter introduced by Heath, Leighton and Rosenberg
[
24
,
25
]
; we omit the definition since we will not need it. Queue-number can be upper bounded in terms of layered treewidth and row treewidth. In particular,
Dujmović et al. 2017
proved that
n
-vertex graphs of bounded layered treewidth have queue-number in
O(\log n)
, while
Dujmović et al. 2020b
proved that graphs of bounded row treewidth have bounded queue-number. (Row treewidth was discovered as a tool for proving that planar graphs have bounded queue-number.) This is a prime example of a difference in behaviour between layered treewidth and row treewidth. Nevertheless, it is open whether graphs of bounded layered treewidth have bounded queue-number. We show that the answer to this question depends entirely on the case of layered treewidth 1.

Corollary 11
.

Graphs of bounded layered treewidth have bounded queue-number if and only if graphs of layered treewidth 1 have bounded queue-number.

Proof.

The forward implication is immediate. Now assume that every graph of layered treewidth 1 has queue-number at most some constant
c
.
Let
G
be a graph with layered treewidth
k
. By
3
,
G
has a
(\leqslant k)
-subdivision
G^{\prime}
with layered treewidth 1. So
\qn(G^{\prime})\leqslant c
.
Dujmović and Wood 2005
proved that for every graph
H
and
(\leqslant s)
-subdivision
H^{\prime}
of
H
, we have
\qn(H)\in O(\qn(H^{\prime})^{2s})
. This bound was improved to
O(\qn(H^{\prime})^{s+1})
in
[
17
]
. Thus
\qn(G)\leqslant O(c^{k+1})
, implying that graphs of bounded layered treewidth have bounded queue-number.
∎

This proof highlights the value of considering the behaviour of a graph parameter on subdivisions. An analogous result holds for nonrepetitive chromatic number
\pi(G)
(using a result of
Nešetřil et al. 2011
to bound
\pi(H)
in terms of
\pi(H^{\prime})
).

Corollary 12
.

Graphs of bounded layered treewidth have bounded nonrepetitive chromatic number if and only if graphs of layered treewidth 1 have bounded nonrepetitive chromatic number.

5
Separating Layered Pathwidth and Row Pathwidth

Recall that
2
asserts that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
there is a tree
T
with
\lpw(T)=1
and
\rpw(T)\geqslant k
. We first show that this theorem, in fact, follows from results in the literature.
Bannister et al. 2019
noted that
\lpw(T)=1
for every tree
T
.

Dvorák et al. 2021
showed that any family
\mathcal{G}
of graphs with bounded row pathwidth has polynomial growth. The family of complete binary trees does not have polynomial growth, so for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a complete binary tree
T
with
\rpw(T)\geqslant k
. This proves
2
.

We now prove the following stronger result.

Theorem 13
.

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a tree
T
with
\pw(T)=\rpw(T)=k
.

Let
T_{d,h}
be the complete
d
-ary tree of height
h
.
It is folklore that
\pw(T_{d,h})=h
for all
d\geqslant 3
(see
[
34
,
20
,
35
]
). So
7
and
14
with
\ell=3
and
w=1
implies
13
.

Lemma 14
.

For all
h\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
w,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every graph
H
and every
H
-partition
(A_{x}:x\in V(H))
of
T_{d,h}
with layered width at most
w
, the graph
H
contains a subgraph isomorphic to
T_{\ell,h}
. Moreover, if
r
is the root vertex of
T_{d,h}
and
r\in A_{z}
, then
z
is the root vertex of a subgraph of
H
isomorphic to
T_{\ell,h}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
h
. The case
h=0
is trivial. Now assume that
h\geqslant 1
.
We may assume that the number of layers is at most the diameter of
T_{d,h}
plus 1, which equals
2h+1
. So
|A_{x}|\leqslant w(2h+1)
for each
x\in V(H)
. Let
r_{1},\dots,r_{d}
be the children of
r
in
T_{d,h}
. Let
T^{1},\dots,T^{d}
be the copies of
T_{d,h-1}
in
T_{d,h}
, where
T^{i}
is rooted at
r_{i}
. At most
w(2h+1)-1
of
T^{1},\dots,T^{d}
intersect
A_{z}
. Without loss of generality,
T^{1},\dots,T^{d-w(2h+1)+1}
do not intersect
A_{z}
.
For each
i\in\{1,\dots,d-w(2h+1)+1\}
, let
z_{i}
be the vertex of
H
such that
r_{i}\in A_{z_{i}}
. Since
T^{i}\cap A_{z}=\emptyset
, we have
z_{i}\neq z
. Since
rr_{i}\in E(T_{d,h})
, we have
zz_{i}\in E(H)
. By induction,
H-z
contains a subgraph
S^{i}
isomorphic to
T_{\ell,h-1}
rooted at
z_{i}
. Let
X
be the intersection graph of
S^{1},\dots,S^{d-w(2h+1)+1}
. If
S^{i}
and
S^{j}
intersect in node
y
of
H
, then
T^{i}
and
T^{j}
both intersect
A_{y}
. Since
|V(S^{i})|\leqslant(\ell+1)^{h-1}
and
|A_{y}|\leqslant w(2h+1)
,
X
has maximum degree
\Delta(X)\leqslant w(2h+1)(\ell+1)^{h}
. Thus
\chi(X)\leqslant w(2h+1)(\ell+1)^{h}+1
. For sufficiently large
d
, we have
|V(X)|>(\ell-1)\chi(X)
. Thus, in any
\chi(X)
-colouring of
X
, some colour class has at least
\ell
vertices. Without loss of generality,
S^{1},\dots,S^{\ell}
are pairwise-disjoint. Hence,
S_{1}\cup\dots\cup S_{\ell}
along with
z
forms a subgraph of
H
isomorphic to
T_{\ell,h}
, rooted at
z
, as desired.
∎

We finish with an open problem: what is
\rpw(T_{2,h})
? It follows from a result of

Dvorák et al. 2021
that
\rpw(T_{2,h})\in\Omega(\frac{h}{\log h})
. Is this tight, or is
\rpw(T_{2,h})\in\Omega(h)
? Obviously
\rpw(T_{2,h})\leqslant\pw(T_{2,h})={\lceil\frac{h}{2}\rceil}
.

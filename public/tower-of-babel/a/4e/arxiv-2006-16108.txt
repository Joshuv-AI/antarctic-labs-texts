Definitions and results

We recall briefly what is useful for the understanding of the paper.

A rank
r

is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module
S
of rank
r
together with a symmetric bilinear form
b
.

A lattice
S
is called

is even for all
x
from
S
.
For any integer

we denote by
{\langle\boldsymbol{n}\rangle}
the lattice
\mathbb{Z}e
where

.

If

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of a lattice

, then the matrix

G(e)=(b(e_{i},e_{j}))
is called the

of

with respect to

An injective homomorphism of lattices is called an embedding.

An embedding

is called

is a free group.
A sublattice is a subgroup equipped with the induced bilinear form. A sublattice
S^{\prime}
of a lattice

is called primitive if the identity map
S^{\prime}\rightarrow S
is a primitive embedding.
The
primitive closure
of
S
inside
S^{\prime}
is defined by

\overline{S}=\{x\in S^{\prime}/mx\in S\hbox{ for some positive integer }m\}.

A lattice

M
such that the index

is finite.

By

we denote the orthogonal sum of two lattices defined in the standard way. We write

orthogonal complement of a sublattice

S

(S)_{S^{\prime}}^{\perp}=\{x\in S^{\prime}/b(x,y)=0\,\,\,\hbox{for all }y\in S\}

2.1.
Discriminant forms

Let
L
be a non-degenerate lattice.
The
dual lattice

L^{*}
of
L
is defined by

L^{*}:=\text{Hom}(L,\mathbb{Z})=\{x\in L\otimes\mathbb{Q}/\,\,\,b(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}\hbox{ for all }y\in L\}.

and the
discriminant group
G_{L}
by

G_{L}:=L^{*}/L.

This group is finite if and only if
L
is non-degenerate. In the latter case, its order is equal to the absolute value of the lattice determinant
\mid\det(G(e))\mid
for any basis
e
of
L
.
A lattice
L
is
unimodular
if
G_{L}
is trivial.

Let
G_{L}
be the discriminant group of a non-degenerate lattice
L
. The bilinear form on
L
extends naturally to a
\mathbb{Q}
-valued symmetric bilinear form on
L^{*}
and induces a symmetric bilinear form

b_{L}:G_{L}\times G_{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

If
L
is even, then
b_{L}
is the symmetric bilinear form associated to the quadratic form defined by

\begin{matrix}q_{L}:G_{L}&\rightarrow&\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z}\\
q_{L}(x+L)&\mapsto&x^{2}+2\mathbb{Z}.\end{matrix}

The latter means that
q_{L}(na)=n^{2}q_{L}(a)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
a\in G_{L}
and
b_{L}(a,a^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}(q_{L}(a+a^{\prime})-q_{L}(a)-q_{L}(a^{\prime}))
, for all
a,a^{\prime}\in G_{L}
, where
\frac{1}{2}:\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
is the natural isomorphism.
The pair
\boldsymbol{(G_{L},b_{L})}
(resp.
\boldsymbol{(G_{L},q_{L})}
) is called the
discriminant bilinear
(resp.
quadratic
)
form
of
L
.

When the even lattice
L
is given by its Gram matrix, we can compute its discriminant form using the following lemma as explained in Shimada
[
21
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
the Gram matrix of
L
and
U
,
V\in Gl_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
such that

UAV=D=\begin{pmatrix}d_{1}&&0\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&d_{n}\\
\end{pmatrix}

with
1=d_{1}=\ldots=d_{k}<d_{k+1}\leq\ldots\leq d_{n}
. Then

G_{L}\simeq\oplus_{i>k}\mathbb{Z}/(d_{i}).

Moreover the
i
th row vector of
V^{-1}
, regarded as an element of
L^{*}
with respect to the dual basis
e_{1}^{*}
, …,
e_{n}^{*}
generate the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}/(d_{i})
.

2.6.
The Kneser-Nishiyama’s method

Recall that a
K3
surface may admit more than one elliptic fibration, but up to isomorphism, there is only a finite number of elliptic fibrations
[
28
]

To establish a complete classification of the elliptic fibrations on the
K3

, we use Nishiyama’s method based on lattice theoretic ideas

17
]
. The technique builds on a converse of Nikulin’s results.

Given an elliptic
K3
surface

, Nishiyama aims at embedding the frames of all elliptic fibrations into a negative-definite lattice, more precisely into a Niemeier lattice of rank
24
. For this purpose, he first determines an even negative-definite lattice
M
such that

q_{M}=-q_{NS(X)},\,\,\,\,\,\,\hbox{rank}(M)+\rho(X)=26.

By theorem
2.1
,

has a Niemeier lattice as an overlattice for each frame

2.6.1.

Denote by

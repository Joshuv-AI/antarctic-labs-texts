BV quantization of braided scalar field theory
R. J. Szabo

Abstract
We address the problem of UV/IR mixing in noncommutative quantum field theories from the perspective of braided L ∞ L_{\infty} -structures and the Batalin–Vilkovisky formalism. We describe the example of braided noncommutative scalar field theory and its quantization using braided homological perturbation theory. The formalism is illustrated through one-loop calculations of the two-point functions for ϕ 4 \phi^{4} -theory in four dimensions and ϕ 3 \phi^{3} -theory in six dimensions. In both cases we find that there are no non-planar diagrams and no UV/IR mixing.



[a]M. Dimitrijević Ćirić

1
Introduction

The quantization of noncommutative field theories is a long-standing problem. In Moyal–Weyl and other types of noncommutative field theories, non-planar Feynman diagrams appear in perturbation theory. At one-loop these diagrams are UV finite unless the external momentum is very small; in that case the UV divergences of the undeformed theories reappear. This behaviour is called UV/IR mixing and it is detrimental to the renormalizability of the field theory
[
1
]
, see e.g.
[
2
]
for a review.

Intensive investigations over the years have been devoted to understanding this problem, see e.g.
[
3
]
for a review. To account for a (twist) deformed Poincaré symmetry, braided deformations of the oscillator algebras of field creation and annihilation operators were suggested in several works, see e.g.
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
. Based on this, it was argued in
[
4
]
that noncommutative scalar quantum field theories with braided symmetry do not show UV/IR mixing in S-matrix elements. This lies in agreement with results of Oeckl’s ‘braided quantum field theory’
[
8
]
, which however has not been extended to theories with gauge symmetries.

Using the twist formalism, braided
L_{\infty}
-algebras and the associated braided noncommutative field theories have been constructed in
[
9
]
. The correspondence between
L_{\infty}
-algebras and the Batalin–Vilkovisky (BV) formalism, reviewed in e.g.
[
10
]
, was generalized to braided
L_{\infty}
-algebras in
[
11
]
, giving another route to braided quantum field theory that is now capable of treating gauge theories. The BV quantization of braided fuzzy scalar field theories was discussed in
[
11
]
, while the BV quantization of braided scalar field theory as well as braided quantum electrodynamics was discussed in
[
12
]
.

In this contribution we address the problem of quantization of noncommutative scalar field theory initiated in
[
12
]
using the underlying braided
L_{\infty}
-algebra and its associated BV formalism; for simplicity, here we will work with the Moyal–Weyl deformation, but more general deformations can be studied analogously, see Appendix
A
. In Section
2
we briefly review the connection between
L_{\infty}
-algebras and the BV formalism in field theory. In Section
3
we introduce braided noncommutative scalar field theory and quantize it using braided homological perturbation theory. We present two explicit examples in Section
4
: one-loop calculations of the two-point functions for braided
\phi^{4}
-theory on
\mathbb{R}^{1,3}
and braided
\phi^{3}
-theory on
\mathbb{R}^{1,5}
. In both cases the two-point functions at one-loop are the same as in the commutative case, confirming the expectations of
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
. This suggests that non-planar diagrams and UV/IR mixing may be absent altogether in braided scalar field theory.

2
Homotopy algebras and BV quantization

In this section we briefly discuss
L_{\infty}
-algebras of field theory, their dual BV description and quantization via the procedure of “homotopy transfer”.

\boldsymbol{L_{\infty}}
-algebras.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
V=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\,V_{k}
with a sequence of graded antisymmetric multilinear maps of degree
2-n
for
n\geq 1
called
n
-brackets:

\displaystyle\ell_{n}:V^{\otimes n}\longrightarrow

\displaystyle V\ ,\quad v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}\longmapsto\ell_{n}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})

\displaystyle\ell_{n}(\dots,v,v^{\prime},\dots)

\displaystyle=\>-(-1)^{|v|\,|v^{\prime}|}\,\ell_{n}(\dots,v^{\prime},v,\dots)\ ,

where
|v|
denotes the degree of a homogeneous element
v\in V
. The
n
-brackets must also fulfil a sequence of relations called homotopy Jacobi identities. The first three relations are given by

\displaystyle\underline{n=1:}\quad\ell_{1}\big(\ell_{1}(v)\big)=0\ ,

(1)

\displaystyle\underline{n=2:}\quad\ell_{1}\big(\ell_{2}(v_{1},v_{2})\big)=\ell_{2}\big(\ell_{1}(v_{1}),v_{2}\big)+(-1)^{|v_{1}|}\,\ell_{2}\big(v_{1},\ell_{1}(v_{2})\big)\ ,

(2)

\displaystyle\underline{n=3:}\quad\ell_{1}\big(\ell_{3}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\big)=-\ell_{3}\big(\ell_{1}(v_{1}),v_{2},v_{3}\big)-(-1)^{|v_{1}|}\,\ell_{3}\big(v_{1},\ell_{1}(v_{2}),v_{3}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt-(-1)^{|v_{1}|+|v_{2}|}\,\ell_{3}\big(v_{1},v_{2},\ell_{1}(v_{3})\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt-\ell_{2}\big(\ell_{2}(v_{1},v_{2}),v_{3}\big)-(-1)^{(|v_{1}|+|v_{2}|)\,|v_{3}|}\,\ell_{2}\big(\ell_{2}(v_{3},v_{1}),v_{2}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt-(-1)^{(|v_{2}|+|v_{3}|)\,|v_{1}|}\,\ell_{2}\big(\ell_{2}(v_{2},v_{3}),v_{1}\big)\ .

(3)

Cyclic
L_{\infty}
-algebras contain an additional structure called a cyclic pairing that is a graded symmetric and non-degenerate bilinear map
\langle-,-\rangle:V\otimes V\to\mathbb{R}
of degree
-3
, satisfying

\displaystyle\langle a_{0},\ell_{n}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})\rangle=(-1)^{n+(|a_{0}|+|a_{n}|)\,n+|a_{n}|\,\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\,|a_{i}|}\ \langle a_{n},\ell_{n}(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1})\rangle\ ,

(4)

for all
n\geq 1
.

As discussed in
[
13
]
, the data of any classical field theory are completely encoded in a corresponding
L_{\infty}
-algebra. In this contribution we restrict to theories that have only irreducible gauge symmetries, for which

V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}\ ,

where
V_{0}
contains gauge parameters,
V_{1}
contains gauge fields,
V_{2}
contains the equations of motion, and
V_{2}
contains the second Noether identities.

\boldsymbol{L_{\infty}}
-algebra of scalar field theory.

We illustrate this formalism on the very simple example of a real massive scalar field
\phi
in four dimensions with
\lambda\,\phi^{4}
-interaction. This theory has no gauge symmetries and therefore
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
, where
V_{1}=V_{2}=C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{1,3})
. The nonvanishing brackets are given by

\displaystyle\ell_{1}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\big(-\Box-m^{2}\big)\,\phi\ ,

(5)

\displaystyle\ell_{3}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3})

\displaystyle=\lambda\,\phi_{1}\,\phi_{2}\,\phi_{3}\ ,

(6)

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi,\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3}\in V_{1}
.
The cyclic pairing of degree
-3
is taken to be

\displaystyle\langle\phi,\phi^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\ \phi\,\phi^{+}

(7)

where
\phi\in V_{1}
and
\phi^{+}\in V_{2}
.

This construction leads to the action functional

\displaystyle\begin{split}S(\phi)&=\frac{1}{2!}\,\langle\phi,\ell_{1}(\phi)\rangle-\frac{1}{3!}\,\langle\phi,\ell_{2}(\phi,\phi)\rangle-\frac{1}{4!}\,\langle\phi,\ell_{3}(\phi,\phi,\phi)\rangle+\cdots\\[4.0pt]
&=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\ \left(\frac{1}{2}\,\phi\,\big(-\Box-m^{2}\big)\,\phi-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\,\phi^{4}\right)\end{split}

(8)

and the equation of motion

\displaystyle F_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\ell_{1}(\phi)-\frac{1}{2}\,\ell_{2}(\phi,\phi)-\frac{1}{3!}\,\ell_{3}(\phi,\phi,\phi)+\cdots=\big(-\Box-m^{2}\big)\,\phi-\frac{\lambda}{3!}\,\phi^{3}=0\ .

This can be extended in the evident way to scalar field theories in any dimension with arbitrary polynomial interactions.

BV formalism and
\boldsymbol{L_{\infty}}
-algebras.

The quantization of field theories possessing gauge symmetries is not straightforward. The standard Faddeev–Popov quantization procedure works well for a large number of theories, such as quantum electrodynamics and Yang–Mills theories. It is based on the path integral approach where ghost fields appear after gauge-fixing. These are scalar fields with fermionic statistics and they are necessary to keep the gauge-fixed theory unitary. The gauge-fixed action still possesses a nilpotent odd global symmetry, the Becchi–Rouet–Stora–Tyutin (BRST) symmetry. Physical states lie in the cohomology of the generator of BRST symmetry,
Q_{\textrm{\tiny BRST}}
.

Faddeev–Popov quantization was later generalized to the Batalin–Vilkovisky (BV) quantization method. One of the main advantages of the BV formalism is that it can be applied to more general field theories, such as theories with reducible and/or open gauge algebras. In particular, the BV formalism was used in quantization of open and closed bosonic string field theory as well as topological field theories.

BV quantization has manifest gauge invariance and introduces antifields as sources for this symmetry. An antifield is introduced for each field and ghost, doubling the number of degrees of freedom in this way. The antibracket
\{-,-\}
is an odd non-degenerate Poisson bracket on the space of fields and antifields. The original classical action
S
is extended to a new BV action

S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}
that satisfies the classical master equation
\{S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}},S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\}=0
, which reproduces in a compact way the
gauge structure of the original theory.

To quantize the theory, the classical master equation is modified to the quantum master equation

\frac{1}{2}\,\{S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}},S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\}-{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}=0\ ,

where now
S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}
represents the quantum BV action and a new operator
{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}
is introduced, called the BV Laplacian. To calculate correlation functions and scattering amplitudes, gauge-fixing has to be performed. In the BV formalism, gauge-fixing is implemented by choosing a Lagrangian submanifold of the space of fields and antifields; this eliminates antifields in terms of functionals of fields. When appropriately implemented, the usual Feynman diagrammatic methods can be used. For a detailed review of the BV formalism, see
[
14
]
.

From an algebraic perspective, the BV formalism constructs a differential graded commutative algebra
1
1

1

The shifted vector space
V[1]
has the same underlying vector space as
V
but with the degrees of its homogeneous
subspaces shifted by 1. For example, an element
A\in V[1]_{1}
has degree
1-1=0
.

\big(\mathrm{Sym}(V[1])^{*},Q_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\big)
for a graded vector space
V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}
which encodes the BV fields (ghosts and fields associated to a given gauge field theory) together with their antifields. The action of the BV differential
Q_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}
on fields
A\in V_{1}
and ghosts
c\in V_{0}
encodes the kinematical gauge symmetry of the field theory, that is, gauge transformations and closure of the gauge algebra. The action of
Q_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}
on the antifields
A^{+}\in V_{2}
incorporates the dynamical brackets of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra, while the BV transformations of the antifields
c^{+}\in V_{3}
correspond to the second Noether identities and the corresponding actions of the gauge parameters. The cohomology of
Q_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}
in degree
0
thus simultaneously encodes the quotients of the space of fields by the equations of motion and by the action of gauge transformations. These are exactly the classical observables of the field theory.

This data is supplemented by the antibracket
\{-,-\}
, which is the canonical graded Poisson bracket compatible with the differential
Q_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}
, whose inverse is the symplectic form
\omega_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}
of degree
-1
on
V
. Using this non-degenerate symplectic pairing we identify the dual
V^{*}\simeq V[1]
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sym}(V[1])^{*}\simeq\mathrm{Sym}(V[2])\ .

Finally, the algebra of classical observables is fully specified by
\big(\mathrm{Sym}(V[2]),Q_{\textrm{\tiny BV}},\{-,-\}\big)
.

Let us now briefly describe how to extract the corresponding
L_{\infty}
-algebra, following
[
10
]
. The action of the BV differential is given by taking the antibracket with the BV action functional
S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}
,
Q_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}=\{S_{{\textrm{\tiny BV}}},-\}
. The action functional can be expanded as

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}=\sum_{m\geq 2}\,S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}^{(m)}\ ,

where
S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}^{(m)}
is the part of
S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}
which is a polynomial of degree
m
in the BV fields. Then the brackets
\ell_{n}
of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra are given by
\big\{S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}^{(n+1)},-\big\}
for
n\geq 1
. Nilpotency
(Q_{\textrm{\tiny BV}})^{2}=0
, or equivalently the classical master equation
\{S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}},S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\}=0
, then translates to the homotopy Jacobi identities for the brackets
\ell_{n}
, and
(V,\{\ell_{n}\})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. The
(-1)
-shifted symplectic structure
\omega_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}
induces a cyclic pairing of degree
-3
on
V
, making it into a cyclic
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

Conversely, starting from an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(V,\{\ell_{n}\})
with a cyclic structure
\langle-,-\rangle
of degree
-3
, choose a basis
\{\mathtt{e}_{k}\}\subset V
and the corresponding dual basis
\{\mathtt{e}^{k}\}\subset V^{*}\simeq V[3]
such that
\langle\mathtt{e}^{k},\mathtt{e}_{l}\rangle=\delta^{k}_{l}
for all
k,l
. Following
[
10
]
, one introduces the ‘‘contracted coordinate functions’’ as the elements
2
2

2

Throughout this contribution we assume the Einstein summation convention over repeated upper and lower indices.

\displaystyle\xi:=\mathtt{e}^{k}\otimes\mathtt{e}_{k}\ \in\ \mathrm{Sym}(V[2])\otimes V

(9)

of degree
1
.

The
L_{\infty}
-structure on
V
naturally extends to the tensor product
\mathrm{Sym}(V[2])\otimes V
through the extended brackets

\displaystyle\ell_{n}^{\,\rm ext}(a_{1}\otimes v_{1},\dots,a_{n}\otimes v_{n}):=\pm\,(a_{1}\odot\cdots\odot a_{n})\otimes\ell_{n}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\ ,

for all
n\geq 1
,
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathrm{Sym}(V[2])
and
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in V
; the explicit Koszul sign factors
\pm
depend on the gradings, see
[

]
. Similarly, the cyclic structure naturally extends to a symmetric non-degenerate pairing
\langle-,-\rangle^{\rm ext}:\big(\mathrm{Sym}(V[2])\otimes V\big)\otimes\big(\mathrm{Sym}(V[2])\otimes V\big)\to\mathrm{Sym}(V[2])
of degree
-3
given by

\displaystyle\langle a_{1}\otimes v_{1},a_{2}\otimes v_{1}\rangle^{\rm ext}:=\pm\,(a_{1}\odot a_{2})\,\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle\ .

With the contracted coordinate functions (
9
), we now set

\displaystyle Q_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\xi=-\sum_{n\geq 1}\,\frac{(-1)^{\frac{n\,(n-1)}{2}}}{n!}\,\ell_{n}^{\,\rm ext}(\xi^{\otimes n})\ .

The homotopy Jacobi identities imply that
(Q_{\textrm{\tiny BV}})^{2}=0
. The
extended pairing
\langle-,-\rangle^{\rm ext}
induces a
(-1)
-shifted symplectic structure

\displaystyle\omega_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}:=-\frac{1}{2}\,\langle\delta\xi,\delta\xi\rangle^{\rm ext}\ \in\ \Omega^{2}(V[1])

whose inverse defines the corresponding antibracket. In this way we recover the differential
Q_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}
and antibracket
\{-,-\}
of the BV formalism directly from the cyclic
L_{\infty}
-algebra of a field theory.

The BV action functional is obtained from the element

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\,\frac{(-1)^{\frac{n\,(n-1)}{2}}}{(n+1)!}\,\langle\xi,\ell_{n}^{\,\rm ext}(\xi^{\otimes n})\rangle^{\rm ext}\ \in\ \mathrm{Sym}(V[2])

of degree
0
. Then

\displaystyle Q_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}=\{S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}},-\}\ ,

and nilpotency
(Q_{\textrm{\tiny BV}})^{2}=0
is equivalent to the classical master equation

\displaystyle\{S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}},S_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\}=0\ .

3.1
Braided

-algebra of scalar field theory

Braided noncommutative field theories

At the classical level, it follows that braided scalar field theory is identical to the standard noncommutative scalar field theory.

Interacting theory.

An interacting braided scalar quantum field theory is captured by the perturbation

\displaystyle\delta={\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}+\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},-\}_{\star}\ .

(25)

The antibracket in (
25
) is the braided graded Poisson bracket

\{-,-\}_{\star}:\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{R}}(V[2])\otimes\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{R}}(V[2])\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{R}}(V[2])[1]

defined by setting

\displaystyle\{\varphi_{a},\varphi_{b}\}_{\star}=\langle\varphi_{a},\varphi_{b}\rangle_{\star}=\pm\,\{\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}(\varphi_{b}),\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}(\varphi_{a})\}_{\star}

for
\varphi_{a}\in V[2]
, and extending this to all of
\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{R}}(V[2])
as a braided graded Lie bracket which is a braided graded derivation on
\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{R}}(V[2])
in each of its slots; for example

\displaystyle\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\odot_{\star}\varphi_{3}\}_{\star}=\langle\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\rangle_{\star}\odot_{\star}\varphi_{3}\pm\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}(\varphi_{2})\odot_{\star}\langle\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}(\varphi_{1}),\varphi_{3}\rangle_{\star}\ .

(26)

As in the undeformed case, the antibracket is compatible with the differential
\ell_{1}
, as a consequence of cyclicity of the inner product
\langle-,-\rangle_{\star}
. It is also related to the braided BV Laplacian through

\displaystyle{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}(a_{1}\odot_{\star}a_{2})={\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}(a_{1})\odot_{\star}a_{2}+(-1)^{|a_{1}|}\,a_{1}\odot_{\star}{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}(a_{2})+\{a_{1},a_{2}\}_{\star}\ ,

(27)

for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{R}}(L[2])
.

For
\lambda\,\phi^{4}
-theory on
\mathbb{R}^{1,3}
, the interaction action
\mathcal{S}_{\rm int}\in\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{R}}(V[2])
in (
25
) is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\rm int}:=-\frac{1}{4!}\,\langle\xi\,,\,\ell^{\star\,{\rm ext}}_{3}(\xi,\xi,\xi)\rangle^{\rm ext}_{\star}\ .

(28)

The contracted coordinate functions
\xi\in\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{R}}(L[2])\otimes V
are given by

\displaystyle\xi=\int_{k}\,\big(\mathtt{e}_{k}\otimes\mathtt{e}^{k}+\mathtt{e}^{k}\otimes\mathtt{e}_{k}\big)\ ,

where
\mathtt{e}_{k}(x)={\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,k\cdot x}
is the basis of plane waves for
V_{1}
with dual basis

\mathtt{e}^{k}(x)=\mathtt{e}_{k}^{*}(x)={\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,k\cdot x}

for
V_{2}
.

These bases are dual with respect to the inner product (
7
), in the sense that

\displaystyle\int_{p}\ \langle\mathtt{e}_{k},\mathtt{e}^{p}\rangle_{\star}\ \mathtt{e}_{p}=\mathtt{e}_{k}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\int_{k}\ \mathtt{e}^{k}\ \langle\mathtt{e}_{k},\mathtt{e}^{p}\rangle_{\star}=\mathtt{e}^{p}\ ,

where throughout we use

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\ {\mathrm{e}}^{\pm\,{\mathrm{i}}\,k\cdot x}=(2\pi)^{4}\,\delta(k)\ .

The star-products among basis fields are

\displaystyle\mathtt{e}_{k}\star\mathtt{e}_{p}={\mathrm{e}}^{\,-\frac{{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\,k\cdot\theta\,p}\ \mathtt{e}_{k+p}\ ,

(29)

while the action of the inverse
{\mathcal{R}}
-matrix on them is given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}(\mathtt{e}_{k}\otimes\mathtt{e}_{p})=\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}(\mathtt{e}_{k})\otimes\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}(\mathtt{e}_{p})={\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,k\cdot\theta\,p}\ \mathtt{e}_{k}\otimes\mathtt{e}_{p}\ .

(30)

As discussed in
[
11
,
16
]
, the interaction action (
28
) satisfies the classical master equation

\displaystyle\ell_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{\rm int})+\frac{1}{2}\,\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathcal{S}_{\rm int}\}_{\star}=0\ ,

and it is annihilated by the braided BV Laplacian,
{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}(\mathcal{S}_{\rm int})=0
. As a consequence, the operator

\displaystyle Q_{{\textrm{\tiny BV}}}=\ell_{1}+{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}+\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},-\}_{\star}

is a differential,
(Q_{{\textrm{\tiny BV}}})^{2}=0
, which describes the correlation functions in terms of a braided quantum
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

To explicitly calculate (
28
), we extend the braided
L_{\infty}
-structure on
V
to
\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{R}}(V[2])\otimes V
via the non-zero brackets
[
16
]

\displaystyle\ell^{\rm{ext}}_{1}(a_{1}\otimes\phi_{1})

\displaystyle=\pm\,a_{1}\otimes\ell_{1}(\phi_{1})\ ,

\displaystyle\ell_{3}^{\star\,{\rm{ext}}}(a_{1}\otimes\phi_{1},a_{2}\otimes\phi_{2},a_{3}\otimes\phi_{3})

\displaystyle=\pm\,\big(a_{1}\odot_{\star}\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}(a_{2})\odot_{\star}\mathrm{R}_{\beta}\,\mathrm{R}_{\sigma}(a_{3})\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\otimes\ell_{3}^{\star}\big(\mathrm{R}^{\beta}\,\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}(\phi_{1}),\mathrm{R}^{\sigma}(\phi_{2}),\phi_{3}\big)\ ,

for
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{R}}(V[2])
and
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3}\in V_{1}
; again we write
\pm
for the Koszul sign factors determined by the gradings of the elements involved in all operations. Similarly, the cyclic structure is extended via the non-zero
\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{R}}(V[2])
-valued pairing

\displaystyle\langle a_{1}\otimes\phi,a_{2}\otimes\phi^{+}\rangle^{\rm ext}_{\star}=\pm\,\big(a_{1}\odot_{\star}\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}(a_{2})\big)\,\langle\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}(\phi),\phi^{+}\rangle_{\star}\ ,

for
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{R}}(V[2])
,
\phi\in V_{1}
and
\phi^{+}\in V_{2}
.

Explicit calculation of (
28
) using (
7
), (
29
) and (
30
) results in

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{\rm int}&=-\frac{1}{4!}\,\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4}}\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\otimes\mathtt{e}_{k_{1}}\,,\,\ell^{\star\,\rm{ext}}_{3}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\otimes\mathtt{e}_{k_{2}},\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\otimes\mathtt{e}_{k_{3}},\mathtt{e}^{k_{4}}\otimes\mathtt{e}_{k_{4}})\rangle^{\rm{ext}}_{\star}\\[4.0pt]
&=-\frac{1}{4!}\,\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4}}\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\otimes\mathtt{e}_{k_{1}}\,,\,\big(\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}})\odot_{\star}\mathrm{R}_{\beta}\,\mathrm{R}_{\sigma}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{4}})\big)\\
&\hskip 170.71652pt\otimes\ell_{3}^{\star}\big(\mathrm{R}^{\beta}\,\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}(\mathtt{e}_{k_{2}}),\mathrm{R}^{\sigma}(\mathtt{e}_{k_{3}}),\mathtt{e}_{k_{4}}\big)\rangle^{\rm{ext}}_{\star}\\[4.0pt]
&=-\frac{1}{4!}\,\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{2}\cdot\theta\,k_{3}+{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{2}\cdot\theta\,k_{4}+{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{3}\cdot\theta\,k_{4}}\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\times\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\otimes\mathtt{e}_{k_{1}}\,,\,(\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{4}})\otimes\ell_{3}^{\star}(\mathtt{e}_{k_{2}},\mathtt{e}_{k_{3}},\mathtt{e}_{k_{4}})\rangle^{\rm{ext}}_{\star}\\[4.0pt]
&=-\frac{1}{4!}\,\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{2}\cdot\theta\,k_{3}+{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{2}\cdot\theta\,k_{4}+{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{3}\cdot\theta\,k_{4}}\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\times\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{4}})\,\langle\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}(\mathtt{e}_{k_{1}}),\ell_{3}^{\star}(\mathtt{e}_{k_{2}},\mathtt{e}_{k_{3}},\mathtt{e}_{k_{4}})\rangle_{\star}\\[4.0pt]
&=-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\,\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,\sum\limits_{a<b}\,k_{a}\cdot\theta\,k_{b}}\ \mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{4}}\ \langle\mathtt{e}_{k_{1}},\mathtt{e}_{k_{2}}\star\mathtt{e}_{k_{3}}\star\mathtt{e}_{k_{4}}\rangle_{\star}\\[4.0pt]
&=-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\,\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,\sum\limits_{a<b}\,k_{a}\cdot\theta\,k_{b}}\ {\mathrm{e}}^{-\,\frac{{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\,\sum\limits_{a<b}\,k_{a}\cdot\theta\,k_{b}}\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt\times(2\pi)^{4}\,\delta(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}+k_{4})\ \mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{4}}\\[4.0pt]
&=:\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4}}\,V_{4}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4})\ \mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{4}}\ .\end{split}

The interaction vertex

\displaystyle V_{4}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4})=-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\ {\mathrm{e}}^{\,\frac{{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\,\sum\limits_{a<b}\,k_{a}\cdot\theta\,k_{b}}\ (2\pi)^{4}\,\delta(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}+k_{4})

(31)

coincides with the vertex of the standard noncommutative

-theory
[

,

\displaystyle V_{4}(\ \ k_{a+1},k_{a}\ \ )={\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{a}\cdot\theta\,k_{a+1}}\ V_{4}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4})

(32)

under interchange of any pair of neighbouring momenta, and also the cyclic symmetry

\displaystyle V_{4}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4})=V_{4}(k_{4},k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})

(33)

which follows from momentum conservation.

The interacting correlation functions of the braided quantum field theory are now given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{n}^{\star}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})^{\rm int}&=\langle 0|{\rm T}[\phi(x_{1})\star\cdots\star\phi(x_{n})]|0\rangle^{\rm int}\\[4.0pt]

or in momentum space representation

\tilde{G}_{n}^{\star}(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})^{\rm int}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\,\mathsf{P}\,\big(({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H}+\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},-\}_{\star}\,\mathsf{H})^{m}\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\cdots\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{n}})\big)\ .

(35)

This is a formal power series expansion in
\hbar
and the coupling constant
\lambda
.

4
Examples: Two-point functions at one-loop

In this final section we consider two illustrative examples: the two-point functions at one-loop order for braided
\phi^{4}
-theory in four dimensions and braided
\phi^{3}
-theory in six dimension. We will present all calculations in momentum space representation.

4.1
Braided
\phi^{4}
-theory

We consider first the braided
\phi^{4}
-theory on
\mathbb{R}^{1,3}
from Section
3
, which was studied in
[
12
]
in position space representation. Let us compute the one-loop correction to the free two-point function from Section
3.2
in momentum space. Using (
34
), this is given by

\tilde{G}_{2}^{\star}(p_{1},p_{2})^{(1)}=({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})^{2}\,\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\}_{\star}\ .

(36)

We first calculate
\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\}_{\star}
using the non-zero pairings

\displaystyle\langle\mathtt{e}^{k},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p})\rangle_{\star}=\langle\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p}),\mathtt{e}^{k}\rangle_{\star}=\langle\mathtt{e}^{p},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{k})\rangle_{\star}=\tilde{\mathsf{G}}(p)\,(2\pi)^{4}\,\delta(k+p)\ ,

(37)

together with the braided derivation property (
26
) of the antibracket as well as the symmetry properties (
32
) and (
33
) of the interaction vertex
V_{4}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4})
. We find

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\}_{\star}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{2}\,\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4}}V_{4}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4})\,\bigl(\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{4}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}})\rangle_{\star}\,\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}}\,

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt+\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{4}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\bigr)\ .

Inserting this into (
36
) gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{G}_{2}^{\star}(p_{1},p_{2})^{(1)}&=\frac{4}{2}\,\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4}}\,V_{4}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4})\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\times\Bigl(\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{4}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}})\rangle_{\star}\,({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})^{2}\,\bigl(\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}}\bigr)\\
&\hskip 71.13188pt+\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{4}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})^{2}\,\bigl(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\bigr)\Bigr)\ .\end{split}

(38)

The free four-point functions in (
38
) are evaluated by using the braided Wick expansion (
23
) in momentum space. We obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})^{2}\,\big(\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}}\big)\\[4.0pt]
&\hskip 28.45274pt=-\hbar^{2}\,\big(\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\\
&\hskip 71.13188pt+{\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{3}\cdot\theta\,k_{2}}\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}})\rangle_{\star}\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\rangle_{\star}+\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}})\rangle_{\star}\big)\\[4.0pt]
&\hskip 28.45274pt=-\hbar^{2}\,(2\pi)^{8}\,\tilde{\mathsf{G}}(p_{2})\,\big(\delta(k_{1}+k_{2})\,\delta(k_{3}+p_{2})\,\tilde{\mathsf{G}}(k_{1})\\
&\hskip 71.13188pt+{\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{3}\cdot\theta\,k_{2}}\,\delta(k_{1}+k_{3})\,\delta(k_{2}+p_{2})\,\tilde{\mathsf{G}}(k_{1})+\delta(k_{1}+p_{2})\,\delta(k_{2}+k_{3})\,\tilde{\mathsf{G}}(k_{2})\big)\ ,\end{split}

(39)

and similarly

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})^{2}\,\big(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\big)\\[4.0pt]
&\hskip 28.45274pt=-\hbar^{2}\,(2\pi)^{8}\,\tilde{\mathsf{G}}(p_{1})\,\big(\delta(k_{1}+p_{1})\,\delta(k_{3}+k_{2})\,\tilde{\mathsf{G}}(k_{2})\\
&\hskip 71.13188pt+{\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{2}\cdot\theta\,k_{1}}\,\delta(p_{1}+k_{2})\,\delta(k_{1}+k_{3})\,\tilde{\mathsf{G}}(k_{1})+\delta(k_{3}+p_{1})\,\delta(k_{2}+k_{1})\,\tilde{\mathsf{G}}(k_{1})\big)\ .\end{split}

(40)

We now substitute (
39
) and (
40
) into (
38
), and resolve the delta-functions. We find that all six contributions are the same. Altogether the one-loop contribution to the two-point function in momentum space is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{2}^{\star}(p_{1},p_{2})^{(1)}=\frac{\hbar^{2}\,\lambda}{2}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{4}\,\delta(p_{1}+p_{2})}{(p_{1}^{2}-m^{2})\,(p_{2}^{2}-m^{2})}\ \int_{k}\,\frac{1}{k^{2}-m^{2}}\ .

(41)

This result agrees with the position space calculation given in
[
12

. It is independent of the deformation parameter and coincides with the classical two-point function (at
\theta=0

[
1
,
2
]
. It also suggests that there are no non-planar Feynman diagrams in perturbation theory, see Figure
2
. This appears to be a consequence of the braided symmetries of the interaction vertex due to the braided
L_{\infty}
-structure, through its interplay with the braided Wick theorem.

4.2
Braided
\phi^{3}
-theory

We extend the example of braided
\lambda\,\phi^{4}
-theory in four dimensions to braided
\lambda\,\phi^{3}
-theory in six dimensions. This is based on the braided
L_{\infty}
-algebra with graded vector space
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
, where
V_{1}=V_{2}=C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{1,5})
, differential
\ell_{1}^{\star}=\ell_{1}=-\square-m^{2}
, and the single non-vanishing higher bracket
\ell_{2}^{\star}
defined as

\displaystyle\ell_{2}^{\star}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\lambda\,\phi_{1}\star\phi_{2}\ ,

(42)

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in V_{1}
.

The calculation of the interaction action functional
\mathcal{S}_{\rm int}\in\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathcal{R}}(V[2])
proceeds as in Section
3.2
. We obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{\rm int}&=-\frac{1}{3!}\,\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\otimes\mathtt{e}_{k_{1}}\,,\,\ell_{2}^{\star\,{\rm ext}}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\otimes\mathtt{e}_{k_{2}},\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\otimes\mathtt{e}_{k_{3}})\rangle^{\rm ext}_{\star}\\[4.0pt]
&=:\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}\,V_{3}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\ \mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\ ,\end{split}

where the interaction vertex is

\displaystyle V_{3}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=-\frac{\lambda}{3!}\ {\mathrm{e}}^{\,\frac{{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\,\sum\limits_{a<b}\,k_{a}\cdot\theta\,k_{b}}\ (2\pi)^{6}\,\delta(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3})\ ,

(43)

with
a,b=1,2,3
. Analogously to (
32
) and (
33
), the vertex function (
43
) has the braided symmetry

\displaystyle V_{3}(\ \ k_{a+1},k_{a}\ \ )={\mathrm{e}}^{\,-{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{a}\cdot\theta\,k_{a+1}}\ V_{3}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})

(44)

under interchange of any pair of neighbouring momenta, and also the cyclic symmetry

\displaystyle V_{3}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=V_{3}(k_{3},k_{1},k_{2})\ .

(45)

The two-point function is defined again as

\tilde{G}_{2}^{\star}(p_{1},p_{2})^{\rm int}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\,\mathsf{P}\,\big(({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H}+\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},-\}_{\star}\,\mathsf{H})^{m}\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\big).

(46)

The one-loop corrections to the free two-point function are given by the sum of two terms

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\tilde{G}_{2}^{\star}(p_{1},p_{2})_{1}^{(1)}={\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H}\,\bigl\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}\,\big(({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})\,\bigl\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\bigr\}_{\star}\big)\bigr\}_{\star}\ ,\\[4.0pt]
&\tilde{G}_{2}^{\star}(p_{1},p_{2})_{2}^{(1)}=({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})^{2}\,\bigl\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}\,\bigl\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\bigr\}_{\star}\bigr\}_{\star}\ .\end{split}

(47)

We start with

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\bigl\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\bigr\}_{\star}=\frac{3}{2}\,\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}\,V_{3}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\,\bigl(\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}})\rangle_{\star}\,\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}}\\
&\hskip 227.62204pt+\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\bigr)\ ,\end{split}

(48)

where the non-zero pairings are of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle\mathtt{e}^{k},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p})\rangle_{\star}=\langle\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p}),\mathtt{e}^{k}\rangle_{\star}=\langle\mathtt{e}^{p},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{k})\rangle_{\star}=\tilde{\mathsf{G}}(p)\ (2\pi)^{6}\,\delta(k+p)\ .\end{split}

To calculate
\tilde{G}_{2}^{\star}(p_{1},p_{2})_{1}^{(1)}
, we apply
{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H}
to (

) and obtain

\displaystyle({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})\,\bigl\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\bigr\}_{\star}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{3}{2}\,\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}\,V_{3}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\,\bigl(\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}})\rangle_{\star}\,({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})\,(\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt+\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}})\bigr)\ .

These two terms result in

\displaystyle\begin{split}({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})\,(\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})&=\frac{2}{3}\,{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,\bigl(\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\,+\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\,-{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{1}\cdot\theta\,k_{2}}\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\bigr)\end{split}

(49)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}})&=\frac{2}{3}\,{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,\bigl(\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}})\rangle_{\star}\,\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\,+\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{2}\cdot\theta\,k_{1}}\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\bigr)\ .\end{split}

(50)

Adding another vertex insertion introduces three more internal momenta, two of which will be contracted via another application of
{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H}
, resulting in

\displaystyle\begin{split}&({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})\,\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}(\mathtt{e}^{r})\}_{\star}=3\,\int_{q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}}\,V_{3}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{q_{3}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{r})\rangle_{\star}\,\langle\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{q_{1}}),\mathtt{e}^{q_{2}}\rangle_{\star}\ ,\end{split}

(51)

where we used the symmetry properties (
44
) and (
45
). Applying (

) to (
49
) and (
50
), and adding all terms, we get the first correction term

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{G}_{2}^{\star}(p_{1},p_{2})_{1}^{(1)}&=\frac{({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,\lambda)^{2}}{6}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{6}\,\delta(p_{1})}{p_{1}^{2}-m^{2}}\ \left[\int_{k}\,\frac{1}{k^{2}-m^{2}}\right]^{2}\ \frac{(2\pi)^{6}\,\delta(p_{2})}{p_{2}^{2}-m^{2}}\\[4.0pt]
&\quad\,+\frac{({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,\lambda)^{2}}{3}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{6}\,\delta(p_{1}+p_{2})}{(p_{1}^{2}-m^{2})\,(p_{2}^{2}-m^{2})}\ \int_{k}\,\frac{1}{(0-m^{2})\,(k^{2}-m^{2})}\ .\end{split}

(52)

We recognise the first term in
\tilde{G}_{2}^{\star}(p_{1},p_{2})_{1}^{(1)}
as the contribution from the disconnected pair of tadpole diagrams and the second term as the contribution from the connected tadpole diagram, see Figure

.

To calculate
\tilde{G}_{2}^{\star}(p_{1},p_{2})_{2}^{(1)}
, we first have to add one more vertex to (

):

\displaystyle\bigl\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}\,\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\}_{\star}\bigr\}_{\star}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\frac{3}{2}\,\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}\,V_{3}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\,\bigl(\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}})\rangle_{\star}\,\bigl\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}\,(\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\bigr\}_{\star}\,

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt+\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,\bigl\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}})\bigr\}_{\star}\bigr)\ .

The first term is given explicitly by

\displaystyle\bigl\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}\,(\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\bigr\}_{\star}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\frac{3}{3}\,\int_{q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}}\,V_{3}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})\,\bigl(\langle\mathtt{e}^{q_{3}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}})\rangle_{\star}\,\mathtt{e}^{q_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{q_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}}\,

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt+\langle\mathtt{e}^{q_{3}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{q_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{q_{2}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}}\,

and similarly for the second term.

The next step is to apply the operator
{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H}
twice in a row, which is equivalent to the braided Wick theorem applied to any product of four fields found under the integral. The result is given by

\displaystyle({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})^{2}\,\bigl\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}\,\bigl\{\mathcal{S}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\star}\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\bigr\}_{\star}\bigr\}_{\star}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{3}{2}\,\int_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}\ \int_{q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}}\,V_{3}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\,V_{3}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})\,\Bigl(\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}})\rangle_{\star}\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{q_{3}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{1}})\rangle_{\star}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\bigl(\langle\mathtt{e}^{q_{1}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{q_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\rangle_{\star}+{\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{2}\cdot\theta\,p_{2}}\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{q_{1}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{q_{2}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\rangle_{\star}

\displaystyle\hskip 227.62204pt+\langle\mathtt{e}^{q_{1}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{q_{2}},\mathsf{G}(\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}})\rangle_{\star}\bigr)+\dots\Bigr)\ ,

where for brevity we display explicitly only the braided Wick expansion of the first product arising.

Unravelling everything, one gets the second correction term

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{G}_{2}^{\star}(p_{1},p_{2})_{2}^{(1)}&=\frac{({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,\lambda)^{2}}{12}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{6}\,\delta(p_{1})}{p_{1}^{2}-m^{2}}\ \left[\int_{k}\,\frac{1}{k^{2}-m^{2}}\right]^{2}\ \frac{(2\pi)^{6}\,\delta(p_{2})}{p_{2}^{2}-m^{2}}\\
&\quad\,+\frac{({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,\lambda)^{2}}{6}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{6}\,\delta(p_{1}+p_{2})}{(p_{1}^{2}-m^{2})\,(p_{2}^{2}-m^{2})}\ \int_{k}\,\frac{1}{(0-m^{2})\,(k^{2}-m^{2})}\\
&\quad\,+\frac{({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,\lambda)^{2}}{2}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{6}\,\delta(p_{1}+p_{2})}{(p_{1}^{2}-m^{2})\,(p_{2}^{2}-m^{2})}\ \int_{k}\,\frac{1}{\big(k^{2}-m^{2}\big)\,\big((p_{1}-k)^{2}-m^{2}\big)}\ .\end{split}

(53)

The first and second terms are the same as the two terms we found in
\tilde{G}_{2}^{\star}(p_{1},p_{2})^{(1)}_{1}
. The third term in
\tilde{G}_{2}^{\star}(p_{1},p_{2})^{(1)}_{2}
comes from a vacuum polarization diagram, see Figure

.

Finally, by adding the two corrections one finds that the one-loop contribution to the two-point function in momentum space is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{G}_{2}^{\star}(p_{1},p_{2})^{(1)}&=-\frac{(\hbar\,\lambda)^{2}}{4}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{6}\,\delta(p_{1})}{p_{1}^{2}-m^{2}}\ \left[\int_{k}\,\frac{1}{k^{2}-m^{2}}\right]^{2}\ \frac{(2\pi)^{6}\,\delta(p_{2})}{p_{2}^{2}-m^{2}}\\
&\quad\,-\frac{(\hbar\,\lambda)^{2}}{2}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{6}\,\delta(p_{1}+p_{2})}{(p_{1}^{2}-m^{2})\,(p_{2}^{2}-m^{2})}\ \int_{k}\,\frac{1}{(0-m^{2})\,(k^{2}-m^{2})}\\
&\quad\,-\frac{(\hbar\,\lambda)^{2}}{2}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{6}\,\delta(p_{1}+p_{2})}{(p_{1}^{2}-m^{2})\,(p_{2}^{2}-m^{2})}\ \int_{k}\,\frac{1}{\big(k^{2}-m^{2}\big)\,\big((p_{1}-k)^{2}-m^{2}\big)}\ .\end{split}

(54)

This is exactly the same result as in the commutative case because braided features that come from different places, such as the vertex and the braided Wick theorem, have cancelled each other out. Additionally, all numerical factors agree with the commutative result. Therefore, we conclude that also in the braided noncommutative
\phi^{3}
-theory in six dimensions, UV/IR mixing is absent from the two-point function at one-loop.

It would be interesting to further extend our analysis to calculate the one-loop beta-function for this model and to check whether the braided noncommutative
\phi^{3}
-theory in six dimensions is still asymptotically free. It would also be interesting to explore the extent to which our conclusions concerning the absence of non-planar diagrams and UV/IR mixing prevail at higher loops and in higher-point correlation functions, in both the
\phi^{3}
and
\phi^{4}
scalar field theories. We plan to investigate these and other problems in future work.

Appendix A
Drinfel’d twist deformation formalism

In this appendix we briefly introduce the basics of Drinfel’d twist deformation formalism and the notation we use in the main text. More detailed and thorough treatments can be found in
[
17
,
18
]
.

In the Drinfel’d twist formalism, a deformation is introduced via a twist element
{\cal F}\in U\mathfrak{v}\otimes U\mathfrak{v}
, where
U\mathfrak{v}
is the universal enveloping algebra of the Lie
algebra of vector fields
\mathfrak{v}:=\Gamma(TM)
on a manifold
M
. We use the notation
{\cal F}=\rm{f}^{\alpha}\otimes\rm{f}_{\alpha}
and
{\cal F}^{-1}=\bar{\rm{f}}^{\alpha}\otimes\bar{\rm{f}}_{\alpha}
.

The invertible
{\mathcal{R}}
-matrix
{\mathcal{R}}\in U\mathfrak{v}\otimes U\mathfrak{v}
encodes the braiding and it is induced by the twist as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}=\mathcal{F}_{21}\,{\cal F}^{-1}=:\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}\ ,

where
\mathcal{F}_{21}=\tau(\mathcal{F})=\mathrm{f}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{f}^{\alpha}
is the twist with its legs swapped. It is easy to see that the
{\mathcal{R}}
-matrix is triangular, that is

{\mathcal{R}}_{21}={\mathcal{R}}^{-1}=\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ .

(A.1)

The simplest class of twists are the abelian twists which are of the form

{\cal F}=\exp\big(-\tfrac{{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\,\theta^{ab}\,X_{a}\otimes X_{b}\big)\ ,

(A.2)

where
(\theta^{ab})
is a constant antisymmetric matrix and
\{X_{a}\}
is a set of commuting vector fields. For these twists
\mathcal{F}_{21}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}
and
{\mathcal{R}}=\mathcal{F}^{-2}
. The standard Moyal–Weyl twist and also the angular twist of
[
19
]
are examples of abelian twists.

The twist (
A.2
) deforms the pointwise product
f\cdot g
on the algebra of functions
C^{\infty}(M)
to the noncommutative star-product

\displaystyle\begin{split}f\star g&=\>\bar{\rm{f}}^{\alpha}(f)\cdot\bar{\rm{f}}_{\alpha}(g)=\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}(g)\star\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}(f)\\[4.0pt]
&=\>f\cdot g+\tfrac{{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\,\theta^{ab}\,X_{a}(f)\cdot X_{b}(g)+O(\theta^{2})\ .\end{split}

(A.3)

Extending the action of
U\mathfrak{v}
to tensor fields using the Lie derivative, the exterior algebra of differential forms
\Omega^{\bullet}(M)
is deformed in a similar way with

\displaystyle\omega_{1}\wedge_{\star}\omega_{2}=\bar{\rm{f}}^{\alpha}(\omega_{1})\wedge\bar{\rm{f}}_{\alpha}(\omega_{2})=(-1)^{|\omega_{1}|\,|\omega_{2}|}\,\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}(\omega_{2})\wedge_{\star}\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}(\omega_{1})\ ,

(A.4)

and

\displaystyle{\rm d}(\omega_{1}\wedge_{\star}\omega_{2})={\rm d}\omega_{1}\wedge_{\star}\omega_{2}+(-1)^{|\omega_{1}|}\,\omega_{1}\wedge_{\star}{\rm d}\omega_{2}\ .

By
|\omega|
we denote the degree of a homogeneous form
\omega
. The exterior derivative
{\rm d}
does not change under the deformation.

We define braided field theories using the twist formalism. A classical field theory on
\mathbb{R}^{1,d-1}
is generically Poincaré invariant, so its
L_{\infty}
-algebra consists of modules and equivariant brackets for the universal enveloping algebra
U\mathfrak{iso}(1,d-1)\subset U\mathfrak{v}
of the Poincaré algebra
\mathfrak{iso}(1,d-1)
. Hence we have to restrict to twists
\mathcal{F}\in U\mathfrak{iso}(1,d-1)\otimes U\mathfrak{iso}(1,d-1)
. We can further restrict to abelian twists
\mathcal{F}\in U\mathfrak{iso}(d-1)\otimes U\mathfrak{iso}(d-1)
constructed from the spatial isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1,d-1}
, as this simplifies some of the analysis in the quantum field theory, such as the treatment of time-ordering, as well as avoiding potential issues with unitarity.

For definiteness, and for the sake of illustration, in the main text we choose the Moyal–Weyl twist

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\exp\big(-\tfrac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\,\theta^{ij}\,\partial_{i}\otimes\partial_{j}\big)\ ,

(A.5)

where
(\theta^{ij})
is a
(d-1){\times}(d-1)
antisymmetric real-valued
matrix, and
\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\in\Gamma(T\mathbb{R}^{d-1})
for
i=1,\dots,d-1
are vector fields generating spatial translations in
\mathbb{R}^{1,d-1}
.

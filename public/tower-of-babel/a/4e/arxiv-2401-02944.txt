An a posteriori error analysis based on equilibrated stresses for finite element approximations of frictional contact
Ilaria Fontana, Daniele A. Di Pietro

Abstract
We consider the unilateral contact problem between an elastic body and a rigid foundation in a description that includes both Tresca and Coulomb friction conditions. For this problem, we present an a posteriori error analysis based on an equilibrated stress reconstruction in the Arnold–Falk–Winther space that includes a guaranteed upper bound distinguishing the different components of the error. This analysis is the starting point for the development of an adaptive algorithm including a stopping criterion for the generalized Newton method. This algorithm is then used to perform numerical simulations that validate the theoretical results.



Keywords:
frictional unilateral contact problem, weakly enforced contact conditions, a posteriori error estimate, equilibrated stress reconstruction, Arnold–Falk–Winther mixed finite element, adaptive algorithms

Introduction

In the field of mechanical engineering, accounting for contact conditions with friction is indispensable for the accurate representation of the behavior of an elastic object. This is particularly crucial in structural studies, such as those involving dams, where ensuring safety demands a thorough analysis. The most frequently used friction conditions in mechanical problems are Tresca and Coulomb conditions.
In this work, we consider the Tresca or Coulomb unilateral contact problems between an elastic body and a rigid foundation.
Numerous techniques exist in the literature for approximating solutions to such problems, including penalty formulations
[
23
]
, mixed formulations
[
19
]
, and methods based on the weak enforcement of contact conditions à la Nitsche
[
7
]
.
Since the original work on Dirichlet boundary conditions
[
24
]
, Nitsche’s technique has been extended to general boundary conditions
[
22
]
and, more recently, to the unilateral contact problem without friction
[
9
]
; see
[
7
]
for a review including subsequent developments.
From the numerical standpoint, this technique is appealing as it does not require the introduction of Lagrange multipliers and results in an easily implementable formulation.
The starting point is the general formulation proposed in
[
1
]
, which covers both Tresca and Coulomb friction cases.

The primary focus of this paper is the extension of the a posteriori error analysis via equilibrated stress reconstruction developed in
[
14
]
to the frictional unilateral contact problem. This technique, presented in
[
16
]
for the Poisson problem, is based on the Prager–Synge inequality
[
25
]
.
We provide a guaranteed upper bound of the dual norm of the error without using any saturation assumption, see Theorems
3
and
6
below. The presented upper bound is expressed in terms of fully computable a posteriori error estimators which distinguish the different sources of error.
Besides the data of the problem (volumetric and surface loadings), these estimators involve the approximate solution
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
and an equilibrated stress reconstruction
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}
, i.e., an
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{H}}({\bf div})
-conforming correction of the stress tensor
\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})
locally in equilibrium with the force source terms.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the frictional unilateral contact problem in both strong and weak forms, along with its discretization à la Nitsche.
Additionally, we provide the main space-related and mesh-related notations that we will use throughout the rest of the paper in Tables
1
and
2
. Section
3
showcases the main results of the work: a basic a posteriori error estimate, a refined version distinguishing the components of the error, and an adaptive algorithm based on the latter. This algorithm includes an adaptive stopping criterion for the linearization iterations. Moreover, we compare the dual norm of the residual with the energy norm. In Section
4
we explicitly propose how to construct an equilibrated stress reconstruction with the right properties by assembling the solutions of local problems on element patches around mesh vertices.
Section
5
validates the results of Section
3
with two numerical examples with Tresca and Coulomb friction conditions, respectively.
Finally, Section
6
concludes the work by presenting results of local and global efficiency.

Continuous problem

We consider a domain
\Omega\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, which represents a body with elastic behavior, and we suppose for simplicity that
\Omega
is a polygon if
d=2
or a polyhedron if
d=3
, so that it can be covered exactly by a finite element mesh.
The boundary of the domain is denoted by
\partial\Omega
and is subdivided into three nonoverlapping parts:
\Gamma_{\rm D}
,
\Gamma_{\rm N}
, and
\Gamma_{\rm C}
(see Figure
1
for a two-dimensional example) such that
\lvert\Gamma_{\rm D}\rvert>0
and
\lvert\Gamma_{\rm C}\rvert>0
, where
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert
is the Hausdorff measure.
The setting of the problem is the following: the body is clamped at
\Gamma_{\rm D}
, it is subject to volumetric forces
\boldsymbol{f}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)
in
\Omega
and to surface forces
\boldsymbol{g}_{\rm N}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{\rm N})
on the portion of the boundary
\Gamma_{\rm N}
, in the reference configuration it is in contact with a rigid foundation on
\Gamma_{\rm C}
, while in the deformed configuration the contact region is included in
\Gamma_{\rm C}
.
On the boundary
\partial\Omega
, we consider the outward unit normal vector
\boldsymbol{n}
that allows us to decompose any displacement field
\boldsymbol{v}
and any density of surface force
\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{v})\boldsymbol{n}
into their normal and tangential components:

\boldsymbol{v}=v^{n}\boldsymbol{n}+\boldsymbol{v}^{\boldsymbol{t}}\qquad\qquad\text{and}\qquad\qquad\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{v})\boldsymbol{n}=\sigma^{n}(\boldsymbol{v})\boldsymbol{n}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{v}).

(2.1)

The frictional unilateral contact problem then reads:
Find the displacement
\boldsymbol{u}\colon\Omega\to\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rm div}\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u})+\boldsymbol{f}=\boldsymbol{0}

\displaystyle\text{in $\Omega$},

(2.2a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u})=\lambda\tr\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{I}_{d}+2\mu\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})

\displaystyle\text{in $\Omega$},

(2.2b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{0}

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma_{\rm D}$},

(2.2c)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{g}_{\rm N}

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma_{\rm N}$},

(2.2d)

\displaystyle u^{n}\leq 0,\ \sigma^{n}(\boldsymbol{u})\leq 0,\ \sigma^{n}(\boldsymbol{u})\,u^{n}=0

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma_{\rm C}$},

(2.2e)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{u})|\leq S(\boldsymbol{u})&\qquad\text{if}\ \boldsymbol{u}^{\boldsymbol{t}}=\boldsymbol{0}\\
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{u})=-S(\boldsymbol{u})\displaystyle\frac{\boldsymbol{u}^{\boldsymbol{t}}}{\left|\boldsymbol{u}^{\boldsymbol{t}}\right|}&\qquad\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma_{\rm C}$}.

(2.2f)

Here,
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{\nabla v}+\boldsymbol{\nabla v}^{\top})
is the strain tensor field,
\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{v})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\text{sym}}
is the Cauchy stress tensor,
\boldsymbol{\rm div}
is the divergence operator acting row-wise on tensor valued functions,
\mu
and
\lambda
denote the Lamé parameters, and
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert
in (
2.2f
) is the Euclidian norm in
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
.

The first contact condition (
2.2e
) is a complementary condition representing non-penetration (
u^{n}\leq 0
) and the absence of normal cohesive forces (if
u^{n}<0
, then
\sigma^{n}(\boldsymbol{u})=0
).
The second contact condition represents the friction condition and makes it possible to include in this formulation both Tresca and Coulomb models (see also
[
1
]
):
S(\boldsymbol{u})=s\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{\rm C})
,
s\leq 0
, for the Tresca friction model, and
S(\boldsymbol{u})=-\mu_{\rm Coul}\,\sigma^{n}(\boldsymbol{u})
for the Coulomb one, where
\mu_{\rm Coul}\geq 0
is the Coulomb friction coefficient.
We remark that, while Tresca friction is easier to implement and analyze since the friction threshold
s
is a known function, Coulomb friction allows us to model the fact that there is no friction when the elastic body is not in contact with the rigid foundation in the deformed configuration.

Remark 1

(Choice of normal contact conditions)
.

In this work, we will focus on the unilateral contact problem with no jump on
\Gamma_{\rm C}
between
\Omega
and the rigid foundation, but it is also possible to adapt the present analysis to other cases by replacing the contact condition (
2.2f
):

•

for the bilateral contact problem, we simply replace it with the condition

;

•

for the unilateral contact problem with normal gap

on
\Gamma_{\rm C}
in the reference configuration, we replace it with the three conditions

u^{n}-g\leq 0,\ \sigma^{n}(\boldsymbol{u})\leq 0,\ \sigma^{n}(\boldsymbol{u})(u^{n}-g)=0.

(2.12)

In the first case, we do not need to introduce the projection operator
[\,\cdot\,]_{\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{-}}
and equation (

) becomes
\sigma^{n}(\boldsymbol{u})=P_{1,\gamma}^{n}(\boldsymbol{u})
while, in the second case, the definition of

has to be modified by replacing

with

.

3
A posteriori error analysis

The goal of this section is to present an a posteriori error estimate based on the notion of equilibrated stress reconstruction.
In this framework, following the approach of
[
14
]
, we measure the error associated with the approximate solution
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
using the dual norm of a residual operator.

3.1
Basic a posteriori error estimate

Starting from the discrete problem (
2.11
) and denoting by
(\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\rm D}(\Omega))^{*}
the dual space of
\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\rm D}(\Omega)
, for all discrete function
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
, we define the

\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h})\in(\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\rm D}(\Omega))^{*}
by its action on the space
\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\rm D}(\Omega)

\begin{split}\left\langle\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h}),\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle&\coloneqq L(\boldsymbol{v})-a(\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{v})+\left(\left[P_{1,\gamma}^{n}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h})\right]_{\mathbb{R}^{-}},v^{n}\right)_{\Gamma_{\rm C}}+\left(\left[\boldsymbol{P}_{1,\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h})\right]_{S_{h}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h})},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{t}}\right)_{\Gamma_{\rm C}}\end{split}

(3.1)

for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\rm D}(\Omega)
. Here,
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between
\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\rm D}(\Omega)
and
(\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\rm D}(\Omega))^{*}
.
Let

|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\boldsymbol{v}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|^{2}\coloneqq\lVert\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{v}\rVert^{2}+\left\lvert\boldsymbol{v}\right\rvert_{{\rm C},h}^{2}\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\rm D}(\Omega),

\left\lvert\boldsymbol{v}\right\rvert_{{\rm C},h}^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\rm C}}\frac{1}{h_{F}}\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert_{F}^{2}\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\rm D}(\Omega).

(3.3)

Given
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
, the dual norm of the residual
\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h})
on the normed space
(\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\rm D}(\Omega),|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\,\cdot\,|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|)
is given by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h})|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{*}}\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\rm D}(\Omega),\,|\kern-0.75346pt|\kern-0.75346pt|\boldsymbol{v}|\kern-0.75346pt|\kern-0.75346pt|=1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h}),\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle,

(3.4)

and the quantity
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{*}}
can be used as a measure of the error committed approximating the exact solution
\boldsymbol{u}
with
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
.

Definition 2

(Equilibrated stress reconstruction)
.

We will call
equilibrated stress reconstruction
any tensor-valued field
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}:\Omega\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
such that:

1.

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}(\textbf{div},\Omega)
,

2.

\left(\boldsymbol{\rm div}\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}+\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{T}=0
for every
\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{0}(T)
and every
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

3.

(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n})|_{F}\in\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\rm N}\cup\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\rm C}
and
\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{F}=\left(\boldsymbol{g}_{\rm N},\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{F}
for every
\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{0}(F)
and every
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\rm N}
.

Given an equilibrated stress reconstruction
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}
, for every element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define the following local error estimators:

\displaystyle\eta_{\text{osc},T}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{h_{T}}{\pi}\lVert\boldsymbol{f}+\boldsymbol{\rm div}\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\rVert_{T},

\displaystyle\text{(oscillation)}

\displaystyle\eta_{\text{str},T}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\rVert_{T},

\displaystyle\text{(stress)}

\displaystyle\eta_{\text{Neu},T}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}^{\rm N}}C_{t,T,F}h_{F}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lVert\boldsymbol{g}_{\rm N}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}\rVert_{F},

\displaystyle\text{(Neumann)}

\displaystyle\eta_{\text{cnt},T}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}^{\rm C}}h_{F}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left\lVert\left[P_{1,\gamma}^{n}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\right]_{\mathbb{R}^{-}}-\sigma^{n}_{h}\right\rVert_{F}.

\displaystyle\text{(normal contact)}

\displaystyle\eta_{\text{frc},T}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}^{\rm C}}h_{F}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left\lVert\left[\boldsymbol{P}_{1,\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\right]_{S_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}_{h}\right\rVert_{F},

\displaystyle\text{(friction)}

where,
C_{t,T,F}
is the constant of the trace inequality
\lVert\boldsymbol{v}-\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{F}\rVert_{F}\leq C_{t,T,F}h_{F}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lVert\boldsymbol{\nabla v}\rVert_{T}
with
\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{F}\coloneq\frac{1}{|F|}\int_{F}\boldsymbol{v}
, valid for every
\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(T)
and any
F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}
(see
[
29
, Theorem 4.6.3]
or
[
13
, Section 1.4]
).

The estimator
\eta_{\text{osc},T}
represents the residual of the volumetric force balance equation (
2.2a
) inside the element
T
,
\eta_{\text{str},T}
the difference between the Cauchy stress tensor computed from
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
and the equilibrated stress reconstruction,
\eta_{\text{Neu},T}
the residual of the Neumann boundary condition (
2.2d
),
\eta_{\text{cnt},T}
the residual of the normal condition (
2.2e
) on the contact boundary, and
\eta_{\text{frc},T}
the residual of the friction condition.

The following result shows a guaranteed upper bound of the dual norm of the residual (
3.1
) based on these local estimators.

Theorem 3

(A posteriori error estimate for the dual norm of the residual)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
be the solution of (
2.11
),
\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})
the residual defined by (
3.1
), and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}
an equilibrated stress reconstruction in the sense of Definition

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{*}}\leq\Biggl(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Bigl((\eta_{\emph{osc},T}+\eta_{\emph{str},T}+\eta_{\emph{Neu},T})^{2}+(\eta_{\emph{cnt},T}+\eta_{\emph{frc},T})^{2}\Bigr)\Biggr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}.

Proof.

Using the regularity of
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}
and of its normal trace established by Properties 1. and 3. in Definition
2
, the following integration by parts formula holds:

(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\nabla v})+\left(\boldsymbol{\rm div}\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}\right)-(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{v})_{\Gamma_{\rm N}}-(\sigma_{h}^{n},v^{n})_{\Gamma_{\rm C}}-(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{t}},\boldsymbol{v^{t}})_{\Gamma_{\rm C}}=0\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\rm D}(\Omega).

(3.5)

Now, fix
\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{H}^{1}_{\rm D}(\Omega)
such that
|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\boldsymbol{v}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|^{2}=\lVert\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\rVert^{2}+\left\lvert\boldsymbol{v}\right\rvert_{{\rm C},h}^{2}=1
and consider the argument of the supremum in the definition (
3.4
) of the dual norm of the residual.
Expanding
L(\cdot)
and
a(\cdot,\cdot)
according to (
2.4
) in the definition (
3.1
) of the residual written for
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
and summing (

) to the resulting expression, we obtain

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),\boldsymbol{v}\rangle&=(\boldsymbol{f}+\boldsymbol{\rm div}\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{v})+(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{v})+(\boldsymbol{g}_{\rm N}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{v})_{\Gamma_{\rm N}}\\
&\quad+\left(\left[P_{1,\gamma}^{n}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\right]_{\mathbb{R}^{-}}-\sigma^{n}_{h},v^{n}\right)_{\Gamma_{\rm C}}+\left(\left[\boldsymbol{P}_{1,\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\right]_{S_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}_{h},\boldsymbol{v^{t}}\right)_{\Gamma_{\rm C}}\eqcolon\mathfrak{T}_{1}+\cdots+\mathfrak{T}_{5},\end{split}

where we have additionally used the symmetry of

The first four terms can be treated as in

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{2}\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\eta_{\text{str},T}\lVert\boldsymbol{\nabla v}\rVert_{T},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{3}\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\eta_{\text{Neu},T}\lVert\boldsymbol{\nabla v}\rVert_{T},

\mathcal{F}_{T}^{\rm C}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{5}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{*}}

.
∎

3.2
Separating the error components

In order to find numerically the approximate solution
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
, we apply an iterative method to the nonlinear problem (
2.11
). In particular, in this work we consider the “generalized Newton method”, also employed in
[
7
]
, where no special treatment is done to account for the fact the projection operators
[\,\cdot\,]_{\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{-}}
and
[\,\cdot\,]_{S_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})}
are not Gateaux-differentiable.
At each Newton iteration
k\geq 1
, we have to solve the linear problem:
Find
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
such that

a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-\left(P_{\text{lin}}^{n,k-1}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k}),v_{h}^{n}\right)_{\Gamma_{\rm C}}-\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{\text{lin}}^{\boldsymbol{t},k-1}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k}),\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{t}}\right)_{\Gamma_{\rm C}}=L(\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h},

(3.6)

where the linearized operators are obtained setting

\begin{split}P_{\text{lin}}^{n,k-1}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h})\coloneqq&\left[P_{1,\gamma}^{n}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k-1})\right]_{\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{-}}+\frac{\partial\left[P_{1,\gamma}^{n}(\boldsymbol{v})\right]_{\boldsymbol{\mathbb{R}}^{-}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}\Bigg|_{\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k-1}}\cdot(\boldsymbol{w}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k-1})\\
=&\begin{cases}0&\qquad\text{if}\ P_{1,\gamma}^{n}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k-1})\leq 0\\
P_{1,\gamma}^{n}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h})&\qquad\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\end{split}

(3.7)

and

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{P}_{\text{lin}}^{\boldsymbol{t},k-1}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h})\coloneqq&\left[\boldsymbol{P}_{1,\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k-1})\right]_{S_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k-1})}+\frac{\partial\left[\boldsymbol{P}_{1,\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{v})\right]_{S_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k-1})}}{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}\Bigg|_{\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k-1}}\cdot(\boldsymbol{w}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k-1})\\
=&\left[\boldsymbol{P}_{1,\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k-1})\right]_{S_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k-1})}+\frac{\mathrm{d}\left[\boldsymbol{x}\right]_{S_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k-1})}}{\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}}\Bigg|_{\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{P}_{1,\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k-1})}\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{1,\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h})-\boldsymbol{P}_{1,\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k-1})\right).\end{split}

(3.8)

3.3

This subsection is devoted to comparing the dual norm of the residual

Proof.

Proceeding as in

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\rm en}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\rangle+\left(\sigma^{n}(\boldsymbol{u})-\left[P_{1,\gamma}^{n}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\right]_{\mathbb{R}^{-}},u^{n}-u^{n}_{h}\right)_{\Gamma_{\rm C}}

(3.18)

\displaystyle+\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{u})-\left[\boldsymbol{P}_{1,\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\right]_{S_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h})},\boldsymbol{u}^{\boldsymbol{t}}-\boldsymbol{u}^{\boldsymbol{t}}_{h}\right)_{\Gamma_{\rm C}}\eqqcolon\mathfrak{T}_{1}+\mathfrak{T}_{2}+\mathfrak{T}_{3}.

For the first two terms we use, respectively, the definition (
3.4

and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality followed by the definition (

) of

&\lesssim\left[\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{\rm C}}h_{F}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{F}^{p+1}\left[\boldsymbol{P}_{1,\gamma}^{\boldsymbol{t}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k})\right]_{S_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k})}-\boldsymbol{\sigma^{t}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k})\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right]^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{h}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{\omega_{\boldsymbol{a}}},\end{split}

and also

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{3}\lesssim\left[\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{a}}^{\rm N}}h_{F}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{F}^{p+1}\boldsymbol{g}_{N}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{k})\boldsymbol{n}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right]^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{h}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}\right\rVert_{\omega_{\boldsymbol{a}}}

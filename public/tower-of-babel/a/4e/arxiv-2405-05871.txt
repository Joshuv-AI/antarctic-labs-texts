The Iwasawa μ \mu -invariant of certain elliptic curves of analytic rank zero
Adithya Chakravarthy

Abstract
. This paper is about the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves over the cyclotomic 𝐙 p \mathbf{Z}_{p} -extension 𝐐 cyc \mathbf{Q}_{\text{cyc}} of 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} . We discuss a deep conjecture of Greenberg that if E / 𝐐 E/\mathbf{Q} is an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at p p , and E ⁡ [ p ] E[p] is irreducible as a Galois module, then the Selmer group of E E over 𝐐 cyc \mathbf{Q}_{\text{cyc}} has μ \mu -invariant zero. We prove new cases of Greenberg’s conjecture for some elliptic curves of analytic rank 0 0 . The proof involves studying the p p -adic L L -function of E E . The crucial input is a new technique using the Rankin-Selberg method.



1.
Introduction

We begin with a fundamental theorem of Iwasawa, which serves as the starting point of Iwasawa theory. Let
K
be a number field and let
K_{\infty}/K
be a
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-extension: a Galois extension with Galois group isomorphic to the additive group
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
of
p
-adic integers. For each integer
n>0
there is a unique subfield
K_{n}
of
K_{\infty}
of degree
p^{n}
over
K
. Iwasawa proved the following now famous theorem about the growth of class numbers in such towers.

Theorem

(Iwasawa)
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
K_{\infty}/K
be a
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
extension with layers
K_{n}
. Suppose that
p^{e_{n}}
is the exact power of
p
dividing the class number of
K_{n}
. Then there exist integers
\mu,\lambda,\nu
such that

e_{n}=\mu p^{n}+\lambda n+\nu

for all sufficiently large values of
n
.

The most important example of a
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
is the so-called
cyclotomic
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-extension
of
K
, denoted
K^{\text{cyc}}/K
. It is defined by letting
K^{\text{cyc}}
be the appropriate subfield of
\cup_{n\geq 1}K(\zeta_{p^{n}})
. Iwasawa has conjectured the following about the cyclotomic
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-extension.

Conjecture 1.1

(Iwasawa’s
\mu=0
Conjecture)
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
K^{\text{cyc}}
be the cyclotomic
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
extension of
K
. Then
\mu=0
.

This conjecture is probably the deepest open problem from classical Iwasawa theory. The only progress towards this comes from a paper of Ferrero and Washington
[
FW79
]
, where they prove the conjecture in the case when
K/\mathbf{Q}
is abelian. Other than this, the conjecture remains wide open and it seems intractable at present.

This paper is about a generalization of Iwasawa’s
\mu=0
conjecture to the Iwasawa theory of
elliptic curves
. We now briefly outline the main aspects of the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves and then state the analogue of Iwasawa’s
\mu=0
conjecture in this context. In a landmark paper
[
Maz72
]
, Mazur observed that many features of classical Iwasawa theory could be used to study elliptic curves. This initiated the study of what we now call the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves. If
E
is an elliptic curve over a number field
K
, let
\Sha(E/K)[p^{\infty}]
denote the
p
-primary part of the Tate-Shafarevich group of
E
over
K
. We then have the following analogue
[
Gre99
, Theorem 1.10]
of Iwasawa’s theorem.

Theorem
.

Let
\mathbf{Q}^{\text{cyc}}=\cup\mathbf{Q}_{n}
denote the cyclotomic
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
extension of
\mathbf{Q}
. Let
E/\mathbf{Q}
be an elliptic curve, and let
p
be a prime of good ordinary reduction. Assume that
\Sha(E/\mathbf{Q}_{n})[p^{\infty}]
is finite for all
n
and let
p^{e_{n}}
be the exact power of
p
dividing
|\Sha(E/\mathbf{Q}_{n})[p^{\infty}]|
. Then there exist integers
\mu,\lambda,\nu
such that

e_{n}=\mu p^{n}+\lambda n+\nu

for all sufficiently large values of
n
.

The quantity
\mu_{p}(E)
in the above formula is called the
\mu
-invariant
of
E
over
\mathbf{Q}^{\text{cyc}}
. (See Section 2 for a more abstract definition of
\mu_{p}(E)
.) In analogy with Iwasawa’s
\mu=0
conjecture for class groups, one might expect that
\mu_{p}(E)
always vanishes. This turns out to be
false
. Indeed, Mazur
[
Maz72
]
discovered that the elliptic curve
X_{0}(11)
has a nonzero
\mu
-invariant at the prime
p=5
. However, in a beautiful paper
[
Gre99
, Conjecture 1.11]
, Greenberg conjectures that one can rescue Iwasawa’s conjecture in the following sense:

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
E/\mathbf{Q}
be an elliptic curve, let
p
be an odd prime of good ordinary reduction. Then there is a
\mathbf{Q}
-isogenous curve
E^{\prime}
such that
\mu_{p}(E^{\prime})=0
.

That is, Greenberg predicts that
\mu
can be made zero after perhaps shifting by an isogeny. In particular, the above conjecture implies:

Conjecture 1.3

(Greenberg’s
\mu=0
Conjecture)
.

Let
E/\mathbf{Q}
be an elliptic curve, and let
p
be an odd prime of good ordinary reduction. If
E[p]
is irreducible as a Galois module, then
\mu_{p}(E)=0
.

Remark 1.4
.

It is known by the work of Drinen
[
Dri03
]
that this conjecture is false over general number fields. So it applies particularly for curves
E
over
\mathbf{Q}
.

Remark 1.5
.

Schneider has given a simple formula for the effect of an isogeny on the
\mu
-invariant of
E
for odd
p
(see
[
Sch87
, Isogeny Formula, Second Form]
). Thus, Greenberg’s conjecture effectively predicts the
\mu
-invariant of any elliptic curve over
\mathbf{Q}
.

Greenberg’s conjecture bifurcates into two cases, one of which seems to be much harder than the other. First, there is the case where
E[p]
is reducible as a Galois module (the "reducible case") and second, there is the case where
E[p]
is irreducible as a Galois module (the "irreducible case"). Most of the progress to date has been in the
reducible
case, where we have the following theorem
[
GV00
, Theorem 1.3]
of Greenberg and Vatsal: if
E[p]
has a
\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}/\mathbf{Q})
-submodule which is either ramified at
p
and even, or unramified at
p
and odd, then
\mu_{p}(E)=0
. This theorem follows from a bootstrapping of the Ferrero-Washington Theorem. This result was furthered by Trifkovic
[
Tri05
]
, who showed that Greenberg’s conjecture was true for infinitely many curves
E
with
E[p]
reducible, for
p=3
or
5
. The core strategy in Trifkovic’s paper was an explicit evaluation of the global duality pairing for finite flat group schemes over rings of integers.

There has also been progress in proving that "
\mu=0
" can be propagated in families of modular forms. In a beautiful paper, Emerton-Pollack-Weston
[
EPW06
]
, showed that "
\mu=0
" is invariant in Hida families. That is, if
f
is a modular form with
\mu=0
, then that implies that every form in the Hida family passing through
f
also has
\mu=0
. On another front, Ray recently
[
Ray24
]
showed the following result for
p=5
: there is a positive proportion of elliptic curves
E/\mathbf{Q}
for which
\mu_{5}(E)=\lambda_{5}(E)=0
. Ray uses results of Bhargava-Shankar
[
BS13
]
on average ranks of Selmer groups. In another paper, Ray
[
Ray23
]
also showed that if
E[p]
is irreducible, if the classical Iwasawa
\mu
-invariant vanishes for the splitting field
\mathbf{Q}(E[p])
, and a purely Galois theoretic condition on
E[p]
holds, Greenberg’s conjecture should hold.

The irreducible case of Greenberg’s conjecture, however, remains unsolved in general. In this paper, we prove new results about the irreducible case of Greenberg’s Conjecture, specifically for some elliptic curves with analytic rank
0
:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
E/\mathbf{Q}
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
. Let
p
be a prime of good ordinary reduction. Assume:

(1)

L(E/K,1)\neq 0
, where
K=\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{-N})
and
L(E/K,s)
denotes the Hasse-Weil
L
-series of
E
over
K
,

(2)

p>100\sqrt{N}(\log N+5)m_{E}
, where
m_{E}
denotes the modular degree of
E
,

(3)

p
does not divide the the Manin constant of
E
,

(4)

p
is not anomalous for
E
, i.e:
p
does not divide
\#E(\mathbf{F}_{p})
,

(5)

N
is a prime congruent to
3
modulo
4
.

Then
\mu_{p}(E)=0
and
\lambda_{p}(E)=0
.

The following are well-known consequences:

Corollary 1.7
.

Keep the notations and assumptions as above. Let
\mathbf{Q}_{\text{cyc}}
denote the cyclotomic
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbf{Q}
and let
\mathbf{Q}_{n}
denote the
n
-th layer of
\mathbf{Q}_{\text{cyc}}
. Then:

(1)

the Mordell-Weil rank of
E(\mathbf{Q}_{n})
is zero for all
n\geq 1
, and

(2)

the
p
-primary part of
\Sha(E/\mathbf{Q})
vanishes.

1.1.
Examples

Let
E_{1}=\texttt{11a1}
be the elliptic curve of conductor
11
. We show in the final section of the paper that
L(E_{1}/\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{-11}),1)\neq 0
. Theorem
1.6
says that if
p>100\sqrt{N}(\log N+5)\,m_{E}\approx 2453.604\dots
is a good ordinary prime, then
\mu_{p}(E_{1})=\lambda(E_{1})=0
. Furthermore, we have
\Sha(E/\mathbf{Q})[p^{\infty}]=0
and the rank of

\mathbf{Q}_{n}
is zero for all

.

As another example, let
E_{2}=\texttt{67a1}
be the elliptic curve of conductor

. We show in the final section of the paper that
L(E_{2}/\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{-67}),1)\neq 0
. If
p>100\sqrt{N}(\log N+5)m_{E}\approx 37671.828\dots
is a good ordinary prime which is not anomalous for

, then
\mu_{p}(E_{2})=\lambda_{p}(E_{2})=0
. Furthermore, we have

However, some lower bound on

is necessary. Indeed, consider the elliptic curve

and

. From the LMFDB
[

, the analytic order of

1.2.
Strategy

Using results of Kato on the Iwasawa main conjecture, it is known that in order to prove that
\mu_{p}(E)=\lambda_{p}(E)=0
, it suffices to show that the
p
-adic
L
-function of
E
is a unit. One knows that if
p
is not anomalous and that
p
does not divide
L(E,1)/\Omega_{E}
, then the
p
-adic
L
-function is a unit. So as long as one avoids the finitely many prime divisors of
L(E,1)/\Omega_{E}
(and the anomalous primes), then we have
\mu_{p}(E)=0
and
\lambda_{p}(E)=0
. But a priori, one has no idea what primes divide
L(E,1)/\Omega_{E}
. The main point of this paper is to give an
explicit upper bound
for which primes divide
L(E,1)/\Omega_{E}
. This is roughly the statement of Theorem
2.2
. We prove Theorem
2.2
using the Rankin-Selberg method.

Remark 1.8
.

In proving Theorem
1.6
, we actually show Greenberg’s Conjecture both for
E
and the quadratic twist
E^{(-N)}
by
-N
.

1.3.
Outline

In the first section, we explain the Iwasawa main conjecture. We then reduce Theorem
1.6
to Theorem
2.2
, which is purely a statement about
L
-values of modular forms. In the next section, we explain the Rankin-Selberg method. In the section after that, we prove Theorem
2.2
. Finally in the last section, we give numerical examples that use the Rankin-Selberg method to calculate Iwasawa invariants.

Preliminaries

In this section,
p
will denote an odd prime and
E/\mathbf{Q}
will be an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at

\mathbf{Q}^{\text{cyc}}
denote the cyclotomic
\mathbf{Z}_{p}

To fix notation, we briefly summarize some facts about the Iwasawa main conjecture for elliptic curves. We will first summarize the objects on the algebraic side of the main conjecture, then summarize the objects on the analytic side, and then state the Iwasawa main conjecture which bridges these two worlds.

The algebraic side

For any algebraic extension
K/\mathbf{Q}
, the Selmer group of
E
over
K
is a certain subgroup of
H^{1}(G_{K},E(\overline{\mathbf{Q}})_{\text{tors}})
, where
G_{K}=\text{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
.
The Selmer group fits into the fundamental exact sequence

0\to E(K)\otimes\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}\to\text{Sel}(E/K)\to\Sha(E/K)\to 0

where
\Sha(E/K)
denotes the Tate-Shafarevich group of
E
over
K
. Let
K=\mathbf{Q}^{\text{cyc}}
. Then we can consider the Selmer group
\text{Sel}(E/\mathbf{Q}^{\text{cyc}})
, and this has an action of
\Gamma=\text{Gal}(\mathbf{Q}^{\text{cyc}}/\mathbf{Q})
. Its
p
-primary subgroup
\text{Sel}(E/\mathbf{Q}^{\text{cyc}})_{p}

\Lambda
-module, where
\Lambda=\mathbf{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. This ring
\Lambda
is called the
Iwasawa algebra
. It is now known from the deep work of Kato
[
Kat04
]
that the Pontryagin dual
X(E/\mathbf{Q}^{\text{cyc}})=\text{Sel}(E/\mathbf{Q}^{\text{cyc}})_{p}^{\vee}
is a finitely generated
torsion

\Lambda
-module. Therefore the structure theorem of finitely generated
\Lambda
-modules (see for example
[
Was97
, Theorem 13.12]
) says that one has a pseudo-isomorphism

X(E/\mathbf{Q}^{\text{cyc}})\sim\left(\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda/(f_{i}(T)^{a_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\oplus_{j=1}^{m}\Lambda/(p^{\mu_{j}})\right),

where the
f_{i}(T)
’s are irreducible distinguished polynomials
1
1

1

A polynomial
f(T)
is

if when you reduce
f
modulo
p
, only the highest degree term remains.
in
\Lambda
. One can then define the algebraic Iwasawa invariants by

\lambda_{E}^{\text{alg}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\text{deg}(f_{i}(T)),\hskip 20.0pt\text{and}\hskip 20.0pt\mu^{\mathrm{alg}}_{p}(E)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\mu_{j}.

The
characteristic ideal
of
X(E/\mathbf{Q}^{\text{cyc}})
is the ideal of
\Lambda
generated by
p^{\mu}f_{1}(T)^{a_{1}}\dots f_{n}(T)^{a_{n}}
.

The analytic side

For an elliptic curve
E/\mathbf{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at a prime
p
and
\chi
an even Dirichlet character, denote by
L(E,\chi,s)
the Hasse Weil
L
-function of
E
twisted by
\chi
. Let
H_{1}(E(\mathbf{C}),\mathbf{Z})^{\pm}
be the eigenspaces under complex conjugation, and let
\Lambda_{E}^{\pm}
be the respective generators of these spaces over
\mathbf{Z}
. The periods
\Omega_{E}^{+}
and
\Omega_{E}^{-}
are defined by

\Omega_{E}^{\pm}=\int_{\Lambda_{E}^{\pm}}\omega_{E},

where
\omega_{E}
is the invariant differential of
E
. We may assume that
\Omega_{E}^{+}
and
i\Omega_{E}^{-}
are real positive. It is known by the work of Shimura that
L(E,\chi,1)/\Omega_{E}^{\chi(-1)}
,
a priori
a transcendental number, is in fact an
algebraic
number.
Mazur and Swinnterton-Dyer have attached to
E
a
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}(E/\mathbf{Q},T)\in\Lambda\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}
satisfying the following interpolation properties. If we write
a_{p}=(p+1)-\#E(\mathbf{F}_{p})
, consider the Hecke polynomial
X^{2}-a_{p}X+p
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}
denote this unique
p
-adic unit root of the Hecke polynomial. Then,

(1)

\mathcal{L}_{p}(E/\mathbf{Q},0)=\left(1-\dfrac{1}{\alpha}\right)^{2}\cdot\dfrac{L(E,1)}{\Omega_{E}^{+}}.

Let
\chi
be an even Dirichlet character of conductor
p^{n}
and
p
-power order. Then,

\mathcal{L}_{p}(E/\mathbf{Q},\chi(1+p)-1)=\dfrac{1}{\alpha^{n+1}}\cdot\dfrac{p^{n+1}}{g(\chi^{-1})}\dfrac{L(E,\chi^{-1},1)}{\Omega_{E}^{+}}.

Using the Weierstrauss preparation theorem
[
Was97
, Theorem 7.3]
, we can define the analytic invariants
\mu^{\mathrm{an}}_{p}(E)
and
\lambda_{E}^{an}
by writing:

\mathcal{L}_{p}(E/\mathbf{Q},T)=p^{\mu^{\mathrm{an}}_{p}(E)}\cdot u(T)\cdot f(T)

where
f(T)
is a distinguished polynomial of degree
\lambda_{E}^{an}
and
u(T)
is a unit in
\Lambda
.
It is known (see
[
SW13
, Proposition 3.7]
) that if
E/\mathbf{Q}
has good ordinary reduction at an
odd

p
, and that
E[p]
is irreducible as a Galois module, then
\mu^{\mathrm{an}}_{p}(E)\geq 0
.
In other words,
\mathcal{L}_{p}(E/\mathbf{Q},T)\in\Lambda
.

Iwasawa Main Conjecture

The Iwasawa
main conjecture
relates the Selmer group on the algebraic side to the
p
-adic
L
-function on the analytic side. Precisely, suppose that
E/\mathbf{Q}
is an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at an
odd
prime
p
, and that
E[p]
is irreducible as a Galois module. Then on the algebraic side, we can look at the characteristic ideal of the
p
-primary Selmer group
X(E/\mathbf{Q}^{\text{cyc}})
; this is an ideal in
\Lambda
. On the analytic side, we can attach to
E
a
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}(E/\mathbf{Q},T)\in\Lambda
. The
main conjecture
asserts that the characteristic ideal of the
p
-primary Selmer group
X(E/\mathbf{Q}^{\text{cyc}})
is generated by
\mathcal{L}_{p}(E/\mathbf{Q},T)
in
\Lambda
. In particular, it implies that
\mu_{E}^{\text{alg}}=\mu_{E}^{\text{an}}
and
\lambda_{E}^{\text{alg}}=\lambda_{E}^{\text{an}}
. Kato
[
Kat04
]
has proven a deep result on one divisibility of the main conjecture, which is the starting point of our proof.

Theorem 2.1

(Kato)
.

Let
E/\mathbf{Q}
be an elliptic curve, and let
p\geq 5
be a prime of good ordinary reduction. Suppose that the mod
p
Galois representation
\overline{\rho_{E,p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}G_{\mathbf{Q}}\to\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
is surjective. Then the characteristic ideal of
X(E/\mathbf{Q}^{\text{cyc}})
divides the ideal generated by
\mathcal{L}_{p}(E/\mathbf{Q},T)
in
\Lambda
. In particular,
\mu_{E}^{\text{alg}}\leq\mu_{E}^{\text{an}}
and
\lambda_{E}^{\text{alg}}\leq\lambda_{E}^{\text{an}}
.

2.1.
Modular Forms

For the rest of the paper, fix an embedding
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
. Kato’s Theorem shows that to prove
\mu^{\text{alg}}_{p}(E)=\lambda^{\text{alg}}_{p}(E)=0
, it suffices to show that
\mu^{\text{an}}_{p}(E)=\lambda^{\text{an}}_{p}(E)=0
. This is equivalent to showing that the constant term of
\mathcal{L}_{p}(E/\mathbf{Q},T)
is nonzero mod
p
; by (
1
), this means

\left(1-\dfrac{1}{\alpha}\right)^{2}\cdot\dfrac{L(E,1)}{\Omega_{E}^{+}}\not\equiv 0\mod p.

(Note that we are viewing
L(E,1)/\Omega_{E}^{+}
as an element of
\overline{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
via our fixed emebedding
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
.) This is really a problem about modular forms, not elliptic curves. To see this, let
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be the newform associated to
E
by modularity. Then we have an equality of
L
-functions:
L(E,s)=L(f,s)
. Define the Petersson norm of

as follows:

\langle f,f\rangle=\int_{\Gamma_{0}(N)\setminus\mathbf{H}}|f|^{2}dx\,dy.

An elliptic curve
E/\mathbf{Q}
is
optimal
if the map
H_{1}(X_{0}(N)(\mathbf{C}),\mathbf{Z})\to H_{1}(E(\mathbf{C}),\mathbf{Z})
is surjective. (See
[
Ste89
]
). We will derive Theorem
1.6
from:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
N
be a prime and let
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a weight
2
newform with rational Fourier coefficients. Let
p
be an odd prime. Assume that

(1)

N
is congruent to
3
modulo
4
,

(2)

L(f,1)L(f,\chi,1)\neq 0
, where
\chi
is the unique quadratic character mod

, and

(3)

p>100\sqrt{N}(\log N+5)\deg\pi_{E}
, where

is an optimal elliptic curve in the isogeny class corresponding to

.

Then
p
does not divide
\dfrac{L(f,1)\,L(f,\chi,1)}{\pi^{2}\langle f,f\rangle}\,\dfrac{g(\chi)}{i}
.

2.2

\implies

Let
E/\mathbf{Q}

. Let

\pi_{E}^{*}(\omega_{E})=c_{E}\cdot 2\pi if(z)dz,

(2)

\Omega_{E}^{+}\Omega_{E}^{-}=\int_{E(\mathbf{C})}\omega_{E}\wedge\overline{\omega_{E}}\\

where

4.
Proof of Theorem

We first need two preliminary results.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
N
be an odd integer. Let
\chi
be an odd primitive quadratic character of conductor

. Then

L(0,\chi)\leq\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2\pi}(\log N+5).

Proof.

In
[

, Corollary 2]
, Ramaré proves the following bound for odd primitive characters
\chi
modulo odd

(6)

|(1-\chi(2)/2)L(1,\chi)|\leq\frac{1}{4}(\log N+5).

(Note that Ramaré does not require
\chi
to be quadratic.) The functional equation for

gives

L(0,\chi)=\dfrac{g(\chi)}{i\pi}L(1,\chi).

Since
|g(\chi)|=\sqrt{N}
for

L(0,\chi)\leq\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2\pi}(\log N+5-2\log(3/2))\leq\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2\pi}(\log N+5),

which completes the proof.
∎

Next we show that algebraic

-values have bounded denominators. An elliptic curve

is

if the map
H_{1}(X_{0}(N)(\mathbf{C}),\mathbf{Z})\to H_{1}(E(\mathbf{C}),\mathbf{Z})
is surjective.

Proposition 4.2

Let
N
be a prime which is
3
mod

be an odd quadratic character mod

(7)

\dfrac{L(f,1)\,L(f,\chi,1)}{\pi^{2}\langle f_{j},f_{j}\rangle}\,\dfrac{g(\chi)}{i}

has denominator
\leq 576m_{E}
, where
E
is an optimal curve in the isogeny class of
f
and
m_{E}
denotes the modular degree of
E
.

The proof of Proposition
4.2
has a distinct thread from the rest of the proof so we prove it in the next section.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
2.2
. Let
N
be a prime which is
3

4

is odd. From Proposition

(8)

where the sum runs over all newforms

and

is the unique normalized Eisenstein series and

is a constant. Looking at the first Fourier coefficient (i.e: the coefficient of

L(0,\chi)=c+\sum_{j}\dfrac{L(f_{j},1)\,L(f_{j},\chi,1)}{\pi^{2}\langle f_{j},f_{j}\rangle}\,\dfrac{g(\chi)}{i}.

It is classical that

\dfrac{L(0,\chi)-c}{\sqrt{N}}=\sum_{j}\dfrac{L(f_{j},1)\,L(f_{j},\chi,1)}{\pi^{2}\langle f_{j},f_{j}\rangle}.

From (

about non-negativity of

Theorem 4.3

Let
N\geq 1

The Rankin-Selberg method allows us to compute the algebraic parts of

-values, which in turn allows us to calculate Iwasawa invariants. We demonstrate this in two examples.

Example 1
.

Let
N=11
and let
\chi
be the unique (odd) quadratic character of conductor
11

) in the space

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{12}+q+3q^{2}+4q^{3}+7q^{4}+6q^{5}+\dots,

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=q-2q^{2}-q^{3}+2q^{4}+q^{5}+\dots.

q
-expansions, we find that

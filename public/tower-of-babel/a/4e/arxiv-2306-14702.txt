Deep Unfolding Enabled Constant Modulus Waveform Design for Joint Communications and Sensing
Prashanth Krishnananthalingam, Nhan Thanh Nguyen, Markku Juntti

Abstract
Joint communications and sensing (JCAS) systems have recently emerged as a promising technology to utilize the scarce spectrum in wireless networks and to reuse the same hardware to save infrastructure costs. In practical JCAS systems, dual functional constant-modulus waveforms can be employed to avoid signal distortion in nonlinear power amplifiers. However, the designs of such waveforms are very challenging due to the nonconvex constant-modulus constraint. The conventional branch-and-bound (BnB) method can achieve optimal solution but at the cost of exponential complexity and long run time. In this paper, we propose an efficient deep unfolding method for the constant-modulus waveform design in a multiuser multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) JCAS system. The deep unfolding model has a sparsely-connected structure and is trained in an unsupervised fashion. It achieves good communications-sensing performance tradeoff while maintaining low computational complexity and low run time. Specifically, our numerical results show that the proposed deep unfolding scheme achieves a similar achievable rate compared to the conventional BnB method with 30 times faster execution time.

To tackle the challenging problem, the branch and bound (BnB) method has been employed to achieve optimal performance. However, its complexity is exponential with the number of transmit antennas and the run time is generally long. These challenges motivate us to leverage machine learning (ML), in particular deep learning (DL) techniques for constant-modulus waveform designs.

However, the existing DL-based JCAS designs mostly relied on the learning capabilities of black-box deep neural networks (DNNs). Consequently, they are not explainable as the conventional model-based optimization design methods. Furthermore, their training is generally a challenging task requiring much effort in fine tuning and generating massive data sets. Thus, many conventional optimizers and data-driven DNNs exhibit major limitations due to their resource-constraints, high complexity, and black-box nature. Alternatively, the deep unfolding approach leverage both domain knowledge and learning capabilities to build explainable DL models that achieve performance gains and are easier to implement

In this paper, we consider a multiuser MIMO JCAS system. We aim at optimizing the communications-sensing performance tradeoff via minimizing the weighted sum of the MUI and the deviation from the benchmark sensing waveform. In particular, the constant-modulus waveform is considered, as motivated by the work of Liu
et al.

The model is trained in an unsupervised strategy to output a reliable constant-modulus waveform. In particular, it has a sparsely connected network architecture, and, thus, it performs the JCAS design with very low computational complexity. We finally present extensive simulation results of the proposed scheme in comparison with the conventional BnB approach
[
38

II
System Model

We consider a multiuser MIMO JCAS system, where the base station (BS) equipped with
N
antennas simultaneously transmits probing signals to the targets and data signals to
K
single-antenna users, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Let
{\mathbf{x}}_{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
be the transmit signal vector in the
m
-th communications time frame, and let
{\mathbf{X}}=[{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}_{M}]
, with
M
being the length of the communication frame. Take
{\mathbf{h}}_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times 1}
as the channel vector corresponds to the
n
-th antenna of the BS, and consider
{\mathbf{H}}=[{\mathbf{h}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{h}}_{N}]
. Consider
{\mathbf{z}}_{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times 1}
as the noise vector corresponds to the
m
-th communications time frame, where
{\mathbf{z}}_{m}\sim CN(0,N_{0}\textbf{I}_{N})
and take
{\mathbf{Z}}=[{\mathbf{z}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{z}}_{M}]
.

The received signal at
K
users can be expressed as

{\mathbf{Y}}={\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{X}}+{\mathbf{Z}}.

(1)

The transmit signal matrix
{\mathbf{X}}
is used for both communications and radar sensing. Hence, each communications symbol is also a snapshot of a radar pulse. The downlink channel
{\mathbf{H}}
is assumed to be flat Rayleigh fading and remains unchanged during a single communications frame/radar pulse. Moreover, the channel is assumed to be perfectly estimated at the BS via pilot symbols.

Let
{\mathbf{s}}_{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times 1}
be the desired symbol vector to be received by the users during the
m
-th communications time frame, and let
\textbf{S}=[{\mathbf{s}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{s}}_{M}]
, whose entries are assumed to be drawn from the same constellation for all users.
To unveil the effects of the MUI in the received signals, let us rewrite the signal model (
1
) as

{\mathbf{Y}}=\textbf{S}+({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{X}}-\textbf{S})+{\mathbf{Z}},

(2)

where the second term, i.e.,
({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{X}}-\textbf{S})
, represents the MUI in the system. The total MUI power can be given as

P_{\textrm{MUI}}=\|{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{X}}-\textbf{S}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i,j}|{\mathbf{h}}_{i}^{T}{\mathbf{x}}_{j}-s_{i,j}|^{2},

(3)

where
s_{i,j}
is the (
i, j
)-th entry of
S
, and
\mathbb{E}(.)
represents the ensemble average with respect to time index. The signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) per frame for the
i
-th user is given as

\gamma_{i}=\frac{\mathbb{E}(|s_{i,j}|^{2})}{\mathbb{E}(|{\mathbf{h}}_{i}^{T}{\mathbf{x}}_{j}-s_{i,j}|^{2})+N_{0}}.

(4)

It is observed that the MUI directly impacts the SINR of the downlink users, which degrades the performance of the communications.

II-A

Radar Model

We use the orthogonal chirp waveform matrix, which is adapted from
[
39
]
as the benchmark waveform,
{\mathbf{X}}_{0}
for the radar system. Moreover, the transmit beam pattern of the JCAS system is given as

P_{\textrm{d}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{M}\ \textbf{a}^{H}(\theta){\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{H}\textbf{a}(\theta),

(5)

where,
\theta
refers to the detection angle and
\textbf{a}(\theta)=[1,e^{j2\pi\Delta sin(\theta)},\ldots,e^{j2\pi(N-1)\Delta sin(\theta)}]\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
is the antenna steering vector with
\Delta
denoting the normalized antenna spacing with respect to the wavelength.

II-B

Problem Formulation

We consider a weighted minimization problem which can be formulated below. Here, the objective function is the weighted sum of the MUI and the distance between
{\mathbf{X}}
and the desired waveform.

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle{\mathbf{X}}}{\mathrm{subject\penalty\ to}}$}][l]{\underset{\displaystyle{\mathbf{X}}}{\mathrm{minimize}}}\quad\rho\|{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{X}}-\textbf{S}\|_{F}^{2}+(1-\rho)\|{\mathbf{X}}-{\mathbf{X}}_{0}\|_{F}^{2}

(6a)

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{{\mathbf{X}}}}{\mathrm{subject\penalty\ to}}$}][c]{{\mathrm{subject\penalty\ to}}}\quad

\displaystyle|x_{i,j}|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{P_{\textrm{T}}}{N}},

(6b)

where
P_{\textrm{T}}
is the total transmit power,
{\mathbf{X}}_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
is the known benchmark radar signal matrix with constant modulus entries,
\rho\in[0,1]
is the weighting factor for the communications systems, and
x_{i,j}
is the (
i, j
)-th entry of
{\mathbf{X}}
.

The feasible region of the optimization problem (
6
) is a circle of radius
\sqrt{\frac{P_{\textrm{T}}}{N}}
for each
x_{i,j}
due to the constant-modulus constraint. Thus, the problem is non-convex and NP-hard. We next propose a deep unfolding waveform design based on a projected gradient descent approach.

III
Proposed Deep Unfolding Waveform Design

III-A

Problem Reformulation

We herein introduce the general idea to solve problem (
6
) based on the deep unfolding approach. To this end, we first rewrite the two terms in the objective function in (
6a
) as:

\|{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{X}}-\textbf{S}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}-{\mathbf{s}}_{i}\|^{2}

(7)

and

\|{\mathbf{X}}-{\mathbf{X}}_{0}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}-{\mathbf{x}}_{0i}\|^{2},

(8)

respectively. Here
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}
,
{\mathbf{s}}_{i}
and
{\mathbf{x}}_{0i}
are the
i
-th columns of
{\mathbf{X}}
,
S
and
{\mathbf{X}}_{0}
respectively. It is observed from (
7
) and (
8
) that the objective function is column-wise separable. Therefore, we consider an optimization problem which aims to optimize the columns of
{\mathbf{X}}
separately for the corresponding columns of
S
and
{\mathbf{X}}_{0}
, instead of (
6
). Furthermore, as the elements of
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}
and
{\mathbf{x}}_{0i}
are constrained to have a constant amplitude, we consider normalized vector variables,
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
and
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
such that
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}=\sqrt{\frac{P_{\textrm{T}}}{N}}{\mathbf{x}}
and
{\mathbf{x}}_{0i}=\sqrt{\frac{P_{\textrm{T}}}{N}}{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
. Here, we have also dropped the column index
i
for notational convenience. We reformulate the optimization problem (
6
) using these newly defined normalized vector variables as

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}}{\mathrm{subject\penalty\ to}}$}][l]{\underset{\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}}{\mathrm{minimize}}}\quad\rho\left\lVert\sqrt{\frac{P_{\textrm{T}}}{N}}{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{s}}\right\rVert^{2}+(1-\rho)\left\lVert\sqrt{\frac{P_{\textrm{T}}}{N}}({\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{x}}_{0})\right\rVert^{2}

(9a)

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{{\mathbf{x}}}}{\mathrm{subject\penalty\ to}}$}][c]{{\mathrm{subject\penalty\ to}}}\quad

\displaystyle|x(n)|

\displaystyle=1,\forall n,

(9b)

where
x(n)
denotes the
n
-th entry of
{\mathbf{x}}
.

Since problem (
6
) and (
9
) are equivalent in the sense that the latter is the column-wise reformulation of (
6
), we focus on (
9
) in the following analysis to develop the deep unfolding network.

Because neural networks generally work with real-valued data, we present the equivalent real-valued problem of (
9
) as follows:

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\bm{\bar{x}}}{\mathrm{subject\penalty\ to}}$}][l]{\underset{\displaystyle\bm{\bar{x}}}{\mathrm{minimize}}}\quad\rho\left\lVert\sqrt{\frac{P_{\textrm{T}}}{N}}\bm{\bar{H}\bar{x}}-\bm{\bar{s}}\right\rVert^{2}+(1-\rho)\left\lVert\sqrt{\frac{P_{\textrm{T}}}{N}}(\bm{\bar{x}}-\bm{\bar{x}}_{0})\right\rVert^{2},\hfil\hfil\displaystyle\displaystyle\hfil\hfil\displaystyle\displaystyle

(10)

where,
\bm{\bar{s}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}({\mathbf{s}})\\
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}({\mathbf{s}})\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}\ ^{{2K}},\bm{\bar{x}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}({\mathbf{x}})\\
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}({\mathbf{x}})\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}\ ^{{2N}},\bm{\bar{x}}_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0})\\
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0})\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}\ ^{{2N}},and\bm{\bar{H}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}({\mathbf{H}})&-\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}({\mathbf{H}})\\
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}({\mathbf{H}})&\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}({\mathbf{H}})\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}\ ^{{2K}\times{2N}}

In light of the projected gradient-descent method, the iterative update procedure of the waveform in the
p
-th iteration can be expressed as

\bm{\hat{x}}_{p}=\prod\left(\bm{\hat{x}}_{p-1}-\delta_{p-1}\frac{\partial f(\bm{\bar{x}})}{\partial\bm{\bar{x}}}\vline_{\bm{\bar{x}}=\bm{\hat{x}}_{p-1}}\right),

(11)

where
\bm{\hat{x}}_{p}
denotes the output vector of the neural network in the

\rho\left\lVert\sqrt{\frac{P_{\textrm{T}}}{N}}\bm{\bar{H}\bar{x}}-\bm{\bar{s}}\right\rVert^{2}+(1-\rho)\left\lVert\sqrt{\frac{P_{\textrm{T}}}{N}}(\bm{\bar{x}}-\bm{\bar{x}}_{0})\right\rVert^{2}
. The iterative update procedure in (

). As long as the DNN is well constructed and trained, it can generate the solution vector
\bm{\bar{x}}
of the problem (
10
) for the given weight factor

III-B

Deep Unfolding Model’s Structure and Training

From (
10
) and (
11
) we have

\bm{\hat{x}}_{p}=\prod\Bigg(\Bigg.\bm{\hat{x}}_{p-1}-\delta_{p-1}\Bigg(\Bigg.2\rho\frac{P_{\textrm{T}}}{N}\bm{\bar{H}}^{T}\bm{\bar{H}}\bm{\hat{x}}_{p-1}-2\rho\sqrt{\frac{P_{\textrm{T}}}{N}}\bm{\bar{H}}^{T}\bm{\bar{s}}\\
+2(1-\rho)\frac{P_{\textrm{T}}}{N}\bm{\hat{x}}_{p-1}-2(1-\rho)\frac{P_{\textrm{T}}}{N}\bm{\bar{x}}_{\textrm{0}}\Bigg.\Bigg)\Bigg.\Bigg).

(12)

Here,
\bm{\hat{x}}_{p}
is the estimated transmit signal vector in the
p
-th iteration. Equation (
12

\bm{\bar{x}_{0}}
,
\bm{\bar{H}}^{T}\bm{\bar{s}}
,
\bm{\bar{H}}^{T}\bm{\bar{H}}
and
\bm{\hat{x}}_{p-1}
. Therefore, these variables are used as inputs to the deep unfolding network. Fig.
2

\bm{\bar{x}_{0}}
,
\bm{\bar{H}}^{T}\bm{\bar{s}}
,
\bm{\bar{H}}^{T}\bm{\bar{H}}
and
\bm{\hat{x}}_{p-1}
and the vector output of the corresponding layer is
\bm{\hat{x}}_{p}
, the estimation of the unknown transmitted vector. The weight matrices for each input are denoted by
\bm{W}_{1}^{p}
,
\bm{W}_{2}^{p}
,
\bm{W}_{3}^{p}
and
\bm{W}_{4}^{p}
. The bias vectors for each input are denoted by
\bm{b}_{1}^{p}
,
\bm{b}_{2}^{p}
,
\bm{b}_{3}^{p}
and
\bm{b}_{4}^{p}
. By inspecting (

Therefore, the neural network has a sparsely connected structure, where the input and the output are connected element-wise in each layer. This helps to reduce the computational complexity significantly. Due to the sparse connections, the weight matrices become diagonal matrices. The activation function is denoted by
\psi(t)

\psi(t)=-1+2(\sigma(t+0.5)-\sigma(t-0.5)),

(13)

where

denotes the rectified linear unit (ReLU) function.
The output of the

-th layer is updated as follows:

\bm{m}_{1}^{p}=\bm{W}_{1}^{p}\bm{\bar{x}}_{0}+\bm{b}_{1}^{p},

(14)

\bm{m}_{2}^{p}=\bm{W}_{2}^{p}\bm{\bar{H}}^{T}\bm{\bar{s}}+\bm{b}_{2}^{p},

(15)

\bm{m}_{3}^{p}=\bm{W}_{3}^{p}\bm{\bar{H}}^{T}\bm{\bar{H}\hat{x}}_{p-1}+\bm{b}_{3}^{p},

(16)

\bm{m}_{4}^{p}=\bm{W}_{4}^{p}\bm{\hat{x}}_{p-1}+\bm{b}_{4}^{p},

(17)

\bm{s}_{p}=\bm{m}_{1}^{p}+\bm{m}_{2}^{p}+\bm{m}_{3}^{p}+\bm{m}_{4}^{p},

(18)

\bm{\hat{x}}_{p}=\psi(\bm{s}_{p}).

(19)

III-C

Computational Complexity

We first note that
\bm{\bar{H}}^{T}\bm{\bar{s}}
and
\bm{\bar{H}}^{T}\bm{\bar{H}}
are computed beforehand and fed to the network.
Since

) - (

FLOPs. The total arithmetic operations per layer will be

FLOPs. Hence, the computational complexity is

IV
Numerical Results

In this section, we present the numerical results to validate the proposed deep unfolding network. The total power budget is set to
P_{\textrm{T}}=30

dBm
and the channel is assumed to be flat Rayleigh fading. Moreover, the losses at the transmitter and receiver are assumed to be cancelled by the amplifier gains at the transmitter and receiver respectively. Each entry of the channel matrix
{\mathbf{H}}
is assumed to follow the standard complex Gaussian distribution. Moreover, we set the number of transmit antennas as
N
= 8 and assume that the ULA with half-wavelength spacing is deployed at the BS. The desired symbol matrix

is assumed to be populated with symbols from the unit-power QPSK alphabet. The length of the communication frame/radar pulse is set as

= 20. The number of layers for the neural network is set as

N_{0}
. The transmit SNR is defined as SNR =

The neural network is implemented in Python with the Tensorflow library. An initial learning rate of 0.0001 and a decaying factor of 0.97 are used. Adam optimizer is used to optimize the neural network. The input

V
Conclusions

In this paper, we proposed a neural network-based solution to the problem of waveform optimization in JCAS systems. Considering the results in section
IV
along with the complexity analysis in section
III-C
, it can be stated that our scheme gives almost the same performance as the BnB algorithm while experiencing reductions in both computational and time complexities. Simulation results validated the claim that our neural network performs as same as the BnB.

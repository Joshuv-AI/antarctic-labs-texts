Attaining Sudan’s decoding radius with no genus penalty for algebraic geometry codes
Isabella Panaccione

Abstract
In this paper we present a decoding algorithm for algebraic geometry codes with error–correcting capacity beyond half the designed distance of the code. This algorithm comes as a fusion of the Power Error Locating Pairs algorithm for algebraic geometry codes and the technique used by Ehrhard in order to correct these codes up to half the designed distance. The decoding radius of this algorithm reaches that of Sudan algorithm, without any penalty given by the genus of the curve.



Key words :
Error correcting codes; algebraic geometry codes; decoding algorithms; error correcting pairs; Sudan algorithm; genus.

On the decoding radius of Sudan algorithm

This section mainly comes from the ideas of Peter Beelen and shows how to get an improved decoding radius for Sudan algorithm with respect to
[
BH08
, §
2.6
]
. This improvement mainly consists in analysing the parameters of the linear system to get Sudan polynomial
Q
. Given a curve of genus
g
, we consider a code
C=\mathcal{C}_{L}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{P},G)
, where
G
is a divisor with
2g-2<\deg G<n
and
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\}
. Let us suppose a vector
\boldsymbol{y}=\boldsymbol{c}+\boldsymbol{e}
is given, where
\w(\boldsymbol{e})=t
and there exists
f\in L(G)
such that

\boldsymbol{c}=(f(P_{1}),\dots,f(P_{n})).

(19)

We denote by
I
the support of the error vector
I=\supp(\boldsymbol{e})
(in particular
|I|=t
). Let
F
be a divisor with
\deg F=n-t-1
.

Original problem (Sudan):

given
\ell\geq 1
, find a polynomial
Q(\boldsymbol{x},y)=Q_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})+Q_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})y+\dots+Q_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{x})y^{\ell}
such that

(

)

Q_{i}(\boldsymbol{x})\in L(F-iG)
for all
i=0,\dots,\ell

(
ii
)

Q(P_{j},y_{j})=0
for all
j=1,\dots,n
.

This problem can be solved with a linear system of
n
equations in
\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\ell(F-iG)

t\leq\frac{2n\ell-\ell(\ell+1)\deg(G)-2}{2(\ell+1)}-g.

(20)

Now we want to show that this decoding radius can be actually optimised. To do so, we consider the following problem.

Modified problem (Sudan):

Given
f
as in (

), find a polynomial

Q(\boldsymbol{x},y)=(y-f(\boldsymbol{x}))(\tilde{Q}_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})+\tilde{Q}_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})y+\dots+\tilde{Q}_{\ell-1}(\boldsymbol{x})y^{\ell-1}),

such that, if we denote by

(

(

t

Proposition B.1

[
Ehr92
, Proposition 1]
(Function version)
If
\deg F+t<d^{*}

L(F-D_{\boldsymbol{e}})=S(F)

Proof.

We recall that we consider here the
S(F)
defined in (
). We already know that
L(F-D_{\boldsymbol{e}})\subseteq S(F)
. Hence we consider now
\Lambda\in S(F)
and we want to show that
\Lambda\in L(F-D_{\boldsymbol{e}})
. In order to do so, we first prove that
\Lambda f_{\boldsymbol{e}}\in L(F+G^{\prime}-D)
. Since
\Lambda\in S(F)
, there exist
g\in L(F+G)
and
h\in L(F+G^{\prime}-D)
such that
\Lambda f_{\boldsymbol{y}}=g+h
. Furthermore
f_{\boldsymbol{y}}=f_{\boldsymbol{c}}+f_{\boldsymbol{e}}
, hence

\Lambda f_{\boldsymbol{c}}-g=h-\Lambda f_{\boldsymbol{e}}.

By way of contradiction let us suppose that
h\neq\Lambda f_{\boldsymbol{e}}
. Since
f_{\boldsymbol{e}}\in L(G^{\prime}-D+D_{\boldsymbol{e}})
, we have

\displaystyle(h-\Lambda f_{\boldsymbol{e}})\geq

\displaystyle\min(-F-G^{\prime}+D,-F-G^{\prime}+D-D_{\boldsymbol{e}})=-F-G^{\prime}+D-D_{\boldsymbol{e}}

\displaystyle(\Lambda f_{\boldsymbol{c}}-g)\geq

\displaystyle\min(-F-G,-F-G)=-F-G.

Hence, in particular

(h-\Lambda f_{\boldsymbol{e}})\geq\max(-F-G^{\prime}+D-D_{\boldsymbol{e}},-F-G)=-F-G+D-D_{\boldsymbol{e}},

that is
h-\Lambda f_{\boldsymbol{e}}\in L(F+G-D+D_{\boldsymbol{e}})
. Though, by hypothesis we have

\deg(F+G-D+D_{\boldsymbol{e}})=\deg F+\deg G-n+t<0,

that is
L(F+G-D+D_{\boldsymbol{e}})=\{0\}
, which is a contradiction since we supposed
h\neq\Lambda f_{\boldsymbol{e}}
. Now we know that
\Lambda f_{\boldsymbol{e}}\in L(F+G^{\prime}-D)
, we can conclude the proof. First, since
\ev_{\mathcal{P}}(f_{\boldsymbol{e}})=\boldsymbol{e}
, we observe that if
P\in\supp(D_{\boldsymbol{e}})
, then

f_{\boldsymbol{e}}\in L(G^{\prime}-D+D_{\boldsymbol{e}})\setminus L(G^{\prime}-D+D_{\boldsymbol{e}}-P).

In particular, for any

, since
\Lambda f_{\boldsymbol{e}}\in L(F+G^{\prime}-D)
and
v_{P}(f_{\boldsymbol{e}})=0
, we get

v_{P}(\Lambda)=v_{P}(\Lambda)+v_{P}(f_{\boldsymbol{e}})=v_{P}(\Lambda f_{\boldsymbol{e}})\geq-v_{P}(F)+1,

that is
\Lambda\in L(F-D_{\boldsymbol{e}})
.

.

Proposition B.3
.

Let us consider
G^{\prime}
as in §

G^{\prime}\geq G
and
L(W+D-G^{\prime})=\{0\}
and let
\varphi
be the map
\varphi:L(F+2G^{\prime})\longrightarrow\Omega(F+2G-D)^{\vee}

This proof is an adaptation of the proof of Remark
given in
[

. It is composed by the following steps:

(1)

\varphi

(2)

\tilde{\varphi}:L(F+2G^{\prime})\rightarrow(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\vee}

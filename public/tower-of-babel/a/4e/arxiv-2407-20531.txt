Low regularity solutions for the Cauchy problem of the ideal incompressible Magnetohydrodynamics equations
Huali Zhang

Abstract
. In Lagrangian coordinates, the local well-posedness of low regularity solutions is established for an ideal incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) system subject to a homogeneous background magnetic field. First, the MHD system is reformulated into a degenerate wave-elliptic system with a particular null structure. By introducing a suitably defined solution space, several refined product estimates are derived. Next, using the inherent null structure, a Klainerman-Machedon type bilinear estimate is obtained for the nonlinear terms. These nice structures and estimates yield the local well-posedness of the ideal incompressible MHD equations in Lagrangian coordinates for initial velocity fields 𝐯 0 ∈ H s ​ ( ℝ n ) \mathbf{v}_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) with s > n + 1 2 s>\frac{n+1}{2} ( n = 2 , 3 , 4 ) (n=2,3,4) . Remarkably, the regularity requirement is lowered by half a derivative compared with the classical exponent s > n 2 + 1 s>\frac{n}{2}+1 .

1.
Introduction

Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) studies the dynamic interplay between velocity and magnetic fields within electrically conducting fluids, including plasmas, liquid metals, seawater, and electrolytes. This paper focuses on establishing the well-posedness of low regularity solutions to the Cauchy problem for the ideal MHD system expressed in Lagrangian coordinates. To introduce the problem, we first present the incompressible MHD system in its Eulerian formulation, given by

1.2.
Motivation

Despite recent advances in higher regularity settings and related free boundary problem, the Cauchy problem for the ideal MHD system remains unsolved at low regularity.

The basic energy estimates of system (
1.1
) gives

\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{b})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s}}\lesssim\|(\mathbf{v}_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0})\|_{H^{s}}\exp\left(\int^{t}_{0}\|(\nabla\mathbf{v},\nabla\mathbf{b})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}d\tau\right).

Therefore, existing well-posedness results
[
10
,
32
,
37
,
38
]
rely on the Sobolev imbedding
H^{\frac{n}{2}+}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
, which permits the estimate

\|(\nabla\mathbf{v},\nabla\mathbf{b})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{b})\|_{H^{s}},\quad s>\frac{n}{2}+1.

However, for regularity indices
s\leq\frac{n}{2}+1
, the quantities
\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}
and
\|\nabla\mathbf{b}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}
can no longer be bounded by the
H^{s}
-norm of the solution. This loss of pointwise control over gradients introduces fundamental analytical difficulties in closing energy estimates, leaving the well-posedness theory in this range an open problem.

From
[
3
,
6
,
17
]
, it is known that the ideal MHD system (
1.1
) can be reformulated as a one-dimensional quasilinear wave system in Eulerian coordinates. In the context of low regularity solutions for quasilinear wave equations, Strichartz estimates are a fundamental tool (see the references
[
24
]
and
[
36
]
for details). A natural approach would be to bound a quantity such as
\|(\nabla\mathbf{v},\nabla\mathbf{b})\|_{L_{t}^{1}L^{\infty}_{x}}
, which has been successfully carried out for related systems such as the incompressible neo-Hookean elastic equations
[
1
,
44
]
. To the best of our knowledge, no Strichartz estimates are available for the one-dimensional wave equation, which creates a major trouble for studying low regularity solutions of (
1.1
) in Eulerian coordinates.

In our paper, a crucial observation is that when the ideal MHD system (
1.1
) is expressed in Lagrangian coordinates, it reduces to a one-dimensional semilinear wave system with a specific null structure–precisely, equations (
1.5
)–(
1.7
). In this reformulation, the principal difficulty in the Eulerian setting–the need to control
\|(\nabla\mathbf{v},\nabla\mathbf{b})\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}
is circumvented. Indeed, we introduce a carefully chosen solution space
H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}
(see (
1.10
) and (
1.11
) below), defined through a norm that respects both the one-dimensional wave operator and the null structure of the nonlinearities. Using the reformulated system (
1.5
)–(
1.7
) and the variable substitution
2
2

2

The matrix
\mathbf{H}=(\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}}{\partial\mathbf{y}})_{n\times n}
, and
\mathbf{E}
is an identity matrix

\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{E}

\begin{split}|\mathbf{G}|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+T^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(1+|\mathbf{G}|^{n^{2}+3n+1}_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}})\|Q_{0}(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G})\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{0}},\end{split}

\|Q_{0}(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G})\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{0}}\lesssim|\mathbf{G}|^{2}_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}.

The above nice structure and estimates strongly motivate the study of low regularity well-posedness for the ideal incompressible MHD system in Lagrangian coordinates. Specifically, we establish the local well-posedness of low regularity solutions to (
1.5
)–(
1.7
) under the assumptions
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
s>\frac{n+1}{2}
, for
n=2,3,4
, and
\mathbf{b}_{0}
being a nonzero constant vector. This provides the first low-regularity well-posedness result for the reduced Lagrangian system (
1.5
)–(
1.7
) in this regularity regime below the classical exponent
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
, and serves as a key step toward identifying the sharp regularity required for the Cauchy problem. Our main results are stated as follows.

1.3.
Statement of the result

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\frac{n+1}{2}<s\leq\frac{n}{2}+1
and
{2\leq n\leq 4}
. Suppose that
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and that
\mathbf{b}_{0}
is a nonzero constant vector. Then there exists
T>0
depending only on
n,s,|\mathbf{b}_{0}|
, and
\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{H^{s}}
such that the system (
1.5
)–(
1.7
) admits a unique solution with

\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{E}\in C([0,T],H^{s-1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap C^{1}([0,T],H^{s-1,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),

where
\mathbf{E}
is a
n\times n
identity matrix. Moreover, the solution map is continuous from
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to

C([0,T];H^{s-1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap C^{1}([0,T];H^{s-1,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

Remark 1.1
.

(1)

Compared to the classical requirement
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
established by Schmidt
[
37
]
and Secchi
[
38
]
, Theorem
1.1
lowers the regularity demand on the initial velocity by half a derivative. It is also interesting to compare our result with the incompressible Euler equations, for which Bourgain and Li
[
4
,
5
]
showed that the condition
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
(
n=2,3
) is in fact sharp.

(2)

For the free boundary problem of the incompressible ideal MHD system, well-posedness holds when the regularity index satisfies
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
, as shown in
[
18
]
. By contrast, for the Cauchy problem, the regularity threshold can be lowered by establishing bilinear estimates on the solutions. Such improvements may not be obtained for free boundary problems.

Remark 1.2
.

(1)

Our work is further motivated by the studies of Andersson-Kapitanski
[
1
]
and Zhang
[
44
]
on the incompressible neo-Hookean elastic equations. We observe that the solution spaces introduced in
[
1
,
44
]
are not directly applicable to the wave component in (
1.5
)–(
1.7
), since the latter involves a degenerate one-dimensional operator. To our knowledge, no Strichartz estimates are available for one-dimensional wave equations. Consequently, if we wish to lower the regularity requirements on the initial data, the problem should be approached through bilinear estimates rather than Strichartz estimates.

(2)

The first key challenge is therefore to define a new solution space that is adapted to the structure of system (
1.5
). A second essential point is to verify that, within this new function framework, the relevant product estimates and bilinear estimates for the null form can be established. This strategy draws inspiration from the works of Klainerman and Machedon
[
23
]
, Foschi and Klainerman
[
16
]
, Klainerman and Selberg
[
25
]
on nonlinear wave equations.

Remark 1.3
.

(1)

The assumption in Theorem
1.1
that
\mathbf{b}_{0}
is a constant vector plays a key role, since the proof relies on the solution representation for linear wave equations with constant coefficients as well as the Fourier transform analysis of the associated null form. For details we refer to Proposition
3.4
and Lemma
4.1
. A natural follow-up question is whether low regularity well-posedness remains valid when
\mathbf{b}_{0}
is asymptotically flat or is a small perturbation of a nonzero constant vector.

(2)

In Section
5
, we require the transformation between norms in Eulerian and Lagrangian coordinates. By Lemma
3.3
, the regularity exponent
s
must satisfy either
s-1\in(0,1]
or
s-2\in(0,1]
. Because we require
s>\frac{n+1}{2}
, this forces the spatial dimension to satisfy
2\leq n\leq 4
.

Remark 1.4
.

It is important to note that Theorem
1.1
holds only in Lagrangian coordinates. Moreover, it is not clear whether this low-regularity Lagrangian solution is equivalent to an Eulerian solution in the corresponding Sobolev spaces. Consequently, Theorem
1.1
does not automatically imply local well-posedness for (
1.1
) in
H^{s}
space.

(\tau-\lambda)\lambda-(\xi_{1}-\eta_{1})\eta_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\tau^{2}-\xi_{1}^{2}-(\tau-\lambda)^{2}+(\xi_{1}-\eta_{1})^{2}-\lambda^{2}+\eta_{1}^{2}\right\}.

Then, we deduce that

\begin{split}|q_{0}(\tau-\lambda,\xi-\eta,\lambda,\eta)|\lesssim&\left<|\tau|-|\xi_{1}|\right>\left<|\tau|+|\xi_{1}|\right>+\left<|\tau-\lambda|+|\xi_{1}-\eta_{1}|\right>\left<|\tau-\lambda|-|\xi_{1}-\eta_{1}|\right>\\
&\ +\left<|\lambda|+|\eta_{1}|\right>\left<|\lambda|-|\eta_{1}|\right>.\end{split}

(4.3)

On the other hand, we also have the following inequality:

\begin{split}|q_{0}(\tau-\lambda,\xi-\eta,\lambda,\eta)|\lesssim&\left<|\tau-\lambda|+|\xi_{1}-\eta_{1}|\right>\left<|\lambda|+|\eta_{1}|\right>.\end{split}

(4.4)

Applying the interpolation formula to (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) yields

\begin{split}&|q_{0}(\tau-\lambda,\xi-\eta,\lambda,\eta)|\\
\lesssim&\left<|\tau-\lambda|+|\xi_{1}-\eta_{1}|\right>^{\varepsilon}\left<|\lambda|+|\eta_{1}|\right>^{\varepsilon}\cdot\big[\left<|\tau|+|\xi_{1}|\right>^{1-\varepsilon}\left<|\tau|-|\xi_{1}|\right>^{1-\varepsilon}\\
&+\left<|\tau-\lambda|+|\xi_{1}-\eta_{1}|\right>^{1-\varepsilon}\left<|\tau-\lambda|-|\xi_{1}-\eta_{1}|\right>^{1-\varepsilon}+\left<|\lambda|+|\eta_{1}|\right>^{1-\varepsilon}\left<|\lambda|-|\eta_{1}|\right>^{1-\varepsilon}\big]\\
=&\left<|\tau|+|\xi_{1}|\right>^{1-\varepsilon}\left<|\tau|-|\xi_{1}|\right>^{1-\varepsilon}\left<|\tau-\lambda|+|\xi_{1}-\eta_{1}|\right>^{\varepsilon}\left<|\lambda|+|\eta_{1}|\right>^{\varepsilon}\\
&+\left<|\tau-\lambda|+|\xi_{1}-\eta_{1}|\right>\left<|\tau-\lambda|-|\xi_{1}-\eta_{1}|\right>^{1-\varepsilon}\left<|\lambda|+|\eta_{1}|\right>^{\varepsilon}\\
&+\left<|\tau-\lambda|+|\xi_{1}-\eta_{1}|\right>^{\varepsilon}\left<|\lambda|+|\eta_{1}|\right>\left<|\lambda|-|\eta_{1}|\right>^{1-\varepsilon},\end{split}

(4.5)

where we take
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
. By using (
4.2
) and (
4.5
), we obtain

|Q_{0}(f,g)|\lesssim|\Lambda_{+}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\big(\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}f\cdot\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}g\big)|+|\Lambda_{+}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}f\cdot\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}g|+|\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}f\cdot\Lambda_{+}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}g|.

Note

\begin{split}\Lambda_{+}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\big(\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}f\cdot\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}g\big)\approx&\Lambda_{+}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}f\cdot\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}g+\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}f\cdot\Lambda_{+}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}g\\
&+\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}f\cdot\Lambda_{+}g+\Lambda_{+}f\cdot\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}g.\end{split}

This implies

\begin{split}\|Q_{0}(f,g)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\lesssim&\|\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}f\cdot\Lambda_{+}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}+\|\Lambda_{+}f\cdot\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\\
&+\|\Lambda_{+}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}f\cdot\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}+\|\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}f\cdot\Lambda_{+}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}.\end{split}

(4.6)

Take
\theta=1-\varepsilon
. Then we have
\theta\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
. Note that
s>\frac{n+1}{2}
. By Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev embeddings, it follows that

\begin{split}\|\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}f\cdot\Lambda_{+}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\lesssim&\|\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\Lambda_{+}g\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
\lesssim&\|\Lambda_{+}^{1-\varepsilon}\left(\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}f\right)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda^{s-1}\left(\Lambda_{+}g\right)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
=&\|\Lambda_{+}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\|\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda^{s-1}\Lambda_{+}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\\
\lesssim&|f|_{H^{0,1}_{\theta}}|g|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}.\end{split}

(4.7)

Similarly, using Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev embeddings again, we obtain

\begin{split}\|\Lambda_{+}f\cdot\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\lesssim&\|\Lambda_{+}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}g\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
\lesssim&\|\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{+}f\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\Lambda_{+}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda^{s-1}\left(\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}g\right)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
=&\|\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{+}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\|\Lambda_{+}\Lambda^{s-1}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\\
\lesssim&|f|_{H^{0,1}_{\theta}}|g|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}},\end{split}

(4.8)

and

\begin{split}\|\Lambda_{+}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}f\cdot\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\lesssim&\|\Lambda_{+}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\|\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\\
\lesssim&\|\Lambda_{+}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\|\Lambda^{s-1}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{+}^{1-\varepsilon}\left(\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}g\right)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\\
\lesssim&|f|_{H^{0,1}_{\theta}}|g|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}.\end{split}

(4.9)

In a similar way, we also obtain

\begin{split}\|\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}f\cdot\Lambda_{+}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\lesssim&\|\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,y_{1}}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\|\Lambda_{+}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}g\|_{L^{2}_{t,y_{1}}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\\
\lesssim&\|\Lambda_{+}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}\Lambda_{+}^{\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\|\Lambda^{s-1}\left(\Lambda_{+}\Lambda_{-}^{1-\varepsilon}g\right)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\\
\lesssim&|f|_{H^{0,1}_{\theta}}|g|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}.\end{split}

(4.10)

Submitting (
4.7
), (
4.8
), (
4.9
), and (
4.10
) to (
4.6
), we have

\begin{split}\|Q_{0}(f,g)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\lesssim&|f|_{H^{0,1}_{\theta}}|g|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}.\end{split}

This implies that

\begin{split}\|Q_{0}(f,g)\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{0}}\lesssim&|f|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}|g|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}.\end{split}

Therefore, the estimate (
4.1
) holds, and we have completed the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
by means of the contraction mapping principle. Since the unknown in (

)–(
1.7
) is the matrix-valued function
\mathbf{H}
, it is convenient to introduce
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{E}
, where
\mathbf{E}
denotes the
n\times n
identity matrix. For simplicity of notation, we assume
\mathbf{b}_{0}=(1,0,\cdots,0)^{\mathrm{T}}=e_{1}
. Indeed, for any nonzero constant vector
\mathbf{b}_{0}
, an orthogonal change of spatial coordinates transforms
\mathbf{b}_{0}
into
|\mathbf{b}_{0}|e_{1}
, and a suitable rescaling of time converts the operator
\partial_{t}^{2}-(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\nabla_{y})^{2}
into
\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}
. Hence the same argument applies in general, with the existence time depending on
|\mathbf{b}_{0}|
.

From (
1.5
)–(
1.7
) we then obtain the following system for
\mathbf{G}
:

\begin{cases}&\widetilde{\square}G^{ia}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x^{i}}),\\
&\Delta_{x}{q}=(\mathbf{H}^{-1})^{kl}(\mathbf{H}^{-1})^{mj}Q_{0}({H}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}jk}},{H}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}lm}}),\\
&(G^{ia},\frac{\partial G^{ia}}{\partial t})|_{t=0}=(0,\frac{\partial v_{0}^{i}}{\partial y^{a}}).\end{cases}

(5.1)

where
\mathbf{H}^{-1}=(\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{E})^{-1}
satisfies (
1.7
). Let
s
be as stated in Theorem
1.1
, and let the two positive constants be

\theta=\frac{3}{4},\ \varepsilon=\frac{1}{4}.

Define a solution space

X_{s,\theta}=\left\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n}):|u|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\leq C_{1}\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{H^{s}}\right\},

(5.2)

where
C_{1}
will be defined later. For
\mathbf{G}\in X_{s,\theta}
, we define the map
M
as follows:

\begin{split}\text{M}G^{ia}=&\chi(t)\left\{D^{-1}\sin(tD)(\frac{\partial v^{i}_{0}}{\partial y^{a}})\right\}+{\widetilde{\square}^{-1}}(1-\phi(T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda_{-}))\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}})\\
&+\chi(\frac{t}{T})\int^{t}_{0}D^{-1}\sin((t-t^{\prime})D)\left\{\phi(T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda_{-})\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}})\right\}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},\end{split}

(5.3)

where
q
satisfies (please refer (
1.6
))

\Delta_{x}q=(\mathbf{H}^{-1})^{kl}(\mathbf{H}^{-1})^{mj}Q_{0}({H}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}jk}},{H}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}lm}}).

(5.4)

Substituting
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{E}
into (
5.4
) yields

\Delta_{x}q=((\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{E})^{-1})^{kl}((\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{E})^{-1})^{mj}Q_{0}({G}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}jk}},{G}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}lm}}).

(5.5)

In (
5.3
), we can also calculate that
\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}})=\frac{\partial x^{j}}{\partial y^{a}}\frac{\partial^{2}q}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}=(\delta^{ja}+G^{ja})(-\Delta_{x})^{-1}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\Delta_{x}q

\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}})=(\delta^{ja}+G^{ja})(-\Delta_{x})^{-1}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\left\{((\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{E})^{-1})^{kl}((\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{E})^{-1})^{mj}Q_{0}(G^{jk},G^{lm})\right\}.

(5.6)

Combining (
5.3
) and (
5.6
), the map
M
depends on
\mathbf{G}
explicitly. By (
5.3
), we can calculate out

\begin{cases}&\widetilde{\square}\text{M}G^{ia}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x^{i}}),\quad\quad[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
&(\text{M}G^{ia},\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\text{M}G^{ia})|_{t=0}=(0,\frac{\partial v^{i}_{0}}{\partial y^{a}}).\end{cases}

Using Proposition
3.4
, we derive

\begin{split}|\text{M}\mathbf{G}|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\leq&C(\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{0}}{\partial y}\|_{H^{s-1}}+T^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\|\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x})\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{\theta+\varepsilon-1}}).\end{split}

(5.7)

Thanks to

, we get

\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(\frac{\partial q}{\partial x})=\mathbf{H}\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}q}{\partial x^{2}}=(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{G})\frac{\partial^{2}q}{\partial x^{2}}.

(5.8)

Substituting (
5.8

\begin{split}|\text{M}\mathbf{G}|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\leq&C\left\{\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+T^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\|\frac{\partial^{2}q}{\partial^{2}x}\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{\theta+\varepsilon-1}}+\|\mathbf{G}\frac{\partial^{2}q}{\partial^{2}x}\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{\theta+\varepsilon-1}})\right\}.\end{split}

For

\begin{split}|\text{M}\mathbf{G}|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\leq&C\left\{\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+T^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(1+|\mathbf{G}|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}})\|\frac{\partial^{2}q}{\partial^{2}x}\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{\theta+\varepsilon-1}}\right\}.\end{split}

(5.9)

For

(5.10)

Considering

\begin{split}\|\frac{\partial^{2}q}{\partial^{2}x}\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{y})}\leq&C(1+\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}}{\partial\mathbf{y}}\|^{\frac{n}{2}+s}_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{y})}})\|\frac{\partial^{2}\bar{q}}{\partial^{2}x}\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x})}\\
\leq&C(1+\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}}{\partial\mathbf{y}}\|^{\frac{n}{2}+s}_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{y})}})\|\Delta_{x}\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x})}\\

\leq&C(1+|\mathbf{G}|^{\frac{n}{2}+s}_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}})(1+|\mathbf{G}|^{2(n-1)}_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}})\|Q_{0}(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G})\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{0}}(1+|\mathbf{G}|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}\times}\mathbb{R}^{n}_{y})}^{n-1})^{\frac{n}{2}+s}\\
\leq&C(1+|\mathbf{G}|^{n^{2}+3n}_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}})\|Q_{0}(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G})\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{0}}.\end{split}

(5.11)

Case 2

. In this case,
s-2\in(0,1]
, using Lemma
3.3
again, we obtain

\begin{split}\|\frac{\partial^{2}q}{\partial^{2}x}\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{y})}=&\|\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(\frac{\partial^{2}q}{\partial^{2}x})\cdot(\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{E})\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{y})}+\|\frac{\partial^{2}q}{\partial^{2}x}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{y})}\\
\leq&\|\frac{\partial^{3}q}{\partial^{3}x}\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{y})}(1+|\mathbf{G}|^{2}_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}})+\|\frac{\partial^{2}q}{\partial^{2}x}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{y})}.\end{split}

For the term

\begin{split}\|\frac{\partial^{3}q}{\partial^{3}x}\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{y})}\leq C\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}}{\partial\mathbf{y}}\|^{\frac{n}{2}+s-2}_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{y})}}\|\frac{\partial^{3}\bar{q}}{\partial^{3}x}\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x})},\end{split}

and

(5.13)

By (

Due to (

Therefore,

is a contraction mapping in
X_{s,\theta}

5.1
). To prove the continuous dependence, let

\begin{cases}&\widetilde{\square}G_{2}^{ia}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}(\frac{\partial p_{2}}{\partial x^{i}}),\\

&(G_{2}^{ia},\frac{\partial G_{2}^{ia}}{\partial t})|_{t=0}=(0,\frac{\partial v_{02}^{i}}{\partial y^{a}}),\end{cases}

where

satisfy

\begin{split}(\mathbf{F}_{1}^{-1})^{ia}=&\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\epsilon_{ii_{2}i_{3}\cdots i_{n}}\epsilon^{aa_{2}a_{3}\cdots a_{n}}(\delta^{i_{2}a_{2}}+G_{1}^{i_{2}a_{2}})\cdots(\delta^{i_{n}a_{n}}+G_{1}^{i_{n}a_{n}}),\\
(\mathbf{F}_{2}^{-1})^{ia}=&\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\epsilon_{ii_{2}i_{3}\cdots i_{n}}\epsilon^{aa_{2}a_{3}\cdots a_{n}}(\delta^{i_{2}a_{2}}+G_{2}^{i_{2}a_{2}})\cdots(\delta^{i_{n}a_{n}}+G_{2}^{i_{n}a_{n}}).\end{split}

Using Proposition
3.4
, we can prove

\begin{split}&|\mathbf{G}_{2}-\mathbf{G}_{1}|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\\
\leq C&\|\mathbf{v}_{02}-\mathbf{v}_{01}\|_{H^{s}}+CT^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}|\mathbf{G}_{2}-\mathbf{G}_{1}|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\left(1+|\mathbf{G}_{1}|^{n^{2}+3n+2}_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}+|\mathbf{G}_{2}|^{n^{2}+3n+2}_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\right).\end{split}

(5.24)

By (
5.24
), for
\mathbf{G}_{1},\mathbf{G}_{2}\in X_{s,\theta}
, if
T
is sufficiently small, then we can get

\begin{split}|\mathbf{G}_{2}-\mathbf{G}_{1}|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\leq C\|\mathbf{v}_{02}-\mathbf{v}_{01}\|_{H^{s}}.\end{split}

(5.25)

Using Lemma
3.1
and estimate (
5.25
) yield

\begin{split}\|\mathbf{G}_{2}-\mathbf{G}_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{[0,T]}H^{s-1,1}}+\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\mathbf{G}_{2}-\mathbf{G}_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}_{[0,T]}H^{s-1}}\leq C\|\mathbf{v}_{02}-\mathbf{v}_{01}\|_{H^{s}}.\end{split}

Therefore, we have proved the continuous dependence of the solutions on the initial data. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In this section, we present a proof of Proposition

6.1.
The estimates for
\chi(t)u_{0}

By (

), we recall
\chi(t)u_{0}=\chi(t)\cos(tD)f+\chi(t)D^{-1}\sin(tD)g
. Let us introduce the following lemma:

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\theta>\frac{1}{2}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
and
(f,g)\in H^{s-1,1}\times H^{s-1,0}
. Then the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\|\chi(t)\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}tD}f\|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\chi\|_{H^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})}\|f\|_{H^{s-1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(6.1)

\displaystyle\|\chi(t)\cos(tD)f\|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\chi\|_{H^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})}\|f\|_{H^{s-1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(6.2)

If
r\in[-1,1]
, and
\mathrm{supp}\widehat{f}\subseteq\{\xi:|\xi_{1}|<m\}
(
m>0
), then we also have

\|\chi(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}rtD}f\|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\lesssim(m^{\theta}\|\chi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\chi\|_{H^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})})\|f\|_{H^{s-1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(6.3)

Moreover, we get

\displaystyle\|\chi(t)D^{-1}\sin(tD)g\|_{H_{\theta}^{s-1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\lesssim

\displaystyle(\|\chi\|_{H^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})}+\|t\chi\|_{H^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})})\|g\|_{H^{s-1,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(6.4)

Proof.

Since the space-time Fourier transform of
\chi(t)\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}tD}f
is
\widehat{\chi}(\tau\mp|\xi_{1}|)\widehat{f}(\xi)
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\|\widehat{\chi}(\tau\mp|\xi_{1}|)\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}\\
=&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1+n}}\left<\xi\right>^{2(s-1)}\left<\xi_{1}\right>^{2}\left<|\tau|-|\xi_{1}|\right>^{2\theta}|\widehat{\chi}(\tau\mp|\xi_{1}|)\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\tau d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
=&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left<\xi\right>^{2(s-1)}\left<\xi_{1}\right>^{2}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left<|\tau|-|\xi_{1}|\right>^{2\theta}|\widehat{\chi}(\tau\mp|\xi_{1}|)|^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\lesssim&\|\chi\|_{H^{\theta}}\|f\|_{H^{s-1,1}}.\end{split}

(6.5)

Thus, the estimate (
6.1
) is established. By using the identity
\cos(tD)f=\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tD}f+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tD}f)
and (
6.5
), we can directly obtain (
6.2
). Note that the space-time Fourier transform of
\chi(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}rtD}{f}
equals

\widehat{\chi}(\tau-r|\xi_{1}|)\widehat{{f}}(\xi).

When
\xi\in\mathrm{supp}\widehat{f}
and
r\in[-1,1]
, it yields

||\tau|-|\xi_{1}||\leq|\tau-r|\xi_{1}||+(1-|r|)|\xi_{1}|\leq|\tau-r|\xi_{1}||+m.

Through calculations, we can deduce that

\begin{split}\|\chi(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}rtD}f\|^{2}_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{1+n})}=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1+n}}\left<|\tau|-|\xi_{1}|\right>^{2\theta}|\widehat{\chi}(\tau-r|\xi_{1}|)|^{2}\left<\xi_{1}\right>^{2}\left<\xi\right>^{2(s-1)}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\tau d\xi\\
\lesssim&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1+n}}\left<|\tau|-|\xi_{1}|\right>^{2\theta}|\widehat{\chi}(\tau-r|\xi_{1}|)|^{2}\left<\xi_{1}\right>^{2}\left<\xi\right>^{2(s-1)}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\tau d\xi\\
\lesssim&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1+n}}(\left<\tau-r|\xi_{1}|\right>^{2\theta}+m^{2\theta})|\widehat{\chi}(\tau-r|\xi_{1}|)|^{2}\left<\xi_{1}\right>^{2}\left<\xi\right>^{2(s-1)}|\widehat{{f}}(\xi)|^{2}d\tau d\xi\\
\lesssim&(\|\chi\|^{2}_{H^{\theta}}+m^{2\theta}\|\chi\|^{2}_{L^{2}})\|f\|^{2}_{H^{s-1,1}}.\end{split}

Thus, we have proved (
6.3
). It remains for us to prove (
6.4
). Give a decomposition

g=g_{1}+g_{2},\quad\textrm{supp}\ \widehat{g}_{1}\subseteq\{\xi:|\xi_{1}|<1\},\ \textrm{and}\ \textrm{supp}\ \widehat{g}_{2}\subseteq\{\xi:|\xi_{1}|\geq 1\}.

We rewrite
\chi(t)D^{-1}\sin(tD)g_{1}
as

\chi(t)D^{-1}\sin(tD)g_{1}=\int^{1}_{0}t\chi(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(2r-1)tD}g_{1}dr,\quad\sup\widehat{g}_{1}\subseteq\{\xi:|\xi_{1}|<1\}.

Due to (
6.3
), we can see

\|\chi(t)D^{-1}\sin(tD)g_{1}\|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\lesssim\|t\chi\|_{H^{\theta}}\|g_{1}\|_{H^{s-1,0}}.

(6.6)

Noting that
\widehat{g}_{2}\subseteq\{\xi:|\xi_{1}|\geq 1\}
, we therefore obtain

\begin{split}\|\chi(t)D^{-1}\sin(tD)g_{2}\|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}=&\|\chi(t)\sin(tD)(D^{-1}g_{2})\|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\\
\lesssim&\|\chi\|_{H^{\theta}}\|D^{-1}g_{2}\|_{H^{s-1,1}}\\
\lesssim&\|\chi\|_{H^{\theta}}\|g_{2}\|_{H^{s-1,0}}.\end{split}

(6.7)

Combining (
6.6
) with (
6.7
) shows that (
6.4
) holds. Therefore, the proof of Lemma
6.1
is complete.
∎

||\tau|-|\xi_{1}||\leq 2\sqrt{2}T^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad\mathrm{for}\ (\tau,\xi)\in\mathrm{supp}\widetilde{F_{1}}.

(6.59)

Substituting (
6.59
) into (
6.12
), we can choose

c_{0}=2+2\sqrt{2}T^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(6.60)

By (
6.36
) in Lemma
6.4
, it follows that

\begin{split}|\chi_{T}u_{1}|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\leq\bar{C}_{T}\|F_{1}\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{0}},\end{split}

(6.61)

where

\begin{split}\bar{C}_{T}\simeq\ &(3c_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|\chi_{T}\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta-1}}+\|t\chi_{T}\|_{H^{\theta}}+\|t\chi_{T}^{\prime}\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta-1}}+\|t^{2}\chi_{T}^{\prime}\|_{H^{\theta}}\right)\\
&+\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\big(\frac{(3c_{0})^{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{j!}\|t^{j+1}\chi_{T}\|_{H^{\theta}}+\frac{(3c_{0})^{j+\frac{1}{2}+\theta}}{j!}\|t^{j+1}\chi_{T}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\quad+\frac{(3c_{0})^{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{j!}\|t^{j+1}\chi_{T}^{\prime}\|_{H^{\theta}}+\frac{(3c_{0})^{j-\frac{1}{2}+\theta}}{j!}\|t^{j+1}\chi^{\prime}_{T}\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{(3c_{0})^{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{j!}\|t^{j}\chi_{T}\|_{H^{\theta}}\big).\end{split}

(6.62)

By (
6.59
) and (
6.60
), we have

\begin{split}\|F_{1}\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{0}}\leq c_{0}^{1-(\theta+\varepsilon)}\|F_{1}\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{\theta+\varepsilon-1}}\leq(3c_{0})^{1-\theta}\|F_{1}\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{\theta+\varepsilon-1}}.\end{split}

(6.63)

Combining (
6.61
) and (
6.63
) yields

\begin{split}|\chi_{T}u_{1}|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\leq{C}_{T}\|F_{1}\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{\theta{+\varepsilon}-1}},\end{split}

(6.64)

where

\begin{split}C_{T}=\bar{C}_{T}(3c_{0})^{1-\theta}.\end{split}

(6.65)

Due to
\theta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
0<T\leq 1
, shows that

\begin{split}\|\chi_{T}\|_{H^{\theta}}\lesssim&T^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}\|\chi\|_{H^{\theta}},\\
\|\chi_{T}\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta-1}}=&T^{\frac{3}{2}-\theta}\|\chi\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta-1}}.\end{split}

(6.66)

Inserting (
6.66
) into (
6.65
) and using (
6.62
) yields

\begin{split}{C}_{T}\lesssim\ &(3c_{0}T)^{\frac{3}{2}-\theta}\left(\|\chi\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta-1}}+\|t\chi\|_{H^{\theta}}+\|t\chi^{\prime}\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta-1}}+\|t^{2}\chi^{\prime}\|_{H^{\theta}}\right)\\
&+\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\big(\frac{(3c_{0}T)^{j+\frac{3}{2}-\theta}}{j!}\|t^{j+1}\chi\|_{H^{\theta}}+\frac{(3c_{0}T)^{j+\frac{3}{2}}}{j!}\|t^{j+1}\chi\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\quad+\frac{(3c_{0}T)^{j+\frac{1}{2}-\theta}}{j!}\|t^{j+1}\chi^{\prime}\|_{H^{\theta}}+\frac{(3c_{0}T)^{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{j!}\|t^{j+1}\chi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{(3c_{0}T)^{j+\frac{1}{2}-\theta}}{j!}\|t^{j}\chi\|_{H^{\theta}}\big).\end{split}

(6.67)

Observing (
6.60
) and noting that
0<T<1
, we derive that

3c_{0}\leq(6+6\sqrt{2})T^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(6.68)

Combining (
6.67
) and (
6.68
), we conclude that

C_{T}\lesssim C_{*}T^{\frac{1}{4}},

(6.69)

where

\begin{split}{C}_{*}=\ &(6+6\sqrt{2})(\|\chi\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta-1}}+\|t\chi\|_{H^{\theta}}+\|t\chi^{\prime}\|_{\dot{H}^{\theta-1}}+\|t^{2}\chi^{\prime}\|_{H^{\theta}})\\
&+\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\frac{(6+6\sqrt{2})^{j+2}}{j!}\left(\|t^{j+1}\chi\|_{H^{\theta}}+\|t^{j+1}\chi^{\prime}\|_{H^{\theta}}+\|t^{j}\chi\|_{H^{\theta}}\right).\end{split}

(6.70)

For
\chi
satisfying (
1.14
), this implies that

is a bounded constant depending only on
\chi
and
\theta
. Summing up our results (
6.64
), (

), and (

), we obtain

\begin{split}|\chi_{T}u_{1}|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\leq{C}_{*}T^{\frac{1}{4}}\|F_{1}\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{\theta+\varepsilon-1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}.}\end{split}

(6.71)

On the other hand, if
(\tau,\xi)\in\mathrm{supp}\widetilde{F_{2}}
, then

||\tau|-|\xi_{1}||>T^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Following the same steps as in the proof of (

\begin{split}|\chi_{T}u_{2}|_{H^{s-1,1}_{\theta}}\lesssim T^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\|F_{2}\|_{H^{s-1,0}_{\theta+\varepsilon-1}},\end{split}

(6.72)

where the term

is handled analogously to the case of
u_{1}
. Adding (

6.71

3.21
). Due to the representation of solutions for linear waves, the function

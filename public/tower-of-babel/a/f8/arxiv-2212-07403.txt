Abstract
. In this paper we explore the weak solutions of the Cauchy problem and an inverse source problem for the heat equation in the quantum calculus, formulated in abstract Hilbert spaces. For this we use the Fourier series expansions. Moreover, we prove the existence, uniqueness and stability of the weak solution of the inverse problem with a final determination condition. We give some examples such as the q q -Sturm–Liouville problem, the q q -Bessel operator, the q q -deformed Hamiltonian, the fractional Sturm-Liouville operator, and the restricted fractional Laplacian, covered by our analysis.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some notations related to the
q
-calculus. We will always assume that
0<q<1
. The
q
-real number
[\alpha]_{q}
is defined by

[\alpha]_{q}:=\frac{1-q^{\alpha}}{1-q}.

The
q
-analogue differential operator
D_{q}f(x)
is defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle D_{q}f(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(qx)}{x(1-q)},

The
q
-derivative of a product of two functions has the form

(2.2)

\displaystyle D_{q}(fg)(x)=f(qx)D_{q}(g)(x)+D_{q}(f)(x)g(x).

The
q
-integral (or Jackson integral) is defined by (see
[
19
]
)

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{x}f(t)d_{q}t=(1-q)x\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}q^{m}f(xq^{m}),

and, more generally,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{a}^{b}f(x)d_{q}x=\int\limits_{0}^{b}f(x)d_{q}x-\int\limits_{0}^{a}f(x)d_{q}x,

provided the sums converge absolutely. Note that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{a}^{x}D_{q}f(t)d_{q}t=f(x)-f(a).

The
q
-version of the integration by parts takes the form

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{a}^{b}f(x)D_{q}g(x)d_{q}x=\left[fg\right]_{a}^{b}-\int\limits_{a}^{b}g(qx)D_{q}f(x)d_{q}x.

Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space and let
\mathcal{L}
be an operator with the discrete spectrum and eigenfunctions
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\in{I}}
in
H
, where
I
is a countable set
(I=\mathbb{N}^{k}
or
I=\mathbb{Z}^{k}
for some
k
). We assume that the operator
\mathcal{L}
is diagonalisable (can be written in the infinite dimensional matrix form) with respect to the orthonormal basis
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\in{I}}
of
H
with the eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
such that
\lambda_{k}>\lambda_{0}>0
, where
\lambda_{0}
is some constant independent of
k
. In further calculus for our analysis we will also require that
\lambda_{k}\rightarrow\infty
. The Plancherel identity takes the form

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H}=\left(\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}\left|\langle u,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

for
u\in{H}
.

We denote the domain of the operator
\mathcal{L}^{k}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
by

\displaystyle Dom(\mathcal{L}^{k}):=\left\{u\in{H}:\mathcal{L}^{i}u\in Dom(\mathcal{L}),\;\;\;i=0,1,2,\cdots,k-1\right\}.

The space
C^{\infty}_{\mathcal{L}}(H):=\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}{Dom}(\mathcal{L}^{k})
is called the space of test functions for
\mathcal{L}
. Moreover, we introduce the Fr
\acute{e}
chet topology of
C^{\infty}_{\mathcal{L}}(H)
with the family of norms

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{k}_{\mathcal{L}}}:=\max\limits_{j\leq{k}}\|\mathcal{L}^{j}u\|_{H},

for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
u\in C^{\infty}_{\mathcal{L}}(H)
, and the space of
\mathcal{L}
-distributions
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(H):=\mathfrak{L}\left(C^{\infty}_{\mathcal{L}}(H),\mathbb{C}\right)
. Consequently, we can also define Sobolev spaces
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{L}}
associated to
\mathcal{L}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{L}}:=\left\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}}(H):\mathcal{L}^{d/2}u\in{H}\right\}

for any
d\in\mathbb{R}
. Using Plancherel’s identity (
2.6
), we can write the norm in the following form:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{L}}}:=\|\mathcal{L}^{d/2}u\|_{H}=\left(\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}\lambda_{k}^{d}\left|\langle u,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We introduce the spaces
C_{q}^{k}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)
and
L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)
as

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C_{q}^{k}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)}:=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}\max\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\|D_{q}^{m}u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{L}}},

and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)}:=\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{L}}},

respectively, where
D_{q}^{k}u=D_{q}\left(D_{q}^{k-1}u\right)
.

As given in
[
9
]
, two
q
-analogues of the exponential function are defined as

(2.8)

\displaystyle e_{q}^{x}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{[k]_{q}},\;\;\;\;E_{q}^{x}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}q^{k(k-1)/2}\frac{x^{k}}{[k]_{q}}.

Moreover, we can rewrite

\displaystyle E_{q}^{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\prod\limits_{i=0}^{N}\left(1+(1-q)q^{i}x\right),

and we have

\displaystyle e_{q}^{x}E_{q}^{-x}=1.

We will be making the following assumption:

Assumption 2.1
.

We assume that
\upsilon
is a continuous function on
[0,T]
and, there are some
\beta,\alpha>0
such that
0<\alpha\leq\upsilon(t)\leq\beta
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

For
0\leq{t}\leq{T}
we denote

(2.9)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\upsilon}(t):=\frac{1}{\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\prod\limits_{i=0}^{N}\left(1+(1-q)tq^{i}\upsilon(q^{i}t)\right)}.

So, by Assumption
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=0}^{N}\left(1+(1-q)tq^{i}\beta\right)}\leq\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=0}^{N}\left(1+(1-q)tq^{i}\upsilon(q^{i}t)\right)}\leq\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=0}^{N}\left(1+(1-q)tq^{i}\alpha\right)},

and it can be written as

(2.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{E_{q}^{\beta{t}}}\leq\gamma_{\upsilon}(t)\leq\frac{1}{E_{q}^{\alpha{t}}}.

Form (
2.1
) and (
2.4
) it follows that

(2.11)

\displaystyle D_{q}\left[\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)\right]=\lambda_{k}\upsilon(t)\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(q\lambda_{k}t)

and

(2.12)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{T}\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}s)d_{q}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\frac{D_{q}\left[\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}s)\right]}{\upsilon(t)}d_{q}s

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}\beta}\int\limits_{0}^{T}D_{q}\left[\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}s)\right]d_{q}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}T)}{\lambda_{k}\beta}

and

(2.13)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{t}\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}s)d_{q}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\int\limits_{0}^{t}\frac{D_{q}\left[\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}s)\right]}{\nu(t)}d_{q}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}\alpha}\int\limits_{0}^{T}D_{q}\left[\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}s)\right]d_{q}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)}{\lambda_{k}\alpha}.

Notation.
The symbol
M\lesssim K
means that there exists
\gamma>0
such that

M\leq\gamma K
, where
\gamma
is a constant.

3.
Direct problem for the q-heat equation

We start to study a Cauchy problem:

(3.1)

\displaystyle D_{q,t}u(t)+\upsilon(t)\mathcal{L}u(t)=f(t),\;\;\;u\in{H},\;\;t>0,

with the initial condition

(3.2)

\displaystyle u(0)=\varphi\in{H}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that Assumption
2.1
holds. Let
d\in\mathbb{R}
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}
and
f\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)
. Then there exists a unique solution of Problem (
3.1
)-(
3.2
):

(3.3)

\displaystyle u\in C^{1}_{q}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}\right).

Moreover, this solution can be written in the form

(3.4)

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}\left[\langle\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\gamma_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\gamma_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)\int\limits_{0}^{t}\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(q\lambda_{k}s)\langle f(s),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}d_{q}s\right]\phi_{k},

which satisfies the estimate

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}}+\|D_{q}u(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{L}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}}+\|f(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}}.

Proof.

Existence.
We start to solve the equation (
3.1
). We can use the system of eigenfunctions, and look for a solution in the series form

(3.6)

\displaystyle u(t)=\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}u_{k}(t)\phi_{k},

for each fixed
t>0
. Such an expansion always exists due to completeness of the set of eigenfunctions
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in
H
. The coefficients will then be given by the Fourier coefficients formula:
u_{k}(t)=\langle u(t),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}
.

We can similarly expand the source function,

(3.7)

\displaystyle f(t)=\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}f_{k}(t)\phi_{k},\;\;\;f_{k}(t)=\langle f(t),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}.

From (
3.6
) we have
\mathcal{L}\phi_{k}=\lambda_{k}\phi_{k},\;\;\;k\in{I}
. Hence,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u(t)=\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}u_{k}(t)\lambda_{k}\phi_{k}

and

(3.9)

\displaystyle D_{q}u(t)=\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}D_{q}u_{k}(t)\phi_{k}.

Substituting (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) into the equation (
3.1
), we find

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}\left[D_{q}u_{k}(t)+\lambda_{k}\upsilon(t)u_{k}(t)\right]\phi_{k}=\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}f_{k}(t)\phi_{k}.

But then, due to the completeness,

(3.11)

\displaystyle D_{q}u_{k}(t)+\lambda_{k}\upsilon(t)u_{k}(t)=f_{k}(t),\;\;\;k\in{I},

which are ODEs for the coefficients
u_{k}(t)
of the series (
3.6
). Using the integrating factor
\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)
and (
2.2
) and (
2.11
), we can rewrite this equation as

(3.12)

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(q\lambda_{k}t)f_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(q\lambda_{k}t)D_{q}u_{k}(t)+\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(q\lambda_{k}t)\lambda_{k}\upsilon(t)u_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(q\lambda_{k}t)D_{q}u_{k}(t)+D_{q}\left[\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)\right]u_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{q}\left[\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)u_{k}(t)\right].

Form (
2.4
) and (
3.10
) we get

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{t}D_{q}\left[\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}s)u_{k}(s)\right]d_{q}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{t}\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(q\lambda_{k}s)f_{k}(s)d_{q}s

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)u_{k}(t)=u_{k}(0)+\int\limits_{0}^{t}\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(q\lambda_{k}s)f_{k}(s)d_{q}s

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle u_{k}(t)=\gamma_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)u_{k}(0)+\gamma_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)\int\limits_{0}^{t}\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(q\lambda_{k}s)f_{k}(s)d_{q}s.

But the initial condition (
3.2
) and (
3.6
) imply

\displaystyle u(0)=\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}u_{k}(0)\phi_{k}=\varphi\;\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\;u_{k}(0)=\langle\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}.

Thus,

(3.13)

\displaystyle u_{k}(t)=\langle\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\gamma_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)+\gamma_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)\int\limits_{0}^{t}\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(q\lambda_{k}s)\langle f(s),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}d_{q}s.

So the solution can be written in the series form as

\displaystyle u(t)=\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}\left[\langle\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\gamma_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)+\gamma_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)\int\limits_{0}^{t}\gamma^{-1}_{\upsilon}(q\lambda_{k}s)\langle f(s),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}d_{q}s\right]\phi_{k},

giving (
3.4
).

Convergence .
By using Assumption
2.1
and (
2.8
) and (
2.10
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)}{\gamma_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}s)}\overset{\text{(2.7)}}{\leq}\frac{E_{q}^{\beta\lambda_{k}s}}{E_{q}^{\alpha\lambda_{k}t}}\leq\frac{E_{q}^{\alpha\lambda_{k}s}}{E_{q}^{\alpha\lambda_{k}t}}\leq 1,\;\;\;0<s\leq t,

since
E_{q}^{\alpha\lambda_{k}t}
is an increasing function for
t>0
. Indeed, using (
2.8
) we see that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[E_{q}^{\alpha\lambda_{k}t}\right]=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}q^{m(m-1)/2}\left[\alpha\lambda_{k}\right]^{k}\frac{m}{[m]_{q}}t^{m-1}\geq 0.

for
t>0
.

Hence, form (
2.10
) and (
3.13
) it follows that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\left|\langle u(t),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(3.13)}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\gamma_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)\left|\langle\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|+\int\limits_{0}^{t}\frac{\gamma_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}t)}{\gamma_{\upsilon}(\lambda_{k}s)}\left|\langle f(s),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|d_{q}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{E_{q}^{\alpha{t}}}\left|\langle\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|+\int\limits_{0}^{t}\left|\langle f(s),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|d_{q}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{1}{E_{q}^{\alpha{t}}},1\right\}\left[\left|\langle\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|+\int\limits_{0}^{t}\left|\langle f(s),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|d_{q}s\right],

and using (
3.11
), we get that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\lambda^{d/2}_{k}\left|D_{q}u_{k}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(3.11)}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\lambda^{d/2+1}_{k}\upsilon(t)\left|\langle\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|+\lambda^{d/2}_{k}\left|\langle f(t),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\left|\langle\lambda^{d/2+1}_{k}\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|+\lambda^{-1}_{k}\left|\langle\lambda^{d/2+1}_{k}f(t),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\left|\langle\mathcal{L}^{d/2+1}\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|+\lambda^{-1}_{0}\left|\langle\mathcal{L}^{d/2+1}f(s),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\{\beta,\lambda^{-1}_{0}\}\left[\left|\langle\mathcal{L}^{d/2+1}\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|+\sup\limits_{0\leq{s}\leq{T}}\left|\langle\mathcal{L}^{d/2+1}f(s),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|\right]

and

(3.16)

\displaystyle\lambda^{d/2}_{k}\left|\langle\mathcal{L}u(t),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{d/2}_{k}\left|\langle\lambda_{k}u_{k}(t),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(3.14)}}{\lesssim}

\displaystyle\left|\langle\lambda^{d/2+1}_{k}\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|+\int\limits_{0}^{t}\left|\langle\lambda^{d/2+1}_{k}f(s),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|d_{q}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\langle\mathcal{L}^{d/2+1}\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|+\int\limits_{0}^{t}\left|\langle\mathcal{L}^{d/2+1}f(s),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|d_{q}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\langle\mathcal{L}^{d/2+1}\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|+T\sup\limits_{0\leq{s}\leq{T}}\left|\langle\mathcal{L}^{d/2+1}f(s),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\{1,T\}\left[\left|\langle\mathcal{L}^{d/2+1}\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|+\sup\limits_{0\leq{s}\leq{T}}\left|\langle\mathcal{L}^{d/2+1}f(s),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|\right].

Since
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}
,
f\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)
, and using the Plancherel identity, we have

\displaystyle\|D_{q}u(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{L}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}^{\frac{d}{2}}D_{q}u(t)\|^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}\lambda_{k}^{d}\left|D_{q}u_{k}(t)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(3.15)}}{\lesssim}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}\left|\langle\mathcal{L}^{d/2+1}\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|^{2}+\sup\limits_{0\leq{s}\leq{T}}\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}\left|\langle\mathcal{L}^{d/2+1}f(s),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)}<\infty,

and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}u(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{L}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}^{d/2}\mathcal{L}u(t)\|^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}\lambda^{d}_{k}\left|\langle\mathcal{L}u(t),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(3.16)}}{\lesssim}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}\left|\langle\mathcal{L}^{d/2+1}\varphi,\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|^{2}+\sup\limits_{0\leq{s}\leq{T}}\sum\limits_{k\in{I}}\left|\langle\mathcal{L}^{d/2+1}f(s),\phi_{k}\rangle_{H}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)}<\infty.

Hence, the above estimates imply that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}u(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{L}}}+\|D_{q}u(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{L}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)}<\infty,

which means that
u\in C_{q}^{1}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{d+2}_{\mathcal{L}}\right)
and this yields the estimate (
4.4
).

Uniqueness.
It only remains to prove the uniqueness of the solution. We assume the opposite, namely that there exist the functions
u(t)
and
v(t)
, which are two different solutions of Problem (
3.1
)-(
3.2
). Thus, we have that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}D_{q,t}u(t)+\varphi(t)\mathcal{L}u(t)=f(t),&\hbox{$t>0$},\\
u(0)=\varphi\in{H},\end{array}\right.

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}D_{q,t}v(t)+\varphi(t)\mathcal{L}v(t)=f(t),&\hbox{$t>0$},\\
v(0)=\varphi\in{H}.\end{array}\right.

We define
W(t)=u(t)-v(t)
. Then the function
W(t)
is a solution of the following problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}D_{q}w(t)+\varphi(t)\mathcal{L}w(t)=f(t),&\hbox{$t>0$},\\
w(0,x)=\varphi\in{H}.\end{array}\right.

From (
3.6
) it follows that

The q-Sturm–Liouville problem
: Let

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle:=\int\limits_{0}^{a}f(x)g(x)d_{q}x,\;\;\;f,g\in L^{2}_{q}\left[0,a\right].

C^{2}_{q,0}[0,a]

M. H. Annaby and Z. S. Mansour considered a basic

\mathcal{L}(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}-\frac{1}{q}D_{q^{-1}}D_{q}y(x)+v(x)y(x)=\lambda y(x),\\
U_{1}(y)=a_{11}y(0)+a_{12}y(0),\\

arbitrary real numbers such that the rank of the matrix

is 2. The basic Sturm- Liouville eigenvalue problem (

) is self adjoint on

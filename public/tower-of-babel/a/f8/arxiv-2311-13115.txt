Introduction

The secant varieties of projective varieties have attracted considerable attention in the last three decades partly because they have found important applications to algebraic statistics and algebraic complexity theory. In recent years, there has been a great deal of progress on the syzygies of secant varieties of smooth projective curves (

\widetilde{T}:=\pi^{-1}(Q)=\mathbf{P}(E|_{Q})=\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}.

Then
\pi_{\widetilde{T}}:=\pi|_{\widetilde{T}}\colon\mathbf{P}(E|_{Q})\to Q
is the canonical projection. Observe that
\widetilde{T}
and
Z
meet along the diagonal with multiplicity
2
.
Note that

T:=\beta(\widetilde{T})\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{d}

is the tangent developable surface of
C
in
\mathbf{P}^{d}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

We have

T\sim-K_{\Sigma}~~\text{ and }~~\beta^{*}T=\widetilde{T}+\frac{2}{d-2}Z.

Proof.

Since
K_{B}+Z+\widetilde{T}\sim 0
, it follows that
T\sim-K_{\Sigma}
. We know that
T\sim 4/(d-2)H
(see Proposition
2.1
). We have
\beta^{*}T=\widetilde{T}+cZ
. Then

\frac{4}{d-2}H=2\pi^{*}A+2cH-c(d-2)\pi^{*}A,

so
c=2/(d-2)
.
∎

Next, we turn to the study of automorphism group of
\Sigma
. The next two propositions are important ingredients of the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 2.4
.

\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{P}^{1})=\operatorname{PGL}(2,\mathbf{C})
.

Proof.

Since every automorphism of
\Sigma
preserves
C=\operatorname{Sing}\Sigma
, the restriction map

\gamma\colon\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(C),~~\varphi\longmapsto\varphi|_{C}

is a well-defined group homomorphism. We show that
\gamma
is an isomorphism. First, we check that
\gamma
is injective. Take any
\varphi\in\ker(\gamma)
so that
\varphi|_{C}=\operatorname{id}_{C}
, i.e.,
\varphi(x)=x
for all
x\in C
. It suffices to confirm that
\varphi(x)=x
for any
x\in\Sigma\setminus C
. We can find a unique line
\ell
in
\mathbf{P}^{d}
passing through
x
such that
\ell
is either a bi-secant line to
C
or a tangent line to
C
. Then
\varphi(\ell)
is a bi-secant line to
C
or a tangent line to
C
, respectively. Since
\varphi|_{C}=\operatorname{id}_{C}
, it follows that
\ell\cap C=\varphi(\ell)\cap C
. Thus
\ell=\varphi(\ell)
. Now, we can choose a hyperplane section
H
of
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{d}
such that
H\cap\ell=\{x\}
. Since
\varphi|_{C}=\operatorname{id}_{C}
, it follows that
H\cap C=\varphi(H)\cap C
. Thus
H=\varphi(H)
. Then we find

\{\varphi(x)\}=\varphi(H\cap\ell)=\varphi(H)\cap\varphi(\ell)=H\cap\ell=\{x\}.

Hence
\gamma
is injective.

Next, we check that
\gamma
is surjective. The group action of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{P}^{1})
on
\mathbf{P}^{1}
naturally induces a group action of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{P}^{1})
on
\mathbf{P}H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(d))=\mathbf{P}^{d}
. More precisely, the natural group action of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbf{C})
on
H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(1))
uniquely determines the given group action of
\operatorname{PGL}(2,\mathbf{C})
on
\mathbf{P}^{1}=\mathbf{P}H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(1))
. Then
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbf{C})
acts on
H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(d))
in such a way that
\varphi(u^{d-k}v^{k})=\varphi(u)^{d-k}\varphi(v)^{k}
for any
\varphi\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbf{C})
, where
u,v
form a basis of
H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(1))
. This group action induces a group action of
\operatorname{PGL}(2,\mathbf{C})
on
\mathbf{P}H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(d))
. In this way, we may regard
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{P}^{1})
as a subgroup
G
of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{P}^{d})
preserving the rational normal curve
C
of degree
d
in
\mathbf{P}^{d}
. The restriction group homomorphism

\mu\colon G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(C),~~\varphi\longmapsto\varphi|_{C}

is an isomorphism. Every
\varphi\in G
sends a bi-secant line (resp. a tangent line) to
C
in
\mathbf{P}^{d}
to a bi-secant line (resp. a tangent line) to
C
in
\mathbf{P}^{d}
. This means that
\varphi|_{\Sigma}\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)
. As
\gamma(\varphi|_{\Sigma})=\varphi|_{C}
, we can conclude that
\gamma
is surjective.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

There are exactly three
\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)
-orbits:
C
,
T\setminus C
, and
\Sigma\setminus T
.

Proof.

Clearly, every automorphism of
\Sigma
preserves
T
and
C
. As
\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Aut}(C)
can permute both sets
\{\text{length two subschemes of $C$}\}
and
\{\text{points of $C$}\}
, it suffices to show that if
\ell
is a bi-secant line or a tangent line to
C
and
x,y\in\ell\setminus C
, then
\varphi(x)=y
for some
\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)
with
\varphi(\ell)=\ell
. Recall that the group action of
\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{PGL}(2,\mathbf{C})
on
\Sigma
extends to a group action on
\mathbf{P}^{d}=\mathbf{P}H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(d))
which is induced from a group action of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbf{C})
on
H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(d))
. Let
u,v
be a basis of
H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(1))

\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbf{C})
on

\varphi(u^{d-k}v^{k})=(\alpha u+\gamma v)^{d-k}(\beta u+\delta v)^{k}~~\text{for}~~\varphi=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbf{C}).

We may regard

as coordinates of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
.

Let
\xi:=C\cap\ell
be the scheme-theoretic intersection. Then
\ell=\mathbf{P}H^{0}(\xi,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(d)|_{\xi})

H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(d))\longrightarrow H^{0}(\xi,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(d)|_{\xi}),

every element of
\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)
gives an isomorphism between bi-secant or tangent lines

Suppose that
\ell

\xi=[1,0]+[0,1]

u^{d}|_{\xi},v^{d}|_{\xi}

\varphi=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&a\end{bmatrix}~~\text{for any}~~a\in\mathbf{C}^{*},

. Such

K

\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbf{C})

Suppose that
\ell

∎

3.
K-polystability

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. We keep using the notations introduced in the previous sections. It is enough to show that the secant variety
\Sigma
of a rational normal curve of degree
d\geq 4
is K-polystable. For this purpose, we utilize Zhuang’s result.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
45
, Corollary 4.14]
)
.

Let
G
be an algebraic group, and
X
be a Fano variety with a
G
-action. If
G
is reductive and
S_{X}(F)<A_{X}(F)
for every
G
-invariant irreducible divisor
F
over
X
, then
X
is K-polystable.

Here we briefly recall basic necessary definitions. Let
X
be a
Fano variety
, which means that
X
has log terminal singularities and
-K_{X}
is ample. Recall that

S_{X}(F):=\frac{1}{(-K_{X})^{\dim X}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{vol}(-K_{X}-xF)dx,

and
A_{X}(F)
is the log discrepancy of
F
. For any divisor
D
on
X
with
\dim X=n
, we define the
volume
of
X
as

\operatorname{vol}(D)=\operatorname{vol}_{X}(D):=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor)}{m^{n}/n!}.

When
F
is a divisor on
\widetilde{X}
, where
f\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
is a birational morphism, we set

\operatorname{vol}(-K_{X}-xF):=\operatorname{vol}_{\widetilde{X}}(f^{*}(-K_{X})-xF).

The
pseudoeffective threshold
of
F
is defined as

\tau_{X}(F):=\sup\{\tau\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}|\operatorname{vol}(-K_{X}-\tau F)>0\}.

The
alpha invariant
of
X
is

\alpha(X):=\inf\{\operatorname{lct}(X,D)\mid-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}D\geq 0\}=\inf_{F}\frac{A_{X}(F)}{\tau_{X}(F)},

where the infimum takes over prime divisors
F
over
X
(
[
4
, Theorem C]
). Here the
log canonical threshold
of
(X,D)
is defined as

\operatorname{lct}(X,D):=\inf\{c>0\mid\text{$(X,cD)$ is a log canonical pair}\}.

The
delta invariant
of
X
is

\delta(X):=\inf_{F}\frac{A_{X}(F)}{S_{X}(F)},

where the infimum takes over prime divisors
F
over
X
(
[
4
, Theorem C]
). When an algebraic group
G
acts on
X
, one can also define the
G
-equivariant alpha invariant
\alpha_{G}(X)
and the
G
-equivariant delta invariant
\delta_{G}(X)
using
G
-invariant divisors, (see
[
45
]
for more details).

In view of Theorem
3.1
, we need to check that
S_{\Sigma}(F)<A_{\Sigma}(F)
for every
\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)
-invariant irreducible divisor
F
over
\Sigma
. Proposition
2.5
says that if
F
is an
\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)
-invariant irreducible divisor over
\Sigma
, then
F=T
or
c_{\Sigma}(F)=C
, where
c_{\Sigma}(F)
is the center of
F
on
\Sigma
. The former case is easy to confirm.

Lemma 3.2
.

S_{\Sigma}(T)=1/4
and
A_{\Sigma}(T)=1
.

Proof.

We have
S_{\Sigma}(T)=\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{3}dx=1/4
. Clearly,
A_{\Sigma}(T)=1
.
∎

Henceforth, we focus on the latter case:
c_{\Sigma}(F)=C
. For this case, we use a variant of Fujita’s result.

Proposition 3.3

(cf.
[
21
, Proposition 3.2]
)
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional Fano variety,
\eta\in X
be a scheme-theoretic point,
F
be an irreducible divisor over
X
with
c_{X}(F)=\overline{\eta}
, and
0<t\leq s
be positive real numbers. Assume the following:

(1)

There is a prime divisor
D_{0}
on
X
with
D_{0}\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}-K_{X}
such that
\operatorname{lct}(X,D_{0})_{\eta}\geq t
.

(2)

\operatorname{ord}_{F}D\leq s^{-1}A_{X}(F)
for any effective
\mathbf{Q}
-divisor
D\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}-K_{X}
with
D_{0}\not\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(D)
.

Then we have

S_{X}(F)\leq\frac{A_{X}(F)}{n+1}\left(\frac{n-1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\right).

Proof.

Even though this was essentially shown in
[
21
, Proof of Proposition 3.2]
, we include the whole proof for reader’s convenience. By Condition
(1)
,
\operatorname{ord}_{F}D_{0}\leq t^{-1}A_{X}(F)
. First, assume that
\operatorname{ord}_{F}D_{0}\leq s^{-1}A_{X}(F)
. Then, together with Condition
(2)
, we get
\operatorname{ord}_{F}D\leq s^{-1}A_{X}(F)
for every effective
\mathbf{Q}
-divisor
D\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}-K_{X}
. Thus
s\leq A_{X}(F)/\tau_{X}(F)
.
By
[
20
, Proposition 2.1]
, we obtain

S_{X}(F)\leq\frac{n}{n+1}\tau_{X}(F)\leq\frac{n}{n+1}s^{-1}A_{X}(F)\leq\frac{A_{X}(F)}{n+1}\left(\frac{n-1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\right).

Next, assume that
\operatorname{ord}_{F}D_{0}>s^{-1}A_{X}(F)
. Take a log resolution
f\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
of
X
such that
F
is a divisor on
\widetilde{X}
. For any effective
\mathbf{Q}
-divisor
D\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}-K_{X}
, there is
e\in[0,1]\cap\mathbf{Q}
such that
D=eD_{0}+(1-e)D^{\prime}
with
-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}D^{\prime}\geq 0
and
D_{0}\not\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(D^{\prime})
. By Condition
(2)
, we have
\operatorname{ord}_{F}D^{\prime}\leq s^{-1}A_{X}(F)
. Choose
x\in(s^{-1}A_{X}(F),\operatorname{ord}_{F}D_{0})\cap\mathbf{Q}
such that
\operatorname{ord}_{F}D\geq x
. Since

x\leq\operatorname{ord}_{F}D\leq e\operatorname{ord}_{F}D_{0}+(1-e)s^{-1}A_{X}(F),

it follows that

d\geq\frac{x-s^{-1}A_{X}(F)}{\operatorname{ord}_{F}D_{0}-s^{-1}A_{X}(F)}.

This implies that
|-mK_{X}-mxF|
has a fixed divisor

m\left(\frac{x-s^{-1}A_{X}(F)}{\operatorname{ord}_{F}D_{0}-s^{-1}A_{X}(F)}\right)D_{0}

for a sufficiently divisible integer
m>0
. Thus we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\operatorname{vol}(-K_{X}-xF)\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle=\operatorname{vol}\left(-f^{*}K_{X}-xF-\frac{x-s^{-1}A_{X}(F)}{\operatorname{ord}_{F}D_{0}-s^{-1}A_{X}(F)}(f^{*}D_{0}-\operatorname{ord}_{F}D_{0})F\right)\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{F}D_{0}-x}{\operatorname{ord}_{F}D_{0}-s^{-1}A_{X}(F)}\right)^{n}\operatorname{vol}(-K_{X}-s^{-1}A_{X}(F)F).\end{array}

By
[
21
, Proposition 3.1]
, we obtain

S_{X}(F)\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\frac{n-1}{s}A_{X}(F)+\operatorname{ord}_{F}D_{0}\right)\leq\frac{A_{X}(F)}{n+1}\left(\frac{n-1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

The next two lemmas allow us to confirm
S_{\Sigma}(F)<A_{\Sigma}(F)
for every
\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)
-invariant irreducible divisor
F
over
\Sigma
with
c_{\Sigma}(F)=C
by applying Proposition
3.3
. To this end, we construct a log resolution of the log pair
(\Sigma,T)
. Recall that
\widetilde{T}=\beta_{*}^{-1}T
and
Z
meet along the diagonal
\Delta=\mathbf{P}^{1}
with multiplicity
2
. Let
\beta_{1}\colon B_{1}\to B
be the blow-up of
B
along
\Delta
with the exceptional divisor
E_{1}
. Then
\widetilde{T}_{1}:=\beta_{1,*}^{-1}\widetilde{T},Z_{1}:=\beta_{1,*}^{-1}Z,E_{1}
meet along a curve
\Delta_{1}=\mathbf{P}^{1}
. Let
\beta_{2}\colon B_{2}\to B_{1}
be the blow-up of
B_{1}
along
\Delta_{1}
with the exceptional divisor
E_{2}
. Then the union of
\widetilde{T}_{2}:=\beta_{2,*}^{-1}\widetilde{T}_{1},Z_{2}:=\beta_{2,*}^{-1}Z_{2},\widetilde{E}_{1}:=\beta_{2,*}^{-1}E_{1},E_{2}
is simple normal crossing, so
f:=\beta_{2}\circ\beta_{1}\circ\beta
is a log resolution of the log pair
(\Sigma,T)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

\operatorname{lct}(\Sigma,T)=(d+2)/(2d)
.

Proof.

We have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}K_{B}=\beta^{*}K_{\Sigma}-\frac{d-4}{d-2}Z~\text{(Proposition \ref{prop:-K_{Sigma}})}&\text{and}&\beta^{*}T=\widetilde{T}+\frac{2}{d-2}Z~\text{(Proposition \ref{prop:beta^*T})};\\[10.0pt]
K_{B_{1}}=(\beta_{1}\circ\beta)^{*}K_{\Sigma}-\frac{d-4}{d-2}Z_{1}+\frac{2}{d-2}E_{1}&\text{and}&(\beta_{1}\circ\beta)^{*}T=\widetilde{T}_{1}+\frac{2}{d-2}Z_{1}+\frac{d}{d-2}E_{1};\\[10.0pt]
K_{B_{2}}=f^{*}K_{\Sigma}-\frac{d-4}{d-2}Z_{2}+\frac{2}{d-2}\widetilde{E}_{1}+\frac{4}{d-2}E_{2}&\text{and}&f^{*}T=\widetilde{T}_{2}+\frac{2}{d-2}Z_{2}+\frac{d}{d-2}\widetilde{E}_{1}+\frac{2d}{d-2}E_{2}.\end{array}

Then

\operatorname{lct}(\Sigma,T)=\min\left\{\frac{A_{\Sigma}(T)}{\operatorname{ord}_{T}T},\frac{A_{\Sigma}(Z)}{\operatorname{ord}_{Z}T},\frac{A_{\Sigma}(E_{1})}{\operatorname{ord}_{E_{1}}T},\frac{A_{\Sigma}(E_{2})}{\operatorname{ord}_{E_{2}}T}\right\}=\frac{A_{\Sigma}(E_{2})}{\operatorname{ord}_{E_{2}}T}=\frac{1+\frac{4}{d-2}}{\frac{2d}{d-2}}=\frac{d+2}{2d}.

We finish the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
F
be an
\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)
-invariant irreducible divisor over
\Sigma
with
c_{\Sigma}(F)=C
. Then we have

\operatorname{ord}_{F}D\leq A_{\Sigma}(F)

for any effective
\mathbf{Q}
-divisor
D\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}-K_{\Sigma}
with
T\not\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(D)
.

Proof.

It suffices to check the assertion when
\operatorname{Supp}(D)
is irreducible. By Proposition
2.3
,

\beta^{*}D\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}\beta^{*}T\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}\pi^{*}2A+\frac{2}{d-2}Z,

so we may write

\beta^{*}D=\beta_{*}^{-1}D+aZ~~\text{for some}~~0\leq a\leq\frac{2}{d-2}.

Then

\operatorname{ord}_{Z}D=a\leq\frac{2}{d-2}=1-\frac{d-4}{d-2}=A_{\Sigma}(Z).

From now on, assume that
F\neq Z
so that
c_{B}(F)=\Delta=\mathbf{P}^{1}
. Recall that
\widetilde{T}=\beta_{*}^{-1}T=\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}
and
Z=\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}
meet along the diagonal
\Delta
with multiplicity
2
. We have

\beta_{*}^{-1}D\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}\Big(\frac{4}{d-2}-2a\Big)H+\pi^{*}a(d-2)A\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}\pi^{*}2A+\Big(\frac{2}{d-2}-a\Big)Z,

so we get

(\beta_{*}^{-1}D)|_{Z}\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}\Big(\frac{4}{d-2}-2a\Big)H|_{Z}+a(d-2)\Delta~~\text{ and }~~(\beta_{*}^{-1}D)|_{\widetilde{T}}\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}\pi_{\widetilde{T}}^{*}2A|_{Q}+\Big(\frac{4}{d-2}-2a\Big)\Delta.

Note that
\mathscr{O}_{Z}(H|_{Z})=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(d)
and
\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{T}}(\pi_{\widetilde{T}}^{*}A|_{Q})=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(2)
.
Then we have

\operatorname{ord}_{\Delta}(\beta_{*}^{-1}D)|_{Z}\leq a(d-2)~~\text{ and }~~\operatorname{ord}_{\Delta}(\beta_{*}^{-1}D)|_{\widetilde{T}}\leq\frac{4}{d-2}-2a.

Thus we obtain

\operatorname{ord}_{\Delta}\beta_{*}^{-1}D\leq\underbrace{\min\left\{a(d-2),\frac{4}{d-2}-2a\right\}\leq\frac{4}{d}}_{\text{the equality holds $\Longleftrightarrow$ $a=\frac{4}{d(d-2)}$}}.

Suppose that
\operatorname{Supp}\big((\beta_{1}\circ\beta)_{*}^{-1}D\big)
does not contain
\Delta_{1}=\widetilde{T}_{1}\cap Z_{1}\cap E_{1}
, which is a unique
\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)
-invariant curve on
B_{1}
. We find

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\operatorname{ord}_{F}D&=&\operatorname{ord}_{F}\beta_{*}^{-1}D+a\operatorname{ord}_{F}Z\\
&=&\operatorname{ord}_{\Delta}\beta_{*}^{-1}D\cdot\operatorname{ord}_{F}E_{1}+a\operatorname{ord}_{F}Z\\
&=&(\operatorname{ord}_{\Delta}\beta_{*}^{-1}D+a)\cdot\operatorname{ord}_{F}E_{1}+a\operatorname{ord}_{F}Z_{1}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\Big(\frac{4}{d}+\frac{2}{d-2}\Big)\operatorname{ord}_{F}E_{1}+\frac{2}{d-2}\operatorname{ord}_{F}Z_{1}.\end{array}

Since

K_{B_{1}}+\Big(\frac{4}{d}E_{1}+Z_{1}\Big)=(\beta_{1}\circ\beta)^{*}K_{\Sigma}+\Big(\frac{4}{d}+\frac{2}{d-2}\Big)E_{1}+\frac{2}{d-2}Z_{1}

and
(B_{1},(4/d)E_{1}+Z_{1})
is a log canonical pair, it follows that

\operatorname{ord}_{F}D\leq\Big(\frac{4}{d}+\frac{2}{d-2}\Big)\operatorname{ord}_{F}E_{1}+\frac{2}{d-2}\operatorname{ord}_{F}Z_{1}\leq A_{\Sigma}(F).

Suppose that
\operatorname{Supp}\big((\beta_{1}\circ\beta)_{*}^{-1}D\big)
does contain
\Delta_{1}
. In this case, notice that

\operatorname{ord}_{\Delta}\beta_{*}^{-1}D\leq\frac{1}{2}\min\{\operatorname{ord}_{\Delta}(\beta_{*}^{-1}D)|_{Z},\operatorname{ord}_{\Delta}(\beta_{*}^{-1}D)|_{\widetilde{T}}\}\leq\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{ord}_{\Delta}(\beta_{*}^{-1}D)|_{\widetilde{T}}\leq\frac{2}{d-2}-a.

Then we find

\operatorname{ord}_{\Delta}\Big(\beta_{*}^{-1}D+aZ+\frac{d-4}{d-2}Z\Big)\leq\frac{2}{d-2}-a+a+\frac{d-4}{d-2}=1.

This implies that the log pair

\left(B,\beta_{*}^{-1}D+aZ+\frac{d-4}{d-2}Z\right)

is log canonical at the generic point of
\Delta
. Since

K_{B}+\beta_{*}^{-1}D+aZ+\frac{d-4}{d-2}Z=\beta^{*}(K_{\Sigma}+D),

it follows that
\operatorname{ord}_{F}D\leq A_{\Sigma}(F)
.
∎

Finally, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
F
be an
\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)
-invariant irreducible divisor over
\Sigma
. In view of Theorem
3.1
, we need to check that
S_{\Sigma}(F)<A_{\Sigma}(F)
. Proposition
2.5
says that
F=T
or
c_{\Sigma}(F)=C
. In the former case, Lemma
3.2
shows
S_{\Sigma}(T)<A_{\Sigma}(T)
. Assume that
c_{\Sigma}(F)=C
. Applying Proposition
3.3
with
t=(d+2)/2d
(by Lemma
3.4
) and
s=1
(by Lemma
3.5
), we find

S_{\Sigma}(F)\leq\frac{A_{\Sigma}(F)}{4}\left(\frac{2}{1}+\frac{2d}{d+2}\right)=A_{\Sigma}(F)\frac{4d+4}{4(d+2)}<A_{\Sigma}(F).

Thus we finish the proof.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The original statement of
[
21
, Proposition 3.2]
replaces Condition (2) in Proposition
3.3
with the following condition:
\operatorname{lct}(X,D)\geq s
for any effective
\mathbf{Q}
-divisor
D\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}-K_{X}
with
D_{0}\not\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(D)
. To prove Theorem
1.1
by applying the original statement of
[
21
, Proposition 3.2]
, we need that
\operatorname{lct}(\Sigma,D)\geq 1
for any effective
\mathbf{Q}
-divisor
D\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}-K_{\Sigma}
with
T\not\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(D)
. However, this is not the case. If
\ell
is a line in
\mathbf{P}^{2}
, then
\beta(\pi^{-1}(\ell))
is a
\mathbf{Q}
-divisor on
\Sigma
such that

\beta(\pi^{-1}(\ell))\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}-\frac{1}{2}K_{\Sigma}~~\text{ and }~~T=\beta(\pi^{-1}(Q))\not\subseteq\beta(\pi^{-1}(\ell)).

Putting
D:=2\beta(\pi^{-1}(\ell))
, we see that
(\Sigma,cD)
is not log canonical whenever
c>1/2
. This means that
\operatorname{lct}(\Sigma,D)\leq 1/2
. Thus the required condition for the original statement of
[
21
, Proposition 3.2]
is not fulfilled. Moreover, this also proves that

\alpha(\Sigma)\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Remark 3.7
.

Recall from
[
45
, Corollary 1.3]
that if a reductive algebraic group
G
acting on a Fano variety
X
of dimension
n
and
\alpha_{G}(X)>n/(n+1)
, then
X
is K-polystable. Lemmas
3.4
and
3.5
show that

\alpha_{\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)}(\Sigma)=\frac{d+2}{2d}.

However, if
d\geq 4
, then
(d+2)/(2d)\leq 2/3
. Thus one cannot prove K-polystability of
\Sigma
in general only estimating
\alpha_{\operatorname{Aut}(\Sigma)}(\Sigma)
.

4.
Anticanonical Polar Cylinders

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
. We keep using the notations introduced in the previous sections. It is enough to show that the secant variety
\Sigma
of a rational normal curve of degree
d\geq 4
admits a
(-K_{\Sigma})
-polar cylinder. Recall that a
(-K_{X})
-polar cylinder
on a
\mathbf{Q}
-Fano variety
X
is a Zariski open subset
U:=X\setminus\operatorname{Supp}(D)
for an effective divisor
D\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}-K_{X}
such that
U\cong\mathbf{C}^{1}\times Z
for an affine variety
Z
.

The universal family map
\sigma
is explicitly given by

\sigma\colon\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}\longrightarrow\mathbf{P}^{2},~~[s,t]\times[u,v]\longmapsto\Big[su,\frac{1}{2}(tu+sv),tv\Big].

Let
x,y,z
be coordinates of
\mathbf{P}^{2}
such that

Q=\sigma(\Delta)=V(y^{2}-xz).

Let
\ell:=V(z)
be the tangent line to
Q
at
P=[1,0,0]\in Q
. Note that

\sigma^{-1}(\ell)=V(v)\cup V(t),

where
V(v)=\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\{[1,0]\}
and
V(t)=\{[1,0]\}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}
. Then

\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}\setminus\sigma^{-1}(\ell)=\mathbf{C}^{1}\times\mathbf{C}^{1}=\mathbf{C}^{2}

with coordinates
s,u
. Note that
\mathbf{P}^{2}\setminus\ell=\mathbf{C}^{2}
with coordinates
x,y
. The restriction map
\sigma|_{\mathbf{C}^{2}}
is given by

\sigma|_{\mathbf{C}^{2}}\colon\mathbf{C}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbf{C}^{2},~~(s,u)\longmapsto\Big(su,\frac{1}{2}(s+u)\Big).

Here we may think that
\mathbf{C}^{2}
is the Hilbert scheme of two points on
\mathbf{C}^{1}=\mathbf{P}^{1}\setminus\{[1,0]\}
.

Now, consider
\mathbf{P}(E)\setminus\pi^{-1}(\ell)=\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{C}^{2}
with the projection

\pi|_{\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{C}^{2}}\colon\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{C}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbf{C}^{2},~~[w_{0},w_{1}]\times(x,y)\longmapsto(x,y).

Recall that
Z\setminus\pi^{-1}(\ell)=\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{P}^{1}\setminus\sigma^{-1}(\ell)=\mathbf{C}^{2}
. The restriction of the inclusion
Z\subseteq\mathbf{P}(E)
is given by

\iota\colon\mathbf{C}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{C}^{2},~~(s,u)\longmapsto[s,1]\times\Big(su,\frac{1}{2}(s+u)\Big).

Note that
s,u
are zeros of the quadratic polynomial
X^{2}-(s+u)X+su
in
X
.
Thus

\iota(\mathbf{C}^{2})=V(w_{0}^{2}-2yw_{0}w_{1}+xw_{1}^{2}).

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
. We construct an effective
\mathbf{Q}
-divisor
D\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}-K_{\Sigma}
such that
\Sigma\setminus\operatorname{Supp}(D)
is a
(-K_{\Sigma})
-polar cylinder isomorphic to
\mathbf{C}^{1}\times(\mathbf{C}^{1}\times\mathbf{C}^{*})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Notice that

\beta(\pi^{-1}(\ell))\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}-\frac{1}{2}K_{\Sigma}~~\text{ and }~~\pi^{-1}(\ell)+\frac{1}{d-2}Z=\beta^{*}(\beta(\pi^{-1}(\ell))\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}\beta^{*}\Big(-\frac{1}{2}K_{\Sigma}\Big).

There is a nonzero section
H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(d))
vanishing at
[1,0]\in\mathbf{P}^{1}
of order
d
. As

H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(d))=H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{2},E),

this section gives a tautological divisor
H_{0}
on
B=\mathbf{P}(E)
. Then

\beta(H_{0})\sim H\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}-\frac{d-2}{4}K_{\Sigma}.

Putting

D:=\frac{4}{d}(\beta(\pi^{-1}(\ell))+\beta(H_{0}))\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}-K_{\Sigma},

we claim that

\Sigma\setminus\operatorname{Supp}(D)=\mathbf{C}^{1}\times(\mathbf{C}^{1}\times\mathbf{C}^{*}).

To this end, note that

\Sigma\setminus\operatorname{Supp}(D)=\Sigma\setminus(\beta(\pi^{-1}(\ell))\cup\beta(H_{0}))=B\setminus(\pi^{-1}(\ell)\cup Z\cup H_{0}).

Recall that
B\setminus\pi^{-1}(\ell)=\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{C}^{2}
. Observe that
H_{0}|_{\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbf{C}^{2}}=V(w_{1})
. Thus

B\setminus(\pi^{-1}(\ell)\cup H_{0})=\mathbf{C}^{1}\times\mathbf{C}^{2}=\mathbf{C}^{3}

with coordinates
w_{0},x,y
. We have

Z|_{\mathbf{C}^{3}}=\iota(\mathbf{C}^{2})|_{\mathbf{C}^{3}}=V(w_{0}^{2}-2yw_{0}+x).

Now, consider the following isomorphism

\gamma\colon\mathbf{C}^{3}\longrightarrow\mathbf{C}^{3},~~(w_{0},x,y)\longmapsto(w_{0},y,w_{0}^{2}-2yw_{0}+x),

under which
V(w_{0}^{2}-2yw_{0}+x)
correspondences to a coordinate plane of
\mathbf{C}^{3}
isomorphic to
\mathbf{C}^{2}
.
Thus we see that

\mathbf{C}^{3}\setminus Z|_{\mathbf{C}^{3}}=\mathbf{C}^{3}\setminus\gamma(Z|_{\mathbf{C}^{3}})=\mathbf{C}^{1}\times\mathbf{C}^{1}\times\mathbf{C}^{*}.

However,

\mathbf{C}^{3}\setminus Z|_{\mathbf{C}^{3}}=\big(B\setminus(\pi^{-1}(\ell)\cup H_{0})\big)\setminus Z=B\setminus(\pi^{-1}(\ell)\cup Z\cup H_{0}),

so we complete the proof.
∎

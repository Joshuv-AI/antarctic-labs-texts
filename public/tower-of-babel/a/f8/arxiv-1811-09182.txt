𝓗 𝒑 \boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}_{p}} -theory of general Dirichlet series
Andreas Defant, Ingo Schoolmann

Abstract
. Inspired by results of Bayart on ordinary Dirichlet series ∑ a n ​ n − s \sum a_{n}n^{-s} , the main purpose of this article is to start an ℋ p \mathcal{H}_{p} -theory of general Dirichlet series ∑ a n ​ e − λ n ​ s \sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s} . Whereas the ℋ p \mathcal{H}_{p} -theory of ordinary Dirichlet series, in view of an ingenious identification of Bohr, may be seen as a sub-theory of Fourier analysis on the infinite dimensional torus 𝕋 ∞ \mathbb{T}^{\infty} , the ℋ p \mathcal{H}_{p} -theory of general Dirichlet series is build as a sub-theory of Fourier analysis on certain compact abelian groups, including the Bohr compactification ℝ ¯ \overline{\mathbb{R}} of the reals. Our approach allows to extend various important facts on Hardy spaces of ordinary Dirichlet series to the much wider setting of ℋ p \mathcal{H}_{p} -spaces of general Dirichlet series.

1.
Introduction

Within the last two decades the theory of ordinary Dirichlet series
\sum a_{n}n^{-s}
saw a remarkable renaissance which in particular led to the solution of some long-standing problems.

A fundamental object in these investigations
is given by the Banach space
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
of all ordinary Dirichlet series
D:=\sum a_{n}n^{-s}
which converge and define a bounded, and then necessarily holomorphic, function on the open right half plane
[Re>0]

(endowed with the supremum norm on
[Re>0]
).

One of the celebrated results in this ’ordinary’ theory is a result of Hedenmalm, Lindqvist and Seip from
[
15
]
which shows that
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
equals the Hardy space
H_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
on the infinitely dimensional torus as Banach spaces . Let us explain this in more detail.
The infinitely dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
is the infinite product of
\mathbb{T}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}\colon|w|=1\}
which forms a natural compact abelian group
on which the Haar measure is given by the normalized Lebesgue measure. The characters of this group are the monomials
z\mapsto z^{\alpha}
, where
\alpha
is a finite sequence
of integers, and
H_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
denotes the closed subspace of all
f\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
such that the Fourier coefficient

\widehat{f}(\alpha)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{\infty}}f(w)w^{-\alpha}dw=0\,,

whenever
\alpha<0
(in the sense that some
\alpha_{k}<0
). Then there is a unique linear isometry

\mathcal{B}\colon H_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})\to\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\,,\,\,\,\,f\sim\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{(\mathbb{N})}}\widehat{f}(\alpha)z^{\alpha}\mapsto D=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n}n^{-s}\,,

which preserves Fourier- and Dirichlet coefficients in the sense that

(1)

\widehat{f}(\alpha)=a_{n}\,\,\text{ whenever }n=p^{\alpha}.

The crucial point here is the fact that each natural number has a unique prime number decompostion
(together with other nontrivial tools like e.g. Diophantine approximation), and the result shows that the theory of ordinary Dirichlet which generate bounded, holomorphic functions on the positive half plane is intimately linked with Fourier analysis on the group
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
.

More generally, Bayart in
[
3
]
developed an
H_{p}
-theory of Dirichlet series. Recall that the Hardy space
H_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})\,,\,1\leq p\leq\infty
, is the closed subspace of all
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})

such that
\widehat{f}(\alpha)=0
if
\alpha<0
. Then the Banach spaces
\mathcal{H}_{p}
of ordinary Dirichlet series by definition (!) is the isometric image of
H_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
under the identification
from (
1
).

But all these ideas fail for general Dirichlet series.
Given a frequency
\lambda:=(\lambda_{n})
(i.e. a non-negative strictly increasing sequence of real numbers tending to
\infty
), a

\lambda
-Dirichlet series is a formal sum

\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}

with complex Dirichlet coefficients
a_{n}
and a complex variable
s
. We point out that making the jump from the ordinary case
\lambda=(\log n)
to arbitrary frequencies reveals challenging consequences.

Much of the ordinary theory relies on ’Bohr’s theorem’, the fact that for each ordinary Dirichlet series the abscissas of uniform convergence and boundedness coincide. This phenomenon fails for general Dirichlet series. Further due to the fundamental theorem of arithmetics each natural number
n
has its prime number decomposition
n=\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}
and so the frequency
(\log n)
can be written as a linear combination of
(\log p_{j})
with natural coefficients. This intimately links the theory of ordinary Dirichlet series with the theory of holomorphic functions on polydiscs, and in particular with the theory of polynomials
\sum c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}

in finitely many complex variables. One of several consequences is that
m
-homogeneous Dirichlet series
\sum a_{n}n^{-s}
, i.e.
a_{n}\neq 0
only if
n
has
m
prim factors, are linked with
m
-homogeneous polynomials.
This way powerful tools enter the game, as e.g. polynomial inequalities (like the Bohnenblust-Hille inequalities, hypercontractivity of convolution with the Poisson kernel, etc.),
m
-linear forms, or polarization. So Fourier analysis on the infinite dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
and infinite dimensional holomorphy on the
open unit ball
B_{c_{0}}
of
c_{0}
enrich the theory of ordinary Dirichlet series considerably. But unfortunately facing general Dirichlet series many of these powerful bridges
seem to collapse. New questions arise which make the theory of general Dirichlet quite demanding.

We define the space
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
analogously to the space
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
.
So
\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
whenever it converges and defines a bounded, and then necessarily holomorphic, function on
[Re>0]
.
For instance, if
\lambda=(n)
, then looking at the transformation
z=e^{-s}
we easily conclude that
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}((n))
is simply
H_{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, the space of all bounded and holomorphic functions on the open unit ball
\mathbb{D}
. And if
\lambda=(\log n)
, then we are in the ordinary case. Hence, in both cases the space
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
can be described in terms of Fourier analysis, that is, it can be considered as a Hardy space, namely
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}((n))=H_{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}((\log n))=H_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
. In view of these two examples the following question arises naturally:

Given an arbitrary frequency
\lambda
,
is it possible to describe
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
in terms of a sort of Hardy space on a compact abelian group?

Inspired by ideas of Bohr and Helson we show that in this much more general situation, the so-called Bohr compactification
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
of the real numbers
\mathbb{R}
is a suitable substitute for the infinite dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
. We note that
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
being a compact abelian group carries a Haar measure, contains
\mathbb{R}
as a dense subset, and that its dual group may be identified again with
\mathbb{R}

(see Section
3.3
for the precise definitions). So in particular ordinary Dirichlet series can be described in terms of functions on
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. This might be surprising since in contrast to the infinitely dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
the Bohr compactification
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is not metrizable and is moreover a sort of one-dimensional object.

Motivated by this observation we introduce a Fourier analysis setting for the study of general Dirichlet series.
We restrict ourserlves to
\lambda
-Dirichlet series with Dirichlet coefficients that actually are Fourier coefficients defined by functions on compact abelian groups
G
of a certain type, namely compact abelian groups allowing a continuous homomorphism
\beta\colon\mathbb{R}\to G
with dense range (this includes
\mathbb{T}
and
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
).
Given a frequency
\lambda=(\lambda_{n})
, we call such a pair
(G,\beta)
a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group whenever every character
e^{-i\lambda_{n}\cdot}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{T}

has an ’extension’
h_{\lambda_{n}}
(which then is unique) as a character on
G
:

G
\mathbb{T}
\mathbb{R}
h_{\lambda_{n}}
e^{-i\lambda_{n}\cdot}
\beta

To see a first non-trivial example, look at the continuous homomorphism

\beta_{\mathbb{T}^{\infty}}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{\infty}\,,\,\,t\mapsto(p_{k}^{-it})\,.

Then by Kronecker’s approximation theorem the pair

(\mathbb{T}^{\infty},\beta_{\mathbb{T}^{\infty}})
forms a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group of the frequency
\lambda=(\log n)
.

Now given such a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define the Hardy space

H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
of all
f\in L_{p}(G)
having Fourier transforms
f:\widehat{G}\to\mathbb{C}
supported in
\{h_{\lambda_{n}}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
Then the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
consists of all Dirichlet series

D=\sum\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\,,\,\,\,f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)

which together with the norm
\|D\|_{p}:=\|f\|_{p}

forms a Banach space.

In fact it will turn out that
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
is independent of the chosen
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
.
Looking at
\lambda=(\log n)
and the
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(\mathbb{T}^{\infty},\beta_{\mathbb{T}^{\infty}})
,
Bayart’s theory of Hardy space
\mathcal{H}_{p}
of Dirichlet series is contained in the
\mathcal{H}_{p}
-theory of general Dirichlet series we intend to present.

Assuming some restrictions on the frequency
\lambda
and given a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
, we prove in Theorem
4.12
that
H_{\infty}^{\lambda}(G)
and
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)

by means of the identification
f\mapsto\sum\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
coincide isometrically, and hence in particular

(2)

\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)=\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(\lambda)\,.

The crucial idea for the proof is inspired by Helson’s theorem
4.15
from
[
18
]

which shows that almost all vertical limits of a general Dirichlet series
\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}

with square summable coefficients, converge pointwise on the positive half plane. In
[
11
]
we extend this result to
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, adding the relevant maximal inequality.
We point out that in
[
15
]
the equality
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}=H_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
is derived by first showing that
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
equals the space
H_{\infty}(B_{c_{0}})
of all bounded and holomorphic functions on the unit ball of
c_{0}
and then proving
H_{\infty}(B_{c_{0}})=H_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
, which can be seen as an ’infinite dimensional variant’ of the classical fact
H_{\infty}(\mathbb{T})=H_{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
. Here we give a somewhat direct proof of
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}=H_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
using tools from Fourier analysis on
\mathbb{R}
.

Inspired by the isometric equality from (
2
), we start
a sort of systematic structure theory of

\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
-spaces of general Dirichlet series modelled along Bayart’s
\mathcal{H}_{p}
-theory of ordinary Dirichlet series.
In Section
3
we invent our setting.
In Section
3.6
we introduce frequencies
\lambda
of integer and natural type, and show how to reproduce
important facts on ordinary Dirichlet series (due to Bohr, Bohnenblust-Hille, Hedenmalm-Lindqvist-Seip, and Bayart) from their analogs on general Dirichlet series (see e.g. Theorem
3.31
and Corollary
3.32
).
In Section
4
we extend some key results on ordinary Dirichlet series to our more general setting. In Theorem
4.16
we show that
the monomials

form a Schauder basis of
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda),\,1<p<\infty
.
For
p=1
we give upper estimates of the basis constant of the

e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
in
\mathcal{H}_{1}(\lambda)
(Corollary
4.17
). In Theorem
4.19
we generalize Bayart’s Montel type theorem from
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}=\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\log n)
to
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
, and in Theorem

we show how Dirichlet series in
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
are determined by their

th Abschnitte. And finally we in Theorem

with certain analytic measures on
\lambda
-Dirichlet groups.

2.
General Dirichlet series

As already mentioned in the introduction a general Dirichlet series is a formal sum of the form
\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
, where
(\lambda_{n})
is a strictly increasing non negative sequence of real numbers (called frequency),
(a_{n})
a sequence of complex coefficients (called Dirichlet coefficients), and
s
a complex variable. All (formal)
\lambda
-Dirichlet series are denoted by
\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
.
The following ‘abscissas’ rule the convergence theory of general Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{c}(D)=\inf\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\mid D\text{ converges on }[Re>\sigma]\right\},

\displaystyle\sigma_{a}(D)=\inf\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\mid D\text{ converges absolutely on }[Re>\sigma]\right\},

\displaystyle\sigma_{u}(D)=\inf\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\mid D\text{ converges uniformly on }[Re>\sigma]\right\}.

By definition
\sigma_{c}(D)\leq\sigma_{u}(D)\leq\sigma_{a}(D)
and in general all these abscissas differ.
Let us mention that general Dirichlet series always define holomorphic functions on
[Re>\sigma_{c}(D)]

(see e.g.
[
14
]
).

In
[
26
]
we introduce the following two spaces of
\lambda
-Dirichlet series. The space
\mathcal{D}^{ext}_{\infty}(\lambda)
of all somewhere convergent
D\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
allowing a holomorphic and bounded extension
f
to the open right half plane
[Re>0]
endowed with the norm
\|D\|_{\infty}:=\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{[Re>0]}|f(s)|
(this in fact is a norm, see
[
26
, Corollary 3.8]
or Corollary
4.11
).
Moreover,
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
is the space of all
\lambda
-Dirichlet series which converge and define a bounded (and then necessarily holomorphic) function on
[Re>0]
. By definition we have
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)\subset\mathcal{D}^{ext}_{\infty}(\lambda)
, and in
[
26
, Theorem 5.2]
it is shown that in general this inclusion is strict and
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
fails to be complete.

But there are sufficient conditions on
\lambda
that forces
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)=\mathcal{D}^{ext}_{\infty}(\lambda)
and the completeness of
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
. Let us collect a few ’analytic’ conditions.
The first one was isolated by Bohr (see
[
6
]
), we denote it by
(BC)
:

\exists\penalty\ l=l(\lambda)>0\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ \delta>0\penalty\ \exists\penalty\ C>0\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ n\in\mathbb{N}:\penalty\ \penalty\ \lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}\geq Ce^{-(l+\delta)\lambda_{n}};

roughly speaking this condition prevents the
\lambda_{n}
’s from getting too close too fast. Secondly a strictly weaker condition of Landau
(LC)
(see
[
22
]
):

\forall\penalty\ \delta>0\penalty\ \exists\penalty\ C>0\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\penalty\ \penalty\ \lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}\geq Ce^{-e^{\delta\lambda_{n}}}.

Another important, say geometric, value associated to
\lambda
is the maximal width of the strip of convergence and non absolutely convergence:

L(\lambda):=\sup_{D\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda)}\sigma_{a}(D)-\sigma_{c}(D)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log(n)}{\lambda_{n}}=\sigma_{c}(\sum e^{-\lambda_{n}s}).

Much of the abstract theory of ordinary Dirichlet series is based on a fundamental theorem of Bohr
[
5
]
which shows that every
D\in\mathcal{D}^{ext}_{\infty}((\log n))
converges uniformly on all half spaces
[\text{Re}>\varepsilon]
for all

\varepsilon>0
, and this then easily implies that
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}^{\text{ext}}((\log n))=\mathcal{D}_{\infty}((\log n))
. In
[
6
]
and
[
22
]
Bohr and Landau extended this result to general Dirichlet series for frequencies
satisfying
(BC)
or
(LC)
, respectively.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a frequency
\lambda
satisfies Bohr’s theorem, whenever every
D\in\mathcal{D}^{ext}_{\infty}(\lambda)
converges uniformly on all half spaces
[\text{Re}>\varepsilon]
for all

\varepsilon>0
.

As in the ordinary case the identity principle implies that for every
\lambda
with Bohr’s theorem we have

\mathcal{D}_{\infty}^{ext}(\lambda)=\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)\,.

Some of the forthcoming results of this article hold under asumptions like ‘
\lambda

satisfies Bohr’s theorem’ and/or ‘
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}^{\text{ext}}(\lambda)=\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
’ and/or ‘
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
is complete’.
Analyzing the works of Bohr and Landau, we in
[
26
, Remark 4.8 and Theorem 5.1]
collect a few conditions which in concrete cases allow
to verify these conditions for concrete frequencies.

Theorem 2.2
.

A frequency
\lambda
satisfies Bohr’s theorem if one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

L(\lambda)=0
,

(2)

\lambda
is
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent,

(3)

(LC)
(this includes
(BC)
).

Moreover, in each of these cases we have
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}^{\text{ext}}(\lambda)=\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
, but non of these conditions is necessary for Bohr’s theorem.

Let us face completeness.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\lambda
be a frequency. Then
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
is complete if one of the following conditions holds

(1)

L(\lambda)=0
,

(2)

\lambda
is
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent,

(3)

L(\lambda)<\infty
and
\mathcal{D}^{ext}_{\infty}(\lambda)=\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
.

Moreover, non of these conditions is necessary.

For later use we finally
recall that Dirichlet series
D\in\mathcal{D}^{ext}_{\infty}(\lambda)
are approximated by their so-called typical means (see again
[
26
, §3]
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\in\mathcal{D}^{ext}_{\infty}(\lambda)
with extension
f
. Then
the sequence of polynomials

R_{x}(D)(s):=\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\right)e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\,,\,\,x>0

converges uniformly to
f
on
[Re>\varepsilon]
as
x\to\infty
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

3.
Fourier analysis setting

In this section we present a new abstract Fourier analysis approach to general Dirichlet series.
Basically
we restrict ourselves to general Dirichlet series with Dirichlet coefficients which actually are Fourier coefficients of functions on certain compact
abelian groups.
This has several advantages.
One is that the class of all general Dirichlet series simply is too large to obtain a good understanding.
Assuming that the Dirchlet coefficients are Fourier coefficients gives more structure and allows to use tools from harmonic analysis like the Hausdorff-Young inequality or Plancherel’s theorem (among others).
A further advantage of our setting is that Bayart’s
\mathcal{H}_{p}
-theory of ordinary Dirichlet series embeds in a natural way. Whereas the
\mathcal{H}_{p}
-theory of ordinary Dirichlet series is basically Fourier
analysis on the infinite dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
, this group fails to be the right model
for general Dirichlet series. In fact, the Bohr compactification
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
of
\mathbb{R}
and products of
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}
(the dual group of the rationals endowed with the discrete topology) turn out to be suitable substitutes. Finally, fixing some
\lambda
, regarding the different realisations of
\lambda
-Dirichlet series of this type, another feature of our approach is that the
\mathcal{H}_{p}
-theory of general Dirichlet series we intend to present will be independent of the chosen suitable group for
\lambda
.

3.1.
Dirichlet groups

We start by introducing the type of groups we are interested in.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a compact abelian group and
\beta\colon\mathbb{R}\to G
a homomorphism. Then we call the pair
(G,\beta)
a Dirichlet group if
\beta
is continuous and has dense range.

We will frequently use the notion of dual maps of homomorphisms
T\colon G\to H
, where
G
and
H
are locally compact abelian groups. The dual map
\widehat{T}\colon\widehat{H}\to\widehat{G}
is then defined by
\widehat{T}(h):=h\circ T
(see
[
25
, §1.2.1, p.6]
for the definition of the dual group
\widehat{G}
). By Pontryagin’s theorem (see
[
25
, §1.7.2, Theorem, p. 28]
) we have
\widehat{\widehat{T}}=T
, and
\widehat{T}
is continuous if
T
is continuous. Moreover, we need the following fact from the theory of locally compact groups.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
T\colon G\to H
be a continuous homomorphism of locally compact abelian groups. Then
T
is injective if and only if
\widehat{T}
has dense range.

For the sake of completeness we give a proof. Given a locally compact abelian group
G
with unit
e
and a subgroup
U
of
G
, we call
U^{\perp}:=\{\gamma\in\widehat{G}\mid\gamma(u)=e\text{ for all }u\in U\}
the orthogonal group of
U
. Then
{(U^{\perp})}^{\perp}=\overline{U}
, where the closure is taken in
G
(see
[
24
, §1.5.2]
).

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

By definition
\ker\widehat{T}=(\text{Im}T)^{\perp}
, and consequently
(\ker\widehat{T})^{\perp}=\overline{\text{Im}T}
. Using
\widehat{\widehat{T}}=T
gives
\ker T=(\text{Im}\widehat{T})^{\perp}
, and hence
(\ker T)^{\perp}=\overline{\text{Im}\widehat{T}}
which proves the claim.
∎

With this, given a Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
, the dual map of
\beta
given by

\widehat{\beta}\colon\widehat{G}\to\widehat{\mathbb{R}},\penalty\ \penalty\ \gamma\mapsto\gamma\circ\beta

is a monomorphism.
Using
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}
(as topological groups) implies that for every
\gamma
there is a unique
x\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\gamma\circ\beta=e^{-ix\cdot}
. Identifying
\widehat{\beta}(\gamma)=x
we obtain an embedding

\widehat{\beta}\colon\widehat{G}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}.

Another property of Dirichlet groups we are going to use is that they are connected (see the proof of Theorem
4.16
); this is an immediate consequence of the fact that, given a connected set,
its image under a continuous map as well as its closure are again connected.

Observe that every character
e^{-ix\cdot}\in\widehat{\beta}(\widehat{G})
, allows a unique ‘extension’ to
G
, say
h_{x}
, such that
h_{x}\circ\beta=e^{-ix\cdot}
.
Conversely, for every
\gamma\in\widehat{G}
there is some
x\in\mathbb{R}
such that
e^{-ix\cdot}=\gamma\circ\beta
, which proves the following

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(G,\beta)
be a Dirichlet group.
The characters
e^{-ix\cdot}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{T}
on
\mathbb{R}
, where
x\in\widehat{\beta}(\widehat{G})
, are precisely those which allow a continuous ‘extension’
h_{x}
to
G
. In particular

\widehat{G}=\{h_{x}\mid x\in\widehat{\beta}(\widehat{G})\}.

More precisely, we should denote the characters of
G
by
h_{x}^{(G,\beta)}
instead of
h_{x}
, but in most situations this in fact will not be necessary.

Motivated by the equality
G=\widehat{\widehat{G}}
(Pontryagin’s duality theorem) for notational convenience we for all
\omega\in G
write

\omega(x):=h_{x}(\omega),\penalty\ x\in\widehat{\beta}(\widehat{G}).

Let us consider some examples of Dirichlet groups.
As explained, if
(G,\beta)
is a Dirichlet group, then
\widehat{G}\subset\mathbb{R}
(via
\widehat{\beta}
).
In fact, the reverse of this implication holds, which gives a handy criterion to establish a list of main examples of Dirichlet groups. The proof of the following proposition is an immediate consequence of Lemma
3.2
; recall that the dual group
\widehat{G}
carries the discrete topology, since
G
is compact (see
[
25
, §1.2.5, Theorem, p. 9]
).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G
be a compact abelian group. If there is an injective homomorphism
T\colon\widehat{G}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, then
(G,\widehat{T})
is a Dirichlet group.

We write
G_{d}
for a group
G
with the discrete topology
d
. In the following we give four central examples –
and start with the ‘mother’ of all of them.

Example 3.5
.

Let
U
be a subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
. Then the topological group
\widehat{U_{d}}
together with the
mapping

\beta_{\widehat{U_{d}}}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\widehat{U_{d}},\penalty\ \penalty\ t\mapsto\left[u\mapsto e^{-itu}\right]

forms a Dirichlet group. In particular, for
U=\mathbb{Z}
and identifying
\mathbb{T}=\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{d}}
, we
obtain the Dirichlet group
(\mathbb{T},\beta_{\mathbb{T}})
, where

\beta_{\mathbb{T}}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{T},\,t\mapsto e^{-it}.

Example 3.6
.

The compact abelian group
\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\widehat{(\mathbb{R},d)}
is the so-called Bohr compactification
of
\mathbb{R}
which forms a Dirichlet group with the embedding

In Proposition

Example 3.7
.

Let

Example 3.8

Let

In view of the preceding examples we explain in which sense the Bohr compactification

Proposition 3.9
.

For every Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
there is a unique
continuous homomorphism

\pi_{G}:\overline{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow G

with dense range and such that

\beta=\pi_{G}\circ\beta_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}
. Moreover,
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is the unique Dirichlet group with this property up to isomorphisms of groups.

Proof.

We define
\pi_{G}
to be the dual map of
\iota\colon\widehat{G}\to(\mathbb{R},d),\penalty\ \iota(\gamma):=\widehat{\beta}(\gamma)

(identifying
\widehat{(\mathbb{R},|\cdot|)}=(\mathbb{R},d)
). Indeed,
\iota
is injective, and consequently

\pi_{G}:=\widehat{\iota}\colon\overline{\mathbb{R}}\to G
has dense range by Lemma
3.2
(and Pontryagin’s duality theorem).
To see the second statement, assume that
(H,\beta_{H})
is another Dirichlet group with the desired property. Then there are two continuous
homomorphisms
\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}\colon H\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and
\pi_{H}\colon\overline{\mathbb{R}}\to H
with dense range and such that
\beta_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}=\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}\circ\beta_{H}
and
\beta_{H}=\pi_{H}\circ\beta_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}
. This implies

\beta_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}=\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}\circ\pi_{H}\circ\beta_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}
and
\beta_{H}=\pi_{H}\circ\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}\circ\beta_{H}
.
Since
\beta_{H}
and
\beta_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}
both have dense range, we get
\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}\circ\pi_{H}=id_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}
and
\pi_{H}\circ\pi_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}=id_{H}
, hence
H=\overline{\mathbb{R}}
.
∎

We close this section introducing several devices which transport tools from Fourier analysis on
\mathbb{R}
to Fourier analysis on Dirichlet groups.
The first two show how to ’restrict’ functions on

to

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(G,\beta)
be a Dirichlet group, and
f\in C(G)
. Then

\int_{G}f(\omega)d\omega=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}f(\beta(t))dt\,.

In particular, for every polynomial

\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}h_{x_{n}}
with
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in\hat{\beta}(\widehat{G})
and
1\leq p<\infty

\int_{G}\bigg|\sum^{N}_{n=1}a_{n}h_{x_{n}}(\omega)\bigg|^{p}dm(\omega)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}\bigg|\sum^{N}_{n=1}a_{n}e^{-ix_{n}t}\bigg|^{p}dt.

Proof.

Obviously, the second equality in an immediate consequence of the first one.
On the left side of the first equality we have a bounded functional on
C(G)
, and the density of the range of
\beta
implies that the set of all functionals
f\to\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}f(\beta(t))dt,\,T>0
is uniformly bounded on

C(G)
. Then, by the density of the polynomials in
C(G)
(see
[
25
, §8.7.3]
), it suffices to check the desired equality for all characters
h_{x},x\in\widehat{\beta}(\widehat{G})
. For
x=0
the conclusion is trivial, and for
x\neq 0
this follows from the fact that
\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}h_{x}(\beta(t))dt=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}e^{-ixt}dt=0
.
∎

Note that Proposition
3.10
is a reformulation of an important fact of the theory of almost periodic functions.
Recall that a continuous and bounded function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is almost periodic if and only if it has a continuous
extension

to
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(see
[

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}f(t)dt=\int_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}F(\omega)dm(\omega).

To deduce a similar result for integrable functions one should
have in mind that
\beta(\mathbb{R})
may be a null set in
G
, hence the restriction of
some function

to the subgroup
\beta(\mathbb{R})
of

in fact may equal
0
. Indeed, choosing the Dirichlet group

with
\beta(t):=e^{-it}
we have

, which is obviously not a null set in
\mathbb{T}
.
But, if

(Example

is a null set in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
(see

or

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
(G,\beta)
be a Dirichlet group and
f\colon G\to\mathbb{C}
be measurable. Then
f_{\omega}:=f(\omega\beta(\cdot))\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is measurable for almost all
\omega\in G
. If, additionally,

f\in L_{\infty}(G)
, then

f_{\omega}\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\|f_{\omega}\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
for almost all
\omega\in G
.
Moreover, for every
f\in L_{1}(G)
and
g\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
the convolution

g*f_{\omega}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\omega\beta(t-y))g(y)dy

is almost everywhere defined on
\mathbb{R}
and measurable.

Proof.

Denote by
\mu
the product measure of the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
and the Haar measure
m
on
\mathbb{R}\times G
.
Since the map

(4)

F\colon\mathbb{R}\times G\to\mathbb{C},\penalty\ \penalty\ (t,\omega)\mapsto f(\omega\beta(t))

is measurable,
f_{\omega}
is measurable on
\mathbb{R}
for almost every
\omega\in G
by the Fubini-Tonelli theorem.

For the proof of the second statement assume that
f\in L_{\infty}(G)
.
We claim that
F\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times G)
and
\|F\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
, which clearly finishes the argument. Let
C>0
and
N\subset G
be a null set such that
|f(\omega)|\leq C
for all
\omega\in G\setminus N
. Since the map

M\colon\mathbb{R}\times G\to G,\,\,M(t,\omega):=\beta(t)\omega

is continuous, the set
\widetilde{N}:=M^{-1}(N)
is measurable. For any fixed
y\in\mathbb{R}
the set
\widetilde{N}_{y}:=N\beta(y)^{-1}=\{\omega\in G\mid\omega\beta(y)\in N\}
is measurable. Since
m
is translation invariant, we still have
m(\widetilde{N}_{y})=0
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore
\mu(\widetilde{N})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}m(\widetilde{N}_{y})d\lambda(y)=0
. By definition,
|F(y,\omega)|\leq C
for all
(y,\omega)\notin\widetilde{N}
. Hence
F\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times G)
and
\|F\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
.

It remains to show the third statement, i.e. there is a null set
N\subset G
such that for all
\omega\notin N
the function
g*f_{\omega}
is defined almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}
and measurable. Take the Poisson kernel
P_{1}(t):=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{1+t^{2}}
on
\mathbb{R}
. Then the map

F(\omega,t,y):=g(y)f(\omega\beta(t-y))P_{1}(t)\colon G\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}

is measurable, and since the Haar measure
m
on
G
is translation invariant (and
\|P_{1}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}=1
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{G}|F(\omega,t,y)|dm(\omega)dtdy

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|f\|_{L_{1}(G)}.

So by the Fubini-Tonelli theorem there is a null set
N\subset G
such that the map

\omega\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(y)f(\omega\beta(t-y))P_{1}(t)dydt

is defined on
G\setminus N
and measurable on
G
. Let
\omega\in G\setminus N
. Since

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(y)f(\omega\beta(t-y))P_{1}(t)dydt<\infty,

another application of the Fubini-Tonelli theorem implies that the map

t\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(y)f(\omega\beta(t-y))P_{1}(t)dy=(g*f_{\omega})(t)P_{1}(t)

is defined for almost all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and measurable. So multiplying with the measurable function
t\mapsto P_{1}^{-1}(t)
we obtain that

g*f_{\omega}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(y)f_{\omega}(t-y)dy

is defined almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}
and measurable.
∎

The second device allows to interpret functions in

Lemma 3.12

Let

The following definition is essential.

Definition 3.13

Let
G
be a compact abelian group
G
,
E\subset\widehat{G}
, and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then we denote
the subspace of all

which have Fourier transforms

supported by

(i.e.

Note that

is closed by continuity of the Fourier transform,
and hence it is a Banach space. Additionally we define

as the subspace of all regular and bounded complex Borel measures

\widehat{\mu}(\gamma)\neq 0

We need the following two tools (Proposition

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
G
be a compact abelian group and
E\subset\widehat{G}
. Then
Pol_{E}(G)
is dense in
(H_{p}^{E}(G),\|\cdot\|_{p})
, and dense in
H_{\infty}^{E}(G)
with respect to the weak star topology
w(L_{\infty}(G),L_{1}(G))
.

Proof.

Let
f\in H_{p}^{E}(G)
. We claim, that there is a sequence
(u_{n})\subset L_{\infty}(G)
such that
(f*u_{n})
converges to
f
with respect to the norm of
H_{p}^{E}(G)
. Then, since all
f*u_{n}\in C(G)
, using the fact that every continuous function in
H_{p}^{E}(G)
can be approximated with respect to the sup-norm on
G
by polynomials in
Pol_{E}(G)
(see
[
25
, §8.7.3]
), the claim follows. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since the translation map
T\colon G\to L_{p}(G),\penalty\ y\mapsto f_{y}:=f(y-\cdot)
is uniformly continuous, there is a zero-neighborhood
V
, such that
\|f-f_{y}\|_{p}<\varepsilon
for all
y\in V
. Let
m
be the Haar measure of
G
. Then
m(V)>0
and
u_{V}:=\frac{1}{m(V)}\chi_{V}\in L_{\infty}(G)
with
\|u_{V}\|_{1}=1
. Since

(f*u_{V})(x)-f(x)=\int_{G}(f(x-y)-f(x))u_{V}(y)\penalty\ dy,

we obtain with Minkowski’s inequality

\displaystyle\|f-f*u_{V}\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{G}\left|\int_{G}(f(x-y)-f(x))u_{V}(y)dy\right|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}\left(\int_{G}\left|(f(x-y)-f(x))u_{V}(y)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\|f-f_{y}\|_{p}|u_{V}(y)|dy\leq\varepsilon.

Further
\widehat{f*u_{V}}=\widehat{f}\,\,\widehat{u_{V}}
, which implies, that
f*u_{V}\in H_{p}^{E}(G)
.
Finally, for the case
p=\infty
note that by the bipolar theorem

\overline{Pol_{E}(G)}^{w^{*}}=\left(Pol_{E}(G)^{\circ_{L^{1}}}\right)^{\circ_{L^{\infty}}}.

Then a direct calculation shows that
Pol_{E}(G)^{\circ_{L_{1}}}=H_{1}^{G\setminus E}(G)
and
\left(H_{1}^{G\setminus E}(G)\right)^{\circ_{L_{\infty}}}=H_{\infty}^{E}(G)
which finishes the proof.
∎

.

Let

(5)

Since

, where we checked that
(G/\ker\phi)^{\widehat{}}=(\ker\phi)^{\perp}=\overline{\text{Im}\widehat{\phi}}=\text{Im}\widehat{\phi}
, since
\widehat{H}
carries the discrete
topology), and hence it suffices to show that
for every
K\subset\operatorname{Im}\widehat{\phi}

H_{p}^{K}(G)=H_{p}^{\iota(K)}(G/\ker\phi)\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,M_{K}(G)=M_{\iota(K)}(G/\ker\phi)\,.

Let
\pi:G\to G/\ker\phi
be the canonical surjection.
Since the Haar measure
m_{\pi}
of
G_{1}/\ker\phi
is the pushforward measure of
\pi
, the map

L_{p}(G/\ker\phi)\to L_{p}(G),\penalty\ \penalty\ f\mapsto f\circ\pi

for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
defines an into isometry. In particular, fixing
K\subset\operatorname{Im}\widehat{\phi}
, we obtain the isometric mapping

(10)

A_{p}\colon H_{p}^{\iota(K)}(G/\ker\phi)\to H_{p}^{K}(G),\penalty\ \penalty\ f\mapsto f\circ\pi.

Since
Pol_{K}(G)
is dense in
H_{p}^{K}(G)
(Proposition
3.14
), we have that
A_{p}
is onto for all
1\leq p<\infty
. For
p=\infty
let
g\in H_{\infty}^{K}(G)
. Then, since
H_{\infty}^{K}(G)\subset H_{p}^{K}(G)
for all
1\leq p<\infty
, by what we have proved so far, there is
f\in\bigcap_{1\leq p<\infty}H_{p}^{\iota(K)}(G/\ker\phi)
such that
\|f\|_{p}=\|g\|_{p}\leq\|g\|_{\infty}
for all
1\leq p<\infty
. Hence,
\lim_{p\to\infty}\|f\|_{p}\leq\|g\|_{\infty}
, and so
f\in H_{\infty}^{\iota(K)}(G/\ker\phi)
,
A_{\infty}(f)=g
and
\|f\|_{\infty}=\lim_{p\to\infty}\|f\|_{p}\leq\|g\|_{\infty}
. This completes the
proof of (
8
).

For the proof of (
9
) we use, given a compact abelian group
L
and
F\subset\widehat{L}
, the notation

C(L)^{\prime}_{F}:=\{\varphi\in C(L)^{\prime}\colon\varphi(\gamma)\neq 0\Rightarrow\gamma\in F\}\,.

Then by the Riesz representation theorem
M_{F}(L)=C(L)^{\prime}_{F}
.

Fix again some
K\subset\operatorname{Im\widehat{\phi}}
.
Restricting the map
A_{\infty}
in (
10
) to continuous functions, we conclude that the map

B_{\infty}\colon C_{\iota(K)}(G/\ker\phi)\to C_{K}(G),\penalty\ \penalty\ f\mapsto f\circ\pi

is also an onto isometry. Now consider the mapping

\Phi\colon C(G)^{\prime}_{K}\to C(G/\ker\phi)^{\prime}_{K},\penalty\ \penalty\ \varphi\mapsto\varphi\circ B_{\infty}.

Then
\Phi
is defined and isometric; indeed, since
\varphi
is supported on
K
, we have

\|\Phi(\varphi)\|=\sup_{g\in C(G/\ker\phi),\penalty\ \|g\|_{\infty}=1}|\varphi(g\circ\pi)|=\sup_{f\in C_{K}(G),\penalty\ \|f\|_{\infty}=1}|\varphi(f)|=\|\varphi\|.

To prove surjectivity choose
\mu\in M(G)
with
\|\mu\|=1
and
\widehat{\mu}=\chi_{\operatorname{Im\widehat{\phi}}}
, where
\chi_{\operatorname{Im}\widehat{\phi}}
denotes the characteristic function on
\operatorname{Im}\widehat{\phi}

(Lemma
3.16
). Then, given
\eta\in C(G/\ker\phi)^{\prime}_{K}
, the functional

\varphi\colon C(G)\to\mathbb{C},\varphi(f):=\eta(f*\mu)
belongs to
C(G)^{\prime}_{K}
and
\Phi(\varphi)=\eta
(using again
C_{\iota(K)}(G/\ker\phi)=C_{\operatorname{Im}\widehat{\phi}}(G)
via
B_{\infty}
). So
\Phi
is an onto isometry, and now applying the Riesz representation theorem, the mapping
\Psi:=\Phi^{-1}
fulfils the claim.
∎

3.3.
Hardy spaces over
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
-Dirichlet groups

The following notion will turn out to be fundamental for our purposes.

Definition 3.18
.

Let
\lambda
be a frequency and
(G,\beta)
a Dirichlet group. Then
G
is called
\lambda
-Dirichlet group whenever
\lambda\subset\widehat{\beta}(\widehat{G})
.

So for instance for ordinary Dirichlet series,
\lambda_{n}=\log(n)
, the groups
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
and
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
are
\log(n)
-Dirichlet groups. But more can be said.

Example 3.19
.

From Example
3.6
we immediately deduce that the pair
(\overline{\mathbb{R}},\beta_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}})

forms a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group for any frequency
\lambda
.

We explain in Section
3.5
that for every frequency
\lambda
there is a suitable map
T_{\lambda}
such that
(\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{\infty},T_{\lambda})
is a
\lambda
-Dirichlet groups, which can be seen as an analog of
(\mathbb{T}^{\infty},\beta_{\mathbb{T}^{\infty}})
from the ordinary case
(see Example
3.7
). Further in Section
3.6
we define classes of frequencies
\lambda
(natural and integer type) for which
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
is a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group (including
\lambda=(\log n)
).

Let
(G,\beta)
be a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then

E=\{h_{\lambda_{n}}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\widehat{G}
, and we define

H_{p}^{\lambda}\left(G\right):=H_{p}^{E}(G).

So
H_{p}^{\lambda}\left(G\right)
is the Banach space of all functions
f\in L_{p}(G)
such that
\widehat{f}(\gamma)\neq 0
only if
\gamma=h_{\lambda_{n}}
for some
n
. Note that, the definition of
H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
also depends on the choice of
\beta
, although our notation does not indicate this.

Theorem 3.20
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
(G,\beta)
a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group. Then

H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)=H_{p}^{\lambda}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})\,\,\,\text{ isometrically}.

More precisely,
there is an onto isometry
\Psi\colon H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)\to H_{p}^{\lambda}(\overline{\mathbb{R}}),f\mapsto g
such that
\widehat{f}(h^{(G,\beta)}_{\lambda_{n}})=\widehat{g}(h^{(\overline{\mathbb{R}},\beta_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}})}_{\lambda_{n}})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

The result is immediate from Proposition
3.9
and Proposition
3.17
.
∎

Corollary 3.21
.

Let
\lambda
be a frequency and
(G,\beta_{G})
and
(H,\beta_{H})
two
\lambda
-Dirichlet groups.
Then

H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)=H_{p}^{\lambda}(H)\,\,\,\text{ isometrically},

where, if
f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
and
g\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(H)

are associated to each other, we have that
\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}}^{(G,\beta_{G})})=\widehat{g}(h_{\lambda_{n}}^{(H,\beta_{H})})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the next result we show that every
f\in H_{\infty}^{\lambda}(G)
in a natural way defines holomorphic functions on the open right half plane; in Section
4.3
we take advantages of this important feature.

Proposition 3.22
.

Let
\lambda
be an arbitrary frequency and
f\in H_{\infty}^{\lambda}(G)
. Then for almost all
\omega\in G
the function

F_{\omega}\colon[Re>0]\to\mathbb{C}\,,\,\,\,F_{\omega}(u+it):=(f_{\omega}*P_{u})(t)

defines a holomorphic function on
[Re>0]
which is bounded by
\|f\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.11
we know that
f_{\omega}\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
for almost all
\omega\in G
(say, for all
\omega
not contained in a null set
M\subset G
). So
F_{\omega}
defines a continuous function on
[Re>0]
(actually a harmonic function on
[Re>0]
, see
[
19
]
). For polynomials
g=\sum_{n=1}^{N}b_{n}e^{-i\lambda_{n}\cdot}
we have that
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
u>0
by Abel summation

\mathcal{F}_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\left(e^{-u|\cdot|}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<\cdot}b_{n}\right)(x)=\frac{1}{u+ix}\sum_{n=1}^{N}b_{n}e^{-(u+ix)\lambda_{n}}=\frac{(g*P_{u})(x)}{u+ix}\,.

Since the right hand side (as a function of
x
) is in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we apply the Fourier transform on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and obtain

(11)

\mathcal{F}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\frac{g*P_{u}}{u+i\cdot}\right)(-\cdot)=e^{-u|\cdot|}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<\cdot}b_{n}\,\,\,\text{in $L_{2}(\mathbb{R})$}.

Now we want to apply this formula for
f_{\omega}
. Therefore fix
u>0
and consider the mapping

\Psi_{u}:L_{2}(G)\to L_{2}(G,L_{2}(\mathbb{R})),\,\,g\mapsto\Big[\omega\mapsto\frac{g_{\omega}*P_{u}}{u+i\cdot}\Big]\,.

This operator is well-defined and bounded. Since the sequence
f^{N}:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}h_{\lambda_{n}}
converges to
f
in
L_{2}(G)
, we obtain some subsequence
(N_{k})_{k}
such that for almost all
\omega\in G
, say
\omega\notin N_{u}\cup M
, where
N_{u}\subset G
is a null set,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{f^{N_{k}}_{\omega}*P_{u}}{u+i\cdot}=\frac{f_{\omega}*P_{u}}{u+i\cdot}\,\,\,\text{in $L_{2}(\mathbb{R})$}\,.

Then by the continuity of the Fourier transform on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and (
11
) we obtain
for all
\omega\notin N_{u}\cup M

(12)

\mathcal{F}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\frac{f_{\omega}*P_{u}}{u+i\cdot}\right)(-\cdot)=e^{-u|\cdot|}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<\cdot}a_{n}\omega(\lambda_{n})\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R})\,.

In particular for all
u>0
and
\omega\notin N_{u}\cup M

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-2u|\cdot|}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<\cdot}a_{n}\omega(\lambda_{n})\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2ux}\left|\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}\omega(\lambda_{n})\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\left(\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}\omega(\lambda_{n})\right)e^{-ux}\right|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2u|x|}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty,

hence
e^{-u|\cdot|}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<\cdot}a_{n}\omega(\lambda_{n})\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
for all
\omega\notin N_{u}\cup M
. Now we write
\mathbb{Q}_{+}:=\mathbb{Q}\cap]0,\infty[=\{q_{1},q_{2},\ldots\}=(q_{n})_{n}
and choosing
u_{n}=q_{n}
we obtain a null set
N:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}N_{q_{n}}\subset G
such that
e^{-u|\cdot|}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<\cdot}a_{n}\omega(\lambda_{n})\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
for all
u\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
and
\omega\notin N\cup M
. So for all
\omega\notin N\cup M
the function

g_{\omega}:[\text{Re}>0]\to\mathbb{C}\,,\,\,g_{\omega}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}\omega(\lambda_{n})\right)e^{-zx}dx

is well-defined and holomorphic. Now together with (
12
) we obtain for all
u\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
and
\omega\notin N\cup M

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}\omega(\lambda_{n})\Big)e^{-(u+i\cdot)x}dx=\mathcal{F}_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\left(e^{-u|\cdot|}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<\cdot}a_{n}\omega(\lambda_{n})\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\left(e^{-u|\cdot|}\sum_{\lambda_{n}<\cdot}a_{n}\omega(\lambda_{n})\right)=\mathcal{F}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\mathcal{F}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\frac{f_{\omega}*P_{u}}{u+i\cdot}\right)(-\cdot)\right)=\frac{f_{\omega}*P_{u}}{u+i\cdot}.

So finally for all
u\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
and for all
\omega\notin N\cup M
and almost all
t

(13)

\frac{F_{\omega}(u+it)}{u+it}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}\omega(\lambda_{n})\Big)e^{-(u+it)x}dx=g_{\omega}(u+it)\,.

Since both sides are continuous functions, equation (
13
) holds on
[Re>0]
. So since

F_{\omega}(z)=zg_{\omega}(z)\,\,\text{on $[\text{Re}>0]$}\,,

F_{\omega}
is indeed holomorphic on the open right half plane for all
\omega\notin N\cup M
.
∎

3.4.
Hardy spaces of general Dirichlet series

We now come to the actual goal of this work.
Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
(G,\beta)
be a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group, and consider the following map which we call Bohr map:

\mathcal{B}\colon H^{\lambda}_{p}(G)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}(\lambda),\,\,\,f\sim\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat{G}}\widehat{f}(\gamma)\gamma\,\,\,\mapsto\,\,\,\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})e^{-\lambda_{n}s}.

The following definition is at the very heart of this work.

Definition 3.23
.

The Hardy space
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
of
\lambda
-Dirichlet series
is the space
of all
\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
for which there is some

f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
such that
a_{n}=\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})
for all
n
.

Together with
the norm
\|D\|_{p}:=\|f\|_{p}
the space
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
clearly forms a Banach space, and then by definition the Bohr map
\mathcal{B}
gives an isometric onto isomorphism
from
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
onto
H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
.
The following immediate consequence of Corollary
3.21
is crucial.

Theorem 3.24
.

\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
is independent of the chosen
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
.

Recall that in the ordinary case
\lambda:=(\log n)
, the groups
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
and
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
are suitable
(\log n)
-Dirichlet groups, which immediately leads to the following consequence.

Corollary 3.25
.

H_{p}^{\log(n)}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})=\mathcal{H}_{p}(\log(n))=H_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty}).

So the above definition of
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
actually coincides with Bayart’s definition of
\mathcal{H}_{p}
in the ordinary case (see
[
3
]
).

Dealing with concrete problems in
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
it suffices to restrict ourselves thinking on
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
(or
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{\infty}
, see Section
3.5
) instead of considering an ’abstract’ Dirichlet group
G
. The choice of the group may depend then on the problem we are interested in. Of course considering particular frequencies like
\lambda=(n)
or
\lambda=(\log n)
it has advantages to work with the torus instead of
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
due to its connection to complex analysis (on products of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
).

Note that now there are two ‘
H_{\infty}
-spaces of
\lambda
-Dirichlet series’ around, namely
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(\lambda)
. In Section
4.3
we show that they coincide if
\mathcal{D}^{ext}_{\infty}(\lambda)=\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
and
L(\lambda)<\infty
(Theorem
4.12
), which answers the question posed in the introduction.

We close this section by giving an internal description of
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
through
\lambda
-Dirichlet polynomials without considering Dirichlet groups. We denote by
Pol(\lambda)
the space of all
\lambda
-Dirichlet polynomials
D=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
. For such polynomials we define

\|D\|_{p}:=\left(\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}\left|\sum^{N}_{n=1}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}it}\right|^{p}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,,

where due to Proposition
3.10
this limit exists and gives a norm on
Pol(\lambda)
. Then the following result is an immediate consequence of
Proposition
3.14
.

Theorem 3.26
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\lambda
be a frequency. Then the space
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
is the completion of
(Pol(\lambda),\|\cdot\|_{p})
.

3.5.
Several variables

As already mentioned in Corollary
3.25
, in the ordinary case
\lambda=(\log n)
the
\mathcal{H}_{p}
-spaces coincide with the Hardy space
H_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
which consists of functions in infinitely many variables. On the other hand by Theorem
4.12
ordinary Dirichlet series can be understood in terms of the ’one dimensional’ object
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. So the (vague) question might arise where are all the variables gone in the general case?

Recall the following notions going back to Bohr (see
[
7
, §5]
). An infinite matrix
R:=(r^{n}_{k})_{n,k\in\mathbb{N}}
of rational numbers is called Bohr matrix
whenever each row
R_{n}=(r_{k}^{n})_{k}
is finite, i.e.
r^{n}_{k}\neq 0
for only finitely many
k
’s.
Given a sequence
\lambda:=(\lambda_{n})
of real numbers, a sequence
B:=(b_{n})
in
\mathbb{R}
is said to be a basis for
\lambda
if it is
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent and for each
n
there is a finite sequence
(r^{n}_{k})_{k}
of rational coefficients
such that
\lambda_{n}=\sum_{k}r^{n}_{k}b_{k}
. In this case,
R:=(r^{n}_{k})_{n,k}
is said to be a Bohr matrix of
\lambda
with respect to the basis
B
. If
\lambda
is a frequency, such a basis always exists
(in fact it can be chosen as a subsequence of
\lambda
), and if
R
is the associated Bohr matrix
R
, we write
\lambda=(R,B)
.

Note that
(\log p_{j})
is a basis of
\lambda=(\log n)
, where
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{j})
is the sequence of prime numbers. In this case all finite sequences of natural numbers form the rows of

R
.

We come back to Example
3.8
within the setting of
\lambda
-Dirichlet groups.

Example 3.27
.

Let
B:=(b_{n})
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent sequence of length

N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
.
Then for every frequency
\lambda=(R,B)
we have that
\big(\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{N},\widehat{T_{B}}\big)
is a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group, where
T_{B}
is the mapping defined in (
3
).

On the other hand, if
R
is a Bohr matrix and all rows
R_{n}
have length
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, then obviously each of these rows belongs to the dual of
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{N}
,
and we denote for
1\leq p\leq\infty
the corresponding Hardy space (see Definition
3.13
) by

H^{R}_{p}\big(\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{N}\big)\,.

Then Example
3.27
and Theorem
3.20
show that in this case the three objects
H_{p}^{R}\big(\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{N}\big)
,
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
, and
H_{p}^{\lambda}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})

can not be distinguished in terms of Fourier- and Dirichlet coefficients.

Theorem 3.28
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
R
be a Bohr matrix. Then for for every frequency
\lambda=(R,B)
with a basis of length
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}

H_{p}^{R}\big(\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{N}\big)=\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)=H_{p}^{\lambda}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})\,\,\,\text{isometrically}\,.

More precisely, there are unique onto isometries between these spaces such that if
F
,
D
, and
f
are associated to each other, then
for each
n

\widehat{F}(R_{n})=a_{n}(D)=\widehat{f}(\lambda_{n})\,.

Thinking of multi indices as rows in the Bohr matrix of
(\log n)
the Hardy type space
H_{p}^{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{N})
could be considered as the ’multi variable’ substitute of
H_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
in the general case. The difference of
H_{p}^{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{N})
and
H_{p}^{\lambda}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})
lies in the kind of perspective: Either the focus lies on the frequency
\lambda
itself or on its coefficients with respect to some decomposition
(R,B)
. Calculations are sometimes easier in
H_{p}^{\lambda}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})
than in
H_{p}^{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{N})
, since we do not have to deal with ’infinitely many variables’. On the other hand the spaces
H_{p}^{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{N})
decompose the frequency with respect to the basis (’separates the variables’), and they are independent of a chosen basis for
\lambda
. Moreover
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{N}
is metrizable (since its dual group is countable).

An immediate consequence of Theorem
3.28
is the following (which may be is not immediate considering
H_{p}^{\lambda}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})
).

Corollary 3.29
.

Assume that
1\leq p\leq\infty
and that
\lambda=(R,B)
and
\widetilde{\lambda}=(\widetilde{R},\widetilde{B})
are two frequencies. If there is a permutation
\phi\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\widetilde{R_{n}}=R_{\phi(n)}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, that is
\lambda
and
\widetilde{\lambda}
have the same Bohr matrix without regard to the order of the rows, then
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)=\mathcal{H}_{p}(\widetilde{\lambda})
(as Banach spaces) and the coefficient are preserved with respect to
\phi
.

For instance choosing
\lambda:=(\log 2^{n})
and
\widetilde{\lambda}=(\log 3^{n})
, then Corollary
3.29
implies
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)=H_{\infty}(\mathbb{T})=\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\widetilde{\lambda})
(which of course can be deduced directly).

3.6.
Frequencies of integer type

In this section we consider frequencies which allow a decomposition
(R,B)
such that the Bohr matrix
R
consists exclusively of integers or natural numbers. We call a Bohr matrix of integers or natural numbers full, if
any possible finite sequence of integers or numbers in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
appears as a row. This includes the Bohr matrix
R
generated by the ordinary case
\lambda=(\log n)
and its basis
B=(\log p_{n})
.
If
\alpha
is a row of
R
, then we shortly write
\alpha\in R
. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
we keep to the standard denoting by
\mathcal{H}_{p}
the Hardy space of ordinary Dirichlet series.

Definition 3.30
.

We call a frequency
\lambda
of integer (resp. natural) type if there is a basis
B
for
\lambda
such that the Bohr matrix
R
only consists of integer (resp. natural) numbers.

So for these frequencies
\lambda=(R,B)
of integer type the group
\mathbb{T}^{N}
is a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(use Example
3.7
), where
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
is the length of the basis
B
, and we obtain the following

Theorem 3.31
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
\lambda=(R,B)
a frequency of integer type with basis of length
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Then there is a unique onto isometry

\psi\colon\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)\to H_{p}^{R}(\mathbb{T}^{N}),\penalty\ \penalty\ D\mapsto F

such that
\hat{F}(\alpha)=a_{n}(D)
for all multi indices
\alpha\in R
and
\lambda_{n}=\sum\alpha_{j}b_{j}
.

Theorem
3.31
also implies that the ordinary
\mathcal{H}_{p}
is in the following sense the ’biggest’ Hardy type space of natural type.

Corollary 3.32
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
\lambda=(R,B)
a frequency of natural type. Then there is a unique into isometry

\psi\colon\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{p},\penalty\ \penalty\ \sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\to\sum b_{n}n^{-s}

such that
a_{n}=b_{\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}}
for all
\alpha\in R
, where
\lambda_{n}=\sum\alpha_{j}b_{j}
.

So, if
\lambda
is of integer or natural type, then the group
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
is appropriate. The following remark can be seen as a sort of converse.

Remark 3.33
.

If
(\mathbb{T}^{\infty},\beta)
is a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group, then
\lambda
is of integer type.

Proof.

By assumption and Lemma
3.2
there is a monomorphism
T\colon\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\lambda\subset\text{Im}\penalty\ T
. Denoting by
e_{k}
the
k
th unit vectors in
\oplus_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{Z}
the sequence
(T(e_{k}))_{k}
is
\mathbb{Z}
-linearly independent and
\lambda\subset\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(T(e_{k}))_{k}
.
∎

3.7.
On Bohr’s problem

A prominent problem in Bohr’s time was to determine the maximal width of the strip of uniform but non absolutely convergence

S(\lambda):=\sup_{D\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda)}\sigma_{a}(D)-\sigma_{u}(D).

Note that we always have
S(\lambda)\leq\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}
by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. In the ordinary case, where
L(\log(n))=1
, a celebrated result is that
S(\log(n))=\frac{1}{2}
(see
[
4
, §5, Theorem V]
,
[
9
]
,
[
16
]
, or
[
24
]
).
So
the question may arise whether
S(\lambda)=\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}
always holds. But this is in general false due to a result of Neder
(see
[
23
, §4]
, actually
\sigma_{b}^{ext}
, see
[
26
]
for definition, is considered instead of
\sigma_{u}
, but Neder’s construction can easily be modified for
\sigma_{u}
).

Proposition 3.34
.

To every
x>0
and
0\leq y\leq\frac{x}{2}
there is a frequency
\lambda
such that
L(\lambda)=x
and
S(\lambda)=y
.

If
\lambda
satisfies Bohr’s theorem, then we may reformulate
S(\lambda)
as follows

S(\lambda)=\sup_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)}\sigma_{a}(D),

which actually in a sense is one of the key steps for proving
S((\log n))=\frac{1}{2}
.
This motivates to define (like already done in the ordinary case, see
[
9
, §12.2]
) the following ’
p
-version’ of
S(\lambda)
:

S_{p}(\lambda):=\sup_{D\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)}\sigma_{a}(D).

Note that
S_{p}(\lambda)
is decreasing in
p
and that
S_{1}(\lambda)\leq L(\lambda)
. In the particular case
p=2
we have
S_{2}(\lambda)=\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}
for all
\lambda
’s. Indeed, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives
S_{2}(\lambda)\leq\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}
, and conversely
D_{\varepsilon}(s)=\sum e^{-\left(\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}+\varepsilon\right)\lambda_{n}}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\in\mathcal{H}_{2}(\lambda)
satisfies
\sigma_{a}(D_{\varepsilon})=\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}-\varepsilon
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

Given
\lambda
, the exact value of
S_{p}(\lambda)
seems hard to determine. But at least if
\lambda
is of full natural type, then we will show that

S(\lambda)=\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}
as a consequence of Theorem
3.32
. For
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent frequencies see Corollary
3.36
.

Corollary 3.35
.

Let
\lambda=(R,B)
be a frequency of natural type, where
R
is full. If
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(n)}{\lambda_{n}}
exists, then

S_{p}(\lambda)=\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Further, if
\lambda
satisfies
(LC)
and
L(\lambda)<\infty
, then there is a Dirichlet series
D\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
with
\sigma_{a}(D)=\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}
and
S(\lambda)=\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}
.

Maximal width of uniform and but non absolute convergence of Dirichlet series for frequencies
\lambda
as in the previous corollary were also studied in the recent article
[
8
]
. There the authors
look at the special case
S(\lambda)=S_{\infty}(\lambda)
assuming
(BC)
, but without assuming that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(n)}{\lambda_{n}}
exists. Their reasoning is based on the fact the set
\operatorname{mon}H_{\infty}(B_{c_{0}})
of monomial convergence is included in
\ell_{2+\varepsilon}\cap\mathbb{D}^{\mathbb{N}}
for all
\varepsilon>0
, which is basically a consequence of a probabilistic argument (Kahane-Zygmund inequality). Their proof extends word by word for
\lambda
’s satisfying
(LC)
. Using the fact that
\operatorname{mon}H_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})=\ell_{2}\cap\mathbb{D}^{\mathbb{N}}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)=H_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
(see
[
9
, §12.4.2]
) the same argument gives
S_{p}(\lambda)=\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}
without any assumption on the
\lambda
’s. We feel that our proof of Corollary
3.35
(under the stronger assumption that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(n)}{\lambda_{n}}
exists) is more elementary.

Proof of Corollary
3.35
.

By Corollary
3.32
we have that
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)=\mathcal{H}_{p}((\log n))
, where the isometry preserves the Dirichlet coefficients. Then by assumption for all
\varepsilon>0
there is
n_{0}
such that
\lambda_{n}(L(\lambda)-\varepsilon)\leq\log(n)\leq\lambda_{n}(L(\lambda)+\varepsilon)
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. From this by an easy calculation we see that
\sigma_{a}(D)=\sigma_{a}(E)L(\lambda)
for all
D\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
and
E\in\mathcal{H}_{p}((\log n))
associated to each other. This gives
S_{p}(\lambda)=L(\lambda)S_{p}((\log n))=\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}
. By Theorem
4.10
and Theorem
4.12
(applied to
(\log n)
) we have
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)\subset\mathcal{D}_{\infty}((\log n))

since
\lambda
is of natural type. Assuming
(LC)
and
L(\lambda)<\infty
we actually have equality (again Theorem
4.12
), and we obtain
S(\lambda)=S((\log n))L(\lambda)=\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}
. Since there is
E\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}((\log n))
with
\sigma_{a}(E)=\frac{1}{2}
(see
[
9
, §4]
), the claim is proven.

∎

Now we consider the case of
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent frequencies
\lambda
. Note that in this case we already know from
[
26
]
that
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(\lambda)=\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)=\ell_{1}(\mathbb{N})
and therefore that
S_{\infty}(\lambda)=S(\lambda)=0
.

Corollary 3.36
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\lambda
be
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent.Then the following isometric equalities hold:

\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)=H^{(1)}_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty}):=\{f\in H_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})\mid\widehat{f}(\alpha)\neq 0\Rightarrow\sum\alpha_{j}=1\}

and

\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)=H^{(1)}_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})\,.

In particular,
S_{p}(\lambda)=\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.32
we know that
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)=\mathcal{B}(H^{(1)}_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty}))=:\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{p}((\log n))
is the space of
1
-homogeneous ordinary Dirichlet series. Since
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{p}((\log n))=\mathcal{H}_{2}((\log n))
isomorphically (by Khinchine’s inequality, see
[
9
, §11.2.2]
) we have
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)=\mathcal{H}_{2}(\lambda)
and so
S_{p}(\lambda)=S_{2}(\lambda)=\frac{L(\lambda)}{2}
.
∎

We close this section by giving an example of
\lambda
which is not of integer type.

Example 3.37
.

Let
(p_{n})
be the sequence of prime numbers and define
\lambda_{1}:=1
,
\lambda_{n}:=\frac{p_{n}^{2}+1}{p_{n}}
,
n\geq 2
. Then clearly
B:=(1,0,\ldots)
is a basis with Bohr matrix

R:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\ldots\\
\frac{2^{2}+1}{2}&0&\ldots\\
\frac{3^{2}+1}{3}&0&\ldots\\
\frac{5^{2}+1}{5}&0&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Assume that
\lambda
is of integer type. Then there is a basis
X=(x,0,\ldots)
of length
1
with an integer Bohr matrix (see
[
7
]
, §5, pp. 121-122). Choose
(k_{n})\subset\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\frac{p_{n}^{2}+1}{p_{n}}=k_{n}\,x

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
x\in\mathbb{Q}
. Now write
x=\frac{a}{b}
, where
a,b\neq 0
. Then

b(p_{n}^{2}+1)=p_{n}k_{n}a.

Since
p_{n}^{2}+1
is not divisible by
p_{n}
, we conclude that
b
is divisible by
p_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
b=0
, a contradiction. Moreover,

\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}=p_{n+1}-p_{n}-\left(\frac{1}{p_{n}}-\frac{1}{p_{n+1}}\right)\geq 1-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\,,

implying that
\lambda
is strictly increasing and satisfies
(BC)
with
l=0
.

4.
Structure theory

Based on the Fourier analysis setting from the preceding section we extend some important cornerstones of the
\mathcal{H}_{p}
-theory of ordinary Dirichlet series to the range of general Dirichlet series.

4.1.
Translations

In this section we fix a frequency
\lambda
, and collect a few independently interesting tools on translations of
\lambda
-Dirichlet series which also later will be important.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
be a
\lambda
-Dirichlet series and
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Then we call

D_{z}(s):=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}

the translation of
D
about
z
.

We call the translation
D_{z}
horizontal if
z\in\mathbb{R}
, and vertical if
z=i\tau
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.
Given a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
and a Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
, we for every
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
have

D_{i\tau}=\sum a_{n}e^{-i\lambda_{n}\tau}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}=\sum a_{n}h_{\lambda_{n}}(\beta(\tau))e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\,.

More generally, we consider so-called vertical limits.

Definition 4.2
.

Given a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(G,\beta)
and a Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
, we call Dirichlet series of the form

D^{\omega}(s)=\sum a_{n}\omega(\lambda_{n})e^{-\lambda_{n}s},\penalty\ \omega\in G

vertical limits of
D
associated to
G
.

In the following we study in which sense
\lambda
-Dirichlet series belonging to
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
are stable under taking vertical limits and horizontal translations.

The first observation shows that the translation invariance of the Haar measure on
G
and the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
immediately imply that all mappings
D\mapsto D^{\omega}

constitute onto isometries on
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(G,\beta)
be a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group,
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
D\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
. Then for

\omega\in G
we have that
D\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
if and only if

D^{\omega}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
, and in this case
and
\|D^{\omega}\|_{p}=\|D\|_{p}
.

The following corollary applies the case
p=\infty
to polynomials.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
(G,\beta)
be a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group. Then for all
\omega\in G
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}\in\mathbb{C}

\sup_{s\in[Re>0]}\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\Big|=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e^{-t\lambda_{n}i}\Big|=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}\omega(\lambda_{n})e^{-t\lambda_{n}i}\Big|\,.

Proof.

Applying Proposition
4.3
to polynomials in
Pol_{\lambda}(G)
(and ’restricting’ them to
\mathbb{R}
) we obtain the second equality. The first equality follows from a standard maximum modulus principle.
∎

Let us prove the analog of Proposition
4.3
for
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(G,\beta)
be a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group,
D:=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
,
and assume that
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
is complete.
Then for all

\omega\in G
we have that
D\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
if and only if

D^{\omega}\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
, and in this case

\|D^{\omega}\|_{\infty}=\|D\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Indeed, by Proposition
2.4

the Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
is approximated by typical means. Hence, given
\omega\in G
and
\varepsilon>0
, by Corollary
4.4
the sequence

\left(\sum_{\lambda_{n}<x}a_{n}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{x}\right)\omega(\lambda_{n})e^{-\varepsilon\lambda_{n}}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\right)_{x\geq 0}

is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
. Then

D^{\omega}_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
and
\|D^{\omega}_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}=\|D_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}
for all
\omega\in G
and
\varepsilon>0
. This
immediately gives
D^{\omega}\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
and
\|D^{\omega}\|_{\infty}=\|D\|_{\infty}
for all
\omega\in G
.
∎

In the rest of this section we justify the chosen name ’vertical limit’.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(G,\beta)
be a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group, and

a
\lambda
-Dirichlet series with

. Then

(1)

For every

there is a sequence

such that

converges to

uniformly on

for all

.

(2)

Assume conversely that for

the vertical translations

converge
uniformly on
[Re>\varepsilon]
for every
\varepsilon>0

to a holomorphic function

on
[Re>0]
. Then there is
\omega\in G
such that

f(s)=D^{\omega}(s)

for all

.

Proof.

(1)
By Pontryagin’s duality theorem we have

(as topological groups), where the right group carries the compact open topology. Hence in the following we interpret
\omega\in G
as a character on
\widehat{G}
. Since
\mathbb{R}
is dense in
G

N\in\mathbb{N}
there is
\tau_{N}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\max_{1\leq j\leq N}|e^{-i\lambda_{j}\tau_{N}}-\omega(\lambda_{j})|\leq\frac{1}{N}.

Hence the sequence
(e^{-i\lambda_{j}\tau_{N}})_{j}
converges to
(\omega(\lambda_{j}))_{j}
in
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
whenever
N
tends to
\infty
.
For each
N
we define
D_{i\tau_{N}}(s):=\sum a_{j}e^{-i\lambda_{j}\tau_{N}}e^{-\lambda_{j}s}
and take
\varepsilon>0
. Then for all

and

we obtain

\displaystyle|D_{i\tau_{N}}(s)-D^{\omega}(s|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|e^{-\lambda_{n}\varepsilon}|e^{-i\lambda_{n}\tau_{N}}-\omega(\lambda_{n})|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}|a_{n}|e^{-\lambda_{n}\varepsilon}+\max_{1\leq n\leq M}|a_{n}|e^{-\lambda_{n}\varepsilon}\sum_{n=1}^{M}|e^{-i\lambda_{n}\tau_{N}}-\omega(\lambda_{n})|.

Since
\sigma_{a}(D)\leq 0
, we may choose
M
and
N
large enough to get the desired conclusion.

(2)
We claim that there is
\omega\in G
and a subsequence
(\tau_{n_{k}})_{k}
such that
(e^{-i\tau_{n_{k}}\lambda_{j}})_{j}
converges to
(\omega(\lambda_{j}))_{j}
in
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
whenever
k
tends to
\infty
.
Then we may argue as in the proof of
(1)
to show that
D_{i\tau_{n_{k}}}
converges to
D^{\omega}
uniformly on
[Re>\varepsilon]
for all
\varepsilon>0
, and consequently
f=D^{\omega}
on
[Re>0]
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}

we define
T_{m}:=\overline{\{\beta(\tau_{n})\mid n\geq m\}}
, where the closure is taken in
G
. Then
\bigcap_{m\in A}T_{m}
for all finite subsets
A\subset\mathbb{N}
, and since
G
is compact, we find some

\omega\in\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{N}}T_{m}\neq\emptyset
. Consider now the following zero neighborhoods in

So for
N=1
there is

such that

, since

. Then for

,

, such that

, since
\omega\in T_{k_{1}}
. Inductively we get a subsequence
(\tau_{n_{k}})_{k}
such that

\max_{1\leq j\leq k}|e^{-i\tau_{n_{k}}\lambda_{j}}-\omega(\lambda_{j})|\leq\frac{1}{k}

for all

. This proves the claim.
∎

4.2.
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)}
equals
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}^{+}_{p}(\lambda)}

The next result shows that a Dirichlet series
D:=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
if and only if all its translations
D_{z},z\in[Re>0]
do with uniformly bounded norms.
For ordinary
\mathcal{H}_{p}
’s this was first proved in
[
10
]
(see also
[
9
, Theorem 11.21]
).

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
D:=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
. Then
D\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
if and only if
D_{z}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
for all
z\in[Re>0]
and
\sup_{z\in[Re>0]}\|D_{z}\|_{p}<\infty
. Moreover, in this case
\sup_{z\in[Re>0]}\|D_{z}\|_{p}=\|D\|_{p}
.

We will give two important applications of this description of
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
in the coming two
sections.

For the proof it is convenient to define the Banach space
\mathcal{H}^{+}_{p}(\lambda)
of all
\lambda
-Dirichlet series such that

\|D\|_{\mathcal{H}^{+}_{p}(\lambda)}:=\sup_{z\in[Re>0]}\|D_{z}\|_{p}<\infty
, and then the preceding equivalence in short tells that
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)=\mathcal{H}^{+}_{p}(\lambda)
holds isometrically.

We show each of the implications of the preceding theorem separately.
The ’if part’ is based on a lemma which relates horizontal translations of Dirichlet series
D\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
(i.e. the Dirichlet coefficients of
D
are Fourier coefficients of a function
f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
) with the convolution of
f
with the Poisson measure
p_{u}
on
G

(see Lemma
3.12
).

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
(G,\beta)
be a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Moreover,
let
f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
and
D\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
with
\mathcal{B}(f)=D
. Then for each
u>0
, we have that
p_{u}*f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
,
D_{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
with

\mathcal{B}(p_{u}*f)=D_{u}
, and
\lim_{u\to 0}D_{u}=D
in
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
(for
p=\infty
with respect to the weak star topology
generated by the duality of
L_{\infty}(G)
and
L_{1}(G)
).

Proof.

Since
\widehat{p_{u}*f}(h_{x})=\widehat{p_{u}}(h_{x})\widehat{f}(h_{x})=e^{-u|x|}\widehat{f}(h_{x})

for all
x\in\widehat{\beta}(\widehat{G})
, we have
p_{u}*f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
with
\|p_{u}*f\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}
, and also
\mathcal{B}(p_{u}*f)=D_{u}
. For the second statement notice that
\lim_{u\to 0}p_{u}*f=f
, where the limit for

1\leq p<\infty
is taken in
L_{p}(G)
, and for
p=\infty
in the weak star topology (check for polynomials, then use density).
∎

Proof of the ’if part’ of Theorem
4.7
.

Proposition
4.3
implies that
\|D_{it}\|_{p}=\|D\|_{p}

(choose
\omega=\beta(t)
). Then together with Lemma
4.8

\sup_{z\in[Re>0]}\|D_{z}\|_{p}=\sup_{u>0}\|D_{u}\|_{p}=\|D\|_{p}\,.\qed

For the proof of the ’only if’ part of Theorem
4.7
we follow an idea which was invented in
[
10
]
.
Given a Banach space
X
, we denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda,X)
the space of all
\lambda
-Dirichlet series
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
, which have coefficients
a_{n}\in X
and which converge and define a bounded (and then necessarily holomorphic) function on
[Re>0]
with values in
X
. Together with the supremum norm this
gives a Banach space provided
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
is complete (see
[
12
]
).

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
, and assume that
\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
is complete. Then the map

\Phi\colon\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda,\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)),\penalty\ \penalty\ \sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\mapsto\sum\left(a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\right)e^{-\lambda_{n}z}

is an into isometry.

In the ordinary case this was proved in
[
10
, Theorem 2.4]
(see also
[
9
, Section 11.3]
), and substituting
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
by any
\lambda
-Dirichlet group
(G.\beta)
our proof follows the same strategy.

Proof.

We consider a
\lambda
-Dirichlet polynomial

D=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
. Then, by the ’if part’ of Theorem
4.7
,
the map

F\colon[Re>0]\to\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda),\penalty\ \penalty\ z\mapsto\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}

is holomorphic and
\|F\|_{\infty}=\|D\|_{p}
. Hence
\Phi
, defined as above
on polynomials only,
is isometric. Let
\Phi
be the unique extension of this isometry to all of
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)

(Theorem
3.26
). We claim that this is the map
\Phi
from the lemma. Indeed,
given
D\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
, let
(D^{N})_{N}
be a sequence of Dirichlet polynomials which in
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
converges to
D
. We write
\Phi(D)=\sum b_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}
, where
b_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
. Then the continuity of
\Phi
implies
b_{n}=\lim_{N}a_{n}(\Phi(D^{N}))=\lim_{N}a_{n}(D^{N})e^{-\lambda_{n}s}=a_{n}(D)e^{-\lambda_{n}s},

the conclusion.
∎

Now we are prepared for the

Proof of the ’only if’ part of Theorem
4.7
.

Let
D=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{+}(\lambda)
, i.e.
D_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)

for all
\varepsilon>0
and

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|D_{\varepsilon}\|_{p}=\|D\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p}^{+}(\lambda)}<\infty
. Hence by Lemma
4.9
the maps

F_{\varepsilon}(D)(z):=\Phi(D_{\varepsilon})(z)=\sum\left(a_{n}e^{-\varepsilon\lambda_{n}}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\right)e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\colon[Re>0]\to\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)

are holomorphic for all

, and bounded uniformly in

by
\|D\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p}^{+}(\lambda)}
. Now the function

F(z):=\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\colon[Re>0]\to\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)

is holomorphic and bounded. Let
\varphi(z):=\frac{1+z}{1-z}\colon\mathbb{D}\to[Re>0]
be the Cayley transformation (with inverse
\varphi^{-1}(s)=\frac{s-1}{s+1}
), and consider the function

. Then
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varphi^{-1}(\varepsilon+it)=\frac{it-1}{it+1}=:w\in\mathbb{T}
and as a matter of fact the so called Stolz region

for any
\alpha>1
contains the set
\{\varphi^{-1}(\varepsilon+it)\mid 0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}\}
for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
(see
[
9
, §11.4]
). Since
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
, being a closed subspace of some
L_{p}(G)
, has the analytic Radon-Nikodym property, the limits
\lim_{S(\alpha,w)\ni z\to\frac{it-1}{it+1}}f(z)
exists for almost all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. But then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F(\varepsilon+it)=\lim_{S(\alpha,w)\ni z\to\frac{it-1}{it+1}}f(z)

exists in
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
for almost all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and equals
D_{it}
(since the Dirichlet coefficients of

are given by
(a_{n}(D)e^{-\lambda_{n}(\varepsilon+it)})
). Now Proposition
4.3
(applied to some admissible
\omega:=\beta(t)
) gives

.

Corollary 4.11

Let

In view of Theorem
2.2
the following results collects a couple of sufficient conditions under which

\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(\lambda)

Theorem 4.12

Let

\lambda
-Dirichlet group, and assume that

\mathcal{B}\colon H_{\infty}^{\lambda}(G)\to\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda),\penalty\ \penalty\ f\mapsto\sum\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})e^{-\lambda_{n}s}.

\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)=\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(\lambda)

For polynomials the proof of the desired norm equality is rather easy. Since
\beta(\mathbb{R})
is dense in

a standard distinguished maximum modulus principle gives the following

Lemma 4.13

Let
(G,\beta)
be a Dirichlet group. Then for all

This gives the desired isometry between
(Pol(\lambda),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
and
(Pol_{\lambda}(G),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
. We have to prove that statement beyond polynomials.

Proof of Theorem
4.10
.

Let

with extension

.
Then by Lemma

the net

for each

with limit, say
f_{\sigma}

for all

\widehat{f_{\sigma}}(h_{\lambda_{n}})=\lim_{x\to\infty}\widehat{R_{x}^{\sigma}}(h_{\lambda_{n}})=a_{n}e^{-\sigma\lambda_{n}}\,,

and

\|f_{\sigma}\|_{\infty}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\|P^{\sigma}_{x}\|_{\infty}=\|D_{\sigma}\|_{\infty}\leq\|F\|_{\infty}.

Now recall that the unit ball of

together with its weak star topology
is metrizable and compact (by the Alaoglu-Bourbarki theorem and the fact that

is separable). Hence,

with limit

4.4.
Schauder basis

It is known that
(n^{-s})
is a Schauder basis for
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\log(n))
in the range
1<p<\infty
(see
[
1
]
). This result extends to arbitrary frequencies
\lambda
.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\lambda
be a frequency. Then the monomials
(e^{-\lambda_{n}s})
form a Schauder basis for
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

Let
(G,\beta)
be any
\lambda
-Dirichlet group. We then claim that
(h_{\lambda_{n}})
is a Schauder basis for
H^{\lambda}_{p}(G)
. By Proposition
3.14
it suffices to verify, that there is a constant
C\geq 1
, such that for all
m>n
and for all
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{m}\in\mathbb{C}

\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{n}a_{k}h_{\lambda_{k}}\Big\|_{p}\leq C\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{m}a_{k}h_{\lambda_{k}}\Big\|_{p}.

By Proposition
3.3
there is a natural order on
\widehat{G}
: We call a character

h_{x},x\in\widehat{\beta}(\widehat{G}),
positive if
x\geq 0
. Then the ’Riesz projection’

\Phi\left(\sum a_{k}h_{x_{k}}\right):=\sum_{x_{k}\geq 0}a_{k}h_{x_{k}}

is bounded on
Pol(G)\subset L_{p}(G)
with norm
C=C(p)>0
(see
[
25
, Theorem 8.7.2.]
, here connectedness of
G
is needed), and hence for
m>n
and each
f:=\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}h_{\lambda_{k}}\in Pol_{\lambda}(G)
we as desired have

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}h_{\lambda_{k}}\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}h_{\lambda_{k}}\Big\|_{p}+\Big\|\sum_{k=n+1}^{m}a_{k}h_{\lambda_{k}}\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}h_{\lambda_{k}}\Big\|_{p}+\Big\|h_{\lambda_{n+1}}\Phi\left(h_{-\lambda_{n+1}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}h_{\lambda_{k}}\right)\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C)\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}h_{\lambda_{k}}\Big\|_{p}.\qed

An equivalent formulation of Theorem
4.16
is that
for
1<p<\infty

all projections

\pi^{N}_{p}:\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda),\,\,\sum a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}\mapsto\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}s}

are uniformly bounded. But for the border cases

and

this in general is false
(e.g., for the frequencies
\lambda=(\log n)
or
\lambda=(n)
). We give an upper bound for the growth of the partial sum operators in
\mathcal{H}_{1}(\lambda)
; thereby the quality of the upper bounds depends on the quality of
\lambda
.

Let
\lambda
be a frequency
and

or
\infty
. Assuming
(BC)
for
\lambda

and, assuming

The case

[

and Landau

[

Given a Banach space

4.6.
N
th Abschnitte

As already mentioned the ’ordinary’
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
isometrically equals
H_{\infty}(B_{c_{0}})
,
identifying Dirichlet and monomial coefficients. A cruicial argument in the proof of this result (see
[
9
, §2.3]
) is that a continuous function
f\colon B_{c_{0}}\to\mathbb{C}
belongs to
H_{\infty}(B_{c_{0}})
if and only if all restriction maps
f_{N}\colon\mathbb{D}^{N}\to\mathbb{C}
belong to
H_{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{N})
with
\sup_{N}\|f_{N}\|_{\infty}<\infty
.

Formulated for ordinary Dirichlet series this says that
D=\sum a_{n}n^{-s}\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
if and only if
all of its so-called
N
-th abschnitte

D|_{N}=\sum a_{n}n^{-s}\,,

where the sum is taken over those
n
which have only the first
N
primes as divisors,
belong to
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
with uniformly bounded norms.
In more vague terms,
D\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
if and only if all its finite dimensional blocks are
in
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
with uniformly bounded norms.

This phenomenon is also true for general Dirichlet series. If
\lambda
is a frequency with decomposition
(R,B)
, then the
N
-th abschnitt
D|_{N}
of a
\lambda
-Dirichlet series
D
is
\sum a_{n}(D)e^{-\lambda_{n}s}
, where
a_{n}(D)\neq 0
implies that
\lambda_{n}\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{N}).

Looking at the identification
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)=H_{p}^{R}(\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{\infty}),D\mapsto F
,
given in Theorem
3.28
,
this restriction
D|_{N}
simply corresponds to

F|_{N}\colon\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{N}\to\mathbb{C}\,,\,\,\,F|_{N}(\omega):=\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{d}}^{\infty}}F(\omega,\eta)dm(\eta)\,,

the ‘restriction’ of
F
to the first
N
variables. Then an application of Hölder’s inequality gives
\|F|_{N}\|_{p}\leq\|F\|_{p}
, and so (again by Theorem
3.28
)
\|D|_{N}\|_{p}\leq\|D\|_{p}
.

Remark 4.21
.

Let
\lambda=(R,B)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
D\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
. Then
D|_{N}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
with
\|D|_{N}\|_{p}\leq\|D\|_{p}
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 4.22
.

Assume that
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
\lambda=(R,B)
is a frequency satisfying one of the conditions of Theorem
4.19
, and
D
a
\lambda
-Dirichlet series. Then
D\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
if and only if
D|_{N}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
for all
N
and
\sup_{N}\|D|_{N}\|_{p}<\infty
. Moreover, in this case
\|D\|_{p}=\sup\|D|_{N}\|_{p}.

Together with Theorem
4.12
we obtain as an immediate consequence the following

Corollary 4.23
.

Assume that
\lambda=(R,B)
satisfies one of the conditions of Theorem
4.19
, and
D
is a
\lambda
-Dirichlet series. Then
D\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
if and only if
D|_{N}\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\lambda)
for all
N
and
\sup_{N}\|D|_{N}\|_{\infty}<\infty
. Moreover, in this case
\|D\|_{\infty}=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\|D|_{N}\|_{\infty}.

In view of the definition of the
\mathcal{H}_{p}(\lambda)
’s from
3.23
we may reformulate Theorem
4.22
in terms of Hardy spaces on

\lambda
-Dirichlet groups.

Theorem 4.24
.

Let
\lambda=(R,B)
be a frequency satisfying one of the condition of Theorem
4.19
,
(G,\beta)
a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group, and
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Then for every sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
in
\mathbb{C}
the following are equivalent:

(1)

\exists\penalty\ f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)\,\,\forall\penalty\ n\in\mathbb{N}:\penalty\ \widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})=a_{n}

(2)

\forall N\in\mathbb{N}\penalty\ \exists f_{N}\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G):

\widehat{f_{N}}(h_{\lambda_{n}})=\begin{cases}a_{n}&\text{if }\lambda_{n}\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{N}),\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}

and
\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{N}\|_{p}<\infty
.

Moreover, in this case
\|f\|_{p}=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{N}\|_{p}
.

Proof.

Although the implication
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
already follows from Remark
4.21
, we like to give another argument of different flavour. For each
N
we consider the subgroup

U_{N}:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{N})\cap\widehat{G}\subset\widehat{G}\,,

and take, according to Lemma
3.16
, some
\mu_{N}\in M(G)
with
\|\mu_{N}\|=1
such that
\widehat{\mu_{N}}=\chi_{U_{N}}
. Then the functions
f_{N}:=f*\mu_{N}\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
fulfill the claim.

It remains to prove that
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
: Let us start with the case
p=1
. Denote by
D^{N}
those Dirichlet series which correspond to the functions
f_{N}
. Then by Theorem
4.19
there is
D\in\mathcal{H}_{1}(\lambda)
and a subsequence
(D^{N_{k}})
such that
D^{N_{k}}_{\varepsilon}\to D_{\varepsilon}

in
\mathcal{H}_{1}(\lambda)

for all
\varepsilon>0
. Then
f:=\mathcal{B}(D)
has the right Fourier coefficients and by Theorem
4.7

\displaystyle\|f\|_{1}=\|D\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|D_{\varepsilon}\|_{1}\leq\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\sup_{N}\|D^{N}_{\varepsilon}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sup_{N}\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|D^{N}_{\varepsilon}\|_{1}=\sup_{N}\|D^{N}\|_{1}=\sup_{N}\|f_{N}\|_{1}.

In the case
1<p\leq\infty
the sequence
(f_{N})_{N}
is bounded,
and hence weakly bounded in
L_{p^{\prime}}(G)
. Then by the Alaoglu-Bourbaki theorem there is
f\in H_{p}^{\lambda}(G)
with the right Fourier coefficients and
\|f\|_{p}\leq\sup_{N}\|f_{N}\|_{p}
.
∎

4.7.
Brothers Riesz theorem

The aim of our last section is to discuss the following brothers Riesz type theorem.

Theorem 4.25
.

Let
\lambda
be a frequency and
(G,\beta)
a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group. Then the Bohr map

\mathcal{B}\colon H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)\to M_{\lambda}(G),\penalty\ \penalty\ f\mapsto f\penalty\ dm

is an onto isometry preserving the Fourier coefficients.

Note that
for the frequency
\lambda=(n)
this result is the classical brothers Riesz theorem on
\mathbb{T}
, and
for the ordinary case
\lambda=(\log n)
it means that the Hardy space
H_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
isometrically equals the space
M_{+}(\mathbb{T}^{\infty})
of all bounded, regular and analytic Borel measures on
\mathbb{T}^{\infty}
(due to Helson and Lowdenslager from
[
20
]
, see also
[
9
, §13.1]
).

The proof of Theorem
4.25
is an immediate consequence of
[
13
, Theorem 4]
, which in terms of Dirichlet groups states that every
\mu\in M(G)

is absolutely continuous
with respect to the Haar measure on
G
, whenever for every
\delta>0
the set
S_{\delta}(\mu):=\{h_{x}\mid x<\delta\text{ and }\widehat{\mu}(h_{x})\neq 0\}
is finite. Clearly, any
\mu\in M_{\lambda}(G)

satisfies this condition.

We now like to give a proof of Theorem
4.25
within the theory of Dirichlet series by applying the Montel Theorem
4.19
. But then we of course have to put the additional assumptions on
\lambda

needed for that theorem. Moreover, we need the existence of so called ‘local units’, which follows as an immediate consequence of
[
25
, Theorem 2.6.8]
.

Lemma 4.26
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
G
a
\lambda
-Dirichlet group. Then there is a sequence of polynomials
(k_{N})_{N}\subset Pol(G)
such that
\widehat{k_{N}}(h_{\lambda_{j}})=1
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and all
j=1,\ldots,N
, and moreover

\sup_{N}\|k_{N}\|_{L^{1}(G)}\leq 1+\varepsilon
.

Actually
[
13
, Theorem 4]
is an easy consequence of the generalized version of the brothers Riesz theorem by Helson and Lowdenslager (see
[
25
, §8.2.3.]
) and Lemma
4.26
. So in this sense the Montel Theorem
4.19
replaces the former result in the proof of Theorem
4.25
(but then for a smaller class of
\lambda
’s).

Proof of Theorem
4.25
under the assumptions on
\lambda

needed in Theorem
4.19
.

Take some
\mu\in M_{\lambda}(G)
and
\varepsilon>0
. We choose
(k_{N})_{N}
from Lemma
4.26
. Then
(\mu*k_{N})_{N}
is bounded in
H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
by
\|\mu\|(1+\varepsilon)
. By Theorem
4.19
(more precisely Corollary
4.20
) there is a subsequence
(N_{k})_{k}
and
f\in H_{1}^{\lambda}(G)
such that
p_{u}*\mu*k_{N_{k}}
converges to
p_{u}*f
for all
u>0
. This implies that
\widehat{f}(h_{\lambda_{n}})=\widehat{\mu}(h_{\lambda_{n}})
for all
n
, and so with Lemma
4.8

we get that

\|f\|_{1}=\lim_{u\to 0}\|p_{u}*f\|_{1}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|\mu\|

for all
\varepsilon>0
. This proves the claim.
∎

We like to mention that Theorem
4.12
,
4.25
and
4.22
have extensions to the setting of vector valued general Dirichlet series (see
[
12
]
).

Handling the inconsistency of systems of min → \min\rightarrow fuzzy relational equations
Ismaïl Baaj

Abstract
In this article, we study the inconsistency of systems of min − → \min-\rightarrow fuzzy relational equations. We give analytical formulas for computing the Chebyshev distances ∇ = inf d ∈ 𝒟 ‖ β − d ‖ \nabla=\inf_{d\in\mathcal{D}}\|\beta-d\| associated to systems of min − → \min-\rightarrow fuzzy relational equations of the form Γ ​ □ → min ​ x = β \Gamma\Box_{\rightarrow}^{\min}x=\beta , where → \rightarrow is a residual implicator among the Gödel implication → G \rightarrow_{G} , the Goguen implication → G ​ G \rightarrow_{GG} or Łukasiewicz’s implication → L \rightarrow_{L} and 𝒟 \mathcal{D} is the set of second members of consistent systems defined with the same matrix Γ \Gamma . The main preliminary result that allows us to obtain these formulas is that the Chebyshev distance ∇ \nabla is the lower bound of the solutions of a vector inequality, whatever the residual implicator used. Finally, we show that, in the case of the min − → G \min-\rightarrow_{G} system, the Chebyshev distance ∇ \nabla may be an infimum, while it is always a minimum for min − → G ​ G \min-\rightarrow_{GG} and min − → L \min-\rightarrow_{L} systems.



Keywords
Fuzzy set theory ; fuzzy relational equations ; Chebyshev approximations

2.1
Notations

The set
[0,1]^{n\times m}
is the set of matrices of size
(n,m)
i.e.,
n
rows and
m
columns, whose components are in
[0,1]
, thus, the set
[0,1]^{n\times 1}
is the set of column vectors of
n
components and
[0,1]^{1\times m}
denotes the set of row matrices of
m
components.

The order relation
\leq
on the set
[0,1]^{n\times m}
is defined by:

A\leq B\quad\text{iff we have}\quad a_{ij}\leq b_{ij}\quad\text{ for all }\quad 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m,

where
A=[a_{ij}]_{1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m}
and
B=[b_{ij}]_{1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m}
.

For
x,y,z,u,\delta\in[0,1]
, we put:

•

x^{+}=\max(x,0)
,

•

\overline{z}(\delta)=\min(z+\delta,1)
,

•

\underline{z}(\delta)=\max(z-\delta,0)=(z-\delta)^{+}

and we have the following equivalence in
[0,1]
:

\mid x-y\mid\leq\delta\Longleftrightarrow\underline{x}(\delta)\leq y\leq\overline{x}(\delta).

(1)

To a column-vector
b=[b_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq n}
and a number
\delta\in[0,1]
, we associate two column-vectors:

\underline{b}(\delta)=[(b_{i}-\delta)^{+}]_{1\leq i\leq n}\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{b}(\delta)=[\min(b_{i}+\delta,1)]_{1\leq i\leq n}.

(2)

Then, from (
1
), we deduce for any
c=[c_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq n}\in[0,1]^{n\times 1}
:

\|b-c\|\leq\delta\Longleftrightarrow\underline{b}(\delta)\leq c\leq\overline{b}(\delta).

(3)

where
\|b-c\|=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\mid b_{i}-c_{i}\mid
.

2.2
T-norms and their associated residual implicators

A triangular-norm (t-norm, see
[
5
]
) is a map
T:[0,1]\times[0,1]\mapsto[0,1]
, which satisfies:

T
is commutative:
T(x,y)=T(y,x)
,

T
is associative:
T(x,T(y,z))=T(T(x,y),z)
,

T
is increasing :
x\leq x^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad y\leq y^{\prime}\,\Longrightarrow\,T(x,y)\leq T(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
,

T
has
1
as neutral element:
T(x,1)=x
.

To the t-norm
T
is associated the residual implicator

{\cal I}_{T}:[0,1]\times[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]:(x,y)\mapsto{\cal I}_{T}(x,y)=\sup\{z\in[0,1]\,\mid\,T(x,z)\leq y\}
.

For all
a,b\in[0,1]
, the main properties of the residual implicator
{\cal I}_{T}
associated to a continuous t-norm
T
are:

•

{\cal I}_{T}(a,b)=\max\{z\in[0,1]\,\mid\,T(a,z)\leq b\}
. Therefore,
T(a,{\cal I}_{T}(a,b))\leq b
.

•

{\cal I}_{T}
is left-continuous and decreasing in its first argument as well as right-continuous and increasing in its second argument.

•

For all
z\in[0,1]
, we have:

T(a,z)\leq b\Longleftrightarrow z\leq{\cal I}_{T}(a,b).

•

We have

\,b\leq{\cal I}_{T}(a,T(a,b))
.

The t-norm
\min
denoted by
T_{M}
, has a residual implicator
\mathcal{I}_{T_{M}}
which is the Gödel implication:

T_{M}(x,y)=\min(x,y)\quad;\quad{\cal I}_{T_{M}}(x,y)=x\underset{G}{\longrightarrow}y=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }x\leq y\\
\ y&\text{ if }x>y\end{cases}.

(4)

The t-norm defined by the usual product is denoted by
T_{P}
. Its associated residual implicator is the Goguen implication:

T_{P}(x,y)=x\cdot y\quad;\quad{\cal I}_{T_{P}}(x,y)=x\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}y=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }x\leq y\\
\frac{y}{x}&\text{ if }x>y\end{cases}.

(5)

Łukasiewicz’s t-norm is denoted by
T_{L}
and its associated residual implicator
{\cal I}_{T_{L}}
:

T_{L}(x,y)=\max(x+y-1,0)={(x+y-1)}^{+}\quad;\quad{\cal I}_{T_{L}}(x,y)=x\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}y=\min(1-x+y,1).

(6)

A system of
\max-T

A=[a_{ij}]\in[0,1]^{n\times m}

b=[b_{i}]\in[0,1]^{n\times 1}

(7)

\Box_{T}^{\max}
is the matrix product that uses the continuous t-norm
T

\max
as the addition.

Using the vector

e=A^{t}\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}b,

(8)

where
A^{t}
is the transpose of
A
and the matrix product
\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}
uses the residual implicator
{\cal I}_{T}
(associated to
T
) as the product and
\min
as the addition, we have the following equivalence proved by Sanchez for
\max-\min

[

]
, and extended to
\max-T

[
8
,
10
]
and Miyakoshi and Shimbo
[

]
:

A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=b\text{ is consistent}\Longleftrightarrow A\Box_{T}^{\max}e=b.

(9)

2.4
Chebyshev distance associated to the second member of a system of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations

To the matrix
A
and the second member
b
of the system
(S)
of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations, see (
7
), is associated the set of vectors
c=[c_{i}]\in[0,1]^{n\times 1}
such that the system
A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=c
is consistent:

\mathcal{C}=\{c=[c_{i}]\in{[0,1]}^{n\times 1}\mid A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=c\text{ is consistent}\}.

(10)

This set allows us to define the Chebyshev distance associated to the second member
b
of the system
(S)
.

Definition 1
.

The Chebyshev distance associated to the second member
b
of the system
(S):A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=b
is:

\Delta=\Delta(A,b)=\inf_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\|b-c\|

(11)

where:

\|b-c\|=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\mid b_{i}-c_{i}\mid.

and

Definition 2
.

A Chebyshev approximation of the second member
b
is an element
c\in\mathcal{C}
, see (
10
), such that
\|b-c\|=\Delta
.

The set of Chebyshev approximations of the second member
b
is:

\mathcal{C}_{b}=\{c\in\mathcal{C}\mid\|b-c\|=\Delta\}.

(12)

We have the following fundamental result, proven for
\max-\min
system in
[
3
]
and extended for
\max-T
systems in
[
1
]
:

\Delta=\min\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta))\}.

(13)

which is based on the following application:

F:[0,1]^{n\times 1}\longrightarrow[0,1]^{n\times 1}:c=[c_{i}]\mapsto F(c)=A\Box_{T}^{\max}(A^{t}\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}c)=[F(c)_{i}]

(14)

where:

\forall i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\},\,F(c)_{i}=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}T(a_{ij},\min_{1\leq k\leq n}{\cal I}_{T}(a_{kj},c_{k})).

(15)

By solving (
13
) in the case of a system of
\max-\min
fuzzy relational equations
A\Box_{T_{M}}^{\max}x=b
, the author of
[
2
]
gave the following analytical formula for computing the Chebyshev distance associated to its second member
b
:

\Delta_{M}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\,\max[(b_{i}-a_{ij})^{+},\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\,\sigma_{G}\,(b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k})],

(16)

where

\sigma_{G}(x,y,z)=\min(\frac{(x-z)^{+}}{2},(y-z)^{+}).

(17)

Similarly, for the case of a system of
\max
-product fuzzy relational equations
A\Box_{T_{P}}^{\max}x=b
, the author of
[
1
]
gave the following analytical formula for computing the Chebyshev distance associated to
b
:

\Delta_{P}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\,\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\sigma_{GG}\,(a_{ij},b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k}),

(18)

where

\sigma_{GG}(u,x,y,z)=\max[(x-u)^{+},\min(\varphi(u,x,y,z),(y-z)^{+})]

(19)

and

\varphi(u,x,y,z)=\begin{cases}\frac{(x\cdot y-u\cdot z)^{+}}{u+y}&\text{ if }u>0\\
x&\text{ if }u=0\end{cases}.

(20)

The author of
[
1
]
also gave the following analytical formula for computing Chebyshev distance associated to the second member
b
of a system of
\max
-Łukasiewicz fuzzy relational equations
A\Box_{T_{L}}^{\max}x=b
:

\Delta_{L}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\,\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\sigma_{L}\,(1-a_{ij},b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k}),

(21)

where

\sigma_{L}(u,x,y,z)=\min(x,\max(v^{+},\frac{(v+y-z)^{+}}{2}))\text{ with }v=x+u-1.

For the
\max-\min
system, the author of
[
2
]
showed that the formula (
16
) allows us to get directly its greatest approximate solution and its greatest Chebyshev approximation of its second member (thus, the set
\mathcal{C}_{b}
is non-empty and
\Delta_{M}
is always a minimum). For
\max
-product and max-Łukasiewicz systems, the author of
[
1
]
showed the same results using the formulas (
18
) and (
21
). From (
16
), the author of
[
2
]
also gave a description of the set
\mathcal{C}_{b}
of Chebyshev approximations, see (
12
) and that of the approximate solutions set of the inconsistent system.

Let us use the Gödel’s implication

, see (

\end{bmatrix}

3.1
Study of the application
G

From the system
(\Sigma):\Gamma\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}x=\beta
, see (
22
), where
\Gamma=[\gamma_{ji}]_{1\leq j\leq m,1\leq i\leq n}\in[0,1]^{m\times n}
, let us compute the components of
G(\xi)
for any
vector
\xi=[\xi_{j}]_{1\leq j\leq m}\in[0,1]^{m\times 1}
.

For
1\leq j\leq m
, we have:

G(\xi)_{j}=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\,{\cal I}_{T}(\gamma_{ji},\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,T(\gamma_{li},\xi_{l})).

The application
G
has the following properties:

Proposition 1
.

\forall\xi\in[0,1]^{m\times 1},\quad\xi\leq G(\xi).

To prove the inequality
\xi_{j}\leq G(\xi)_{j}
for each
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, we rely on the inequality
{\cal I}_{T}(a,T(a,b))\geq b
.

Proof.

Let
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, we have:

\displaystyle G(\xi)_{j}

\displaystyle=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\,{\cal I}_{T}(\gamma_{ji},\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,T(\gamma_{li},\xi_{l}))

\displaystyle\geq\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\,{\cal I}_{T}(\gamma_{ji},T(\gamma_{ji},\xi_{j}))

\displaystyle\geq\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\,\xi_{j}

\displaystyle=\xi_{j}.

∎

Proposition 2
.

The application
G
is increasing ; given
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in[0,1]^{m\times 1}
we have:

\xi\leq\xi^{\prime}\Longrightarrow G(\xi)\leq G(\xi^{\prime}).

Proof.

The increasing property of the application
G
is a consequence of the increasing property of the t-norm
T
and that of its associated residual implicator
{\cal I}_{T}
with respect to the second variable. It results that the following two applications are also increasing:

[0,1]^{n\times 1}\rightarrow[0,1]^{m\times 1}:c\mapsto\Gamma\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}c\quad;\quad[0,1]^{m\times 1}\rightarrow[0,1]^{n\times 1}:\xi\mapsto\Gamma^{t}\Box_{T}^{\max}\xi.

∎

Proposition 3
.

The application
G
is idempotent i.e.,
G\circ G=G
.

To prove the idempotent property of
G
, we have to show that
\forall\xi\in[0,1]^{m\times 1}
,
G(G(\xi))=G(\xi)
. The proof is a consequence of the relation:
\forall\xi\in[0,1]^{m\times 1},\quad\Gamma^{t}\Box_{T}^{\max}G(\xi)=\Gamma^{t}\Box_{T}^{\max}\xi
, which we will deduce from a property of the application
F
, see (
14
).

Proof.

Let
\xi\in[0,1]^{m\times 1}
be fixed and we put
c=\Gamma^{t}\Box_{T}^{\max}\xi\in[0,1]^{n\times 1}
. Thus,
\Gamma^{t}\Box_{T}^{\max}x=c
is a consistent system and
\xi
one of its solution. We remind that the application
F
of
[
1
,
2
]
, see (
14
), associated to the matrix
A:=\Gamma^{t}
satisfies
F(c)=c
.

We have:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{t}\Box_{T}^{\max}G(\xi)

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{t}\Box_{T}^{\max}(\Gamma\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}(\Gamma^{t}\Box_{T}^{\max}\xi))

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{t}\Box_{T}^{\max}(\Gamma\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}c)

\displaystyle=F(c)

\displaystyle=c=\Gamma^{t}\Box_{T}^{\max}\xi.

The equality
\Gamma^{t}\Box_{T}^{\max}G(\xi)=\Gamma\Box_{T}^{\max}\xi
leads to:

G(G(\xi))=\Gamma\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}(\Gamma^{t}\Box_{T}^{\max}G(\xi))=\Gamma\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}(\Gamma^{t}\Box_{T}^{\max}\xi)=G(\xi).

∎

Proposition 4
.

The application
G
is right-continuous.

If the application

{\cal I}_{T}:[0,1]^{2}\rightarrow[0,1]

is continuous, then
G:[0,1]^{m\times 1}\rightarrow[0,1]^{m\times 1}
is also continuous.

The right-continuity of the application
G
means that for any sequence
(\xi^{(k)})
verifying:

\forall k,\xi^{(k)}\geq\xi\text{ and }(\xi^{(k)})\rightarrow\xi
, we have
(G(\xi^{(k))})\rightarrow G(\xi)
. Note that, in
[0,1]^{m\times 1}
, the convergence of a sequence of vectors
(\eta^{(k)})
to a vector
\eta\in[0,1]^{m\times 1}
is the convergence component by component:

\forall j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\},\,\eta^{(k)}_{j}\rightarrow\eta_{j}
.

Proof.

The right-continuity of the application
G
is a consequence of the continuity of the t-norm
T
and the right-continuity with respect to the second variable of the residual implicator
\mathcal{I}_{T}
associated to
T
. It is obtained by applying the general theorems on continuity
[
12
]
, which also allow, in the case where
{\cal I}_{T}
is continuous, to obtain the continuity of the application
G
.
∎

In the following subsection, the application

fuzzy relational equations.

Chebyshev distance associated to the second member of a system of

In this subsection, we begin by defining the set

To the matrix

and

.

(30)

In the case of a system of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations, the authors of

is the lower bound of the solutions of a vector inequality involving the application

Similarly, for a system of

.

We rely on the following sets:

Notation 1
.

E=\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)\}.

(31)

For all
1\leq j\leq m
:

E_{j}=\{\delta\in[0,1]\,\mid\,G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}\},\quad\nabla_{j}=\inf E_{j}.

(32)

{\cal A}_{j}=\{i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}\,\mid\,\gamma_{ji}>0\},

(33)

We need the following lemma:

Lemma 1
.

We have:

1.

E=\bigcap_{1\leq j\leq m}E_{j}.

2.

for all
\delta,\delta^{\prime}\in[0,1]
and
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, we have:

\delta\in E_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta\leq\delta^{\prime}\,\Longrightarrow\,\delta^{\prime}\in E_{j}.

Proof.

The proof of the first statement is easy:
for any
\delta\in[0,1]
, we have:

\delta\in E\,\Longleftrightarrow\,G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\forall j\in\{1,\dots,m\}\,,\,G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\forall j\in\{1,\dots,m\}\,,\,\delta\in E_{j}.

Let
\delta\in E_{j}
and
\delta^{\prime}\in[0,1]
satisfy the inequality
\delta\leq\delta^{\prime}
. Then, using the increasing (Proposition
2
) of
G
, we obtain:

G(\underline{\beta}(\delta^{\prime}))_{j}\leq G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta^{\prime})_{j}.

Thus, we obtain
G(\underline{\beta}(\delta^{\prime}))_{j}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta^{\prime})_{j}
, i.e.,
\delta^{\prime}\in E_{j}
.
∎

The Chebyshev distance
\nabla
(Definition
3
) of a
\min-\rightarrow
system
(\Sigma):\Gamma\Box_{\rightarrow}^{\min}x=\beta
, see (
22
) is:

Theorem 1
.

We have:

\nabla=\inf\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)\}=\inf E.

(34)

Proof.

We put

\nabla^{\prime}=\inf E.
Let us show that we have

\nabla=\nabla^{\prime}
in two steps:

1.

\nabla\leq\nabla^{\prime}
: we have to show:

\forall\delta\in E,\,\nabla\leq\delta.

Let
\delta\in E
, we put
d=G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))
. From (
28
), we deduce that
d\in\mathcal{D}
.
Then, we obtain:

\|\beta-G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))\|=\|\beta-d\|\geq\nabla.

From (Proposition
1
), we have
\underline{\beta}(\delta)\leq G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))
and
\delta\in E
. So, we deduce the double inequality

\underline{\beta}(\delta)\leq G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta).

Using the inequality
(
3
), we conclude that:

\|\beta-d\|=\|\beta-G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))\|\leq\delta

and finally
\nabla\leq\|\beta-d\|\leq\delta
.

2.

\nabla^{\prime}\leq\nabla
: we have to show:

\forall d\in{\cal D}\,,\,\nabla^{\prime}\leq\|\beta-d\|.

Let
d\in{\cal D}
, we put

\delta=\|\beta-d\|
. From (
3
), we deduce:

\underline{\beta}(\delta)\leq d\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta).

Using that
G
is increasing (Proposition
2
) and the equivalence (
25
), we obtain:

G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))\leq G(d)=d\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta),

so
\delta\in E
and by definition of
\nabla^{\prime}
, we have

\nabla^{\prime}\leq\delta=\|\beta-d\|
.

∎

(Theorem
1
) states that
\inf_{d\in\mathcal{D}}\|\beta-d\|=\nabla=\inf E
. In fact, in the following sections, we will see that, whatever the residual implicator used among the Gödel implication, the Goguen implication and the Łukasiewicz implication, we have:

\nabla=\min_{d\in\mathcal{D}}\|\beta-d\|\Longleftrightarrow\nabla=\min E.

From the above lemma and theorem, we deduce the following useful decomposition of the Chebyshev distance
\nabla
:

Proposition 5
.

\nabla=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\nabla_{j}

(35)

where:

\nabla_{j}=\inf\{\delta\in[0,1]\,\mid\,G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}\}=\inf E_{j}.

(36)

Proof.

We will prove the equality

\nabla=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\nabla_{j}
in the following two steps:

1.

\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\nabla_{j}\leq\nabla
; we have to show:

\forall\delta\in E,\,\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\nabla_{j}\leq\delta.

Using (Lemma
1
) the equality
E=\bigcap_{1\leq j\leq m}E_{j}
, we deduce that for any
\delta\in E
, we have:

\forall j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}\,\,\nabla_{j}=\inf E_{j}\leq\delta.

Thus,
\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\nabla_{j}\leq\delta.

2.

\nabla\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\nabla_{j}

To rely on the properties of a lower bound, we use the well-known equivalence:

\nabla\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\nabla_{j}\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\forall\varepsilon>0\,,\,\nabla<\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\nabla_{j}+\varepsilon.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. For any

j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, there is an element
\delta_{j}\in E_{j}
such that

\delta_{j}<\nabla_{j}+\varepsilon.

We put
\delta=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\delta_{j}
. Then, for any

j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, we get:

\delta_{j}\in E_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta_{j}\leq\delta.

Using (Lemma
1
), we conclude that
\delta\in E
and:

\nabla\leq\delta=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\delta_{j}<\max_{1\leq j\leq m}(\nabla_{j}+\varepsilon)=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\nabla_{j}+\varepsilon.

∎

As we will see in the next sections, the formula for computing
\nabla_{j}
, involves the value
1-\beta_{j}
(thus using the
j
-th component
\beta_{j}
of the second member
\beta
), whatever the residual implicator used among the Gödel implication, the Goguen implication and the Łukasiewicz implication.

Lemma 2
.

For any

j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, we have:

1.

1-\beta_{j}\in E_{j}
,

2.

\nabla_{j}\leq 1-\beta_{j}
.

In particular, we have:

\beta_{j}=1\Longrightarrow\nabla_{j}=0.

Proof.

For
\delta=1-\beta_{j}
, we have
\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}=1
, so the inequality

G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}=1
is satisfied and therefore we have
1-\beta_{j}\in E_{j}
.

As we proved
1-\beta_{j}\in E_{j}
, the inequality
\nabla_{j}\leq 1-\beta_{j}
is a consequence of the definition
\nabla_{j}=\inf E_{j}
.
∎

Chebyshev distance associated to the second member of systems based on

composition

, see (Definition

\min-\rightarrow_{G}
fuzzy relational equations

We remind from (Proposition

\nabla=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\nabla_{j}

and the set

is defined in (

We use the following notations:

Notation 2
.

For
1\leq j\leq m,1\leq i\leq n
, to each coefficient
\gamma_{ji}
of the matrix
\Gamma

(\Sigma)
, see (

•

V(j,i)=\big\{l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}\mid\gamma_{ji}\leq\gamma_{li}\big\}
,

•

,

•

\zeta_{ji}=\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\sigma_{G}(\beta_{l},\gamma_{li},\beta_{j})
.

where
\sigma_{G}(x,y,z)=\min(\dfrac{(x-z)^{+}}{2},(y-z)^{+})
was defined in (

) from
[

]
.

To give the formula for computing
\nabla_{j}
for
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, see (
36
), we will study the solving of the involved inequality
G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}
.

The following result gives an equivalent definition of the function
\sigma_{G}
introduced in
[
2
]
, see (
17
), which is adapted to our needs:

Lemma 3
.

For all
x,y,z,\delta\in[0,1]
, we have:

\min(y,\underline{x}(\delta))\leq\overline{z}(\delta)\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\sigma_{G}(x,y,z)\leq\delta.

Proof.

We have:

y-\overline{z}(\delta)=\max(y-z-\delta,y-1).

\displaystyle\underline{x}(\delta)-\overline{z}(\delta)

\displaystyle=\max(\underline{x}(\delta)-z-\delta,\underline{x}(\delta)-1)

\displaystyle=\max[\max(x-\delta,0)-z-\delta,\underline{x}(\delta)-1]

\displaystyle=\max[\max(x-\delta-z-\delta,-z-\delta),\underline{x}(\delta)-1]

\displaystyle=\max(x-z-2\delta,-z-\delta,\underline{x}(\delta)-1).

We remark that:
y-1\leq 0,\,-z-\delta\leq 0,\text{ and }\underline{x}(\delta)-1\leq 0
.

We have:

\displaystyle\min(y,\underline{x}(\delta))\leq\overline{z}(\delta)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\,\min(y-\overline{z}(\delta),\underline{x}(\delta)-\overline{z}(\delta))\leq 0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\,y-\overline{z}(\delta)\leq 0\,\text{ or }\,\underline{x}(\delta)-\overline{z}(\delta))\leq 0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\,y-z-\delta\leq 0\,\text{ or }\,x-z-2\delta\leq 0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\,(y-z)^{+}\leq\delta\,\text{ or }\,\dfrac{(x-z)^{+}}{2}\leq\delta

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\,\min(\dfrac{(x-z)^{+}}{2},(y-z)^{+})\leq\delta

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\,\sigma_{G}(x,y,z)=\min(\dfrac{(x-z)^{+}}{2},(y-z)^{+})\leq\delta.

∎

Example 2
.

Let
x=0.6,y=0.4
and

. We have
\min(y,x)>z
. We compute
\delta=\sigma_{G}(x,y,z)=0.2
, then the inequality

is satisfied.

We establish the following important lemma, which will provide important information in (Proposition

, in the case where

:

Lemma 4
.

Let
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}

2.

there exists

\gamma_{ji}>0,\quad\theta_{ji}<\delta\quad\text{ and }\quad\zeta_{ji}\leq\delta.

Proof.

\Longrightarrow

We have:

G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}(\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{G}(\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})))\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}=\beta_{j}+\delta<1.

We deduce that there exists an index

\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{G}(\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}))=G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}=\beta_{j}+\delta<1

\gamma_{ji}>\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})\quad\text{ and }\quad\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})\leq\beta_{j}+\delta

\gamma_{ji}>0\text{ and }\forall l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}\,,\,\gamma_{ji}>\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})\text{ and }\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})\leq\beta_{j}+\delta.

(39)

By (Lemma
3
), we know that for all
l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, we have:

\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})\leq\beta_{j}+\delta=\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}\Longleftrightarrow\sigma_{G}(\beta_{l},\gamma_{li},\beta_{j})\leq\delta

so (

\zeta_{ji}=\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\sigma_{G}(\beta_{l},\gamma_{li},\beta_{j})\leq\delta.

To establish the inequality
\theta_{ji}<\delta
, we will show that:

\forall l\in V(j,i)\,,\,\beta_{l}-\gamma_{ji}<\delta.

Let
l\in V(j,i)
i.e.,
\gamma_{ji}\leq\gamma_{li}
and by (

\gamma_{ji}>\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})

\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})=\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}<\gamma_{ji}.

Then, we have:

\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-\gamma_{ji}=\max(\beta_{l}-\delta,0)-\gamma_{ji}=\max(\beta_{l}-\gamma_{ji}-\delta,-\gamma_{ji})<0

\beta_{l}-\gamma_{ji}<\delta
and finally

\theta_{ji}=\max_{l\in V(j,i)}\beta_{l}-\gamma_{ji}<\delta
.

\Longleftarrow
:

Let
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}

\gamma_{ji}>0,\quad\theta_{ji}<\delta,\quad\zeta_{ji}\leq\delta.

We have:

G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}=\min_{1\leq i^{\prime}\leq n}(\gamma_{ji^{\prime}}\rightarrow_{G}(\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\min(\gamma_{li^{\prime}},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}))\leq\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{G}(\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})).

We will show that we have:

\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{G}(\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}.

From the general equality:
x\rightarrow_{G}\max(y,z)=\max(x\rightarrow_{G}y,x\rightarrow_{G}z)

\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{G}(\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}))=\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{G}\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}).

\forall l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\},\,\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{G}\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}=\beta_{j}+\delta.

l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}

\bullet\,
We suppose that
l\in V(j,i)
i.e.,

\beta_{l}-\gamma_{li}\leq\beta_{l}-\gamma_{ji}\leq\theta_{ji}<\delta.

We deduce:

\beta_{l}-\gamma_{li}-\delta\leq\beta_{l}-\gamma_{ji}-\delta<0\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma_{li}\geq\gamma_{ji}>0.

So:

\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-\gamma_{li}=\max(\beta_{l}-\gamma_{li}-\delta,-\gamma_{li})<0

which leads to

\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})=\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}
.
On the other hand,
\beta_{l}-\gamma_{ji}-\delta<0\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma_{ji}>0
lead to

\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-\gamma_{ji}=\max(\beta_{l}-\gamma_{ji}-\delta,-\gamma_{ji})<0

so

\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}<\gamma_{ji}
and we obtain

\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{G}\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})=\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}).

By taking into account
\sigma_{G}(\beta_{l},\gamma_{li},\beta_{j})\leq\zeta_{ji}\leq\delta

\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}

so

\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{G}\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}=\beta_{j}+\delta.

\bullet\,
If we suppose

\gamma_{li}\geq\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})

\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{G}\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})=\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}).

As by hypothesis we have

\min(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}.

So:

We have established (

.

\theta_{11}=\beta_{1}-\gamma_{11}=0.1-0.6=-0.5
,

•

\theta_{12}=\max(\beta_{1}-\gamma_{12},\beta_{2}-\gamma_{12})=\max(0.1-0.49,0.4-0.49)=-0.09
,

•

\zeta_{11}=\max(\sigma_{G}(0.1,0.6,0.1),\sigma_{G}(0.4,0.26,0.1))=0.15
,

•

\zeta_{12}=\max(\sigma_{G}(0.1,0.49,0.1),\sigma_{G}(0.4,0.9,0.1))=0.15
.

We take
\delta=0.15
and we have
\gamma_{11}>0
,
\theta_{11}<\delta
and
\zeta_{11}=\delta
.

Then we observe that
G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{1}=\min(0.6\rightarrow_{G}0.25,0.49\rightarrow_{G}0.25)=0.25\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{1}=0.25
. So the inequality is solved with
\delta=0.15
.

Proposition 6
.

Let
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
. Suppose that
\nabla_{j}<1-\beta_{j}
. Then, there exists
i^{0}\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
such that:

\gamma_{ji^{0}}>0\quad\text{ and }\quad\nabla_{j}\geq\max(\theta_{ji^{0}},\zeta_{ji^{0}}).

Proof.

The inequality
\nabla_{j}<1-\beta_{j}
and the properties of the lower bound
\nabla_{j}=\inf E_{j}
allow us to find a sequence
(\delta_{k})
in
[0,1]
such that:

\forall k\,,\,\delta_{k}\in E_{j}\,\,\text{i.e}\,\,G(\underline{\beta}(\delta_{k}))_{j}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta_{k})_{j},\quad(\delta_{k})\rightarrow\nabla_{j},\quad\forall k\,,\,\nabla_{j}\leq\delta_{k}<1-\beta_{j}.

By applying (Lemma
4
) to each
\delta_{k}
, we get an integer
i_{k}\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
such that:

\gamma_{ji_{k}}>0,\quad\theta_{ji_{k}}<\delta_{k},\quad\zeta_{ji_{k}}\leq\delta_{k}.

The sequence of integers
k\mapsto i_{k}
takes its values in the finite set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, so by (Theorem 3.6) of
[
12
]
, it admits a subsequence
k\mapsto i_{\alpha(k)}
, which is
stationary
(see the proof of (Theorem 3.6) of
[
12
]
). Therefore, we have an integer
i^{0}\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
such that:

\forall k\,,\,i_{\alpha(k)}=i^{0}.

Then, we deduce:

\gamma_{ji^{0}}>0,\quad\forall k\,,\,\theta_{ji^{0}}<\delta_{\alpha(k)},\quad\zeta_{ji^{0}}\leq\delta_{\alpha(k)}.

By passage to the limit when
k\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain:

\theta_{ji^{0}}\leq\nabla_{j},\quad\zeta_{ji^{0}}\leq\nabla_{j}.

∎

As a direct consequence, we have:

Corollary 1
.

Let
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
. Suppose that
\nabla_{j}<1-\beta_{j}
. Then, the set
{\cal A}_{j}
, see (

), is non empty.

From (Proposition
6
) and (Corollary

), we establish a formula for computing
\nabla_{j}
if
\nabla_{j}<1-\beta_{j}
:

Proposition 7
.

Suppose that
\nabla_{j}<1-\beta_{j}
, then we have:

\nabla_{j}=\min_{i\in{\cal A}_{j}}\,\max(\theta_{ji},\zeta_{ji}).

Proof.

We put
\tau_{j}=\min_{i\in{\cal A}_{j}}\,\max(\theta_{ji},\zeta_{ji})
and we will show

\nabla_{j}=\tau_{j}
in the following two steps:

•

\nabla_{j}\geq\tau_{j}
: by (Proposition
6
), we have an index
i^{0}\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
such that:

\gamma_{ji^{0}}>0,\quad\max(\theta_{ji^{0}},\zeta_{ji^{0}})\leq\nabla_{j}.

Therefore
i^{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
and
\max(\theta_{ji^{0}},\zeta_{ji^{0}})\leq\nabla_{j}
and we deduce:

\tau_{j}\leq\max(\theta_{ji^{0}},\zeta_{ji^{0}})\leq\nabla_{j}.

•

\nabla_{j}=\tau_{j}
. To prove this equality, we proceed by contradiction: we suppose that
\nabla_{j}\neq\tau_{j}
. We then have
\nabla_{j}>\tau_{j}
. The set

being non-empty (Corollary

), let
i\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
such that
\tau_{j}=\max(\theta_{ji},\zeta_{ji})
, so we have:

\gamma_{ji}>0,\quad\max(\theta_{ji},\zeta_{ji})=\tau_{j}<\nabla_{j}.

(41)

We take a number
\delta
verifying
\tau_{j}<\delta<\nabla_{j}
, then
\delta
satisfy:

and the index

By (Lemma

∎

We then give the explicit analytical formula for computing the Chebyshev distance

Theorem 2
.

\nabla=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\nabla_{j}\quad\text{where for }j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\},\quad\nabla_{j}=\min(1-\beta_{j},\tau_{j})

\text{ with }\quad\tau_{j}=\min_{i\in{\cal A}_{j}}\,\max(\theta_{ji},\zeta_{ji})\quad\text{ and the convention }\min_{\emptyset}=1.

Following (Proposition
5
), we have to prove that
\text{for all }j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\},\nabla_{j}=\inf E_{j}
is equal to
\min(1-\beta_{j},\tau_{j})
.

Proof.

We distinguish the following cases:

•

If
\beta_{j}=1
, we deduce from (Lemma
2
) that

\nabla_{j}=0=\min(1-\beta_{j},\tau_{j}).

•

Suppose that
\beta_{j}<1
. We then have:

–

If
\mathcal{A}_{j}=\emptyset
, then by (Lemma
2
) and (Corollary

), we have
\nabla_{j}=1-\beta_{j}
and
\tau_{j}=1
(by the convention). Therefore,
\nabla_{j}=\min(1-\beta_{j},\tau_{j}),

–

If
\mathcal{A}_{j}\neq\emptyset
, then there exists an index

such that
\tau_{j}=\max(\theta_{ji},\zeta_{ji})
.

Let us prove the inequality
\nabla_{j}\leq\tau_{j}
by contradiction.

Assume that we have
\tau_{j}<\nabla_{j}
. We know from (Lemma
2
) that
\nabla_{j}\leq 1-\beta_{j}
. Let
\delta
be a number verifying

, so
\nabla_{j}=\inf E_{j}\leq\delta

To summarize, we know that:

so
\nabla_{j}=\min(1-\beta_{j},\tau_{j})
.
∎

We illustrate this theorem:

Example 4
.

We continue (Example

As we will see in (Example

),

(the set

does not necessarily admit a minimum element. Our aim in what follows is to characterize the case in which

admits a minimum element i.e.,

We know from (Lemma

Lemma 5
.

Suppose that we have

, then for all

Proof.

\displaystyle\zeta_{ji}

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\sigma_{G}(\beta_{l},\gamma_{li},\beta_{j})

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\min(\dfrac{(\beta_{l}-\beta_{j})^{+}}{2},(\gamma_{li}-\beta_{j})^{+})

We use the following sets:

Notation 3
.

For
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
:

(42)

Lemma 6
.

Suppose we have
\beta_{j}<1
.
We have:
\widetilde{\nabla}_{j}\in E_{j}
.

Proof.

We distinguish the following two cases:

•

If
\mathcal{F}_{j}=\emptyset
, then
\widetilde{\nabla}_{j}=1\in E_{j}
.

•

If
\mathcal{F}_{j}\neq\emptyset
, let

From

By taking

We deduce from (Lemma

The characterization of the case where

admits a minimum element, here

Proposition 8
.

Suppose we have
\nabla_{j}<1-\beta_{j}
. Then, we have:

\nabla_{j}\leq\widetilde{\nabla}_{j}
,

2.

\nabla_{j}\in E_{j}\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\nabla_{j}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{j}.

We may have

Proof.

We know from (Lemma
6
) that
\widetilde{\nabla}_{j}\in E_{j}

implies that
\nabla_{j}\leq\widetilde{\nabla}_{j}.

To prove the equivalence of the second statement, since from (Lemma

\widetilde{\nabla}_{j}\in E_{j}

\nabla_{j}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{j}

\nabla_{j}\in E_{j}.

To prove the implication

, let us first remark that the hypothesis

.

As we suppose

by contradiction.

Suppose we have

We then have:

By applying (Lemma

) to

Finally, we have:

\nabla_{j}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{j}
.

We immediately deduce from (Lemma

Corollary 2
.

\nabla_{j}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{j}

.

We then deduce:

Corollary 3
.

If
\nabla\in E
, then

is the lowest Chebychev approximation for
\beta
. Moreover,
\xi=\Gamma^{t}\Box_{\min}^{\max}\underline{\beta}(\nabla)
is an approximate solution for the system

(\Sigma):\Gamma\Box_{\rightarrow_{G}}^{\min}x=\beta
.

(From the second part of the following proof, one can see that if

By definition of the application
G
, we have

is a consistent second member. By definition of

By applying the inequality (

.

The formula for computing the Chebyshev distance associated to the second member of a system of

\nabla=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\nabla_{j}

(Proposition

) and the set

is defined in (

, i.e.,

is always the minimum element of the set

although the Goguen’s implication

(Theorem

(Definition

We begin by introducing a new function based on the function

, see (

), which was introduced in

1
]
:

P(u,x,y,z)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }u=0,\\
0&\text{ if }y=0,\\
\sigma_{GG}(\dfrac{u}{y},x,1,z)&\text{ if }u>0\text{ and }y>0\end{cases}.

(43)

We have:

\sigma_{GG}(\dfrac{u}{y},x,1,z)=\max[(x-\dfrac{u}{y})^{+},\min(\dfrac{(xy-uz)^{+}}{u+y},1-z)].

(44)

We use the following notations:

Notation 4
.

For
1\leq j\leq m,1\leq i\leq n

\gamma_{ji}
of the matrix
\Gamma
of the system
(\Sigma)
, see (

,

•

The function
P
, see (

), allows us to solve a particular inequality:

Lemma 8
.

For all
u,x,y,z,\delta\in[0,1]
with
u>0
and
\delta<1-z
, we have:

\dfrac{y\,\underline{x}(\delta)}{u}\leq\overline{z}(\delta)\,\Longleftrightarrow\,P(u,x,y,z)\leq\delta.

Proof.

We remind that if
y>0
, the following equivalence was established in (Proposition 2) of
[
1
]
:

\underline{x}(\delta)\leq\dfrac{u}{y}(1\rightarrow_{GG}\overline{z}(\delta))\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\sigma_{GG}(\dfrac{u}{y},x,1,z)\leq\delta.

(45)

\Longrightarrow
:

If
y=0
, we have

P(u,x,y,z)=0\leq\delta.

If
y>0
, from the inequality
\overline{z}(\delta)<1
, we deduce :

\underline{x}(\delta)\leq\dfrac{u}{y}\overline{z}(\delta)=\dfrac{u}{y}(1\rightarrow_{GG}\overline{z}(\delta)).

So, by (

), we have
P(u,x,y,z)=\sigma_{GG}(\dfrac{u}{y},x,1,z)\leq\delta
.

\Longleftarrow
:

Suppose that

.

\bullet

If
y=0
, the inequality
\dfrac{y\,\underline{x}(\delta)}{u}\leq\overline{z}(\delta)
is trivial.

\bullet

If

, since
\overline{z}(\delta)<1
, the inequality to prove is:

\underline{x}(\delta)\leq\dfrac{u}{y}\overline{z}(\delta))=\dfrac{u}{y}(1\rightarrow_{GG}\overline{z}(\delta)).

As we have

, by (

.

Proof.

\Longrightarrow
:

We have:

G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{GG}[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\gamma_{li}\cdot\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}]\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}=\beta_{j}+\delta<1.

We deduce that there exists
i\in\{1,2,\dots n\}
such that:

\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{GG}[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}]=.G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}=\beta_{j}+\delta<1

which implies

\gamma_{ji}>\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{GG}[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}]=\dfrac{1}{\gamma_{ji}}\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}\leq\beta_{j}+\delta

so we have

\gamma_{ji}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\forall l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}\,,\,\gamma_{ji}>\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}\quad\text{ and }\quad\dfrac{\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}}{\gamma_{ji}}\leq\beta_{j}+\delta=\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}<1.

(46)

By (Lemma
8
), since
\delta<1-\beta_{j}
, we know that for all
l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, we have:

\dfrac{\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}}{\gamma_{ji}}\leq\beta_{j}+\delta=\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}\Longleftrightarrow P(\gamma_{ji},\beta_{l},\gamma_{li},\beta_{j})\leq\delta

so
\zeta_{ji}:=\max_{1\leq l\leq m}P(\gamma_{ji},\beta_{l},\gamma_{li},\beta_{j})\leq\delta
.

To establish the inequality
\theta_{ji}<\delta
, we just have to show that we have:

\forall l\in W(j,i)\,,\,\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}<\delta.

(We have
W(j,i)\not=\emptyset
, because
\gamma_{ji}>0
, so
j\in W(j,i)
)

Let
l\in W(j,i)
i.e.,

0<\gamma_{ji}\leq\gamma_{li}
, and we deduce from (
46
):

\dfrac{\gamma_{li}}{\gamma_{ji}}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}=\max(\dfrac{\gamma_{li}}{\gamma_{ji}}\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{li}}{\gamma_{ji}}\delta,0)<1.

Therefore, we have:

\dfrac{\gamma_{li}}{\gamma_{ji}}\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{li}}{\gamma_{ji}}\delta<1

so
\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}<\delta
and

\theta_{ji}:=\max_{l\in W(j,i)}(\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}})<\delta.

\Longleftarrow
:

Let
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
such that

\gamma_{ji}>0,\quad\theta_{ji}<\delta,\quad\zeta_{ji}\leq\delta.

We have:

G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}=\min_{1\leq i^{\prime}\leq n}\gamma_{ji^{\prime}}\rightarrow_{GG}[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\gamma_{li^{\prime}}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}]\leq\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{GG}[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}]

and it suffices to show that:

\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{GG}[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}]\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}

From the general equality
x\rightarrow_{GG}\max(y,z)=\max(x\rightarrow_{GG}y,x\rightarrow_{GG}z)
, we deduce:

\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{GG}[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}]=\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{GG}\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}.

We will establish that

\forall l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}\,,\,\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{GG}\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}=\beta_{j}+\delta.

(47)

Let
l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
. We distinguish the following two cases:

•

Suppose that
l\in W(j,i)
, i.e.,
\gamma_{ji}\leq\gamma_{li}
. We have
\gamma_{li}>0
. Then, we have

\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}\leq\theta_{ji}<\delta.

We deduce:

\dfrac{\gamma_{li}}{\gamma_{ji}}\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{li}}{\gamma_{ji}}\delta<1

.
so

\dfrac{\gamma_{li}}{\gamma_{ji}}\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}=\max(\dfrac{\gamma_{li}}{\gamma_{ji}}\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{li}}{\gamma_{ji}}\delta,0)<1

which leads to

\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{GG}\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}=\dfrac{\gamma_{li}}{\gamma_{ji}}\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}.

By the hypothesis, we have
P(\gamma_{ji},\beta_{l},\gamma_{li},\beta_{j})\leq\zeta_{ji}\leq\delta
.

As
\gamma_{ji}>0
and
\delta<1-\beta_{j}
, by (Lemma
8
), we deduce:

\dfrac{\gamma_{li}}{\gamma_{ji}}\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}\leq\beta_{j}+\delta.

So, in this case, (
47
) is proved.

•

Suppose that
l\in W(j,i)^{c}
i.e.
\gamma_{ji}>\gamma_{li}
. Since
\gamma_{li}\geq\gamma_{li}\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}
, we have:

\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{GG}\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}=\dfrac{\gamma_{li}}{\gamma_{ji}}\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}.

As in the previous case, by the inequality
P(\gamma_{ji},\beta_{l},\gamma_{li},\beta_{j})\leq\zeta_{ji}\leq\delta
and (Lemma
8
) we then deduce:

\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{GG}\gamma_{li}\,\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}=\dfrac{\gamma_{li}}{\gamma_{ji}}\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}\leq\beta_{j}+\delta.

So, we proved (
47
).

∎

\displaystyle=\min(0.6\rightarrow_{GG}(\max_{1\leq l\leq 2}\gamma_{l1}\cdot\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}),0.49\rightarrow_{GG}(\max_{1\leq l\leq 2}\gamma_{l2}\cdot\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}))

and then:

and

. So the inequality

For solving the inequality, we rely on:

Although for the t-norm product, its associated residual implicator
\rightarrow_{GG}

We begin by establishing two useful lemmas to show that we have
\nabla_{j}\in E_{j}
for all
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
u,x,y,z\in[0,1]
satisfy the following two conditions:

1.

0<u<y

2.

0<x-\dfrac{u}{y}<1-z
.

Then, we have :

\dfrac{(xy-uz)^{+}}{u+y}=\dfrac{xy-uz}{u+y}>x-\dfrac{u}{y}.

Proof.

Since by hypothesis we have
x-\dfrac{u}{y}>0
and
u+y>0
,
We just have to show the strict inequality

\dfrac{xy-uz}{u+y}>x-\dfrac{u}{y}
.

We have:

\displaystyle\dfrac{xy-uz}{u+y}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{xy+xu-xu-uz}{u+y}

\displaystyle=x-\dfrac{u}{u+y}(x+z)

\displaystyle>x-\dfrac{u}{u+y}(1+\dfrac{u}{y})

\displaystyle=x-\dfrac{u}{y}>0.

∎

Lemma 11
.

We have:

\forall i\in{\cal A}_{j}\,,\,\theta_{ji}\leq\zeta_{ji}.

Proof.

Let
i\in{\cal A}_{j}
, i.e.,
\gamma_{ji}>0
, we deduce:

W(j,i)=\{l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}\,\mid\,\gamma_{ji}\leq\gamma_{li}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\gamma_{li}>0\}\not=\emptyset.

We recall that
\theta_{ji}:=\max_{l\in W(j,i)}(\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}})
and

\zeta_{ji}=\max_{1\leq l\leq m}P(\gamma_{ji},\beta_{l},\gamma_{li},\beta_{j})
.

For all
l\in W(j,i)
, let us show the inequality:

\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}\leq\zeta_{ji}.

Since
\gamma_{ji}>0
and
\gamma_{li}>0
, we have:

\displaystyle\zeta_{ji}

\displaystyle\geq P(\gamma_{ji},\beta_{l},\gamma_{li},\beta_{j})

\displaystyle=\max((\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}})^{+},\,\min(\dfrac{(\beta_{l}\gamma_{li}-\beta_{j}\gamma_{ji})^{+}}{\gamma_{ji}+\gamma_{li}},1-\beta_{j}))\quad\text{see (\ref{eq:egalitesursigmagg})}

\displaystyle\geq(\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}})^{+}

\displaystyle\geq\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}.

Therefore

\zeta_{ji}\geq\max_{l\in W(j,i)}(\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}})=\theta_{ji}.

∎

We are now able to show that for
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, we have
\nabla_{j}\in E_{j}
i.e.,
\nabla_{j}=\min E_{j}
, and therefore
\nabla\in E
i.e.,
\nabla=\min E
.

Theorem 4
.

For all
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, we have:

\nabla_{j}=\min E_{j}\quad\text{ i.e.,}\quad\nabla_{j}\in E_{j}.

Therefore:

\nabla=\min E.

Proof.

From (Lemma
2
), we know that
1-\beta_{j}\in E_{j}
and
\nabla_{j}\leq 1-\beta_{j}
.

It remains to show that
\nabla_{j}=\min E_{j}
under the hypothesis
\nabla_{j}<1-\beta_{j}
.

From (Theorem
3
) and (Lemma
11
) we know:

\nabla_{j}=\min(1-\beta_{j},\tau_{j})=\tau_{j}<1\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{j}=\min_{i\in{\cal A}_{j}}\max(\theta_{ji},\zeta_{ji})=\min_{i\in{\cal A}_{j}}\zeta_{ji}.

Therefore

{\cal A}_{j}\not=\emptyset
. Let
i\in{\cal A}_{j}
i.e.,
\gamma_{ji}>0
, such that:

\nabla_{j}=\tau_{j}=\max(\theta_{ji},\zeta_{ji})=\zeta_{ji}.

(48)

To show that
\nabla_{j}\in E_{j}
, we will prove that
\nabla_{j}
satisfies the second assumption of (Lemma
9
). Taking into account the hypothesis
\nabla_{j}<1-\beta_{j}
and the equality
\nabla_{j}=\zeta_{ji}
, see (
48
), we have to show the strict inequality:

\theta_{ji}<\zeta_{ji}.

(49)

Let us prove this strict inequality
\theta_{ji}<\zeta_{ji}
in the following two steps:

\bullet\,
If
\nabla_{j}=0
: we must prove that
\theta_{ji}<0
.

Since
W(j,i)\neq\emptyset
(we have
j\in W(j,i)
), let
l\in W(j,i)
be such that

\theta_{ji}=\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}
.
We have

\displaystyle 0=\nabla_{j}=

\displaystyle\zeta_{ji}

\displaystyle\geq P(\gamma_{ji},\beta_{l},\gamma_{li},\beta_{j})

\displaystyle=\max((\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}})^{+},\min(\dfrac{(\beta_{l}\gamma_{li}-\beta_{j}\gamma_{ji})^{+}}{\gamma_{ji}+\gamma_{li}},1-\beta_{j}))\quad\text{see (\ref{eq:egalitesursigmagg})}

Since
1-\beta_{j}>\nabla_{j}=0
, we deduce
\dfrac{(\beta_{l}\gamma_{li}-\beta_{j}\gamma_{ji})^{+}}{\gamma_{ji}+\gamma_{li}}=0
, which implies

\beta_{l}\gamma_{li}\leq\beta_{j}\gamma_{ji}
. We have:

\displaystyle\theta_{ji}

\displaystyle=\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}\beta_{j}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}(\beta_{j}-1)<0.

\bullet\,
If
\nabla_{j}>0
: let us prove by contradiction the strict inequality
\theta_{ji}<\zeta_{ji}
.

Assume we have

\theta_{ji}\geq\zeta_{ji}.
Then, by (Lemma
11
) we have:

\theta_{ji}=\zeta_{ji}.

Let
l\in W(j,i)
be such that
\theta_{ji}=\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}
. So we have

\nabla_{j}=\zeta_{ji}=\theta_{ji}=\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}>0.

(50)

We then have:

\displaystyle 1-\beta_{j}

\displaystyle>\nabla_{j}

\displaystyle=\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}

\displaystyle=\zeta_{ji}

\displaystyle\geq P(\gamma_{ji},\beta_{l},\gamma_{li},\beta_{j})\quad\text{see (\ref{eq:egalitesursigmagg})}

\displaystyle=\max(\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}},\min(\dfrac{(\beta_{l}\gamma_{li}-\beta_{j}\gamma_{ji})^{+}}{\gamma_{ji}+\gamma_{li}},1-\beta_{j})).

Therefore:

\min(\dfrac{(\beta_{l}\gamma_{li}-\beta_{j}\gamma_{ji})^{+}}{\gamma_{ji}+\gamma_{li}},1-\beta_{j})\leq\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}<1-\beta_{j}.

This last inequality implies:

\dfrac{(\beta_{l}\gamma_{li}-\beta_{j}\gamma_{ji})^{+}}{\gamma_{ji}+\gamma_{li}}\leq\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}<1-\beta_{j}.

We put
u=\gamma_{ji},x=\beta_{l},y=\gamma_{li},z=\beta_{j}
. By the previous calculations, we have:

•

0<u<y
because
l\in W(j,i)
and
\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}=\nabla_{j}>0
,

•

0<x-\dfrac{u}{y}=\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}<1-\beta_{j}=1-z
.

Applying (Lemma
10
), we get:

\dfrac{(xy-uz)^{+}}{u+y}>x-\dfrac{u}{y}.

We replace
u,x,y,z
with their values. This leads to the following inequality:

\dfrac{(\beta_{l}\gamma_{li}-\beta_{j}\gamma_{ji})^{+}}{\gamma_{ji}+\gamma_{li}}>\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}

which contradicts

\dfrac{(\beta_{l}\gamma_{li}-\beta_{j}\gamma_{ji})^{+}}{\gamma_{ji}+\gamma_{li}}\leq\beta_{l}-\dfrac{\gamma_{ji}}{\gamma_{li}}.

We have therefore shown the strict inequality

\theta_{ji}<\zeta_{ij}
, see (
49
). Therefore
\nabla_{j}\in E_{j}
.

As a consequence, since we proved that for all
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
we have
\nabla_{j}\in E_{j}
, we deduce from (Lemma
1
) and (Proposition

) that
\nabla=\min E\text{ i.e., }\nabla\in E
.
∎

, see (

Same proof as (Corollary

∎

In this section, we study the Chebyshev distance

, see (Definition

), associated to the second member of a system of

, see (

is the Łukasiewicz implication, see (

To establish the formula for computing the Chebyshev distance
\nabla
, we prove a version of (Lemma
4
) (or (Lemma
9
)) for the
\min-\rightarrow_{L}
system (Lemma
13
). In (Lemma

), we have retained the two notations
\nabla_{j}
and
\zeta_{ji}
from previous sections with their own definitions for the
\min-\rightarrow_{L}
system (but not the notation
\theta_{ji}
, which is not needed).

Since Łukasiewicz’s implication is continuous with respect to its two variables, establishing the formula for
\nabla
(Theorem

), is easier than for
\min-\rightarrow_{G}
and
\min-\rightarrow_{GG}
systems.

We remind that we have
\nabla=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\nabla_{j}
where
\nabla_{j}=\inf E_{j}
(Proposition
5
) and the set
E_{j}
is defined in (
32
). Unlike the
\min-\rightarrow_{G}
system, we will show that in the case of the
\min-\rightarrow_{L}
system (as it is the same for the
\min-\rightarrow_{GG}
system), for all
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
, we always have
\nabla_{j}\in E_{j}
, i.e.,
\nabla_{j}
is always the minimum element of the set
E_{j}
. By applying (Lemma
1
), we obtain
\nabla=\min E
, where
E
is defined in (
31
). Therefore, for a
\min-\rightarrow_{L}
system, the Chebyshev distance
\nabla
is always a minimum, see (Corollary
8
). In fact, we can always compute the lowest Chebyshev approximation of the second member
and an approximate solution of the system
(\Sigma)
, see (Corollary
7
).

We rely on the following function:

L(u,v,x,y)=\max((u-y)^{+},\min((x-v)^{+},\dfrac{(x-y+u-v)^{+})}{2})).

(51)

and we introduce new notations:

Notation 5
.

For
1\leq j\leq m,1\leq i\leq n
, to each coefficient
\gamma_{ji}
of the matrix
\Gamma
of the system
(\Sigma)
, see (
22
), we associate:

\zeta_{ji}=\max_{1\leq l\leq m}L(1-\gamma_{ji},1-\gamma_{li},\beta_{l},\beta_{j}).

The function
L
allows us to solve an inequality:

Lemma 12
.

Let
u,v,x,y
in
[0,1]
. For all
\delta\in[0,1-y[
, we have the following equivalence:

(\underline{x}(\delta)-v)^{+}\leq\overline{y}(\delta)-u\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\delta\geq L(u,v,x,y).

(52)

Proof.

\Longrightarrow
:

\bullet
We have
\delta\geq(u-y)^{+}
. Indeed, we deduce from the inequality of the left side of (
52
) and the hypothesis
y+\delta<1
that we have:

0\leq\overline{y}(\delta)-u=\min(y+\delta-u,1-u)=y+\delta-u,

so

\delta\geq u-y
. As
\delta\geq 0
, we obtain that
\delta\geq(u-y)^{+}
.

\bullet
Let us prove
\delta\geq\min((x-v)^{+},\dfrac{(x-y+u-v)^{+})}{2})
.

The associativity of the function
\max
(i.e,
\max(x,y,z)=\max(\max(x,y),z)
leads to the equality:

(\underline{x}(\delta)-v)^{+}=(x-\delta-v)^{+}.

(53)

Indeed, we have:

\displaystyle(\underline{x}(\delta)-v)^{+}

\displaystyle=\max(\underline{x}(\delta)-v,0)

\displaystyle=\max(\underline{x}(\delta),v)-v

\displaystyle=\max(\max(x-\delta,0),v)-v

\displaystyle=\max(x-\delta,0,v)-v

\displaystyle=\max(x-\delta-v,-v,0)

\displaystyle=\max((x-\delta-v)^{+},-v)=(x-\delta-v)^{+}

We then deduce:

\displaystyle(\underline{x}(\delta)-v)^{+}=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow(x-\delta-v)^{+}=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow x-\delta\leq v

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow x-v\leq\delta

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow(x-v)^{+}\leq\delta.

(54)

It is clear that we have:

\delta\geq(x-v)^{+}\Longrightarrow\delta\geq\min((x-v)^{+},\dfrac{(x-y+u-v)^{+})}{2}).

Suppose now that
\delta<(x-v)^{+}
. Then by (
6
), we have:

0<(\underline{x}(\delta)-v)^{+}=\underline{x}(\delta)-v.

and the hypothesis becomes:

0<\underline{x}(\delta)-v\leq\overline{y}(\delta)-u=y+\delta-u

The inequality
\underline{x}(\delta)-v\leq\overline{y}(\delta)-u=y+\delta-u
is equivalent to

\max(x-\delta,0)-v-[y+\delta-u]=\max(x-y+u-v-2\delta,-v-[y+\delta-u])\leq 0.

so
x-y+u-v-2\delta\leq 0
. As
\delta\geq 0
, we also have
(x-y+u-v)^{+}\leq 2\delta
. Finally, we have:

\delta\geq\dfrac{(x-y+u-v)^{+}}{2}\geq\min((x-v)^{+},\dfrac{(x-y+u-v)^{+}}{2}).

To summarize, we have proven that
\delta\geq\max((u-y)^{+},\min((x-v)^{+},\dfrac{(x-y+u-v)^{+})}{2}))=L(u,v,x,y).

\Longleftarrow:

We must show the inequality:
(\underline{x}(\delta)-v)^{+}\leq\overline{y}(\delta)-u.

We note that, by hypothesis, we have
\overline{y}(\delta)=y+\delta<1
.

\bullet\,
Let us show that
\overline{y}(\delta)-u=y+\delta-u
is positive. In fact, we deduce from the hypothesis:

\delta\geq(u-y)^{+}\geq u-y\quad\text{ so }\quad y+\delta-u\geq 0.

(55)

\bullet
If
(\underline{x}(\delta)-v)^{+}=0
, the inequality
(\underline{x}(\delta)-v)^{+}\leq\overline{y}(\delta)-u
is clear.

\bullet
Suppose that
(\underline{x}(\delta)-v)^{+}>0
. We deduce from (
53
) that:

0<(x-\delta-v)^{+}=x-\delta-v\quad\text{so}\quad\delta<x-v=(x-v)^{+}.

As we suppose that

\delta\geq\max((u-y)^{+},\min((x-v)^{+},\dfrac{(x-y+u-v)^{+}}{2}))
, we deduce:

\delta\geq\dfrac{(x-y+u-v)^{+}}{2}\geq\dfrac{x-y+u-v}{2}.

(56)

Let us show now the inequality
(\underline{x}(\delta)-v)^{+}\leq\overline{y}(\delta)-u
. We have:

\displaystyle(\underline{x}(\delta)-v)^{+}-[\overline{y}(\delta)-u]

\displaystyle=\underline{x}(\delta)-v-[y+\delta-u]

\displaystyle=\max(x-y+u-v-2\delta,-v-[y+\delta-u])\leq 0,

because the numbers
x-y+u-v-2\delta
and
-v-[y+\delta-u]
are negative (see (
55
) and (
56
)).

∎

The version of (Lemma
4
) (or (Lemma

)) for the

system is:

Lemma 13
.

Let
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
.
For all
\delta\in[0,1]
such that
\delta<1-\beta_{j}
, there is an equivalence between the following two statements:

1.

G(\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}

2.

There exists
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
such that:

\delta\geq\zeta_{ji}.

Proof.

\Longrightarrow:

We have:

\displaystyle G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}

\displaystyle=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\,\gamma_{ji}\rightarrow_{L}[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,T_{L}(\gamma_{li},\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l})]

\displaystyle=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\,\min(1-\gamma_{ji}+[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+}],1)

The hypothesis implies that we have an index
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
such that:

G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}=\min(1-\gamma_{ji}+[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+}],1)\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}.

As we suppose that
\beta_{j}+\delta<1
, we have
\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}=\beta_{j}+\delta<1
, and we deduce:

G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}=1-\gamma_{ji}+[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+}]\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}<1.

For all
l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, we deduce:

(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}-(1-\gamma_{ji}).

Let us show that
\forall l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}\,,\,\delta\geq L(1-\gamma_{ji},1-\gamma_{li},\beta_{l},\beta_{j})
. We have for all
l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
:

(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}-(1-\gamma_{ji}).

We put
u=1-\gamma_{ji},v=1-\gamma_{li},x=\beta_{l},y=\beta_{j}
. With these notations, we can easily check that we have:

\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1=\underline{x}(\delta)-v\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}-(1-\gamma_{ji})=\overline{y}(\delta)-u.\quad

The previous inequality is rewritten as:

(\underline{x}(\delta)-v)^{+}\leq\overline{y}(\delta)-u

and we also have
\delta<1-\beta_{j}=1-y
.

By applying (Lemma
12
), we obtain
\delta\geq L(u,v,x,y)=L(1-\gamma_{ji},1-\gamma_{li},\beta_{l},\beta_{j})
.

We have shown
\delta\geq\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,L(1-\gamma_{ji},1-\gamma_{li},\beta_{l},\beta_{j})=\zeta_{ji}
.

\Longleftarrow
:

Suppose we have an index
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
such that:

\delta\geq\zeta_{ji}=\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,L(1-\gamma_{ji},1-\gamma_{li},\beta_{l},\beta_{j}).

We have:

G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}=\min_{1\leq i^{\prime}\leq n}\,\min(1-\gamma_{ji^{\prime}}+[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,(\gamma_{li^{\prime}}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+}],1)\leq\min(1-\gamma_{ji}+[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+}],1).

Let us show the inequality

\min(1-\gamma_{ji}+[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+}],1)\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j},

(57)

which will lead to the inequality to be proved.

We have:

1-\gamma_{ji}+[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+}]=\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,(1-\gamma_{ji}+(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+}).

We deduce:

\displaystyle\min(1-\gamma_{ji}+[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+}],1)

\displaystyle=\min([\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,(1-\gamma_{ji}+(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+})],1)

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,\min(1-\gamma_{ji}+(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+},1).

To prove (
57
), it is sufficient to check that
\forall l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\},\,\min(1-\gamma_{ji}+(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+},1)\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}
.

In fact, we just have to show that for all
l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
, that we have:

1-\gamma_{ji}+(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+}\leq\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}.

(58)

Let
l\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
. By hypothesis, we have:

\delta\geq\zeta_{ji}\geq P(1-\gamma_{ji},1-\gamma_{li},\beta_{l},\beta_{j}).

As

(\underline{x}(\delta)-v)^{+}\leq\overline{y}(\delta)-u.

Therefore:

(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+}=(\underline{x}(\delta)-v)^{+}\leq\overline{y}(\delta)-u=\overline{\beta}(\delta)_{j}-(1-\gamma_{ji})

This last inequality is equivalent to the inequality (

For all

Proof.

For any
\delta\in[0,1]

G(\underline{\beta}(\delta))_{j}=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\,\min(1-\gamma_{ji}+[\max_{1\leq l\leq m}\,(\gamma_{li}+\underline{\beta}(\delta)_{l}-1)^{+}],1).

Since the functions

(59)

We now give the explicit analytical formula for computing the Chebyshev distance
\nabla
(see (Definition

) and (Proposition

\beta

Theorem 5
.

\nabla=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\nabla_{j}\quad\text{where for }\quad j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\},\quad\nabla_{j}=\min(1-\beta_{j},\tau_{j})

\text{ with }\quad\tau_{j}=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\,\zeta_{ji}.

Proof.

From (Lemma
2
), we know that
\nabla_{j}\leq 1-\beta_{j}
.

Let us show the following implication:

\nabla_{j}<1-\beta_{j}\Longrightarrow\nabla_{j}=\tau_{j}.

Indeed, if
\nabla_{j}<1-\beta_{j}
, as we have
\nabla_{j}\in E_{j}
, applying (Lemma

), we obtain an index

such that
\nabla_{j}\geq\zeta_{ji}
. From the definition of
\tau_{j}
, we deduce:

\tau_{j}\leq\zeta_{ji}\leq\nabla_{j}.

As
\nabla_{j}<1-\beta_{j}
, we get
\tau_{j}<1-\beta_{j}
. By applying (Lemma
14
), we finally obtain
\nabla_{j}\leq\tau_{j}
, so
\nabla_{j}=\tau_{j}
.

Since the inequality
\nabla_{j}\leq 1-\beta_{j}
is true (Lemma

) and using the two implications

\nabla_{j}<1-\beta_{j}\Longrightarrow\nabla_{j}=\tau_{j}\quad\text{and by (Lemma \ref{lemma:lastlemmaforL})}\quad\tau_{j}<1-\beta_{j}\Longrightarrow\nabla_{j}\leq\tau_{j}

lead us to obtain the equality
\nabla_{j}=\min(1-\beta_{j},\tau_{j}).

7
Conclusion

In this article, we studied the inconsistency of systems of
\min-\rightarrow
fuzzy relational equations. We expressed the Chebyshev distance
\nabla=\inf_{d\in\mathcal{D}}\|\beta-d\|
associated to an inconsistent system of
\min-\rightarrow
fuzzy relational equations
\Gamma\Box_{\rightarrow}^{\min}x=\beta
, where
\mathcal{D}
is the set of second members of consistent systems defined with the same matrix
\Gamma
, as the lower bound of the solutions of a vector inequality. From this result, we gave explicit analytical formulas for computing the Chebyshev distances for systems of
\min-\rightarrow
fuzzy relational equations
\Gamma\Box_{\rightarrow}^{\min}x=\beta
, where
\rightarrow
is a residual implicator among the Gödel implication, the Goguen implication or Łukasiewicz’s
implication. An important result is that in the case of the
\min-\rightarrow_{G}
system, the Chebyshev distance
\nabla
may be an infimum while for
\min-\rightarrow_{GG}
and
\min-\rightarrow_{L}
systems the Chebyshev distance
\nabla
is always a minimum. For
\min-\rightarrow_{G}
systems, we added a sufficient condition for
\nabla
to be a minimum. For
\min-\rightarrow_{GG}
systems,
\min-\rightarrow_{L}
systems, or
\min-\rightarrow_{G}
systems whose Chebyshev distance has been verified as a minimum, we can always compute the lowest Chebyshev approximation of the second member and an approximate solution. However, for the
\min-\rightarrow_{G}
systems whose Chebyshev distance has been verified as not being a minimum, the set of Chebyshev approximations of its second member is empty.

As perspectives, we may study the structure of the approximate solutions set of a system of
\min-\rightarrow
fuzzy relational equations
\Gamma\Box_{\rightarrow}^{\min}x=\beta
and that of the set of Chebyshev approximations of the second member
\beta
with respect to the Chebyshev distance
\nabla
. For a
\max-\min
system, the structure of these sets was given in
[
2
]
. One of the difficulties encountered is the determination of the set of maximal Chebyshev approximations of the second member
\beta
, which may be obtained from the solutions of a particular system of
\min-\rightarrow
inequalities (as it is done for a
\max-\min
system in
[
2
]
). Currently, to our knowledge, there is no extensive study of the solutions set of systems of
\min-\rightarrow
inequalities. Similarly to
[
2
]
, we may also develop a learning method of approximate weight matrices based on
\min-\rightarrow
composition according to training data.

As applications, we have the complete solution of the problem of the invertibility of a fuzzy matrix for
\min-\rightarrow
composition, where
\rightarrow
is the Gödel implication, the Goguen implication or Łukasiewicz’s
implication: we know the set of matrices which admit a pre-inverse or a post-inverse. In
[
15
]
, this problem was tackled in the case of the
\max-\min
composition.

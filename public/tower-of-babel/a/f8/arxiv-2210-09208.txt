Chebyshev pseudosite matrix product state approach for the spectral functions of electron-phonon coupling systems
Pei-Yuan Zhao, Ke Ding, Shuo Yang

Abstract
The electron-phonon ( e e -ph) coupling system often has a large number of phonon degrees of freedom, whose spectral functions are numerically difficult to compute using matrix product state (MPS) formalisms. To solve this problem, we propose a new and practical method that combines the Chebyshev MPS and the pseudosite density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) algorithm. The Chebyshev vector is represented by a pseudosite MPS with global U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) fermion symmetry, which maps 2 N p 2^{N_{p}} bosonic degrees of freedom onto N p N_{p} pseudosites, each with two states. This approach can handle arbitrary e e -ph coupling Hamiltonians where pseudosite DMRG performs efficiently. We use this method to study the spectral functions of the doped extended Hubbard-Holstein model in a regime of strong Coulomb repulsion, which has not been studied extensively before. Key features of the excitation spectra are captured at a modest computational cost. Our results show that weak extended e e -ph couplings can increase the spectral weight of the holon-folding branch at low phonon frequencies, in agreement with angle-resolved photoemission observations on one-dimensional (1D) cuprates.

In this article, we provide a simple and computationally efficient method for calculating the spectral functions of strongly correlated systems with extended
e
-ph couplings.
We combine the
U(1)
-symmetric pseudosite MPS (SPS-MPS) formalism with the Chebyshev MPS approach for the first time.
This method is used to investigate the (anti-)photoemission spectral functions of the doped extended HHM with large Hubbard

For SPS-MPS, the fermionic and bosonic degrees of freedom of a lattice site are replaced by multiple pseudosites, where a real boson with

The transformation from the original Hamiltonian to the pseudosite Hamiltonian involves reshaping and decomposing the matrix product operator (MPO), which can be easily done in the MPO-MPS representation.
The global

Singh et al. 2010
;
Singh et al. 2011

The Chebyshev pseudosite MPS approach is used to calculate the zero-temperature spectral functions of an electron-phonon coupling system

\displaystyle G(\omega)=\langle\psi_{0}|\hat{O}^{\dagger}\delta(\omega-\hat{H}+E_{0})\hat{O}|\psi_{0}\rangle,

-symmetric pseudosite MPSs and MPOs.
Our method can calculate the spectral functions of an arbitrary
e
-ph coupling Hamiltonian as long as pseudosite DMRG can efficiently compute its ground state.

We first calculate the Green’s function of the Holstein polaron (HP) and the (anti-)photoemission spectral functions of the half-filled HHM in the Mott insulator phase to serve as a benchmark.

II.2
Chebyshev pseudosite MPS method

We now introduce the Chebyshev pseudosite MPS method step by step.
First, the standard DMRG
White 1992
;
White 1993
;
White 2005
;
Jeckelmann and White 1998
is used to compute the ground state
|\psi_{0}\rangle
with an eigenenergy
E_{0}
.
After that, the spectral function Eq. (
1
) is expanded by the Chebyshev polynomials
Weiße et al. 2006

\displaystyle T_{n}(\omega^{\prime}),\ -1\leq\omega^{\prime}\leq 1.

(11)

The domain of the spectral function Eq. (
1
) is
\omega\in[0,E_{\mathrm{max}}-E_{0}]
, where
E_{\mathrm{max}}
is the maximal eigenvalue of the Hamiltonian.
While
E_{\mathrm{max}}
scales with system size, the non-zero spectral weight can only be found in
\omega\in[0,\mathcal{W}_{A}]
, where
\mathcal{W}_{A}
is the spectral width.
In practice, we use the energy window
\omega\in[-\omega_{2\mathrm{max}},\omega_{1\mathrm{max}}]
, where
\omega_{1\mathrm{max}}
is set to
2\mathcal{W}_{A}\sim 3\mathcal{W}_{A}
, and
\omega_{2\mathrm{max}}
is chosen depending on the model.

Since the proper domain of
T_{n}(\omega^{\prime})
is
[-1,1]
, we map the energy window to this range.

\displaystyle\omega\mapsto\omega^{\prime},\ \omega^{\prime}=\frac{\omega}{a}+b,\ \omega^{\prime}\in[-W^{\prime},W^{\prime}],

(12)

where

\displaystyle a=\frac{\omega_{1\mathrm{max}}+\omega_{2\mathrm{max}}}{2W^{\prime}},\ b=\frac{\omega_{2\mathrm{max}}-\omega_{1\mathrm{max}}}{\omega_{2\mathrm{max}}+\omega_{1\mathrm{max}}}W^{\prime},

(13)

and
W^{\prime}
is a number slightly smaller than
1
.
Correspondingly, the Hamiltonian is rescaled and shifted.

\displaystyle\hat{H}\mapsto\hat{H}^{\prime},\ \hat{H}^{\prime}=\frac{\hat{H}-E_{0}}{a}+b,\ E^{\prime}\in[b,\frac{E_{\mathrm{max}}-E_{0}}{a}+b].

(14)

The spectral function is further represented as

\displaystyle G(\omega)

\displaystyle=\langle\psi_{0}|\hat{O}^{\dagger}\delta(\omega+E_{0}-\hat{H})\hat{O}|\psi_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}\langle\psi_{0}|\hat{O}^{\dagger}\delta(\omega^{\prime}-\hat{H}^{\prime})\hat{O}|\psi_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=G(\omega^{\prime}).

(15)

The first
N_{\mathrm{C}}
terms of
T_{n}(\omega^{\prime})
are employed to expand
G(\omega^{\prime})
approximately,

\displaystyle\delta(\omega^{\prime}-\hat{H}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{1-\omega^{\prime 2}}}[g_{0}+2\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{C}}-1}g_{n}T_{n}(\hat{H}^{\prime})T_{n}(\omega^{\prime})],

(16)

\displaystyle G(\omega^{\prime})=\frac{1}{a\pi\sqrt{1-\omega^{\prime 2}}}[g_{0}\mu_{0}+2\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{C}}-1}g_{n}\mu_{n}T_{n}(\omega^{\prime})],

(17)

where

\displaystyle\mu_{n}=\langle\psi_{0}|\hat{O}^{\dagger}T_{n}(\hat{H}^{\prime})\hat{O}|\psi_{0}\rangle

(18)

are the Chebyshev moments.
The damping factors
g_{n}
are used to suppress the Gibbs oscillations
Weiße et al. 2006
caused by the truncation of the Chebyshev series.
In this study, we use the Jackson damping

\displaystyle g_{n}=\frac{(N_{\mathrm{C}}-n+1)\mathrm{cos}\frac{\pi n}{N_{\mathrm{C}}+1}+\mathrm{sin}\frac{\pi n}{N_{\mathrm{C}}+1}\mathrm{cot}\frac{\pi}{N_{\mathrm{C}}+1}}{N_{\mathrm{C}}+1}.

(19)

Since the Chebyshev polynomials
T_{n}(\omega^{\prime})
are known, once we have calculated the expansion coefficients
\mu_{n}
in Eq. (
18
), the spectral function Eq. (
17
) will be obtained.

\mu_{n}
can be computed by performing Chebyshev iterations

\displaystyle|t_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\hat{O}|\psi_{0}\rangle,\ \ \ \ |t_{1}\rangle=\hat{H}^{\prime}|t_{0}\rangle,

\displaystyle|t_{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\hat{H}^{\prime}|t_{n}\rangle-|t_{n-1}\rangle,

(20)

where
|t_{n}\rangle
are Chebyshev vectors and

\displaystyle\mu_{n}=\langle t_{0}|t_{n}\rangle.

(21)

The focus of this article is on the photoemission and antiphotoemission spectral functions of the eHHM with a fixed number of fermions,

\displaystyle A^{(-)}(k,\omega)=\langle\psi_{0}|\hat{c}_{k,\sigma}^{\dagger}\delta(-\omega+E_{0}-\hat{H})\hat{c}_{k,\sigma}|\psi_{0}\rangle,

(22)

\displaystyle A^{(+)}(k,\omega)=\langle\psi_{0}|\hat{c}_{k,\sigma}\delta(\omega+E_{0}-\hat{H})\hat{c}_{k,\sigma}^{\dagger}|\psi_{0}\rangle.

(23)

We refer to

as the sum of photoemission (
22
) and antiphotoemission (

\displaystyle A(k,\omega)=A^{(-)}(k,\omega)+A^{(+)}(k,\omega),\int A(k,\omega)d\omega=1.

(24)

The Fourier transformations of fermionic creation and annihilation operators are introduced for open boundary conditions

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{k,\sigma}^{\dagger}

IV
Conclusion

In summary, we propose a simple and efficient approach for calculating the spectral functions of
e
-ph coupling systems by combing the
U(1)
-symmetric pseudosite MPS and the Chebyshev MPS approach.
We map a real boson with
2^{N_{p}}
states to
N_{p}
hard-core boson pseudosites by reshaping and decomposing the bosonic MPO.
We demonstrate the effectiveness of this method by numerically solving the Green’s function of the Holstein polaron and the spectra of the half-filled HHM in the Mott insulator phase.
We show that key aspects of the excitation spectra can be captured at a modest computational cost.
Furthermore, we investigate the doped eHHM with on-site, NN, and NNN
e
-ph interactions and find that extended
e
-ph couplings can significantly influence the spectra.
These interactions may play an important role in the physics of high-temperature superconductors.

Our approach is compatible with Fermi-Bose Hamiltonians where pseudosite DMRG performs well.
It works for Hamiltonians with long-range
e
-ph couplings and may be extended to higher dimensions by combining pseudosite methods with projected entangled-pair states (PEPS)
Verstraete and Cirac 2004
;
Cirac et al. 2021
.
Given its flexibility and efficiency, we believe our method will be a valuable and essential tool for investigating strongly correlated electron-phonon coupling systems.

Model spaces invariant under composition operators
P. Muthukumar, Jaydeb Sarkar

Abstract
. Given a holomorphic self-map φ \varphi of 𝔻 \mathbb{D} (the open unit disc in ℂ \mathbb{C} ), the composition operator C φ ​ f = f ∘ φ C_{\varphi}f=f\circ\varphi , f ∈ H 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ) f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{\mathbb{D}}) , defines a bounded linear operator on the Hardy space H 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ) H^{2}(\mathbb{\mathbb{D}}) . The model spaces are the backward shift-invariant closed subspaces of H 2 ​ ( 𝔻 ) H^{2}(\mathbb{\mathbb{D}}) , which are canonically associated with inner functions. In this paper, we study model spaces that are invariant under composition operators. Emphasis is put on finite-dimensional model spaces, affine transformations, and linear fractional transformations.

1.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the open unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
and let
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
be the Hardy space over
\mathbb{D}
. For
\varphi
a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
, define the
composition operator

C_{\varphi}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
by

C_{\varphi}f=f\circ\varphi\qquad(f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})).

It follows from the Littlewood subordination principle that
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}))

[
22
]
. Here
\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}))
denote the set of all bounded linear operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

The theory of composition operators is highly interdisciplinary with its natural connections to complex analysis, linear dynamics, complex geometry, and functional analysis. For instance, the question of the existence of nontrivial invariant subspaces of bounded linear operators on Hilbert spaces can be formulated in terms of minimal invariant subspaces of composition operators induced by hyperbolic automorphisms of
\mathbb{D}

[
18
,
19
,
20
]
.

The theme of this paper is also in line with the invariant subspaces of composition operators, but restricted to model (or inner function based quotient) spaces of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Recall that a function
\theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
is said to be
inner
if
|\theta|=1
a.e. on
\partial\mathbb{D}
in the sense of nontangential boundary values, where
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
denotes the commutative Banach algebra of all bounded holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. Given an inner function
\theta
, the
model space

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is the quotient space

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\ominus\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\cong H^{2}(\mathbb{D})/\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).

The aim of this paper is to describe some results and methods of the calculation of model spaces that are invariant under composition operators. Note, on the other hand, a celebrated result of A. Beurling
[
1
]
states that a closed subspace
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is invariant under
M_{z}^{*}
if and only if
\mathcal{Q}
is a model space. Here
M_{z}f=zf
,
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, and
M_{z}^{*}
is the backward shift

M_{z}^{*}f=\frac{f(z)-f(0)}{z}\qquad(f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})).

In other words, we are interested in joint invariant subspaces of the (non commuting) pair
(M_{z}^{*},C_{\varphi})
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Yet another motivation comes from the recent classification
[
2
]
of joint invariant subspaces of the non commuting pair
(M_{z},C_{\varphi})
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. In this context, we further remark that the concept of joint invariant subspaces of
(M_{z},C_{\varphi})
was independently introduced by Chalendar and Partington
[
4
]
and Mahvidi
[
16
]
. For recent results related to the subject we refer to
[
2
,
14
,
17
]
and the references therein.

Often results on composition operators are case-based. For instance, the theory of
C_{\varphi}
corresponding to holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
as

\varphi(z)=az+b\text{ or }\frac{az+b}{cz+d},

is extremely demanding and complete answers to many basic questions are not known (cf.
[
7
,
6
]
, and also see
[
11
,
15
]
for more recent accounts). Even norm computations and invariant subspaces of
C_{\varphi}
for linear fractional transformations are known to be case-based (cf.
[
3
,
18
]
). In fact, the results of this paper are also no exception. For example, Theorem
3.1
states: Let
\varphi
be a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
. Fix
\alpha\in\mathbb{D}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and consider the inner function

\theta(z)=\left(\frac{z-\alpha}{1-\bar{\alpha}z}\right)^{n}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Then
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
if and only if there exist scalars
a,b
, and
c(\neq 0)
such that

(1.1)

\varphi(z)=\begin{cases}a+bz&\mbox{if }\alpha=0\text{ and }n>1\\
\frac{1-c}{\bar{\alpha}}+cz&\mbox{if }\alpha\neq 0,\end{cases}

where,
|a|+|b|\leq 1
and
|\frac{1-c}{\bar{\alpha}}|+|c|\leq 1
.

Note that the above model space
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is an
n
-dimensional Hilbert space. Even though we all know the structure of finite-dimensional model spaces (they are essentially parameterized by
n
-Blaschke products) but we do not know a general method to classify holomorphic self-maps
\varphi
of
\mathbb{D}
for which a given finite-dimensional model space remains invariant under
C_{\varphi}
(see Question
3.3
). In order to make this problem more convincing, in Example
3.4
we consider the
2
-dimensional model space
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
corresponding to the (special) Blaschke product
\theta(z)=z\frac{z-\alpha}{1-\bar{\alpha}z}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and prove that
C_{\varphi}\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
if and only if
\varphi
is the special Möbius map

\varphi(z)=\frac{(c_{1}-1)+(\bar{\alpha}+c_{2})z}{\bar{\alpha}(c_{1}+c_{2}z)}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

for some scalars
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that at least one of them is nonzero (see (
3.2
)). The above answers do not seem to tell us much about a possible answer to our general question.

We also put special emphasis on composition operators induces by linear fractional symbols (cf. Theorem
4.5
):
Let
\theta
be an inner function, and let
\varphi(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}
be a nonconstant holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
, where
ad-bc=1
. Suppose
\sigma(z)=\frac{\bar{a}z-\bar{c}}{-\bar{b}z+\bar{d}}
and
\theta(0)=0
. If we denote
\psi=(\frac{\theta}{z}\circ\sigma)(\frac{\theta}{z})^{-1}
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
.

(2)

\psi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
or
\psi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
.

(3)

\frac{\theta}{z}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is invariant under
C_{\sigma}
.

A similar (but not same as above) consideration also holds for composition operators induced by affine transformations (see Theorem
4.3
).

In the application part, we consider direct interpretations of our results to model spaces and reducing subspaces of composition operators. For instance, Theorem
5.3
states that:

(1)

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
for all inner function
\theta
if and only if
\varphi
is the identity map.

(2)

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
for all holomorphic self-map
\varphi
if and only if
\theta(z)=\alpha z
or
\theta\equiv\alpha
, where
\alpha
is an unimodular constant.

We now discuss the issue of reducing subspaces of composition operators from our perspective. Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. A closed subspace
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is called reducing subspace for

(or
\mathcal{M}
reduces

) if both
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\bot}
are invariant under
T
. It is known that
M_{z}
on

is irreducible, that is,
M_{z}
has no nontrivial reducing subspaces. In the setting of composition operators, if
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
\text{Re}(\alpha)>0
, then
C_{\varphi_{\alpha}}
is also irreducible
[
18
, Theorem 1.3]
, where

\varphi_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{(2-\alpha)z+\alpha}{-\alpha z+(2+\alpha)}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Another example is the composition operator corresponding to univalent loxodromic type self-map with nonzero Denjoy-Wolff point
[
13
, Theorem 1]
. At the other extreme, we discuss reducibility of compositor operators restricted to model spaces. In Theorem
5.5
, we prove the following: Let
\varphi
be a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{D}
,
n\geq 1
, and suppose
\theta(z)=\left(\frac{z-\alpha}{1-\bar{\alpha}z}\right)^{n}
. Then
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
reduces
C_{\varphi}
if and only if there exist a scalar
c
,
|c|\leq 1
, and
\psi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
,
\|\psi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, such that

\varphi(z)=\begin{cases}z\psi&\mbox{if }\alpha=0\text{ and }n=1\\
cz&\mbox{if }\alpha=0\text{ and }n\geq 2\\
z&\mbox{if }\alpha\neq 0.\end{cases}

Finally, we make a general remark. Recall that a linear fractional transformation
\varphi(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}
is a self-map of
\mathbb{D}
if and only if

|b\bar{d}-a\bar{c}|+|ad-bc|\leq|d|^{2}-|c|^{2}.

In particular, since
\varphi
is a holomorphic self-map, the inequalities following (
1.1
) are automatically true. In this paper, whenever no confusion can arise, we will not explicitly mention the above inequality for linear fractional transformations that are self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
. For instance, the above inequality is applicable to Theorem
3.1
, Example
3.4
, and Theorem
4.3
.

2.
Beurling type invariant subspaces

We begin with the formal definition of Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Recall that the Hardy space is the function Hilbert space of all holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
whose power series have square-summable coefficients. The following equivalent description of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is well known:

H^{2}(\mathbb{D})=\Big\{f\in\text{Hol}(\mathbb{D}):\|f\|:=\Big[\sup_{0\leq r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{it})|^{2}dt\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty\Big\}.

Also we denote the closed unit ball of
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
(which is popularly known as Schur class) by

{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{D})=\{\psi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}):\|\psi\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|\psi(z)|\leq 1\}.

We follow the standard notation: Given a bounded linear operator
T
on some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, the lattice of all closed
T
-invariant subspaces is denoted by
\text{Lat}T
, that is

\text{Lat}T=\{\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{H}\text{ closed subspace}:T\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}\}.

By Beurling’s Theorem, we know that

\text{Lat}M_{z}=\{\{0\},\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}):\theta\text{ inner}\},\text{ and }\text{Lat}M_{z}^{*}=\{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}),\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}:\theta\text{ inner}\}.

A closed subspace
\mathcal{S}\subseteq H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is said to be a
Beurling type invariant subspace
if
\mathcal{S}=\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
for some inner function
\theta
, or equivalently (in view of Beurling),
\mathcal{S}
is invariant under
M_{z}
. The purpose of this short section is to recall the classification of Beurling type invariant subspaces of composition operators
[
2
, Theorem 2.3]
. However, the present version is a simple refinement of
[
2
]
, and we only prove the new component of the result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\theta
be an inner function and
\varphi
be a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\in\text{Lat}C_{\varphi}
.

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{\theta\circ\varphi}{\theta}\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{D})
.

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{\theta\circ\varphi}{\theta}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
.

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{\theta\circ\varphi}{\theta}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.
The equivalence
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
follows from
[
2
, Theorem 2.3]
. Since

{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{D})\subsetneq H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\subsetneq H^{2}(\mathbb{D}),

it follows that
(2)\Rightarrow(3)\Rightarrow(4)
. Therefore, it suffices to verify that
(4)\Rightarrow(2)
. Suppose that
\frac{\theta\circ\varphi}{\theta}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Since
\theta
is inner, we have

\left|\frac{\theta\circ\varphi}{\theta}(e^{it})\right|=|(\theta\circ\varphi)(e^{it})|\leq 1\mbox{~for all~}t\in[0,2\pi]\mbox{~a.e.}

Therefore,
\frac{\theta\circ\sigma}{\theta}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
with
\left\|\frac{\theta\circ\varphi}{\theta}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1

[
9
, Theorem 2.11]
, that is,
\frac{\theta\circ\varphi}{\theta}\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{D})
, which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

This result will play a key role in what follows.

3.
Finite-dimensional model spaces

Recall that the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is associated with the Szegö kernel
k:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, where

k(z,w)=(1-z\bar{w})^{-1}\qquad(z,w\in\mathbb{D}).

Therefore, the linear span of
\{k(\cdot,w):w\in\mathbb{D}\}
is dense in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, and
f(w)=\langle f,k(\cdot,w)\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}
for all
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
w\in\mathbb{D}
. For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{D}
, define the
Blaschke factor

b_{\alpha}
by

b_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{z-\alpha}{1-\bar{\alpha}z}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

It is known that a model space
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is finite dimensional if and only if
\theta
is a finite Blaschke product (cf.
[
21
, Theorem 3.8]
). More specifically, if
\text{dim}\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}=n<\infty
, then there exists
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\in\mathbb{D}
, and
n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}=n
and

\theta=\prod_{i=1}^{m}b_{\alpha_{i}}^{n_{i}}.

Moreover,
\{c_{\alpha_{i}}^{(l_{i})}:0\leq l_{i}\leq n_{i}-1,1\leq i\leq m\}

is a basis for
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
(see
[
12
, Proposition 5.16]
), where

c_{\alpha}^{(t)}:=\frac{z^{t}}{(1-\bar{\alpha}z)^{t+1}}\qquad(t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}).

In the following, we prove that a “simple” finite dimensional models space is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
if and only if
\varphi
is a special affine map.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{D}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and suppose
\theta(z)=\left(\frac{z-\alpha}{1-\bar{\alpha}z}\right)^{n}
. Then
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\in\text{Lat}C_{\varphi}
if and only if there exist scalars
a,b
, and
c(\neq 0)
such that

\varphi(z)=\begin{cases}a+bz&\mbox{if }\alpha=0\text{ and }n>1\\
\frac{1-c}{\bar{\alpha}}+cz&\mbox{if }\alpha\neq 0.\end{cases}

Proof.
In view of the preceding discussion, we have

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}=\text{span}\left\{\frac{p(z)}{(1-\bar{\alpha}z)^{n}}:p\in\mathbb{C}[z],\text{deg }p\leq n-1\right\}.

Suppose
\alpha=0
. Then
\theta(z)=z^{n}
and

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}=\mbox{span}\{1,z,z^{2},\ldots,z^{n-1}\}.

Let
\varphi=a+bz
. Clearly, if
f=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j}z^{j}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
, then

f\circ\varphi=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j}\varphi^{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j}(a+bz)^{j},

and hence
C_{\varphi}f=f\circ\varphi\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
. For the converse part, suppose
C_{\varphi}\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
. Since the identity map
f(z)=z
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
, is in
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
, if follows that

f\circ\varphi=\varphi\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta},

which implies that
\varphi
is a polynomial. Also note that
g\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
, where
g(z)=z^{n-1}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then

g\circ\varphi=\varphi^{n-1}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta},

which implies that

(n-1)\mbox{deg}(\varphi)=\mbox{deg}(\varphi^{n-1})\leq n-1.

Since
n-1\neq 0
, it follows that
\mbox{deg}(\varphi)\leq 1
, and hence
\varphi
is an affine map. Now suppose that
\alpha\neq 0
, and let
C_{\varphi}\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
. Since
k(\cdot,\alpha)\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
, it follows that

k(\cdot,\alpha)\circ\varphi=\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\bar{\alpha}\varphi}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta},

and hence, there exists a polynomial
p
of degree
\leq n-1
such that

\frac{1}{1-\bar{\alpha}\varphi}=\frac{p(z)}{(1-\bar{\alpha}z)^{n}}.

In particular,
\varphi
is a nonconstant polynomial. Suppose
\mbox{deg}(\varphi)=m>0
. Since

g=\frac{1}{(1-\bar{\alpha}z)^{n}}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta},

we have

g\circ\varphi=\frac{1}{(1-\bar{\alpha}\varphi)^{n}}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}.

Thus, there exists
q\in\mathbb{C}[z]
such that

(1-\bar{\alpha}\varphi)^{n}q(z)=(1-\bar{\alpha}z)^{n}.

Comparing the degrees of both sides, we have
mn\leq n
, which implies
\mbox{deg}(\varphi)=1
. Then
\mbox{deg}(q)=0
, and hence
q
is a nonzero constant. Therefore, there exists a nonzero scalar
c
such that
1-\bar{\alpha}\varphi=c(1-\bar{\alpha}z)
, that is

\varphi(z)=\frac{1-c}{\bar{\alpha}}+cz.

We now turn to the converse direction. Let
c\neq 0
and
\varphi(z)=\frac{1-c}{\bar{\alpha}}+cz
. Then
1-\bar{\alpha}\varphi=c(1-\bar{\alpha}z)
, and hence

\left(\frac{1}{1-\bar{\alpha}\varphi}\right)^{l}\in\mbox{span}\left\{\frac{1}{(1-\bar{\alpha}z)^{j}}:j=1,\ldots,n\right\}=\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}

for all
l=1,2,\ldots,n
. It follows that
f\circ\varphi\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
for all
f\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We would like to point out that even affine transformations (as symbols) play a prominent role in the complexity of composition operators (cf. Deddens
[
7
]
).

Remark 3.2
.

In the context of Theorem
3.1
, we note that if
\alpha=0
and
n=1
, then
\mathcal{Q}_{z}=(zH^{2}(\mathbb{D}))^{\bot}
, which is the one dimensional subspace of all constant functions in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Clearly,
\mathcal{Q}_{z}
is invariant under every composition operator.

We do not know the answer to the following compelling question.

Question 3.3
.

Consider the finite Blaschke product
\prod_{i=1}^{m}b_{\alpha_{i}}
corresponding to
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
. Characterize holomorphic self-maps
\varphi
of
\mathbb{D}
such that

C_{\varphi}\mathcal{Q}_{\prod_{i=1}^{m}b_{\alpha_{i}}}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{\prod_{i=1}^{m}b_{\alpha_{i}}}.

We also put forward the following simpler (two-dimensional) version: Suppose
\alpha\neq\beta
in
\mathbb{D}
, and suppose
\theta=b_{\alpha}b_{\beta}
. Classify holomorphic self-maps
\varphi
of
\mathbb{D}
such that
C_{\varphi}\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
.

We do not know the complete answer to this question but the following special case (
\alpha\neq 0
and
\beta=0
):

Example 3.4
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{D}
be a nonzero scalar, and suppose
\theta=zb_{\alpha}
. Let
\varphi
be a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
. Then
C_{\varphi}\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
if and only if
\varphi
is the special Möbius map

(3.2)

\varphi(z)=\frac{(c_{1}-1)+(\bar{\alpha}+c_{2})z}{\bar{\alpha}(c_{1}+c_{2}z)}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

for some scalars
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that at least one of them is nonzero. Indeed, here the
2
-dimensional model space
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is given by
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}=\text{span}\{1,k(\cdot,\alpha)\}
. As the space of all constant functions is invariant under every composition operators (see Remark
3.2
),
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
if and only if

k(\cdot,\alpha)\circ\varphi=\frac{1}{1-\bar{\alpha}\varphi}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}.

This is equivalent to the fact that

\frac{1}{1-\bar{\alpha}\varphi}=\frac{c_{1}+c_{2}z}{1-\bar{\alpha}z}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

for some scalars
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that at least one of them is nonzero, which is equivalent to (
3.2
). This completes the proof of the claim.

Putting together the representations of linear fractional transformations from (
3.2
) and Theorem
3.1
, it is perhaps evident that the answer to Question
3.3
will be also case-based.

4.
Linear fractional symbols

Our aim in this section is to describe the structure of model invariant subspaces of composition operators whose symbols are linear fractional transformations mapping
\mathbb{D}
into itself. We are also motivated by the intricate analysis presented in Cowen
[
6
]
and Deddens
[
7
]
.

Before we begin with a general observation, we introduce the following (standard) notation: For each
\varphi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, we denote by
M_{\varphi}
the multiplication operator
M_{\varphi}f=\varphi f
,
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. It is well known that
M_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}))
with
\|M_{\varphi}\|=\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
for all
\varphi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, and
\{M_{z}\}^{\prime}=\{M_{\varphi}:\varphi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\}
, where
\{M_{z}\}^{\prime}
denotes the commutator of
M_{z}
.

Let
\varphi
be a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
and let
\theta
be an inner function. Clearly,
C_{\varphi}\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
if and only if

C_{\varphi}^{*}(\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}))\subseteq\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, which is, by the Douglas range inclusion theorem
[
8
, Theorem 1]
(as
\mbox{ran}(C_{\varphi}^{*}M_{\theta})\subseteq\mbox{ran}M_{\theta}
), equivalent to the existence of some
X\in\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}))
such that

C_{\varphi}^{*}M_{\theta}=M_{\theta}X.

Now if the above equality holds, then
\theta X(f)=C_{\varphi}^{*}(\theta f)
for all
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, and hence zeros of
\theta
are also zeros of
C_{\varphi}^{*}(\theta f)
and

\text{multiplicity}_{w}\theta\leq\text{multiplicity}_{w}C_{\varphi}^{*}(\theta f),

for all
w\in\mathcal{Z}(\theta)
(the zero set of
\theta
). This implies that
\frac{1}{\theta}C_{\varphi}^{*}(\theta f)
is a well defined holomorphic function on
\mathbb{D}
. Therefore, we have proved the following general (but perhaps less practical) characterization.

Lemma 4.1
.

Q_{\theta}
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
if and only if
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\mapsto\frac{1}{\theta}C_{\varphi}^{*}M_{\theta}f
defines a bounded linear operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Now we turn to linear fractional transformations. The following adjoint formula is due to Cowen
[
6

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\varphi(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}

Then

Note that if
\varphi

\theta(0)=\langle 1,\theta\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}=0

In what follows, we denote by

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\theta
be an inner function, and let
\varphi(z)=az+b,a\neq 0
. Suppose
\sigma(z)=\frac{\bar{a}z}{1-\bar{b}z}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\in\text{Lat}C_{\varphi}
.

(2)

\frac{\theta\circ\sigma}{\theta}\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
.

(3)

\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\in\text{Lat}C_{\sigma}
.

Proof.
If we apply Theorem
4.2
to the case when
c=0
and
d=1
, we get

C_{\varphi}^{*}=M_{\psi}C_{\sigma},

where
\psi(z)=\frac{1}{1-\bar{b}z}
. Now suppose that
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
. By Lemma
4.1
, we know that the map

Xf=\frac{1}{\theta}C_{\varphi}^{*}M_{\theta}f\qquad(f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})),

defines a bounded linear operator
X
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, and hence, using
C_{\varphi}^{*}=M_{\psi}C_{\sigma}
, we have

Xf=\frac{1}{\theta}C_{\varphi}^{*}M_{\theta}f=\frac{1}{\theta}\psi(\theta\circ\sigma)(f\circ\sigma)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}),

for all
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Applying this with
f=1
yields

\psi\frac{\theta\circ\sigma}{\theta}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).

But since
\frac{1}{\psi}=1-\bar{b}z\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, it follows that
\frac{\theta\circ\sigma}{\theta}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Theorem
2.1
then implies that
\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is invariant under
C_{\sigma}
. This proves
(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)
.

Now we assume that
C_{\sigma}(\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}))\subseteq\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Again, Theorem
2.1
implies that
\frac{\theta\circ\sigma}{\theta}\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{D})
, and therefore

\psi\frac{\theta\circ\sigma}{\theta}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})

\frac{1}{\theta}C_{\varphi}^{*}M_{\theta}
defines a bounded linear operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, and hence,
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}

(3)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(1)
and completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Now we consider model spaces that are invariant under composition operators induced by linear fractional transformations. However, we will be restricted to inner functions that vanish at the origin. In fact, our main tool is the following adjoint formula
[
23
, Equation (3.6)]
, which concerns functions vanishing at the origin:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\varphi(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}
be a nonconstant holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
, where
ad-bc=1
.
If
\sigma(z)=\frac{\bar{a}z-\bar{c}}{-\bar{b}z+\bar{d}}
, then

C_{\varphi}^{*}f(z)=z\sigma^{\prime}(z)\frac{f(\sigma(z))}{\sigma(z)}\qquad(f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}),f(0)=0).

As pointed our earlier, if
\theta
is an inner function, then the constant function
1\in\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
if and only if
\theta(0)=0
. In the following, under the assumption that the inner functions vanish at the origin, we present a classification of model spaces that are invariant under composition operators induced by linear fractional transformations. Recall that
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
is either
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
or
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\theta
be an inner function,
\theta(0)=0
, and let
\varphi(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}
be a nonconstant holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
, where
ad-bc=1
. Suppose
\sigma(z)=\frac{\bar{a}z-\bar{c}}{-\bar{b}z+\bar{d}}
and

\psi=\frac{\frac{\theta}{z}\circ\sigma}{\frac{\theta}{z}}.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}\in\text{Lat}C_{\varphi}
.

(2)

\psi\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
.

(3)

\frac{\theta}{z}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\in\text{Lat}C_{\sigma}
.

Proof.
Since
\theta(0)=0
, we see that
\frac{\theta}{z}
is an inner function. Suppose that
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
. Lemma
4.1
implies that

Xf=\frac{1}{\theta}C_{\varphi}^{*}(\theta f)\qquad(f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})),

defines a bounded linear operator
X
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
C_{\varphi}^{*}M_{\theta}=M_{\theta}X
. In view of Lemma
4.4
, it then follows that

Xf=\sigma^{\prime}\frac{z(\theta\circ\sigma)}{\theta\sigma}(f\circ\sigma)=\sigma^{\prime}\psi(f\circ\sigma),

where
\sigma^{\prime}
is the derivative of
\sigma
. In particular

X1=\sigma^{\prime}\psi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}),

from which, using the fact that
\frac{1}{\sigma^{\prime}}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})

\psi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. And finally, by Theorem
2.1
,
\displaystyle\frac{\theta}{z}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is invariant under
C_{\sigma}
, which proves
(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)
.

Now if
(3)
is true, then Theorem
2.1
implies that
(2)
is true, that is,
\psi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
. In particular, since
\sigma^{\prime}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
,
X
(defined as above) defines a bounded linear operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and satisfy the equality
C_{\varphi}^{*}M_{\theta}=M_{\theta}X
. By Lemma
4.1
,
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
, which complete the proof of
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
.
∎

We do not know, in general, how to classify an inner function
\theta
for which
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is invariant under a composition operator induced by a linear fractional transformation.

5.
Applications

The main goal of this section is to discuss some direct applications of our main results to some elementary invariant subspaces of composition operators.

First, we prove that the identity map is the only self-map of
\mathbb{D}
, which leaves all model spaces invariant.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
. Then
\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
for all inner function
\theta
if and only if
\varphi
is the identity map.

Proof.
If
\varphi(z)=z
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
C_{\varphi}=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}
(the identity operator). In particular,
\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
. Conversely, suppose
\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\in\mbox{Lat}C_{\varphi}
for all inner function
\theta
. Fix
\alpha\in\mathbb{D}
. Then
\alpha
is the only zero of the Blaschke factor (which is also an inner function)
b_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{\alpha-z}{1-\bar{\alpha}z}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Since
b_{\alpha}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\in\mbox{Lat}C_{\varphi}
, by assumption, Theorem
2.1
implies that

\frac{b_{\alpha}\circ\varphi}{b_{\alpha}}\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{D}).

This forces
b_{\alpha}(\varphi(\alpha))=0
, and hence
\varphi(\alpha)=\alpha
. Since
\alpha\in\mathbb{D}
is arbitrary, we have that
\varphi(z)=z
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Before examining the other extreme property of the invariant subspaces of composition operators, we need to recall the following fact which concerns fixed points of holomorphic self-maps
[
2
, Corollary 3.4]
: Let
\varphi
be a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
and let
w\in\mathbb{D}
be the fixed point of
\varphi

\theta
is an inner function and
\theta(w)\neq 0
, then
\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
if and only if
\theta\circ\varphi=\theta
.

Recall that a point
w\in\mathbb{D}
is said to be a
fixed point
of a holomorphic self-map
\varphi
of
\mathbb{D}

\varphi(w)=w.

Theorem 5.2

Let
\theta
be an inner function. Then
\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\in\text{Lat}C_{\varphi}

Proof.
If

\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\in\mbox{Lat}C_{\varphi}

Conversely, assume that
\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\in\text{Lat}C_{\varphi}
for all holomorphic self-map
\varphi
of
\mathbb{D}

\theta
has no zeros in
\mathbb{D}

\theta(\alpha)=0

\alpha\in\mathbb{D}

\theta

\theta(\beta)\neq 0

\varphi=b_{\beta}\circ b_{\alpha}.

Note that
\varphi
is an inner function and
\varphi(\alpha)=\beta

Since

We conclude this section by proving a reducing property of composition operators. Here we will be restricted to finite-dimensional model spaces, as in Theorem
3.1
. Before we begin this discussion, we recall the following result
[
2
, Corollary 2.4]
:

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
B
be a Blaschke product and let
\varphi
be a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
. Then
BH^{2}(\mathbb{D})\in\text{Lat}C_{\varphi}
if and only if
\text{multiplicity}_{w}B\leq\text{multiplicity}_{w}{B\circ\varphi}
for all
w\in\mathbb{D}
such that
B(w)=0
.

We are now ready for the reducing subspace property of elementary composition operators.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\varphi
be a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{D}
,
n\geq 1
, and suppose
\theta(z)=\left(\frac{z-\alpha}{1-\bar{\alpha}z}\right)^{n}
. Then
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
reduces
C_{\varphi}
if and only if there exist a scalar
c
,
|c|\leq 1
, and
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{D})
such that

\varphi(z)=\begin{cases}z\psi&\mbox{if }\alpha=0\text{ and }n=1\\
cz&\mbox{if }\alpha=0\text{ and }n\geq 2\\
z&\mbox{if }\alpha\neq 0.\end{cases}

Proof.
Suppose
\alpha=0
. If
n=1
, then the one-dimensional subspace
(zH^{2}(\mathbb{D}))^{\bot}=\mathcal{Q}_{z}
is invariant under every composition operator (see Remark
3.2
), where
zH^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
if and only if
\varphi=z\psi
for some
\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{D})
(see Theorem
5.4
). If
n\geq 2
, then, by Theorem
3.1
,
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
if and only if
\varphi
is an affine map. By Theorem
5.4
,
\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is invariant under
C_{\varphi}
if and only if
\varphi=z\psi
for some
\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{D})
. In particular,
\varphi(0)=0
, and hence
\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
reduces
C_{\varphi}
if and only if
\varphi(z)=cz
for some
|c|\leq 1
.

Finally, assume that
\alpha\neq 0
. If
\varphi
is the identity map, then
C_{\varphi}=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}
, and hence
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
reduces
C_{\varphi}
. Next, we assume that
\mathcal{Q}_{\theta}
reduces
C_{\varphi}
. By Theorem
3.1
, there exists a scalar
c(\neq 0)
such that
\varphi(z)=\frac{1-c}{\bar{\alpha}}+cz
. Again, by Theorem
5.4
,
(\theta\circ\varphi)(\alpha)=0
, and hence
\varphi(\alpha)=\alpha
. Now

\frac{1-c}{\bar{\alpha}}+c\alpha=\alpha,

is equivalent to

(1-c)(1-|\alpha|^{2})=0.

As
|\alpha|<1
, we must have
c=1
, that is,
\varphi(z)=z
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Finally, we would like to point out that the present theory of model spaces and composition operators complement the recent paper
[
2
]
. The classification of Beurling type invariant subspaces of composition operators (
[
2
, Theorem 2.3]
or Theorem
2.1
) also plays an important role in our consideration, and evidently, some of our results are in duality (cf. Theorem

and

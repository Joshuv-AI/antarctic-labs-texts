Abstract
. The convex hulls of face-vertex incident vectors of 3-face-colorable convex polytopes are computed. It’s found that every such convex hull is a d d -polytope with d + 2 d+2 or d + 3 d+3 vertices. Utilizing Gale transform and Gale diagram, we calculate its combinatorial structure. Finally, a necessary and sufficient criterion for combinatorial equivalence of two such convex hulls is given.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we focus on a class of 3-face-colorable convex polytope. A convex polytope
P
is
3-face-colorable
(
3-colorable
in abbreviation) if each facet of
P
can be assigned a number(color) among 1, 2, and 3, such that adjoin facets are equipped with pairwise different colors. Hence a 3-colorable convex polytope must be 3-dimensional and simple(each vertex belongs to exact three faces).

There is a conjecture proposed by D. W. Barnette in 1969, which says that the 1-skeleton of any 3-colorable polytope is Hamiltonian, i.e., such graph has a cycle passing through each vertex once. The conjecture is still open nowadays.

In this paper, we always assume that
P
is a 3-colorable polytope with
2n
vertices(3-colorable polytopes must have even number of vertices). Applying the Euler’s formula, we know that
P
has
2n
vertices,
3n
edges and
n+2
faces, i.e. the f-vector of
P
is
(2n,3n,n+2).

Let
V(P)
be the set of vertices of
P
. For any face
f
of
P
, set

\displaystyle\xi_{f}:

\displaystyle V(P)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\{0,1\}

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }v\in f;\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Now choose an order on
V(P)
, and identify
V(P)
by
\{1,2,\cdots,2n\}(|V|=2n)
. Then
\xi_{f}
could be identified by a
2n
-entries vector.
\xi_{f}
is called the
face-vertex incident vector
of
P
corresponding to face
f
.
Let
S
be the set of all face-vertex incident vectors of
P
, i.e.,

S=S(P)=\{\xi_{f}~|~f\text{ is a face of }P\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2n}.

As pointed out in
[

, when

is treated as a set of vectors in

where

is the two-element field,

[
2

In this paper we investigate the convex hull

Theorem

Theorem 2.1
.

\dim\widetilde{P}=\begin{cases}n-1,&\text{ if }m_{1}=m_{2}=m_{3};\\

Proof.

Set

A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0&-1\\
0&1&\cdots&0&-1\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots&-1\\
0&&\cdots&1&-1\end{bmatrix}_{(n+1)\times(n+2)}

A\begin{bmatrix}\xi_{f_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\xi_{f_{n+2}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\xi_{f_{1}}-\xi_{f_{n+2}}\\
\vdots\\
\xi_{f_{n+1}}-\xi_{f_{n+2}}\end{bmatrix}

Then
\dim\widetilde{P}=\text{Rank}_{\mathbb{R}}\begin{bmatrix}\xi_{f_{1}}-\xi_{f_{n+2}}\\
\vdots\\
\xi_{f_{n+1}}-\xi_{f_{n+2}}\end{bmatrix}=\text{Rank}_{\mathbb{R}}(A\begin{bmatrix}\xi_{f_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\xi_{f_{n+2}}\end{bmatrix})
.

As an analogy of corollary 5.4 in
[
1
]
,
\text{Rank}_{\mathbb{R}}S=n.

Applying the Sylvester rank inequality,

(n+1)+n-(n+2)\leq\dim\widetilde{P}\leq\min\{n+1,n\},

i.e.,

n-1\leq\dim\widetilde{P}\leq n.

Now we claim that
\dim\widetilde{P}=n-1
if and only if
m_{1}=m_{2}=m_{3}
.

Notice that
\sum_{f\in V_{i}}\xi_{f}=\underline{1},i=1,2,3,
where
\underline{1}
is the
2n
-entries vector whose entries are all 1(
[
1
]
Proposition 5.6 (2)).
Let
\widetilde{\xi_{f}}
be the vector obtained by adding another entry 1 at the end of
\xi_{f}
.
It’s easy to check that
\text{Rank}\{\widetilde{\xi_{f}}~|~f\}=\dim\widetilde{P}+1.
Hence

\sum_{f\in V_{i}}\widetilde{\xi_{f}}=(\underline{1},m_{i}),i=1,2,3.

Choose arbitrarily two vectors
v
and
w
in
V_{2}
and
V_{3}
respectively. It’s easy to check that

\{\xi_{f}~|~f\}-\{v,w\}
are linearly independent and

can be uniquely written as linear combinations:

v=\sum_{f\in V_{1}}\xi_{f}-\sum_{g\in V_{2}-\{v\}}\xi_{g},w=\sum_{f\in V_{1}}\xi_{f}-\sum_{g\in V_{3}-\{w\}}\xi_{g}.

\dim\widetilde{P}=n-1\Leftrightarrow\text{Rank}\{\widetilde{\xi_{f}}~|~f\}=n\Leftrightarrow\text{ both }\widetilde{v}\text{ and }\widetilde{w}
can be uniquely written as linear combinations of
\{\widetilde{\xi_{f}}~|~f\}-\{\widetilde{v},\widetilde{w}\}\Leftrightarrow\widetilde{v}=\sum_{f\in V_{1}}\widetilde{\xi_{f}}-\sum_{g\in V_{2}-\{v\}}\widetilde{\xi_{g}}=(v,m_{1}-m_{2}+1),\widetilde{w}=\sum_{f\in V_{1}}\widetilde{\xi_{f}}-\sum_{g\in V_{3}-\{w\}}\widetilde{\xi_{g}}=(w,m_{1}-m_{3}+1)\Leftrightarrow m_{1}=m_{2}=m_{3}.

∎

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
P
and
P^{\prime}
be two 3-dimensional convex 3-colorable polytopes. Then
\widetilde{P}
and
\widetilde{P^{\prime}}
are combinatorially equivalent if and only if

(1)

P
and
P^{\prime}
has same number of faces( or vertices, or edges, equivalently);

(2)

m(P)
and
m(P^{\prime})
has same type;

(3)

m_{2}(P)=m_{2}(P^{\prime})
.

Proof.

It’s sufficient to prove the necessarity.
Suppose
\widetilde{P}\cong\widetilde{P^{\prime}}
.

(1) Since their differences of number of vertices and dimension must be equal, both
m(P)
and
m(P^{\prime})
are of type IV or not. Notice that
\dim\widetilde{P}=f_{2}(P)-3
if
m(P)
is of type IV, and
\dim\widetilde{P}=f_{2}(P)-2
otherwise, where
f_{2}(P)
is the number of faces of
P
. Hence
\dim\widetilde{P}=\dim\widetilde{P^{\prime}}
implies that
f_{2}(P)=f_{2}(P^{\prime}).

(2) Suppose both
m(P)
and
m(P^{\prime})
are not of type IV, i.e.,
\widetilde{P}
and
\widetilde{P^{\prime}}
are both convex
n
-polytopes with
n+2
vertices.

There is a criterion in
[
3
]
p.108, which says that
\widetilde{P}\cong\widetilde{P^{\prime}}
if and only if
(\bar{m}_{0},\bar{m}_{1},\bar{m}_{-1})=(\bar{m}^{\prime}_{0},\bar{m}^{\prime}_{1},\bar{m}^{\prime}_{-1})
or
(\bar{m}_{0},\bar{m}_{1},\bar{m}_{-1})=(\bar{m}^{\prime}_{0},\bar{m}^{\prime}_{-1},\bar{m}^{\prime}_{1})
, where
\bar{m}_{j}
’s and
\bar{m}^{\prime}_{j}
’s are multiplicities of
j
in the Gale diagram of
\widetilde{P}
and
\widetilde{P^{\prime}}
respectively,
j=0,\pm 1.

Notice that
m_{3}\geq m_{2}\geq m_{1}
. Comparing the multiplicities of Gale diagrams in the Proposition
4.1
,
4.2
and
4.3
, we get that
\widetilde{P}\cong\widetilde{P^{\prime}}
implies that
(\bar{m}_{0},\bar{m}_{1},\bar{m}_{-1})=(\bar{m}^{\prime}_{0},\bar{m}^{\prime}_{1},\bar{m}^{\prime}_{-1})
. Hence
m(P)
and
m(P^{\prime})
must be of same type.
Furthermore,
m_{2}(P)=m_{2}(P^{\prime})
.

Of course, when
m(P)
and
m(P^{\prime})
are of type IV,
m_{2}(P)=m_{2}(P^{\prime})
since
\dim\widetilde{P}=3m_{2}(P)-3=\dim\widetilde{P^{\prime}}=3m_{2}(P^{\prime})-3
.

∎

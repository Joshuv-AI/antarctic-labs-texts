Therefore, by Proposition
2
and Lemmas
2
and
2
we get

\displaystyle\lVert A\rVert_{a,b}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\lVert A\rVert_{a,1}\bigr)^{\theta_{1}}\cdot\bigl(\lVert A\rVert_{a,a^{\ast}}\bigr)^{1-\theta_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(2^{\frac{1}{a}}\cdot\lVert A\rVert\bigr)^{1-\frac{a}{b^{\ast}}}\cdot\lVert A\rVert^{\frac{a}{b^{\ast}}}

\displaystyle=\bigl(2^{\frac{1}{a}}\bigr)^{1-\frac{a}{b^{\ast}}}\cdot\lVert A\rVert

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}-1}\cdot\lVert A\rVert\,.

In addition, the bilinear form
A_{0}
given by

A_{0}(\bm{\mathrm{x}}\,,\bm{\mathrm{y}})=x_{1}y_{1}+x_{1}y_{2}+x_{2}y_{1}-x_{2}y_{2}

satisfies
\lVert A_{0}\rVert=2
and

\lVert A_{0}\rVert_{a,b}=\Biggl(\,\sum_{k=1}^{2}\biggl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{2}\,\big\lvert A_{0}\bigl(\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(k)},\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(j)}\bigr)\big\rvert^{a}\biggr)^{\frac{b}{a}}\Biggr)^{\frac{1}{b}}=2^{\frac{1}{b}+\frac{1}{a}-1}\cdot\lVert A_{0}\rVert

for all
a,b\in(0,\infty]
(not just for the values
a
and
b
considered in this case; see
[
diniz
]
), which shows that the value
2^{\frac{1}{b}+\frac{1}{a}-1}
cannot be decreased. Thus, in this case we have
\mathsf{L}_{a,b}^{\mathbb{R}}=2^{\frac{1}{b}+\frac{1}{a}-1}
.

\bm{(a,b)\in}

RIV

We have
a\in[1,2]
and
b\in[2,a^{\ast}]
. If
b=\infty
, then
a=1
(because
b\leq a^{\ast}
), and the result follows from Remark
2
(
\mathsf{L}_{1,\infty}^{\mathbb{R}}=1=2^{\frac{1}{1}+\frac{1}{\infty}-1}
). If
b\neq\infty
, then
(b,a)
lies in Region RIII, hence
\mathsf{L}_{b,a}^{\mathbb{R}}=2^{\frac{1}{b}+\frac{1}{a}-1}
, and since
0<a\leq b<\infty
, Remark
1
implies
\mathsf{L}_{a,b}^{\mathbb{R}}\leq\mathsf{L}_{b,a}^{\mathbb{R}}
. Therefore
\mathsf{L}_{a,b}^{\mathbb{R}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}-1}
, and the opposite inequality follows by considering the same bilinear form
A_{0}
from the Region RIII case.

\bm{(a,b)\in}

RI

We have
a\in[1,\infty]
and
b\in[a^{\ast},\infty]
, hence
\lVert A\rVert_{a,b}\leq\lVert A\rVert_{a,a^{\ast}}
by the embedding of the
\ell_{p}
spaces. Also, since the point
(a,a^{\ast})
lies in one of the Regions RIII or RIV, it follows that
\lVert A\rVert_{a,a^{\ast}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{a^{\ast}}-1}\cdot\lVert A\rVert=\lVert A\rVert
. Consequently we have
\lVert A\rVert_{a,b}\leq\lVert A\rVert
, which shows that
\mathsf{L}_{a,b}^{\mathbb{R}}=1
.
∎

{lemma}

which settles the cases
a=2

\displaystyle\lVert A\rVert_{a,1}

3.
Variants of
Khinchin
’s inequality

Khinchin
’s inequality for
Steinhaus
variables plays a crucial role in the improvement of estimates for the constants in inequalities of the type
Littlewood
’s
4/3
(and their extended multilinear versions) for complex scalars (see
[
ABPS
,
cnpr
]
). For a very recent approach to
Khinchin
’s inequality we refer to
[
rama
]
.

In this section we obtain, among other results, a version of
Khinchin
’s inequality for
Steinhaus
variables (Theorem
3
). This result is an analog of Proposition
2
, which deals with
Rademacher
functions; see Equation (
2.2
). The proof of Proposition
2
depends crucially on the Contraction Principle from
[
diestel
]
*p. 231, which is stated only for randomized sums (independent symmetric real-valued random variables), and we do not know if a similar principle holds for independent symmetric complex-valued random variables.

For this reason, our proof of Theorem
3
relies instead on ideas from
[
cnpr
]
, where another kind of
Khinchin
’s inequality previously developed by
Ron Blei
is studied (we also consider a variant of such inequality; see Theorem
3
). In this way, we prove that a certain optimal constant
\mathsf{S}_{r}
(see Equation
3.4
) coincides with the optimal constants
\mathsf{L}_{1,r}^{\mathbb{C}}
and
\mathsf{L}_{r,1}^{\mathbb{C}}
, for all
r\in[2,\infty]
; this is precisely the analog of Proposition
2
:
2^{\frac{1}{r}}=\mathsf{L}_{1,r}^{\mathbb{R}}=\mathsf{L}_{r,1}^{\mathbb{R}}
(Theorem
1
).

From now on,
N
denotes a positive integer and
\aa
denotes a tuple
(a_{1},\dotsc,a_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
.

It is well known that, by considering the dyadic expansion of
2^{N}t
for each
t\in[0,1]
, one can prove the equality

\int_{0}^{1}\ \Bigg\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}(t)\,a_{j}\Bigg\rvert\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{\tt\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}}\Bigg\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}t_{j}\Bigg\rvert\,,

where the functions
r_{j}
are given by Equation (
2.2
). In this way, Proposition
2
can be restated as follows: for all
r\in[2,\infty]
and any
\aa
we have

(3.1)

\biggl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\lvert a_{j}\rvert^{r}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{r}}\Biggl(\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{\tt\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}}\,\Bigg\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}t_{j}\Bigg\rvert\,\Biggr)\,.

The counterpart for the average

\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{\tt\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}}\,\Bigg\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}t_{j}\Bigg\rvert

in the complex framework is

(3.2)

\biggl(\frac{1}{2\pi}\biggr)^{N}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dotsi\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Bigg\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\mathrm{e}^{\bm{\mathrm{i}}t_{j}}\Bigg\rvert\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\dotsb\mathrm{d}t_{N}\,.

Notice that this quantity is precisely the expected value

(3.3)

\mathbb{E}\Bigg\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\varepsilon_{j}\Bigg\rvert\,,

where
(\varepsilon_{1},\dotsc,\varepsilon_{N})
is a
tuple of Steinhaus variables
(that is, a tuple of independent and identically distributed random variables with uniform distribution on the complex unit circle).

Jerzy Sawa
showed that
\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}
is the least value
\mathsf{S}
such that

\Biggl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\lvert a_{j}\rvert^{2}\Biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\mathsf{S}\cdot\mathbb{E}\Bigg\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\varepsilon_{j}\Bigg\rvert

for all
N
and any
\aa
; see
[
sawa
]
. This result is known as
Khinchin’s inequality for Steinhaus variables
. A generalization, in the spirit of Proposition
2
, follows from the embedding of the
\ell_{p}
spaces: for all
r\in[2,\infty]
there is an optimal constant
\mathsf{S}_{r}\in[1,\infty)
such that

(3.4)

\Biggl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\lvert a_{j}\rvert^{r}\Biggr)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\mathsf{S}_{r}\cdot\mathbb{E}\Bigg\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\varepsilon_{j}\Bigg\rvert

for any
N\geq 1
and any
\aa
. Moreover, we have
\mathsf{S}_{r}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}
.

Our goal is to prove the following characterization of the values
\mathsf{S}_{r}
:

{theorem}

For all
r\in[2,\infty]
we have
\mathsf{S}_{r}=\mathsf{L}_{1,r}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathsf{L}_{r,1}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

In what follows we state a
Khinchin
-type inequality that extends inequality (
3.1
) (see Remark
3
) and recovers inequality (
3.4
) as a limiting case (Corollary
3
). First, we need to introduce some notation and results.

For each integer
M\geq 2
, let

T_{M}=\biggl\{\exp\biggl(\frac{2\pi j}{M}\,\bm{\mathrm{i}}\biggr)\colon j=0,\dotsc,M-1\biggr\}\,.

In addition, for any bilinear form
A\colon\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{C}^{n_{2}}\to\mathbb{C}
we define the norm
\lVert A\rVert_{M}
by

\lVert A\rVert_{M}=\sup\,\bigl\{\lvert A(\bm{\mathrm{z}}\,,\bm{\mathrm{w}})\rvert\colon\bm{\mathrm{w}}\in T_{M}^{n_{2}}\ ;\ \lvert z_{k}\rvert\leq 1\ \textnormal{for}\ k=1,\dotsc,n_{1}\bigr\}\,.

{proposition}

[
[
cnpr
]
*Theorem 2.3]

If
M\geq 3
, then

\lVert A\rVert_{M}\leq\lVert A\rVert\leq\sec\biggl(\frac{\pi}{M}\biggr)\cdot\lVert A\rVert_{M}

for all bilinear forms
A\colon\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{C}^{n_{2}}\to\mathbb{C}
.

For a tuple
\aa
, we define

(3.5)

E_{M}(\aa)=\frac{1}{M^{N}}\sum_{\tt\in T_{M}^{N}}\,\Bigg\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}t_{j}\Bigg\rvert\,.

Using the dominated convergence theorem, it is possible to prove that

(3.6)

\lim_{M\to\infty}E_{M}(\aa)=\mathbb{E}\Bigg\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\varepsilon_{j}\Bigg\rvert\,.

We need the following auxiliary result:

{lemma}

[
[
cnpr
]
*Lemma 2.4]

If
M\geq 2
and
\bm{\mathrm{w}}\in T_{M}^{N}
, then for all
\aa
we have

E_{M}(\aa)=E_{M}\bigl((a_{j}w_{j})_{j=1}^{N}\bigr)\,.

Now we are ready to state and prove the announced
Khinchin
-type inequality, valid for
r\in[2,\infty]
. We emphasize that this result is already known for
r=2
; see
[
blei
]
*Chapter II §6).

{theorem}

[
Blei–Khinchin
inequality]

Given
r\in[2,\infty]
and
M\geq 3
we have, for all
N
and any
\aa
:

\Biggl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\lvert a_{j}\rvert^{r}\Biggr)^{1/r}\leq\mathsf{L}_{1,r}^{\mathbb{C}}\cdot\sec\biggl(\frac{\pi}{M}\biggr)\cdot E_{M}(\aa)\,.

{remark}

By Theorem
1
and Equation (
3.5
) we may rewrite inequality (
3.1
) as

\Biggl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\lvert a_{j}\rvert^{r}\Biggr)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\mathsf{L}_{1,r}^{\mathbb{R}}\cdot E_{2}(\aa)\,.

In this sense, the result of Theorem
3
constitutes a complement for inequality (
3.1
).

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Let us fix an enumeration of the set
T_{M}^{N}
, say
\bm{\tau}^{(1)},\dotsc,\bm{\tau}^{(M^{N})}
, with
\bm{\tau}^{(k)}=\bigl(\tau_{1}^{(k)},\dotsc,\tau_{N}^{(k)}\bigr)
for each
k
. Consider the bilinear form
A\colon\mathbb{C}^{M^{N}}\times\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\mathbb{C}
given by

A\bigl(\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(k)},\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(j)}\bigr)=\frac{a_{j}\tau_{j}^{(k)}}{M^{N}}\quad(k\in\{1,\dotsc,M^{N}\}\ ;\ j\in\{1,\dotsc,N\})\,.

Let
j\in\{1,\dotsc,N\}
. Since
\big\lvert\tau_{j}^{(k)}\big\rvert=1
for each
k
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M^{N}}\,\big\lvert A\bigl(\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(k)},\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(j)}\bigr)\big\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M^{N}}\frac{\lvert a_{j}\rvert\cdot\big\lvert\tau_{j}^{(k)}\big\rvert}{M^{N}}

\displaystyle=\lvert a_{j}\rvert\,\sum_{k=1}^{M^{N}}\frac{1}{M^{N}}

\displaystyle=\lvert a_{j}\rvert\,.

Thus, by Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
3
we have

\displaystyle\Biggl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\lvert a_{j}\rvert^{r}\Biggr)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=\Biggl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{N}\biggl(\,\sum_{k=1}^{M^{N}}\,\big\lvert A\bigl(\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(k)},\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(j)}\bigr)\big\rvert\biggr)^{r}\,\Biggr)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{L}_{1,r}^{\mathbb{C}}\cdot\lVert A\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{L}_{1,r}^{\mathbb{C}}\cdot\sec\biggl(\frac{\pi}{M}\biggr)\cdot\lVert A\rVert_{M}\,.

In order to finish the proof it is sufficient to prove that
\lVert A\rVert_{M}\leq E_{M}(\aa)
, which amounts to have
\lvert A(\bm{\mathrm{z}}\,,\bm{\mathrm{w}})\rvert\leq E_{M}(\aa)
for all
\bm{\mathrm{z}}\in\mathbb{C}^{M^{N}}
with
\sup_{1\leq k\leq M^{N}}\,\lvert z_{k}\rvert\leq 1
and all
\bm{\mathrm{w}}\in T_{M}^{N}
. But this is true: in fact, for any such
\bm{\mathrm{z}}
and
\bm{\mathrm{w}}
we have

\displaystyle\lvert A(\bm{\mathrm{z}},\bm{\mathrm{w}})\rvert

\displaystyle=\Bigg\lvert\sum_{k=1}^{M^{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}A\bigl(\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(k)},\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(j)}\bigr)z_{k}w_{j}\Bigg\rvert

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M^{N}}\Bigg\lvert\sum_{k=1}^{M^{N}}\Biggl(z_{k}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}w_{j}\tau_{j}^{(k)}\Biggr)\Bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M^{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{M^{N}}\,\lvert z_{k}\rvert\cdot\Bigg\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}w_{j}\tau_{j}^{(k)}\Bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M^{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{M^{N}}\ \Bigg\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}w_{j}\tau_{j}^{(k)}\Bigg\rvert\,,

and the definition of the elements
\tau_{j}^{(k)}
implies that the last term above is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M^{N}}\sum_{\tt\in T_{M}^{N}}\ \Bigg\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}t_{j}w_{j}\Bigg\rvert

\displaystyle=E_{M}\bigl((a_{j}w_{j})_{j=1}^{N}\bigr)

\displaystyle=E_{M}(\aa)\,,

by Lemma
3
.
∎

{corollary}

Given
r\in[2,\infty]
we have, for all
N
and any
\aa
:

\Biggl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\lvert a_{j}\rvert^{r}\Biggr)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\mathsf{L}_{1,r}^{\mathbb{C}}\cdot\mathbb{E}\Bigg\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\varepsilon_{j}\Bigg\rvert\,.

Proof.

Just take
M\to\infty
in Theorem
3
, and use Equation (
3.6
).
∎

{lemma}

For all
r\in[2,\infty]
and every continuous bilinear form
A\colon c_{0}\times c_{0}\to\mathbb{C}
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Biggl(\,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\,\big\lvert A\bigl(\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(k)},\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(j)}\bigr)\big\rvert^{r}\Biggr)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\mathsf{S}_{r}\cdot\lVert A\rVert\,.

Proof.

Proceed exactly as in the proof of Lemma
2
, by using (
3.4
) instead of (
2.1
) and
\mathsf{S}_{r}
instead of
2^{\frac{1}{r}}
, and by substituting the integral
\int_{0}^{1}\big\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}(t)\,A\bigl(\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(k)},\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(j)}\bigr)\big\rvert\,\mathrm{d}t
with the integral formula for the value
\mathbb{E}\big\lvert\sum_{j=1}^{N}A\bigl(\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(k)},\bm{\mathrm{e}}^{(j)}\bigr)\,\varepsilon_{j}\big\rvert
, via Equation (
3.2
).
∎

Now we can easily prove the main result of this section.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Let
r\in[2,\infty]
. By Corollary
3
we have
\mathsf{S}_{r}\leq\mathsf{L}_{1,r}^{\mathbb{C}}
, and Lemma
3
implies
\mathsf{L}_{r,1}^{\mathbb{C}}\leq\mathsf{S}_{r}
. Finally, if
r\neq\infty
, then Remark
1
implies
\mathsf{L}_{1,r}^{\mathbb{C}}\leq\mathsf{L}_{r,1}^{\mathbb{C}}
(because
r>1
), whereas for
r=\infty
we have
\mathsf{L}_{1,r}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathsf{L}_{r,1}^{\mathbb{C}}=1
by Theorem
2.1
. Putting together these inequalities we obtain
\mathsf{S}_{r}=\mathsf{L}_{1,r}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathsf{L}_{r,1}^{\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

4.
(q,s)
-cotype constants of
\ell_{1}

Let
q\in[2,\infty]
and
s\in(0,\infty)
. A (real or complex)
Banach
space
X
is said to have
(q,s)
-
cotype
(see
[
diestel
]
*Remark 11.5(a)) if there is a constant
\mathsf{C}\in[1,\infty)
such that, for all
N\geq 1
and any
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N}\in X
,

(4.1)

\Biggl(\,\sum_{k=1}^{N}\,\lVert x_{k}\rVert^{q}\Biggr)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\mathsf{C}\cdot\Biggl(\int_{0}^{1}\Bigg\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{N}r_{k}(t)\,\cdot x_{k}\Bigg\rVert^{s}\,\mathrm{d}t\Biggr)^{\frac{1}{s}}\,,

where the functions
r_{k}
are given by Equation (
2.2
). The smallest value of
\mathsf{C}
is denoted by
\mathsf{C}_{q,s}(X)
, the
(q,s)
-
cotype constant of

X
.

The notion of cotype emerged in the early 1970s; however, previous results in disguise about the cotype of
L^{p}
-spaces existed as far back as the 1930s (by
Władysław Orlicz
). It is one of the cornerstones of modern
Banach
space theory and reflects the interplay between geometry and probability in
Banach
spaces.

Regarding the general theory, we have the following results:

{proposition}

[
[
pelsansan
]
*Lemma 1]

For every
Banach
space
X
and any
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N}\in X
, we have

\max_{1\leq k\leq N}\ \lVert x_{k}\rVert\leq\int_{0}^{1}\Bigg\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}(t)\,\cdot x_{j}\Bigg\rVert\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

Equivalently, for every
Banach
space
X
we have
\mathsf{C}_{\infty,1}(X)=1
.

{proposition}

[
Kahane–Khinchin
’s inequality
[
diestel
]
*pp. 211]

For any
s,s^{\prime}\in(0,\infty)
, there exists an optimal constant
\mathsf{K}_{s,s^{\prime}}\in[1,\infty)
such that

\Biggl(\int_{0}^{1}\Bigg\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}(t)\,\cdot x_{j}\Bigg\rVert^{s^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Biggr)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\leq\mathsf{K}_{s,s^{\prime}}\times\Biggl(\int_{0}^{1}\Bigg\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}(t)\,\cdot x_{j}\Bigg\rVert^{s}\,\mathrm{d}t\Biggr)^{\frac{1}{s}}\,,

regardless of the choice of a
Banach
space
X
and vectors
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N}\in X
.

Consequently, a
Banach
space has
(q,s_{0})
-cotype for some
s_{0}\in(0,\infty)
if and only if has
(q,s)
-cotype for all
s\in(0,\infty)
, and in such a case we have
\mathsf{C}_{q,s}(X)\leq\mathsf{C}_{q,s_{0}}(X)\cdot\mathsf{K}_{s,s_{0}}
.

The embedding of the
\ell_{p}
spaces applied to the left side of Equation (
4.1
) implies that the cotype constants are decreasing in the parameter
q
: if a
Banach
space
X
has
(q,s)
-cotype, then it has
(\tilde{q},s)
-cotype for any
\tilde{q}\in(q,\infty]
, with
\mathsf{C}_{\tilde{q},s}(X)\leq\mathsf{C}_{q,s}(X)
.

Concerning the constants
\mathsf{K}_{s,s^{\prime}}
: from the embedding of the spaces
L^{p}([0,1])
we get
\mathsf{K}_{s,s^{\prime}}=1
whenever
s^{\prime}\leq s
; this fact, together with Propositions
4
and
4
, implies that
\mathsf{C}_{\infty,s}(X)=1
for every
Banach
space
X
and all
s\geq 1
.

{proposition}

[
[
latole
]
*Remark 2]

For all
s,s^{\prime}
with
s\in(0,1]
and
s^{\prime}\in[s,2]
we have
\mathsf{K}_{s,s^{\prime}}=2^{\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}
. As a consequence, for all
s\in(0,\infty)
we have
\mathsf{K}_{s,1}=2^{\max\{\frac{1}{s}-1,0\}}
.

On the other hand, it is well known that the sequence space
\ell_{1}=\ell_{1}(\mathbb{K})
has
(q,s)
-cotype for all
q\in[2,\infty]
and all
s\in(0,\infty)
. To the best of our knowledge, the exact value of the constants
\mathsf{C}_{q,s}(\ell_{1})
is not available in the literature.

{theorem}

For all
q\in[2,\infty]
and
s\in(0,\infty)
we have
2^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\mathsf{C}_{q,s}(\ell_{1})\leq 2^{\frac{1}{q}+\max\{\frac{1}{s}-1,0\}}
. In particular, for
s\in[1,\infty)
we have
\mathsf{C}_{q,s}(\ell_{1})=2^{\frac{1}{q}}
.

Proof.

Let
q\in[2,\infty]
and
s\in(0,\infty)

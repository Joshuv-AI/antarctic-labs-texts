Extending a conjecture of Graham and Lovász on the distance characteristic polynomial
Aida Abiad, Boris Brimkov, Sakander Hayat, Antonina P. Khramova, Jack H. Koolen

Abstract
Graham and Lovász conjectured in 1978 that the sequence of normalized coefficients of the distance characteristic polynomial of a tree of order n n is unimodal with the maximum value occurring at ⌊ n 2 ⌋ \lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor . In this paper we investigate this problem for block graphs. In particular, we prove the unimodality part and we establish the peak for several extremal cases of uniform block graphs with small diameter.

Introduction

In their seminal work on spectral properties of the distance matrix
D
of a tree
T
, Graham and Lovász
[
13
]
showed that, for a tree
T
and
c_{k}(T)
denoting the coefficient of
x^{k}
in
\text{det}(D(T)-xI)=(-1)^{n}p_{D(G)}(x)
, the quantity
d_{k}(T)=(-1)^{n-1}c_{k}(T)/2^{n-k-2}
is determined as a fixed linear combination of the number of certain subtrees in
T
. The values
d_{k}(T)
are called the
normalized coefficients
of the distance characteristic polynomial of
T
.

A sequence
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}
of real numbers is
unimodal
if there is a
k
such that
a_{i-1}\leq a_{i}
for
i\leq k
and
a_{i}\geq a_{i+1}
for
i\geq k
. Graham and Lovász
[
13
]
conjectured that the sequence
d_{0}(T),\ldots,d_{n-2}(T)
of normalized coefficients of the distance characteristic polynomial of a tree is unimodal with the maximum value occurring at
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
for a tree
T
of order
n
.
Little progress on this problem is known. Collins
[
9
]
confirmed the conjecture for stars, and also showed that for a paths the sequence is unimodal with a maximum value at
(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}})n
. Thus, Graham and Lovász conjecture was reformulated as follows:

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
13
,
9
]

The normalized coefficients of the distance characteristic polynomial of any tree with
n
vertices are unimodal with peak between
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
and
\lceil(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}})n\rceil
.

Aalipour et al.
[
1
]
confirmed the unimodality part of Conjecture
1.1
by proving that the sequence of normalized coefficients is
indeed always unimodal; in fact, they proved the stronger statement that this sequence is log-concave.

A natural and widely used generalization of trees are block graphs (also known as clique trees). A connected graph is a
block graph
or
clique tree
if its blocks (
2
-connected components) are cliques. Graham, Hoffman, and Hosoya
[
14
]
showed that the determinant of the distance matrix of any graph only depends on the determinant of its
2
-connected components.
Bapat and Sivasubramanian
[
5
]
extended the result of the determinant of the distance matrix of a tree by Graham and Lovász from
[
13
]
to block graphs, and Das and Mohanty
[
10
]
did the same for multi-block graphs. Also the distance eigenvalues of a block graph have received some attention
[
11
,
20
,
17
,
23
]
. The study of distance matrix has a long history, and many results concerning the distance matrix and distance eigenvalues are reported in the literature, for a survey we refer the reader to the survey papers by Aouchiche and Hansen
[
3
]
and Hogben and Reinhart
[
16
]
.

In this article we consider the sequence of coefficients
c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
of the distance characteristic polynomial of a block graph. Motivated by Conjecture
1.1
, we investigate the following question:

Question 1.2
.

Is the sequence of coefficients
c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
of the distance characteristic polynomial of a block graph unimodal, and where does it peak?

This paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we start by recalling some definitions and preliminary results. In Section
3
we develop a general theory regarding the eigenvalues of metric spaces. We use this in Section
4.1
to show the unimodality part of Question
1.2
for block graphs, which extends the corresponding result for trees by Aalipour
et al.

[
1
]
. In Section
4.2
we prove that the peak part of Question
1.2
holds for several extremal classes of block graphs with small diameter. As a corollary of our results we obtain Collins’ result for stars
[
9
]
. Although we show that the peak can move quite a bit when considering block graphs, our results provide evidence that Conjecture
1.1
might still hold in a more general setting.

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
G=(V,E)
denotes an undirected, simple, connected and loopless graph with
n
vertices. The

of a connected graph

whose
(i,j)
-entry equals the distance between the vertices

and
v_{j}
, i.e., the length of a shortest path between
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
. Dependence on

may be omitted when it is clear from the context. The characteristic polynomial of

is defined by
p_{D}(x)=\det(xI-D)
and is called the

of
G
. Since

is a real symmetric matrix, all of the roots of the distance characteristic polynomial are real.

A metric space can be attached to any connected graph

in the following way. The

of
G
, denoted

, is the metric where for all

is the distance between

and

in
G
. Then,

is a metric space, called the

associated with

.

A

of a graph

is a vertex whose deletion increases the number of connected components of

. A

of

is a maximal connected subgraph of

which has no cut vertices. Thus, a block is either a maximal
2-connected subgraph, or a cut-edge, or an isolated vertex, and every such
subgraph is a block. Two blocks of

can overlap in at most one vertex, which is a cut-vertex; hence, every edge of

lies in a unique block, and

is the
union of its blocks. A connected graph

is called a

if all of its blocks are cliques. In

Given two graphs

whose vertex

The Cartesian product

For integers

A metric space

The cases when the graph

The

3
On
\ell_{1}
-embeddability of metric spaces

Metric spaces whose distance matrix has exactly one positive eigenvalue have received much attention since the work of Deza and Laurent
[
11
]
. Metric properties of regular graphs have also been investigated by Koolen
[
19
]
.

In this section we develop a general theory regarding the eigenvalues of metric spaces. The main result of this section, Theorem
3.3
, will be used to show the unimodality part of Question
1.2
.

We say that a metric space
(X,d)
is
of negative type
if for all weight functions
w:X\to\mathbb{Z}
with
\sum\limits_{x\in X}w(x)=0
we have

\sum\limits_{x\in X}\sum\limits_{y\in X}w(x)w(y)d(x,y)\leq 0.

(1)

If the same inequality holds for all weight functions with
\sum\limits_{x\in X}w(x)=1
, then we say that
(X,d)
is
hypermetric
. It is fairly easy to show that
(X,d)
being hypermetric implies it is of negative type. An alternative way to define metric spaces of negative type is by using Schoenberg’s Theorem
[
21
]
: a metric space
(X,d)
is of negative type if and only if
(X,\sqrt{d})
is isometrically embeddable in the Eucledian space.

A metric space
(X,d)
is said to be
\ell_{1}
-embeddable
if it can be embedded isometrically into the
\ell_{1}
-space (
\mathbb{R}^{m},d_{\ell_{1}}
) for some integer
m\geq 1
. Here,
d_{\ell_{1}}
denotes the
\ell_{1}
-distance defined by

d_{\ell_{1}}(x,y):=\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq m}|x_{i}-y_{i}|\penalty\ \penalty\ \textrm{for}\penalty\ x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

One of the basic results of
\ell_{1}
-embeddable metric spaces is a characterization in terms of cut semimetrics. Given a subset
S
of the
n
-set
V_{n}:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the
cut semimetric

\delta_{S}
is the distance on
V_{n}
defined as

\delta_{S}(i,j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\hbox{$i\in S,\penalty\ j\in V_{n}\setminus S$,}\\
0,&\hbox{$i,j\in S$, {or} $i,j\in V_{n}\setminus S$.}\end{array}\right.

Note that every cut semimetric is clearly
\ell_{1}
-embeddable. In fact, a distance
d
is
\ell_{1}
-
embeddable
if and only if it can be decomposed as a nonnegative linear combination of cut semimetrics. We also note that
\ell_{1}
-embeddability of
(X,d)
implies it is hypermetric
[
22
,
18
]
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
(X,d)
be the graphic metric space and associated with
G
. We have the following implications:

\displaystyle(X,d)\text{ is $\ell_{1}$-embeddable}

\displaystyle\Downarrow

\displaystyle(X,d)\text{ is hypermetric}

\displaystyle\Downarrow

\displaystyle(X,d)\text{ is of negative type}

\displaystyle\Downarrow

The distance matrix of
G
has exactly one positive eigenvalue

For more details on the metric hierarchy we refer the reader to the book by Deza and Laurent
[
11
]
.

Let (
X_{1},d_{1}
) and (
X_{2},d_{2}
) be two metric spaces. Their direct product is the metric space
(X_{1}\times X_{2},d_{1}\otimes d_{2})
where, for
x_{1},y_{1}\in X_{1}
,
x_{2},y_{2}\in X_{2}
,

d_{1}\otimes d_{2}\big((x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{1},y_{2})\big)=d_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})+d_{2}(x_{2},y_{2}).

(2)

Note that for path metrics, the direct product operation corresponds to the Cartesian product of graphs. Namely, if
G
and
H
are two connected graphs, then the direct product of their path metrics coincides with the path metric of the Cartesian product of
G
and
H
. The following lemmas provide a relation between the metric hierarchy and the direct product of respective metric spaces.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
d_{i}
be a distance on the set
X_{i}
, for
i=1,2
. Then
(X_{1}\times X_{2},d_{1}\otimes d_{2})
is
\ell_{1}
-embeddable if and only if both
(X_{1},d_{1})
and
(X_{2},d_{2})
are
\ell_{1}
-embeddable.

Proof.

Since both
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are
\ell_{1}
-embeddable, they can be decomposed as a nonnegative linear combination of cut semimetrics. Therefore, if
d_{1}=\sum\limits_{S\subseteq X_{1}}a_{S}\delta_{S}
and
d_{2}=\sum\limits_{T\subseteq X_{2}}b_{T}\delta_{T}
, then by (
2
) we obtain that

d_{1}\otimes d_{2}=\sum\limits_{S\subseteq X_{1}}a_{S}\delta_{S\times X_{2}}+\sum\limits_{T\subseteq X_{2}}b_{T}\delta_{X_{1}\times T}.

This implies that
d_{1}\otimes d_{2}
is decomposed into nonnegative linear combination of cut semimetrics. Thus
d_{1}\otimes d_{2}
is
\ell_{1}
-embeddable.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
d_{i}
be a distance on the set
X_{i}
, for
i=1,2
. Then
(X_{1}\times X_{2},d_{1}\otimes d_{2})
is hypermetric (resp. of negative type) if and only if both (
X_{1},d_{1}
) and (
X_{2},d_{2}
) are hypermetric (resp. of negative type).

Proof.

Similar to Lemma
3.1
, it suffices to show that the inequality (
1
) holds for
d_{1}\otimes d_{2}
assuming
(X,d_{1})
and
(X,d_{2})
are hypermetric (resp. of negative type). By definition we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(x_{1},x_{2})\in X_{1}\times X_{2}}\sum\limits_{(y_{1},y_{2})\in X_{1}\times X_{2}}w(x_{1},x_{2})w(y_{1},y_{2})(d_{1}\otimes d_{2})\left((x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{1},y_{2})\right)=

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{x_{1}\in X_{1}}\sum\limits_{x_{2}\in X_{2}}\sum\limits_{y_{1}\in X_{1}}\sum\limits_{y_{2}\in X_{2}}w(x_{1},x_{2})w(y_{1},y_{2})\left(d_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})+d_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})\right)=

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{x_{1}\in X_{1}}\sum\limits_{y_{1}\in X_{1}}\left(\sum\limits_{x_{2}\in X_{2}}w(x_{1},x_{2})\right)\left(\sum\limits_{y_{2}\in X_{2}}w(y_{1},y_{2})\right)d_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})+

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{x_{2}\in X_{2}}\sum\limits_{y_{2}\in X_{2}}\left(\sum\limits_{x_{1}\in X_{1}}w(x_{1},x_{2})\right)\left(\sum\limits_{y_{1}\in X_{1}}w(y_{1},y_{2})\right)d_{2}(x_{2},y_{2}).

For a weight function
w:X_{1}\times X_{2}\to\mathbb{Z}
such that

\sum\limits_{(x_{1},x_{2})\in X_{1}\times X_{2}}w(x_{1},x_{2})=1\text{ (resp. $0$),}

we define
w_{1}:X_{1}\to\mathbb{Z}
and
w_{2}:X_{2}\to\mathbb{Z}
such that

w_{1}(x)=\sum\limits_{x_{2}\in X_{2}}w(x,x_{2})

and

w_{2}(x)=\sum\limits_{x_{1}\in X_{1}}w(x_{1},x).

Note that

\sum\limits_{x_{1}\in X_{1}}w_{1}(x_{1})=\sum\limits_{(x_{1},x_{2})\in X_{1}\times X_{2}}w(x_{1},x_{2})=1\text{ (resp. $0$),}

and thus for
(X_{1},d_{1})
being hypermetric (resp. of negative type) and the weight function
w_{1}
we have

\sum\limits_{x_{1}\in X_{1}}\sum\limits_{y_{1}\in X_{1}}w_{1}(x_{1})w_{1}(y_{1})d_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})\leq 0.

Similarly, we have

\sum\limits_{x_{2}\in X_{2}}\sum\limits_{y_{2}\in X_{2}}w_{2}(x_{2})w_{2}(y_{2})d_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})\leq 0.

The inequality (
1
) for
d_{1}\otimes d_{2}
then follows.
∎

Theorem 3.3

Let

4
Extension of Graham and Lovász conjecture to block graphs

Recall that, for a graph
G
,
c_{k}(G)
denotes the coefficient of
x^{k}
in

\text{det}(D(G)-xI)=(-1)^{n}p_{D(G)}(x).

The coefficients of the distance polynomial of a tree all have a common factor of
(-1)^{n-1}2^{n-k-2}
due to a result of Graham and Lovász
[
13
]
. We say that the quantities
d_{k}(T)=(-1)^{n-1}c_{k}(T)/2^{n-k-2}
are the
normalized coefficients
of the distance characteristic polynomial of a tree
T
. Due to such common factor in the coefficients of trees
[
12
]
, Conjecture
1.1
uses the normalized coefficients. We should note that we will not use the normalized coefficients to investigate Question
1.2
.

In this section we show that the sequence of coefficients of the distance characteristic polynomial of a block graph is unimodal, and we establish the peak for several extremal classes of block graphs with small diameter.

4.1
Unimodality

To answer the unimodality part of Question
1.2
, we will follow a similar approach as it was done in
[
1
]
to show the unimodality of trees. The main idea relies on the fact that the distance matrix of a block graph on
n
vertices has one positive and
n-1
negative eigenvalues. We begin with a preliminary result, which extends the known result for trees by Edelberg, Garey and Graham
[
12
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The coefficients of the distance characteristic polynomial of a block graph
G
satisfy

(-1)^{n-1}c_{k}(G)>0\quad\text{for }0\leq k\leq n-2.

Proof.

It was shown in
[
20
, Theorem 3.2]
that the distance matrix
D(G)
of a block graph
G
has one positive and
n-1
negative eigenvalues.
We now extend the argument given in
[
12
, Theorem 2.3]
that
(-1)^{n-1}c_{k}(T)>0
for
0\leq k\leq n-2
for a tree
T
, given that its distance matrix has one positive and
n-1
negative eigenvalues. Let the eigenvalues of the distance matrix
D(G)
of a block graph
G
be denoted by
\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2},\dots,-\lambda_{n}
, where
\lambda_{i}>0
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Then the distance characteristic polynomial is

\displaystyle\det(D(G)-xI)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}(x-\lambda_{1})(x+\lambda_{2})\cdots(x+\lambda_{n})=

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}(x-\lambda_{1})\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}g_{n-1-k}x^{k}=

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\left(x^{n}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}(g_{n-k}-\lambda_{1}g_{n-k-1})x^{k}-\lambda_{1}g_{n-1}\right),

where
g_{k}
is the sum of all
k
-fold products of
\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n}
. Then,
c_{n-1}(G)=g_{1}-\lambda_{1}
, but also
c_{n-1}(G)=-c_{n}(G)\text{tr}(D)=0
; thus,
g_{1}=\lambda_{1}
. Then, since
g_{n-k}-\lambda_{1}g_{n-k-1}=g_{n-k}-g_{1}g_{n-k-1}<0
for
k=1,\dots,n-2
,
and since for
k=0
,
-\lambda_{1}g_{n-1}=-g_{1}g_{n-1}=-\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}<0
, it follows that
(-1)^{n-1}c_{k}(G)>0
for
0\leq k\leq n-2
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

For a block graph
G
, the sequence of coefficients of the distance characteristic polynomial
(-1)^{n-1}c_{0}(G),\ldots,(-1)^{n-1}c_{n-2}(G)
is unimodal.

Proof.

First, it follows from Lemma
4.1
that if
G
is a block graph, then the coefficients of the distance characteristic polynomial satisfy
(-1)^{n-1}c_{k}(G)>0\quad\text{for }0\leq k\leq n-2
.

Since the distance matrix
D
is a real symmetric matrix, the distance characteristic polynomial is real-rooted. From Lemma
2.2
(i)
, it follows that the sequence is log-concave.

Moreover, since

(-1)^{n-1}c_{k}(G)>0\quad\text{for }0\leq k\leq n-2,

then Lemma
2.2
(ii) implies that the sequence of coefficients of the distance characteristic polynomial is unimodal.
∎

4.2
Peak location

In this section we answer the peak location part of Question
1.2
for several extremal families of uniform block graphs with small diameter.

The idea is to derive an explicit formula for the coefficients and use the unimodality to find the peak. However, while the method for obtaining the peak for stars and paths relies on the algebraic properties of the corresponding distance matrix
[
9
]
, for block graphs we will exploit several of its spectral properties.

Consider the
windmill graph

W(k,t)
, which is a block graph formed by joining
k
cliques of size
t
at a shared universal vertex. The following result uses Stirling’s approximation to prove an estimate for the peak location of a windmill graph for large
k
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Consider a windmill graph
W(k,t)
with
k\geq 2
and
t\geq 3
, so that
n=|V|=k(t-1)+1
. Then, the sequence of coefficients is unimodal, and as
k
approaches infinity the peak of the sequence occurs at
\frac{kt(t-1)}{2(t+1)}+O(\log k)
.

Proof.

The distance polynomial of the graph is

p_{D}(x)=(x+1)^{(t-2)k}(x+t)^{k-1}(x^{2}-(t-2+2(t-1)(k-1))x-k(t-1)).

To locate the peak of
p_{D}(x)
, it is sufficient to consider
(x+1)^{(t-2)k}(x+t)^{k-1}
.
We know
(x+1)^{(t-2)k}
peaks at
\frac{k(t-2)}{2}
. The coefficients of
(x+t)^{k-1}
are defined by the formula
f_{i}={k-1\choose i}t^{k-1-i}
. By calculating explicitly
f_{\frac{k}{t+1}-2},f_{\frac{k}{t+1}-1},f_{\frac{k}{t+1}},f_{\frac{k}{t+1}+1}
and using unimodality of binomial coefficients we conclude the sequence
f_{i}
peaks at
\frac{k}{t+1}
.

Define

p=\frac{k(t-2)}{2}+\frac{k}{t+1}=\frac{kt(t-1)}{2(t+1)},

and let
d_{i}
be the coefficients of
(x+1)^{(t-2)k}(x+t)^{k-1}
. Then

d_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i}{(t-2)k\choose i-j}{k-1\choose j}t^{k-1-j}.

We define
m_{i}=\max\limits_{j}{(t-2)k\choose i-j}{k-1\choose j}t^{k-1-j}
, the maximal term in the sum. Then we have
d_{i}\leq k\cdot m_{i}
for all
i
. On the other hand,
d_{p}
has

{(t-2)k\choose(t-2)k/2}{k-1\choose k/(t+1)}t^{k-1-k/(t+1)}

as one of the terms in its sum, so
d_{p}
is greater than that. The idea is to show that
km_{i}\leq{(t-2)k\choose(t-2)k/2}{k-1\choose k/(t+1)}t^{k-1-k/(t+1)}
for all
i\leq k-1
, which implies
d_{i}\leq d_{p}
.

To find
m_{i}
, we apply Stirling’s formula to
{(t-2)k\choose i-j}{k-1\choose j}t^{k-1-j}
and find its derivative with respect to
j
. Note that for
n\to\infty
, we have

\log n!=n\log n-n+\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi n)+O(\log n),\text{ or }

n!-\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}\sqrt{2\pi}=n^{O(1)},

meaning that
n!
and
\frac{n^{n}}{e^{n}}\sqrt{2\pi n}
are asymptotically equivalent. Also observe that since the sequence is unimodal it is sufficient to find a local peak among the coefficients with high values of
k
and
n-k
. Hence, for large enough
n
,
k
, and
n-k
we may derive

{n\choose k}=\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}\sim\frac{\sqrt{2\pi n}n^{n}e^{n-k}e^{k}}{2\pi\sqrt{k(n-k)}k^{k}(n-k)^{n-k}e^{n}}=\frac{n^{n}\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi k(n-k)}k^{k}(n-k)^{n-k}}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\left.{(t-2)k\choose i-j}{k-1\choose j}t^{k-1-j}\right.\sim

\displaystyle\sim\frac{((t-2)k)^{k(t-2)+1/2}(k-1)^{k-1+1/2}}{2\pi(i-j)^{i-j+1/2}((t-2)k-i+j)^{(t-2)k-i+j+1/2}j^{j+1/2}(k-1-j)^{k-1-j+1/2}},

and its derivative is then equal to
0
if and only if

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{(i-j)(k-1-j)}{j(k(t-2)-i+j)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2j}-\frac{1}{2(i-j)}-\frac{1}{2(k-1-j)}+\frac{1}{2(k(t-2)-i+j)},

\displaystyle\frac{(i-j)(k-1-j)}{j(k(t-2)-i+j)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{e^{1/j}e^{1/(k(t-2)-i+j)}}{e^{1/(i-j)}e^{1/(k-1-j)}}.}

Since
\sqrt{e^{1/z}}
and
1
are asymptotically equivalent for
z\to\infty
, we may assume

\displaystyle\frac{(i-j)(k-1-j)}{j(k(t-2)-i+j)}

\displaystyle\sim 1,

\displaystyle\frac{i(k-1)}{k(t-1)-1}

\displaystyle\sim j

For
k\to\infty
we may assume
j=\frac{i}{t-1}
and then the inequality we want to prove is

k{(t-2)k\choose i-i/(t-1)}{k-1\choose i/(t-1)}t^{k-1-i/(t-1)}\leq{(t-2)k\choose(t-2)k/2}{k-1\choose k/(t+1)}t^{k-1-k/(t+1)}.

This follows for the two inequalities:

k{(t-2)k\choose i-i/(t-1)}t^{k/(t+1)}\leq{(t-2)k\choose(t-2)k/2}t^{i/(t-1)}\text{ and }{k-1\choose i/(t-1)}\leq{k-1\choose k/(t+1)}.

The latter inequality follows from
\frac{i}{t-1}\leq\frac{k}{t+1}
for
k\to\infty
. The former inequality can be shown using Stirling approximation.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

One may wonder on the extension to a windmill graph in which the cliques are not all of the same size. Consider a block graph with one universal vertex and
k_{i}
cliques of size
t_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,l\}
and some
l
, all sizes
t_{1},\dots,t_{l}
are distinct. Then the distance characteristic polynomial takes form

p_{D}(x)=(x+1)^{n-k-1}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}(x+t_{i})^{k_{i}-1}p_{B}(x),

where
p_{B}(x)
is the characteristic polynomial of the quotient matrix corresponding to the coarsest partition into
l+1
vertex subsets
X_{0},X_{1},\dots X_{l}
:
X_{0}
only contains the universal vertex, and
X_{i}
has all vertices (except for the universal one) from all
k_{i}
cliques of size
t_{i}
. Then the problem of finding
m_{i}
as it is defined above is equivalent to a problem of finding a peak of an
l
-dimensional function, which complicates the application of the approximation approach even for small
l
.

The
friendship graph
(or
Dutch windmill graph
), denoted
F_{2k+1}
, is the graph
W(k,3)
obtained by joining
k
copies of
K_{3}
by a common vertex so that
n=|V|=2k+1
. As a direct consequence of Theorem
4.3
we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
F_{2k+1}
be the friendship graph on
n=|V|=2k+1
vertices. Then, the sequence of coefficients is unimodal, and as
k
approaches infinity the peak of the sequence occurs at
3k/4+O(\log k)
.

Using a different and non-asymptotic approach, next we show that the windmill graph with 2 cliques,
W(2,t)
, has distance characteristic polynomial coefficients with peak exactly at
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
G
be a graph obtained by adding a universal vertex to the disjoint union of two cliques
K_{t}
, and let
n=|V(G)|=2t+1
. Then the sequence of coefficients of the distance characteristic polynomial of
G
is unimodal with peak at
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.

Proof.

Consider an equitable partition of
G
into three subsets, one is the universal vertex and the other two correspond to cliques
K_{t}
. Using the quotient matrix of this partition, we can compute the distance characteristic polynomial of
G
to be

\displaystyle p_{D}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x+1)^{2t-2}(-(t+1)-x)(-2t-(3t-1)x+x^{2})=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x+1)^{2t-2}(ax^{3}+bx^{2}+cx+d),

where
a=-1
,
b=2t-2
,
c=3t^{2}+4t-1
, and
d=2t^{2}+2t
.
Multiplying the binomial expansion of
(x+1)^{2t-2}
by
(ax^{3}+bx^{2}+cx+d)
and combining the coefficients of terms with the same power, we obtain

\displaystyle p_{D}(x)

\displaystyle=\left[d{2t-2\choose 0}\right]x^{0}+

\displaystyle+\left[c{2t-2\choose 0}+d{2t-2\choose 1}\right]x^{1}+

\displaystyle+\left[b{2t-2\choose 0}+c{2t-2\choose 1}+d{2t-2\choose 2}\right]x^{2}+

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{2t-5}\left[a{2t-2\choose i}+b{2t-2\choose i+1}+c{2t-2\choose i+2}+d{2t-2\choose i+3}\right]x^{i+3}+

\displaystyle+\left[a{2t-2\choose 2t-4}+b{2t-2\choose 2t-3}+c{2t-2\choose 2t-2}\right]x^{2t-1}+

\displaystyle+\left[a{2t-2\choose 2t-3}+b{2t-2\choose 2t-2}\right]x^{2t}+

\displaystyle+\left[a{2t-2\choose 2t-2}\right]x^{2t+1}.

Let
c_{i}
be the coefficient of
x^{i}
in
p_{D}(x)
. Then, for
t\geq 4
and
j\geq 0
,

c_{t-j}=a{2t-2\choose t-3-j}+b{2t-2\choose t-2-j}+c{2t-2\choose t-1-j}+d{2t-2\choose t-j}.

(3)

Using the formula
{n\choose k}=\frac{(n-k+1)}{k}{n\choose k-1}
, we can rewrite (
3
) as

\displaystyle c_{t-j}

\displaystyle=\binom{2t-2}{t-3-j}\left(a+b\cdot\frac{j+t+1}{t-j-2}+c\cdot\frac{j+t+1}{t-j-2}\cdot\frac{j+t}{t-j-1}\right.+

\displaystyle+\left.d\cdot\frac{j+t-1}{t-j-2}\cdot\frac{j+t}{t-j-1}\cdot\frac{j+t+1}{t-j}\right)=

\displaystyle=\binom{2t-2}{t-3-j}\cdot f(t,j),

where
f(t,j)=\frac{t(j+t)\left(t\left(j^{2}+j(3-4t)-t(5t+11)+2\right)+2\right)}{(j-t)(j-t+1)(j-t+2)}
.
Then,
c_{t-j}\geq c_{t-(j+1)}
if and only if

\binom{2t-2}{t-3-j}\cdot f(t,j)\geq\binom{2t-2}{t-3-(j+1)}\cdot f(t,j+1),

which is equivalent to

\frac{f(t,j)}{t-3-j}\geq\frac{f(t,j+1)}{t+j+2}.

(4)

It can be verified using a software with symbolic algebra for rational functions that (
4
) holds for all integers
j
and
t
with
t\geq 4
and
0\leq j<t-3
; for
t\leq 3
, it can be verified that
c_{t-j}\geq c_{t-(j+1)}
by explicitly computing the distance characteristic polynomial. Similarly as above, it can be shown that
c_{t+j}\geq c_{t+(j+1)}
for all
t
and
j\geq 0
. Thus, it follows that
c_{0}\leq\ldots\leq c_{t}\geq\ldots\geq c_{2t+1}
, and hence the distance characteristic polynomial of
G
is unimodal with peak at
t=\lfloor\frac{2t+1}{2}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.
∎

Consider now the class of
barbell graphs

B(t,\ell)
, which are obtained by connecting two cliques
K_{t}
with a path on
\ell
vertices by identifying the leaves of the path with one of the vertices in each clique. We also consider
lollipop graphs

L(t,\ell)
, which are obtained by adding a path on
\ell
vertices to a single clique
K_{t}
so that one of the leaves of the path is also a vertex of the clique. We begin by locating the peak of the distance characteristic polynomial for a barbell graph with
\ell\in\{2,3,4,5\}
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
G
be a barbell graph
B(t,\ell)
, so that
|V(G)|=n=2t+\ell-2
. Then the sequence of coefficients of the distance characteristic polynomial of
G
is unimodal with peak at
t-1
if
\ell=2
and at
t
if
\ell\in\{3,4,5\}
.

Proof.

We prove the claim for
\ell=2
; the cases
\ell\in\{3,4,5\}
can be shown analogously.
Consider a quotient partition into
4
vertex sets:
2
of them are the two vertices of the path of length
2
connecting the cliques and the other
2
correspond to the remaining
t-1
vertices of each clique. We then have the quotient matrix

B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}t-2&1&2&3(t-1)\\
t-1&0&1&2(t-1)\\
2(t-1)&1&0&t-1\\
3(t-1)&2&1&t-2\\
\end{array}\right)

with characteristic polynomial

p_{B}(x)=-x^{4}+(2t-4)x^{3}+\left(8t^{2}-4t-4\right)x^{2}+\left(14t^{2}-12t\right)x+5t^{2}-4t.

From
[
16
, Theorem 3.3]
, it follows that the distance characteristic polynomial of
G
is

p_{D}(x)=(x+1)^{2t-4}\left(-x^{4}+(2t-4)x^{3}+\left(8t^{2}-4t-4\right)x^{2}+\left(14t^{2}-12t\right)x+5t^{2}-4t\right).

Let
a=-1
,
b=2t-4
,
c=8t^{2}-4t-4
,
d=14t^{2}-12t
, and
e=5t^{2}-4t
. We can use

(x+1)^{2t-4}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{2t-4}\binom{2t-4}{k}x^{k}

to write down a formula in the case
5\leq k\leq 2t-4
:

c_{k}=a\binom{2t-4}{k-4}+b\binom{2t-4}{k-3}+c\binom{2t-4}{k-2}+d\binom{2t-4}{k-1}+e\binom{2t-4}{k}.

Using the identity
\binom{n}{k}=\frac{n-k+1}{k}\binom{n}{k-1}
we obtain

\displaystyle c_{k}

\displaystyle=\binom{2t-4}{k-4}\left(a+\frac{2t-k}{k-3}\cdot b+\frac{2t-k-1}{k-2}\cdot\frac{2t-k}{k-3}\cdot c+\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\frac{2t-k-2}{k-1}\cdot\frac{2t-k-1}{k-2}\cdot\frac{2t-k}{k-3}\cdot d\right.+

\displaystyle+\left.\frac{2t-k-3}{k}\cdot\frac{2t-k-2}{k-1}\cdot\frac{2t-k-1}{k-2}\cdot\frac{2t-k}{k-3}\cdot e\right)=

\displaystyle=\binom{2t-4}{k-4}f(t,k).

Then,
c_{k}\geq c_{k-1}
if and only if

\binom{2t-4}{k-4}f(t,k)\geq\binom{2t-4}{k-5}f(t,k-1),

which by applying the identity
\binom{n}{k}=\frac{n-k+1}{k}\binom{n}{k-1}
leads to

\frac{f(t,k)}{k-4}\geq\frac{f(t,k-1)}{2t-k+1}.

If
k=t
then the simplified form of the above inequality is

\frac{21t^{4}-35t^{3}+t^{2}+3t+6}{t^{4}-8t^{3}+17t^{2}+2t-24}\leq 0,

and for
k=t-1
we have

\frac{33t^{5}+41t^{4}-183t^{3}+7t^{2}+114t-24}{(t-5)(t-4)(t-3)(t-2)(t+2)}\geq 0.

It is straightforward to verify that if
t\geq 6
, the inequality for the
k=t-1
case holds, but the one for
k=t
does not, meaning
c_{t-2}\leq c_{t-1}>c_{t}
. Since the sequence of coefficients is unimodal by Theorem
4.2
, this implies that
t-1
is indeed the peak location. The cases
t\leq 5
can be checked by straightforward calculation.
∎

An analogous argument as in the proof of Theorem
4.7
can also be used to show the peak location of lollipop graphs with small
\ell
.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
G
be a lollipop graph
L(t,\ell)
, so that
|V(G)|=n=t+\ell-1
. Let
\ell\in\{2,3,4,5\}
. Then the sequence of coefficients of the distance characteristic polynomial of
G
is unimodal with peak at
\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
.

5
Concluding remarks

We end up by discussing another extremal case of block graphs, namely, block paths. According to SageMath simulations, the peak of a block path seems to be located between
\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\rfloor
and
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. A natural approach to extend the result from paths to block paths would be to generalize Collins proof for the distance characteristic polynomial of paths
[
9
]
and calculate the distance characteristic polynomial coefficients of block paths using the formula for edge-weighted block graphs (see Theorem
2.1
). In general, the coefficient formula is given by

c_{n-k}=(-1)^{n-k}\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{k}\leq n}\det D[v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}],

where
D[v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}]
is a
k\times k
submatrix of a
D
whose rows and columns are indexed by vertices
v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}
. The sum is over all possible ways to choose
k
out of
n
vertices.
The main idea of Collins’ approach is to interpret
D[v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}]
as a distance matrix of an edge-weighted block graph, and then use Theorem
2.1
to calculate its determinant. However, for the case when the blocks within the path have size at least
3
, the description of blocks of
D[v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}]
can get very irregular: depending on a particular choice of
k
vertices, they can be anything from
2
- to
k
-cliques with weights of edges seemingly following no pattern. Thus, for the more general setting of block graphs, it seems hopeless to use an analogous approach as Collins does for trees.

Our computational results suggest the following question:

Question 5.1
.

Are the coefficients of the distance characteristic polynomial of any block graph with
n
vertices unimodal with peak between
\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\rfloor
and
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
?

We conclude by observing that the formula for the normalized coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of a tree
[
13
, page 81]
probably also holds for uniform block graphs. If this was the case, then we could define the normalized coefficients for a
t
-uniform block graph (the distance eigenvalues of Hamming graphs
[
2
, Section 3.1]
suggest that there exists such a common factor).

High-resolution MHz time- and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy based on a tunable vacuum ultraviolet source
Lukas Hellbrück, Michele Puppin, Fei Guo, Daniel D. Hickstein, Siham Benhabib, Marco Grioni, J. Hugo Dil, Thomas LaGrange, Henrik M. Rønnow, Fabrizio Carbone

Abstract
Time and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (trARPES) allows direct mapping of the electronic band structure and its dynamic response on femtosecond timescales. Here, we present a new ARPES system, powered by a new fiber-based femtosecond light source in the vacuum ultraviolet (VUV) range, accessing the complete first Brillouin zone for most materials. We present trARPES data on Au ( 111) , polycrystalline Au, Bi 2 ​ Se 3 \text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Se}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} and TaTe 2 \text{TaTe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} , demonstrating an energy resolution of 21 meV 21\text{\,}\mathrm{meV} with a time resolution of < < 360 fs 360\text{\,}\mathrm{fs} , at a high repetition rate of 1 MHz 1\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz} . The system is integrated with an extreme ultraviolet (EUV) high harmonic generation (HHG) beamline, enabling excellent tunability of the time-bandwidth resolution.

In such experiments, a femtosecond laser pulse is used to generate an excitation whose effect can then be tracked in the electronic band structure with momentum resolution as a function of time, on femtosecond timescales.

Using tunable light sources from
6\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}100\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
is desirable for addressing specific electronic states in many materials, with the time and energy resolution tailored to the particular effect and dynamics under investigation.
A high signal-to-noise ratio is needed while simultaneously avoiding space-charge effects, leading to artifacts and broadened spectra.
Due to current technological limitations, a single light source capable of covering all these requirements is lacking.
Here, we present a new trARPES system utilizing a tunable light source operating in the Vacuum Ultraviolet (VUV) regime, working in the range of

An additional benefit of the light source is the high stability due to the fiber-based front end, significantly increasing the ease of use by reducing the laborious daily realignments.
This alignment stability further leads to a stable VUV flux over multiple days, even when switching between different harmonics.
Due to this reproducibility, the light source can be used under various conditions with little to no setup time when changing the characteristics of the source.
The reproducibility also allowed us to integrate the new light source into an existing HHG beamline, thus enabling full flexibility in the choice of photon energies and experimental time-energy scales, with the possibility of switching between the two in a matter of seconds.

trARPES experiments require a high photon energy, femtosecond light source, capable of overcoming the sample’s work function (
4\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}5\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}

Photoelectron spectroscopies are susceptible to space charge effects, limiting the energy resolution, when multiple photoelectrons are emitted within a short light pulse.
High pulse repetition rates (

Limiting the fluence makes observing far out-of-equilibrium effects highly challenging for high repetition rate systems, better suited to exploring the weak perturbation regime.

A systematic study of the total energy resolution and the laser’s spectral bandwidth was done by measuring spectra of polycrystalline

In ARPES, the total energy resolution is a consequence of the material’s intrinsic spectral width, broadened by the probe pulse spectral bandwidth and by the detection system’s energy resolution.
Therefore, to evaluate the spectral bandwidth of the probe pulse, it is necessary to separate it from the other contributions.
This is achieved by measuring the same spectra under the same conditions with a Helium lamp, with a spectral bandwidth of a few
\mathrm{meV}
, which is a negligible contribution to the combined energy resolution and thereby corresponds to the detection system broadening, noted as
\Delta E_{He}
.
The total energy resolution is determined by plotting the EDC integrated over the whole momentum range and fitting a linear density of states multiplied with a Fermi-Dirac distribution and convolved with a Gaussian broadening.
This results in the following fitting function

I=\left[c+\frac{1}{e^{(E-E_{F})/k_{B}T}+1}\right]*g(E,\Delta E)

(1)

E-E_{F}
, Boltzmann constant
k_{B}
, temperature
T
, and
*

We characterized the time resolution of the measurement setup by measuring the band-filling process above
E_{F}
and its subsequent decay on both
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Se}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
\text{TaTe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

As described in the section above,
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Se}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
hosts a Dirac cone crossing the Fermi level, which can be occupied above
E_{F}
upon pump excitation and is, therefore, a good candidate for evaluating the performance of the trARPES setup.
The sample was cleaved in situ, and measurements were carried out at room temperature.
The 10
th
harmonic (
10.8\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
) was chosen as a probe beam for the pump-probe measurements, while the
1.2\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
fundamental beam was used to pump the material.
Both samples were investigated with a repetition rate of
1\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
, similar to the previous section.
The slit with a width of
100\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
(see Fig.
1
) was used for the experiment for better temporal pump-probe cross-correlation.

We observe a fast band filling of the unoccupied part of the Dirac cone, followed by slow relaxation on the order of picoseconds, similar to previous reports
wang_measurement_2012
;
crepaldi_ultrafast_2012
.
Figure
5
shows cuts of the Dirac cone filling for four different time delays.
Since the population of the Dirac cone occurs on a short
\mathrm{fs}

To extract the time resolution, an exponential decay with a step at time-zero, convoluted with a Gaussian is fitted to the data, as given by the expression

I=\left[c+A\theta(t_{0})e^{\left(\frac{-(t-t_{0})}{\tau}\right)}\right]*g(t,\Delta t)

(2)

with
c
as constant background,
A
as amplitude,
\theta
as a Heavyside function centered at
t_{0}
,
\tau
as decay rate, and
*
indicates the convolution operation.
The width of the Gaussian
g(t,\Delta t)
represents the temporal instrument response function.
The resulting time trace is plotted with the fit function in Fig.
5
.
A time resolution of
360\text{\,}\mathrm{fs}
as the FWHM of the Gaussian can be extracted for a slit-width of
100\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
.

VI
Conclusion

We presented a measurement system consisting of a new state-of-the-art VUV light source and an HHG extreme ultraviolet (EUV) source combined, performing trARPES experiments.
The VUV light source operates at high repetition rates of
0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}2\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
, significantly reducing space charge issues, while also suffering less from sample heating.
A spectral bandwidth of
17\text{\,}\mathrm{meV}
was observed while maintaining a time resolution of <
360\text{\,}\mathrm{fs}
.
The pulse-front tilt induced by the monochromator’s grating mainly limits the time resolution.
However, our experiments indicate that a near-NI monochromator layout is sufficient for trARPES experiments at high energy resolution in the VUV regime.
The achieved setup resolution of
\Delta E=$21\text{\,}\mathrm{meV}$
and
\Delta t=$360\text{\,}\mathrm{fs}$
is equal or slightly worse than conventional third harmonics-based setups
peli_time-resolved_2020
;
lee_high_2020
;
kawaguchi_time-_2023
.
A way to improve the time resolution of our setup could be the introduction of a double grating monochromator, compensating the pulse front tilt of the probe pulse and sacrificing little in energy resolution.
Additionally, the HCHG process used to create the higher harmonics offers tunability between
7.2\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}10.8\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
, enabling larger energy and momentum selectivity, which is not possible in third harmonics-based setups.

The VUV light source is incorporated into the existing HHG beamline, extending the tunability to the EUV regime between
20\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}110\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
at low repetition rates from
2\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}6\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
.
The HHG beamline offers the advantage of a higher temporal resolution of <
100\text{\,}\mathrm{fs}
at a reduced energy resolution of <
150\text{\,}\mathrm{meV}
.
Together, these two complementary beamlines allow for extreme flexibility to tackle new complex materials and conduct comprehensive studies in a lab environment.
The trARPES setup described in this article can be tuned in an extreme photon energy range, from the less accessible VUV to the deep EUV regime.
Additionally, it allows both the observation of very fast phenomena and the dynamics of lower energy features, such as high-temperature superconducting gap throughout the FBZ.

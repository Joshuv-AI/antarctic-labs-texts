Abstract

1
Introduction

Sneddon considered in
[
8
, § 2.2]
the following Bessel series in two variables:

(1.1)

S_{q}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{J_{\alpha}(xj_{m,\nu})J_{\beta}(yj_{m,\nu})}{j_{m,\nu}^{q}J_{\nu+1}(j_{m,\nu})^{2}},

where
0<x
,
0<y
,
x+y<2
and
\{j_{m,\nu}\}_{m\geq 0}
are the zeros of the Bessel function
J_{\nu}
of order
\nu
.

The purpose of this paper is to compute explicitly these Sneddon-Bessel series for

q_{n}=2n+\alpha+\beta-2\nu+2,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

under mild conditions on the parameters
\alpha,\beta
and
\nu
.

The problem of the explicit summation of Bessel series is a classical topic, but there is no doubt that it remains of interest today (
[
1
, § 6.8]
,
[
4
]
) and active research is still being done (specially in applied mathematics, mathematical physics and engineering).

In order to state our result in full detail, we need some notation. For
\nu\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{-1,-2,\dots\}
, let us consider the entire function

(1.2)

\Phi_{\nu}(z)=2^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu+1)\frac{J_{\nu}(z)}{z^{\nu}}=\Gamma(\nu+1)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}(z/2)^{2n}}{n!\,\Gamma(n+\nu+1)}.

Define now the polynomial
\delta_{n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)
(of degree
2n
),
n\geq 0
, by the generating function

\frac{\Phi_{\alpha}(xz)\Phi_{\beta}(yz)}{\Phi_{\nu}(z)^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta_{n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)z^{2n}.

This generating function allows the explicit computation of the polynomials
\delta_{n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)
recursively from the Taylor coefficients of the functions
\Phi_{\alpha}
,
\Phi_{\beta}
and
\Phi_{\nu}
.

We also define recursively the functions
\phi_{n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(t)
,
n\geq 0
, by

(1.3)

\displaystyle\phi_{0}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu}\binom{\alpha}{\nu}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\nu-\alpha,\nu}{\beta+1};t^{2}\right)\mathclose{}

(1.4)

\displaystyle\phi_{n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\nu-2n}\left(\frac{1}{2(\alpha+1)}\phi_{n-1}^{\alpha+1,\beta,\nu}(t)+\frac{t^{2}}{2(\beta+1)}\phi_{n-1}^{\alpha,\beta+1,\nu}(t)\right),

where as usual
{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{c};t\right)\mathclose{}
denotes the hypergeometric function

{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{c};t\right)\mathclose{}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(a+m)\Gamma(b+m)\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)\Gamma(c+m)}\cdot\frac{t^{m}}{m!}.

If we write

\delta_{n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=\sum_{2j+2k\leq 2n}A_{2j,2k,n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}x^{2j}y^{2k},

then for
\nu\notin\{0,1,\dots,n\}
,
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<2n+1+\operatorname{Re}\alpha+\operatorname{Re}\beta
and
0<y\leq x<1
(and also for
x+y=2
if
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<2n+\operatorname{Re}\alpha+\operatorname{Re}\beta
), we show that

(1.5)

S_{q_{n}}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)^{2}x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}}{2^{q_{0}}\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}\bigg(x^{2n-2\nu}\phi_{n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(y/x)\\
-\sum_{2j+2k\leq 2n}\frac{A_{2j,2k,n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}}{j+k+\nu-n}x^{2j}y^{2k}\bigg),

if
n\geq 0
, and

(1.6)

S_{q_{n}}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=\frac{x^{\alpha-2\nu+2n}y^{\beta}\Gamma(\nu-n)}{2^{q_{n}}\Gamma(\beta+1)\Gamma(n+\alpha-\nu+1)}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\nu-n,\nu-\alpha-n}{\beta+1};\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)\mathclose{},

if
n<0
. The case
\nu\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
can be computed by passing to the limit.

Some particular cases of (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) are already known. For instance:

(a)

The case
x=1
,
\alpha=\mu+\nu+1
,
\beta=\nu
and
n=-1
is
[
7
, p. 690, (9)]
.

(b)

The case
\alpha=\beta=\nu
,
n\geq 0
,
0\leq y\leq x\leq 1
, is packaged in the Kneser-Sommerfeld expansion (see
[
5
, (2)]
)

(1.7)

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{J_{\nu}(xj_{m,\nu})J_{\nu}(yj_{m,\nu})}{(j_{m,\nu}^{2}-z^{2})J_{\nu+1}(j_{m,\nu})^{2}}=\frac{\pi J_{\nu}(yz)}{4J_{\nu}(z)}\big(Y_{\nu}(z)J_{\nu}(xz)-J_{\nu}(z)Y_{\nu}(xz)\big)

(more precisely:
S_{q_{n}}^{\nu,\nu,\nu}(x,y)
,
n\geq 0
, are the Taylor coefficients at
z=0
of the analytic function of
z
on the right hand side).

(c)

The case
n<0
was computed by Sneddon
[
8
, § 2.2]
and more recently by Martin
[
5
]
.

The content of the paper is as follows. In Section
3
we use the calculus of residues to find a partial fraction expansion of functions of the form
\frac{f(z)}{\Phi_{\nu}(z)^{2}}
, where
f
is an entire function satisfying a suitable bound in
\mathbb{C}
(see Theorem
1
for details).

Theorem
1
leads us to a partial differential equation for the Sneddon-Bessel series
S_{q_{n}}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)
,
n\geq 0
, which we solve in Section
4
to find (
1.5
). Then, the identity (
1.6
) can be easily deduced from the case
n=0
by differentiation.

Using our identity (
1.5
) for the Sneddon-Bessel series we find in Section
5
some extensions of the Kneser-Sommerfeld expansion (
1.7
), among which is the following:

(1.8)

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{j_{m,\nu}^{\nu-\beta}J_{\nu}(xj_{m,\nu})J_{\beta}(yj_{m,\nu})}{(j_{m,\nu}^{2}-z^{2})J_{\nu+1}(j_{m,\nu})^{2}}=\frac{\pi J_{\beta}(yz)}{4z^{\beta-\nu}J_{\nu}(z)}\big(Y_{\nu}(z)J_{\nu}(xz)-J_{\nu}(z)Y_{\nu}(xz)\big),

if
\operatorname{Re}\nu<\operatorname{Re}\beta+1
and
0\leq y\leq x\leq 1
(as usual
Y_{\nu}
denotes the Bessel function of the second kind).

2
Preliminaries

The zeros of the function
\Phi_{\nu}(w)
defined by (
1.2
), that is, the zeros of the even function
J_{\nu}(w)/w^{\nu}
, are simple and can be ordered as a double sequence
\{j_{m,\nu}\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}
with
j_{-m,\nu}=-j_{m,\nu}
and
0\leq\operatorname{Re}j_{m,\nu}\leq\operatorname{Re}j_{m+1,\nu}
for
m\geq 1
(
[
9
, § 15.41, p. 497]
). Although these zeros depend on
\nu
, we will often omit this dependence to avoid unnecessary complications in the notation. The imaginary part of these zeros is bounded and, when
m
is a sufficiently large integer, there is exactly one zero in the strip
m\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{Re}\nu+\frac{\pi}{4}<\operatorname{Re}z<(m+1)\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{Re}\nu+\frac{\pi}{4}
(
[
9
, § 15.4, p. 497]
), so that

\lim_{m\to+\infty}\frac{|j_{m}|}{\pi m}=1.

It follows from the estimate

J_{\nu}(z)=2^{1/2}(\pi z)^{-1/2}\left(\cos\Big(z-\frac{\nu}{2}\pi-\frac{\pi}{4}\Big)+o(1)\right),\quad z\to\infty

(
[
6
, Eq. 10.7.8]
, see also
[
9
, § 7.21(1), p. 199]
) that

J_{\nu}(z)^{2}+J_{\nu+1}(z)^{2}=\frac{2}{\pi z}\left(1+e^{2|\operatorname{Im}z|}o(1)\right),\quad z\to\infty,

where the limit
z\to\infty
is to be taken inside a sector
|\arg(z)|\leq\pi-\delta
. Thus,

(2.1)

0<c\leq|J_{\nu+1}(j_{m})^{2}j_{m}|\leq C

for some constants
c
and
C
not depending on
m
. In terms of
\Phi_{\nu+1}
,

0<c\leq|\Phi_{\nu+1}(j_{m})|^{2}|j_{m}|^{2\operatorname{Re}\nu+3}\leq C

for some constants
c
and
C
not depending on
m
.

Bessel functions satisfy the bound

(2.2)

|J_{\nu}(z)|\leq C\frac{e^{|\operatorname{Im}z|}}{|z|^{1/2}},

for
|z|
large enough, with a constant
C
depending only on
\nu
.
To be precise, for
|z|>\varepsilon>0
and
\nu
on a compact set
K
, there is a constant
C
depending only on
\varepsilon
and
K
, as follows from
[
6
, Eq. 10.4.4 and § 10.17(iv)]
.

For
\mu
and
\eta
satisfying
\operatorname{Re}\mu>\operatorname{Re}\eta>-1
, consider the integral transform
T_{\mu,\eta}
given by

(2.3)

T_{\mu,\eta}(f)(x)=\frac{1}{2^{\mu-\eta-1}\Gamma(\mu-\eta)}\int_{0}^{1}f(xs)s^{2\eta+1}(1-s^{2})^{\mu-\eta-1}\,ds

(with a small abuse of notation, we will often write
T_{\mu,\eta}(f(x))
if it does not cause confusion).

Sonin’s formula for the Bessel functions (
[
9
, 12.11(1), p. 373]
) can be written as

(2.4)

\frac{J_{\mu}(x)}{x^{\mu}}=\frac{2^{\eta+1-\mu}}{\Gamma(\mu-\eta)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{J_{\eta}(xs)}{(xs)^{\eta}}s^{2\eta+1}(1-s^{2})^{\mu-\eta-1}\,ds=T_{\mu,\eta}\left(\frac{J_{\eta}(x)}{x^{\eta}}\right),

valid for
\operatorname{Re}\mu>\operatorname{Re}\eta>-1
.
For
2\operatorname{Re}\eta+r+2>0
, we also have

(2.5)

T_{\mu,\eta}(x^{r})=\frac{\Gamma(\eta+\frac{r}{2}+1)}{2^{\mu-\eta}\Gamma(\mu+\frac{r}{2}+1)}\,x^{r},

as follows from the identity

\int_{0}^{1}s^{a}(1-s^{2})^{b}\,ds=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{a+1}{2})\Gamma(b+1)}{2\Gamma(\frac{a+1}{2}+b+1)},\qquad\operatorname{Re}a,\ \operatorname{Re}b>-1.

The identities (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) can be extended for
\operatorname{Re}\eta<-1
as follows. For complex numbers
\mu
,
\eta
and a positive integer
h
satisfying
\operatorname{Re}\eta>-\frac{h}{2}-1
,
\operatorname{Re}\mu>\operatorname{Re}\eta+h
, consider the integral transform
T_{\mu,\eta,h}
given by

(2.6)

T_{\mu,\eta,h}(f)(x)=\frac{(-1)^{h}2^{\eta+1-\mu}\Gamma(2\eta+2)}{\Gamma(\mu-\eta)\Gamma(2\eta+2+h)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d^{h}}{ds^{h}}(f(xs)(1-s^{2})^{\mu-\eta-1})s^{2\eta+h+1}\,ds.

To be precise,
\eta
should not be half a negative integer (in case it is we will manage somehow).

It is then easy to check that

\displaystyle T_{\mu,\eta,h}(x^{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\eta+\frac{r}{2}+1)}{2^{\mu-\eta}\Gamma(\mu+\frac{r}{2}+1)}\,x^{r},

(2.7)

\displaystyle T_{\mu,\eta,h}\left(\frac{J_{\eta}(x)}{x^{\eta}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{\mu}(x)}{x^{\mu}}.

3
Partial fraction decomposition of Bessel functions

In this section, we use the calculus of residues to find a partial fraction expansion of functions of the form
\frac{f(z)}{\Phi_{\nu}(z)^{2}}
, where
f
is an entire function with some growth control.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f
be an entire function satisfying

|f(z)|\leq c(1+|z|)^{N}e^{\kappa|\operatorname{Im}z|},\quad z\in\mathbb{C},

for certain constants
c>0
,
N\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\kappa\leq 2
. Let
\nu\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{-1,-2,\dots\}
and
n
be a nonnegative integer such that

N+1+2\operatorname{Re}\nu<n,\quad\text{if }\,\kappa=2,

or

N+2\operatorname{Re}\nu<n,\quad\text{if }\,\kappa<2.

Then

\frac{1}{n!}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left(\frac{f(t)}{\Phi_{\nu}(t)^{2}}\right)\\
=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}4(\nu+1)^{2}\frac{\big((2\nu+1)t-(2\nu-n)j_{m}\big)f(j_{m})-j_{m}(j_{m}-t)f^{\prime}(j_{m})}{(j_{m}-t)^{n+2}j_{m}^{3}\Phi_{\nu+1}(j_{m})^{2}},

where the convergence is uniform in bounded subsets of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{j_{m}:m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\}
.

Proof.

Let us fix
t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{j_{m}:m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\}
and consider the holomorphic function
\frac{f(w)}{(w-t)^{n+1}\Phi_{\nu}(w)^{2}}
. It has a pole at
t
of order
n+1
, and a double pole at each
j_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. The residue at
t
is, therefore,

\frac{1}{n!}\frac{d^{n}}{dw^{n}}\left(\frac{f(w)}{\Phi_{\nu}(w)^{2}}\right)_{w=t},

while the residue at each
j_{m}
is

(3.1)

\lim_{w\to j_{m}}\frac{d}{dw}\left(\frac{(w-j_{m})^{2}f(w)}{(w-t)^{n+1}\Phi_{\nu}(w)^{2}}\right)\\
=\frac{d}{dw}\left(\frac{f(w)}{(w-t)^{n+1}}\right)_{w=j_{m}}\left(\lim_{w\to j_{m}}\frac{w-j_{m}}{\Phi_{\nu}(w)}\right)^{2}\\
+\frac{f(j_{m})}{(j_{m}-t)^{n+1}}\frac{d}{dw}\left(\left(\frac{w-j_{m}}{\Phi_{\nu}(w)}\right)^{2}\right)_{w=j_{m}}.

Let us consider separately the last term:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dw}\left(\left(\frac{w-j_{m}}{\Phi_{\nu}(w)}\right)^{2}\right)_{w=j_{m}}

\displaystyle=2\frac{1}{\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime}(j_{m})}\lim_{w\to j_{m}}\frac{\Phi_{\nu}(w)-(w-j_{m})\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime}(w)}{\Phi_{\nu}(w)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime}(j_{m})}\lim_{w\to j_{m}}\frac{-(w-j_{m})\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}(w)}{2\Phi_{\nu}(w)\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime}(w)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime}(j_{m})}\cdot\frac{-\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}(j_{m})}{2\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime}(j_{m})\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime}(j_{m})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}(j_{m})}{\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime}(j_{m})^{3}}.

Now, the identities

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{z}{2(\nu+1)}\Phi_{\nu+1}(z),

\displaystyle\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=-\Phi_{\nu}(z)+\frac{2\nu+1}{2(\nu+1)}\Phi_{\nu+1}(z)

(see
[
9
, § 3.2, p. 45]
) prove that

-\frac{\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}(j_{m})}{\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime}(j_{m})^{3}}=\frac{4(\nu+1)^{2}(2\nu+1)}{j_{m}^{3}\Phi_{\nu+1}(j_{m})^{2}},

so that, going back to (
3.1
) and using (
3.2
) again, the residue at
j_{m}
is

\displaystyle\frac{(j_{m}-t)f^{\prime}(j_{m})-(n+1)f(j_{m})}{(j_{m}-t)^{n+2}}\Big(\frac{1}{\Phi_{\nu}^{\prime}(j_{m})}\Big)^{2}+\frac{f(j_{m})}{(j_{m}-t)^{n+1}}\cdot\frac{4(\nu+1)^{2}(2\nu+1)}{j_{m}^{3}\Phi_{\nu+1}(j_{m})^{2}}

\displaystyle\begin{split}=\frac{(j_{m}-t)f^{\prime}(j_{m})-(n+1)f(j_{m})}{(j_{m}-t)^{n+2}}\cdot\frac{4(\nu+1)^{2}}{j_{m}^{2}\Phi_{\nu+1}(j_{m})^{2}}\\
\hbox{}+\frac{f(j_{m})}{(j_{m}-t)^{n+1}}\cdot\frac{4(\nu+1)^{2}(2\nu+1)}{j_{m}^{3}\Phi_{\nu+1}(j_{m})^{2}}\end{split}

\displaystyle=4(\nu+1)^{2}\,\frac{j_{m}(j_{m}-t)f^{\prime}(j_{m})+\big((2\nu-n)j_{m}-(2\nu+1)t\big)f(j_{m})}{(j_{m}-t)^{n+2}j_{m}^{3}\Phi_{\nu+1}(j_{m})^{2}}.

Thus, if
D=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=A\}
is a large circle of radius
A>|t|
with the only condition, at the moment, that none of the points
j_{m}
lie in
D
, the calculus of residues gives

(3.3)

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{D}\frac{f(w)}{(w-t)^{n+1}\Phi_{\nu}(w)^{2}}\,dw=\frac{1}{n!}\frac{d^{n}}{dw^{n}}\left(\frac{f(w)}{\Phi_{\nu}(w)^{2}}\right)_{w=t}\\
+\sum_{|j_{m}|<A}4(\nu+1)^{2}\,\frac{j_{m}(j_{m}-t)f^{\prime}(j_{m})+\big((2\nu-n)j_{m}-(2\nu+1)t\big)f(j_{m})}{(j_{m}-t)^{n+2}j_{m}^{3}\Phi_{\nu+1}(j_{m})^{2}}.

Now, the value of
A
can be chosen arbitrarily large and such that there exists some constant
c>0
, independent of
A
, satisfying

c\,\frac{e^{|\operatorname{Im}w|}}{|w|^{1/2}}\leq|J_{\nu}(w)|

for
w\in D
(see
[
2
, formula (2.4)]
). Thus,

\bigg|\frac{f(w)}{(w-t)^{n+1}\Phi_{\nu}(w)^{2}}\bigg|=C\,\frac{|f(w)||w^{\nu}|^{2}}{|w-t|^{n+1}|J_{\nu}(w)|^{2}}\leq C\,\frac{A^{N+1+2\operatorname{Re}\nu}e^{(\kappa-2)|\operatorname{Im}w|}}{(A-|t|)^{n+1}}

for
w\in D
, where
C
is a constant, independent of
A
, but possibly different at each occurrence. The natural parametrization of
D
then gives

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{D}\frac{f(w)}{(w-t)^{n+1}\Phi_{\nu}(w)^{2}}\,dw\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{A^{N+2+2\operatorname{Re}\nu}e^{(\kappa-2)A|\sin s|}}{(A-|t|)^{n+1}}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{2C}{\pi}\frac{A^{N+2+2\operatorname{Re}\nu}}{(A-|t|)^{n+1}}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}e^{(\kappa-2)A|\sin s|}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C}{\pi}\frac{A^{N+2+2\operatorname{Re}\nu}}{(A-|t|)^{n+1}}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}e^{(\kappa-2)A2s/\pi}\,ds.

Now, the last integral is obviously a constant if
\kappa=2
, while it is
O(A^{-1})
if
\kappa<2
.
Taking this bound into (
3.3
) and letting
A
be arbitrarily large proves the theorem.
∎

Evaluating at
t=0
the identity of Theorem
1
, gives

(3.4)

\frac{1}{n!}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left(\frac{f(t)}{\Phi_{\nu}(t)^{2}}\right)_{t=0}=-4(\nu+1)^{2}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{(2\nu-n)f(j_{m})+j_{m}f^{\prime}(j_{m})}{j_{m}^{n+4}\Phi_{\nu+1}(j_{m})^{2}},

under the assumption that

\displaystyle N+1+2\operatorname{Re}\nu

\displaystyle<n,\quad\text{if }\kappa=2,

\displaystyle N+2\operatorname{Re}\nu

\displaystyle<n,\quad\text{if }\kappa<2.

We then define the double Bessel numbers
\delta_{n}^{f,\nu}
by

\delta_{n}^{f,\nu}=\frac{1}{n!}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left(\frac{f(t)}{\Phi_{\nu}(t)^{2}}\right)_{t=0}.

These are the Taylor coefficients of
\frac{f(t)}{\Phi_{\nu}(t)^{2}}
at
t=0
, in other words,

\frac{f(t)}{\Phi_{\nu}(t)^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta_{n}^{f,\nu}t^{n}

in a neighbourhood of
0
.

4
Summing Sneddon-Bessel series explicitly

Our goal is to sum the Sneddon-Bessel series

\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{J_{\alpha}(xj_{m})J_{\beta}(yj_{m})}{j_{m}^{2n+\alpha+\beta-2\nu+2}J_{\nu+1}(j_{m})^{2}},

where
\alpha,\beta,\nu\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{-1,-2,\dots\}
,
0<x
,
0<y
,
x+y\leq 2
, and
n
is an integer.
To this end, let us take

(4.1)

\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\Phi_{\alpha}(xj_{m})\Phi_{\beta}(yj_{m})}{j_{m}^{2n+4}\Phi_{\nu+1}(j_{m})^{2}},

with the condition that

(4.2)

2\operatorname{Re}\nu<2n+1+\operatorname{Re}\alpha+\operatorname{Re}\beta.

According to (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), this guarantees that the series converges absolutely. These series are related to the Sneddon-Bessel series (
1.1
) by

(4.3)

S_{q_{n}}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=\frac{\Gamma(\nu+2)^{2}x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}}{2^{\alpha+\beta-2\nu-2}\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y).

Under the stronger condition

(4.4)

2\operatorname{Re}\nu<2n+\operatorname{Re}\alpha+\operatorname{Re}\beta,

termwise differentation in (
4.1
) is allowed. In particular, we obtain

(4.5)

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=-\frac{x}{2(\alpha+1)}\xi_{n-1,\alpha+1,\beta,\nu}(x,y)

(and the same for the other partial derivative).

4.1
The case
n\geq 0

Let us assume firstly that
n
is a nonnegative integer (later on we will address the case when
n
is negative).

The function
f(z)=\Phi_{\alpha}(xz)\Phi_{\beta}(yz)
meets the conditions of Theorem
1
with
N=-\operatorname{Re}\alpha-\operatorname{Re}\beta-1
and
\kappa=x+y
, and the condition
N+2\operatorname{Re}\nu<2n
of Theorem
1
is therefore (
4.2
). Thus, (
3.4
) becomes

(4.6)

\frac{\delta_{n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)}{-8(\nu+1)^{2}}=(2\nu-2n)\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)+x\frac{\partial\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)}{\partial x}+y\frac{\partial\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)}{\partial y},

where the function

(4.7)

\delta_{n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(2n)!}\frac{d^{2n}}{dz^{2n}}\left(\frac{\Phi_{\alpha}(xz)\Phi_{\beta}(yz)}{\Phi_{\nu}(z)^{2}}\right)_{z=0}

is a polynomial in
x^{2}
and
y^{2}
(that is, even powers of
x
and
y
) which could be computed recursively from the Taylor coefficients of the functions
\Phi_{\alpha}
,
\Phi_{\beta}
and
\Phi_{\nu}
involved. Notice that

(4.8)

\frac{\Phi_{\alpha}(xz)\Phi_{\beta}(yz)}{\Phi_{\nu}(z)^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta_{n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)z^{2n}.

Let us write

(4.9)

\delta_{n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=\sum_{2j+2k\leq 2n}A_{2j,2k,n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}x^{2j}y^{2k},

and assume also, for simplicity, that
\nu\neq 0,1,\dots,n
.
Then, it is easy to see that the solution to (
4.6
) is

(4.10)

\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=\frac{1}{16(\nu+1)^{2}}\bigg({-}\sum_{2j+2k\leq 2n}\frac{A_{2j,2k,n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}}{j+k+\nu-n}\,x^{2j}y^{2k}\\
+x^{2n-2\nu}\phi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(y/x)\bigg),

if (
4.4
) holds, where
\phi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}
is a one variable function to be determined. In case
\nu\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
, some logarithmic terms appear also.

Before going on, let us focus on the dependence of these functions and constants on the parameter
\alpha
and
\beta
. It is apparent from (
4.7
) and (
4.9
) that each
A_{2j,2k,n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}
is a rational function of
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\nu
.
If
n
,
\nu
,
x
,
y
and
\beta
(respectively,
\alpha
) are fixed, then the function
\Phi_{\alpha}(xj_{m})
is holomorphic on
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{-1,-2,\dots\}
, and so is
\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)
(resp.,
\beta
) under the condition (
4.2
) (the series involved converge uniformly on
\alpha
-compacts, as follows from (
1.2
) and (
2.2
)). The same applies therefore to
\phi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(y/x)
. This analytic dependence on
\alpha
(resp.,
\beta
) will eventually allow us to extend some identities by analytic continuation.

Thus, formula (
4.10
), which in principle requires (
4.4
) to hold, extends to the whole range (
4.2
) in this way: firstly, (
4.5
) can be written as

\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=-\frac{2\alpha}{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\xi_{n+1,\alpha-1,\beta,\nu}(x,y)

on the whole range (
4.2
); using now (
4.10
) on the right-hand side gives an expression for
\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)
with holomorphic coefficients, which by analytic continuation must equal the coefficients in (
4.10
).

In view of (
4.10
), it is enough to find the function
\phi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}
to explicitly determine the function
\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}
.
So let us now find a recursion for the functions
\phi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}
. Given
0<t<1
, let us write

\varphi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(s)=\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(s,ts)

for
s
small enough.
Then (
4.5
) yields

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(s,ts)+t\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(s,ts)

\displaystyle=-\frac{s}{2(\alpha+1)}\xi_{n-1,\alpha+1,\beta,\nu}(s,ts)-\frac{st^{2}}{2(\beta+1)}\xi_{n-1,\alpha,\beta+1,\nu}(s,ts).

The coefficient of
s^{2n-1-2\nu}
on the right-hand side, as follows from (
4.10
), is

\frac{1}{16(\nu+1)^{2}}\left(-\frac{1}{2(\alpha+1)}\phi_{n-1,\alpha+1,\beta,\nu}(t)-\frac{t^{2}}{2(\beta+1)}\phi_{n-1,\alpha,\beta+1,\nu}(t)\right).

On the other hand, (
4.10
) translates into

\varphi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(s)=\frac{1}{16(\nu+1)^{2}}\bigg({-}\sum_{2j+2k\leq 2n}\frac{A_{2j,2k,n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}}{j+k+\nu-n}\,s^{2j+2k}t^{2k}\\
+s^{2n-2\nu}\phi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(t)\bigg),

so that the coefficient of
s^{2n-1-2\nu}
in
\varphi^{\prime}_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(s)
is

\frac{2n-2\nu}{16(\nu+1)^{2}}\phi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(t).

Equating both formulas for the coefficient of
s^{2n-1-2\nu}
results in

(4.11)

\phi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(t)=\frac{1}{2\nu-2n}\left(\frac{1}{2(\alpha+1)}\phi_{n-1,\alpha+1,\beta,\nu}(t)+\frac{t^{2}}{2(\beta+1)}\phi_{n-1,\alpha,\beta+1,\nu}(t)\right).

This recursion reduces the problem of finding
\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)
to the case
n=0
, so let us concentrate on this. We first consider the case
\alpha=\beta=\nu
, then address the general case. Observe that condition (
4.2
) holds for
n=0
,
\alpha=\beta=\nu
. Now, (
4.7
) gives
\delta_{0}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=1
, so that (
4.10
) is

\xi_{0,\nu,\nu,\nu}(x,y)=\frac{1}{16(\nu+1)^{2}}\bigg({-}\frac{1}{\nu}+x^{-2\nu}\phi_{0,\nu,\nu,\nu}(y/x)\bigg).

Since
\Phi_{\nu}(j_{m})=0
, the definition (
4.1
) trivially gives

\xi_{0,\nu,\nu,\nu}(1,t)=0,\quad 0<t<1.

Therefore,
\phi_{0,\nu,\nu,\nu}(t)=\frac{1}{\nu}
for
0<t<1
and (by symmetry)

(4.12)

\xi_{0,\nu,\nu,\nu}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{16\nu(\nu+1)^{2}}\left(-1+x^{-2\nu}\right),&\text{if }y\leq x,\\
\frac{1}{16\nu(\nu+1)^{2}}\left(-1+y^{-2\nu}\right),&\text{if }x<y.\end{cases}

Let us now find
\xi_{0,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)
. By symmetry, we can assume that
y\leq x
without loss of generality. Sonin’s formula (
2.4
) easily gives

\xi_{0,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=\frac{4\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(\nu+1)^{2}\Gamma(\alpha-\nu)\Gamma(\beta-\nu)}\\
\quad\times\iint_{[0,1]\times[0,1]}\xi_{0,\nu,\nu,\nu}(xr,ys)r^{2\nu+1}s^{2\nu+1}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-\nu-1}(1-s^{2})^{\beta-\nu-1}\,dr\,ds

with the additional restrictions
\operatorname{Re}\alpha>\operatorname{Re}\nu>-1
,
\operatorname{Re}\beta>\operatorname{Re}\nu>-1
.
To evaluate this integral, let us separate the square
[0,1]\times[0,1]
into the sets

\displaystyle A_{x,y}

\displaystyle=\{(r,s):xr\geq ys\},

\displaystyle B_{x,y}

\displaystyle=\{(r,s):xr<ys\},

so that

\xi_{0,\nu,\nu,\nu}(x\sqrt{r},y\sqrt{s})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{16\nu(1+\nu)^{2}}\left(-1+x^{-2\nu}r^{-2\nu}\right),&(r,s)\in A_{x,y},\\
\frac{1}{16\nu(1+\nu)^{2}}\left(-1+y^{-2\nu}s^{-2\nu}\right),&(r,s)\in B_{x,y}.\end{cases}

The above integral is therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\nu(\nu+1)^{2}}\Bigg(

\displaystyle{-}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}r^{2\nu+1}s^{2\nu+1}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-\nu-1}(1-s^{2})^{\beta-\nu-1}\,dr\,ds

\displaystyle+\iint_{A_{x,y}}x^{-2\nu}r^{-2\nu}r^{2\nu+1}s^{2\nu+1}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-\nu-1}(1-s^{2})^{\beta-\nu-1}\,dr\,ds

\displaystyle+\iint_{B_{x,y}}y^{-2\nu}s^{-2\nu}r^{2\nu+1}s^{2\nu+1}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-\nu-1}(1-s^{2})^{\beta-\nu-1}\,dr\,ds\Bigg).

The first of these three integrals is immediate:

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}r^{2\nu+1}s^{2\nu+1}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-\nu-1}(1-s^{2})^{\beta-\nu-1}\,dr\,ds=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha-\nu)\Gamma(\beta-\nu)\Gamma(\nu+1)^{2}}{4\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}.

Taking into account that
y\leq x
, the second integral is

\displaystyle\iint_{A_{x,y}}x^{-2\nu}r^{-2\nu}r^{2\nu+1}s^{2\nu+1}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-\nu-1}(1-s^{2})^{\beta-\nu-1}\,dr\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=x^{-2\nu}\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\int_{y^{2}s^{2}/x^{2}}^{1}r(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-\nu-1}\,dr\bigg)s^{2\nu+1}(1-s^{2})^{\beta-\nu-1}\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{x^{-2\nu}}{2(\alpha-\nu)}\int_{0}^{1}\left(1-\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}s^{2}\right)^{\alpha-\nu}s^{2\nu+1}(1-s^{2})^{\beta-\nu-1}\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{x^{-2\nu}\Gamma(\nu+1)\Gamma(\beta-\nu)}{4(\alpha-\nu)\Gamma(\beta+1)}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\nu-\alpha, \nu+1}{\beta+1};\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)\mathclose{},

where the integral representation of the hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}
is used.

The third integral is

\displaystyle\iint_{B_{x,y}}y^{-2\nu}s^{-2\nu}r^{2\nu+1}s^{2\nu+1}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-\nu-1}(1-s^{2})^{\beta-\nu-1}\,dr\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=y^{-2\nu}\int_{0}^{y^{2}/x^{2}}\bigg(\int_{x^{2}r^{2}2/y^{2}}^{1}s(1-s^{2})^{\beta-\nu-1}\,ds\bigg)r^{2\nu+1}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-\nu-1}\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{y^{-2\nu}}{2(\beta-\nu)}\int_{0}^{y^{2}/x^{2}}\left(1-\frac{x^{2}}{y^{2}}r^{2}\right)^{\beta-\nu}r^{2\nu+1}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-\nu-1}\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{y^{2}x^{-2\nu-2}}{2(\beta-\nu)}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t^{2})^{\beta-\nu}t^{2\nu+1}\left(1-\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}t^{2}\right)^{\alpha-\nu-1}\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{y^{2}x^{-2\nu-2}\Gamma(\nu+1)\Gamma(\beta-\nu+1)}{4(\beta-\nu)\Gamma(\beta+2)}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\nu-\alpha+1,\nu+1}{\beta+2};\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)\mathclose{}.

Putting together all the pieces,

\xi_{0,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=\frac{1}{16\nu(\nu+1)^{2}}\Bigg({-1}+\frac{x^{-2\nu}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\nu+1)\Gamma(\alpha-\nu+1)}\\
\times\bigg({}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\nu-\alpha,\nu+1}{\beta+1};\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)\mathclose{}+\frac{y^{2}(\alpha-\nu)}{x^{2}(\beta+1)}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\nu-\alpha+1,\nu+1}{\beta+2};\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)\mathclose{}\bigg)\Bigg).

Finally, the elementary relation

{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b+1}{c};t\right)\mathclose{}-t\,\frac{a}{c}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a+1,b+1}{c+1};t\right)\mathclose{}={}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{c};t\right)\mathclose{}

gives

(4.13)

\displaystyle\xi_{0,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\nu(\nu+1)^{2}}\left(-1+x^{-2\nu}\binom{\alpha}{\nu}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\nu-\alpha,\nu}{\beta+1};\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)\mathclose{}\right),

valid for
\operatorname{Re}\alpha,\operatorname{Re}\beta>\operatorname{Re}\nu>-1
, and
0<y\leq x
,
x+y<2
.

Assuming that
\operatorname{Re}\nu>-1
, the identity (
4.13
) extends to the whole range given by (
4.2
), i.e.,
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<1+\operatorname{Re}\alpha+\operatorname{Re}\beta
, by an argument of analyticity (and also for
x+y=2
if
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<\operatorname{Re}\alpha+\operatorname{Re}\beta
).

Let us consider now the case
\operatorname{Re}\nu<-1
. Take a positive integer
h
such that
\operatorname{Re}\nu>-h/2-1
and
\alpha,\beta
satisfying
\operatorname{Re}\alpha,\operatorname{Re}\beta>\operatorname{Re}\nu+h
. Let us assume for the moment that
\nu
is not half a negative integer; using the integral transform
T_{\alpha,\nu,h}
defined by (
2.6
) acting on
x
and
T_{\beta,\nu,h}
acting on
y
, we get from (
2.7
):

\displaystyle\xi_{0,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=\frac{4\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(\nu+1)^{2}\Gamma(\alpha-\nu)\Gamma(\beta-\nu)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(2\nu+2)^{2}}{\Gamma(2\nu+2+h)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\iint\limits_{[0,1]\times[0,1]}\frac{\partial^{2h}}{\partial r^{h}\,\partial s^{h}}\Big(\xi_{0,\nu,\nu,\nu}(xr,ys)(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-\nu-1}(1-s^{2})^{\beta-\nu-1}\Big)(rs)^{2\nu+h+1}\,dr\,ds.

The function
\xi_{0,\nu,\nu,\nu}(x,y)
is given by (
4.12
). Therefore,
\xi_{0,\nu,\nu,\nu}
is analytic in
\nu
and so is the function on the right hand side of the above identity (on the region
\operatorname{Re}\nu>-h/2-1
). For
\nu>-1
, integrating by parts we deduce that this function is equal to

(4.14)

\frac{4\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(\nu+1)^{2}\Gamma(\alpha-\nu)\Gamma(\beta-\nu)}\\
\times\iint_{[0,1]\times[0,1]}\xi_{0,\nu,\nu,\nu}(xr,ys)(1-r^{2})^{\alpha-\nu-1}(1-s^{2})^{\beta-\nu-1}(rs)^{2\nu+1}\,dr\,ds.

Proceeding as before, we deduce that for
\nu>-1
and
0<y\leq x<1
, the function (
4.14
) is equal to the function on the right hand side of (
4.13
), which is also analytic in
\nu
. This proves the identity (
4.13
) also for
\operatorname{Re}\nu>-h/2-1
and
\operatorname{Re}\alpha,\operatorname{Re}\beta>\operatorname{Re}\nu+h
. Using again an argument of analyticity on the variables
\alpha
and
\beta
, we prove that (
4.13
) holds indeed for
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<1+\operatorname{Re}\alpha+\operatorname{Re}\beta
(and also for
x+y=2
if
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<\operatorname{Re}\alpha+\operatorname{Re}\beta
). The requirement that
\nu
is not half a negative integer can be suppressed by continuity.

By the way, this means that

(4.15)

\phi_{0,\alpha,\beta,\nu}(t)=\frac{1}{\nu}\binom{\alpha}{\nu}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\nu-\alpha,\nu}{\beta+1};t^{2}\right)\mathclose{},\quad 0<t<1,

which, together with (
4.11
), allows to find the functions
\phi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}
and, therefore,
\xi_{n,\alpha,\beta,\nu}
for every positive integer

.

For the sake of completeness, we display in full extension the cases

.

4.1.1
The case

Identities (
4.3
) and (
4.13
) give

S_{q_{0}}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(x,y)=\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)^{2}x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}}{2^{q_{0}}\nu\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}\left(-1+x^{-2\nu}\binom{\alpha}{\nu}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\nu-\alpha,\nu}{\beta+1};\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)\mathclose{}\right),

valid for
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<1+\operatorname{Re}\alpha+\operatorname{Re}\beta
,
\nu\neq 0
, and
0<y\leq x
,
x+y<2
(and also for
x+y=2
if
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<\operatorname{Re}\alpha+\operatorname{Re}\beta
).

Now,
S_{q_{0}}^{\alpha,\beta,0}(x,y)
can be obtained taking

in the above formula: on one hand, after writing the hypergeometric function as a power series and looking for a hypergeometric representation of the resulting limit, it turns out that

\lim_{\nu\to 0}\frac{1}{\nu}\frac{x^{-2\nu}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\nu+1)\Gamma(\alpha-\nu+1)}\left(-1+{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\nu-\alpha,\nu}{\beta+1};\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)\mathclose{}\right)\\

On the other hand, L’Hôpital’s rule gives

where
H_{\alpha}=\gamma+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}

\alpha
(as usual

Then, we conclude that

S_{q_{0}}^{\alpha,\beta,0}(x,y)\\
=\frac{x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}}{2^{q_{0}}\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}\biggl(-2\log x+H_{\alpha}-\frac{\alpha}{\beta+1}\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1,1,1-\alpha}{2,\beta+2};\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)\mathclose{}\biggr).

), (

), (

+\frac{x^{2-2\nu}\binom{\alpha}{\nu}}{2\nu(\nu-1)}\bigg(\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\nu-\alpha-1,\nu}{\beta+1};\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)\mathclose{}}{\alpha+1-\nu}+\frac{\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\nu-\alpha,\nu}{\beta+2};\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)\mathclose{}}{\beta+1}\bigg)\Bigg),

+\frac{\alpha}{\beta+1}\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1,1,1-\alpha}{2,\beta+2};\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}}\right)\mathclose{}\Bigg).

4.3
The one variable case

We will need later the following Sneddon-Bessel series in one variable:

(4.16)

\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\alpha,\nu}(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{J_{\alpha}(xj_{m,\nu})}{j_{m,\nu}^{2n+\alpha-2\nu+2}J_{\nu+1}(j_{m,\nu})^{2}},

where
0\leq x\leq 2
.

If we assume
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<2n+1/2+\operatorname{Re}\alpha
, then the uniform convergence of the Sneddon-Bessel series (
1.1
) holds also for
y=0
, hence the Sneddon-Bessel series (
4.16
) arises after dividing the Sneddon-Bessel series (
1.1
) by
y^{\beta}
and taking
y\to 0
. This can be done in the identities (
1.5
) and (
1.6
). To this end, we have to compute the sequence

d_{n}^{\alpha,\nu}=\phi_{n}^{\alpha,\beta,\nu}(0),\quad n\geq 0.

After some easy computations, using (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), we arrive at

d_{n}^{\alpha,\nu}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\nu-n)}{2^{2n}\Gamma(\nu+1)^{2}\Gamma(n+1+\alpha-\nu)},\quad n\geq 0.

Let us define the polynomial
\delta_{n}^{\alpha,\nu}(x)
(of degree
2n
),
n\geq 0
, by the generating function

(4.17)

\frac{\Phi_{\alpha}(xz)}{\Phi_{\nu}(z)^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta_{n}^{\alpha,\nu}(x)z^{2n}.

This generating function allows the explicit computation of the polynomials
\delta_{n}^{\alpha,\nu}(x)
recursively from the Taylor coefficients of the functions
\Phi_{\alpha}
and
\Phi_{\nu}
.

If we write

(4.18)

\delta_{n}^{\alpha,\nu}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{j,n}^{\alpha,\nu}x^{2j},

then for
\nu\notin\{0,1,\dots,n\}
,
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<2n+1/2+\operatorname{Re}\alpha
and
0<x<2

(and also for
x=2
if
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<2n-1/2+\operatorname{Re}\alpha
), we have

(4.19)

\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\alpha,\nu}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)^{2}x^{\alpha}}{2^{\alpha-2\nu+2}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\bigg(\frac{\binom{\alpha}{\nu}\Gamma(\alpha-\nu+1)\Gamma(\nu-n)}{2^{2n}\Gamma(\nu+1)\Gamma(n+1+\alpha-\nu)}\,x^{2n-2\nu}\\
-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{A_{j,n}^{\alpha,\nu}}{j+\nu-n}x^{2j}\bigg),

if
n\geq 0
, and

\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\alpha,\nu}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\nu-n)}{2^{2n+\alpha-2\nu+2}\Gamma(n+\alpha-\nu+1)}\,x^{\alpha-2\nu+2n},

if
n<0
.

For instance, for
n=0
we get

\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\alpha,\nu}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)^{2}x^{\alpha}}{2^{2+\alpha-2\nu}\nu\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\left(-1+\binom{\alpha}{\nu}x^{-2\nu}\right),

assuming
0<x<2
,
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<\frac{1}{2}+\operatorname{Re}\alpha
, and
\nu\neq 0
(also for
x=2
if
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<\operatorname{Re}\alpha
). And for
\nu=0
and
-\frac{1}{2}<\operatorname{Re}\alpha
,

\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\alpha,0}(x)=\frac{x^{\alpha}}{2^{\alpha+1}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\Bigl(-\log x+\frac{1}{2}H_{\alpha}\Bigr)

which was previously computed using a different method in
[
3
, (4)]
.

And for
n=1
, the corresponding Sneddon-Bessel series is

\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\alpha,\nu}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)\Gamma(\nu-1)x^{\alpha}}{2^{3+\alpha-2\nu}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\left(\frac{(\nu-1)x^{2}}{2(\alpha+1)}-\frac{\nu}{\nu+1}+\frac{\binom{\alpha}{\nu}x^{2-2\nu}}{2(\alpha+1-\nu)}\right),

assuming
0<x<2
,
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<\frac{5}{2}+\operatorname{Re}\alpha
, and
\nu\neq 0,1
.

The cases
\nu=0,1
can be deduced taking limits as
\nu\to 0
and
\nu\to 1
, respectively. As a result,

\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\alpha,0}(x)=\frac{x^{\alpha}}{2^{\alpha+3}\Gamma(\alpha+2)}\biggl(x^{2}\log x-\frac{1+H_{\alpha+1}}{2}\,x^{2}+\alpha+1\biggr)

for
-\frac{5}{2}<\operatorname{Re}\alpha
, and

\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\alpha,1}(x)=\frac{x^{\alpha}}{2^{\alpha+1}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\biggl(-\log x+\frac{-3+2H_{\alpha}}{4}+\frac{1}{2(\alpha+1)}\,x^{2}\biggr)

whenever
-\frac{1}{2}<\operatorname{Re}\alpha
.

5
Extending the Kneser-Sommerfeld expansion

In this section we use the identities (
4.10
) to prove some extensions of the Kneser-Sommerfeld expansion (
1.7
).

For the sake of completeness, we first prove the Kneser-Sommerfeld expansion (
1.7
).
In terms of the functions
\Phi_{\nu}
defined by (
1.2
), the identity to be proved is

(5.1)

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Phi_{\nu}(xj_{m,\nu})\Phi_{\nu}(yj_{m,\nu})}{j_{m,\nu}^{2}(j_{m,\nu}^{2}-z^{2})\Phi_{\nu+1}(j_{m,\nu})^{2}}\\
=\frac{\pi J_{\nu}(yz)\big(Y_{\nu}(z)J_{\nu}(xz)-J_{\nu}(z)Y_{\nu}(xz)\big)}{16(\nu+1)^{2}(xy)^{\nu}J_{\nu}(z)}.

Write
\varphi(x,y,z)
and
\psi(x,y,z)
for the left and right hand sides of (
5.1
), respectively. Using the geometric series, we can write

\varphi(x,y,z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Phi_{\nu}(xj_{m,\nu})\Phi_{\nu}(yj_{m,\nu})}{j_{m,\nu}^{2}(j_{m,\nu}^{2}-z^{2})\Phi_{\nu+1}(j_{m,\nu})^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\xi_{n,\nu,\nu,\nu}(x,y)z^{2n},

where the function
\xi_{n,\nu,\nu,\nu}(x,y)
is defined by (
4.1
).

Consider now the partial differential equation

(5.2)

-\frac{\Phi_{\nu}(xz)\Phi_{\nu}(yj_{m,\nu})}{8(\nu+1)^{2}\Phi_{\nu}(z)^{2}}=2\nu U(x,y,z)-z\frac{\partial U}{\partial z}(x,y,z)+x\frac{\partial U}{\partial x}(x,y,z)+y\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}(x,y,z).

On the one hand, using the partial differential equation (
4.6
) for
\xi_{n}(x,y)
and (
4.8
), we get that
\varphi(x,y,z)
satisfies the partial differential equation (
5.2
). On the other hand, it is a matter of computation to check that the function
\psi(x,y,z)
satisfies the partial differential equation (
5.2
) as well.
Hence, we deduce that

\varphi(x,y,z)-\psi(x,y,z)=x^{-2\nu}\rho(y/x,z/x),

for certain two-variable function
\rho
. But the definition of
\varphi
and
\psi
as both sides of (
5.1
) shows that

\varphi(1,y,z)=\psi(1,y,z)=0,

so that
\rho=0
and the identity (
5.1
) holds.

For
\operatorname{Re}\beta>\operatorname{Re}\nu>-1
, the identity (
1.8
) follows by applying the integral transform
T_{\beta,\nu}
defined by (
2.3
) acting in the variable
y
to both sides of the Kneser-Sommerfeld expansion (
1.7
) and using Sonin’s formula (
2.4
). With a standard argument of analyticity, the identity (
1.8
) extends to
-1<\operatorname{Re}\nu<\operatorname{Re}\beta+1
.

If
\operatorname{Re}\nu<-1
, we can take a positive integer
h
satisfying
\operatorname{Re}\nu>-h/2-1
. When
\operatorname{Re}\beta>\operatorname{Re}\nu+h
, using the integral transform
T_{\beta,\nu,h}
defined by (
2.6
), we prove the identity (
1.8
) for
\operatorname{Re}\beta>\operatorname{Re}\nu+h
, and using an argument of analyticity for
\operatorname{Re}\nu<\operatorname{Re}\beta+1
.

The Kneser-Sommerfeld expansion has the following one variable version:

(5.3)

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{j_{m,\nu}^{\nu}J_{\nu}(xj_{m,\nu})}{(j_{m,\nu}^{2}-z^{2})J_{\nu+1}(j_{m,\nu})^{2}}=\frac{\pi z^{\nu}}{4J_{\nu}(z)}\big(Y_{\nu}(z)J_{\nu}(xz)-J_{\nu}(z)Y_{\nu}(xz)\big),

valid for
\operatorname{Re}\nu<1/2
(it follows easily dividing the identity (
1.7
) by
y^{\nu}
and then taking limit as
y\to 0
).

Now, the well-known properties of the Bessel function of the second kind
Y_{\nu}
allow us to rewrite (
5.3
) as

(5.4)

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{j_{m,\nu}^{\nu}J_{\nu}(xj_{m,\nu})}{(j_{m,\nu}^{2}-z^{2})J_{\nu+1}(j_{m,\nu})^{2}}=\frac{\pi z^{\nu}}{4\sin(\pi\nu)J_{\nu}(z)}\big(J_{\nu}(z)J_{-\nu}(xz)-J_{-\nu}(z)J_{\nu}(xz)\big)

(as usual, if
\nu=n
is a nonnegative integer, the function on the right can be understood as the limit as
\nu\to n
).

We finish this paper proving the following extension of the identity (
5.4
):

(5.5)

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{j_{m,\nu}^{2\nu-\alpha}J_{\alpha}(xj_{m,\nu})}{(j_{m,\nu}^{2}-z^{2})J_{\nu+1}(j_{m,\nu})^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{4\sin(\pi\nu)J_{\nu}(z)}\\
\times\Bigg(\frac{x^{\alpha-2\nu}J_{\nu}(z)\,{}_{1}F_{2}\mathopen{}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1}{-\nu+1,\alpha-\nu+1};-\frac{(xz)^{2}}{4}\right)\mathclose{}}{2^{\alpha-2\nu}\Gamma(-\nu+1)\Gamma(\alpha-\nu+1)}-z^{2\nu-\alpha}J_{-\nu}(z)J_{\alpha}(xz)\Bigg),

valid for
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<\operatorname{Re}\alpha+1/2
,
\alpha-\nu+1\neq 0,-1,-2,\dots
,
\nu\notin\mathbb{Z}
and
0<x\leq 2
(for
\nu=n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can extend it passing to the limit
\nu\to n
).

For
\operatorname{Re}\nu<1/2
and
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<\operatorname{Re}\alpha+1/2
, the proof is similar to that of identity (
1.8
). For
\operatorname{Re}\alpha>\operatorname{Re}\nu
and
-1<\operatorname{Re}\nu<1/2
, the identity (
5.5
) follows by applying the integral transform
T_{\alpha,\nu}
defined by (
2.3
) to both sides of the one variable Kneser-Sommerfeld expansion (
5.4
): in the left hand side we use Sonin’s formula (
2.4
) and in the right hand side the identity (
2.5
) applied to the power expansion of
J_{-\nu}(xz)
.
Using a standard argument of analyticity, the identity (
5.5
) extends to
-2<2\operatorname{Re}\nu<\operatorname{Re}\alpha+1/2
. If
\operatorname{Re}\nu<-1
, we can take a positive integer
h
satisfying
\operatorname{Re}\nu>-h/2-1
and use the integral transform
T_{\alpha,\nu,h}
defined by (
2.6
).

In order to extend the identity (
5.4
) to
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<\operatorname{Re}\alpha+1/2
,
\alpha-\nu+1\neq 0,-1,-2,\dots
,
\nu\notin\mathbb{Z}
and
0<x\leq 2
, we proceed as follows. First of all, since both sides of the identity (
5.4
) are analytic functions of
z
, it would be enough to prove (
5.4
) for
|z|
small enough. To this end, let us find suitable bounds for the Sneddon-Bessel series (
4.16
). Looking at (
4.19
), for each
\nu
-compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
we have

(5.6)

\bigg|\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)\binom{\alpha}{\nu}\Gamma(\alpha-\nu+1)\Gamma(\nu-n)x^{\alpha+2n-2\nu}}{2^{\alpha-2\nu+2+2n}\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(n+1+\alpha-\nu)}\bigg|\leq c_{\alpha,K},

whith a positive constant
c_{\alpha,K}
depending only on
\alpha
and the
\nu
-compact
K
; this follows from the fact that
0<x\leq 2
and

\displaystyle\Gamma(\nu-n)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)}{\nu(\nu-1)\cdots(\nu-n)},

\displaystyle\Gamma(n+1+\alpha-\nu)

\displaystyle=(n+\alpha-\nu)\cdots(\alpha-\nu+2)(\alpha-\nu+1)\Gamma(\alpha-\nu+1).

Now let us consider the analytic function

F_{j,\nu}(z)=\frac{z^{2j}}{\Phi_{\nu}(z)^{2}}.

for each
j\geq 0
. There exists some constant
C_{K}
depending on the
\nu
-compact
K
such that

|F_{j,\nu}(z)|\leq C_{K},

on the circle
|z|=1/2
. Then, Cauchy’s integral formula gives

\bigg|\frac{F_{j,\nu}^{(2n)}(0)}{(2n)!}\bigg|\leq C_{K}2^{2n}.

Using again Cauchy’s integral formula, together with (
4.18
) and (
4.17
), it follows that

(5.7)

|A^{\alpha,\nu}_{j,n}|=\bigg|\frac{\Phi_{\alpha}^{(2j)}(0)F_{j,\nu}^{(2n)}(0)}{(2j)!\,(2n)!}\bigg|\leq d_{\alpha}C_{K}2^{4n}.

Inserting the estimates (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) in (
4.19
) proves that

(5.8)

|\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,\nu}_{n}(x)|\leq e_{\alpha,K}2^{6n},

where
e_{\alpha,K}
is a constant depending only on
\alpha
and the
\nu
-compact
K
. Now, the power series expansion of
(j_{m,\nu}^{2}-z^{2})^{-1}
and the definition (
4.16
) lead to

(5.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{j_{m,\nu}^{2\nu-\alpha}J_{\alpha}(xj_{m,\nu})}{(j_{m,\nu}^{2}-z^{2})J_{\nu+1}(j_{m,\nu})^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\alpha,\nu}(x)z^{2n}.

To be precise: the estimate (
5.8
) shows that
if
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<\operatorname{Re}\alpha+1/2
,
\alpha-\nu+1\neq 0,-1,-2,\dots
,
\nu\notin\mathbb{Z}
,
0<x\leq 2
, and
|z|<1/2^{3}
, the identity (
5.9
) holds and the right hand side is an analytic function of
\nu
. Since we have already proved that the identity (
5.5
) for
-1<\operatorname{Re}\nu<1/2
and its right hand side is also an analytic function of
\nu
, we deduce that the identity (
5.5
) holds indeed for
2\operatorname{Re}\nu<\operatorname{Re}\alpha+1/2
,
\alpha-\nu+1\neq 0,-1,-2,\dots
,
\nu\notin\mathbb{Z}
,
0<x\leq 2
.

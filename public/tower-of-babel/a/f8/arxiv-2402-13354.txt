Artinian Gorenstein algebras of embedding dimension four and socle degree three over an arbitrary field. Thanks: We made extensive use of Macaulay2 [ 8 ] . We are very appreciative of this computer algebra system.
Sabine El Khoury and Andrew R. Kustin

Abstract
. Let 𝒌 {\boldsymbol{k}} be an arbitrary field, 𝔄 {A} be a standard graded Artinian Gorenstein 𝒌 {\boldsymbol{k}} -algebra of embedding dimension four and socle degree three, and π : P → 𝔄 \pi:P\to{A} be a surjective graded homomorphism from a polynomial ring with four variables over 𝒌 {\boldsymbol{k}} onto 𝔄 {A} . We give the minimal generators of the kernel of π \pi and the minimal homogeneous resolution of 𝔄 {A} by free P P -modules. We give formulas for the entries in the matrices in the resolution in terms of the coefficients of the Macaulay inverse system for 𝔄 {A} . We have implemented these formulas in Macaulay2 scripts. The ideal ker ⁡ π \ker\pi has either 6 6 , 7 7 , or 9 9 minimal generators. The number of minimal generators and the precise form of the minimal resolution are determined by the rank of a 3 × 3 3\times 3 symmetric matrix of constants that we call SM \operatorname{SM} . If ker ⁡ π \ker\pi requires more than six generators, then we prove that ker ⁡ π \ker\pi is the sum of two linked perfect ideals of grade three. If the ker ⁡ π \ker\pi is six-generated, then we prove that 𝔄 {A} is a hypersurface section of a codimension three Gorenstein algebra. Our approach is based on the structure of Gorenstein-linear resolutions and the theorem that, except for exactly one exception, 𝔄 {A} has the weak Lefschetz property.

Remark 3.3
.

Remark 14.1
.

Recall that if
P
is the polynomial ring
\boldsymbol{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]=\operatorname{Sym}_{\bullet}P_{1}
, then the graded dual of
P
is the Divided Power Algebra
D_{\bullet}P_{1}^{*}
. The monomials
\binom{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}{i}
form a basis for
\operatorname{Sym}_{i}P_{1}
(see Convention
2.5
) and the elements

\{x_{1}^{*(e_{1})}\cdots x_{n}^{*(e_{n})}\mid e_{1}+\dots+e_{n}=i\}

form a basis for
D_{i}P_{1}^{*}
. The structure of
D_{\bullet}P_{1}^{*}
as a
\operatorname{Sym}_{\bullet}P_{1}
- module is given by

(14.1.1)

x_{1}^{f_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{f_{n}}(x_{1}^{*(e_{1})}\cdots x_{n}^{*(e_{n})})=\begin{cases}x_{1}^{*(e_{1}-f_{1})}\cdots x_{n}^{*(e_{n}-f_{n})},&\text{if $f_{j}\leq e_{j}$ for all $j$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The notation that Macaulay2 uses for the element

x_{1}^{*(e_{1})}\cdots x_{n}^{*(e_{n})}

of
D_{\bullet}P_{1}^{*}
is
x_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{e_{n}}
.
Consequently, in this section, when we write “Let Phi be the element
yx^{2}+xzw+xw^{2}+z^{3}+w^{3}
of
D_{3}P_{1}^{*}
”, we are thinking, “Let Phi be the element
y^{*}x^{*(2)}+x^{*}z^{*}w^{*}+x^{*}w^{*(2)}+z^{*(3)}+w^{*(3)}
of
D_{3}P_{1}^{*}
”.

The Macaulay2 command contract(
x_{1}^{f_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{f_{n}},x_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{e_{n}})
produces (
14.1.1
).

Oriented singquandles and related algebraic structures
M. Bonatto

Abstract
. In this paper we consider the algebraic structures related to invariants of topological structures introduced respectively in [ CEKL22 ] and [ ADEM19 ] . Our main results is to show how all these structures are closely related to each other using the language of binary operations.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 20L05, 57K10

Introduction

Algebraic invariants are relevant tools in topology, in particular towards the study of knots and virtual knots. The study of invariants based on binary algebraic structures have been started by the seminal work of Joyce
[
Joy82
]
and Matveev
[
Mat82
]
that first introduced quandles as binary algebraic structures encoding the classical Reidemeister moves. The same approach has been adapted to virtual knots and the related Reidemeister moves and so
oriented singquandles
have been defined
[
CCE20
]
. These structures can be studied in a purely algebraic way and an axiomatization of them (alternative to the original one) can be found in
[
BC21
]
.

Similarly, some algebraic structures have also been defined in the context of topological study of proteins
[
ADEM19
]
and RNA folding
[
CEKL22
]
in order to produce invariants able to distinguish topological objects.

In the present paper we offer new axiomatizations of all such structures, namely
stuquandles
and
oriented bondles
and we show that all of them can be reduced to the concept of oriented singquandle. Indeed stuquandles are just given by a pair of oriented singquandles structure over the same underlying set (see Corollary
1.6
) and oriented bondles are stuquandles with an additional compatibility condition between the quandle and one of the two oriented singquandles structures (see Theorem
1.9
).

Our approach is purely algebraic so we do not provide any detail on the underlying topology and we refer the reader to the above mentioned papers for all the missing information. Nonetheless, we wonder if a topological argument can show the same relation between the algebraic structures we deal with in the paper.

The paper is organized as follows: in the first section we recall the basics about binary algebraic structures and right quasigroups and quandles in particular. The second one is about Oriented singquandles, and we resume the main results of
[
BC21
]
. In the next two sections we deal with stuquandles and oriented bondles and we show that such structured can be build from oriented singquandles. In the last section we show that the family of 2-reductive quandles provide examples of oriented bondles.

1.
Algebraic structures

1.1.
Binary algebraic structures

A binary algebraic structure is a set
Q
endowed with a set of binary operations. Let
(Q,\cdot)
be a binary algebraic structure, we can define the left and right multiplications mappings with respect to
\cdot
as

\displaystyle L^{[\cdot]}_{x}:y\mapsto x\cdot y,\quad R_{x}^{[\cdot]}:y\longrightarrow y\cdot x

for every
x\in Q
(we often denote such mappings just as
L_{x}
and
R_{x}
). We can define the opposite of
\cdot
as
x\cdot^{op}y=y\cdot x
for every
x,y\in Q
.

Binary operations and binary functions are essentially the same thing. Indeed, given a binary algebraic structure
(Q,\cdot)
we can define the map
M[\cdot](x,y)=x\cdot y
. On the other hand, given a binary map
f:Q\times Q\longrightarrow Q
we can define the binary operation
x\cdot_{f}y=f(x,y)
on
Q
.

A bijective map
f
on
Q
is an automorphism of
(Q,\cdot)
if
f(x\cdot y)=f(x)\cdot f(y)
for every
x,y\in Q
. We denote the group of automorphism of
(Q,\cdot)
as
\mathrm{Aut}(Q,\cdot)
.

A
right quasigroup
is a binary algebraic structure
(Q,\cdot,/)
such that

(x\cdot y)/y\approx x\approx(x/y)\cdot y

hold. The right multiplication mappings of
(Q,\cdot)
are bijective and in particular
x/y=R_{y}^{-1}(x)
for every
x,y\in Q
. Given
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we define
(Q,\cdot^{n},/^{n})
where

x\cdot^{n}y=R_{y}^{n}(x),\quad x/^{n}y=R_{y}^{-n}(x).

Note that
(Q,\cdot^{n},/^{n})
is also a right quasigroup.

We say that
(Q,\cdot,/)
is:

(i)

idempotent
if
x\cdot x\approx x
;

(ii)

projection
, if
x\cdot y\approx x
;

(iii)

involutory
if
(x\cdot y)\cdot y\approx x
(namely
\cdot=/
);

(iv)

a
rack
if
(x\cdot y)\cdot z\approx(x\cdot z)\cdot(y\cdot z)
.

Idempotent racks are called
quandles
. In the paper we deal with sets with several binary operations as
(Q,\cdot,*,/)
and usually
(Q,*,/)
is a quandle. We denote by
\rho_{x}
the right multiplication with respect to
*
for every
x\in Q
.

1.2.
Oriented singquandles

In
[
CCE20
]
an algebraic structure related to invariants of singular knots was introduced. An
oriented singquandle
is a tuple
(Q,*,/,R_{1},R_{2})
where
(Q,*,/)
is a (right) quandle and
R_{1},R_{2}:Q\times Q\longrightarrow Q
such that the following axioms hold:

(OS1)

\displaystyle R_{1}(x,y)*z

\displaystyle=R_{1}(x*z,y*z)

(OS2)

\displaystyle R_{2}(x,y)*z

\displaystyle=R_{2}(x*z,y*z)

(OS3)

\displaystyle(y*x)*z

\displaystyle=(y*R_{1}(x,z))*R_{2}(x,z)

(OS4)

\displaystyle R_{1}(x,y)*R_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=R_{2}(y,x*y)

(OS5)

\displaystyle R_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=R_{1}(y,x*y)

Setting
y\cdot x=R_{1}(x,y)
, and taking (
OS5
) as the definition of
R_{2}
by
\cdot
and
*
as
R_{2}(x,y)=(x*y)\cdot y
, we can rewrite the definition as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

An oriented singquandle is a binary algebraic structure
(Q,\cdot,*,/)
where
(Q,*,/)
is a (right) quandle and
such that the following identities hold:

(OS1’)

\displaystyle(y\cdot x)*z

\displaystyle\approx(y*z)\cdot(x*z)

(OS2’)

\displaystyle(x\cdot(y\cdot x))*z

\displaystyle\approx(x*z)\cdot((y*z)\cdot(x*z))

(OS3’)

\displaystyle(y*x)*z

\displaystyle\approx(y*(z\cdot x))*((x*z)\cdot z))

(OS4’)

\displaystyle(y\cdot x)*((x*y)\cdot y)

\displaystyle\approx(y*(x*y))\cdot(x*y)

Clearly, if
(Q,*,/,R_{1},R_{2})
is an oriented singquandle in the sense of
[
CCE20
]
, then
(Q,(\cdot_{R_{1}})^{op},*,/)
is an oriented sinquandle in the sense of Definition
1.1
. On the other hand, if
(Q,\cdot,*,/)
satisfies the axioms in Definition
1.1
, then
(Q,*,/,M[{\cdot^{op}}],T[\cdot])
where
T[\cdot](x,y)=(x\cdot y)*y
is an oriented singquandle in the sense of . Such assignements provide a one-to-one correspondence between the two type of structures. Thus, we stick to
1.1
as the definition of oriented singquandles.

We already showed in
[
BC21
]
that the axioms above can be simplified as follows.

Proposition 1.2
.

[
BC21
, Proposition 3.2]

Let
(Q,\cdot,*,/)
be a binary algebraic structure. The following are equivalent:

(i)

(Q,\cdot,*,/)
is an oriented singquandle.

(ii)

(Q,*,/)
is a quandle and the following identities hold:

(1)

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)*z

\displaystyle\approx(x*z)\cdot(y*z),

(2)

\displaystyle(z*y)*x

\displaystyle\approx(z*(x\cdot y))*((y*x)\cdot x).

Note that if
(Q,*,/)
is a projection quandle,
(Q,\cdot,*,/)
is an oriented singquandle for every binary operation
\cdot
. On the other hand if
(Q,\cdot)
is a projection quandle, then
(Q,\cdot,*,/)
is an oriented singquandle for every quandle
(Q,*,/)
.

Example 1.3
.

Let
(Q,*,/)
be a quandle. Then
(Q,*,*,/)
is an oriented singquandle if and only if

\displaystyle\rho_{x}\rho_{y}=\rho_{(y*x)*x}\rho_{x*y}

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}^{2}\rho_{y}\rho_{x}^{-2}\rho_{y}\rho_{x}\rho_{y}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}\rho_{y*x}\rho_{y/x}\rho_{y}^{-1}

i.e.
\rho_{y}^{2}=\rho_{y*x}\rho_{y/x}
. Note that if
(Q,*,*,/)
is an oriented singquandle if and only if
(Q,/,/,*)
is an oriented sinquandle. For instance if
(Q,*,/)
is involutory then
(Q,*,*,/)
is an oriented singquandle.

1.3.
Stuquandles

In
[
CEKL22
]
a new algebraic structure related to stuck knots have been defined. Let us recall the definition: let
(Q,*,/,R_{1},R_{2})
be an oriented singquandle and let
R_{3},R_{4}:Q\times Q\longrightarrow Q
. If the following axioms hold for all
x,y,z\in Q

(ST1)

\displaystyle R_{3}(y,x)*R_{4}(y,x)

\displaystyle=R_{4}(x*y,y),

(ST2)

\displaystyle R_{4}(y,x)

\displaystyle=R_{3}(x*y,y),

(ST3)

\displaystyle R_{3}(y*x,z)

\displaystyle=R_{3}(y,z/x)*x,

(ST4)

\displaystyle R_{4}(y,z/x)

\displaystyle=R_{4}(y*x,z)/x,

(ST5)

\displaystyle(x*R_{4}(y,z))/y

\displaystyle=(x/R_{3}(y,z))*z.

we say that
(Q,*,R_{1},R_{2},R_{3},R_{4})
is a stuquandle.

Similarly to what we did for Oriented singquandles we are going to translate the definition in terms of binary operations.

If we denote by
R_{3}(x,y)=x\circ y
, then (
ST2
) shows that
R_{4}
is defined in terms of
*
and
\circ
as
R_{4}(x,y)=(y*x)\circ x
for every
x,y\in Q
. Moreover, (
ST3
) is just saying that
\rho_{x}\in\mathrm{Aut}(Q,\circ)
for every
x\in Q
. The identity (
ST4
) follows since
\rho_{x}\in\mathrm{Aut}(Q,*)
and
R_{4}
is defined in terms of
*
and
\circ
. Hence we can rewrite the definition above as follows.

Definition 1.4
.

A stuquandle is a binary algebraic structure
(Q,\cdot,\circ,*,/)
such that
(Q,\cdot,*,/)
is an oriented sinquandles and the following identities hold:

(ST1’)

\displaystyle(y\circ x)*((x*y)\circ y)

\displaystyle\approx(y*(x*y))\circ(x*y),

(ST3’)

\displaystyle(x\circ y)*z

\displaystyle\approx(x*z)\circ(y*z),

(ST5’)

\displaystyle(x*((z*y)\circ y)))/y

\displaystyle\approx(x/(y\circ z))*z.

The next theorem shows that equations (
ST1’
), (
ST3’
) and (
ST5’
) provide an equivalent axiomatizations of oriented singquandles.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(Q,*,/)
be a quandle. The following are equivalent:

(i)

the binary algebraic structure
(Q,\circ,*,/)
satisfies (
ST1’
), (
ST3’
) and (
ST5’
).

(ii)

(Q,\circ,*,/)
is an oriented singquandle (in the sense of Definition
1.1
).

Proof.

Note that (
ST5’
) is equivalent to have
\rho_{y}^{-1}\rho_{(z*y)\circ y}=\rho_{z}\rho_{y\circ z}^{-1}
, i.e.
\rho_{y}\rho_{z}=\rho_{(z*y)\circ y}\rho_{y\circ z}
. Namely (
ST5’
) can be rewritten as

(ST5”)

\displaystyle(x*z)*y=(x*(y\circ z))*((z*y)\circ y).

Note that (
ST1’
) is the very same identity as (
OS4’
), and so according to Proposition (
1.2
) it follows by (
ST3’
) and (
ST5”
).
∎

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
(Q,\cdot,\circ,*,/)
be a binary algebraic structure. The following are equivalent:

(i)

(Q,\cdot,\circ,*,/)
is a stuquandle.

(ii)

(Q,\cdot,*,/)
and
(Q,\circ,*,/)
are oriented singquandles.

Example 1.7
.

Let
(Q,\cdot,*,/)
be an oriented singquandle. Then
(Q,\cdot,\cdot,*,/)
is a stuquandle.

1.4.
Oriented Bondles

Let us introduce the algebraic structure defined in
[
ADEM19
]
in connection with the topological study of the structure of proteins. Let
(Q,*,/,R_{1},R_{2})
be an oriented singquandle and let
R_{3}:Q\times Q\longrightarrow Q
. If the following axioms hold for all
x,y,z\in Q

(OB1)

\displaystyle R_{3}(y*z,x*z)

\displaystyle=R_{3}(y,x)*z

(OB2)

\displaystyle R_{3}(x/z,y/z)

\displaystyle=R_{3}(x,y)/z

(OB3)

\displaystyle(z/R_{3}(x,y))*x

\displaystyle=(z/y)*R_{3}(y,x)

(OB4)

\displaystyle R_{3}(x,y)/y

\displaystyle=R_{3}(x/R_{3}(y,x),y).

we say that
(Q,*,R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})
is an oriented bondle.

Let us switch to binary operations by setting
R_{3}(x,y)=y\bullet x
. The identities (
OB1
) and (
OB2
) are both equivalent to have that
\rho_{x}\in\mathrm{Aut}(Q,\bullet)
for every
x\in Q
, thus we can keep just (
OB1
).

Definition 1.8
.

An oriented bondle is a binary algebraic structure
(Q,\cdot,\bullet,*,/)
such that
(Q,\cdot,*,/)
is an oriented singquandle and the following identities hold:

(OB1’)

\displaystyle(x\bullet y)*z

\displaystyle\approx(x*z)\bullet(y*z),

(OB3’)

\displaystyle(z/(y\bullet x))*x

\displaystyle\approx(z/y)*(x\bullet y),

(OB4’)

\displaystyle(y\bullet x)/y

\displaystyle\approx y\bullet((x/(x\bullet y)).

The following theorem shows that also oriented bondles are constructed by a pair of oriented singquandles with an additional compatibility condition.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
(Q,\circ,\bullet,*)
be a binary algebraic structure. The following are equivalent:

(i)

(Q,\circ,\bullet,*,/)
is an oriented bondle.

(ii)

(Q,\circ,*,/)
and
(Q,\bullet,*,/)
are oriented singquandles and

(3)

\displaystyle(y*x)\bullet x\approx(y*(x\bullet y))\bullet x

holds.

Proof.

The identity (
OB3’
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\rho_{x\bullet y}\rho_{y}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}\rho_{y\bullet x}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}\rho_{y\bullet x}^{-1}\rho_{x}^{-1}\rho_{x}=\rho_{(y\bullet x)*x}^{-1}\rho_{x}

i.e.

\displaystyle\rho_{x}\rho_{y}(z)

\displaystyle=(z*y)*x

\displaystyle\rho_{(y\bullet x)*x}\rho_{x\bullet y}(z)

\displaystyle=(z*(x\bullet y))*((y\bullet x)*x)

\displaystyle=(z*(x\bullet y))*((y*x)\bullet(x*x))

\displaystyle=(z*(x\bullet y))*((y*x)\bullet x)

Therefore
(Q,\bullet,*,/)
is an oriented singquandle.

Let us consider (
OB4’
) and write is as

\displaystyle y\bullet x

\displaystyle=(y\bullet(x/(x\bullet y))*y=y\bullet((x*y)/((x*y)\bullet y))

\displaystyle=((y*((x*y)\bullet y))\bullet(x*y))/((x*y)\bullet y)=\rho_{(x*y)\bullet y}^{-1}((y*((x*y)\bullet y))\bullet(x*y)).

Since
\rho_{(x*y)\bullet y}^{-1}=(\rho_{y}\rho_{x}\rho_{y\bullet x}^{-1})^{-1}=\rho_{y\bullet x}\rho_{x}^{-1}\rho_{y}^{-1}
we have that

\displaystyle\rho_{x}\rho_{y\bullet x}^{-1}(y\bullet x)

\displaystyle=\rho_{x}(y\bullet x)=(y*x)\bullet x=\rho_{y}^{-1}((y*((x*y)\bullet y))\bullet(x*y))=(y*(x\bullet y))\bullet x.

and so (
OB4’
) is equivalent to (
3
).

∎

The identity (
3
) does not hold for every oriented singquandles. Let
Q=\{1,2,3\}
and

(Q,\bullet)=\begin{tabular}[]{| c c c|}\hline\cr 2&1&3\\
2&2&2\\
1&3&2\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,,\quad(Q,*)=(Q,/)=\begin{tabular}[]{| c c c|}\hline\cr 1&3&1\\
2&2&2\\
3&1&3\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}\,,

Then
(Q,\bullet,*,/)
is a oriented singquandles that does not satisfies (
3
).

Example 1.10
.

Let
(Q,\circ,\bullet,*,/)
be an oriented bondle. If the right multiplication mappings with respect to
\bullet
are injective, then
y*x=y*(x\bullet y)
. If
\bullet=*
then
y*x=y*(x*y)=(y*x)*y
. Then
(Q,*,*,*,/)
is a bondle if and only if
\rho_{y}^{2}=\rho_{y*x}\rho_{y/x}
and
y*x\approx(y*x)*y
(or equivalently
y\approx y*(y/x)
) holds. If
(Q,*,/)
is involutory, i.e.
*=/
we have that
(Q,*,*,*,*)
is a bondle if and only if
y\approx y*(y*x)
holds.

2.
2-reductive quandles

A quandle is
2
-reductive
if the following identity hold:

x*(y*z)\approx x*(y*u).

In
[
JPSZD15
]
, a construction for such quandles was provided: let
\{A_{i}\,:\,i\in I\}
a family of abelian groups,
\{c_{i,j}\,:\,i,j\in I\}
such that
c_{i,j}\in A_{j}
for every
i,j\in I
and
A_{j}=\langle c_{i,j},\,i\in I\rangle
. We define two binary operations on the set
\mathcal{A}=\bigcup_{i\in I}A_{i}
as

x*y=x+c_{i,j},\quad x/y=x-c_{i,j}

whenever
x\in A_{j}
and
y\in A_{i}
. Then
(\mathcal{A},*,/)
is a quandle called
affine mesh
and we denote it by
((A_{i})_{i\in I},(c_{i,j})_{i,j\in I}))
. Affine meshes completely describe
2
-reductive quandles.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
JPSZD15
, Theorem 6.9]

[
PJD20
, Theorem 4.1]

Let
(Q,*,/)
be a quandle. The following are equivalent:

(i)

(Q,*,/)
is
2
-reductive.

(ii)

\mathrm{RMlt}(Q,*,/)
is abelian.

(iii)

(Q,*,/)
is isomorphic to an affine mesh
((A_{i})_{i\in I},(c_{i,j})_{i,j\in I}))
.

In particular, note that if
\mathrm{RMlt}(Q,*,/)
is abelian, then
\mathrm{RMlt}(Q,*^{n},/^{n})
is also abelian for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let us conclude the section showing that
2
-reductive quandles can be used to construct examples of oriented singquandles and oriented bondles.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(Q,*,/)
be a
2
-reductive quandle. Then
(Q,*^{n},*^{m},/^{m})
is an oriented singquandle for every
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Clearly
\rho_{x}\in\mathrm{Aut}(Q,*^{n},/^{n})
, i.e. (
1
) holds. Moreover
\rho_{x}=\rho_{h(x)}
for every
x\in Q
and every
h\in\mathrm{RMlt}(Q)
, and so we have:

(z*^{m}x)*^{m}y=(z*^{m}(x*^{n}y))*^{m}((y*^{m}x)*^{n}x),

i.e. also (
2
) holds.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(Q,*,/)
be a
2
-reductive quandle. Then
(Q,*^{n},*^{m},*,/)
is a bondle for every
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

According to Lemma
2.2
,
(Q,*^{n},*,/)
and
(Q,*^{m},*,/)
are oriented singquandles. Moreover since
\rho_{x*^{m}y}=\rho_{x}
we have that
y*x=y*(x*^{m}y)
.
∎

On the p p -adic values of the weight two Eisenstein series for supersingular primes
P. Guerzhoy

Abstract
. The weight two Eisenstein series may be considered as the first example of a Katz p p -adic modular form. Classically, its values are defined for the primes of ordinary reduction. We investigate a modified definition which applies uniformly to all primes of good reduction, both ordinary and supersingular. We show that, in the case of complex multiplication, these p p -adic values coincide with the algebraic value of this Eisenstein series.

Remark 1

Let us indicate some directions to generalize definition (

3.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall briefly the definition of and some basic facts about
{\mathbb{D}}(F)\simeq H^{1}_{\textup{cris}}(F)
for a one-dimensional formal group
F
. We refer to
[
1
, Section 2]
for a detailed survey. All formal group laws under consideration in this paper are one-dimensional, and we suppress this qualifier. We then investigate the behavior of a certain basis of
{\mathbb{D}}(F)
under a formal group law isomorphism (Proposition
2
). In conclusion, we make a simple but useful remark related to the action of the Artin symbol.

Let
p
be a rational prime.
Throughout, we assume that
H
is a finite normal extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and that

is not ramified in

. As in the Introduction, we denote by

the ring of integers, by

the valuation ring, by
\hat{{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
its completion, and by
\hat{H_{\mathfrak{p}}}
the field of fractions of
\hat{{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. Then
\hat{H_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is a Galois extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, with a cyclic Galois group
\Gal(\hat{H_{\mathfrak{p}}}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
generated by the Frobenius automorphism. The image of this Galois group under the inclusion

\Gal\left(\hat{H_{\mathfrak{p}}}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\right)\hookrightarrow\Gal(H/{\mathbb{Q}})

is the decomposition group
D_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset\Gal(H/{\mathbb{Q}})
generated by the image of Frobenius which is the Artin symbol, and we will denote by

the action of Frobenius on

with

b^{\phi}=\left(\frac{H/{\mathbb{Q}}}{{\mathfrak{p}}}\right)(b)\hskip 11.38109pt\textup{whenever $b\in H\subset\hat{H_{\mathfrak{p}}}$.}

For a formal power series
\xi\in\hat{H_{\mathfrak{p}}}((u))
, we define the action coefficient-wise, namely for
\xi=\sum_{n}a(n)u^{n}
, we write

\xi^{\phi}:=\sum_{n}a(n)^{\phi}u^{n}.

The embeddings
{\mathcal{O}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hookrightarrow\hat{{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
allow us to consider
a formal group law

defined over

or

as a formal group law over

. Such a formal group law is determined by its logarithm which we write as

For such a formal group law, we define following
[
12
]
,

is the usual formal derivative of the power series

,
and

The map

We stick to

to the formal group associated with its

over

We adopt the definition of modularity from

: we say that an elliptic curve

is a normal extension of

taking the normal closure if necessary. A composition with translation allows us to always assume that the cusp at infinity maps to the neutral element of

Of course,

is modular (and so is any rational elliptic curve), while there are modular elliptic curves besides those which are rational. We now introduce notations in order to state a weaker (but much simpler and more general) version of Honda’s theorem for modular elliptic curves.

Denote by

For a modular (over

\psi^{*}\omega=g(q)\frac{dq}{q}

We now assume that the map

Change equation (

Theorem 2
.

In the above notations, let

be the formal group law over

The formal group law

Remark 2

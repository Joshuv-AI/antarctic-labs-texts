On extensions of the Jacobson–Morozov theorem to even characteristic
David I. Stewart, Adam R. Thomas

Abstract
. Let G G be a simple algebraic group over an algebraically closed field 𝕜 \Bbbk of characteristic 2 2 . We consider analogues of the Jacobson–Morozov theorem in this setting. More precisely, we classify those nilpotent elements with a simple 3 3 -dimensional Lie overalgebra in 𝔤 := Lie ⁡ ( G ) \mathfrak{g}:=\Lie(G) and also those with overalgebras isomorphic to the algebras Lie ⁡ ( SL 2 ) \Lie(\mathrm{SL}_{2}) and Lie ⁡ ( PGL 2 ) \Lie(\mathrm{PGL}_{2}) . This leads us to calculate the dimension of Lie automiser 𝔫 𝔤 ​ ( 𝕜 ⋅ e ) / 𝔠 𝔤 ​ ( e ) \mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Bbbk\cdot e)/\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{g}}(e) for all nilpotent orbits; in even characteristic this quantity is very sensitive to isogeny.

1.
Introduction

The Jacobson–Morozov theorem states that every nilpotent element
e
in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of a semisimple complex algebraic group
G
can be extended to an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(e,h,f)\in\mathfrak{g}^{3}
satisfying the famous commutator relations

[h,e]=2e,\ [h,f]=-2f,\ \text{and }[e,f]=h.

In recent work
[
ST18
]
building on previous efforts by Pommerening, Premet and Kostant—among others—the authors gave an exhaustive account of when the statement of the theorem holds for a reductive algebraic group
G
over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
p\geq 3
, with sharp statements about uniqueness of the embedding up to conjugacy. It turned out that
all
nilpotent elements can be embedded into
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples with essentially one exception: in
G_{2}
when
p=3
, a nilpotent element in the exceptional orbit (usually labelled
\tilde{A}_{1}^{(3)}
) lives in no subalgebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
.

As can be seen from the commutator relations above, there is some awkwardness in finding the right analogue of the statement of the Jacobson–Morozov theorem in characteristic
2
and there seem to be three reasonable approaches. Firstly, one may take the straight modulo
2
reduction of the relations (
\star
) to get the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}=\Lie(\mathrm{SL}_{2})
, in which
h
is central, so that
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
is no longer simple, and indeed the reduced elements
(\bar{e},\bar{h},\bar{f})
generate a (nilpotent) Heiseinberg algebra. Taking instead a minimal lattice with
[h,e]=e
,
[h,f]=f
and
[e,f]=2h
, then the reduction modulo
2
gets generators and relations for the ‘dual’ Lie algebra
\mathfrak{pgl}_{2}=\Lie(\mathrm{PGL}_{2})
, which is evidently still not simple: it has a
2
-dimensional abelian
p
-nilpotent ideal
\langle e,f\rangle
on which
\ad(h)
acts as the identity. However, setting
[h,e]=e
,
[h,f]=f
and
[e,f]=h
we do indeed get a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
of dimension
3
, which is unique up to isomorphism.

one sets

Theorems
4.1
,
5.1
and
6.1
give a complete list of those nilpotent orbits

such that

\mathfrak{s}

\mathfrak{pgl}_{2}
and

respectively. When

or

\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Bbbk\cdot e)
is stricly larger than the centraliser
\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Bbbk\cdot e)

4.1
and
5.1
.

\Bbbk\cdot e
is always
1

lies in an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-subalgebra; or

and

where one can do a direct check. Hence the calculation of the automiser is only interesting when

2.2.
Nilpotent orbits

Since char
\Bbbk>0
, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
comes with a
[p]
map,
x\mapsto x^{[p]}
, which is
p
-semilinear in
\Bbbk
and satisfies
\ad(x^{[p]})=(\ad(x))^{p}
.
An element
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is
nilpotent
if
x^{[p]^{r}}=0
for some
r\geq 0
; it is
semisimple
if
x
is in the
\Bbbk
-span of
x^{[p]},\dots,x^{[p]^{r}}
for some
r\geq 0
;
it is
toral
if
x^{[p]}=x
; and a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
with a basis of toral elements is called a
torus
. Since
\mathfrak{g}=\Lie(G)
for
G
simple, its Cartan subalgebras are maximal tori. The properties above are invariant under conjugacy by
g\in G(k)
. In particular, an orbit
\mathcal{O}_{e}=G(k)\cdot e
of elements of
\mathfrak{g}
is called a nilpotent orbit if it consists of nilpotent elements. The union of all nilpotent orbits of
\mathfrak{g}
is denoted
\mathcal{N}:=\mathcal{N}(\mathfrak{g})
and called the
nilpotent cone
.

For the general theory of nilpotent orbits and algebro-geometric properties of
\mathcal{N}
we refer the reader to
[
Jan04
]
. For complete data of the classification of the nilpotent orbits we recommend
[
LS04
]
, but we give a digest of the salient points below.

2.3.

2.5.

Given an embedding

\varphi:\mathrm{O}_{2n+1}\to\mathrm{O}_{2n+2}

Let

be a non-degenerate codimension-

on which a non-singular quadratic form has been fixed. Then there is a natural embedding

[

, Section 5.6]
to describe the nilpotent cone of

\mathcal{S}(e)=((k_{i})_{i=1}^{r},(l_{i})_{i=1}^{s},(m_{i})_{i=1}^{s},m)
and suppose

. Define
m_{s+1}=m
. Then
\mathcal{S}(e^{\prime})=((k_{i})_{i=1}^{r},(l_{i})_{i=1}^{s},(m_{i})_{i=1}^{s+1})
.

\varphi:\mathrm{O}_{2m}\to\mathrm{Sp}_{2m}
.

A non-degenerate quadratic form on

preserved by

gives rise to a bilinear form on

is characterised by admitting a representative

In

we learn that

with

Lemma 4.2

The nilpotent cone

\{\lambda E+\mu e+\nu h+\rho f+\sigma F\mid\lambda\rho=\mu\nu,\mu\sigma=\nu\rho\}.

Proof.

If

is represented by the matrix

\begin{bmatrix}\nu&\mu&\lambda\\
\rho&0&\mu\\
\sigma&\rho&\nu\end{bmatrix},

with characteristic polynomial
\chi_{N}(t)=t^{3}+(\nu^{2}+\lambda\sigma)t+\lambda\rho^{2}+\sigma\mu^{2}

Marcin Łyczak

Abstract

This logic is a modern realization of R. Carnap’s old ideas on modality, as logical necessity (J. Symb. Logic, 1946) [ 1 ] . We place it within the modern framework of quantified modal logic with variable domains. We prove the completeness theorem using a property of a kind of normal form, and the fact that in the canonical frame, the relation on all maximal consistent sets, R = { ⟨ Γ , Δ ⟩ : ∀ A ( □ ⊢ A ∈ Γ ⟹ A ∈ Δ ) } R=\{\langle\Gamma,\Delta\rangle\colon\forall A(\mathbin{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\Box$}$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.18753pt\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.43057pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.43057pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\mbox{\tiny{$\boldsymbol{\vdash}$}}$}$}}\hskip-2.18753pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}A\in\Gamma\Longrightarrow A\in\Delta)\} , is a universal relation (both are unprovable in known quantified modal logics). 1
Introduction

In 1946, R. Carnap formulated the
modal functional calculus
(
\mathsf{MFC}
), which is a version of quantified modal logic with an operator intended to express
logical necessity

[
1
]
. Carnap’s logic is not a typical modal quantified logic. It does not contain free variables, is stronger than
\mathsf{S5}
(formulas like
\neg\Box\exists xFx
are provable), and identity holds only between the same variables or constants, although identical objects are necessarily identical. In short,
\mathsf{MFC}
is far from contemporary first-order modal logic. Its models were built from sets of atomic formulas and their negations. There is no valuation and interpretation of predicates. Differences and similarities to Kripke’s semantics can be found in
[
4
]
. Recently, two important results on Carnap’s logic have been published. M. Cresswell proved the completeness of a variant of Carnap’s axiomatization, keeping Carnap’s original semantics almost untouched
[
2
]
. M. Madows formulated modern non-modal semantics for Carnap’s logic, together with a corresponding tableau system
[
5
]
. Madows interpreted logical necessity in a first-order structures as a quantification over all (parametrically definable) subsets of the domain of the structure. We believe that his approach can be considered a faithful modern representation of Carnap’s approach to logical necessity. Carnap’s necessity, as well as the necessities from the two cited works, actually does not express being a first-order thesis. For example, formulas like
\Box A\to\forall xA
,
\neg\Box\exists xy(x\not=y)
, and
\neg\Box\exists xy(x=y)
should be provable if we were to think of
\Box
as a logical necessity.

As far as we know, there is no formalized version of Carnap’s ideas in the literature where logical necessity explicitly expresses being a first-order thesis. We aim to build a first-order modal logic with an operator expressing first-order theses and interpret it in a variant of variable domain semantics. For simplicity, we do not introduce function symbols and constants. To show that our modality
\mathbin{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\Box$}$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.18753pt\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.43057pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.43057pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\mbox{\tiny{$\boldsymbol{\vdash}$}}$}$}}\hskip-2.18753pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}
is factually correct, we prove that the satisfiability of any formula
\mathbin{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\Box$}$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.18753pt\raisebox{1.72218pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.43057pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.43057pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\mbox{\tiny{$\boldsymbol{\vdash}$}}$}$}}\hskip-2.18753pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}A
in any model implies its logical validity and being a thesis, as we prove the completeness theorem.

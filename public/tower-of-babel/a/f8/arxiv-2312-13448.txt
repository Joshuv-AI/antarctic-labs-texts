Implied CO 2 {}_{\textbf{2}} -Price and Interest Rate of Carbon
Christian Fries

Abstract
By its nature, the so-called social cost of carbon (SCC(t)) will likely not cover the cost induced by climate change (damage cost and abatement cost) if it is used as a CO 2 -price. It is a marginal price only. We define an implied CO 2 -price that covers the climate change-induced costs. The price can be interpreted as a polluter pays principle . A numerical analysis using a classical DICE model reveals that the cost-implied CO 2 price is around 500 $/tCO 2 , while the corresponding price associated with the SCC is about 50 $/tCO 2 . In addition, we define the internal rate of return of carbon abatement and calculate it for the classical DICE model. This rate is much higher than the model’s discount rate, which may suggest the advantage of financing abatement by loans.

2.3
Separating Costs from Past Emissions

The price
K_{\mathrm{par}}

K_{\mathrm{par}}^{*}\ :=\ \frac{\int_{0}^{T}\frac{C(t)-C_{\mathrm{D}}^{*}(t)}{N(t)}\ \mathrm{d}t}{\int_{0}^{T}E(t)\ \mathrm{d}t}\text{.}

(3)

2.4
Social Cost of Carbon

The Social Cost of Carbon is defined as

SCC(t):=\frac{\partial V(0)}{\partial E(t)}\big/\frac{\partial V(0)}{\partial Z(t)}\text{,}

where
V(0)
is the total discounted welfare,
E(t)
is the time
t
emissions measured in tCO
{}_{\text{2}}
and
Z(t)
is the time
t
consumption measured in Dollar.

The SCC expresses the change in the welfare due to a change of the time
t
emission per change of time
t
consumption (GDP), resulting in the equivalent welfare change. The result of this definition is a time-
t
value; that is, the SCC is
undiscounted
.

In the original work on the dice model one sometimes finds
\frac{\partial V(0)}{\partial E(t)}\big/\frac{\partial V(0)}{\partial Z(t)}\equiv\frac{\partial Z(t)}{\partial E(t)}
, but this expression is mathematically not correct (it helps however to get an intuitive understanding for the SCC).

If
SCC(t)
is used as a CO
{}_{\text{2}}
price, then the total value paid for CO
{}_{\text{2}}
emissions is

V_{\mathrm{SCC}}(0)\ :=\ \int_{0}^{T}\left(SCC(t)\cdot E(t)\right)\frac{N(0)}{N(t)}\ \mathrm{d}t\text{.}

For this, we can calculate an equivalent swap rate
K_{\mathrm{SCC}}
such that

\int_{0}^{T}\left(SCC(t)\cdot E(t)-K_{\mathrm{SCC}}\cdot N(t)\cdot E(t)\right)\frac{N(0)}{N(t)}\ \mathrm{d}t\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\ 0\text{.}

(4)

The solution of (
4
) is given by
K=K_{\mathrm{SCC}}
with

K_{\mathrm{SCC}}\ =\ \frac{\int_{0}^{T}\left(SCC(t)\cdot E(t)\right)\frac{1}{N(t)}\ \mathrm{d}t}{\int_{0}^{T}\ E(t)\ \mathrm{d}t}\text{.}

(5)

Here
K_{\mathrm{SCC}}
represents a fair constant price for all times, while the social cost of carbon varies over time. Figure
3
shows the (discounted) difference of the two
SCC(t)/N(t)-K_{\mathrm{SCC}}
.

2.6
Discussion

The price
K_{\mathrm{par}}
in the financial product in (
1

2
emissions. It can be understood as a

The price

SCC(t)

That
SCC(0)
is lower than
K_{\mathrm{SCC}}
implies that the present generation is paying less (compared to its contribution measured in terms of emissions). The difference

K_{\mathrm{SCC}}\ -\ \frac{SCC(t)}{N(t)}

can be interpreted as a measure of inter-generational inequality. For a discussion on inter-generational inequality, see
[

.

That
K_{\mathrm{SCC}}<K_{\mathrm{par}}
implies that taking the SCC as a CO

3
An Implied Interest Rate of Carbon

3.1
Definition

In the following, we consider an alternative to the social cost of carbon. Let
\mu
denote the calibrated abatement policy,
C_{\mathrm{A}}(t)
the time-
t
abatement cost and
C_{\mathrm{D}}(t)
the time-
t
damage cost.
A local change
\mathrm{d}\mu(t)
in the abatement factor
\mu(t)
generates a change in the emissions, a change to the abatement cost in
t
,
\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{\mathrm{A}}(t)}{\mathrm{d}\mu(t)}
and a change in the future damage cost in
s\geq t
,
\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{\mathrm{D}}(s)}{\mathrm{d}\mu(t)}
.

If an increase in the emission in
t
is linked to a reduction in the abatement level, it saves abatement cost but is repaid with damage cost. This can be considered a loan: increasing emissions, we borrow abatement cost and pay back by damage cost.

For this loan, we may calculate an
internal rate of return

r^{\mathrm{SCC}}(t)
as the solution of the equation

\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{\mathrm{A}}(t)}{\mathrm{d}\mu(t)}+\sum_{t_{k}\geq t}\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{\mathrm{D}}(t_{k})}{\mathrm{d}\mu(t)}\exp(-r^{\mathrm{SCC}}(t)\cdot(t_{k}-t))\ =\ 0\text{.}

We may then compare the rate
r^{\mathrm{SCC}}(t)
with the model’s interest rate
r
. If
r^{\mathrm{SCC}}(t)>r
(and
r
is the rate to finance a loan), then funding abatement with a loan will be profitable for the society.

3.2
The Implied Interest Rate of Carbon in the Classical DICE Model

We calculate
t\mapsto r^{\mathrm{SCC}}(t)
in a classical DICE model. Figure
4
shows the result for a model with a calibrated abatement policy. The internal rate of return of carbon is much higher than the model’s discount rate. For a DICE-2016 with the discount rate
r=1.5\%
, we find
r^{\mathrm{SCC}}(t)\approx 4\%
for the first ten years.

r^{\mathrm{SCC}}
for the DICE model from the model components. Differentiating the model’s objective function with respect to
\mu(t_{j})
we find from
\mathrm{d}V(0)/\mathrm{d}\mu(t_{j})\ =\ 0
that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\ =\ \int_{0}^{T}\frac{\mathrm{d}U(t)}{\mathrm{d}\mu(t_{j})}\frac{N(0)}{N(t)}\mathrm{d}t\ =\ \int_{t_{j}}^{T}\frac{\mathrm{d}U(t)}{\mathrm{d}\mu(t_{j})}\frac{N(0)}{N(t)}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ =\ \int_{t_{j}}^{T}\sum_{t_{k}\geq t_{j}}\frac{\mathrm{d}U(t)}{\mathrm{d}C(t_{k})}\frac{\mathrm{d}C(t_{k})}{\mathrm{d}\mu(t_{j})}\frac{N(0)}{N(t)}\mathrm{d}t\ =\ \sum_{t_{k}\geq t_{j}}\frac{\mathrm{d}C(t_{k})}{\mathrm{d}\mu(t_{j})}\left(\int_{t_{j}}^{T}\frac{\mathrm{d}U(t)}{\mathrm{d}C(t_{k})}\frac{N(0)}{N(t)}\mathrm{d}t\right)\text{.}

That is

\sum_{t_{k}\geq t_{j}}\frac{\mathrm{d}C(t_{k})}{\mathrm{d}\mu(t_{j})}\cdot\exp\left(-r^{\mathrm{SCC}}(t_{j})(t_{k}-t_{j})\right)\ =\ \sum_{t_{k}\geq t_{j}}\frac{\mathrm{d}C(t_{k})}{\mathrm{d}\mu(t_{j})}\cdot\left(\frac{R(t_{j},t_{k})}{R(t_{j},t_{j})}\right)\text{,}

(6)

with

R(t_{j},t_{k})\ =\ \int_{t_{j}}^{T}\frac{\mathrm{d}U(t)}{\mathrm{d}C(t_{k})}\frac{N(0)}{N(t)}\mathrm{d}t\text{.}

Here
U
denotes the model’s utility function. The rate
r^{\mathrm{SCC}}
is the sum of the discount rate and a weighted average of the utility rate.

3.3
Discussion

The rate
r^{\mathrm{SCC}}
can indicate if it is profitable to finance abatement by a loan. If borrowing at a lower rate
r<r^{\mathrm{SCC}}
is possible, then abatement should be financed by a loan,
[
2
]
.

For the classical DICE model, we found that the rate
r^{\mathrm{SCC}}
is much higher than the discount rate. This finding does not contradict the fact that the model is in its calibrated equilibrium. It is explained by the objective function, balancing discounted marginal utility changes, but not cost, see
[
3
]

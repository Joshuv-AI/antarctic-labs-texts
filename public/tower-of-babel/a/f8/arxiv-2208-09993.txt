On the Sombor index of graphs with given connectivity and number of bridges


Abstract
Recently in 2021, Gutman introduced the Sombor index of a graph, a novel degree-based topological index. It has been shown that the Sombor index efficiently models the thermodynamic properties of chemical compounds. Assume 𝔹 n k \mathbb{B}_{n}^{k} (resp. 𝕍 n k \mathbb{V}_{n}^{k} ) comprises all graphs with order n n having number of bridges (resp. vertex-connectivity) k k . Horoldagva & Xu (2021) characterized graphs achieving the maximum Sombor index of graphs in 𝔹 n k \mathbb{B}_{n}^{k} . This paper characterizes graphs achieving the minimum Sombor index in 𝔹 n k \mathbb{B}_{n}^{k} . Certain auxiliary operation on graphs in 𝔹 n k \mathbb{B}_{n}^{k} are introduced and employed for the characterization. Moreover, we characterize graphs achieving maximum Sombor index in 𝕍 n k \mathbb{V}_{n}^{k} . Some open problems, which naturally arise from this work, have been proposed at the end. 2020 MCS: 05C35, 05C09, 05C92. Keywords: Graph; Sombor index; Bridges; Vertex-connectivity.

Introduction

Dependence of physicochemical properties of a compound on its molecular structure
is a cornerstone idea in contemporary chemistry. Information regarding this structural
dependence is frequently retrieved by employing various advance mathematical methods
such as graph-theoretic topological descriptors. These numerical invariants have diverse
amount of potential applications in chemistry, pharmacy, material science, among others,

[
19

38

. Degree-based topological invariants
[
15

are structured based on degrees
of vertices in a chemical graphs. Their mathematical and computational simplicity make
them one of most successful class of graphical indices
[

Lately, a novel degree-based index known as the Sombor index is put forwarded by
Gutman in 2021. The defining structure of the Sombor index is:

SO(\Gamma)=\sum_{uv\in E(\Gamma)}\sqrt{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(u)^{2}+\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(v)^{2}},

in which
\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(x)
refers to the valency/degree of
x\in V(\Gamma)
.

Immediately after its inception, the chemical applicability of the Sombor index has been studied.
Redžepović
[
34

introduced the Sombor index in quantitative structure property
relationship (QSPR) modeling of entropy and heat of vaporization of alkanes. Experimental results
of the study asserted that the Sombor index possesses a strong correlation potential with these physicochemical
properties. Moreover, the Sombor index has been proven to have potential in simulating thermodynamic
properties of hydrocarbons and it showed a significant potential in the simulation.
After its chemical applicability was reported, the Sombor index has been studied by

Preliminaries

Denoted by
\Gamma=(V,E)
, a graph comprises sets
V
of vertices and
E
of edges.
The number
n
(resp.
\epsilon
) is frequently used to denote the number of vertices (resp. edges)
i.e. order (resp. size) of
\Gamma
. We use
(n,\epsilon)-
graph to symbolize an
n
-vertex graph
comprising
\epsilon
number of edges. For any
y\in V(\Gamma)
, let
\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(y)
be the
number of vertices connected to
y
by an edge. A vertex- (resp. edge-) deleted subgraph
\Gamma-xy

(resp.
\Gamma-y
) is constructed by removing
xy\in E(\Gamma)
(resp.
y\in V(\Gamma)
and incident edges) from
\Gamma
.
We denote the
n
-vertex cycle (resp. path) by
C_{n}
(resp.
P_{n}
).

The number of edges traversed by a path is said to be its length. For a pair
x,y\in V(\Gamma)
,
the distance
d(x,y)
is a shortest path’s length between them.
The diameter
D(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma
i.e.
D(\Gamma)=\max\{d(x,y):x,y\in V(\Gamma)\}
is the largest distance in
\Gamma
.
An
x-y
geodesic is a path having length
d(x,y)
.
A pair of vertices
x,y\in V(\Gamma)
are said to be antipodal if
d(x,y)=D(\Gamma)
.
A vertex
x
in
\Gamma
in said to be cut-vertex of
\Gamma
if
\Gamma-x
is disconnected.
Similarly, an edge
xy
in
\Gamma
is called a bridge (or cut-edge), if
\Gamma-xy
has two components.
A edge cut (resp. vertex cut) of
\Gamma
is
T\subseteq E(\Gamma)
(resp.
S\subseteq V(\Gamma)
)
whose removal results in
\Gamma
to be disconnected. The minimum size
k
of a vertex-cut (resp. edge-cut)
is known as the vertex-connectivity (resp. edge-connectivity).

Next, we present some preliminary results which are employed in subsequent sections.
The following is a standard result in graph theory.

Theorem 1

[
43
]

An edge
e\in E(\Gamma)
in
\Gamma
is a bridge if and only if no cycle of
\Gamma
contains
e
.

Theorem
1
suggest the following corollary.

Corollary 1

A graph
\Gamma
of order
n
is a tree if and only if
\Gamma
has
n-1
bridges.

Since a
(n,\epsilon)
-graph is a tree if and only if
\epsilon=n-1
. Thus, we obtain:

Proposition 1

[
43
]

Assume
\Gamma
is an
(n,\epsilon)
-graph having at least one cycle. Then
\epsilon\geq n
.

The following result shows that antipodal vertices in a tree are the pendent vertices.

Proposition 2

[
43
]

Let
x,y\in V(\Gamma)
be two antipodal vertices in a tree
T
. Then,
\mathrm{deg}_{T}(x)=\mathrm{deg}_{T}(y)=1
.

The definition of the Sombor index of a graph suggests the following elementary observation.

Lemma 1

For an edge-deleted subgraph
\Gamma-xy
, where
xy\in E(\Gamma)
, we have
SO(\Gamma)>SO(\Gamma-xy)
.

Suppose, on contrary, that
\Gamma

Claim 1

All cycles in
\Gamma

Proof:

Assume
\Gamma

for

application of Lemma
5
implies that there exists
\Gamma^{\prime}

.
This contradicts the choice of
\Gamma
which, in turn, shows the claim.

By Claim
1
, we conclude that all cycles in
\Gamma
are edge-disjoint.
Next, by successive application of Lemma
3
, there exists
a graph
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
implying
SO(\Gamma)>SO(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})
. Thus, it again contradicts the choice of
\Gamma
,
which shows that
\Gamma
is unicyclic, say, with cycle
C
.

Since
G
is connected and has
k
bridges, this implies by Theorem
1
that
there are exactly
k
edges which do not lies on
C
. This leads us to conclude that
C
has length
n-k
.

Finally, we elaborate the
\delta
-operation by employing it to a
\Gamma\in\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
.
Assume that
\Gamma
comprises
vertices
u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{r}
,
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{s}
,
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{l}
, and

w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{\xi}
such that
u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{r}
form a cycle of length
r=n-k
and
s\geq 0
.
Let
\Gamma_{\delta}=\Gamma-x_{s}w_{1}+v_{l}w_{1}
.
In this way, the resulting graph
\Gamma_{\delta}

obtained by applying the
\delta
-operation will be called the
\delta
-switched graph.
Figure
5
employs the
\delta
-operation on
\Gamma\in\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
.

Figure 5:
Application of the
\delta
-operation on
\Gamma\in\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
. It has been used in Lemma
7
.

Lemma 7

Assume
\Gamma_{\delta}
is obtained by applying the
\delta
-operation on
\Gamma\in\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
as explained in Figure
5
.
We have

SO(\Gamma_{\delta})<SO(\Gamma).

Proof:

The graph
\Gamma
assumes a cycle
C_{r}=u_{1}u_{2}\dots u_{r}
, a path
u_{1},x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{s}

where
s=0,1,2,\dots,k
and two pendant paths, say
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{l}
and
w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{\xi}
,
attached with
x_{s}
(
u_{1}
if
s=0
). Note that
s+l+\xi=k
. Between
SO(\Gamma)
and
SO(\Gamma_{\delta})
, this shifting of edges generates
the following relation:

\displaystyle SO(\Gamma)-SO(\Gamma_{\delta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(x_{s-1})}^{2}+{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(x_{s})}^{2}}+\sqrt{{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(v_{1})}^{2}+{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(x_{s})}^{2}}+

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(w_{1})}^{2}+{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(x_{s})}^{2}}+\sqrt{{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(v_{l-1})}^{2}+{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(v_{l})}^{2}}-

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma_{\delta}}(x_{s-1})}^{2}+{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma_{\delta}}(x_{s})}^{2}}-\sqrt{{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma_{\delta}}(v_{1})}^{2}+{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma_{\delta}}(x_{s})}^{2}}-

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma_{\delta}}(v_{l-1})}^{2}+{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma_{\delta}}(v_{l})}^{2}}-\sqrt{{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma_{\delta}}(v_{l})}^{2}+{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma_{\delta}}(w_{1})}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sqrt{2^{2}+3^{2}}+\sqrt{2^{2}+3^{2}}+\sqrt{2^{2}+3^{2}}+\sqrt{2^{2}+1^{2}}-

\displaystyle\sqrt{2^{2}+2^{2}}-\sqrt{2^{2}+2^{2}}-\sqrt{2^{2}+2^{2}}-\sqrt{2^{2}+2^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3\sqrt{13}-8\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{5}>0

This completes the proof.

4
Sombor index with a given number of bridges of graphs

This section employs auxiliary results from Section
3
to evaluate a
lower bound on the Sombor index in
\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
. Graphs attaining the lower bound
have also been classified. For
n\geq 3
and
k=0,1,2,\dots,n-3,n-1
,
let
C_{n-k}
be a cycle having length
n-k
and
u_{1}
be an arbitrary vertex of
C_{n-k}
.
We obtain the family
\mathbb{P}_{n}^{k}
by attaching a path
P_{k+1}
on
k+1
vertices to
u_{1}\in V(C_{n-k})
. Thus, it implies that
\mathbb{P}_{n}^{k}

a family belonging to
\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
having order
n
and
k
number of bridges. See Figure
6
for a depiction of
the family
\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
. It is worth noticing that for an
n
-vertex in
\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
, we have either
k=n-1
or

0\leq k\leq n-3
.

The graph
\mathbb{P}_{n}^{k}
.

Finally, we have sufficient tools to present the main result.

Theorem 2

Assume that
\Gamma
is a bridgeless graph of order
n
, i.e.
\Gamma\in\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
such that
k=0
. Then

SO(\Gamma)\geq\sqrt{8}~n,

where equality is satisfied
\iff

\Gamma\cong C_{n}
.

Proof:

Since
\Gamma
bridgeless, by Theorem
1
, each edge of
\Gamma
lies on some cycles of
\Gamma
.
Thus
\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(v)\geq 2
for any
v\in V(\Gamma)
. Since
\Gamma
is connected and contains at least one cycle,
by Proposition
1
, we obtain that
m\geq n
. Therefore, we have:

\displaystyle SO(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{uv\in E\left(\Gamma\right)}\sqrt{{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(u)}^{2}+{\mathrm{deg}_{\Gamma}(v)}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{uv\in E\left(\Gamma\right)}\sqrt{{2}^{2}+{2}^{2}}=\sqrt{8}~m\geq\sqrt{8}~n.

By Lemma
6
, the graph
\Gamma\in\mathbb{G}_{n}^{0}
achieving the minimum has a unique cycle of length

n
, thus we obtain that
\Gamma\cong C_{n}
, since we have
k=0
.

Routine calculations show that
SO(C_{n})=\sqrt{8}~n
which, in turn, implies that the equality is
satisfied
\iff

\Gamma\cong C_{n}\cong\mathbb{P}_{n}^{0}
.

Next, we show a sharp lower bound for graphs in
\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
where
k\geq 1
.
In Theorem
3
, we present the result for graphs in
\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}

with
k=1
.

Theorem 3

Assume that
\Gamma
is a graph in
\mathbb{G}_{n}^{1}
. So, we have

SO(\Gamma)\geq 2(n-3)\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{10}+2\sqrt{13},

such that equality is satisfied
\iff

\Gamma\cong\mathbb{P}_{n}^{1}
.

Proof:

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{G}_{n}^{1}
such that
SO(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq SO(\Gamma)
for any
\Gamma\in\mathbb{G}_{n}^{1}
. Then, by
Lemma
6
,
\Gamma^{\prime}
has a unique cycle of length
n-1
, which implies that
\Gamma^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{P}_{n}^{1}
.
Since
SO(\mathbb{P}_{n}^{1})=2(n-3)\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{10}+2\sqrt{13}
, we obtain that

SO(\Gamma)\geq 2(n-3)\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{10}+2\sqrt{13}
for any
\Gamma\in\mathbb{G}_{n}^{1}
.
This proves the result.

A similar sharp lower bound for

2\leq k\leq n-3
has been shown herewith.

Theorem 4

Assume that
\Gamma
is a graph in
\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
for
2\leq k\leq n-3
. Thus, we have

SO(\Gamma)\geq 2(n-4)\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{5}+3\sqrt{13},

such that equality is satisfied
\iff

\Gamma\cong\mathbb{P}_{n}^{k}
.

Proof:

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
such that
SO(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq SO(\Gamma)
for any
\Gamma\in\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
, then by
Lemma
6
,
\Gamma^{\prime}
has a unique cycle of length
n-k
. By a successive application
of Lemma
4
and
7
, we obtain that
\Gamma^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{P}_{n}^{k}
. Routine
calculations show that
SO(\mathbb{P}_{n}^{k})=2(n-4)\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{5}+3\sqrt{13}
. Thus, we have

SO(\Gamma)\geq 2(n-4)\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{5}+3\sqrt{13}
for any
\Gamma\in\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
, where
2\leq k\leq n-3
.
This completes the proof.

And finally, we show a sharp lower bound for
\Gamma\in\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
with
k=n-1
.
Note that by Corollary
1
,
\Gamma
is a tree.

Theorem 5

For an
n
-vertex tree, we obtain

SO(T)\geq 2(n-3)\sqrt{2}+2\sqrt{5},

such that the equality is satisfied
\iff

T\cong P_{n}\cong\mathbb{P}_{n}^{n-1}
.

Proof:

First, we show the following claim for a tree
T\in\mathbb{B}^{n}_{k}
achieving the
minimum Sombor index.

Claim 2

1\leq\mathrm{deg}_{T}(u)\leq 2
for any
u\in V(T)
.

Proof of Claim
2
.

We show the claim by contradiction. We assume the existence of a
y\in V(T)
, on contrary, satisfying

\mathrm{deg}_{T}(y)\geq 3
. A successive application of Lemma
2
by assuming
y=u_{k}\in V(T_{\tau})

(see Figure
1
) generates a tree
T^{\prime}
satisfying
SO(T)>SO(T^{\prime})
. However,
T
was assumed to possess
minimum Sombor index. This arises a contradiction. This proves the claim.

Claim
2
implies that
T\cong P_{n}
, as
T
is connected.
One could calculate that
SO(P_{n})=2(n-3)\sqrt{2}+2\sqrt{5}
, which
completes the proof.

Now, in view of Theorems
2
,
3
,
4
, and
5
,
we conclude the main result as follows.

Theorem 6

Let
\Gamma\in\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
. Then

SO(\Gamma)\geq\left\{\begin{split}&\sqrt{8}~n;&&\text{for}\quad k=0,\\
&2(n-3)\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{10}+2\sqrt{13};&&\textrm{for}\quad k=1,\\
&2(n-4)\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{5}+3\sqrt{13};&&\textrm{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq n-3,\\
&2(n-3)\sqrt{2}+2\sqrt{5};&&\textrm{for}\quad k=n-1,\end{split}\right.

such that the equality is satisfied
\iff

\Gamma\cong\mathbb{P}_{n}^{k}
.

5
Sombor index of graphs with given vertex-connectivity

In this section, we give a sharp upper bound on
SO(\Gamma)
for graphs in
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{k}
.
For this we need the following crucial result:

Lemma 8

Let
k_{1},\,k,\,k_{2}
and
n
be positive integers such that
n=k_{1}+k+k_{2}
with
k_{1}\geq k_{2}
. Also let

\displaystyle f(k_{1},\,k_{2})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\,{k_{1}\choose 2}\,(k+k_{1}-1)+\sqrt{2}\,{k\choose 2}\,(n-1)+\sqrt{2}\,{k_{2}\choose 2}\,(k+k_{2}-1)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~+kk_{1}\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1}-1)^{2}}+kk_{2}\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}}.

Then
f(k_{1}+1,\,k_{2}-1)-f(k_{1},\,k_{2})>0
.

Proof:
We have

\displaystyle f(k_{1}+1,\,k_{2}-1)-f(k_{1},\,k_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\,\left[{k_{1}+1\choose 2}\,(k+k_{1})-{k_{1}\choose 2}\,(k+k_{1}-1)\right]+\sqrt{2}\,\left[{k_{2}-1\choose 2}\,(k+k_{2}-2)-{k_{2}\choose 2}\,(k+k_{2}-1)\right]

\displaystyle+k\,(k_{1}+1)\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1})^{2}}-kk_{1}\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1}-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle+k\,(k_{2}-1)\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}}-kk_{2}\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}}.

(1)

Now,

\displaystyle{k_{1}+1\choose 2}\,(k+k_{1})-{k_{1}\choose 2}\,(k+k_{1}-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{1}}{2}\,\Big[(k_{1}+1)\,(k+k_{1})-(k_{1}-1)\,(k+k_{1}-1)\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{1}}{2}\,(2k+3k_{1}-1)

(2)

and

\displaystyle{k_{2}-1\choose 2}\,(k+k_{2}-2)-{k_{2}\choose 2}\,(k+k_{2}-1)

\displaystyle=-\frac{k_{2}-1}{2}\,\Big[k_{2}\,(k+k_{2}-1)-(k_{2}-2)\,(k+k_{2}-2)\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{2}-1}{2}\,(2k+3k_{2}-4),

(3)

{\bf Claim\,1.}

\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1})^{2}}\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}}>\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}}\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1}-1)^{2}}.

{\bf Proof\,of\,Claim\,1.}
We have to prove that

\Big[(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1})^{2}\Big]\,\Big[(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}\Big]>\Big[(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}\Big]\,\Big[(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1}-1)^{2}\Big],

that is,

\displaystyle(n-1)^{2}\,\Big[(k+k_{1})^{2}+(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}\Big]+(k+k_{1})^{2}\,(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}>(n-1)^{2}\,\Big[(k+k_{1}-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+(k+k_{1}-1)^{2}\,(k+k_{2}-1)^{2},

that is,

2(n-1)^{2}\,(k_{1}-k_{2}+1)>\Big[(k+k_{1})^{2}-2\,(k+k_{1})+1\Big]\,(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}-(k+k_{1})^{2}\,\Big[(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}-2\,(k+k_{2}-1)+1\Big],

that is,

2(n-1)^{2}\,(k_{1}-k_{2}+1)>(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}-(k+k_{1})^{2}+2\,(k+k_{1})\,(k+k_{2}-1)\,(k_{1}-k_{2}+1),

which is true always as
k_{1}\geq k_{2}
, that is,
k+k_{2}-1<k+k_{1}
and
n-1>k+k_{1}>k+k_{2}-1
. This proves the
Claim 1
.

{\bf Claim\,2.}

\displaystyle\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1})^{2}}-\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1}-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~>\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}}-\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}}.

{\bf Proof\,of\,Claim\,2.}
We have to prove that

\displaystyle\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1})^{2}}+\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~>\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}}+\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1}-1)^{2}},

that is,

\displaystyle(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1})^{2}+(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}+2\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1})^{2}}\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}}

\displaystyle~~~~>(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}+(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1}-1)^{2}+2\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\times\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1}-1)^{2}},

that is,

\displaystyle k_{1}-k_{2}+1+\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1})^{2}}\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}}\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1}-1)^{2}}>0,

which is true as
k_{1}\geq k_{2}
and by
Claim 1
. This proves the
Claim 2
.

Using (
2
), (
3
) with
k_{1}\geq k_{2}
and
Claim 2
in (
1
), we obtain

\displaystyle~~f(k_{1}+1,\,k_{2}-1)-f(k_{1},\,k_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{1}}{2}\,(2k+3k_{1}-1)-\frac{(k_{2}-1)}{2}\,(2k+3k_{2}-4)+kk_{1}\,\Big[\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1})^{2}}-\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1}-1)^{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+kk_{2}\,\Big[\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}}-\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+k\,\Big[\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1})^{2}}-\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k_{2}}{2}\,(2k+3k_{2}-1)-\frac{(k_{2}-1)}{2}\,(2k+3k_{2}-4)+kk_{2}\,\Big[\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1})^{2}}-\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1}-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}}-\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+k\,\Big[\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2})^{2}}-\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}}\Big]>0

as
\frac{k_{1}}{2}\,(2k+3k_{1}-1)>\frac{(k_{2}-1)}{2}\,(2k+3k_{2}-4)
and
\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2})^{2}}-\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-2)^{2}}>0
.
This completes the proof of the result.

Denote by
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{k}
the set of
n
-vertex connected graphs with vertex-connectivity
k
, where
1\leq k\leq n-1
.

Theorem 7

Let
\Gamma\in\mathbb{V}_{n}^{k}
with
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Then

SO(\Gamma)\leq\sqrt{2}\,{n-k-1\choose 2}\,(n-2)+\sqrt{2}\,{k\choose 2}\,(n-1)+k\,(n-k-1)\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(n-2)^{2}}+k\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+k^{2}}

with equality if and only if
\Gamma\cong(K_{n-k-1}\cup K_{1})\vee K_{k}
.

Proof:

When
k=n-1
, there is only one graph
K_{n}
, which can be viewed as a special case of
(K_{n-k-1}\cup K_{1})\vee K_{k}
with
k=n-1
. We now assume that
1\leq k\leq n-2
. Let
\Omega
be a graph in
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{k}
such that
SO(\Gamma)\leq SO(\Omega)
. Since
k
is the vertex connectivity of graph
\Omega
, let
S=\{v_{1},\,v_{2},\ldots,\,v_{k}\}\subseteq V(\Omega)
be the vertex cut and
|S|=k
. By Lemma
1
, we obtain that
\Omega[S]
must be a complete graph
K_{k}
.
We now prove that
\Omega-S
has exactly two components. To the contrary, assume that there exist at least three components
\Omega_{1}
,
\Omega_{2}
and
\Omega_{3}
with
v_{i}\in V(\Omega_{i})
for
i=1,\,2
. Then we find that
\Omega+v_{1}v_{2}
is a connected graph of order
n
with vertex connectivity
k
, with a higher Sombor index than that of

\Omega
by Lemma
1
, contradicting to the choice of
\Omega
. Now we assume that
\Omega-S=\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}
, where
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are the two
components of
\Omega-S
. Again by Lemma
1
, we conclude that
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are all cliques and each vertex in
S
is incident
to all vertices in
\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}
. Hence
\Omega\cong(K_{k_{1}}\cup K_{k_{2}})\vee K_{k}
, where
k_{1}+k_{2}=n-k
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
k_{1}\geq k_{2}
. Then

\displaystyle SO(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{uv\in E\left(\Omega\right)}\sqrt{{\mathrm{deg}_{\Omega}(u)}^{2}+{\mathrm{deg}_{\Omega}(v)}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\,{k_{1}\choose 2}\,(k+k_{1}-1)+\sqrt{2}\,{k\choose 2}\,(n-1)+\sqrt{2}\,{k_{2}\choose 2}\,(k+k_{2}-1)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~+kk_{1}\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{1}-1)^{2}}+kk_{2}\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(k+k_{2}-1)^{2}}=f(k_{1},\,k_{2}),~\mbox{(say)}.

If
k_{2}=1
, then
\Omega\cong(K_{n-k-1}\cup K_{1})\vee K_{k}
and hence the equality holds. Otherwise,
k_{2}\geq 2
. By Lemma
8
, we obtain

f(k_{1},\,k_{2})<f(k_{1}+1,\,k_{2}-1)<\cdots<f(k_{1}+k_{2}-2,2)<f(k_{1}+k_{2}-1,1).

From the above results, we obtain

\displaystyle SO(\Gamma)\leq SO(\Omega)\leq f(k_{1},\,k_{2})

\displaystyle<f(k_{1}+k_{2}-1,1)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\,{n-k-1\choose 2}\,(n-2)+\sqrt{2}\,{k\choose 2}\,(n-1)

\displaystyle~~~~~+k\,(n-k-1)\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+(n-2)^{2}}+k\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+k^{2}}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.

6
Conclusions

This paper studies the Sombor index of
n
-vertex graphs having
k
bridges i.e.
\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
.
Recently in 2021, Horoldagva & Xu
[
22
]
found a sharp upper bound on the Sombor index of graphs in
\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}

As a continuance of this study, in this paper,
we find a sharp lower bound on the Sombor index of graphs in
\mathbb{B}_{n}^{k}
.
Moreover, graphs achieving the lower bound have been characterized.
Moreover, we find a sharp upper bound on the Sombor index of graphs in
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{k}
. Corresponding
extremal graphs achieving the bound have been characterized.

Let
\mathbb{E}_{n}^{k}
be the set of all
n
-vertex graphs with edge connectivity at most
k
.
Here we would like to remark the following:

Remark 1

The problem of finding maximum Sombor index
of graphs in
\mathbb{E}_{n}^{k}
is similar to Theorem
7
and the same
unique graph i.e.
(K_{n-k-1}\cup K_{1})\vee K_{k}
is expected to achieve the maximum.

Having said that, we propose the following open problems on the Sombor index of graphs.

Problem 1

Find minimal graphs with respect to the Sombor index among graphs in
\mathbb{V}_{n}^{k}
.

Problem 2

Find minimal graphs with respect to the Sombor index among graphs in
\mathbb{E}_{n}^{k}
.

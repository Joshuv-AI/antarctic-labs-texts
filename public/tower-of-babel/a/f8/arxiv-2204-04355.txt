Of Shadows and Gaps in Spatial Search
Ada Chan, Chris Godsil, Christino Tamon, Weichen Xie

Abstract
Spatial search occurs in a connected graph if a continuous-time quantum walk on the adjacency matrix of the graph, suitably scaled, plus a rank-one perturbation induced by any vertex will unitarily map the principal eigenvector of the graph to the characteristic vector of the vertex. This phenomenon is a natural continuous-time analogue of Grover search. The spatial search is said to be optimal if it occurs with constant fidelity and in time inversely proportional to the shadow of the target vertex on the principal eigenvector. Extending a result of Chakraborty et al. ( Physical Review A , 102 :032214, 2020), we prove a simpler characterization of optimal spatial search. Based on this characterization, we observe that some families of distance-regular graphs, such as Hamming and Grassmann graphs, have optimal spatial search. We also show a matching lower bound on time for spatial search with constant fidelity, which extends a bound due to Farhi and Gutmann for perfect fidelity. Our elementary proofs employ standard tools, such as Weyl inequalities and Cauchy determinant formula. Keywords : Quantum walk, spatial search, spectral gap, perturbation.

Our improvement is obtained through tighter estimates on the leading eigenvalue perturbations

We also observe a critical condition (hidden in previous analyses) for the strict interlacing between

Our original motivation for this work was to understand obstructions to optimal spatial search.
To this end, we found a collection of necessary conditions for spatial search which are largely

. Aside from being a crucial ingredient for proving the

These conditions can potentially be adapted to other classes such as a small Cartesian product of cycles.

The proofs we employ are elementary as they only use basic tools from matrix theory which
do not appeal to perturbative methods. We nevertheless adopt standard asymptotic arguments commonly
used in random graphs and complexity of algorithms.

Background

We assume the standard inner product

All vectors are assumed normalized under the

The set of all

The spectrum

In this work, we will focus primarily on Hermitian matrices whose eigenvalues are guaranteed to be real.
We adopt the notation

We call a Hermitian matrix

.

In stating the spectral decomposition of a Hermitian matrix, say
A=\sum_{r=1}^{d}\theta_{r}E_{r}
,
we always assume the distinct eigenvalues are listed in decreasing order, that is,
\theta_{1}>\theta_{2}>\ldots>\theta_{d}
.
Recall that
E_{r}
is the orthogonal projection onto the eigenspace corresponding to eigenvalue
\theta_{r}

where

is Hermitian with

,
\sum_{r=1}^{d}E_{r}=I
, and

if

The

of a vector

\Supp_{A}(w)=\{\theta_{r}:\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert\neq 0\},

(2)

which is the set of eigenvalues whose eigenspaces are not fully contained in the subspace

.
For each positive integer

, we let

S_{k}=\sum_{r=2}^{d}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{(\theta_{1}-\theta_{r})^{k}}.

(3)

These are spectral parameters which will play an important role in characterizing optimal spatial search.
They originally appeared in Childs and Goldstone
[
5
]
.
Further background on matrix theory may be found in Horn and Johnson

]
.

•

•

•

Given that most results are asymptotic, we assume that

Our focus is on a family of graphs

and hence most assertions are asymptotic in nature and will depend on

as it tends to

.
When it is clear, we simply write

for the family of graphs, and even simply

is understood
from context.
Given a graph
G=(V,E)
that is undirected, its adjacency matrix
A(G)
is a matrix whose

-entry
is

, and is

.
For example, this may include the case of signed graphs (
\pm 1

\pm i

A continuous-time quantum walk on
G

is governed by the
Schrödinger equation defined by

\rho^{\prime}(t)=-i[H(G),\rho(t)]

where
\rho(t)
is a positive semidefinite matrix of unit trace (also called a density matrix).
Here,
[A,B]=AB-BA

[
13

Given that spatial search is heavily influenced by the seminal work
[
1
]
,
in the rest of this paper we will call a graph with the optimal spatial search property

.

2.1 Definition

Let
\{G_{n}\}
be a family of graphs with normalized adjacency matrix
H(G_{n})
where
E_{1}
is
the orthogonal projection onto its principal eigenspace.
We say
G_{n}
is
\gamma
-
Groverian
if for any
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
\left\lVert E_{1}w\right\rVert\neq 0
,
the density matrix evolution defined by

\rho(t)=U(t)\rho(0)U(t)^{-1},\ \hskip 21.68121pt\ \mbox{ with $\rho(0)=E_{1}$},

where
U(t)=\exp(-it(\gamma H(G_{n})+{w}{w}^{\dagger}))
,
there is a time
\tau=O_{n}(1/\left\lVert E_{1}w\right\rVert)
so that the fidelity satisfies

f(\tau):=\Tr({w}{w}^{\dagger}\rho(\tau))=\Omega_{n}(1).

(5)

The family of graphs is Groverian if it is
\gamma
-Groverian for some
\gamma>0
.

The condition on time is the
quadratic speedup
requirement that is the hallmark signature of Grover search.
This is because the probability of measuring
w
given the state
E_{1}
is
\left\lVert E_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}
,
and hence generating
w
has a geometric time of
1/\left\lVert E_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}
.

For a spatial search algorithm to be fully constructive, most previous works require the graphs be vertex transitive.
This assumption was made in Farhi and Gutmann
[
4
]
and in Childs and Goldstone
[
5
]
.
This guarantees that the choice of the scaling parameter
\gamma
and the time

in Definition
2.1

are not dependent on the vertex
w
. But, as pointed out by Meyer and Wong
[
14
]
, we can also allow graphs whose
automorphism group has a constant number of orbits. They observed that the search algorithm can simply
check each orbit separately as most of the relevant spectral parameters are constant within each orbit
*
*

*

A pertinent
example given in
[
14
]
is the
barbell
graph
obtained from connecting two disjoint cliques
K_{n}
by a single edge; this graph has two orbits with sizes

2
and
2n-2
, respectively.
.
Note that a vertex transitive graph has only a single orbit.
In this work, we will place the same assumption on our graphs.

Given that most of our statements hold for Hermitian matrices, we will view graphs largely
through their Hermitian adjacency matrices. To that end, we fix a convenient terminology to
capture a triplet of a Hermitian matrix, a unit norm vector and a positive scalar that will play
a central role in all of our assertions.

2.2 Assumption

We call
(H,w,\gamma)
a
tuplet
if the following (notational) assumptions hold.

(a)

H\in\mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is a normalized Hermitian matrix whose spectral decomposition is
H=\sum_{r}\theta_{r}E_{r}
.
Recall that as
H
is normalized, its eigenvalues lie in
[0,1]
and
1
is a simple eigenvalue.

(b)

w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a vector with unit norm which satisfies
\left\lVert E_{1}w\right\rVert\neq 0
and
\left\lVert E_{1}w\right\rVert=o_{n}(1)
.
Whenever it is clear from context, we will use the abbreviated notation
\epsilon_{1}:=\left\lVert E_{1}w\right\rVert
.

(c)

\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
is a positive scalar whereby the perturbed matrix
\gamma H+{w}{w}^{\dagger}
has
the spectral decomposition
\sum_{p}\zeta_{p}F_{p}
.

3
Time Lower Bounds

We motivate the condition on time in Definition
2.1
.
Farhi and Gutmann
[
4
]
proved that spatial search with fidelity
1
on a vertex-transitive graph
requires time
\Omega_{n}(\sqrt{n})
.
We extend their result to show a time lower bound of
\Omega_{n}(1/\epsilon_{1})
for constant fidelity
which applies to arbitrary graphs.
Note that
\epsilon_{1}=1/\sqrt{n}
for vertex-transitive graphs whenever
w
denote the characteristic
vector of a vertex.
This matching lower bound justifies the choice of the optimal time.

3.1 Theorem

Let
(H,w,\gamma)
be a tuplet where
H
is
\gamma
-Groverian at time
\tau
.
Then,
\tau=\Omega_{n}(1/\epsilon_{1})
.

Proof.

The first part of the proof follows
[
4
]
but in the density matrix language.
Let
H_{w}=H_{0}+{w}{w}^{\dagger}
, where
H_{0}=\gamma H
.
We compare two density matrix evolutions given by

\rho^{\prime}_{w}(t)=-i[H_{w},\rho_{w}(t)],\ \ \ \rho^{\prime}_{0}(t)=-i[H_{0},\rho_{0}(t)]

(6)

with
\rho_{w}(0)=\rho_{0}(0)=E_{1}
.
Note
\rho_{0}(t)=E_{1}
for all
t
.
Assume for now that the fidelity is one or
\rho_{w}(\tau)={w}{w}^{\dagger}
.
We will remove this assumption later.

The proof proceeds by analyzing bounds on
\left\lVert\rho_{w}(t)-\rho_{0}(t)\right\rVert^{2}
.
First, by taking derivative, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\rho_{w}(t)-\rho_{0}(t)\right\rVert^{2}=-2\langle\rho_{w}(t),\rho_{0}(t)\rangle^{\prime}.

From the product rule and Equation (
6
), we see that

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{w}(t),\rho_{0}(t)\rangle^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{w}(t),\rho^{\prime}_{0}(t)\rangle+\langle\rho^{\prime}_{w}(t),\rho_{0}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\langle[H_{w},\rho_{w}(t)],E_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\langle w,[E_{1},\rho_{w}(t)]w\rangle.

Now, notice that

\displaystyle|\langle w,[E_{1},\rho_{w}(t)]w\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2|\langle w,E_{1}\rho_{w}(t)w\rangle|,\ \mbox{ as $|x^{\dagger}[A,B]x|\leq 2|x^{\dagger}(AB)x|$}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left\lVert E_{1}w\right\rVert\left\lVert\rho_{w}(t)w\right\rVert,\ \mbox{ since $|\langle a,b\rangle|\leq\left\lVert a\right\rVert\left\lVert b\right\rVert$}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\epsilon_{1},\ \hskip 14.45377pt\mbox{ because $\left\lVert\rho_{w}(t)w\right\rVert\leq 1$.}

Putting these together, we get

\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\rho_{w}(t)-\rho_{0}(t)\right\rVert^{2}\right|\ \leq\ 4\epsilon_{1}.

By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, we obtain

\left\lVert\rho_{w}(\tau)-\rho_{0}(\tau)\right\rVert^{2}\ =\ \int_{0}^{\tau}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert\rho_{w}(t)-\rho_{0}(t)\right\rVert^{2}\right)dt\ \leq\ 4\epsilon_{1}\tau.

(7)

Since
\rho_{w}(\tau)={w}{w}^{\dagger}
, we have

\left\lVert\rho_{w}(\tau)-\rho_{0}(\tau)\right\rVert^{2}=2(1-\epsilon_{1}^{2})
, and therefore

1-\epsilon_{1}^{2}\ \leq\ 2\epsilon_{1}\tau,

which yields the lower bound
\tau=\Omega_{n}(1/\epsilon_{1})
.

Finally, we remove the assumption
\rho_{w}(\tau)={w}{w}^{\dagger}
.
Suppose that
\left\lVert\rho_{w}(\tau)-{w}{w}^{\dagger}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\delta
, for some
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Our strategy is to reduce this case to the former case by using the following inequality.

3.2 Fact

(Triangle Inequality for Squared Norm)
For matrices
A,B,C\in\mat_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,

\left\lVert A-C\right\rVert^{2}\leq 2\left\lVert A-B\right\rVert^{2}+2\left\lVert B-C\right\rVert^{2}.

Proof.

Squaring the triangle inequality, we get

\left\lVert A-C\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left\lVert A-B\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert B-C\right\rVert^{2}+2\left\lVert A-B\right\rVert\left\lVert B-C\right\rVert.

Now, observe
0\leq(\left\lVert A-C\right\rVert-\left\lVert B-C\right\rVert)^{2}
.
∎

Applying Fact
3.2
, we see that

2\left\lVert\rho_{w}(\tau)-\rho_{0}(\tau)\right\rVert^{2}\geq\left\lVert{w}{w}^{\dagger}-\rho_{0}(\tau)\right\rVert^{2}-2\left\lVert\rho_{w}(\tau)-{w}{w}^{\dagger}\right\rVert^{2}\geq\left\lVert{w}{w}^{\dagger}-\rho_{0}(\tau)\right\rVert^{2}-2\delta.

Thus, we have

\left\lVert\rho_{w}(\tau)-\rho_{0}(\tau)\right\rVert^{2}\ \geq\ (1-\epsilon_{1}^{2}-\delta)

which can be combined with Equation (
7
) to obtain
\tau=\Omega_{n}(1/\epsilon_{1})
.
∎

Interlacing

We review some relevant tools from matrix theory and prove a few preliminary results.

4.1
Weyl Inequalities

A theorem of Weyl on eigenvalue interlacing is key to our analysis.
Given that we restate the theorem slightly, we prove it for completeness.

4.1 Lemma

(Subspace intersection, Lemma 4.2.3 in
[
11
]
)

Let
W_{1},\ldots,W_{k}
be subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let
d=\dim W_{1}+\ldots+\dim W_{k}-(k-1)n
.
If
d\geq 1
, then
\dim(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}W_{i})\geq d
.
In particular, there is a unit vector in
W_{1}\cap\ldots\cap W_{k}
.

Proof.

Note that
\dim(W_{1}\cap W_{2})+\dim(W_{1}+W_{2})=\dim W_{1}+\dim W_{2}
, which implies

\dim(W_{1}\cap W_{2})\geq\dim W_{1}+\dim W_{2}-n
. So, if
\dim W_{1}+\dim W_{2}-n\geq 1
, then

W_{1}\cap W_{2}
contains a nonzero vector. The claim follows by induction.
∎

4.2 Theorem

(Weyl Interlacing, restatement of Lemma 4.3.1 in
[
11
]
)

Let
A,B
be two
n\times n
Hermitian matrices.
For
i=1,\ldots,n
, we have

\lambda_{i}(A+B)\ \leq\ \lambda_{i-j}(A)+\lambda_{j+1}(B),\ \ \mbox{ $j=0,\ldots,i-1$}

(8)

with equality if and only if there is
v\neq 0
so that
(A+B)v=\lambda_{i}(A+B)v
,

Av=\lambda_{i-j}(A)v
, and
Bv=\lambda_{j+1}(B)v
,
and we have

\lambda_{i+j}(A)+\lambda_{n-j}(B)\ \leq\ \lambda_{i}(A+B),\ \ \mbox{ $j=0,\ldots,n-i$}

(9)

with equality if and only if there is
v\neq 0
so that
(A+B)v=\lambda_{i}(A+B)v
,

Av=\lambda_{i+j}(A)v
, and
Bv=\lambda_{n-j}(B)v
.

Proof.

For
i=1,\ldots,n
, let
z_{i}
,
x_{i}
, and
y_{i}
be orthonormal eigenvectors of
A
,
B
, and
A+B
, respectively,
corresponding to their
i
th largest eigenvalues.

We define

W_{1}=\spn\{z_{i-j},\ldots,z_{n}\}
,

W_{2}=\spn\{x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
,
and

W_{3}=\spn\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{i}\}
.
Let
d_{1}=\dim W_{1}=n-i+j+1
,
d_{2}=\dim W_{2}=n-j
, and
d_{3}=\dim W_{3}=i
.
Since
\dim(W_{1}\cap W_{2}\cap W_{3})=d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}-\dim(W_{1}+W_{2}+W_{3})\geq d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}-2n=1
,
the subspace
W_{1}\cap W_{2}\cap W_{3}
contains a nonzero vector
v
.
For any nonzero
v\in W_{1}\cap W_{2}\cap W_{3}
, we have

\lambda_{i}(A+B)\leq v^{\dagger}(A+B)v\leq\lambda_{i-j}(A)+\lambda_{j+1}(B),\ \mbox{ for $j=0,\ldots,i-1$. }

(10)

Equality is achieved for
i,j
if and only if there is a nonzero
v\in W_{1}\cap W_{2}\cap W_{3}

for which
(A+B)v=\lambda_{i}(A+B)v
,
Av=\lambda_{i-j}(A)v
, and
Bv=\lambda_{j+1}(B)v
.

For the second inequality, observe that
\lambda_{i}(-A)=-\lambda_{n-i+1}(A)
. Therefore,

-\lambda_{n-i+1}(A+B)=\lambda_{i}(-A-B)\leq\lambda_{i-j}(-A)+\lambda_{j+1}(-B)=-\lambda_{n-i+j+1}(A)-\lambda_{n-j}(B)

(11)

which implies

\lambda_{n-i+j+1}(A)+\lambda_{n-j}(B)\leq\lambda_{n-i+1}(A+B).

(12)

Now, rename
n-i+1
to
i
and keep
j
(and hence
n-i+j+1
to
i+j
). This yields

\lambda_{i+j}(A)+\lambda_{n-j}(B)\leq\lambda_{i}(A+B),\ \mbox{ for $j=0,\ldots,n-i$. }

(13)

Equality is achieved for
i,j
if and only if there is a nonzero
v\in W_{1}\cap W_{2}\cap W_{3}

for which
(A+B)v=\lambda_{i}(A+B)v
,
Av=\lambda_{i+j}(A)v
, and
Bv=\lambda_{n-j}(B)v
with

W_{1}=\spn\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{i+j}\}
,

W_{2}=\spn\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-j}\}
,
and

W_{3}=\spn\{y_{i},\ldots,y_{n}\}
.
∎

4.3 Lemma

(Strict Interlacing)
Let
H
be a normalized
n\times n
Hermitian matrix with spectral decomposition
H=\sum_{r}\theta_{r}E_{r}
.
Let
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a vector with unit norm where
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\Supp_{H}(w)
.
Then for any
\gamma>0
, the two largest eigenvalues of
\gamma H+{w}{w}^{\dagger}
are
simple and they strictly interlace the two largest eigenvalues of
\gamma H
.

Proof.

We apply Theorem
4.2
with
A=\gamma H
and
B={w}{w}^{\dagger}
.
Note
\lambda_{1}(H)=\lambda_{1}({w}{w}^{\dagger})=1
are simple eigenvalues, and
\lambda_{j}({w}{w}^{\dagger})=0
for
j=2,\ldots,n
.
From Equation (
9
), with
i=1
and
j=0
, we get

\lambda_{1}(\gamma H)+\lambda_{n}({w}{w}^{\dagger})=\lambda_{1}(\gamma H)\leq\lambda_{1}(\gamma H+{w}{w}^{\dagger}).

(14)

Similarly, from Equation (
8
), with
i=2
and
j=1
, we get

\lambda_{2}(\gamma H+{w}{w}^{\dagger})\leq\lambda_{1}(\gamma H)+\lambda_{2}({w}{w}^{\dagger})=\lambda_{1}(\gamma H).

(15)

Both of the inequalities above are strict since
\lambda_{1}(H)
is simple and
E_{1}w\neq 0
implies
{w}{w}^{\dagger}z_{1}\neq 0
where
z_{1}
is the principal eigenvector with
{z_{1}}{z_{1}}^{\dagger}=E_{1}
.

Next, we apply Theorem
4.2
with
i=2
and
j=0
in Equation (
9
), to get

\lambda_{2}(\gamma H)+\lambda_{n}({w}{w}^{\dagger})=\lambda_{2}(\gamma H)\leq\lambda_{2}(\gamma H+{w}{w}^{\dagger}).

(16)

Since
\theta_{2}\in\Supp_{H}(w)
, there exists an eigenvector
z_{2}
corresponding to
\lambda_{2}(H)
so that
{w}{w}^{\dagger}z_{2}\neq 0
. For any nonzero vector
v
from the subspace
W_{1}\cap W_{2}\cap W_{3}
(as constructed in the proof of Theorem
4.2
),
with
W_{1}=\spn\{{z_{1}},{z_{2}}\}
, we always have
{w}{w}^{\dagger}z_{2}\neq 0

and hence the inequality in Equation (
16
) is strict.
∎

4.2
Cauchy’s Equality

The next formula due to Cauchy can be derived from the determinant of bordered matrices (see
[
11
]
).

4.4 Lemma

(Cauchy)
For any
n\times n
matrix
A
, any vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we have

\det(A+xy^{\dagger})=\det(A)+y^{\dagger}\Adj(A)x.

(17)

Moreover, if
A
is nonsingular, then

\det(A+xy^{\dagger})=\det(A)(1+y^{\dagger}A^{-1}x).

(18)

We apply the above lemma to provide sharper estimates for the case when
\gamma=S_{1}

on the locations of the two largest perturbed eigenvalues relative to the unperturbed principal eigenvalue.

4.5 Proposition

Let
(H,w,S_{1})
be a tuplet. Then

\epsilon_{1}^{2}\ <\ \zeta_{1}-S_{1}\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{S_{1}}\epsilon_{1}.

(19)

Moreover, if
\theta_{2}\in\Supp_{H}(w)
, then

0\ <\ S_{1}-\zeta_{2}\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{S_{1}}\epsilon_{1}.

(20)

Proof.

Let
\tilde{H}=S_{1}H+{w}{w}^{\dagger}
and
\Delta_{r}=1-\theta_{r}
, for all
r
.
Note that
\phi(\tilde{H},t)=\det((tI-S_{1}H)-{w}{w}^{\dagger})
.
Applying Lemma
4.4
and assuming
m_{r}
is the multiplicity of
\theta_{r}
, we get

\displaystyle\phi(\tilde{H},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det(tI-S_{1}H)(1-{w^{\dagger}}{(tI-S_{1}H)^{-1}w})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{r}(t-S_{1}\theta_{r})^{m_{r}}\right)\left(1-\sum_{r}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{(t-S_{1}\theta_{r})}\right).

The bound
\zeta_{1}-S_{1}>\epsilon_{1}^{2}
follows by verifying that
\phi(\tilde{H},S_{1}+\epsilon_{1}^{2})
is negative.
Observe that if
\phi(\tilde{H},S_{1}+\beta)>0
,
then
\zeta_{1}-S_{1}\leq\beta
.
We write the condition
\phi(\tilde{H},S_{1}+\beta)>0
as

1\ >\ \sum_{r}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{S_{1}\Delta_{r}+\beta}\ =\ \frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\beta}+\sum_{r\neq 1}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{S_{1}\Delta_{r}+\beta}.

Because
\Delta_{r}\leq 1
, for each
r
, it follows that
(1+\beta/S_{1})^{-1}
is an upper bound
for the sum
\sum_{r\neq 1}\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}/(S_{1}\Delta_{r}+\beta)
.
So, to satisfy the previous inequality, it suffices to require

1\ >\ \frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\beta}+\frac{1}{1+\beta/S_{1}}.

After straightforward calculations, we obtain

\beta^{2}-\epsilon_{1}^{2}\beta-S_{1}\epsilon_{1}^{2}>0
. The roots of this quadratic equation are given by

\beta_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon_{1}^{2}\pm\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}^{4}+4S_{1}\epsilon_{1}^{2}}).

The positive root is given by
\beta_{+}=O_{n}(\sqrt{S_{1}}\epsilon_{1})
, since
\epsilon_{1}\ll 1
,
which proves Equation (
19
).

We denote
\delta_{-}=\zeta_{2}-S_{1}
.
By Lemma
4.3
,
|\delta_{-}|>0
.
Now, observe that if
\phi(\tilde{H},S_{1}-\beta)>0
then
|\delta_{-}|\leq\beta
.
As above, we write the condition
\phi(\tilde{H},S_{1}-\beta)>0
as

1\ <\ \sum_{r}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{S_{1}\Delta_{r}-\beta}\ =\ -\frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\beta}+\sum_{r\neq 1}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{S_{1}\Delta_{r}-\beta}.

This yields

\sum_{r\neq 1}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{S_{1}\Delta_{r}}\frac{1}{1-\beta/(S_{1}\Delta_{r})}\ >\ 1+\frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\beta}.

As
\Delta_{r}\leq 1
, for
r\geq 2
, we note that
1/(1-\beta/S_{1})
is a lower bound
for the expression on the left-hand side. Therefore, we may require instead

\frac{1}{1-\beta/S_{1}}\ >\ 1+\frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\beta}

which simplifies to

\beta^{2}+\epsilon_{1}^{2}\beta-S_{1}\epsilon_{1}^{2}\ >\ 0
.
The maximum root of the quadratic polynomial is given by

\beta=\frac{1}{2}(-\epsilon_{1}^{2}+\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}^{4}+4S_{1}\epsilon_{1}^{2}})\ \sim\ \sqrt{S_{1}}\epsilon_{1}

which proves Equation (
20
).
∎

5
Necessary Gaps

We describe necessary conditions for graphs to be Groverian, but first we derive some useful
preliminary observations. Our analysis borrows heavily ideas from Chakraborty
et al.

[
7
]
.

We start by restating the machinery in
[
7
]
(specifically, Theorem 4) using our notation
for the sake of consistency and to point out certain explicit assumptions that are required.
Given a tuplet
(H,w,\gamma)
, for each
p
, we have

(\gamma H+{w}{w}^{\dagger})F_{p}=\zeta_{p}F_{p}
or

{w}{w}^{\dagger}F_{p}=(\zeta_{p}I-\gamma H)F_{p},

(21)

which implies

\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}w=(\zeta_{p}I-\gamma H)F_{p}w.

(22)

If
\zeta_{p}\not\in\Sp(\gamma H)
holds, it clearly guarantees

\frac{F_{p}w}{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}=(\zeta_{p}I-\gamma H)^{-1}w,

(23)

which further yields

\frac{1}{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}=\sum_{r}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{(\zeta_{p}-\gamma\theta_{r})^{2}}.

(24)

Moreover, Equation (
23
) yields an expression of unity

1=\sum_{r=1}^{d}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\zeta_{p}-\gamma\theta_{r}}.

(25)

Returning to Equation (
22
), after multiplying both sides by
E_{1}
, we derive

\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}E_{1}w=(\zeta_{p}-\gamma)E_{1}F_{p}w.

(26)

By Theorem
4.2
, since
\left\lVert E_{1}w\right\rVert\neq 0
, we have strict interlacing where
\zeta_{2}<\gamma<\zeta_{1}
.
Furthermore,
\gamma\neq\zeta_{r}
, for all
r=1,\ldots,n
.
This allows us to write

E_{1}F_{p}w\ =\ \frac{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\zeta_{p}-\gamma}E_{1}w,\ \ \ \mbox{ $p=1,\ldots,m$.}

(27)

Now, analyzing the fidelity of the quantum walk with the Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}=\gamma H+{w}{w}^{\dagger}
, we get

f(t)\ =\ \Tr(ww^{\dagger}e^{-it\tilde{H}}E_{1}e^{it\tilde{H}})\ =\ \sum_{p,q}e^{-it(\zeta_{p}-\zeta_{q})}w^{\dagger}F_{p}E_{1}F_{q}w.

(28)

After expanding
w^{\dagger}F_{p}E_{1}F_{q}w
to
(w^{\dagger}F_{p}E_{1})(E_{1}F_{q}w)
since
E_{1}^{2}=E_{1}
,
we use Equation (
27
) to substitute
E_{1}F_{q}w
and
(E_{1}F_{p}w)^{\dagger}
to get

f(t)\ =\ \sum_{p}e^{-it\zeta_{p}}\frac{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\zeta_{p}-\gamma}\sum_{q}e^{it\zeta_{q}}\frac{\left\lVert F_{q}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\zeta_{q}-\gamma}\left\lVert E_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}

since
(w^{\dagger}E_{1})(E_{1}w)=\left\lVert E_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}
. Thus,

f(t)\ =\ \epsilon_{1}^{2}\left|\sum_{p}e^{-it\zeta_{p}}\frac{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}{(\zeta_{p}-\gamma)}\right|^{2}.

(29)

By triangle inequality, the above becomes

f(t)\ \leq\ \epsilon_{1}^{2}\left(\sum_{p}\frac{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}{|\zeta_{p}-\gamma|}\right)^{2}.

(30)

Finally, from Equation (
29
) at the time of origin
t=0
, we get another expression of unity,

1\ =\ \left|\frac{\left\lVert F_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\zeta_{1}-\gamma}-\sum_{p\geq 2}\frac{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\gamma-\zeta_{p}}\right|

(31)

since the first term
\left\lVert F_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}/(\zeta_{1}-\gamma)
and the subsequent terms
\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}/(\zeta_{p}-\gamma)
,
for
p\geq 2
, differ in sign because
\zeta_{1}>\gamma>\zeta_{p}
.
As
|A-B|\geq|A|-|B|
by triangle inequality,
the last equation immediately implies the following pair of inequalities:

\sum_{p\geq 2}\frac{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\gamma-\zeta_{p}}\leq\frac{\left\lVert F_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\zeta_{1}-\gamma}+1,\ \ \ \frac{\left\lVert F_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\zeta_{1}-\gamma}\leq\sum_{p\geq 2}\frac{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\gamma-\zeta_{p}}+1.

(32)

Using the above, we are ready to observe some necessary conditions for a graph
G
to be Groverian.
In the next theorem, we show some conditions on
\epsilon_{1}
in relation to the eigenvalue gaps
between the largest unperturbed eigenvalue of
\gamma H(G)
and the perturbed eigenvalues
\zeta_{m}

of
\gamma H(G)+{w}{w}^{\dagger}
.
In particular, for a graph
G
to be Groverian,

\epsilon_{1}
must be asymptotically equal to the first gap
\delta_{+}:=\zeta_{1}-\gamma
,
it must be asymptotically greater or equal to the second gap
\delta_{-}:=\gamma-\zeta_{2}
,
and
it must be asymptotically equal to some gap
\gamma-\zeta_{p}
, for some
p\geq 2
,
but not necessarily the second gap.
Recall that by strict interlacing, we know that
\gamma\theta_{2}<\zeta_{2}<\gamma<\zeta_{1}
,
where
\theta_{2}
is the second largest eigenvalue of
H(G)
.

The first two observations in the following theorem are implicit in the proof of
Theorem 4 in
[
7
]
but it will be useful to restate them in our notation below.
The third observation appears to be new.

5.1 Theorem

For a tuplet
(H,w,\gamma)
, suppose one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

\epsilon_{1}\not\asymp\zeta_{1}-\gamma
(equivalently,
\epsilon_{1}\ll\zeta_{1}-\gamma
or
\epsilon_{1}\gg\zeta_{1}-\gamma
), or

(ii)

\epsilon_{1}\ll\gamma-\zeta_{2}
, or

(iii)

\gamma-\zeta_{p-1}\ll\epsilon_{1}\ll\gamma-\zeta_{p}
, for some
p\geq 3
.

Then,
H
is not
\gamma
-Groverian.

Proof.

We treat each condition as a separate case.

Case (i)
: Denote
\delta_{+}:=\zeta_{1}-\gamma
and assume that
\delta_{+}\not\asymp\epsilon_{1}
.
From Equation (
30
), we apply Equation (
32
) to obtain the following upper bound

f(t)\ \leq\ \epsilon_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{2\left\lVert F_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\delta_{+}}+1\right)^{2}\ =\ \left(2\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{\delta_{+}}\left\lVert F_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}+\epsilon_{1}\right)^{2},

(33)

which shows that fidelity goes to zero if
\epsilon_{1}\ll\delta_{+}
.
For the opposite direction, apply Equation (
24
) with
p=1
to get

\frac{1}{\left\lVert F_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}}\ =\ \sum_{r=1}^{d}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{(\zeta_{1}-\gamma\theta_{r})^{2}}.

But, the sum is bounded from below by its first term, and so

\left\lVert F_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}\leq\delta_{+}^{2}/\epsilon_{1}^{2}
.
Returning to Equation (
33
) and using the preceding upper bound,

f(t)\leq\left(\frac{2\delta_{+}}{\epsilon_{1}}+\epsilon_{1}\right)^{2},

(34)

which shows that fidelity goes to zero if
\delta_{+}\ll\epsilon_{1}
.

Case (ii)
:
Let
\delta_{-}:=\zeta_{2}-\gamma
.
We start with Equation (
30
)
and use Equation (
31
) to rewrite a term in the upper bound as

\sum_{p}\frac{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}{|\gamma-\zeta_{p}|}\ \leq\ 1+2\sum_{p\geq 2}\frac{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\gamma-\zeta_{p}}\ \leq\ 1+2\frac{(1-\left\lVert F_{1}w\right\rVert^{2})}{|\delta_{-}|},

(35)

since
|\delta_{-}|\leq\gamma-\zeta_{p}
for each
p\geq 2
.
Thus, we derive an upper bound on the fidelity,

f(t)\leq\left(\frac{2\epsilon_{1}}{|\delta_{-}|}(1-\left\lVert F_{1}w\right\rVert^{2})+\epsilon_{1}\right)^{2},

(36)

which shows that fidelity tends to zero if
\epsilon_{1}\ll|\delta_{-}|
.

Case (iii)
:
Starting with Equation (
21
), after multiplying by
E_{1}
, we derive

E_{1}{w}{w}^{\dagger}F_{p}=(\zeta_{p}-\gamma)E_{1}F_{p}.

Taking a product with itself but for index
q
, we get

\epsilon_{1}^{2}F_{q}{w}{w}^{\dagger}F_{p}=(\zeta_{q}-\gamma)(\zeta_{p}-\gamma)F_{q}E_{1}F_{p}.

Upon taking the trace, this yields

\epsilon_{1}^{2}\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}=(\zeta_{p}-\gamma)^{2}\langle F_{p},E_{1}\rangle.

(37)

By Equation (
28
), we have

\sum_{p,q}e^{-it(\zeta_{p}-\zeta_{q})}w^{\dagger}F_{p}E_{1}F_{q}w=\sum_{p,q}e^{-it(\zeta_{p}-\zeta_{q})}\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{(\zeta_{p}-\gamma)}\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{(\zeta_{q}-\gamma)}\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}\left\lVert F_{q}w\right\rVert^{2},

where we have used Equation (
27
) twice (for both
p
and
q
).
Now, note we may apply Equation (
37
) to “flip” one of the ratios as follows:

\sum_{p,q}e^{-it(\zeta_{p}-\zeta_{q})}\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{\zeta_{p}-\gamma}\frac{\zeta_{q}-\gamma}{\epsilon_{1}}\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}\langle F_{q},E_{1}\rangle.

This allows us to partition the sums around
m
based on whether
\epsilon_{1}\ll\zeta_{p}-\gamma
or
\epsilon_{1}\gg\zeta_{p}-\gamma
.
In all cases, the corresponding terms tend to
0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

5.1
Failure around
S_{1}

We describe necessary conditions on
\gamma
relative to
S_{1}
.
First, we show failure whenever
\gamma
is too large relative to
S_{1}

(which is an alternate restatement of the first half of Theorem 4 in
[
7
]
).

5.2 Theorem

For a tuplet
(H,w,\gamma)
,
if
\epsilon_{1}S_{1}\ll\gamma-S_{1}
, then
H
is not
\gamma
-Groverian.

Proof.

Starting with Equation (
25
) with
p=1
, we see

1\ =\ \sum_{r=1}^{d}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\zeta_{1}-\gamma\theta_{r}}\ =\ \frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\delta_{+}}+\sum_{r=2}^{d}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\gamma(1-\theta_{r})+\delta_{+}}\ \leq\ \frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\delta_{+}}+\frac{S_{1}}{\gamma}.

After minor rearrangements, we obtain

\delta_{+}\ \leq\ \epsilon_{1}^{2}\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-S_{1}}\ =\ \epsilon_{1}^{2}\left(1+\frac{S_{1}}{\gamma-S_{1}}\right)\ \ll\ \epsilon_{1}(1+o_{n}(1))

(38)

which proves the claim by appealing to Theorem
5.1
, case
(i)
.
∎

Next, we show failure conditions when
\gamma
is centered around
S_{1}
.
We will use this later to show that cycles are not Groverian.

5.3 Theorem

Let
(H,w,\gamma)
be a tuplet where there is a constant
c>0
so that
\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert\leq c\epsilon_{1}
, for all
r\geq 2
.
Let

I_{\alpha}=\{r\geq 2:\gamma<\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}(1-\theta_{r})^{-1}\}.

If
\gamma=S_{1}(1+o_{n}(1))
and
|I_{\alpha}|\geq 2c^{2}
, for some
\alpha\in(1,2)
,
then
H
is not
\gamma
-Groverian.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.1
, it suffices to show
\delta_{+}\ll\epsilon_{1}
,
where
\delta_{+}=\zeta_{1}-\gamma
.
Let
\Delta_{r}=1-\theta_{r}
.
Our plan is to prove
\delta_{+}=O_{n}(\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha})
for the given
\alpha\in(1,2)
.
By Lemma
4.4
, it is enough to show
\phi(H,\gamma+\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha})>0
or
equivalently

1\ >\ \sum_{r=1}^{d}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\gamma\Delta_{r}+\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}}\ =\ \epsilon_{1}^{2-\alpha}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{r\geq 2}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\Delta_{r}+(\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}/\gamma)}.

Let us focus on the last summation. After splitting the summation into two parts, we may bound
each part from above as follows,

\sum_{r\geq 2}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\Delta_{r}+(\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}/\gamma)}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\geq 2:\\
\Delta_{r}<\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}/\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{2\Delta_{r}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\geq 2:\\
\Delta_{r}\geq\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}/\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}.

So, to prove the claim, it suffices to show that

1\ >\ \epsilon_{1}^{2-\alpha}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{r\geq 2}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}-\frac{1}{2\gamma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\geq 2:\\
\Delta_{r}<\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}/\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}.

(39)

Assume
\gamma=S_{1}+\beta
for some
\beta=o_{n}(S_{1})
. Then, we require that

1\ >\ \epsilon_{1}^{2-\alpha}+\frac{1}{1+(\beta/S_{1})}\underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{S_{1}}\sum_{r\geq 2}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}\right)}_{=1}-\frac{1}{2\gamma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\geq 2:\\
\gamma<\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}/\Delta_{r}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\Delta_{r}},

(40)

which is equivalent to

\frac{1}{2\gamma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\geq 2:\\
\gamma<\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}/\Delta_{r}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}+o_{n}(1)\ >\ \epsilon_{1}^{2-\alpha}.

(41)

Therefore, we may omit the
o_{n}(1)
term as it suffices to satisfy

\frac{1}{2\gamma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\geq 2:\\
\gamma<\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}/\Delta_{r}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}\ >\ \epsilon_{1}^{2-\alpha}\ \ \mbox{ or }\ \ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\geq 2:\\
\gamma<\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}/\Delta_{r}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}\frac{\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}}{\gamma\Delta_{r}}\ >\ 2.

If
\epsilon_{1}\leq c\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert
, for all
r\geq 2
,
the claim follows as
|I_{\alpha}|\geq 2c^{2}
.
∎

5.2
Near Perfect Fidelity

When the fidelity is
1-o_{n}(1)
, we show that the principal perturbed eigenspace must have a significant
overlap with the subspace spanned by the principal eigenspace and the target state.

5.4 Theorem

Let
(H,w,\gamma)
be a tuplet.
If
H
is
\gamma
-Groverian with fidelity
1-o_{n}(1)
, then
the principal eigenvector of
\gamma H+{w}{w}^{\dagger}
is an equal superposition of

w
and the principal eigenvector of
H
, up to
o_{n}(1)
terms.

Proof.

Let
z_{1}
and
y_{1}
be the principal eigenvectors of
H
and
\gamma H+{w}{w}^{\dagger}
, respectively.
Thus,
E_{1}=z_{1}z_{1}^{\dagger}
and
F_{1}=y_{1}y_{1}^{\dagger}
.
Starting with Equation (
33
) and then applying Equation (
27
) with
p=1
, we get

f(t)\ \leq\ 4\frac{\left\lVert F_{1}w\right\rVert^{4}}{\delta_{+}^{2}}\left\lVert E_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}\ =\ 4\left\lVert E_{1}F_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}\ =\ 4|\langle y_{1},z_{1}\rangle|^{2}|\langle y_{1},w\rangle|^{2}.

If
f(t)=1-o_{n}(1)
, we obtain
1-o_{n}(1)\leq 2|\langle y_{1},z_{1}\rangle||\langle y_{1},w\rangle|
.
Since
|\langle y_{1},z_{1}\rangle|^{2}+|\langle y_{1},w\rangle|^{2}\leq 1+o_{n}(1)
, we have

0\leq(|\langle y_{1},z_{1}\rangle|-|\langle y_{1},w\rangle|)^{2}=o_{n}(1)
, which proves the claim.
∎

6
Sufficient Gap

In this section, we prove our optimal characterization of
S_{1}
-Groverian graphs under a simpler assumption.
First, we justify that the choice of
\gamma=S_{1}
is almost best possible.
By Equation (
25
) with
p=1
, we have

1=\frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\delta_{+}}+\sum_{r\neq 1}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\delta_{+}+\gamma\Delta_{r}}\asymp\epsilon_{1}+\sum_{r\neq 1}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\delta_{+}+\gamma\Delta_{r}}

as
\epsilon_{1}\asymp\delta_{+}

is a necessary condition due to Theorem
5.1
(i)
.
Recall that
f_{n}\asymp g_{n}
denotes that
f_{n}
and
g_{n}
are within constant factors of each other.
Therefore,

1-\epsilon_{1}\asymp\frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{r\neq 1}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}\frac{1}{1+(\epsilon_{1}/\gamma\Delta_{r})}.

If
\gamma\gtrsim\sqrt{S_{1}}
, since
\epsilon_{1}\ll\sqrt{S_{1}}\Delta_{2}
, we have
\epsilon_{1}/\gamma\Delta_{r}=o_{n}(1)
.
This shows

\gamma(1-\epsilon_{1})\asymp\sum_{r\neq 1}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}\frac{1}{1+(\epsilon_{1}/\gamma\Delta_{r})}=S_{1}(1+o_{n}(1)).

As
\epsilon_{1}=o_{n}(1)
, we have
\gamma\asymp S_{1}
.

The next theorem is our main characterization for optimal spatial search for
\gamma=S_{1}
.

6.1 Theorem

Let
(H,w,\gamma)
be a tuplet where
\theta_{2}\in\Supp_{H}(w)
.
If
\epsilon_{1}\ll\sqrt{S_{1}}\Delta_{2}
, then

H
is
S_{1}
-Groverian if and only if
S_{2}/S_{1}^{2}\asymp 1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.3
, we know that
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
are simple and
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\not\in\Sp(S_{1}H)
.
Therefore, let
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
be their corresponding eigenvectors.
Define
\delta_{+}=\zeta_{1}-S_{1}
and
\delta_{-}=\zeta_{2}-S_{1}
. Also, let
\Delta_{r}=1-\theta_{r}
.
Using Equation (
25
) with
p=1,2
, we derive

1\ =\ \sum_{r=1}^{d}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\zeta_{p}-S_{1}\theta_{r}}\ =\ \frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\delta_{\pm}}+\sum_{r=2}^{d}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{S_{1}\Delta_{r}}\frac{1}{1+\delta_{\pm}/(S_{1}\Delta_{r})}.

(42)

Note
(1+\alpha)^{-1}=1-\alpha+\alpha^{2}(1+\alpha)^{-1}
holds for all
\alpha\neq-1
.
Using
\alpha=\delta_{\pm}/(S_{1}\Delta_{r})
,
after reorganizing and cancelling terms, we arrive at

\frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\delta_{\pm}^{2}}\ =\ \frac{1}{S_{1}^{2}}\sum_{r=2}^{d}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\Delta_{r}^{2}}\frac{S_{1}\Delta_{r}}{\delta_{\pm}+S_{1}\Delta_{r}}.

(43)

Thus far, this is similar to the first portion of the proof of Theorem 2 in
[
7
]
.
But, now we exploit the sharper estimates given by Proposition
4.5
which will
considerably simplify the rest of our proof.

6.2 Lemma

\epsilon_{1}^{2}/\delta_{\pm}^{2}\sim S_{2}/S_{1}^{2}
.

Proof.

Proposition
4.5
shows that
|\delta_{\pm}|=O_{n}(\sqrt{S_{1}}\epsilon_{1})
,
and as
\epsilon_{1}\ll\sqrt{S_{1}}\Delta_{2}
, we get
|\delta_{\pm}|\ll S_{1}\Delta_{2}
.
Applying this to Equation (
43
) proves the claim.
∎

6.3 Lemma

2\epsilon_{1}^{2}/\delta_{+}^{2}\sim 1/\left\lVert F_{1}w\right\rVert^{2}

and

2\epsilon_{1}^{2}/\delta_{-}^{2}\sim 1/\left\lVert F_{2}w\right\rVert^{2}
.

Proof.

We apply Equation (
24
) to
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
, and after some rearrangements, we get

\frac{1}{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}=\frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\delta_{\pm}^{2}}+\frac{1}{S_{1}^{2}}\sum_{r=2}^{d}\frac{\left\lVert E_{r}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\Delta_{r}^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{\pm}/(S_{1}\Delta_{r}))^{2}}\ \ \ \mbox{ ($p=1,2$).}

(44)

Proposition
4.5
shows that
|\delta_{\pm}|=O_{n}(\sqrt{S_{1}}\epsilon_{1})
,
which combined with the assumption
\epsilon_{1}\ll\sqrt{S_{1}}\Delta_{2}
yields
|\delta_{\pm}|\ll S_{1}\Delta_{2}
.
Applying this to Equation (
44
), we obtain

\frac{1}{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}\ =\ \frac{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\delta_{\pm}^{2}}+\frac{S_{2}}{S_{1}^{2}}(1+o_{n}(1))\ \sim\ \frac{2\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{\delta_{\pm}^{2}}\ \ \ \mbox{ ($p=1,2$),}

(45)

as
\epsilon_{1}^{2}/\delta_{\pm}^{2}\sim S_{2}/S_{1}^{2}
by Lemma
6.2
.
∎

The use of density matrices simplifies the proof of the following result
as most arguments involving phase factors are no longer necessary.

6.4 Lemma

If
t=O_{n}(\sqrt{S_{2}}/S_{1}\epsilon_{1})
, then
f(t)=\Omega_{n}(S_{1}/\sqrt{S_{2}})
.

Proof.

By Equation (
26
), we have

E_{1}F_{p}w=\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}E_{1}w/(\zeta_{p}-S_{1})
.
For
p=1,2
,
using Lemma
6.3
to replace
\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}
, we obtain

\left\lVert E_{1}F_{p}w\right\rVert=\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{|\delta_{\pm}|}\sim\frac{|\delta_{\pm}|}{2\epsilon_{1}}.

(46)

Let
\delta=(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2})/2
. Then, the fidelity is given by

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Tr({w}{w}^{\dagger}\sum_{p,q}e^{-it(\zeta_{p}-\zeta_{q})}F_{p}E_{1}F_{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p,q}e^{-it(\zeta_{p}-\zeta_{q})}\langle E_{1}F_{p}w,E_{1}F_{q}w\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p,q}e^{-it(\zeta_{p}-\zeta_{q})}\epsilon_{1}^{2}\frac{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\zeta_{p}-S_{1}}\frac{\left\lVert F_{q}w\right\rVert^{2}}{\zeta_{q}-S_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1-\cos(2\delta t))\frac{\delta^{2}}{\epsilon_{1}^{2}}+\epsilon_{1}^{2}\sum_{p,q\neq 1,2}e^{-it(\zeta_{p}-\zeta_{q})}\frac{\left\lVert F_{p}w\right\rVert^{2}}{S_{1}-\zeta_{p}}\frac{\left\lVert F_{q}w\right\rVert^{2}}{S_{1}-\zeta_{q}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{2}}{\epsilon_{1}^{2}},\ \ \ \mbox{ by setting $t=\frac{\pi}{2}\delta^{-1}$.}

Now, note that
\delta=\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{+}+|\delta_{-}|)\asymp\epsilon_{1}
.
∎

We have shown that if
S_{1}^{2}/S_{2}=\Theta_{n}(1)
then
H
is
S_{1}
-Groverian.
It remains to show the converse.

6.5 Lemma

If
f(t)=\Omega_{n}(1)
, for some
t=O_{n}(1/\epsilon_{1})
, then
S_{2}/S_{1}^{2}=\Theta_{n}(1)
.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive. If
S_{2}/S_{1}^{2}\not\asymp 1
, or equivalently,
\epsilon_{1}^{2}\not\asymp\delta_{+}^{2}

by Lemma
6.2
, then
H
is not Groverian by Theorem
5.1
(i)
.
Note
\epsilon_{1}^{2}\asymp\delta_{+}^{2}
if and only if
\epsilon_{1}\asymp\delta_{+}
.
∎

Lemma
6.4
and
6.5
completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remarks
.
We now compare Theorem
6.1
in the context of the known results in
[
6
,
7
]
.
Notice that Theorem
6.1
implies the main result (Lemma 1) in Chakraborty
et al.

[
6
]
.
Their result requires the constant gap condition
\Delta_{2}=\Omega_{n}(1)
which is a stronger assumption
because of the following observation.
In what follows, we denote
\epsilon_{2}=\left\lVert E_{2}w\right\rVert
.

6.6 Fact

Suppose
\epsilon_{1}=o_{n}(1)
. If
\Delta_{2}=\Omega_{n}(1)
, then
S_{1}\Delta_{2}=\Theta_{n}(1)
.

Proof.

Note
\epsilon_{2}^{2}+\Delta_{2}(1-\epsilon_{1}^{2}-\epsilon_{2}^{2})\leq S_{1}\Delta_{2}\leq 1-\epsilon_{1}^{2}
.
The upper bound
S_{1}\Delta_{2}=O_{n}(1)
holds immediately.
If
\epsilon_{2}=o_{n}(1)
, then
S_{1}\Delta_{2}=\Omega_{n}(1)
follows as
\Delta_{2}=\Omega_{n}(1)
.
Otherwise,
S_{1}\Delta_{2}=\Omega_{n}(1)
holds from
\epsilon_{2}=\Omega_{n}(1)
.
∎

We also note the following relation between
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
(which can be compared to Lemma 5 in
[
7
]
).

6.7 Fact

S_{1}^{2}/S_{2}\leq 1-\epsilon_{1}^{2}
.

Proof.

Consider a random variable
Z
where
Z=1/\Delta_{r}
with probability

\epsilon_{r}^{2}/(1-\epsilon_{1}^{2})
, for
r=2,\ldots,d
.
Then,
\mathbb{E}[Z]=S_{1}/(1-\epsilon_{1}^{2})
and
\mathbb{E}[Z^{2}]=S_{2}/(1-\epsilon_{1}^{2})
.
Now,
\mathbb{E}[Z^{2}]\geq\mathbb{E}[Z]^{2}
since variance is always nonnegative.
∎

Next, observe that
S_{1}\geq 1-\epsilon_{1}^{2}
.
If
S_{1}\leq 1
, then both
S_{1}
and
\sqrt{S_{1}}
are constant; otherwise,
\sqrt{S_{1}}\leq S_{1}
.
To summarize, it is clear that
\sqrt{S_{1}}\lesssim S_{1}\lesssim\sqrt{S_{2}}
.

As for the main result in Chakraborty
et al.

[
7
]
, their theorem requires the assumption

\epsilon_{1}\ll S_{1}S_{2}/S_{3}
and
\epsilon_{1}\ll\sqrt{S_{2}}\Delta_{2}
.
In Theorem
6.1
, we replace these two assumptions with a single assumption

\epsilon_{1}\ll\sqrt{S_{1}}\Delta_{2}
. The latter assumption is only slightly stronger
than
\epsilon_{1}\ll\sqrt{S_{2}}\Delta_{2}
since under the regime of interest, namely,
S_{2}/S_{1}^{2}=\Theta_{n}(1)
,
they are asymptotically equivalent.

Cliques

The normalized eigenvalues of
K_{n}
are
1
(with multiplicity
1
) and
-1/(n-1)
(with multiplicity
n-1
).
So, cliques are Groverian (in fact, with fidelity
1
) since
\Delta_{2}
is constant.
This was, of course, the original observation of Farhi and Gutmann
[
4
]
.

Expanders

A graph
G=(V,E)
is called an
(n,d,c)
-expander if
G
has
n
vertices, maximum degree
d
, and for each set
of vertices
W
of size
|W|\leq n/2
, we have
|N(W)|\geq c|W|
, where
N(W)
is the set of vertices not in

W
but is adjacent to some vertex in
W
. Here,
c
is called the expansion which is required to be constant.
It is known that if
G
is
d
-regular on
n
vertices, then it is a
(n,d,\Delta_{2}/2)
-expander provided

\Delta_{2}=\Theta_{n}(1)
(see
[
15
]
). So, expanders are Groverian since
\Delta_{2}
is constant.
This is the main observation of Chakraborty
et al.

[
6
]
.

Strongly Regular Graphs

A graph
G_{n}
is called
strongly regular
with parameter
(n,k,a,c)

if it is a
k
-regular on
n
vertices where every pair of adjacent vertices have
a
common neighbors
and every pair of non-adjacent vertices have
c
common neighbors.
Let
\theta_{1}>\theta_{2}
be the non-principal eigenvalues.
Then,
k-c=\theta_{1}(-\theta_{2})
, which implies
\theta_{1}=(k-c)/(-\theta_{2})
.
If
G_{n}
is primitive but not a conference graph, then
-\theta_{2}\geq 2
.
Therefore,
\theta_{1}\leq(k-c)/2<k/2
. This shows that
\Delta_{2}
is constant.
On the other hand, if
G_{n}
is a conference graph then
k=(n-1)/2
and
\theta_{1}=(\sqrt{n}-1)/2

which implies
\Delta_{2}=1-o_{n}(1)
. Thus, strongly regular graphs are Groverian.
This provides an alternative proof of the result due to Janmark
et al.

[
8
]
.

Hamming graphs

The Hamming graph
H(n,q)
, with
q
is constant, has
(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{n}
as its vertices where
two
n
-tuples are connected if they differ in exactly one dimension.
As
H(n,q)
has
q^{n}
vertices with eigenvalues

\theta_{r}=n(q-1)-qr
with multiplicity
m_{r}=\binom{n}{r}(q-1)^{r}
, for
r=0,\ldots,n
,
we have
\Delta_{2}=q/n(q-1)\sim 1/n
and
\epsilon_{1}=1/q^{n/2}
.
Since
S_{1}=\Theta_{n}(1)
, the Hamming graph
H(n,q)
is Groverian as

\epsilon_{1}\ll\sqrt{S_{1}}\Delta_{2}
.
To see why
S_{1}=\Theta_{n}(1)
, notice that

S_{1}=\frac{1}{q^{n}}\sum_{r=0}^{n}\binom{n}{r}\frac{n(q-1)}{rq}(q-1)^{r}\sim\frac{1}{q^{n}}\sum_{r}\binom{n+1}{r+1}(q-1)^{r}\asymp 1.

For the
n
-cube or
H(n,2)
, this was observed by Childs and Goldstone
[
5
]
.

Johnson graphs

The Johnson graph
J(n,k)
has as its vertices the set of
k
-subsets of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
denoted
\binom{[n]}{k}
, where two
k
-subsets
A
and
B
are connected if
|A\cap B|=k-1
.
So,
J(n,k)
has
\binom{n}{k}
vertices with eigenvalues

\theta_{r}=(k-r)(n-k-r)-r
with multiplicity
m_{r}=\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n}{r-1}
, for
r=0,\ldots,k
.
Notice
\epsilon_{1}=1/\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}\sim n^{-k/2}
and

\Delta_{2}=\frac{\theta_{0}-\theta_{1}}{\theta_{0}-\theta_{k}}=\frac{n}{k(n-k+1)}\sim\frac{1}{k}

which is constant if
k
is.
As
\Delta_{2}=\Omega_{n}(1)
, this shows that
J(n,k)
, for
k\geq 3
, is Groverian.
This recovers the results of Wong
[
9
]
and Tanaka
et al.

[
10
]
.

Grassmann graphs

The Grassmann graph
G_{q}(n,k)
has as its vertices the set of
k
-subspaces of the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}

where two
k
-subspaces
A
and
B
are connected if
\dim(A\cap B)=k-1
.
We assume
n\geq 2k
.
The number of vertices of
G_{q}(n,k)
is

N=\mbox{$\begin{bmatrix}{n}\\
{k}\end{bmatrix}_{q}$}\sim q^{k(n-k)}.

The eigenvalues are given by

\theta_{r}=q^{r+1}\mbox{$\begin{bmatrix}{k-r}\\
{1}\end{bmatrix}_{q}$}\mbox{$\begin{bmatrix}{n-k-r}\\
{1}\end{bmatrix}_{q}$}-\mbox{$\begin{bmatrix}{r}\\
{1}\end{bmatrix}_{q}$}\sim q^{n-r-1}

with multiplicity

m_{r}=\mbox{$\begin{bmatrix}{n}\\
{r}\end{bmatrix}_{q}$}-\mbox{$\begin{bmatrix}{n}\\
{r-1}\end{bmatrix}_{q}$},

for
r=0,\ldots,k
.
Notice
\epsilon_{1}=1/\sqrt{N}
and the normalized eigenvalue gap is

\Delta_{2}=1-\frac{\theta_{1}}{\theta_{0}}\sim 1-\frac{1}{q},

which is constant if
q
is.
As
\Delta_{2}=\Omega_{n}(1)
, this shows that
G_{q}(n,k)
, for
k\geq 2
, is Groverian.
Note
k=1
recovers the cliques.

Distance-regular graphs with classical parameters

The eigenvalues of distance-regular graphs with classical parameters
(d,q,\alpha,\beta)

are given in Jurišić and Vidali
[
16
]
(see Lemma 2). In particular,

\theta_{0}=[d]_{q}\beta
and
\theta_{1}=[d-1]_{q}(\beta-\alpha)-1
, which implies

\Delta_{2}=1-\frac{[d-1]_{q}(\beta-\alpha)-1}{[d]_{q}\beta}\sim 1-\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q}\frac{\alpha}{\beta}

is constant provided
\alpha\ll\beta
.

Cycles

The results from Section
5
can be used to prove that cycles are not Groverian.
The normalized eigenvalues of
C_{n}
are given by

\theta_{r}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\cos(2\pi(r-1)/n))
,
r=1,\ldots,n
.
Using
\cos(x)\sim 1-x^{2}/2
, we have
\Delta_{r}\sim r^{2}/n^{2}
, for small positive values of
r
.
Since
C_{n}
is a circulant,
\epsilon_{r}\sim 1/\sqrt{n}
for all
r=1,\ldots,n
.
Note that

S_{1}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{1-\cos(2\pi r/n)}\sim 2\int_{2\pi/n}^{\pi-2\pi/n}\frac{dx}{1-\cos(x)}=2\cot(\pi/n)=\Theta_{n}(n).

Next, we show spatial search fails as
\gamma
ranges over all values.

Case (i).

\gamma=S_{1}+\omega_{n}(\sqrt{n})
:
Notice Theorem
5.2
applies since
\sqrt{n}=\epsilon_{1}S_{1}\ll\gamma-S_{1}=\omega_{n}(\sqrt{n})
.

Case (ii).

\gamma=S_{1}\pm o_{n}(n)
:
Here, Theorem
5.3
applies since
\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}/\Delta_{r}>S_{1}+\beta
holds, for
\alpha\in(1,2)
.
With
|\beta|=o_{n}(n)
, evidently
n^{-\alpha/2}\times n^{2}\ \gg\ cn\pm o_{n}(n)
.

Case (iii).

\gamma=O_{n}({n})
:
To apply Theorem
5.3
, we rewrite Equation (
39
) as

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\geq 2:\\
\Delta_{r}<\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}/\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{\epsilon_{r}^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}\ >\ \gamma\epsilon_{1}^{2-\alpha}+S_{1}-\gamma,

(47)

since
\gamma>0
.
Because
\gamma=O_{n}({n})
,
S_{1}=O_{n}(n)
, and
\epsilon_{1}=1/\sqrt{n}
, the right-hand side is at most
O_{n}(n)
.
Next, we determine the set of indices
r
so that
\Delta_{r}<\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}/\gamma
which are
included in the summation. The smallest the upper bound
\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}/\gamma
can be is
1/n^{1+\alpha/2}
.
We have

\Delta_{r}\sim\frac{r^{2}}{n^{2}}\ll\frac{1}{n^{1+\alpha/2}},\ \ \ \mbox{ for $r=O_{n}(1)$.}

Thus, if we restrict the indices for which
r\leq B
, for a large enough constant
B
, we get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\geq 2:\\
\Delta_{r}<\epsilon_{1}^{\alpha}/\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{\epsilon_{r}^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}\ \geq\ \sum_{r=2}^{B}\frac{\epsilon_{r}^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}\ \sim\ \sum_{r=2}^{B}\frac{n^{-1}}{r^{2}n^{-2}}\ =\ \Omega_{n}(n).

Thus, Equation (
47
) is satisfied provided
B
is large enough; whence spatial search fails.

8
Concluding remarks

In this work, we proved a simpler characterization of graphs with the optimal spatial search property
(therein called Groverian). This improves a previous characterization obtained by Chakraborty
et al.

[
7
]
.
We applied this characterization to recover known results about some families of Groverian graphs and also
to find new families of Groverian graphs.
Along the way, we also proved a lower bound for spatial search on arbitrary graphs for constant fidelity.
This extends a known lower bound due to Farhi and Gutmann
[
4
]
which holds for vertex transitive graphs
and for fidelity that is one. We also developed a family of necessary conditions for a graph to be Groverian.
Our necessary conditions, which are built upon observations developed by Chakraborty
et al.

[
7
]
, can
be applied to provide rigorous proofs to show why some families of graphs (for example, cycles)

We conclude with some open questions from the present work:

1.

Is the Groverian property determined by spectra?
Moreover, is the condition
\epsilon_{1}\ll\sqrt{S_{1}}\Delta_{2}
necessary for optimal characterization?

2.

Can a family of graphs be Groverian with non-constant
S_{1}
?
To the best of our knowledge, all families of graphs known to be Groverian satisfy
S_{1}=\Theta_{n}(1)
.

3.

When is the Groverian property (almost) periodic?

4.

How robust is the Groverian property against noise? Regev and Schiff

for the discrete-time case.

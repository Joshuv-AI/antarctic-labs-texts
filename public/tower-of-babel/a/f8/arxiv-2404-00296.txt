Special subsets of discrete groups and Arens regularity of ideals in Fourier and group algebras
Reza Esmailvandi 1 , Mahmoud Filali 2 , Jorge Galindo 3

Abstract
. Let 𝒜 \mathscr{A} be a weakly sequentially complete Banach algebra containing a bounded approximate identity that is an ideal in its second dual 𝒜 ∗ ⁣ ∗ \mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast} , we call such an algebra a W ​ e S ​ e ​ B ​ a ​ i W\raisebox{0.50003pt}{e}\raisebox{-0.50003pt}{S}e\raisebox{0.50003pt}{B}ai algebra. In the present paper we examine the Arens regularity properties of closed ideals of algebras in the W e S e B ai class. We observe that, although W e S e B ai algebras are always strongly Arens irregular, a variety of Arens regularity properties can be observed within their closed ideals. After characterizing Arens regular ideals and strongly Arens irregular ideals, we proceed to particularize to the main examples of W e S e B ai algebras, the convolution group algebras L 1 ​ ( G ) L^{1}(G) , G G compact, and the Fourier algebras A ⁡ ( Γ ) A(\Gamma) , Γ \Gamma discrete and amenable. We find examples of Arens regular ideals in L 1 ​ ( G ) L^{1}(G) and A ⁡ ( Γ ) A(\Gamma) , both reflexive and nonreflexive and examples of strongly Arens irregular ideals that are not in the W e S e B ai class. For this, we construct, in many noncommutative groups, a new class of Riesz sets which are not Λ ⁡ ( p ) \Lambda(p) , for any p > 1 p>1 . Our approach also shows that every infinite Abelian group contains a Rosenthal set that is not Λ ⁡ ( p ) \Lambda(p) , for any p > 0 p>0 . These latter results could be of independent interest.

[

These ideals need not possess bounded approximate identities and, because of that, many conventional approaches to Arens (ir-)regularity do not apply to them. However, it is still possible to use the bounded approximate identities of their ambient algebras to bring some of their structure back.

Let
\mathscr{A}

\mathscr{A}^{*}

\displaystyle\langle\varphi\cdot a,b\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi,ab\rangle,\qquad\qquad\langle a\cdot\varphi,b\rangle=\langle\varphi,ba\rangle

\displaystyle\langle m\cdot\varphi,a\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle m,\varphi\cdot a\rangle,\qquad\langle\varphi\cdot m,a\rangle=\langle m,a\cdot\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle\langle mn,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle m,n\cdot\varphi\rangle,\qquad\langle m\diamondsuit n,\varphi\rangle=\langle n,\varphi\cdot m\rangle.

When

for every

is said Arens regular.

We say that a bounded net

-limit

(\mathscr{A}^{**},\diamondsuit)

The

(e_{\alpha})

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a Banach algebra. Then the topological centres
\mathcal{Z}_{1}(\mathscr{A}^{**})
and
\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathscr{A}^{**})
of

are defined as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{1}(\mathscr{A}^{**})

\displaystyle=\{m\in\mathscr{A}^{**}:\text{the map}~n\mapsto mn~\text{is weak*-weak* continuous on }\mathscr{A}^{**}\}

\displaystyle=\{m\in\mathscr{A}^{**}:mn=m\diamondsuit n~\text{for all }~n\in\mathscr{A}^{**}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathscr{A}^{**})

\displaystyle=\{m\in\mathscr{A}^{**}:\text{the map}~n\mapsto n\diamondsuit m~\text{is weak*-weak* continuous on }\mathscr{A}^{**}\}

\displaystyle=\{m\in\mathscr{A}^{**}:nm=n\diamondsuit m~\text{for all }~n\in\mathscr{A}^{**}\}.

It is clear that
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{1}(\mathscr{A}^{**})\cap\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathscr{A}^{**})
. The algebra
\mathscr{A}
is therefore Arens regular if and only if

\mathcal{Z}_{1}(\mathscr{A}^{**})=\mathscr{A}^{**}=\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathscr{A}^{**}).

When
\mathscr{A}
is commutative both topological centers coincide with the algebraic center

\{m\in\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}\colon mn=nm\mbox{ for all }n\in\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}\}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a Banach algebra. Then
\mathscr{A}
is is called left strongly Arens irregular (lsAir) if

\mathcal{Z}_{1}(\mathscr{A}^{**})=\mathscr{A}
, right strongly Arens irregular (rsAir) if

\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathscr{A}^{**})=\mathscr{A}
, and strongly Arens irregular
(sAir) if

\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathscr{A}^{**})=\mathscr{A}=\mathcal{Z}_{1}(\mathscr{A}^{**}).

Remark 2.3
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a Banach
*

\mathscr{A}

\displaystyle m\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}(\mathscr{A}^{**})\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad m^{*}\in\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathscr{A}^{**}).

Hence, for a Banach

. lsAir, rsAir and sAir are equivalent.

In

Definition 4.1

Remarks 4.2

(i)

If

(ii)

By Theorem

In a Banach algebra with a bai, a closed ideal

\displaystyle m\cdot\varphi=(em)\cdot\varphi=a\cdot\varphi\quad\text{ for every}\;\varphi\in{\mathscr{J}}^{*},\;

\displaystyle\text{equivalently,}\;\varphi\cdot m=\varphi\cdot(m\diamondsuit e)=\varphi\cdot a\text{ for every}\;\;\varphi\in{\mathscr{J}}^{*}.

Proof.

If

Lemma 4.5

\displaystyle=|\langle\,m\cdot(n\cdot\varphi-a_{\varepsilon}\cdot\psi_{\varepsilon}),b\,\rangle|

5.
Arens regularity

We deal in this section with Arens regularity of closed ideals
{\mathscr{J}}
. The first theorem determines precisely when
{\mathscr{J}}
is Arens regular. It shows in particular that weak Riesz ideals characterize Arens regular ideals in any Banach algebra, which is an ideal in its second dual
\mathscr{A}^{**}
and has a bai. The equivalence of the first two statements of the theorem was
proved in
[
13
]
for closed ideals in the group algebra of a compact Abelian group.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a two-sided ideal in
\mathscr{A}^{**}
. Then for every
m,n,p\in\mathscr{A}^{**}
and
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}^{*}
, we have

(i)

(p\cdot\varphi)\cdot m=p\cdot(\varphi\cdot m).

(ii)

(m\diamondsuit n)p=m\diamondsuit(np).

Proof.

For the first statement,
let
a\in\mathscr{A}.
Then, using the facts that
ma\in\mathscr{A}
and
ap\in\mathscr{A}
, we find

\displaystyle\langle(p\cdot\varphi)\cdot m,a\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle p\cdot\varphi,m\diamondsuit a\rangle=\langle p\cdot\varphi,ma\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(ma)p,\varphi\rangle=\langle m(ap),\varphi\rangle=\langle m\diamondsuit(ap),\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle ap,\varphi\cdot m\rangle=\langle a,p\cdot(\varphi\cdot m)\rangle,

and so
(p\cdot\varphi)\cdot m=p\cdot(\varphi\cdot m),
as required.

The second statement follows from the first as

\displaystyle\langle(m\diamondsuit n)p,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle m\diamondsuit n,p\cdot\varphi\rangle=\langle n,(p\cdot\varphi)\cdot m\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle n,p\cdot(\varphi\cdot m)\rangle=\langle np,\varphi\cdot m\rangle=\langle m\diamondsuit(np),\varphi\rangle.

∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a Banach algebra, which is an ideal in its second dual
\mathscr{A}^{**}

and has a bai. Let
{\mathscr{J}}
be a closed ideal of
\mathscr{A}
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

{\mathscr{J}}
is Arens regular.

(ii)

i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{1}({\mathscr{J}}^{**})\cap\mathcal{Z}_{2}({\mathscr{J}}^{**}).

(iii)

{\mathscr{J}}
is a weak Riesz ideal in
\mathscr{A}.

(iv)

i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}{\mathscr{J}}^{\ast\ast}=\{0\}
and
{\mathscr{J}}^{\ast\ast}\diamondsuit i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}=\{0\}.

Proof.

There is no need to prove that (i) implies (ii).

We now prove that (ii) implies (iii).
So, let’s assume that
i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{1}({\mathscr{J}}^{**})
. Then,
for arbitrary
r\in i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}
,
m\in{\mathscr{J}}^{**}
and
\varphi\in{\mathscr{J}}^{*}
,
we have

\langle r,m\cdot\varphi\rangle=\langle rm,\varphi\rangle=\langle r\diamondsuit m,\varphi\rangle=0,

and so

m\cdot\varphi\in i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp\perp}=\overline{i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))}.

Similarly,
\varphi\cdot m\in\overline{i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))}
when

i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{2}({\mathscr{J}}^{**})
.

To see that (iii) implies (i), we
let
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
be arbitrarily chosen in
{\mathscr{J}}^{**}
.

Then, for every
\varphi\in{\mathscr{J}}^{*},
we have

\displaystyle\langle m_{1}m_{2},\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle m_{1},m_{2}\cdot\varphi\rangle=\langle m_{1}\diamondsuit e,m_{2}\cdot\varphi\rangle=\langle(m_{1}\diamondsuit e)m_{2},\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(m_{1}\diamondsuit(em_{2}),\varphi\rangle=\langle(em_{2}),\varphi\cdot m_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle m_{2},\varphi\cdot m_{1}\rangle=\langle m_{1}\diamondsuit m_{2},\varphi\rangle,

where the second and sixth equalities are due to the assumption that

{{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{*}}\;\cup\;{{\mathscr{J}}^{*}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{**}}\subseteq\overline{i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))},

and the fourth equality is due to Lemma

5.1
.
Therefore,
m_{1}m_{2}=m_{1}\diamondsuit m_{2}
and Arens regularity of
{\mathscr{J}}
follows.

We have now checked that Statements (i) through (iii) are equivalent.

Since elements of
i^{\ast}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}
are right annihilators for the first Arens product and left annihilators for the second, we see that statements (ii) and (iv) are clearly equivalent and the theorem is proved.
∎

A Riesz ideal in
\mathscr{A}
is clearly Arens regular. This can be either deduced directly
from the decomposition (
3.8
), or from the theorem above, for Riesz ideals are weakly Riesz.
When

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a Banach algebra with a bai, and suppose that it is an ideal in its second dual
\mathscr{A}^{**}
.
Let
{\mathscr{J}}
be a small-1-1 ideal of
\mathscr{A}
. Then

{\mathscr{J}}^{**}{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{1}({\mathscr{J}}^{**})\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\diamondsuit{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{2}({\mathscr{J}}^{**}).

In particular,
{\mathscr{J}}
is sAir if and only if it is reflexive.

Proof.

We show first that
i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{1}({\mathscr{J}}^{**}).

Fix a mixed identity
e
of
\mathscr{A}^{**}
. Let

p=rm
with
m\in{\mathscr{J}}^{**}
and
r\in i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}
.
Then for every

and

, we find

(em)(en)=e(mn)\in{\mathscr{J}}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{1}({\mathscr{J}}^{**}),

and so

p(en+s)=(rm)n=r(emn)=0.

On the other hand, since
\varphi\cdot(rm)=0
for all
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}^{*}
, we have

p\diamondsuit(en+s)=p\diamondsuit(en+s)=0.

Hence

and the containment

is proved.

Since, by the decomposition of Theorem

{\mathscr{J}}^{**}{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\subseteq{\mathscr{J}}+i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}{\mathscr{J}}^{**},

(5.1)

the first inclusion in the theorem follows.

The inclusion
{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\diamondsuit{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{2}({\mathscr{J}}^{**})

is proved in the same manner.

Finally, since
i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\subseteq{\mathscr{J}}^{\ast\ast}{\mathscr{J}}^{\ast\ast}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{1}({\mathscr{J}}^{\ast\ast})

and
WAP(\mathscr{A})^{\perp}\mathscr{A}^{\ast\ast}\cap\mathscr{A}=\{0\}

In the same way,
{\mathscr{J}}
can only be rsAir when

{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\diamondsuit i^{*}(WAP(\mathscr{A}))^{\perp}=\{0\}.

Proposition
5.2
then shows that

must be Arens regular and, hence, reflexive.

∎

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a Banach algebra and let
{\mathscr{J}}
be a closed ideal of
\mathscr{A}
.

(i)

If the linear span of
{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{*}
is dense in
{\mathscr{J}}^{*},
then
\mathcal{Z}_{1}({\mathscr{J}}^{**})\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{1}(\mathscr{A}^{**}).

(ii)

If the linear span of
{\mathscr{J}}^{*}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{**}
is dense in
{\mathscr{J}}^{*}
,
then
\mathcal{Z}_{2}({\mathscr{J}}^{**})\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathscr{A}^{**}).

(iii)

If the linear span of
{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{*}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{**}
is dense in
{\mathscr{J}}^{*}
,
then
\mathcal{Z}_{i}({\mathscr{J}}^{**})\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{i}(\mathscr{A}^{**}),

i=1,2.

Proof.

We shall prove the first statement, the second statement is proved in the same manner.
Let
m\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}({\mathscr{J}}^{**})
,
p\in\mathscr{A}^{**}
and

\varphi\in\mathscr{A}^{*}
. Assume first that
i^{*}(\varphi)=n\cdot i^{*}(\psi)
for some
\psi\in\mathscr{A}^{*}
and
n\in{\mathscr{J}}^{**}
. Then, we find

\displaystyle\langle mp,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle mp,i^{*}(\varphi)\rangle=\langle mp,n\cdot i^{*}(\psi)\rangle=\langle(mp)n,i^{*}(\psi)\rangle=\langle m(pn),i^{*}(\psi)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle m\diamondsuit(pn),i^{*}(\psi)\rangle=\langle(m\diamondsuit p)n,i^{*}(\psi)\rangle=\langle m\diamondsuit p,n\cdot i^{*}(\psi)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle m\diamondsuit p,i^{*}(\varphi)\rangle=\langle m\diamondsuit p,\varphi\rangle,

using the fact that
{\mathscr{J}}^{**}
is an ideal in
\mathscr{A}^{**}
for the first and last equalities, and
m\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}({\mathscr{J}}^{**})
for the fifth and Lemma
5.1
for the sixth.

The condition on the linear span of
{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{*}
being dense in
{\mathscr{J}}^{*}

yields then

mp=m\diamondsuit p\quad\text{for every}\quad p\in\mathscr{A}^{**},

that is,
m\in Z_{1}(\mathscr{A}^{**}).
Thus
\mathcal{Z}_{1}({\mathscr{J}}^{**})\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{1}(\mathscr{A}^{**}).

To prove Statement (iii), recall that
({\mathscr{J}}^{**}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{*})\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{**}={\mathscr{J}}^{**}\cdot({\mathscr{J}}^{*}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{**})
by Lemma
5.1
(i).
So, if the linear span of
{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{*}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{**}
is dense in
{\mathscr{J}}^{*}
,
then each of the linear spans of
{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{*}
and
{\mathscr{J}}^{*}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{**}
is dense in
{\mathscr{J}}^{*}
.
Statements (i) and (ii) imply therefore that
\mathcal{Z}({\mathscr{J}}^{**})\subseteq Z(\mathscr{A}^{**}).

∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a W
e
S
e
B
ai

{\mathscr{J}}
be a closed ideal of
\mathscr{A}
.

(i)

If
{\mathscr{J}}
is left faithful, then
{\mathscr{J}}
is lsAir if and only if the linear span of
{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{*}
is dense in
{\mathscr{J}}^{*}.

(ii)

If
{\mathscr{J}}
is right faithful, then
{\mathscr{J}}
is rsAir if and only if the linear span of
{\mathscr{J}}^{*}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{**}
is dense in
{\mathscr{J}}^{*}
.

(iii)

If
{\mathscr{J}}
is either left faithful or right faithful, then
{\mathscr{J}}
is sAir if and only if the linear span of
{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{*}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{**}

{\mathscr{J}}^{*}.

Proof.

We prove Statement (i), the second statement follows in the same way.
Suppose that the linear span of
{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{*}
is dense in
{\mathscr{J}}^{*}.

Then by lemma
6.1
,
\mathcal{Z}_{1}({\mathscr{J}}^{**})\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{1}(\mathscr{A}^{**}).

Since

is lsAir by
[
3

\mathcal{Z}_{1}({\mathscr{J}}^{**})={\mathscr{J}}
.

For the converse, suppose that the linear span of
{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{*}
is not dense in
{\mathscr{J}}^{*}

and pick a nonzero
m\in{\mathscr{J}}^{**}
which is zero on
{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{*}
.
Then clearly
mn=0
for every
n\in{\mathscr{J}}^{**}
.
On the other hand,

\langle\varphi\cdot m,a\rangle=\langle m,a\cdot\varphi\rangle=0\quad\text{}\quad\varphi\in{\mathscr{J}}^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad a\in{\mathscr{J}}.

It follows that

, and so

Therefore

Note that

is not lsAir, as required.

As for Statement (iii), we apply again Lemma

by Statement (i). So

Thus

on

is lsAir. Therefore,

Since in particular

In the same manner, using the fact that

is right faithful.

In either cases, we conclude that
{\mathscr{J}}^{**}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{*}\cdot{\mathscr{J}}^{**}
must be dense in
{\mathscr{J}}^{*}
.

Extreme non-Arens regularity

As seen in Proposition

B

For this, we check that these ideals satisfy the conditions required in
[
16
, Corollary 3.10]

for a Banach algebra to be enAr.

The reader is directed for
[
16
]
or
[
17
]

for definitions required in the proofs.

Definition 7.1
.

\ell^{1}
-base
: Let
E
be a normed space. A bounded sequence
B=\{a_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an
\ell^{1}
-basis
in
E
, with constant
K>0
,
when the inequality

\sum_{n=1}^{p}|z_{k}|\leq K\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{p}z_{k}a_{n_{k}}\right\|

holds for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
and for every possible choice of scalars
z_{1},\ldots,z_{p}
and elements
a_{n_{1}},\ldots,a_{n_{p}}
in
B.

For the next proof, we need to recall the well-known theorem of Rosenthal
[
50
, Theorem 1]
, which asserts that a bounded sequence in a Banach space, always has a subsequence that is either weakly Cauchy or is an
\ell^{1}
-basis.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a non-unital Banach algebra in the W
e
S
e
B
ai
class whose bai
(e_{n})_{n}
is sequential,
and let
{\mathscr{J}}
be an ideal in
\mathscr{A}
that is not small-1-1. Then
there is a linear bounded map from the quotient space
{\mathscr{J}}^{*}/WAP({\mathscr{J}})
onto
\ell^{\infty}.

In particular,
{\mathscr{J}}
is non-Arens regular.
If
{\mathscr{J}}
is in addition separable, then
{\mathscr{J}}
is enAr.

Proof.

Let
e
be a mixed identity associated with
(e_{n})_{n}

, and pick
p,q\in{\mathscr{J}}^{**}
such that
epq\notin{\mathscr{J}}
.
Since
{\mathscr{J}}
is an ideal in
\mathscr{A}
, we see that the sequence
(e_{n}pq)_{n}
is in
{\mathscr{J}}
. This sequence cannot have any weakly Cauchy subsequence.
For, otherwise, there would exist a subsequence
(e_{n_{k}}pq)_{k}
with weak limit
a
and, due to weak sequential completeness,
a\in{\mathscr{J}}
, i.e.,

\lim_{n}e_{n_{k}}pq=a
in the weak topology
\sigma(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{A}^{*})
(the limit
a
is in
{\mathscr{J}}
since
{\mathscr{J}}
is convex and so weakly closed).
So if
f
is any mixed identity associated with
(e_{n_{k}})_{k}
, this implies that
fpq=a\in{\mathscr{J}}.

Therefore,

epq=(ef)pq=e(fpq)=ea=a\in{\mathscr{J}},

whence a contradiction.
Hence, by Rosenthal theorem we can assume, upon taking a suitable subsequence, that
(e_{n}pq)_{n}
is an
\ell^{1}
-base. Let then

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\{a_{n}=e_{n}p~:n\in\mathbb{N}\}

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\{b_{n}=e_{n}q~:n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

The sets
A
and
B
are in
{\mathscr{J}}
since
{\mathscr{J}}
is an ideal in
\mathscr{A}.

Consider as well the upper and lower triangles defined by
A
and
B
, given by

\displaystyle T^{u}_{AB}

\displaystyle=\left\{a_{n}b_{m}\colon\;n<m\right\}\;\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle T^{l}_{AB}

\displaystyle=\left\{a_{n}b_{m}\colon\;m<n\right\}.

Define

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=\{x_{nm}=e_{n}pq~:n<m\}

\displaystyle X_{2}

\displaystyle=\{x_{nm}=e_{m}pq~:m<n\}.

Since
{\mathscr{J}}
is an ideal in
\mathscr{A}
, we see that
e_{n}p\in{\mathscr{J}}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and so we have

\displaystyle\lim_{m}\|x_{nm}-a_{n}b_{m}\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{m}\|e_{n}pq-(e_{n}p)(e_{m}q)\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m}\|e_{n}p-(e_{n}p)e_{m}\|\|q\|=0

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus,

\lim_{m}\|x_{nm}-a_{n}b_{m}\|=0\;\text{for each}\;n\in\mathbb{N}.

The same argument gives

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\|x_{nm}-a_{n}b_{m}\|=0\;\text{for each}\;m\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence the sets
X_{1},X_{2}
,
approximate, respectively, segments in
T^{u}_{AB}
and
T^{l}_{AB}
in the sense of
[
16
, Definition 3.3]
. Since
(e_{n}pq)_{n}
is an
\ell^{1}
-base as well,
X_{1}
is horizontally injective according to
[
16
, Definition 3.2]
).
Similarly,
X_{2}
is vertically injective. Now,
[
16
, Corollary 3.10]
completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 8.1

Let

For any

8.1.
Infinite dimensional reflexive ideals

To describe reflexive ideals in
A(\Gamma)
, we will make use of the non-commutative

-spaces,
L^{p}(\Gamma)
,
p\geq 1
.
If
\tau
is the canonical trace in
VN(\Gamma)
, given by
\tau(T)=\langle\,T\delta_{e},\delta_{e}\,\rangle
(
\delta_{e}\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
), the norm of

is defined by

\|T\|_{p}^{p}=\tau(|T|^{p})

and
L^{p}(\Gamma)
is made of operators in

for which this norm is finite. If

,
the translation operator by

, is always in

.

We refer to
[

]
for a summary of properties of noncommutative

-spaces. In particular that
L^{1}(\Gamma)
is isometrically isomorphic to the predual of

, i.e., to
A(\Gamma)
.

We start our work with a definition.

Definition 8.2
.

Let
G
and
\Gamma
be, as fixed above, a compact group and a discrete group, respectively.

•

We say that
E\subseteq\widehat{G}
is a
\Lambda(p)
-set,

, if there are
0<q<p
and
C>0
such that

\|P\|_{p}\leq C\,\|P\|_{q},

for every linear combination
P
of matrix coefficients of representations belonging to
E
i.e., for every trigonometric polynomial

supported on
E
.

•

We say that
E\subseteq\Gamma
is a
\Lambda(p)
-set,
p>2
, if there are
1<q<p
and
C>0
such that

\|f\|_{\mbox{\tiny$L^{p}(\Gamma)$}}\leq C\,\|f\|_{\mbox{\tiny$L^{q}(\Gamma)$}},

for every linear combination

of translation operators by elements of
E
.

The notion of

-set was introduced by Rudin
[
51
]
for Abelian groups and by Picardello
[
43
]
for discrete noncommutative groups. Sections 0 and 1 in
[
24
]
provide a good introduction to

-sets. Note that a set that is

for some

is automatically

for every

. It is a consequence of Hölder’s inequality that if

is a

-set,

, then

and

. The proof of this can be found in
[
27
, Theorem 37.7]
. The same proof works in
L^{p}(\Gamma)
replacing Hölder’s inequality in

for Hölder’s inequality in
L^{p}(\Gamma)
(see the introduction of
[
44
]
). In particular both
L^{1}_{E}(G)
and
L^{1}_{E}(\Gamma)
are reflexive if
E
is a
\Lambda(p)
-set.

We summarize here our last remark.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
E\subset\Gamma
(
E\subset\widehat{G}
). If
E

is a
\Lambda(p)
-set,

, then

(respectively

) is reflexive.

In the case of compact groups, this can be pushed to a characterization. Although the paper
[
26
]
is on Abelian groups, the proof actually works for all compact groups.

Theorem 8.4

(Hare,
[
26
]
)
.

If
E\subset\widehat{G}
,
G
a compact group, then
L_{E}^{1}(G)
is reflexive if and only if

is a
\Lambda(1)
-set.

While not every algebra in W
e
S
e
B
ai
contains a reflexive infinite dimensional ideal, see Theorem

below, many of them indeed do. A theorem of Picardello to the effect that every discrete group contains an infinite

-set can be used, along with Theorem

, to see that Fourier algebras of discrete groups are among them.

Theorem 8.5

(Theorem 1 of
[
43
]
, see also
[

]
)
.

Every discrete group
\Gamma
contains an infinite subset

is reflexive.

In the case of

the situation is more complicated. Denote by

the set of (equivalences classes of) irreducible unitary representations of dimension
n
. We say that

is

is finite for every

Theorem 8.6
.

Let

be a compact group. If

\widehat{G}
contains an infinite subset

such that

is reflexive.
If

Proof.

The first statement is proved in

.

Giulini and Travaglini prove in

8.2.
Arens-regular ideals that are not reflexive

Proposition
5.2
shows that Riesz ideals of W
e
S
e
B
ai
algebras are Arens regular. An ideal
L_{E}^{1}(G)
in
L^{1}(G)
with
E\subseteq\widehat{G}
, is a Riesz ideal precisely when
M_{E}(G)=L_{E}^{1}(G)
, i.e., when
E
is a
Riesz
set. The same can be said about ideals
A_{E}(\Gamma)
in
A(\Gamma)
, cf. Section
4

The eponymous theorem of the Riesz brothers at the origin of the term Riesz set, shows that
L^{1}_{\mathbb{N}}(\mathbb{T})
is an Arens regular ideal of
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and it is certainly not reflexive. The literature contains several constructions of Riesz sets that are inspired by this classical result but which do not part much from its original spirit, see e.g.,
[
4
,
41
]
and the survey paper
[
35
]
.
Blei, see, e.g.,
[
5
]
constructs Riesz sets of a different sort, they are, actually, sets with even stronger interpolation properties that are not
\Lambda(p)
for any
p
. His examples, however, cannot be constructed in every Abelian group. We present here a rather general construction, partly based on Blei’s, that yields examples of Riesz sets
E\subseteq\Gamma
, and hence of Arens regular ideals in
A_{E}(\Gamma)
,
\Gamma
not necessarily commutative, so that
A_{E}(\Gamma)
is not reflexive. When put together with known results, this will be used to show that every Abelian discrete group contains a Riesz set (even a Rosenthal set) that is not
\Lambda(1)
.

We begin with a few preliminaries, starting with the following Lemma
that basically follows from
[
22
, Lemma 1.1]
. Recall (see
[
30
]
or
[
42
]
) that
B(\Gamma)=A(\Gamma)\oplus B_{s}(\Gamma)
where
B_{s}(\Gamma)
is a closed translation invariant subspace of
B(\Gamma)
. If
\phi=\phi_{a}+\phi_{s}\in B(\Gamma)
, then

\|\phi\|=\|\phi_{a}\|+\|\phi_{s}\|.

This is the Lebesgue decomposition in
B(\Gamma)
.

Lemma 8.7
.

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group and
E\subseteq\Gamma
. Then either
E
is a Riesz set or there exist
x\in E

and

\phi\in B_{E}(\Gamma)
such that
\phi(x)=0
and
\phi_{s}(x)\neq 0.

Proof.

If
E
is not Riesz,
there is
\psi\in B_{E}(\Gamma)
with
\psi_{s}\neq 0
. We pick
x\in E
such that
\psi_{s}(x)\neq 0
and define

\phi:=\psi-\psi(x)\delta_{x}\in B_{E}(\Gamma)
. Clearly,
\phi(x)=0.

If
\phi_{s}(x)=0
, the equality
\phi_{s}=\psi_{s}

yields the contradictory equality
0\neq\psi_{s}(x)=\phi_{s}(x)=0
.
∎

The following definition marks the target of our construction.

Definition 8.8
.

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group and
K
be a locally compact group. We will say that a countable set
E\subset\Gamma
is
convergently embedded
in
K
if there is a group isomorphism
\alpha\colon\langle\,E\,\rangle\to K
with dense range so that
\alpha(E)
is relatively compact and has exactly only one limit point
p\in K
,
p\notin\alpha(E)
.

When
\Gamma
is Abelian, Blei
[
5
]
proves that every set
E
that is convergently embedded in some compact Abelian group
K
is a
Rosenthal set
, i.e., every essentially bounded function on
G
with Fourier transform supported on
E
is equal almost everywhere to a continuous function (that is,
L^{\infty}_{E}(G)=C_{E}(G)
), see
[
5
, Theorem B]
. Rosenthal sets are known to be Riesz sets,
[
12
,
39
]
. We now set to extend this to the Fourier algebra of non-Abelian groups and see that such sets can be found in a wide class of groups.

Theorem 8.9
.

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group. If
E\subset\Gamma
is convergently embedded in
K
for some locally compact group
K
, then
E
is a Riesz set.

Proof.

We will let
p
denote the only limit point of
\alpha(E)
in
K
.

Assume
E
is not Riesz. By Lemma
8.7
, there is
x_{0}\in E
and
\phi\in B_{E}(\Gamma)
such that
\phi(x_{0})=0
and
\phi_{s}(x_{0})\neq 0
. Let
U\subseteq K
be a
neighbourhood of
p
with
\alpha(x_{0})\notin U
.

By Lemma 3.1 of
[
38
]
, there are, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a neighbourhood
V_{n}
of
p
and

u_{n}\in A(K)
such that

{\left.\kern-1.2ptu_{n}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{V_{n}}}=1,\quad{\left.\kern-1.2ptu_{n}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{G\setminus U}}=0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|u_{n}\|\leq 1+\frac{1}{n}.

Since
\overline{\alpha(E)}
is compact and
p
is the only limit point of
\alpha(E)
,
\alpha(E)\setminus V_{n}
has to be finite. Let
F_{n}
be the inverse image of this set under
\alpha
.
F_{n}
is therefore a finite subset of
E
with
\alpha(F_{n})=\alpha(E)\setminus V_{n}
. Define next

\phi_{n}:=\phi-\sum_{x\in F_{n}}\phi(x)\delta_{x}\in B(\Gamma).

Then
\supp\phi_{n}\subseteq E\cap\alpha^{-1}(V_{n})
. Define now
\underline{u_{n}}
to be
u_{n}\circ\alpha
on
\langle\,E\,\rangle
and 0 elsewhere. Then
\underline{u_{n}}\in B(\Gamma)
, and we have

\phi_{n}(1-\underline{u_{n}})=0.

(8.1)

Observe as well that, by
[
32
, Theorem 2.2.1 (ii)]

\|\underline{u_{n}}\|=\|u_{n}\|\leq 1+\frac{1}{n}.

Let for each
n
,
\phi_{n}=\left(\phi_{n}\right)_{a}+\left(\phi_{n}\right)_{s}
be its Lebesgue decomposition. Since
\sum_{x\in F_{n}}\phi(x)\delta_{x}\in A(\Gamma)
, we know that

\left(\phi_{n}\right)_{s}=\phi_{s}
.

From (
8.1
), we deduce then that

\phi_{s}(1-\underline{u_{n}})=-\left(\phi_{n}\right)_{a}(1-\underline{u_{n}})\in A(\Gamma).

(8.2)

We then deduce that, since
\phi_{s}\underline{u_{n}}=\phi_{s}-\phi_{s}(1-\underline{u_{n}})
, the first summand being singular and the second being in
A(\Gamma)
,

\|\phi_{s}\underline{u_{n}}\|=\|\phi_{s}\|+\|\phi_{s}(1-\underline{u_{n}})\|.

Thence,

\displaystyle\|\phi_{s}(1-\underline{u_{n}})\|

\displaystyle=\|\phi_{s}\underline{u_{n}}\|-\|\phi_{s}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{s}\|\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)-\|\phi_{s}\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\|\phi_{s}\|.

Now,

\|\phi_{s}\left(1-\underline{u_{n}}\right)\|\geq\|\phi_{s}(1-\underline{u_{n}})\|_{\infty}\geq\left|\phi_{s}(x_{0})\right|.

By taking
n
large enough so that
\left|\phi_{s}(x_{0})\right|>\dfrac{1}{n}\|\phi_{s}\|
, the desired contradiction is reached.
∎

We will need the following result that follows from well-known estimates.

Lemma 8.10
.

Let
\Gamma
be a cyclic group of order
N
(
1\leq N\leq\infty
) with character group
G
and let
\gamma\in\Gamma
be its generator.
Consider for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
u_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\delta_{\gamma^{j}}\in A(\Gamma)
and its Fourier transform
\widehat{u_{n}}\colon G\to\mathbb{T}
. For
0<p<1
, the following estimates hold.

\displaystyle(1)\mbox{ If $N=\infty$, }\mbox{there are }C,D_{p}>0\mbox{ such that for each }n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\|\widehat{u_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(G)}\geq C\log n\mbox{ and }\|\widehat{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq D_{p},

\displaystyle(2)\mbox{ If $N<\infty$, }\,\;\|\widehat{u_{N}}\|_{L^{1}(G)}=1\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|\widehat{u_{N}}\|_{L^{p}(G)}=N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

Proof.

We represent
G
as the circle group
\mathbb{T}
if the order of
\gamma
is infinite and as
\mathbb{T}_{N}=\{e^{2\pi ij/N}\colon 1\leq j\leq N\}
if the order of
\gamma
is
N<\infty
. Then, in both cases,

\left|\widehat{u_{n}}(e^{2\pi it})\right|=\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{2\pi{ijt}}\right|=\left|\frac{\sin n\pi t}{\sin\pi t}\right|.

In the case of infinite order, the easy part of a well-known estimate (see Exercise II.1.1 in
[
34
]
) can be used to show that, form some
C>0
,

\|\widehat{u_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\geq C\log n.

(8.3)

On the other hand, noting that

\left|\frac{1}{\sin(\pi t)}\right|\leq\frac{2}{\pi t},\mbox{ for all $0<t<1$},

one has that, for some constant
D>0
,

\|\widehat{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq D\left(\frac{1}{1-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(8.4)

When
\Gamma
is finite,
u_{N}=\mathbf{1}
, the constant 1-function and
\widehat{u_{N}}=N\delta_{1}
(recall that although
\Gamma
and
G
are isomorphic, their Haar measures are different, (normalized for
G
and counting measure for
\Gamma
). Hence
\|\widehat{u_{N}}\|_{L^{1}(G)}=1
and
\|\widehat{u_{N}}\|_{L^{p}(G)}=N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
.
∎

Reflexivity of subspaces of
L^{1}(G)
can be characterized through uniform integrability of its unit ball, see e.g.
[
56
, Theorem III.C.12]
. This was used in
[
26
]
to show that

is not reflexive if
E
is not a
\Lambda(1)
-set. In the case of preduals of von Neumann algebras, such as
A(\Gamma)
, one can use Lemma
8.11
below as a substitute. This will require some notation.
Recall that, in a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, every self adjoint linear functional
\phi\in\mathcal{M}^{\ast}
, can be decomposed as
\phi=(\phi^{+}-\phi^{-})
, with
\phi^{+},\,\phi^{-}
positive functionals. If
\phi=V|\phi|
denotes the polar decomposition of
\phi
, then

\phi^{+}=\frac{1}{2}\left(|\phi|+\phi\right),\quad\phi^{-}=\frac{1}{2}\left(|\phi|-\phi\right)).

A general
\phi\in\mathcal{M}^{\ast}
can then be written as
\phi=\phi_{1}+i\phi_{2}
with

\phi_{1}=\dfrac{1}{2}(\phi+\phi^{\ast})
and
\phi_{2}=\dfrac{i}{2}(\phi^{\ast}-\phi)
, and both
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are self adjoint.
We denote then

[\phi]=\phi_{1}^{+}+\phi_{1}^{-}+\phi_{2}^{+}+\phi_{2}^{-}.

Lemma 8.11

(Lemma 5.6 of
[
53
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra with predual
\mathcal{M}_{\ast}
. If
K\subset\mathcal{M}_{\ast}
is relatively weakly compact, then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a finite set
F_{\varepsilon}\subseteq K
and
\delta>0
such that if
p
is a projection in
\mathcal{M}
and

\langle\,[\phi],p\,\rangle<\delta
for every
\phi\in F_{\varepsilon}
, then
\left|\langle\,\phi,p\,\rangle\right|<\varepsilon
for every
\phi\in K
.

Lemma 8.12
.

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group. Let
\{\chi_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a subset of
\Gamma
such that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the order

o(\chi_{n})
of
\chi_{n}
is larger than
n
. Put
k(n)=o(\chi_{n})
if the order of
\chi_{n}
is finite and
k(n)=n
, otherwise, and define

\displaystyle F_{n}

\displaystyle=\left\{\chi_{n}^{j}\colon 1\leq j\leq k(n)\right\}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad E=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F_{n}.

Then
A_{E}(\Gamma)
is not reflexive.

Proof.

Consider the functions

u_{n}=B_{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k(n)-1}\delta_{\chi_{n}^{j}}\in A(\Gamma),

where
B_{n}
is chosen so that
\|u_{n}\|_{A(\Gamma)}=1
. If
G_{n}
is the character group of the cyclic subgroup
\Gamma_{n}\subseteq\Gamma
generated by
\chi_{n}
, then by Proposition 2.4.1 of
[
32
]
, one has that
\|\widehat{u_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(G_{n})}=\|u_{n}\|_{A(\Gamma_{n})}=\|u_{n}\|_{A(\Gamma)}=1
.

Put
\alpha_{n}=\|\widehat{u_{n}}\|_{L^{1/2}(G_{n}})
. By Lemma
8.10
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\|\widehat{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(G)}}{\|\widehat{u_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(G)}}=0,

hence

\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}=0.

We now define

\mathcal{A}_{n}=\left\{x\in G_{n}\colon|\widehat{u_{n}}(x)|>\frac{1}{2\alpha_{n}}\right\}.

Then, letting
\mathrm{m}_{\mbox{\tiny$G_{n}$}}
denote the (normalized) Haar measure of
G_{n}
,

\mathrm{m}_{\mbox{\tiny$G_{n}$}}(\mathcal{A}_{n})\leq 2\alpha_{n}.

(8.5)

On the other hand, since

\left|\widehat{u_{n}}(x)\right|\leq\left(\frac{1}{2\alpha_{n}}\right)^{1/2}\left|\widehat{u_{n}}(x)\right|^{1/2}
, for every
x\in\mathcal{A}_{n}^{c}
, we have that

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\widehat{u_{n}}\|_{L^{1/2}(G_{n})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\int_{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{c}}|\widehat{u_{n}}(x)|^{1/2}d\mathrm{m}_{\mbox{\tiny$G_{n}$}}(x)\geq\left(2\alpha_{n}\right)^{1/2}\int_{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{c}}|\widehat{u_{n}}(x)|d\mathrm{m}_{\mbox{\tiny$G_{n}$}}(x).

It follows that

\int_{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{c}}|\widehat{u_{n}}(x)|d\mathrm{m}_{\mbox{\tiny$G_{n}$}}(x)\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}}

and, hence, that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}|\widehat{u_{n}}(x)|d\mathrm{m}_{\mbox{\tiny$G_{n}$}}(x)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}}.

(8.6)

Let now
r_{n}\colon A(\Gamma)\to A(\Gamma_{n})
be the restriction mapping (see
[
32
, Proposition 2.6.6]
) and define
p_{n}\in VN(\Gamma)
as
p_{n}=r_{n}^{\ast}\left(\widehat{\mbox{\large${\mathbf{1}}$}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}}\right)
, with
r_{n}^{\ast}\colon VN(\Gamma_{n})\to VN(\Gamma)
being the adjoint of
r_{n}
and
\widehat{\mbox{\large${\mathbf{1}}$}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}}
the Fourier transform of
\mbox{\large${\mathbf{1}}$}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}\in L^{\infty}(G_{n})
.

Observe that

\langle\,\delta_{x},p_{n}\,\rangle=0,\quad\mbox{ if }x\notin\Gamma_{n}.

(8.7)

Suppose now that
A_{E}(\Gamma)
is reflexive, then the set
\{u_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is relatively weakly compact. Pick
0<\varepsilon<1-\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}}
. By Lemma
8.11
, there are then
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}
and
\delta>0
such that, for any projection
p
in
VN(\Gamma)
,

\langle\,[u_{n_{i}}],p\,\rangle<\delta,\;i=1,\ldots k,\quad\implies\left|\langle\,u_{n},p\,\rangle\right|<\varepsilon\mbox{ for every $n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

(8.8)

Pick, for each
i=1,\ldots,k
, a function
v_{i}\in A(\Gamma)
with finite support
\mathcal{F}_{i}\subseteq\Gamma

such that

\|v_{i}-[u_{n_{i}}]\|<\frac{\delta}{2}

Next, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
i=1,\ldots,k
we define
w_{i,n}\in A(\Gamma)

as
v_{i}
on
\Gamma_{n}
and 0 elsewhere. Then, by (

),

\displaystyle\langle\,v_{i},p_{n}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\,w_{i,n},p_{n}\,\rangle.

Since the support of each
v_{i}
is finite, we will be able to find
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle w_{i,n}

\displaystyle=w_{i},\mbox{ for every $i=1,\ldots,k$ and every $n\geq n_{0}$}.

We finally observe that, putting
D=\max_{i}\|w_{i}\|_{\ell^{1}(\Gamma)}
,

\left|\langle\,w_{i},p_{n}\,\rangle\right|=\left|\int_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}\widehat{w_{i}}(x)d\mathrm{m}_{\mbox{\tiny$G_{n}$}}(x)\right|\leq D\,\mathrm{m}_{\mbox{\tiny$G_{n}$}}(\mathcal{A}_{n}),

for each

and each

. Since, (

),
\mathrm{m}_{\mbox{\tiny$G_{n}$}}(\mathcal{A}_{n})\leq 2\alpha_{n}
, and
\lim_{n}\alpha_{n}=0
, we deduce that

large enough,
\left|\langle\,w_{i},p_{n}\,\rangle\right|<\delta/2
(

) and, hence, for such

and any

,

\displaystyle\langle\,[u_{n_{i}}],p_{n}\,\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left|\langle\,[u_{n_{i}}]-v_{i},p_{n}\,\rangle\right|+\left|\langle\,w_{i},p_{n}\,\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|[u_{n_{i}}]-v_{i}\|\cdot\|p_{n}\|+\left|\langle\,w_{i},p_{n}\,\rangle\right|=\delta.

The choice of

, …,

allows us to conclude (by condition (

)), that if

were reflexive, then one would have that, given

large enough,

\langle\,u_{m},p_{n}\,\rangle<\varepsilon,\mbox{ for every $m\in\mathbb{N}$}.

But we know, (

), that for every

,

\left|\langle\,u_{n},p_{n}\,\rangle\right|>1-\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}}>\varepsilon,

showing that
A_{E}(\Gamma)
cannot be reflexive.

Theorem 8.13
.

\Gamma

8.3.
Strongly Arens irregular ideals, with and without a bai

In a W

In particular, as already deduced in Corollary

the ideals

The natural question of whether ideals

The following general construction yields such ideals.

Theorem 8.18
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a Banach algebra Let
J_{B}
and
J_{1}
be closed ideals of
\mathscr{A}
, such that
J_{B}
has a bai and is sAir. If
J_{B}
and
J_{1}
are orthogonal, i.e.,
J_{B}J_{1}=J_{1}J_{B}=\{0\}
, then
the internal direct sum
J_{0}=J_{B}\oplus J_{1}
is a closed ideal and

\mathcal{Z}_{i}(J_{0}^{\ast\ast})=\mathcal{Z}_{i}(J_{B}^{\ast\ast})+\mathcal{Z}_{i}(J_{1}^{\ast\ast}),\quad i=1,2.

Proof.

We first see that
J_{0}
is a closed ideal. Let
e_{B}
be the right identity in
J_{B}^{**}
associated with the bai in
J_{B}.

If
(u_{n})_{n}
is a sequence contained in
J_{B}
and
(v_{n})_{n}
is a sequence contained in
J_{1}
such that
\lim_{n}u_{n}+v_{n}=a\in\mathscr{A}
, then
e_{B}u_{n}=u_{n}
and
e_{B}v_{n}=0
for every
n,
and so

\lim_{n}u_{n}=\lim_{n}e_{B}u_{n}=\lim_{n}e_{B}(u_{n}+v_{n})=e_{B}a\in J_{B}.

This automatically implies that
\lim_{n}v_{n}=a-e_{B}a
and hence that
a\in J_{0}
.

We next see that
J_{0}^{\ast\ast}=J_{B}^{\ast\ast}\oplus J_{1}^{\ast\ast}
. This will follow at once if we see that

\|a+b\|\geq\|a\|,\mbox{ whenever $a\in J_{B}$ and $b\in J_{1}$}.

(8.12)

To see this, let
\varepsilon>0
and let
\phi\in\mathscr{A}^{\ast}
,
\|\phi\|\leq 1
be such that

\|a\|\leq\left|\langle\,a,\phi\,\rangle\right|+\varepsilon.

Since
\langle\,a,e_{B}\cdot\phi\,\rangle=\langle\,a,\phi\,\rangle
and
\langle\,b,e_{B}\cdot\phi\,\rangle=0
, one has that

\|a+b\|\geq\left|\langle\,a+b,e_{B}\cdot\phi\,\rangle\right|=\left|\langle\,a,\phi\,\rangle\right|\geq\|a\|-\varepsilon,

and inequality (
8.12
) is satisfied.

Let now
i=1
. The proof is the same when
i=2.

Let
m,q\in J_{0}^{\ast\ast}
. By the above, we write
m=m_{B}+m_{1}
and
q=q_{B}+q_{1}
with
m_{B},q_{B}\in J_{B}^{\ast\ast}
and
m_{1},q_{1}\in J_{1}^{\ast\ast}
.
Then,

m_{1}q_{B}=m_{1}\lozenge q_{B}=m_{B}q_{1}=m_{B}\lozenge q_{1}=0.

So,

\begin{split}mq&=(m_{B}+m_{1})(q_{B}+q_{1})=m_{B}q_{B}+m_{1}q_{1}\mbox{ and }\\
m\lozenge q&=(m_{B}+m_{1})\lozenge(q_{B}+q_{1})=m_{B}\lozenge q_{B}+m_{1}\lozenge q_{1}.\end{split}

(8.13)

Accordingly, if
m\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}(J_{0}^{\ast\ast})
,
then taking
q_{1}=0
in (
8.13
), we find
m_{B}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}(J_{B}^{\ast\ast})
. Taking
q_{B}=0
in (
8.13
), we find

m_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}(J_{1}^{\ast\ast}).
Thus,

\mathcal{Z}_{1}(J_{0}^{\ast\ast})\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{1}(J_{B}^{\ast\ast})+\mathcal{Z}_{1}(J_{1}^{\ast\ast}).

The reverse inclusion is also quick as it is clear that

if
m_{B}q_{B}=m_{B}\lozenge q_{B}
and
m_{1}q_{1}=m_{1}\lozenge q_{1}.

∎

Corollary 8.19
.

Let
\mathscr{A}

B

be closed ideals of

(i)

(ii)

is Arens regular but not reflexive, then

Assume first that

Composition operators between Toeplitz kernels
Yuxia Liang, Jonathan R. Partington

Abstract
. Recently, it was shown that the image of a Toeplitz kernel of dimension greater than 1 1 under composition by an inner function is nearly S ∗ S^{*} -invariant if and only if the inner function is an automorphism. Building on this, we determine the minimal Toeplitz kernel containing the image of a Toeplitz kernel under a composition operator with a general inner symbol, and extend this to weighted composition operators. Specifically, the corresponding cases for minimal model spaces are also given, thereby extending known work on the action of composition operators on model spaces. Finally, we use the equivalences between Toeplitz kernels to derive the explicit maximal vectors for several Toeplitz kernels, with symbols expressed in terms of composition operators and inner functions.

1.
Preliminaries

Let
H^{2}:=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
denote the standard Hardy space of the unit disc
\mathbb{D},
which embeds isometrically into
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, where
\mathbb{T}
is the unit circle endowed with the normalized Lebesgue measure
m
.
It holds that

L^{2}(\mathbb{T})=H^{2}\oplus\overline{H_{0}^{2}},
with
H^{2}=\bigvee\{z^{n}:\;n\geq 0\}
and
\overline{H_{0}^{2}}=\bigvee\{z^{n}:\;n<0\}=\overline{zH^{2}}
. Let
H^{\infty}:=H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
be the Banach algebra of bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D},
of which the set of invertible elements is denoted by
\mathcal{G}H^{\infty},
similarly for
L^{\infty}:=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and
\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
. Additionally, the Smirnov class (see, e.g.
[
21
, Lemma 4.4.2]
) is defined as

\mathcal{N}_{+}=\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}):\;\mbox{there exist}\;f_{1},\;f_{2}\in H^{\infty}\;\mbox{such that}\;f=f_{1}/f_{2}\;\mbox{and}\;f_{2}\;\mbox{is outer}\}.

Here an outer function
f\in H^{2}
is one that can be written in the form

f(z)=\alpha\exp\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{e^{iw}+z}{e^{iw}-z}k(e^{iw})dw\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

where
k
is a real-valued integrable function and
|\alpha|=1.

Given
g\in L^{\infty},
the Toeplitz operator
T_{g}:\;H^{2}\rightarrow H^{2}
is

(T_{g}f)(\lambda)=P_{H^{2}}(g\cdot f)(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{g(\zeta)f(\zeta)}{1-\overline{\zeta}\lambda}dm(\zeta),

where
P_{H^{2}}
is the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
onto
H^{2}
. In particular, the Toeplitz operator
T_{z}
is the unilateral shift
S
on
H^{2}
defined by
[Sf](z)=zf(z).
Its adjoint operator on
H^{2}
is the backward shift
[S^{*}f](z)=(f(z)-f(0))/z.

The well-known Beurling theorem characterises the nontrivial subspaces of
H^{2}
that are invariant under
S
as being of the form
\theta H^{2},
where
\theta
is an inner function, i.e.,
\theta\in H^{\infty}
such that its modulus is
1
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}.
Meanwhile, the model spaces
K_{\theta}:=H^{2}\ominus\theta H^{2}=\ker T_{\overline{\theta}}
are
S^{*}
-invariant.

With the development of
S
- or
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces in spaces on various domains, the concept of near invariance emerges.
Recall that a closed subspace
\mathcal{M}\subseteq H^{2}
is said to be nearly
S^{*}
-invariant if whenever
f\in\mathcal{M}
and
f(0)=0,
then
S^{*}f\in\mathcal{M}.
That is,
\mathcal{M}\subseteq H^{2}
is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant if the zeros of functions in
\mathcal{M}
can be divided out without leaving the space.
The study of nearly
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces in
H^{2}
was first explored by Hayashi in
[
12
]
, Hitt in
[
13
]
, and then Sarason
[
22
,
23
]
in relation to the kernels of Toeplitz operators. Subsequently, Câmara and Partington conducted a systematic investigation of near invariance and Toeplitz kernels (see, e.g.
[
1
,
2
]
). After that we extended the near invariance to left invertible operators on Hilbert space and the shift semigroup on
L^{2}(0,\infty)
(see, e.g.
[
14
,
15
]
).

Hitt proved the following well-known characterization of nearly
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces in
H^{2}
, with a vectorial generalization formulated in
[
6
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
13
, Proposition 3]
The nearly
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces of
H^{2}
have the form
\mathcal{M}=uK
, with
u\in\mathcal{M}
of unit norm,
u(0)>0,
and
u
orthogonal to all elements of
\mathcal{M}
vanishing at the origin,
K
an
S^{*}
-invariant subspace, and the operator of multiplication by
u
isometric from
K
into
H^{2}
.

The kernels of Toeplitz operators on
H^{2}
are always nearly
S^{*}
-invariant. Model spaces, a specific type of Toeplitz kernel, are studied in relation to topics like truncated Toeplitz operators, Schrödinger operators, Hankel operators and classical extremal problems (see, e.g.
[
11
, Chapter 5]
). Recently, Fricain, Hartmann and Ross
[
10
]
provided a necessary and sufficient condition for a function
F
to multiply one model space
K_{\theta}
into another model space. This is also related with the important work of Crofoot in
[
9
]
.
Câmara and Partington later explored model spaces and Toeplitz operators in general
H^{p}
spaces on the upper half-plane (
[
5
]
), especially posing and characterising the maximal vectors in a model space. In
[
3
]
, they introduced a new perspective by considering multipliers between Toeplitz kernels in terms of the maximal vectors and surjective multipliers (see Theorem
3.2
).

Composition operators on the Hardy space are fundamental objects in operator theory, with connections to complex analysis, functional analysis and linear dynamics (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
19
,
20
]
). For composition operators on model spaces, Mashreghi and Shabankhah
[
17
]

provided a complete description of bounded composition operators on model spaces
K_{B}
for a finite Blaschke product
B
, showing that these composition operators form a group on
K_{B}
. They also characterized the minimal model space
K_{v}
containing the image
C_{\psi}(K_{\theta})
in the following theorem. Here the minimal model space
K_{v}
containing
C_{\psi}(K_{\theta})
means that
v
divides all other inner functions with the inclusion property.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
18
, Theorem 2.1]
Let
\psi
and
\theta
be inner functions, and let

\displaystyle v(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\theta\circ\psi,&\;\theta(0)\neq 0\;\mbox{and}\;\psi(0)=0,\\
z(\theta\circ\psi),&\;\theta(0)\neq 0\;\mbox{and}\;\psi(0)\neq 0,\\
z(\theta\circ\psi)/\psi,&\;\theta(0)=0.\end{array}\right.

Then the mapping
C_{\psi}:\;K_{\theta}\rightarrow K_{v}
is well-defined and bounded. Moreover,
K_{v}
is the minimal model space containing
C_{\psi}(K_{\theta}).

We generalize this result in Theorems
3.10
and
3.17
.

Model spaces are themselves Toeplitz kernels, and we recently proved that if
\psi
is an automorphism, then the image of any Toeplitz kernel under
C_{\psi}
remains a Toeplitz kernel, thus nearly
S^{*}
-invariant.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
16
, Theorem 2.1]

Let
\psi
be an automorphism and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
be a Toeplitz kernel with
F\in L^{\infty}
; then it follows that

C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G},\;\mbox{where}\;G=(F\circ\psi)\frac{\psi}{z}.

However, the next theorem shows that if the inner function
\psi
is not an automorphism, the image of the Toeplitz kernel under
C_{\psi}
no longer retains near
S^{*}
-invariance.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
16
, Theorem 2.11]

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a subspace of
H^{2}
of dimension at least
2
, and let
\psi
be an inner function that is not an automorphism on
\mathbb{D}
. Then
C_{\psi}(\mathcal{M})
is not nearly
S^{*}
-invariant (and thus not a Toeplitz kernel).

These two results imply that for an
F\in L^{\infty}
such that
\mathop{\rm dim}\nolimits\ker T_{F}>1
, and an inner function
\psi
,
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant if and only if
\psi
is an automorphism on
\mathbb{D}
. Especially, Theorem
1.4
says that otherwise
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
is no longer a Toeplitz kernel and therefore motivates us to find the minimal Toeplitz kernel containing it when
\psi
is an arbitrary inner function.

In this paper, we address the aforementioned question in three distinct sections. In the main result, Theorem
2.5
, we prove the minimal Toeplitz kernel with symbol
(F\circ\psi)\psi/z
contains
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
for an inner
\psi
and
F\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
, while also describing the relations between their maximal vectors.
As a special case, this provides an alternative and concise proof of the key result, Theorem
1.2
, originally established by Mashreghi and Shabankhah. In Section 3, building on
[
4
, Theorem 5.1]
, we characterize the equivalent condition that ensures
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
is a Toeplitz kernel through Carleson measures in Proposition
3.4
. We then extend this by combining the multiplier and composition operators to formulate the minimal Toeplitz kernels containing
C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
or
uC_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
. These lead to two extensions of Theorem
1.2
in Theorems
3.10
and
3.17
. Finally, in Section 4, we use the equivalences between Toeplitz kernels to derive several results concerning the maximal vectors of Toeplitz kernels. Specially, we provide an example showing that the sum of two Toeplitz kernels is not necessarily nearly
S^{*}
-invariant when their intersection is
\{0\}.

2.
Minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
for an inner
\psi

In this section, we first review the definitions of the minimal Toeplitz kernel containing a vector and the maximal vector for a Toeplitz kernel (including model spaces). We then consider the same ideas for the image of a Toeplitz kernel under a composition operator.

Definition 2.1
.

[
3
, Definition 2.1]
For a function
k\in H^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, we write
{\rm K_{min}}(k)
for the minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
k,
that is,
{\rm K_{min}}(k)=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
for some
g\in L^{\infty},
with
k\in{\rm K_{min}}(k),
while
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h}
for every
h\in L^{\infty}
such that
k\in\ker T_{h}.
Meanwhile,
k
is called a maximal vector (or a maximal function) for the Toeplitz kernel
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
.

The existence of minimal kernels and maximal vectors in
H^{2}
was presented in the following theorem, of which the upper half-plane case can be found in
[
1
, Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 5.1]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
2
, Theorem 3.3]
Let
h\in H^{2}\setminus\{0\}
and
h=IO
be its inner-outer factorization. Then there exists a minimal Toeplitz kernel
{\rm K_{min}}(h)
containing
h
, namely

{\rm K_{min}}(h)=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}\overline{IO}/O}

and
h
is a maximal vector for
{\rm K_{min}}(h)
.

The definition of the minimal Toeplitz kernel containing a subspace can be stated similarly. Additionally, all the maximal vectors for a Toeplitz kernel can be characterized as follows.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
3
, Theorem 2.2]
Let
g\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
be such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\neq\{0\}.
Then
k
is a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
if and only if
k\in H^{2}
and
k=g^{-1}\overline{z}\bar{p}
, where
p
is outer in
H^{2}.

2.1.
The minimal Toeplitz kernel containg
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
.

In this subsection, we examine the transformation of Toeplitz kernels under composition operators with inner function symbols and derive explicit formulas for the minimal Toeplitz kernels that contain their images. Meanwhile, the correspondence of maximal vectors between Toeplitz kernels is characterized.

In our proof, the following theorem plays a crucial role in establishing the equivalent conditions for the equalities of Toeplitz kernels.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
2
, Corollary 7.9]
Let
g,h\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
be such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h}
are nontrivial. Then
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h}
if and only if

\frac{g}{h}=\frac{\overline{p}}{\overline{q}},\;\;p,q\in H^{2}\;\mbox{outer}.

If moreover
hg^{-1}\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty},
then we have

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h}\;\;\mbox{if and only if}\;\;hg^{-1}\in\mathcal{G}\overline{H^{\infty}}.

We can now present our first main result.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\psi
be inner and
F\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\neq\{0\}
. Then there exists a minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
,
which is
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z}
. Moreover, if
k
is a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
,
then
k\circ\psi
is a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z}
.
Further,
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z}
if and only if
\psi
is an automorphism on
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

With
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\neq\{0\}
and
k
a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
, we decompose
k=IO
with
I
inner and
O
outer in
H^{2}
. Then Theorem
2.2
implies that

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}\overline{IO}/O}.

(2.1)

Next we consider the minimal kernel
{\rm K_{min}}(k\circ\psi)
containing
k\circ\psi=(I\circ\psi)(O\circ\psi)\in C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})\subseteq H^{2}.

[
11
, Proposition 6.5]
implies that
I\circ\psi
is inner and
[
8
, Theorem 4.27]
implies that
O\circ\psi
is outer, so we have

\displaystyle{\rm K_{min}}(k\circ\psi)=\ker T_{\frac{\overline{z}\overline{k\circ\psi}}{O\circ\psi}}.

(2.2)

Now take any nonzero element
h
of
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F},
then (
2.1
) implies that

\frac{\overline{z}\overline{IO}}{O}h\in\overline{zH^{2}}.

We deduce that

\frac{(\overline{I\circ\psi})(\overline{O\circ\psi})}{O\circ\psi}(h\circ\psi)\in\overline{H^{2}},

and then we further derive that

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\overline{z}\overline{k\circ\psi}}{O\circ\psi}\Big)(h\circ\psi)=\Big(\overline{z}\frac{(\overline{I\circ\psi})(\overline{O\circ\psi})}{O\circ\psi}\Big)(h\circ\psi)\in\overline{zH^{2}}.

This indicates that
C_{\psi}(h)\in{\rm K_{min}}(k\circ\psi)
for any
h\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F},
implying

\displaystyle C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})\subseteq{\rm K_{min}}(k\circ\psi).

Since
C_{\psi}(k)\in C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}),
any Toeplitz kernel containing
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
must contain
{\rm K_{min}}(k\circ\psi)
. Thus we conclude that
{\rm K_{min}}(k\circ\psi)
is the minimal kernel containing
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
.

Finally, using (
2.1
), Theorem
2.4
yields that

F=\overline{z}\frac{\overline{IO}}{O}\frac{\overline{p}}{\overline{q}}

with
p,q\in H^{2}
outer. This further implies

zF=\frac{\overline{IO}}{O}\frac{\overline{p}}{\overline{q}}

with
p,q\in H^{2}
outer. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\overline{z}\overline{k\circ\psi}}{O\circ\psi}\Big)\frac{\overline{p\circ\psi}}{\overline{q\circ\psi}}=\frac{\overline{z}(\overline{I\circ\psi})(\overline{O\circ\psi})}{O\circ\psi}\frac{\overline{p\circ\psi}}{\overline{q\circ\psi}}=(F\circ\psi)\frac{\psi}{z}.

(2.3)

[
8
, Theorem 4.27]
shows
p\circ\psi
and
q\circ\psi
are outer functions. Using (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and Theorem
2.4
, we obtain that

{\rm K_{min}}(k\circ\psi)=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z},

which is the minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}).

Moreover,
k\circ\psi
is a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z}.

Meanwhile Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
imply

C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z}
if and only if
\psi
is an automorphism on
\mathbb{D}
, ending the proof.∎

Letting
F=\overline{\theta}
in Theorem
2.5
, some corollaries on model spaces follow.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\psi
and
\theta
be two inner functions such that
K_{\theta}\neq\{0\}
; then there is a minimal Toeplitz kernel
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(\overline{\theta}\circ\psi)\psi/z}
containing
C_{\psi}(K_{\theta})
and equality holds if and only if
\psi
is an automorphism. For an automorphism
\psi
, it holds that

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(\overline{\theta}\circ\psi)\psi/z}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}}}K_{\theta\circ\psi}.

Here we should recall a proposition concerning the inclusion.

Proposition 2.7
.

[
3
, Proposition 2.16]

Let
g,h\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h}
are nontrivial. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

({\rm i})

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h};

({\rm ii})

hg^{-1}\in\overline{\mathcal{N}_{+}};

({\rm iii})
there exists a maximal vector
k
for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
such that
k\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h}.

If moreover
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
contains a maximal vector
k
with
k,k^{-1}\in L^{\infty},
then each of the above conditions is equivalent to

({\rm iv})

k\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h}\cap H^{\infty}.

Using Corollary
2.6
and Proposition
2.7
, we present a new proof of Theorem
1.2
, a result also found in
[
11
, Theorem 6.4]
.

Proof.

Since
K_{v}
is the minimal model space containing
C_{\psi}(K_{\theta})
, Corollary
2.6
implies that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(\overline{\theta}\circ\psi)\psi/z}\subseteq K_{v}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{v}}.
By Proposition
2.7
, we derive that

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(\overline{\theta}\circ\psi)\psi/z}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{v}}\;\;\mbox{if and only if}\;\;\frac{\overline{v}z}{(\overline{\theta}\circ\psi)\psi}\in\overline{\mathcal{N}_{+}}.

This means

\frac{v\psi}{z(\theta\circ\psi)}\in\mathcal{N}_{+},

which is unimodular. By the definition of the Smirnov class, it follows that

\frac{v\psi}{z(\theta\circ\psi)}\in H^{\infty}\subseteq H^{2}.

Thus we seek the minimal inner function
\phi
such that

v=z(\theta\circ\psi)\phi/\psi\in H^{2}.

For the case
\theta(0)\neq 0
and
\psi(0)=0,
then
z
divides
\psi
and so we take
\phi(z)=\psi(z)/z
, which gives
v(z)=(\theta\circ\psi)(z).

For the case
\theta(0)\neq 0
and
\psi(0)\neq 0,
then we take
\phi=\psi
, which gives
v(z)=z(\theta\circ\psi)(z).

For the case
\theta(0)=0
, then
\psi
cancels and so we take
\phi=1
, which gives
v(z)=z(\theta\circ\psi)(z)/\psi(z),
ending the proof.
∎

The following proposition describes a surjective composition operator between Toeplitz kernels.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
F\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\neq\{0\}
and
\psi
be inner. Let
k
denote a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
and suppose
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
is also a Toeplitz kernel, then
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})={\rm K_{min}}(k\circ\psi)
. This holds if and only if
\psi
is an automorphism on
\mathbb{D}.

Proof.

Let
G\in L^{\infty}
be such that
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}.
We have
k\circ\psi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}
and then
{\rm K_{\min}}(k\circ\psi)\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}
. Theorem
2.5
implies that

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}=C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z}={\rm K_{\min}}(k\circ\psi).

Hence
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}={\rm K_{\min}}(k\circ\psi).
That is,
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})={\rm K_{min}}(k\circ\psi)=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z}
, which is equivalent to
\psi
being an automorphism on
\mathbb{D}
, ending the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.11

Let

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}

({\rm iii})

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}

We note if
h\in L^{\infty}\cap\overline{\mathcal{N}_{+}}

Corollary 2.12
.

With the same assumptions as in Proposition
2.11
, if
H(F^{-1}\circ\psi)z/\psi\in L^{\infty}
, then

C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}
if and only if
H(F^{-1}\circ\psi)z/\psi\in\overline{H^{\infty}}.

Letting
H=F
in Proposition
2.11
, there follows a corollary.

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
\psi
be inner and
F\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
is nontrivial. If
F(F^{-1}\circ\psi)z/\psi\in\overline{\mathcal{N}_{+}}
or
k\circ\psi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
for a maximal vector
k\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
, then
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}.

Now choosing
k=S^{*}\theta
with
k,\;k^{-1}\in L^{\infty},
Proposition
2.11
implies the corollary below.

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
\psi,\theta
be inner and
H\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
such that
K_{\theta}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}
are nontrivial. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

({\rm i})

C_{\psi}(K_{\theta})\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H};

({\rm ii})

H(\theta\circ\psi)z/\psi\in\overline{\mathcal{N}_{+}};

({\rm iii})

C_{\psi}(S^{*}\theta)\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}\cap H^{\infty}.

Remark 2.15
.

The function
\frac{\theta(z)-\theta(w)}{z-w}
is also a maximal vector for
K_{\theta}
for every

So
C_{\psi}(K_{\theta})\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}
if and only if
\frac{\theta\circ\psi(z)-\theta(w)}{\psi(z)-w}\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}
for some

3.
Minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
and
uC_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})

In this section, we examine the action of both the multiplier and composition operator on Toeplitz kernels. The explicit formulas for the minimal Toeplitz kernels containing
C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
and
uC_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
are provided in Theorems
3.6
and
3.13
, which indicate two extensions of Theorem
1.2
found in Theorems
3.10
and
3.17
.
These results are based on the following theorem by Câmara and Partington on the minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
4
, Theorem 5.1]
Let
g\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
and
u\in H^{2}
such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\neq\{0\}
and
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\subseteq H^{2}.
Then there exists a minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
, which is
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g\overline{u}/u_{o}},
where
u_{o}
is the outer factor of
u.
Moreover, if
\phi_{m}
is a maximal function for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g},
then
u\phi_{m}
is a maximal function for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g\overline{u}/u_{o}}.

It is natural to ask when
u\in H(\mathbb{D})
acts as a surjective multiplier between two Toeplitz kernels, which is addressed below.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
3
, Theorem 3.2]

Let
g,h\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h}
are nontrivial. Then a function
u\in H(\mathbb{D})
satisfies

u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h}
if and only if

(i)

u\in\mathcal{C}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g})
and
u^{-1}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h})
;

(ii)
for some (or indeed, for every) maximal vector
k\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g},
the function
uk
is a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h}.

Here
u\in\mathcal{C}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g})
means that
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\subseteq L^{2}(\mathbb{T}).
Particularly,
u
multiplies a model space
K_{\theta}
into
H^{2}
if and only if
|u|^{2}dm
is a Carleson measure for
K_{\theta}
. Since
1-\overline{\theta(0)}\theta\in K_{\theta}\cap H^{\infty}
is invertible, it follows that
u\in H^{2}.
However, a multiplier between two general Toeplitz kernels may not lie in
H^{2}
(see Example
3.5
with
m=2
).

3.1.
The conditions for
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F\overline{u}/u_{o}}

In the following proposition, we use Theorem
3.2
to provide an equivalent characterization of when
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
is a Toeplitz kernel.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
F\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
and
u\in H^{2}
be outer such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\neq\{0\}
and
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\subseteq H^{2}.
Then
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F\overline{u}/u}
if and only if
u\in\mathcal{C}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
and
u^{-1}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F\overline{u}/u}).

It is known that
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
is always closed if
u^{-1}\in L^{\infty}
. In general,
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
is not always closed, even when
u
is outer: see the example
(1+z)K_{z\phi^{\delta}}
in
[
15
, Proposition 3.2]
with the singular inner function
\phi^{\delta}(z)=\exp(-\delta(1+z)/(1-z))
with atom at
1
and
\delta>0
. Since
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
cannot be a Toeplitz kernel when
u
has an inner factor, we formulate a more strict condition on
u
such that
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
is a Toeplitz kernel.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
F\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
and
u\in H^{2}
be outer such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\neq\{0\}
and
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\subseteq H^{2}.
If
u^{-1}\in H^{\infty},
it holds that

\displaystyle u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F\overline{u}/u}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F/u}.

(3.1)

Proof.

First, Theorem
3.1
implies
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F\overline{u}/u}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F/u},
where the equality follows from the fact
u^{-1}\in H^{\infty}
and Theorem
2.4
. For the converse inclusion, suppose
f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F\overline{u}/u}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F/u}
, then it holds that

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{f}{u}\right)=\left(\frac{F}{u}\right)f\in\overline{zH^{2}},

which implies
f/u\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
due to the fact
u^{-1}\in H^{\infty}
(which is the multiplier algebra of
H^{2}
). And then
f\in u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}.
In sum, (
3.1
) holds.
∎

As a slight generalization of the example in
[
3
, page 560]
, we provide an outer function
u
satisfying Proposition
3.3
, but
u^{-1}\notin H^{\infty}
such that
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
is a Toeplitz kernel.

Example 3.5
.

Let
u(z)=(z-1)^{\frac{1}{m}}
(
m\geq 2
) be the principal branch and
F(z)=z^{-\frac{m+1}{m}}
with
\arg z\in[0,2\pi)
on
\mathbb{T}
, then it holds that

u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{z^{-\frac{m+1}{m}}\left(\frac{\bar{z}-1}{z-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}}=K_{z}.

3.2.
The minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})

Based on the condition
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\subseteq H^{2},
we formulate the minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
in the theorem below.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
F\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
and
u\in H^{2}
such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\neq\{0\}
and
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\subseteq H^{2}.
For any inner function
\psi,
there exists a minimal Toeplitz kernel
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}
containing
C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
, where

\displaystyle H=\psi(F\circ\psi)\frac{\overline{u}\circ\psi}{z(u_{o}\circ\psi)}

(3.2)

and
u_{o}
is the outer factor of
u.
Moreover, if
\phi_{m}
is a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F},
then
C_{\psi}(u\phi_{m})
is a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}.

Proof.

Since
\phi_{m}
is a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F},
Theorem
3.1
implies
u\phi_{m}
is a maximal vector for the minimal Toeplitz kernel
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F\overline{u}/u_{o}}
containing
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
. That is,

u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\subseteq{\rm K_{\min}}(u\phi_{m})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F\overline{u}/u_{o}}.

So Theorem
2.5
gives

C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})\subseteq C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F\overline{u}/u_{o}})\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}

with
H
defined in (
3.2
). This can also be proved by the facts
C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=(u\circ\psi)C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
and then Theorem
2.5
together with Theorem
3.1
imply

(u\circ\psi)C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})\subseteq(u\circ\psi)\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}.

Next we show
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}
is the minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}).

By decomposing
\phi_{m}=IO
with
I
inner and
O
outer in
H^{2},
we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}\overline{\phi_{m}}/O}.

(3.3)

Next we consider the minimal kernel
{\rm K_{\min}}(C_{\psi}(u\phi_{m}))
for the maximal vector
C_{\psi}(u\phi_{m})\in C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})\subseteq H^{2}.
Theorem
2.2
implies

\displaystyle{\rm K_{\min}}(C_{\psi}(u\phi_{m}))=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\frac{\overline{z}(\overline{u}\circ\psi)(\overline{\phi_{m}}\circ\psi)}{(u_{o}\circ\psi)(O\circ\psi)}}.

(3.4)

Since
C_{\psi}(u\phi_{m})\in C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}),
any Toeplitz kernel containing
C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
must also contain

{\rm K_{min}}(C_{\psi}(u\phi_{m}))
. Thus we conclude
{\rm K_{min}}(C_{\psi}(u\phi_{m}))
is the minimal kernel containing

C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
. And then we show
{\rm K_{min}}(C_{\psi}(u\phi_{m}))=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}
with
H
given in (
3.2
).

Using (
3.3
), Theorem
2.4
yields that

F=\overline{z}\frac{\overline{\phi_{m}}}{O}\frac{\overline{p}}{\overline{q}}

with
p,q\in H^{2}
outer. This further implies

\psi(F\circ\psi)=\frac{\overline{\phi_{m}}\circ\psi}{O\circ\psi}\frac{\overline{p}\circ\psi}{\overline{q}\circ\psi}

with
p,q\in H^{2}
outer. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\overline{z}(\overline{u}\circ\psi)(\overline{\phi_{m}}\circ\psi)}{(u_{o}\circ\psi)(O\circ\psi)}\Big)\frac{\overline{p}\circ\psi}{\overline{q}\circ\psi}=\frac{\overline{z}(\overline{u}\circ\psi)}{(u_{o}\circ\psi)}\Big(\frac{\overline{\phi_{m}}\circ\psi}{O\circ\psi}\frac{\overline{p}\circ\psi}{\overline{q}\circ\psi}\Big)=\frac{(\overline{u}\circ\psi)}{z(u_{o}\circ\psi)}\psi(F\circ\psi)=H.

(3.5)

Since
p\circ\psi
and
q\circ\psi
are outer functions, and using (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and Theorem
2.4
, we conclude that

{\rm K_{min}}(C_{\psi}(u\phi_{m}))=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H},

which is a minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}).
This completes the proof.
∎

Let
\psi
be an automorphism; then
C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}
can be characterized as below.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
F\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
and
u\in H^{2}
be outer such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\neq\{0\}
and
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\subseteq H^{2}.
For an automorphism
\psi
on
\mathbb{D}
,
C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}
if and only if
u\circ\psi\in\mathcal{C}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z})
and
u^{-1}\circ\psi\in\mathcal{C}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H})
with

\displaystyle H=\psi(F\circ\psi)\frac{(\overline{u}\circ\psi)}{z(u\circ\psi)}.

Proof.

Noting that
C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=(u\circ\psi)C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})
, Theorem
2.5
implies

C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z}

for an automorphism
\psi
. Then
C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}
is equivalent to

(u\circ\psi)\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{H}.

So the desired equivalence follows from Theorems
3.2
and
3.6
.∎

A sufficient condition on
u
is similarly established in the proposition below.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
F\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
and
u\in H^{2}
be outer such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\neq\{0\}
and
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\subseteq H^{2}.
For an automorphism
\psi
on
\mathbb{D}
, if
u^{-1}\in H^{\infty},
it follows that

\displaystyle C_{\psi}(u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\frac{\psi(F\circ\psi)}{z(u\circ\psi)}}.

Proof.

From the assumptions, Theorem
2.5
implies that

C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z}.

Further considering
u
is outer and
u^{-1}\in H^{\infty},
it yields that
u\circ\psi
is outer and
u^{-1}\circ\psi\in H^{\infty}.
These facts together with Proposition
3.4
imply that

(u\circ\psi)\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\frac{(F\circ\psi)\psi}{z(u\circ\psi)}},

ending the proof.∎

In particular, every subspace of the form
C_{\psi}(uK_{\theta})
where
\theta
inner and
u\in H^{2}
,
is contained in a minimal Toeplitz kernel – this is a nontrivial generalization of
[
4
, Corollary 5.2]
.

Corollary 3.9
.

Every subspace of
H^{2}
of the form
C_{\psi}(uK_{\theta})
, where
u\in H^{2}
,
\theta
and
\psi
are inner functions, is contained in a minimal Toeplitz kernel
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\psi(\overline{\theta}\circ\psi)\frac{(\overline{u}\circ\psi)}{z(u_{o}\circ\psi)}}
, where
u_{o}
is the outer factor of
u.

As an extension of Theorem
1.2
, we formulate the minimal model space containing
C_{\psi}(uK_{\theta})
with inner functions
\theta
and
u.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
u
,
\theta
and
\psi
be three inner functions such that
K_{\theta}\neq\{0\}
, and define

\displaystyle\eta(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}z(\theta\circ\psi)(u\circ\psi)/\psi,&\theta(0)=0\;\mbox{or}\;u(0)=0,\\
(\theta\circ\psi)(u\circ\psi),&\theta(0)\neq 0,u(0)\neq 0\;\mbox{and}\;\psi(0)=0,\\
z(\theta\circ\psi)(u\circ\psi),&\theta(0)\neq 0,\;\;u(0)\neq 0\;\mbox{and}\;\psi(0)\neq 0.\end{array}\right.

Then
\eta
is inner and the composition operator

C_{\psi}:\;uK_{\theta}\rightarrow K_{\eta}

is well-defined and bounded. Moreover,
K_{\eta}
is the smallest model space containing
C_{\psi}(uK_{\theta})
.

Proof.

Since
K_{\eta}
is the minimal model space containing
C_{\psi}(uK_{\theta})
, Corollary
3.9
implies that

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\psi(\overline{\theta}\circ\psi)(\overline{u}\circ\psi)/z}\subseteq K_{\eta}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{\eta}}.
Using Proposition
2.7
, it follows that

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\psi(\overline{\theta}\circ\psi)(\overline{u}\circ\psi)/z}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{\eta}}\;\;\mbox{if and only if}\;\;\frac{\overline{\eta}z}{\psi(\overline{\theta}\circ\psi)(\overline{u}\circ\psi)}\in\overline{\mathcal{N}_{+}}.

This means that

\frac{\eta\psi}{z(\theta\circ\psi)(u\circ\psi)}\in\mathcal{N}_{+},

which is unimodular. Thus the definition of the Smirnov class implies

\frac{\eta\psi}{z(\theta\circ\psi)(u\circ\psi)}\in H^{\infty}\subseteq H^{2}.

Hence we must find the minimal inner function
\phi
such that

\eta(z)=\frac{z(\theta\circ\psi)(u\circ\psi)}{\psi}\phi\in H^{2}.

For the case
\theta(0)=0
or

then
\psi
divides

or

For the case
\theta(0)\neq 0
and

we divide into two subcases: if

we take

\eta(z)=(\theta\circ\psi)(u\circ\psi);
if
\psi(0)\neq 0,
we take
\phi=\psi
it follows
\eta(z)=z(\theta\circ\psi)(u\circ\psi),
ending the proof. ∎

Remark 3.11
.

3.3.
The minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
uC_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})

To begin, we present a lemma about multipliers between minimal Toeplitz kernels. We then use it to formulate the minimal Toeplitz kernel containing the image of
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}
under the weighted composition operator
uC_{\psi}.

Lemma 3.12
.

If
u
,
v
,

then
uK_{\min}(v)\subseteq K_{\min}(uv)
.

Proof.

Write
u=u_{i}u_{o}
and

in their inner–outer decompositions. By Theorem
2.2
, given any

\frac{\overline{u}_{i}\overline{u}_{o}\overline{v}_{i}\overline{v}_{o}\overline{z}}{u_{o}v_{o}}uh=\frac{\overline{u}_{o}\overline{v}_{i}\overline{v}_{o}\overline{z}}{v_{o}}h=\overline{u}_{o}\frac{\overline{v}_{i}\overline{v}_{o}\overline{z}}{v_{o}}h\in\overline{zH^{2}}.

That is

ending the proof. ∎

Now we can find the minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
uC_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})

.

F\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
,

. Next we prove that
{\rm K_{min}}(u(\phi_{m}\circ\psi))=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}

Decomposing
\phi_{m}=IO
with
I
inner and
O
outer in
H^{2},
it follows that

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}\overline{\phi_{m}}/O}.

(3.8)

Meanwhile, Theorem
2.2

\displaystyle{\rm K_{\min}}(u(\phi_{m}\circ\psi))=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\frac{\overline{z}\overline{u}(\overline{\phi_{m}}\circ\psi)}{u_{o}(O\circ\psi)}}.

2.4

F=\overline{z}\frac{\overline{\phi_{m}}}{O}\frac{\overline{p}}{\overline{q}}

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
F\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
,
\psi\in H^{2}
be an automorphism on
\mathbb{D}
and
u
be an outer function such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F}\neq\{0\}
and
uC_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})\subseteq H^{2}
. Then
uC_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}
if and only if
u\in\mathcal{C}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z})
and
u^{-1}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G})
, where
G
is given in (
3.7
).

Proof.

Theorem
2.5
shows
C_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z}
for the automorphism
\psi
on
\mathbb{D}
. So
uC_{\psi}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{F})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}
is equivalent to
u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{(F\circ\psi)\psi/z}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}
. Theorem
3.2
further gives the desired results. ∎

Remark 3.15
.

Letting
u^{-1}\in H^{\infty}
in Proposition
3.14
, the desired equality still holds.

Additionally, the minimal Toeplitz kernel containing the subspace
uC_{\psi}(K_{\theta})
with inner
\theta
is given in the corollary below.

Corollary 3.16
.

Let
\theta
be inner and
u\in H^{2}
such that
uC_{\psi}(K_{\theta})\subseteq H^{2}
. For any inner function
\psi,
there exists a minimal Toeplitz kernel
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\psi(\overline{\theta}\circ\psi)\overline{u}/(zu_{o})}
containing
uC_{\psi}(K_{\theta})
, where
u_{o}
is the outer factor of
u.

This gives rise to an expression for the minimal model space containing
uC_{\psi}(K_{\theta})
with inner functions
u
and
\theta
.

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
u
,
\theta
and
\psi
be three inner functions such that
K_{\theta}\neq\{0\}
, and define

\displaystyle\eta(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}z(\theta\circ\psi)u/\psi,&\theta(0)=0,\\
(\theta\circ\psi)u,&\theta(0)\neq 0\;\mbox{and}\;\psi(0)=0,\\
z(\theta\circ\psi)u,&\theta(0)\neq 0\;\mbox{and}\;\psi(0)\neq 0.\end{array}\right.

Then
\eta
is inner and the weighted composition operator

uC_{\psi}:\;K_{\theta}\rightarrow K_{\eta}

is well-defined and bounded. Moreover,
K_{\eta}
is the smallest model space containing
uC_{\psi}(K_{\theta})
.

Proof.

We find
\eta
such that
K_{\eta}
is the minimal model space containing
uC_{\psi}(K_{\theta})
, Corollary
3.16
implies that

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\psi(\overline{\theta}\circ\psi)\overline{u}/z}\subseteq K_{\eta}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{\eta}}.
Proposition
2.7
yields that

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\psi(\overline{\theta}\circ\psi)\overline{u}/z}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{\eta}}\;\;\mbox{if and only if}\;\;\frac{\overline{\eta}z}{\psi(\overline{\theta}\circ\psi)\overline{u}}\in\overline{\mathcal{N}_{+}}.

That is,

\frac{\eta\psi}{z(\theta\circ\psi)u}\in\mathcal{N}_{+},

which is unimodular. Hence the definition of the Smirnov class implies

\frac{\eta\psi}{z(\theta\circ\psi)u}\in H^{\infty}\subseteq H^{2}.

So we need to find the minimal inner function
\phi
such that

\eta(z)=\frac{z(\theta\circ\psi)u}{\psi}\phi\in H^{2}.

For the case
\theta(0)=0
, it follows that
\psi
divides
\theta\circ\psi
, and so take
\phi(z)=1
, which gives
\eta(z)=z(\theta\circ\psi)u/\psi.

For the case
\theta(0)\neq 0
, we divide into two subcases: if
\psi(0)=0,
we take
\phi(z)=\psi(z)/z
, and it follows that
\eta(z)=(\theta\circ\psi)u;
if
\psi(0)\neq 0,
we take
\phi(z)=\psi(z)
and then
\eta(z)=z(\theta\circ\psi)u,
finishing the proof. ∎

Remark 3.18
.

Letting
u=1
in Theorem
3.17
, Theorem
1.2
follows.

4.
Maximal vectors for Toeplitz kernels with compositional inner symbols

In this final section, we generalise the above results in the sense of equivalence (see, e.g.
[
3
,
5
]
). To be specific, we formulate the maximal vectors for several Toeplitz kernels (including model spaces) with symbols
expressed in terms of composition operators and inner functions.

Definition 4.1
.

[
3
, Definition 2.23]
If
G_{1},G_{2}\in L^{\infty}
, we say that
G_{1}\sim G_{2}
if and only if there are functions
H_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
and
H_{-}\in\mathcal{G}\overline{H^{\infty}},
such that

\displaystyle G_{1}=H_{-}G_{2}H_{+}.

(4.1)

Definition 4.2
.

[
3
, Definition 2.24]
If
G_{1},G_{2}\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
, such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G_{1}}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G_{2}}
are nontrivial, we say that

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G_{1}}\sim\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G_{2}}
if and only if

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G_{1}}=H_{+}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G_{2}}\;\;\mbox{with}\;H_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}.

Definitions
4.1
and
4.2
imply that if
G_{1}\sim G_{2}
via (
4.1
) then

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G_{1}}=H_{+}^{-1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G_{2}}
, that is,
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G_{1}}\sim\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G_{2}}.
In the sequel, we want to express the maximal vector for a Toeplitz kernel
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h_{-}(g\circ\psi)h_{+}}=h_{+}^{-1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g\circ\psi}
in terms of a maximal vector of
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g},
where
\psi
is inner,
h_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}\;\mbox{and}\;\;h_{-}\in\mathcal{G}\overline{H^{\infty}}.
The first two propositions concern the maximal vectors for Toeplitz kernels
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}h_{-}(g\circ\psi)h_{+}}=h_{+}^{-1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}(g\circ\psi)}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}\psi h_{-}(g\circ\psi)h_{+}}=h_{+}^{-1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}\psi(g\circ\psi)}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
k
is a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g},
where
g\in L^{\infty},
then
h_{+}^{-1}(k\circ\psi)\psi
is a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}h_{-}(g\circ\psi)h_{+}}=h_{+}^{-1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}(g\circ\psi)}
for every inner function
\psi
and every
h_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty},\;h_{-}\in\mathcal{G}\overline{H^{\infty}}.
That is,

\displaystyle{\rm K_{\min}}(h_{+}^{-1}(k\circ\psi)\psi)=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}h_{-}(g\circ\psi)h_{+}}=h_{+}^{-1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}(g\circ\psi)}.

(4.2)

Proof.

From Theorem
2.3
, if
{\rm K_{\min}}(k)=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g},
then
gk=\overline{zp}
for an outer function
p\in H^{2}
. Therefore we further have

\psi(g\circ\psi)(k\circ\psi)=\overline{p}\circ\psi.

It holds that
h_{+}^{-1}(k\circ\psi)\psi\in H^{2}
, which satisfies

\displaystyle(\overline{z}h_{-}(g\circ\psi)h_{+})(h_{+}^{-1}(k\circ\psi)\psi)=\overline{z}h_{-}(\overline{p}\circ\psi).

(4.3)

Since
p\circ\psi
is outer and
h_{-}\in\mathcal{G}\overline{H^{\infty}}
, the above equation, together with Theorem
2.3
, implies the required result.
∎

And then (
4.2
) implies
{\rm K_{\min}}((k\circ\psi)\psi)=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}(g\circ\psi)}
due to
h_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}.
This reduces to a corollary on model spaces.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\theta
and
\psi
be two inner functions such that
K_{\theta}\neq\{0\}
; then
C_{\psi}(S^{*}\theta)\psi
is a maximal vector for
K_{z(\theta\circ\psi)}.
That is,

K_{\min}(C_{\psi}(S^{*}\theta)\psi)=K_{z(\theta\circ\psi)}.

Replacing
h_{+}^{-1}(k\circ\psi)\psi\in H^{2}
by
h_{+}^{-1}(k\circ\psi)\in H^{2}
in (
4.3
), we deduce the following proposition.

Proposition 4.5
.

If
k
is a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g},
where
g\in L^{\infty},
then
h_{+}^{-1}(k\circ\psi)
is a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}\psi h_{-}(g\circ\psi)h_{+}}=h_{+}^{-1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}\psi(g\circ\psi)}
for every inner function
\psi
and every
h_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty},\;h_{-}\in\mathcal{G}\overline{H^{\infty}}.
That is,

{\rm K_{\min}}(h_{+}^{-1}(k\circ\psi))=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}\psi h_{-}(g\circ\psi)h_{+}}=h_{+}^{-1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}\psi(g\circ\psi)}.

And then it holds that
{\rm K_{\min}}(k\circ\psi)=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{z}\psi(g\circ\psi)}
due to
h_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}.
This indicates Corollary
2.10
by letting
g:=\overline{\theta}
and
k:=S^{*}\theta
with an inner
\theta
.

For the case
\psi(0)=0,
we replace
h_{+}^{-1}(k\circ\psi)\psi\in H^{2}
by
h_{+}^{-1}(k\circ\psi)\psi/z\in H^{2}
in (
4.3
), we deduce the proposition below.

Proposition 4.6
.

If
k
is a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g},
where
g\in L^{\infty},
then
h_{+}^{-1}(k\circ\psi)\psi/z
is a maximal vector for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h_{-}(g\circ\psi)h_{+}}=h_{+}^{-1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g\circ\psi}
for every inner function
\psi
with
\psi(0)=0
and every
h_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty},\;h_{-}\in\mathcal{G}\overline{H^{\infty}}.
That is,

{\rm K_{\min}}(h_{+}^{-1}(k\circ\psi)\psi/z)=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h_{-}(g\circ\psi)h_{+}}=h_{+}^{-1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g\circ\psi}.

The above formula ensures
{\rm K_{\min}}((k\circ\psi)\psi/z)=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g\circ\psi}
when
\psi(0)=0
. Taking
g:=\overline{\theta}
and
k:=S^{*}\theta
with an inner
\theta
, a corollary follows on model spaces.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\eta
and
\psi
be inner functions with
\psi(0)=0
; then
C_{\psi}(S^{*}\eta)\psi/z
is a maximal vector for
K_{\eta\circ\psi}
. That is,

{\rm K_{\min}}(C_{\psi}(S^{*}\eta)\psi/z)=K_{\eta\circ\psi}.

Remark 4.8
.

Additionally, Corollary
4.7
can be used to give another family of maximal vectors for the model space
K_{\theta},
which is an explicit special case of Theorem
2.3
.

Let
\theta
be an inner function with
\theta(0)=0,

h_{+}=(1+\overline{a}\beta)^{-1}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
and
h_{-}=1+a\overline{\beta}\in\mathcal{G}\overline{H^{\infty}}
with
\beta=(\theta-a)/(1-\overline{a}\theta)
and
|a|<1
; then

\displaystyle\overline{h_{-}^{-1}}\frac{1}{1-\overline{a}\theta}\frac{\theta}{z}=\frac{h_{+}}{1-\overline{a}\theta}\frac{\theta}{z}

(4.4)

is a maximal vector for
K_{\theta}

for almost all such
a.

Proof.

Recall the automorphism on
\mathbb{D}
is
\varphi_{a}(z)=(a-z)/(1-\overline{a}z)
with
|a|<1.
Then each inner function
\theta
can be decomposed into

\theta=h_{-}\beta h_{+}

with
\beta
inner (see, e.g.
[
21
]
), where

\beta=\frac{\theta-a}{1-\overline{a}\theta}=-\varphi_{a}\circ\theta.

Then we deduce

\overline{\theta}=\overline{h_{+}}(-\overline{\varphi_{a}}\circ\theta)\overline{h_{-}}

with
\overline{h_{+}}\in\mathcal{G}\overline{H^{\infty}}
and
\overline{h_{-}}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}.
Definition
4.1
implies
\overline{\theta}\sim(-\overline{\varphi_{a}}\circ\theta)
and
K_{\theta}=\overline{h_{-}}^{-1}K_{-\overline{\varphi_{a}}\circ\theta}.
So letting
\eta:=-\varphi_{a}
and
\psi:=\theta
in Corollary
4.7
and using
S^{*}\eta=S^{*}(-\varphi_{a})=(1-|a|^{2})/(1-\bar{a}z)
, it yields that

\overline{h_{-}}^{-1}((S^{*}\eta)\circ\psi)\psi/z=\overline{h_{-}}^{-1}\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{1-\bar{a}\theta}\frac{\theta}{z}.

Then the function in (
4.4
) is the desired maximal vector for
K_{\theta}
with
\theta(0)=0
.
∎

Using the
S^{*}
-invariance of model spaces, we obtain an extension of Corollary
4.4
. An inner function
\theta
is called the least common multiple (LCM) of the family of inner functions
\{\theta_{i}:\;i\in I\}
, if each
\theta_{i}
divides
\theta
, and
\theta
divides every other inner function that is divisible by each
\theta_{i}.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\psi
and
\theta_{i}
be inner functions for
i=1,2,\cdots,n
. Then there exists a minimal kernel
K
containing
\{C_{\psi}(S^{*}\theta_{i})\psi:\;i=1,2,\cdots,n\}
and for
\theta={\rm LCM}(\theta_{1},\;\theta_{2},\;\cdots,\;\theta_{n}),
we have

K=K_{z(\theta\circ\psi)}={\rm clos}_{H^{2}}(K_{z(\theta_{1}\circ\psi)}+\cdots+K_{z(\theta_{n}\circ\psi)})=K_{z(\theta_{i}\circ\psi)}\oplus z(\theta_{i}\circ\psi)K_{(\theta\circ\psi)(\overline{\theta_{i}}\circ\psi)}.

Proof.

Corollary
4.4
implies
C_{\psi}(S^{*}\theta_{i})\psi
is a maximal vector for
K_{z(\theta_{i}\circ\psi)}
and
[
2
, Theorem 4.3]
indicates
K_{\widetilde{\theta}}
is the minimal model space containing
\{C_{\psi}(S^{*}\theta_{i})\psi:\;i=1,2,\cdots,n\}
with

\widetilde{\theta}={\rm LCM}(z(\theta_{1}\circ\psi),\;z(\theta_{2}\circ\psi),\;\cdots,\;z(\theta_{n}\circ\psi)).

Since
\widetilde{\theta}=z(\theta\circ\psi),
the desired results follow.
∎

Motivated by Theorem
4.9
, it is natural to inquire whether the sum of two Toeplitz kernels (or even nearly
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces) is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant. First, the well-known lemma by Coburn provides a very interesting property possessed by the class of Toeplitz operators.

Lemma 4.10
.

[
21
, Lemma B4.5.6]
Let
\varphi\in L^{\infty},
then at least one of the kernels
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\varphi}
or
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\varphi}^{*}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{\varphi}}
has to be trivial.

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{\infty},
then
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\varphi}+\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\varphi}^{*}
is always a Toeplitz kernel, so it is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant.

A similar result holds for arbitrary nearly
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
in
H^{2}
when
M_{1}\cap M_{2}\neq\{0\}.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
be two nearly
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces with
M_{1}\cap M_{2}\neq\{0\},
then
M_{1}+M_{2}
is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant.

Proof.

Since
M_{1}\cap M_{2}\neq\{0\},
there is a nonzero function
g\in M_{1}\cap M_{2}.
We may assume
g(0)\neq 0
and then if
(f_{1}+f_{2})(0)=0
, we have a
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
(f_{1}-\lambda g)(0)=0
and
(f_{2}+\lambda g)(0)=0.
Then if we denote
h_{1}:=(f_{1}-\lambda g)/z
and
h_{2}:=(f_{2}+\lambda g)/z
, it follows that
h_{i}\in M_{i}
due to the near
S^{*}
-invariance of
M_{i}
for
i=1,2
, so
S^{*}(f_{1}+f_{2})=h_{1}+h_{2}\in M_{1}+M_{2},
completing the proof.
∎

Example 4.13
.

However, the sum of two Toeplitz kernels need not be nearly
S^{*}
-invariant in
H^{2}
. One Toeplitz kernel is obtained from Proposition
3.4
by taking
F(z)=\bar{z}
and
u(z)=(z+2)^{2}
with
u^{-1}\in H^{\infty}
; then it follows that

(z+2)^{2}K_{z}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{z}(\bar{z}+2)^{2}/(z+2)^{2}}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{z}^{3}b(z)^{2}},

where
b
is the Blaschke product
b(z)=\frac{z+1/2}{1+z/2}.

Let
M_{1}=\mathbb{C}=K_{z}
and
M_{2}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
with
g(z):=\overline{z}^{3}b(z)^{2}
be
the two Toeplitz kernels satisfying
M_{1}\cap M_{2}=\{0\}
. Then
M_{1}+M_{2}
is not nearly
S^{*}
-invariant, since it contains
z^{2}+4z
but not
z+4.

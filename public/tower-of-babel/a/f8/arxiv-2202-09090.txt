Cut-and-join operators in cohomological field theory and topological recursion
Alexander Alexandrov

Abstract
. We construct a cubic cut-and-join operator description for the partition function of the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion for a local spectral curve with simple ramification points. In particular, this class contains partition functions of all semi-simple cohomological field theories. The cut-and-join description leads to an algebraic version of topological recursion. For the same partition functions we also derive N families of the Virasoro constraints and prove that these constraints, supplemented by a deformed dimension constraint, imply the cut-and-join description.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14N35, 81R10, 05A15

Keywords: cohomological field theories, cut-and-join operators, Virasoro constraints, topological recursion

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Givental decomposition formula

Cohomological field theories were introduced by Kontsevich and Manin
[
KM94
]
to describe universal properties of Gromov–Witten theory. A cohomological field theory (CohFT) is given by a family of tensors
\Omega_{g,n}
. These tensors satisfy the compatibility conditions and are labeled by the number of the marked points
n
and genus
g
for all stable combinations

2g-2+n>0
. For any CohFT
\Omega
let us consider its partition function, dependent on the formal variables
{\bf T}=\{T^{a}_{k};1\leq a\leq N,k\geq 0\}
,

(1.1)

Z_{\Omega}=\exp\left(\sum_{g,n}\frac{\hbar^{2g-2+n}}{n!}\sum_{k_{j}}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\Omega_{g,n}(e_{a_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes e_{a_{n}})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\psi_{j}^{k_{j}}T_{k_{j}}^{a_{j}}\right)\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf T}][\![\hbar]\!].

We also call it the (total) ancestor potential, because for CohFTs associated with the Gromov–Witten theory on some variety, function
Z_{\Omega}
is the generating function of the so-called ancestor Gromov–Witten invariants. The most important example here is the Kontsevich–Witten tau-function associated with the Gromov–Witten theory of a point, see Section
2.1
.

Investigation of the Gromov–Witten invariants and, more generally, cohomological field theories, in particular in the higher genera, is a challenging problem. In his seminal works, Givental
[
Giv01a
,
Giv01b
]
introduced a group action on the CohFT partition functions. The Givental group action can be lifted to the action on the space of CohFTs themselves
[
Sha09
,
Tel12
]
. In particular, in
[
Tel12
]
Teleman proves that an extension of the Givental group, which we call the Givental–Teleman group, acts transitively on the space of semi-simple CohFTs. CohFTs here are not required to be conformal or have a flat unit. It implies that the partition function of an arbitrary semi-simple CohFT can be described by the Givental decomposition formula,

(1.2)

Z_{\Omega}=\widehat{R}\widehat{T}\widehat{\Delta}\cdot\prod_{a=1}^{N}\tau_{1}(\hbar,{\bf T^{a}}).

Here
\widehat{R}
is a Givental group operator given by a quantization of the twisted loop group element
[
Giv01a
]
,
\widehat{T}
is a translation operator, and
\widehat{\Delta}
rescales the topological expansion parameter
\hbar
. The Kontsevich–Witten tau-function
\tau_{1}
is a solution of the Korteweg–De Vries (KdV) hierarchy which governs the intersection theory of
\psi
classes on the moduli spaces of punctured Riemann surfaces.

The Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion
[
EO07
,
EO09
]
is a universal procedure, which allows us to construct a family of symmetric differentials on a spectral curve with additional structures on it. For many known examples these differentials encode interesting invariants of enumerative geometry and mathematical physics.
These differentials can also be combined in a canonical way to create a generating function
Z_{S}
, dependent on the formal variables
{\bf T}
.

As it was shown by Dunin-Barkowski, Orantin, Shadrin, and Spitz
[
DBOSS14
]
, partition functions of the semi-simple cohomological field theories are closely related to the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion. Namely, the ancestor potential of a semi-simple CohFT with flat unit can be identified with the partition function of the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion on a certain local spectral curve,
Z_{S}=Z_{\Omega}
. It means, in particular, that the partition function of the associated Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion is given by the decomposition formula (
1.2
).

This relation was generalized by Chekhov and Norbury
[
CN19
]
. This generalization describes Givental’s decomposition formula for the partition functions on all, possibly irregular, local spectral curves with simple ramifications, that is, the curves which near ramification points are similar to the Airy curve
(x=\frac{z^{2}}{2},y=z)
or the Bessel curve
(x=\frac{z^{2}}{2},y=\frac{1}{z})
. This generalization also describes some degenerate CohFTs. To describe this generalization one has to consider a slightly more general Givental decomposition formula.
Namely, for a Givental group operator
\widehat{R}
, which in the most general case is not a quantization of the twisted loop group element, a translation operator
\widehat{T}
, and an
\hbar
-rescaling operator
\widehat{\Delta}

we consider the generalized ancestor potential (see Definition
7
below)

(1.3)

Z:=\widehat{R}\widehat{T}\widehat{\Delta}\cdot\prod_{a=1}^{N}\tau_{\alpha_{a}}(\hbar,{\bf T^{a}}).

Here
\alpha_{a}\in\{0,1\}
, and the Brézin–Gross–Witten tau-function
\tau_{0}
is also a solution of the KdV hierarchy, which is related to certain intersection numbers on the moduli spaces
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

[
Nor20
]
.
For a local, possibly irregular, spectral curve with
N
simple ramification points the partition function
Z_{S}
of the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion is given by the Givental decomposition formula (
1.3
). The operators
\widehat{R}
,
\widehat{T}
,
\widehat{\Delta}
can be described in terms of the topological recursion data, see Section
4.3
.
The functions
\tau_{\alpha_{a}}
are associated with
N
ramifications points, and
\alpha_{a}=1
(
\alpha_{a}=0
) for regular (irregular) ramification points.

1.2.
Main statement

Partition functions of CohFTs and the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion have many interesting properties
[
Dub96
,
Man99
,
EO07
,
EO09
,
Pan18
,
CN19
]
, which make them so attractive. In this paper, we establish a new
general property of all such functions. More precisely, we prove that for an arbitrary Givental operator
\widehat{R}
, a translation operator
\widehat{T}
, and a
\hbar
-rescaling operator
\widehat{\Delta}
the generalized ancestor potential (
1.3
) is described by a cubic cut-and-join operator. In particular, it means that this cut-and-join description is valid for the partition functions of semi-simple CohFTs and Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion for local, possibly irregular, spectral curves with simple ramifications. This allows us to describe a generalized ancestor potential by an algebraic version of the topological recursion, which, unlike the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion, is linear and is given in terms of polynomials and differential operators.

Let

(1.4)

\widehat{J}_{k}^{a}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{(2k-1)!!\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k-1}^{a}}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,&\mathrm{for}\quad k>0,\\[3.0pt]
\displaystyle{\frac{T_{|k|}^{a}}{(2|k|-1)!!}}&\mathrm{for}\quad k\leq 0.\end{cases}

In Section
5.4
, for a generalized ancestor potential, given by (
1.3
), we construct a cubic operator

(1.5)

\widehat{W}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\leq j\leq k,\\
i+j+k\geq 0\end{subarray}}A_{a,b,c}^{i,j,k}\widehat{J}^{a}_{i}\widehat{J}^{b}_{j}\widehat{J}^{c}_{k}+\sum_{j=-1}^{\infty}B_{a}^{j}\widehat{J}^{a}_{j},

where the coefficients
A_{a,b,c}^{i,j,k}
and
B_{a}^{j}
are independent of
{\bf T}
and
\hbar
. We call it the cut-and-join operator.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem, which describes all generalized ancestor potentials (
1.3
) by cubic cut-and-join operators (
1.5
).

Theorem 1
.

A generalized ancestor potential satisfies

(1.6)

Z=\exp\left(\hbar\widehat{W}\right)\cdot 1.

Formula (
1.6
) follows from the properties of the operators
\widehat{\Delta}
,
\widehat{T}
, and
\widehat{R}
and the cut-and-join description of the Kontsevich–Witten and Brézin–Gross–Witten tau-functions
[
Ale11
,
Ale18
]
; we prove it in Section
5
. The result was conjectured in
[
Ale21b
, Conjecture 2.1]
.
By this theorem a generalized ancestor potential is a solution of the cut-and-join equation

(1.7)

\frac{\partial}{\partial\hbar}Z=\widehat{W}\cdot Z.

Formula (
1.6
) gives a unique solution of this equation in
{\mathbb{C}}[\![{\bf T}]\!][\![\hbar]\!]
with
Z\big|_{\hbar=0}=1
.

The cut-and-join description allows us to reconstruct a generalized ancestor potential by a linear recursion.
Consider the topological expansion

(1.8)

Z=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Z^{(k)}\hbar^{k}.

Coefficients
Z^{(k)}
are polynomials in
{\bf T}
. From Theorem
1
we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.1
.

The coefficients of the topological expansion of a generalized ancestor potential satisfy the algebraic topological recursion

(1.9)

Z^{(k)}=\frac{1}{k}\widehat{W}\cdot Z^{(k-1)}

with the initial condition
Z^{(0)}=1
.

The operator
\widehat{W}
describes a change of topology. This is why we call it the cut-and-join operator.
Cut-and-join formulas should be very convenient computationally because of the linear structure of the algebraic topological recursion.

There are several families of the partition functions in enumerative geometry for which the cut-and-join description of the form (
1.6
) is known, see e.g.,
[
MS09
,
Ale11
,
Ale18
,
Ale21b
,
Ale23
]
. For these examples the cut-and-join description is closely related to the Virasoro constraints. Therefore, it is natural to expect the cut-and-join operators to be related to the Virasoro constraints for the general setup of Theorem
1
.

In Section
6.1
with any generalized ancestor potential for
a=1\dots,N
,
m\geq-\alpha_{a}
we associate the operators

(1.10)

\widehat{L}_{m}^{a}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{j<k},\\
{j+k\geq m+1}\end{subarray}}L^{a;j,k}_{m;b,c}\widehat{J}_{j}^{b}\widehat{J}_{k}^{c}-\frac{1}{2\hbar}\sum_{k=m+\alpha_{a}}U^{a,b}_{m,k}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}^{b}}+\frac{\delta_{m,0}}{16}-\frac{\delta_{m,-1}}{2}p_{a},

where the coefficients
L^{a;j,k}_{m;b,c}
,
U^{a,b}_{k,m}
, and
p_{a}
are independent of
{\bf T}
and
\hbar
.
By construction, the operators
\widehat{L}_{m}^{a}
satisfy the commutation relations of a subalgebra of the direct sum of
N
copies of the Virasoro algebra

(1.11)

\left[\widehat{L}_{k}^{a},\widehat{L}_{m}^{b}\right]=\delta^{ab}(k-m)\widehat{L}_{k+m}^{a}.

Theorem 2
.

A generalized ancestor potential satisfies the Virasoro constraints

(1.12)

\widehat{L}_{m}^{a}\cdot Z=0,\quad\quad a=1\dots,N,\quad m\geq-\alpha_{a}.

These Virasoro constraints are closely related to the quantum Airy structure by Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
KS18
]
. In particular, the quantum Airy structure describes the topological recursion for the local spectral curves with regular simple ramification points
[
ABCO17
]
. We show that the Virasoro constraints (
1.12
) and the quantum Airy structure associated with them describe also the partition functions for the irregular spectral curves.

To arrive at Theorem
1
we need to supplement the Virasoro constraints (
1.12
) with an auxillary equation

(1.13)

\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial\hbar}Z=\widehat{H}\cdot Z,

where

(1.14)

\widehat{H}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j,\\
i+j\geq 1\end{subarray}}H_{a,b}^{i,j}\widehat{J}^{a}_{i}\widehat{J}^{b}_{j}

and the coefficients
H_{a,b}^{i,j}
do not depend on
{\bf T}
and
\hbar
. This equation is a deformation of the dimension constraint for the product of the tau-functions
\tau_{\alpha}
. We show that the cut-and-join equation of the form (
1.7
) follows from this equation and the Virasoro constraints (
1.12
).

It is known that the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion, cohomological field theories and
quantum Airy structures are closely related and essentially, under certain additional conditions, are equivalent to each other. In this paper we partially explain how these general structures are related to the cut-and-join operators, algebraic topological recursion,
and Virasoro constraints.

1.3.
Open problems

There are many open questions related to the observations made in this paper. Let us briefly describe some of them.

The Kontsevich–Witten and Brézin–Gross–Witten tau-functions have very nice matrix model descriptions. Moreover, there are two different matrix models for the Brézin–Gross–Witten tau-function
[
MMS96
]
, namely the unitary integral and the generalized Kontsevich model. However, the matrix models for the partition functions of the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion or cohomological field theories are not known except for a few examples. We hope that the cut-and-join operators, constructed in this paper, should help to derive the matrix integrals for the partition functions given by the Givental decomposition formula (
1.3
). For this purpose it can be helpful to reformulate the obtained cut-and-join operators in the Miwa parametrization. Cubic cut-and-join description implies that the generating functions should be given by certain sum over the graphs with at most cubic vertices, which also can be related to matrix models.

In this paper, we describe how to construct the cut-and-join operators using the elements of Givental’s decomposition formula. However, to construct the Givental operators themselves for given spectral curve data or a Frobenius manifold, and then to find the coefficients of the cut-and-join operator explicitly is, in general, a highly non-trivial task. Therefore, it would be desirable to describe the coefficients of the cut-and-join operators directly in terms of the underlying topological recursion data. For the semi-simple conformal Frobenius manifolds it should be possible to describe the cut-and-join operators in terms of the Frobenius structure. The Virasoro constraints for the calibrated partition functions have a simple explicit form
[
DZ99
,
Giv01a
]
, we expect that the cut-and-join operators and the
N
-families of the Virasoro constraints can be described with the help of the similar elements. Moreover, in this case it should be possible to extend the cut-and-join and the Virasoro description obtained in this paper to the descendant potentials.

Any cubic operator of the form (
1.5
) generates some function
Z
.
From our construction it is not clear which properties of the cubic operators
\widehat{W}
correspond to the space of generalized ancestor potentials. Moreover, since such operators are not unique, there should be a natural way, probably related to the symplectic structure for CohFT, to fix the ambiguity and to classify all generalized ancestor potentials by these operators.
We also expect that the cut-and-join operators can describe not only the partition functions, but the semi-simple CohFTs themselves. The cut-and-join operators should provide an alternative formulation of Teleman’s classification of semi-simple CohFTs.

We do not discuss integrability in this paper. We expect that the cut-and-join description should be helpful for investigation of integrable properties of the corresponding partition functions. Nice integrable properties, in particular the KP type integrability, should be related to the distinguished representation theory interpretations of the cut-and-join operators and existence of the planar global spectral curve.

It is very challenging to generalize the construction to non-semi-simple CohFTs corresponding to the topological recursion with higher ramification points. In this case the partition functions should satisfy the higher W-constraints and we expect them to be described by the families of the higher degree cut-and-join operators.

We hope to come back to these problems in the upcoming publications.

1.4.
Conventions

We use a non-standard normalization of the variables
T_{k}
, which contains an additional factor of
\hbar

compared to the standard normalization. This change of normalization corresponds to transition from the
genus expansion
of the generating functions

(1.15)

\exp\left(\sum_{g,n\geq 0}\hbar^{2g-2}{\mathcal{F}}_{g,n}\right),

where
g
is the genus,
n
is the number of the marked points, to the
topological expansion

(1.16)

\exp\left(\sum_{g,n\geq 0}\hbar^{2g-2+n}{\mathcal{F}}_{g,n}\right),

because the latter is more convenient for our needs.

By
\widehat{\cdot}
we denote the differential operators (actually, some formal completions of the space of polynomial differential operators) acting on the space of functions of the variables
{\bf T}=\{T_{k}^{a}\}
, where
k\geq 0
and
a
, if any, runs an appropriate range. For an operator
\widehat{A}
and a function
F({\bf T})
we denote by
\widehat{A}\cdot F
the result of action of the operator on the function. We denote by
a,b,c,d
the indices, associated with the
N
-dimensional space
V
. We assume the Einstein summation convention when these indices repeat twice. The index
\alpha
takes values in
\{0,1\}
.

Organization of the paper

The main goal of Sections
2
–
4
is to recall the basic ingredients of cohomological field theories, Givental–Telemann formalism, and the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion; they do not contain any new material. In Section
5
we construct a cubic cut-and-join description of the generalized ancestor potentials. This class includes the partition functions
of the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion on (possibly irregular) local spectral curves with simple ramification points and semi-simple CohFTs. In Section
6
we construct
N
families of the Virasoro constraints and a deformed dimension constraint for generalized ancestor potential. We use these constraints to derive the cut-and-join description and discuss possible ambiguities of this description.

2.
Cut-and-join operators for KW and BGW tau-functions

In this section, we remind the reader of some properties of the tau-functions of the KdV hierarchy, playing the main role in our construction. In particular, we describe the Virasoro constraints and their relation to the cut-and-join operators.

2.1.
Intersection numbers and KdV tau-function

Denote by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
the Deligne–Mumford compactification of the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
of all compact Riemann surfaces of genus
g
with
n
distinct marked points. It is a non-singular complex orbifold of dimension
3g-3+n
, which is empty unless the
stability condition

(2.1)

\displaystyle 2g-2+n>0

is satisfied.

In his seminal paper
[
Wit91
]
, Witten initiated new directions in the study of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. For each marking index
i
consider the cotangent line bundle
{\mathbb{L}}_{i}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, whose fiber over a point
[\Sigma,z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is the complex cotangent space
T_{z_{i}}^{*}\Sigma
of
\Sigma
at
z_{i}
. Let
\psi_{i}\in H^{2}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})
denote the first Chern class of
{\mathbb{L}}_{i}
. We consider the intersection numbers

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left<\tau_{k_{1}}\tau_{k_{2}}\cdots\tau_{k_{n}}\right>_{g}:=\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\psi_{1}^{k_{1}}\psi_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{k_{n}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}.

The integral on the right-hand side of (
2.2
) vanishes unless the stability condition (
2.1
) is satisfied, all
k_{i}
are non-negative integers, and the dimension constraint

(2.3)

3g-3+n=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}

holds true. Let
T_{i}
,
i\geq 0
, be formal variables and let

(2.4)

\tau_{KW}:=\exp\left(\sum_{g,n}\hbar^{2g-2+n}F_{g,n}\right),

where

(2.5)

F_{g,n}:=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\geq 0}\left<\tau_{k_{1}}\tau_{k_{2}}\cdots\tau_{k_{n}}\right>_{g}\frac{\prod T_{k_{i}}}{n!}.

A parameter
\hbar
here is introduced to trace the Euler characteristic of the punctured curve
\Sigma
, therefore it is associated with the
topological expansion
of the generating function.

Witten’s conjecture
[
Wit91
]
, proved by Kontsevich
[
Kon92
]
, states that the partition function
\tau_{KW}
becomes a tau-function of the KdV hierarchy after the change of variables
T_{n}=(2n+1)!!t_{2n+1}
.

Theorem 3

(
[
Kon92
]
)
.

The generating function
\tau_{KW}

is a tau-function of the KdV hierarchy in the variables
t_{k}
.

Below we call it the Kontsevich–Witten (KW) tau-function. Integrability of the KW tau-function immediately follows from Kontsevich’s matrix integral representation, for more detail see
[
Ale21a
]
and references therein.

2.2.
Norbury’s classes and KdV tau-function

Another KdV tau-function we consider is the Brézin–Gross–Witten (BGW) tau-function. This tau-function conjecturally governs the intersection theory on the moduli spaces
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, but this time with the insertions of the fascinating Norbury’s
\Theta
-classes.
Norbury’s
\Theta
-classes are the cohomology classes,
\Theta_{g,n}\in H^{4g-4+2n}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
. We refer the reader to
[
Nor23
,
Nor20
,
KN23
]
for a detailed description. Consider the intersection numbers

(2.6)

\left<\tau_{k_{1}}\tau_{k_{2}}\cdots\tau_{k_{n}}\right>_{g}^{\Theta}=\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\Theta_{g,n}\psi_{1}^{k_{1}}\psi_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{k_{n}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}.

Again, the integral on the right-hand side vanishes unless the stability condition (
2.1
) is satisfied, all
k_{i}
are non-negative integers, and the dimension constraint

(2.7)

g-1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}

holds. Consider the generating function of the
intersection numbers of
\Theta
and
\psi
classes,

(2.8)

F^{\Theta}_{g,n}:=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\geq 0}\left<\tau_{k_{1}}\tau_{k_{2}}\cdots\tau_{k_{n}}\right>_{g}^{\Theta}\frac{\prod T_{k_{i}}}{n!}

and

(2.9)

\tau_{\Theta}=\exp\left(\sum_{g,n}\hbar^{2g-2+n}F_{g,n}^{\Theta}\right).

For this generating function Norbury has suggested a direct analog of Witten’s conjecture
[
Nor20
, Conjecture 1]
, recently proven by Chidambaram, Garcia–Failde, and Giacchetto.

Theorem 4

(
[
CGFG22
]
)
.

The generating function
\tau_{\Theta}

is the BGW tau-function of the KdV hierarchy in the variables
t_{k}

(2.10)

\tau_{\Theta}=\tau_{BGW}.

Norbury also shows that
\tau_{\Theta}
is related to the super Riemann surfaces.

The BGW model was introduced in lattice gauge theory 40 years ago. KdV integrability of the BGW model in the weak coupling limit follows from the relation to the generalized Kontsevich model and was established by Mironov, Morozov, and Semenoff
[
MMS96
]
. For more details on BGW tau-function see also
[
Ale18
]
.

The KW and BGW tau-functions can be described in terms of the Virasoro constraints which completely specify the generating functions and are indirectly equivalent to the integrability. In the next section we describe these constraints and their solution in terms of the cut-and-join operators.

2.3.
Virasoro constraints and cut-and-join operators

Let us introduce the operators

(2.11)

\widehat{J}_{k}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{(2k-1)!!\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k-1}}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,&\mathrm{for}\quad k>0,\\[3.0pt]
\displaystyle{\frac{T_{|k|}}{(2|k|-1)!!}}&\mathrm{for}\quad k\leq 0.\end{cases}

Consider the
bosonic current

(2.12)

\begin{split}\widehat{J}(z)&:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{T_{k}}{(2k-1)!!}z^{2k}+\frac{(2k+1)!!}{z^{2k+2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}}\right)\\
&=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{\widehat{J}_{k}}{z^{2k}}.\end{split}

Let us consider the Virasoro algebra associated with the bosonic current
\widehat{J}(z)
,

(2.13)

\widehat{L}(z)=:\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{\widehat{L}_{k}}{z^{2k+2}},

where

(2.14)

\widehat{L}(z)=\frac{1}{2}{\scriptstyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{*}{*}}}\widehat{J}(z)^{2}{\scriptstyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{*}{*}}},

and the normal ordering
{\scriptstyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{*}{*}}}\dots{\scriptstyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{*}{*}}}
puts all
\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}}
to the right of all
T_{k}
. The Virasoro operators are given by
\widehat{L}_{m}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j+k=m+1}{\scriptstyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{*}{*}}}\widehat{J}_{j}\widehat{J}_{k}{\scriptstyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{*}{*}}}
or

(2.15)

\widehat{L}_{m}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=-m-1}\frac{T_{i}T_{j}}{(2i-1)!!(2j-1)!!}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k+2m+1)!!}{(2k-1)!!}T_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k+m}}\\
+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=m-1}(2i+1)!!(2j+1)!!\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial T_{i}\partial T_{j}}.

Here and below we assume that
T_{k}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}}
vanish for negative
k
. The operators
\widehat{J}_{m}
and
\widehat{L}_{m}
satisfy the commutation relations of the Heisenberg–Virasoro algebra

(2.16)

\begin{split}\left[\widehat{J}_{k},\widehat{J}_{m}\right]&=(2k-1)\delta_{k+m,1},\\
\left[\widehat{L}_{k},\widehat{J}_{m}\right]&=-(2m-1)\widehat{J}_{k+m},\\
\left[\widehat{L}_{k},\widehat{L}_{m}\right]&=2(k-m)\widehat{L}_{k+m}+\frac{1}{6}k(2k^{2}+1)\delta_{k+m,0}.\end{split}

Let us denote

(2.17)

3.1.
Cohomological field theory

Cohomological field theories were introduced by Kontsevich and Manin
[
KM94
]
. A cohomological field theory (CohFT) is given by a family of cohomology classes on the moduli spaces of stables curves. CohFTs were introduced to axiomatize universal properties of Gromov–Witten theory, they are closely related to the structure of the Frobenius manifolds
[
Dub96
,
Man99
]
.

On the Deligne–Mumford moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
consider the gluing map, which glues the last two marked points

(3.1)

q:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g-1,n+2}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}.

Similarly, identifying the last marked points on the two separate curves, we have a map

(3.2)

p:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{1},n_{1}+1}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{2},n_{2}+1}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

with
g=g_{1}+g_{2}
and
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
. The images of both maps
q
and
p
are in the boundary
\partial\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\backslash{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

Let
\pi
be the forgetting map for the marked point
n+1
,

(3.3)

\pi:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}.

Consider a pair
(V,\eta)
, consisting of an
N
-dimensional complex vector space
V
equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric 2-form
\eta
. We assume that
N
is finite. A cohomological field theory consists of a system of tensors

(3.4)

\Omega_{g,n}\in H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes(V^{*})^{\otimes n}

defined for all stable cases with
2g-2+n>0
. For
n=0
,
\Omega_{g,0}\in H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g},{\mathbb{Q}})
. The elements
\Omega_{g,n}
must satisfy the following conditions

i)

Each
\Omega_{g,n}
is
S_{n}
-invariant, where the symmetric group
S_{n}
acts by permutation of both the
n
marked points on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and the
n
copies of
V^{*}
.

ii)

The collection
\Omega
is compatible with the gluing maps

(3.5)

\begin{split}q^{*}\Omega_{g,n}(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{n})&=\Omega_{g-1,n+2}(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{n}\otimes\Delta),\\
p^{*}\Omega_{g,n}(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{n})&=\Omega_{g_{1},n_{1}+1}\otimes\Omega_{g_{2},n_{2}+1}\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n_{1}}v_{j}\otimes\Delta\otimes\bigotimes_{j=n_{1}+1}^{n}v_{j}\right)\end{split}

for all
v_{j}\in V
. Here
\Delta\in V^{\otimes 2}
is the bivector dual to
\eta
and
q^{*},p^{*}
are the pull-backs of (
3.1
), (
3.2
).

If the space
V
also contains a distinguished
unit
element
{{\mathbbm{1}}}\in V
, it must also satisfy

iii)

(3.6)

\Omega_{0,3}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes{\mathbbm{1}})=\eta(v_{1},v_{2})

and

(3.7)

\begin{split}\Omega_{g,n+1}(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{n}\otimes{\mathbbm{1}})&=\pi^{*}\Omega_{g,n}(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{n})\end{split}

for all
v_{j}\in V
. Here
\pi^{*}
is the pull-back of the forgetful map (
3.3
). Equation
(
3.6
) is essentially a non-degeneracy condition.

Definition 2
.

A collection of tensors
\Omega_{g,n}
satisfying properties
i)
and
ii)
is a
cohomological field theory
(CohFT). If
iii)
is also satisfied, then it is a
CohFT with flat unit
. A CohFT is
degenerate
if (
3.6
) is not satisfied.

A CohFT defines a commutative and associative
quantum product

\bullet
on
V

(3.8)

\eta(v_{1}\bullet v_{2},v_{3})=\Omega_{0,3}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes v_{3}).

If a CohFT has a unit, then
{\mathbbm{1}}
is the identity for the quantum product.

With any CohFT we associate its
partition function

(3.9)

Z_{\Omega}=\exp\left(\sum_{g,n}\frac{\hbar^{2g-2+n}}{n!}\sum_{k_{j}}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\Omega_{g,n}(e_{a_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes e_{a_{n}})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\psi_{j}^{k_{j}}T_{k_{j}}^{a_{j}}\right)\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf T}][\![\hbar]\!]

with respect to a basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{N}\}
of
V
. The partition function depends on the choice of the basis, however, the functions associated with different basis are related to each other by linear changes of variables
{\bf T}
.
Here and below the summation is over all stable cases, and contributions with
2g-2+n\leq 0
vanish by definition. In the framework of Gromov–Witten theory these partition functions are the ancestor potentials.

Remark 3.1
.

A fundamental example of CohFT is given by the trivial CohFT

(3.10)

V={\mathbb{C}},\quad\eta(e_{1},e_{1})=1,\quad{\mathbbm{1}}=e_{1},\quad\Omega_{g,n}(e_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes e_{1})=1.

Its partition function is given by the Kontsevich–Witten tau-function, described in Section
2
.

3.2.
Semi-simple CohFTs and normalized canonical basis

A finite dimensional algebra given by the quantum product is
semi-simple
if there exists a basis
e_{a}^{\can}\in V
, such that

(3.11)

e_{a}^{\can}\bullet e_{b}^{\can}=\delta_{ab}e_{a}^{\can}.

No summation over
a
is assumed here.
A CohFT is semi-simple if the algebra
(V,\bullet)
is semi-simple. Below in this section we consider only the semi-simple cases.
In the
canonical
basis
e_{a}^{\can}
the form
\eta
is also diagonal

(3.12)

\eta(e_{a}^{\can},e_{b}^{\can})=\frac{\delta_{a,b}}{\Delta_{a}}.

Remark 3.2
.

The set
(V,\eta,\bullet)
describes a semi-simple Frobenius algebra. By certain translation one can extend this algebra, at least locally, to a Frobenius manifold
[
Man99
]
.
The basis
e_{a}^{\can}\in V
is associated with the canonical coordinates on the Frobenius manifold.

The degree
0
part

(3.13)

\Omega^{0}_{g,n}\in H^{0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes(V^{*})^{\otimes n}

of a CohFT
\Omega
is a
topological quantum field theory
(TQFT). It is uniquely determined by
\Omega_{0,3}=\Omega_{0,3}^{0}
and the bilinear form
\eta
. If
\Omega
is
a CohFT with a flat unit, then TQFT
\Omega^{0}_{g,n}
is also a CohFT with a flat unit.
The trivial CohFT, described in Remark
3.1
, is a TQFT with
\Delta=1
.

We also introduce a
normalized canonical
basis
\tilde{e}_{a}:=\sqrt{\Delta_{a}}e_{a}^{\can}
where we fix some choice of the sign of the square root. This basis is orthonormal,

(3.14)

\eta(\tilde{e}_{a},\tilde{e}_{b})=\delta_{a,b}.

TQFT corresponding to a semi-simple CohFT is completely described by

(3.15)

\Omega^{0}_{g,n}(\tilde{e}_{a_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\tilde{e}_{a_{n}})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\Delta_{a}^{g-1+\frac{n}{2}}}\quad\quad&\mathrm{if}\,\,\,\tilde{e}_{a_{1}}=\dots=\tilde{e}_{a_{n}}=\tilde{e}_{a},\\[2.0pt]
\displaystyle{0}&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proposition 3.1
.

A semi-simple CohFT always contains a unit element
{\mathbbm{1}}
satisfying (
3.6
). Moreover, this unit element is always flat for the corresponding TQFT.

Proof.

By (
3.15
)

(3.16)

{\mathbbm{1}}=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\frac{\tilde{e}_{a}}{\sqrt{\Delta_{a}}}

satisfies (
3.6
). For the corresponding TQFT
\Omega^{0}_{g,n}
this unit is flat, as follows from (
3.15
) and (
3.7
).
∎

If not specified otherwise, below we will work in the normalized canonical basis and
V
can be identified with
{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
.

3.3.
Givental–Teleman group

Givental
[
Giv01a
]
introduced a group action on the CohFT partition functions. Givental’s group action can be lifted to the action on the space of CohFTs themselves
[
Sha09
,
Tel12
]
. This approach uses the stable graphs; we do not consider it here and focus on the action on the partition functions, for more details see
[
Pan18
,
PPZ15
,
Nor23
]

The loop group
LGL(V)
consists of
GL(V)
valued formal series in
z
. The elements
U(z)\in LGL(V)
satisfying the symplectic condition

(3.17)

U(z)U^{\star}(-z)=\Id

constitute the
twisted loop group

L^{(2)}GL(V)\subset LGL(V)
. Here
\star
denotes the adjoint with respect to the metric
\eta
.

We consider the so-called upper-triangular and lower-triangular subgroups, respectively
L^{(2)}_{+}GL(V)
and
L^{(2)}_{-}GL(V)
. Below we denote the elements of
L^{(2)}_{+}GL(V)
(respectively
L^{(2)}_{-}GL(V)
) by R (respectively S).

Definition 3
.

An
R
-matrix is an element

(3.18)

R(z)=\Id+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}R_{j}z^{j}\in\Id+z\End(V)[\![z]\!]

satisfying the symplectic condition

(3.19)

R(z)R^{\star}(-z)=\Id.

An
S
-matrix is an element

(3.20)

S(z)=\Id+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}S_{j}z^{-j}\in\Id+z^{-1}\End(V)[\![z^{-1}]\!]

satisfying the symplectic condition

(3.21)

S(z)S^{\star}(-z)=\Id.

We have
R(z)\in L^{(2)}_{+}GL(V)
and
S(z)\in L^{(2)}_{-}GL(V)
. The group
L^{(2)}_{+}GL(V)
acts on the space of semi-simple CohFTs
[
Tel12
]
,
\Omega\rightarrow R\Omega
.

Let

. For a CohFT
\Omega
we define a collection of cohomology classes

(3.22)

T\Omega_{g,n}(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{n})=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{m!}\pi_{m,*}\Omega_{g,n+m}(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{n}\otimes T(\psi_{n+1})\otimes\dots\otimes T(\psi_{n+m})),

where

is the map forgetting the last

3.4.
Givental–Teleman group action on the partition functions

Following Givental
[
Giv01a
]
, on the space

of formal Laurent series in

(3.24)

\Omega(f,g)=\Res_{z}\eta(f(-z),g(z)).

For the natural decomposition

(3.25)

{H}={H}_{+}\oplus{H}_{-},

where the subspaces
{H}_{+}=\sspan_{V}\{1,z,z^{2},\dots\}
and
{H}_{-}=\sspan_{V}\{z^{-1},z^{-2},z^{-3},\dots\}
are Lagrangian, we introduce the Darboux coordinate system
(p_{k}^{a},q_{k,a})
with

(3.26)

{\mathcal{J}}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(q_{k}^{a}z^{k}\tilde{e}_{a}+(-z)^{-k-1}p_{k,a}\tilde{e}^{a}\right)\in H.

Here
\tilde{e}^{a}
is a basis in
V
dual to
\tilde{e}_{a}
with respect to
\eta
. Since
\{\tilde{e}_{a}\}
is the normalized canonical basis, we have
\tilde{e}^{a}=\tilde{e}_{a}
.

Definition 5
.

An operator
A:H\rightarrow H
is an
infinitesimal symplectic transformation
if

(3.27)

\Omega(Af,g)+\Omega(f,Ag)=0.

Operator
B:H\rightarrow H
is a
symplectic transformation
if

(3.28)

\Omega(Bf,Bg)-\Omega(f,g)=0.

After Givental, for an infinitesimal symplectic transformation
A

we consider the quadratic Hamiltonian

(3.29)

H_{A}:=\frac{1}{2}\Omega(A{\mathcal{J}},{\mathcal{J}}).

This defines a Lie algebra isomorphism:

(3.30)

H_{\left[A,B\right]}=\left\{H_{A},H_{B}\right\},

where the Poisson bracket is given by

(3.31)

\left\{H_{A},H_{B}\right\}:=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\partial H_{A}}{\partial p_{i,a}}\frac{\partial H_{B}}{\partial q_{i}^{a}}-\frac{\partial H_{B}}{\partial p_{i,a}}\frac{\partial H_{A}}{\partial q_{i}^{a}}\right).

Using the standard Weyl quantization we quantize these operators to order
\leq 2
linear differential operators

(3.32)

\widehat{A}=\widehat{H}_{A}:=\frac{1}{2}{\scriptstyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{*}{*}}}\Omega(A\widehat{\mathcal{J}},\widehat{\mathcal{J}}){\scriptstyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{*}{*}}},

where

(3.33)

\widehat{\mathcal{J}}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(z^{k}T_{k}^{a}\tilde{e}_{a}+(-z)^{-k-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}^{a}}\tilde{e}^{a}\right)

and
{\scriptstyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{*}{*}}}\dots{\scriptstyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{*}{*}}}
denotes the standard bosonic normal ordering, which puts all
\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}^{a}}
to the right of all

.

Remark 3.4
.

Note that we do not introduce the so-called dilaton shift here. This provides a modification of the original Givental formalism
[

,
Giv01b
]
, where the dilaton shift plays an essential role.

This leads a central extension of the original algebra, with the commutator

(3.34)

\left[\widehat{A},\widehat{B}\right]=\widehat{\left[A,B\right]}+\mathcal{C}(H_{A},H_{B}),

where the so-called 2-cocycle term satisfies

(3.35)

\mathcal{C}\left(p_{i,a}p_{j,b},q_{k}^{c}q_{m}^{d}\right)=-\mathcal{C}\left(q_{k}^{c}q_{m}^{d},p_{i,a}p_{j,b}\right)=\delta_{i,k}\delta_{j,m}\delta_{a,c}\delta_{b,d}+\delta_{i,m}\delta_{j,k}\delta_{a,d}\delta_{b,c}

and vanishes for all other elements
H_{A}
. For the symplectic transformations we define

(3.36)

\widehat{e^{A}}:=e^{\widehat{A}}.

It is easy to see that elements of the twisted loop group

describe symplectic transformations.
For the R-matrices we have

(3.37)

R(z)=\exp(r(z)),

where
r(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r_{k}z^{k}
,

are endomorphisms of

are self-adjoint for

odd and skew-self-adjoint for

even with respect to
\eta
.
Note, that we are working in the normalized canonical basis, for which

is elementary (

After quantization, using (

(3.38)

\widehat{R}:=\exp\left(\widehat{r}\right),

where

\widehat{r}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(r_{k})_{a}^{b}T_{j}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k+j}^{b}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j+1}(r_{k})^{ab}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial T_{j}^{a}\partial T_{k-j-1}^{b}}\right).

. Let us recall that the Einstein summation convention is assumed for the indices

associated with the space

.

For a translation

we put

(3.40)

\widehat{T}:=\exp\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\Delta T_{j}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{j}^{a}}\right).

Let us also introduce the operator
\widehat{\Delta}
acting on the product of the KW tau-functions as

\widehat{\Delta}\cdot\prod_{a=1}^{N}\tau_{1}(\hbar,{\bf{T}}^{a}):=\prod_{a=1}^{N}\tau_{1}(\hbar\sqrt{\Delta_{a}},{\bf{T}}^{a}).

The right-hand side is a partition function of TQFT described by (

)–(

By Theorem

Givental’s decomposition formula
:

Corollary 3.4

For the CohFT associated with a Frobenius algebra

and an element

4.2.
Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion

CohFTs, including the degenerate ones associated with the Norbury classes, are closely related to another universal construction of mathematical physics. The Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion allows us to recover an infinite tower of correlation functions starting from a (not necessarily globally defined)
spectral curve

C
, two meromorphic differentials on it,
\hbox{d}x
and
\hbox{d}y
, and a canonical bilinear differential
B(z_{1},z_{2})
for
z_{1},z_{2}\in C
. We call the set

(4.6)

S=\left(C,B,x,y\right)

the
topological recursion data
. For the specific choices of the topological recursion data obtained differentials encode different invariants of mathematics and physics, e.g. ones related to the Gromov–Witten theory, knots, topological strings and Hurwitz numbers. Let us briefly review the formalism of topological recursion, see
[
EO07
,
EO09
,
CN19
]
for more details.

Let
C
be a Riemann surface (actually, it can be an open subset of a Riemann surface, possibly non-compact, that is, the spectral curve can be
local
). On this curve we consider two functions,
x
and
y
; sometimes the spectral curve can be specified by these functions. More specifically, we consider the differential
\hbox{d}x
with an assumption that it is a meromorphic differential with finitely many simple critical points
p_{1},\dots,p_{N}
. Below we call these points the
simple ramification points
. We assume that the function
y
is meromorphic in the neighbourhoods of the zeros of
\hbox{d}x
. We allow the spectral curve to be
irregular

[
CN19
]
, that is, the singularities of
y
(which are at most simple poles) may coincide with the zeroes of
\hbox{d}x
. The bidifferential
B
is symmetric on
C\times C
, with a double pole on the diagonal with double residue equal to 1, and has no other poles in the neighbourhoods of the zeros of
\hbox{d}x
.
In particular, on
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1}
the canonical bilinear differential is the Cauchy differentiation kernel

(4.7)

B_{C}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{\hbox{d}z_{1}\hbox{d}z_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}},

where
z
is a global coordinate.

With this input, on the Cartesian product
C^{n}
we construct a system of symmetric differentials (or
correlators
)
\omega_{g,n}
,
g\geq 0
,
n\geq 1
, given by

(4.8)

\displaystyle\omega_{0,1}(z_{1})=y(z_{1})\hbox{d}x(z_{1});

\displaystyle\omega_{0,2}(z_{1},z_{2})=B(z_{1},z_{2});

where
z
are some local coordinates in the neighbourhoods of the zeros of
\hbox{d}x
.
For
2g-2+n>0
we use the
Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion

(4.9)

\begin{split}\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\coloneqq\ &\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Res_{z\to p_{i}}\frac{\int^{z}_{\sigma_{i}(z)}B_{C}(z_{1},\cdot)}{\omega_{0,1}(z_{1})-\omega_{0,1}(\sigma_{i}(z_{1}))}\Bigg(\omega_{g-1,n+1}(z,\sigma_{i}(z),z_{\llbracket n\rrbracket\setminus\{1\}})\\
&+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g_{1}+g_{2}=g,I_{1}\sqcup I_{2}={\llbracket n\rrbracket\setminus\{1\}}\\
(g_{1},|I_{1}|),(g_{2},|I_{2}|)\not=(0,0)\end{subarray}}\omega_{g_{1},1+|I_{1}|}(z,z_{I_{1}})\omega_{g_{2},1+|I_{2}|}(\sigma_{i}(z),z_{I_{2}})\Bigg),\end{split}

where
\sigma_{i}
is the deck transformation of
x
near
p_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,N
. For the stable cases
2g-2+n>0
these differentials are meromorphic. We would not go into the discussion of the version of this topological recursion, for more detail e.g.
[
EO07
]
. The topological recursion (
4.9
) has a natural description in terms of graphs.

The
g
th
symplectic invariant
for
g\geq 2
associated with the spectral recursion data
S
is defined by

(4.10)

F^{(g)}=\frac{1}{2-2g}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Res_{z\to p_{i}}\Phi(z)\omega_{g,1}(z),

where
\Phi(z)=\int^{z}y\hbox{d}x
is a primitive of
y(z)\hbox{d}x(z)
. Definitions of
F^{(0)}
and
F^{(1)}
can be found in
[
EO07
]
. We will identify
\omega_{g,0}=F^{(g)}
. All correlators
\omega_{g,n}

with
2-2g-n<0
are called
stable
, and the others are called unstable.

For any topological recursion data
S
for
a=1,\dots,N
let us define the auxiliary differentials on
C

(4.11)

\xi^{a}_{0}(q)=\Res_{p=p_{a}}\frac{B(p,q)}{\sqrt{2(x(p)-x(p_{a}))}},\quad\xi^{a}_{k}=-\hbox{d}\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^{k-1}\frac{\xi^{a}_{0}}{\hbox{d}x},\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},

where
p,q\in C
.
The correlators
\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
, obtained by the topological recursion (
4.9
), are polynomials in
\xi^{a}_{k}
.
With a topological recursion data
S
we associate a generating function made of only stable correlators with neglected unstable terms,

(4.12)

Z_{S}=\exp\left(\sum_{2g-2+n>0}\frac{\hbar^{2g-2+n}}{n!}\omega_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\Big|_{\xi^{a}_{k}=T_{k}^{a}}\right).

Let us consider the simplest case with one critical point of
\hbox{d}x
, that is,
N=1
. On the genus zero spectral curve
C=\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1}
with a fixed global coordinate
z
, we consider a differential
\hbox{d}x
with a unique zero. The KW tau-function
\tau_{1}({\bf t})
is described by the topological recursion on the Airy curve
x=\frac{1}{2}y^{2}

[
EO09
]

(4.13)

S=\left(\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1},B_{C},\frac{1}{2}z^{2},z\right).

It was conjectured in
[
Ale18
]
and proven in
[
DN18
]
that the BGW tau-function
\tau_{0}({\bf t})
is described by the topological recursion on the Bessel curve
xy^{2}=\frac{1}{2}
with the topological recursion data

(4.14)

S=\left(\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1},B_{C},\frac{1}{2}z^{2},\frac{1}{z}\right).

For these cases the stable correlators of topological recursion are related to the intersection numbers by

(4.15)

\omega_{g,n}^{\alpha}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\hbox{d}_{1}\dots\hbox{d}_{n}\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\geq 0}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\Theta_{g,n}^{1-\alpha}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\psi_{j}^{a_{j}}\left(-\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\right)^{a_{j}}z_{j}^{-1}

for
\alpha=1
and
\alpha=0
respectively.

For these two tau-functions translation of variables
{\bf T}
leads to the change of
y
described by Proposition 4.8 of Chekhov–Norbury:

Proposition 4.2

(
[
CN19
]
)
.

The generating function of the topological recursion data

(4.16)

S=\left(\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1},B_{C},\frac{1}{2}z^{2},z^{2\alpha-1}+\sum_{k=2\alpha}^{\infty}y_{k}z^{k}\right)

is obtained via translation of the appropriate tau-function

(4.17)

Z_{S}=\tau_{\alpha}\left(\left\{T_{k}-\frac{(2k-1)!!}{\hbar}y_{2k-1}\right\}\right).

Note that only
T_{k}
with
k\geq\alpha+1
are translated.

Remark 4.1
.

Actually, the proposition of Chekhov–Norbury is more general and describes
y=\sum_{k=2\alpha-1}^{\infty}y_{k}z^{k}
with
y_{2\alpha-1}\neq 1
.
We do not need this more general version here.

4.3.
From topological recursion to Givental’s decomposition

The general relationship between the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion and intersection numbers on the moduli spaces was investigated by Eynard in
[
Eyn14
]
.
In
[
DBOSS14
]
Dunin-Barkowski, Orantin, Shadrin, and Spitz prove that the partition function of a semi-simple CohFT with flat unit in the Givental decomposition form coincides with the partition function of the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion on a certain regular local spectral curve. The identification uses the comparison between two graph expansions. Later Chekhov and Norbury
[
CN19
]
generalized this identification to the case of CohFTs without flat unit. In general, they correspond to the topological recursion on the irregular spectral curves. In this section we recall the identification of the main ingredients of two theories. We mostly follow
[
CN19
,
Nor23
]
.

Let
S
be an arbitrary topological recursion data with local spectral curve (possibly irregular), introduced in the previous section.
With any critical point
p_{a}
,
a=1,\dots,N
, we associate an index
\alpha_{a}\in\{0,1\}
such that
\alpha_{a}=1
for regular critical points and
\alpha_{a}=0
for the irregular critical points.
In the neighbourhood of a critical point
p_{a}
we consider a local coordinate
\zeta
such that
x=x(p_{a})+\zeta^{2}/2
. Then in this neighbourhood

(4.18)

y=\sum_{k=\alpha_{a}-1}^{\infty}y_{k}^{a}\zeta^{k}

with
y_{2\alpha_{a}-1}^{a}\neq 0
.
We put

(4.19)

\Delta_{a}=\left(y_{2\alpha_{a}-1}^{a}\right)^{-2}.

Under the
rescaling

y\mapsto\epsilon y
the correlators of the topological recursion change as

(4.20)

\omega_{g,n}\mapsto\epsilon^{2-2g-n}\omega_{g,n}.

Therefore, the generating function
\tau_{\alpha_{a}}(\hbar\sqrt{\Delta_{a}},{\bf T^{a}})
, obtained by the action of the operator
\widehat{\Delta}

(4.21)

\widehat{\Delta}\prod_{a=1}^{N}\tau_{\alpha_{a}}(\hbar,{\bf T^{a}})=\prod_{a=1}^{N}\tau_{\alpha_{a}}(\hbar\sqrt{\Delta_{a}},{\bf T^{a}}),

describes the partition function of the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion on the curve
S=\left(\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1},B_{C},\frac{1}{2}z^{2},z/\sqrt{\Delta_{a}}\right)
if
\alpha_{a}=1
or
S=\left(\mathbb{C}\mathrm{P}^{1},B_{C},\frac{1}{2}z^{2},\frac{1}{z\sqrt{\Delta_{a}}}\right)

if
\alpha_{a}=0
.

Let

(4.22)

T^{a}(z)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{z\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\Delta_{a}}{2\pi z}}\int_{\gamma_{a}}\hbox{d}y\,e^{\frac{x(p_{a})-x}{z}}\right)},\quad&p_{a}\,\,\,\mathrm{regular},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle{1-\sqrt{\frac{\Delta_{a}}{2\pi z}}\int_{\gamma_{a}}y\hbox{d}x\,e^{\frac{x(p_{a})-x}{z}}},\quad&p_{a}\,\,\,\mathrm{irregular},\end{cases}

where
\gamma_{a}
is an arc of the steepest descent contour around the critical point
p_{a}
and we take the asymptotic expansion of the integral at small
|z|
. We have
T^{a}(z)=\sum_{k=1+\alpha_{a}}\Delta T_{k}^{a}z^{k}
with

(4.23)

\Delta T_{k}^{a}=-(2k-1)!!\frac{y_{2k-1}^{a}}{y_{2\alpha_{a}-1}^{a}}.

For the regular points we put
\Delta T_{1}^{a}:=0
.
Consider the corresponding translation operator

(4.24)

\widehat{T}=\exp\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Delta T_{k}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}^{a}}\right).

Using (
4.11
) we also introduce the matrix

(4.25)

\left(R^{-1}(z)\right)^{a}_{b}=-\sqrt{\frac{z}{2\pi}}\int_{\gamma_{b}}\xi^{a}_{0}e^{\frac{x(p_{b})-x}{z}}

and its quantization
\widehat{R}
given by (
3.38
).

Remark 4.2
.

To get the CohFT with a flat unit we have to consider the regular spectral curve, where the bidifferential
B
and the function
y
are related by

(4.26)

\left(R^{-1}(z)\right)^{a}_{b}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta_{b}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}\int_{\gamma_{a}}\hbox{d}y\,e^{\frac{x(p_{a})-x}{z}}.

In this case
R(z)
and
T(z)
satisfy (
3.23
).

Givental’s decomposition of the partition function for the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion is described by the following theorem.

Theorem 7

(
[
DBOSS14
,
CN19
]
)
.

Consider topological recursion data
S=(C,B,x,y)
with a local (possible irregular) spectral curve
S
. There exist operators
\widehat{R}
,
\widehat{T}
and
\widehat{\Delta}
defined by (
4.25
), (
4.24
), and (

) determined explicitly by

, such that

(4.27)

Z_{S}=\widehat{R}\widehat{T}\widehat{\Delta}\cdot\prod_{a=1}^{N}\tau_{\alpha_{a}}(\hbar,{\bf T^{a}}).

Let us stress that for a local spectral curve the matrix (

) in general does not satisfy the symplectic condition (
3.19
),
R\notin L^{(2)}_{+}GL(V)
. However, the operator
\widehat{R}
in (

If for the regular spectral curve the matrix (

Remark 4.3
.

It is easy to see that the approach of

is applicable to the general case of Theorem

5.
Cut-and-join operators

In this section, we prove that the partition functions of semi-simple CohFTs and Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion, considered in the previous sections, can be described by cubic cut-and-join operators. It implies, in particular, that these partition functions satisfy the algebraic topological recursion.

5.1.
Generalized ancestor potentials

Let us consider
V={\mathbb{C}}^{N}
with an orthonormal basis
\tilde{e}_{a}
. We choose
\alpha_{a}\in\{0,1\}
for
a=1,\dots,N
. For any
R(z)=\exp(r(z))\in\Id+z\End(V)[\![z]\!]
,
T(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Delta T_{j}^{a}z^{j}\tilde{e}_{a}\in zV[\![z]\!]
with
\Delta T_{1}^{a}=0
if
\alpha_{a}=1
, and
\Delta=(\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{N})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
we consider a Givental operator

(5.1)

\widehat{R}:=\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(r_{k})_{a}^{b}T_{j}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k+j}^{b}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j+1}(r_{k})^{ab}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial T_{j}^{a}\partial T_{k-j-1}^{b}}\right)\right),

a translation operator

(5.2)

\widehat{T}:=\exp\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Delta T_{k}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}^{a}}\right),

and a rescaling operator

(5.3)

\widehat{\Delta}\cdot\prod_{a=1}^{N}\tau_{\alpha_{a}}(\hbar,{\bf{T}}^{a}):=\prod_{a=1}^{N}\tau_{\alpha_{a}}(\hbar\sqrt{\Delta_{a}},{\bf{T}}^{a})

introduced in (
3.38
), (
4.24
), (
3.41
) respectively.

Definition 7
.

With the operators
\widehat{R}
,
\widehat{T}
, and
\widehat{\Delta}
we define the
generalized ancestor potential

(5.4)

Z({\bf T}):=\widehat{R}\widehat{T}\widehat{\Delta}\cdot\prod_{a=1}^{N}\tau_{\alpha_{a}}(\hbar,{\bf T^{a}}).

Below we show that this is a well defined element of
{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf T}][\![\hbar]\!]
.

If
\alpha_{a}=1
for all

and

, then
(R(z),T(z))
is nothing but an arbitrary element of the Givental–Teleman group, and

coincides with the partition function

of the corresponding semi-simple CohFT in the coordinates associated with normalized canonical basis, see Corollary
3.4
. If
\alpha_{a}=0
for all

and

, then

coincides with the partition function
Z_{\Omega^{\Theta}}
of semi-simple CohFT coupled to
\Theta
classes, see Proposition
4.1
. If
R(z)
,
T(z)
and
\Delta
are given by (
4.25
), (
4.22
), and (

) respectively, then

coincides with the partition function
Z_{S}
of the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion on a possibly irregular local spectral curve, see Theorem
7
.

In this section, we construct a cut-and-join description for the generalized ancestor potential
Z
. The cut-and-join operators for the tau-functions
\tau_{\alpha_{a}}
are described by Theorem
5
,
their deformations given by the subsequent action of the operators
\widehat{\Delta}
,
\widehat{T}
, and
\widehat{R}
are described correspondingly in Sections
5.2
,
5.3
, and
5.4
.

5.2.
Cut-and-join operators for semi-simple TQFTs

Let

(5.5)

\widehat{J}(z)={\rm diag}\,\left(\widehat{J}^{1}(z),\dots,\widehat{J}^{N}(z)\right)

be a diagonal matrix with the entries
\widehat{J}^{a}(z)=\sum\widehat{J}_{k}^{a}z^{-2k}
given by the bosonic current (

{\bf T}^{a}
. Let also

and
\widehat{L}(z)={\scriptstyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{*}{*}}}\widehat{J}(z)^{2}{\scriptstyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{*}{*}}}
.
We consider the operator

(5.7)

\widehat{W}_{D}=\Res_{z}{\rm Tr}\,v(z)\left(\widehat{J}(z)_{+}\widehat{L}(z)+\frac{1}{8z^{2}}\widehat{J}(z)\right),

where we take the trace in the space
V
.
By Theorem

(5.8)

\prod_{a=1}^{N}\tau_{\alpha_{a}}(\hbar,{\bf T^{a}})=\exp\left(\hbar\widehat{W}_{D}\right)\cdot 1.

Let
\sqrt{\Delta}={\rm diag}\,(\sqrt{\Delta_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{\Delta_{N}})

Remark 5.7
.

We construct the cut-and-join operator in the normalized canonical basis, however, the form of the cut-and-join operator (

.

The coefficients of the topological expansion of a generalized ancestor potential satisfy the algebraic topological recursion

is less or equal to

(or

\alpha_{a}=0

(5.58)

consists of contributions with
(g,n)=(0,3)
and
(g,n)=(1,1)
.

Remark 5.8
.

For CohFTs associated with the Gromov–Witten theory the partition function
Z
is a generating function of the Gromov–Witten ancestor invariants. In this case, to arrive at the gravitational descendants one has to apply a properly chosen element

supplemented by certain translations. It describes the so-called calibration of the Frobenius structure. The meaning of the group
L^{(2)}_{-}GL(V)
for general CohFTs is not clear yet.

It is easy to see that such action does not respect the form (
5.47

However, we expect that for the homogeneous semi-simple Frobenius manifolds with flat unit and the calibration based on the solution of the Dubrovin’s connection there exists a cubic cut-and-join operator, which describes the descendant generating function up to the unstable terms.

5.5.
Bosonic Fock space formalism

Theorem
8
can be reformulated in terms of the bosonic Fock space. Let us consider the bosonic operators
J_{k}^{a}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
a\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
satisfying the commutation relations

(5.59)

\left[{J}_{k}^{a},{J}_{m}^{b}\right]=(2k-1)\delta_{k+m,1}\delta_{a,b}.

By definition, the Fock space
\mathcal{F}
is generated by commuting creation operators
J_{k}^{a}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}
. These operators act on the vacuum vector
\left|0\right>
, while the operators
J_{k}^{a}
with
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
annihilate the vacuum,

(5.60)

J_{k}^{a}\left|0\right>=0,\quad k>0.

For the co-vacuum
\left<0\right|
we have

(5.61)

\left<0\right|J_{k}^{a}=0,\quad k\leq 0,

and this property together with the relation
\left<0\right|1\left|0\right>=1
fixes a map
{\mathcal{F}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
. Then the identity (
5.49
) has an equivalent form

(5.62)

Z=\left<0\right|e^{\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{a=1}^{N}T_{k}^{a}J_{k+1}^{a}}e^{\sum_{{i\leq j\leq k,i+j+k\geq 0}}A_{a,b,c}^{i,j,k}{J}^{a}_{i}{J}^{b}_{j}{J}^{c}_{k}+\sum_{j=-1}^{\infty}{B}_{a}^{j}{J}^{a}_{j}}\left|0\right>.

6.

In this section, we describe
N

5.4
). In particular, it gives the Virasoro constraints for the partition functions of all semi-simple CohFTs and Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion for possibly irregular local spectral curves with simple ramification points.

Remark 6.1
.

A family of the Virasoro constraints for the case of homogenous semi-simple CohFT with a flat unit was constructed by Milanov
[
Mil14
]
. As it is mentioned in
[

Here we generalize this result.

We also derive a version of the dimension constraint for a generalized ancestor potential and show how the cut-and-join equation follows from this constraint and the Virasoro constraints. Moreover, we describe possible ambiguities of the cut-and-join operator.

6.1.

2.15
) in the variables
{\bf T}^{a}
.
After conjugation with the translation operator

(6.3)

\widehat{T}=\exp\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Delta T_{k}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}^{a}}\right)

we have

(6.4)

\widehat{T}\widehat{\Delta}\widehat{L}_{k}^{\alpha_{a}}\widehat{\Delta}^{-1}\widehat{T}^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\widehat{L}_{k}({\bf T}^{a})-\frac{(2k+2\alpha_{a}+1)!!}{2\hbar\sqrt{\Delta_{a}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k+\alpha_{a}}^{a}}\\
+\frac{1}{2\hbar}\sum_{m=1+\alpha_{a}}^{\infty}\frac{(2k+2m+1)!!}{(2m-1)!!}\Delta T_{m}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k+m}^{a}}+\frac{\delta_{k,0}}{16}.

Then we have

(6.5)

\widehat{L}_{k}^{a}=\frac{1}{2}\widehat{L}_{k}({\bf T}^{a})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,\ell,\\
\ell-m>k\end{subarray}}X^{a,c;m}_{b;k,\ell}T^{b}_{m}\frac{\partial}{\partial T^{c}_{\ell}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,\ell,\\
\ell+m\geq k\end{subarray}}V^{a,b,c}_{k,m,\ell}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial T_{m}^{b}\partial T_{\ell}^{c}}\\
-\frac{1}{2\hbar}\sum_{m=k+\alpha_{a}}U^{a,b}_{k,m}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{m}^{b}}+\frac{\delta_{k,0}}{16}-\frac{\delta_{k,-1}}{2}p_{a}.

Here
X^{a,c;m}_{b;k,\ell}
,
V^{a,b,c}_{k,m,\ell}
,
U^{a,b}_{k,m}
, and
p^{a}=(r_{1})^{aa}
do not depend on
\hbar
and
{\bf T}
. The leading coefficient
U^{a,b}_{k,m}
is not affected by conjugation by
\widehat{R}
and coincides with the one in (
6.2
),

(6.6)

U^{a,b}_{k,k+\alpha_{a}}=\delta_{a,b}\frac{(2k+2\alpha_{a}+1)!!}{\sqrt{\Delta_{a}}}.

From the upper triangular structure of the operator
\widehat{R}
it is clear that all coefficients
X^{a,c;m}_{b;k,\ell}
,
V^{a,b,c}_{k,m,\ell}
and
U^{a,b}_{k,m}
are well defined. They are polynomials in the entries of the matrices
r_{k}
and depend at most linearly on
\Delta_{a}^{-1/2}
and
\Delta T_{m}^{a}
.

The operators satisfy the Virasoro commutation relations

(6.7)

\left[\widehat{L}_{k}^{a},\widehat{L}_{m}^{b}\right]=\delta^{ab}(k-m)\widehat{L}_{k+m}^{a}.

Therefore, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 9
.

A generalized ancestor potential satisfies the Virasoro constraints

(6.8)

\widehat{L}_{m}^{a}\cdot Z=0,\quad\quad a=1\dots,N,\quad m\geq-\alpha_{a}.

Remark 6.2
.

A linear change of variables by an operator
\widehat{\Psi}
of the form (
5.14
) does not affect the statement of the theorem,
but changes the coefficients in the operators
\widehat{L}_{k}^{a}
leaving it quadratic in
\widehat{J}^{b}_{j}
.

When
R(z)\in L^{(2)}_{+}GL(V)
, the coefficients of the operators
\widehat{L}_{k}^{a}
can be found with the help of Givental’s quantization and the commutation relations (
3.34
).

The Virasoro constraints of Dubrovin–Zhang and Givental
[
DZ99
,
Giv01a
]
correspond to the diagonal combination of the operators (
6.5
),

(6.9)

\widehat{L}_{m}=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\widehat{L}_{m}^{a}.

For the particular choices of the operators
\widehat{R}
,
\widehat{T}
and
\widehat{\Delta}
, described in Sections
3
and
4
, the partition function (
5.4
) describes the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion and semi-simple CohFTs. Therefore, we immediately have the following corollaries.

Corollary 6.1
.

The partition function of the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion for a (possibly irregular) local spectral curve with simple ramification points
satisfies the Virasoro constraints (
6.8
),

(6.10)

\widehat{L}_{m}^{a}\cdot Z_{S}=0,\quad\quad a=1\dots,N,\quad m\geq-\alpha_{a}.

Corollary 6.2
.

The partition function of a semi-simple CohFT satisfies the Virasoro constraints (
6.8
),

(6.11)

\widehat{L}_{m}^{a}\cdot Z_{\Omega}=0,\quad\quad a=1\dots,N,\quad m\geq-\alpha_{a}.

The last corollary is a generalization of the results by Milanov
[
Mil14
]
for the cases without flat unit.

6.2.
Uniqueness of the solution

By construction, a solution for the Virasoro constraints (
6.8
) exists.
Let us show that the Virasoro constraints (
6.8
) have a unique (up to a normalization) solution with topological expansion. To prove it we need a simple lemma.

Let for some finite
\tilde{k}<\infty
,

(6.12)

\widehat{O}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\tilde{k}}\widehat{O}_{k}

be a differential operator in
{\bf T}
where
\deg\widehat{O}_{k}=k
(see (
2.27
)).

Lemma 6.3
.

If the operator
\widehat{O}
is independent of
\hbar
,
then the equation

(6.13)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2k+1)T_{k}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}^{a}}Z=\hbar\,\widehat{O}\cdot Z

has a unique, up to normalization, solution
with the topological expansion,
Z\in{\mathbb{C}}[\![{\bf T}]\!][\![\hbar]\!]
. Moreover, the coefficients of the topological expansion of the solution are polynomials,
Z\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf T}][\![\hbar]\!]
.

Proof.

For a solution with the topological expansion

(6.14)

Z({\bf T})=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}Z^{(m)}\hbar^{m}

the coefficients satisfy

(6.15)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2k+1)T_{k}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}^{a}}Z^{(m)}=\widehat{O}\cdot Z^{(m-1)}.

For
m=0
we have

(6.16)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2k+1)T_{k}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}^{a}}Z^{(0)}=0,

therefore
Z^{(0)}\in{\mathbb{C}}
does not depend on
{\bf T}
. Let us show that all higher
Z^{(m)}
are polynomials in
{\bf T}
, which can be uniquely determined from equation (
6.15
) and the initial condition
Z^{(0)}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Namely, for
Z^{(m)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Z^{(m)}_{k}
, where
\deg Z^{(m)}_{k}=k
, we have

(6.17)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2k+1)T_{k}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}^{a}}Z^{(m)}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kZ^{(m)}_{k}.

Then the equation

(6.18)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}kZ^{(m)}_{k}=\widehat{O}\cdot Z^{(m-1)}

determines all
Z^{(m)}_{k}
from
Z^{(m-1)}
. Since
\tilde{k}<+\infty
, the sum over
k
is finite and
Z^{(m)}_{k}=0
for
k>m\tilde{k}
, therefore
Z^{(m)}
is polynomial. The recursion does not define
Z^{(m)}_{0}
, therefore
Z({\bf T})
is defined up to a normalization
Z({\bf 0})\in{\mathbb{C}}[\![\hbar]\!]
.
∎

If
\widehat{O}_{k}=0
for all
k>0
, then the only solution is
Z({\bf T})=Z({\bf 0})\in{\mathbb{C}}[\![\hbar]\!]
.

Proposition 6.4
.

The Virasoro constraints (
6.8
) have a unique, up to normalization
Z({\bf 0})\in{\mathbb{C}}[\![\hbar]\!]
, solution of the form
Z({\bf T})\in{\mathbb{C}}[\![{\bf T}]\!][\![\hbar]\!]
.

Proof.

For each
a=1,\dots,N
and
k\geq-\alpha_{a}
let us consider a linear combination of the Virasoro operators
(
6.5
) of the form

(6.19)

\widehat{K}_{k}^{a}=\widehat{L}_{k}^{a}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}Q^{a}_{k,m;b}\widehat{L}_{k+m}^{b},

such that it contains only one term proportional to
\hbar^{-1}

(6.20)

\widehat{K}_{k}^{a}=\frac{1}{2}\widehat{L}_{k}({\bf T}^{a})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,\ell,\\
\ell-m>k\end{subarray}}\tilde{X}^{a,c;m}_{b;k,\ell}T^{b}_{m}\frac{\partial}{\partial T^{c}_{\ell}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,\ell,\\
m+\ell\geq k\end{subarray}}\tilde{V}^{a,b,c}_{k,m,\ell}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial T_{m}^{b}\partial T_{\ell}^{c}}\\
-\frac{1}{2\hbar}\frac{(2k+2\alpha_{a}+1)!!}{\sqrt{\Delta_{a}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k+\alpha_{a}}^{a}}+\frac{\delta_{k,0}}{16}-\frac{\delta_{k,-1}}{2}\tilde{p}_{a}.

The matrices
Q^{a}_{k,m;b}
can be obtained from the inversion of the matrices
U^{a,b}_{k,m}
in (
6.5
) and are well defined because of the triangular structure of the latter. The coefficients
\tilde{X}^{a,c;m}_{b;k,\ell}
,
\tilde{V}^{a,b,c}_{k,m,\ell}
, and
\tilde{p}_{a}
are independent of
{\bf T}
and
\hbar
.

For these operators we also have

(6.21)

\widehat{K}_{k}^{a}\cdot Z=0,\quad\quad a=1,\dots,N,\quad k\geq-\alpha_{a}.

Let

(6.22)

\widehat{\tilde{K}}_{k;a}:=\frac{2\sqrt{\Delta_{a}}}{(2k+1)!!}\widehat{K}_{k-\alpha_{a}}^{a}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}^{a}}.

We have

(6.23)

\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}^{a}}Z=\hbar\widehat{\tilde{K}}_{k;a}\cdot Z,\quad\quad a=1,\dots,N,\quad k\geq 0,

and

(6.24)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2k+1)T_{k}^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}^{a}}Z=\hbar\,\widehat{O}\cdot Z,

where

(6.25)

\widehat{O}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2k+1)T_{k}^{a}\widehat{\tilde{K}}_{k;a}

is independent of
\hbar
. This operator
\widehat{O}
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
6.3
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

The partition function of the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion for a (possibly irregular) local spectral curve with simple ramification points
is determined by the Virasoro constraints (
6.8
) up to a normalization.

Corollary 6.6
.

The partition function of a semi-simple CohFT is determined by the Virasoro constraints (
6.8
) up to a normalization.

Remark 6.3
.

From Proposition
6.4
it follows that for the generalized ancestor potential the Virasoro constraints (
6.8
) imply the existence of the cut-and-join description of the form

(6.26)

Z=\exp\left(\hbar\widehat{W}\right)\cdot Z_{0},

where
Z_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}[\![\hbar]\!]
does not depend on
{\bf T}
.

The commutation relations between the operators
\widehat{K}_{k}^{a}
follow from (
6.7
). The operators

\widehat{K}_{k}^{a}
describe the quantum Airy structure of Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
KS18
]
. The quantum Airy structure for the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion on the regular spectral curve was described by Andersen, Borot, Chekhov, and Orantin
[
ABCO17
]
. The Virasoro constraints for the regular spectral curves was earlier considered in
[
BBC+18
]
. We see that this structure also appears for the irregular spectral curves. Moreover, we see that the quantum Airy structure for the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion on the (possibly irregular) local spectral curves with simple ramifications is equivalent to the Virasoro constraints (
6.8
).

While the choice of calibration, given by an element of the
L^{(2)}_{-}GL(V)
, breaks the quantum Airy structure, it does not spoil the Virasoro algebra of linear constraints if the resulting Virasoro operators are well defined.

We see that the Virasoro constraints, cut-and-join operators, the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion, and CohFTs are closely related to each other.

6.3.
From Virasoro constraints to cut-and-join operator

Let

(6.27)

\widehat{H}:=\widehat{R}\widehat{V}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{N}\frac{\widehat{L}_{0}({\bf T}^{a})}{2\alpha_{a}+1}\right)\widehat{V}^{-1}\widehat{R}^{-1},

where
\widehat{V}
is the Virasoro group element (
5.22
).

Proposition 6.7
.

A generalized ancestor potential satisfies

(6.28)

\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial\hbar}Z=\widehat{H}\cdot Z.

Proof.

By the dimension constraints (
2.18
),

(6.29)

\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial\hbar}\prod_{a=1}^{N}\tau_{\alpha_{a}}(\hbar,{\bf{T}}^{a})=\sum_{b=1}^{N}\frac{\widehat{L}_{0}({\bf T}^{b})}{2\alpha_{b}+1}\cdot\prod_{a=1}^{N}\tau_{\alpha_{a}}(\hbar,{\bf{T}}^{a}).

The operators
\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial\hbar}
and
\sum_{a=1}^{N}\frac{\widehat{L}_{0}({\bf T}^{a})}{1+2\alpha_{a}}
commute with the operator
\widehat{\Delta}
, the first of them also commutes with the operators
\widehat{V}
and
\widehat{R}
. The statement of the proposition follows from (
5.27
).
∎

By construction, operator
\widehat{H}
is bilinear in
\widehat{J}_{k}^{a}
,

(6.30)

\widehat{H}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j,\\
i+j\geq 1\end{subarray}}H_{a,b}^{i,j}\widehat{J}^{a}_{i}\widehat{J}^{b}_{j}.

Equation (
6.28
) fixes the normalization of the partition function
Z({\bf 0})\in{\mathbb{C}}[\![\hbar]\!]
up to an
\hbar
-dependent constant
Z({\bf 0})\Big|_{\hbar=0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

Remark 6.4
.

If
R(z)\in L^{(2)}_{+}GL(V)
, the operator
\widehat{H}
corresponds to a certain symplectic transformation and can be obtained by Givental’s quantization, considered in Section
3.4
.

With the help of the relations (
6.23
) we have

(6.31)

\widehat{H}\cdot Z=\hbar\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j,\\
i+j\geq 1\end{subarray}}H_{a,b}^{i,j}(2j-1)!!\widehat{J}^{a}_{i}\widehat{\tilde{K}}_{j-1}^{b}\cdot Z.

Therefore, by Proposition
6.7

(6.32)

\frac{\partial}{\partial\hbar}\cdot Z=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j,\\
i+j\geq 1\end{subarray}}H_{a,b}^{i,j}(2j-1)!!\widehat{J}^{a}_{i}\widehat{\tilde{K}}_{j-1}^{b}\cdot Z.

The operator in the right-hand side is of the form (
5.47
), therefore the cut-and-join description can be derived from the Virasoro constraints (
6.8
) and the deformed dimension constraints (
6.28
).

However, it is not clear if the operator in the right-hand side of (
6.32
) coincides with the operator
\widehat{W}
in (
5.47
).
Consider the operators

(6.33)

\widehat{M}_{k,m}^{a,b}=\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{m}^{a}}\widehat{\tilde{K}}_{k}^{b}-\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}^{b}}\widehat{\tilde{K}}_{m}^{a}.

We have
\widehat{M}_{k,m}^{a,b}=-\widehat{M}_{m,k}^{b,a}
, therefore, it is enough to consider
k\geq m
.
From (
6.23
) we see, that

(6.34)

\widehat{M}_{k,m}^{a,b}\cdot Z=0.

For
k\neq m
the operators
\widehat{M}_{k,m}^{a}
are non-trivial. They are cubic in
\widehat{J}^{a}_{k}
with coefficients independent of
\hbar
.
Therefore, for arbitrary
C_{a,b}^{k,m}\in{\mathbb{C}}
the operator

(6.35)

\widehat{W}(C)=\widehat{W}+\sum_{0\leq m\leq k}C_{a,b}^{k,m}\widehat{M}_{k,m}^{a,b},

where
\widehat{W}
is given by (
5.44
), describes the cut-and-join operator for the partition function
Z
,

(6.36)

\frac{\partial}{\partial\hbar}Z=\widehat{W}(C)\cdot Z.

We expect, that this is the most general form of a cubic cut-and-join operator for a generalized ancestor potential
Z
and the operator in the right-hand side of (
6.32
) is given by (
6.35
) for some
C_{a,b}^{k,m}
.

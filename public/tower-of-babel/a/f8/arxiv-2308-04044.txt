On the waist and width inequality in complete 3-manifolds with positive scalar curvature
Yevgeny Liokumovich, Zhichao Wang

Abstract
. We show that a complete non-compact 3-manifold with scalar curvature bounded below by a positive constant admits a singular foliation by surfaces of controlled area and diameter.



1.
Introduction

In
[
7
]
*§3.10, Width/Waist Conjecture Gromov conjectured that a complete manifold with scalar curvature
R\geq 6
admits a singular foliation by surfaces of area and diameter
bounded by a universal constant. This conjecture was proved for compact 3-manifolds in
[
10
]
. In this paper the result is generalized to complete non-compact 3-manifolds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a complete non-compact 3-manifold with scalar curvature
R\geq 6
. Then there exists a proper Morse function

f:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
each connected component
\Gamma
of
f^{-1}(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}_{M}(\Gamma)<18\pi;

\displaystyle\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma)\leq 33\pi.

For compact manifolds it was proved in
[
10
]
that one can construct a foliation by surfaces of
controlled area and diameter and genus at most
2
(the maximal Heegaard genus of a compact
3-manifold with positive scalar curvature). We conjecture that it should also be possible to prove a similar genus bound in Theorem
1.1
.

Recall that the
Urysohn
1
-width
of a Riemannian three-manifold
M
is the infimum of real numbers
d\geq 0
with the property that there exist a Morse function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, each connected component of
f^{-1}(t)
has diameter bounded by
d
from above. For three-manifolds with
R\geq 6
and zero first Betti number, Gromov-Lawson
[
4
]
proved an upper bound for Urysohn 1-width by considering level sets of the distance function to a fixed point; in
[
7
]
*§3.10, Property A, Gromov proved the Urysohn 1-width upper bound without the topological conditions; in
[
3
]
Chodosh and Li proved the Urysohn 1-width upper bound for
3
-dimensional mu-bubbles in
4
-manifolds with positive scalar curvature. For closed manifolds, the work of Maximo and the first author
[
10
]
implies a new proof of the Urysohn 1-width bound. Inspired by their work, the second author with Zhu proved the Urysohn 1-width upper bound for mean convex domains (possibly non-compact) with non-negative Ricci curvature in
[
15
]
. Urysohn width bounds were used in
[
2
]
to obtain topological classification results for four- and five-manifolds with positive scalar curvature. As a direct corollary, Theorem
1.1
gives a new proof of the Urysohn 1-width upper bound of three-manifolds with
R\geq 6
.

Idea of the proof

Our proof relies on methods from
[
10
]
, construction of
\mu
-bubbles and ideas of Gromov and Song
on construction of minimal hypersurfaces in non-compact manifolds (
[
5
]
,
[
14
]
, see also
[
1
]
).

We first cut the complete three-manifold
(M,g)
along minimal surfaces with index less than or equal to 1. As a result, if these surfaces are countable (e.g. the metric
g
is bumpy), then such a cutting process decomposes
M
into countably many regions. Then by adapting an argument of Gromov
[
5
]
and Song
[
14
]
,
the interior of these regions does not contain any closed minimal surfaces. These regions are said to be
geometrically prime
, a generalization of a similar notion in the compact case
[
10
]
.

The key step is to decompose the geometrically prime regions into countably many small pieces with diameter and boundary area upper bounds; see Definition
3.1
for the concept of
admissible regions
.

For prime regions with an unstable boundary component, we will use
\mu
-bubble techniques and constrained minimization to construct finitely many surfaces with controlled size to decompose it as a union of an admissible region and finitely many regions with exactly one mean convex boundary component; see Lemma
3.3
. By repeating this argument, we obtain the desired decomposition for geometrically prime regions with unstable boundary components.

For those geometrically prime regions whose boundaries are stable minimal surfaces (or the boundary is empty) we use arguments in
[
5
]

[
14
]
to obtain a two-sided surface with controlled size and non-vanishing mean curvature. Then for the mean convex region, the argument in the last paragraph gives the desired decomposition. For the mean concave region, we cut off an admissible region so that its complement consists of finitely many connected regions with the property that their boundary contains exactly one non-minimal component (the others are stable minimal surfaces). Moreover, each non-minimal boundary component has controlled size and non-vanishing mean curvature. Observe that we have dealt with these cases. Thus, by repeating the process, we obtain the desired decomposition.

It remains to construct a Morse foliation for each admissible regions. To do this, we regard the region as a subdomain in geometrically prime regions and then consider mean curvature flow starting on the mean convex surfaces. Note that the diameter upper bound may not be preserved in the flow.
To obtain the diameter bound we intersect level sets of the flow with admissible regions and make necessary surgeries
so that they have regularity of level sets of a Morse function, similarly to how it was done in
[
10
]
and
[
1
]
. Combining all of the foliations together finishes the proof.

Outline

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we present some known results.
We first introduce the bumpy metric theorem in Section
2.1
; the area and diameter bounds for minimal surfaces with index less than or equal to one and
\mu
-bubbles are provided in Section
2.2
. Finally, in Section
2.3
, we introduce the concept of geometrically prime regions and a dichotomy proposition by adapting arguments from
[
5
]

[
14
]
. Section
3
is devoted to three decomposition lemmas. They are used to decompose geometrically prime regions into compact domains of controlled diameter and boundary area.
In Section
4
, we construct foliation in each compact region and prove our main theorem.

2.
Preliminary

2.1.
Bumpy metrics

Let
M
be a complete (possibly non-compact) Riemannian manifold. We say that
metric
g
on
M
is bumpy if there are no closed minimal submanifolds with non-zero Jacobi fields. By
[
20
]
*Theorem 2.1 (see also
[
17
]
*Theorem 2.1) for any metric
g
on
M
there exists a sequence
g_{i}
of smooth bumpy metrics on
M
with
|g-g_{i}|_{C^{3}}\rightarrow 0
. Hence, without any loss of generality we can assume that our metric is bumpy.
We will also use the bumpy metric theorem with partially fixed metrics.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
20
]
*Theorem 3.1)
.

Let
(N,\gamma_{0})
be a complete Riemannian manifold. Let
U\subset N
be an open set. Consider the class
\Gamma
of smooth metrics
\gamma^{\prime}
on
N
such that
\gamma^{\prime}
and
\gamma_{0}
agree on
N\setminus U
. Then a generic metric
\gamma\in\Gamma
has the following property: if
\Sigma
is a closed, minimal immersed submanifold of
N
and if each connected component of
\Sigma
intersects
U
, then
\Sigma
has no nontrivial Jacobi fields.

2.2.
Existence of surfaces with bounded size

In this part, we present the existence of surfaces with controlled area and diameter. Those surfaces will be used to decompose the manifold into small pieces.

The classical candidate surfaces for decomposition are minimal surfaces with index less than or equal to 1. In the following result, the area estimates in the second item are from Marques-Neves
[
12
]
*Proposition A.1 and the diameter bounds are due to Schoen-Yau
[
13
]
*Theorem 1 (see also
[
4
]
).

Lemma 2.2

(
[
10
]
*Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.4)
.

Let
(M^{3},g)
be a complete three-manifold and
\Sigma\subset M
be a two-sided closed embedded minimal surface. Suppose that
R\geq 6
in a neighbourhood of
\Sigma
.

(1)

If
\Sigma
is stable, then

\mathrm{diam}_{\Sigma}\leq\frac{2\pi}{3},\quad\mathrm{Area}\leq\frac{4\pi}{3}.

(2)

If
\Sigma
has index one, then

\mathrm{diam}_{\Sigma}\leq\frac{4\pi}{3},\quad\mathrm{Area}\leq\frac{16\pi}{3}.

Schoen-Yau
[
13
]
*Theorem 1 also gives a local version for surfaces-with-boundary.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
10
]
*Theorem 2.3)
.

Let
(M^{3},g)
be a complete three-manifold and
\Sigma\subset M
be an embedded stable minimal surface with boundary. Suppose that
R\geq 6
in a neighbourhood of
\Sigma
. Then

\mathrm{dist}_{\Sigma}(x,\partial\Sigma)\leq\frac{2\pi}{3}.

This implies the following Frankel property.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(N,\partial N,g)
be a complete smooth Riemannian three-manifold with smooth boundary and
R\geq 6
. Suppose that each connected component of
\partial N
is a closed surface with non-negative mean curvature. Suppose that there are two connected component
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
that are not stable minimal surfaces. Then there is a two-sided closed stable minimal surface in
(N\setminus\partial N,g)
.

Proof.

Since
\Sigma_{i}
(
i=1,2
) is not a stable minimal surface, then
\Sigma_{i}
is either non-minimal or minimal but unstable. In either case, we can perturb it slightly to be strictly mean convex. Thus without loss of generality, we assume that
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
are mean convex. Let
\gamma
be a curve connecting
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
.
Then by taking the area minimizer among all surface that is homologous to
\Sigma_{1}
, there is a stable two-sided minimal surface intersecting
\gamma
.
Such a minimizer must be compact because of Lemma
2.2
. This completes the proof of Lemma
2.4
.
∎

After cutting along minimal surfaces with index less than or equal to 1, we will use the
\mu
-bubble technique to subdivide a manifold with positive scalar curvature into submanifolds with boundary components of controlled size.

For a subset
\mathcal{K}
of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and
r>0
, let

N_{r}^{g}(\mathcal{K})=\{x\in M:\operatorname{dist}_{(M,g)}(x,\mathcal{K})<r\}.

We will drop the superscript
g
whenever it is clear which Riemannian metric is being used.
The following lemma is a consequence of arguments in Zhu
[
22
]
*Theorem 1.1, Gromov
[
7
]
and Chodosh-Li
[
3
]
*§6.2 (see also
[
6
]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
N
be a manifold with boundary and
R\geq 6
. Suppose that each connected component of
\partial N
is a stable minimal surface. Then for each compact region
\Omega
, there exists a compact region
\hat{\Omega}
with

\Omega\subset\hat{\Omega}\subset N_{\frac{4}{3}\pi}(\Omega)

and for each connected component
\Gamma\subset\partial\hat{\Omega}\setminus\partial N
, it is one of the following cases:

(1)

\Gamma
is diffeomorphic to
S^{2}
and

\mathrm{diam}_{N}\Gamma\leq\pi;\quad\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma)<2\pi;

(2)

\Gamma
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
and

\mathrm{diam}_{N}\Gamma\leq\pi;\quad\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma)<\pi;

(3)

\Gamma
is a free boundary disc and

\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Gamma)\leq\pi\text{ for }x\in\Gamma;\quad\mathrm{diam}_{N}\Gamma\leq\frac{8}{3}\pi\quad\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma)<\pi;

Proof.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be a small perturbation of

N_{\frac{2}{3}\pi}(\Omega)=\{x\in N:\operatorname{dist}(x,\Omega)<\frac{2}{3}\pi\}
, so that
\partial\Omega_{0}
is smooth.
Let
d:N\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote a signed distance function from
\partial\Omega_{0}
,
d(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega_{0})

if
x\notin\Omega_{0}
and
d(x)=-\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega_{0})
if
x\in\Omega_{0}
. Note that
d
is Lipschitz.
Denote the interior of
N_{\frac{4}{3}\pi}(\Omega)\setminus\Omega
by
U
and let

\rho:\overline{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{0}
perturbation of
d
that is smooth and satisfies

\rho(x)=-\frac{2}{3}\pi
for
x\in\partial\Omega
,

\rho(x)=\frac{2}{3}\pi
for
x\in\partial N_{\frac{4}{3}\pi}(\Omega)

and
|\nabla\rho|<1+\varepsilon
for some small
\varepsilon<\frac{1}{100}
.
Define a smooth function
h
on
U
by

h(x):=(1+\varepsilon)\tan\Big[\frac{3}{4}\rho(x)\Big].

By
[
3
]
*Propositions 12 and 15 there exists a minimizer
\mathcal{K}
, which minimizes

\mathcal{A}^{h}(\mathcal{K}^{\prime}):=\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\setminus\partial N)-\int_{U}(\chi_{\mathcal{K}^{\prime}}-\chi_{\Omega_{0}})h\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}

among all compact region
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
satisfying

\Omega\subset\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\subset\{x\in N;\operatorname{dist}_{N}(x,\Omega)<\frac{4}{3}\pi\}.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected component of
\partial\mathcal{K}\setminus\partial N
. Then
\Gamma
is a smoothly embedded prescribed mean curvature surface with free boundary, with scalar mean curvature
H=h
and meeting
\partial N
orthogonally. The minimizing property gives that for all smooth functions
\phi
defined on
\Gamma
,

\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla\phi|^{2}-(|A|^{2}+\mathrm{Ric}(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n})+\langle\mathbf{n},\nabla h\rangle)\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}\geq\int_{\partial\Gamma}A_{\partial N}(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n})\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1},

where
A
and
A_{\partial N}
are the second fundamental forms of
\Gamma
and
\partial N
;
\mathbf{n}
is the unit normal vector field of
\Gamma
. Now let
\phi\equiv 1
. Recall that

|A|^{2}+\mathrm{Ric}(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n})=\frac{1}{2}(|A|^{2}+|H|^{2}+R)-K_{\Gamma}.

Using
|A|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}|H|^{2}
(by Cauchy inequality) and
2\pi\chi(\Gamma)=\int_{\Gamma}K_{\Gamma}d\mathcal{H}^{2}-\int_{\partial\Gamma}A_{\partial N}(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n})d\mathcal{H}^{1}
(by Gauss-Bonnet and minimality of
\partial N
) we obtain

2\pi\chi(\Gamma)\geq\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}|A|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}h^{2}+\langle\nabla h,\mathbf{n}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}R\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{2}\geq\int_{\Gamma}\frac{3}{4}h^{2}-|\nabla h|+3\geq(\frac{9}{4}-\varepsilon)\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma)>2\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma),

where
\chi(\Gamma)
is the Euler characteristic of
\Gamma
;
H^{\partial N}
is the mean curvature of
\partial N
.
If
\Gamma
is non-orientable, then
\Gamma
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
and
\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma)<\pi
. If
\Gamma
is orientable and closed, then
\Gamma
is diffeomorphic to
S^{2}
and
\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma)<2\pi
. If
\Gamma
is orientable and has non-empty boundary, then
\Gamma
is a free boundary disc and
\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma)<\pi
.

It remains to prove the diameter upper bound.
By applying the same argument as in
[
11
]
*Proposition 2.2, we conclude that

•

if
\Gamma
is closed, then
\mathrm{diam}_{N}\Gamma\leq\pi
;

•

if
\Gamma
is a disc, then
\operatorname{dist}_{N}(x,\partial\Gamma)\leq\pi
for
x\in\Gamma
.

Since each connected component of
\partial N
is a stable minimal surface, then by Lemma
2.2
, we have that its diameter is bounded by
\frac{2}{3}\pi
. If
\Gamma
is a disc it then follows that
\mathrm{diam}_{N}\Gamma\leq\frac{2}{3}\pi+2\pi=\frac{8}{3}\pi
.

Hence, Lemma
2.5
is proved.
∎

2.3.
Geometrically prime regions

We will use the concept of
geometrically prime regions
, which was defined in
[
10
]
*Definition 2.5. Note that the region here may be non-compact.

Definition 2.6
.

A region
N
is
geometrically prime
if

(1)

N\setminus\partial N
does not contain any closed embedded minimal surfaces;

(2)

each connected component of
\partial N
is a compact minimal surface with index less than or equal to 1.

Remark 2.7
.

Let
N
be a geometrically prime region with
R\geq 6
. We conclude that each embedded closed two-sided surface
\Sigma\subset N\setminus\partial N
separates
N
. Suppose not, then there exists an area minimizer
\Sigma
in the homology class. Then
\Sigma
is compact by Lemma
2.3
. This gives a contradiction.

We use the dichotomy theorem about existence of a minimal surface or a mean convex foliation from
[
5
]
and
[
14
]
*Theorem 2.1 to prove the following result.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
N
be a (possibly non-compact) three-manifold with (possibly empty) boundary, bumpy metric and
R\geq 6
. Suppose that all boundary components of

N
are stable minimal surfaces and that
N
does not contain any closed one-sided embedded minimal surfaces with stable double covers or two-sided stable embedded minimal surfaces in its interior.
Then there are two possibilities:

(1)

N\setminus\partial N
contains a closed two-sided minimal surface of index one;

(2)

N\setminus\partial N
does not contain any closed embedded minimal surfaces. Let
\Omega\subset N
be a compact region with smooth boundary so that each connected component of
\partial N
is either contained in
\partial\Omega
or disjoint from it. In this case, there exists a connected, closed, two-sided, embedded
C^{1,1}
surface
\Sigma\subset N\setminus(\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega)
intersecting
\partial\Omega
and having mean curvature vector pointing towards
\Omega
.

Proof.

If
N
is compact, then we can apply min-max theory to obtain an index 1 two-sided embedded minimal surface in the interior of
N
(see
[
21
]
*Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.3
[
8
]
*Theorem 1.5). Now we assume that
N
is non-compact.

Suppose that
N\setminus\partial N
does not admit an index
1
two-sided embedded closed minimal surface in its interior.

Let
\Omega\subset N
be a compact region with smooth boundary so that each connected component of
\partial N
is either contained in
\partial\Omega
or disjoint from it. We will show that there exists a connected, closed, two-sided, embedded surface
\Sigma\subset N\setminus(\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega)
intersecting
\partial\Omega
and having mean curvature vector pointing towards
\Omega
.
We argue as in
[
14
]
*Theorem 2.1.
Let
\mathcal{K}\supset N_{3\pi}(\Omega)
be a set with smooth boundary; we can assume that each connected component of

\partial N
is either contained in
\partial\mathcal{K}
or disjoint from it. Define a new metric
\widetilde{g}
that coincides with
g
outside of
N^{g}_{\frac{\pi}{2}}(\partial\mathcal{K}\setminus\partial N)

and so that
\partial\mathcal{K}\setminus\partial N
is mean convex with respect to
\widetilde{g}
. By Theorem
2.1
, we may assume that metric
\widetilde{g}
is bumpy.
By the choice of
\widetilde{g}
and Lemma
2.3
, for each closed, connected two-sided minimal surface
\Gamma\subset(\mathcal{K},\widetilde{g})
of index less than or equal to one, it is either disjoint from
N_{r}(\Omega)
or disjoint from
N_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{g}(\partial\mathcal{K}\setminus\partial N)
.

Consider mean curvature flow
\{\partial\mathcal{K}_{t}\}
starting on
\mathcal{K}_{0}=\mathcal{K}
in
(\mathcal{K},\widetilde{g})
. By
[
18
]
*Theorem 11.1, there exist two possibilities:

(i)

there exists
t^{\prime}>0
, such that
\partial\mathcal{K}_{t^{\prime}}\cap\partial\Omega
is non-empty;

(ii)

there exists set
B
,
\Omega\subset B\subset\mathcal{K}
, with
\partial B
a smooth stable minimal surface in
(\mathcal{K},\widetilde{g})
.

Consider the first possibility. Let
\hat{t}<t^{\prime}
be the first time of the flow touching
\Omega
. Now consider the constrained minimizing problem
\mathcal{P}(\Omega,\mathcal{K};\widetilde{g})
(see
[
9
]
[
16
]
*Section 2.4 for the regularity): minimizing
\mathrm{Area}(\partial\Omega^{\prime};\widetilde{g})
among all
\Omega^{\prime}
so that
\Omega\subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{K}
and
\partial\Omega^{\prime}
is smooth. Denote by
\hat{\Omega}
a constrained minimizer of
\mathcal{P}(\Omega,\mathcal{K};\widetilde{g})
. Clearly,
\partial\hat{\Omega}\setminus\Omega
is a stable minimal surface w.r.t.
\widetilde{g}
. Since
\partial\mathcal{K}_{t}\setminus\partial N
is mean convex, then by Maximum Principle
[
19
]
*Theorem 1,
\partial\mathcal{K}_{t}\setminus\partial N
does not intersect
\partial\hat{\Omega}\setminus\Omega
for all
t\in[0,\hat{t})
. Moreover, we have that
\hat{\Omega}\subset\mathcal{K}_{\hat{t}}
. In the next claim, we will prove that
\partial\hat{\Omega}
intersects
\partial\Omega
. Denote by
\Sigma
the connected component of
\partial\hat{\Omega}
that intersect
\Omega
. Then this is the desired surface.

Claim 1
.

\partial\hat{\Omega}
intersects
\partial\Omega
.

Proof of Claim
1
.

Suppose not, then
\partial\hat{\Omega}
is a stable minimal surface w.r.t.
\widetilde{g}
. Since
(N\setminus\partial N,g)
has no two-sided stable minimal surface, then each connected component of
\partial\hat{\Omega}\setminus\partial N
intersects
N_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{g}(\partial\mathcal{K}\setminus\partial N)
. By Lemma
2.3
, for each
x\in\partial\hat{\Omega}
,

\operatorname{dist}_{N}(x,N_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{g}(\partial\mathcal{K}\setminus\partial N)\leq\frac{2\pi}{3}.

It follows that

\operatorname{dist}_{N}(\partial\hat{\Omega}\setminus\partial N,\Omega)\geq 3\pi-\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{2\pi}{3}>\pi,

which implies that
\hat{\Omega}\supset\{x\in N;\operatorname{dist}_{N}(x,\Omega)\leq\pi\}.
This contradicts the fact of
\hat{\Omega}\subset\mathcal{K}_{\hat{t}}
. Then Claim
1
is proved.
∎

Suppose we are in the second possibility. We can then apply the min-max construction to find a two-sided index
1
minimal surface
S
. By the diameter estimate in Lemma
2.2

and our assumption that there are no two-sided index
1
minimal surfaces in
(N,g)
, we have that
S
does not intersect
\Omega
. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{1}
denote a connected component of
\mathcal{K}\setminus S

that contains
\Omega
. We can perturb
S\subset\partial\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{1}
to the inside using the first eigenfunction of the stability operator to obtain
set
\mathcal{K}^{1}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{1}
with mean convex boundary. We can now apply mean curvature flow to
\mathcal{K}^{1}
and arrive at the same two possibilities
(i)
and
(ii)
as before. If we are in situation
(i)
,
we obtain mean convex surface
\Sigma
as above. If we are in situation
(ii)
, we apply the min-max construction again. Since the metric is bumpy, we can encounter possibility
(ii)
only finitely many times.

So far we know that mean convex surface
\Sigma
exists.
Suppose
\Omega
contains an embedded minimal surface
\Sigma^{\prime}
in its interior. By our assumption

\Sigma^{\prime}
is either two-sided and unstable or one-sided with unstable double cover. Consider
manifold with boundary
\Omega^{\prime\prime}
obtained as metric completion of
\Omega\setminus\Sigma^{\prime}
.
Then by Lemma
2.4
at least one connected component of
\Omega^{\prime\prime}
(and hence
\Omega
)
contains a stable two-sided minimal surface in its interior, contradicting the assumption of the theorem.
Since this is true for all compact domains
\Omega
, we conclude that possibility (
2
) of the theorem holds.
∎

The next theorem asserts that we can decompose
M
into geometrically prime regions.
We will say that a countable collection of smooth connected 3-manifolds
with boundary
\{U_{i}\}
is a
decomposition
of a 3-manifold
M
if there exists
a countable collection of disjoint embedded closed surfaces
\{\Sigma_{j}\}
in
M
,
such that there exists a Riemannian isometry from
M\setminus\bigcup_{j}\Sigma_{j}
to the
union of interior of
\{U_{i}\}
. (For example,
M=\mathbb{RP}^{3}
and
\{U\}
is a decomposition of
M
consisting of
one element
U
equal to the metric completion of an open ball obtained by removing a non-trivial embedded
\mathbb{RP}^{2}\subset M
.) By a slight abuse of notation we will identify the interiors of
U_{i}
and the corresponding
isometric subsets of
M
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
M
be a (possibly non-compact) manifold with (possibly empty) boundary, bumpy metric and
R\geq 6
.
There exists a decomposition
\{N_{i}\}
of
M
into countably many geometrically prime submanifolds
N_{i}
.

Proof.

Since the metric is bumpy there exists a countable maximal collection of disjoint minimal surfaces
\{\Sigma_{i}\}
, such that each
\Sigma_{i}
satisfies one of the following three possibilities:
\Sigma_{i}
two-sided and stable;
\Sigma_{i}
is two-sided and index
1
; or
\Sigma_{i}
is one-sided and has a stable double cover. We claim that
M\setminus\bigsqcup_{i}\Sigma_{i}
is the desired collection of geometrically prime submanifolds.

Let
N
be the metric completion of a connected component of
M\setminus\bigsqcup_{i}\Sigma_{i}
. Then we finish the proof by considering the following two possibilities.

•

If all boundary component of
\partial N
are stable, or if
M=N
(and the set
\{\Sigma_{i}\}
is empty),
then by Proposition
2.8
,
N\setminus\partial N
does not contain any closed minimal surface, and hence
N

is geometrically prime.

•

If
\partial N
has a boundary component that is a minimal surface of index
1
, we now prove the conclusion by contradiction. Suppose on the contrary that there exists a closed embedded minimal surface
\Gamma
in
N\setminus\partial N
. Then by assumption,
\Gamma
is either unstable or one-sided and has an unstable double cover. In either case, we cut

along

and let

be the metric completion. Then
\partial\widetilde{N}
contains two unstable minimal surfaces of
\widetilde{N}
. By Lemma
2.4
,
\widetilde{N}\setminus\partial\widetilde{N}
contains a two-sided stable minimal surface. This gives a contradiction.

∎

3.
Decomposition of geometrically prime regions

Let
M
be three dimensional Riemannian manifolds with
R\geq 6
. In this section,
N
is always a geometrically prime region in
M
.
We will divide such an
N
into compact regions with controlled diameter and boundary area. More precisely, we will divide

N
into subsets satisfying the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
N\subset M
be a geometrically prime region,
R\geq 6
. Then a compact region
\Omega\subset N
is
admissible
if

(1)

\partial\Omega
is closed;

(2)

each connected component
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\partial\Omega
satisfies

\mathrm{diam}_{N}\Gamma^{\prime}\leq 6\pi;

moreover,

\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq 4\pi\text{ if $\Gamma^{\prime}$ is mean concave};\quad\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma^{\prime})\leq\frac{16}{3}\pi\text{ if $\Gamma^{\prime}$ is mean convex};

(3)

one connected component
\Gamma
of
\partial\Omega
is weakly mean convex and the others are weakly mean concave; moreover,

\mathrm{Area}(\partial\Omega)\leq\frac{32}{3}\pi;

(4)

for each
x\in\Omega
the distance to the mean convex component
\Gamma
of
\partial\Omega
satisfies

\operatorname{dist}_{N}(x,\Gamma)<6\pi.

In particular, we have
\mathrm{diam}_{N}(\Omega)<18\pi
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
N
be a geometrically prime region. Then
N
can be decomposed into countably many admissible regions
\{\Omega_{j}\}
.

To prove Proposition
3.2
, we will need three lemmas about existence of separating surfaces of
controlled size and mean curvature.

3.1.
Decomposition of mean convex regions

The first lemma concerns existence of a mean convex surface of controlled size in a subset of
N
with mean convex boundary. It gives that for a subdomain of a geometrically prime region with a mean convex boundary component (which is the unique non-minimal boundary component) one can cut off an admissible region near the mean convex boundary.

Let
(N,g)
be a Riemannian manifold with smooth boundary. In this section, for any compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset N

N_{r}(\mathcal{K})=\{x\in N:\operatorname{dist}_{N}(x,\mathcal{K})<r\}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

be a geometrically prime region. Let
\Sigma
be a two-sided connected surface with non-vanishing mean curvature. Then
\Sigma
separates
N

\Omega

(1)

(2)

\operatorname{dist}_{N}(\partial\hat{\Omega}\setminus\partial\Omega,\Sigma)\geq\frac{\pi}{4}

(3)

(4)

\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma^{\prime})<\frac{7}{3}\pi

\mathrm{diam}_{N}\Gamma^{\prime}<6\pi
;

(5)

\mathrm{Area}(\partial\hat{\Omega}\setminus\Sigma)<\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma)
.

In particular,
\hat{\Omega}
is admissible provided that
\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma)\leq\frac{16}{3}\pi
and
\mathrm{diam}_{N}\Sigma\leq 6\pi
.

Since

(3.1)

\mathrm{diam}_{N}\Sigma_{1}<5\pi
and
\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma_{1})<\frac{8\pi}{3}
.

Then
\Sigma_{1}
separates
N
into two regions by Remark
2.7
; denote by
K_{0}
the one so that
\Sigma_{1}
is mean convex. Then we construct
\Sigma_{j}
inductively.

Suppose that we have
\Sigma_{j}
and
K_{j-1}
. Note that
\Sigma_{i}
is the common boundary of
K_{i-1}
and
K_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,j-1
. It follows that

\partial(N\setminus\cup_{i=0}^{j-1}K_{i})\setminus\partial N=\Sigma_{j}.

Then we apply Lemma
3.5
again for
\Omega=N\setminus\cup_{i=0}^{j-1}K_{i}
and
\Sigma=\Sigma_{j}
to get a two-sided embedded closed mean convex surface
\Sigma_{j+1}
so that

•

\Sigma_{j+1}
has mean curvature vector pointing towards
\Sigma_{j}
;

•

\mathrm{diam}_{N}\Sigma_{j+1}<5\pi
and
\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma_{j+1})<\frac{8\pi}{3}
;

•

\operatorname{dist}_{N}(\Sigma_{j+1},\Sigma_{j})\geq\frac{\pi}{3}
,
\sup\{\operatorname{dist}_{N}(x,\Sigma_{j});x\in\Sigma_{j+1}\}<3\pi
.

Hence Step I is finished by letting
K_{j}
be the region bounded by
\Sigma_{j}
and
\Sigma_{j+1}
.
∎

Then by Lemma
3.4
(with
d_{0}<3\pi
therein), there exists a compact region
\hat{K}_{j}\subset K_{j}
so that

•

\partial\hat{K}_{j}\setminus\partial N
is a closed embedded surface containing
\Sigma_{j}
and
\Sigma_{j+1}
;

•

\partial\hat{K}_{j}\setminus\partial K_{j}
is mean concave;

•

\sup\{\operatorname{dist}_{N}(x,\Sigma_{j+1});x\in\hat{K}_{j}\}<3\pi+3\pi
;

•

\mathrm{Area}(\partial\hat{K}_{j}\setminus(\Sigma_{j}\cup\Sigma_{j+1}))<\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma_{j+1})
.

Here the third item together with the diameter bound of
\Sigma_{j+1}
implies that
\mathrm{diam}_{N}(\hat{K}_{j})<17\pi
; the last item gives that

\mathrm{Area}(\partial\hat{K}_{j}\setminus\Sigma_{j+1})<\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma_{j+1})+\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma_{j})<\frac{16\pi}{3}.

Recall that by Remark
2.7
, each two-sided embedded surface separates
N
. Then
K_{j}\setminus\hat{K}_{j}
consists of finitely many connected components. Moreover, for each connected component
K^{\prime}
,
\partial K^{\prime}\setminus\partial N
is a connected mean convex surface
\Gamma^{\prime}
satisfying

\mathrm{diam}_{N}\Gamma^{\prime}<6\pi,\quad\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma^{\prime})<\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma_{j+1})\leq\frac{8\pi}{3}.

Let
\Omega
be one of the connected components of
K_{j}\setminus\hat{K}_{j}
or
K_{0}
. Denote by
B=\partial\Omega\setminus\partial N
. Then
B
is a connected mean convex surface with

\mathrm{diam}_{N}B<6\pi,\quad\mathrm{Area}(B)<\frac{8\pi}{3}.

It suffices to divide
\Omega
into compact regions as required.

Step II:

For each
\Omega
as above, it can be divided into countably many admissible compact regions
\{R_{i}\}
.

Proof of Step II.

We prove it by induction. By Lemma
3.3
, there exists a compact connected region
R_{1}
containing
B
so that

•

\partial R_{1}\setminus\partial\Omega
is a closed embedded surface in
N
and has mean curvature vector pointing away from
\Sigma
;

•

\operatorname{dist}_{N}(\partial R_{1}\setminus\partial\Omega,B)\geq\frac{\pi}{4}
, and
\sup\{\operatorname{dist}_{N}(x,B);x\in R_{1}\setminus\partial\Omega\}\leq 3\pi
;

•

each connected component of
\partial R_{1}\setminus\partial\Omega
has diameter less than
6\pi
;

•

\mathrm{Area}(\partial R_{1}\setminus B)<\mathrm{Area}(B)<\frac{8\pi}{3}
.

Observe that for each connected component
\Omega^{\prime}
of
\Omega\setminus R_{1}
,
B^{\prime}:=\partial\Omega^{\prime}\setminus\partial\Omega
is a connected mean convex surface and

\mathrm{diam}_{N}B^{\prime}<6\pi,\quad\mathrm{Area}(B^{\prime})<\frac{8\pi}{3}.

Thus we can repeat the above argument. This finishes Step II.
∎

We now consider the case when one connected component
\Sigma
of
\partial N
has index one.
Then by Lemma
2.2
,

\mathrm{diam}_{N}\Sigma\leq\frac{4}{3}\pi,\quad\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma)\leq\frac{16}{3}\pi.

Since
\Sigma
is unstable, then there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}
of
\Sigma
and a diffeomorphism
\phi:\Sigma\times[0,\delta)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{\delta}
so that
\phi(\Sigma\times\{t\})
has mean curvature vector pointing away from
\Sigma
.
It follows that

\mathrm{Area}(\phi(\Sigma\times\{\delta\}))<\frac{16}{3}\pi,\quad\mathrm{diam}_{N}<2\pi.

Thus
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}
is an admissible region. Let
K
be the metric completion of
N\setminus\mathcal{N}_{\delta}
. Then by Lemma
3.3
, there exists an admissible region
\hat{K}
containing
\phi(\Sigma\times\{\delta\})
so that the metric completion of
K\setminus\hat{K}
consists of finitely many regions
\{\hat{\Omega}_{j}\}
satisfying

•

\partial\hat{\Omega}_{j}\setminus\partial N
is a closed embedded surface having mean curvature vector pointing away from
\phi(\Sigma\times\{\delta\})
;

•

\mathrm{diam}_{N}(\partial\hat{\Omega}_{j}\setminus\partial N)<6\pi
and
\mathrm{Area}(\partial\hat{\Omega}_{j}\setminus\partial N)<\frac{7\pi}{3}<\frac{8\pi}{3}
.

Then applying the argument in Step II, each
\{\hat{\Omega}_{j}\}
can be divided into countably many admissible regions. Therefore, we conclude that
N
can be divided into admissible regions.
∎

4.
Foliation of admissible and prime regions and proof
of Theorem
1.1

By modifying the mean curvature flow with surgery it was proved in
[
10
]
that compact geometrically prime regions admit a singular foliation with bounded area. Similarly we will obtain foliations of admissible regions by singular surfaces with area bounds.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}
be a closed interval or a ray
[0,\infty)
.
Given a set
U\subset M
, we say that a family of closed surfaces
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t\in A}
is a Morse foliation of
U
if there exists a proper Morse function
f:U\rightarrow A
with
f^{-1}(t)=\Sigma_{t}
,
t\in A
.

Below we recall some basic results about Morse foliations of subsets of a Riemannian manifold.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
M^{3}
be a Riemannian manifold and
\Sigma\subset M

be a smooth closed surface in
M
.
For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta(\Sigma,\varepsilon)>0
,
such that
U=N_{\delta}(\Sigma)
admits a Morse foliation
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
with
\Sigma_{0}=\partial U
,

\Sigma_{1}=\{p\}
for a point
p
in the interior of
U
.
Moreover, we have
\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma_{t})\leq 2\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma)+\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Observe that for an arbitrarily small
r>0
we can choose

\delta>0
sufficiently small, so that
U
is an interval bundle over
\Sigma
,
\pi:U\rightarrow\Sigma
, and
for every
p\in\Sigma
we have that
\pi^{-1}(B_{r}(p))
is

(1+\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{1000})
-bilipschitz diffeomorphic
to
B_{r}^{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(0)\times[-\delta,\delta]
.
In the construction below we will use local coordinates
(x,s)
,
x\in\Sigma
,

t\in[-\delta,\delta]
, on
U
.

Fix a Morse function
g:\Sigma\rightarrow[0,1]

and consider a family of surfaces with boundary
\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}

defined by
\Sigma^{\prime}_{t}=(\pi\circ g)^{-1}(t)
.
Observe that for sufficiently small
\delta>0
we have
that
\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma^{\prime}_{t})<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
.
Also, we can slightly modify
the family near critical points of
g
to turn it into a family of level sets of a Morse
function on
U
.
Indeed, if
x
is a critical point of
g
of index
0
or
2
with critical value
g(x)=t^{\prime}
, then
in the neighbourhood of
\pi^{-1}(x)

surfaces
\Sigma^{\prime}_{t}
look like a family of cylinders
\{C_{t}\times[-\delta,\delta]\}
.
It is straightforward to modify this family to a family of concentric
spheres around point
(x,0)
as
t\rightarrow t^{\prime}
. Similarly, if
x
is a critical point of
g
of index
1
,
then surfaces
\Sigma^{\prime}_{t}
near
(x,0)
look like a family
\{H_{t}\times[-\delta,\delta]\}

where
H_{t}
’s are hyperbolas. Creating a small neck that connects two connected components of

H_{t}\times[-\delta,\delta]
and opening up the neck we modify this family of surfaces in the
neighbourhood of
\pi^{-1}(x)
to a family of level sets of Morse function with a cricial point of Morse index
1
.

After these modifications we obtain a Morse function
f:U\rightarrow[0,1]

with critical points in the interior of
U
and the area of fibers bounded by

\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. We can now apply
[
1
]
*Lemma 4.1
to obtain the desired Morse foliation of
U
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega
be an admissible region.
Suppose
S_{0}
is a closed (possibly empty) subset of

\partial\Omega
and
S_{1}=\partial\Omega\setminus S_{0}
.
Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a Morse foliation
\{\Sigma_{t}\}
of
\Omega
, such that

•

if
S_{0}
is empty, then
\Sigma_{0}=\{p\}
is a point in the
interior of
\Omega
and
\Sigma_{1}=S_{1}
;

•

if
S_{0}
is non-empty, then
\Sigma_{0}=S_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1}=S_{1}
;

and
\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma_{t})\leq 3\mathrm{Area}(\partial\Omega)+\varepsilon<33\pi
.

Proof.

Let
U
denote a small tubular neighbourhood of

\partial\Omega
in
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}=\Omega\setminus U
.
Note that each connected component
U_{i}
of
U
can be foliated by
a family of surfaces
\{\Gamma^{i}_{t}\}
, where
\Gamma^{i}_{0}=\Gamma^{i}
is a connected component of

\partial\Omega
and
\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma^{i}_{t})\leq\mathrm{Area}(\Gamma^{i})+\delta

for
\delta>0
that can be chosen arbitrarily small by making neighbourhood
U

small enough.

Consider
\Omega
as a subset of
M
and define Mean Curvatue Flow with surgery starting on the mean convex
boundary component
S
of
\partial\Omega
. As in
[
10
]
we can
modify this family of surfaces to obtain a Morse function

f^{\prime}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with the area of fibers at most

\mathrm{Area}(S)+\delta
. (Note that some of the fibers
may not be closed surfaces and may have boundary in
\partial\Omega\setminus S
.)
We can also assume that
f^{\prime}
is a Morse function
when restricted to
\Omega^{\prime}
and
\partial\Omega^{\prime}
.
By
[
1
]
*Lemma 4.1 there exists a Morse foliation
\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{t}\}
of
\Omega^{\prime}

starting at a point
\Sigma^{\prime}_{0}=\{p\}
in the interior of
\Omega^{\prime}
, ending on
\Sigma^{\prime}_{1}=\partial\Omega^{\prime}

and satisfying
\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma^{\prime}_{t})\leq\mathrm{Area}(S)+\mathrm{Area}(\partial\Omega^{\prime})+2\delta
.

If
S_{0}
is empty then we can extend
\{\Sigma^{\prime}_{t}\}
to
U
to obtain the desired Morse foliation of
\Omega
.

Suppose
S_{0}
is not empty. Let
\tilde{U}
denote the union of connected components
of
U
that intersect
S_{0}
and
\{\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{0}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
denote the foliation of

\tilde{U}
by surfaces isotopic to
S_{0}
, as defined above.
Let
\{\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{1}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
denote the corresponding foliation
of
U\setminus\tilde{U}
.
Let
V
denote a subset of
\tilde{U}
bounded by
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{0}

and
\tilde{\Gamma}_{1}^{0}
.
By Lemma
4.2
we can define
a Morse foliation
\{\tilde{\Sigma}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
of

V
that starts on
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{0}\cup\tilde{\Gamma}_{1}^{0}
and terminates
at a point in the interior of
V
.

We can now define the desired Morse foliation of
\Omega
by setting

\Sigma_{t}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{0}&\text{ for $t\in[0,\frac{1}{4})$},\\
&\Sigma^{\prime}_{4t-1}\cup\tilde{\Gamma}_{t}^{0}&\text{ for $t\in[\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})$},\\
&\tilde{\Sigma}_{4t-2}\cup\tilde{\Gamma}_{2t-1}^{1}&\text{ for $t\in[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4})$},\\
&\tilde{\Gamma}_{2t-1}^{1}&\text{ for $t\in[\frac{3}{4},1]$}.\end{aligned}\right.

It is straightforward to check that with the definition above we have
\Sigma_{0}=S_{0}
,
\Sigma_{1}=S_{1}

and the area bound is satisfied.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
M
be a complete
3
-manifold with scalar curvature
R\geq 6
.
We perturb the metric on
M
to a bumpy metric. We will construct the
desired Morse foliation for the perturbed metric and note that the diameter
and area bounds for surfaces in the folation will be worse by at most
\varepsilon

for the original metric, where
\varepsilon>0
depends on the perturbation and
can be chosen arbitrarily small.

By Theorem
2.9
and Proposition
3.2

there exists a decomposition of
M
into countably many admissible regions
\mathcal{U}=\{\Omega_{i}\}
.
Define graph
G
, where each vertex
V_{\Omega_{i}}
corresponds to an admissible region

\Omega_{i}
and two vertices
V_{\Omega_{i}}
and
V_{\Omega_{j}}
,
i\neq j
, are connected by an edge if
and only if the inclusions of
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{j}
share a common boundary component in
M
.

Choose an admissible region
\Omega_{0}\in\mathcal{U}
.
Let
i_{\Omega_{0}}:\Omega_{0}\rightarrow M
denote the inclusion of
\Omega_{0}

into
M
that is a Riemannian isometry in the interior of
\Omega_{0}
.
Let
S_{1}(\Omega_{0})
denote the image of the boundary components of
\Omega_{0}

on which
i_{\Omega_{0}}
is a 2-to-1 map (for example, if

i_{\Omega_{0}}
sends the interior of

\Omega_{0}
onto the interior of
\mathbb{RP}^{3}
with some balls and nontrivial
\mathbb{RP}^{2}

removed).
By Lemma
4.3
, for a sufficiently small
\delta>0
,
there exists a Morse function
f:\Omega_{0}\setminus N_{\delta}(\partial\Omega_{0})\rightarrow[0,1-\delta]
, such that
f^{-1}(0)
is a point in the interior of
\Omega_{0}
and
f^{-1}(1)=\partial(\Omega_{0}\setminus N_{\delta}(\partial\Omega_{0}))
and the fibers of
f

satisfy the desired area and diameter bounds. We can extend
f
to a Morse foliation of

i_{\Omega_{0}}(\Omega_{0})
,
f:i_{\Omega_{0}}(\Omega_{0})\rightarrow[0,1]
, by applying Lemma
4.2
in the
\delta
-neighbourhood
of
S_{1}(\Omega_{0})
and foliating the rest of
i_{\Omega_{0}}(N_{\delta}(\partial\Omega_{0}))
by level sets of the distance function.

Let
V_{n}
denote the collection of all vertices in graph
G
that are
at a graph distance
n
from vertex
V_{\Omega_{0}}
and let
U_{n}
denote closure of the
union of the corresponding regions in
M
. Suppose, by induction,
that we have defined a Morse function
f:\bigcup_{k=0}^{n-1}U_{k}\rightarrow[0,n]

with fibers of the function satisfying the desired area and diameter bounds and, moreover,
we have that
f(x)=n
for
x\in\partial\bigcup_{k=0}^{n-1}U_{k}
.

For each admissible region
\Omega^{\prime}
corresponding to a vertex in
V_{n}
we decompose
\partial\Omega^{\prime}

into four closed subsets.
Let
S_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})=\partial i_{\Omega^{\prime}}({\Omega^{\prime}})\cap\partial U_{n-1}
,

S_{1}(\Omega^{\prime})
denote the connected components of
i_{\Omega^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega^{\prime})

on which
i_{\Omega^{\prime}}
is a double covering,

S_{2}(\Omega^{\prime})=\partial i_{\Omega^{\prime}}({\Omega^{\prime}})\cap\partial(U_{n}\setminus i_{\Omega^{\prime}}({\Omega^{\prime}}))
,
and

S_{3}(\Omega^{\prime})=\partial i_{\Omega^{\prime}}({\Omega^{\prime}})\setminus(S_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})\cup S_{1}(\Omega^{\prime})\cup S_{2}(\Omega^{\prime}))
.
Note that
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
and
S_{3}
may be empty.

For a sufficiently small
\delta>0
we can apply Lemma
4.3

to define
f
on
\bigcup i_{\Omega}(\Omega)\setminus N_{\delta}(S_{1}(\Omega)\cup S_{2}(\Omega)\cup S_{3}(\Omega))
, where the union is over all sets
\Omega\in\mathcal{U}
correspond to
vertices in
V_{n}
, mapping it onto

[n,n+1-\delta]
, so that it agrees with the inductive definition of
f
on
f^{-1}(n)
and

f^{-1}(n+1-\delta)=\partial\bigcup N_{\delta}(S_{1}(\Omega)\cup S_{2}(\Omega)\cup S_{3}(\Omega))
.
We can then apply Lemma
4.2
to extend
f
on

\bigcup N_{\delta}(S_{1}(\Omega)\cup S_{2}(\Omega))
, mapping it to
[n+1-\delta,n+1]
.
Finally, the set
\bigcup N_{\delta}(S_{3}(\Omega))\cap\Omega
can be foliated by surfaces
isotopic to
\bigcup S_{3}(\Omega)
, giving us a Morse foliation with
f=n+1
on
\partial\bigcup_{k=0}^{n}U_{k}
. This finishes the inductive construction of
f
.

It follows from the definition of admissible regions
and the area and diameter estimates above
that for each connected component
\Sigma
of
f^{-1}(x)
we have
\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma)<33\pi
.
Since each surface is contained inside an admissible region we have that
the extrinsic diameter satisfies
\mathrm{diam}_{M}\Sigma<18\pi
.
∎

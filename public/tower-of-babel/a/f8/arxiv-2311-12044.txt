On Modular Approach to Diophantine Equation x 4 − y 4 = n ​ z p x^{4}-y^{4}=nz^{p} over Number Fields
Erman IŞIK

Abstract
. Recent results of Freitas, Kraus, Şengün and Siksek give sufficient criteria for the asymptotic Fermat’s Last Theorem to hold over various number fields. In this paper, we prove asymptotic results about the solutions of the Diophantine equation x 4 − y 4 = n ​ z p x^{4}-y^{4}=nz^{p} over various number fields using the modular method. For instance, we prove that the asymptotic generalised Fermat Theorem for the equation x 4 − y 4 = 2 α ​ z p x^{4}-y^{4}=2^{\alpha}z^{p} holds for infinitely many quadratic number fields.



1.
Introduction

The study of
generalized Fermat-type equations
, namely those of the form

Ax^{p}+By^{q}=Cz^{r}

for fixed non-zero coprime integers
A,B,C
, is one of the oldest and most widely studied topics in mathematics. In general, finding all solutions of such an equation is a difficult task, as there is no known general method that would allow us to produce solutions of a given Diophantine equation.

In
[
Wil95
]
, Andrew Wiles proved Fermat’s Last Theorem (FLT) using his celebrated modularity theorem for elliptic curves together with Ribet’s level lowering theorem
[
Rib90
]
. Since then, several mathematicians have attempted to extend this approach to various Diophantine equations and number fields. The results known until
2020
are summarised in
[
IKÖ20
, Tables 1 and 2]
.

The strategy to tackle Diophantine equations via Wiles’ approach, which is known as the “modular method”, starts with an elliptic curve attached to a putative solution of the given equation. Then, using many celebrated results of the area, the problem can be reduced to one of the following: either computing newforms of a certain level, or computing all elliptic curves of a given conductor with particular information about the torsion subgroup and rational isogeny, or computing all solutions to an
S
-unit equation. It is important to note that none of these computations is straightforward in general. For instance, some fundamental theorems, such as the modularity of elliptic curves, that go into the proof are for the time being conjecture only.

Recently, there has been important progress towards generalisation of the modular method over a larger class of number fields. In
[
FS15a
]
, using the modularity of elliptic curves over real quadratic fields, Freitas and Siksek proved the asymptotic FLT for infinitely many real quadratic fields. Namely, they showed that there is a constant
B_{K}
depending only on the real quadratic field
K
, such that the only solutions to the equation
x^{p}+y^{p}=z^{p}
with the exponent
p>B_{K}
are the trivial ones. In
[
ŞS18
]
, Şengün and Siksek proved the asymptotic FLT for any number field
K
by assuming two deep but standard conjectures from the Langlands program such as Serre’s modularity conjecture. In
[
Dec16
,
KO20
]
, Deconinck and Kara-Özman extented these results to the generalized Fermat-equation
Ax^{p}+By^{p}=Cz^{p}
(see also
[
OS22
]
). In
[
IKÖ20
,
IKÖ22
]
, Işık, Kara and Özman studied the solutions of the Fermat-type equations
x^{p}+y^{p}=z^{2}
and
x^{p}+y^{p}=z^{3}
over various number fields utilising a similar approach carried out in
[
FS15a
,
ŞS18
]
. In
[
Moc22
]
, Mocanu improved the results in
[
IKÖ20
,
IKÖ22
]
and proved similar results over totally real number fields. Following the ideas in
[
Fre15
]
to construct the relevant Frey curve, Mocanu proved asymptotic results for the Fermat-equation
x^{p}+y^{p}=z^{r}
over totally real number fields in
[
Moc23
]
.

Let
K
be a number field and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be its ring of integers. For a prime number
p
and a positive integer
n
such that
{\rm ord}_{\mathfrak{q}}(n)<p
for all primes
{\mathfrak{q}}
of
K
dividing
n
, consider the equation

x^{4}-y^{4}=nz^{p},\quad x,y,z\in\mathcal{O}_{K}.

When
K={\mathbb{Q}}
, in
[
Pow83
]
, Powell proved that the equation
x^{4}-y^{4}=nz^{p}
has no integer solutions with
p\nmid xyz
. Terai-Osada
[
TO92
]
and Cao
[
Cao99
]
have extended this analysis to the equations
x^{4}+dy^{4}=z^{p}
and
cx^{4}+dy^{4}=z^{p}
respectively, proving that there are no certain types of solutions under certain assumptions. The methods used by these authors involve factoring the left-hand side of the equations over the appropriate quadratic field and applying a strategy analogous to Kummer’s work on Fermat’s Last Theorem.

The equation
x^{4}-y^{4}=nz^{p}
has been studied in
[
Dar93
,
D0̧7
,
Ben21
]
using the modular method, again, over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. For instance, in
[
Dar93
]
, Darmon proved that the equation
x^{4}-y^{4}=z^{p}
has no non-trivial solution if
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, or if
2\mid z
. Note that the results in
[
Tor22
]
can be generalized to this equation over number fields other than
{\mathbb{Q}}
. In
[
D0̧7
]
, Dabrowski proved that the equation

x^{4}-y^{4}=2^{\alpha}\ell^{\beta}z^{p},\text{ for a fixed prime }\ell\text{ with }\ell\neq 2^{k}\pm 1\text{ with an integer }k,

has no solutions in coprime positive integers
x,y
and suitably large prime exponent
p
. Some stronger results have been obtained in a number of papers, under the additional assumption that
y
is prime; see, for example,
[
LT10
,
Sav09
,
Yan13
,
BDLT11
]
.

The main objective of the present article is to give asymptotic results for the equation
x^{4}-y^{4}=nz^{p}
over various number fields.

1.2.
A brief overview of the strategy

Let
K
be a number field and let
G_{K}:={\rm Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
denote its absolute Galois group. Let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}
be a fixed prime of
K
lying above
2
. We summarise the modular method to tackle the Diophantine equation
x^{4}-y^{4}=nz^{p}
over
K
in the following steps:

Frey curve:

To a putative solution
(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{3}
to the equation (
2.1
) with
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}\mid c
, we attach a Frey elliptic curve
E/K
with full
2
-torsion over
K
, having semistable reduction outside the primes dividing
2n
, and potentially multiplicative reduction at
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}
.

Modularity and Level Lowering:

For totally real number fields, we show that the Frey curve
E
is modular. In general, we assume some hypothesis to get an automorphic form
{\mathfrak{f}}
over
K
, which lies in a space that does not depend on the solution
(a,b,c)
and the exponent
p
, such that

\overline{\rho}_{E,p}\sim\overline{\rho}_{{\mathfrak{f}},\varpi},

where
\varpi
is some prime
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathfrak{f}}
above
p
.

Eichler-Shimura:

In this step, we obtain (assuming
p
large enough) an elliptic curve
E_{\mathfrak{f}}/K
with full
2
-torsion over
K
, having potentially good reduction outside the primes of
K
dividing
2n
and potentially multiplicative reduction at
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}
.

Contradiction:

We first establish the relation between elliptic curves with full
2
-torsion over
K
, having potentially good reduction outside
S
, and solutions of the
S
-unit equation (
1.2
), and then obtain a contradiction using the assumptions. For Theorem
1.11
, we show that the elliptic curve we obtained in the previous step cannot exist.

3.
Modularity of the Frey Curve
E

If
E
is an elliptic curve over a number field

, we say that

is modular if there is an isomorphism of a compatible system of Galois representations

\rho_{E,p}\sim\rho_{{\mathfrak{f}},\varpi},

where
{\mathfrak{f}}
is an automorphic form over

of weight
2
with coefficient field
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathfrak{f}}
and

is a prime in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathfrak{f}}
lying above
p

3.1.
Totally real case

In the totally real case,
{\mathfrak{f}}
comes from a Hilbert eigenform of parallel weight
2
over
K
. The following result is due to Freitas, Le Hung and Siksek
[
FLHS15
, Thereoms 1 and 5]
:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
K
be a totally real number field. There are at most finitely many
\overline{K}
-isomorphism classes of non-modular elliptic curves
E
over
K
. Moreover, if

is real quadratic, then all elliptic curves over

are modular.

Moreover, Derickx, Najman and Siksek proved the following result in
[
DNS20
]
:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
K
be a totally real cubic number field and
E
be an elliptic curve over
K
. Then
E
is modular.

Further results have been obtained for quartic totally real fields by Josha Box
[
Box22
]
.

Theorem 3.3

(Theorem 1.1,
[
Box22
]
)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a totally real quartic number field not containing a square root of
5
. Then
E
is modular.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
K
be a totally real number field, and fix
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}\in S_{K}
. There is some constant
A_{K}
, depending only on
K
, such that for any non-trivial primitive solution
(a,b,c)
to the equation (
2.1
) with and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}\mid c
and the exponent
p>A_{K}
, the Frey curve
E
is modular.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
3.1
that there are at most finitely many possible
\overline{K}
-isomorphism classes of elliptic curves over
K
which are non modular. Let
j_{1},\dots,j_{n}\in K
denote the
j
-invariants of these classes. Define
\lambda:=2^{2}\frac{a^{2}-b^{2}}{a^{2}+b^{2}}
so that the
j
-invariant of
E
is

j(\lambda)=2^{2}(64-\lambda^{2})^{3}\lambda^{-4}.

We can assume that
\lambda\not\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, since these
\lambda
would lead to
j(\lambda)\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, and it is known that all rational elliptic curves are modular. Each equation
j(\lambda)=j_{i}
has at most six solutions in
K
. Thus there are values
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}\in K
(where
m\leq 6n
) such that if
\lambda\neq\lambda_{k}
for all
k
, then the elliptic curve
E
with the
j
-invariant

is modular. If
\lambda=\lambda_{k}
for some
k
, then, using
{\rm ord}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(a^{2}+b^{2})={\rm ord}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(2)
and
{\rm ord}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(a^{2}-b^{2})=p{\rm ord}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(c)+{\rm ord}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(n)-{\rm ord}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(2)
(see Remark
2.2
), we deduce that

{\rm ord}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(\lambda_{k})=p{\rm ord}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(c)+{\rm ord}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(n)\geq p.

As
\lambda_{k}
is fixed, it gives an upper bound on

for each

∎

Remark 3.5

The finiteness part of Theorem

]
. The bound in Falting’s Theorem is ineffective, and so is the bound

.

Let

7.
Proof of Main Theorems

In this section, we will handle the last step of the modular method and establish the proofs of the statements given in Section
1.1
.

7.1.
S
-unit equations and elliptic curves

Let
K
be a number field,
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be its ring of integers, and
S
be a finite set of prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Definition 7.1
.

An
S
-unit
is an element
\alpha\in K
such that the principal fractional ideal generated by
\alpha
can be written as a product of the prime ideals in
S
. Namely, the set of
S
-units
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}
can be defined as:

\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}=\{\alpha\in K^{\times}\;:\;{\rm ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)=0\;\text{for all}\;{\mathfrak{p}}\not\in S\}.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
K
be a number field and
S
a finite set of prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. The
S
-unit equation
is the equation

(7.1)

\lambda+\mu=1,\;\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}.

If
S=\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}=\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
, so this equation is called the
unit equation
.

Let
\Lambda_{S}=\{(\lambda,\mu):\lambda+\mu=1,\;\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}\}
. Observe that the
S
-unit equation (
7.1
) has trivial set of solution
\{(-1,2),(2,-1),(1/2,1/2)\}
. Following
[
FS15a
]
these solutions will be called
irrelevant
solutions, while other solutions will be called
relevant
solutions.

Theorem 7.3

(Siegel 1921, Parry 1950)
.

Let
K
be a number field and
S
a finite set of prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. The
S
-unit equation

\lambda+\mu=1,\;\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}.

has only finitely many solutions.

Note that the original proofs due to Siegel and Parry are non-effective. Baker’s theory of linear forms in logarithms gave effective bounds although they are very large for the solutions. Various algorithms and bounds can be found in the literature, see for example
[
dW89
,
Sma99
,
AKM+21
,
vKM23
]
.

Before going into the details of the proofs, we will state the relationship between solutions to
S
-unit equations and certain families of elliptic curves. Consider an elliptic curve
E^{\prime}
with full
2
-torsion over
K
having potentially good reduction outside
S
. It then follows that it has the form

Y^{2}=(X-e_{1})(X-e_{2})(X-e_{3}),

where
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in K
are distinct. Let
\lambda:=\frac{e_{3}-e_{1}}{e_{2}-e_{1}}
be the
\lambda
-invariant of the elliptic curve (see
[
Sil09
, III.1]
). Write the
Legendre form
of
E^{\prime}

E_{\lambda}:Y^{2}=X(X-1)(X-\lambda).

It then follows from
[
Sil09
, III.1]
that the
j
-invariant of
E_{\lambda}
is

(7.2)

j(\lambda)=2^{8}\frac{(\lambda^{2}-\lambda+1)^{3}}{\lambda^{2}(1-\lambda)^{2}}.

Note that
\lambda\in K
as
E^{\prime}
has full
2
-torsion over
K
. Let
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
denote the symmetric group on three letters
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
. The action of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
on the set
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
can be extended to an action on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(K)-\{0,1,\infty\}
via the ratio
\lambda=\frac{e_{3}-e_{1}}{e_{2}-e_{1}}
. Under the action of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
, the orbit of
\lambda
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(K)-\{0,1,\infty\}
is the set:

(7.3)

M_{\lambda}:=\left\{\lambda,\frac{1}{\lambda},1-\lambda,\frac{1}{1-\lambda},\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1},\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\right\}.

Moreover, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the
\overline{K}
-isomorphism classes of the elliptic curves with full
2
-torsion over
K
and the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
-orbits of elements of
\lambda\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(K)-\{0,1,\infty\}
(see
[
OS22
, Lemma 5.2]
).

As
E^{\prime}
has potentially good reduction away from
S
, the
j
-invariant
j_{E^{\prime}}
belongs to
\mathcal{O}_{S}
. From the equation (
7.2
), we can deduce that
\lambda\in K
satisfies a degree six monic polynomial with coefficients in
\mathcal{O}_{S}
implying
\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{S}
. On the other hand, notice that
1/\lambda
and
1/\mu
(with
\mu:=1-\lambda
) are solutions of (
7.2
) and hence elements of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
. Therefore, we conclude that
(\lambda,\mu)
is a solution of the
S
-unit equation (
7.1
).

Let

Suppose now, as in the statement of Theorem

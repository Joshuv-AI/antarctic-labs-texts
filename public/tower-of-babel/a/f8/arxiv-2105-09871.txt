Abstract
Tuza famously conjectured in 1981 that in a graph without k + 1 k+1 edge-disjoint triangles, it suffices to delete at most 2 ​ k 2k edges to obtain a triangle-free graph. The conjecture holds for graphs with small treewidth or small maximum average degree, including planar graphs. However, for dense graphs that are neither cliques nor 4-colourable, only asymptotic results are known. Here, we confirm the conjecture for threshold graphs, i.e. graphs that are both split graphs and cographs, and for co-chain graphs with both sides of the same size divisible by 4 4 .

Introduction

If we can “pack” at most
k
disjoint objects of some type in a given graph, how many elements do we need to “cover” all appearances of such an object in the graph? Erdős and Pósa famously proved that if a graph contains at most
k
pairwise vertex-disjoint cycles, then there is a set of at most
f(k)
vertices that intersects every cycle
[
8
]
.
While the exact best value of function
f
is yet unknown, the asymptotic behaviour was recently determined to be
f(k)=\Theta(k\log k)

[
5
]
.

In this paper, we focus on edge-disjoint triangles; we refer the interested reader to
[
16
]
for a dynamic survey on other objects.
For a graph
G
, we call every family of pairwise edge-disjoint triangles a
triangle packing
, and every subset of edges intersecting all triangles in
G
a
triangle hitting
. We denote by
\mu(G)
the maximum size of a triangle packing in
G
, and by
\tau(G)
the minimum size of a triangle hitting in
G
.
Trivially, there is a set of at most
3\mu(G)
edges that intersect every triangle. We are concerned with improving that bound, following Tuza’s conjecture from 1981.

Conjecture 1

(Tuza
[
17
]
)
.

For any graph
G
it holds
\tau(G)\leq 2\mu(G)
.

1
, if true, is tight for
K_{4}
and
K_{5}
.
Gluing together copies of
K_{4}
and
K_{5}
along vertices, it is easy to build an infinite family of connected graphs for which
1
is tight.
However, for larger cliques, it is known that the ratio
\tau(K_{p})/\mu(K_{p})
tends to
3/2
as
p
increases
[
9
]
.
In addition, Haxell and Rödl
[
11
]
proved that
\tau(G)\leq 2\mu(G)+o(|V(G)|^{2})
for any graph
G
, meaning
1
is asymptotically true when
\tau(G)
is quadratic with respect to
|V(G)|
.
Those seem to indicate that
1
should be easier for dense graphs than for sparse graphs.
Conversely, it is asymptotically tight in some classes of dense graphs
[
2
]
.
If we focus on
hereditary graph
classes (i.e. classes that contain every induced subgraph of a graph in the class), the conjecture has only been confirmed for a few graph classes. Those classes include most notably graphs of treewidth at most
6

[
4
]
, 4-colourable graphs
[
1
]
, and graphs with maximum average degree less than
7

[
15
]
.

A good candidate for an interesting dense hereditary graph class is the class of
split graphs
, i.e. graphs whose vertex set can be partitioned into two sets: one that induces a clique, the other inducing an independent set.
However,
1
remains a real challenge even when restricted to split graphs.
Another good candidate for an interesting dense hereditary graph class is the class of
cographs
, i.e. graphs with no induced path on four vertices. As an initial step, we focus on graphs that are both split graphs and cographs, i.e.
threshold
graphs.
While this may seem like a small step, it is arguably the first dense hereditary superclass of cliques where the conjecture is confirmed.

Theorem 1
.

If
G
is a threshold graph, then
\tau(G)\leq 2\mu(G)
.

In the latter part of the paper, we show that similar tools with more involved analysis can be used to verify
1
also for specific co-chain graphs.
A graph
G
is a
co-chain graph
(or sometimes alternatively called
co-difference graph
) if its vertex set can be partitioned into two sets
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
such that
G[K_{1}]
and
G[K_{2}]
are cliques and there is an ordering
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
on the vertices of
K_{1}
and an ordering
d_{1},\dots,d_{m}
on the vertices of
K_{2}
with
N[c_{i+1}]\subseteq N[c_{i}]
for all
1\leq i<n
and
N[d_{i}]\subseteq N[d_{i+1}]
for all
1\leq i<m
.
We call
\left(K_{1},K_{2}\right)
a
co-chain representation
of
G
.
We say that
G
is an
even balanced
co-chain graph if additionally
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are of the same size that is divisible by four.

Theorem 2
.

If
G
is an even balanced co-chain graph, then
\tau(G)\leq 2\mu(G)
.

Theorem

2
can be seen as a very first step towards attacking
1
on (mixed) unit interval graphs as those graphs can be modelled as a
concatenation
of co-chain graphs.
That is, vertices of graph
G
are partitioned into
r
cliques
C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}
where each
\left(C_{i},C_{i+1}\right)
induce a co-chain graph and
G
contains no other edges; see
[
12
,
13
]
for more details.
The simplest object for further study might be a
k
-path, which can be viewed as a concatenation of well-structured same-sized co-chain graphs.

Finally, it is worth mentioning that
1
is known to hold as soon as we consider
multi
-packing
[
6
]
, and in particular it holds in its fractional relaxation
[
14
]
. Another angle of attack consists of lowering the bound of
3
step by step for all graphs.
The best, and in fact only, such bound is slightly under
2.87

[
10
]
.

The following lemma describes how to pack triangles in complete split graphs.

Lemma 4

(

)

Let

\left|K\right|=\left|S\right|=k

Then we can find an (optimal) triangle packing
\mathsf{TP}

such that:

1.

It uses all edges from

\mathsf{TP}
contains exactly one edge from

2.

If

Consider a graph
G

\left|K^{\prime}\right|=k

\left|S^{\prime}\right|=k-1

By

\nicefrac{{k}}{{2}}

Each such matching fully joined to a different vertex in

The collection of all

Removing any vertex

yields a balanced graph with both sides of odd size, in which edges not packed into triangles (participating in triangles whose vertex

.
On the other hand, by adding a single vertex

∎

Corollary 5
.

Let

(a)

If

If

We say that we

Threshold graphs

A graph
G=(V,E)
is a

K=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}\}

S=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{s}\}

We identify

(K,S)

The threshold representation of a threshold graph may not be unique.
We prove that it can be chosen such that the clique contains a vertex which is not adjacent to any vertex of the independent set.

Lemma 7

For every threshold graph
G=(V,E)
there exists a threshold representation
(K,S)
such that there is a vertex

Proof.

We fix a threshold representation

of
G

Suppose for all

Then, since

We obtain a new threshold representation

K^{\prime}\coloneqq K\cup\{w\}
and
S^{\prime}\coloneqq S\setminus\{w\}
.
Since
S
is an independent set,

has no neighbours in
S^{\prime}
.
∎

Therefore,

We assume without loss of generality throughout the entire proof that

We split the analysis into two main cases.

3.1
The case

In this case

Suppose there is an edge

, which yields a contradiction.
Similarly, there are no edges between
K^{\operatorname{bot}}_{1}{}
and

.
In particular, there are no edges between
K^{\operatorname{top}}_{2}{}
and
K^{\operatorname{bot}}_{1}{}
.

We choose a triangle hitting
\mathsf{TH}
obtained by taking all edges within
K^{\operatorname{top}}_{1}{}
,

,

, vertices in

only have neighbours in the independent set
K^{\operatorname{bot}}_{1}{}\cup K^{\operatorname{bot}}_{2}{}\setminus X_{2}
, while vertices in
K^{\operatorname{bot}}_{1}{}
only have neighbours in the independent set
K^{\operatorname{top}}_{1}{}\cup X_{2}
. Therefore the set
\mathsf{TH}
is indeed a triangle hitting of
G
.

\tau(G)\leq|\mathsf{TH}|=4\binom{\ell}{2}+\ell x_{1}+\ell x_{2}-x_{1}x_{2}=4\binom{\ell}{2}+\ell x_{1}+(\ell-x_{1})x_{2}.

Indeed, we note that we counted edges between
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
once in term
\ell x_{1}
and once in term
\ell x_{2}
which we compensate by subtracting the last term

.

Let us now create a sufficiently large triangle packing.
First, we pack all edges of

with vertices in

and also all edges of

with vertices in

; we denote the set of these triangles by

By

,

contains

Observe that
2|A|-|\mathsf{TH}|=-\ell x_{1}-(\ell-x_{1})x_{2}
.
First, we sort out the single case where
x_{1}=\ell
, and, in consequence,
x_{2}=\ell
by definition of
X_{1}
and

together with the assumption that
x_{2}\leq\ell
.

3.1.1
The subcase
x_{1}=x_{2}=\ell

In this case,
|\mathsf{TH}|=4\binom{\ell}{2}+\ell^{2}
.
As

is a clique, by

6
we can pack at least

Together with

, we obtain a triangle packing
\mathsf{TP}
.
If
\ell\geq 5
, then
2\mathsf{TP}-\mathsf{TH}\geq\frac{2}{3}\left(\binom{2\ell}{2}-\ell-1\right)-\ell^{2}=\frac{1}{3}\left(\ell^{2}-4\ell-2\right)\geq 0
.
If
\ell=4
,
lemma

6
gives us a stronger bound without the term
-2
, leading to
2\mathsf{TP}-\mathsf{TH}\geq\frac{1}{3}\left(\ell^{2}-4\ell\right)=0
.
Both cases imply
2\mu(G)\geq\tau(G)
.

3.1.2
The subcase
x_{1},x_{2}<\ell

Now, we consider the remaining case where
x_{1}<\ell
, and, in consequence,
x_{2}<\ell
.

We choose a triangle packing
\mathsf{TP}
as follows (see
fig.

9
).
We take the set
A
of triangles as defined before.
Recall that
2|A|-|\mathsf{TH}|=-\ell x_{1}-(\ell-x_{1})x_{2}
.
We create a set
B
of triangles by packing edges of
K^{\operatorname{bot}}_{2}{}
with vertices in
X_{1}
.
By
corollary

5
(
a
) and as
x_{1}<\ell
,
B
is of size
\ell/2\cdot x_{1}
.
We create another set of triangles
C
by packing edges of
X_{1}
with vertices of
K^{\operatorname{top}}_{1}{}\setminus X_{1}
.
Next, let
D
be the set of triangles created by packing edges of
K^{\operatorname{top}}_{1}{}\setminus X_{1}
with vertices in
X_{2}
.
It is clear that all triangles in
\mathsf{TP}=A\cup B\cup C\cup D
are mutually edge-disjoint, therefore
\mathsf{TP}
is indeed a triangle packing.

Let us first settle the case that
x_{1}
is even.
As
2(|A|+|B|)-|\mathsf{TH}|=-(\ell-x_{1})x_{2}
if
x_{1}<\ell
, it remains to show that
2\left|\mathsf{TP}\setminus\left(A\cup B\right)\right|=2(\left|C\right|+\left|D\right|)\geq{{(\ell-x_{1})x_{2}}}
.

If
\ell-x_{1}>x_{2}
, then
2|D|=(\ell-x_{1})x_{2}
by
corollary

5
(
a
).
So, assume that
\ell-x_{1}\leq x_{2}
.
Consequently,
\ell-x_{1}\leq x_{1}
and thus
{\ell}/{2}\leq x_{1}
.
If
x_{1}=\ell/2
, then, by
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\ell/2
, we have
x_{2}=\ell/2
as well.
Thus, as
\ell\geq 4
,
2(|C|+|D|)-(\ell-x_{1})x_{2}=4\binom{\ell/2}{2}-\ell^{2}/4=\ell(\ell-4)/4\geq 0
.
For
\ell-x_{1}<x_{1}
we get
2|C|=x_{1}(\ell-x_{1})\geq x_{2}(\ell-x_{1})
.
Therefore, we always have
2\left|C\cup D\right|\geq{{(\ell-x_{1})x_{2}}}
for even
x_{1}
, and so
2\mu(G)\geq 2\mathsf{TP}\geq\mathsf{TH}\geq\tau(G)
.

In case
x_{1}
is odd, we add one additional triangle to our triangle packing as follows.
Note that if there is no edge between
K^{\operatorname{bot}}_{1}{}
and
K^{\operatorname{bot}}_{2}{}
, then all edges between
K^{\operatorname{top}}_{1}{}
and
K^{\operatorname{top}}_{2}{}
hit all triangles between
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
, therefore taking these edges instead of edges between
K^{\operatorname{top}}_{1}{}
and
K^{\operatorname{bot}}_{2}{}
creates a triangle hitting
\mathsf{TH}^{\prime}
of size at most
4\binom{\ell}{2}+x_{1}\ell
as all the edges between
K^{\operatorname{top}}_{1}{}
and
K^{\operatorname{top}}_{2}{}
have one endpoint in
X_{1}
.
As
x_{1}<\ell
, we obtain
2\mu(G)\geq 2(|A|+|B|)\geq|\mathsf{TH}^{\prime}|\geq\tau(G)
.
Thus we can assume that there is at least one edge
uv
with
u\in K^{\operatorname{bot}}_{1}{}
and
v\in K^{\operatorname{bot}}_{2}{}
.

Note in particular that
v\in X_{2}
as every edge between
K^{\operatorname{bot}}_{1}{}
and
K^{\operatorname{bot}}_{2}{}
has one endpoint in
X_{2}
.
Observe that
|K^{\operatorname{top}}_{1}{}\setminus X_{1}|=\ell-x_{1}
is odd, so there exists an unpacked matching between
K^{\operatorname{top}}_{1}{}\setminus X_{1}
and
X_{2}
(not containing edges used in triangles from set
D
).
Indeed, each maximal matching in
K^{\operatorname{top}}_{1}{}\setminus X_{1}
constructed according to
lemma

3
omits a different vertex
u_{1}\in K^{\operatorname{top}}_{1}{}\setminus X_{1}
, so after the matching is fully joined with a vertex
u_{2}\in X_{2}
, as in
corollary

5
, the edge
u_{1}u_{2}
remains unpacked. A collection of all such edges gives the desired matching.
Let
w\in K^{\operatorname{top}}_{1}{}\setminus X_{1}
be a vertex such that
wv
is an edge of the mentioned unpacked matching.
Finally, as
\ell
is even, a star with center in
K^{\operatorname{top}}_{1}{}
is not used in any triangle in
A
, by
lemma

4
.
Note that the center of this star can be chosen arbitrarily among vertices of
K^{\operatorname{top}}_{1}{}
by
lemma

4
; let us choose
w
to be the center.
Therefore,
uvw
is a triangle which is edge-disjoint with every triangle in
A\cup B\cup C\cup D
and we may set
\mathsf{TP}^{\operatorname{odd}}=\mathsf{TP}\cup\{uvw\}
for odd
x_{1}
.

Recall that
2(|A|+|B|)-|\mathsf{TH}|=-(\ell-x_{1})x_{2}
. Similarly as before, we need to prove that

2\left|\mathsf{TP}^{\operatorname{odd}}\setminus\left(A\cup B\right)\right|=2(\left|C\right|+\left|D\right|+1)\geq{{(\ell-x_{1})x_{2}}}.

If
\ell-x_{1}\leq x_{2}
, then again
\ell-x_{1}\leq x_{1}
and thus
{\ell}/{2}\leq x_{1}
.
The case
\ell/2=x_{1}
can be handled exactly as in the even case.
So assume further
\ell-x_{1}<x_{1}
, then using
corollary

5
we obtain

2(|C|+|D|)=(x_{1}-1)(\ell-x_{1})+2\binom{\ell-x_{1}}{2}=(x_{1}-1)(\ell-x_{1})+(\ell-x_{1})(\ell-x_{1}-1)=(\ell-x_{1})(\ell-2)
.
Consequently,
2(|C|+|D|+1)-(\ell-x_{1})x_{2}=2+(\ell-x_{1})(\ell-2-x_{2})
.
Observe that, for
x_{2}\leq\ell-2
, we already get
(\ell-x_{1})(\ell-2-x_{2})\geq 0
.
We have
x_{1}=\ell-1
because
x_{1}
is odd and
\ell
is even.
For
x_{2}=\ell-1
, we have
x_{1}=\ell-1
because
x_{2}\leq x_{1}<\ell
.
Thus
2+(\ell-x_{1})(\ell-2-x_{2})=2+1\cdot(-1)\geq 0
.
Therefore, we obtain
2\left(\left|C\right|+\left|D\right|+1\right)\geq{(\ell-x_{1})x_{2}}
.

If
\ell-x_{1}>x_{2}
,
then
2|D|=(\ell-x_{1}-1)x_{2}=(\ell-x_{1})x_{2}-x_{2}
.
Hence in this case,
D
alone does not suffice as it is missing
x_{2}
triangles.
We therefore need
2\left|C\right|+2\geq x_{2}
.
We use
corollary

5
to analyse the size of
C
.

If
x_{1}\leq\ell-x_{1}
, then
2|C|+2-x_{2}\geq x_{1}(x_{1}-1)-x_{2}+2\geq(x_{2}-1)^{2}+1\geq 1
as
x_{1}(x_{1}-1)\geq x_{2}(x_{2}-1)
.
If
x_{1}>\ell-x_{1}
, then,

2|C|+2-x_{2}=(x_{1}-1)(\ell-x_{1})-x_{2}+2\geq x_{1}-x_{2}+1\geq 1
, as
\ell-x_{1}\geq 1
and
x_{1}\geq x_{2}
.
So in both cases we obtain
2\left|C\right|+2\geq x_{2}+1\geq x_{2}
.

We conclude that
2\mu(G)\geq 2\mathsf{TP}^{\operatorname{odd}}\geq\mathsf{TH}\geq\tau(G)
.

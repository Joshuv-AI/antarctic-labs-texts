On fast convergence rates for generalized conditional gradient methods with backtracking stepsize Thanks: Karl Kunisch was supported by the ERC advanced grant 668998 (OCLOC) under the EU’s H2020 research program.
Karl Kunisch, Daniel Walter

Abstract
. A generalized conditional gradient method for minimizing the sum of two convex functions, one of them differentiable, is presented. This iterative method relies on two main ingredients: First, the minimization of a partially linearized objective functional to compute a descent direction and, second, a stepsize choice based on an Armijo-like condition to ensure sufficient descent in every iteration. We provide several convergence results. Under mild assumptions, the method generates sequences of iterates which converge, on subsequences, towards minimizers. Moreover a sublinear rate of convergence for the objective functional values is derived. Second, we show that the method enjoys improved rates of convergence if the partially linearized problem fulfills certain growth estimates. Most notably these results do not require strong convexity of the objective functional. Numerical tests on a variety of challenging PDE-constrained optimization problems confirm the practical efficiency of the proposed algorithm.

Introduction

The conditional gradient method,
[
17
]
, provides a simple tool for the solution of constrained minimization problems of the form

(
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{U}_{ad}}
)

\displaystyle\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}}f(u)

where
\mathcal{U}_{ad}
is a convex, compact subset of a Banach space
\mathcal{U}
and
f
is a smooth convex function. This algorithm generates a sequence of iterates
u^{k}
by minimizing the linearization of
f
to obtain a descent direction and updating the iterate by forming a convex combination:

(1.1)

\displaystyle v^{k}\in\argmin_{v\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}}\langle\nabla f(u^{k}),v\rangle,\penalty\ u^{k+1}=u^{k}+s^{k}(v^{k}-u^{k}),\penalty\ s^{k}\in[0,1].

While it is well-known that this method admits a
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
rate of convergence,
[
22
]
, it comes with two desirable properties which outweigh its poor theoretical performance. First, every iteration only requires the solution of a constrained linear minimization problem. This is often easier or computationally less demanding than e.g. the calculation of a projection. We refer to
[
8
]
for some finite dimensional examples. Second, the descent direction
v^{k}
can always be chosen as an extremal point of
\mathcal{U}_{ad}
. Depending on the particular constraint set
\mathcal{U}_{ad}
, this might lead to iterates
u^{k}
exhibiting certain structural features such as sparsity or low rank in the early iterations. These advantages make algorithms of the form (
1.1
) attractive in a large variety of interesting problems. For examples we point to
[
23
,
22
]
and the references therein.

While the sublinear rate of this method is known to be tight in general,
[
7
]
, improved convergence results have already been derived in a variety of settings. For example, if

and
\mathcal{U}_{ad}
are strongly convex and the unique minimizer to (

P

U

⁢
a

More recently, there has been an increased interest in generalizing conditional gradient methods to problems of the form

\displaystyle v^{k}\in\argmin_{v\in\mathcal{U}}\left[\langle\nabla f(u^{k}),v\rangle+g(v)\right],\penalty\ u^{k+1}=u^{k}+s^{k}(v^{k}-u^{k}),\penalty\ s^{k}\in[0,1].

⁢

In the present manuscript a first step is taken at closing this gap. More concretely, our contributions are as follows: We propose a GCG method in the spirit of (

2.
Problem setting and optimality conditions

Throughout the paper we assume that there is a separable Banach space
\mathcal{C}
which is the topological predual space of
\mathcal{U}
, i.e.
\mathcal{C}^{*}\simeq\mathcal{U}
. The corresponding duality pairing is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. This makes
\mathcal{U}
a Banach space with respect to the canonical norm

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}=\sup_{\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{C}}\leq 1}\langle\varphi,u\rangle\quad\text{for all }u\in\mathcal{U}.

A sequence
u^{k}\in\mathcal{U}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, is called weak* convergent with limit
\bar{u}
, denoted by
u^{k}\rightharpoonup^{*}\bar{u}
, if

\displaystyle\langle\varphi,u^{k}\rangle\rightarrow\langle\varphi,\bar{u}\rangle\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{C}.

Moreover the following assumptions concerning Problem (

P

) are made.

Assumption 2.1
.

\mathbf{A1}
.

The function
f\colon\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex, weak* continuous and Gâteaux-differentiable. Moreover there exists a weak*-to-strong continuous mapping
\nabla f\colon\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{C}
with

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(u)(\delta u)=\langle\nabla f(u),\delta u\rangle\quad\forall u,\delta u\in\mathcal{U}.

\mathbf{A2}
.

The function
g\colon\mathcal{U}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is proper, convex, weak* lower semicontinuous, and coercive, i.e.

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}\rightarrow+\infty\Rightarrow g(u)/\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}\rightarrow+\infty.

\mathbf{A3}
.

The functional
j=f+g
is radially unbounded and satisfies
\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}j(u)>-\infty
.

It is also convenient to introduce for each
u\in\mathcal{U}
the sublevel set

(2.1)

\displaystyle E_{j}(u)=\left\{\,v\in\mathcal{U}\;|\;j(v)\leq j(u)\,\right\}.

Using Assumption
2.1
, we see that
E_{j}(u)
is weak* sequentially compact if
j(u)<+\infty
.
This also guarantees the existence of at least one minimizer to (

P

).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold. Then the set
E_{j}(u)
is weak* (sequentially) compact for every
u\in\dom j
. Moreover there exists at least one minimizer
\bar{u}
to (

P

).

Proof.

First observe that
j
is weak* lower semicontinuous, see Assumption
2.1

\mathbf{A1}
and
\mathbf{A2}
. Now let
v_{k}\in E_{j}(u)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, be an arbitrary sequence.
Since
j
is radially unbounded, Assumption
2.1

\mathbf{A3}
,
E_{j}(u)
, and thus also
v_{k}
, is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{U}
. Hence, due to the sequential Banach-Alaoglu theorem, there is
\bar{v}\in\mathcal{U}
and a subsequence of
v_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, denotes by the same subscript with
v_{k}\rightharpoonup^{*}\bar{v}
. The weak* lower semicontinuity of

now yields

\displaystyle j(\bar{v})\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}j(v_{k})\leq j(u)

and thus

. Consequently

is weak* sequentially compact. The existence of a minimizer to (

∎

In addition to Assumption
2.1
we will require the following Lipschitz-like condition on
\nabla f
throughout the paper.

Assumption 2.3
.

There is a norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{*}
on
\mathcal{U}
such that:

A4

Every sequence
u_{k}\in\mathcal{U}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, which is bounded w.r.t.
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathcal{U}}
is also bounded w.r.t.
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{*}
.

A5

For every

there exists

only depending on
E_{j}(u)
such that we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(u_{1})-\nabla f(u_{2}),u_{3}-u_{4}\rangle\leq L_{u}\lVert u_{1}-u_{2}\rVert_{*}\lVert u_{3}-u_{4}\rVert_{*}

for all

.

In the analysis of (

), a major role is played by its first order necessary and sufficient optimality conditions which, see e.g.
[

, are given by the variational inequality

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(\bar{u}),\bar{u}-u\rangle+g(\bar{u})\leq g(u)\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{U}.

For our purposes it will be beneficial to make a connection between (
2.2
) and the

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Psi(u)=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{U}}\left[\langle\nabla f(u),u-v\rangle+g(u)-g(v)\right]\geq 0.

It turns out that its zeros are precisely the minimizers of (

gives an upper bound on the suboptimality of

.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
be given. Then
\bar{u}
satisfies the first order optimality condition (
2.2
) if and only if
\Psi(\bar{u})=0
. Additionally, for every

we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle j(u)-\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}}j(u)\leq\Psi(u),

and
\Psi
is weak* lower semicontinuous.

Proof.

Assume that
\bar{u}
fulfills (
2.2
). Reordering yields

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(\bar{u}),\bar{u}-u\rangle+g(\bar{u})-g(u)\leq 0\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{U}.

Maximizing with respect to
u\in\mathcal{U}
, on both sides we conclude
\Psi(\bar{u})\leq 0
. Since
\Psi
assumes non-negative values only, the statement follows.
Conversely if
\bar{u}
fulfills
\Psi(\bar{u})=0

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(\bar{u}),\bar{u}\rangle+g(\bar{u})\leq\langle\nabla f(\bar{u}),u\rangle+g(u)\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{U}

By rearranging both sides we arrive at (

Finally, let a sequence

For each

\displaystyle\Psi(u^{k})\geq\langle\nabla f(u^{k}),u^{k}-v\rangle+g(u^{k})-g(v).

Taking the limes inferior for

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Psi(u^{k})\geq\langle\nabla f(\bar{u}),\bar{u}-v\rangle+g(\bar{u})-g(v),

due to the weak* convergence of

and the continuity properties of

and

and
\mathbf{A2}
. Since

.
∎

3.
Generalized Frank-Wolfe methods with backtracking stepsize

This section is devoted to the numerical solution of Problem (

\displaystyle v^{k}\in\argmin_{v\in\mathcal{U}}\left[\langle\nabla f(u^{k}),v\rangle+g(v)\right],\penalty\ u^{k+1}=(1-s^{k})u^{k}+s^{k}v^{k}.

\displaystyle\min_{v\in\mathcal{U}}\left[\langle\nabla f(u),v\rangle+g(v)\right]

\displaystyle S(u)=\left\{\,v\in\argmin\eqref{def:linprob}\;|\;u\in E_{j}(u^{0})\,\right\}

Definition 1

Convergence Analysis

This section addresses the convergence properties of Algorithm

\displaystyle r_{j}(u^{k})\coloneqq j(u^{k})-\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}}j(u)

. For this purpose we proceed in two steps. First, in Section

converges (on subsequences) towards minimizers of (

While it is known that this sublinear rate of convergence is tight for general problems,
[

, we will improve on it, in a second step, if there is a minimizer
\bar{u}
which satisfies

(4.1)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(\bar{u}),u-\bar{u}\rangle+g(u)-g(\bar{u})\geq\theta\,\lVert u-\bar{u}\rVert^{q}_{*}\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{U}

and an exponent

Recalling the variational inequality (

. In the case

of the subdifferential

at

with respect to

[

. Due to the convexity of

, (

\displaystyle r_{j}(u)=j(u)-j(\bar{u})\geq\theta\,\lVert u-\bar{u}\rVert_{*}^{q}\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{U}

for

. This also allows to derive convergence rates for the sequence of iterates

. We stress that both, the improved rates for

as well as the results for

, solely rely on (

donot
require any uniform convexity assumptions of

.

Of course, (

) represents an additional, non-trivial, requirement which has to be discussed for each instance of Problem (

Lemma 4.1

Let Assumptions

Due to the convexity of the sublevel set

\displaystyle j(u^{k}_{s})-j(u^{k})=

\displaystyle-s\langle\nabla f(u^{k}),u^{k}-v^{k}\rangle+g(u^{k}_{s})-g(u^{k})+\int_{0}^{s}\!\langle\nabla f(u_{\sigma})-\nabla f(u^{k}),v^{k}-u^{k}\rangle\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\sigma,

\displaystyle-s\langle\nabla f(u^{k}),u^{k}-v^{k}\rangle+g(u^{k}_{s})-g(u^{k})\leq-s\left(\langle\nabla f(u^{k}),u^{k}-v^{k}\rangle+g(u^{k})-g(v^{k})\right),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\!\langle\nabla f(u_{\sigma})-\nabla f(u^{k}),v^{k}-u^{k}\rangle\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle r_{j}(u^{k+1})\leq\left(1-q_{k}\right)\,r_{j}(u^{k})

\displaystyle\alpha\,\min\left\{\frac{2\gamma(1-\alpha)\Psi(u^{k})}{L_{u^{0}}\,\lVert v_{k}-u_{k}\rVert_{*}^{2}},1\right\}\geq q_{k}\coloneqq\alpha\,\min\left\{\frac{2\gamma(1-\alpha)}{L_{u^{0}}\,\bar{c}},1\right\}

Hence, Proposition

\displaystyle r_{j}(u^{k+1})\leq\max\left\{1-\frac{2\alpha\gamma(1-\alpha)}{L_{u^{0}}\bar{c}^{2}},1-\alpha\right\}r_{j}(u^{k}).

By induction over

and
v^{k}
, respectively, then follow from (

).
∎

In order to deal with the case

we need yet another recursive lemma.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
h_{k}\geq 0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, denote a sequence of numbers which satisfy

\displaystyle h_{k+1}\leq\max\left\{\delta,1-Ch_{k}^{\beta}\right\}\,h_{k}

for all

and some

,

.
Then we also have

\displaystyle h_{k}\leq\frac{M}{(k+n)^{1/\beta}}

with constants

\displaystyle n\coloneqq\frac{2-(1/\delta)^{\beta}}{(1/\delta)^{\beta}-1},\penalty\ M\coloneqq\max\left\{h_{0}n^{1/\beta},\frac{1}{\delta\left(\left(\beta-(1-\beta)(2^{\beta}-1)C\right)\right)^{1/\beta}}\right\}.

Proof.

This result can be proven analogously to
[

, Theorem 1]
where the particular case of

was treated. We omit a detailed proof at this point.
∎

With Lemma

at hand, the following improved sublinear rate can be derived.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let Assumption
4.6
hold with

. Then Algorithm

either terminates after

,

\displaystyle\delta=1-\alpha,\penalty\ \beta=1-\frac{2}{q(q-1)},\penalty\ \bar{c}=c_{1}+c_{2},\penalty\ C=\frac{2\alpha\gamma(1-\alpha)}{L_{u^{0}}\bar{c}^{2}}.

Then there is an index

for all

Proof.

Again, since Assumption
4.6
subsumes Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
, Theorem
4.5
holds and we have

for all

\displaystyle\lVert v^{k}-u^{k}\rVert_{*}\leq\bar{c}\,r_{j}(u^{k})^{\frac{1}{q(q-1)}}\quad\forall k\geq K

where

Similarly to the Theorem

we now estimate

\displaystyle\alpha\,\min\left\{\frac{2\gamma(1-\alpha)\Psi(u^{k})}{L_{u^{0}}\,\lVert v_{k}-u_{k}\rVert_{*}^{2}},1\right\}\geq q_{k}\coloneqq\alpha\,\min\left\{\frac{2\gamma(1-\alpha)}{L_{u^{0}}\,\bar{c}^{2}r_{j}(u^{k})^{\frac{2}{q(q-1)}}},1\right\}

∎

Convergence rates for the iterates

5.1.
Bang-bang-off control

As a first example consider an elliptic control problem of the form

\displaystyle\min_{u\in L^{2}(\Omega),\penalty\ y\in L^{2}(\Omega)}\left[\frac{1}{2}\|y-y_{d}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\beta\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right]

\displaystyle u\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}\coloneqq\left\{\,u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\;|\;u_{a}(x)\leq u(x)\leq u_{b}(x)\,\right\}

\displaystyle u_{a},u_{b}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),\penalty\ u_{a}(x)\leq 0\leq u_{b}(x).

Proposition 5.1
.

There exists a linear and weak-to-strong continuous selfadjoint operator

\displaystyle K\colon L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)

\displaystyle-\Delta y=u\penalty\ \mbox{in }\Omega,\penalty\ y=0\penalty\ \mbox{on }\partial\Omega.

in the weak sense.
Moreover there exists

with

(5.4)

\displaystyle\lVert Ku\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert u\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

The existence of

[

.
∎

Consequently, we substitute

and consider the reduced problem

\displaystyle\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}}j(u)\coloneqq\left[\frac{1}{2}\|Ku-\widehat{y}_{d}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\beta\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right],

The functions

We first require the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
p\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be given and define

. Then there is
\sigma>0
with

(5.10)

\displaystyle\lVert p\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-(p,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq 2\sigma\lVert p\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert u-v\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall v,\penalty\ \lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 1.

Proof.

Since
L^{2}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space, the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is uniformly convex of power type
2
, i.e there is
\sigma>0
such that for every

we have

\displaystyle\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 1,\penalty\ \lVert u_{3}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sigma\lVert u_{1}-u_{2}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Rightarrow\left\|(u_{1}+u_{2})/2+u_{3}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 1,

see e.g.
[
40
]
. Now fix
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 1
, and let
z\in L^{2}(\Omega)
with
\lVert z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sigma\lVert u-v\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
be arbitrary. Then using that
(u,p)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\|p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
we have

(5.11)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\lVert p\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&-&({p},v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=({p},u-v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\[6.45831pt]
&=&-2\,({p},(u+v)/2+z)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+2\,({p},z+u)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq 2\,({p},z)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\end{array}

where we used
\lVert(u+v)/2+z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 1
and thus

\displaystyle-2\,({p},(u+v)/2+z)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq-2\lVert{p}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=-2\,({p},u)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Taking the supremum over all
z\in L^{2}(\Omega)
with
\lVert z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sigma\lVert u-v\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
on both sides of (
5.11
) yields

\displaystyle\|p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-({p},u)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq 2\,\sup_{\lVert z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sigma\lVert u-v\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}({p},z)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=2\sigma\lVert{p}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\lVert u-v\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for all
\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 1
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.13

Let Assumption

\displaystyle(\bar{p},u-\bar{u})_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\alpha\int_{I}\left(\lVert u(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\lVert\bar{u}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}t\geq\theta\lVert u-\bar{u}\rVert^{1+1/\kappa}_{L^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}.

where a stationary optimal control problem with pointwise, i.e. affine, control constraints is considered. We
fix an arbitrary

denotes a generic constant independent of

\displaystyle(\bar{p},u-\bar{u})_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle+\alpha\int_{I}\left(\lVert u(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\lVert\bar{u}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +\int_{\lVert\bar{p}(t)\rVert<\alpha}(\bar{p}(t),u(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\alpha\lVert u(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}t

using that
\bar{u}(t)=0
whenever
\lVert\bar{p}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\alpha
. For
\varepsilon>0
introduce the subsets

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\left\{\,t\in I\;|\;\lVert\bar{p}(t)\rVert\leq\alpha-\varepsilon\,\right\}\subset\mathcal{I}\coloneqq\left\{\,t\in I\;|\;\lVert\bar{p}(t)\rVert<\alpha\,\right\}.

Now we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\lVert\bar{p}(t)\rVert<\alpha}(\bar{p}(t),u(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\alpha\lVert u(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon\lVert u\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon};L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\lVert u-\bar{u}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathcal{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}-\varepsilon\lVert u-\bar{u}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathcal{I}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon};L^{2}(\Omega))}

\tilde{c}
denotes a constant for which without loss of generality we assume

. Again we utilized
\bar{u}(t)=0
for

as well as Assumption
5.11
. Choosing
\varepsilon=\tilde{c}^{-\frac{2}{\kappa}}\|u-\bar{u}\|^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}_{L^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}

such that

(5.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\lVert\bar{p}(t)\rVert<\alpha}(\bar{p}(t),u(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\alpha\lVert u(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}t\geq c\lVert u-\bar{u}\rVert^{1+1/\kappa}_{L^{1}(\mathcal{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}.

Similarly define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\left\{\,t\in I\;|\;\lVert\bar{p}(t)\rVert\geq\alpha+\varepsilon\,\right\}\subset\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\left\{\,t\in I\;|\;\lVert\bar{p}(t)\rVert>\alpha\,\right\}.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{p}(t)

\displaystyle,u(t)-\bar{u}(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\alpha\left(\lVert u(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\lVert\bar{u}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ +\int_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\bar{p},\lVert u(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left(\bar{p}(t)/\lVert\bar{p}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)+u(t)\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}t.

Here we have used

\displaystyle-(\bar{p}(t),\bar{u}(t))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=M\lVert\bar{p}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\lVert\bar{u}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\bar{p}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\penalty\ \lVert u(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq M

for a.e

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\lVert\bar{p}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\alpha\right)

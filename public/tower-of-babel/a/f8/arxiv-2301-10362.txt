Black hole perturbations in Maxwell-Horndeski theories Preprint: WUCG-23-02
Ryotaro Kase 1 and Shinji Tsujikawa 2

Abstract
We study the linear stability of black holes in Maxwell-Horndeski theories where a U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge-invariant vector field is coupled to a scalar field with the Lagrangian of full Horndeski theories. The perturbations on a static and spherically symmetric background can be decomposed into odd- and even-parity modes under the expansion of spherical harmonics with multipoles l l . For l ≥ 2 l\geq 2 , the odd-parity sector contains two propagating degrees of freedom associated with the gravitational and vector field perturbations. In the even-parity sector, there are three dynamical perturbations arising from the scalar field besides the gravitational and vector field perturbations. For these five propagating degrees of freedom, we derive conditions for the absence of ghost/Laplacian stabilities along the radial and angular directions. We also discuss the stability of black holes for l = 0 l=0 and l = 1 l=1 , in which case no additional conditions are imposed to those obtained for l ≥ 2 l\geq 2 . We apply our general results to Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton-Gauss-Bonnet theory and Einstein-Born-Infeld-dilaton gravity and show that hairy black hole solutions present in these theories can be consistent with all the linear stability conditions. In regularized four-dimensional Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity with a Maxwell field, however, exact charged black hole solutions known in the literature are prone to instabilities of even-parity perturbations besides a strong coupling problem with a vanishing kinetic term of the radion mode.

Introduction

Black holes (BHs) are the fundamental object arising as a solution to
the Einstein field equation in General Relativity (GR).
On a static and spherically symmetric background, the
Schwarzschild geometry characterized by a single mass parameter
M

is a unique asymptotically flat solution in GR without matter.
In the presence of a Maxwell field, there is a static
BH with an electric charge
q

known as a Reissner-Nordström (RN) solution.
Allowing the rotation of BHs leads to a Kerr solution containing an
angular momentum
J

In Einstein-Maxwell theory without additional matter, there is a uniqueness
theorem stating that stationary and asymptotically-flat BHs are characterized only by three parameters, i.e.,

;

In 4DEGB gravity, the hairy BH said before

In open string theory, there are possible corrections to the Maxwell action arising from couplings of the Abelian gauge field to bosonic strings

;
Fradkin and Tseytlin 1985a
;
Abouelsaood et al. 1987

The tree-level effective electromagnetic action coincides with

{\cal L}=(4/b^{2})(1-\sqrt{1-2b^{2}F})

At leading order in the expansion with respect to a small coupling constant

In the four-dimensional Einstein-BI gravity, there is an exact BH

;
Cai et al. 2004
;
Dey 2004
.
One can deal with such a nonlinear electromagnetism by considering

If there is a scalar field

.

Tamaki and Torii 2001
;
Yazadjiev 2005
;

In this paper, we study the stability of static and spherically
symmetric BH solutions in four-dimensional
Maxwell-Horndeski theories where the scalar field
\phi
with
the Horndeski Lagrangian is coupled
to a
U(1)
gauge-invariant vector field
through the coupling
G_{2}(\phi,X,F)
.
A similar study was performed in Ref.
Gannouji and Baez 2022

for the Lagrangian
{\cal L}=G_{2}(\phi,X,F)+G_{4}(\phi)R
, but
our analysis is more general in that the scalar field sector is
described by the full Horndeski Lagrangian.
By doing this, we can accommodate the stabilities of hairy
BH solutions present in all the theories
mentioned above, especially those containing the GB term.

We decompose the types of perturbations into the odd-
and even-parity sectors and derive all the linear stability
conditions of five dynamical perturbations.
In particular we will derive the propagation speeds of
even-parity perturbations along the angular direction
in the limit of large multipoles
l
, which are missing
in most of the papers about BH perturbations
in the literature
Kobayashi et al. 2014
;
Gannouji and Baez 2022
.
We note that, in full Horndeski theories with a perfect fluid,
all the linear stability conditions including the angular propagation
speeds were derived in Ref.
Kase and Tsujikawa 2022
, which can be applied to the BH case as well (see also Ref.
Kase et al. 2020
).
Indeed, the angular Laplacian stability is important to
exclude hairy BHs arising in nonminimal derivative coupling
theories
Minamitsuji et al. 2022a
;
Minamitsuji et al. 2022a

and in 4DEGB gravity
Tsujikawa 2022
.
Neutron stars with scalar hairs present
in the same theories are also prone to similar instability problems
Kase and Tsujikawa 2021
;
Kase and Tsujikawa 2022
;
Minamitsuji and Tsujikawa 2022
.

After deriving all the linear stability conditions of odd- and even-parity perturbations in Maxwell-Horndeski
theories, we will apply them to concrete hairy BH solutions present in
Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory,
Einstein-BI-dilaton gravity,
Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton-GB theory,
and 4DEGB gravity. While the first three theories allow the existence of
charged BHs consistent with all the linear stability conditions,
the exact charged BH solution present
in 4DEGB gravity suffers from Laplacian instability of even-parity
perturbations as well as the strong coupling problem.
The nature of instabilities is similar to what was found for the
uncharged exact BH solution in 4DEGB gravity
Tsujikawa 2022
.
Thus, our stability criteria in
Maxwell-Horndeski theories are useful to
exclude some BH solutions or constrain
allowed parameter spaces.
The second-order actions of odd- and
even-parity perturbations and resulting field equations of motion can be also applied to the computation of BH quasinormal modes.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.

In Sec.

,
l=0
, and
l=1
, in turn.
In Sec.
V
, we apply our general results to the stability of

Sec.
VI
is devoted to conclusions.

\displaystyle h_{ta}=\sum_{l,m}Q(t,r)E_{ab}\nabla^{b}Y_{lm}(\theta,\varphi)\,,\qquad h_{ra}=\sum_{l,m}W(t,r)E_{ab}\nabla^{b}Y_{lm}(\theta,\varphi)\,,

The vector field

We expand the action (

, and

After integrating the action

{\cal S}_{\rm odd}=\sum_{l}L\int{\rm d}t{\rm d}r\,{\cal L}_{\rm odd}\,,

where

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm odd}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{h}}{4\sqrt{f}}{\cal H}\left(\dot{W}-Q^{\prime}+\frac{2Q}{r}\right)^{2}-\frac{\sqrt{h}}{\sqrt{f}}G_{2,F}A_{0}^{\prime}\left(\dot{W}-Q^{\prime}+\frac{2Q}{r}\right)\delta A+(L-2)\left(\frac{{\cal F}Q^{2}}{4r^{2}\sqrt{fh}}-\frac{\sqrt{fh}}{4r^{2}}{\cal G}W^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{fh}}G_{2,F}\left(\dot{\delta A}^{2}-fh\delta A^{\prime 2}-\frac{Lf}{r^{2}}\delta A^{2}\right)\,,

with

\displaystyle{\cal H}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2G_{4}+2h\phi^{\prime 2}G_{4,X}-h\phi^{\prime 2}G_{5,\phi}-\frac{h^{2}\phi^{\prime 3}G_{5,X}}{r}\,,

(24)

\displaystyle{\cal F}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2G_{4}+h\phi^{\prime 2}G_{5,\phi}-h\phi^{\prime 2}\left(\frac{1}{2}h^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}+h\phi^{\prime\prime}\right)G_{5,X}\,,

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2G_{4}+2h\phi^{\prime 2}G_{4,X}-h\phi^{\prime 2}\left(G_{5,\phi}+{\frac{f^{\prime}h\phi^{\prime}G_{5,X}}{2f}}\right)\,.

(26)

III.1
l\geq 2

We first derive linear stability conditions for the multipoles
l\geq 2
.
To identify the dynamical DOFs, it is convenient to
consider the following Lagrangian

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm odd}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{h}}{4\sqrt{f}}{\cal H}\left[2\chi\left(\dot{W}-Q^{\prime}+\frac{2Q}{r}-\frac{2G_{2,F}A_{0}^{\prime}}{{\cal H}}\delta A\right)-\chi^{2}\right]-\frac{1}{{\cal H}}\frac{\sqrt{h}}{\sqrt{f}}G_{2,F}^{2}A_{0}^{\prime 2}\delta A^{2}+(L-2)\left(\frac{{\cal F}Q^{2}}{4r^{2}\sqrt{fh}}-\frac{\sqrt{fh}}{4r^{2}}{\cal G}W^{2}\right)

(27)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{fh}}G_{2,F}\left(\dot{\delta A}^{2}-fh\delta A^{\prime 2}-\frac{Lf}{r^{2}}\delta A^{2}\right)\,,

where we introduced an auxiliary field
\chi
. Variation of the Lagrangian (
27
) with respect to

\chi
leads to

\chi=\dot{W}-Q^{\prime}+\frac{2Q}{r}-\frac{2G_{2,F}A_{0}^{\prime}}{{\cal H}}\delta A\,.

(28)

Substituting Eq. (
28
) into Eq. (
27
), we find that
the Lagrangian (
27
) is equivalent to (
23
).
Varying (
27
) with respect to
W
and
Q
, respectively,
we obtain

\displaystyle(L-2)f{\cal G}W+r^{2}{\cal H}\,\dot{\chi}=0\,,

(29)

\displaystyle\left[2(L-2){\cal F}Q+4rh{\cal H}\chi+2r^{2}h{\cal H}\chi^{\prime}+r^{2}({\cal H}h^{\prime}+2h{\cal H}^{\prime})\chi\right]f-r^{2}f^{\prime}h{\cal H}\chi=0\,.

(30)

We solve Eqs. (
29
) and (
30
) for
W

and
Q
, and take the
t
and
r
derivatives of
W

and
Q
, respectively.
Substituting them into Eq. (
27
) and integrating it
by parts, the resulting
second-order Lagrangian can be expressed in the form

{\cal L}_{\rm odd}=\dot{\vec{\mathcal{X}}}^{t}{\bm{K}}\dot{\vec{\mathcal{X}}}+\vec{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime t}{\bm{G}}\vec{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}+\vec{\mathcal{X}}^{t}{\bm{M}}\vec{\mathcal{X}}\,,

(31)

where
{\bm{K,G,M}}
are
2\times 2
symmetric matrices, and
\vec{\mathcal{X}}
is a vector field defined by

\vec{\mathcal{X}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\chi\\
\delta A\end{array}\right)\,.

(32)

Note that
\chi
and
\delta A
correspond to the dynamical
perturbations arising from the gravity and vector field
sectors, respectively.
The nonvanishing components of
{\bm{K,G,M}}
are

\displaystyle K_{11}=\frac{r^{2}\sqrt{h}\,{\cal H}^{2}}{4(L-2)f^{3/2}{\cal G}}\,,\qquad K_{22}=\frac{G_{2,F}}{2\sqrt{fh}}\,,\qquad G_{11}=-fh\frac{{\cal G}}{{\cal F}}K_{11}\,,\qquad G_{22}=-fhK_{22}\,,

\displaystyle M_{11}=-\frac{\sqrt{h}\,{\cal H}}{4(L-2)\sqrt{f}}\left(L-2+\alpha_{M}^{\prime}-\frac{2}{r}\alpha_{M}\right)\,,\qquad M_{22}=-\frac{G_{2,F}(Lf{\cal H}+2r^{2}hA_{0}^{\prime 2}G_{2,F})}{2r^{2}{\cal H}\sqrt{fh}}\,,

\displaystyle M_{12}=-\frac{\sqrt{h}A_{0}^{\prime}G_{2,F}}{2\sqrt{f}},

(33)

where

\alpha_{M}\equiv-\frac{r^{2}h{\cal H}}{\cal F}\left(\frac{{\cal H}^{\prime}}{\cal H}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2f}+\frac{h^{\prime}}{2h}+\frac{2}{r}\right)\,.

(34)

The ghosts are absent under the conditions
K_{11}>0
and

K_{22}>0
. These translate to

\displaystyle{\cal G}>0\,,

(35)

\displaystyle G_{2,F}>0\,,

(36)

respectively, which correspond to the no-ghost conditions of
gravitational and vector field perturbations
in the odd-parity sector.

The perturbation equations of motion for
\chi
and
\delta A

follow by varying the Lagrangian (
31
) with respect to these variables. For the propagation of

\chi
and
\delta A
along the radial direction,
we assume solutions to the perturbation equations in the form

\vec{\mathcal{X}}^{t}\propto e^{i(\omega t-kr)}
.
In the short-wavelength limit
k\to\infty
,
the dispersion relation is given by

{\rm det}\left(\omega^{2}{\bm{K}}+k^{2}{\bm{G}}\right)=0
. The radial propagation speed
c_{r}

in proper time can be obtained by substituting
\omega=\sqrt{fh}\,c_{r}k

into the dispersion relation.
This gives the following two solutions

\displaystyle c_{r1,{\rm odd}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{G_{11}}{fhK_{11}}=\frac{{\cal G}}{{\cal F}}\,,

(37)

\displaystyle c_{r2,{\rm odd}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{G_{22}}{fhK_{22}}=1\,,

(38)

which are the squared propagation speeds of
\chi
and
\delta A
, respectively.
Under the no-ghost condition (
35
),
the Laplacian stability of
\chi
is ensured for

{\cal F}>0\,.

(39)

Since the second propagation speed squared (
38
) is luminal,
there is no Laplacian instability for
\delta A
.

In the large multipole limit
L=l(l+1)\gg 1
, the matrix
{\bm{M}}

gives contributions to the propagation speed
c_{\Omega}

along the angular direction.
In this limit, we have

M_{11}\simeq-\frac{{\sqrt{h}\,\cal H}}{4\sqrt{f}}\,,\qquad M_{22}\simeq-\frac{\sqrt{f}G_{2,F}}{2r^{2}\sqrt{h}}L\,.

(40)

Substituting solutions of the form

\vec{\mathcal{X}}^{t}\propto e^{i(\omega t-l\theta)}

into the perturbation equations,
the dispersion relation yields

{\rm det}(\omega^{2}{\bm{K}}+{\bm{M}})=0
.
The angular propagation speed in proper time is given by

c_{\Omega}=\hat{c}_{\Omega}/\sqrt{f}
,
where
\hat{c}_{\Omega}=r\,{\rm d}\theta/{\rm d}t
.
We substitute
\omega^{2}=\hat{c}_{\Omega}^{2}l^{2}/r^{2}=c_{\Omega}^{2}fl^{2}/r^{2}
into the dispersion relation
and solve it for
c_{\Omega}^{2}
.
In the limit
l\gg 1
, we obtain the following two solutions

\displaystyle c_{\Omega 1,{\rm odd}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{r^{2}M_{11}}{l^{2}fK_{11}}=\frac{{\cal G}}{{\cal H}}\,,

(41)

\displaystyle c_{\Omega 2,{\rm odd}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{r^{2}M_{22}}{l^{2}fK_{22}}=1\,,

(42)

which correspond to the squared angular propagation
speeds of
\chi
and
\delta A
, respectively.
Under the no-ghost condition (
35
),
the Laplacian instability of
\chi
is absent for

{\cal H}>0\,.

(43)

The angular propagation speed of
\delta A
is luminal, so
there is no Laplacian instability.

We note that the stability conditions of
\chi
are identical to
those derived in Ref.
Kobayashi et al. 2012
without a
vector field
A_{\mu}
.
This means that the presence of
A_{\mu}
coupled to
the scalar field of the form
G_{2}(\phi,X,F)
does not modify the odd-parity
stability conditions in the gravitational sector.
The odd-parity perturbation of
A_{\mu}

propagates luminally, without a ghost for
G_{2,F}>0
.

III.2

We also study the odd-parity stability of dipolar perturbations (

Since

in Eq. (

Moreover, the metric components

vanish identically

we choose the gauge

Varying the Lagrangian (
23

and setting

at the end, we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{\cal E}=0\,,\qquad\left(r^{2}{\cal E}\right)^{\prime}=0\,,

Integrating two differential equations in (

Q^{\prime}-\frac{2}{r}Q+\frac{2G_{2,F}A_{0}^{\prime}}{{\cal H}}\delta A=\frac{1}{\cal H}\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}\frac{{\cal C}}{r^{2}}\,,

(47)

where
{\cal C}
is a constant.
On using this relation to eliminate

from

23

{\cal L}_{\rm odd}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{fh}}\left[G_{2,F}\dot{\delta A}^{2}-G_{2,F}fh\delta A^{\prime 2}-\frac{2G_{2,F}(f{\cal H}+r^{2}hG_{2,F}A_{0}^{\prime 2})}{r^{2}{\cal H}}\delta A^{2}+\frac{f{\cal C}^{2}}{2r^{4}{\cal H}}\right]\,.

.
The ghost is absent so long as the first term in the
square bracket of Eq. (
48
) is positive, i.e.,

(49)

which is the same as the no-ghost condition of
\delta A

derived for
l\geq 2
.
In the short-wavelength limit, the dominant contributions to
Eq. (
48
) are the first and second terms in the square bracket.
Then, the radial propagation speed squared of
\delta A

in proper time is given by

c_{r,{\rm odd}}^{2}=1\,,

(50)

which is luminal.
Thus, the stability of dipolar perturbations does not add any
new conditions to those obtained for

.

V
Application to concrete theories with hairy BHs

Theories with the action (
2

a wide variety of hairy BH solutions known in the literature.
In this section, we apply the linear stability conditions derived

We will focus on the case

V.1
Nonminimally coupled k-essence with a gauge field

We begin with a subclass of Maxwell-Horndeski given by the action

The analysis of BH perturbations in this case was also

In the following, we will investigate all the linear

The stability of odd-parity perturbations requires that

(117)

\chi

\delta A

In the even-parity sector, the quantity (

\phi^{\prime}

the no-ghost condition

G_{2,X}G_{4}+3G_{4,\phi}^{2}>0\,.

For a minimally coupled scalar field

reduces to
G_{2,X}>0

The radial propagation speeds of both
\psi
and

are luminal (
c_{r1,{\rm even}}^{2}=c_{r3,{\rm even}}^{2}=1

To ensure the Laplacian stability of
\delta\phi
along the
radial direction, we require that

c_{r2,{\rm even}}^{2}=1+\frac{2G_{4}X[G_{2,XX}(G_{2,F}+2FG_{2,FF})-2FG_{2,FX}^{2}]}{(G_{2,X}G_{4}+3G_{4,\phi}^{2})(G_{2,F}+2FG_{2,FF})}>0\,,

(120)

Along the angular direction, the quantities (
98

(

B_{1}=-1\,,\qquad B_{2}=1\,,

(121)

c_{\Omega+,{\rm even}}^{2}=1\,,\qquad c_{\Omega-,{\rm even}}^{2}=1\,.

(122)

for the perturbations
\psi
and
\delta\phi

The angular stability of vector field perturbation

c_{\Omega 3,{\rm even}}^{2}=\frac{G_{2,F}}{G_{2,F}+2FG_{2,FF}}>0\,.

(123)

Under the no-ghost condition

the inequality (

In the following, we will study the stability of hairy BHs in two

V.1.1
Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory

In bosonic heterotic string

V.1.2

V.3

The GB curvature invariant

It would be also of interest to address the stability of even-parity BH perturbations in such theories.
These issues are left for future works.

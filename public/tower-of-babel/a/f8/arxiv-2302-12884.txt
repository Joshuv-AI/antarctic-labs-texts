Moving Target Detection via Multi-IRS-Aided OFDM Radar
Zahra Esmaeilbeig, Arian Eamaz, Kumar Vijay Mishra, Mojtaba Soltanalian

Abstract
An intelligent reflecting surface (IRS) consists of passive reflective elements capable of altering impinging waveforms. The IRS-aided radar systems have recently been shown to improve detection and estimation performance by exploiting the target information collected via non-line-of-sight paths. However, the waveform design problem for an IRS-aided radar has remained relatively unexplored. In this paper, we consider a multi-IRS-aided orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) radar and study the theoretically achievable accuracy of target detection. In addition, we jointly design the OFDM signal and IRS phase-shifts to optimize the target detection performance via an alternating optimization approach. To this end, we formulate the IRS phase-shift design problem as a unimodular bi-quadratic program which is tackled by a computationally cost-effective approach based on power-method-like iterations. Numerical experiments illustrate that our proposed joint design of IRS phase-shifts and the OFDM code improves the detection performance in comparison with conventional OFDM radar.

In particular, we formulate the detection problem as a hypothesis test to decide the presence of a target in a particular range cell. Then, we jointly design the OFDM signal and the IRS phase-shifts to enhance the receiver operating characteristics (RoC) associated with moving target detection. We make the following assumptions about the IRS-aided OFDM radar and target parameters:

A1

“Bandwidth-invariant Doppler”: The bandwidth of OFDM signal is much smaller than the carrier frequency, i.e.,

A2

“Slow Target”: The Doppler frequency of the target does not change during one coherent processing interval (CPI) i.e.

A3

“Narrow surveillance area”: The radar is deployed in a region, where the range of the target is much greater than the width or cross-range extent of the surveillance area. The relative time gaps between any two signals received from NLoS paths are very small in comparison to the LoS round trip delays, i.e.,
\tau_{m}\approx\tau_{0}=\frac{2d_{tr}}{c}

m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}

A4

“Frequency-invariant IRS phase-shift”: The IRS platforms impose the same phaseshifts over all subcarrier frequencies and therefore the IRS phase-shift matrix is not indexed over different frequencies, i.e.,

-radar path for

III
Target Detection

In order to decide whether a target is present in a particular known range-cell, we perform binary hypothesis testing between
\mathcal{H}_{0}
(target-free hypothesis) and
\mathcal{H}_{1}
(target-present hypothesis), that is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}:

\displaystyle\quad\mathbf{Y}_{{}_{\textrm{OFDM}}}=\mathbf{N},

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}:

\displaystyle\quad\mathbf{Y}_{{}_{\textrm{OFDM}}}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{P}(\boldsymbol{\nu})+\mathbf{N}.

(19)

The likelihood ratio is
[

,
22
]

\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{OFDM}};\boldsymbol{\nu}\right)=\frac{f_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{OFDM}};\boldsymbol{\nu},\mathbf{X},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1}\right)}{f_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{OFDM }};\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{0}\right)},

where

and

are the likelihood functions under
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}
, respectively and
\boldsymbol{\nu}
is the Doppler frequency under test. Since the
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
and target parameters are unknown, we employ a generalized likelihood ratio test (GLRT) by replacing the unknowns with their maximum likelihood estimates (MLEs) in the
\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{OFDM}};\boldsymbol{\nu}\right)
to obtain the GLRT for our detection problem (
18

\mathcal{T}_{{}_{\text{GLR}}}=\frac{f_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{OFDM}};\boldsymbol{\nu},\widehat{\mathbf{X}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{1}\right)}{f_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{OFDM }};\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{0}\right)}\hskip 9.24994pt\underset{\mathcal{H}_{2}}{\overset{\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\gtrless}}\gamma,

(21)

where
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{0}
and
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{1}
are the MLEs of
\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}
under
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1},\widehat{\mathbf{X}}
is the MLE of
\mathbf{X}
under
\mathcal{H}_{1}
, and

is the detection threshold
[
27
,
22
]
.
The MLEs of unknown covariance matrices are

\displaystyle\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{OFDM}}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{P}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\right)^{\mathrm{H}}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{OFDM}}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{P}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\right),

(22)

\displaystyle\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{Y}_{\text{OFDM}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{Y}_{\text{OFDM}}.

(23)

Therefore,
In the Gaussian noise scenario, the GLR becomes

We have the probability of false alarm (

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{T}_{{}_{{\textrm{GLR}}}}>\gamma|\mathcal{H}_{0}\right)=Q_{{}_{rL}}(\gamma)

is the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of chi-squared distribution and

IV
Joint Waveform and IRS Phase-Shift Design

As discussed earlier in Section
III
, the probability of detection for a given probability of false alarm is a monotonically increasing function of the

. Hence, the design problem may also be formulated to maximize the SNR with respect to the system parameters. The joint waveform and IRS phase-shift design problem is

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{v},\boldsymbol{a}}{\textrm{maximize}}\hskip 9.24994ptf(\boldsymbol{a},\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle\textrm{subject to}\hskip 9.24994pt\boldsymbol{a}^{H}\boldsymbol{a}=1.

(29)

We resort to a task-specific cyclic algorithm, wherein we cyclically optimize (

) for
\boldsymbol{a}
and
\mathbf{v}

[

,
20
]
.
To ensure the unimodularity constraint of IRS phase-shift, we need a projection-based optimization method.

IV-A

OFDM Waveform Design

Problem (

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}:\;\;\;

\displaystyle\underset{\boldsymbol{a}}{\textrm{maximize}}\hskip 9.24994pt\boldsymbol{a}^{\mathrm{H}}\left[\left(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{P}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\mathbf{P}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{H}}\right)^{\top}\odot\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\right]\boldsymbol{a}

\displaystyle\textrm{subject to}\hskip 9.24994pt\boldsymbol{a}^{H}\boldsymbol{a}=1.

The optimal OFDM coefficient

is the eigenvector corresponding to the dominant eigenvalue of
\left(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{P}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\mathbf{P}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{H}}\right)^{\top}\odot\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}
, evaluated based on the

at each iteration

\boldsymbol{a}^{(s+1)}=\frac{\left[\left(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{P}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\mathbf{P}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{H}}\right)^{\top}\odot\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\right]\boldsymbol{a}^{(s)}}{\left\|\left[\left(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{P}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\mathbf{P}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{H}}\right)^{\top}\odot\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\right]\boldsymbol{a}^{(s)}\right\|_{2}},\;s\geq 0.

(31)

IV-B

IRS Beamforming Design

To design the phase-shift parameters, we maximize the SNR metric with respect to
\mathbf{v}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{2}:\;\;\;\;\underset{\mathbf{v}}{\textrm{maximize}}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994ptf(\boldsymbol{a},\mathbf{v}).

(32)

In what follows, we show that
\mathcal{P}_{2}
can be written as a unimodular quadratic program (UQP). To tackle
\mathcal{P}_{2}
with respect to
\mathbf{v}
, we adopt a computationally efficient procedure of the
power-method-like iterations (PMLI)
algorithm
[
33
]
.
This method closely resembles the widely used power method for computing the dominant eigenvalue/vector pairs of matrices.

The UQP is defined as

\underset{\mathbf{s}\in\Omega^{n}}{\textrm{maximize}}\quad\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{s}.

(33)

The sequence of unimodular vectors at the
t
-th PMLI iteration is

\mathbf{s}^{(t+1)}=e^{\textrm{j}\operatorname{arg}\left(\mathbf{G}\mathbf{s}^{(t)}\right)},

(34)

VI
Summary

We investigated the moving target detection problem using a multi-IRS-aided OFDM radar. The IRS phase-shifts along with the OFDM transmit signal coefficients were designed by taking advantage of an alternating optimization of the non-centrality parameter of the GLRT. We showed that maximizing the non-centrality parameter improved the probability of detection. By means of numerical investigations, we demonstrated that our proposed method enhances the
P_{{}_{\textrm{D}}}
over the non-IRS OFDM radar systems.

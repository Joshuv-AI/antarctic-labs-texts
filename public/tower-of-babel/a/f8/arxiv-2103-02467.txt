Rank deficiency of random matrices
Vishesh Jain, Ashwin Sah, Mehtaab Sawhney

Abstract
. Let M n M_{n} be a random n × n n\times n matrix with i.i.d. Bernoulli ⁡ ( 1 / 2 ) \operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2) entries. We show that for fixed k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , lim n → ∞ 1 n log 2 ℙ [ corank M n ≥ k ] = − k . \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}\mathbb{P}[\operatorname{corank}M_{n}\geq k]=-k.



1.
Introduction

A fundamental, and intensely studied, problem in combinatorial random matrix theory is the determination of the probability of singularity of
n\times n
random Bernoulli matrices (i.e.
n\times n
matrices for which each entry is independently
0
or
1
with equal probability). The study of this problem was initiated in work of Komlós
[
11
]
. After intermediate works over a period of over 50 years
[
10
,
15
,
16
,
2
]
, the breakthrough work of Tikhomirov
[
17
]
showed that for any fixed
p\in(0,1/2]
,

\mathbb{P}[M_{n}(\operatorname{Ber}(p))\text{ is singular}]=(1-p+o_{n}(1))^{n},

where we use the notation
M_{m\times n}(\xi)
to denote an
m\times n
random matrix with i.i.d. entries distributed as
\xi
, and the lighter notation
M_{n}(\xi)
for
M_{n\times n}(\xi)
. Also,
\operatorname{Ber}(p)
is the random variable which takes on the value
1
with probability
p
and
0
with probability
1-p
.

By considering the probability of a row of the matrix being
0
, one sees that the result of Tikhomirov is optimal up to the
o_{n}(1)
term. Recently, several works
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
12
,
1
]
have addressed the more refined question of determining the probability of singularity of
M_{n}(\xi)
up to a
(1+o_{n}(1))
factor; in contrast, the aforementioned result of Tikhomirov determines this probability only up to a subexponential (in
n
) factor. While these works have succeeded in the case of sparse Bernoulli matrices (with sparsity allowed to depend on
n
)
[
7
,
12
,
1
]
, as well as in the case of a fixed
\xi
which is not uniform on its support
[
8
,
9
]
, we note that for the case of
\operatorname{Ber}(1/2)
, the estimate of Tikhomirov remains essentially the best known.

An equivalent condition to singularity of
M_{n}(\xi)
is that the corank of
M_{n}(\xi)
is at least
1
. Given this formulation, the following question immediately suggests itself: given
k\geq 1
, what is the probability that the corank of
M_{n}(\xi)
is at least
k
? In the case when
\xi=\operatorname{Ber}(p)
for fixed
p\in(0,1/2]
, by considering the event that first
k
rows of the matrix are identically
0
, we see that this probability is at least
(1-p)^{nk}
. The previously best-known upper bound appears to be due to Kahn, Komlós, and Szemerédi
[
10
]
, who showed that there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
f(k)\to 0
as
k\to\infty
such that

\mathbb{P}[\operatorname{corank}M_{n}(\operatorname{Ber}(1/2))\geq k]\leq f(k)^{n}.

The above simple lower bound shows that the decay of
f(k)
can be at most
2^{-k}
; it has been suggested (cf.
[
18
, Section 4]
, ‘It is tempting to conjecture…’) that this rate of decay is essentially sharp, i.e. that

\mathbb{P}[\operatorname{corank}M_{n}(\operatorname{Ber}(1/2))\geq k]=(1/2+o_{n}(1))^{kn}.

The main result of this paper confirms this belief.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
p\in(0,1/2]
and let
\xi=\operatorname{Ber}(p)
. Fix
k\geq 1
and
\epsilon>0
. Then, for
n\geq n_{\ref{thm:main}}(p,k,\epsilon)
, we have

\mathbb{P}[\operatorname{corank}M_{n}(\xi)\geq k]=(1-p+\epsilon)^{kn}.

Remark
.

A modification of our proof, with
Proposition

2.7
replaced by the corresponding versions in
[
8
,
9
]
, shows that for any fixed
\xi
which is supported on finitely many points,

\mathbb{P}[\operatorname{corank}M_{n}(\xi)\geq k]\leq(\max_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}[\xi=z]+o_{n}(1))^{kn}.

We conjecture that, in general, the following holds.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Fix a random variable
\xi
supported on finitely many points. Fix
k\geq 1
and
\epsilon>0
. Then for
n\geq n_{\ref{conj:general}}(\xi,k,\epsilon)
, we have

\mathbb{P}[\operatorname{corank}M_{n}(\xi)\geq k]\leq(\mathbb{P}[\xi=0]+\epsilon)^{kn}+(\mathbb{P}[\xi_{1}=\cdots=\xi_{k+1}]+\epsilon)^{n},

where
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k+1}
are independent samples of
\xi
.

A stronger conjecture is that the dominant contribution to the probability of the corank being at least
k
comes from the event of having
k-t+1
rows equal up to sign and
t
rows zero for some
0\leq t\leq k
, or the same for columns (i.e., this controls the probability up to a
(1+o_{n}(1))
factor). All such events contribute, for instance, when
\xi=\operatorname{Ber}(1/2)
. However, in the case when
\xi=\operatorname{Ber}(p)
,
p\in(0,1/2)
, the event of having
k
rows which are identically zero is exponentially more likely than any of the other events. In concurrent and independent work, Huang
[
6
]
has proved this stronger conjecture (with accompanying singular value bounds) for sufficiently sparse Bernoulli matrices i.e.
\xi=\operatorname{Ber}(p_{n})
with

1\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_{n}\cdot n}{\log{n}}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_{n}\cdot n}{\log{n}}<\infty;

it is plausible that, combined with the techniques in
[
12
]
, the upper bound on the
\limsup
can be relaxed (perhaps even up to
p_{n}\leq c
for some small constant
c
). In the complementary dense case considered here, we leave the resolution of this stronger conjecture as a subject for future research.

Finally, we mention that in recent years, there have been several other works on the (co)rank of random matrices (cf.
[
4
,
3
]
). However, the focus of these works is on the asymptotic determination of the (co)rank of various models, which is different from our focus on the determination of (sharp) non-asymptotic rates to have corank at least
k
for matrices which are of full rank with high probability.

1.1.
Notation

Given a positive integer
N\geq 1
, let
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
be the set of unit vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Let
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2}
be the Euclidean norm. For a matrix
A=(A_{ij})
, let
\lVert A\rVert
be its spectral norm (i.e.,
\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}
operator norm) and let
\lVert A\rVert_{\operatorname{HS}}
be its Hilbert-Schmidt norm, defined as

\lVert A\rVert_{\operatorname{HS}}^{2}=\sum A_{ij}^{2}.

We let
[N]
denote the discrete interval
\{1,\dots,N\}
. Given an
m\times n
matrix
A
and a subset
S\subseteq[n]
of columns, we let
A_{S}
be the
m\times|S|
submatrix of
A
consisting only of the columns in
S
.

For an
\mathbb{R}^{N}
-valued random variable
\xi
and a real number
r\geq 0
, we define the Lévy concentration function by

\mathcal{L}(\xi,r):=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathbb{P}[\lVert\xi-z\rVert_{2}\leq r].

Note that the case
N=1
coincides with the usual (scalar) Lévy concentration function. We let
\ell_{1}(\mathbb{Z})
denote the set of functions
f\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(z)|<\infty
.

We also make use of asymptotic notation. Given functions
f,g
, we write
f=O_{\alpha}(g)
or
f\lesssim_{\alpha}g
to mean
f\leq C_{\alpha}g
, where
C_{\alpha}
is some constant depending on
\alpha
. We write
f=\Omega_{\alpha}(g)
or
f\gtrsim_{\alpha}g
to mean
f\geq c_{\alpha}g
, where
c_{\alpha}>0
is some constant depending on
\alpha
. Finally, we write
f=\Theta_{\alpha}(g)
to mean that both
f=O_{\alpha}(g)
and
f=\Omega_{\alpha}(g)
hold. For parameters
\epsilon,\delta
, we write
\epsilon\ll_{\alpha}\delta
to mean that
\epsilon\leq c_{\alpha}(\delta)
for a sufficiently decaying function
c_{\alpha}
depending on
\alpha
.

Finally, we will omit floors and ceilings when they make no essential difference.

Proof of
Theorem

2.1.
Preliminaries

We collect some (by now) standard notions in the non-asymptotic theory of random matrices. For parameters
\delta,\rho\in(0,1)
and an integer
n\geq 1
,
\operatorname{Comp}_{n}(\delta,\rho)
denotes the set of unit vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which have Euclidean distance at most
\rho
to the set of
\delta n
-sparse vectors.
\operatorname{Incomp}_{n}(\delta,\rho):=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus\operatorname{Comp}_{n}(\delta,\rho)
. When the ambient dimension is clear from context, we will drop the subscript
n
.

We will need to consider the anticoncentration behavior of a vector with respect to i.i.d.
\operatorname{Ber}(p)
random variables. For this, we will use the threshold function, which was isolated in the work of Tikhomirov
[
17
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
p\in(0,1/2]
,
L\geq 1
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define

\mathcal{T}_{p}(x,L):=\sup\bigg\{t\in(0,1):\mathcal{L}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}x_{i},t\bigg)>Lt\bigg\},

where
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}
are independent
\operatorname{Ber}(p)
random variables.

2.2.
Overview of the proof

In this subsection, we present the (short) proof of
Theorem

1.1
, modulo the key
Propositions

2.2
and
2.7
, which we will formally state and prove in the subsequent subsections.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

For
\delta,\rho\in(0,1)
, let
\mathcal{E}_{C}(\delta,\rho)
be the event that for every
(n-k)\times n
sub-matrix
A
of
M_{n}(\xi)
, and for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
such that
Ax=0
, we have that
x\in\operatorname{Comp}(\delta,\rho)
. In words, the right-kernel unit vectors of every
(n-k)\times n
sub-matrix of
M_{n}(\xi)
are in
\operatorname{Comp}(\delta,\rho)
.

Denote the rows of
M_{n}(\xi)
by
R_{1},\dots,R_{n}
. If
\operatorname{rank}M_{n}(\xi)\leq n-k
, then there must be some
k
rows of
M_{n}(\xi)
which are in the span of the remaining
n-k
rows. Since the rows are i.i.d, it follows from the union bound that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\operatorname{rank}M_{n}(\xi)\leq n-k]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{C}(\delta,\rho)]+\mathbb{P}[\operatorname{rank}M_{n}(\xi)\leq n-k\wedge\mathcal{E}_{C}(\delta,\rho)^{c}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{C}(\delta,\rho)]+\binom{n}{k}\mathbb{P}[\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{k}\in\operatorname{span}(R_{k+1},\ldots,R_{n})\}\wedge\mathcal{E}_{C}(\delta,\rho)^{c}].

In
Proposition

2.2
, which is the key innovation of this work, we will show that there exist
\delta,\rho\in(0,1)
(depending on
k,p,\epsilon
) such that

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{C}(\delta,\rho)]\leq(1-p+\epsilon)^{kn}.

Now, fix this choice of
\delta,\rho
and denote the corresponding event
\mathcal{E}_{C}(\delta,\rho)
simply by
\mathcal{E}_{C}
. Since
\binom{n}{k}\leq n^{k}\leq(1+\epsilon)^{n}
for
n
sufficiently large, it remains to show that

\mathbb{P}[\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{k}\in\operatorname{span}(R_{k+1},\ldots,R_{n})\}\wedge\mathcal{E}_{C}^{c}]\leq(1-p+\epsilon)^{nk}.

In
Proposition

2.7
, we show the following dichotomy: consider the
(n-k)\times n
matrix
M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)
formed by the rows
R_{k+1},\ldots,R_{n}
. With probability at least
1-4^{-kn}
, either

•

every unit vector in the right-kernel of
M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)
is in
\operatorname{Comp}(\delta,\rho)
, or

•

there is a unit vector
v=v(R_{k+1},\ldots,R_{n})
in the right-kernel of
M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)
with

\mathcal{T}_{p}(v,L_{\ref{prop:structure}})\leq(1-p+\epsilon)^{n},

where
L_{\ref{prop:structure}}
is a constant depending on
k,p,\epsilon
.

Note that, on the event
\mathcal{E}_{C}^{c}
, the first case cannot occur. Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the set of possible realizations of
R_{k+1},\ldots,R_{n}
for which the second case occurs; for every such realisation
a\in\mathcal{A}
, we have a unit vector
v=v(a)
satisfying the conclusion of the second case. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[R_{1},\ldots,R_{k}\in\operatorname{span}(R_{k+1},\ldots,R_{n})\wedge\mathcal{E}_{C}^{c}]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{P}[\langle R_{1},v(a)\rangle=\cdots=\langle R_{k},v(a)\rangle=0]+4^{-kn}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\ref{prop:structure}}^{k}(1-p+\epsilon)^{kn}+4^{-kn}.

The last inequality uses the independence of the rows, the definition of the threshold function, and the property
\mathcal{T}_{p}(v(a),L_{\ref{prop:structure}})\leq(1-p+\epsilon)^{n}
. The result now follows upon rescaling
\epsilon
.
∎

2.3.
Compressible vectors

In this subsection, we prove
Proposition

2.2
. Recall the event
\mathcal{E}_{C}(\delta,\rho)
defined at the start of the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Fix
p\in(0,1/2]
,
k\geq 1
, and
\epsilon>0
. There exist
\delta,\rho\in(0,1)
(depending on
p,k,\epsilon
) such that

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{C}(\delta,\rho)]\leq(1-p+\epsilon)^{kn}.

The proof of
Proposition

2.2
requires two ingredients, the first of which is the classical Kolmogorov-Lévy-Rogozin anticoncentration inequality.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
be independent random variables. Then, for any real numbers
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}>0
and any real number
r\geq\max_{i\in[n]}r_{i}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i},r\bigg)\leq\frac{C_{\ref{lem:LKR}}r}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1-\mathcal{L}(\xi_{i},r_{i}))r_{i}^{2}}},

where
C_{\ref{lem:LKR}}>0
is an absolute constant.

The second ingredient is a version of restricted invertibility which allows one to select a subset of columns of full rank.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
5
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
U
be an
n\times m
matrix of rank
n
. Then, there exists a subset
\mathcal{S}\subseteq[m]
of columns of size
|\mathcal{S}|=n
such that

\lVert U_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\rVert_{\operatorname{HS}}^{2}\leq(m-n+1)\cdot\operatorname{Tr}[(UU^{T})^{-1}].

The following is the key lemma in the proof of
Proposition

2.2
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Fix
p\in(0,1/2]
and
k\geq 1
. There exists
\theta=\theta_{\ref{lem:levy-estimate}}(p,k)>0
for which the following holds. If
M
is a
k\times n
matrix whose rows are orthonormal vectors and
x
is an
n
-dimensional random vector with independent
\operatorname{Ber}(p)
components, then

\mathcal{L}(Mx,\theta)\leq(1-p)^{k}.

Remark
.

Our proof shows that
\theta(p,k)
can be taken to be of size
\operatorname{poly}(p)\exp(-\Omega(k))
. We suspect this is far from the truth and conjecture that one can take
\theta(p,k)
to be of size
\operatorname{poly}(p,1/k)
.

Proof.

For each
1\leq i\leq k
, let
\mathcal{T}_{i}\subseteq[n]
be the indices
j\in[n]
corresponding to the
\lfloor C_{\ref{lem:LKR}}^{2}25^{k}/p\rfloor
(which is
\geq k
) largest values
|M_{ij}|
, where (for concreteness) we break ties according to the natural ordering of the integers. Let
\mathcal{T}=\cup_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{T}_{i}
. Since
\sum_{j=1}^{n}|M_{ij}|^{2}=1
for every
i\in[k]
, it follows that for every
j\in\mathcal{T}^{c}
,

|M_{ij}|\leq 5^{-k}\sqrt{p}/C_{\ref{lem:LKR}}\quad\forall i\in[k].

We have two cases.

Case I:
There exists some
i\in[k]
such that
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{T}^{c}}|M_{ij}|^{2}\geq 4^{-k}
. In this case, by applying
Lemma

2.3
with
r=5^{-k}\sqrt{p}/(3C_{\ref{lem:LKR}})
and
r_{j}=|M_{ij}|/3
, we find that

\mathcal{L}\bigg(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{T}^{c}}M_{ij}x_{j},r\bigg)\leq C_{\ref{lem:LKR}}\frac{C_{\ref{lem:LKR}}^{-1}5^{-k}\sqrt{p}/3}{2^{-k}\sqrt{p/9}}<2^{-k}\leq(1-p)^{k}.

Since

\mathcal{L}(Mx,r)\leq\mathcal{L}((Mx)_{i},r)\leq\mathcal{L}\bigg(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{T}^{c}}M_{ij}x_{j},r\bigg),

we have the required conclusion with
\theta=r=5^{-k}\sqrt{p}/(3C_{\ref{lem:LKR}})
.

Case II:
For every
i\in[k]
,
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{T}^{c}}|M_{ij}|^{2}\leq 4^{-k}
. Let
U=M_{\mathcal{T}}
denote the
k\times|\mathcal{T}|
matrix formed by the columns of
M
corresponding to
\mathcal{T}
. Note that
k\leq|\mathcal{T}|\leq C_{\ref{lem:LKR}}^{2}k25^{k}/p
. Let
C_{1},\dots,C_{k}
denote the columns of
M
. Since
MM^{T}=I
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert I-UU^{T}\rVert

\displaystyle=\lVert MM^{T}-UU^{T}\rVert\leq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{T}^{c}}\lVert C_{j}C_{j}^{T}\rVert

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{T}^{c}}\lVert C_{j}\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i\in[k]}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{T}^{c}}|M_{ij}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq k4^{-k}<1/2.

In particular, the eigenvalues of
UU^{T}
are in
(1/2,1]
so that

\operatorname{Tr}[(UU^{T})^{-1}]<2k.

Therefore, by
Lemma

2.4
, there exists a set of columns
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
of size
|\mathcal{S}|=k
such that

\lVert M_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\rVert_{\operatorname{HS}}^{2}=\lVert U_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\rVert_{\operatorname{HS}}^{2}\leq 2C_{\ref{lem:LKR}}^{2}k^{2}25^{k}/p.

Hence, the smallest singular value of
M_{\mathcal{S}}
, denoted by
\sigma_{k}(M_{\mathcal{S}})
, must satisfy

\sigma_{k}(M_{\mathcal{S}})\geq\frac{\sqrt{p}}{2C_{\ref{lem:LKR}}k5^{k}}.

We claim that for

r=\frac{\sqrt{p}}{5C_{\ref{lem:LKR}}k5^{k}},

we have that

\mathcal{L}(Mx,r)\leq(1-p)^{k}.

Since

\mathcal{L}(Mx,r)\leq\mathcal{L}(M_{\mathcal{S}}x_{\mathcal{S}},r),

it suffices to show that

\mathcal{L}(M_{\mathcal{S}}x_{\mathcal{S}},r)\leq(1-p)^{k}.

Since
x_{\mathcal{S}}
is a
k
-dimensional vector with i.i.d.
\operatorname{Ber}(p)
entries, it follows that
x_{\mathcal{S}}
is supported on
\{0,1\}^{k}
with maximum atom probability
(1-p)^{k}
. Moreover, by definition of the smallest singular value, we see that for any
x\neq y\in\{0,1\}^{k}
,

\|M_{\mathcal{S}}x-M_{\mathcal{S}}y\|_{2}\geq\sigma_{k}(M_{\mathcal{S}})\|x-y\|_{2}\geq\sigma_{k}(M_{\mathcal{S}}),

which shows that

\mathcal{L}(M_{\mathcal{S}}x_{\mathcal{S}},r)\leq\max_{x\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\mathbb{P}[x_{\mathcal{S}}=x]=(1-p)^{k}.

Thus, in either case, we can take

\theta=\frac{\sqrt{p}}{5C_{\ref{lem:LKR}}k5^{k}}.\qed

The previous lemma allows us to quickly deduce the following which, in the special case
k=1
, is the usual ‘invertibility with respect to a single vector’ (cf.
[
17
, Lemma 3.5]
).

Lemma 2.6
.

Fix
p\in(0,1/2]
,
k\geq 1
, and
\epsilon>0
. Let
\xi=\operatorname{Ber}(p)
. There exists
c_{\ref{lem:fixed-vectors}}=c_{\ref{lem:fixed-vectors}}(p,k,\epsilon)>0
for which the following holds. Let
n\geq n_{\ref{lem:fixed-vectors}}(p,k,\epsilon)
and let
V=[v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}]
be an
n\times k
matrix with orthonormal columns. Then,

\mathbb{P}[\lVert M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)V\rVert_{\operatorname{HS}}\leq c_{\ref{lem:fixed-vectors}}\sqrt{n}]\leq(1-p+\epsilon)^{kn}.

Proof.

Denote the rows of
M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)
by
R_{1},\ldots,R_{n-k}
. Let
\theta=\theta_{\ref{lem:levy-estimate}}(p,k)
. Note that if

\lVert M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)V\rVert_{\operatorname{HS}}\leq\theta\sqrt{\epsilon^{\prime}(n-k)},

then at most
\epsilon^{\prime}(n-k)
rows
R_{i}
can satisfy
\lVert R_{i}V\rVert_{2}>\theta
. Denote the set of these rows by
\mathcal{I}
. By
Lemma

2.5
and the independence of the rows, we have for all
I\subseteq[n]
,
|I|\leq\epsilon^{\prime}(n-k)
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{I}=I]\leq\mathbb{P}[\lVert R_{i}V\rVert_{2}\leq\theta\hskip 5.69054pt\forall i\in I^{c}]\leq\prod_{i\in I^{c}}\mathbb{P}[\lVert V^{T}(R_{i})^{T}\rVert_{2}\leq\theta]\leq(1-p)^{k\cdot(1-\epsilon^{\prime})(n-k)}.

Therefore, by the union bound over the choice of
I
, we have

\mathbb{P}[\lVert M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)V\rVert_{\operatorname{HS}}\leq\theta\sqrt{\epsilon^{\prime}(n-k)}]\leq n\cdot\binom{n-k}{\epsilon^{\prime}(n-k)}(1-p)^{k\cdot(1-\epsilon^{\prime})(n-k)}.

Therefore taking
c_{\ref{lem:fixed-vectors}}=\theta\sqrt{\epsilon^{\prime}}/2
for sufficiently small
\epsilon^{\prime}=\epsilon^{\prime}(p,k,\epsilon)
and taking
n
sufficiently large gives the desired conclusion.
∎

Combining this with a standard epsilon-net argument allows us to prove
Proposition

2.2
.

Proof of
Proposition

2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}(\delta,\rho)
denote the event that for every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
such that

(M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi))x=0,

we have
x\in\operatorname{Comp}(\delta,\rho)
. By the union bound,

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{C}(\delta,\rho)]\leq\binom{n}{k}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}(\delta,\rho)],

so that (after rescaling
\epsilon
) it suffices to show that

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}(\delta,\rho)]\leq(1-p+\epsilon)^{kn}.

Since the right-kernel of
M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)
has dimension at least
k
, it follows that on the event
\mathcal{E}(\delta,\rho)
, we can find
k
orthonormal vectors,
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
, such that
v_{i}\in\operatorname{Comp}(\delta,\rho)
and
(M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi))v_{i}=0
. Let
V
denote the
n\times k
matrix with columns
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
. Then,

(2.1)

M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)V=0.

Let
H\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the subspace of vectors
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
such that
x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}=0
.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{K}
be the event that the operator norm of
(M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi))|_{H}
(i.e., the linear operator from
H
to
\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
which coincides with
M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)
on its domain) is at most
K\sqrt{n}
. Since
\xi
is sub-Gaussian, it follows from standard estimates (cf.
[
17
, Lemma 3.4]
) that

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{K}]\geq 1-\exp(-c_{\xi}K^{2}n).

In particular, by choosing
K
to be of order
\sqrt{k}
, we can ensure that this probability is at least
1-4^{-kn}
. Then, by the union bound, it suffices to show that

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}(\delta,\rho)\cap\mathcal{E}_{K}]\leq(1-p+\epsilon)^{kn}.

We will show this by combining
Lemma

2.6
with a standard epsilon-net argument. Let
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
be a sufficiently small parameter to be chosen later. A standard volumetric net, ‘densified’ in the all-ones direction (to account for the fact that
M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)((1,\dots,1)^{T}/\sqrt{n})=\Theta(n)
) shows (cf.
[
17
, Proposition 3.6]
) that there exists a (deterministic) net
\mathcal{N}
of
\operatorname{Comp}(\delta,\rho)
, of size at most
(C/\epsilon^{\prime})^{\delta n}
(where
C
is allowed to depend on
K
), such that for any
x\in\operatorname{Comp}(\delta,\rho)
, there exists
y\in\mathcal{N}
such that on the event
\mathcal{E}_{K}
,

\|M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)(x-y)\|_{2}\leq(\epsilon^{\prime}+\rho)K\sqrt{n}.

Let
\mathcal{V}
denote the set of all
n\times k
matrices whose columns are orthonormal vectors in
\operatorname{Comp}(\delta,\rho)
. Then, by considering the
k
-fold product of
\mathcal{N}
, we obtain a net of
\mathcal{V}
. Using the standard trick of replacing points in this net by the closest point in
\mathcal{V}
(see e.g.
[
14
, Lemma 4.2]
), we can obtain a (deterministic) net
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
of size
|\mathcal{M}|\leq(C/\epsilon^{\prime})^{\delta kn}
such that for every
V\in\mathcal{V}
, there exists
V^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}
such that, on the event
\mathcal{E}_{K}
,

\|M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)(V-V^{\prime})\|_{\operatorname{HS}}\leq 2(\epsilon^{\prime}+\rho)K\sqrt{kn}.

Therefore, by
Equation

2.1
and the union bound, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}(\delta,\rho)\cap\mathcal{E}_{K}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\{\exists V\in\mathcal{V}:M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)V=0\}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{V^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}}\mathbb{P}[\|M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)V^{\prime}\|_{\operatorname{HS}}\leq 2(\epsilon^{\prime}+\rho)K\sqrt{kn}]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{C}{\epsilon^{\prime}}\right)^{\delta kn}\cdot(1-p+\epsilon/2)^{kn}

\displaystyle\leq(1-p+\epsilon)^{kn},

where the penultimate line follows from
Lemma

2.6
by taking
\rho=\epsilon^{\prime}
,
n
sufficiently large, and
\epsilon^{\prime}
sufficiently small depending on
k,p,\epsilon
, and the last line follows by taking
n
sufficiently large and
\delta
sufficiently small depending on
\epsilon^{\prime},\epsilon
. ∎

2.4.
Incompressible vectors

Recall the notion of the threshold of a vector (
Definition

2.1
). The following is the structure theorem/dichotomy used in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
. The case
k=1
is implicit in
[
17
]
, although the statement given here is closer to the one in work of the authors
[
9
, Proposition 3.7]
.

Proposition 2.7

(Modification of
[
9
, Proposition 3.7]
)
.

Let
\delta,\rho,\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
k\geq 1
. There exist
L_{\ref{prop:structure}}=L_{\ref{prop:structure}}(k,\delta,\rho,p,\epsilon)
and
n_{\ref{prop:structure}}=n_{\ref{prop:structure}}(k,\delta,\rho,p,\epsilon)
such that for all
n\geq n_{\ref{prop:structure}}
, with probability at least
1-4^{-kn}
, exactly one of the following holds.

•

Every unit vector
v
in the right-kernel of
M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)
is in
\operatorname{Comp}(\delta,\rho)
, or

•

there is a unit vector
v
in the right-kernel of
M_{(n-k)\times n}(\xi)
with
\mathcal{T}_{p}(v,L_{\ref{prop:structure}})\leq(1-p+\epsilon)^{n}
.

Remark
.

The proof of
Proposition

2.7
follows the usual format of taking a dyadic decomposition of possible values for the threshold function, performing randomized rounding on potential kernel vectors at the correct scale, and then tensorizing the resulting small ball probabilities. The difference in the statement above compared to the versions in
[
17
,
8
,
9
]
is that we are missing
k
rows as opposed to
1
row. Additionally, we are considering the independent threshold model rather than the “multislice” models considered in
[
9
]
, which actually simplifies the proof. Further, since
k
is sufficiently small compared to
n
, the difference in row counts has essentially no effect on the union bound computation.

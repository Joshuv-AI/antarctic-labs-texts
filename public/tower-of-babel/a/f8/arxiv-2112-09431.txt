1.
Introduction

The aim of this article is to answer the following question:

Question 1.1
.

Is there a higher dimensional analog of the construction of expander graphs using Cayley graphs of discrete groups having Property (T)?

The construction we are referring to is originally due to Margulis, who used relative Property (T) of the pair
(\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{Z}^{2})

[
22
]
, later generalized by Alon and Milman
[
1
]
. They considered a discrete group
\Gamma
with Property (T) whose Cayley graph is
X
and showed that the family of quotients graphs
\{\Lambda_{i}\backslash X\}_{i}
, where
\{\Lambda_{i}\}_{i}
are finite index subgroups of
\Gamma
, are expanders. We replace expander graphs by
higher dimensional spectral expanders
. Following
[
21
, Definition 2.3]
, they are a family of
d
-dimensional bounded degree (each vertex is contained in a bounded number of cells) finite simplicial complexes, with number of vertices going to infinity, such that the
upper Laplacians
, acting on the
i^{\text{th}}
simplicial cochain groups with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
,
0<i<d-1
, have
spectral gaps
bounded below by a positive constant. (See §
3
for definition of upper Laplacian and spectral gap.) The higher analog of having Property (T), equivalently, being
1
-Kazhdan
, is being
n
-Kazhdan
. Following
[
11
]
, a group
\Gamma
is said to be
n
-Kazhdan
(resp.
strongly

-Kazhdan
) if
H^{n}(\Gamma,\pi)=0
(resp.
H^{i}(\Gamma,\pi)=0,1\leq i\leq n
), for any unitary representation
\pi
of
\Gamma
. To find a higher analog of a Cayley graph, one notes that the Cayley graph of a group
\Gamma
is the
1
skeleton of the universal cover of a
K(\Gamma,1)
CW complex having exactly one vertex. The natural
n
dimensional analog is the
n
-skeleton of the universal cover of a
K(\Gamma,1)
complex. To make sure that we get a family of finite complexes we assume that
\Gamma
is of
type
F_{n}
, that is, there exists a
K(\Gamma,1)
CW complex, whose
n
skeleton is finite. Note that when
n=1
, this comes for free, since any discrete group with Property (T) is finitely generated, that is, of type
F_{1}
. Further, in the
n=1
case, by choosing finite index subgroups that do not contain the set of generators, we can ensure that the family of graphs have no loops, that is, they are 1 dimensional simplicial complexes. For higher dimensions, we use a classical result of Whitehead which says that any finite CW complex is homotopy equivalent to a finite simplicial complex, see
[
31
, Theorem 13]
or
[
17
, Theorem 2C.5]
. Thus we choose a
K(\Gamma,1)
complex which is a
simplicial
complex with finite
n
-skeleton. We answer Qn.
1.1
. in the following manner.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group of type
F_{n}
and let

be the
n

K(\Gamma,1)
simplicial complex with finite
n

be a family of finite index subgroups of
\Gamma

The assumption of

-Kazhdan is superfluous in the above statement. All we need is
H^{i}(\Gamma,\hat{\oplus}_{i}L^{2}(\Lambda_{i}\backslash\Gamma))=0,1\leq i\leq n

\tau
) and the family of corresponding Cayley graphs being expanders
[

, see Corollary

is at most countable. Hence

Corollary 1.3

Let
\Gamma
be a torsion free cocompact lattice in the isometry group of a non-Hermitian Riemannian symmetric space of non-compact type and of rank

Let us explain why these examples are new, but related, both in source and in spirit of proof, to the most well know examples of higher spectral expanders - cocompact lattices in simple algebraic groups
G
over non-archimedean local fields. These groups act on contractible simplicial complexes
X
, known as the Bruhat-Tits buildings. On the archimedean side, lattices in semisimple linear Lie groups
G
act on contractible manifolds

, known as symmetric spaces. Thus

. Working on the archimedean side, for each

[
23

gave a local criterion, involving the curvature tensor, for

to be

[
15
]
gave an analogous local criterion, in the non-archimedean setting, for vanishing of

, which proved Serre’s conjecture that

for
1\leq p<\text{rk}(G)
, provided the residue field is large enough. As noted by Borel
[
9

, the

in fact produces a spectral gap for the

upper Laplacian for any finite simplicial complex given such a gap for Laplacians acting on vertices of links of

Thus, the Garland method shows that quotients of Bruhat Tits building, by cocompact lattices in groups over fields with large residue fields, are spectral expanders. Garland’s method was generalized by Zuk
[

]
, Pansu
[

and Ballman-Światkowski
[

[

]

[

]
,
[

]

[

]
. Examples of 2-Kazhdan groups obtained by the same method was used by de Chiffre, Glebsky, Lubotzky and Thom
[

to produce examples of groups that are not Frobenius approximated. On the other hand Matsushima’s result was generalized to twisted coefficients by Mok
[

, which implies Corollary

We note here that Garland’s local point of view, has been adopted as definition of higher spectral expander by Oppenheim
[

and exmaples of such expanders have been constructed by Kaufman and Oppenheim
[

]
,
[

Theorem 1.4

Let

2.
Reduced cohomology

2.1.
Welldefinedness

Reduced 1-cohomology of groups has been studied in the context of Property (T), most notably by Shalom
[
29
]
. More generally, reduced group cohomology of any degree has been studied by Blanc
[
8
]
, Austin
[
2
]
and Bader and Nowak
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
. The cochain complexes that are used to define reduced group cohomology are either induced by the bar resolution
[
10
, Ch. I, §5]
or the resolution obtained from the simplicial chain complex of the universal cover of a
K(\Gamma,1)
simplicial complex. The fact that the different chain complexes, all with appropriate product topology on them, give the same reduced cohomology is well known to experts. But for completeness, we begin with a proof of this fact in an fairly general setting.

Let
C:=\{C^{l},d_{l}\}_{l}
be a cochain complex of topological vector spaces over a topological field
k
such that each
d_{l}
is a continuous map. Since
d_{l}
is continuous,
\ker d_{l}
is closed. Therefore
\overline{\text{im }d_{l-1}}\subset\ker d_{l}
and the quotient
\overline{H^{l}}(C):=\ker d_{l}/\overline{\text{im }d_{l-1}}
is the
l^{\emph{th}}
reduced cohomology
of
C
. Let
C^{\prime}:=\{C^{\prime l},d^{\prime}_{l}\}
be another such cochain complex. If
f
is a
continuous
chain homomorphism from
C
to
C^{\prime}
, then it induces a map
\overline{f}_{*}:\overline{H^{*}}(C)\to\overline{H^{*}}(C^{\prime})
. Note that if
f
is null homotopic in the usual sense then
\overline{f}_{*}
is
0
. Therefore, if
f:C\to C^{\prime}
and
g:C^{\prime}\to C
are continuous chain maps such that
fg-I
and
gf-I
are null homotopic in the usual sense, then
\overline{H^{*}}(C)\cong\overline{H^{*}}(C^{\prime})
as topological vector spaces.

Now suppose
\Gamma
is a discrete group and
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
is its group algebra over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\{\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]\}_{l}
be a free resolution of
\mathbb{C}
, where
\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]
is the free module over
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
with generating set
Y^{l}
. Let
(\pi,V_{\pi})
be a unitary representation of
\Gamma
. Consider the cochain complex of topological vectors spaces
\{\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}(\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}],V_{\pi})\}_{l}
, where the topology on
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}(\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}],V_{\pi})
is induced from the isomorphism

(1)

\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}(\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}],V_{\pi})\cong V_{\pi}^{Y^{l}},

and the product topology on
V_{\pi}^{Y^{l}}
. The reduced cohomology of this cochain complex is called the
reduced cohomology of
\Gamma
with coefficients in
\pi
and is denoted by
\overline{H^{*}}(\Gamma,\pi)
. The fact that the coboundary maps are continuous follows from Lemma
2.1
below. Moreover,
\overline{H^{*}}(\Gamma,\pi)
is independent of the choice of free resolution since the chain homotopies induced by the chain homotopy equivalences between different free resolutions are continuous, again by Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y]\to\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Z]
be a
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
equivariant map. Let
(\pi,V_{\pi})
be a unitary representation of
\Gamma
.
Then
f^{*}:\emph{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}(\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Z],V_{\pi})\to\emph{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}(\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y],V_{\pi})
, with topologies as in
(
1
)
, is continuous.

Proof.

Let
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}\subset\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}(\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Z],V_{\pi})
be a net such that
\phi_{\alpha}\to\phi
. We want to show that
f^{*}\phi_{\alpha}\to f^{*}\phi
. Convergence with respect to product topology is point wise convergence. Therefore we have to show
f^{*}\phi_{\alpha}(y)\to f^{*}\phi(y)
in
V_{\pi}
, for all
y\in Y
. Let
f(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{i}z_{i}
, where
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C},\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma
and

. Then the following computation implies the convergence.

\displaystyle\|(f^{*}\phi_{\alpha}-f^{*}\phi)(y)\|=\|(\phi_{\alpha}-\phi)(f(y))\|=\|(\phi_{\alpha}-\phi)(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{i}z_{i})\|

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|\|\pi(\gamma_{i})(\phi_{\alpha}-\phi)(z_{i})\|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|\|(\phi_{\alpha}-\phi)(z_{i})\|.\qed

2.2.
Hodge theory

Fix a natural number
n
and suppose we have a free resolution
\{\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]\}_{l}
, where
Y^{l}
is finite for all
l\leq n
. This happens in particular when
\Gamma
is of type
F_{n}
and
Y^{l}
is the set of
l
simplices of a
K(\Gamma,1)
simplicial complex
Y
with finite
n+1
skeleton. Then we can identify each element of
Y^{l}
with a fixed lift of it in the universal cover
X
of
Y
. Then the elements of
Y^{l}
can be thought of as orbit representatives of the free
\Gamma
action on the set
X^{l}
of
l
simplices in
X
. Hence the simplicial chain complex of
X
can be identified with
\{\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]\}_{l}
. Since
Y^{l}
is a finite set, the RHS of (
1
) is a direct sum of finitely many Hilbert spaces and hence is a Hilbert space. Thus for
l\leq n
we have a cochain complex of Hilbert spaces and bounded maps. This is a familiar set up in
L^{2}
-cohomology. The abstract form in which we need this is given in
[
4
, §3]
. We will summarize some results from
[
4
, §3]
that we need.

We say a cochain complex
C=\{C^{l},d_{l}\}_{l}
is a
Hilbert cochain complex
if
C^{l}
is a Hilbert space and
d_{l}
is a bounded operator, for each
l
. In this case we can define the adjoint of
d_{l}
, which we denote by
\partial_{l+1}
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{l}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{l+1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{l-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{l}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial_{l}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial_{l+1}}

The
upper, lower and full Laplacian for
C
in degree

are defined as

\Delta^{+}_{l}:=\partial_{l+1}d_{l},~\Delta^{-}_{l}:=d_{l-1}\partial_{l},~\Delta_{l}:=\Delta^{+}_{l}+\Delta^{-}_{l},

respectively. The space
C^{l}
can be written as an orthogonal direct sum of the following closed subspaces.

\displaystyle C^{l}_{0}:=\ker d_{l}\cap\ker\partial_{l},~C^{l}_{-}

\displaystyle:=\overline{\text{im }d_{l-1}},~C^{l}_{+}:=\overline{\text{im }\partial_{l+1}}.

\displaystyle C^{l}=C^{l}_{-}

\displaystyle\oplus C^{l}_{0}\oplus C^{l}_{+}.

The kernel of
d_{l}
is
C^{l}_{0}\oplus C^{l}_{-}
and that of
\partial_{l}

C^{l}_{+}\oplus C^{l}_{0}
. An immediate implication is
\overline{H^{l}}(C)\cong C^{l}_{0}
. The operators
d_{l}
and
\partial_{l}

\displaystyle d_{l}:C^{l}\twoheadrightarrow C^{l}_{+}\xrightarrow{\bar{d}_{l}}C^{l+1}_{-}\hookrightarrow C^{l+1},

\displaystyle\partial_{l}:C^{l}\twoheadrightarrow C^{l}_{-}\xrightarrow{\bar{\partial}_{l}}C^{l-1}_{+}\hookrightarrow C^{l-1},

where first maps in both compositions are orthogonal projections, the last maps are inclusions and the middle maps

are injective with dense images. Define

(2)

\bar{\Delta}^{+}_{l}:=\bar{\partial}_{l+1}\bar{d}_{l}:C^{l}_{+}\to C^{l}_{+},~\bar{\Delta}^{-}_{l}:=\bar{d}_{l-1}\bar{\partial}_{l}:C^{l}_{-}\to C^{l}_{-}.

The following result is crucial for us, we supply a proof for the reader’s convenience.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
4
, Proposition 16]
Let
C
be a Hilbert cochain complex. The following are equivalent.
(1)

H^{l+1}(C)
is reduced.
(2)

\bar{\Delta}^{+}_{l}
is invertible.
(3)

\bar{\Delta}^{-}_{l+1}
is invertible.

Proof.

We will show (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2). The proof of (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) is similar.
H^{l+1}(C)
is reduced if and only if
d_{l}
is surjective onto
\overline{\text{im }d_{l}}=C^{l+1}_{-}
, if and only if
\bar{d}_{l}
is an isomorphism. Hence
\bar{\partial}_{l+1}
, which is adjoint of
\bar{d}_{l}
, is an isomorphism. Therefore
\bar{\Delta}^{+}_{l}=\bar{\partial}_{l+1}\bar{d}_{l}
is invertible. On the other hand, suppose
\bar{\Delta}^{+}_{l}
is invertible. Then
\bar{\partial}_{l+1}
is surjective. Since it is already injective, therefore it is an isomorphism. Hence its adjoint
\bar{d}_{l}
is also an isomorphism.
∎

2.3.
Hilbert direct sum

Recall that the
Hilbert direct sum

\hat{\oplus}_{i}H_{i}
is the completion of the direct sum
\oplus_{i}H_{i}
of a family
\{H_{i}\}_{i}
of Hilbert spaces. If
T_{i}
are bounded operators on
H_{i}
such that
\sup_{i}\|T_{i}\|
is finite, then the induced map on the dense subspace
\oplus_{i}H_{i}
of
\hat{\oplus}_{i}H_{i}
extends to a unique bounded operator
\oplus_{i}T_{i}
on
\hat{\oplus}_{i}H_{i}
, called the
direct sum of the operators
T_{i}
.

We say a family of Hilbert cochain complexes
\{C_{i}=\{C^{l}_{i},(d_{i})_{l}\}_{l}\}_{i}
has
uniformly bounded coboundaries
if
\sup_{i}\|(d_{i})_{l}\|
is finite for each
l
. In this case, the Hilbert cochain complex obtained by taking the Hilbert direct sum of the cochain spaces
C^{l}_{i}
and the direct sum of the coboundary operators
(d_{i})_{l}
will be called the
Hilbert direct sum of
C_{i}
and will be denoted by
\hat{\oplus}C_{i}
.

We need the following fact about spectrum of direct sum of self adjoint operators.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
30
, Theorem 2.23]

Let
\{H_{i}\}_{i}
be a family of Hilbert spaces. Let
T_{i}
be a self adjoint operator on
H_{i}
, for all
i
, such that
\sup_{i}\|T_{i}\|
is finite. Then

\sigma(\oplus_{i}T_{i})=\overline{\cup_{i}\sigma(T_{i})}.

Let
T
be a positive operator on a Hilbert space. We say
T
has an
essential gap
if there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\sigma(T)\subset\{0\}\cup[\epsilon,\infty)

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\{C_{i}\}_{i}
be a family of Hilbert cochain complexes with uniformly bounded coboundaries. For any
l
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

H^{l+1}(\hat{\oplus}_{i}C_{i})
is reduced.

(2)

(\Delta^{-}_{i})_{l+1}
, for all
i
, admit a uniform essential gap.

(3)

(\Delta^{+}_{i})_{l}
, for all
i
, admit a uniform essential gap.

Proof.

We will show (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2). The argument for (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) is entirely analogous. Let
\Delta^{-}_{l+1}
denote the lower Laplacians in degree
l+1
of the cochain complex
\hat{\oplus}_{i}C_{i}
. Note that
\sigma(\Delta^{-}_{l+1})\subset\{0\}\cup\sigma(\bar{\Delta}^{-}_{l+1})
. Since, by definition, any operator is invertible if and only if its spectrum does not contain
0
, therefore
\bar{\Delta}^{-}_{l+1}
is invertible if and only if
\Delta^{-}_{l+1}
has an essential gap. By Theorem
2.3
, this is equivalent to a uniform essential gap for
(\Delta_{i}^{-})_{l+1}
for all
i
. On the other hand, by Proposition
2.2
,
\bar{\Delta}^{-}_{l+1}
is invertible if and only if
H^{l+1}(\hat{\oplus}_{i}C_{i})
is reduced. This completes the proof.
∎

We will apply the above result to group cohomology. First we need a lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\{\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]\}_{l\leq n}
be a partial free resolution of

by
\mathbb{C}\Gamma

such that for any unitary representation

of

the

of the cochain complex

satisfies
\|d^{\pi}_{l}\|\leq M_{l}

Proof.

Since
Y^{l}
is finite,
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}(\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}],V_{\pi})\cong\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}V_{\pi}
, where
\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]^{*}:=\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}(\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}],\mathbb{C}\Gamma)
is the dual right
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
module. Let
\delta_{j}
denote the
j^{\text{th}}
boundary map for the chain complex
\{\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]\}_{l\leq n}
. Let
\delta_{j}^{*}
denote the dual
\mathbb{C}\Gamma

d^{\pi}_{l}=\delta_{l+1}^{*}\otimes 1_{\pi}
, where

is the identity map on

(cf. Lemma

). Let

be the basis of
\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]^{*}
dual to the basis
Y^{l}
of
\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]
. For each

,
\delta_{l+1}^{*}(f^{l}_{j})=\sum_{i\in I_{l+1}}f^{l+1}_{i}a_{ij}
, for some

. Any element of
\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}V_{\pi}
is of the form
\sum_{j}f^{l}_{j}\otimes v_{j}
, where

. In fact

\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}V_{\pi}\cong V_{\pi}^{Y^{l}}

\|\sum_{j}f^{l}_{j}\otimes v_{j}\|^{2}=\sum_{j}\|v_{j}\|_{\pi}^{2}
. We have

\displaystyle\|d^{\pi}_{l}(\sum_{j}f^{l}_{j}\otimes v_{j})\|^{2}=\|(\sum_{j}\delta_{l+1}^{*}(f^{l}_{j})\otimes v_{j})\|^{2}=\|\sum_{i,j}f^{l+1}_{i}a_{ij}\otimes v_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\|\sum_{i}f^{l+1}_{i}\otimes\sum_{j}\pi(a_{ij})v_{j}\|^{2}=\sum_{i}\|\sum_{j}\pi(a_{ij})v_{j}\|_{\pi}^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j}\|\pi(a_{ij})\|^{2}\|v_{j}\|_{\pi}^{2}.

Any

is of the form

, where
\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\gamma_{k}\in\Gamma
. Then

, which is independent of
\pi
. Thus there exists

such that

for any

.
∎

A version of Shapiro’s Lemma

Given a simplicial complex

. Recall that the

\mathbb{C}[Y^{l}]

with a certain boundary map. See

for details. Dualizing the chain complex we get the

of

with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}

The

of upper Laplacian

orthogonal to

Lemma 3.3

Let

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\Gamma\emph{-\textbf{mod}}\to\emph{\textbf{Vect}}_{\mathbb{C}}\hskip 42.67912pt\text{and}\hskip 28.45274pt\mathbb{C}\Gamma\emph{{-}\textbf{mod}}\to\emph{\textbf{Vect}}_{\mathbb{C}}

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
V
be a cyclic right
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
module with cyclic vector
v
. Let
I
be the right ideal annihilating

. The two functors below are naturally isomorphic.

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\Gamma\emph{-\textbf{mod}}\to\emph{\textbf{Vect}}_{\mathbb{C}}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{and}\hskip 28.45274pt\mathbb{C}\Gamma\emph{{-}\textbf{mod}}\to\emph{\textbf{Vect}}_{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle M\mapsto V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}M\hskip 91.04872ptM\mapsto M/IM

Proof.

Let
\pi:M\to M/IM
be the quotient map. The map

\displaystyle V\times M\to M/IM,

\displaystyle(va,m)\to\pi(am),

is well defined since if we replace
va
by

, where
b\in I
, then
\pi((a+b)m)=\pi(am)+\pi(bm)=\pi(am)
. It is also
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
bilinear and hence induces a map
V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}M\to M/IM
. We will show that this map is a natural transformation. Let
\phi:M_{1}\to M_{2}
be a morphism in
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
-
mod
. The image
\phi_{I}
of
\phi
under the first functor is defined by the commutativity of the following diagram:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{1}/IM_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{2}/IM_{2}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{I}}

We have to show the following diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}M_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{1}/IM_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V\otimes_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}M_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{2}/IM.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1\otimes\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{I}}

The composition of the top horizontal and right vertical maps is given by

va\otimes m\mapsto\pi_{1}(am)\mapsto\phi_{I}(\pi_{1}(am))=\pi_{2}(\phi(am)),

va\otimes m\mapsto va\otimes\phi(m)\mapsto\pi_{2}(a\phi(m))=\pi_{2}(\phi(am)).

\pi(m)\mapsto v\otimes m\mapsto\pi(m).\qed

\sum_{k}f^{l}_{k}\otimes w_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]^{*}\otimes V_{\pi}
and
\sum_{i}f^{l-1}_{i}\otimes v_{i}\in\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l-1}]\otimes V_{\pi}
. We have to show

\langle\sum_{i,j}f^{l}_{j}a_{ij}\otimes v_{i},\sum_{k}f^{l}_{k}\otimes w_{k}\rangle=\langle\sum_{i}f^{l-1}_{i}\otimes v_{i},\sum_{k,j}f^{l-1}_{j}\iota(a_{jk})\otimes w_{k}\rangle.

The LHS simplifies as,

\displaystyle\langle\sum_{i,j}f^{l}_{j}\otimes\rho(a_{ij})v_{i},\sum_{k}f^{l}_{k}\otimes w_{k}\rangle=\sum_{i,j}\langle f^{l}_{j}\otimes\rho(a_{ij})v_{i},\sum_{k}f^{l}_{k}\otimes w_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\langle f^{l}_{i}\otimes\rho(a_{ij})v_{i},f^{l}_{j}\otimes w_{j}\rangle=\sum_{i,j}\langle\rho(a_{ij})v_{i},w_{j}\rangle_{\rho},

while the RHS simplifies as

\displaystyle\langle\sum_{i}f^{l-1}_{i}\otimes v_{i},\sum_{k,j}f^{l-1}_{j}\otimes\rho(\iota(a_{jk}))w_{k}\rangle=\sum_{k,j}\langle\sum_{i}f^{l-1}_{i}\otimes v_{i},f^{l-1}_{j}\otimes\rho(\iota(a_{jk}))w_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,j}\langle f^{l-1}_{j}\otimes v_{j},f^{l-1}_{j}\otimes\rho(\iota(a_{jk}))w_{k}\rangle=\sum_{k,j}\langle v_{j},\rho(\iota(a_{jk}))w_{k}\rangle_{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,j}\langle v_{j},\rho(a_{jk})^{*}w_{k}\rangle_{\rho}=\sum_{k,j}\langle\rho(a_{jk})v_{j},w_{k}\rangle_{\rho}.\qed

Theorem 4.4
.

[
30
, Theorem 2.19]
Let
T
be a bounded self adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
H
. Then

\inf\sigma(T)=\inf_{v\neq 0}\frac{\langle Tv,v\rangle}{\langle v,v\rangle}.

The quantity
\langle Tv,v\rangle/\langle v,v\rangle
is called the
Rayleigh quotient of
v
for
T
.

The key observation for the proof of Theorem
1.4
is the following result.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group of type
F_{n+1}
. For all
l\leq n
, the following map is continuous at all
\pi
satisfying
\inf\sigma((\Delta_{\pi})_{l})=0
.

\displaystyle\widehat{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\to[0,\infty)

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle\mapsto\inf\sigma((\Delta_{\pi})_{l}).

Proof.

Let us find the expression for the Rayleigh quotient of a generic element
\sum_{i}f_{i}^{l}\otimes v_{i}\in\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]^{*}\otimes V_{\pi}
for
(\Delta_{\pi})_{l}
. By Lemma
4.3
,
(\Delta_{\pi})_{l}
is of the form
\Phi\otimes 1
, where
\Phi
is given by a matrix
D
, whose elements we denote by
d_{ij},(i,j)\in I_{l}\times I_{l}
. Then the numerator of the Rayleigh quotient is

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle(\Delta_{\pi})_{l}(\sum_{i}f^{l}_{i}\otimes v_{i}),\sum_{k}f^{l}_{k}\otimes v_{k}\rangle=\langle\sum_{i,j}f_{j}\otimes\pi(d_{ij})v_{i},\sum_{k}f^{l}_{k}\otimes v_{k}\rangle\\
=~&\sum_{i,j}\langle f_{j}\otimes\pi(d_{ij})v_{i},\sum_{k}f^{l}_{k}\otimes v_{k}\rangle=\sum_{i,j}\langle f_{j}\otimes\pi(d_{ij})v_{i},f^{l}_{j}\otimes v_{j}\rangle=\sum_{i,j}\langle\pi(d_{ij})v_{i},v_{j}\rangle_{\pi},\end{split}

while the denominator is
\sum_{i}\|v_{i}\|^{2}
. For a fixed
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the map
(v,w)\mapsto\langle\pi(\gamma)v,w\rangle_{\pi}
is a sesquilinear form on
V_{\pi}
. Hence by the polarization identity for sesquilinear forms (see for example
[
16
, Proposition A.59]
), any matrix coefficient can be written as a linear combination of diagonal matrix coefficients, that is, functions of positive type. Thus the final expression in (
8
) can be written as a linear combination of functions of positive type associated to
\pi
evaluated at the finite number of elements in
\Gamma
which appear in
d_{ij}
, for all
i,j
. The terms
\|v_{i}\|^{2}
in the denominator are also functions of positive type evaluated at the identity. Thus the Rayleigh quotient is of the form
F(\phi_{1}(\gamma_{1}),\cdots,\phi_{m}(\gamma_{m}))
, where
F
is a rational function,
\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{m}
are functions of positive type and
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{m}\in\Gamma
. Note that as we vary over different representations
\pi
and different elements
\sum_{i}f_{i}^{l}\otimes v_{i}
in
\mathbb{C}\Gamma[Y^{l}]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}\Gamma}V_{\pi}
, the functions
\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{m}
vary, but not the elements
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{m}
of
\Gamma
. Let
K:=\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{m}\}
.

To show continuity we will show that if
\{\pi_{\alpha}\}
is a net in
\widehat{\Gamma}
such that
\pi_{\alpha}\to\pi
, with
\inf\sigma((\Delta_{\pi})_{l})=0
, then
\inf\sigma((\Delta_{\pi_{\alpha}})_{l})\to 0
. Fix any
\epsilon>0
. By Theorem
4.4
and the above discussion, there exists functions of positive type
\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{m}
associated to
\pi
such that

(9)

F(\phi_{1}(\gamma_{1}),\cdots,\phi_{m}(\gamma_{m}))<\epsilon/2.

By continuity of
F
, there exists
\delta>0
such that whenever
x_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
satisfies
|x_{i}-\phi_{i}(\gamma_{i})|<\delta
for all
1\leq i\leq m
,
|F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})-F(\phi_{1}(\gamma_{1}),\cdots,\phi_{m}(\gamma_{m}))|<\epsilon/2
. Let
\tilde{W}(\pi,\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{m},K,\delta)\subset\widehat{\Gamma}
be the set of representations
\rho
such that there exists functions of positive type
\psi_{1},\cdots,\psi_{m}
associated to
\rho
with
|\phi_{i}(\gamma)-\psi_{i}(\gamma)|<\delta
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and
\gamma\in K
. Since
\pi
is irreducible, by
[
6
, Proposition F.2.4]
,
\tilde{W}(\pi,\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{m},K,\delta)
is a neighbourhood of
\pi
. Since
\pi_{\alpha}\to\pi
, there exists
\alpha_{0}
such that for all
\beta>\alpha_{0}
,
\pi_{\beta}\in\tilde{W}(\pi,\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{m},K,\delta)
. Then there exist functions of positive type
\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m}
associated to
\pi_{\beta}
such that

(10)

F(\xi_{1}(\gamma_{1}),\cdots,\xi_{m}(\gamma_{m}))-F(\phi_{1}(\gamma_{1}),\cdots,\phi_{m}(\gamma_{m}))<\epsilon/2.

Combining (
9
) and (
10
) we get
F(\xi_{1}(\gamma_{1}),\cdots,\xi_{m}(\gamma_{m}))<\epsilon
. Hence
\inf\sigma((\Delta_{\pi_{\beta}})_{l})<\epsilon
, for all
\beta>\alpha_{0}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

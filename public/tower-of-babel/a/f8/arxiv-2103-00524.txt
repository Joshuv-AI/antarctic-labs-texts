Abstract
. Let G ⊂ ℝ n G\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n} be an open convex set which is either bounded or contains a translation of a convex cone with nonempty interior. It is known that then, for every modulus ω \omega , every function on G G which is both semiconvex and semiconcave with modulus ω \omega is (globally) C 1 , ω C^{1,\omega} -smooth. We show that this result is optimal in the sense that the assumption on G G cannot be relaxed. We also present direct short proofs of the above mentioned result and of some its quantitative versions. Our results have immediate consequences concerning (i) a first-order quantitative converse Taylor theorem and (ii) the problem whether f ∈ C 1 , ω ​ ( G ) f\in C^{1,\omega}(G) whenever f f is continuous and smooth in a corresponding sense on all lines. We hope that these consequences are of an independent interest.

1.
Introduction

First we recall a classical reasult on smoothness of functions which are both semiconvex and semiconcave (i.e., semiconvex and semiconcave with linear modulus).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space,
G\subset X
an open convex set and
f
a function on
G
. We say that

f
is semiconvex, if there exists
C\geq 0
and a continuous convex function
g
on
G

such that

f(x)=g(x)-\frac{C}{2}\cdot\|x\|^{2},\ \ \ x\in G.

Then we say that
f
is semiconvex with constant
C
.
We say that
f
is semiconcave on
G
(with constant
C
) if
-f
is semiconvex on
G

(with constant
C
).

Let
X
,
G
and
f
be as in Definition
1.1
. Then it
is very easy to show (see Remark
2.2
) that

(1.1)

f^{\prime}\ \text{exists and is Lipschitz with constant}\ C\\
\Longrightarrow\ \ \ f\ \text{ is both semiconvex and semiconcave with constant}\ C.

Rather suprisingly, also the converse of (
1.1
) holds:

(1.2)

f\ \text{ is both semiconvex and semiconcave with constant}\ C\\
\Longrightarrow\ \ \ f^{\prime}\ \text{exists and is Lipschitz with constant}\ C.

Some bibliographic notes concerning this well-known interesting
nontrivial result are contained
in Section
3
, where also its short elementary proof is presented.

Consequently, we have that

(1.3)

f\ \text{ is both semiconvex and semiconcave with constant}\ C\\
\Longleftrightarrow\ \ \ f^{\prime}\ \text{exists and is Lipschitz with constant}\ C.

In particular,

(1.4)

f\ \text{is both semiconvex and semiconcave}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ f\in C^{1,1}(G).

In the present article
we study analogues of (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) for functions
which are both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave. We use the following
terminology.

Definition 1.2
.

We denote by
{\cal M}
the set of all
\omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
which are non-decreasing and satisfy
\lim_{t\to 0+}\omega(t)=0
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space,
G\subset X
an open convex set and
\omega\in{\cal M}
.
We say that a continuous
f:G\to{\mathbb{R}}
is semiconvex with modulus
\omega
(or
\omega
-semiconvex for short) if

f(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\leq\lambda f(x)+(1-\lambda)f(y)+\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x-y\|\omega(\|x-y\|)

for every
x,y\in G
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
. We say that
f:G\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
\omega
-semiconcave if
-f
is
\omega
-semiconvex.

Remark 1.4
.

If
X
is a Hilbert space, it is easy to show (cf.
[
CaSi
, p. 30]
)
that
f
is semiconvex on
G\subset X
with constant
C\geq 0
if and only if
f
is

\omega
-semiconvex with modulus
\omega(t)=\frac{C}{2}t,\ t\geq 0
.

Remark 1.5
.

The notion of
\omega
-semiconvex functions is very close to
Rolewicz’s notion of
strongly
\alpha(\cdot)
-paraconvex functions
(which has
in
[
Rol3
]
slightly different sense than in
[
Rol2
]
). Namely
(see e.g.
[
DZ1
, Remark 2.11]
)
if
\omega\in{\cal M}
and

\alpha(t)=t\omega(t),\ t\geq 0
, then (by the definition from
[
Rol3
]
),

f
is
\omega
-semiconvex
\Longrightarrow

f
is
strongly
\alpha(\cdot)
-paraconvex
\Longrightarrow

f
is
(2\omega)
-semiconvex.

Thus most results conserning
\omega
-semiconvex functions have obvious
consequences for strongly
\alpha(\cdot)
-paraconvex functions (and vice versa).
We will not formulate such consequences of our results.

Definition 1.6
.

If
G
is an open subset of a normed linear space and
\omega\in{\cal M}
, then we denote by
C^{1,\omega}_{*}(G)
the set of all Fréchet differentiable
f:G\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
such that
f^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous with modulus
\omega
.
We set
C^{1,\omega}(G):=\bigcup_{K>0}C^{1,K\omega}_{*}(G)
.

Note that so defined
C^{1,\omega}
-smoothness, which coincides with (global)

C^{1,1}
-smoothness in the case of a linear
\omega
, is common in the literature.
We will sometimes use also (stronger)
C_{*}^{1,\omega}
-smoothness (which
is somewhere, e.g. in
[
Jo
]
and
[
JZ
]
, called “
C^{1,\omega}
-smoothness”).

Let us first mention that a (weaker) version
of (
1.1
) for general moduli is also easy
(see Remark
2.2
). It asserts that, if
X
,
G
and
\omega
are as in Definition
1.3
, then

(1.5)

f\in C^{1,\omega}_{*}(G)\Longrightarrow\ \text{$f$ is both $\omega$-semiconvex and $\omega$-semiconcave on}\ G.

However (if
X
,
G
and
\omega
are as in Definition
1.3
)
the implication

(1.6)

f\ \text{is both $\omega$-semiconvex and $\omega$-semiconcave on $G$}\ \Longrightarrow\ f\in C^{1,\omega}(G)

does not hold in general.
The validity of (
1.6
) (or of its local versions) was studied
e.g. in
[
CaSi
, Theorem 3.3.7]
,
[
JTZ
]
and
[
Kr
]

(and essentially already in
[
Rol1
]
for
\omega(t)=t^{\alpha}
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
).
As far as we know, the strongest and the most general versions of
(
1.6
) follow from versions of Converse Taylor Theorem proved in
[
Jo
]
and
[
HJ
]
.
In particular (see
[
Kr
, Theorem 2.6 and Remark 2.7]
),

[
HJ
, Corollary 126]
easily implies the following result.

Theorem 1.7
.

[
Kr
]

Let
X
be a normed linear space and
G\subset X
an open convex set. Suppose that
G
is either bounded or has the property that there are
a\in X
,
r>0
and
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset X
,

\|u_{n}\|=n
, such that
B(a+u_{n},nr)\subset G
for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then, for each modulus
\omega\in{\cal M}
and for each function
f
on
G
,

f\ \text{is both $\omega$-semiconvex and $\omega$-semiconcave on}\ G\ \Longrightarrow\ f\in C^{1,\omega}(G).

Remark 1.8
.

Using (
1.5
), we easily see that the assertion of Theorem
1.7
implies the
following analogue of (
1.4
):

(1.7)

f\ \text{is both $(K\omega)$-semiconvex and $(K\omega)$-semiconcave}\ \text{for some}\ K>0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ f\in C^{1,\omega}(G).

For a local version of (
1.7
) see
[
JTZ
, Theorem 6.1]
. See also Proposition

6.8

for reformulations of this characterization of
C^{1,\omega}
-smoothness on some convex sets.

Let us note that an easy compactness argument shows that an unbounded open convex
G\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

satisfies the assumption of Theorem
1.7
if and only if it contains a translation of a convex cone with nonempty interior.

The main result of the present article is the following theorem which shows that, for
X={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, Theorem
1.7
is optimal in the sense that the assumption on

G
cannot be relaxed. It follows immediately from Proposition
5.13
(proved in Section
5
) via Remark
5.7
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
G\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) be an unbounded open convex set which does not contain a translation of a convex cone with nonempty interior. Then there exists a concave modulus
\omega\in{\cal M}

with
\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=\infty
and a function
f
which is both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave on
G
but
f\notin C^{1,\omega}(G)
.

Since
[
HJ
, Corollary 126]
is a rather general result (working with classes
C^{k,\omega}(G)

for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
), we find useful to
present (in Section
4
) direct
proofs (which partly follow arguments from
[
HJ
]
) of Theorem
1.7
and of some its quantative versions (sharper then those which directly follow from the proof of
[
HJ
, Corollary 126]
). Namely, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space and
\emptyset\neq G\subset X
an open convex set.
Let
\omega\in{\cal M}
and let
f
be a function on
G
which is both

\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave. Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

If
G
is bounded, then
f^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous with modulus

6e_{G}\omega
, where
e_{G}:=\frac{\operatorname{diam}G}{\sup\{r:B(a,r)\subset G\}}
.

(ii)

If
a\in X
,
r>0
and
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset X
with

\|u_{n}\|=n
are such that
B(a+u_{n},nr)\subset G
for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then

f^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous with modulus
12\,(1+1/r)\omega
.

Remark 1.11
.

(i)

The proof of
[
HJ
, Corollary 126]
gives
the multiplicative constants 15 and 30 instead of our constants
6 and 12.

(ii)

In the special cases when
G=X
or
\omega
is linear,
we can assert that
f
is
C_{*}^{1,4\omega}
-smooth
(see Corollary
4.3
and Remark
4.5
).

In the last Section
6
we show that
our results have immediate consequences (or reformulations) concerning

(i)

a first-order quantitative
converse Taylor theorem and

(ii)

the problem whether

f\in C^{1,\omega}(G)
whenever
f
is continuous and
C^{1,\omega}_{*}
-smooth on all lines.

We hope that these versions of our new results can be interesting also for readers which are not interested in
\omega
-semiconvex functions.

At the end of Section
6
, a natural open question is formulated.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Basic definitions

In the following,
X
will be always a real normed linear space and
X^{*}
its dual space.
The norm and the origin in any normed linear space will be denoted
by
\|\cdot\|
and
0
, respectively. We consider real Hilbert spaces which can be finite-dimensional. By the derivative
f^{\prime}
of a
function
f
defined on a subset of
X
we always mean the Fréchet derivative and the notion
of
C^{1}
-smoothness has the standard sense.
The symbol
B(x,r)
(
\overline{B}(x,r)
) denotes the open (closed) ball with centre
x
and radius
r
in
X
.
For
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the symbols

\overline{A}
and
\operatorname{span}A
denote the closure and the linear span of
A
, respectively.
By a cone in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we mean a set
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
\lambda x\in C
for each

x\in C
and
\lambda>0
. A ray in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a set of the form
\rho=\{a+tv:\ t\geq 0\}
,
where
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
0\neq v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. By the dimension
\dim(C)
of a convex set

C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we mean the dimension of the affine hull of
C
(see
[
Roc
, p. 12]
).

2.2.
\omega
-semiconvexity and
\omega
-semiconcavity

We will frequently use the obvious fact that if
f
is
\omega
-semiconvex (resp.
\omega
-semiconcave) on
G
and
c>0
, then
the function
cf
is
(c\omega)
-semiconvex (resp.
(c\omega)
-semiconcave).

Further, it follows easily from Definition
1.3
that
\omega
-semiconvexity is equivalent to “
\omega
-semiconvexity on all lines”. More precisely, the following statement holds.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space,
G\subset X
an open convex set,

\omega\in{\cal M}
, and let
f
be a continuous function on
G
. Then
the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

f
is
\omega
-semiconvex on
G
.

(ii)

For every
a\in G
and
v\in X
,
\|v\|=1
, the function

f_{a,v}:t\mapsto f(a+tv)
defined on the open interval

\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}:\ a+tv\in G\}
is
\omega
-semiconvex.

Remark 2.2
.

(i)

Using Lemma
2.1
, it is easy to see that, to prove implications (
1.1
) and (
1.5
),
it is sufficient to prove them for
X={\mathbb{R}}
. The validity of (
1.1
) for
X={\mathbb{R}}
is almost
obvious. Indeed, the assumption of (
1.1
) implies that
g(x):=f(x)+\frac{C}{2}x^{2}

is convex on
G
, since
g^{\prime}(x)=f^{\prime}(x)+Cx
is clearly nondecreasing on the interval
G
.
And the validity of (
1.5
) for
X={\mathbb{R}}
immediately follows
e.g. from
[
CaSi
, Proposition 2.1.2]
.

(ii)

Using Lemma
2.1
, Remark
1.4
and (
1.1
), we obtain that if
\omega
is linear,
then we can assert in (
1.5
) even that
f
is both
\tilde{\omega}
-semiconvex and

\tilde{\omega}
-semiconcave, where
\tilde{\omega}:=\frac{1}{2}\omega
. However, for general modulus it is not true even for
X={\mathbb{R}}
and
\tilde{\omega}:=C\omega
, where
C<1
is any absolute constant. Indeed, if
0<C<1
is given, we choose
p\in(1,2)
such that

C<1/p
and set
\omega(t):=t^{p-1}
,
t\geq 0
, and
f(x):=x^{p}/p,\ x\in(0,1)
.
Then
f^{\prime}(x)=\omega(x),\ x\in(0,1),
and consequently the concavity of
\omega
implies that
f
is
C_{*}^{1,\omega}
-smooth. However (as observed in
[
Kr2
, Remark 2.10 (ii)]
), it is not difficult to show that

f
is not
(C\omega)
-semiconcave.

We will need the following fact which is an easy consequence of (
1.5
)
and Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space,
G\subset X
an open convex set,
\omega\in{\cal M}
and let
f
be a Fréchet differentiable function on
G
.
Suppose that

(2.1)

|f^{\prime}(y)(y-x)-f^{\prime}(x)(y-x)|\leq\|y-x\|\omega(\|y-x\|),\quad x,y\in G.

Then
f
is both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave on
G
.

Proof.

We will first observe that if
f_{a,v}
is as any function as in Lemma
2.1
(ii), then

(2.2)

f_{a,v}
is
C^{1,\omega}_{*}
-smooth.

Indeed, for each
t_{1}\neq t_{2}
from
the domain of
f_{a,v}
we can apply (
2.1
) to
x:=a+t_{1}v
and
y:=a+t_{2}v
, and obtain

|(f_{a,v})^{\prime}(t_{2})-(f_{a,v})^{\prime}(t_{1})|=|f^{\prime}(a+t_{2}v)(v)-f^{\prime}(a+t_{1}v)(v)|\\
=\frac{1}{|t_{2}-t_{1}|}|f^{\prime}(a+t_{2}v)((t_{2}-t_{1})v)-f^{\prime}(a+t_{1}v)((t_{2}-t_{1})v)|\leq\omega(|t_{2}-t_{1}|),

Therefore each
f_{a,v}
is
\omega
-semiconvex by (
1.5
) and so

f
is
\omega
-semiconvex by Lemma
2.1
.
Using the same argument to
-f
, we obtain that

f
is also

\omega
-semiconcave.
∎

An other basic tool for us is the following essentially well-known result.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space,
G\subset X
an open convex set and
\omega\in{\cal M}
.
Let a function
f
be both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave on
G
. Then
f
is Fréchet differentiable on
G
and

(2.3)

|f(y)-f(x)-f^{\prime}(x)(y-x)|\leq\|y-x\|\,\omega(\|y-x\|),\ \ \ x,y\in G.

Proof.

A proof in the case
X={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
can be found in
[
CaSi
]
(see the proof of

[
CaSi
, (3.23), p. 60]
). The proof of the general case is factually contained
in the proof of
[
Kr
, Theorem 2.6]
.
For the convenience of the reader, we shortly repeat the argument from
[
Kr
]
.
It is based on
[
Rol2
, Theorem 3]
(cf. also
[
Rol3
, Proposition 2]
)
which easily implies (cf.
[
Kr
]
or
[
DZ1
, Corollary 4.5]
) that

\omega
-semiconvexity of
f
implies that for each
x\in G
there exists
\Phi_{x}\in X^{*}

such that

f(x+h)-f(x)-\Phi_{x}(h)\geq-\|h\|\omega(\|h\|)\ \ \text{whenever}\ \ \ x+h\in G.

Since
-f
is also
\omega
-semiconvex, for each
x\in G
there exists
\Psi_{x}\in X^{*}

such that

-f(x+h)+f(x)-\Psi_{x}(h)\geq-\|h\|\omega(\|h\|)\ \ \text{whenever}\ \ \ x+h\in G.

Adding these two inequalities, we easily obtain that
\Psi_{x}=-\Phi_{x}
and

|f(x+h)-f(x)-\Phi_{x}(h)|\leq\|h\|\omega(\|h\|)\ \ \text{whenever}\ \ \ x+h\in G,

which implies that
f^{\prime}(x)=\Phi_{x}
and so (
2.3
) holds.
∎

Lemma
2.4
and Remark
1.4
immediately imply the following well-known
result.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space,
G\subset X
an open convex set, and let
f
be a function which is both
semiconvex and semiconcave with constant
C>0
on
G
. Then
f
is Fréchet differentiable on
G
and

(2.4)

|f(y)-f(x)-f^{\prime}(x)(y-x)|\leq\frac{C}{2}\|y-x\|^{2},\ \ \ x,y\in G.

The following result (which clearly implies that the converse of Corollary
2.5
holds) is also essentially well-known, but we prefer to present a simple proof.

Lemma 2.6
.

If
X
,
G
,
\omega
are as in Lemma
2.4
and

f
satisfies (
2.3
), then
f
is both
(2\omega)
-semiconvex and
(2\omega)
-semiconcave. Moreover, if
\omega
is linear,
then
f
is both

\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave.

Proof.

For each
x\in G
,
y\in G
, we can apply (
2.3
) for
x:=y
and
y:=x
and obtain

|f(x)-f(y)-f^{\prime}(y)(x-y)|\leq\|x-y\|\,\omega(\|x-y\|).

Adding this inequality with (
2.3
) and using the triangle inequality, we obtain

|f^{\prime}(y)(y-x)-f^{\prime}(x)(y-x)|\leq 2\|y-x\|\omega(\|y-x\|).

Since we have proved that (
2.1
) implies (
2.2
), we obtain
that each function
f_{a,v}
is
C_{*}^{1,2\omega}
-smooth and consequently
both
(2\omega)
-semiconvex and
(2\omega)
-semiconcave by (
1.5
) (and even
both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave by Remark
2.2
(ii)
if
\omega
is linear). So the assertions of the lemma folow by Lemma
2.1
.
∎

2.3.
Recession cones

In the proof of our main result, we will use some properties of recession cones (called sometimes also
asymptotic cones) of unbounded convex sets
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. We refer to the exposition in
[
Roc
]
,
however we denote the recession cone of
A
by
{\rm rec}\,(A)
(and not
0^{+}A
as in
[
Roc
]
).

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\emptyset\neq A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a convex set. By the recession cone of
A
we mean
the set
{\rm rec}\,(A)
of all
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
x+\lambda y\in A
for each
x\in A
and
\lambda\geq 0
.

We will need the following two lemmas on open convex sets which easily follow from results proved
in
[
Roc
]
for closed convex sets.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\emptyset\neq G\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open convex set. Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

{\rm rec}\,(G)={\rm rec}\,(\overline{G})
.

(ii)

{\rm rec}\,(G)
is a closed convex cone in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
0\in{\rm rec}\,(G)
.

(iii)

{\rm rec}\,(G)=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\ \exists x\in G\ \forall\lambda\geq 0:\ x+\lambda y\in G\}
.

(iv)

If
G
is unbounded then
{\rm rec}\,(G)\setminus\{0\}\neq\emptyset
.

(v)

\dim({\rm rec}\,(G))=\dim(\operatorname{span}({\rm rec}\,(G)))
.

(vi)

\dim({\rm rec}\,(G))=n
if and only if
G
contains a translation of a cone
with nonempty interior.

Proof.

Property (i) follows from
[
Roc
, Corollary 8.3.1]
and (ii) from
[
Roc
, Theorem 8.2]

and (i). Theorem
[
Roc
, Theorem 8.3]
and (i) imply (iii). Property (iv) follows
from
[
Roc
, Theorem 8.4]
and (i). Property (v) is obvious since
0\in{\rm rec}\,(G)
.
Using (iii) and (v), it is easy to prove (vi).
∎

The following lemma is an easy consequence of important
[
Roc
, Theorem 9.1]
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
G\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an unbounded open convex set and let
L:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

be a linear surjection such that
L^{-1}(\{0\})\cap{\rm rec}\,(G)=\{0\}
.
Then
{\rm rec}\,(L(G))=L({\rm rec}\,(G))
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.9
(i) we have
{\rm rec}\,(G)={\rm rec}\,(\overline{G})
. So
L^{-1}(\{0\})\cap{\rm rec}\,(\overline{G})=\{0\}

and thus
[
Roc
, Theorem 9.1]
(applied to
C:=\overline{G}
) implies that
L(\overline{G})

is closed and
{\rm rec}\,(L(\overline{G}))=L({\rm rec}\,(\overline{G}))
. It is an easy well-known fact
that
L(G)
is an open convex set. Continuity of
L
implies
L(\overline{G})\subset\overline{L(G)}
and
so
L(\overline{G})=\overline{L(G)}
since
L(\overline{G})
is closed. Therefore

{\rm rec}\,(L(\overline{G}))={\rm rec}\,(L(G))
by Lemma
2.9
(i) and the equality
{\rm rec}\,(L(G))=L({\rm rec}\,(G))
follows.
∎

3.
Linear modulus

The present short section is devoted to result (
1.2
) which is rewritten below as Theorem
3.1
. We do not know
the origin of this interesting useful result which is well-known for a long time.

It is stated (for
G=X
) in
[
LL
, p. 265]
(1986), where it is written (without a reference) that the
case
G=X={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is well-known and it is shown how the general case easily follows from the finite-dimensional case.
The case
G=X
is completely proved in
[
HP
]
(1989).
Let us note that a weaker version of Theorem
3.1
which asserts that
f\in C^{1,1}(G)

only, is factually proved in
[
Rol1
]
(1979) (for
G=X
and Lipschitz
f
) and
very easily
follows
(for
G=X
) from
[
Mo
, Proposition 10.b]
(1965) which asserts that if
g
and
h
are continuous convex functions such that
g(x)+h(x)=(1/2)\|x\|^{2},\ x\in X
, then
g^{\prime}
is Lipschitz with constant
1
. The equality
G=X
is vital in
[
Mo
]
and
[
HP
]
.

Several proofs of Theorem
3.1
work with second derivatives of
f
. For example,
[
CaSi
, Corollary 3.8]

and
[
BC
, Exercise 3.8, p. 183]
use distributional derivatives, and
[
BPP
, Lemma 2.7]
and
[
BBEM
, Theorem 2.3]

use Aleksandrov’s theorem on a.e. second differentiability of convex functions.

We present below a short elementary proof using the procedure (based on (
3.2
))
which will be used also in the proof of Lemma
4.2
. Our proof is similar
to elementary proofs given in
[
Iv
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
BAJN
, Corollary 5.1]
and we guess
that a similar proof is (essentially) contained also in a much older article.

Theorem 3.1

(Well-known theorem)
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space,
G\subset X
an open convex set
and let a function
f
on
G
be both semiconvex and semiconcave with constant
C
.
Then
f
is differentiable on
G
and
f^{\prime}
is Lipschitz with constant
C
.

Proof.

By Corollary
2.5
we know that
f^{\prime}
exists on
G
and (
2.4
) holds.
First we will show that if

u,v\in G
,
u\neq v
, then

(3.1)

\overline{B}\bigg(\frac{u+v}{2},\frac{\|v-u\|}{2}\bigg)\subset G\ \Longrightarrow\ \|f^{\prime}(v)-f^{\prime}(u)\|\leq C\|v-u\|.

To this end, suppose that the assumption of (
3.1
) holds. Without any loss of generality
we can suppose that

(3.2)

f(u)-f(v)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(u)(v-u)-\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(v)(u-v)\leq 0,

since otherwise we could work with
\tilde{f}:=-f
instead of
f
.
Now consider an arbitrary
w\in X
,
\|w\|=\|v-u\|
. Then we have

\frac{1}{2}(u+v+w)\in G
and therefore, using (
2.4
) and (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(v)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{2}w\bigg)-f^{\prime}(u)\bigg(\frac{1}{2}w\bigg)

\displaystyle=f\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(u+v+w)\bigg)-f(u)-f^{\prime}(u)\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(w+v-u)\bigg)

\displaystyle+f(u)-f(v)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(u)(v-u)-\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(v)(u-v)

\displaystyle-\bigg(f\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(u+v+w)\bigg)-f(v)-f^{\prime}(v)\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(w-v+u)\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{8}\|w+(v-u)\|^{2}+\frac{C}{8}\|w-(v-u)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{4}\|w\|^{2}+\frac{C}{4}\|v-u\|^{2}=\frac{C}{2}\|v-u\|^{2}

and hence

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}(v)-f^{\prime}(u)\|=\sup_{w\in X,\|w\|=\|u-v\|}\frac{f^{\prime}(v)(w)-f^{\prime}(u)(w)}{\|w\|}\leq C\|v-u\|.

Now consider arbitrary
x,y\in G
,
x\neq y
. We can clearly choose
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
so large
that, for each
1\leq i\leq n
,

\overline{B}\bigg(\frac{z_{i-1}+z_{i}}{2},\frac{\|z_{i}-z_{i-1}\|}{2}\bigg)\subset G,\ \text{where}\ z_{i}:=x+\frac{i}{n}(y-x),\quad i\in\{0,\dots,n\}.

Then (
3.1
) implies that
\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})-f^{\prime}(z_{i-1})\|\leq C\|z_{i}-z_{i-1}\|

for each
1\leq i\leq n
, and consequently

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}(y)-f^{\prime}(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})-f^{\prime}(z_{i-1})\|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}C\|z_{i}-z_{i-1}\|=C\|y-x\|.

∎

4.
Results for a general modulus

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.10
and interesting Corollary
4.3
. Other
more special versions of implication (
1.6
) (for
X={\mathbb{R}}
or for special
\omega
) can be found in Remark
4.5

and Remark
5.3
.

Remark 4.1
.

Note that in the proof of Lemma
4.2
(and so also in the proofs of
Corollary
4.3
and Theorem
1.10
)
the assumption
that
f
is both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave is applied
via property (
2.3
) only.

Proceeding similarly as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the following estimate.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space,
G\subset X
an open convex set,
\omega\in{\cal M}
, and let

f
be a function on
G
which is both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave.
If
y,z\in G
,
r>0
and
\overline{B}(y,r)\subset G
, then

(4.1)

\|f^{\prime}(z)-f^{\prime}(y)\|\leq 2\bigg(1+\frac{\|z-y\|}{r}\bigg)\omega\bigg(\frac{1}{2}r+\frac{1}{2}\|z-y\|\bigg).

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we may suppose that

\displaystyle f(y)-f(z)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(y)(z-y)-\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(z)(y-z)\leq 0.

Then for every
w\in X
,
\|w\|=r
, we obtain, using
\frac{1}{2}(z+y+w)\in G
and (
2.3
),

\displaystyle f^{\prime}

\displaystyle(z)\bigg(\frac{1}{2}w\bigg)-f^{\prime}(y)\bigg(\frac{1}{2}w\bigg)

\displaystyle=f\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(z+y+w)\bigg)-f(y)-f^{\prime}(y)\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(w+z-y)\bigg)

\displaystyle+f(y)-f(z)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(y)(z-y)-\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(z)(y-z)

\displaystyle-\bigg(f\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(z+y+w)\bigg)-f(z)-f^{\prime}(z)\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(w-z+y)\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|w+(z-y)\|\omega\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\|w+(z-y)\|\bigg)+\frac{1}{2}\|w-(z-y)\|\omega\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\|w-(z-y)\|\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq(r+\|z-y\|)\|\omega\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(r+\|z-y\|)\bigg)

and hence

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}(z)-f^{\prime}(y)\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{w\in X,\|w\|=r}\frac{f^{\prime}(z)(w)-f^{\prime}(y)(w)}{\|w\|}

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigg(1+\frac{\|z-y\|}{r}\bigg)\omega\bigg(\frac{1}{2}r+\frac{1}{2}\|z-y\|\bigg).

∎

Using (
4.1
) for
z\neq y
and
r:=\|z-y\|
, we immediately obtain the following result.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space and
\omega\in{\cal M}
.
Suppose that a function

on

is both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave.
Then

is uniformly continuous with modulus

.

Remark 4.4
.

Corollary

even for linear modulus. It is factually shown in

.
They (factually) show that then (

) holds with

and

, which implies

that

Using Lemma
4.2
, we give also a relatively short proof
of Theorem
1.10
formulated in the introduction.

Proof of Theorem
1.10
.

(i) Suppose that
G
is bounded.

Let
x,x+h\in G
and
\varepsilon>0
. We find
y_{0}\in G
and
r_{0}>0
such that
\overline{B}(y_{0},r_{0})\subset G
and
\operatorname{diam}G/r_{0}\leq e_{G}+\varepsilon
.
Set
\lambda:=\|h\|/\operatorname{diam}G
,
s:=x+\frac{1}{2}h
,
y:=\lambda y_{0}+(1-\lambda)s
and
r:=\lambda r_{0}
. Then

\overline{B}(y,r)=\lambda\overline{B}(y_{0},r_{0})+(1-\lambda)\{s\}\subset G\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \frac{\|h\|}{e_{G}+\varepsilon}\leq r\leq\frac{\|h\|}{e_{G}}.

Clearly
\|s-y_{0}\|\leq\operatorname{diam}G-r_{0}
and so

\|s-y\|=\|\lambda(s-y_{0})\|=\frac{\|h\|}{\operatorname{diam}G}\|s-y_{0}\|\leq\|h\|\left(1-\frac{r_{0}}{\operatorname{diam}G}\right)\leq\|h\|-\frac{1}{e_{G}+\varepsilon}\|h\|.

Consequently, if

z=x
or
z=x+h
, we have
\|z-y\|\leq\frac{3}{2}\|h\|-\frac{1}{e_{G}+\varepsilon}\|h\|
.
Using this inequality, estimates of
r
and the obvious inequality
e_{G}\geq 2
, we obtain

2\left(1+\frac{\|z-y\|}{r}\right)\leq 2\left(1+\frac{3}{2}(e_{G}+\varepsilon)-1\right)=3(e_{G}+\varepsilon),\ \text{and}

\frac{1}{2}r+\frac{1}{2}\|z-y\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\|h\|}{e_{G}}+\frac{3}{2}\,\|h\|-\frac{1}{e_{G}+\varepsilon}\,\|h\|\right)<\|h\|.

Therefore (
4.1
) gives

\|f^{\prime}(z)-f^{\prime}(y)\|\leq 3(e_{G}+\varepsilon)\omega(\|h\|)

and

\|f^{\prime}(x+h)-f^{\prime}(x)\|\leq\|f^{\prime}(x+h)-f^{\prime}(y)\|+\|f^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(y)\|\leq 6(e_{G}+\varepsilon)\omega(\|h\|)

which implies the assertion of (i).

(ii) Suppose that

a\in X
,
r>0
and
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are such that
\|u_{n}\|=n
and
B(a+u_{n},nr)\subset G
for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Let
x,x+h\in G
. Then there exists
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
x,x+h\in\overline{B}(a,n)
. Set
B:=G\cap B(a,(1+r)n)
. Then
x,x+h,a+u_{n}\in B
. Since
B(a+u_{n},rn)\subset B
, we have

\displaystyle e_{B}\leq\frac{\operatorname{diam}B}{rn}\leq\frac{2(1+r)n}{rn}=2\left(1+\frac{1}{r}\right).

Thus
\|f^{\prime}(x+h)-f^{\prime}(x)\|\leq 12(1+1/r)\omega(\|h\|)
by the case (i).
\square

Let

5.
Optimality in finite-dimansional spaces

By Remark
4.5
(iii), we know that if
\omega
is linear, then the implication
(
1.6
) holds for each open convex subset
G
of an arbitrary normed linear space. This assertion holds also for some nonlinear

\omega\in{\cal M}
(cf. Remark
5.3
below), but the following example shows that for the
most interesting nonlinear moduli this assertion does not hold.

Example 5.1
.

Let
X={\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
G:={\mathbb{R}}\times(0,1)
. Let
\omega\in{\cal M}
be such
that

(5.1)

\liminf_{t\to 0+}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}>0\ \ \text{and}\ \ \liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}=0.

Then the implication (
1.6
) does not hold.

Proof.

Set

g(x):=x_{1}x_{2},\ \ x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in G.

By (
5.1
) we can choose
1>\delta>0
and
c>0
such that

ct\leq\omega(t)
for each
t\in(0,\delta]
. Then the monotonicity of
\omega
implies that
c\delta\leq\omega(t)

for each
t\in(\delta,\infty)
.
We will show that

(5.2)

g
is both
\left(\frac{2}{c\delta}\omega\right)
-semiconvex
and
\left(\frac{2}{c\delta}\omega\right)
-semiconcave on
G
.

To this
end consider
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in G
and
h=(h_{1},h_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with

x+h\in G
. Note that
|h_{2}|<1
.
We have

|g(x+h)-g(x)-g^{\prime}(x)(h)|=|(x_{1}+h_{1})(x_{2}+h_{2})-x_{1}x_{2}-(x_{2}h_{1}+x_{1}h_{2})|=|h_{1}h_{2}|.

Now we distinguish two cases. If
\|h\|\leq\delta
, then

|g(x+h)-g(x)-g^{\prime}(x)(h)|=|h_{1}h_{2}|\leq\|h\|\cdot\|h\|\leq\|h\|\,\frac{1}{c}\omega(\|h\|).

If
\|h\|>\delta
, we have

|g(x+h)-g(x)-g^{\prime}(x)(h)|=|h_{1}h_{2}|\leq|h_{1}|\leq\|h\|\,\frac{1}{c\delta}\omega(\|h\|).

So Lemma
2.6
implies (
5.2
), and consequently the function

f:=\frac{c\delta}{2}g

is both
\omega
-semiconvex
and
\omega
-semiconcave on
G
.

However, we will show that
f\notin C^{1,\omega}(G)
. Suppose to the contrary that
f^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous with modulus
D\omega
on
G
for some
D>0
.
Then also
h(x_{1},x_{2}):=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}
is uniformly continuous with modulus
E\omega
on
G
for some
E>0
. By (
5.1
) there exists
t_{0}>0
such that

E\omega(t_{0})<t_{0}
and therefore

t_{0}=h\bigg(t_{0},\frac{1}{2}\bigg)=h\bigg(t_{0},\frac{1}{2}\bigg)-h\bigg(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigg)\leq E\omega(t_{0})<t_{0},

which is a contradiction.
∎

.

We have proved that if
\omega\in{\cal M}
has properties (

), then there exists
a

function
f
on the strip
{\mathbb{R}}\times(0,1)
which is both
\omega
-semiconvex and

\omega
-semiconcave, and for which

is uniformly continuous on the ray

with modulus

for no
D>0
.

Remark 5.3
.

(i)

If
\liminf_{t\to 0+}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}=0
, then
(for arbitrary
X
and
G
)
f
is
\omega
-semiconvex (resp.

\omega
-semiconcave) on
G
if and only if
f
is continuous
and convex (resp. concave) on
G
(see e.g.
[
Kr
, p. 3, (2)]
). Consequently
(
1.6
) clearly holds for arbitrary
X
and

.

(ii)

If
\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}>0
, it is not difficult to
prove that (

) holds for
X={\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and an arbitrary
G\subset X
.

It follows that condition (
5.1
) in Example
5.1
cannot be relaxed.

The proof of Theorem
1.9
for
n=2
is based on the constructions of the following two lemmas.
(The case of general
n
needs a nontrivial inductive argument.)

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\eta:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous nondecreasing nonconstant concave function with
\eta(0)=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\eta(t)/t=0
.
Then there exists a concave modulus
\omega
such that

(i)

\omega(t)\geq\min(1,t),\ \ t\in[0,\infty),

(ii)

the function

g(t):=\frac{\eta(t)\cdot\log t}{t\cdot\omega(t)},\ t\in[1,\infty),
is bounded,

(iii)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)/\log t=0
,

(iv)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=\infty
.

Proof.

First we will construct a concave modulus
\tilde{\omega}\in{\cal M}
, for which
conditions (i)-(iii) hold.
Set
\tilde{\omega}(t):=t,\ \ t\in[0,1]
, and

\tilde{\omega}(t):=1+\frac{1}{\eta(1)}\cdot\int_{1}^{t}\frac{\eta(u)}{u^{2}}\ du,\ \ t\geq 1.

Then clearly
\tilde{\omega}\in{\cal M}
and (i) holds for
\tilde{\omega}
.

Note that the function
u\mapsto\frac{\eta(u)}{u}
is nonincreasing on
[1,\infty)
by concavity of
\eta
.
So the function
u\mapsto\frac{\eta(u)}{u^{2}}
is nonincreasing on
[1,\infty)
too. Consequently
we obtain that
\tilde{\omega}
is concave on
[1,\infty)
and since

\tilde{\omega}_{+}^{\prime}(1)=1
, we obtain that
\tilde{\omega}
is concave on
[0,\infty)
.

Since
u\mapsto\frac{\eta(u)}{u}
is nonincreasing on
[1,\infty)
, we obtain
that, for
1<u\leq t
, we have

\frac{\eta(u)}{u^{2}}\geq\frac{\eta(t)}{t}\cdot\frac{1}{u},

consequently

\tilde{\omega}(t)\geq\frac{1}{\eta(1)}\cdot\frac{\eta(t)}{t}\cdot\int_{1}^{t}\frac{1}{u}\ du=\frac{1}{\eta(1)}\cdot\frac{\eta(t)}{t}\cdot\log t,\ \ t\geq 1,

and so (ii) for
\tilde{\omega}
follows.

Finally, (iii) for
\tilde{\omega}
follows by L’Hospital’s rule, since

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\int_{1}^{t}\frac{\eta(u)}{u^{2}}\ du}{\log t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\frac{\eta(t)}{t^{2}}}{\frac{1}{t}}=0.

Now it is easy to see that the modulus

\omega(t):=\tilde{\omega}(t)+\sqrt{\log(1+t)},\ \ t\in[0,\infty],

has all demanded properties.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\eta:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a nondecreasing nonconstant continuous concave function such that
\eta(0)=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\eta(t)/t=0
.
Set

H:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{1}>1,\;|x_{2}|<\eta(x_{1})\}

and

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2}):=\log(x_{1})x_{2},\quad(x_{1},x_{2})\in H.

Then there exists a concave
\omega\in{\cal M}
such that

(5.3)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=\infty,\ \ \ \ \lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)/\log t=0,

and

(5.4)

f
is both
(C\omega)
-semiconvex and
(C\omega)
-semiconcave
for some
C>0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.4
there exists a concave
\omega\in{\cal M}
such that
(
5.3
) and conditions (i), (ii) of Lemma
5.4
hold. So we
can find
D>0
such that

(5.5)

\eta(t)\log(t)\leq Dt\omega(t)\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \eta(t)\leq Dt,\quad t\geq 1.

To prove (
5.4
), we will use
Lemma
2.3
.

So we consider arbitrary

x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in H
,
y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in H
and set
h=(h_{1},h_{2}):=y-x
. By the symmetry,
we can suppose that
h_{1}\geq 0
.
Now we will estimate from above

V:=|f^{\prime}(y)(y-x)-f^{\prime}(x)(y-x)|=|f^{\prime}(x+h)(h)-f^{\prime}(x)(h)|.

An elementary computation gives

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{x_{2}+h_{2}}{x_{1}+h_{1}}\cdot h_{1}+\log(x_{1}+h_{1})h_{2}-\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\,h_{1}-\log(x_{1})h_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq h_{1}\,\left|\frac{x_{2}+h_{2}}{x_{1}+h_{1}}-\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\right|+|h_{2}|\,(\log(x_{1}+h_{1})-\log(x_{1}))=:A+B.

We have

A=h_{1}\,\left|\frac{h_{2}x_{1}-h_{1}x_{2}}{(x_{1}+h_{1})x_{1}}\right|\leq\frac{h_{1}|h_{2}|}{x_{1}+h_{1}}+\frac{(h_{1})^{2}|x_{2}|}{(x_{1}+h_{1})x_{1}}.

Using Lemma
5.4
(i) and
x_{1}>1
, we obtain

\frac{h_{1}|h_{2}|}{x_{1}+h_{1}}\leq\min(\|h\|,\|h\|^{2})=\|h\|\min(1,\|h\|)\leq\|h\|\omega(\|h\|)

and, using also
|x_{2}|\leq\eta(x_{1})\leq Dx_{1}
,

\frac{(h_{1})^{2}|x_{2}|}{(x_{1}+h_{1})x_{1}}\leq D\,\frac{(h_{1})^{2}}{x_{1}+h_{1}}\leq D\,\min(\|h\|,\|h\|^{2})\leq D\,\|h\|\omega(\|h\|),

and consequently
A\leq(D+1)\,\|h\|\omega(\|h\|)
.

If
h_{1}\leq 1
, then
h_{1}\leq\omega(h_{1})
by Lemma
5.4
(i), and therefore

\displaystyle B=|h_{2}|\log\bigg(1+\frac{h_{1}}{x_{1}}\bigg)\leq|h_{2}|\frac{h_{1}}{x_{1}}\leq|h_{2}|h_{1}\leq|h_{2}|\omega(h_{1})\leq\|h\|\omega(\|h\|).

If
1\leq h_{1}\leq x_{1}
, note that Lemma
5.4
(i) implies
1\leq\omega(h_{1})

and obviously
|h_{2}|\leq 2\eta(x_{1}+h_{1})
. Therefore we obtain, using also (
5.5
),

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=|h_{2}|\log\bigg(1+\frac{h_{1}}{x_{1}}\bigg)\leq 2\eta(x_{1}+h_{1})\frac{h_{1}}{x_{1}}\leq 2\frac{\eta(2x_{1})}{x_{1}}h_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 4Dh_{1}\leq 4Dh_{1}\omega(h_{1})\leq 4D\|h\|\omega(\|h\|).

If
x_{1}\leq h_{1}
, then we obtain, using
|h_{2}|\leq 2\eta(x_{1}+h_{1})
, (
5.5
) and concavity of

\omega
,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=|h_{2}|(\log(x_{1}+h_{1})-\log(x_{1}))

\displaystyle\leq 2\eta(x_{1}+h_{1})\log(x_{1}+h_{1})\leq 2D(x_{1}+h_{1})\omega(x_{1}+h_{1})

\displaystyle\leq 4Dh_{1}\omega(2h_{1})\leq 8Dh_{1}\omega(h_{1})\leq 8D\|h\|\omega(\|h\|).

Using the above estimates, we obtain
V=A+B\leq(9D+1)\,\|h\|\omega(\|h\|)
.
So Lemma
2.3
implies that (
5.4
) holds for
C:=9D+1
.
∎

The proof of our main result in general
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
use an inductive
argument which needs the following notion.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\omega\in\cal M
.
Then we denote by
\cal G_{n}(\omega)
the set of all open convex sets

G\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for which there exist a ray
\rho\subset G
,
C>0
and a function
f\in C^{1}(G)

which is both
(C\omega)
-semiconvex and
(C\omega)
-semiconcave and for which

f^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous on
\rho
with modulus
D\omega

for no
D>0
.

Remark 5.7
.

If
G\in\cal G_{n}(\omega)
, then the implication (
1.6
) does not hold. Indeed,
then the function
f^{*}:=(1/C)f
(where
C
and
f
are as in Definition
5.6
) is both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave
but clearly
f^{*}\notin C^{1,\omega}(G)
.

Remark 5.8
.

Note that Remark
5.2
immediately implies that, if
\omega\in{\cal M}
has properties
(
5.1
), then

{\mathbb{R}}\times(0,1)\in\cal G_{2}(\omega)
.

The following result on further unbounded open convex subsets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
easily follows from
Lemma
5.5
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
u
be
a positive nondecreasing concave function on
(1,\infty)
and let
l
be a negative
nonincreasing convex function on
(1,\infty)

such that
\lim_{x\to\infty}u(x)/x=0
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}l(x)/x=0
.
Then there exists a concave modulus
\omega\in{\cal M}
such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=\infty
and

(5.6)

G:=\{(x,y):\ x>1,\ l(x)<y<u(x)\}\in\cal G_{2}(\omega).

Proof.

Set
h(x):=u(x)-l(x),\ x>1
,
a:=\max(h(2),h^{\prime}_{+}(2))
and

b:=2a-h(2)
. Clearly
a>0
,
b>0
.
Let
\eta(x):=ax
for
x\in[0,2]
and
\eta(x):=h(x)+b
for

x\in(2,\infty)
. Then
\eta
is clearly a nonnegative
nonconstant nondecreasing continuous concave function on
[0,\infty)
such that

\eta(0)=0
,

\lim_{x\to\infty}\eta(x)/x=0
and

G\subset H:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{1}>1,\;|x_{2}|<\eta(x_{1})\}.

Let
f(x_{1},x_{2}):=\log(x_{1})x_{2},\ (x_{1},x_{2})\in H
, and let

\omega\in{\cal M}
be a modulus which corresponds to
\eta
and
f

by Lemma
5.5
.
Suppose to the contrary
G\notin\cal G_{2}(\omega)
.
Since
G\subset H
and (
5.4
) implies
that
f
is both
(C\omega)
-semiconvex and
(C\omega)
-semiconcave
on
G
for some
C>0
, we obtain that

f^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous with modulus
D\omega
on the ray

\rho:=[2,\infty)\times\{0\}\subset G
for some
D>0
.
Consequently

\bigg|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}}(t,0)-\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}}(2,0)\bigg|=|\log t-\log 2|\leq D\omega(t-2),\ \ t>2,

which clearly contradicts (
5.3
).
∎

The following lemma is geometrically obvious.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a two-dimensional Hilbert space and
C\subset V
a two-dimensional closed convex cone such that
\{v,-v\}\subset C
for no
v\neq 0
. Then there exists
0\neq w\in V
such that

\operatorname{span}(\{w\})\cap C=\{0\}.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
\omega\in{\cal M}
be concave.
Let
n\geq k\geq 2
, let
G\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open convex set, and let

L:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
be a linear surjection. Suppose
that
H:=L(G)\in\cal G_{k}(\omega)
and

{\rm rec}\,(H)\subset L({\rm rec}\,(G))
.
Then
G\in\cal G_{n}(\omega)
.

Proof.

Since
H\in\cal G_{k}(\omega)
, there exist a ray
\rho\subset H
and a function
f\in C^{1}(H)

which is both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave and for which

f^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous on
\rho
with modulus
D\omega

for no
D>0
.
Set

\tilde{f}(y):=f(L(y)),\ \ y\in G.

Then
\tilde{f}\in C^{1}(G)
and there exists
C>0
such that

\tilde{f}
is both
(C\omega)
-semiconvex and
(C\omega)
-semiconcave
on
G
by Lemma
2.7
.

We can write
\rho=\{a+t\,v:\ t\in[0,\infty)\}
,
where
a\in H
and
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\setminus\{0\}
. Then

v\in{\rm rec}\,(H)
by Lemma
2.9
(iii) and so, by the assumptions, there exists

\tilde{v}\in{\rm rec}\,(G)
with
L(\tilde{v})=v
. Choose
\tilde{a}\in G

with
L(\tilde{a})=a
. Then
\tilde{\rho}:=\{\tilde{a}+t\,\tilde{v}:\ t\in[0,\infty)\}\subset G
. It is
easy to see that
L(\tilde{\rho})=\rho
and, for
\alpha:=\|\tilde{v}\|/\|v\|
,

(5.7)

\alpha\cdot\|L(\tilde{x})-L(\tilde{y})\|=\|\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}\|\ \ \text{for each}\ \ \tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in\tilde{\rho}.

Now suppose, to the contrary, that
G\notin\cal G_{n}(\omega)
.
Then there exists
\tilde{D}>0
, such that

(5.8)

\|(\tilde{f})^{\prime}(\tilde{x})-(\tilde{f})^{\prime}(\tilde{y})\|\leq\tilde{D}\,\omega(\|\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}\|),\ \ \ \tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in\tilde{\rho}.

Consider the dual mapping
L^{*}:({\mathbb{R}}^{k})^{*}\to({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{*}
defined by
L^{*}(\varphi):=\varphi\circ L
,

\varphi\in({\mathbb{R}}^{k})^{*}
. Since
L
is surjective,
L^{*}
is injective and so

L^{*}:({\mathbb{R}}^{k})^{*}\to L^{*}(({\mathbb{R}}^{k})^{*})
is a linear isomorphism. So there exists
\beta>0

such that

(5.9)

\beta\|\varphi\circ L\|\geq\|\varphi\|\ \ \text{for each}\ \ \varphi\in({\mathbb{R}}^{k})^{*}.

Now consider arbitrary
x,y\in\rho
and find
\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in\tilde{\rho}
such that

L(\tilde{x})=x
,
L(\tilde{y})=y
. Using (
5.9
) for
\varphi:=f^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(y)
, (
5.8
),
(
5.7
) and the concavity of
\omega
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\beta\|(f^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(y))\circ L\|=\beta\|(f^{\prime}(L(\tilde{x}))-f^{\prime}(L(\tilde{y}))\circ L\|

\displaystyle=\beta\|(\tilde{f})^{\prime}(\tilde{x})-(\tilde{f})^{\prime}(\tilde{y})\|\leq\beta\tilde{D}\omega(\|\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}\|)

\displaystyle=\beta\tilde{D}\omega(\alpha\|x-y\|)\leq\beta\tilde{D}\max(1,\alpha)\omega(\|x-y\|).

So
f^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous on
\rho
with modulus

D\omega
for
D:=\beta\tilde{D}\max(1,\alpha)
, which
is a contradiction.
∎

As an easy consequence we obtain the following fact.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
\omega\in{\cal M}
be concave. Let
n\geq 2
,
G\in\cal G_{n}(\omega)
, and let
A:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an affine bijection. Then
A(G)\in\cal G_{n}(\omega)
.

Proof.

If
A
is linear, then the assertion easily follows from Lemma
5.11
. If
A
is a translation, then the proof is obvious. So the lemma
clearly follows.
∎

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
G\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an unbounded
open convex set which contains no translation of a convex cone with nonempty interior.
Then there exists a concave modulus
\omega\in\cal M
with

\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=\infty
such that

G\in\cal G_{n}(\omega)
.

Proof.

We will proceed by induction on
n
.

a) Let
n=2
. Then our assumptions and Lemma
2.9
(iv), (vi) imply that
\dim({\rm rec}\,(G))=1
. On account of Lemma
5.12
we can suppose
(1,0)\in{\rm rec}\,(G)
.
Now we will distinguish two cases.

If also
-(1,0)\in{\rm rec}\,(G)
, then clearly
G={\mathbb{R}}\times H
for an
open convex set
H\subset{\mathbb{R}}
. Since
\dim({\rm rec}\,(G))=1
,
H

is a bounded open interval. By Lemma
5.12
we can suppose
H=(0,1)
and so
G\in\cal G_{2}(\omega)
e.g. for
\omega(t)=\sqrt{t}
by Remark
5.8
.

If
-(1,0)\notin{\rm rec}\,(G)
, then we can suppose (by Lemma
5.12
) that

\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:(x,0)\in G\}=(1,\infty)
. We can also suppose that
a support line to
\overline{G}
at
(1,0)
is parallel to the
y
-axis. For
x>1
, set
u(x):=\sup\{y:(x,y)\in G\}
and

l(x):=\inf\{y:(x,y)\in G\}
. It is easy to show that
u
is
a positive nondecreasing concave function on
(1,\infty)
,
l
is a negative nonincreasing
convex function on
(1,\infty)
and

G=\{(x,y):\ x>1,\ l(x)<y<u(x)\}.

Then
A:=\lim_{x\to\infty}u(x)/x=0
, since otherwise

(1,A/2)\in{\rm rec}\,(G)
and thus
\dim({\rm rec}\,(G))=2
. Similarly
we obtain
\lim_{x\to\infty}l(x)/x=0
.
So our assertion follows from Lemma
5.9
.

b) Now suppose that
n\geq 3
is given and that the proposition
“holds for
n-1
”. We will distinguish two cases.

b1) There exists
v\neq 0
such that
\{v,-v\}\subset{\rm rec}\,(G)
(i.e.,
G
contains a line).

By Lemma
5.12
, we can suppose that
v=(0,\dots,0,1)
and thus
there exists an open convex set
H\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
such that
G=H\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then clearly
\dim({\rm rec}\,(H))<n-1
and so the inductive assumption gives that
H\in\cal G_{n-1}(\omega)
for some concave
\omega\in\cal M
with

\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=\infty
. Let
L(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})
.
Then clearly
L(G)=H

and
{\rm rec}\,(H)\subset L({\rm rec}\,(G))
.
Therefore
G\in\cal G_{n}(\omega)
by Lemma
5.11
.

b2) There is no
v\neq 0
such that
\{v,-v\}\subset{\rm rec}\,(G)
.

We will show that there exists a linear surjection
L:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
such that

(5.10)

L^{-1}(\{0\})\cap{\rm rec}\,(G)=\{0\}\ \ \text{and}

(5.11)

\ \ \dim(L({\rm rec}\,(G)))<n-1.

If
\dim({\rm rec}\,(G))<n-1
, it is easy (using Lemma
2.9
(v)) to find
L
satisfying
(
5.10
) and observe that (
5.11
) holds.

In the opposite case Lemma
2.9
(v),(vi) imply that
\dim({\rm rec}\,(G))=n-1
and there exist linearly independent vectors

v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1}\in{\rm rec}\,(G)
. Now we apply Lemma
5.10

to the space
V:=\operatorname{span}\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
and the cone
C:=V\cap\,{\rm rec}\,(G)
(which is closed by Lemma
2.9
(ii))
and obtain a corresponding vector
w
. Then any linear surjection
L:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}

with
L(w)=0
clearly satisfies (
5.10
). Moreover,
L(w)=0
implies that
the vectors
L(v_{1})
and
L(v_{2})
are linearly dependent and
consequently
\dim(L({\rm rec}\,(G)))<n-1
.

So there exists
L
for which (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) hold.
Using (
5.10
) and Lemma
2.10
we obtain

{\rm rec}\,(L(G))=L({\rm rec}\,(G))
. So
\dim({\rm rec}\,(L(G)))<n-1
and consequently

L(G)\in\cal G_{n-1}(\omega)
for some concave
\omega\in\cal M
with

\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=\infty
by the induction assumption.
Therefore we obtain

G\in\cal G_{n}(\omega)
by
Lemma
5.11
.
∎

6.
Consequences and open questions

As already mentioned, our results are very closely related to a first-order quantitative
converse Taylor theorem and to the problem whether

f\in C^{1,\omega}(G)
whenever
f
is continuous and smooth in a corresponding sense on all lines.

To describe precisely these relations, first
recall
[
HJ
, Definition 121]
in the special case of real-valued functions and

p=1
(which deal with approximation of a function by polynomials of degree

p\leq 1
).

Definition 6.1
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space and
G\subset X
an open set. We say that a function

f:G\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
UT^{1}
-smooth on
G
with modulus
\omega\in{\cal M}
(or shortly
UT^{1}_{\omega}
-smooth) if for each
x\in G

there exist
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
such that

(6.1)

|f(x+h)-(b+x^{*}(h))|\leq\|h\|\,\omega(\|h\|)\ \ \ \text{whenever}\ \ \ x+h\in G.

Remark 6.2
.

Note that if (
6.1
) holds then clearly
b=f(x)
and
x^{*}=f^{\prime}(x)
.
So
f
is
UT^{1}_{\omega}
-smooth on
G
if and only if
(
2.3
) holds.

Further, we will use the following terminology.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space,
G\subset X
an open convex set,

\omega\in{\cal M}
, and let
f
be a function on
G
.
We will say that
f
is
C^{1,\omega}_{*}
-smooth on all lines if,
for every
a\in G
and
v\in X
,
\|v\|=1
, the function

f_{a,v}:t\mapsto f(a+tv)
defined on the open interval

\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}:\ a+tv\in G\}
is
C^{1,\omega}_{*}
-smooth.

Using this notation, we can reformulate some already mentioned facts as follows.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space,
G\subset X
an open convex set,

\omega\in{\cal M}
, and let
f
be a continuous function on
G
.
Then the following implications hold.

(i)

If
f
is both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave, then
f
is
UT^{1}_{\omega}
-smooth.

(ii)

If
f
is
UT^{1}_{\omega}
-smooth, then

f
is both
(2\omega)
-semiconvex and
(2\omega)
-semiconcave.

(iii)

If
f
is
C^{1,\omega}_{*}
-smooth on all lines,
then
f
is both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave.

(iv)

If
f
is both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave, then
f
is
C^{1,2\omega}_{*}
-smooth on all lines.

Proof.

Assertions (i) and (ii) are reformulations of Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.6
, respectively.
Using (
1.5
) and Lemma
2.1
we obtain (iii). Lemma
2.1
together with
Remark
4.5
(ii) give (iv).
∎

Remark 6.5
.

If
\omega
in Proposition
6.4
is linear, then the converse of (i) holds by Lemma

2.6
and the converse of (iv) holds by Remark
2.2
(ii) and Lemma
2.1
.

Using Proposition
6.4
, Remark
4.1
and Remark
6.2
, we easily see that

Theorem
1.10
and Corollary
4.3
remain true if we replace the property
“
f
is both

\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave” by the
property that “
f

is
UT^{1}
-smooth function with modulus

\omega
” or by the
property that
“
f
is continuous and
C^{1,\omega}_{*}
-smooth on all lines”.

So we obtain in the very special case
p=1
,
Y={\mathbb{R}}
(and finite modulus
\omega
) a more precise
qualitative versions of converse Taylor theorem
[
HJ
, Corollary 126]
and also of

[
JZ
, Proposition 12]
and
[
HJ
, Theorem 127]
(which deal with functions which are
“
C^{p,\omega}_{*}
-smooth on all lines”).

In particular, we obtain the following version of Corollary
4.3
.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space and
\omega\in{\cal M}
.
Suppose that a continuous function
f
on
X
is

C_{*}^{1,\omega}
-smooth on all lines.
Then
f
is
C_{*}^{1,4\omega}
-smooth on
X
.

Further, Proposition
6.4
shows that our basic result Theorem
1.9
can be reformulated in the following
form.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
G\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) be an unbounded open convex set which does not contain a translation of a convex cone with nonempty interior. Then there exists a concave modulus
\omega\in{\cal M}

with
\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=\infty
and a continuous function
f

such that

(i)

f
is
C^{1,\omega}_{*}
-smooth on all lines,

(ii)

is
UT^{1}
-smooth function with modulus

\omega
and

(iii)

.

In particular, the assumptions on
G
of
[

, Corollary 126]

cannot be relaxed in the case

.

Finally observe that, by Proposition
6.4
, equivalence (
1.7
) can be extended
as follows.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
X
and
G
be as in Theorem
1.7
. Then, for each
\omega\in{\cal M}
and for each function
f
on

, the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

f

-smooth for some

.

-smooth on all lines for some

.

The following natural (cf. Remark
5.3
) question remains open.

Question 6.9
.

Let
\omega\in{\cal M}
,
n\geq 2
and let
G\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open convex set
which contains no translation of a convex cone
with nonempty interior. Does there exist a function
f
on
G
which is both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave and does not belong to
C^{1,\omega}(G)
, if

(i)

\liminf_{t\to 0+}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}>0
and
\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}=0
?

(ii)

\omega
is concave,
\liminf_{t\to 0+}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}>0
and
\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}=0
?

(iii)

\omega(t)=t^{\alpha},\ t\geq 0,
for some
0<\alpha<1
?

(iv)

\omega(t)=\sqrt{t},\ t\geq 0
?

Twisted Jacobian algebras as endomorphism algebras of equivariant matrix factorizations
Sangwook Lee

Abstract
. For a polynomial W W with an isolated singularity, we can consider the Jacobian ring as an invariant of the singularity. If in addition we have a group action on the polynomial ring with W W fixed, we are led to consider the twisted Jacobian ring which reflects the equivariant structure as well. Our main result is to show that the twisted Jacobian ring is isomorphic to an endomorphism ring of the ”twisted diagonal” matrix factorization. As an application, we suggest a way to investigate Floer theory of Lagrangian submanifolds which represent homological mirror functors.



1.
Introduction

Given a nontrivial automorphism group
G
of the space-time, it is natural to consider
G
-equivariant 2-dimensional topological field theory (2d TFT for short). Turaev formulated closed
G
-equivariant 2d TFT as a
G
-graded algebra
\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}_{g}
(which was later called
Turaev algebra
) satisfying a number of axioms which include
G
-twisted commutativity,
G
-invariance of the pairing, trace axiom etc. If we take the invariant of each graded piece
\mathcal{C}_{g}
by centralizer
Z_{g}
, then it gives rise to the orbifold theory (see
[
1
,
18
]
).

There is a well-known geometric example of Turaev algebras. When
X
is a compact almost complex manifold and
G
is a finite group acting on
X
, Fantechi-Göttsche
[
9
]
defined an algebra
\mathcal{H}(X,G)
, so-called the
stringy cohomology
of
(X,G)
. It was shown that
\mathcal{H}(X,G)
satisfies axioms of Turaev algebras. It is also known that the
G
-invariant subalgebra
\mathcal{H}(X,G)^{G}
is isomorphic to the orbifold cohomology
H_{orb}^{*}([X/G])
(see also
[
12
]
).

We recall another important example of TFTs from singularity theory which is the main topic of this paper. Let
X
be a variety and
W
be an algebraic function on
X
. When we want to investigate the singularity of
W
, we are led to consider an important invariant, so-called Jacobian ring (denoted by
{\rm Jac}(W)
) which is the function ring of the singular locus of
W
. Together with residue pairing, Jacobian ring is a Frobenius algebra which encodes a (non-equivariant) closed 2d TFT.
If a group
G
acts on the variety
X
and the function
W
is invariant under the action, then it is natural to consider
G
-equivariant invariants of the singularity. If
G
is finite and abelian, such a pair
(W,G)
is called a
Landau-Ginzburg orbifold
which is an interesting object in mirror symmetry. For simplicity we only consider
(W,G)
where
W
is a polynomial in
R=k[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
(
k
is a field of characteristic
0
) which has a singularity at the origin, and
G
is a finite abelian group which acts diagonally on
R
.
It is natural to ask how we can construct an algebra structure which corresponds to the closed
G
-equivariant 2d TFT for
(W,G)
. For convenience, let us introduce its name as
twisted Jacobian algebra
of
(W,G)
and denote it by
{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,G)
. There is a natural candidate for the underlying module structure of
{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,G)
as

(1.1)

{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,G)=\bigoplus_{g\in G}{\rm Jac}(W^{g})\cdot\xi_{g}

where
W^{g}:=W|_{{\rm Fix}(g)}
and
\xi_{g}
is a formal generator with
|\xi_{g}|=n-\dim({{\rm Fix}(g)})
.

We recall earlier works on the construction of twisted Jacobian algebras.

•

Kaufmann
[
11
]
classified possible algebraic structures on (
1.1
) in terms of discrete torsions of
G
. He also observed that one needs to allow nontrivial characters which modify Turaev’s axioms to accommodate more general classes of singularities.

•

Basalaev-Takahashi-Werner
[
3
]
investigated
{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,G)
in the context of mirror symmetry of Frobenius manifolds which essentially involve primitive forms. They axiomatized twisted Jacobian algebras employing primitive forms, and obtained existence and uniqueness result for twisted Jacobian algebras of invertible polynomials together with diagonal symmetry group actions.

•

Shklyarov
[
16
]
gave an explicit multiplication formula of
{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,G)
for arbitrary Landau-Ginzburg orbifolds. He deduced the formula from the cup product on Hochschild cohomology of a curved algebra with crossed product coefficient.

•

He-Li-Li
[
10
]
deduced a combinorial multiplication formula together with verification of Kaufmann’s axioms (concerning not only multiplications, but also pairings and trace axiom etc) for invertible polynomials.

In this paper, we will focus on the product structure on
{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,G)
.

Recall that taking invariant of a closed
G
-equivariant 2d TFT by centralizers gives rise to the orbifold theory. It is also the case for twisted Jacobian algebras. Namely, if we take
G
-invariant of
{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,G)
, it becomes a commutative Frobenius algebra
{\rm Jac}(W,G)
which is called the
orbifold Jacobian algebra
of
(W,G)
. Orbifold Jacobian algebras naturally arise from categories as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
13
,
16
]
)
.

We have an algebra isomorphism

(1.2)

{\rm Jac}(W,G)\cong HH^{*}(MF_{G}(W))

where
HH^{*}(MF_{G}(W))
is the Hochschild cohomology of the dg category of
G
-equivariant matrix factorizations of
W
.

Computation of Hochschild cohomology of a dg category is in general not easy. For a dg category of matrix factorizations, however, we can realize the Hochschild cochain complex very explicitly. The original definition (due to Toën
[
17
]
) of Hochschild cochain complex of a dg category
\mathcal{A}
is

C^{*}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}):=hom_{R\underline{Hom}_{c}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A})}(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{A}},\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{A}}),

where
R\underline{Hom}_{c}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A})
is the dg category of continuous endofunctors of
\mathcal{A}
.

The notion of
kernels
for dg functors between categories of matrix factorizations enables us to compute Hochschild cohomology. The theory of kernels for functors between (possibly non-affine) matrix factorization categories was extensively studied in
[
2
]
. For affine case, we have the following results.

•

If
\mathcal{A}=MF(W)
, then
R\underline{Hom}_{c}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A})\simeq MF(W\boxminus W)
, and the identity functor
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{A}}
corresponds to a Koszul matrix factorization
\Delta_{W}
of the diagonal ideal.

•

If
\mathcal{A}=MF_{G}(W)
, then
R\underline{Hom}_{c}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A})\simeq MF_{G\times G}(W\boxminus W)
and the identity functor
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{A}}
corresponds to a
(G\times G)
-equivariant Koszul matrix factorization
\Delta_{W}^{G\times G}
of the
twisted
diagonal ideal.

Therefore we can reformulate (
1.2
) as follows.

(1.3)

{\rm Jac}(W,G)\cong{\rm Hom}_{MF_{G\times G}(W\boxminus W)}(\Delta_{W}^{G\times G},\Delta_{W}^{G\times G}).

The isomorphism (
1.3
) gives a clue to interpret
{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,G)
in terms of matrix factorizations.
Our approach towards multiplication formula of
{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,G)
is via category
MF_{1\times G}(W\boxminus W)
. Such a choice of the category is natural in the sense that
{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,G)
can be thought of a
G
-lifting of
{\rm Jac}(W,G)
. The following is our main theorem.

Theorem A
.

For a Landau-Ginzburg orbifold
(W,G)
, we have an algebra isomorphism

(1.4)

{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,G)\cong{\rm Hom}_{MF_{1\times G}(W\boxminus W)}(\Delta_{W}^{G\times G},\Delta_{W}^{G\times G}).

Shklyarov’s multiplication formula comes from Hochschild cohomology of a curved algebra which is the endomorphism algebra of a compact generator of the category. On the other hand, we obtain the formula inside the whole category of matrix factorizations.
Since we take an explicit Koszul matrix factorization and consider its endomorphisms, the product is described by calculus of Clifford generators which are nothing but differential forms and interior products.
In our formulation, complicatedness appears in the expression of closed endomorphisms of
\Delta_{W}^{G\times G}
.
Therefore, classification of closed endomorphisms of
\Delta_{W}^{G\times G}
is the most important starting point.
We will observe that we recover Shklyarov’s formula by restricting our multiplication of endomorphisms to the twisted diagonal.

The main motivation of this work is based on the mirror symmetry. Singularity often appears as a mirror counterpart of a symplectic manifold. Roughly, a Lagrangian submanifold
{\mathbb{L}}\subset X
which can be ”deformed” gives rise to an algebraic function
W_{\mathbb{L}}
on the deformation space. In some case, the singularity theory of
W_{\mathbb{L}}
is ”equivalent” to the symplectic geometry of
X
itself, in which case we say that
W_{\mathbb{L}}
is a
Landau-Ginzburg mirror
to
X
. A concrete mirror symmetry statement was given by Kontsevich as follows.

Conjecture 1.2

(Homological Mirror Symmetry)
.

Let
X
be a symplectic manifold and
W:\check{X}\to k
be a Landau-Ginzburg mirror to
X
. Then we have an equivalence of categories

(1.5)

Fuk(X)\simeq MF(W).

For some symplectic manifolds, we can construct an explicit (curved) Yoneda functor
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{L}}
giving the equivalence (
1.5
) (see
[
4
,
5
]
for example) with the representing Lagrangian submanifold
{\mathbb{L}}
. In
[
7
]
it was shown that Lagrangian Floer theory of
{\mathbb{L}}
is deeply related to
{\rm Jac}(W_{\mathbb{L}})
. We consider a technical assumption (which is Assumption
5.2
) which is expected to be satisfied by representing Lagrangians for mirror functors, and is indeed true for various examples. If
{\mathbb{L}}\subset X
satisfies Assumption
5.2
, then the following holds:

HF({\mathbb{L}}^{x},{\mathbb{L}}^{x})\otimes R\cong{\rm Jac}(W_{\mathbb{L}})

where
R
means the function ring of the deformation space of
{\mathbb{L}}
and
x
means its coordinates. We refer to
[
7
]
for details and examples.

The story becomes even more interesting for
\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}\subset X/G
, where
X/G
is a global quotient orbifold and
\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}
satisfies Assumption
5.2
. Let
\pi:X\to X/G
be the quotient map. It was shown in
[
7
]
that their
{\widehat{G}}
-invariant subalgebras are indeed isomorphic as algebras.

(1.6)

\big(HF\big(\pi^{-1}(\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}^{x}),\pi^{-1}(\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}^{x})\big)\otimes R\big)^{\widehat{G}}\cong{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W_{\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}},{\widehat{G}})^{\widehat{G}}.

Our application of Theorem
A
is that we indeed have an isomorphism of ”
{\widehat{G}}
-liftings” of above.

Theorem B
.

We have an algebra isomorphism

(1.7)

HF\big(\pi^{-1}(\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}^{x}),\pi^{-1}(\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}^{x})\big)\otimes R\cong{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W_{\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}},{\widehat{G}}).

Proof.

We combine the following algebra isomorphism in
[
7
]
with (
1.4
):

HF\big(\pi^{-1}(\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}^{x}),\pi^{-1}(\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}^{x})\big)\otimes R\cong{\rm Hom}_{MF_{1\times{\widehat{G}}}(W_{\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}}\boxminus W_{\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}})}(\Delta_{W_{\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}}}^{{\widehat{G}}\times{\widehat{G}}},\Delta_{W_{\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}}}^{{\widehat{G}}\times{\widehat{G}}}).\qed

We conclude the introduction by the following nontrivial result on the structure of Floer cohomology of
\pi^{-1}(\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}^{x})
.

Theorem C
.

HF\big(\pi^{-1}(\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}^{x}),\pi^{-1}(\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}^{x})\big)\otimes R
is a
{\widehat{G}}
-twisted commutative algebra.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the notion of twisted Jacobian algebras following
[

]
. Then we give a brief overview on categories of equivariant matrix factorizations. In particular, we investigate the structure of a kernel
\Delta_{W}^{G\times G}
for
\mathrm{Id}_{MF_{G}(W)}
in detail. We conclude Section

by showing that if we consider
(1\times G)

, we classify all closed
(1\times G)
-equivariant endomorphisms of
\Delta_{W}^{G\times G}

\Delta_{W}^{G\times G}
is a double complex and we find its closed elements by ”extending leading order terms”. In Section
4
we compare multiplications of our closed endomorphisms of
\Delta_{W}^{G\times G}
and those in
[
16
]
. In Section
5
we connect our main result to Lagrangian Floer theory and investigate an example of Floer cohomology in
T^{2}
with
\mathbb{Z}_{6}
-action.

(4)

Sometimes we just write

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, first we briefly recall the construction of twisted Jacobian algebras mainly following
[
16
]
. Then we collect some essential ingredients concerning equivariant matrix factorizations. Finally we reconstruct the underlying module of a twisted Jacobian algebra in terms of matrix factorizations.

2.1.
Twisted Jacobian algebras

Definition 2.1
.

The
Jacobian algebra
of
W\in k[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
is defined by

{\rm Jac}(W):=\frac{k[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]}{(\frac{\partial W}{\partial x_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial W}{\partial x_{n}})}.

Before introducing
G
-twisted Jacobian algebras of Landau-Ginzburg orbifolds, we need to recall the following.

Definition 2.2
.

The
n
th graded Clifford algebra
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
k
-algebra

\mathrm{Cl}_{n}:=k[\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n},\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n}],\;\;|\theta_{i}|=-1,\;\;|\partial_{i}|=1\;\;\text{for all $i$}

together with relations

(2.1)

\theta_{i}\theta_{j}=-\theta_{j}\theta_{i},\;\;\partial_{i}\partial_{j}=-\partial_{j}\partial_{i},\;\;\partial_{i}\theta_{j}=-\theta_{j}\partial_{i}+\delta_{ij}.

Notation 2.3
.

For an ordered subset
I=\{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\}\subset\{1,\cdots,n\}
, we denote

\theta_{I}:=\theta_{i_{1}}\cdots\theta_{i_{r}},\quad\partial_{I}:=\partial_{i_{1}}\cdots\partial_{i_{r}}.

Let
W
be a polynomial in variables
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
. We define a difference operator

\nabla_{j}^{x\to(x,y)}W:=\frac{W(y_{1},\cdots,y_{j},x_{j+1},\cdots,x_{n})-W(y_{1},\cdots,y_{j-1},x_{j},\cdots,x_{n})}{y_{j}-x_{j}}.

A successive application of difference operators like
\nabla_{i}^{y\to(y,z)}\nabla_{j}^{x\to(x,y)}W
for
i\leq j
means that we obtain a polynomial in variables
(x,y)
first by
\nabla_{j}^{x\to(x,y)}
, then we treat it as a polynomial only in
y
-variables(that is, consider
x
-variables as constants) and apply
\nabla_{i}^{y\to(y,z)}
.

Definition 2.4
.

[
[
16
]
]
The
G
-twisted Jacobian algebra
of a Landau-Ginzburg orbifold
(W,G)
is a
(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times G)
-graded algebra

{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,G)=\bigoplus_{h\in G}{\rm Jac}(W^{h})\cdot\xi_{h}

where
\xi_{h}
is a formal generator of degree
d_{h}\;{\rm mod}\;2
. The structure constant
\sigma_{g,h}\in{\rm Jac}(W^{gh})
for the product of
\xi_{g}
and
\xi_{h}
is given by the coefficient of
{\theta_{I_{gh}}}:=\prod_{i\in I_{gh}}{\theta_{i}}
in the following expression

(2.2)

\frac{1}{d_{g,h}!}\Upsilon\big((\lfloor H_{W}(x,g\cdot x,x)\rfloor_{gh}+\lfloor H_{W,g}(x)\rfloor_{gh}\otimes 1+1\otimes\lfloor H_{W,h}(g\cdot x)\rfloor_{gh})^{d_{g,h}}\otimes{\theta_{I_{g}}}\otimes{\theta_{I_{h}}}\big),

where

•

\displaystyle H_{W}(x,y,z):=\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\nabla_{i}^{y\to(y,z)}\nabla_{j}^{x\to(x,y)}(W)\partial_{j}\otimes\partial_{i}
.

•

\displaystyle H_{W,g}:=\sum_{i,j\in I_{g},i<j}\frac{1}{1-g_{i}}\nabla_{i}^{x\to(x,x^{g})}\nabla_{j}^{x\to(x,g\cdot x)}(W)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\in R[\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n}]
. Recall that

x_{i}^{g}=\begin{cases}x_{i}&{\rm if\;}i\in I^{g}\\
0&{\rm if\;}i\in I_{g}.\end{cases}

The operations
\nabla_{i}^{x\to(x,g\cdot x)}
and
\nabla_{i}^{x\to(x,x^{g})}
are computed from
\nabla_{i}^{x\to(x,y)}
followed by substitutions

and

, respectively.

•

\lfloor f\rfloor_{g}:=[f^{g}]\in{\rm Jac}(W^{g}).

•

d_{g,h}:=\frac{d_{g}+d_{h}-d_{gh}}{2}
. Define
\sigma_{g,h}=0
if

is not an integer.

•

The map
\Upsilon
is defined as

\Upsilon:R[\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n}]^{\otimes 2}\otimes R[{\theta_{1}},\cdots,{\theta_{n}}]^{\otimes 2}\to R[{\theta_{1}},\cdots,{\theta_{n}}],

p_{1}({\partial})\otimes p_{2}({\partial})\otimes q_{1}({\theta})\otimes q_{2}({\theta})\mapsto(-1)^{|q_{1}||p_{2}|}p_{1}(q_{1})\cdot p_{2}(q_{2}),

where
p_{i}(q_{i})
means the action of
p_{i}(\partial)
on
q_{i}(\theta)
on
k[\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}]
via (
2.1
).

The algebra in Definition

2.2.
Categories of matrix factorizations

We recall categorical invariants of singularities.

Definition 2.6
.

•

A
matrix factorization

(P,d)
of
W
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded free
R
-module
P=P^{0}\oplus P^{1}
and a degree
1
endomorphism

of

such that
d^{2}=W\cdot\mathrm{id}
.

•

For
j\in\mathbb{Z}/2
, a
morphism of degree
j
from
(P,d)
to
(Q,d^{\prime})
is an
R
-linear map

to

. The

be a group which acts on

•

]
)

[

)

(
[

]
)

We focus on a specific type of matrix factorizations. It will be used to describe kernels for identity functors of

Definition 2.12
.

Let

. Let

Then the following matrix factorization

Let

W(y)-W(x):=W(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})-W(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in S.

Denote

W(y)-W(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i}-x_{i})\nabla_{i}W,

so we can construct a Koszul matrix factorization

Theorem 4.5
.

Define an
R
-module homomorphism

\displaystyle\Phi:{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,G)

\displaystyle\to\bigoplus_{g\in G}{\rm Hom}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{g}),

\displaystyle\xi_{h}

\displaystyle\mapsto[\exp(\eta_{h^{-1}})(\theta_{I_{h^{-1}}})].

Then

(1)

\Phi
is
G
-equivariant.

(2)

\Phi(\xi_{h_{1}}\bullet\xi_{h_{2}})=h_{2}^{-1}\Phi(\xi_{h_{1}})\cup\Phi(\xi_{h_{2}}).

Proof.

Recall that

h^{\prime}\cdot\xi_{h}=\prod_{l\in I_{h}}(h^{\prime}_{l})^{-1}\cdot\xi_{h},

where the scalar
h^{\prime}_{l}\in k^{*}
is defined by
h^{\prime}\cdot x_{l}=h^{\prime}_{l}x_{l}
.

On a summand
{\rm Hom}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{h^{-1}})

h^{\prime}\cdot\theta_{I_{h^{-1}}}=\prod_{l\in I_{h^{-1}}}(h^{\prime}_{l})^{-1}\cdot\theta_{I_{h^{-1}}},

but it is clear that
I_{h^{-1}}=I_{h}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle h^{\prime}\cdot(g_{ji}^{h^{-1}}\theta_{I_{h^{-1}}\setminus\{i\}}\partial_{j})

\displaystyle=(h^{\prime}\cdot g_{ji}^{h^{-1}})\rho(h^{\prime})(\theta_{I_{h^{-1}}\setminus\{i\}}\partial_{j})

\displaystyle=h^{\prime}\cdot\Big(\frac{({\overline{W}}^{h^{-1}}_{j,i}-{\overline{W}}^{h^{-1}}_{j-1,i})-({\overline{W}}^{h^{-1}}_{j,i-1}-{\overline{W}}^{h^{-1}}_{j-1,i-1})}{(y_{j}-x_{j})(x_{i}-{h^{-1}}x_{i})}\Big)\rho(h^{\prime})(\theta_{I_{h^{-1}}\setminus\{i\}}\partial_{j})

\displaystyle=(h^{\prime}_{j}h^{\prime}_{i})^{-1}g_{ji}^{h^{-1}}\cdot\frac{\prod_{l\in I_{h^{-1}}}(h^{\prime}_{l})^{-1}}{(h^{\prime}_{i}h^{\prime}_{j})^{-1}}\theta_{I_{h^{-1}}\setminus\{i\}}\partial_{j}

\displaystyle=\prod_{l\in I_{h^{-1}}}(h^{\prime}_{l})^{-1}\cdot g_{ji}^{h^{-1}}\theta_{I_{h^{-1}}\setminus\{i\}}\partial_{j},

where the third equality comes from
G
-invariance of

By induction, we conclude that

and

for

. Then

I_{h_{1}h_{2}}=I_{h_{2}^{-1}h_{1}^{-1}}=\{1,\cdots,k,j+1,\cdots,m\}

and

d_{h_{1},h_{2}}=\frac{d_{h_{1}}+d_{h_{2}}-d_{h_{1}h_{2}}}{2}=j-\frac{k+i}{2}=j-k+\frac{k-i}{2}.

By Lemma
4.2
, the coefficient

\big\langle(h_{2}^{-1})_{*}\big(h_{2}^{-1}\cdot\exp(\eta_{h_{1}^{-1}})(\theta_{I_{h_{1}^{-1}}})\big)\cdot\exp(\eta_{h_{2}^{-1}})(\theta_{I_{h_{2}^{-1}}}),\theta_{I_{h_{2}^{-1}h_{1}^{-1}}}\big\rangle

5.
Application: Lagrangian Floer theory

The original motivation of this work was to enhance the understanding of Floer theory for representing objects of homological mirror functors. We briefly review how we obtain a mirror algebraic function (often called a
potential
) of a symplectic manifold. Let
M
be a symplectic manifold and
{\mathbb{L}}\subset M
be a Lagrangian submanifold.
Suppose that
{\mathbb{L}}
is weakly unobstructed, i.e. there is a space
\mathcal{MC}({\mathbb{L}})
of cochains of
{\mathbb{L}}
such that the
{A_{\infty}}
-structure
\{m_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
can be deformed by elements of
\mathcal{MC}({\mathbb{L}})
, so that the obstruction for
m_{1}^{2}=0
vanishes. Elements of
\mathcal{MC}({\mathbb{L}})
are called
weak bounding cochains
. The potential
W_{\mathbb{L}}:{\mathcal{MC}}({\mathbb{L}})\to k
is given by counting holomorphic discs whose boundaries are on
{\mathbb{L}}
and decorated by weak bounding cochains. Suppose that
0
is the critical value of
W_{\mathbb{L}}
. Then we can define an
{A_{\infty}}
-functor

\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{L}}:Fuk(M)\to MF(W_{\mathbb{L}})

by the curved Yoneda functor induced by
{\mathbb{L}}
. (For detail see e.g.
[
4
]
, in which
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{L}}
was named as
localized mirror functor.
)

Our primary examples are orbifold spheres
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{a,b,c}
which are quotients of Riemann surfaces by finite groups. On
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{a,b,c}
, there is an immersed Lagrangian
\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}
which is called the Seidel Lagrangian (see
[
15
]
). It was proved in
[
4
]
that
\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}
is weakly unobstructed, and the localized mirror functor
\mathcal{F}^{\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}}
is an equivalence if
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{a,b,c}
is elliptic or hyperbolic. Furthermore,
{\mathcal{MC}}(\bar{{\mathbb{L}}})
consists of linear combinations of three odd degree immersed generators. Hence the potential
W_{\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}}
is an element of
k\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket
. If
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{a,b,c}
is spherical or elliptic, then
W_{\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}}
is in
k[x,y,z]
.

Let us focus on the following three elliptic orbispheres (see Figure

1_{{\mathbb{L}}}
is isomorphic to
{\rm Jac}(W)
. It has 5 more sectors indexed by
\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{5}\in\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}
.
To describe those Floer cohomology elements more precisely, we refer to some previous works.
Let us take an example
\xi_{\rho_{2}}
, where
I_{\rho_{2}}=\{2,3\}
. By combining Proposition 4.8 and Proposition 9.1 of
[
7
]
, we conclude that
\xi_{\rho_{2}}
corresponds to the Floer cohomology element

\Phi(m_{2}(X_{2},X_{3}))=\Phi(c\cdot\bar{X_{1}})+\alpha\in HF\big(({\mathbb{L}},b),({\mathbb{L}},b)\big)\otimes R

for some

is the sum of lower degree elements.
\xi_{\rho_{1}}
corresponds to

\Phi(m_{2}(X_{1},m_{2}(X_{2},X_{3}))=\Phi(c^{\prime}\cdot pt_{\mathbb{L}})+\alpha^{\prime}\in HF\big(({\mathbb{L}},b),({\mathbb{L}},b)\big)\otimes R

for some
c^{\prime}
and
\alpha^{\prime}
. Similarly for other generators,
\xi_{\rho_{4}}
correspond to Floer cocycles whose leading order cochains are
\Phi(\bar{X_{1}})
, and
\xi_{\rho_{3}}
corresponds to the Floer cocycle whose leading order cochain is
\Phi(\bar{X_{2}})

We can check that
d_{\rho_{0},\rho_{i}}=0
(
i=1,2,3
),
d_{\rho_{1},\rho_{5}}=d_{\rho_{5},\rho_{1}}=3
,
d_{\rho_{2},\rho_{4}}=d_{\rho_{4},\rho_{2}}=d_{\rho_{3},\rho_{3}}=2
are only integers among
d_{g,h}
for

. By
d_{\rho_{0},\rho_{i}}=0
, we have a few straightforward computations:

\xi_{\rho_{0}}\bullet\xi_{\rho_{i}}=\xi_{\rho_{i}}.

Computation of
\xi_{\rho_{1}}\bullet\xi_{\rho_{5}}
,
\xi_{\rho_{3}}\bullet\xi_{\rho_{3}}
and
\xi_{\rho_{2}}\bullet\xi_{\rho_{4}}
comes from the formula (

). By braided super-commutativity, we easily deduce that

(5.2)

\xi_{\rho_{1}}\bullet\xi_{\rho_{5}}=-\xi_{\rho_{5}}\bullet\xi_{\rho_{1}},\quad\xi_{\rho_{2}}\bullet\xi_{\rho_{4}}=\xi_{\rho_{4}}\bullet(\rho_{4})^{-1}\xi_{\rho_{2}}=\xi_{\rho_{4}}\bullet\xi_{\rho_{2}}.

Actually, we can identify
\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}
-invariant elements of
{\rm Jac}^{\prime}(W,\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z})
as images of elements in
H^{*}(T^{2})
via
Kodaira-Spencer map
, which is a ring isomorphism. In particular, two odd degree elements
\xi_{\rho_{1}}
and
\xi_{\rho_{5}}
are
\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}
-invariant elements and correspond to degree 1 cohomology classes of
T^{2}
via Kodaira-Spencer map, so it is straightforward that their product is nontrivial and skew-symmetric. We refer to
[
7
]
for the detail.

Let us illustrate the computation of

Summarizing in terms of Floer cohomology, we have the following.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\bar{{\mathbb{L}}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{2,3,6}
. Let
({\mathbb{L}},b)
be as above. Then we have a decomposition

HF\big(({\mathbb{L}},b),({\mathbb{L}},b)\big)\otimes R=\bigoplus_{\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}}HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{i}},

where

\displaystyle HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{0}}\cong

\displaystyle{\rm Jac}(W)\cdot 1_{\mathbb{L}},

\displaystyle HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{1}}\cong

\displaystyle k\cdot\langle pt_{\mathbb{L}}+aX+bY+cZ\rangle,

\displaystyle HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{5}}\cong

\displaystyle k\cdot\langle pt_{\mathbb{L}}+a^{\prime}X+b^{\prime}Y+c^{\prime}Z\rangle,

\displaystyle HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{2}}\cong

\displaystyle k\cdot\langle\bar{X}+d\cdot 1_{\mathbb{L}}\rangle,

\displaystyle HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{4}}\cong

\displaystyle k\cdot\langle\bar{X}+d^{\prime}\cdot 1_{\mathbb{L}}\rangle,

\displaystyle HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{3}}\cong

\displaystyle\big(k[x_{2}]/(x_{2}^{2})\big)\cdot\langle\bar{Y}+l\cdot 1_{\mathbb{L}}\rangle,

and we have nontrivial multiplications between following pairs of components:

\displaystyle HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{0}}\otimes HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{i}}\to

\displaystyle HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{i}},

\displaystyle HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{1}}\otimes HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{5}}\to

\displaystyle HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{0}},

\displaystyle HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{2}}\otimes HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{4}}\to

\displaystyle HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{0}},

\displaystyle HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{3}}\otimes HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{3}}\to

\displaystyle HF({\mathbb{L}},{\mathbb{L}})_{\rho_{0}}.

The multiplication is super-commutative by (
5.2
).

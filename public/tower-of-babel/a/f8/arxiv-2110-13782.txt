Axial constants and sectional regularity of homogeneous ideals
M. DeBellevue, A. Lebovitz, Y. Li, M. Lotfi, S. Mohite, X. Pan, M. S. Pathak, S. Roshan Zamir, A. Seceleanu, X. Zhang

Abstract
. We introduce a notion of sectional regularity for a homogeneous ideal I I , which measures the regularity of its general sections with respect to linear spaces of various dimensions. It is related to axial constants defined as the intercepts on the coordinate axes of the set of exponents of monomials in the reverse lexicographic generic initial ideal of I I . We show the equivalence of these notions and several other homological and ideal-theoretic invariants. We also establish that these equivalent invariants grow linearly for the family of powers of a given ideal.



1.
Introduction

The generic initial ideal (gin) of a homogeneous ideal
I
of a polynomial ring captures a number of important features of
I
. When computed with respect to the reverse lexicographic monomial order,
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
retains two of the most significant measures for the homological complexity of
I
: the projective dimension and Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, as shown by Bayer and Stillman in
[
4
]
. These groundbreaking results have subsequently been refined and generalized by Bayer–Charalambous–Popescu
[
2
]
and Aramova–Herzog
[
1
]
to show that
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
retains also the extremal Betti numbers of
I
; see
1.3
for an illustration of this notion.

In this paper we enlarge the scope of the algebraic invariants which can be read off generic initial ideals by focusing on invariants termed
axial constants
, a terminology taken from
[
6
]
. These constants are defined below and have been studied implicitly by Mayes
[
18
,
19
]
, Dumnicki–Szemberg–Szpond–Tutaj-Gasińska
[
8
,
9
]
, and Chauhan–Walker
[
6
]
, from an asymptotic perspective.

Definition 1.1
.

For a homogeneous ideal
I
of the polynomial ring
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
and an integer
1\leq i\leq d
define the
i
-th
axial constant
of
I
to be

a_{i}(I)=\min\{j\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid x_{i}^{j}\in\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)\}

If no power of
x_{i}
is contained in
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
then we set
a_{i}(I)=\infty
.

Our main contribution is to point out a multitude of ways in which axial constants can be interpreted as measures of the homological complexity of
I
. For a glimpse of their significance, note that axial constants simultaneously generalize the notion of initial degree and that of Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity. Indeed, it has been noted in
[
18
,
8
,
6
]
that
a_{1}(I)
is the least degree of a nonzero element of
I
. Moreover, in characteristic zero, if the codimension of
I
is
\operatorname{ht}(I)=c
and
R/I
is Cohen-Macaulay then one has
a_{c}(I)=\operatorname{reg}(R/I)+1=\operatorname{reg}(I)
.

One aim of this paper is to vastly generalize these observations to give homological and ring-theoretic interpretations for the intermediate axial constants
a_{i}(I)
with
1<i<\operatorname{ht}(I)
. In the process we introduce the notion of
sectional regularity
for homogeneous ideals.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of a polynomial ring
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
and let
i
be an integer
0\leq i\leq d
. The
i
-th
sectional regularity
number of the ideal
I
is defined for sufficiently general linear forms
\ell_{i+1},\ldots,\ell_{d}
as

\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)=\operatorname{reg}(I+(\ell_{i+1},\ldots,\ell_{d})).

One of our main results,
4.1
, shows that there is an equality
\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)=a_{i}(I)
for
1\leq i\leq\operatorname{ht}(I)
. However sectional regularity distinguishes itself as a better behaved invariant for the range
\operatorname{ht}(I)<i\leq d
, where it remains finite, in contrast to the axial numbers. A multitude of additional invariants are shown to be equivalent to the two discussed above in
4.1
.

We provide a formula for sectional regularity in terms of generic annihilator numbers
\alpha_{ij}(R/I)
. This notion, introduced in
[
1
]
, is defined as follows: for a module
M
set
M^{(i)}=M/(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{i-1})
, where
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{d}
are linear forms having the property

\alpha_{ij}(M)=\dim_{k}(0:_{M^{(i)}}\ell_{i})_{j}<\infty\text{ for }0\leq i\leq d-1,j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Such linear forms exist by the theory of almost regular sequences introduced in
[
21
]
and developed in
[
1
]
, among others. We rely heavily on this useful tool following in the footsteps of similar applications in
[
22
,
16
]
. We establish in
3.7
the identity

\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)=\max\{j\mid\alpha_{tj}(R/I)\neq 0,\ t\geq d-i\}+1.

This allows us to formulate a theory of
coextremal annihilator numbers
which complements the more familiar notion of extremal annihilator numbers from
[
1
]
. In detail, we call
\alpha_{ij}(R/I)
a coextremal annihilator number if it satisfies
\alpha_{ij}(R/I)\neq 0
and

\alpha_{st}(R/I)=0\text{ for all pairs }(s,t)\neq(i,j)\text{ with }s\geq i,t\geq j.

An coextremal annihilator number sits at a south-east corner of the region formed by the nonzero entries in the table of annihilator numbers. For comparison, an extremal annihilator number satisfies
\alpha_{ij}(R/I)\neq 0
and
\alpha_{st}(R/I)=0
for
(s,t)\neq(i,j),s\leq i,t\geq j
, thus marking a south-west corner. We illustrate these notions in connection to axial constants in the following example.

.

Beyond the setting of monomial ideals

extends the correspondence illustrated in

to a correspondence between coextremal annihilator numbers and axial constants of arbitrary homogeneous ideals given by

\alpha_{d-i,j}(R/I)\text{ is coextremal}\iff a_{i-1}(I)<a_{i}(I)=j+1.

This correspondence does not pin down the values of the coextremal annihilator numbers. It would be an interesting pursuit to understand what these values measure.

We now preview the organization of our paper and its main results. After providing necessary background on generic initial ideals in section

3.9
. Section
4

\operatorname{reg}(I^{n})=dn+e

that the similar result does indeed hold.

2.
Background

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of a polynomial ring
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
. Throughout this paper we assume that
k
is an infinite field, which is a necessary condition for generic initial ideals to be well-defined. The remarkable notion of generic initial ideals was introduced by Galligo in characteristic zero
[
12
]
and Bayer and Stillman in arbitrary characteristic
[
4
]
.

Fix a monomial order on
R
so that
x_{1}>x_{2}>\cdots>x_{d}
. A monomial order that will be used predominantly in this paper is the graded reverse lexicographic order, for which
x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{a_{d}}>x_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{b_{d}}
if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}>\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{i}
or
\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{i}
and for the largest index
i
so that
a_{i}\neq b_{i}
we have
a_{i}<b_{i}
.
The largest monomial with respect to a fixed order among those whose coefficients are nonzero in a polynomial
f
is called the
leading term
of
f
and denoted
in(f)
. The initial ideal of
I
with respect to this order is

\operatorname{in}(I)=\left(\operatorname{in}(f)\mid f\in I\right).

The natural action of the general linear group
\operatorname{GL}_{d}(k)
on
R_{1}\cong k^{d}
extends to an action on
R
via
g\cdot f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=f(\sum_{j=1}^{d}g_{1j}x_{j},\ldots,\sum_{j=1}^{d}g_{dj}x_{j})
if
g=(g_{ij})
. Thus each element of
g\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}(k)
yields an automorphism of
R
, which we call a linear change of coordinates. We denote the image of
I
under the corresponding automorphims by
g\cdot I
. The generic initial ideal of
I
is obtained by first applying to
I
a sufficiently general linear change of coordinates followed by taking the initial ideal. The details of the construction of the generic initial ideal are as follows.

Definition 2.1

(
[
4
,
12
]
)
.

Fix a homogeneous ideal
I
and a monomial order on
R
. There exists a nonempty Zariski open set of
U_{gin}\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{d}(k)
so that
\operatorname{in}(g\cdot I)
does not depend on
g
for
g\in U_{gin}(I)
. The ideal
\operatorname{in}(g\cdot I)
for
g\in U_{gin}(I)
is termed the
generic initial ideal
of
I
and denoted
\operatorname{gin}(I)
.

Whenever we refer to the generic initial ideal of a homogeneous ideal
I
with respect to the graded reverse lexicographic order, we use the notation
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
instead of
\operatorname{gin}(I)
.

A monomial ideal
J
is termed
strongly stable
provided that whenever
i<j
and
\mu
is a monomial such that
\mu x_{j}\in J
, we have
\mu x_{i}\in J
as well. A monomial ideal is called
Borel-fixed
if it is fixed by the action of the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices of
GL_{d}(K)
. If
\operatorname{char}(k)=0
the notions of Borel fixed and strongly stable are equivalent. In positive characteristic, strongly stable implies Borel fixed but not conversely. Borel fixed ideals which are not strongly stable appear in
4.2
and
4.3
.

Definition 2.2
.

Recall that the Hilbert function of an
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded module
M
over a graded
k
-algebra
R
is the function
H(M):{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
given by
H(M)(i)=\dim_{k}M_{i}
.
The
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
of
M
is

\operatorname{reg}(M)=\max\{j-i\mid{\rm Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,k)_{j}\neq 0\}
.
When
M
has finite length this invariant can also be expressed as
\operatorname{reg}(M)=\max\{i\mid H(M)(i)\neq 0\}
.

It is useful to recall several properties of generic initial ideals.

Remark 2.3

(Properties of gins)
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of
R=k[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
.

(1)

[
10
, Theorem 15.26]
The Hilbert functions of
I
and
\operatorname{gin}(I)
agree:
H(I)=H(\operatorname{gin}(I))
.

(2)

[
3
]
The regularity of
I
and
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
agree:
\operatorname{reg}(I)=\operatorname{reg}(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I))

(3)

[
10
, Corollary 15.25]

If
P\in\operatorname{Ass}(R/\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I))
, then
P=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j})
and
\operatorname{ht}(I)\leq j\leq d-\operatorname{depth}(I)
.

(4)

[
12
,
4
]

\operatorname{gin}(I)
is Borel fixed

(5)

[
12
]

[
14
, Proposition 4.2.4]
If
\operatorname{char}(k)=0
or
\operatorname{char}(k)
is larger than any exponent appearing in the monomial generators of
\operatorname{gin}(I)
, then
\operatorname{gin}(I)
is strongly stable.

3.
Sectional regularity

In this section we develop the notion of sectional regularity introduced in
1.2
. The name is meant to suggest that this invariant captures the regularity of a general linear subspace section of a given quotient ring or module. It bears some similarities to the notion of partial regularity introduced by Trung in
[
22
]
, which in turn is equivalent to the
\ell
-regularity from the work of Bayer–Charalambous–Popescu
[
2
]
. However we emphasize that these invariants are not the same as the sectional regularity introduced here. Sectional regularity has been previously considered from a geometric perspective in
[
5
]
and in subsequent work deriving from this source.

A main goal of this section is to establish well-definedness of sectional regularity for a homogeneous ideal
I
as introduced in
1.2
. This entails demonstrating the existence of a nonempty Zariski open subset
U_{sreg}(I)\subset GL_{d}(k)
so that for each
\gamma=(g_{ij})\in U_{sreg}(I)
, setting
\ell_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}g_{ij}x_{j}
yields the same value for the integer
\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)=\operatorname{reg}(I+(\ell_{i+1},\ldots,\ell_{d}))
. This will be shown in
3.5
.

A useful tool in our work is the notion of almost regular sequence. This was introduced under the name of filter regular sequence in
[
21
]
and further developed in
[
1
]
. Here we follow the presentation in
[
14
, §4.3.1]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated module over the polynomial ring
R=k[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
. An element
\ell\in R

is an
almost regular element
on
M
if the module
(0:_{M}\ell)=\{m\in M\mid\ell m=0\}
is a finite dimensional
k
-vector space.

A sequence of elements
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{s}
in
R
is an
almost regular sequence
on
M
if for each
i
the element
\ell_{i+1}
is almost regular on
M/\left(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{i}\right)
.

We will be interested in almost regular sequences consisting of linear forms. In the case of cyclic quotients by generic initial ideals, a canonical example of an almost regular sequence is given by the sequence of variables.

Example 3.2

(
[
14
, Proposition 4.3.3]
)
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal over
R=k[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
. Then
x_{d},\ldots,x_{1}
is almost regular on
R/\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
.

Since the above example is fundamental to this work, in order to keep the notation closely aligned with the example, we shall use regular sequences indexed descendingly, that is, we shall work with elements
\ell_{d},\ldots,\ell_{1}
which form an
almost regular sequence
on a module
M
. By
3.1
this means that
\ell_{i-1}
is almost regular on
M/\left(\ell_{d},\ldots,\ell_{i})\right)
for each
2\leq i\leq d
. Whenever we cite results from the literature such as
3.3
below we adapt the indexing to match this convention.

A crucial fact about almost regular sequence is that, given a module
M
, a sufficiently general
k
-basis of
R_{1}
yields an almost regular sequence of linear forms on
M
.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
14
, Theorem 4.3.6]
)
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded module over
R=k[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
.
To each
\gamma=(g_{ij})\in\operatorname{GL}_{d}(k)
we associate the sequence
\ell_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}g_{ij}x_{j}
with
1\leq i\leq d
. Then there exists a nonempty Zariski open subset
U_{ar}(M)\subset\operatorname{GL}_{d}(k)
such that
\ell_{d},\ldots,\ell_{1}
is almost regular on
M
for all
\gamma\in U_{ar}
.

In particular, there exists a
k
-basis of
R_{1}
which is an almost regular sequence on M.

One can use almost regular sequences of linear forms to compute regularity.

Lemma 3.4

(
[
10
, Proposition 20.20]
)
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded module over the ring
R=k[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
.
If
\ell\in R_{1}
is almost regular on
M
then

\operatorname{reg}(M)=\max\{\operatorname{reg}\left(M/(\ell)\right),\operatorname{reg}(0:_{M}\ell)\}.

Together these facts allow us to relate the regularity of an ideal and that of its generic initial ideal modulo appropriate almost regular sequences.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of a polynomial ring
R=k[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
. Then there exists a nonempty Zariski open subset
U_{sreg}(I)\subset GL_{d}(k)
so that for each
\gamma=(g_{ij})
in
U_{sreg}(I)
setting
\ell_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}g_{ij}x_{j}
yields that
\ell_{d},\ldots,\ell_{1}
is an almost regular sequence on
R/I
. Moreover, for all
0\leq i\leq d-1
, one has

H\left(I+(\ell_{i+1},\ldots,\ell_{d})\right)=H\left(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d})\right).

\operatorname{reg}\left(I+(\ell_{i+1},\ldots,\ell_{d})\right)=\operatorname{reg}\left(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d})\right).

Proof.

We set
U_{gin}^{-1}(I)=\{\gamma\in GL_{d}(k)\mid\gamma^{-1}\in U_{gin}(I)\}
, where
U_{gin}(I)
is the Zariski open set in
2.1
. Since
U_{gin}(I)
is an open set, so is
U_{gin}^{-1}(I)
; see
[
14
, Lemma 4.3.8]
. Set
U_{sreg}(I)=U_{gin}^{-1}(I)\cap U_{ar}(R/I)
, where
U_{ar}
is the Zariski open set in
3.3
. Since
\gamma\in U_{ar}(R/I)
, the sequence
\ell_{d},\ldots,\ell_{1}
is almost regular on
R/I
.

We proceed to establish the claimed identities. The isomorphisms

\displaystyle I+(\ell_{i+1},\cdots,\ell_{d})\cong\gamma^{-1}(I+(\ell_{i+1},\cdots,\ell_{d}))=\gamma^{-1}(I)+(x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{d})

give the first equality in the sequence

\displaystyle H(I+(\ell_{i+1},\cdots,\ell_{d}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(\gamma^{-1}(I)+(x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{d}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(\operatorname{in}(\gamma^{-1}(I)+(x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{d})))\quad\text{ by }\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:properties}\text{ (1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(\operatorname{in}(\gamma^{-1}(I))+(x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{d}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)+(x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{d}))\qquad\text{ since }\gamma^{-1}\in U_{gin}(I).

As a byproduct of this equality we obtain another useful identity. Set

M^{(i)}=R/(I+(\ell_{i+1},\cdots,\ell_{d})),\quad N^{(i)}=R/(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)+(x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{d}))

and note that
H(M^{(i)})=H(N^{(i)})
as shown above.
Then the exact sequences

0\to(0:_{M^{(i)}}\ell_{i})(-1)\to M^{(i)}(-1)\xrightarrow{\cdot\ell_{i}}M^{(i)}\to M^{(i-1)}\to 0

0\to(0:_{N^{(i)}}x_{i})(-1)\to N^{(i)}(-1)\xrightarrow{\cdot x_{i}}N^{(i)}\to N^{(i-1)}\to 0

and the equalities
H(M^{(i)})=H(N^{(i)})
yield
H(0:_{M^{(i)}}\ell_{i})=H(0:_{N^{(i)}}x_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq d
. Since the regularity of the finite length modules
(0:_{M^{(i)}}\ell_{i})
and
(0:_{N^{(i)}}x_{i})
is determined by their Hilbert function it follows that
\operatorname{reg}(0:_{M^{(i)}}\ell_{i})=\operatorname{reg}(0:_{N^{(i)}}x_{i})
.

We now establish the claim regarding regularity by induction on
i
. The case
i=0
holds true because
M^{(0)}=N^{(0)}=k
.
3.4
(1) cast in terms of our current notation, where
\ell_{i}
is almost regular on
M^{(i)}
and
M^{(i-1)}=M^{(i)}/(\ell_{i})
, yields

(3.1)

\operatorname{reg}(M^{(i)})=\max\left\{\operatorname{reg}(M^{(i-1)}),\operatorname{reg}(0:_{M^{(i)}}\ell_{i})\right\}

and similarly

\operatorname{reg}(N^{(i)})=\max\left\{\operatorname{reg}(N^{(i-1)}),\operatorname{reg}(0:_{N^{(i)}}x_{i})\right\}.

The inductive hypothesis guarantees
\operatorname{reg}(M^{(i-1)})=\operatorname{reg}(N^{(i-1)})
and prior considerations yield
\operatorname{reg}(0:_{M^{(i)}}\ell_{i})=\operatorname{reg}(0:_{N^{(i)}}x_{i})
, therefore the above identities imply
\operatorname{reg}(M^{(i)})=\operatorname{reg}(N^{(i)})
. From these equalities the last claim follows.
∎

We now present an alternate formula for the sectional regularity. This can be formulated in terms of generic annihilator numbers, which we now define.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of a polynomial ring
R
with
\dim(R)=d
and let
\ell_{d},\ldots,\ell_{1}
be an almost regular sequence for
R/I
. Let
M^{(j)}=R/(I+(\ell_{j+1},\ldots,\ell_{d}))
and define

\alpha_{ij}(R/I)=\dim_{k}(0:_{M^{(d-i)}}\ell_{d-i})_{j}\text{ for }0\leq i\leq d-1

to be the
generic annihilator numbers
of
R/I
. We do not specify values for annihilator numbers if
i=d
; although these appear in the literature (see
[
1
]
) they are not relevant here.

Corollary 3.7
.

Continuing with the notation in
3.6

one has

\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)=\max\{\operatorname{reg}(0:_{M^{(t)}}\ell_{t})\mid t\leq i\}+1=\max\{j\mid\alpha_{sj}(R/I)\neq 0,s\geq d-i\}+1.

Consequently the sequence of sectional regularity numbers is nondecreasing, that is,

\operatorname{sreg}_{1}(I)\leq\operatorname{sreg}_{2}(I)\leq\ldots\leq\operatorname{sreg}_{d}(I).

Proof.

Applying equation (
3.1
) repeatedly yields

\operatorname{reg}(M^{(i)})=\max\left\{\operatorname{reg}\left(M^{(0)}\right),\operatorname{reg}\left(0:_{M^{(t)}}\ell_{t}\right)\mid t\leq i\right\}.

Since
M^{(0)}=k
, and
\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)=\operatorname{reg}(M^{(i)})+1
by definition, the first claimed equality follows.
The second is obtained by noting that

\operatorname{reg}\left(0:_{M^{(d-t)}}\ell_{d-t}\right)=\max\{j\mid\alpha_{tj}(R/I)\neq 0\}
.
Either one of the descriptions for
\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)
obtained above yields that this sequence is non-decreasing.
∎

We now introduce a new definition of coextremal annihilator numbers.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of a polynomial ring
R
. A generic annihilator number
\alpha_{ij}
as defined in
3.6
is called a
coextremal generic annihilator number
if it satisfies
\alpha_{ij}(R/I)\neq 0
and
\alpha_{st}(R/I)=0
for all pairs
(s,t)\neq(i,j)
with
s\geq i,t\geq j
.

The following result specifies the meaning of the coextremal annihilator numbers.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of a polynomial ring
R
with
\dim(R)=d
.

If
\alpha_{d-i,j}(R/I)
is a coextremal generic annihilator number then
\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)=j+1
.
Moreover, if
\operatorname{sreg}_{i-1}(I)<\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)=j+1
then
\alpha_{d-i,j}(R/I)
is a coextremal annihilator number.

Consequently the coextremal annihilator numbers control both the jumps and the values attained by the sequence

\operatorname{sreg}_{1}(I)\leq\operatorname{sreg}_{2}(I)\leq\ldots\leq\operatorname{sreg}_{d}(I).

Proof.

Suppose
\alpha_{d-i,j}(R/I)
is a coextremal generic annihilator number. Then from
3.8
it follows that
\max\{j\mid\alpha_{st}(R/I)\neq 0,s\geq d-i\}=j
. Combining this with
3.7
yields
\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)=j+1
.

Suppose now that we have
\operatorname{sreg}_{i-1}(I)<\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)=j+1
. This leads to the inequality

\operatorname{sreg}_{i-1}(I)=\max\{\operatorname{reg}(0:_{M^{(u)}}\ell_{u})\mid u\leq i-1\}+1<\max\{\operatorname{reg}(0:_{M^{(u)}}\ell_{u})\mid u\leq i\}+1=\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I),

which forces
\operatorname{reg}(0:_{M^{(i)}}\ell_{i})>\operatorname{reg}(0:_{M^{(u)}}\ell_{u})
for each
u<i
. Casting this in terms of generic annihilator numbers yields

j=\max\{t\mid\alpha_{d-i,t}(R/I)\neq 0\}>\max\{t\mid\alpha_{d-u,t}(R/I)\neq 0\text{ for some }u<i\},

which implies
\alpha_{st}(R/I)=0
for
(s,t)\neq(d-i,j),s\geq d-i,t\geq j
, that is,
\alpha_{d-i,j}(R/I)
is coextremal.

Finally, to justify how the coextremal annihilator numbers control the sequence of sectional regularities it suffices to see that the distinct values in the sequence are given by

\{\operatorname{sreg}_{1}(I),\operatorname{sreg}_{2}(I),\ldots,\operatorname{sreg}_{d}(I)\}=\{j+1\mid\alpha_{ij}(R/I)\text{ is extremal for some }i\}

and the jumps are given by
\operatorname{sreg}_{i-1}(I)<\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)
if and only if there exists
j
such that
\alpha_{d-i,j}(R/I)
is a coextremal annihilator number. Due to its monotonicity, the sequence of sectional regularities is completely determined by these two pieces of information.
∎

Finally, we discuss an alternate approach to almost regular sequences that uses generic linear forms, that is, linear forms whose coefficients are variables in an extension field of the coefficient field of
R

same
set of such forms gives an almost regular sequence on

finitely generated
R
-module. Therefore this approach is better suited to arguments where

Notation 3.10
.

Let
u_{ij}
be distinct variables with
1\leq i,j\leq d
, set

to be the fraction field of

, and
R^{\prime}=F\otimes_{k}R=F[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
. Additionally, for any
R
-module

and for

The following is the analogue of
3.5
for generic forms.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of a polynomial ring
R=k[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
. According to
3.10
, let
I^{\prime}=IR^{\prime}
and set

L_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}u_{ij}x_{j}
. Then
L_{d},\ldots,L_{1}
is an almost regular sequence on

and the following identities hold for each

H\left(I^{\prime}+(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d})\right)=H\left(\operatorname{gin}(I)+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d})\right).

\operatorname{reg}\left(I^{\prime}+(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d})\right)=\operatorname{reg}\left(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d})\right).

Proof.

It is shown e. g. in
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
that for any
R
-module
M
, the sequence
L_{d},\ldots,L_{1}
and any permutation thereof is almost regular on
M^{\prime}
.

Set
\phi:R^{\prime}\to R^{\prime}
be the automorphism sending
L_{i}\mapsto x_{i}
. To be precise, consider the matrix
(v_{ij})=(u_{ij})^{-1}
with entries in
F
. Then
\phi
is defined by
\phi(x_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{d}v_{ij}x_{j}
. We claim that
\operatorname{in}(\phi(I))=\operatorname{gin}(I)R^{\prime}
.

Suppose
\operatorname{in}(\phi(I^{\prime}))=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{s})
with
m_{i}=\operatorname{in}(f_{i})
for some polynomials
f_{i}\in I^{\prime}
and let
c_{i}
be the coefficient of
m_{i}
in

. Then
c_{i}\in F
are rational functions of
u_{ij}
. Consider the Zariski open set
U^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{d^{2}}_{k}
where none of the denominators and numerators of the functions

vanish and set
U=U_{gin}^{-1}(I)\cap U^{\prime}
.
Let
\gamma=(g_{ij})\in U
and
e_{\gamma}:R^{\prime}\to R
the evaluation homomorphism that maps

. Then

for any

. The membership

shows there is a containment
\operatorname{in}(\phi(I))\subseteq\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)R^{\prime}
. Since there are equalities

H(\operatorname{in}(\phi(I)))=H(\phi(I^{\prime}))=H(I^{\prime})=H(I)=H(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I))=H(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)R^{\prime}),

the preceding containment is in fact an equality.

Set
M^{(i)}=R/(I^{\prime}+(L_{i+1},\cdots,L_{d}))
and

Then

\displaystyle H(M^{(i)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(\phi(M^{(i)}))=H\left(R/(\phi(I^{\prime})+(x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{d}))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\left(R/\operatorname{in}(\phi(I^{\prime})+(x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{d}))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\left(R/\operatorname{in}(\phi(I^{\prime}))+(x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{d})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\left(R/(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)R^{\prime}+(x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{d})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(N^{(i)}).

Now the proof of

can be repeated verbatim to deduce

∎

4.
Equivalent invariants

In this section we offer multiple equivalent characterizations for the notion of sectional regularity, establishing in particular the equivalence of sectional regularity and axial constants. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of a polynomial ring
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
with
k
an infinite field of characteristic zero or larger than any exponent appearing in the generators of the reverse lexicographic generic initial ideal
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
. The following are equal:

(1)

the
i
-th sectional regularity number
\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)
(see
1.2
)

(2)

\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I))=\operatorname{reg}(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}))

(3)

the largest
i
-th partial degree of a minimal generator of
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
(see
4.10
)

(4)

the
i
-th axial constant

(see
1.1
), provided
a_{i}(I)<\infty

(5)

\operatorname{red}_{d-i}(R/I)+1

is an
s
-reduction number for
R/I
(see
4.14
), provided
\operatorname{red}_{d-i}(R/I)<\infty

Moreover, the quantities listed in (1) and (2) and, separately, those listed in (4) and (5) are equal without any restrictions on characteristic.

Proof.

This result collects together
3.5
, which shows (1)=(2),
4.12
, which shows (2)=(3),

4.7
, which shows (2)=(4), and
4.16
, which shows (4)=(5).
The hypothesis on the characteristic ensures that

is strongly stable; see
[
4
, Proposition 2.7]
or
[

, Proposition 4.2.4]
. This technical condition is needed in the proofs of

and

and

. This justifies the last claim regarding those equalities that are independent of the characteristic of the base field.
∎

The hypothesis on the characteristic of the base field in

is needed, as illustrated by the next examples. In these examples we employ gradings of the polynomial ring termed partial gradings, denoted
\deg_{i}
, and given by
\deg_{i}(x_{j})=1
if

and
\deg_{i}(x_{j})=0
for

Example 4.2
.

Consider the ideal

, where
\operatorname{char}(k)=p
.
It is easy to see that

and that this ideal is not strongly stable, however it is Borel-fixed. Moreover, one has

•

a_{i}(I)=p
for each
1\leq i\leq d

•

\operatorname{sreg}_{I}(I)=\operatorname{reg}(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}))=\operatorname{reg}(x_{1}^{p},\ldots,x_{i}^{p},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d})=i(p-1)+1

•

the largest
i
-th partial degree of a minimal generator of
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
is
p

•

the

-reduction numbers are
\operatorname{red}_{s}(R/I)=(p-1)(d-s)
cf.
[

Thus here the axial constants agree with the the largest
i
-th partial degree of a minimal generator of
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
and the sectional regularity numbers satisfy
\operatorname{sreg}(I)=\operatorname{red}_{d-i}(R/I)+1
. However the axial constant and the sectional regularity disagree when

.

In the previous example the connection between axial constants and the largest

-th partial degree of a minimal generator of

appears more robust than we prove in

. However the next example shows that our result is in fact the best possible.

Example 4.3
.

Let

be a prime integer and set

if

.
Consider the ideal

, where

Then

, the axial constants are

for

but the largest partial degree of a minimal generator of

We now proceed to show the equivalences between the families of constants in

.

4.1.
Axial constants

To study the axial constants, we first establish some useful properties for the sequence formed by these invariants.

Remark 4.4
.

From
4.1
and
3.7
one deduces that if
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
is a strongly stable ideal, the axial constants form a non-decreasing sequence

a_{1}(I)\leq a_{2}(I)\leq\cdots\leq a_{d}(I).

However the sequence of axial constants is usually unbounded. We proceed by delineating the range in which the axial constants are finite.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal and assume
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
is strongly stable. Then
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
if and only if
1\leq i\leq\operatorname{ht}(I)
.

Proof.

According to
1.1
,
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
if and only
x_{i}\in\sqrt{\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)}
. Since
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
is strongly stable, the associated primes of
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
are of the form
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j})
with
\operatorname{ht}I\leq j
; see
2.3
(3). Therefore we have

\sqrt{\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)}=\bigcap_{P\in\operatorname{Ass}(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I))}P=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\operatorname{ht}(I)})

and consequently
x_{i}\in\sqrt{\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)}
if and only if
1\leq i\leq\operatorname{ht}(I)
. The conclusion now follows.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose
J
is a strongly stable monomial ideal of a polynomial ring
R
. Then the monomial
x_{i}^{j}
is in
J
if and only if
(J+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}))_{j}=R_{j}.

Proof.

The “if” direction is clear. For the “only if” direction, suppose
x_{i}^{j}\in J
. We show that
L=J+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d})
contains all monomials of degree
j
from which the desired statement follows. Let
m
be a monomial of degree
j.
If
x_{k}
divides
m
for some
k>i
then it immediately follows that
m\in L
. Otherwise
m
is expressed in terms of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}
, and as
x_{i}^{j}\in J
one can apply the strongly stable exchange property repeatedly to
x_{i}^{j}
to obtain that
m\in J\subseteq L
.
∎

Now we are ready to give a homological characterization of the axial constants. The alert reader will realize that the next result justifies the equality
a_{i}(I)=\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)
for
1\leq i\leq\operatorname{ht}(I)
advertised in
4.1
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of a polynomial ring and assume that
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
is strongly stable. Then

(1)

a_{i}(I)=\operatorname{reg}(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}))
provided
a_{i}(I)<\infty
.

(2)

there exists a nonempty Zariski open subset
U\subset GL_{d}(k)
so that for each
\gamma=(g_{ij})\in U
setting
\ell_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}g_{ij}x_{j}
yields

a_{i}(I)=\operatorname{reg}(I+(\ell_{i+1},\ldots,\ell_{d}))
.

(3)

setting
F={\rm Frac}(k[u_{ij}])
,
R^{\prime}=F[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
and
I^{\prime}=IR^{\prime}
and considering the generic linear forms
L_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}u_{ij}x_{j}
yields

a_{i}(I)=\operatorname{reg}(I^{\prime}+(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d}))
.

Proof.

(1) By
4.5
,
a_{i}(I)
is finite if and only if
1\leq i\leq\operatorname{ht}(I)
. Under this assumption,
R/\left(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d})\right)
is a zero-dimensional ring, which implies that

\operatorname{reg}(\operatorname{gin}(I)+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}))=\min\{j\mid\operatorname{gin}(I)+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}))_{j}=R_{j}\}.

The desired conclusion now follows by means of
4.6
.

Assertions (2) and (3) follow from (1) by means of
3.5
and
3.11
.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

The first axial constant is easily seen from
1.1
to be the least degree of an element of
I
. Assuming
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
is strongly stable and
\dim(R/I)=\operatorname{depth}(R/I)
, we now compute the largest finite axial constant,

, where

. By

a_{c}(I)=\operatorname{reg}(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)+(x_{c+1},\ldots,x_{d}))=\operatorname{reg}(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I))=\operatorname{reg}(I),

where the middle equality holds because
2.3
(4) ensures that
x_{c+1},\ldots,x_{d}
is a regular sequence on

, and the last equality follows from
2.3
(3).

Given the close relationship between sectional regularity numbers and generic annihilator numbers observed in section
3
, we are now able to characterize axial constants in terms of the coextremal annihilator numbers.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of a polynomial ring
R
with
\dim(R)=d
and
\operatorname{ht}(I)=c
. Then, setting
a_{0}(I)=-\infty
we have
a_{i-1}(I)<a_{i}(I)=j+1<\infty
if and only if
\alpha_{d-i,j}(R/I)
is coextremal and moreover

\{a_{1}(I),a_{2}(I),\ldots,a_{c}(I)\}=\{j\mid\alpha_{i,j}(R/I)\text{ is coextremal for some }i\}.

Thus the coextremal generic annihilator numbers control both the jumps and the values attained by the sequence

a_{1}(I)\leq a_{2}(I)\leq\ldots\leq a_{c}(I)
.

Proof.

It follows from
4.7
that
a_{i}(I)=\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I)
. Therefore this result transcribes the findings of
3.9
in the case when the two invariants agree.
∎

4.2.
Partial degrees

It is well known that the regularity of a strongly stable ideal is given by the largest degree of a minimal generator. In this section we generalize this framework to capture the sectional regularity of a strongly stable ideal in terms of the largest partial degree of a minimal generator. We now proceed to define the notion of partial degree.

Definition 4.10
.

Given a monomial
\mu=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\ldots x_{d}^{a_{d}}
and an index
1\leq i\leq d
we define the
i
-th
partial degree
of
\mu
to be
\deg_{i}(\mu)=\sum_{j\leq i}a_{j}
. This yields a grading on the polynomial ring
k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
, which we also denote
\deg_{i}
.

Note that
\deg_{d}
is the standard grading. Monomial ideals are homogeneous with respect to
\deg_{i}
for each
i
.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
J
be a monomial ideal of a polynomial ring
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
. For each
1\leq i\leq d
we define

\omega_{i}(J)=\max\{\deg_{i}(\mu)\mid\mu\text{ a minimal monomial generator of }J\}
.

It is well-known that the regularity of strongly stable ideals is equal to the largest among the degrees of their minimal generators. Below we establish a version of this fact for sectional regularity.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
J
be a strongly stable monomial ideal of a polynomial ring. Then for
1\leq i\leq d
one has

\operatorname{reg}(J+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}))=\omega_{i}(J)
.

Proof.

Let
L
be the ideal generated by the minimal monomial generators of
J
that are not divisible by
x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}
. Since
J
is assumed strongly stable, so is
L
. Therefore by means of the Eliahou-Kervaire resolution
[
11
]
there is an equality
\operatorname{reg}(L)=\omega_{i}(L)
.

The identity

J+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}))=L+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}))

yields

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\left(J+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\left(L+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\left(L\right)=\omega_{i}(L),

where the second equality follows because
x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}
form a regular sequence on
L
.
It remains to show that
\omega_{i}(J)=\omega_{i}(L)
in order to complete the proof.

Suppose
\mu=x_{1}^{a_{1}},\ldots,x_{i}^{a_{i}}\mu^{\prime}
is a monomial minimal generator of
J
so that
\mu^{\prime}\not\in(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})
and
\deg_{i}(\mu)=\omega_{i}(J)
. We show
\mu\in L
, equivalently,
\deg(\mu^{\prime})=0
.
Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
\deg(\mu^{\prime})>0
. Then, applying the strongly stable property, yields

x_{1}^{a_{1}},\ldots,x_{i}^{a_{i}+1}\mu^{\prime}/x_{j}\in J

for some
j>i
so that
x_{j}\mid\mu^{\prime}
. The displayed monomial is a multiple of a minimal generator

\mu^{\prime\prime}=x_{1}^{b_{1}},\ldots,x_{i}^{b_{i}}\mu^{\prime\prime\prime}\in J,

where
b_{k}\leq a_{k}
for
1\leq k\leq i-1
,
b_{i}\leq a_{i}+1
, and
\mu^{\prime\prime\prime}\mid\mu^{\prime}/x_{j}.
If
b_{i}\leq a_{i}
then
\mu
is a multiple of
\mu^{\prime\prime}
contradicting minimality of
\mu
. Thus
b_{i}=a_{i}+1
and since
\mu
has the largest
\deg_{i}
among the minimal generators of
J
we must have
b_{1}+\ldots b_{i-1}<a_{1}+\ldots+a_{i-1}.
So, at least one of the exponents
b_{k}
satisfies
b_{k}<a_{k}
. Applying the strongly stable property to
\mu^{\prime\prime}
gives that

x_{1}^{b_{1}}\ldots x_{k}^{b_{k}+1}\ldots x_{i}^{a_{i}}\mu^{\prime\prime\prime}\in J

Since
\mu
is a multiple of the above monomial, this contradicts the minimality of
\mu
and finishes the proof.
∎

As a consequence of
4.12
and
4.7
we can now give a new characterization of the axial constants in terms of partial degrees.

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of a polynomial ring and assume that
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
is strongly stable. Then for each
i
such that
a_{i}(I)<\infty
we have
a_{i}(I)=\omega_{i}(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I))
.

4.3.
s
-reduction numbers

The relationship between regularity and reduction numbers is well known starting with pioneering work in
[
23
]
. The
s
-reduction numbers were introduced in
[
20
]
. The fact that reduction numbers remain unchanged under taking generic initial ideals was shown in
[
22
]
and their interpretation for Borel-fixed ideals recalled in
4.15
was introduced in
[
16
]
.

Definition 4.14
.

Let
A
be a standard graded algebra over an infinite field
k
. An ideal
{\mathfrak{q}}=(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{s})
, where
\ell_{i}
are linear forms of
A
, is called an
s
-reduction
of
A
(or of the homogeneous maximal ideal of
A
) if
{\mathfrak{q}}_{t}=A_{t}
for
t\gg 0
. The
reduction number
of
A
with respect to
{\mathfrak{q}}
is

r_{\mathfrak{q}}(A)=\min\{t\mid{\mathfrak{q}}_{t+1}=A_{t+1}\}.

The
s
-reduction number
of
A
is defined as

r_{s}(A)=\min\{r_{\mathfrak{q}}(A)\mid{\mathfrak{q}}=(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{s})\text{ is a reduction of }A\}.

It is not hard to see that any
s
-reduction of
A
satisfies
s\geq\dim(A)
. If
s=\dim(A)
then
r(A):=r_{s}(A)
is called the reduction number of
A
. If
s<\dim(A)
then we set
r_{s}(A)=\infty
by convention.

The following characterization for the
s
-reduction numbers of strongly stable monomial ideals can be found in
[
16
]
.

Lemma 4.15

(
[
16
, Corollary 1.4]
)
.

Let
J
be a Borel fixed monomial ideal of a polynomial ring of dimension
d
. For any
s\geq\dim(R/J)
we have

r_{s}(R/J)=\min\{t\mid x_{d-s}^{t+1}\in I\}
.

Now we can give a characterization of the axial constants in terms of
s
-reduction numbers.

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of a polynomial ring
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
. For any
0\leq i\leq d
we have

a_{i}(I)=r_{d-i}(R/I)+1.

Proof.

From
4.5
and the above considerations it follows that
a_{i}(I)=\infty
if and only if
i>\operatorname{ht}(I)
if and only if
d-i<\dim(R/I)
if and only if
r_{d-i}(R/I)=\infty
.

Now assume
i\leq\operatorname{ht}(I)
, whence
d-i\geq\dim(R/I)
. Since
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
is a strongly stable monomial ideal,
4.15
gives

r_{d-i}(R/\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I))=\min\{t\mid x_{i}^{t+1}\in\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)\}=a_{i}(I)-1.

It was shown in
[
22
]
that
r_{s}(R/I)=r_{s}(R/\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I))
, which finishes the proof.
∎

5.
Growth of the equivalent invariants for powers of ideals

Applying a fixed algebraic invariant
\theta
to a family of homogeneous ideals
{\mathcal{F}}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
gives rise to
a function
n\mapsto\theta(I_{n})
. A natural endeavor is to study the growth of such functions.

In this section we fix a homogeneous ideal
I
of a polynomial ring and focus on the sequence of powers of
I
, namely
{\mathcal{F}}=\{I^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
. It is well known that the regularity of powers of
I
is given by an eventually linear function
\operatorname{reg}(I^{n})=dn+e
for
n\gg 0
by the celebrated work of Cutkosky–Herzog–Trung
[
7
]
and Kodyialam
[
17
]
.
We use this to show below that applying each of the invariants listed in
4.1
to
{\mathcal{F}}
also yields an eventually linear function.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of a polynomial ring
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
with
k
an infinite field of characteristic zero or larger than any exponent appearing in the generators of the reverse lexicographic generic initial ideal
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I)
. Fix an integer
0\leq i\leq d
. Then there exist nonnegative integers
d,e\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I^{n})=dn+e
for
n\gg 0.

Proof.

By
4.1
we have
\operatorname{sreg}_{i}(I^{n})=\operatorname{reg}(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I^{n})+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}))
, so it suffices to prove the claim for the function
f(n)=\operatorname{reg}(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I^{n})+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}))
.
As in
3.10
, let
F={\rm Frac}(k[u_{ij}])
, and set
R^{\prime}=F\otimes_{k}R=F[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}],I^{\prime}=IR^{\prime}
and

L_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}u_{ij}x_{j}
. By
3.11
we have the following identities

(5.1)

f(n)=\operatorname{reg}(\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I^{n})+(x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d}))=\operatorname{reg}(I^{n}R^{\prime}+(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d})).

Now set
\overline{R^{\prime}}=R^{\prime}/(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d})
and
\overline{I^{\prime}}=\left(I^{\prime}+(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d})\right)/(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d})
and note that

\displaystyle\overline{I^{\prime}}^{\,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I^{\prime}+(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d})\right)^{n}/(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I^{\prime\,n}+(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d})\right)/(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I^{n}R^{\prime}+(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d})\right)/(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d}).

Thus there is a graded ring isomorphism

\frac{\overline{R^{\prime}}}{\overline{I^{\prime}}^{\,n}}\cong\frac{R^{\prime}}{I^{n}R^{\prime}+(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d})},

which yields equalities

\operatorname{reg}\left(R^{\prime}/\overline{I^{\prime}}^{\,n}\right)=\operatorname{reg}\left(R^{\prime}/(I^{n}R^{\prime}+(L_{i+1},\ldots,L_{d}))\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{reg}\left(\overline{I^{\prime}}^{\,n}\right)=f(n).

Now the eventual linearity of
f(n)
follows from the results on regularity of powers in
[
7
,
17
]
applied to
I^{\prime}
.
∎

Naturally, it follows that the other invariants listed
4.1
also grow linearly.

Corollary 5.2
.

Under the assumptions of
5.1
the following functions are eventually linear:

(1)

f(n)=a_{i}(I^{n})
for a fixed integer
0\leq i\leq\operatorname{ht}(I)
,

(2)

f(n)=
the largest
i
-th partial degree of a minimal generator of
\operatorname{gin_{rev}}(I^{n})
.

(3)

f(n)=\operatorname{red}_{d-i}(R/I^{n})
for a fixed integer
0\leq i\leq\operatorname{ht}(I)
.

This result has been previously shown for the reduction numbers
\operatorname{red}(R/I^{n})=\operatorname{red}_{d-\operatorname{ht}(I)}(R/I^{n})
in
[
15
]
. To our knowledge the remaining assertions of the above corollary are new.

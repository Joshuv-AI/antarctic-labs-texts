Multiband linear cellular automata and endomorphisms of algebraic vector groups Thanks: JB was supported by the National Science Centre, Poland under grant no. 2018/29/B/ST1/01340
Jakub Byszewski, Gunther Cornelissen

Abstract
. We propose a correspondence between certain multiband linear cellular automata—models of computation widely used in the description of physical phenomena—and endomorphisms of certain algebraic unipotent groups over finite fields. The correspondence is based on the construction of a universal element specialising to a normal generator for any finite field. We use this correspondence to deduce new results concerning the temporal dynamics of such automata, using our prior, purely algebraic, study of the endomorphism ring of vector groups. These produce ‘for free’ a formula for the number of fixed points of the n n -iterate in terms of the p p -adic valuation of n n , a dichotomy for the Artin–Mazur dynamical zeta function, and an asymptotic formula for the number of periodic orbits. Since multiband linear cellular automata simulate higher order linear automata (in which states depend on finitely many prior temporal states, not just the direct predecessor), the results apply equally well to that class.

Introduction

This paper proposes a bridge between (small parts of) the world of computational physics and the world of algebraic geometry in positive characteristic.

Suppose

Theorem F

There is an injective group homomorphism

(ii)

(iii)

Corollaries of the correspondence

Since the group

, we use an inverse limit over trace maps to arrive at an uncountable limit space, which turns out to be a free rank-

module under the action of the complete modular group ring of the absolute Galois group of
\mathbf{F}_{\hskip-1.22911ptp}
. By contrast, the direct limit of these spaces under inclusions is
\mathbf{G}_{a}(\overline{\mathbf{F}_{\hskip-1.22911ptp}})
, which is a countable abelian group. We are using
\mathscr{F}
as a receptacle for the existence of a compatible set of normal bases for a tower of additive groups of finite fields.
Finding compatible models of the entire field structure (i.e. finding the structure constants of the operation of multiplication on compatible vector space bases) is much more difficult, see
[
15
, §11.7]
.

1.2.
Definition of the map
\iota

Choose an element
\alpha=\varprojlim\alpha_{N}\in\mathscr{F}
with
\alpha_{N}\in\mathbf{F}_{\hskip-1.22911ptp^{N}}

Definition 1.2.1

The map

(i)

(ii)

\iota_{\alpha}

(iii)

(iv)

.

Proof.

Part (

) is clear. For (

and all

For part (

For (

Suppose that

(which is finite by the finite-type assumption).

Since

In

is a non-zero periodic sequence in

3.
Open problems

We finish the paper with a few open problems and questions related to extending the ‘bridge’ between the theory of algebraic groups and cellular automata.

(i)

A vector group is a particular kind of unipotent group. If
G
is a general connected unipotent algebraic group and
\sigma\in\End(G)
is a surjective algebraic group endomorphism, there exists a finite filtration of
G
by characteristic (hence
\sigma
-invariant) algebraic subgroups such that all quotients are isomorphic to vector groups
\mathbf{G}_{a}^{r}
(of possibly different dimensions
r
), and
\sigma
induces an endomorphism of those
[
2
, Chapter 8]
. Furthermore, the number of fixed points of
\sigma
on
G
(if finite) is the product of that on the different quotients
[
2
, Proposition 4.8.1]
. Thus, the study of dynamics of endomorphisms
\sigma
of unipotent algebraic groups (which can be assumed to be surjective by replacing
G
by
\sigma^{n}G
for suitable
n
) is reduced to the case where the group is a vector group. For a general unipotent group
G
with an endomorphism
\sigma
defined over
\mathbf{F}_{\hskip-1.22911ptp}
, is there a generalisation of additive cellular automata that are filtered by structures whose quotients are linear cellular automata, corresponding to the filtration of
G
with vector group quotients stable by
\sigma
?

(ii)

Can one generalise the main theorem from vector groups to more general algebraic groups? For which algebraic groups
G
is there a map
\iota\colon G(\overline{\mathbf{F}_{\hskip-1.22911ptp}})\rightarrow G(\mathbf{F}_{\hskip-1.22911ptp})^{\mathbf{Z}}
with similar properties? It seems best to specialise first to the case where
G
is commutative or where
G
is unipotent. In other examples, such as
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
for
p=2
, any group homomorphism
\iota\colon\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbf{F}_{\hskip-1.22911pt2}})\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{\hskip-1.22911pt2})^{\mathbf{Z}}
is trivial (this is because for
N\geqslant 2
the group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{\hskip-1.22911pt2^{N}})\cong\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{\hskip-1.22911pt2^{N}})
is simple, and so any homomorphism
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{\hskip-1.22911pt2^{N}})\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{\hskip-1.22911pt2})\cong S_{3}
is trivial).

(iii)

What happens if
\overline{\mathbf{F}_{\hskip-1.22911ptp}}
is replaced by
\overline{K}
for an arbitrary field
K
of characteristic
p>0
with
r>1
? The results and proofs from
[
2
]
do not apply anymore. In this case, it might be interesting to construct an appropriate theory for cellular automata and then apply it to the algebraic group side, reversing the philosophy of this paper.

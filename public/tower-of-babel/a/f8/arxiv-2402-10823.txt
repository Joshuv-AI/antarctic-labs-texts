Structure results for torus fixed loci
Jarod Alper, Felix Janda

Abstract
. Motivated by localization theorems on moduli spaces, we prove a structural classification of Deligne–Mumford stacks with an action of a torus where the induced action on the coarse moduli space is trivial. We also establish a general local structure theorem for morphisms of algebraic stacks.



1.
Introduction

The main goal of this paper is to provide a description of
Deligne–Mumford stacks
X
with a torus action where
the induced action on the coarse moduli space
X_{\cms}
is trivial.
Such actions arise naturally in moduli theory as the fixed locus of a torus action on a Deligne–Mumford stack (see
Remark

1.6
). The motivation for this paper is that an explicit understanding of such actions could be useful for applications of localization theorems.

A prototypical example is the non-trivial
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
B\mu_{r}
arising from the Kummer sequence
0\to\mu_{r}\to T=\mathbb{G}_{m}\xrightarrow{r}\mathbb{G}_{m}\to 0
: the induced map
B\mu_{r}\to BT
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-torsor as it’s the base change of
\Spec\Bbbk\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}
. The main theorem below states essentially that every
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on a Deligne–Mumford stack
X
, which is trivial on
X_{\cms}
, is built from this nontrivial action on
B\mu_{r}
for some
r
.

1.1.
The main theorem

We work over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=[X/\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}]\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
be a separated, finite type, and relatively tame Deligne–Mumford morphism of algebraic stacks (i.e.,
X
is a separated, finite type, and tame Deligne–Mumford stack). Assume that
\mathcal{X}
is reduced and connected, and that the
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-action on the coarse moduli space
X_{\cms}
of
X
is trivial.

(1)

There is a diagonalizable group
\mu_{r}:=\mu_{r_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mu_{r_{n}}
for a unique tuple
r=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})
of integers with
r_{1}|r_{2}|\cdots|r_{n}
and a central closed subgroup
\mu_{r,\mathcal{X}}\subset I_{\mathcal{X}/B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}}
of the relative inertia stack restricting to a subgroup
\mu_{r,X}\subset I_{X}
such that the
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-action on
X
descends to a trivial
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-action on the Deligne–Mumford stack
Y:=X\hskip-2.84544pt\fatslash\hskip 2.84544pt\mu_{r,X}
and there is a factorization

\textstyle{\mathcal{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{X}\hskip-2.84544pt\fatslash\hskip 2.84544pt\mu_{r,\mathcal{X}}\cong Y\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X_{\cms}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}}

which satisfies the following universal property:
if
\mathcal{X}\to Y^{\prime}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\to X_{\cms}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
is another factorization where
Y^{\prime}
is a Deligne–Mumford stack, then there is a morphism
g\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
unique up to unique isomorphism fitting in the diagram

\textstyle{\mathcal{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g\times\id}
\textstyle{X_{\cms}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}}
\textstyle{Y^{\prime}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}.\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

(2)

Consider the exact sequence
1\to\mu_{r}\to T\to\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\to 1
where
T=\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
is defined by
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\mapsto(t_{1}^{r_{1}},\ldots,t_{n}^{r_{n}})
,
and let

BT\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
be the induced
\mu_{r}
-gerbe.
The
\mu_{r}\times\mu_{r}
-gerbe
X\times BT\to Y\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
factors as

X\times BT\to\mathcal{X}\to Y\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}.

In particular,
\mathcal{X}\cong p_{1}^{*}X\wedge^{\mu_{r}}p_{2}^{*}BT
over
Y\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
.

(3)

There is a central closed fppf subgroup
T\times\mathcal{X}\subset I_{\mathcal{X}}
and a factorization

\textstyle{\mathcal{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{X}\hskip-2.84544pt\fatslash\hskip 2.84544pt\mu_{r,\mathcal{X}}\cong Y\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{1}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{X}\hskip-2.84544pt\fatslash\hskip 2.84544ptT\cong Y}

and
\mathcal{X}\hskip-2.84544pt\fatslash\hskip 2.84544ptT
is identified with the rigidification
(\mathcal{X}\hskip-2.84544pt\fatslash\hskip 2.84544pt\mu_{r,\mathcal{X}})\hskip-2.84544pt\fatslash\hskip 2.84544pt\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
by
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}=T/\mu_{r}
.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{X}\to Y
is the
T
-gerbe associated
to the
\mu_{r}
-gerbe
X\to Y
with respect to the
canonical inclusion of group schemes
\mu_{r}\subset T
.

1.2.
Special cases and our proof strategy

Given the technical nature of the statement and the subtleties arising from torus actions on Deligne–Mumford stack, we will try to unpack the conclusion in a series of remarks and examples.

Remark 1.2

(Torus equivariant interpretation)
.

Part (
1
) asserts that
Y
is the largest Deligne–Mumford stack with trivial
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-action factoring
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-equivariantly as
X\to Y=X\hskip-2.84544pt\fatslash\hskip 2.84544pt\mu_{r,X}\to X_{\cms}
. Note that there is an identification
X_{\cms}\cong Y_{\cms}
of coarse moduli spaces.
Note also that the assumption that the
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-action on
X_{\cms}
is trivial translates to the condition that the relative coarse moduli space of
\mathcal{X}\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
is
X_{\cms}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
.

For part (
2
), let
X_{\triv}
be the
\mu_{r}
-gerbe over
Y
which is isomorphic to
X
as Deligne–Mumford stacks over
Y
but is given the trivial
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-action. Then the
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-equivariant
\mu_{r}
-gerbe
X\to Y
is obtained from the possibly
non-trivial

\mu_{r}
-gerbe
X_{\triv}\to Y
with
trivial

\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-action
by twisting by the
trivial

\mu_{r}
-gerbe
B\mu_{r,Y}\to Y
with
non-trivial

\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-action, i.e. there is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-equivariant isomorphism of
\mu_{r}
-gerbes

X\cong X_{\triv}\wedge^{\mu_{r}}B\mu_{r,Y}.

While the
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-action on
X
may be non-trivial, part (
2
) also asserts that there is a smallest integer
r
such that the action becomes trivial after the reparameterization
T=\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
defined by
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\mapsto(t_{1}^{r_{1}},\ldots,t_{n}^{r_{n}})
.

Example 1.3

(Case of a classifying stack)
.

The crucial example—both to understand the conclusion of the main theorem and its proof—is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-action on a classifying stack
X=BG
of a finite group
G
. Such an action corresponds to an extension

(1.1)

1\to G\to\Gamma\to\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\to 1

of algebraic groups (see
Example

3.2
) where
\mathcal{X}=[X/\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}]=B\Gamma
.
We let
T=\Gamma^{0}\subset\Gamma
be the identity component. As
T
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
(see §
4.1
), the intersection
T\cap G
is isomorphic to
\mu_{r}
for a unique tuple of integers
r=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})
with
r_{1}|\cdots|r_{n}
.
By
Lemmas

4.1
and
4.2
,
T
and
\mu_{r}
are central in
\Gamma
, and there is an identification
\overline{\Gamma}\cong\overline{G}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
where
\overline{G}:=G/\mu_{r}
and
\overline{\Gamma}:=\Gamma/\mu_{r}
. Part (
1
) of
Theorem

1.1
is the identifications

\mathcal{X}\hskip-2.84544pt\fatslash\hskip 2.84544pt\mu_{r}\cong B\overline{\Gamma}\cong\underbrace{B\overline{G}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}}_{Y\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}}

over
B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
while (
2
) is the factorization

\underbrace{BG\times BT}_{X\times BT}\to\underbrace{B\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}\to\underbrace{B\overline{\Gamma}}_{B\overline{G}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}}.

and (
3
) is the factorization

\underbrace{B\Gamma}_{\mathcal{X}}\to\underbrace{B\overline{\Gamma}}_{\mathcal{X}\hskip-2.84544pt\fatslash\hskip 2.84544pt\mu_{r}}\to\underbrace{B\overline{G}}_{\mathcal{X}\hskip-2.84544pt\fatslash\hskip 2.84544ptT}.

Example 1.4

(Case of a quotient stack)
.

Let
G
be a finite group acting on a connected, reduced, and separated algebraic space
U
of finite type over
\Bbbk
. Suppose that
X:=[U/G]\to BG
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-equivariant morphism such that the induced
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-action on the quotient algebraic space
U/G=[U/G]_{\cms}
is trivial. Then
B\Gamma=[BG/\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}]
for an extension
\Gamma
of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
by a finite group
G
as in (
1.1
). We set
\mathcal{X}=[U/\Gamma]
and we define
T=\Gamma^{0}
,
\mu_{r}=G\cap T
,
\overline{G}=G/\mu_{r}
, and
\overline{\Gamma}=\Gamma/\mu_{r}
as above.

Since
U
is reduced and
U\to U/G
is quasi-finite and equivariant under
T\to\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
, the action of
T
(and thus
\mu_{r}
) on
U
must be trivial. This gives a central subgroup
\mu_{r,U}
of the stabilizer group scheme of the action of
G
on
U
and thus a central subgroup
\mu_{r,\mathcal{X}}\subset I_{\mathcal{X}/B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}}
of the relative inertia.
Then (
1
)-(
3
) of
Theorem

1.1
are expressed with the factorization

\underbrace{[U/G]\times BT\cong[U/(G\times T)]}_{X\times BT}\to\underbrace{[U/\Gamma]}_{\mathcal{X}}\to\underbrace{[U/\overline{\Gamma}]\cong[U/\overline{G}]\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}}_{\mathcal{X}\hskip-2.84544pt\fatslash\hskip 2.84544pt\mu_{r}}\to\underbrace{[U/\overline{G}]}_{\mathcal{X}\hskip-2.84544pt\fatslash\hskip 2.84544ptT}.

Remark 1.5

(Root gerbes)
.

In the case of a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action, the
\mu_{r}
-gerbe
X\to Y
will
sometimes be a root gerbe and correspondingly so will

\mathcal{X}\to Y\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
This occurs for instance in
Section

2.1.1
below.
Recall that the
r
th root gerbe
\sqrt[r]{(X,L)}
of a line bundle

L
on
X
is defined as the fiber product of the map

X\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}
classifying
L
and the
r
th power map

B\mathbb{G}_{m}\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
In fact,
X\to Y
is a root gerbe if and only if its cohomology
class in
H^{2}(Y_{\rm et},\mu_{r})
is the image of a line bundle

[L]\in H^{1}(Y_{\rm et},\mathbb{G}_{m})
under the boundary map induced from the
Kummer sequence
1\to\mu_{r}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}\to 1
.
If
X\to Y
is a root gerbe corresponding to a line bundle
L
on

Y
, then the root gerbe corresponding to
\mathcal{X}\to Y\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}

is
\mathcal{X}\cong\sqrt[r]{(Y\times B\mathbb{G}_{m},L\boxtimes[1])}
where

[1]
is the weight-
1
representation of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
.

Remark 1.6
.

Torus actions and fixed loci are ubiquitous in moduli theory.
In fact, localization with respect to a torus action on the moduli
space of stable maps as in
[
GP99
]
is one of the main
techniques in Gromov–Witten theory, and
Theorem

1.1
describes
the equivariant geometry of the fixed loci in detail.
In
Section

2.1
, we give two examples illustrating

Theorem

1.1
for moduli spaces of stable maps.

Remark 1.7

(Strategy of proof in the general case)
.

Our strategy to handle the general case of an algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}=[X/\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}]
over
B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
is to reduce to the case where
X=[U/G]
is a quotient by a finite group. To this end,
we prove a general étale local structure theorem of morphisms
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
(see
Theorem

1.13
). When
\mathcal{Y}=B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
, the theorem implies that any point of
X
has a
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-equivariant étale neighborhood

W=[\Spec A/G]\to X
or in other words an étale neighborhood
\mathcal{W}=[W/\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}]\to\mathcal{X}
over
B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
.
We then show that the construction of the subgroup
\mu_{r,\mathcal{W}}\subset I_{\mathcal{W}/B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}}
descends a central subgroup
\mu_{r,\mathcal{X}}\subset I_{\mathcal{X}/B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}}
and that the factorizations in parts (
1
)-(
3
) can be checked étale locally.

Remark 1.8

(Necessity of hypotheses)
.

The connectedness assumption in
Theorem

1.1
is not essential.
If
X
is disconnected, then there will still be a central subgroup
of
I_{\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}
with the desired properties.
On each connected component, this subgroup will be isomorphic to

\mu_{r}
for some tuple of integers
r
, but these integers will
depend on the connected component.

On the other hand, the conclusion of
Theorem

1.1
may fail if

\mathcal{X}
is nonreduced.
Consider the affine scheme
U=\Spec(\Bbbk[\epsilon]/\epsilon^{2})
,
and consider the action of
\Gamma=\mathbb{G}_{m}
on
U
defined by

\epsilon\mapsto t\epsilon
.
The quotient stack
X=[U/\mu_{2}]
by the subgroup

\mu_{2}\subset\Gamma
is a nonreduced, connected Deligne–Mumford
stack with coarse moduli
U/\mu_{2}=\Spec\Bbbk
.
There is an induced action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}=\Gamma/\mu_{2}
on
X
, and the
quotient
\mathcal{X}=[X/\mathbb{G}_{m}]=[U/\Gamma]
is a nonreduced and
connected algebraic stack over
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
via the structure morphism

\mathcal{X}\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}
induced by the squaring map
\Gamma\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
Note that
X\to\Spec\Bbbk
and
\mathcal{X}\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}
are not

\mu_{2}
-gerbes.

On the other hand, the theorem does extend to the nonreduced case if
we modify the hypotheses:

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=[X/\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}]\to B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
be a quasi-separated, finite
type, and relatively Deligne–Mumford morphism of algebraic stacks.
Assume that the
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-action on
\mathcal{X}
is trivial after a
reparameterization of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
.
Then the conclusion of
Theorem

1.1
holds.

Remark 1.10
.

In fact, as we will see in
Remark

3.3
below, when
\mathcal{X}

is separated and reduced, a torus action trivial on coarse moduli is
automatically also trivial after a reparameterization, and so

Theorem

1.9
generalizes
Theorem

1.1
.

Remark 1.11

(Restriction to stabilizers)
.

In the setting of
Theorem

1.1
, for every point
x\in X(\Bbbk)
, there is a short exact sequence
1\to\Stab_{X}(x)\to\Stab_{\mathcal{X}}(x)\to\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\to 1
. The central subgroup
\mu_{r,\mathcal{X}}\subset I_{\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}}
restricts to the subgroup
\mu_{r}=\Stab_{\mathcal{X}}(x)^{0}\cap\Stab_{X}(x)\subset\Stab_{X}(x)
.

Remark 1.12
.

This paper was inspired and strengthens the second author’s work

[
CJR22
]
(joint with Q. Chen and Y. Ruan) on punctured R-maps
in the context of gauged linear sigma models (GLSM).
In fact, as explained in Section

below
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-actions
play a central role in the geometry of GLSM.
In particular, the notation for the groups in the sequence
(
1.1
) follows the one used in the work of
Polishchuk–Vaintrob
[

, § 3.2]
, and Fan–Jarvis–Ruan

[
FJR18

.

As
\mu
-gerbes play a central role in the structure of torus fixed loci of Deligne–Mumford stacks, it is interesting to study the behavior of invariants such as Picard and Chow groups under taking

It would also be interesting to study the following generalizations.

1.3.
A local structure theorem for morphisms

Our proof of
Theorem

1.1
uses a special case of the following theorem, which we believe is independently interesting. It is established using techniques from
[
AHR20
]
and
[
AHR21
]
.

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a morphism of quasi-separated algebraic stacks locally of finite type over
\Bbbk
with affine stabilizers. Let
x\in\mathcal{X}(\Bbbk)
and
y=f(x)\in\mathcal{Y}(\Bbbk)
be its image. Assume that the stabilizers
G_{x}
and
G_{y}
are
linearly reductive. Then there exist

(1)

affine schemes
\Spec A
and
\Spec B
with actions of
G_{x}
and
G_{y}
, respectively;

(2)

\Bbbk
-points
x^{\prime}\in\Spec A
and
y^{\prime}\in\Spec B
;

(3)

a morphism
g\colon\Spec A\to\Spec B
of affine schemes equivariant with respect to
G_{x}\to G_{y}
; and

(4)

étale morphisms
([\Spec A/G_{x}],x^{\prime})\to(\mathcal{X},x)
and
([\Spec B/G_{y}],y^{\prime})\to(\mathcal{Y},y)
inducing isomorphisms of stabilizer groups at
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}

such that the diagram

\textstyle{BG_{x}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{[\Spec A/G_{x}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{\mathcal{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{BG_{y}}
\textstyle{[\Spec B/G_{y}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{Y}}

is commutative.
Moreover, if
\mathcal{X}
and

have separated diagonal (resp. affine diagonal), then the morphisms

A variant of this theorem was recently established in

2.
Examples and Application to gauged linear sigma models

2.1.
Examples in moduli theory

2.1.1.
Stable maps to a Hirzebruch surface

Let
S=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathcal{O}(n)\oplus\mathcal{O})
be a Hirzebruch surface.
We may then consider the Deligne–Mumford stack

M=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,0}(S,rF)
of genus zero, unpointed
stable maps to
S
whose curve class is
r
times the fiber class
F
.
Equip
S
with a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action that scales the fibers, such that

[S/\mathbb{G}_{m}]\cong\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}}((\mathcal{O}(n)\boxtimes[1])\oplus\mathcal{O})
, where
[1]
is the weight one representation of

\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
By post-composition, this equips
M
with a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action.

One of the fixed loci of this
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action corresponds to the closed
substack
X
of “Galois covers”
C\to S
that are
r
-fold covers
of a fiber of
S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
totally ramified at two points in
C
.
We may identify
X
with the root gerbe
\sqrt[r]{(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(n))}
,
where the associated map
X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
records the image of
C
in

\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and the universal
r
th root corresponds to the normal bundle
of
C
at one of the ramification points.

The quotient
\mathcal{X}=[X/\mathbb{G}_{m}]
may be identified with

\sqrt[r]{(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathcal{O}(n)\boxtimes[1])}
.
This illustrates
Theorem

1.1
(
1
), (
2
)
with
Y=\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

2.1.2.
Stable maps to projective space

Consider the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
given by distinct weights
\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{N}
, and the induced
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{N},d)
.
We illustrate
Theorem

1.1
for each connected component of the fixed locus
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{N},d)^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
. Following
[
GP99
, §4]
, each connected component corresponds to a marked graph
\Gamma
as follows. If
f\colon C\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
is a stable map fixed by
\mathbb{G}_{m}
, then
f(C)
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-invariant curve, and the image of every marked point, node, contracted component, and ramification point is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-fixed point of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Since the weights are distinct, the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-fixed points of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
are the points
p_{0},\ldots,p_{N}
corresponding to the standard basis elements and the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-invariant curves are the lines connecting
p_{i}
to
p_{j}
. Each non-contracted component maps to one of these
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-invariant curves and is ramified over two points, which implies that each such component is rational and that the map
f
restricted to this component is determined by its degree. The graph
\Gamma
corresponding to
f
has vertices corresponding to the connected components of
f^{-1}(\{p_{0},\ldots,p_{N}\})
and has edges corresponding to the non-contracted component. An edge
e
is incident to a vertex
v
if the corresponding components of
C
intersect. In addition, each edge
e
is marked with the degree
d_{e}
of the map from the non-contracted component to the line in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
, and each vertex
v
is marked with the arithmetic genus
g(v)
of the corresponding component with the convention that
g(v)=0

We record a special case of

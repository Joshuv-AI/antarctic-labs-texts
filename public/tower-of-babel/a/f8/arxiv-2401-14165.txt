Pseudogap in S ​ r 2 − x ​ L ​ a x ​ I ​ r ​ O 4 Sr_{2-x}La_{x}IrO_{4} : Gor’kov-Teitel’baum thermal activation model Preprint: APS/123-QED
Jalaja Pandya 1 , Devarshi Dave 2 , Navinder Singh 1 1 Physical Research Laboratory, Ahmedabad, India, PIN: 380009. 2 Chaitanya School, Gandhinagar

Abstract
Recently, Hall effect measurements are done on Lanthanum doped Strontium Iridate S ​ r 2 − x ​ L ​ a x ​ I ​ r ​ O 4 Sr_{2-x}La_{x}IrO_{4} which is 5d analogue of cuprates hsu2023carrier . Hall effect measurements show that the effective carrier density n H n_{H} exhibits a crossover from n H ∼ x n_{H}\sim x to n H ∼ 1 + x n_{H}\sim 1+x near x ≃ 0.16 x\simeq 0.16 . This is very similar to that found in cuprates around p ≃ 0.19 p\simeq 0.19 . It is proposed that a pseudogap (PG) state in S ​ r 2 − x ​ L ​ a x ​ I ​ r ​ O 4 Sr_{2-x}La_{x}IrO_{4} exists and is ending at x ≃ 0.16 x\simeq 0.16 hsu2023carrier . However, PG boundary (in doping-temperature phase diagram) remains unknown. In this work, we apply a very successfull the Gor’kov-Teitel’baum Thermal Activation (GTTA) model to S ​ r 2 − x ​ L ​ a x ​ I ​ r ​ O 4 Sr_{2-x}La_{x}IrO_{4} and obtain its PG phase boundary and draw an updated phase diagram. Our results agree with previously known signatures of PG phase in this system seo2017infrared ; de2015collapse . Using results from GTTA model we also obtain the evolution of ”Fermi arcs” in this system as a function temperature for doping concentration x ≃ 0.08 x\simeq 0.08 which are in qualitative agreement with the reported results.

The Gorkov-Teitelbaum Thermal Activation (GTTA) model was introduced by Lev P. Gor’kov and Gregory B. Teitel’baum in 2006
PhysRevLett.97.247003
. It is a phenomenological model for number of charge carriers which was derived from the Hall effect data of
La_{2-x}Sr_{x}CuO_{4}

PhysRevLett.97.247003
. The experimental Hall effect data of
La_{2-x}Sr_{x}CuO_{4}
can be rationalized by the GTTA model as follows:

R_{H}=\frac{1}{n_{Hall}e}

(1)

n_{Hall}(x,T)=n_{0}(x)+n_{1}(x)e^{[-\Delta(x)/T]}

(2)

The first term
n_{0}(x)
, is the temperature independent term which incorporates the carrier density due to external hole doping. The second term is temperature dependent term which can be interpreted as thermal activation contribution of carriers above the gap.
n_{0}(x)
varies linearly with
x
for low doping (up to
x=0.07
). For
x>0.07
,
n_{0}(x)
evolves non-linearly with
x
.
n_{1}(x)\simeq 2.8
(roughly constant) for doping concentrations (
x<0.19
) and drops abruptly for doping concentrations (
x>0.19
).
\Delta(x)
is referred as the activation energy and corresponds to the energy difference between the ”Fermi arc” and the band bottom for
La_{2-x}Sr_{x}CuO_{4}

PhysRevLett.97.247003
. The extracted
\Delta(x)
decreases linearly with increase in
x
till
x=0.20
. The
\Delta(x)
values are in agreement with ARPES results.

We applied this very successful model (GTTA) to
Sr_{2-x}La_{x}IrO_{4}

\Delta
\simeq

III
Fermi Arcs

The phenomenon of ”Fermi arcs” is observed in cuprates
kondo2013formation
. It is also observed in this iridate system
peng2022electronic
. In cuprates at very low doping PG is of d-wave symmetry which depects a node near
(\pi,\pi)
direction. At higher dopings and higher temperatures these nodes extend to form an arc lie feature in ARPES spectra around the nodal direction
kondo2013formation
. These features are called ”Fermi arcs”.

Using the free electron model for 2-dimensional materials (similar to the approach used by Gor’kov and Teitel’baum
gor2014two
), the relation between number of charge carriers per unit area (N) and the arclength of Fermi arc measured in terms of arc angle
\Delta\phi
is,

N=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\Delta\phi}\,d\theta\int_{0}^{k_{F}}k\,dk.

(3)

Thus,

N=\frac{k_{F}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\Delta\phi,

(4)

where,
k_{F}
is the magnitude of Fermi wave vector (in x-y plane). N can be calculated from carrier density (
n_{Hall}
) of the material. Thus Eq. 4 can be rewritten as follows:

\Delta\phi=\frac{4\pi^{2}(n_{Hall}c)}{nk_{F}^{2}},

(5)

where, c is the lattice constant along the Ir-O-Sr direction i.e.
25.96\AA

crawford1994structural
and
n
is the number of Ir-O planes in a unit cell of
Sr_{2}IrO{4}
. Since only one quadrant of Brillouin zone is usually displayed reporting ARPES measurements, we divide
\Delta\phi
calculated from Eq.
5
by
4
to represent the ”Fermi arcs”.

IV
Conclusions

We have applied the very successful GTTA model to
Sr_{2-x}La_{x}IrO_{4}
system and extracted its PG boundary (crossover) using the Hall-effect data
hsu2023carrier
. It is observed that PG opens at very high temperatures (
\sim
1200 K) near low doping (
x\simeq 0.036
) and then it decreases approximately linearly as doping is increased. As displayed in Fig. 1, the PG boundary when extrapolated vanishes at around (
x\simeq 0.16
) which is the critical doping for the pseudogap opening as discovered in
hsu2023carrier
. Our results also agree with some experimental data for PG previously reported in this system
seo2017infrared
;
de2015collapse
. We note that this doping dependence of PG boundary is very similar to that found in cuprates. We also schematically show the evolution of the “Fermi arcs” using the carrier density calculated from the GTTA model at temperatures 80K and 200K for a given doping concentration (
x\simeq 0.08
) which are in qualitative agreement with the reported results
kim2016observation
. We also draw an updated phase diagram for
Sr_{2-x}La_{x}IrO_{4}
in Fig.
5
. The magnetic phase diagram of the material is redrawn using the extracted data from Fig. 12 of
chen2015influence
. On top of the previously known magnetic phase, we find a region of the PG phase boundary for
Sr_{2-x}La_{x}IrO_{4}
in Fig.
5
. This PG phase boundary should be refined through future experiments and analysis of
Sr_{2-x}La_{x}IrO_{4}
system.

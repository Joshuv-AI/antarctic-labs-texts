On pseudoholomorphic map between almost Hermitian manifolds
Chiakuei Peng Xiaowei Xu*

Abstract
. In this paper, we use the canonical connection instead of Levi-Civita connection to study the smooth maps between almost Hermitian manifolds, especially, the pseudoholomorphic ones. By using the Bochner formulas, we obtian the C 2 C^{2} -estimate of canonical second fundamental form, Liouville type theorems of pseudoholomorphic maps, pseudoholomorphicity of pluriharmonic maps, and Simons integral inequality of pseudoholomorphic isometric immersion.

2.1.
Geometry of canonical connection

Let
(M,J,g)
be a
2m
-dimensional almost Hermitian manifold, which means that
J
is an almost complex structure on
M
and Riemannian metric
g
is
J
-invariant.
The tangent bundle of
M
denoted by
TM

TM\otimes\mathbb{C}
denoted by
T^{\mathbb{C}}M
. By extending the almost complex structure

\mathbb{C}
-linearly to
T^{\mathbb{C}}M
, we obtain the decomposition

(2.1)

T^{\mathbb{C}}M=T^{\prime}M\oplus T^{\prime\prime}M,

where
T^{\prime}M
and
T^{\prime\prime}M
are the eigenspaces of
J
corresponding to eigenvalues
\sqrt{-1}
and
-\sqrt{-1}
respectively. Similarly, by extending
J
to forms, one can decompose
k
-forms into a sum of
(p,q)
-forms with
p+q=k
. Extending the almost Hermitian metric
\mathbb{C}
-linearly to
T^{\mathbb{C}}M
, then it induces a Hermitian metric on
T^{\prime}M
, still denoted by
g
. Locally, we can choose a unitary frame
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
with the dual coframe
\{\theta^{1},\ldots,\theta^{m}\}
so that

(2.2)

g=\theta^{i}\otimes\overline{\theta^{i}},\hskip 28.45274ptdV_{g}=(\sqrt{-1})^{m}\theta^{1}\wedge\overline{\theta^{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta^{m}\wedge\overline{\theta^{m}}.

The fundamental 2-form corresponding to the Hermitian metric
g
is given by

(2.3)

\omega=\sqrt{-1}\;\theta^{i}\wedge\overline{\theta^{i}}.

Let
\nabla
be an affine connection on
TM
, which is called
almost-Hermitian
if it satifies

(2.4)

\nabla J=0,\hskip 28.45274pt\nabla g=0.

It is easy to see that such connections always exist on any almost Hermitian manifold. One can refer to
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
for details. Locally, it follows from (
2.4
) that the
connection
1
-forms

\{\theta^{j}_{i}\}
are given by

(2.5)

\nabla e_{i}=\theta_{i}^{j}e_{j},\hskip 28.45274pt\theta^{j}_{i}+\overline{\theta^{i}_{j}}=0.

The first and second structure equations of
\nabla
are determined by

(2.6)

d\theta^{i}=-\theta^{i}_{j}\wedge\theta^{j}+\Theta^{i},

(2.7)

d\theta^{i}_{j}=-\theta^{i}_{k}\wedge\theta^{k}_{j}+\Omega^{i}_{j},\hskip 14.22636pt\Omega^{j}_{i}+\overline{\Omega^{i}_{j}}=0,

where the
2
-forms
\Theta^{i}
,
\Omega^{i}_{j}
are called
torsion
and
curvature
of
\nabla
, respectively.
It is well known (see
[
5
]
) that there exists a unique almost Hermitian connection
\nabla
on
TM
whose
(1,1)
-part satisfies

(2.8)

(\Theta^{i})^{(1,1)}=0.

which is called
canonical connection
on
(M,J,g)
. It is just the Chern connection when
J
is integrable. We will always to use
\nabla
to stand for the canonical connection on almost Hermitian manifold.

There are several important families of almost Hermitian manifold defined by using the fundamental 2-form, Levi-Civita connection and almost complex structure. We say that
(M,J,g)
is
almost-Kählerian
if
d\omega=0
, that it is
quasi-Kählerian
if
(d\omega)^{(1,2)}=0
, and that it is
semi-Kählerian
if
d^{*}\omega=0
, where

is the codifferential operator of

with respect to the metric

. To character them, we set

(2.9)

\Theta^{i}:=L^{i}_{jk}\,\theta^{j}\wedge\theta^{k}+N^{i}_{\overline{j}\,\overline{k}}\,\overline{\theta^{j}}\wedge\overline{\theta^{k}}

with
L^{i}_{jk}=-L^{i}_{kj}
,
N^{i}_{\overline{j}\,\overline{k}}=-N^{i}_{\overline{k}\,\overline{j}}
. Then, in terms of
L^{i}_{jk}
and
N^{i}_{\overline{j}\,\overline{k}}
, it is easy to check that
(M,J,g)
is almost-Kählerian if and only if

(2.10)

L^{i}_{jk}=0\hskip 8.5359ptand\hskip 8.5359ptN^{i}_{\overline{j}\,\overline{k}}+N^{j}_{\overline{k}\,\overline{i}}+N^{k}_{\overline{i}\,\overline{j}}=0,

it is quasi-Kählerian if and only if

(2.11)

L^{i}_{jk}=0,

and it is semi-Kählerian if and only if

(2.12)

L_{j}:=\sum\limits_{i}L^{i}_{ij}=0.

On the other hand, by using the Levi-Civita connection, we call
(M,J,g)
is
nearly-Kählerian
if
(D_{X}J)X=0
for any tangent vector
X
on
M
. This is equivalent to

(2.13)

L^{i}_{jk}=0\hskip 8.5359ptand\hskip 8.5359ptN^{i}_{\overline{j}\,\overline{k}}=N^{j}_{\overline{k}\,\overline{i}}.

We also set

(2.14)

\Omega^{i}_{j}:=R^{i}_{jk\ell}\,\theta^{k}\wedge\theta^{\ell}+R^{i}_{jk\overline{\ell}}\,\theta^{k}\wedge\overline{\theta^{\ell}}+R^{i}_{j\overline{k}\,\overline{\ell}}\,\overline{\theta^{k}}\wedge\overline{\theta^{\ell}}

with
R^{i}_{jk\ell}=-R^{i}_{j\ell k}
,
R^{i}_{j\overline{k}\,\overline{\ell}}=-R^{i}_{j\overline{\ell}\,\overline{k}}
. The skew-Hermitian of curvature forms
\Omega^{i}_{j}
implies

(2.15)

R^{i}_{jk\ell}=\overline{R^{j}_{i\overline{\ell}\,\overline{k}}},\hskip 28.45274ptR^{i}_{jk\overline{\ell}}=\overline{R^{j}_{i\ell\overline{k}}}.

For a nonzero vectors

the

of canonical connection in direction

, and
for two nonzero vectors

we call

of canonical connection in the directions

. We say the holomorphic bisectional curvature is bounded below by

and bounded above by

for all nonzero
(1,0)
-type vectors

. The tensors
R^{\prime}_{k\overline{\ell}}:=R^{i}_{ik\overline{\ell}}
,
R^{\prime\prime}_{k\overline{\ell}}:=R^{\ell}_{ki\overline{i}}
are called the
first Ricci curvature
,
second Ricci curvature
of the canonical connection, which will be denoted by
Ric^{\prime}_{M}
,

, respectively. We say the first (resp. second) Ricci curvature is bounded below by

R^{\prime}_{k\overline{\ell}}X^{k}\overline{X^{\ell}}\geq A|X|^{2}
(resp.

(1,0)
-type vector

(2,0)
-part of curvature can be defined. We will say

Let

(2.16)

The

\nabla du=u_{i,j}\,\theta^{j}\otimes\theta^{i}+u_{i,\overline{j}}\,\overline{\theta^{j}}\otimes\theta^{i}+u_{\overline{i},j}\,\theta^{j}\otimes\overline{\theta^{i}}+u_{\overline{i},\overline{j}}\,\overline{\theta^{j}}\otimes\overline{\theta^{i}}.

We call a smooth function

Let

Similarly, for a vector field

Thus, these two Laplacian and two divergences are equal respectively, when

4.
Liouville type theorems

We will use the properties of pseudoholomorphic maps and the Bochner formulae to get Liouville type results, which are similar to the theory of harmonic map. The main tools are the maximum principle and integral estimates.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

Proof

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Box(u\circ f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(u\circ f)_{,k\overline{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(u_{\alpha}a^{\alpha}_{k}+u_{\overline{\alpha}}\,\overline{a^{\alpha}_{\overline{k}}})_{,\overline{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(u_{\alpha}a^{\alpha}_{k})_{,\overline{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(u_{\alpha,\beta}\,a^{\beta}_{\overline{k}}+u_{\alpha,\overline{\beta}}\,a^{\overline{\beta}}_{\overline{k}})a^{\alpha}_{k}+u_{\alpha}a^{\alpha}_{k,\overline{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\overline{\beta}}\,a^{\alpha}_{k}\,\overline{a^{\beta}_{k}}.

is a constant.

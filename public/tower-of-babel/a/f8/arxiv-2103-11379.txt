A variational interpretation of Restricted Additive Schwarz with impedance transmission condition for the Helmholtz problem
Shihua Gong, Martin J. Gander, Ivan G. Graham and Euan A. Spence

Abstract
In this paper we revisit the Restricted Additive Schwarz method for solving discretized Helmholtz problems, using impedance boundary conditions on subdomains (sometimes called ORAS). We present this method in its variational form and show that it can be seen as a finite element discretization of a parallel overlapping domain decomposition method defined at the PDE level. In a recent companion paper, the authors have proved certain contractive properties of the error propagation operator for this method at the PDE level, under certain geometrical assumptions. We illustrate computationally that these properties are also enjoyed by its finite element approximation, i.e., the ORAS method.



1
The Helmholtz problem

Motivated by the large range of applications, there is currently great interest in designing and analysing preconditioners for finite element discretisations of the Helmholtz equation

\displaystyle-(\Delta+k^{2}\,)u\ =\ f\quad\text{on}\quad\Omega\,,

(1)

on a
d-
dimensional domain
\Omega
(
d=2,3
), with
k
the (assumed constant, but possibly large) angular frequency. While the methods presented easily apply to quite general scattering problems and geometries, we restrict attention here to the interior impedance problem, where
\Omega
is bounded, and the boundary condition is

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial n}-{\rm i}k\right)u=g\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\,,

(2)

where
\partial u/\partial n
is the outward-pointing normal derivative of
u
on
\Omega
.

The weak form of problem (
1
), (
2
) is to seek
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle a(u,v)=F(v):=\int_{\Omega}f\bar{v}~dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}g\bar{v}~ds,

(3)

\text{where}\quad\quad a(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u.\nabla\overline{v}-k^{2}u\overline{v})-{\rm i}k\int_{\partial\Omega}u\overline{v},\quad\text{for}\quad u,v\in H^{1}(\Omega).

2
Parallel iterative Schwarz method

To solve (
1
), (
2
), we shall consider domain decomposition methods, based on a set of Lipschitz polyhedral
subdomains
\{\Omega_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
,
forming an overlapping cover of
\Omega
and equipped with a partition of unity:

\{\chi_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
, such that

\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{for each}\quad\ell:&\ \mathrm{supp}\chi_{\ell}\subset\overline{\Omega_{\ell}},\quad 0\leq\chi_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{x})\leq 1\,\,\text{when }\boldsymbol{x}\in\overline{\Omega_{\ell}},\\
&\\
\quad\text{ and }&\sum_{\ell}\chi_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{x})=1\,\text{ for all }\boldsymbol{x}\in\overline{\Omega}.\end{array}\right\}

(4)

Then, the parallel Schwarz method for (
1
), (
2
) with Robin (impedance) transmission conditions is: given
u^{n}
defined on
\Omega
, we solve the local problems:

\displaystyle-(\Delta+k^{2})u_{\ell}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=f\quad

\displaystyle\text{in }\ \Omega_{\ell}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{\ell}}-{\rm i}k\right)u_{\ell}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{\ell}}-{\rm i}k\right)u^{n}\quad

\displaystyle\text{on }\ \partial\Omega_{\ell}\backslash\partial\Omega\,,

(6)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{\ell}}-{\rm i}k\right)u_{\ell}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=g\quad

\displaystyle\text{on }\ \partial\Omega_{\ell}\cap\partial\Omega.

(7)

Then the next iterate is the weighted sum of the local solutions

\displaystyle u^{n+1}:=\sum_{\ell}\chi_{\ell}u_{\ell}^{n+1}.

(8)

Information is shared between neighbouring subdomains at each iteration via
(
8
).

In

5
) – (
8
) in the

U(\Omega):=\big\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega):\,\Delta v\in L^{2}(\Omega),\,\partial v/\partial n\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)\big\},

and its local analogues
U(\Omega_{\ell})
.
Using the fact that any function

has impedance trace
(\partial/\partial n-{\rm i}k)v\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
on any Lipschitz curve
\Gamma\subset\Omega_{\ell}
, we prove in

5
) – (

) is well-defined in the space

, and letting
\mathbf{e}^{n}=(e_{1}^{n},\ldots,e_{N}^{n})
, we prove in

that
\mathbf{e}^{n+1}={\mathcal{T}}\mathbf{e}^{n},
where under certain geometric assumptions,
{\mathcal{T}}
has the ‘power contraction’ property

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{T}}^{N}\|\ll 1,

(9)

with respect to the product norm on
\prod_{\ell}U_{0}(\Omega_{\ell})
, where
U_{0}(\Omega_{\ell})
is the subspace of functions
v\in U(\Omega_{\ell})
, for which
\Delta v+k^{2}v=0
on
\Omega_{\ell}
.
Analogously to

, the norm of

is the
L^{2}
norm of its impedance data on
\partial\Omega_{\ell}
.
See the remarks in §
5
, especially (

), for a more precise explanation of (
9
).

The aim of this note is to show that a natural finite element analogue of (
5
) – (

) corresponds to a preconditioned Richardson-type iterative method for the finite element approximation of (

), where the preconditioner is a Helmholtz-orientated version of the popular Restricted Additive Schwarz method.
This preconditioner is given several different names in the literature – WRAS-H (Weighted RAS for Helmholtz)

, ORAS (Optimized Restricted Additive Schwarz)
st2007optimized
;
DoJoNa:15
;
GoGrSp:20
, IMPRAS1 (RAS with impedance boundary condition)

. However it has not previously been directly connected via a variational argument to the iterative method (
5
) – (
8
) in the Helmholtz case, although there are algebraic discussions (e.g.,

,
(
DoJoNa:15
, §2.3.2)
). We also demonstrate numerically in §

Method (

)–(

) is an example of methods
studied more generally in the Optimized Schwarz literature (e.g.,

),
where Robin (or more sophisticated) transmission conditions

Although the transmission condition (

ORAS is arguably the most successful one-level parallel method for Helmholtz problems. It can be applied on very general geometries, does not depend on parameters, and can even be robust to increasing
k

3
Variational formulation of RAS with impedance transmission condition (ORAS)

Here we formulate a finite element approximation of (
1
), (
2
) and show that it coincides with ORAS. We introduce
a nodal finite element space
{\mathcal{V}}^{h}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
consisting of continuous piecewise polynomials of total degree
\leq p
on a conforming mesh
{\mathcal{T}}^{h}
. Functions in
{\mathcal{V}}^{h}
are uniquely determined by their values at
nodes in
\overline{\Omega}
, denoted
\{x_{j}:j\in{\mathcal{I}}\}
, for some index set
{\mathcal{I}}
. The local space on
\overline{\Omega_{\ell}}
is

{\mathcal{V}}_{\ell}^{h}:=\{v_{h}|_{\overline{\Omega_{\ell}}}~:~v_{h}\in{{\mathcal{V}}}^{h}\}
with corresponding nodes denoted
\{x_{j}:j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\ell}\}
, for some
{\mathcal{I}}_{\ell}\subset{\mathcal{I}}
.

Using the sesquilinear form
a
and right-hand side
F
appearing in (
3
), we can define the discrete
operators
{\mathcal{A}}_{h},F_{h}:{\mathcal{V}}^{h}\mapsto({\mathcal{V}}^{h})^{\prime}

by

({\mathcal{A}}_{h}u_{h})(v_{h}):=a(u_{h},v_{h})\quad\text{and}\quad F_{h}(v_{h})=F(v_{h}),\quad\text{for all}\quad u_{h},v_{h}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h}.

(10)

Analogously, on each subdomain
\Omega_{\ell}
, we define
{\mathcal{A}}_{h,\ell}:{\mathcal{V}}_{\ell}^{h}\rightarrow({\mathcal{V}}_{\ell}^{h})^{\prime}
by

({\mathcal{A}}_{h,\ell}u_{h,\ell})(v_{h,\ell}):=a_{\ell}(u_{h,\ell},v_{h,\ell}).

We also need prolongations
\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}^{\top},\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{\top}:{\mathcal{V}}_{\ell}^{h}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}^{h}
defined for all
v_{h,\ell}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{h}_{\ell}
by

(\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}^{\top}v_{h,\ell})(x_{j})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v_{h,\ell}(x_{j})&\quad{j\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\ell}},\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise, }\end{array}\right.\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{\top}v_{h,\ell}=\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}^{\top}(\chi_{\ell}v_{h,\ell}).

(11)

Note the subtlety in (
11
): The extension
\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}^{\top}v_{h,\ell}
is defined
nodewise
: It coincides with
v_{h,\ell}
at nodes in
\overline{\Omega_{\ell}}
and vanishes at nodes in
\Omega\backslash\overline{\Omega_{\ell}}
. Thus
\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}^{\top}v_{h,\ell}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{h}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
. This is
an
H^{1}-
conforming finite element approximation of the zero extension of
v_{h,\ell}
to all of
{\Omega}
. (The zero extension is not in
H^{1}(\Omega)
in general.) We define the restriction operator
\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}:{\mathcal{V}}_{h}^{\prime}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}_{h,\ell}^{\prime}
by duality, i.e., for all

F_{h}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h}^{\prime}
,

(\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}F_{h})(v_{h,\ell}):=F_{h}(\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}^{\top}v_{h,\ell}),\quad v_{h,\ell}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\ell}^{h}.

Then the ORAS preconditioner is the operator
{\mathcal{B}}_{h}^{-1}:{\mathcal{V}}_{h}^{\prime}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}_{h}
defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{h}^{-1}\ :=\ \sum_{\ell}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{\top}{\mathcal{A}}_{h,\ell}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}.

(12)

This preconditioner can also be written in terms of operators
\mathcal{Q}_{h,\ell}:{\mathcal{V}}^{h}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}_{\ell}^{h}
defined for all
u_{h}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{h}
by

\displaystyle a_{\ell}(\mathcal{Q}_{h,\ell}u_{h},v_{h,\ell})\ =\ a(u_{h},\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}^{\top}v_{h,\ell}),\quad\text{for all}\quad v_{h,\ell}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{h}_{\ell},

(13)

where
\mathcal{R}^{\top}_{h,\ell}
is defined in (
11
), and then

{\mathcal{B}}_{h}^{-1}=\sum_{\ell}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\top}_{h,\ell}\mathcal{Q}_{h,\ell}
.
The corresponding preconditioned Richardson iterative method can be written as

u^{n+1}_{h}=u^{n}_{h}+{\mathcal{B}}_{h}^{-1}(F_{h}-{\mathcal{A}}_{h}u_{h}^{n}).

(14)

The matrix realisation of (
14
) is given in §
5
.

4
Connecting the parallel iterative method with ORAS

In this section, we show that a natural finite element approximation of (
5
)–(
8
) yields (
14
).
First, to write (
5
) - (
8
) in a residual correction form, we introduce the “corrections”
\delta_{\ell}^{n}:=u_{\ell}^{n+1}-u^{n}|_{\overline{\Omega_{\ell}}},
. With this definition we have

\displaystyle-(\Delta+k^{2})\delta_{\ell}^{n}

\displaystyle=f+(\Delta+k^{2})u^{n}\quad\text{in }\ \Omega_{\ell}\,,

(15)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{\ell}}-{\rm i}k\right)\delta_{\ell}^{n}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on }\ \partial\Omega_{\ell}\backslash\partial\Omega\,,

(16)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{\ell}}-{\rm i}k\right)\delta_{\ell}^{n}

\displaystyle=g-\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{\ell}}-{\rm i}k\right)u^{n}\quad\text{on }\ \partial\Omega_{\ell}\cap\partial\Omega\,,

(17)

and then

\displaystyle u^{n+1}=u^{n}+\sum_{\ell}\chi_{\ell}\delta_{\ell}^{n}.

(18)

Note, there is more subtlety here: Because of (
8
),
u^{n}|_{\overline{\Omega_{\ell}}}
is
not
the same as
u^{n}_{\ell}
. The theory in
GoGaGrLaSp:21
can be used to show that (
15
)–(
18
) is still well-posed in
U(\Omega)
. Multiplying (
15
) by
v_{\ell}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\ell})
, integrating by parts and using (
16
), (
17
),
\delta_{\ell}^{n}
satisfies, for
v_{\ell}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\ell})
,

\displaystyle a_{\ell}(\delta_{\ell}^{n},v_{\ell})

\displaystyle\ =\ \int_{\Omega_{\ell}}f\,\overline{v_{\ell}}\ +\ \int_{\partial\Omega_{\ell}\cap\partial\Omega}g\,\overline{v_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\ +\ \ \int_{\Omega_{\ell}}(\Delta+k^{2})u^{n}\,\overline{v_{\ell}}\ -\ \int_{\partial\Omega_{\ell}\cap\partial\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{\ell}}-{\rm i}k\right)u^{n}\,\overline{v_{\ell}}.

(19)

To implement the finite element discretization of this, we will need to handle the case when
u^{n}
on the right-hand side is replaced by a given iterate
u_{h}^{n}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{h}
and when the test function

v_{\ell}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\ell})
is replaced by
v_{h,\ell}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{h}_{\ell}
. The third term on the right hand side of (
19
) then requires integration by parts to make sense. Using the nodewise extension
\mathcal{R}^{\top}_{h,\ell}
we replace the third and fouth terms in (
19
) by

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\Delta+k^{2})u^{n}\,\overline{\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}^{\top}v_{h,\ell}}\ -\ \int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial n}-{\rm i}k\right)u^{n}\,\,\overline{\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}^{\top}v_{h,\ell}}\ =\ -a(u^{n},\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}^{\top}v_{h,\ell}),

(20)

where the right-hand side is obtained from the left via integration by parts over
\Omega
. This leads to the FEM analogue of (
15
) – (
18
): Suppose
u_{h}^{n}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{h}
is given. Then

\displaystyle u_{h}^{n+1}:=u_{h}^{n}+\sum_{\ell}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{h,\ell}^{\top}\delta_{h}^{n},

(21)

where (using
(
19
), (
20
) and (
10
)),

a_{\ell}(\delta_{h,\ell}^{n},v_{h,\ell})\ =\ \mathcal{R}_{\ell,h}F_{h}\ -\ a(u^{n},\mathcal{R}_{\ell,h}^{\top}v_{\ell,h}).

Thus,

\delta_{h,\ell}^{n}\ =\ {\mathcal{A}}_{h,\ell}^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{h,\ell}(F_{h}-{\mathcal{A}}_{h}u_{h}^{n}).

Combining this with (
21
), we obtain exactly (
14
).

5
Numerical results

Denoting the nodal bases for
{\mathcal{V}}^{h}
and
{\mathcal{V}}_{\ell}^{h}
by

\{\phi_{j}\}

and
\{\phi_{\ell,j}\}
respectively, we introduce stiffness matrices

{\mathsf{A}}_{i,j}:=a(\phi_{j},\phi_{i})

and

({\mathsf{A}}_{\ell})_{i,j}:=a_{\ell}(\phi_{\ell,j},\phi_{\ell,i})
, and the load vector
f_{i}:=F_{h}(\phi_{i})
. Then we can write (
14
) as

\mathsf{\mathbf{u}}^{n+1}=\mathsf{\mathbf{u}}^{n}+{\mathsf{B}}^{-1}(\mathsf{\mathbf{f}}-{\mathsf{A}}\mathsf{\mathbf{u}}^{n}).

(22)

Here

\mathsf{\mathbf{u}}^{n}
is the coefficient vector of
u_{h}^{n}
with respect to the nodal basis of
{\mathcal{V}}^{h}
, and

{\mathsf{B}}^{-1}=\sum_{\ell}\tilde{{\mathsf{R}}}_{\ell}^{\top}{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}^{-1}{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell}\,,

where

({\mathsf{R}}_{\ell}^{\top})_{p,q}:=(\mathcal{R}_{\ell}^{\top}\phi_{\ell,q})(x_{p})
,
(\tilde{{\mathsf{R}}}_{\ell}^{\top})_{p,q}:=(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\ell}^{\top}\phi_{\ell,q})(x_{\ell,p}),

and
{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell}=({\mathsf{R}}_{\ell}^{\top})^{\top}.

In this section, (motivated by (
9
)), we numerically investigate the contractive property of the ORAS iteration (
22
). Letting
\mathsf{\mathbf{u}}
be the solution of

{\mathsf{A}}\mathsf{\mathbf{u}}=\mathsf{\mathbf{f}},

we can combine with (
22
) to obtain the error propagation equation

\mathsf{\mathbf{u}}^{n+1}-\mathsf{\mathbf{u}}={\mathsf{E}}(\mathsf{\mathbf{u}}^{n}-\mathsf{\mathbf{u}}),\quad\text{where}\quad{\mathsf{E}}={\mathsf{I}}-{\mathsf{B}}^{-1}A.

Since
\sum_{\ell}\tilde{{\mathsf{R}}}^{\top}_{\ell}{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell}={\mathsf{I}}
, we can write

{\mathsf{E}}=\sum_{\ell}\tilde{{\mathsf{R}}}_{\ell}^{\top}({\mathsf{R}}_{\ell}-{\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}^{-1}{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell}{\mathsf{A}})=\tilde{{\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}}^{\top}({\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}-{\mathsf{\mathbf{Q}}}),

where
\tilde{{\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}}^{\top}
is the row vector of matrices:
\tilde{{\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}}^{\top}=(\tilde{{\mathsf{R}}}_{1}^{\top},\tilde{{\mathsf{R}}}_{2}^{\top},\cdots,\tilde{{\mathsf{R}}}_{N}^{\top})
, and
{\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}=({\mathsf{R}}_{1};{\mathsf{R}}_{2};\cdots;{\mathsf{R}}_{N})
, and
{\mathsf{\mathbf{Q}}}=({\mathsf{A}}_{1}^{-1}{\mathsf{R}}_{1}{\mathsf{A}};{\mathsf{A}}_{2}^{-1}{\mathsf{R}}_{2}{\mathsf{A}},\cdots,{\mathsf{A}}_{N}^{-1}{\mathsf{R}}_{N}{\mathsf{A}})
are column vectors.
Then it is easily seen that
{\mathsf{E}}\tilde{{\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}}^{\top}=\tilde{{\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}}^{\top}{\mathsf{\mathbf{T}}}
, where
{\mathsf{\mathbf{T}}}:=({\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}-{\mathsf{\mathbf{Q}}})\tilde{{\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}}^{\top}
. Moreover, since
\tilde{{\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}}^{\top}{\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}={\mathsf{I}}
, we have
{\mathsf{\mathbf{T}}}{\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}\tilde{{\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}}^{\top}={\mathsf{\mathbf{T}}}
, and so it follows that

\displaystyle{\mathsf{E}}^{s}=\tilde{{\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}}^{\top}{\mathsf{\mathbf{T}}}^{s}{\mathsf{\mathbf{R}}}\quad\text{for any}\quad s\geq 1,\quad

(23)

As explained in
(

, §5.1)
,
{\mathsf{\mathbf{T}}}
is a discrete version of the operator
{\mathcal{T}}
appearing in (
9
) above.
In

, we study fixed point iterations with matrix

{\mathsf{\mathbf{T}}}
and use these to illustrate various properties of the fixed point operator
{\mathcal{T}}

in the product norm described above. In this paper we consider only the norms of

To compute the norm of
{\mathsf{E}}^{s}
, we introduce the vector norm:
\|\mathsf{\mathbf{u}}\|_{1,k}^{2}=\mathsf{\mathbf{u}}^{*}{\mathsf{D}}_{k}\mathsf{\mathbf{u}}
, for

\|{\mathsf{E}}^{s}\|:=\max_{0\neq\mathsf{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}}\frac{\|{\mathsf{E}}^{s}\mathsf{\mathbf{v}}\|_{1,k}}{\|\mathsf{\mathbf{v}}\|_{1,k}},\quad\text{for integers}\quad s\geq 1,

. This is computed using the SLEPc facility within the package FreeFEM++

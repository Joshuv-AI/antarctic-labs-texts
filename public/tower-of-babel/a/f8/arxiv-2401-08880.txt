Hamiltonian analysis in Lie-Poisson gauge theory
Francesco Bascone, Maxim Kurkov

Abstract
Lie-Poisson gauge formalism provides a semiclassical description of noncommutative U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge theory with Lie algebra type noncommutativity. Using the Dirac approach to constrained Hamiltonian systems, we focus on a class of Lie-Poisson gauge models, which exhibit an admissible Lagrangian description. The underlying noncommutativity is supposed to be purely spatial. Analysing the constraints, we demonstrate that these models have as many physical degrees of freedom as there are present in the Maxwell theory.

2
Basics of the (Lie-) Poisson gauge formalism

First we discuss the noncommutative geometric origin of the (Lie-) Poisson gauge theory. After that we introduce the main technical elements of the formalism, which will be used in the subsequent analysis of Hamiltonian constraints in Sec.
3
.

a. Poisson gauge theory and noncommutative geometry

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a
n
-dimensional manifold, representing the space-time. The local coordinates on
\mathscr{M}
are denoted through
x^{\mu}
,
\mu=0,...,n-1
. The noncommutative structure of
\mathscr{M}
is described by the Kontsevich star product,

f(x)\star g(x)=f(x)\cdot g(x)+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\{f(x),g(x)\}+\dots,\quad f,g\in C^{\infty}(\mathscr{M}),

(2.1)

where the Poisson bracket

\{f,g\}=\Theta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}f\,\partial_{\nu}g,\quad f,g\in C^{\infty}(\mathscr{M}),

(2.2)

is related to the Poisson bivector
\Theta^{\mu\nu}
, and dots stand for the remaining terms, which contain higher derivatives of
f
and
g
.

Let
A_{\mu}(x)
be a
U(1)
gauge connection on
\mathscr{M}
.
According to the proposal of
[
7
]
, noncommutative deformations of the
U(1)
gauge theory must satisfy the two postulates listed below.

•

Infinitesimal gauge transformations must close the non-commutative gauge algebra,

\left[\delta_{f},\delta_{g}\right]A_{\mu}(x)=\delta_{-\mathrm{i}[f(x),g(x)]_{\star}}A_{\mu}(x),

(2.3)

where

[f(x),g(x)]_{\star}:=f(x)\star g(x)-g(x)\star f(x),\qquad f,g\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathscr{M}).

(2.4)

•

At the commutative limit the infinitesimal gauge transformations must recover the standard Abelian gauge transformations,

\lim_{\Theta\rightarrow 0}\delta_{f}A_{\mu}(x)=\partial_{\mu}f(x).

(2.5)

In the semiclassical approximation,

[f(x),g(x)]_{\star}\simeq\mathrm{i}\{f(x),g(x)\},

(2.6)

so the full non-commutative algebra (
2.3
) reduces to the
Poisson gauge algebra
,

\left[\delta_{f},\delta_{g}\right]A_{\mu}=\delta_{\{f,g\}}A_{\mu},\qquad f,g\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathscr{M}).

(2.7)

By definition, the
Poisson gauge theory
is a field theoretical model with the gauge transformations, closing the algebra (
2.7
) and obeying the commutative limit (
2.5
). In other words, the PGT is a semiclassical approximation of the noncommutative
U(1)
gauge theory, constructed along the lines of
[
7
]
.

In this paper we shall focus on a special class of extremely useful Poisson structures, which are linear in the space-time coordinates,

\Theta^{\mu\nu}(x)=f_{\alpha}^{\mu\nu}\,x^{\alpha}.

(2.8)

The tensor
\Theta
is a Poisson bivector iff

f_{\mu}^{\tau\alpha}f_{\alpha}^{\beta\delta}+f_{\mu}^{\beta\alpha}f_{\alpha}^{\delta\tau}+f_{\mu}^{\delta\alpha}f_{\alpha}^{\tau\beta}=0,

(2.9)

what is nothing but the Jacobi identity for the structure constants of a Lie algebra.
In
[
12
]
the PGTs of this type were called
Lie-Poisson gauge theories
. From now on we limit ourselves to the Lie-Poisson gauge models only.

b. General construction

The key technical elements of the formalism are the field-dependent matrices
\gamma(A)
and
\rho(A)
, which solve the master equations,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\gamma_{\mu}^{\nu}\partial^{\mu}_{A}\gamma^{\xi}_{{\lambda}}-\gamma^{\xi}_{\mu}\partial_{A}^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}_{\lambda}=\gamma^{\mu}_{\lambda}f_{\mu}^{\nu\xi}\\
\gamma^{\nu}_{\lambda}\partial_{A}^{\lambda}\rho_{\xi}^{\mu}\,+\rho_{\xi}^{\lambda}\partial_{A}^{\mu}\gamma_{\lambda}^{\nu}=0\end{array}\right.,\qquad\partial_{A}^{\lambda}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial A_{\lambda}},

(2.10)

and tend to the identity matrix at the commutative limit,

\lim_{\Theta\to 0}\gamma=\mathbb{I},\qquad\lim_{\Theta\to 0}\rho=\mathbb{I}.

(2.11)

The explicit solutions of these equations were obtained in
[
9
]
and
[
20
]
for arbitrary structure constants

The infinitesimal Poisson gauge transformations, which close the gauge algebra (

2.5

\delta_{f}A_{\mu}(x)=\gamma_{\mu}^{\nu}(A(x))\partial_{\nu}f(x)+\left\{A_{\mu}(x),f(x)\right\},\qquad f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathscr{M}).

(2.12)

Another important constituent of the PGT formalism is the deformed field strength
\mathscr{F}_{\mu\nu}

\delta_{f}\mathscr{F}_{\mu\nu}(x)=\left\{\mathscr{F}_{\mu\nu}(x),f(x)\right\},\qquad f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathscr{M}),

(2.13)

and reproduces the standard Abelian field strength at the commutative limit,

\lim_{\Theta\rightarrow 0}\mathscr{F}_{\mu\nu}(x)=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}(x)-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}(x)=:F_{\mu\nu}.

(2.14)

The expression for this field strength was obtained in

\mathscr{F}_{\mu\nu}(x)=\rho_{\mu}^{\alpha}(A(x))\rho_{\nu}^{\beta}(A(x))\left(\gamma_{\alpha}^{\delta}(A(x))\partial_{\delta}A_{\beta}-\gamma_{\beta}^{\delta}(A(x))\partial_{\delta}A_{\alpha}+\left\{A_{\alpha},A_{\beta}\right\}\right).

(2.15)

From now on we assume that
\mathscr{M}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the coordinates

are Cartesian. We also assume that
\mathscr{M}
is equipped with the Minkowski metric,

\eta_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}\big(+1,\,-1,...,-1\big)_{\mu\nu}=\eta^{\mu\nu},

(2.16)

which will be used to rise and to lower indices. The spatial coordinates

Iff the structure constants

(2.17)

(2.18)

where the local gauge-covariant Lagrangian density

At the commutative limit the expression (

Consider a Lie algebra type noncommutativity, which does not involve the time variable

3
Hamiltonian constraint analysis

Below we derive and analyse the primary and the secondary constraints. We shall see that these constraints are of the first class, whilst no other constraints are present.

a. Canonical Hamiltonian and primary constraints

Consider a Lagrangian functional,

L[A,\dot{A}]=\int_{\Sigma(x^{0})}{\mathrm{d}}^{n-1}x\,\mathscr{L},

(3.1)

where the Lagrangian density is defined by Eq. (
2.19

\Sigma(x^{0})=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}

Hereafter we use a dot notation for the time derivative, in particular,

\dot{A}_{\mu}=\frac{\partial A_{\mu}}{\partial x^{0}}.

Introducing the momenta, conjugated to

\Pi^{\mu}:=\frac{\delta L}{\delta\dot{A}_{\mu}}=\frac{\partial\mathscr{L}}{\partial\dot{A}_{\mu}}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{F}^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial\mathscr{F}_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial\dot{A}_{\mu}}=-\rho_{\alpha}^{\mu}\mathscr{F}^{0\alpha},

(3.2)

\phi^{1}(x)\thickapprox 0,\qquad\phi^{1}(x):=\Pi^{0}(x),

(3.3)

One can easily check that no other primary constraints are present, and the generalised velocities

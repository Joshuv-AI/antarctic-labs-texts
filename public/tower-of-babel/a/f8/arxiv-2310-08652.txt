Arithmetic Deformation of Line Bundles
David Urbanik, Ziquan Yang

Abstract
We introduce a new method to study mixed characteristic deformation of line bundles. In particular, for sufficiently large smooth projective families f : 𝒳 → 𝒮 f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{S} defined over the ring of N N -integers 𝒪 L ​ [ 1 / N ] \mathcal{O}_{L}[1/N] of a number field L L , we produce a proper closed subscheme ℰ ⊊ 𝒮 \mathcal{E}\subsetneq\mathcal{S} outside of which all line bundles appearing in positive characteristic fibres of f f admit characteristic zero lifts. This in particular applies to elliptic surfaces over ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} and projective hypersurfaces in ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} of degree d ≥ 5 d\geq 5 . We also study the locus in ℰ \mathcal{E} in more detail in the h 0 , 2 = 2 h^{0,2}=2 case.

Introduction

1.1 Main Results

In
[
Del81
]
, Deligne proved that every line bundle on a K3 surface in positive characteristic lifts to characteristic
0
along with the surface. Deligne’s theorem and its extensions have historically played a pivotal role in addressing the Tate conjecture for K3 surfaces and varieties alike in positive characteristic, because a mainstream method relies on considering characteristic
0
liftings and connecting the Tate conjecture to its Hodge-theoretic analogue, i.e., the Lefschetz
(1,1)
-theorem (e.g.,
[
Nyg83
,
NO85
,
Mad15
,
HYZ23
]
). To explore the potential for future advancements in this direction, we pose the following question:
In an arithmetic family, can we generate all line bundles on the characteristic
p
fibers by specializing line bundles on the characteristic
0
fibers?

To set up a context for the question, let
L\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be a number field and
\mathcal{O}_{L}
be its ring of integers. Suppose that
\mathcal{S}
is a smooth quasi-projective
\mathcal{O}_{L}[1/N]
-scheme for some
N\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
with
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
connected, and
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{S}
is a polarized smooth projective family with geometrically connected fibers. Then we provide the following answer to address the above question.

Theorem (1.1.1)
.

Suppose that
f_{\mathbb{C}}
has large monodromy and sufficiently large period image. Then there exists a proper closed subscheme
\mathcal{E}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}
such that for every algebraically closed field
k
of positive characteristic and
s\in\mathcal{S}(k)\setminus\mathcal{E}(k)
, every line bundle
\xi
on the fibre
\mathcal{X}_{s}
lifts to characteristic
0
.

By “
\xi
lifts to characteristic
0
”, we mean that there exists a finite extension
K
of
W(k)[1/p]
and a point
\widetilde{s}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
lifting
s
, such that
\xi
lies in the image of the specialization map
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{\widetilde{s}})\to\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
. We now define the conditions in the hypothesis. Their necessity will be discussed in §
6.3
.

Definition (1.1.2)
.

Let
S
be a connected
\mathbb{C}
-variety and
X\to S
be a smooth projective morphism with connected fibers.

•

We say that
X\to S
has
large monodromy
if for some
\mathbb{C}
-point
s\in S
not lying on the Noether-Lefschetz locus, the Zariski closure of the image of the monodromy representation

\pi_{1}(S,s)\to\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{H}^{2}(X_{s},\mathbb{Q}))

contains
\mathrm{SO}(\mathrm{T}^{2}(X_{s},\mathbb{Q}))
as the identity component. Here we are equipping
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X_{s},\mathbb{Q})
with the symmetric bilinear form induced by the chosen polarization, and taking
\mathrm{T}^{2}(X_{s},\mathbb{Q})
to be the orthogonal component of the subspace of
(1,1)
-classes.

•

We say that
X\to S
has
sufficiently large period image
if for some (and hence a general)
\mathbb{C}
-point
s\in S
, the Kodaira-Spencer map

T_{s}S\to\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega^{1}_{X_{s}}),\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}}))

(1)

has image of dimension at least
h^{0,2}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{s}})
.

The proof of Thm
(1.1.1)
yields structural theorems for the
arithmetic Noether-Lefschetz (NL) locus,
and the relative Picard scheme
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{S}}
. We say that a proper closed reduced subscheme
Z\subseteq\mathcal{S}
is a component of the arithmetic NL locus if it is the scheme-theoretic image of an irreducible component of the reduced subscheme of
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{S}}
which does not dominant
\mathcal{S}
. Then the arithmetic NL locus, which we shall denote by
\mathcal{N}
, is the collection of all such (typically infinitely many) components. It is characterized by the property that a geometric point
s\to\mathcal{S}
factors through
\mathcal{N}
if and only if
\mathrm{rank\,}\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal{X}_{s})>\mathrm{rank\,}\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\eta}})
, where
\bar{\eta}
is a geometric generic point of
\mathcal{S}
. Then we have the following theorem.

Theorem (1.1.3)
.

In the situation of Thm
(1.1.1)
, the restriction of
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{S}}
over
\mathcal{S}\backslash\mathcal{E}
is syntomic (i.e., flat and lci) over
\mathcal{O}_{L}[1/N]
, and the restriction of every component of
\mathcal{N}
over
\mathcal{S}\backslash\mathcal{E}
is flat over
\mathcal{O}_{L}[1/N]
and has codimension exactly
h^{0,2}(\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\eta}})
in
\mathcal{S}\backslash\mathcal{E}
.

In particular, the above theorem implies that when restricted to
\mathcal{S}\backslash\mathcal{E}
the arithmetic NL locus
\mathcal{N}
can be recovered from the variation of Hodge structures (VHS) on
R^{2}f_{\mathbb{C}*}\mathbb{Q}
, as every component of
\mathcal{N}
is the Zariski closure of some component of the usual (Hodge-theoretic) NL locus over
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C})
. We also remark that our method will give an explicit construction of
\mathcal{E}
: Away from small primes,
\mathcal{E}
is the Zariski closure of a so-called “atypical Hodge locus” on
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
(
[
BKU22
]
) and the locus where the Kodaira-Spencer map has rank
<h^{0,2}
. The issue of determining
\mathcal{E}
effectively is discussed at the end of §
1.3
.

To illustrate what might happen on
\mathcal{E}
, we give a thorough analysis in the case when
h^{0,2}=2
. See §
5.2
. One conclusion we can draw is the following (cf.
(5.2.4)
):

Theorem (1.1.4)
.

In the situation of
(1.1.1)
, suppose in addition for every
s\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C})
,
h^{0,2}(\mathcal{X}_{s})=2
and the Kodaira-Spencer map (
1
) has rank
\geq 2
.

Then for
p\gg 0
, the following holds: Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
and take any
s\in S(k)
and
\xi\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
. Up to equi-characteristic deformations of the pair
(s,\xi)
,
\xi^{m}
is a sum of liftable line bundles for some
m\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
.

The upshot of the above conclusion is that, when
p\gg 0
and
s
lies on
\mathcal{E}
even if
\xi
is non-liftable, it still comes from specializing and deforming line bundles in characteristic
0
, albeit in a more indirect way. However, in general we expect that the complexity of possible behaviours that occur over the locus
\mathcal{E}
to grow quickly as
h^{0,2}
increases.

1.2 Applications

We will give applications of our results to general type and elliptic surfaces. Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
.

Theorem (1.2.1)
.

Let
d\geq 5
be a number. If
p\gg 0
, then for a general degree
d
hypersurface
Y
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}
, every
\xi\in\mathrm{Pic}(Y)
is liftable.

Here the word “general” means that
Y
comes from a Zariski open dense subscheme of a natural parameter space. Note that the above surfaces are of general type.

Let
\pi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be an elliptic surface (with a zero section) of height
h:=\mathrm{deg\,}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\vee}
. We say that
X
is
admissible
if all geometric fibers of
\pi
are irreducible. This condition is generically satisfied by elliptic surfaces over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with a fixed
h
.

Theorem (1.2.2)
.

If
p\gg 0
, then for a general admissible
X
over
k
, every
\xi\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
is liftable.

When
h=3
,
(1.1.4)
allows us to remove the word “general” at the expense of a weaker conclusion:

Theorem (1.2.3)
.

If
p\gg 0
,
h=3
and
X
is admissible, then for every
\xi\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
, we may equi-characteristically deform
(X,\xi)
to another pair
(X^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})
, such that some positive power of
\xi^{\prime}
is a sum of liftable line bundles.

The precise forms of the above theorems are given in §
6
.

Finally we remark that elliptic surfaces are of particular interest because their line bundles give rise to rational points on their generic fibers, which are elliptic curves over function fields. Hence lifting results in the style of
(1.2.2)
suggest a way of constructing all rational points on an elliptic curve over a global function field by eventually appealing to the Lefschetz
(1,1)
-theorem. This idea was indeed used before, in an earlier work of the second author
[
HYZ23
]
joint with Hamacher and Zhao, to establish a new case of the function field BSD conjecture. It is our hope that
(1.2.2)
is a start for future progress.

1.3 Methods

The key new idea in our work is that one can apply the theory of Hodge-theoretic unlikely intersections over
\mathbb{C}
to understand the liftability of line bundles in positive characteristic. This is noteworthy because the former is highly transcendental whereas the latter is completely algebraic. To explain how this works, let us first give an informal overview of how line bundles deform, starting with the
\mathbb{C}
-fibre
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
. If
Z\subset\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
is a component of the Noether-Lefschetz locus coming from deforming a line bundle
\xi
on
\mathcal{X}_{s}
for some
s\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C})
, then analytically locally at
s
one can view
Z
as the set of points
t
where parallel transport of the Betti class
c_{1}(\xi)
to
t
remains in
\mathrm{Fil}^{1}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}_{t}/\mathbb{C})
, the middle piece of the Hodge filtration. Then it is not hard to see that
Z
is locally cut out at
s
by
h^{0,2}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
equations.

Now, by generalizing a result of Deligne
[
Del81
, Prop. 1.5]
, we explain in
(2.2.9)
that under some mild assumptions the same statement also applies on the integral level. Suppose that
k
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
,
s
is a
k
-point on
\mathcal{S}
and
\xi\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
, and assume for simplicity that
\mathcal{O}_{L}[1/N]
is unramified over
\mathbb{Z}[1/N]
. Then there is formal subscheme
\mathrm{Def}(\xi)
inside the formal completion of
\mathcal{S}{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}_{L}[1/N]}W(k)
at
s
such that
\mathrm{Def}(\xi)
is the maximal locus to which
\xi
deforms and is cut out by
h^{0,2}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
equations. The analogue of the locus
Z
in the preceding paragraph is the Zariski closure of
\mathrm{Def}(\xi)
in
\mathcal{S}
, which we shall denote by
\mathcal{Z}
. But — and this where the connection to unlikely intersections appears — passing to the integral level gains us a dimension. More specifically, if
\xi
does not deform to characteristic zero, which is to say that
\mathrm{Def}(\xi)
has no characteristic zero fiber, then the codimension of
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
inside
\mathcal{S}_{k}
must be
at most

h^{0,2}(\mathcal{X}_{s})-1
.

Let us first explain why it would be unusual to see such a phenomenon in characteristic zero. Indeed, if we consider again the locus
Z\subset\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
in the first paragraph, then we can alternatively define the germ of
Z
at
s
in the following way: consider a Hodge-theoretic period map
\psi:B\to\widecheck{D}
on a small ball
B\subset\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C})
around
s
, and define
Z\cap B
as
\psi^{-1}(\widecheck{D}_{c_{1}(\xi)})
, where
\widecheck{D}_{c_{1}(\xi)}\subset\widecheck{D}
is a flag subvariety of codimension
h^{0,2}
. Then if
\Gamma_{\psi}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\times\widecheck{D}
is the graph of
\psi
, the intersection between
\Gamma_{\psi}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\times\widecheck{D}_{c^{1}(\xi_{s})}
is now unlikely: it has codimension at most
\dim\widecheck{D}+(h^{0,2}-1)
, smaller than the “expected” codimension of
\dim\widecheck{D}+h^{0,2}
.

Let us call such a
Z
giving rise to an unlikely intersection of this type an ‘‘atypical Hodge locus’’.

Let us make two further comments about our large monodromy and period image hypotheses. The methods of this paper should apply even when
f
does not have large monodromy, although the reasoning becomes more complicated. In order to apply the Ax-Schanuel theorem, the flag variety
\widecheck{D}
must be taken to be an orbit of the algebraic monodromy group. If this group shrinks, so does
\widecheck{D}
, which means that the expected codimension of
Z
becomes smaller. One then has to take this into account at the crystalline level, which to our knowledge requires knowing that the global cycles defining the monodromy group satisfy absolute-Hodge-like properties. We plan future work to deal with this more refined situation.

Secondly, the “sufficiently large period image” assumption is necessary to ensure that if
Z
is atypical, it is also positive-dimensional (in the sense of period dimension). As we explain in §
6.3
below, our results are false without this assumption, and the fact that geometric Zilber-Pink reasoning requires the intersections to be positive dimensional allows one to see the necessity of this assumption in a different way.

Comment on Effectivity

If the family in
(1.1.1)
or
(1.1.3)
is known to satisfy some stronger conditions (take
R=\mathcal{O}_{L}[1/N]
case in §
4.1
), then the locus
\mathcal{E}
appearing in the statements of the theorems and various loci which appeared in the sketch above can in principle be computed by an algorithm run on a sufficiently powerful computer, given explicit projective equations for the family as input. In the case of the generic fibres of these loci (i.e., if we were to compute them over a characteristic zero field), the necessary theory for writing down these algorithms is developed in
[
Urb23
]
. In particular,
[
Urb23
]
gives explicit algorithms for computing the Hodge bundle, Hodge filtration, and Gauss-Manin connection from a set of projective equations as well as for computing all of the jet-related constructions we will use. Subject to limitations in the computational literature concerning algebro-geometric constructions over a more general base ring
R=\mathcal{O}_{L}[1/N]
— for instance, we are not aware of a detailed algorithmic approach to Chevalley’s Theorem on images of constructible sets in this context — this could be generalized to compute the locus in our paper as well.

1.4 Notations and Conventions

Let
S
be a scheme. For a point or geometric point
s
on
S
, we write
k(s)
for the field of definition for
s
. An alg-geometric point on
S
is a geometric point
s\to S
such that
k(s)
is algebraically (as opposed to just separably) closed. For a geometric point
s\to S
, we denote its image on the underlying topological space of
S
by
s^{\flat}
. We will often consider smooth projective morphisms
\mathcal{X}\to S
such that
h^{i,j}(\mathcal{X}_{s}):=\dim_{k(s)}\mathrm{H}^{j}(\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}})
is independent of
s\in S
. In this case, we will simply write this number as
h^{i,j}
when the family
\mathcal{X}/S
is understood.

Suppose that
R^{\prime}\to R
is a morphism between complete local rings. Then we define the
scheme theoretic image
of the induced morphism
\mathrm{Spf}(R)\to\mathrm{Spf}(R^{\prime})
to be the formal subscheme
\mathrm{Spf}(R^{\prime\prime})\subseteq\mathrm{Spf}(R^{\prime})
, where
R^{\prime\prime}=R^{\prime}/(\ker(R^{\prime}\to R))
. Note that
\mathrm{Spec}(R^{\prime\prime})\subseteq\mathrm{Spec}(R^{\prime})
is the scheme theoretic image of
\mathrm{Spec}(R)\to\mathrm{Spec}(R^{\prime})
in the usual sense
[
Sta22
, 01R5]
. We abbreviate “local complete intersection” to “lci”.

When
S
is a smooth complex variety, by a variation of Hodge structures
\mathbb{V}
on
S
we shall mean a
\mathbb{Q}
-VHS unless otherwise noted. Moreover, we write the
\mathbb{Q}
-local system as
\mathbb{V}_{B}
and the filtered flat vector bundle as
\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{dR}}
, so that
\mathbb{V}
is given by the pair
(\mathbb{V}_{B},\mathbb{V}_{\mathrm{dR}})
.

When
S
is a scheme and
\mathcal{X}\to S
is a smooth projective morphism with geometrically connected fibers, we use
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
to denote the relative Picard scheme (see §
2.1
below). If
k
is a field and
X
is a geometrically connected smooth projective variety over
k
, then we let
\mathscr{P}^{0}_{X/k}
denote the identity component of
\mathscr{P}_{X/k}
. The Néron-Severi group
\mathrm{NS}(X)
of
X/k
is defined to be the group of connected components
\mathscr{P}_{X/k}/\mathscr{P}^{0}_{X/k}
. For two line bundles
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
, we say that they are algebraically equivalent (denoted by
\xi\sim\xi^{\prime}
) if they give the same class in
\mathrm{NS}(X)
; similarly, we write
\xi\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\xi^{\prime}
if their classes in
\mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
differ by a
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
-multiple.

Deformation Theory

2.1 Generalities on Picard Schemes

We briefly recall some basic facts about the (relative) Picard scheme and set up some preliminary lemmas.
In this section, we shall always assume that
S
is a base scheme and
f:\mathcal{X}\to S
is a smooth and projective morphism with connected geometric fibers.

By a fundamental theorem of Artin and Grothendieck (
[
BLR90
, §8.4 Thm 3]
, see also
[
Kle05
, Thm 4.8]
), the sheafification of the presheaf on the fppf site of
S
defined by

T\mapsto\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{T})/\mathrm{Pic}(T)

is representable by a separated scheme
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
over
S
which is locally of finite type; moreover, every closed subscheme of
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
which is of finite type over
S
is projective over
S
. We often implicitly use the fact that the formation of
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
commutes with base change (
[
Kle05
, Ex. 4.4]
). Another handy fact we shall often use is that if
f
admits a section, then
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{X}/S}(S)=\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{S})/\mathrm{Pic}(S)
(
[
BLR90
, §8.1 Prop. 4]
), i.e., sheafification does not add new global sections.

The following fact is a special case of
[
Kle05
, Prop. 5.20]
.

Proposition (2.1.1)
.

Assume that for every geometric point
s\to S
, the identity component
\mathscr{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}/s}
of
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}/s}
is smooth of a fixed dimension. Then
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
has an open and closed subscheme
\mathscr{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
which is of finite type over
S
and whose fiber above a geometric point
s\to S
is
\mathscr{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}/s}
. Morevoer, if
S
is reduced, then
\mathscr{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
is smooth over
S
.

Definition (2.1.2)
.

We will often consider pairs of the form
(s,\xi)
where
s\to S
is a alg-geometric point and
\xi\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
is a line bundle on
\mathcal{X}_{s}
. We refer to such a pair as a LB-pair. Note that a LB-pair corresponds bijectively to an alg-geometric point on
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
.

Definition (2.1.3)
.

Let
\mathscr{P}(S,s,\xi)
be the localization of
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
at
(s,\xi)^{\flat}
(i.e., the spectrum of the local ring of
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
at
(s,\xi)^{\flat}
), and
Z(S,s,\xi)\subseteq S
be the scheme-theoretic image of
\mathscr{P}(S,s,\xi)
. We say that
(s,\xi)
is
liftable
(resp.
strongly liftable
) if
\mathscr{P}(S,s,\xi)
has a non-empty (resp. topologically dense) characteristic
0
fiber. When
S
is an integral scheme, we say that
(s,\xi)
is
global
(on
S
) if
\mathscr{P}(S,s,\xi)
dominates
S
.

Below we give a few lemmas about the above definition.

Lemma (2.1.4)
.

Let
(V,(\pi))
be a DVR with fraction field
F
and
S
be a
V
-scheme. Let
Y
be an
F
-scheme admitting a quasi-compact
V
-morphism to
S
. Then the scheme-theoretic image
Z
of
Y
is flat over
V
.

Proof.

We may assume that
S=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
and
Y=\mathrm{Spec}(B)
are affine. Suppose that
Z_{F}\to S
is given by a morphism of
V
-algebras
\varphi:A\to B
. Then
Z
is given by
A/\ker(\varphi)
(
[
Sta22
, 01R8]
). The flatness of
Z
is equivalent to the
\pi
-torsion-freeness of
A/\ker(\varphi)
, i.e., if
a\not\in\ker(\varphi)
, then

2.2 Local Deformation Spaces

(2.2.1)

Let
\kappa
be a field of characteristic
0
,
R
be a complete local
\kappa
-algebra with residue field
\kappa
. Let
S:=\mathrm{Spf}(R)
and
s
be the special point. Suppose that
f:\mathcal{X}\to S
is a smooth projective family with geometrically connected fibers. Let
\xi\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
be any element. Then there exists a closed formal subscheme
\mathrm{Def}(\xi)\subseteq S
characterized by the property that for every Artin
\kappa
-algebra
A
, an element
\widetilde{s}
in
S(A)
lies in
\mathrm{Def}(\xi)(A)
if and only if
\xi
deforms, possibly non-uniquely, to
\mathcal{X}_{\widetilde{s}}
. It is not hard to construct
\mathrm{Def}(\xi)
explicitly using the standard deformation theory of line bundles: Let
(\mathcal{H},\nabla,\mathrm{Fil}^{\bullet}\mathcal{H})
be the filtered flat vector bundle
R^{2}f_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
. Then
c_{1,\mathrm{dR}}(\xi)
extends uniquely to a horizontal section
\sigma
of
\mathcal{H}
, as
\mathcal{H}
admits a flat trivialization
(\mathcal{H},\nabla)\simeq\mathcal{H}_{s}{\otimes}(R,d)
(see for example the proof of
[
BO78
, Prop. 2.16]
). Then
\mathrm{Def}(\xi)=\mathrm{Spf}(R/I)
where
I
is the minimal ideal such that the image of
\sigma
vanishes in
(\mathcal{H}/\mathrm{Fil}^{1}\mathcal{H}){\otimes}_{R}(R/I)
. In particular, it is clear that
\mathrm{Def}(\xi)
is generated by
h^{0,2}:=\mathrm{rank\,}\mathcal{H}/\mathrm{Fil}^{1}\mathcal{H}
elements.

Our goal in this section is to prove the analogue of
(2.2.1)
in positive and mixed characteristic. The difficulty to overcome in this case is that in general
R^{2}f_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
does not admit a flat trivialization.

Definition (2.2.2)
.

Let
S
be a noetherian (possibly formal) scheme and
f:\mathcal{X}\to S
be a smooth projective morphism with geometrically connected fibers. We say that
\mathcal{X}/S
is an

family if the following conditions hold:

(i)

the Hodge-de Rham spectral sequence degenerates at
E_{1}
page;

(ii)

the Hodge cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{j}(\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{X}/S}):=R^{i}f_{*}\Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^{j}
is locally free for every
(i,j)
.

(iii)

for every prime
p
, the crystal of coherent sheaves
R^{r}f_{\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathrm{cris}*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
on
\mathrm{Cris}(S_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is locally free for every
r
.

When
S
is connected, we shall denote
\mathrm{rank\,}\mathrm{H}^{j}(\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{X}/S})
by

.

Remark (2.2.3)
.

The above assumptions are used to make sure that various cohomology modules commute with base change: (i) and (ii) easily imply that for each
r
,

is locally free. Moreover, using
[
Har77
, III Cor. 12.7]
, one quickly checks by induction that for any morphism
T\to S
between noetherian (formal) schemes, the natural base change morphisms
\mathrm{H}^{j}(\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{X}/S}){\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\mathcal{O}_{T}\to\mathrm{H}^{j}(\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{X}_{T}/T})
and hence
\mathrm{H}^{r}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S){\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\mathcal{O}_{T}\to\mathrm{H}^{r}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}_{T}/T)
are isomorphisms for all

. Similarly, suppose that
T
is a noetherian
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme over
S_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
and there is PD morphism

between PD thickenings

of

and
S^{\prime}
of

. Then the natural morphism

(R^{r}f_{\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathrm{cris}*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}})_{S^{\prime}}{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}_{S^{\prime}}}\mathcal{O}_{T^{\prime}}\to(R^{r}f_{T,\mathrm{cris}*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{T}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}})_{T^{\prime}}

is an isomorphism. One may deduce this from the commutivity with base change on the level of derived categories
[
BO83
, Thm 7.8]
and the universal coefficient theorem.

The following lemma follows from a famous theorem of Deligne-Illusie
[

]
, standard base change theorem for coherent cohomology, and crystalline de-Rham comparison isomorphisms.

Up to enlarging

•

We introduce the following notations: Let
B
be a base scheme and
\mathcal{V}
be a vector bundle of rank

over

. We denote by
\mathbb{A}(\mathcal{V})
the associated
\mathbb{A}^{r}
-bundle over

,

6.1 Elliptic Surfaces

Let
\pi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
be an elliptic surface (with zero section) over a field
k
. Define
L:=(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\vee}
to be the fundamental line bundle and set
h:=\mathrm{deg}(L)
to be the
height
of
L
. Note that if
h=1
, then
X
is rational, and if
h=2
, then
X
is an elliptic K3 surface. In general, we have that the inregularity
q=0
and
h=\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=p_{g}-1
, where
p_{g}=h^{2,0}(X)
is the geometric genus. We say that
X
is
admissible
if all fibers of
\pi
are irreducible.

In order to parametrize all
X
with height
h
, we consider
\mathbb{P}^{1}
over
\mathbb{Z}[1/6]
and let
\varpi
be the structural morphism
\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[1/6])
. Set
\mathcal{V}_{i}
to be the vector bundle over
\mathbb{Z}[1/6]
given by
\varpi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(ih)
. Let the coordinates of
\mathcal{P}:=\mathbb{A}(\mathcal{V}_{4}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{6})^{*}\times_{\mathbb{Z}[1/6]}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2h)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(3h)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})
be denoted by
((a_{4},a_{6}),[x:y:z])
and set
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
to be the subscheme defined by (cf.
[
Kas77
, Thm 1]
)

y^{2}z=x^{3}-a_{4}xz^{2}-a_{6}z^{3}.

Note that the morphism
\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{A}(\mathcal{V}_{4}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{6})^{*}
carries a natural action by
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
, the latter is given by

\lambda\cdot((a_{4},a_{6}),[x:y:z])\mapsto((\lambda^{4}a_{4},\lambda^{6}a_{6}),[\lambda^{2}x:\lambda^{3}y:z]).

These actions commute and both stabilize
\mathcal{P}
. Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{A}(\mathcal{V}_{4}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{6})^{*}
be the open subscheme over which
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
is smooth and
\mathcal{X}
be the restriction of
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
to
\mathcal{S}
. It is well known that a geometric point
s\to\mathbb{A}(\mathcal{V}_{4}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{6})^{*}
factors through
\mathcal{S}
if and only if the fiber
\mathcal{X}_{s}
is an admissible elliptic surface.

The moduli stack
\mathsf{M}
over
\mathbb{Z}[1/6]
of admissible elliptic surfaces over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of height
h
is given by the quotient of
\mathcal{S}
by the action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
. One easily computes that
\mathsf{M}
has relative dimension
10h-2
over
\mathbb{Z}[1/6]
. To avoid working with stacks, we consider
\mathcal{S}
.

Lemma (6.1.1)
.

Assume that

. Then for every

This is a consequence of

. We just need to explain why the hypothesis there is satisfied: Note that the Euler characteristic of a nodal curve (resp. cuspidal) of
1
(resp.
2
), and that of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
is

. Therefore, the topological Hurwitz formula tells us that there are at least
6h
singular fibers. The line bundle

in

is the same as our

∎

.

There exists an open dense subscheme

6.2 Complete Intersection Surfaces

Fix some
r\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
and a tuple
(d_{1},\cdots,d_{r})\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}^{\oplus r}
. Consider
\mathbb{P}^{r+2}
over
\mathbb{Z}
and let
\varpi:\mathbb{P}^{r+2}\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
denote the structural morphism. Let

\overline{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r+2}\times_{\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{P}(\varpi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r+2}}(d_{i})),\hskip 20.00003pt\left(\textrm{resp. }\mathcal{S}\subseteq\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{P}(\varpi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r+2}}(d_{i}))\right)

be the universal complete intersection of
r
hypersurfaces of degree
d_{1},\cdots,d_{r}
(resp. the open subscheme over which
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
is smooth). Denote the restriction of
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
to
\mathcal{S}
by
\mathcal{X}
.

Theorem (6.2.1)
.

Assume the following two numerical conditions:

(i)

\sum d_{i}>r+3

(ii)

for some (and hence every)
s\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C})
,
\dim\mathrm{H}^{1}(T_{\mathcal{X}_{s}})\geq h^{2,0}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
.

Then there exists an open dense
U\subseteq\mathcal{S}
such that every LB-pair
(s,\xi)
with
s
lying on
U
is liftable.

Proof.

For every
s\in\mathcal{S}
, the canonical divisor
K_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}
of
\mathcal{X}_{s}
is the restriction of the
(-r-3+\sum d_{i})
-multiple of the hyperplane section on
\mathbb{P}^{r+2}_{k(s)}
, so condition
(i)
ensures that
K_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}
is ample. By
[
Pet76
, Cor. 1.9, Thm 3.5]
and their proofs, one infers that the natural map
T_{s}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(T_{\mathcal{X}_{s}})
is surjective for every
s\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C})
. Then Thm 5.4
ibid
further tells us that the Kodaira-Spencer map
\mathrm{H}^{1}(T_{\mathcal{X}_{s}})\to\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}),\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}))
is injective. Finally, in order to apply
(1.1.1)
to
\mathcal{S}
, it suffices to check the large monodromy condinition on
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
. This follows from
[
Bea86
, Thm 5]
.
∎

Remark (6.2.2)
.

The formulas for the numbers
h^{p,q}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
and
\dim\mathrm{H}^{1}(T_{\mathcal{X}_{s}})
for some (and hence every)
s\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C})
, in terms of
d_{1},\cdots,d_{r}
are completely known [SGA7, éxposé XI, théorème 2.3], so (ii) is indeed a purely numerical condition. In particular, when
r=1
, any
d_{1}\geq 5
will satisfy the hypothesis of
(6.2.1)
. This gives
(1.2.1)
.

6.3 Non-examples

We remark on the assumptions (a)
f_{\mathbb{C}}
has large monodromy and (b)
f_{\mathbb{C}}
generically has sufficiently big period image in
(1.1.1)
and
(1.1.3)
. The upshot is that (a) should be relaxable whereas (b) is more rigid.

To begin, there is a quick counterexample to the conclusion when neither (a) nor (b) holds: Suppose that
\mathcal{S}=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{L}[1/N])
and
\mathcal{X}
is a K3 surface over
\mathcal{S}
. Then for infinitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}
, the reduction
\mathcal{X}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
has higher geometric Picard number than
\mathcal{X}_{L}
(
[
Sha+22
, Thm 1.1]
,
[
Tay22
, Thm 1.1]
). At such
\mathfrak{p}
, we can find an irreducible component of
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
which admits a
\overline{k(\mathfrak{p})}
-point but not an
\overline{L}
-point, and hence is not flat over
S
. Therefore, both
(1.1.1)
and
(1.1.3)
are false in this case. On the other hand, for families with
h^{0,2}=1
, we can relax (a) to “
\mathrm{Mon}(R^{2}f_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{Q},s)
is positive dimensional for some (and hence every)
s\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C})
”, which is in fact equivalent to (b). This is a key observation we used in
[
HYZ23
]
and interested reader can take a look at the introduction.

The example below illustrates that even if (a) is satisfied, (b) cannot be dropped:

Proposition (6.3.1)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be as in
(6.2.1)
with
r=1
and
d_{1}=d
being an even number
\geq 6
. Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
be a closed subscheme which is smooth and of relative dimension
1
over an open part of
\mathbb{Z}
, and such that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}
contains a Hodge-generic point for the VHS given by
\mathrm{H}^{2}
of
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{C}}
over
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Assume the Tate conjecture for hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
over finite fields and the Zilber-Pink conjecture for families of hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{bad}}
be the set of closed points
s\in\mathcal{S}
such that there exist some alg-geometric point
\bar{s}
over
s
and
\xi\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{\bar{s}})
for which the pair
(\bar{s},\xi)
is non-liftable on
\mathcal{C}
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{bad}}
is Zariski dense on
\mathcal{C}
.

Note that if the Zariski closure of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}
in
\mathbb{P}(\varpi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{C}}}(d))
is a Lefschetz pencil, then condition (a) is satisfied.

Proof.

Let
s\in\mathcal{S}
be any closed point and
\ell
be a prime
\neq\mathrm{char\,}k(s)
. The Weil conjectures imply that the Frobenius eigenvalues on
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\textrm{{\'{e}}t}}(\mathcal{X}_{\bar{s}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))
which are not roots of unity come in pairs. As
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\textrm{{\'{e}}t}}(\mathcal{X}_{\bar{s}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))
is even dimensional by the assumption that
d
is even, the Tate conjecture implies that
\mathrm{rank\,}\mathrm{NS}(\mathcal{X}_{\bar{s}})
is even. Therefore, there exists a line bundle
\xi\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{X}_{\bar{s}})
which does not have the same class as
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\bar{s}}}(1)
.

Suppose now that the LB-pair
(\bar{s},\xi)
lifts to characteristic
0
. Then by choosing an isomorphism between
\mathbb{C}
and the algebraic closure of
W(\bar{s})[1/p]
, we obtain a
\mathbb{C}
-point on the NL locus. Because
\mathcal{C}
is Hodge-generic, the expected codimension of a NL locus in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}
is
h^{0,2}>1
. By the Zilber-Pink conjecture formulated in
[
BKU22
, Conj. 2.5]
this implies the NL locus of
\mathcal{C}
is a finite set of points, which implies that
s
lies in the Zariski closure of finitely many
\mathbb{C}
-points on
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

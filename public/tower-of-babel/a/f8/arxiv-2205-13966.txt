Gradient damage models for heterogeneous materials
Annika Bach, Teresa Esposito, Roberta Marziani, Caterina Ida Zeppieri

Abstract
. In this paper we study the asymptotic behaviour of phase-field functionals of Ambrosio and Tortorelli type allowing for small-scale oscillations both in the volume and in the diffuse surface term. The functionals under examination can be interpreted as an instance of a static gradient damage model for heterogeneous materials. Depending on the mutual vanishing rate of the approximation and of the oscillation parameters, the effective behaviour of the model is fully characterised by means of Γ \Gamma -convergence.

1.
Introduction

Damage models for elastic materials describe the degradation of the elastic properties of a body as a consequence of some applied loads
[
30
,
31
]
. The total energy of an elastic material undergoing damage then depends on two variables: the deformation
u:A\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
(
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
open and bounded representing the reference configuration of the material) and an internal variable
v:A\to[0,1]
measuring at each point the damage state of the material (the value
v=1
corresponding to the original sound state and the value
v=0
corresponding to the totally damaged state).
In a periodically heterogeneous setting and at fixed time, this energy can be described in terms of phase-field functionals of the form

(1.1)

\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u,v,A)=\int_{A}(v^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon})f\Big(\frac{x}{\delta_{\varepsilon}},\nabla u\Big)\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{A}(1-v)^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\varepsilon\int_{A}h\Big(\frac{x}{\delta_{\varepsilon}},\nabla v\Big)\,{\rm d}x\,,

where
\varepsilon>0
is a small parameter,
0<\eta_{\varepsilon}\ll\varepsilon
, and
\delta_{\varepsilon}>0
is infinitesimal, as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
The integrands
f
and
h
are
(0,1)^{n}
-periodic in the first variable and satisfy growth and coercivity conditions of order
2
in the second variable (see Section
2
for the full list of assumptions). Correspondingly,
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
is defined for
(u,v)\in W^{1,2}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})\times W^{1,2}(A;[0,1])
.

The three terms in (
1.1
) can be interpreted as follows. The first term accounts for the stored elastic energy and reflects the worsening of the elastic properties of the material due to the damage process. Namely, in the regions where the damage occurs, that is, where
v\simeq 0
, the deformation gradient
\nabla u
becomes very large in norm and hence
u
singular.
The second term represents the energy dissipated in the damage process, hence it is maximal when the material is totally damaged.
Together with the third term, which penalises the spatial variations of
v
, it forces the damage to localise for small
\varepsilon
in diffuse regions of size proportional to
\varepsilon
,
around the set where
|\nabla u|
blows up. Then, asymptotically, the damage-localisation gives rise to sharp cracks and the functionals in (
1.1
) are expected to behave, in the limit, as a fracture model.

In a homogeneous setting, choosing
f(x,\nabla u)=|\nabla u|^{2}
, and
h(x,\nabla v)=|\nabla v|^{2}
, the functionals
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
reduce to the classical Ambrosio-Tortorelli model which is indeed known to
\Gamma
-converge to the prototypical brittle fracture model given by the Mumford-Shah functional
[
3
,
4
]
.
If now instead
\eta_{\varepsilon}\sim\varepsilon
, then the static damage model described by
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
can be shown to
\Gamma
-converge to a fracture model of cohesive type
[
23
]
(see also
[
25
,
26
]
).

Moreover, in a heterogeneous scale-free setting
[
24
]
, that is, when functionals of the form

(1.2)

\int_{A}v^{2}f(x,\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{A}(1-v)^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\varepsilon\int_{A}h(x,\nabla v)\,{\rm d}x

are considered, the corresponding
\Gamma
-limit is given by the nonhomogeneous, anisotropic brittle energy à la Griffith

\int_{A}f(x,\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x+2\int_{S_{u}\cap A}\sqrt{h(x,\nu_{u})}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where now
u
belongs to
GSBV^{2}(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
; i.e.
, is a generalised special function of bounded variation
[
2
,
22
]
. In this functional framework
\nabla u
denotes the approximate differential of
u
,
S_{u}
its discontinuity set, and
\nu_{u}
the normal to
S_{u}
.

In the recent work
[
7
]
the authors analysed the limit behaviour of a general class of heterogeneous, scale-dependent phase-field functionals of the form

(1.3)

\int_{A}v^{2}f_{\varepsilon}(x,\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{A}g_{\varepsilon}(x,v,\varepsilon\nabla v)\,{\rm d}x

where
f_{\varepsilon}
and
g_{\varepsilon}
belong to suitable classes of integrands including, in particular, the choices

f_{\varepsilon}(x,\nabla u)=f\Big(\frac{x}{\delta_{\varepsilon}},\nabla u\Big),\quad g_{\varepsilon}(x,v,\nabla v)=(1-v)^{2}+h\Big(\frac{x}{\delta_{\varepsilon}},\nabla v\Big),

with
f
and
h
as in (
1.1
). The main result in
[
7
]
establishes that the functionals in (
1.3
) essentially behave like (
1.2
). That is, the (possibly sequence-dependent)
\Gamma
-limit of (
1.3
) is a free-discontinuity functional of the form

\int_{A}f_{\infty}(x,\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{S_{u}\cap A}g_{\infty}(x,\nu_{u})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where
f_{\infty}
and
g_{\infty}
can be characterised in terms of limits of suitable scaled minimisation problems. Furthermore, the minimisation problem
providing
f_{\infty}
only involves
f_{\varepsilon}
while the formula providing
g_{\infty}
only involves the minimisation of
g_{\varepsilon}
(over pairs
(u,v)
along which the first term in (
1.3
) vanishes). In this respect, the
\Gamma
-convergence result in
[
7
]
can be seen as the phase-field analogue of the decoupling result for free-discontinuity
functionals proven in
[
16
]
. In particular, we notice that in the regular elliptic setting the limit decoupling immediately implies that the
\Gamma
-limit of (
1.3
) does not depend on the jump opening of
u
and hence is a brittle energy.

Building upon the limit decoupling obtained in
[
7
]
it can be proven (
cf.
Theorem
4.2
and Proposition
4.5
) that the functionals
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
-converge to a free-discontinuity functional of the form

(1.4)

\int_{A}f_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{S_{u}\cap A}g^{\ell}_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nu_{u})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where
f_{\mathrm{hom}}
is given by the classical formula of periodic homogenisation
[
10
,
29
]
; i.e.
,

f_{\rm hom}(\xi)=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{1}{r^{n}}\inf\Biggl\{\int_{Q_{r}(0)}f(x,\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x\colon u\in W^{1,2}(Q_{r}(0);\mathbb{R}^{m})\,,u=u_{\xi}\ \text{near}\ \partial Q_{r}(0)\Biggr\},

while the homogeneous surface energy density
g^{\ell}_{\mathrm{hom}}
will depend on the parameter

\ell=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\in[0,+\infty],

or, in other words, on the mutual vanishing rate of the approximation and the oscillation parameters. Then, the main contribution of the present paper consists in the characterisation of
g^{\ell}_{\mathrm{hom}}
in the three limit regimes:
\ell=0
,
\ell\in(0,+\infty)
, and
\ell=+\infty
(
cf.
Theorem
3.1
, Propositions
5.1
,
6.1
, and
7.2
).

We notice that in the one-dimensional setting
; i.e.
, for
n=m=1
, the computation of the
\Gamma
-limit of the damage model (
1.1
) can be carried out directly, by hands, without resorting to the general convergence result in
[
7
]
, as shown in
[
6
]
.
On the other hand, we observe that the analysis to determine
g^{\ell}_{\mathrm{hom}}
shares some similarities with the homogenisation of phase-transition functionals of Modica-Mortola type as in
[
5
]
. We also mention here the work
[
28
]
where the stochastic analogue of
[
5
]
is considered, though only for
\delta_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon
. Moreover, in the papers
[
14
,
18
,
19
,
27
]
further variants of phase-transition functionals are considered where a transition-scale and an oscillation-scale appear at the same time and interact in the
\Gamma
-limit.

We now give a brief heuristic account of the
\Gamma
-convergence result, Theorem
3.1
, in the three regimes
\ell=0
,
\ell\in(0,+\infty)
, and
\ell=+\infty
. As already observed, thanks to the general analysis developed in
[
7
]
, it is not difficult to show that the functionals
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
behave like

(1.5)

\int_{A}(v^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon})f_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{A}(1-v)^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\varepsilon\int_{A}h\Big(\frac{x}{\delta_{\varepsilon}},\nabla v\Big)\,{\rm d}x.

Then, if
\ell=0
, which corresponds to the case
\varepsilon\ll\delta_{\varepsilon}
, we can regard
\delta_{\varepsilon}
and the variable
x/\delta_{\varepsilon}
as fixed and let first
\varepsilon\to 0
. In this way, arguing as in the case of (
1.2
)
we would get the
\delta_{\varepsilon}
-dependent free-discontinuity functionals

(1.6)

\int_{A}f_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x+2\int_{S_{u}\cap A}\sqrt{h\Big(\frac{x}{\delta_{\varepsilon}},\nu_{u}\Big)}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Therefore, appealing to
[
13
]
, letting
\delta_{\varepsilon}\to 0
yields the homogeneous free-discontinuity function (
1.4
) with surface energy density
g_{\mathrm{hom}}^{0}
given by

\begin{split}g^{0}_{\rm hom}(\nu)=2\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\inf\Biggl\{\int_{S_{u}\cap Q^{\nu}_{r}(0)}&\sqrt{h(x,\nu_{u})}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\colon\\
&u\in BV(Q_{r}^{\nu}(0);\{0,e_{1}\})\,,u=u^{\nu}\ \text{near}\ \partial Q^{\nu}_{r}(0)\Biggr\}\,,\end{split}

where
u^{\nu}
denotes the jump function defined as

u^{\nu}(x)=\begin{cases}e_{1}&\text{if }\;x\cdot\nu\geq 0\,,\cr 0&\text{if }\;x\cdot\nu<0\,.\end{cases}

We notice that in this regime the passage from
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
(or, equivalently, from (
1.5
)) to the functionals (
1.6
) can be made rigorous by combining the Modica-Mortola trick with a classical argument of Ambrosio based on the co-area formula. These two ingredients allow us to estimate from below the surface term in (
1.5
)
with an
\varepsilon
-independent heterogeneous and anisotropic perimeter functional and then to conclude (see the proof of Proposition
5.1
).

If
\ell\in(0,+\infty)
, which corresponds to the case
\varepsilon\sim\delta_{\varepsilon}
, approximation and homogenisation procedure cannot be decoupled and
g^{\ell}_{\mathrm{hom}}
is given by an asymptotic cell-formula in which the whole Modica-Mortola term in (
1.1
) appears. More precisely, in this regime
g^{\ell}_{\mathrm{hom}}
is given by

(1.7)

\begin{split}g^{\ell}_{\rm hom}(\nu)=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\inf\Biggl\{\int_{Q^{\nu}_{r}(0)}&\left((1-v)^{2}+h(\ell x,\nabla v)\right)\,{\rm d}x\colon\\
&v\in W^{1,2}(Q^{\nu}_{r}(0))\,,0\leq v\leq 1\colon\,\exists u\in W^{1,2}(Q_{r}^{\nu}(0);\mathbb{R}^{m})\ \text{with}\\
&v\nabla u=0\ \text{a.e. in}\ Q_{r}^{\nu}(0)\ \text{and}\ (u,v)=(\bar{u}^{\nu},\bar{v}^{\nu})\ \text{near}\ \partial Q^{\nu}_{r}(0)\Biggr\}\,,\end{split}

where
\bar{u}^{\nu}
is a suitable regularisation of
u^{\nu}
while
\bar{v}^{\nu}
is equal to
1
on most of the boundary of the cube
Q^{\nu}_{r}(0)
and equal to
0
in a neighbourhood of the hyperplane
x\cdot\nu=0
, and chosen in a way such that
\bar{v}^{\nu}\nabla\bar{u}^{\nu}=0
a.e. in
Q_{r}^{\nu}(0)
(see Section
2.1
,
(k)
for the definition of
(\bar{u}^{\nu},\bar{v}^{\nu})
and
cf.
[
7
, Theorem 3.5]
).
We notice that in view of the growth conditions satisfied by
f
(see
(

⁢
f
1

)
), the first term in
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
vanishes on those pairs
(u,v)
which are admissible in (
1.7
).

Eventually, if
\ell=+\infty
, which corresponds to the case
\varepsilon\gg\delta_{\varepsilon}
, heuristically, we can first let
\delta_{\varepsilon}\to 0
thus getting the spatially homogeneous functionals

(1.8)

\int_{A}(v^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon})f_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{A}(1-v)^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\varepsilon\int_{A}h_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nabla v)\,{\rm d}x,

where

\begin{split}h_{\rm hom}(w)=\inf\Biggl\{\int_{(0,1)^{n}}h(x,\nabla v+w)\,{\rm d}x\colon v\in W_{0}^{1,2}((0,1)^{n})\Biggr\}\,.\end{split}

Hence, letting
\varepsilon\to 0
and invoking
[
24
]
give

\int_{A}f_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nabla u)\,{\rm d}x+2\int_{S_{u}\cap A}\sqrt{h_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nu)}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

in (
1.4
), so that in this case

g^{\infty}_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nu)=2\sqrt{h_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nu)}.

We notice that in this regime the most delicate step in the proof of the
\Gamma
-converge result is to show that
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
actually behaves like (
1.8
) or, in other words, that the passage to the limit as
\delta_{\varepsilon}\to 0
can be rigorously justified.
To do so we show that we can replace the
v
-variables of a sequence
(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})
with equi-bounded energy with a suitably averaged sequence
(\widetilde{v}_{\varepsilon})
such that

(1.9)

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{A}(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\varepsilon\int_{A}h\Big(\frac{x}{\delta_{\varepsilon}},\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\Big)\,{\rm d}x\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{A}(1-\widetilde{v}_{\varepsilon})^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\varepsilon\int_{A}h_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nabla\widetilde{v}_{\varepsilon})\,{\rm d}x+o(1).

However, in order to ensure that after this replacement the corresponding volume term

(1.10)

\int_{A}(\widetilde{v}_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon})f\Big(\frac{x}{\delta_{\varepsilon}},\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\Big)\,{\rm d}x,

or, equivalently,

\int_{A}(\widetilde{v}_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon})f_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\,{\rm d}x,

remains uniformly bounded, we need some additional information on the blow-up rate of
|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|
which shall be related to the scale of oscillations
\delta_{\varepsilon}
in the following way

(1.11)

\int_{A}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\sim\frac{1}{\delta_{\varepsilon}}.

The latter is then enforced by requiring that in the regime
\varepsilon\gg\delta_{\varepsilon}
the infinitesimal parameter
\eta_{\varepsilon}
appearing in (
1.1
) is exactly of order
\delta_{\varepsilon}
so that, thanks to the growth conditions satisfied by
f
, the assumption in (
1.11
) is automatically satisfied by the
u
-variables of any sequence
(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})
with equi-bounded energy.

To conclude we would like to briefly comment on the additional assumption (
1.11
) which might be a drawback of our specific method of proof, inspired by
[
5
]
(see the proof of Proposition
7.2
). In fact, on the one hand the definition of the auxiliary sequence
(\widetilde{v}_{\varepsilon})
is somehow dictated by the key estimate (
1.9
) which, in its turn, is compatible with an analogous estimate for (
1.10
) only if also this term can be put in some relation with the oscillation parameter
\delta_{\varepsilon}
. On the other hand, from a modelling point of view, an assumption on the convergence rate to zero of
\eta_{\varepsilon}
(so to enforce (
1.11
)) does not appear to be too restrictive. We finally observe that also in the homogenisation of the Modica-Mortola functionals
[
5
]
the regime
\ell=+\infty
is the most delicate one and requires an additional assumption on the vanishing rate of
\delta_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

2.
Setting of the problem and preliminary results

In this section we introduce some useful notation, define the functionals under examination, and recall some preliminaries.

2.1.
Notation

We start collecting the notation we are going to employ throughout.

(a)

m,n\geq 1
are fixed positive integers; we set
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{0}:=\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}
;

(b)

\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:=\{\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\nu_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\nu_{n}^{2}=1\}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}_{\pm}:=\{\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\colon\pm\nu_{i(\nu)}>0\}
, where

;

(c)

\mathcal{L}^{n}
and and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
denote, respectively, the Lebesgue measure and the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(d)

\mathcal{A}
denotes the collection of all open and bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Lipschitz boundary. If
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
by
A\subset\subset B
we mean that
A
is relatively compact in
B
;

(e)

Q
denotes the open unit cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with sides parallel to the coordinate axis, centred at the origin; for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
we set
Q_{r}(x):=rQ+x
. If
x=0
we simply write
Q_{r}
.
Moreover,
Q^{\prime}
denotes the open unit cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
with sides parallel to the coordinate axis, centred at the origin, for every
r>0
we set
Q_{r}^{\prime}:=rQ^{\prime}
;

(f)

for every
\nu\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
let
R_{\nu}
denote an orthogonal
(n\times n)
-matrix such that
R_{\nu}e_{n}=\nu
; we also assume that
R_{-\nu}Q=R_{\nu}Q
for every
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
R_{\nu}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n\times n}
if
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{n}
, and that the restrictions of the map

, to

(g)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}

Q^{\nu}_{r}(x):=R_{\nu}Q_{r}(0)+x
. If

we simply write
Q^{\nu}_{r}
and we set

;

(h)

for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
we let

be the linear function whose gradient is equal to

; i.e.
,
u_{\xi}(x):=\xi x
, for every

;

(i)

for

;

(j)

let
{\rm u}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
,

{\rm v}{\rm u}^{\prime}\equiv 0
in

;

ii.

({\rm u}(t),{\rm v}(t))=(\chi_{(0,+\infty)}(t),1)
for
|t|>1
;

(k)

for
\nu\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}

\bar{u}^{\nu}(x):={\rm u}(x\cdot\nu)e_{1}\,,\quad\bar{v}^{\nu}(x):={\rm v}(x\cdot\nu)\,;

(l)

for

We now introduce the functional spaces relevant for our problem. Given a

For

SBV(A;\mathbb{R}^{m})
(resp.

) of special functions of bounded variation (resp. of generalised special functions of bounded variation) on

for every
B\in\mathcal{B}^{n}
, where
\mathcal{B}^{n}

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In (

(which makes sense also for

),

the set of approximate discontinuity points of

[u]:=u^{+}-u^{-}

S_{u}
, and
\nu_{u}

For

Lemma 7.1

Appendix

In this short section we state and prove Lemma
A.1
, from which estimate (
7.3
) in Lemma
7.1
follows. A similar estimate has been obtained in the proof of
[
5
, Proposition 4.10]
for the
L^{1}
-norm.

Lemma A.1
.

There exists
c=c(n)>0
such that for every open sets
A,A^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
A^{\prime}\subset\subset A
, every
v\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and every
r
satisfying

0<r<\frac{2\dist(A^{\prime},\partial A)}{(2+\sqrt{n})\sqrt{n}},

there holds

\int_{A^{\prime}}|v-v_{r}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq c\,r^{2}\int_{A}|\nabla v|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,,

where

v_{r}(x):=\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{Q_{r}(x)}v(y)\,{\rm d}y\,.

Proof.

Let
A,A^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and such that
A^{\prime}\subset\subset A
; for any
r>0
set

J_{r}:=\{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon Q_{r}(rz)\cap A^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\}\,.

For any
v\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have

(A.1)

\begin{split}\int_{A^{\prime}}|v(x)-v_{r}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x&\leq\sum_{z\in J_{r}}\int_{Q_{r}(rz)}|v(x)-v_{r}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\\
&\leq 2\sum_{z\in J_{r}}\bigg(\int_{Q_{r}(rz)}|v(x)-v_{r}(rz)|^{2}+\int_{Q_{r}(rz)}|v_{r}(rz)-v_{r}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\bigg)\,.\end{split}

We estimate the two terms on the right-hand side of (
A.1
) using the Poincaré Inequality and the continuity of the translation operator.

By a scaling argument, for every
z\in J_{r}
we have

(A.2)

\|v-v_{r}(rz)\|_{L^{2}(Q_{r}(rz))}\leq c_{P}r\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(Q_{r}(rz);\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,,

where
c_{P}>0
is the constant for the Poincaré inequality in the unit cube.
Also note that for any
z\in J_{r}
and
x\in Q_{r}(rz)
there exists
x_{0}\in A^{\prime}\cap Q_{r}(rz)
with
|x-x_{0}|<\sqrt{n}r
, hence

(A.3)

Q_{r}(rz)\subset A\quad\text{as long as}\ r<\frac{\dist(A^{\prime},\partial A)}{\sqrt{n}}\,.

Moreover, the cubes
Q_{r}(rz)
,
z\in J_{r}
are pairwise disjoint.
Thus, summing up (
A.2
), for
r>0
satisfying (
A.3
) we obtain

(A.4)

\sum_{z\in J_{r}}\int_{Q_{r}(rz)}|v(x)-v_{r}(rz)|^{2}\leq c_{P}^{2}r^{2}\int_{A}|\nabla v|^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

Then, it remains to estimate the second term in (
A.1
). An application of Jensen’s Inequality yields for any
z\in J_{r}

(A.5)

\begin{split}\int_{Q_{r}(rz)}|v_{r}(rz)-v_{r}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x&=\int_{Q_{r}(rz)}\bigg|\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{Q_{r}(rz)}v(y)-v(y+x-rz))\,{\rm d}y\bigg|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\\
&\leq\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{Q_{r}(rz)}\int_{Q_{r}(rz)}|v(y)-v(y+x-rz)|^{2}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\,.\end{split}

Since
|x-rz|\leq\sqrt{n}r/2
for any
x\in Q_{r}(rz)
, the continuity of the shift operator in Sobolev spaces (see,
e.g.
,
[
15
, Proposition 9.3]
) yields

\big\|v-v\big(\cdot+(x-rz)\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(Q_{r}(rz))}\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}r\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(Q_{(1+\sqrt{n})r}(rz);\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad\text{for every}\ x\in Q_{r}(rz)\,

and therefore

(A.6)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{Q_{r}(rz)}\int_{Q_{r}(rz)}|v(y)-v(y+x-rz)|^{2}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x&\leq\frac{n}{4}r^{2}\int_{Q_{(1+\sqrt{n})r}(rz)}|\nabla v(y)|^{2}\,{\rm d}y\,.\end{split}

Moreover, for any
z\in J_{r}
and
x\in Q_{(1+\sqrt{n})r}(rz)
there exists
x_{0}\in Q_{r}(rz)\cap A^{\prime}
with
|x-x_{0}|<(2+\sqrt{n})\sqrt{n}r/2
, hence

(A.7)

Q_{(1+\sqrt{n})r}(rz)\subset A\quad\text{if }\ r<\frac{2\dist(A^{\prime},\partial A)}{(2+\sqrt{n})\sqrt{n}}\,.

We observe that the cubes
Q_{(1+\sqrt{n})r}(rz)
,
z\in J_{r}
are not pairwise disjoint. Nevertheless, since for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in J_{r}
with
Q_{(1+\sqrt{n})r}(rz_{1})\cap Q_{(1+\sqrt{n})r}(rz_{2})\neq\emptyset
we have
|z_{1}-z_{2}|\leq(1+\sqrt{n})\sqrt{n}
, each cube
Q_{(1+\sqrt{n})r}(rz)
intersects only
N
cubes, with
N
independent of
r
. Thus, summing up the estimates in (
A.5
) and (
A.6
), for
r>0
as in (
A.7
) we obtain

(A.8)

\sum_{z\in J_{r}}\int_{Q_{r}(rz)}|v_{r}(rz)-v_{r}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq N\frac{n}{4}r^{2}\int_{A}|\nabla v|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,.

Eventually the claim follows by combining (
A.1
), (
A.4
), and (
A.8
).

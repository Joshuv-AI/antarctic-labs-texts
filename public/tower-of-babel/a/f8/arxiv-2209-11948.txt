Khovanov-Rozansky 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 N \mathfrak{sl}_{N} -homology for periodic links
Maciej Borodzik, Wojciech Politarczyk, Ramazan Yozgyur

Abstract
. For an m m -periodic link L L , we show that the Khovanov-Rozansky 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 N \mathfrak{sl}_{N} -homology carries an action of the group ℤ m \mathbb{Z}_{m} . As an example of applications, we prove an analog of the periodicity criterion of Borodzik–Politarczyk using 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 N \mathfrak{sl}_{N} -homology instead of Khovanov homology.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

Let
L\subset S^{3}
be a link. For
m\geq 2
, we say that
L
is
m
-periodic
, if it is invariant under a semi-free
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-action on
S^{3}

and
L
is disjoint from the fixed point set.
Given a periodic link, we ask in what way the symmetry of the link is reflected in the structure of link invariants.
An exemplary answer is the Murasugi formula
[
22
]
. Not only it gives a useful periodicity
criterion, but also it relates the Alexander polynomial of
L
and the Alexander polynomial of its quotient.

The symmetry of the link is also visible in Khovanov homology.
Equivariant Khovanov homology for periodic links was defined in
[
24
]
.
If
R
is the coefficient ring for Khovanov homology and the group algebra
R[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
is semisimple, the
equivariant Khovanov homology of a link is equal as an
R
-module, to the Khovanov homology. That is to say, the group action on
S^{3}

induces a well-defined group action on the Khovanov homology modules
\kh(L;R)
. Using this fact, the
first two authors established an obstruction for periodicity of link based on Khovanov homology. This obstruction refines
previously known obstructions based on the Jones polynomial
[
32
,
33
,
23
]
.
Furthermore, a relation between Khovanov homology of the link and of its quotient was recently
found in
[
3
,
31
]
.

The goal of the present article is to prove the results of
[
4
,
24
]
in
the case of
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology.

\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology for links was introduced by Khovanov and Rozansky
[
11
,
10
]

as a generalization of Khovanov homology.
The original construction
used matrix factorizations. Over the years, an alternative approach using webs and foams has been developed, see

[
26
,
29
,
5
]
. This approach, sketched in
Section
2
below, is a generalization of Bar-Natan’s definition of Khovanov homology
[
1
]
.
This combinatorial definition
turns out to be very well-suited for studying periodic links.

1.2.
Main results

In this paper we use the approach of
[
23
,
24
,
4
]
in the context of

\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology. Namely, we show that for a periodic link, the symmetry group
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
of the link induces
a
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-action on its
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology.
The action does not depend on the choice of the link diagram, up to equivariant isotopy.
The existence of the action can be used to obtain periodicity obstructions, generalizing the results from
[
4
]
.
Recall that the graded Euler characeristic of the Khovanov homology is the Jones polynomial.
Equivariant Khovanov homology allows for a refinement of the Jones polynomial, called
difference Jones polynomials
, see

[
23
]
.
Analogously, the Euler characteristic of
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology yields a well-studied Reshetikin-Turaev polynomial, known
also as the
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-polynomial. For a periodic link, we define analogs of difference Jones polynomials for
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology.

We establish a skein spectral sequence for a change of an orbit of crossings
in
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology. An analogous skein spectral sequence was considered in
[
24
]
for Khovanov homology of
a periodic link.
The skein spectral sequence implies a relation between the difference
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-polynomials under a change of an orbit of
crossings. As in
[
4
]
, this allows us to define periodicity criterion.

Unfortunately, the new criterion,
like the criterion using Khovanov homology
[
4
]
, cannot obstruct periodicity of order
3
and
4
.
The proof of this failure is slightly more involved than in the Khovanov homology case.

1.3.
Structure of paper

Section
2
recollects basic facts about webs and foams. We build mostly on previous papers like
[
5
,
26
,
29
]
. We aim to give precise references for readers who are not that familiar with
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology.
Section
2
finishes with the definition of Khovanov-Rozansky homology as a module over the ring of symmetric polynomials.
Section
3
discusses specializations of this definition. We give
a precise construction of the Lee-Gornik spectral sequence. Next, we study properties
of Lee-Gornik homology.

The core of the paper is Section
4
. We study Khovanov-Rozansky homology for periodic links. We prove the main
result of this paper, namely Theorem
4.7
, stating that for
m
periodic links, there is a
well-defined action of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
on Khovanov-Rozansky homology.

The remaining part of the paper is devoted to exemplary applications of Theorem
4.7
. In Section
5
we study the skein spectral sequence that appears when an
orbit
of crossings on a periodic link diagram is changed. This section
is based on analogous results from Khovanov homology
[
23
,
24
]
. In Section
6

we relate equivariant Khovanov-Rozansky homology to Reshetikhin-Turaev polynomials. By analogy to
[
23
]
, we
construct the so-called difference polynomials. Section
6
ends up with periodicity criterion, Theorem
6.18
,
which is a generalization of the corresponding result
[
4
]
for Khovanov homology.

The main difference between the present paper and previous papers on equivariant Khovanov homology, lies in the control of signs. While
Koszul’s sign rule is sufficient to control signs for non-equivariant
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology, one has to be much more careful, while constructing
the group actions. The situation is more complex than in Khovanov homology, because the underlying modules of Khovanov bracket have
canonical bases. A careful analysis of sign assignments is the main technical difficulty in the present paper.

Terminology
.

In the literature, the notion of ‘equivariant
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology’ is often used in the context of homology over the ring of symmetric polynomials. In the present paper, we use the word ‘equivariant’ only in the context of the group action on a periodic link.

Throughout the paper,
N
denotes a fixed integer greater than
1
. Unless specified otherwise, all links are oriented.

Webs and foams can be combined into a category.

3.
Specializations

Fix
N>0
. Recall that we denote by
\mathbb{S}_{N}
the ring of symmetric polynomials in
N
variables with complex coefficients.

3.1.
Algebraic specialization of modules

Recall that
\cSym_{N}
denotes the category of finitely generated, graded projective
\mathbb{S}_{N}
-modules. The grading is referred to as the
quantum grading
.
As
\mathbb{S}_{N}
is naturally
isomorphic to the ring of polynomials in
N
variables, Quillen–Suslin theorem guarantees that projective
\mathbb{S}_{N}
-modules are free.
That is to say,
an object of
\cSym_{N}
is a direct sum of finite number of copies of
\mathbb{S}_{N}
, possibly with a grading shift.
We indicate such shift by
\mathbb{S}_{N}\{q^{a}\}
with
a\in\mathbb{Z}
. To be more precise, if we denote by
(\mathbb{S}_{N})^{k}
the
k
-graded part of
\mathbb{S}_{N}
, then
(\mathbb{S}_{N}\{q^{a}\})^{k}=(\mathbb{S}_{N})^{k-a}
.
A degree-
k
morphism
\phi\,\colon\,\mathbb{S}_{N}\{q^{a}\}\to\mathbb{S}_{N}\{q^{b}\}
, is the same as a map
\mathbb{S}_{N}\to\mathbb{S}_{N}
of degree
k+b-a
, hence it is a multiplication by a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\frac{k+b-a}{2}
(recall that
the indeterminates have degree two).

Denote by
\Sym^{N}(\mathbb{C})
the
N
-th symmetric product of
\mathbb{C}
, i.e.,
\Sym^{N}(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}^{N}/S_{N}
, where the symmetric group
S_{N}
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
by permuting coordinates.
In other words, elements of
\Sym^{N}(\mathbb{C})
are unordered
N
-tuples of complex numbers.
Let
\Sigma=[z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{N}]\in\Sym^{N}(\mathbb{C})
.
We say that
\Sigma
is
generic
if the elements of
\Sigma
are pairwise distinct, and
singular
if
\Sigma=[0,0,\ldots,0]
.
For a polynomial
P\in\mathbb{S}_{N}
, we denote by
P(\Sigma)
complex number resulting from the evaluation of
P
at
\Sigma
.
As
P
is symmetric, the value
P(\Sigma)
is well-defined.
The
N
-tuple
\Sigma
endows
\mathbb{C}
with a structure of a left
\mathbb{S}_{N}
-module, via
P\cdot z=P(\Sigma)z
, for
P\in\mathbb{S}_{N}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
We will denote by
\mathbb{C}_{\Sigma}
the
\mathbb{S}_{N}
-module structure on
\mathbb{C}
determined by
\Sigma
.

Definition 3.1

(Specialization functor)
.

The functor

\ev^{\Sigma}\colon\cSym_{N}\to\mathbf{Vect}(\mathbb{C}),\quad\ev^{\Sigma}(M)=M\otimes_{\mathbb{S}_{N}}\mathbb{C}_{\Sigma},

is called the
specialization functor
.
The functor
\ev^{\Sigma}
is called generic or singular, according to whether
\Sigma
is generic or singular.

Remark 3.2
.

We note that the specialization functor is usually not exact,
because
\mathbb{C}_{\Sigma}
is not a flat
\mathbb{S}_{N}
-module.

3.2.
Algebraic specialization of cochain complexes

Suppose
C^{*}
is a cochain complex of graded, free
\mathbb{S}_{N}
-modules.
We form two cochain complexes.

Definition 3.3

(Generic and singular specialization of complexes)
.

•

The
singular specialization

C^{*}_{0}
is the complex obtained by applying the singular specialization functor
\ev^{\Sigma}
to
C^{*}
with
\Sigma=[0,\dots,0]
;

•

The
generic specialization

C^{*}_{gen}
is the complex obtained by applying the generic specialization functor
\ev^{\Sigma}
to
C^{*}
with
\Sigma
generic.

Both the singular specialization and the generic specialization are cochain complexes whose underlying vector
spaces carry a grading. For instance,
if

(3.4)

C^{i}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\mathbb{S}_{N}\{q^{a_{ij}}\},

then
C^{i}_{0}=C^{i}_{gen}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\mathbb{C}\{q^{a_{ij}}\}
. We study how does the differential behave with respect to the grading.
Suppose the cochain complex
C^{*}
is written as in (
3.4
).
We can split the differential
d^{i}\colon C^{i}\to C^{i+1}
into components
d_{i,k\ell}\colon\mathbb{S}_{N}\{q^{a_{ik}}\}\to\mathbb{S}_{N}\{q^{a_{i+1,\ell}}\}
,
where
1\leq k\leq n_{i}
,
1\leq\ell\leq n_{i-1}
.
The map
d_{i,k\ell}
, having degree
0
, is the multiplication by a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\frac{a_{i+1,l}-a_{i,k}}{2}
.
The singular evaluation of a homogeneous polynomial of degree
s=\frac{a_{i+1,l}-a_{i,k}}{2}
can be non-zero only if
a_{i,k}=a_{i+1,\ell}
. That is to say, the differential
d^{i}_{0}
of the complex
C^{*}_{0}
preserves the grading.

The situation of a cochain complex
C^{*}_{gen}
is different, because the evaluation can be non-zero if
a_{i+1,\ell}-a_{i,k}\geq 0
.
That is to say, the differential
d^{i}_{gen}
does not decrease the grading.
In particular,
(C^{i}_{gen},d^{i}_{gen})
is a
filtered
cochain complex.

For later use we record the following statement.

Proposition 3.5
.

There exists a spectral sequence, whose first page is
H^{*}(C^{*}_{0})
and whose
E^{\infty}
-page is
H^{*}(C^{*}_{gen})
.

Proof.

The differentials
d^{i}\colon C^{i}\to C^{i+1}
can be decomposed as a sum
d^{i0}+d^{i1}+\dots
,
where
d^{is}
is given by a matrix of homogeneous polynomials of degree
s
.
After performing a generic specialization,
d^{is}
becomes the map
d^{is}_{gen}
increasing the grading by
2s
. That is,
d^{i}_{gen}=d^{i0}_{gen}+d^{i1}_{gen}+\dots
.
The graded part of
d^{i}_{gen}
is equal to
d^{i0}_{gen}
.

Specialization of
d^{is}
with all variables zero gives the zero map, unless
s=0
. That is,
d^{i}_{0}=d^{i0}_{0}
.
The non-zero map
d^{i0}_{0}
is equal to

d^{i0}_{gen}
, because a degree zero polynomial is necessarily constant.
Therefore, the graded part of
d^{i}_{gen}
is equal to the differential
d^{i}_{0}
.

Summarizing,
(C^{*}_{gen},d^{i}_{gen})
is a filtered cochain complex, whose graded part is
d^{i}_{0}
. A classical argument shows the existence of the spectral sequence.
∎

3.3.
Geometric specialization

We have already discussed the algebraic specialization functor

Definition 3.6

Fix

The evaluation

Definition 3.7

(The category

The category

is the category with the same objects as

Morphisms in

\Hom_{\Sigma\mathbf{Foam}_{N}}(V,W):=\Hom_{\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}^{*}_{N}}(V,W)/\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}(V).

Here,

we set

and we set
\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}(V)=\bigcap\ker\phi_{G,\Sigma}
. Note that
\Hom_{\Sigma\mathbf{Foam}_{N}}(V,W)
has a structure of a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space.

The functor
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
descends to a functor from the category
\Sigma\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
to the category of vector spaces.
Namely, we set

\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}(V)=\Hom_{\Sigma\mathbf{Foam}_{N}}(\emptyset,V).

We will use
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
to define a perturbed
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology and to describe the Lee-Gornik spectral sequence. Before that,
we discuss gradings. For this we distinguish two special cases.

We let

be the singular specialization of
\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
, i.e.,
0\mathbf{Foam}_{N}:=\Sigma\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
for
\Sigma=[0,\dots,0]
.
We let
\Sigma^{\prime}\in\Sym^{N}(\mathbb{C})
be generic.
We write
\Sigma^{\prime}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
for the
\Sigma\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
category relative to
\Sigma^{\prime}
.

The category

is graded. Indeed, each foam
F
of positive degree evaluates to
0
under
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
, hence
all the morphisms in

are given by foams of degree
0
between
q^{k}W
and
q^{\ell}W^{\prime}
with
k=\ell
.

The degree of a foam does not behave well under specializing to other variables than zero.
A foam
F
from
W
to
W^{\prime}
induces a morphism
q^{k}W\to q^{\ell}W^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
, where
k-\ell=\deg F
.
Now after specializing to
\Sigma^{\prime}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
, the foam

gives a linear map from

to
q^{\ell}W^{\prime}
. The map can be non-trivial
even if

. In particular, there might be non-trivial morphisms
in
\Sigma^{\prime}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
, between
q^{k}W
and
q^{\ell}W^{\prime}
as long as

. In other words,
\Sigma^{\prime}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
is a
filtered
category.

from

3.5.
Mirrors

Suppose
L
is a link and
L^{\prime}
is its mirror.
It is well-known that Khovanov homologies of
L
and of
L^{\prime}
are related. We now describe
an analogous result for
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology. The statement should be well-known to the experts, however, we could not find a precise statement
in the literature. To fix the terminology, we will assume that if
D
is a diagram for
L
, the diagram
D^{\prime}
for
L^{\prime}

is obtained by changing all crossings of
D
.

Proposition 3.11
.

We have an isomorphism
\KR^{k,r}_{N}(L)\cong\KR^{-k,-r}_{N}(L^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
D
be a diagram representing
L
. Assume it has
n
crossings,
which we enumerate
\Cr(D)=\{1,\dots,n\}
.
Consider the generalized cube of resolutions
\Cube(D)
for
L
with a given sign assignment; see Subsection
2.5
.
To each vertex
I
we associate

\mathcal{F}_{0}(D_{I})
, where
D_{I}
is the corresponding resolution of
D
. The differentials are induced from
differentials in
Figure
6
via the functor
\mathcal{F}_{0}
.

If
D^{\prime}
is the diagram
of the mirror, there is a correspondence of crossings of
D
and
D^{\prime}
.
For
I\in\Cube(D)
,
I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
, denote by
I^{\prime}
the ‘dual’ resolution
(-i_{1},\dots,-i_{n})\in\Cube(D^{\prime})
. The webs
D^{\prime}_{I^{\prime}}
and
D_{I}
are isomorphic by construction, but the associated homological and quantum gradings
are inverted. This is due the fact that the resolution of the negative crossing is dual to the resolution of the positive
crossing, see Subsection
2.5
.
That is, there exists a map
\iota\colon\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})\to\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})
of
\mathbb{S}_{N}
-modules given by
the identity
\iota(\mathcal{F}_{0}(D_{I}))=\mathcal{F}_{0}(D^{\prime}_{I^{\prime}})
: we say ‘identity’ because the resolutions
D_{I}
and
D^{\prime}_{I^{\prime}}
are the same.
In particular,
\iota
is canonically defined.

The differentials in the mirror complex are inverted. That is, if a differential from
\mathcal{F}_{0}(D_{I_{1}})
to
\mathcal{F}_{0}(D_{I_{2}})

is given a matrix in some basis, the differential from
\mathcal{F}_{0}(D^{\prime}_{I^{\prime}_{2}})
to
\mathcal{F}_{0}(D^{\prime}_{I^{\prime}_{1}})
is given by the transpose matrix.

All the above means that, with a fixed choice of bases of
\mathcal{F}_{0}(D_{I})
, we have an identification of cochain complexes

\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})=\Hom_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{F}_{0}\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}},\mathbb{C})

with underlying gradings reversed. As
\mathbb{C}
is a field, we obtain

H^{k,r}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})\cong H^{-k,-r}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}).

This is precisely the statement of the theorem.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

The above proof does not immediately generalize to show the relations between
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology over
\mathbb{S}_{N}
of a link with its mirror.
While it is true that
\mathcal{F}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})
is the dual cochain complex of
\mathcal{F}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})
, the Universal Coefficient Spectral Sequence shows that in general it is not true that
H^{k,r}(\mathcal{F}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}))\not\cong H^{-k,-r}(\mathcal{F}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}))

3.6.
Computation of Lee-Gornik homology

Computation of Lee-Gornik homology relies on the same principle as computation of Bar-Natan’s perturbation of Khovanov
homology
[
2
]
. A throughout discussion of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
case is done in
[
30
]
.

Let
f
be a facet of a foam with label
a
. We consider the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{f}
of all possible decorations of
f
(recall that a decoration assigns to
f
a symmetric polynomial in
a
variables) modulo all decorations that make
F
be a zero map in
\Sigma^{\prime}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
. The algebra structure is given by addition and multiplication of polynomials
associated to the face. This is called
the
algebra of decorations
.

Theorem 3.13

(see
[
30
, Lemma 4.2]
,
[
5
, Lemma 2.28]
)
.

The algebra of decorations
\mathcal{A}_{f}
is a direct sum of one dimensional summands indexed by subsets
A\subset\Sigma
of cardinality
a
.
There is a generator of each summand,
\mathbf{1}_{A}
, which is an idempotent in
\mathcal{A}_{f}
.

Suppose now
W
is a web and
F
is the identity foam from
W
to
W
. Consider the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{F}
of all possible decorations of
F

modulo decoration evaluating to zero under
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
. Formally,

\mathcal{A}_{F}
is a quotient of the
direct sum of algebras
\mathcal{A}_{f}
associated to faces of
F
.

Theorem 3.14

(see
[
5
, Lemma 3.10]
)
.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{F}
is the direct sum of one-dimensional summands. The summands are in bijection with colorings of all facets
by subsets of
\Sigma
as in Theorem
3.13
satisfying the admissibility condition of
Definition
2.7
.

Recall, after
[
5
, Section 3.2]
that if
\mathcal{C}
is a category, then the
Karoubi envelope
of
\mathcal{C}
is the category
obtained by formally splitting all idempotents of
\mathcal{C}
. In more detail, the category
\mathbf{Kar}(\mathcal{C})
has as objects pairs
(O,e)
,
where
O
is an object in
\mathcal{C}
and
e\colon O\to O
is an idempotent. A morphism between
(O,e)
and
(O^{\prime},e^{\prime})
is a map

f\in\Mor_{\mathcal{C}}(O,O^{\prime})
such that
f\circ e=e^{\prime}\circ f
.

Consider the category
\Sigma^{\prime}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
. Let
W
be a web and let
F
be the identity foam. A decoration of the identity foam by
subsets of
\Sigma^{\prime}
as in Theorem
3.14
induces a decoration of
W
by elements of
\Sigma^{\prime}
and vice versa.
In particular, a decoration of
W
by elements of
\Sigma^{\prime}
corresponds to an idempotent in
\Sigma^{\prime}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
. A decorated web can be regarded as an object in the Karoubi envelope of
\Sigma^{\prime}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
; compare
[
30
, Section 4.2]
.

Definition 3.15

(Category
\mathbf{Kar}^{0}(\Sigma^{\prime}\mathbf{Foam}_{N})
)
.

The category
\mathbf{Kar}^{0}(\Sigma^{\prime}\mathbf{Foam}_{N})
is the full subcategory of the Karoubi envelope of
\Sigma^{\prime}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
containing all objects of the form
(W,F_{W})
, where
W
is an
N
-web and
F_{W}
is an identity foam colored by
subsets of
\Sigma^{\prime}
.

Example 3.16

(see
[
30
, Corollary 3.19]
)

Let
W
be a web.
In
\mathbf{Kar}^{0}(\Sigma^{\prime}\mathbf{Foam}_{N})
we have the following relation:

W=\sum_{\mathcal{D}}(W,\mathcal{D}),

where the sum is over all admissible decorations

of

.

Proposition 3.19
.

Let

4.
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology for periodic links

In this section we let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{m}
be the group acting on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
by rotation about the axis
(0,0)\times\mathbb{R}
.
Assume
L
is a link in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
which is preserved by
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
and disjoint from the rotation axis. Let
D
be a periodic link diagram for
L
.

4.1.
Group actions on
\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}

Before proceeding to the main part of this section, let us make some preparations.
For any finite group
G
, let
BG
denote the category with a single object
\ast
and
\Hom_{BG}(\ast,\ast)=G
.
Suppose
\mathcal{B}
is an additive category, functors
X\colon BG\to\mathcal{B}
correspond bijectively to objects of
\mathcal{B}
equipped with an action of
G
.
We refer to such functors as
G
-objects.
We will denote by
\mathcal{B}[G]:=\operatorname{Fun}(BG,\mathcal{B})
the category of functors
X\colon BG\to\mathcal{B}
.
Equivalently,
\mathcal{B}[G]
is the category of
G
-objects in
\mathcal{B}
.
Morphisms in
\mathcal{B}[G]
are natural transformation of functors
X\colon BG\to\mathcal{B}
, which translate into
G
-equivariant maps of
G
-objects.
In particular, taking
\mathcal{B}=\cSym_{N}
and
G=\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, we obtain the category
\cSym_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
of
\mathbb{S}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-modules, which are free as
\mathbb{S}_{N}
-modules.
Similarly, we can consider the category
\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
of
N
-foams equipped with an action of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
.
In the same manner, we obtain categories

\operatorname{Kom}\left(\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}\right)[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]

and

\operatorname{Kom}\left(\cSym_{N}\right)[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
.

To construct the
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-equivariant
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology of a periodic link, we need to prove three things, namely:

•

Existence of the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
on
\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}
(Proposition
4.1
);

•

Equivariance of the evaluation functor
\mathcal{F}
, implying the existence of a
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-action on
\mathcal{F}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})
(Proposition
4.5
);

•

Independence of the action on the choice of a periodic diagram (Theorem
4.7
).

We begin with the first part.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
D
is a periodic link diagram. Then, there is an action of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
on
\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}
induced by rotating resolution diagrams of
D
.
In other words,
\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}
becomes an object in
\operatorname{Kom}\left(\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}\right)[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
.

Proof.

The proof resembles the argument of
[
24
]
.
Fix a generator
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
and let
\rho_{g}\colon D\to D
be a cobordism realizing the rotation by
g
.
We choose specific
\rho_{g}
, which is the trace of a path of rotations of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where the rotation angle increases linearly from
0
to
\frac{2\pi}{m}
.
To be more precise, we consider the map
r\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\times I\to\mathbb{R}
with
r(-,t)
being the rotation of the plane by
\frac{2\pi t}{m}
. Then
\rho_{g}=r(D\times I)
.
As
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
acts on
D
and permutes cyclically crossings of
D
, there is an induced action of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}

on
\Cube(D)
, which we denote by
(g,I)\mapsto gI
.
The action of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
on
\Cube(D)
extends to an action on
\SCube(D)
.
For a given diagram,
gD_{I}=D_{gI}
, where
g
acts on
D_{I}
by rotation.

Consider a sign assignment
\mathscr{s}
on
D
. For a fixed generator
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, we define the sign assignment
g\mathscr{s}
via the following formula.

(4.2)

g\mathscr{s}(gI,gI^{\prime}):=\mathscr{s}(I,I^{\prime}).

The sign assignment
g\mathscr{s}
need not be equal to
\mathscr{s}
, but we know that

(4.3)

g\mathscr{s}-\mathscr{s}=\partial\mathscr{t}

for some
0
-cochain
\mathscr{t}
on
\SCube(D)
.
By Lemma
2.26
, upon adding a constant cochain,
if necessary, we may and will assume that
\mathscr{t}
vanishes on the element
(0,0,\dots,0)
in
\Cube(D)
. Define
\mathcal{G}_{g}:=\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}\rho_{g},\mathscr{t}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}
.
To be more precise, for any
I
in
\Cube(D)
, the
I
-th component of the map
\mathcal{G}_{g}
is defined as

\mathcal{G}_{g,I}=(-1)^{\mathscr{t}(I)}\rho_{g,I}.

We argue that
\mathcal{G}_{g}
is a cochain map, i.e., if
d
is the differential on
\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}
, then
d\mathcal{G}_{g}=\mathcal{G}_{g}d
.
To see this, take resolutions
I,I^{\prime}\in\Cube(D)
such that
I^{\prime}
is an immediate successor of
I
.
Let
\delta_{I,I^{\prime}}
be the foam giving the component of the differential from
I
to
I^{\prime}
as in Figure
6
(up to sign determined by
\mathscr{s}(I,I^{\prime})
).
We have the following diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{gI}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{I^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{gI^{\prime}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-1)^{\mathscr{t}(I)}\rho_{g,I}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-1)^{\mathscr{s}(gI,gI^{\prime})}g\delta_{I,I^{\prime}}}

The vertical maps are the components of the differential in
\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}
from

The foams

.

It remains to prove that
(\mathcal{G}_{g})^{m}=\operatorname{id}
.
Note that
\mathcal{G}_{g^{2}}(D_{I})=(-1)^{\mathscr{t}(I)+\mathscr{t}(gI)}\rho_{g,gI}\circ\rho_{g,I}

\mathcal{G}_{g^{m}}(D_{I})=(-1)^{\mathscr{t}(I)+\dots+\mathscr{t}(g^{m-1}I)}\rho_{g,g^{m-1}I}\circ\cdots\circ\rho_{g,gI}\circ\rho_{g,I}=(-1)^{\mathscr{t}(I)+\dots+\mathscr{t}(g^{m-1}I)}\operatorname{id}_{D_{I}}.

Set
\widetilde{\mathscr{t}}(I)=\mathscr{t}(I)+\dots+\mathscr{t}(g^{m-1}I)

By (

), we have
\partial\widetilde{\mathscr{t}}=0
.
Therefore,
\widetilde{\mathscr{t}}
is a

\widetilde{\mathscr{t}}
is constant.
As

is fixed by the group action, the value of

Hence,

This shows that
(\mathcal{G}_{g})^{m}=\mathcal{G}_{g^{m}}=\operatorname{id}
, that is,
\mathcal{G}

\mathbb{Z}_{m}

\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}

∎

Remark 4.4
.

As pointed out in Lemma
2.26
, the choice of the
0
-cochain
\mathscr{t}
on
\SCube(D)
satisfying
g\mathscr{s}-\mathscr{s}=\partial\mathscr{t}
is not unique.
By Remark
2.33
, choosing different
0
-cochain
\mathscr{t}^{\prime}
, gives

\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}\rho_{g},\mathscr{t}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}=-\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}\rho_{g},\mathscr{t}^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}.

For odd

, setting

implies that
\widetilde{\mathscr{t}}(I)=1
, which means that
(\mathcal{G}_{g})^{m}
is
not the identity, so the choice

On the other hand, for even

by a sign, exchanges the

Consequently, the condition that fixes the value of

.

The second step of the construction, is to show that the evaluation functor
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}\to\cSym_{N}
descends to a
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-equivariant evaluation functor

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\colon\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]\to\cSym_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}].

Proposition 4.5
.

The evaluation functor
\mathcal{F}
from Definition
2.18
extends to a
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-equivariant evaluation functors

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}

\displaystyle\colon\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]\to\cSym_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}],

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}

\displaystyle\colon\operatorname{Kom}\left(\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}\right)[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]\to\operatorname{Kom}\left(\cSym_{N}\right)[\mathbb{Z}_{m}].

Proof.

Observe that if we are given an additive functor
F\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
between additive categories
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
, there is an induced functor

F\circ(-)\colon\mathcal{B}[G]\to\mathcal{C}[G],\quad(X\colon BG\to\mathcal{B})\mapsto(F\circ X\colon BG\to\mathcal{C}).

Taking
\mathcal{B}=\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}
and
\mathcal{C}=\cSym_{N}
and
F=\mathcal{F}
the evaluation functor, we obtain the
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-equivariant evaluation functor

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}}=\mathcal{F}\circ(-)\colon\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]\to\cSym_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}].

Similarly, taking
\mathcal{B}=\operatorname{Kom}\left(\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]\right)=\operatorname{Kom}\left(\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}\right)[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
and
\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{Kom}\left(\cSym_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]\right)=\operatorname{Kom}\left(\cSym_{N}\right)[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
, we obtain the second functor.

∎

The most difficult part is the invariance of our construction under equivariant isotopies of periodic links.
Recall from
[
24
]
that every
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-equivariant isotopy of periodic links can be realized as a sequence of
equivariant Reidemeister moves
, i.e., equivariant analogs of ordinary
Reidemeister moves.
If
L
is an
m
-periodic link and
D_{1}
,
D_{2}
are two
m
-periodic diagrams representing
L
, we say that
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-equivalent periodic diagrams
, if they are equivariantly
isotopic, i.e., they can be connected by a sequence of equivariant Reidemeister moves.

Remark 4.6
.

Observe that given an
m
-periodic link and two
m
-periodic diagrams of
L
, the given periodic diagrams may not be equivalent in general.
Indeed, consider the torus link
T(2,4)
.
Taking two different representations of
T(2,4)
as a braid closure, we obtain two
2
-periodic diagrams of
T(2,4)
, such that for one of the diagrams the symmetry exchanges components of the link and
for the other diagram it preserves components.
Obviously, these
2
-periodic diagrams of
T(2,4)
cannot be equivalent.

Theorem 4.7
.

If
D
and
D^{\prime}
are
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-equivalent
m
-periodic link diagrams, then there is a chain homotopy equivalence between
\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}
and
\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}
in the category
\operatorname{Kom}\left(\mathbb{S}\mathbf{Foam}_{N}\right)

and an induced quasi-isomorphism between
\mathcal{F}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})
and
\mathcal{F}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})
in
\operatorname{Kom}\left(\cSym_{N}\right)[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
.

Proof.

Any two periodic diagrams
D,D^{\prime}
of the same periodic link
L
are related by a sequence of equivariant Reidemeister moves;
see
[
24
, Proposition 2.6]
.
Therefore, to prove Theorem

, we need to prove the following key lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose
D^{\prime}
is a diagram of
L
obtained from
D
by a single equivariant Reidemeister move.
Then, the equivariant Reidemeister move induces a chain homotopy equivalence
\Phi\colon\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}\to\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}
in

and an induced quasi-isomorphism

.

4.8
to Subsection
4.4
. We will now deduce

from Lemma

.
Suppose
D
and

are two link diagrams of the same periodic link. As they are connected by a sequence of equivariant

\Phi\colon\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}\to\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}

∎

4.2.
Equivariant
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology

Suppose
L
is an
m
-periodic link and choose a periodic link diagram
D
for it. The cochain complex
C^{*}(D):=\mathcal{F}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})
is a graded cochain complex of
\mathbb{S}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-modules.
By Theorem
4.7
, the
\mathbb{S}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-quasi-isomorphism class of
C^{*}(D)
depends only on the equivalence class of
D
.
Denote by
\KR^{*}_{\mathbb{S}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]}(L)
the cohomology of
C^{*}(D)
as a
\mathbb{S}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-module.

Proposition 4.9
.

The
\mathbb{S}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-module structure on
\KR^{*}_{\mathbb{S}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]}(L)
descends via the evaluation functor to a well-defined
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-module structure on
\KR_{N}^{*}(L)
and
\Lee_{N}^{*}(L)
.
The Lee–Gornik spectral sequence exists in the category of finitely generated
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-modules.

Proof.

Suppose
D
and
D^{\prime}
are two equivalent
m
-periodic link diagrams of
L
.
We know that
D
and
D^{\prime}
are related by a sequence of equivariant Reidemeister moves, in particular
D
and
D^{\prime}

are related by a sequence of ordinary Reidemeister moves.
That is, there exists a chain homotopy equivalence
h\colon C^{*}(D)\to C^{*}(D^{\prime})
in the category
\operatorname{Kom}\left(\Sym_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]\right)
.
By Theorem
4.7
, the map induced by
h
on cohomology of
C^{*}(D)
and
C^{*}(D)
, is a
\mathbb{S}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-linear isomorphism.

Choose
\Sigma\in\Sym^{N}(\mathbb{C})
and consider the cochain complexes
C^{*}_{\Sigma}(D)=\ev^{\Sigma}(C^{*}(D))
and
C^{*}_{\Sigma}(D^{\prime})=\ev^{\Sigma}(C^{*}(D^{\prime}))
.
The map
h_{\Sigma}=\ev^{\Sigma}(h)
is a chain homotopy equivalence in the category of complexes of
\mathbb{C}
-modules.
In particular
h_{\Sigma}
induces an isomorphism on cohomology of
C^{*}_{\Sigma}(D)
and
C^{*}_{\Sigma}(D^{\prime})
.

Since the evaluation
\ev^{\Sigma}
commutes with the
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-action, the map
h_{\Sigma}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-equivariant.
Consequently, the map induced by
h_{\Sigma}
on cohomology is also
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-equivariant.
Since
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
is a semi-simple algebra, we can use Schur’s Lemma to conclude that every cochain complex in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
splits.
In particular, it follows that
h_{\Sigma}
is a chain homotopy equivalence in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
.

In particular, choosing
\Sigma
to be singular or generic, we recover that
\KR^{*}_{N}(L)
and
\Lee_{N}^{*}(L)
have a well-defined structure of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-modules.

We now want to prove that the spectral sequence from
\KR^{*}_{N}(L)
to
\Lee_{N}^{*}(L)
exists in the category of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-modules.
Observe that the evaluation map commutes with the
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-action.
Following the proof of Proposition
3.5
,
we decompose
d^{i}_{gen}=d^{i}_{0}+d^{i}_{1}+\dots
. The decomposition is
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-equivariant, because the terms
d^{i}_{j}
come from
the specialization of degree
j
terms in the differential in
\mathcal{F}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})
, and the
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-action preserves the quantum degree.
Consequently, the spectral sequence can be defined over the ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
.
∎

We now pass to the definition of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-equivariant Khovanov–Rozansky homology.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
L
be an
m
-periodic link. The
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-equivariant Khovanov–Rozansky
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology

\EKR^{k,r}_{N}

is the group
\KR^{k,r}_{N}
with the
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-module structure coming from the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
on
\KR^{*}_{N}(L)
.
Likewise, the
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-equivariant Lee
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology

\ELee^{k}_{N}
is the group
\Lee^{k}
with the
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-structure
coming from the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
on
\Lee^{*}_{N}(L)
.

Next result relies on the convention we adopted that the mirror link diagram is obtained by switching all the crossings (‘the mirror is parallel to the plane’). The other convention (‘the mirror is perpendicular to the plane’) leads to a similar result, but the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
is
inverted.

Proposition 4.11
.

Suppose
L
is an
m
-periodic link with periodic diagram
D
. Let
L^{\prime}
be the mirror. Then, for any
k,r
there is a map
of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-modules

\EKR^{k,r}_{N}(L)\cong\EKR^{-k,-r}_{N}(L^{\prime}).

Proof.

The isomorphism at the level of vector spaces over
\mathbb{C}
was established in Proposition
3.11
. We need to argue that this isomorphism commutes with the group action.
We use the notation of the proof of Proposition
3.11
.
The group
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
acts on
\Cube(D)
and on
\Cube(D^{\prime})
in the same manner, that is,
(gI)^{\prime}=gI^{\prime}
.
Moreover, mirroring a resolution commutes with rotating, that is,
gD_{I}=D_{gI}
and
gD^{\prime}_{I^{\prime}}=D^{\prime}_{gI^{\prime}}
.
With this identification, it is easy to see that the map
\iota\colon\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})\to\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})
given by

commutes with the group action.

Choose a basis of
\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})
as
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-module.
Then,
\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})
has the same basis.
Using this basis, we can write a
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-isomorphism of modules.

(4.12)

\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})\xrightarrow{\cong}\Hom_{\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]}(\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}),\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]).

This isomorphism maps a basis to the dual basis.

Note that with a choice of a basis, the differential on
\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})
is the transpose of the differential
on
\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})
. Therefore, the identity (
4.12
) is in fact an isomorphism of cochain complexes.
The group ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
is semisimple, so
\Hom_{\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]}(\cdot,\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}])
is exact. It follows that

H^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D^{\prime}\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}))=\Hom(H^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}),\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}])
. The latter group is (non-canonically)
isomorphic to
H^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}))
.
∎

4.3.
Decomposition of

-homology

This subsection is based on

24

where an analogous decomposition is constructed for Khovanov homology (that is, for

problems. We present a decomposition of

and

into simple
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]

This leads to a third grading of

Fix a generator

.
The group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
is semisimple, and we have a decomposition

\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{C}_{\xi_{m}^{j}},

where
\mathbb{C}_{\xi_{m}^{j}}
denotes the
\xi_{m}^{j}
-eigenspace of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]

\xi_{m}=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{m}\right)
and
j=0,1,\ldots,m-1

The above decomposition is obtained by writing

(4.13)

1=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}e_{j},

where
e_{0},e_{1}\ldots,e_{m-1}
are pairwise orthogonal idempotents, i.e.,
e_{j}e_{k}=\delta_{kl}e_{j}
, for
0\leq j,k\leq m-1
.
Furthermore, for any
j=0,1,\ldots,m-1
, we have
g\cdot e_{j}=\xi_{m}^{j}e_{j}
.

In particular, we can present any
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-module
M
as a direct sum over different representations of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
.
More precisely,

(4.14)

M=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m-1}M_{\xi_{m}^{i}},\quad\text{where }M_{\xi_{m}^{i}}:=e_{i}M

is the
\xi_{m}^{i}
-eigenspace of
M
, for
i=0,1,\ldots,m-1
.
Since the idempotents
e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{m-1}
are pairwise orthogonal, it follows easily that for any finitely generated
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-modules
M
and
N
, we have

(4.15)

\Hom_{\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]}(M_{\xi_{m}^{j}},N_{\xi_{m}^{k}})=0,

unless
j=k
.

Remark 4.16
.

Observe that since we work over complex symmetric polynomials, the formula (
4.13
) holds also in
\mathbb{S}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
.
Consequently, the group ring
\mathbb{S}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
admits a decomposition into eigenspaces of the action of the chosen generator
g
:

\mathbb{S}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{S}_{N,\xi_{m}^{j}},\quad S_{N,\xi_{m}^{j}}:=e_{j}\mathbb{S}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}],

where
\mathbb{S}_{N,\xi_{m}^{j}}
are ideals consisting of
\xi_{m}^{j}
-eigenvectors of the action of
g
, for
j=0,1,\ldots,m-1
.
Consequently, any finitely generated
\mathbb{S}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]
-module
M
admits a decomposition into eigenspaces of
g

(
4.14
’)

M=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{m-1}M_{\xi_{m}^{j}},\quad M_{\xi_{m}^{j}}:=e_{j}M.

Furthermore, in analogy to (

are finitely generated

-modules, then

(
4.15
’)

\Hom_{\mathbb{S}_{N}[\mathbb{Z}_{m}]}(M_{\xi_{m}^{j}},N_{\xi_{m}^{k}})=0,

unless
j=k
.
Consequently, if

is an

-periodic link diagram, the

-equivariant Khovanov-Rozansky homology of

In fact, there is a decomposition on the level of the cochain complex,

\mathcal{F}\left(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}})\right)=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathcal{F}_{\xi_{m}^{j}}\left(\mathopen{\hbox{${[}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${[}$}}D\mathclose{\hbox{${]}$}\kern-1.38889pt\hbox{${]}$}}\right)

and, for

From decomposition (
4.14
’), we create the following splitting

]

\LeeP_{N}(K)=q^{s_{N}(K)}(q^{-N+1}+q^{-N+3}+\dots+q^{N-1})
,
where
s_{N}(K)
is the Lewark’s
s_{N}
-invariant; see
[
16
]
.

The following statement is an immediate consequence of Lemma
4.19
.

Lemma 6.6
.

If the action of the symmetry group on the set of components of
L
is trivial, then
\LeeP_{N,d}
is equal to
\LeeP_{N}
if

d=1
, and to zero otherwise.

The next result relates the polynomials
\KRP
and
\LeeP
. Its proof is the same as in the Khovanov case, see
[
4
, Proposition 2.17]
.
See also
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
and
[
16
, Proposition 5.2]
.

Proposition 6.7
.

For any link
L
there are polynomials
R_{1},\dots
with non-negative coefficients such that

\KRP_{N}(L)=\LeeP_{N}(L)+(1+tq^{2N})R_{1}+(1+tq^{4N})R_{2}+\dots.

Moreover, if
L
is
m
periodic and
d|m
, then

\KRP_{N,d}(L)=\LeeP_{N,d}(L)+(1+tq^{2N})R^{d}_{1}+(1+tq^{4N})R^{d}_{2}+\dots

for polynomials
R^{d}_{1},\dots
with non-negative coefficients.

Proof.

The statement follows from the same argument as in
[
4
, Proposition 2.17]
. The
differential on the
k
-th page of the Lee-Gornik spectral sequence (Theorem
3.10
) has
bidegree
(1,2Nk)
; compare
[
15
, Section 3.2]
.
The factor
2
before
Nk
comes from the grading of
X_{i}
-variables in
\mathbb{S}_{N}
.

The polynomials
R_{k}
and
R^{d}_{k}
are Poincaré
polynomials associates to the image of the differential on the
E_{k}
-page, compare
[
4
, Proposition 2.16]
.
∎

6.2.
The Reshetikhin–Turaev
\RT_{N}
polynomials

Recall
[
7
,
25
]
that the HOMFLYPT polynomial of an oriented link in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the polynomial
X(a,b)
uniquely defined by
its value on the unknot and the skein relation

(6.8)

aX_{L_{+}}(a,b)-a^{-1}X_{L_{-}}(a,b)=bX_{L_{0}}(a,b),

where
L_{+}
,
L_{0}
and
L_{-}
are links differing at a single crossing.

For
N\geq 0
we consider the following specialization of the HOMFLYPT polynomial.

(6.9)

\RT_{N}(q)=X(q^{N},q-q^{-1})

with the normalization that

\RT_{N}(\textrm{unknot})=\frac{q^{N}-q^{-N}}{q-q^{-1}}.

The polynomial
\RT_{0}
(with a slightly different normalization) is the Alexander polynomial,
\RT_{1}\equiv 1
, and
\RT_{2}
is the Jones polynomial.
For
N>2
we obtain a polynomial, which we refer to as the
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-polynomial of
L
.

Remark 6.10
.

The polynomials
\RT_{N}
were introduced originally by Reshetikhin and Turaev
[
28
,
34
]
via representation theory.
We refer to
[
21
]
for relations with webs.

It was shown by
[
11
,
10
]
that
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology categorifies the
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
polynomial.
For later use, we recall the statement.

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
L
be a link and
\KR_{N}^{k,r}(L)
its
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology. Then

\RT_{N}(L)=\sum_{k,r}(-1)^{k}q^{r}\dim\KR_{N}^{k,r}(L)=\KRP_{N}|_{t=-1}.

The skein relation for the HOMFLYPT polynomial specializes to the skein relation for the
\RT_{N}
polynomial.

6.3.
Difference polynomials

Assume now that
m=p^{\ell}
is a prime power and let
D
be an
m
-periodic diagram of an
m
-periodic link
L
. The

\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
homology of
L
decomposes as in (
4.17
). We set

\RT_{N,j}=\KRP_{N,p^{j}}|_{t=-1},

where
\KRP_{N,d}
is as in (
6.3
).

For future use, we note the following corollary of Proposition
4.11
.

Corollary 6.13
.

If
L
is a
p^{n}
periodic link and
L^{\prime}
its mirror, then
\RT_{N,j}(L)(q)=\RT_{N,j}(L^{\prime})(q^{-1})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.11
, there is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces
\EKR^{k,r,m}(L)=\EKR^{-k,-r,m}(L^{\prime})
. The statement
follows by definition of
\RT_{N,j}(L)
.
∎

We define the
difference
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-polynomials via

\DP_{N,j}(D)=\begin{cases}\RT_{N,p^{j}}(D)-\RT_{N,p^{j+1}}(D)&0\leq j<\ell\\
\RT_{N,p^{\ell}}(D)&j=\ell.\end{cases}

Suppose
L_{+}
,
L_{-}
and
L_{0}
are three
m=p^{\ell}
-periodic links for for the periodic resolutions, that is,
L_{+}
differs from
L_{-}
and from
L_{0}

on an orbit of crossings.

Proposition 6.14

(compare
[
23
, Theorem 4.7]
)
.

We have the following relations between
\DP_{N,j}(D)
polynomials.

(1)

For
j=0
the following formula holds

q^{mN}\DP_{N,0}(L_{+})-q^{-mN}\DP_{N,0}(L_{-})=(q^{-m}-q^{m})\DDJ_{N,0}(L_{0}).

(2)

For any
0\leq j<\ell
, the following congruence is satisfied

q^{mN}\DP_{N,\ell-j}(L_{+})-q^{-mN}\DP_{N,\ell-j}(L_{-})\equiv(q^{-m}-q^{m})\DDJ_{N,\ell-j}(L_{0})\pmod{q^{p^{j}}-q^{-p^{j}}}.

Proof.

We argue as in the proof of
[
23
, Theorem 3.6]
.
Suppose that
\{E_{r}^{\ast,\ast},d_{r}\}_{r\geq 1}
is a spectral sequence of graded finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces which converges to a double graded
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
H^{\ast,\ast}
.
Suppose, furthermore, that the spectral sequence collapses at a finite stage.
For any
r\geq 1
define the
Poincaré polynomials
of the page
E_{r}^{\ast,\ast}
,

P(E_{r}^{\ast,\ast})=\sum_{i,j}t^{i+j}\operatorname{qdim}_{\mathbb{C}}E_{r}^{i,j}.

Recall that for a graded
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
V^{\ast}
, we define

\operatorname{qdim}_{\mathbb{C}}=\sum_{i}q^{i}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V^{i}.

From
[
20
, Exercise 1.7]
, we deduce that for any
r\geq 1
,

(6.15)

P(E_{r}^{\ast,\ast})(-1,q)=P(E_{\infty}^{\ast,\ast})(-1,q)=\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{qdim}_{\mathbb{C}}H^{i,\ast}.

Fix a
p^{\ell}
-periodic diagram
D
.
We apply the formula (
6.15
) to spectral sequences constructed in Proposition
5.11
.
We obtain

P(E_{1}^{\ast,\ast}(p^{\ell-u}))(-1,q)=P(E_{\infty}^{\ast,\ast})(-1,q)=\RT_{N,\ell-u}(D).

The description of the
E_{1}^{\ast,\ast}(p^{\ell-u})
given in Proposition
5.11
implies that
P(E_{1}^{\ast,\ast})(-1,q)
is a linear combination of polynomials
\RT_{N,j}(D_{\widehat{I}})
, where

\widehat{I}\in A_{k}(X)
and appropriate
j
.
Consequently,

(6.16)

\begin{split}\DP_{N,\ell-u}(D)&=\RT_{N,\ell-u}(D)-\RT_{N,\ell-u+1}(D)=\\
&=P(E_{1}^{\ast,\ast}(p^{\ell-u}))(-1,q)-P(E_{1}^{\ast,\ast}(p^{\ell-u+1}))(-1,q).\end{split}

We can now apply formula (
6.16
) to
\DP_{N,\ell-u}(L_{+})
and
\DP_{N,\ell-u}(L_{+})
obtaining

\displaystyle\DP_{N,\ell-u}(L_{+})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{p^{\ell}}\sum_{s=u}^{\ell}\sum_{{\widehat{I}}\in A_{k}^{s}(L_{+},X)}(-1)^{k}q^{-p^{\ell}(N-1)-k}\DP_{N,s-u}(D_{{\widehat{I}}}),

\displaystyle\DP_{N,\ell-u}(L_{-})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-p^{\ell}}^{0}\sum_{s=u}^{\ell}\sum_{{\widehat{I}}\in A_{p^{\ell}+k}^{s}(L_{-},X)}(-1)^{k}q^{p^{\ell}(N-1)+p^{\ell}-k}\DP_{N,s-u}(D_{{\widehat{I}}}).

Since,
A_{k}(L_{+},X)=A_{p^{\ell}-k}(L_{-},X)
, we obtain

q^{p^{\ell}N}\DP_{N,\ell-u}(L_{+})-q^{-p^{\ell}N}\DP_{N,\ell-u}(L_{-})=\sum_{k=0}^{p^{\ell}}\sum_{s=u}^{\ell}\sum_{{\widehat{I}}\in A_{k}^{s}(L_{+},X)}(-1)^{k}(q^{p^{\ell}-k}-q^{-p^{\ell}+k})\DP_{N,s-u}(D_{{\widehat{I}}}).

Observe that
A_{k}^{s}(L_{+},X)
is empty unless
p^{s}
divides
k
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle q^{p^{\ell}N}\DP_{N,\ell-u}(L_{+})-q^{-p^{\ell}N}\DP_{N,\ell-u}(L_{-})-(q^{p^{\ell}}-q^{-p^{\ell}})\DP_{N,\ell-u}(L_{0})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{p^{\ell}-1}\sum_{s=u}^{\ell}\sum_{{\widehat{I}}\in A_{k}^{s}(L_{+},X)}(-1)^{k}(q^{p^{\ell}-k}-q^{-p^{\ell}+k})\DP_{N,s-u}(D_{{\widehat{I}}}).

For
u=\ell
, the right-hand side of the above equality is zero, i.e.

q^{p^{\ell}N}\DP_{N,0}(L_{+})-q^{-p^{\ell}N}\DP_{N,0}(L_{-})=(q^{p^{\ell}}-q^{-p^{\ell}})\DP_{N,0}(L_{0}),

as desired.

Suppose now that
0\leq u<\ell
.
For any
u\leq s\leq\ell
and
k
divisible by
p^{s}
, if
{\widehat{I}}\in A_{k}^{s}(L_{+},X)
, we have

(q^{p^{\ell}-k}-q^{-p^{\ell}+k})\DP_{N,s-u}(D_{{\widehat{I}}})\equiv 0\pmod{q^{p^{u}}-q^{p^{u}}}.

Consequently,

q^{p^{\ell}N}\DP_{N,0}(L_{+})-q^{-p^{\ell}N}\DP_{N,0}(L_{-})\equiv(q^{p^{\ell}}-q^{-p^{\ell}})\DP_{N,0}(L_{0})\pmod{q^{p^{u}}-q^{p^{-u}}}.

∎

Corollary 6.17
.

Let
I_{p^{\ell}}
be the ideal in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
generated by polynomials

(q^{p^{\ell}}-q^{p^{-\ell}}),p\cdot(q^{p^{\ell}-1}-q^{-p^{\ell}+1}),\ldots,p^{\ell-1}(q^{p}-q^{-p}),p^{\ell}.

If
L
is a
p^{\ell}
-periodic link, then

\RT_{N}(L)(q)\equiv\RT_{N}(L)(q^{-1})\pmod{I_{p^{\ell}}}.

Proof.

The proof is essentially a repetition of an analogous result in
[
23
]
. For the reader’s convenience,
we present a sketch.

A
p^{\ell}
-periodic link can be transformed to its mirror by a sequence of equivariant crossing changes.
Each such crossing change, by virtue of Proposition
6.14
, changes the difference polynomials
\DP_{N,j}

by a multiple of
q^{p^{\ell-j}}-q^{-p^{\ell-j}}
. From the definition of difference polynomials,
we recover that an equivariant crossing change changes the polynomial
\RT_{N,j}
by a multiple of
q^{p^{\ell-j}}-q^{-p^{\ell-j}}
.
The relation (
6.4
), evaluated at
t=-1
shows, that under an equivariant crossing change changes
the polynomial
\RT_{N}
by an element belonging to
I_{p^{\ell}}
. This means, that the polynomial
\RT_{N}
of a link and of its mirror
differ by an element from
I_{p^{\ell}}
. We conclude by Corollary
6.13
.
∎

6.4.
Periodicity criterion

The following result ports the periodicity criterion of
[
4
]
to the case of
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
-homology.

Theorem 6.18
.

Suppose
L
is an
m=p^{\ell}
periodic knot with
p
-prime.
Then, there exist polynomials
\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{P}_{1},\dots
such that

\KRP_{N}=\mathcal{P}_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(p^{j}-p^{j-1})\mathcal{P}_{j}.

Here
\mathcal{P}_{0},\dots
are Laurent polynomials in
t,q
such that

(P-1)

the Laurent polynomial
\mathcal{P}_{0}
can be presented as
\mathcal{P}_{0}=q^{s_{N}(L)}(q^{1-N}+q^{3-N}+\dots+q^{N-1})+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+tq^{Nj})\mathcal{S}_{0j}(t,q)
, while the Laurent polynomials
\mathcal{P}_{k}
,
k>0
can be presented as
\mathcal{P}_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+tq^{Nj})\mathcal{S}_{kj}(t,q)
.

(P-2)

The Laurent polynomials
\mathcal{S}_{kj}
,
k\geq 0
, from item
(P-1)
have non-negative coefficients;

(P-3)

the polynomials
\mathcal{P}_{k}
,
k\geq 0
satisfy the following congruence relation:
\mathcal{P}_{k}(-1,q)-\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(-1,q)\equiv\mathcal{P}_{k}(-1,q^{-1})-\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(-1,q^{-1})\pmod{q^{p^{\ell-k}}-q^{-p^{\ell-k}}}
.

Here,
s_{N}(L)
is the Lewark’s
s
-invariant; see Lemma
6.5
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same line as the proof of the main result in
[
4
]
. For integral
k,r
, we have
\KR^{k,r}_{N}(L)=\EKR^{k,r}_{N}(L)
as
vector spaces. The latter admits the decomposition as in (
4.17
):

\EKR_{N}^{k,r}(L)=\bigoplus_{d|m}\EKR_{N}^{k,r,d}(L).

As
m=p^{\ell}
, writing
\mathcal{P}_{j}=P_{N,p^{j}}
as the Poincaré polynomial of
\EKR_{N}^{\bullet,\bullet,p^{j}}(L)

we have

\KRP_{N}(L)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}(p^{j}-p^{j-1})\mathcal{P}_{j}.

Further properties of
\mathcal{P}_{j}
are deduced from the Lee–Gornik spectral sequence. As the Lee–Gornik spectral sequence is
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-equivariant,
the submodule
\EKR_{N}^{\bullet,\bullet,p^{j}}
converges to
\ELee_{N}^{\bullet,p^{j}}
. Therefore, by the second
part of Proposition
6.7
, with
\mathcal{S}_{jk}=R^{p^{j}}_{k}
, we have:

\mathcal{P}_{j}=\LeeP_{N,p^{j}}(L)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+tq^{2Nk})\mathcal{S}_{jk}.

The sum is finite (the spectral sequence degenerates at a finite page, because its
E_{1}
page is finite dimensional over
\mathbb{C}
),
and all the
\mathcal{S}_{jk}
have non-negative coefficients.

The computation of
\ELee
in Lemma
6.6
, together with Lemma
6.5
, yields

\LeeP_{N,p^{0}}(L)=q^{s_{N}(L)}(q^{-N+1}+q^{-N+3}+\dots+q^{N-1}),

whereas
\LeeP_{N,p^{j}}(L)=0
for
j>0
. This proves
(P-1)
and
(P-2)
.

The congruence relation of
(P-3)

is proved using Proposition
6.14
. Namely,
(\mathcal{P}_{j}-\mathcal{P}_{j+1})|_{t=-1}
is, by definition, the difference
polynomial
\DP_{N,j}
. Proposition
6.14
indicates
that changing an orbit of crossings on a diagram, does not affect

\DP_{N,j}
modulo the ideal generated by
q^{p^{n-j}}-q^{-p^{n-j}}
. Changing all orbits of crossings turns a link into its mirror.
By Corollary
6.13
, the polynomial
\DP_{N,j}
of a
p^{n}
-symmetric link
and of its mirror are congruent modulo
q^{p^{n-j}}-q^{-p^{n-j}}
. This proves
(P-3)
and concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

6.5.
Periodicity 3 and 4

We will now show that the criterion cannot obstruct a knot from being
3
or
4
-periodic. We begin with the following result.

Theorem 6.19

(
[
8
]
)
.

If
K
is a knot and
X
its HOMFLY-PT polynomial, then
X(a,b)=T(a,b)q(a,b)+1
, where
q(a,b)
is a Laurent polynomial
with integer coefficients and
T(a,b)=a^{4}-2a^{2}+1-a^{2}b^{2}
is the HOMFLY-PT polynomial for the trefoil.

From Theorem
6.19
and (
6.9
) we deduce the following result.

Corollary 6.20
.

The
\RT_{N}
polynomial of a knot
K
has form

\RT_{N}(q)=A(q)(q^{4N}-2q^{2N}+1-q^{2N}(q-q^{-1})^{2})+1,

where
A(q)
is a Laurent polynomial with integer coefficients.

Define

T_{N}=q^{4N}-2q^{2N}+1-q^{2N}(q-q^{-1})^{2}.

Lemma 6.21
.

•

If
\zeta_{6}
is a root of unity of order
6
, then
T_{N}(\zeta_{6})=0
unless
3|N
;

•

If
\zeta_{8}
is a root of unity of order
8
and
N
is odd, then
T_{N}(\zeta_{8})=0
.

Proof.

To prove the first part,
we check the statement directly for
N=1,2
. From this, the statement follows for general
N
congruent to
1
or
2
modulo
3
.
The second part is proved in the same way.
∎

Corollary 6.22
.

For any knot
K
, we have the congruences
\RT_{N}(q)-\RT_{N}(q^{-1})\equiv 0\bmod q^{3}-q^{-3}
,

\RT_{N}(q)-\RT_{N}(q^{-1})\equiv 0\bmod q^{4}-q^{-4}
.

Proof.

We begin with the first part.
This congruence is equivalent to saying that for any root of unity
\zeta_{6}
of order
6
, it holds
\RT_{N}(\zeta_{6})-\RT_{N}(\zeta_{6}^{-1})=0
.
Consider two cases. Suppose
N
is not a multiple of
3
. By Corollary
6.20
and
Lemma
6.21
, we have
\RT_{N}(\zeta_{6})=1
, so
\RT_{N}(\zeta_{6})-\RT_{N}(\zeta_{6}^{-1})=0
.

Suppose
3|N
. We have the following decomposition of the Khovanov–Rozansky polynomial, see Proposition
6.7
.

\KRP_{N}(t,q)=q^{s}(q^{1-N}+q^{3-N}+\dots+q^{N-1})+\sum_{j}(1+tq^{2Nj})R_{j}(t,q).

Now the
\RT_{N}
polynomial is obtained by substituting
t=-1
. The term
(1+tq^{2Nj})
for
t=-1
and
q=\zeta_{6}

is equal to zero. On the other hand,

q^{1-N}+q^{3-N}+\dots+q^{N-1}=\frac{q^{N}-q^{-N}}{q-q^{-1}}.

The latter expression is zero, when evaluated at a root of unity of order dividing
2N
. That is to say

\RT_{N}(\zeta_{6})=\KRP_{N}(-1,\zeta_{6})=0.

The second congruence is proved analogously. First, assume that
N
is odd. Then,
\RT_{N}(\zeta_{8})=1
by the same argument
combining Corollary
6.20
and Lemma
6.21
.
Suppose
4|N
. Then, the same argument as above shows that
\KRP_{N}(-1,\zeta_{8})=0
. It remains to deal with the case
N=4k+2
.
Suppose
\zeta_{8}
is such that
\zeta_{8}^{4k}=1
.
Consider
X(a,b)
, the HOMFLYPT polynomial for the knot. From the formula
\RT_{N}(q)=X(q^{N},q-q^{-1})
we immediately deduce
that
\RT_{N}(\zeta_{8})=\RT_{2}(\zeta_{8})
. Now,
\RT_{2}
is the Jones polynomial. It was proved in
[
4
, Section 4.6]
that
\RT_{2}(\zeta_{8})-\RT_{2}(\zeta_{8}^{-1})=0
.
Note that the formula
\RT_{N}(\zeta_{8})=\RT_{2}(\zeta_{8})
holds also if
N=8k+2
regardless of the sign of
\zeta_{8}^{4}
.

The remaining case is when
N=8k-2
and
\zeta_{8}^{4}=-1
. Write
X(a,b)=\sum\alpha_{ij}a^{i}b^{j}
.
Note that the skein relation (
6.8
) implies, that
\alpha_{ij}=0
unless
j
is even
(remember, that we deal with knots).
As
\RT_{N}(q)=X(q^{N},q-q^{-1})
, we have

\RT_{N}(\zeta_{8})-\RT_{N}(\zeta_{8}^{-1})=\sum\alpha_{ij}\zeta_{8}^{Ni}(\zeta_{8}-\zeta_{8}^{-1})^{j}-\zeta_{8}^{-Ni}(-\zeta_{8}+\zeta_{8}^{-1})^{j}=\\
\sum\alpha_{ij}(\zeta_{8}^{-2i}-\zeta_{8}^{2i})(\zeta_{8}-\zeta_{8}^{-1})^{j}=-\RT_{2}(\zeta_{8})+\RT_{2}(\zeta_{8}^{-1}).

However,
\RT_{2}
is the Jones polynomial. It was proved in
[
4
, Section 4.6]
,
that
\RT_{2}(q)-\RT_{2}(q^{-1})
is divisible by
q^{4}-q^{-4}
. Therefore, the latter quantity is zero.
∎

Corollary 6.23
.

Suppose
K
is a knot. Set
\mathcal{P}_{0}=\KRP_{N}
,
\mathcal{S}_{0j}=R_{j}
, where
R_{j}
is as in Proposition
6.7
.
Then
\mathcal{S}_{0j},\mathcal{P}_{0}
satisfy the statement of Theorem
6.18
regardless of whether or not
K
is really
3
or
4
-periodic.

Proof.

We prove the corollary for
3
-periodicity, the case of
4
-periodicity is analogous.
Item
(P-1)
is satisfied by definition.
It follows from Proposition
6.7
that
\mathcal{S}_{0j}
have non-negative coefficients. The congruence
(P-3)
is
a direct consequence of Corollary
6.22
.
∎

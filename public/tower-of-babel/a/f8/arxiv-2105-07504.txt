Mean curvature flow in Homology and foliations of hyperbolic 3 3 -manifolds Thanks: FVP was supported by the Minerva Research Foundation and by NSF grant DMS-2001997.
Marco A. M. Guaraco, Vanderson Lima, Franco Vargas Pallete

Abstract
. We study global aspects of the mean curvature flow of non-separating hypersurfaces S S in closed manifolds. For instance, if S S has non-vanishing mean curvature, we show its level set flow converges smoothly towards an embedded minimal hypersurface Γ \Gamma . We prove a similar result for the flow with surgery in dimension 2. As an application we show the existence of monotone incompressible isotopies in manifolds with negative curvature. Combining this result with min-max theory, we show that quasi-Fuchsian and hyperbolic 3 3 -manifolds fibered over S 1 \mathrm{S}^{1} admit smooth entire foliations whose leaves are either minimal or have non-vanishing mean curvature. We also conclude the existence of outermost minimal surfaces for quasi-Fuchsian ends and study their continuity with respect to variations of the quasi-Fuchsian metric.



1.
Main results

Let
S
be an embedded hypersurface in a Riemannian
manifold
M
. It is an interesting question whether one can evolve
S
by the mean curvature flow towards an embedded minimal hypersurface
\Gamma
. As singularities naturally emerge during the evolution, weak notions of mean curvature flow are necessary in order to continue the process for large times. In some cases, regularity theories for weak flows are developed under the crucial assumption that the evolving hypersurface is a boundary. A natural question in this context is whether those results can be extended to non-separating evolutions.

We first look at the
level set flow
approach (also called “biggest flow” in
[
16
]
). In
[
32
]
, B. White developed a regularity theory for this flow assuming
S
is the boundary of a compact mean-convex set. One of the key observations in this case is that the evolution is monotone and encloses a region satisfying an outward minimizing property. When
S
is non-separating the global geometry of
M
might complicate this description: not only
S
does not bound a region but its evolution might repeat points of
M
(see
Figure

1
).

Throughout this paper we assume that
3\leq\dim M\leq 7
and say that
S\subset M
has
non-vanishing mean curvature
if its mean curvature vector satisfies
\vec{H}(p)\neq 0,\forall\,p\in S
.

Our first result is:

1.1 Theorem
.

Let
S\subset M
be a non-separating hypersurface with non-vanishing mean curvature. If
t\mapsto F_{t}(S)
is the level set flow of
S
, then there exists a stable minimal hypersurface
\Gamma
, such that
F_{t}(S)\to\Gamma
smoothly as
t\to\infty
.

Next, we study in more detail the evolution of a 2-dimensional surface
S
. For this, we look at
the mean-convex mean curvature flow with surgery
from
[
10
,
12
]
(a similar theory was developed by
[
5
]
). First, we extend the results from
[
10
,
5
]
to the non-separating case:

1.2 Theorem
.

Let
M
be a closed
3
-manifold and
S\subset M
a non-separating surface with non-vanishing mean curvature. Let
\Gamma
be the limit of the level set flow of
S
. Then, there exists a mean curvature flow with surgery starting at
S
such that, after finitely many surgeries, converges smoothly to
\Gamma
with the same multiplicity as the level set flow.

The mean-convex mean curvature flow with surgery has been used in
[
10
]
to construct smooth mean-convex foliations of
3
-balls by
2
-spheres (see also
[
6
,
7
,
23
]
for other applications). Building on these ideas, our next goal is to prove the existence of strictly monotone mean-convex isotopies of incompressible surfaces, both in the separating and non-separating case (see
Definition

6.1
). More precisely, we show:

1.3 Theorem
.

Let
M
be a closed
3
-manifold and
S\subset M
a connected incompressible surface with non-vanishing mean curvature. Let
\Gamma
be the limit of the level set flow of
S
.

Then,
\Gamma
contains an incompressible component
\Gamma^{\prime}
such that:

(a1)

\Gamma^{\prime}
has multiplicity
1
and is diffeomorphic to
S
, or

(a2)

\Gamma^{\prime}
has multiplicity
2
, is one-sided and the boundary of a tubular neighborhood of
\Gamma^{\prime}
is diffeomorphic to
S
.

(b)

\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{\prime}
is a (possibly empty) union of copies of
\mathrm{S}^{2}
and
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
.

Moreover, if
(a1)
holds and
\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{\prime}=\emptyset
, then there exists a strictly monotone mean-convex isotopy between
S
and
\Gamma
.

This result suits particularly well the context of negatively curved
3
-manifolds. In this case, minimal surfaces cannot be diffeomorphic to
\mathrm{S}^{2}
or
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
as a consequence of the Gauss formula and the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem. Therefore,
\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{\prime}=\emptyset
holds automatically. We show:

1.4 Theorem
.

Let
M
be a
3
-manifold with negative sectional curvature and
S\subset M
an incompressible surface with non-vanishing mean curvature. Assume that one of the following holds:

(1)

M
is closed and
S
is non-separating, or

(2)

K\subset M
is a mean-convex component of
\partial K
,
K\subset M
is compact and the components of
\partial K\setminus S\neq\emptyset
are either minimal or mean-convex.

Then, there exists a strictly monotone mean-convex isotopy between
S
and the stable minimal surface
\Gamma
.

Combining this result with min-max theory we obtain:

1.5 Theorem
.

Let
M
be a hyperbolic
3
-manifold and
S
a surface of genus at least
2
. Assume that:

(1)

M\simeq S\times\mathbb{R}
, with a quasi-Fuchsian metric or

(2)

M
fibers over
\mathrm{S}^{1}
, with fiber
S
.

Then,
M
admits a smooth foliation by surfaces on the isotopy class of the fiber, such that each leaf is either minimal or has non-vanishing mean curvature.

The construction of these foliations was one of the original motivations we had in mind when we began this work. The ends of a general quasi-Fuchsian manifold admit a unique foliation by constant mean curvature surfaces isotopic to the fiber
[
24
]
. One might wonder if this constant mean curvature foliation can be extended globally. This is known to be impossible in certain cases
[
14
]
. A weaker question is whether one can construct global foliations such that the mean curvature of each leaf does not change sign.
Theorem

1.5
answers this affirmatively.

As a byproduct, we obtain the existence of outermost minimal surfaces in quasi-Fuchsian ends. We finish this work by studying the continuity properties of these surfaces with respect to perturbations in the family of quasi-Fuchsian metrics. We summarize our findings as:

1.6 Theorem
.

A marked end of a quasi-Fuchsian manifold
(S\times\mathbb{R},h)
admits a minimal surface
\Sigma_{out}(h)
which is outermost with respect to that end. The map
h\mapsto\Sigma_{out}(h)
is continuous on an open set of total measure of the family of quasi-Fuchsian metrics. Discontinuities of the map
h\mapsto\Sigma_{out}(h)
exist and in those cases
\Sigma_{out}(h)
is degenerate stable and not locally area-minimizing. Finally, the function
h\mapsto\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_{out}(h))
is upper semi-continuous.

Although we explicitly consider the case of quasi-Fuchsian manifolds of the form
S\times\mathbb{R}
, where
S
is a closed surface of genus
g\geq 2
, our existence and continuity results concerning the outermost minimal surface are valid for more general hyperbolic 3-manifolds. For instance, these results apply to expanding ends with incompressible boundary in convex cocompact hyperbolic 3-manifolds (see
[
8
]
).

1.7 Remark
.

Theorem

1.6
shows complete hyperbolic metrics can contain degenerate stable minimal surfaces that do not look like strictly stable or unstable minimal surfaces, but rather as “saddle” type surface. In the notiation of
Definition

7.5
, these surfaces have a tubular neighborhood of “mixed type” (see also surface IV in
Figure

5
).

Examples, ideas and organization

The flow of a non-separating surface might repeat points of
M
, even when the surface has non-vanishing mean curvature. This is illustrated in
Figure

1
.

To overcome this problem, we consider a covering space
\pi:\widetilde{M}\to M
with the property that the class
[S]\subset H_{n-1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
lifts to a separating class in
\widetilde{M}
. This covering is illustrated in
Figure

2
. Its main properties can be found in
Definition

4.1
,
Lemma

4.2
,
Remark

4.3
. The main observation is that the lift of area-minimizing hypersurfaces in
[S]
produce minimal barriers restricting level set flows to compact regions of
\widetilde{M}
. This is discussed in
Lemma

4.4
. Regularity theories for compact evolutions can be applied to the region bounded by one of the lift of
S
, which is an unbounded closed set of
\widetilde{M}
. This is explained in
Proposition

4.7
. The inclusion principle then implies that the boundary of this flow on
\widetilde{M}
projects well back to
M
. This is the content of
Proposition

4.5
and
Corollary

4.6
. We emphasize that this is a general construction and up to that point there are no curvature assumptions on
S
. Finally, the proof of
Theorem

1.1
is in
Section

4.8
.

For simplicity the surfaces in the figure are disjoint, but this does not have to be the case.

A difference with the separating case is that components of
\Gamma
might occur with high multiplicity. This can happen even when the initial condition has non-vanishing mean curvature. An example with multiplicity 3 is illustrated in
Figure

3
. The same idea can be used to produce limits with arbitrarily high multiplicity. However, one can still control the multiplicity of some components in some situations, e.g.
Theorem

1.3
,
Theorem

1.4
or, if
M
is irreducible then one automatically has that
\Gamma^{\prime}
has multiplicity one if
S
is non-separating and incompressible.

Next, we study the mean curvature flow with surgery in dimension 2. This flow requires choosing parameters that control the dimensions of the surgeries and the possibility of discarding components after surgery. To prove
Theorem

1.2
, we first notice that if these parameters are fine enough, then the flow with surgery can be continued smoothly towards
\Gamma
. This is the content of
Theorem

5.11
(a similar result was proved in
[
5
]
). Then we use that the level set flow projects well (
Corollary

4.6
) in combination with a result of Lauer (
Proposition

5.10
) to conclude that surgeries also project well as long as the surgery parameters are fine enough. This is in
Theorem

5.12
. Finally, the proof of
Theorem

1.2
can be found in
Section

5.13
.

We then focus on the situation in which
S
is incompressible. A key observation in the proof of
Theorem

1.3
is that despite surgery and discarding, the flow always has exactly one incompressible component (
Corollary

6.6
). This is because, on one hand, neck surgeries correspond to compressing disks (
Definition

6.3
), and on the other, incompressible components are never discarded as a consequence of the canonical neighborhood theorem (
Corollary

5.9
).

In negative curvature, the flow of an incompressible surface only splits mean-convex
3
-balls which eventually become extinct. Haslhofer-Ketover
[
10
]
constructed monotone mean-convex isotopies from such balls to a marble tree (
Figure

4
, left). To construct the isotopy from
Theorem

1.3
we first show that, instead of performing surgeries on the incompressible component, one can use the isotopy from
[
10
]
to obtain an incompressible surface with marble trees attached to it (
Figure

4
, right). One then retracts the marble trees as in
[
10
]
, first contracting balls in loose ends, then retracting the tubes attached to them into the next balls on the three, and so on. After retracting all the trees one can continue the flow normally until hitting a new surgery time. This is how we obtain a monotone isotopy between
S
and
\Gamma
in
Theorem

1.3
. Then
Theorem

1.4
is obtained after checking that the multiplicity conditions apply in negative curvature.

A marble tree (left) and an incompressible torus with marble trees attached (right)

Next, we construct global foliations on quasi-Fuchsian manifolds and hyperbolic manifolds that fiber over the circle. The idea is that one can always partition such manifolds by a maximal set of disjoint stable minimal surfaces in the isotopy class of
S\times\{t\}
. This partition can be further refined by adding minimal surfaces of index one via a min-max construction on each region bounded by two locally area-minimizing surfaces. Finally, one can use
Theorem

1.4
to foliate the region from an unstable minimal surface towards a stable one.

Figure 5.

I and IV are outermost. IV is not locally area-mininizing (we show that this is rare, but it happens). II is constructed via a min-max of sweeoputs joining I and III.

In the last section, we study the continuity of outermost minimal surfaces in quasi-Fuchsian manifolds (I and IV in
Figure

5
) with respect to variations of the quasi-Fuchsian metric. These metrics are parametrized by the two conformal classes on
S
at the boundary at infinity, modulo diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity. This space is represented as the product of Teichmuller space
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}(S)
with itself and is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{12g-12}
, where
g
is the genus of
S
. We prove
Theorem

1.6
in several stages in
Section

8
. The main observation for the continuity is that a large set of quasi-Fuchsian metrics are what we call
bumpy along the fiber
. This means that there are no degenerate minimal surfaces in the isotopy class of
S\times\{t\}
.
In addition, we derive the existence of discontinuities by combining the analysis from
[
30
]
with the theorem of invariance of domain. As a result we conclude that there are outermost surfaces which are stable but not locally-area minimizing (see surface IV in
Figure

5
).

3.
The level set flow

Let
M
be a closed manifold and
K\subset M
a closed set. The
level set flow
starting at
\Gamma
(also called “biggest flow” in
[
16
]
) consists of the maximal one-parameter family of closed sets

t\mapsto F_{t}(K)

starting at
F_{0}(K)=K
and satisfying the avoidance principle when compared with any compact classical mean curvature flow (see item (4) below). This property is also described by saying that
F_{t}(K)
is
the maximal set-theoretic subsolution of the mean curvature flow
starting at
K
(see
[
16
, Subsections 4A and 4H]
).

The following are standard properties of the level set flow. For their proofs, we refer the reader to:
[
15
,
16
,
32
]
.

3.1.
Properties

Let
S,K,
and
K^{\prime}
compact subsets of
M
, then:

(1)

\cup_{t\geq 0}F_{t}(K)\times\{t\}
is a closed subset of
M\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

(2)

If
S=\partial K
is an embedded hypersurface then
\partial F_{t}(K)
coincides with the classical mean curvature flow of
S
up to its first singular time.

(3)

F_{t}(F_{s}(K))=F_{t+s}(K)
, for all
t,s\geq 0
.

(4)

Avoidance principle
: If
K\cap K^{\prime}=\emptyset
, then

F_{t}(K)\cap F_{t}(K^{\prime})=\emptyset\text{ and}

F_{t}(K)\cup F_{t}(K^{\prime})=F_{t}(K\cup K^{\prime}),

for all
t\geq 0
.

(5)

Inclusion principle
:
K\subset K^{\prime}\implies F_{t}(K)\subset F_{t}(K^{\prime})
, for all
t\geq 0
.

(6)

Interior inclusion principle
:
K\subset\operatorname{int}K^{\prime}\implies F_{t}(K)\subset\operatorname{int}F_{t}(K^{\prime}),
for all
t\geq 0
.

(7)

Finite speed
: Let
r_{0}>0
and assume
\operatorname{Ric}\geq-C
on
B(p,r_{0})\subset M
. There exists
\tau=\tau(C)>0
, such that

F_{t_{0}}(K)\cap B(p,r_{0})=\emptyset\implies F_{t}(K)\cap\{p\}=\emptyset,

for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau]
.

(8)

Homology classes
: If
S
is a non-separating embedded hypersurface, then
F_{t}(S)
never vanishes.

Following
[
32
]
, we say that a compact set
K\subset M
is
mean-convex
if

F_{t}(K)\subset\operatorname{int}K

for all small
t
. This is the case if
\partial K
is
strictly mean-convex
, i.e. smooth and its mean curvature vector is never zero and always points towards the interior of
K
.

A regularity and structure theory for the level set flow of mean-convex sets was developed by B. White
[
32
,
33
,
31
]
(see also
[
11
,
9
]
). The following theorem summarizes some aspects from
[
31
, Theorem 1]
and
[
32
, Theorem 11.1]
.

3.2 Theorem
.

Let
K\subset M
be a compact set such that
\partial K
is smooth and strictly mean-convex. Then

(1)

F_{s}(K)\subset\operatorname{int}F_{t}(K)
, for all
0\leq t<s
.

(2)

F_{t}(\partial K)=\partial F_{t}(K)
, for all
t\geq 0
.

(3)

Outward minimizing property:
\operatorname{Area}(\partial F_{t}(K))\leq\operatorname{Area}(\partial S)
, for all
F_{t}(K)\subset S\subset\overline{S}\subset\operatorname{int}K
, and
t>0
.

(4)

F_{t}(K)
is smooth for a.e.
t\geq 0
and
t
large enough.

(5)

The limit set
K_{\infty}=\cap_{t\geq 0}F_{t}(K)
has finitely many connected components and its boundary
\partial K_{\infty}
is a disjoint union of embedded stable minimal surfaces.

(6)

As
t\to\infty
,
F_{t}(\partial K)
converges smoothly towards
\partial K_{\infty}
with multiplicity
1
or
2
. More precisely, if
C
is a component of
K_{\infty}
, then the convergence towards
\partial C
is one-sheeted if
\operatorname{int}C\neq\emptyset
and two-sheeted if
\operatorname{int}C=\emptyset
.

4.
The level set flow in homology

In this section, we assume that
S\subset M
is an oriented embedded hypersurface that is non-separating, i.e.
M\setminus S
is connected. Our first goal is to find a canonical way of relating the evolution
F_{t}(S)
to the level set flow of a boundary. As it turns out, this is possible with the aid of a covering space
\pi:\widetilde{M}\to M
which is associated to the homology class of
S
. This covering space was previously used by Ilmanen in the proof of item (8) from Properties
3.1
, see
[
15
]
. We now construct this covering.

First, one thinks of
\overline{M}=M\setminus S
as a compact manifold with boundary. Using the orientation of
S
we divide the boundary components of
\overline{M}
into two groups
\partial\overline{M}=S^{\prime}\cup S^{\prime\prime}
. Components in
S^{\prime}
(resp.
S^{\prime\prime}
) are those with normal vector pointing towards the interior (resp. exterior) of
\overline{M}
. Notice that both
S^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime\prime}
are diffeomorphic to
S
. Next, we label as
\overline{M}_{i}
, for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, disjoint identical copies of
\overline{M}
. We form
\widetilde{M}
by gluing together these pieces. More precisely:

4.1 Definition
.

Given
S\subset M
as above, we denote by
\widetilde{M}
the connected manifold obtained from
\cup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{M}_{i}
by gluing together
S^{\prime\prime}_{i-1}
with
S^{\prime}_{i}
, for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. The projection given by the inclusion
\overline{M}_{i}\to M
is denoted as

\pi:\widetilde{M}\to M.

There is also a natural isometry given by the identity
\overline{M}_{i}\to\overline{M}_{i+1}
, which we denote as

\rho:\widetilde{M}\to\widetilde{M}.

Before initiating the discussion on the level set flow, we need to establish some basic properties of
\widetilde{M}
. The following lemma will be useful for producing canonical lifts of disconnected hypersurfaces in the class
[S]
(see
Remark

4.3
).

4.2 Lemma
.

The map
\pi:\widetilde{M}\to M
is a regular covering space and
\rho
generates its group of deck transformations. Moreover,
\widetilde{M}
depends only on
[S]\in H_{n-1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. By this we mean that repeating the construction from
Definition

4.1
using any non-separating hypersurface
\Sigma\subset[S]
yields the same
\widetilde{M}
, up to isometries.

Proof.

Every class
[S]\in H_{n-1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
has an associated
\Phi_{[S]}\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
via Poincaré duality. The homomorphism

\Phi_{[S]}:H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}

is the
algebraic intersection number
of the class
[S]
. To compute
\Phi_{[S]}([\gamma])
, for
[\gamma]\in H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, one first chooses smooth and transversal representatives
S
and
\gamma
, so that
S\cap\gamma
is a discrete set of points with signs determined by the orientations of
M
,
S
and
\gamma
. It is a classical result that adding these signs together produces an integer
\Phi_{[S]}([\gamma])
that depends only on the classes
[S]
and
[\gamma]
, but not on the representatives
S
and
\gamma
.

Since
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
is the abelianization of
\pi_{1}(M)
, this induces an homomorphism
\phi_{[S]}:\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathbb{Z}
. The subgroup
\ker\phi_{[S]}\subset\pi_{1}(M)
consists of exactly those closed loops that have intersection number zero with
[S]
, as 1-cycles.

Claim
.

\pi_{*}(\pi_{1}(\widetilde{M}))=\ker\phi_{[S]}.

Let
\widetilde{\gamma}:[0,1]\to\widetilde{M}
be a continuous path with end points on the same fiber, i.e.
\pi(\widetilde{\gamma}(0))=\pi(\widetilde{\gamma}(1))
, so that
\gamma=\pi(\widetilde{\gamma})
is a closed loop on
M
. Then, if
\widetilde{\gamma}(0)\in\overline{M}_{i_{0}}
and
\widetilde{\gamma}(0)\in\overline{M}_{i_{1}}
, the intersection number
\phi_{[S]}([\gamma])
is exactly
i_{1}-i_{0}
. For closed loops in
\widetilde{M}
, we must have
i_{0}=i_{1}
. This shows
\pi_{*}(\pi_{1}(\widetilde{M}))\subset\ker\phi_{[S]}
. For the same reason, if
\phi_{[S]}([\gamma])=0
, any lift of
\gamma
is a closed loop in
\widetilde{M}
. This shows the other contention and proves the claim.

Finally, notice that
\ker\phi_{[S]}
is a normal subgroup of
\pi_{1}(M)
, so it determines a unique regular covering
\widetilde{M}
with deck transformation group
\pi_{1}(M)/\ker\phi_{[S]}\simeq\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\ker\phi_{[S]}
depends only on
[S]
, the classification of covering spaces implies that repeating the construction above with another non-separating hypersurface in
[S]
would produce the same covering space, up to an isometry.
∎

4.3 Remark
.

When considering a lift
\widetilde{S}
of a non-separating hypersurface
S
, we have to be careful where different connected components go to if we want lifts to be homologous on
\widetilde{M}
. The construction above gives us

\pi^{-1}(S)
as the disjoint union of
S^{\prime\prime}_{i-1}=S^{\prime}_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, all diffeomorphic to
S
. From now on, we assume

\widetilde{S}:=S^{\prime}_{0}.

We also define the closed ends

\widetilde{S}^{+}:=\cup_{i\geq 0}\overline{M}_{i}

and

\widetilde{S}^{-}:=\cup_{i<0}\overline{M}_{i}.

If
\Sigma\in[S]
is non-separating, then we define
\widetilde{\Sigma}
as the lift given by the construction above when applied to
\Sigma
and shifted through multiples of
\rho
so that it intersects
M_{0}
but not
M_{i}
,
i<0
. We define the closed ends
\widetilde{\Sigma}^{+}
and
\widetilde{\Sigma}^{-}
accordingly, so that their boundary is
\widetilde{\Sigma}
.

4.4 Lemma
.

Let
\Sigma
be a hypersurface minimizing the area in
[S]
. Then,
\Sigma
is non-separating and lifts to
\widetilde{\Sigma}\subset\widetilde{M}
in the sense of
Remark

4.3
. Moreover, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
large enough,

X_{k}:=\rho^{-k}(\widetilde{\Sigma}^{+})\cap\rho^{k}(\widetilde{\Sigma}^{-})

is a smooth compact with minimal boundary and

\widetilde{S}\subset\operatorname{int}X_{k}.

Proof.

Although
\Sigma
might be disconnected, no combination of its components forms a boundary. Otherwise we would produce a competitor for the area in the same homology class after deleting those components. Therefore,
M\setminus\Sigma
is connected, i.e.
\Sigma
is non-separating. The rest of the statement follows from directly from
Remark

4.3
and
Definition

4.1
.
∎

In
[

5.
Mean-convex mean curvature flow with surgery

We begin covering fundamental aspects of the mean-convex mean curvature flow with surgery for surfaces due to Haslhofer-Kleiner
[
12
]
(another version of this flow was developed around the same time by Brendle-Huisken in
[
4
,
5
]
). Our presentation follows the one in
[
10
]
which is made in the context of Riemannian manifolds (see also
[
23
]
).

The existence theory of
[
12
,
10
]
is formulated using a class of flows called
(\alpha,\delta,\mathbb{H})
-flows. We know explain these parameters. Roughly speaking,
\alpha
is determined by the extrinsic geometry of the initial domain. The other two parameters can be chosen. The number
\delta
denotes the fineness/regularity of the surgery necks. The triple
\mathbb{H}=(H_{trig},H_{neck},H_{th})
, with
H_{trig}>H_{neck}>H_{th}>1
, specifies when and where surgeries are performed. More precisely, when the maximum of the mean curvature reaches
H_{trig}
, surgeries are performed on a disjoint collection of necks with curvature
H_{neck}
. These necks separate the
trigger part

\{H=H_{trig}\}
from the
thick part

\{H=H_{th}\}
. After surgeries are performed components containing the trigger part are discarded. The flow is then able to continue until the maximum of the mean curvature reaches
H_{trig}
once more.

We present the definitions necessary to describe the surgeries performed on the flow with surgery of
[
10
]
.

5.1 Definition
.

(see Definition 7.2,
[
10
]
)
Let
U
be an open region of
M
. We say that a smooth family of smooth domains moving my mean curvature flow

has a strong
\delta
-neck with center
p

at time

, if

and the following condition holds:

•

\{s^{-1}\cdot\exp_{p}^{-1}(K_{t_{0}+s^{2}t}-p)\cap B_{2s/\delta}(p)\cap U\}_{t\in(-1,t_{0}]}
is
\delta
-close in the
C^{[1/\delta]}(V)
-norm to the evolution of a solid round cylinder

whose radius is equal to
1
at

,

where
V=B_{1/\delta}\cap\big[s^{-1}\cdot\exp_{p}^{-1}(B_{2s/\delta}\cap U)\big]\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

5.2 Definition
.

(see Definition 2.8,
[
6
]
)

(1)

(2)

5.3 Definition
.

(see Definition 2.4
[

and Definition 6.2
[
6

Let

be the final slice of a smooth flow having a strong

\delta^{\prime}(\delta)

, for all

. The existence of this sequence follows from the combination of

(see formula for spectrum at the end of the proof of

and

respectively.

For the definition of

and

[

. We can form a canonical sweepout in the following way. First, by

, applied to

in the metric

to a stable minimal surface

with

\Gamma_{1}

and
\Gamma_{1}

-mean-convex and have area strictly less than

S\times\{t\}

\max\{\operatorname{Area}(\Gamma_{0},g_{n}),\operatorname{Area}(\Gamma_{1},g_{n})\}<\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_{n},g_{n})\leq\max_{t\in[\delta,1-\delta]}\operatorname{Area}(S_{t},g_{n}).

The topology of

[
18

. In particular, there is a sequence of surfaces obtained by performing neck-surgeries and discarding components on leaves of the sweepouts in the family. Each component of the sequence converges to a component of
\Sigma_{n}
as a one-sheeted graph if the component of

is orientable, or as a two-sheeted graph otherwise. It is a classical result that there are no non-orientable closed surfaces embedded in

(see
[
3

.

Moreover,
\Sigma_{n}\cap S\times[\delta,1-\delta]\neq\emptyset
. Otherwise,

and

, for

or

Finally, by Ben Sharp’s compactness theorem

,

Let

∎

8.
Outermost minimal surfaces in quasi-fuchsian manifolds

Let
S
be a closed orientable surface of genus
g\geq 2
. There exists a set of hyperbolic metrics on
S\times\mathbb{R}
, called
quasi-Fuchsian metrics
, that form the setting of a rich deformation theory. In
Theorem

1.5
we showed that for such metrics, each end of
S\times\mathbb{R}
admits a mean-convex foliation ending at an outermost minimal surface in the isotopy class of
S\times\{t\}
.

Our goal in this section, is to study the continuity of this surface with respect of deformations of the metric. We begin by summarizing some properties of the space of quasi-Fuchsian metrics which are necessary for our results. Detailed references are presented accordingly.

8.1.
Properties of the space of quasi-Fuchsian metrics

From now on, we denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of quasi-Fuchsian metrics on
S\times\mathbb{R}
, where
S
is as in the beginning of the section. The following are well-known facts:

(1)

\mathcal{M}
admits a topology which makes it homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{6g-6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6g-6}
.

(2)

If
\{h_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}
converges to
h\in\mathcal{M}
on this topology, then we can choose a large smooth compact set
C\subset S\times\mathbb{R}
, such that:

(a)

C
is diffeomorphic to
S\times[-1,1]
.

(b)

(C,h_{i})\to(C,h)
in the smooth topology.

(c)

(\partial C,h)
is strictly mean-convex.

(d)

Every closed minimal surfaces of
(S\times\mathbb{R},h_{i})

and
(S\times\mathbb{R},h)
is contained in
(C,h_{i})
and
(C,h)
, respectively.

(3)

For every
h\in\mathcal{M}
,
(S\times\mathbb{R},h)
contains at least one locally area-minimizing embedded surface in the isotopy class of
S\times\{t\}
.

(4)

A metric
h
in the diagonal of
\mathcal{M}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{6g-6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6g-6}
, is called
Fuchsian
. If
h
is Fuchsian, then
(S\times\mathbb{R},h)
contains exactly one closed minimal surface.

(5)

If
h
and
h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{6g-6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6g-6}
are images of each other under the symmetry along the diagonal of
\mathbb{R}^{6g-6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6g-6}
, then there exists an isometry
(S\times\mathbb{R},h)\to(S\times\mathbb{R},h^{\prime})
isotopic to the map
(p,t)\mapsto(p,-t)
.

Proof.

(1) is the well-known Bers’ simultaneous uniformization theorem, that parametrizes the set of quasi-Fuchsian metrics by the conformal structures of the surfaces at the infinity of each end. The space of such conformal structures is the Teichmuller space which is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{6g-6}
(see
[
30
]
). There are several notions of convergence for hyperbolic spaces all of which are equivalent in the case of 3-dimensional quasi-Fuchsian metrics. The type of limits described (2)(b) are called
geometric limits
(see
[
25
, Section 2.2]
). Each quasi-Fuchsian manifold has a compact convex core, which is a compact set containing all closed minimal surfaces (see
[
1
, Corollary 5.6]
). The convex cores converge geometrically (see
[
22
, Corollary 7.34]
and the discussion right before the corollary). Moreover, from
[
24
]
, we can pick any large smooth compact set cylinder
C
containing the convex core and strictly mean-convex boundary. Together, these show (2)(a),(2)(c) and (2)(d). For discussions about (3) and (4) we refer the reader to
[
30
]
. Finally, (5) comes from the uniqueness and symmetry of Bers’ simultaneous uniformization.
∎

The following proposition follows directly from the work of Taubes
[
29
]
. In fact, we only need the result for stable minimal surfaces in the fiber. That case is also easily derived from the earlier work of Uhlenbeck
[
30
]
.

8.2 Proposition
.

There exists an open set
\mathcal{M}^{*}\subset\mathcal{M}
, having total measure on
\mathcal{M}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{6g-6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6g-6}
, such that every
h\in\mathcal{M}^{*}
is
bumpy along the fiber
, i.e.,
(S\times\mathbb{R},h)
contains no degenerate minimal surfaces in the isotopy class of
S\times\{t\}
.

There are quasi-Fuchsian metrics admitting many minimal surfaces:

8.3 Theorem
.

[
14
, Theorem 1.5 and Section 5.2]

For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
h\in\mathcal{M}
such that
(S\times\mathbb{R},h)
contains at least
N
disjoint locally area minimizing minimal surfaces in the isotopy class of
S\times\{t\}
.

Given
h\in\mathcal{M}
, we denote by
\Sigma_{out}(h)
the outermost minimal surface obtained from applying
Theorem

1.4
to a mean-convex slice deep in the end
\big(S\times(0,+\infty),h\big)
, i.e. the positive end. Let
I(h)
and
II(h)
be the metric and second fundamental form of
\Sigma_{out}(h)
with respect to
(S\times\mathbb{R},h)
, respectively. We notice that in our context all the surfaces in the isotopy class of
S\times\{t\}
have a defined
marking
associated to the quasi-Fuchsian space
\mathcal{M}
. Using this marking we denote by
\mu(h)
the unique hyperbolic metric in the conformal class of
I(h)
and
u(h)
the smooth function such that
I(h)=e^{2u(h)}\mu(h)
. Equivalently, we can think of
\mu(h)
as a class of complex structures on
S
modulo diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity. Since
\Sigma_{out}(h)
is minimal in
(S\times\mathbb{R},h)
, following
[
30
, Section 4]
, we can represent the second fundamental form
II(h)
as a holomorphic quadratic differential
\alpha(h)
on
(S,\mu(h))
. Moreover, it is shown in
[
30
, Section 4]
that
u(h)=u(\mu(h),\alpha(h))
.

In what follows, we study the continuity properties of the map

h\mapsto(\mu(h),\alpha(h)).

8.4 Remark
.

The space of parameters
(\mu,\alpha)
, consisting of
\mu
, a hyperbolic metric on
S
, and
\alpha
, a holomorphic quadratic differential, is identified with the cotangent bundle of Teichmuller space
T^{*}\mathcal{T}
. This is a
12g-12
dimensional smooth real manifold. In
[
30
]
, Uhlenbeck constructs a bijective map from a neighborhood of the zero section
(\mu,0)
to a set of quasi-Fuchsian metrics known as Almost-Fuchsian metrics. The map

h\mapsto(\mu(h),\alpha(h)),

which we study, extends the inverse of Uhlenbeck’s bijection.

8.5 Proposition
.

The map
h\mapsto(\mu(h),\alpha(h))
is injective on
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

First notice that
\Sigma_{out}(h)
is not only incompressible, but has the same fundamental group of
S\times\mathbb{R}
. Then, the representation map constructed in
[
30
, Theorem 5.5]
recovers the quasi-Fuchsian metric
h
from
(\mu(h),\alpha(h))
. This shows the map
h\mapsto(\mu(h),\alpha(h))
has an inverse from its image.
∎

8.6 Proposition
.

If
\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})
is locally area-minimizing in
(S\times\mathbb{R},h_{0})
, then the map
h\mapsto(\mu(h),\alpha(h))
is continuous at
h_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
. In particular, it is continuous on
\mathcal{M}^{*}
.

Proof.

First, it is well known that if
\Sigma
is a closed minimal surface of genus
g
in a hyperbolic
3
-manifold, then

\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma)\leq 4\pi(g-1).

Together, with the curvature bounds for stable minimal surfaces from
[
27
]
, this implies that a sequence of embedded stable minimal surfaces in the isotopy class of
S\times\{t\}
converges to another stable minimal surface. We notice this is true even when the ambient metrics are also varying but converging smoothly to a limit metric. In addition, since there are no one-sided closed embedded surfaces in
S\times\mathbb{R}
, it follows that the convergence is graphical, therefore the limit belongs to the same isotopy class.

Let
\{h_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of metrics converging to
h_{0}
. Since
\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})
is locally area-minimizing, and in view of theorem
Theorem

7.2
, it has a contracting tubular neighborhood in the sense of
Definition

7.5
, which, by abuse of notation, we denote as
\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
. Therefore, for
i
large enough
(\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon),h_{i})
is a strictly mean-convex cylinder. In particular, it contains a locally area-minimizing surface
\Gamma_{i}
in the isotopy class of
S\times\{t\}
. By our previous discussion,
\Gamma_{i}
converges to
\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})
, which is the only closed minimal surface contained in
(\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon),h_{0})
. Since
\Sigma_{out}(h_{i})
is the outermost surface, this implies that it is deeper inside of the end, when compared to
\Gamma_{i}
. In particular,
\Sigma_{out}(h_{i})
must converge to a stable minimal surface which is deeper inside of then when compared to
\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})
. Since
\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})
is the only such surface, it follows that
\Sigma_{out}(h_{i})\to\Sigma(h_{0})
smoothly. This proves the proposition.
∎

8.7 Proposition
.

If
\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})
is degenerate stable with respect to
(S\times\mathbb{R},h_{0})
, then
h_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
is in the closure of the singular set of the map
h\mapsto(\mu(h),\alpha(h))
.

Proof.

Assume that the proposition does not hold. Then, the restriction of
h\mapsto(\mu(h),\alpha(h))
to a ball around
h_{0}
is an injective, continuous function from an open ball of
\mathbb{R}^{12g-12}
to the
(12g-12)
-dimensional smooth manifold of parameters
(\mu,\alpha)
. By the theorem of invariance of domain, the map must be an homeomorphism, and therefore open. However, as described by Uhlenbeck in
[
30
, Diagram 4.3, Theorem 4.4]
, there are no stable minimal surfaces in any hyperbolic 3-manifold with data
(\mu(h_{0}),(1+\varepsilon)\alpha(h_{0}))
, for any
\varepsilon>0
. This contradicts the openness of the map.
∎

The following is a standard continuity argument.

8.8 Corollary
.

There exists
h_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})
is degenerate stable.

Proof.

Given
h\in\mathcal{M}
, denote by
N(h)
the maximum number of disjoint locally area-minimizing surfaces in the isotopy class of
S\times\{t\}
, contained in
(S\times\mathbb{R},h)
. From
Theorem

7.2
and
Theorem

1.4
we know

1\leq N(h)<+\infty.

Now consider a smooth curve
t\mapsto h_{t}\in\mathcal{M}
, for
0\leq t\leq 1
starting at a Fuchsian metric
h_{0}
and ending at a quasi-Fuchsian metric
h_{1}
given by
Theorem

8.3
. Therefore
N(h_{0})=1
and
N(h_{1})>1
.

Let

\tau=\sup\{t\in[0,1]:N(h_{s})=1,\forall s\leq t\}.

First, we show that
N(h_{\tau})=1
. Otherwise
N(h_{\tau})>1
, so there are disjoint locally area-minimizing surfaces in
(S\times\mathbb{R},h_{\tau})
. We can select disjoint tubular neighborhoods for the surfaces that are contracting. For metrics near
h_{\tau}
, these neighborhoods are still cylinders
S\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
with mean convex boundary. Therefore, they each contain at least one locally area-minimizing surface. This contradicts the fact that
N(h_{\tau-\varepsilon})=1
, for
\varepsilon>0
small enough.

Therefore,
\Sigma_{out}(h_{\tau})
is the only closed locally-area minimizing minimal surface of
(S\times\mathbb{R},h_{\tau})
. If
\Sigma_{out}(h_{\tau})
is degenerate then we are done. If not, then it would be strictly stable and by the Inverse function theorem there is a fixed tubular neighborhood of
\Sigma_{out}(h_{\tau})
which is contracting for all metrics close to
h_{\tau}
. By definition there is a sequence
\varepsilon_{i}>0
, such that
(S\times\mathbb{R},h_{\tau+\varepsilon_{i}})
contains at least two stable minimal surfaces. Both surfaces cannot converge towards
\Sigma_{out}(h_{\tau})
as
n\to\infty
. Otherwise, they would eventually be contained in the contracting tubular neighborhood. Which is a contradiction. Since
N(h_{\tau})=1
, one of the sequence converges to a degenerate stable which is not locally-areaminimizing.
∎

Combining
Proposition

8.6
and
Corollary

8.8
we obtain

8.9 Corollary
.

The singular set of the map
h\mapsto(\mu(h),\alpha(h))
is not empty. In particular, there are infinitely many
h_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
, such that
\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})
is degenerate stable with a tubular neighborhood of mixed type.

Finally, we study the continuity of the area of the outermost.

8.10 Proposition
.

The map
h\mapsto\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_{out}(h))
is upper semicontinuous.

Proof.

If
\Sigma_{out}
is continuous at
h_{0}
there is nothing to prove. Assume that
h_{0}
is a point of discontinuity for the outermost and let
\{h_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of quasi-Fuchsian metrics converging to
h_{0}
. Since
\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})
has a mixed tubular neighborhood, we can find another minimal surface below it using the level set flow (e.g. in
Figure

5
we would find III running the flow from IV). Notice that the area of this surface is strictly less than
\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})
. Since there are only finitely many stable surfaces, we can repeat this process until we reach a locally area-minimizing surface
\Sigma
. For
i
large enough, the metrics
h_{i}
all contain a locally area minimizing surface near
\Sigma
. In particular, the outermost
\Sigma_{out}(h_{i})
has to be deeper in the end. Therefore, as
i\to\infty
,
\Sigma_{out}(h_{i})
must converge to a surface deeper in the end compared to
\Sigma
. By maximum principle it must be one of the surfaces we just constructed by running the flow from
\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})
. It follows that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_{out}(h_{i}))\leq\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma_{out}(h_{0})).

∎

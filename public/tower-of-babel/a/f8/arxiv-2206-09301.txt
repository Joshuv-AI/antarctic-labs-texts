Low-temperature asymptotics for transverse autocorrelator of the magnetically polarized Ising chain studied by ordinary and nested Dyson equations
P. N. Bibikov

Abstract
Suggesting two versions for the Plakida-Tserkovnikov algorithm breakdown in the low-temperature regime, we derive ordinary and nested Dyson equations for the transverse autocorrelator of the magnetically polarized Ising chain. Using them we get the corresponding low-temperature asymptotics for the autocorrelator. We show that the ordinary Dyson equation results in a correct o ⁡ ( e − β ​ E g ​ a ​ p ) o({\rm e}^{-\beta E_{gap}}) account of the magnon creation process, while the nested Dyson equation additionally gives the correct o ⁡ ( e − β ​ E g ​ a ​ p ) o({\rm e}^{-\beta E_{gap}}) contribution associated with transitions from magnons to bound two-magnon states. The obtained result may be useful for the extension of the suggested approach on the polarized X ​ X ​ Z XXZ -chain.

\langle\langle{\cal A},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\equiv\frac{1}{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt{\rm e}^{izt}\langle[{\cal A}(t),{\cal B}]\rangle=\frac{1}{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt{\rm e}^{izt}\langle[{\cal A},{\cal B}(-t)]\rangle,\qquad z\equiv\omega+i\epsilon.

(2)

As usual

{\cal A}(t)\equiv{\rm e}^{i\hat{H}t}{\cal A}{\rm e}^{-i\hat{H}t},\qquad\langle{\cal A}\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{Z(T)}{\rm tr}\Big({\rm e}^{-\beta\hat{H}}{\cal A}\Big),\qquad Z(T)={\rm tr}\Big({\rm e}^{-\beta\hat{H}}\Big),

(3)

where

is the corresponding Hamiltonian. From (2) readily follows

\displaystyle z\langle\langle{\cal A},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\langle[{\cal A},{\cal B}]\rangle+\langle\langle{\cal A},[\hat{H},{\cal B}]\rangle\rangle_{\omega},

(4)

\displaystyle z\langle\langle{\cal A},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\langle[{\cal A},{\cal B}]\rangle+\langle\langle[{\cal A},\hat{H}],{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.

(5)

Besides
\langle\langle{\cal A},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}
possesses the spectral representation

\langle\langle{\cal A},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\frac{1}{Z(T,N)}\sum_{\mu,\nu}\frac{{\rm e}^{-\beta E_{\nu}}-{\rm e}^{-\beta E_{\mu}}}{z+E_{\nu}-E_{\mu}}\langle\nu|{\cal A}|\mu\rangle\langle\mu|{\cal B}|\nu\rangle,

According to the well known relation
{\rm Im}(x+i\epsilon)^{-1}=-\pi\delta(x)

I(\omega,T)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{Z(T,N)}\sum_{\mu,\nu}{\rm e}^{-\beta E_{\nu}}|\langle\nu|O|\mu\rangle|^{2}\delta(\omega+E_{\nu}-E_{\mu}).

(8)

The so called
XXZ

\hat{H}^{(XXZ)}=-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Big[\frac{J_{\bot}}{2}\Big({\bf S}^{+}_{n}{\bf S}^{-}_{n+1}+{\bf S}^{-}_{n}{\bf S}^{+}_{n+1}\Big)+J_{z}\Big({\bf S}^{z}_{n}{\bf S}^{z}_{n+1}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)+h\Big({\bf S}_{n}^{z}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\Big],

[
5
]
.
Here,
{\bf S}_{n}^{z}
and
{\bf S}_{n}^{\pm}={\bf S}_{n}^{x}\pm i{\bf S}_{n}^{y}

n

[{\bf S}_{m}^{z},{\bf S}_{n}^{\pm}]=\pm\delta_{mn}{\bf S}_{n}^{\pm},\qquad[{\bf S}_{m}^{+},{\bf S}_{n}^{-}]=2\delta_{mn}{\bf S}_{n}^{z},

(10)

and without a loss of generality we postulate

h>0.

(11)

Hamiltonian (9) acts in the tensor product of
N

{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
spaces attached to the sites of the chain

{\cal H}={\mathbb{C}}^{2}\otimes\dots\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}

Under the condition (11) supplemented by

h+J_{z}-|J_{\bot}|>0,

(12)

|\emptyset\rangle=|\uparrow\rangle\otimes\dots\otimes|\uparrow\rangle,

(13)

|\uparrow\rangle

{\bf S}^{z}|\uparrow\rangle=\frac{1}{2}|\uparrow\rangle,\qquad{\bf S}^{z}|\downarrow\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}|\downarrow\rangle.

(14)

Moreover under (11) and (12) the spectrum of (9) is gapped and the lowest-energy excitations form the magnon branch
[
6
]

|k\rangle={\bf S}^{-}(-k)|\emptyset\rangle,\qquad\hat{H}|\emptyset\rangle=E_{magn}(k)|\emptyset\rangle.

(15)

parameterized by crystal momentum
k

{\bf S}(q)\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\rm e}^{-iqn}{\bf S}_{n},

(16)

and

E_{magn}(k)=h+J_{z}-J_{\bot}\cos{k},

(17)

so (12) takes the following form

E_{gap}=h+J_{z}-|J_{\bot}|>0.

(18)

.

One of the most important dynamical characteristics of the
XXZ

S(\omega,q,T)
which corresponds to

O={\bf S}^{+}(q)

O^{\dagger}={\bf S}^{-}(-q)

{\bf S}^{-}(-q)

|\emptyset\rangle\xrightarrow{{\bf S}^{-}(-q)}|q\rangle.

(20)

At

k_{0}

|k_{0}\rangle\xrightarrow{{\bf S}^{-}(-q)}|k_{1},k_{2},scatt\rangle.

Due to the conservation of total crystal momentum, one should have in (21)

k_{1}+k_{2}=k_{0}+q.

(22)

According to (22) the delta-function in (8) will give

\omega=E_{magn}(k_{1})+E_{magn}(k_{2})-E_{magn}(k_{1}+k_{2}-q).

(23)

But for a given

From the presented example, follows an instructive idea that the low-temperatures broadening may be well elaborated

[

The DSF corresponding to (27) is equal to the transverse autocorrelator obtained long ago

\langle\langle{\bf S}^{+}(q),{\bf S}^{-}(-q)\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\langle\langle{\bf S}_{n}^{+},{\bf S}_{n}^{-}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.

Formula (28) immediately follows from the equality

The latter, in its turn, may be readily obtained from the definition (2), (3) under a substitution of (27).
As it has been shown in

and

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(T)\equiv\langle{\bf S}^{z}_{n}\rangle,\qquad\sigma_{2}(T)\equiv\langle{\bf S}^{z}_{n}{\bf S}^{z}_{n+1}\rangle,\qquad\sigma_{3}(T)\equiv\langle{\bf S}^{z}_{n-1}{\bf S}^{z}_{n}{\bf S}^{z}_{n+1}\rangle,

Since as in

A_{j}(T)=\langle F_{n}^{(j)}\rangle,\qquad j=+,0,-,

where

F^{(\pm)}_{n}=\frac{1}{2}{\bf S}_{n}^{z}\pm{\bf V}_{n}^{z}+2{\bf W}_{n}^{z},\qquad F_{n}^{(0)}={\bf S}_{n}^{z}-4{\bf W}_{n}^{z},

\displaystyle F^{(+)}_{n}=2(1-Q_{n-1}){\bf S}^{z}_{n}(1-Q_{n+1}),

\displaystyle F^{(0)}_{n}=2{\bf S}^{z}_{n}(Q_{n-1}+Q_{n+1}-2Q_{n-1}Q_{n+1}),

\displaystyle F^{(-)}_{n}=2Q_{n-1}{\bf S}^{z}_{n}Q_{n+1}.

(35)

Here

{\bf V}^{j}_{n}\equiv({\bf S}_{n-1}^{z}+{\bf S}_{n+1}^{z}){\bf S}_{n}^{j},\qquad{\bf W}^{j}_{n}\equiv{\bf S}_{n-1}^{z}{\bf S}_{n+1}^{z}{\bf S}_{n}^{j},\qquad j=+,-,z,

and for

Q_{n}\equiv\frac{1}{2}-{\bf S}^{z}_{n},

(37)

one has

Q_{m}|\emptyset\rangle=0,\qquad[Q_{m},{\bf S}^{-}_{n}]=\delta_{mn}{\bf S}^{-}_{n}.

(38)

The spectrum of the Ising model is well known
[

and the actual low-energy states will be the following

\displaystyle|m\rangle={\bf S}^{-}_{m}|\emptyset\rangle,\qquad\hat{H}|m\rangle=E_{1}|m\rangle

\displaystyle|m_{1},m_{2};isol\rangle={\bf S}^{-}_{m_{1}}{\bf S}^{-}_{m_{2}}|\emptyset\rangle,\qquad m_{2}-m_{1}>1,

\displaystyle\hat{H}|m_{1},m_{2};isol\rangle=E_{2,isol}|m_{1},m_{2};isol\rangle,

\displaystyle|m,m+1;string\rangle={\bf S}^{-}_{m}{\bf S}^{-}_{m+1}|\emptyset\rangle,

\displaystyle\hat{H}|m,m+1;string\rangle=E_{2,string}|m,m+1;string\rangle

\displaystyle|m,m+1,m+2;string\rangle={\bf S}^{-}_{m}{\bf S}^{-}_{m+1}{\bf S}^{-}_{m+2}|\emptyset\rangle,

\displaystyle\hat{H}|m,m+1,m+2;string\rangle=E_{3,string}|m,m+1,m+2;string\rangle.

Comparing (39) with the corresponding excitations of the

|m\rangle
are the Ising limits of one-magnon states,

The corresponding energies

E_{1}=E_{gap}=h+J_{z},\qquad E_{M,isol}=M(h+J_{z})=ME_{1},\qquad E_{M,string}=Mh+J_{z},

E_{gap}=E_{1}
(which in fact is the reduced form of (18) related to

follows from (40) and (12).

According to (14), (38) and (35), one has

\displaystyle F^{(+)}_{n}|\emptyset\rangle=|\emptyset\rangle,\qquad F^{(+)}_{n}|m\rangle=(1-\delta_{m-1,n}-\delta_{m+1,n}-2\delta_{mn})|m\rangle

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(0)}|\emptyset\rangle,\qquad F_{n}^{(0)}|m\rangle=(\delta_{m-1,n}+\delta_{m+1,n})|m\rangle,

\displaystyle F_{n}^{(-)}|\emptyset\rangle=F_{n}^{(-)}|m\rangle=0,\qquad F_{n}^{(-)}|m_{1},m_{2},isol\rangle=\delta_{m_{1},n-1}\delta_{m_{2},n+1}|m_{1},m_{2},isol\rangle.

(41)

So with the use of the notation

\zeta\equiv{\rm e}^{-\beta E_{gap}}={\rm e}^{-\beta(h+J_{z})},

(42)

(33) and (41) yield

\displaystyle A_{+}(T)=\frac{1+(N-4)\zeta+o(\zeta)}{1+N\zeta+o(\zeta)}=1-4\zeta+o(\zeta),

\displaystyle A_{0}(T)=\frac{2\zeta+o(\zeta)}{1+o(1)}=2\zeta+o(\zeta),\qquad A_{-}(T)=\frac{\zeta^{2}+o(\zeta^{2})}{1+o(1)}=\zeta^{2}+o(\zeta^{2}).

(43)

According to (43), the
\omega_{+}
-peak has a zero activation energy, while the activation energies of
\omega_{0}
- and
\omega_{-}
-peaks
are correspondingly
E_{gap}
and

.

A physical interpretation of the expansions (43) becomes elementary under an account of the following relations

\omega_{+}=E_{1}-0=E_{2,isol}-E_{1},\qquad\omega_{0}=E_{2,string}-E_{1}\qquad\omega_{-}=E_{3,string}-E_{2,isol}.

(44)

As it follows from (44), in the few-particle level the
\omega_{+}
-peak originates from the processes (20) and (21). The

\omega_{0}
-peak originates from (26). At last, the
\omega_{-}
-peak originates from the new process

|m,m+2;isol\rangle\xrightarrow{{\bf S}^{-}(-q)}|m,m+1,m+2;string\rangle.

(45)

In principle, formulas (29)-(32) may be reproduced by the Plakida-Tserkovnikov approach because in this special case,
the corresponding continued fraction is finite. This result, however, is useless for generalization on the

XXZ
chain, whose DSF should have an infinite continued fraction representation.
That is why in the present paper, working within the Plakida-Tserkovnikov approach, we try to reproduce the formula (29) and the
low-temperature asymptotics (43)
up to the order

o(\zeta)
by breaking the continued fraction. This is done by cluster expansion
of the corresponding high-level Green functions (which naturally appear in the Plakida-Tserkovnikov algorithm) according to
the spectral formula (7).

As a result, we show by direct calculation that the correct
o(\zeta)
approximation for the
A_{+}(T)
term in (29)
(which agrees both with (43) and (30)) may be reproduced under the breakdown of the Plakida-Tserkovnikov algorithm after its first cycle.
The corresponding reduction of the continued fraction has the usual form (25) associated with the
ordinary
Dyson equation. The
A_{0}(T)
term is also reproduced within this approach; however, its denominator does not agree with (29).
The
A_{-}(T)
term cannot be obtained within the approach based on the ordinary Dyson equation.
A more complicated reduction is based on the breakdown of the Plakida-Tserkovnikov algorithm after its second cycle.
It yields the nested Dyson equation and gives correct
o(\zeta)
expansions (which agree both with (43) and (30)) both for

A_{+}(T)
and
A_{0}(T)
terms in (29).

2
Derivation of the ordinary Dyson equation

2.1
Extraction of the first order irreducible parts

Let

\theta_{0}(T)\equiv\frac{\langle[[O_{0},\hat{H}],O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle}{\langle[O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle},\qquad O_{0}\equiv O,\qquad O_{0}^{\dagger}\equiv O^{\dagger}.

(46)

Since for an arbitrary operator
{\cal A}
it will be
{\rm Tr}({\rm e}^{-\beta\hat{H}}[{\cal A},\hat{H}])=0
, we have

\theta_{0}(T)-\bar{\theta}_{0}(T)=\frac{\langle[[O_{0},\hat{H}],O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle-\langle[[O_{0}^{\dagger},\hat{H}],O_{0}]\rangle}{\langle[O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle}=\frac{\langle[[O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}],\hat{H}]\rangle}{\langle[O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle}=0.

(47)

According to (46), a two (temperature-dependent) adjoint operators

O_{1}(T)\equiv[O_{0},\hat{H}]-\theta_{0}(T)O_{0},\qquad O_{1}^{\dagger}(T)=[\hat{H},O_{0}^{\dagger}]-\theta_{0}(T)O_{0}^{\dagger},

(48)

which will be called the
first order irreducible parts
of
O_{0}
and
O_{0}^{\dagger}
,
satisfy the
first order irreducibility conditions

\langle[O_{1}(T),O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle=0,\qquad\langle[O_{0},O_{1}^{\dagger}(T)]\rangle=0.

(49)

Substitutions of
{\cal B}=O_{0}^{\dagger}
into (4) and
{\cal A}=O_{0}
into (5) under an account of (46)-(48) yield

\displaystyle(z-\theta_{0}(T))\langle\langle{\cal A},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\langle[{\cal A},O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle+\langle\langle{\cal A},O_{1}^{\dagger}(T)\rangle\rangle_{\omega},

(50)

\displaystyle(z-\theta_{0}(T))\langle\langle O_{0},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\langle[O_{0},{\cal B}]\rangle+\langle\langle O_{1}(T),{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.

(51)

Taking now
{\cal A}=O_{0}
in (50) and
{\cal B}=O_{0}^{\dagger}
in (51), one readily gets

\displaystyle(z-\theta_{0}(T))\langle\langle O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\langle[O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle+\langle\langle O_{0},O_{1}^{\dagger}(T)\rangle\rangle_{\omega},

(52)

\displaystyle(z-\theta_{0}(T))\langle\langle O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\langle[O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle+\langle\langle O_{1}(T),O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.

(53)

At the same time, according to (49), the substitution of
{\cal A}=O_{1}(T)
into (50) gives

(z-\theta_{0}(T))\langle\langle O_{1}(T),O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\langle\langle O_{1}(T),O_{1}^{\dagger}(T)\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.

(54)

2.2
Introduction of the first level irreducible Green function

At this stage, we should express
\langle\langle O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}
from
\langle\langle O_{1}(T),O^{\dagger}_{1}(T)\rangle\rangle_{\omega}^{(1)}
,
where for two arbitrary operators
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
, the
first level irreducible Green function
is defined as

\langle\langle{\cal A},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}^{(1)}\equiv\langle\langle{\cal A},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}-\frac{\langle\langle{\cal A},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\langle\langle O_{0},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}}{\langle\langle O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}}.

(55)

From definition (55) immediately follows the
first level irreducibility condition

\langle\langle{\cal A}+\xi_{0}O_{0},{\cal B}+\eta_{0}O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}^{(1)}=\langle\langle{\cal A},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}^{(1)},\qquad\xi_{0},\,\eta_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}},

(56)

which, in its turn, gives

\langle\langle{\cal A},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}^{(1)}=\langle\langle O_{0},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}^{(1)}=0.

(57)

According to (54)

\langle\langle O_{1}(T),O_{1}^{\dagger}(T)\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\cdot\langle\langle O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\langle\langle O_{1}(T),O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}(z-\theta_{0}(T))\cdot\langle\langle O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.

(58)

The substitution of the right side of (52) into the right side of (58) yields

\langle\langle O_{1}(T),O_{1}^{\dagger}(T)\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\langle\langle O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\langle\langle O_{1}(T),O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\Big(\langle[O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle+\langle\langle O_{0},O_{1}^{\dagger}(T)\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\Big),

(59)

or equivalently

\langle[O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle\langle\langle O_{1}(T),O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\langle\langle O_{1}(T),O_{1}^{\dagger}(T)\rangle\rangle_{\omega}^{(1)}\langle\langle O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.

(60)

Extracting now
\langle\langle O_{1}(T),O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}
in the left side of (60) from (53), one readily gets the ordinary Dyson equation

\langle[O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle\Big((z-\theta_{0}(T))\langle\langle O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}-\langle[O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle\Big)=\langle\langle O_{1}(T),O_{1}^{\dagger}(T)\rangle\rangle_{\omega}^{(1)}\langle\langle O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega},

(61)

or equivalently

\langle\langle O,O^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\equiv\langle\langle O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\cfrac{\langle[O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle}{z-\theta_{0}(T)-\cfrac{\langle\langle O_{1}(T),O_{1}^{\dagger}(T)\rangle\rangle_{\omega}^{(1)}}{\langle[O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle}}\,.

(62)

A correspondence between (62) and the more usual form (25) was studied in
[
7
]
.

3
Derivation of the nested Dyson equation

3.1
Equations on the first level irreducible Green functions

Let
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
be
first order irreducible
operators so that

\langle[{\cal A},O_{0}^{\dagger}]\rangle=\langle[O_{0},{\cal B}]\rangle=0.

(63)

The substitution of (63) into (4) and (5) yields

\displaystyle z\langle\langle O_{0},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\langle\langle O_{0},[\hat{H},{\cal B}]\rangle\rangle_{\omega},

(64)

\displaystyle z\langle\langle{\cal A},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}=\langle\langle[{\cal A},\hat{H}],O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}.

(65)

Representing now
z\langle\langle{\cal A},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle^{(1)}_{\omega}
in two equivalent forms

\displaystyle z\langle\langle{\cal A},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle^{(1)}_{\omega}=z\langle\langle{\cal A},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}-\frac{z\langle\langle{\cal A},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}\cdot\langle\langle O_{0},{\cal B}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}}{\langle\langle O_{0},O_{0}^{\dagger}\rangle\rangle_{\omega}},

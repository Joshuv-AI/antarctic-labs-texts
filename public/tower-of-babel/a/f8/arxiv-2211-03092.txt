Hilbert’s energy-momentum tensor extended
Yakov Itin

Abstract
A variational derivative of a Lagrangian with regard to the metric tensor is used in classical field models to define Hilbert’s energy-momentum tensor for a matter field. In solid-state physics, constitutive relationships between fundamental field variables are a topic that is covered by a broad variety of models. In this context, a constitutive tensor of higher order replaces the of the second-order metric tensor. For the classical field models of gravity and electrodynamics, a similar premetric description with a linear constitutive relation has recently presented. In this paper, we analyze the extension of the Hilbert definition of the energy-momentum tensor to models with general linear constitutive law. Differential forms are required for the covariant treatment of integrals on a differential manifold. The Lagrangian, electromagnetic current, and energy-momentum current must all be represented as twisted 4-forms, 3-forms, and vector-valued 3-forms, respectively. For an arbitrary linear map on forms, we derive a commutative variation identity that allows direct variation procedures without having to deal with the individual components. One can deal with Maxwell-type Lagrangians in any dimension by restricting the linear map to the generalized Hodge dual map (constitutive law). The Hilbert energy-momentum current, which is defined as a variation derivative of the Lagrangian with regard to a coframe field, is derived in differential form. It is demonstrated that the commutative variation identity is closely connected to the explicit form of the energy-momentum current. This construction is applied to a number of field models having a general linear constitutive law.

2.3
Variation operator on differential forms

The Lagrangian
n
-form
{\cal L}
, as well as its ingredients (lower order differentials forms), are functions of physical fields. These fields typically play a variety of roles in the variation procedure. The collection of physical variables can be separated into three subgroups within a given physics model:

(i)

Dynamical variables:
These variables are assumed to have a non-zero variation. Field equations for these variables characterizing the dynamics of the physical fields are produced using the variation technique.

(ii)

Semi-dynamical variables:
Despite the absence of field equations for these variables, it is presumed that semi-dynamical variables have non-zero variation.

(iii)

Non-dynamical variables:
These variables are not included in any way in the variation procedure.

Let us give an illustration: In classical vacuum electrodynamics, the electric and magnetic fields are dynamical variables, whereas the electric current is a non-dynamical variable. The metric tensor presents in the Lagrangian as a semi-dynamical variable without its own field equation. Hilbert’s expression for the electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor results from the variation of the metric tensor.

In order to account for variations in the physics field and independent variations in the geometric structure, we consider a differential manifold
{\cal M}
of a dimension
n
endowed with the coframe field
\vartheta^{\alpha}
.
The coframe variable will be regarded as semi-dynamical in the models that do not account for the effects of gravity. In gravity models, such as telleparalel gravity, it is viewed as a fully dynamical variable. The coframe must additionally be confined to orthonormal in the standard metric GR.

(3)

\delta{\cal F}\wedge\diamond{\cal F}=\delta(d{\cal A})\wedge\diamond{\cal F}=d(\delta{\cal A})\wedge\diamond{\cal F}

(55)

Extracting the total derivative we are left with

\delta{\cal F}\wedge\diamond{\cal F}=d\Big(\delta{\cal A}\wedge\diamond{\cal F}\Big)-\delta{\cal A}\wedge(-1)^{p-1}d\diamond{\cal F}.

(56)

Assume the variation of the potential
{\cal A}
and the coframe
\vartheta^{\alpha}
to be independent one on the other.
Neglecting the total derivative term we are left with
the field equation for
{\cal F}
in the form

\boxed{d\diamond{\cal F}=0}

(57)

The second term in (
52
) is identified as
Hilbert’s energy-momentum current
of the field
{\cal F}

\Sigma_{\alpha}=\frac{\delta{\cal L}}{\delta\vartheta^{\alpha}}

(58)

Explicitly, we have

\boxed{\Sigma_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\Big((-1)^{p}{\cal F}\wedge(e_{\alpha}\rfloor\diamond{\cal F})-(e_{\alpha}\rfloor{\cal F})\wedge\diamond{\cal F}\Big).}

(59)

Observe that this expression is a straightforward consequence of the commutative identity (
39
).
It is not depend on the field expression and even does not require the introduction of the potential. For an arbitrary untwisted
p
-form
{\cal F}
, the expressions for
\Sigma_{\alpha}
are twisted
(n-1)
-forms. Then they can serve as an invariant integrand over arbitrary hypersurface in
\cal M
.

Using the interior product identity

e_{\alpha}\rfloor({{\cal F}\wedge\diamond{\cal F}})=(e_{\alpha}\rfloor{{\cal F})\wedge\diamond{\cal F}}+(-1)^{p}{{\cal F}\wedge(e_{\alpha}\rfloor\diamond{\cal F}})

(60)

we obtain two additional forms of the energy-momentum current expression

\Sigma_{\alpha}=e_{a}\rfloor{\cal L}+(-1)^{p}{\cal F}\wedge(e_{\alpha}\rfloor\diamond{\cal F}).

(61)

and

\Sigma_{\alpha}=-e_{\alpha}\rfloor{\cal L}-(e_{\alpha}\rfloor{\cal F})\wedge\diamond{\cal F}.

(62)

For an
n
-form
\cal F
, we have from (

\Sigma_{\alpha}=e_{a}\rfloor{\cal L}
. For a scalar form
\cal F
, Eq.(

\Sigma_{\alpha}=-e_{a}\rfloor{\cal L}
.

We summarize the results for the Maxwell-type model as follows:

•

The twisted
n

{\cal S}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}{\cal F}\wedge{\cal H}

(63)

•

The untwisted
p

(64)

•

The energy-momentum tensor is expressed as

\Sigma_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\Big((-1)^{p}{\cal F}\wedge(e_{\alpha}\rfloor{\cal H})-(e_{\alpha}\rfloor{\cal F})\wedge{\cal H}\Big).

(66)

3.2
Energy-momentum current vs energy-momentum tensor

Although the
(n-1)
-form current
\Sigma_{\alpha}
is the proper description of the energy-momentum quantity, it is useful to have an alternative tensor description.
We will now derive a relationship between the energy-momentum current
\Sigma_{\alpha}
and Hilbert’s energy-momentum tensor
T^{ij}
.

Recall that the current
\Sigma_{\alpha}
is defined implicitly via the variation relation

\delta{\cal L}=\Sigma_{\alpha}\wedge\delta\vartheta^{\alpha}.

(67)

Then
\Sigma_{\alpha}
is a vector-valued twisted
(n-1)
-form with
n^{2}
independent components.
The Lagrangian
n
-form
{\cal L}
can be turned into a conventional Lagrangian function
L
when a volume element on the manifold is prescribed.
For this, we use a relation
{\cal L}=L\,{\rm vol}
.
For a tensor representation of the energy-momentum current, we need a basis of twisted
(n-1)
-forms. It can be constructed from the set
\{e_{\alpha}\rfloor{\rm vol}\}
. Then the twisted form
\Sigma_{\alpha}
is expressed as

\Sigma_{\alpha}=\Theta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\left(e_{\beta}\rfloor{\rm vol}\right),

(68)

or, equivalently,

\vartheta^{\beta}\wedge\Sigma_{\alpha}=\Theta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}{\rm vol}.

(69)

With respect to the smooth non-degenerate transformations of the coframe, the coefficients
\Theta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
constitute an ordinary (untwisted) tensor. This tensor has the same
n^{2}
independent components as the vector-valued
(n-1)
-form
\Sigma_{\alpha}
. Note that the definition of
\Theta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
requires only a volume structure on the manifold. Such structure is prescribed by an arbitrary smooth twisted
n
-form. In particular,
\Theta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
can be defined on a manifold without a prescribed metric.

Let us turn now to Hilbert’s energy-momentum tensor. It can also be defined implicitly using the variation relation

\delta{\cal L}=\frac{1}{2}T^{ij}\delta g_{ij}{\rm vol}.

(70)

When the volume element is identified as
{\rm vol}=\sqrt{-g}d^{n}x
, this definition corresponds to the widely-used variation derivative expression

T^{ij}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta L}{\delta g_{ij}}

(71)

The existence of a richer structure on the manifold—-metric structure is required by definition (
70
). The tensor
T^{ij}
is evidently symmetric and composed of only
n(n-1)/2
independent components. Furthermore, this tensor is referred to the holonomic coordinates
x^{i}
basis (rather than the unholonomic basis
\vartheta^{\alpha}
) and transformed by the normal tensor law under the coordinate transformations.
Taking these restrictions into account, we are able to establish a relation between two energy-momentum quantities.

Proposition 4
.

Let a manifold be endowed with a metric
g_{ij}
and an orthonormal coframe
\vartheta^{\alpha}=\vartheta^{\alpha}_{i}dx^{i}
such that

g_{ij}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}\vartheta^{\alpha}{}_{i}\,\vartheta^{\beta}{}_{j}.

(72)

The coframe energy-momenum current
\Sigma_{\alpha}
and the Hilbert energy momentum tensor
T^{ij}
are related as

T^{\alpha\beta}{\rm vol}=\frac{1}{2}\Sigma_{\gamma}\wedge\left(\eta^{\alpha\gamma}\vartheta^{\beta}+\eta^{\beta\gamma}\vartheta^{\alpha}\right),

(73)

where

T^{\alpha\beta}=T^{ij}\vartheta^{\alpha}{}_{i}\,\vartheta^{\beta}{}_{j}.

(74)

Proof.

It is convenient to express the variation of the Lagrangian in term of the variation matrix
\varepsilon^{\alpha}{}_{\gamma}
satisfying the equation
\delta\vartheta^{\alpha}=\varepsilon^{\alpha}{}_{\gamma}\vartheta^{\gamma}
.
Substituting expression (
68
) into Eq.(
67
) we have

\delta{\cal L}=\Theta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\left(e_{\beta}\rfloor{\rm vol}\right)\wedge\delta\vartheta^{\alpha}.

(75)

In terms of the variation matrix, it reads

\delta{\cal L}=-\Theta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\varepsilon^{\alpha}{}_{\gamma}\vartheta^{\gamma}\wedge\left(e_{\beta}\rfloor{\rm vol}\right).

(76)

Using (
19
) we obtain a compact expression

\delta{\cal L}=-\Theta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\varepsilon^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\,{\rm vol}.

(77)

To deal with Hilbert’s energy-momentum tensor, we must express the variation of the metric tensor in terms of the variation of the coframe. Using (
70
) we write

\delta g_{ij}=\delta(\eta_{\alpha\beta}\vartheta^{\alpha}{}_{i}\,\vartheta^{\beta}{}_{j})=\eta_{\alpha\beta}\left(\delta(\vartheta^{\alpha}{}_{i})\,\vartheta^{\beta}{}_{j}+\vartheta^{\alpha}{}_{i}\,\delta(\vartheta^{\beta}{}_{j})\right).

(78)

In terms of the dual frame components
e_{\rho}{}^{i}
satisfying
e_{\rho}{}^{i}\vartheta^{\mu}{}_{i}=\delta^{\mu}_{\rho}
, we obtain

\delta\vartheta^{\mu}=(\delta\vartheta^{\mu}{}_{i})dx^{i}=(\delta\vartheta^{\mu}{}_{i})e_{\rho}{}^{i}\vartheta^{\rho}.

(79)

Thus

\varepsilon^{\mu}{}_{\rho}\vartheta^{\rho}=(\delta\vartheta^{\mu}{}_{i})e_{\rho}{}^{i}\vartheta^{\rho},

(80)

or, equivalently,

\delta\vartheta^{\mu}{}_{i}=\varepsilon^{\mu}{}_{\rho}\vartheta^{\rho}{}_{i}.

(81)

Consequently the variation of the metric tensor is presented in the form

\delta g_{ij}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}\left(\varepsilon^{\alpha}{}_{\rho}\,\vartheta^{\rho}{}_{i}\vartheta^{\beta}{}_{j}+\varepsilon^{\beta}{}_{\rho}\vartheta^{\alpha}{}_{i}\,\vartheta^{\rho}{}_{j}\right).

(82)

Hence the metric variation of the Lagrangian (
70
) is presented as

\delta{\cal L}=\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\left(\varepsilon^{\alpha}{}_{\rho}\,T^{\rho\beta}+\varepsilon^{\beta}{}_{\rho}T^{\alpha\rho}\right){\rm vol}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}\varepsilon^{\alpha}{}_{\rho}\,T^{\rho\beta}{\rm vol},

(83)

where
T^{\alpha\beta}
is defined in (
74
).

We will now compare two expressions of
\delta{\cal L}
given in equations (
77
) and (
83
). Even though these expressions appear to be very similar, they have different features. Eq.(
77
) is independent of the metric, whereas Eq.(
83
) explicitly contains the metric tensor. Moreover, the expression in (
77
) contains the asymmetric tensor
\Theta_{\alpha}{}^{\alpha}
, while the expression in (
83
) contains the symmetric tensor
T^{\rho\beta}
. As a result, the corresponding variations of the Lagrangian in (
83
) are only dependent on the symmetric variations of the coframe. Indeed, using the notation
\varepsilon_{\beta\rho}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}\varepsilon^{\alpha}{}_{\rho}
we have from (
83
) the symmetry relation
\varepsilon_{\beta\rho}=\varepsilon_{\rho\beta}
. Through using metric tensor, we rewrite now (
77
) as
\delta{\cal L}=-\Theta^{\alpha\beta}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}\,{\rm vol}
and only employ the symmetric variation matrix in this expression.
As a result of comparing two expressions for
\delta{\cal L}
, we have

T^{\alpha\beta}=-\frac{1}{2}(\Theta^{\alpha\beta}+\Theta^{\alpha\beta}).

(84)

In terms of differential forms, this equation is expressed as

T^{\alpha\beta}\,{\rm vol}=-\frac{1}{2}(\eta^{\gamma\alpha}\vartheta^{\beta}\wedge\Sigma_{\gamma}+\eta^{\gamma\beta}\vartheta^{\alpha}\wedge\Sigma_{\gamma}).

(85)

The latter expression is equivalent to (
73
).
∎

3.3
Trace of Hilbert’s energy-momentum current

The trace of the energy-momentum current
\Theta_{\alpha}{}^{\alpha}
prescribes in field theory essential physical quantity, such as photon mass. In differential-form approach, we can construct a scalar-valued
n
-form
\vartheta^{\alpha}\wedge\Sigma_{\alpha}
that has one component as the scalar
\Theta_{\alpha}{}^{\alpha}
.
Using Eq.(
48
), we have

\vartheta^{\alpha}\wedge\Sigma_{\alpha}=\Theta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\vartheta^{\alpha}\wedge\left(e_{\beta}\rfloor{\rm vol}\right)=\Theta_{\alpha}{}^{\alpha}\,{\rm vol}.

(86)

Consequently, the trace of the energy-momentum tensor is equivalent to the
n
-form
\vartheta^{\alpha}\wedge\Sigma_{\alpha}

Proposition 5
.

The trace term for a Maxwell-type Lagrangian with an arbitrary
p
-form strength
{\cal F}
is expressed as

\vartheta^{\alpha}\wedge\Sigma_{\alpha}=(2p-n){\cal L}.

(87)

In particular, the energy momentum current is traceless if and only if the space is even-dimensional and
{\cal F}
is a middle form.

Proof.

Calculate using (
48
)

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\alpha}\wedge\Sigma_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\vartheta^{\alpha}\wedge\Big((-1)^{p}{\cal F}\wedge(e_{a}\rfloor\diamond{\cal F})-(e_{a}\rfloor{\cal F})\wedge\diamond{\cal F}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big[({\cal F}\wedge\vartheta^{\alpha}\wedge(e_{a}\rfloor\diamond{\cal F})-\vartheta^{\alpha}\wedge(e_{a}\rfloor{\cal F})\wedge\diamond{\cal F}\Big]

Using the first of the relations (

(89)

and

(91)

∎

3.4

The energy-momentum current
\Sigma_{\alpha}
is equivalent to a mixed tensor
T_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
with
n^{2}
components. The metric tensor
\eta^{\alpha\beta}
must be used to convert this tensor into a covariant tensor

The same

(n-2)

{\cal W}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}e_{\alpha}\rfloor\Sigma_{\beta}.

(92)

Proposition 6
.

The Hilbert energy-momentum current
\Sigma_{\alpha}
represents the symmetric tensor iff
the
(n-2)
-form
{\cal W}
vanishes.

Proof.

Using the tensor presentation (
68
), we derive

{\cal W}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}e_{\alpha}\rfloor\left(\Theta_{\beta}{}^{\nu}e_{\nu}\rfloor{\rm vol}\right)=\Theta^{\alpha\nu}\left(e_{\alpha}\rfloor e_{\nu}\rfloor{\rm vol}\right)=\Theta^{[\alpha\beta]}\left(e_{\alpha}\rfloor e_{\beta}\rfloor{\rm vol}\right)

Thus the
(n-2)

indeed represents the skew-symmetric part of the energy-momentum tensor. The equation

means symmetry of the energy-momentun current.

Proposition 7

For the Maxwell-type Lagrangian

4
Applications

In this part, we will look at certain applications of the formalism described above. We will be dealing with viable physics models in the ordinary fourth-dimensional space-time. Our aim is to discuss different types of the dual map. In all models, the energy-momentum current follows from the same expression (
59
). In the first pair of examples, we are dealing with the well-known models in vacuum. In this case, the dual map is proportional to the ordinary Hodge map. Particularly, in the tangent space, we employ the conventional version of the Minkowski metric
\eta_{\alpha\beta}={\rm diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)
.
In the second pair of examples, we consider premetric models of electromagnetism and gravity.

4.1
Scalar field

For a complex scalar field
\varphi
on a 4-dimensional manifold, the Lagrangian 4-form can be expressed using the differential forms formalism. We assume

{\cal L}=\frac{1}{2}d\varphi\wedge*\,\overline{d\varphi}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\varphi*\,\overline{\varphi},

(97)

where the star
*
denotes Hodge’s operator while the bar states for the complex conjugate. Both of these two operators are defined for differential forms of arbitrary order.
The only forms for which we must demand their definition in (
97
) are those of the order
p=0,1
.
Two terms in (
97
) can be regarded as generalized Maxwell-type Lagrangians (
63
). The generalized Hodge map can be constructed in relation to the general scheme as a product of Hodge’s dual and complex conjugate operators,
\diamond w=*\,\overline{w}
.

To illustrate how the Lagrangian 4-form (
97
) relates to the standard tensor representation, we can express the field strength in a non-holonomic and a holonomic (coordinate) basis, respectively, as follows:

d\varphi=\varphi_{\mu}\vartheta^{\mu}=\varphi_{,i}dx^{i}\qquad{\rm with}\qquad\varphi_{,i}=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x^{i}}=\varphi_{\mu}\vartheta^{\mu}_{i}.

(98)

The coframe
\vartheta^{\mu}
is assumed to be pseudo-orthonormal with respect to the metric tensor
g_{ij}=\eta_{\mu\nu}\vartheta^{\mu}_{i}\vartheta^{\nu}_{j}
.

The Lagrangian 4-form is then expressed in standard form
{\cal L}=L{\rm vol}
, with

L=\frac{1}{2}\eta^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\mu}\overline{\varphi}_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\varphi\,\overline{\varphi}=\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\varphi_{,i}\overline{\varphi}_{,j}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\varphi\,\overline{\varphi}.

(99)

This Lagrangian scalar can only be used to derive the energy-momentum tensor when the factor
\sqrt{-g}
is included in the equation.
However, we will move forward with the differential form variation that was previously described.
The field equations (
57
) takes the form

d*d\overline{\varphi}=m^{2}*\overline{\varphi}.

(100)

This equation is applicable also on a curved manifold.

Let us turn to the energy-momentum tensor. For the first term in (
97
) we can identify the field strength as
{\cal F}=d\varphi
of the order
p=1
. We choose the constitutive map acted on 1-form
{\cal F}
as
\diamond{\cal F}=-*\overline{{\cal F}}
. Then the Lagrangian takes the canonical form
{\cal L}=-(1/2){\cal F}\wedge\diamond{\cal F}
and the general expressions (
59
,
61
,
62
) for the energy-momentum current are applicable. We have the first part of the energy-momentum current (
66

{}^{(1)}\Sigma_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\Big({d\varphi}\wedge(e_{\alpha}\rfloor*d\overline{\varphi})+(e_{\alpha}\rfloor{d\varphi})\wedge*d\overline{\varphi}\Big).

(101)

In components, this expression takes the form

{}^{(1)}\Sigma_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{\mu}\overline{\varphi}_{\nu}\left(2\delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}\eta^{\beta\nu}-\delta^{\beta}_{\alpha}\eta^{\mu\nu}\right)e_{\beta}\rfloor{\rm vol},

(102)

where the the standard energy-momentum expression for the scalar field is visible.

Even the second term in (
97
) can be formally brought into the canonical form
{\cal L}=-(1/2){\cal F}\wedge\diamond{\cal F}
. For this we can choose the zeroth-order“strength”
{\cal F}=\varphi
and the dual map
\diamond{\cal F}=*\overline{{\cal F}}
. Thus we can use the energy-momentum current expression (
66
) once more.
We have

{}^{(2)}\Sigma_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\varphi\overline{\varphi}\,(e_{\alpha}\rfloor{\rm{vol}}).

(103)

The total energy-momentum current is expressed as a sum

\Sigma_{\alpha}={}^{(1)}\Sigma_{\alpha}+{}^{(2)}\Sigma_{\alpha}
.

The trace of the the energy-momentum current is determined by the product
\vartheta^{\alpha}\wedge\Sigma_{\alpha}
. We obtain

\vartheta^{\alpha}\wedge\Sigma_{\alpha}=(-\eta^{\mu\nu}\varphi_{\mu}\overline{\varphi}_{\nu}+2m^{2}\varphi\overline{\varphi})\,{\rm{vol}}.

(104)

Note, that the definition of Lagrangian (
97
), the field equation (
100
), and the energy-momentum currents (
102
), (
103
) do not require the
\diamond
-operator to be defined on forms of order
p\neq 0,1
.

4.2
Electromagnetism in vacuum

The electromagnetic field in vacuum is described on a 4-dimensional manifold by a Lagrangian of the type (
48
) with the general linear operator proportional to the Hodge dual operator
\diamond=*
.

The field strength
{\cal F}
is an untwisted 2-form. It is assumed to be exact, i.e.,
{\cal F}
is expanded in term of the potential 1-form
{\cal A}
such that

{\cal F}=d{\cal A}.

(105)

Using the phenomenological source—twisted 3-form of the electric current
{\cal J}
, we express a Lagrangian 4-form as

{\cal L}=-\frac{1}{2}{\cal F}\wedge*{\cal F}-{\cal A}\wedge{\cal J}

(106)

In this setting, the potential 1-form
{\cal A}
is assumed to be a dynamical variable, the coframe
\vartheta^{\alpha}
appearing implicitly in the definition of Hodge’s map is a semi-dynamical variable, while the 3-form source
{\cal J}
is non-dynamical variable.
Applying variations of the Lagrangian with respect to the potential
{\cal A}
and to the coframe field
\vartheta^{\alpha}
, we obtain the field equation

d*{\cal F}={\cal J}

(107)

and the energy-momentum current

\Sigma_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\Big((e_{a}\rfloor{\cal F})\wedge*{\cal F}-(e_{a}\rfloor{\cal*F})\wedge{\cal F}\Big)

(108)

Since the strength
{\cal F}
acting on 4-dimensional manifold, this current is traceless, due to Proposition 4. It is also symmetric—the equation
{\cal W}=0
can be proved using the algebraic properties of the Hodge map, see
[
18
]
.

Substituting into Eq.(
62

{\cal F}=\frac{1}{2}F_{\mu\nu}\vartheta^{\mu}\wedge\vartheta^{\nu},\qquad\qquad\diamond{\cal F}=\frac{1}{2}H^{\rho\sigma}(e_{\rho}\rfloor e_{\sigma}\rfloor{\rm vol}

(109)

we obtain the familiar expression of the energy-momentum tensor

T_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=L\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}F_{\alpha\mu}H^{\mu\beta}

(110)

Note that this expression is valid not only in the standard Maxwell’s electrodynamics on a flat space but also on a curved manifold.

4.3
Premetric electromagnetism

Premetric electromagnetism model is described by two second-order differential forms: an untwisted form
{\cal F}
of the
field strength
and a twisted form
{\cal H}
of the
electromagnetic excitation
. Both forms are defined on a differential 4-dimensional manifold without a prescribed metric. The form
{\cal F}
is assumed to be exact, i.e., an untwisted 1-form
{\cal A}
of
potential
is defined

{\cal F}=d{\cal A}.

(111)

The Lagrangian is assumed in the standard Maxwell-type form

{\cal L}=\frac{1}{2}{\cal F}\wedge{\cal H}-{\cal A}\wedge{\cal J}.

(112)

Here
{\cal J}
is a twisted 3-form of
electric current
. It is a non-dynamical phenomenological quantity that is not included in the variation procedure.

Two 2-order differential forms
{\cal F}
and
{\cal H}
are not independent. In a wide range of field strength, they are related by linear
constitutive relation

{\cal H}=\kappa{\cal F},

(113)

where
\kappa
is a linear operator. It is assumed to satisfy the defining properties of the generalized Hodge map listed in Definition 3. Note that
\kappa
is required to be defined on the 2-forms only. The commutative formula (
39
) holds even in this restricted case.

To express the operator
\kappa
explicitly, we expand the 2-forms in the coframe basis

{\cal F}=\frac{1}{2}F_{\alpha\beta}\vartheta^{\alpha}\wedge\vartheta^{\beta},\qquad{\cal H}=\frac{1}{2}H_{\alpha\beta}\vartheta^{\alpha}\wedge\vartheta^{\beta}.

(114)

In this description, the
F_{\alpha\beta}
are components of an ordinary tensor, while
H_{\alpha\beta}
are components of a pseudo-tensor.
The operator
\kappa
is defined as a pseudo-tensor related the components of the field strength to the components of excitation form

H_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma\delta}F_{\gamma\delta}\,.

(115)

Both forms are assumed to be related to the same basis.

Since
\kappa_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma\delta}
is skew-symmetric in two pairs of its indices it is convenient to deal with an equivalent pseudo-tensor

\chi^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=\kappa_{\mu\nu}{}^{\gamma\delta}\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}

(116)

Here
\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}
is a permutation pseudo-tensor.
Observe the symmetry relations

\chi^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=-\chi^{\beta\alpha\gamma\delta}=-\chi^{\alpha\beta\delta\gamma}\,.

(117)

Consequently the pseudo-tensor
\chi^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
has 36 independent components. It can be irreducibly decomposed
[
16
]
into a sum of three independent sub-tensors: the principal part of 20 components, the skewon part of 15 components, and the axion part of 1 component. The skewon part does not contribute to the Lagrangian, while the axion part does not contribute in Hilbert’s energy-momentum tensor
[
16
]
. Thus we restrict ourselves to the principle part of the constitutive tensor of 20 independent components. Note that all these components are observable in solid-state physics. As a result the constitutive tensor is assumed to satisfy the additional symmetry relations

\chi^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=\chi^{\gamma\delta\alpha\beta},\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\chi^{[\alpha\beta\gamma\delta]}=0\,.

(118)

The constitutive relation (
113
) can be viewed as a linear map between two spaces of the second-order differential forms.

Although, the constitutive relation (
113
) is defined for the second-order differential forms only, the commutation formula can be applied also in this case. Consequently, we are coming to the field equation (
65
)

d{\cal H}=J

(119)

and the energy-momentum current (
66
)

\Sigma_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\Big((e_{a}\rfloor{\cal F})\wedge\kappa{\cal F}-{\cal F}\wedge(e_{a}\rfloor\kappa{\cal F})\Big).

(120)

In term of the forms
{\cal F}
and
{\cal H}
, it reads

\Sigma_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\Big((e_{a}\rfloor{\cal F})\wedge{\cal H}-{\cal F}\wedge(e_{a}\rfloor{\cal H})\Big).

(121)

This current is traceless due to the general fact given in Proposition 4.

In
[
16
]
, the energy-momentum current expression was postulated as an independent axiom based on an extension of the vacuum formula. Here we derived it from the Lagrangian by the variation relation.

4.4
Premetric gravity

The premetric gravity model
[

]
,
[

is based on two Maxwell-type equations

d{\cal H}_{\alpha}=\Sigma_{\alpha}\qquad d{\cal F}^{\alpha}=0.

(122)

Here
{\cal H}_{\alpha}

In topologically good area, the homogeneous field equation can be resolved in term of potential

(123)

Since the set of 1-forms
\vartheta^{\alpha}
is defined up to a total derivative, they can be chosen linearly independent. Then
\vartheta^{\alpha}
not only a potential but also a reference basis.
We express the 2-forms an this basis

{\cal F}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}F^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}\vartheta^{\mu}\wedge\vartheta^{\nu},\qquad{\cal H}_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}H_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\nu}e_{\mu}\rfloor e_{\nu}\rfloor{\rm vol}.

(124)

Here
e_{\alpha}
is a frame dual to
\vartheta^{\alpha}
, i.e.,
e_{\alpha}\rfloor\vartheta^{\beta}=\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}
.

The tensors
F^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}
and
H_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\nu}
are assumed to be connected by linear homogeneous constitutive relation

H_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\chi_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\nu}{}_{\beta}{}^{\rho\sigma}{}F^{\beta}{}_{\rho\sigma}

(125)

The constitutive tensor
\chi_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\nu}{}_{\beta}{}^{\rho\sigma}
is skew-symmetric in the pairs of its upper indices.

\chi_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\nu}{}_{\beta}{}^{\rho\sigma}=-\chi_{\alpha}{}^{\nu\mu}{}_{\beta}{}^{\rho\sigma}=-\chi_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\nu}{}_{\beta}{}^{\sigma\rho}.

(126)

Consider a Lagrangian of the Maxwell-type form

{\cal L}=-\frac{1}{2}{\cal F}^{\alpha}\wedge{\cal H}_{\alpha}+{}^{\rm(mat)}{\cal L}(\psi,d\psi,\vartheta^{\alpha}),

(127)

where
\psi
represents the matter field.

Since the Lagrangian satisfied the condition of the general Maxwell-type action,
the field equation takes the form (
65
)

d{\cal H_{\alpha}}={}^{\rm(gr)}\Sigma_{\alpha}+{}^{\rm(mat)}\Sigma_{\alpha}.

(128)

Here the matter energy-momentum current is defined as

{}^{\rm(mat)}\Sigma_{\alpha}=\frac{\delta}{\delta\vartheta^{\alpha}}{{}^{\rm(mat)}{\cal L}(\psi,d\psi,\vartheta^{\alpha})},

(129)

while the gravity-energy-momentum current is given in the form(
66
)

{}^{\rm(gr)}\Sigma_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\Big((e_{a}\rfloor{\cal F^{\beta}})\wedge{\cal H}_{\beta}-{\cal F^{\beta}}\wedge(e_{a}\rfloor{\cal H}_{\beta})\Big).

(130)

When a constitutive pseudotensor
\chi_{\alpha}{}^{\mu\nu}{}_{\beta}{}^{\rho\sigma}
is restricted to a special metric form
[
20
]
and Eq.(
128
) is rewritten as

d{\cal H_{\alpha}}-{}^{\rm(gr)}\Sigma_{\alpha}={}^{\rm(mat)}\Sigma_{\alpha}.

(131)

it turns to be equivalent to the standard Einstein’s equation.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we are looking for an extension of Hilbert’s definition of the energy-momentum tensor to premetric field models. In this case, the metric tensor is replaced by a general constitutive law that relates the basic fields in the model. We explain how the variation procedure can be applied on fields presented by differential form instead of separated components. In this formalism, the commutative relation for the mapped forms is derived. Then we express the Lagrangian density in term of Hodge-type dual map and derive the corresponding energy-momentum current (twisted vector-valued 3-form). This expression turns out to be straightforwardly related to the commutative relation. The applications of the energy-momentum current formula to specific field models yields the expressions appearing in literature. In the case of a metric constitutive map, these formulas was derived from a Lagrangian. For pure premetric models, such formulas was previously postulated.

The applications of the current results can be used in more complicated field models such as non-local electrodynamics
[
22
]
, non-local gravity
[
23
]
, non-linear electrodynamics
[
24
]
, and Finsler modified electrodynamics
[
25
]
.

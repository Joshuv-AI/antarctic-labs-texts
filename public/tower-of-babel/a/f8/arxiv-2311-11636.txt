\inf_{\varepsilon_{\chi}\leq|t|\leq x^{2}}\mathbb{D}(g,\chi(n)n^{it};x)\geq\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\log\log x}+O_{\chi}(1).

Moreover, if
\chi
is real-valued then

\inf_{|t|\leq 1}\mathbb{D}(g,\chi(n)n^{it};x)\geq\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{D}(g,\chi;x)+O(1).

Proof.

This is
[
20
, Lem. C.1]
.
∎

3.3.
Auxiliary results about multiplicative functions

As in
[
19
]
, Tao’s theorem on logarithmically-averaged binary correlations plays a crucial role in our analysis.

Theorem 3.5

(Tao,
[
24
]
)
.

Let
\eta>0
and let
a,c\in\mathbb{N}
,
b,d\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfy
ad-bc\neq 0
. Let
g_{1},g_{2}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
be 1-bounded multiplicative functions with the property that for some
x\geq x_{0}(\eta)
,

\frac{1}{\log x}\left|\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{g_{1}(an+b)g_{2}(cn+d)}{n}\right|\geq\eta.

Then there are primitive Dirichlet characters
\psi_{j}=\psi_{j,x}
with conductor
O_{\eta}(1)
and real numbers
t_{j}=t_{j,x}
with
|t_{j}|=O_{\eta}(x)
such that for
j=1,2
,

\mathbb{D}(g_{j},\psi_{j}(n)n^{it_{j}};x)^{2}=\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1-{\text{Re}}(g_{j}(p)\overline{\psi}_{j}(p)p^{-it_{j}})}{p}=O_{\eta}(1).

In addition, we will also need the following quantitative version of Halász’s theorem on mean values of multiplicative functions, which provides similar but more precise data about functions with large partial sums than Tao’s theorem.

Theorem 3.6

(Halász-Montgomery-Tenenbaum Inequality)
.

Let
x\geq 3
and
T\geq 1
. Let
g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
be a multiplicative function. Then

\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}g(n)\ll(1+M_{g})e^{-M_{g}}+\frac{1}{T},

where
M_{g}=M_{g}(x;T)
is defined by

M_{g}(x;T):=\min_{|t|\leq T}\mathbb{D}(g,n^{it};x)^{2}=\min_{|t|\leq T}\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1-{\text{Re}}(g(p)p^{-it})}{p}.

Proof.

This is
[
25
, Cor. III.4.12]
.
∎

The following variant of Shiu’s theorem, which is a consequence of a result due to Pollack

]
, will allow us to work conveniently with sums over shifted primes in Section

.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

and let

let

be a set of

non-zero residue classes, and let

Then, uniformly over all multiplicative functions

we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\in\mathscr{S}\end{subarray}}f(n)\ll_{k}\frac{X}{\log X}\exp\left(\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{f(p)-\nu(p)}{p}\right).

Proof.

This is immediate from
[

, taking
A_{1}=2
in the definition of

there, and
y=X
.

4.
Applying the projection method: the archimedean argument

Given
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
and a multiplicative function
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
recall the notation

\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b}:=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:f(n+a)=f(n)+b\}.

In this section we use the compositions
|f|_{t}:=|f|^{it}
to study the sequence of magnitudes
(|f(p)|)_{p}
when
\overline{\delta}(\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b})>0
. In most aspects this is identical to the procedure in
[
19
, Sec. 3]
, where
b=0
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be a multiplicative function that satisfies

(18)

\sum_{p:|f(p)|\neq 1}\frac{1}{p}=\infty,\quad\sum_{p:f(p)=0}\frac{1}{p}<\infty.

Let
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
, and assume that
\overline{\delta}(\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b})>\delta
for some
\delta\in(0,1/2)
.
Then there is a
0<r\ll_{\delta}1
and a
O_{\delta}(1)
-sparse sequence of primes
\mathcal{S}_{\delta}

such that

\frac{1}{2}<|f(p)|/p^{r}<2\text{ for all }p\notin\mathcal{S}_{\delta}.

Remark 4.2
.

We may assume that for
f
in Conjecture
1.5
the set
\{p\in\mathcal{P}:f(p)=0\}
is sparse, and thus Conjecture
1.5
classifies all non-trivial functions for which
\overline{\delta}(\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b})>0
for some
ab\neq 0
.

Indeed, suppose otherwise. Then the multiplicative function
1_{f(n)\neq 0}
has vanishing logarithmic mean:

\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{1_{f(n)\neq 0}}{n}\ll\exp\left(-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
f(p)=0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\right)=o(1),\quad x\rightarrow\infty.

It follows that
f(n+a)=f(n)=0
for a logarithmic density
1
set of
n
. In particular,
\delta(\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b})=0
whenever
ab\neq 0
, contradicting our hypothesis. There is therefore no loss in assuming both of the conditions (
18
) here.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Note that if
n\in\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b}
satisfies
|f(n)|>|b|\varepsilon^{-1}
then

f(n+a)/f(n)\in(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon).

Our arguments treat precisely those
n
for which such a condition holds. The following lemma implies that the converse inequality
|f(n)|\leq|b|\varepsilon^{-1}
holds for very few
n
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that
f
satisfies both conditions in (
18
). Then for any fixed
M\geq 1
we have

\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
0<|f(n)|\leq M\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}=o(1).

Remark 4.4
.

It is not difficult to see that the proof furthermore implies the stronger bound

\frac{1}{x}|\{n\leq x:0<|f(n)|\leq M\}|=o(1)\text{ as }x\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}:=\{p^{\nu}:|f(p^{\nu})|\neq 0,1\}
, and define
\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(n)
to be the number of prime powers
p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{S}
with
p^{\nu}||n
.
Since
f
takes integer values,
|f(p^{\nu})|\geq 2
for all
p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{S}
. Thus,

\text{if }0<|f(n)|\leq M\text{ then }\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(n)\leq\frac{\log(2M)}{\log 2}.

According to (
18
), as
X\rightarrow\infty
we have

E_{\mathcal{S}}(X):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}\leq X\\
p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
|f(p)|\neq 0,1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\rightarrow\infty.

Furthermore, by Lemma
3.1
(and (
14
)),

\frac{1}{X}\sum_{X\leq n<2X}|\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(n)-E_{\mathcal{S}}(X)|^{2}\ll E_{S}(X),\quad X\geq 2.

Therefore when
X\geq X_{0}(M)
we have

\displaystyle|\{X\leq n<2X:0<|f(n)|\leq M\}|

\displaystyle\leq|\{X\leq n<2X:|\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(n)-E_{\mathcal{S}}(X)|>E_{\mathcal{S}}(X)/2\}|

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{E_{\mathcal{S}}(X)^{2}}\sum_{n\leq 2X}|\omega_{\mathcal{S}}(n)-E_{\mathcal{S}}(X)|^{2}\ll\frac{X}{E_{\mathcal{S}}(X)}.

Set
z:=x^{1/E_{\mathcal{S}}(x)}\geq x^{1/(2\log\log x)}
. Taking a logarithmic average and decomposing over dyadic scales,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
0<|f(n)|\leq M\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{z<y=2^{j}\leq x}\frac{1}{y}|\{y\leq n<2y:0<|f(n)|\leq M\}|+O\left(\frac{\log z}{\log x}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{z<y=2^{j}\leq x}\frac{1}{E_{\mathcal{S}}(y)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log x}}\ll\frac{1}{E_{\mathcal{S}}(z)},

and the claim follows as
x
(and thus also
z
) tends to infinity.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Let
(X_{j})_{j}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
be an infinite increasing subsequence on which

\overline{\delta}(\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b})=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log X_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X_{j}\\
f(n+a)=f(n)+b\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
f(n)\neq 0
, define
g(n):=\log|f(n)|
.
Observe that

\text{if }f(n+a)=f(n)+b\text{ with }f(n+a)f(n)\neq 0,\text{ and }|f(n)|\geq 10|b|

then it follows that

|g(n+a)-g(n)|=|\log|f(n+a)/f(n)||\leq\max\{\log(1+|b/f(n)|),-\log(1-|b/f(n)|)\}<\frac{1}{2},

and in particular that

1_{f(n+a)=f(n)+b}\leq 2\max\{1-|g(n+a)-g(n)|,0\}.

In order to use this we first discard those
n
for which
f(n)f(n+a)=0
or
0<|f(n)|\leq 10|b|
.

Let
j\geq j_{0}(\delta,|b|)
be large and set
X=X_{j}
. Observe that since
b\neq 0
, by Lemma
4.3
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
f(n+a)=f(n)+b\\
f(n)f(n+a)=0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\log X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
f(n+a)=b\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{\log X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
f(n)=-b\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{\log X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq X\\
0<|f(m)|\leq|b|\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}+\frac{a}{\log X}<\delta^{2}.

Moreover, by the same argument we also have

\frac{1}{\log X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
0<|f(n)|\leq 10|b|\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}<\delta^{2}.

Thus, if
j\geq j_{0}(\delta)
we find that

\displaystyle\delta\leq\frac{1}{\log X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
f(n+a)=f(n)+b\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
f(n)f(n+a)\neq 0\\
|f(n)|\geq 10|b|\end{subarray}}\frac{1_{f(n+a)=f(n)+b}}{n}+O(\delta^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\log X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
f(n)f(n+a)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\max\{1-|g(n+a)-g(n)|,0\}}{n}+O(\delta^{2}).

Recall the notation
|f|_{\beta}(n):=|f(n)|^{i\beta}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
f(n)\neq 0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Taking
B=\delta^{-2}
, this is sufficient to establish the conclusion of
[
19
, Lem. 3.2]
, namely that
the set

\left\{\alpha\in[-B,B]:\left|\frac{1}{\log X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
f(n+a)f(n)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{|f|_{\alpha}(n+a)|f|_{-\alpha}(n)}{n}\right|\geq\delta/16\pi\right\}

has Lebesgue measure
\geq\delta/16\pi
for each
X
along an infinite subsequence of
(X_{j})_{j}
.
As discussed in
[
19
, Sec. 3]
, the conclusion of
[
19
, Prop. 3.1]
follows from this, and so there is a
0\leq r\ll_{\delta}1
and a set of primes
\mathcal{S}_{\delta}
outside of which

e^{-\delta^{2}}\leq|f(p)|/p^{r}\leq e^{\delta^{2}}.

Since
\delta\in(0,1/2)
we deduce that
e^{\delta^{2}}<2
, and so

\sum_{p:|f(p)|/p^{r}\notin(1/2,2)}\frac{1}{p}\leq\sum_{p\in\mathcal{S}_{\delta}}\frac{1}{p}<\infty.

Now if
r=0
then as
f(p)\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\sum_{p:|f(p)|\neq 1}\frac{1}{p}=\sum_{p:|f(p)|\notin(1/2,2)}\frac{1}{p}<\infty,

a contradiction.
Therefore,
r>0
as required.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Given that
r>0
, we see that for
p\geq p_{0}(\delta)
,

p\notin\mathcal{S}_{\delta}\Rightarrow|f(p)|\geq p^{r}/2>1.

In particular, using Proposition
4.1
we conclude that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}\leq X\\
|f(p^{\nu})|\neq 0,1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}=\log\log X+O\left(1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\in\mathcal{S}_{\delta}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\right)=\log\log X+O_{\delta}(1),

which quantitatively refines the first hypothesis in (
18
).

5.
The non-Archimedean Argument: Reduction to local power maps

5.1.
Setting up Proposition
5.4

The objective of this section is to prove Proposition
5.4
. Since its precise statement is somewhat technical we defer providing it for the moment and roughly describe its content here.

Since
\overline{d}(\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b})\geq\overline{\delta}(\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b})>\delta
we can find an infinite increasing sequence
(x_{k})_{k}
such that

(19)

|\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b}\cap[1,x_{k}]|\geq\delta x_{k},\quad k\geq k_{0}.

Proposition
5.4
asserts that there are positive integers
A,D=O_{\delta}(1)
such that at each such scale
x_{k}
, for the vast majority of primes
\ell\leq x_{k}^{1/A}
the power
f^{D}
of
f

behaves like a homomorphism

\varphi:(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/q_{\ell}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}

for any prime power
q_{\ell}|f(\ell)
of our choice. More precisely, outside of a sparse set
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
of primes
p\leq x_{k}
,

f(p)^{D}\equiv\varphi(a)\pmod{q_{\ell}}\text{ whenever }p\equiv a\pmod{\ell}.

In the case that
\ell|f(\ell)
we may select
q_{\ell}=\ell
, and deduce that
f^{D}
is (essentially) a
local power map
modulo
\ell
, i.e. that for some
0\leq g_{\ell}<\ell-1
,

f(p)^{D}\equiv p^{g_{\ell}}\pmod{\ell},\quad p\in[2,x_{k}]\backslash\mathcal{T}_{\ell}.

In the next section we will see how to “glue” together these local conditions to establish global information about
f
.

To this end, we begin by showing that
\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b}
contains many multiples of primes
\ell
for which
(b,f(\ell))=1
. This will allow us to show that

f(n+a)=f(m\ell+a)\equiv b\pmod{q_{\ell}}

for many multiples
n=m\ell\in\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b}
, a condition that we can then study using the compositions
\{f^{\chi}\}_{\chi\pmod{q_{\ell}}}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(x_{k})_{k}
be the sequence for which (
19
) holds. Then there is a collection of sets
\mathcal{P}_{\delta,k}
of prime powers that satisfy

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}\leq x_{k}\\
p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}_{\delta,k}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}\ll\delta^{-2},\quad k\geq 1

such that if
k\geq k_{0}(\delta)
and
p^{\nu}\leq x_{k}
,
p^{\nu}\notin\mathcal{P}_{\delta,k}
then

|\{n\leq x_{k}:n\in\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b},\,p^{\nu}||n\}|\geq\frac{\delta x_{k}}{4p^{\nu}}.

Proof.

Let
k\geq k_{0}(\delta)
, and define
a_{n}:=1_{\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b}}(n)
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For each
p^{\nu}\leq x_{k}
define also

\Delta_{p^{\nu}}(x_{k}):=\left|\frac{p^{\nu}}{x_{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x_{k}\\
p^{\nu}||n\end{subarray}}a_{n}-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{1}{x_{k}}\sum_{n\leq x_{k}}a_{n}\right|,

and define

\mathcal{P}_{\delta,k}:=\{p^{\nu}\leq x_{k}:\Delta_{p^{\nu}}(x_{k})>\delta/4\}.

By Lemma
3.2
,

\sum_{p^{\nu}\leq x_{k}}\frac{\Delta_{p^{\nu}}(x_{k})^{2}}{p^{\nu}}\ll\frac{1}{x_{k}}\sum_{n\leq x_{k}}|a_{n}|^{2}\ll 1,

so that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}_{\delta,k}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}\leq x_{k}\\
\Delta_{p^{\nu}}(x_{k})>\delta/4\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}\leq\frac{16}{\delta^{2}}\sum_{p^{\nu}\leq x_{k}}\frac{\Delta_{p^{\nu}}(x_{k})^{2}}{p^{\nu}}\ll\delta^{-2}.

On the other hand, note that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x_{k}\\
p^{\nu}||n\end{subarray}}a_{n}=|\{n\leq x_{k}:n\in\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b},\,p^{\nu}||n\}|,

and using (
19
),

\sum_{n\leq x_{k}}a_{n}=|\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b}\cap[1,x_{k}]|\geq\delta x_{k}.

Hence, for each
p^{\nu}\leq x_{k}
with
p^{\nu}\notin\mathcal{P}_{\delta,k}
we have

|\{n\leq x_{k}:n\in\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b},\,p^{\nu}||n\}|\geq\frac{x_{k}}{p^{\nu}}\left(\delta\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)-\Delta_{p^{\nu}}(x_{k})\right)\geq\frac{\delta x_{k}}{4p^{\nu}},

as required.
∎

Set
\mathcal{P}_{f}:=\{p^{\nu}:|f(p^{\nu})|\neq 0,1\}
. Given
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\geq k_{0}(\delta)
define also

\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k}:=\mathcal{P}_{f}\backslash(\mathcal{P}_{\delta,k}\cup\mathcal{S}_{\delta}),\quad\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k;m}:=\{p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k}:(m,f(p^{\nu}))=1\}.

Combining Remark
4.5
and Lemma
5.1
,

(20)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}\leq x_{k}\\
p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}_{f}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}+O\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathcal{S}_{\delta}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}\leq x_{k}\\
p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}_{\delta,k}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}\right)=\log\log x_{k}+O_{\delta}(1).

The next lemma shows that (
20
) holds with
\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k}
replaced by
\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k;m}
, for any
m
fixed.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}\leq x_{k}\\
p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k;m}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}=\log\log x_{k}+O_{\delta,\omega(m)}(1).

Proof.

For each prime
\ell|m
define the set

\mathcal{B}_{\ell}:=\{p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}_{f}:\ell|f(p^{\nu})\},\quad\mathcal{B}_{m}:=\bigcup_{\ell|m}\mathcal{B}_{\ell}.

Then
(m,f(p^{\nu}))>1
if and only if
p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k;m}=\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{m}
. We will show that

(21)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}\leq x_{k}\\
p^{\nu}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}\ll_{\delta,\omega(m)}1,\quad k\rightarrow\infty.

Thus, assume for the sake of contradiction that (
21
) fails. For convenience, given
\ell|m
we introduce the notation

B_{\ell}(y):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\gamma}\leq y\\
p^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{B}_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\gamma}},\quad y\geq 2,

noting that
B_{m}(y)\rightarrow\infty
as
y\rightarrow\infty
by assumption.

Let

\nu:=\max_{\ell|m}\nu_{\ell}(b)
, and select
k_{0}=k_{0}(\delta,\omega(m))
sufficiently large so that

B_{m}(x_{k})\geq\omega(m)\max\{\delta^{-2},20(\nu+1)\}\text{ for all }k\geq k_{0}.

By the union bound we have

(22)

\max_{\ell|m}B_{\ell}(x_{k})\geq\frac{1}{\omega(m)}\sum_{\ell|m}B_{\ell}(x_{k})\geq\frac{1}{\omega(m)}B_{m}(x_{k})\geq\max\{\delta^{-2},20(\nu+1)\}.

Let
\ell_{0}
denote a prime factor of
m
that maximises the left-hand side of (
22
),
and write

\omega_{\ell_{0}}(n):=|\{p^{\gamma}||n:p^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{B}_{\ell_{0}}\}|=|\{p^{\gamma}||n:\ell_{0}|f(p^{\gamma})\}|,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

By Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle|\{n\leq x_{k}:\omega_{\ell_{0}}(n)<B_{\ell_{0}}(x_{k})/2\}|

\displaystyle\leq|\{n\leq x_{k}:|\omega_{\ell_{0}}(n)-B_{\ell_{0}}(x_{k})|>B_{\ell_{0}}(x_{k})/2\}|

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{B_{\ell_{0}}(x_{k})^{2}}\sum_{n\leq x_{k}}|\omega_{\ell_{0}}(n)-B_{\ell_{0}}(x_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll x_{k}/B_{\ell_{0}}(x_{k}).

Since
B_{\ell_{0}}(x_{k})\geq\delta^{-2}
, it follows that for all but
O(\delta^{2}x_{k})
integers
n\leq x_{k}
we have

(23)

\min\{\omega_{\ell_{0}}(n),\omega_{\ell_{0}}(n+a)\}\geq B_{\ell_{0}}(x_{k})/2\geq 10(\nu+1).

If
\delta
is small enough then by (
19
) there are
\geq\delta x_{k}/2
integers
n\leq x_{k}
for which

f(n+a)=f(n)+b,

and (
23
) holds. Now, since
f
is multiplicative we have
f(p^{\gamma})|f(n)
whenever
p^{\gamma}||n
, whence

\ell_{0}^{\omega_{\ell_{0}}(n)}|f(n),\text{ i.e. }\nu_{\ell_{0}}(f(n))\geq\omega_{\ell_{0}}(n).

Therefore, by (
23
),

\nu_{\ell_{0}}(f(n+a))\geq\min\{\nu_{\ell_{0}}(f(n)),\nu_{\ell_{0}}(f(n+a))\}\geq 10(\nu+1)>\nu_{\ell_{0}}(b).

On the other hand, since
\nu_{\ell_{0}}(f(n))\neq\nu_{\ell_{0}}(b)
the non-archimedean property of
\nu_{\ell_{0}}
yields

\nu_{\ell_{0}}(f(n+a))=\nu_{\ell_{0}}(f(n)+b)=\min\{\nu_{\ell_{0}}(f(n)),\nu_{\ell_{0}}(b)\}\leq\nu_{\ell_{0}}(b),

which is a contradiction. The claim follows.
∎

For
k\geq k_{0}(\delta)
consider a prime

6.
Local and global power maps

6.1.
The Fabrykowski-Subbarao Conjecture and a result of N. Jones

Let
\ell
be a prime. Recall that a a map
F:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is said to be a
local power map
modulo
\ell
if there is an integer
0\leq g_{\ell}<\ell-1
such that

F(n)\equiv n^{g_{\ell}}\pmod{\ell}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

If
F
is a completely multiplicative function then
F
is a local power map modulo
\ell
if and only if

(36)

F(p)\equiv p^{g_{\ell}}\pmod{\ell}\text{ for all }p\in\mathcal{P}.

For comparison, the conclusion of Proposition
5.4

states that the map
F(n)=f(n)^{D}
satisfies

(37)

F(p)\equiv p^{g_{\ell}}\pmod{\ell}\text{ for all }p\leq x_{k},\,p\notin\mathcal{T}_{\ell},

for each
\ell\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k;2b}\cap[\ell_{0},x_{k}^{1/A}]
with
\ell|f(\ell)
. That is,
f^{D}
behaves like a local power map at all such primes
\ell
, with the complications arising from the exceptional set
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
and the potential dependence of
g_{\ell}
on the scale
x_{k}
.

Fabrykowski and Subbarao conjectured that a multiplicative function that is a local power map modulo infinitely many primes must be a global power map, i.e., a monomial.

Conjecture 6.1

(Fabrykowski-Subbarao,
[
3
]
)
.

Suppose
F:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is a multiplicative function such that for infinitely many primes
\ell
,

F(n+\ell)\equiv F(n)\pmod{\ell}\text{ for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Then
there is a non-negative integer
k
such that
F(n)=n^{k}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Conjecture
6.1
is equivalent to the following statement:

Let
F:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be a completely multiplicative function for which there is an infinite sequence of primes
S_{F}
with the property that
for each
\ell\in S_{F}
there is an integer
0\leq g_{\ell}<\ell-1
such that

(38)

F(n)\equiv n^{g_{\ell}}\pmod{\ell}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then there is a non-negative integer
k
such that
F(n)=n^{k}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Conjecture
6.1
trivially implies this conjecture, so it suffices to show that if

satisfies the hypotheses of Conjecture
6.1
then it must be completely multiplicative and satisfy (
38
) for some
0\leq g_{\ell}<\ell-1
and each
\ell\in S_{F}
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
let
S_{F}(d):=\{\ell\in S_{F}:\ell\nmid d\}
, and let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. For each
\ell\in S_{F}(mn)
select distinct primes
q_{m},q_{n}
such that

for each

; such primes exist by Dirichlet’s theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions. It follows that
q_{m},q_{n}
are coprime, and moreover
mn\equiv q_{m}q_{n}\pmod{\ell}
. Thus,
F(mn)\equiv F(q_{m}q_{n})\pmod{\ell}
, and moreover by multiplicativity we get

F(q_{m}q_{n})=F(q_{m})F(q_{n}).

This implies that

F(mn)\equiv F(q_{m}q_{n})\pmod{\ell}\equiv F(q_{m})F(q_{n})\pmod{\ell}\equiv F(m)F(n)\pmod{\ell}.

We thus obtain that

\ell|(F(mn)-F(m)F(n))\text{ for all }\ell\in S_{F}(mn).

Since
S_{F}
is an infinite set, so is

, and we deduce that
F(mn)=F(m)F(n)
. As

were arbitrary,

must be completely multiplicative.

Next, fix
\ell\in S_{F}
and let

be a generator of
\Xi_{\ell}
.
By (a),

is a completely multiplicative function, and moreover since

is periodic modulo

we have

Thus,

is completely multiplicative, periodic modulo

and satisfies

whenever
\ell|n
. By definition,

must be a Dirichlet character modulo
\ell
.
Since
\chi_{\ell}
generates the group of such characters there must therefore exist

such that

\chi_{\ell}(F(n))=F^{\chi_{\ell}}(n)=\chi_{\ell}^{g_{\ell}}(n)=\chi_{\ell}(n^{g_{\ell}})\text{ for all }n.

Since
\chi_{\ell}
is injective on
\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
, we deduce that
F(n)\equiv n^{g_{\ell}}\pmod{\ell}
for all

, as required.
∎

Jones makes heavy use of Kummer theory, which is well-exposed in his article. We will merely quote as a black box the relevant facts about Kummer extensions, as needed.

For the time being, fix a prime

. We claim that we can find primes

with

, so we assume that

with

, we see that the set of primes

with

\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{T}(p)\cap\mathfrak{P}_{m}(X)}\frac{1}{\ell}>\frac{1}{100}\sum_{\ell\in\mathfrak{P}_{m}(X)}\frac{1}{\ell}

has a sum of reciprocals satisfying

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq P\\
\eqref{eq:largeTp}\text{ holds}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\ll_{\delta}1.

Let

Thus, we may select

) holds for

.
Furthermore, taking

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{1}^{r^{\prime}}\leq p\leq P\\

\eqref{eq:largeTp}\text{ fails}\\

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{1}\leq p\leq P\\

p_{1}|f(p)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{1}\leq p\leq P_{1}^{r^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\

\displaystyle=L_{f}(P)-L_{f}(P_{1})-B_{p_{1}}(P)-O_{\delta}\left(1\right)\geq L_{f}(P)-2L_{f}(P)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}L_{f}(P),

for

sufficiently large. Thus, we may select
p_{2}\in(\mathcal{L}_{f}\backslash\mathcal{S}_{\delta})\cap[P_{1}^{r^{\prime}},P]

.

Now, let
\zeta_{m}
be a primitive root of unity of order

Let also

. Following the proof of Case 1 of Lemma 5.8 of

[L_{m}:\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})]\geq m^{3}\text{ whenever $m>c_{f}$}.

Since also

,

[L_{m}:\mathbb{Q}]\geq m^{3}(m-1)\text{ for all }Y\leq m\leq Z.

For later reference, we note that the set of

that ramify in

are precisely those dividing the absolute discriminant

, and by

D_{L_{m}}\text{ divides }(p_{1}p_{2}f(p_{1}p_{2}))^{[L_{m}:\mathbb{Q}]}m^{5[L_{m}:\mathbb{Q}]}.

In particular,

.
Furthermore, as

, and since

for

leads to

\log D_{L_{m}}\leq[L_{m}:\mathbb{Q}]\log(P^{2(r+2)}m^{5})\ll m^{5}\log m,

a bound that we will invoke shortly.

Step 3: Estimation of
\mathcal{H}_{m}(X)
using Theorem
6.7

Having defined
L_{m}
, we now split

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{m}(X)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathfrak{P}_{m}(X)}\frac{1}{\ell}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathfrak{P}_{m}(X)\\
\ell\nmid p_{1}p_{2}f(p_{1}p_{2})\\
f(p_{i})^{D}\equiv p_{i}^{g_{\ell}}\pmod{\ell}\\
i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell|p_{1}p_{2}f(p_{1}p_{2})\\
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{m}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathfrak{P}_{m}(X)\\
p_{1}\text{ or }p_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}.

Since
p_{1},p_{2}\leq P
and
p_{1},p_{2}\notin\mathcal{S}_{\delta}
, we have

\max\{p_{1},p_{2},|f(p_{1})|,|f(p_{2})|\}\leq P^{r+1}<Y\leq m.

Thus,
\ell\nmid p_{1}p_{2}f(p_{1}p_{2})
for any prime
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{m}
, as each of these must obviously exceed
m
. In particular,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell|p_{1}p_{2}f(p_{1}p_{2})\\
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{m}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}=0.

Furthermore, by the union bound, the definition of
\mathcal{T}(p_{i})
and property (iii) above, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathfrak{P}_{m}(X)\\
p_{1}\text{ or }p_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}\leq\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{T}(p_{1})\cap\mathfrak{P}_{m}(X)}\frac{1}{\ell}+\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{T}(p_{2})\cap\mathfrak{P}_{m}(X)}\frac{1}{\ell}\leq\frac{1}{50}\sum_{\ell\in\mathfrak{P}_{m}(X)}\frac{1}{\ell}=\frac{1}{50}\mathcal{H}_{m}(X).

Thus, upon rearranging we deduce that

(50)

\mathcal{H}_{m}(X)\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathfrak{P}_{m}(X)\\
\ell\nmid p_{1}p_{2}f(p_{1}p_{2})\\
f(p_{i})^{D}\equiv p_{i}^{d_{\ell}}\pmod{\ell}\\
i=1,2\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}.

Note that the primes in the sum on the right-hand side of (
50
) are all unramified in
L_{m}
, and by
[
9
, Cor. 5.11]
there is a conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}_{4}
of prime ideals of
L_{m}
such that

\text{if }\ell\in\mathfrak{P}_{m}(X)\text{ and }\ell\nmid p_{1}p_{2}f(p_{1}p_{2})\text{ then }\text{Frob}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{C}_{4}.

Let now
2\leq\tilde{X}\leq X
be defined implicitly by

\log\tilde{X}=CZ^{5}(\log Z)^{3},

where
C>0
is a large absolute constant to be specified shortly. Splitting the sum in (
50
) at
X
and applying partial summation, we deduce that

(51)

\mathcal{H}_{m}(X)\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq X\\
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{m}\\
\text{Frob}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{C}_{4}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m<\ell\leq\tilde{X}\\
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{m}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}+\int_{\tilde{X}}^{X}\frac{\pi_{\mathcal{C}_{4}}(v,L_{m}/\mathbb{Q})}{v^{2}}dv+\frac{1}{m\log\tilde{X}}.

By Lemma
6.6
and partial summation,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m<\ell\leq\tilde{X}\\
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{m}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m<n\leq m^{10}\\
n\equiv 1\pmod{m}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}+\int_{m^{10}}^{\tilde{X}}\frac{\pi(v;m,1)}{v^{2}}dv+\frac{1}{m\log m}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\log m}{m}+\frac{1}{m}\log\log\tilde{X}\ll\frac{1}{m}\log Z.

Next, as
p_{1}\neq p_{2}
we have
[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m},p_{1}^{1/m},p_{2}^{1/m}):\mathbb{Q}]=m^{2}(m-1)
, so we may combine (
48
) with
[
9
, Lem. 5.12]
to obtain

\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{4}|}{\text{Gal}(L_{m}/\mathbb{Q})}\leq\frac{2}{m(m-1)}.

Moreover, for each
Y\leq m\leq Z
and each
\tilde{X}\leq v\leq X
(with
C
chosen sufficiently large in the definition of
\tilde{X}
) Theorem
6.7
gives

\pi_{\mathcal{C}_{4}}(v,L_{m}/\mathbb{Q})\ll\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{4}|}{|\text{Gal}(L_{m}/\mathbb{Q})|}\pi(v)\ll\frac{v}{m^{2}\log v}.

Inserting this bound into (
51

, and bounding trivially elsewhere, we obtain

\mathcal{H}_{m}(X)\ll\frac{1}{m^{2}}\int_{\tilde{X}}^{X}\frac{dv}{v\log v}+\frac{1}{m}(\log Z+1)\ll\frac{1}{m^{2}}\log\log X+\frac{1}{m}\log Z,

uniformly over primes
m\in[Y,Z]
.

Concluding the proof:

Summing over the primes
Y\leq m\leq Z
and inserting the resulting bound together with (
44

6.2
), we obtain

(52)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq X\\
\ell\in S_{f}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}\ll\left(\frac{\log Y}{\log Z}+\frac{1}{Y\log Y}\right)\log\log X+(\log Z)\log\left(\frac{\log Z}{\log Y}\right)+\log\log Y.

We now select

Y=(\log\log X)^{1/2},\quad Z=\exp((\log\log X)^{1/2}),

so that
Y\leq Z\leq(\log X)^{1/6}
, as needed. Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq X\\
\ell\in S_{f}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}\ll(\log\log X)^{1/2}\log\log\log X,

which is
o(\log\log X)
as
X\rightarrow\infty
, as claimed.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

The constraint
\log y\gg m^{5}(\log m)^{3}
required to apply Theorem
6.7
limited the choices of
Y
and
Z
in the above proof, and we may do much better by assuming the Extended Riemann Hypothesis.
As stated in Theorem
6.7
, for any conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\text{Gal}(L/K)
one has under ERH that

\pi_{\mathcal{C}}(y,L/\mathbb{Q})\ll\frac{|\mathcal{C}|}{|G|}\frac{y}{\log y}\text{ for }y\gg(\log D_{L})^{2}(\log\log D_{L})^{4}.

In particular, when
L=L_{m}
with
m\in[Y,Z]
as in the proof above, we may instead take
\tilde{X}=Z^{10}(\log Z)^{6}
.

Using
\tilde{X}>Y
and keeping track only of the primes
\tilde{X}<\ell\leq X
(which has the effect that the first term in each of the bounds (
44
) and (
51
) can be removed), the above proof gives

(53)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{X}<\ell\leq X\\
\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{f}\cap\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k;2b}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}\ll\left(\frac{\log Y}{\log Z}+\frac{1}{Y\log Y}\right)\log\log X+\frac{\log\log Z}{\log\tilde{X}},

which is valid as long as
X
is sufficiently large subject to the upper bound
X\leq x_{k}
.

Set now
J:=\lfloor\log\log X\rfloor
. For each
j_{0}\leq j\leq J
, where
j_{0}\gg_{\delta}1
, take

Y_{j}=j^{12},\,Z_{j}=\exp(j^{20}),\,\tilde{X}_{j}=Z_{j}^{10}(\log Z_{j})^{6}\text{ and }X_{j}=e^{e^{j}}.

Applying (
53
) with
X,\tilde{X},Y
and
Z
replaced by
X_{j},\tilde{X}_{j},Y_{j}
and
Z_{j}
, respectively, we find

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{j-1}<\ell\leq X_{j}\\
\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{f}\cap\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k;2b}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\exp(20j^{20})<\ell\leq X_{j}\\
\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{f}\cap\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k;2b}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{X}_{j}<\ell\leq X_{j}\\
\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{f}\cap\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k;2b}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}\ll\frac{1}{j^{10}}

(the constructions of the extensions

may be relativised to

, as long as

are sufficiently large with respect to
\delta
). Since
X_{J}\leq X<X_{J+1}
, summing over

yields

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq X\\
\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{f}\cap\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k;2b}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}\leq\sum_{j_{0}+1\leq j\leq J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{j-1}<\ell\leq X_{j}\\
\ell\in S_{f}\cap\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k;2b}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}+O_{\delta}(1)\ll\sum_{j>j_{0}}\frac{1}{j^{10}}+O_{\delta}(1)\ll_{\delta}1.

Combined with Lemma

, we thus obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq X\\
\ell\in S_{f}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq X\\
\ell\in S_{f}\cap\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k;2b}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq x_{k}\\
\ell\notin\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k;2b}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}\ll_{\delta}1,

uniformly over
k
, and thus over

. We hence deduce assuming ERH that

must be

-sparse, which contradicts the hypotheses of Proposition

.

Remark 6.9
.

One may ask whether the full strength of the ERH is actually needed in the conditional proof. In fact, the success of our method relies only on the convergence as
J\rightarrow\infty
of the series

\sum_{j_{0}<j\leq J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{j-1}<\ell\leq X_{j}\\
\ell\in S_{f}\cap\mathcal{G}_{\delta,k;2b}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}.

For this to work, it is enough to be able to take
Z_{j}
so that
\log Z_{j}\geq(\log\log X_{j})^{1+c}
for some
c>0
. While this range of parameters is not accessible using unconditional estimates in Theorem
6.7
(the range available is rather of the form
\log Z_{j}\geq(\log\log X_{j})^{c}
, with
c\in(0,1/5)
), it does hold if one assumes the existence of fairly narrow zero-free regions for all of the Hecke
L
-functions of suitable cyclic subfields of each of the extensions
L_{m}
,
m\in[Y,Z]
. For instance, applying
[
16
, Thm. 7.1]
, we see that if
\mathbb{Q}\subseteq E_{m}\subseteq L_{m}
has (cyclic) Galois subgroup
H_{m}\leq\text{Gal}(L_{m}/\mathbb{Q})
then we have

\psi_{\mathcal{C}}(x,L_{m}/\mathbb{Q}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq x\\
\text{Frob}_{p}\in\mathcal{C}\end{subarray}}\log p\ll\frac{|\mathcal{C}|}{|\text{Gal}(L_{m}/\mathbb{Q})|}x

as long as
m\leq Z\leq x^{1/6}
(so that
n_{L_{m}}\leq\log D_{L_{m}}\ll m^{5}\log m=o(x)
) and each of the Hecke
L
-functions
L(s,\chi,L_{m}/E_{m})
,
\chi\in H_{m}
, has no zeroes in a region

\text{Re}(s)>1-\delta_{m},\quad|\text{Im}(s)|\leq T_{m},\quad\delta_{m}:=\frac{C(\log m+\log\log x)}{\log x},\,T_{m}:=m^{5}(\log x)^{2}.

To access the range
\log Z_{j}\geq(\log\log X_{j})^{1+c}
it would suffice therefore to have access to a zero-free region in which
\text{Re}(s)>1-\frac{(\log\log X_{j})^{2}}{\log X_{j}}
and
|\text{Im}(s)|\ll\exp((\log\log X_{j})^{2+\varepsilon})
, for example. Any such zero-free region would be implied by one for the corresponding Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{L_{m}}
, and thus something far weaker than ERH would suffice.

The potential for such improvements under the assumption of weak zero-free regions suggests a possible unconditional improvement to Proposition
6.3
by invoking zero-density estimates. This seems reasonable in light of the fact that in constructing the extensions
L_{m}
we had a significant amount of flexibility in the choice of primes satisfying conditions (i)-(iii) of Step 2, and thus there are rather substantial collections of extensions that could be produced in this way. However, currently existing zero-density estimates for Dedekind zeta functions, such as those of
[
21
]
and
[
26
]
are effective for collections of fields in which the Galois group is either of fixed or very slowly growing size (see for instance
[
26
, Thm. 1.1]
, where the quality of the bound depends exponentially on the size
|G|
of the Galois group of the number fields in the collection). In our case, however,
\text{Gal}(L_{m}/\mathbb{Q})
grows polynomially with
m\in[Y,Z]
, which unfortunately makes these latter results unsuitable.

6.3.
Proof of Proposition
6.4

The proof of Proposition
6.4
relies on a few elementary sieve lemmas.

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
C>0
and let
S
be a set of prime powers that is
C
-sparse.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
write

n_{S}:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}||n\\
p^{\nu}\in S\end{subarray}}p^{\nu}

to denote the product of all prime factors of
n
that belong to
S
.
Then for any
\varepsilon>0
there is a
Z_{0}=Z_{0}(\varepsilon,C)
such that if
Z\geq Z_{0}
then for any
X
sufficiently large,

N_{S}(X;Z):=|\{n\leq X:n_{S}>Z\}\ll\varepsilon X.

Proof.

Since
n_{S}|n
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the claim is immediate if
Z>X
. We assume henceforth that
Z\leq X
. As in Section
4
, define

\omega_{S}(n):=|\{p^{k}||n:p^{k}\in S\}|,\quad E_{S}(Y):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq Y\\
p^{k}\in S\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}.

By assumption,
E_{S}(Y)\leq C
for all
Y\geq 2
.

Let
Y_{1}=\sqrt{\log Z}
and
Y_{2}=e^{\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\log Z}}
. For
Z_{0}
large enough and
Z\geq Z_{0}
we have
Y_{1}\geq 2C\geq 2E_{S}(X)
, so that by Lemma
3.1
,

|\{n\leq X:\omega_{S}(n)>Y_{1}\}|\leq|\{n\leq X:|\omega_{S}(n)-E_{S}(X)|>Y_{1}/2\}|\ll\frac{1}{Y_{1}^{2}}\sum_{n\leq X}|\omega_{S}(n)-E_{S}(X)|^{2}\ll\frac{X}{Y_{1}}.

Furthermore, we also note that

|\{n\leq X:\exists\ p^{\nu}||n,\ p^{\nu}\in S\text{ and }p^{\nu}>Y_{2}\}|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y_{2}<p^{\nu}\leq X\\
p^{\nu}\in S\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
p^{\nu}||n\end{subarray}}1\ll X\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}>Y_{2}\\
p^{\nu}\in S\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}.

Now for any
n\leq X
satisfying
\omega_{S}(n)\leq Y_{1}
and
\max\{p^{\nu}\in S:p^{\nu}||n\}\leq Y_{2}
,

n_{S}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}||n\\
p^{\nu}\in S\end{subarray}}p^{\nu}\leq Y_{2}^{\omega_{S}(n)}\leq Y_{2}^{Y_{1}}<Z.

Consequently, by the union bound

\displaystyle N_{S}(X;Z)

\displaystyle\leq|\{n\leq X:\omega_{S}(n)>Y_{1}\}|+|\{n\leq X:\exists\,p^{\nu}||n,\,p^{\nu}\in S\text{ and }p^{\nu}>Y_{2}\}|

\displaystyle\ll X\left(Y_{1}^{-1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\nu}>Y_{2}\\
p^{\nu}\in S\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}\right)\ll\varepsilon X,

as long as
Z\geq Z_{0}
and
Z_{0}
is chosen large enough, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 6.11
.

Given
\eta\in(0,1)
, define
\mathcal{R}_{\eta}
to be the set of primes
\ell
such that

|\{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{\times}:\,a\text{ is a primitive root}\}|<\eta(\ell-1).

Then as
X\rightarrow\infty
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq X\\
\ell\in\mathcal{R}_{\eta}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}\ll\eta^{100}\log\log X.

Proof.

By partial summation, it suffices to show that

|\mathcal{R}_{\eta}\cap[2,y]|\ll\eta^{100}\frac{y}{\log y},\quad y\geq y_{0}.

It is a classical fact that there are
\phi(\ell-1)
distinct primitive roots modulo a prime
\ell
. Thus, defining the multiplicative function
g(n):=\left(n/\phi(n)\right)^{100}
, we have

\ell\in\mathcal{R}_{\eta}\text{ if and only if }g(\ell-1)>\eta^{-100}.

Note that
g(p^{k})=1+O(1/p)
for all prime powers
p^{k}
, so that
g(n)\leq d(n)
for all sufficiently large
n\geq 1
. Markov’s inequality and Lemma
3.7

thus combine to yield

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{\eta}\cap[2,y]|\leq\eta^{100}\sum_{\ell\leq y}g(\ell-1)\leq\eta^{100}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y-1\\
p|(n+1)\Rightarrow p>y^{1/10}\end{subarray}}g(n)

\displaystyle\ll\eta^{100}\frac{y}{\log y}\exp\left(\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{g(p)-1_{p\leq y^{1/10}}}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\eta^{100}\frac{y}{\log y},

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 6.12
.

Let

\sum_{p:|f(p)|\neq p}\frac{1}{p}=\infty,\quad\sum_{p:|f(p)|\neq 1}\frac{1}{p}=\infty\text{ and }\sum_{p:f(p)=0}\frac{1}{p}<\infty.

Assume one of the following:

(a)

Conjecture
1.5
holds,

(b)

ERH holds and
\sum_{p|f(p)}p^{-1}=\infty
, or

(c)

d_{\text{Dir}}(\{p\in\mathcal{P}:p|f(p)\})>0
.

Then for all
C>0
we have

\delta(\{n\in\mathbb{N}:f(n)\neq 0,\,|f(n+1)-f(n)|\leq C\})=0.

Proof.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that

\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
f(n)\neq 0\\
|f(n+1)-f(n)|\leq C\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}=\delta>0.

By the pigeonhole principle, there is an integer
b\in[-C,C]
such that

\limsup_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
f(n)\neq 0\\
f(n+1)-f(n)=b\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}\geq\frac{\delta}{2\lfloor C\rfloor+1},

and therefore

(60)

\delta(\{n\in\mathbb{N}:f(n)\neq 0,\,f(n+1)=f(n)+b\})\neq 0.

Suppose first that
b\neq 0
. Assuming either (a),(b) or (c) in conjunction with Theorem
1.6
, we find that

\sum_{p:|f(p)|\neq p}\frac{1}{p}<\infty,

contradicting the first of the conditions in (
59
).

On the other hand, if
b=0
then by
[
19
, Thm. 1.1]
there are no multiplicative functions satisfying (
59
) for which (
60
) holds (here the condition
f(n)\neq 0
is needed). Thus, in either case we obtain a contradiction, and the claim follows.
∎

Next, we record how the assumption
f(n)\rightarrow\infty
is related to the frequency of prime power values
f(p^{\nu})=1
.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be a multiplicative function. Then

(61)

\limsup_{C\rightarrow\infty}\,\overline{d}\{n\in\mathbb{N}:1\leq f(n)\leq C\}>0

if and only if

(62)

\sum_{p^{\nu}:f(p^{\nu})\neq 1}\frac{1}{p^{\nu}}<\infty.

Proof.

If (
62
) fails then Lemma
4.3
(more precisely Remark
4.4
) implies that

d\{n\in\mathbb{N}:1\leq f(n)\leq C\}=0

for all
C>0
, as desired.

Conversely, suppose that (
62
) holds. For each
y\geq 1
and
C\geq 1
we of course have

\frac{1}{y}|\{n\leq y:1\leq f(n)\leq C\}|\geq\frac{1}{y}|\{n\leq y:f(n)=1\}|\geq\frac{1}{y}|\{n\leq y:\mu^{2}(n)=1,\,p||n\Rightarrow f(p)=1\}|.

By a zero-dimensional sieve, it is easily verified that as
y\rightarrow\infty
this last expression is

\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
f(p)\neq 1\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+o(1)\geq\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\exp\left(-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
f(p)\neq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\right)+o(1)>0.

Hence, (
61
) holds as required.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is multiplicative, such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
f(p)\neq p\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}=\infty.

Further, suppose that for each
C>0
we have

d\{n\in\mathbb{N}:f(n)\leq C\}=0.

By Lemma
8.2
, it follows that

\sum_{p:f(p)\neq 1}\frac{1}{p}=\infty.

Since
f(p)\neq 0
for all
p
, Corollary
8.1
implies that

\delta\{n\in\mathbb{N}:|f(n+1)-f(n)|\leq C\}=0\text{ for all }C>0

under the assumption of ERH, assuming furthermore that
\sum_{p|f(p)}1/p=\infty
, as claimed.
∎

9.
Proof of Proposition
1.8

To prove Proposition
1.8
we will construct the prime sets
\mathcal{T}
via the probabilistic method.

Let us introduce the following notation. Given a set of primes
\mathcal{T}
we define the Liouville-type function
\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}

to be the completely multiplicative function such that for each prime
p
,

\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(p)=\begin{cases}-1\text{ if }p\in\mathcal{T}\\
+1\text{ if }p\notin\mathcal{T}.\end{cases}

Fix the sparse set
\mathcal{S}
. Let
(X_{p})_{p\notin\mathcal{S}}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables on an auxiliary probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{B},\mathbb{P})
with

\mathbb{P}(X_{p}=0)=\mathbb{P}(X_{p}=1)=1/2.

We now define a random set of primes

(63)

\mathbf{T}:=\{p\notin\mathcal{S}:X_{p}=1\}.

In the next several lemmas we collect some facts about
\mathbf{T}
.

(In the remainder of this section we follow the convention that the trivial character is primitive.)

Lemma 9.1
.

For
k\geq 1
define the sequence
x_{k}:=e^{e^{k^{3}}}
. Then with the above scheme, the following statements hold almost surely (i.e. on a set
A\subseteq\Omega
with
\mathbb{P}(A)=1
):

(a)

for each
k\geq k_{0}(\omega)
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathbf{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\geq\frac{1}{4}\log\log x_{k},

(b)

for every real primitive Dirichlet character
\chi\pmod{q}
with
1<q\leq k
,

\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathbf{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(p)}{p}\right|\leq\frac{1}{10}\log\log x_{k},\quad k\geq k_{0}(\omega).

Proof.

By linearity of expectation we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathbf{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\notin\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\frac{X_{p}}{p}\right)=\sum_{p\leq x_{k}}\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{p})}{p}+O\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\log\log x_{k}+O(1).

Therefore, by independence the variance satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathbf{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\log\log x_{k}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p,q\leq x_{k}\\
p,q\notin\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{p}X_{q})}{pq}-\frac{1}{4}(\log\log x_{k})^{2}+O(\log\log x_{k})

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\notin\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{p})}{p}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\leq x_{k}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}-\frac{1}{4}\log\log x_{k}^{2}+O(\log\log x_{k})

\displaystyle=O(\log\log x_{k}).

By Chebyshev’s inequality, we deduce that

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathbf{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\leq\frac{1}{4}\log\log x_{k}\right)\leq 16\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathbf{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\log\log x_{k}\right)^{2}}{(\log\log x_{k})^{2}}\ll\frac{1}{\log\log x_{k}}\ll\frac{1}{k^{3}}.

Next,
suppose
1<q\leq k\leq\log\log x_{k}
and let
\chi
be a real primitive character modulo
q
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathbf{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(p)}{p}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p,q\leq x_{k}\\
p,q\notin\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(pq)}{pq}+O\left(\sum_{p\leq x_{k}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\leq x_{k}}\frac{\chi(p)}{p}+O(1)\right)^{2}+O(1).

By the Siegel-Walfisz theorem and partial summation (see e.g.
[
8
, Cor. 5.29]
),

(64)

\sum_{p\leq x_{k}}\frac{\chi(p)}{p}\ll\sum_{p\leq e^{k}}\frac{1}{p}+\int_{e^{k}}^{x_{k}}\left|\sum_{p\leq v}\chi(p)\right|\frac{dv}{v^{2}}\ll\log k+k^{1/2}k^{-10}\ll\log\log\log x_{k}.

Combining these two estimates and using Chebyshev’s inequality, we find

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathbf{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(p)}{p}\right|\geq\frac{1}{10}\log\log x_{k}\right)\ll\frac{(\log\log\log x_{k})^{2}}{(\log\log x_{k})^{2}}\ll\frac{(\log k)^{2}}{k^{4}}\ll\frac{1}{k^{3}}.

Since there are
\ll k
real primitive characters with conductor
\leq k
, by the union bound we deduce that the probability that either (a) fails or that (b) fails for some
\chi\pmod{q}
,
1<q\leq k
is

\ll\frac{1}{k^{3}}+k\cdot\frac{1}{k^{3}}\ll\frac{1}{k^{2}}.

By the Borel-Cantelli lemma (see
[
4
, VIII.3]
), we deduce that, on a set of
\omega\in\Omega
with probability
1
, both

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathbf{T}(\omega)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\geq\frac{1}{4}\log\log x_{k}+O(1)

and also

\max_{1<q\leq k}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathbf{T}(\omega)\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(p)}{p}\right|\leq\frac{1}{10}\log\log x_{k}

hold for all
k\geq k_{0}(\omega)
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 9.2
.

For every real primitive character
\chi
,

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{D}(\lambda_{\mathbf{T}},\chi;x)=\infty

almost surely.

Proof.

Let
\chi
be a real primitive character with modulus
q\geq 1
. Given
k\geq q
we have

(65)

\mathbb{D}(\lambda_{\mathbf{T}},\chi;x_{k})^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathbf{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{1+\chi(p)}{p}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\notin\mathbf{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{1-\chi(p)}{p}=\sum_{p\leq x_{k}}\frac{1-\chi(p)}{p}+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathbf{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(p)}{p}.

If
q=1
then by Lemma
9.1
the right-hand side of (
65
) is simply

2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathbf{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\log x_{k}

for all
k\geq k_{0}(\omega)
almost surely.

If
1<q\leq k
then combining Lemma
9.1
with the Siegel-Walfisz theorem (as in (
64
)), the right-hand side of (
65
) is bounded from below as

\displaystyle\geq\log\log x_{k}-\left|\sum_{p\leq x_{k}}\frac{\chi(p)}{p}\right|-2\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x_{k}\\
p\in\mathbf{T}\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(p)}{p}\right|+O(1)

\displaystyle\geq\log\log x_{k}+O(\log\log\log x_{k})-\frac{1}{5}\log\log x_{k}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\log x_{k}

for
k\geq k_{0}(\omega)
almost surely. Thus, we obtain

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\text{ real primitive}\\
q\leq k\end{subarray}}\mathbb{D}(\lambda_{\mathbf{T}},\chi;x_{k})^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\log x_{k},\quad k\geq k_{0}(\omega)

almost surely.

Therefore, for any real primitive character
\chi
modulo
q_{0}
, any
k\geq\max\{k_{0}(\omega),q_{0}\}
and any
x\geq x_{k}
,

\mathbb{D}(\lambda_{\mathbf{T}},\chi;x)^{2}\geq\inf_{q\leq k}\mathbb{D}(\lambda_{\mathbf{T}},\chi_{q};x_{k})^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\log x_{k}+O(1)

almost surely. Taking
k\rightarrow\infty
forces
x\rightarrow\infty
, and the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 9.3
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is any countable collection of subsets of primes,

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{C})=0.

In particular, for any set
A
with
\mathbb{P}(A)>0
, the collection

\{\mathbf{T}(\omega):\omega\in A\}

is uncountable.

Proof.

For any fixed set of primes
\mathcal{T}
and any
N\geq 1
the probability

(66)

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{T}=\mathcal{T})\leq\mathbb{P}(\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq N\\
p\notin\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\{X_{p}=1_{p\in T}\})\leq 2^{-\pi_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}(N)},

where
\pi_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}(N):=\pi(N)-|\mathcal{S}\cap[2,N]|
. Since
\mathcal{S}
is sparse we have
\pi_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}(N_{k})\geq\tfrac{2}{3}\pi(N_{k})
on an infinite increasing sequence
(N_{k})_{k}
. Taking
N\rightarrow\infty
along
(N_{k})_{k}
, we deduce that
\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{T}=\mathcal{T})=0
. Thus, by countable additivity of the measure
\mathbb{P}
we find

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{C})=\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{T}=\mathcal{T})=0.

This establishes the first claim.

For the second, observe that if
\{\mathbf{T}(\omega):\omega\in A\}
were countable then there would exist a collection
\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{T}_{j})_{j}
such that

\omega\in A\Rightarrow\mathbf{T}(\omega)\in\mathcal{C}.

By the previous claim,

0<\mathbb{P}(A)\leq\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{C})=0,

which is a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.8
.

Set
\mathcal{S}_{d}:=\mathcal{S}\backslash\{p:p|d\}
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a realisation of the random set
\mathbf{T}
defined in (
63
). By Lemma
9.2
, with probability
1
we have

(67)

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{D}(\lambda_{\mathcal{T}},\chi;X)=\infty

for each fixed real primitive character
\chi
, and Lemma
9.3
shows that the collection of
\mathcal{T}
for which this holds is uncountable. For the remainder of the proof we fix some realisation
\mathcal{T}
satisfying (
67
).

Define now the set

\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{T}}:=\{dpm:\,p\in S_{d},\,p|m\Rightarrow p\notin\mathcal{S}\text{ and }\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(m)=+1\}.

Let
\tilde{d}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p||d\end{subarray}}p
. Thus, if
d=1
or
d
is squarefull then
\tilde{d}=1
; otherwise,
\tilde{d}>1
. Define also
k:=db/a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
k\neq 0
.
We define the completely multiplicative function
f=f_{\mathcal{T}}
at primes via

f(p)=\begin{cases}\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(p)p&\text{ if $p\notin\mathcal{S}$}\\
P^{+}(|k|)&\text{ if $p=P^{+}(\tilde{d})$}\\
pk/f(P^{+}(\tilde{d}))&\text{ if $p\in\mathcal{S}_{d}$}\\
1&\text{ if $p|d$, $p\neq P^{+}(\tilde{d})$.}\end{cases}

Since
k\neq 1
by assumption,
f
clearly satisfies items (i) and (ii). Note furthermore that
f(d)=f(P^{+}(\tilde{d}))
divides
k
, and is thus a divisor of
b
as in item (iii). It thus remains to show that
f
so-defined satisfies item (iv).

Consider the set

\mathcal{N}^{\prime}:=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n,n+a\in\mathcal{N}\}.

If
n\in\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
then we can find primes
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
and
m_{1},m_{2}
not divisible by any prime
p\in\mathcal{S}
, such that

n=m_{1}p_{1}d,\quad n+a=m_{2}p_{2}d=d(m_{1}p_{1}+a/d),

and
\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(m_{1})=\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(m_{2})=+1
.
By construction we have
f(d)=f(P^{+}(\tilde{d}))
. Thus,

\displaystyle f(n+a)

\displaystyle=f(d)f(m_{2})f(p_{2})=f(P^{+}(\tilde{d}))\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(m_{2})m_{2}(k/f(P^{+}(\tilde{d})))p_{2}=km_{2}p_{2},

\displaystyle f(n)+b

\displaystyle=f(d)f(m_{1})f(p_{1})+k_{1}k_{2}a/d=k(\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(m_{1})m_{1}p_{1}+a/d)=k(m_{1}p_{1}+a/d).

Since
m_{2}p_{2}=m_{1}p_{1}+a/d
, we deduce that
f(n+a)=f(n)+b
, and so

\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{f,a,b}.

To complete the proof we will show that
\overline{\delta}(\mathcal{N}^{\prime})>0
.

Since
|\mathcal{S}_{d}|\geq 2
and the sets

\mathcal{N}_{q}:=\{dqm:p|m\Rightarrow p\notin\mathcal{S}\text{ and }\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(m)=+1\},\quad q\in\mathcal{S}_{d}

are mutually disjoint, it suffices to show that there are
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
with
p_{1}\neq p_{2}
and
(p_{1}p_{2},a)=1
such that

\{n\in\mathcal{N}_{p_{1}}:n+a\in\mathcal{N}_{p_{2}}\}\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{\prime}

has positive upper logarithmic density. To this end, we will estimate

\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\in\mathcal{N}_{p_{1}},\,n+a\in\mathcal{N}_{p_{2}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}=\frac{1}{d\log x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n^{\prime}\leq x/d\\
n^{\prime}=m_{1}p_{1},\,n^{\prime}+a/d=m_{2}p_{2}\\
(m_{1}m_{2},\mathcal{S})=1\\
\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(m_{1})=\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(m_{2})=+1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}.

By the Chinese remainder theorem, we can find
0\leq u<p_{2}
so that

n^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{p_{1}},\,n^{\prime}+a/d\equiv 0\pmod{p_{2}}\text{ if and only if }n^{\prime}\equiv up_{1}\pmod{p_{1}p_{2}}.

Define the multiplicative function
g(n):=1_{(n,\mathcal{S})=1}
.
Using the changes of variables

n^{\prime}=mp_{1}p_{2}+up_{1},\quad v:=(up_{1}+a/d)/p_{2}\in\mathbb{Z},

and the identity
1_{\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(m)=+1}=(1+\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(m))/2
, we can recast the above expression as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4d\log x}\sum_{m\leq x/dp_{1}p_{2}}\frac{g(mp_{2}+u)g(mp_{1}+v)}{m}(1+\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(mp_{2}+u))(1+\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(mp_{1}+v))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\{0,1\}}\frac{1}{4d\log x}\sum_{m\leq x/dp_{1}p_{2}}\frac{g\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}^{\eta_{1}}(mp_{2}+u)g\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}^{\eta_{2}}(mp_{1}+v)}{m}.

Since
g(p)=1
for all
p
outside the sparse set
\mathcal{S}
, the pretentious triangle inequality (
15
) implies that for any fixed, real primitive character
\chi
,

\mathbb{D}(g\lambda_{\mathcal{T}},\chi;x)\geq\mathbb{D}(\lambda_{\mathcal{T}},\chi;x)-\mathbb{D}(g,1;x)=\mathbb{D}(\lambda_{\mathcal{T}},\chi;x)-\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\right)^{1/2}=\mathbb{D}(\lambda_{\mathcal{T}},\chi;x)-O(1).

Combining (
67
) with Lemma
3.4
, we deduce therefore that

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{|t|\leq x}\mathbb{D}(g\lambda_{\mathcal{T}},\chi;x)=\infty

for every fixed Dirichlet character
\chi
(real or complex). As
d,p_{1},p_{2},u
and
v
are all fixed, Theorem
3.5
yields

\frac{1}{4d\log x}\sum_{m\leq x/dp_{1}p_{2}}\frac{g\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}^{\eta_{1}}(mp_{2}+u)g\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}^{\eta_{2}}(mp_{1}+v)}{m}=o(1)

for each
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\neq(0,0)
. Thus,

(68)

\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\in\mathcal{N}_{p_{1}},\,n+a\in\mathcal{N}_{p_{2}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}=\frac{1}{4d\log x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq x\\
(mp_{1}+u,\mathcal{S})=(mp_{2}+v,\mathcal{S})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}+o(1).

By partial summation, it now suffices to show that the set

\{m\in\mathbb{N}:(mp_{2}+u,\mathcal{S})=(mp_{1}+v,\mathcal{S})=1\}

has positive density.
By a zero-dimensional sieve this density is

\frac{1}{p_{1}p_{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{1}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{2}}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{S}\\
p\nmid p_{1}p_{2}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right),

which is
>0
as
2\notin\mathcal{S}
.

When
\mathcal{T}=\emptyset
the condition
\lambda_{\mathcal{T}}(m)=+1
in the definition of
\mathcal{N}
is trivial, and (
68
) holds with the prefactor
1/4
replaced by
1
. The same conclusion follows.
∎

.

The above proof may easily be modified to allow

, though the analysis differs according to whether
2
divides
d
and/or

. We leave this to the interested reader.

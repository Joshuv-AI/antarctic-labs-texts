Abstract
. We obtain several estimates for bilinear form with Kloosterman sums. Such results can be interpreted as a measure of cancellations amongst with parameters from short intervals. In particular, for certain ranges of parameters we improve some recent results of Blomer, Fouvry, Kowalski, Michel, and Milićević (2014) and Fouvry, Kowalski and Michel (2014).

11D79, 11L07

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a sufficiently large
prime. For integers
m
and
n
we define
the Kloosterman sum

{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(m,n)=\sum_{x=1}^{p-1}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(mx+n\overline{x}_{p}\right),

where
\overline{x}_{p}
is the multiplicative inverse of
x
modulo
p
and

{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(z)=\exp(2\pi iz/p).

Furthermore, given two intervals

{\mathcal{I}}=[K+1,K+M],\ {\mathcal{J}}=[L+1,L+N]\subseteq[1,p-1],

and two sequences of weights
{\mathcal{A}}=\{\alpha_{m}\}_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}=\{\beta_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}
, we define the bilinear sums of Kloosterman sums

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})=\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}\alpha_{m}\beta_{n}{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(mn,1).

We also consider the following special cases

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})={\mathcal{S}}_{p}\left({\mathcal{A}},\{1\}_{n=1}^{N};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}}\right)=\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}\alpha_{m}{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(mn,1),\\
&{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})={\mathcal{S}}_{p}\left(\{1\}_{m=1}^{M},\{1\}_{n=1}^{N};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}}\right)=\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(mn,1),\\
&{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{I}})={\mathcal{S}}_{p}\left(\{1\}_{m=1}^{M},\emptyset;{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}}\right)=\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(m,1).\end{split}

Making the change of variable
x\to nx\pmod{p}
, one immediately observes that
{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(mn,1)={\mathcal{K}}_{p}(m,n)
, thus we also have

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})=\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(m,n).

We aslo define, for real
\sigma>0
,

\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{\sigma}=\left(\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}|\alpha_{m}|^{\sigma}\right)^{1/\sigma}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\|{\mathcal{B}}\|_{\sigma}=\left(\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}|\beta_{n}|^{\sigma}\right)^{1/\sigma}

with the usual convention

\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{\infty}=\max_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}|\alpha_{m}|\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\|{\mathcal{B}}\|_{\infty}=\max_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}|\beta_{n}|.

By the Weil bound we have

|{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(m,n)|\leq 2p^{1/2},

see
[
7
, Theorem 11.11]
. Hence

(1.1)

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})\leq 2\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{1}\|{\mathcal{B}}\|_{1}p^{1/2}.

We are interested in studying cancellations amongst Kloosterman sums and
thus improvements of the trivial bound (
1.1
).

Throughout the paper, as usual
A\ll B
is equivalent to the inequality
|A|\leq cB

with some constant
c>0

(all implied constants are absolute throughout the paper).

2.
Previous results

First we note that by a very special case of a much more general
result of Fouvry, Michel, Rivat and Sárközy
[
4
, Lemma 2.3]
we have

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{I}})\ll p\log p

which for
M\geq p^{1/2}\log p

improves the trivial bound
{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{I}})\leq 2Mp^{1/2}
following from (
1.1
).
Recently, Fouvry, Kowalski,
Michel, Raju, Rivat and Soundararajan
[
5
, Corollary 1.6]

have given the bound

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{I}})\ll Mp^{1/2}(\log p)^{-\eta}

provided that
M\geq p^{1/2}(\log p)^{-\eta}
with some absolute
constant
\eta>0
.

It is also easy to derive from
[
13
, Theorem 7]
that

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})\ll MNp^{1/4}+M^{1/2}N^{1/2}p^{1+o(1)},

which improves the trivial bound from (
1.1
) for

MN\geq p^{1+\varepsilon}
for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
.

The sums
{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})

have been estimated by Fouvry, Kowalski and Michel
[
3
, Theorem 1.17]

as a part of a much more general result about sums of so-called
trace functions
.
Then, by
[
3
, Theorem 1.17(2)]
, for initial intervals

{\mathcal{I}}=[1,M]
and
{\mathcal{J}}=[1,N]
,
we have

(2.1)

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})\leq\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{1}p^{1+o(1)}.

Furthermore, by a result of Blomer, Fouvry, Kowalski, Michel, and Milićević
[
1
, Theorem 6.1]
,
also for an initial interval
{\mathcal{I}}
and an arbitrary interval
{\mathcal{J}}
, with

MN\leq p^{3/2}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad M\leq N^{2}

we have

(2.2)

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})\leq\left(\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{1}\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{2}\right)^{1/2}M^{1/12}N^{7/12}p^{3/4+o(1)}.

One can also find in
[
1
,
3
,
10
]
a series of other bounds
on the sums
{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})
and also on more general sums.

Finally, Khan
[
9
]
has given a nontrivial estimate
for the analogue of
{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{I}})
modulo a fixed prime power which is nontrivial
already for
M\geq p^{\varepsilon}
.

3.
New results

We start with the sums
{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})
and present a bound which improves (
1.1
) already for
MN\geq p^{1/2+\varepsilon}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

We have,

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})\ll(p+MN)p^{o(1)}.

We now estimate
{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})
.

Theorem 3.2
.

We have,

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})\ll\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{2}N^{1/2}p.

We can re-write the bounds (
2.1
) and (
2.2
)
in terms of the
\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{\infty}
as

(3.1)

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})\ll\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{\infty}Mp^{1+o(1)}

and

(3.2)

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})\ll\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{\infty}M^{5/6}N^{7/12}p^{3/4+o(1)},

respectively, and the bound of Theorem
3.2
as

(3.3)

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})\ll\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{\infty}M^{1/2}N^{1/2}p^{1+o(1)}.

We now see for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
the bound (
3.3
)
improves (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) for

N<Mp^{-\varepsilon}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad M^{4}N\geq p^{3+\varepsilon}

respectively,
and also applies to intervals
{\mathcal{I}}
and
{\mathcal{J}}
at arbitrary positions.

4.
Preparations

We need the following simple result.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
integers
X
and
Y
with
1\leq X,Y<p
,
the congruence

xy\equiv 1\pmod{p},\qquad 1\leq|x|\leq X,\ 1\leq|y|\leq Y

has at most
(XY/p+1)p^{o(1)}
solutions.

Proof.

Writing
xy\equiv 1\pmod{p}
as
xy=1+kp
for some
integer
k
with
|k|\leq XY/p
and using the bound on the divisor function,
see
[
7
, Equation (1.81)]
,
we get the desired estimate.

\sqcap
\sqcup

We also need the following well-known result, which dates back to
Vinogradov
[
15
, Chapter 6, Problem 14.a]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For arbitrary set
{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
and complex
numbers
\varphi_{u}
and
\psi_{v}
with

\sum_{u\in{\mathcal{U}}}|\varphi_{u}|^{2}\leq\varPhi\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}|\psi_{v}|^{2}\leq\varPsi,

we have

\left|\sum_{u\in{\mathcal{U}}}\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}\varphi_{u}\psi_{v}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(uv)\right|\leq\sqrt{\varPhi\varPsi p}.

5.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

For an integer
u
we define

\|u\|_{p}=\min_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|u-kp|

as the distance to the closest integer.
Then, changing the order of summation, we obtain

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})\ll\sum_{x=1}^{p-1}\min\left\{M,\frac{p}{\|x\|_{p}}\right\}\min\left\{N,\frac{p}{\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}}\right\},

see
[
7
, Bound (8.6)]
.
We now write

(5.1)

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})\ll MNS_{1}+MpS_{2}+NpS_{3}+p^{2}S_{4},

where

\begin{split}S_{1}&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
\|x\|_{p}\leq p/M\\
\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}\leq p/N\end{subarray}}^{p-1}1,\qquad S_{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
\|x\|_{p}\leq p/M\\
\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}>p/N\end{subarray}}^{p-1}\frac{1}{\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}},\\
S_{3}&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
\|x\|_{p}>p/M\\
\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}\leq p/N\end{subarray}}^{p-1}\frac{1}{\|x\|_{p}},\qquad S_{4}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
\|x\|_{p}>p/M\\
\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}>p/N\end{subarray}}^{p-1}\frac{1}{\|x\|_{p}\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}}.\end{split}

By Lemma
4.1
we immediately obtain

(5.2)

S_{1}\leq(p/MN+1)p^{o(1)}.

To estimate
S_{2}
, we define
I=\left\lceil\log p\right\rceil
and write

S_{2}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{I}S_{2,i},

where

\begin{split}S_{2,i}&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
\|x\|_{p}\leq p/M\\
e^{i+1}p/N\geq\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}>e^{i}p/N\end{subarray}}^{p-1}\frac{1}{\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}}\\
&\ll e^{-i}Np^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
\|x\|_{p}\leq p/M\\
e^{i+1}p/N\geq\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}>e^{i}p/N\end{subarray}}^{p-1}1\ll e^{-i}Np^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
\|x\|_{p}\leq p/M\\
\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}\leq e^{i+1}p/N\end{subarray}}^{p-1}1.\end{split}

Now we use Lemma
4.1
again to derive

(5.3)

\begin{split}S_{2}&\leq\sum_{i=0}^{I}\left(M^{-1}+e^{-i}Np^{-1}\right)p^{o(1)}\\
&\ll(I+1)M^{-1}p^{o(1)}+Np^{-1+o(1)}\sum_{i=0}^{I}e^{-i}\\
&\ll(I+1)M^{-1}p^{o(1)}+Np^{-1+o(1)}\\
&\ll M^{-1}p^{o(1)}+Np^{-1+o(1)}.\end{split}

Similarly we obtain

(5.4)

S_{3}\leq N^{-1}p^{o(1)}+Mp^{-1+o(1)}.

Finally, we write

S_{4}\leq\sum_{i,j=0}^{I}S_{4,i,j},

where

\begin{split}S_{4,i,j}&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
e^{i+1}p/M\geq\|x\|_{p}>e^{i}p/M\\
e^{j+1}p/N\geq\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}>e^{j}p/N\end{subarray}}^{p-1}\frac{1}{\|x\|_{p}\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}}\\
&\ll e^{-i-j}MNp^{-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
e^{i+1}p/M\geq\|x\|_{p}>e^{i}p/M\\
e^{j+1}p/N\geq\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}>e^{j}p/N\end{subarray}}^{p-1}1\\
&\ll e^{-i-j}MNp^{-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
\|x\|_{p}\leq e^{i+1}p/M\\
\|\overline{x}_{p}\|_{p}\leq e^{j+1}p/N\end{subarray}}^{p-1}1.\end{split}

Applying Lemma
4.1
one more time, we obtain

\begin{split}S_{4,i,j}&\ll e^{-i-j}MNp^{-2}\left(e^{i+j}p/MN+1\right)p^{o(1)}\\
&=(p^{-1}+e^{-i-j}MNp^{-2})p^{o(1)}.\end{split}

Hence

(5.5)

\begin{split}S_{4}&\leq\sum_{i,j=0}^{I}(p^{-1}+e^{-i-j}MNp^{-2})p^{o(1)}\\
&\leq(I+1)^{2}p^{-1+o(1)}+MNp^{-2+o(1)}\\
&\leq p^{-1+o(1)}+MNp^{-2+o(1)}.\end{split}

Combining (
5.2
), (
5.3
), (
5.4
) and (
5.5
)
we obtain the result.

6.
Proof of Theorem
3.2

Changing the order of summation, as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
we obtain

\begin{split}{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{A}};{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})&=\left|\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sum_{x=1}^{p-1}\alpha_{m}\gamma_{x}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(m\overline{x}_{p})\right|,\end{split}

where

|\gamma_{x}|\leq\min\left\{N,\frac{p}{\|x\|_{p}}\right\}.

Thus, similarly to the proof of Theorem
3.1
we define
I=\left\lceil\log p\right\rceil
and write

(6.1)

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}({\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}})\ll|S_{0}|+\sum_{i=1}^{I}|S_{i}|,

where

\begin{split}S_{0}&=\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
\|x\|_{p}\leq p/N\end{subarray}}^{p-1}\alpha_{m}\gamma_{x}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(m\overline{x}_{p}),\\
S_{i}&=\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
e^{i+1}p/N\geq\|x\|_{p}>e^{i}p/N\end{subarray}}^{p-1}\alpha_{m}\gamma_{x}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(m\overline{x}_{p}),\qquad i=1,\ldots,I.\end{split}

Now use Lemma
4.2
, we have

(6.2)

|S_{0}|\ll\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{2}N\sqrt{(p/N+1)p}\leq\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{2}N^{1/2}p.

Also, for
i=1,\ldots,I
, using that if
e^{i+1}p/N\geq\|x\|_{p}>e^{i}p/N

then
\gamma_{x}\ll Ne^{-i}
, hence, by Lemma
4.2
, we obtain

\begin{split}S_{i}&=\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
e^{i+1}p/N\geq\|x\|_{p}>e^{i}p/N\end{subarray}}^{p-1}\alpha_{m}\gamma_{x}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(m\overline{x}_{p})\\
&\ll\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{2}(N^{2}e^{-2i}e^{i}p/N)^{1/2}p^{1/2}=e^{-i/2}\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{2}N^{1/2}p.\end{split}

Therefore,

(6.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{I}|S_{i}|\ll\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{2}N^{1/2}p\sum_{i=1}^{I}e^{-i/2}\ll\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{2}N^{1/2}p.

Combining (
6.2
) and (
6.3
),
we obtain the result.

7.
Comments

It is also natural to consider cancellations between some other exponential and character sums.
For example, in
[
14
]
one can find some bound on the following sums

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{S}}_{p}(f,{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}};{\mathfrak{C}})=\sum_{(u,v)\in{\mathfrak{C}}}\alpha_{u}\beta_{v}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(v/f(u)),\\
&{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(f,{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}};{\mathfrak{C}})=\sum_{(u,v)\in{\mathfrak{C}}}\alpha_{u}\beta_{v}\chi(v+f(u)),\end{split}

(where
\chi
is a multiplicative character modulo
p
),
over a
convex
set

{\mathfrak{C}}\subseteq[1,U]\times[1,V]
,
with some integer
1\leq U,V<p
.

Here we also note that one can also obtain a nontrivial cancellation for sums

{\mathcal{H}}_{k,p}(a;{\mathcal{I}})=\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}{\mathcal{G}}_{k,p}(am)

of Gaussian sums

{\mathcal{G}}_{k,p}(a)=\sum_{x=0}^{p-1}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(ax^{k}\right)

with a positive integer
k\mid p-1
.
Indeed, we define

\tau_{p}(a;\chi)=\sum_{x=1}^{p-1}\chi(x){\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(ax\right),

where
\chi
is a multiplicative
character; we refer to
[
7
, Chapter 3]
for a background on multiplicative
characters. Then by the orthogonality of characters, we have

{\mathcal{G}}_{k,p}(a)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi^{k}=\chi_{0}\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\overline{\chi}(a)\tau_{p}(a;\chi),

where the summation is over all nonprincipal multiplicative characters
\chi
modulo
p
, such that
\chi^{k}

is the principal character
\chi_{0}
, see also
[
11
, Theorem 5.30]
.
Using that
|\tau_{p}(a;\chi)|=p^{1/2}
for any nonprincipal multiplicative characters
\chi

and integer
a
with
\gcd(a,p)=1
, we derive

|{\mathcal{H}}_{k,p}(a;{\mathcal{I}})|=p^{1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi^{k}=\chi_{0}\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\overline{\chi}(a)\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}\overline{\chi}(m).

Thus applying the Burgess bound, see
[
7
, Equation (12.58)]
,
we derive that

(7.1)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{H}}_{k,p}(a;{\mathcal{I}})&\ll M^{1-1/\nu}p^{1/2+(\nu+1)/(4\nu^{2})}(\log p)^{1/\nu}\\
&=M^{1-1/\nu}p^{(2\nu^{2}+\nu+1)/(4\nu^{2})}(\log p)^{1/\nu}\end{split}

for any fixed
k\mid p-1
and
\nu=1,2,\ldots
.

Similarly, for general quadratic polynomials
f(X)=aX^{2}+bX
, with
\gcd(a,p)=1
, we can define
the double sums

{\mathcal{F}}_{p}(a,b;{\mathcal{I}})=\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sum_{x=0}^{p-1}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(m\left(ax^{2}+bx\right)\right).

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\sum_{x=0}^{p-1}

\displaystyle{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}(ax^{2}+bx)={\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(-\frac{b^{2}}{4a}\right)\sum_{x=0}^{p-1}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(a\left(x+b/(2a)\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(-\frac{b^{2}}{4a}\right)\sum_{x=0}^{p-1}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(ax^{2}\right)=\left(\frac{a}{p}\right){\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(-\frac{b^{2}}{4a}\right){\mathcal{G}}_{2,p}(1),

where
(a/p)
is the Legendre symbol of
a
modulo
p
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}\sum_{x=0}^{p-1}{\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(m(ax^{2}+bx)\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{G}}_{2,p}(1)\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(\frac{am}{p}\right){\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(-\frac{(bm)^{2}}{4am}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{a}{p}\right){\mathcal{G}}_{2,p}(1)\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{I}}}\left(\frac{m}{p}\right){\mathbf{\,e}}_{p}\left(-\frac{b^{2}m}{4a}\right).

Now, using the bound of Burgess
[
2
]
on
short mixed sums (see
[
6
,
8
,
12
]
for
various generalisations) we easily derive that
for any fixed
\nu=2,3,\ldots
we have

(7.2)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{F}}_{p}(a,b;{\mathcal{I}})&\ll M^{1-1/\nu}p^{1/2+1/(4(\nu-1))}(\log p)^{2}\\
&=M^{1-1/\nu}p^{(2\nu-1)/(4(\nu-1))}(\log p)^{2},\end{split}

where the implied constant may depend on
\nu
.

We note that the bounds (
7.1
) and (
7.2
) are nontrivial
provided that
M\geq p^{1/4+\varepsilon}
for any fixed
\varepsilon>0

and sufficiently large
p
.

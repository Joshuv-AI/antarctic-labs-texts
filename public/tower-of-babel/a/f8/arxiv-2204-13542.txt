Abstract
. We prove that reiteratively hypercyclic operators have perfect spectrum. Consequently, it follows that there exist separable infinite dimensional Banach spaces that do not support any reiteratively hypercyclic operator. For this, we study ℱ \mathcal{F} -recurrence and almost ℱ \mathcal{F} -recurrence of operators for general families and in particular for a special class of families, called block families.

1.
Introduction and main results

Reiterative hypercyclicity is a strengthening of hypercyclicity, which is the main object
of study in linear dynamics—an emerging field at the intersection of operator theory and
functional analysis, closely tied to the invariant subspace problem
[
5
,
7
]
. Within
this framework, there exists a strict hierarchy of linear dynamical notions, including frequent
hypercyclicity or chaoticity, which implies reiterative hypercyclicity, and reiterative hypercyclicity, in turn, implies weak mixing and hence hypercyclicity
[
9
]
. It is a known
fact that any infinite-dimensional separable Banach space supports a weakly mixing operator
[
2
,
8
,
25
]
.

In 2011, Argyros and Haydon
[
3
]
(developing the techniques for the construction of Hereditarily Indecomposable spaces
[
24
]
) constructed a Banach space with the peculiar property of supporting very
few operators. Specifically, every operator on this space can be expressed as a sum of a scalar
multiple of the identity plus a compact operator. This construction is notable for various
properties it possesses. Indeed, Lomonosov’s famous theorem shows that every operator
that commutes with a non-zero compact operator must have a non-trivial closed invariant
subspace. As a consequence, every continuous linear operator on the Argyros-Haydon space
is guaranteed to have a non-trivial closed invariant subspace, making it a groundbreaking
discovery in the field of operator theory. As a matter of fact, no other infinite-dimensional
separable Banach space with this property is currently known besides the one constructed
by Argyros and Haydon. So the Argyros-Haydon space remains a unique and significant
example in this regard.

It has been established that neither a chaotic nor a frequently
hypercyclic operator can be supported by the Argyros-Haydon space
[
10
,
39
]
.

The concept of reiterative hypercyclicity is inspired in that of frequent hypercyclicity but it shares some properties with hypercyclic operators, e.g. for a reiteratively hypercyclic operator (in contrast to a frequently hypercyclic one) the set of reiteratively hypercyclic vectors is residual, and every hypercyclic vector is automatically reiteratively hypercyclic. It is then natural to ask
the following question (see e.g.
[
13
, Question 4.10]
or
[
34
, Question 2.6]
).

Question 1.1
.

Does every infinite dimensional separable Banach space support reiteratively hypercyclic operators?

The primary purpose of this note is to establish an additional property of the Argyros-Haydon space, namely, its failure to support any reiteratively hypercyclic operator, in particular answering Question
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

The Argyros-Haydon space does not support a reiteratively hypercyclic operator.

In relation to this theorem it would be interesting to know if a Banach space without frequently hypercyclic operators could support a reiteratively hypercyclic operator (see Question
5.7
).

We obtain our main result as a consequence of our investigations on the relationship between the concept of almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrence and
\mathcal{F}
-hypercyclicity.
Recurrence is one of the oldest and most studied concepts in the theory of dynamical systems. In contrast, in linear dynamics the main concept is that of hypercyclicity and a systematic study of recurrence began only around 10 years ago with the work of Costakis, Manoussos and Parissis
[
19
,
20
]
.

On the other hand, based on the seminal articles
[
9
,
11
]
much of the interest in linear dynamics shifted towards the study of
\mathcal{F}
-hypercyclicity for different hereditarily upward (or Furstenberg) families of subsets of the natural numbers, hence including in a single theory concepts as hypercyclity, frequent hypercyclicity, reiterative hypercyclicity or chaos (see also
[
14
]
), among others.

At this point, it turns out to be natural to investigate
\mathcal{F}
-recurrence of linear operators for general families
\mathcal{F}
, a task recently initiated by Bonilla, Grosse-Erdmann, López-Martínez and Peris
[
13
]
. One of the main goals in
[
13
]
was to determine conditions under which an operator that is hypercyclic and
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent is necessarily
\mathcal{F}
-hypercyclic. Our point of view is slightly different, and a question we ask is whether hypercyclicity together with a strong property of recurrence (
\mathcal{F}
-recurrence) implies a weak property of frequent hypercyclicity (
\mathcal{G}
-hypercyclicity, for a family
\mathcal{G}
that is bigger than
\mathcal{F}
). It is worth mentioning that hypercyclicity together with the strongest possible form of recurrence, density of periodic points, does not necessarily imply frequently hypercyclicity. This was proved by Menet in
[
36
]
who, answering one of the main open questions at that moment in linear dynamics, exhibited an example of an operator that is chaotic but not upper frequently hypercyclic.

With this aim in mind we study the block family
b\mathcal{F}
associated to a given family
\mathcal{F}
, which is essentially formed by sets that are union of finite blocks of a given set
A\in\mathcal{F}
. We also introduce the notion of almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrence and investigate the relationship between
b\mathcal{F}
-hypercyclicity and almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrence plus hypercyclicity.

An application of our results on almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrence to the family of sets of positive upper Banach density
leads us to the following theorem which implies our main result.

Theorem 1.3
.

On real or complex locally convex spaces, no compact perturbation of the identity can be reiteratively hypercyclic.

Almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrence is also important for the understanding of the dynamics
of backward shift operators: in
[
11
]
, it was proved that, when the family
\mathcal{F}
has the property of being
upper
,
\mathcal{F}
-hypercyclic unilateral backward shifts are characterized by two conditions. We show in Section
4
that exactly the same two conditions characterize almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent unilateral backward shifts for general, non necessarily upper, families. We also prove that under the assumption of
\mathcal{F}
being an upper family, every almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent unilateral backward shift is necessarily
\mathcal{F}
-hypercyclic. Moreover, we also show that two very similar conditions characterize almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent bilateral backward shifts, but in this case it is unclear if they are also
\mathcal{F}
-hypercyclic.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is dedicated to the study of the spectrum
of reiteratively hypercyclic operators. We establish that the spectrum of a reiteratively
hypercyclic operator is perfect (Theorem
2.6
). Consequently, Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
follow.
The proof of Theorem
2.6
relies on the notion of almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrence.

In Section
3
, we delve into the concept of almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrence for general families. Among
other results, we demonstrate that for block families, if an operator is hypercyclic and almost

\mathcal{F}
-recurrent, then it is
b\mathcal{F}
-hypercyclic (Proposition
3.6
). Additionally, we establish a converse
of this result for adjoint operators: if an adjoint operator is almost
b\mathcal{F}
-recurrent, then it is
also almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent (Theorem
3.12
). We also establish that for CuSP families, almost

\mathcal{F}
-recurrence remains invariant under unimodular scalar multiplications (Theorem
3.16
).

In section
4
we study almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrence for backward shifts. We give a characterization of almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent unilateral and bilateral backward shifts. We also show several examples and counterexamples.

2.
Spectrum of reiteratively hypercyclic operators

In this section we prove the results stated in the Introduction and, in particular that
there are infinite dimensional separable Banach spaces not supporting reiteratively hypercyclic operators (Theorem
1.2
). These results will be deduced from Theorem
2.2

The proofs of some preliminary results are delayed to the next section. Unless explicitly specified, all the results hold for both complex and real Banach spaces.

Let us first recall some definitions.

The

The

\liminf_{n}\frac{\#(A\cap[0,n])}{n+1}

\mathbb{N}_{0}
with positive lower density is denoted by
\underline{\mathcal{D}}
.

An operator

(see
[

]

x\in X
such that for any nonempty open set

the set of return times
N_{T}(x,U):=\{n\geq 1\,:\,T^{n}(x)\in U\}
(or just
N(x,U)
if the operator is clear from the context) has positive upper Banach density.

An operator
T
is
recurrent
if for every nonempty open set
U
there is
x\in U
such that
N(x,U)\neq\emptyset.

A vector

is
recurrent
for

if for every nonempty open set
U
containing

the set of return times
N(x,U)\neq\emptyset.
By
[

, an operator is recurrent if and only if it has a dense set of recurrent vector.

A vector

(see

]
) for

if for every nonempty open set

containing

the set of return times

has positive upper Banach density. The operator

We also need to introduce the following notion of recurrence, which is slightly weaker than reiterative recurrence (see also Definition

Definition 2.1

Let

Note that by

A map

We finish this section showing the main consequences of Theorem

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space. If

is almost reiteratively recurrent then either

has perfect spectrum (i.e. the spectrum doesn’t have isolated points) or

has a factor that is a unimodular multiple of the identity.

Proof.

Suppose that
T
is almost reiteratively recurrent and that

(the spectrum of

\sigma(T)
has an isolated point
\lambda

such that

and
\sigma(T|_{M_{1}})=\sigma(T)\setminus\{\lambda\}

Consider
\tilde{T}=T|_{M_{2}}:M_{2}\to M_{2}
. Then
\tilde{T}
is a factor of
T
with

the projection from

onto

. Therefore
\tilde{T}
is almost reiteratively recurrent.
In particular,

\tilde{T}
is recurrent. It follows by
[

that
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
. Since
\sigma(\tilde{T})=\{\lambda\}

Corollary 2.7

Reiteratively hypercyclic operators on complex Banach spaces have perfect spectrum.

From the above theorem we can deduce that no compact perturbation of the identity is reiteratively hypercyclic and that there are infinite dimensional and separable Banach spaces without reiteratively hypercyclic operators.

3.1.
Preliminaries

A family of subsets of the natural numbers
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
is said to be
hereditary upward
provided that if
A\in\mathcal{F}
and
A\subseteq B
, then
B
belongs also to
\mathcal{F}
. Hereditary upward families are also sometimes called
Furstenberg families
. Given an hereditary upward family, we say that an operator
T
on a space
X
is
\mathcal{F}
-hypercyclic
if there is
x\in X
for which the set
N_{T}(x,U):=\{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:T^{n}(x)\in U\}
of return times belong to
\mathcal{F}
, for every nonempty open set
U\subset X
.
A vector
x\in X
is
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent
for
T
if
N_{T}(x,V)\in\mathcal{F}
for every neighborhood
V
of
x
. An operator is called
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent
if the set of
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent vectors is dense. The set of return times
N_{T}(x,U)
will be denoted by
N(x,U)
when the operator is clear from the context.

Thus, for example, if we denote by
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
the family of infinite sets,
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
-hypercyclicity and
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
-recurrence are simply hypercyclity and recurrence respectively.

Let

Definition 3.3

Given an hereditary upward family

we define the block family

in the following way:

such that for every finite subset

of

.

Proposition 3.4

(Basic properties)
.

Let

be hereditary upward families, then

(1)

\mathcal{F}\subseteq b\mathcal{F}
;

(2)

bb\mathcal{F}=b\mathcal{F}
;

(3)

If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
then
b\mathcal{F}\subseteq b\tilde{\mathcal{F}}

Proof.

(1) and (3) are immediate. Proof of (2). By (1) we have that
b\mathcal{F}\subseteq bb\mathcal{F}
so that one inclusion holds.

Let

, hence there is

such that for every finite subset

of

there is
n
such that

. Also, since

, there is

such that for every finite subset
R
of
F
there is
n
such that

.
Therefore, if
R
is a finite subset of

and

, there is

such that

, which proves that

.

∎

T^{n+jk}(x)\subseteq V
for every
j\geq 0
.
In other words,
\{(T^{k})^{j}T^{n}x:j\geq 0\}:=Orb_{T^{k}}(T^{n}x)\subset V
. Let
Y:=\overline{co}(Orb_{T^{k}}(T^{n}x))\subseteq\overline{V}\subseteq U
(here,
\overline{co}
denotes the closed convex hull).
Note that
Y
is weakly
∗
-compact because it is a closed subset of the weakly
∗
-compact
\overline{V}
. Thus, since

T^{k}Orb_{T^{k}}(T^{n}x)\subseteq Orb_{T^{k}}(T^{n}x)
, we have that
Y
is a
T^{k}
-invariant, convex and weakly
∗
-compact set. An application of the Schauder-Tychonoff Theorem (see
[
41
]
) implies that there is a fixed point
y
of
T^{k}
in
Y
, i.e.
T^{k}y=y\in U.

∎

The following result is similar to Theorem
3.12
. We prove it here for the sake of completeness. Note that since the family
\underline{\mathcal{D}}
is left-shift invariant (see e.g.
[
11
]
) Theorem
2.5
follows as an immediate corollary. We will denote by
j_{X}
to the canonical inclusion
X\hookrightarrow X^{**}.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
T
be a linear operator on a Banach space
X
and let
\mathcal{F}
be a left-shift invariant and hereditary upward family. If
T
is almost
{b\mathcal{F}}
-recurrent then for each nonempty open set
U\subseteq X^{**}
such that
U\cap j_{X}(X)\neq\emptyset
, there is
z\in U
such that

N_{T^{**}}(z,U)\in\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq X^{**}
be a nonempty open set such that
U\cap j_{X}(X)\neq\emptyset.
Consider
V
a nonempty open ball of
X
such that
j_{X}(V)\subseteq U
and whose weak
∗
closure,
\overline{j_{X}(V)}^{w*}\subseteq U
.
Note that by Goldstine’s Theorem (see e.g.
[
18
]
),
\overline{j_{X}(V)}^{w*}
is a closed ball of
X^{**}
. Therefore,
\overline{j_{X}(V)}^{w*}
is a weakly
∗
-compact set
[
18
]
.

Let
x\in V
be such that
N_{T}(x,V)\in b\mathcal{F}
and
R\in\mathcal{F}
be such that for every finite subset
F
of
R
there is
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
F+n\subset N_{T}(x,V)
. Let
R_{n}=R\cap[0,n]
and
a_{n}
such that
R_{n}+a_{n}\subset N_{T}(x,V)
for every
n\geq 1
. Let
r_{0}=\min R
.

Since
\overline{j_{X}(V)}^{w*}\subseteq X^{**}
is weakly
∗
-compact there is a vector
z\in\overline{j_{X}(V)}^{w*}
which is a weak
∗
-limit point of the set
j_{X}(\{T^{a_{n}+r_{0}}(x):n\mathbb{N}\})
. We claim that
R-r_{0}\subseteq N_{T^{**}}(z,U)
. Indeed, let
r\in R
and note that
r\in R_{n}
(and hence
T^{a_{n}+r}(x)\in V
) for every
n\geq r
. Since
z
is a weak
∗
-limit point of the set
j_{X}(\{T^{a_{n}+r_{0}}(x):\,n\geq r\})
and
T^{**}
is weak
∗
-weak
∗
-continuous, we have that

(T^{**})^{r-r_{0}}(z)
is a weak
∗
-limit point of the set
(T^{**})^{r-r_{0}}\left(j_{X}(\{T^{a_{n}+r_{0}}(x):\,n\geq r\})\right)=j_{X}(\{T^{a_{n}+r}(x):\,n\geq r\})\subset j_{X}(V)
. This implies that
(T^{**})^{r-r_{0}}(z)\in\overline{j_{X}(V)}^{w*}\subset U
and thus
R-r_{0}\subseteq N_{T^{**}}(z,U)
.
By left-shift invariance we obtain that
N_{T^{**}}(z,U)\in\mathcal{F}.

∎

Recall that an hereditary upward family
\mathcal{F}
is said to have the CuSP property provided that for any finite covering
I_{1}\ldots I_{q}
of
\mathbb{N}
and any numbers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{q}
in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then

A\in\mathcal{F}\Rightarrow\Big(\bigcup_{j=1}^{q}n_{j}+\left(A\cap I_{j}\right)\Big)\in\mathcal{F}.

Families with the CuSP property were introduced in
[
13
, Section 4]
, but the concept has its origins in
[
5
, Section 6.3.3]
.
It is known that the families
\underline{\mathcal{D}}
,
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
,
\overline{\mathcal{BD}}
,
\mathcal{S}yn
and
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
all have the CuSP property (see
[
13
, Lemma 4.1]
), while the families
\mathcal{AP}_{b}
and
\mathcal{IAP}
don’t (see
[
14
, Remark 3.23]
). Note that the following result has Proposition
2.4
as a particular case.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an hereditary upward family with the CuSP property. If
T
is almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent then, for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
with
|\lambda|=1
,
\lambda T
is almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
with
|\lambda|=1
.
Given a recurrent vector
y
and an open set
U
containing
y
, we define

\Lambda(y,U)=\{\mu\in\mathbb{T}\,:\,\exists\ n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ such that }T^{n}(y)\in U\text{ and }\mu=\lambda^{n}\}.

We also consider
\Lambda(y)
the intersection of the closures of
\Lambda(y,U)
, i.e.,

\Lambda(y)=\bigcap_{U\text{ open, }y\in U}\overline{\Lambda(y,U)}.

Note that
\Lambda(y)\neq\emptyset
. Indeed, since the
\overline{\Lambda(y,U)}
are all compact, it suffices to show that the collection
\{\overline{\Lambda(y,U)}:U\text{ is an open set containing }y\}
has the finite intersection property.
Let
U_{1},\ldots U_{N}
be open sets containing
y
. Let
U^{\prime}=\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}U_{j}
, which is nonempty because
y\in U^{\prime}
. Since
y
is recurrent, it follows that there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T^{n}(y)\in U^{\prime}\subseteq U_{j}
for every
1\leq j\leq N
. This shows that
\lambda^{n}\in\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\Lambda(y,U_{j})\subseteq\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\overline{\Lambda(y,U_{j})}
.

Proceeding as in
[
13
, Theorem 4.4]
, it follows that

\Lambda(y)
is a multiplicative semigroup of the unit circle.

Indeed, let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\Lambda(y)
. Let
U_{1}
be an open set containing
y
and let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\mu_{1}\in\overline{\Lambda(y,U_{1})},

there is
n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|\lambda^{n_{1}}-\mu_{1}|<\varepsilon
and such that
T^{n_{1}}(y)\in U_{1}
. Let
U_{2}
be an open set containing
y
such that
T^{n_{1}}(U_{2})\subseteq U_{1}
. Since
\mu_{2}\in\Lambda(y)\subseteq\overline{\Lambda(y,U_{2})}
, there is
n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T^{n_{2}}(y)\in U_{2}
and
|\lambda^{n_{2}}-\mu_{2}|<\varepsilon
. Altogether we find that
T^{n_{1}+n_{2}}(y)\in T^{n_{1}}(U_{2})\subset U_{1}
. Hence,
\lambda^{n_{1}}\lambda^{n_{2}}\in\Lambda(y,U_{1})
. Finally,

|\mu_{1}\mu_{2}-\lambda^{n_{1}}\lambda^{n_{2}}|\leq|\mu_{1}(\mu_{2}-\lambda^{n_{2}})|+|(\mu_{1}-\lambda^{n_{1}})\lambda^{n_{2}}|<2\varepsilon.

This implies that
\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\in\overline{\Lambda(y,U_{1})}
for every open set
U_{1}
containing
y
. Therefore
\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\in\Lambda(y).

It is known that any closed multiplicative subsemigroup of
\mathbb{T}
is either
\mathbb{T}
or
G_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
(the group of
n
-th roots of the unity), see for example
[
30
, p.170]
. Thus, for each
y,
we have
\Lambda(y)=\mathbb{T}
or
\Lambda(y)=G_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that for real Banach spaces we are only concerned with semigroups of
\mathbb{T}\cap\mathbb{R}
, thus the only possibilities are
\Lambda(y)=G_{1}
or
\Lambda(y)=G_{2}.

Recall that since
T
is almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent, it is recurrent
[
19
, Proposition 2.1]
.
Let
U
be a nonempty open set and consider an open ball
U^{\prime}
and
\varepsilon>0
such that
B(1,\varepsilon)U^{\prime}\subseteq U,
where
B(1,\varepsilon)
denotes the open disc of center 1 and radius
\varepsilon.
We will show that there is
x\in U^{\prime}
such that
N_{\lambda T}(x,U)\in\mathcal{F}.

We will divide the proof in three cases:

Case 1: There is a recurrent vector
y\in U^{\prime}
such that
\Lambda(y)=\mathbb{T}.

This case can be proved as in
[
13
, Theorem 4.4]
.
Let
y\in U^{\prime}
be a recurrent vector such that
\Lambda(y)=\mathbb{T}.
In particular, we have that
\Lambda(y,V)
is dense in
\mathbb{T}
, for every open set
V
containing
y
.

Thus, for every
\mu\in\mathbb{T}
there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T^{n}(y)\in U^{\prime}
and such that
|\lambda^{n}-\mu|<\varepsilon
. By the compactness of
\mathbb{T}
, there are
N\in\mathbb{N}
and natural numbers
n_{j}
, with
1\leq j\leq N
, such that
T^{n_{j}}(y)\in U^{\prime}
for every
1\leq j\leq N
and such that

(3)

\mathbb{T}\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}B(\lambda^{n_{j}},\varepsilon).

Let
W
be an open set containing
y
such that
T^{n_{j}}(W)\subseteq U^{\prime}
for every
1\leq j\leq N
. Let
x\in W
be such that
A:=N_{T}(x,W)\in\mathcal{F}
. Note that
by (
3
), the sets

I_{j}=\{n:\lambda^{n+n_{j}}\in B(1,\varepsilon)\}
, with
1\leq j\leq N
, form a cover of
\mathbb{N}
. Indeed, if
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is
1\leq j_{0}\leq N
such that
\lambda^{-n}\in B(\lambda^{n_{j_{0}}},\varepsilon).
Thus,
|\lambda^{n+n_{j_{0}}}-1|\leq|\lambda^{n}||\lambda^{n_{j_{0}}}-\lambda^{-n}|<\varepsilon,
which implies that
n\in I_{j_{0}}.

If
n\in A\cap I_{j}
we have that

\displaystyle\lambda^{n+n_{j}}T^{n+n_{j}}(x)\in B(1,\varepsilon)T^{n_{j}}(W)\subseteq B(1,\varepsilon)U^{\prime}\subseteq U.

We have proved that
\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}n_{j}+(I_{j}\cap N_{T}(x,W))\subset N_{\lambda T}(x,U)
.
It follows by the CuSP property that
N_{\lambda T}(x,U)\in\mathcal{F}
. This proves the first case.

The case when
\Lambda(y)\neq\mathbb{T}
is rather more involved than the
\mathcal{F}
-recurrence analogue in
[
13
, Theorem 4.4]
, and we must consider two different situations. For a finite group
G
, let
\textrm{\bf{ord}}(G)
denote its cardinality.

Case 2:
\sup\{\textrm{ord}(\Lambda(y)):y\in U^{\prime}\}=\infty
and
\Lambda(y)\neq\mathbb{T}
for every recurrent vector
y\in U^{\prime}
.

Let
n_{0}
be such that

(4)

\mathbb{T}\subseteq\bigcup_{j=0}^{n-1}B(e^{2\pi i\frac{j}{n}},\frac{\varepsilon}{2})

for every
n\geq n_{0}
.

By assumption, there are a recurrent vector
y\in U^{\prime}
and
N\geq n_{0}
such that
\Lambda(y)=G_{N}
. Since each
e^{2\pi i\frac{j}{N}}\in\Lambda(y)
, there is for each
0\leq j\leq N-1
, an
n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T^{n_{j}}(y)\in U^{\prime}
and such that
|\lambda^{n_{j}}-e^{2\pi i\frac{j}{N}}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

It follows by (
4
) that for every
\mu\in\mathbb{T}
, there is
0\leq j\leq N-1
such that
|\mu-\lambda^{n_{j}}|<\varepsilon
and such that
T^{n_{j}}(y)\in U^{\prime}.
So, we obtain (
3
) and conclude as we did in
the first case.

Case 3:
\sup\{\textbf{{ord}}(\Lambda(y)):y\in U^{\prime},\,y\textrm{ recurrent}\}<\infty.

We first show that there exists an open set
U^{\prime\prime}
such that
U^{\prime\prime}\subseteq U^{\prime}
with the property that there are
G_{r_{1}},\ldots,G_{r_{N}}
satisfying that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

(5)

\text{the set }\{y\in U^{\prime\prime}:y\textrm{ recurrent and }\Lambda(y)=G_{n}\}\text{ is}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{dense in }U^{\prime\prime},&\text{ if }n\in\{r_{1},\dots,r_{N}\},\\
\text{empty},&\text{ if }n\notin\{r_{1},\dots,r_{N}\}.\end{array}\right.

Indeed, since
\sup\{\textrm{\bf{ord}}(\Lambda(y)):y\in U^{\prime},\,y\textrm{ recurrent}\}<\infty
and
\Lambda(y)\neq\emptyset
for each recurrent vector
y
, there are
G_{s_{1}},\ldots G_{s_{K}}
such that for every recurrent vector
y\in U^{\prime}
there is
1\leq k\leq K
such that
\Lambda(y)=G_{s_{k}}
. For each
1\leq k\leq K
, consider
D_{k,U^{\prime}}=\{y\in U^{\prime}:y\textrm{ recurrent and }\Lambda(y)=G_{s_{k}}\}
.
Since
\bigcup_{1\leq k\leq K}D_{k,U^{\prime}}=\{\text{recurrent vectors of $T$ in }U^{\prime}\}
and the closure of the set of recurrent vectors for
T
contains

U^{\prime}
, there must be some
1\leq k_{0}\leq K
such that
\overline{D_{{k_{0}},U^{\prime}}}
has nonempty interior.
To simplify notation, we suppose that
k_{0}=1
. Let
U_{1}
be the interior of
\overline{D_{{1},U^{\prime}}}
. Note that
U_{1}\subseteq U^{\prime}
.

If
D_{2,U_{1}}:=\{y\in U_{1}:y\textrm{ recurrent and }\Lambda(y)=G_{s_{2}}\}
is dense in
U_{1}
, we set
U_{2}=U_{1}
. Otherwise, consider a nonempty open set
U_{2}\subset U_{1}
such that
U_{2}\cap\overline{D_{2,U_{1}}}=\emptyset
.
Inductively, if
D_{j,U_{j-1}}:=\{y\in U_{j-1}:y\textrm{ recurrent and }\Lambda(y)=G_{s_{j}}\}
is dense in
U_{j-1}
, we set
U_{j}=U_{j-1}
. Otherwise, consider a nonempty open set
U_{j}\subset U_{j-1}
such that
U_{j}\cap\overline{D_{j,U_{j-1}}}=\emptyset
.

We have constructed nonempty
open sets
U_{1}\supseteq U_{2}\supseteq\dots\supseteq U_{K}
such that for each
1\leq j\leq K,
the sets
D_{j,U_{K}}:=\{y\in U_{K}:y\textrm{ recurrent and }\Lambda(y)=G_{s_{j}}\}
are either dense in
U_{K}
or empty. Then if
G_{r_{1}},\dots,G_{r_{N}}\in\{G_{s_{1}},\dots,G_{s_{K}}\}
are the groups
G_{r_{j}}
such that
\{y\in U_{K}:y\textrm{ recurrent and }\Lambda(y)=G_{r_{j}}\}
is dense in
U_{K}
, the set
U^{\prime\prime}=U_{K}
satisfies (
5
).

Given a nonempty open set
W
, consider

\beta(W):=\{\mu\in\mathbb{T}:\exists n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and a nonempty open set }V\subseteq W\text{ such that }T^{n}(V)\subseteq W,\mu=\lambda^{n}\}.

Note that

(6)

\beta(W)=\bigcup_{y\in Rec(W)}\Lambda(y,W),

where
Rec(W)
denotes the set of recurrent vectors in
W
.
Indeed, if
y
is a recurrent vector in
W
and
T^{n}(y)\in W
, then there is an open set
V
around
y
such that
T^{n}(V)\subseteq W
. This implies that
\bigcup_{y\in Rec(W)}\Lambda(y,W)\subseteq\beta(W)
.

Conversely, if
\mu\in\beta(W)
, then there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a nonempty open set
V\subseteq W
such that
T^{n}(V)\subseteq W
and such that
\mu=\lambda^{n}
. Let
y\in V
, be a recurrent vector. Hence,
T^{n}(y)\in W
and
\mu=\lambda^{n}
. We conclude that
\mu\in\Lambda(y,W).
Hence (
6
) follows.

Let
U^{\prime\prime},G_{r_{1}},\dots,G_{r_{N}},
satisfying (
5
) and define

\beta=\bigcap_{W\subseteq U^{\prime\prime}}\overline{\beta(W)}.

Claim

\beta=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}G_{r_{k}}
.

Indeed, let
\mu\in\beta
. Then, there are a sequence natural numbers
(n_{j})_{j\geq 1}
and a decreasing chain of nonempty open sets
V_{n_{j}}\subseteq\overline{V_{n_{j}}}\subseteq V_{n_{j-1}}
(which can be taken to be open balls whose diameter goes to 0) such that
T^{n_{j}}(V_{n_{j}})\subseteq V_{n_{j-1}}
,
\lambda^{n_{j}}\in\beta(V_{n_{j-1}})
and
\lambda^{n_{j}}\to\mu
. Since
(\overline{V_{n_{j}}})_{j\geq 1}
have diameter decreasing to 0, it follows, by the well known Cantor’s intersection theorem for complete metric spaces, that there is
y\in U^{\prime\prime}
such that
\cap_{j\geq 1}\overline{V_{n_{j}}}=\{y\}
. Since
(V_{n_{j}})_{j\geq 1}
is a neighbourhood basis of
y
and
T^{n_{j}}(y)\in V_{n_{j-1}}
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
y
is a recurrent vector. This implies that
\mu\in\overline{\bigcap_{V\text{ open },V\ni y}\Lambda(y,V)}\subseteq\Lambda(y)=G_{r_{k}}
for some
1\leq k\leq N
.

Conversely, let
1\leq k\leq N
and let
W\subseteq U^{\prime\prime}
be a nonempty open set. By (
5
), there is a recurrent vector
y\in W
such that
\Lambda(y)=G_{r_{k}}
. Therefore,
G_{r_{k}}\subseteq\overline{\Lambda(y,W)}\subseteq\overline{\beta(W)}.
This implies that
G_{r_{k}}\subseteq\beta.

We will show now that there is
W^{\prime}\subseteq U^{\prime\prime}
such that

(7)

\beta(W^{\prime})\subseteq\bigcup_{1\leq k\leq N,0\leq j\leq r_{k}-1}B(e^{2\pi i\frac{j}{r_{k}}},\frac{\varepsilon}{2})=\bigcup_{s\in G_{r_{k}},1\leq k\leq N}B(s,\frac{\varepsilon}{2}).

Suppose otherwise, then
for each nonempty open set
W\subseteq U^{\prime\prime}
, there is some
n_{W}
such that
\lambda^{n_{W}}\in\beta(W)\setminus\bigcup_{s\in G_{r_{k}},1\leq k\leq N}B(s,\frac{\varepsilon}{2}),
which implies that there is some open set
V_{W}\subseteq W
such that
T^{n_{W}}(V_{W})\subseteq W
and
|\lambda^{n_{W}}-s|\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for every
{s\in G_{r_{k}},1\leq k\leq N}
. Hence, we can construct inductively a sequence of
natural numbers
(n_{j})_{j\geq 1}
and a decreasing chain of nonempty open sets
(V_{n_{j}})_{j\geq 1}\subset U^{\prime\prime}
whose diameter tend to 0, with
\overline{V_{n_{j}}}\subseteq V_{n_{j-1}}
such that
T^{n_{j}}(V_{n_{j}})\subseteq V_{n_{j-1}}
, and such that
|\lambda^{n_{j}}-s|\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for every
s\in\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}G_{r_{k}}
. Moreover, passing to a subsequence of
(\lambda^{n_{j}})_{j\geq 1}
, we may assume that there is
\mu\in\mathbb{T}
such that
\lambda^{n_{j}}\to\mu
.

Consider
\{y\}=\bigcap\overline{V_{n_{j}}}
. Since for every
j\geq 1
,
T^{n_{j}}y\in T^{n_{j}}V_{n_{j}}\subseteq V_{n_{j-1}}\subseteq\dots\subseteq V_{n_{1}}
, we have that
y
is a recurrent vector and that
\lambda^{n_{j}}\in\Lambda(y,V_{n_{l}})
for every
l
such that
1\leq l<j
. Thus
\mu\in\overline{\Lambda(y,V_{n_{l}})}
for every
l\in\mathbb{N}
and hence
\mu\in\Lambda(y)
(because for all open sets
U
containing
y
there exists
l\geq 1
such
that
V_{n_{l}}\subseteq U
).
Since
\Lambda(y)=G_{r_{k_{0}}}
for some
1\leq k_{0}\leq N
, we have that
\mu\in\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}G_{r_{k}}
. This is a contradiction because we assumed that
|\lambda^{n_{j}}-s|\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for every
s\in\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}G_{r_{k}}.

So, let
W^{\prime}\subseteq U^{\prime\prime}
be an open set satisfying (
7
).

Let
y_{1}\in W^{\prime}
be a recurrent vector such that
\Lambda(y_{1})=G_{r_{1}}
. Therefore, by the definition of
\Lambda(y_{1})
, there is for each
0\leq j\leq r_{1}-1
a natural number
n_{1,j}
such that
T^{n_{1,j}}(y_{1})\in W^{\prime}
and
|\lambda^{n_{1,j}}-e^{-2\pi i\frac{j}{r_{1}}}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

For each
0\leq j\leq r_{1}-1
, let
W_{1,j}
be an open set that contains
y_{1}
such that
T^{n_{1,j}}(W_{1,j})\subseteq W^{\prime}
and let
W_{1}:=\cap_{j=0}^{n_{1}}W_{1,j}\subset W^{\prime}
, which is nonempty because
y\in W_{1,j}
for every
0\leq j\leq r_{1}-1
.

Take now
y_{2}\in W_{1}
be a recurrent vector such that
\Lambda(y_{2})=G_{r_{2}}
. Proceeding as before we obtain an open set
W_{2}\subseteq W_{1}
and natural numbers
n_{2,j}
for
0\leq j\leq r_{2}-1
, such that
T^{n_{2,j}}(W_{2})\subseteq W_{1}
and
|\lambda^{n_{2,j}}-e^{-2\pi i\frac{j}{r_{2}}}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Repeating the argument for each
G_{r_{k}}
,
1\leq k\leq N
, we construct a nonempty open set
W^{\prime\prime}:=W_{N}\subset W^{\prime}
and natural numbers
{n_{k,j}}
with
1\leq k\leq N
and
0\leq j\leq r_{k}-1
such that

(8)

T^{n_{k,j}}(W^{\prime\prime})\subseteq W^{\prime}\text{ and }|\lambda^{n_{k,j}}-e^{-2\pi i\frac{j}{r_{k}}}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

for every
1\leq k\leq N,

0\leq j\leq r_{k}-1.

Since
T
is almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent there is some
x\in W^{\prime\prime}

such that
A:=N_{T}(x,W^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{F}
. We will prove that
N_{\lambda T}(x,U)\in\mathcal{F}
.

For each
1\leq k\leq N
and
0\leq j\leq r_{k}-1
consider

I_{k,j}=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\lambda^{n+n_{k,j}}\in B(1,\varepsilon)\}.

Let
n\in A
. By continuity, there is an open set
V\subseteq W^{\prime\prime}
around
x
such that

\lambda^{n}\in\beta(W^{\prime\prime})\subseteq\beta(W^{\prime})\subseteq\bigcup_{1\leq k\leq N,0\leq j\leq r_{k}-1}B(e^{2\pi i\frac{j}{r_{k}}},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}),

We have proved that

Consider
\delta>0

and define
\overline{\mathcal{BD}}_{\delta}=\{A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:\overline{\mathcal{BD}}(A)\geq\delta\}
. It is known that no Banach space supports a
\overline{\mathcal{BD}}_{\delta}
-hypercyclic operator, as shown in

. However, if

is
the identity operator with

, then

is

\overline{\mathcal{BD}}_{\delta}
-recurrent. Specifically, for any open set

containing

, we have

, implying that

. In fact, we
will prove that these operators are the only ones that exhibit
almost
\overline{\mathcal{BD}}_{\delta}
-recurrence.

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
T
be a continuous mapping on a nonempty connected open set
V
and let
\delta>0
be such that for every nonempty open set
U\subseteq V
there is
x_{U}\in U
such that

. Then, there is
j\leq\delta^{-1}
such that
T^{j}|_{V}=Id_{V},
where
Id_{V}
is the identity operator on
V.

Proof.

Let’s proceed by contradiction. Consider
k>\delta-1
. We will demonstrate that for every
nonempty open set
U\subseteq V
, there exists
j
with
1\leq j\leq k
such that
T^{j}(U)\cap U\neq\emptyset
. Indeed,
note that
\overline{\mathcal{BD}}_{\delta}=\bigcup_{r\geq\delta}\bigcap_{N\geq 1}\mathcal{A}_{r,N}
, where
A\in\mathcal{A}_{r,N}
if and only if there exists
m\geq 0
such
that
\frac{\#\{A\cap[m,m+N]\}}{(N+1)}>r
. By assumption, there exists
x_{U}\in U
such that

N(x_{U},U)\in\overline{\mathcal{BD}}_{\delta}
. Specifically,
N(x_{U},U)\in A_{r,k}
for some

. Hence, there exists
m\geq 0

such that
\#\{N(x_{U},U)\cap[m,m+k]\}>(k+1)r>k\delta>1
. In other words, there exist
n\geq 0

and
n+j
with
1\leq j\leq k
such that
\{n,n+j\}\subset N(x_{U},U)
. Therefore,
T^{n}(x_{U})\in U
and

T^{j}(T^{n}(x_{U}))\in U.

Suppose that there exists
x\in V
such that
T^{j}(x)\neq x
for all
j
with
1\leq j\leq k
. Thus there are
V_{j}
open neighbourhoods of
T^{j}x
such that
x\notin\overline{V_{j}}
, for
j=1,\dots,k.
By continuity, there is some open neighbourhood

of

such that
T^{j}(V_{0})\subseteq V_{j}
and
V_{0}\cap V_{j}=\emptyset
for all

with

. This is a contradiction, because it implies that
T^{j}(V_{0})\cap V_{0}=\emptyset
for

This implies that every vector in

is periodic of period at most
k
.
It is easy
to check that the set
\{x\in{V}:{T^{j}x}=x\}
of
periodic vectors of period
{j}
contains all its accumulation
points; hence, it is closed. Therefore, the connectivity of
{V}

implies that all vectors in

must have period

, for some

i.e.
T^{j}|_{V}=Id_{V}
.
∎

The fact that any Banach space is connected combined with Lemma 3.17, yields the following
result.

Corollary 3.18
.

Let

be an operator acting on a Banach space

is almost
\overline{\mathcal{BD}}_{\delta}
-recurrent, then

, for some

.

Proposition 4.1

Let

\mathcal{F}

-hypercyclic.

Let

Corollary 4.3
.

There exists a unilateral weighted backward shift on

In
[

]
it was proved that for

spaces, reiteratively hypercyclic weighted backward shifts are indeed frequently hypercyclic. The main Theorem of
[
17
]
states that, on spaces with boundedly complete unconditional basis, upper frequently hypercyclic weighted backward shifts are both frequently hypercyclic and chaotic (recall that chaotic operators are those which are hypercyclic and have a dense set of periodic vectors).
Recall that a basis
\{e_{n}\}
is boundedly complete if it satisfies that for each sequence of scalars
(a_{n})_{n}
such that
\Big(\sum_{0\leq k\leq n}a_{k}e_{k}\Big)_{n\geq 0}
is bounded, the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}e_{k}
is convergent. Recall also that a basis
\{e_{n}\}
is unconditional with unconditional constant

if for all

\Big\|\sum_{0\leq k\leq n}a_{k}e_{k}\Big\|\leq C\Big\|\sum_{0\leq k\leq n}b_{k}e_{k}\Big\|
whenever
a_{0},\dots,a_{n},b_{0},\dots,b_{n}
are scalars such that
|a_{k}|\leq|b_{k}|
for
0\leq k\leq n
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with boundedly complete basis
\{e_{n}:\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
. Let
B_{w}
be a weighted shift on

. Then the following are equivalent.

i)

B_{w}
is almost reiteratively recurrent;

ii)

B_{w}
is reiteratively hypercyclic;

iii)

B_{w}
is upper frequently
hypercyclic;

iv)

B_{w}
is
\overline{\mathcal{LD}}
- hypercyclic.

If moreover, the basis is unconditional, then i)-iv) are equivalent to

v)

B_{w}
is frequently hypercyclic and

vi)

B_{w}
is chaotic.

Proof.

As usual, it is sufficient to prove the result in the unweighted case.
Since
b\overline{\mathcal{LD}}=b\overline{\mathcal{D}}=\overline{\mathcal{BD}}
, by Corollary
4.2
, it suffices to show that
B
is an adjoint operator.
It is well known that
\{e_{n}\}
is boundedly complete if and only if

is isomorphic to

and

[

Consider

, i.e.

The last assertion now follows from
[

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an upper uniformly finite invariant family. The
following are equivalent:

i)

B_{w}
is almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent;

ii)

B_{w}
is
\mathcal{F}
-hypercyclic.

Any chaotic operator is almost
\mathcal{S}yn
-recurrent. In the case of unweighted backward shifts acting on
Banach spaces with unconditional basis we have the following.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with unconditional basis. The backward
shift operator
B
is almost
\mathcal{S}yn
-recurrent if and only if
it is chaotic.

Proof.

Recall that
B
is chaotic if and only if
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e_{n}
converges, see e.g.
[
30
, Theorem 4.6]
.
If
B
is almost
\mathcal{S}yn
-recurrent then, by Theorem
4.5
, there is a syndetic set
A
such that
\sum_{n\in A}e_{n}
converges. Since
A
has bounded gaps, there is
b>0
such that
\mathbb{N}\subseteq\bigcup_{0\leq j\leq b}A-j
.

If
C>0
denotes the constant of the basis we obtain that
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}e_{n}
is convergent because

\left\|\sum_{n\geq N}e_{n}\right\|\leq C(b+1)\left\|\sum_{0\leq j\leq b}B^{j}\left(\sum_{n\in A\cap[N,+\infty)}e_{n}\right)\right\|,

which tends to 0 as
N\to\infty
.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

There exists a unilateral weighted backward shift
B_{w}
on
c_{0}
which is almost
\mathcal{\overline{D}}
-recurrent but not almost
\mathcal{\underline{D}}
-recurrent.

Proof.

In
[
6
, Theorem 13]
an example of an upper frequently hypercyclic but not frequently hypercyclic weighted backward shift in
c_{0}
was given. Since the operator is upper frequently hypercyclic, it is almost
\overline{\mathcal{D}}
-recurrent. The main argument given by the authors to prove that the operator is not frequently hypercyclic is that for any set of positive lower density
A
,
\lim_{n\in A}\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{n}\nrightarrow\infty
(See specifically [step 5.3, Theorem 13]
[
6
]
). By equivalence ii) a) of Theorem
4.8
applied to
c_{0}
, it follows that the operator is not almost frequently hypercyclic.
∎

In
[
14
]
it was observed that there exists a weighted backward shift in
c_{0}
such that it is
\mathcal{AP}\!_{b}
-hypercyclic but not upper frequently hypercyclic (see also
[
9
]
). In particular, the operator is almost
\mathcal{AP}\!_{b}
-recurrent and almost
\mathcal{PS}yn
-recurrent. On the other hand, since chaotic operators weighted backward shifts are frequently hypercyclic, the operator can not be chaotic. By Corollary
4.10
, it follows that the operator is not almost
\mathcal{S}yn
-recurrent.

We now consider bilateral shifts. Our main focus will be on the unweighted case, but as usual, all the results can be translated to the weighted case.

Let
X
be a Banach space with basis
\{e_{n}:\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
where the (unweighted) backward shift
B
, defined by
B(e_{n})=e_{n-1}
, is continuous. A conjugation argument similar to the unilateral case allows to transfer the results to the weighted shift setting. The bilateral weighted backward shift with weights
(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
on
X
is conjugate to the unweighted backward shift on
X(v):=\{x=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{n}e_{n}\,:\,\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{n}v_{n}e_{n}\in X\},
with
v_{n}=(w_{n+1}\dots w_{0})
in case
n<0
,
v_{0}=1
and
v_{n}=(w_{1}\dots w_{n})^{-1}
in case
n>0
.
Indeed, the mapping
\phi_{v}:X(v)\to X
,
(\phi_{v}(x))_{n}\mapsto(x_{n}v_{n})_{n}
is an isomorphism and it satisfies that
\phi_{v}B=B_{w}\phi_{v}
. Then
B:X(v)\to X(v)
and
B_{w}:X\to X
are factors of each other (see again
[
30
, Section 4.1]
for more details). In particular,
B
is either
\mathcal{F}
-hypercyclic,
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent or almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent if and only if
B_{w}
is.

In
[
29
]
a characterization of
\mathcal{F}
-hypercyclic bilateral shifts was proved, which involves a sequence of sets
(A_{p})_{p}
that interact with each other. We next present a characterization of almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent bilateral shifts which is simpler in the sense that it only needs a single set
A
, and is very similar to the characterization of the unilateral shifts. It should be mentioned that an advantage of the characterization
[
29
, Theorem 14]
is that it is in some sense symmetric, and this allows the author to deal with the inverse operator.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finitely and left-shift invariant hereditary upward family.
Let
\{e_{n}:\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
be a basis of the Banach space
X
and
B
a well defined backward shift. Consider the following statements.

i)

B
is almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent;

ii)

for every
\varepsilon>0
and every
p
there is a set
A=A_{p,\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{F}
such that

(a)

\sum_{n\in A}e_{n+p}
converges and,

(b)

for any
m\in A
,

\Big\|\sum_{n\neq m,n\in A}e_{n-m+p}\Big\|<\varepsilon.

Then
ii)\Rightarrow i)
. If the basis is unconditional
ii)\Leftrightarrow i)
.

Since recurrent bilateral backward shifts are hypercyclic
[
13
]
, the above conditions are also equivalent to hypercyclicity plus almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrence.

We will only sketch the proof of the theorem because it is similar to the proof Theorem
4.5
.

Proof.

By the continuity of
B
there is
\beta_{p}
such that
\|x\|<\beta_{p}
then
|x_{j}|<1/2
for every
j\leq 2p
.

i)\Rightarrow ii)

Let
y=\sum_{-p\leq j\leq p}je_{j}
. Thus, there are
x\in X
and
A\in\mathcal{F}
such that
\|B^{n}(x)-y\|<\min\{\frac{\varepsilon}{C},\beta_{p}\}
for every
n\in A
, where
C
is the unconditional basis constant.
Then
A
will satisfy conditions
(a)
and
(b)
.

For
n\in A
,
|j|\leq p
,
|x_{n+j}-j|<\frac{1}{2}
and thus
|\frac{1}{x_{n+j}}|<2
for
j\neq 0
.
Unconditionality then implies that

\sum_{n\in A}e_{n+p}=\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{x_{n+p}}x_{n+p}e_{n+p}

converges and thus we have
(a)
.

For
(b)
, let
m\in A
. Then we see as in the proof of Theorem
4.5
that
[B^{m}(x)-y]_{n-m+j}=x_{n+j}
for every
n\in A
and
|j|\leq p
.
Then,

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{n\in A,n\neq m}e_{n-m+p}\Big\|=\Big\|\sum_{n\in A,n\neq m}\frac{1}{x_{n+p}}[B^{m}(x)-y]_{n-m+p}e_{n-m+p}\Big\|\leq 2C\|B^{m}(x)-y\|\leq\varepsilon.

ii)\Rightarrow i)
By the denseness of the finite sequences, it is
enough to show that for any open ball
U
centered at a finitely supported vector, there is
x\in U
such that

N(x,U)\in\mathcal{F}
. Let
U
be an open ball of center
y=\sum_{j=-p}^{p}y_{j}e_{j}
and radius
\varepsilon>0
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{F}
given by
ii)
with to respect
\tilde{\varepsilon}=\frac{\varepsilon}{M(2p+1)\|y\|_{\infty}}
, where
M:=\max_{0\leq k\leq p}\|B\|^{k}
.
Considering
\varepsilon<\frac{\beta_{p}}{M^{2p}}
, we may assume that
|n-m|>p
for any
n\neq m\in A
.

Let
x=\sum_{n\in A}\sum_{-p}^{p}y_{j}e_{n+j}\in X
.
Since
x=\sum_{j=-p}^{p}y_{j}B^{p-j}(\sum_{n\in A}e_{n+p})
, it is well defined by
(a)
. And, by
(b)
, if
m\in A
we have that

\displaystyle\Big\|B^{m}(x)-y\Big\|

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{n\neq m,n\in A}\sum_{j=-p}^{p}y_{j}e_{n+j-m}\Big\|=\Big\|\sum_{j=-p}^{p}y_{j}\sum_{n\neq m,n\in A}e_{n+j-m}\Big\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=-p}^{p}|y_{j}|\|B^{p-j}\sum_{n\neq m,n\in A}e_{n+p-m}\|<\varepsilon.

Let
m_{1}=\min A
. Then,
B^{m_{1}}(x)\in U
and for
m\in A,m>m_{1}
we have that
B^{m-m_{1}}(B^{m_{1}}(x))=B^{m}(x)\in U
. By shift invariance this shows that
B
is almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent.

∎

Via a conjugation, the result for weighted backward shifts reads as follows.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finitely and left-shift invariant hereditary upward family.
Let
\{e_{n}:\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
be a basis of the Banach space
X
and
B_{w}
a well defined weighted backward shift. Consider the following statements.

i)

B_{w}
is almost
\mathcal{F}
-recurrent;

ii)

for every
\varepsilon>0
and every
p\in\mathbb{N}
there is a set
A=A_{p,\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{F}
such that

(a)

\sum_{n\in A}v_{n+p}e_{n+p}
converges and,

(b)

for any
m\in A
,

\Big\|\sum_{n\neq m,n\in A}v_{n-m+p}e_{n-m+p}\Big\|<\varepsilon,

where

in case

,

and

Then

While we were writing the manuscript we learned about the article

Theorem 5.1

(Grivaux and Matheron,
[
28
]
)
.

Let
(X,T)
be a Polish dynamical system. Assume that
X
is endowed with a Hausdorff topology
\tau
coarser than the original topology such that every point of
X
has a neighborhood basis(with respect to the original topology) consisting of
\tau

T
is continuous with respect to the topology
\tau

(1)

T
admits an invariant measure with full support;

(2)

for each open set
V\neq\emptyset
, there is an ergodic measure
\mu_{V}
for
T
such that
\mu_{V}(V)>0
;

(3)

T
is almost frequently recurrent.

It should be mentioned that the concept of frequently recurrence used in
[
28
]
is actually almost frequent recurrence as we define it here.

Theorem 5.2

(Grivaux and López-Martínez
[
26
]
)
.

Let
X^{*}
be a dual separable Banach space and let
T
be an adjoint operator that is almost reiteratively recurrent. Then
T
supports an invariant measure with full support.

Proof.

Since
T
is almost reiteratively recurrent and it is an adjoint operator, by Theorem
3.12
, it follows that it is almost
{\mathcal{D}}
-recurrent. Theorem
5.1
, applied to the
w^{*}
-topology (using that closed balls are
w^{*}
-compact) then implies that
T
supports an invariant measure with full support.
∎

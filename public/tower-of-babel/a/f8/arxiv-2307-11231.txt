Abstract
. We study well-posedness for a non-integrable generalization of the fifth order KdV, the second member in the KdV heirarchy. In particular, we use differentiation-by-parts to establish well-posedness for s > 35 / 64 s>35/64 in low modulation restricted norm spaces, as well as non-linear smoothing of order ε < min ⁡ ( 2 ​ ( s − 35 / 64 ) , 1 ) \varepsilon<\min(2(s-35/64),1) . As corollaries, we obtain unconditional well-posedness for the non-integrable fifth order KdV for s > 1 s>1 and global well-posedness for the integrable fifth order KdV for s ≥ 1 s\geq 1 . We also show local well-posedness for the non-integrable fifth order KdV for s > 1 / 2 s>1/2 , contingent upon the conjectured L 8 L^{8} Strichartz estimate. As an application of the nonlinear smoothing we obtain non-trivial upper bounds on the upper Minkowski dimension of the solution to the non-integrable fifth order KdV.

n_{1}+n_{2}=0

n,n_{1},\cdots,n_{p}
so that

n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}.

With that in mind, we define

\Phi_{p}(n_{1},\cdots,n_{p}):=\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{p}n_{i}\bigg)-\sum_{i=1}^{p}n_{i}^{5}=n^{5}-\sum_{i=1}^{p}n_{i}^{5},

for which we find the following propositions.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
|n_{1}|\geq|n_{2}|\geq|n_{3}|
. Then at least one of the following must be true

I)

“Resonance”-
n_{i}=n
for some
1\leq i\leq 3
,

II)

|n_{3}|\gtrsim|n_{1}|
,

III)

|\Phi_{3}(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})|=|n^{5}-n_{1}^{5}-\cdots n_{3}^{5}|\gtrsim n_{1}^{4}
.

Proof.

Note that we have the factorization

(5)

n^{5}-n_{1}^{5}-n_{2}^{5}-n_{3}^{5}=\frac{5}{2}(n_{1}+n_{2})(n_{1}+n_{3})(n_{2}+n_{3})(n^{2}+n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}+n_{3}^{2}).

If we first assume that there is no resonance, then we find that the above is non-zero. Assuming now that
|n_{3}|\ll|n_{1}|
, we find that
|(n_{1}+n_{3})(n_{1}+n_{2})(n_{2}+n_{3})|\gtrsim n_{1}^{2}
, so that

|(n_{1}+n_{2})(n_{1}+n_{3})(n_{2}+n_{3})(n^{2}+n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}+n_{3}^{2})|\gtrsim n_{1}^{4},

as desired.
∎

Piggybacking off of Proposition
3.1
we establish the following more general decomposition lemma.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
|n_{1}|\geq|n_{2}|\geq\cdots\geq|n_{p}|
, and
p\geq 4
. Denote
\tilde{n}_{i}=\sum_{j=i}^{p}n_{j}
, so we have that
n=\tilde{n}_{1}
and
n_{p}=\tilde{n}_{p}
. Then at least one of the following must be true

I)

“Resonance”-
n_{i}=n
for some
1\leq i\leq p
,

II)

n_{1}^{*}+n_{2}^{*}=0
,

III)

|n_{3}|\gtrsim|n_{1}|
,

IV)

|\Phi_{p}(n_{1},\cdots,n_{p})|=|n^{5}-n_{1}^{5}-\cdots n_{p}^{5}|\gtrsim n_{1}^{4}
,

V)

n_{3}^{4}|n_{4}|\gtrsim n_{1}^{4}
.

Remark 3.3
.

The third condition is also contained in the last condition by mean-zero preservation, so we will often drop it and handle it with the case
n_{3}^{4}n_{4}\gtrsim n_{1}^{4}
. Additionally, we don’t care about the specifics of the decomposition of the Fourier space into these cases– any such will work.

Proof.

For
p\geq 4
we may write

\displaystyle n^{5}-n_{1}^{5}-\cdots n_{p}^{5}

\displaystyle=(n^{5}-n_{1}^{5}-n_{2}^{5}-\tilde{n}_{3}^{5})+(\tilde{n}_{3}^{5}-n_{3}^{5}-\tilde{n}_{4}^{5})+\cdots+(\tilde{n}_{p-1}^{5}-n_{p-1}^{5}-n_{p}^{5})

\displaystyle=\Phi_{3}(n_{1},n_{2},\tilde{n}_{3})+\sum_{j=3}^{p-1}\Phi_{2}(n_{j},\tilde{n}_{j+1}).

Hence, if there is no resonance,
n_{1}+n_{2}\neq 0
, and
|n_{3}|\ll|n_{1}|
, then by (
5
)
the first term satisfies

\displaystyle|\Phi_{3}(n_{1},n_{2},\tilde{n}_{3})|

\displaystyle=5/2|(n_{1}+n_{2})(n_{1}+\tilde{n}_{3})(n_{2}+\tilde{n}_{3})|(n^{2}+n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}+\tilde{n}_{3}^{2})\gtrsim n_{1}^{4}.

Indeed, the first term is non-zero by assumption, and the lack of resonance ensures that
n_{1}+\tilde{n}_{3},n_{2}+\tilde{n_{3}}\neq 0
. Additionally, if
|n_{1}|\gg|n_{2}|
, then the first two factors are, in magnitude,
\gtrsim|n_{1}|
each, and if
|n_{2}|\sim|n_{1}|
, then the middle factors must each be
\gtrsim|n_{1}|
in magnitude.

It follows that if
|\Phi_{p}(n_{1},\cdots,n_{p})|\ll n_{1}^{4}
, then we must have

\big|\sum_{j=3}^{p-1}\Phi_{2}(n_{j},\tilde{n}_{j+1})\big|\gtrsim n_{1}^{4}.

However, the remaining terms must satisfy

|\Phi_{2}(n_{j},\tilde{n}_{j+1})|=|\tilde{n}_{j}n_{j}\tilde{n}_{j+1}|(\tilde{n}_{j}^{2}+n_{j}^{2}+\tilde{n}_{j+1}^{2})\lesssim n_{3}^{4}|n_{4}|,

so that

n_{1}^{4}\lesssim\big|\sum_{j=3}^{p-1}\Phi_{2}(n_{j},\tilde{n}_{j+1})\big|\lesssim n_{3}^{4}|n_{4}|,

which is the final case.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

For brevity, we denote the property that
|\Phi_{p}(n_{1},\cdots,n_{p})|\gtrsim(n_{1}^{*})^{4}
by
\mathcal{P}_{p}(n_{1},\cdots,n_{p})
. Similarly, we denote the property that
|\Phi_{p}(n_{1},\cdots,n_{p})|\ll(n_{1}^{*})^{4}
by
\mathcal{P}_{p}^{c}(n_{1},\cdots,n_{p})
.

\widehat{u}_{t}=\sum_{\Omega\neq 0}e^{it\Omega}m(n_{1},n_{2})\widehat{u}(n_{1})\widehat{u}(n_{2})\\

Remark 4.6

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

\langle\tau+n^{5}\rangle\gtrsim N_{1}^{4}

for some

without the summation constraint
\mathcal{P}_{m}(n_{1},\cdots,n_{m})
.

Proof.

The proof of the above proposition is standard, but included for completeness. Before beginning we note that we may assume that the space-time Fourier transforms of

are all positive, and that

Note that the assumption

\langle\tau_{j}+n_{j}^{5}\rangle\gtrsim n_{1}^{4}

\bigg\|P_{N}\mathcal{F}_{x}^{-1}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}\\

\big\|\tfrac{1}{\langle\tau+n^{5}\rangle}\mathcal{F}_{x,t}(\cdot)\big\|_{\ell^{2}_{n}L^{1}_{\tau}}\lesssim\|\cdot\|_{X^{0,-1/2+}}.

We now assume case

, in which case we first take a minor loss, which will make later work easier:

(31)

\displaystyle\bigg\|P_{N}\mathcal{F}_{x}^{-1}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}\\
\mathcal{P}_{m}(n_{1},\cdots,n_{m})\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\widehat{\varphi}_{n_{i}}\bigg)\bigg\|_{X^{0,-3/4}_{T}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim T^{\theta}\bigg\|P_{N}\mathcal{F}_{x}^{-1}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}\\
\mathcal{P}_{m}(n_{1},\cdots,n_{m})\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\widehat{\varphi}_{n_{i}}\bigg)\bigg\|_{X^{0,-(1/2-)}}.

and invoke duality for

\displaystyle\bigg\|P_{N}\mathcal{F}_{x}^{-1}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}\\
\mathcal{P}_{m}(n_{1},\cdots,n_{m})\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\widehat{\varphi}_{n_{i}}\bigg)\bigg\|_{X^{0,-(1/2-)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int_{\tau=\tau_{1}+\cdots\tau_{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}\\

Lemma 5.2

(Propagator Terms)
.

∎

|n_{1}|\geq|n_{2}|\geq\cdots\geq|n_{5}|
, and
\ell=1
.

We first perform a Littlewood-Paley decomposition as in Proposition
3.2
. If we have
N_{1}\sim N
, then we have by Proposition
5.1
that

\displaystyle\|C_{*}(\tilde{u})\|_{Z^{s+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\theta}\sum_{N\sim N_{1}\geq N_{2}\cdots N_{5}}N^{\varepsilon-1}\|P_{N_{1}}(\tilde{u})\|_{Y^{s}}\prod_{i=2}\|P_{N_{i}}(\tilde{u})\|_{Y^{1/2+}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\theta}\|\tilde{u}\|_{Y^{s}_{T}}^{5},

for
0\leq\varepsilon<1.
If we have
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gg N
then we find

\displaystyle\|C_{*}(\tilde{u})\|_{Z^{s+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\theta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\sim N_{2}\geq\cdots\geq N_{5}\\
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gg N\end{subarray}}\frac{N_{1}^{2}N^{s+\varepsilon+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}}{N_{1}N^{2}}N_{2}^{1/p}N_{3}^{1/2+}N_{4}^{1/2+}\prod_{i=1}^{5}\|P_{N_{i}}(\tilde{u})\|_{Y^{0}}.

When
1/2<s<1
we may again choose
\frac{1}{p}=s-\frac{1}{2}
and
0\leq\varepsilon\leq 1
, so that we find

\displaystyle\frac{N_{1}^{2}N^{s+\varepsilon+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}}{N_{1}N^{2}}N_{2}^{1/p}N_{3}^{1/2+}N_{4}^{1/2+}N_{5}^{1/2+}

\displaystyle=N_{1}N_{2}^{s-1/2}N_{3}^{1/2+}N_{4}^{1/2+}N_{5}^{1/2+}

\displaystyle\sim N_{1}^{s}N_{2}^{1/2}N_{3}^{1/2+}N_{4}^{1/2+}.

Cauchy-Schwarz then finishes both of these cases. When
s\geq 1
the proof follows by the above estimates and taking
p=2.

Case II,
n_{1}^{*}+n_{2}^{*}=0
:

In this case we will assume that
n_{2}^{*}\gg n_{3}^{*}
, as otherwise we can handle it in Case III. Since we cannot have
n_{\ell}+n_{\ell+1}=0
, we see that (just as in the
High\times High\mapsto Low
situation):

\max(n_{1},\cdots,n_{\ell}+n_{\ell+1},\cdots,n_{5})\gtrsim n_{1}^{*},

and hence
m_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ell}=O(n_{1}^{*}/n)=O(n_{2}^{*}/n)
, as we’re in case II.

For
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
s>1/2
it follows by taking the supremum in
t
and invoking the algebra property of
H^{s}
that

\displaystyle\|C_{*}(\tilde{u})\|_{X^{s+\varepsilon,0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sum_{n_{1}+n_{2}=0}|n_{1}|\widehat{\tilde{u}}(n_{1})\widehat{\tilde{u}}(n_{2})\right\|_{C^{0}_{t}}\left\|\langle n\rangle^{s+\varepsilon-1}\sum_{n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}=n}\widehat{\tilde{u}}(n_{1})\widehat{\tilde{u}}(n_{2})\widehat{\tilde{u}}(n_{3})\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}\ell^{2}_{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{1/2}_{x}}^{2}\|\tilde{u}\|_{Y^{s}_{T}}^{3}\lesssim\|\tilde{u}\|_{Y^{s}_{T}}^{5}.

Case III,
(n_{3}^{*})^{4}n_{4}\gtrsim(n_{1}^{*})^{4}
:

Note first that
\max(n_{1},\cdots,n_{\ell}+n_{\ell+1},\cdots,n_{5})\gtrsim n_{3}^{*}
, and hence if
n\gtrsim n_{4}^{*}
then
m^{\ell}_{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies (
45
), and the proof of Lemma
5.7
holds with an extra application of
Y^{1/2+}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}_{x,t}
. We now assume that
n_{4}^{*}\gg n
, in which case

\langle n\rangle^{s+\varepsilon}m_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ell}=O\left(\frac{|n|^{s+\varepsilon}(n_{1}^{*})^{s}(n_{3}^{*})^{2}\sqrt{n_{4}^{*}}}{nn_{3}^{*}}\right)=O\left(\frac{(n_{1}^{*})^{s-(1/2+)}(n_{2}^{*}n_{3}^{*}n_{4}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+}}{|n|^{1/2+}}\right).

It then follows by duality with
w\in X^{-s-\varepsilon,1/2-}
that we have

\displaystyle\|C_{*}(\tilde{u})\|_{X^{s+\varepsilon,-(1/2-)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|J_{x}^{-s-\varepsilon-(1/2+)}w\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{\infty-}_{t}}\|J_{x}^{s-(1/2+)}\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,t}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\|J_{x}^{1/2+\varepsilon/3+}\tilde{u}\|_{L^{24/7-}_{x,t}}^{3}\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\|_{X^{-s-\varepsilon,1/2-}}\|\tilde{u}\|_{Y^{s}_{T}}\|\tilde{u}\|_{X^{s,1/4-}_{T}}\|\tilde{u}\|_{Y^{\frac{1}{2}+}_{T}}^{3},

for

0\leq\varepsilon<3\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right).

We find a small factor of
T
from the space in the
L^{{24/7-}}_{x,t}
estimates. As the other portion of the norm follows in exactly the same fashion after Cauchy-Schwarz in
\tau
we conclude the proof.

Note that the case when
n\gtrsim n_{4}^{*}
requires the hypothesis on the
L^{8}
estimate, as in Lemma
5.7
. Additionally, the other portion of the
Z
norm is handled by the above estimates as in the previous lemmas.
∎

Proof of Smoothing.

Upon integration we find the equivalent Duhamel formula as

(64)

\displaystyle\tilde{u}-

\displaystyle\{B_{1}(\tilde{u})+B_{2}(\tilde{u})+B_{3}(\tilde{u})\}+W_{t}\{B_{1}(u_{0})+B_{2}(u_{0})+B_{3}(u_{0})-u_{0}\}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}W_{t-s}\left(C_{*}(\tilde{u})+smooth\right)\,ds.

For
s>\frac{1+a}{2},

0\leq\varepsilon<\min(2s-1-a,1)
, and
t<T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}})
we apply the lemmas in this section to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\mbox{LHS of }\eqref{Equation: Duhamel formula for smoothing}\right\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s+\varepsilon}_{x}}\lesssim\|C_{*}(\tilde{u})+smooth\|_{Z^{s+\varepsilon}_{T}}\lesssim C(\|u\|_{Y^{s}})\lesssim C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}).

An application of Lemma
6.4
and the local well-posedness bound handles all of the terms remaining on the left hand side of (
64
), giving the desired result

\|\tilde{u}-W_{t}u_{0}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s+\varepsilon}_{x}}\lesssim C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}),

by the triangle inequality.
∎

7.
Unconditional Well-posedness

For this section we will not need to rely on showing cancellation in the resonant terms with symbols that are
O(1)
, so our equation under consideration (again, modulo constants) is

(65)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}W_{-t}

\displaystyle\tilde{u}=\partial_{t}W_{-t}B_{1}(\tilde{u})+\partial_{t}W_{-t}B_{2}(\tilde{u})+\partial_{t}W_{-t}B_{3}(\tilde{u})

\displaystyle\qquad+W_{-t}(\mathcal{NR}_{1}(\tilde{u})+\mathcal{NR}_{2}(\tilde{u})+\mathcal{NR}_{3}(\tilde{u})+C(\tilde{u}))

\displaystyle\qquad+W_{-t}(R_{1}(\tilde{u})+R_{2}(\tilde{u})+R_{3}(\tilde{u}))

\displaystyle\qquad+W_{-t}(\mathcal{RE}_{1}(\tilde{u})+\mathcal{RE}_{2}(\tilde{u})+\mathcal{RE}_{3}(\tilde{u})+\mathcal{RE}_{4}(\tilde{u})).

Since the only meaningful differences between estimates for
Y^{s}
and
C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}
are applications of Strichartz estimates and modulation considerations, we will only show the proofs for the
\mathcal{NR}_{i}
,
R_{i}
, and
C(\tilde{u})
terms.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
s>1
. Then following bounds hold

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{NR}_{1}+\mathcal{NR}_{2}+\mathcal{NR}_{3})(\tilde{u})\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{3}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{4}

\displaystyle\|(R_{1}+R_{2}+R_{3})(\tilde{u})\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{4}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{6}

\displaystyle\|C(\tilde{u})\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{5}.

Proof.

Beginning with the first term, we recall that we lack resonance and that the situation
n_{1}^{*}+n_{2}^{*}=0
was already handled in Lemma
5.7
(without appeal to Strichartz estimates or modulation considerations). It thus suffices to consider the case that
(n_{3}^{*})^{4}n_{4}^{*}\gtrsim(n_{1}^{*})^{4}
.

As
\mathcal{NR}_{1}
carries three large similar frequencies, we have by Lemma
4.4
that the symbol for all terms is
O((n_{1}^{*})^{2}/n_{3}^{*})
. It follows by the case restriction that all of the symbols satisfy

O\left(\frac{(n_{1}^{*})^{2}}{n_{3}^{*}}\right)=O\left(\sqrt{n_{2}^{*}n_{3}^{*}n_{4}^{*}}\right).

Restricting ourselves to the harder
\mathcal{NR}_{2}
and
\mathcal{NR}_{3}
, we find by Hölders and Sobolev embedding that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{NR}_{2}(\tilde{u})+\mathcal{NR}_{3}(\tilde{u})\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(J_{x}^{s}\tilde{u})(J_{x}^{1/2}\tilde{u})^{3}\|_{C^{0}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{4},

for
s>1
.

The second bound follows from the fact that, by (
33
), (
34
), and (
55
) all of their symbols are
O(1)
. Restricting ourselves to
R_{3}
, and find by the symbol being
O(1)
that

\displaystyle\|R_{3}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}\lesssim\|\tilde{u}^{4}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}\lesssim\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{4},

for
s>1/2
.

As for the last bound, we note that (
51
) gives

\displaystyle m_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ell}=O\left(\frac{n_{\ell}^{2}+n_{\ell+1}^{2}}{|n|\max(n_{1},\cdots,n_{\ell}+n_{\ell+1},\cdots,n_{5})}\right)=O\left(\frac{(n_{1}^{*})^{2}}{n^{2}}\right).

If
n_{1}^{*}\gg n_{2}^{*}
, then the symbol is
O(1)
and the result is immediate as in the
R_{j}
estimate. We now assume that
n_{1}^{*}\sim n_{2}^{*}\gg|n|
, so that we obtain by Cauchy-Schwarz and Sobolev embedding:

\|C(\tilde{u})\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}\lesssim\|J_{x}^{s-2}((J_{x}\tilde{u})^{2}u^{3})\|_{C^{0}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\\
\lesssim\|(J_{x}^{s}\tilde{u})^{2}\tilde{u}^{3}\|_{C^{0}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\lesssim\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{2}\|\tilde{u}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{1/2+}_{x}}^{3},

for
s\geq 1
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.8
.

Modulo constants, we define the operators
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}[v]

\displaystyle=W_{t}\{B_{1}(u_{0})+B_{2}(u_{0})+B_{3}(u_{0})-u_{0}\}-\{B_{1}(v)+B_{2}(v)+B_{3}(v)\}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}W_{t-s}\left(C(v)+\mathcal{NR}_{1}(v)+\mathcal{NR}_{2}(v)+\mathcal{NR}_{3}(v)+l.o.t\right)\,ds,

and

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}[v]

\displaystyle=W_{t}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}W_{t-s}\left(\partial_{x}\left((\partial_{x}^{2}v)v+v(\partial_{x}^{2}v)\right)+\mathcal{K}(v)\partial_{x}v\right)\,ds.

These are the operators associated to the Duhamel representation of the equivalent formulation of the problem after and before the differentiation-by-parts procedure, respectively. Combining these two, we obtain the operator

\Gamma^{N}[v]=\begin{cases}\widehat{\Gamma_{1}[v]}(n)&|n|\geq N\\
\widehat{\Gamma_{2}[v]}(n)&|n|<N,\end{cases}

which acts as a workaround for the lack of any time factor
T
on the
B
terms.

Since
B_{1}
,
B_{2},
and
B_{3}
are all smoother, we find

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\Gamma_{1}(v)}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T(\|v\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{3}+\|v\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{5})

\displaystyle+N^{-\alpha}(\|v\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{2}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}+\|v\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{5}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{5})+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}},

for some
\alpha>0
. We similarly find

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\Gamma_{2}(v)}\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T(N^{3}\|v\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{3}+N\|v\|_{C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{3})+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}.

Since similar bounds hold for the difference, we see that we may select
N=N(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}})
large enough to make

N^{-\alpha}(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{5})\ll\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}},

and then
T
small enough to make both

\displaystyle T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{3}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{5})

\displaystyle\ll\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}

\displaystyle T(N^{3}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{2}+N\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}^{3})

\displaystyle\ll\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}.

This enables us to perform a contraction argument in
C^{0}_{t}H^{s}_{x}
for
s>1
, and hence standard arguments allow us to conclude unconditional well-posedness.
∎

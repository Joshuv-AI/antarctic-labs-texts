Criticality of global monopole charges in diverse dimensions
Hong-Ming Cui and Zhong-Ying Fan

Abstract




In this work, we construct charged AdS black holes with a global monopole charge in diverse dimensions and study the thermodynamics. We find a critical monopole charge below which the solution exhibits Van-der Waals like behaviors. In the context of holography, this could be intepreted using the boundary degrees of freedoms. As an example, we study the phase diagram in the four dimensions analytically. We further analyze the microstructures of the solution using Ruppeiner geometry. We find that repulsive interactions dominates for black holes in a wide range of temperatures. However, around the critical point, attractive interactions is dominant and the Ruppeiner scalar curvature shows universal behaviors: it has a critical exponent
2
and coefficient
-1/8
in diverse dimensions. Universality of the results is interpreted from the scaling behavior of free energy near the critical point for Van-der Waals like fluids.

1
Introduction

Einstein’s gravity with a negative cosmological constant admits black hole solutions in asymptotically anti-de Sitter (AdS) spacetimes. Generally the solutions have richer phase structures relative to the asymptotically flat counterparts. In the past decade, people was interested in studying extended thermodynamics of AdS black holes, in which coupling constants in the Lagrangian were taken as thermodynamic variables. In
[
1
]
, by treating the cosmological constant as a thermodynamic variable, referred to as thermodynamic pressure
P
, it was established that charged AdS black holes exhibit
P-V
criticality, similar to the liquid-gas transition of Van-der Waals fluids. More interestingly, Ruppeiner geometry associated to thermodynamic fluctuations of the volume (or pressure) partly illustrates the microstructures of the black holes
[
2
,
3
]
.
Recently the thermodynamics of charged AdS black holes was further extended by introducing central charges in the boundary CFTs
[
4
,
5
]
. This corresponds to varying both the cosmological constant and the Newton’s gravitational constant. An advantage of this is the Van-der Waals like behaviors of AdS black holes can be interpreted in the context of holography. Generalization to higher curvature theories can be found in
[
6
,
7
]
.

In this work, we would like to construct global monopoles swallowed by charged AdS black holes in diverse dimensions and study the thermodynamics. Previously, the topic was partly studied in
[
8
,
9
]
in the four dimensions but using a ghost-like Goldstone field. This is however less interesting in physics since the solution is generally unstable. We resolve this issue by using a canonical multiplet and obtain the correct solution. Similar to the asymptotically flat case
[
10
]
, the global monopole produces a strong gravitational field but has little contributions to the black hole mass. However, the metric becomes globally singular because of a deficit solid angle. We define the conical deficit (up to a constant factor) to be the
global monopole charge
. We find that the solution exhibits new Van-der Waals like behaviors associated to this new charge and its conjugate. The critical point depends on the ratio of the electric charge
Q
to (certain power of) the central charge
C
in the boundary. As a consequence, all dual fluids with a same ratio will have the same critical point, leading to a critical line on the
Q-C
plane.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we consider Einstein-Maxwell theory with a cosmological constant, which is minimally coupled to a multiplet scalar field and construct charged AdS black holes with a global monopole charge in diverse dimensions. In section 3, we derive the first law for the solutions and extend the thermodynamics appropriately. In section 4, we study criticality of global monopole charges in the four dimensions in details. We derive the coexistence curve and analyze the phase diagram analytically. We further study the microstructures of the solution using the Ruppeiner geometry. In section 5, we generalize the results to higher dimensions and compute various critical exponents. We briefly conclude in section 6.

2
Charged black holes with a global monopole

Global monopoles form as a result of breaking of a global gauge symmetry. The simplest model producing a global monopole is described by a multiplet scalar field (
2
). To find charged AdS black hole solutions outside a global monopole, we consider Einstein-Maxwell theory with a cosmological constant, minimally coupled to a multiplet
\phi^{a}
in diverse dimensions

\displaystyle I=\int d^{D}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\left({\frac{1}{16\pi G}}(R-2\Lambda)-{\frac{1}{16\pi}}F^{2}+\mathcal{L}[\phi^{a}]\right)\,,

(1)

where
a=1\,,2\,,\cdots\,,D-1
,
\Lambda=-{\frac{(D-1)(D-2)}{2L^{2}}}
is the cosmological constant and
L
is the AdS radius. The Lagrangian of the scalar field is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\phi^{a})^{2}-V(\phi^{a})\,,\quad V(\phi^{a})={\frac{\lambda}{4}}(\phi^{a}\phi^{a}-\eta^{2})^{2}\,,

(2)

where
\eta
is the scale of gauge symmetry breaking. The model has a global O(
D-1
) symmetry, which will be spontaneously broken to O(
D-2
). For a typical unification scale
\eta\sim 10^{16}\mathrm{Gev}\ll\ell_{P}^{-1}
, where
\ell_{P}
is the Plank length. This is the four dimensional case. Generalization to diverse dimensions, the scale
\eta
should be bounded by
\ell_{P}^{-{\frac{D-2}{2}}}
on dimensional grounds.

The Einstein field equations is given by

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}-{\frac{1}{2}}Rg_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G\left(T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{EM}}+T_{\mu\nu}^{GM}\right)\,,

(3)

where

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{EM}}={\frac{1}{8\pi}}\left(F_{\mu\alpha}F^{\alpha}_{\,\,\,\nu}-{\frac{1}{4}}g_{\mu\nu}F^{2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{GM}=\partial_{\mu}\phi^{a}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{a}-g_{\mu\nu}\left[{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\phi^{a})^{2}+V(\phi^{a})\right]\,.

(4)

The equation of motion of the scalar field reads

\square\phi^{a}={\frac{\partial V}{\partial\phi^{a}}}\,.

(5)

The generally static spherically symmetric charged solution takes the form of

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\tilde{f}(r)dt^{2}+f(r)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega_{D-2}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle A=a(r)\,dt\,,\qquad\phi^{a}=\eta h(r){\frac{x^{a}}{r}}\,,

(6)

where
d\Omega_{D-2}^{2}
is the metric of a unit
\mathrm{S}^{D-2}
and
x^{a}x^{a}=r^{2}
. Generally speaking, full solutions to
h(r)
and the metric functions
\tilde{f}(r)\,,f(r)
can only be found numerically. However, since most of the monopole energy is concentrated in a small region near the core, whose size is essentially determined by the scale of gauge symmetry breaking
\delta\sim\eta^{-{\frac{2}{D-2}}}
, we may take the monopole core
\delta\ll r_{+}
in diverse dimensions, where
r_{+}
denotes the black hole horizon radius. Under this approximation,
h(r)=1
and the potential
V(\phi^{a})
vanishes outside the horizon. The stress tensor of the multiplet greatly simplifies to

\displaystyle T^{0\mathrm{(GM)}}_{\,\,\,0}=T^{1\mathrm{(GM)}}_{\,\,\,1}=-{\frac{(D-2)\eta^{2}}{2r^{2}}}\,,\qquad T^{i\mathrm{(GM)}}_{\,\,\,i}=-{\frac{(D-4)\eta^{2}}{2r^{2}}}\,,

(7)

where
i=1\,,2\,,\cdots\,,D
. Consequently, the metric function
\tilde{f}(r)=f(r)
so that the electrostatic potential can be solved immediately from Maxwell’s equation

a(r)=-{\frac{4\pi Q}{(D-3)\Omega_{D-2}\,r^{D-3}}}\,,

(8)

where
\Omega_{D-2}=2\pi^{(D-1)/2}/\Gamma\left({\frac{D-1}{2}}\right)
is the volume of a unit
\mathrm{S}^{D-2}
and
Q
is the electric charge carried by the black hole. Finally, the metric function can be solved as

f(r)=1-2\zeta-{\frac{16\pi GM}{(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}\,r^{D-3}}}+{\frac{32\pi^{2}GQ^{2}}{(D-1)(D-3)\Omega_{D-2}^{2}\,r^{2D-6}}}+{\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}}\,,

(9)

where

\displaystyle\zeta={\frac{4\pi G\eta^{2}}{D-3}}\,,

(10)

is referred to as the
global monopole charge
. Clearly the presence of a global monopole charge does not contribute to the black hole mass
1
1

1

Precisely speaking, the monopole has energy
M_{\mathrm{core}}\sim\eta^{{\frac{2}{D-2}}}
which is much less than the black hole mass.
. All its physical effect is inducing a conical deficit at asymptotical infinity. To see this, let
t\rightarrow t/\sqrt{1-2\zeta}
,
r\rightarrow r\sqrt{1-2\zeta}
, one finds asymptotically

ds^{2}\Big|_{r\rightarrow\infty}=-\left({\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}}+1\right)dt^{2}+{\frac{dr^{2}}{{\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}}+1}}+(1-2\zeta)\,r^{2}d\Omega_{D-2}^{2}\,.

(11)

Clearly there exists a conical deficit for the solid angle
\Delta=\Omega_{D-2}\,\zeta
. Besides, the monopole charge should be upper bounded as
\zeta<1/2
, which translates into
\eta<\sqrt{{\frac{D-3}{8\pi}}}\,\ell_{P}^{-{\frac{D-2}{2}}}
.

3
Extended black hole thermodynamics

We proceed to study thermodynamics of the charged AdS black holes with a global monopole. Since the monopole charge is also an integration constant of the solution, the first law of thermodynamics acquires an extra contribution from a new pair of conjugates
(U\,,\zeta)

\displaystyle dM=TdS+\Phi dQ+Ud\zeta\,,

(12)

where
U
is the chemical potential dual to
\zeta
.
The various quantities are given by

\displaystyle M={\frac{(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}\,r_{+}^{D-3}}{16\pi G}}\left(Z+{\frac{r_{+}^{2}}{L^{2}}}\right)+{\frac{2\pi Q^{2}}{(D-3)\Omega_{D-2}\,r_{+}^{D-3}}}\,,

\displaystyle T={\frac{1}{4\pi r_{+}}}\left[(D-3)Z+(D-1){\frac{r_{+}^{2}}{L^{2}}}\right]-{\frac{8\pi GQ^{2}}{(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}^{2}r_{+}^{2D-5}}}\,,

\displaystyle S={\frac{\Omega_{D-2}\,r_{+}^{D-2}}{4G}}\,,\quad\Phi={\frac{4\pi Q}{(D-3)\Omega_{D-2}\,r_{+}^{D-3}}}\,,\quad U=-{\frac{(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}}{8\pi Gr_{+}^{D-3}}}\,,

(13)

where
Z\equiv 1-2\zeta
is a useful combination here and after. It turns out that there exists a critical monopole charge below which the solution shows new Van-der Waals like behaviors in diverse dimensions.

However before studying this in details, we would like to extend the black hole thermodynamics by introducing the thermodynamic pressure
[
1
]
and the central charge
[
4
]
dual to the boundary

\displaystyle P=-{\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G}}\,,

\displaystyle C={\frac{kL^{D-2}}{16\pi G}}\,,

(14)

where
k
is a constant depending on details of holographic systems.
The first law is extended to

\displaystyle dM=TdS+\Phi dQ+Ud\zeta+V_{C}dP+\mu dC\,,

(15)

where the thermodynamic volume
V_{C}
and the chemical potential
\mu
dual to the central charge are given by

\displaystyle V_{C}={\frac{\Omega_{D-2}r_{+}^{D-1}}{D(D-1)}}+{\frac{\Omega_{D-2}L^{2}Zr_{+}^{D-3}}{D(D-1)}}+{\frac{32\pi^{2}GL^{2}Q^{2}}{D(D-1)(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}r_{+}^{D-3}}}\,,

\displaystyle\mu=-{\frac{2(D-1)\Omega_{D-2}r_{+}^{D-1}}{DkL^{D}}}+{\frac{2\Omega_{D-2}Zr_{+}^{D-3}}{DkL^{D-2}}}+{\frac{64\pi^{2}GQ^{2}}{D(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}kL^{D-2}r_{+}^{D-3}}}\,.

(16)

Here the thermodynamic volume
V_{C}
should not be confused with the original one defined at constant
G

[
11
]
. The two volumes are related by

V=V_{C}+\mu{\frac{\partial C}{\partial P}}\Big|_{G}=V_{C}-{\frac{(D-2)\mu C}{2P}}\,.

(17)

Though it is widely believed that
V
obeys the reverse isoperimetric inequality, this new thermodynamic volume
V_{C}
generally does not
[
4
]
. The Smarr relation is given by

M={\frac{D-2}{D-3}}\big(TS+\mu C\big)+\Phi Q-{\frac{2}{D-3}}PV_{C}\,,

(18)

where the monopole charge does not have any contributions (since
\zeta
is dimensionless).

Dependence of the various thermodynamic quantities on the pressure
P
and the central charge
C
can be seen by using the inverse formula

\displaystyle G={\frac{1}{16\pi}}\left({\frac{k}{C}}\right)^{2/D}\left[{\frac{(D-1)(D-2)}{P}}\right]^{1-2/D}\,,\quad L=\left[{\frac{(D-1)(D-2)C}{kP}}\right]^{1/D}\,.

(19)

Since we are interested in
U-\zeta
criticality, we will work in canonical ensemble which has fixed charges
(Q\,,C\,,P\,,Z)
or equivalently
(Q\,,G\,,L\,,\zeta)
. The equation of state of the dual fluid can be formally written as
T=T(U\,,\zeta)
. Explicit expressions for the equation of state will be presented later when we focus on certain dimensions. Here we shall point out that the existence of a critical point and the Van-der Waals-like behaviors associated to the monopole charge does not rely on the extended thermodynamics introduced above. All we have done in this part is trying to interpret the results in the context of holography, as will be shown later.

4
Monopole charge criticality in 4-dimensions

To study the
U-\zeta
criticality in details, we consider the four dimensions at first. The black hole solution reads

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+{\frac{1}{f(r)}}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\,,

\displaystyle f(r)=1-2\zeta-{\frac{2GM}{r}}+{\frac{GQ^{2}}{r^{2}}}+{\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}}\,.

(20)

The relevant thermodynamic quantities are given by

\displaystyle M={\frac{r_{+}^{3}}{2GL^{2}}}+{\frac{Z}{2G}}r_{+}+{\frac{Q}{2r_{+}}}\,,

\displaystyle T={\frac{3r_{+}}{4\pi L^{2}}}+{\frac{Z}{4\pi r_{+}}}-{\frac{GQ^{2}}{4\pi r_{+}^{3}}}\,,

\displaystyle S={\frac{\pi r_{+}^{2}}{G}}\,,\quad U=-{\frac{r_{+}}{G}}\,.

(21)

Furthermore, the equation of state
T(U\,,\zeta)
can be written explicitly as

T=-{\frac{2Z}{U}}\sqrt{{\frac{2PC}{3k}}}-{\frac{3kU}{64\pi^{2}C}}+{\frac{32\pi PCQ^{2}}{3kU^{3}}}\,,

(22)

where we have adopted the relation
U=-8\pi r_{+}\sqrt{{\frac{2PC}{3k}}}
. Using inflection point condition
0=\partial T/\partial r_{+}=\partial^{2}T/\partial r_{+}^{2}
, we obtain the critical point

\displaystyle r_{c}={\frac{3^{1/4}\sqrt{Q}}{2^{3/4}\pi^{1/4}P^{1/4}}}\,,

\displaystyle Z_{c}={\frac{3Q}{2}}\sqrt{{\frac{k}{\pi C}}}\,,

\displaystyle T_{c}={\frac{P^{1/4}}{6^{1/4}\pi^{5/4}}}\sqrt{{\frac{kQ}{C}}}\,.

(23)

It is immediately seen that the critical monopole charge depends on the ratio of electric charge to (square root of) the central charge in the boundary. In fact, this gives a critical line on the
Q-C
plane: all the dual fluids with a same ratio
Q/\sqrt{C}
will have the same critical point. Interestingly, the product

{\frac{T_{c}r_{c}}{Z_{c}}}={\frac{1}{3\pi}}\,,

(24)

is a constant, independent of the charges on the boundary (but unfortunately we do not find a proper interpretation for this). Define dimensionless variables
t=T/T_{c}
,
u=U/U_{c}
,
z=Z/Z_{c}
. The equation of state translates into the “law of corresponding state”

\displaystyle t=\alpha u+{\frac{\beta}{u}}+{\frac{\gamma}{u^{3}}}\,,

(25)

where the various parameters are given by

\alpha={\frac{3}{8}}\,,\quad\beta={\frac{3z}{4}}\,,\quad\gamma=-{\frac{1}{8}}\,.

(26)

This is a very useful relation in solving the coexistence curve as well as computing various critical exponents.

\zeta<\zeta_{c}

F=M-TS

It is easily seen that for a smaller monopole charge
\zeta<\zeta_{c}
, the swallowtail behavior emerges. It implies a first order phase transition for a small monopole charge but no such transition for a large monopole charge. The situation is somehow similar to the electric charge criticality
[

4.1
Coexistence curve and the phase diagram

A remarkable feature of the four dimensional solution is the coexistence curve can be derived analytically. We will generalize the method developed in
[
13
]
and adopt the Maxwell’s area law on the
T-S
plane.

Figure 3:
Maxwell’s area law on the
T-S
graph. The oscillatory region is unphysical and should be replaced by an isotherm
t^{*}
, according to the area law. The rectangular region below the isotherm is the coexistence region. We have set
z={\frac{9}{8}}
, that is
\zeta={\frac{18\zeta_{c}-1}{16}}
and
t^{*}\approx 1.09
.

For this purpose, it will be convenient for us to work with the law of corresponding state (
25
). However, to keep the discussions below as general as possible, we will not specify the parameters
(\alpha\,,\beta\,,\gamma)
until the end of this subsection. The equal area law gives

\displaystyle t^{*}\left(u_{g}^{2}-u_{l}^{2}\right)=\int^{u^{2}_{g}}_{u^{2}_{l}}t(u)du^{2}\,,

(28)

where
u_{l}
and
u_{g}
(
u_{l}<u_{g}
) denotes the normalized chemical potential of the small and the large black hole on the the isotherm
t=t^{*}
, respectively (see Fig.
3
for an illustration). Integrating the law of corresponding state yields

t^{*}={\frac{2}{u_{g}+u_{l}}}\left[{\frac{\alpha}{3}}(u^{2}_{g}+u^{2}_{l}+u_{g}\,u_{l})+\beta+{\frac{\gamma}{u_{g}\,u_{l}}}\right]\,.

(29)

On the other hand, the two phases must have the same temperature

\displaystyle t^{*}=t(u_{g})=t(u_{l})\,,

\displaystyle t^{*}={\frac{t(u_{g})+t(u_{l})}{2}}\,,

(30)

which leads to

\displaystyle\left(u_{g}+u_{l}\right)^{2}-{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}}(u_{g}u_{l})^{3}+{\frac{\beta}{\gamma}}(u_{g}u_{l})^{2}-u_{g}u_{l}=0\,,

\displaystyle t^{*}={\frac{u_{g}+u_{l}}{2}}\left[\alpha+{\frac{\beta}{u_{g}\,u_{l}}}+\gamma{\frac{(u_{g}+u_{l})^{2}-u_{g}\,u_{l}}{(u_{g}\,u_{l})^{3}}}\right]\,.

(31)

It follows that the equations (
29
) and (
31
) can be solved analytically by taking
u_{g}u_{l}
and
u_{g}+u_{l}
as independent variables. One finds

\displaystyle\left[\alpha(u_{g}u_{l})^{2}+3\gamma\right]\left[\beta u_{g}\,u_{l}-\alpha(u_{g}u_{l})^{2}+3\gamma\right]=0\,,

(32)

which gives two set solutions

\displaystyle\alpha(u_{g}u_{l})^{2}+3\gamma=0\quad

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\quad u_{g}u_{l}=\sqrt{-{\frac{3\gamma}{\alpha}}}\,,

(33)

\displaystyle\beta u_{g}\,u_{l}-\alpha(u_{g}u_{l})^{2}+3\gamma=0\quad

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\quad u_{g}u_{l}={\frac{\beta\pm\sqrt{\beta^{2}+12\alpha\gamma}}{2\alpha}}\,\,.

(34)

However, the second solution is unphysical since it will lead to
u^{2}_{g}=u^{2}_{l}={\frac{\beta\pm\sqrt{\beta^{2}+12\alpha\gamma}}{2\alpha}}
. From (
33
), we obtain the physical solutions

\displaystyle u^{2}_{g,\,l}={\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\left[3\beta-4\sqrt{-3\alpha\gamma}\pm\sqrt{3(3\beta-2\sqrt{-3\alpha\gamma})(\beta-2\sqrt{-3\alpha\gamma})}\,\right]\,,

(35)

where the sign “
+
” (“
-
”) corresponds to the saturated large (small) black hole, respectively.
Substituing the parameters (
26
) into (
35
) and (
29
), we finally obtain

\displaystyle u_{g,l}=\sqrt{3z-2\pm\sqrt{(3z-1)(3z-3)}}\,,

(36)

and the coexistence curve on the
t-z
plane

\displaystyle t^{*}(z)=\sqrt{{\frac{3z-1}{2}}}\,.

(37)

Notice that
z>1/3
is guaranteed by the upper bound of the monopole charge
\zeta<1/2
.

4.2
Rupepeiner geometry and microstructures

Figure 6:
Density plot for the normalized scalar curvature
R_{N}
in the
D=4
dimensions.

Having established the phase diagram, we move to study microstructures of the dual fluids using Ruppeiner geometry. In thermodynamic fluctuation theory, the distance between two neighboring fluctuation states
\Delta l^{2}
is related to the probability of finding the macroscopic system between the neighboring states
P(x^{0}\,,\,\cdots,x^{N})\varpropto e^{-{\frac{1}{2}}\Delta l^{2}}
, where

\displaystyle\Delta l^{2}=-{\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial x^{\mu}\partial x^{\nu}}}\Delta x^{\mu}\Delta x^{\nu}\,.

(39)

We choose the temperature
T
and the chemical potential
U
to be the independent fluctuation variables. The line element reads

\displaystyle dl^{2}={\frac{C_{U}}{T^{2}}}dT^{2}-{\frac{1}{T}}\left({\frac{\partial\zeta}{\partial U}}\right)_{T}dU^{2}\,,

(40)

where
C_{U}=T\left({\frac{\partial S}{\partial T}}\right)_{U}
is the heat capacity at constant
U
. However, since
U\propto\sqrt{S}
, the heat capacity
C_{U}
vanishes identically. To solve this issue, we adopt the trick in
[
3
]
: taking
C_{U}
to be a nonzero constant at first and then sending
C_{U}\rightarrow 0
. In this limit, the Ruppeiner scalar curvature diverges as
R\propto 1/C_{U}
. Then we define a normalized scalar curvature
R_{N}\equiv RC_{U}
and propose that it describes the microstructures of the dual fluids: a positive (negative)
R_{N}
indicating that repulsive (attractive) interactions dominates.

Evaluation of the normalized scalar curvature yields

\displaystyle R_{N}=-{\frac{(3u^{4}+1)(8tu^{3}-3u^{4}-1)}{2(4tu^{3}-3u^{4}-1)^{2}}}\,.

(41)

The behavior of
R_{N}
is depicted in Fig.
6
. Roughly speaking,
R_{N}
is very small in most of the parameters space. However, close to the curve

\displaystyle t_{div}={\frac{3u^{4}+1}{4u^{3}}}\,,

(42)

the curvature
R_{N}
changes dramatically. This curve exactly corresponds to a divergent heat capacity
C_{\zeta}
defined at constant monopole charge, signaling critical point of the transition. On this curve,
R_{N}
goes to negative infinity. Besides, there exists a sign-changing curve across which
R_{N}
changes the sign

\displaystyle t_{0}={\frac{3u^{4}+1}{8u^{3}}}\,.

(43)

It follows that for
t>t_{0}
,
R_{N}<0
, implying the attractive interactions dominates in this region. Otherwise,
R_{N}
will be positive and the repulsive interactions dominates instead.

In Fig.
7
, we illustrate the coexistence curve (black solid line), the sign-changing curve (red dot-dashed line) as well as the diverging curve (blue dashed line), respectively. The shaded (unshaded) regions correspond to a positive (negative) curvature
R_{N}
, indicating that the repulsive (attractive) interactions dominates. However, any features above the coexistence curve are tentative since the equation of state we adopted is inapplicable in the coexistence region. The results that we have confidence to state are those below the coexistence curve. In region II, repulsive interactions dominates in the microstructures, including most of the saturated small black holes. However, around the critical point, attractive interactions is dominant. To explain this, let us expand the scalar curvature in powers of
\delta t=t-1
. We deduce

\displaystyle R_{N}=-{\frac{1}{8\delta t^{2}}}\pm{\frac{1}{(2\delta t)^{3/2}}}+\cdots\,,

(44)

where “+/-” sign corresponds to the saturated small/large black holes, respectively. To leading order
R_{N}
has a critical exponent
2
and the coefficient

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta t\rightarrow 0}R_{N}\delta t^{2}=-{\frac{1}{8}}\,.

(45)

In fact, these results are universal to charged AdS black holes with a global monopole in diverse dimensions and match with the Van-der Waals fluid perfectly
[
3
]
. Universality of the result could be interpreted from the scaling behavior of free energy near the critical point, see the next section.

5
Monopole charge criticality in higher dimensions

We continue studying the critical phenomenon of monopole charges in higher dimensions. The equation of state
T=T(U\,,\zeta)
is listed as follows

\displaystyle T={\frac{1}{4\pi r_{+}}}\left[(D-3)Z+(D-1){\frac{r_{+}^{2}}{L^{2}}}\right]-{\frac{8\pi GQ^{2}}{(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}^{2}r_{+}^{2D-5}}}\,,

(46)

where

\displaystyle r_{+}^{D-3}=-{\frac{(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}}{8\pi GU}}\,.

(47)

Evaluation of the critical point using the inflection point condition yields

\displaystyle r_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[{\frac{2(D-3)(2D-5)\pi Q^{2}}{\Omega_{D-2}^{2}P}}\right]^{{\frac{1}{2D-4}}}\,,

(48)

\displaystyle Z_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{2\big[(D-2)(D-1)\big]^{{\frac{D-2}{D}}}[2\pi(D-3)(2D-5)]^{{\frac{1}{D-2}}}}{(2D-5)\pi}}{\frac{k^{{\frac{2}{D}}}Q^{{\frac{2}{D-2}}}}{\Omega_{D-2}^{{\frac{1}{D-2}}}C^{{\frac{2}{D}}}P^{{\frac{D-4}{D(D-2)}}}}}\,,

(49)

\displaystyle T_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{\big[(D-2)(D-1)\big]^{{\frac{D-2}{D}}}[2\pi(D-3)(2D-5)]^{{\frac{1}{2D-4}}}}{(2D-5)\pi}}{\frac{k^{{\frac{2}{D}}}Q^{{\frac{1}{D-2}}}}{\Omega_{D-2}^{{\frac{1}{D-2}}}C^{{\frac{2}{D}}}P^{{\frac{3D-8}{2D(D-2)}}}}}\,.

(50)

Again the critical monopole charge depends on the ratio of the electric charge to (certain power of) the central charge:
Q/C^{{\frac{D-2}{D}}}
. In addition, the product

\displaystyle{\frac{T_{c}r_{c}}{Z_{c}}}={\frac{(D-3)^{2}}{(2D-5)\pi}}\,,

(51)

turns out to be a constant in any given dimensions.

Working with dimensionless variables
t=T/T_{c}\,,u=U/U_{c}\,,z=Z/Z_{c}
, we deduce the law of corresponding state

\displaystyle t={\frac{2D-5}{4(D-2)}}u^{{\frac{1}{D-3}}}+{\frac{2D-5}{4(D-3)}}zu^{-{\frac{1}{D-3}}}-{\frac{u^{-{\frac{2D-5}{D-3}}}}{4(D-2)(D-3)}}\,,

(52)

and the normalized free energy
f=F/F_{c}

\displaystyle f={\frac{D-1}{4}}z\,u+{\frac{D-1}{4(D-2)u}}-{\frac{(D-3)^{2}}{4(D-2)}}\,u^{{\frac{D-1}{D-3}}}\,.

(53)

The
F-T
digrams for higher dimensional solutions are depicted in Fig.

. The existence of multi-phases and the transitions between them (the coexistence curve) for a small monopole charge
\zeta<\zeta_{c}
as well as the full phase digram are quite similar to the four dimensional case. We shall not discuss them anymore.

To proceed, we study the microstructures for higher dimensional solutions using the Ruppeiner geometry. We deduce

\displaystyle R_{N}=-{\frac{\left[(2D-5)\,u^{{\frac{2(D-2)}{D-3}}}+1\right]\left[4(D-2)t\,u^{{\frac{2D-5}{D-3}}}-(2D-5)\,u^{{\frac{2(D-2)}{D-3}}}-1\right]}{2\left[2(D-2)t\,u^{{\frac{2D-5}{D-3}}}-(2D-5)\,u^{{\frac{2(D-2)}{D-3}}}-1\right]^{2}}}\,.

(54)

Again there exists a diverging curve

\displaystyle t_{div}={\frac{(2D-5)\,u^{{\frac{2(D-2)}{D-3}}}+1}{2(D-2)\,u^{{\frac{2D-5}{D-3}}}}}\,,

(55)

on which
R_{N}
goes to negative infinity and a sign-changing curve

\displaystyle t_{0}={\frac{(2D-5)\,u^{{\frac{2(D-2)}{D-3}}}+1}{4(D-2)\,u^{{\frac{2D-5}{D-3}}}}}\,,

(56)

across which the attractive and the repulsive interactions changes from one another. In Fig.

, we depict these curves as well as the coexistence lines (which are computed numerically) for
D=5
and

dimensions, respectively. Similar to the four dimensional case, small black holes are dominated by repulsive interactions whereas the large black holes (at higher temperatures
t>t_{0}
) are dominated by attractive interactions. In particular, attractions always dominates around the critical point. The leading order behavior of the scalar curvature (
45
) near the critical point is universal to diverse dimensions.

Universality of the result could be interpreted using the two-scale factor universality hypothesis
[
14
]
. Near the critical point, the singular part of the free energy connected to critical phenomena can be promoted to be a generalized homogeneous function
[
15
,
16
]
:

\displaystyle F_{s}=c_{1}|\delta t|^{\beta(\delta+1)}Y\big(c_{2}\,v|\delta t|^{-\beta\delta}\big)\,,

(57)

where
\beta\,,\delta
are the critical exponents defined as usual (see next subsection),
v=u-1
is the order parameter and
Y()
is a smooth function, obeying the conditions
Y(0)<0
and
Y^{\prime}(0)=0
. Note that the constants
c_{1}\,,c_{2}
are two scale factors, which are material dependent and differentiate between systems even in the same universality class. Actually, in modern approach to critical phenomena, the behavior of free energy can be elegantly explained using renormalization group analysis in quantum field theory.

By straightforward calculations, we deduce

\displaystyle R={\frac{\beta(\beta-\alpha)T_{c}}{2(2-\alpha)(1-\alpha)c_{1}Y(0)}}\,\delta t^{\alpha-2}\,,

(58)

\displaystyle C_{U}=-{\frac{(2-\alpha)(1-\alpha)c_{1}Y(0)}{T_{c}}}\,\delta t^{-\alpha}\,,

(59)

where we have made use of the scaling equation
2-\alpha=\beta(\delta+1)
. It is immediately seen that the critical behaviors of both
R
and
C_{U}
depend on details of the fluids, through the scale factor
c_{1}
. However, the leading order behavior of the normalized curvature
R_{N}
is universal

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta t\rightarrow 0}R_{N}\delta t^{2}=-{\frac{\beta(\beta-\alpha)}{2}}\,,

(60)

depending only on the critical exponents
\alpha\,,\beta
. For Van-der Waals like fluids,
\alpha=0\,,\beta=1/2
, this reproduces (
45
) exactly. The result is robust since neither a constant
C_{U}
nor the limit
C_{U}\rightarrow 0
plays an essential role.

5.1
Critical exponents

We move to compute the various critical exponents for
U-\zeta
criticality in diverse dimensions. The exponents
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
,
\delta
are defined as follows:

\bullet
Exponent
\alpha
describes the behavior of the specific heat at constant
U
:

\displaystyle C_{U}=T\left({\frac{\partial S}{\partial T}}\right)_{U}\propto\lvert\delta t\rvert^{-\alpha}\,.

(61)

\bullet
Exponent
\beta
describes the behavior of the order parameter
u_{g}-u_{l}
on the given isotherm

\displaystyle u_{g}-u_{l}\propto\lvert\delta t\rvert^{\beta}\,.

(62)

\bullet
Exponent
\gamma
determines the behavior of the isothermal compressibility
\kappa_{T}

\displaystyle\kappa_{T}=-{\frac{1}{U}}\left({\frac{\partial U}{\partial\zeta}}\right)_{T}\propto\lvert\delta t\rvert^{-\gamma}\,.

(63)

\bullet
Exponent
\delta
describes the following behavior on the critical isotherm
T=T_{c}
:

\displaystyle\lvert\zeta-\zeta_{c}\rvert\propto\lvert U-U_{c}\rvert^{\delta}\,.

(64)

Firstly, since the entropy
S
is a function of
U
, the specific heat
C_{U}
vanishes identically. This implies
\alpha=0
.

To compute
\beta
, we first rewrite the law of corresponding state as

\displaystyle z={\frac{4(D-3)}{2D-5}}(\delta t+1)(\upsilon+1)^{{\frac{1}{D-3}}}-{\frac{D-3}{D-2}}(\upsilon+1)^{{\frac{2}{D-3}}}+{\frac{1}{(D-2)(2D-5)(\upsilon+1)^{2}}}\,.

(65)

Expanding the equation to relevant orders in the vicinity of the critical point yields

\displaystyle z=1+{\frac{4D-12}{2D-5}}\delta t+{\frac{4}{2D-5}}\delta t\upsilon-{\frac{2}{3(D-3)^{2}}}\upsilon^{3}+O(\delta t\upsilon^{2}\,,\,\upsilon^{4})\,.

(66)

During the phase transition, the temperature remains a constant, which gives

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=1+{\frac{4D-12}{2D-5}}\delta t+{\frac{4}{2D-5}}\delta t\upsilon_{g}-{\frac{2}{3(D-3)^{2}}}\upsilon_{g}^{3}

(67)

\displaystyle=1+{\frac{4D-12}{2D-5}}\delta t+{\frac{4}{2D-5}}\delta t\upsilon_{l}-{\frac{2}{3(D-3)^{2}}}\upsilon_{l}^{3}\,,

Besides, using Maxwell’s area law, one has

\displaystyle 0=\int^{\upsilon_{g}}_{\upsilon_{l}}\upsilon dz=\int^{\upsilon_{g}}_{\upsilon_{l}}\left[{\frac{4\delta t}{2D-5}}-{\frac{2\upsilon^{2}}{3(D-3)^{2}}}\right]\upsilon d\upsilon=0\,.

(68)

Combining the equations, one finds the solution is
\upsilon_{g}=-\upsilon_{l}\propto\sqrt{-\delta t}
. This leads to
\beta={\frac{1}{2}}
.

Differentiating (
66
), we get

\displaystyle\left({\frac{\partial U}{\partial\zeta}}\right)_{T}={\frac{2D-5}{4}}{\frac{\upsilon_{c}\,z}{z_{c}\,\delta t}}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\gamma=1\,.

(69)

Finally, according to (
66
), the critical isotherm
\delta t=0
adheres to

\displaystyle z-1=-{\frac{2\upsilon^{3}}{3(D-3)^{2}}}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\delta=3\,.

(70)

This completes our derivations.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we constructed charged AdS black holes with a global monopole in diverse dimensions. The presence of a monopole charge
\zeta
and its thermodynamic conjugate
U
contributes to the first law of thermodynamics. We found that there exists a critical monopole charge
\zeta_{c}
below which the solution exhibits Van-der Waals like behaviors. In the context of holography, this could be interpreted using the boundary degrees of freedoms. The critical point depends on the ratio of the electric charge to (certain power of) the central charge.

As an example, we derived the coexistence curve in the four dimensions and studied the phase diagram analytically. The results of higher dimensions are qualitatively similar. We also investigated the microstructures of the solution using thermodynamic geometry. We defined a normalized scalar curve
R_{N}
and established that the leading order behavior (
45
) near the critical point is universal to diverse dimensions. Universality of the results is interpreted from the scaling behavior of free energy near the critical point for Van-der Waals like fluids. All these results improve our understanding of the critical phenomenon for charged AdS black holes with a global monopole charge significantly.

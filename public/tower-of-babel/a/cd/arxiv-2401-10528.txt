Irreducibility of some Quot schemes on Nodal curves
Parvez Rasul

Abstract
. Let C C be an integral projective nodal curve over ℂ \mathbb{C} , of arithmetic genus g ⩾ 2 g\geqslant 2 . Let E E be a vector bundle on C C of rank r r and degree e e . Let Quot C / ℂ ​ ( E , k , d ) {\rm Quot}_{C/\mathbb{C}}(E,k,d) denote the Quot scheme of quotients of E E of rank k k and degree d d . We show that Quot C / ℂ ​ ( E , k , d ) {\rm Quot}_{C/\mathbb{C}}(E,k,d) is irreducible for d ≫ 0 d\gg 0 .

1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a reduced and irreducible projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
of arithmetic genus
g
.
For a coherent sheaf
F
on
C
of rank
k
, the degree of
F
is defined as

(1.1)

\deg F:=\chi(F)-k(1-g)\,.

Let
E
be a vector bundle on
C
of rank
r
and degree
e
.
Let
{\rm Quot}_{C/\mathbb{C}}(E,k,d)
denote Grothendieck’s Quot scheme of coherent quotients of
E
of rank
k
and degree
d
.
We will use the simpler notation
Q_{k,d}(E)
to denote the quot scheme
{\rm Quot}_{C/\mathbb{C}}(E,k,d)
.
Quot schemes have played a central role in the study of vector bundles on curves, in particular for constructing and understanding geometry of the moduli spaces.
Quot schemes also arise as a compactification of the space of maps into Grassmannians.

When
C
is a smooth curve, the quot scheme

Q_{0,d}(E)
of torsion quotients of
E
is a smooth projective variety, which has been studied extensively.
We refer the reader to the recent works
[
BDH15
]
,
[
BGS22
]
,
[
OS23
]
,
[
OP21
]
and references therein.
There are few results when
C
is singular.
In
[
Reg82
]
, the author shows that if
C
is embeddable in a smooth surface then
the quot scheme
Q_{0,d}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus r}_{C})
is irreducible. It is also proved that the compactification of moduli spaces of vector bundles on such curves is irreducible.
Some sort of converse is proved in
[
KK81
]
, which shows that if
C
is not embeddable in a smooth surface then the quot scheme

Q_{0,d}(\omega)
is reducible, where
\omega
is the dualizing sheaf on
C
.
The authors use this to show that the compactified Jacobian of such
C
is reducible.
In
[
Ran05
]
, the punctual Hilbert scheme parametrizing degree-
d
subschemes of
C
supported at the node is shown to be a chain of
d-1
rational curves meeting transversely.
It is also proved that the relative Hilbert scheme
\textrm{Hilb}(X/B)
parametrizing length-
m
subschemes in the fibres of the family
X/B
is smooth, where
X
is a 1-parameter smooth family of curves over
B
.

We come to the case
k>0
and when the curve
C
is smooth.
In genus 0 case, when
E
is the trivial bundle, Stromme proved that the Quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus r}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})
is smooth and irreducible and computed its Picard group in
[
Str87
]
.
Let
C
be smooth of genus
g>0
. When
E
is trivial, it is proved in
[
BDW96
]
that the Quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus r})
is irreducible and generically smooth for
d\gg 0
.
The authors also show that
Q_{k,d}(\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus r})
is a local complete intersection for large
d
.
For any vector bundle
E
on
C
, it is proved in
[
PR03
]
that the quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(E)
is irreducible and generically smooth when
d\gg 0
.
The Picard group of the Quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(E)
is computed in
[
GS22
]
for
d\gg 0
.
The authors also show that if
d\gg 0
then the Quot scheme is an integral, normal variety which is a local complete intersection and locally factorial.

In this article we extend some of the above results to the case when
C
is a nodal curve.
Degeneration techniques have found many applications in moduli problems and in enumerative geometry,
for example, see
[
LW15
]
,
[
Gol19
]
and references therein.
Consequently, it is interesting to study quot schemes over nodal curves.
In
[
Gol19
]
, the author defines a nice intersection theory on the quot scheme over nodal curves by relating it to the Quot schemes on the normalization.
In order to do this, the author shows that, for a very general vector bundle
E
on
C
, the unique top dimensional component of the quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(E)
is generically smooth and contains all the points corresponding to torsion-free quotients.

In this article we prove the following theorem.

Theorem

(Theorem
6.15
)
.

Let
C
be a reduced irreducible projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
of arithmetic genus
g\geqslant 2
with exactly one ordinary node and smooth elsewhere.
Let
E
be a vector bundle on
C
of rank
r
and degree
e
.
Let
k
be an integer such that
0<k<r
.
Then there is a number
d_{Q}(E,k)
such that if
d\geqslant d_{Q}
then the quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(E)
is irreducible and generically smooth of dimension
dr-ke+k(r-k)(1-g)
.

We use various results on the torsion-free sheaves on nodal curves from
[
Bho06
]
, in which the nodal curve is assumed to have genus
g\geqslant 2
.
For this reason, we impose the condition
g\geqslant 2
on the genus of the curve.

Remark 1.2
.

Throughout the article and hence in Theorem
6.15
, we have considered the curve
C
to have a single node. However we emphasize that all the results in the article can be seen to generalise easily to the case when
C
has more than one nodes.
So we have Theorem
6.15
for any integral projective nodal curve
C
of arithmetic genus
g>n
, where
n
is the number of nodes of
C
.

It would be interesting to know if a similar result holds when
C
is an integral projective curve.
From
[
Vak06
]
it is known that, in general the Hilbert schemes can be quite pathological.
In
[
GS22
]
, it is proved that
for large
d
, the quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(E)
over a smooth curve is an integral variety which is a local complete intersection and locally factorial.
It is interesting to know what kind of singularities occur in the quot schemes over nodal curves.

We will discuss our strategy briefly.
The strategy we use is similar to that in
[
PR03
, Section 6]
,
however, some modifications and new inputs are required.
It will be convenient for the reader if we first discuss the strategy of
[
PR03
]
, where the theorem is proved for smooth curves.

(1)

In Theorem 4.1 of
[
PR03
]
, the authors compute an upper bound for the dimension of the quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(E)
which is as follows:

\dim Q_{k,d}(E)\leqslant k(r-k)+(d-m(E,k))r,\quad\textrm{ for all }d\geqslant m(E,k)\,.

The number
m(E,k)
is the minimal degree of a quotient of
E
of rank
k
.

(2)

The authors prove that the quot scheme always contains a stable locally free quotient if
d
is large enough.
Then using the irreducibility of the moduli space of stable locally free sheaves on
C
, it is concluded that
there is a unique component of the quot scheme whose general point corresponds to a stable bundle quotient.
Let us call this component
\mathcal{P}
.
The dimension of
\mathcal{P}
is
dr-ke+k(r-k)(1-g)
.

(3)

The authors prove that for large
d
, a general vector bundle quotient of
E
can be deformed to a stable vector bundle quotient.
A key ingredient for this step is the upper bound for the dimension of the quot scheme given in step (
1
).
This proves the component
\mathcal{P}
contains all the points corresponding to locally free quotients.

(4)

Let
Z
denote the locus of points in the quot scheme which correspond to quotients with torsion.
The authors show that when
d
is large,

\dim Z<dr-ke+k(r-k)(1-g)
.
There is a cohomological lower bound for the dimension of any component of the quot scheme, which is
dr-ke+k(r-k)(1-g)
in this case.
This proves that the points in
Z
cannot be general in any component of the quot scheme.
This shows that a general point in any component corresponds to a locally free quotient and so the component has to be
\mathcal{P}
.
Thus, the quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(E)
is irreducible when
d
is sufficiently large.

In the case of nodal curves, we proceed as follows.
The first two steps are very similar to that in
[
PR03
]
.
However, dealing with quotients with torsion is substantially harder in the nodal case.

(a)

In a similar fashion, as in
[
PR03
]
, we show that there is a unique component of the quot scheme whose generic point corresponds to a stable locally free quotient, when
d
is large.
Let us call this component
\mathcal{P}
.
This component has dimension
dr-ke+k(r-k)(1-g)
.

(b)

Using Lemma 2.4 of
[
Bho06
]
,
we show that for large
d
, a general locally free quotient in the quot scheme can be deformed to a stable locally free quotient.
This proves that the component
\mathcal{P}
contains all the points corresponding to locally free quotients, when
d
is large.
Similarly using Lemma 2.3 of
[
Bho06
]
,
we show that for large
d
, a general torsion-free quotient in the quot scheme can be deformed to a locally free quotient.
This proves that
\mathcal{P}
contains all the points corresponding to torsion-free quotients, when
d
is large.
To show the existence of deformations mentioned above, some
H^{1}
vanishing results are required.
These results rely on dimension estimates of few subsets of the quot schemes.
For example, the codimension of the locus
Y_{\mu_{0}}=\{[E\to F]\in Q_{k,d}^{{\rm tf}}(E):\mu_{\min}(F)<\mu_{0}\}
increases linearly with
d
when
d\gg 0
(Lemma
3.21
).
This is implicit in
[
PR03
, Lemma 6.3]
, and is also used in the proof of
[
GS22
, Lemma 4.9]
.

(c)

It remains to deal with the locus of quotients with torsion.
Let
Z
denote the locus of points in the quot scheme which correspond to quotients with torsion.
It is natural to try and follow step (
4
) to deal with this case.
For this we need a good cohomological lower bound.
In the case of smooth curves, any coherent sheaf is direct sum of a locally free sheaf and a torsion sheaf.
However, for nodal curves, this is not the case.
As a result, computing a good cohomological lower bound becomes difficult.
Using
[
EL99
, Lemma 2]
, we compute the following lower bound

\textrm{{cohomological lower bound}}=dr-ke+k(r-k)(1-g)-(r-k)(2r+k)\,.

Unfortunately, unlike in the case of smooth curves (step (
4
)), the upper bound on the dimension of
Z
is not less than the
cohomological lower bound
.
Thus, the strategy in step (
4
) fails to generalize to the nodal case.

Instead, we do the following.
Let
Z^{g}
denote the subset of
Z
which contains all those quotients
[E\to F]
such that
F
is free at the node. Then
Z^{g}
is open in
Z
.
We observe that the dimension of
Z^{g}
is less than the
cohomological lower bound
and using this we conclude that
Z^{g}
is inside the component
\mathcal{P}
.
Define
Z^{b}:=Z\setminus Z^{g}
.
We show that if
Z^{b}
is open in a component say
\mathcal{W}(\neq\mathcal{P})
of the Quot scheme,
then a general point of
\mathcal{W}
is in the closure of
Z^{g}
.
This is a contradiction as
Z^{g}\subset\mathcal{P}
.
This proves that
Z^{b}
actually lies in the component
\mathcal{P}
.
To show that a general point
[q:E\to F]
of
\mathcal{W}
is in the closure of
Z^{g}
,
ideally, we would want a deformation of quotients whose general member corresponds to a point in
Z^{g}

and a special member corresponds to the point
[q:E\to F]
.
However, as
F
has torsion,
such a deformation cannot be constructed using
the same technique as in step (
b
).
Rather, we deform the inclusion
\ker q\hookrightarrow E
to an inclusion which gives a point in
Z^{g}
.
To construct such a deformation, we will require
\textnormal{Ext}^{1}(\ker q,E)
to vanish for a general point
[q:E\to F]
in
\mathcal{W}
.
In Proposition
6.8
, we prove that this condition is indeed satisfied if
Z^{b}
is open in
\mathcal{W}
.
This proof uses dimension estimate of the subset

X_{\mu_{0}}=\{[q:E\to F]\in Q_{k,d}(E):F\text{ is torsion free and }\mu_{\max}(\ker q)>\mu_{0}\}
(Lemma
3.29
),
which is obtained by applying Lemma
3.21
to the quot scheme
Q_{r-k,d-e}(E^{\vee})
.

The organization of the paper is as follows.
In section
2
, we go through some preliminary lemmas regarding the sheaves on nodal curves
and then we prove the main lemma which estimates a
(\hom-\textnormal{ext}^{1})
term.
This lemma directly gives us the cohomological lower bound for the quot scheme which is mentioned in step
(
c
)
.
In section
3
, we compute the dimension estimates of a few subsets of the quot scheme which will help to prove some
H^{1}
and
\textnormal{Ext}^{1}
vanishing results later.
Section
4
is occupied by the step (
a
) mentioned above.
This section is self-contained and does not involve any result from the first two sections.
In section
5
, we carry out the step (
b
).
In section
6
, we deal with the locus of points corresponding to the quotients with torsion and carry out step (
c
) to prove the main theorem
6.15
.

2.
Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a sheaf of rank
r
on a smooth projective curve
X
over
\mathbb{C}
.
For each rank
k<r
, define

m(G,k)=\min_{\rank(F)=k}\left\{\deg(F):F\textrm{ is a quotient of }G\right\}\,.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve and
Y
be a projective scheme over
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
\mathcal{G}
be a coherent sheaf on
X\times Y
which is flat over
Y
.
Then for a fixed
k<\rank(\G)
,
the function
y\mapsto m(\mathcal{G}_{y},k)
is lower semicontinuous as a function from
Y
to
\mathbb{Z}
.
As a consequence the set
\{m(\mathcal{G}_{y},k)\}_{y\in Y}
is finite for fixed
k
.

Proof.

Let
l
be an integer and define the set

U=\{y\in Y:m(\mathcal{G}_{y},k)>l\}\,.

We need to prove that
U
is open in
Y
.
Since the family of sheaves
\{\mathcal{G}_{y}\}_{y\in Y}
on
X
is bounded, by
[
HL10
, Lemma 1.7.6]
we have a sheaf
\mathcal{B}
on
X
such that each
\mathcal{G}_{y}
is a quotient of
\mathcal{B}
.
Now a quotient of
\mathcal{G}_{y}
is also a quotient of
\mathcal{B}
.
Hence
m(\mathcal{G}_{y},k)\geqslant m(\mathcal{B},k)
for all
y\in Y
.
If
l<m(\mathcal{B},k)
then
U=Y
and we are done.
If
m(\mathcal{B},k)\leqslant l
,
consider the relative Quot schemes

{\rm Quot}_{X\times Y/Y}(\mathcal{G},k,i)

for each
i
with
m(\mathcal{B},k)\leqslant i\leqslant l
.
Let
\sigma_{i}:{\rm Quot}_{X\times Y/Y}(\mathcal{G},k,i)\to Y

be the structure maps.
Then

U=Y\setminus\bigcup_{i=m(\mathcal{B},k)}^{l}\sigma_{i}({\rm Quot}_{X\times Y/Y}(\mathcal{G},k,i))\,.

Since each
\sigma_{i}
is proper map,
U
is open in
Y
.
∎

Let
C
be a reduced irreducible, projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
of arithmetic genus
g\geqslant 2
with
exactly one singular point
x
.
Further we assume that the singularity at
x
is an ordinary node.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{C,x}
denote the local ring of
C
at
x
and
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
denote the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{C,x}
.
Let
\kappa(x)
denote the residue field of
\mathcal{O}_{C,x}
.
We will also use
\kappa(x)
to mean the skyscraper sheaf on
C
supported at the nodal point
x
.

Definition 2.3

(Type of a sheaf)
.

Let
F
be a coherent sheaf on
C
of rank
k
such that the stalk
F_{x}
at the node
x
is torsion-free.
From
[
Ses82
, Proposition 2, Part 8, page 168]
,
we know that there exists an integer
a
satisfying
0\leqslant a\leqslant k

such that the stalk
F_{x}=\mathcal{O}_{C,x}^{\oplus k-a}\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{\oplus a}
.
We call such
F
torsion free at node of type
a
.
If
F
is torsion free at node of type
a
then
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(F\otimes\kappa(x))=k+a
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
A
and
B
be sheaves on
C
of rank
r_{A},r_{B}
and degree
d_{A},d_{B}
respectively.
Assume that
A
and
B
are
torsion-free of type
a
and
b
respectively. Then

\hom(B,A)-\textnormal{ext}^{1}(B,A)=r_{B}d_{A}-r_{A}d_{B}+r_{A}r_{B}(1-g)-ab\,.

Proof.

As
A
and
B
both are torsion-free, using
[
Bho06
, Lemma 2.5(B)]
we get that

\textnormal{ext}^{1}(B,A)=\textrm{h}^{1}(C,\mathscr{H}om(B,A))+2ab\,.

So we have

\displaystyle\hom(B,A)-\textnormal{ext}^{1}(B,A)

\displaystyle=\hom(B,A)-\textrm{h}^{1}(C,\mathscr{H}om(B,A))-2ab

\displaystyle=\textrm{h}^{0}(C,\mathscr{H}om(B,A))-\textrm{h}^{1}(C,\mathscr{H}om(B,A))-2ab

\displaystyle=\chi(\mathscr{H}om(B,A))-2ab.

Again
[
Bho06
, Lemma 2.5(B)]
says that the degree of the sheaf
\mathscr{H}om(B,A)
is

r_{B}d_{A}-r_{A}d_{B}+ab\,.

So using (
1.1
), we have

\begin{split}\hom(B,A)-\textnormal{ext}^{1}(B,A)&=\chi(\mathscr{H}om(B,A))-2ab\\
&=r_{B}d_{A}-r_{A}d_{B}+ab+r_{A}r_{B}(1-g)-2ab\\
&=r_{B}d_{A}-r_{A}d_{B}+r_{A}r_{B}(1-g)-ab\,.\end{split}

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

0\to B\to E\to A\to 0

be a short exact seqeunce of torsion-free sheaves and
E
be locally free.
Then type of
A
and type of
B
are equal.

Proof.

Let the type of
A
be
a
and the type of
B
be
b
.
Tensoring the exact sequence

0\to B\to E\to A\to 0

with
\kappa(x)
, we get a sequence

0\to\textnormal{Tor}^{1}(A,\kappa(x))\to B|_{x}\to E|_{x}\to A|_{x}\to 0\,.

Now
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(A|_{x})=\text{rank}(A)+a
and similarly
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(B|_{x})=\text{rank}(B)+b
.
So
b=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\textnormal{Tor}^{1}(A,\kappa(x))-a
.
Also
\textnormal{Tor}^{1}(A,\kappa(x))=\textnormal{Tor}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{\oplus a},\kappa(x))=2a
.
It follows that
b=a
.
∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{2}\,.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{2}}

Since
\pi
is an isomorphism outside the point
x
, it is clear that
\theta=\pi^{*}\psi
on
C\setminus\{x\}
.
Now the set over which these two maps agree is closed in
C

containing
C\setminus\{x\}
,
so it has to be the whole of
C
.
This proves that
\theta=\pi^{*}\psi
on
C
.
Hence
\theta:F_{1}\to F_{2}
is an isomorphism satisfying
\theta\circ\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{2}
, which proves that
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
corresponds to the same point in
Q_{k,d}^{{{\rm tf}},a}(E)
.
This proves injectivity of
f^{a}
on closed points.
∎

We will use the map
f_{a}
to bound the dimension of some subsets of the Quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(E)
using bounds of related subsets in the quot schemes
{\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\pi^{*}E,k,d^{\prime})
.
We start by computing dimension bound for a subset of
{\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\pi^{*}E,k,d^{\prime})
.
The following lemma is contained in the proof of
[
PR03
, Lemma 6.3]
.
However we give a detailed proof for the convenience of the reader.
For ease of notation let us denote
\pi^{*}E
by
\widetilde{E}
for rest of this section.

Let
d^{\prime}
,
d_{0}
be integers and let
k_{0}
be an integer such that
0<k_{0}<k
.
Let
\widetilde{Z}
be the subset of
{\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\widetilde{E},k,d^{\prime})
defined as follows,

(3.16)

\widetilde{Z}=\{[\widetilde{E}\to G]\in{\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\widetilde{E},k,d^{\prime}):\exists\textrm{ a quotient }G\to G_{0}\textrm{ of rank }k_{0}\textrm{ and degree }d_{0}\}\,.

Lemma 3.17
.

There exist constants
C_{1}(E,k,k_{0},d_{0})
and
\alpha_{1}(E,k,k_{0},d_{0})
such that
we have

\dim\widetilde{Z}\leqslant d^{\prime}(r-k_{0})+C_{1}\quad\quad\text{ for any }\quad d^{\prime}\geqslant\alpha_{1}\,.

Proof.

Let
A
denote the Quot scheme
{\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\widetilde{E},k_{0},d_{0})
.
Let
\mathscr{S}_{0}
denote the universal kernel on
\widetilde{C}\times A
.
Consider the relative quot scheme

D:={\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}\times A/A}(\mathscr{S}_{0},k-k_{0},d^{\prime}-d_{0})\,.

A closed point of
D
corresponds to a pair
([q_{0}],[\varphi])
where

[q_{0}:\widetilde{E}\to F_{0}]\in A
and

\varphi:\ker(q_{0})\to B
is a quotient of rank
k-k_{0}
and degree
d^{\prime}-d_{0}
.
Given such a point, we can construct a quotient
q:\widetilde{E}\to F

using the following push-out diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(q_{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0\phantom{\,.}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0\,.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

Clearly, the degree of
F
is
d^{\prime}

and so
q
corresponds to a point of
{\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\widetilde{E},k,d^{\prime})
.
It can be checked that there is a map of schemes

g:D\to{\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\widetilde{E},k,d^{\prime})

which sends the point
([q_{0}],[\varphi])
to the point
[q]
.
Given any point
[q:\widetilde{E}\to F]
in
\widetilde{Z}
,
we have a quotient
[q_{0}:\widetilde{E}\to F_{0}]\in A
(by definition of the locus
\widetilde{Z}
)
and we get a quotient

\ker q_{0}\to B\to 0
of rank
k-k_{0}
and degree
d^{\prime}-d_{0}
.
This shows that
g
maps onto the subset
\widetilde{Z}
.
Consequently, we have the following upper bound

(3.18)

\displaystyle\dim\widetilde{Z}

\displaystyle\leqslant\dim D\leqslant\dim A+\max_{q_{0}\in A}\left\{\dim\left({\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\ker q_{0},d^{\prime}-d_{0},k-k_{0})\right)\right\}\,.

Using
[
PR03
, Theorem 4.1]
we have, for all
d^{\prime}-d_{0}\geqslant m(\ker q_{0},k-k_{0}))
,

\dim\left({\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\ker q_{0},d^{\prime}-d_{0},k-k_{0})\right)\leqslant(k-k_{0})(r-k)+(d^{\prime}-d_{0}-m(\ker q_{0},k-k_{0}))(r-k_{0})\,.

As the collection
\{\ker q_{0}:q_{0}\in A\}
is a bounded family, by Lemma
2.2
, the set
\{m(\ker q_{0},k-k_{0}):q_{0}\in A\}
is finite.
So there exist constants
M^{\prime}(E,k)
and
M^{\prime\prime}(E,k)
such that

M^{\prime}\leqslant m(\ker q_{0},k-k_{0})\leqslant M^{\prime\prime}\quad\textrm{ for all }[q_{0}]\in A.

It follows that, for all
d^{\prime}\geqslant M^{\prime\prime}+d_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\dim\left({\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\ker q_{0},d^{\prime}-d_{0},k-k_{0})\right)\leqslant(k-k_{0})(r-k)+

\displaystyle(d^{\prime}-d_{0}-M^{\prime})(r-k_{0})\,.

Consequently, from (
3.18
) we have

\displaystyle\dim\widetilde{Z}

\displaystyle\leqslant\dim A+\max_{q_{0}\in A}\left\{\dim\left({\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\ker q_{0},d^{\prime}-d_{0},k-k_{0})\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\dim({\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\widetilde{E},k_{0},d_{0}))+(k-k_{0})(r-k)+(d^{\prime}-d_{0}-M^{\prime})(r-k_{0})

\displaystyle\leqslant d^{\prime}(r-k_{0})+\dim({\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\widetilde{E},k_{0},d_{0}))+(k-k_{0})(r-k)-(d_{0}+M^{\prime})(r-k_{0})\,.

We define
\alpha_{1}:=M^{\prime\prime}+d_{0}
and
C_{1}:=\dim({\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\widetilde{E},k_{0},d_{0}))+(k-k_{0})(r-k)-(d_{0}+M^{\prime})(r-k_{0})
.
This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
d_{0}
and
k_{0}
be integers such that
0<k_{0}<k
.
Let
Y_{d_{0},k_{0}}
be a subset of
Q^{{\rm tf}}_{k,d}(E)
defined as follows

(3.20)

Y_{d_{0},k_{0}}=\{[E\to F]\in Q^{{\rm tf}}_{k,d}(E):\pi^{*}F\textrm{ has a quotient of rank $k_{0}$ and degree }d_{0}\}\,.

Then there exist constants
C_{2}(E,k,k_{0},d_{0})
and
\alpha_{2}(E,k,k_{0},d_{0})
such that

\dim Y_{d_{0},k_{0}}\leqslant d(r-k_{0})+C_{2}\quad\quad\text{ for any }\quad d\geqslant\alpha_{2}\,.

Proof.

For the sake of notation, we denote
Y_{d_{0},k_{0}}
simply by
Y
in this proof.
For any type
a
, let
Y^{a}:=Y\cap Q_{k,d}^{{{\rm tf}},a}(E)
.
Then
Y
is disjoint union of the finitely many subsets
Y^{a}
and so it is enough to bound the dimension of
Y^{a}
.
Fix a type
a
.
Consider the map defined in Proposition
3.11
,

f_{a}:Q_{k,d}^{{{\rm tf}},a}(E)\to{\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\widetilde{E},k,d+a)\,.

Consider the subset
\widetilde{Z}
of
{\rm Quot}_{\widetilde{C}/\mathbb{C}}(\widetilde{E},k,d+a)

as defined in (
3.16
), taking
d^{\prime}=d+a
.
It is clear that the image
f_{a}(Y^{a})
is contained in
\widetilde{Z}
.
Using Lemma
3.17
, we get constants
C_{1}
and
\alpha_{1}
such that

\dim\widetilde{Z}\leqslant(d+a)(r-k_{0})+C_{1}\quad\text{ for }d+a\geqslant\alpha_{1}\,.

As
f_{a}
is injective on points, we have the following

\dim Y^{a}=\dim(f^{a}(Y^{a}))\leqslant\dim\widetilde{Z}\leqslant(d+a)(r-k_{0})+C_{1}\quad\text{ for }d\geqslant\alpha_{1}\,.

Taking
\alpha_{2}:=\alpha_{1}
and
C_{2}:=k(r-k_{0})+C_{1}
,
we have

\dim Y\leqslant d(r-k_{0})+C_{2}\text{ for }\quad d\geqslant\alpha_{2}\,.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.21
.

Given a number
\mu_{0}
,
define the subset
Y_{\mu_{0}}
of
Q^{{\rm tf}}_{k,d}(E)
as follows

Y_{\mu_{0}}=\{[E\to F]\in Q^{{\rm tf}}_{k,d}(E):\mu_{\min}(F)<\mu_{0}\}\,.

Then there exist constants
C_{3}(E,k,\mu_{0})
and
\alpha_{3}(E,k,\mu_{0})
such that

\dim Y_{\mu_{0}}\leqslant d(r-1)+C_{3}\quad\quad\text{ for any }\quad d\geqslant\alpha_{3}\,.

Proof.

Let
[q:E\to F]\in Y_{\mu_{0}}
be a closed point.
Let
F\to F^{\prime}\to 0
denote the minimal destabilizing quotient of
F
.
Note that
F^{\prime}
is torsion-free and
\mu(F^{\prime})=\mu_{\min}(F)<\mu_{0}
.
Again
F^{\prime}
is a quotient of
E
, so
\mu(F^{\prime})\geqslant\mu_{\min}(E)
.
Thus
\mu_{\min}(E)\leqslant\mu(F^{\prime})<\mu_{0}
.
As
1\leqslant\text{rank}(F^{\prime})\leqslant k-1
, we have

\mu_{\min}(E)\leqslant\deg(F^{\prime})<(k-1)\mu_{0}
.
Using Lemma
3.5
, we see that

\mu_{\min}(E)\leqslant\deg(\pi^{*}F^{\prime})<(k-1)\mu_{0}+k
.
In particular, there are only finitely many choices for the degree of
\pi^{*}F^{\prime}
.
Recall the locus
Y_{d_{0},k_{0}}
from (
3.20
).
As
\pi^{*}F^{\prime}
is a quotient of
\pi^{*}F
,
the point
[q:E\to F]
is in the locus

Y_{\deg(\pi^{*}F^{\prime}),\rank(\pi^*F')}\,.

Thus, we can write
Y_{\mu_{0}}
as a union of finitely many subsets

Y_{d_{0},k_{0}}
with
1\leqslant k_{0}\leqslant k-1
.
Now the lemma follows easily using Lemma
3.19
.
∎

Before going to the next lemma, let us recall the following result.

Lemma 3.22

(Corollary in Appendix, Page 87 of
[
OS79
]
)
.

Let
A\to B
be a flat local homomorphism of noetherian local rings.
Let
N
be a
B
-module of finite type which is
A
-flat and satisfies
\textnormal{Ext}^{1}(\overline{N},\overline{B})=0
,
where
\overline{B}=B\otimes_{A}k
and
\overline{N}=N\otimes_{A}k
for the residue field
k
of
A
.
Then
\textrm{Hom}_{B}(N,B)
is
A
-flat and

\textrm{Hom}_{B}(N,B)\otimes_{A}k=\textrm{Hom}_{\overline{B}}(\overline{N},\overline{B})
.

Lemma 3.23
.

For any degree
d
, there is a map of Quot schemes

\mathcal{V}:Q_{k,d}^{{\rm tf}}(E)\to Q^{{\rm tf}}_{r-k,d-e}(E^{\vee})

which sends a closed point
[q:E\to F]
to the point
[E^{\vee}\to(\ker q)^{\vee}]
.
Moreover, the map
\mathcal{V}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Since
E,k
and
d
are fixed in this lemma, let us write
Q^{{\rm tf}}
instead of
Q_{k,d}^{{\rm tf}}(E)
in this proof.
Let
p_{C}:C\times Q^{{\rm tf}}\to C
denote the projection map.
Consider the universal short exact sequence
on
C\times Q^{{\rm tf}}

(3.24)

0\to\mathscr{S}\to p_{C}^{*}E\to\mathcal{F}\to 0\,.

The sheaves
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathscr{S}
are flat over
Q^{{\rm tf}}
.
Consider the duals

\mathcal{F}^{\vee}:=\mathscr{H}om(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{O}_{C\times Q^{{\rm tf}}})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathscr{S}^{\vee}:=\mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{S},\mathcal{O}_{C\times Q^{{\rm tf}}})\,.

We claim that the sheaf
\mathcal{F}^{\vee}
is flat over
Q^{{\rm tf}}
.
Since flatness is a local property, it is enough to check that the stalk
(\mathcal{F}^{\vee})_{(c,q)}
is flat over the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{Q^{{\rm tf}},q}
for any closed point
(c,q)\in C\times Q^{{\rm tf}}
.
Let
(c,[q:E\to F])
be a closed point of
C\times Q^{{\rm tf}}
.
Taking
A=\mathcal{O}_{Q^{{\rm tf}},q}
,

B=\mathcal{O}_{C,c}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Q^{{\rm tf}},q}

and

,
we are in the situation of Lemma
3.22
.
Note that
\overline{B}
is the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{C,c}

and
\overline{N}
is the stalk
F_{c}
.
As
F
is a torsion-free sheaf over
C
,
so the stalk
F_{c}
is a torsion-free
\overline{B}
-module.
If
c
is a non-singular point then clearly
\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{C,c}}(F_{c},\mathcal{O}_{C,c})=0
.
For the node
x
, we have
\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{C,x}}(\mathfrak{m}_{x},\mathcal{O}_{C,x})=0
(for example see
[
Bho06
, Lemma 2.1]
).
Hence,
\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{C,x}}(F_{x},\mathcal{O}_{C,x})=0
.
This gives us

.
Using Lemma
3.22
,
we conclude that
(\mathcal{F}^{\vee})_{(c,q)}
is
\mathcal{O}_{Q^{{\rm tf}},q}
-flat,
hence,
\mathcal{F}^{\vee}
is
Q^{{\rm tf}}
-flat, and
the natural map
\mathcal{F}^{\vee}|_{C\times\{q\}}\longrightarrow F^{\vee}
is an isomorphism.
Similarly, replacing
\mathcal{F}
by
\mathscr{S}
in the above argument we get that
\mathscr{S}^{\vee}
is
Q^{{\rm tf}}
-flat
and the natural map
\mathscr{S}^{\vee}|_{C\times\{q\}}\longrightarrow S^{\vee}
is an isomorphism.

Dualizing the short exact sequence (
3.24
)
we get the exact sequence

(3.25)

0\to\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\to(p_{C}^{*}E)^{\vee}\to\mathscr{S}^{\vee}\to\mathscr{E}xt^{1}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{O}_{C\times Q^{{\rm tf}}})\to 0\,.

Restricting on the fiber

, we get a sequence of maps

\mathcal{F}^{\vee}|_{C\times\{q\}}\to(p_{C}^{*}E)^{\vee}|_{C\times\{q\}}\to\mathscr{S}^{\vee}|_{C\times\{q\}}\to\mathscr{E}xt^{1}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{O}_{C\times Q^{{\rm tf}}})|_{C\times\{q\}}

such that the rightmost map is surjective.
Restriction of the short exact sequence (
3.24
) on the fiber

gives the short exact sequence

(3.26)

Dualizing the short exact sequence (
3.26
),
by Lemma 2.2(1) of
[
Bho06
]

we get another short exact sequence

(3.27)

0\to F^{\vee}\to E^{\vee}\to(S_{F})^{\vee}\to 0\,.

So we have the following diagram in which the vertical arrows are the natural maps and the bottom sequence is exact

(3.28)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}^{\vee}|_{C\times\{q\}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(p_{C}^{*}E)^{\vee}|_{C\times\{q\}}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(S_{F})^{\vee}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

It follows from the diagram that

is surjective.
Using Nakayama Lemma we conclude that the map

(p_{C}^{*}E)^{\vee}\longrightarrow\mathscr{S}^{\vee}
is surjective.
So we conclude that
\mathscr{E}xt^{1}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{O}_{C\times Q^{{\rm tf}}})=0

and we have a short exact sequence

The quotient

\mathcal{V}:Q^{{\rm tf}}\to Q_{r-k,d-e}(E^{\vee})\,.

which sends the closed point

to the point

.
It is clear that the image of

.

Given a number

\mathcal{V}:Q_{k,d}^{{\rm tf}}(E)\to Q^{{\rm tf}}_{r-k,d-e}(E^{\vee})

4.
Irreducible components containing stable locally free quotients

In this section we will show that for large enough
d
, there is a unique irreducible component of
Q_{k,d}(E)
whose general point corresponds to a stable locally free quotient.
Before proving the theorem we prove few lemmas which will be required in the proof of the theorem.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists an integer
d_{1}=d_{1}(E,k)
such that for all stable locally free sheaf
F
of rank
k
and degree
d\geqslant d_{1}
on
C
, the sheaf

E^{\vee}\otimes F
is generated by global sections and
H^{1}(E^{\vee}\otimes F)=0
.

Proof.

This can be proved exactly as it is proved in smooth curve case in
[
PR03
, Lemma 6.1]
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

If
d\geqslant d_{1}
, then for any quotient
E\to F\to 0
where
F
is a stable locally free sheaf of rank
k
and degree
d
, we have
\textrm{h}^{1}(S_{F}^{\vee}\otimes F)=0
where
S_{F}
is the kernel of the quotient
E\to F
.
As a consequence we have

\chi(S_{F}^{\vee}\otimes F)=h^{0}(S_{F}^{\vee}\otimes F)=dr-ke+k(r-k)(1-g)

Proof.

As
d\geqslant d_{1}
, from lemma
4.1
we have
h^{1}(E^{\vee}\otimes F)=0
. Using the long exact sequence of cohomology obtained from the short exact sequence
0\to F^{\vee}\otimes F\to E^{\vee}\otimes F\to S_{F}^{\vee}\otimes F\to 0
, we get that
h^{1}(S_{F}^{\vee}\otimes F)=0
.
Using
(
1.1
) we get
h^{0}(S_{F}^{\vee}\otimes F)=\chi(S_{F}^{\vee}\otimes F)=dr-ke+k(r-k)(1-g)
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
F
be a stable locally free sheaf of rank
k
and degree
d
such that the sheaf
\mathscr{H}om(E,F)=E^{\vee}\otimes F
is globally generated and
H^{1}(E^{\vee}\otimes F)=0
.
Then a general homomorphism from
E
to
F
is surjective.

Proof.

The idea of the proof is taken from the proof of
[
GS22
, Lemma 7.12]
.
Consider the space
\mathbb{P}(\textrm{Hom}(E,F)^{\vee})
which parametrizes non-zero maps
E\to F
.
Let
T
be the subset of
\mathbb{P}(\textrm{Hom}(E,F)^{\vee})
which contains all the maps
\varphi:E\to F
such that
\varphi|_{c}=0
for atleast one point
c\in C
.
Then
T
is a closed subset and as a set

T=\bigcup_{c\in C}\mathbb{P}(\textrm{Hom}(E,F\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{c})^{\vee})

where
\mathfrak{m}_{c}
denotes the sheaf of ideals of the point
c
.
As
E^{\vee}\otimes F
is globally generated and
H^{1}(E^{\vee}\otimes F)=0
,

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists a number
d_{2}:=d_{2}(E,k)
such that if
d\geqslant d_{2}
then the locus of all stable locally free quotients in

is irreducible.

Proof.

Using
[
New78
, Lemma 5.2’]
we get the number

such that any stable locally free sheaf of rank
k
and degree

is globally generated.
Define

Assume

.
Following the proof of irreduciblity of moduli space of stable locally free sheaves
(see
[
LP97
, Theorem 8.5.2]
),
one can construct a space

which parametrizes pairs
(L,e_{L})
where

is a line bundle of degree

on

and

is an extension of the form

(4.6)

There is a universal sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on

.
Let

denote the open subset containing all the points such that

is a stable locally free sheaf.
Let

denote the moduli space of stable locally free sheaves on

of rank

and degree

.
The restriction of the universal sheaf

on

.

is irreducible and the map

is surjective.

is the set of all the stable locally free sheaves on

of rank

and degree

.

Let

The sheaf

and Guauert’s theorem.

Theorem 4.7
.

If

then there is a unique component of

whose general point corresponds to a quotient
[E\to F]

such that

Fix a stable bundle

.
Using Lemma

Let us first recall Serre duality for nodal curves.
As

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists a number

such that if

then we have the following.

be an irreducible component of

whose general point corresponds to a torsion-free quotient.

of

satisfies

First let us define

.
Let

.
For any

, consider the set

, we get constants

such that

Thus, if

Now we assume

be an irreducible component of

, so (

for which
\phi_{y}
is injective is open.
∎

Theorem 6.13
.

Let
k
be an integer such that
\frac{r}{2}<k<r
. Then there is a number
d_{9}(E,k)

such that if
d\geqslant d_{9}
then
the quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(E)
is irreducible.

Proof.

We define

d_{9}:=\max\{d_{4},d_{5},d_{7},e-d_{8}\}\,.

Assume
d\geqslant d_{9}
.
As
d\geqslant d_{4}
, recall that, by Theorem
5.7
or Corollary
5.8
, there is a unique irreducible component
\mathcal{P}
of
Q_{k,d}(E)
which contains the torsion-free locus
Q^{{\rm tf}}_{k,d}(E)
.
We want to show that there is no other component of the quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(E)
.

On the contrary, let us assume that
\mathcal{W}
is an irreducible component of
Q_{k,d}(E)
different from
\mathcal{P}
.
So any closed point in
\mathcal{W}
corresponds to a quotient with torsion.
Let
[q:E\to F]
be a general point in
\mathcal{W}
.
Let
S_{F}
denote the kernel of
q
.
Then
S_{F}
is a torsion-free sheaf on
C
of rank
r-k
and degree
e-d
.
Using Proposition
5.10
, we get a family
\mathscr{S}
of torsion-free sheaves on
C
of rank
r-k
and degree
e-d
, parametrized by an integral scheme
T
such that the sheaf
\mathscr{S}_{t}
is stable locally free for a general point
t\in T
and
\mathscr{S}_{t_{0}}=S_{F}
for some
t_{0}\in T
.
Let
p_{C}:C\times T\to T
and
p_{T}:C\times T\to T
denote the projections.
We consider the sheaf
\mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{S},p_{C}^{*}E)
and let
\mathcal{G}
be its push-forward on
T
,

\mathcal{G}:={p_{T}}_{*}(\mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{S},p_{C}^{*}E))\,.

Proposition
6.8
shows that we can assume
\textnormal{Ext}^{1}(S_{F},E)=0
.
We replace
T
by the open subset containing points
t
such that
\textnormal{Ext}^{1}(\mathscr{S}_{t},E)=0
.
Clearly
t_{0}\in T
.
Using
[
Lan83
, Theorem 1.4]
, we conclude that
G
is a locally free sheaf on
T
.
So the projectivization
\mathbb{P}:=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\vee})
is irreducible.
Let
\eta:\mathbb{P}\to T
be the structure map.
We have the tautological quotient of sheaves on
\mathbb{P}
,

\eta^{*}\mathcal{G}^{\vee}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)\longrightarrow 0\,,

that is,

(6.14)

\eta^{*}({p_{T}}_{*}(\mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{S},p_{C}^{*}E)))^{\vee}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)\longrightarrow 0\,.

Let
\rho_{\mathbb{P}}
and
\widetilde{\eta}
denote the maps as shown in the following Cartesian diagram and
\rho_{C}:C\times\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{P}
denote the projection

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C\times\mathbb{P}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C\times T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T\,.}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{\mathbb{P}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{T}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}

Dualizing (

) and taking adjoint, we get a map of sheaves on
C\times\mathbb{P}
,

\rho_{\mathbb{P}}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(-1)\longrightarrow\widetilde{\eta}^{*}\mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{S},p_{C}^{*}E)\,.

Tensoring with
\widetilde{\eta}^{*}\mathscr{S}

and composing with the natural map

\widetilde{\eta}^{*}\textrm{Hom}(\mathscr{S},p_{C}^{*}E)\otimes\widetilde{\eta}^{*}\mathscr{S}\to\widetilde{\eta}^{*}p_{C}^{*}E
,
we get a map of sheaves on
C\times\mathbb{P}
,

\Psi:\widetilde{\eta}^{*}\mathscr{S}\otimes\rho_{\mathbb{P}}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(-1)\longrightarrow\widetilde{\eta}^{*}p_{C}^{*}E\,.

A closed point
u\in\mathbb{P}
corresponds to a pair
(t=\eta(u),\psi_{u}:\mathscr{S}_{t}\to E)
, where
\psi_{u}
is a non-zero map.
Restriction of
\Psi
on
C\times\{u\}
is the map
\psi_{u}
itself.
Let
U
be the following subset of
\mathbb{P}
,

U:=\{u\in\mathbb{P}:\Psi|_{C\times\{u\}}:\mathscr{S}_{\eta(u)}\longrightarrow E\textrm{ is injective }\}\,.

Lemma

shows that

is an open subset of
\mathbb{P}
.
There is a point

which corresponds to the pair

.
Clearly this point is in
U
, which shows that
U
is non-empty.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be the cokernel of

.
For any
u\in U
, we have the short exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{S}_{\eta(u)}\to E\to\mathcal{B}_{u}\to 0\,.

It follows that the Hilbert polynomial of
\mathcal{B}_{u}

.

\mathcal{B}

.

.

.

.

, we conclude that for a general morphism
\alpha:\mathscr{S}_{\eta(u)}\longrightarrow E
, the cokernel of

.

.

.

.

.

.

, this proves that

Theorem 6.15
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle on
C
of rank
r
and degree
e

and let
k
be an integer such that
0<k<r
.
Then there is a number
d_{Q}(E,k)
such that if
d\geqslant d_{Q}
then the quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(E)
is irreducible.
Moreover,
Q_{k,d}(E)
is generically smooth and has dimension

dr-ke+k(r-k)(1-g)
.

Proof.

If
k>\frac{r}{2}
then we are done using Theorem
6.13
, taking
d_{Q}(E,k):=d_{9}(E,k)
.
Otherwise we have
0<k\leqslant\frac{r}{2}
.
In this case, we will use Theorem
6.13
to prove irreduciblity of the quot scheme.
Let
t
be the integer
t:=r-2k+1>0

so that we have

k+t>\frac{r+t}{2}\,.

We define
r^{\prime}:=r+t
and
k^{\prime}:=k+t
.
So the new pair
r^{\prime},k^{\prime}
satisfies

r^{\prime}>k^{\prime}>\frac{r^{\prime}}{2}\,.

Let
E^{\prime}
denote the vector bundle
E\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus t}
which has rank
r^{\prime}
and degree
e
.
Applying Theorem
6.13
for the bundle
E^{\prime}
,
we get a number
d^{\prime}_{9}:=d_{9}(E^{\prime},k^{\prime})

such that if
d\geqslant d^{\prime}_{9}
then the quot scheme
Q_{k^{\prime},d}(E^{\prime})
is irreducible.
We will give a surjective map from an open subset of
Q_{k^{\prime},d}(E^{\prime})
to the quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(E)
.
That will prove irreducibility of
Q_{k,d}(E)
when
d\geqslant d^{\prime}_{9}
.

We define

d_{Q}:=\max\{d^{\prime}_{9},d_{2}(E,k)\}\,.

Assume
d\geqslant d_{Q}
.
Let
Q^{\prime}
denote the reduced scheme structure on the irreducible quot scheme
Q_{k^{\prime},d}(E^{\prime})
.
So
Q^{\prime}
is integral.
There is a universal short exact sequence of sheaves on
C\times Q^{\prime}
,

0\to\mathscr{S}^{\prime}\to p_{C}^{*}E^{\prime}\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\to 0

where
p_{C}:C\times Q^{\prime}\to Q^{\prime}
is the projection.
Let
\Psi
denote the composition

\mathscr{S}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow p_{C}^{*}E^{\prime}=p_{C}^{*}(E\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus t})\twoheadrightarrow p_{C}^{*}E\,.

The locus of points
q^{\prime}\in Q^{\prime}
for which the map
\Psi_{q^{\prime}}
is injective is an open subset by lemma
6.12
.
Let
U
denote this open locus.
Since
U
is integral, using the Hilbert polynomial, it can be checked easily that the sheaf

\text{coker}(\Psi)|_{C\times U}
is flat over
U
.
So the quotient of sheaves
p_{C}^{*}E|_{C\times U}\to\text{coker}(\Psi)|_{C\times U}\to 0

on
C\times U
defines a map of schemes

g:U\longrightarrow Q_{k,d}(E)\,.

Let us see the pointwise description of the map
g
.
Let
[q^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to F^{\prime}]\in Q_{k^{\prime},d}(E^{\prime})
be a closed point in
U
.
Let
S^{\prime}
denote the kernel of
q^{\prime}
.
As
[q^{\prime}]
is in
U
, so the composition

S^{\prime}\hookrightarrow E^{\prime}\to E
is injective.
Let
B
denote the cokernel of this inclusion
S\hookrightarrow E
.
Then
B
is a sheaf of rank
k
and degree
d
.
The map
g
sends the point
[q^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to F^{\prime}]
to the point
[E\to B]
.
To see that the map
g
is surjective on closed points,
let
[q:E\to F]
be a closed point in the quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(E)
.
Using
q
, we get another quotient

\widetilde{q}:=q\oplus Id:E^{\prime}=E\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus t}\longrightarrow F\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus t}\,.

Clearly
F\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus t}
is a sheaf of rank
k^{\prime}
and degree
d
.
So
\widetilde{q}
corresponds to a closed point in
Q^{\prime}
.
Moreover,
\ker\widetilde{q}=\ker q\oplus 0\subset E^{\prime}
,
which implies that
[\widetilde{q}]
is contained in the open set
U
.
It is easy to see that
g([\widetilde{q}])=[q]
.
This shows that the map
g
is surjective on closed points.
As
Q^{\prime}
is irreducible, so the quot scheme
Q_{k,d}(E)
is also irreducible. This proves the theorem.

The second assertion follows from Theorem
4.7
, as
d\geqslant d_{2}(E,k)
.
∎

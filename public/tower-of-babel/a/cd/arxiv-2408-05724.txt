A note on p p -adic higher Mahler measures
Yu Katagiri

Abstract
. Kurokawa, Lalín and Ochiai introduced and studied the higher Mahler measures, which are generalization of the classical Mahler measure. In this article, we introduce p p -adic higher Mahler measures and prove p p -adic analogues of Akatsuka’s results.

Primary: 11R06; secondary: 11M99

1.
Introduction

Let
|\cdot|_{\infty}
be the usual absolute value on
\mathbb{C}
. For a non-zero Laurent polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
of
n
variables, the
Mahler measure
of
f
is given by

\displaystyle m(f)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{n}}\int_{T^{n}}\log|f(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})|_{\infty}\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}\in\mathbb{R},

where

\displaystyle T^{n}=\{(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid|z_{1}|_{\infty}=\dots=|z_{n}|_{\infty}=1\}

is the
n
-torus. This integral in fact exists even if
f
has zeroes on
T^{n}
.
It is known that Mahler measures appear in several areas and relate with various objects, for example, entropies in dynamical systems, and special values of
L
-functions in number theory (see
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
15
]
and
[
5
]
for instance).

Kurokawa, Lalín and Ochiai considered the following generalization
[
12
, Definition 1]
: for a non-zero Laurent polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
and a non-negative integer
k
, we define

\displaystyle m_{k}(f)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{n}}\int_{T^{n}}\log^{k}|f(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})|_{\infty}\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}\in\mathbb{R}

and call
m_{k}(f)
the
k
-higher Mahler measure
of
f
. Note that
m_{1}(f)=m(f)
and
m_{0}(f)=1
. Furthermore, Akatsuka introduced the
zeta Mahler measure
of
f
to be a holomorphic function

\displaystyle Z(s,f)\coloneqq\frac{1}{(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{n}}\int_{T^{n}}|f(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})|^{s}_{\infty}\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}},

where
s
is a complex variable
[
1
, Section 1]
.
This integral converges absolutely in
{\rm Re}(s)>\sigma_{0}(f)
for some
\sigma_{0}(f)\leq 0

[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
and
Z(s,f)
has the Taylor coefficients
m_{k}(f)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle Z(s,f)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{m_{k}(f)}{k!}s^{k}.

The following two examples are known in previous works.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
1
, Theorem 4(1)]
)
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
c>2
and
f(t)=t^{2}-ct+1
. Then, for any
s\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\displaystyle Z(s,f)=\alpha_{c}^{s}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-s,-s;1;\beta_{c}^{2}\right),

where
\alpha_{c}
and
\beta_{c}
are the roots of
f(t)=0
with
0<\beta_{c}<1<\alpha_{c}
, and the generalized hypergeometric series
{}_{r+1}F_{r}
is given by

\displaystyle{}_{r+1}F_{r}\left(a_{1},\cdots,a_{r+1};b_{1},\cdots,b_{r};z\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1})_{n}\cdots(a_{r+1})_{n}}{(b_{1})_{n}\cdots(b_{r})_{n}n!}z^{n}

with the Pochhammer symbol
(\cdot)_{n}
defined by
(a)_{0}=1
and
(a)_{n}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)
for
n\geq 1
.

Remark 1.2
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
c>2
and
f(t)=t^{2}-ct+1
. Since

\displaystyle\frac{d^{k}Z}{ds^{k}}(0,f)=m_{k}(f)

for any
k\geq 0
, we can compute

\displaystyle m_{2}(f)

\displaystyle=\log^{2}\alpha_{c}+2\operatorname{Li}_{2}(\beta_{c}^{2}),

\displaystyle m_{3}(f)

\displaystyle=\log^{3}\alpha_{c}+6(\log\alpha_{c})\operatorname{Li}_{2}(\beta_{c}^{2})-12\operatorname{Li}_{(1,2)}(\beta_{c}^{2})

by Theorem
1.1
. Here, for a tuple
\textbf{k}=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{r}
, the
multiple polylogarithm

\operatorname{Li}_{\textbf{k}}(t)
is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Li}_{\textbf{k}}(t)=\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}}\frac{t^{m_{r}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}

and
\operatorname{Li}_{\textbf{k}}(t)
is absolutely convergent on
|t|_{\infty}<1
.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
12
, Theorem 17]
,
[
1
, Theorem 6]
)
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{R}
with
c>4
and
f(t_{1},t_{2})=t_{1}+t_{1}^{-1}+t_{2}+t_{2}^{-1}+c
. Then we have

\displaystyle Z(s,f)=c^{s}{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\frac{1}{2},-\frac{s}{2},\frac{1-s}{2};1,1;\frac{16}{c^{2}}\right).

Remark 1.4
.

The Mahler measure of
f(t_{1},t_{2})=t_{1}+t_{1}^{-1}+t_{2}+t_{2}^{-1}+c
was studied by Rodriguez-Villegas
[
15
]
. He proved

(1.2)

\displaystyle m(f)=\log c-\frac{2}{c^{2}}{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2},1,1;2,2,2;\frac{16}{c^{2}}\right).

In this article, we introduce a
p
-adic analogue of higher Mahler measures and consider
p
-adic analogues of the above theorems.
Let
p
be a prime and
\mathbb{C}_{p}
be the completion of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
with the norm
|\cdot|
normalized so that
|p|=p^{-1}
. In
[
3
]
, Besser and Deninger introduced the
p
-adic Mahler measure
, which is a
p
-adic analogue of the Mahler measures, as follows: for
f\in\mathbb{C}_{p}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
with no zeroes of
p
-adic absolute value one, we define

\displaystyle m_{p}(f)\coloneqq\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
(N,p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N^{n}}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{N}^{n}}\log_{p}f(\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{n}).

Here,
\mu_{N}
is the set of the
N
-th roots of unity in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
and
\log_{p}:\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p}
is the
p
-adic logarithm normalized so that
\log_{p}p=0
. (It is known that the multiplicative group of
\mathbb{C}_{p}
has the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times}\simeq p^{\mathbb{Q}}\times\mu_{ur}\times U_{0},

where
\mu_{ur}
is the set of roots of unity in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
whose order in
\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times}
is prime to
p
, and
U_{0}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\mid|z-1|<1\}
.
If we denote by
\langle a\rangle
the corresponding part of
a
in
U_{0}
for
a\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times}
, then the value
\log_{p}a
is given by

\displaystyle\log_{p}a=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n}(\langle a\rangle-1)^{n}.

See also
[
17
, Proposition 5.4]
.)

Besser and Deninger studied
p
-adic analogues of several known results of the classical Mahler measure. Especially, they proved that the
p
-adic Mahler measure relates to the regulator map into (modified) syntomic cohomology. This is a
p
-adic analogue of the remarkable relation between the Mahler measure and the regulator map into Deligne cohomology, which was proved by Deninger
[
5
]
. (See
[
3
, Section 2]
for the detail.) Moreover, Deninger introduced the
p
-adic entropy, which is a
p
-adic analogue of the periodic entropy, and proved that it relates to the
p
-adic Mahler measure. This corresponds to an analogue of
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
in dynamical systems. (See
[
6
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
11
]
for the theory of the
p
-adic entropy.)

In the same way as Besser-Deninger’s definition, we define the
p
-adic
k
-higher Mahler measure
by

(1.3)

\displaystyle m_{p,k}(f)\coloneqq\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
(N,p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N^{n}}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{N}^{n}}\log_{p}^{k}f(\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{n})

for
k\geq 1
. (See Section 2 for the existence of
m_{p,k}(f)
.) The following is one of our main results.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
with
0<|\beta|<1<|\alpha|
. Then, for each
k\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle m_{p,k}((t-\alpha)(t-\beta))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log_{p}^{k}\alpha+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j\leq k\\
i,j\geq 1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i+j}\frac{k!}{(k-i-j)!}\left(\log_{p}^{k-i-j}\alpha\right)\operatorname{Li}_{\left(\{1\}^{i-1}\ast\{1\}^{j-1},2\right)}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right),

where
\ast
is the harmonic product defined in Section 3.

We note that the radius of the
p
-adic convergence of the multiple polylogarithm series is
1
(see the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 1.6]
for example), and hence the statement of Theorem
1.5
makes sense.

We further introduce a
naive

p
-adic analogue of the zeta Mahler measure as the formal power series whose Taylor coefficients are
m_{p,k}(f)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle Z_{p}(X,f)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{m_{p,k}(f)}{k!}X^{k}\in\mathbb{C}_{p}[[X]].

In this article, we call
Z_{p}(X,f)
the
p
-adic zeta Mahler measure
of
f
. In Section 4, we verify that
p
-adic zeta Mahler measures converge on the closed unit disc under certain conditions and prove the following theorems, which are
p
-adic analogues of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
K
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(inside
\mathbb{C}_{p}
) of ramified index
e<p-1
and
\alpha,\beta\in K
with
0<|\beta|<1<|\alpha|
. Then, for any
s\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
with
|s|\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle Z_{p}(s,(t-\alpha)(t-\beta))=\langle\alpha\rangle^{s}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-s,-s;1;\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right),

where
\langle a\rangle^{s}
is given by

\displaystyle\langle a\rangle^{s}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{s}{n}(\langle a\rangle-1)^{n}

with

\displaystyle\binom{s}{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{s(s-1)\cdots(s-n+1)}{n!}&n\geq 1,\\
1&n=0.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
K
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(inside
\mathbb{C}_{p}
) of ramified index
e<p-1
and
c\in K
with
|c|>1
. Put
f(t_{1},t_{2})=t_{1}+t_{1}^{-1}+t_{2}+t_{2}^{-1}+c
. Then, for any
s\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
with
|s|\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle Z_{p}(s,f)=\langle c\rangle^{s}{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\frac{1}{2},-\frac{s}{2},\frac{1-s}{2};1,1;\frac{16}{c^{2}}\right).

Kurokawa, Lalín, Ochiai and Akatsuka considered and computed more examples of higher Mahler measures and zeta Mahler measures, containing the case
0\leq c\leq 2
of Theorem
1.1
. However, we may not obtain analogues of these results as long as we use the limit
(\ref{deflim m_p})
for the definition of the
p
-adic (higher) Mahler measures. This is because the limit
(\ref{deflim m_p})
does not exist if a Laurent polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}_{p}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
has a zero of
p
-adic absolute value one (see the remark written below Proposition 1.3 in
[
3
]
). This also explains our assumptions about the absolute values of coefficients in our results.

Notation:
In this paper, let
p
be a prime and
\mathbb{C}_{p}
be the completion of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
with the
p
-adic norm
|\cdot|
normalized by
|p|=p^{-1}
. We denote by
\mathfrak{m}_{p}
the maximal ideal of the ring of integers in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
. For a positive constant
r
, we define

\displaystyle D(r^{-})

\displaystyle=\{z\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\mid|z|<r\},

\displaystyle D(r)

\displaystyle=\{z\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\mid|z|\leq r\}.

2.
p
-adic higher Mahler measures

In this section, we show the existence of
p
-adic higher Mahler measures and verify some properties. The contents in this section are mainly based on
[
3
, Section 1]
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
3
, Section 1]
)
.

For a function
f:\mu_{ur}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p}
, if the limit

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{T_{p}^{n}}f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}\coloneqq\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
(N,p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N^{n}}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{N}^{n}}f(\zeta)

exists, we call it the Shnirelman integral of
f
and say
f
is (Shnirelman) integrable.

We have the following properties of the Shnirelman integrals.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
3
, Lemma 1.1]
)
.

(1)

If the limit in
(\ref{Sch int})
exists, then we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{T_{p}^{n}}f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}\right|\leq\sup_{\zeta\in\mu_{ur}^{n}}\{|f(\zeta)|\}.

(2)

Define an affinoid algebra
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathbb{C}_{p})
over
\mathbb{C}_{p}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}(\mathbb{C}_{p})\coloneqq\left\{\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{v}t_{1}^{v_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{v_{n}}\mid a_{v}\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\ \text{with}\ |a_{v}|\to 0\ \text{as}\ |v_{1}|_{\infty}+\cdots+|v_{n}|_{\infty}\to\infty\right\}.

Then each
f=\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{v}t_{1}^{v_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{v_{n}}\in\mathcal{R}(\mathbb{C}_{p})
defines a well-defined map from
\mu_{ur}^{n}
to
\mathbb{C}_{p}
and we have

\displaystyle\int_{T_{p}^{n}}f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}=a_{0}.

The proofs of following two lemmas are elementary and hence omitted.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that a family
\{f_{m}:\mu_{ur}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p}\}_{m}
of functions converges uniformly to a function
f:\mu_{ur}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p}
.

(1)

If
f_{m}
is integrable for any
m\geq 1
, then the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{T_{p}^{n}}f_{m}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}

exists and we have

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{T_{p}^{n}}f_{m}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}=\int_{T_{p}^{n}}f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}.

(2)

If there exists a constant
C>0
such that
|f_{m}(z)|\leq C
for any
m\geq 1
and
z\in\mu_{ur}^{n}
, then
\{f_{m}^{j}\}_{m}
converges uniformly to
f^{j}
for each
j\geq 0
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that a function
f:\mu_{ur}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p}
is integrable. Then, for any
1\leq i\leq n
, the function
f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\log_{p}z_{i}
is integrable and the integral is
0
.

The following lemma is well known in rigid analysis and plays an important role to consider
p
-adic Mahler measures.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
3
, Lemma 1.2]
)
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}_{p}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
. Then
f
does not vanish in any point of
T_{p}^{n}
if and only if we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle f(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})=at_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{l_{n}}(1+g(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}))

for some
a\in\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times},(l_{1},\cdots,l_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
g\in\mathfrak{m}_{p}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
.

Combining above lemmas, we can check the existence of
p
-adic higher Mahler measures as follows.

Proposition 2.6

(cf.
[
3
, Proposition 1.3]
)
.

Suppose that
f\in\mathbb{C}_{p}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
does not vanish in any point of
T_{p}^{n}
and write
f
as in
(\ref{lem 1.2})
. Then, for each
k\geq 1
, the Shnirelman integral

\displaystyle m_{p,k}(f)\coloneqq\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\log_{p}^{k}f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}

exists and is given by

\displaystyle m_{p,k}(f)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\left(\log_{p}^{i}a\right)b_{k-i},

where
b_{j}=\lim_{M\to\infty}[\{\sum_{m=1}^{M}(-1)^{m+1}g(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})^{m}/m\}^{j}]_{0}
and
[\cdot]_{0}
means the
0
-th coefficient.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle m_{p,k}(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\left\{\log_{p}az_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{l_{n}}(1+g(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}))\right\}^{k}\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\left(\log_{p}^{i}az_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{l_{n}}\right)\left(\log_{p}^{k-i}(1+g(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}))\right)\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}

and, for each
0\leq i\leq n
,

\displaystyle\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\left(\log_{p}^{i}az_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{l_{n}}\right)\left(\log_{p}^{k-i}(1+g(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}))\right)\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
(N,p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N^{n}}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{N}}\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{0},\cdots,i_{n}\geq 0\\
i_{0}+\cdots+i_{n}=i\end{subarray}}\frac{i!}{i_{0}!\cdots i_{n}!}\left(\log_{p}^{i_{0}}a\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(l_{j}\log_{p}\zeta_{j}\right)^{i_{j}}\right\}\times

\displaystyle\left(\log_{p}^{k-i}(1+g(\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{n}))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
(N,p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N^{n}}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{N}}\left(\log_{p}^{i}a\right)\left(\log_{p}^{k-i}(1+g(\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{n}))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\log_{p}^{i}a\right)\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\log_{p}^{k-i}(1+g(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}))\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}.

Since
\{\sum_{m=1}^{M}(-1)^{m+1}g(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})^{m}/m\}_{M\geq 1}
converges uniformly to
\log_{p}(1+g(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}))
on
\mu_{ur}^{n}
, Lemma
2.3
implies that

\displaystyle\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\log_{p}^{k-i}(1+g(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}))\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to\infty}\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\left\{\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{(-1)^{m+1}}{m}g(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})^{m}\right\}^{k-i}\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}=b_{k-i}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We call
m_{p,k}(f)
the
p
-adic
k
-higher Mahler measure
of
f
and note that
m_{p,1}(f)=m_{p}(f)
, which was introduced by Besser and Deninger. The following properties follow from Proposition
2.6
.

Proposition 2.7
.

(1)

For any
(l_{1},\cdots,l_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and any positive integer
k
, we have
m_{p,k}(t_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{l_{n}})=0
.

(2)

Suppose that
f\in\mathbb{C}_{p}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
does not vanish in any point of
T_{p}^{n}
. For any
n\times n
matrix
S\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\cap{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
, we have
m_{p,k}(f(t^{S}))=m_{p,k}(f(t))
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

In this section, we explain Hoffman’s algebraic setup of the multiple polylogarithm to make the statement of Theorem
1.5
precisely, and prove it.

Let
\mathfrak{H}=\mathbb{Q}\langle e_{0},e_{1}\rangle
be the noncommutative polynomial algebra of indeterminates
e_{0},e_{1}
over
\mathbb{Q}
. We also define its subalgebras
\mathfrak{H}^{0}
and
\mathfrak{H}^{1}
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}^{0}=\mathbb{Q}+e_{1}\mathfrak{H}e_{0}\subset\mathfrak{H}^{1}=\mathbb{Q}+e_{1}\mathfrak{H}.

For any positive integer
k
, we put
e_{k}\coloneqq e_{1}e_{0}^{k-1}
. We identify a tuple
(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{r}
(such a tuple is called an
index
) with the monomial
e_{k_{1}}\cdots e_{k_{r}}\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}
.

We define the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
{\rm Li}:\mathfrak{H}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}[[t]]
by
1\mapsto 1
and
e_{k_{1}}\cdots e_{k_{r}}\mapsto\operatorname{Li}_{(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r})}(t)
, where
\operatorname{Li}_{(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r})}(t)
is the multipolylogaritm series given by
(
1.1
)
. (We note that if
e_{k_{1}}\cdots e_{k_{r}}\in\mathfrak{H}^{0}
, i.e.,
k_{r}\geq 2
, the limit
\lim_{t\to 1}\operatorname{Li}(e_{k_{1}}\cdots e_{k_{r}})
converges to the multiple zeta value
\zeta(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r})
with respect to the Euclidean topology of
\mathbb{R}
. This correspondence gives the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
\zeta:\mathfrak{H}^{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.)

Definition 3.1
.

We define the
\mathbb{Q}
-bilinear commutative product
\ast:\mathfrak{H}^{1}\times\mathfrak{H}^{1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{1}
by the bilinearity and the recurrence relations

(1)

for each
w\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}
,
w\ast 1=1\ast w=w
,

(2)

for any
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
v,w\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}
,

\displaystyle e_{k}v\ast e_{l}w=e_{k}(v\ast e_{l}w)+e_{l}(e_{k}v\ast w)+e_{k+l}(v\ast w).

The product
\ast
is called the
harmonic product
on
\mathfrak{H}^{1}
.

The harmonic product
\ast
has the following properties.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
8
]
)
.

(1)

For any
w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}
, we have
w_{1}\ast(w_{2}\ast w_{3})=(w_{1}\ast w_{2})\ast w_{3}
and
w_{1}\ast w_{2}=w_{2}\ast w_{1}
.

(2)

We equip
\mathfrak{H}^{1}
with the multiplication
\ast
. Then
\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{\ast}\coloneqq(\mathfrak{H}^{1},+,\ast)
forms a commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra.

(3)

The map
{\rm Li}:\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{\ast}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}[[t]]
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra homomorphism.

Hoffman also introduced the shuffle product
\shuffle
on
\mathfrak{H}
, which has similar properties as Proposition
3.2
. (Combining these properties of these two product, we obtain the double shuffle relation of multiple zeta values.)

We also define a variant product to state a preliminary lemma as follows.

Definition 3.3

(
[
9
, Section 2]
)
.

We define the
\mathbb{Q}
-bilinear map
\circledast:e_{1}\mathfrak{H}\times e_{1}\mathfrak{H}\rightarrow e_{1}\mathfrak{H}e_{0}
by the bilinearity and the recurrence relations

\displaystyle ve_{k}\circledast we_{l}=(v\ast w)e_{k+l}

for any
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
v,w\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}
. We call the product
\circledast
the
circled harmonic product
.

We use the following lemma in the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any positive integers
k
and
l
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Li}_{(\{1\}^{k-1}\ast\{1\}^{l-1},2)}(t)={\rm Li}_{(\{1\}^{k}\circledast\{1\}^{l})}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{k-1}<m\\
0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{l-1}<m\end{subarray}}\frac{t^{m}}{m_{1}\cdots m_{k-1}n_{1}\cdots n_{l-1}m^{2}}.

Proof.

Since indices
(\{1\}^{k-1}\ast\{1\}^{l-1},2)
and
(\{1\}^{k}\circledast\{1\}^{l})
correspond to monomials
(e_{1}^{k-1}\ast e_{1}^{l-1})e_{2}
and
e_{1}^{k}\circledast e_{1}^{l}
respectively, the first equality follows from the definition of the circled harmonic product. The second equality follows from the same argument at the end of Section 2 in
[
9
]
.
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We compute

\displaystyle m_{p,k}((t-\alpha)(t-\beta))=

\displaystyle\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\left\{\log_{p}(z-\alpha)+\log_{p}(z-\beta)\right\}^{k}\frac{dz}{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\left(\log_{p}^{k-i}(z-\alpha)\right)\left(\log_{p}^{i}(z-\beta)\right)\frac{dz}{z}.

and put

\displaystyle I(i)\coloneqq\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\left(\log_{p}^{k-i}(z-\alpha)\right)\left(\log_{p}^{i}(z-\beta)\right)\frac{dz}{z}

for
0\leq i\leq k
. We see that

\displaystyle I(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\log_{p}^{k}(z-\alpha)\frac{dz}{z}=\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\left\{\log_{p}\alpha+\log_{p}\left(1-\frac{z}{\alpha}\right)\right\}^{k}\frac{dz}{z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\left(\log_{p}^{k-j}\alpha\right)\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\log_{p}^{j}\left(1-\frac{z}{\alpha}\right)\frac{dz}{z}=\log_{p}^{k}\alpha.

Here, we used the fact that
\log_{p}^{j}(1-z/\alpha)\in\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathbb{C}_{p})\cap z\mathbb{C}_{p}[[z]]
for
j\geq 1
and Lemma
2.2
(2) in the last equality. By the similar argument, we also obtain
I(k)=0
. For
1\leq i\leq k-1
, we have

\displaystyle I(i)

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\left\{\log_{p}\alpha+\log_{p}\left(1-\frac{z}{\alpha}\right)\right\}^{k-i}\left\{\log_{p}z+\log_{p}\left(1-\frac{\beta}{z}\right)\right\}^{i}\frac{dz}{z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k-i}\binom{k-i}{j}\left(\log_{p}^{k-i-j}\alpha\right)\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\left(\log_{p}^{j}\left(1-\frac{z}{\alpha}\right)\right)\left(\log_{p}^{i}\left(1-\frac{\beta}{z}\right)\right)\frac{dz}{z}

by using Lemma
2.4
and the fact that the integral for
j=0
in the summation is zero. Since it follows from
[
2
, Lemma 1(ii)]
that
\log_{p}^{j}(1-z/\alpha)=(-1)^{j}j!\operatorname{Li}_{\{1\}^{j}}(z/\alpha)
and
\log_{p}^{j}(1-\beta/z)=(-1)^{i}i!\operatorname{Li}_{\{1\}^{i}}(\beta/z)
, we have

\displaystyle I(i)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-i}(-1)^{i+j}i!j!\binom{k-i}{j}\left(\log_{p}^{k-i-j}\alpha\right)\times

\displaystyle\int_{T_{p}}\left\{\sum_{0<l_{1}<\cdots<l_{j}}\frac{1}{l_{1}\cdots l_{j}}\left(\frac{z}{\alpha}\right)^{l_{j}}\right\}\left\{\sum_{0<l^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<l^{\prime}_{i}}\frac{1}{l^{\prime}_{1}\cdots l^{\prime}_{i}}\left(\frac{\beta}{z}\right)^{l^{\prime}_{i}}\right\}\frac{dz}{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-i}(-1)^{i+j}i!j!\binom{k-i}{j}\left(\log_{p}^{k-i-j}\alpha\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<l_{1}<\cdots<l_{j-1}<l\\
0<l^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<l^{\prime}_{i-1}<l\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{l_{1}\cdots l_{j-1}l^{\prime}_{1}\cdots l^{\prime}_{i-1}l^{2}}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-i}(-1)^{i+j}i!j!\binom{k-i}{j}\left(\log_{p}^{k-i-j}\alpha\right)\operatorname{Li}_{\left(\{1\}^{i-1}\ast\{1\}^{j-1},2\right)}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right).

Here, we used Lemma
2.2
(2) in the second equality and Lemma
3.4
in the last equality. We conclude that

\displaystyle m_{p,k}((t-\alpha)(t-\beta))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log_{p}^{k}\alpha+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\binom{k}{i}\sum_{j=1}^{k-i}(-1)^{i+j}i!j!\binom{k-i}{j}\left(\log_{p}^{k-i-j}\alpha\right)\operatorname{Li}_{\left(\{1\}^{i-1}\ast\{1\}^{j-1},2\right)}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log_{p}^{k}\alpha+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j\leq k\\
i,j\geq 1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i+j}\frac{k!}{(k-i-j)!}\left(\log_{p}^{k-i-j}\alpha\right)\operatorname{Li}_{\left(\{1\}^{i-1}\ast\{1\}^{j-1},2\right)}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right).

∎

Example 3.5
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
with
0<|\beta|<1<|\alpha|
and
f(t)=(t-\alpha)(t-\beta)
. By Theorem
1.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle m_{p,2}(f)

\displaystyle=\log_{p}^{2}\alpha+2\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right),

\displaystyle m_{p,3}(f)

\displaystyle=\log_{p}^{3}\alpha+6(\log_{p}\alpha)\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)-12\operatorname{Li}_{(1,2)}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right),

which are
p
-adic analogues of Remark
1.2
.

At the end of this section, we prove the following proposition, which is a
p
-adic analogue of (
1.2
).

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
with
|c|>1
and
f(t_{1},t_{2})=t_{1}+t_{1}^{-1}+t_{2}+t_{2}^{-1}+c
. Then we have

\displaystyle m_{p}(f)=\log_{p}c-\frac{2}{c^{2}}{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2},1,1;2,2,2;\frac{16}{c^{2}}\right).

Proof.

Put
g(t_{1},t_{2})=c^{-1}(t_{1}+t_{1}^{-1}+t_{2}+t_{2}^{-1})\in\mathfrak{m}_{p}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},t_{2}^{\pm 1}]
. Then we compute

\displaystyle m_{p}(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{p}^{2}}\log_{p}c\left(1+g(z_{1},z_{2})\right)\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\frac{dz_{2}}{z_{2}}

\displaystyle=\log_{p}c+\int_{T_{p}^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{m}g(z_{1},z_{2})^{m}\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\frac{dz_{2}}{z_{2}}

\displaystyle=\log_{p}c-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-c^{-1})^{m}}{m}\int_{T_{p}^{2}}(z_{1}+z_{1}^{-1}+z_{2}+z_{2}^{-1})^{m}\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\frac{dz_{2}}{z_{2}}

\displaystyle=\log_{p}c-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-c^{-1})^{m}}{m}\int_{T_{p}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\cdots+i_{4}=m\\
i_{1},\cdots,i_{4}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{m!}{i_{1}!\cdots i_{4}!}z_{1}^{i_{1}-i_{2}}z_{2}^{i_{3}-i_{4}}\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\frac{dz_{2}}{z_{2}}

\displaystyle=\log_{p}c-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{c^{-2m}}{2m}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{(2m)!}{(i!(m-i)!)^{2}}.

Here, we used Lemma
2.2
(2) in the last equality. Since
(2m)!=4^{m}\cdot m!\cdot(1/2)_{m}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}^{2}=\binom{2m}{m}=\frac{4^{m}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}{m!},

we obtain

\displaystyle m_{p}(f)

\displaystyle=\log_{p}c-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{c^{-2m}}{2m}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{4^{m}\cdot m!\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}{(i!(m-i)!)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\log_{p}c-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}{2m\cdot m!}\left(\frac{4}{c^{2}}\right)^{m}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=\log_{p}c-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}{(1)_{m}}\frac{1}{2m\cdot m!}\left(\frac{16}{c^{2}}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=\log_{p}c-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)_{m-1}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)_{m-1}}{4\cdot(2)_{m-1}}\frac{1}{2m\cdot m!}\left(\frac{16}{c^{2}}\right)^{m-1}\frac{16}{c^{2}}

\displaystyle=\log_{p}c-\frac{2}{c^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)_{m-1}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)_{m-1}}{(2)_{m-1}(2)_{m-1}}\frac{(1)_{m-1}}{(2)_{m-1}}\frac{(1)_{m-1}}{(m-1)!}\left(\frac{16}{c^{2}}\right)^{m-1}

\displaystyle=\log_{p}c-\frac{2}{c^{2}}{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2},1,1;2,2,2;\frac{16}{c^{2}}\right).

∎

4.
p
-adic zeta Mahler measures

In this section, we make some observation on
p
-adic zeta Mahler measures and prove the rest of our main results.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{C}_{p}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
and suppose that
f
does not vanish in any point of
T_{p}^{n}
.

(1)

There exists a constant
r>0
such that
Z_{p}(X,f)
is
p
-adically convergent in
D(r^{-})
.

(2)

If there exists a constant
c>1/(p-1)
such that
|m_{p,k}(f)|\leq p^{-ck}
for any positive integer
k
, then
Z_{p}(X,f)
is
p
-adically convergent in
D(1)
.

Proof.

(1) We write
f
as in (
2.2
). Note that, for each
(\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{n})\in\mu_{ur}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\left|\log_{p}\left(1+g(\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{n})\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m+1}}{m}g(\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{n})^{m}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{m\geq 1}\left\{|m|^{-1}|g|^{m}_{\text{Gauss}}\right\}\eqqcolon C_{f}<\infty,

where
|g|_{\text{Gauss}}
is the Gauss norm of
g
. Hence Lemma
2.2
(1) implies that

\displaystyle|m_{p,k}(f)|\leq\sup_{\zeta\in\mu_{ur}^{n}}\left\{\left|\log_{p}f(\zeta)\right|^{k}\right\}\leq\left(\max\left\{|\log_{p}a|,C_{f}\right\}\right)^{k}\leq p^{-Ck}

for some
C\in\mathbb{R}
and we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left|\frac{m_{p,k}(f)}{k!}\right|^{\frac{1}{k}}\leq p^{\frac{1}{p-1}-C}\eqqcolon r.

Thus
Z_{p}(X,f)
converges in
D(r^{-})
.

(2) Since we can take
C=c
and
r>1
in the proof of the assertion (1),
Z_{p}(X,f)
converges in
D(1)
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Let
K
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of ramified index
e<p-1
. If
f\in K[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
does not vanish in any point of
T_{p}^{n}
, then
|m_{p,k}(f)|\leq p^{-k/e}
for any positive integer
k
. Hence the assumption of Proposition
4.1
(2) is satisfied.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
K
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of ramified index
e<p-1
and suppose that
f\in K[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
does not vanish in any point of
T_{p}^{n}
. Then, for any
s\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
with
|s|\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle Z_{p}(s,f)=\int_{T_{p}^{n}}\exp\left(s\log_{p}f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\right)\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\cdots\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}}.

Proof.

For any
s\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
with
|s|\leq 1
, the family
\{\sum_{k=0}^{m}(s\log_{p}f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}))^{k}/k!\}_{m\geq 0}
converges uniformly to
\exp\left(s\log_{p}f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\right)
on
\mu_{ur}^{n}
. Hence the proposition follows from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Finally, we prove Theorem
1.6
and Theorem
1.7
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

For any
\zeta\in\mu_{ur}
, we have

\displaystyle\exp(s\log_{p}(\zeta-\alpha)(\zeta-\beta))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(s\left(\log_{p}\alpha+\log_{p}\zeta+\log_{p}\left(1-\frac{\zeta}{\alpha}\right)+\log_{p}\left(1-\frac{\beta}{\zeta}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(s\log_{p}\langle\alpha\rangle\right)\exp\left(s\log_{p}\left(1-\frac{\zeta}{\alpha}\right)\right)\exp\left(s\log_{p}\left(1-\frac{\beta}{\zeta}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\alpha\rangle^{s}\left(1-\frac{\zeta}{\alpha}\right)^{s}\left(1-\frac{\beta}{\zeta}\right)^{s}.

Here we note that
|s\log_{p}\langle\alpha\rangle|\leq|\langle\alpha\rangle-1|<p^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
and that
\exp\left(s\log_{p}\langle\alpha\rangle\right)=\langle\alpha\rangle^{s}
(see Lemma 5.5 and Proposition 5.7 in
[
17
]
).
Hence, using Proposition
4.3
and Lemma
2.2
(2), we compute

\displaystyle Z_{p}(s,(t-\alpha)(t-\beta))

\displaystyle=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
(N,p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{N}}\exp(s\log_{p}(\zeta-\alpha)(\zeta-\beta))

\displaystyle=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
(N,p)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{N}}\langle\alpha\rangle^{s}\left(1-\frac{\zeta}{\alpha}\right)^{s}\left(1-\frac{\beta}{\zeta}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha\rangle^{s}\int_{T_{p}}\left(1-\frac{\zeta}{\alpha}\right)^{s}\left(1-\frac{\beta}{\zeta}\right)^{s}\frac{dz}{z}

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha\rangle^{s}\int_{T_{p}}\left\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{s}{n}\left(-\frac{z}{\alpha}\right)^{n}\right\}\left\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{s}{n}\left(-\frac{\beta}{z}\right)^{n}\right\}\frac{dz}{z}

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha\rangle^{s}\left\{1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{s}{n}^{2}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^{n}\right\}

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha\rangle^{s}\left\{1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-s)_{n}(-s)_{n}}{(1)_{n}n!}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^{n}\right\}=\langle\alpha\rangle^{s}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-s,-s;1;\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right).

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Put
g(t_{1},t_{2})=c^{-1}(t_{1}+t_{1}^{-1}+t_{2}+t_{2}^{-1})\in\mathfrak{m}_{p}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},t_{2}^{\pm 1}]
. By the similar computation as the proofs of Proposition
3.6
and Theorem
1.6
, we find that

\displaystyle Z_{p}(s,f)

\displaystyle=\langle c\rangle^{s}\int_{T_{p}^{2}}\left(1+g(z_{1},z_{2})\right)^{s}\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\frac{dz_{2}}{z_{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle c\rangle^{s}\int_{T_{p}^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{s}{m}g(z_{1},z_{2})^{m}\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}}\frac{dz_{2}}{z_{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle c\rangle^{s}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{s}{2m}c^{-2m}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{(2m)!}{(i!(m-i)!)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle c\rangle^{s}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(-\frac{s}{2}\right)_{m}\left(\frac{1-s}{2}\right)_{m}}{(1)_{m}(1)_{m}}\left(\frac{4}{c^{2}}\right)^{m}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle c\rangle^{s}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(-\frac{s}{2}\right)_{m}\left(\frac{1-s}{2}\right)_{m}}{(1)_{m}(1)_{m}}\left(\frac{4}{c^{2}}\right)^{m}\frac{4^{m}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\langle c\rangle^{s}{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\frac{1}{2},-\frac{s}{2},\frac{1-s}{2};1,1;\frac{16}{c^{2}}\right).

∎

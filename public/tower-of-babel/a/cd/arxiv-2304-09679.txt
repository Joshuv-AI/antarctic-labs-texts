Sparse graphs without long induced paths Thanks: This work was supported by the ANR projects GRALMECO (ANR-21-CE48-0004) and DISTANCIA (ANR-17-CE40-0015).
Oscar Defrain, Jean-Florent Raymond

Abstract
Graphs of bounded degeneracy are known to contain induced paths of order Ω ⁡ ( log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) \Omega(\log\log n) when they contain a path of order n n , as proved by Nešetřil and Ossona de Mendez (2012). In 2016 Esperet, Lemoine, and Maffray conjectured that this bound could be improved to Ω ⁡ ( ( log ⁡ n ) c ) \Omega((\log n)^{c}) for some constant c > 0 c>0 depending on the degeneracy. We disprove this conjecture by constructing, for arbitrarily large values of n n , a graph that is 2-degenerate, has a path of order n n , and where all induced paths have order O ⁡ ( ( log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) 2 ) O((\log\log n)^{2}) . We also show that the graphs we construct have linearly bounded coloring numbers. Keywords: typical induced subgraphs, degeneracy, coloring numbers.

Introduction

In this paper logarithms are binary. For an integer
k
, a graph
G
is said to be
k
-degenerate
if every subgraph of
G
has a vertex of degree at most
k

such that

is

-degenerate is called the

Bounded degeneracy forms a very general notion of graph sparsity that includes classes of bounded treewidth, planar graphs, and more generally graph classes of bounded expansion. Because its definition sets constraints on all subgraphs, it is also used in numerous inductive proofs and greedy algorithms, for instance for graph coloring.

In the

Theorem 1.1

(

Let

and let

be a

-degenerate graph. If

has a path of order

has an induced path of order at least

\frac{\log\log n}{\log(k+1)}.

As every induced path is a path, the above result establishes a duality between long paths and induced paths for graphs of bounded degeneracy. Relating these objects is a very natural goal as they are among the most basic structures in graph theory. So it should not be a surprise that it was already investigated decades earlier, when Galvin, Rival, and Sands proved the following general result.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
GRS82
]
)

There is a function

such that for every

has an induced path of order at least

This result can be related to the study of

(i.e., substructures that can be found in every member of
\mathcal{G}
), that originates from Ramsey’s theorem and is currently receiving increasing attention when these structures are induced subgraphs.

If we consider graph classes that are closed under taking induced subgraphs, the above result describes the most general setting where such a function
f
could exist. Indeed, cliques and bicliques have paths that visit all their vertices, yet they do not have induced paths on more than 2 or 3 vertices, respectively. So any hereditary class where a result such as Theorem
1.2
holds should exclude arbitrarily large cliques and bicliques, and this amounts to excluding arbitrarily large biclique subgraphs.
On the other hand, the function
f
defined in the proof of Theorem
1.2
is not optimal for many
K_{s,s}
-subgraph free graph classes (see below) and possibly not optimal neither for the statement of the theorem.

Relating the maximum orders of paths and induced paths is also of interest in the context of graph sparsity theory because of the following connection to the parameter treedepth.
For a graph
G
, if we denote by
\ell
the maximum order of an induced path in it and by

\log(\ell+1)\leq t\leq\ell.

Therefore in graph classes where bounds such as that of Theorem

We refer to Chapter 6 in
[
NOdM12

for a definition of treedepth, a proof of the above inequality, and a discussion on the links between induced paths and treedepth.

Case
{i}=3

A vertex

for some

has the following neighbors in

Case

in

) and possibly a neighbor connected via a rib. The first type of neighbor does not exist in

We proved that every subgraph of

Ribs, sources, and their properties

In the rest of the paper we fix

.
A node

is a

if there is an interval

such that

has depth

.
Intuitively this means that in

the clique

will send ribs to vertices deeper (i.e., in

for the descendants

of

of depth

.

sends ribs to the predecessors of

induced by

Let

be an induced path of
G_{\ell}
. The following definition is crucial in the rest of the proof:

such that

and
\pi(v)
is an internal node of

of

as the minimum rank of a source

Remark 5.1
.

Let

and let

of rank

1.

if

is an internal node of

In a graph

Lemma 5.2

.

Proof.

The node

is a source so there is an interval

\depth(t)=j

of

By Remark

there is no other interval

Let

6
The induced paths of
G_{\ell}
are short

An induced path

of
G_{\ell}
is called

if
\depth(v_{i})\leq\depth(v_{j})
for all
1\leq i<j\leq q
.
It is called

if there exists

such that

and

are monotone.

Lemma 6.1
.

There is a constant
c_{\ref{lem:bluegreen}}
such that the following holds.
Let
Q
be an induced path in
G_{\ell}
such that no source in
B_{\ell}
is
Q
-special.
Then
|Q|\leq c_{\ref{lem:bluegreen}}\cdot\ell
.

Proof.

Let
Q=v_{1}\dots v_{q}
be such an induced path.
The proof is split into three claims.

Claim 6.2
.

The path
Q
is bimonotone

Proof.

By Lemma
5.5
there is a unique node
t\in V(B_{\ell})
minimizing the depth and whose bag is visited by

.
Let
k
be the minimum index such that

belongs to the bag of

.
We now show that
\depth(v_{k})\leq\dots\leq\depth(v_{1})

Let us assume toward a contradiction that it is not the case, and let

be the maximum integer such that
\depth(v_{i})>\depth(v_{i-1})

Take

minimum such that
\depth(v_{j+1})<\depth(v_{i})
.
By the choice of

we note that the vertices in the subpath

have equal depth. Because the edges in

only connect vertices of bags corresponding to

-comparable vertices of

, two adjacent vertices of equal depth necessarily belong to the same bag. Therefore,
\{v_{i},\dots,v_{j}\}
is a subset of the bag of a single node

.
Furthermore by Remark
5.3
the edges
v_{i-1}v_{i}
and

are either tree edges connecting a clique vertex to a top-predecessor, or ribs.

We distinguish cases depending on the type of the edges
v_{i-1}v_{i}
and
v_{j}v_{j+1}

In the case where both

and
v_{j}v_{j+1}
are tree edges,

and

belong to the same clique

(where

is the parent of

in

is induced.
We now distinguish cases depending on whether

In the case where

is a rib with

in the top edge of some source

and

would both be part of the top edge of

, contradicting the fact that

is induced. So it is a tree edge. As

,

is an internal node of

. Hence

is

-special, a contradiction.
The remaining case where

is a rib is symmetric.
This proves that such a

does not exist.
The proof of the monotonicity of

is symmetric, yielding the bimonotonicity of

.

\lrcorner

By the virtue of Claim
6.2
there is an integer
k\in\left\{2,\dots,q\right\}
such that each of
v_{k}\dots v_{1}
and
v_{k}\dots v_{q}
is monotone. We choose such a
k
minimum and set

as the maximum integer such that

.

Claim 6.3
.

For every

with

, we have
\tau(v_{i})\leq\tau(v_{j})
.

The proof is by contradiction.
Let

be the minimum integer such that

and let

.
By Lemma

and since

is a source,

belongs to the clique

,

is a tree edge and we get that

.
If

Thus

\lrcorner

A symmetric proof shows the following symmetric statement.

For every

Given a total ordering
\sigma
of the vertices of a graph

and an integer

, we say that a vertex

-reachable from a vertex

(with respect to
\sigma
) if
y<_{\sigma}x
and there is a path
P
of length (i.e., number of edges) at most
r
such that every internal vertex
z
of
P
satisfies
x<z
.

The
r
-coloring number

\col_{r}(G)
is the minimum integer
k
such that there is a total ordering
\sigma
on
V(G)
such that at most
k
vertices are
r
-reachable from every vertex of
G

[
KY03
]
.

The value
\col_{1}(G)
is exactly the degeneracy of
G
. We saw in Lemma
4.3
that the
G_{\ell}
’s have degeneracy 2. Below we show that furthermore their coloring numbers are bounded from above by a linear function of
r
, i.e., Theorem
1.8
, that we restate here for convenience.

See
1.8

Proof.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
. We will prove that for every integer
r\geq 1
,
\col_{r}(G_{\ell})\leq 2r+8.

Let
\sigma
be any total order on
V(G_{\ell})
such that for every
u,v\in V(G_{\ell})
, if
\pi(u)\prec\pi(v)
then
u<_{\sigma}v
. Let

.

Claim 7.1
.

Let
x_{1}\in V(G_{\ell})
and let
x_{1}\dots x_{p}
(for some

) be a path witnessing that
x_{p}
is
r
-reachable from
x_{1}
.
Then
\pi(x_{p})\preceq\pi(x_{1})
and for every

,
\pi(x_{1})\preceq\pi(x_{i})
.

Proof.

We start with the first part of the statement. Towards a contradiction, let us suppose that
\pi(x_{p})\npreceq\pi(x_{1})
. By definition of reachability we have
x_{p}<_{\sigma}x_{1}
, so by definition of
\sigma
we deduce that
\pi(x_{1})
and
\pi(x_{p})
are
\prec
-incomparable.
Recall that in
G_{\ell}
, if

is an edge then
\pi(u)
and
\pi(v)
are
\prec
-comparable. So in
G_{\ell}
,
x_{1}
and
x_{p}
are separated by the union of the bags of the common ancestors of
\pi(x_{1})
and
\pi(x_{p})
. Hence for some

, the node
\pi(x_{i})
is a common ancestor of
\pi(x_{1})
and
\pi(x_{p})
. But then
x_{i}<_{\sigma}x_{p}
, a contradiction. Hence
\pi(x_{p})\preceq\pi(x_{1})
.
The argument is similar for the second part of the statement: assuming it does not hold for some
i\in\left\{2,\dots,p-1\right\}
, we deduce that
\pi(x_{1})
and
\pi(x_{i})
are
\prec
-incomparable and thus there is a
j\in\left\{2,\dots,i-1\right\}
such that
\pi(x_{j})
is a common ancestor of
\pi(x_{1})
and

Let

Claim 7.2

At most 8 vertices

Let

we get

In this paper we described a construction disproving Conjecture

So far there is no evidence that the

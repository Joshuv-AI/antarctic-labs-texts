Transitive irreducible Lie superalgebras of vector fields
Arkady Onishchik

Abstract
Let 𝔡 \mathfrak{d} be the Lie superalgebra of superderivations of the sheaf of sections of the exterior algebra of the homogeneous vector bundle E E over the flag variety G / P G/P , where G G is a simple finite-dimensional complex Lie group and P P its parabolic subgroup. Then, 𝔡 \mathfrak{d} is transitive and irreducible whenever E E is defined by an irreducible P P -module V V such that the highest weight of V ∗ V^{*} is dominant. Moreover, 𝔡 \mathfrak{d} is simple; it is isomorphic to the Lie superalgebra of vector fields on the superpoint, i.e., on a 0 | n 0|n -dimensional supervariety.

In this paper, I show that such Lie superalgebras naturally appear in the study of homogeneous vector bundles over complex homogeneous flag varieties
G/P

\mathcal{E}

{Lemma}

1) The formulas (
4
) show that
\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
can be naturally considered as a sheaf of filtered Lie superalgebras.
2) The sheaves
\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
are naturally isomorphic.

Proof.

1) It is easy to verify that

\delta{\mathcal{I}}^{r}\subset{\mathcal{I}}^{r+p}\quad\text{for any $\delta\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits_{(p)}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}$ and $r\in{\mathbb{Z}}$}.

(5)

This implies that
[\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits_{(p)}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}},\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits_{(q)}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}]\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits_{(p+q)}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
.
2) To prove this, observe that every element
\delta\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits_{(p)}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
determines, thanks to eq. (
5
), linear mappings

\tilde{\delta}_{r}:{\mathcal{I}}^{r}/{\mathcal{I}}^{r+1}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{I}}^{r+p}/{\mathcal{I}}^{r+p+1}\text{~~for all $r$}.

It is subject to a direct verification that the mappings
\tilde{\delta}_{r}
form a derivation
\tilde{\delta}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits_{p}\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
. The sheaf
\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits_{(p+1)}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
is the kernel of the mapping
\delta\mapsto\tilde{\delta}
. Thus we obtain an injective sheaf homomorphism

\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits_{p}\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits_{p}\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}.

Using local splitness of supermanifolds, it is not difficult to show that this homomorphism is, moreover, surjective. Finally, a direct verification shows that this is an isomorphism of sheaves of graded Lie superalgebras.
∎

(6)

Split supermanifolds

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}=\Lambda({\mathcal{F}})

{Theorem}
For any
p\geqslant-1

\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits_{p}\Omega\cong\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}^{p}\oplus\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}^{p+1}.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}^{p}

{Corollary}

There exist isomorphisms

In particular,

The latter isomorphism is described as follows: every vector field

{Lemma}

\displaystyle[\gamma,\delta](s)=\gamma(\delta s)+(-1)^{p+1}\delta(\gamma s)\text{~~for any $s\in{\mathcal{F}}$}.\hfill\qed

{Example}

{Lemma}

of

is completely reducible, the irreducible representation with highest weight

is contained in

and

{Corollary}

If

{\Gamma(M,{\mathcal{E}}_{\varphi})=0}

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}
, where
\tilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is also an ideal. Obviously, our assumptions imply that there is a decomposition into a semi-direct sum

{\mathfrak{d}}_{0}=\END E_{\varphi}\ltimes\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},

where
\END E_{\varphi}
is an ideal and
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
a subalgebra. Let us show that this sum is actually direct.
Consider the induced representation
\psi
of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
in
\END E_{\varphi}
. The weights of the induced representation
\varphi\otimes\varphi^{*}
are of the form
\mu=\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}

\nu_{1}

\varphi

\Pi_{i}
should enter the expression of any dominant weight in terms of
\Pi

[

[
R
]

Since
(dt)|_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}}
is injective,
G_{i}\not\subset P
for all

. Therefore, if
\mu

\mu(h_{\beta})=0
for all
\beta\in\Pi_{i}
and any such
i
.
Since
\varphi\otimes\varphi^{*}
is completely reducible, we can apply Theorem
5
which implies that
\psi(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})=0

[\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\END E_{\varphi}]=0

The homogeneity of the supermanifold
(M,\widetilde{\mathcal{O}})
proved above implies that the action of
\END E_{\varphi}=\END E_{\varphi^{*}}
in
{\mathfrak{d}}_{-1}
is exact. The radical
{\mathfrak{r}}

{\mathfrak{d}}_{0}

\END E_{\varphi}
and is non-zero since
{\mathbb{C}}\varepsilon\subset{\mathfrak{r}}

. Hence,
{\mathfrak{r}}={\mathbb{C}}\varepsilon
.
Therefore,
\END E_{\varphi}={\mathbb{C}}\varepsilon\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}
and
{\mathfrak{d}}_{0}=\END E_{\varphi}\oplus\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
are reductive Lie algebras with 1-dimensional centers. Any ideal of
{\mathfrak{d}}_{0}

\END E_{\varphi}

\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}

\lambda

5
, the action of the Lie algebra
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
in
{\mathfrak{d}}_{-1}

{\mathfrak{d}}_{0}
-action.
Observe that the assumptions of Theorem

\varphi

{\mathfrak{d}}
for the supermanifolds

{Theorem}
Let

be a flag variety of the simple Lie group

.
Then,
\END T={\mathbb{C}}\varepsilon
and
\Gamma(M,\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}^{p})=0

{\mathfrak{d}}=\Gamma(M,\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits\Omega)

\hat{\mathfrak{d}}

Proof.

The equality
\END T={\mathbb{C}}\varepsilon
is proved in
[
I
]

5

Let

\alpha-\beta_{i_{1}}-\ldots-\beta_{i_{p}},\text{~~where~~}\alpha,\beta_{i_{1}},\ldots,\beta_{i_{p}}\in\Pi^{+}

Obviously, such a weight can not be dominant. Applying item 1) of Corollary

The Lie superalgebra

spoken about in Theorem

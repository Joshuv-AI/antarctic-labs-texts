Given a reductive
p
-adic group
G
, there are two distinct approaches to constructing types for
G
(in the sense of
[
BK98
]
), each of which relies on differing hypotheses (and, whenever these two constructions overlap, they produce the same types). Firstly, one has the construction of Yu, Kim–Yu and Fintzen
[
Yu01
,
KY17
,
Fin21
]
, which is valid for an arbitrary tamely ramified group
G
, but which only produces types for every inertial equivalence class if one imposes additional hypotheses on the prime number
p
. This construction is notable in that it is uniform across all such
G
.

In contrast, the constructions of Bushnell–Kutzko, Stevens, and Blasco–Blondel, each of which tackles only a specific family of groups, come with the advantage of requiring no tameness assumptions on the prime
p
. In each, the key is the Bushnell–Kutzko construction of
semisimple characters
for
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(F)

[
BK93
,
BK99
]
, which are a family of characters of certain pro-
p
subgroups of
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(F)
satisfying a number of remarkable properties. The constructions of Stevens
[
Ste05
,
Ste08
]
, and Blasco–Blondel
[
BB12
]
each rely on exploiting the relationships between
G
and
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(F)
in order to define a new family of semisimple characters by restriction of those of Bushnell–Kutzko. This approach by nature reveals a network of relationships between the theories of types for the various groups for which such a construction has been carried out. Nowadays such relationships fall under the umbrella term of
endo-equivalence
, with the case of
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(F)
and classical groups being just one particularly simple example of a more general phenomenon
[
KSS21
]
.

Since the constructions of Yu
et al.
treat each group in abstraction, such relationships among related groups do not immediately follow—while they do produce a family of semisimple characters (with a more straightforward construction than is seen in the Bushnell–Kutzko setting, due to the hypotheses on
p
), to date the only results relating these constructions have been between
G
and its derived subgroup
[
Bou21
]
. Our goal in this paper is to show that, in the setting of Yu
et al.
’s construction, the relationship between semisimple characters for
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(F)
and semisimple characters for classical groups admits a significant generalization.

Before stating our results, it is useful to quickly recap the approaches of
[
Ste05
]
and
[
BB12
]
, which provide our motivating examples. In
[
Ste05
]
, Stevens considers a classical group

, identified as the fixed points in
\tilde{G}=\mathbf{GL}_{n}(F)
of a Galois involution
\gamma
. The Bushnell–Kutzko construction, along with a generalization due to Stevens in
loc. cit.
, associates to each
semisimple stratum

(\mathfrak{a},\beta)
for
\tilde{G}
a finite set
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}(\mathfrak{a},\beta)
of semisimple characters of a pro-
p
open subgroup
\tilde{H}_{+}(\mathfrak{a},\beta)
of
\tilde{G}
. Stevens shows that if

is

i.e.

is

and equip this group with a set

of semisimple characters by restriction. It transpires that the semisimple characters obtained in this way still have the desired properties, and this approach ultimately leads to a construction of types for classical groups
[

]

[

The technical result underlying the present paper is a generalization of this relationship to the Yu setting, and to a wider class of groups. Specifically, we consider an arbitrary tamely ramified group
G
, equipped with a finite group
\Gamma
of algebraic automorphisms, the order of which is coprime to

G^{\Gamma}
is a closed reductive subgroup of

]

[

,
BB12
]

The construction of semisimple characters for the group

\Sigma

\Sigma

Theorem 1

(Theorem
4.4
)
.

Suppose that
\Sigma
is a

-stable truncated datum. Then the group
H_{+}(\Sigma)
is stable under the action of
\Gamma
, and the pair
(H_{+}(\Sigma)^{\Gamma},\theta_{\Sigma})
is a semisimple character for
G^{\Gamma}
.

In fact, this is explicit: we are able to write down a datum

for

such that
(H_{+}(\Sigma)^{\Gamma},\theta_{\Sigma})=(H_{+}(\Sigma^{\Gamma}),\theta_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}})
.

Given such a natural relationship between the semisimple characters of

for

Theorem 2

(Theorem

)
.

Let

These two results give a uniform framework for some important questions of branching to reductive subgroups that can otherwise only be treated case-by-case. For example, taking

Notation

Let

, with ring of integers

, maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, and finite residue field

. We fix a choice of additive character

\mathfrak{p}
but non-trivial on

. Write
\mathrm{val}
for a valuation of

and, for any finite extension

of

, denote also by
\mathrm{val}
its unique
F
-normalized extension to

. We let
\bar{F}
denote a separable algebraic closure of

.

Let
\mathbf{G}
denote a connected reductive group defined over

, and write

for its group of rational points, equipped with the locally profinite topology. We use similar notation for closed subgroups without comment. The Lie algebra of the algebraic group

, with its

-rational points being

; we denote the duals of these algebras by

and

, respectively. For any suitable object

\mathrm{Cent}_{G}(*)

We write

. Given a point

for the subgroup of

which fixes

; this group carries the Moy–Prasad filtration

; the group

is the parahoric subgroup associated to

, and for any

is a pro-
p
normal subgroup of

. For any

we abbreviate

and for any

.
In fact, as established in
[

]

the groups

are schematic, in the sense that there exist canonical smooth connected affine

is a connected reductive

-algebraic group whose group of
\mathfrak{f}
-points is

.

Similarly, for any

and

we have a filtration of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{o}
-lattices
\mathfrak{g}_{x,r}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{x,r+}
; this induces a corresponding filtration on

by setting

. For any

with

.

We fix a finite subgroup
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{Aut}_{F}(\mathbf{G})
, and assume that

is coprime to the order of
\Gamma
. Via the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbf{G}
, we obtain natural actions of

on

, on

, on

, and on

. Given a
\Gamma

, we write

for the set of points

which are fixed by
\Gamma
.

To ease notation, if
\mathbf{H}
is a
\Gamma
-stable subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
, then we write
\mathbf{H}^{[\Gamma]}=(\mathbf{H}^{\Gamma})^{\circ}
for the connected component of
\mathbf{H}^{\Gamma}
, and set

, to be distinguished from

as necessary.

By

]
,

is a closed reductive subgroup of

, whence

is a closed subgroup of

; moreover, there is a family of

-equivariant embeddings

, each of which identifies

Finally, throughout the paper we impose the hypothesis that

2.
Data and semisimple characters

In this section, we recall the fundamental notion of a datum for constructing a type, following the approach of
[
Fin21
]
(although with our indices running in the opposite direction, following the convention from the initial definition in
[
Yu01
]
). We then introduce various groups and representations associated to a datum,
choosing notation similar to that found in
[
LN21
, §5]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
truncated datum
for
G
is a tuple
\Sigma=(\vec{G},x,\vec{r},\vec{X},\vec{\phi})
consisting of the following objects.

(i)

A sequence
\vec{G}=(\mathbf{G}^{0}\subsetneq\mathbf{G}^{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathbf{G}^{d}\subseteq\mathbf{G}^{d+1}=\mathbf{G})
of twisted Levi subgroups of
\mathbf{G}
, each of which is defined over
F
(and splits over a tamely ramified extension of
F
).

(ii)

A point
x\in\mathscr{B}(G^{0})\subset\mathscr{B}(G^{1})\subset\cdots\subset\mathscr{B}(G)
, relative to a fixed choice of embeddings of buildings.

(iii)

A sequence
\vec{r}=(0<r_{0}<r_{1}<\cdots<r_{d-1}<r_{d})
of real numbers; we set
s_{i}=r_{i}/2
and
s_{-1}=0
throughout.

(iv)

A sequence
\vec{X}=(X^{0},X^{1},\ldots,X^{d})
consisting of elements
X^{i}\in(\mathfrak{g}^{i})^{*}
which are
almost strongly stable and
\mathbf{G}^{i+1}
-generic of depth
-r_{i}
at
x\in\mathscr{B}(G^{i+1})
. (We recall these terms from
[
Fin21
, Def. 3.2, Def. 3.5]
in the next section.) Further, for each
0\leq i\leq d
we require
\mathrm{Cent}_{G^{i+1}}(X^{i})=G^{i}
.

(v)

A sequence
\vec{\phi}=(\phi^{0},\phi^{1},\ldots,\phi^{d})
of characters
\phi^{i}
of
G^{i}
of depth
r_{i}
realized by
X^{i}
, that is, such that via the Moy-Prasad isomorphism
G^{i}_{x,s_{i}+:r_{i}+}\cong\mathfrak{g}^{i}_{x,s_{i}+:r_{i}+}
, the restriction of
\phi^{i}
to
G^{i}_{x,s_{i}+}
is given by
\psi\circ X^{i}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Omitting (v) from the definition above gives the “truncated extended data” of
[
Fin21
]
, which parametrize strictly larger collections of representations. It is convenient for us to include the sequence of characters
\vec{\phi}
so that our truncated data directly parametrize semisimple characters, as below.

Definition 2.3
.

A truncated datum
\Sigma
is completed to a datum
\tilde{\Sigma}=(\Sigma;\sigma)
if in addition to (i) through (v) above, we have

(vi)

\sigma
is an irreducible representation of
G^{0}_{x}
that is trivial on
G^{0}_{x,0+}
and whose restriction to
G^{0}_{x,0}
is a sum of cuspidal representations inflated from
G^{0}_{x,0:0+}
.

Remark 2.4
.

Given a datum
\tilde{\Sigma}
, by omitting reference to the sequence
\vec{X}
and letting
M^{0}
denote the Levi subgroup of
G^{0}
associated to the point
x
by
[
MP96
, §6.3]
, one recovers a datum in the sense of
[
KY17
]
. A consequence of the main result of
[
Fin21
]
is that every irreducible representation of
G
contains a datum satisfying (i)–(vi).

Let us now define two key compact open subgroups of
G
, each equipped with an irreducible representation, derived from these data. Note that in
[
Yu01
]
, our group
H_{+}(\Sigma)
is denoted
K_{+}
and our group
J(\Sigma)
is denoted
{}^{\circ}K
.

First, to a truncated datum
\Sigma
one may attach the pro-
p
group

(2.1)

H_{+}(\Sigma)=G_{x,0+}^{0}G_{x,s_{0}+}^{1}\cdots G_{x,s_{d}+}^{d+1}.

To define the
semisimple character

\theta_{\Sigma}
of
H_{+}(\Sigma)
, we proceed as follows.

For each
i
such that

, let
\mathbf{Z}=Z(\mathbf{G}^{i})^{\circ}
; then

decomposes

into a direct sum of isotypic subspaces such that
\mathfrak{g}^{i}
is the

for the sum of the remaining isotypic components.

From the pair
(X^{i},\phi^{i})
we construct a character
\hat{\phi}^{i}
of

following
[
Yu01
, §4]
:
since

is of depth
-r_{i}
,

defines a character of

that is trivial on
\mathfrak{g}^{i}_{x,r_{i}+}

of

Since

Write again

for its restriction to the group

is then defined as

Next, to

If we now complete

). Moreover, under our hypotheses on

{\tilde{\Sigma}}

[

]
.

3.
\Gamma
-stable truncated data

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{Aut}_{F}(\mathbf{G})
be as in Section
1
. We begin with some straightforward lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{S}\subset\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
be a maximal
F
-torus of
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
. Then
\mathbf{T}=\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{S})
is a
\Gamma
-stable maximal
F
-torus of
\mathbf{G}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{H}=(\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{S}))^{\circ}
. Then
\mathbf{H}
is a twisted Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
, hence a closed connected reductive
F
-subgroup. Since
\mathbf{S}
is fixed pointwise by
\Gamma
,
\mathbf{H}
is
\Gamma
-stable. By
[
PY02
]
,
\mathbf{H}^{[\Gamma]}
is a connected reductive group containing
\mathbf{S}
in its centre.

Suppose that the derived group
\mathbf{H}^{\prime}=[\mathbf{H},\mathbf{H}]
were non-trivial. This group is
\Gamma
-stable, and so again
(\mathbf{H}^{\prime})^{[\Gamma]}
is a (non-trivial) reductive group. Choose a non-trivial
F
-torus
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\subset(\mathbf{H}^{\prime})^{[\Gamma]}
. Then
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
commutes with
\mathbf{S}
and
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{S}
is finite, so that the torus
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{S}
is of rank strictly larger than
\mathbf{S}
and yet contained in
\mathbf{H}^{[\Gamma]}\subset\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
, which contradicts the maximality of
\mathbf{S}
. Therefore
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{T}
is a
\Gamma
-stable torus. ∎

Remark 3.2
.

Note that since

is coprime to the orders of the absolute Weyl groups of both
\mathbf{G}
and
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
, and
\mathbf{G}
splits over a tamely ramified extension of

, by
[

, Lem. 2.2]
this implies that every

or of

splits over a tamely ramified extension of

. Thus the tori

and
\mathbf{T}
of the lemma split over a tamely ramified extension, but as remarked by
[

]
, it is not generally true that

splits over

even if

does.

Continuing in the setting of Lemma
3.1
, let
\Phi(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})
denote the set of (absolute) roots of
\mathbf{G}
relative to
\mathbf{T}
and

\Phi(\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]},\mathbf{S})
the set of (absolute) roots of
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
relative to
\mathbf{S}
. The elements

of
\Phi(\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]},\mathbf{S})
occur as restrictions to
\mathbf{S}
of certain roots of
\mathbf{G}
relative to
\mathbf{T}
. To describe these, note that the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbf{T}
and

induces an action on the set of roots, and in consequence for each root

\Gamma_{\alpha}
on the root space
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})_{\alpha}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The roots of
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
relative to
\mathbf{S}
are the restrictions of those roots

\mathbf{G}
relative to
\mathbf{T}
for which
\Gamma_{\alpha}
admits a nonzero fixed vector on
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})_{\alpha}
, that is,

\Phi(\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]},\mathbf{S})=\{\alpha|_{\mathbf{S}}\ |\ \alpha\in\Phi(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T}),\ \mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})_{\alpha}^{\Gamma_{\alpha}}\neq\{0\}\}.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma\cdot\alpha
denote the orbit of a root
\alpha\in\Phi(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})
and let
a=\alpha|_{\mathbf{S}}
be the common restriction to
\mathbf{S}
. The action of
\Gamma
on
\oplus_{\beta\in\Gamma\cdot\alpha}\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})_{\beta}
is induced from the action of
\Gamma_{\alpha}
on
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})_{\alpha}
, implying the result.
In particular, for any nonzero vector
Y
in
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})_{\alpha}^{\Gamma_{\alpha}}
, taking the average over its
\Gamma
-orbit

\overline{Y}=\frac{1}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{\alpha}]}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma/\Gamma_{\alpha}}\gamma\cdot Y

yields a
\Gamma
-fixed sum of linearly independent vectors, so a nonzero vector in
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]})_{a}
.
∎

Our next lemmas relate to
\mathrm{Lie}(G)^{*}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

There is a canonical identification
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]})^{*}=(\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})^{*})^{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

We have
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})^{\Gamma}=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]})
.
Since
p
does not divide
|\Gamma|
, Maschke’s theorem applies, so that both
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})
and
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})^{*}
are completely decomposable as
\Gamma
-modules. Thus there is a natural
F
-linear isomorphism
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]})^{*}\rightarrow(\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})^{*})^{\Gamma}
defined by extending any functional
f
on
\mathrm{Lie}(G^{[\Gamma]})
by zero on the unique
\Gamma
-invariant complement in
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})
; its inverse is given by restriction.
∎

We write
\mathfrak{g}^{\Gamma}
for
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})^{\Gamma}(F)=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]})(F)
, and similarly for the dual.

Given a nonzero element
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and a point
x\in\mathscr{B}(G)
, the
depth of
X
at
x
is defined to be
d_{G}(x,X)=\mathrm{max}\{r\ |\ X\in\mathfrak{g}_{x,r}^{*}\}
. The
depth
of
X
is
d_{G}(X)=\mathrm{sup}\{d_{G}(x,X)\ |\ x\in\mathscr{B}(G)\}
, when the supremum exists.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
and
x\in\mathscr{B}(G)
are both
\Gamma
-fixed. Then, viewing
X
as an element of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\Gamma})^{*}
and
x
as a point in
\mathscr{B}(G^{[\Gamma]})
, we have
d_{G}(x,X)=d_{G^{[\Gamma]}}(x,X)
.

Proof.

Taking
\Gamma
-fixed points preserves Moy–Prasad filtrations on the Lie algebra, by
[
Por14
, Lem. 2.8]
. That is, for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathscr{B}(G^{[\Gamma]})
, we have
(\mathfrak{g}^{\Gamma})_{x,r}=\mathfrak{g}^{\Gamma}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{x,r}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}_{x,r}^{*}
is defined as
\{X\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\ |\ \forall Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{x,-r+},\ X(Y)\in\mathfrak{p}\}
, taking
\Gamma
-fixed points preserves the filtration on the dual as well.
∎

We now define almost stable and almost strongly stable elements, following
[
Fin21
, Def. 3.1]
.

We say an element
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is
almost stable
if its
\mathbf{G}
-orbit in
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})^{*}
is closed. Note that if we identify
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{g}
via a non-degenerate
G
-equivariant bilinear form, then almost stable elements correspond to semisimple elements of the Lie algebra. In particular, by
[
AD02
, Lemma 3.3.4]
, such elements have finite depth.

Now suppose
X
is almost stable and let
r=d(x,X)
; then the corresponding quotient element
\overline{X}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{x,r:r+}
is nonzero. We say that
X
is
almost strongly stable
at
x
if the orbit of
\overline{X}
in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{x,r:r+}
under the special fibre of the
\mathfrak{o}
-scheme
\mathbf{G}_{x,0}
does not contain
0
in its Zariski closure. This is the condition that the coset

X+\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{x,r+}
is nondegenerate, in the sense of
[
AD02
]
; for
p
sufficiently large (and again identifying
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{g}
), it is equivalent to the statement that this coset does not contain any nilpotent elements.

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose that
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is almost stable under the coadjoint action of
\mathbf{G}
and that
\Gamma\cdot X=X
. Then, viewed as an element of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\Gamma})^{*}
,
X
is almost stable under the coadjoint action of
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
.

Proof.

Since
X
is
\Gamma
-fixed, it lies in
(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\Gamma}
. Since
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
is a closed subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
, the orbit of
X
under
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
is also closed, and is contained in the
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
-invariant subspace
(\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})^{*})^{\Gamma}\simeq\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]})^{*}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose that
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is almost stable under the coadjoint action of
\mathbf{G}
, and that
\Gamma\cdot X=X
. Let
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbf{G}}(X)
. Then
(\mathbf{G}^{\prime})^{[\Gamma]}=\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}}(X)
.
In particular,
(\mathbf{G}^{\prime})^{[\Gamma]}
is a twisted Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
.

Proof.

From the identification
(\mathfrak{g}^{\Gamma})^{*}=(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\Gamma}
, one sees that
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}\cap\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}=\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}}(X)
. Since by Lemma
3.6
,
X
is almost stable relative to
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
, we have by
[
Fin21
, Lem. 3.4]
that this intersection is a twisted Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
. Moreover, as recapped in
loc. cit.
, the hypotheses on
p
ensure it is therefore connected and thus equal to
(\mathbf{G}^{\prime})^{[\Gamma]}
. ∎

We have the inclusion
Z(\mathbf{G}^{\prime})^{[\Gamma]}\subseteq Z({\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}^{[\Gamma]})^{\circ}
, but note that equality should fail in general (
cf.
Lemma
3.3
).

We now recall the notion of
genericity
, as defined in
[
Fin21
, Def. 3.5]
. Let
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be an almost stable element and let
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbf{G}}(X)
. Let
\mathbf{T}
be a maximal
F
-torus contained in
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
that splits over a tamely ramified extension
E
of
F
. Let
\mathscr{A}(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T},E)
, respectively
\mathscr{A}(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T},E)
, denote the apartment of
\mathscr{B}(\mathbf{G},E)
, respectively
\mathscr{B}(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},E)
, corresponding to
\mathbf{T}
. Let
\Phi=\Phi(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})
and
\Phi^{\prime}=\Phi(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T})
be the corresponding root systems. For each
\alpha\in\Phi
, let
H_{\alpha}=d\alpha(1)\in\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{T})
denote the corresponding coroot. By construction, we have
X(H_{\alpha})=0
for each
\alpha\in\Phi^{\prime}
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be an almost stable element. Choose a maximal
F
-torus
\mathbf{T}
in
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbf{G}}(X)
and
x\in\mathscr{A}(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T},E)\cap\mathscr{B}(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},F)\subset\mathscr{B}(\mathbf{G},F)
. We say
X
is
\mathbf{G}
-generic of depth
r
relative to
x
if
d(x,X)=r
and furthermore
\mathrm{val}(X(H_{\alpha}))=r
for each
\alpha\in\Phi(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})\setminus\Phi(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T})
.

Note that if
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
then the condition in the definition is only that
d(x,X)=r
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose two almost stable elements
X
and
Y
are
\mathbf{G}
-generic of depth

r
and
s
, respectively, relative to a point
x
, and
r<s
. If
\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbf{G}}(X)=\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbf{G}}(Y)
then
X+Y
is almost stable and
\mathbf{G}
-generic of depth
r
relative to
x
.

Proof.

Let

\mathbf{G}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbf{G}}(X)\subset\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbf{G}}(X+Y)
. Under the
\mathbf{G}
-equivariant identification of
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})
with
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G})^{*}
, the almost stable elements
X
and
Y
correspond to semisimple elements of the centre of
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G}^{\prime})
, so their sum is again almost stable. Thus by
[
Fin21
, Lemma 3.4]
the centralizer of
X+Y
is again connected. Since
d(x,X+Y)=\min\{d(x,X),d(x,Y)\}=r
, we have
\mathrm{val}((X+Y)(H_{\alpha}))=\mathrm{val}(X(H_{\alpha}))=r
for all
\alpha\in\Phi(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})\setminus\Phi(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T})
. Thus in particular
\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbf{G}}(X+Y)\subset\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
and genericity follows.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose that
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is almost strongly stable and
\mathbf{G}
-generic of depth
r
at
x\in\mathscr{B}(G)
, and that both
x
and
X
are
\Gamma
-fixed. Then
X
is almost strongly stable and
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
-generic of depth
r
at
x\in\mathscr{B}(G^{[\Gamma]})
.

Proof.

Set
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathbf{G}}(X)
; this is
\Gamma
-stable. Since
X
is almost stable and generic of depth
r
at
x
, by
[
Fin21
, Lemma 3.11]
we have
x\in\mathscr{B}(G^{\prime})
. In fact
x\in\mathscr{B}(G^{\prime})^{\Gamma}=\mathscr{B}({G^{\prime}}^{[\Gamma]})
and
X
, viewed as an element of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\Gamma})^{*}
, also has depth
r
relative to
x
, by Lemma
3.5
. To carry out the proof we now make a choice of torus and move to a more suitable point
y
, as follows.

Let
\mathbf{S}
be a maximal
\Gamma
-fixed torus in
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{T}
its centralizer, which by Lemma
3.1
is a
\Gamma
-stable maximal torus. Let
E
be a tamely ramified extension of
F
over which
\mathbf{T}
splits, and choose a point

y\in\mathscr{A}({\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}^{[\Gamma]},\mathbf{S},E)\cap\mathscr{B}({G^{\prime}}^{[\Gamma]})\subset\mathscr{A}(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T},E)\cap\mathscr{B}(G^{\prime})
.
By
[
Fin21
, Lemma 4.4]
,
X
is also almost strongly stable and generic of depth
r
at
y
, so in particular
X\in(\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{y,r})^{\Gamma}
.

Since
X
is
\mathbf{G}
-generic of depth
r
relative to
y
, we have that
X(H_{\alpha})=0
for all
\alpha\in\Phi(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T})
and that
\mathrm{val}(X(H_{\alpha}))=r
for all
\alpha\in\Phi(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})\setminus\Phi(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T})
. We next show these conditions hold for the corresponding roots in
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
.
Let
a\in\Phi(\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]},\mathbf{S})
and choose
\alpha\in\Phi(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})
as in Lemma
3.3
such that
\alpha|_{\mathbf{S}}=a
. Then the coroot
H_{a}\in\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{S})
is a
\Gamma
-fixed vector in the span of
\{H_{\gamma\cdot\alpha}\mid\gamma\in\Gamma/\Gamma_{\alpha}\}
. It follows that it must be a (nonzero) multiple of
\tilde{H}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma/\Gamma_{\alpha}}H_{\gamma\cdot\alpha}
. In particular,
\alpha(\tilde{H})\in\mathbb{Z}
is nonzero and so
H_{a}=2(\alpha(\tilde{H}))^{-1}\tilde{H}
.
Thus, since
X
is
\Gamma
-fixed, we may evaluate

X(H_{a})=2(\alpha(\tilde{H}))^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma/\Gamma_{\alpha}}X(H_{\gamma\cdot\alpha})=\frac{2|\Gamma/\Gamma_{\alpha}|}{\alpha(\tilde{H})}X(H_{\alpha}).

By Lemma
3.5
,
X
has depth
r
at
y
. If
a\in\Phi({\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}^{[\Gamma]},\mathbf{S})
, we have
X(H_{a})=0
. If instead

a\in\Phi({\mathbf{G}}^{[\Gamma]},\mathbf{S})\setminus\Phi({\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}^{[\Gamma]},\mathbf{S})
then since

we have the inequality
\mathrm{val}(X(H_{a}))\geq r
. Since
\mathrm{val}(X(H_{\alpha}))=r
and
p
does not divide
2|\Gamma|
by hypothesis, we conclude from the preceding that
\mathrm{val}(X(H_{a}))=r
.
Thus
X
is
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
-generic of depth
r
relative to
y
.

Since by Lemma
3.6

X
is almost stable relative to
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
, applying
[
Fin21
, Lemma 3.8]
yields that
X
is almost strongly stable at
y
. Finally, since both

and
y
lie in
\mathscr{B}({G^{\prime}}^{[\Gamma]})
, we conclude that
X
is almost strongly stable and
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
-generic of depth
r
relative to
x
.
∎

With this preparation, we are now able to give a straightforward formulation of the class of truncated data of

that we are interested in.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
\Sigma=(\vec{G},x,\vec{r},\vec{X},\vec{\phi})
be a truncated datum for

. We say that
\Sigma
is
\Gamma
-stable
if
\Gamma
fixes both the point

and each of the

.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
\Sigma=(\vec{G},x,\vec{r},\vec{X},\vec{\phi})
be a
\Gamma
-stable truncated datum for
G
. Define a tuple
\Sigma^{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{ext}}=(\vec{G}^{[\Gamma]},x,\vec{r},\vec{X},\vec{\phi})
, where:

(i)

\vec{G}^{[\Gamma]}
is the twisted Levi sequence in
\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}
consisting of the groups
\mathbf{G}^{i,[\Gamma]}=(\mathbf{G}^{i})^{[\Gamma]}
;

(ii)

each element
X^{i}\in((\mathfrak{g}^{i})^{*})^{\Gamma}
is viewed as a element of
(\mathfrak{g}^{i,[\Gamma]})^{*}
by restriction; and

(iii)

each character
\phi^{i}
is viewed as a character of
G^{i,[\Gamma]}
by restriction.

For each
i
such that there exists

for which
\mathbf{G}^{i,[\Gamma]}=\mathbf{G}^{i+k,[\Gamma]}
, replace the tuple of subsequences
((\mathbf{G}^{i,[\Gamma]},\ldots,\mathbf{G}^{i+k,[\Gamma]}),(r_{i},\ldots,r_{i+k}),(X^{i},\ldots,X^{i+k}),(\phi^{i},\ldots,\phi^{i+k}))
with the tuple
(\mathbf{G}^{i,[\Gamma]},r_{i+k},\sum_{j=i}^{i+k}X^{j},\prod_{j=i}^{i+k}\phi^{j})

Then the resulting tuple
\Sigma^{\Gamma}
is a truncated datum for

.

Proof.

We verify the conditions of Definition
2.1
. By Lemma
3.7
and Remark

, (i) holds for
\Sigma^{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{ext}}
and thus for
\Sigma^{\Gamma}
, with the exception that the inclusions may not be strict for the former tuple. Conditions (ii) and (iii) are given and (v) is immediate. By Lemma
3.10
, (iv) holds for
\Sigma^{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{ext}}
and by Lemma
3.9
, recalling that each

is of depth
-r_{i}
, it thus holds for

.
∎

The difference between
\Sigma^{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{ext}}
and

is effectively only cosmetic. For the purposes of defining the pairs
(H(\Sigma^{\Gamma}),\theta_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}})
and
(J(\Sigma^{\Gamma}),\kappa_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}})
, we may use

and the truncated datum

interchangeably, as we make precise in the following sections. In all cases we retain and refer only to the indices

of the original datum.

4.
Semisimple characters associated to
\Gamma
-stable truncated data

We now show that the restriction of truncated data corresponds to a restriction of semisimple characters. We begin with some results about the compatibility of Moy–Prasad filtration subgroups.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
x\in\mathscr{B}(G^{[\Gamma]})=\mathscr{B}(G)^{\Gamma}
. For all
r>0
, one has

G_{x,r}\cap G^{[\Gamma]}=(G^{[\Gamma]})_{x,r}.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{Aut}_{F}(\mathbf{G})
and
\Gamma
fixes
x
, we have that
G_{x,r}
is
\Gamma
-stable (see, for example,
[
AD02
, Lem. 2.2.1]
). Since
x\in\mathscr{B}(G)^{\Gamma}
and
p
does not divide
|\Gamma|
, it follows that in fact
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathfrak{o}}(\mathbf{G}_{x,r})
. Thus

\mathbf{G}_{x,r}\cap\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]}=(\mathbf{G}_{x,r})^{[\Gamma]}.

By
[
Por14
, Lemma 2.8]
we have
\mathfrak{g}_{x,r}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\Gamma}=(\mathfrak{g}^{\Gamma})_{x,r}=(\mathfrak{g}^{[\Gamma]})_{x,r}
, whence the
\mathfrak{o}
-Lie algebra of
(\mathbf{G}_{x,r})^{[\Gamma]}
coincides with that of
(\mathbf{G}^{[\Gamma]})_{x,r}
; the result follows by connectedness.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

During the proof of
[
Por14
, Lem. 2.7]
, Portilla shows that in the setting of the above lemma we have

(G^{[\Gamma]})_{x,0}=G_{x,0}\cap G^{[\Gamma]};

he includes the proof that
(G^{[\Gamma]})_{x,0+}=G_{x,0+}\cap G^{[\Gamma]}
as
[
Por14
, Prop. 5.1]
.

Given a
\Gamma
-stable datum
\Sigma
, each twisted Levi subgroup
\mathbf{G}^{i}
occuring in
\Sigma
is
\Gamma
-stable, so by Lemma
4.1
and the remark following, for each
0\leq i\leq d+1
and any
s\geq 0
we have

((G^{i})^{[\Gamma]})_{x,s}=(G_{x,s}^{i})^{[\Gamma]};

we write
G_{x,s}^{i,[\Gamma]}
for this group.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be a
\Gamma
-stable twisted Levi subgroup of
G
and
x\in\mathscr{B}(G^{\prime})^{\Gamma}\subset\mathscr{B}(G)
. For any
0<t<s
let
K
be a
\Gamma
-stable subgroup of
G_{x,s}
normalized by
G^{\prime}_{x,t}
and containing
G^{\prime}_{x,s}
. Then we have

(G_{x,t}^{\prime}K)^{\Gamma}={G^{\prime}}^{[\Gamma]}_{x,t}K^{\Gamma}.

Proof.

Evidently
{G^{\prime}}^{[\Gamma]}_{x,t}K^{\Gamma}\subseteq(G_{x,t}^{\prime}K)^{\Gamma}
. If
t=s
then as
G^{\prime}_{x,s}\subseteq K
the result is immediate. Fix
t\in(0,s)
and suppose the result has been shown for all
r
satisfying
t<r\leq s
; this amounts to a finite set of distinct equalities since the filtrations are discrete.

Let
h=gk\in(G_{x,t}^{\prime}K)^{\Gamma}
with
g\in G_{x,t}^{\prime}
and
k\in K
. Then for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
we have that
\gamma(gk)=gk
, and therefore

g^{-1}\gamma(g)=k\gamma(k^{-1})\in G_{x,t}^{\prime}\cap K=G_{x,s}^{\prime}.

Consequently, for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
we have

g\equiv\gamma(g)\quad\text{mod }G_{x,s}^{\prime}.

Now let
\bar{g}
denote the image of
g
in
G_{x,t:t+}^{\prime}
; since
s>t
the preceding implies that
\bar{g}\in(G_{x,t:t+}^{\prime})^{\Gamma}
. By the proof of
[
Por14
, Lem. 2.28]
, we have a natural isomorphism

(\mathfrak{g}_{x,t:t+}^{\prime})^{\Gamma}\simeq(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime[\Gamma]})_{x,t:t+};

whence via the Moy–Prasad isomorphism we may choose a representative
g^{\prime}
of
\bar{g}
satisfying
g^{\prime}\in(G^{\prime[\Gamma]})_{x,t}
. By Lemma
4.1
we have
g^{\prime}\in(G^{\prime}_{x,t})^{\Gamma}
. Thus
(g^{\prime})^{-1}gk\in(G_{x,t+}^{\prime}K)^{\Gamma}
, which by induction is equal to
G_{x,t+}^{\prime[\Gamma]}K^{\Gamma}
. We conclude that

gk\in g^{\prime}G_{x,t+}^{\prime[\Gamma]}K^{\Gamma}\subset G_{x,t}^{\prime[\Gamma]}K^{\Gamma}
, as required.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that
\Sigma
is a
\Gamma
-stable truncated datum for
G
and
let
\Sigma^{\Gamma}
be the corresponding truncated datum for

. Then one has an equality of semisimple characters

(H_{+}(\Sigma^{\Gamma}),\theta_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}})=(H_{+}(\Sigma)^{\Gamma},\theta_{\Sigma}|_{H_{+}(\Sigma)^{\Gamma}}).

Proof.

Let
\Sigma=(\vec{G},x,\vec{r},\vec{X},\vec{\phi})
. Recall that

H_{+}(\Sigma)=G^{0}_{x,0+}G^{1}_{x,s_{0}+}\cdots G^{d+1}_{x,s_{d}+}
.
Inductively applying Lemma
4.3
yields the equality

H_{+}(\Sigma)^{\Gamma}=G^{0,[\Gamma]}_{x,0+}G^{1,[\Gamma]}_{x,s_{0}+}\cdots G^{d+1,[\Gamma]}_{x,s_{d}+},

which is the group
H_{+}(\Sigma^{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{ext}})
as defined in (
2.1
).
Since
0<s_{0}<\cdots<s_{d}
, if
G^{i,[\Gamma]}=G^{i+1,[\Gamma]}=\cdots=G^{i+k,[\Gamma]}
, then

G^{i,[\Gamma]}_{x,s_{i-1+}}G^{i+1,[\Gamma]}_{x,s_{i}+}\cdots G^{i+k,[\Gamma]}_{x,s_{i+k-1}+}=G^{i,[\Gamma]}_{x,s_{i-1}+}.

Thus we have
H(\Sigma^{\Gamma})=H(\Sigma^{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{ext}})=H_{+}(\Sigma)^{\Gamma}
.

For each
0\leq i\leq d
, we now compare the characters
\hat{\phi}^{i}
defined in (

) produced from these various tuples.

Let
\mathbf{Z}^{i}=Z(\mathbf{G}^{i})^{\circ}
and
\mathbf{Z}^{i}_{\Gamma}=Z(\mathbf{G}^{i,[\Gamma]})^{\circ}
and
as usual let
Z^{i}=\mathbf{Z}^{i}(F)
and
Z^{i}_{\Gamma}=\mathbf{Z}^{i}_{\Gamma}(F)
.
Then under
Z^{i}
we have the isotypic decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}^{i}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
where
\mathfrak{g}^{i}
is the
1
-isotypic subspace. Since
Z^{i}
is
\Gamma
-stable, so is
\mathfrak{n}
, and taking
\Gamma
-fixed points yields a decomposition
\mathfrak{g}^{[\Gamma]}=\mathfrak{g}^{i,[\Gamma]}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{\Gamma}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}^{i,[\Gamma]}
is the
1
-isotypic component of
\mathfrak{g}^{[\Gamma]}
under
Z^{i}_{\Gamma}
, this
Z^{i}_{\Gamma}
-invariant decomposition is isotypic. It follows that
\tilde{\phi^{i}}
, the character of
G_{x,s_{i}+}
arising from the trivial extension across
\mathfrak{n}
of
\psi\circ X^{i}
, restricts to the extension
\tilde{\phi}^{i,\Gamma}
of
\psi\circ X^{i}
to
G^{[\Gamma]}_{x,s_{i}+}
. Consequently, the character
\hat{\phi}^{i}
of
G^{i}_{x}G_{x,s_{i}+}
restricts to the corresponding character
\hat{\phi}^{i,[\Gamma]}
of
G^{i,[\Gamma]}_{x}G^{[\Gamma]}_{x,s_{i}+}
.

Now suppose that in the above setting we have
G^{i,[\Gamma]}=G^{i+1,[\Gamma]}=\cdots=G^{i+k,[\Gamma]}
and have therefore in
\Sigma^{\Gamma}
replaced
(X^{i},\cdots,X^{i+k})
with
\sum_{j=i}^{i+k}X^{j}
. It suffices to note that the restriction to
\mathfrak{g}^{[\Gamma]}_{x,s_{i+k}+}
of
\psi\circ\sum_{j=i}^{i+k}X^{j}=\prod_{j=i}^{i+k}\psi\circ X^{j}
lifts to a well-defined character of this smallest subgroup
G^{[\Gamma]}_{x,s_{i+k}+}
, and that it moreover agrees with
\prod_{j=i}^{i+k}\phi^{j}
on the intersection
G^{i,[\Gamma]}_{x}\cap G^{[\Gamma]}_{x,s_{i+k}+}
. Thus in the notation of (
2.2
), we have

\prod_{j=i}^{i+k}\hat{\phi}^{j,[\Gamma]}=\widehat{\prod_{j=i}^{i+k}\phi^{j,[\Gamma]}}.

From these two paragraphs it follows that on the subgroup
H_{+}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
we have the desired equality of semisimple characters

\theta_{\Sigma}|_{H_{+}(\Sigma)^{\Gamma}}=\theta_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{ext}}}=\theta_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
.
∎

5.
Restricting representations to
G^{[\Gamma]}

We now fix a
\Gamma
-stable truncated datum
\Sigma=(\vec{G},x,\vec{r},\vec{X},\vec{\phi})
and complete it to a datum
(\Sigma;\sigma)
as in Definition
2.3
; in order to simplify the notation in this section, we also denote this completed datum by
\Sigma
, understanding
\sigma
to be fixed.
Via
[
MP96
, §6.3]
, the point
x\in\mathscr{B}(G^{0})
defines a Levi subgroup
M^{0}
of
G^{0}
; let
M
denote the
G
-centralizer of the centre of
M^{0}
, so that
M
is a Levi subgroup of
G
. Then
(J(\Sigma),\lambda_{\Sigma})
is a
G
-cover of
(J(\Sigma)\cap M),\lambda_{\Sigma})
, and every irreducible subquotient of
\textrm{c-}\mathrm{Ind}_{J(\Sigma)\cap M}^{M}\ \lambda_{\Sigma}
is a supercuspidal representation of
M
, with any two such subquotients differing by a twist by some unramified character of
M
. Let
\pi_{M}
denote any such subquotient.

Given a parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
with Levi factor
M
, one has a finite-length parabolically induced representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}\ \pi_{M}\otimes 1
. Throughout this section,
\pi
will denote an arbitrary (not necessarily irreducible) subquotient of
\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}\ \pi_{M}
. The representation
\pi|_{G^{[\Gamma]}}
is very often a non-semisimple representation of infinite length. Nonetheless, in this section we prove a result which allows one to explicitly identify a number of irreducible subquotients of
\pi|_{G^{[\Gamma]}}
, up to
G^{[\Gamma]}
-inertial equivalence. We do this by studying the decomposition of
\lambda_{\Sigma}|_{J(\Sigma)\cap G^{[\Gamma]}}
into irreducible components.

Recall that one has a decomposition
\lambda_{\Sigma}=\sigma\otimes\kappa_{\Sigma}
, with
\kappa_{\Sigma}
being a Heisenberg–Weil lift of
\theta_{\Sigma}
. Since
\Sigma
is
\Gamma
-stable, one has by Theorem
4.4
that
H_{+}(\Sigma)\cap G^{[\Gamma]}=H_{+}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
and
\theta_{\Sigma}|_{H_{+}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})}=\theta_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
. Our first goal is to establish an analogous relationship between the Heisenberg–Weil representations.

Lemma 5.1
.

The group
J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
is contained in
J(\Sigma)\cap G^{[\Gamma]}
with finite index.

Proof.

Let
J_{+}(\Sigma)=G_{x,0+}^{0}G_{x,s_{0}}^{1}\cdots G_{x,s_{d}}^{d+1}
. This is a
\Gamma
-invariant normal subgroup of
J(\Sigma)
of finite index and by inductively applying Lemma
4.3
we deduce that
J_{+}(\Sigma)^{\Gamma}=J_{+}(\Sigma^{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{ext}})
, which in turn is simply equal to
J_{+}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
. Taking
\Gamma
-fixed points of the short exact sequence

1\to J_{+}(\Sigma)\to J(\Sigma)\to G^{0}_{x}/G^{0}_{x,0+}\to 1

gives an exact sequence

1\to J_{+}(\Sigma)^{\Gamma}\to J(\Sigma)^{\Gamma}\to(G^{0}_{x}/G^{0}_{x,0+})^{\Gamma}.

Since
J_{+}(\Sigma)^{\Gamma}=J_{+}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})\subseteq J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
, this yields a coarse estimate

[J(\Sigma)\cap G^{[\Gamma]}:J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})]\leq[J(\Sigma)^{\Gamma}:J_{+}(\Sigma)^{\Gamma}]\leq|(G^{0}_{x}/G^{0}_{x,0+})^{\Gamma}|.

∎

Note that the representation
\kappa_{\Sigma}
of
J(\Sigma)
restricts to
H_{+}(\Sigma)
as a sum of copies of
\theta_{\Sigma}
, and so must restrict further to
H_{+}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
as a sum of copies of
\theta_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
. In particular, any irreducible component of
\kappa_{\Sigma}|_{J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})}
restricts to
H_{+}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
as a sum of copies of
\theta_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The restriction to
J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
of
\kappa_{\Sigma}
is
\kappa_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
-isotypic.

Proof.

The representation
\kappa_{\Sigma}
is defined as a tensor product
\bigotimes_{i=0}^{d}\kappa_{\Sigma}^{i}
of certain representations
\kappa_{\Sigma}^{i}
. To prove the proposition, we recall the construction of these representations following
[
HM08
]
and show at the same time that for each
i
,
\kappa_{\Sigma}^{i}|_{J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})}
is
\kappa_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}^{i}
-isotypic. We choose notation compatible with
[
LN21
, Proposition 7.5]
; in particularly note that the groups denoted with script
\mathcal{J}
here are those denoted with
J
in
[
Yu01
]
.

For each
0\leq i\leq d
, let
J^{i}(\Sigma)=J(\Sigma)\cap G^{i}
. Our first step is to define the groups

\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma)=(G^{i},G^{i+1})_{x,(r_{i},s_{i})}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{J}^{i+1}_{+}(\Sigma)=(G^{i},G^{i+1})_{x,(r_{i},s_{i}+)},

that are “almost complements” of
J^{i}(\Sigma)
in
J^{i+1}(\Sigma)
in the sense that for each
i
, they give the equality
J^{i+1}(\Sigma)=J^{i}(\Sigma)\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma)
and their intersection is merely
G^{i}_{x,r_{i}}
.

Choose a maximal torus
\mathbf{S}
in
\mathbf{G}^{0,[\Gamma]}
with tamely ramified splitting field
E
such that
x\in\mathscr{A}(\mathbf{G}^{0,[\Gamma]},\mathbf{S},E)\cap\mathscr{B}(G^{0})
. Let
\mathbf{T}
be its centralizer, which is a
\Gamma
-stable maximal torus by Lemma
3.1
. Let
\Phi^{i}=\Phi(\mathbf{G}^{i},\mathbf{T},E)
and
\Phi^{i}_{\Gamma}=\Phi(\mathbf{G}^{i,[\Gamma]},\mathbf{S},E)
be the corresponding root systems. Writing
U_{\alpha}
for the root subgroup corresponding to
\alpha
over
E
, with filtration subgroups
U_{\alpha,x,s}
, we define

\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma)(E)=\langle\mathbf{T}(E)_{r_{i}},U_{\alpha,x,r_{i}},U_{\beta,x,s_{i}}\ |\ \alpha\in\Phi^{i},\beta\in\Phi^{i+1}\setminus\Phi^{i}\rangle,

and set
\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma)(E)\cap G^{i+1}
. The group
\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})=\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma^{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{ext}})
is defined analogously. The compatibility of the root systems shown in Lemma
3.1
gives us the inclusion
\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})\subseteq\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma)
.
Replacing
s_{i}
with
s_{i}+
in the above definition gives also
\mathcal{J}_{+}^{i+1}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})\subseteq\mathcal{J}_{+}^{i+1}(\Sigma)
.

The character
\hat{\phi}^{i}
, restricted to
\mathcal{J}_{+}^{i+1}(\Sigma)\subseteq G^{i+1}_{x,s_{i}+}
, has kernel denoted
\mathcal{N}^{i+1}(\Sigma)
.
Note that this kernel is uniquely determined by the image of
X^{i}
in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{x,-r_{i}:-r_{i}+}
.
Since the character
\hat{\phi}^{i,[\Gamma]}
associated to
\Sigma^{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{ext}}
is the restriction of the character
\hat{\phi}^{i}
associated to
\Sigma
, we have
\mathcal{N}^{i+1}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})\subset\mathcal{N}^{i+1}(\Sigma)
.

We next define

\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma}=\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma)/\mathcal{N}^{i+1}(\Sigma),\text{and}\;\mathcal{Z}_{\Sigma}=\mathcal{J}^{i+1}_{+}(\Sigma)/\mathcal{N}^{i+1}(\Sigma),

as well as the analogous groups defined relative to
\Sigma^{\Gamma}
. The group
\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma}
is a
p
-Heisenberg group with centre
\mathcal{Z}_{\Sigma}
. By construction, the inclusion
\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})\subseteq\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma)
induces an embedding
\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}\to\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma}
. Since the centres are both isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the map
\mathcal{Z}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}\to\mathcal{Z}_{\Sigma}
induced by the embedding is an isomorphism.

Since
\hat{\phi}^{i}
gives a (non-trivial) character of
\mathcal{Z}_{\Sigma}\cong\mathcal{Z}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
, the Stone–von Neumann theorem associates to
\hat{\phi}^{i}
both a unique irreducible representation
\eta_{\Sigma}^{i}
of
\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma}
and a unique irreducible representation
\eta_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}^{i}
of
\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
.
In particular, any irreducible component of
\eta_{\Sigma}^{i}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}}
is an irreducible representation of
\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
with central character
\hat{\phi}^{i}
; by uniqueness, the restriction
\eta_{\Sigma}^{i}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}}
must be
\eta_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}^{i}
-isotypic.

Next, we define the group
\mathcal{W}_{\Sigma}=\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma)/\mathcal{J}_{+}^{i+1}(\Sigma)
. When nontrivial, this group naturally carries the structure of an
\mathfrak{f}
-vector space, on which the character
\hat{\phi}^{i}
defines a symplectic form by composing with the commutator. Carrying out the analogous construction with
\Sigma^{\Gamma}
yields an embedding

\mathcal{W}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}\to\mathcal{W}_{\Sigma}
that respects these symplectic structures. As shown carefully in
[
Yu01
, §10]
, via the conjugation action of
J^{i}(\Sigma)
on
\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma)
we may by the theory of the Heisenberg–Weil lift extend
\eta_{\Sigma}^{i}
to a representation
\hat{\eta}_{\Sigma}^{i}
of
J^{i}(\Sigma)\ltimes\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma)
, and similarly for
\Sigma^{\Gamma}
.
Moreover, up to a minor choice (that can be made consistently) when

and
\dim(\mathcal{W}_{\Sigma})=2

[
HM08
, §2.3]
, this extension is unique.
It therefore follows that the restriction of
\hat{\eta}_{\Sigma}^{i}
to
J^{i}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})\ltimes\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
is
\hat{\eta}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}^{i}
-isotypic.

Note that when
G^{i,[\Gamma]}=G^{i+1,[\Gamma]}
we simply have
\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})=\mathcal{J}^{i+1}_{+}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})=G^{i,[\Gamma]}_{x,r_{i}}
, so that
\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}=\mathcal{Z}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
,
\eta^{i}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}=\hat{\phi}^{i}
and
\mathcal{W}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
is trivial. In this case,
\eta^{i}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}=1\ltimes\phi^{i,[\Gamma]}|_{G^{i,[\Gamma]}_{x,r_{i}}}
and
\mathcal{J}^{i+1}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})\subset J^{i}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})=J^{i+1}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
.

Finally, the representation
\kappa_{\Sigma}^{i}
is defined by making the identification

in order to define

\kappa_{\Sigma}^{i}(gj)=\phi^{i}(g)\hat{\eta}^{i}_{\Sigma}(g,j),

for

and

. This representation may then be inflated to the group

. Following the same process for

yields the representation
\kappa^{i}_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
, and we deduce
that the restriction of
\kappa_{\Sigma}^{i}
is
\kappa_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}^{i}
-isotypic, as required.
∎

From this, one immediately deduces the following.

Corollary 5.3
.

Any irreducible component of
(\sigma\otimes\kappa_{\Sigma})|_{J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})}
is isomorphic to
\xi\otimes\kappa_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
for some irreducible component
\xi
of
\sigma|_{J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})}
.

We therefore have achieved a decomposition into irreducible components of
\lambda_{\Sigma}|_{J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})}
. Let us now apply this to the study of
\pi|_{G^{[\Gamma]}}
.

If the representation
\xi
is an irreducible component of
\sigma|_{J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})}
that is cuspidal when viewed as a representation of
G_{x,0:0+}^{0,[\Gamma]}
, then
\xi\otimes\kappa_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
is an
\mathfrak{s}
-type, for
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}
the inertial equivalence class in
G^{[\Gamma]}
of a supercuspidal representation of
M^{[\Gamma]}
, the Levi subgroup corresponding to
\Sigma^{\Gamma}

[
KY17
, Thm. 7.5]
.

If not, then it is not initially clear which inertial equivalence classes
\xi\otimes\kappa_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
may intertwine with. In order to deal with this issue, we carry out a process established in
[
LN21
, 7.2–7.5]
in order to construct, for each component
\xi
, an open subgroup
J_{\xi}
of
J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
and an irreducible component
\lambda_{\xi}
of
\xi\otimes\kappa_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}|_{J_{\xi}}
such that
(J_{\xi},\lambda_{\xi})
is an
\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}
-type for some
G^{[\Gamma]}
-inertial equivalence class
\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}
. Rather than repeat the arguments of
[
LN21
, 7.2–7.5]

mutatis mutandis
, we simply outline the key steps of the construction:

•

The representation
\xi
is an irreducible representation of
J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
that is trivial on
J_{+}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
, and therefore descends to an irreducible representation of
J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})/J_{+}(\Sigma^{\Gamma})\simeq G_{x}^{0,[\Gamma]}/G_{x,0+}^{0,[\Gamma]}
. By Clifford theory, further restriction gives a sum of pairwise
G_{x}^{0,[\Gamma]}
-conjugate irreducible representations of
G_{x,0:0+}^{0,[\Gamma]}
, which is a finite group of Lie type. Fix an element
\xi^{\prime}
of this
G_{x}^{0,[\Gamma]}
-orbit, and let
(\mathsf{L}_{\xi}^{\prime},\zeta_{\xi}^{\prime})
denote a representative of its cuspidal support.

•

Using
[
LN21
, Lem. 7.2]
, we associate to
\mathsf{L}_{\xi}^{\prime}
a point
u_{\xi}\in\mathscr{B}(M^{0,[\Gamma]})
. The point
u_{\xi}
in turn defines a Levi subgroup of
G^{0,[\Gamma]}
, which we denote by
L_{\xi}^{0}
.

•

Via the inclusion
G_{u_{\xi}}^{0,[\Gamma]}\hookrightarrow G_{x}^{0,[\Gamma]}
, one may choose an irreducible component
\zeta_{\xi}
of
\xi|_{G_{u_{\xi}}^{0,[\Gamma]}}
such that the restriction of
\zeta_{\xi}
to
G_{u_{\xi},0}^{0,[\Gamma]}
is a sum of
G_{u_{\xi}}^{0,[\Gamma]}
-conjugates of
\zeta_{\xi}^{\prime}
.

•

Set
\lambda_{\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma}}=\zeta_{\xi}\otimes\kappa_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
. It then follows from
[
LN21
, Lem. 7.3]
that the tuple
(\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma};\zeta_{\xi})
derived from
(\Sigma^{\Gamma};\sigma)
by replacing

with
u_{\xi}
and
\sigma
with
\zeta_{\xi}
is a datum with corresponding type
(J(\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma}),\lambda_{\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma}})
.
Moreover,
[
LN21
, Lem. 7.2]
shows that
J(\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma})\subset J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
, and it follows from
[
LN21
, Prop. 7.5]
that

\mathrm{Hom}_{J(\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma})}(\lambda_{\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma}},\ \xi\otimes\kappa_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}})\neq 0.

Let
\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}
denote the
G^{[\Gamma]}
-inertial equivalence class such that
(J(\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma}),\lambda_{\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma}})
is an
\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}
-type.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\Sigma
and
\pi
be as above. For each irreducible component
\xi
of
\sigma|_{J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})}
, there exists an irreducible subquotient
\pi_{\xi}
of
\pi|_{G^{[\Gamma]}}
that has inertial support
\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}
.

Proof.

Since
\pi
contains
\lambda_{\Sigma}
, it contains a copy of
\lambda_{\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma}}
for each representation
\xi
, which implies that
\pi|_{G^{[\Gamma]}}
has a non-trivial projection onto
\mathrm{Rep}_{\lambda_{\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma}}}(G^{[\Gamma]})
, the category of smooth representations generated by their
\lambda_{\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma}}
-isotypic vectors, for each
\xi
. As the pair
(J(\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma}),\lambda_{\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma}})
is an
\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}
-type, one has
\mathrm{Rep}_{\lambda_{\Sigma_{\xi}^{\Gamma}}}(G^{[\Gamma]})=\mathrm{Rep}^{\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}}(G^{[\Gamma]})
, the corresponding Bernstein block. The representation
\pi
therefore has non-trivial projection onto this block, and the image of
\pi
under this projection, being finitely generated, admits an irreducible quotient. By construction, any such irreducible quotient has inertial support
\mathfrak{s}_{\xi}
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

In general, this does not provide an exhaustive list of the inertial supports of representations occuring in
\pi|_{G^{[\Gamma]}}
. The finite-length representation
\pi
is generated by its
\lambda_{\Sigma}
-isotypic vectors, hence by its vectors on which
J(\Sigma^{\Gamma})
acts as
\xi\otimes\kappa_{\Sigma^{\Gamma}}
for some
\xi
, but only as a representation of
G
. In general, one may apply Mackey’s theorem to say that

\pi|_{G^{[\Gamma]}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Res}_{G^{[\Gamma]}}^{G}\textrm{c-}\mathrm{Ind}_{J(\Sigma)}^{G}\ \lambda_{\Sigma}=\bigoplus_{G^{[\Gamma]}\backslash G/J(\Sigma)}\textrm{c-}\mathrm{Ind}_{{}^{g}J(\Sigma)\cap G^{[\Gamma]}}^{G^{[\Gamma]}}\ {}^{g}\lambda_{\Sigma}.

What we have done is describe (exhaustively) the inertial equivalence classes of those subrepresentations of
\pi|_{G^{[\Gamma]}}
which are contained within the trivial Mackey summand

\textrm{c-}\mathrm{Ind}_{J(\Sigma)\cap G^{[\Gamma]}}^{G^{[\Gamma]}}\ \lambda_{\Sigma}.

However, the other summands are far less amenable to description. For example, while it is true that for any
g\in G
, conjugation of the pair
(J(\Sigma),\lambda_{\Sigma})
gives the pair
(J({}^{g}\Sigma),\lambda_{{}^{g}\tilde{\Sigma}})
, where
{}^{g}\Sigma
is still a datum for
G
, the datum
{}^{g}\Sigma
need not be
\Gamma
-stable. In any situations where
{}^{g}\Sigma

is

\Gamma
-stable, then we may similarly describe the inertial equivalence classes of representations occurring within the Mackey summand

\textrm{c-}\mathrm{Ind}_{{}^{g}J(\Sigma)\cap G^{[\Gamma]}}^{G^{[\Gamma]}}\ {}^{g}\lambda_{\Sigma}.

One expects, however, that many of these summands do not correspond to
\Gamma
-stable data.

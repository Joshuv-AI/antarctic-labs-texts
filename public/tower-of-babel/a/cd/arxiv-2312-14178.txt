Drude weight in presence of nonlocal potentials
Raffaele Resta

Abstract
The nonlocal potential contributes an extra term to the velocity operator; I show here that such term affects the formal expression of the Drude weight in a nontrivial way. Notably, the present main result fixes a disturbing discrepancy in the Dreyer-Coh-Stengel sum rule [Phys. Rev. Lett. 128 , 095901 (2022)].



Since the early times of norm-conserving pseudopotentials in the late 1970s it became clear that in presence of nonlocality the electronic velocity operator
\hat{\bf v}
differs from
\hat{\bf p}/m
by an extra term, owing to the basic definition
\hat{\bf v}=i[\hat{H},{\bf r}]/\hbar
.

Within band-structure theory one writes the Schrödinger Eq. as

\hat{H}_{\bf k}|u_{j{\bf k}}\rangle=\epsilon_{j{\bf k}}|u_{j{\bf k}}\rangle,

(1)

where the eigenstates
|u_{j{\bf k}}\rangle
are lattice-periodical with band energies
\epsilon_{j{\bf k}}
, and

\hat{H}_{\bf k}={\rm e}^{-i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}\hat{H}{\rm e}^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}=\frac{1}{2m}\left(\hat{\bf p}+\hbar{\bf k}\right)^{2}+\hat{V}_{{\bf k}}.

(2)

The velocity of a Bloch electron in band
j
is then

{\bf v}_{j{\bf k}}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial\epsilon_{j{\bf k}}}{\partial{\bf k}}=\langle u_{j{\bf k}}|\,\left(\frac{\hat{\bf p}+\hbar{\bf k}}{m}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial\hat{V}_{{\bf k}}}{\partial{\bf k}}\right)\,|u_{j{\bf k}}\rangle,

(3)

and the macroscopic current density in dimension
d
is (for double band occupancy)

{\bf j}=-\frac{2e}{\hbar}\sum_{j}\int_{\rm BZ}\!\!\!\!\![d{\bf k}]f(\epsilon_{j{\bf k}})\frac{\partial\epsilon_{j{\bf k}}}{\partial{\bf k}},

(4)

where
[d{\bf k}]=d{\bf k}/(2\pi)^{d}
and
f(\epsilon)
is the Fermi occupation factor.

Little attention has been devoted so far to nonlocal effects on the Drude weight. The customary expressions—derived in the local case—are

;
rap157

D^{(\rm local)}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\sum_{j}\int_{\rm BZ}\!\!\!\!\![d{\bf k}]f(\epsilon_{j{\bf k}})\,\frac{\partial^{2}\epsilon_{j{\bf k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}\partial k_{\beta}},

(5)

D^{(\rm local)}_{\alpha\beta}=-2\pi e^{2}\sum_{j}\int_{\rm BZ}\!\!\!\!\![d{\bf k}]f^{\prime}(\epsilon_{j{\bf k}})\;v_{jk_{\alpha}}v_{jk_{\beta}};

(6)

the Fermi-volume and the Fermi-surface expressions are related to each other by an integration by parts; Eq. (
6
) also admits a semiclassical derivation.

To the best of the author’s knowledge, Eqs. (
5
) and (
6
) are adopted as they stand in the existing literature, even when nonlocal potentials are adopted;
Dreyer22
;
Marchese23
in the following, I am going to show that the above expressions need instead to be modified in the nonlocal case.

In presence of a macroscopic field
{\bf E}
the vector potential contributes to the velocity by the term
e{\bf A}/c
, linear in time. The time-derivative of Eq. (
4
) is then, to lowest order in
{\bf E}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}j_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2e}{\hbar}\sum_{j}\int_{\rm BZ}\!\!\!\!\![d{\bf k}]f(\epsilon_{j{\bf k}})\frac{\partial^{2}\epsilon_{j{\bf k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}\partial A_{\beta}}\partial_{t}A_{\beta}

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2ec}{\hbar}\sum_{j}\int_{\rm BZ}\!\!\!\!\![d{\bf k}]f(\epsilon_{j{\bf k}})\frac{\partial^{2}\epsilon_{j{\bf k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}\partial A_{\beta}}E_{\beta};

this clearly shows that the many-electron system undergoes free acceleration.

In the local case one may exploit

\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bf A}}=\frac{e}{\hbar c}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bf k}},

(8)

in order to obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}j_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2e^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\sum_{j}\int_{\rm BZ}\!\!\!\!\![d{\bf k}]f(\epsilon_{j{\bf k}})\frac{\partial^{2}\epsilon_{j{\bf k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}\partial k_{\beta}}E_{\beta}

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D^{(\rm local)}_{\alpha\beta}}{\pi}E_{\beta}.

The Fourier transform of Eq. (
9
) yields the familiar expression

j_{\alpha}(\omega)=\sigma^{(\rm Drude)}_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)E_{\beta},

(10)

\sigma^{(\rm Drude)}_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)=D^{(\rm local)}_{\alpha\beta}\left[\delta(\omega)+\frac{i}{\pi\omega}\right];

(11)

a derivation can be found e.g. in the Appendix of Ref.
rap160
.

In the nonlocal case Eq. (
8
) no longer holds; it is then expedient to define

\displaystyle\tilde{\bf v}_{j{\bf k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\langle u_{j{\bf k}}|\,\left(\hat{\bf p}+\hbar{\bf k}\right)\,|u_{j{\bf k}}\rangle=\frac{c}{e}\langle u_{j{\bf k}}|\,\frac{\partial\hat{H}_{{\bf k}}}{\partial{\bf A}}\,|u_{j{\bf k}}\rangle

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{e}\frac{\partial\epsilon_{j{\bf k}}}{\partial{\bf A}}.

Integrating by parts Eq. (
7
) one gets

\displaystyle\partial_{t}j_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2ec}{\hbar}\sum_{j}\int_{\rm BZ}\!\!\!\!\![d{\bf k}]f^{\prime}(\epsilon_{j{\bf k}})\frac{\partial\epsilon_{j{\bf k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\frac{\partial\epsilon_{j{\bf k}}}{\partial A_{\beta}}E_{\beta};

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D_{\alpha\beta}}{\pi}E_{\beta}.,

where the Drude weight is now expressed as

D_{\alpha\beta}=-2\pi e^{2}\sum_{j}\int_{\rm BZ}\!\!\!\!\![d{\bf k}]f^{\prime}(\epsilon_{j{\bf k}})\;v_{jk_{\alpha}}\tilde{v}_{jk_{\beta}},

(14)

to be compared to Eq. (
6
).

The Drude weight measures the inverse inertia of the many-electron system. In the special case of a flat potential its value is

D^{(\rm free)}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\pi e^{2}n}{m}\delta_{\alpha\beta},

(15)

where
n
is the density of conducting electrons; in the general case one then recasts
D_{\alpha\beta}
as

D_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\pi e^{2}n^{*}_{\alpha\beta}}{m},

(16)

where
n^{*}_{\alpha\beta}
has the meaning of the effective electron density contributing to the adiabatic current.

;
rap157

Remarkably, the improved expression of Eq. (
14
) fixes a disturbing discrepancy in the sum rule obeyed by the Born effective charges in metals, recently found by Dreyer, Coh, and Stengel.
Dreyer22
Suppose that the sublattice
s
is displaced with velocity
{\bf v}_{s}
: the dimensionless Born tensor is by definition (in either insulators or metals)

Z^{*}_{s,\alpha\beta}=Z_{s}\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{V_{\rm cell}}{e}\frac{\partial j_{\alpha}}{\partial v_{s\beta}},

(17)

where
eZ_{s}
is the bare core charge and
V_{\rm cell}
is the unit-cell volume. When the whole lattice is rigidly translated with velocity
{\bf v}
, the linearly-induced current density (due to electrons and nuclei) is

j^{(\rm total)}_{\alpha}=\frac{e}{V_{\rm cell}}\left(\sum_{s}Z^{*}_{s,\alpha\beta}\right)v_{\beta}.

(18)

In the insulating case no current may flow for a rigid translation of the whole solid, whence the basic relationship
\sum_{s}Z^{*}_{s,\alpha\beta}=0
, called the acoustic sum rule. In the metallic case some current instead flows; in the reference frame of the nuclei it is carried solely by the electrons, all moving with velocity
-{\bf v}
, and whose effective density is
n^{*}_{\alpha\beta}
. Therefore from Eq. (
16
)

j^{(\rm total)}_{\alpha}=e\,n^{*}_{\alpha\beta}v_{\beta}=\frac{mD_{\alpha\beta}}{\pi e}v_{\beta}.;

(19)

by comparing Eq. (
19
) to Eq. (
18
) one gets the Dreyer-Coh-Stengel sum rule:

\frac{1}{V_{\rm cell}}\sum_{s}Z^{*}_{s,\alpha\beta}=\frac{m}{\pi e^{2}}D_{\alpha\beta}.

(20)

As said above, the sum rule is exact—even in the nonlocal case—provided Eq. (
14
) is adopted for the Drude weight.

In insulators the Born effective charges manifest themselves in the phonon spectra: they generate a depolarization field
{\bf E}
which contributes to the restoring force at the zone center. No such contribution may exist in metals in the adiabatic limit, since macroscopic fields are screened therein (Faraday-cage effect). A few calculations of Born charges in metals are available;
Dreyer22
;
Marchese23
their possible experimental manifestations—e.g. in the case of a binary metal—are presently under discussion.

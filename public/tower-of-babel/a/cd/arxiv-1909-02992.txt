A proof of N. Takahashi’s conjecture for ( ℙ 2 , E ) (\mathbb{P}^{2},E) and a refined sheaves/Gromov-Witten correspondence Mathematics Subject Classification: 14N35
Pierrick Bousseau

Abstract
. We prove N. Takahashi’s conjecture determining the contribution of each contact point in genus- 0 0 maximal contact Gromov–Witten theory of ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} relative to a smooth cubic E E . This is a new example of a question in Gromov–Witten theory which can be fully solved despite the presence of contracted components and multiple covers. The proof relies on a tropical computation of the Gromov–Witten invariants and on the interpretation of the tropical picture as describing wall-crossing in the derived category of coherent sheaves on ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} , giving a translation of the original Gromov–Witten question into a known statement about Euler characteristics of moduli spaces of one-dimensional Gieseker semistable sheaves on ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} . The same techniques allow us to prove a new sheaves/Gromov–Witten correspondence, relating Betti numbers of moduli spaces of one-dimensional Gieseker semistable sheaves on ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} , or equivalently refined genus- 0 0 Gopakumar–Vafa invariants of local ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} , with higher-genus maximal contact Gromov–Witten theory of ( ℙ 2 , E ) (\mathbb{P}^{2},E) . The correspondence involves the non-trivial change of variables y = e i ​ ℏ y=e^{i\hbar} , where y y is the refined/cohomological variable on the sheaf side, and ℏ \hbar is the genus variable on the Gromov–Witten side. We explain how this correspondence can be heuristically motivated by a combination of mirror symmetry and hyperkähler rotation.

Introduction

0.1.
Genus-
0
Gromov–Witten theory of
(\mathbb{P}^{2},E)

Let
E
be a smooth cubic curve in the complex projective plane

\mathbb{P}^{2}
. For every
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we consider
degree-
d
rational curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
intersecting

E
at a single point. The space of degree-
d
rational curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is of dimension
3d-1
.
As a generic degree-
d
curve intersects
E
in
3d
points, a single intersection point with
E
should define a constraint of codimension
3d-1
.
Therefore, we expect that counting degree-
d
rational curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
intersecting
E
at a single point should be a well-posed enumerative problem.

We formulate a precise version of this enumerative problem using Gromov–Witten theory. Let

\overline{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2}/E,d)
be the moduli space of
genus-
0
degree-
d
stable maps to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
relative to
E
,
with maximal contact order with
E
at a single point.
According to general relative Gromov–Witten theory

[
Li01
,
Li02
]
, the moduli space
\overline{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2}/E,d)
is a proper Deligne–Mumford stack, admitting a zero-dimensional
virtual fundamental class
[\overline{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2}/E,d)]^{\mathrm{virt}}
.
The corresponding genus-
0
maximal contact Gromov–Witten invariant of
(\mathbb{P}^{2},E)
is defined by

N_{0,d}^{\mathbb{P}^{2}/E}\coloneq\int_{[\overline{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2}/E,d)]^{\mathrm{virt}}}1\in\mathbb{Q}\,.

The main advantage of the Gromov–Witten definition over a more naive enumerative definition is that
N_{0,d}^{\mathbb{P}^{2}/E}
is independent of the specific choice of
E
. This follows from the general deformation invariance property of Gromov–Witten theory. The main drawback of the Gromov–Witten definition is that the moduli space
\overline{M}_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2}/E,d)
is in general of positive dimension and contains stable maps which are very far from being immersions.
In particular, the invariants

N_{0,d}^{\mathbb{P}^{2}/E}
are in general non-integers and their direct geometric meaning is unclear. Nevertheless, a general theme in Gromov-Witten theory is that it is often possible to reorganize Gromov-Witten invariants to form so-called BPS counts which are integers, sometimes more geometrically meaningful, and often have better properties.

Let
K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
be the noncompact Calabi-Yau 3-fold defined as total space of the canonical line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-3)
of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, also known as local
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
For every
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, let
N_{0,d}^{K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}}
be the genus-
0
degree-
d
Gromov-Witten invariant of

K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
. According to the local-relative correspondence of van Garrel-Graber-Ruddat
[
vGGR19
]
, we have, for every
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, the relation

N_{0,d}^{\mathbb{P}^{2}/E}=(-1)^{d-1}3dN_{0,d}^{K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}}

between maximal contact Gromov-Witten invariants of
(\mathbb{P}^{2},E)
and Gromov-Witten invariants of

K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
.
The invariants
N_{0,d}^{K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}}

can be computed by torus localization and the answer can be expressed in the framework of local mirror symmetry
[
CKYZ99
]
.
Therefore, the relative Gromov-Witten invariants
N_{0,d}^{\mathbb{P}^{2}/E}
can be effectively computed.

We define relative BPS counts
n_{0,d}^{\mathbb{P}^{2}/E}
by the formula

(1)

(-1)^{d-1}N_{0,d}^{\mathbb{P}^{2}/E}=\sum_{\ell|d}\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}n_{0,\frac{d}{\ell}}^{\mathbb{P}^{2}/E}\,,

where the sum is over the positive divisors of
d
. The BPS counts underlying the
invariants
N_{0,d}^{K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}}
are the genus-
0
Gopakumar-Vafa invariants

defined by the formula

(2)

N_{0,d}^{K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}}=\sum_{\ell|d}\frac{1}{\ell^{3}}n_{0,\frac{d}{\ell}}^{K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}}\,,

where the sum is again over the positive divisors of
d
.
The local-relative correspondence can be rewritten at the BPS level as

n_{0,d}^{\mathbb{P}^{2}/E}=3dn_{0,d}^{K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}}
. It is known
(see Proposition
2.3.2
) that the BPS invariants

are integers of sign
(-1)^{d-1}
.
It follows that the relative BPS invariants
n_{0,d}^{\mathbb{P}^{2}/E}
are also integers of sign
(-1)^{d-1}
. Therefore, the BPS structure underlying the invariants
N_{0,d}^{\mathbb{P}^{2}/E}
can be considered as understood.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

Every semistable object

not supported in dimension

Proof.

Through the classical spectral cover construction, the category
\operatorname{Coh}_{\leqslant 1}(K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})

is equivalent to the category of Higgs sheaves
(F,\phi)
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, with

corresponds to semistability for Higgs sheaves.

Let

be a semistable Higgs sheaf on

with

\phi=0
. Remark that
(F,\phi)

semistable implies that

The sheaf

-invariant quotient of

and a

Therefore, if

7.
Some heuristic explanation

As in the rest of the paper, we consider
E
a smooth cubic in the complex projective plane

\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
We denote by
V
the complement of
E

in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. As
E
is an anticanonical divisor of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, the noncompact surface
V
is naturally
holomorphic symplectic.

In this section, we give an heuristic conceptual explanation for the main result of the paper: the link between Gromov-Witten invariants of
(\mathbb{P}^{2},E)
and Donaldson-Thomas invariants of
K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
through the scattering diagram
S(\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{in}}_{y^{+}})
.

From the Gromov-Witten point of view,
S(\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{in}}_{y^{+}})
is living in
a tropical version of

(\mathbb{P}^{2},E)
, and so morally (in some appropriate tropical limit) the base of a Strominger-Yau-Zaslow (SYZ) torus fibration on
V
. On the other hand, from the Donaldson-Thomas point of view,
S(\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{in}}_{y^{+}})
is living in a space of stability conditions on
\operatorname{D}^{b}_{0}(K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})
, more precisely in (a triple cover of) the stringy Kähler moduli space of
K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
(see
section

3.4
).
Therefore, we need to understand why the base of the SYZ fibration on
(\mathbb{P}^{2},E)
should be indentified with (a triple cover of) the stringy Kähler moduli space of
K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
.
Furthermore, we would like to understand why the holomorphic curves in the SYZ fibration of
V
, described tropically by

S(\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{in}}_{y^{+}})
, should be related to
Bridgeland semistable objects in

\operatorname{D}^{b}_{0}(K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})
, whose wall-crossing behavior is described by
S(\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{in}}_{y^{+}})
.
Our heuristic explanation will have three steps:

(1)

Hyperkähler rotation for
V
.

(2)

Suspension from dimension
2
to dimension
3
.

(3)

Mirror symmetry for
K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
.

7.1.
Hyperkähler rotation for
V

It was recently shown
[
CJL19
]

that
V
admits an hyperkähler metric and a
special Lagrangian torus fibration

\pi\colon V\rightarrow B\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with three singular fibers.
Tropicalization of holomorphic curves in
V
naturally live in
B
(after tropical limit).
It is also shown in
[
CJL19
]
that, after hyperkähler rotation, this torus fibration becomes

, which is the fiberwise compactified mirror of

(see
[
AKO06

In other words, despite being very different as algebraic complex manifolds

admits a stucture of elliptic fibration),

are diffeomorphic and there exists an hyperkähler metric which is compatible with both complex structures.
In order keep the exposition short,
we suppress the discussion of the exact match of parameters
(there is in fact a family of hyperkähler metrics, and the mirror of

\mathbb{P}^{2}
has complex and Kähler moduli).

Under hyperkähler rotation, holomorphic curves in
V

with boundary on torus fibers of the Lagrangian torus fibration become open special Lagrangian submanifolds
in
M
with boundary on fibers of the elliptic fibration. More precisely, the special Lagrangians submanifolds obtained in that way are special Lagrangian submanifolds of a given specific phase.

7.4.
Conclusion

Combining the previous steps, starting with an
holomorphic curve in
V=\mathbb{P}^{2}-E
with boundary on

\pi^{-1}(t)
, we get an open special Lagrangian submanifold in
M
with boundary on
\pi^{-1}(t)
, then a closed special Lagrangian submanifold in

t

\operatorname{D}^{b}_{c}(K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})

The special Lagrangians submanifolds obtained from holomorphic curves by hyperkähler rotation have a specific phase,
and so the resulting
t

\operatorname{D}^{b}_{c}(K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})
have a central charge of specific phase. This explains why

S(\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{in}}_{y^{+}})
describes semistable objects with a central charge of specific phase (more precisely, purely imaginary).

It is an heuristic explanation and not a proof for at least two reasons.
First, directly constructing Bridgeland stability conditions on Fukaya categories such as
F(Y)
is quite non-trivial.
Second, even assuming that we can directly prove the existence of the expected Bridgeland stability conditions on
F(Y)
, one should prove that in the hyperkähler rotation step, the virtual counts of holomorphic curves given by Gromov-Witten theory coincide with the virtual counts of special Lagrangians given by applying Donaldson-Thomas theory to
F(Y)
. At the higher-genus/refined level, as in
section

with the refined Donaldson-Thomas invariants extracted from the moduli spaces of special Lagrangians.
The difficulty is in the virtual aspect of both sides (e.g. curves in Gromov-Witten theory are far from embedded in general). A non-trivial indication

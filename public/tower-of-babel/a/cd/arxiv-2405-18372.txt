The Jacquet-Langlands correspondence of von Neumann dimensions over arithmetic groups
Jun Yang

Abstract
We show the local-global compatibility of each local Plancherel measure and the Tamagawa measure under the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence of GL ⁡ ( n ) \GL(n) . We prove that the global Jacquet-Langlands correspondence preserves von Neumann dimensions over arithmetic groups.

By

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{v}^{\prime}(\pi_{v}^{\prime})}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{v}(\pi_{v})}

5
The local-global compatibility of Plancherel and Tamagawa measures

This section is devoted to Theorem
5.1
, which gives the compatibility of the local Plancherel measures and the global Tamagawa measures with respect to the local (thus global) Jacquet-Langlands correspondence.

We will show a single cuspidal automorphic representation is not measurable with respect to the Plancherel measure of an adelic group
G(\mathbb{A})
.
Thus it is not feasible to consider the adelic Plancherel measure.
Instead, we show that the global correspondence preserves each local Plancherel measure once the local Tamagawa measures are chosen as the Haar measures.

For an
F
-group
G
, the Plancherel measure of an adelic group
G(\mathbb{A})
is known to be the restricted product of local Plancherel measures, which is described as follows (see
[
47
]
and
[
13
, §2]
).
Let
\{(X_{i},\mu_{i}):i\in I\}
be a set of measure spaces.
Let
I_{0}\subset I
such that
I\backslash I_{0}
is finite.
For each
i\in I_{0}
, we take a subset
Y_{i}\subset X_{i}
such that
\mu_{i}(Y_{i})
is finite and
\prod_{i\in I_{0}}\mu_{i}(Y_{i})
is convergent.
For any finite subset
S\subset I
satisfying
S\cup I_{0}=I
, we define

(X_{S},\nu_{S})=(\prod_{i\in S}X_{i})\times(\prod_{i\notin S}Y_{i})
.

Note for
S^{\prime}\subset S
, there is a natural embedding of measure spaces
X_{S^{\prime}}\subset X_{S}
.
The
restricted product of
\{(X_{i},\mu_{i}):i\in I\}
with respect to
\{Y_{i}:i\in I_{0}\}
is defined by

\prod^{\prime}_{i\in I}(X_{i},Y_{i},\mu_{i})=\varinjlim_{S}X_{S}=\{(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod X_{i}|x_{i}\in Y_{i}\text{~for~almost~all~}i\}
,

which will also be denoted simply by
(X,\nu)
.
A subset
Z\subset X
is measurable if and only if
Z\cap X_{S}
is measurable for all finite
S
and its measure is given by

\mu(Z)=\sup_{S}\nu_{S}(Z\cap X_{S})
.

Now we take
I=V
,
I_{0}=V_{f}
and
X_{v}=\Pi_{\tp}(G(F_{v}))
together with
Y_{v}=\Pi_{\rm urmf}(G(F_{v}))
(the set of equivalence class of unramified irreducible representations of
G(F_{v})
) for each
v\in V_{f}
.
Let
\nu_{v}
be the local Plancherel measure on
\Pi_{\tp}(G(F_{v}))
.
Then the Plancherel measure
\nu_{\mathbb{A}}
of
G(\mathbb{A})
is the restricted product of
\{\Pi_{\tp}(G(F_{v}),\nu_{v}):v\in V\}
with respect to
\{\Pi_{\rm urmf}(G(F_{v})):i\in V_{f}\}
.
Therefore each cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi=\otimes\pi_{v}\in\Pi_{0}(G)
is not
\nu_{\mathbb{A}}
-measurable as
\pi_{v}
is unramified almost everywhere and thus is not measurable with respect to
\nu_{v}
.
Hence we should consider the global correspondence of each local Plancherel measure.

We take the local Tamagawa measure
\mu_{v}
as the Haar measure on the local groups
\GL(nd,F_{v})
and
\GL(n_{v},D_{v})
over
F_{v}
respectively, which are defined in Section
3.1
and Section
4.1
.
For each
v
, we let
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{v},\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu^{\prime}_{v}
be the Plancherel density determined by
\mu_{v}
with respect to the Harish-Chandra measure
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega_{v}
on the local tempered dual
\Pi_{\tp}(\GL(n_{v},D_{v}))
and
\Pi_{\tp}(\GL(n_{v},D_{v}))
respectively.

Theorem 5.1
.

There exists local Haar measures
\mu_{v},\mu^{\prime}_{v}
on
G_{v},G^{\prime}_{v}
such that

1.

\prod\mu_{v},\prod\mu^{\prime}_{v}
are the Tamagawa measures on
G(\mathbb{A})
and
G^{\prime}(D(\mathbb{A}))
respectively;

2.

the local Jacquet-Langlands correspondence preserves the associated Plancherel measures, i.e.,
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{v}(\pi_{v})=\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu^{\prime}_{v}(\pi^{\prime}_{v})
if
\pi_{v}
and
\pi^{\prime}_{v}
are corresponded.

3.

the global Jacquet-Langlands correspondence of tempered cupsidal automorphic representations preserves each local Plancherel measures, i.e.,
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu_{v}(\pi_{v})=\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu^{\prime}_{v}(\pi^{\prime}_{v})
for each
v
if
\pi=\otimes_{v\in V}\pi_{v}
and
\pi^{\prime}=\otimes_{v\in V}\pi^{\prime}_{v}
correspond.

Proof
:
It is known that the product of the local Haar measures is the Tamagawa measure by
[
43
, §29.8]
.
The second property follows Proposition
3.2
and Proposition
4.5
.
The last one follows the fact that
\GL(n,D_{v})\cong\GL(nd,F_{v})
for
v\notin\ram(G^{\prime})
and also the the second one for
v\in\ram(G^{\prime})
.

6
The global correspondence of von Neumann dimensions

We will show that the global Jacquet-Langlands correspondence preserves von Neumann dimensions over arithmetic groups (see Theorem
6.6
and Corollary
6.7
).

6.1
A dimension formula for twisted group von Neumann algebras

We will generalize Theorem
2.3
.
Let
\sigma\in H^{2}(G,\mathbb{T})
be a
2
-cocycle of a unimodular type I group
G
(see Section
B
for type I groups).
Let
\Pi(G,\sigma)
be the equivalence class of
\sigma
-projective irreducible representations of
G
, i.e., if
(\pi,H)\in\Pi(G,\sigma)
, then
\pi(g)\pi(h)=\sigma(g,h)\pi(gh)
for
g,h\in G
.
There is a standard Borel measure
\nu=\nu_{G,\sigma}
on
\Pi(G,\sigma)
which gives the Plancherel theorem for the
\sigma
-projective left and right regular representations of
G
(see Theorem
B.1
).

Suppose
X
is a measurable subset of

\Pi(G,\sigma)
such that
\nu(X)<\infty
.
Define

H_{X}=\int_{X}^{\oplus}H_{\pi}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(\pi)
,

which is the direct integral of the spaces
H_{\pi}
with
\pi\in X
.
Suppose
\{e_{k}(\pi)\}_{k\geq 1}
is an orthonormal basis of its underlying Hilbert space
H_{\pi}
.
We have the following natural left
G
-equivariant isometric isomorphism from
H_{X}
to a subspace of
L^{2}(G)
:

\displaystyle H_{X}

\displaystyle\cong~~~\int_{X}^{\oplus}H_{\pi}\otimes e_{1}(\pi)^{*}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(\pi)

(14)

\displaystyle v=\int_{X}^{\oplus}v(\pi)d\nu(\pi)

\displaystyle\mapsto~~~\int_{X}v(\pi)\otimes e_{1}(\pi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(\pi),

Therefore we will not distinguish these two spaces and denote them both by
H_{X}
.

Consider the projection
P_{X}\colon L^{2}(G)\to H_{X}
defined on a dense subspace of
L^{2}(G)
as follows:

\int_{\widehat{G}}^{\oplus}\left(\sum\limits_{i,j\geq 1}a_{i,j}(\pi)e_{j}(\pi)\otimes e_{i}(\pi)^{*}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(\pi)\mapsto\int_{\widehat{X}}^{\oplus}\left(\sum\limits_{j\geq 1}a_{1,j}(\pi)e_{j}(\pi)\otimes e_{1}(\pi)^{*}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(\pi)

,

where all but finite
a_{i,j}(\pi)\in\mathbb{C}
are zero for
\nu
-almost all
\pi
.
Observe that
P_{X}
commutes with the
\sigma
-projective right regular representation
\rho_{\sigma}
, i.e.,
\rho_{\sigma}\circ P_{X}=P_{X}\circ\rho_{\sigma}
.
We have a unitary representation of
G
on
H_{X}
and denote it by
\rho_{\sigma,X}
.

Given two vectors
v=\int_{X}^{\oplus}v(\pi)d\nu(\pi)
and
w=\int_{X}^{\oplus}w(\pi)d\nu(\pi)
in
H_{X}
with
v(\pi),w(\pi)\in H_{\pi}
, we have
v(\pi)\otimes w(\pi)^{*}\in H_{\pi}\otimes H_{\pi}^{*}
.
The corresponding Hilbert-Schmidt operator will also be denoted by
v(\pi)\otimes w(\pi)^{*}
(via the isomorphism
H_{\pi}\otimes H_{\pi}\cong B(H)_{\rm H.S.}
, the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators).
We define a function on
G
by

C_{v,w}(g)=\langle\rho_{\sigma,X}(g^{-1})v,w\rangle_{H_{X}}=\int_{X}\Tr(\pi(g)^{*}v(\pi)\otimes w(\pi)^{*})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(\pi)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For
v,w\in H_{X}
, we have
C_{v,w}\in L^{2}(G)
.
Moreover,

\langle C_{v_{1},w_{1}},C_{v_{2},w_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}=\int_{X}\Tr(v_{1}(\pi)\otimes w_{1}(\pi)^{*}\cdot w_{2}(\pi)\otimes v_{2}(\pi)^{*})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(\pi)
,

for any vectors
v_{1},v_{2},w_{1},w_{2}\in H_{X}
.

Proof
:
Since
v_{i}(\pi)\otimes w_{i}(\pi)^{*}
is Hilbert-Schmidt,
v_{1}(\pi)\otimes w_{1}(\pi)^{*}\cdot w_{2}(\pi)\otimes v_{2}(\pi)^{*}
is a trace-class operator.
The integral is bounded by the norm of
v,w
in
H_{X}
and hence well-defined.
As
v(\pi)=w(\pi)=0
for
\pi\in\widehat{G}\backslash X
, we have

C_{v,w}(g)=\int_{X}\Tr(\pi(g)^{*}v(\pi)\otimes w(\pi)^{*})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(\pi)=\int_{\widehat{G}}\Tr(\pi(g)^{*}v(\pi)\otimes w(\pi)^{*})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(\pi)
.

By Theorem
B.1
, we conclude that
v\otimes w^{*}=\int_{X}v(\pi)\otimes w(\pi)^{*}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(\pi)=\widehat{C_{v,w}}
, the Fourier transform of the function
C_{v,w}
on
G
.
Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\langle C_{v_{1},w_{1}},C_{v_{2},w_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{G}}\Tr\left(\widehat{C_{v_{1},w_{1}}}(\pi)\widehat{C_{v_{1},w_{1}}}(\pi)^{*}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(\pi)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\Tr\left(\widehat{C_{v_{1},w_{1}}}(\pi)\widehat{C_{v_{1},w_{1}}}(\pi)^{*}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(\pi)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\Tr\left(v_{1}(\pi)\otimes w_{1}(\pi)^{*}\cdot w_{2}(\pi)\otimes v_{2}(\pi)^{*}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu(\pi)

Thus we also obtain
C_{v,w}\in L^{2}(G)
.

Suppose
\Gamma
is a lattice in
G
and
M=\mathcal{L}(\Gamma,\sigma)
, the
\sigma
-twisted group von Neumann algebra of
\Gamma
(see Section
2.3
).
Note the restricted representation
\rho_{\sigma,X}|_{\Gamma}
makes the Hilbert space
H_{X}
an
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma,\sigma)
-module, whose von Neumann dimension can be obtained as follows.
Let
\{d_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(D)
.

Lemma 6.2
.

With the assumption above, we have

\dim_{M}(H_{X})=\sum_{k\geq 1}\|Pd_{k}\|_{H_{X}}^{2}
.

Proof
:
Let
u
be the inclusion
H_{X}\to L^{2}(G)
.
We have
u^{*}u={\rm id}_{H_{X}}
and
uu^{*}=P_{X}
.
Note

On the right-hand side,

The commutant

is generated by the finite sums of the form

,

This is equivalent to say

\Tr_{M^{\prime}}(x)=\Tr_{L^{2}(D)}(a_{e})
.

Let

We have

\Tr_{M^{\prime}}(x)=\Tr_{L^{2}(D)}(a_{e})=\Tr_{L^{2}(G)}(Qa_{e}Q)=\Tr_{L^{2}(G)}(QxQ)

(15)

As

and we obtain

\displaystyle\dim_{M}(H_{X})

\displaystyle=\Tr_{M^{\prime}}(P)=\Tr_{L^{2}(G,dg)}(QPQ)

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}\langle QPQd_{k},d_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}=\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}\langle Qd_{k},PQd_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}\langle d_{k},Pd_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}=\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}\langle Pd_{k},Pd_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{k\geq 1}\langle Pd_{k},Pd_{k}\rangle_{H_{X}}=\sum\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\|Pd_{k}\|_{H_{X}}^{2}

Theorem 6.3
.

Let

\nu_{G,\sigma}
-measurable subset of

Proof

\displaystyle{\rm covol}(\Gamma)

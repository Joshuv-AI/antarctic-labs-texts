Abstract
We revisit processes generated by iterated random functions driven by a stationary and ergodic sequence. Such a process is called strongly stable if a random initialization exists, for which the process is stationary and ergodic, and for any other initialization the difference of the two processes converges to zero almost surely. Under some mild conditions on the corresponding recursive map, without any condition on the driving sequence we show the strong stability of iterations. Several applications are surveyed such as generalized autoregression and queuing. Furthermore, new results are deduced for Langevin-type iterations with dependent noise and for multitype branching processes.

Introduction

We are studying stochastic processes defined by iterating random functions.
For a measurable function
F:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
consider the following iteration:

X_{n}=X_{n}(v)
such that

\displaystyle X_{n+1}=F(X_{n},Z_{n+1}),

(1)

where the driving sequence
\{Z_{i}\}_{1}^{\infty}
is a stochastic process with values in
\mathbb{R}^{k}

In the standard setup,
\{Z_{i}\}_{1}^{\infty}
is independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) and
so
\{X_{i}(v)\}_{0}^{\infty}
is a homogeneous Markov process,
see Diaconis and Freedman
[
17
]
and Iosifescu
[
29
]
.
Furthermore, (

1

is replaced by

,
then the resulting iteration

is called the

,
see Propp and Wilson
[
39
]
,
[
40
]
.
Clearly,

and

have the same distribution for each

In the present paper the main role is played by another type of iteration, called

defined as follows:
For a

X^{(k)}_{n}=X^{(k)}_{n}(0)

\displaystyle X^{(k)}_{n+1}=F(X^{(k)}_{n},Z_{n+1}),\quad n\geq k,

i.e., the iteration starts at negative time

This iteration scheme is also often used and appears e.g. in

[
10
]

[

]

[

]

Under mild conditions, Diaconis and Freedman

has the limit distribution

.
As in the standard setup of Markov chains, if

More general schemes have also been considered, where

such processes are treated under the name

Brandt, Franken and Lisek
[
12
]
called the stationary process
\{X^{\prime}_{i}\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}

\{Z^{\prime}_{i}\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}

1

\{Z_{i}\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}

\{Z^{\prime}_{i}\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}
having the same distribution.

\{X_{i}\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}

(X_{i},Z_{i})

1

In this paper, we study the strong solutions.
Using the a.s. limit of the negative iteration we construct such solutions under mild conditions.
Actually, we investigate the following novel concept of
strong stability
.

Definition 1
.

The class of random processes
\{X_{n}(v),v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}
is called strongly stable, if

(I)

there exists a random vector
V^{*}
such that
\{X_{i}(V^{*})\}_{0}^{\infty}
is
stationary and ergodic,

(II)

and for any random vector
V
,
X_{n}(V)-X_{n}(V^{*})\to 0
a.s.

Note that in the definition above, the random initial vector
V
may depend on
the entire trajectory of
\{Z_{i}\}_{1}^{\infty}
. As a result, the concept of
strong stability may seem overly demanding. Furthermore, for integer valued processes from (II) follows that there is a random index
\tau
such that for all
n>\tau
, we have
X_{n}(V)=X_{n}(V^{*})
. In other words,
\{X_{i}(V)\}_{0}^{\infty}

is

with
\{X_{i}(V^{*})\}_{0}^{\infty}
. This stronger notion of stability was introduced by Lindvall
[
33
]
, and discussed also in Foss and Tweedie
[
20
]
.
Traditional proofs establishing the existence of a unique limiting distribution for Markov chains on Polish spaces under Doeblin’s minorization condition involve representing transitions through iterated i.i.d. random maps and a coupling argument
[
5
]
. In this way, it can be shown that the iteration starting from any possibly random initial value is forward coupled with its stationary counterpart.

The aim of this paper is to show the strong stability of
\{X_{n}(v),v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}
in great generality, for several relevant models and important applications. As in Debaly and Truquet
[
16
]
, under some mild conditions on the function
F
, we show that the a.s. limiting process

\displaystyle X_{n}^{*}=\lim_{k\to-\infty}X^{(k)}_{n}

exists and is stationary and ergodic.
Brandt, Franken and Lisek
[
12
]
had similar results in the particular case
of monotonic
F
, see the definition (
11
) below.

Our main results are stated and proved in Section
2

generalized Langevin dynamics are surveyed in Sections
3
,
4
,
5
, respectively.
Finally, Section
6
treats multi-type generalized Galton-Watson processes.

2
Iterated ergodic function systems

Defining

\displaystyle F_{n}(x):=F(x,Z_{n}),\ n\in\mathbb{Z}

we can write

\displaystyle X_{n}^{(k)}(v)=F_{n}\circ\dots\circ F_{k+1}(v),\quad k\leq 0,\,\,n\geq k,

where the empty composition is defined as the identity function.

In the sequel,
|\cdot|
will refer to the standard Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For a function
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, set

\displaystyle\|g\|=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|g(x)-g(y)|}{|x-y|}.

The following theorem is an extension of Theorem 5.1 in
[
17
]

and
Theorem 6.2 in
[
29
]
. It is contained in Theorem 3 of Elton
[
21
]

(except for proving (II)). We provide a proof for completeness.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that
\{Z_{i}\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}
is a stationary and ergodic sequence.
Suppose that

(i)

{\mathbb{E}}\{(\log\|F_{1}\|)^{+}\}<\infty,

(ii)

and for some
n
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\{\log\|F_{n}\circ\dots\circ F_{1}\|\}<0.

(3)

Then the class
\{X_{n}(v),v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}
is strongly stable.

Notice that (
3
) is a sort of long run contraction condition here.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

For the stationary and ergodic process
\mathbf{Z}=\{Z_{i}\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}
, let
f_{n}
,

n=1,2,\dots
be vector valued functions
such that
f_{i}(T^{i}\mathbf{Z})=X_{i}(0)
, where
T
stands for the shift transformation.
Let’s calculate
f_{n}(\mathbf{Z})
.
If the process
\{X^{(k)}_{n}\}
is defined in (
2
), then

\displaystyle f_{n}(\mathbf{Z})=X^{(-n)}_{0}(0),

i.e.,
X^{(-n)}_{0}(0)
is the value of the process at time
0
, when the process started
at negative time
-n
with the
0
vector.

We show that under the conditions (i) and (ii),

\displaystyle X^{(-n)}_{0}(0)\mbox{ is a.s. convergent to a random vector }V^{*}.

(4)

It will be clear that
V^{*}=f(\mathbf{Z})
for some suitable functional
f
so we
will in fact show

\displaystyle f_{n}(\mathbf{Z})\to f(\mathbf{Z})\mbox{ a.s.}

(5)

As for (
4
), we show that this sequence is a.s. a Cauchy sequence, namely even

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|X^{(-n)}_{0}(0)-X^{(-n-1)}_{0}(0)|<\infty

holds a.s. Notice that iterating (
2
) yields

\displaystyle X^{(-n-1)}_{-n}(0)

\displaystyle=F(X^{(-n-1)}_{-n-1}(0),Z_{-n})=F(0,Z_{-n})=X^{(-n)}_{-n}(F(0,Z_{-n})),

so

\displaystyle X^{(-n)}_{0}(0)-X^{(-n-1)}_{0}(0)

\displaystyle=X^{(-n)}_{0}(0)-X^{(-n)}_{0}(F(0,Z_{-n}))

\displaystyle=F_{0}\circ\dots\circ F_{-n+1}(0-F(0,Z_{-n})).

Thus,

\displaystyle|X^{(-n)}_{0}(0)-X^{(-n-1)}_{0}(0)|

\displaystyle\leq\|F_{0}\circ\dots\circ F_{-n+1}\|\cdot|F(0,Z_{-n})|.

We will show that the ergodicity of
\{Z_{i}\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}
together with (i) and (ii) implies

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|F_{0}\circ\dots\circ F_{-n+1}\|\cdot|F(0,Z_{-n})|<\infty

(6)

a.s. and so (
4
) is verified.

In the sequel, the key ingredient is Proposition 2 in Elton
[
21
]
, which is the extension of
Fürstenberg and Kesten
[
22
]
.
For proving (
6
), note that by Proposition 2 in
[
21
]
, the condition (
3
) implies that
the sequence

\displaystyle E_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\{\log\|F_{0}\circ\dots\circ F_{-n+1}\|\}\to E,\ n\to\infty

with
E<0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\log\|F_{0}\circ\dots\circ F_{-n+1}\|\to E

(7)

a.s.
Note that
E
is called Lyapunov exponent.
Next, we argue as in Proposition 6.1 in Iosifescu
[
29
]
.
One has that

\displaystyle\|F_{0}\circ\dots\circ F_{-n+1}\|

\displaystyle=e^{n\frac{1}{n}\log\|F_{0}\circ\dots\circ F_{-n+1}\|}.

(
7
) implies that there are a random integer
n_{0}
and
a>0
such that, for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\log\|F_{0}\circ\dots\circ F_{-n+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq-a<0.

Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\|F_{0}\circ\dots\circ F_{-n+1}\|\cdot|F(0,Z_{-n})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}|F(0,Z_{-n})|e^{-na}.

Since
\lambda\ln^{+}|F(0,Z_{-1})|
is integrable for all
\lambda>0
, it follows
that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P(\lambda\ln|F(0,Z_{-1})|>n)<\infty.{}

Applying this observation
for
\lambda:=2/\alpha
the Borel-Cantelli lemma implies that
|F(0,Z_{-1})|<e^{na/2}
holds except for finitely many

almost surely, which
implies
\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}|F(0,Z_{-n})|e^{-na}<\infty
almost surely.

Because of
X_{i}(V^{*})=f(T^{i}\mathbf{Z})
,
\{X_{i}(V^{*})\}_{0}^{\infty}
is stationary and ergodic so (I) in the Definition

is proved.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|X_{n}(V)-X_{n}(V^{*})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|F_{n}\circ\dots\circ F_{1}\|\cdot|V-V^{*}|\to 0

a.s., as before. Thus, (II) is verified, too.

\Box

Theorem
1
applies, in particular, under the one-step contraction condition (

) below.

Proposition 1
.

Assume that
\{Z_{i}\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}
is a stationary and ergodic sequence
such that the distribution of
Z_{1}
is denoted by
\mu
.
Suppose that

\int|F(0,z)|\mu(dz)<\infty,

and

|F(x,z)-F(x^{\prime},z)|\leq K_{z}|x-x^{\prime}|

with

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\{\log K_{Z_{1}}\}<0.

(8)

Then, the class
\{X_{n}(v),v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}
is strongly stable.

Proof.

This proposition is an easy consequence of Theorem
1
, since

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\{\log\|F_{n}\circ\dots\circ F_{1}\|\}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\{\log(\|F_{n}\|\cdots\|F_{1}\|)\}

\displaystyle=n{\mathbb{E}}\{\log\|F_{1}\|\}

\displaystyle\leq n{\mathbb{E}}\{\log K_{Z_{1}}\}

\displaystyle<0.

\Box

Definition 2
.

We say that the class of strongly stable random processes
\{X_{n}(v),v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}
satisfies the strong
law of large numbers (SLLN), if

{\mathbb{E}}\{V^{*}\}
is well-defined and finite, and for any
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{n}\sum^{n}_{i=1}X_{i}(v)={\mathbb{E}}\{V^{*}\}

a.s.

Remark 1
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1
,
if
V^{*}
has a well-defined and finite expectation
{\mathbb{E}}\{V^{*}\}
then we have SLLN:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum^{n}_{i=1}X_{i}(v)-{\mathbb{E}}\{V^{*}\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum^{n}_{i=1}X_{i}(V^{*})-{\mathbb{E}}\{V^{*}\}\right|+\frac{1}{n}\sum^{n}_{i=1}|X_{i}(v)-X_{i}(V^{*})|.

By Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem, the first term on the right
hand side tends to
0
a.s., while the a.s. convergence of the second term follows from (II).

Remark 2
.

Now we discuss some conditions guaranteeing
{\mathbb{E}}\{|V^{*}|\}<\infty
.
By Fatou’s lemma and the triangle inequality, we can write

{\mathbb{E}}\{|V^{*}|\}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\{|X_{0}^{(-n)}(0)|\}{\leq}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\{|X_{0}^{(-k)}(0)-X_{0}^{(-k+1)}(0)|\}

which we can estimate further and obtain

{\mathbb{E}}\{|V^{*}|\}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\{\lVert F_{0}\circ\ldots\circ F_{-k+1}\rVert\cdot|F(0,Z_{-k})|\}.

For the sake of simplicity, assume for the moment that
z\mapsto|F(0,z)|
is bounded by some
constant
C
.
By stationarity, it is enough to investigate
{\mathbb{E}}\{\lVert F_{k}\circ\ldots\circ F_{1}\rVert\}
.
Here, either we can prescribe a “contractivity in the long run”-type condition like

\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}^{1/k}\{\lVert F_{k}\circ\ldots\circ F_{1}\rVert\}<1,

(9)

and then the
n
-th root test gives the desired result (that is,
{\mathbb{E}}\{|V^{*}|\}<\infty
), or,
by Hölder’s inequality, we can write

{\mathbb{E}}\{\lVert F_{k}\circ\ldots\circ F_{1}\rVert\}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{k}{\mathbb{E}}^{1/k}\{\lVert F_{j}\rVert^{k}\}={\mathbb{E}}\{\lVert F_{1}\rVert^{k}\},

and thus we have

{\mathbb{E}}\{|V^{*}|\}\leq\sup_{z}|F(0,z)|{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lVert F_{1}\rVert^{k}\right\}\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\frac{\lVert F_{1}\rVert}{1-\lVert F_{1}\rVert}\right\}.

We should assume here that
\lVert F_{1}\rVert<1
a.s.,
moreover
{\mathbb{E}}\left\{\frac{\lVert F_{1}\rVert}{1-\lVert F_{1}\rVert}\right\}<\infty

hence this approach looks much more restrictive than requiring (
9

As pointed out in Truquet
[
45
]
, the above long-time contractivity
condition (
9

3
) in Theorem
1
or, equivalently, (
7
).
On the other hand, in the i.i.d. case, (
9
) reduces to
{\mathbb{E}}\{\lVert F_{1}\rVert\}<1
.
If
{\mathbb{E}}\{\lVert F_{1}\rVert\}<1
fails in the i.i.d. case then

can easily happen,
see the example in Remark

Remark 3
.

Concerning the strong law of large numbers, we should have to verify

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\{\sup_{n}|X^{(-n)}_{0}(0)|\}<\infty.

(10)

Note that
X^{(0)}_{0}(0)=0
, and thus

\displaystyle\sup_{n}|X^{(-n)}_{0}(0)|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|X^{(-k+1)}_{0}(0)-X^{(-k)}_{0}(0)|.

One possibility would be to check

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left\{|X^{(-k+1)}_{0}(0)-X^{(-k)}_{0}(0)|\right\}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left\{|F(0,Z_{-k})|\prod_{j=-k+1}^{0}K_{Z_{j}}\right\}<\infty.

Here is a counterexample, however, that this is not true in general.
Let
d=k=1
and
\{Z_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be i.i.d. such that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\{Z_{0}=e^{-2}\}=2/3\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathbb{P}}\{Z_{0}=e^{2}\}=1/3.

Put
X_{0}=v>0
and

\displaystyle X_{n+1}=Z_{n+1}\cdot X_{n}.

Clearly
K_{Z_{0}}=Z_{0}
with
{\mathbb{E}}\{\log K_{Z_{0}}\}=-2/3<0
and so the conditions of Proposition
1
are satisfied.
Furthermore,
{\mathbb{E}}\{K_{Z_{0}}\}={\mathbb{E}}\{Z_{1}\}=2/3e^{-2}+1/3e^{2}>1
.
By independence,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\{X_{n+1}\}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\{Z_{n+1}\}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}\{X_{n}\}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\{Z_{1}\}^{n+1}\cdot v

\displaystyle=(2/3e^{-2}+1/3e^{2})^{n+1}\cdot v

\displaystyle\to\infty,

as

.
Therefore,
{\mathbb{E}}\{V^{*}\}=\infty
.

Next, the contraction condition of Theorem

We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{t}
the sigma-algebra generated by

,
-\infty<j\leq t
. Furthermore, we use the notation

for the positive orthant endowed with the usual coordinate-wise partial ordering i.e.

Proposition 2
.

Assume that
\{Z_{i}\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}
is a stationary and ergodic sequence, and
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
is monotonic in its first argument:

\displaystyle F(x,z)\leq F(x^{\prime},z)\mbox{ if }x\leq x^{\prime},

(11)

For a fixed
1\leq p<\infty
, suppose that there exist a constant
0\leq\rho<1
and

such that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|F(x,Z_{1})|_{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{0}]\leq\rho|x|_{p}+K

(12)

, and

{\mathbb{E}}[|F(0,Z_{0})|_{p}]<\infty.

(13)

Then, the class
\{X_{n}(v),v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}
is strongly stable.
Furthermore, the strong law of large numbers (SLLN) is satisfied.

Notice that (
12

,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|F(x,Z_{t+1})|_{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq\rho|x|_{p}+K

(14)

almost surely for all

. Borovkov
[

]
, Foss and Tweedie

]

Proof.

This proposition extends the Foster-Lyapunov stability argument to a non-Markovian setup. We apply
the notations in the proof of Theorem

,

are order-preserving maps, and

\displaystyle X^{(-n-1)}_{0}(0)-X^{(-n)}_{0}(0)

\displaystyle=X^{(-n)}_{0}(F(0,Z_{-n}))-X^{(-n)}_{0}(0)

\displaystyle=F_{0}\circ\ldots F_{-n+1}(F(0,Z_{-n}))-F_{0}\circ\ldots F_{-n+1}(0)

is monotonically increasing and so (

As for (I), we need that

. Easily seen that

From (

{\mathbb{E}}[|X_{i+1}^{(-n)}(0)|_{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{i}]={\mathbb{E}}[|F(X_{i}^{(-n)}(0),Z_{i+1})|_{p}\mid\mathcal{F}_{i}]\leq\rho|X_{i}^{(-n)}(0)|_{p}+K,

\ i\geq-n.

Iterating this leads to

{\mathbb{E}}[|X^{(-n)}_{0}(0)|_{p}]\leq\rho^{n}{\mathbb{E}}[|X_{-n}^{(n)}(0)|_{p}]+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}K\rho^{j}\leq\frac{K}{1-\rho}<\infty,

hence

\Box

In the i.i.d. case, Bougerol and Picard

For the stationary and ergodic case, we now reprove the sufficiency part of Theorem 2.5 of Bougerol and Picard

Proposition 3
.

Assume that
\{(A_{n},B_{n})\}
is stationary and ergodic such that

{\mathbb{E}}\{\log^{+}\|A_{0}\|\}<\infty

{\mathbb{E}}\{\log^{+}|B_{0}|\}<\infty

{\mathbb{E}}\{\log||A_{0}\ldots A_{-n}||\}<0

(16)

for some
n

then the class
\{X_{n}(v),v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}
is strongly stable.

Proof.

Since
\|A_{n}A_{n-1}\dots A_{1}\|
corresponds to
\|F_{n}\circ\dots\circ F_{1}\|
, we
can verify
the conditions of Theorem

(ii)
and the integrability

\Box

Remark 5
.

Note that the argument above showed up first in Fürstenberg and Kesten

They proved that if

In the rest of this section we recall results about a similar iteration which are based on negative iterations
(though they cannot be treated by our results in this paper). Let us consider

when there are given observation sequences of random, symmetric and positive semi-definite

and random

followed by an averaging:

If

to

Györfi and Walk

studied the case of dependent

Assume that the sequence

\displaystyle\lim_{n}(X_{n}-X_{n}^{*})=0

a.s. with an asymptotic bias vector

5
The Langevin iteration

For a measurable function
H:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
the Langevin iteration is defined as follows:
set
X_{n}=X_{n}(v)
such that
X_{0}=v
, and

X_{n+1}=X_{n}-\lambda H(X_{n},Y_{n})+\sqrt{2\lambda}N_{n+1},\quad n\geq 0,

(18)

where
\{Y_{i}\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}
and
\{N_{i}\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}
are random sequences.
In the literature of stochastic approximation,
\lambda>0
is called step size, while in machine learning.
it is called learning rate.

The simplest case is where the sequences
N_{n}
and
Y_{n}
are independent,
Y_{n}
is i.i.d. and
N_{n}
is i.i.d. standard
d
-dimensional Gaussian.
This algorithm was introduced in Welling and Teh
[
47
]
and later analysed by
a large corpus of literature which we cannot review here. It is called “stochastic gradient Langevin
dynamics” and it can be used for sampling from high-dimensional, not necessarily log-concave distributions
and for finding the global minimum of high-dimensional functionals.
In this context,

Y_{n}
repesents a data sequence (obtained, for instance, by averaging a big dataset over randomly chosen
minibatches),
N_{n}
is artificially added noise to guarantee that the process does not get stuck near
local minima.

The case where the data sequence
Y_{n}
may be a dependent stationary process (but
N_{n}
is still i.i.d. standard Gaussian)
has been treated less extensively:
see Dalalyan
[
15
]
and Bakhagen et al.
[
4
]
for the convex and Chau et al.
[
14
]
for the non-convex settings.

Another stream of literature, starting from Hairer
[
28
]
, concentrated on
stochastic differential equations driven by coloured Gaussian noises.
The discrete-time case of difference equations was treated in Varvenne
[
46
]
.
This setting corresponds to the case where in (
18
),
Y_{n}

is constant and
N_{n}
is a dependent Gaussian sequence.

We know of no studies so far that allowed
both

Y_{n}
and
N_{n}
to be only stationary.
We manage to establish strong stability in this case, under reasonable assumptions.

Defining

F(x,z)=x-\lambda H(x,y)+\sqrt{2\lambda}u,\ z=(y,u)

and
Z_{i+1}:=(Y_{i},N_{i+1})
, Proposition
1
implies the following:

Corollary 1
.

Assume that the sequence
\{(Y_{i},N_{i+1})\}_{-\infty}^{\infty}
is stationary and ergodic,
and for a
\lambda_{0}>0
and for all
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}

{\mathbb{E}}|-\lambda H(0,Y_{1})+\sqrt{2\lambda}N_{2}|<\infty,

and

|x-\lambda H(x,z)-(x^{\prime}-\lambda H(x^{\prime},z))|\leq K_{z}|x-x^{\prime}|

with (
8
).
Then, for all
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}
,
the class
\{X_{n}(v),v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}
is strongly stable.

Remark 6
.

Next, we prove the convergence of the iterative scheme (
18
) assuming only that
H
satisfies

\left\langle\partial_{1}H(x,y)v,v\right\rangle\geq m(y)|v|^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad|\partial_{1}H(x,y)|\leq M(y)

(19)

with measurable
m,M:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to[0,\infty)
.
(This is a parametric form of the usual
strong convexity condition
. One can replace it by a so-called
dissipativity
condition and hence extend the analysis beyond the convex case. However, this direction requires a different technology.) We introduce
g(t)=H(tx^{\prime}+(1-t)x,y)
, and thus we can write

|F(x,y)-F(x^{\prime},y)|^{2}=|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}-2\lambda\left\langle x-x^{\prime},g(0)-g(1)\right\rangle+\lambda^{2}|g(0)-g(1)|^{2},

where

g(0)-g(1)=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{1}H(tx^{\prime}+(1-t)x,y)(x-x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}t.

Using (
19
), we estimate
|g(0)-g(1)|\leq M_{y}|x-x^{\prime}|

and

\left\langle x-x^{\prime},g(0)-g(1)\right\rangle\geq m_{y}|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}
,
and arrive at

|F(x,y)-F(x^{\prime},y)|\leq\left(1+\lambda^{2}M_{y}^{2}-2\lambda m_{y}\right)^{1/2}|x-x^{\prime}|.

In (
19
), without the loss of generality, we can assume that
\mathbb{E}(m_{Z_{1}})<\infty
.
Furthermore, requiring
\mathbb{E}(M_{y}^{2})<\infty
, we can set
\lambda
such that conditions of Proposition
1
,
i.e (
8
), are satisfied.
It is also not restrictive to assume that
\mathbb{E}(m_{Z_{1}})^{2}<\mathbb{E}(M_{Z_{1}}^{2})
.
Since
K_{Z_{1}}=\left(1+\lambda^{2}M_{Z_{1}}^{2}-2\lambda m_{Z_{1}}\right)^{1/2}
, Jensen’s inequality implies

\mathbb{E}(K_{Z_{1}})\leq\left(1+\lambda^{2}\mathbb{E}(M_{Z_{1}}^{2})-2\lambda\mathbb{E}(m_{Z_{1}})\right)^{1/2}<1

whenever
\lambda<\frac{2\mathbb{E}(m_{Z_{1}})}{\mathbb{E}(M_{Z_{1}}^{2})}
.

Remark 7
.

Strong convexity is a usual assumption in the stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics
literature, see
[
15
,
18
,
4
]
. Remark
6
above shows that our results
cover this case. It would, however, be nice to weaken this condition to dissipativity
(see
[
14
,
34
]
). It seems that such a generalization requires much more
advanced techniques, see e.g.
[
46
]
.

Remark 8
.

Note that the iteration (
17
) in Section
3
is a special case of the Langevin iteration (
18
) such that

H(X_{n},A_{n+1})=A_{n+1}X_{n}

(20)

with the difference that for (
20
) only convexity holds and not strong convexity.
In a least-squares regression setup it is important that
A_{n}
is assumed positive semi-definite only
and not necessarily positive definite.

6
Generalized multi-type Galton–Watson process

We follow the notation of Kevei and Wiandt
[
31
]
.
A
d
-type Galton–Watson branching process with immigration (GWI process)

{\bf X}_{n}=(X_{n,1},\ldots,X_{n,d}),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

is defined as

\begin{cases}{\bf X}_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{X_{n-1,1}}{\bf A}_{n,j,1}+\ldots+\sum_{j=1}^{X_{n-1,d}}{\bf A}_{n,j,d}+{\bf B}_{n},\qquad n\geq 1,\\
{\bf X}_{0}=v,\end{cases}

where
v\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,

\{{\bf A}_{n,j,i},\,{\bf B}_{n}:n,j\in\mathbb{N},\,i\in\{1,\dots,d\}\}

are random vectors with non-negative integer coordinates.
Here
X_{n,i}
is the number of
i
-type individuals in the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
generation
of a population,
{\bf A}_{n,j,i}
is the vector of the number of offsprings produced by the
j^{\mathrm{th}}

individual of type
i
belonging to the

(n-1)^{\mathrm{th}}
generation, and
{\bf B}_{n}
is the vector of the number of
immigrants.

Let
{\bf C}_{n}:=\{{\bf A}_{n,j,i}:j\in\mathbb{N},\,i\in\{1,\dots,d\}\}
.
In the standard setup the families of random variables

\{{\bf C}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
\{{\bf B}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
are independent and

({\bf C}_{n},\,{\bf B}_{n})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
is a sequence of independent vectors.
The process
\{{\bf X}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is called homogeneous, when
({\bf C}_{n},\,{\bf B}_{n})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
are identically distributed, otherwise it is inhomogeneous.
In this section we study the generalization of the homogeneous case, when
\{Z_{n}:=({\bf C}_{n},\,{\bf B}_{n}):n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is
a stationary and ergodic process. As before, we extend this stationary process to the timeline
\mathbb{Z}
.
We furthermore assume for each
i
that
{\bf A}_{1,j,i}
has, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, the same conditional law with
respect to
\mathcal{F}_{0}
.

Note that the state space of
Z_{n}
is
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
. It can easily be checked that all the
arguments of our paper apply for such state spaces, too.

Homogeneous multi-type GWI processes has been introduced and studied by
Quine
[
41
,
42
]
. In
[
41
]
necessary and sufficient condition is
given for the existence of stationary distribution in the subcritical case.
A complete answer is given by Kaplan
[
30
]
. Also Mode
[
37
]
gives
a sufficient condition for the existence of a stationary distribution, and
in a special case he shows that the limiting distribution is
a multivariate Poisson process with independent components.

Branching process models are extensively used in various parts of natural sciences,
in biology, epidemiology, physics, computer science, among other subjects.
In particular, multi-type GWI processes were used
to determine the asymptotic mean and covariance matrix of deleterious
and mutant genes in a stationary population by Gladstien and Lange
[
24
]
,
and in non-stationary population by Lange and Fan
[
32
]
.
Another rapidly developing area where multi-type GWI processes can be applied
is the theory of polling systems.
Resing
[
43
]
pointed out that a large variety of polling models can be
described as a multi-type GWI process.
Resing
[
43
]
, van der Mei
[
36
]
, Boon
[
6
]
, Boon et al.
[
7
]

and Altman, Fiems
[
1
]
investigated several communication protocols applied in
info-communication networks with differentiated services.
There are different quality of services,
for example, some of them are delay sensitive
(telephone, on-line video, etc.), while others tolerate some delay (e-mail, internet,
downloading files, etc.). Thus, the services are grouped into service classes such
that each class has an own transmission protocol like priority queueing. In the papers
mentioned above the
d
-type Galton–Watson process has been used, where the process was
defined either by the sizes of the active user populations of the
d
service classes,
or by the length of the
d
priority queues.
For the general theory and applications of multi-type Galton–Watson processes
we refer to Mode
[
37
]
and Haccou et al.
[
27
]
.

Define the random row vectors
m_{i}:={\mathbb{E}}[\mathbf{A}_{1,1;i}|\mathcal{F}_{0}]
,

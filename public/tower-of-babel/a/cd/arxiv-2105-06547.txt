Vectorial variational problems in L ∞ L^{\infty} constrained by the Navier-Stokes equations Thanks: E.C. has been financially supported through the UK EPSRC scholarship GS19-055 Thanks: B.M. has been partially financially supported through the Croatian Science Foundation project IP-2019-04-1140
Ed Clark, Nikos Katzourakis, Boris Muha

Abstract
. We study a minimisation problem in L p L^{p} and L ∞ L^{\infty} for certain cost functionals, where the class of admissible mappings is constrained by the Navier-Stokes equations. Problems of this type are motivated by variational data assimilation for atmospheric flows arising in weather forecasting. Herein we establish the existence of PDE-constrained minimisers for all p p , and also that L p L^{p} minimisers converge to L ∞ L^{\infty} minimisers as p → ∞ p\to\infty . We further show that L p L^{p} minimisers solve an Euler-Lagrange system. Finally, all special L ∞ L^{\infty} minimisers constructed via approximation by L p L^{p} minimisers are shown to solve a divergence PDE system involving measure coefficients, which is a divergence-form counterpart of the corresponding non-divergence Aronsson-Euler system.

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded set and let also
n\geq 2
and
\nu,T>0
. Consider the Navier-Stokes equations

(1.1)

\left\{\ \begin{array}[]{lll}&\ \ \partial_{t}u\,-\nu\Delta u\,+(u\cdot\mathrm{D})u\,+\mathrm{D}\mathrm{p}\,-f=\,y,&\ \ \text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\ \ \mathrm{div}\,u\,=0,&\ \ \text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\ \ u(\cdot,0)=u_{0},&\ \ \text{ on }\Omega,\\
&\ \ u=\,0,&\ \ \text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\end{array}\right.

and for brevity let us henceforth symbolise
\nabla u:=(\mathrm{D}u,\partial_{t}u)
and
\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T)
, where
\mathrm{D}u=(\partial_{x_{1}}u,...,\partial_{x_{n}}u)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
symbolises the spatial gradient. The system of PDEs (
1.1
) describes the velocity
u:\Omega_{T}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the pressure
\mathrm{p}:\Omega_{T}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
of a flow, for some given initial data
u_{0}:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
with source
f:\Omega_{T}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here the map
y:\Omega_{T}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a parameter and should be understood as a (deterministic) noise or error. Let also
N\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose we are given a mapping

(1.2)

\mathrm{Q}\ :\ \ \Omega_{T}\times\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{(n+1)\times n}\times\mathbb{R}\big)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

A problem of interest in the geosciences, in particular in data assimilation for atmospheric flows in relation to weather forecasting (see e.g.
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
), can be formulated as follows: find solutions
(u,\mathrm{p})
to (
1.1
) such that, in an appropriate sense,

(1.3)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}y\,\approx\,0,&\\
\mathrm{Q}(\cdot,\cdot,u,\nabla u,\mathrm{p})-q\,\approx\,0,&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \end{split}\right.

where
q:\Omega_{T}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a vector of given measurable “data” arising from some specific measurements, taken through the “observation operator”
\mathrm{Q}
of (
1.2
). In (
1.1
) and (
1.3
),
y
represents an error in the measurements which forces the Navier-Stokes equations to be satisfied only approximately for solenoidal (divergence-free) vector fields. Namely, we are looking for solutions to (
1.1
) such that simultaneously the error
y
vanishes, and also the measurements
q
match the prediction of the solution
(u,\mathrm{p})
through the observation operator (
1.2
). In application,
\mathrm{Q}
is typically some component (e.g. linear projection or nonlinear submersion) of the atmospheric flow that we can observe. Unfortunately, the data fitting problem (
1.3
) is severely ill-posed; an exact solution may well not exist, and even if it does, it may not be unique.

In this paper, inspired by the methodology of data assimilation, especially variational data assimilation in continuous time (for relevant works we refer e.g. to
[
13
,
19
,
26
,
29
,
30
,
44
,
46
,
52
]
), we seek to minimise the misfit functional

(u,\mathrm{p},y)\,\mapsto\,(1-\lambda)\big\|\mathrm{Q}(\cdot,\cdot,u,\nabla u,\mathrm{p})-q\big\|\,+\,\lambda\big\|y\big\|

over all admissible triplets
(u,\mathrm{p},y)
which satisfy (
1.1
), for a fixed weight
\lambda\in(0,1)
. The role of this weight is to obtain essentially a Pareto family of extremals, one for each value
\lambda
, even though in this paper we do not pursue further this viewpoint of vector-valued minimisation (the interested reader may e.g. consult
[
21
]
). The standard approach to data assimilation is to use Hilbert space methods (or least squares in the discrete case), hence minimising in
L^{2}
. The novelty of our approach, which is also justified from the viewpoint of applications, is to consider instead
minimisation in
L^{\infty}
, namely by interpreting the norms above as
L^{\infty}
ones (or maxima in the discrete case). There is a significant advantage of considering a min-max problem instead of minimising the squared averages: the misfit becomes uniformly small throughout the space-time domain
\Omega_{T}
and not just on average, hence large “spikes” of deviations from zero are at the outset excluded.

When one passes from a variational problem for an integral norm to one for the supremum norm, even though this is justified from the viewpoint of desired outputs, it poses some serious challenges. The
L^{\infty}
norm is neither differentiable nor strictly convex, and the space
L^{\infty}
is neither reflexive nor separable. Additionally, with respect to the domain argument, the
L^{\infty}
norm is not additive but only sub-additive. Further, one would also need estimates for (
1.1
) in appropriate subspaces of
L^{\infty}
for weakly differentiable functions, which, to the best of our knowledge, do not exist even for linear strongly elliptic systems (see e.g.
[
33
]
). Even then, if one somehow solves the
L^{\infty}
minimisation problem (by using, for instance, the direct method of the Calculus of Variations as in
[
24
]
, under the appropriate quasiconvexity assumptions for
|\mathrm{Q}-q|+|y|
as in
[
12
]
), the analogue of the Euler-Lagrange equations for the
L^{\infty}
problem cannot be derived directly by perturbation/sensitivity methods due to the lack of smoothness of the
L^{\infty}
norm.

In this paper, to overcome the difficulties described above, we follow the methodology of the relatively new field of Calculus of Variations in
L^{\infty}
(see e.g.
[
22
,
36
]
for a general introduction to the scalar-valued theory), and in particular the ideas from
[
38
,
39
,
40
,
41
]
involving higher order and vectorial problems, as well as problems involving PDE-constraints, which have only recently started being investigated. To this end, we follow the approach of solving the desired
L^{\infty}
variational problem by solving respective approximating
L^{p}
variational problems for all
p
, and obtain appropriate compactness estimates which allow to pass to the limit as
p\to\infty
. The case of finite
p>2
studied herein is also of independent interest, especially for numerical discretisation schemes in
L^{\infty}
(see e.g.
[
42
,
43
]
), but in this paper we treat it mostly as an approximation device to solve efficiently the
L^{\infty}
problem. The idea of this approach is based on the observation that, for a fixed essentially bounded function on a domain of finite measure, the
L^{p}
norm tends to the
L^{\infty}
norm of the function as
p\to\infty
.

In order to state our hypotheses and main results, let us set

(1.4)

\mathrm{K}\big(x,t,\eta,A,a,r\big)\,:=\,\mathrm{Q}\big(x,t,\eta,A,a,r\big)\,-\,q(x,t)

(note that in (
1.4
)
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}
is treated as two arguments and the two arguments
(A,a)
are for
\nabla u=(\mathrm{D}u,\partial_{t}u)
, which we conveniently display abbreviated as one) and, by considering the (strong) divergence operator
\mathrm{div}:W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\longrightarrow L^{1}(\Omega)
, we henceforth assume that

(1.5)

\left\{\ \ \begin{array}[]{ll}(a)&\Omega\text{ is bounded and has $C^{2}$ boundary }\partial\Omega,\\
(b)&u_{0}\in\big(W^{2,{\infty}}\cap W^{1,\infty}_{0}\big)({\Omega};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap\mathrm{ker}(\mathrm{div}),\\
(c)&f\in L^{\infty}({\Omega}_{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\ \ \ \&\ \ \ q\in L^{\infty}({\Omega}_{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\\
(d)&\mathrm{K}(x,t,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)\text{ is $C^{1}$ for almost every }(x,t),\\
(e)&\mathrm{K}(\cdot,\cdot,\eta,A,a,r)\text{ is $L^{\infty}$ for all }(\eta,A,a,r),\\
(f)&|\mathrm{K}(x,t,\eta,A,\cdot,\cdot)|^{2}\text{ is convex for all }(x,t,\eta,A).\end{array}\right.

Then, for any
p\in(1,\infty)
, we define the
L^{p}
misfit
\mathrm{E}_{p}:\mathfrak{X}^{p}({\Omega}_{T})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
by setting

(1.6)

{\mathrm{E}}_{p}\big(u,\mathrm{p},y\big)\,:=\,(1-\lambda){\big\|\mathrm{K}(\cdot,u,\nabla u,\mathrm{p})\big\|}_{\dot{L}^{p}({\Omega}_{T})}+\,\lambda\|y\|_{\dot{L}^{p}({\Omega}_{T})}.

We note that in (
1.6
) and subsequently, the dotted
\dot{L}^{p}
quantities are regularisations of the respective norms at the origin, obtained by regularising the Euclidean norm in the respective target space:

(1.7)

\|h\|_{\dot{L}^{p}({\Omega}_{T})}:=\,\big\||h|_{(p)}\big\|_{L^{p}({\Omega}_{T})},\ \ \ \ |\cdot|_{(p)}\,:=\,\sqrt{|\cdot|^{2}+p^{-2}}.

Further, since we will only be dealing with finite measures, we will always be using the normalised
L^{p}
norms in which we replace the integral over the domain with the respective average, for example for
L^{p}(\Omega_{T})
with the
(n+1)
-Lebesgue measure, the norm will be

\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}:=\left(\,-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{T}}|h|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\!\right)^{\!\!1/p}.

The admissible minimisation class
\mathfrak{X}^{p}({\Omega}_{T})
over which
\mathrm{E}_{p}
is considered, is defined as follows:

(1.8)

\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega_{T})\,:=\,\Big\{(u,\mathrm{p},y)\in\mathcal{W}^{p}(\Omega_{T})\ :\ (u,\mathrm{p},y)\text{ satisfies weakly }\eqref{1.1}\Big\},

where

(1.9)

\mathcal{W}^{p}(\Omega_{T})\,:=\,W^{2,1;p}_{\mathrm{L},\sigma}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times W^{1,0;p}_{\sharp}(\Omega_{T})\times L^{p}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The rather complicated functional spaces appearing in (
1.9
) are defined as follows. The space
W^{2,1;p}_{\mathrm{L},\sigma}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of solenoidal maps which are
W^{2,p}
in space and
W^{1,p}
in time, and also laterally vanishing on
\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
:

(1.10)

\left\{\begin{split}W^{2,1;p}_{\mathrm{L},\sigma}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})\,&:=\,L^{p}\big((0,T);W^{2,p}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\big)\bigcap W^{1,p}\big((0,T);L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\big),\\
W^{2,p}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\,&:=\,\big(W^{2,p}\cap W^{1,p}_{0}\big)(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathrm{ker}(\mathrm{div}).\end{split}\right.\!\!

The space
W^{1,0;p}_{\sharp}(\Omega_{T})
consists of scalar-valued functions which are
W^{1,p}
in space with zero average, and
L^{p}
in time:

(1.11)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}W^{1,0;p}_{\sharp}(\Omega_{T})\,&:=\,L^{p}\big((0,T);W^{1,p}_{\sharp}(\Omega)\big),\\
W^{1,p}_{\sharp}(\Omega)\,&:=\,\left\{g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\ :\ \int_{\Omega}g\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}=0\right\}.\end{split}\right.

The associated norms in these spaces are the expected ones, namely

(1.12)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}\|v\|_{W^{2,1;p}_{\mathrm{L},\sigma}(\Omega_{T})}\,&:=\,\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\,+\,\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\,+\,\|\mathrm{D}^{2}v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})},\\
\|g\|_{W^{1,0;p}_{\sharp}(\Omega_{T})}\,&:=\,\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\,+\,\|\mathrm{D}g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}.\end{split}\right.

Note also that the divergence-free condition for
u
in (
1.1
) has now been incorporated in the functional space
W^{2,1;p}_{\mathrm{L},\sigma}(\Omega_{T})
. Finally, the
L^{\infty}
misfit
\mathrm{E}_{\infty}:\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}({\Omega}_{T})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by setting

(1.13)

\mathrm{E}_{\infty}\big(u,\mathrm{p},y\big)\,:=\,(1-\lambda){\big\|\mathrm{K}(\cdot,\cdot,u,\nabla u,\mathrm{p})\big\|}_{L^{\infty}({\Omega}_{T})}+\,\lambda\|y\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega}_{T})},

where the admissible class
\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
is given by

(1.14)

\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})\,:=\,\bigcap_{1<p<\infty}\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega_{T}).

Note that the natural topology of
\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
is not induced by a complete norm in a Banach space, but instead its topology is defined as the locally convex topology induced from the ambient Frechét space
\smash{\bigcap_{1<p<\infty}\mathcal{W}^{p}(\Omega_{T})}
. Notwithstanding, no difficulties will arise from this fact, which is a necessity that stems from the lack of
W^{2,\infty}
-
W^{1,\infty}
estimates for (
1.1
). In particular,
\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
is
not
obtained by considering the strictly smaller Cartesian product space

\mathcal{W}^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})\,=\,W^{2,1;\infty}_{\mathrm{L},\sigma}(\Omega_{T})\times\smash{W^{1,0;\infty}_{\sharp}(\Omega_{T})}\times L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

However, we will assume that the solution
(u,\mathrm{p})
to (
1.1
) is strong and satisfies
W^{2,p}
-
W^{1,p}
estimates for any finite
p
. This is deducible under assumption (
1.5
) in the case of
n=2
(see e.g.
[
32
,
53
]
), and also under smallness conditions on
u_{0}
in any dimension

(see e.g.
[
2
,
34
,
54
]
). Hence, our additional hypothesis is

(1.15)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}&\text{Either}\\
&\hskip 130.0ptn=2,\phantom{{}_{\big]}}\\
&\text{or, $n\geq 3$ but for any $p\in(1,\infty)$, exists $C>0$ depending only on $p$ and}\\
&\text{on $\partial\Omega$, $T$, $\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}$, such that}\\
&\ \ \|u\|_{W^{2,1;p}_{\mathrm{L},\sigma}(\Omega_{T})}+\,\|\mathrm{p}\|_{W^{1,0;p}_{\sharp}(\Omega_{T})}\,\leq\,C\big(\|u_{0}\|_{W^{2-\frac{2}{p},p}(\Omega)}+\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\big),\phantom{\bigg]}\\
&\text{when $(u,\mathrm{p})$ solves weakly \eqref{1.1} with $y\equiv 0$.}\end{split}\right.\!\!\!

Assumption (
1.15
), albeit restrictive, is compatible with situations of interest in weather forecasting (see e.g.
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
). Our first main result concerns the existence of
\mathrm{E}_{p}
-minimisers in
\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
, the existence of
\mathrm{E}_{\infty}
-minimisers in
\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})

To state our second main result, we first need to introduce some notation. For any
M\in\mathbb{N}
and

\mathfrak{M}_{p}\ :\ \ L^{p}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{M})\longrightarrow L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{M}),

where

\mathfrak{M}_{p}(V)\,:=\,\frac{|V|_{(p)}^{p-2}\,V}{\big(\|V\|_{\dot{L}^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\big)^{p-1}}.

Here

and therefore

\smash{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{M})}

(1.20)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}\mathrm{K}[u,\mathrm{p}]\,:=&\,\mathrm{K}\big(\cdot,\cdot,u,\nabla u,\mathrm{p}\big),\\
\mathrm{K}_{\eta}[u,\mathrm{p}]\,:=&\,\mathrm{K}_{\eta}\big(\cdot,\cdot,u,\nabla u,\mathrm{p}\big),\\
\mathrm{K}_{(A,a)}[u,\mathrm{p}]\,:=&\,\mathrm{K}_{(A,a)}\big(\cdot,\cdot,u,\nabla u,\mathrm{p}\big),\\
\mathrm{K}_{r}[u,\mathrm{p}]\,:=&\,\mathrm{K}_{r}\big(\cdot,\cdot,u,\nabla u,\mathrm{p}\big),\end{split}\right.

for
\mathrm{K}
and its partial derivatives
\mathrm{K}_{\eta},\mathrm{K}_{(A,a)},\mathrm{K}_{r}
with respect to the arguments for

and

respectively.

Theorem 2

(Variational Equations in
L^{p}
)
.

Suppose that (
1.5
) and (

) hold true. Then, for any

\Psi_{p}\,\in\,\Big(W^{2-\frac{2}{p},p}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\Big)^{*}

), we follow a slightly different approach which does not lead an Aronsson-Euler type system; instead, it leads to a
divergence structure
PDE system. However, there is a toll to be paid, as the divergence PDEs arising as necessary conditions involve measures as auxiliary parameters whose determination becomes part of the problem. Notwithstanding, the central point of this idea is to use a scaling in the Euler-Lagrange equations before letting

, which is different from the scaling used to (formally) derive the Aronsson-Euler equations as

.

In the light of the above comments, our final main result concerns the satisfaction of necessary PDE conditions for the PDE-constrained minimisers in
L^{\infty}
constructed in Theorem

)

Suppose that (

2.
Preliminaries and the main estimates

We begin by recording for later use the following modified Hölder inequality for the dotted
\dot{L}^{p}
regularised “norms” defined in (
1.7
): for any
1\leq q\leq p<\infty
and
h\in L^{p}(\Omega_{T})
, we have the inequality

\|h\|_{\dot{L}^{q}(\Omega_{T})}\,\leq\,\|h\|_{\dot{L}^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\,+\,\sqrt{q^{-2}-p^{-2}},

which can be very easily confirmed by a direct computation. Next, we continue with a brief discussion regarding the weak interpretation of the equations (
1.27
)-(
1.28
). As already noted in the introduction, since
(\Sigma_{\infty},\sigma_{\infty})
are not in general neither product measures nor absolutely continuous with respect to the
(n+1)
-Lebesgue measure on
\overline{\Omega_{T}}=\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T]
, one needs to use the disintegration “slicing” theorem for Young measures in order to express them appropriately, as follows. Since
\sigma_{\infty}
is a vector measure in
\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega_{T}};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by the Radon-Nikodym theorem, we may decompose

\sigma_{\infty}\,=\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\infty}}{\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|}\,\|\sigma_{\infty}\|,

where
\|\sigma_{\infty}\|\in\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega_{T}})
is the scalar total variation measure and
{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\infty}}/{\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|}
is the vector-valued Radon-Nikodym derivative of
\sigma_{\infty}
with respect to
\|\sigma_{\infty}\|
. Fix now any
h\in L^{1}(\overline{\Omega_{T}},\|\sigma_{\infty}\|)
. By the disintegration “slicing” theorem for Young measure (see se.g.
[
27
, Theorem 3.2, p. 179]
), we have the representation formula

\int_{\overline{\Omega_{T}}}h\,\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|\,=\,\int_{[0,T]}\!\bigg(\int_{\overline{\Omega}}h(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|_{t}(x)\bigg)\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|^{o}(t)

where the measure
\|\sigma_{\infty}\|^{o}\in\mathcal{M}([0,T])
and the family of measures
(\|\sigma_{\infty}\|_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega})
are defined as follows:

\|\sigma_{\infty}\|^{o}\,:=\,\|\sigma_{\infty}\|\big(\overline{\Omega}\times\cdot\,\big),\ \ \ \ \ \|\sigma_{\infty}\|_{t}(A)\,:=\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|\big(A\times\cdot\,\big)}{\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|\big(\overline{\Omega}\times\cdot\,\big)}(t),\text{ for }A\subseteq\overline{\Omega}\text{ Borel}.

Namely,
\|\sigma_{\infty}\|^{o}
is one of the marginals of
\sigma_{\infty}
and for
\|\sigma_{\infty}\|^{o}
-a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, the measure
\|\sigma_{\infty}\|_{t}
evaluated at
A
is defined as the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the measure
\|\sigma_{\infty}\|\big(A\times\cdot\,\big)
with respect to
\|\sigma_{\infty}\|\big(\overline{\Omega}\times\cdot\,\big)
at the point
t\in[0,T]
. Then, in view of (
1.28
), by choosing
\mathrm{p}
in the form
\mathrm{p}(x,t)=\pi(x)\tau(t)
, we have

\begin{split}0\,&=\,\int_{\overline{\Omega_{T}}}\mathrm{D}\mathrm{p}\cdot\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\infty}\\
&=\,\int_{\overline{\Omega_{T}}}\bigg(\mathrm{D}\mathrm{p}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\infty}}{\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|\\
&=\,\int_{[0,T]}\!\bigg(\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\bigg(\mathrm{D}\mathrm{p}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\infty}}{\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|}\bigg)(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|_{t}(x)\bigg)\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|^{o}(t)\\
&=\,\int_{[0,T]}\!\bigg(\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\bigg(\mathrm{D}\pi(x)\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\infty}}{\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|}(x,t)\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|_{t}(x)\bigg)\tau(t)\,\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|^{o}(t).\end{split}

The arbitrariness of
\tau
implies that for
\|\sigma_{\infty}\|^{o}
-a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\bigg(\mathrm{D}\pi(x)\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\infty}}{\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|}(x,t)\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|_{t}(x)\,=\,0.

When restricting our attention to those test function for which
\pi|_{\partial\Omega}\equiv 0
, we obtain the next weak interpretation of (
1.28
):

\mathrm{div}\bigg(\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\infty}}{\mathrm{d}\|\sigma_{\infty}\|}(\cdot,t)\,\|\sigma_{\infty}\|_{t}\bigg)\,=\,0,\ \ \ \text{ in }\Omega,

for
\|\sigma_{\infty}\|^{o}
-a.e.
t\in[0,T]
. Similar considerations apply also to equation (
1.27
), but the arguments are considerably more complicated.

Next we prove a general compact embedding lemma by means of interpolation theory.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose that
p>n+2
. Then, there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that the space

W^{2,1;p}(\Omega_{T})\,:=\,L^{p}\big((0,T);W^{2,p}(\Omega)\big)\bigcap W^{1,p}\big((0,T);L^{p}(\Omega)\big)

is compactly embedded in the space
C^{0,\alpha}\big([0,T];C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\big)
.

Proof of Lemma

4
. Let us use the abbreviated space notation

\mathrm{X}_{1}\,:=\,W^{2,p}(\Omega),\ \ \mathrm{X}_{0}\,:=\,L^{p}(\Omega)

and select
\theta
such that

\frac{p+n}{2p}\,<\,\theta\,<\,\frac{p-1}{p},

which is possible since

\frac{p-1}{p}-\frac{p+n}{2p}\,=\,\frac{p-(n+2)}{2p}\,>\,0.

Since
1-\theta>1/p
, direct application of the interpolation result in
[
1
, Theorem 5.2]
for the exponents
s_{0}:=1
,
s_{1}:=0
and
p_{0}\equiv p_{1}:=p
yields that space
W^{2,1;p}(\Omega_{T})
is compactly embedded in the space
C^{0,\alpha}\big([0,T];\mathrm{X}\big)
, where
0<\alpha<1-\theta-1/p
and
\mathrm{X}=(\mathrm{X}_{0},\mathrm{X}_{1})_{\theta,p}
symbolises the real interpolation between the Banach spaces
\mathrm{X}_{0}
and
\mathrm{X}_{1}
. Now it remains to identify the space
\mathrm{X}
. By using standard results in interpolation theory (see e.g.
[
55
, Theorem 4.3.1.1 and formula (2.4.2/9)]
or
[
56
]
for Lipschitz domains) we get:

\big(L^{p}(\Omega),W^{2,p}(\Omega)\big)_{\theta,p}\,=\,\mathrm{B}^{2\theta}_{pp}(\Omega)\,=\,W^{2\theta,p}(\Omega).

Since
2\theta>1+n/p
, by the standard Sobolev embedding theorem for fractional spaces (e.g.
[
25
, Theorem 8.2]
, we have that
W^{2\theta,p}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in the space
C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, where
0<\alpha\leq 2\theta-1-n/p
. The conclusion ensues.
∎

Remark 5
.

Let us now record for later use the following simple inclusion of space (which is in fact a continuous embedding):

C^{0,\alpha}\big([0,T];C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\big)\,\subseteq\,C^{0,\alpha}\big(\overline{\Omega_{T}}\big).

Indeed, for any
h\in C^{0,\alpha}\big([0,T];C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\big)
, we compute

\begin{split}\big|h(t_{1},x_{1})-h(t_{2},x_{2})\big|\,&\leq\,|h(t_{1},x_{1})-h(t_{2},x_{1})|+|h(t_{2},x_{1})-h(t_{2},x_{2})|\\
&\leq\,\|h(t_{1},\cdot)-h(t_{2},\cdot)\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\,+\,\|h(t_{2},\cdot)\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha}\\
&\leq\,\big(|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\alpha}+|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha}\big)\|h\|_{C^{0,\alpha}([0,T];C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}))}\end{split}

which establishes the claim.

Lemma 6
.

Suppose that assumptions (
1.5
) and (
1.15
) are satisfied. We have that

\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})\,\neq\,\emptyset

(and consequently we have
\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega_{T})\neq\emptyset

). Further, if

Further, if

We note that the constants above also depend on

, but as all these are fixed throughout this paper, we suppress denoting the explicit dependence on them.

Proof of Lemma

6
. By assumptions (
1.5
)(b)-(
1.5
)(c), we have that the triplet

, where

satisfies that

, and in fact lies also in the smaller space

Next, if

with

whilst by assumptions (

for some

3.
Minimisers of

In this section we establish Theorem

Proof of Theorem

1
. Fix

. By Lemma
4
,
\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega_{T})\neq\emptyset
, therefore
0\leq\inf_{\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\mathrm{E}_{p}<\infty

\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
is sequentially weakly compact. Note now that

is trivially convex, and by the identity

\big\|\mathrm{K}\big(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u,\partial_{t}u,\mathrm{p}\big)\big\|^{p}_{\dot{L}^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\,=\,\int_{\Omega_{T}}\Big(\big|\mathrm{K}\big(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u,\partial_{t}u,\mathrm{p}\big)|^{2}+p^{-2}\Big)^{\!\frac{p}{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n+1},

assumption (
1.5
)(f) yields that

(\partial_{t}u,\mathrm{p})\,\mapsto\,\big\|\mathrm{K}\big(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u,\partial_{t}u,\mathrm{p}\big)\big\|^{p}_{\dot{L}^{p}(\Omega_{T})}

is also convex. Again by Lemma
6
and by standard results in the Calculus of Variations (see e.g.
[
24
]
), it follows that
\mathrm{E}_{p}
is weakly lower semicontinuous in
\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
as the convex combination of the

-th roots of two weakly lower semicontinuous functionals. Hence,
\mathrm{E}_{p}
attains its infimum at some

(u_{p},\mathrm{p}_{p},y_{p})_{p>n+2}

(u,\mathrm{p},y)\in\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
and any
q\leq p
, minimality and the Hölder inequality for the dotted
\dot{L}^{p}
functionals yield

\mathrm{E}_{p}(u_{p},\mathrm{p}_{p},y_{p})\,\leq\,\mathrm{E}_{p}(u,\mathrm{p},y)\,\leq\,\mathrm{E}_{\infty}(u,\mathrm{p},y)\,+\,p^{-1}.

By choosing

6
and a standard diagonal argument, we have that the family of minimisers is weakly precompact in

4
and Remark
5
,

is compactly embedded in
\smash{C^{0,\alpha}\big([0,T];C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{n})\big)}
. Hence, for any sequence of indices

, there exists
(u_{\infty},\mathrm{p}_{\infty},y_{\infty})\in\cap_{q\in(n+2,\infty)}\mathcal{W}^{q}(\Omega_{T})
and a subsequence denoted again as

such that (
1.17
) holds true. Additionally, due to these modes of convergence, it follows that

Since as we have already shown,
\mathrm{E}_{q}
is weakly lower semicontinuous in

, by letting

for any

. The above inequality establishes on the one hand that

minimises
\mathrm{E}_{\infty}
over

, and on the other hand by choosing

4.

In this section we establish the proof of Theorem

\mathrm{E}_{p}\ :\ \ \mathcal{W}^{p}(\Omega_{T})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}.

Next, we introduce a mapping on

Proof of Theorem

2
. By assumption (

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{E}_{p}\ &:\ \ \mathcal{W}^{p}(\Omega_{T})\longrightarrow\big(\mathcal{W}^{p}(\Omega_{T})\big)^{*},\\
(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{E}_{p})_{(\bar{u},\bar{\mathrm{p}},\bar{y})}(u,\mathrm{p},y)\,&=\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\mathrm{E}_{p}\Big(\bar{u}+\varepsilon u,\bar{\mathrm{p}}+\varepsilon\mathrm{p},\bar{y}+\varepsilon y\Big)\end{split}

can be easily computed and is given by the formula

\begin{split}(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{E}_{p})_{(\bar{u},\bar{\mathrm{p}},\bar{y})}(u,\mathrm{p},y)\,=\,p(1-\lambda)\,&{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{T}}}\!\Big(\mathrm{K}_{\eta}[\bar{u},\bar{\mathrm{p}}]\cdot u\,+\,\mathrm{K}_{(A,a)}[\bar{u},\bar{\mathrm{p}}]:\nabla u+\,\mathrm{K}_{r}[\bar{u},\bar{\mathrm{p}}]\,\mathrm{p}\Big)\cdot\\
&\cdot\mathfrak{M}_{p}\big(\mathrm{K}[\bar{u},\bar{\mathrm{p}}]\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\,+\,p\lambda\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{T}}}\mathfrak{M}_{p}(\bar{y})\cdot y\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n+1},\end{split}

where the operator
\mathfrak{M}_{p}:L^{p}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{M})\longrightarrow L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{M})
(for
M\in\{N,n\}
) is given by (
1.19
) and we have used the notation introduced in (
1.20
). Next, we note that the mapping
\mathrm{G}
which incorporates the PDE constraint is also Fréchet differentiable and it can be easily confirmed that its derivative

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{G}\ &:\ \ \mathcal{W}^{p}(\Omega_{T})\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}\bigg(\mathcal{W}^{p}(\Omega_{T}),L^{p}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times W^{2-\frac{2}{p},p}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\bigg),\\
&(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{G})_{(\bar{u},\bar{\mathrm{p}},\bar{y})}(u,\mathrm{p},y)\,=\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\mathrm{G}\Big(\bar{u}+\varepsilon u,\bar{\mathrm{p}}+\varepsilon\mathrm{p},\bar{y}+\varepsilon y\Big)\end{split}

is given by the formula

\begin{split}\big(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{G}\big)_{(\bar{u},\bar{\mathrm{p}},\bar{y})}(u,\mathrm{p},y)\,=\,\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}u-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\mathrm{D})\bar{u}+(\bar{u}\cdot\mathrm{D})u+\mathrm{D}\mathrm{p}-y\\
u(\cdot,0)\end{array}\right].\end{split}

We now claim that the differential

(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{G})_{(\bar{u},\bar{\mathrm{p}},\bar{y})}\ :\ \ \mathcal{W}^{p}(\Omega_{T})\longrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times W^{2-\frac{2}{p},p}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})

is a surjective map, for any
(\bar{u},\bar{\mathrm{p}},\bar{y})\in\mathcal{W}^{p}(\Omega)
. This is equivalent to the statement that for any
p>n+2
, the linearised Navier-Stokes problem

\left\{\ \ \begin{array}[]{rr}\partial_{t}u-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\mathrm{D})\bar{u}+(\bar{u}\cdot\mathrm{D})u+\mathrm{D}\mathrm{p}\,=\,F,&\text{ in }\Omega_{T},\\
\mathrm{div}\,u\,=\,0,&\text{ in }\Omega_{T},\\
u(\cdot,0)\,=\,v,&\text{ on }\Omega,\\
u\,=\,0,&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\end{array}\right.

has a solution
(u,\mathrm{p})\in W^{2,1;p}_{\mathrm{L},\sigma}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times W^{1,0;p}_{\sharp}(\Omega_{T})
, for any
\bar{u}\in W^{2,1;p}_{\mathrm{L},\sigma}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and any data

(F,v)\,\in\,L^{p}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times W^{2-\frac{2}{p},p}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

This is indeed the case, and it is a consequence of a classical result of Solonnikov
[
53
, Th. 4.2]
for
n=3
and of Giga-Sohr
[
35
, Th. 2.8]
for
n>3
, as a perturbation of the Stokes problem. As a consequence, the assumptions of the generalised Kuhn-Tucker theorem hold true (see e.g. Zeidler
[
57
, Cor. 48.10 & Th. 48B]
). Hence, there exists a Lagrange multiplier

\begin{split}\Lambda_{p}\ \in\ &\Big(L^{p}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times W^{2-\frac{2}{p},p}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})\Big)^{*}\end{split}

such that

\begin{split}\big(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{E}_{p}\big)_{(u_{p},\mathrm{p}_{p},y_{p})}(u,\mathrm{p},y)\,=\,\Big\langle\big(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{G}\big)_{(u_{p},\mathrm{p}_{p},y_{p})}(u,\mathrm{p},y),\,\Lambda_{p}\Big\rangle,\\
\end{split}

for any
(u,{\mathrm{p}},y)\in\mathcal{W}^{p}(\Omega)
. By standard duality arguments, the Riesz representation theorem and by taking into account the form of the differentials
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{E}_{p}
and
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{G}
, we may identify
\Lambda_{p}
with a pair of Lagrange multipliers

(\phi_{p},\Psi_{p})\,\in\,L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\Big(W^{2-\frac{2}{p},p}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})\Big)^{*}

such that, the constrained minimiser
\big(u_{p},p_{p},y_{p}\big)\in\mathfrak{X}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
satisfies the equation

\begin{split}&(1-\lambda)\int_{\Omega_{T}}\!\Big(\mathrm{K}_{\eta}[u_{p},{\mathrm{p}}_{p}]\cdot u\,+\,\mathrm{K}_{(A,a)}[u_{p},{\mathrm{p}}_{p}]:\nabla u+\,\mathrm{K}_{r}[u_{p},{\mathrm{p}}_{p}]\,\mathrm{p}\Big)\cdot\\
&\hskip 50.0pt\cdot\mathfrak{M}_{p}\big(\mathrm{K}[u_{p},{\mathrm{p}}_{p}]\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\ +\ \lambda\int_{\Omega_{T}}\mathfrak{M}_{p}(y_{p})\cdot y\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\Big(\partial_{t}u-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\mathrm{D})u_{p}+(u_{p}\cdot\mathrm{D})u+\mathrm{D}\mathrm{p}-y\Big)\cdot\phi_{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\,+\,\langle\Psi_{p},u(\cdot,0)\rangle,\end{split}

for any
(u,\mathrm{p},y)\in\mathcal{W}^{p}(\Omega_{T})
. We note that here we have tacitly rescaled
(\phi_{p},\Psi_{p})
by multiplying them with the factor
p(\mathcal{L}^{n+1}(\Omega_{T}))^{-1}
, in order to remove the averages arising from
\mathrm{\mathrm{E}}_{p}
on the left hand side and to be able to obtain non-trivial limits as
p\to\infty
of the multipliers themselves later on. By using linear independence, the above equation actually decouples to the triplet of relations

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}&(1-\lambda)\int_{\Omega_{T}}\!\Big(\mathrm{K}_{\eta}[u_{p},{\mathrm{p}}_{p}]\cdot u\,+\,\mathrm{K}_{(A,a)}[u_{p},{\mathrm{p}}_{p}]:\nabla u\Big)\cdot\mathfrak{M}_{p}\big(\mathrm{K}[u_{p},{\mathrm{p}}_{p}]\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\ \\
&=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\Big(\partial_{t}u-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\mathrm{D})u_{p}+(u_{p}\cdot\mathrm{D})u\Big)\cdot\phi_{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\,+\,\langle\Psi_{p},u(\cdot,0)\rangle,\end{split}\right.

\begin{split}(1-\lambda)\int_{\Omega_{T}}\!\big(\mathrm{K}_{r}[u_{p},{\mathrm{p}}_{p}]\,\mathrm{p}\big)\cdot\mathfrak{M}_{p}\big(\mathrm{K}[u_{p},{\mathrm{p}}_{p}]\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\,=\,\int_{\Omega_{T}}\mathrm{D}\mathrm{p}\cdot\phi_{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n+1},\end{split}

\begin{split}\lambda\int_{\Omega_{T}}\mathfrak{M}_{p}(y_{p})\cdot y\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\,=\,-\int_{\Omega_{T}}y\cdot\phi_{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n+1}.\end{split}

The arbitrariness of
y\in L^{p}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the third equation readily yields that the multiplier
\phi_{p}
equals

\phi_{p}\,=\,-\lambda\,\mathfrak{M}_{p}(y_{p}).

By substituting this into the first two equations, we see that the theorem has been established. ∎

The equations for

PDE-constrained minimisers

does not depend on
(\partial_{t}u,\mathrm{p})
and we will invoke the symbolisations (

3

\begin{split}\frac{\sigma_{p}(A_{p,\varepsilon}\cap B)}{\mathcal{L}^{n+1}(A_{p,\varepsilon}\cap B)}\,\leq\,\bigg(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|y_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})}-\varepsilon}\bigg)^{\!p-1}.\end{split}

The above estimate in particular implies that
\sigma_{p}(A_{p,\varepsilon})\longrightarrow 0
as

for any

Remark 7
.

It is perhaps worth noting (in relation to the preceding arguments in the proof of (
1.32
)) that the modes of convergence

\|y_{p}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\longrightarrow\,\|y_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ y_{p}\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}y_{\infty}\ \text{ in }L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})

as
p\to\infty
, in general by themselves do not suffice to obtain
y_{p}\longrightarrow y_{\infty}
in any strong sense, hence precluding the derivation of a stronger property than (
1.32
), along the lines of (
1.31
). A simple counter-example, even in one dimension, is the following: let

Fluctuation results for size of the vacant set for random walks on discrete torus
Partha Dey, Daesung Kim

Abstract
. We consider one or more independent random walks on the d ⩾ 3 d\geqslant 3 dimensional discrete torus. The walks start from vertices chosen independently and uniformly at random. We analyze the fluctuation behavior of the size of some random sets arising from the trajectories of the random walks at a time proportional to the size of the torus. Examples include vacant sets and the intersection of ranges. The proof relies on a refined analysis of tail estimates for hitting time and can be applied for other vertex-transitive graphs.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 60G50, 60F99.

1.
Introduction

Consider finitely many independent random walks on the
d\geqslant 3
dimensional discrete torus with large side length, each starting from vertices chosen independently and uniformly at random. We are interested in the mean and fluctuation behavior of the size of some random sets arising from the trajectories of the random walks at a time proportional to the size of the torus. Examples include the vacant set or the set of vertices not visited by any of the walks; the size of the intersection of ranges, among others.

In particular, we fix a positive integer
\ell\geqslant 1
, where
\ell
denotes the number of independent random walks. The discrete torus is denoted by
\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}=(\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z})^{d}
, with side length
n
. Note that
n
controls the size of the graph and we are interested in the large
n
limit with a fixed
d
. We consider
\ell
many independent
\frac{1}{2}
–lazy random walks
(X_{t,i})_{t\geqslant 0},i\in[\ell]:=\{1,2,\ldots,\ell\}
starting from
X_{0,i}=\xi_{i},i\in[\ell]
, respectively, where
\xi_{i}
’s are i.i.d. uniformly distributed over
\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}
. We will use
\pi=\pi_{n}
to denote the uniform distribution over
\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}
.

We define the range of the
i
-th random walk at time
t
as

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{i}(t):=\{X_{s,i}\mid s=0,1,\ldots,t\}\quad\text{ for }i=1,2,\ldots,\ell;

the size of vacant set,
i.e.,
the number of vertices not visited by any of the
\ell
walks at time
t
as

(2)

\displaystyle V_{n}^{(\ell)}(t):=\left|\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{R}_{i}(t)\right|,

and the size of the intersection of ranges at time
t
as

(3)

\displaystyle R_{n}^{(\ell)}(t):=\left|\cap_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{R}_{i}(t)\right|.

If we define
\tau_{i}(v)
as the hitting time at the vertex
v
for the
i
-th random walk,
i.e.,

\displaystyle\tau_{i}(v):=\min\{t\geqslant 0\mid X_{t,i}=v\},

then we have for all
t\geqslant 0

\displaystyle V_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)=\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{i}(v)>t\}}\text{ and }R_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)=\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{i}(v)\leqslant t\}}.

One can easily check that

\E V_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)=n^{d}\pr_{\pi}(\tau_{1}(0)>t)^{\ell}\text{ and }\E R_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)=n^{d}\pr_{\pi}(\tau_{1}(0)\leqslant t)^{\ell}.

In particular when
t/n^{d}\to u
we have
n^{-d}\E V_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)\to e^{-\ell u/G(0)}
and
n^{-d}\E R_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)\to(1-e^{-u/G(0)})^{\ell}
as
n\to\infty
where

\displaystyle G(\xi)=G_{1/2}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\text{ expected number of visits to $\xi$}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{ for a $1/2$\ndash lazy random walk on $\mathds{Z}^{d}$ starting from }0.

The above result follows from standard literature, for instance see
[
7
]
*Equation (2.26),
[
1
]
*Theorem 1 or
[
21
]
*Proposition 3.7. Moreover, for an
\varepsilon
–lazy random walk, one can easily check that
G_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)=(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}G_{\ast}(\cdot)
, where
G_{\ast}
is the classical Green’s function for the simple random walk on
\mathds{Z}^{d}
. If needed, we will use
G(0;\mathds{Z}^{d})
instead of
G(0)
to emphasize the dependence on the dimension
d
.

For
\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
, we define

\displaystyle g_{n}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi)-\pr_{\pi}(X_{t}=\xi))

\displaystyle\text{ and }g_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}t(\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi)-\pr_{\pi}(X_{t}=\xi)).

Note that, heuristically
g_{n}(0)
is the difference between the expected number of visits to 0 by two
\frac{1}{2}
–lazy random walks on
\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
starting from the origin and the uniform distribution, respectively, up to a large multiple of the mixing time. Moreover, for fixed
\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}
, we have
g_{n}(\xi)\to G(\xi)
as
n\to\infty
. One can easily check that

g^{\prime}_{n}(0)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}}g_{n}(\xi)^{2}-g_{n}(0)>0.

We remark that
g_{n}^{\prime}(0)
stays bounded in
d\geqslant 5
and
g_{n}^{\prime}(0)
grows at rate
\log n
when
d=4
, and at rate
n
when
d=3
. See Lemma
2.1
for an upper bound on the growth rate of
g_{n}(\xi)
and
g_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)
. We write down the mean behavior with first order correction in the following Lemma.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
d\geqslant 3
and
\ell\geqslant 1
be fixed. Define
u:=(t+1)/n^{d}\in(0,\infty)
, then

\displaystyle\E(V_{n}^{(\ell)}(t))=n^{d}e^{-\frac{\ell u}{g_{n}(0)}}+\ell\left(\frac{ug_{n}^{\prime}(0)}{g_{n}(0)^{3}}+\frac{u}{2g_{n}(0)^{2}}-\frac{g^{\prime}_{n}(0)}{g_{n}(0)^{2}}\right)+O(n^{-d+3}).

Our first main result is the following explicit variance for
V_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)
. Define

(4)

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,\ell}^{2}(t):=\var(V_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\geqslant 5
and
\ell\geqslant 1
be fixed. Assume that
t/n^{d}\to u\in(0,\infty)
. There exists a function
\nu_{d}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sigma_{n,\ell}^{2}(t)=\nu_{d}(2\ell u/G(0)).

Moreover, we have an explicit formula for
\nu_{d}(u)
given by

(5)

\displaystyle\nu_{d}(u)=e^{-u}\sum_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{uG(\xi)}{G(0)+G(\xi)}\right)-1-\frac{uG(\xi)}{G(0)+G(\xi)}+\frac{uG(\xi)^{2}(G(\xi)-\mathds{1}_{\{\xi=0\}})}{G(0)^{2}(G(0)+G(\xi))}\right).

Note that the limiting variance in (
5
) is strictly positive as
e^{x}-1-x\geqslant x^{2}/2>0
for
x>0
and
G(0)>1
. Also, it is finite as
\sum_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}}G(\xi)^{2}<\infty
for
d\geqslant 5
.

One can see from the proof of Theorem
1.2
that
n^{-d}\sigma_{n,\ell}^{2}(t)
is governed by
\sum_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}}g_{n}(\xi)^{2}
. This sum can be written as
g_{n}^{\prime}(0)+g_{n}(0)
, whose growth rate is

h_{d}(n):=\begin{cases}n&\text{ when }d=3,\\
\log n&\text{ when }d=4\text{ and},\\
1&\text{ when }d\geqslant 5.\end{cases}

Thus, it is expected that the correct scaling order for the variance is
n^{4}
when
d=3
and
n^{4}\log n
when
d=4
, and heuristically the variance with right scaling converges to the sum of the second order term of the exponential in (
5
).
The following theorem affirms that this is indeed the case.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d\in\{3,4\}
and
\ell\geqslant 1
be fixed. Assume that
t/n^{d}\to u\in(0,\infty)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{d}h_{d}(n)}\sigma_{n,\ell}^{2}(t)=\nu_{d}(2\ell u/G(0)).

where
h_{3}(n)=n
,
h_{4}(n)=\log n
,

(6)

\displaystyle\nu_{d}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{d}u^{2}e^{-u},

and

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}:=\frac{9}{\pi^{4}\cdot G(0;\mathds{Z}^{3})^{2}}\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}^{3}}\left\|v\right\|^{-4},\quad\alpha_{4}:=\frac{16}{\pi^{4}\cdot G(0;\mathds{Z}^{4})^{2}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log n}\sum_{\|v\|\leqslant n}\|v\|^{-4}.

One can quickly check that the limit in
\alpha_{4}
exists and is finite. Note that this constant also appears in the fluctuation behavior for competing random walks in
[
19
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

One can extend the fluctuation behavior of the vacant set from discrete-time random walks to continuous-time random walks. Let
(T(s))_{s\geqslant 0}
be a Poisson process with intensity 1, independent of
X_{t}
. Let
Y_{s}=X_{T(s)}
and
\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)
be the vacant set of
Y_{s}
up to time
t
. Then, the variance of
\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)
can be computed via conditioning with Lemma
1.1
and Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
. Indeed, it follows that

\displaystyle\var(\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(\ell)}(t))=\E(\var(\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)\mid T(t)=s))+\var(\E(\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)\mid T(t)=s)),

for
t\approx un^{d}
. Then, we apply the fluctuation results for
V_{n}^{(\ell)}(s)
.

Remark 1.5
.

We remark that the order of the variance
\sigma_{n,\ell}^{2}(t)
at
t\approx un^{d}
is the same as that of the variance for competing random walks in
[
19
]
. Unlike our proof, which relies on an elementary and analytic approach using the generating function of hitting probabilities, Miller
[
19
]
made use of the conditioning argument at the mixing time to obtain the cancellation in the expansion of the variance, which leads to the precise asymptotic for the variance. It would be interesting to get a probabilistic proof and interpretation of our results on the asymptotic of the variance
\sigma_{n,\ell}^{2}(t)
.

We can generalize the above result to the size of the intersection of ranges and similar sets in the following way. For any subset
I\subseteq[\ell]
, we define

\displaystyle R_{n,\ell}^{I}(t):=\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}}\prod_{i\in I}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{i}(v)\leqslant t\}}\prod_{j\in I^{c}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{j}(v)>t\}}

as the number of vertices in
\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}
that are visited by walks indexed by
I
but not by walks indexed by
[\ell]\setminus I
at time
t
. We define the random vector indexed by
I\subseteq[\ell]

\boldsymbol{R}_{n,\ell}(t):=(R_{n,\ell}^{I}(t))_{I\subseteq[\ell]}.

Note that
R_{n,\ell}^{\emptyset}(t)=V_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)
and
R_{n}^{[\ell]}(t)=R_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)
as defined in (
2
) and (
3
). We can compute the variance-covariance structure for the random vector
\boldsymbol{R}_{n,\ell}(t)
when
t\approx un^{d}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

For
I,J\subseteq[\ell]
and
t/n^{d}\to u\in(0,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{d}h_{d}(n)}\cov(R_{n,\ell}^{I}(t),R_{n,\ell}^{J}(t))

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{m=0}^{\left|I\cup J\right|}\theta_{k,r,m}(\exp(-u/G(0)))\cdot\nu_{d}(2(\ell-m)u/G(0))

where
k=\left|I\cap J\right|,r=\left|I\Delta J\right|
,
\nu_{d}(\cdot)
is as given by (
5
)–(
6
), and

\displaystyle\theta_{k,r,m}(a):=\sum_{j=(m-k)_{+}}^{r\wedge m}\binom{k}{m-j}\binom{r}{j}(1-2a)^{m-j}(-1)^{r-j}a^{j}.

Remark 1.7
.

Under suitable assumptions, our analysis could be applied to general vertex-transitive graphs with
g_{n}(0)
bounded above. In particular, we consider the vacant set of an
\varepsilon
–lazy random walk on the hyper-cube
\mathds{Z}_{2}^{n}
. Since we have explicit formulas for the Green’s function, the eigenvalues, and the eigenfunctions as in
[
9
]
*Section 7, we can apply our method to obtain the fluctuation behavior of the vacant set as
n\to\infty
. Suppose
\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{n}_{2}
belongs to the
k
-th level for
0\leqslant k\leqslant n
, then the Green’s function is given by

\displaystyle g_{n}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)2^{n}}\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}^{n}_{2},v\neq 0}\frac{n}{\left\|v\right\|_{1}}\cdot(-1)^{\langle v,\xi\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)2^{n}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{n}{i}\binom{n}{i}-2\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{i-1}}\sum_{j=i}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\right)

see
[
6
]
*Section 4 and
[
9
]
*Example 4. In particular,
g_{n}(0)\to 2/(1-\varepsilon)
as
n\to\infty
. Similarly, using the spectral representation one can compute that
g^{\prime}_{n}(0)\to 4/(1-\varepsilon)^{2}-2/(1-\varepsilon)
. Thus,
\sum_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}_{2}^{n}}g_{n}(\xi)^{2}-g_{n}(0)^{2}\to 0
. Then, when
t/2^{n}\to u
, the variance of the size of the vacant set
V_{n}^{(\ell)}(t)
is of order
2^{n}
and

2^{-n}\var(V_{n}^{(\ell)}(t))\to\nu((1-\varepsilon)\ell u)\text{ with }\nu(x)=e^{-x}(e^{x/2}-1-(1-\varepsilon)x/4).

One might need to verify the detail somewhere else for other vertex-transitive graphs such as the Cayley graph of the symmetric group
S_{n}
.

The first step in the proof of Theorem
1.2
and
1.3
is the following simplification, which follows from the transitivity of the graph and independence of the random walks:

(7)

\displaystyle\var(V_{n}^{(\ell)}(t))

\displaystyle=n^{d}\sum_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}}\left(\pr(\tau(0,\xi)>t)^{\ell}-\pr(\tau(0)>t)^{2\ell}\right)

where

\displaystyle\tau(0,\xi):=\inf\{t\geqslant 0:X_{t}\in\{0,\xi\}\}

is the hitting time of the set
\{0,\xi\}
for the random walk
X_{t}
. In particular, we need a precise estimate of the tail behavior for the hitting time.

Lemma 1.8
.

For
d\geqslant 3
,
\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
, and
u=(t+1)/n^{d}\in(0,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\pr_{\pi}(\tau(0,\xi)>t)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{u}{f_{n}(\xi)}}\left(1+\frac{u}{n^{d}}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}_{n}(\xi)}{f_{n}(\xi)^{3}}+\frac{1}{2f_{n}(\xi)^{2}}\right)-\frac{f_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)}{n^{d}f_{n}(\xi)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+O(n^{-2d+3})

where

(8)

\displaystyle f_{n}(\xi):=\frac{1}{2}(g_{n}(0)+g_{n}(\xi)),\quad f_{n}^{\prime}(\xi):=\frac{1}{2}(g_{n}^{\prime}(0)+g_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)).

Remark 1.9
.

It is easy to check that
0\leqslant f^{\prime}_{n}(\xi)\leqslant f^{\prime}_{n}(0)
for all
\xi
using the spectral representation (
12
). Moreover,
n^{-d}\sum_{\xi}f^{\prime}_{n}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}_{n}(0).

1.1.
Related Literature

We first review the relevant literature on the range of simple random walks on the square lattice
\mathds{Z}^{d}
. Dvoretzky and Erdös
[
10
]
proved the strong law of large numbers for the range on
\mathds{Z}^{d}
for
d\geqslant 2
. A central limit theorem was obtained by Jain and Orey
[
12
]
. They showed that for strongly transient random walks, the variance of the range up to time
t
is of order
t
, and the range with a suitable normalization converges to normal distribution. Note that a simple random walk on
\mathds{Z}^{d}
is strongly transient if and only if
d\geqslant 5
. Later, Jain and Pruitt
[
13
]
considered the general transient case. It was shown in
[
13
]
that the variance of the range up to time
t
is of order
t
if
d=4
, and
t\log t
if
d=3
, and that the central limit theorem holds for
d=3,4
. The recurrent case (
d=2
) was studied by Le Gall
[
16
]
, who proved that if
\mathcal{R}_{t}
is the range up to time
t
, then
(\mathcal{R}_{t}-\E(\mathcal{R}_{t}))/(t(\log t)^{-2})
converges to the intersection local time of a planar Brownian motion, which is a non-Gaussian distribution.

There have been efforts to study the capacity of the range of random walks on
\mathds{Z}^{d}
. For a finite set
A\subset\mathds{Z}^{d}
in
d\geqslant 3
, the capacity of
A
is defined by the probability that a simple random walk starting from
A
never returns to
A
. Jain and Orey
[
12
]
showed the law of large numbers for the capacity of the range of a simple random walk when
d\geqslant 5
. Later, Chang
[
8
]
extended the result for
d=3,4
. Furthermore, it was shown in
[
8
]
that when
d=3
, the capacity of the range converges to that of Brownian motion. Recently, Asselar, Schapira, and Sousi
[
3
,
4
]
derived the central limit theorems for the capacity of the range when
d\geqslant 6
and
d=4
.

Sznitman
[
20
]
introduced the random interlacement model to study the trace left by a simple random walk in a discrete torus
\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
for a time
n^{d}
. The random interlacement at level
u
, denoted by
\mathcal{I}^{u}
, can be constructed via a Poisson point process on the set of doubly infinite nearest neighbor paths on
\mathds{Z}^{d}
with intensity measure given in terms of the Newtonian capacity. The model provides the local picture of the trace left by a simple random walk in a discrete torus.

One of the applications of the random interlacement model is to investigate the percolative properties of the vacant set on
\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
up to time proportional to the size of the discrete torus. It was shown in
[
20
]
that there exists a critical
u_{\ast}
such that the vacant set does not percolate for large
u>u_{\ast}
when
d\geqslant 3
, and percolates for small
u<u_{\ast}
when
d\geqslant 7
. Later, Teixeira and Windisch
[
21
]
showed that if
u>0
is large enough, then the volumes of all the components of the vacant set are of order
(\log n)^{\lambda(u)}
and if
u>0
is small enough then there exists a macroscopic component. They also proved that if
d\geqslant 5
, the macroscopic component is unique in the small
u
regime. The proofs of these results are based on couplings of a simple random walk on
\mathds{Z}^{d}
with the random interlacement.

Unlike the percolation of the vacant set, the fluctuation of the size of the vacant set does not have any threshold at the time level
u
. This is because the local behavior of the random walk is crucial in the study of the percolative property. At the same time, the variance of the size of the vacant set depends on both global and local pictures.

The random interlacement captures the local behavior of a simple random walk on the discrete torus, in a sense that one can be approximated by the other as
n\to\infty
in a box of size smaller order than the size of the torus. Consider the vacant set left by the random interlacement
\mathcal{I}^{u}
in a box
[-n/2,n/2)^{d}
, say
W^{u}_{n}:=[-n/2,n/2)^{d}\setminus\mathcal{I}^{u}
. Since
\pr(\{x,y\}\subset W^{u}_{n})=\exp(-2u/(G_{\ast}(0)+G_{\ast}(x-y)))
where
G_{\ast}(x)
is the Green’s function for
\mathds{Z}^{d}
, the variance of the vacant set
W^{u}_{n}
can be computed as

\displaystyle\var(W^{u}_{n})=n^{d}\sum_{\xi\in[-\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2})^{d}}\left(e^{-\frac{2u}{G_{\ast}(0)+G_{\ast}(\xi)}}-e^{-\frac{2u}{G_{\ast}(0)}}\right)+o(1).

Note that the sum in the right-hand side does not converge as
n\to\infty
for
d\geqslant 3
, while the sum in (
5
) does.
Compared to Theorem
1.2
, one can see that the contribution from the global fluctuation matters in the variance computation of the vacant set
V^{(l)}_{n}(t)
.

The vacant set
\mathcal{V}_{n}(t)
at a time of order
n^{d}\log n^{d}
was studied in
[
2
,
5
,
18
]
. The cover time
\tau_{\mathrm{cov}}
is the maximum of the hitting times
\tau(\xi)
over
\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
. Let
t_{\mathrm{cov}}=\max_{x}\E_{x}[\tau_{\mathrm{cov}}]
. It is well-known that
t_{\mathrm{cov}}=C_{d}n^{d}\log n^{d}(1+o(1))
as
n\to\infty
. Belius
[
5
]
proved that the fluctuations of
\tau_{\mathrm{cov}}
are governed by the Gumbel distribution, in a sense that
\tau_{\mathrm{cov}}/(C_{d}n^{d})-\log n^{d}
converges to Gumbel in law as
n\to\infty
for
d\geqslant 3
. He also showed that the scaling limit of the vacant set up to time
C_{d}n^{d}\log n^{d}
as a set-valued process in
(\mathds{R}/\mathds{Z})^{d}
is indeed a Poisson point process. Heuristically, the Gumbel fluctuation of the cover time implies that, at a time of order
n^{d}\log n^{d}
, the hitting times
\tau(\xi)
,
\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
, are approximately almost exponential and independent. A natural question is the limiting behavior of the vacant set
\mathcal{V}_{n}(t)
at a time
t=\alpha t_{\mathrm{cov}}(1+o(1))
for
\alpha>0
. Miller and Sousi
[
18
]
showed that there are two thresholds
\alpha_{0}(d)<\alpha_{1}(d)
such that
\mathcal{V}_{n}(t)
is approximately Bernoulli random variable indexed by
\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
if
\alpha>\alpha_{1}(d)
, and the total variation distance between
\mathcal{V}_{n}(t)
and Bernoulli random variable is 1 if
\alpha<\alpha_{0}(d)
.

Miller
[
19
]
investigated the fluctuation behavior of the trace by competing random walks. Consider
\ell
independent simple random walks
(X_{t,i})
,
i\in[\ell]
, on
\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
. We assume that each site
\xi
is painted by
i
irreversibly at time
t
if
X_{t,i}=\xi
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{i}(t)
be the set of sites painted by
i
up to time
t
. Miller
[
19
]
computed the limiting behavior of the variance of
\left|\mathcal{A}_{i}(\infty)\right|
for
d\geqslant 3
and
\ell=2
.
Indeed, it was shown that the order of the variance of
\left|\mathcal{A}_{i}(\infty)\right|
has the same order as the sum of squares of the
\mathds{Z}^{d}
Green’s function, which coincides with our fluctuation behavior. He also extended the result to vertex-transitive graphs with some assumptions on the mixing time and provided precise limits of the variances for the hypercube and the Cayley graph of
S_{n}
as a corollary.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations and Conventions

For the rest of the article we use
X_{t}
and
S_{t}
for
\frac{1}{2}
–lazy random walks on
\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
and
\mathds{Z}^{d}
, respectively. We explicitly write the dependence on
n,d
when needed. For a set
A\subseteq\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}
, the hitting time for
A
is denoted by

\tau(A):=\inf\{t\geqslant 0:X_{t}\in A\}.

We will add extra subscript
i
when working with the
i
–th lazy random walk. If
A=\{x\}
or
A=\{x,y\}
, we simply write
\tau(x)
and
\tau(x,y)
, instead of
\tau(A)
. Let
\pr
and
\E
be the probability and the expectation of
X_{t}
starting from the uniform distribution
\pi_{n}
on
\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}
. We will use
\pr_{\xi}
and
\E_{\xi}
to denote the probability and the expectation of
X_{t}
starting from
X_{0}=\xi
.
For
v=(v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{d})
in
\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, we use the notation

\left\|v\right\|_{p}:=\bigl(|v_{1}|^{p}\wedge(n-|v_{1}|)^{p}+|v_{2}|^{p}\wedge(n-|v_{2}|)^{p}+\cdots+|v_{d}|^{p}\wedge(n-|v_{d}|)^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}.

For simplicity, we drop the subscript
p
when
p=2
, that is,
\left\|v\right\|=\left\|v\right\|_{2}
.

We will write
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}
, when there exists a finite positive constant
c
such that
a_{n}\leqslant cb_{n}
for all
n
. Similarly, we will use
a_{n}\simeq b_{n}
, when
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}
and
b_{n}\lesssim a_{n}
. We will also use
a_{n}=O(b_{n})
or
b_{n}=\Omega(a_{n})
for
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}
and
a_{n}=\Theta(b_{n})
for
a_{n}\simeq b_{n}
.

2.2.
Green’s Function

We recall the Green’s functions and their basic properties on
\mathds{Z}^{d}
and
\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
. For further detail, we refer
[
9
]
*Section 7 for the discrete torus, and
[
15
,
14
]
for the lattice.
The Green’s function on
\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
for a
\frac{1}{2}
–lazy simple random walk is defined by

(9)

\displaystyle g_{n}(\xi,\eta)=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(\pr_{\xi}(X_{t}=\eta)-\pi(\eta)).

For simplicity, we use the notation
g_{n}(\xi):=g_{n}(0,\xi)
and drop the subscript
n
when there is no ambiguity. For a
\frac{1}{2}
–lazy simple random walk
S_{t}
on
\mathds{Z}^{d}
, the Green’s function
G(\xi,\eta)
is the expected number of visit to
\eta
from
\xi
,

\displaystyle G(\xi,\eta)=\E_{\xi}\left(\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}=\eta\}}\right)=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\pr_{\xi}(X_{t}=\eta).

Let
G(\xi):=G(0,\xi)
.

The Green’s function has the following spectral representation. The Laplacian matrix for the random walk is given by
\Delta_{n}=I-P_{n}=\frac{1}{2}(I-\frac{1}{2d}A_{n})
where
A_{n}
is the adjacency matrix of
\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
, that is,
A_{n}(\xi,\eta)=1
if
\xi
is a neighbor of
\eta
for
\xi,\eta\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
, and otherwise 0. Let
e_{n}(x):=\exp(2\pi ix/n)
, for
x\in\mathds{R}
. It is well-known that

(10)

\displaystyle\varphi_{v}(\xi)=n^{-\frac{d}{2}}e_{n}(\langle v,\xi\rangle),\quad\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}

for
v\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
gives a complete set of orthonormal eigenfunctions for
\Delta_{n}
with the corresponding eigenvalues

(11)

\displaystyle\lambda_{v}:=\frac{1}{2}\biggl(1-\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\cos\bigl(2\pi v_{j}/n\bigr)\biggr)=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi v_{j}/n)\in[0,1].

In particular,
\Delta_{n}
is diagonalizable and

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(\xi,\eta)=\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}}\lambda_{v}\varphi_{v}(\xi)\overline{\varphi_{v}(\eta)}\quad\text{ for }\xi,\eta\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}

where
\bar{z}
denotes the complex conjugate of
z\in\mathds{C}
.
Then, the Green’s function can be written as

(12)

\displaystyle g_{n}(\xi,\eta)=\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{v}}\varphi_{v}(\xi)\overline{\varphi_{v}(\eta)}=n^{-d}\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{v}}e_{n}(\langle\xi-\eta,v\rangle).

Using the spectral representation, one can see that the Green’s function
G(\xi)
for
\mathds{Z}^{d}
(
d\geqslant 3
) is the limit of
g_{n}(\xi)
as
n\to\infty
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle G(\xi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{n}(\xi)=\int_{[0,1]^{d}}\frac{d}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{j})}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x}\,dx\text{ for all }\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}.

Let
\varphi_{d}(x):=\frac{d}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{j})}
for
x\in[0,1]^{d}
, then
G(\xi)
is the Fourier transform of
\varphi
. By Plancherel’s identity (
[
11
]
*Proposition 3.1.16), if
d\geqslant 5
then

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{d}}\varphi_{d}(x)^{2}\,dx=\sum_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}}G(\xi)^{2}.

This fact will be used in the proof of the main results (see (
20
)).

2.3.
Generating Function

The Green’s generating function for the torus is defined by

(13)

\displaystyle g_{n}(\xi,\eta;z):=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left(\pr_{\xi}(X_{t}=\eta)-\pi(\eta)\right)z^{t}\text{ for }z\in\mathds{C},\left|z\right|<1.

For simplicity, we use
g_{n}(\xi;z)=g_{n}(0,\xi;z)
and drop the subscript
n
if there is no ambiguity. Note that the spectral representation provides

(14)

\displaystyle g_{n}(\xi,\eta;z)=\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{1-z\widehat{\lambda}_{v}}\varphi_{v}(\xi)\overline{\varphi_{v}(\eta)}=n^{-d}\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{e_{n}(\langle\xi-\eta,v\rangle)}{1-z\widehat{\lambda}_{v}}

where
\widehat{\lambda}_{v}:=1-\lambda_{v}
. Also note that
g_{n}(\xi;z)
is defined for
z\notin\{\widehat{\lambda}_{v}^{-1}\mid v\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\}
and
g_{n}(\xi)=g_{n}(\xi;1)
. Moreover, the Green’s generating function defined in (
13
) satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(\xi;z)=0\text{ for all }z.

Let

\displaystyle g_{n}^{\prime}(\xi;1):=\frac{d}{dz}g_{n}(\xi;z)\Big|_{z=1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}t\left(\pr_{\xi}(X_{t}=\eta)-\pi(\eta)\right)

\displaystyle=n^{-d}\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}(1-\lambda_{v})\lambda_{v}^{-2}e_{n}(\langle\xi,v\rangle).

We simply denote by
g_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)=g_{n}^{\prime}(\xi;1)
. One can easily check that

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}}g_{n}(\xi)^{2}=n^{-d}\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\lambda_{v}^{-2}=g_{n}(0)+g_{n}^{\prime}(0).

For
\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}
and
z\in\mathds{C}
with
\left|z\right|<1
, we define
G^{\prime}(\xi;z)=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}z^{t}\pr_{0}(S_{t}=\xi)
and

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(\xi)=\frac{d}{dz}G(\xi;z)|_{z=1}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}t\pr_{0}(S_{t}=\xi)>0

where
S_{t}
is a
\frac{1}{2}
–lazy simple random walk on
\mathds{Z}^{d}
. Note that
\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)=G^{\prime}(\xi)
for each
\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}
when
d\geqslant 5
. The Green’s generating function has a probabilistic interpretation. Consider two independence
\frac{1}{2}
–lazy simple random walks on
\mathds{Z}^{d}
,
S_{t,1}
and
S_{t,2}
starting at
0
and
\xi
respectively. Then,
\E\left|\{(s,t)\mid S_{s,1}=S_{t,2}\}\right|
, expected size of the intersection of two random walk trajectories (counted with multiplicities), can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}^{d}}G(v)G(v-\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t,s=0}^{\infty}\pr_{0,\xi}(S_{t,1}=S_{s,2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t,s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}^{d}}\pr_{0}(S_{t,1}=v)\pr_{\xi}(S_{s,2}=v)

(15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t,s=0}^{\infty}\pr_{0}(S_{t+s}=\xi)=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(t+1)\pr_{0}(S_{t}=\xi)=G(\xi)+G^{\prime}(\xi).

In this paper, the main results are given in terms of the Green’s function
G
for a
\frac{1}{2}
–lazy random walk on
\mathds{Z}^{d}
. If needed, one can replace
G
with the standard Green’s function
G_{\ast}
with minor modification. If
G_{\varepsilon}(\xi)
is the Green’s function for the
\varepsilon
–lazy random walk on
\mathds{Z}^{d}
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, then one can see that

\displaystyle G_{\varepsilon}(\xi)=\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}G_{\ast}(\xi),\qquad G_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\xi)=\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}G_{\ast}(\xi)+\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}G_{\ast}^{\prime}(\xi).

In particular, we have
G(\xi)=2G_{\ast}(\xi)
and
G^{\prime}(\xi)=2G_{\ast}(\xi)+4G_{\ast}^{\prime}(\xi)
.

The following Lemma
2.1
will be used to compute the growth rate of
g_{n}^{\prime}(0)
depending on
n
and the dimension
d
by taking
k=2
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
k\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\lambda_{v}^{-k}\simeq\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }d>2k\\
\log n&\text{ if }d=2k\\
n^{2k-d}&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

As a direct consequence, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}g_{n}(0;z)\biggl|_{z=1}\simeq\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }d>2k+2\\
\log n&\text{ if }d=2k+2\\
n^{2k+2-d}&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

From (
11
), it is easy to see that
\lambda_{v}\simeq\left\|v\right\|^{2}/(dn^{2})
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\lambda_{v}^{-k}\simeq n^{2k}\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\left\|v\right\|^{-2k}\simeq n^{2k}\sum_{r=1}^{n}r^{-2k}r^{d-1}\simeq\begin{cases}n^{d}&\text{ if }d>2k\\
n^{d}\log n&\text{ if }d=2k\\
n^{2k}&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

The second assertion follows from the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}g_{n}(0;z)\biggl|_{z=1}=\frac{k!}{n^{d}}\sum_{v\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\lambda_{v}^{-(k+1)}(1-\lambda_{v})^{k}

and this completes the proof.

\blacksquare

The next Lemma
2.2
tells us that
g_{n}(\xi)
and
g^{\prime}_{n}(\xi)
converge to 0 as
\left\|\xi\right\|
becomes large, uniformly in
n
, which will be frequently used in the proofs of the main results. The proof is given in Section
4
.

Lemma 2.2
.

We have

\displaystyle g_{n}(\xi)

\displaystyle=O(n^{-\min\{d-2,2\}}+(1+\left\|\xi\right\|)^{-\min\{d-2,(d+2)/2\}})\quad\text{ for }d\geqslant 3,

\displaystyle g^{\prime}_{n}(\xi)

\displaystyle=O(n^{-\min\{d-4,(d-2)/2\}}+(1+\left\|\xi\right\|)^{-\min\{d-4,d/2\}})\quad\text{ for }d\geqslant 5

uniformly in
n,\xi\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}
.

Let

(16)

\displaystyle f_{n}(\xi;z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}(g_{n}(0;z)+g_{n}(\xi;z))\qquad\text{ and }\qquad f_{n}(\xi):=f_{n}(\xi;1).

The generating function for
\pr(\tau(0,\xi)>t)
can be expressed in terms of the function
f_{n}(\xi;z)
as follows (see
[
7
]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

For
\left|z\right|<1
and
\xi\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}
, we have

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}z^{t}\pr(\tau(0,\xi)>t)=\frac{f_{n}(\xi;z)}{n^{-d}+(1-z)f_{n}(\xi;z)}.

From Lemma
2.3
, finding a precise estimate on the hitting probability
\pr(\tau(0,\xi)>t)
, boils down to a refined analysis on the coefficients of the series expansion of the function on the right-hand side in (
17
). We investigate the series expansion of such functions in Section
3
. We give a proof of Lemma
2.3
for completeness.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Suppose
\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
\left|z\right|<1
. Let

g^{+}_{n}(\xi;z):=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}z^{t}\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi).

Note that
g^{+}_{n}(\xi;z)=g_{n}(\xi;z)+n^{-d}(1-z)^{-1}
and
\sum_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}}g^{+}_{n}(\xi;z)=(1-z)^{-1}
. For
x\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle g^{+}_{n}(x;z)+g^{+}_{n}(x+\xi;z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}z^{t}\pr_{0}(X_{t}\in\{x,x+\xi\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\sum_{s=0}^{t}z^{t}\pr_{0}(X_{t}\in\{x,x+\xi\},\tau(x,x+\xi)=s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}z^{t+s}\pr_{0}(X_{t+s}\in\{x,x+\xi\},\tau(x,x+\xi)=s).

Applying the Markov property at
\tau_{x}
and
\tau_{x+\xi}
, we have

\displaystyle\pr_{0}(X_{t+s}=x,\tau(x,x+\xi)=s)

\displaystyle\quad=\pr_{0}(X_{t+s}=x,\tau(x,x+\xi)=s=\tau(x))+\pr_{0}(X_{t+s}=x,\tau(x,x+\xi)=s=\tau(x+\xi))

\displaystyle\quad=(\pr_{0}(X_{t}=0)+\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi))\pr_{0}(\tau(x,x+\xi)=s).

Thus, we get

\displaystyle g^{+}_{n}(x;z)+g^{+}_{n}(x+\xi;z)

\displaystyle=(g^{+}_{n}(0;z)+g^{+}_{n}(\xi;z))\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}z^{s}\pr_{0}(\tau(x,x+\xi)=s).

Averaging over
x
, we have

\displaystyle 2n^{-d}(1-z)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(2n^{-d}(1-z)^{-1}+2f_{n}(\xi;z))\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}z^{s}\pr(\tau(0,\xi)=s)

\displaystyle\text{ or }\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}z^{s}\pr(\tau(0,\xi)=s)

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{-d}}{n^{-d}+(1-z)f_{n}(\xi;z)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}z^{t}\pr(\tau(0,\xi)>t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\sum_{s=t+1}^{\infty}z^{t}\pr(\tau(0,\xi)=s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\sum_{t=0}^{s-1}z^{t}\pr(\tau(0,\xi)=s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}(1-z^{s})(1-z)^{-1}\pr(\tau(0,\xi)=s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-z}\left(1-\frac{n^{-d}}{n^{-d}+(1-z)f_{n}(\xi;z)}\right)=\frac{f_{n}(\xi;z)}{n^{-d}+(1-z)f_{n}(\xi;z)}.

The same argument holds for the case
\xi=0
.

\blacksquare

From the uniform convergence of
g_{n}(\xi)
and
\left|g_{n}(\xi)\right|\leqslant g_{n}(0)
, one can guess that
f_{n}(\xi)
is uniformly away from 0 in
n
. The next lemma asserts that this is the case. The proof is given in Section
4
.

Lemma 2.4
.

There exists
C>0
independent of
n
, such that
f_{n}(\xi)\geqslant C
for all
\xi\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}
.

3.
Series expansion

Fix an integer
k\geqslant 1
. Let
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{i},i\in[k]
be a sequence of positive real numbers and
\zeta_{0}:=1<\zeta_{1}<\zeta_{2}<\cdots<\zeta_{k}
be an increasing sequence of real numbers. Define the function

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}(\zeta_{i}-z)^{-1}\text{ for }z\notin\{\zeta_{i},i\in[k]\}.

We notice that
f
is denoted by a generic function of such form only in this section and different from the one in (
16
).

Lemma 3.1
.

The degree
k
polynomial given by

P_{k}(z):=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}\prod_{0\leqslant j\leqslant k,j\neq i}(\zeta_{j}-z)=(\alpha_{0}+(1-z)f(z))\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant k}(\zeta_{j}-z)

has
k
distinct real roots
\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}<\cdots<\gamma_{k}
. Moreover, we have
1<\gamma_{1}<\zeta_{1}<\gamma_{2}<\zeta_{2}<\cdots<\gamma_{k}<\zeta_{k}
and if
\gamma:=1+\alpha_{0}/f(1)<\zeta_{1}
, then

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\alpha_{0}}{f(\gamma)}\leqslant\gamma_{1}\leqslant 1+\frac{\alpha_{0}}{f(1)}.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

We note that the function
\phi(z):=\alpha_{0}(1-z)^{-1}+f(z)
is continuous and strictly increasing in each of the interval
(\zeta_{i-1},\zeta_{i})
for
i\in[k]
. Moreover,
\phi(\zeta_{i}-)=\infty,\phi(\zeta_{i}+)=-\infty
for each
i=0,1,\ldots,k
. Thus there is a root of
\phi
in the interval
(\zeta_{i-1},\zeta_{i})
, say
\gamma_{i}
, for
i\in[k]
. Now, any root of
\phi
is also a root of
P_{k}(z):=\phi(z)(1-z)\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant k}(\zeta_{j}-z)
. Since
P_{k}
is a degree
k
polynomial, it has exactly
k
roots and thus
\gamma_{i},i\in[k]
are all the roots of
P_{k}
.

Note that,
f
is strictly increasing in each of the interval
(1,\zeta_{1})
. Assume that,
\gamma:=1+\alpha_{0}/f(1)<\zeta_{1}
. It is easy to check that
\hat{\phi}(z):=\alpha_{0}+(1-z)f(z)
satisfies
\hat{\phi}(1)=\alpha_{0}>0
and
\hat{\phi}(\gamma)=\alpha_{0}-\alpha_{0}f(\gamma)/f(1)<\alpha_{0}-\alpha_{0}=0
. Thus
\gamma_{1}<\gamma
.

Moreover,
\alpha_{0}+(1-\gamma_{1})f(\gamma_{1})=0
implies that
\gamma_{1}=1+\alpha_{0}/f(\gamma_{1})>1+\alpha_{0}/f(\gamma)
as
f
is increasing and we are done.

\blacksquare

Note that we have

\displaystyle f(\gamma_{i})=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\gamma_{i}-1}>0,f^{\prime}(\gamma_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}(\zeta_{j}-\gamma_{i})^{-2}>0\text{ for }i\in[k].

In applications we have
\alpha_{0}/f(1)\approx n^{-d}\ll\zeta_{1}-1\approx n^{-2}
for
d\geqslant 3
and thus we have a good control on the first root
\gamma_{1}\approx 1+\alpha_{0}/f(1)
.

Let
\hat{\alpha}_{i},i\in[k]
be another sequence of positive real numbers. Define the function

\displaystyle\hat{f}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{\alpha}_{i}(\zeta_{i}-z)^{-1}\text{ for }z\notin\{\zeta_{i},i\in[k]\}

and

\displaystyle g(z):=\frac{\hat{f}(z)}{\alpha_{0}+(1-z)f(z)}\text{ for }z\notin\{\gamma_{i},i\in[k]\}.

Here we define
g(\zeta_{i}):=\frac{\hat{\alpha}_{i}}{(1-\zeta_{i})\alpha_{i}}
so that
g
is continuously differentiable everywhere except at
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\ldots,\gamma_{k}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have

\displaystyle g(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}-z}\cdot\frac{\hat{f}(\gamma_{i})/f(\gamma_{i})}{1+\alpha_{0}f^{\prime}(\gamma_{i})f(\gamma_{i})^{-2}}\text{ for }z\notin\{\gamma_{i},i\in[k]\}.

In particular, the coefficient of
z^{t},t\geqslant 0
in the series expansion of
g
around
0
is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\hat{f}(\gamma_{i})}{f(\gamma_{i})}\cdot\frac{\gamma_{i}^{-t-1}}{1+\alpha_{0}f^{\prime}(\gamma_{i})f(\gamma_{i})^{-2}}=\frac{\hat{f}(\gamma_{1})}{f(\gamma_{1})}\cdot\frac{\gamma_{1}^{-t-1}}{1+\alpha_{0}f^{\prime}(\gamma_{1})f(\gamma_{1})^{-2}}+e(t)

where

\displaystyle\left|e(t)\right|\leqslant\max_{i\in[k]}\left|\frac{\hat{f}(\gamma_{i})}{f(\gamma_{i})}\right|\cdot\zeta_{1}^{-t}.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

We can write
g
as a ratio of a degree
(k-1)
and a degree
k
-polynomial, as

\displaystyle g(z)=\frac{\hat{f}(z)\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\zeta_{i}-z)}{(\alpha_{0}+(1-z)f(z))\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\zeta_{i}-z)}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\hat{\alpha}_{i}\prod_{1\leqslant j\leqslant k,j\neq i}(\zeta_{j}-z)}{\sum_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}\prod_{0\leqslant j\leqslant k,j\neq i}(\zeta_{j}-z)}.

The denominator has
k
distinct real roots given by
\gamma_{i},i\in[k]
. Thus we can write

\displaystyle g(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}(\gamma_{i}-z)^{-1}

for some real numbers
a_{i},i\in[k]
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle a_{i}=\lim_{z\to\gamma_{i}}(\gamma_{i}-z)g(z)=\frac{-\hat{f}(\gamma_{i})}{(\alpha_{0}+(1-z)f(z))^{\prime}|_{z=\gamma_{i}}}=\frac{\hat{f}(\gamma_{i})}{f(\gamma_{i})+(\gamma_{i}-1)f^{\prime}(\gamma_{i})}.

Finally we used the fact that
f(\gamma_{i})=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\gamma_{i}-1}>0
.

The coefficient result follows since
(\gamma_{i}-z)^{-1}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{i}^{-t-1}z^{t}
for
\left|z\right|<1
and the bound on
e(t):=\sum_{i=2}^{k}a_{i}\cdot\gamma_{i}^{-t-1}
follows as
\gamma_{i}>\zeta_{1}>1
for all
i>1
and

\displaystyle\left|e(t)\right|\leqslant\sum_{i=2}^{k}\left|a_{i}\right|\cdot\gamma_{i}^{-t-1}\leqslant\zeta_{1}^{-t}\cdot\max_{i\in[k]}\left|\frac{\hat{f}(\gamma_{i})}{f(\gamma_{i})}\right|\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\gamma_{i}^{-1}}{1+\alpha_{0}f^{\prime}(\gamma_{1})/f(\gamma_{1})^{2}}

and the last sum is
f(0)/(\alpha_{0}+f(0))<1
.

\blacksquare

In our case, we have
\gamma_{1}\approx 1+\Theta(\alpha_{0}),\alpha_{0}=\Theta(n^{-d}),t=\Theta(n^{d}),\zeta_{1}=1+\Theta(n^{-2})
, thus the error term is
\exp(-\Theta(n^{d-2}))
whereas the first term is
\Theta(1)
.

4.
Proofs of Auxiliary Results

4.1.
Proof of Lemma
2.2

By symmetry and translation invariance, it suffices to assume that
0\leqslant\xi_{j}\leqslant\frac{n}{2}
for all
j=1,2,\ldots,d
. Recall that
\pi(\xi)=n^{-d}
is the uniform distribution. Let
T_{\text{mix}}
be the mixing time. It is well known (see
[
17
]
*Chapter 4) that
T_{\text{mix}}=O(n^{2})
and there exist
\bar{\gamma}>0,c>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}}\left|\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi)-\pi(\xi)\right|=O(n^{-d}e^{-\bar{\gamma}t/n^{2}})

for
t\geqslant cn^{2}
. In particular,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=cn^{2}}^{\infty}\left|\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi)-\pi(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle=O(n^{-d+2})

\displaystyle\text{ and }\sum_{t=cn^{2}}^{\infty}t\left|\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi)-\pi(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle=O(n^{-d+4}).

Clearly,
\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi)=0
for
t<\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}
. Thus, what is left is to control the contribution for
\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}\leqslant t\leqslant cn^{2}
.

Let
S_{t}
be a
\frac{1}{2}
–lazy random walk on
\mathds{Z}^{d}
. We can couple the two random walks
(S_{t},X_{t})
on
\mathds{Z}^{d},\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}
, respectively, by defining
X_{t}=S_{t}\mod n
, coordinate-wise. Then

\displaystyle\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi)=\sum_{k\in\mathds{Z}^{d},\left\|\xi+nk\right\|_{1}\leqslant t}\pr_{0}(S_{t}=\xi+nk).

In time
t=O(n^{2})
, the random walk
S_{t}
can visit upto distance of order
n
with high probability and
\left\|\xi+nk\right\|_{1}=\Omega(\max\{n,\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}\})
for
k\neq 0
.
In fact, from the local limit theorem (see
[
15
]
*Theorem 1.2.1), it follows that there exists
K>0
such that

\displaystyle\pr_{0}(S_{t}=\xi+nk)\lesssim\left(\frac{d}{2\pi t}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}e^{-\frac{d\left\|\xi+nk\right\|^{2}}{2t}}+\left\|\xi+nk\right\|^{-2}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}

for
t\geqslant K
. Assume that
\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}\geqslant K
. We have

\displaystyle I_{1}:=\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathds{Z}^{d}\\
\left\|\xi+nk\right\|_{1}\leqslant t,\left\|k\right\|\leqslant\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}\left\|\xi+nk\right\|^{-2}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}\lesssim\left\|\xi\right\|^{-2}\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}\simeq\left\|\xi\right\|^{-\frac{d+2}{2}}.

If
\left\|k\right\|>\sqrt{d}
, using
\left\|\xi/n\right\|\leqslant\sqrt{d}/2
, we have
\frac{1}{2}\left\|k\right\|<\left\|k+\xi/n\right\|<2\left\|k\right\|
and

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathds{Z}^{d}\\
\left\|\xi+nk\right\|_{1}\leqslant t,\left\|k\right\|>\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}\left\|\xi+nk\right\|^{-2}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-2}\sum_{k\in\mathds{Z}^{d},\left\|k\right\|<Ct/n}\left\|k\right\|^{-2}\simeq n^{-d}t^{d-2}.

Thus

\displaystyle I_{2}:=\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathds{Z}^{d}\\
\left\|\xi+nk\right\|_{1}\leqslant t,\left\|k\right\|>\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}\left\|\xi+nk\right\|^{-2}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}

\displaystyle\simeq n^{-d}\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}t^{\frac{d}{2}-2}\simeq n^{-2}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle I_{3}:=\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathds{Z}^{d}\\
\left\|\xi+nk\right\|_{1}<t,\left\|k\right\|\leqslant\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-\frac{d\left\|\xi+nk\right\|^{2}}{2t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-\frac{d\left\|\xi\right\|^{2}}{2t}}

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-\frac{d\left\|\xi\right\|^{2}}{2t}}\simeq\left\|\xi\right\|^{2-d}

and

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathds{Z}^{d}\\
\left\|\xi+nk\right\|_{1}<t,\left\|k\right\|>\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-\frac{d\left\|\xi+nk\right\|^{2}}{2t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathds{Z}^{d},\left\|k\right\|<Ct/n}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-\frac{dn^{2}\left\|k\right\|^{2}}{8t}}\lesssim n^{2-d}.

In the last inequality, we used the fact that
\sum_{k\in\mathds{Z}^{d}}s^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-C\|k\|^{2}/s}\lesssim 1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|g_{n}(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{t=cn^{2}}^{\infty}\left|\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi)-\pi(\xi)\right|+\sum_{t=0}^{cn^{2}}\pi(\xi)+\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi)

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{2-d}+I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{2-d}+\left\|\xi\right\|^{-\frac{d+2}{2}}+\left\|\xi\right\|^{2-d}+n^{2-d}+n^{-2}.

We now prove the second assertion. Since
g_{n}(\xi;z)=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}z^{t}(\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi)-\pi(\xi))
,
g_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)
can be written as

\displaystyle g_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)=\frac{d}{dz}g_{n}^{\prime}(\xi;z)\biggr|_{z=1}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}t(\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi)-\pi(\xi)).

As before, it suffices to estimate the summation over
\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}\leqslant t\leqslant cn^{2}
. Let
\beta\in(1,2)
. Then

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathds{Z}^{d}\\
\left\|\xi+nk\right\|_{1}<n^{\beta}\end{subarray}}\left\|\xi+nk\right\|^{-2}t^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\xi\right\|^{-2}\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}t^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}+n^{-2}\sum_{t=0}^{cn^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathds{Z}^{d}\\
\left\|k\right\|<Cn^{\beta-1}\end{subarray}}\left\|k\right\|^{-2}t^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\xi\right\|^{-\frac{d}{2}}+n^{(2-d)(2-\beta)}

and

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathds{Z}^{d}\\
\left\|\xi+nk\right\|_{1}<n^{\beta}\end{subarray}}t^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}e^{-\frac{\left\|\xi+nk\right\|^{2}}{t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}t^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}e^{-\frac{d\left\|\xi\right\|^{2}}{2t}}+\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathds{Z}^{d}\\
\left\|k\right\|<Cn^{\beta-1}\end{subarray}}t^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}e^{-\frac{dn^{2}\left\|k\right\|^{2}}{8t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\xi\right\|^{4-d}+n^{4-d}.

By
[
15
]
*Lemma 1.5.1, we get

\displaystyle J_{3}:=\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}\sum_{n^{\beta}\leqslant\left\|\xi+nk\right\|_{1}<t}t\pr_{0}(S_{t}=\xi+nk)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}\sum_{n^{\beta}\leqslant\left\|\xi+nk\right\|_{1}<t}t\pr_{0}(\left\|S_{t}\right\|\geqslant cn^{\beta+1})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}\sum_{n^{\beta}\leqslant\left\|\xi+nk\right\|_{1}<t}te^{-\frac{cn^{\beta+1}}{t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{d+4}e^{-cn^{\beta-1}}.

Thus, the local central limit theorem yields that for large
\left\|\xi\right\|
,

\displaystyle|g_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{t=cn^{2}}^{\infty}t\left|\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi)-\pi(\xi)\right|+\sum_{t=0}^{cn^{2}}t\pi(\xi)+\sum_{t=\left\|\xi\right\|_{1}}^{cn^{2}}t\pr_{0}(X_{t}=\xi)

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{4-d}+J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{4-d}+n^{(2-d)(2-\beta)}+n^{d+4}e^{-cn^{\beta-1}}+\left\|\xi\right\|^{-\frac{d}{2}}+\left\|\xi\right\|^{4-d}

as desired.

\displaystyle w_{n}=w=2\ell u/g_{n}(0),

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}_{n}^{d}}(e^{-\frac{\ell u}{f(\xi)}}-e^{-\frac{2\ell u}{f(0)}})

A direct computation yields

|I\cap J|=k,\ |I^{c}\cap J^{c}|=k^{\prime},\ |I\Delta J|=r,\ \pr(\tau(0)>t)=a\text{ and }\pr(\tau(0,\xi)>t)=b.

Then
k+k^{\prime}+r=\ell
and

\displaystyle n^{-d}\E(R_{n,\ell}^{I}(t)R_{n,\ell}^{J}(t))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}}(1-2a+b)^{k}b^{k^{\prime}}(a-b)^{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\binom{k}{i}\binom{r}{j}(1-2a)^{i}(-1)^{r-j}a^{j}b^{k-i+k^{\prime}+r-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}}\sum_{m=0}^{k+r}b^{\ell-m}\left(\sum_{j=(m-k)_{+}}^{r\wedge m}\binom{k}{m-j}\binom{r}{j}(1-2a)^{m-j}(-1)^{r-j}a^{j}\right)

(1)

As we have seen, our proofs are based on a precise tail probability of the hitting time at two points, which follows from an analytic observation on the generation function of hitting probabilities. A natural question is whether one can obtain an accurate tail behavior on the hitting time based on a probabilistic argument such as conditioning or constructing an appropriate coupling. Such an approach might give us an intuitive explanation of the asymptotic behavior and enable us to understand how the variance is created and grows.

(2)

Once the limiting behavior of the mean and the variance of the size of the vacant set is known, it is natural to ask if the size of the vacant set with an appropriate normalization converges to a limiting distribution. Simulation results for
d=3,4,5
(see Figure
1
) suggest that Gaussian central limit theorem hold for all
d\geqslant 3
and
u>0
. It would also be interesting to figure out the covariance structure of the vacant sets at different times and see if the vacant set’s size as a stochastic process indexed by
u
converges to a Gaussian process.

(3)

Instead of the size of the vacant set, one can consider the vacant set
\mathcal{V}^{(\ell)}_{n}(un^{d})
as a set-valued process indexed by
u
. Then, it would be interesting to see the scaling limit of the vacant set up to a time proportional to the size of
\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
as a subset of the continuum torus
[0,1]^{d}
. Note that up to the cover time of order
n^{d}\log n
, the scaling limit of the vacant set is studied by
[
5
,
18
]
.

(4)

Miller
[
19
]
studied the trace by two competing random walks on the discrete torus
\mathds{Z}^{d}_{n}
until the torus is fully covered. It is natural to consider the trace at a time scale proportional to
n^{d}
. In particular, one can ask how the variance behaves as the time level grows and see if there is a time threshold for the limiting variance.

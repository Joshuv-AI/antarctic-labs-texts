Densities and Weights of Quotients of Precompact Abelian Groups Thanks: This work is supported by NSFC grant NO. 12271258.
Dekui Peng

Abstract
. The topological group version of the celebrated Banach-Mazur problem asks wether every infinite topological group has a non-trivial separable quotient group. It is known that compact groups have infinite separable metrizable quotient groups. However, as dense subgroups of compact groups, precompact groups may admit no non-trivial separable quotient groups, so also no non-trivial metrizable quotient groups. In this paper, we study the least cardinal 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} (resp. 𝔫 \mathfrak{n} ) such that every infinite precompact abelian group admits a quotient group with density ≤ 𝔪 \leq\mathfrak{m} (resp. with weight ≤ 𝔫 \leq\mathfrak{n} ). It is shown that if 2 < 𝔠 = 𝔠 2^{<\mathfrak{c}}=\mathfrak{c} , then 𝔪 = 𝔠 \mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{c} and 𝔫 = 2 𝔠 \mathfrak{n}=2^{\mathfrak{c}} . A more general problem is to describe the set Q ​ W ​ ( G ) QW(G) of all possible weights of infinite proper quotient groups of a precompact abelian group G G . We prove that for every subset E E of the interval [ ω , 𝔠 ] [\omega,\mathfrak{c}] , there exists a precompact abelian group G G with Q ​ W ​ ( G ) = E QW(G)=E . If ω ∈ E \omega\in E , then G G can be chosen to be pseudocompact. In an appendix, we give an example to show that a non-totally disconnected locally compact group may admit no separable quotient groups. This answers an open problem posed by Leiderman, Morris and Tkachenko.

1.
Introduction

The famous Banach-Mazur Problem asks whether every infinite-dimensional Banach space has a separable infinite-dimensional quotient Banach space.
This problem, of course due to Banach and Mazur, is known to be considered in 1930s.
Although the separable quotient problem for general Banach spaces remains open, positive answers for special classes of Banach
spaces have been obtained, for example, the space
C(X)
of continuous functions on an infinite compact space
X
(E. Lacy
[
14
]
and H.P. Rosenthal
[
21
]
) and dual spaces of infinite-dimensional Banach spaces (S.A. Argyros, P. Dodos and V. Kanellopoulos
[
1
]
).
The separable quotient problem for topological groups asking if every infinite topological group admit a non-trivial (or infinite) separable quotient group has been formulated and studied since 2019, see
[
8
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
.

It is a well-known result of Gleason, Montgomery-Zippin and Yamabe that compact groups as well as connected locally compact groups (indeed, all connected-by-compact locally compact groups) are projective limits of (separable) Lie groups
[
19
]
; so they all admit non-trivial quotient Lie groups.
In particular, connected locally compact groups admit metrizable and separable connected non-trivial, hence infinite, quotient groups.
It seems that there is no enough evidence to ensure that profinite groups have infinite metrizable quotient groups because their quotient Lie groups are finite.
However, Leiderman, Morris and Tkachenko proved that profinite groups indeed have such quotient groups
[
15
]
by showing that every profinite group admits a continuous homomorphism into a countably infinite product of finite groups with infinite image.
The author would like to note that the result can also be obtained from the following simple observation: let
G
be an infinite profinite group and
H_{1}>H_{2}>...>H_{n}>....
a strictly decreasing chain of open normal subgroups of
G
.
The intersection
H=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}H_{n}
is a
G_{\delta}
set and a closed normal subgroup in
G
.
Thus
G/H
is metrizable (and separable).
It is evident that
G/H
is infinite; otherwise the chain
H_{1}>H_{2}>...
stops at some
n
.

Let us recall that a topological group
G
is called
precompact
if for every identity neighborhood
U
one can find a finite subset
F
of
G
such that
G=FU
.
By a theorem of Weil (see
[
23
]
), a topological group is precompact if and only if it embeds into a compact group as a dense subgroup.
The family of precompact groups seems to be one of the classes of topological groups closest to that of compact groups.
So we naturally expect that precompact groups also have the property.
Unfortunately, even an abelian precompact group may have no non-trivial separable quotient groups.
Such a group was constructed by Leiderman, Morris and Tkachenko in
[
15
]
.
It should be noted that metrizable precompact groups are always separable. So their example also shows that precompact abelian groups may admit no non-trivial metrizable quotient groups.
On the other hand, as a dense subgroup of some compact group, every infinite precompact (abelian) group
G
admits a continuous homomorphism
\varphi
into an infinite metrizable compact group with dense image.
So
G/\ker\varphi
is isomorphic to a dense subgroup of an infinite metrizable compact group as abstract groups.
Since infinite metrizable compact groups has cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
, one has that
d(G/\ker\varphi)\leq|G/\ker\varphi|\leq\mathfrak{c}
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Every infinite precompact (abelian) group admits a quotient group with density
\leq\mathfrak{c}
.

Since every topological group
G
satisfies
w(G)\leq 2^{d(G)}
, we also obtain:

Proposition 1.2
.

Every infinite precompact (abelian) group admits a quotient group with weight
\leq 2^{\mathfrak{c}}
.

So we arise the following question:

Question 1.3
.

What are the least cardinals
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{n}
such that every infinite abelian precompact group admits

(i)

an infinite quotient group with density
\leq\mathfrak{m}
;

(ii)

an infinite quotient group with weight
\leq\mathfrak{n}
?

It follows immediately from the example of Leiderman, Morris and Tkachenko that both
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{n}
are uncountable.
And by our analysis above, one has
\mathfrak{m}\leq\mathfrak{c}
and
\mathfrak{n}\leq 2^{\mathfrak{c}}
.
We especially wonder if the equalities hold.
Note that if we assume
CH
, the continuum hypothesis, then the result of Leiderman-Morris-Tkachenko yields that
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{c}
.
And by carefully analyzing their construction, one can also obtain that
\mathfrak{n}=2^{\mathfrak{c}}
if
\mathfrak{c}^{+}=2^{\mathfrak{c}}
.
So, we have:

Proposition 1.4
.

If the generalized continuum hypothesis holds, then
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{c}
and
\mathfrak{n}=2^{\mathfrak{c}}
.

In the following, we shall show that, in order to obtain the same result, one may only assume that
2^{<\mathfrak{c}}=\mathfrak{c}
, which is a consequence of Martin’s Axiom.

We also discuss the following problem which seems much more complicated:

Problem 1.5
.

Let
G
be an infinite precompact abelian group. Describe the sets
QD(G)
and
QW(G)
,
where

•

QD(G)=\{d(K):K~\mbox{is~an~infinite~proper~quotient~group~of}~G\}
; and

•

QW(G)=\{w(K):K~\mbox{is~an~infinite~proper~quotient~group~of}~G\}.

Clearly, for infinite compact abelian groups
G
, one has
QW(G)
is the interval
[\omega,w(G)]
.
It is an easy excise to check that this also holds for infinite
totally minimal abelian groups
, i.e., topological abelian groups
G
such that every surjective continuous homomorphism with domain
G
is open.
Note that according to a theorem of Prodanov
[
20
]
, totally minimal abelian groups are precompact.
As a matter of fact, a stronger version of the result discovered by Prodanov and Stoyanov states that every
minimal
abelian group (see Section
4
) is precompact.

We provide several results about the sets
QD(G)
and
QW(G)
.
In particular, it is shown that each subset of the interval
[\omega,\mathfrak{c}]
coincides with
QW(G)
for some precompact abelian group
G
.
Similarly, every subset of
[\omega,\mathfrak{c}]
containing
\omega
equals
QW(G)
for a pseudocompact abelian group
G
.

Our main tool is the duality of precompact abelian groups discovered by Comfort and Ross.
For a precompact abelian group
G
,
G^{*}
denotes the group of continuous homomorphisms of
G
into the 1-dimensional torus, endowed with the pointwise convergence topology.
Although the topological isomorphism
G\cong(G^{*})^{*}
is known by many authors (see, for example,
[
10
]
), the functor property of
∗
has been less discussed.
We shall give some basic facts about the functor with proofs in the following.

1.1.
Notation and Terminology

All topological spaces are assumed to be Hausdorff, unless otherwise stated.
In this paper we only discuss abelian groups, but we are not going to omit this word “abelian”.
Abelian groups are written additively, including the torus
\mathbb{T}
, so the neutral element of an abelian group is denoted by
0
.
Let
X
be a subset of a group
G
, the subgroup of
G
generated by
X
is denoted by
\langle X\rangle
.
If
X=\{g\}
, we shall write simply
\langle g\rangle
instead of
\langle\{g\}\rangle
.
A subset
X
of an abelian group
G
is called
independent
if for every finite subset
\{x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{k}\}
of
X
and integers
n_{1},n_{2},...,n_{k}
,
n_{1}x_{1}+n_{2}x_{2}+...+n_{k}x_{k}=0
implies that
n_{1}x_{1}=n_{2}x_{2}=...=n_{k}x_{k}=0
.
One immediately obtain that
\langle X\rangle
is a free abelian group if
X
is an independent set of non-torsion elements.
The
rank
of
G
, denoted by
r(G)
, is the cardinality of a maximal independent consisting only elements of infinite and prime power order.
This cardinal does not depend on the choice of the independent subsets; so it is a well-defined cardinal invariant.
If
Y
is a subset of
X
, the morphism
Y\hookrightarrow X
always stands for the inclusion.

We denote by
\mathbb{Z}
the additive group of integers, by
\mathbb{Q}
the rationals and by
\mathbb{R}
the reals.
If
G
is a topological group, then
G_{d}
is the group
G
with discrete topology.
The theory of uniform spaces of A. Weil, developed in
[
23
]
, made it possible to treat topological groups from these positions, since every topological group has several natural compatible uniform structures.
The completion with respect to the left uniformity (resp. two-sided) of a topological group is called the
Weil completion
(resp.
Raĭkov completion
).
A topological group coincides with its Weil completion (resp. Raĭkov completion) is called
Weil complete
(resp.
Raĭkov complete
).
The Weil completion of a topological group is not in general a group under the naturally extended operation.
However this does not happen to the Raĭkov completion.
If the Weil completion of a topological group is again a topological group, then the two completions automatically coincide.
Precompact groups and topological abelian groups are in this case, since the left uniformity and the two-sided uniformity coincide on a precompact or abelian topological group.
Throughout the paper, we mainly discuss precompact (abelian) groups. So we call the Weil completion (which is also the Raĭkov completion) of a precompact (abelian) group only the
completion
for convenience. Evidently, the completion of a precompact (abelian) group is a compact (abelian) group.
In the following, we shall denote the completion of
G
by
\varrho G
.
For
G
a topological abelian group, a
(continuous) character
means a (continuous) homomorphism of
G
into the torus
\mathbb{T}
.
For a topological abelian group
G
which is either compact or discrete, we denote by
\widehat{G}
the Pontryagin dual group of
G
, i.e., the group
cHom(G,\mathbb{T})
of continuous characters of
G
endowed with the compact-open topology.
The famous and beautiful Pontryagin Duality states that
G
and
\widehat{\widehat{G}}
are isomorphic as topological groups.
So, throughout this paper, we shall identify
G
with
\widehat{\widehat{G}}
; thus every element
g
in
G
is a continuous character of
\widehat{G}
in the sense
g:\chi\to\chi(g)
, for all
\chi\in\widehat{G}
.
Basic properties of Pontryagin Duality can be found in any book on Topological Groups, so we omit them.

Let
G
be an abelian group which is compact or discrete and
H
be a subgroup of
\widehat{G}
, we denote by
H^{\perp}
the subgroup of

consisting those elements

such that
\chi(g)=0
for all
\chi\in H
.
One can easily check that
(H^{\perp})^{\perp}=\overline{H}
.
The following result which is easy to check particularly implies that for every continuous character

,

is dense in

if and only if

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
G
be an abelian group which is discrete or compact and

a closed subgroup of

. Let also

(i)

For a set
S
, we are going to use

to stand for the cardinality of

.
We denote by

the first infinite ordinal (so it is also a cardinal), by

For

The

For a topological space

Since topological groups are Tychonoff, the above inequality holds for all topological groups.
If

Thus metrizable precompact groups are exactly precompact groups with countable weight.
And if

The Comfort-Ross Duality for Precompact Abelian Groups

For
G
an abelian group and
S
a set of characters of
G
.
The coarsest topology on
G
making all
\chi\in S
continuous is called the topology
generated by
S
.
This topology is evidently a group topology, which is Hausdorff if and only if
S
separates the points of
G
.
Let
\mathcal{T}(G)
be the set of all precompact topology on
G
with the order that
\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}
only if
\tau_{1}
is coarser than
\tau_{2}
, and
\mathcal{S}(\widehat{G})
the poset of dense subgroups of
\widehat{G}
ordered by inclusion.

Theorem 1.7
.

[
3
]
[Comfort-Ross Duality] Let
G
be an (abstract) abelian group, for every precompact group topology
\tau
, we denote by
(G,\tau)^{*}
the group of continuous characters of
(G,\tau)
. Then we have:

(a)

(G,\tau)^{*}
is a dense subgroup of
\widehat{G_{d}}
;

(b)

a subgroup of
\widehat{G_{d}}
generates a Hausdorff topology on
G
if and only if it is dense;

(c)

the mapping
\tau\to(G,\tau)^{*}
is an isomorphism of the posets
\mathcal{T}(G)
and
\mathcal{S}(G)
, with the inversion maps every dense subgroup
S
of
\widehat{G_{d}}
to the topology on
G
generated by
S
.

In the above theorem and the following,
G_{d}
denotes the discrete group having the same underlying group with
G
.
Then the Comfort-Ross duality yields that
G^{*}
is a dense subgroup of
\widehat{G_{d}}
.
The topology of
G^{*}
inherited from
\widehat{G_{d}}
is therefore the topology of pointwise convergence.
With this topology,
G^{*}
is again a precompact group, and is called the
Comfort-Ross dual group
(or briefly
C-R dual group
) of
G
.

One of the most important consequences of Comfort-Ross duality is the following:

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
G
be a precompact abelian group. Then the evaluation
\Psi:G\to(G^{*})^{*},g\to\Psi_{g}
is a topological isomorphism, where
\Psi_{g}
is the morphism
G^{*}\to\mathbb{T}
sending
\chi
to
\chi(g)
for all
\chi\in G^{*}
.

The above theorem allows us to identify
G
with the bi-dual group
(G^{*})^{*}
.

Proposition 1.9
.

For every precompact abelian group
G
we have
w(G)=|G^{*}|
.

Proof.

Let
H=\varrho G
, then we obtain
w(G)=w(H)=|\widehat{H}|=|G^{*}|
.∎

Proposition 1.10
.

Let
G
be a precompact abelian group. The following assertions hold:

(i)

of

;

(ii)

(iii)

Let us denote the underlying group of

A continuous homomorphism

Corollary 1.17
.

For a precompact abelian group

and an infinite cardinal

such that

, the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

(b)

for every subset

(c)

\widehat{G_{d}}

We first note that by the theorem of Comfort and Ross,

is a dense subgroup of
\widehat{G_{d}}
; and by Proposition

,

has weight
\geq\kappa

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b). Since

of

Let

be the closure of

(b)

has weight

.
So the subgroup

has cardinality
\leq\mu

By (b), there exists a non-trivial continuous character

From now on, we only discuss Hausdorff topological groups.

.

Now we let

Theorem 2.2
.

\ln\mathfrak{c}^{+}\leq\mathfrak{m}
.

Let

be an infinite cardinal. A family

is called a

Lemma 2.3
.

[

If

, then
\nu
admits an AD family of size

, the maximum possible number.

Proposition 2.4
.

Under the assumption of

, the compact group

3.

Now let us consider the problem what can the sets

be for a precompact abelian group? More precisely, does every set

Let us first consider the intervals.
As we noticed, for infinite compact abelian groups

.
If we discuss the parallel question for

Since compact groups

.
However, when

Proposition 3.1
.

∎

Assume that

Then by our construction,

This implies that

So (i) is proved.

Now let us prove (ii):

Claim 2.

H
is pseudocompact.

Proof of Claim 2.

According to Fact

(ii), we have to check that every countable subgroup of

carries the Bohr topology.
Note that

is exactly the group

with the coarest topology making all

Thus, it suffices to show that for every countable subgroup

of

and every homomorphism

extending

Since

is countable and

is the free abelian group over

, the subgroup generated by

As

is divisible,

over

The mapping of

In other words, there exists

∎

The property (iv) follows from the next claim immediately.

Claim 3.
If

is a closed subgroup of

Let

Then

Comments and Problems

Throughout this paper, we discuss the sets

and

for precompact abelian groups.
The result of Leiderman, Morris and Tkachenko shows that there exists a precompact abelian group

and

(so

Following this line, we find that under the assumption of

However, the author does not know whether this remains valid in

without any other set-theoretic assumption.

Question 4.1
.

Do
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{c}
and
\mathfrak{n}=2^{\mathfrak{c}}
hold in

?

In Theorem
3.7
, it is shown that the set

can be rather arbitrary.
So it is natural to consider the same problem for

.
One may ask can we replace
QW(G)
by

in Theorem

?
The question divides into two parts:

(i)

does every subset of
[\omega,\mathfrak{c}]
equals
QD(G)
for some precompact abelian group

?

(ii)

if

is a subset of

and
\tau\in QW(G)
, can the cardinality of the set of quotient groups

of

with

be an arbitrary cardinal
\leq\mathfrak{c}
?

We shall see that the answers to both the two questions are “no”.
To see this, let us first introduce an lemma which is “folklore”.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

be a topological group and

a closed subgroup.
If

is a subset of

such the image of

in the coset space

is dense and

is a dense subset of

is a dense subset of

.
In particular,

Proof.

Let

be a non-empty open set in

Since the image of

in

is dense,

.
So there exists

such that

is an non-empty open subset of

.
Thus,

intersects the dense subset

of

.

To see the second assertion, one may assume that

is a dense subset of

such that

and let

be a subset of

such that

and

is injective, where

Then the above argument ensures that

is dense in

for each dense subset

of

.
Particularly,

can be chosen to have cardinality

.
Thus
d(G)\leq|XY|\leq|X||Y|=d(G/N)d(N)
.∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let

be a precompact abelian group with

.
Then there exists
\tau
many quotient groups

of

such that

.

Proof.

Note that since

, every subset of

with cardinality

generates a dense subgroup of a proper closed subgroup of

.
So, one may define by induction a subset
\{x_{\alpha}:\alpha<\tau\}
of

such that
x_{\alpha}
is not in the closure

of
\langle\{x_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}\rangle
.
The inequality

holds for each

since

has a dense subgroup generated by
\alpha
many elements.
Then by Lemma

, each

has density

.∎

The proposition immediately implies the following facts.

Corollary 4.4
.

If

is a set of cardinalities such that

for some precompact abelian group

, then

has the largest possible element

, so

.

Corollary 4.5
.

If
G
is a precompact abelian group and

, then

has at least
\tau

\tau
.

The following problem remains open:

Problem 4.6
.

Characterize subsets
E
of
[\omega,\mathfrak{c}]
such that
E=QD(G)
for some precompact abelian group
G
.

Let us come back to the separable quotient problem.
We say a topological group
G
satisfying
separable quotient theorem
(resp.
metrizable quotient theorem
) if
G
has an infinite quotient group which is separable (resp. metrizable).
In the case
G
is precompact, metrizable quotient theorem implies separable quotient theorem.
We have known that pseudocompact groups satisfies metrizable quotient theorem, but in general, precompact abelian groups may fail to satisfy even separable quotient theorem.

Another important class of precompact abelian groups is the class of
minimal
abelian groups
[
5
]
. A topological group
G
is called minimal if every continuous bijective homomorphism
\varphi:G\to H
of topological groups is open, i.e., is a topological isomorphism.
Evidently, totally minimal groups are exactly topological groups with all quotient groups minimal.
It is known as the Prodanov-Stoyanov Theorem
[
5
]
that every minimal abelian group is precompact.
We raise the separable (metrizable) quotient problem for minimal abelian groups.

Question 4.7
.

Do minimal abelian groups satisfy separable (metrizable) quotient theorem?

Note that we have provided a partial answer in the section of introduction that totally minimal abelian groups satisfies both the two theorems.

Now let us give a negative answer to the metrizable part of Question
4.7
, while, the rest part remains open.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
G
be a precompact abelian group, then

(i)

G
is minimal if and only if
G^{*}
contains no proper dense subgroup;

(ii)

G
is totally minimal if and only if every subgroup of
G^{*}
is closed.

Proof.

(i) By the definition of minimal groups, a precompact abelian group
G
is minimal if and only if it does not admit a strictly coarser group topology,
equivalently, if and only if
G^{*}
does not admit a proper dense subgroup, by Proposition
1.13
.

(ii) A precompact abelian group
G
is totally minimal if and only if every quotient group of
G
is minimal, which is equivalent to that no closed subgroup of
G^{*}
contains a proper dense subgroup.
This is exactly the case that every subgroup of
G^{*}
is closed.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

There exists a minimal abelian group
G
of which every infinite metrizable quotient group has weight
\geq\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{Z}(4)
be the cyclic group of order
4
and
H
the compact group
\mathbb{Z}(4)^{\omega}
.
Strengthen the topology of
H
by letting all homomorphisms from
H
to the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}(2)
of order
2
continuous.
Let
N
be the socle of
H
, i.e.,
N=\{h\in H:2h=0\}
.
Since the kernel of every homomorphism of
H
to
\mathbb{Z}(2)
contains
N
, the new topology coincides with the original one on
N
.
In the following,
H
is always assumed to have the new topology we just obtained.

Let
M
be a proper subgroup of
H
.
Then
M
is contained in a subgroup
M^{\prime}
of
H
with index
2
in
H
.
The choice of the topology on

yields that

is closed, in particular,

is not dense in

.
So

does not admit a proper dense subgroup.
Let

be an infinite closed subgroup of

.
Then

is infinite.
Note that

is compact, so

is also compact and

.

Let

.
According to Lemma
4.8
,

is minimal.
Since every infinite closed subgroup of

.
∎

Appendix A
Separable Quotient Problem for Non-totally Disconnected Locally Compact Groups

In this appendix, we give an answer to the following question raised by Leiderman, Morris and Tkachenko in
[
15
]
, namely, the separable quotient problem for non-totally disconnected locally compact group.

Question A.1
.

[
15
, Problem 1.29]
Does every non-totally disconnected locally compact group
G
have a separable quotient group which is (i) non-trivial; (ii) infinite;
(iii) metrizable; or (iv) infinite metrizable?

Recall that again every connected locally compact group admits a non-trivial separable metrizable quotient group.
However, not all locally compact groups satisfy separable (metrizable) quotient theorem.
Indeed, in
[
24
, Corollary 3.3]
, for any infinite cardinal
\tau
, Willis constructed a totally disconnected topologically simple group
G
with an open compact subgroup
U
such that
w(U)=\tau
.
Thus, if
\tau
is large enough, i.e.,
\tau>\mathfrak{c}
, then
U
is not separable or metrizable.
Since
U
is open in
G
,
G
fails to be separable or metrizable as well.
Being also topologically simple,
G
satisfies neither separable quotient theorem nor metrizable quotient theorem.

Since the connected component of the identity of a topological group is closed and normal, locally compact topologically simple groups are either connected or totally disconnected.
For the connected case, by the solution of Hilbert’s fifth problem, such groups must be Lie; so they are metrizable and separable.
Therefore, a negative answer to Question
A.1
cannot be obtained by constructing a topologically simple group.
But still, we can use the construction of Willis.
Our following theorem answers Question
A.1
(i), so the whole question, in negative.

Theorem A.2
.

For every infinite cardinal
\mu
, there exists a non-totally disconnected locally compact group
G
of which all non-trivial quotient groups have local weight
\geq 2^{\mu}
and density
\geq\mu
.

Our main tool is the
wreath product
of topological groups.
Let
G,H
be groups,
X
a set.
Let also
\varphi:G\times X\to X
be a group action.
This action induces an action
\varphi^{\prime}
of
G
on
H^{X}
by automorphisms by letting

g\cdot(h_{x})=(h_{gx})
, i.e, the
x
-th coordinate of
g\cdot(h_{x})
is
h_{gx}
.
The semidirect product
H^{X}\rtimes_{\varphi^{\prime}}G
is denoted by
H\wr_{\varphi}G
(or, briefly,
H\wr G
) and called the
wreath product
of
H
by
G
(via
\varphi
).

We shall generalize this definition to topological groups, with the special case that
X
is discrete.
When we say a topological space
X
is a
G
-space for a topological group
G
, we mean there exists an action
\varphi:G\times X\to X
by self-homeomorphisms which is a continuous with respect to the product topology.

Theorem A.3
.

Let
G,H
be topological groups and
X
a discrete
G
-space.
Then the wreath product
H\wr G=H^{X}\rtimes G
with the natural product topology is a topological group.

Proof.

In
[
5
, Proposition 7.2.1]
, a criterion for a semidirect product of topological groups
A
by
B
(with the product topology) is given:
it is precisely the case that the group action
B\times A\to A
by conjugations is continuous.
Thus, we only need to prove that the action
\varphi^{\prime}:G\times H^{X}\to H^{X}
is continuous, where
\varphi
is the continuous action of
G
on
X
and
\varphi^{\prime}
is induced by
\varphi
in the natural way.

To see that
\varphi^{\prime}
is continuous, we take a non-empty open subset
U
of
H^{X}
and
g\in G,(h_{x})\in H^{X}
such that
g\cdot(h_{x})=(h_{gx})\in U
.
Note that
H^{X}
has the product topology.
So we may assume without loss of generality that
U=V^{y}:=\{(h_{x})\in H^{X},h_{y}\in V\}
, where
V
is open in
H
and
y\in X
, since the family
\{V^{x}:V\subseteq H~\mbox{open and~}x\in X\}
forms a subbase of
H^{X}
.

Let
y^{\prime}=g^{-1}y
and
U^{\prime}=V^{y^{\prime}}
.
Since
X
is discrete, there exists an open neighborhood
O
of
g
such that
g^{\prime}y^{\prime}=y
for all
g^{\prime}\in O
.
Thus,

g^{\prime}\cdot U^{\prime}=g^{\prime}\cdot V^{y^{\prime}}=V^{g^{\prime}y^{\prime}}=V^{y}=U.

In other words,
\varphi^{\prime}(O\times U^{\prime})\subseteq U
.
So,
\varphi^{\prime}
is continuous.
∎

Recall that a connected, compact, and topologically simple group must be simple as an abstract group
[
9
, Theorem 9.9.6]
.
So we prefer to call such groups simple for convenience and there will be no confusion.
In the following, we shall see that closed normal subgroups of powers of connected, compact, simple groups are quite “regular”.
For the sake of simplicity, for a product group
G=\prod_{x\in X}N_{x}
and a subset
Y
of
X
, we shall consider
\prod_{y\in Y}N_{y}
, in particular, each
N_{x}
, as a subgroup of
G
in a natural way.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
N
be a non-trivial connected, compact, simple group and
X
be any set.
Let also
G=\prod_{x\in X}N_{x}
, where each
N_{x}
is a copy of
N
.
Then for every non-trivial closed normal subgroup
H
of
G
, there exists a subset
Y
such that
H=\prod_{y\in Y}N_{y}
.

Proof.

Let
p_{x}:G\to N_{x}
be the canonical projection for each
x\in X
.
Since
p_{x}(H)
is a normal subgroup of the simple group
N_{x}
,
H_{x}:=p_{x}(H)
equals either
N_{x}
or
\mathbf{1}
.
Let us denote by
Y
the subset of
X
consisting all the elements
x\in X
with
H_{x}=N_{x}
.
Then we have

(A.1)

H\subseteq\prod_{y\in Y}N_{y}.

Clearly,
Y
is non-empty since
H
is non-trivial.

For each
y\in Y
, we claim that
N_{y}\subset H
, which is equivalent to that
N_{y}\cap H\neq\mathbf{1}
.
Suppose for a contradiction that there exists
y\in Y
such that
N_{y}
trivially meets

.
Note that non-trivial connected compact simple groups are non-abelian.
So there exists

such that
aba^{-1}b^{-1}\neq 1
.
Take
h\in H
with
p_{y}(h)=b
.
Since both
N_{y}
and
H
are normal in
G
, one has that

aha^{-1}h^{-1}\in N_{y}\cap H=\mathbf{1}.

Thus, we have that

, which induces a contradiction.
Now we proved the claim that each

is contained in
H
.
Since
\{N_{y}:y\in Y\}
generates a dense subgroup of
\prod_{y\in Y}N_{y}
and

is compact, the inclusion

(A.2)

\prod_{y\in Y}N_{y}\subseteq H

holds.
By (
A.1
) and (

), we complete the proof.
∎

Case 2.

In summary, we obtain that

,

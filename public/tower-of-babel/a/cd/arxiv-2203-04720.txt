Multi-sublinear operators and their commutators on product generalized mixed Morrey spaces
Mingquan Wei

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the boundedness for a large class of multi-sublinear operators T m T_{m} generated by multilinear Calderón-Zygmund operators and their commutators T m , i b ​ ( i = 1 , ⋯ , m ) T^{b}_{m,i}~(i=1,\cdots,m) on the product generalized mixed Morrey spaces M q 1 → φ 1 ( ℝ n ) × ⋯ × M q m → φ m ( ℝ n ) M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times M^{\varphi_{m}}_{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) . We find the sufficient conditions on ( φ 1 , ⋯ , φ m , φ ) (\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{m},\varphi) which ensure the boundedness of the operator T m T_{m} from M q 1 → φ 1 ( ℝ n ) × ⋯ × M q m → φ m ( ℝ n ) M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times M^{\varphi_{m}}_{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) to M q → φ ​ ( ℝ n ) M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) . 1.
Introduction

As we know, the multilinear Calderón-Zygmund theory is a natural generalization of the linear case. The
multilinear Calderón-Zygmund operators were first introduced by Coifman
and Meyer [
6
] and were later systematically studied by Grafakos and Torres [
8
].

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
r>0
, let
B(x,r)
be the open ball centered at
x
with radius
r
,
B^{c}(x,r)
denote its complement and
|B(x,r)|
be the Lebesgue measure of the ball
B(x,r)
. We denote by
\vec{f}
the
m
-tuple

(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})
. Similarly, we also denote
\vec{y}=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{m})
and
d\vec{y}=dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}
.
For
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional Euclidean space and
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
m
-fold
product spaces.

Let
\vec{f}\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The multi-sublinear maximal operator
M_{m}
is defined by

M_{m}(\vec{f})(x)=\sup_{r>0}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B}|f_{i}(y_{i})|dy_{i}.

When
m=1
, we denote by
M_{1}=M
, the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator.

For
p_{i}>1,~i=1,\cdots,m
, and
1/p=1/p_{1}+\cdots+1/p_{m}
, the boundedness of
M_{m}
form product Lebesgue spaces
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is obvious by using the boundedness of
M
on
L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and Hölder’s inequaly. One can also refer to [
18
] for the weighted boundedness of
M_{m}
.

In [
8
], Grafakos and Torres studied the multilinear Calderón-Zygmund operators. We say
K_{m}
is a multilinear Calderón-Zygmund operator if for some
q_{1},\cdots,q_{m}

\in[1,\infty)
and
q\in(0,\infty)
with
1/q=1/q_{1}+\cdots+1/q_{m}
, it can be extended to a bounded multilinear operator from
L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{q_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and if there exists a kernel function
K(x,y_{1},\cdots,y_{m})
defined off the diagonal
x=y_{1}=\cdots=y_{m}
satisfying

|K(x,y_{1},\cdots,y_{m})|\leq C\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}|x-y_{k}|\right)^{-mn},

such that
K_{m}
can be written as

K_{m}(\vec{f})(x)=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}}K(x,y_{1},\cdots,y_{m})f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})dy_{1}\cdots y_{m},

for
x\notin\cap_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{supp}f_{j}
.
Moreover, for some
\epsilon>0
,
K(x,y_{1},\cdots,y_{m})
satisfies the regularity condition

|K(x,y_{1},\cdots,y_{m})-K(x^{\prime},y_{1},\cdots,y_{m})|\leq C\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\epsilon}}{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}|x-y_{k}|\right)^{mn+\epsilon}},

whenever
2|x-x^{\prime}|\leq\max_{1\leq k\leq m}|x-y_{k}|
, and also for each fixed
k
with
1\leq k\leq m
,

|K(x,y_{1},\cdots,y_{k},\cdots,y_{m})-K(x,y_{1},\cdots,y^{\prime}_{k},\cdots,y_{m})|\leq C\frac{|y_{k}-y^{\prime}_{k}|^{\epsilon}}{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}|x-y_{k}|\right)^{mn+\epsilon}},

whenever
2|y_{k}-y^{\prime}_{k}|\leq\max_{1\leq k\leq m}|x-y_{k}|
.
Here and in what follows,
C
is a constant which may vary from line to line.

Grafakos and Torres [
8
] proved that

K_{m}
is bounded from
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p_{i}>1~(i=1,\cdots,m)

and
1/p=1/p_{1}+\cdots+1/p_{m}
, and bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{1/m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We also refer the reader to [
18
] for the weighted estimates of
K_{m}
.

As extensions of the commutators of linear or sublinear operators, the multilinear commutators also attract much attention.

Let
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{m})\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\vec{f}\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The multi-sublinear maximal commutator
M^{\vec{b}}_{m}
is defined by

M^{\vec{b}}_{m}(\vec{f})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}M^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}(\vec{f}),

where

M^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}(\vec{f})(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B}|b(x)-b(y_{i})||f_{i}(y_{i})|dy_{i}\times\sup_{r>0}\prod_{k=1,k\neq i}^{m}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B}|f_{k}(y_{k})|dy_{k}.

When
m=i=1
, we denote by
M^{b}_{1,1}=M^{b}
, the maximal commutator of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator.

To study the boundedness of the commutators of some integral operators, we need the bounded mean oscillation space first introduced by John and Nirenberg [
17
]. A locally integrable function
f
belongs to BMO if

\|f\|_{\rm BMO}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(x)-f_{B(x,r)}|dx<\infty.

We will use the following notation: if
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{m})\in{\rm BMO}^{m}
, then we denote by the norm
\|\vec{b}\|_{{\rm BMO}^{m}}=\sup_{i=1,\cdots,m}\|{b_{i}}\|_{{\rm BMO}}
.

Similar to the boundedness of
M_{m}
, for
\vec{b}\in{\rm BMO}^{m}
, one can get the boundedness of
M^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}
from
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
p_{i}>1,~i=1,\cdots,m
, and
1/p=1/p_{1}+\cdots+1/p_{m}
by using the boundedness of
M^{b_{i}}
on
L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see [
7
]) and Hölder’s inequaly. Therefore,
M^{\vec{b}}_{m}
is also bounded from
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under the same conditions.

Now we review the definition of the multilinear commutator of
K_{m}
, see, for instance, [
18
].

Let
\vec{b}\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\vec{f}\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The
m
-linear commutator
K^{\vec{b}}_{m}
is defined by

K^{\vec{b}}_{m}(\vec{f})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}K^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}(\vec{f}),

where

K^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}(\vec{f})=b_{i}K_{m}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{i},\cdots,f_{m})-K_{m}(f_{1},\cdots,b_{i}f_{i},\cdots,f_{m}).

The operator
K^{\vec{b}}_{m}
was proved to be bounded from
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p_{i}>1~(i=1,\cdots,m)
and
1/p=1/p_{1}+\cdots+1/p_{m}
by Lerner et al. [
18
].

The multi-sublinear maximal operator and multilinear Calderón-Zygmund
operators play a key role in the multilinear harmonic analysis, see [
8
,
19
]. To unify the multi-sublinear maximal operator and the multilinear Calderón-Zygmund operators, Lin and Lu [
19
] introduced a class of multi-sublinear operators
T_{m}
, which satisfies
that for any
\vec{f}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with compact support and
x\notin\cap_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{supp}f_{j}
,

|T_{m}(\vec{f})(x)|\leq C\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}}\frac{|f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})|}{|(x-y_{1},\cdots,x-y_{m})|^{mn}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m},

(1)

where
C
is independent of
\vec{f}
and
x
.

Similarly, for
i\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
,
b\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define a class of multi-sublinear commutators
T^{b}_{m,i}
, which satisfies
that for any
\vec{f}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with compact support and
x\notin\cap_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{supp}f_{j}
,

|T^{b}_{m,i}(\vec{f})(x)|\leq C\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}}\frac{|b(x)-b(y_{i})|}{|(x-y_{1},\cdots,x-y_{m})|^{mn}}|f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})|dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m},

(2)

where
C
is independent of
\vec{f}
and
x
.

Obviously, the multi-sublinear maximal operator and multilinear Calderón-Zygmund
operators satisfy the size condition (
1
). Similarly, the commutators
M^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}
and
K^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}

satisfy the size condition (
2
).

The operator
T_{m}
satisfying (
1
) has been proved to be bounded from product Morrey spaces to Morrey space [
19
], and bounded from product generalized Morrey spaces to generalized Morrey space [
27
] under some mild assumptions. In this paper, we will further prove the boundedness of the multi-sublinear operator
T_{m}
satisfying
condition (
1
) generated by multilinear Calderón-Zygmund operators from product generalized mixed Morrey spaces
M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times M^{\varphi_{m}}_{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the generalized mixed Morrey space

M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, we also establish the boundedness of the multi-sublinear commutator
T^{b}_{m,j}
satisfying
condition (
2
) generated by the commutator of multilinear Calderón-Zygmund operators from
M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times M^{\varphi_{m}}_{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to

M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Finally, as applications we apply our main theorems to the multi-sublinear maximal operator, the multilinear Calderón-Zygmund operators and their commutators.

2.
Definitions and preliminaries

Throughout the paper, we use the following notations.

The letter
\vec{p}
denotes
n
-tuples of the numbers in
[0,\infty]
, (
n\geq 1
),
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n})
. By definition, the inequality
0<\vec{p}<\infty
means
0<p_{i}<\infty
for all
i
. For
1\leq\vec{p}\leq\infty
, we denote
\vec{p}^{\prime}=(p^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,p^{\prime}_{n})
, where
p^{\prime}_{i}
satisfies

\frac{1}{p_{i}}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{i}}=1
. By
A\lesssim B
, we
mean that
A\leq CB
for some constant
C>0
, and
A\sim B
means that
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the class of all Lebesgue measurable functions.
By
L^{\vec{p}}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we mean the collection of all functions
f
such that
f\chi_{E}\in L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all bounded and measurable sets
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The letter
\mathbb{C}
is the set of the complex numbers and
\mathbb{N}
is the set of all non-negative integers.

In order to study the local properties of solutions to the partial differential equations, Morrey [
5
] introduced the classical Morrey space
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which consists of all
functions
f\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with finite norm

\|f\|_{M^{p}_{q}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{p}-{\frac{1}{q}}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(B(x,r))},

where
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
.
Note that
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
p=q
, and
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
p=\infty
. If
q>p
, then
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\Theta
, where
\Theta
is the set of all functions equivalent to 0 on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. One can see [
1
,
5
,
23
]
and some related papers for the boundedness of some classical operators on
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Recently, Guliyev et al. [
4
,
12
,
11
,
14
] extended
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the generalized Morrey spaces
M^{\varphi}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and studied the regularity of solutions of elliptic equations in divergence form in the generalized Morrey spaces.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
and
\varphi(x,r):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a Lebesgue measurable function. A function
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to
M^{\varphi}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if it satisfies

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{\varphi}_{q}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}\varphi(x,r)^{-1}|B(x,r)|^{-\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{q}(B(x,r))}<\infty.

(3)

From the definition, one can recover the classical Morrey space
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by taking
\varphi(x,r)=|B(x,r)|^{-1/p}
.
The boundedness of some sublinear operators and their commutators on
M^{\varphi}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
was obtained by Guliyev et al. [
11
]. Moreover, the boundedness of some multi-sublinear operators from
M^{\varphi_{1}}_{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times M^{\varphi_{m}}_{q_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to

M^{\varphi}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
was also studied in [
13
,
16
,
29
].

In 2019, Nogayama [
21
] considered a new Morrey space, with the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm replaced by the mixed Lebesgue norm
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is call the mixed Morrey space. We point out that there exists another mixed Morrey space, which used the iteration of Morrey norm, introduced by Ragusa and Scapellato [
24
]. For the boundedness of various operators on these mixed Morrey spaces of iteration type, see [
2
,
25
,
26
].

To make the definition clear, we first recall the definition of mixed Lebesgue spaces.
Let
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n})\in(0,\infty]^{n}
. Then the mixed Lebesgue norm
\|\cdot\|_{\vec{p}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\vec{p}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\cdots\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})|^{p_{1}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}dx_{2}\right)^{\frac{p_{3}}{p_{2}}}\cdots dx_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a measurable function. If
p_{j}=\infty
for some
j=1,\cdots,n
, then we have to make appropriate modifications. We define the mixed Lebesgue space
L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

to be the set of all
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\|f\|_{\vec{p}}<\infty
.
We refer the reader to [
3
] for more details of mixed Lebesgue spaces.

Let
1\leq\vec{q}<\infty,1\leq p<\infty
and
n/p\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}1/{q_{i}}
. A function
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to the mixed Morrey space
M^{p}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if

\|f\|_{M^{p}_{\vec{q}}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{p}-{\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}\right)}}\|f\chi_{B(x,r)}\|_{\vec{q}}<\infty.

Obviously, we return to the classical Morrey space
M^{p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
\vec{q}=q
. We point out that in [
20
,
21
], the author used the cubes to define the mixed Morrey spaces. One can verify the equivalence between the two definitions without any difficulty.

The mixed Morrey space is also an appropriate substitution of the Lebesgue space to study the mapping properties of some operators in harmonic analysis. In [
20
,
21
], the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
, the singular integral operators
K
, the fractional interal operator
I_{\alpha}
and its commutator
I^{b}_{\alpha}
were proved to be bounded in
M^{p}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For the atom decomposition and the Olsen inequality for the mixed Morrey spaces, we refer the readers to [
22
].

In order to unify the two types of Morrey spaces, we introduced generalized mixed Morrey spaces in [
28
], see also [
30
]. Now we give the precise definition of generalized mixed Morrey spaces.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq\vec{q}<\infty
, and
\varphi(x,r):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a Lebesgue measurable function. A function
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to the mixed Morrey space
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if

\|f\|_{M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}\varphi(x,r)^{-1}\|\chi_{B(x,r)}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{-1}\|f\chi_{B(x,r)}\|_{\vec{q}}<\infty.

Clearly, generalized mixed Morrey spaces contain generalized Morrey spaces and mixed Morrey spaces as special cases. In fact, if
\vec{q}=q
, then
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=M^{\varphi}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=M^{p}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
\varphi(x,r)=|B(x,r)|^{-1/p}
.

A basic result is that the generalized mixed Morrey space
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Banach space.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq\vec{q}<\infty
, and
\varphi(x,r):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a Lebesgue measurable function. Then
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Banach space.

Proof.

Let
f_{k}
be a Cauchy sequence in
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then for each
\epsilon>0
, there exists some integer
N(\epsilon)>0
, such that for all
i,j>N(\epsilon)
, we have
\|f_{i}-f_{j}\|_{M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}}<\epsilon
. Let
n_{k}=N(\frac{1}{2^{k}})
. For the above particular choice of
\epsilon
, there is a subsequence
\{f_{n_{k}}\}
, such that
\|f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_{k}}\|_{M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}}<\frac{1}{2^{k}}
.

We set

f(x)=f_{n_{1}}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(f_{n_{k+1}}(x)-f_{n_{k}}(x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and

h(x)=|f_{n_{1}}(x)|+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|f_{n_{k+1}}(x)-f_{n_{k}}(x)|,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Define

S_{N}f=f_{n_{1}}+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_{k}})=f_{n_{N}}

and

S_{N}h=|f_{n_{1}}|+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}|f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_{k}}|.
By using Minkowski’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|S_{N}h\|_{M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}\varphi(x,r)^{-1}\|\chi_{B(x,r)}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{-1}\|h\chi_{B(x,r)}\|_{\vec{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}\varphi(x,r)^{-1}\|\chi_{B(x,r)}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{-1}\|f_{n_{1}}\chi_{B(x,r)}\|_{\vec{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}\varphi(x,r)^{-1}\|\chi_{B(x,r)}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}^{-1}\|(f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_{k}})\chi_{B(x,r)}\|_{\vec{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|f_{n_{1}}\|_{M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}}+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\leqslant\|f_{n_{1}}\|_{M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}}+1.

As a consequence,
\|h\|_{M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}}<\infty
. Since
|f|\leqslant h
, we have
\|f\|_{M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}}\leqslant\|h\|_{M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}}<\infty
. Therefore,
f\in M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In view of

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\|f-f_{n_{N}}\|_{M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}\|f_{n_{k+1}}-f_{n_{k}}\|_{M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{2^{N-1}}=0,

we deduce that the sequence
\{f_{n_{k}}\}
converges to
f
in
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Thus, the Cauchy sequence
\{f_{k}\}
also converges to
f
in
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We are done.
∎

In [
28
], the author obtained the boundedness of some sublinear operators on
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In this paper, we will further extend the results in [
28
] to the multi-sublinear situation.

3.
Boundedness of
T_{m}
from

M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times M^{\varphi_{m}}_{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to

M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

In this section, we investigate the boundedness of
T_{m}
satisfying the size condition (
1
) from product generalized mixed Morrey spaces

M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times M^{\varphi_{m}}_{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the
generalized mixed Morrey space
M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We first prove the Guliyev local estimate (see, for example, [
11
,
14

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
m\geq 2
,

for all

\displaystyle\|T_{m}(\vec{f^{0}})\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B)}\leq\|T_{m}(\vec{f^{0}})\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle r^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{ij}}}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{\vec{q_{i}}}(2B)}\int_{2r}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{ij}}}}

\displaystyle r^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{ij}}}\int_{2r}^{\infty}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{\vec{q_{i}}}(B(x_{0},t))}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{ij}}}}.

(6)

\beta_{i}=0

\beta_{j}=\infty

\displaystyle\|T_{m}(f_{1}^{\infty},f_{2}^{\infty},f^{0}_{3},\cdots,f^{0}_{m})\|_{L^{\vec{q}}(B)}

\displaystyle r^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}\times r^{-n(m-2)}\int_{(2B)^{c}}\frac{|f_{1}(y_{1})|}{|x_{0}-y_{1}|^{n}}dy_{1}\times\int_{(2B)^{c}}\frac{|f_{2}(y_{2})|}{|x_{0}-y_{2}|^{n}}dy_{2}\times\prod_{i=3}^{m}\int_{2B}|f_{i}(y_{i})|dy_{i}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle r^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}\times r^{-n(m-2)}\int_{(2B)^{c}}\frac{|f_{1}(y_{1})|}{|x_{0}-y_{1}|^{n}}dy_{1}\times\int_{(2B)^{c}}\frac{|f_{2}(y_{2})|}{|x_{0}-y_{2}|^{n}}dy_{2}\times\prod_{i=3}^{m}r^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q^{\prime}_{ij}}}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{\vec{q_{i}}}(2B)}

\varphi,\varphi_{k}~(k=1,\cdots,m):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be Lebesgue measurable functions satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}\left(1+\ln{\frac{t}{r}}\right)\frac{\varphi_{i}(x,t)}{t}dt\times\prod_{k=1,k\neq i}^{m}\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{k}(x,t)}{t}dt\lesssim\varphi(x,r).

(11)

If
b\in{\rm BMO}
,
T^{b}_{m,i}
is a multi-sublinear operator satisfying condition (
2
), and bounded from
L^{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
T^{b}_{m,i}
is also bounded from
M^{\varphi_{1}}_{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times M^{\varphi_{m}}_{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to

M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

By using Lemma
4.1
and (
11
), we have

\displaystyle\|T^{b}_{m,i}(\vec{f})\|_{M^{\varphi}_{\vec{q}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}\varphi(x,r)^{-1}\int_{r}^{\infty}\left(1+\ln\frac{t}{r}\right)t^{-1-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{ij}}}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{\vec{q_{i}}}(B(x_{0},t))}dt

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1,k\neq i}^{m}\int_{r}^{\infty}t^{-1-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{kj}}}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{\vec{q_{k}}}(B(x_{0},t))}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0}\varphi(x,r)^{-1}\int_{r}^{\infty}\left(1+\ln\frac{t}{r}\right)\frac{\varphi_{i}(x,t)}{t}\varphi_{i}(x,t)^{-1}\|\chi_{B(x,t)}\|_{L^{\vec{q_{i}}}}^{-1}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{\vec{q_{i}}}(B(x_{0},t))}dt

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1,k\neq i}^{m}\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{k}(x,t)}{t}\varphi_{k}(x,t)^{-1}\|\chi_{B(x,t)}\|_{L^{\vec{q_{k}}}}^{-1}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{\vec{q_{k}}}(B(x_{0},t))}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{m}\|f_{k}\|_{M^{\varphi_{k}}_{\vec{q_{k}}}}.

We are done.
∎

5.
Some applications

In this section, we give some applications of our main theorems. We will show that many multi-sublinear operators and their commutators satisfy the assumptions in Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
4.1
. Therefore, we can obtain the boundedness of these operators on product generalized mixed Morrey spaces by using our main results.

To prove the boundedness of
M_{m}
,
M^{\vec{b}}_{m}
and
M^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}
from
L^{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we need the boundedness of
M
and
M^{b}
on mixed norm space
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Lemma 5.1
.

([
21
])
Let
1<\vec{q}<\infty
. Then

\|M(f)\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}.

(12)

Lemma 5.2
.

([
21
])
Let
1<\vec{q}<\infty
and
b\in{\rm BMO}
. Then

\|M^{b}(f)\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\|b\|_{\rm BMO}\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}.

(13)

Now we give the boundedness of multi-sublinear maximal operator and its commutator on product generalized mixed Morrey spaces.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
m\geq 2
,
1<\vec{q_{i}}<\infty
for all
i=1,\cdots,m
and
1/\vec{q}=1/\vec{q_{1}}+\cdots+1/\vec{q_{m}}
. Then
M_{m}
is
bounded from
L^{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

The simple inequality

\displaystyle M_{m}(\vec{f})(x)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{m}M(f_{i})(x)

together with Lemma
5.1
and Hölder’s inequality on mixed Lebesgue spaces gives us that for all
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})\in L^{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\displaystyle\|M_{m}(\vec{f})\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|M(f_{i})\|_{L^{\vec{q_{i}}}}\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{\vec{q_{i}}}}.

∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
m\geq 2
,
1<\vec{q_{k}}<\infty
for all
k=1,\cdots,m
and
1/\vec{q}=1/\vec{q_{1}}+\cdots+1/\vec{q_{m}}
. If
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{m})\in{\rm BMO}^{m}
, then both
M^{\vec{b}}_{m}
and
M^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}
are
bounded from
L^{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

We only need to prove the boundedness of
M^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}
, and the boundedness of
M^{\vec{b}}_{m}
follows directly. Obviously,

\displaystyle M^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}(\vec{f})(x)\leq M^{b_{i}}(f_{i})(x)\times\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{m}M(f_{j})(x).

(14)

Inequality (
14
) together with Lemma
5.1
, Lemma
5.2
and Hölder’s inequality on mixed Lebesgue spaces implies that for all
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})\in L^{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\displaystyle\|M^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}(\vec{f})\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\leq\|M^{b_{i}}(f_{i})\|_{L^{\vec{q_{i}}}}\times\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{m}\|M(f_{j})\|_{L^{\vec{q_{j}}}}\lesssim\|b\|_{\rm BMO}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{\vec{q_{j}}}}.

∎

To verify the boundedness of
K_{m}
,
K^{\vec{b}}_{m}
and
K^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}
from
L^{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we need the extrapolation theory on mixed Lebesgue spaces.
The extrapolaton theory on mixed Lebesgue spaces relies on the classical
A_{p}
weight (see [
7
]).

Definition 5.1
.

For
1<p<\infty
, a locally integrable function
w:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is said to be an
A_{p}
weight if

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{p}}=\sup_{B}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}<\infty,

where
B
is taken over all the balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

A a locally integrable function
w:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is said to be an
A_{1}
weight if for any ball
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(y)dy\leq Cw(x),~~~a.e.x\in B

for some constant
C>0
. The infimum of all such
C
is denoted by
[w]_{A_{1}}
. We denote
A_{\infty}
by the union of all
A_{p}~(1\leq p<\infty)
functions.

For a non-negative function
w\in L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
0<p<\infty
, the weighted Lebesgue space
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of all
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Now we give the extrapolation theorem on mixed Lebesgue spaces.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
0<q_{0}<\infty
,
\vec{q}=(q_{1},\cdots,q_{n})\in(0,\infty)^{n}
, and
\mathfrak{F}
be a family of ordered pairs of non-negative measurable functions. Suppose for all
w\in A_{1}
and
(f,g)\in\mathfrak{F}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{q_{0}}w(x)dx\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)^{q_{0}}w(x)dx.

(15)

Then if
q_{0}<\vec{q}
, the inequality

\|f\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}

(16)

holds for all pairs
(f,g)\in\mathfrak{F}
such that the left-hand side is finite.

We refer the reader to [
28
] for the proof of Theorem
5.3
.

The following two lemmas offer the weighted norm estimates for
K_{m}
,
K^{\vec{b}}_{m}
and
K^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
q>0
and
w
be a weight in
A_{\infty}
, then there exists a constant
C>0
(depending the
A_{\infty}
constant of
w
) so that the inequality

\|K_{m}(\vec{f})\|_{L^{q}_{w}}\leq C\|M_{m}(\vec{f})\|_{L^{q}_{w}}

holds for all bounded funtions
\vec{f}
with compact support.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
q>0
and
w
be a weight in
A_{\infty}
. Suppose
\vec{b}\in{\rm BMO}^{m}
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
(depending the
A_{\infty}
constant of
w
) so that the inequalitis

\|K^{\vec{b}}_{m}(\vec{f})\|_{L^{q}_{w}}\leq C\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{m}M^{2}(f_{j})\right\|_{L^{q}_{w}}

and

\|K^{\vec{b}}_{m,i}(\vec{f})\|_{L^{q}_{w}}\leq C\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{m}M^{2}(f_{j})\right\|_{L^{q}_{w}}

hold for all bounded funtions
\vec{f}
with compact support, where
M^{2}f_{j}=M(Mf_{j})
.

We refer the readers to [
18
] for the proof of the above two lemmas. Now we can prove the boundedness of multilinear Calderón-Zygmund operators and their commutators on product mixed Lebesgue spaces.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
m\geq 2
,
1<\vec{q_{i}}<\infty
for all
i=1,\cdots,m
and
1/\vec{q}=1/\vec{q_{1}}+\cdots+1/\vec{q_{m}}
. Then
K_{m}
is
bounded from
L^{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})\in L^{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since the class of bounded functions with compact support
are dense in
L^{\vec{q_{i}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})~(i=1\cdots,m)
(see [
3
]), it suffices to prove Theorem
5.4
for all
f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}\in L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the bounded functions with compact support.

We define a sequence of operators
\{K^{k}_{m}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, where

K^{k}_{m}(\vec{f})(x)=\min\{K_{m}(\vec{f}),k\}\chi_{B(0,k)}(x),

and consider the following fimily

\mathfrak{F}=\left\{\left(K^{k}_{m}(\vec{f}),\prod_{i=1}^{m}M(f_{i})\right):~\vec{f}\in(L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{m},~k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}\right\}.

It follows from Lemma
5.3
that for any
0<p_{0}<\infty
and
w\in A_{1}\subseteq A_{\infty}
,

\displaystyle\|K_{m}(\vec{f})\|_{L^{p_{0}}_{w}}\lesssim\|M_{m}(\vec{f})\|_{L^{p_{0}}_{w}}\lesssim\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}M(f_{i})\|_{L^{p_{0}}_{w}},

for every pair
\left(K^{k}_{m}(\vec{f}),\prod_{i=1}^{m}M(f_{i})\right)\in\mathfrak{F}
.

So, to use Theorem
5.3
for all ordered pairs in
\mathfrak{F}
, we need to check

\|K^{k}_{m}(\vec{f})\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}<\infty
for every
\vec{f}\in(L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{m}
.
It is always the case since
\|\chi_{B(0,k)}\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}<\infty
.

Now, we can apply Theorem
5.3
to each pair in
\mathfrak{F}
and get

\displaystyle\|K^{k}_{m}(\vec{f})\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}M(f_{i})\right\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}

for all
\vec{f}\in(L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{m}
.
From the proof of Theorem
5.1
, we know

\displaystyle\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}M(f_{i})\right\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{\vec{q_{i}}}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\|K^{k}_{m}(\vec{f})\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{\vec{q_{i}}}}

for all
\vec{f}\in(L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{m}
.
Noting that
{K^{k}_{m}(\vec{f})}(x)
increases almost everywhere to
K_{m}(\vec{f})(x)
, we get from the monotone convergence theorem on mixed Lebesgue spaces (see [
3
]) that

\displaystyle\|K_{m}(\vec{f})\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|K^{k}_{m}(\vec{f})\|_{L^{\vec{q}}}\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{\vec{q_{i}}}}.

∎

Let
m\geq 2
,

for all
k=1,\cdots,m
and

and

are
bounded from
L^{\vec{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{\vec{q_{m}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
5.4
, the proof is just an iteration of the proof for Theorem
5.4
, so we omit the details.
∎

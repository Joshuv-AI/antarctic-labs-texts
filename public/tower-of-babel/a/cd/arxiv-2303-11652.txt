Boundedness of p p -adic Hardy-Hilbert type integral operator on Block spaces
Salman Ashraf

Abstract
. In this paper, we estimate an operator norm of dilation operators on block spaces ( 𝔅 r , α ​ ( ℚ p ) \mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}) ) over p p -adic field. With this estimate, we establish boundedness of p p -adic Hardy-Hilbert type integral operator on 𝔅 r , α ​ ( ℚ p ) \mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}) . Moreover as application to our result, we obtain the p p -adic Hilbert inequality, p p -adic Hardy inequality and p p -adic Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya inequality on 𝔅 r , α ​ ( ℚ p ) \mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}) .

Introduction

Classical block space is a generalization of Lebesgue space. Zorko
[
44

[
44

has mean zero, therefore later in
[

[
34
,
43
]

\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya inequality for Lebesgue space
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
were established in
[
22
]
, which unify several important results in analysis such as the Hardy inequality and the Hilbert inequality. Also, the Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya inequality for block spaces on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
were established in
[
24
]
. One can refer to
[
19
,
26
,
41
,
42
]
for detail study of Hardy inequality and Hilbert inequality and it’s related topics.

The main aim of this paper is to establish boundedness of
p
-adic Hardy-Hilbert type integral operator on block spaces over
p
-adic field. As consequences, we establish
p
-adic Hardy inequality,
p
-adic Hilbert inequality and
p
-adic Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya inequality for block spaces over
p
-adic field. In
[
23
]
, K. P. Ho introduced and discussed some fundamental properties of block spaces over locally compact Vilenkin group. He also obtained the boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function on block spaces over locally compact Vilenkin group. Since additive group of
p
-adic field is an example of locally compact Vilenkin group, therefore in section 2, we define the block spaces over
p
-adic field by the idea in
[
23
,
27
]
.

In 2020, Huabing Li and Jianjun Jin
[
28
]
introduced and studied the
p
-adic Hardy-Hilbert type integral operator. It should be pointed out that the
p
-adic Hardy-Hilbert type integral operator includes many classical operators in
p
-adic Harmonic analysis such as the
p
-adic Hardy operator,
p
-adic Hilbert operator and
p
-adic Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operator. Let us now recall the definition of
p
-adic Hardy-Hilbert type integral operator.

Let
f
be a nonnegative integrable function on
\mathbb{Q}_{p},
and
\mathcal{K}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a homogeneous function of degree
-1,
that is,
\mathcal{K}(\xi x,\xi y)=\xi^{-1}\mathcal{K}(x,y)
for any
\xi>0.
Then
p
-adic Hardy-Hilbert type integral operator with the kernel
\mathcal{K}
is defined by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}^{p}f(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}}\mathcal{K}(|x|_{p},|y|_{p})f(y)dy,~~~~~x\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}.

Let us now see that for some special case of the kernel
\mathcal{K},~\mathscr{T}^{p}
reduces to some important operators
in
p
-adic analysis.

(i)

Let us choose

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(|x|_{p},|y|_{p})=\dfrac{1}{|x|_{p}~+~|y|_{p}},

then we have
p
-adic Hilbert operator defined by

(1.2)

\displaystyle H^{p}f(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}}\dfrac{f(y)}{|x|_{p}~+~|y|_{p}}dy,~~~~~~~~~x\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}.

(ii)

Let us choose

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(|x|_{p},|y|_{p})=|x|_{p}^{-1}\Phi_{E}(|y|_{p}),

where
\Phi_{E}
is the characteristic function of
E=\{y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}:|y|_{p}\leq|x|_{p}\},
then we have
p
-adic Hardy operator

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{p}f(x)=\dfrac{1}{|x|_{p}}~\int\limits_{|y|_{p}\leq|x|_{p}}f(y)dy,~~~~~~~~~~~x\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}.

(iii)

By choosing

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(|x|_{p},|y|_{p})=\dfrac{(|x|_{p}|y|_{p})^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}}{\text{max}\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}^{\lambda+1}},~~~~\lambda\geq 0,

we obtain the operator

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{p}f(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}}\dfrac{(|x|_{p}|y|_{p})^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}}{\text{max}\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}^{\lambda+1}}f(y)dy,~~~~~~~~~~~x\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}.

Observe that for
\lambda=0,~\mathscr{D}^{p}
reduces to
p
-adic Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operator defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}^{p}f(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}}\dfrac{f(y)}{\text{max}\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}}dy,~~~~~~~~~x\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}.

Then

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The field of
p
-adic numbers (
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
)

Let
p
be any fixed prime in
\mathbb{Z}.
Define the
p
-adic absolute value (or
p
-adic norm)
|\cdot|_{p}
on
\mathbb{Q}
by

\displaystyle|x|_{p}=\begin{cases}p^{-\gamma}~&\text{if}~~x=p^{\gamma}\frac{m}{n}\\
0~&\text{if}~~x=0,\\
\end{cases}

where
\gamma,m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
m,n
are not divisible by
p.
The field of
p
-adic numbers, denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{p},
is the completion of the field of rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
with respect to the metric
d_{p}(x,y)=|x-y|_{p}.
It is easy to see that
p
-adic absolute value satisfy the following properties:

(a)

|xy|_{p}=|x|_{p}|y|_{p}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
;

(b)

|x+y|_{p}\leq\text{max}\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\},
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,

The property (b) is called the
ultrametric inequality(or the non-Archimedean property)
. It follows that

|x+y|_{p}=\text{max}\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}~\text{if}~|x|_{p}\neq|y|_{p}.\\

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with natural operations and topology induced by the
metric
d_{p}
is a locally compact, non-discrete, complete and totally disconnected field. It is also well known that any non-zero
p
-adic number
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
can be uniquely represented in the canonical series

(2.1)

\displaystyle x=p^{\gamma}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}c_{l}p^{l},

where
c_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
and
c_{0}\neq 0.
The series (
2.1
) is convergence in
p
-adic norm since
|c_{l}p^{l}|_{p}\leq p^{-l}.
For
a\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z},
we denote by

\displaystyle B^{k}(a)=\{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}:|x-a|_{p}\leq p^{k}\},

\displaystyle S^{k}(a)=\{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}:|x-a|_{p}=p^{k}\},

respectively, a ball and a sphere of radius
p^{k}
and center at
a.
We use the notations
B^{k}=B^{k}(0)
and
S^{k}=S^{k}(0).
The set
\{B^{k}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{p}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
satisfies the following:

(i)

\{B^{k}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{p}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is a base for neighborhood system of identity in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and
B^{k}\subset B^{k+1},~k\in\mathbb{Z};

(ii)

B^{k},~k\in\mathbb{Z},
is open, closed and compact in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
;

(iii)

\mathbb{Q}_{p}=\bigcup\limits_{{k=-\infty}}^{+\infty}B^{k}~~\text{and}~~\{0\}=\bigcap\limits_{{k=-\infty}}^{+\infty}B^{k}.

We also have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}\setminus\{0\}=\bigcup\limits_{{k=-\infty}}^{+\infty}S^{k}.

Since additive group of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is a locally compact Abelian group, we choose a Haar measure
dx
on additive group of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
normalized so that

\displaystyle|B^{0}|=\int_{B^{0}}dx=1,

where
|E|
denotes the Haar measure of a measurable set
E
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}.
Then by a simple calculation, the Haar measures of any balls and spheres can be obtained. Especially, we frequently use

\displaystyle|B^{k}|=p^{k}~~~~\text{and}~~~~|S^{k}|=p^{k}(1-p^{-1}).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
S(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
be the space of complex-valued, locally constant and compactly supported functions on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}.
The space
S(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is called the Schwartz space over
\mathbb{Q}_{p},
has the following properties:

(1)

If
\phi\in S(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),
then
\phi
is continuous.

(2)

If
\phi\in S(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),
then there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\text{supp}~\phi\subset B^{k}.

(3)

If
\phi\in S(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),
then there exists
l\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\phi
is constant on the cosets of
B^{l}.

(4)

S(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is dense in
L^{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),~1\leq r<\infty.

We refer to
[
35
,
40
]
for details of
p
-adic field and proof of statements discussed in this subsection.

2.2.
Block spaces

We begin with definition of
p
-adic central Morrey spaces (see
[
15
,
16
]
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\alpha
be a non-negative real number and
1\leq r<\infty.
Then the
p
-adic central Morrey space is defined by

\displaystyle M_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\{f\in L_{\text{loc}}^{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})~:~\|f\|_{M_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}<\infty\},

where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}=\Bigg(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}~\dfrac{1}{|B^{k}|^{\alpha r}}\int_{B^{k}}|f(x)|^{r}dx\Bigg)^{1/r}.

It is clear that
M_{r,0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=L^{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<r<\infty.
A function
a:\mathbb{Q}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is said to be a central
(r,\alpha)
- block if there exist
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\text{supp}~a\subset B^{n}
and satisfies

\displaystyle\|a\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}~\leq~|B^{n}|^{-\alpha}.

The block space
\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
generated by central
(r,\alpha)
-blocks are defined by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\{f\in L_{\text{loc}}^{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}):f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}a_{k},~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|<\infty~\text{and each}~a_{k}~\text{is a central}~(r,\alpha)\text{-block}\}.

The block space
\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}=\inf\bigg\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|~:~f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}a_{k}\bigg\},

where the infimum is taken over all such decompositions of
f.

According to the definition of
\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),
for any
\text{central}~(r,\alpha)\text{-block}~b,
we have

\displaystyle\|b\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\leq 1.

For
r\in(1,\infty),
let
r^{\prime}
be the conjugate of
r.
We have Hölder’s inequality for
M_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

[
28
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1<r<\infty
and
\alpha>0.
If
f\in M_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
g\in\mathfrak{B}_{r^{\prime},\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}|f(x)g(x)|dx\leq\|f\|_{M_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\|g\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r^{\prime},\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}.

2.3.
Subspace of Morrey spaces

Zorko
[
44
]
proved that set of continuous functions with compact support (
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) is not dense in Morrey spaces (
L^{p,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) and she introduced an important subset of
L^{p,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
so-called
Zorko subspace

L_{0}^{p,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
which is defined as the closure of
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the
L^{p,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm. Adams and Xiao
[
1
]
stated that
L_{0}^{p,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the predual of block spaces (
H^{p^{\prime},\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) and the three spaces
L_{0}^{p,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})-H^{p^{\prime},\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})-L^{p,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are similar to
VMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})-H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})-BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see
[
34
]
). In
[
25
]
, Izumi, Sato and Yabuta considered Morrey spaces on unit circle
T
and proved in detail that
L_{0}^{p,\lambda}(\textbf{T})
is the predual of block spaces.

Motivated by above work, for
1<r<\infty
and
\alpha>0,
we consider the function

\displaystyle f(x):=\begin{cases}|x|_{p}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{r}},&~~~|x|_{p}~\leq 1,\\
0,&~~~|x|_{p}~>1.\end{cases}

Then
f\in M_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and for any
g\in S(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),
we get
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}~\dfrac{1}{|B^{k}|^{\alpha r}}\int_{B^{k}}|f(x)-g(x)|^{r}dx\Bigg)^{1/r}\geq c>0.

Hence, functions in
M_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
cannot be approximated by functions in
S(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).
In particular,
S(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is not dense in
M_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),
and therefore we define
\widetilde{M}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
as the closure of
S(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
in
M_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).

Analogous to the classical case, one could expect the following:

(2.2)

\widetilde{M}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})~\overset{\ast}{\longrightarrow}~\mathfrak{B}_{r^{\prime},\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\overset{\ast}{\longrightarrow}~M_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),

and the spaces
\widetilde{M}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})-\mathfrak{B}_{r^{\prime},\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})-M_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
have a relationship alike to
VMO(\mathbb{Q}_{p})-H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})-BMO(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).
The reader might find the papers
[
30
,
38
,
32
]
, where the spaces
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),~BMO(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
VMO(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
are studied and it is also pointed out that
BMO(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
can be characterize as the dual of the space
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).

The following theorem establish Minkowski’s integral inequality for block spaces
\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
1<r<\infty
and
\alpha>0.
Let
F
be a function on the product space
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that
\|F(\cdot,y)\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
then we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}F(\cdot,y)dy\bigg\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\|F(\cdot,y)\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}dy.

Proof.

Let
r^{\prime}
be the conjugate of
r
and suppose
g\in\widetilde{M}_{r^{\prime},\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
with
\|g\|_{\widetilde{M}_{r^{\prime},\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\leq 1
. Write

\displaystyle G(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}F(x,y)dy.

By using Hölder’s inequality for
M_{r^{\prime},\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
we have that

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}G(x)g(x)dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}|F(x,y)||g(x)|dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\|F(x,y)\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\|g\|_{M_{r^{\prime},\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}dy

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\|F(x,y)\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\|g\|_{\widetilde{M}_{r^{\prime},\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}dy

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\|F(x,y)\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}dy.

Taking now on the left the supremum over
g\in\widetilde{M}_{r^{\prime},\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
with
\|g\|_{\widetilde{M}_{r^{\prime},\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\leq 1,
we obtain that
G\in(\widetilde{M}_{r^{\prime},\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}))^{*}.
Now by (
2.2
) we have
G\in\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and (
2.3
) is valid.
∎

3.
Main Results

First, we study the dilation operators on block spaces. Let
\tau(\neq 0)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and for any function
f
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p},
consider the dilation operator of the form

(\mathcal{D}_{\tau}f)(x)=f(\tau x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}.

The following theorem, which discovers the boundedness of dilation operator on
\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),
is needed in order to prove the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1<r<\infty
and
\alpha>0.
Then, for all
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),
we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\leq 2|\tau|_{p}^{-(1/r+\alpha)}\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),
then by the definition of
\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
there exist a sequence of scalers
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and family of central
(r,\alpha)
- blocks with the support
B^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(3.1)

f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}a_{k},

and for any
\epsilon>0,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|<(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}.

Since
a_{k}
is a central
(r,\alpha)
- block with the support
B^{n},
therefore we see that
\mathcal{D}_{\tau}a_{k}
is a central
(r,\alpha)
- block with the support
\tau^{-1}B^{n}
and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}a_{k}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}

\displaystyle=|\tau|_{p}^{-1/r}\|a_{k}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}

\displaystyle\leq|\tau|_{p}^{-1/r}|B^{n}|^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle=|\tau|_{p}^{-1/r}|\tau|_{p}^{-\alpha}|\tau^{-1}B^{n}|^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle=|\tau|_{p}^{-(1/r+\alpha)}|\tau^{-1}B^{n}|^{-\alpha}.

From (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\tau}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}\mathcal{D}_{\tau}a_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\tau|_{p}^{-(1/r+\alpha)}\lambda_{k}|\tau|_{p}^{(1/r+\alpha)}\mathcal{D}_{\tau}a_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}b_{k},

where
\gamma_{k}=|\tau|_{p}^{-(1/r+\alpha)}\lambda_{k}
and
b_{k}=|\tau|_{p}^{(1/r+\alpha)}\mathcal{D}_{\tau}a_{k}.
By the definition of block spaces, we have
\mathcal{D}_{\tau}f\in\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\gamma_{k}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\tau|_{p}^{-(1/r+\alpha)}|\lambda_{k}|

\displaystyle\leq|\tau|_{p}^{-(1/r+\alpha)}(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}.

As
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\leq 2|\tau|_{p}^{-(1/r+\alpha)}\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}.

∎

The following
p
-adic Hardy-Hilbert type integral inequality is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
1<r<\infty,~\alpha>0
and let
p
-adic Hardy-Hilbert type integral operator
\mathscr{T}^{p}
is defined by (
1.1
). If
\mathcal{K}
satisfies

(3.2)

\displaystyle C_{r,\alpha}=2(1-p^{-1})\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}<\infty.

Then

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{T}^{p}f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}\leq C_{r,\alpha}\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})},

for all
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}).

Proof.

Let
y=x\tau
in (
1.1
), then , by
dy=|x|_{p}d\tau
we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}^{p}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}}\mathcal{K}(|x|_{p},|x\tau|_{p})f(\tau x)|x|_{p}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}}|x|_{p}^{-1}\mathcal{K}(1,|\tau|_{p})f(\tau x)|x|_{p}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}}\mathcal{K}(1,|\tau|_{p})f(\tau x)d\tau

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}~\int\limits_{S^{k}}\mathcal{K}(1,|\tau|_{p})\mathcal{D}_{\tau}f(x)d\tau

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})~\int\limits_{S^{k}}\mathcal{D}_{\tau}f(x)d\tau.

Let us first apply the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}
on both sides of (
3.3
) and then using Theorem
2.4
and Theorem
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{T}^{p}f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})~\bigg\|\int\limits_{S^{k}}\mathcal{D}_{\tau}f(x)d\tau\bigg\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})~\int\limits_{S^{k}}\|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})~\int\limits_{S^{k}}2|\tau|_{p}^{-(1/r+\alpha)}\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}d\tau

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})2p^{-k(1/r+\alpha)}|S^{k}|\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})2p^{-k(1/r+\alpha)}p^{k}(1-p^{-1})\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}

\displaystyle=C_{r,\alpha}\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}.

Therefore (
3.2
), assures that for all
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}),
we have

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{T}^{p}f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}\leq C_{r,\alpha}\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}.

∎

As a consequence of Theorem
3.2
, we obtain the
p
-adic Hilbert type inequality on
\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
1<r<\infty,~\alpha>0
and let
p
-adic Hilbert operator is defined by (
1.2
). If
0<1/r+\alpha<1
then there is a constant
C>0
such that for any
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})

\displaystyle\|H^{p}f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{K}(|x|_{p},|y|_{p})=\dfrac{1}{|x|_{p}~+~|y|_{p}}.
Notice that,
\mathcal{K}
is a nonnegative homogeneous function of degree
-1,
and

\displaystyle C_{r,\alpha}

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\bigg(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{0}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}~+~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\bigg(\mathcal{K}(1,1)~+~\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-1}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}~+~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\bigg(\dfrac{1}{2}~+~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{-k})p^{k(1/r+\alpha-1)}~+~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\bigg(\dfrac{1}{2}~+~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg[\dfrac{p^{k}p^{k(1/r+\alpha-1)}}{p^{k}+1}~+~\dfrac{p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}}{1+p^{k}}\bigg]\bigg)

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\bigg(\dfrac{1}{2}~+~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{1+p^{k}}(p^{k(1/r+\alpha-1+1)}~+~p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)})\bigg),

since we have
0<1/r+\alpha<1,
which implies that
C_{r,\alpha}<\infty,
and therefore Theorem
3.2
gives the
p
-adic Hilbert type inequality on
\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}).

∎

We also obtain the
p
-adic Hardy type inequality on
\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}).

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
1<r<\infty,~\alpha>0
and let
p
-adic Hardy operator is defined by (
1.3
). If
0<1/r+\alpha<1
there is a constant
C>0
such that for any
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}^{p}f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{K}(|x|_{p},|y|_{p})=|x|_{p}^{-1}\Phi_{E}(|y|_{p}),
where
\Phi_{E}
is the characteristic function of
E=\{y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}:|y|_{p}\leq|x|_{p}\},
and it is obviously a nonnegative homogeneous function of degree
-1.
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle C_{r,\alpha}

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\sum_{k=-\infty}^{0}p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\bigg(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}p^{k(1/r+\alpha-1)}\bigg)<\infty,~~~~~~~~~(\because 1/r+\alpha<1).

Hence, according to Theorem
3.2
, we have
p
-adic Hardy type inequality on
\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p}).

∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
1<r<\infty,~\alpha>0
and let the operator
\mathscr{D}^{p}
is defined by (
1.4
). If
-\frac{\lambda}{2}<1/r+\alpha<\frac{\lambda}{2}+1
then there is a constant
C>0
such that for any
f\in\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{D}^{p}f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}.

Proof.

Let

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(|x|_{p},|y|_{p})=\dfrac{(|x|_{p}|y|_{p})^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}}{\text{max}\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}^{\lambda+1}},~~~~~\lambda\geq 0.

We find that
\mathcal{K}
is a nonnegative homogeneous function of degree
-1,
and

\displaystyle C_{r,\alpha}

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\bigg(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{0}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}~+~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\bigg(\mathcal{K}(1,1)~+~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{-k})p^{k(1/r+\alpha-1)}~+~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(1,p^{k})p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\bigg(1~+~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}p^{-\frac{k\lambda}{2}}p^{k(1/r+\alpha-1)}~+~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}p^{-k(\frac{\lambda}{2}+1)}p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2(1-p^{-1})\bigg(1~+~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg[p^{k(1/r+\alpha-1-\frac{\lambda}{2})}~+~p^{-k(1/r+\alpha-1+\frac{\lambda}{2}+1)}\bigg]\bigg)<\infty,~~~~(\because-\frac{\lambda}{2}<1/r+\alpha<\frac{\lambda}{2}+1).

Consequently, the boundedness of
\mathscr{D}^{p}
on
\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})
is assured by Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Taking
\lambda=0
in kernal (
3.4
), we get the p-adic Hardy-Littlewood- Pólya inequality on
\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})
as follows:

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{P}^{p}f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}_{r,\alpha}(\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{p})}.

Asymptotic Betti numbers for hard squares in the homological liquid regime Thanks: MK gratefully acknowledges the support of NSF awards #1839358 and #2005630.
Hannah Alpert, Matthew Kahle, Robert MacPherson

Abstract
. We study configuration spaces C ⁡ ( n , p , q ) C(n;p,q) of n n ordered unit squares in a p p by q q rectangle. Our goal is to estimate the Betti numbers for large n n , j j , p p , and q q . We consider sequences of area-normalized coordinates, where ( n p ​ q , j p ​ q ) (\frac{n}{pq},\frac{j}{pq}) converges as n n , j j , p p , and q q approach infinity. For every sequence that converges to a point in the “feasible region” in the ( x , y ) (x,y) -plane identified in [ ABK+20 ] , we show that the factorial growth rate of the Betti numbers is the same as the factorial growth rate of n ! n! . This implies that (1) the Betti numbers are vastly larger than for the configuration space of n n ordered points in the plane, which have the factorial growth rate of j ! j! , and (2) every point in the feasible region is eventually in the homological liquid regime.

A typical phase diagram might look something like Figure

Motivated in part by these suggestions, in

For a point in parameter space—that is, we specify the shapes of the particles and their container, as well as the degree of homology—we say that the point is in

•

the homological solid regime if homology is trivial,

•

the homological gas regime if the inclusion map into the ambient configuration space of points induces an isomorphism on homology, and

The homological liquid regime is the most interesting regime topologically. In
[

1.1

Lemma 1.2

Let

Permutation families of independent cycles

We define a

\psi

\phi\colon\thinspace T^{j}\rightarrow C(n;p,q)

\psi\circ\phi\colon\thinspace T^{j}\rightarrow T^{j}

\log
of the denominator and
\log n_{i}!
is

\frac{\sum_{\ell}\log(n_{i,\ell}!)}{\log(n_{i}!)}\leq\frac{n_{i}\cdot\log\ (3(k+1)^{2})!}{\log\ n_{i}!}\rightarrow 0,

so we have

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\ \dim H_{j_{i}}[C(n_{i};p_{i},q_{i})]}{\log\ n_{i}!}\geq 1.

Combining this lower bound with the upper bound of Lemma
1.2
, we conclude that
\dim H_{j_{i}}[C(n_{i};p_{i},q_{i})]
has the factorial growth rate of
n_{i}!
.
∎

6.
Open problems

We close with a few suggestions for future directions.

(1)

Although some headway is made in
[
ABK+20
]
, we still do not have necessary and sufficient conditions
for
H_{j}[C(n;p,q)]\neq 0
. We do not believe that there is any nontrivial homology outside the feasible region
\mathcal{R}
. A slightly weaker necessary condition for nontrivial homology, simpler to state, is the following.

Conjecture 6.1
.

Suppose that
H_{j}[C(n;p,q)]\neq 0
. Let
x=\frac{n}{pq}
and
y=\frac{j}{pq}
. Then
y\leq\min\{x-\frac{8}{9}x^{2},1-x,\frac{1}{4}\}
.

For comparison, it is shown in
[
ABK+20
]
that
y\leq\min\{x,1-x,\frac{1}{3}\}
.

It seems to us that even if the conjecture holds, this necessary condition is probably not quite sufficient. For example, if
p=q=5
,
n=12
, and
j=6
, then
(x,y)
is in the feasible region, but we expect that
H_{j}[C(n;p,q)]=0
.

(2)

Describe the boundary between the homological liquid and gas regimes. In other words, give necessary and sufficient conditions on
n,j,p,q
for the natural inclusion map
i:C(n;p,q)\hookrightarrow C(n)
to induce an isomorphism
i_{*}:H_{j}[C(n;p,q)]\to H_{j}[C(n)]
.

Conjecture 6.2
.

Suppose that
p,q\geq j+2
. Then

i_{*}:H_{j}[C(n;p,q)]\to H_{j}[C(n)]

is an isomorphism if and only if
pq-n\geq(j+1)(j+2)
.

This conjecture is consistent with the data we computed for
n\leq 6
in
[
ABK+20
]
. Note that
pq-n
is the amount of “space” on the board, i.e., the total number of squares in the rectangle minus the number of sliding pieces.

(3)

Sharper estimates for the Betti numbers would be welcome. It may be that Theorem
1.1
can be improved to the following statement capturing the “second-order” asymptotics.

Conjecture 6.3
.

Let
(x,y)
be any point in the interior of
\mathcal{R}
. Let
n_{i},j_{i},p_{i},q_{i}
be sequences such that
p_{i}\leq q_{i}
,
p_{i}\rightarrow\infty
,
\frac{n_{i}}{p_{i}q_{i}}\rightarrow x
, and
\frac{j_{i}}{p_{i}q_{i}}\rightarrow y
. Then

\log\dim H_{j_{i}}[C(n_{i};p_{i},q_{i})]=n_{i}\log n_{i}+C_{x,y}n_{i}+o(n_{i}).

Here
C_{x,y}
is a constant that only depends on
x
and
y
.

(4)

Estimates for the Betti numbers of configuration spaces of hard disks in a box (rather than hard squares) would be interesting. These configuration spaces were studied earlier, for example in
[
BBK14
]
and
[
Alp17
]
, but so far little seems to be known. For instance, for
n
unit disks in a
p
by
q
rectangle, can we find values of
x=\frac{n}{pq}
and
y=\frac{j}{pq}
for which we can prove the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
?

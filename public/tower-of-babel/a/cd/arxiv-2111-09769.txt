UUITP-54/21 Nijenhuis tensor and invariant polynomials
F. Bonechi, J. Qiu, M. Tarlini, E. Viviani

Abstract
We discuss the diagonalization problem of the Nijenhuis tensor in a class of Poisson-Nijenhuis structures defined on compact hermitian symmetric spaces. We study its action on the ring of invariant polynomials of a Thimm chain of subalgebras. The existence of ϕ \phi\, - minimal representations defines a suitable basis of invariant polynomials that completely solves the diagonalization problem. We prove that such representations exist in the classical cases AIII, BDI, DIII and CI, and do not exist in the exceptional cases EIII and EVII. We discuss a second general construction that in these two cases computes partially the spectrum and hints at a different behavior with respect to the classical cases.



1
Introduction

The notion of symplectic groupoid was introduced by A. Weinstein (
[
16
]
) with the problem of quantization of the underlying Poisson manifold in mind. The basic idea is that a proper quantization must be compatible with the additional groupoid structure so that the output of the procedure is an algebra, regarded as the algebra of operators. If the quantization scheme is given by geometric quantization, both prequantization and polarization should be compatible with the groupoid structures. In
[
17
]
it has been shown that the natural notion of compatible prequantization is encoded in a central extension of the symplectic groupoid; moreover, if the groupoid is prequantizable as a symplectic manifold, then such compatible prequantization always exists and is unique.
In
[
8
]
, where this approach has been revived, a natural notion of
multiplicative polarization
has been introduced. Finding polarizations that make geometric quantization work is in general highly demanding, so that there are basically only two big classes of symplectic manifolds where this program can be completed: cotangent and Kahler manifolds. The corresponding polarizations are not in general compatible with the groupoid structure so that this ambitious picture is bit meagre in terms of concrete examples.

In
[
2
]
it was shown that a non degenerate
symplectic Poisson-Nijenhuis structures
(PN), a particular example of bihamiltonian geometry, is a source of such multiplicative polarizations. A symplectic
PN
structure on a smooth manifold
M
consists of a symplectic structure
\omega
and a Poisson structure
\pi
such that
\omega^{-1}
and
\pi
are compatible (
i.e.

[\pi,\omega^{-1}]=0
). Among the consequences of this property, the Nijenhuis tensor
N=\pi\circ\omega
has vanishing torsion and defines a hierarchy of compatible Poisson structures
N^{n}\circ\omega^{-1},n=0,1,\ldots
. When
N
has maximal rank, the hamiltonian forms

\Omega^{1}_{ham}=\{\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M),d\alpha=d_{N}\alpha=0\}\;,

(1)

where
d_{N}
is the algebroid differential,
define a lagrangian polarization that is in general very singular and so unfit for standard geometric quantization. As explained in
[
12
]
, the PN structure can be integrated to a
multiplicative PN structure

N_{\cal G}
on the symplectic groupoid
{\cal G}
integrating any Poisson structure of the hierarchy so that the polarization defined by
N_{\cal G}
is multiplicative too. The polarization defined by the hamiltonian forms is still singular but it allows one to define the topological groupoid of Lagrangian leaves and, upon regularity assumptions, the groupoid of
Bohr-Sommerfeld leaves
. One can then consider its convolution algebra as the quantization. This procedure was worked out in
[
3
]
for
{\mathbb{C}}P_{n}
where the
Bruhat-Poisson structure
(
[
14
]
)
\pi
is compatible with the Fubiny-Study symplectic form
\omega
. The
C^{*}
-algebra of
the groupoid of Bohr-Sommerfeld leaves coincides with the
C^{*}
-algebra of quantum homogeneous spaces, as shown in
[
15
]
. This is a particular case of a class of
PN
structures defined on compact hermitian symmetric spaces
M_{\phi}
, introduced in
[
10
]
,
where
\pi
is the Bruhat-Poisson structure and
\omega
the KKS symplectic form.

Motivated by the problem of quantization, the study of these PN geometries was started in
[
4
]
, where the diagonalization of the Nijenhuis tensor was solved for the classical cases (AIII, BI, DIII, CI).
Let
M_{\phi}=K/K_{\phi}
be a compact hermitian symmetric space, where
K
is a compact simple Lie group,
{\mathfrak{k}}
its Lie algebra and
K_{\phi}\subset K
the subgroup integrating
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}
the Lie subalgebra defined by the non compact root
\phi
. In particular it was proved that
i
) if
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of any solution
{\cal M}
of the matrix equation, called the
master equation
,

Nd{\cal M}=d{\cal M}^{-}{\cal M}+{\cal M}d{\cal M}^{+}+rd{\cal M}\,,\;\;\;d{\cal M}^{+}+d{\cal M}^{-}=kd{\cal M}\,

(2)

for some
k,r\in{\mathbb{C}}
then
\tilde{\lambda}=k\lambda+r
is an eigenvalue of
N
;
ii
) the
{\mathfrak{k}}
-moment map evaluated in a
\phi\,
-minimal representation (Definition
6.1
) satisfies the master equation. Such representations were provided for the classical cases. By a case by case analysis, it was proved that for each of the classical cases there exists a chain of subalgebras

{\mathfrak{k}}\supset{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}\supset{\mathfrak{k}}_{2}\ldots\supset{\mathfrak{k}}_{n}=\mathfrak{t}

where
\mathfrak{t}
is a Cartan subalgebra, such that the corresponding minors of the moment map in the
\phi\,
-minimal representation satisfy (
2
). We refer to it as Thimm chain of subalgebras (
[
6
]
) The exceptional
EIII,EVII
spaces were not considered.

In this paper we discuss a new approach to this problem.
We focus more on the structure of the Hamiltonian forms
\Omega^{1}_{ham}(M_{\phi})
rather than the eigenvalues of
N
and describe them in terms of invariant polynomials with respect to the Thimm chain. Notice that Nijenhuis eigenvalues are in general only continuous functions and their derivatives have singularities. Since the multiplicative polarization of the groupoid integrates the hamiltonian forms on
M_{\phi}
, it is important that we describe them in terms of global variables. Moreover, we start the study of the exceptional cases that were not discussed in
[
4
]
.

Let us describe in some details our results. We consider the subcomplex
(\Omega^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}_{pol},d)
of the de Rham complex generated by the invariant polynomials
S({\mathfrak{k}}_{1})^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}
of any subalgebra
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}
satisfying the compatibility condition (
11
) with the complex structure
J
of
{\mathfrak{k}}
. We compute in Proposition
4.2
the general formula (
13
) for
d_{N}p
where
p\in S({\mathfrak{k}}_{1})^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}
and we write the sufficient condition (
15
) that implies that
d_{N}p
is
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}
-basic.
This condition is satisfied if
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}={\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}
for all compact hermitian symmetric spaces so that
d_{N}p
is a
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}
-basic form (Proposition
8.3
).

We consider Thimm chains of subalgebras satisfying (
11
) in Section
5
.
We are able to prove that the sufficient condition (
15
) is satisfied for each subalgebra provided a
\phi\,
-minimal representation of
{\mathfrak{k}}
exists and we compute explicitly the action of
d_{N}
on a suitable basis of invariant polynomials (Theorem
6.3
). In particular, in this case
(\Omega^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}}_{pol},d_{N})
is a subcomplex of the Nijenhuis complex and the diagonalization is easily obtained (Corollary
6.4
). These
\phi\,
-minimal representations exist for the classical cases: in particular the diagonalization results of
[

]
are better understood in this more conceptual Lie theoretical framework.

We prove also that these representations do not exist for EIII and EVII (Proposition

Corollary

does not apply to these cases. We can still use the general result of Proposition

that implies that

are

-basic forms, where

for EIII and

for EVII. We give its explicit description in Section

is a subcomplex of the Nijenhuis complex for EIII. In the case of EVII a new phenomenon occurs that suggests that we have to consider a polynomial ring of invariants bigger than

. These results show that the exceptional cases behave quite differently with respect to the classical cases and open the way to the complete solution that we plan to address in a future paper.

Compact Hermitian symmetric spaces

We fix here notations and basic facts of compact hermitian symmetric spaces (see
[
18
,
11
]
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra and let
{\mathfrak{k}}
be its compact real form. We denote with
(,)
the Killing form. Let us fix a compact Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}
and let us denote by
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}}
its complexification. We denote with
\Delta
,

\Delta^{+}
and
\Pi
the roots, a choice of positive roots and the simple roots, respectively. For each
\alpha\in\Delta
we
denote with
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
the root space and we fix a root vector
e_{\alpha}
. We define
J:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
as

J(\mathfrak{t})=0\,,\;\;\;\;J(e_{\alpha})=i\ {{\rm sign}}(\alpha)e_{\alpha}\;.

(3)

We fix the normalization of the root vectors as
(e_{\alpha},e_{-\alpha})(\alpha,\alpha)=2
so that
[e_{\alpha},e_{-\alpha}]=h_{\alpha}
, where
h_{\alpha}
is the coroot vector of
\alpha
.

A simple root
\phi
is called non compact (see
[
18
]
) if the decomposition
of any
\alpha\in\Delta^{+}
along
\phi
is either
0
or
1
.
Roots (and positive roots) are decomposed accordingly as
\Delta=\Delta_{c}\coprod\Delta_{nc}
where
\Delta_{c}
are the compact roots and
\Delta_{nc}
the non compact ones. Since the sum of two compact roots, if it is a root, is compact, then

{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}=\mathfrak{t}\dot{+}_{\alpha\in\Delta_{c}^{+}}(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\dot{+}\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha})\cap{\mathfrak{k}}

(4)

is a subalgebra. Its center
{\mathfrak{z}}_{\phi}
is one dimensional and is generated by
\rho_{\phi}\in\mathfrak{t}
defined as
\alpha(\rho_{\phi})=0
for each
\alpha\in\Delta_{c}

and normalized as
\phi(\rho_{\phi})=i
. We get the decomposition

{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}={\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}^{\prime}\oplus{\mathfrak{z}}_{\phi}

(5)

with
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}^{\prime}
semisimple. We denote with
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}^{\perp}=\dot{+}_{\alpha\in\Delta_{nc}^{+}}(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\dot{+}\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha})\cap{\mathfrak{k}}
the orthogonal complement with respect to the Killing form. We have that

J|_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}^{\perp}}=[\rho_{\phi},-]\,,\;\;\;(J|_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}^{\perp}})^{2}=-1\;.

(6)

If
K
and
K_{\phi}\subset K
integrate
{\mathfrak{k}}
and
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}
, we denote with
M_{\phi}=K/K_{\phi}
the corresponding homogeneous space, that is a compact hermitian symmetric space. After identifying
{\mathfrak{k}}
with
{\mathfrak{k}}^{*}
,
M_{\phi}
can be realized as an adjoint orbit
M_{\phi}=K\rho_{\phi}K^{-1}
. We denote with
\omega
the Konstant-Kirillov-Souriau symplectic form and with
\mu:M_{\phi}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{k}}
the moment map for the hamiltonian
K
-action. We will denote with
X^{\sharp}
the fundamental vector field
associated to
X\in{\mathfrak{k}}
and with
v_{f}=\omega^{-1}df
the hamiltonian vector field of
f\in C^{\infty}(M_{\phi})
.

Let us consider the non compact real form

\mathfrak{g}_{0}={\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}\dot{+}i{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}^{\perp}

of
\mathfrak{g}
where the Cartan involution
\theta
is defined as
\theta|_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}}={\rm id}
and
\theta_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}^{\perp}}=-{\rm id}
. A
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is decomposed as
{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{t}_{0}\oplus i{\mathfrak{a}}
; it is maximally non compact when the dimension of
{\mathfrak{a}}
is as large as possible. A maximal abelian subalgebra

is unique up to

-conjugation (see
[
11
]
). We refer to
\dim{\mathfrak{a}}
as the rank of

.

AIII

{\mathfrak{k}}={\mathfrak{su}}(n+1)

,

.

BDI

and

,

3
Nijenhuis tensor on compact hermitian symmetric spaces

We collect here the basic facts that we need about Poisson-Nijenhuis geometry (see for instance
[
2
]
for more details) and we introduce the examples we are going to study. For background in Poisson-Lie groups and Poisson homogeneous spaces see for instance
[
13
]
. Let
M
be a smooth manifold,
\omega
be a symplectic form and
\pi
be a Poisson tensor on
M
that are compatible,
i.e.

[\omega^{-1},\pi]=0\;.

(7)

As a consequence the
(1,1)
-tensor
N=\pi\circ\omega
has vanishing torsion

T(N)(v_{1},v_{2})=N([Nv_{1},v_{2}]+[v_{1},Nv_{2}]-N[v_{1},v_{2}])-[Nv_{1},Nv_{2}]=0\;.

(8)

We remark that the PN geometry defined by (
7
) has been recently
generalized in terms of compatibility between the Nijenhuis tensor and Dirac structures, together with the lift to Lie groupoids, in
[
5
]
.

Let
\Omega(M)
denote the graded ring of differentiable forms and let
\iota_{N}:\Omega(M)\rightarrow\Omega(M)
the degree zero derivation defined as
\iota_{N}(f)=0
and
\iota_{N}(\nu)=N^{t}\nu
for all
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
\nu\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. As a consequence of the vanishing of the Nijenhuis torsion

d_{N}=[d,\iota_{N}]=d\iota_{N}-\iota_{N}d

(9)

squares to zero. Remark that
d_{N}f=Ndf
for
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
. We refer to the complex
\Omega_{N}=(\Omega(M),d_{N})
as the
Nijenhuis complex
. We have clearly that
[d,d_{N}]=0
; Hamiltonian forms defined in (
1
) are then those one forms that are closed with respect to both
d
and
d_{N}
.

The symplectic form
\omega:TM\rightarrow T^{*}M
defines an isomorphism of complexes between
(\Omega(M),d_{N})
and the complex
(\Gamma(\Lambda TM),d_{\pi})
computing the Lichnerowicz Poisson cohomology of
\pi
.

Let
K
a compact simple Lie group; we denote with
{\mathfrak{k}}
its Lie algebra and with
\mathfrak{t}
a choice of Cartan subalgebra. Let
\phi
be a non compact root,
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}
the corresponding Lie subalgebra and
K_{\phi}\subset K
the subgroup. Let
\rho_{\phi}
be the generator of the one dimensional center
{\mathfrak{z}}(k_{\phi})
normalized by
\phi(\rho_{\phi})=i
. The homogeneous space
K/K_{\phi}
can be realized as the adjoint orbit
M_{\phi}
of
\rho_{\phi}
, endowing it with the KKS symplectic form
\omega
. The moment map of the
K
-action is denoted

\mu:M_{\phi}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{k}}^{*}\equiv{\mathfrak{k}},

where the identification is done thanks to the Killing form.

The standard Poisson-Lie structure
\pi_{K}
on
K
is defined
for each
k\in K
as

\pi_{K}(k)=l_{k}(r)-r_{k}(r)

where
r
is the classical
r
-matrix

r=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}e_{\alpha}\wedge e_{-\alpha}\;.

The subgroup
K_{\phi}
is a Poisson subgroup and induces a Poisson structure
on
M_{\phi}
that we denote with
\pi
and we call the
Bruhat-Poisson structure
. The homogeneous
K
-action on
M_{\phi}
is a Poisson action,
i.e.
for each
X\in{\mathfrak{k}}
we have that

[X^{\sharp},\pi]=\delta(X)^{\sharp}\;,

where
\delta:{\mathfrak{k}}\rightarrow\Lambda^{2}{\mathfrak{k}}
is the cocycle encoding the dual Lie algebra on
{\mathfrak{k}}^{*}
(see Thm.2.6 in
[
14
]
).

The compatibility between the KKS symplectic from and the Bruhat-Poisson structure
\pi
has been proven in
[
10
]
.

We skip all details concerning the Bruhat-Poisson structure, that can be found for instance in
[
14
]
. In this paper we will only need the following formula that has been proven in Theorem 6.1 of
[
4
]

d_{N}\mu=d\mu-[J(d\mu),\mu]\;.

(10)

The following Lemma is a consequence of the fact that the
K
-action on
M_{\phi}
is a Poisson action. Let
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}
any Lie subalgebra and let
(\Omega^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},d)
be the complex of
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}
invariant forms.

Lemma 3.1
.

(\Omega^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},d_{N})
is a subcomplex of the Nijenhuis complex.

Proof
. Since the
{\mathfrak{k}}
-action (and then
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}
) is hamiltonian, the cochain map
\omega:(\Omega,d_{N})\rightarrow(\Gamma(\Lambda TM),d_{\pi})
exchanges the
{\mathfrak{k}}
-actions via Lie derivatives on forms and multivector fields. So it is enough to prove the statement on the LP complex. Indeed, for each
X\in{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}
and
A\in\Gamma(\Lambda TM)
such that
L_{X^{\sharp}}(A)=0
we have

L_{X^{\sharp}}(d_{\pi}(A))=[X^{\sharp},[\pi,A]]=[[X^{\sharp},\pi],A]=[\delta(X)^{\sharp},A]=0\,.

∎

4
Nijenhuis operator and invariant polynomials

We saw in Lemma
3.1
that invariant forms with respect to
{\mathfrak{k}}
(and to any subalgebra) form a subcomplex of the Nijenhuis complex. Here we ask the same question about the subcomplex generated by invariant polynomials.

We limit ourselves to a class of subalgebras that is compatible with the complex structure
J
defined in (
3
). Let
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}
be a Lie subalgebra and

be the subgroup integrating it.
It is clear that
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}^{\perp}
is invariant under the adjoint action of
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}
. Let us ask now that both
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}^{\perp}
are

-invariant and that
for each
X\in{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}

[\operatorname{ad}_{X},J|_{k_{1}^{\perp}}]=0\;.

(11)

We denote with

the orthogonal projections to
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}^{\perp}

Lemma 4.1
.

If
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}
satisfies the conditions (
11
) then, for each
\xi,\eta\in{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}^{\perp}

{\rm pr}_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}([J\xi,\eta]+[\xi,J\eta])=0

Proof
. For each

we have that

(X,[J\xi,\eta]+[\xi,J\eta])=([X,J\xi],\eta)+([X,\xi],J\eta)=(J[X,\xi],\eta)+([X,\xi],J\eta)=0

where in the second equality we used (
11
) and in the last one the antisymmetry of

with respect to the Killing form. ∎

We denote with
\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}=M_{\phi}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}
the moment map and let

{\mathfrak{k}}
-moment map given by

-basic forms and with

.
We would like to study the conditions under which

Proposition 4.2
.

i
)

If

As a consequence of (

\displaystyle d_{N}p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\tilde{p}(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},\dots,d_{N}\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},\ldots,\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}})=r\tilde{p}(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},\dots,\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},d_{N}\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}})

\displaystyle dp-r\tilde{p}(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},\dots,\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},[Jd\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}]+dA_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}})\;.

\iota_{X^{\sharp}}(d_{N}p)=-r\tilde{p}(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},\dots,\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},[X,A_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}])=-r\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\tilde{p}(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},\ldots,[A_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}],\ldots,X)=0.

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let

\{q,p\}_{\pi}=0\;.

(17)

Let us suppose that (

Proof
. Let

be the hamiltonian vector field of

\displaystyle\{q,p\}_{\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iota_{v_{q}}(d_{N}p)=-r\tilde{p}(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},\ldots,\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},v_{q}(A_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}))=-r\tilde{p}(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},\ldots,\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},[X_{q},A_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\tilde{p}(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}},\ldots[X_{q},\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}],\ldots A_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}})

where

5
The Thimm chain of subalgebras

We recall here the Thimm method for constructing integrable models (see
[
6
]
for details). Let us consider
{\mathfrak{k}}\supset{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}\supset{\mathfrak{k}}_{2}
nested Lie subalgebras and let
q\in S({\mathfrak{k}}_{1})^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}
and
p\in S({\mathfrak{k}}_{2})^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{2}}
. Since
\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{2}}=p_{21}\circ\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}
, where
p_{21}:{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}^{*}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{k}}_{2}^{*}
we have that

\{\mu^{*}_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{2}}p,\mu^{*}_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}q\}=\{\mu^{*}_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}p_{21}^{*}p,\mu^{*}_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}q\}=\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}^{*}\{p_{21}^{*}p,q\}_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}^{*}}=0

since
S^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}({\mathfrak{k}}_{1})\subset C^{\infty}({\mathfrak{k}}_{1}^{*})
is the centre of the Poisson algebra. Let us consider now a chain of Lie subalgebras

{\mathfrak{k}}\equiv{\mathfrak{k}}_{0}\supset{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}\supset\ldots{\mathfrak{k}}_{n}\;

and let
K_{i}\subset K
the corresponding subgroups. Let us denote with

S({\mathfrak{k}}\supset{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}\ldots\supset{\mathfrak{k}}_{n})=\cup_{i}S({\mathfrak{k}}_{i})^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}}

(20)

the union of invariant polynomials of any subalgebra of the chain. By the above observation, all these polynomials are in involution.

Let us discuss if they are independent and so form an integrable model. The following two properties are equivalent:
i
) the ring of invariant functions
C^{\infty}(M)^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}}
is abelian with respect to the Poisson bracket;
ii
) for each coadjoint orbit
O\subset{\mathfrak{k}}^{*}
the symplectic reduction
\mu_{\mathfrak{k}}^{-1}(O)/K
is a point. If one of the above condition (and then both) is true then the
K
-action is said to be
multiplicity free
(
[
7
]
). If for each
K_{i}
- orbit
O\subset M
, the action of
K_{i-1}
on
O
is multiplicitely free then the hamiltonians in (
20
) define an integrable model.

Let us consider now the case
M=M_{\phi}
; we want to see the conditions under which the hamiltonians (
20
) commute also with respect to the Poisson structure
\pi
. Let us choose a non compact root
\phi_{1}
of
{\mathfrak{k}}
and let
\rho_{1}
the normalized generator of the one dimensional centre
{\mathfrak{z}}_{1}
of
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{1}}
.
In general this root
\phi_{1}
can be different from
\phi
involved in the definition of the hermitian symmetric space.

Let
{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}={\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{1}}={\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{1}}^{\prime}\oplus{\mathfrak{z}}_{1}
with
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{1}}^{\prime}
being simple. Let
\phi_{2}
be a non compact root of
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{1}}^{\prime}
with
\rho_{2}
the normalized generator of the one dimensional center
{\mathfrak{z}}_{2}
of
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{2}}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{1}}^{\prime}
. Let
{\mathfrak{k}}_{2}={\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{2}}\oplus{\mathfrak{z}}_{1}={\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{2}}^{\prime}\oplus{\mathfrak{z}}_{2}\oplus{\mathfrak{z}}_{1}
. We get the decomposition

{\mathfrak{k}}={\mathfrak{k}}_{2}\dot{+}{\mathfrak{k}}_{2}^{\perp}={\mathfrak{k}}_{2}\dot{+}({\mathfrak{k}}_{2}^{\perp}\cap{\mathfrak{k}}_{1})\dot{+}{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}^{\perp}={\mathfrak{k}}_{2}\dot{+}({\mathfrak{k}}_{2}^{\perp}\cap{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{1}}^{\prime})\dot{+}{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}^{\perp}\;,

where the third equality follows because we included
{\mathfrak{z}}_{1}
in the definition of
{\mathfrak{k}}_{2}
. We remark that the perpendicular is always taken in
{\mathfrak{k}}
.
We can iterate the procedure and choose
\phi_{i}
non compact root of
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{i-1}}^{\prime}
with
\rho_{i}
the normalized generator of the centre
{\mathfrak{z}}_{i}
of

{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{i}}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{i-1}^{\prime}}
and define

{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}={\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{i}}\oplus{\mathfrak{z}}_{i-1}\oplus\ldots\oplus{\mathfrak{z}}_{1}\;.

(21)

We get the
i-th
decomposition of
{\mathfrak{k}}

{\mathfrak{k}}={\mathfrak{k}}_{i}\dot{+}({\mathfrak{k}}_{i}^{\perp}\cap{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{i-1}}^{\prime})\dot{+}{\mathfrak{k}}_{i-1}^{\perp}={\mathfrak{k}}_{i}\dot{+}({\mathfrak{k}}_{i}^{\perp}\cap{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{i-1}}^{\prime})\dot{+}({\mathfrak{k}}_{i-1}^{\perp}\cap{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{i-2}}^{\prime})\ldots\dot{+}{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}^{\perp}\;.

(22)

We remark that
J
acts on each addend
{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}^{\perp}\cap{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{i-1}^{\prime}}
of (
22
) as
\operatorname{ad}_{\rho_{i}}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Every subalgebra
{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}
satisfies condition (
11
).

Proof
. By construction the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}
is included in
{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}
for each
i
. Moreover, given any root
\alpha
, either the root vector
e_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}
or
e_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}^{\perp}
. This implies that
{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}
and
{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}^{\perp}
are
J
-invariant. Equation (
11
) is straightforward if
X\in\mathfrak{t}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}
. Let
X=e_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}
and
e_{\beta}\in{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}^{\perp}
be two root vectors.
If
\alpha+\beta
is not a root then both terms are zero; if
\alpha+\beta
is a root then it is non compact and it will be positive if and only if
\beta
is positive so that

[X,J(e_{\beta})]=J[X,e_{\beta}]
. ∎

We will see in the examples that, for each simple compact Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{k}}
, it is possible to find such a chain that ends with
{\mathfrak{k}}_{n}=\mathfrak{t}
the Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{k}}
. Such a choice will not be unique. We call such a chain

{\mathfrak{k}}\supset{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}\supset{\mathfrak{k}}_{2}\ldots\supset{\mathfrak{k}}_{n}=\mathfrak{t}

(23)

a Thimm chain.

6
The
\phi\,
-minimal representations

Let
\phi
be a non compact root of the simple Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{k}}
and let
\rho_{\phi}
the normalized generator of the center
{\mathfrak{z}}
of
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}
. Let
R_{\Lambda}:{\mathfrak{k}}\rightarrow{\rm End}(V(\Lambda))
be an irreducible representation of highest weight
\Lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
. It is easy to see that
V(\Lambda)
decomposes in eigenspaces of
\rho_{\phi}
as

V(\Lambda)=\oplus_{\ell\geq 0}V(\Lambda)_{\ell}

(24)

where
V(\Lambda)_{\ell}
are
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}
-invariant,
\rho_{\phi}|_{V(\Lambda)_{\ell}}=\Lambda_{\phi}-i\,\ell
with
\Lambda_{\phi}\equiv\Lambda(\rho_{\phi})
and
e_{\alpha}:V(\Lambda)_{\ell}\rightarrow V(\Lambda)_{\ell-1}
for every non compact positive root
\alpha
.

Definition 6.1
.

We say that
R_{\Lambda}
is
\phi\,
-minimal if the sum in (
24
) runs over
\ell=0,1
and
V(\Lambda)_{0}
is irreducible. In this case we denote
V_{+}\equiv V(\Lambda)_{0}
and
V_{-}\equiv V(\Lambda)_{1}
.

Let now
\phi
be the noncompact root defining the Hermitian symmetric space
M_{\phi}
and let us suppose that
V(\Lambda)
is
\phi\,
-minimal. Since
M_{\phi}
is the adjoint orbit of
\rho_{\phi}
, the moment map
\mu_{\Lambda}=R_{\Lambda}(\mu)
in the representation

R_{\Lambda}
satisfies then

(\mu_{\Lambda}-\Lambda_{\phi})(\mu_{\Lambda}-\Lambda_{\phi}+i)=\mu_{\Lambda}^{2}-(2\Lambda_{\phi}-i)\mu_{\Lambda}+\Lambda_{\phi}(\Lambda_{\phi}-i)=0\;.

(25)

Let us consider now the Thimm chain (
23
) and let us suppose that
V(\Lambda)
is also
\phi_{1}
-minimal
as
V(\Lambda)=V_{+}^{1}\oplus V_{-}^{1}
. We can go on along the Thimm chain (
23
) and let us assume that
V_{+}^{1}
is
\phi_{2}
-minimal as

V_{+}^{1}=V_{+}^{2}\oplus V_{-}^{2}
and so on. At level
i
, we assume that
V_{+}^{i-1}
is
\phi_{i}
-minimal so that
V^{i-1}_{+}=V^{i}_{+}\oplus V^{i}_{-}
. At each level
i
, we get the decomposition of
V
into
{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}
-representation as

V(\Lambda)=W^{i}_{+}\oplus W^{i}_{-}

(26)

where
W^{i}_{+}=V^{i}_{+}
and
W^{i}_{-}=V^{i}_{-}\oplus V^{i-1}_{-}\ldots\oplus V^{1}_{-}
. If these conditions are satisfied we say that the representation
R_{\Lambda}
is minimal with respect to the chain (
23
).

Lemma 6.2
.

If the representation
R_{\Lambda}
is minimal with respect to the Thimm chain (
23
) then each
\xi\in{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}^{\perp}
decomposes with respect to (
26
) as

R_{\Lambda}(\xi)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\nu\cr-\nu^{\dagger}&\lambda\end{array}\right)\,,\;\;\;R_{\Lambda}(J(\xi))=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&i\nu\cr i\nu^{\dagger}&J(\lambda)\end{array}\right)

Proof
.
Let us consider the decomposition (
26
) of
V
in
{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}
representations. It is clear that
{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}^{\perp}:W_{+}^{i}\rightarrow W_{-}^{i}
. Indeed
if
\xi\in{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}^{\perp}\cap{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{i-1}}^{\prime}
then
\xi:W_{+}^{i}=V_{+}^{i}\rightarrow V_{-}^{i}\subset W^{i}_{-}
; if
\xi\in{\mathfrak{k}}_{i-1}^{\perp}
then
\xi:W_{+}^{i}=V_{+}^{i}\subset V_{+}^{i-1}\rightarrow V_{-}^{i-1}\subset W^{i}_{-}
. Let
\alpha
be a positive root such that
e_{\alpha}\in({\mathfrak{k}}_{i}^{\perp}\cap{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi_{i-1}}^{\prime})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
, then
e_{\alpha}|_{W_{+}^{i}}=e_{\alpha}|_{V_{+}^{i}}=0
; if

then
e_{\alpha}|_{W_{+}^{i}}=e_{\alpha}|_{V_{+}^{i}}=(e_{\alpha}|_{V^{i-1}_{+}})|_{V_{+}^{i}}=0
. ∎

Let us consider now the the ring of
{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}
-invariant polynomials generated by the traces in the
W^{i}_{+}
representation: let us define

I_{r}^{(i)}(X)=\frac{i^{r}}{r}\textrm{Tr}_{W^{i}_{+}}(X^{r})\;.

(27)

Theorem 6.3
.

We compute

d_{N}I_{r}^{(i)}=-2i\Lambda_{\phi}dI_{r}^{(i)}+2dI_{r+1}^{(i)}\;.

(28)

Proof
. We then have to compute (
14
) in the representation
W^{i}_{+}
. If we write

R_{\Lambda}(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}R_{W^{i}_{+}}(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}})&\nu_{i}\cr-\nu_{i}^{\dagger}&R_{W^{i}_{-}}(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}})+\lambda_{i}\end{array}\right)\,,\;\;\;

then by using Lemma

defined in (

From (

R_{W^{i}_{+}}(A_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}})=-iR_{W^{i}_{+}}(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}})^{2}+(2i\Lambda_{\phi}+1)R_{W^{i}_{+}}(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}})-\Lambda_{\phi}(1+i\Lambda_{\phi})\;.

(29)

Let us now insert it in (

and get

\displaystyle d_{N}I_{r}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle dI_{r}^{(i)}+r\tilde{I_{r}}^{(i)}\left(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}},\ldots,id(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}}^{2})-(2i\Lambda_{\phi}+1)d\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}}\right)

\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}=(v_{\lambda},h_{\alpha_{i}}h_{\alpha_{j}}v_{\lambda})=(v_{\lambda},[e_{\alpha_{i}},e_{-\alpha_{i}}][e_{\alpha_{j}},e_{-\alpha_{j}}]v_{\lambda})\;,

where
(,)
here denotes the scalar product that makes
V(\Lambda)
unitary.
Since
\alpha_{i}
are non compact roots, in a
\phi\,
-minimal representation
e_{\alpha_{i}}e_{\alpha_{j}}=0
; since
\alpha_{i}\in P_{\phi}
are mutually orthogonal then
[e_{\pm\alpha_{i}},e_{\mp\alpha_{j}}]=0
for
i\not=j
. As a consequence
\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}=0
. ∎

We then conclude that the non zero eigenvalues of
\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}}
in the representation
V_{+}
are
\{\Lambda_{\phi}+if_{i}\}_{i=1}^{{\rm rk}(M_{\phi})}
. By applying the formula of Corollary
6.4

ii
) we see that
-2f_{i}
is a Nijenhuis eigenvalue,
i.e.
(

) holds.

9
A formula for
S({\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi})^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}}
on
EIII
and
EVII

We are going to discuss here the exceptional cases. We saw in the previous section that minimal representations do not exist for
{\mathfrak{e}}_{6}
and
{\mathfrak{e}}_{7}
so that we cannot apply Corollary
6.4
to the Thimm chains discussed in Section
5
. For
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}
, that is the first subalgebra of the chain, we can apply Proposition
8.3
: we are going to compute the explicit form of the subcomplex of the Nijenhuis complex generated by
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}
-invariant polynomials.

9.1
EIII

Let us consider
M_{\phi}=E_{6}/SO(10)\times SO(2)
. We are going to give the explicit formulas for the action of the Nijenhuis tensor on
{\mathfrak{so}}(10)\oplus{\mathfrak{so}}(2)
-invariant polynomials.

By using the description of positive roots and non compact positive roots of
{\mathfrak{e}}_{6}
given in (
49
) and (
51
) respectively, we get that

P_{\phi}=\left\{\phi=\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon-\sum_{i=1}^{5}\epsilon_{i}),\psi=\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon+\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3}+\epsilon_{4}-\epsilon_{5})\right\}\subset\Delta^{+}_{nc}

(62)

are orthogonal noncompact roots. Since
{\rm rk}(M_{\phi})=2
, this subset is maximal. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{P_{\phi}}
be the spaces spanned by
\{X_{\phi}=i(e_{\phi}+e_{-\phi}),X_{\psi}=i(e_{\psi}+e_{-\psi})\}
, according to Section
8
. Thus, formulas (
55
) and (
56
) are written as

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}=\rho_{\phi}+f_{\phi}\,ih_{\phi}+f_{\psi}\,ih_{\psi},

(63)

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}=g_{\phi}JX_{\phi}+g_{\psi}JX_{\psi}.

Since
{\rm rk}(M_{\phi})=2
the formulas computed in (
59
) completely describe the action of
d_{N}
on
{\mathfrak{so}}(10)\oplus{\mathfrak{so}}(2)
-invariant polynomials. Indeed from (
62
) we have that
c_{\phi}=i(h_{\alpha_{\phi}},\rho_{\phi})=c_{\psi}=i(h_{\alpha_{\psi}},\rho_{\phi})=-1
while
(\rho_{\phi},\rho_{\phi})=-\frac{4}{3}
. Then,
p_{n}(f)=-f_{\phi}^{n}-f_{\psi}^{n}
and we have the following polynomial relation

p_{3}=-\frac{3}{2}p_{1}p_{2}-\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{3}.

From (
60
) we get that

d_{N}p_{1}=-dp_{2}\,,\;\;\;\;d_{N}p_{2}=\frac{4}{6}d\left(3p_{1}p_{2}+p_{1}^{3}\right)\;

so that
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
generate the subcomplex
(\Omega^{{\mathfrak{so}}(10)\oplus{\mathfrak{so}}(2)}_{pol},d_{N})
. We can conclude that

d_{N}f_{\phi}=-2f_{\phi}df_{\phi}\,,\;\;\;\;d_{N}f_{\psi}=-2f_{\psi}df_{\psi}\,,

i.e.

-2f_{\phi}
and
-2f_{\psi}
are Nijenhuis eigenvalues.

9.2
EVII

The case
M_{\phi}=E_{7}/E_{6}\times SO(2)
presents a new feature. Indeed, since
{\rm rk}(M_{\phi})=3
we need three generators to describe
\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}}^{*}(S({\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}))^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}}
. Two of them can be
I_{1,0}=(\rho_{\phi},\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}})
and
I_{2,0}=(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}},\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}})
, we have to look for the third one. The general formulas (
59
) involve also
I_{1,1}=(\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}},A_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}})
which, by looking at the expression given in (
59
),
as a cubic polynomials in the
f
’s, is clearly independent. The problem is now to understand if
I_{1,1}\in\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}}^{*}(S({\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi})^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}})
. In the previous case of
EIII
, since the rank was
2
,
I_{1,1}
, as a polynomial of (maximal) degree
3
in two variables, was clearly generated by
I_{1,0}
and
I_{2,0}
. The next independent generator in
S({\mathfrak{e}}_{6})^{{\mathfrak{e}}_{6}}
appears in degree
5
; we can write it as
I_{5,0}=\textrm{Tr}_{V_{\Lambda}}\mu_{{\mathfrak{e}}_{6}}^{5}
for some representation
V_{\Lambda}
of
{\mathfrak{e}}_{6}
; it is a polynomial of degree
5
in the
f
-variables. By counting the
f
-degree, we easily conclude that
I_{1,1}
cannot be polynomially generated by
\{I_{1,0},I_{2,0},I_{5,0}\}
and
I_{5,0}
is a polynomial function of
\{I_{1,0},I_{2,0},I_{1,1}\}
. By inverting this relation,
I_{1,1}
can be expressed as a non polynomial function of the moment map,
i.e.

I_{1,1}\in\mu_{{\mathfrak{e}}_{6}}^{*}(C^{\infty}({\mathfrak{e}}_{6}^{*})^{{\mathfrak{e}}_{6}})
. We can then conclude that if we want to preserve the polynomial structure, we are forced to deal with the bigger ring that includes also polynomials in the variables
A_{{\mathfrak{e}}_{6}}
. More importantly, the computation of
d_{N}I_{1,1}
requires an extension of (
13
) to this bigger ring of invariants.

10
Conclusions

In this paper we discussed two different approaches to the problem of diagonalizing the Nijenhuis tensor
N
on the hermitian symmetric spaces
M_{\phi}=K/K_{\phi}
. The first one is based on the existence of a special representation of
{\mathfrak{k}}
that we call
\phi\,
-minimal: in this case we can define a Thimm chain of subalgebras
{\mathfrak{k}}\supset{\mathfrak{k}}_{1}\ldots\supset{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}\ldots
together with the set of generators
I_{r}
of the ring of invariant polynomials
S({\mathfrak{k}}_{i})^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{i}}
that make the diagonalization problem easily solved.

The second construction is based on a local parametrization around
\rho_{\phi}\in M_{\phi}
. This method is valid for all cases, including the exceptional ones, and produces the subset of the spectrum related to the subalgebra
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}
. It detects a different behavior of the EVII case: in fact the ring of invariant polynomials
S({\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi})^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}}
is not preserved by the algebroid differential
d_{N}
and the bigger ring of polynomials of
\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}}
and
A_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}}
must be taken into consideration. Formula (
13
) must be then generalized. Moreover, preliminary computations for the second subalgebra
{\mathfrak{k}}_{2}={\mathfrak{so}}(8)\oplus{\mathfrak{so}}(2)
in the Thimm chain of
EIII
show that the sufficient condition
[\mu_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{2}},A_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{2}}]=0
in (
15
) is not satisfied. These facts indicate that the cases
EIII
and
EVII
have an exceptional behavior. It is possible that it will be convenient to relate it to the fact that
S({\mathfrak{k}})^{\mathfrak{k}}\not=S({\mathfrak{a}}_{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}^{\perp}})^{{\mathfrak{k}}_{\phi}}
for the exceptional cases proved by Helgason in
[
9
]
. This problem will be addressed in a future publication.

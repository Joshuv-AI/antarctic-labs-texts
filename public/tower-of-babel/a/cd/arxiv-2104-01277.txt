Abstract
. We develop new local T ​ 1 T1 theorems to characterize Calderón-Zygmund operators that extend boundedly or compactly on L p ​ ( ℝ n , μ ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu) with μ \mu a measure of power growth. The results, whose proofs do not require random grids, allow the use of a countable collection of testing functions. As a corollary, we describe the measures μ \mu of the complex plane for which the Cauchy integral defines a compact operator on L p ​ ( ℂ , μ ) L^{p}(\mathbb{C},\mu) .

1.
Introduction

The
T1
theorem characterizes boundedness of Calderón-Zygmund operators
T
in terms of the functions

T1
and
T^{*}1
.
On the other hand, the local
T1
theorem attains similar characterization
using the action of
T
and
T^{*}

over a system of indicator functions
(\chi_{Q})_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}
of all cubes
with edges parallel to the coordinate axes.

The idea of a local
T1
theorem was first introduced in 1990 by M. Christ
[
3
]

in connection with the geometric description of removable compact sets for bounded analytic functions (known as Pailenvé’s problem).
His motivation was that, in principle,
finding a system of local testing functions should be easier than identifying a single function over which the operator behaves well globally.
This approach was shown to be right
at the turn of the century
when
F. Nazarov, S. Treil and A. Volberg proved
the first local
T1
and
Tb
theorems for non-doubling measures

[
18
]
and
[
20
]

(see also
[
19
]
and
[
21
]
).

Since then,
research work on this subject has been continuously growing with special focus on
more general criteria of boundedness (
[
1
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
13
]
), variants that apply to new settings (
[
12
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
15
]
) and applications to PDEs (
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
).
The articles

[
7
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
17
]

and the books
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
23
]

provide detailed accounts of the evolution of this theory.

Few years ago, papers
[
24
]
,
[
25
]

presented global
T1
and
Tb
theorems characterizing compactness of Calderón-Zygmund operators.
These results can be used to prove
compactness of many double layer potential operators
(see
[
25
]
). In turn, this allows
via Fredholm Theory (see
[
6
]
) to deduce invertibility of the Laplacian on a large class of domains.
Following this line of research, the current paper
introduces a local
T1
Theorem for non-doubling measures, that is, a
criterion of boundedness and compactness that relies on the action of the operator over
a family of
indicator functions of dyadic cubes
(Theorems
4.1
and

4.2
).

All known proofs of
T1
theorems
on non-doubling spaces employ randomization methods
to deal with the fact that, when
using the kernel decay, estimates of the dual pair

\langle T\chi_{I},\chi_{J}\rangle

grow like the logarithms of both the distance between the cubes
I,J
and the ratio between their side lengths. To overcome this issue, the method considers grids of general cubes rather than only the grid of dyadic cubes.
In the space of all these grids, cubes with close boundaries and
very different sizes are rare and thus, they can be assigned a small probability. Then, by averaging among all grids, the contribution of such cubes can be made arbitrarily small.
The costs of this method include
a delicate technique of decomposition called surgery and
the requirement by hypothesis of a non-contable family of testing functions (one per each cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
).

But there is a catch. Once randomization is applied and the proof of boundedness is complete, the
estimate
|\langle T\chi_{I},\chi_{J}\rangle|\leq\|T\|\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}
shows that the dual pair with any two cubes
never blows up.
Random cubes were used to tame ghost singularities that
did not exist.

We introduce a new
approach that does not use random grids and so, it
avoids many technicalities of randomization.
The method
allows the use of
a countable family of testing functions.

Regarding compactness, we provide an application to the Cauchy integral operator with a non-doubling measure, which is defined by

C_{\mu}f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{f(w)}{w-z}\,d\mu(w).

It is known that if the measure is defined by the indicator function of a unit line segment
S
, that is

d\mu_{S}=\chi_{S}d\mathcal{H}^{1}
with
\mathcal{H}^{1}
the one-dimensional Hausdorff measure in
\mathbb{C}
,
then
C_{\mu_{S}}

is bounded but not compact on

L^{2}(\mu_{S})
.
On the other hand, if the measure is defined by the indicator function of a unit square
Q
, that is

d\mu_{Q}=\chi_{Q}dm
with
m
the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{C}
, then
C_{\mu_{Q}}
is compact on

L^{2}(\mu_{Q})
.
Theorem
4.4
describes for which measures
\mu
of the complex plane the Cauchy integral operator
C_{\mu}
can be compactly extended on

L^{2}(\mu)
.

The outline of the paper is as follows. In section 2, 3, and 4 we introduce some notation,
define the class of operators under study and state
the main results respectively. In section 5, we study a smooth truncation of the kernel, while
in section 6 we describe the Haar wavelet system. Section 7 focuses on obtaining estimates for the action of the operator over Haar wavelets.
In section 8 we deal with the paraproducts and
section 9 is devoted to the proof of the main result Theorem
4.2
.

3.
Measure, kernel, and operator.

3.1.
Non-homogeneous measure

We describe the class of measures for which the theory applies.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which without loss of generality we assume to be positive.

We say that
\mu
has power growth if there is
0<\alpha\leq n
such that

\mu(I)\lesssim\ell(I)^{\alpha}
for
all
I\in\mathcal{C}
.

We now define three densities of the measure:
for
I\in\mathcal{C}
, let

\rho(I)=\frac{\mu(I)}{\ell(I)^{\alpha}},

\displaystyle{\rho_{\rm in}(I)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in I\\
0<\lambda<\ell(I)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(I\cap B(t,\lambda))}{\lambda^{\alpha}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in I\\
\lambda>0\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(I\cap B(t,\lambda))}{\lambda^{\alpha}}}

where
B(t,\lambda)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}/|t-x|<\lambda\}
,
and given
0<\delta\leq 1
,

\rho_{\rm out}(I)=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\mu(mI)}{\ell(mI)^{\alpha}}\frac{1}{m^{\frac{\delta}{2}+1}}.

With them, we denote

(2)

\rho_{\mu}(I)=\rho_{\rm in}(I)+\rho_{\rm out}(I).

Remark 3.2
.

In the definition of
\rho_{\rm in}
one can substitute the balls
B(t,\lambda)
by dyadic cubes just by taking the smallest
Q\in\mathcal{D}(I)
with
B(t,\lambda)\subset Q
.

The sum in the definition of
\rho_{\rm out}
is comparable to

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(tI)}{\ell(tI)^{\alpha}}\frac{dt}{t^{\frac{\delta}{2}+1}}\approx\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-k\frac{\delta}{2}}\frac{\mu(2^{k}I)}{\ell(2^{k}I)^{\alpha}}.

If
\mu
satisfies the power growth for
n
dimensional cubes on
\mathcal{D}
, then it satisfies the same power growth for
r
dimensional cubes on
\mathcal{D}_{\partial}^{r}
. To show this,
we note that each
r
dimensional dyadic cube
I\in\mathcal{D}_{\partial}^{r}
is in the border of an
n
-dimensional dyadic cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}
with the same side length and then

\mu(I)\leq\mu(Q)\lesssim\ell(Q)^{\alpha}=\ell(I)^{\alpha}
.

3.2.
Compact Calderón-Zygmund kernel and its associated operator

We now describe the class of kernels and operators for which the theory applies.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mu
be a positive Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with power growth
0<\alpha\leq n
.

A function
K:(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:t=x\}\to\mathbb{C}
is a Calderón-Zgymund kernel if
it is bounded on compact sets of its domain
and
there exist
0<\delta\leq 1

and bounded functions
L,S,D:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)

satisfying

(3)

|K(t,x)-K(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\lesssim\Big(\frac{(|t-t^{\prime}|+|x-x^{\prime}|)}{|t-x|}\Big)^{\delta}\frac{F(t,x)}{|t-x|^{\alpha}},

with
F(t,x)=L(|t-x|)S(|t-x|)D(|t+x|)
,
whenever
2(|t-t^{\prime}|+|x-x^{\prime}|)<|t-x|
.

We
say that
Kd\mu\times d\mu
is a compact Calderón-Zygmund kernel
if (
3
) holds
and

(4)

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell(I)\rightarrow\infty}L(\ell(I))\rho_{\mu}(I)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\ell(I)\rightarrow 0}S(\ell(I))\rho_{\mu}(I)

\displaystyle=\lim_{{\rm\,rdist}(I,\mathbb{B})\rightarrow\infty}D({\rm\,rdist}(I,\mathbb{B}))\rho_{\mu}(I)=0.

Remark 3.4
.

Since a dilation of a function satisfying any of the limits in (
4
) satisfies the same limit, namely

{\mathcal{D}}_{\lambda}(L\rho_{\mu})(a)=L(\lambda^{-1}a)\rho_{\mu}(\lambda^{-1}a)
also satisfies the first limit, we omit universal constants in the argument of the functions.

Notation 3.5
.

Given three cubes
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}\in\mathcal{C}
,
we denote

F(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})=L(\ell(I_{1}))S(\ell(I_{2}))D({\rm\,rdist}(I_{3},\mathbb{B}))

and
F(I)=F(I,I,I)
.
Then the limits in (
4
) can be written as

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{I\in\mathcal{\mathcal{}}D_{M}^{c}}F(I)\rho_{\mu}(I)=0.

Given
0<\delta\leq 1
, we define

(5)

\tilde{D}(I)=\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-k\frac{\delta}{2}}D({\rm\,rdist}(2^{k}I,\mathbb{B})).

We note that if
D
satisfies (
4
), then by Lebesgue’s Domination Theorem so does
\tilde{D}
.

In
[
24
]
it was proved, only for the one-dimensional case and when
\alpha=n=1
, that the smoothness condition
(
3
) and the mild
assumption
\lim_{|t-x|\rightarrow\infty}K(t,x)=0

imply the following pointwise decay condition:

(6)

|K(t,x)|\lesssim\frac{F(t,x)}{|t-x|^{\alpha}},

with
F(t,x)=L(|t-x|)S(|t-x|)D(|t+x|)
.
This is also the case when

F\equiv 1
.

Definition 3.6
.

A linear operator
T
is associated with
a Calderón-Zygmund kernel

if the following representation holds

Tf(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(t)K(t,x)\,d\mu(t)

By

the double integral is absolutely convergent with

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|f(t)K(t,x)\,|d\mu(t)\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\frac{\mu({\rm supp\,}f)}{{\rm dist}(x,{\rm supp\,}f)^{\alpha}}.

5.
The truncated operators

In this section
we define and study the properties of a particular smooth truncation of Calderón-Zygmund operators.
We start the section with a technical result.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

I\in\mathcal{D}
, and
x\in I
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{I}\frac{1}{|t-x|^{\alpha-1}}d\mu(t)\lesssim\ell(I)\rho_{\rm in}(I).

Proof.

For
k\geq 0
, let

S_{k}=\{t\in I/|t-x|\leq 2^{-k}\ell(I)\}
and

C_{k}=S_{k}\setminus S_{k+1}=\{t\in I/2^{-(k+1)}\ell(I)<|t-x|\leq 2^{-k}\ell(I)\}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{I}\frac{1}{|t-x|^{\alpha-1}}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{2^{(\alpha-1)(k+1)}}{\ell(I)^{\alpha-1}}\mu(S_{k}\setminus S_{k+1})

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell(I)^{1-\alpha}\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{(\alpha-1)k}(\mu(S_{k})-\mu(S_{k+1})).

We write
a_{k}=\mu(S_{k})
and
evaluate the previous expression using
Abel’s formula: for large
R
we have

\displaystyle\ell(I)^{1-\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{R}2^{(\alpha-1)k}(a_{k}-a_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\ell(I)^{1-\alpha}\Big(a_{0}-a_{R+1}2^{(\alpha-1)R}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{R}a_{k}(2^{(\alpha-1)k}-2^{(\alpha-1)(k-1)})\Big).

Since
a_{0}\leq\mu(I)=\rho(I)\ell(I)^{\alpha}\leq\rho_{\rm in}(I)\ell(I)^{\alpha}
,
for the first term we have

\ell(I)^{1-\alpha}a_{0}\leq\ell(I)\rho_{\rm in}(I).

Similarly,
since

a_{R+1}=\mu(S_{R+1})\leq\mu(I\cap B(x,2^{-(R+1)}\ell(I)))\leq\rho_{\rm in}(I)2^{(R+1)\alpha}\ell(I)^{\alpha},

the absolute value of the second term can be bounded by

\ell(I)^{1-\alpha}a_{R+1}2^{(\alpha-1)R}\lesssim\ell(I)\rho_{\rm in}(I)2^{-R}\leq\ell(I)\rho_{\rm in}(I).

Meanwhile, since since
a_{k}=\mu(S_{k})\leq\rho_{\rm in}(I)2^{k\alpha}\ell(I)^{\alpha}
,
the absolute value of the last term is bounded by

\displaystyle\ell(I)^{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{R}a_{k}2^{(\alpha-1)k}(1-2^{-(\alpha-1)})\lesssim\ell(I)\rho_{\rm in}(I)\sum_{k=1}^{R}2^{-k}\lesssim\ell(I)\rho_{\rm in}(I).

∎

locally integrable, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{Q}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(Q)}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I}

almost everywhere with respect to
\mu
.

Proof.

If
\mu(Q)=0
then
\mu(I)=0
for every

I\in{\rm ch}(Q)
. Witht this, both
\Delta_{Q}=0
and

\psi_{I}=0
and so, the equality is trivial.

For
\mu(Q)\neq 0
,
from (
16
)
and
{\displaystyle\mu(Q)\langle f\rangle_{Q}=\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(Q)}\mu(I)\langle f\rangle_{I}}

we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{Q}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(Q)}\langle f\rangle_{I}\chi_{I}-\langle f\rangle_{Q}\chi_{Q}=\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(Q)}\langle f\rangle_{I}\Big(\chi_{I}-\frac{\mu(I)}{\mu(Q)}\chi_{Q}\Big)

(17)

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(Q)}\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle f\rangle_{I}\psi_{I},

where the last equality holds even for those terms for which
\mu(I)=0
since in that case
\langle f\rangle_{I}=0
.

Also from (
16
) we have for each
I\in{\rm ch}(Q)
,

(18)

\langle\Delta_{Q}f\rangle_{I}=\langle f\rangle_{I}-\langle f\rangle_{Q}

and so

\displaystyle\Delta_{Q}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(Q)}\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\Delta_{Q}f\rangle_{I}\psi_{I}+\langle f\rangle_{Q}\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(Q)}\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{I}.

For the first term, we compute the coefficients:
for
\mu(I)=0
, we have
\psi_{I}=0
and so,

\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\Delta_{Q}f\rangle_{I}\psi_{I}=0=\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I}
. Meanwhile,
for
\mu(I)\neq 0
, we can use (
18
) to write

\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\Delta_{Q}f\rangle_{I}=\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int f(x)\Big(\frac{\chi_{I}(x)}{\mu(I)}-\frac{\chi_{Q}(x)}{\mu(Q)}\Big)d\mu(x)=\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle.

We now denote by
Q^{\prime}
the union of cubes
I\in{\rm ch}(Q)
such that
\mu(I)\neq 0
. Then for the second term we have

\displaystyle\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(Q)}\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{I}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in{\rm ch}(Q)\\
\mu(I)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{I}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in{\rm ch}(Q)\\
\mu(I)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\Big(\chi_{I}-\frac{\mu(I)}{\mu(Q)}\chi_{Q}\Big)

(19)

\displaystyle=\chi_{Q^{\prime}}-\frac{\mu(Q^{\prime})}{\mu(Q)}\chi_{Q}=-\chi_{Q\setminus Q^{\prime}}=0

almost everywhere since
\mu(Q\setminus Q^{\prime})=0
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
f
bounded, compactly supported
and with mean zero with respect to
\mu
. Then

(20)

\int f(x)g(x)d\mu(x)=\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\int\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}\\
2^{-M}\leq\ell(I)\leq 2^{M}\end{subarray}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I}(x)g(x)d\mu(x)

for

g
bounded and compactly supported.

Proof.

By dividing
f
into up to
2^{n}
functions if necessary, we can assume that
\sup f
and
\sup g
are contained in one quadrant of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
we can choose
S\in{\mathcal{D}}
with
\sup f\cup\sup g\subset S
.

We first note that the function inside the integral in the right hand side of (
20
) can be written as

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}\\
2^{-M}\leq\ell(I)\leq 2^{M}\end{subarray}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}\\
2^{-(M-1)}\leq\ell(Q)\leq 2^{M+1}\end{subarray}}\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(Q)}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I}.

By Lemma
6.3
, for every
g
bounded and compactly supported on
S
, we have
\mu
-almost everywhere that

\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(Q)}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I}=\Delta_{Q}f.

Then
we can write the right hand side of (
20
) as

\displaystyle\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle\int\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}(S)\\
2^{-(M-1)}\leq\ell(Q)\leq 2^{M+1}\end{subarray}}\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(Q)}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I}(x)g(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\int\sum_{-M\leq k\leq M}\Delta_{k}f(x)g(x)d\mu(x).

Now
we can choose
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that

2^{-M}\leq\ell(S)\leq 2^{M+1}
.
And for
x\in S
,
we select
I,J\in\mathcal{D}
such that
x\in J\subset S\subset I
,

\ell(J)=2^{-M}
, and
\ell(I)=2^{M+1}
. Then, by summing a telescopic series, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{-M\leq k\leq M}\hskip-14.22636pt\Delta_{k}f(x)

\displaystyle=E_{M}f(x)-E_{-(M+1)}f(x)

\displaystyle=\langle f\rangle_{J}\chi_{J}(x)-\langle f\rangle_{I}\chi_{I}(x)=\langle f\rangle_{J}\chi_{J}(x).

since
S\subset I
and
f
has mean zero.

With this

\displaystyle\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\int\sum_{-M\leq k\leq M}\Delta_{k}f(x)g(x)d\mu

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\int_{S}E_{M}f(x)g(x)d\mu(x).

Since
f
is locally integrable, by Lebesgue’s Differentiation Theorem we have that

E_{M}f

converges to
f

pointwise almost everywhere with respect to
\mu
when
M
tends to infinity. Moreover,
since
|E_{M}f(x)g(x)|\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\chi_{S}(x)
,
we can use Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem to
conclude the result.
∎

Similar work shows the validity of the following result:

Lemma 6.5
.

We remind that
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
denotes the family of open dyadic cubes and
\partial\mathcal{D}
denotes the union of the borders of all dyadic cubes.
For
I\in\mathcal{D}
, we denote

\tilde{I}=I\setminus\partial I\in\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
.

Let
f
be integrable, compactly supported, and with mean zero with respect to
\mu
.
We denote
f_{1}=f-f\chi_{\partial\mathcal{D}}
. Then
the equality

\displaystyle f_{1}=\sum_{I\in\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{\tilde{I}}

holds

\mu
-almost everywhere.

6.2.
A variation of the Haar wavelet system

We now define a new Haar wavelet system and show that the analog of lemma
6.3

holds.
These wavelets will be used when dealing with the paraproducts.

Definition 6.6
.

Let
Q,J_{p}\in\mathcal{D}
and we denote
c_{J_{p}}=c(J_{p})
. For
I\in\mathcal{D}
with

\mu(I)\neq 0
, we define

\psi_{I,J_{p}}^{{\rm full}}(t)=\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{\chi_{I}(c_{J_{p}})}{\mu(I)}-\frac{\chi_{I_{p}}(c_{J_{p}})}{\mu(I_{p})}\Big)\chi_{Q}(t).

If
\mu(I)=0
, we define
\psi_{I,J_{p}}^{{\rm full}}\equiv 0
.

We omit the dependence of
\psi_{I,J_{p}}^{{\rm full}}
on the cube
Q
.
We note that
\psi_{I,J_{p}}^{{\rm full}}=0
if
J_{p}\cap I_{p}=\emptyset
, and that

\psi_{I,J_{p}}^{{\rm full}}\chi_{I}=\psi_{I}\chi_{I}
when
J_{p}\subseteq I
.

We define
the localized averaging operators by

\hat{E}_{R}(f)=\langle f\rangle_{R}\chi_{R}(c_{J_{p}})\chi_{Q}

and the corresponding localized differences

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}_{R}f

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(R)}\hat{E}_{I}f\Big)-\hat{E}_{R}f

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(R)}\langle f\rangle_{I}\chi_{I}(c_{J_{p}})\Big)\chi_{Q}-\langle f\rangle_{R}\chi_{R}(c_{J_{p}})\chi_{Q}.

The following result is the analog of Lemma
6.3
for the localized difference operator.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let

R\in{\mathcal{D}}
and
J_{p}\in\mathcal{D}
with
\ell(J_{p})<\ell(R)
and
\mu(J_{p})\neq 0
.
Then

\hat{\Delta}_{R}(f)=\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(R)}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I,J_{p}}^{{\rm full}}

for
f
bounded, compactly supported and with mean zero.

Proof.

If
\mu(R)=0
then both sides of the equality are zero. If
\mu(R)\neq 0
, we reason as follows.
Since

\mu(R)\langle f\rangle_{R}=\int_{R}fd\mu=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in{\rm ch}(R)\\
\mu(I)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\int_{I}fd\mu=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in{\rm ch}(R)\\
\mu(I)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\mu(I)\langle f\rangle_{I},

we have

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}_{R}(f)

\displaystyle=\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in{\rm ch}(R)\\
\mu(I)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\!\langle f\rangle_{I}\Big(\chi_{I}(c_{J_{p}})-\frac{\mu(I)}{\mu(R)}\chi_{R}(c_{J_{p}})\Big)\chi_{Q}=\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in{\rm ch}(R)\\
\mu(I)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\langle f\rangle_{I}\psi_{I,J_{p}}^{\rm full}.

Now, by (
18
), we have

\langle f\rangle_{I}=\langle\Delta_{R}f\rangle_{I}+\langle f\rangle_{R}

and so,

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}_{R}(f)=\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(R)}\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\Delta_{R}f\rangle_{I}\psi_{I,J_{p}}^{\rm full}+\langle f\rangle_{R}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in{\rm ch}(R)\\
\mu(I)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{I,J_{p}}^{\rm full}.

We have as before that

\langle\Delta_{R}f\rangle_{I}=\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle
.

On the other hand,
let now
R^{\prime}
be the union of cubes
I\in{\rm ch}(R)
such that
\mu(I)\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in{\rm ch}(R)\\
\mu(I)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{I,J_{p}}^{{\rm full}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in{\rm ch}(R)\\
\mu(I)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\Big(\chi_{I}(c_{J_{p}})-\frac{\mu(I)}{\mu(R)}\chi_{R}(c_{J_{p}})\Big)\chi_{Q}

\displaystyle=(\chi_{R^{\prime}}(c_{J_{p}})-\chi_{R}(c_{J_{p}}))\chi_{Q}=-\chi_{R\setminus R^{\prime}}(c_{J_{p}})\chi_{Q}.

With this,

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}_{R}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(I_{p})}\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I,J_{p}}^{\rm full}-\langle f\rangle_{R}\chi_{R\setminus R^{\prime}}(c_{J_{p}})\chi_{Q}.

If
\chi_{R\setminus R^{\prime}}(c_{J_{p}})\neq 0
then

c(J_{p})\in R\setminus R^{\prime}
. With this,
since
R\cap J_{p}\neq\emptyset
and
\ell(J_{p})<\ell(R)
,
we have
J_{p}\subsetneq R
. Moreover, since

\mu(J_{p})\neq 0
, we also have

J_{p}\subset I\in{\rm ch}{(R)}
with
\mu(I)\neq 0
, that is,
J_{p}\subset R^{\prime}
. But this is contradictory since it implies

\chi_{R\setminus R^{\prime}}(c_{J_{p}})=0
.

∎

6.3.
Orthogonality and Bessel inequality of the Haar wavelet systems

The following lemma summarizes the orthogonality properties of the Haar wavelets.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
I,J\in\mathcal{D}
or
I,J\in\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
. Then

\int\psi_{I}(x)d\mu(x)=0.

If
\mu(I)=0
then
\langle\psi_{I},\psi_{J}\rangle=0
,
while if
\mu(I)\neq 0
then

(21)

\langle\psi_{I},\psi_{J}\rangle=\delta(I_{p},J_{p})\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{\delta(I,J)}{\mu(I)}-\frac{1}{\mu(I_{p})}\Big),

where we denote
\delta(I,J)=1
if
I=J
and zero otherwise.
In addition, if
\mu(I)\neq 0
we have

\|\psi_{I}\|_{L^{q}(\mu)}\lesssim\mu(I)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{q}}
.

Proof.

The first equality is trivial.
Equality (
21
) is also
trivial when
I_{p}\cap J_{p}=\emptyset
.
When
I_{p}\subsetneq J_{p}
,
\psi_{J}
is constant on the support of
\psi_{I}
and so the dual pair is zero due to the mean zero of
\psi_{I}
. Symmetrically, we have the same result when
J_{p}\subsetneq I_{p}
.

On the other hand, for
J_{p}=I_{p}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{I},\psi_{J}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int\Big(\frac{\chi_{I}(x)}{\mu(I)}-\frac{\chi_{I_{p}}(x)}{\mu(I_{p})}\Big)\Big(\frac{\chi_{J}(x)}{\mu(J)}-\frac{\chi_{J_{p}}(x)}{\mu(J_{p})}\Big)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\Big(\frac{\mu(I\cap J)}{\mu(J)}-\frac{\mu(I)}{\mu(I_{p})}\Big).

For
I\neq J
, since
I\cap J=\emptyset
,
we have

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{I},\psi_{J}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\mu(I_{p})},

while for
I=J
, we get

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{I},\psi_{J}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mu(I)(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}-\frac{1}{\mu(I_{p})}).

On the other hand, for
\mu(I)\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\|\psi_{I}\|_{L^{q}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\|\chi_{I}\|_{L^{q}(\mu)}+\frac{1}{\mu(I_{p})}\|\chi_{I_{p}}\|_{L^{q}(\mu)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}}+\frac{1}{\mu(I_{p})^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}}\Big)\leq 2\mu(I)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{q}}

since
\mu(I)\leq\mu(I_{p})
.
∎

Despite the Haar wavelets we have chosen do not constitute an orthogonal system of functions, they still safisfy Parseval’s identity as we see in the next lemma.

Lemma 6.9
.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mu)

\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}|\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle|^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}.

Proof.

We have from Lemma
6.4

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)f(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\int f(x)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}\\
2^{-M}\leq\ell(I)\leq 2^{M}\end{subarray}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I}(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}\\
2^{-M}\leq\ell(I)\leq 2^{M}\end{subarray}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}|\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle|^{2}.

∎

6.4.
Characterization of compactness

In this section,
we explain how to use the Haar wavelets to characterize compactness on
L^{2}(\mu)
of Calderón-Zygmund operators.

Definition 6.10
.

Let
(\psi_{I})_{I\in{\mathcal{D}}}
be a Haar wavelet system of
L^{2}(\mu)
.
For every
M\in\mathbb{N}

we define the lagom projection operator by

P_{M}f=\sum_{I\in{\mathcal{D}}_{M}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I},

where
\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\psi_{I}(x)d\mu(x)
.
We also define
P_{M}^{\perp}f=f-P_{M}f
.

We note that
P_{M}^{*}f=P_{M}f
.

Remark 6.11
.

When we deal with boundedness, we can consider

M=0
and so,
P_{M}f=0
and
P_{M}^{\perp}f=f
.

Lemma 6.12
.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mu)

\|P_{M}^{\perp}f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}\leq\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}_{M}^{c}}|\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle|^{2}.

Proof.

By Parseval’s identity of Lemma
6.9

we have

\displaystyle\|P_{M}^{\perp}f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|f-P_{M}f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}\\
\mu(I)\neq 0\end{subarray}}|\langle f-P_{M}f,\psi_{I}\rangle|^{2}

For
I\in\mathcal{D}
with
\mu(I)\neq 0
, by the definition of
P_{M}
,

\displaystyle\langle P_{M}f,\psi_{I}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{D}_{M}}\langle f,\psi_{J}\rangle\langle\psi_{J},\psi_{I}\rangle.

From (
21
), we know that
\langle\psi_{J},\psi_{I}\rangle=0
if
I_{p}\neq J_{p}
. Now we see that

J\in\mathcal{D}_{M}
and
I_{p}=J_{p}
imply
I\in\mathcal{D}_{M+1}
.

Since
J\in\mathcal{D}_{M}
, we have

2^{-M}\leq\ell(J)\leq 2^{M}
and

{\rm\,rdist}(J,\mathbb{B}_{2^{M}})\leq M
.
Moreover,
I_{p}=J_{p}
implies
\ell(I)=\ell(J)
and

{\rm dist}(I,J)=0
. With this,
we have
2^{-M}\leq\ell(I)\leq 2^{M}
and

1+\frac{{\rm dist}(I,\mathbb{B}_{2^{M}})}{2^{M}}\leq 1+\frac{{\rm dist}(J,\mathbb{B}_{2^{M}})+\ell(I)}{2^{M}}\leq{\rm\,rdist}(J,\mathbb{B}_{2^{M}})+1\leq M+1.

Then

{\rm\,rdist}(I,\mathbb{B}_{2^{M+1}})=1+\frac{{\rm dist}(I,\mathbb{B}_{2^{M+1}})}{2^{M+1}}\leq 1+\frac{{\rm dist}(I,\mathbb{B}_{2^{M}})}{2^{M}}\leq M+1
.

With this, we now reason as follows.
If
I\in\mathcal{D}_{M+1}^{c}
, then
I_{p}\neq J_{p}
for all
J\in\mathcal{D}_{M}
and so, by
(
21
), we have

\langle\psi_{J},\psi_{I}\rangle=0
. Then

\langle P_{M}f,\psi_{I}\rangle=0
and thus

\langle f-P_{M}f,\psi_{I}\rangle=\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle
.

On the other hand, if
I\in\mathcal{D}_{M+1}
we have
again by (
21
) that

\displaystyle\langle

\displaystyle P_{M}f,\psi_{I}\rangle=\sum_{J\in{\rm ch}(I_{p})}\langle f,\psi_{J}\rangle\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\frac{\delta(I,J)}{\mu(I)}-\frac{1}{\mu(I_{p})})

\displaystyle=\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle-\frac{\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\mu(I_{p})}\sum_{J\in{\rm ch}(I_{p})}\langle f,\psi_{J}\rangle\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle

since

\sum_{J\in{\rm ch}(I_{p})}\langle f,\psi_{J}\rangle\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\int f(x)\sum_{J\in{\rm ch}(I_{p})}\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{J}(x)d\mu(x)=0

by (
6.1
).
Therefore

\langle f-P_{M}f,\psi_{I}\rangle=0
.

With both results, and
\mathcal{D}_{M}\subset\mathcal{D}_{M+1}^{c}
we get

\displaystyle\|P_{M}^{\perp}f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}_{M+1}^{c}}|\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle|^{2}\leq\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}_{M}^{c}}|\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle|^{2}.

∎

Remark 6.13
.

Previous work also shows that

\|P_{M}f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}_{M+1}}|\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle|^{2}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

and so,
\|P_{M}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu)}\leq 1
and
\|P_{M}^{\perp}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu)}\leq 1
.

Corollary 6.14
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. Then

(22)

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\|P_{M}^{\perp}f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.9
we have

\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}|\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle|^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}<\infty
. Then, by
Lemma
6.12
,

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\|P_{M}^{\perp}f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}=\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}_{M+1}^{c}}|\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle|^{2}=0.

∎

To prove the main result Theorem
4.2
, we will show that the truncated operators

T_{\gamma,Q}
are uniformly bounded or compact on

L^{2}(\mu)
with estimates independent on
Q
and
\gamma
.

Lemma 6.15
.

Let
T
be
a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\mu)
.
Let
(\psi_{I})_{I\in{\mathcal{D}}}
be the Haar wavelet system.
Then

(23)

\displaystyle\langle Tf,g\rangle=\sum_{I,J\in\mathcal{D}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\langle g,\psi_{J}\rangle\langle T\psi_{I},\psi_{J}\rangle

for all
f,g
compactly supported and integrable.

Proof.

Let

P_{M}
be the lagom projection related to the Haar wavelet frame. Since
T
is bounded, we have

\displaystyle|\langle Tf,g\rangle-\langle TP_{M}f,P_{M}g\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq|\langle T(f-P_{M}f),g\rangle|+|\langle TP_{M}f,g-P_{M}g\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\|T\|\|f-P_{M}f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle+\|T\|\|P_{M}f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\|g-P_{M}g\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

Since by (
22
) we have
\|f-P_{M}f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
and
\|g-P_{M}g\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
tend to zero, so does the left hand side of previous chain of inequalities.
∎

Corollary 6.16
.

With the same hypotheses of Lemma
6.15
, let

P_{M}
be the lagom projection related to the Haar wavelet frame. Then

\langle P_{M}^{\perp}TP_{M}^{\perp}f,g\rangle=\sum_{I,J\in\mathcal{D}_{M}^{c}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\langle g,\psi_{J}\rangle\langle T\psi_{I},\psi_{J}\rangle

for all
f,g
compactly supported and integrable.

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle\langle P_{M}^{\perp}TP_{M}^{\perp}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle TP_{M}^{\perp}f,P_{M}^{\perp}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Tf,g\rangle-\langle Tf,P_{M}g\rangle-\langle TP_{M}f,g\rangle+\langle TP_{M}f,P_{M}g\rangle.

Then by (
23
) the last expression coincides with the right hand side of the statement:

\displaystyle\langle P_{M}^{\perp}TP_{M}^{\perp}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{I,J\in\mathcal{D}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}\\
J\in\mathcal{D}_{M}\end{subarray}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}_{M}\\
J\in\mathcal{D}\end{subarray}}+\sum_{I,J\in\mathcal{D}_{M}}\Big)\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\langle g,\psi_{J}\rangle\langle T\psi_{I},\psi_{J}\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}\\

Lemma 7.2

Proof.

Since

is non-decreasing,

\displaystyle 2(1+\frac{|t+c_{J_{p}}|}{1+|t-c_{J_{p}}|})

\displaystyle\geq 2+\frac{|t+c_{J_{p}}|}{1+|t-c_{J_{p}}|}

\displaystyle\geq 2+\frac{2|c_{J_{p}}|}{1+|t-c_{J_{p}}|}-\frac{|t-c_{J_{p}}|}{1+|t-c_{J_{p}}|}

\displaystyle 1+\ell(\langle I_{p},J_{p}\rangle)+|c_{J_{p}}|

\displaystyle 1+\frac{|c_{J_{p}}|}{1+\ell(\langle I_{p},J_{p}\rangle)}

Proof.

We assume

\displaystyle\psi_{I}(t)-{\psi}^{\rm full}_{I,J}(t)=\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}[\varphi_{I}(t)-\varphi_{I}(c_{J_{p}})\chi_{Q}(t)-\varphi_{I_{p}}(t)+\varphi_{I_{p}}(c_{J_{p}})\chi_{Q}(t)].

\displaystyle\hskip 50.0pt|t

\displaystyle-c(J_{p})|\geq\frac{\ell(J_{p})}{2}+{\rm dist}(I_{p}\backslash I^{\prime},J_{p})

\displaystyle|\langle T\Phi_{k},\psi_{J}\rangle|

As in previous proposition,

\psi_{J}=\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\frac{1}{\mu(J)}\chi_{J}-\frac{1}{\mu(J_{p})}\chi_{J_{p}})

Meanwhile, we can write previous two inequalities in a unified way as follows:

\displaystyle|\psi_{I}^{{\rm out}}(t)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\chi_{Q\setminus I}(t)\chi_{I}(c_{J_{p}})

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt+\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\chi_{I}(t)\chi_{I_{p}\setminus I}(c_{J_{p}})+\frac{1}{\mu(I_{p})}\chi_{Q\setminus I}(t)\chi_{I_{p}\setminus I}(c_{J_{p}})\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\chi_{I}(c_{J_{p}})+\frac{1}{\mu(I_{p})}\chi_{I_{p}}(c_{J_{p}})+\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\chi_{I}(t)\chi_{I_{p}\setminus I}(c_{J_{p}})\Big)

(28)

\displaystyle|\langle T\Phi_{k},\psi_{J}\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{R\in\{I,I_{p}\}}\sum_{S\in\{J,J_{p}\}}\frac{\chi_{R}(c_{J_{p}})}{\mu(R)}\frac{1}{\mu(S)}

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt\int_{S}\int_{\Delta_{k}}|K(t,x)-K(t,c_{J_{p}})|\,d\mu(t)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle+\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{S\in\{J,J_{p}\}}\frac{\chi_{I_{p}\setminus I}(c_{J_{p}})}{\mu(I)}\frac{1}{\mu(S)}

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt\int_{S}\int_{I\cap\Delta_{k}}|K(t,x)-K(t,c_{J_{p}})|\,d\mu(t)d\mu(x).

We now estimate the double integral in the right hand size of (
28
), starting with the first one which we denote by
{\rm Int}
.
We fix
R\in\{I,I_{p}\}
and
S\in\{J,J_{p}\}
. For

and so, we can use the smoothness property (

\displaystyle 2(1+\frac{|t+c(J_{p})|}{1+|t-c(J_{p})|})

\displaystyle{\rm Int}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\ell(J)^{\delta}}{(2^{k}\ell(J))^{\delta+\alpha}}\mu(I)\mu(S)F(J,J,2^{k}J)

\displaystyle|\langle T\Phi_{k},\psi_{J}\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k\delta}F(J,J,2^{k}J)\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 100.0pt+\chi_{I_{p}\setminus I}(c_{J_{p}})\frac{\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(2^{k}\ell(J))^{\alpha}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{R\in\{I,I_{p}\}}\Big(\frac{\mu(J\cap R)}{\mu(R)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{-k\delta}F(J,J,2^{k}J)\frac{\mu(2^{k+3}J)}{(2^{k+3}\ell(J))^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle+\chi_{I_{p}\setminus I}(c_{J_{p}})\frac{\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(J)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\ell(J)^{\alpha}}2^{-k(\alpha+\delta)}F(J,J,2^{k}J)

that

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\{I,I_{p}\}}\hskip-8.5359pt\Big(\frac{\mu(J\cap R)}{\mu(R)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}L(\ell(J))S(\ell(J))\hskip-2.84544pt\sum_{k\geq m_{0}}2^{-k\delta}\hskip-2.84544pt\frac{\mu(2^{k+3}J)}{(2^{k+3}\ell(J))^{\alpha}}D({\rm\,rdist}(2^{k}J,\mathbb{B}))

\displaystyle 2^{-m_{0}\delta}\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-k\delta}\frac{\mu(2^{k+3}2^{m_{0}}J)}{\ell(2^{k+3}2^{m_{0}}J)^{\alpha}}D({\rm\,rdist}(2^{k}(2^{m_{0}}J),\mathbb{B}))

\displaystyle 2^{-m_{0}(\alpha+\delta)}

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-k(\alpha+\delta)}D({\rm\,rdist}(2^{k}(2^{m_{0}}J),\mathbb{B}))

\displaystyle\lesssim{\rm\,inrdist}(I_{p},J_{p})^{-(\alpha+\delta)}\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-k(\alpha+\delta)}D({\rm\,rdist}(2^{k}\lambda J,\mathbb{B}))

with

8.
Paraproducts

The proof of Theorem
4.2
is divided in two parts: we first deal with the part associated to
\psi_{I,J}^{{\rm full}}
, which corresponds to the paraproduct case in the classical proof.
Then we use the estimates of the bump lemma for the remaining part.

For the first part of the proof we will use
the classical Carleson’s Embedding Theorem

Lemma 8.1

(Carleson Embedding Theorem)
.

Let
(a_{I})_{I\in\mathcal{D}}
be a collection of non-negative numbers.
Then

(30)

\displaystyle\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}a_{I}|\langle f\rangle_{I}|^{2}\lesssim\sup_{I\subset\mathcal{D}}\Big(\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in\mathcal{D}(I)\end{subarray}}a_{J}\Big)\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}

for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
.

The following proposition deals with the paraproduct part of the operator. The proof provided follows the work in
[
18
]
.

Proposition 8.2

(Paraproducts)
.

Let
Q\in\mathcal{C}
and
\theta\in(0,1)
be fixed.
We define the following bilinear forms:
for
f,g
bounded functions with
{\rm supp\,}f\cup{\rm supp\,}g\subset Q
and mean zero,

\displaystyle\Pi(f,g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}(Q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in\mathcal{D}(I_{p})\\
{\rm\,inrdist}(J_{p},I_{p})>\lambda_{\theta}\end{subarray}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\langle g,\psi_{J}\rangle\langle T\psi_{I,J}^{{\rm full}},\psi_{J}\rangle

\displaystyle\Pi^{\prime}(f,g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{D}(Q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}(J_{p})\\
{\rm\,inrdist}(I_{p},J_{p})>\lambda_{\theta}\end{subarray}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\langle g,\psi_{J}\rangle\langle T\psi_{I},\psi_{J,I}^{\rm full}\rangle

with

\lambda_{\theta}=1+{\rm ec}(I,J)^{-\theta}
.

Then
given
\epsilon>0
there exist

M_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
independent of
Q
and the functions
f,g
such that for all
M>M_{0}

|\Pi(P_{M}^{\perp}f,P_{M}^{\perp}g)|+|\Pi^{\prime}(P_{M}^{\perp}f,P_{M}^{\perp}g)|\lesssim\epsilon\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

Proof.

By symmetry, we only need to work with
\Pi
.
By writing
f
as the sum of
2^{n}

functions (the restriction of
f
to each quadrant), we can assume that
{\rm supp\,}f\subset Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
.

By Lemma
6.4
, we can
by rewriting
\Pi(P_{M}^{\perp}f,P_{M}^{\perp}g)
in the following way

\displaystyle\Pi(P_{M}^{\perp}f,P_{M}^{\perp}g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{D}_{M}^{c}(Q)}\langle g,\psi_{J}\rangle\langle T(\hskip-14.22636pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}_{M}^{c}(Q)\\
J_{p}\subset I_{p},{\rm\,inrdist}(J_{p},I_{p})>\lambda_{\theta}\end{subarray}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I,J}^{{\rm full}}),\psi_{J}\rangle.

We can assume that the first sum in previous expression only contains terms for which
\mu(J_{p})\neq 0
since otherwise
\psi_{J}\equiv 0
.
Then, since in the second sum we have

\ell(J_{p})<\ell(I_{p})
,
by Lemma
6.7
,
we have

\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(I_{p})}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I,J}^{{\rm full}}=\hat{\Delta}_{I_{p}}(f)=\Big(\sum_{I\in{\rm ch}(I_{p})}\hat{E}_{I}f\Big)-\hat{E}_{I_{p}}f,

where

\hat{E}_{I}f=\langle f\rangle_{I}\chi_{I}(c_{J_{p}})\chi_{Q}
.

The inner sum takes place under the condition

{\rm\,inrdist}(J_{p},I_{p})>\lambda_{\theta}\geq 2
.
Then,
for
J_{p}\in\mathcal{D}_{M}^{c}(Q)
,
let

\lambda
be the smallest integer such that

{\rm\,inrdist}(J_{p},I_{p})>\lambda
. Let also

J_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{D}(Q)

be the smallest cube such that

J_{p}\subset J_{\lambda}
and
{\rm\,inrdist}(J_{p},J_{\lambda})>\lambda
.
If such cube does not exist, we then define

J_{\lambda}=\emptyset
.
Now,
by summing a telescopic sum, we have for
J\in{\mathcal{D}}_{M}^{c}(Q)
fixed and
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that

,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}_{M}^{c}(Q)\\
J_{p}\subset I_{p},{\rm\,inrdist}(J_{p},I_{p})>\lambda\end{subarray}}\langle f,\psi_{I}\rangle\psi_{I,J_{p}}^{{\rm full}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{p}\in\mathcal{D}_{M}^{c}(Q)\\
J_{p}\subset I_{p},{\rm\,inrdist}(J_{p},I_{p})>\lambda\end{subarray}}\sum_{I^{\prime}\in{\rm ch}(I_{p})}\langle f,\psi_{I^{\prime}}\rangle\psi_{I^{\prime},J_{p}}^{{\rm full}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{p}\in\mathcal{D}_{M}^{c}(Q)\\
J_{p}\subset I_{p},{\rm\,inrdist}(J_{p},I_{p})>\lambda\end{subarray}}\hat{\Delta}_{I_{p}}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{R\in{\rm ch}(J_{\lambda})}\hat{E}_{R}f-\hat{E}_{Q^{\prime}}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{R\in{\rm ch}(J_{\lambda})}\langle f\rangle_{R_{J}}\chi_{Q},

where
Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
such that

\sup f\subset Q^{\prime}
and so,

\langle f\rangle_{Q^{\prime}}=0
.
The cardinality of
{\rm ch}(J_{\lambda})
is
2^{n}

\epsilon>0
there

dependent on

.

for each

\displaystyle\mu(C_{k})

\displaystyle\langle TP_{2M}^{\perp}P_{M_{N}}f,P_{M}^{\perp}P_{M_{N}}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\langle Tf_{i-1,\partial},g_{i}\rangle+\langle Tf_{i},g_{i,\partial}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle Tf_{k,\partial},g_{k,\partial}\rangle,

where
f_{i},g_{i}
are zero on
\partial\mathcal{D}^{i}(Q)
and
\langle Tf_{k,\partial},g_{k,\partial}\rangle=0
as we saw before.

We now prove that for
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
and

g
such that is zero on
\partial\mathcal{D}^{i}(Q)

we have

(45)

\displaystyle\langle Tf,g\rangle=\langle T_{0}f,g\rangle+\langle a_{i}f,g\rangle,

where
a_{i}
is
such that
|\langle a_{i}f,g\rangle|\lesssim\epsilon\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
. Similarly

\langle f,T^{*}g\rangle=\langle f,T_{0}^{*}g\rangle+\langle f,b_{i}g\rangle

for every function
f
that is zero on
\partial\mathcal{D}^{i}(Q)
, where
b_{i}
is
such that
|\langle f,b_{i}g\rangle|\lesssim\epsilon\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
.

Assuming these equalities, we can prove the statement. Let
j_{i}
large enough so that

|\langle(T_{0}-T_{\gamma_{j_{i}},Q})f_{i-1,\partial},g_{i}\rangle|<\epsilon
. Then

(46)

\displaystyle|\langle Tf_{i-1,\partial},g_{i}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq|\langle T_{\gamma_{j_{i}},Q}f_{i-1,\partial},g_{i}\rangle|+|\langle(T_{0}-T_{\gamma_{j_{i}},Q})f_{i-1,\partial},g_{i}\rangle|

\displaystyle+|\langle a_{i}f_{i-1,\partial},g_{i}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\langle T_{\gamma_{j_{i}},Q}f_{i-1,\partial},g_{i}\rangle|+2\epsilon\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mu)},

and similar for

|\langle Tf_{i,\partial},g_{i,\partial}\rangle|
. Then
with (
44
) and (
46
), we get

\displaystyle|\langle TP_{2M}^{\perp}P_{M_{N}}f,P_{M}^{\perp}P_{M_{N}}g\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sup_{\gamma,Q}|\langle T_{\gamma,Q}f_{i-1,\partial},g_{i}\rangle|+|\langle T_{\gamma,Q}f_{i},g_{i,\partial}\rangle|

\displaystyle+2k\epsilon\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mu)},

which is comparable with the statement.

We now work to prove (
45
). Let
D=T-T_{0}
.
Then we prove that
\langle Df,g\rangle=\langle a_{i}f,g\rangle
.
Let
I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
such that

2Q\subset I^{\prime}
and let
I\in\mathcal{D}(2^{-1}Q)
. We first show that,
for all
g\in L^{2}(\mu)
such that
g
is zero on
\partial\mathcal{D}^{i}(Q)
,

(47)

\displaystyle\langle D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle=\langle\chi_{I}D(\chi_{I^{\prime}}),g\rangle.

For this we proceed as follows.
We first assume that
g
satisfies the additional condition such that
{\rm dist}(\sup g,I)>0
.
For
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, let
j_{I}\in\mathbb{N}
be large enough so that

|\langle(T_{\gamma_{j_{I}},Q}-T_{0})(\chi_{I}),g\rangle|<\epsilon^{\prime}
and

{\rm dist}(\sup g,I)>2\gamma_{j_{I}}
. Then

\langle D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle=\langle(T-T_{\gamma_{j_{I}},Q})(\chi_{I}),g\rangle+\langle(T_{\gamma_{j_{I}},Q}-T_{0})(\chi_{I}),g\rangle.

Since
\sup g\cap I=\emptyset
,
x\in\sup g\subset 2^{-1}Q
and
t\in I\subset 2^{-1}Q
, we have

\langle(T-T_{\gamma_{j_{I}},Q})(\chi_{I}),g\rangle=\int\int_{I}K(t,x)\phi(\frac{|t-x|}{\gamma_{j_{I}}})d\mu(t)g(x)d\mu(x)=0

due to the facts that
and
{\rm supp\,}\phi\subset[-2,2]
and

|t-x|\geq{\rm dist}(I,\sup g)>2\gamma_{j_{I}}

. Then

|\langle D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle|=|\langle(T_{\gamma_{j_{I}},Q}-T_{0})(\chi_{I}),g\rangle|<\epsilon^{\prime}.

Since the inequality holds for all
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, we conclude that
\langle D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle=0

for all
g\in L^{2}(\mu)
such that
{\rm dist}(\sup g,I)>0
.

Now for all
\lambda>0
we denote

I_{\lambda}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}/{\rm dist}(x,I)<\lambda\ell(I)\}
.
By previous reasoning, since

{\rm dist}(\sup(1-\chi_{I_{\lambda}}),I)\geq\lambda\ell(I)>0
, we have
for all
g\in L^{2}(\mu)
that are zero in
\partial\mathcal{D}(Q)
,

(48)

\displaystyle\langle D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(1-\chi_{I_{\lambda}})D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle+\langle\chi_{I_{\lambda}}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\chi_{I_{\lambda}}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle.

Now, if we denote
I^{c}=I^{\prime}\setminus I
, we can write

\langle\chi_{I_{\lambda}}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle=\langle D(\chi_{I^{\prime}}),g\chi_{I_{\lambda}}\rangle-\langle D(\chi_{I^{c}}),g\chi_{I_{\lambda}}\rangle.

By previous reasoning we have that

\langle D(\chi_{I^{c}}),h\rangle=0

for all
h\in L^{2}(\mu)
such that
{\rm dist}(\sup h,I^{c})>0
.
Then, as in (
48
),

\langle D(\chi_{I^{c}}),g\chi_{I_{\lambda}}\rangle=\langle\chi_{(I^{c})_{\lambda}}D(\chi_{I^{c}}),g\chi_{I_{\lambda}}\rangle.

With this, for
\lambda>0
and
g\in L^{2}(\mu)

that is zero on
\partial\mathcal{D}^{i}(Q)
, we have

\langle D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle=\langle\chi_{I_{\lambda}}D(\chi_{I^{\prime}}),g\rangle-\langle\chi_{I_{\lambda}}\chi_{(I^{c})_{\lambda}}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle.

And since
\lambda>0
is arbitrary, we get

(49)

\displaystyle\langle D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\langle\chi_{I_{\lambda}}D(\chi_{I^{\prime}}),g\rangle-\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\langle\chi_{I_{\lambda}}\chi_{(I^{c})_{\lambda}}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle.

For the first term, we reason as follows.
By the testing condition on
T
and the boundedness of

T_{\gamma,Q}
, we have
\|\chi_{I^{\prime}}D(\chi_{I^{\prime}})\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\lesssim\mu(I^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then

D(\chi_{I^{\prime}})g

is integrable on
I^{\prime}
.
Since
I_{\lambda}\subset I^{\prime}
,

|\chi_{I_{\lambda}}D(\chi_{I^{\prime}})g|\leq\chi_{I^{\prime}}|D(\chi_{I^{\prime}})g|\in L^{1}(\mu)
. Moreover,

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\chi_{I_{\lambda}}=\chi_{\bar{I}}
,
where

\bar{I}\in\mathcal{C}
is the closed cube defined by the closure of
I
.
Then, by Lebesgue’s Dominated Theorem,

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\langle\chi_{I_{\lambda}}D(\chi_{I^{\prime}}),g\rangle=\langle\chi_{\bar{I}}D(\chi_{I^{\prime}}),g\rangle,=\langle\chi_{I}D(\chi_{I^{\prime}}),g\rangle.

The last equality holds because
g
is zero on
\partial\mathcal{D}^{i}(Q)
.

For the second term in (
49
), we work differently.
Let
I_{-\lambda}=(1-\lambda)I
.
Then

(50)

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{I_{\lambda}}\chi_{(I^{c})_{\lambda}}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle=\langle\chi_{I\setminus I_{-\lambda}}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle\ +\langle\chi_{I_{\lambda}\setminus\bar{I}}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle.

The first new term can be treated as before:

\|\chi_{I}D(\chi_{I})\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\lesssim\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and so,

D(\chi_{I})g
is integrable on
I
.
Moreover,
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\chi_{I_{-\lambda}}=\chi_{\partial I}
and

|\chi_{I\setminus I_{-\lambda}}D(\chi_{I})g|\leq\chi_{I}|D(\chi_{I})g|\in L^{1}(\mu)
.
Then, by
Lebesgue’s Dominated Theorem

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\langle\chi_{I\setminus I_{-\lambda}}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle=\langle\chi_{\partial I}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle=0,

since
g
is zero on
\partial\mathcal{D}^{i}(Q)
.

For the second term in (
50
), we proceed as follows.
Let
S_{r}=\{x\in I_{\lambda}\setminus\bar{I}:2^{-(r+1)}\ell(I_{\lambda}\setminus\bar{I})<{\rm dist}(x,I)\leq 2^{-r}\ell(I_{\lambda}\setminus\bar{I})\}
.
Then since

I_{\lambda}\setminus\bar{=}\bigcup_{r=0}^{\infty}S_{r}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{I_{\lambda}\setminus\bar{I}}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle=\langle\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{r=0}^{R}\chi_{S_{r}}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle

Then, by Fatou’s lemma,

(51)

\displaystyle|\langle\chi_{I_{\lambda}\setminus\bar{I}}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\langle\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{r=0}^{R}\chi_{S_{r}}|D(\chi_{I})|,|g|\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{R\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{r=0}^{R}\langle\chi_{S_{r}}|D(\chi_{I})|,|g|\rangle

Given

\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, we choose for
j_{r}
, dependent on
r
,
\lambda
,
\epsilon^{\prime}
,
I
and
g
such that

2\gamma_{j_{r}}<2^{-(r+1)}\ell(I_{\lambda}\setminus\bar{I})
and

\|(T_{\gamma_{j_{r}},Q}-T_{0})(\chi_{I})\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}<\epsilon^{\prime}2^{-r}
.
Now we decompose as

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{S_{r}}|D(\chi_{I})|,|g|\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle\chi_{S_{r}}|(T-T_{\gamma_{j_{r}},Q})(\chi_{I})|,|g|\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\chi_{S_{r}}|(T_{\gamma_{j_{r}},Q}-T_{0})(\chi_{I}),|g|\rangle.

The second term can be bounded by

\displaystyle\|(T_{\gamma_{j_{r}},Q}-T_{0})(\chi_{I})\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}<\epsilon^{\prime}2^{-r}

For the first term, we denote

D_{r}=T-T_{\gamma_{j_{r}},Q}
. Since

S_{r}\cap I=\emptyset
,

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{S_{r}}|D_{r}(\chi_{I})|,|g|\rangle=\int_{S_{r}}\Big|\int_{I}K(t,x)\phi(\frac{|t-x|}{\gamma_{j_{r}}})d\mu(t)\Big||g(x)|d\mu(x)=0

since

2\gamma_{j_{r}}<^{-(r+1)}\ell(I_{\lambda}\setminus\bar{I})<{\rm dist}(x,I)\leq|t-x|
.
With both estimates we continue the estimate in (
51
) as

\displaystyle|\langle\chi_{I_{\lambda}\setminus\bar{I}}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\epsilon^{\prime}2^{-r}\lesssim\epsilon^{\prime}

for all
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
. Then

\langle\chi_{I_{\lambda}\setminus\bar{I}}D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle=0
. Finally, by combining the decompositions in (
49
) and (
50
), we have

\langle D(\chi_{I}),g\rangle=\langle\chi_{I}D(\chi_{I^{\prime}}),g\rangle
,
which is the equality claimed in (
47
).

Now we use (
47
) to prove (
45
). By telescoping sums, we can write that
f_{i,\partial}=\sum_{j}\langle f\rangle_{I_{j}}\chi_{I_{j}}
with
I_{j}\in(\mathcal{D}^{i})_{M}^{c}(Q)
such that

I_{j}\subset Q\subset 2^{-1}I^{\prime}
and small enough so that

F_{T}(I_{j})<\epsilon
. Then we are going to consider only functions with the described decomposition.
In that case, by (
47
),

\displaystyle\langle D(f),g\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\langle f\rangle_{I_{j}}\langle D(\chi_{I_{j}}),g\rangle=\sum_{j}\langle f\rangle_{I_{j}}\langle\chi_{I_{j}}D(\chi_{I^{\prime}}),g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle fD(\chi_{I^{\prime}}),g\rangle=\langle a_{i}f,g\rangle.

with
a_{i}=D(\chi_{I^{\prime}})
. We now show
that

\displaystyle|\langle a_{i}f,g\rangle|\lesssim\epsilon\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

Let
I,J\in\mathcal{D}^{i}(Q)
,
\tilde{J}\in\mathcal{\tilde{D}}^{i}(Q)
with
\ell(I)=\ell(J)
and such that

\tilde{J}
is the interior of
J
. By the definition of

a_{i}
and (
47
) again, we have

(52)

\displaystyle\langle a_{i}\chi_{I},\chi_{\tilde{J}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\chi_{I\cap J}D(\chi_{I^{\prime}}),\chi_{\tilde{J}}\rangle=\langle D(\chi_{I\cap J}),\chi_{\tilde{J}}\rangle

Now, if
I\cap J=\emptyset
, we get

\langle a_{i}\chi_{I},\chi_{\tilde{J}}\rangle=0
.
Otherwise, since
\ell(I)=\ell(J)
, we have
I=J
. Then, by (
52
), the testing condition on
T
and the uniform boundedness of
T_{\gamma,Q}
, we get

\displaystyle|\langle a_{i}\chi_{I},\chi_{\tilde{J}}\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle D\chi_{J},\chi_{\tilde{J}}\rangle|\leq|\langle T\chi_{J},\chi_{\tilde{J}}\rangle|+|\langle T_{\gamma,Q}\chi_{J},\chi_{\tilde{J}}\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|\chi_{J}T\chi_{J}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\|\chi_{J}T_{\gamma,Q}\chi_{J}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)})\mu(\tilde{J})

\displaystyle\lesssim F_{T}(J)\mu(\tilde{J})<\epsilon\mu(\tilde{J}).

We denote now

F=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}^{i}(Q)_{N}}\langle f\rangle_{I}\chi_{I}
and

G=\sum_{\tilde{I}\in\mathcal{\tilde{D}}^{i}(Q)_{N}}\langle g\rangle_{\tilde{I}}\chi_{\tilde{I}}
, with

F_{T}(I)<\epsilon
and
\tilde{I}
being the interior of
I
. Then

\displaystyle|\langle a_{i}F,G\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}^{i}(Q)_{N}}\sum_{\tilde{J}\in\mathcal{\tilde{D}}^{i}(Q)_{N}}\langle f\rangle_{I}\langle g\rangle_{\tilde{J}}\langle a_{i}\chi_{I},\chi_{\tilde{J}}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}^{i}(Q)_{N}}|\langle f\rangle_{I}||\langle g\rangle_{\tilde{I}}||\langle D\chi_{I},\chi_{\tilde{I}}\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}^{i}(Q)_{N}}|\langle f\rangle_{I}||\langle g\rangle_{\tilde{I}}|\mu(\tilde{I})

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\Big(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}^{i}(Q)_{N}}|\langle f\rangle_{I}|^{2}\mu(I)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{\tilde{I}\in\mathcal{\tilde{D}}^{i}(Q)_{N}}|\langle g\rangle_{\tilde{I}}|^{2}\mu(\tilde{I})^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

∎

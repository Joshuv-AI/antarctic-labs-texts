Uniform estimates for a family of Poisson problems: ‘rounding off the corners’ Thanks: V.N. has been partially supported by ANR grant OpART ANR-23-CE40-0016.
Benoît Daniel, Simon Labrunie, Victor Nistor

Abstract
. We prove uniform solvability estimates for certain families of elliptic problems posed in a bounded family of domains (for example, a sequence that converges to another domain). We provide uniform estimates both in weighted and in usual Sobolev spaces. When the limit domain is a polygon and the other domains are smooth, our results amount to “rounding off” the corners of the limit domain. The technique of proof is based on a suitable conformal modification of the metric, which makes the union of the domains a manifold with boundary and relative bounded geometry.

35R01, 35J15, 46E35, 58J32

1.
Introduction

We prove
uniform solvability estimates
for certain families of
elliptic problems posed in a family of domains that is bounded in
a certain sense. The two dimensional case and the higher dimensional
case are somewhat different, so in this paper we include the results
that are common to the two cases, as well as some of the results that
are specific to the two dimensional case.

1.1.
Informal statement of the main result

While most of our results hold in all dimensions and for
suitably bounded families of domains, the most complete
and easiest to state form of our main results is in two dimensions
for a convergent sequence of domains,
so we first formulate informally our main results in this case.
Thus let

\Omega_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

be sequence of
bounded, Lipschitz domains
in the plane,

n\in\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
. We assume
that this sequence “converges” in a certain sense as
n\to\infty

to a domain
\Omega_{\infty}
and we study to what extent we can
obtain
uniform
estimates for the solutions of the
Poisson problems

(1)

\begin{cases}\ \Delta u_{n}\,=\,f_{n}&\text{ in }\ \Omega_{n}\\
\ u_{n}\,=\,0&\text{ on }\,\partial\Omega_{n}\,,\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}\,.\end{cases}

Let us assume that the limit
domain
\Omega_{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a polygonal domain.
We prove that, under suitable bounded geometry assumptions on the
domains
\Omega_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, there exists
\delta_{U}>0

such that, for any
\delta<\delta_{U}
, there exists
C_{\delta}>0

such that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
and any
f_{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{n})
,
the unique solution
u_{n}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{n})
to the Poisson
problem (
1
) satisfies the estimate

(2)

\|u_{n}\|_{H^{1+\delta}(\Omega_{n})}\leq C_{\delta}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{n})}\,.

(See Theorem
3.10
.) Our results remain true if the family

\Omega_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is only bounded, in a certain sense (thus, not
necessarily convergent). Also, as mentioned above, most of our results
are for domains
\Omega_{n}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
.

1.2.
Weighted spaces and the setting

We now outline the setting, the assumptions, and the method
of proof for our main result, Theorem
3.10
, as
well as the statements of some intermediate results.

1.2.1.
Weighted spaces

Most of our intermediate results are usually formulated in the
Babuška-Kondratiev spaces and are interesing on their own.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open subset and

f:G\to(0,\infty)
be a measurable
function. Then the

Babuška-Kondratiev space associated to
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

a\in\mathbb{R}
, the domain
G
, and the weight
f
is the space

\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(G;f){\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\{\,u:G\to\mathbb{C}\mid f^{|\alpha|-a}\partial^{\alpha}u\in L^{2}(G;dx^{2})\,,\ |\alpha|\leq m\,\}\,.

The Babuška-Kondratiev spaces are also called
weighted
Sobolev spaces.
The Babuška-Kondratiev spaces are
(essentially) the usual Sobolev spaces associated to some metrics
conformally equivalent to the Euclidean one. If
G
is a
manifold with boundary and relative bounded geometry
in the metric
f^{-2}dx^{2}
(see Section

2.1
), then
\mathcal{K}_{d/2}^{m}(G;f)=H^{m}(G;f^{-2}dx^{2})
,
the usual Sobolev space on
G
defined with respect to the
metric
f^{-2}dx^{2}
, provided that

df/f\in W^{\infty,\infty}(G;f^{-2}dx^{2})

[
2
]

(a particular case of this result is proved in our paper,
for completeness).
This fact will be important in our proofs.

1.2.2.
Summary of main notations and assumptions

We next introduce in more detail our setting. We also formulate
some of the assumptions needed
for our main result.

All our domains will be
open, bounded sets.

We shall let

(iv)

\hat{g}_{n}:=r_{n}^{-2}dx^{2}

is

is

Recall

3
]
that a manifold with

is a manifold with
boundary and relative bounded geometry such that the distance to the boundary
is bounded globally (Definition
2.15
). These concepts are
recalled in detail in Section

. We next introduce the most significant
assumption on the sets
\Omega_{n}
and

,

.

Assumption 1.3
.

For each

In addition to the metric

.
Let

Since the domains

where

1.3.
Statement of the main result

We shall assume in this subsection that
d=2
and hence consider
the
Laplacian

\Delta=\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2}
on
various spaces. It will always be defined on functions vanishing at
the boundary, that is, we consider the
Dirichlet Laplacian

\Delta_{D}
.
In addition to this “
D
,” we may decorate the Laplacian
\Delta
with some
additional indices that will specify its domain (as in the next theorem).

Theorem 1.4
.

We assume
1.2
and
1.3
.
In particular,
r=r_{n}:=\eta(_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(x,\mathcal{V}_{n}))
and

\hat{g}=r_{n}^{-2}dx^{2}
on
\Omega_{n}\equiv\{n\}\times\Omega_{n}
and

We also assume that
(U,\hat{g})

has

(Definition

).
Then, there exists

\delta_{U}>0
such that, for every
|a|<\delta_{U}
and every

m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}:=\{0,1,\ldots\}
, the Dirichlet Laplacian

induces an isomorphism

\Delta_{D,m,a}:\mathcal{K}_{a+1}^{m+1}(U;r)\cap\{u|_{\partial U}=0\}\to\mathcal{K}_{a-1}^{m-1}(U;r)\,.

This is a consequence of the well-posedness of the Laplacian on
manifolds with finite width
[
3
]
, where the concept of
“finite width” was introduced. This result and the definiton
of manifolds with finite width is recalled in Section
2.1
.
When a single domain is considered (i.e.,

is reduced to
a single element, the result of the last theorem is a classical theorem
due to Kondratiev
[

,
26
]

Recall that if the domain

is an isomorphism.
An interpolation argument and the direct sum decomposition
in (
3
) then give the following consequence.

Corollary 1.5
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.4
, for any

\delta<\delta_{U}
, there exists a constant

such that Equation (

) holds, that is,
for any

and any

,
we have

\|\Delta_{D}^{-1}f_{n}\|_{H^{1+\delta}(\Omega_{n})}\leq C_{\delta}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{n})}\,.

In Section
4
we provide conditions on the sequences

(\Omega_{n},\mathcal{V}_{n})
such that the conditions of Theorem
1.4

and its corollary, Corollary
1.5
, are satisfied (most notably,
such that
(U,\hat{g})
be a manifold with boundary
and relative bounded geometry). We also provide a concrete, explicit
construction of such a sequence for any given curvilinear polygonal

\Omega_{\infty}
.

We allow the case

are empty. Then the functions

will be constant and

is actually smooth (no corners). Even
in that case, our results are new. See

[

,
18
,
29

for other types of boundedness

2.1.
Manifolds with bounded geometry

Throughout this paper,
(M,g)
will be a

smooth Riemannian manifold

(without boundary) of dimension
d
, unless specified otherwise.
where, we recall,
g
is a smoothly varying inner product on
the tangent spaces
T_{p}M
,
p\in M
. If
E\to M
is a smooth vector
bundle, we let
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M;E)
be the space of smooth sections of
E
.
A
connection
on
E
is a differential operator

(4)

\begin{gathered}\nabla^{E}:{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M;E)\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M;T^{*}M\otimes E)\quad\mbox{satisfying}\\
\nabla^{E}(f\xi)\,=\,df\otimes\xi+f\nabla^{E}\xi\,.\end{gathered}

We also write
\nabla_{X}\xi:=\langle X,\nabla\xi\rangle\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M;E)

if
X\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M;TM)
, where the pairing
\langle\,,\,\rangle

is the contraction
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M;TM)\otimes{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M;T^{*}M)\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M)
.
If
E
is endowed with a metric
g^{E}
and
\nabla^{E}
satisfies

g^{E}(\nabla_{X}\xi,\eta)+g^{E}(\xi,\nabla_{X}\eta)\,=\,Xg^{E}(\xi,\eta)\,,

then we say that
\nabla^{E}
is
metric preserving.

We endow
TM
with the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{M}\colon{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M;TM)\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M;T^{*}M\otimes TM)
, which is the unique metric
preserving connection on
TM
that is also “torsion-free,” in the
sense that
\nabla_{X}^{M}Y-\nabla_{Y}^{M}X=[X,Y]
for all (smooth) vector
fields
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M;TM)
.

We endow each tensor bundle
T^{*\otimes k}M
with the induced
(Levi-Civita) tensor product connection:

\nabla_{X}(\xi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\xi_{k}):=\nabla_{X}(\xi_{1})\otimes\ldots\otimes\xi_{k}+\xi_{1}\otimes\nabla_{X}(\xi_{2})\otimes\ldots\otimes\xi_{k}+\ldots+\xi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\nabla_{X}(\xi_{k})\,.

We also endow all other tensor bundles with the induced tensor
product connections, generically denoted by
\nabla
. (Thus we do not
try to always distinguish between the connections on different vector
bundles, except for the Levi-Civita connection.)

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
M
has
totally bounded curvature

if its curvatures
R^{M}:=(\nabla^{M})^{2}
and all its covariant
derivatives
(\nabla^{M})^{k}R^{M}
are bounded.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval. A
C^{1}
-curve
\gamma:I\to M

is a
geodesic
if
\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}(t)}^{M}\gamma^{\prime}(t)=0
. It is
locally distance minimizing and uniquely determined by any
\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})
,

t_{0}\in I
. If
M=\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the usual metric, then a geodesic is just
a (part of a) straight line.
Let
\exp^{M}\colon\mathcal{U}\subset TM\to M
be the geodesic exponential map
associated to the metric (where
\mathcal{U}
is a suitable open neighborhood of
the zero section), that is,
\exp^{M}(v)=\gamma_{v}(1)
, where
\gamma_{v}
is
the unique geodesic with
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=v
.
By
\exp_{p}^{M}\colon\mathcal{U}\cap T_{p}M\to M
we denote the
restriction of the geodesic
exponential map to
T_{p}M\cap\mathcal{U}
.

We shall need the
injectivity radius

\mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}}(M)
of a Riemannian
manifold
(M,g)
. To define it, for any metric space
(X,d)
,

p\in X
, and
r\in\mathbb{R}
, we let

(5)

B_{r}^{X}(x){\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\{y\in X\mid d(x,y)<r\}\,,

that is, the
open ball of radius
r
and center
x
in
X
.

(We shall usually assume that
r>0
, to obtain a non-empty set.)
In particular,
B_{r}^{M}(p)
will be the open
ball of radius
r
in
M
centered at
p\in M
. We endow
T_{p}M

with the induced metric, so
B_{r}^{T_{p}M}(0)
is the set of tangent
vectors at
M
in
p
of length
<r
. With this preparation, we
let

(6)

\begin{gathered}\mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}^{M}}(p)\mathrel{\ :=\ }\sup\{r\mid\exp_{p}^{M}\colon B_{r}^{T_{p}M}(0)\to B_{r}^{M}(p)\text{ is a diffeomorphism}\}\ \ \mbox{ and}\\
\mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}}(M)\mathrel{\ :=\ }\inf_{p\in M}\mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}^{M}}(p).\end{gathered}

The following concept will play a crucial role in this paper.

Definition 2.2
.

A boundaryless Riemannian smooth manifold
(M,g)
is said to have

bounded geometry
if
M
has totally bounded curvature
(Definition
2.1
)
and
\mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}}(M)>0
.

There exist many equivalent definitions of manifolds with bounded
geometry in the literature, see also
[
1
,
13
,
17
,
24
,
25
,
32
]
.
We now include some examples of manifolds with bounded geometry,
some of which will be needed in what follows.

Examples 2.3
.

The following are manifolds with bounded geometry:

(i)

A closed manifold (i.e. a smooth, compact manifold without
boundary).

(ii)

with the standard
(Euclidean) metric

dx^{2}:=(dx_{1})^{2}+(dx_{2})^{2}+\ldots+(dx_{d})^{2}\,.

(iii)

(iv)

Let us notice, however, that, if

In relation to the phenomenon mentioned at the end of the last remark,
we shall need the following concept (recall the notation of
Equation
6
):

Definition 2.4
.

Let
N
be a Riemannian manifold with
totally bounded curvature. A subset
K\subset N
is said
to be a subset of

if

\inf_{x\in K}\mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}^{N}}(x)>0.

We have the following simple result (compare with Corollary 2.24 of
[
3
]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold
without boundary

and
W_{i}\subset M
,
i=1,\ldots,k
, be open subsets.
We assume that

(i)

(W_{i},g)
has totally bounded curvature for any
i=1,\ldots,k
,

(ii)

there exist subsets

of relative positive injectivity

Then

Proof.

The first condition ensures that
M
has
totally bounded curvature. Let

r{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\big(\inf_{x\in W_{i}^{\prime}}\ \mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}^{W_{i}}}(x)\big)\,.

Then
r>0
, by the second assumption (see Definition
2.4
).
Let

be arbitrary. Let

be such that

. Then
the geodesic ball

of radius

is a diffeomorphic image of the exponential map, again by the
second assumption. This shows
that the injectivity radius of

is
\geq r>0
.
∎

denote the metric on

.

Let us rescale our metric so that

We shall use Lemma

For each

2.2.
Manifolds with boundary and relative bounded geometry

Let now
M_{0}
be a Riemannian manifold
with boundary,

then
\mathop{r_{\mathrm{inj}}}(M_{0})=0
, so a manifold with non-empty
boundary will never have bounded geometry in the sense of the above
definition. The way around this conundrum was found by Schick

[
31
]
, who has defined the concept of “manifold with
boundary and bounded geometry,” (we shall call these manifolds
“manifolds with boundary and
relative
bounded geometry,”
to avoid confusions). We recall the equivalent definition of
manifolds with boundary and relative bounded geometry in
[
3
]
.
The main point of that definition is to assume that the boundary

\partial M_{0}
of

is a suitable submanifold of a
(boundaryless) manifold
M
with bounded geometry.

Let hence
M
be a (boundaryless) manifold with bounded geometry
and let us consider a hypersurface
H\subset M
, that is, a
submanifold
H
of
M
of codimension
\dim(M)-\dim(H)=1
.
We assume that
H
carries a globally defined unit normal vector

\nu

(14)

\exp^{\perp}(x,t)\mathrel{\ :=\ }\exp_{x}^{M}(t\nu_{x})

be the exponential in the direction of the chosen unit normal vector.
We shall need the
second fundamental form

\mathrm{I}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{H}
of

in

\mathrm{I}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{H}(X,Y)\nu\mathrel{\ :=\ }\nabla^{M}_{X}Y-\nabla^{H}_{X}Y

[
28
, page 149]

is the Levi-Civita connection
of a Riemannian manifold

, we have

\mathrm{I}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{H}(X,Y)\mathrel{\ :=\ }g(\nu,\nabla^{M}_{X}Y)

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold of bounded geometry and

be a hypersurface with unit normal vector field

on

. We say that

(ii)

(iii)

We shall denote by

If

Example 2.10

Let

be a

compact
hypersurface (it may have a non-empty boundary).

is a bounded geometry hypersurface in

.

We are ready now to recall the definition of a central concept
in this paper.

Definition 2.11
.

We shall say that
M_{0}

is a manifold with boundary and

is isometrically contained
in a (boundaryless) Riemannian manifold

is a bounded geometry hypersurface in

Note that we use the term
“manifold with boundary and

bounded geometry,”
which we think is more
precise than the term

“manifold with boundary and bounded geometry”
used in

[
3
,
31
]
and in other papers; in particular, it

We will also need the following results concerning hypersurfaces.
Let

If

We use the notation recalled in the above paragraph. Let

This follows from well-known arguments; see for instance

Let

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold and
\sigma

Let

\mathrm{I}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{H}_{\sigma}(X,Y)=\sigma\mathrm{I}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{H}(X,Y)-g\left(\nu,\nabla^{M}\sigma\right)g(X,Y)

\displaystyle 2g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)

2.3.

3.1.
Babuška-Kondratiev (weighted Sobolev) spaces

We consider

arbitrary and the setting

See, especially, assumption

In particular, the sets
\mathcal{V}_{n}=\{p_{1n},p_{2n},\ldots,p_{kN}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

the manifold
(\widehat{M},\hat{g}){\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\cup_{n\in\mathfrak{I}}(\{n\}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\smallsetminus\mathcal{V}_{n}),\hat{g}_{n})

\hat{g}_{n}{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}r_{n}^{-2}dx^{2}
,
is as in Proposition
2.6
.
We shall also consider

We let

be as in Assumption

, that is,

is the

\Omega_{n}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
G\subset\widehat{M}
be an open subset.
The Babuška-Kondratiev spaces on
G
associated to
the weight
r
are then

\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(G;r){\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\{\,u:G\to\mathbb{C}\mid r^{|\alpha|-a}\partial^{\alpha}u\in L^{2}(G,dx^{2})\,,\ |\alpha|\leq m\,\}\,.

(Compare to the Introduction.) We endow the spaces
\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(G;r)

with the resulting norms. Recall that

\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(\Omega_{n};r_{n}){\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\{\,u:\Omega_{n}\to\mathbb{C}\mid r_{n}^{|\alpha|-a}\partial^{\alpha}u\in L^{2}(\Omega_{n})\,,\ |\alpha|\leq m\,\}

(Definition
1.1
).
It follows that

\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(U;r)\simeq\oplus_{n\in\mathfrak{I}}\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})\,,

(Hilbert space direct sum). The same relation holds also for
\widehat{M}
.

The following lemma is also well-known and explains why we are
interested in Sobolev spaces on manifolds. The following lemma
follows, for instance, from the fact that
r
is an admissible
weight (i.e.
dr/r\in W^{\infty,\infty}(M;TM)
, where
TM

is endowed with the metric
\hat{g}
, see Lemma

2.7
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\widehat{M}\subset\mathfrak{I}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

be as in Proposition
2.6
, in particular, its
metric is
\hat{g}=r^{-2}dx^{2}
. Then

(i)

We have an isomorphism of
normed spaces
H^{m}(\widehat{M};\hat{g})\simeq\mathcal{K}_{d/2}(\widehat{M};r)

(ii)

Let us also assume that
U{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\cup_{n\in\mathfrak{I}}\{n\}\times\Omega_{n}\subset\widehat{M}
is a manifold with boundary
and relative bounded geometry, then

H^{m}(U;\hat{g})\,=\,\mathcal{K}_{d/2}^{m}(U;r)\,.

Proof.

The map

W^{\infty,\infty}(\widehat{M})^{d}\ni(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{d})\to r(f_{1}\partial_{1}+f_{2}\partial_{1}+\ldots f_{d}\partial_{d})\in W^{\infty,\infty}(\widehat{M};\widehat{M})

is an isomorphism of Fréchet spaces.
In other words, the vector fields
r\partial_{j}
form a topological
(or Fréchet) basis of all the vector fields on
\widehat{M}
with bounded
covariant derivatives. Hence the assumptions of Proposition 3.2 of

[
16
]
or Proposition 5.17(iv)

[
22
]
are satisfied. (These vectors are also an orthonormal basis
at every point of
\widehat{M}
, which is more than is required by these propositions.)
Since
\widehat{M}
has bounded geometry,
by Proposition
2.6
, these propositions then give (the intuitively
obvious) first relation of the following sequence of equalities

\displaystyle H^{m}(\widehat{M};\hat{g})

\displaystyle\,=\,\{u:\widehat{M}\to\mathbb{C}\mid(r\partial)^{\alpha}u\in L^{2}(\widehat{M};\hat{g})\,,\ \forall\,|\alpha|\leq m\}

\displaystyle\,=\,\{u:\widehat{M}\to\mathbb{C}\mid r^{-d/2}(r\partial)^{\alpha}u\in L^{2}(\widehat{M};dx^{2})\,,\ \forall\,|\alpha|\leq m\}

\displaystyle\,=\,\{u:\widehat{M}\to\mathbb{C}\mid r^{|\alpha|-d/2}\partial^{\alpha}u\in L^{2}(\widehat{M};dx^{2})\,,\ \forall\,|\alpha|\leq m\}\ =:\ \mathcal{K}_{d/2}^{m}(\widehat{M};r)\,.

The second equality above follows from the fact that the volume form with
respect
\hat{g}
is
r^{-d}
times the volume form for
dx^{2}

and the third equality above follows from the Lemma
2.8
.
All the maps are continuous, and hence, by Banach’s theorem, they are
isomorphisms of the corresponding normed spaces. The second part is proved
in exactly the same way, but using only Proposition 3.2 of
[
16
]

(which was proved for manifolds with boundary and relative bounded geometry)
and the assumption that
U
has relative bounded geometry (instead of
Proposition
2.6
).
∎

See also
[
2
]
. The first part also follows immediately
from the results in
[
4
]
because
\widehat{M}
is a Lie manifold.
In the same spirit, we shall need the following lemma (which is
well-known for bounded domains).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Then multiplication by
r^{b}
induces an
isomorphism

r^{b}:\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(U;r)\to\mathcal{K}_{a+b}^{m}(U;r)\,,

whose norm depends continuously on
b
.

Proof.

This also follows from Corollary
2.7
.
Indeed, the norm
u\in\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(U;r)
is

\|u\|_{\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(U;r)}^{2}{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|r^{|\alpha|-a}\partial^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}(U;dx^{2})}^{2}\,.

We have

r^{|\alpha|-a-b}\partial^{\alpha}(r^{b}u)\,=\,\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}{\alpha\choose\beta}r^{|\alpha-\beta|-b}\big(\partial^{\alpha-\beta}r^{b}\big)r^{|\beta|-a}\partial^{\beta}u\,.

Let us write
A\lesssim B
when there is a
C>0

independent of the relevant data (independent of
u

in the case below) such that
A\leq CB
.
Since
r^{|\alpha-\beta|-b}\partial^{\alpha-\beta}r^{b}\in L^{\infty}(\widehat{M})
, by Corollary
2.7
,
we successively obtain

\displaystyle\|r^{b}u\|_{\mathcal{K}_{a+b}^{m}(U;r)}^{2}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|r^{|\alpha|-a-b}\partial^{\alpha}(r^{b}u)\|_{L^{2}(U;dx^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\ \lesssim\ \sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}\|r^{|\alpha-\beta|-b}\partial^{\alpha-\beta}r^{b}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|r^{|\beta|-a}\partial^{\beta}u\|_{L^{2}}\ \lesssim\ \|u\|_{\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(U;r)}^{2}\,.

Using the continuity in
b
in Corollary
2.7

and by separating the terms with
\beta=0
from the other ones,
the same calculation gives

\|r^{b}u\|_{\mathcal{K}_{a+b}^{m}(U;r)}^{2}\leq\|u\|_{\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(U;r)}^{2}+C|b|\,.

(because all the terms with
\beta\neq 0
contain a factor of
b
),
with
C
independent of
a
(but possibly depending on
\alpha
).
Replacing
u
with
r^{-b}u
, using the continuity of multiplication
with
r^{-b}
and then adjusting the indices, we
obtain

\|u\|_{\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(U;r)}^{2}\leq\|r^{b}u\|_{\mathcal{K}_{a+b}^{m}(U;r)}^{2}+C|b|\,,

and hence the continuity of the norm at
b=0
. The continuity at other
values follow from the continuity at 0 by reparametrization in
a
and

b
.
∎

Uniform estimates in Babuška-Kondratiev spaces

We now turn to the precise statements and proofs of Theorem

From now on

In addition to Assumption

we also assume from now on Assumption

,

We are ready now to prove our first main result, which is

Theorem 3.4
.

We continue to assume
1.2
and
1.3
,
in particular, the manifold with boundary
(U,\hat{g}):=\cup_{n\in\mathfrak{I}}(\Omega_{n},r_{n}^{-2}dx^{2})

Let us also assume
d=2
, so
\Omega_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
then the Dirichlet Laplacian induces isomorphisms

\Delta_{D,m}{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\Delta:\mathcal{K}_{1}^{m+1}(U;r)\cap\{u|_{\partial U}=0\}\to\mathcal{K}_{-1}^{m-1}(U;r)\,,\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\,.

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{\hat{g}}
be the Laplace operator on
U
associated to the
metric
\hat{g}
. Let
H^{j}(U,\hat{g})
be the Sobolev space of order
j
defined by
the metric
\hat{g}
, as before. It follows from Theorem
2.17

that, for any

,

(15)

\Delta_{\hat{g}}:H^{m+1}(U,\hat{g})\cap H_{0}^{1}(U;\hat{g})\to H^{m-1}(U,\hat{g})\,,

is an isomorphism. As in Lemma
3.2

(16)

H^{m}(U,\hat{g})\simeq\mathcal{K}_{1}^{m}(U;r)\,,

the index one in
\mathcal{K}_{1}^{m}
is half of the ambient dimension
(which is 2).
We also have that
\Delta_{\hat{g}}=r^{2}\Delta_{D,m}
(this is specific to

of equations (
15
) and (
16

of Lemma (
3.3
) with the relation
\Delta_{\hat{g}}=r^{2}\Delta_{D,m}
.
∎

Given the direct sum decomposition

\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(U;r)\simeq\oplus_{n\in\mathfrak{I}}\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})
(Hilbert space direct sum, see

and that the Laplacian

Corollary 3.5
.

We keep the assumptions and notation of Theorem
3.4
.
Then the Dirichlet Laplacian
\Delta_{D}
induces an isomorphism

\Delta_{D,m,n}{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\Delta:\mathcal{K}_{1}^{m+1}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})\cap\{u|_{\partial\Omega_{n}}=0\}\to\mathcal{K}_{-1}^{m-1}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})\,,\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\,,

such that the norms of the operators

are uniformly bounded in

.

A perturbation argument applied to the continuous
family

then yields the proof
of Theorem
1.4
. We give here a slightly stronger
formulation. We also use the formulation obtained
by decomposing our spaces as a direct sum
in
n\in\mathfrak{I}
, as in the previous corollary.

Theorem 3.6
.

We keep the assumptions and notation of Theorem

3.4
, so, in particular,
d=2
.
Then there exists
\delta_{U}>0
with the following property.
For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and any
|a|<\delta_{U}
,
the Dirichlet Laplacian
\Delta_{D}
induces isomorphisms

\Delta_{D,m,n,a}{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\Delta:\mathcal{K}_{a+1}^{m+1}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})\cap\{u|_{\partial\Omega_{n}}=0\}\to\mathcal{K}_{a-1}^{m-1}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})

and

Proof.

Only the indices
n
and
a
make a difference, so we shall write

\Delta_{n,a}
instead of
\Delta_{D,m,n,a}
(

“D” is only to remind us that we are using Dirichlet boundary

\Delta_{n,a}
differ, their form does not.
At distance
\geq R/5
to
\mathcal{V}_{n}
,

is constant and hence we have

r^{-a}\Delta_{n,a}r^{a}=r_{n}^{-a}\Delta_{n,a}r_{n}^{a}=\Delta_{n,a}
.
Next, let us assume we are on
\Omega_{n}\equiv\{n\}\times\Omega_{n}
,

n\in\mathfrak{I}
at distance
\leq R/5

P_{jn}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}

by our assumptions, see point (
ii
)
in Section
1.2

Let
\rho
be the distance function to
p_{jn}
.
Then
r_{n}=\eta(\rho)
. Let

A{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\frac{\rho\eta^{\prime}(\rho)}{\eta(\rho)}\,.

Our assumptions on
\eta
imply that
A
has support inside the ball
of radius

and that, near

, it is equal to

. Hence

extends to a function all of whose derivatives are bounded, and
hence to a multiplier of all the spaces
\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(U;r)

(Equivalently,
A
defines a bounded operator on each

\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})

and the norms of these operators are bounded uniformly in

.)
In polar coordinates
(\rho,\theta)
near that vertex, we have

(17)

\begin{gathered}r^{-a}\Delta_{n,a}r^{a}-\Delta_{n,0}\,=\,\rho^{-2}\big((\rho\partial_{\rho}+aA)^{2}+\partial_{\theta}^{2})-\rho^{-2}\big((\rho\partial_{\rho})^{2}+\partial_{\theta}^{2})\\
\,=\,\rho^{-2}\big[2aA\rho\partial_{\rho}+a\rho\partial_{\rho}(A)+a^{2}A^{2}]\end{gathered}

Since the operators
A:\mathcal{K}_{b}^{m}(U;r)\to\mathcal{K}_{b}^{m}(U;r)
and

\rho\partial_{\rho}:\mathcal{K}_{b}^{m}(U;r)\to\mathcal{K}_{b}^{m-1}(U;r)
are bounded,
we obtain that the norm

\|r^{-a}\Delta_{n,a}r^{a}-\Delta_{n,0}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{1}^{m+1}(U;r),\mathcal{K}_{-1}^{m+1}(U;r)}\leq C_{m}a\,,

with
C_{m}
independent of
n\in\mathfrak{I}
and

a\in[-\gamma,\gamma]

(but depending on
m
and
\gamma
).

We know from Theorem
3.4

that
\Delta_{n,0}
is invertible and the norm of its inverse
is independent of

. Hence, for
a
small enough
(
|a|<\delta_{U,m}
, depending on
m
at this time),

r^{-a}\Delta_{n,a}r^{a}

to the claimed invertibility of
\Delta_{n,a}
in view of
the fact that

(Corollary

).

We are just left to prove that we can choose the bound for

independently of

. Indeed, let
\delta_{U,0}

be such that, for all

\Delta_{n,a}{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\Delta:\mathcal{K}_{a+1}^{1}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})\cap\{u|_{\partial\Omega_{n}}=0\}\to\mathcal{K}_{a-1}^{-1}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})

∎

3.3.
Returning to the usual Sobolev spaces

Recall that a
contraction
is a continuous linear map of norm

\leq 1
between normed spaces. We shall use the following lemma
(in which it is crucial that we have chosen the weights
r_{n}\leq 1
).
We use the notation and hypotheses of Assumptions
1.2

and Assuptions
1.3
, which were briefly recalled
in the previous section.

Lemma 3.7
.

For all
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
n\in\mathfrak{I}
,
the maps
\mathcal{K}_{m}^{m}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})\to H^{m}(\Omega_{n})

are continuous of norm
\leq 1
(i.e.
contractions
).
Similarly, if
a\leq b
, then the map
\mathcal{K}_{b}^{m}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})\to\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})
is a contraction.

Proof.

Recall that
\eta\leq R/6\leq 1/6
. Then,
for any multi-index
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq m
, we have

r_{n}^{|\alpha|-m}\geq 6^{m-|\alpha|}\geq 1.

Hence

\|r_{n}^{|\alpha|-m}\partial^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{n})}\geq\|\partial^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{n})}\,,

which gives
\|u\|_{H^{m}(\Omega_{n})}\leq\|u\|_{\mathcal{K}_{m}^{m}(\Omega_{n})}
, as desired.
The second part follows similarly from the fact that

r_{n}\leq 1
.
∎

We need one final lemma before being able to relate our results
in weighted spaces to the usual Sobolev spaces.
Given three Banach spaces
X,Y,Z
,
X\cup Y\subset Z
,
we shall denote by
[X,Y]_{\theta}
their complex interpolation
space, where
\theta\in(0,1)
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open subset, let
f>0
be a
continuous function on
G
, and let
\theta\in(0,1)
. We then
have the isomorphism

[\mathcal{K}_{a}^{m}(G;f),\mathcal{K}_{b}^{m}(G;f)]_{\theta}\simeq\mathcal{K}_{(1-\theta)a+\theta b}^{m}(G;f).

We obtain the following needed corollary.

Corollary 3.9
.

For all
a\in[0,1]
and
n\in\mathfrak{I}
,
the map
\mathcal{K}_{1+a}^{2}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})\to H^{1+a}(\Omega_{n})

is well defined and continuous of norm
\leq 1
.

Proof.

For
a=1
, the result is simply Lemma
3.7
for

m=2
. The map
\mathcal{K}_{1}^{2}(\Omega_{n})\to\mathcal{K}_{1}^{1}(\Omega_{n})\to H^{1}(\Omega_{n})
is a contraction as composition of contractions
(again by Lemma
3.7
). This gives the result for

a=0
. For
a\in(0,1)
, the result follows by interpolation,
see Lemma
3.8
, since our spaces
H^{s}(G)

are defined by interpolation.
∎

Using the continuous map
\mathcal{K}_{1+\delta}^{2}(\Omega_{n})\to H^{1+\delta}(\Omega_{n})
,
we obtain the desired result for
\delta:=a-\delta_{U}
,
as follows.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\Omega_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a sequence of
domains as in Theorem
3.6
(i.e.
satisfying assumptions
1.2

and
1.3
). Let
\delta_{U}>0
be as in
Theorem
3.6
and let
|a|<\delta_{U}
,
a\leq 1
.
Then there exists
C_{H}>0
such that, for any

f_{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{n})
,
n\in\mathfrak{I}
,

\|\Delta_{D,n}^{-1}f_{n}\|_{H^{1+a}}\leq C_{H}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{n})}\,.

Proof.

Let
C_{K}
be the bound of the inverses of the
operators

\Delta_{D,n}:\mathcal{K}_{1+a}^{2}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})\cap\{u|_{\partial\Omega_{n}}=0\}\to\mathcal{K}_{-1+a}^{0}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})

in Theorem
3.6
,

.
We have
L^{2}(\Omega_{n})=\mathcal{K}_{0}^{0}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})\to\mathcal{K}_{-1+a}^{0}(\Omega_{n};r_{n})
,
where the last map is a contraction (since

).
Hence, combining this discussion with Corollary

,
we obtain

\|\Delta_{D,n}^{-1}f_{n}\|_{H^{1+a}(\Omega_{n})}\leq\|\Delta_{D,n}^{-1}f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{1+a}^{2}(\Omega_{n})}\leq C_{K}\|f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{a-1}^{0}(\Omega_{n})}\\
\leq C_{K}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{n})}\,.

This completes the proof with

.
∎

4.
Approximating a polygon

We now construct an example of a family of domains

More precisely, given a straight polygonal domain

,
we now construct a completely explicit sequence of smooth,

converge to

. For this choice of domains,
we check that all the conditions of Assumptions

1.2
and
1.3
are satisfied.
(So, from now on, we shall use the
notation
of those assumptions,
but we do not assume them to be satisfied; we rather prove
that they are satisfied).

In this section, we continue to assume

(so our domains

as in the last two sections, Sections

4.1.
Curvilinear polygonal domains

We provide now a rather general definition of a
“curvilinear polygonal domain.”

Definition 4.1
.

Let
M
be a two-dimensional smooth manifold and
D\subset M
.
We shall say that
D
is
a curvilinear polygonal domain

in
M
if the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

D
is open in
M
and
\overline{D}
is compact.

(ii)

There exist a finite set of points

\mathcal{V}{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\{q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,q_{N}\}\subset\partial D{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\overline{D}\smallsetminus D

and
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-curves
\gamma_{k}:[0,1]\to M
,

k=1,\ldots,N
, such that
\gamma_{k}(0)=q_{k}
and

\gamma_{k}(1)=q_{k+1}
(we set
q_{N+1}=q_{1}
), satisfying
also the following conditions.

(iii)

The sets
\gamma_{k}\big((0,1)\big)
are disjoint.

(iv)

\partial D\smallsetminus\mathcal{V}=\cup_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}\big((0,1)\big).

(v)

For each
k
,
\gamma_{k}
is smooth on
(0,1)
.

Thus, the sets
\gamma_{k}((0,1))
together with
\mathcal{V}
, form a
partition of the boundary
\partial D
of
D
and

\partial D=\cup_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}\big([0,1]\big)
. Note that we

do not
assume that any of the
\gamma_{k}
is contained in
a straight line, hence the terminology “curvilinear polygonal domain.”
Note also then that the sides (or edges)
\gamma_{k}([0,1])
of
D
do not
have self-intersections (they may intersect only in
\mathcal{V}
). See also

[
12
,
23
,
26
]
. More general polygonal
domains were used in
[
27
]
(in that paper,
cracks and self-intersections were allowed, the point being that
these “degeneracies” did not affect the optimal rates of convergence
in the Finite Element Method).

Remark 4.2
.

The set
\mathcal{V}
of Definition
4.1
is
not

determined uniquely by
D
(in fact, the
set
\mathcal{V}
can always be enlarged). However, there will always
be a
minimal
such set, which we will
denote by
\mathcal{V}_{D}
; it is uniquely determined by the condition
that, for all
k=1,\ldots,N
,

\gamma_{k}
and
\gamma_{k+1}
cannot be joined in a smooth
curve (we set
\gamma_{N+1}=\gamma_{1}
). The elements
of this minimal set
\mathcal{V}_{D}
are called
the vertices
of
D
.

Given two points
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d
arbitrary here),
we shall let
[AB]
denote the
straight line segment

joining
A
and
B
. Thus
[AB]
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We shall use this for
d=2
to say that
D
is a

straight polygonal domain
if, for all
k
,

\gamma_{k}([0,1])=[q_{k}q_{k+1}]
. That is, all edges of

D
are straight line segments.

The sets

and

Let

\bullet

Remarks

and

for

. We shall use homotheties.

Notation 4.3
.

For

and

It follows from definitions that

Remark 4.4
.

Let

is

.

The following remark summarizes some additional properties of

and

.

Remark 4.5
.

Let

(ii)

Finally, the following remark explains the need for

Let

4.3.
Proof that the ‘distinguished’
U
has finite width

We use the notation and constructions introduced in the previous
sections (see, for example, Assumptions
1.2
and

1.3
). However, we stress that, in this section,
we are
not assuming

1.2
and

1.3
, but rather prove that they are satisfied by our
constructions.

Let us recall first some of the needed constructions and notations
from Subsection
1.2
. First, we endow each

\Omega_{n}
,
n\in\mathfrak{I}
, with
the metric
\hat{g}_{n}=r_{n}^{-2}(dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2})
, where

dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}
is the usual (euclidean) metric on
\mathbb{R}^{2}

and
r_{n}(x):=\eta_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathcal{V}_{n}))
, with

\eta
is our fixed function of Assumption
1.2
.
Let
U
be the
disjoint union
of the domains
\Omega_{n}
,

n\in\mathfrak{I}
, that is

U{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\cup_{n\in\mathfrak{I}}\{n\}\times\Omega_{n}\subset\mathfrak{I}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\,,

as in Equation (
3.1
).
We endow
U
with the metric
\hat{g}
that on the component
\Omega_{n}

coincides with
\hat{g}_{n}
. Thus, if
r(n,x)=r_{n}(x)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

x\in\Omega_{n}
, then

(22)

\hat{g}\,=\,r^{-2}(dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2})\,.

If the sequence
\Omega_{n}
is as in the previous section
(Section
4.2
), then we shall call the associated

U
the
distinguished

U
.

We will now show that the geodesic curvature of
\partial U
and it
derivatives of all orders are bounded. This is enough in view of
Remark
4.7
. We choose
\rho=R/2
and
rescale such that
4\rho=R=1
. (So
R_{0}\geq 2
.)
We shall need the following remark.

Remark 4.7
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold of dimension
d=2
,
then
H
is a curve. Let
s
be a choice of arc-length coordinate on
H
,
let
\partial_{s}
be the associated unit length tangent vector field
to
H
, and let
\nu
the unit normal to
H
. Then
g(\partial_{s},\nu)=0

and
g(\nu,\nabla^{H}_{\partial_{s}}\partial_{s})=0
.
Therefore, the second fundamental form
\mathrm{I}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{H}
of
H
is given by

\mathrm{I}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{H}(\partial_{s},\partial_{s})\,=\,g(\nu,\nabla^{M}_{\partial_{s}}\partial_{s}-\nabla^{H}_{\partial_{s}}\partial_{s})\,=\,g(\nu,\nabla^{M}_{\partial_{s}}\partial_{s})\,=\,-g(\partial_{s},\nabla^{M}_{\partial_{s}}\nu)\,=:\,\kappa(s),

that is,
\mathrm{I}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{H}(\partial_{s},\partial_{s})
coincides with the geodesic curvature

\kappa(s)
of
H
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\sigma
be a smooth function on an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
The geodesic curvature
\mathring{\kappa}
of a curve in
U
in the
Euclidean metric
dx^{2}
and its geodesic curvature
\kappa
in the
metric
\sigma^{2}dx^{2}
are related by

(23)

\kappa=\frac{\mathring{\kappa}}{\sigma}-\frac{\langle\mathring{\nabla}\sigma,\mathring{\nu}\rangle}{\sigma^{2}}

where
\mathring{\nu}
is the unit (for the Euclidean metric) normal
vector
to the curve,
\mathring{\nabla}
is the Levi-Cività connection of
the Euclidean metric, and
\langle\,,\,\rangle
is the Euclidean inner product.

Proof.

This formula is well-known and follows from Lemma
2.13
and Remark
4.7
.
Let
\mathring{\mathrm{I}}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{H}
be the second fundamental form of
H
for the metric
dx^{2}
and and unit normal
\mathring{\nu}
. Then
\frac{1}{\sigma}\mathring{\nu}
has norm
1
for the metric
\sigma^{2}dx^{2}
, so we can consider the second fundamental form
\mathrm{I}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{H}_{\sigma}
of
H
for the metric
\sigma^{2}dx^{2}
and unit normal
\frac{1}{\sigma}\mathring{\nu}
.

Let
s
be a choice of arc-length coordinate on
H
for the Euclidean metric
dx^{2}
.
We have

\mathring{\kappa}=\mathring{\mathrm{I}}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{H}(\partial_{s},\partial_{s}),

and, since
\frac{1}{\sigma}\partial_{s}
has norm
1
for the metric
\sigma^{2}dx^{2}
,

\kappa=\mathrm{I}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{H}_{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}\partial_{s},\frac{1}{\sigma}\partial_{s}\right)=\frac{1}{\sigma}\mathring{\mathrm{I}}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{H}(\partial_{s},\partial_{s})-\frac{\langle\mathring{\nabla}\sigma,\mathring{\nu}\rangle}{\sigma^{2}}=\frac{\mathring{\kappa}(s)}{\sigma}-\frac{\langle\mathring{\nabla}\sigma,\mathring{\nu}\rangle}{\sigma^{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Consider the metric
\tilde{g}=\frac{dx^{2}}{\eta(|x|)^{2}}
on

\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
.
Then, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a constant

such that
the curvature
\tilde{\kappa}
with respect
\tilde{g}
of any Euclidean straight

\left|\frac{d^{k}\tilde{\kappa}}{d\tilde{s}^{k}}\right|\leq C_{k}
,
where
\tilde{s}
is the arc-length for the metric
\tilde{g}
.

Proof.

If
D
is an oriented Euclidean straight line, possibly passing through

,
then let
\tilde{\kappa}_{D}(x)
be its curvature in the metric
\tilde{g}
at a
point

. Outside the Euclidean ball

\tilde{g}=\frac{1}{6}dx^{2}
, so
\tilde{\kappa}_{D}(x)=0
if
|x|\geq 1/5
.

Let

. We proceed by contradiction. Assume that there exists a sequence
of Euclidean straight lines
(D_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
and a sequence of points

(x_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
with

for all

and

\left|\frac{d^{k}\tilde{\kappa}_{m}}{d\tilde{s}^{k}}(x_{m})\right|\to+\infty
as

, where
\tilde{\kappa}_{m}:=\tilde{\kappa}_{D_{m}}
. Since rotations
around
0
are isometries of the metric
\tilde{g}
, we may assume that the
Euclidean straight lines
D_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, are parallel.

Since
\frac{d^{k}\tilde{\kappa}_{m}}{ds^{k}}(x)=0
if
|x|>1/5
, for
m
large enough
we have
|x_{m}|\leq 1/5
. Hence, up to extracting a subsequence, we may assume
that
x_{m}
converges in the Euclidean distance to a limit point
x_{\infty}

as

\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}}\Omega_{n}

see Assumption
1.3
.

Assume that

also converges to
x_{\infty}
in the

\tilde{g}
as

, and also

converges smoothly on

to the Euclidean straight line

and parallel to the

,

. Then, for suitable

Consequently,

Lemma 4.10

the metric
\hat{g}_{\infty}
vanishes (indeed, around any point of one
of these edges, the Euclidean reflexion across the edge is a local
isometry of
\hat{g}_{\infty}
; alternatively, this also follows from
Lemma (
4.8
)). Hence, it
suffices to prove that, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a
constant
A_{k}>0
such that

\left|\frac{d^{k}\kappa_{n}}{ds^{k}}\right|\leq A_{k}
on
\partial\Omega_{n}

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Fix
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
.

If
x\in B_{\rho/(2n)}(p_{nj})
, then

\min_{p\in\mathcal{V}_{n}}|x-p|=|x-p_{nj}|<\frac{1}{8},

so
r_{n}(x)=|x-p_{nj}|
. Consquently, the map

h_{p_{j},n}
induces an isometry between
(B^{*}(p_{nj},\rho/(2n)),\hat{g}_{n})

and
(B^{*}(p_{j1},\rho/2),\hat{g}_{1})
(where
B^{*}
is the ball minus the center).
Since
c_{nj}\subset B^{*}(p_{nj},\rho/(2n))
and
h_{p_{j},n}(c_{nj})=c_{j1}
,
we deduce that
\left|\frac{d^{k}\kappa}{ds^{k}}\right|
is bounded
on
\bigcup_{n=1}^{+\infty}\{n\}\times c_{nj}
by

\max_{c_{j1}}\left|\frac{d^{k}\kappa_{1}}{ds^{k}}\right|
.

Also, since the balls
B_{1/5}(p_{1n}),\dots,B_{1/5}(p_{Nn})
are
pairwise disjoint, each point of
(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{n},\hat{g}_{n})
has
a neighborhood which is isometric to an open set of

(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},\tilde{g})
. Consequently, we
deduce from Lemma
4.9
that

\left|\frac{d^{k}\kappa}{ds^{k}}\right|
is bounded on

\bigcup_{n=1}^{+\infty}\{n\}\times\ell_{nj}
by
C_{k}
.
Let

A_{k}{\mathrel{\ :=\ }}\max\left(\{C_{k}\}\cup\left\{\max_{c_{j1}}\left|\frac{d^{k}\kappa_{1}}{ds^{k}}\right|\mid j\in\{1,\dots,N\}\right\}\right).

In conclusion,
\left|\frac{d^{k}\kappa}{ds^{k}}\right|
is bounded on

\partial U
by
A_{k}
.
∎

We are ready to state one of the main technical results.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\Omega_{\infty}
be a straight polygonal domain
and let
\Omega_{n}
be as defined in
4.2
.
We let, as usual
U:=\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}}\{n\}\times\Omega_{n}

and
r(n,x):=r_{n}(x)
. Then
(U,\partial U)

with the metric
\hat{g}=r^{-2}dx^{2}
is a manifold of finite width
(that is, a manifold with boundary and
relative bounded geometry with
U\ni x\to\operatorname{dist}_{U}(x,\partial U)

bounded in
x
).

Proof.

Recall that
R=1
. Let
(\widehat{M},\hat{g})
be as in
Proposition
2.6
, in particular,

\hat{g}=\frac{dx^{2}}{\eta(|x-\mathcal{V}_{n}|)^{2}}
on
\{n\}\times\Omega_{n}
. We know from that proposition that
(\widehat{M},\hat{g})

is a manifold with bounded geometry.

The boundary of
U
is
\partial U=\cup_{n=0}^{\infty}\{n\}\times\partial\Omega_{n}
. To prove that
U
has bounded geometry, it is enough
to prove that
\partial U
is a bounded geometry hypersurface in
\widehat{M}
.
Since
\partial U
is one-dimensional, its second fundamental form
\mathrm{I}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{\partial U}

satisfies
\mathrm{I}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{\partial U}(\partial_{s},\partial_{s})=-\hat{g}(\partial_{s},\nabla_{\partial_{s}}\nu)=\kappa(s)
,
where
\nabla
denotes the Riemannian connection of
\hat{g}
,
s
arc-lenghth
on
\partial U
,
\nu
the unit normal to
\partial U
(with compatible orientation)
and
\kappa
the geodesic curvature of
\partial U
(see Remark
4.7
).
Consequently, the fact that

\|(\nabla^{\partial U})^{k}\mathrm{I}\hskip-0.85358pt\mathrm{I}^{\partial U}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
for all

k\geq 0
follows from Lemma
4.10
.
This proves condition (iii) of
Definition
2.9
. (Condition (i) is obvious.)

We claim that
r_{\partial U}>0
. We have

r_{\partial U}=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}}r_{\partial\Omega_{n}}
.
One has
r_{\partial\Omega_{\infty}}>0
since, outside a compact region,

\partial\Omega_{\infty}
consists of parallel straight lines in each cylindrical
end
B_{1/8}(p_{j})\setminus\{p_{j}\}
. Also,
by Lemma
2.12
, there exists
C>0
such that

r_{\partial\Omega_{n}}\geq\min\left(C,\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\partial\Omega_{n}}\right)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. So, it suffices to prove that

\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\omega_{\partial\Omega_{n}}>0
.

Assume that
\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\omega_{\partial\Omega_{n}}=0
. Then there exist a
function
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, sequences
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and

(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
x_{n}\in\partial\Omega_{\varphi(n)}
,

t_{n}\neq 0
,
x^{\prime}_{n}:=\exp_{\varphi(n)}^{\perp}(x_{n},t_{n})\in\partial\Omega_{\varphi(n)}
,

t_{n}\to 0
and
\varphi(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, where

\exp_{\varphi(n)}^{\perp}
stands for
\exp^{\perp}
in the metric

r_{\varphi(n)}^{-2}dx^{2}
. We can assume that
|t_{n}|
is minimal,
i.e.,
\exp_{\varphi(n)}^{\perp}(x_{n},t)\notin\partial\Omega_{\varphi(n)}

if
|t|<|t_{n}|
. Extracting a subsequence, we can assume that
x_{n}

converges in the Euclidean distance to a point

x_{\infty}\in\partial\Omega_{\infty}
, because
\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}}\Omega_{n}
is bounded,
see Assumption
1.3
. Also, let
\gamma_{n}
be the curve
parametrized by
t\mapsto\exp_{\varphi(n)}^{\perp}(x_{n},t)
between

x_{n}
and
x^{\prime}_{n}
. Since the functions
r_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, are uniformly
bounded, the Euclidean length of
\gamma_{n}
also tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

Assume that
x_{\infty}\notin\mathcal{V}_{\infty}
. The curve
\gamma_{n}
is a geodesic
for the metric
r_{\varphi(n)}^{-2}dx^{2}
meeting
\partial\Omega_{\varphi(n)}
at

x_{n}
and
x^{\prime}_{n}
, orthogonally at
x_{n}
. As
n\to\infty
, the Euclidean total
absolute curvature of
\gamma_{n}
tends to
0
(since its Euclidean length
tends to
0
and the Euclidean curvature of geodesics for the metrics

r_{m}^{-2}dx^{2}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, is uniformly bounded in a neighborhood of
x_{\infty}
).
So, in the limit, we get two arcs of
\partial\Omega_{\infty}
meeting at
x_{\infty}
.
This is impossible, since
\partial\Omega_{\infty}
is embedded.

Consequently,
x_{\infty}=p_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. We consider the
curve
\Gamma
defined as the union of
c_{j1}
and the two half-lines starting
at the endpoints of
c_{j1}
such that the union is smooth (in particular, these
half lines are parallel to the edges of
\partial\Omega_{\infty}
that meet at
p_{j}
).
For the metric
\frac{dx^{2}}{|x-p_{j1}|^{2}}
, it has a positive normal injectivity
radius
r_{\Gamma}>0
(its ends are asymptotic to half lines issued from
p_{j}
,
which are parallel geodesics).

For
n
large enough, one has

\gamma_{n}\subset B_{1/16}(p_{j})\subset B_{1/8}(p_{j\varphi(n)})
. But the
homothety
h_{p_{j},\varphi(n)}
maps isometrically

\left(B_{1/8}(p_{j\varphi(n)}),r_{\varphi(n)}^{-2}dx^{2}\right)
onto

\left(B_{\varphi(n)/8}(p_{j1}),\frac{dx^{2}}{|x-p_{j1}|^{2}}\right)
and

\left(\partial\Omega_{\varphi(n)}\cap B_{1/8}(p_{j\varphi(n)}),r_{\varphi(n)}^{-2}dx^{2}\right)
onto
\left(\Gamma\cap B_{\varphi(n)/8}(p_{j1}),\frac{dx^{2}}{|x-p_{j1}|^{2}}\right)
. Consequently,
t_{n}\geq r_{\Gamma}>0
,
which contradicts the fact that
t_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. This concludes
the proof that
r_{\partial U}>0
.

We now check that
U
has finite width. By construction, all
\Omega_{n}

are included in
\overline{\Omega}_{1}
, which is compact. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

y\in\Omega_{n}
. Outside the balls
B_{1/8}(p_{nj})
,

j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
,
we have
r_{n}\geq 1/8
. So, if
y\notin B_{1/8}(p_{nj})
for all

j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, then
\operatorname{dist}_{U}(y,\partial\Omega_{n})
is less than a
constant that does not depend on
n
and
y
. If
y\in B_{1/8}(p_{nj})

for some
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, then there is an arc of a Euclidean circle
centered at
p_{nj}
that is contained in
\overline{\Omega}_{n}
and that
joins
y
to a point of
\partial\Omega_{n}
; since the length of this circle
for the metric
r_{n}^{-2}dx^{2}
is
2\pi
, we get

\operatorname{dist}_{U}(y,\partial\Omega_{n})\leq 2\pi
. This concludes the proof.
∎

In particular, our domains
\Omega_{n}
defined in this section
and their “limit”
\Omega_{\infty}
satisfy the conditions of
Theorem
3.10
, and hence we have the following result:

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
\Omega_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, be the
domains constructed in this section. Then there
exists
\delta_{U}>0
with the following property.
Let
|a|<\delta_{U}
,
a\leq 1
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
.
Then there exists
C_{H}>0
such that, for any

f_{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{n})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
,

\|\Delta_{D,n}^{-1}f_{n}\|_{H^{1+a}}\leq C_{H}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{n})}\,.

As we have mentioned earlier, when the limit domain
\Omega_{\infty}

is actually smooth (no corners), the Babuška-Kondratiev spaces
we used become the usual Sobolev spaces. Even in that case, our
results are new.

Sign patterns that allow algebraic positivity
Sunil Das

Abstract
A real square matrix is algebraically positive if there exists a real polynomial f f such that f ⁡ ( A ) f(A) is a positive matrix. In this paper, we give a sufficient condition for a sign pattern matrix to allow algebraic positivity, and give some methods to construct higher-order algebraically positive matrices from some lower-order algebraically positive matrices. We also propose two conjectures related to the problem of allowing algebraic positivity.



Keywords:

Algebraically positive, Sign pattern, Allow algebraic positivity, Irreducible matrix, AP-irreducible.

AMS Subject Classifications:

15B35, 15B48, 05C50.

Introduction

A real matrix is said to be positive if its every entry is positive. Kirkland, Qiao, and Zhan introduced the concept of algebraically positive matrices in 2016. A real square matrix
A
is algebraically positive if there exists a real polynomial
f
such that
f(A)
is a positive matrix. They gave the following characterization of algebraically positive matrices.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

A real square matrix is algebraically positive if and only if it has a simple real eigenvalue and corresponding left and right positive eigenvectors.

A sign pattern matrix is a matrix whose every entry is a symbol from the set
\{+,-,0\}
. The qualitative class of a matrix
A
, denoted by
Q(A)
, is defined by the set of all real matrices obtained from
A
by replacing
‘
+
’
by some positive number,
‘
-
’
by some negative number and
‘0’
by the zero number. A sign pattern matrix
A
allows a property
P
if at least one matrix in
Q(A)
has the property
P
and requires property
P
if all matrices in
Q(A)
have the property
P
.

Kirkland, Qiao, and Zhan
[
5
]
characterized symmetric tridiagonal sign pattern matrices that allow algebraic positivity. Later, Das and Bandopadhyay
[
2
]
generalized this result to find all tree sign pattern matrices allowing algebraic positivity, and Abagat and Pelejo
[
1
]
described all 3-by-3 sign pattern matrices that allow algebraic positivity. In this paper, we present a class of sign pattern matrices that allow algebraic positivity and give some methods to construct higher-order algebraically positive matrices from some lower-order algebraically positive matrices.

Let
D
be a directed graph with the vertex set
V(D)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{r}\}
. We denote the arc from vertex

in

(\overrightarrow{v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}}}),(\overrightarrow{v_{i_{2}},v_{i_{3}}}),\ldots,(\overrightarrow{v_{i_{m-1}},v_{i_{m}}}),(\overrightarrow{v_{i_{m}},v_{i_{1}}})
of arcs such that all the vertices
v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},\ldots,v_{i_{m}}
are distinct. A sequence
(\overrightarrow{v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}}}),(\overrightarrow{v_{i_{2}},v_{i_{3}}}),\ldots,(\overrightarrow{v_{i_{m-1}},v_{i_{m}}})
of arcs, where all the vertices
v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},\ldots,v_{i_{m}}
are distinct, is said to be a directed path from

to
v_{i_{m}}
. The directed graph

. Moreover,

Let us recall from
[
4
]
that a sign pattern matrix
X
is said to be a subpattern of
A
if
X
is obtained from
A
by replacing some (possibly none) of its nonzero entries with 0. In this case,
A
is said to be a super-pattern of
X
. The digraph of a sign pattern matrix
A
of order
n
, denoted by
D(A)
, is defined by a directed graph with vertex set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, where
(\overrightarrow{i,j})
is an arc of

if and only if
a_{ij}\neq 0
.

In Section
2
, we give some methods to construct higher-order algebraically positive matrices from some lower-order algebraically positive matrices, and in Section
3
, we give a sufficient condition for a sign pattern matrix to allow algebraic positivity. We follow a constructive approach to prove it. In Section
4
, we give an example to illustrate the methodology of that approach. Finally, we conclude the paper with two conjectures related to the problem of allowing algebraic positivity.

Let

Theorem 3.1

(

Every algebraically positive matrix is irreducible.

Theorem 3.2

(
[

If a sign pattern matrix

(

Let

We have the following result about AP-irreducible sign pattern matrices.

Lemma 3.10

Suppose that

Proof.

Let

be the distinct irreducible components of

with cardinalities

\alpha_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\alpha_{m}=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}

. Since

is minimally AP-irreducible,

\alpha
and
\beta
of

,

contains at most one nonzero entry, which is
-
(because
A[\alpha,\beta]
contains no
+
for any two distinct irreducible components
\alpha
and
\beta
of

be a sign pattern matrix of order

x_{ij}=\begin{cases}-,&\mbox{if }a_{pq}=-\mbox{ for some }p\in\alpha_{i}\mbox{ and }q\in\alpha_{j};\\

Lemma 3.11

Lemma 3.12

Let
a_{12}\neq 0

is a simple eigenvalue of

a_{21}&0&\operatorname{\bf y}^{T}\\
\hline\cr\operatorname{\bf x}&\operatorname{\bf 0}&R\end{array}\right]

\hline\cr\operatorname{\bf x}&R\end{array}\right].

Moreover, a right eigenvector
\operatorname{\bf w}
and a left eigenvector
\operatorname{\bf z}
of

u_{i+1},&\mbox{if }i\geq 2.\end{cases}\qquad\qquad z_{i}=\begin{cases}v_{2},&\mbox{if }i=1;\\
v_{i+1},&\mbox{if }i\geq 2.\end{cases}

It is easy to verify that

and
\operatorname{\bf z}^{T}B=\lambda\operatorname{\bf z}^{T}

\hline\cr\operatorname{\bf 0}&B-\lambda I\end{array}\right]

∎

Conclusion

We have seen that if
A
is a minimally AP-irreducible sign pattern matrix of order
n
with all diagonal entries equal to
0
such that
A[\alpha,\beta]
contains no
+
for any two distinct irreducible components
\alpha
and
\beta
of
A_{+}
, then
A
has at most
2n-2
nonzero entries. We have also seen in Section
2
that if
A
allows an algebraically positive matrix with a simple positive eigenvalue, then we can construct a higher-order algebraically positive matrix keeping the condition of AP-irreducibility intact. So we hope that the following results are true.

Conjecture 5.1
.

If
A
is a minimally AP-irreducible sign pattern matrix of order
n
with all diagonal entries equal to
0
, then
A
has at most
2n-2
nonzero entries.

Conjecture 5.2
.

A sign pattern matrix
A
allows algebraic positivity if and only if either
A
or
-A
is AP-irreducible.

It should be noted that the above two conjectures are valid for all known algebraically positive matrices in
[
1
,
2
,
5
]
.

Let
\mathcal{AP}
be the set of all sign pattern matrices that allow algebraic positivity. To prove Conjecture
5.2
, it is enough to show that all AP-irreducible sign pattern matrices with zero diagonal allow algebraic positivity.

Proposition 5.3
.

If
\mathcal{AP}
contains all AP-irreducible sign pattern matrices with zero diagonal, then it contains all AP-irreducible sign pattern matrices.

Proof.

Let
A
be an AP-irreducible sign pattern matrix of order
n
. Let
X
be a subpattern of
A
such that
X
is minimally AP-irreducible. Since
X
is minimally AP-irreducible,
x_{ii}\neq-
for all
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. Suppose that
x_{ii}=+
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
(without loss of generality). For every
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
, delete the row and the column corresponding to the index
i
, and append two rows and two columns corresponding to the indices
i^{\prime}
and
i^{\prime\prime}
such that

1.

both
(i^{\prime},i^{\prime\prime})
-th entry and
(i^{\prime\prime},i^{\prime})
-th entry are
+
,

2.

for every
j\neq i^{\prime\prime}
,
(i^{\prime},j)
-th entry is
0
and
(j,i^{\prime})
-th entry is the
(j,i)
-th entry of
A
,

3.

for every
j\neq i^{\prime}
,
(j,i^{\prime\prime})
-th entry is
0
and
(i^{\prime\prime},j)
-th entry is the
(i,j)
-th entry of
A
.

Let
Y
be the new sign pattern matrix. Then
Y
is minimally AP-irreducible and all diagonal entries of
Y
are 0, and thus
Y
allows algebraic positivity. Since
(i^{\prime},i^{\prime\prime})
-th entry is the only nonzero entry in
i^{\prime}
-th row,
Y
allows an algebraically positive matrix with a simple positive eigenvalue and corresponding left, right positive eigenvectors. Therefore by Lemma
3.12
and Remark
3.13
,
X
allows an algebraically positive matrix with a simple positive eigenvalue and corresponding left, right positive eigenvectors. Then by Theorem
1.1
and
3.6
,
A
allows algebraic positivity. Therefore
\mathcal{AP}
contains all AP-irreducible sign pattern matrices.
∎

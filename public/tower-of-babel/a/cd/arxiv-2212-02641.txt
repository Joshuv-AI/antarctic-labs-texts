Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to begin a systematic study of functional inequalities on symmetric spaces of noncompact type of higher rank. Our first main goal of this study is to establish the Stein-Weiss inequality, also known as a weighted Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, for the Riesz potential on symmetric spaces of noncompact type. This is achieved by performing delicate estimates of ground spherical function with the use of polyhedral distance on symmetric spaces and by combining the integral Hardy inequality developed by Ruzhansky and Verma with the sharp Bessel-Green-Riesz kernel estimates on symmetric spaces of noncompact type obtained by Anker and Ji. As a consequence of the Stein-Weiss inequality, we deduce Hardy-Sobolev, Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev, Gagliardo-Nirenberg and Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities on symmetric spaces of noncompact type. The second main purpose of this paper is to show the applications of aforementioned inequalities for studying nonlinear PDEs on symmetric spaces. Specifically, we show that the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality can be used to establish small data global existence results for the semilinear wave equations with damping and mass terms for the Laplace-Beltrami operator on symmetric spaces.

1.
Introduction

The study of functional inequalities and weighted functional inequalities plays a significant role in the investigation of problems in differential geometry, harmonic analysis, partial differential equations and several other areas of mathematics. In particular, these inequalities have been used extensively to study existence results related to several important non-linear equations. In this paper, we establish several important functional inequalities including Stein-Weiss inequality, Hardy-Sobolev, Gagliardo-Nirenberg and Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities on the higher rank Riemannian symmetric spaces of noncompact type and present some applications to study global existence of wave equations with dumping and mass term associated to the Laplace-Beltrami operator on symmetric spaces. Riemannian symmetric spaces are a very important class of non-positively curved Riemannian manifolds. Recently, many researchers contributed to the development of certain important functional inequalities on non-positively curved manifolds using different suitable methods from the Fourier analysis and geometric analysis. But most of them were only confined to rank one symmetric space, e.g., real or complex hyperbolic spaces and their different models and, in this case, one uses the Helgason-Fourier analysis.
One fundamental functional inequality in the Euclidean harmonic analysis is the classical Stein-Weiss inequality established by Stein and Weiss
[
63
]
. It states that:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<\lambda<N
,
1<p<\infty
,
\alpha<\frac{N(p-1)}{p}
,
\beta<\frac{N}{q}
,
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
and
\frac{\lambda-\alpha-\beta}{N}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
. For
1<p\leq q<\infty
, we have

(1.1)

\||x|^{-\beta}I_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\||x|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
u
. Here the Riesz potential
I_{\lambda}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is defined
as

(1.2)

I_{\lambda}u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{N-\lambda}}dy,\,\,\,\,0<\lambda<N.

The unweighted version of inequality (
1.1
) was proved by Sobolev
[
61
]
by extending a multi-dimensional version of the Hardy-Littlewood inequality
[
36
]
. For this reason the unweighted case of (
1.1
) is called the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality.
In other words, the Stein-Weiss inequality is a radially weighted version of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality.

In the Euclidean space setting, many researchers have studied generalisations of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev and the Stein-Weiss inequalities. There are several works devoted to the analysis of the best constants and extremisers of the aforementioned inequalities. One of the main objectives of this paper is to prove the Stein-Weiss inequality on symmetric spaces of noncompact type. The strategy of this paper follows that of
[

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a symmetric space of noncompact type of dimension
n\geq 3
. Let
0<\sigma<n
,
1<p<\infty
,
\alpha<\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}
,
\beta<\frac{n}{q},

\alpha+\beta\geq 0
and
\frac{\sigma-\alpha-\beta}{n}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
. Then, for
1<p\leq q<\infty
and for sufficiently large
\xi>0,
there exists a positive constant
C
independent by
u
, such that

(1.3)

\left(\int_{X}\left|\int_{X}G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)dy\right|^{q}\,|x|^{-\beta q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\||x|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}(X)}.

Here
G_{\xi,\sigma}
denotes the Bessel-Green-Riesz Kernel on the Riemannian symmetric space
X
of any rank (see Section
2
for the definition), and
|x|
denotes the distance between
0:=eK\in X
and the point
x\in X
.

As in the Euclidean setting, the Stein-Weiss inequality (
1.3
) implies the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality on Riemannian symmetric spaces of noncompact type by choosing
\alpha=0
and
\beta=0.
It states that:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a symmetric space of noncompact type of dimension
n\geq 3
. Suppose that
0<\sigma<n
,
1<p<\infty
and
\frac{\sigma}{n}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}

(1.4)

\left(\int_{X}\left|\int_{X}G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)dy\right|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\left(\int_{X}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
C
is a positive constant and independent of
u
.

The Hardy inequality is one of the well-known inequalities of G. H. Hardy which basically says that:

(1.5)

\left\|\frac{f}{|x|}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{p}{N-p}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\,\,\,\,1<p<N,

where
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\nabla
is the Euclidean gradient. It is known that the constant
\frac{p}{N-p}
is sharp.

One of our main aims is to show an analogue of the classical multi-dimensional Hardy inequality on symmetric spaces of noncompact type. As a consequence, we obtain the uncertainly principle in our setting. In fact, we first establish the following inequality, an analogue of the Hardy-Sobolev inequality on symmetric spaces of noncompact type.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a symmetric space of noncompact type of dimension
n\geq 3
. Suppose that
0<\sigma<n
,
1<p\leq q<\infty
and
0\leq\beta<\frac{n}{q}
such that
\frac{\sigma-\beta}{n}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
. Then for
u\in H^{\sigma,p}(X)
, we have

(1.6)

\left\|\frac{u}{|x|^{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{q}(X)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
u
.

Here
H^{\sigma,p}(X)
denotes the Sobolev space on the symmetric space
X
defined as in (
2.7
) in the next section.

If we take
q=p
and
0<\sigma<\frac{n}{p}
in Theorem
1.4
, then inequality (
1.6
) gives the the following Hardy inequality on symmetric spaces

(1.7)

\left\|\frac{u}{|x|^{\sigma}}\right\|_{L^{p}(X)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)}.

The uncertainly principle on symmetric spaces can be derived
from (
1.7
). Indeed, we will show that

(1.8)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)}\||x|^{\sigma}u\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(X)}.

The classical Sobolev inequality (or a continuous Sobolev embedding) is one of prominent functional inequalities widely used to study partial differential equations. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a measurable set and let
1<p<N
. Then the (classical) Sobolev inequality is formulated as

(1.9)

\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\,\,\,u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega),

where
C=C(N,p)>0
is a positive constant,
p^{*}=\frac{Np}{N-p}
and
\nabla
is a standard gradient on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The best constant of this inequality was obtained by Talenti in
[
67
]
and Aubin in
[
8
]
.

E. Gagliardo
[
29
]
and L. Nirenberg
[
51
]
independently, obtained an (interpolation) inequality, widely known as the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, which says that

(1.10)

\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|^{N(p^{*}-2)/2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u\|^{(2p^{*}-N(p^{*}-2))/2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\,\,u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where

\begin{cases}2\leq p^{*}\leq\infty\,\,\text{for}\,\,N=2,\\
2\leq p^{*}\leq\frac{2N}{N-2}\,\,\text{for}\,\,N>2.\end{cases}

Sobolev and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities have several applications in PDEs and variational principles. In this paper, we establish the Sobolev inequality and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on symmetric spaces of noncompact type which we will state as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
X
be a symmetric space of noncompact type of dimension
n\geq 3
. Suppose
0<\sigma<n
,
\tau>0
,
p>1
,
\sigma p<n
,
\nu\geq 1
,
a\in(0,1]
and

(1.11)

\frac{1}{\tau}=a\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\sigma}{n}\right)+\frac{(1-a)}{\nu}.

Then there exists positive constant
C
such that

(1.12)

\|u\|_{L^{\tau}(X)}\leq C\|u\|^{a}_{H^{\sigma,p}}\|u\|^{1-a}_{L^{\nu}(X)}.

By choosing
a=1
in the above theorem we obtain the Sobolev inequality on symmetric spaces of noncompact type. Indeed, for
0<\sigma<n
and
1<p\leq q<\infty
such that
\frac{\sigma}{n}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
we obtain

(1.13)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}(X)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)}.

We note that the Sobolev inequality on symmetric spaces and on the general Lie groups was previously obtained by many authors; see
[
69
,
64
,
16
,
57
]
.

We will show applications of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality to prove the small data global existence of the solution for the semilinear PDEs on symmetric spaces of noncompact type. For this, the following particular version of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality will be useful.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X
be a symmetric space of noncompact type of dimension
n\geq 3
. Let
\tau\in\left[2,\frac{2n}{n-2}\right]
and
a=\frac{n(\tau-2)}{\tau}.

Then we have

(1.14)

\|u\|_{L^{\tau}(X)}\leq C\|u\|^{a}_{H^{1,2}(X)}\|u\|^{1-a}_{L^{\tau}(X)}.

In one of their pioneering works, L. Caffarelli, R. Kohn and L. Nirenberg in
[
17
]
established the following inequality:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
N\geq 1
, and let
l_{1}
,
l_{2}
,
l_{3}
,
a,\,b,\,d,\,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
l_{1},l_{2}\geq 1
,

l_{3}>0,\,\,0\leq\delta\leq 1,
and

(1.15)

\frac{1}{l_{1}}+\frac{a}{N},\,\,\,\frac{1}{l_{2}}+\frac{b}{N},\,\,\,\frac{1}{l_{3}}+\frac{\delta d+(1-\delta)b}{N}>0.

Then,

(1.16)

\||x|^{\delta d+(1-\delta)b}u\|_{L^{l_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\||x|^{a}\nabla u\|^{\delta}_{L^{l_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\||x|^{b}u\|^{1-\delta}_{L^{l_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\,\,\,u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

if and only if

(1.17)

\frac{1}{l_{3}}+\frac{\delta d+(1-\delta)b}{N}=\delta\left(\frac{1}{l_{1}}+\frac{a-1}{N}\right)+(1-\delta)\left(\frac{1}{l_{2}}+\frac{b}{N}\right),\\
a-d\geq 0,\,\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,\delta>0,\\
a-d\leq 1,\,\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,\delta>0\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\frac{1}{l_{3}}+\frac{\delta d+(1-\delta)b}{N}=\frac{1}{l_{1}}+\frac{a-1}{N},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
u
.

It is worth noting that the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality includes aforementioned well-known inequalities, such
as the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality, Hardy–Sobolev inequality and Sobolev inequality. We refer to
[

In this paper, we obtain the following Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality on symmetric spaces of noncompact type.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
X
be a symmetric space of noncompact type of dimension
n\geq 3
and
\sigma
be such that
0<\sigma<n
. Suppose
p>1
,
0<q<\tau<\infty

and

. Let

be real numbers such that
0\leq(c(1-a)-b)\leq\frac{n(q-(1-a)\tau)}{q\tau}

\frac{\sigma-n}{n}-\frac{(c(1-a)-b)q}{an}+\frac{q-(1-a)\tau}{a\tau q}-\frac{1}{p}+1=0

such that

(1.18)

\||x|^{b}u\|_{L^{\tau}(X)}\leq C\|u\|^{a}_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)}\||x|^{c}u\|^{1-a}_{L^{q}(X)}.

2.1.
Riemannian symmetric spaces

Let
G
be a noncompact, connected, semisimple Lie group with finite center and let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup. The homogeneous space
X:=G/K
is a Riemannian symmetric space of noncompact type. Let us assume that
\theta
is a fixed Cartan involution on the Lie algbra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
associated with the Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
at the Lie algebra level, where
\mathfrak{t}
and
\mathfrak{p}
are
+1
and
-1
eigenspaces of
\theta
respectively. It is known that if
\mathfrak{B}
is the Cartan killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
then
\mathfrak{B}
induces the
G
-invariant metric
d
on
X
by identifying the tangent space at origin
eK
of
X
with
\mathfrak{p}
and by restricting
\mathfrak{B}
to
\mathfrak{p}.
The distance between two points
x_{1}=g_{1}K
and
x_{2}=g_{2}K
of
X
will be denoted by
d(x_{1},x_{2}).
We will also use the notation
|x|
to denote
d(0,x),
the distance between
0=eK\in X
and the point
x\in X.
Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a maximal abelian subalgebra of
\mathfrak{p}
and let
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
be its dual space. The dimension of
\mathfrak{a}
is called the rank of
X.
We denote
\dim\mathfrak{a}=l.
For
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}^{*},
we define

\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}:=\{Y\in\mathfrak{a}:[H,Y]=\alpha(H)Y,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,H\in\mathfrak{a}\}.

Then the set of restricted root of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
is denoted by
\Sigma
and defined as

\Sigma:=\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}\backslash\{0\}:\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\neq 0\}.

We denote
m_{\alpha}=\dim(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha})
for
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}.
Let us choose a connected component in
\mathfrak{a}
in a manner that
\alpha\neq 0
for all
\alpha\in\Sigma.

Denote by
\mathfrak{a}^{+}
the connected component, called a positive Weyl chamber.
Now, with respect to
\mathfrak{a}^{+},
we define positive roots and positive indivisible roots by
\Sigma^{+}=\{\alpha\in\Sigma:\alpha>0\,\,\text{on}\,\,\mathfrak{a}^{+}\}
and
\Sigma^{+}_{0}=\{\alpha\in\Sigma:\frac{\alpha}{2}\notin\Sigma^{+}\}
respectively.
We set
\mathfrak{n}=\oplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.
Then
\mathfrak{n}
is a nilpotent subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}.

We denote the half sum of positive roots counted with multiplicities
m_{\alpha}
by
\rho:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}m_{\alpha}\alpha.
The dimension and the pseudo-dimension of
X
will be denoted by
n
and
\nu
respectively, that is,
n=l+\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}m_{\alpha}
and
\nu=l+2|\Sigma^{+}_{0}|.
The Iwasawa decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
is given by
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
on the Lie algebra level. On the Lie group level, if we write
N=\exp\mathfrak{n}
and
A=\exp\mathfrak{a},
then we get the Iwasawa decomposition of
G=KAN.
This means every
g\in G
can be uniquely written as
g=k(g)\,\exp(H(g))\,n(g),
where
k(g)\in K,H(g)\in\mathfrak{a}
and
n(g)\in N.
The maps
(k,a,n)\mapsto kan
is a global diffeomorphism of
K\times A\times N
onto
G.

Let
\Delta
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
X
with respect to the
G
-invariant Riemannian metric and
dx
be the corresponding measure. It is known that the
L^{2}
-spectrum of
\Delta
is
(-\infty,-|\rho|^{2}).
Let
M
be the centralizer of
A
in
K
and
M^{\prime}
be the normalizer of
A
in
K.
Then
M
is the normal subgroup of
M^{\prime}
and normalize
N.
The factor group
W=M^{\prime}/M
is a finite group of order
|W|
, called the Weyl group of
X.
The action of the Weyl group
W
on
\mathfrak{a}
is given by an adjoint action. It acts as a group of orthogonal transformations (preserving the Cartan-Killing form) on
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
by
(s\lambda)(H)=\lambda(s^{-1}\cdot H)
for
H\in\mathfrak{a},

\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
and
s\in W,
where
g.Y=\text{Ad}(g)(Y)
for
g\in G,\,Y\in\mathfrak{g}.
We fix a normalized Haar measure on
dk
on the compact group
K
and the Haar measure
dn
on
N.
We have the decompositions

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\,G\,=\,N\,(\exp\mathfrak{a})\,K\quad&\textnormal{(Iwasawa)},\\
\,G\,=\,K\,(\exp\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}})\,K\quad&\textnormal{(polar)}.\end{cases}

The Haar measure on
G
corresponding to the Iwasawa decomposition and the polar decomposition can be described as, for any
f\in C_{c}(G),

\int_{G}f(g)\,dg=\int_{K}\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\int_{N}f(k(\exp Y)n)\,e^{2\langle\rho,Y\rangle}dn\,dk\,dY,

and

\int_{G}f(g)\,dg=\int_{K}\int_{\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}}\int_{K}f(k_{1}\exp{Y}k_{2})J(\exp Y)dk_{1}\,dY\,dk_{2},

respectively. Here the density
J(Y)
for
Y\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
is given by

\displaystyle\textstyle J(\exp Y)\,=\,c\prod\limits_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\,(\sinh\langle{\alpha,H}\rangle)^{m_{\alpha}}\,\asymp\,\prod\limits_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\Big\{\frac{\langle\alpha,H\rangle}{1+\langle\alpha,H\rangle}\Big\}^{m_{\alpha}}\,e^{2\langle\rho,H\rangle},

where
c
is a normalizing constant. Using the polar (Cartan) decomposition we can define another distance on
X
called the polyhedral distance on
X
defined as
d^{\prime}(xK,yK):=\langle\rho/|\rho|,(y^{-1}x)^{+}\rangle
for all
x,y\in G,
where
(y^{-1}x)^{+}
is the
\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
-component of
y^{-1}x
in the polar decomposition. It was proved in
[
2
]
that the Riemannian distance
d
and the polyhedral distance
d^{\prime}
are equivalent. Any function
f

defined on
X
can be thought of as a function on
G
which is right
G
-invariant under the action of
K.
Then it follows that we have a
G
-invariant measure
dx
on
X
such that

(2.1)

\int_{X}f(x)\,dx=\int_{K/M}\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}f(k\exp Y)\,J(\exp Y)\,dY\,dk_{M},

where
dk_{M}
is the
K
-invariant measure on
K/M.

2.2.
Helgason-Fourier transform on Riemannian symmetric spaces

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
be the complexification of
\mathfrak{a}^{*},
that is, the set of the all complex-valued real linear functionals on
\mathfrak{a}.
The usual extension of the Killing form
\mathfrak{B}
on
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
by duality and conjugate linearity is again denoted by
\mathfrak{B}.
For a nice function
f
the Helgason Fourier transform of
\mathcal{H}f
is a function on
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\times K/M
defined by

(2.2)

(\mathcal{H}f)(\lambda,kM):=\int_{X}f(x)e^{\langle i\lambda-\rho,H(g^{-1}k)\rangle}\,dx,\quad\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*},\,\,kM\in K/M,

whenever the integral exists. At times, we also denote
\mathcal{H}f
by
\widehat{f}.

Let us introduce Harish-Chandra’s elementary spherical function in the following form:

(2.3)

\varphi_{\lambda}(g):=\int_{K}e^{-(i\lambda+\rho)}H(g^{-1}k)dk,\,\,\forall k\in K,\,\,\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}.

The elementary spherical function
\varphi_{0}
satisfies the following global estimate
[
4
, Proposition 2.2.1]
:

(2.4)

\varphi_{0}(\exp H)\asymp\left(\prod_{\alpha\in\sum_{0}^{+}}(1+\langle\alpha,H\rangle)\right)e^{-\rho(H)},\,\,\,\forall H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}.

Moreover, we have

(2.5)

|\varphi_{\lambda}(g)|\leq\varphi_{0}(g)\leq 1,\,\,\,\,\forall\lambda\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{+}}.

For
\xi\in[0,\infty),\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, let
G_{\xi,\sigma}(x)
be the Schwartz kernel of the operator
(-\Delta-|\rho|^{2}+\xi^{2})^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}}
if it exists. We call

G_{\xi,\sigma}(x)
the Bessel-Green-Riesz kernel or simply the Riesz potential. The Riesz potential satisfies the following estimate (see [Theorem 4.2.2,
[
4
]
]).

(2.6)

\begin{split}G_{\xi,\sigma}(x)\asymp\begin{cases}\begin{cases}|x|^{\sigma-n},\,\,\,&0<\sigma<n\\
\log\left(\frac{1}{|x|}\right),\,\,\,&\sigma=n\\
1,&\sigma>n\end{cases},\,\,\,\,|x|<1,\\
|x|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}-|\Sigma^{+}_{0}|}\varphi_{0}(x)e^{-\xi|x|},\,\,\xi>0,\,\,\sigma>0,\,\,|x|\geq 1.\,\,\,\end{cases}\end{split}

Throughout this paper, the symbol
A\asymp B
means that
\exists\,C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that
C_{1}A\leq B\leq C_{2}A
.

The Sobolev space on the symmetric space of noncompact type
X
for
0<\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and
1<p<\infty
is defined as

(2.7)

H^{\sigma,p}(X):=\left\{u:u\in L^{p}(X),\,\,(-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}u\in L^{p}(X)\right\},

endowed with the norm

(2.8)

\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)}:=\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}u\|_{L^{p}(X)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(X)}.

Then it follows from
[
64
, Theorem 4.4]
that

(2.9)

\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)}\approx\|(\xi^{2}-|\rho|^{2}-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}u\|_{L^{p}(X)},

where
\xi
is large enough.

Here after, whenever we deal with
G_{\xi,\sigma}
we will always assume that
\xi
is large enough.

2.3.
Integral Hardy inequalities on metric measure spaces

Let us consider metric measure spaces
\mathbb{X}
with a Borel measure
dx
allowing for the following
polar decomposition
at
a\in{\mathbb{Y}}
: we assume that there is a locally integrable function
\lambda\in L^{1}_{loc}
such that for all
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{X})
we have

(2.10)

\int_{\mathbb{Y}}f(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{r}}f(r,\omega)\lambda(r,\omega)\,d\omega_{r}\,dr,

for the set
\Sigma_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{Y}:d(x,a)=r\}\subset\mathbb{Y}
with a measure on it denoted by
d\omega_{r}
, and
(r,\omega)\rightarrow a
as
r\rightarrow 0
. Examples of such measure measure spaces are Euclidean spaces, homogeneous Lie groups and Riemannian symmetric spaces of noncompact type. We can denote
|x|_{a}:=d(x,a).

The class of such metric measure spaces was introduced in
[
55
]
, where the following integral Hardy inequality was obtained.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
1<p\leq q<\infty
and let
s>0
. Let
\mathbb{Y}
be a metric measure space with a polar decomposition (
2.10
) at
a
.
Let
u,v>0
be measurable functions positive a.e in
\mathbb{Y}
such that
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{Y}\backslash\{a\})
and
v^{1-p^{\prime}}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{Y})
. Denote

\displaystyle U(x):={\int_{\mathbb{Y}\backslash{B(a,|x|_{a})}}u(y)dy}\quad\text{and}\quad V(x):=\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}v^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dy.

Then the inequality

(2.11)

\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{Y}}\bigg(\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}|f(y)|dy\bigg)^{q}u(x)dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{Y}}{|f(x)|}^{p}v(x)dx\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds for all measurable functions
f:\mathbb{Y}\to{\mathbb{C}}
if and only if any of the following equivalent conditions holds:

(1)

\mathcal{D}_{1}:=\sup\limits_{x\not=a}\bigg\{U^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)V^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}(x)\bigg\}<\infty.

(2)

\mathcal{D}_{2}:=\sup\limits_{x\not=a}\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{Y}\backslash{B(a,|x|_{a})}}u(y)V^{q(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-s)}(y)dy\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{q}}V^{s}(x)<\infty.

(3)

\mathcal{D}_{3}:=\sup\limits_{x\not=a}\bigg\{\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}u(y)V^{q(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+s)}(y)dy\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{q}}V^{-s}(x)<\infty
, provided that
u,v^{1-p^{\prime}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{Y})
.

(4)

\mathcal{D}_{4}:=\sup\limits_{x\not=a}\bigg\{\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}v^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)U^{p^{\prime}(\frac{1}{q}-s)}(y)dy\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}U^{s}(x)<\infty.

(5)

\mathcal{D}_{5}:=\sup\limits_{x\not=a}\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{Y}\backslash{B(a,|x|_{a})}}v^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)U^{p^{\prime}(\frac{1}{q}+s)}(y)dy\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}U^{-s}(x)<\infty
, provided that
u,v^{1-p^{\prime}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{Y})
.

Moreover, the constant
C
for which (
2.11
) holds and quantities
\mathcal{D}_{1}-\mathcal{D}_{5}
are related by

(2.12)

\mathcal{D}_{1}\leq C\leq\mathcal{D}_{1}(p^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}p^{\frac{1}{q}},

and

\mathcal{D}_{1}\leq\left(\max\left\{1,{p^{\prime}}{s}\right\}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{D}_{2},\;\mathcal{D}_{2}\leq\left(\max\left\{1,\frac{1}{p^{\prime}s}\right\}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{D}_{1},

\left(\frac{sp^{\prime}}{1+p^{\prime}s}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{D}_{3}\leq\mathcal{D}_{1}\leq(1+sp^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{q}}\mathcal{D}_{3},

\mathcal{D}_{1}\leq(\max\left\{1,qs\right\})^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\mathcal{D}_{4},\;\mathcal{D}_{4}\leq\left(\max\left\{1,\frac{1}{qs}\right\}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\mathcal{D}_{1},

\left(\frac{sq}{1+qs}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\mathcal{D}_{5}\leq\mathcal{D}_{1}\leq(1+sq)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\mathcal{D}_{5}.

Similarly, in
[
55
]
, the authors obtained the adjoint integral Hardy inequality in the following form:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
1<p\leq q<\infty
and let
s>0
. Let
\mathbb{Y}
be a metric measure space with a polar decomposition (
2.10
) at
a
.
Let
u,v>0
be measurable functions positive a.e in
\mathbb{Y}
such that
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{Y}\backslash\{a\})
and
v^{1-p^{\prime}}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{X})
. Denote

\displaystyle U(x):={\int_{{B(a,|x|_{a})}}u(y)dy}\quad\text{and}\quad V(x):=\int_{\mathbb{Y}\backslash B(a,|x|_{a})}v^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dy.

Then the inequality

(2.13)

\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{Y}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{Y}\setminus B(a,|x|_{a})}|f(y)|dy\bigg)^{q}u(x)dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{Y}}{|f(x)|}^{p}v(x)dx\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds for all measurable functions
f:\mathbb{Y}\to{\mathbb{C}}
if and only if any of the following equivalent conditions holds:

(1)

\mathcal{D}^{*}_{1}:=\sup\limits_{x\not=a}\bigg\{U^{\frac{1}{q}}(x)V^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}(x)\bigg\}<\infty.

(2)

\mathcal{D}^{*}_{2}:=\sup\limits_{x\not=a}\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{Y}\backslash{B(a,|x|_{a})}}u(y)V^{q(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-s)}(y)dy\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{q}}V^{s}(x)<\infty.

(3)

\mathcal{D}^{*}_{3}:=\sup\limits_{x\not=a}\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{Y}\setminus B(a,|x|_{a})}u(y)V^{q(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+s)}(y)dy\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{q}}V^{-s}(x)<\infty
, provided that
u,v^{1-p^{\prime}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{Y})
.

(4)

\mathcal{D}^{*}_{4}:=\sup\limits_{x\not=a}\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{Y}\setminus B(a,|x|_{a})}v^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)U^{p^{\prime}(\frac{1}{q}-s)}(y)dy\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}U^{s}(x)<\infty.

(5)

\mathcal{D}^{*}_{5}:=\sup\limits_{x\not=a}\bigg\{\int_{{B(a,|x|_{a})}}v^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)U^{p^{\prime}(\frac{1}{q}+s)}(y)dy\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}U^{-s}(x)<\infty
, provided that
u,v^{1-p^{\prime}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{Y})
.

Remark 2.3
.

In this paper, for using integral Hardy inequalities, we use conditions
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}^{*}_{1}
in the previous theorems.

3.
Stein-Weiss and Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequalities on symmetric spaces of noncompact type

Let us show the Stein-Weiss inequality on symmetric space of noncompact type. The proof is an adaption of the argument in
[
56
]
that was developed for the graded Lie groups, however here, it depends on the induced geometry of the space in a more substantial way.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a symmetric space of noncompact type of dimension
n\geq 3
and rank
l\geq 1
. Let
0<\sigma<n
,
1<p<\infty
,
\alpha<\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}
,
\beta<\frac{n}{q},

\alpha+\beta\geq 0
and
\frac{\sigma-\alpha-\beta}{n}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
. Then, for sufficiently large
\xi>0
and
1<p\leq q<\infty,
we have

(3.1)

\left(\int_{X}\left|\int_{X}G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)dy\right|^{q}\frac{dx}{|x|^{\beta q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\||x|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}(X)},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
u
.

Proof.

Let us begin the proof with the quantity on the left hand side of (
3.1
) and divide it into three part as follows:

(3.2)

\begin{split}\int_{X}\left|\int_{X}G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)dy\right|^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx\leq C\left(I^{q}_{1}+I^{q}_{2}+I^{q}_{3}\right),\end{split}

where

I^{q}_{1}=\int_{X}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|\,dy\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx,

I^{q}_{2}=\int_{X}\left(\int_{B\left(0,2|x|\right)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|\,dy\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx,

I^{q}_{3}=\int_{X}\left(\int_{X\setminus B\left(0,2|x|\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|\,dy\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx.

Now, we will estimate
I_{i}^{q},i=1,2,3.
This will be completed in three different steps.

Step 1.
In this step we consider
I_{1}^{q}
.
By using the triangle inequality for the Riemannian distance with
2|y|\leq|x|
, we obtain

|x|\leq|y^{-1}x|+|y|\leq|y^{-1}x|+\frac{|x|}{2},

which implies that

\frac{|x|}{2}\leq|y^{-1}x|.

Let us now consider the two different cases.

Case (a).
When
\frac{|x|}{2}\geq 1.

Let us first find an estimate of
G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)
in terms of
|x|,
using the fact
\frac{|x|}{2}\leq|y^{-1}x|,
in the range when
2|y|\leq|x|.
Note that by (
2.6
), we have

G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)\asymp|y^{-1}x|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}-|\Sigma_{0}^{+}|}\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}x)e^{-\xi|y^{-1}x|}.

Since the Riemannian distance and the polyhedral distance are equivalent and
G_{\xi,\sigma}
is
K
-biinvariant, we have

G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x):=G(\exp(y^{-1}x)^{+})\asymp|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}-|\Sigma_{0}^{+}|}\varphi_{0}(\exp(y^{-1}x)^{+})e^{-\xi|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|}.

The estimate (
2.4
) of the ground spherical function
\varphi_{0}(\exp(y^{-1}x)^{+})\lesssim|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|^{|\Sigma_{0}^{+}|}e^{-\langle\rho,(y^{-1}x)^{+}\rangle}
yields,

G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)\lesssim|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}-|\Sigma_{0}^{+}|}|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|^{|\Sigma_{0}^{+}|}e^{-\langle\rho,\,(y^{-1}x)^{+}\rangle}e^{-\xi|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|}.

Now, by
\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle\leq\langle\rho,y^{+}\rangle+\langle\rho,(y^{-1}x)^{+}\rangle,

\frac{|x^{+}|}{2}\leq|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|\leq\frac{3|x^{+}|}{2}
using
|y^{+}|\leq\frac{|x^{+}|}{2},
and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

\displaystyle G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)\lesssim

\displaystyle|x^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}\,e^{-\langle\rho,\,x^{+}\rangle}e^{\langle\rho,y^{+}\rangle}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|x^{+}|}

\displaystyle|x^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}\,e^{-\langle\rho,\,x^{+}\rangle}e^{|\rho||y^{+}|}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|x^{+}|}\lesssim|x^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}\,e^{-\langle\rho,\,x^{+}\rangle}e^{|\rho|\frac{|x^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|x^{+}|}.

Therefore, we obtain

(3.3)

\begin{split}I^{q}_{1}&=\int_{X}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|\,dy\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx\\
&\leq C\int_{X}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}u(y)dy\right)^{q}\left(|x^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}\,e^{-\langle\rho,\,x^{+}\rangle}e^{|\rho|\frac{|x^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|x^{+}|}\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx.\end{split}

Our aim is to show the following inequality,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}u(y)dy\right)^{q}\left(|x^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}\,e^{-\langle\rho,\,x^{+}\rangle}e^{|\rho|\frac{|x^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|x^{+}|}\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{X}|y|^{\alpha p}|u(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and, for this purpose, we need to check the condition
\mathcal{D}_{1}
in Theorem
2.1
which turns out to be the following,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\neq 0}\left(\int_{X\backslash B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|z^{+}|^{q\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}\,e^{-q\langle\rho,\,z^{+}\rangle}e^{q|\rho|\frac{|z^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z^{+}|q}|z|^{-\beta q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|z|^{\alpha p(1-p^{\prime})}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\sup_{R>0}\left(\int_{|z|\geq R}|z^{+}|^{q\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}\,e^{-q\langle\rho,\,z^{+}\rangle}e^{q|\rho|\frac{|z^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z^{+}|q}|z|^{-\beta q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{\alpha p(1-p^{\prime})}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},

by setting
|x|/2=R\geq 1.

By using the Cartan decomposition with
R\geq 1
in this case we get

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\int_{|z|\geq R}|z^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}q}\,e^{-q\langle\rho,\,z^{+}\rangle}e^{q|\rho|\frac{|z^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z^{+}|q}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\\
&\leq C\int_{\{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:|H|\geq R\}}|H|^{q\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}-\beta q}\,e^{-q\langle\rho,\,H\rangle}e^{q|\rho|\frac{|H|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|H|q}e^{2\langle\rho,H\rangle}dH\\
&\leq Ce^{-\xi q\frac{R}{4}}\int_{\{H\in\mathfrak{a}:|H|\geq R\}}|H|^{q\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}-\beta q}e^{-\left(\frac{\xi}{4}-\frac{|\rho|}{2}\right)|H|q}\,e^{-(q-2)\langle\rho,\,H\rangle}dH\\
&\leq Ce^{-\xi q\frac{R}{4}},\end{split}

when
q\geq 2
and
\xi
large enough (e.g.
\xi\geq 2|\rho|
). For
q<2,
with same calculations as in (
3.6
) we get

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\int_{|z|\geq R}|z^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}q}\,e^{-q\langle\rho,\,z^{+}\rangle}e^{q|\rho|\frac{|z^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z^{+}|q}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\\
&\leq Ce^{-\xi q\frac{R}{4}}\int_{\{H\in\mathfrak{a}:|H|\geq R\}}|H|^{q\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}-\beta q}e^{-\left(\frac{\xi}{4}-\frac{|\rho|}{2}\right)|H|q}\,e^{(2-q)\langle\rho,\,H\rangle}dH\\
&\leq Ce^{-\xi q\frac{R}{4}}\int_{\{H\in\mathfrak{a}:|H|\geq R\}}|H|^{q\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}-\beta q}e^{-\left(\frac{\xi}{4}-\frac{|\rho|}{2}\right)|H|q+(2-q)|\rho||H|}dH\\
&\leq Ce^{-\xi q\frac{R}{4}}\int_{\{H\in\mathfrak{a}:|H|\geq R\}}|H|^{q\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}-\beta q}e^{-\left(\frac{\xi}{4}+\frac{q}{2}|\rho|-2|\rho|\right)|H|}dH\leq Ce^{-\xi q\frac{R}{4}},\end{split}

whenever
\frac{\xi}{4}+\frac{q}{2}|\rho|-2|\rho|\geq 0,
which holds as
\xi
is large enough.

Let us consider the second integral,

(3.8)

\begin{split}\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{\alpha p(1-p^{\prime})}dx&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p(1-p^{\prime})=-p^{\prime}}}{{=}}\int_{0<|z|<1}|z|^{-\alpha p^{\prime}}dz+\int_{1\leq|z|\leq R}|z|^{-\alpha p^{\prime}}dz\\
&\leq C\int_{\{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:|H|<1\}}|H|^{-\alpha p^{\prime}}|H|^{n-l}dH+C\int_{\{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:1\leq|H|\leq R\}}|H|^{-\alpha p^{\prime}}e^{2|\rho||H|}dH\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}r^{-\alpha p^{\prime}}r^{n-l}r^{l-1}dr+Ce^{2|\rho|R}\int_{1}^{R}r^{-\alpha p^{\prime}+l-1}dr\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha<\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}}}{{=}}C(1+e^{2|\rho|R}).\end{split}

By combining the estimates (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) with the fact that
\xi
is sufficiently large (e.g.,
\xi\geq 8|\rho|
) and substituting back in (
3
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sup_{R>0}\left(\int_{|z|\geq R}|z^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}q}\,e^{-q\langle\rho,\,z^{+}\rangle}e^{q|\rho|\frac{|z^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z^{+}|q}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{\alpha p(1-p^{\prime})}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{R>0}e^{-\xi\frac{R}{4}}(1+e^{2|\rho|R})^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq C\sup_{R>0}e^{-\xi\frac{R}{4}}(1+e^{2|\rho|R})<+\infty,

as
p^{\prime}>1
and
\xi
is sufficiently large.

Case (b).
When
0<\frac{|x|}{2}<1.
Again, we need to consider the integral

I^{q}_{1}=\int_{X}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|\,dy\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx.

Since
\frac{|x|}{2}\leq|y^{-1}x|,
we can divide the study of this integral into two parts: one when
|y^{-1}x|<1
and the second when
|y^{-1}x|\geq 1.
In fact, the case when
|y^{-1}x|\geq 1,
is similar to the Case (a). So by proceeding similar to case (a) with the obtained estimate of
G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)
when
|y^{-1}x|\geq 1,
we have

(3.9)

\begin{split}I^{q}_{1}&=\int_{X}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|\,dy\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx\\
&\leq C\int_{X}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}u(y)dy\right)^{q}\left(|x^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}\,e^{-\langle\rho,\,x^{+}\rangle}e^{|\rho|\frac{|x^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|x^{+}|}\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx.\end{split}

To establish the inequality

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}u(y)dy\right)^{q}\left(|x^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}\,e^{-\langle\rho,\,x^{+}\rangle}e^{|\rho|\frac{|x^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|x^{+}|}\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{X}|y|^{\alpha p}|u(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

we need to verify that the following holds,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}=\sup_{R>0}\left(\int_{|z|\geq R}|z^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}q}\,e^{-q\langle\rho,\,z^{+}\rangle}e^{q|\rho|\frac{|z^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z^{+}|q}|z|^{-\beta q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{\alpha p(1-p^{\prime})}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}<\infty.

Here
0<R=|x|/2<1.

In order to check that
\mathcal{D}_{1}<\infty,
using the Cartan decomposition we get

(3.12)

\begin{split}&\int_{|z|\geq R}|z^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}q}\,e^{-q\langle\rho,\,z^{+}\rangle}e^{q|\rho|\frac{|z^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z^{+}|q}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\\
&=\int_{R\leq|z|<1}|z^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}q}\,e^{-q\langle\rho,\,z^{+}\rangle}e^{q|\rho|\frac{|z^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z^{+}|q}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\int_{|z|\geq 1}|z^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}q}\,e^{-q\langle\rho,\,z^{+}\rangle}e^{q|\rho|\frac{|z^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z^{+}|q}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\\
&\leq C\Big\{\int_{\{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:R\leq|H|<1\}}|H|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}q-\beta q}\,e^{-q\langle\rho,\,H\rangle}e^{q|\rho|\frac{|H|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|H|q}e^{2\langle\rho,H\rangle}dH\\
&\quad\quad\quad+\int_{\{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:|H|\geq 1\}}|H|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}q-\beta q}\,e^{-q\langle\rho,\,H\rangle}e^{q|\rho|\frac{|H|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|H|q}e^{2\langle\rho,H\rangle}dH\Big\}=C\{J_{1}+J_{2}\}.\end{split}

It is easy to see that
J_{1}<\infty
is an integral of a continuous function over a compact set. To see that
J_{2}<\infty
one can argue verbatim as in (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) by considering two case
q\geq 2
and
q<2
with
\xi
sufficiently large (in this case,
\xi>2|\rho|
will work). Therefore, we get

(3.13)

\int_{|z|\geq R}|z^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}q}\,e^{-q\langle\rho,\,z^{+}\rangle}e^{q|\rho|\frac{|z^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z^{+}|q}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\leq C.

Also, we compute the following integral for
0<R<1
with
\alpha<\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}
:

(3.14)

\begin{split}\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{\alpha p(1-p^{\prime})}dz&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p(1-p^{\prime})=-p^{\prime}}}{{\leq}}C\int_{0}^{R}r^{-\alpha p^{\prime}+n-1}dr\\
&=CR^{-\alpha p^{\prime}+n}\leq C<\infty.\end{split}

Thus, from (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{1}=\sup_{R>0}\left(\int_{|z|\geq R}|z^{+}|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}q}\,e^{-q\langle\rho,\,z^{+}\rangle}e^{q|\rho|\frac{|z^{+}|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z^{+}|q}|z|^{-\beta q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{\alpha p(1-p^{\prime})}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq C<\infty.\end{split}

Next, we consider the remaining case, when
|y^{-1}x|<1.
In this case, using (
2.6
) we have
G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)\asymp|y^{-1}x|^{\sigma-n}
for
0<\sigma<n.
So, by using
\frac{|x|}{2}\leq|y^{-1}x|
we get
G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)\asymp|y^{-1}x|^{\sigma-n}\leq\frac{|x|^{\sigma-n}}{2}\asymp G_{\xi,\sigma}(\frac{x}{2})
and therefore,

(3.15)

\begin{split}I^{q}_{1}=&\int_{X}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|\,dy\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\leq C\int_{X}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}u(y)dy\right)^{q}\left(G_{\xi,\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx.\end{split}

To show the inequality

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}\left(\int_{B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}u(y)dy\right)^{q}\left(G_{\xi,\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\left(\int_{X}|y|^{\alpha p}|u(y)|^{p}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

it is enough to show that,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}=\sup_{R>0}\left(\int_{|z|\geq R}\left(G\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)\right)^{q}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{\alpha p(1-p^{\prime})}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}<\infty.

Here
0<R=|x|/2<1.

Then we have

(3.17)

\begin{split}\int_{|z|\geq R}\left(G_{\xi,\sigma}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)\right)^{q}|z|^{-\beta q}dz&=\int_{R\leq|z|\leq 1}\left(G_{\xi,\sigma}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dz+\int_{|z|\geq 1}\left(G_{\xi,\sigma}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)\right)^{q}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\\
&\asymp\int_{R\leq|z|\leq 1}|z|^{q(\sigma-n)}|z|^{-\beta q}dz+\int_{|z|\geq 1}\left(|z|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}-|\Sigma^{+}_{0}|}\varphi_{0}(z)e^{-\xi|z|}\right)^{q}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\\
&\leq C\int_{\{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:R\leq|H|\leq 1\}}|H|^{(\sigma-n)q-\beta q}|H|^{n-l}dH\\
&+C\int_{\{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:|H|\geq 1\}}|H|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}q-|\Sigma^{+}|q-\beta q}e^{-\xi q\frac{|H|}{2}}e^{2|\rho||H|}dH\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{estlar1}}}{{\leq}}C\int_{2R}^{2}r^{(\sigma-n)q-\beta q+n-1}dr+Ce^{-\xi\frac{R}{2}}\\
&\leq C(R^{(\sigma-n)q-\beta q+n}+e^{-\xi\frac{R}{2}}).\end{split}

Also, we compute the following integral for
0<R<1
with
\alpha<\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}
:

(3.18)

\begin{split}\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{\alpha p(1-p^{\prime})}dz&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{estlar2}}}{{\leq}}C\int_{0}^{2R}r^{-\alpha p^{\prime}+n-1}dr\\
&=CR^{-\alpha p^{\prime}+n}.\end{split}

Thus, from (
3.17
), (
3.18
) and
\frac{\sigma-\alpha-\beta}{n}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
, we have

\begin{split}\left(\int_{|z|\geq R}G_{\xi,\sigma}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{q}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{\alpha p(1-p^{\prime})}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}&\leq C\left(R^{\frac{(\sigma-n)q-\beta q+n}{q}+\frac{{-\alpha p^{\prime}+n}}{p^{\prime}}}+R^{\frac{{-\alpha p^{\prime}+n}}{p^{\prime}}}e^{-\xi\frac{R}{2}}\right)\\
&=C\left(1+R^{\frac{{-\alpha p^{\prime}+n}}{p^{\prime}}}e^{-\xi\frac{R}{2}}\right).\end{split}

Therefore, in this case we get

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{1}=\sup_{R>0}\left(\int_{|z|\geq R}G_{\xi,\sigma}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{q}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{\alpha p(1-p^{\prime})}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}&\leq C\sup_{R>0}\left(1+R^{\frac{{-\alpha p^{\prime}+n}}{p^{\prime}}}e^{-\xi\frac{R}{2}}\right)\\
&<\infty.\end{split}

The application of Theorem
2.1
completes the proof of case (b).

Step 2.
In this step, we consider
I_{3}
. By using the triangle inequality for the Riemannian distance with
|y|\geq 2|x|
, we get

(3.19)

|y|\leq|y^{-1}x|+|x|\leq|y^{-1}x|+\frac{|y|}{2},

therefore, we have
|x|\leq\frac{|y|}{2}\leq|y^{-1}x|
.
By arguing exactly in the same way as in Case (a) of Step 1 we estimate that

G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)\lesssim|y|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}e^{-\langle\rho,y\rangle}e^{|\rho|\frac{|y|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|y|}.

Thus, we obtain

(3.20)

\begin{split}I_{3}^{q}&=\int_{X}\left(\int_{X\setminus B\left(0,2|x|\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|\,dy\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx\\
&\leq\int_{X}\left(\int_{X\setminus B\left(0,2|x|\right)}\left(|y|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}e^{-\langle\rho,y\rangle}e^{|\rho|\frac{|y|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|y|}\right)u(y)dy\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx.\end{split}

If we show the following condition,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{*}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\neq 0}\left(\int_{B(0,2|x|)}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{X\backslash B(0,2|x|)}\left(|z|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}e^{-\langle\rho,z\rangle}e^{|\rho|\frac{|z|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z|}\right)^{p^{\prime}}|z|^{-\alpha p^{\prime}}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

(3.21)

\displaystyle=\sup_{R>0}\left(\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{|z|\geq R}\left(|z|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}e^{-\langle\rho,z\rangle}e^{|\rho|\frac{|z|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z|}\right)^{p^{\prime}}|z|^{-\alpha p^{\prime}}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},

then by using the conjugate integral Hardy inequality (see Theorem
2.2
), we get

(3.22)

\begin{split}\int_{X}\left(\int_{X\setminus B\left(0,2|x|\right)}\left(|y|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}e^{-\langle\rho,y\rangle}e^{|\rho|\frac{|y|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|y|}\right)u(y)dy\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx\leq C\left(\int_{X}|x|^{\alpha p}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}.\end{split}

Let us check the condition (
3
). Similarly to the previous step, we consider two cases
0<R<1
and
R\geq 1
.
Firstly, let us consider
R\geq 1
and by using the Cartan decomposition, we get, same as (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), that

(3.23)

\begin{split}\int_{|z|\geq R}\left(|z|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}e^{-\langle\rho,z\rangle}e^{|\rho|\frac{|z|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z|}\right)^{p^{\prime}}|z|^{-\alpha p^{\prime}}dz\leq Ce^{-\xi p^{\prime}\frac{R}{2}},\end{split}

for sufficiently large
\xi
. Then let us compute the first integral in (
3
), we get

(3.24)

\begin{split}\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{-\beta q}dz&=\int_{|z|\leq 1}|z|^{-\beta q}dz+\int_{1<|z|\leq R}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}r^{-\beta q+n-1}dr+C\max\{1,R^{-\beta q}\}\int_{1<|x|\leq R}dx\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta<\frac{n}{q}}}{{\leq}}C+CR^{-\beta q+n}e^{2|\rho|R}\\
&\leq C(1+R^{-\beta q+n}e^{2|\rho|R}).\end{split}

By combining (
3.24
) and (
3.23
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}^{*}=\sup_{R>0}\left(\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{|z|\geq R}\left(|z|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}e^{-\langle\rho,z\rangle}e^{|\rho|\frac{|z|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z|}\right)^{p^{\prime}}|z|^{-\alpha p^{\prime}}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{R>0}Ce^{-\xi\frac{R}{2}}(1+R^{\frac{-\beta q+n}{q}}e^{\frac{2|\rho|R}{q}})<\infty,

thanks to sufficiently large
\xi
.

Let us consider the case
0<R<1
. Similarly to (
3.24
), for
0<R<1
we get

(3.25)

\begin{split}\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{-\beta q}dz&\leq C\int_{\{H\in\overline{\mathbb{a}^{+}}:|H|\leq R\}}|H|^{-\beta q}|H|^{n-l}dH\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{R}r^{-\beta q}r^{n-1}dr\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta<\frac{n}{q}}}{{=}}CR^{-\beta q+n}\leq C.\end{split}

Next we will estimate the second integral of (
3
) for
0<R<1
. Thus we have, same as (
3.13
), that

(3.26)

\begin{split}\int_{|z|\geq R}\left(|z|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}e^{-\langle\rho,z\rangle}e^{|\rho|\frac{|z|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z|}\right)^{p^{\prime}}|z|^{-\alpha p^{\prime}}dz\leq C<\infty.\end{split}

Finally, by combining (
3.25
) and (
3.26
) we get

\mathcal{D}_{1}^{*}=\sup_{R>0}\left(\int_{|z|\leq R}|z|^{-\beta q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{|z|\geq R}\left(|z|^{\frac{\sigma-l-1}{2}}e^{-\langle\rho,z\rangle}e^{|\rho|\frac{|z|}{2}}e^{-\frac{\xi}{2}|z|}\right)^{p^{\prime}}|z|^{-\alpha p^{\prime}}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq C<\infty.

Therefore, (
3.20
) and (
3.22
) implies that

(3.27)

I^{q}_{3}\leq C\left(\int_{X}|x|^{\alpha p}|u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Step 3.
Let us now focus on the remaining case of
I_{2}.

We need to show that

(3.28)

I_{2}=\left(\int_{X}\left(\int_{B\left(0,2|x|\right)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|\,dy\right)^{q}|x|^{-\beta q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\||x|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}(X)}.

We rewrite
I_{2}
in the following form:

(3.29)

\begin{split}I_{2}^{q}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}}\left(\int_{B(0,2|x|)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|dy\right)^{q}\frac{dx}{|x|^{\beta q}}.\end{split}

Since
|x|^{\beta q}
is non-decreasing with respect to
|x|
near the origin, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
k_{0}\leq-3
such that
x\mapsto|x|^{\beta q}
is non-decreasing for all
x
satisfying
0<|x|<2^{k_{0}+1}.
Thus, it makes sense to decompose
I_{2}^{q}
into two parts as follows:

(3.30)

I_{2}^{q}=I^{q}_{2,1}+I^{q}_{2,2},

where

(3.31)

I^{q}_{2,1}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}\int_{2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}}\left(\int_{B(0,2|x|)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|dy\right)^{q}\frac{dx}{|x|^{\beta q}},

and

(3.32)

I^{q}_{2,2}=\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}\int_{2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}}\left(\int_{B(0,2|x|)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|dy\right)^{q}\frac{dx}{|x|^{\beta q}}.

First, we show that
G_{\xi,\sigma}\in L^{r}(X)
for
r\in[1,\infty]
such that
1-\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q},
which will play a significant role in our proof. Indeed,

(3.33)

\begin{split}&\int_{X}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(x)|^{r}dx\\
&=\int_{|x|\leq 1}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(x)|^{r}dx+\int_{|x|>1}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(x)|^{r}dx\\
&=\int_{|x|\leq 1}|x|^{r(\sigma-n)}dx+\int_{|x|>1}|x|^{\frac{r(\sigma-l-1)}{2}-r|\Sigma^{+}_{0}|}\varphi_{0}(x)^{r}e^{-\xi|x|r}dx\\
&=\int_{\{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:|H|\leq 1\}}|H|^{r(\sigma-n)}H^{n-l}dH+\int_{\{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:|H|\geq 1\}}|H|^{\frac{r(\sigma-l-1)}{2}}e^{-r\langle\rho,H\rangle}e^{-\xi|H|r}e^{\langle\rho,H\rangle}dH\\
&\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}s^{r(\sigma-n)+(n-1)}ds+\int_{\{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:|H|\geq 1\}}|H|^{\frac{r(\sigma-l-1)}{2}}e^{-(r-1)\langle\rho,H\rangle}e^{-\xi|H|r}dH<\infty,\end{split}

since
r(\sigma-n)+(n-1)>0
by
\frac{\sigma-n-\alpha-\beta}{n}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}+1=0
with
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
and
r\in[1,\infty]
.

Let us first estimate
I_{2,2}^{q}
. By using estimate (
3.33
), Young’s inequality for
1+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}
with
1\leq r\leq\infty
and by setting
\tilde{u}(y)=|y|^{\alpha}u(y)
, we establish

(3.34)

\begin{split}I_{2,2}^{q}&=\sum_{k=-k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}\int_{2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}}\left(\int_{B(0,2|x|)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|dy\right)^{q}\frac{dx}{|x|^{\beta q}}\\
&=\sum_{k=-k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}\int_{2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}}\left(\int_{B(0,2|x|)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|x|^{-\alpha}|x|^{\alpha}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|dy\right)^{q}\frac{dx}{|x|^{\beta q}}\\
&\leq C\sum_{k=-k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}\int_{2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}}\left(\int_{B(0,2|x|)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|y|^{\alpha}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|dy\right)^{q}\frac{dx}{|x|^{\alpha q+\beta q}}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta\geq 0}}{{\leq}}C\sum_{k=-k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}\int_{2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}}\left(\int_{B(0,2|x|)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)|y|^{\alpha}u(y)|dy\right)^{q}dx\\
&=C\sum_{k=-k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}\int_{2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}}\left(\int_{B(0,2|x|)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)\tilde{u}(y)|dy\right)^{q}dx.\end{split}

Note that from the conditions
2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}
and
|x|\leq 2|y|\leq 4|x|
we deduce that
2^{k-1}\leq|y|\leq 2^{k+3}.
Therefore, from (
3.34
) we further have

(3.35)

\begin{split}I_{2,2}^{q}&\leq C\sum_{k=-k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}\int_{2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}}\left(\int_{2^{k-1}\leq|y|\leq 2^{k+3}}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)\tilde{u}(y)|dy\right)^{q}dx\\
&\leq C\sum_{k=-k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}\int_{X}\left(\int_{2^{k-1}\leq|y|\leq 2^{k+3}}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)|\,|\tilde{u}(y)|dy\right)^{q}dx\\
&=C\sum_{k=-k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}\||G_{\xi,\sigma}|*(|\tilde{u}||\chi_{\{2^{k-1}\leq|\cdot|\leq 2^{k+3}\}})\|^{q}_{L^{q}(X)}\\
&\leq C\sum_{k=-k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}\|G_{\xi,\sigma}\|^{q}_{L^{r}(X)}\|\tilde{u}\chi_{\{2^{k-1}\leq|\cdot|\leq 2^{k+3}\}}\|^{q}_{L^{p}(X)}\\
&\leq C\|G_{\xi,\sigma}\|^{q}_{L^{r}(X)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\tilde{u}\chi_{\{2^{k-1}\leq|\cdot|\leq 2^{k+3}\}}\|^{q}_{L^{p}(X)}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{esti1}}}{{\leq}}C\||x|^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}(X)}^{q}.\end{split}

Finally, we estimate
I_{2,1}^{q}.
By combining the triangle inequality for the Riemannian distance with
|x|\leq 2|y|\leq 4|x|,
one can deduce that
|y^{-1}x|\leq|y|+|x|\leq 3|x|.
Since
|x|\leq 2|y|\leq 4|x|
and
2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}
with
k\leq k_{0}
, it yields that

(3.36)

|y^{-1}x|\leq 3|x|\leq 3\cdot 2^{k+1}\leq 3\cdot 2^{k_{0}+1}<1,\,\,\text{for}\,k_{0}\in[-\infty,-3].

Thus, by using (
3.36
) with Young’s inequality for
1+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}
, we establish

(3.37)

\begin{split}I^{q}_{2,1}&=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}\int_{2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}}\left(\int_{B(0,2|x|)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|dy\right)^{q}\frac{dx}{|x|^{\beta q}}\\
&=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}\int_{2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}}\left(\int_{B(0,2|x|)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|y|^{-\alpha}|y|^{\alpha}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)u(y)|dy\right)^{q}\frac{dx}{|x|^{\beta q}}dx\\
&\leq C\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{(-\alpha-\beta)qk}\int_{2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}}\left(\int_{B(0,2|x|)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{|x|}{2}\right)}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)\tilde{u}(y)|dy\right)^{q}dx\\
&\leq C\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{(-\alpha-\beta)qk}\int_{2^{k}\leq|x|\leq 2^{k+1}}\left(\int_{2^{k-1}\leq 2|y|\leq 2^{k+2}}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)|\,|\tilde{u}(y)|dy\right)^{q}dx\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ocendisk3}}}{{\leq}}C\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{(-\alpha-\beta)qk}\int_{X}\left(\int_{X}[|G_{\xi,\sigma}(y^{-1}x)|\chi_{\{0<|y^{-1}x|\leq 3\cdot 2^{k+1}\}}][|\tilde{u}(y)|\chi_{\{2^{k-1}\leq 2|y|\leq 2^{k+2}\}}]dy\right)^{q}dx\\
&=C\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{(-\alpha-\beta)qk}\int_{X}|(|G_{\xi,\sigma}|\chi_{\{0<|\cdot|\leq 3\cdot 2^{k+1}\}}*|\tilde{u}|\chi_{\{2^{k-1}\leq 2|\cdot|\leq 2^{k+2}\}})(x)|^{q}dx\\
&=C\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{(-\alpha-\beta)qk}\|(|G_{\xi,\sigma}|\chi_{\{0<|\cdot|\leq 3\cdot 2^{k+1}\}}*|\tilde{u}|\chi_{\{2^{k-1}\leq 2|\cdot|\leq 2^{k+2}\}})\|^{q}_{L^{q}(X)}\\
&\leq C\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{(-\alpha-\beta)qk}\|G_{\xi,\sigma}\chi_{\{0<|\cdot|\leq 3\cdot 2^{k+1}\}}\|^{q}_{L^{r}(X)}\|\tilde{u}\chi_{\{2^{k-1}\leq 2|\cdot|\leq 2^{k+2}\}}\|^{q}_{L^{p}(X)}.\end{split}

Computing
\|G_{\xi,\sigma}\chi_{\{0<|\cdot|\leq 3\cdot 2^{k+1}\}}\|_{L^{r}(X)}^{q}
with (

), we obtain

(3.38)

\begin{split}\|G_{\xi,\sigma}\chi_{\{0<|\cdot|\leq 3\cdot 2^{k+1}\}}\|_{L^{r}(X)}^{q}&=\left(\int_{\{x\in X:0<|x|\leq 3\cdot 2^{k+1}\}}|G_{\xi,\sigma}(x)|^{r}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{r}}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 3.2^{k+1}<1}}{{=}}\left(\int_{\{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}:0<|H|\leq 3\cdot 2^{k+1}\}}|H|^{r(\sigma-n)}H^{n-l}dH\right)^{\frac{q}{r}}\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{3\cdot 2^{k+1}}s^{r(\sigma-n)+n-1}ds\right)^{\frac{q}{r}}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r(\sigma-n)+n>0}}{{\leq}}C2^{\frac{(r(\sigma-n)+n)kq}{r}}.\end{split}

From assumptions
\frac{\sigma-n-\alpha-\beta}{n}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}+1=0
and
1-\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
, we have

(3.39)

(-\alpha-\beta)+(\sigma-n)+\frac{n}{r}=0.

Putting (
3.38
) and (
3.39
) in (
3.37
) and recalling that
k_{0}\leq-3
we establish

(3.40)

\begin{split}I^{q}_{2,1}&\leq C\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{(-\alpha-\beta)qk}\|G_{\xi,\sigma}\chi_{\{0<|\cdot|\leq 3\cdot 2^{k+1}\}}\|^{q}_{L^{r}(X)}\|\tilde{u}\chi_{\{2^{k-1}\leq 2|\cdot|\leq 2^{k+2}\}}\|^{q}_{L^{p}(X)}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ocenGI2}}}{{\leq}}C\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k_{0}}2^{\left((-\alpha-\beta)+(\sigma-n)+\frac{n}{r}\right)qk}\|\tilde{u}\chi_{\{2^{k-1}\leq 2|\cdot|\leq 2^{k+2}\}}\|^{q}_{L^{p}(X)}\\

&\leq C\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\|\tilde{u}\chi_{\{2^{k-1}\leq 2|\cdot|\leq 2^{k+2}\}}\|^{q}_{L^{p}(X)}\\
&=C\|\tilde{u}\|^{q}_{L^{p}(X)}.\end{split}

Then by putting together (

) and (

completing the proof of (

∎

4.
Hardy, Sobolev, Hardy-Sobolev, Gagliardo-Nirenberg and Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities on symmetric spaces

In this section, we show the Hardy, Sobolev, Hardy-Sobolev, Gagliardo-Nirenberg and Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities on symmetric spaces. First, we show the Hardy-Sobolev inequality on symmetric spaces.

Theorem 4.1

(Hardy-Sobolev inequality)
.

Let
X
be a symmetric space of noncompact type of dimension
n\geq 3
and rank
l\geq 1.
Suppose that
0<\sigma<n
,
1<p\leq q<\infty
and
0\leq\beta<\frac{n}{q}
are such that
\frac{\sigma-\beta}{n}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
. Then for all
u\in H^{\sigma,p}(X)
, we have

(4.1)

\left\|\frac{u}{|x|^{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{q}(X)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
u
.

Corollary 4.2

(Hardy inequality)
.

For
q=p
and
0<\sigma<\frac{n}{p}
in Theorem
4.1
, the inequality (
4.1
) gives the Hardy inequality on symmetric space, that is,

(4.2)

\left\|\frac{u}{|x|^{\sigma}}\right\|_{L^{p}(X)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)}.

Corollary 4.3

(Uncertainty principle)
.

By Theorem
4.2
, we have the uncertainly principle on symmetric spaces of noncompact type, that is,

(4.3)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)}\||x|^{\sigma}u\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(X)}.

Corollary 4.4

(Sobolev inequality)
.

By taking
\beta=0
in Theorem
4.1
, we obtain the Sobolev inequality on symmetric space of noncompact type, that is, for
0<\sigma<n
and
1<p\leq q<\infty
such that
\frac{\sigma}{n}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
we obtain

(4.4)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}(X)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)}.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By (
2.6
) and (
2.9
), we have that, for
u\in H^{\sigma,p}(X)
there is
f\in L^{p}(X)
such that

(4.5)

\begin{split}u=(-\Delta-|\rho|^{2}+\xi^{2})^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}}f=G_{\xi,\sigma}*f.\end{split}

Then by using this with Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

(4.6)

\begin{split}\left\|\frac{u}{|x|^{\beta}}\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}(X)}&=\int_{X}\left|(-\Delta-|\rho|^{2}+\xi^{2})^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}}f(x)\right|^{q}\frac{dx}{|x|^{\beta q}}\\
&=\int_{X}|(G_{\xi,\sigma}*f)(x)|^{q}\frac{dx}{|x|^{\beta q}}\\
&\leq C\|f\|^{q}_{L^{p}(X)}=C\|(-\Delta-|\rho|^{2}+\xi^{2})^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}u\|^{q}_{L^{p}(X)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{equiso}}}{{\leq}}C\|u\|^{q}_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)},\end{split}

completing the proof.
∎

Let us now present the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on symmetric space of noncompact type.

Theorem 4.5

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality)
.

Let
X
be a symmetric space of noncompact type of dimension
n\geq 3
and rank
l\geq 1
. Suppose
0<\sigma<n
,
\tau>0
,
p>1
,
\sigma p<n
,
\nu\geq 1
,
a\in(0,1]
and

(4.7)

\frac{1}{\tau}=a\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\sigma}{n}\right)+\frac{(1-a)}{\nu}.

Then there exists positive constant
C
such that we have

(4.8)

\|u\|_{L^{\tau}(X)}\leq C\|u\|^{a}_{H^{\sigma,p}}\|u\|^{1-a}_{L^{\nu}(X)},

where
C
is independent of
n.

Proof.

By using Hölder’s inequality with
1=\frac{a\tau}{q}+\frac{(1-a)\tau}{\nu}
where
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\sigma}{n}
and Sobolev inequality, we have

(4.9)

\begin{split}\|u\|^{\tau}_{L^{\tau}(X)}&=C\int_{X}|u(x)|^{a\tau}|u(x)|^{(1-a)\tau}dx\\
&\leq\left(\int_{X}|u(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{a\tau}{q}}\left(\int_{X}|u(x)|^{\nu}dx\right)^{\frac{(1-a)\tau}{\nu}}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Sobolevin}}}{{\leq}}C\|u\|^{a\tau}_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)}\|u\|^{(1-a)\tau}_{L^{\nu}(X)},\end{split}

completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.6

(4.10)

\|u\|_{L^{\tau}(X)}\leq C\|u\|^{a}_{H^{1,2}(X)}\|u\|^{1-a}_{L^{\tau}(X)}.

By taking

and

, we get (

).
∎

Remark 4.7

In Theorem

Then let us show the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality in the following theorem:

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
X
be a symmetric space of noncompact type of dimension
n\geq 3
and rank
l\geq 1
such that
0<\sigma<n
. Suppose
p>1
,
0<q<\tau<\infty
such that
a\in\left(\frac{\tau-q}{\tau},1\right]
and
p\leq\frac{a\tau q}{q-(1-a)\tau}

be real numbers such that

\frac{\sigma-n}{n}-\frac{(c(1-a)-b)q}{an}+\frac{q-(1-a)\tau}{a\tau q}-\frac{1}{p}+1=0
. Then there exists a positive constant independent of

such that

(4.11)

\||x|^{b}u\|_{L^{\tau}(X)}\leq C\|u\|^{a}_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)}\||x|^{c}u\|^{1-a}_{L^{q}(X)}.

Proof.

By Hölder’s inequality with
\frac{q-(1-a)\tau}{q}+\frac{(1-a)\tau}{q}=1
and Hardy-Sobolev inequality (
4.1
) with assumptions of this theorem, we obtain

(4.12)

\begin{split}\||x|^{b}u\|^{\tau}_{L^{\tau}(X)}&=\int_{X}|x|^{b\tau}|u(x)|^{\tau}dx\\
&=\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)|^{a\tau}}{|x|^{(c(1-a)-b)\tau}}\frac{|u(x)|^{(1-a)\tau}}{|x|^{-c(1-a)\tau}}dx\\
&\leq\left(\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)|^{a\tau\frac{q}{q-(1-a)\tau}}}{|x|^{(c(1-a)-b)\tau\frac{q}{q-(1-a)\tau}}}dx\right)^{\frac{q-(1-a)\tau}{q}}\left(\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)|^{q}}{|x|^{-cq}}dx\right)^{\frac{(1-a)\tau}{q}}\\
&=\left(\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)|^{\frac{a\tau q}{q-(1-a)\tau}}}{|x|^{\frac{c(1-a)-b}{a}\frac{a\tau q}{q-(1-a)\tau}}}dx\right)^{a\tau\frac{q-(1-a)\tau}{a\tau q}}\left(\int_{X}\frac{|u(x)|^{q}}{|x|^{-cq}}dx\right)^{\frac{(1-a)\tau}{q}}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{HardySobolev}}}{{\leq}}C\|u\|_{H^{\sigma,p}(X)}^{a\tau}\||x|^{c}u\|^{(1-a)\tau},\end{split}

completing the proof.
∎

5.
Nonlinear wave equation with a damped term associated with the Laplace-Beltrami operator on symmetric spaces

In this section, we show small data global existence for the semilinear wave equation with damped term involving the Laplace-Beltrami operator on symmetric spaces of noncompact type. In
[
54
]
and
[
68
]
, similar questions have been treated for Rockland operators on graded Lie groups and for Dunkl Laplacian on Euclidean spaces. The strategy here follows that of
[
54
]
. The main aim of this section is to obtain the global existence result for the Cauchy problem involving the shifted Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{x}=\Delta+|\rho|^{2}.
We consider the shifted Laplace-Beltrami operator instead of usual Laplace-Beltrami operator just to make computations clear and simple, otherwise the result still holds true for the usual Laplace-Beltrami operator.

(5.1)

\begin{split}\begin{cases}u_{tt}(x,t)-\Delta_{x}u(x,t)+bu_{t}(x,t)+mu(x,t)=f(u)(x,t),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\,\,\,\,x\in X,\\
u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x),\,\,\,\,x\in X,\end{cases}\end{split}

where
b,m>0
and
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following conditions:

(5.2)

f(0)=0,

and

(5.3)

|f(u)-f(v)|\leq C(|u|^{p-1}+|v|^{p-1})|u-v|.

To show the global existence of a solution of Cauchy problem (
5.1
) we need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
u
be a solution of (
5.1
) with
f=0
,
u_{0}\in H^{s,2}
and
u_{1}\in H^{s-1,2}
. Then there exists
\delta>0
such that

(5.4)

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s,2}}\leq Ce^{-\delta t}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{s,2}}+\|u_{1}\|^{2}_{H^{s-1,2}}\right),

and

(5.5)

\|u_{t}\|^{2}_{H^{s,2}}\leq Ce^{-\delta t}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{s+1,2}}+\|u_{1}\|^{2}_{H^{s,2}}\right).

Proof.

By applying Helgason’s Fourier transform in (
5.1
) with respect to the variable
x
, we obtain

(5.6)

\begin{split}\begin{cases}\hat{u}_{tt}+|\lambda|^{2}\hat{u}+b\hat{u}_{t}+m\hat{u}=0,\\
\hat{u}(0)=\hat{u}_{0},\\
\hat{u}_{t}(0)=\hat{u}_{1}.\end{cases}\end{split}

Therefore, the solution of (
5.6
) is given by

(5.7)

\begin{split}\hat{u}=\begin{cases}e^{-\frac{b}{2}t}\left[\hat{u}_{0}\left(\cosh\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}{2}t+\frac{b}{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}\sinh\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}{2}t\right)+\frac{2}{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}\hat{u}_{1}\sinh\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}{2}t\right],\,\,b>2\gamma,\\
\ e^{-\frac{b}{2}t}\left[\hat{u}_{0}\left(1+\frac{b}{2}t\right)+t\hat{u}_{1}\right],\,\,b=2\gamma,\\
e^{-\frac{b}{2}t}\left[\hat{u}_{0}\left(\cos\frac{\sqrt{4\gamma-b^{2}}}{2}t+\frac{b}{\sqrt{4\gamma-b^{2}}}\sin\frac{\sqrt{4\gamma-b^{2}}}{2}t\right)+\frac{2}{\sqrt{4\gamma-b^{2}}}\hat{u}_{1}\sin\frac{\sqrt{4\gamma-b^{2}}}{2}t\right],\,\,b<2\gamma,\end{cases}\end{split}

where
\gamma=\sqrt{m+|\lambda|^{2}}
.

First, let us consider the case when
b>2\gamma
.
We know that

(5.8)

\frac{1-e^{-t}}{t}\leq 1,\,\,\,\,t\geq 0,

and for any
c>0
, we have

(5.9)

bct\leq e^{bct},\,\,\,\,t\geq 0,

so that

(5.10)

(bct)e^{-\frac{bt}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}{2}}\leq e^{-\frac{b(1-c)t}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}{2}t}\leq e^{-\frac{b(1-c)t}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4m}}{2}t},

thanks to
b>2\gamma=2\sqrt{m+|\lambda|^{2}}>2\sqrt{m}.

Thus, if we choose
0<c<1-\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4m}}{b}
, we get

(5.11)

(bct)e^{-\frac{bt}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}{2}}\leq e^{-\frac{b(1-c)t}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4m}}{2}t}\leq e^{-\delta t}.

Therefore, by using these facts we establish

(5.12)

\begin{split}|\hat{u}|&=\big|e^{-\frac{b}{2}t}\big[\hat{u}_{0}\left(\cosh\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}{2}t+\frac{b}{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}\sinh\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}{2}t\right)\\
&+\frac{2}{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}\hat{u}_{1}\sinh\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}{2}t\big]\big|\\
&\leq Ce^{-\frac{b}{2}t-\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}{2}t}\left(|\hat{u}_{0}|\left(1+\frac{bt}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}|\hat{u}_{1}|\right)\\
&\leq Ce^{-\delta t}\left(|\hat{u}_{0}|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{b^{2}-4\gamma}}|\hat{u}_{1}|\right).\end{split}

The function
\frac{\xi^{2}+|\lambda|^{2}}{b^{2}-4(m+|\lambda|^{2})}
is bounded for all
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, using the Plancherel formula for the Helgason-Fourier transform, we have

(5.13)

\begin{split}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s,2}}&=\frac{1}{|W|}\int_{\mathbb{a}^{*}\times K}(\xi^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})^{s}|\hat{u}|^{2}\frac{d\lambda dk}{|c(\lambda)|^{2}}\\
&\leq Ce^{-2\delta t}\int_{\mathbb{a}^{*}\times K}(\xi^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})^{s}\left(|\hat{u}_{0}|^{2}+\frac{1}{b^{2}-4\gamma}|\hat{u}_{1}|^{2}\right)\frac{d\lambda dk}{|c(\lambda)|^{2}}\\
&=Ce^{-2\delta t}\int_{\mathbb{a}^{*}\times K}\left((\xi^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})^{s}|\hat{u}_{0}|^{2}+\frac{(\xi^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})^{s}}{b^{2}-4(m+|\lambda|^{2})}|\hat{u}_{1}|^{2}\right)\frac{d\lambda dk}{|c(\lambda)|^{2}}\\
&\leq Ce^{-2\delta t}\int_{\mathbb{a}^{*}\times K}\left((\xi^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})^{s}|\hat{u}_{0}|^{2}+(\xi^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})^{s-1}|\hat{u}_{1}|^{2}\right)\frac{d\lambda dk}{|c(\lambda)|^{2}}\\
&=Ce^{-2\delta t}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{s,2}}+Ce^{-2\delta t}\|u_{1}\|^{2}_{H^{s-1,2}}.\end{split}

Let us consider the case
b=2\gamma.
By using previous computation in (
5.11
), we get

\begin{split}|\hat{u}|&=\left|e^{-\frac{b}{2}t}\left[\hat{u}_{0}\left(1+\frac{b}{2}t\right)+t\hat{u}_{1}\right]\right|\leq Ce^{-\delta t}\left(|\hat{u}_{0}|+|\hat{u}_{1}|\right).\end{split}

From

Theorem 5.2

The proof of this theorem is based Banach fixed point theorem. First, let us consider the following Banach space

(5.20)

(Lu)(x,t)=\psi(x,t)+\int_{0}^{t}[K(f(u))](x,t-s)ds,

5.1

(5.21)

\begin{split}\begin{cases}u_{tt}(x,t)-\Delta_{x}u(x,t)+bu_{t}(x,t)+mu(x,t)=0,\\

u_{t}(x,0)=v(x),\,\,\,\,x\in X.\end{cases}\end{split}

For simplicity, let us denote

J(f(u))(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}[Kf(u)](x,t-s)ds.

Firstly, let us show that the

(5.22)

\|Lu-Lv\|_{Z}\leq c\|u-v\|_{Z},\,\,\,\text{for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,u,v\in Z,

where
c\in(0,1)
.

For all
t>0
,
u,v\in Z
and by using (
5.2
), Hölder’s inequality with
\frac{2(p-1)}{2p}+\frac{2}{2p}=1
, Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s inequality (
4.10
), Young’s inequality and equivalence of the norms, we compute

(5.23)

\begin{split}&\|f(u)(\cdot,t)-f(v)(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}\leq\int_{X}|f(u)(x,t)-f(v)(x,t)|^{2}dx\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{f1}}}{{\leq}}C\int_{X}\left[|u(x,s)|^{2(p-1)}+|v(x,s)|^{2(p-1)}\right]|u(x,s)-v(x,s)|dx\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle HI}}{{\leq}}C\left[\|u(\cdot,t)\|^{2(p-1)}_{L^{2p}(X)}+\|v(\cdot,t)\|^{2(p-1)}_{L^{2p}(X)}\right]\|u(\cdot,t)-v(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2p}(X)}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{GNPDE}}}{{\leq}}C\left[\|u\|^{2(p-1)a}_{H^{1,2}(X)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2(p-1)(1-a)}+\|v\|^{2(p-1)a}_{H^{1,2}(X)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2(p-1)(1-a)}\right]\|u-v\|^{2a}_{H^{1,2}(X)}\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2(1-a)}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle YI}}{{\leq}}C\left[\|u\|^{2(p-1)}_{H^{1,2}(X)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2(p-1)}+\|v\|^{2(p-1)}_{H^{1,2}(X)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2(p-1)}\right]\left[\|u-v\|^{2}_{H^{1,2}(X)}+\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}\right]\\
&\leq C\left[\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|^{2(p-1)}_{L^{2}(X)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2(p-1)}+\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}v\|^{2(p-1)}_{L^{2}(X)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2(p-1)}\right]\\
&\times\left[\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}(u-v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}+\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}\right]\\
&\leq C\left[\left(\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(X)}\right)^{2(p-1)}+\left(\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(X)}\right)^{2(p-1)}\right]\\
&\times\left(\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}(u-v)\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(X)}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

By using definition of the norm on the space
Z
, for all
u\in Z
we have

(5.24)

(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\delta t}(\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(X)})\leq\|u\|_{Z}\leq M,

then we get

(5.25)

\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(X)}\leq(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\delta t}M.

Putting this estimate in (
5.23
), we get

(5.26)

\begin{split}\|f(u)(\cdot,t)-f(v)(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}&\leq C\left[\left(\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(X)}\right)^{2(p-1)}+\left(\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(X)}\right)^{2(p-1)}\right]\\
&\times\left(\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}(u-v)\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(X)}\right)^{2}\\
&\leq C(1+t)^{p-1}e^{-2\delta(p-1)t}M^{2p-2}\left(\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}(u-v)\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(X)}\right)^{2}\\
&\leq C(1+t)^{p}e^{-2\delta pt}M^{2p-2}\|u-v\|^{2}_{Z}.\end{split}

for all
t>0
and
u,v\in Z
.

Then, via the equivalence of the norms on
H^{1,2}
-spaces, we have

(5.27)

\begin{split}&\|Lu-Lv\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}(Lu-Lv)\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(Lu-Lv)\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&=\|J(f(u))-J(f(v))\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}(J(f(u))-J(f(v)))\|_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&\quad\quad\quad+\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[J(f(u))-J(f(v))\right]\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&\leq C\|J(f(u))-J(f(v))\|_{H^{1,2}(X)}+\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[J(f(u))-J(f(v))\right]\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}.\end{split}

Let us now compute the first norm that appears in the right hand side of the last estimate. First, we note that

Kf(u)(x,t)-Kf(v)(x,t)=K(f(u)-f(v))(x,t).

By combining the above equality with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Lemma
5.1
and (
5.26
), we obtain

(5.28)

\begin{split}\|J(f(u))-J(f(v))\|^{2}_{H^{1,2}(X)}&=\int_{X}\left|\int_{0}^{t}(\xi^{2}-\Delta_{x})^{\frac{1}{2}}[K(f(u))-K(f(v))]ds\right|^{2}dx\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle C-S\,I}}{{\leq}}Ct\int_{0}^{t}\int_{X}\left|(\xi^{2}-\Delta_{x})^{\frac{1}{2}}[K(f(u))-K(f(v))]\right|^{2}dxds\\
&=Ct\int_{0}^{t}\int_{X}\left|(\xi^{2}-\Delta_{x})^{\frac{1}{2}}K[f(u)-f(v)]\right|^{2}dxds\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ocensol}}}{{\leq}}Cte^{-2\delta t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\delta s}\|f(u)-f(v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}ds\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{focen2}}}{{\leq}}CM^{2p-2}te^{-2\delta t}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{p}e^{2\delta s}e^{-2\delta p}\|u-v\|^{2}_{Z}ds\\
&=CM^{2p-2}te^{-2\delta t}\|u-v\|^{2}_{Z}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{p}e^{-2\delta(p-1)s}ds\\
&\leq CM^{2p-2}te^{-2\delta t}\|u-v\|^{2}_{Z}.\end{split}

We know that
Kf(u)(x,t)
is a solution of (
5.21
), then
K(f(u))(x,0)=0
. By using this fact, we have

(5.29)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}J(f(u))(x,t-s)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{t}[K(f)](x,t-s)ds=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}[K(f)](x,t-s)ds,

and therefore, similarly to (
5.28
), with the use of Lemma
5.1
(with
s=0
) and (
5.26
), we obtain

(5.30)

\begin{split}\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(J(f(u))-J(f(v)))\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}&=\int_{X}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}[K(f(u))-K(f(v))]ds\right|^{2}dx\\
&\leq\int_{X}t\int_{0}^{t}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}[K(f(u))-K(f(v))]\right|^{2}dsdx\\
&=Ct\int_{0}^{t}\int_{X}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}[K(f(u))-K(f(v))]\right|^{2}dxds\\
&=Ct\int_{0}^{t}\int_{X}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}K[f(u)-f(v)]\right|^{2}dxds\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ocensolt}}}{{\leq}}Cte^{-2\delta t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\delta s}\|f(u)-f(v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}ds\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{focen2}}}{{\leq}}CM^{2p-2}te^{-2\delta t}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{p}e^{2\delta s}e^{-2\delta p}\|u-v\|^{2}_{Z}ds\\
&=CM^{2p-2}e^{-2\delta t}t\|u-v\|^{2}_{Z}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{p}e^{-2\delta(p-1)s}ds\\
&\leq CM^{2p-2}te^{-2\delta t}\|u-v\|^{2}_{Z}.\end{split}

Putting (
5.28
) and (
5.30
) in (
5.27
), we have

(5.31)

\begin{split}&\|Lu-Lv\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}(Lu-Lv)\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(Lu-Lv)\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&\leq C\|J(f(u))-J(f(v))\|_{H^{1,2}(X)}+\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[J(f(u))-J(f(v))\right]\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ocen55},\eqref{ocen555}}}{{\leq}}CM^{p-1}t^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\delta t}\|u-v\|_{Z}.\end{split}

From the last fact we establish,

(5.32)

\begin{split}&(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\delta t}\left[\|Lu-Lv\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}(Lu-Lv)\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(Lu-Lv)\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}\right]\\
&\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\delta t}M^{p-1}t^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\delta t}\|u-v\|_{Z}\\
&=C\left(\frac{t}{1+t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}M^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{Z}\\
&\leq CM^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{Z}\\
&=C_{1}M^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{Z},\end{split}

and by taking supremum, we get

(5.33)

\|Lu-Lv\|_{Z}\leq C_{1}M^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{Z}.

By choosing
M^{p-1}=\frac{c}{C_{1}}
, where
c\in(0,1)
, we show (
5.22
), it means

(5.34)

\|Lu-Lv\|_{Z}\leq c\|u-v\|_{Z}.

After that, let us prove

(5.35)

\|Lu\|_{Z}\leq M,\,\,\,\,\,\forall u\in Z.

By taking
v=0
and
f(0)=0
in (
5.33
), we get

(5.36)

\|Lu-L0\|_{Z}=\|Lu-\psi\|_{Z}\leq C_{1}M^{p-1}\|u\|_{Z}\leq C_{1}M^{p}.

By Lemma
5.1
and assumption of this theorem , we have

(5.37)

\|\psi\|_{Z}\leq C_{2}(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1,2}(X)}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(X)})\leq C_{2}\varepsilon.

By combining these facts with choosing
\varepsilon\leq\frac{M(1-c)}{C_{2}}=\varepsilon_{0}
where
c
is defined in (
5.34
), we get

(5.38)

\begin{split}\|Lu\|_{Z}=\|Lu-\psi+\psi\|_{Z}&\leq\|Lu-\psi\|_{Z}+\|\psi\|_{Z}\\
&\leq C_{1}M^{p}+C_{2}\varepsilon\\
&\leq cM+C_{2}\varepsilon_{0}\\
&=cM+C_{2}\frac{M(1-c)}{C_{2}}\\
&=M,\end{split}

completing the proof the
L
is a contraction on
Z.
Therefore, Banach fixed point theorem guarantee ensure the existence of a global solution.

The uniqueness of the solution can be easily deduced using the above estimates. Indeed, suppose that there are two solutions
u,v\in Z
of the Cauchy problem (
5.1
). Set
\eta=u-v
and fix
t^{*}>0.
Since
u
and
v
are fixed point of
L
, using the previous computations (
5.26
), (
5.28
) and (
5.30
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\eta(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1,2}(X)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big\|J(f(u))(t,\cdot)-J(f(v))(t,\cdot)\big\|_{H^{1,2}(X)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\big\|\partial_{t}(J(f(u))-J(f(v)))(t,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Cte^{-2\delta t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\delta s}\|f(u(s,\cdot))-f(v(s,\cdot))\|_{2}^{2}ds

(5.39)

\displaystyle\leq Cte^{-2\delta t}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|u(s,\cdot)\|_{H^{1,2}(X)}^{2(p-1)}+\|v(s,\cdot)\|_{H^{1,2}(X)}^{2}\right)\|\eta(s,\cdot)\|_{H^{1,2}(X)}^{2}ds.

So, from the definition of
Z
and using the equivalence of the norms (
2.9
) we see that the map
t\mapsto\|u(s,\cdot)\|_{H^{1,2}(X)}^{2(p-1)}+\|v(s,\cdot)\|_{H^{1,2}(X)}^{2}
is continuous and therefore, it is bounded on the compact set
[0,t^{*}].
So inequality (
5
) takes the form

\|\eta(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1,2}(X)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}\leq Cte^{-2\delta t}\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta(s,\cdot)\|_{H^{1,2}(X)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}ds,

and, so by Gronwall’s lemma, we obtain

\|\eta(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1,2}(X)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\eta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}=0,

for all
0<t<t^{*},
which eventually shows that
\eta=0
in
[0,t^{*}]\times X.
Finally, since
t^{*}
is arbitrary it implies that
\eta\equiv 0
and hence
u=v.
This completes the proof of the theorem.

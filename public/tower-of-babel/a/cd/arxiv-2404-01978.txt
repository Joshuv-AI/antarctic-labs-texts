Irreversibility as divergence from equilibrium
David Andrieux

Abstract
The entropy production is commonly interpreted as measuring the distance from equilibrium. However, this explanation lacks a rigorous description due to the absence of a natural equilibrium measure. The present analysis formalizes this interpretation by expressing the entropy production of a Markov system as a divergence with respect to particular equilibrium dynamics. These equilibrium dynamics correspond to the closest reversible systems in the information-theoretic sense. This result yields new lower bounds for the entropy production and novel links between nonequilibrium thermodynamics and information geometry.



I
Entropy production of Markov chains

Let’s consider a Markov chain characterized by a transition matrix
P=(P_{ij})
on a finite state space of size
N
(our results directly extends to continuous time processes).
The Markov chain is primitive, i.e.,
P^{n}
has all positive entries for
n
larger than some
n_{0}
.
The chain
P
then admits a unique stationary distribution
\pi
.

The entropy production of
P
in the steady state
\pi
takes the form
NP77
;
S76
;
H05

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}S=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ij}(\pi_{i}P_{ij}-\pi_{j}P_{ji})\ln\frac{P_{ij}}{P_{ji}}\,.

(1)

This expression only depends on
P
and its stationary distribution
\pi
.
It vanishes for a reversible chain where detailed balance is satisfied,
\pi_{i}P_{ij}=\pi_{j}P_{ji}
for all
i,j
.

The entropy production (
1
) involves the reversed probabilities
P_{ji}
, which are proportional to the time-reversed dynamics
P_{ij}^{*}=(\pi_{j}/\pi_{i})P_{ji}
.
Intuitively, irreversibility thus arises from the difference between the dynamical randomness of the forward and the time-reversed processes.
This is formalized by writing the entropy production as
G04
;
A07

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}S=D(P|P^{*})\,,

(2)

where

\displaystyle D(P|Q)=\sum_{ij}\pi_{i}P_{ij}\ln\frac{P_{ij}}{Q_{ij}}\geq 0

is the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence rate between
P
and
Q
.
Here the KL-divergence is extended to first-order Markov chains, which can be shown to be well-defined, non-negative, and vanishing only when
P=Q

MS94
;
S96
.
Note that in this case
D(P|P^{*})=D(P^{*}|P)
even though
D
is not symmetric in general.

II
Irreversibility as divergence from equilibrium

Looking at the entropy production (
1
), no connections to other dynamics beyond
P^{*}
are apparent.
Yet, the entropy production can also be expressed as a divergence with respect to specific equilibrium systems
P^{x}
associated with
P
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{i}S=D_{S}\left(P^{x},P\right)\,,

(3)

where

\displaystyle D_{S}(P,Q)=D(P|Q)+D(Q|P)

is the symmetrized KL divergence.
The relationship (
3
) holds for the two equilibrium dynamics

\displaystyle P^{m}=(P+P^{*})/2\quad{\rm and}\quad P^{e}=s\Big[\left(P\circ P^{*}\right)^{(1/2)}\Big]\,.

Here
\circ
denotes the Hadamard product,
P^{(1/2)}
is the elementwise exponentiation, and the mapping
s
transforms a positive matrix
Q
into a stochastic one as
s[Q]=(1/\rho)\,{\rm diag}(\alpha)^{-1}\,Q\,{\rm diag}(\alpha)
, where
\rho
is the largest eigenvalue of
K
and
\alpha
its corresponding right eigenvector.

The two dynamics
P^{m}
and
P^{e}
play a special role in information geometry, as highlighted in ref.
WW21
for the standard KL-divergence.
Indeed,
P^{m}
and
P^{e}
correspond to the closest equilibrium systems to
P
in the information-theoric sense
A16
;
WW21
:

\displaystyle P^{m}=\arg\min_{Q}D(P|Q),\quad P^{e}=\arg\min_{Q}D(Q|P)\,,

where the minimization is performed over the space
\Sigma
of compatible equilibrium dynamics
FN01
.

Here, we see that introducing the symmetrized divergence
D_{S}
allows to connect these concepts to nonequilibrium thermodynamics.
This connection is further discussed in the last section while Eq. (
3
) is demonstrated in the appendix.

III
Lower bounds for the entropy production

Expression (
3
) provides new bounds for the entropy production.
Using that
D\geq 0
and the Pythagorean identities
D(P|Q)=D(P|P^{m})+D(P^{m}|Q)
and
D(Q|P)=D(Q|P^{e})+D(P^{e}|P)
valid for a reversible
Q

WW21
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}S\geq 2D\left(P^{m}|P\right)\geq 2\max\left[D\left(P^{e}|P\right),D\left(P^{m}|P^{e}\right)\right]\geq 0

and

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}S\geq 2D\left(P|P^{e}\right)\geq 2\max\left[D\left(P|P^{m}\right),D\left(P^{m}|P^{e}\right)\right]\geq 0\,.

Additional bounds can be derived from these formulae.
For example,
D(P^{e}|P)=-\ln\rho
with
\rho
the largest eigenvalue of
P^{(1/2)}\circ P^{*(1/2)}
(see appendix for the demonstration).
Then, using the standard bound
M00
for the Perron eigenvalue leads to

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}S\geq-2\,\ln\left[\max_{i}\frac{1}{\pi_{i}}\,\sum_{j}\sqrt{\pi_{i}P_{ij}\pi_{j}P_{ji}}\right]\geq 0\,.

(4)

This bound vanishes at equilibrium where detailed balance is satisfied.
Writing
4(\pi_{i}P_{ij}\pi_{j}P_{ji})=(\pi_{i}P_{ij}+\pi_{j}P_{ji})^{2}-(\pi_{i}P_{ij}-\pi_{j}P_{ji})^{2}
shows
that it captures both the symmetric and anti-symmetric components of the dynamics.

In addition, the expression (
3
) is more efficient than the traditional formula (
2
) in estimating the entropy production from trajectories, as the overlap with the equilibrium measures
P^{x}
is larger than with the time-reversed
P^{*}
.
This can be seen by noting that both
D(P|P^{x})<D(P|P^{*})
and
D(P^{x}|P)<D(P|P^{*})
for any nonequilibrium
P
(for a reversible
P
all these dynamics coincide and the divergence rates vanish)
FN07
.

As an example, the entropy production of a Brownian particle in a moving optical trap or a driven RC circuit depends on the overlap between two stationary measures
A07
;
A08
.
As the driving force increases, this overlap decreases, which necessitates using exponential statistics to accurately extract the entropy production from the recorded trajectories. In contrast, the overlap with the equilibrium measure remains higher regardless of the driving force, thus offering significantly better statistical power.
Another example of this effect will be provided in the next section, where we derived exact results for a molecular motor model.

IV
A molecular motor model example

We consider a Markov chain representing a molecular motor with
2\ell
states corresponding to different conformations of the protein complex.
These states form a cycle of periodicity
2\ell
corresponding to a revolution by 360° for a rotary motor or a reinitialization step for a linear motor.
The motor alternates between two types of states according to the transition matrix
AG06

\displaystyle P=\begin{pmatrix}0&p_{1}&&&&1-p_{1}\\
1-p_{2}&&p_{2}&&&\\
&1-p_{1}&&p_{1}&&\\
&&\ddots&0&\ddots&\\
&&&1-p_{1}&0&p_{1}\\
p_{2}&&&&1-p_{2}&0\end{pmatrix}_{2\ell\times 2\ell}

The matrix
P
is doubly stochastic, so that its stationary state the uniform distribution
{\bf\pi}=(1,1,\cdots,1)/2\ell
for all parameters
p_{1},p_{2}
.

Given the symmetries of the model, a dynamics
P
is determined by the two parameters
(p_{1},p_{2})
.
The time-reversed chain
P^{*}=(1-p_{2},1-p_{1})
since the steady state is uniform.
The equilibrium reference dynamics
P^{x}
can also be determined analytically:

\displaystyle P^{m}=\frac{1}{2}(p_{1}-p_{2}+1,p_{2}-p_{1}+1)

(5)

and

\displaystyle P^{e}=\frac{1}{\Sigma}(\sqrt{p_{1}(1-p_{2})},\sqrt{p_{2}(1-p_{1})})\,,

(6)

where
\Sigma=\sqrt{p_{1}(1-p_{2})}+\sqrt{p_{2}(1-p_{1})}
.

A direct calculation then shows that
2D_{S}(P^{m},P)=2D_{S}(P^{e},P)=\Delta_{i}S
, with the entropy production

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}S=\frac{1}{2}(p_{1}+p_{2}-1)\ln\left(\frac{p_{1}p_{2}}{(1-p_{1})(1-p_{2})}\right)\,.

As expected, the entropy production is given by the product
J\times A
, with
J=(p_{1}+p_{2}-1)/2\ell
the average current and
A=\ell\ln[p_{1}p_{2}/((1-p_{1})(1-p_{2}))]
the affinity
S76
;
AG06
.

The lower bound (
4
) on the entropy production takes the form

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}S\geq-2\ln\Big[\sqrt{p_{1}(1-p_{2})}+\sqrt{p_{2}(1-p_{1})}\Big]\geq 0\,.

(7)

The bound vanishes at equilibrium where
p_{1}=1-p_{2}
, and it has the desirable property to grow indefinitely as the affinity tends to
\pm\infty
, i.e. when
p_{1}
or
p_{2}
tends to either zero or one (Figure
1
).
In this model, the lower bound equals
2D(P^{e}|P)
.

We also see that the overlap between the distributions
P
and
P^{x}
is greater than between
P
and
P^{*}
as both
D(P|P^{x})\leq D(P|P^{*})
and
D(P^{x}|P)\leq D(P|P^{*})
for any
P
(Figure
1
).
As discussed in the previous section, estimating the entropy production from trajectories will thus be easier using expression (
3
) rather than the traditional formula (
2
).

V
Perspective: Nonequilibrium transport and information geometry

Expression (
3
) shows that irreversibility can be interpreted as arising from an ’information divergence’ with respect to equilibrium
FN02
.
The reference equilibrium dynamics
P^{m}
and
P^{e}
belong to the
m
and the
e
geodesics introduced in information geometry using the standard KL-divergence (Figure
2
)
N05
;
A16
;
WW21
.
However, the connnection with nonequilibrium thermodynamics only emerges when considering the symmetrized KL-divergence. Notably, the symmetrized KL-divergence is obtained by integrating the Fisher information along the
e
and
m
geodesics (Theorem 3.2 in
A16
).

Figure 2:
Geometry of the space of Markov chains
.
The set of equilibrium dynamics is represented as a two-dimensional manifold
\Sigma
.
The entropy production is given by the symmetrized KL divergence
D_{S}
between
P
and
P^{e}
or
P^{m}
, and is obtained by integrating the Fisher information along the
e
-geodesic and the
m
-geodesic (solid lines).
By symmetry, the integration can also be performed along the geodesics connecting
P^{e}
or
P^{m}
to
P^{*}
(dashed lines), leading to the alternative formula
\Delta_{i}S=D_{S}(P^{x},P^{*})
.

In parallel, recent advances in stochastics thermodynamics revealed that transport properties display hidden structures and symmetries, including far from equilibrium
A12
;
A22
;
A24
.
These structures form of dynamical equivalence classes, which, remarkably, contain
P^{e}
and
P^{m}
and the corresponding geodesics
A12
;
A25
.

The finding (
3
) now expresses the entropy production as a divergence from equilibrium along both the
e
- and
m
-geodesics.
Taken together, these results deepen the connections between thermodynamics and information geometry, and suggest new geometric structures impacting transport, especially far from equilibrium

;

.

Appendix: Demonstration of the main result (
3
)

Using the Pythagorean identities
D(P|Q)=D(P|P^{m})+D(P^{m}|Q)
and
D(Q|P)=D(Q|P^{e})+D(P^{e}|P)
valid for a reversible
Q
(Theorem 6.1 in ref.
WW21
), we obtain that

\displaystyle D(P^{e}|P)+D(P|P^{e})=D(P^{m}|P)+D(P|P^{m})

or

\displaystyle D_{S}(P^{e},P)=D_{S}(P^{m},P)\,.

That is, if we demonstrate the relationship (
3
) for either
P^{m}
or
P^{e}
, the other follows automatically.
Nonetheless, it is instructive to prove both cases explicitly.
To this end, it will be useful to introduce the relative entropies

\displaystyle h(P|Q)=-\sum_{i}\pi_{i}P_{ij}\ln Q_{ij}

so that

\displaystyle D(P|Q)=h(P|Q)-h(P|P)\,.

Let’s first demonstrate the case
P^{x}=P^{e}
.
The symmetrized divergence reads

\displaystyle D_{S}(P,P^{e})=D(P^{e}|P)+D(P|P^{e})\,.

The same log ratios
\ln P_{ij}/P^{e}_{ij}
appear in both terms on the right hand side, and take the form

\displaystyle\ln\frac{P_{ij}}{P^{e}_{ij}}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{P_{ij}}{P_{ji}}+\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\pi_{i}}{\pi_{j}}+\ln\frac{\alpha_{i}}{\alpha_{j}}+\ln\rho\,,

where we used that
P^{e}_{ij}=(1/\rho)\,(\alpha_{j}/\alpha_{i})\sqrt{P_{ij}P_{ji}(\pi_{j}/\pi_{i})}
.
Inserting this expression into
D(P^{e}|P)
we get that

\displaystyle D(P^{e}|P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1/2)[h(P^{e}|P)-h(P^{e}|P^{*})]-\ln\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\ln\rho\,.

Here we used that the terms
\ln\pi_{i}/\pi_{j}
and
\ln\alpha_{i}/\alpha_{j}
vanish when averaged over a stochastic dynamics (see for example Lemma 4.3 (iii) in ref.
WW21
) to get the first equality.
For the second equality, Lemma 4.3 (ii) from reference
WW21
shows that
h(P^{e}|P)-h(P^{e}|P^{*})=0
since
P^{e}
is reversible and the log ratios
\ln P_{ij}/P_{ji}
are antisymmetric in
(i,j)
.

In parallel we have

\displaystyle D(P|P^{e})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1/2)[h(P|P^{*})-h(P|P)]+\ln\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1/2)\Delta_{i}S+\ln\rho\,.

Here we also used that the terms
\ln\pi_{i}/\pi_{j}
and
\ln\alpha_{i}/\alpha_{j}
vanish when averaged over a stochastic dynamics.
The last equality uses that
h(P|P^{*})-h(P|P)=D(P|P^{*})
is the entropy production (
1
).
Summming the last two equations the terms
\pm\ln\rho
cancel each other and we obtain Eq. (
3
).
\square

Let’s now demonstrate Eq. (
3
) when
P^{x}=P^{m}
.
We have

\displaystyle D_{S}(P^{m},P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[h(P|P^{m})-h(P|P)]

\displaystyle+[h(P^{m}|P)-h(P^{m}|P^{m})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1/2)[h(P|P^{*})-h(P|P)]

\displaystyle+(1/2)[h(P|P^{m})-h(P^{*}|P^{m})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1/2)[h(P|P^{*})-h(P|P)]\,.

The first equality is obtained by expressing the KL divergences in terms of relative entropies.
In the second equality, we used that
h(P^{m}|Q)=(1/2)h(P|Q)+(1/2)h(P^{*}|Q)
and that
h(P^{*}|P)=h(P|P^{*})
. These two relations hold because
\pi
is also the stationary distribution of
P^{*}
and
P^{m}
.
The third equality comes from Lemma 4.3 (i) in reference
WW21
.
Then, the last expression
h(P|P^{*})-h(P|P)=D(P|P^{*})
is the entropy production (
2
).
\square

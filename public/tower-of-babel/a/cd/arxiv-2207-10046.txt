Adaptive Step-Size Methods for Compressed SGD
Adarsh M. Subramaniam, Akshayaa Magesh, Venugopal V. Veeravalli Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign

Abstract
Compressed Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) algorithms have been recently proposed to address the communication bottleneck in distributed and decentralized optimization problems, such as those that arise in federated machine learning. Existing compressed SGD algorithms assume the use of non-adaptive step-sizes (constant or diminishing) to provide theoretical convergence guarantees. Typically, the step-sizes are fine-tuned in practice to the dataset and the learning algorithm to provide good empirical performance. Such fine-tuning might be impractical in many learning scenarios, and it is therefore of interest to study compressed SGD using adaptive step-sizes. Motivated by prior work on adaptive step-size methods for SGD to train neural networks efficiently in the uncompressed setting, we develop an adaptive step-size method for compressed SGD. In particular, we introduce a scaling technique for the descent step in compressed SGD, which we use to establish order-optimal convergence rates for convex-smooth and strong convex-smooth objectives under an interpolation condition and for non-convex objectives under a strong growth condition . We also show through simulation examples that without this scaling, the algorithm can fail to converge. We present experimental results on deep neural networks for real-world datasets, and compare the performance of our proposed algorithm with previously proposed compressed SGD methods in literature, and demonstrate improved performance on ResNet-18, ResNet-34 and DenseNet architectures for CIFAR-100 and CIFAR-10 datasets at various levels of compression.

Introduction

Consider the optimization problem of minimizing the sum of
n

\min_{x}f(x)=\min_{x}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(x).

(1)

A practical approach to solving the optimization problem in (

is sampled at random at a given iteration step, and the iterate

is updated using the gradient of
f_{i}
. The main problem of interest in this paper is one where compressed versions of the gradients are used in the SGD iterations. The use of compressed gradients allows for the alleviation of the communication bottleneck in distributed and decentralized optimization settings such as those that arise in federated learning
[

]
.

There have been several recent papers on compressed SGD algorithms (see, e.g.,

Existing works on compressed SGD algorithms assume the use of diminishing or constant step-sizes based on system parameters to show convergence properties. In applications to machine learning, the step-sizes are fine tuned to the dataset and the machine learning model to provide good empirical performance. However, such fine tuning can be impractical and can result in sub-optimal rates of convergence. Hence it is of interest to study adaptive step-size methods for compressed SGD.

Adaptive step-size techniques for (uncompressed) SGD have been studied in several recent works (see, e.g.,
[

. The condition essentially implies that there exists a point

in the optimization problem (

Several other recent works,

The use of this condition has also been supported by theoretical works

Motivated by adaptive step-size techniques for uncompressed SGD, we develop an adaptive step-size technique for compressed SGD that results in faster convergence at the same compression level than existing non-adaptive step-size compressed SGD algorithms. To this end, we develop a

Our Contributions

1.

We introduce the idea of scaling for the classical Armijo step-size search, and demonstrate that it can accelerate convergence in certain asymmetric optimization problems.

2.

Using a scaling technique, we prove that the Armijo step-size search
[

3.

We propose a computationally feasible and efficient adaptive step-size search compressed SGD algorithm that uses the scaling technique and the biased

We demonstrate that our algorithm for compressed SGD outperforms existing non-adaptive SGD methods on neural network training tasks at various levels of compression.

III
Compressed SGD with Adaptive Step-Size

Our proposed compressed SGD algorithm is motivated by the idea of scaling, where the step-size returned by the Armijo rule is scaled by a factor
a
in the descent step. We now discuss the intuition for such scaling, and propose an algorithm for compressed SGD with Armijo step-size search and scaling.

III-A

Armijo Step-Size Search with Scaling

Optimization problems that arise in machine learning applications are usually asymmetric, and have gradients that do not point in the direction of a minimizer. For example, consider the function
f(x)=\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{4}+\frac{x_{2}^{2}}{9}
. The negative gradient at a point is orthogonal to the tangent and does not necessarily point towards the minimizer
(0,0)
. Hence, GD with Armijo step-size search may suffer from slower convergence if
\alpha_{\mathrm{max}}
is large. In order to alleviate this issue, we introduce the idea of scaling where the step-size
\alpha_{t}
returned by Armijo’s rule (
2
) is scaled by a factor
a<2\sigma
, and
\eta_{t}\triangleq a\alpha_{t}
is used as the step-size in the descent step, i.e.,

x_{t+1}=x_{t}-\eta_{t}\nabla f(x_{t}).

It is important to note that the stopping criterion for Armijo’s rule (
2
) uses
\alpha_{t}
(and not
\eta_{t}
) to scale the gradient. In the Appendix (Figure
5
), we plot the loss for GD with Armijo step-size search for the asymmetric function

f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{10}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{2^{i}}
, and show that the scaled GD algorithm outperforms the non-scaled one by several orders of magnitude in terms of convergence time to the minimum.

Another aspect of GD algorithm with Armijo step-size search is that while the algorithm converges for all values of
\sigma\in(0,1)
, a convergence rate of
O(1/T)
for convex objectives (where
T
is the number of iterations of the GD algorithm) has been proven only for values of
\sigma\geq 0.5

[
18
]
. On the other hand, commonly used values of
\sigma
in practice are in the range
[10^{-5},\ 10^{-1}]

[
30
]
. With scaling added to Armijo step-size search, we prove a convergence rate of
O(\frac{1}{T})
for convex objectives for all values of
\sigma\in(0,1)
. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first such proof, and is presented in Section
IX
of the Appendix.

III-B

Compressed SGD with Armijo Step-Size Search and Scaling (CSGD-ASSS)

Following
[
3
]
, the method that we used for gradient compression is one that utilizes memory feedback to compensate for the error due to compression. The corresponding update rule for the iterate at time
t
is given by:

\displaystyle x_{t+1}=x_{t}-top_{k}(m_{t}+\eta_{t}\nabla f_{i_{t}}(x_{t}))

(6)

where
\eta_{t}
is the step-size,
i_{t}
is the index of the function sampled at time
t
, and
m_{t}
is the error value due to compression that is updated as in step
8
of Algorithm
2
.

In the compressed SGD setting, even if the negative gradient
-\nabla f_{i_{t}}(x_{t})
points towards a minimizer of
f
,
the descent direction
-top_{k}(m_{t}+\eta_{t}\nabla f_{i_{t}}(x_{t}))
may be skewed away from the minimizer. Hence step-size search based on Armijo’s rule (without scaling) used in
[
15
]
for uncompressed SGD may diverge (exponentially) for compressed SGD, as we show in Section
IV-C

Motivated by our results on scaling for GD with Armijo step-size search, we propose scaling as a fix for compressed SGD with Armijo step-size search as discussed below.

At iteration

, the CSGD-ASSS algorithm implements Armijo step-size search (Algorithm
1

f_{i_{t}}
and returns a value

that satisfies

f_{i_{t}}(x_{t}-\alpha_{t}\nabla f_{i_{t}}(x_{t}))\leq f_{i_{t}}(x_{t})-\sigma\alpha_{t}||f_{i_{t}}(x_{t})||^{2}.

(7)

The step-size at iteration

is subsequently chosen as
\eta_{t}=a\alpha_{t}

is a scaling factor. The algorithm computes the compressed gradient with feedback error

and scaled step-size

as

g_{t}=top_{k}(\eta_{t}\nabla f_{i_{t}}(x_{t})+m_{t}).

(8)

The iterate and error for iteration

x_{t+1}=x_{t}-g_{t}

and

m_{t+1}=\eta_{t}\nabla f_{i_{t}}(x_{t})+m_{t}-top_{k}(\eta_{t}\nabla f_{i_{t}}(x_{t})+m_{t}).

IV-A

Experimental Setup

We test our CSGD-ASSS algorithm on neural network architectures ResNet 18, ResNet 34 and DenseNet 121, on CIFAR 100 and CIFAR 10 datasets. Consistent with compression schemes in previous works

We set the initial value of

For the Armijo step-size search parameter

Note on computational complexity:
The Armijo step-size search of the CSGD-ASSS algorithm with
\omega=1.2
and
\rho=0.8

With each gradient being several GigaBytes in size, and several hundred worker nodes communicating with the master node, the communication time would still remain the major bottleneck when implementing our proposed algorithm in federated/distributed learning scenarios.

V
Conclusion

We have motivated and presented a scaling technique for Armijo step-size search based adaptive gradient descent methods. We showed how the scaling technique can be used in establishing convergence results, in convex, strongly convex, and non-convex settings, when Armijo step-size search is applied in the context of compressed SGD. In simulations, we have shown that our proposed Compressed SGD with Armijo Step-Size Search and Scaling (CSGD-ASSS) algorithm outperforms the corresponding non-adaptive compressed SGD algorithm on ResNet 18, ResNet 34 and DenseNet architectures trained on CIFAR 100 and CIFAR 10 datasets at various levels of compression. We have also demonstrated through simulations the important role of scaling for convergence of compressed SGD with Armijo step-size search. A distributed version of the CSGD-ASSS algorithm is presented in the Appendix with convergence guarantees. For future work, one could study the use of modifications such as local iterations, momentum, other error-feedback operators, and adaptive gradient methods with the CSGD-ASSS algorithm.

\displaystyle a\leq\min{\{\frac{[(\frac{p}{p+1})+1]}{\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathrm{min}}v(L+\theta G)},\frac{\theta\epsilon}{\beta L^{2}+\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathrm{min}}pL^{2}}\}}.

(113)

Hence, in the statement of the theorem,

\displaystyle\hat{a}

\displaystyle=\min{\{\frac{[(\frac{p}{p+1})+1]}{\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathrm{min}}v(L+\theta G)},\frac{\theta\epsilon}{\beta L^{2}+\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathrm{min}}pL^{2}}\}},

(114)

and if
a=\hat{a}
, then

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{UB}(\hat{a}).

(115)

Hence proved.

∎

VIII
Distributed Compressed SGD with Armijo Step-Size Search (DCSGD-ASSS)

The CSGD-ASSS algorithm can be extended to the distributed setting with the same convergence rates as the central/single worker node setting. In this section, we present the algorithm and convergence results for DCSGD-ASSS.

VIII-A

Notation

In the distributed setting, the superscript indicates the corresponding worker node. For instance,
g_{t}^{(k)}
is the compressed gradient of worker node
k
at time
t
. We assume for simplicity, the dataset is equally split among the workers and
n=NM
, where
n
is the total number of data points,
M
is the number of data points in each worker and
N
is the number of worker nodes. The
i
th data point at worker
k
is
f_{i}^{(k)}(x)
.

VIII-B

Algorithm

Consider the distributed setting in which there is one central node and
N
worker nodes. The function to be minimized is

\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{N}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}f^{(k)}(x)}

(116)

where
f^{(k)}(x)=\frac{1}{M}{\sum_{i=1}^{M}f_{i}^{(k)}(x)}
.
We consider a distributed algorithm consistent with
[
3
,
32
]
. Each worker node samples a data point
f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x)
, searches for a step-size
\alpha_{t}^{(k)}
with the Armijo step-size search algorithm, computes
a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}g_{t}^{(k)}
, and transmits it to the central node, where
g_{t}^{(k)}
is the compressed gradient. The central node averages the compressed gradients and computes the descent step
x_{t+1}=x_{t}-\big(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}g_{t}^{(k)}/N\big)
and transmits
x_{t+1}
to the worker nodes.

g_{t}^{(k)}=top_{k}(m_{t}^{(k)}+a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t}))

to

VIII-C

Results

The convergence results for DCSGD-ASSS are proved using the perturbed iterate analysis method. Let
\hat{x}_{0}=x_{0}
and
\hat{x}_{t+1}=\hat{x}_{t}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})}
. The Lemma
13
is used in the proof of convergence.

Lemma 13
.

For any time
t
,

x_{t}-\hat{x}_{t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}m_{t}^{(k)}.

(117)

Proof.

We prove this by induction. Note that
m_{0}^{(k)}=0
for all
k
. For
t=1
,

\displaystyle x_{1}

\displaystyle=x_{0}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{top_{k}(a\alpha_{0}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{0}}^{(k)}(x_{0}))},

(118)

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{1}

\displaystyle=\hat{x}_{0}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a\alpha_{0}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{0}}^{(k)}(x_{0})}.

(119)

Then,

x_{1}-\hat{x}_{1}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}({a\alpha_{0}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{0}}^{(k)}(x_{0})-top_{k}(a\alpha_{0}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{0}}^{(k)}(x_{0}))})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{m_{1}^{(k)}}.

(120)

Let
x_{t}-\hat{x}_{t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}m_{t}^{(k)}
. For
t+1
,

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle=x_{t}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{top_{k}(m_{t}^{(k)}+a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t}))},

(121)

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\hat{x}_{t}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})}.

(122)

Thus,

\displaystyle x_{t+1}-\hat{x}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\big({m_{t}^{(k)}+a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t}))-top_{k}(m_{t}^{(k)}+a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t}))}\big)

(123)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{m_{t+1}^{(k)}}.

∎

Theorem 14
.

(Distributed-Convex) Let
f_{i}^{(k)}(x)
be convex and
L_{i}^{(k)}
smooth for
i\in[M]
,
k\in[N]
, and
f_{i}^{(k)}
satisfy the interpolation condition. Then there exists
\hat{a}
such that if
a<\hat{a}
, the distributed compressed SGD with Armijo step-size search for
\sigma\in(0,1)
and scale factor
a
satisfies

\displaystyle E\left[f\left(\frac{1}{T}{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}x_{t}}\right)\right]-E[f(x^{*})]

(124)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\delta_{2}T}(E[||\hat{x}_{0}-x^{*}||^{2}])

where for any
0<\epsilon<\zeta
,
\zeta\triangleq\frac{\sigma\gamma}{(2-\gamma)}
and
L_{\mathrm{max}}\triangleq\max_{i,k}L_{i}^{(k)}
,

\displaystyle\delta_{2}\triangleq\rho\frac{2(1-\sigma)}{L_{\mathrm{max}}}\Big(2a-\frac{a^{2}}{\sigma}-\frac{a^{2}}{\sigma p(\epsilon)}-(1-\gamma)\left(1+\frac{1}{r(\epsilon)}\right)\frac{a^{2}}{\sigma}\Big),

(125)

\displaystyle\hat{a}=(\zeta-\epsilon).

The values
p(\epsilon),r(\epsilon)
are as stated in Lemma
12
.

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle\|\hat{x}_{t+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\hat{x}_{t}-x^{*}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})}\|^{2}

(126)

\displaystyle=\|\hat{x}_{t}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})}\|^{2}-2\langle\hat{x}_{t}-x^{*},\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{x}_{t}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}\|\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})\|^{2}}-\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle\hat{x}_{t}-x^{*},{a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})}\rangle

\displaystyle=\|\hat{x}_{t}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}\|\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})\|^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle x_{t}-\hat{x}_{t},{a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})}\rangle+\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle x^{*}-{x}_{t},{a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})}\rangle,

where the inequality arises from Jensen’s inequality.

Since each
f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}
is convex,

\langle x^{*}-x_{t},a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})\rangle\leq a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}(f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x^{*})-f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})).

(127)

From Lemma
4
, for any
p>0
,

\langle x_{t}-\hat{x}_{t},\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})}\rangle\leq p\|x_{t}-\hat{x}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{p}\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})}\|^{2}.

(128)

From Jensen’s inequality,

\langle x_{t}-\hat{x}_{t},\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})}\rangle\leq p\|x_{t}-\hat{x}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{pN}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}\|\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})\|^{2}.

(129)

Thus,

\displaystyle\|\hat{x}_{t+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{x}_{t}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\big({a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}\|\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})\|^{2}}\big)+p\|x_{t}-\hat{x}_{t}\|^{2}

(130)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{pN}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}\|\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})\|^{2}+\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}(f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x^{*})-f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t}))

(131)

\displaystyle=\|\hat{x}_{t}-x^{*}\|^{2}+p\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{m_{t}^{(k)}}\|^{2}+(1+\frac{1}{p})\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}\|\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})\|^{2}}

(132)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt-\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}e_{t}^{(k)}

(133)

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{x}_{t}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{p}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|m_{t}^{(k)}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\frac{1}{p})}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}\|\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})\|^{2}}

(134)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt-\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}e_{t}^{(k)},

(135)

where
e_{t}^{(k)}\triangleq f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})-f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x^{*})
, (
133
) is from Lemma
13
and (
135
) is from Jensen’s inequality.

We have from the compression property (Lemma
6
) and Lemma
5
that for each
k
and some
r>0
,

\displaystyle||m_{t+1}^{(k)}||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\gamma)||m_{t}^{(k)}+a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f^{(k)}_{i_{t}}(x_{t})||^{2}

(136)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\gamma)\big((1+r)||m_{t}^{(k)}||^{2}+(1+\frac{1}{r})a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}||\nabla f^{(k)}_{i_{t}}(x_{t})||^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\gamma)(1+r)||m^{(k)}_{t}||^{2}+(1-\gamma)(1+\frac{1}{r})a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}||\nabla f^{(k)}_{i_{t}}(x_{t})||^{2}.

Averaging the above over all
k
(the workers),

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}||m_{t+1}^{(k)}||^{2}\leq\frac{(1-\gamma)(1+r)}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{||m^{(k)}_{t}||^{2}}+\frac{(1-\gamma)(1+\frac{1}{r})}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}||\nabla f^{(k)}_{i_{t}}(x_{t})||^{2}}.

(137)

Adding (
137
) and (
135
), we get,

\displaystyle\|\hat{x}_{t+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{||m_{t+1}^{(k)}||^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{x}_{t}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{p}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|m_{t}^{(k)}\|^{2}

(138)

\displaystyle+(\frac{p+1}{pN})\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}\|\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})\|^{2}}-\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a\alpha_{t}^{(k)}e_{t}^{(k)}

\displaystyle+\frac{(1-\gamma)(1+r)}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{||m^{(k)}_{t}||^{2}}

\displaystyle+(1-\gamma)(1+\frac{1}{r})\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}||\nabla f^{(k)}_{i_{t}}(x_{t})||^{2}}.

For any worker
k
, from the Armijo step-size search stopping condition,

\displaystyle\|\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma\alpha_{t}^{(k)}}(f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})-f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t}-\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t}))).

(139)

From the interpolation condition we have that
f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t}-\alpha_{t}^{(k)}\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t}))\geq f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x^{*})
. Thus,

a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}\|\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})\|^{2}\leq\frac{a^{2}\alpha_{t}^{(k)}}{\sigma}(f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})-f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x^{*})).

(140)

Summing over all
k
we get,

\sum_{k=1}^{N}a^{2}(\alpha_{t}^{(k)})^{2}\|\nabla f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{a^{2}\alpha_{t}^{(k)}}e_{t}^{(k)}.

(141)

Using the above in (
138
),

\displaystyle\|\hat{x}_{t+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{||m_{t+1}^{(k)}||^{2}}{N}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{x}_{t}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{p+(1-\gamma)(1+r)}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{||m^{(k)}_{t}||^{2}}

(142)

\displaystyle+\frac{\frac{a^{2}}{\sigma}(1+\frac{1}{p}+(1-\gamma)(1+\frac{1}{r}))-2a}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\alpha_{t}^{(k)}}e_{t}^{(k)}.

From the definitions in Theorems
1
,
2
,

\displaystyle\beta_{3}(p,r)=\Big(p+(1-\gamma)(1+r)\Big)

(143)

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{1}(p,r)=2/\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma}+\frac{1}{\sigma p}+\frac{(1-\gamma)(1+\frac{1}{r})}{\sigma}\Big)

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{2}(p,r)=\Big(2a-\frac{a^{2}}{\sigma}-\frac{a^{2}}{\sigma p}-(1-\gamma)(1+\frac{1}{r})\frac{a^{2}}{\sigma}\Big)

From Lemma
12
,
\exists\ (p(\epsilon),r(\epsilon))
such that

\displaystyle\Big(p(\epsilon)+(1-\gamma)(1+r(\epsilon))\Big)=1-\delta<1

(144)

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{1}(p(\epsilon),r(\epsilon))>\zeta-\epsilon.

Choosing
(p,r)=(p(\epsilon),r(\epsilon))
in
143
,
if
a\leq\zeta-\epsilon
, then
a\leq\tilde{a}_{1}(p(\epsilon),r(\epsilon))
.

It is shown in the proof of Theorem
2
, that
a\leq\tilde{a}_{1}(p(\epsilon),r(\epsilon))
implies
\tilde{a}_{2}(p(\epsilon),r(\epsilon))>0
. Also,
\alpha_{t}\geq\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathrm{min}}\rho
and
e_{t}^{(k)}=f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})-f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x^{*})
. Substituting these values in (
142
),

\displaystyle\|\hat{x}_{t+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{N}{||m_{t+1}^{(k)}||^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{x}_{t}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{||m^{(k)}_{t}||^{2}}

(145)

\displaystyle-\frac{\tilde{a}_{2}(p(\epsilon),r(\epsilon))}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathrm{min}}\rho}(f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})-f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x^{*})),

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{min}}\rho\big(\tilde{a}_{2}(p(\epsilon),r(\epsilon))\big)}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x_{t})-f_{i_{t}}^{(k)}(x^{*}))

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{x}_{t}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|\hat{x}_{t+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}

(146)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(||m^{(k)}_{t}||^{2}-||m_{t+1}^{(k)}||^{2}).

Taking expectation
E_{i_{t}}
with respect to the sampled data points chosen at all nodes

at time

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathrm{min}}\rho\big(\tilde{a}_{2}(p(\epsilon),r(\epsilon))\big(f(x_{t})-f(x^{*}))

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(||m^{(k)}_{t}||^{2}-E_{i_{t}}[||m_{t+1}^{(k)}||^{2}]).

Proof of Scaled Armijo Step-Size Search GD

A proof for
O(\frac{1}{T})
convergence of Armijo step-size search GD (CSGD-ASSS without the stochasticity in
f_{i_{t}}
) with scaling for all
\sigma\in(0,1)
and convex function
f
is presented in this section.

Theorem 15
.

(Deterministic-Uncompressed-Convex) Let
f(x)
be convex and
L
smooth. The Armijo step-size search gradient descent for
\sigma\in(0,1)
and scale factor
a<2\sigma
satisfies

\displaystyle f(\bar{x}_{T})-f(x^{*})\leq\frac{||x_{0}-x^{*}||^{2}}{\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathrm{min}}\rho(2a-\frac{a^{2}}{\sigma})T}

(150)

where
\bar{x}_{T}=\frac{1}{T}{\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}x_{i}}
.

Proof.

The descent step is
x_{t+1}=x_{t}-\alpha_{t}a\nabla f(x_{t})
where
a
is the scaling factor and
x^{*}
is the minimum.

\displaystyle||x_{t+1}-x^{*}||^{2}

\displaystyle=||x_{t}-\alpha_{t}a\nabla f(x_{t})-x^{*}||^{2}

(151)

\displaystyle=||x_{t}-x^{*}||^{2}+\alpha_{t}^{2}a^{2}||\nabla f(x_{t})||^{2}

\displaystyle-2\alpha_{t}a\langle x_{t}-x^{*},\nabla f(x_{t})\rangle

From the convexity of
f
,

\langle x^{*}-x_{t},\nabla f(x_{t})\rangle\leq f(x^{*})-f(x_{t}).

(152)

This implies

\displaystyle||x_{t+1}-x^{*}||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq||x_{t}-x^{*}||^{2}+\alpha_{t}^{2}a^{2}||\nabla f(x_{t})||^{2}

(153)

\displaystyle+2\alpha_{t}a(f(x^{*})-f(x_{t})).

The non-scaled iterate
\tilde{x}_{t}
(
\tilde{x}_{t}=x_{t}-\alpha_{t}\nabla f(x_{t})
) corresponding to the step-size
\alpha_{t}
returned by the Armijo step-size search satisfies

\displaystyle f(\tilde{x}_{t+1})-f(x_{t})\leq-\alpha_{t}\sigma||\nabla f(x_{t})||^{2}.

(154)

This implies

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}^{2}a^{2}||\nabla f(x_{t})||^{2}\leq\frac{a^{2}\alpha_{t}}{\sigma}(f(x_{t})-f(\tilde{x}_{t+1})).

(155)

Substituting (
155
) in (
153
),

\displaystyle||x_{t+1}-x^{*}||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq||x_{t}-x^{*}||^{2}+\frac{a^{2}\alpha_{t}}{\sigma}(f(x_{t})-f(\tilde{x}_{t+1}))

(156)

\displaystyle+2\alpha_{t}a(f(x^{*})-f(x_{t})).

Since
f(\tilde{x}_{t+1})\geq f(x^{*})
,

\displaystyle||x_{t+1}-x^{*}||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq||x_{t}-x^{*}||^{2}+\frac{a^{2}\alpha_{t}}{\sigma}(f(x_{t})-f(x^{*}))

(157)

\displaystyle+2\alpha_{t}a(f(x^{*})-f(x_{t})),

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}(2a-\frac{a^{2}}{\sigma})(f(x_{t})-f(x^{*}))\leq||x_{t}-x^{*}||^{2}-||x_{t+1}-x^{*}||^{2}

(158)

(2a-\frac{a^{2}}{\sigma})>0\iff a<2\sigma
.
Without scaling the factor
(2a-\frac{a^{2}}{\sigma})
would be
(2-\frac{1}{\sigma})
, thus limiting
\sigma\in[0.5,1)
in
[
15
]
.

For Armijo step-size search,
\alpha_{t}\geq\frac{2(1-\sigma)}{L}\rho
. Using this lower bound, summing over the horizon
T
and using Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle f(\frac{1}{T}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}x_{t}})-f(x^{*})\leq\frac{||x_{0}-x^{*}||^{2}}{\frac{2(1-\sigma)}{L}\rho(2a-\frac{a^{2}}{\sigma})T}.

(159)

Hence proved.

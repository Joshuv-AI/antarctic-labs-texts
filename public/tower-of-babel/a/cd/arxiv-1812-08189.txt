Subleading Power Rapidity Divergences and Power Corrections for 𝒒 𝑻 q_{T} Preprint: MIT-CTP 5087 DESY 18-207
Markus A. Ebert, Ian Moult, Iain W. Stewart, Frank J. Tackmann, Gherardo Vita, and Hua Xing Zhu

Abstract
A number of important observables exhibit logarithms in their perturbative description that are induced by emissions at widely separated rapidities. These include transverse-momentum ( q T q_{T} ) logarithms, logarithms involving heavy-quark or electroweak gauge boson masses, and small- x x logarithms. In this paper, we initiate the study of rapidity logarithms, and the associated rapidity divergences, at subleading order in the power expansion. This is accomplished using the soft collinear effective theory (SCET). We discuss the structure of subleading-power rapidity divergences and how to consistently regulate them. We introduce a new pure rapidity regulator and a corresponding MS ¯ \overline{\rm MS} -like scheme, which handles rapidity divergences while maintaining the homogeneity of the power expansion. We find that power-law rapidity divergences appear at subleading power, which give rise to derivatives of parton distribution functions. As a concrete example, we consider the q T q_{T} spectrum for color-singlet production, for which we compute the complete q T 2 / Q 2 q_{T}^{2}/Q^{2} suppressed power corrections at 𝒪 ⁡ ( α s ) \mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}) , including both logarithmic and nonlogarithmic terms. Our results also represent an important first step towards carrying out a resummation of subleading-power rapidity logarithms.

2
Rapidity Divergences and Regularization at Subleading Power

Rapidity divergences naturally arise in the calculation of observables sensitive to the transverse momentum of soft emissions. In a situation where we have a hard interaction scale
Q
and the relevant transverse momentum
k_{T}
of the fields is small compared to that scale,
\lambda\sim k_{T}/Q\ll 1
, the appropriate effective field theory (EFT) is SCET
II

Bauer:2002aj
, which contains modes with the following momentum scalings

\displaystyle n{-}\text{collinear}:\quad k_{n}\sim Q\,(\lambda^{2},1,\lambda)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle k^{-}/Q\sim 1\,,

(1)

\displaystyle{\bar{n}}{-}\text{collinear}:\quad k_{{\bar{n}}}\sim Q\,(1,\lambda^{2},\lambda)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle k^{-}/Q\sim\lambda^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\text{soft}:\hskip 51.21504ptk_{s}\sim Q\,(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle k^{-}/Q\sim\lambda\,.

Here we have used lightcone coordinates
(n\cdot k,{\bar{n}}\cdot k,k_{\perp})\equiv(k^{+},k^{-},k_{\perp})
, defined with respect to two lightlike reference vectors
n^{\mu}
and
{\bar{n}}^{\mu}
. For concreteness, we take them to be
n^{\mu}=(1,0,0,1)
and
{\bar{n}}^{\mu}=(1,0,0,-1)
.
Unlike SCET
I
where the modes are separated in virtuality, in SCET
II
the modes in the EFT have the same virtuality, but are distinguished by their longitudinal momentum (
k^{+}
or
k^{-}
), or equivalently, their rapidity
e^{2y_{k}}=k^{-}/k^{+}
. This separation into modes at hierarchical rapidities introduces divergences, which arise when
k^{+}/k^{-}\to\infty
or
k^{+}/k^{-}\to 0

Collins:1992tv
;
Manohar:2006nz
;
Collins:2008ht
;
Chiu:2012ir
;
Vladimirov:2017ksc
. These so-called rapidity divergences are not regulated by dimensional regularization, which is boost invariant and therefore cannot distinguish modes that are only separated in rapidity.

Rapidity divergences can be regulated by introducing a rapidity regulator that breaks boost invariance, allowing the modes to be distinguished, and logarithms associated with the different rapidity scales to be resummed. The rapidity divergences cancel between the different sectors of the effective theory, since they are not present in the full theory. They should not be thought of as UV, or IR, but as arising from the factorization in the EFT. By demanding invariance with respect to the regulator, one can derive renormalization group evolution equations (RGEs) in rapidity.
In SCET, a generic approach to rapidity evolution was introduced in refs.
Chiu:2011qc
;
Chiu:2012ir
. These rapidity RGEs allow for the resummation of large logarithms associated with hierarchical rapidity scales.

At leading power in the EFT expansion, the structure of rapidity divergences and the associated rapidity renormalization group are well understood by now, and they have been studied to high perturbative orders (see e.g. ref.
Li:2016ctv
at three-loop order). Indeed, in certain specific physical situations involving two lightlike directions, rapidity divergences can be conformally mapped to UV divergences
Hatta:2008st
;
Caron-Huot:2015bja
;
Caron-Huot:2016tzz
;
Vladimirov:2016dll
;
Vladimirov:2017ksc
, giving a relation between rapidity anomalous dimensions and standard UV anomalous dimensions. However, little is known about the structure of rapidity divergences or their renormalization beyond the leading power.

In this section, we discuss several interesting features of rapidity divergences at subleading power, focusing on the perturbative behavior at next-to-leading order (NLO). At subleading power there are no purely virtual corrections at NLO, and so we will focus on the case of the rapidity regularization of a single real emission, which allow us to identify and resolve a number of subtleties.
After a brief review of the structure of rapidity-divergent integrals at leading power in sec.
2.1
, we discuss additional issues that arise at subleading power in sec.
2.2

\eta

2.3

Finally, in sec.
2.4

2.1
Review of Rapidity Divergences at Leading Power

We begin by reviewing the structure of rapidity divergent integrals at leading power. As mentioned above, we restrict ourselves to the case of a single on-shell real emission, which suffices at NLO. Defining
\delta_{+}(k^{2})=\theta(k^{0})\delta(k^{2})
,
its contribution to a cross section sensitive to the transverse momentum
{\vec{k}}_{T}
of the emission is schematically given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma({\vec{k}}_{T})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{2}{k_{T}^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}k^{0}\mathrm{d}k^{z}\,\delta_{+}(k^{2})\,g(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k_{T}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{k^{-}}\,g(k)\Big|_{k^{+}=k_{T}^{2}/k^{-}}=\frac{1}{k_{T}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{+}}{k^{+}}\,g(k)\Big|_{k^{-}=k_{T}^{2}/k^{+}}\,.

(2)

Here, we have extracted the overall
1/k_{T}^{2}
behaviour, and
g(k)
is an observable and process dependent function, containing the remaining phase-space factors and amplitudes.
The precise form of
g(k)
is unimportant, except for the fact that it includes
kinematic constraints on the integration range of
k^{\pm}
,

g(k)\sim\theta(k^{\pm}-k_{\text{min}}^{\pm})\,\theta(k_{\text{max}}^{\pm}-k^{\pm})\,.

(3)

For our discussion we take

such that we can work in
d=4

In the full theory, eq. (
2.1

or

3

In the effective theory, one expands eq. (

1

\underbrace{\,k_{\text{min}}^{\pm}\to 0\,}_{\text{soft and collinear limits}}\,,\qquad\underbrace{\,k_{\text{max}}^{\pm}\to+\infty\,}_{\text{soft limit}}\,,

such that individual soft and collinear contributions acquire explicit divergences as
k^{\pm}\to 0
or
k^{\pm}\to\infty

R(k,\eta)

\lim_{\eta\to 0}R(k,\eta)=1

To describe the limit

1

To be specific, the soft limit of eq. (
2.1

1

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma_{s}({\vec{k}}_{T})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{k_{T}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}k_{s}^{-}}{k_{s}^{-}}\,g(k_{s})\Big|_{k_{s}^{+}=k_{T}^{2}/k_{s}^{-}}\,R(k_{s},\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k_{T}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{k^{-}}\,g_{s}(0)\,R(k,\eta)\times\bigl[1+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda)\bigr]\,.

(5)

Since the leading-power result must scale like

, the

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma_{n}({\vec{k}}_{T})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{k_{T}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}k_{n}^{-}}{k_{n}^{-}}\,g(k_{n})\Big|_{k_{n}^{+}=k_{T}^{2}/k_{n}^{-}}\,R(k_{n},\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k_{T}^{2}}\int_{0}^{Q}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{k^{-}}\,g_{n}\biggl(\frac{k^{-}}{Q}\biggr)\,R(k,\eta)\times\bigl[1+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda)\bigr]\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\bar{n}}({\vec{k}}_{T})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{k_{T}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}k_{\bar{n}}^{+}}{k_{\bar{n}}^{+}}\,g(k_{\bar{n}})\Big|_{k_{\bar{n}}^{-}=k_{T}^{2}/k_{\bar{n}}^{+}}\,R(k_{\bar{n}},\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k_{T}^{2}}\int_{0}^{Q}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{+}}{k^{+}}\,g_{\bar{n}}\biggl(\frac{k^{+}}{Q}\biggr)\,R(k,\eta)\times\bigl[1+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda)\bigr]\,.

(6)

In this case, only the lower bound on
k^{\pm}
is removed by the power expansion, while the upper limit is given by the relevant hard scale

Without the rapidity regulator, the integrals in eqs. (

2.1

2.2
Rapidity Regularization at Subleading Power

We now extend our discussion to subleading power, where we will find several new features.
First, while at leading power, rapidity divergences arise only from gluons, at subleading power rapidity divergences can arise also from soft quarks. Soft quarks have also been rapidity-regulated to derive the quark Regge trajectory
Moult:2017xpp
.
Here, since we consider only the case of a single real emission crossing the cut, this simply means that we must regulate both quarks and gluons. More generally, one would have to apply a rapidity regulator to all operators in the EFT, as has been done for the case of forward scattering in ref.
Rothstein:2016bsq
. It would be interesting to understand if these subleading rapidity divergences can also be conformally mapped to UV divergences of matrix elements, as was done for the rapidity divergences in the leading power
q_{T}
soft function in refs.
Vladimirov:2016dll
;
Vladimirov:2017ksc
.

Second, the structure of rapidity divergences becomes much richer at subleading power, placing additional constraints on the form of the rapidity regulator to maintain a simple power expansion. This more interesting divergence structure follows directly from power counting.
For example, the subleading corrections to the soft limit can be obtained by expanding the integrand in eq. (

) to higher orders in

-collinear particles with momenta

\displaystyle p_{1}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=(Q-k^{-})\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}+k_{\perp}^{\mu}+\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{Q-k^{-}}\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}\,,\qquad p_{2}^{\mu}=k^{-}\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}-k_{\perp}^{\mu}+\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{k^{-}}\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}\,.

(12)

The associated Lorentz-invariant kinematic variable is given by

\displaystyle s_{12}=(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}=\frac{k_{T}^{2}Q^{2}}{k^{-}(Q-k^{-})}\,.

(13)

Expanding any function of
s_{12}
in
k_{T}
thus gives rise to additional factors of the large momentum
k^{-}
.
Thus, in general, expanding
g(k_{n})
in the collinear limit can give rise to both positive and negative powers of
k^{-}
that accompany the power-suppression in
k_{T}^{2}
.
These factors are of course not completely independent, as the sum of all soft and collinear contributions must be rapidity finite, i.e., any rapidity divergences induced by these additional powers of
k^{-}
must in the end cancel against corresponding divergences in the soft and/or other collinear contributions.
In summary, the generic form of integrals in the collinear expansion is given by

\displaystyle I_{n}^{(\alpha)}(R)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{Q}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{k^{-}}\biggl(\frac{k^{-}}{Q}\biggr)^{\alpha}g_{n}\biggl(\frac{k^{-}}{Q}\biggr)R(k,\eta)\,,

(14)

\displaystyle I_{\bar{n}}^{(\alpha)}(R)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{Q}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{+}}{k^{+}}\biggl(\frac{k^{+}}{Q}\biggr)^{\alpha}g_{\bar{n}}\biggl(\frac{k^{+}}{Q}\biggr)R(k,\eta)\,.

(15)

Here,
g_{n}(x)
and
g_{\bar{n}}(x)
are regular functions as
x\to 0
.
At LP, only
\alpha=0
contributes, which gives rise to logarithmic divergences,
while at subleading power for
\alpha\neq 0
we again encounter power-law divergences.
As we will see in sec.
2.4
, these power-law divergences have a nontrivial effect,
namely they lead to derivatives of PDFs in the perturbative expansion for hadron collider processes.

The presence of power-law divergences at subleading power also implies that more care must be taken to ensure that the regulator does not unnecessarily complicate the power counting of the EFT. For example, with the exponential regulator
Li:2016axz
, or with a hard cutoff, power-law divergences lead to the appearance of powers of the regulator scale, and hence break the homogeneity of the power expansion of the theory.

Furthermore, at leading power one also has the freedom to introduce and then drop subleading terms to simplify any stage of the calculation. While this may seem a general feature and not appear very related to the regularization of rapidity divergences, we will see in a moment that this freedom, explicitly or not, is actually used in most of the rapidity regulators in the literature.

In summary, having a convenient-to-use regulator at subleading power imposes stronger constraints than at leading power.
In particular, we find that the regulator

•

must be able to regulate not only Wilson lines, but all operators, including those generating soft quark emissions,

•

must be able to deal not only with logarithmic divergences, but also with power-law divergences without violating the power counting of the EFT by inducing power-law mixing,

•

and should be homogeneous in the power-counting parameter
\lambda
to minimize mixing between different powers.

The first requirement means one cannot use regulators acting only on Wilson lines, such as taking Wilson lines off the light-cone as in ref.
Collins:1350496
, the
\delta
regulator as used in refs.
Chiu:2009yx
;
GarciaEchevarria:2011rb
, and the
\eta
regulator as used in refs.
Chiu:2011qc
;
Chiu:2012ir
, while the
\eta
regulator as modified and employed in refs.
Rothstein:2016bsq
;
Moult:2017xpp
and the analytic regulator of ref.
Becher:2011dz
can be used.
The second requirement is satisfied by all dimensional regularization type regulators, such as the
\eta
regulator or analytic regulator, but not by those that are more like a hard cutoff, including the exponential regulator
Li:2016axz
.
To highlight the last point, in the following we discuss in more detail the properties of the
\eta
regulator at subleading power.

2.2.1
The
\eta
Regulator at Subleading Power

In the
\eta
regulator, one regulates the
k^{z}
momentum of emissions through the regulator function
(see eq. (
7
))

R_{z}(k,\eta)=w^{2}\biggl|\frac{2k^{z}}{\nu}\biggr|^{-\eta}=w^{2}\nu^{\eta}|k^{-}-k^{+}|^{-\eta}\,.

(16)

For a single massless emission this corresponds to regulating its phase-space integral as

\int\mathrm{d}^{d}k\,\delta_{+}(k^{2})\quad\to\quad\int\mathrm{d}^{d}k\,\delta_{+}(k^{2})\,R_{z}(k,\eta)=w^{2}\nu^{\eta}\int\mathrm{d}^{d}k\,\delta_{+}(k^{2})\,|k^{-}-k^{+}|^{-\eta}\,.

(17)

In the soft limit
k^{+}\sim k^{-}\sim\lambda Q
, the regulator is homogeneous in
\lambda
and therefore does not need to be expanded. The prototypical soft integral in eq. (
11
) evaluates to

\displaystyle I_{s}^{(\alpha)}(R_{z})

\displaystyle=w^{2}\nu^{\eta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{k^{-}}\biggl(\frac{k^{-}}{Q}\biggr)^{\alpha}\biggl|k^{-}-\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{k^{-}}\biggr|^{-\eta}

\displaystyle=w^{2}\biggl(\frac{\nu}{k_{T}}\biggr)^{\eta}\biggl(\frac{k_{T}}{Q}\biggr)^{\alpha}\cos\biggl(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}\biggr)\,\sin\biggl(\frac{\eta\pi}{2}\biggr)\,\frac{1}{\pi}\Gamma(1-\eta)\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{\eta}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{\eta}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\Bigr)\,.

(18)

Symmetry under
\alpha\leftrightarrow-\alpha
implies that

\displaystyle I_{s}^{(-\alpha)}(R_{z})=\biggl(\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{Q^{2}}\biggr)^{-\alpha}I_{s}^{(\alpha)}(R_{z})\,.

(19)

This reflects the symmetry under exchanging
k^{-}\leftrightarrow k^{+}
,
which is not broken by the
\eta
regulator.
One can easily deduce the behavior as
\eta\to 0
from eq. (
2.2.1
).
Since
\sin(\eta)\sim\eta
, a pole in
\eta
can only arise if both
\Gamma
functions have poles,
which requires
\alpha=0
.
A finite result is obtained if exactly one
\Gamma
function yields a pole, which requires
\alpha
to be even.
For odd
\alpha
, the expression vanishes at
\eta=0
.
Hence, the exact behavior for
\eta\to 0
is given by

\displaystyle I_{s}^{(0)}(R_{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\eta}+\ln\frac{\nu^{2}}{k_{T}^{2}}+{\mathcal{O}}(\eta)\,,

\displaystyle I_{s}^{(\alpha)}(R_{z})

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle(\alpha~\mathrm{odd})\,,

\displaystyle I_{s}^{(\alpha)}(R_{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{|\alpha|}\biggl(\frac{k_{T}}{Q}\biggr)^{\alpha}+{\mathcal{O}}(\eta)\qquad

\displaystyle(\alpha~\mathrm{even})\,.

(20)

In particular, since the
\eta
regulator behaves like dimensional regularization,
it is well-behaved for power-law divergences and the soft integrals only give rise
to poles from the logarithmic divergences.

In the collinear sector, the behavior is more complicated at subleading power,
because the regulator factor
2k^{z}=k^{-}-k^{+}
is not homogeneous in
\lambda
.
At leading power
Chiu:2011qc
;
Chiu:2012ir
;
Rothstein:2016bsq
, one takes advantage of the fact that
2k^{z}\to k^{-}
in the
n
-collinear limit and
2k^{z}\to k^{+}
in the
{\bar{n}}
-collinear limit, so that the expanded result correctly regulates the collinear cases, and makes it symmetric under the exchange
n\leftrightarrow{\bar{n}}
. A fact that will be important for our analysis is that this power expansion induces higher order terms. These terms have never been considered in the literature since they are not important at leading power.
However, at subleading power one can no longer neglect the subleading component of the regulator.
Implementing the
\eta
regulator at subleading power in the collinear limits
thus requires to expand the regulator eq. (
16
) itself,

\displaystyle R_{z}(k_{n},\eta)

\displaystyle=w^{2}\nu^{\eta}\biggl|k_{n}^{-}-\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{k_{n}^{-}}\biggl|^{-{\eta}}=w^{2}\,\biggl|\frac{k_{n}^{-}}{\nu}\biggl|^{-\eta}\,\biggl[1+{\eta}\,\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{(k_{n}^{-})^{2}}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\biggr]\,,

\displaystyle R_{z}(k_{\bar{n}},\eta)

\displaystyle=w^{2}\biggl|\frac{k_{\bar{n}}^{+}}{\nu}\biggr|^{-\eta}\biggl[1+{\eta}\,\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{(k_{\bar{n}}^{+})^{2}}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\biggr]\,.

(21)

Applying this to the general LP integral in the
n
-collinear sector, eq. (
14
) with
\alpha=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{n}^{(0)}(R_{z})

\displaystyle=w^{2}\int_{0}^{Q}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{k^{-}}\biggl|\frac{k^{-}}{\nu}\biggl|^{-\eta}g_{n}\biggl(\frac{k^{-}}{Q}\biggr)

\displaystyle\quad+\eta\,w^{2}\,\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{Q^{2}}\int_{0}^{Q}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{k^{-}}\biggl|\frac{k^{-}}{\nu}\biggl|^{-\eta}\biggl(\frac{k^{-}}{Q}\biggr)^{-2}g_{n}\biggl(\frac{k^{-}}{Q}\biggr)+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\,,

(22)

and analogously for
I_{\bar{n}}^{(0)}(R_{z})
.
Here, the first line is the standard LP integral, while the second line arises from expanding the regulator and is suppressed by
k_{T}^{2}/Q^{2}\sim\lambda^{2}
.
While it is also proportional to
\eta
, the remaining integral can produce a
1/\eta
rapidity divergence to yield an overall finite contribution.

In sec.
3
, we will see explicitly that these terms from expanding the regulator are crucial to obtain the correct final result at subleading power.
However, in practice they are cumbersome to track in the calculation and yield complicated structures.
To establish an all-orders factorization theorem, the mixing of different orders in the power expansion due to the regulator becomes a serious complication. Hence, it is desirable to employ a rapidity regulator that is homogeneous in
\lambda
. We will present such a regulator in the following sec.
2.3
.

2.3
Pure Rapidity Regularization

We wish to establish a rapidity regulator that is homogeneous at leading power such that it does not mix LP and NLP integrals, as observed in sec.
2.2.1
for the
\eta
regulator.
This can be achieved by implementing the regulator similar to the
\eta
regulator of refs.
Chiu:2011qc
;
Chiu:2012ir
;
Rothstein:2016bsq
, but instead of regulating the momentum
k^{z}
with factors of
w|2k^{z}/\nu|^{-\eta/2}
, one regulates the rapidity
y_{k}
of the momentum
k^{\mu}
, where

\displaystyle y_{k}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{{\bar{n}}\cdot k}{n\cdot k}\,.

(23)

To implement a regulator involving rapidity we use
3
3

3

Note that we can implement the pure rapidity regulator in terms of label and residual momentum operators for example as

w^{2}\,\upsilon^{\eta}\,\biggl|\frac{{\bar{n}}\cdot({\mathcal{P}}+\partial)}{n\cdot({\mathcal{P}}+\partial)}\biggr|^{-\eta/2}\,.

(24)

where the label momentum operator
{\mathcal{P}}
picks out the large
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{0})
momentum component
of the operator it acts on, while
\partial
picks out the
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda)
or
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})
components.
In this case, the operator

\displaystyle\hat{Y}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{{\bar{n}}\cdot({\mathcal{P}}+\partial)}{n\cdot({\mathcal{P}}+\partial)}

(25)

picks out the rapidity of the operator it acts on.

factors of

w^{2}\upsilon^{\eta}\left|\frac{{\bar{n}}\cdot k}{n\cdot k}\right|^{-\eta/2}=w^{2}\upsilon^{\eta}e^{-y_{k}\eta}\,.

(26)

Here we have defined a rapidity scale
\upsilon
(
\upsilon
)
which is the analog of the scale
\nu
(
\nu
) in the
\eta
regulator.
Although
\upsilon
is dimensionless, in contrast to the dimensionful
\nu
, it still shares the same properties as pure dimensional regularization. In particular, it will give rise to poles in
\eta
that can be absorbed in
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
-like rapidity counterterms.
To ensure
\upsilon
independence of eq. (
26
), we introduced a bookkeeping parameter
w=w(\upsilon)
in analogy to the bookkeeping parameter
w(\nu)
in the
\eta
regulator, see eq. (
9
) and ref.
Chiu:2012ir
.
Also note that this regulator does not affect UV renormalization, which in SCET
II
arises from transverse momenta going to infinity and thus is orthogonal to regulating rapidity.

We call eq. (
26
) the
pure rapidity regulator
, and pure rapidity regularization the procedure of regulating rapidity divergences using eq. (
26
). When only the
1/\eta
poles are subtracted we then refer to the renormalized result as being in the
pure rapidity renormalization scheme
.

If we want to make the rapidity scale
\upsilon
into a true rapidity scale
\Upsilon
, then we can change variables as

\displaystyle\upsilon\equiv e^{\Upsilon}\,.

(27)

With this definition eq. (
26
) becomes

\displaystyle w^{2}\upsilon^{\eta}e^{-y_{k}\eta}\equiv w^{2}e^{\eta(\Upsilon-y_{k})}\,,

(28)

and the factor regulating divergences depends on a rapidity difference between the scale parameter
\Upsilon
and
y_{k}
.

It is interesting to consider the behavior of amplitudes regulated with eq. (
28
) under a reparameterization transformation known as RPI-III
Manohar:2002fd
, which takes
n^{\mu}\to e^{-\beta}n^{\mu}
and
{\bar{n}}^{\mu}\to e^{\beta}{\bar{n}}^{\mu}
for some, not necessarily infinitesimal, constant
\beta
.
For a single collinear sector, this can be interpreted as a boost transformation.
Since RPI transformations can be applied independently for each set of collinear basis vectors
\{n_{i},\bar{n}_{i}\}
they in general constitute a broader class of symmetry transformations in SCET.
Prior to including a regulator for rapidity divergences all complete SCET amplitudes are invariant under such transformations.
All previous rapidity regulators violate this symmetry.
For the pure rapidity regulator in eq. (
28
) we have
y_{k}\to y_{k}+\beta
, so the transformation is quite simple.
4
4

4

Any operators that are defined such that they transform under RPI-III, will do so by a factor
e^{k\beta}
, where
k
is their RPI-III charge.
The pure rapidity regulator therefore has an RPI-III charge of
-\eta
.
This leads to rapidity-renormalized collinear and soft functions in SCET which carry this charge.
When considering any observable like a cross section, the combined charge of the renormalized functions describing this observable is zero.

It can be compensated by defining the rapidity scale to transform like a rapidity,

\Upsilon\to\Upsilon+\beta
.
Therefore, the
\upsilon^{\eta}
factor in the regulator does for RPI-III what the usual

\mu^{\epsilon}
factor does for the mass-dimensionality in dimensional regularization.

As an example of the application of this new regulator, we consider again a real emission with momentum
k^{\mu}
. The regulator function
R(k,\eta)
that follows from eq. (
26
) is given by

\displaystyle R_{Y}(k,\eta)

\displaystyle=w^{2}\,\upsilon^{\eta}\,\biggl|\frac{k^{-}}{k^{+}}\biggr|^{-\eta/2}=w^{2}\,\upsilon^{\eta}\,e^{-\eta\,y_{k}}\,.

(29)

The real-emission phase space is then regulated as

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}^{d}k\,\delta_{+}(k^{2})

\displaystyle\quad\to\quad\int\mathrm{d}^{d}k\,\delta_{+}(k^{2})\,R_{Y}(k,\eta)=\int\mathrm{d}^{d}k\,\delta_{+}(k^{2})\,w^{2}\,\upsilon^{\eta}\,e^{-\eta\,y_{k}}\,.

(30)

A peculiar feature of the pure rapidity regulator is that it renders the prototypical soft integrals scaleless such that they vanish. That is, using eq. (
29
) in
eq. (
11
), we obtain

\displaystyle I_{s}^{(\alpha)}(R_{Y})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{k^{-}}\biggl(\frac{k^{-}}{Q}\biggr)^{\alpha}R_{Y}(k,\eta)=w^{2}\,\upsilon^{\eta}\,k_{T}^{\eta}\,Q^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k^{-}\,(k^{-})^{\alpha-\eta-1}=0\,.

(31)

The final integrals are scaleless and vanish for all integer values of
\alpha
, just like scaleless integrals vanish in dimensional regularization.
5
5

5

Technically one can find terms of the form
1/\eta-1/\eta
, which can be set to zero via analytic continuation in the standard manner.

Considering the collinear sectors, the prototypical collinear integrals in eq. (
14
) with
R_{Y}(k,\eta)
become

\displaystyle I_{n}^{(\alpha)}(R_{Y})

\displaystyle=w^{2}\,\upsilon^{\eta}\,k_{T}^{+\eta}\,Q^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{Q}\mathrm{d}k^{-}\,(k^{-})^{\alpha-\eta-1}\,g_{n}\biggl(\frac{k^{-}}{Q}\biggr)\,,

\displaystyle I_{\bar{n}}^{(\alpha)}(R_{Y})

\displaystyle=w^{2}\,\upsilon^{\eta}\,k_{T}^{-\eta}\,Q^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{Q}\mathrm{d}k^{+}\,(k^{+})^{\alpha+\eta-1}\,g_{\bar{n}}\biggl(\frac{k^{+}}{Q}\biggr)\,.

(32)

Although the regulator does not act symmetrically in the
n
-collinear and
{\bar{n}}
-collinear sectors, the asymmetry is easy to track by taking
\eta\leftrightarrow-\eta
and
\upsilon\leftrightarrow 1/\upsilon
when swapping
n\leftrightarrow{\bar{n}}
and
k^{+}\leftrightarrow k^{-}
.
Since
R_{Y}(k,\eta)
is homogeneous in
\lambda
, it does not generate any subleading power terms, in contrast to eq. (
2.2.1
) for the
\eta
regulator. In particular, the LP integral becomes

\displaystyle I_{n}^{(0)}(R_{Y})

\displaystyle=w^{2}\,\upsilon^{\eta}\,k_{T}^{+\eta}\int_{0}^{Q}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{(k^{-})^{1+\eta}}\,g_{n}\biggl(\frac{k^{-}}{Q}\biggr)

\displaystyle=w^{2}\,\Bigl(\upsilon\frac{k_{T}}{Q}\Bigr)^{\eta}\int_{0}^{Q}\mathrm{d}k^{-}\biggl[-\frac{1}{\eta}\,\delta(k^{-})+\frac{1}{Q}{\mathcal{L}}_{0}\biggl(\frac{k^{-}}{Q}\biggr)\,+\mathcal{O}(\eta)\biggr]g_{n}\biggl(\frac{k^{-}}{Q}\biggr)\,,

(33)

where we used the standard distributional identity
1/x^{1+\eta}=-\delta(x)/\eta+{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(x)+\mathcal{O}(\eta)
to extract the
1/\eta
divergence. (See sec.
2.4
below for a more general
discussion.)
Taking
\eta\to-\eta
, the analogous
1/\eta
pole in the
{\bar{n}}
-collinear sector
has the opposite sign, such that the
1/\eta
poles cancel when adding the
n
-collinear and
{\bar{n}}
-collinear contributions.
This is a general feature in all cases where the soft contribution vanishes as in eq. (
31
).

Some comments about the features of the pure rapidity regulator are in order:

•

It involves the rapidity

e^{2y_{k}}\equiv\frac{{\bar{n}}\cdot k}{n\cdot k}\,,

(34)

and therefore breaks boost invariance as required to regulate rapidity divergences. The boost invariance is restored by the dimensionless
\upsilon
rapidity scale, analogous to how the dimensionful mass scale
\mu
in dimensional regularization restores the dimensionality.

•

Rapidity divergences appear as
1/\eta
poles, allowing the definition of the pure rapidity renormalization scheme as a dimensional regularization-like scheme.

•

At each order in perturbation theory, the poles in
\eta
and the
\upsilon
-dependent pieces cancel when combining the results for the
n
-collinear,
{\bar{n}}
-collinear, and soft sectors.

•

The pure rapidity regulator is homogeneous
6
6

6

In cases where it is possible to combine label and residual momenta in the phase space integral that needs to be rapidity regulated.
in the SCET power counting parameter
\lambda
. Therefore it does not need to be power expanded, and hence does not mix contributions at different orders in the power expansion.

•

For the case of a single real emission considered here:

–

Soft integrals and zero-bin
Manohar:2006nz
integrals are scaleless and vanish.

–

It follows that the
\eta
poles and the
\upsilon
dependent pieces cancel between the
n
-collinear and
{\bar{n}}
-collinear sectors.

–

The results for the
n
-collinear and
{\bar{n}}
-collinear sectors are not identical but are trivially related by taking
\eta\leftrightarrow-\eta
and
\upsilon\leftrightarrow 1/\upsilon
when swapping
n\leftrightarrow{\bar{n}}
.

The introduction of this new pure rapidity regulator allows us to regulate rapidity divergences at any order in the EFT power expansion, while maintaining the power counting of the EFT independently at each order.

Although in this paper we will only use pure rapidity regularization for a single real emission at fixed order, we note that one can derive a rapidity renormalization group for the pure rapidity regulator by imposing that the cross section must be independent of
\upsilon
.
Similar to the
\eta
regulator, this regulator is not analytical and can also be used to properly regulate virtual and massive loops. This will be discussed in detail elsewhere.

To conclude this section we note that the pure rapidity regulator can be seen as a particular case of a broader class of homogeneous rapidity regulators given by

\displaystyle R_{c}(k,\eta)=w^{2}\,\upsilon^{(1-c)\eta/2}\,\biggl|\frac{k^{-}}{\nu}\biggr|^{-\eta/2}\biggl|\frac{k^{+}}{\nu}\biggr|^{-c\eta/2}\,,

(35)

where
c\neq 1
is an arbitrary parameter governing the antisymmetry between the
n
-collinear and
{\bar{n}}
-collinear sectors.
As for the pure rapidity regulator, this regulator is homogeneous in
\lambda
and renders the same class of soft integrals scaleless.
However, it requires an explicit dimensionful scale
\nu
to have the correct mass dimension.
Note that for
c=1
, eq. (
35
) only depends on the boost invariant product
k^{+}k^{-}
and therefore does not regulate rapidity divergences.
For
c=-1
, it recovers the pure rapidity regulator and the dependence on
\nu
cancels.
Lastly, for
c=0
and massless real emissions, eq. (
35
) essentially reduces to the regulator of ref.
Becher:2011dz
.
We choose
c=-1
because it yields the same finite terms in the
n
-collinear and
{\bar{n}}
-collinear functions, and thus has enhanced symmetry.
Choosing a different value of
c
shifts terms between the two sectors, see also appendix
C
. The combined result is always independent of
c
.

2.4
Distributional Treatment of Power Law Divergences

To complete our treatment of rapidity divergences at subleading power, we show how their distributional structure can be consistently treated when expanded against a general test function.
In particular, we will see that the power-law rapidity divergences lead to derivatives of PDFs.

In the collinear limit at NLP, we obtain divergent integrals of the form

\int_{0}^{Q}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{Q}\frac{g_{n}(k^{-}/Q)}{(k^{-}/Q)^{a+\eta}}\,,

(36)

which appear for both the
\eta
regulator (with
a=1-\alpha=1,2,3
at NLO) and the pure rapidity regulator (with
a=1-\alpha=1,2
at NLO).

The function
g_{n}(k^{-}/Q)
is defined to be regular for
k^{-}/Q\to 0
.
If it is known analytically, we can in principle evaluate the integral in eq. (
36
) analytically and expand the result for
\eta\to 0
to obtain the regularized expression.
However,
g_{n}(k^{-}/Q)
is typically not given in analytic form.
In particular, for
pp
collisions it contains the parton distribution functions (PDFs)
f(x)
.
Therefore, to extract the rapidity divergence, we need to expand
1/{(k^{-})^{a+\eta}}
in
\eta
in a distributional sense.
To do so, we first change the integration variable from
k^{-}
to the dimensionless variable
z
defined through
k^{-}=Q(1-z)
,
such that eq. (
36
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,\frac{\tilde{g}(z)}{(1-z)^{a+\eta}}\,,\qquad\tilde{g}(z)=g_{n}(1-z)\,.

(37)

In eq. (
37
), the rapidity divergence arises as
z\to 1
.
For
a=1
, it can be extracted using the standard distributional identity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-z)^{1+\eta}}=-\frac{\delta(1-z)}{\eta}+{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z)+{\mathcal{O}}(\eta)\,,

(38)

where
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(y)=[\theta(y)/y]_{+}
is the standard plus distribution and we remind the reader that its convolution against a test function
\tilde{g}(z)
is given by

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,\tilde{g}(z){\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z)=\int_{x}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,\frac{\tilde{g}(z)-\tilde{g}(1)}{1-z}+\tilde{g}(1)\underbrace{\int_{x}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z)}_{\ln(1-x)}\,,\quad x\in[0,1]\,.

(39)

For
a>1
, these distributions need to be generalized to higher-order plus distributions subtracting higher derivatives as well.
For example, for
a=2
one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-z)^{2+\eta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta^{\prime}(1-z)}{\eta}-\delta(1-z)+{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{++}(1-z)+{\mathcal{O}}(\eta)\,,

(40)

where the second-order plus function
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{++}(1-z)
regulates the quadratic divergence
1/(1-z)^{2}
.
Its action on a test function
\tilde{g}(z)
is given by a double subtraction,

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,\tilde{g}(z){\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{++}(1-z)

\displaystyle=\int_{x}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,\frac{\tilde{g}(z)-[\tilde{g}(1)+\tilde{g}^{\prime}(1)(z-1)]}{(1-z)^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{g}(1)\underbrace{\int_{x}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{++}(1-z)}_{-x/(1-x)}+\,\tilde{g}^{\prime}(1)\underbrace{\int_{x}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,(z-1){\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{++}(1-z)}_{-\ln(1-x)}\,.

(41)

In appendix
B
, we give more details on these distributions, generalizing to arbitrary
a\geq 1
.
Note that the second-order plus function has also appeared for example in ref.
Mateu:2012nk
.

Eq. (
40
) implies the appearance of derivatives of delta functions,
\delta^{\prime}(1-z)
, which will induce derivatives of the PDFs that are contained in
\tilde{g}(z)
. The appearance of such derivatives in subleading power calculations was first shown in ref.
Moult:2016fqy
in the context of SCET
I
-like observables. However, in such cases they arose simply from a Taylor expansion of the momentum being extracted from the PDF.
Here, they also arise from power-law divergences, a new mechanism to induce derivatives of PDFs.
Recently, power-law divergences inducing derivatives of PDFs have appeared also in the study of SCET
I
-like observables involving multiple collinear directions at subleading power
Bhattacharya:2018vph
. We believe they are a general feature of calculations beyond leading power.

In practice, the higher-order distributions can be cumbersome to work with.
Instead, we find it more convenient to use integration-by-parts relations to reduce the divergence in eq. (
37
) to the linear divergence
1/(1-z)
, which yields explicit derivatives of the test function.
For the cases
a=2
and
a=3
we encounter in sec.
3
, this gives

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\frac{\tilde{g}(z)}{(1-z)^{2+\eta}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}^{\prime}(1)\biggl(\frac{1}{\eta}-1\biggr)-\frac{\tilde{g}(x)}{1-x}-\int_{x}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,\tilde{g}^{\prime}(z){\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z)+{\mathcal{O}}(\eta)\,,

(42)

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\frac{\tilde{g}(z)}{(1-z)^{3+\eta}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}^{\prime\prime}(1)\biggl(-\frac{1}{2\eta}+\frac{3}{4}\biggr)-\frac{\tilde{g}(x)+(x-1)\tilde{g}^{\prime}(x)}{2(1-x)^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}^{1}\mathrm{d}z\,\tilde{g}^{\prime\prime}(z){\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z)+{\mathcal{O}}(\eta)\,.

(43)

Equations (
42
) and (
2.4
) can be used to write the kernels fully in terms of a standard
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
, but they must be applied within the integral to directly yield derivatives of the test function
\tilde{g}(z)
.

In our application in sec.
3
,
\tilde{g}(z)
will always involve the PDF
f(x/z)
and vanish at
z=x
.
We can thus also write eqs. (
42
) and (
2.4
) as operator equations,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-z)^{2+\eta}}

\displaystyle\quad\to\quad\biggl[\biggl(\frac{1}{\eta}-1\biggr)\delta(1-z)-{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z)\biggr]\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\,,

(44)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-z)^{3+\eta}}

\displaystyle\quad\to\quad\biggl[\delta(1-z)\biggl(-\frac{1}{2\eta}+\frac{3}{4}\biggr)+\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z)\biggr]\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}^{2}z}+\frac{\tilde{g}^{\prime}(x)}{2(1-x)}\delta(1-z)\,.

(45)

Note that the second relation is quite peculiar, as we have to add the boundary term proportional to
g^{\prime}(x)
,
and thus cannot be interpreted as a distributional relation.
In our calculation in sec.
3
, this term will not contribute due to an overall suppression by
\eta
, such that only the divergent term in eq. (
45
) needs to be kept.

3
Power Corrections for Color-Singlet
q_{T}
Spectra

In this section we use our understanding of rapidity regularization at subleading power
to compute the perturbative power corrections to the transverse momentum
q_{T}
in color-singlet production
at invariant mass
Q
, which is one of the most well studied observables in QCD.
Schematically, the cross section differential in
q_{T}
can be expanded as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(0)}}{\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}+\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2)}}{\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}+\cdots\,,

(46)

where
\sigma^{(0)}
is the leading-power cross section and
\sigma^{(2n)}
the N
n
LP cross section.
In general, in this section we will denote power suppression in
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{n})
with
\lambda\sim q_{T}/Q
relative to the leading-power result through superscripts
(n)
.
The
\sigma^{(2n)}
terms scale like

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2n)}}{\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{q_{T}^{2}}\biggl(\frac{q_{T}^{2}}{Q^{2}}\biggl)^{n}\,,

(47)

and hence only the LP cross section is singular as
q_{T}\to 0
.
In particular,
\sigma^{(0)}
contains Sudakov double logarithms
\log^{2}(Q/q_{T})
.

The factorization of
\sigma^{(0)}
in terms of transverse-momentum dependent PDFs (TMDPDFs) was first shown by Collins, Soper, and Sterman in refs.
Collins:1981uk
;
Collins:1981va
;
Collins:1984kg
and later elaborated on by Collins in ref.
Collins:1350496
.
Its structure was also studied in refs.
Catani:2000vq
;
deFlorian:2001zd
;
Catani:2010pd
.
The factorization was also studied in the framework of SCET by various groups, see e.g. refs.
Becher:2010tm
;
GarciaEchevarria:2011rb
;
Chiu:2012ir
.
Using the notation of ref.
Chiu:2012ir
, the factorized LP cross section for the production of a color-singlet final state
L
with invariant mass
Q
and total rapidity
Y
in a proton-proton collision can be written as
7
7

7

We suppress that for gluon-gluon fusion,
H
and
B
carry polarization indices.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(0)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}^{2}{\vec{q}}_{T}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\!\sum_{i,j}H_{ij}(Q,\mu)\!\int\!\!\mathrm{d}^{2}{\vec{b}}_{T}\,e^{\mathrm{i}{\vec{q}}_{T}\cdot{\vec{b}}_{T}}\tilde{B}_{i}\bigl(x_{a},{\vec{b}}_{T},\mu,\nu\bigr)\tilde{B}_{j}\bigl(x_{b},{\vec{b}}_{T},\mu,\nu\bigr)\tilde{S}(b_{T},\mu,\nu)\,,\!

(48)

where
x_{a,b}=Qe^{\pm Y}/E_{\mathrm{cm}}
are the momentum fractions carried by the incoming partons.
In eq. (
48
),
H_{ij}
is the hard function describing virtual corrections to the underlying hard process
ij\to L
,
the
\tilde{B}_{i}
are TMD beam functions in Fourier space and
\tilde{S}
is the TMD soft function in Fourier space.
While
H_{ij}
only depends on the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
renormalization scale
\mu
, the beam and soft functions also depend on the rapidity renormalization scale
\nu
.

For nonperturbative
q_{T}\sim b_{T}^{-1}\sim\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
, the
\tilde{B}_{i}
become genuinely nonperturbative functions,
while for perturbative
q_{T}\sim b_{T}^{-1}\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
they can be matched perturbatively onto PDFs,

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{i}(x,{\vec{b}}_{T},\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}\,\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}\Bigl(\frac{x}{z},{\vec{b}}_{T},\mu,\nu\Bigr)f_{j}(z,\mu)\,.

(49)

The perturbative kernels
{\mathcal{I}}_{ij}
are known to two loops
Catani:2011kr
;
Catani:2012qa
;
Gehrmann:2012ze
;
Gehrmann:2014yya
,
and the soft function
\tilde{S}
is known to three loops
Echevarria:2015byo
;
Luebbert:2016itl
;
Li:2016ctv
.
This has allowed resummation to next-to-next-to-next-to leading logarithmic accuracy
Bizon:2017rah
;
Chen:2018pzu
;
Bizon:2018foh
.

Recently, there has been some progress towards a nonperturbative factorization of the NLP cross section
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2)}/\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}
, which involves higher twist PDFs
Balitsky:2017gis
;
Balitsky:2017flc
.
Here, we are interested in studying the perturbative power corrections to the NLP terms,
where one can perform an OPE to match onto standard PDFs.
At subleading power, the perturbative kernels also involve (higher) derivatives of distributions,
which can always be reduced to standard distributions acting on derivatives of PDFs.
The NLP cross section at
{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha_{s})
thus takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2,1)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}=\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)\,\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\int_{x_{a}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{a}}{z_{a}}\int_{x_{b}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{b}}{z_{b}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl[f_{i}\biggl(\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}\biggr)f_{j}\biggl(\frac{x_{b}}{z_{b}}\biggr)C_{f_{i}f_{j}}^{(2,1)}(z_{a},z_{b},q_{T})+\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}f^{\prime}_{i}\biggl(\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}\biggr)\frac{x_{b}}{z_{b}}f^{\prime}_{j}\biggl(\frac{x_{b}}{z_{b}}\biggr)C_{f^{\prime}_{i}f_{j}^{\prime}}^{(2,1)}(z_{a},z_{b},q_{T})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}f^{\prime}_{i}\biggl(\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}\biggr)f_{j}\biggl(\frac{x_{b}}{z_{b}}\biggr)C_{f_{i}^{\prime}f_{j}}^{(2,1)}(z_{a},z_{b},q_{T})+f_{i}\biggl(\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}\biggr)\frac{x_{b}}{z_{b}}f^{\prime}_{j}\biggl(\frac{x_{b}}{z_{b}}\biggr)C_{f_{i}f_{j}^{\prime}}^{(2,1)}(z_{a},z_{b},q_{T})\biggr]\,,

(50)

where
\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}
is the LO partonic cross section which serves as an overall normalization.
The
C^{(2,1)}_{ab}
are perturbative coefficients, expressed in terms of distributions, and we suppress the explicit
Q
and
Y
dependence in the kernels
C^{(2,1)}_{ab}
.
In general, at order
\alpha_{s}^{n}
their logarithmic structure is

\displaystyle C^{(2,n)}_{ab}(z_{a},z_{b},q_{T})=\sum_{m=0}^{2n-1}C^{(2,n)}_{ab,m}(z_{a},z_{b})\ln^{m}\frac{Q^{2}}{q_{T}^{2}}\,.

(51)

More explicitly, at NLO they have the form

\displaystyle C^{(2,1)}_{ab}(z_{a},z_{b},q_{T})=C^{(2,1)}_{ab,1}(z_{a},z_{b})\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{q_{T}^{2}}+C^{(2,1)}_{ab,0}(z_{a},z_{b})\,,

(52)

i.e. they only contain a single logarithm
\ln(Q^{2}/q_{T}^{2})
and a
q_{T}
-independent piece.
(Note that due to the dependence on
z_{a,b}
, it will yield a
Q^{2}
and
Y
dependence.)
We emphasize that in the form given here, all logarithms have been extracted,
and the
q_{T}
distribution is directly expressed in terms of PDFs and their derivatives.

In the following, we will derive a master formula to obtain the NLO NLP kernels
C^{(2,1)}_{ab}

for arbitrary color-singlet processes, as well as the explicit results for Higgs and Drell-Yan production.
The study of higher perturbative orders, and the derivation of a factorization and resummation is left to future work.
However, we do wish to comment on one complication which occurs for
q_{T}
at higher orders, that we have not addressed. Unlike for beam thrust, at NNLO and beyond, one can have power-suppressed contributions at small
q_{T}
from two hard partons in the final state that are nearly back-to-back such that their transverse momenta balance to give a small total
q_{T}
. At NNLO, this is at most a constant power correction, since it is not logarithmically enhanced. but at higher orders it can have a logarithmic contribution. These power corrections are of a different nature than those discussed here, and are not captured as an expansion in the soft and collinear limits about the Born process.

The remainder of this section is organized as follows. In sec.
3.1
, we derive the
master formula for the NLP corrections using the
\eta
regulator, showing in particular that the terms from expanding the regulator contribute. In sec.
3.2
, we rederive this master formula in pure rapidity regularization, which will be simpler due to the fact that one does not have additional terms from the expansion of the regulator, and due to the fact that the soft sector is scaleless.
In sec.
3.3
, we then apply the master formula to derive explicit results for Drell-Yan and gluon-fusion Higgs production. In sec.
3.4
, we discuss our results and compare them with
the known NLP results for beam thrust. Finally in sec.
3.5
, we provide a numerical
validation of our results.

3.1
Master Formula for Power Corrections to Next-to-Leading Power

We consider the production of a color-singlet final state
L
at fixed invariant mass
Q
and rapidity
Y
,
measuring the magnitude of its transverse momentum
q_{T}^{2}=|{\vec{q}}_{T}|^{2}
.
The underlying partonic process is

\displaystyle a(p_{a})+b(p_{b})\to L(p_{1},\cdots)+X(k_{1},\cdots)\,,

(53)

where
a,b
are the incoming partons and
X
denotes additional QCD radiation.
Following the notation of ref.
Ebert:2018lzn
, we express the cross section as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\mathrm{d}\zeta_{a}\mathrm{d}\zeta_{b}\,\frac{f_{a}(\zeta_{a})\,f_{b}(\zeta_{b})}{2\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{2}}\int\!\biggl(\prod_{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}k_{i}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(2\pi)\delta_{+}(k_{i}^{2})\biggr)\int\!\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}q}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,|{\mathcal{M}}(p_{a},p_{b};\{k_{i}\},q)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{(d)}(p_{a}+p_{b}-k-q)\,\delta(Q^{2}-q^{2})\,\delta\biggl(Y-\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{q^{-}}{q^{+}}\biggr)\,\delta\bigl(q_{T}^{2}-|{\vec{k}}_{T}|^{2}\bigr)\,.

(54)

Here, the incoming momenta are given by

\displaystyle p_{a}^{\mu}=\zeta_{a}E_{\mathrm{cm}}\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}\,,\qquad p_{b}^{\mu}=\zeta_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}\frac{{\bar{n}}^{\mu}}{2}\,,

(55)

k=\sum_{i}k_{i}
is the total outgoing hadronic momentum, and
q
is the total leptonic momentum.
In particular,
{\vec{k}}_{T}=\sum_{i}\vec{k}_{i,T}
is the vectorial sum of the transverse momenta of all emissions.
Since the measurements are not affected by the details of the leptonic final state,
the leptonic phase-space integral has been absorbed into the matrix element,

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{M}}(p_{a},p_{b};\{k_{i}\},q)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}\Phi_{L}(q)\,|{\mathcal{M}}(p_{a},p_{b};\{k_{i}\},\{p_{j}\})|^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\Phi_{L}(q)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}p_{j}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(2\pi)\delta_{+}(p_{j}^{2}-m_{j}^{2})\,(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{(d)}\Bigl(q-\sum_{j}p_{j}\Bigr)\,.

(56)

The matrix element
{\mathcal{M}}
also contains the renormalization scale
\mu^{2\epsilon}
,
as usual associated with the renormalized coupling
\alpha_{s}(\mu)
, and may also contain virtual corrections.

There is an important subtlety when measuring the transverse momentum
q_{T}
using dimensional regularization,
as the individual transverse momenta
\vec{k}_{i,T}
are continued to
2-2\epsilon
dimensions.
The measurement function
\delta(q_{T}^{2}-|{\vec{k}}_{T}|^{2})
in eq. (
3.1
) can thus be interpreted
either as measuring the magnitude in
2-2\epsilon
dimensions or the projection onto
2
dimensions.
This scheme dependence cancels in the final result, but can lead to different intermediate results.
At the order we are working, both choices give identical results, so for simplicity of the following manipulations
we specify to measuring the magnitude in
2-2\epsilon
dimension.
For detailed discussions, see e.g. refs.
Jain:2011iu
;
Luebbert:2016itl
.

The
\delta
functions measuring the invariant mass
Q
and rapidity
Y
fix the incoming momenta to be

\displaystyle\zeta_{a}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E_{\mathrm{cm}}}\Bigl(k^{-}+e^{+Y}\sqrt{Q^{2}+k_{T}^{2}}\Bigr)\,,\quad\zeta_{b}(k)=\frac{1}{E_{\mathrm{cm}}}\Bigl(k^{+}+e^{-Y}\sqrt{Q^{2}+k_{T}^{2}}\Bigr)\,.

(57)

Equation (
3.1
) can now be simplified to

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int\!\biggl(\prod_{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}k_{i}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(2\pi)\delta_{+}(k_{i}^{2})\biggr)\frac{f_{a}(\zeta_{a})\,f_{b}(\zeta_{b})}{2\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}A(Q,Y;\{k_{i}\})\,\delta\bigl(q_{T}^{2}-|{\vec{k}}_{T}|^{2}\bigr)\,,

(58)

where we introduced the abbreviation

\displaystyle A(Q,Y;\{k_{i}\})\equiv|{\mathcal{M}}(p_{a},p_{b},\{k_{i}\},q=p_{a}+p_{b}-k)|^{2}\,.

(59)

This emphasizes that the squared matrix element depends only on the Born measurements
Q
and
Y
,
which fix the incoming momenta through eqs. (
55
) and (
57
), and the emission momenta
k_{i}
.
The restriction that
\zeta_{a,b}\in[0,1]
is kept implicit in eq. (
58
) through the support of the proton PDFs.

3.1.1
General Setup at NLO

For reference, we start with the LO cross section following from eq. (
58
),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\mathrm{LO}}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})}{2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}A^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)\,\delta\bigl(q_{T}^{2}\bigr)\,,

(60)

where

\displaystyle x_{a}=\frac{Qe^{Y}}{E_{\mathrm{cm}}}\,,\quad x_{b}=\frac{Qe^{-Y}}{E_{\mathrm{cm}}}\,,

(61)

and
A^{\mathrm{LO}}
is the squared matrix element in the Born kinematics, see eq. (
59
).
For future reference, we also define the LO partonic cross section,
\hat{\sigma}^{\rm LO}(Q,Y)
, by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\mathrm{LO}}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)\,f_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})\,\delta(q_{T}^{2})\,,\qquad\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)=\frac{A^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)}{2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\,.

(62)

At NLO, the virtual correction only contributes at leading power and is proportional to
\delta(q_{T}^{2})
.
At subleading power, it suffices to consider the real correction, given from eq. (
58
) by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,(2\pi)\delta_{+}(k^{2})\,\frac{f_{a}(\zeta_{a})\,f_{b}(\zeta_{b})}{2\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\,A(Q,Y;\{k\})\,\delta\bigl(q_{T}^{2}-|{\vec{k}}_{T}|^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{T}^{-2\epsilon}}{(4\pi)^{2-\epsilon}\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{k^{-}}\frac{f_{a}(\zeta_{a})\,f_{b}(\zeta_{b})}{2\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\,A(Q,Y;\{k\})\bigg|_{k^{+}=q_{T}^{2}/k^{-}}\,.

(63)

In the following, we will mostly keep the symbol
k^{+}
often leaving the use of the relation
k^{+}=k_{T}^{2}/k^{-}=q_{T}^{2}/k^{-}
to the end,
since this makes the symmetry under
k^{+}\leftrightarrow k^{-}
manifest.
The integral in eq. (
3.1.1
) is finite as the physical support of the PDFs,
0\leq\zeta_{a,b}\leq 1
, cuts off the integral in
k^{-}
.
As discussed in sec.
2.1
, these constraints will be expanded for small
q_{T}\ll Q
, after which the integral becomes rapidity divergent.
To regulate the integral, we use the
\eta
regulator where one inserts a factor of
w^{2}|2k^{z}/\nu|^{-\eta}
into the integral,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{T}^{-2\epsilon}}{(4\pi)^{2-\epsilon}\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{k^{-}}w^{2}\nu^{\eta}\biggl|k^{-}-\frac{q_{T}^{2}}{k^{-}}\biggr|^{-\eta}\frac{f_{a}(\zeta_{a})\,f_{b}(\zeta_{b})}{2\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\,A(Q,Y;\{k\})\,.

(64)

We now wish to expand eq. (
64
) in the limit of small
\lambda\sim q_{T}/Q\ll 1
.
Using the knowledge from the EFT, this can be systematically achieved by employing the scaling of eq. (
1
),

\displaystyle n{-}\text{collinear}:\quad k_{n}\sim Q\,(\lambda^{2},1,\lambda)\,,

(65)

\displaystyle{\bar{n}}{-}\text{collinear}:\quad k_{{\bar{n}}}\sim Q\,(1,\lambda^{2},\lambda)\,,

\displaystyle\text{soft}:\hskip 51.21504ptk_{s}\sim Q\,(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda)\,,

for the momentum
k
.
By inserting each of these scalings into eq. (
64
) and expanding the resulting expression
to first order in
\lambda
, one precisely obtains the soft and beam functions as defined
in the
\eta
regulator. This illustrative exercise is shown explicitly in appendix
A
.
Here, we are interested in the first nonvanishing power correction, which occurs at
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})\sim{\mathcal{O}}(q_{T}^{2}/Q^{2})
. We will explicitly show that the
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda)
linear power correction vanishes.
To compute the
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})
result, we will consider the soft and collinear cases separately, deriving master formulas for all scalings
applicable to any color-singlet production.
The power-suppressed operators and Lagrangian insertions required to calculate these directly will be presented in ref.
Chang:2019xxxxx
.

3.1.2
Soft Master Formula for
q_{T}

We first consider the case of a soft emission
k\sim Q(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda)
.
In this limit, the incoming momenta from eq. (
57
) are expanded as

\displaystyle\zeta_{a}(k)

\displaystyle=x_{a}\biggl[1+\frac{k^{-}e^{-Y}}{Q}+\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{2Q^{2}}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{3})\biggr]\equiv x_{a}\biggl[1+\Delta_{a}^{(1)}+\Delta_{a}^{(2)}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{3})\biggr]\,,

\displaystyle\zeta_{b}(k)

\displaystyle=x_{b}\biggl[1+\frac{k^{+}e^{+Y}}{Q}+\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{2Q^{2}}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{3})\biggr]\equiv x_{b}\biggl[1+\Delta_{b}^{(1)}+\Delta_{b}^{(2)}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{3})\biggr]\,,

(66)

where as usual
k^{+}=k_{T}^{2}/k^{-}=q_{T}^{2}/k^{-}
,
x_{a,b}=Qe^{\pm Y}/E_{\mathrm{cm}}
as in eq. (
61
),
and the terms in square brackets correspond to
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{0})
,

{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{1})
, and
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})
, respectively.
It follows that the PDFs and flux factor are expanded as

\displaystyle\Phi\equiv\frac{f_{a}(\zeta_{a})f_{b}(\zeta_{b})}{\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})}{x_{a}x_{b}}+\frac{1}{x_{a}x_{b}}\biggl\{\frac{k^{-}e^{-Y}}{Q}\bigl[x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})-f_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\bigr]+(\rm{sym.})\biggr\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{x_{a}x_{b}}\biggl\{\frac{(k^{-}e^{-Y})^{2}}{Q^{2}}\Bigl[f_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})-x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})+\frac{1}{2}x_{a}^{2}f^{\prime\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{2Q^{2}}\bigl[x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})-x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})\bigr]+(\rm{sym.})\biggr\}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{3})

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{x_{a}x_{b}}\bigl[\Phi^{(0)}+\Phi^{(1)}+\Phi^{(2)}\bigr]+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{3})\,.

(67)

Here,
(\rm{sym.})
denotes simultaneously flipping
a\leftrightarrow b
and letting
k^{-}\to k^{+},Y\to-Y
.
For brevity, we introduced the abbreviation
\Phi^{(n)}
for the
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{n})
pieces.
Note that we expanded to the second order in
\lambda
, as the
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{1})
piece will vanish
and the first nonvanishing correction in fact arises at
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})
.

The expansion of the matrix element is process dependent, and we define the expansion in the soft limit through

\displaystyle A_{s}(Q,Y;\{k\})=A_{s}^{(0)}(Q,Y;\{k\})+A_{s}^{(1)}(Q,Y;\{k\})+A_{s}^{(2)}(Q,Y;\{k\})+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda)\,.

(68)

The LP matrix element scales as
A_{s}^{(0)}\sim\lambda^{-2}
,
such that
\int\mathrm{d}k_{T}^{2}\,A_{s}^{(0)}\sim\lambda^{0}
.
The next two matrix elements are each suppressed by an additional order in
\lambda
relative to the one before.

Plugging the expansions eqs. (
3.1.2
) and (
68
) back into eq. (
64
) and collecting terms in
\lambda
,
the soft limit through
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})
is obtained as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{s}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{T}^{-2\epsilon}}{(4\pi)^{2-\epsilon}\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\frac{1}{2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{k^{-}}w^{2}\nu^{\eta}\biggl|k^{-}-\frac{q_{T}^{2}}{k^{-}}\biggr|^{-\eta}

(69)

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl\{\Phi^{(0)}A_{s}^{(0)}(Q,Y;\{k\})+\Bigl[\Phi^{(0)}A_{s}^{(1)}(Q,Y;\{k\})+\Phi^{(1)}A_{s}^{(0)}(Q,Y;\{k\})\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad+\Bigl[\Phi^{(0)}A_{s}^{(2)}(Q,Y;\{k\})+\Phi^{(1)}A_{s}^{(1)}(Q,Y;\{k\})+\Phi^{(2)}A_{s}^{(0)}(Q,Y;\{k\})\Bigr]\biggr\}\,.

The first term in curly brackets is the leading-power result, the second term the
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda)
contribution, and the last line contains the
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})
contribution.
Since each of these terms has a homogeneous scaling in
\lambda
,
they can only contribute integer powers of
k^{-}
, yielding integrals of
the form
I_{s}^{(\alpha)}(R_{z})
given in eq. (
2.2.1
).

Leading Power [
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{0})
]

The leading soft limit of the squared amplitude
A
is universal and given by

\displaystyle A_{s}^{(0)}(Q,Y;\{k\})

\displaystyle=\frac{16\pi\alpha_{s}\mu_{\rm MS}^{2\epsilon}\mathbf{C}}{k_{T}^{2}}\times A^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)\,,

(70)

where
\mu_{\rm MS}
is the renormalization scale in the MS scheme and
\mathbf{C}=C_{F},C_{A}
is the Casimir constant for the
q\bar{q}
and
gg
channel,
and the limit vanishes for any other channel.
The cross section at LP thus becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(0)}_{s}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=I_{s}^{(0)}(R_{z})\,\frac{q_{T}^{-2\epsilon}}{(4\pi)^{2-\epsilon}\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\frac{\Phi^{(0)}A_{s}^{(0)}(Q,Y;\{k\})}{2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\,.

(71)

In sec.
A.1
, we use this to compute the known bare LP soft function at NLO as a cross check.

{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda)

Here, we show that power corrections at
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda)\sim{\mathcal{O}}(q_{T}/Q)
vanish at NLO.
At this order, we can let
\epsilon\to 0
to obtain the cross section from eq. (
69
) as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{s}^{(1)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{2}x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{k^{-}}w^{2}\nu^{\eta}\biggl|k^{-}-\frac{q_{T}^{2}}{k^{-}}\biggr|^{-\eta}

(72)

\displaystyle\quad\times\Bigl[\Phi^{(0)}A_{s}^{(1)}(Q,Y;\{k\})+\Phi^{(1)}A_{s}^{(0)}(Q,Y;\{k\})\Bigr]\,.

From eq. (
3.1.2
), the expansion of the phase space is given by

\displaystyle\Phi^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{-}e^{-Y}}{Q}\bigl[x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})-f_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\bigr]+(\rm{sym.})\,.

(73)

From eq. (
70
), we know that
A^{(0)}_{s}\sim A^{\mathrm{LO}}/k_{T}^{2}
,
so
\Phi^{(1)}A^{(0)}\sim k^{-},k_{T}^{2}/k^{-}
.
Hence, this contribution to eq. (
72
) is proportional to
I_{s}^{(\pm 1)}(R_{z})=0
, see eq. (
2.2.1
) for odd
\alpha
, and therefore vanishes.
The NLP expansion
A_{s}^{(1)}
of the matrix element is suppressed by
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda)
relative to
A^{\mathrm{LO}}
,
which from power counting can only be given by either
k^{-}
or
k^{+}=k_{T}^{2}/k^{-}
.
Hence, the
\Phi^{(0)}A^{(1)}
term is also proportional to
I_{s}^{(\pm 1)}(R_{z})=0
and vanishes as well.

More generally, power counting combined with the behavior of the integrals in eq. (
2.2.1
) shows that at NLO, the power expansion is in
q_{T}^{2}/Q^{2}
. It would be interesting to extend this proof to higher perturbative orders.
We also remark that the collinear limit will not have a
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda)
expansion at all,
and thus the consistency condition that rapidity divergences cancel between soft and collinear sectors
already implies that the soft NLP result cannot contribute to the leading logarithm.

Next-to-Leading Power [
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})
]

The first nonvanishing power correction thus arises at
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})\sim{\mathcal{O}}(q_{T}^{2}/Q^{2})
.
To derive a general master formula at this order, we decompose the expansion of the matrix element
according to the possible dependence on
k^{\pm}
, which follows from power counting and mass dimension,

\displaystyle A_{s}^{(0)}(Q,Y;\{k\})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k_{T}^{2}}\,\overline{A}^{(0)}(Q,Y)\,,

\displaystyle A_{s}^{(1)}(Q,Y;\{k\})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k_{T}^{2}}\biggl[\frac{k^{+}}{Q}\,\overline{A}^{(1)}_{+}(Q,Y)+\frac{k^{-}}{Q}\,\overline{A}^{(1)}_{-}(Q,Y)\biggr]\,,

\displaystyle A_{s}^{(2)}(Q,Y;\{k\})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k_{T}^{2}}\biggl[\frac{(k^{+})^{2}}{Q^{2}}\,\overline{A}^{(2)}_{++}(Q,Y)+\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{Q^{2}}\,\overline{A}^{(2)}_{00}+\frac{(k^{-})^{2}}{Q^{2}}\,\overline{A}^{(2)}_{--}(Q,Y)\biggr]\,.

(74)

The expansion is defined such that all
\overline{A}^{(i)}
have the same mass dimension.
We now only need to plug eq. (
3.1.2
) back into eq. (
69
), collect the powers of
k^{-}

(using that
k^{+}=k_{T}^{2}/k^{-}
) and apply eq. (
2.2.1
).
Only terms proportional to
I_{s}^{(0)}(R_{z})
will yield a divergence in
\eta
,
and thus constitute the LL correction at NLP, while all other terms contribute at NLL.
We find

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2),\text{LL}}_{s}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{2}x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\frac{1}{Q^{2}}w^{2}\biggl(\frac{2}{\eta}+\ln\frac{\nu^{2}}{q_{T}^{2}}\biggr)\times\biggl\{

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptf_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\biggl[\overline{A}^{(2)}_{00}-e^{-Y}\overline{A}^{(1)}_{+}(Q,Y)-e^{Y}\overline{A}^{(1)}_{-}(Q,Y)\biggr]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})\biggl[e^{-Y}\overline{A}^{(1)}_{+}(Q,Y)-\frac{1}{2}\overline{A}^{(0)}(Q,Y)\biggr]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+f_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\biggl[e^{+Y}\overline{A}^{(1)}_{-}(Q,Y)-\frac{1}{2}\overline{A}^{(0)}(Q,Y)\biggr]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\,\overline{A}^{(0)}(Q,Y)\biggr\}\,,

(75)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2),\text{NLL}}_{s}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{2}x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\frac{1}{Q^{2}}\times\biggl\{

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274ptf_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\biggl[\overline{A}^{(0)}(Q,Y)\Bigl(e^{-2Y}+e^{+2Y}\Bigr)+\overline{A}^{(2)}_{++}(Q,Y)+\overline{A}^{(2)}_{--}(Q,Y)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-e^{-Y}\overline{A}^{(1)}_{-}(Q,Y)-e^{Y}\overline{A}^{(1)}_{+}(Q,Y)\biggr]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})\biggl[e^{-Y}\overline{A}^{(1)}_{-}(Q,Y)-e^{-2Y}\overline{A}^{(0)}(Q,Y)\biggr]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+f_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\biggl[e^{+Y}\overline{A}^{(1)}_{+}(Q,Y)-e^{+2Y}\overline{A}^{(0)}(Q,Y)\biggr]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+x_{a}^{2}f^{\prime\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,f_{b}(x_{b})\,\frac{e^{-2Y}}{2}\overline{A}^{(0)}(Q,Y)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+f_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}^{2}f^{\prime\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\,\frac{e^{+2Y}}{2}\overline{A}^{(0)}(Q,Y)\biggr\}\,.

(76)

An interesting feature of eq. (
3.1.2
) is the appearance of double derivatives of the PDFs,
arising from the expansion of
f[\zeta(k)]
through
{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{2})
.
Most terms in eqs. (
3.1.2
) and (
3.1.2
) also exhibit an explicit
rapidity dependence, which is surprising for the boost-invariant observable
q_{T}
.
In fact, we will see explicitly that the full soft expansion exactly cancels against
rapidity-dependent terms in the collinear expansions, yielding a rapidity-independent final result. This behavior is expected since the rapidity dependence arises from the rapidity-dependent regulator, and therefore we expect that they should cancel in the final regulator independent result.

3.1.3
Collinear Master Formula for
q_{T}

We next consider the case of a

-collinear emission

(80)

\displaystyle A(Q,Y;\{k\})=A_{n}^{(0)}(Q,Y;\{k\})+A_{n}^{(2)}(Q,Y;\{k\})+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})\,.

(81)

{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda)

Next, we switch the integration variable in eq. (

\displaystyle k^{-}=Qe^{Y}\frac{1-z_{a}}{z_{a}}\,,\qquad\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k^{-}}{k^{-}}=\int_{x_{a}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{a}}{z_{a}(1-z_{a})}\,,

(83)

In sec.

,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2),\text{LL}}_{n}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\frac{q_{T}^{2}}{2Q^{2}}\frac{1}{2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\,w^{2}\biggl(\frac{1}{\eta}-\ln\frac{Qe^{Y}}{q_{T}}+\ln(\upsilon)\biggr)

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl\{f_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\Bigl[\overline{A}^{(2)\,\prime}_{n}(1)-2\overline{A}^{(0)\,\prime}_{n}(1)\Bigr]+f_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\Bigl[\overline{A}^{(0)}_{n}(1)+2\overline{A}^{(0)\,\prime}_{n}(1)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad+x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\Bigl[\overline{A}^{(0)}_{n}(1)-\overline{A}^{(2)}_{n}(1)\Bigr]-2x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\overline{A}^{(0)}_{n}(1)\biggr\}\,.

(91)

In the
{\bar{n}}
-collinear limit, one has to replace the regulator factor

\biggl|\frac{k^{+}}{\nu}\biggr|^{-\eta}=\biggl|\frac{Qe^{-Y}}{\nu}\biggr|^{-\eta}\biggl|\frac{1-z_{b}}{z_{b}}\biggr|^{-\eta}~\to~\upsilon^{\eta}\biggl|\frac{k^{-}}{k^{+}}\biggr|^{-\eta/2}=\upsilon^{\eta}\biggl|\frac{k^{+}}{q_{T}}\biggr|^{\eta}=\upsilon^{\eta}\biggl|\frac{Qe^{-Y}}{q_{T}}\biggr|^{\eta}\biggl|\frac{1-z_{b}}{z_{b}}\biggr|^{\eta}

(92)

and drop terms in
\eta/e^{-2Y}
in eq. (
3.1.3
).
The NLP LL result is then obtained from eq. (
3.1.3
) by replacing
\eta\to-\eta,\nu\to q_{T}/\upsilon
and exchanging
a\leftrightarrow b
as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2),\text{LL}}_{\bar{n}}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\frac{q_{T}^{2}}{2Q^{2}}\frac{1}{2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\,w^{2}\biggl(-\frac{1}{\eta}-\ln\frac{Qe^{-Y}}{q_{T}}-\ln(\upsilon)\biggr)

(93)

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl\{f_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\Bigl[\overline{A}^{(2)\,\prime}_{\bar{n}}(1)-2\overline{A}^{(0)\,\prime}_{\bar{n}}(1)\Bigr]+f_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\Bigl[\overline{A}^{(0)}_{\bar{n}}(1)-\overline{A}^{(2)}_{\bar{n}}(1)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad+x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\Bigl[\overline{A}^{(0)}_{\bar{n}}(1)+2{\overline{A}^{(0)\,\prime}_{\bar{n}}}(1)\Bigr]-2x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\overline{A}^{(0)}_{\bar{n}}(1)\biggr\}\,.

Summing eqs. (
3.2
) and (
93
), the poles in
\eta
precisely cancel,
and the dependence on
e^{Y}
and
\upsilon
cancel as well to yield a pure logarithm in
\ln(Q/q_{T})
.
This cancellation has to occur between the two collinear sectors,
as there are no contributions from the soft sector.

The NLP NLL result for the pure rapidity regulator is identical to that in eq. (
3.1.3
) upon dropping all rapidity-dependent pieces,
which we have explicitly verified by repeating the derivation in sec.
3.1.3
using the pure rapidity regulator.
This provides a highly nontrivial check of our regularization procedure, and our understanding of subleading-power rapidity divergences.

3.3
Next-to-leading Power Corrections at NLO

In this section, we give explicit results for the full NLP correction at NLO
for gluon-fusion Higgs and Drell-Yan production in all partonic channels.
Since both are
s
-channel processes, their power corrections
are always proportional to their Born cross sections, and we express the NLP result at
{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha_{s})
as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2,1)}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}=\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q)\,\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\int_{x_{a}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{a}}{z_{a}}\int_{x_{b}}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{b}}{z_{b}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl[f_{i}\biggl(\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}\biggr)f_{j}\biggl(\frac{x_{b}}{z_{b}}\biggr)C_{f_{i}f_{j}}^{(2,1)}(z_{a},z_{b},q_{T})+\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}f^{\prime}_{i}\biggl(\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}\biggr)\frac{x_{b}}{z_{b}}f^{\prime}_{j}\biggl(\frac{x_{b}}{z_{b}}\biggr)C_{f^{\prime}_{i}f_{j}^{\prime}}^{(2,1)}(z_{a},z_{b},q_{T})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}f^{\prime}_{i}\biggl(\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}\biggr)f_{j}\biggl(\frac{x_{b}}{z_{b}}\biggr)C_{f_{i}^{\prime}f_{j}}^{(2,1)}(z_{a},z_{b},q_{T})+f_{i}\biggl(\frac{x_{a}}{z_{a}}\biggr)\frac{x_{b}}{z_{b}}f^{\prime}_{j}\biggl(\frac{x_{b}}{z_{b}}\biggr)C_{f_{i}f_{j}^{\prime}}^{(2,1)}(z_{a},z_{b},q_{T})\biggr]\,.

(94)

Here, we suppress the explicit
Q
and
Y
dependence in the kernels
C^{(2,1)}_{ab}
.

The required
H+j
and
Z+j
amplitudes are conveniently expressed in terms of the Mandelstam variables

\displaystyle s_{ab}

\displaystyle=2p_{a}\cdot p_{b}=Q^{2}+2q_{T}^{2}+\Bigl(k^{+}e^{Y}+k^{-}e^{-Y}\Bigr)\sqrt{Q^{2}+q_{T}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle s_{ak}

\displaystyle=-2p_{a}\cdot k=-q_{T}^{2}-k^{+}e^{+Y}\sqrt{Q^{2}+q_{T}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle s_{bk}

\displaystyle=-2p_{b}\cdot k=-q_{T}^{2}-k^{-}e^{-Y}\sqrt{Q^{2}+q_{T}^{2}}\,,

(95)

which allows us to straightforwardly obtain the LP and NLP expansions in both the soft and collinear limits,
as required by the collinear and soft master formulas.
In the following, we only give the final results after combining soft,

n
-collinear, and
{\bar{n}}
-collinear power corrections. The results were computed
separately using both regulators, which provides a highly nontrivial check of our calculation.

3.3.1

We first consider on-shell Higgs production in gluon fusion in the

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q)=\frac{A^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q,Y)}{2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}=2\pi\delta(Q^{2}-m_{H}^{2})\frac{|{\mathcal{M}}_{gg\to H}^{\mathrm{LO}}(Q)|^{2}}{2Q^{2}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{2}}\,.

(96)

d=4-2\epsilon

Dawson:1990zj
;
Djouadi:1991tka

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{LO}}_{gg\to H}(Q)|^{2}

(97)

n
-collinear, and

\displaystyle C_{f_{g}f_{g}}^{(2,1)}(z_{a},z_{b},q_{T})

\displaystyle=2C_{A}\frac{1}{Q^{2}}\biggl\{\biggl[-\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{q_{T}^{2}}-1\biggr]\delta(1-z_{a})\delta(1-z_{b})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\delta(1-z_{a})\biggl[2+\frac{2}{z_{b}^{2}}+\frac{1}{z_{b}}+z_{b}+3z_{b}^{2}-{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z_{b})\biggr]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\biggl[4+\frac{2}{z_{a}}-2z_{a}+5z_{a}^{2}-3z_{a}^{3}-{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z_{a})\biggr]\delta(1-z_{b})\biggr\}\,,

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\delta(1-z_{a})\biggl[4+\frac{2}{z_{b}}-2z_{b}+5z_{b}^{2}-3z_{b}^{3}-{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z_{b})\biggr]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\biggl[2+\frac{2}{z_{a}^{2}}+\frac{1}{z_{a}}+z_{a}+3z_{a}^{2}-{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z_{a})\biggr]\delta(1-z_{b})\biggr\}\,,

3.3
) yields the NLP cross section for

3.4
Discussion

Since the full calculation of the power corrections is rather involved, and contains a number of moving pieces, here we highlight several interesting features of the calculation, and compare them to
the perturbative power corrections for beam thrust. For the purposes of this discussion, it is convenient to recall the form of the LL power corrections for the Born partonic configurations

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2),\text{LL}}_{gg\to Hg}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}_{gg\to H}(Q)\times\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{A}}{4\pi}\frac{2}{Q^{2}}\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{q_{T}^{2}}\Bigl[8f_{g}(x_{a})f_{g}(x_{b})+f^{gg}_{\text{uni}}(x_{a},x_{b})\Bigr]\,,

(111)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2),\text{LL}}_{q{\bar{q}}\to Vg}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}_{q\bar{q}\to V}(Q)\times\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\frac{2}{Q^{2}}\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{q_{T}^{2}}\Bigl[f^{q\bar{q}}_{\text{uni}}(x_{a},x_{b})\Bigr]\,,

where

\displaystyle f^{gg}_{\text{uni}}(x_{a},x_{b})

\displaystyle=-x_{a}f^{\prime}_{g}(x_{a})f_{g}(x_{b})-f_{g}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{g}(x_{b})+2x_{a}f^{\prime}_{g}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{g}(x_{b})\,,

(112)

\displaystyle f^{q{\bar{q}}}_{\text{uni}}(x_{a},x_{b})

\displaystyle=-x_{a}f^{\prime}_{q}(x_{a})f_{\bar{q}}(x_{b})-f_{q}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{\bar{q}}(x_{b})+2x_{a}f^{\prime}_{q}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{\bar{q}}(x_{b})\,,

(113)

are identical up to switching of the labels on the PDFs. For the channels with a quark emission, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2),\text{LL}}_{gq\to Hq}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}_{gg\to H}(Q)\times\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\frac{2}{Q^{2}}\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{q_{T}^{2}}\Bigl[f_{g}(x_{a})f_{q}(x_{b})+f^{gq}_{\text{uni}}(x_{a},x_{b})\Bigr]\,,

(114)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2),\text{LL}}_{gq\to Vq}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}^{\mathrm{LO}}_{q\bar{q}\to Z}(Q)\times\frac{\alpha_{s}T_{F}}{4\pi}\frac{2}{Q^{2}}\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{q_{T}^{2}}\,\Bigl[f^{qg}_{\text{uni}}(x_{b},x_{a})\Bigr]\,,

(115)

where

\displaystyle f^{gq}_{\text{uni}}(x_{a},x_{b})

\displaystyle=x_{a}f^{\prime}_{g}(x_{a})f_{q}(x_{b})\,,

(116)

\displaystyle f^{qg}_{\text{uni}}(x_{b},x_{a})

\displaystyle=f_{g}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{q}(x_{b})\,,

(117)

are again identical up to the switching of the labels on the PDFs.

First, we note that these results involve a more complicated structure of derivatives than the power corrections to the SCET
I
beam thrust observable, where at most a single derivative appeared in a given term
Moult:2016fqy
;
Boughezal:2016zws
;
Moult:2017jsg
. Furthermore, for beam thrust, at LL there are no derivatives for the channels involving quark emission. Interestingly, the explanation for this arises from very different reasons in the soft and collinear sectors. In the soft sector, it is a simple consequence of the modified power counting of the soft modes, which implies that they must be expanded to two orders in the power counting. In the collinear sector, where the power counting is the same for
q_{T}
and beam thrust, it arises from the presence of the power law singularities, which must be expanded against the PDFs. The cancellation of rapidity divergences between the soft and collinear sectors therefore exhibits a much more nontrivial relationship.

Another feature of the LL power corrections is the independence from explicit factors of the color-singlet rapidity
Y
, suggesting that the expansion parameter is indeed
q_{T}^{2}/Q^{2}
, as is expected from the fact that
q_{T}
is boost invariant. In fact, the rapidity dependence is induced purely by the PDFs and their derivatives. This is particularly interesting for the case of Drell-Yan, where the only terms that contribute arise from derivatives acting on the PDFs, which leads to a more nontrivial rapidity dependence, and in particular, a rapidity dependence that is different from that at leading power. This has potentially interesting implications for power corrections for
q_{T}
subtractions, and we will show this rapidity dependence numerically in sec.
3.5
.

It is also interesting to discuss the universality of these results between Higgs and Drell-Yan production. For the case of beam thrust, the LL results are related by a Casimir scaling,
C_{A}\leftrightarrow C_{F}
. Here we see explicitly that this is not the case for
q_{T}
. However, we see that all terms involving the derivatives of the PDFs are universal up to exchanges of the partonic indices, and it is only the coefficients of the
ff
PDF structure that are non-universal. One way of understanding this difference in universality between beam thrust, which is an SCET
I
observable, and
q_{T}
, which is an SCET
II
observable, is the different power counting of the soft sector. Since soft momenta in SCET
II
scale as
{\cal O}(\lambda)
rather than
{\cal O}(\lambda^{2})
this requires that for
q_{T}
the soft matrix element must be expanded to one higher power, at which point there is a breaking of their universality.
However, the terms involving derivatives of the PDF get part of their power suppression from expanding the momenta entering the PDFs, and therefore are effectively expanded to the same power as for an SCET
I
observable such as beam thrust. It would be interesting to understand this universality structure in more detail, in particular how it extends to other processes, and to higher orders.

4
Conclusions

In this paper, we have studied in detail the structure and consistent regularization of rapidity divergences at subleading order in the power expansion. We have discussed several
new features appearing at subleading power that put additional requirements on the rapidity regulator.
As a result, most of the rapidity regulators that have been used in the literature at leading power
become either unsuitable or inconvenient at subleading power.
In particular, we have shown that the
\eta
regulator, which in principle can be applied at subleading power, is not homogeneous in the power expansion, which leads to undesirable complications at subleading power.
We have introduced a new pure rapidity regulator, which is homogeneous in the power counting. It allows us to regulate rapidity divergences appearing in
q_{T}
distributions at any order in the power expansion, while respecting the power counting of the EFT. This significantly simplified the analysis of rapidity divergences and the associated logarithms at subleading power. It would be interesting to study its application to other physical problems of interest and to further study its properties.

We have also found a rich structure of power-law divergences at subleading power, which can
have a nontrivial effect on the final NLP result. Furthermore, at subleading power, rapidity divergences arise not only from gluons, but also from quarks. It would be interesting to further understand their formal properties.

As an explicit application of our formalism to a physical observable, we considered the
q_{T}
spectrum for color-singlet production, for which we computed the complete NLP corrections, i.e., including both the logarithmic and nonlogarithmic contributions, at fixed
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
.
This provides a highly nontrivial test of our regulator.
In this case, the power-law rapidity divergences have the effect of inducing derivatives of the PDFs in the final NLP result for the
q_{T}
spectrum. We also find that unlike for the case of beam thrust, where the LL power corrections for Higgs and Drell-Yan production are related by
C_{A}\leftrightarrow C_{F}
, this is not the case for the LL power corrections for
q_{T}
, which have a different structure for these two processes.

Our results represent a first important step in systematically studying subleading power corrections for observables with rapidity divergences. It opens the door for addressing a number of interesting questions. It will be important to extend our results and to better understand the structure of subleading-power rapidity divergences at higher perturbative orders. As a particularly interesting application, the power corrections for the
q_{T}
spectrum can be used to improve the numerical performance and to better understand the systematic uncertainties of
q_{T}
subtractions, whose feasibility at next-to-next-to-next-to-leading order has recently been demonstrated in ref.
Cieri:2018oms
for Higgs production. We also hope that recent advances in the renormalization at subleading power, which has enabled the all-orders resummation of subleading-power logarithms, can also be extended to enable the resummation of subleading-power rapidity logarithms, with possible applications in a variety of contexts.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-z)^{1+\eta}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta(1-z)}{\eta}+\biggl[\frac{1}{(1-z)^{1+\eta}}\biggr]_{+}^{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta(1-z)}{\eta}+{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(1-z)-\eta{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(1-z)+{\mathcal{O}}(\eta^{2})\,.

(134)

Here
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}(x)=\bigl[\ln^{n}x/x\bigr]_{+}^{1}
is defined in terms of standard plus distributions, which regulate functions
g(x)
with support
x\geq 0
diverging less than
1/x^{2}
as
x\to 0
.
The defining properties of such plus distributions are

\displaystyle\bigl[g(x)\bigr]_{+}^{1}

\displaystyle=g(x)\,,\qquad x\neq 0\,,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}x\,\bigl[g(x)\bigr]_{+}^{1}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(135)

One can also treat the power-law divergences in eq. (
133
) similar to eq. (
B
) using higher-order plus distributions.
We therefore generalize eq. (
B
) as

\displaystyle\bigl[g(x)\bigr]_{+(a)}^{1}

\displaystyle=g(x)\,,\qquad x\neq 0\,,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}x\,x^{k}\,\bigl[g(x)\bigr]_{+(a)}^{1}

\displaystyle=0\,,\hskip 34.14322pt\forall\,k<a\,,

(136)

where
g(x)
has support
x\geq 0
and diverges less than
1/x^{1+a}
as
x\to 0
.
For
a=1
, this naturally reduces to eq. (
B
).
For
a=2
, one obtains the
++
distributions used e.g. in ref.
Mateu:2012nk
.

The distributions defined in eq. (
B
) can be integrated against any test function
f(x)
that is at least
a{-}1
-times differentiable at
x=0
.
To be specific, consider the example integral

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\mathrm{d}x\,f(x)\bigl[g(x)\bigr]_{+(a)}^{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\mathrm{d}x\,\biggl[f(x)-\sum_{k=0}^{a-1}\frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!}x^{k}\biggr]\bigl[g(x)\bigr]_{+(a)}^{1}+\sum_{k=0}^{a-1}\frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!}\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\mathrm{d}x\,x^{k}\bigl[g(x)\bigr]_{+(a)}^{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\mathrm{d}x\,\biggl[f(x)-\sum_{k=0}^{a-1}\frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!}x^{k}\biggr]g(x)-\sum_{k=0}^{a-1}\frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!}\int_{x_{0}}^{1}\mathrm{d}x\,x^{k}g(x)\,,

(137)

where we assume
x_{0}>0
and
f^{(k)}(0)
is the
k
-th derivative of
f(x)
at
x=0
.
In eq. (
B
), we used that the term in square brackets in the first integral behaves as
{\mathcal{O}}(x^{a})
and thus cancels the divergent behavior of
g(x)
as
x\to 0
, which allows us to drop the plus prescription in the first integral in the last line.
In the second integral, we used eq. (
B
) to change the integration bounds from
[0,x_{0}]
to
[x_{0},1]
. In the latter interval,
g(x)
is regular and the plus prescription can be dropped.

The power-law divergence in eq. (
133
) can be regularized in terms of the higher-order plus distributions in eq. (
B
) as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-z)^{a+\eta}}

\displaystyle=\biggl[\frac{1}{(1-z)^{a+\eta}}\biggr]_{+(a)}^{1}+\sum_{k=0}^{a-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\frac{\delta^{(k)}(1-z)}{1+k-a-\eta}\,,\qquad a\in\mathbb{N}\,.

(138)

This result can be verified by integrating both sides against a test function
(1-z)^{m}
with
m<a
, and treating
\eta
as in dimensional regularization to render all integrals finite.
In eq. (
138
),
\delta^{(k)}(1-z)
is the
k
-th derivative on
\delta(1-z)
, which thus induces a sign
(-1)^{k}
in an integral over
z
and picks out the
k
-th derivative of any test function it acts on.
Note that only the
k=a-1
term in eq. (
138
) diverges for
\eta\to 0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-z)^{a+\eta}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\eta}\frac{(-1)^{a-1}}{(a-1)!}\delta^{(a-1)}(1-z)+\biggl[\frac{1}{(1-z)^{a}}\biggr]_{+(a)}^{1}+\sum_{k=0}^{a-2}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\frac{\delta^{(k)}(1-z)}{1+k-a}+{\mathcal{O}}(\eta)\,,

(139)

so irrespective of the power
a
, any power law divergence
(1-z)^{-a-\eta}
has exactly one single pole.

Appendix C
Derivation of the Master Formula for Generic
c

In secs.
3.1
and
3.2
, we derived master formulas for the NLP correction to the
q_{T}
spectrum using the
\eta
regulator and the pure rapidity regulator, respectively.
In sec.
2.3
, we also introduced a class of homogeneous rapidity regulators spanned by a parameter
c\neq 1
.
Here, we give the master formulas for this regulator for generic
c\neq 1
.
In this regulator, the soft contribution is scaleless and vanishes, similar to the pure rapidity regulator.
Thus, one only needs to consider the
n
-collinear and
{\bar{n}}
-collinear limits.

The derivation of the
n
-collinear expansion proceeds similar to the calculation shown in sec.
3.1.3
.
One can also obtain it from the result for the pure rapidity regulator, eq. (
3.2
), using the replacement

\displaystyle\upsilon^{\eta}\biggl|\frac{k^{-}}{k^{+}}\biggr|^{-\eta/2}=\upsilon^{\eta}q_{T}^{\eta}|k^{-}|^{-\eta}

\displaystyle\quad\to\quad

\displaystyle\upsilon^{(1-c)\eta/2}\biggl|\frac{k^{-}}{\nu}\biggr|^{-\eta/2}\biggl|\frac{k^{+}}{\nu}\biggr|^{-c\eta/2}=\biggl[\upsilon\Bigl(\frac{\nu}{q_{T}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1+c}{1-c}}\biggr]^{(1-c)\eta/2}q_{T}^{(1-c)\eta/2}|k^{-}|^{-(1-c)\eta/2}\,.

(140)

Thus, in eq. (
3.2
) one has to shift
\eta\to(1-c)\eta/2
and
\upsilon\to\upsilon(\nu/q_{T})^{\frac{1+c}{1-c}}
, giving

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2),\text{LL}}_{n}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\frac{q_{T}^{2}}{2Q^{2}}\frac{1}{2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\,w^{2}\biggl(\frac{2}{(1-c)\eta}-\ln\frac{Qe^{Y}}{q_{T}}+\frac{1+c}{1-c}\ln\frac{\nu}{q_{T}}+\ln(\upsilon)\biggr)

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl\{f_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\Bigl[\overline{A}^{(2)\,\prime}_{n}(1)-2\overline{A}^{(0)\,\prime}_{n}(1)\Bigr]+f_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\Bigl[\overline{A}^{(0)}_{n}(1)+2{\overline{A}^{(0)\,\prime}_{n}}(1)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad+x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\Bigl[\overline{A}^{(0)}_{n}(1)-\overline{A}^{(2)}_{n}(1)\Bigr]-2x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\overline{A}^{(0)}_{n}(1)\biggr\}\,.

(141)

This result is well-defined for all
c\neq 1
, whereas one encounters two explicit poles as
c\to 1
.
This behavior is expected because for
c=1
the regulator depends on the boost-invariant product
k^{+}k^{-}=q_{T}^{2}
and therefore does not regulate rapidity divergences, as explained at the end of sec.
2.3
.
For
c=-1
we recover the result of pure rapidity regularization of eq. (
3.2
).
In this case, the
\nu
dependence in the regulator eq. (
35
) cancels,
which is reflected by the vanishing of the coefficient of
\ln(\nu/q_{T})
in eq. (
C
).

In the
{\bar{n}}
-collinear limit, the regulator for arbitrary
c\neq 1
is obtained from the pure rapidity regulator through

\displaystyle\upsilon^{\eta}\biggl|\frac{k^{-}}{k^{+}}\biggr|^{-\eta/2}=\upsilon^{\eta}q_{T}^{-\eta}|k^{+}|^{\eta}

\displaystyle\quad\to\quad

\displaystyle\upsilon^{(1-c)\eta/2}\biggl|\frac{k^{-}}{\nu}\biggr|^{-\eta/2}\biggl|\frac{k^{+}}{\nu}\biggr|^{-c\eta/2}=\biggl[\upsilon\Bigl(\frac{\nu}{q_{T}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1+c}{1-c}}\biggr]^{(1-c)\eta/2}q_{T}^{-(1-c)\eta/2}|k^{+}|^{(1-c)\eta/2}\,.

(142)

Thus, in eq. (
93
) one has to shift
\eta\to(c-1)\eta/2
and
\upsilon\to\upsilon(\nu/q_{T})^{\frac{1+c}{1-c}}
, giving

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{(2),\text{LL}}_{{\bar{n}}}}{\mathrm{d}Q^{2}\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}q_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\frac{q_{T}^{2}}{2Q^{2}}\frac{1}{2x_{a}x_{b}E_{\mathrm{cm}}^{4}}\,w^{2}\biggl(\frac{2}{(c-1)\eta}-\ln\frac{Qe^{-Y}}{q_{T}}-\frac{1+c}{1-c}\ln\frac{\nu}{q_{T}}-\ln(\upsilon)\biggr)

(143)

\displaystyle\quad\times\biggl\{f_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\Bigl[\overline{A}^{(2)\,\prime}_{{\bar{n}}}(1)-2\overline{A}^{(0)\,\prime}_{{\bar{n}}}(1)\Bigr]+f_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\Bigl[\overline{A}^{(0)}_{{\bar{n}}}(1)-\overline{A}^{(2)}_{{\bar{n}}}(1)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad+x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})f_{b}(x_{b})\Bigl[\overline{A}^{(0)}_{{\bar{n}}}(1)+2{\overline{A}^{(0)\,\prime}_{{\bar{n}}}}(1)\Bigr]-2x_{a}f^{\prime}_{a}(x_{a})\,x_{b}f^{\prime}_{b}(x_{b})\overline{A}^{(0)}_{{\bar{n}}}(1)\biggr\}\,.

Summing eqs. (
C
) and (
143
), the poles in
\eta
precisely cancel,
and the dependence on
c
,
\upsilon
and
e^{Y}
cancels as well to yield a pure logarithm in
\ln(Q/q_{T})
.
As for the pure rapidity regulator, this cancellation has to occur between the two collinear sectors,
since the soft sector does not give a contribution.

The NLP NLL result is identical to that in pure rapidity regularization, which is given by eq. (
3.1.3
) upon dropping all regulator-dependent pieces, as explained in sec.
3.2
. This provides another check of our regularization procedure.

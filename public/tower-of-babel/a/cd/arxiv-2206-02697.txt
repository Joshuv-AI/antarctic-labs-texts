Convergence and local-to-global results for p p\mspace{1mu} -superminimizers on quasiopen sets
Anders Björn

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, several convergence results for
fine
p\mspace{1mu}
-(super)minimizers on quasiopen sets
in metric spaces are obtained.
For this purpose, we
deduce a Caccioppoli-type inequality
and local-to-global principles for fine
p\mspace{1mu}
-(super)minimizers
on quasiopen sets.
A substantial part of these considerations is to show that
the functions belong to a suitable local fine Sobolev space.
We prove our results for a complete metric space equipped with a doubling measure
supporting a
p\mspace{1mu}
-Poincaré inequality
with
1<p<\infty
. However,
most of the results are new also for unweighted
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.

Key words and phrases
:
Caccioppoli inequality,
convergence,
doubling measure,
fine
p\mspace{1mu}
-minimizer,
fine
p\mspace{1mu}
-superminimizer,
fine supersolution,
finely open set,
complete metric space,
nonlinear fine potential theory,
Poincaré inequality,
quasiopen set.

(2020):

1
Introduction

Superharmonic functions in classical potential theory are in general
only continuous with respect to the so-called fine topology, rather than the
standard topology.
This is one reason for studying
finely (super)harmonic functions on
finely open sets.
Their theory has been developed
mainly in the linear axiomatic setting,
see e.g. the monographs
by Fuglede
[
10
]
and Lukeš–Malý–Zajíček
[
17
]
.

Finely open sets are
special cases of
quasiopen sets, which
coincide with
the superlevel sets of Sobolev functions.
As such, they carry nontrivial Sobolev test functions and are thus
suitable for partial differential equations, such as the
p\mspace{1mu}
-Laplace
equation
\Delta_{p}u=0
.
In the nonlinear case, i.e. for
p\neq 2
, a study of
fine
p\mspace{1mu}
-(super)solutions to such (and more general)
equations was conducted in 1992 by Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
15
]

on quasiopen sets in unweighted
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
That theory was further extended by Latvala
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
, in
particular for
p=n
.
Quasiopen sets also
appear as solutions to certain shape optimization problems
as e.g. in Buttazzo–Dal Maso
[
9
]

and Fusco–Mukherjee–Zhang
[
12
]
.

In this paper we continue our study
of fine
p\mspace{1mu}
-(super)minimizers on quasiopen sets in metric spaces
initiated in
[
7
]
.
We consider a complete metric space
X
equipped with a doubling measure
\mu

supporting a
p\mspace{1mu}
-Poincaré inequality with
1<p<\infty
.
In this setting,
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic functions are defined
as minimizers of the
p\mspace{1mu}
-energy using upper gradients.
Since there is (in general) no differential equation, we talk about
fine
p\mspace{1mu}
-(super)minimizers, rather than fine

p\mspace{1mu}
-(super)solutions as in
[
15
]
.
On
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, these two notions coincide
[
7
, Proposition 5.3]
.
From now on we drop the parameter
p
and just write fine (super)minimizer.

It turns out that
metric spaces are well suited for studying these notions on finely and
quasiopen sets, since such sets can easily be seen as metric spaces in
their own right, see
[
3
]
–
[
8
]
.
The function space naturally associated with fine superminimizers
on metric spaces is the
Newton–Sobolev space
N^{1,p}
and its local fine version
N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}

defined through compactly contained
p\mspace{1mu}
-strict subsets.
A set
V\subset U
is a
p\mspace{1mu}
-strict subset
of
U
if
there is

\eta\in N^{1,p}(X)
such that
\eta=1
on
V
and
\eta=0

outside
U
.
This is denoted

V\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\subset}}U
, and similarly
V\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U

when also
V\Subset U
.

The following is a special case of our main result.
Even this special case is stronger than the earlier monotone convergence
results on unweighted
\mathbf{R}^{n}
proved in
[
15
, Theorems 4.2 and 4.3]
,
see the introduction to Section
7
.
We are not aware of any other similar convergence results
in the nonlinear case.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
U\subset X
be quasiopen and

\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of fine superminimizers in
U

such that
u_{j}\geq f
a.e. in
U
,
j=1,2,\dots
,
for some
f\in N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
.

Assume that one of the following conditions holds
for
u:=\liminf_{j\to\infty}u_{j}
:

1.

u
is a.e.-bounded
;

2.

u\in N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
;

3.

|u-f_{V}|\leq M_{V}
a.e. in every finely open
V\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\subset}}U

for some
f_{V}\in N^{1,p}(V)
and
M_{V}\geq 0
.

Then
u
is a fine superminimizer in
U
.

For monotone sequences of fine minimizers this reduces to
the following simpler statement.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
U\subset X
be quasiopen and

\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a monotone, or uniformly converging, sequence of fine minimizers in
U
.

Then
u:=\lim_{j\to\infty}u_{j}
is a fine minimizer in
U
,
provided that
one of the conditions
1
–
3
in Theorem
1.1

holds.

One of our motivations for studying such
convergence results is
that they provide fundamental tools for further studies,
such as fine Perron solutions, which we pursue in a
forthcoming paper.
For
p\mspace{1mu}
-Laplace type equations on open sets in weighted
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, such monotone convergence
results were obtained and systematically used in
Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
13
]
.
For developing fine potential theory, convergence results
are even more central since there are less other tools available.

A property usually taken as an axiom in most
axiomatic potential theories is the sheaf property,
which says that if
u
is superharmonic in
U_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha
,
then it is superharmonic in
\bigcup_{\alpha}U_{\alpha}
.
The definitions of
finely superharmonic functions in Fuglede
[
10
, Definition 8.1]

(in the linear theory)
and
in Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
15
, Definition 5.5]

(in the nonlinear theory)
are based on (and yield) the sheaf property.
This property
is usually obvious for
supersolutions of differential equations on open sets as well,
but is not known in connection with minimization problems,
see
[
1
, Open problems 9.22 and 9.23]
.

Our notion of fine superminimizers
is on open sets equivalent to the standard
superminimizers,
by Corollary 5.6 in
[
7
]
.
The same is true for the
fine supersolutions introduced
in
[
15
]
.
In contrast, Fuglede
[
11
, Théorème 3.2]
gave an example
of a finely harmonic function in the sense of
[
10
]

on the entire space
\mathbf{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
,
which has an essential singularity at the origin and is
therefore

not
superharmonic on all of
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
On the other hand, Lukeš–Malý–Zajíček
[
17
, Section 12.A]

use a more restrictive definition of finely superharmonic functions than Fuglede.
Their definition lacks the sheaf property, but
instead has the property that a finely
superharmonic function on an open set is a standard superharmonic function,
similarly to our fine superminimizers.
See also Remark
6.6
.

As a partial substitute for the absent sheaf property, we obtain several
“local-to-global principles” in Section
6
.
The following is a special case of Corollary
6.5
,
which generalizes
[
15
, Theorem 4.2 (a)]
.
The removability of
E
generalizes
[
7
, Lemma 8.1]
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
U\subset X
be quasiopen and
E\subset U
be such that
{C_{p}}(E)=0
.
Assume that
u
is a fine
(
super
)
minimizer in
V

for every finely open
V\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U\setminus E
.

Then
u
is a fine
(
super
)
minimizer in
U
,
provided that
one of the conditions
1
–
3
in Theorem
1.1

holds.

These “local-to-global principles”
play a crucial role when deducing the convergence
results in Section
7
, including Theorem
1.1
.
The obstacle problem, studied in
[
3
]
and
[
7
]
,
is also used as a fundamental tool.
As we shall see, the most difficult
part when showing that a function is a fine superminimizer
is often to show that it belongs to the fine local space
N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
.
A principal tool for achieving this
is the following Caccioppoli-type inequality,
proved in Section
5
.

Theorem 1.4
.

(Caccioppoli-type inequality)

Let
u
be a fine superminimizer in
U
such that
|u-f|\leq M

a.e. in
U
for some
f\in N^{1,p}(U)
and
M\geq 0
.
Then

\int_{U}g_{u}^{p}\eta^{p}\,d\mu\leq 2^{p}\int_{U}\eta^{p}g_{f}^{p}\,d\mu+(4pM)^{p}\int_{U}g_{\eta}^{p}\,d\mu

(1.1)

for every nonnegative
\eta\in N^{1,p}(X)
with
\eta=0
outside
U
.

The compactly contained
p\mspace{1mu}
-strict subsets
V
, appearing in
the definitions of
N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}
and fine superminimizers, as well as in
Theorem
1.1
and
Proposition
1.3
, were introduced by
Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
15
]
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
as a substitute for
compactly contained open subsets, which are usually used to define
the local space
N^{1,p}_{\rm loc}
and supersolutions on open sets.
One of the
main difficulties, when dealing with the fine local space
N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}

and fine superminimizers
is that we do not know
the answer to the following question.

Open problem 1.5
.

Given
A\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U
, is there
W
such that

A\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}W\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U
?

This is easily seen to be true if
U
is open, but not known for
quasiopen
U
, even in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
As a consequence, some of our results and formulations are more cumbersome,
such as
Theorem
1.1
and
Proposition
1.3

(and their generalizations Corollary
6.5
and Theorem
7.4
).
This is also reflected in the formulation
of Theorem 4.2 in
[
15
]
.
Even the existence of
W
such that

A\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\subset}}W\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U
would have important consequences,
cf. condition
4
in Corollary
6.5

and Theorem
7.4
.

The outline of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
we present the main background definitions
from nonlinear potential theory in metric spaces, while in
Section
3
we introduce the fine topology
and the fine local Newtonian space
N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
.
Following our recent paper
[
7
]
,
we introduce fine superminimizers and the fine obstacle problem
in Section
4
.

In Section
5
we deduce the Caccioppoli-type inequality
(Theorem
1.4
),
whereas Section
6

is devoted to characterizations and local-to-global principles
for fine superminimizers.
Finally, in Section
7
we deduce several
convergence results for fine superminimizers
and one for fine minimizers.
Throughout the paper we utilize most of the results
obtained in
[
7
]
.

Notation and preliminaries

In this section, we introduce
the necessary metric space concepts used in this paper.
For brevity, we refer to
Björn–Björn–Latvala
[
4
]
,
[
6
]

for more
extensive introductions, and references to the literature.
See also the monographs Björn–Björn
[
1
]
and
Heinonen–Koskela–Shanmugalingam–Tyson
[
14
]
,
where the theory of upper gradients and Newtonian spaces
is thoroughly developed with proofs.

Let
X
be a metric space equipped
with a metric
d
and a positive complete Borel measure
\mu

such that
\mu(B)<\infty
for all balls
B\subset X
.
We also assume that
1<p<\infty
.

A
curve
is a continuous mapping from an interval,
and a
rectifiable
curve is a curve with finite length.
We will only consider curves which are nonconstant, compact and rectifiable,
and they can therefore be parameterized by their arc length
ds
.
A property holds for
p\mspace{1mu}
-almost every curve

if the curve family
\Gamma
for which it fails has zero
p\mspace{1mu}
-modulus,
i.e. there is
\rho\in L^{p}(X)
such that

\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds=\infty
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

A measurable
function
g:X\to[0,\infty]
is a
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak
upper gradient

of
u:X\to{\overline{\mathbf{R}}}:=[-\infty,\infty]

if for
p\mspace{1mu}
-almost all curves

\gamma:[0,l_{\gamma}]\to X
,

|u(\gamma(0))-u(\gamma(l_{\gamma}))|\leq\int_{\gamma}g\,ds,

where the left-hand side is
\infty

whenever at least one of the
terms therein is infinite.
If
u
has a
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradient in
L^{p}_{\rm loc}(X)
, then
it has a
minimal
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradient

g_{u}\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}(X)

in the sense that

g_{u}\leq g
a.e. for every
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradient
g\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}(X)
of
u
.

For measurable
u
, we let

\|u\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}=\biggl(\int_{X}|u|^{p}\,d\mu+\inf_{g}\int_{X}g^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p},

where the infimum is taken over all
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradients of
u
.
The
Newtonian space
on
X
is

N^{1,p}(X)=\{u:\|u\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}<\infty\}.

The space
N^{1,p}(X)/{\sim}
, where
u\sim v
if and only if
\|u-v\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}=0
,
is a Banach space and a lattice.
In this paper it is convenient to assume that functions in
N^{1,p}(X)

are defined everywhere (with values in
{\overline{\mathbf{R}}}
),
not just up to an equivalence class in the corresponding function space.

For an arbitrary set
A\subset X
, we let

N^{1,p}_{0}(A)=\{u|_{A}:u\in N^{1,p}(X)\text{ and }u=0\text{ on }X\setminus A\}.

Functions from
N^{1,p}_{0}(A)
can be extended by zero in
X\setminus A
and we
will regard them in that sense when needed.

The
Sobolev capacity
of an arbitrary set
A\subset X
is

{C_{p}^{X}}(A)=\inf_{u}\|u\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}^{p},

where the infimum is taken over all
u\in N^{1,p}(X)
such that

u\geq 1
on
A
.

A property holds
quasieverywhere
(q.e.) if the set of points for which it fails has capacity zero.
The capacity is the correct gauge
for distinguishing between two Newtonian functions.
If
u\in N^{1,p}(X)
, then
v\sim u
if and only if
v=u
q.e.
Moreover, if
u,v\in N^{1,p}(X)
and
v=u
a.e., then
v=u
q.e.
The superscript in
{C_{p}^{X}}
indicates dependence on the underlying space.
We will usually omit
X
and write
{C_{p}}
instead of
{C_{p}^{X}}
.

A set
U\subset X
is
quasiopen
if for every

\varepsilon>0
there is an open set
G\subset X
such that
{C_{p}}(G)<\varepsilon

and
G\cup U
is open. The quasiopen sets do not in general form a topology,
see Remark 9.1 in
[
3
]
.
However it follows easily from the countable subadditivity of
{C_{p}}

that countable unions and finite intersections of quasiopen sets are
quasiopen.
Moreover, quasiopen sets are measurable.
Various characterizations of quasiopen sets can be found in
Björn–Björn–Malý
[
8
]
.
If
U\subset X
is
quasiopen then
{C_{p}^{U}}
and
{C_{p}^{X}}
have the same zero sets in
U
,
by Proposition 4.2 in
[
8
]

(and Remark 3.5 in Shanmugalingam
[
20
]
).

For a measurable set
E\subset X
, the Newtonian space
N^{1,p}(E)

and the capacity
{C_{p}^{E}}
are
defined by
considering
(E,d|_{E},\mu|_{E})
as a metric space in its own right.

If
E\subset A
are subsets of
X
, then
the
variational capacity
of
E
with respect to
A
is

\capp_{p}(E,A)=\inf_{u}\int_{X}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu,

where the infimum is taken over all
u\in N^{1,p}_{0}(A)

such that
u\geq 1
on
E
.
If no such function
u
exists then
\capp_{p}(E,A)=\infty
.

The measure
\mu
is
doubling
if
there is
C>0
such that for all balls

B=B(x_{0},r):=\{x\in X:d(x,x_{0})<r\}
in
X
,
we have

0<\mu(2B)\leq C\mu(B)<\infty
,
where
\lambda B=B(x_{0},\lambda r)
.
In this paper, all balls are open.

The space
X
supports a
p\mspace{1mu}
-Poincaré inequality
if
there are
C>0
and
\lambda\geq 1

such that for all balls
B\subset X
,
all integrable functions
u
on
X
and all
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradients
g
of
u
,

\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|u-u_{B}|\,d\mu\leq C\diam(B)\biggl(\frac{1}{\mu(\lambda B)}\int_{\lambda B}g^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p},

(2.1)

where
u_{B}:=\int_{B}u\,d\mu/\mu(B)
and we implicitely assume that

0<\mu(B)<\infty
for all balls
B
.

In
\mathbf{R}^{n}
equipped with a doubling measure
d\mu=w\,dx
, where

dx
denotes Lebesgue measure, the
p\mspace{1mu}
-Poincaré inequality (
2.1
)
is equivalent to the
p\mspace{1mu}
-admissibility
of the weight
w
in the
sense of Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
13
]
, see
Corollary 20.9 in
[
13
]

and Proposition A.17 in
[
1
]
.
Moreover, in this case
g_{u}=|\nabla u|
a.e. if
u\in N^{1,p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})

and the capacities
{C_{p}}
and
\capp_{p}
coincide
with the corresponding capacities in
[
13
]

(see
[
1
, Theorem 6.7]
and
[
2
, Theorem 5.1]
).

As usual, we set

u_{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}}=\max\{u,0\}
.

3
Fine topology,
N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
and

p\mspace{1mu}
-strict subsets

Throughout the rest of the paper, we assume
that
X=(X,d)
is a complete metric space
equipped with a doubling measure
\mu

supporting a
p\mspace{1mu}
-Poincaré inequality
with
1<p<\infty
.
Unless said otherwise,
U
will be a nonempty
quasiopen set.

To avoid pathological situations we also assume that
X

contains at least two points (and thus must be uncountable due to the
Poincaré inequality).
In this section we recall the basic facts about the fine topology
and Newtonian functions on quasiopen sets.

Definition 3.1
.

A set
E\subset X
is
thin
at
x\in X
if

\int_{0}^{1}\biggl(\frac{\capp_{p}(E\cap B(x,r),B(x,2r))}{\capp_{p}(B(x,r),B(x,2r))}\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{dr}{r}<\infty.

A set
V\subset X
is
finely open
if

X\setminus V
is thin at each point
x\in V
.

In the definition of thinness,
we use the convention that the integrand
is 1 whenever
\capp_{p}(B(x,r),B(x,2r))=0
.
It is easy to see that the finely open sets give rise to a
topology, which is called the
fine topology
.
Every open set is finely open, but the converse is not true in general.
A function
u:V\to{\overline{\mathbf{R}}}
, defined on a finely open set
V
, is

finely continuous
if it is continuous when
V
is equipped with the
fine topology and
{\overline{\mathbf{R}}}
with the usual topology.
Pointwise fine continuity is defined analogously.
The fine interior, fine boundary and fine closure of
E

are denoted
\fineint E
,
\partial_{p}E
and
{{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\displaystyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\textstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.0pt\overline{\phantom{\scriptscriptstyle E}\kern 0.0pt}$\hss}{E}}}\mspace{1mu}}^{p}
, respectively.
See
[
1
, Section 11.6]
and
Björn–Björn–Latvala
[
4
]

for further discussion on thinness and the fine topology
on metric spaces.

The following result shows that there is a close
connection between quasiopen and finely open sets.

Theorem 3.2
.

(Theorem 3.4 in
[
7
]
)

The following conditions are equivalent for any set
U\subset X
:

1.

U
is quasiopen
;

2.

U=V\cup E
for some finely open
V
and a set
E

with
{C_{p}}(E)=0
;

3.

{C_{p}}(U\setminus\fineint U)=0
;

4.

U=\{x:u(x)>0\}
for some
u\in N^{1,p}(X)
.

To define fine superminimizers, we first need
appropriate fine local Sobolev spaces.
Here
p\mspace{1mu}
-strict subsets will play a key role, as a substitute for relatively compact subsets.
Recall that
V\Subset U
if
\overline{V}
is a compact subset of
U
.

Definition 3.3
.

A set
A\subset U
is a
p\mspace{1mu}
-strict subset
of
U
if
there is a function
\eta\in N^{1,p}_{0}(U)
such that
\eta=1
on
A
.
We will write
A\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\subset}}U
, and similarly
A\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U

when also
A\Subset U
.

A function
u
belongs to
N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
if
u\in N^{1,p}(V)

for all finely open
V\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U
.

In the definition of
p\mspace{1mu}
-strict subsets, it can be equivalently
required that in addition
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
as in Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
15
]
; just
replace
\eta
with
\min\{\eta,1\}_{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}}
.
Note that
A\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\subset}}U
if and only if
\capp_{p}(A,U)<\infty
.
Lemma 3.3 in Björn–Björn–Latvala
[

Remark 3.4

Lemma 3.7
.

4
Fine superminimizers and the obstacle problem

The following definition
is from
[
7
, Definition 5.1]
.
On unweighted
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
15
, Section 3.1]

gave an equivalent definition (of fine supersolutions) already in
1992, cf. Proposition 5.3 in
[
7
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

A function
u\in N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
is a

fine minimizer
(
resp. fine
superminimizer
)
in
U
if

\int_{V}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu\leq\int_{V}g_{u+\varphi}^{p}\,d\mu

for every finely open
V\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U

and for every (resp. every nonnegative)

\varphi\in N^{1,p}_{0}(V)
.
Moreover,
u
is a
fine subminimizer

if
-u
is a fine superminimizer.

A
finely
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic function
is a finely continuous
fine minimizer.

Note that, unlike for minimizers and
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic functions on
open sets,

it is not known
whether every fine minimizer
can be modified on a set of zero capacity so that it becomes
finely continuous and thus finely
p\mspace{1mu}
-harmonic,
see the discussion in Section 9 in
[
7
]
.
Of course, being a function from
N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
,
every fine (super/sub)minimizer
is finely continuous at q.e. point.

By Lemma 5.4 in
[
7
]
,
a function is a
fine minimizer if and only
if it is both a fine subminimizer and a fine superminimizer.
We refer to

[
7
]
for
further discussion on fine superminimizers
and the Newtonian space
N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
.
The following lattice property will be used
several times.

Lemma 4.2
.

(Corollary 5.8 in
[
7
]
)

If
u
and
v
are fine superminimizers in
U
,
then
\min\{u,v\}
is also a fine superminimizer in
U
.

The obstacle problem will be a fundamental tool when
studying fine (super)minimizers.
See
[
3
]
and
[
7
]

for earlier studies of the obstacle problem on nonopen sets
in metric spaces.
It was shown therein that such theories are
not natural beyond
finely open or quasiopen sets.
In particular, it was shown in Björn–Björn
[
3
, Theorem 7.3]

that
N^{1,p}_{0}(E)=N^{1,p}_{0}(\fineint E)
for an arbitrary set
E
.

Definition 4.3
.

Assume that
U
is a bounded nonempty
quasiopen set with
{C_{p}}(X\setminus U)>0
.
Let
f\in N^{1,p}(U)
and
\psi:U\to{\overline{\mathbf{R}}}
.
Then we define

{\cal K}_{\psi,f}(U)=\{v\in N^{1,p}(U):v-f\in N^{1,p}_{0}(U)\text{ and }v\geq\psi\ \text{q.e. in }U\}.

A function
u\in{\cal K}_{\psi,f}(U)

is a
solution of the
{\cal K}_{\psi,f}(U)
-obstacle problem

if

\int_{U}g^{p}_{u}\,d\mu\leq\int_{U}g^{p}_{v}\,d\mu\quad\text{for all }v\in{\cal K}_{\psi,f}(U).

Note that the boundary data
f
are only required to belong to
N^{1,p}(U)
,
i.e.
f
need not be defined on
\partial U

or the fine boundary
\partial_{p}U
.

If
{\cal K}_{\psi,f}(U)\neq\varnothing
,
then there is
a solution
u
of the
{\cal K}_{\psi,f}(U)
-obstacle problem,
which is unique q.e.,
see Theorem 4.2 in
[
3
]
.
Moreover,

u
is a fine superminimizer,
by Theorem 6.2
in
[
7
]
.
A comparison principle for
obstacle problems was obtained in
Corollary 4.3 in
[
3
]
.

5
The Caccioppoli-type inequality

Caccioppoli inequalities are important tools in
PDEs and nonlinear potential theory.
In this section, we are going to deduce the
Caccioppoli-type inequality in Theorem
1.4
.
In Section
7
, we will use it
to deduce
the fundamental convergence theorems for increasing
and decreasing sequences of fine superminimizers.

Assume first that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
and that
W:=\{x:\eta(x)>0\}\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U
.
Then
W
is quasiopen by Theorem
3.2
,
and
u\in N^{1,p}(W)
by Remark
3.4
.
It follows from Lemma
3.5
that
we can assume that
|u-f|\leq M
everywhere.
It thus follows
from the Leibniz and chain rules
[
1
, Theorem 2.15 and 2.16]

that
v:=u+\eta^{p}(f+M-u)\in N^{1,p}(W)
.
Lemma 2.4 in Kinnunen–Martio
[
16
]

(or
[
1
, Lemma 2.18]
)
implies that

g_{v}\leq(1-\eta^{p})g_{u}+\eta^{p}g_{f}+p(f+M-u)\eta^{p-1}g_{\eta}.

(In
[
16
]
and
[
1
]
it is assumed that
\eta\in\Lip(X)
,
but since
u-f
is bounded
it is enough to require that
\eta\in N^{1,p}(X)
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, as here.
The proof is the same, just restrict attention to curves
\gamma
on
which also
\eta
is absolutely continuous.)
As
u
is a fine superminimizer in
U
and
0\leq v-u\in N^{1,p}_{0}(W)
,
we obtain using the convexity of the function
t\mapsto t^{p}
that

\displaystyle\int_{W}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{W}g_{v}^{p}\,d\mu\leq\int_{W}\biggl((1-\eta^{p})g_{u}+\eta^{p}\Bigl(g_{f}+p|f+M-u|\frac{g_{\eta}}{\eta}\Bigr)\biggr)^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{W}(1-\eta^{p})g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu+2^{p}\int_{W}\eta^{p}\biggl(g_{f}^{p}+(2pM)^{p}\frac{g^{p}_{\eta}}{\eta^{p}}\biggr)\,d\mu.

Since
g_{u}\in L^{p}(W)
, subtracting the first integral on the right-hand
side from both sides of the inequality yields (
1.1
)
and proves the statement when
0\leq\eta\leq 1
and
\{x:\eta(x)>0\}\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U
.

For general
0\leq\eta\in N^{1,p}_{0}(U)
, Proposition
3.8

provides us with finely open
V_{j}\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U

and an increasing sequence of nonnegative bounded functions

\eta_{j}\in N^{1,p}_{0}(V_{j})
such that

\|\eta-\eta_{j}\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}\to 0

and
\eta_{j}\nearrow\eta
a.e., as
j\to\infty
.
Applying (
1.1
) to
V_{j}
and suitable multiples of
\eta_{j}
,
we get

\int_{U}g_{u}^{p}\eta^{p}\,d\mu=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{V_{j}}g_{u}^{p}\eta_{j}^{p}\,d\mu\leq 2^{p}\int_{U}\eta^{p}g_{f}^{p}\,d\mu+(4pM)^{p}\int_{U}g_{\eta}^{p}\,d\mu

by using monotone convergence.
∎

In the next sections we will several times need to
deduce that certain functions belong to
N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
.
In order to do so, the following result will be convenient.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
E
be a set with
{C_{p}}(E)=0
and
assume that
u=\lim_{j\to\infty}u_{j}
q.e. in
U
, where each

u_{j}
,
j=1,2,\dots
, is a fine superminimizer
in
V
for
every finely open
V\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U\setminus E
.
Let
W\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\subset}}U
be a bounded measurable set.
Assume that

|u_{j}-f|\leq M

a.e. in
U
,
j=1,2,\dots
, for some
f\in N^{1,p}(U)
and
M\geq 0
.
Then
u\in N^{1,p}(W)
and

\int_{W}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{W}g_{u_{j}}^{p}\,d\mu\leq 2^{p}\int_{U}g_{f}^{p}\,d\mu+(4pM)^{p}\int_{U}g_{\eta}^{p}\,d\mu,

(5.1)

where
\eta\in N^{1,p}_{0}(U)
is any function such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
in

U
and
\eta=1
in
W
.

A natural question is of course whether

u
is a fine superminimizer
in
U
, but we postpone that to later.
As we shall see, this proposition is useful also for the constant
sequence
u_{j}=u
,
j=1,2,\dots
, as it gives the
Caccioppoli-type inequality (
1.1
) under weaker assumptions
than Theorem
1.4
.

Let
\eta\in N^{1,p}_{0}(U)
be such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
in
U

and
\eta=1
in
W
.
Since
{C_{p}}(E)=0
, we have
\eta\in N^{1,p}_{0}(U\setminus E)
.
By Proposition
3.8
,
there are
finely open sets
V_{k}\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U\setminus E
and
nonnegative bounded functions
\eta_{k}\in N^{1,p}_{0}(V_{k})
such that

\|\eta-\eta_{k}\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}\to 0

and
\eta_{k}\nearrow\eta
q.e. as
k\to\infty
.

Since each
u_{j}
is a fine superminimizer
in every
V_{k}
, we get using
the Caccioppoli-type inequality (Theorem
1.4
)
and monotone convergence that

\displaystyle\int_{W}g_{u_{j}}^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{U}g_{u_{j}}^{p}\eta^{p}\,d\mu=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{V_{k}}g_{u_{j}}^{p}\eta_{k}^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{V_{k}}(2^{p}\eta^{p}_{k}g_{f}^{p}+(4pM)^{p}g_{\eta_{k}}^{p})\,d\mu\leq\int_{U}(2^{p}g_{f}^{p}+(4pM)^{p}g_{\eta}^{p})\,d\mu<\infty,

i.e.
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence in
N^{1,p}(W)

(since it is bounded in
L^{p}(W)
by assumption).
Hence, by Corollary 6.3 in
[
1
]
,
u\in N^{1,p}(W)
and
(
5.1
) holds.
∎

6
Characterizations of fine superminimizers

In this section we will deduce several characterizations of fine
superminimizers, which will
be used later on.
The first
characterization, based on Lemma
4.2
,
will be used in the proofs of
Proposition
6.2

and Corollary
7.3
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
v
be a fine superminimizer in
U
and
u\in N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
.
Then
u
is a fine superminimizer in
U
if and only if

\min\{u,v+k\}
is a fine superminimizer in
U

for every
k=1,2,\dots
.

Assume first that
u_{k}:=\min\{u,v+k\}
is a fine superminimizer
for every
k=1,2,\dots
.
Let
V\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U
be finely open
and
\varphi\in N^{1,p}_{0}(V)
be nonnegative.
As
u,v\in N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
, they are finite q.e. and
u_{k}\nearrow u

q.e. in
V
, as
k\to\infty
.
Let
E_{k}:=\{x\in V:u(x)>v(x)+k\}
.
Since
V
is bounded, we have
\mu(E_{k})\to 0
.
Using monotone and dominated convergence (since

v+\varphi\in N^{1,p}(V)
), we then get

\int_{V}g_{u_{k}+\varphi}^{p}\,d\mu=\int_{V\setminus E_{k}}g_{u+\varphi}^{p}\,d\mu+\int_{E_{k}}g_{v+\varphi}^{p}\,d\mu\to\int_{V}g_{u+\varphi}^{p}\,d\mu,\quad\text{as $k\to\infty$.}

Applying this also with
\varphi\equiv 0

and using that
u_{k}
is a fine superminimizer yields

\int_{V}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{V}g_{u_{k}}^{p}\,d\mu\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{V}g_{u_{k}+\varphi}^{p}\,d\mu=\int_{V}g_{u+\varphi}^{p}\,d\mu.

As
V
and
\varphi

were arbitrary this shows that
u
is
a fine superminimizer.
The converse implication follows directly from
Lemma
4.2
.
∎

The following
result
will be used to prove
Theorem
1.1

and its more general version Theorem
7.4
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Assume that
v
is a fine superminimizer in
U
and let

u:U\to{\overline{\mathbf{R}}}
be such that
|u-f|\leq M
a.e. in
U
for some

f\in N^{1,p}(U)
and
M\geq 0
.

If
\min\{u,v+k\}
is a fine superminimizer in
U

for every
k=1,2,\dots
, then
u\in N^{1,p}(W)
for every
bounded quasiopen
W\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\subset}}U
.
Moreover,

u
is a fine superminimizer in
U
.

Let
W\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\subset}}U
be a bounded quasiopen set
and
\eta\in N^{1,p}_{0}(U)
be such that

0\leq\eta\leq 1
and
\eta=1
in
W
.
By Proposition
3.8
,
there are finely open sets

V^{\prime}_{1}\subset V^{\prime}_{2}\subset\dots\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U

and functions
0\leq\eta^{\prime}_{k}\in N^{1,p}_{0}(V^{\prime}_{k})
such that

\|\eta-\eta^{\prime}_{k}\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}\to 0

and
\eta^{\prime}_{k}\nearrow\eta
q.e. as
k\to\infty
.

For each
k=1,2,\dots
, let

\displaystyle M_{k}

\displaystyle>\|g_{v-f}\|_{L^{p}(V^{\prime}_{k})},

\displaystyle V_{k}

\displaystyle=\{x\in W:f(x)<v(x)+k\},

\displaystyle W^{\prime}_{k}

\displaystyle=\{x\in V^{\prime}_{k}:f(x)<v(x)+k+M_{k}\}.

Note that both
V_{k}
and
W^{\prime}_{k}

are quasiopen, by
Theorem
3.2
.
Since
f,v\in N^{1,p}(V^{\prime}_{k})
and
\eta^{\prime}_{k}\in N^{1,p}_{0}(V^{\prime}_{k})
, we see that

0\leq\eta_{k}:=\min\biggl\{\frac{(v+k+M_{k}-f)_{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}}}{M_{k}},2\eta^{\prime}_{k},1\biggr\}\in N^{1,p}_{0}(V^{\prime}_{k}),

by Lemma 2.37 in
[
1
]
.
The
p\mspace{1mu}
-weak upper gradient of
\eta_{k}
is estimated by

\displaystyle\int_{V^{\prime}_{k}}g_{\eta_{k}}^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{V^{\prime}_{k}}\max\Bigl\{\frac{g_{v-f}}{M_{k}},2g_{\eta^{\prime}_{k}}\Bigr\}^{p}\,d\mu

(6.1)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{V^{\prime}_{k}}\biggl(\frac{g_{v-f}^{p}}{M_{k}^{p}}+2^{p}g_{\eta^{\prime}_{k}}^{p}\biggr)\,d\mu<1+2^{p}\int_{U}g_{\eta^{\prime}_{k}}^{p}\,d\mu\to 1+2^{p}\int_{U}g_{\eta}^{p}\,d\mu,

as
k\to\infty
.
Moreover,
\eta_{k}=0
in
V^{\prime}_{k}\setminus W^{\prime}_{k}
and hence

\eta_{k}\in N^{1,p}_{0}(W^{\prime}_{k})
.
On the other hand,
\eta_{k}=1
on
W_{k}:=\{x\in V_{k}:\eta^{\prime}_{k}(x)>\tfrac{1}{2}\}
,
and so

W_{k}\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\subset}}W^{\prime}_{k}\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U.

In
W^{\prime}_{k}\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U
we have by Lemma
3.5
that

u=\min\{u,v+k+M_{k}+M\}\quad\text{q.e.},

and so
u
is a fine superminimizer in
W^{\prime}_{k}

(since we may assume that
M_{k}
and
M
are integers).
The Caccioppoli inequality (Theorem
1.4
with
U

replaced by
W^{\prime}_{k}
) now implies that
u\in N^{1,p}(W_{k})
and

\displaystyle\int_{W_{k}}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}\int_{W^{\prime}_{k}}\eta_{k}^{p}g_{f}^{p}\,d\mu+(4pM)^{p}\int_{W^{\prime}_{k}}g_{\eta_{k}}^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}\int_{U}g_{f}^{p}\,d\mu+(4pM)^{p}\int_{V^{\prime}_{k}}g_{\eta_{k}}^{p}\,d\mu.

(6.2)

Now, for q.e.
x\in W
, there exists
k
such that
\eta^{\prime}_{k}(x)>\tfrac{1}{2}
and

f(x)<v(x)+k
and hence
x\in W_{k}
.
It follows that
W=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}W_{k}\cup Z
, where
{C_{p}}(Z)=0
.

Since
W_{1}\subset W_{2}\subset\dots
, inserting
(
6.1
) into (
6
) together with
monotone convergence implies that
\int_{W}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu<\infty
.
As
|u-f|\leq M
a.e. in
W
, we conclude that
u\in N^{1,p}(W)
.

Applying this to an arbitrary finely open
W\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U

shows that
u\in N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
,
and the superminimizing property follows from
Proposition
6.1
.
∎

The following lemma relates fine superminimizers to obstacle problems.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
u
be a fine superminimizer in
U
,
and let
W\subset U
be a bounded quasiopen set
such that
u\in N^{1,p}(W)
and
{C_{p}}(X\setminus W)>0
.
Then
u
is a solution of the
{\cal K}_{u,u}(W)
-obstacle problem.

Let
v\in{\cal K}_{u,u}(W)
.
Then
\varphi:=v-u\geq 0
q.e. in
W
and
\varphi\in N^{1,p}_{0}(W)
.
By Lemma 5.5 in
[
7
]
,

\int_{W}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu=\int_{\{\varphi=0\}}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu+\int_{\{\varphi>0\}}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu\leq\int_{\{\varphi=0\}}g_{v}^{p}\,d\mu+\int_{\{\varphi>0\}}g_{v}^{p}\,d\mu=\int_{W}g_{v}^{p}\,d\mu.

Thus
u
is a solution of the
{\cal K}_{u,u}(W)
-obstacle problem.
∎

We next turn to “local-to-global principles”
for fine superminimizers, which will be crucial later on.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
u\in N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
and
E
be a set with
{C_{p}}(E)=0
.
Then the following are equivalent
:

1.

u
is a fine superminimizer in
U
;

2.

u
is a fine superminimizer in
V

for every finely open

(
or equivalently quasiopen
)

V\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U\setminus E

such that
{C_{p}}(X\setminus V)>0
;

3.

u
is a solution of the
{\cal K}_{u,u}(V)
-obstacle problem
for every finely open

(
or equivalently quasiopen
)

V\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U\setminus E
such that
{C_{p}}(X\setminus V)>0
.

Note that the condition
{C_{p}}(X\setminus V)>0

is automatically satisfied for arbitrary
V\Subset U\setminus E

if either
X
is unbounded or
U\setminus E\neq X
,
as in these cases
\overline{V}
is a compact subset of
X

whose complement is a nonempty open set.

The implication
1
\Rightarrow
2
is trivial,
while
2
\Rightarrow
3
follows from Lemma
6.3
.
It remains to show
3
\Rightarrow
1
.

To this end, consider first the case when
X
is unbounded or

U\setminus E\neq X
.
Let
V\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U
be finely open and
\varphi\in N^{1,p}_{0}(V)
be nonnegative.
Since
{C_{p}}(E)=0
, we have
\varphi\in N^{1,p}_{0}(V\setminus E)
.
Proposition
3.8

provides us with finely open
V_{j}\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}V\setminus E

and an increasing sequence of bounded functions

0\leq\varphi_{j}\in N^{1,p}_{0}(V_{j})
such that
\varphi_{j}\nearrow\varphi
q.e. and

\|\varphi-\varphi_{j}\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
.
Then
X\setminus\overline{V}_{j}

is open and nonempty and hence
{C_{p}}(X\setminus V_{j})>0
.
Thus, by assumption,
u
is a solution of the
{\cal K}_{u,u}(V_{j})
-obstacle problem.
Since
u+\varphi_{j}\in{\cal K}_{u,u}(V_{j})
,
we get for each
j
that

\biggl(\int_{V_{j}}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}\leq\biggl(\int_{V_{j}}g_{u+\varphi_{j}}^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}\leq\biggl(\int_{V}g_{u+\varphi}^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}+\|\varphi-\varphi_{j}\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}.

Letting
j\to\infty
and using monotone convergence shows that

\int_{V}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu\leq\int_{V}g_{u+\varphi}^{p}\,d\mu.

As
V
and
\varphi
were arbitrary, it
follows that
u
is a fine superminimizer in
U
.

Finally, assume that
U\setminus E=X
is bounded.
By assumption,
u
is a solution of the
{\cal K}_{u,u}(\Omega)
-obstacle problem
for every open
\Omega\Subset X
with
{C_{p}}(X\setminus\Omega)>0
.
Hence, it follows from Proposition 9.25 in
[
1
]
that

u
is a superminimizer in
X
, and thus
a fine superminimizer in
U=X
, by Corollary 5.6 in
[
7
]
.
∎

,
as well as the following more general version of it.

Remark 6.6
.

Combining
Theorem 4.2 (a) in Kilpeläinen–Malý
[

]
with

shows that
a function on a quasiopen set in

\mathbf{R}^{n}

or is bounded,
is a fine supersolution if and only if it is a quasilocal fine supersolution
in the sense of
[

, Definition 4.1]
.
We do not know if the corresponding equivalence holds in metric spaces.
If it does then it would
answer Open problem 9.23 in
[

]
in the affirmative
and at least partially yield
the sheaf property,
cf. the discussion
after Theorem

.

The definition of quasilocal fine supersolutions
is also based on and yields the sheaf property.
Moreover, Example 5.2 in
[

]
demonstrates that, in contrast to fine supersolutions,

can fail to be supersolutions in the usual sense.
See
the discussions after Theorem

above
and before Example 5.2 in
[

]
,
and also Lemma 5.7 in Latvala
[

]
.

7
Sequences of fine superminimizers

In this section we are going to deduce convergence results for
fine superminimizers.
Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
15
, Theorem 4.3]
deduced
monotone convergence results for
quasilocal fine supersolutions on unweighted
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
When combined with Theorem 4.2 (a) (with
V=U
) in
[
15
]

their results
yield Proposition
7.1

and Corollary
7.3
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}

under the additional assumptions that

U
is bounded and
u\in N^{1,p}(U)
.
For the counterpart of Proposition
7.1
they also require that

u
is bounded from below.
Using our local-to-global Proposition
6.4
,
one can now
remove the first additional assumption of
[
15
]
and replace
the second one with
u\in N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
or
u
being bounded.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a decreasing sequence of
fine superminimizers in
U
.
If
u:=\lim_{j\to\infty}u_{j}\in N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
, then
u
is a fine superminimizer in
U
.

Let
V\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U
be a finely open set such that
{C_{p}}(X\setminus V)>0
,
and
v
be a solution of the
{\cal K}_{u,u}(V)
-obstacle problem.
As
u_{j}
is a solution of the
{\cal K}_{u_{j},u_{j}}(V)
-obstacle problem
(by Lemma
6.3
),
the comparison
principle
[
3
, Corollary 4.3]

implies that
v\leq u_{j}
q.e. in
V
.
Since this holds for all
j
, we have
v\leq u
q.e. in
V
.

On the other hand, by the definition of the obstacle problem,

v\geq u
q.e. in
V
, and thus
u=v
q.e. in
V
.
Hence,
u
is also a solution of the
{\cal K}_{u,u}(V)
-obstacle problem.
By Proposition
6.4
,
u
is a fine superminimizer in
U
.
∎

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be an increasing sequence of
fine superminimizers in
U
such that
|u_{j}-f|\leq M

a.e. in
U
for some
f\in N^{1,p}(U)
and some
M\geq 0
.

Then
u:=\lim_{j\to\infty}u_{j}

is a fine superminimizer in
U
.

The proof has been inspired by the proof of
Theorem 6.1 in Kinnunen–Martio
[
16
]
.

Lemma
5.1

implies that
u\in N^{1,p}_{\textup{fine-loc}}(U)
and that (
5.1
)
holds for every finely open
W\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U
.
To prove the superminimizing property,
let
V_{0}\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U
be finely open
and let
\varphi_{0}\in N^{1,p}_{0}(V_{0})
be nonnegative.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary.
By Proposition
3.8
there is
a finely open

V^{\prime}\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}V_{0}
and a bounded
0\leq\varphi\in N^{1,p}_{0}(V^{\prime})
so that

\|\varphi-\varphi_{0}\|_{N^{1,p}(V_{0})}<\varepsilon.

(7.1)

By Lemma 3.3 in
[
5
]
,

V_{0}
has a base of fine neighbourhoods
W^{\prime}\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}V_{0}
,
and thus, by the quasi-Lindelöf principle

[
5
, Theorem 3.4]
, we can find a countable collection

W_{j}\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}V_{0}
of finely open sets such that

{C_{p}}(V_{0}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}W_{j})=0
.
Since
g_{u}\in L^{p}(V_{0})
, dominated convergence implies that
there is
m
such that

\int_{V_{0}\setminus V}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu<\varepsilon,\quad\text{where }V=V^{\prime}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}W_{j}.

(7.2)

Moreover,
V\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}V_{0}
is finely open.
By the definition of
p\mspace{1mu}
-strict subsets, there exists

\eta_{0}\in N^{1,p}_{0}(V_{0})
such that
0\leq\eta_{0}\leq 1
and
\eta_{0}=1
in
V
.
Then

W:=\bigl\{x\in V_{0}:\eta_{0}(x)>\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr\}\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U

and
W
is quasiopen, by Theorem
3.2
.
Moreover,
\eta:=(2\eta_{0}-1)_{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle+$}}}}\in N^{1,p}_{0}(W)

and
\eta=1
on
V
.

Let
v=u+\varphi
.
Then
0\leq v-u_{j}\leq\varphi+2M
a.e.
Since
\eta
and
\varphi
are bounded, it thus follows
from the Leibniz rule
[
1
, Theorem 2.15]

that
\psi_{j}:=\eta(v-u_{j})\in N^{1,p}_{0}(W)
.
As in the proof of
the Caccioppoli-type inequality (Theorem
1.4
)
in Section
5
,
we have

g_{u_{j}+\psi_{j}}\leq(1-\eta)g_{u_{j}}+\eta g_{v}+(v-u_{j})g_{\eta}\quad\text{a.e. in }W,

by Lemma 2.4 in Kinnunen–Martio
[
16
]

(or
[
1
, Lemma 2.18]
).
Since
W\mathrel{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle p}{\Subset}}U
is quasiopen, we get from the
superminimizing property of
u_{j}
that

\displaystyle\biggl(\int_{W}g_{u_{j}}^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\biggl(\int_{W}g_{u_{j}+\psi_{j}}^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\biggl(\int_{W}((1-\eta)g_{u_{j}}+\eta g_{v})^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}+\biggl(\int_{W}(v-u_{j})^{p}g_{\eta}^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=:\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j}.

Using the elementary inequality

(\alpha+\beta)^{p}\leq\alpha^{p}+p\beta(\alpha+\beta)^{p-1},\quad\alpha,\beta\geq 0,

together with the convexity of
t\mapsto t^{p}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{W}g_{u_{j}}^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{W}((1-\eta)g_{u_{j}}+\eta g_{v})^{p}\,d\mu+p\beta_{j}(\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j})^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{W}(1-\eta)g_{u_{j}}^{p}\,d\mu+\int_{W}\eta g_{v}^{p}\,d\mu+p\beta_{j}(\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j})^{p-1}.

As
u_{j}\in N^{1,p}(W)
, we can subtract the first term on the right-hand
side from both sides of the inequality and obtain that

\int_{V}g_{u_{j}}^{p}\,d\mu\leq\int_{W}\eta g_{u_{j}}^{p}\,d\mu\leq\int_{W}g_{v}^{p}\,d\mu+p\beta_{j}(\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j})^{p-1}.

(7.3)

Since
2\eta_{0}>1
on
W
, the
Caccioppoli-type inequality (Theorem
1.4
)
shows that

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\biggl(\int_{W}g_{u_{j}}^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}+\biggl(\int_{W}g_{v}^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\biggl(\int_{V_{0}}(4^{p}\eta_{0}^{p}g_{f}^{p}+(8pM)^{p}g_{\eta_{0}}^{p})\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}+\biggl(\int_{W}g_{v}^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p},

i.e. the sequence
\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is bounded.
At the same time, since
g_{\eta}=0
a.e. in
V
and
v=u
outside
V
,
we have by dominated convergence and the fact that

|(u-u_{j})g_{\eta}|\leq 2Mg_{\eta}\in L^{p}(W)
,

\beta_{j}=\biggl(\int_{W\setminus V}(u-u_{j})^{p}g_{\eta}^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}\to 0,\quad\text{as }j\to\infty.

Altogether this shows that the last term in (
7.3
)
tends to
0
, as
j\to\infty
.
Using (
7.3
) together with (
5.1
) applied to
V
,
we obtain that

\biggl(\int_{V}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}\leq\biggl(\int_{V_{0}}g_{v}^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}<\biggl(\int_{V_{0}}g_{u+\varphi_{0}}^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}+\varepsilon,

where the last estimate follows from
(
7.1
) and the fact that

g_{v}=g_{u+\varphi}\leq g_{u+\varphi_{0}}+g_{\varphi-\varphi_{0}}\quad\text{a.e.\ in }V_{0}.

Finally, (
7.2
) yields

\int_{V_{0}}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu=\int_{V}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu+\int_{V_{0}\setminus V}g_{u}^{p}\,d\mu<\biggl(\biggl(\int_{V_{0}}g_{u+\varphi_{0}}^{p}\,d\mu\biggr)^{1/p}+\varepsilon\biggr)^{p}+\varepsilon.

As
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary this completes the proof.
∎

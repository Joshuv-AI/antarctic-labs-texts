Primordial Gravitational Waves Assisted by Cosmological Scalar Perturbations
Yan-Heng Yu, Sai Wang

Abstract
Primordial gravitational waves are a crucial prediction of inflation theory, and their detection through their imprints on the cosmic microwave background is actively being pursued. However, these attempts have not yet been successful. In this paper, we propose a novel approach to detect primordial gravitational waves by searching for a signal of second-order tensor perturbations. These perturbations were produced due to nonlinear couplings between the linear tensor and scalar perturbations in the early universe. We anticipate a blue-tilted tensor spectral index, and suggest that the tensor-to-scalar ratio can potentially be measured with high precision using a detector network composed of the ground-based Einstein Telescope and the space-borne LISA project on a decade timescale.

Primordial gravitational waves, originated from quantized tensor modes of perturbed metric in the very early universe, are one of the most important predictions of cosmic inflation theory

;

;

;

;

Efforts have been made to detect primordial tensor perturbations on small scales, which are detectable by space-borne and ground-based gravitational-wave interferometers

;

;
Meerburg et al. 2015
;
Lasky et al. 2016
;

Creminelli et al. 2014
;
Rubakov 2014

;

Considering the absence of measurements of primordial tensor perturbations on large scales and the difficulties in generating a blue-tilted tensor spectrum on small scales, it is important to give serious consideration to any new mechanisms that can enhance the tensor spectral amplitude without requiring extraordinary assumptions.

Our proposal suggests that during the early universe, the linear scalar perturbations could have modulated the primordial tensor perturbations, resulting in the production of second-order tensor perturbations with a significantly blue-tilted power spectrum. This anticipated signal can potentially be detected by ongoing and planned ground-based detectors such as the Advanced LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA

;

Furthermore, scalar perturbations are believed to contribute to the formation of primordial black holes, which are considered as a viable candidate for dark matter

;
Carr and Kuhnel 2020
. Additionally, they are expected to produce scalar-induced gravitational waves, which can be detected by planned space-borne detectors such as the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna

;

This paper investigates the theory of second-order tensor perturbations and the possible multi-band measurements of modulated primordial and scalar-induced gravitational waves using a future detector network consisting of the ground-based Einstein Telescope and the space-borne Laser Interferometer Space Antenna. Conclusion.

In the early universe, the linear tensor perturbations were modulated with bump-spectral scalar perturbations to produce second-order tensor perturbations. The resulting tensor spectral index was found to be
(2+n_{t})
, which may have a significant blue tilt. Currently, plans are underway to develop next-generation ground-based gravitational-wave detectors that could provide accurate measurements of the tensor-to-scalar ratio within the next decade. However, such measurements require the existence of both inflationary tensor perturbations and linear scalar perturbations with a bumpy power spectrum, making it difficult to discuss their specifics until the measurements are completed. If future multi-band measurements are able to detect the anticipated signal of second-order tensor perturbations, it could provide valuable insights into the physics of cosmic inflation and help constrain inflation models. While scientists are actively pursuing measurements of cosmic microwave background B-mode polarization (see review in Ref.
Kamionkowski and Kovetz 2016
), our proposal offers an alternative approach to accurately measure primordial tensor perturbations.

C.1
Basic Equations of Second-order Tensor Perturbations

We start with a perturbed spatially-flat Friedman-Robertson-Walker metric in the conformal Newtonian gauge

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=a^{2}(\eta)\ \left\{-(1+2\phi)\ \mathrm{d}\eta^{2}+\left[(1-2\phi)\ \delta_{ij}+h_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{h}_{ij}\right]\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\right\}\ ,

(15)

where
a(\eta)
is the scale factor at the conformal time
\eta
,
\phi
and
h_{ij}
denote the linear scalar and tensor perturbations, and
\tilde{h}_{ij}
denotes second-order tensor perturbations induced by
\phi
and
h_{ij}
. We expand
\phi
and
h_{ij}
(
\tilde{h}_{ij}
in the same way) in Fourier space

\displaystyle\phi(\eta,\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\ \phi_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta)e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\ ,

(16a)

\displaystyle h_{ij}(\eta,\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda=+,\times}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\ h_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}(\eta)\epsilon_{\mathbf{k},ij}^{\lambda}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\ ,

(16b)

where polarization tensors are

\epsilon^{+}_{\mathbf{k},ij}=\left(e_{i}e_{j}-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mue\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{i}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mue\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{j}\right)/\sqrt{2}

and

\epsilon^{\times}_{\mathbf{k},ij}=\left(e_{i}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mue\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{j}+\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mue\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{i}e_{j}\right)/\sqrt{2}
,
with orthonormal vectors
e_{i}
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mue\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{i}
being transverse to the wavevector
\mathbf{k}
. For adiabatic perturbations, the evolution of the Fourier components
\phi_{\mathbf{k}}
and
h^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}
are governed by

\displaystyle\ddot{\phi}_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta)+3(1+w)\mathcal{H}\dot{\phi}_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta)

\displaystyle+wk^{2}\phi_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta)=0\ ,

(17a)

\displaystyle\ddot{h}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}(\eta)+2\mathcal{H}\dot{h}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}(\eta)

\displaystyle+k^{2}h_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}(\eta)=0\ ,

(17b)

where an overdot denotes a derivative with respect to
\eta
,
\mathcal{H}\equiv\dot{a}/a
is the comoving Hubble parameter, and
w\equiv p/\rho
is the state parameter with
p
and
\rho
being pressure and energy density of the Universe, respectively. Further, the solutions of
Eq.

17
can be written as the primordial curvature (tensor) perturbations
\zeta_{\mathbf{k}}
(
H_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}
), times the scalar (tensor) transfer function
T_{s}
(
T_{t}
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta)=\frac{3+3w}{5+3w}T_{s}(k\eta)\zeta_{\mathbf{k}}\ ,\ \ h_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}(\eta)=T_{t}(k\eta)H_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}\ .\end{split}

(18)

The dimensionless primordial power spectrum
\mathcal{P}_{s}(k)
and
\mathcal{P}_{t}(k)
are defined as the two-point correlation function, i.e.,

\displaystyle\langle\zeta_{\mathbf{k}}\zeta_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\frac{2\pi^{2}}{k^{3}}\mathcal{P}_{s}(k)\ ,\ \ \langle H^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}H^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\frac{2\pi^{2}}{k^{3}}\mathcal{P}_{t}(k)\ .

(19)

where
\langle...\rangle
denotes the ensemble average, the Kronecker symbol
\delta^{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}

and the Dirac function
\delta(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime})
reflect the independence between two polarizations of the tensor perturbations and the conservation of momentum, respectively.

Based on the cosmological perturbation theory, each polarization component of the second-order tensor perturbation is composed of three terms, i.e.,
\tilde{h}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}=\tilde{h}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{}^{ss}+\tilde{h}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{}^{st}+\tilde{h}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{}^{tt}
, where the superscripts
s
and
t
stand for contributions from the linear scalar and tensor perturbations, respectively, and the equation of motion of the second-order gravitational waves
\tilde{h}^{\lambda\alpha\beta}_{\mathbf{k}}
(
\alpha\beta=ss,st,tt
) is given by

\ddot{\tilde{h}}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{}^{\alpha\beta}+2\mathcal{H}\dot{\tilde{h}}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{}^{\alpha\beta}+k^{2}\tilde{h}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{}^{\alpha\beta}=4\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{}^{\alpha\beta}\ .

(20)

The explicit expression of the source term
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{}^{\alpha\beta}
in Eq. (
20
) is obtained to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{}^{ss}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\ \epsilon^{\lambda,lm}_{\mathbf{k}}q_{l}q_{m}\Big[2\phi_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}\phi_{\mathbf{q}}+\frac{4}{3(1+w)}\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}\dot{\phi}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}+\phi_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}\right)\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}\dot{\phi}_{\mathbf{q}}+\phi_{\mathbf{q}}\right)\Big]\ ,

(21a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{}^{st}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\ \epsilon^{\lambda,lm}_{\mathbf{k}}\epsilon_{\mathbf{q},lm}^{\lambda_{1}}\Big\{-3\ddot{\phi}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}h_{\mathbf{q}}^{\lambda_{1}}-2(4+3w)\mathcal{H}\dot{\phi}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}h_{\mathbf{q}}^{\lambda_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\ -[(1+2w)(k^{2}+q^{2})-4wk^{c}q_{c}]\phi_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}h_{\mathbf{q}}^{\lambda_{1}}\Big\}\ ,

(21b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{}^{tt}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\ \frac{\epsilon^{\lambda,lm}_{\mathbf{k}}}{2}\bigg\{\epsilon^{\lambda_{1},b}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},l}\epsilon^{\lambda_{2}}_{\mathbf{q},bm}{k^{2}}\dot{h}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}^{\lambda_{1}}\dot{h}_{\mathbf{q}}^{\lambda_{2}}+\Big[\epsilon^{\lambda_{1},b}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},l}\epsilon^{\lambda_{2}}_{\mathbf{q},bm}{(k^{c}q_{c}-q^{2})}-2\epsilon^{\lambda_{1},bc}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}\epsilon^{\lambda_{2}}_{\mathbf{q},bm}\ q_{c}q_{l}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\ -\ \epsilon^{\lambda_{1}}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},mc}\epsilon^{\lambda_{2}}_{\mathbf{q},bl}\ (k^{b}-q^{b})q^{c}-\epsilon^{\lambda_{1}}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},bc}\epsilon^{\lambda_{2}}_{\mathbf{q},lm}\ q^{b}q^{c}-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\lambda_{1},bc}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}\epsilon^{\lambda_{2}}_{\mathbf{q},bc}\ q^{l}q^{m}\Big]h_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}^{\lambda_{1}}h_{\mathbf{q}}^{\lambda_{2}}\bigg\}\ .

(21c)

To establish a contact with primordial perturbations during the inflationary stage, we rewrite the source term
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{}^{\alpha\beta}
above in the form of

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda ss}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\ Q_{ss}^{\lambda}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})\ k^{2}f_{ss}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|,q,\eta)\ \zeta_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}\zeta_{\mathbf{q}}\ ,

(22a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda st}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\ Q_{st}^{\lambda\lambda_{1}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})\ k^{2}f_{st}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|,q,\eta)\ \zeta_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}H_{\mathbf{q}}^{\lambda_{1}}\ ,

(22b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda tt}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{5}Q_{tt,i}^{\lambda\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})\ k^{2}f_{tt,i}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|,q,\eta)\right)H_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}^{\lambda_{1}}H_{\mathbf{q}}^{\lambda_{2}}\ .

(22c)

(23a)

\displaystyle Q_{st}^{\lambda\lambda_{1}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})

(23b)

\displaystyle Q_{tt,1}^{\lambda\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle\equiv-\epsilon^{\lambda,lm}_{\mathbf{k}}\epsilon^{\lambda_{1},bc}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}\ \epsilon_{\mathbf{q},bm}^{\lambda_{2}}\ (k-q)_{l}q_{c}/k^{2}+\Big((\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})\leftrightarrow\mathbf{q}\ \mathrm{term}\Big)\ ,

\displaystyle Q_{tt,3}^{\lambda\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle\equiv\ \epsilon^{\lambda,lm}_{\mathbf{k}}\ \epsilon^{\lambda_{1}}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},mc}\ \epsilon_{\mathbf{q},lb}^{\lambda_{2}}\ k^{b}k^{c}/k^{2}\ ,

\displaystyle Q_{tt,4}^{\lambda\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle\equiv\ \frac{1}{2}\ \epsilon^{\lambda,lm}_{\mathbf{k}}\ \epsilon^{\lambda_{1}}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},bc}\ \epsilon^{\lambda_{2}}_{\mathbf{q},lm}\ k^{b}k^{c}/k^{2}+\Big((\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})\leftrightarrow\mathbf{q}\ \mathrm{term}\Big)\ ,

\displaystyle Q_{tt,5}^{\lambda\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle\equiv-\epsilon^{\lambda,lm}_{\mathbf{k}}\ \epsilon^{\lambda_{1},bc}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}\ \epsilon_{\mathbf{q},bc}^{\lambda_{2}}\ (k-q)_{l}q_{m}/k^{2}\ .

(23c)

where
Q_{tt,i}^{\lambda\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}
is defined in a symmetric form with respect to
\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}
and
\mathbf{q}
, which facilitates the subsequent manipulation in Eq. (
32

in Eq. (

\displaystyle f_{ss}

\displaystyle=\frac{6(1+w)}{5+3w}\ T_{s}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|\eta)\ T_{s}(q\eta)+\frac{12(1+w)}{(5+3w)^{2}}\bigg[\ \frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}\dot{T}_{s}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|\eta)\ T_{s}(q\eta)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}T_{s}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|\eta)\ \dot{T}_{s}(q\eta)+\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\dot{T}_{s}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|\eta)\ \dot{T}_{s}(q\eta)\bigg]\ ,

(24a)

\displaystyle f_{st}

\displaystyle=\frac{3+3w}{5+3w}\ \bigg[-\frac{3}{k^{2}}\ \ddot{T}_{s}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|\eta)\ T_{t}(q\eta)-\frac{2(4+3w)\mathcal{H}}{k^{2}}\ \dot{T}_{s}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|\eta)\ T_{t}(q\eta)

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\left(1+\frac{q^{2}}{k^{2}}+\frac{2w\ |\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)\ T_{s}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|\eta)\ T_{t}(q\eta)\bigg]\ ,

(24b)

\displaystyle f_{tt,1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(1-\frac{|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|^{2}}{k^{2}}-\frac{q^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)T_{t}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|\eta)\ T_{t}(q\eta)+\frac{1}{2k^{2}}\ \dot{T}_{t}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|\eta)\ \dot{T}_{t}(q\eta)\ ,

\displaystyle f_{tt,2}

\displaystyle=f_{tt,3}=f_{tt,4}=2f_{tt,5}=-\frac{1}{2}\ T_{t}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|\eta)\ T_{t}(q\eta)\ .

(24c)

We can solve Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\eta^{2}}G_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)+\left(k^{2}-\frac{1}{a(\eta)}\frac{\partial^{2}a(\eta)}{\partial\eta^{2}}\right)G_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)=\delta(\eta,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\ ,

and obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{}^{\alpha\beta}(\eta)=4\int^{\eta}d\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\ \frac{a(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)}{a(\eta)}kG_{\mathbf{k}}(\eta,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda}{}^{\alpha\beta}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\ .

(26)

(27a)

describes the evolution of the source terms.

The dimensionless energy-density spectrum of the second-order tensor perturbations, i.e., the energy density per logarithmic frequency normalized with the critical energy density of the early universe, is given by
Inomata et al. 2017

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{\alpha\beta}(\eta,k)=\frac{1}{24}\left(\frac{k}{\mathcal{H}}\right)^{2}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}_{\tilde{h}}(\eta,k)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\ ,

where the overbar denotes the oscillation average and the power spectrum of the second-order tensor perturbations

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{h}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\lambda\alpha\beta}\tilde{h}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{\lambda^{\prime}\alpha\beta}\rangle=\delta^{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\delta(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\ \frac{2\pi^{2}}{k^{3}}\ \mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}_{\tilde{h}}(k)\ .

(30)

The total spectrum is
\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}=\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{ss}+\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{st}+\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{tt}

Wang et al. 2019

\displaystyle h^{2}\Omega_{\mathrm{gw,0}}^{\alpha\beta}(k)=h^{2}\Omega_{\mathrm{r,0}}\times\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{\alpha\beta}(\eta,k)\ ,

(31)

where the corresponding one for photons and neutrinos is

, with

being the dimensionless Hubble constant
Aghanim et al. 2020

By neglecting the non-Gaussianity of the primordial curvature perturbations, we can use Wick’s theorem and get

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{ss}\propto\langle\zeta_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}\zeta_{\mathbf{q}}\zeta_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}\zeta_{\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}\rangle=\Big[\delta(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime})+\delta(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}^{\prime})\Big]\delta(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\ \frac{2\pi^{2}\mathcal{P}_{s}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|)}{|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|^{3}}\frac{2\pi^{2}\mathcal{P}_{s}(q)}{q^{3}}\ ,

(32a)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{st}\propto\langle\zeta_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}H^{\lambda_{1}}_{\mathbf{q}}\zeta_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}H^{\lambda_{1}^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{1}^{\prime}}\delta(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime})\delta(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\ \frac{2\pi^{2}\mathcal{P}_{s}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|)}{|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|^{3}}\frac{2\pi^{2}\mathcal{P}_{t}(q)}{q^{3}}\ ,

(32b)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{tt}\propto\langle H_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}^{\lambda_{1}}H_{\mathbf{q}}^{\lambda_{2}}H_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{1}^{\prime}}H_{\mathbf{q}^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{2}^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\left[\delta^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{1}^{\prime}}\delta^{\lambda_{2}\lambda_{2}^{\prime}}\delta(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{q}^{\prime})+\delta^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}^{\prime}}\delta^{\lambda_{2}\lambda_{1}^{\prime}}\delta(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}^{\prime})\right]\delta(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\ \frac{2\pi^{2}\mathcal{P}_{t}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|)}{|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|^{3}}\frac{2\pi^{2}\mathcal{P}_{t}(q)}{q^{3}}\ .

(32c)

We can finally obtain the general expression of

C.3
The Disparities of ”Scalar-Scalar” and ”Scalar-Tensor” Modes under Large-Momentum and Small-Momentum Coupling Limits

We do some limit analysis in
u=|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|/k\rightarrow 0
and
v=|\mathbf{q}|/k\rightarrow 1

to demonstrate the differences between
\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{ss}
and
\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{st}
, which can provide an explanation for why the large-momentum modes can be enhanced in
\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{st}
, but not in
\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{ss}
.

(a) One of the differences is “projection factors”
Q_{\alpha\beta}
, which describes the geometric relations between the momenta and polarization tensors of the linear perturbations. As shown in Eq. (
34
), we have
Q^{2}_{ss}(u,v)\rightarrow 0
in
u\rightarrow 0,v\rightarrow 1
limit, implying a suppression on the couplings between small-momentum scalar and large momentum tensor. However, as for “scalar-tensor” mode in Eq. (
34
), we have
Q^{2}_{st}(u,v)\rightarrow 2
keeping a constant in
u\rightarrow 0,v\rightarrow 1
limit, which means there is always a non-vanishing “effective quadrupole moment” in “scalar-tensor” mode.

(b) Another difference is “kernel function”
I_{\alpha\beta}
, coming from the different transfer functions between scalar and tensor perturbations. As shown in Eq. (
40
), for “scalar-scalar” mode,
I^{2}_{ss}\rightarrow\mathrm{const}
in
u\rightarrow 0,v\rightarrow 1
limit. However, for “scalar-tensor” mode in Eq. (
40
),
I^{2}_{st}\propto u^{-2}
in
u\rightarrow 0,v\rightarrow 1
limit, providing an enhancement factor related to small-momentum scalar perturbations.

In summary, the differences between
\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{ss}
and
\Omega_{\mathrm{gw}}^{st}
at high frequencies result from distinct behaviors of the “projection factors”
Q_{\alpha\beta}
(related to geometry) and “kernel function”
I_{\alpha\beta}
(related to time evolution) in the limit
u\rightarrow 0,\ v\rightarrow 1
.

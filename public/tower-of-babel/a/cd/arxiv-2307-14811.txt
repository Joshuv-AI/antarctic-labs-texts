Abstract
Dark matter, if represented by a ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -symmetric scalar field, can manifest as both particles and condensates. In this paper, we study the evolution of an oscillating homogeneous condensate of a ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -symmetric scalar field in a thermal plasma in an FLRW universe. We focus on the perturbative regime where the oscillation amplitude is sufficiently small so that parametric resonance is inefficient. This perturbative regime necessarily comprises the late stage of the condensate decay and determines its fate. The coupled coarse-grained equations of motion for the condensate, radiation, and spacetime are derived from first principles using nonequilibrium quantum field theory. We obtain analytical expressions for the relevant microscopic quantities that enter the equations of motion and solve the latter numerically. We find that there is always a nonvanishing relic abundance for a condensate with a ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} symmetry that is not spontaneously broken. This is because its decay rate decreases faster than the Hubble parameter at late times due to either the amplitude dependence or the temperature dependence in the condensate decay rate. Consequently, accounting for the condensate contribution to the overall dark matter relic density is essential for ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} scalar singlet dark matter.

Model and the condensate equation of motion

2.1
Model

We consider the following model

\displaystyle S[\Phi,\chi]=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\biggl[\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\Phi)(\partial^{\mu}\Phi)+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\chi)(\partial^{\mu}\chi)-V(\Phi,\chi)\biggr]\,,

V(\Phi,\chi)=\frac{m_{\phi}^{2}}{2}\Phi^{2}+\frac{m_{\chi}^{2}}{2}\chi^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\phi}}{4!}\Phi^{4}+\frac{\lambda_{\chi}}{4!}\chi^{4}+\frac{g}{4}\Phi^{2}\chi^{2}\,.

\mathrm{d}{s}^{2}=\mathrm{d}{t}^{2}-a^{2}(t)\mathrm{d}{\mathbf{x}}^{2}\,,

(3)

\sqrt{-g}=a^{3}(t)

Expanding

2.2
Coupled fundamental equations of motion for the condensate and plasma.

In addition to the condensate
\varphi
, our system also includes a plasma. The plasma can be described by the two-point functions of the fluctuation fields,
\langle\phi\phi\rangle\equiv\Delta_{\phi}
,
\langle\chi\chi\rangle\equiv\Delta_{\chi}

\Gamma_{\rm 2PI}[\varphi,\Delta_{\phi},\Delta_{\chi}]

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Gamma_{\rm 2PI}[\varphi,\Delta_{\phi},\Delta_{\chi}]}{\delta\varphi(x)}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(6a)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Gamma_{\rm 2PI}[\varphi,\Delta_{\phi},\Delta_{\chi}]}{\delta\Delta_{\phi}(x,x^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(6b)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Gamma_{\rm 2PI}[\varphi,\Delta_{\phi},\Delta_{\chi}]}{\delta\Delta_{\chi}(x,x^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(6c)

The 2PI effective action

\displaystyle\left(\Box+m_{\phi}^{2}\right)\varphi(x)+\frac{\lambda_{\phi}}{6}\varphi^{3}(x)+\frac{\lambda_{\phi}}{2}\varphi(x)\Delta_{\phi}(x,x)+\frac{g}{2}\varphi(x)\Delta_{\chi}(x,x)=0\,,

The small-field regime.

The plasma can be driven out of equilibrium essentially because of the
\varphi
-dependent masses (see e.g.,
[
68
]
). In this paper, we are interested in the evolution of the condensate at a sufficiently late stage so that

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{\phi}}{2}\varphi^{2}\ll M^{2}_{\phi}\equiv m_{\phi}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\phi}}{2}\varphi^{2}+m^{2}_{\phi,\rm th}\,,\quad\frac{g}{2}\varphi^{2}\ll M^{2}_{\chi}\equiv m_{\chi}^{2}+\frac{g}{2}\varphi^{2}+m_{\chi,\rm th}^{2}\,,

(9)

where
m_{\phi,\rm th}
and
m_{\chi,\rm th}
are thermal corrections to the masses of
\phi
and
\chi
particles. The first condition also indicates that the oscillation is quasi-harmonic. This late stage is still interesting because it determines the fate of the oscillating condensate, i.e., whether the condensate can have a nonvanishing relic abundance. The conditions in Eq. (
9
) define the so-called small-field regime. In this case, one can perform a perturbative expansion in
\varphi
in
\Delta_{\phi,\chi}[\varphi]
and at the leading order, the two-point functions are independent of
\varphi
. In the small-field regime, it is expected that the damping due to the out-of-equilibrium effects mentioned above becomes inefficient as the particle masses are not sensitive to the change of
\varphi
anymore. Furthermore, the small-field regime is precisely the regime in which nonperturbative particle production from condensate oscillations can be neglected and the condensate decays mainly via perturbative processes
[
71
]
.

In the small-field regime, we have to consider other types of damping for the condensate oscillations. Going beyond the one-loop of the 2PI effective action, one has non-local terms in the condensate EoM which describe direct interactions between the condensate and particles induced by the
\varphi
-dependent vertices. Such interactions cause damping even if the plasma is in thermal equilibrium. This is the damping more often studied in the literature
[
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
36
,
44
]
and is the focus of the present work.

The above observation also applies to bubble wall dynamics in cosmological first-order phase transitions. The local EoM (
7
) is usually used for studying the bubble expansion. In the ultrarelativistic regime, the third and fourth terms, usually written as

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}m^{2}_{i}(\phi)}{\mathrm{d}\phi}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}{\bf p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{i}}\,f_{i}(p,z)\,,

(10)

give rise to the Bödeker-Moore thermal friction
[
72
,
73
]
. However, there is also additional friction caused by particle-production processes induced by condensate-dependent vertices, see e.g. Refs.
[
74
,
75
]
. This force is not captured by the local terms in Eq. (
7
) but rather is encoded in the non-local terms when one goes beyond the one-loop of the 2PI effective action
[
76
]
.

2.3
Condensate equation of motion in the small-field regime

\Delta_{\phi,\chi}[\varphi=0]

3
Coupled coarse-grained equations of motion

In order to understand the energy transfer between the condensate and plasma, it is sufficient to study the EoM of the envelope function, Eq. (
14a
). Indeed, the energy of the condensate can be approximated as

\displaystyle\rho_{\varphi}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\varphi}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}M^{2}_{\phi}\varphi^{2}\approx\frac{1}{2}M^{2}_{\phi}A(t)^{2}\,,

(17)

where we have assumed that

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{A}}{A}\ll M_{\phi}\,,\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}t}\ll M_{\phi}\,.

(18)

The above conditions imply that the amplitude and the frequency of oscillations do not change much during one oscillation. Therefore, the evolution of
A(t)
contains information on the dissipation of the condensate energy. From Eq. (
14a
) we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\varphi}+3H\rho_{\varphi}+\left(2\gamma-\frac{\dot{M}_{\phi}}{M_{\phi}}\right)\rho_{\varphi}+\frac{2\sigma\rho_{\varphi}^{2}}{M_{\phi}^{2}}=0\,.

(19)

The
\gamma
and
\sigma
depend on the temperature and thus are time dependent. Eq. (
19
) is accompanied by the EoM for the radiation energy density and the Friedmann equation

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\rm R}+4H\rho_{\rm R}-\left(2\gamma-\frac{\dot{M}_{\phi}}{M_{\phi}}\right)\rho_{\varphi}-\frac{2\sigma\rho_{\varphi}^{2}}{M_{\phi}^{2}}=0\,,

(20)

\displaystyle H^{2}=\frac{8\pi(\rho_{\varphi}+\rho_{\rm R})}{3M^{2}_{\rm P}}\,,

(21)

where
M_{\rm P}
is the Planck mass.
The radiation energy density is related to the temperature through

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm R}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{30}g_{*}T^{4}\,,

(22)

where
g_{*}
is the effective number of relativistic degrees of freedom.
Here we have assumed that during the dissipation of the condensate, there are no other forms of energy densities.

If we define the number density of the condensate quanta
n_{\varphi}
through
n_{\varphi}=\rho_{\varphi}/M_{\phi}
,
then Eq. (
19

\displaystyle\dot{n}_{\varphi}+3Hn_{\varphi}=-2\gamma n_{\varphi}-\frac{2\sigma}{M_{\phi}}n_{\varphi}^{2}\,.

(23)

This implies that the comoving number density of the condensate quanta is conserved for vanishing
\gamma
and
\sigma

4
“Freeze-out” of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
condensate

Before we present some exact numerical results, let us first estimate the condition for the “freeze-out” of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
condensate. From Eq. (
19
), one can read the condensate “decay rate” as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\varphi}=2\gamma-\frac{\dot{M}_{\phi}}{M_{\phi}}+\frac{2\sigma\rho_{\varphi}}{M^{2}_{\phi}}\,.

(34)

Note that this decay rate is not necessarily equal to the damping rate defined from the envelop function
A(t)

[
36
]
. Nevertheless, it can still characterise how fast the condensate depletes its energy into the plasma. An efficient decay requires
\Gamma_{\varphi}\;\raisebox{-3.00003pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\displaystyle>}}{{\sim}}$}\;H
so that one can use
\Gamma_{\varphi}\sim H
as an estimate of the “freeze-out” time of the condensate. The full evolution is complicated because there could be some reheating effects due to the energy transfer from the condensate to the plasma. However, the “freeze-out” of the condensate typically occurs after the reheating. At that stage, one would have
\rho_{\rm R}\gg\rho_{\varphi}
,
7
7

7

If the initial conditions already satisfy this, then reheating effects are negligible.
and

\displaystyle H\sim 1.66\times\sqrt{g_{*}}T^{2}\sim\mathcal{O}(10)\,T^{2}\sim\frac{1}{t}\,.

(35)

\gamma
channels.

In the high-temperature limit,
\gamma
exhibits a decrease proportional to the temperature
T
as the Universe cools down. When the temperature drops to the regime where
M_{\phi}\sim m_{\phi}
,
\gamma
decreases with a quadratic dependence on
T
, thus scaling as
T^{2}
. At lower temperatures,
\gamma
experiences an exponential suppression. As a consequence, when the temperature drops to a sufficiently low value,
\gamma
decreases faster than
H
.

\sigma

While
\sigma
is not vanishing even for
T\rightarrow 0
provided
m_{\phi}>m_{\chi}
, the condensate decay rate due to the sigma channel is proportional to
\rho_{\varphi}
which decreases fast. For the case of
\gamma=0

,
\rho_{\varphi}
drops as

[
44
]
while
H
drops as
1/t
. With nonvanishing
\gamma

Damping due to the change of
M_{\phi}
.

In the high-temperature limit, one has

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{M}_{\phi}}{M_{\phi}}=\frac{(\lambda_{\phi}+g)T\dot{T}/24}{{m^{2}_{\phi}+(\lambda_{\phi}+g)T^{2}/24}}\sim\frac{\dot{T}}{T}

(36)

which scales as
H

In summary,
\Gamma_{\varphi}

\gamma

\gamma

6
Conclusion and discussion

In this work, we have studied the evolution of homogeneous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-symmetric scalar condensates in the early Universe in the perturbative regime where the oscillation amplitude is sufficiently small so that dissipation occurs via perturbative processes. This also indicates that the oscillation is quasi-harmonic. Otherwise, self-interactions of the scalar field would lead to significant parametric resonance. This perturbative regime necessarily comprises the late stage of the condensate evolution and determines the fate of the condensate. Our analysis is based on the CTP formalism which naturally accommodates quantum statistical effects of the plasma
as well as the backreaction effects from particle production.

We have derived, in the perturbative regime, the coupled coarse-grained EoMs for the condensate energy, radiation, and spacetime from the original non-local condensate EoM obtained from nonequilibrium quantum field theory. This derivation makes use of multiple-scale analysis that was first introduced in Ref.
[
36
]
to study the dissipation of scalar background fields. In this work, we have extended the analysis in Ref.
[
36
]
to take into account both the expanding spacetime and the time dependence in the temperature. The coarse-grained EoMs depend on two microscopic quantities
\gamma
and
\sigma
, defined in Eq. (
2.3
), which have an interpretation in terms of particle production. We have presented a very detailed computation of these two quantities. The analytic result for
\gamma
contributed from the sunset diagram generated by the
\varphi\phi\chi^{2}
interaction (the second diagram from Eq. (
12a
)) is a new result. The coupled EoMs have been solved numerically.

The key finding in this work is that a scalar condensate with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry cannot transfer all its energy to the plasma. The condensate decay rate is given in Eq. (
34
) and depends on both the temperature and the oscillation amplitude (through
\rho_{\varphi}
). As a result, the condensate decay rate always decreases faster than the Hubble parameter at late times (see the analysis in Sec.
4
). Therefore, similar to the freeze-out process for particles, the condensate comoving energy density approaches a constant once the condensate decay rate drops below the Hubble parameter. A homogeneous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-symmetric scalar condensate must have a nonvanishing relic density. This should put additional constraints on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-symmetric scalar fields as DM.

In our model, we did not consider spontaneous symmetry breaking. If
m_{\chi}^{2}<0
, as it would be when
\chi
is identified as the Higgs, there may be spontaneous symmetry breaking during the evolution of the condensate. If such spontaneous symmetry breaking occurs, we should have a cubic interaction term
v\Phi^{2}\chi
where
v
is the symmetry broken value of the
\chi
field. In the presence of the condensate, one has the vertex
v\varphi\phi\chi
which leads to the following additional diagram in the effective action that contributes to the condensate decay,

\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\chi

\Phi

\Phi

v_{\phi}

v_{\phi}\varphi\chi^{2}

\varphi\rightarrow\chi\chi

At last, since our analysis is based on the one and two-loop diagrams given in Eqs. (
12
), one may wonder whether higher loop processes would spoil our conclusion or not. In particular, it is known that in computing correlation functions in the zero-momentum, zero-frequency limit, e.g., in studying hydrodynamic transport coefficients
[
69
]
or damping coefficients in the slow-roll regime or at very high temperatures
[
68
]
, resummation of an infinity set of diagrams is required. This is because a near on-shell singularity appears whenever there is a product of two equal four-momentum propagators. However, such near on-shell singularity only appears in the zero-momentum, zero-frequency limit of the correlation functions. Since we do not assume
T\gg M_{\phi}
(high temperature) nor
\Gamma_{\varphi}\gg M_{\phi}
(over-damped or slow-roll), the self-energies in our problem are always evaluated at finite frequencies related to
M_{\phi}
depending on the powers of
\varphi
associated with the external vertices (e.g.,
\gamma
and
\sigma
in Eq. (
2.3
) are evaluated at
M_{\phi}
and
2M_{\phi}
, respectively). Therefore, the near on-shell singularity does not appear in our case. For scalar field theory at finite temperature, there is the famous problem with infrared divergences, essentially related to the Matsubara zero mode
[
89
]
. Such an infrared problem is cured if one does a daisy resummation. This has already been done with the thermal corrections being included in the propagators used in this work.

Out-of-equilibrium dynamics is an initial-value problem with the initial conditions given by a density matrix at a given time,
\rho_{D}(t_{i})
. The expectation value of an observable
\mathcal{O}
at time

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}(t)\rangle={\rm Tr}\left\{\rho_{D}(t_{i})\mathcal{O}(t)\right\}\,,

(A38)

where
\mathcal{O}(t)=\exp(\mathrm{i}H(t-t_{i}))\mathcal{O}(t_{i})\exp(-\mathrm{i}H(t-t_{i}))
. The operator is sandwiched between the same state. For this reason, the CTP formalism is also called in-in formalism. Viewed from the Schrödinger picture, the state evolves first forward in time from
t_{i}
to

.

\mathcal{C}

All the information in a quantum field system is encoded in its correlation functions. For most purposes, it is sufficient to study the one- and two-point functions. For the scalar field
\Phi
, there are two independent connected two-point functions

\displaystyle\langle\Phi(x_{1})\Phi(x_{2})\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\langle\phi(x_{1})\phi(x_{2})\rangle\equiv\Delta_{\phi}^{>}(x_{1},x_{2})\,,

(A39a)

\displaystyle\langle\Phi(x_{2})\Phi(x_{1})\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=\langle\phi(x_{2})\phi(x_{1})\rangle\equiv\Delta_{\phi}^{<}(x_{1},x_{2})\,.

(A39b)

They are called

Here and in what follows, analogous expressions also apply for the
\chi

\phi

\chi
. Since the generating functional is formulated on the closed time contour
\mathcal{C}
, we would have two-point functions time-ordered on
\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}(x_{1},x_{2})=\theta_{\mathcal{C}}(t_{1},t_{2})\Delta_{\phi}^{>}(x_{1},x_{2})+\theta_{\mathcal{C}}(t_{2},t_{1})\Delta_{\phi}^{<}(x_{1},x_{2})\,.

(A40)

To distinguish the times on the forward and backward contours in
\Delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}

+
” and the backward time “
-
”:
x^{+}=\{t^{+},\mathbf{x}\}
,
x^{-}=\{t^{-},\mathbf{x}\}
. According to the
\mathcal{C}
-ordering, one would have

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}(x_{1}^{+},x_{2}^{-})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\phi}^{<}(x_{1},x_{2})\,,

(A41a)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}(x_{1}^{-},x_{2}^{+})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\phi}^{>}(x_{1},x_{2})\,,

(A41b)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}(x_{1}^{+},x_{2}^{+})

\displaystyle=\langle T\phi(x_{1})\phi(x_{2})\rangle\,,

(A41c)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi}(x_{1}^{-},x_{2}^{-})

\displaystyle=\langle\overline{T}\phi(x_{1})\phi(x_{2})\rangle\,,

(A41d)

where
T
and
\overline{T}
denote the normal time-ordering and anti-time-ordering operators, respectively.

Compared to the closed time variable, a more convenient notation is the “double-field” formulation where one distinguishes the fields on the forward contour from those on the backward contour while using a common time variable. For example, one denotes
\Phi(x^{+})
as
\Phi^{+}(x)
,
\Phi(x^{-})

\Phi^{-}(x)

\chi

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{C}}[\Phi,\chi]=S[\Phi^{+},\chi^{+}]-S[\Phi^{-},\chi^{-}]\,,

(A42)

S_{\mathcal{C}}[\Phi,\chi]

\displaystyle\Delta^{+-}_{\phi}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\phi}^{<}(x_{1},x_{2})\,,

(A43a)

\displaystyle\Delta^{-+}_{\phi}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\phi}^{>}(x_{1},x_{2})\,,

(A43b)

\displaystyle\Delta^{++}_{\phi}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\langle T\phi(x_{1})\phi(x_{2})\rangle\,,

(A43c)

\displaystyle\Delta^{--}_{\phi}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\langle\overline{T}\phi(x_{1})\phi(x_{2})\rangle\,.

(A43d)

The indices “
\pm

Usually, it is convenient to introduce the spectral and statistical correlation functions defined as, respectively,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\phi}^{-}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\langle[\Phi(x_{1}),\Phi(x_{2})]\rangle_{c}=\mathrm{i}\left(\Delta_{\phi}^{-+}(x_{1},x_{2})-\Delta_{\phi}^{+-}(x_{1},x_{2})\right)\,,

(A44a)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\phi}^{+}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle\{\Phi(x_{1}),\Phi(x_{2})\}\rangle_{c}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta_{\phi}^{-+}(x_{1},x_{2})+\Delta_{\phi}^{+-}(x_{1},x_{2})\right)\,.

(A44b)

The spectral correlation function encodes the information about the spectrum of the theory and the statistical correlation function gives the information about occupation numbers of different modes for the fluctuations.

The Schwinger-Dyson equations for the one- and two-point functions can be derived from the one-particle-irreducible (1PI) or two-particle-irreducible (2PI) effective actions
[

If the effective action is not truncated, the 1PI and 2PI give the same EoMs. For a given order in the loop expansion, the 2PI effective action includes resummation in the propagators compared to the 1PI effective action. The definition of the 2PI effective action is based on the generating functional with local and non-local sources,

\displaystyle Z[J_{\Phi},J_{\chi},K_{\Phi},K_{\chi}]=\int\mathcal{D}\Phi^{+}\mathcal{D}\Phi^{-}\mathcal{D}\chi^{+}\mathcal{D}\chi^{-}\,

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\mathrm{i}\left[S[\Phi^{+},\chi^{+}]-S[\Phi^{-},\chi^{-}]+J^{A}_{\Phi,x}\Phi^{A}_{x}+J^{A}_{\chi,x}\chi^{A}_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{A}_{x}K^{AB}_{\Phi,xy}\Phi^{B}_{y}+\frac{1}{2}\chi^{A}_{x}K^{AB}_{\chi,xy}\chi^{B}_{y}\right]\right).

(A45)

where
A,B=\pm
, and we have used the DeWitt notation

\displaystyle J^{A}_{\Phi,x}\Phi^{A}_{x}=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,J^{A}_{\Phi}(x)\Phi^{A}(x)\,,

(A46a)

\displaystyle\Phi^{A}_{x}K^{AB}_{\Phi,xy}\Phi^{B}_{y}=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\mathrm{d}^{4}y\,\Phi^{A}(x)K^{AB}_{\Phi}(x,y)\Phi^{B}(y)\,.

(A46b)

The Einstein summation convention is understood for repeated indices. One can easily check that

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\ln Z[J_{\Phi},J_{\chi},K_{\Phi},K_{\chi}]}{\mathrm{i}\delta J^{A}_{\Phi}(x)}

\displaystyle=\langle\Phi^{A}(x)\rangle\equiv\varphi^{A}(x)\,,

(A47a)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\ln Z[J_{\Phi},J_{\chi},K_{\Phi},K_{\chi}]}{\mathrm{i}\delta K^{AB}_{\Phi,xy}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta^{AB}_{\phi}(x_{1},x_{2})+\varphi^{A}(x)\varphi^{B}(y)\right)\,,

(A47b)

and similarly for quantities involving
\chi
. Note that in the above equations, the one- and two-point functions on the RHS are defined with corresponding sources.
The 2PI effective action is then defined as the Legendre transform,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm 2PI}[\varphi,\bar{\chi},\Delta_{\phi},\Delta_{\chi}]=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\,\ln Z[J_{\Phi},J_{\chi},K_{\Phi},K_{\chi}]-J^{A}_{\Phi,x}\varphi^{A}_{x}-J^{A}_{\chi,x}\bar{\chi}^{A}_{x}\,,

(A48)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}K^{AB}_{\Phi,xy}(\varphi^{A}_{x}\varphi^{B}_{y}+\Delta^{AB}_{\phi,xy})-\frac{1}{2}K^{AB}_{\chi,xy}(\bar{\chi}_{x}^{A}\bar{\chi}_{y}^{B}+\Delta_{\chi,xy}^{AB})\,,

(A49)

where
\bar{\chi}^{A}=\langle\chi^{A}\rangle
. The Schwinger-Dyson equations for the one- and two-point functions read

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Gamma_{\rm 2PI}[\varphi,\bar{\chi},\Delta_{\phi},\Delta_{\chi}]}{\delta\varphi^{A}(x)}=-J^{A}_{\Phi}(x)-K^{AB}_{\Phi,xy}\varphi^{B}_{y}\,,

(A50a)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Gamma_{\rm 2PI}[\varphi,\bar{\chi},\Delta_{\phi},\Delta_{\chi}]}{\delta\Delta^{AB}_{\phi/\chi}(x,y)}=-\frac{1}{2}K^{AB}_{\Phi/\chi}(x,y)\,.

(A50b)

Taking all the
J
and
K
to be vanishing, we obtain the EoMs in the absence of external sources

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\delta\Gamma_{\rm 2PI}[\varphi,\bar{\chi},\Delta_{\phi},\Delta_{\chi}]}{\delta\varphi^{+}(x)}\right|_{\varphi^{-}=\varphi^{+}=\varphi}=0\,,

(A51)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\delta\Gamma_{\rm 2PI}[\varphi,\bar{\chi},\Delta_{\phi},\Delta_{\chi}]}{\delta\Delta^{AB}_{\phi/\chi}(x,y)}\right|_{\varphi^{-}=\varphi^{+}=\varphi}=0\,.

(A52)

In the Feynman-diagram representation of the
2
PI effective action, we now have two types of vertices: the + and - types. Depending on the vertices, one would have four different types of propagators:
\Delta_{\phi/\chi}^{++}
,
\Delta_{\phi/\chi}^{+-}
,
\Delta_{\phi/\chi}^{-+}
, and
\Delta_{\phi/\chi}^{--}
. For example, a propagator connecting two + type vertices is of type
\Delta^{++}
, etc. Solving first on-shell for the two-point functions and assuming that
\bar{\chi}=0
, one gets
\Delta_{\phi}[\varphi]
and
\Delta_{\chi}[\varphi]
as functionals of the condensate
\varphi
. Plugging them back into the 2PI effective action gives the EoM for the condensate

\left.\frac{\delta\Gamma_{\text{2PI}}[\varphi,\Delta_{\phi},\Delta_{\chi}]}{\delta\varphi(x)}\right|_{\Delta_{\phi}=\Delta_{\phi}[\varphi],\,\Delta_{\chi}=\Delta_{\chi}[\varphi]}=0\,.

(A53)

Arriving here, one can follow Section 2 of Ref.
[
36
]
to perform the small-field expansion of the on-shell two-point functions
\Delta_{\phi}[\varphi]
and
\Delta_{\chi}[\varphi]
. To the lowest order, they are independent of
\varphi
and become the free thermal equilibrium propagators. Properly truncating the effective action, one ends up with Eq. (
11
).

B.1.1
Summary of the results

Let us first introduce a quantity
V^{-}
that in position space reads

\displaystyle V^{-}(x-x^{\prime})\equiv V_{\phi}^{-}(x-x^{\prime})+V_{\chi}^{-}(x-x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=\phi,\chi}c_{a}\left[\left(G^{-+}_{a}(x-x^{\prime})\right)^{2}-\left(G^{+-}_{a}(x-x^{\prime})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=-2\mathrm{i}\sum_{a=\phi,\chi}c_{a}G_{a}^{+}(x-x^{\prime})G_{a}^{-}(x-x^{\prime})\,,

(B66)

where
c_{\phi}=-3\mathrm{i}\lambda_{\phi}^{2}/2
,
c_{\chi}=-3\mathrm{i}g^{2}/2
. The imaginary part of the retarded proper four-vertex function in momentum space,
{\rm Im}\widetilde{V}^{\rm R}(p)
, is related to
\widetilde{V}^{-}(p)
through (see, e.g., Ref.
[
110
]
)

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\widetilde{V}^{\rm R}(p)=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\widetilde{V}^{-}(p)\,,

(B67)

where
\widetilde{V}^{-}(p)
is the Fourier transform of
V^{-}(x-x^{\prime})
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{-}(p)=\sum_{a=\phi,\chi}\widetilde{V}^{-}_{a}(p)=-2\mathrm{i}\sum_{a=\phi,\chi}c_{a}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left(G^{+}_{a}(k)G^{-}_{a}(p-k)\right)\,.

(B68)

The real part,
{\rm Re}\widetilde{V}^{\rm R}(p)
, is related to the imaginary part via the Kramers-Kronig relation

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\widetilde{V}^{\rm R}(p)=\mathcal{P}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}}{\pi}\frac{{\rm Im}\widetilde{V}^{\rm R}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{p})}{\omega^{\prime}-p_{0}}\,.

(B69)

Substituting the Kubo-Martin-Schwinger (KMS) relation

\displaystyle G_{a}^{+}(q)=-\mathrm{i}\left(\frac{1}{2}+f_{\rm B}(q_{0})\right)G_{a}^{-}(q)

(B70)

into Eq. (
B68
) and using the definition for the spectral function,
G^{-}_{a}(k)=\mathrm{i}\rho_{a,\mathbf{k}}(k_{0})
, one gets

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{-}_{a}(p)

\displaystyle=c_{a}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p-k_{1}-k_{2})[1+f_{\rm B}(k_{1,0})+f_{\rm B}(k_{2,0})]\rho_{a,\mathbf{k}_{1}}(k_{0})\rho_{a,\mathbf{k}_{2}}(k_{2,0})

\displaystyle\equiv c_{a}I_{a}(p)\,,

(B71)

where we have defined the integral
I_{a}(p)
. Using Eq. (
B67
), one finally arrives at

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\widetilde{V}^{R}_{a}(p)\equiv-\frac{\mathrm{i}c_{a}}{2}I_{a}(p)\,.

(B72)

Substituting into the integral
I_{a}(p)
the free thermal spectral function

\displaystyle\rho_{a,\mathbf{k}}(k_{0})=2\pi{\rm sign}(k_{0})\delta(k_{0}^{2}-\omega_{a,\mathbf{k}}^{2})\,,

(B73)

where
\omega^{2}_{a,\mathbf{k}}=M_{a}^{2}+\mathbf{k}^{2}
, one obtains (see below for the detailed derivation)

\displaystyle I_{a}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta(p^{2}-4M_{a}^{2})}{8\pi|\mathbf{p}|}\left[\left(\Omega_{a,-}-\Omega_{a,+}\right)+2T\log\left(\frac{f_{\rm B}(\Omega_{a,-})}{f_{\rm B}(\Omega_{a,+})}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{\theta(-p^{2})}{8\pi|\mathbf{p}|}\left[-2\Omega_{a,+}+2T\log\left|\frac{f_{\rm B}(\Omega_{a,-})}{f_{\rm B}(\Omega_{a,+})}\right|\right]\,,

(B74)

where

\displaystyle\Omega_{a,\pm}=\frac{p_{0}}{2}\pm\frac{|{\mathbf{p}}|}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{(2M_{a})^{2}}{p^{2}}}\,.

(B75)

Taking
\mathbf{p}\to\mathbf{0}
, one obtains

I_{a}(p_{0},\mathbf{0})=\frac{1}{8\pi}\sqrt{1-\frac{4M_{a}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}}\theta\left(p_{0}^{2}-4M_{a}^{2}\right)\left(1+2f_{\rm B}\left(\frac{p_{0}}{2}\right)\right)\,.

(B76)

Therefore

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\widetilde{v}_{a}^{\rm R}(p_{0})={\rm Im}\widetilde{V}^{\rm R}_{a}(p_{0},\mathbf{0})=-\frac{\mathrm{i}c_{a}}{16\pi}\sqrt{1-\frac{4M_{a}^{2}}{p_{0}^{2}}}\theta\left(p_{0}^{2}-4M_{a}^{2}\right)\left(1+2f_{\rm B}\left(\frac{p_{0}}{2}\right)\right)\,.

(B77)

Taking further

and substituting the above into the definition of

, one obtains Eq. (
26

\displaystyle-2\pi\mathrm{i}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\beta M_{\phi}}-1\right)\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{k}_{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}|\mathbf{k}_{1}|\,|\mathbf{k}_{1}|^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int^{1}_{-1}\mathrm{d}\cos\theta

\displaystyle\times\frac{\delta(M_{\phi}+\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}-\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}}-\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{1}+\mathbf{k}_{2}})}{8\,\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}}\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{1}+\mathbf{k}_{2}}}\left[1+f_{\rm B}(\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}})\right]f_{\rm B}(\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}})f_{\rm B}(\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{1}+\mathbf{k}_{2}})\,.

(B156)

The
\delta
-function is satisfied when

\displaystyle M_{\phi}+\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}-\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}}-\sqrt{M^{2}_{\chi}+|\mathbf{k}_{1}|^{2}+|\mathbf{k}_{2}|^{2}+2|\mathbf{k}_{1}||\mathbf{k}_{2}|\cos\theta}=0\,.

(B157)

Since
-1\leq\cos\theta\leq 1
, Eq. (
B157

\displaystyle\sqrt{M^{2}_{\chi}+\left(|\mathbf{k}_{1}|-|\mathbf{k}_{2}|\right)^{2}}\leq M_{\phi}+\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}-\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}}\leq\sqrt{M^{2}_{\chi}+\left(|\mathbf{k}_{1}|+|\mathbf{k}_{2}|\right)^{2}}\,,

(B158)

which can be reduced to

\displaystyle\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}}\leq M_{\phi}+\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}\,,

(B159a)

\displaystyle\Big|(M_{\phi}+\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}-\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}})^{2}-(\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}}^{2}+\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}^{2}-M_{\phi}^{2})\Big|\leq 2|\mathbf{k}_{1}||\mathbf{k}_{2}|\,.

(B159b)

Taking the square of Eq. (
B159b
) gives

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}}^{2}+\Sigma_{2}\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}}+\Sigma_{3}\leq 0\,,

(B160)

where

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=4\left(M_{\phi}^{2}+M_{\chi}^{2}+2M_{\phi}\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}\right)\,,

(B161)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=-8M_{\phi}\left(M_{\phi}+\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}\right)^{2}\,,

(B162)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{3}

\displaystyle=4M_{\phi}^{2}\left(M^{2}_{\phi}-M_{\chi}^{2}+2M_{\phi}\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}+2\omega^{2}_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}\right)\,.

(B163)

Since
\Sigma_{1}>0

B160

\displaystyle\Omega^{-}_{1}\leq\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}}\leq\Omega^{+}_{1}\,,

(B164)

where

\displaystyle\Omega^{-}_{1}

\displaystyle=M_{\phi}\,,

(B165)

\displaystyle\Omega^{+}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{\phi}(M_{\phi}^{2}-M_{\chi}^{2}+2M_{\phi}\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}+2\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}^{2})}{M_{\phi}^{2}+M_{\chi}^{2}+2M_{\phi}\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}}\,.

(B166)

It can be shown that

\displaystyle\Omega^{+}_{1}\leq M_{\phi}+\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}-M_{\chi}\,,

(B167)

so that Eq. (
B159a
) is automatically satisfied when
\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}}\leq\Omega_{1}^{+}
.

In conclusion, the integral over
\cos\theta
in Eq. (

\displaystyle\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}\geq M_{\chi}\,,\qquad\Omega^{-}_{1}\leq\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}}\leq\Omega^{+}_{1}\,.

(B168)

Then, we have

\displaystyle J^{B}_{\chi}(M_{\phi},\mathbf{0})=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\beta M_{\phi}}-1\right)}{\mathrm{i}\,32\pi^{3}}\int^{\infty}_{M_{\chi}}\mathrm{d}\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}\int_{\Omega^{-}_{1}}^{{\Omega^{+}_{1}}}\mathrm{d}\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}}\left[1+f_{\rm B}(\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}})\right]f_{\rm B}(\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}})f_{\rm B}(M_{\phi}+\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}-\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}})\,.

(B169)

Doing the integral over
\omega_{\phi,\mathbf{k}_{1}}

\displaystyle J^{B}_{\chi}(M_{\phi},\mathbf{0})=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\beta M_{\phi}}-1}{\mathrm{i}\,32\pi^{3}\beta}\int^{\infty}_{M_{\chi}}\mathrm{d}\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}}[1+f_{\rm B}(\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}})]f_{\rm B}(M_{\phi}+\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\ln\left[\frac{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\beta\Omega^{+}_{1}}-1\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\beta(M_{\phi}+\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}})}-\mathrm{e}^{\beta\Omega_{1}^{-}}\right)}{\left(\mathrm{e}^{\beta(M_{\phi}+\omega_{\chi,\mathbf{k}_{2}})}-\mathrm{e}^{\beta\Omega_{1}^{+}}\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\beta\Omega^{-}_{1}}-1\right)}\right]\,.

(B170)

For

. For general

, the expressions for

are too complicated to evaluate a closed form of

Appendix C
Solving the condensate equation of motion with a time-dependent mass term

The condensate EoM (
11
) has been solved in Refs.
[
36
,
44
]
for constant
M_{\phi}
using multiple-scale analysis
[
34
,
35
]
. In this section, we solve it when
M_{\phi}
is time-dependent but adiabatic,
\dot{M}_{\phi}/M_{\phi}^{2}\ll 1
.

As a standard procedure for perturbation theory, one puts a
bookkeeping
parameter
\varepsilon
in front of all perturbatively small terms in the EoM. When doing the perturbative expansion, the higher the power of
\varepsilon
a quantity is associated with, the smaller the quantity is. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\ddot{\varphi}(t)+M_{\phi}^{2}\varphi(t)+3\varepsilon H\dot{\varphi}(t)+\varepsilon\frac{\lambda_{\phi}\varphi^{3}(t)}{6}+\varepsilon\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,\pi_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\varphi(t^{\prime})+\varepsilon\frac{\varphi(t)}{6}\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,v_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\varphi^{2}(t^{\prime})=0\,.

(C172)

There is a hierarchy in the time scales in the evolution of the condensate. The shorter time scale corresponds to the oscillation frequency, and the longer time scale corresponds to the damping rate. One can understand this by imaging a form of the condensate evolution
\varphi(t)=A(t)B(t)
where
B(t)
is an oscillating function and
A(t)
is the envelope function. One can expect that

\displaystyle M_{\phi}\sim\frac{1}{B(t)}\frac{\mathrm{d}B(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}\gg\frac{1}{A(t)}\frac{\mathrm{d}A(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}\,.

(C173)

Therefore, when taking time derivatives of
\varphi
, one can obtain quantities at different orders in magnitude. In order to trace these quantities, we thus formally introduce two different time variables. In the above example, one can write it as
\varphi(t,\tau)=A(\tau)B(t)
with
\tau=\varepsilon t
where
\varepsilon
, as in Eq. (
C172
), is only a bookkeeping parameter. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\varphi}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{\partial\varphi(t,\tau)}{\partial\tau}+\frac{\partial\varphi(t,\tau)}{\partial t}=\varepsilon\frac{\mathrm{d}A(\tau)}{\mathrm{d}\tau}B(t)+A(\tau)\frac{\mathrm{d}B(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}\,.

(C174)

This way, the smaller term is now associated with a factor
\varepsilon
. These two-time variables are used when solving the above equation for constant
M_{\phi}

[
36
,
44
]
.

Here we consider a slowly evolving
M_{\phi}(\tau)
where
\tau
is still the slow time variable. It turns out that in this case the fast time variable now is
[
35
]

\displaystyle\eta=f(t)=\int^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}M_{\phi}(\varepsilon t^{\prime})\,.

(C175)

We shall see shortly the advantage of introducing the
\eta
time variable. We will also assume
H=H(\tau)
.

Now let
\varphi
take the following form

\varphi(t)=\varphi(t,\tau;\varepsilon)=\varphi_{0}(t,\tau)+\varepsilon\varphi_{1}(t,\tau)+\varepsilon^{2}\varphi_{2}(t,\tau)+...\,,

(C176)

where

\varphi_{n}(t,\tau)=\frac{1}{n!}\frac{\partial^{n}\varphi(t,\tau;\varepsilon)}{\partial\varepsilon^{n}}\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}

(C177)

are the coefficients.
Below, we will still use the time variable
t
inside the integrals which then should be understood as
t=f^{-1}(\eta)
. Now one can directly expand the first four terms of Eq. (
C172
) in powers of
\varepsilon
.
To expand the non-local terms of Eq. (
C172
) in powers of
\varepsilon
, we need some simplification.
Assuming that the solution for the condensate
\varphi
varies only slightly in the slow time
\tau
during the window provided by the kernel of the non-local terms, we can Taylor-expand
\varphi(t^{\prime},\tau^{\prime};\varepsilon)
at
\tau
as

\displaystyle\varphi(t^{\prime},\tau^{\prime};\varepsilon)=\varphi(t^{\prime},\tau;\varepsilon)+\varepsilon(t^{\prime}-t)\frac{\partial\varphi(t^{\prime},\tau;\varepsilon)}{\partial\tau}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}(t^{\prime}-t)^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi(t^{\prime},\tau;\varepsilon)}{\partial\tau^{2}}+...\,.

(C178)

Indeed, from Eqs. (
B65
) and (
B55
), one can see that the scale of
the window is
1/M_{\phi}
while
(\mathrm{d}A(\tau)/\mathrm{d}\tau)\times(1/M_{\phi})\ll A(\tau)
(cf. Eq. (
C173
)).

Then, up to the second order in
\varepsilon
, the non-local terms could be written as

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{t_{i}}^{t}

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,\pi_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\varphi(t^{\prime},\tau^{\prime};\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,\pi_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\varphi(t^{\prime},\tau;\varepsilon)-\varepsilon^{2}\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,(t-t^{\prime})\pi_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\frac{\partial\varphi(t^{\prime},\tau;\varepsilon)}{\partial\tau}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})\,,

(C179)

and

\displaystyle\varepsilon\frac{\varphi(\eta,\tau;\varepsilon)}{6}\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,v_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\varphi^{2}(t^{\prime},\tau^{\prime};\varepsilon)=\varepsilon\frac{\varphi(\eta,\tau;\varepsilon)}{6}\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,v_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\varphi^{2}(t^{\prime},\tau;\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\varphi(\eta,\tau;\varepsilon)}{3}\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,(t-t^{\prime})v_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\varphi(t^{\prime},\tau;\varepsilon)\frac{\partial\varphi(t^{\prime},\tau;\varepsilon)}{\partial\tau}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})\,.

(C180)

Now, we are able to organise Eq. (
C172
) in powers of
\varepsilon
. At the leading order
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{0})
, we have

\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{0}(\eta,\tau)}{\partial\eta^{2}}+\varphi_{0}(t,\tau)=0\,,

(C181)

which has the solution

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(\tau,t)={\rm Re}\left[R(\tau)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\eta}\right]\,.

(C182)

Here, we see that using the time variable
\eta
, the leading-order equation takes the simple form of an EoM of a harmonic oscillator.

R(\tau)
can only be determined when one looks into the equation at the order
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
, which reads

\displaystyle M_{\phi}^{2}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{1}(\eta,\tau)}{\partial\eta^{2}}+\varphi_{1}(\eta,\tau)\right]=-2M_{\phi}(\tau)\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{0}(\eta,\tau)}{\partial\eta\partial\tau}-\frac{\mathrm{d}M_{\phi}(\tau)}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\frac{\partial\varphi_{0}(\eta,\tau)}{\partial\eta}-3H(\tau)M_{\phi}(\tau)\frac{\partial\varphi_{0}(\eta,\tau)}{\partial\eta}

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda_{\phi}}{6}\varphi_{0}^{3}(\eta,\tau)-\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,\pi_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\varphi_{0}(t^{\prime},\tau)-\frac{\varphi_{0}(\eta,\tau)}{6}\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,v_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\varphi_{0}^{2}(t^{\prime},\tau)\,.

(C183)

The non-local terms in Eq. (
C183
) can be written as

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,\pi_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\varphi_{0}(t^{\prime},\tau)=

\displaystyle-\text{Re}\left[R(\tau)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\eta}\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,\pi_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\int^{t}_{t^{\prime}}d\tilde{t}\,M_{\phi}(\varepsilon\tilde{t})}\right]\,,

(C184)

and

\displaystyle-\frac{\varphi_{0}(\eta,\tau)}{6}\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,v_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\varphi_{0}^{2}(t^{\prime},\tau)=-\frac{1}{12}\text{Re}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\eta}[R(\tau)]^{2}R^{*}(\tau)\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,v_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\frac{1}{24}\text{Re}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\eta}[R(\tau)]^{2}R^{*}(\tau)\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,v_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\int^{t}_{t^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\,M_{\phi}(\varepsilon\tilde{t})}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\frac{1}{24}\text{Re}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-3\mathrm{i}\eta}[R(\tau)]^{3}\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,v_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\int^{t}_{t^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\,M_{\phi}(\varepsilon\tilde{t})}\right]\,.

(C185)

Taking into account that the kernels of the non-local terms only extend over several oscillation periods, one can make further simplifications. First, one can neglect any early-time transient effects due to initial conditions in the non-local terms by taking
t_{i}\rightarrow-\infty
. Second, the following approximation could be made (also for the corresponding term with
v_{\rm R}
)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,\pi_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\int^{t}_{t^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\,M_{\phi}(\varepsilon\tilde{t})}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,\pi_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}M_{\phi}(\varepsilon t)(t-t^{\prime})}\,,

(C186)

provided that
M(\tau)
varies slightly within the extension of the kernels. Note that we could also replace
t
by
+\infty
in the upper limit of the integrals of
t^{\prime}
because of the Heaviside step function in the definitions of the retarded self-energy and the retarded four-vertex function (cf. Eq. (
B61
)).
Therefore, we could actually recognize the integrals on the RHS of Eqs. (
C184
) and (
C
) as Fourier transforms

\displaystyle\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,\pi_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\int^{t}_{t^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\,M_{\phi}(\varepsilon\tilde{t})}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,\pi_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}M_{\phi}(\tau)(t-t^{\prime})}=\widetilde{\pi}_{\rm R}(M_{\phi}(\tau))\,,

(C187a)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,v_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\approx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,v_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}0(t-t^{\prime})}=\widetilde{v}_{\rm R}(0)\,,

(C187b)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{i}}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,v_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\int^{t}_{t^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\,M_{\phi}(\varepsilon\tilde{t})}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\,v_{\rm R}(t-t^{\prime})\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}M_{\phi}(\tau)(t-t^{\prime})}=\widetilde{v}_{\rm R}(2M_{\phi}(\tau))\,.

(C187c)

Using the definitions given in Eq. (
2.3
),
Eq. (
C183
) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{1}(\eta,\tau)}{\partial\eta^{2}}+\varphi_{1}(\eta,\tau)=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{M^{2}_{\phi}}\text{Re}\Bigg\{\mathrm{i}M_{\phi}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\eta}\bigg[\frac{\mathrm{d}R(\tau)}{\mathrm{d}\tau}+\Big(\frac{3}{2}H(\tau)+\frac{1}{2M_{\phi}}\frac{\mathrm{d}M_{\phi}(\tau)}{\mathrm{d}\tau}+\mathrm{i}\mu+\gamma\Big)R(\tau)

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{\mathrm{i}\lambda_{\phi}}{16M_{\phi}}+\mathrm{i}\alpha+\frac{\sigma}{2}\Big)[R(\tau)]^{2}R^{*}(\tau)\bigg]\Bigg\}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{M^{2}_{\phi}}\text{Re}\Bigg\{\mathrm{i}M_{\phi}\mathrm{e}^{-3\mathrm{i}\eta}\bigg(\frac{\mathrm{i}\lambda_{\phi}}{48M_{\phi}}+\mathrm{i}\alpha_{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\bigg)[R(\tau)]^{3}\Bigg\}\,,

(C188)

where we used

\text{Im}[\widetilde{v}_{\rm R}(0)]=0\,,

(C189)

which is due to that the imaginary part of the Fourier-transformed proper four-vertex is anti-symmetric.

The EoM governing
\varphi_{1}(t,\tau)
describes a harmonic oscillator that is subject to two external oscillatory forces. The first two lines on the RHS of Eq. (
C188

M_{\phi}
, which aligns with the natural frequency of
\varphi_{1}(t,\tau)

\varphi_{1}(t,\tau)
being proportional to time

, causing an unbounded increase in amplitude. To circumvent the emergence of non-physical, spurious resonances in the solution, we require

\frac{\mathrm{d}R(\tau)}{\mathrm{d}\tau}+\Big(\frac{3}{2}H(\tau)+\frac{1}{2M_{\phi}}\frac{\mathrm{d}M_{\phi}(\tau)}{\mathrm{d}\tau}+\mathrm{i}\mu+\gamma\Big)R(\tau)+\Big(\frac{\mathrm{i}\lambda_{\phi}}{16M_{\phi}}+\mathrm{i}\alpha+\frac{\sigma}{2}\Big)[R(\tau)]^{2}R^{*}(\tau)=0\,.

(C190)

Making the following Ansatz

R(\tau)=A(\tau)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}f(\tau)}\,,

(C191)

where
A(\tau)
and

are real functions of
\tau

C190

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}A(\tau)}{\mathrm{d}\tau}+\left(\gamma+\frac{3}{2}H(\tau)+\frac{1}{2M_{\phi}}\frac{\mathrm{d}M_{\phi}(\tau)}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\right)A(\tau)+\frac{\sigma}{2}[A(\tau)]^{3}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(C192a)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}f(\tau)}{\mathrm{d}\tau}-\mu-\left(\frac{\lambda_{\phi}}{16M_{\phi}(t)}+\alpha\right)[A(\tau)]^{2}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(C192b)

Arriving here, one can take
\tau=t
.

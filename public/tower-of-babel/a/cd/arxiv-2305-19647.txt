Some properties of ℐ ∗ \mathcal{I}^{*} -sequential topological space
H. Sabor Behmanush, M. Küçükaslan

Abstract
. In this paper, we will define ℐ ∗ \mathcal{I}^{*} -sequential topology on a topological space ( X , τ ) (X,\tau) where ℐ \mathcal{I} is an ideal of the subset of natural numbers ℕ \mathbb{N} . Besides the basic properties of the ℐ ∗ \mathcal{I}^{*} -sequential topology, we proved that ℐ ∗ \mathcal{I}^{*} -sequential topology is finer than ℐ \mathcal{I} -sequential topology. Further, we will discus main properties of ℐ ∗ \mathcal{I}^{*} sequential continuity and ℐ ∗ \mathcal{I}^{*} sequential compactness.

Recall the notion of statistical convergence in a topological space. For any subset
A

\mathbb{N}

\delta(A):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left|\{k\in\mathbb{N}:k\leq n\}\right|

Let

be a topological space, a sequence
\tilde{x}=(x_{n})\subseteq X
is said to convergent statistically to a point

\delta(\{n\in\mathbb{N},x_{n}\notin U\})=0,

holds for any neighborhood
U
of

.

Equivalently, if

\delta(\{n\in\mathbb{N},x_{n}\in U\})=1,

holds. It is denoted by

st-\lim_{x\to\infty}x_{n}=x\ or\ x_{n}\overset{st}{\rightarrow}x

For a sequence
\tilde{x}=(x_{n})
, let a subset
U
of
X
, denote by

A_{U}(\tilde{x}):=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:x_{n}\notin U\}

which is also denoted by

.

It is easy to see in

Now, we recall the concept of sequential topological space, let

Definition 5

Theorem 1

Let

A sequence

\mathcal{I}_{Fin}

Proof.

Let
x_{n}=t_{n}+s_{n}
, for all

where
t_{n}{\rightarrow}x(\mathcal{I}_{Fin})
and
(s_{n})
is non zero only on set from ideal
\mathcal{I}
. Since
t_{n}{\rightarrow}x(\mathcal{I}_{Fin})
, then for any neighborhood
U
of

\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\ t_{n}\notin U\}\notin F_{in}

holds. Let

M=\mathbb{N}-\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\ t_{n}\notin U\}.

Then,
s_{n}=0
for all

and this implies that for any neighborhood

x_{n}\in U
holds for all

x_{n}\overset{I^{*}}{\rightarrow}x

Conversely, let

M\in F(\mathcal{I})
and

such that

holds for all

Define the sequence

and the sequence

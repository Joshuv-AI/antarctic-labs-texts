Existence and multiplicity of solutions to magnetic Kirchhoff equations in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
Pablo Ochoa

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the existence and multiplicity of weak solutions to a general type of Kirchhoff equations in magnetic fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. Specifically, we appeal to Critical Point Theory to prove the existence of non-trivial solutions under the so-called Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition. We also state the existence of ground-state solutions. Moreover, multiplicity results which yield the existence of an unbounded sequence of solutions are also provided. Finally, we show existence under a weak-type Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition formulated in the framework of Orlicz spaces.

1.
Introduction

In this work, we consider magnetic Kirchhoff equations involving the fractional operator
\left(-\Delta_{g}^{A}\right)^{s}
defined for a smooth function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{C}
as

(1.1)

\left(-\Delta_{g}^{A}\right)^{s}u(x):=\text{P.V. }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{g\left(|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|\right)}{|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|}D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)\dfrac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+s}}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
s\in(0,1)
,
A:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a given vector field,
g=G^{\prime}
is the derivative of an N-function, and

D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)=\dfrac{u(x)-e^{i(x-y)\cdot A\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)}u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}.

Also, P. V. stands for ‘in principal value’. We refer the reader to Section
2
for details regarding
N
-functions. The operator
\left(-\Delta_{g}^{A}\right)^{s}
is called the magnetic fractional
g
-Laplacian and it arises as the gradient of the non-local magnetic energy functional:

\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}G\left(|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|\right)\,d\mu,

in the sense that

\lim_{t\to 0}\dfrac{\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u+tv)-\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u)}{t}=\mathcal{R}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\dfrac{g\left(|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|\right)}{|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|}D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)\overline{D_{s}^{A}v(x,y)}\,d\mu\right)=\left\langle\left(-\Delta_{g}^{A}\right)^{s}u,v\right\rangle.

Here, we use the notation

d\mu=\dfrac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{N}}.

Moreover, according to
[
14
, Theorem 1.4]
, the operator
\left(-\Delta_{g}^{A}\right)^{s}
may be seen as an approximation of the local operator
-\Delta_{\overline{g}}^{A}
arising as the gradient of the energy functional

\mathcal{I}_{\overline{G}}^{A}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\overline{G}\left(|\nabla u-iAu|\right)dx,

where
\overline{g}=\overline{G}^{\prime}
and
\overline{G}
is the spherical limit of
G
(see
[
14
]
). For instance, when
G(t)=|t|^{p}
,
p>1
, the spherical limit of
G
is
\overline{G}(t)=K_{N,p}|t|^{p}
, where
K_{N,p}:=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|x_{n}|^{p}d\sigma/p
. More examples are given in
[
15
]
. The approximation is in the following sense: for each
0<s<1
and appropriate
f
, let
u_{s}
be the unique solution of

\left(-\Delta_{g}^{A}\right)^{s}=f\quad\text{in }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad u_{s}=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,

then as
s\to 1
,
u_{s}\to u
in
L^{G}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})
, where
u
satisfies the local equation

-\Delta_{\overline{g}}^{A}u=f,\quad\text{in }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad u=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

When
G(t)=|t|^{2}
, the derivative of the corresponding energy functional
\mathcal{I}_{\overline{G}}^{A}
gives rise to the (real part of the) magnetic Schrödinger operator defined as

(1.2)

-(\nabla-iA)^{2}u(x)=-\Delta u(x)+2iA(x)\cdot\nabla u+|A(x)|^{2}u+iu\text{ div}A(x).

The magnetic Schrödinger operator has been studied extensively in the last decades. We refer the reader to the references
[

[

Observe that when

(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u(x)=\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g\Big(\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\Big)\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+s}}

for a sufficiently smooth function

. Observe that when

, we get the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
. Problems with non local diffusion that involve integro-differential operators
have been intensively studied in the last years. These nonlocal operators
appear when we model different physical situations as anomalous diffusion and

In this work, we consider global Kirchhoff magnetic equations in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces of the form:

(1.3)

M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u)\right)\left(-\Delta_{g}^{A}\right)^{s}u+V(x)\dfrac{g(|u|)}{|u|}u=f(x,|u|)u\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is the potential,

is a given function, and
M:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the Kirchhoff (tension) function. Kirchhoff
[
23

introduced a model for a vibrating string given by

\rho\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}u-\left(\frac{\rho_{0}}{h}+\frac{E}{2L}\int_{0}^{L}\big|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\big|\right)\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}u=0,

where
L
is the length of the string,

the area of the cross-section, E is the Young modulus of the material,
\rho
is the mass density, and
\rho_{0}
the initial tension. That equation constitutes an extension of the D’Alembert’s classical wave equation, since it is taken into account the changes in length of the string under vibration. There have been further generalizations of the Kirchhoff model. In particular, we quote the nice article
[
20
]
, where the authors studied the existence of non-negative weak solutions for a nonlocal Kirchhoff type problem of the form

M\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy\right)(-\Delta)^{s}u=\lambda f(x,u)+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u.

In this case, the tension
M
depends on the length of the string via the fractional norm of

.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
N-functions and basic properties

In this section we introduce basic definitions and preliminary results related to Orlicz spaces. We start recalling the definition of an N-function.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
G\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called an N-function if it admits the representation

G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)d\tau,

where the function
g
is right-continuous for
t\geq 0
, positive for
t>0
, non-decreasing and satisfies the conditions

g(0)=0,\quad g(\infty)=\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)=\infty.

By
[
24
, Chapter 1]
, an N-function has also the following properties:

(1)

G is continuous, convex, increasing, even and
G(0)=0
.

(2)

G is super-linear at zero and at infinite, that is

\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{G(x)}{x}=0

and

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{G(x)}{x}=\infty.

Indeed, the above conditions serve as an equivalent definition of N-functions.

An important property for N-functions is the following:

Definition 2.2
.

We say that the N-function G satisfies the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition if there exists
C>2
such that

G(2x)\leq CG(x)\,\,\text{~~~for all~~}x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Examples of functions satisfying the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition are:

•

G(t)=t^{p}
,
t\geq 0
,
p>1
;

•

G(t)=t^{p}(|\log(t)|+1)
,
t\geq 0
,
p>1
;

•

G(t)=(1+|t|)\log(1+|t|)-|t|
;

•

G(t)=t^{p}\chi_{(0,1]}(t)+t^{q}\chi_{(1,\infty)}(t)
,
t\geq 0
,
p,q>1
.

According to
[
24
, Theorem 4.1, Chapter 1]
, a necessary and sufficient condition for an N-function to satisfy the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition is that there is
p^{+}>1
such that

(2.1)

\frac{tg(t)}{G(t)}\leq p^{+},~~~~~\forall\,t>0.

In particular, (
2.1
) implies that an N-function satisfying the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition does not increase more rapidly than a power function (see (
2.8
) below). Consequently, the following N-function does not satisfies the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition:

G(t)=e^{|t|}-|t|-1.

Associated to
G
is the N-function complementary to it which is defined as follows:

(2.2)

\widetilde{G}(t):=\sup\left\{tw-G(w)\colon w>0\right\}.

Moreover, the following representation holds for
\widetilde{G}
:

\widetilde{G}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g^{-1}(s)\,ds,

where
g^{-1}
is the right-continuous inverse of
g
. We recall that the role played by
\widetilde{G}
is the same as the conjugate exponent functions when
G(t)=t^{p}
,
p>1
.

The definition of the complementary function assures that the following Young-type inequality holds

(2.3)

at\leq G(t)+\widetilde{G}(a)\text{ for every }a,t\geq 0.

We also quote the following useful lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
15
, Lemma 2.9]

Let
G
be an N-function. If
G
satisfies (
2.1
) then

\tilde{G}(g(t))\leq(p^{+}-1)G(t),

where
g=G^{\prime}
and
\tilde{G}
is the complementary function of
G.

By
[
24
, Theorem 4.3, Chapter 1]
, a necessary and sufficient condition for the N-function
\widetilde{G}
complementary to
G
to satisfy the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition is that there is
p^{-}>1
such that

(2.4)

p^{-}\leq\frac{tg(t)}{G(t)},~~~~~\forall\,t>0.

From now on, we will assume that the N-function
G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)d\tau
satisfies the following growth behaviour:

(2.5)

1<p^{-}\leq\frac{tg(t)}{G(t)}\leq p^{+}<\infty,~~~~~\forall t>0.

Hence,
G
and
\widetilde{G}
both satisfy the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition. Extending
g
to the whole of
\mathbb{R}
by setting
g(t)=-g(-t)
for
t\leq 0
, we get that (
2.5
) holds for all
t\in\mathbb{R},\,t\neq 0.
Another useful condition which is assumed in the manuscript is the following:

(2.6)

p^{-}-1\leq\dfrac{tg^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\leq p^{+}-1,~~~~~\forall t>0.

Again, (
2.6
) holds for any
t\in\mathbb{R},\,t\neq 0.
Observe that (
2.6
) implies (
2.5
). An example of an N-function satisfying (
2.6
) is

G(t)=t^{p}(|\log(t)|+1),\quad p>\tfrac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}.

From (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), there follows the next comparison of N-functions with power functions:

(2.7)

{\min\left\{a^{p^{-}-1},a^{p^{+}-1}\right\}g(b)\leq g(ab)\leq\max\left\{a^{p^{-}-1},a^{p^{+}-1}\right\}g(b),}

and

(2.8)

\min\left\{a^{p^{-}},a^{p^{+}}\right\}G(b)\leq G(ab)\leq\max\left\{a^{p^{-}},a^{p^{+}}\right\}G(b),

for all
a,b
, where we use the convention
t^{\alpha}=|t|^{\alpha-1}t
.

Given two
N
-functions
A
and
B
, we say that
B
is essentially larger than
A
, denoted by

A\ll B,
if for any
c>0
,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{A(ct)}{B(t)}=0.

Main assumptions

In this section, we state the main assumptions for the data of problem (

We start with additional assumptions for the N-function

(HG)_{1}
Assume

In connection with

Remark 2.4
.

Observe that when
G(t)=t^{p}
,

,
(HG)_{2}
holds when

.

Regarding the function

, we will assume:

(Hf)_{1}
The function

is a Carathéodory function such that

(Hf)_{2}
There exist a constant

(Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition) Let

Then there exists

such that

below.

Remark 2.5
.

i) The assumption
(Hf)_{1}
implies that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is
\delta>0
such that

|f(x,t)|\leq\varepsilon g^{\prime}(t)\quad\text{ if }|t|\leq\delta.

Now if

, we have

(2.9)

\begin{split}|f(x,t)|\leq c_{0}(1+b^{\prime}(t))\leq c_{0}b^{\prime}(t)\left(1+\dfrac{1}{b^{\prime}(t)}\right)\leq c_{0}b^{\prime}(t)\left(1+\dfrac{1}{(p_{B}^{-}-1)\delta^{p_{B}^{+}-2}}\right).\end{split}

As a result,

|f(x,t)|\leq\varepsilon g^{\prime}(t)+C_{\varepsilon}b^{\prime}(t),\text{ for all }t.

ii) Observe that the condition

in

implies for

\dfrac{B(t)}{G(\lambda t)}\leq\dfrac{t^{p_{B}^{-}}}{\lambda^{p^{+}}t^{p^{+}}},

For

, we will suppose the following:

The function

(HM)_{3}
There is

Assumption

The assumptions on the potential

2.3.
Functional framework

2.4.
Lebesgue and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

We recall the notation

d\mu=\dfrac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{N}}

and we introduce

D_{s}u(x,y):=\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
G
be an N-function,
s\in(0,1)
and
V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable and bounded function. We define Lebesgue-Orlicz spaces as follows:

L^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}):=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{C},\,\text{u is measurable and }\rho_{G}(u)<\infty\right\}

and

L_{V}^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}):=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{C},\,\text{u is measurable and }\rho_{G,V}(u)<\infty\right\},

where

\rho_{G}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(|u(x)|)\,dx,\quad\text{and }\quad\rho_{G,V}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(|u(x)|)V(x)\,dx.

The spaces
L^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
and
L_{V}^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
are equipped with the Luxemburg norms:

\|u\|_{G}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\rho_{G}\left(\dfrac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}

and

\|u\|_{G,V}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\rho_{G,V}\left(\dfrac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\},

respectively. The fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces are given as

W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})=:\left\{u\in L^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}),\rho_{s,G}(u)<\infty\right\},

and

W^{s,G}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})=:\left\{u\in L_{V}^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}),\rho_{s,G}(u)<\infty\right\},

where

\rho_{s,G}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}G(|D_{s}u(x,y)|)\,d\mu.

The norms in
W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
and in
W_{V}^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
are

\|u\|_{s,G}:=\|u\|_{G}+[u]_{s,G}\quad\text{and }\quad\|u\|_{s,G}:=\|u\|_{G,V}+[u]_{s,G},

where

[u]_{s,G}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\rho_{s,G}\left(\dfrac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

For simplicity, when the above spaces are restricted to real-valued functions, we will omit
\mathbb{C}
in their notation.

The continuity of the embedding
W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
in
L^{B}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is provided in the next theorem from
[
9
, Theorem 2]

Theorem 2.8

Lemma 2.9
.

Assume that
U=U(x,y)\in L_{\mu}^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})
and
V=V(x,y)\in L_{\mu}^{\tilde{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})
. Then,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}U(x,y)V(x,y)\,d\mu\leq 2[U]_{s,G}[V]_{s,\tilde{G}}.

Proof.

By definition,

[U]_{s,G}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}G\left(\dfrac{|U(x,y)|}{\lambda}\right)\,d\mu\leq 1\right\}.

In particular,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}G\left(\dfrac{|U(x,y)|}{[U]_{s,G}}\right)\,d\mu\leq 1.

A similar inequality holds for

\dfrac{U(x,y)}{[U]_{s,G}}.\dfrac{V(x,y)}{[V]_{s,G}}\leq G\left(\dfrac{U(x,y)}{[U]_{s,G}}\right)+\tilde{G}\left(\dfrac{V(x,y)}{[V]_{s,G}}\right),

and integrating, we obtain

(2.10)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}U(x,y)V(x,y)\,d\mu&\leq[U]_{s,G}.[V]_{s,G}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}G\left(\dfrac{U(x,y)}{[U]_{s,G}}\right)\,d\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}G\left(\dfrac{V(x,y)}{[V]_{s,G}}\right)\,d\mu\right)\\
&\leq 2[U]_{s,G}.[V]_{s,G}.\end{split}

2.5.
Magnetic spaces

For a given vector field
A:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we recall

D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)=\dfrac{u(x)-e^{i(x-y)\cdot A\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)}u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
G
be an N-function,
s\in(0,1)
and
A:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a smooth vector field. We define magnetic fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces as follows:

W_{A}^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}):=\left\{u\in L^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}):\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u)<\infty\right\}

and the weighted space

W_{A,V}^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}):=\left\{u\in L_{V}^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}):\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u)<\infty\right\},

where

\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}G\left(|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|\right)\,d\mu.

The spaces
W_{A}^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
and
W_{A,V}^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
are Banach spaces when equipped with the Luxemburg norms:

\|u\|^{A}_{s,G}:=\|u\|_{G}+[u]_{s,G}^{A}

and

\|u\|^{A}_{s,G,V}:=\|u\|_{G,V}+[u]_{s,G}^{A}

where the seminorm
[u]_{s,G}^{A}
is given by

[u]_{s,G}^{A}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\rho_{s,G}^{A}\left(\dfrac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

The relation between magnetic fractional spaces and
W^{s,G}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is stated in the next theorem. The proof of the theorem relies on the following diamagnetic inequality:

(2.11)

||u(x)|-|u(y)||\leq\bigg|u(x)-e^{i(x-y)\cdot A\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)}u(y)\bigg|,

which is valid for any finite and measurable magnetic potential field
A
and any finite and measurable complex function
u
(for the proof see
[
13
, Lemma 3.1 and Remark 3.2]
). The meaning of (
2.11
) is that removing the magnetic field (
A=0
) allows to decrease the kinetic energy by replacing
u
with
|u|
(keeping
|u|^{2}
unaltered).

Theorem 2.12
.

The embedding
W^{s,G}_{V,A}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})\hookrightarrow W^{s,G}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is continuous in the sense that if
u\in W^{s,G}_{V,A}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
then
|u|\in W^{s,G}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\||u|\|_{s,G,V}\leq\|u\|_{s,G,V}^{A}.

We also point out that since
V_{0}=\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V>0
, it follows that

\||u|\|_{s,G}\leq C\||u|\|_{s,G,V},

for some
C>0
. Indeed, assume without loss of generality that
V_{0}<1
. Let
\lambda>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}B\left(\dfrac{|u|}{\lambda}\right)V(x)\,dx\leq 1.

Then,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}B\left(\dfrac{|u|}{V_{0}^{-1/p_{B}^{-}}\lambda}\right)\,dx&=V_{0}\left(\dfrac{1}{V_{0}}\right)^{p_{B}^{-}/p_{B}^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}B\left(\dfrac{|u|}{V_{0}^{-1/p_{B}^{-}}\lambda}\right)\,dx\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}B\left(\dfrac{|u|}{\lambda}\right)V(x)\,dx\leq 1\qquad(\text{by }\eqref{G product}).\end{split}

Thus,

\||u|\|_{s,G}\leq V_{0}^{-1/p_{B}^{-}}\||u|\|_{s,G,V}.

Hence, the embedding
W^{s,G}_{V,A}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})\hookrightarrow W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is also continuous.

Finally, we mention the following optimal fractional Sobolev inequality from
[
1
]
. Assume that the N-function
G
satisfies:

\int^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{t}{G(t)}\right)^{\frac{s}{N-s}}\,dt=\infty

and

\int_{0}\left(\dfrac{t}{G(t)}\right)^{\frac{s}{N-s}}\,dt<\infty.

The optimal N-function for the compact embedding is defined as

G_{N/s}(t)=G(H^{-1}(t)),\quad t\geq 0

where

H(t)=\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\tau}{G(\tau)}\right)^{\frac{s}{N-s}}\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{N-s}{N}},\quad t\geq 0.

The optimal embedding is the next result
[
1
, Theorem 6.1]
.

Theorem 2.13
.

The following embedding is continuous:

W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{G_{N/s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Moreover, there is a constant
C=C(s,N)>0
such that

\|u\|_{G_{N/s}}\leq C[u]_{s,G}.

The result is optimal in the sense that if the embedding also holds for another Orlicz function, then the space
L^{G_{N/s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is continuously embedded into the later.

2.7.
Notion of solutions

We start with the definition of weak solutions to (
1.3
).

Definition 2.16
.

We say that
u\in W_{A,V}^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
is a weak solution of the equation (
1.3
) if for any
v\in W_{A,V}^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
, there holds

(2.12)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{R}\bigg[M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u)\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\dfrac{g\left(|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|\right)}{|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|}D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)\overline{D_{s}^{A}v(x,y)}\,d\mu\\
&\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{g(|u|)}{|u|}u\overline{v}V(x)\,dx\bigg]=\mathcal{R}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,|u|)u\overline{v}\,dx\bigg].\end{split}

As a motivation for the definition of weak solutions, consider first the following integral functional
\mathcal{I}:W_{A,V}^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(2.13)

\mathcal{I}(u):=\mathcal{M}\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u)\right)+\rho_{G,V}(u)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,|u|)\,dx,\quad u\in W_{A,V}^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}).

Then calculating the Gateaux derivatives, we obtain:

\dfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}\mathcal{M}\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u+\varepsilon v)\right)\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=\mathcal{R}\bigg[M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u)\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\dfrac{g\left(|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|\right)}{|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|}D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)\overline{D_{s}^{A}v(x,y)}\,d\mu\bigg],

\dfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}\rho_{G,V}(u+\varepsilon v)\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=\mathcal{R}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{g(|u|)}{|u|}u\overline{v}V(x)\,dx\bigg]

and

\dfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,|u+\varepsilon v|)\,dx\right)\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=\mathcal{R}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,|u|)u\overline{v}\,dx\bigg].

Hence,
u\in W_{A,V}^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
is a weak solution of
(
1.3
)
if and only if
u
is a critical point of
\mathcal{I}
.

Moreover, observe that all the terms in (
2.12
) are well-defined. Indeed, we first have

(2.14)

\begin{split}&\bigg|\dfrac{g\left(|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|\right)}{|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|}D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)\overline{D_{s}^{A}v(x,y)}\bigg|\leq g\left(|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|\right).|D_{s}^{A}v(x,y)|\\
&\qquad\leq\tilde{G}\left(g\left(|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|\right)\right)+G\left(|D_{s}^{A}v(x,y)|\right)\\
&\qquad\leq G\left(|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|\right)+G\left(|D_{s}^{A}v(x,y)|\right)\in L^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{2N}),\end{split}

where we have used (
2.3
) in the second line and Lemma
2.3
in the third line. Regarding the second term in (
2.12
), it is finite by Holder’s inequality since
g(|u|)\in L_{V}^{\tilde{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
v\in L_{V}^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Finally, for
\varepsilon>0
, we get from Remark
2.5

(2.15)

\begin{split}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,|u|)u\overline{v}\,dx\bigg|&\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g^{\prime}(|u|)|u||v|\,dx+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}b^{\prime}(|u|)|u||v|\,dx\\
&\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(p^{+}-1)g(|u|)|v|\,dx+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(p_{B}^{+}-1)b(|u|)|v|\,dx\qquad(\text{by }\eqref{cG0})\\
&=I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

Since
g(|u|)\in L^{\tilde{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by Lemma
2.3
, Hölder’s inequality implies that
I_{1}
is finite. Regarding
I_{2}
, we have by Young’s inequality (
2.3
) that

|I_{2}|\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\tilde{B}(b(|u|))+B(|u|)\right)\,dx\leq C\rho_{B}(u).

The last term is finite because of the embedding
W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})\hookrightarrow L^{B}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

3.
Existence of solutions: proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section we will prove the existence of solutions to (
1.3
). It will be enough to prove that
\mathcal{I}
has critical points. In order to state that, we shall establish first some general properties of the functional
\mathcal{I}
.

Throughout the section we assume
(HG)_{1}-(HG)_{2}
,
(HM)_{1}-(HM)_{2}
and
(Hf)_{1}-(Hf)_{3}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The functional
\mathcal{I}
belongs to the class
C^{1}(W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Given
u\in W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
, the Gateaux derivative of
\mathcal{I}
at
u
is

\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)=\mathcal{I}_{1}^{\prime}(u)+\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\prime}(u)+\mathcal{I}_{3}^{\prime}(u),

where

\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{1}^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle=\mathcal{R}\bigg[M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u)\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\dfrac{g\left(|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|\right)}{|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|}D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)\overline{D_{s}^{A}v(x,y)}\,d\mu\bigg],

\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle=\mathcal{R}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{g(|u|)}{|u|}u\overline{v}V(x)\,dx\bigg]

and

\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle=\mathcal{R}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,|u|)u\overline{v}\,dx\bigg].

Assume that
u_{n}\to u
in
W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
. Then
|u_{n}|\to|u|
in
W_{V}^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Let
u_{n_{k}}
be any subsequence of
u_{n}
. By Proposition
7.2
, there is
h\in L^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L_{V}^{B}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and a further subsequence, that we do not relabel, such that

(3.1)

|u_{n_{k}}|\leq h\quad a.e.

We shall prove the continuity of
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{2}
. The argument is similar for
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{1}
. Suppose that
\|v\|_{s,G,V}^{A}\leq 1
, we have

\bigg|\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\prime}(u_{n_{k}})-\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle\bigg|=\bigg|\mathcal{R}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\dfrac{g(|u_{n_{k}}|)}{|u_{n_{k}}|}u_{n_{k}}-\dfrac{g(|u|)}{|u|}u\right)\overline{v}V(x)\,dx\bigg]\bigg|.

Observe that by Lemma
2.3
,

\rho_{\tilde{G}}\left(\dfrac{g(|u_{n_{k}}|)}{|u_{n_{k}}|}u_{n_{k}}\right)\leq C\rho_{G}(u_{n_{k}})<\infty.

Hence, by Hölder’s inequality, we get

(3.2)

\bigg|\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\prime}(u_{n_{k}})-\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle\bigg|\leq C\bigg\|\bigg|\dfrac{g(|u_{n_{k}}|)}{|u_{n_{k}}|}u_{n_{k}}-\dfrac{g(|u|)}{|u|}u\bigg|\bigg\|_{\tilde{G},V}.\||v|\|_{G,V}.

Since

\dfrac{g(|u_{n_{k}}|)}{|u_{n_{k}}|}u_{n_{k}}-\dfrac{g(|u|)}{|u|}u\to 0\quad a.e.

and

\bigg|\dfrac{g(|u_{n_{k}}|)}{|u_{n_{k}}|}u_{n_{k}}-\dfrac{g(|u|)}{|u|}u\bigg|\leq g(h)+g(|u|)\in L^{\tilde{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

by Lebesgue’s Theorem we get

\rho_{\tilde{G}}\left(\dfrac{g(|u_{n_{k}}|)}{|u_{n_{k}}|}u_{n_{k}}-\dfrac{g(|u|)}{|u|}u\right)\to 0\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Hence, (
3.2
) yields the convergence
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{2}(u_{n_{k}})\to\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{2}(u)
. Since this holds for any subsequence, we prove the convergence of the whole sequence
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{2}(u_{n})\to\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{2}(u)
.

Let us prove the continuity of
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{3}
. As before, we consider an arbitrary subsequence
u_{n_{k}}
and we assume, without loss of generality, that (
3.1
) holds. Then, for
c>0
to be chosen later,

(3.3)

\begin{split}\bigg|\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{3}^{\prime}(u_{n_{k}})-\mathcal{I}_{3}^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle\bigg|&=\int_{\left\{h<c\right\}}|f(x,|u_{n_{k}}|)u_{n_{k}}-f(x,|u|)u||v|\,dx+\\
&+\int_{\left\{h\geq c\right\}}|f(x,|u_{n_{k}}|)u_{n_{k}}-f(x,|u|)u||v|\,dx.\end{split}

We let
A_{1}=\left\{h<c\right\}
and
A_{2}=\left\{h\geq c\right\}
. Since
G\ll B
and

\lim_{|t|\to 0}\dfrac{B(t)}{G(t)}=0,

we have that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{g(t)}{b(t)}=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{|t|\to 0}\dfrac{b(t)}{g(t)}=0.

Therefore, we may choose
c>0
such that

(3.4)

g(t)\leq b(t)\text{ for }t>c.

Moreover, there is
\delta>0
,
\delta<c
, such that

b(t)\leq g(t)\text{ for }t\in[0,\delta].

Hence, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\delta
and
c
such that

(3.5)

b(t)\leq g(t)+Cg(t)\chi_{[\delta,c]}

for any
t\in[0,c]
, and where
\chi_{[\delta,c]}
is the characteristic of the interval
[\delta,c]
.

Over the set
A_{1}
, we then have by (
3.5
) and Remark
2.5
that

|f(x,|u|)u|\leq C\varepsilon g^{\prime}(|u|)|u|+C_{\varepsilon}b^{\prime}(|u|)|u|\leq C_{\varepsilon}g(h)+C_{\varepsilon}g(h)\leq Cb(h).

Hence,
|f(x,|u|)|u||\in L^{\tilde{G}}(A_{1})
and it is dominated by
Cb(h)
. Similarly,

|f(x,|u_{n_{k}}|)u_{n_{k}}|\in L^{\tilde{G}}(A_{1}).

Moreover, over the set
A_{2}
, we appeal to (
3.4
) to conclude that

|f(x,|u|)u|,|f(x,|u_{n_{k}}|)u_{n_{k}}|\in L^{\tilde{B}}(A_{2})

and they are majorized by
Cb(h)
. Hence, applying Hölder’s inequality in (
3.3
) gives

(3.6)

\begin{split}\bigg|\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{3}^{\prime}(u_{n_{k}})-\mathcal{I}_{3}^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle\bigg|&\leq C\bigg(\||f(x,|u_{n_{k}}|)u_{n_{k}}-f(x,|u|)u|\|_{\tilde{G},A_{1}}\||v|\|_{G,A_{1}}\\
&+\||f(x,|u_{n_{k}}|)u_{n_{k}}-f(x,|u|)u|\|_{\tilde{B},A_{2}}\||v|\|_{B,A_{2}}\bigg).\end{split}

Finally, by Lebesgue’s Theorem, it follows that
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{3}(u_{n_{k}})\to\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{3}(u)
. This ends the proof.

The following is the definition of the Palais-Smale condition (PS in brief) introduced in
[
27
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that the functional
\mathcal{I}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition if any sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}\subset W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
such that

\left\{\mathcal{I}(u_{n})\right\}
is bounded and
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, admits a (strongly) convergent subsequence in
W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
.

In the next lemma we prove that
\mathcal{I}
satisfies the PS-condition.

Lemma 3.3
.

The functional
\mathcal{I}
satisfies the PS-condition in
W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

Let
\left\{u_{n}\right\}\subset W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
such that

\left\{\mathcal{I}(u_{n})\right\}
is bounded and
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. We take
C>0
such that

(3.7)

|\mathcal{I}(u_{n})|\leq C\quad\text{and }\quad|\left\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n}),u_{n}\right\rangle|\leq C\|u_{n}\|^{A}_{s,G,V}.

First, we suppose that there is
d>0
such that

(3.8)

d=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}[u_{n}]_{s,G}^{A}.

Then, we have according to (
3.7
) that

(3.9)

\begin{split}C+C\|u_{n}\|^{A}_{s,G,V}&\geq\mathcal{I}(u_{n})-\frac{1}{\mu}\left\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n}),u_{n}\right\rangle\quad(\text{recall }\mu>1)\\
&\geq\mathcal{M}\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})\right)-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})\right)\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})+\left(1-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\rho_{G,V}(u_{n})\quad(\text{by }\eqref{G1})\\
&-\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\mu F(x,|u_{n}|)-f(x,|u_{n}|)|u_{n}|^{2}\right)\,dx\\
&\geq\frac{1}{\theta}M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})\right)\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})\right)\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})\\
&+\left(1-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\rho_{G,V}(u_{n})\qquad(\text{by }(HM)_{2}\text{ and }(Hf)_{3})\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{\theta}-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})\right)\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})+\left(1-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\rho_{G,V}(u_{n})\\
&\geq\delta\left(\frac{1}{\theta}-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})+\left(1-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\rho_{G,V}(u_{n})\quad(\text{by }(HM)_{2}\text{ with }\delta=\delta(d)).\end{split}

Next, observe that if
\|u_{n_{k}}\|_{s,G,V}^{A}\to\infty
for a subsequence, then we may distinguish the following cases. We assume first that
[u_{n_{k}}]_{s,G}^{A}
is unbounded and
\|u_{n_{k}}\|_{G,V}
is bounded. By (
3.9
), we get

(3.10)

\begin{split}C+C\|u_{n_{k}}\|^{A}_{s,G,V}&\geq\delta\left(\frac{1}{\theta}-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\left([u_{n_{k}}]_{s,G}^{A}\right)^{p^{-}}+\left(1-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\min\left\{\|u_{n_{k}}\|^{p^{-}}_{G,V},\|u_{n_{k}}\|^{p^{+}}_{G,V}\right\}.\end{split}

Dividing (
3.10
) by
[u_{n_{k}}]_{s,G}^{A}
and letting
k\to\infty
, we get a contradiction. A similar reasoning applies when
\|u_{n_{k}}\|_{G,V}
is unbounded and
[u_{n_{k}}]_{s,G}^{A}
is bounded. Finally, if
\|u_{n_{k}}\|^{A}_{s,G,V}
and
\|u_{n_{k}}\|_{G,V}
are simultaneously unbounded, we get
from (

) that

C+C\|u_{n_{k}}\|^{A}_{s,G,V}\geq 2^{1-p^{-}}\min\left\{\delta\left(\frac{1}{\theta}-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right),\left(1-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\right\}\left(\|u_{n_{k}}\|^{A}_{s,G,V}\right)^{p^{-}},

which yields a contradiction as
k\to\infty
.

Hence, we obtain that
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is bounded in
W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
. So, there is
u\in W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
so that, up to a subsequence that we do not re label,
u_{n}-u\rightharpoonup 0
in
W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
. By Theorem
2.12
and Theorems
2.14
and
2.15
, we have that

(3.11)

|u_{n}|\to|u|\text{ in }L_{V}^{B}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\text{and }\,\,u_{n}\to u\text{ in }L^{G}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}),

with
B
from the assumption
(Hf)_{2}
. In particular,
u_{n}\to u
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Next, define the linear functionals

\left\langle F_{1}(w),v\right\rangle:=\mathcal{R}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\dfrac{g\left(|D_{s}^{A}w(x,y)|\right)}{|D_{s}^{A}w(x,y)|}D_{s}^{A}w(x,y)\overline{D_{s}^{A}v(x,y)}\,d\mu\right]

and

for

so that we may write

\left\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(w),v\right\rangle=M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(w)\right)\left\langle F_{1}(w),v\right\rangle+\left\langle F_{2}(w),v\right\rangle-\mathcal{R}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,|w|)w\overline{v}\,dx\right].

Observe that

\dfrac{g\left(|D_{s}^{A}w(x,y)|\right)}{|D_{s}^{A}w(x,y)|}D_{s}^{A}w(x,y)\in L^{\tilde{G}}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})\text{ and }\,\overline{D_{s}^{A}v(x,y)}\in L^{G}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{2N}),

hence by Lemma
2.9
, we get that
F_{1}(w)
belongs to the dual of
W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
. Therefore,

(3.12)

\left\langle F_{1}(w),u-u_{n}\right\rangle\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty,

for any
w
. Observe that

(3.13)

\begin{split}o(1)&=\left\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n})-\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),u_{n}-u\right\rangle\\
&=M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})\right)\left\langle F_{1}(u_{n})-F_{1}(u),u_{n}-u\right\rangle+\left\langle F_{2}(u_{n})-F_{2}(u),u_{n}-u\right\rangle\\
&+\left(M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})\right)-M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u)\right)\right)\left\langle F_{1}(u),u_{n}-u\right\rangle-\mathcal{R}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(f(x,|u_{n}|)u_{n}-f(x,|u|)u)\overline{u_{n}-u}\,dx\right].\end{split}

The term

\left(M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})\right)-M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u)\right)\right)\left\langle F_{1}(u),u_{n}-u\right\rangle

converges to
0
since
M\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})\right)
remains bounded and by (
3.12
). Next, we claim that

(3.14)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(f(x,|u_{n}|)u_{n}-f(x,|u|)u)\overline{u_{n}-u}\,dx=o(1)\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Indeed, for
\varepsilon>0
and by Remark
2.5
we have

(3.15)

\begin{split}&\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(f(x,|u_{n}|)u_{n}-f(x,|u|)u)\overline{(u_{n}-u)}\,dx\bigg|\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(g^{\prime}(|u_{n}|)|u_{n}|+g^{\prime}(|u|)|u|)|u-u_{n}|\,dx\\
&\qquad+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(b^{\prime}(|u_{n}|)|u_{n}|+b^{\prime}(|u|)|u|)|u_{n}-u|\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq C\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\tilde{G}(g(|u_{n}|)+g(|u|))+G(|u_{n}-u|))\,dx+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(b(|u_{n}|)+b(|u|))|u_{n}-u|\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq C\varepsilon(\rho_{G}(u_{n})+\rho_{G}(u)+\rho_{G}(u_{n}-u))+C_{\varepsilon}\|b(|u_{n}|)+b(|u|)\|_{L^{\tilde{B}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{B}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\end{split}

By Theorem
2.8
, the terms
\rho_{G}(u_{n})+\rho_{G}(u)+\rho_{G}(u_{n}-u)
and
\|b(|u_{n}|)+b(|u|)\|_{L^{\tilde{B}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
remains uniformly bounded. Moreover, we know that
\rho_{B}(u_{n})-\rho_{B}(u)\to 0
by Holder’s inequality and (

[
16

, which follows also for complex functions) implies that

\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{B}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0,

which yields as

that

\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(f(x,|u_{n}|)u_{n}-f(x,|u|)u)\overline{(u_{n}-u)}\,dx\bigg|=C\varepsilon+o(1).

Sending
\varepsilon\to 0
after taking

3.14
).

Next, we analyse in (

\begin{split}&\left\langle F_{1}(u_{n})-F_{1}(u),u_{n}-u\right\rangle\\
&=\mathcal{R}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigg(\dfrac{g(|D_{s}^{A}u_{n}(x,y)|)}{|D_{s}^{A}u_{n}(x,y)|}D_{s}^{A}u_{n}(x,y)-\dfrac{g(|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|)}{|D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)|}D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)\bigg)\cdot\overline{D_{s}^{A}(u_{n}-u)(x,y)}\,d\mu\bigg]\\
&\,\,=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\dfrac{g(|a|)}{|a|}a-\dfrac{g(|b|)}{|b|}b\right)\cdot(a-b)\,d\mu,\end{split}

where

a=(\mathcal{R}(D_{s}^{A}u_{n}(x,y)),\,\mathcal{I}(D_{s}^{A}u_{n}(x,y))),\quad b=(\mathcal{R}(D_{s}^{A}u(x,y)),\,\mathcal{I}(D_{s}^{A}u(x,y))).

Applying Lemma
7.1

\left\langle F_{1}(u_{n})-F_{1}(u),u_{n}-u\right\rangle\geq C\rho_{G}(D_{s}^{A}u_{n}-D_{s}^{A}u).

Similarly,

Therefore, the previous inequalities, (

as

strongly in

Now, if

\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}[u_{n}]_{s,G}^{A}=0

then we have two further cases. First, if

[u_{n}]_{s,G}^{A}\to 0.

Observe that since

Therefore,
u_{n}\to 0
in
W^{s,G}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
. By Theorem
2.15
, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\to 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Moreover, by (
3.16
) and (
3.9
),
\|u_{n}\|_{G,V}
is uniformly bounded. Hence, again by (
3.16
),
u_{n}
is bounded in
W^{s,G}_{A,V}
and so
|u_{n}|
is bounded in
W^{s,G}_{V}
. By Theorem
2.14
,
|u_{n}|\to 0
in

. Consequently,
u_{n}
converges to
0
in
W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
. This ends the proof.

In the next two lemmas we prove that the functional

satisfies the geometric conditions to apply the Mountain Pass Theorem.

Lemma 3.4
.

The exist
r,a>0
such that

\mathcal{I}(u)\geq a

for all
u\in W^{s,G}_{V,A}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
satisfying

\|u\|^{A}_{s,G,V}=r.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{s,G}_{V,A}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
such that
\|u\|^{A}_{s,G,V}=r
, with
0<r<<1
to be chosen later.

First, observe that Remark
2.5
implies

(3.17)

\begin{split}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,|u|)\,dx\bigg|&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{|u|}|f(x,s)|s\,ds\,dx\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{|u|}\left(\varepsilon g^{\prime}(s)+C_{\varepsilon}b^{\prime}(s)\right)s\,ds\,dx\\
&\leq\varepsilon(p^{+}-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{|u|}g(s)\,ds\,dx+C_{\varepsilon}(p_{B}^{+}-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{|u|}b(s)\,ds\,dx\\
&\leq\varepsilon(p^{+}-1)\rho_{G}(u)+C_{\varepsilon}(p_{B}^{+}-1)\rho_{B}(u)\\
&\leq\dfrac{\varepsilon(p^{+}-1)}{V_{0}}\rho_{G,V}(u)+C_{\varepsilon}\dfrac{(p_{B}^{+}-1)}{V_{0}}\rho_{B,V}(u).\end{split}

Moreover, by Theorems
2.12
and
2.14

\begin{split}\rho_{B,V}(u)&\leq\max\left\{\||u|\|^{p_{B}^{+}}_{B,V},\||u|\|^{p_{B}^{-}}_{B,V}\right\}\\
&\leq C\max\left\{\||u|\|^{p_{B}^{+}}_{s,G,V},\||u|\|^{p_{B}^{-}}_{s,G,V}\right\}\\
&\leq C\max\left\{(\|u\|^{A}_{s,G,V})^{p_{B}^{+}},(\|u\|^{A}_{s,G,V})^{p_{B}^{-}}\right\}=Cr^{p_{B}^{-}}.\end{split}

Hence,

(3.18)

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(u)&=\mathcal{M}\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u)\right)+\rho_{G,V}(u)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,|u|)\,dx\\
&\geq\mathcal{M}\left(([u]_{s,G}^{A})^{p^{+}}\right)+\rho_{G,V}(u)-\dfrac{\varepsilon(p^{+}-1)}{V_{0}}\rho_{G,V}(u)-CC_{\varepsilon}\dfrac{(p_{B}^{+}-1)}{V_{0}}r^{p_{B}^{-}}\\
&\geq\mathcal{M}(1)([u]_{s,G}^{A})^{p^{+}\theta}+\left(1-\dfrac{\varepsilon(p^{+}-1)}{V_{0}}\right)\|u\|^{p^{+}\theta}_{G,V}-CC_{\varepsilon}\dfrac{(p_{B}^{+}-1)}{V_{0}}r^{p_{B}^{-}}\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used
(HM)_{2}
and
\theta>1
. Let
\varepsilon>0
so that

\beta:=1-\dfrac{\varepsilon(p^{+}-1)}{V_{0}}\leq\mathcal{M}(1),

then (
3.18

\mathcal{I}(u)\geq\beta 2^{1-p^{+}\theta}r^{p^{+}\theta}-CC_{\varepsilon}\dfrac{(p_{B}^{+}-1)}{V_{0}}r^{p_{B}^{-}}.

Observe that the last term is positive for small
r
since
p_{B}^{-}>p^{+}
and
\theta\in(1,p_{B}^{-}/p^{+})
by assumptions
(Hf)_{2}
and
(HM)_{2}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

There is
u_{0}\in W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
such that
\|u_{0}\|_{s,G,V}^{A}>r
and
\mathcal{I}(u_{0})<a
.

Proof.

First, observe that from
(Hf)_{3}
we get that
\gamma(t)=F(x,t)t^{-\mu}

Moreover, since

|f(x,t)t|\leq g^{\prime}(t)t
for
t\in[0,\delta]
, for some
\delta\in(0,1)

(Hf)_{2}

|f(x,t)|\leq 2C,\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,\,t\in[\delta,1].

As a result,

f(x,t)t\geq-\left(g^{\prime}(t)+2C\right)t\quad\text{ for all }t\in[0,1]

which gives

(3.20)

F(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,s)s\,ds\geq-\int_{0}^{t}g^{\prime}(s)s\,ds-Ct^{2}\geq-(p^{+}-1)G(t)-Ct^{2}.

Combining (

t>0

(3.21)

F(x,t)\geq C|t|^{\mu}-C(G(t)+t^{2}).

Let

with
\|u\|_{s,G,V}^{A}>1

[u]_{s,G}^{A}=1

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(tu)&=\mathcal{M}\left(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(tu)\right)+\rho_{G,V}(tu)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,t|u|)\,dx\\
&\leq\mathcal{M}(1)\left[\rho_{s,G}^{A}(tu)\right]^{\theta}+\rho_{G,V}(tu)-Ct^{\mu}\|u\|^{\mu}_{L^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\dfrac{C}{V_{0}}\rho_{G,V}(tu)+Ct^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\

as

since
\mu>p^{+}\theta>2
.

[

:

Theorem 3.6

4.
Existence of ground-state solutions: proof of Theorem
1.2

Throughout the section we assume
(HG)_{1}-(HG)_{2}
,
(HM)_{1}-(HM)_{3}
and
(Hf)_{1}-(Hf)_{3}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

Define the set of non-trivial critical points

\mathcal{C}:=\left\{u\in W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})\setminus\left\{0\right\}:\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)=0\right\}.

Then, by Theorem
1.1
,
\mathcal{C}\neq\emptyset
. Let

c_{m}:=\inf\left\{\mathcal{I}(u):u\in\mathcal{C}\right\}.

We first prove that
c_{m}\geq 0
. Indeed, let
u\in\mathcal{C}
. Since
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)=0
, we have

(4.1)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x,|u|)\,dx&\leq\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,|u|)|u|^{2}\,dx\\
&\leq\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}\left(M(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u))\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u)+\rho_{G,V}(u)\right).\end{split}

Hence,

(4.2)

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(u)&\geq\mathcal{M}(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u))+\rho_{G,V}(u)-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}\left(M(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u))\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u)+\rho_{G,V}(u)\right)\\
&\geq\left(\dfrac{1}{\theta}-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)M(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u))\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u)+\left(1-\dfrac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\rho_{G,V}(u)\qquad(\text{by }(HM)_{2}\text{ and }(Hf)_{3})\\
&\geq 0.\end{split}

Thus,
c_{m}\geq 0
.

Moreover, observe that
c_{m}
is attainable. In fact, let
u_{n}
be a minimizing sequence in
\mathcal{C}
. Then,
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n})=0
and
0\leq c_{m}\leq\mathcal{I}(u_{n})\leq C
for all
n
, so
u_{n}
is a PS-sequence. By Lemma
3.3
, there is a subsequence of
u_{n}
, not re-label, and
u\in W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
such that

u_{n}\to u\quad\text{ in }W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}).

Hence,
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)=0
and

c_{m}=\mathcal{I}(u).

Next, we prove that
c_{m}>0
. To get a contradiction, assume that
c_{m}=0
. Then, there is a sequence
u_{n}\in W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
such that

(4.3)

\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{I}(u_{n})\to 0.

By (
4.2
), we get

(4.4)

u_{n}\to 0\quad\text{in }W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}).

Moreover,
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u_{n})=0
implies

(4.5)

\begin{split}&M(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n}))\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})+\rho_{G,V}(u_{n})\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,|u_{n}|)|u_{n}|^{2}\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[\varepsilon g^{\prime}(|u_{n}|)+C_{\varepsilon}b^{\prime}(|u_{n}|)\right]|u_{n}|^{2}\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq\dfrac{\varepsilon(p^{+}-1)p^{+}}{V_{0}}\rho_{G,V}(u_{n})+C_{\varepsilon}\dfrac{(p_{B}^{+}-1)p_{B}^{+}}{V_{0}}\rho_{B}(u_{n})\\
&\qquad\leq\dfrac{\varepsilon(p^{+}-1)p^{+}}{V_{0}}\rho_{G,V}(u_{n})+C.C_{\varepsilon}(\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A})^{p_{B}^{-}}.\end{split}

Consequently,

(4.6)

M(\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n}))\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})+\left(1-\dfrac{\varepsilon(p^{+}-1)p^{+}}{V_{0}}\right)\rho_{G,V}(u_{n})\leq C.C_{\varepsilon}(\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A})^{p_{B}^{-}}.

Since
M(t)\geq c_{0}t^{\theta-1}
for
t\in[0,1]
by
(HM)_{3}
, we derive from (
4.6
) that

(4.7)

c_{0}\left([u_{n}]_{s,G}^{A}\right)^{p^{+}\theta}+\left(1-\dfrac{\varepsilon(p^{+}-1)p^{+}}{V_{0}}\right)\|u_{n}\|^{p^{+}}_{G,V}\leq C.C_{\varepsilon}(\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A})^{p_{B}^{-}}.

Also, since
\theta p^{+}>p^{+}
and
\|u_{n}\|^{p^{+}}_{G,V}\leq 1
, it follows from (
4.7
) that

(4.8)

c_{0}\left([u_{n}]_{s,G}^{A}\right)^{p^{+}\theta}+\left(1-\dfrac{\varepsilon(p^{+}-1)p^{+}}{V_{0}}\right)\|u_{n}\|^{p^{+}\theta}_{G,V}\leq C.C_{\varepsilon}(\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A})^{p_{B}^{-}}.

Choose now
\varepsilon
small so that

c_{0}=1-\dfrac{\varepsilon(p^{+}-1)p^{+}}{V_{0}},

then by (
4.8
) we have

c_{0}2^{1-\theta p^{+}}(\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A})^{p^{+}\theta}\leq C.C_{\varepsilon}(\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A})^{p_{B}^{-}},

which is a contradiction since
p^{+}\theta<p_{B}^{-}
by
(HM)_{2}
and (
4.8
) holds. As a result, we have shown that there is a non-trivial ground state solution
u_{m}
such that

\mathcal{I}(u_{m})=c_{m}>0.

Remark 4.1
.

Observe that Theorem

We will prove Theorem

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{B}

\mathcal{I}(0)=0

(ii)

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We already know that
\mathcal{I}
satisfies the PS-condition, is even and
\mathcal{I}(0)=0
. We put
\mathcal{B}=W^{s,G}_{A,V}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
and we let
\mathcal{B}^{+}=\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{-}=\left\{0\right\}

. Hence, it is remains to prove

.

Let
W\subset\mathcal{B}
be a finite dimensional subspace. Put

W=\text{span}\left\{u_{1},...,u_{k}\right\}.

In
W
we define the norm

\|u\|=\max\left\{|\alpha_{i}|:u=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}u_{i}\right\}.

Since
W
is finite dimensional, this norm is equivalent to the given norm in
\mathcal{B}
. Assume that
\|u\|=R
,
R>1
to be chosen later, and that
|\alpha_{i_{0}}|=\|u\|
for some
1\leq i_{i_{0}}\leq k
. Then

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(u)&\leq\mathcal{M}\left(C_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\max\left\{|\alpha_{i}|^{p^{+}},|\alpha_{i}|^{p^{-}}\right\}\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{i})\right)+C_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\max\left\{|\alpha_{i}|^{p^{+}},|\alpha_{i}|^{p^{-}}\right\}\rho_{G}(u_{i})\quad(\text{by }\eqref{G product})\\
&-C_{3}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\alpha_{i}|^{\mu}\|u_{i}\|_{\mu}^{\mu}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\alpha_{i}|^{2}\|u_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\bigg)\quad(\text{by \eqref{F ineq} and observing }(a+b)^{q}\geq a^{q}+b^{q},\,q\geq 1),\\
&\leq C\left(R^{\theta p^{+}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{i})\right)^{\theta}+R^{p^{+}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{G}(u_{i})+R^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|u_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\right)-C_{3}R^{\mu}\|u_{i_{0}}\|_{\mu}^{\mu}\leq 0,\end{split}

for
R
large enough, since by assumption
\mu>p^{+}\theta>2
. Therefore, the conclusion follows.

6.
Existence of solutions with a weak Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition: proof of Theorem
1.4

Throughout this section, we replace hypothesis
(Hf)_{3}
with the following weak-type Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition:

(Hf)^{\prime}_{3}
There exists a non negative function
h:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\mu F(x,t)-f(x,t)t^{2}\leq h(x,t)\quad\text{ for all }(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,\infty).

Here,
h
satisfies the following growth assumption:

|h(x,|t|)|\leq\tilde{B}(|t|)+\phi(x)\quad\text{ for all $x$ and $t$,}

where
\phi\in L^{1}(\Omega)
,
\phi\geq 0
, and
\tilde{B}
is an N-function satisfying (
2.6
) and

p_{\tilde{B}}^{+}<p^{-}.

Observe that
W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{\tilde{B}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
continuously. Moreover, we assume that

(6.1)

\lim_{|t|\to\infty}\dfrac{F(x,|t|)}{|t|^{p^{+}\theta}}=\infty.

In order to prove Theorem
1.4
, we first show how to get that
\mathcal{I}
satisfies the PS-condition. Observe that assumption
(Hf)_{3}
was used in the proof of Lemma
3.3
in the chain of inequalities (
3.9
). First, we have

(6.2)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x,|u_{n}|)\,dx&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{B}(|u_{n}|)\,dx+\|\phi\|_{1}\\
&=\rho_{\tilde{B}}(u_{n})+\|\phi\|_{1}\\
&\leq\max\left\{\||u_{n}|\|^{p^{+}_{\tilde{B}}}_{\tilde{B}},\||u_{n}|\|^{p^{-}_{\tilde{B}}}_{\tilde{B}}\right\}+\|\phi\|_{1}\\
&\leq C\max\left\{\||u_{n}|\|^{p^{+}_{\tilde{B}}}_{s,G},\||u_{n}|\|^{p^{-}_{\tilde{B}}}_{s,G}\right\}+\|\phi\|_{1}\\
&\leq C\max\left\{(\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A})^{p^{+}_{\tilde{B}}},(\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A})^{p^{-}_{\tilde{B}}}\right\}+\|\phi\|_{1}.\end{split}

Hence, under
(Hf)^{\prime}_{3}
, (
3.9
) becomes

(6.3)

\begin{split}&C+C\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A}\geq\delta\left(\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})+\left(1-\frac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\rho_{G,V}(u_{n})-\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x,|u_{n}|)\,dx\\
&\quad\geq\delta\left(\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})+\left(1-\frac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\rho_{G,V}(u_{n})\\
&\qquad\quad-C\max\left\{(\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A})^{p^{+}_{\tilde{B}}},(\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A})^{p^{-}_{\tilde{B}}}\right\}-\|\phi\|_{1}.\end{split}

Assume that (a subsequence of)
\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A}
tends to
\infty
, then (
6.3
) gives

(6.4)

\begin{split}&C(1+\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A}+(\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A})^{p^{+}_{\tilde{B}}})\geq\delta\left(\frac{1}{\theta}-\frac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\rho_{s,G}^{A}(u_{n})+\left(1-\frac{p^{+}}{\mu}\right)\rho_{G,V}(u_{n})\\
&\quad\geq C_{1}\min\left\{([u_{n}]^{A}_{s,G})^{p^{+}},([u_{n}]^{A}_{s,G})^{p^{-}}\right\}+C_{2}\min\left\{\|u_{n}\|_{G,V}^{p^{+}},\|u_{n}\|_{G,V}^{p^{-}}\right\}.\end{split}

If
[u_{n}]^{A}_{s,G}\to\infty
and
\|u_{n}\|_{G,V}
is bounded, dividing both sides of (
6.4
) by
[u_{n}]^{A}_{s,G}
and letting
n\to\infty
give a contradiction since
p^{+}_{\tilde{B}}<p^{-}
. The argument is similar if
[u_{n}]^{A}_{s,G}
is bounded and
\|u_{n}\|_{G,V}
is unbounded. Finally, if both
[u_{n}]^{A}_{s,G},\|u_{n}\|_{G,V}\to\infty
, then (
6.4
) yields

C(1+\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A}+(\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A})^{p^{+}_{\tilde{B}}})\geq C_{1}([u_{n}]^{A}_{s,G})^{p^{-}}+C_{2}\|u_{n}\|_{G,V}^{p^{-}}\geq C_{3}(\|u_{n}\|_{s,G,V}^{A})^{p^{-}},

and again we get a contradiction since
p^{-}-p^{+}_{\tilde{B}}>0
.

Finally, assumption (
6.1
) is used in the proof of Lemma
3.5
. Indeed, (
3.19
) becomes now

F(x,|t|)\geq C|t|^{p^{+}\theta}

for all
t\geq t_{0}
and for
C>\mathcal{M}(1)
. Hence, (
3.21
) is now

F(x,t)\geq C|t|^{p^{+}\theta}-C(G(t)+t^{2}),\quad t>0.

Finally, for
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
with
\|u\|_{s,G,V}^{A}>1
and

, and for large

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(tu)&\leq\mathcal{M}(1)\left[\rho_{s,G}^{A}(tu)\right]^{\theta}+\rho_{G,V}(tu)-Ct^{\mu}\|u\|^{p^{+}\theta}_{p^{+}\theta}+\dfrac{C}{V_{0}}\rho_{G,V}(tu)+Ct^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\leq\mathcal{M}(1)t^{p^{+}\theta}+t^{p^{+}}\|u\|_{G,V}^{p^{+}}-Ct^{p^{+}\theta}\|u\|^{p^{+}\theta}_{p^{+}\theta}+Ct^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{2}\to-\infty,\end{split}

as

since

and by the choice of

.

7.1.
Inequality for N-functions

Lemma 7.1
.

Suppose that
G
is an N-function satisfying (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and
(HG)_{1}
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
we have

\left\langle\dfrac{g(|a|)}{|a|}a-\dfrac{g(|b|)}{|b|}b,a-b\right\rangle\geq CG(|a-b|).

Proof.

We start with some calculations:

\begin{split}&\left\langle\dfrac{g(|a|)}{|a|}a-\dfrac{g(|b|)}{|b|}b,a-b\right\rangle\\
&\qquad=\left\langle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\left(\dfrac{g(|sa+(1-s)b|)}{|sa+(1-s)b|}(sa+(1-s)b)\right)\,ds,a-b\right\rangle\\
&\qquad=\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{g^{\prime}(|sa+(1-s)b|)|sa+(1-s)b|^{2}-g(|sa+(1-s)b|)|sa+(1-s)b|}{|sa+(1-s)b|^{2}}|a-b|^{2}\,ds\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{g(|sa+(1-s)b|)}{|sa+(1-s)b|}|a-b|^{2}\,ds\\
&\qquad\geq(p^{-}-1)\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{g(|sa+(1-s)b|)}{|sa+(1-s)b|}|a-b|^{2}\,ds\qquad(\text{ by }\,\eqref{cG0}).\end{split}

As in
[
12
, Lemma 4.4]
, we consider some cases. If
|a|\geq|a-b|
, then

|sa+(1-s)b|\geq||a|-(1-s)|a-b||\geq s|a-b|.

Thus,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{g(|sa+(1-s)b|)}{|sa+(1-s)b|}|a-b|^{2}\,ds&\geq\dfrac{1}{p^{-}-1}\int_{0}^{1}g^{\prime}(|sa+(1-s)b|)|a-b|^{2}\,ds\\
&\geq\dfrac{1}{p^{-}-1}\int_{0}^{1}g^{\prime}(s|a-b|)|a-b|^{2}\,ds\\
&=\frac{1}{p^{-}-1}|a-b|g(s|a-b|)\bigg|_{0}^{1}\\
&\geq\dfrac{p^{-}}{p^{-}-1}G(|a-b|).\end{split}

On the other hand, if
|a|<|a-b|
, then

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{g(|sa+(1-s)b|)}{|sa+(1-s)b|}|a-b|^{2}\,ds\geq p^{-}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{G(|sa+(1-s)b|)}{|sa+(1-s)b|^{2}}|a-b|^{2}\,ds\\
&\geq\frac{p^{-}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}G(|sa+(1-s)b|)\,ds\,\quad(\text{since }|sa+(1-s)b|^{2}\leq(2-s)|a-b|^{2})\\
&=\dfrac{p^{-}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}G\left(\sqrt{|sa+(1-s)b|^{2}}\right)\,ds\\
&\geq\dfrac{p^{-}}{2}G\left(\sqrt{\int_{0}^{1}|sa+(1-s)b|^{2}}ds\right)\\
&=\dfrac{p^{-}}{2}G\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{|a|^{2}+\left\langle a,b\right\rangle+|b|^{2}}{3}}ds\right)\\
&\geq CG(|a-b|),\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used that

|a|^{2}+\left\langle a,b\right\rangle+|b|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}|a-b|^{2}.

This finishes the proof.

7.2.
A convergence principle in Orlicz spaces

Proposition 7.2
.

Suppose that
u_{n}\to u
in two Orlicz-Lebesgue spaces
L_{V}^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
L_{V}^{B}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then, there exists a function
h\in L_{V}^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L_{V}^{B}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and a subsequence
u_{n_{i}}
such that

|u_{n_{i}}(x)|\leq h(x)\quad a.e..

Proof.

Since
u_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in both spaces, for each positive integer
i
, there exist
n_{i}<n_{i+1}
such that

\|u_{n_{i+1}}-u_{n_{i}}\|_{G,V}<2^{-i}\quad\text{and }\quad\|u_{n_{i+1}}-u_{n_{i}}\|_{B,V}<2^{-i}.

Let

f_{k}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|u_{n_{i+1}}(x)-u_{n_{i}}(x)|.

Then,

\|f_{k}\|_{G,V}<1\quad\text{and }\quad\|f_{k}\|_{B,V}<1.

Hence, by Lemma
2.10

\rho_{G,V}(f_{k})<1\quad\text{and }\quad\rho_{B,V}(f_{k})<1.

Letting

f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|u_{n_{i+1}}(x)-u_{n_{i}}(x)|,

we get by Fatou’s Lemma, that

\rho_{G,V}(f)<1\quad\text{and }\quad\rho_{B,V}(f)<1.

As a result,
f\in L_{V}^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L_{V}^{B}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Putting

h=u_{n_{1}}+f

we get
|u_{n_{i}}|\leq h
a.e. and
h\in L_{V}^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L_{V}^{B}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
This ends the proof.

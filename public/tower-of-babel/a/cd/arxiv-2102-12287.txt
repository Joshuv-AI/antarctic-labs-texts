Division properties in exterior algebras of free modules and logarithmic residua
B. Jakubczyk

Abstract
. Let M M be a free module of rank m m over a commutative unital ring R R and let N N be its free submodule. We consider the problem when a given element of the exterior product Λ p ​ M \Lambda^{p}M is divisible, in a sense, over elements of the exterior product Λ r ​ N \Lambda^{r}N , r ≤ p r\leq p . Precisely, we give conditions under which an element η ∈ Λ p ​ M \eta\in\Lambda^{p}M can be expressed as a finite sum of skew-products of elements of Λ r ​ N \Lambda^{r}N and elements of Λ p − r ​ M \Lambda^{p-r}M . For a given basis ω 1 , … , ω k \omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{k} in N N the elements of Λ p − r ​ M \Lambda^{p-r}M are unique in a specified sense. Necessary and sufficient conditions for such divisibility take a simple form, provided that the submodule is embedded in M M with singularities having the depth larger then p − r + 1 p-r+1 . In the special case where r = k = r ​ a ​ n ​ k ​ N r=k=rankN the divisibility property means that η = Ω ∧ γ \eta=\Omega\wedge\gamma where Ω = ω 1 ∧ ⋯ ∧ ω k \Omega=\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{k} and γ ∈ Λ p − k ​ M \gamma\in\Lambda^{p-k}M . More detailed statements of these results are then used to state criteria for existence and uniqueness of algebraic logarithmic residua when the “divisor” is defined by elements f 1 , … , f k ∈ R f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in R . Special cases are multidimensional logarithmic residua in complex analysis.



1.
Introduction

Consider a commutative unital ring
R
and let
M
be a free
R
-module of finite rank
m
. We will denote by
\Lambda^{q}M
the
q
th exterior product of
M
and
\Lambda M=\oplus_{q=0}^{m}\Lambda^{q}M
will be the exterior algebra generated by
M
.
We shall consider division problems in the algebra
\Lambda M
related to the following elementary questions.

Consider two elements
\eta\in\Lambda^{p}M
and
\alpha\in\Lambda^{r}M
. If
p\geq r
then it is natural to ask

Question 1.

When
\eta
is divisible over
\alpha
, i.e., there exists
\gamma\in\Lambda^{p-r}M
such that

\eta=\alpha\wedge\gamma\ ?

This question is largely open for general
R
. In the case where
R
is the field of real numbers
\mathbb{R}
and
M
is the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
the following general result of Dacorogna and Kneuss
[
4
]
answers the question. There exists such
\gamma
if and only if
\ker\alpha\subset\ker\eta
, where one defines
\ker\alpha=\{\beta\in\Lambda^{m-p}\mathbb{R}^{m}|\alpha\wedge\beta=0\}
and
\ker\eta=\{\beta\in\Lambda^{m-p}\mathbb{R}^{m}|\eta\wedge\beta=0\}
.

For general
R
we will answer Question 1 in the special case when
\alpha
is a product of
\omega_{i}\in M
. Namely, let
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{k}
be fixed elements of
M
, where
1\leq k\leq m
. We will answer

Question 2.

When there exists
\gamma\in\Lambda^{p-k}M
such that

\eta=\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{k}\wedge\gamma?

Question 2, similarly as two other ones stated below, is nontrivial when the submodule of
M
generated by the elements
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{k}
is singularly embedded in
M
which means that the elements are linearly independent but can not be completed to a basis of
M
.

We can also ask if for a given
\eta\in M
it is possible to find elements
a_{i}\in R
such that
\eta=\sum_{i}a_{i}\omega_{i}
. More generally,

Question 3.

Given
\eta\in\Lambda^{p}M
, is it possible to represent
\eta
as

\eta=\sum_{i}\omega_{i}\wedge\gamma_{i},

with some
\gamma_{i}\in\Lambda^{p-1}M
?

An answer to this question was given in the main theorem of
[
11
]
for Noetherian rings
R
. Questions 2 and 3 are particular cases of the following main problem considered in the paper.

Question 4.

Given
\eta\in\Lambda^{p}M
and
1\leq r\leq k
such that
p\geq r
, when one can write

[formula]

\eta=\sum\omega_{J}\wedge\gamma_{J}

with some
\gamma_{J}\in\Lambda^{p-r}M
, where
\omega_{J}\in\Lambda^{r}M
are of the form
\omega_{J}=\omega_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{j_{r}}
?

Such questions appear in differential geometry when one considers k-tuples of differential 1-forms or vector fields on a manifold
N
. Singularities of such tuples (points where they are linearly dependent) are unavoidable if the topology of the manifold is not sufficiently trivial, see
[
9
]
. Already for
k=1
and nonzero Euler characteristic of
N
every smooth vector field or differential 1-form on
N
has a singular point. Also any holomorphic 1-form on a smooth complex projective variety of general type must vanish at some point
[
10
]
. Answers to above questions may help to deal with such local singularities (as was the case in
[
8
]
), in which case
R
will be the ring of germs of functions on manifolds in the holomorphic, real analytic, or
C^{\infty}
category (where
R
is non-Noetherian).

Our first main result, statement (i) in Theorem
2.1
in the next section, will give a criterium for
\eta
to be representable in the form (R). To have such a representation we will assume that
p-r+2<d
, where
d
is the depth of the ideal
I(\Omega)
generated by the coefficients of the k-form

\Omega=\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{k}.

Without this assumption an analogous representation holds for
\eta
replaced by
a^{n}\eta
with
a
an arbitrary element of the ideal
I(\Omega)
and
n
sufficiently large (statement (ii) of Theorem
2.1
). The proof of Theorem
2.1
is postponed to Section 6. A uniqueness property of the representation is stated in Theorem
2.8
. Finally, an equivalent statement of Theorem
2.1
, formulated as a property of a (singularly embedded) free submodule of
M
, is given in Theorem
2.11
. In the Appendix we recall properties of the depth used in stating some of the results.

In Section 3 we state direct consequences of results from Section 2 in the case where, in addition to the earlier data, we are given a finitely generated ideal
{\mathcal{I}}\subset R
. In this case we define residua as results of the division discussed in Secton 2 factorized via the submodule
{\mathcal{I}}M
and state a general theorem on their existence and uniqueness.

More specific residua, called logarithmic, are defined in Section 4 where
M
is the module of derivations of
R
and the elements
\omega_{i}\in M
are replaced with the algebraic differentials
df_{i}\in M^{*}
of generators
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}
of
{\mathcal{I}}
. Finally, in Section 5 we translate the theorem on algebraic logarithmic residua to a theorem on multidimensional residua in complex analysis.

We do not assume that the ring
R
and the module
M
are Noetherian which makes the proofs more delicate.

2.
Main results

Let
R
denote a commutative ring with unity and let
M
be a free module over
R
of finite rank
m
. We will denote its
q
th exterior product by
\Lambda^{q}M
, with identifications
\Lambda^{0}M=R
,
\Lambda^{1}M=M
, and
\Lambda^{q}M=0
for
q<0
and
q>m
. Note that, choosing a basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{m}
in
M
and
q\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, we have a natural basis in
\Lambda^{q}M
which consists of exterior products

[formula]

e_{I}=e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{q}},\ \ 1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{q}\leq m.

Elements
\eta\in\Lambda^{q}M
can be written as
\eta=\sum_{I}a_{I}e_{I}
, with unique coefficients
a_{I}\in R
,
I=i_{1}\cdots i_{q}
.

Consider a sequence of elements

\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{k}\in M,

1\leq k\leq m
, which will be fixed throughout the section. We will use the following notation. For a given
0\leq r\leq k
we will denote by
{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)
the set of
strictly monotone multiindices

{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)=\{J=j_{1}\cdots j_{r}:\,1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{r}\leq k\},

including the empty one
J=\emptyset
with
r=0
. Sometimes, when
r
is not fixed, it will be convenient to denote the length
r
of
J
by
\langle J\rangle
. Given
J\in{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)
, we will denote

\omega_{J}=\omega_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{j_{r}}.

where
\omega_{J}=1
when
J=\emptyset
.

Denote
\Omega=\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{k}
. Using the basis (E) with
q=k
we can write
\Omega=\sum_{I}a_{I}e_{I}
where
a_{I}\in R
and the sum is taken over
I\in{\mathcal{J}}(k,m)
. The ideal generated by the coefficients
a_{I}
,

I(\Omega)=\operatorname{span}_{R}\{a_{I},I\in{\mathcal{J}}(k,m)\},

is independent of the choice of the basis in
M
and will play a crutial role in our considerations.

Recall that the depth of a proper ideal
I\subset R
, denoted
\operatorname{depth}I
, is the largest length of a regular sequence in
I
(see Appendix for a brief recall of properties of regular sequences). In Noetherian rings we have
\operatorname{depth}I\leq\operatorname{height}I\leq\operatorname{gen}I
(in Cohen-Macaulay rings
\operatorname{depth}I=\operatorname{height}I
), where
\operatorname{gen}I
denotes the minimal number of generators of the ideal
I
. Additionally, one defines
\operatorname{depth}R=\infty
. Recall that
\operatorname{rank}M=m
.

Theorem 2.1

(Existence)
.

Fix integers
1\leq p,r,s\leq m
such that
p\geq r
and
r+s=k+1
.

(i) Assume that

[formula]

p-r\leq\operatorname{depth}I(\Omega)-2.

Then an element
\eta\in\Lambda^{p}M
can be represented in the form

[formula]

\eta=\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)}\omega_{J}\wedge\gamma_{J},\ \ \text{for\ some}\ \ \gamma_{J}\in\Lambda^{p-r}M,

if and only if

[formula]

\omega_{I}\wedge\eta=0,\ \ \text{for\ all}\ \ I\in{\mathcal{J}}(s,k).

(ii) Without assuming the depth condition (DC), for any
\eta\in\Lambda^{p}M
satisfying (B) there exists
n>0
(not depending on
\eta
if the module of
\eta
satisfying (B) is finitely generated) such that for
b=a^{n}
, with arbitrary
a\in I(\Omega)
, we have

[formula]

b\eta=\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)}\omega_{J}\wedge\gamma_{J},\ \ \text{for\ some}\ \ \gamma_{J}\in\Lambda^{p-r}M.

Conversely, if (A’) holds for some non-zero-divisor
b\in R
then (B) holds, too.

Remark 2.2
.

Implication (A)
\Rightarrow
(B) is trivial and holds for any
0\leq p\leq m
without assuming condition (DC). Namely, multiplying both sides of (A) by
\omega_{I}
with
I\in{\mathcal{J}}(s,k)
gives zero on the right hand side as each product
\omega_{I}\wedge\omega_{J}
, with
J\in{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)
, contains twice some
\omega_{i}
due to
r+s=k+1
. For the same reason condition (A’) implies that
b\eta\wedge\omega_{I}=0
for all
I\in{\mathcal{J}}(s,k)
. Thus, if (A’) holds for a non-zero-divisor
b\in R
then (B) holds, too. Note that both statements are empty or trivial if
\Omega=0
.

Remark 2.3
.

For Noetherian
R
we have
d:=\operatorname{depth}I(\Omega)\leq m-k+1
, if
I(\Omega)\not=R
. Indeed,
I(\Omega)
is generated by the
k\times k
minors of the
k\times m
matrix of the coefficients of the forms
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{k}
. Let
I
be the ideal generated by the first

\omega_{1}
. We see that any

I(\Omega)\subset I
. Thus we have
d\leq\operatorname{depth}I
. The conclusion follows from
\operatorname{depth}I\leq\operatorname{gen}I
, since
R
is Noetherian, and
\operatorname{gen}I\leq m-k+1
.

For

the first statement in the theorem reduces to the ensuing known facts.

Uniqueness of the representations of

Theorem 2.8

(Uniqueness)
.

Let
\operatorname{rank}M\geq p\geq r\geq 1
and
1\leq k\leq m
. If the ideal
I(\Omega)
contains a non-zero-divisor then the following holds.

(i) If
\eta\in\Lambda^{p}M
is given in the form (A) then the elements
\gamma_{J}
,
J\in{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)
, are unique when restricted to
K
which means that
\gamma_{J}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p-r})
are determined by
\eta
and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p-r}\in K
.

(ii) More generally, if
b
is a non-zero-divisor in

and
b\eta=\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)}\omega_{J}\wedge\gamma_{J}
then the elements
\gamma_{J}/b
are unique, independent of

, when restricted to
K
. This means that
\gamma_{J}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p-r})/b
, treated as elements of the total ring of fractions of
R
, are determined by
\eta
and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p-r}\in K
.

Recall that the
total ring of fractions
of the ring

is the localization
R_{fr}:=S^{-1}R
of

with respect to the multiplicative set
S
of all non-zero-divisors in
R
. In this case the natural ring homomorphism

is injective.
(In particular, if

is an integral domain then

is a subring of the field of fractions of

Any free
R
-module

has a similar embedding into its localization
M_{fr}:=S^{-1}M\simeq(R_{fr})^{m}
. The free dual module
M_{fr}^{*}
is naturally isomorphic to the localization
S^{-1}M^{*}
of
M^{*}
. Finally, the inclusion
K\subset M^{*}
extends to a unique inclusion of the localizations
K_{fr}:=S^{-1}K\subset S^{-1}M^{*}
. This implies the following

Remark 2.9
.

The forms
\gamma_{J}/b
can be treated as elements of
S^{-1}(\Lambda^{p-r}M)\simeq\Lambda^{p-r}M_{fr}

The theorem immediately follows from the following more general lemma. Let

be non-zero-divisors in

S_{[b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime}]}:=\{(b^{\prime})^{m}(b^{\prime\prime})^{n}\}_{m,n\geq 0}

Lemma 2.10
.

If

b^{\prime}\eta=\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)}\omega_{J}\wedge\gamma^{\prime}_{J}\quad\text{and}\quad b^{\prime\prime}\eta=\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)}\omega_{J}\wedge\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{J},

then for all
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p-r}\in K
we have
\gamma^{\prime}_{J}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p-r})/b^{\prime}=\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{J}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p-r})/b^{\prime\prime}
in the localization
S^{-1}_{[b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime}]}R
of

.

Proof.

Multiplying both sides of the former equality by
b^{\prime\prime}
and of the latter equality by
b^{\prime}
and subtracting one from the other we get

[formula]

0=\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)}\omega_{J}\wedge\gamma_{J},\ \ \text{where}\ \ \gamma_{J}=b^{\prime\prime}\gamma^{\prime}_{J}-b^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{J}.

We will prove that
\gamma_{J}|_{K}=0
. Given
\alpha\in M^{*}
, we will use the operator of interior product
\alpha\rfloor:\Lambda^{q}M\to\Lambda^{q-1}M
defined by
\alpha\rfloor\gamma=\gamma(\alpha,\cdot,\dots,\cdot)

is treated as

.

Consider arbitrary elements
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p-r}\in K
. Since
\alpha_{i}\rfloor\omega_{j}=0
, we have

\alpha_{p-r}\rfloor\cdots\alpha_{1}\rfloor(\omega_{J}\wedge\gamma_{J})=(-1)^{r(p-r)}g_{J}\,\omega_{J}

where

belongs to
R
. Applying the operator
\alpha_{p-r}\rfloor\cdots\alpha_{1}\rfloor
to both sides of (U) gives then

\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)}g_{J}\omega_{J}=0.

We claim that this equality and the assumption that

contains a non-zero-divisor imply that

for all

. To see this let us fix

and multiply both sides in (U) by

, where

4.
Logarithmic residua

In algebraic and complex analytic geometry a special role is played by the notion of logarithmic residue. Generally, this is a differential form defined on the divisor (pole) of a meromorphic form. Here we define meromorphic forms and their residua in a purely algebraic way as a special case of the algebraic residue from the preceding section. One of our motivations is to make it sufficienly general so that it is applicable in certain problems in differential geometry where appearence of singularities is natural.

b_{J}=bc_{J}=a_{1}^{n}c_{J}
, for some
c_{J}\in R
. Plugging such
b_{J}
to the formula for
a_{1}^{n}\eta
gives

a_{1}^{n}\eta=a_{1}^{n}\sum_{J}c_{J}\omega_{J},\ \ c_{J}\in R.

The fact that
a_{1}^{n}
is a non-zero-divisor in
R
implies that
\eta=\sum_{J}c_{J}\omega_{J}
, which was to be shown.

The case
\bf r<\min\{p,k\}
.

To prove statement (i) for all
r<\min\{k,p\}
assume it is true for
r+1
. Denote
d=\operatorname{depth}I(\Omega)
and let
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}
be a regular sequence in
I(\Omega)
where, by our assumption,
d\geq p-r+2
. By Lemma
6.3
the sequence
a_{1}^{n},a_{2},\dots,a_{d}
is also regular for any
n
. Condition (B) implies condition (A’), by statement (ii), thus for
\eta
satisfying (B) we have

a_{1}^{n}\eta=\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)}\omega_{J}\wedge\gamma_{J}

for some
n\geq 1
and
\gamma_{J}\in\Lambda^{p-r}(M)
.

Denote
b=a_{1}^{n}
. As earlier, we introduce the quotient ring
\bar{R}=R/bR
and the quotient modules
\bar{M}=M/bM
,
\Lambda^{i}\bar{M}\simeq\Lambda^{i}M/b\Lambda^{i}M
. Under canonical homomorphisms elements of the original ring and of the corresponding modules have their canonical images, denotesd with bars, in the quotient ring and modules. For
\bar{\Omega}=\bar{\omega}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\bar{\omega}_{k}
we have
\operatorname{depth}I(\bar{\Omega})\geq d-1
since the sequence
\bar{a}_{2},\dots,\bar{a}_{d}\in I(\bar{\Omega})
is regular. When replacing the elements in the above equality with their counterparts in the quotient objects we get zero on the left side, thus

0=\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)}\bar{\omega}_{J}\wedge\bar{\gamma}_{J}

where
\bar{\omega}_{J}=\bar{\omega}_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\bar{\omega}_{j_{r}}
and
\bar{\gamma}_{J}\in\Lambda^{p-r}\bar{M}
. Pick a multiindex
J\in{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)
and let
J^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{J}}(r^{\prime},k)
be its complement satisfying
r^{\prime}+r=k
and
J\bigcap J^{\prime}=\emptyset
. Multiplying both sides of the above equality by
\bar{\omega}_{J^{\prime}}
we obtain

0=\bar{\omega}_{J^{\prime}}\wedge\bar{\omega}_{J}\wedge\bar{\gamma}_{J}=\pm\bar{\Omega}\wedge\bar{\gamma}_{J},

since all other products in the sum vanish as they contain a repeated
\bar{\omega}_{i}
. We can now use Lemma
6.2
with
\bar{\gamma}_{J}\in\Lambda^{p-r}\bar{M}
playing the role of
\eta
and
\Omega
replaced with
\bar{\Omega}
. Namely,
p-r<\operatorname{depth}I(\Omega)-1
, by the depth assumption (DC), and
d=\operatorname{depth}I(\Omega)\leq\operatorname{depth}I(\bar{\Omega})+1
, by the inequality mentioned earlier. Therefore the asumption
p-r<\operatorname{depth}I(\bar{\Omega})
required in the lemma is satisfied and we deduce that

\bar{\gamma}_{J}=\sum_{i}\bar{\omega}_{i}\wedge\bar{\gamma}_{J,i}

for some
\bar{\gamma}_{J,i}\in\Lambda^{p-r-1}\bar{M}
.

The above equaliy can be written in the original modules as

\gamma_{J}=\sum_{i}\omega_{i}\wedge\gamma_{J,i}+b\xi_{J},

for some
\gamma_{J,i}\in\Lambda^{p-r-1}M
and
\xi_{J}\in\Lambda^{p-r}M
. Plugging such
\gamma_{J}
to the formula for
a_{1}^{n}\eta
and taking into account that
b=a_{1}^{n}
we obtain

a_{1}^{n}(\eta-\sum_{J}\omega_{J}\wedge\xi_{J})=\sum_{J}\sum_{i}\omega_{J}\wedge\omega_{i}\wedge\gamma_{J,i}.

Multiplying both sides by
\omega_{I^{\prime}}
, with arbitrary
I^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{J}}(k-r,k)
, we find that

a_{1}^{n}(\eta-\sum_{J}\omega_{J}\wedge\xi_{J})\wedge\omega_{I^{\prime}}=0

since each product
\omega_{J}\wedge\omega_{i}\wedge\omega_{I^{\prime}}
vanishes as it has a repeated
\omega_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. From the fact that
a_{1}^{n}
is a non-zero-divisor we deduce that

(\eta-\sum_{J}\omega_{J}\wedge\xi_{J})\wedge\omega_{I^{\prime}}=0,

for any
I^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{J}}(k-r,k)
. Thus the form
\eta-\sum_{J}\omega_{J}\wedge\xi_{J}
satisfies condition (B) with
s^{\prime}=k-r
.

Putting
r^{\prime}=r+1
we have
r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}=k+1
. Thus, we we can use the induction assumption that statement (i) holds for
r+1
and conclude that the element
\eta^{\prime}=\eta-\sum\omega_{J}\wedge\xi_{J}
has a representation

\eta-\sum_{J}\omega_{J}\wedge\xi_{J}=\sum_{\hat{J}}\omega_{\hat{J}}\wedge\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{J}}

with
\hat{J}
in
{\mathcal{J}}(r+1,k)
and some
\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{J}}\in\Lambda^{p-r-1}\bar{M}
. Since
\omega_{\hat{J}}
are products of
r+1
forms among
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{k}
, the sum on the right side can be rearranged to a sum
\sum_{J}\omega_{J}\wedge\widetilde{\gamma}_{J}
, with
J\in{\mathcal{J}}(r,k)
and
\widetilde{\gamma}_{J}\in\Lambda^{p-r}M
.
This implies that
\eta
can be written in the form (A) and ends the proof.

\Box

7.
Appendix: properties of the depth

Let
R
be a commutative ring with unit. Recall that a sequence of elements
a_{1},\dots,a_{q}
of
R
is called
regular
if
(a_{1},\dots,a_{q})\not=R
and
a_{i}
is a non-zero-divisor on
R/(a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1})
for
i=1,\dots,q
(in particular,
a_{1}
is a non-zero-divisor on
R
). Here
(a_{1},\dots,a_{i})
denotes the ideal in
R
generated by the elements
a_{1},\dots,a_{i}
.

Given a proper ideal
I\subset R
, the
depth
of
I
, denoted
\operatorname{depth}I
, is the supremum of lengths of regular sequences in
I
. Additionally, one defines
\operatorname{depth}R=\infty
. Below we list several properties which can be useful when verifying the depth condition (DC).

(1)

If
I_{1},I_{2}
are ideals in
R
and
I_{1}\subset I_{2}
then
\operatorname{depth}I_{1}\leq\operatorname{depth}I_{2}
(trivial).

(2)

If
a_{1},\dots,a_{q}\in I
is a regular sequence then
\operatorname{depth}I\geq\operatorname{depth}I/(a_{1},\dots,a_{q})+q
.

(3)

If for a fixed
i
the sequences
a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1},b,a_{i+1},\dots,a_{r}
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1},c,a_{i+1},\dots,a_{r}
are regular, and
a_{i}=bc
, then
a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1},a_{i},a_{i+1},\dots,a_{r}
is regular. Vice versa, if
a_{1},\dots,a_{i},\dots,a_{r}
is regular, with
a_{i}=bc
, and
(a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1},b,a_{i+1},\dots,a_{r})R\not=R
then
a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1},b,a_{i+1},\dots,a_{r}
is regular.

(4)

A sequence
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}
is regular if and only if
a_{1}^{i_{1}},\dots,a_{r}^{i_{r}}
is regular for given
i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\geq 1
(follows from (3)). Therefore, the depth of
I
is equal to the depth of the radical of
I
.

For Propertites (3) and (4), see e.g.
[
3
]
, Exercises in Chapter 7.1.

For
R
Noetherian we have the following additional properties (cf.
[
5
]
or
[
3
]
, Chapter 7.1).

(5)

If
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}
is a maximal (with respect to inclusion) regular sequence in
I
then
r=\operatorname{depth}I
.

(6)

Any regular sequence
a_{1},\dots,a_{q}
in a proper ideal
I\subset R
can be completed to a maximal regular sequence
a_{1},\dots,a_{q},\dots,a_{r}
in
I
.

(7)

If
a_{1},\dots,a_{q}\in I
is a regular sequence then
\operatorname{depth}I=\operatorname{depth}(I/(a_{1},\dots,a_{q}))+q
(follows from (5) and (6)).

(8)

If
R
is local then any permutation of a regular sequence is regular.

(9)

The depth, the hight and the minimal number of generators of
I
, denoted
\operatorname{gen}I
, satisfy the inequalities
\operatorname{depth}I\leq\operatorname{height}I\leq\operatorname{gen}I
.

(10)

\operatorname{depth}I=\operatorname{gen}I
if and only if
I
is generated by a regular sequence.

(11)

If
R
is a Cohen-Macauley ring then
\operatorname{depth}I=\operatorname{height}I
for any ideal
I\in R
(and vice versa).

(12)

If
\omega=(a_{1}\dots,a_{r})\in R^{r}
and the ideal
I
in
R
generated by
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}
is proper and nonzero then
\operatorname{depth}I
is equal to the maximal
p
such that
i
th cohomology
H_{i}:\operatorname{Im}\partial_{i-1}/\ker\partial_{i}
in the Koszul complex
\partial_{i}:\Lambda^{i}R^{r}\to\Lambda^{i+1}R^{r}
defined by
\eta\mapsto\omega\wedge\eta
vanishes for all
i<p
.

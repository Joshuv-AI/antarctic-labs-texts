Lambek Invariants of Commutative Squares in a Homological Category Thanks: The work of the first author was carried out in the framework of the State Task to the Sobolev Institute of Mathematics (Project FWNF–2022–0006). August 9, 2026
Yaroslav Kopylov, Vadim Leshkov

Abstract
. We consider the Lambek invariants (introduced by Joachim Lambek in 1964) in the context of semiexact and homological categories in the sense of Grandis. We generalize the Lambek isomorphism theorem to semiexact and homological categories. Throughout the text, for completeness we give proofs of some lemmas which are known in the additive case for the case of semiexact and homological categories. Key words and phrases: commutative square, semiexact category, homological category, Lambek invariants Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 18G50



1.
Introduction

In 1964, in
[
7
]
, Lambek proved the following assertion:

Given a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}

of groups and group homomorphisms with exact rows, there is a natural
isomorphism

({\rm Im}\,b\cap{\rm Im}\,f^{\prime})/{\rm Im}\,(bf)\cong{\rm Ker}(cg)/({\rm Ker}\,b\cdot{\rm Ker}\,g).

For a commutative
square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

in the category of groups we put:

{\rm Im}\,S:=({\rm Im}\,b\cap{\rm Im}\,g)/{\rm Im}\,(bf),\quad{\rm Ker}\,S:={\rm Ker}\,(bf)/({\rm Ker}\,a\cdot{\rm Ker}\,f).

Since
{\rm Ker}\,a
and
{\rm Ker}\,f
are normal subgroups in
{\rm Ker}(bf)
,
their product
{\rm Ker}a\cdot{\rm Ker}f
is the normal subgroup generated by them, and hence the quotient group
{\rm Ker}\,S
is always defined, whereas
{\rm Im}(bf)
is not always a normal subgroup in
{\rm Im}\,b\cap{\rm Im}\,g
.
However, in Lambek’s theorem
{\rm Im}(bf)=b({\rm Ker}\,g)\trianglelefteq({\rm Im}\,b\cap{\rm Im}\,f^{\prime})
.

Later Leicht
[
8
]
extended the Lambek’s theorem to a wider class of categories that
includes the category of groups
\mathcalb{G\!r\!p}
, thus giving a homological proof.
In
[
9
]
, Nomura in particular proved Lambek’s isomorphism
in a Puppe exact category.

In
[
5
]
, these invariants were considered in a quasi-abelian category, and
Lambek’s isomorphism was established provided that the morphism
b

is exact.

In the present article, we prove Lambek’s isomorphism in the framework of Grandis homological
categories (see
[
3
,
4
]
). These categories were introduced as a tool for developing
homological algebra “in a strogly non-abelian setting.” In recent years, Grandis homological
categories have found applications in homological algebra and the theory of approximate representations of groups
[
1
,
2
]
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the definitions
of various classes of categories with null morphisms and prove some auxiliary assertions.
In Section
3
, we introduce the canonical Lambek morphism in a semiexact category
and prove that this is an isomorphism in a Grandis homological category.

Lemma 1
.

The following are equivalent:

(i)

f
is an
\mathcalb{N}
-monomorphism;

(ii)

{\rm ker}\,f\in\mathcalb{N}
;

(iii)

{\rm ker}(fh)={\rm ker}\,h
for any
h
.

Dually, the following are equivalent:

(i)

f
is an
\mathcalb{N}
-epimorphism;

(ii)

{\rm coker}\,f\in\mathcalb{N}
;

(iii)

{\rm coker}(kf)=\mathrm{coker}\,k
for any
k
.

Let
(\mathcalb{C},\mathcalb{N})
be a category with null morphisms.
Consider the following two axioms
[
4
]
.

Axiom 1
.

The ideal
\mathcalb{N}
is closed.

Axiom 2
.

Every morphism has a kernel and cokernel with respect to
\mathcalb{N}
.

A category with null morphisms is called
semiexact
if it satisfies Axioms
1
and
2
.
Semiexact categories are also called
ex1-categories
.

Consider a morphism
f\colon A\to B
in a semiexact category
(\mathcalb{C},\mathcalb{N})
.
Note that
({\rm coker}\,f)f
is null.
Hence, there exists a unique morphism
f^{\prime}\colon A\to{\rm Im}\,f
such that
f=({\rm im}\,f)f^{\prime}
.
Note that
f^{\prime}({\rm ker}\,f)
is null.
Hence,
f^{\prime}
factors through the coimage of f, i.e. there exists a morphism
\overline{f}\colon{\rm Coim}\,f\to{\rm Im}\,f
.

(1)

f=({\rm im}\,f)\,\overline{f}\,({\rm coim}\,f).

We refer to (

of

Two normal decompositions of

The morphism
\overline{f}
in (

is defined uniquely once
{\rm im}\,f
and
{\rm coim}\,f
are chosen.
If
\overline{f}
is an isomorphism, then the morphism

exact
.
Usually, when

is exact, we omit the middle isomorphism and write
f=({\rm im}\,f)\,({\rm coim}\,f)
.
Omitting the middle isomorphism is appropriate because it is always possible to choose the correct isomorphic representatives of kernels and cokernels to make
\overline{f}
equal to the indentity morphism.

We prove several lemmas which are useful for the results of the present work.

The following lemma is an analog of
[
6
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 2
.

Given morphisms
f\colon X\to Y
and
g\colon Y\to Z
in a semiexact category
(\mathcalb{C},\mathcalb{N})
, the following identities hold:

\mathrm{coker}(g\,(\mathrm{im}\,f))=\mathrm{coker}(gf),\quad\mathrm{ker}((\mathrm{coim}\,g)\,f)=\mathrm{ker}(gf).

Proof.

Observe that
{\rm coker}(g({\rm im}\,f))gf\in\mathcalb{N}
.
Given a morphism
x\colon Z\to W
with
xgf\in\mathcalb{N}
, there exists a unique morphism
y\colon{\rm Coker}\,f\to W
such that
xg=y({\rm coker}\ f)
.
Hence,
xg({\rm im}f)=y({\rm coker}\ f)({\rm im}f)\in\mathcalb{N}
and there exists a unique
z\colon{\rm Coker}(g({\rm im}\,f))\to W
such that
x=z({\rm coker}(g({\rm im}\,f)))
.
Therefore,
{\rm coker}(g({\rm im}\,f))
is a cokernel of
gf
.
The second identity is obtained by duality.
∎

Corollary 1
.

In a semiexact category
(\mathcalb{C},\mathcalb{N})
, given morphisms
f
and
g
for which
gf
is defined, we have:

(i)

if kernels are closed under composition and
g
is a kernel then
\mathrm{im}(gf)=g(\mathrm{im}\,f)
;

(ii)

dually, if cokernels are closed under composition and
f
is a cokernel then
\mathrm{coim}(gf)=(\mathrm{coim}\,g)f
.

Proof.

(i) Observe that by Lemma
2
we have

{\rm im}(gf)={\rm ker}({\rm coker}(gf))={\rm ker}\,{\rm coker}(g({\rm ker}({\rm coker}\,f))).

Note that
g({\rm ker}({\rm coker}\,f))
is a kernel.
Hence, it is equal to its image.
Assertion (ii) holds by duality.
∎

Lemma 3

(Properties of exact morphisms)
.

The following assertions hold in a semiexact category
(\mathcalb{C},\mathcalb{N})
:

(i)

\mathrm{ker}\,\alpha
is an exact
\mathcalb{N}
-monomorphism and
\mathrm{coker}\,\alpha
is an exact
\mathcalb{N}
-epimorphism for any morphism
\alpha
;

(ii)

\alpha=\mathrm{im}\,\alpha
if and only if
\alpha
is an exact
\mathcalb{N}
-monomorphism and
\alpha=\mathrm{coim}\,\alpha
if and only if
\alpha
is an exact
\mathcalb{N}
-epimorphism;

(iii)

if
f\colon A\to B
is a morphism such that
f=hg
, where
h
is a kernel and
g
is a cokernel, then
f
is exact, i.e.,
f=(\mathrm{im}\,f)\,(\mathrm{coim}\,f)
.

Proof.

(i)

It is obvious that kernels are
\mathcalb{N}
-monomorphisms and cokernels are
\mathcalb{N}
-epimorphisms.
We show that (co)kernels are exact.
Suppose that
\beta
is a kernel:
\beta={\rm ker}\,\alpha
for some morphism
\alpha
.
Consider its normal decomposition.
Note that

\beta=({\rm im}\,\beta)\overline{\beta}({\rm coim}\,\beta)=({\rm im}\,\beta)\,\overline{\beta}\,\overbrace{({\rm coker}(\,\underbrace{{\rm ker}\,\beta}_{\in\mathcalb{N}}\,))}^{{\rm id}},

because
\beta
is a kernel.
Assume without loss of generality that
\beta={\rm ker}\,\alpha=({\rm im}\,\beta)\overline{\beta}
.
Hence,
{\rm im}\,\beta={\rm ker}({\rm coker}\,\beta)={\rm ker}({\rm coim}\,\alpha)
.
By Corollary
1
,
{\rm ker}({\rm coim}\,\alpha)={\rm ker}\,\alpha
.
Therefore,
{\rm im}\,\beta={\rm ker}\,\alpha
.
Hence,
\overline{\beta}
is an isomorphism and
\beta
is exact.
Consequently, kernels are exact and, by duality, cokernels are also exact.

(ii)

If
\alpha={\rm im}\,\alpha
then
\alpha
is an exact
\mathcalb{N}
-monomorphism by the definition of an exact morphism and the definition of a kernel.
Suppose that
\alpha
is an exact
\mathcalb{N}
-monomorphism.
Hence,
\alpha=({\rm im}\,\alpha)({\rm coim}\,\alpha)
and
\alpha
has a null kernel:
{\rm ker}\,\alpha\in\mathcalb{N}
.
Hence,
{\rm coim}\,\alpha={\rm coker}({\rm ker}\,\alpha)
is an isomorphism and
\alpha
is its own image.
Dually,
\alpha={\rm coim}\,\alpha
if and only if
\alpha
is an exact
\mathcalb{N}
-epimorphism.

(iii)

Note that
f\,({\rm ker}\,g)=hg\,({\rm ker}\,g)\in\mathcalb{N}
.
Hence, there exists a unique morphism
\alpha\colon{\rm Ker}\,g\to{\rm Ker}\,f
such that
{\rm ker}\,g=({\rm ker}\,f)\,\alpha
.
Also, note that

and

is an
\mathcalb{N}
-monomorphism.
Hence,
g\,({\rm ker}\,f)\in\mathcalb{N}
and
{\rm ker}\,f
factors through
{\rm ker}\,g

\beta\colon{\rm Ker}\,f\to{\rm Ker}\,g
such that
{\rm ker}\,f=({\rm ker}\,g)\,\beta
.
By the universal property of the kernel, we get
\alpha\beta={\rm id}_{{\rm Ker}\,f}
and
\beta\alpha={\rm id}_{{\rm Ker}\,g}
.
Hence, the kernels of
f
and
g
are isomorphic and, dually, the cokernels of
f
and
h
are isomorphic.

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

2.2.
Homological categories

Let
(\mathcalb{C},\mathcalb{N})
be a semiexact category.
Consider the following axiom:

Axiom 3
.

Kernels and cokernels are closed under composition.

A semiexact category is called an
ex2-category

[
4
, 2.1.3]
if it satisfies Axiom
3
.

Axiom 4
.

Consider a kernel

i\colon A\rightarrowtail B
and a cokernel

q\colon B\twoheadrightarrow C

such that
(\mathrm{coker}\,i)(\mathrm{ker}\,q)\in\mathcalb{N}
.
Then there exist a cokernel

\pi\colon A\twoheadrightarrow H

and a kernel

\iota\colon H\rightarrowtail C

which make the following square commute:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

The object
H
in Axiom
4
is called a
homology object
.

An ex2-category is called
homological
(or
ex3-category
) if it satisfies Axiom
4
.
It is proved in
[
4
, 2.2.2]
that for an ex2-category, Axiom
4
is equivalent to the following axiom:

Axiom 4*
.

The pullback of a pair
\bullet\twoheadrightarrow\bullet\leftarrowtail\bullet
is of the form
\bullet\leftarrowtail\bullet\twoheadrightarrow\bullet
.
Dually, the pushout of a pair
\bullet\leftarrowtail\bullet\twoheadrightarrow\bullet
is of the form
\bullet\twoheadrightarrow\bullet\leftarrowtail\bullet
.

It is important to note that in the category of groups
\mathcalb{G\!r\!p}
is semiexact and it is not homological and not even an ex2-category.
In
\mathcalb{G\!r\!p}
the pullback of a pair
\bullet\twoheadrightarrow\bullet\leftarrowtail\bullet
exists and has the form
\bullet\leftarrowtail\bullet\twoheadrightarrow\bullet
. However, the composition of two kernels
need not be a kernel in
\mathcalb{G\!r\!p}
.

\mathcalb{N}
-monomorphism.
By commutativity we have
\chi=l({\rm coker}\,\psi)
, hence,
{\rm ker}\,\chi={\rm ker}(l({\rm coker}\,\psi))={\rm im}\,\psi
.
Therefore, (
5
) is exact at
{\rm Ker}\,g
.
Dually, if
g
is exact then
\varepsilon
is exact and (
5
) is exact at
{\rm Coker}\,f
.
∎

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that in diagram

and

Theorem 2

(The Lambek isomorphism)
.

Suppose that in diagram

in a homological category

Proof.

Consider diagram (

.
By Lemma
4
,

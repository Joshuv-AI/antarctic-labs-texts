Singular and fractional integral operators on weighted local Morrey spaces
Javier Duoandikoetxea and Marcel Rosenthal

Abstract
. We obtain a characterization of the weighted inequalities for the Riesz transforms on weighted local Morrey spaces. The condition is sufficient for the boundedness on the same spaces of all Calderón-Zygmund operators suitably defined on the functions of the space. In the case of the fractional maximal operator and the fractional integral we obtain a characterization valid for exponents satisfying the Sobolev relation. For power weights we get sharp results in a larger range of exponents for the usual versions of weighted Morrey spaces.

1.
Introduction

We define for
1\leq p<\infty
the weighted local (also known as central) Morrey space
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)
as the class of measurable functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which

(1.1)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)}:=\sup_{B}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi(B)}\int_{B}|f|^{p}w\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all the Euclidean balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered at
0
. Here
w
is a weight, that is, a nonnegative locally integrable function, and
\varphi
is a function defined on the set of balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered at
0
, with values in
(0,\infty)
. For the global Morrey spaces all the Euclidean balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are taken.

The most studied cases of
\varphi
correspond to the weighted Morrey spaces introduced by N. Samko in
[
17
]
, and by Y. Komori and S. Shirai in
[
9
]
. In general, we need to impose some conditions on
\varphi
to avoid trivial spaces. We fix some conditions in Section
2
.

The characterization of the weights
w
for which the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator is bounded on global Morrey spaces is an open problem even for the most familiar cases of
\varphi
mentioned in the preceding paragraph. Nevertheless, a remarkable result of
S. Nakamura, Y. Sawano, and H. Tanaka in
[
16
]
gives necessary and sufficient conditions for the boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
on weighted local Morrey spaces. In
[
4
]
the authors of this paper obtained a simplified characterization of the same result in the form of a Muckenhoupt-type condition adapted to the Morrey setting. More precisely, we showed that the condition

(1.2)

\sup_{B}\frac{\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)}\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)^{\prime}}}{|B|}<\infty

is necessary and sufficient for the boundedness of
M
on
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)
. (The notation
X^{\prime}
stands for the Köthe dual of the Banach lattice
X
. See Subsection
2.2
.) In this paper we deal first with singular integral operators and show that the slightly stronger condition

(1.3)

\sup_{B}\frac{\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)}\|M\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)^{\prime}}}{|B|}<\infty

is necessary and sufficient for the boundedness of the Riesz transforms on
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)
. This is valid for
1<p<\infty
, and for
p=1
the result holds if we consider weak-type inequalities. The sufficiency is extended to all Calderón-Zygmund operators. We remark that due to the lack of density of smooth functions in the Morrey-type spaces the operators need to be defined in a convenient way. Condition (
1.3
) is the limiting case of the sufficient condition obtained in
[
4
, Theorem 7.3]
by using extrapolation of Lebesgue-weighted inequalities.

Next we work with the fractional maximal operator and the fractional integrals. In this case our results are limited to a particular form of the spaces. In the classical paper
[
14
]
, B. Muckenhoupt and R. Wheeden characterized the weights
w
such that the fractional integral
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{p}(w^{p})
to
L^{q}(w^{q})
, when
p
and
q
are related by the Sobolev condition
1/p-1/q=\alpha/n
. The characterizations we obtain for the boundedness of the fractional operators on weighted local Morrey spaces are also limited by the same condition on the exponents and similar conditions on the weights. More precisely, we obtain necessary and sufficent conditions for the boundedness of
M_{\alpha}
and
I_{\alpha}
from
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})
to
\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi^{q},w^{q})
, with
p
and
q
as above. They are given by expressions which are similar to (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), respectively. In the case of
M_{\alpha}
a necessary and sufficient condition was already obtained in
[
16
]
. As we did for the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator, we simplify the characterization and give it by means of a single condition, namely,

(1.4)

\sup_{B}\frac{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{q}(\varphi^{q},w^{q})}\|\chi_{B}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})^{\prime}}}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}<\infty.

For the characterization of the boundedness of
I_{\alpha}
we need a stronger condition as happened for singular integrals. In this case the condition reads

(1.5)

\sup_{B}\frac{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{q}(\varphi^{q},w^{q})}\|(M\chi_{B})^{1-\alpha/n}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})^{\prime}}}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}<\infty.

A term of the form
(M\chi_{B})^{1-\alpha/n}
already appeared in the characterization of two-weighted Lebesgue inequalities for
M_{\alpha}
and
I_{\alpha}
(see
[
19
]
and
[
21
]
).

It is known that in the case of unweighted global Morrey spaces the range of
p
and
q
can be extended to
1/p-1/q=\alpha/(n-\lambda)
(where
\varphi(B)=r_{B}^{\lambda}
), due to a result of D. R. Adams. Weighted estimates on global Morrey spaces in the Adams range were obtained by T. Iida, Y. Komori-Furuya, and E. Sato in
[
8
]
, and more generally by S. Nakamura, Y. Sawano, and H. Tanaka in
[
15
]
. It is known that for unweighted local Morrey spaces the result in the Adams range does not hold (see
[
10
]
). We extend the impossibility of Adams-type estimates also to the weighted setting (Theorem
5.2
).

In our approach in
[
4
]
a crucial role was played by the local Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator, where local refers to the use of balls separated from the origin. These operators and their weighted inequalities were studied in
[
13
]
and
[
6
]
. In this paper we also need local versions of the operators we study. The weighted results we use were given in
[
7
]
and are presented in Subsection
2.4
.

In Section
7
we consider power weights and obtain sharp results for the special cases of weighted local Morrey spaces of Samko and Komori-Shirai type.

Notation

•

Given a set
A
,
\chi_{A}
is its characteristic function,
|A|
is its Lebesgue measure, and for a weight
v
,
v(A)
is the integral of
v
over
A
(but notice that
\varphi
is not a weight in the definition of the norm of the Morrey space);

•

for a ball
B
,
r_{B}
will denote its radius and
c_{B}
its center; for
\lambda>0
,
\lambda B
denotes the ball with center
c_{B}
and radius
\lambda r_{B}
;
\widetilde{B}
is the smallest ball centered at the origin containing
B
, that is,
\widetilde{B}=B(0,|c_{B}|+r_{B})
;

•

For
\varphi
defined on balls we denote as
\varphi^{p}
the function such that
\varphi^{p}(B)=\varphi(B)^{p}
;

•

F\lesssim G
means that the inequality
F\leq c\,G
holds with a constant
c
depending only on the ambient parameters; and
F\sim G
means that both
F\lesssim G
and
G\lesssim F
hold.

2.
Preliminaries

The most used versions of weighted Morrey spaces correspond to the choices
\varphi(B)=r_{B}^{\lambda}
(
0<\lambda<n
), introduced by N. Samko in
[
17
]
, and

\varphi(B)=w(B)^{\lambda/n}
(
0<\lambda<n
), introduced by Komori and Shirai in
[
9
]
. Generalized weighted (global and local) Morrey spaces have been considered by several authors. For general weighted local Morrey spaces we refer for instance to
[
16
]
.

2.1.
Conditions on
\varphi

We assume that
\varphi
is doubling, that is,

\varphi(2B)\lesssim\varphi(B)

for
B
centered at the origin.
We also assume that it satisfies a reverse doubling property of the form

(2.1)

\frac{\varphi(B(0,r))}{\varphi(B(0,R))}\lesssim\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\delta},

for some
\delta>0
. From this property it follows that if
0<b<1
it holds that

(2.2)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\varphi(B(0,b^{j}R))\lesssim\varphi(B(0,R)).

For a measure
\mu
the doubling condition on all the Euclidean balls of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
implies the reverse doubling property (see
[
20
, Lemma 20]
). When
\varphi
is of the form
\varphi(B)=\mu(B)^{\lambda}
for a doubling measure
\mu
, then (
2.1
) is satisfied.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
\varphi
satisfies (
2.2
). Then
the norm (
1.1
) is equivalent to

(2.3)

\sup_{B:r_{B}=\kappa|c_{B}|}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi(\widetilde{B})}\int_{B}|f|^{p}w\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

where
\kappa\in(0,1)
is fixed.

We recall that
\widetilde{B}
denotes the smallest ball centered at the origin containing
B
. The main idea for this reduction comes from
[
16
]
. We adapt here to local Morrey spaces the proof given in
[
3
, Proposition 2.1]
for global Morrey spaces.

Proof.

It is clear that (
2.3
) is not greater than (
1.1
) because
B\subset\widetilde{B}
.

Let
b>1/3
. Set
A_{j}:=B(0,b^{j}R)\setminus B(0,b^{j+1}R)
. There exists
C(n)
depending only on the dimension
n
such that the annulus
A_{j}
can be covered by
C(n)
balls
B_{k,j}:=B(y_{k,j},r_{j})
,
k=1,\dots,C(n)
, for which
|y_{k,j}|=b^{j}(1+b)R/2
(that is,
y_{k,j}
is equidistant from the spheres of the boundary of the annulus) and
r_{j}=b^{j}(1-b)R
(the width of the annulus). By construction
\widetilde{B_{k,j}}
is independent of
k
and satisfies
B(0,b^{j}R)\subset\widetilde{B_{k,j}}\subset B(0,b^{j-1}R)
. Then
\varphi(\widetilde{B_{k,j}})\lesssim\varphi(B(0,b^{j-1}R))
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(B(0,R))}\int_{B(0,R)}|f|^{p}w=\frac{1}{\varphi(B(0,R))}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{A_{j}}|f|^{p}w

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varphi(B(0,R))}\,C(n)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\varphi(B(0,b^{j-1}R))\sup_{k,j}\frac{1}{\varphi(\widetilde{B_{k,j}})}\int_{B_{k,j}}|f|^{p}w.

The balls
B_{k,j}
satisfy
r_{B}=\kappa|c_{B}|
for
\kappa=2(1-b)/(1+b)
. Using (
2.2
) we obtain the control of (
1.1
) by (
2.3
).
∎

Remark 2.2
.

For a function
f
supported in a ball
B
with
r_{B}\leq\kappa|c_{B}|
(
0<\kappa<1
) it holds that

(2.4)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)}\sim\left(\frac{1}{\varphi(\widetilde{B})}\int_{B}|f|^{p}w\right)^{1/p}.

This is because the balls
B(0,R)
with
R<|c_{B}|-r_{B}
do not intersect
B
and
\varphi(B(0,R))
is essentially constant for
|c_{B}|-r_{B}<R<|c_{B}|+r_{B}
, due to the properties of
\varphi
. In particular, if
f
is the characteristic function of
B
,

(2.5)

\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)}\sim\left(\frac{w(B)}{\varphi(\widetilde{B})}\right)^{1/p}.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Define
\psi(B)=\varphi(B)r_{B}^{\gamma}
and
v(x)=w(x)|x|^{\gamma}
. If both
\psi
and
\varphi
satisfy (
2.2
), then
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)=\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\psi,v)
.

Proof.

It is enough to realize that for the balls
B
involved in (
2.3
) if
x\in B
we have
|x|\sim r_{B}
, and also
r_{B}\sim r_{\widetilde{B}}
.
∎

The spaces need not be the same without the reduction of the norm to (
2.3
). For instance, if
\varphi(B)=r_{B}^{\lambda}
with
\lambda>0
and
w(x)\equiv 1
, the space
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)
is not trivial, but
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\psi,v)=\{0\}
if
\lambda+\gamma
is negative.

2.2.
The Köthe dual (associate space) of a Banach lattice

Let
X
be a Banach lattice of measurable functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(a Banach space in which
|g|\leq|f|
a.e. and
f\in X
implies
g\in X
and
\|g\|_{X}\leq\|f\|_{X}
). We define its Köthe dual as the space
X^{\prime}
collecting the measurable functions
g
such that

(2.6)

\|g\|_{X^{\prime}}:=\sup\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|fg|:f\in X\text{ and }\|f\|_{X}\leq 1\right\}<\infty.

It is also a Banach lattice. An immediate consequence of (
2.6
) is the Hölder-type inequality

(2.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|fg|\leq\|f\|_{X}\|g\|_{X^{\prime}}.

2.3.
Weak-type local Morrey spaces

We define for
1\leq p<\infty
the weighted weak-type local Morrey space
W\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)
as the collection of measurable functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which

(2.8)

\|f\|_{W\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)}=\sup_{B}\sup_{t>0}\frac{t(w(\{x\in B:|f(x)|>t\})^{1/p}}{\varphi(B)^{1/p}}<\infty.

It is clear that
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)
is continuously embedded in
W\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)
. Also that for a measurable set
E
, it holds

(2.9)

\|\chi_{E}\|_{W\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)}=\|\chi_{E}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)}.

In particular, we have (
2.5
) with
W\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)
instead of
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,w)
.

2.4.
The local Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator, local fractional operators and local singular integrals

For fixed
\kappa\in(0,1)
we define the basis
\mathcal{B}_{\kappa,\text{loc}}
as the family of balls
B
such that
r_{B}<\kappa|c_{B}|
. For
\alpha\in[0,n)
we define the operator
M_{\kappa,\alpha,\text{loc}}
associated to
\mathcal{B}_{\kappa,\text{loc}}
acting on
f
at
x\neq 0
as

(2.10)

M_{\kappa,\alpha,\text{loc}}f(x)=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{B}_{\kappa,\text{loc}}}\frac{1}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{B}|f|.

For
\alpha=0
this is the local Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator, which we denote simply as
M_{\kappa,\text{loc}}
. For
\alpha\in(0,n)
it is the local fractional maximal operator. Notice that the term local here is associated to a family of balls different from the one used in the definition of the local Morrey spaces.

C.-C. Lin and K. Stempak studied in
[
13
]
a variant of
M_{\kappa,\text{loc}}
and characterized its weighted inequalities by a local
A_{p}
condition. E. Harboure, O. Salinas, and B. Viviani obtained in
[
6
]
a similar result in a more general setting, and in
[
7
]
they studied weighted inequalities for the fractional case and for singular integrals. Their result applied to the operator in (
2.10
) says the following: let
1<p<n/\alpha
and
q
given by
1/p-1/q=\alpha/n
. Then
M_{\kappa,\alpha,\text{loc}}
is bounded from
L^{p}(w^{p})
to
L^{q}(w^{q})
if and only if
w
satisfies the
A_{p,q,\text{loc}}
condition

(2.11)

\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{\kappa,\text{loc}}}\frac{w^{q}(B)^{1/q}\,w^{-p^{\prime}}(B)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{|B|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}<\infty.

For
\alpha=0
this is the usual
A_{p}
condition adapted to the local setting (written for
w^{p}
instead of
w
), and for
\alpha>0
this is the local version of the condition for fractional operators in
[
14
]
. For
p=1
and
q=n/(n-\alpha)
the condition is modified as usual,

(2.12)

\frac{w^{n/(n-\alpha)}(B)}{|B|}\lesssim\inf_{x\in B}w(x)^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}\quad\text{for}\quad B\in\mathcal{B}_{\kappa,\text{loc}},

and it characterizes the weak-type boundedness of
M_{\kappa,\alpha,\text{loc}}
from
L^{1}(w)
to
L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha},\infty}(w)
.

In the mentioned papers the authors also proved that the conditions are independent of
\kappa
, so that we do not use this parameter in the notation of the weight class. It is clear that the usual
A_{p,q}
weights are in
A_{p,q,\text{loc}}
and it is immediate to check that
|x|^{\alpha}w(x)\in A_{p,q,\text{loc}}
whenever
w\in A_{p,q}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

In
[
7
]
the local fractional integral
I_{\kappa,\alpha,\text{loc}}
is defined as

(2.13)

I_{\kappa,\alpha,\text{loc}}f(x)=\int_{\{y:|y-x|<\kappa|x|\}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,dy.

It is proved that the boundedness from
L^{p}(w^{p})
to
L^{q}(w^{q})
of
I_{\kappa,\alpha,\text{loc}}
is characterized also by (
2.11
), and the weak-type for
p=1
by (
2.12
).

A local version of Calderón-Zygmund operators was also introduced in
[
7
]
. We do not need this general concept for our work, but only the following particular result.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
7
, Corollary 5.2]

Let
T
be a Calderón-Zygmund operator in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
. Define

(2.14)

T_{\kappa}f(x)=T(f\chi_{B(x,\kappa|x|)})(x).

Then
T_{\kappa}
is bounded on
L^{p}(w)
for
1<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p,\text{loc}}
, and of weak type (1,1) with respect to weights in
A_{1,\text{loc}}
.

3.
Necessary condition for singular integrals

A Calderón-Zygmund operator
T
is an operator bounded on
L^{2}
and associated with a standard kernel
K
in the sense that if
f
is a compactly supported
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function, and
x\notin\text{supp}\,f
,
Tf(x)
is given by

(3.1)

Tf(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(x,y)f(y)\,dy.

A standard kernel
K
must satisfiy a size condition

(3.2)

|K(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

and a regularity condition which we will not need to detail here (see
[
5
, Subsection 8.1.1]
, for instance).
Using the
L^{2}
boundedness of
T
it is shown in
[
5
, Proposition 8.1.9]
that if
f
is bounded and compactly supported, and
x\notin\text{supp}\,f
, the representation (
3.1
) holds as an absolutely convergent integral. We will assume that when we extend the operator to local Morrey spaces we keep such representation for bounded compactly supported functions.

We obtain the necessary condition for Riesz transforms in the general setting of Banach lattices. We recall that the Riesz transform
R_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq n
) is defined (usually up to a constant factor) as the following principal value integral:

(3.3)

R_{j}f(x)=\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{x_{j}-y_{j}}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,f(y)dy.

The definition makes sense for smooth functions. It is a Calderón-Zygmund operator whose kernel is given by

K(x,y)=\frac{x_{j}-y_{j}}{|x-y|^{n+1}}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach lattice of measurable functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that contains the characteristic functions of balls. If the Riesz transforms are bounded on
X
, then

(3.4)

\sup_{B}\frac{\|\chi_{B}\|_{X}\|M\chi_{B}\|_{X^{\prime}}}{|B|}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We give first a lemma, which will be used in the proof of the theorem.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
B
and
B^{\prime}
balls with the same radius such that
c_{B^{\prime}}=c_{B}+4r_{B}\mathbf{e}_{j}
, where
\mathbf{e}_{j}
is the unit vector in the direction of the
j
-th component. If
R_{j}
is bounded on
X
, then
\|\chi_{B}\|_{X}\sim\|\chi_{B^{\prime}}\|_{X}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in B
and
y\in B^{\prime}
. We have
y_{j}>x_{j}
and
y_{j}-x_{j}\sim|x-y|\sim r_{B}
. Then

|R_{j}\chi_{B^{\prime}}(x)|=\int_{B^{\prime}}\frac{y_{j}-x_{j}}{|x-y|^{n+1}}dy\sim 1.

Hence
\chi_{B}(x)\lesssim|R_{j}\chi_{B^{\prime}}(x)|
and taking norms in
X
and using the boundedness of
R_{j}
we deduce that
\|\chi_{B}\|_{X}\lesssim\|\chi_{B^{\prime}}\|_{X}
. Changing the roles of
B
and
B^{\prime}
we obtain the reverse inequality and the lemma is proved.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

In the case of the Morrey spaces, the norm of
\chi_{B}
in
L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)
and in
WL\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)
is the same. It is enough to assume that
R_{j}
is bounded from
L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)
to
WL\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)
to get the conclusion of the lemma.

X
, using the boundedness of
R_{1}
, and Lemma
3.2
we obtain

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{2Q}|f(y)|{M\chi_{B}(y)}dy\ \|\chi_{B}\|_{X}\lesssim\|f\|_{X}.

Adding this estimate to the sum for
j=1,\dots,n
of the estimates (
3.6
) we get

(3.7)

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(y)|{M\chi_{B}(y)}dy\ \|\chi_{B}\|_{X}\lesssim\|f\|_{X}.

We obtained (
3.7
) for bounded functions with compact support. Given a general
f
in
X
, for each
N>1
we define

f_{N}(x)=\min\{|f(x)|,N\}\chi_{|x|\leq N}(x).

Since
X
is a Banach lattice,
f_{N}\in X
and
\|f_{N}\|_{X}\leq\|f\|_{X}
. We can apply (
3.7
) to
f_{N}
to obtain

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f_{N}(y)|{M\chi_{B}(y)}dy\ \|\chi_{B}\|_{X}\lesssim\|f_{N}\|_{X}\leq\|f\|_{X}.

Using the monotone convergence theorem we get (
3.7
) for every
f\in X
.
Taking the supremum over the functions
f
such that
\|f\|_{X}\leq 1
we obtain (
3.4
).
∎

4.
Calderón-Zygmund operators on weighted local Morrey spaces

Calderón-Zygmund operators are first defined on smooth functions and can be extended by density to other spaces like weighted
L^{p}
spaces, for instance. Nevertheless, due to the lack of density of smooth functions on the weighted local Morrey spaces used in the results of this section, we need to give an appropriate definition. To this end, we proceed as follows. Let
T
be a Calderón-Zygmund operator associated to the kernel
K
and
f\in L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)
with
w
satisfying (
1.3
). Since (
1.3
) is stronger than (
1.2
), we know that
w\in A_{p,\textrm{loc}}
(see
[
4
, Lemma 6.2]
). Let
B
be a ball such that
|c_{B}|=6r_{B}
. Decompose
f
as
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, where
f_{1}=f\chi_{2B}
. We have that
f_{1}
is in
L^{1}
, hence
Tf_{1}
is well defined. The integrability of
f_{1}
is a consequence of the inequality

\int_{2B}|f|\leq\left(\int_{2B}|f|^{p}w\right)^{1/p}w^{1-p^{\prime}}(2B)^{1/p^{\prime}},

where the last term is finite because
w\in A_{p,\text{loc}}
. (For
p=1
the last term is replaced by
\sup_{x\in 2B}w(x)^{-1}
.)

On the other hand, for
x\in B
, we define

(4.1)

Tf_{2}(x)=\int_{(2B)^{c}}K(x,y)f(y)dy.

This integral is well defined because for
x\in B
we have

|K(x,y)\chi_{(2B)^{c}}(y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|B|}M\chi_{B}(y),

using the size condition (
3.2
) of the kernel
K
, and
M\chi_{B}\in L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)^{\prime}
by (
1.3
). Moreover, we have the following pointwise estimate:

(4.2)

\left|\int_{(2B)^{c}}K(x,y)f(y)dy\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{|B|}\|M\chi_{B}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)}.

Thus we define
Tf
on
B
as
Tf_{1}+Tf_{2}
. We need to check that if
B^{\prime}
is another ball such that
|c_{B^{\prime}}|=6r_{B^{\prime}}
and
B\cap B^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, both definitions of
Tf
coincide almost everywhere on
B\cap B^{\prime}
, that is,

T(f\chi_{2B})(x)+\int_{(2B)^{c}}K(x,y)f(y)dy=T(f\chi_{2B^{\prime}})(x)+\int_{(2B^{\prime})^{c}}K(x,y)f(y)dy,

for almost every
x\in B\cap B^{\prime}
. This can be written as

\displaystyle T(f(\chi_{2B}-\chi_{2B^{\prime}}))(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{2B\setminus 2B^{\prime}}K(x,y)f(y)dy-\int_{2B^{\prime}\setminus 2B}K(x,y)f(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(x,y)\,[f(\chi_{2B}-\chi_{2B^{\prime}})](y)dy.

The equality holds because
f(\chi_{2B}-\chi_{2B^{\prime}})
is in
L^{1}
and
x\in B\cap B^{\prime}
is outside the support of
f
. Indeed, the representation formula (
3.1
) can be used for
L^{1}
functions as proved in
[
5
, Proposition 8.2.2]
.

A priori estimates with Morrey norms are not enough to extend singular integral operators (and others) to the whole Morrey space. This question, disregarded in a number of papers, was addressed already in
[
1
]
, where duality was used to define the Calderón-Zygmund operator on the corresponding Morrey space. Since then different ways had been considered as can be seen, for instance, in
[
18
, Section 10.4, vol. I]
. Our definition can be viewed as the natural extension to the weighted local Morrey space of the definition of the operator in
L^{1}
in the sense that for functions in
L^{1}\cap L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)
they coincide. In
[
12
]
, for instance, a similar definition appears in a quite general context.

We notice that for
x\in B
we have
2B\subset B(x,3/5|x|)
. Indeed, if
y\in 2B
,

|y-x|\leq|y-c_{B}|+|c_{B}-x|\leq 3r_{B}\leq\frac{3}{5}|x|,

because
|c_{B}|=6r_{B}
. Then
f\chi_{2B}=(f\chi_{2B})\chi_{B(x,3/5|x|)}
, hence

(4.3)

T(f\chi_{2B})=T_{3/5}(f\chi_{2B})

in the sense of (
2.14
). By using
[
7
, Corollary 5.2]
we know that
T_{3/5}
is bounded on
L^{p}(w)
for
w\in A_{p,\text{loc}}
for
1<p<\infty
, and is of weak-type
(1,1)
with respect to
w
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Let
T
be a Calderón-Zygmund operator and let
w
be a weight satisfying (
1.3
).
Then the operator
T
defined as indicated is bounded on
L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)
.

Let
p=1
. Then
T
is of weak type
(1,1)
, that is, it is bounded from
L\mathcal{M}^{1}(\varphi,w)
to
WL\mathcal{M}^{1}(\varphi,w)
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a ball such that
|c_{B}|=6r_{B}
. Decompose
f
as
f_{1}+f_{2}
as before. On the one hand, using (
4.2
) we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\varphi(B)}\int_{B}|Tf_{2}|^{p}w\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{w(B)}{\varphi(B)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\frac{1}{|B|}\|M\chi_{B}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\chi_{B}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)}\frac{1}{|B|}\|M\chi_{B}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)}.

On the other hand, taking into account (
4.3
) and that
T_{3/5}
is bounded on
L^{p}(w)
for
w\in A_{p,\text{loc}}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(B)}\int_{B}|Tf_{1}|^{p}w

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varphi(B)}\int_{B}|T_{3/5}f_{1}|^{p}w

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varphi(B)}\int_{2B}|f|^{p}w\lesssim\|f\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)}^{p}.

The theorem is proved for
1<p<\infty
.

In the case
p=1
the part corresponding to
Tf_{2}
is still valid, and for
Tf_{1}
we replace the
L^{p}(w)
-boundedness by the weak-type (1,1) estimate for
T_{3/5}
.
∎

Combining this result with the necessary condition of the previous section we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

The Riesz transforms are bounded on
L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi,w)
for
1<p<\infty
if and only if
w
satisfies (
1.3
). The Riesz transforms are bounded from
L\mathcal{M}^{1}(\varphi,w)
to
WL\mathcal{M}^{1}(\varphi,w)
if and only if (
1.3
) holds for
p=1
.

5.
Necessary conditions for fractional operators

We recall that the fractional maximal operator
M_{\alpha}
is defined for locally integrable functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as in (
2.10
), but without restrictions on the balls, that is, the supremum is taken over all balls containing
x
. The fractional integral
I_{\alpha}
is defined as in (
2.13
) with the integral extended to all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We also recall the pointwise bound
M_{\alpha}f(x)\leq I_{\alpha}f(x)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach lattice and
Y
a normed space.

(a) If the fractional maximal operator
M_{\alpha}
is bounded from
X
to
Y
, then

(5.1)

\sup_{B}\frac{\|\chi_{B}\|_{Y}\|\chi_{B}\|_{X^{\prime}}}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}<\infty.

(b) If the fractional integral operator
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
X
to
Y
, then

(5.2)

\sup_{B}\frac{\|\chi_{B}\|_{Y}\|(M\chi_{B})^{1-\alpha/n}\|_{X^{\prime}}}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}<\infty.

Proof.

(a) Let
B
be a ball. By definition of
M_{\alpha}
we have

\frac{1}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{B}|f|\leq M_{\alpha}f(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in B,

that is,

\left(\frac{1}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{B}|f|\right)\chi_{B}(x)\leq M_{\alpha}f(x).

Taking the norm in
Y
and assuming that
M_{\alpha}
is bounded from
X
to
Y
,

\left(\frac{1}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int\chi_{B}|f|\right)\|\chi_{B}\|_{Y}\leq\|M_{\alpha}f\|_{Y}\lesssim\|f\|_{X}.

Taking the supremum over
f
such that
\|f\|_{X}\leq 1
we obtain (
5.1
).

(b) Let
B
be a ball and
x\in B
. Then

|x-y|\lesssim r_{B}+|c_{B}-y|
. Hence,

M\chi_{B}(y)\sim\frac{|B|}{(r_{B}+|c_{B}-y|)^{n}}\lesssim\frac{|B|}{|x-y|^{n}}.

Therefore, for
x\in B
,

I_{\alpha}(|f|)(x)=\int\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,dy\gtrsim\int|f(y)|\left(\frac{M\chi_{B}(y)}{|B|}\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,dy.

We deduce that

\left[\frac{1}{|B|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\int|f|(M\chi_{B})^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\right]\chi_{B}(x)\lesssim I_{\alpha}(|f|)(x),

and proceeding as in part (a) we obtain (
5.2
).
∎

We obtain in the following theorem a restriction on the exponents of the local Morrey spaces for the boundedness of the fractional operators. It is enough to state it for
M_{\alpha}
because of the pointwise bound between
M_{\alpha}
and
I_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

If the fractional maximal operator
M_{\alpha}
is bounded from
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,u)
to
W\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\psi,v)
, then

(5.3)

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{\alpha}{n}.

Proof.

Let
x\neq 0
be a Lebesgue point of both
u
and
v
, such that
v(x)\neq 0
. Let
B_{r}=B(x,r)
with
4r\leq|x|
. Let
\widehat{B}=B(0,2|x|)
. Then

\|\chi_{B_{r}}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi,u)}\sim\left(\frac{u(B_{r})}{\varphi(\widetilde{B}_{r})}\right)^{1/p}\sim\left(\frac{u(B_{r})}{\varphi(\widehat{B})}\right)^{1/p},

and

\|\chi_{B_{r}}\|_{W\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\psi,v)}=\|\chi_{B_{r}}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\psi,v)}\sim\left(\frac{v(B_{r})}{\psi(\widehat{B})}\right)^{1/q}.

Using that
r^{\alpha}\chi_{B_{r}}(y)\leq M_{\alpha}\chi_{B_{r}}(y)
, from the boundedness assumption we deduce

r^{\alpha}\left(\frac{v(B_{r})}{\psi(\widehat{B})}\right)^{1/q}\lesssim\left(\frac{u(B_{r})}{\varphi(\widehat{B})}\right)^{1/p},

which can be written as

(5.4)

r^{\alpha+n/q-n/p}\left(\frac{v(B_{r})}{r^{n}}\right)^{1/q}\lesssim\left(\frac{u(B_{r})}{r^{n}}\right)^{1/p}\frac{\psi(\widehat{B})^{1/q}}{\varphi(\widehat{B})^{1/p}}.

Since
x
is a Lebesgue point of
u
and
v
, we have

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{v(B_{r})}{r^{n}}=v(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{u(B_{r})}{r^{n}}=u(x).

Hence, letting
r
tend to zero in (
5.4
), we see that the inequality can only hold if
\alpha+n/q-n/p\geq 0
.
∎

This theorem extends to a general weighted setting a result which is known in the unweighted setting with
\varphi(B)=\psi(B)=r_{B}^{\lambda}
. A counterexample is in
[
10
]
; see also
[
18
, Example 158]
. This is in contrast with the case of global Morrey spaces, where Adams extended the unweighted result to the range
1/p-1/q=\alpha/(n-\lambda)
. For weighted inequalities in the Adams range on global Morrey spaces see
[
8
]
and
[
15
]
. Let us mention that it is possible to obtain an Adams-type result on (unweighted) local Morrey spaces if we restrict the action of the operator to radial functions (see
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
).

6.
Sufficient conditions for fractional operators

Before characterizing the weights for
M_{\alpha}
we prove a lemma, similar to Lemma 6.2 in
[
4
]
. We use the notation
\varphi^{p}
to denote the function such that
\varphi^{p}(B)=\varphi(B)^{p}
as mentioned in the introduction.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
1\leq p<n/\alpha
and
1/p-1/q=\alpha/n
. Then
w\in A_{p,q,\text{loc}}
as defined in (
2.11
) if and only if
w
satisfies (
1.4
) restricted to the balls
B
such that
r_{B}\leq\kappa|c_{B}|
for some fixed
\kappa\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a ball such that
r_{B}\leq\kappa|c_{B}|
. We have from (
2.5
) that

(6.1)

\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi^{q},w^{q})}\sim\left(\frac{w^{q}(B)}{\varphi^{q}(\widetilde{B})}\right)^{1/q}=\frac{w^{q}(B)^{1/q}}{\varphi(\widetilde{B})}.

Let
p>1
. We claim that

(6.2)

\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})^{\prime}}\sim w^{-p^{\prime}}(B)^{1/p^{\prime}}\varphi(\widetilde{B}).

On the one hand we have

\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})^{\prime}}\geq\frac{1}{\|w^{-p^{\prime}}\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})}}\int_{B}w^{-p^{\prime}}\sim w^{-p^{\prime}}(B)^{1/p^{\prime}}\varphi(\widetilde{B}),

where we used (
2.4
) for the norm of
w^{-p^{\prime}}\chi_{B}
.

On the other hand,

\int_{B}|f|\leq\left(\int_{B}|f|^{p}w^{p}\right)^{1/p}w^{-p^{\prime}}(B)^{1/p^{\prime}}\leq\varphi(\widetilde{B})\|f\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})}w^{-p^{\prime}}(B)^{1/p^{\prime}}.

Taking the supremum over the functions
f
with
\|f\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})}\leq 1
we prove the claim (
6.2
).

Therefore,

\frac{\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi^{q},w^{q})}\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})^{\prime}}}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}\sim\frac{w^{q}(B)^{1/q}w^{-p^{\prime}}(B)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}},

and this proves the lemma for
p>1
.

Let
p=1
. We have (
6.1
) with
q=n/(n-\alpha)
. Let
w\in A_{1,n/(n-\alpha),\text{loc}}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{B}|f|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B}|f|w\ \left(\frac{|B|}{w^{n/(n-\alpha)}(B)}\right)^{1-\alpha/n}

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(\widetilde{B})\left(\frac{|B|}{w^{n/(n-\alpha)}(B)}\right)^{1-\alpha/n}\|f\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{1}(\varphi,w)}.

From here we obtain

\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{1}(\varphi,w)^{\prime}}\lesssim\varphi(\widetilde{B})\left(\frac{|B|}{w^{n/(n-\alpha)}(B)}\right)^{1-\alpha/n},

which together with (
6.1
) implies that
w
satisfies (
1.4
) for the selected balls.

Let
E
be a subset of
B
of positive measure. Then

\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{1}(\varphi,w)^{\prime}}\geq\frac{1}{\|\chi_{E}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{1}(\varphi,w)}}\int_{B}\chi_{E}\sim\frac{\varphi(\widetilde{B})|E|}{w(E)}.

Using (
6.1
) and (
1.4
) we obtain

(6.3)

\left(\frac{w^{n/(n-\alpha)}(B)}{|B|}\right)^{1-\alpha/n}\lesssim\frac{w(E)}{|E|}.

With
E=\{x\in B:w(x)\leq\inf_{B}w+\epsilon\}
we obtain

\left(\frac{w^{n/(n-\alpha)}(B)}{|B|}\right)^{1-\alpha/n}\lesssim\inf_{B}w+\epsilon.

Since this holds for any
\epsilon>0
we get the
A_{1,n/(n-\alpha),\text{loc}}
condition.
∎

In the following theorem we obtain the characterization of weighted inequalities for
M_{\alpha}
and
I_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
0<\alpha<n
,
1<p<n/\alpha
, and
1/p-1/q=\alpha/n
.

(a) The fractional maximal operator
M_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})
to
L\mathcal{M}^{q}(\varphi^{q},w^{q})
if and only if (
1.4
) holds.

(b) The fractional integral operator
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})
to
L\mathcal{M}^{q}(\varphi^{q},w^{q})
if and only if (
1.5
) holds.

(c) For
p=1
and
q=n/(n-\alpha)
both results hold with
M_{\alpha}
and
I_{\alpha}
bounded from
L\mathcal{M}^{1}(\varphi,w)
to
WL\mathcal{M}^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}(\varphi^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}},w^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}})
.

Proof.

The necessity of all cases has been proved in the previous section. Let us prove the sufficiency.

(a) Let
B
with
|c_{B}|=6r_{B}
. Given a nonnegative function
f
we decompose it as
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, where
f_{1}=f\chi_{2B}
. Using the subadditivity of
M_{\alpha}
we have

M_{\alpha}f(y)\leq M_{\alpha}f_{1}(y)+M_{\alpha}f_{2}(y).

Since
f_{1}
is supported in
2B
, to compute
M_{\alpha}f_{1}(y)
for
y\in B
we only need to consider balls
B(y,\rho)
with
\rho<3r_{B}
. Moreover, for
r_{B}<\rho<3r_{B}
we can replace
B(y,\rho)
with
2B
. Therefore,

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}f_{1}(y)

\displaystyle\sim\sup_{\rho\leq r_{B}}\frac{1}{|B(y,\rho)|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\int_{B(y,\rho)}|f|+\frac{1}{|2B|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\int_{2B}|f|

\displaystyle\sim M_{1/5,\alpha,\textrm{loc}}f_{1}(y)+\frac{1}{|2B|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\int_{2B}|f|.

On the other hand, since
f_{2}
is supported on
(2B)^{c}
, to compute
M_{\alpha}f_{2}(y)
for
y\in B
one only needs to consider balls of radius greater than
r_{B}
, and as a consequence,
M_{\alpha}f_{2}
is almost constant on
B
. Then

M_{\alpha}f_{2}(y)\sim\sup_{B^{\prime}\supset B}\frac{1}{|B^{\prime}|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\int_{B^{\prime}}|f_{2}|\quad\text{ for }y\in B.

Altogether, we have for all
y\in B
that

(6.4)

M_{\alpha}f(y)\sim M_{1/5,\alpha,\textrm{loc}}(f\chi_{2B})(y)+\sup_{B^{\prime}\supset B}\frac{1}{|B^{\prime}|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\int_{B^{\prime}}|f_{2}|.

For the last term we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B^{\prime}|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\int_{B^{\prime}}|f|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B^{\prime}|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\|f\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})}\|\chi_{B^{\prime}}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})}}{\|\chi_{B^{\prime}}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{q}(\varphi^{q},w^{q})}}\leq\frac{\|f\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})}}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{q}(\varphi^{q},w^{q})}},

where we used successively (
2.7
), (
1.4
) for
B^{\prime}
, and
B\subset B^{\prime}
.

Now we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\varphi(\widetilde{B})^{q}}\int_{B}(M_{\alpha}f)^{q}w\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\varphi(\widetilde{B})^{q}}\int_{B}\left(M_{1/5,\alpha,\textrm{loc}}(f\chi_{2B})+\frac{\|f\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})}}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{q}(\varphi^{q},w^{q})}}\right)^{q}w^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\varphi(\widetilde{B})^{q}}\int_{2B}|f|^{p}w^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\|f\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})},

where we used that
w\in A_{p,q,\text{loc}}
, which is implied by (
1.4
) according to Lemma
6.1
. Taking into account that
\widetilde{2B}\subset 2\widetilde{B}
, we obtain the required bound using the doubling property of
\varphi
.

(b) Let
f
be nonnegative. We take a ball
B
as in part (a) and decompose
f
as
f_{1}+f_{2}
. From the linearity of
I_{\alpha}
we have

I_{\alpha}f=I_{\alpha}f_{1}+I_{\alpha}f_{2}.

For
x\in B
we have
I_{\alpha}f_{1}(x)=I_{3/5,\alpha,\text{loc}}f_{1}(x)
, where the local fractional operator has been defined in (
2.13
). Condition (
1.5
) is stronger than (
1.4
), hence
w\in A_{p,q,\text{loc}}
.
Using the weighted boundedness of
I_{3/5,\alpha,\text{loc}}
from
L^{p}(w^{p})
to
L^{q}(w^{q})
for
w\in A_{p,q,\text{loc}}
, this term works as the first term in the right-hand side of (
6.4
).

On the other hand, for
x\in B
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 2B
we have
M\chi_{B}(y)\sim|B||x-y|^{-n}
. Hence,

I_{\alpha}f_{2}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 2B}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y)\left(\frac{M\chi_{B}(y)}{|B|}\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,dy.

Using (
2.7
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\varphi(\widetilde{B})^{q}}\int_{B}(I_{\alpha}f_{2})^{q}w^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|f\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})}\|(M\chi_{B})^{1-\alpha/n}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})^{\prime}}}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi(\widetilde{B})^{q}}\int_{B}w^{q}\right)^{1/q}

and (
1.5
) suffices to conclude.

(c) For
p=1
we proceed as in (a) and (b), but when dealing with the local operators acting on
f_{1}
the weighted inequalities are of weak-type. The rest of the proof is the same.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

For weights
w
in
A_{p,q,\text{loc}}
one only needs to verify (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) for balls centered at the origin. Balls
B
such that
|c_{B}|\leq 6r_{B}
, can be replaced by
\widetilde{B}
, because
B\subset\widetilde{B}
and
|B|\sim|\widetilde{B}|
. For balls with
|c_{B}|>6r_{B}
,
A_{p,q,\text{loc}}
is enough for
M_{\alpha}
due to Lemma
6.1
, but also for
I_{\alpha}
, as a consequence of the proof of Theorem
6.2
, where to obtain the bound for
I_{\alpha}f_{1}
we only use that
w\in A_{p,q,\text{loc}}
.

The proof of the theorem can be adapted to the case
1/p-1/q<\alpha/n
with appropriate weights.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
0<\alpha<n
,
0\leq\gamma<\alpha
,
1\leq p<n/\gamma
and
1/p-1/q=\gamma/n
. Assume that
w\in A_{p,q,\text{loc}}
. Set
v_{\gamma}(x)=|x|^{\gamma-\alpha}w(x)
.

Let
p>1
. The fractional maximal operator
M_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})
to
L\mathcal{M}^{q}(\varphi^{q},v_{\gamma}^{q})
if

(6.5)

\sup_{B}\frac{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{q}(\varphi^{q},v_{\gamma}^{q})}\|\chi_{B}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})^{\prime}}}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}<\infty

for balls centered at the origin.

The fractional integral operator
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})
to
L\mathcal{M}^{q}(\varphi^{q},v_{\gamma}^{q})
if

(6.6)

\sup_{B}\frac{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{q}(\varphi^{q},v_{\gamma}^{q})}\|(M\chi_{B})^{1-\alpha/n}\|_{L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\varphi^{p},w^{p})^{\prime}}}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}<\infty.

for balls centered at the origin.

For
p=1
and
q=n/(n-\gamma)
the results hold for the boundedness from
L\mathcal{M}^{1}(\varphi,w)
to
WL\mathcal{M}^{\frac{n}{n-\gamma}}(\varphi^{\frac{n}{n-\gamma}},v_{\gamma}^{\frac{n}{n-\gamma}})
.

Proof.

First we proof the following pointwise inequalities:

(6.7)

M_{\kappa,\alpha,\text{loc}}f(x),I_{\kappa,\alpha,\text{loc}}(|f|)(x)\lesssim|x|^{\alpha-\gamma}M_{\kappa,\gamma,\text{loc}}f(x).

Let
B
a ball containing
x
and such that
r_{B}<\kappa|c_{B}|
. Then

\frac{1}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{B}|f(y)|\,dy\leq\frac{r_{B}^{\alpha-\gamma}}{|B|^{1-\gamma/n}}\int_{B}|f(y)|\,dy\lesssim\frac{|x|^{\alpha-\gamma}}{|B|^{1-\gamma/n}}\int_{B}|f(y)\,dy,

from which (
6.7
) follows for
M_{\kappa,\alpha,\text{loc}}
.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\int_{\{y:|y-x|<\kappa|x|\}}

\displaystyle\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,dy=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\{y:2^{-j-1}\kappa|x|\leq|y-x|<2^{-j}\kappa|x|\}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(2^{-j}\kappa|x|)^{\alpha-\gamma}\frac{1}{(2^{-j}\kappa|x|)^{n-\gamma}}\int_{\{y:|y-x|<2^{-j}\kappa|x|\}}|f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim|x|^{\alpha-\gamma}M_{\kappa,\gamma,\text{loc}}f(x).

We proceed as in the proof of the previous theorem. The action of the operators on
f_{2}
is treated in the same way as before. When working with
f_{1}
, using (
6.7
) the needed estimate is shifted to
M_{\kappa,\gamma,\text{loc}}
.
∎

From Theorem
5.1
we know that (
6.5
) and (
6.6
) for all balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are necessary for the boundedness of the operators. In the case
\gamma<\alpha
of Theorem
6.4
we add the condition
w\in A_{p,q,\text{loc}}
to get the sufficiency. Working as in Lemma
6.1
we can prove that
w\in A_{p,q,\text{loc}}
implies (
6.5
), but we do not know whether the opposite holds for
\gamma<\alpha
. This is why we do not get a full characterization as was the case for
\gamma=\alpha
.

7.
Power weights

In this section we obtain sharp power weighted inequalities in the case of the Samko and Komori-Shirai versions of the weighted local Morrey spaces. We use the notation
w_{\beta}(x)=|x|^{\beta}
.

7.1.
Weighted spaces of Samko type

The Samko type spaces are defined with the condition
\varphi_{\lambda}(B)=r_{B}^{\lambda}
with
\lambda>0
. First we give a lemma.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
1\leq p<p_{0}<\infty
and
\lambda,\mu>0
. Let
\beta
and
\delta
be related by

(7.1)

\beta+\frac{n-\lambda}{p}=\delta+\frac{n-\mu}{p_{0}}.

Then
\|f\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda},w_{\beta}^{p})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p_{0}}(\varphi_{\mu},w_{\delta}^{p_{0}})}
.

Proof.

We can use (
2.3
) for the norm in the Morrey spaces. Let
B
be a ball such that
|c_{B}|=4r_{B}
. Then we have
r_{\widetilde{B}}\sim|c_{B}|\sim|x|
for
x\in B
. Using these equivalences and Hölder’s inequality we get

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{r_{\widetilde{B}}^{\lambda}}\int_{B}|f(x)|^{p}|x|^{\beta p}dx\right)^{1/p}\lesssim|c_{B}|^{-\frac{\lambda}{p}+\beta+\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n}{p_{0}}}\left(\int_{B}|f|^{p_{0}}\right)^{1/p_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|c_{B}|^{-\frac{\lambda}{p}+\beta+\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n}{p_{0}}+\frac{\mu}{p_{0}}-\delta}\left(\frac{1}{r_{\widetilde{B}}^{\mu}}\int_{B}|f(x)|^{p_{0}}|x|^{\delta p_{0}}dx\right)^{1/p_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p_{0}}(\varphi_{\mu},w_{\delta}^{p_{0}})},

where we used (
7.1
). The result follows.
∎

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
\varphi_{\lambda}(B)=r_{B}^{\lambda}
with
\lambda>0
. If
M_{\alpha}
is bounded from
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},w_{\beta_{1}}^{p})
to
W\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi_{\lambda_{2}},w_{\beta_{2}}^{q})
, then

(7.2)

\beta_{2}-\beta_{1}-\frac{n-\lambda_{1}}{p}+\frac{n-\lambda_{2}}{q}+\alpha=0.

Moreover,

(7.3)

\alpha\leq\beta_{1}+\frac{n-\lambda_{1}}{p}<n.

If
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},w_{\beta_{1}}^{p})
to
W\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi_{\lambda_{2}},w_{\beta_{2}}^{q})
the same conditions are necessary, and moreover the left-hand side inequality in (
7.3
) is strict.

Using (
7.2
) we can write (
7.3
) in the equivalent form

0\leq\beta_{2}+\frac{n-\lambda_{2}}{q}<n-\alpha.

This implies that
\beta_{2}q\geq\lambda_{2}-n
, and from (
7.3
) we also have
\beta_{1}p>\lambda_{1}-n
.

Proof.

Let
B
be such that
4r_{B}\leq|c_{B}|
. Then

\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},w_{\beta_{1}}^{p})}\sim\left(\frac{w_{\beta_{1}}^{p}(B)}{\varphi_{\lambda_{1}}(\widetilde{B})}\right)^{1/p}\sim\left(\frac{|c_{B}|^{\beta_{1}p}r_{B}^{n}}{|c_{B}|^{\lambda_{1}}}\right)^{1/p}=|c_{B}|^{\beta_{1}-\lambda_{1}/p}r_{B}^{n/p}.

Similarly,

\|r_{B}^{\alpha}\chi_{B}\|_{W\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi_{\lambda_{2}},w_{\beta_{2}}^{q})}=r_{B}^{\alpha}\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi_{\lambda_{2}},w_{\beta_{2}}^{q})}\sim|c_{B}|^{\beta_{2}-\lambda_{2}/q}r_{B}^{\alpha+n/q}.

Using
r_{B}^{\alpha}\chi_{B}\leq M_{\alpha}\chi_{B}
we have

|c_{B}|^{\beta_{2}-\lambda_{2}/q}r_{B}^{\alpha+n/q}\lesssim|c_{B}|^{\beta_{1}-\lambda_{1}/p}r_{B}^{n/p},

that is,

|c_{B}|^{\beta_{2}-\lambda_{2}/q-\beta_{1}+\lambda_{1}/p}r_{B}^{\alpha+n/q-n/p}\lesssim 1.

For fixed
c_{B}
letting
r_{B}
tend to zero we obtain the condition (
5.3
). Take now
r_{B}=|c_{B}|/4
and we obtain

|c_{B}|^{\beta_{2}-\lambda_{2}/q-\beta_{1}+\lambda_{1}/p+\alpha+n/q-n/p}\lesssim 1.

Since
|c_{B}|
can be any number in
(0,\infty)
we deduce the necessity of (
7.2
).

An alternative proof of (
7.2
) can be obtained using a scale argument.

The necessity of the first inequality in (
7.3
) is deduced from the fact that the characteristic function of a ball centered at the origin is not in
W\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi_{\lambda_{2}},w_{\beta_{2}}^{q})
for
\beta_{2}q+n-\lambda_{2}<0
(see
[
2
, Proposition 5.3]
). On the other hand, the function
|x|^{-n}\chi_{B(0,1)}
is in
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},w_{\beta_{1}}^{p})
for
\beta_{1}+(n-\lambda_{1})/p\geq n
, and it is not locally integrable, hence the necessity of the second inequality in (
7.3
) follows.

From the pointwise inequality
M_{\alpha}f(x)\leq I_{\alpha}(|f|)(x)
it is clear that the conditions are necessary for
I_{\alpha}
. We next show that the equality in (
7.3
) is not possible for
I_{\alpha}
.

Assume that
\beta_{1}p+n-\lambda_{1}=\alpha p
(equivalently,
\beta_{2}q+n-\lambda_{2}=0
due to (
7.2
)). Take
f(y)=|y|^{-\alpha}\chi_{B(0,1)}
. Then
f\in{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},w_{\beta_{1}}^{p})}
. For
|x|<1/2
,

I_{\alpha}f(x)\geq\int_{2|x|<|y|<1}\frac{|y|^{-\alpha}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,dy\gtrsim\int_{2|x|<|y|<1}|y|^{-n}\,dy\sim-\log(2|x|).

The function
-\log(2|x|)\chi_{|x|<1/2}
does not belong to
W\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi_{\lambda_{2}},w_{\beta_{2}}^{q})
for
\beta_{2}q+n-\lambda_{2}=0
. This rules out the equality case in (
7.3
) for
I_{\alpha}
.
∎

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
0<\alpha<n
,
0\leq\gamma\leq\alpha
,
1\leq p<n/\gamma
, and
1/p-1/q=\gamma/n
. Set
\varphi_{\lambda}(B)=r_{B}^{\lambda}
for
\lambda>0
.

(1)

Let
1<p<n/\gamma
. Then
M_{\alpha}
is bounded from
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},w_{\beta_{1}})
to
\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi_{\lambda_{2}},w_{\beta_{2}})
if and only if (
7.2
) and (
7.3
) hold. If
p=1
and
q=n/(n-\gamma)
in (
7.2
) and (
7.3
), then
M_{\alpha}
is bounded from
\mathcal{LM}^{1}(\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},w_{\beta_{1}})
to
W\mathcal{LM}^{n/(n-\gamma)}(\varphi_{\lambda_{2}},w_{\beta_{2}})
.

(2)

The same result holds for
I_{\alpha}
instead of
M_{\alpha}
except that in the left-hand side condition of (
7.3
) the inequality is strict.

Proof.

The necessity of the conditions have been proved in Proposition
7.2
. Let us check the sufficiency.

Case
\gamma=\alpha
and
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}:=\beta
. Then (
7.2
) reduces to
\lambda_{1}/p=\lambda_{2}/q
.
Using Theorem
6.2
we need to check (
1.4
) for
\varphi(B)=r_{B}^{\lambda_{1}/p}=r_{B}^{\lambda_{2}/q}
. We only need to verify it for balls centered at the origin, because
w_{\beta}\in A_{p,q,\text{loc}}
.

Let
B
be a ball centered at the origin. Then

\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi_{\lambda_{2}},w_{\beta}^{q})}\sim r_{B}^{\frac{n-\lambda_{2}}{q}+\beta}

and

(7.4)

\|\chi_{B}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},w_{\beta}^{p})^{\prime}}\lesssim r_{B}^{n-\frac{n-\lambda_{1}}{p}-\beta}.

The last one is similar to
[
3
, Lemma 2.12 (b)]
. Consequently, (
1.4
) holds for balls centered at the origin.

For
I_{\alpha}
we need to check (
1.5
). By Remark
6.3
we only need to consider
B
centered at the origin. We use that

M\chi_{B}(x)\sim\chi_{B}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-kn}\chi_{2^{k}B\setminus 2^{k-1}B}(x).

With (
7.4
) for
2^{k}B
we have

\|(M\chi_{B})^{1-\alpha/n}\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},w_{\beta}^{p})^{\prime}}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k(n-\alpha)}(2^{k}r_{B})^{n-\frac{n-\lambda_{1}}{p}-\beta}\lesssim r_{B}^{n-\frac{n-\lambda_{1}}{p}-\beta},

where in the last step we use that the exponent of
2^{k}
is negative due to the strict inequality assumed in (
7.3
). Consequently, (
1.5
) is satisfied.

Case
\gamma=\alpha
and
\beta_{2}\neq\beta_{1}
. If
\beta_{2}>\beta_{1}
, let
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}=\lambda_{1}+(\beta_{2}-\beta_{1})p
. Then using Proposition
2.3
we have

\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},w_{\beta_{1}}^{p})=\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda_{1}^{\prime}},w_{\beta_{2}}^{p}).

Condition (
7.2
) becomes
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}/p=\lambda_{2}/q
and (
7.3
) is satisfied with
\beta_{2}
and
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}
instead of
\beta_{1}
and
\lambda_{1}
. Thus we can apply the result in the first part of the proof to deduce the boundedness from
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda_{1}^{\prime}},w_{\beta_{2}}^{p})
to
\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi_{\lambda_{2}},w_{\beta_{2}}^{q})
, which is the desired result.

The proof for
\beta_{1}>\beta_{2}
is similar, setting
\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=\lambda_{2}+(\beta_{1}-\beta_{2})q
and taking into account that

\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi_{\lambda_{2}},w_{\beta_{2}}^{q})=\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi_{\lambda_{2}^{\prime}},w_{\beta_{1}}^{q}).

Case
\gamma<\alpha
.
Although it would be possible to use Theorem
6.4
we prefer to proceed
using Lemma
7.1
to provide a different insight.

Given
p,q,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
such that (
7.2
) and (
7.3
) hold, we choose
p_{0},q_{0},\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
such that

\displaystyle p\geq p_{0},\ q\leq q_{0},\ \frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{q_{0}}=\frac{\alpha}{n},\ \mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0,

\displaystyle\beta_{1}+\frac{n-\lambda_{1}}{p}=\delta_{1}+\frac{n-\mu_{1}}{p_{0}},\text{ and }\beta_{2}+\frac{n-\lambda_{2}}{q}=\delta_{2}+\frac{n-\mu_{2}}{q_{0}}.

Then the corresponding conditions (
7.2
) and (
7.3
) hold for
p_{0},q_{0},\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
, and
p_{0}
and
q_{0}
satisfy the conditions in the previous part of the proof. Using Lemma
7.1
we obtain

\displaystyle\|M_{\alpha}f\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\varphi_{\lambda_{2}},w_{\beta_{2}}^{q})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|M_{\alpha}f\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{q_{0}}(\varphi_{\mu_{2}},w_{\delta_{2}}^{q_{0}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p_{0}}(\varphi_{\mu_{1}},w_{\delta_{1}}^{p_{0}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda_{1}},w_{\beta_{1}}^{p})}.

In the case
p=1
one has to work with weak-type inequalities, but the proof has only small changes and we do not write the details here.
∎

The result for
n/p-n/q=\alpha
and
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}
is in
[
16
]
. When
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=0
this is known as Spanne’s result (usually stated for global Morrey spaces).

7.2.
Weighted spaces of Komori-Shirai type

Given a weight
w
, let us denote
\psi_{w,\lambda}(B)=w(B)^{\lambda/n}
. The weighted local Morrey spaces of Komori-Shirai type are defined by
L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\psi_{w,\lambda},w)
with
\lambda>0
. The typical estimates for fractional operators are not exactly between two spaces of Komori-Shirai type. For instance, when we say that
M_{\alpha}
and
I_{\alpha}
are bounded from
L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\psi_{w^{q},\lambda},w^{p})
to
L\mathcal{M}^{q}(\psi_{w^{q},\lambda},w^{q})
(see
[
9
]
for global Morrey spaces) the first space is not of Komori-Shirai type as defined above. To agree with the definition it should be
L\mathcal{M}^{p}(\psi_{w^{p},\lambda},w^{p})
.

Nevertheless, in the case of power weights, the results can be stated in such a way that both spaces are of Komori-Shirai type. This holds because those spaces can be represented as Samko-type spaces as shown in the following proposition.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
\beta>-n
. Then

(7.5)

\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\psi_{w_{\beta},\lambda},w_{\beta})=\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda(1+\beta/n)},w_{\beta})=\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\varphi_{\lambda},w_{\beta(1-\lambda/n)}).

A similar result holds for weak-type spaces.

Proof.

In all cases we can use the restriction of the Morrey norm to (
2.3
). Notice that for the balls in (
2.3
) we have the equivalence
|c_{B}|\sim r_{B}\sim|x|
for
x\in B
. Then
w_{\beta}(B)\sim r_{B}^{\beta+n}
and the result follows.
∎

Using this equality of spaces we can rewrite Theorem
7.3
in terms of Komori-Shirai type spaces. We state it as a corollary.

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
0<\alpha<n
,
0\leq\gamma\leq\alpha
, and
1/p-1/q=\gamma/n
.
Let
1<p<n/\gamma
. Then
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
\mathcal{LM}^{p}(\psi_{w_{\beta_{1}}^{p},\lambda_{1}},w_{\beta_{1}}^{p})
to
\mathcal{LM}^{q}(\psi_{w_{\beta_{2}}^{q},\lambda_{2}},w_{\beta_{2}}^{q})
if and only if

(7.6)

(n-\lambda_{2})\left(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{\beta_{2}}{n}\right)-(n-\lambda_{1})\left(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\beta_{1}}{n}\right)+\alpha=0.

and

(7.7)

\alpha<(n-\lambda_{1})\left(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\beta_{1}}{n}\right)<n.

With
p=1
and
q=n/(n-\gamma)
the above conditions are necessary and sufficient for
I_{\alpha}
to be bounded from
\mathcal{LM}^{1}(\psi_{w_{\beta_{1}},\lambda_{1}},w_{\beta_{1}})
to
W\mathcal{LM}^{n/(n-\gamma)}(\psi_{w_{\beta_{2}}^{n/(n-\gamma)},\lambda_{2}},w_{\beta_{2}}^{n/(n-\gamma)})
.

Similar results hold for
M_{\alpha}
adding the equality at the left-hand side of (
7.7
).

Notice that for the local integrability of
w_{\beta_{1}}^{p}
and
w_{\beta_{2}}^{q}
we need
\beta_{1}p+n>0
and
\beta_{2}q+n>0
. From (
7.7
) we deduce
\lambda_{1}<n
and together with (
7.6
) we obtain the necessity of
\lambda_{2}<n
. For
M_{\alpha}
in the equality case of the left-hand side of (
7.7
)
\lambda_{2}=n
is admissible too.

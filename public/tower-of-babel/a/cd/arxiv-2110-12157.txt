Wenshuai Jiang, Weimin Sheng, Huaiyu Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study Ricci flow on compact manifolds with a continuous initial metric. It was known from Simon that the Ricci flow exists for a short time. We prove that the scalar curvature lower bound is preserved along the Ricci flow if the initial metric has a scalar curvature lower bound in distributional sense provided that the initial metric is W 1 , p W^{1,p} for some n < p ≤ ∞ n<p\leq\infty . As an application, we use this result to study the relation between Yamabe invariant and Ricci flat metrics. We prove that if the Yamabe invariant is nonpositive and the scalar curvature is nonnegative in distributional sense, then the manifold is isometric to a Ricci flat manifold.

However, it has not been well understood for scalar curvature lower bounds. One of a fundamental property for classical scalar curvature lower bounds is that they are preserved by Ricci flow. Ricci flow is firstly introduced by Hamilton
[
Ha82
]
, which comes to be an powerful tool in geometry and has been used to prove the Poincaré Conjecture, see
[

]

The lower bounds preserving property is still true for scalar curvature lower bounds in some weak sense and it is quite useful in the study of low-regularity Riemannian metrics with some weak scalar curvature conditions. Gromov introduced a scalar curvature lower bound in some weak sense, which could be defined for
C^{0}
metrics, see
[
Gm14
]
. Bamler and Burkhardt-Guim considered it by a Ricci flow approach, they elaborated on defining a weak sense scalar curvature lower bound which is required to be preserved by Ricci flow, see
[
Ba16
]

[
Bu19

[

]
. It remains an open problem whether the converse is true (see
[
Bu19
]
). Schoen also considered scalar curvature on low-regularity metrics. He proposed a question that if the Yamabe invariant
\sigma(M)
is nonpositive, the metric admits singularity in a subset and the scalar curvature is at least
\sigma(M)
away from the singular set, then whether we can prove that the metric is smooth and Ricci flat provided that the singular set is small, see
[
LM19
]
. Li and Mantoulidis
[

]

3
-manifolds with metrics admitting point singularities. For more related results, see the survey of Sormani
[
So21
]
.

McFeron and Székelyhidi
[
MS12
]
proved that if a Lipschitz metric is smooth away from a hypersurface satisfying certain conditions on the mean curvature and the metric has nonnegative scalar curvature pointwisely away from the hypersurface, then the scalar curvature will be nonnegative pointwisely on the whole manifold under Ricci flow. This property appears to be quite useful in the study of the positive mass theorem, especially for the rigidity part of the positive mass theorem, see
[
MS12
]
. (See also
[
Mi02
]

n-2

W^{1,p}
metrics. LeFloch and Mardare defind a scalar curvature lower bound in distributional sense for
W^{1,p}
metrics. This was further studied by
[

]
in asymptotically flat manifolds. Lee and Lefloch

proved a positive mass theorem on spin manifolds with

We improve some of the above results. The main theorem is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M^{n}
be a compact manifold with a metric
g\in W^{1,p}(M)

(n<p\leq\infty)
. Assume the scalar curvature
R_{g}\geq a
for some constant
a
in distributional sense, and let
g(t),t\in(0,T_{0}]
be the Ricci flow starting from the metric
g
. Then for any
t\in(0,T_{0}]
, there holds
R_{g(t)}\geq a
on
M
.

In
[

]
, Burkhardt-Guim introduced a weak scalar curvature lower bound for
C^{0}
-metric using Ricci flow. By comparing with the definition in
[
Bu19
]

1.1

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
M^{n}
be a compact manifold with a metric
g\in W^{1,p}(M)

(n<p\leq\infty)
. Assume the scalar curvature
R_{g}\geq a
for some constant
a
in distributional sense, then the scalar curvature
R_{g}\geq a
in the sense of
[
Bu19
]
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we can prove the following result:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
(
n\geq 3
) be a compact manifold with
g\in W^{1,p}(M)

(n<p\leq\infty)
. Assume the distributional scalar curvature

, then either
(M,g)
is isometric to a Ricci flat manifold or there exists a smooth positive scalar curvature metric on

By Theorem

2.
Approximation of Singular Metrics

Let
M^{n}
be a compact smooth manifold with a metric
g\in C^{0}(M)\cap W^{1,2}(M)

and let
h
be a smooth metric on
M
such that
C^{-1}h\leq g\leq Ch
for some constant
C>1
. Throughout this paper,
\tilde{\nabla}
and the dot product will denote the Levi-Civita connection and the inner product taken with respect to
h
respectively, We define the distirbutional scalar curvature of
g
as in
[
LM07
,
LL15
,
LS15
]
,

\displaystyle\langle R_{g},\varphi\rangle:=\int_{M}\left(-V\cdot\tilde{\nabla}\left(\varphi\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}\right)+F\varphi\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}\right)d\mu_{h},\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M),

(2.1)

where
\mu_{h}
is the Lebesgue measure taken with respect to
h
,
V
is a vector field and
F
is a scalar field, defined as:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ij}^{k}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}g^{kl}\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{i}g_{jl}+\tilde{\nabla}_{j}g_{il}-\tilde{\nabla}_{l}g_{ij}\right),

(2.2)

\displaystyle V^{k}

\displaystyle:=g^{ij}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}-g^{ik}\Gamma_{ji}^{j}=g^{ij}g^{k\ell}(\tilde{\nabla}_{j}g_{i\ell}-\tilde{\nabla}_{\ell}g_{ij}),

(2.3)

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle:={\rm{tr}}_{g}\tilde{{\rm Ric}}-\tilde{\nabla}_{k}g^{ij}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}+\tilde{\nabla}_{k}g^{ik}\Gamma_{ji}^{i}+g^{ij}\left(\Gamma_{k\ell}^{k}\Gamma_{ij}^{\ell}-\Gamma_{j\ell}^{k}\Gamma_{ik}^{\ell}\right),

(2.4)

where
\tilde{{\rm Ric}}
is the Ricci tensor of
h
. By
[
LL15
]
,
\langle R_{g},\varphi\rangle
coincides with the integral
\int_{M}R_{g}\varphi d\mu_{g}
in classical sense if
g\in C^{2}(M)
and
\langle R_{g},\varphi\rangle
is independent of
h
for any
g\in C^{0}(M)\cap W^{1,2}(M)
. For more details about the distirbutional scalar curvature and related results, see
[
LL15
]
,
[
LM07
]
,
[
LS15
]
,
[
JSZ21
]
.

Let
a
be some constant, we say that the distirbutional scalar curvature of
g
is at least
a
if
\langle R_{g},\varphi\rangle-a\int_{M}\varphi d\mu_{g}\geq 0
for any nonnegative function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
. We will abbreviate this inequality as
\langle R_{g}-a,\varphi\rangle\geq 0
.

The following mollification lemma has been proved in
[

Lemma 2.1

(
[

]
)

Let

be a compact smooth manifold and

) metric on
M
, then there exists a family of smooth metric

,

, such that

Under this mollification, we have an estimate of the distirbutional scalar curvature. The following lemma has been essentially proved in

Lemma 2.2

(

Let

Proof.

Let
h
be a smooth metric on
M
with
C^{-1}h<g<Ch
, here and below

will denote some positive constant which depends on

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\left(\int_{M}|V_{\delta}-V|^{n}d\mu_{h}+\int_{M}|F_{\delta}-F|^{n/2}d\mu_{h}\right)=0.

(2.5)

We also have

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\int_{M}\left|\tilde{\nabla}\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}-\tilde{\nabla}\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}\right|^{n}d\mu_{h}=0.

Using triangular inequality and Hölder inequality, we can calculate that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{M}F_{\delta}u\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}d\mu_{h}-\int_{M}Fu\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}d\mu_{h}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\Big|F_{\delta}u\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}-Fu\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}\Big|d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}|F_{\delta}u-Fu|\Big|\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}\Big|d\mu_{h}+\int_{M}|Fu|\Big|\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}-\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}\Big|d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M}|F_{\delta}u-Fu|d\mu_{h}+\sup_{M}\Big|\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}-\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}\Big|\int_{M}|Fu|d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{M}|F_{\delta}-F|^{n/2}d\mu_{h}\right)^{2/n}\left(\int_{M}|u|^{n/(n-2)}d\mu_{h}\right)^{(n-2)/n}+\sup_{M}\Big|\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}-\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}\Big|\left(\int_{M}|F|^{n/2}d\mu_{h}\right)^{2/n}\left(\int_{M}|u|^{n/(n-2)}d\mu_{h}\right)^{(n-2)/n}.

\left(\int_{M}|u|^{n/(n-2)}d\mu_{h}\right)^{(n-2)/n}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,\frac{n}{n-1}}(M)},

we get that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{M}F_{\delta}u\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}d\mu_{h}-\int_{M}Fu\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}d\mu_{h}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\left(C\left(\int_{M}|F_{\delta}-F|^{n/2}d\mu_{h}\right)^{2/n}+C\sup_{M}\Big|\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}-\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}\Big|\left(\int_{M}|F|^{n/2}d\mu_{h}\right)^{2/n}\right)\|u\|_{W^{1,\frac{n}{n-1}}(M)}.

Similarly, for the term involving
V
, we can calculate that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{M}

\displaystyle V\cdot\tilde{\nabla}\left(u\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}\right)d\mu_{h}-\int_{M}V_{\delta}\cdot\tilde{\nabla}\left(u\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}\right)d\mu_{h}\Big|

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}|V-V_{\delta}|\cdot\Big|\tilde{\nabla}\left(u\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}\right)\Big|d\mu_{h}+\int_{M}|V|\cdot\Big|\tilde{\nabla}\left(u\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}-u\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}\right)\Big|d\mu_{h}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|V-V_{\delta}|^{n}d\mu_{h}\right)^{1/n}\left(\int_{M}\Big|\tilde{\nabla}\left(u\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}\right)\Big|^{n/(n-1)}d\mu_{h}\right)^{(n-1)/n}

(2.9)

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{M}|V|^{n}d\mu_{h}\right)^{1/n}\left(\int_{M}\Big|\tilde{\nabla}\left(u\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}-u\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}\right)\Big|^{n/(n-1)}d\mu_{h}\right)^{(n-1)/n}.

(2.10)

Using Sobolev inequality and Hölder inequality again, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}

\displaystyle\Big|\tilde{\nabla}\left(u\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}\right)\Big|^{n/(n-1)}d\mu_{h}

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(n)\int_{M}\left|\tilde{\nabla}u\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}\right|^{n/(n-1)}d\mu_{h}+C(n)\int_{M}\left|u\tilde{\nabla}\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}\right|^{n/(n-1)}d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}u|^{n/(n-1)}d\mu_{h}+C\left(\int_{M}|u|^{n/(n-2)}d\mu_{h}\right)^{(n-2)/(n-1)}\left(\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}|^{n}d\mu_{h}\right)^{1/(n-1)}

(2.13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}u|^{n/(n-1)}d\mu_{h}+C\|u\|_{W^{1,\frac{n}{n-1}}(M)}^{n/(n-1)}\left(\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}\frac{d\mu_{g_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{h}}|^{n}d\mu_{h}\right)^{1/(n-1)}

\displaystyle|\langle R_{g_{\delta}},u\rangle-\langle R_{g},u\rangle|\leq\epsilon\|u\|_{W^{1,\frac{n}{n-1}}(M)},\forall u\in C^{\infty}(M),\forall\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}),

(2.23)

3.
Estimates on Ricci flow

The Ricci flow was introduced by Hamilton
[
Ha82
]
. Its definition is as follows:

Definition 3.1

(Ricci flow)
.

The Ricci flow on
M
is a family of time dependent metrics
g(t)
such that

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(t)=-2{\rm Ric}_{g(t)},

where
{{\rm Ric}_{g}}
is the Ricci curvature tensor of
g
.

The main theorem in this section is the following:

Theorem 3.2
.

There exists an
\epsilon(n)>0
such that, for any compact
n
-manifold
M
with a
W^{1,p}
metric
\hat{g}
with
n<p\leq+\infty
, there exists a
T_{0}=T_{0}(n,g)>0
and a family of metrics
g(t)\in C^{\infty}(M\times(0,T_{0}]),t\in(0,T_{0}]
which solves Ricci flow for
t\in(0,T_{0}]
, and satisfies

(1)

\lim_{t\to 0}d_{GH}((M,g(t)),(M,\hat{g}))=0.

(2)

|{\rm{Rm}}(g(t))|(t)\leq\frac{C(n,\hat{g},p)}{t^{\frac{n}{4p}+\frac{3}{4}}}
,
\forall t\in(0,T_{0}]
.

(3)

\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\int_{M}|{\rm{Rm}}(g(t))|^{2}d\mu_{g(t)}dt\leq C(n,\hat{g},p)
,

where
C(n,\hat{g},p)
is a positive constant independent of
t
.

Remark 3.1
.

In this paper, we assume that
T_{0}\leq 1
for convenience.

Remark 3.2
.

The existence of
T_{0}
and
g(t)
and (1) have been proved by Simon (see Theorem
3.5
). Shi-Tam
[
ST18
]
also got similar estimates as (2). Here we give a proof by using Moser’s iteration.

To prove Theorem
3.2
. We consider
h
-flow (see
[
Si02
]
).

Definition 3.3
.

Given a constant
\delta\geq 0
, a metric
h
is called to be
(1+\delta)
-fair to
g
, if
h
is
C^{\infty}
,

\sup_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{j}{\rm{Rm}}(h)|=k_{j}<\infty,

and

(1+\delta)^{-1}h\leq g\leq(1+\delta)h\quad on\ M.

Here and below,
\tilde{\nabla}
means the covariant derivative taken with respect to
h
.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
M
be a compact manifold and
g
be a
C^{0}
metric on
M
, then for any
0<\epsilon<1
, there exists a smooth metric
h
which is
(1+\epsilon)
-fair to
g
. For a proof, see the remarks below
[

Definition 3.4
.

[
h
-flow] Given a background smooth metrics

-flow is a family of metrics

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{ij}=-2R_{ij}+\nabla_{i}V_{j}+\nabla_{j}V_{i},

where the derivatives are taken with respect to
g

V_{j}=g_{jk}g^{pq}(\Gamma_{pq}^{k}-\tilde{\Gamma}_{pq}^{k}),

and
\Gamma
and
\tilde{\Gamma}
are the Christoffel symbols of
g
and
h
respectively.

h
-flow is equivalent to the Ricci flow modulo an action of diffeomorphisms (see
[
Si02
]
), thus we only need to prove Theorem
3.2
for
h
-flow. Firstly we need the following theorem, which has been proved by Simon
[
Si02
]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

(
[
Si02
, Theorem 1.1]
)
There exists an
\epsilon(n)>0
such that, for any compact
n
-manifold
M
with a complete
C^{0}

\hat{g}
and a

(1+\frac{\epsilon(n)}{2})
-fair to

and a family of metrics

h
-flow for

,

(1+\epsilon(n))
-fair to

(1)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\sup_{x\in M}|g(x,t)-\hat{g}(x)|=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\sup_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{i}g(t)|\leq\frac{c_{i}(n,k_{1},...,k_{i})}{t^{i/2}},\forall t\in(0,T_{0}],i\geq 1,

.

In the condition of Theorem

, if for some

, there holds

,
\forall t\in(0,T]
, then for the same

,
|\tilde{\nabla}g|(t)\leq\frac{C(n,h,A,p)}{t^{\frac{n}{2p}}}
,
\forall t\in(0,T]
also holds, where
C(n,h,A,p)
is a positive constant independent of

.

Proof.

By
[
Sh89
]
, we have the evolution equation of
|\tilde{\nabla}g_{ij}|^{2}
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\tilde{\nabla}g_{ij}|^{2}=

\displaystyle g^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}|\tilde{\nabla}g_{ij}|^{2}-2g^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}(\tilde{\nabla}g_{ij})\cdot\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}(\tilde{\nabla}g_{ij})

\displaystyle+\text{\~{Rm}}*g^{-1}*g^{-1}*g*\tilde{\nabla}g*\tilde{\nabla}g+g^{-1}*g*\tilde{\nabla}\text{\~{Rm}}*\tilde{\nabla}g

\displaystyle+g^{-1}*g^{-1}*\tilde{\nabla}g*\tilde{\nabla}g*\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g+g^{-1}*g^{-1}*g^{-1}*\tilde{\nabla}g*\tilde{\nabla}g*\tilde{\nabla}g*\tilde{\nabla}g,

where the derivatives and norms are taken with respect to
h
, and R̃m is the Riemannian curvature tensor of
h
.
Thus

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\tilde{\nabla}g_{ij}|^{2}-g^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}|\tilde{\nabla}g_{ij}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-C_{1}(n,h)|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C(n,h)|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{2}+C(n,h)|\tilde{\nabla}g|

\displaystyle\quad+C(n)|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{4}+C_{2}(n)|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{2}|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|

\displaystyle\leq-C_{1}(n,h)|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C(n,h)|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{2}+C(n,h)|\tilde{\nabla}g|

\displaystyle\quad+C(n)|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{4}+\frac{C_{2}(n)}{2\epsilon}|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{4}+\frac{C_{2}(n)\epsilon}{2}|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|^{2},\forall\epsilon>0,

here and below
C
and
C_{i}
s are positive constants independent of
t
and
C
varies from line to line. Take
\epsilon=\frac{C_{1}}{C_{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\tilde{\nabla}g_{ij}|^{2}-g^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}|\tilde{\nabla}g_{ij}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-C(n,h)|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|^{2}+C(n,h)(|\tilde{\nabla}g|+|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{2}+|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{4})

(3.1)

\displaystyle\leq C(n,h)(|\tilde{\nabla}g|+|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{2}+|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{4}).

(3.2)

Denote
f=|\tilde{\nabla}g_{ij}|^{2}+1
, then we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f-g^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}f\leq C(n,h)(f+f^{\frac{1}{2}}+f^{2})\leq C(n,h)f(1+f)

Denote
v=1+f
, then

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f-g^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}f\leq C(n,h)fv.

(3.3)

Suppose that
T\in(0,1]
is a constant such that
\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}g(t)|^{p}d\mu_{h}\leq 10A
,
\forall t\in(0,T]
. Then
v
has uniformly bounded
L^{\frac{p}{2}}{(M)}
-norm on
[0,T]
, in other words, we have

\int_{M}v^{\frac{p}{2}}d\mu_{h}\leq C(n,h)A+C(n,h),\forall t\in[0,T].

For any
q>0
, we multiple
f^{q}
to equation (
3.3
) and integrate it, then we get

\int_{M}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f-g^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}f\right)f^{q}d\mu_{h}\leq C(n,h)\int_{M}f^{q+1}vd\mu_{h}.

(3.4)

Thus we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}f^{q+1}d\mu_{h}\leq C(n,h)

\displaystyle\left[-\int_{M}(\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}g^{\alpha\beta})(\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}f)f^{q}d\mu_{h}\right.

\displaystyle-\int_{M}g^{\alpha\beta}(\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}f)(\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}f^{q})d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{M}f^{q+1}vd\mu_{h}\right].

(3.5)

By Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{M}(\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}g^{\alpha\beta})(\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}f)f^{q}d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,h)\int_{M}f^{\frac{1}{2}}|\tilde{\nabla}f|f^{q}d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\epsilon\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}f|^{2}f^{q-1}d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C_{3}}{\epsilon}\int_{M}f^{q+2}d\mu_{h},\forall\epsilon>0.

(3.6)

And we have

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{M}g^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}f\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}f^{q}d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle=-q\int_{M}(g^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}f\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}f)f^{q-1}d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq-C_{4}q\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}f|^{2}f^{q-1}d\mu_{h}.

(3.7)

Recall that
v=f+1
and hence
f^{q+2}\leq f^{q+1}v
. Take
\epsilon=\frac{C_{4}q}{2C_{3}}
in (
3
) and combine inequality (
3
), (
3
) and (
3
), then we get

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{q+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}f^{q+1}d\mu_{h}+C(n,h)q\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}f|^{2}f^{q-1}d\mu_{h}\leq C(n,h)\left(1+\frac{1}{q}\right)\int_{M}f^{q+1}vd\mu_{h}.

(3.8)

Since

\displaystyle\quad\int_{M}\big|\tilde{\nabla}f^{\frac{q+1}{2}}\big|^{2}d\mu_{h}=\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{M}\big|\tilde{\nabla}f\cdot f^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\big|^{2}d\mu_{h},

we get

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{q+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}f^{q+1}d\mu_{h}+C(n,h)\frac{q}{(q+1)^{2}}\int_{M}\big|\tilde{\nabla}f^{\frac{q+1}{2}}\big|^{2}d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,h)\frac{q+1}{q}\int_{M}f^{q+1}vd\mu_{h}.

(3.9)

If we let
q\geq\frac{p}{2}-1>0
, then
0<\frac{p-2}{p}\leq\frac{q}{q+1}\leq 1
,
thus we have

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}f^{q+1}d\mu_{h}+\int_{M}\big|\tilde{\nabla}f^{\frac{q+1}{2}}\big|^{2}d\mu_{h}\leq C_{5}(n,h,p)q\int_{M}f^{q+1}vd\mu_{h}.

(3.10)

For the last term, use Hölder inequality and since
p>n
we can use interpolation inequality, then we get

\displaystyle\int_{M}f^{q+1}vd\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M}v^{\frac{p}{2}}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{M}(f^{q+1})^{\frac{p}{p-2}}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{6}(n,h,A)\left[\epsilon\left(\int_{M}(f^{q+1})^{\frac{n}{n-2}}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}+\epsilon^{-\mu}\int_{M}f^{q+1}d\mu_{h}\right],

(3.11)

where
\mu=(\frac{n-2}{n}-\frac{p-2}{p})/(\frac{p-2}{p}-1)=\frac{p-n}{n}
.
We also have the Sobolev inequality

\int_{M}\big|\tilde{\nabla}f^{\frac{q+1}{2}}\big|^{2}d\mu_{h}\geq C_{7}(n,h)\left(\int_{M}(f^{q+1})^{\frac{n}{n-2}}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}.

(3.12)

Take
\epsilon=\frac{C_{7}}{2C_{5}C_{6}q}
and combine (
3.10
), (
3
) and (
3.12
), then we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}f^{q+1}d\mu_{h}+\left(\int_{M}(f^{q+1})^{\frac{n}{n-2}}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,h,A,p)q^{1+\mu}\int_{M}f^{q+1}d\mu_{h}.

(3.13)

For any
0<t^{\prime}<t^{\prime\prime}<T^{\prime}\leq T\leq 1
, let

\psi(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}0,&\text{if }0\leq t\leq t^{\prime},\\
\frac{t-t^{\prime}}{t^{\prime\prime}-t^{\prime}},&\text{if }t^{\prime}\leq t\leq t^{\prime\prime},\\
1,&\text{if }t^{\prime\prime}\leq t\leq T.\end{array}\right.

Multiplying (
3
) by
\psi
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}\psi f^{q+1}d\mu_{h}+\psi\left(\int_{M}(f^{q+1})^{\frac{n}{n-2}}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq[C(n,h,A,p)q^{1+\mu}\psi+\psi^{\prime}]\int_{M}f^{q+1}d\mu_{h}.

(3.14)

Integrating it with respect to
t
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t^{\prime\prime},T^{\prime}]}\int_{M}f^{q+1}d\mu_{h}+\int_{t^{\prime\prime}}^{T^{\prime}}\left(\int_{M}(f^{q+1})^{\frac{n}{n-2}}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}dt

\displaystyle\leq[C(n,h,A,p)q^{1+\mu}+\frac{1}{t^{\prime\prime}-t^{\prime}}]\int_{t^{\prime}}^{T^{\prime}}\int_{M}f^{q+1}d\mu_{h}dt.

(3.15)

Then we have

\displaystyle\quad\int_{t^{\prime\prime}}^{T^{\prime}}\int_{M}f^{(q+1)(1+\frac{2}{n})}d\mu_{h}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t^{\prime\prime}}^{T^{\prime}}\left(\int_{M}f^{q+1}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\int_{M}f^{(q+1)\frac{n}{n-2}}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[t^{\prime\prime},{T^{\prime}}]}\left(\int_{M}f^{q+1}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\int_{t^{\prime\prime}}^{T^{\prime}}\left(\int_{M}f^{(q+1)\frac{n}{n-2}}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}dt

\displaystyle\leq[C(n,h,A,p)q^{1+\mu}+\frac{1}{t^{\prime\prime}-t^{\prime}}]^{1+\frac{2}{n}}\left(\int_{t^{\prime}}^{T^{\prime}}\int_{M}f^{q+1}d\mu_{h}dt\right)^{1+\frac{2}{n}},\forall q\geq\frac{p}{2}-1.

(3.16)

Denote

H(q,\tau)=\left(\int_{\tau}^{T^{\prime}}\int_{M}f^{q}d\mu_{h}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\forall q\geq\frac{p}{2},0<\tau<T^{\prime}.

Then equation (
3
) can be shortly writen as

H(q(1+\frac{2}{n}),t^{\prime\prime})\leq[{C(n,h,A,p)}q^{1+\mu}+\frac{1}{t^{\prime\prime}-t^{\prime}}]^{\frac{1}{q}}H(q,t^{\prime}).

Fix
0<t_{0}<t_{1}<T^{\prime}\leq 1
,
q_{0}\geq\frac{p}{2}
and set
\chi=1+\frac{2}{n}
,
q_{k}=q_{0}\chi^{k}
,
\tau_{k}=t_{0}+(1-\frac{1}{\chi^{k}})(t_{1}-t_{0})
. Then we have (see also
[
Ji16
]
,
[
JWZ17
]
)

\displaystyle H(q_{k+1},\tau_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq[C(n,h,A,p)q_{k}^{1+\mu}+\frac{1}{t_{1}-t_{0}}\frac{\chi}{\chi-1}\chi^{k}]^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}H(q_{k},\tau_{k})

\displaystyle=[C(n,h,A,p)q_{0}^{1+\mu}\chi^{k(1+\mu)}+\frac{1}{t_{1}-t_{0}}\frac{n+2}{2}\chi^{k}]^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}H(q_{k},\tau_{k})

\displaystyle\leq[\frac{C(n,h,A,p,q_{0})}{t_{1}-t_{0}}]^{\frac{1}{q_{k}}}\chi^{\frac{k(1+\mu)}{q_{k}}}H(q_{k},\tau_{k}),

where in the last inequality we use
0<t_{0}<t_{1}<T
.
By iteration, we get

\displaystyle H(q_{m+1},\tau_{m+1})

\displaystyle\leq[\frac{C(n,h,A,p,q_{0})}{t_{1}-t_{0}}]^{\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{1}{q_{k}}}\chi^{\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{k(1+\mu)}{q_{k}}}H(q_{0},\tau_{0})

\displaystyle\leq C(n,h,A,p,q_{0})(\frac{1}{t_{1}-t_{0}})^{\frac{n+2}{2q_{0}}}H(q_{0},\tau_{0}),

since
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{q_{k}}=\frac{n+2}{2q_{0}}
and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k(1+\mu)}{q_{k}}
converges.
Letting
m\to\infty
, we get

H(p_{\infty},\tau_{\infty})\leq C(n,h,A,p,q_{0})(\frac{1}{t_{1}-t_{0}})^{\frac{n+2}{2q_{0}}}H(q_{0},\tau_{0}),\forall q_{0}\geq\frac{p}{2},

where
p_{\infty}=+\infty,\tau_{\infty}=t_{1}
.
Letting
q_{0}=\frac{p}{2}
, we have

H(\infty,t_{1})\leq C(n,h,A,p)(\frac{1}{t_{1}-t_{0}})^{\frac{n+2}{p}}H(\frac{p}{2},t_{0}).

Thus we have

\sup_{(y,t)\in M\times[t_{1},T^{\prime}]}f(y,t)\leq C(n,h,A,p)\frac{1}{(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\frac{n+2}{p}}}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T^{\prime}}A^{\frac{p}{2}}dt\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}.

Letting

and

, we get

\sup_{y\in M}f(y,T^{\prime})\leq C(n,h,A,p)\frac{1}{(T^{\prime})^{\frac{n}{p}}},\forall T^{\prime}\in(0,T].

Thus we have

|\tilde{\nabla}g|(t)\leq\frac{C(n,h,A,p)}{t^{\frac{n}{2p}}},\forall t\in(0,T],

which completes the proof of the lemma.

Proof of Theorem

.

The existence of the

-flow

To prove that (1) and (2) hold for some

, let

.

We denote

\phi(t)=\int_{M}f^{\frac{p}{2}}d\mu_{h}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(t)=\int_{M}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(f^{\frac{p}{2}})d\mu_{h}.

(3.17)

We denote

\mathcal{T}=\bigg\{T\in(0,1]\big|\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}g(t)|^{p}d\mu_{h}\leq 10A,\forall x\in M,\forall t\in[0,T]\bigg\},

and
T_{\max}=\sup\mathcal{T}
.
Take

, then equation (

) gives

\phi^{\prime}(t)\leq C(n,h,p)\int_{M}f^{p/2}vd\mu_{h},

where

.
By Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle v=f+1\leq 1+\frac{C(n,h,A,p)}{t^{n/p}},\forall t\in(0,T_{\max}].

Thus we get

\phi^{\prime}(t)\leq\left(1+\frac{C(n,h,A,p)}{t^{n/p}}\right)\int_{M}f^{p/2}d\mu_{h},\forall t\in(0,T_{\max}].

(3.18)

Since

,
we have

\phi^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{C(n,h,A,p)}{t^{n/p}}\phi(t),\forall t\in(0,T_{\max}],

By integration, we get

\log\phi(t)\leq\log\phi(0)+C(n,h,A,p)t^{1-n/p},\forall t\in(0,T_{\max}],

\phi(t)\leq\phi(0)e^{C(n,h,A,p)t^{1-n/p}},\forall t\in(0,T_{\max}],

Since

(3.19)

Suppose that if we fix

,

,

,

. Thus we get that the conclusion (1) and (2) hold simultaneously for some

.

To prove (3), recall that Simon’s result, Theorem

, gives

\sup_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{i}g(t)|\leq\frac{c_{i}(n,h)}{t^{i/2}},\quad\forall t\in(0,T_{0}].

We choose a finite atlas for
M
such that
h
is uniformly equivalent to the Euclidean metric of
each chart. For any chart
(U,\Phi)
, we let
\bar{f}
denote any component function
\tilde{\nabla}_{i}g_{jk}(t)
of
\tilde{\nabla}g(t)
, and let

denote the Euclidean metric of
(U,\Phi)
. We can assume that

. We choose an arbitrary point

and let
\gamma(u)
be a curve satisfying
\gamma(0)=p
and
\gamma^{\prime}(u)\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}
, where
\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}
is the coordinate vector field of
(\Phi,U)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\frac{d}{du}\bar{f}\mathop{\scalebox{.6}{$\circ$}}\gamma(u)du=\bar{f}\mathop{\scalebox{.6}{$\circ$}}\gamma(s)-\bar{f}(p),\forall s\in(0,s_{0}),

is a positive constant independent of the chart and

, and
s_{0}
is small enough such that
\gamma(u)\in U
for any

.

On the one hand, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\frac{d}{du}\bar{f}\mathop{\scalebox{.6}{$\circ$}}\gamma(u)du=\int_{0}^{s}d\bar{f}|_{\gamma(u)}(\gamma^{\prime}(u))du=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial x^{i}}(\gamma(u))du.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\bar{f}\mathop{\scalebox{.6}{$\circ$}}\gamma(s)-\bar{f}(p)\leq 2\sup_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}g(t)|\leq\frac{C(n,h,A,p)}{t^{\frac{n}{2p}}}.

Thus there exists
u_{0}\in(0,s)
, such that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial x^{i}}(\gamma(u_{0}))\leq\frac{C(n,h,A,p)}{st^{\frac{n}{2p}}}.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial x^{i}}(\gamma(u_{0}))-\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial x^{i}}(\gamma(0))

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}\bar{f}}{(\partial x^{i})^{2}}(\gamma(u))du

\displaystyle\geq-u_{0}C(n,h)\sup_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{3}g(t)|

\displaystyle\geq-s\frac{C(n,h)}{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial x^{i}}(p)=\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial x^{i}}(\gamma(0))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial x^{i}}(\gamma(u_{0}))+s\frac{C(n,h)}{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(n,h,A,p)}{st^{\frac{n}{2p}}}+s\frac{C(n,h)}{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

(3.20)

The right hand side of (
3
) attains its minimum for
s=C(n,h,A,p)t^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{n}{4p}}
. Note that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{n}{4p}}=0
, thus there exists
T_{0}=T_{0}(n,h,A,p)
, such that for any
t\in(0,T_{0})
, we have
C(n,h,A,p)t^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{n}{4p}}\leq s_{0}
.
Therefore, we have that for any
t\in(0,T_{0})
, the right hand side of (
3
) attains its minimum for some
s\in(0,s_{0})
, and the minimum value is
\frac{C(n,h,A,p)}{t^{\frac{3}{4}+\frac{n}{4p}}}
.

Since
p
is an arbitrary point on
M
and
2^{-1}h_{0}\leq h\leq 2h_{0}
, we get

|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|(t)\leq\frac{C(n,h,p,A)}{t^{\frac{n}{4p}+\frac{3}{4}}}.

Thus (3) holds for some
T_{0}(n,h,A,p)
, which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Theorem
3.2
(2) follows immediately from Theorem
3.6
. Moreover, for Theorem
3.2
(3), we just need to prove the following lemma:

Lemma 3.8
.

In the condition of Theorem
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g(t)|^{2}d\mu_{h}dt\leq C(n,h,A,p).

Proof.

We calculate as the proof of Lemma
3.7
, but this time we preserve the
|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|^{2}
term in (
3.1
). Integrate both sides of the inequality (
3.3
), then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}fd\mu_{h}+\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|^{2}d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,h)\int_{M}g^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}fd\mu_{h}+C(n,h)\int_{M}fvd\mu_{h},

where
f=|\tilde{\nabla}g_{ij}|^{2}+1
and
v=f+1
, here and below
C
and
C_{i}
s denote a positive constant and
C
varies from line to line. Using integration by parts twice, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}fd\mu_{h}+\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|^{2}d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,h)\int_{M}\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}g^{\alpha\beta}fd\mu_{h}+C(n,h)\int_{M}fvd\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(n,h)\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|fd\mu_{h}+C(n,h)\int_{M}fvd\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(n,h)\epsilon\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|^{2}d\mu_{h}+C_{1}(n,h)\epsilon^{-1}\int_{M}f^{2}d\mu_{h}+C(n,h)\int_{M}fvd\mu_{h},\forall\epsilon>0.

Taking
\epsilon=\frac{C_{1}}{2}
, since
f\leq v

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}fd\mu_{h}+\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|^{2}d\mu_{h}\leq C_{2}(n,h)\int_{M}fvd\mu_{h}.

(3.21)

For the last term, using Hölder inequality and interpolation inequality, we get

\displaystyle\int_{M}fvd\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M}v^{\frac{p}{2}}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{M}f^{\frac{p}{p-2}}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}(n,h,A)\left[\epsilon\left(\int_{M}f^{\frac{n}{n-2}}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}+\epsilon^{-\mu}\int_{M}fd\mu_{h}\right],

(3.22)

where
\mu=(\frac{n-2}{n}-\frac{p-2}{p})/(\frac{p-2}{p}-1)=\frac{p-n}{n}
.
We also have the Sobolev inequality

\left(\int_{M}f^{\frac{n}{n-2}}d\mu_{h}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq C_{4}(n,h)\int_{M}\left|\tilde{\nabla}f^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}d\mu_{h}.

(3.23)

Since

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}f^{\frac{1}{2}}=\tilde{\nabla}(|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\tilde{\nabla}\langle\tilde{\nabla}g,\tilde{\nabla}g\rangle}{2(|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\langle\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g,\tilde{\nabla}g\rangle}{2(|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g||\tilde{\nabla}g|}{(|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|,

(3.24)

Taking
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2C_{2}C_{3}C_{4}}
, by (
3.21
), (
3
), (
3.23
) and (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}fd\mu_{h}+\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|^{2}d\mu_{h}\leq C(n,h,A,p)\int_{M}fd\mu_{h}.

By Theorem
3.6
,
\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}g(t)|^{p}d\mu_{h}\leq 10A
,
\forall t\in(0,T_{0}]
. Since

, we have
\int_{M}fd\mu_{h}\leq C(n,h,A,p)
, thus we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{M}fd\mu_{h}+\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|^{2}d\mu_{h}\leq C(n,h,A,p).

Integrate it, we get

\displaystyle\int_{M}f(T_{0})d\mu_{h}-\int_{M}f(0)d\mu_{h}+\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|^{2}d\mu_{h}dt\leq C(n,h,A,p).

Since
\int_{M}f(T_{0})d\mu_{h}\geq 0
and
\int_{M}f(0)d\mu_{h}\leq C(n,h,A,p)

\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}^{2}g|^{2}d\mu_{h}dt\leq C(n,h,A,p),

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

4.
Conjugate heat equation

Let
(M^{n},\hat{g})
be a compact manifold let
g(t)
be the Ricci flow starting from the metric
\hat{g}
. In this section we suppose that
\hat{g}
is smooth. Let
\mu_{g(t)}
be the Lebesgue measure taken with respect to
g(t)
, and let
R_{\hat{g}}
and
R_{g(t)}
denote the distirbutional scalar curvature of
\hat{g}
and
g(t)
respectively. Let
\tilde{\varphi}
be an arbitrary nonnegative function in
C^{\infty}(M)
and take any
T\in(0,T_{0}]
. We consider the following conjugate heat equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}=-\Delta_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}+R_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}\quad on\ M\times[0,T],\\
&\left.\varphi_{t}\right|_{t=T}=\tilde{\varphi},\end{array}\right.

where
\Delta_{g(t)}
is the Laplacian taken with respect to
g(t)
.

For fixed
(x,t)\in M\times(0,T_{0}]
, the conjugate heat kernel on the Ricci flow background is the function
K(x,t;\cdot,\cdot)
, defined for
0\leq s<t
and
y\in M
and satisfying

\displaystyle(-\partial_{s}-\Delta_{y}+R(y,s))K(x,t;y,s)=0\qquad and\qquad\lim_{s\to t^{-}}K(x,t;y,s)=\delta_{x}(y),

where the Laplacian is taken with respect to
g(s)
, and
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac operator supported on
\{x\}
.
K
also satisfies
(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x})K(x,t;y,s)=0
, where
\Delta_{x}
is taken with respect to
g(t)
.

By directly calculation(see also
[
BZ17
]
), we get that equation (
4
) has a solution with the explicity expression

\varphi_{t}(x)=\int_{M}K(y,T;x,t)\tilde{\varphi}(y)d\mu_{g(T)}(y).

(4.3)

By the maximum principle, we get that this solution is nonnegative and unique. Furthermore, by this expression we can see that
\varphi_{t}
is uniformly bounded. Our main purpose in this section is proving the following estimates for
\varphi_{t}
which will be used in the proof of our main theorem.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume as above, then
\varphi_{t}
satisfies

(1)

\varphi_{t}\leq C(n,h,A,p,\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}})
,
\forall t\in[0,T].

(2)

\int_{M}|\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}|^{2}_{g(t)}d\mu_{g(t)}\leq C(n,h,A,p,\tilde{\varphi})
,
\forall t\in[0,T].

(3)

\int_{M}(R_{g(t)}-a)\varphi_{t}d\mu_{g(t)}
is monotonously increasing with respect to
t
.

Proof.

To see (1), by equation (
4.3
), we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}(x)\leq\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{M}K(y,T;x,t)d\mu_{g(T)}(y).

(4.4)

We denote
F(t,T)=\int_{M}K(y,T;x,t)d\mu_{g(T)}(y)
, then we have
\lim_{T\to t^{+}}F(t,T)=1
, and

\displaystyle\partial_{T}F(t,T)=\int_{M}\left(\Delta_{y}K(y,T;x,t)-R_{T}K(y,T;x,t)\right)d\mu_{g(T)}(y),

where we have used the standard evolution equation
\partial_{T}d\mu_{g(T)}=-R_{T}d\mu_{g(T)}
. By divergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Delta_{y}K(y,T;x,t)d\mu_{g(T)}(y)=0.

Thus by Theorem
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{T}F(t,T)\leq\frac{C(n,h,A,p)}{T^{\alpha}}F(t,T),

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Since
\lim_{T\to t^{+}}F(t,T)=1
, by integration we have

\displaystyle F(t,T)\leq C(n,h,A,p),\forall 0\leq t<T\leq T_{0}.

(4.5)

By (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}\leq C(n,h,A,p,\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}}),\forall t\in[0,T],

which proves (1).

To prove (2), denote
E(t)=\int_{M}|\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}|^{2}_{g(t)}d\mu_{g(t)}
. By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E(t)=\int_{M}\left(-R_{g(t)}|d\varphi_{t}|_{g(t)}^{2}+2{\rm Ric}_{g(t)}(\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t},\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t})+2\langle\nabla_{g(t)}\partial_{t}\varphi_{t},\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}\rangle_{g(t)}\right)d\mu_{g(t)}.

(4.6)

By (
4
) and using Bochner formula we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\nabla_{g(t)}\partial_{t}\varphi_{t},\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}\rangle_{g(t)}d\mu_{g(t)}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}-\langle\nabla_{g(t)}(\Delta_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}-R_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}),\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}\rangle_{g(t)}d\mu_{g(t)}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(-\langle\nabla_{g(t)}\Delta_{g(t)}\varphi_{t},\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}\rangle_{g(t)}+\langle\nabla_{g(t)}(R_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}),\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}\rangle_{g(t)}\right)d\mu_{g(t)}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{g(t)}|\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}|_{g(t)}^{2}+|\nabla_{g(t)}^{2}\varphi_{t}|_{g(t)}^{2}+{\rm Ric}_{g(t)}(\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t},\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad+\langle\nabla_{g(t)}(R_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}),\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}\rangle_{g(t)}\right)d\mu_{g(t)}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left[\left(|\nabla_{g(t)}^{2}\varphi_{t}|_{g(t)}^{2}+{\rm Ric}_{g(t)}(\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t},\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t})\right)-R_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}\Delta_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}\right]d\mu_{g(t)}.

(4.7)

Since
|\Delta_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}|_{g(t)}^{2}\leq C(n)|\nabla_{g(t)}^{2}\varphi_{t}|^{2}
, using Cauchy inequality, (
4
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\nabla_{g(t)}\partial_{t}\varphi_{t},\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}\rangle_{g(t)}d\mu_{g(t)}\geq\int_{M}\left({\rm Ric}_{g(t)}(\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t},\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t})-C(n)R_{g(t)}^{2}\varphi_{t}^{2}\right)d\mu_{g(t)}.

(4.8)

By (
4.6
) and (
4.8
), since
|R_{g(t)}|_{g(t)}\leq c(n)|{\rm Ric}_{g(t)}|_{g(t)}
, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E(t)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}\left(4{\rm Ric}_{g(t)}(\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t},\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t})-R_{g(t)}|\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}|_{g(t)}^{2}-C(n)R_{g(t)}^{2}\varphi_{t}^{2}\right)d\mu_{g(t)}

\displaystyle\geq-C(n,h,A,p)\int_{M}|{\rm Ric}_{g(t)}||\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}|^{2}d\mu_{g(t)}-C(n,h,A,p)\int_{M}R_{g(t)}^{2}\varphi_{t}^{2}d\mu_{g(t)}.

(4.9)

By Theorem
3.2
and (1), we have for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E(t)\geq-\frac{C(n,h,A,p)}{t^{\alpha}}\int_{M}|\nabla_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}|^{2}d\mu_{g(t)}-C(n,h,A,p,\tilde{\varphi})\int_{M}R_{g(t)}^{2}d\mu_{g(t)}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(E(t)+1\right)\geq-\frac{C(n,h,A,p)}{t^{\alpha}}\left(E(t)+1\right)-C(n,h,A,p,\tilde{\varphi})\int_{M}R_{g(t)}^{2}d\mu_{g(t)}.

Dividing both sides by
\left(E(t)+1\right)
, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\log\left(E(t)+1\right)\geq-\frac{C(n,h,A,p)}{t^{\alpha}}-C(n,h,A,p,\tilde{\varphi})\int_{M}R_{g(t)}^{2}d\mu_{g(t)}.

By Lemma
3.8
,
\int_{M}R_{g(t)}^{2}d\mu_{g(t)}
is integrable on
(0,T)
. Since
\varphi_{T}=\tilde{\varphi}
,
E(T)\leq C(n,h,A,p,\tilde{\varphi})
and
\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}
is integrable on
(0,T)
, we can integrate the inequality above and get

E(t)\leq C(n,h,A,p,\tilde{\varphi}),\forall t\in[0,T].

Thus we complete the proof of (2).

To prove (3), we can directly calculate that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{M}(R_{g(t)}-a)\varphi_{t}d\mu_{g(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left[\left(\Delta_{g(t)}R_{g(t)}+2|{\rm Ric}_{g(t)}|_{g(t)}^{2}\right)\varphi_{t}+(R_{g(t)}-a)\left(-\Delta_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}+R_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}\right)+(R_{g(t)}-a)\varphi_{t}\left(-R_{g(t)}\right)\right]d\mu_{g(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}2|{\rm Ric}_{g(t)}|_{g(t)}^{2}\varphi_{t}d\mu_{g(t)}+\int_{M}\left(\Delta_{g(t)}R_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}-R_{g(t)}\Delta_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}\right)d\mu_{g(t)}+a\int_{M}\Delta_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}d\mu_{g(t)}.

By integration by parts, we have that

\int_{M}\left(\Delta_{g(t)}R_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}-R_{g(t)}\Delta_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}\right)d\mu_{g(t)}=0,\int_{M}\Delta_{g(t)}\varphi_{t}d\mu_{g(t)}=0.

Thus we have

\partial_{t}\int_{M}(R_{g(t)}-a)\varphi_{t}d\mu_{g(t)}\geq 0.

Therefore
\int_{M}(R_{g(t)}-a)\varphi_{t}d\mu_{g(t)}
is monotonously increasing. Hence we finish the proof of (3) and thus the proof of the proposition.
∎

5.
Proof of the main theorem

In this section, we will give the proof of our main theorem. Let us restate Theorem
1.1
as follows.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
M^{n}
be a compact manifold with a metric
\hat{g}\in W^{1,p}(M)

(n<p\leq\infty)
. Assume the distirbutional scalar curvature
R_{\hat{g}}\geq a
for some constant
a
, and let
g(t),t\in(0,T_{0}]
be the Ricci flow starting from the metric
\hat{g}
. Then for any
t\in(0,T_{0}]
, there holds
R_{g(t)}\geq a
on
M
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, we get a family of smooth metrics
\hat{g}_{\delta}
which converges to
\hat{g}
both in
C^{0}
-norm and
W^{1,p}
-norm. Then by Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}R_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}\varphi d\mu_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}-\langle R_{\hat{g}},\varphi\rangle\right|\leq b^{\prime}_{\delta}\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,\frac{n}{n-1}}(M)},\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M),\forall\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}],

(5.1)

where
b^{\prime}_{\delta}
is a positive function of
\delta
which only depends on
\hat{g}
and satisfies
\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}b^{\prime}_{\delta}=0
and
\delta_{0}
is some positive constant small enough.

Moreover, we have
\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\left\|\frac{d\mu_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{\hat{g}}}-1\right\|_{C^{0}(M)}=0
, thus by Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}\varphi d\mu_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}-\int_{M}\varphi d\mu_{\hat{g}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{M}\varphi\left(\frac{d\mu_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{\hat{g}}}-1\right)d\mu_{\hat{g}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{d\mu_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{\hat{g}}}-1\right\|_{C^{0}(M)}\int_{M}|\varphi|d\mu_{\hat{g}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\hat{g})\left\|\frac{d\mu_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}}{d\mu_{\hat{g}}}-1\right\|_{C^{0}(M)}\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,\frac{n}{n-1}}(M)}.

(5.2)

By triangular inequality, combining (
5.1
) and (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}(R_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}-a)\varphi d\mu_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}-\langle R_{\hat{g}}-a,\varphi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{M}R_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}\varphi d\mu_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}-\langle R_{\hat{g}},\varphi\rangle\right|+|a|\left|\int_{M}\varphi d\mu_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}-\int_{M}\varphi d\mu_{\hat{g}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq b_{\delta}\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,\frac{n}{n-1}}(M)},\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M),\forall\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}],

(5.3)

where
b_{\delta}
is a positive function of
\delta
which only depends on
a
,
n
,
\hat{g}
and satisfies
\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}b_{\delta}=0
.

By the condition on
R_{\hat{g}}
we have

\langle R_{\hat{g}}-a,\varphi\rangle\geq 0,\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M),\varphi\geq 0.

(5.4)

Let
g_{\delta}(t)
,
g(t)
be the Ricci flow starting from the metric
\hat{g}_{\delta}
and
\hat{g}
respectively, and let
R_{g_{\delta}(t)}
,
R_{g(t)}
and
d\mu_{g_{\delta}(T)}
,
d\mu_{g(t)}
be the scalar curvature and the volume form of
g_{\delta}(t)
,
g(t)
respectively. Take an arbitrary
T\in(0,T_{0}]
and an arbitrary nonnegative function
\tilde{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}(M)
. For any
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
, we let
\varphi_{t}
be the solution of the conjugate heat equation taken with repsect to the family of metrics
g_{\delta}(t)
and with
\varphi_{t}|_{t=T}=\tilde{\varphi}
, then
\varphi_{0}\geq 0
, and by Proposition
4.1
(3), (
5
) and (
5.4
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}(R_{g_{\delta}(T)}-a)\tilde{\varphi}d\mu_{g_{\delta}(T)}\geq\int_{M}(R_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}-a)\varphi_{0}d\mu_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}\geq-b_{\delta}\|\varphi_{0}\|_{W^{1,\frac{n}{n-1}}(M)}.

(5.5)

By Hölder inequality and Proposition
4.1
(1) and (2), since
g_{\delta}(t),(\delta,t)\in(0,\delta_{0}]\times[0,T_{0}]
and
h
are uniformly equivalent, by Hölder inequality we have

\|\varphi_{0}\|_{W^{1,\frac{n}{n-1}}(M)}\leq C(n,h,p)\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}+\|\nabla_{\hat{g}_{\delta}}\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(M)}\leq C(n,h,A,p,\tilde{\varphi}),

and as mentioned in Remark
3.5
, it is known in
[
Si02
]
that for any fixed
T\in(0,T_{0}]
,
g_{\delta}(T)
smoothly converges to
g(T)
as
\delta
tends to
0^{+}
.

Thus we can let
\delta\to 0^{+}
in inequality (
5.5
), and get

\displaystyle\int_{M}(R_{g(T)}-a)\tilde{\varphi}d\mu_{g(T)}\geq 0,\forall T\in(0,T_{0}],\forall\tilde{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}(M).

Since
g(t)
is a smooth metric for
t\in(0,T_{0}]
, we get that
R_{g(t)}\geq a
pointwisely on
M
, which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Recall that
R_{g(t)}
satisfies the standard evolution equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}R_{g(t)}=\Delta R_{g(t)}+2|{\rm Ric}_{g(t)}|^{2}.

(5.6)

Noting that
R_{g}\geq 0
in distributional sense, by Theorem
1.1
we see that
R_{g(t)}\geq 0
for any
t\in(0,T_{0})
. There are two cases needed to be handled.
First, if
R_{g(t)}\equiv 0
on
M\times(0,T_{0}]
, then (
5.6
) shows that
{\rm Ric}_{g(t)}\equiv 0
on
M\times(0,T_{0}]
. Then by Ricci flow equation we have that
g(t)=g(s)
,
\forall t,s\in(0,T_{0}]
. By (1) of Theorem
3.2
we directly have that
(M,\hat{g})
is isometric to
(M,g(t))
.
On the other hand, if
R_{g(t)}\not\equiv 0
on
M\times(0,T_{0}]
, then by the maximal principle we get that
R_{g(t)}>0
on
M
for any
t\in(0,T_{0})
. Since
g(t)
is smooth and
R_{g(t)}>0
pointwisely on
M
, the theorem holds.
∎

6.
Further questions

In this section, let us discuss some problems related to singular metrics. To study the scalar curvature of low-regularity metrics, Schoen proposed such a conjecture (see
[
LM19
]
):

Conjecture 6.1

(Schoen)
.

Let
g
be a
C^{0}
metric on
M
which is smooth away from a submanifold
\Sigma\subset M
with codim
(\Sigma\subset M)\geq 3
,
\sigma(M)\leq 0
and
R_{g}\geq 0
on
M\setminus\Sigma
, then
g
smoothly extends to
M
and
{\rm Ric}_{g}\equiv 0
.

Gromov also considered scalar curvature with low-regularity metrics, see
[
Gm14
]
. For a Ricci flow approach, see also
[
Ba16
]
,
[
Bu19
]
. Motivated by their results, we will have the following natural question:

Question:
Let
(M^{n},g,\Sigma)
be a compact manifold with
g\in C^{0}(M)
and
g
is smooth away from
\Sigma
, and
R_{g}\geq 0
on
M\setminus\Sigma
. What is the condition on
\Sigma
such that there exists a smooth metric on
M
with nonnegative scalar curvature? What is the condition on
\Sigma
such that the Ricci flow starting from
g
has nonnegative scalar curvature for time
t>0
?

Remark 6.1
.

Comparing with the conjecture of Schoen and the structure of nonnegative scalar curvature in
[
SY79b
]
, one may expect that
\Sigma
is a codimensional three submanifold for the above question.

Remark 6.2
.

From our result and the main result in
[
JSZ21
]
, if we assume
g\in C^{0}\cap W^{1,p}(M)

(n<p\leq\infty)
, the condition for
\Sigma
should be
\mathcal{H}^{n-\frac{p}{p-1}}(\Sigma)<\infty
when
n<p<\infty
or
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Sigma)=0
when
p=\infty
.

Appendix A
Lower scalar curvature bounds on singular metrics

In this appendix, we check that if
g
is
C^{2}
away from a closed subset
\Sigma
with
\mathcal{H}^{n-\frac{p}{p-1}}(\Sigma)<\infty
when
n<p<\infty
or
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Sigma)=0
when
p=\infty
, and
R_{g}\geq a
pointwisely on
M\setminus\Sigma
, then
R_{g}\geq a
in distributional sense. Though it is proved in
[
JSZ21
]
, for the sake of completeness, we give a proof here.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
M^{n}
be a smooth manifold with
g\in C^{0}\cap W^{1,p}_{loc}(M)
with
n\leq p\leq\infty
. Assume
g
is smooth away from a closed subset
\Sigma
with
\mathcal{H}^{n-\frac{p}{p-1}}(\Sigma)<\infty
if
n<p<\infty
or
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Sigma)=0
if
p=\infty
, and assume
R_{g}\geq a
on
M\setminus\Sigma
for some constant
a
, then
\langle R_{g}-a,u\rangle\geq 0
for any nonnegative
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

To prove the Lemma
A.1
, we need a standard cut-off function lemma (see
[
JSZ21
, Lemma A.1]
and
[
Ch03
]
):

Lemma A.2
.

Let
(M^{n},h)
be a smooth manifold. Assume
\Sigma\subset M
is a closed subset. Then there exists a sequence of cut-off function
\varphi_{\epsilon}
of
\Sigma
such that the following holds:

(1)

0\leq\varphi_{\epsilon}\leq 1
and
\varphi\equiv 0
in a neighborhood of
\Sigma
and
\varphi_{\epsilon}\equiv 1
on
M\setminus B_{\epsilon}(\Sigma)
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Sigma)=0
, then
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi_{\epsilon}|(x)dx=0.

(3)

If
\mathcal{H}^{n-p}(\Sigma)<\infty
with
p>1
, then
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi_{\epsilon}|^{p}(x)dx=0.

proof of Lemma
A.1
.

Let
\eta_{\epsilon}\geq 0
be a sequence of smooth cut-off functions of
\Sigma
as in Lemma
A.2
such that

(1)

\eta_{\epsilon}\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
\Sigma
,

(2)

{\rm supp~}\eta_{\epsilon}\subset B_{\epsilon}(\Sigma)
and
0\leq\eta_{\epsilon}\leq 1
.

(3)

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{M}|\nabla\eta_{\epsilon}|^{p/(p-1)}d\mu_{h}=0
.

For any
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
with
u\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\langle R_{g}-a,u\rangle=\langle R_{g}-a,\eta_{\epsilon}u\rangle+\langle R_{g}-a,(1-\eta_{\epsilon})u\rangle.

(A.1)

Noting that for any
\epsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle\langle R_{g}-a,(1-\eta_{\epsilon})u\rangle=\int_{M\setminus\Sigma}(R_{g}-a)(1-\eta_{\epsilon})ud\mu_{g}\geq 0.

(A.2)

Thus to prove the lemma, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}|\langle R_{g}-a,\eta_{\epsilon}u\rangle|=0.

(A.3)

Actually, noting that
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
, by definition,
\langle R_{g},u\rangle=\int_{M}\left(-V\cdot\tilde{\nabla}\left(u\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}\right)+Fu\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}\right)d\mu_{h}
, we can estimate

\displaystyle|\langle R_{g}-a,\eta_{\epsilon}u\rangle|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}|V|\cdot\left|\tilde{\nabla}(\eta_{\epsilon}u\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}})\right|d\mu_{h}+\int_{M}|F-a|\cdot\eta_{\epsilon}u\frac{d\mu_{g}}{d\mu_{h}}d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{M}|V||\bar{\nabla}\eta_{\epsilon}|d\mu_{h}+C\int_{M}|V||\tilde{\nabla}g|\eta_{\epsilon}d\mu_{h}+C\int_{M}|V|\eta_{\epsilon}d\mu_{h}+C\int_{M}|F-a|\eta_{\epsilon}d\mu_{h}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{p}d\mu_{h}\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{M}\left(|\tilde{\nabla}\eta_{\epsilon}|^{p/(p-1)}+|\eta_{\epsilon}|^{p/(p-1)}\right)d\mu_{h}\right)^{(p-1)/p}

\displaystyle+C\left(\left(\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}g|^{p}d\mu_{h}\right)^{2/p}+1\right)\left(\int_{M}|\eta_{\epsilon}|^{p/(p-2)}d\mu_{h}\right)^{(p-2)/p},

here and below
C=C(n,p,h,g)
will denote a positive constant independent of
\epsilon
and varies from line to line.

By Hölder inequality and Sobolev inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|\eta_{\epsilon}|^{p/(p-1)}d\mu_{h}\right)^{(p-1)/p}\leq C(h)\left(\int_{M}|\eta_{\epsilon}|^{p/(p-2)}d\mu_{h}\right)^{(p-2)/p}\leq C(h)\left(\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}\eta_{\epsilon}|^{p/(p-1)}d\mu_{h}\right)^{(p-1)/p}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle|\langle R_{g}-a,\eta_{\epsilon}u\rangle|\leq C\left(\int_{M}|\tilde{\nabla}\eta_{\epsilon}|^{p/(p-1)}d\mu_{h}\right)^{(p-1)/p}.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0
, by the properties of
\eta_{\epsilon}
we get (
A.3
). Thus we finish the proof of the lemma for
n<p<\infty
.

When
p=\infty
, the arguement above still works. (See also
[
LL15
]
.) Thus the lemma holds.
∎

Quantitative compactness estimates for stochastic conservation laws
K. H. Karlsen

Abstract
. We present a quantitative compensated compactness estimate for stochastic conservation laws, which generalises a previous result of Golse & Perthame [ 8 ] for deterministic equations. With a stochastic modification of Kružkov’s interpolation lemma, this estimate provides bounds on the rate at which a sequence of vanishing viscosity solutions becomes compact.



1.
Introduction

During the last decade many authors investigated the well-posedness of
hyperbolic conservation laws perturbed by stochastic source terms, see

[
5
,
6
]
and the list
of references in
[
7
]
.
The initial-value problem for these Itô-type
SPDEs take the form

(1.1)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}u+\Div\!f(x,u)=\sigma(x,u)\dot{W}(t)+R(x,u),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{split}

where
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{d})
is the flux vector,

R
is the “deterministic” source term,

u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the initial
function, and
T>0
is a final time.
The term
\sigma\dot{W}(t)
is a stochastic forcing term,
where
W
is a cylindrical Wiener
process
[
4
]
with noise amplitude
\sigma
.
We will refer to the SPDEs (
1.1
)
as stochastic conservation laws. Stochastic conservation laws
are used to model a wide variety of physical systems
that are subject to random fluctuations and have
wave-propagating behavior.

We fix a stochastic basic
\mathcal{S}

consisting of a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
,
and a complete right-continuous filtration

\left\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}_{t\in[0,T]}
. The solution
u
, the Wiener process
W
,
and all other relevant processes, are always understood as defined
on
\mathcal{S}
and to be appropriately measurable with respect to the
filtration
\left\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}_{t\in[0,T]}
. We refer
to
[
7
, Pages 38, 40]
for precise
regularity and growth assumptions on
f
,
\sigma
,
R
.
For a precise definition of entropy/kinetic solutions and a
corresponding well-posedness theorem, see

[
7
, Section 5]
. Under the assumptions
that
R\equiv 0
and
f=f(u)
, we
refer to the original works
[
6
]
(on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
) and

[
5
]
(on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
).

In this paper, we are interested in deriving
quantitative estimates
that can be used to prove the
convergence in
L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}
of sequences

\left\{u_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of approximate
solutions to (
1.1
). As a concrete example,
consider the parabolic SPDE

(1.2)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}u_{n}+\Div\!f(x,u_{n})-\varepsilon_{n}\Delta u_{n}=\sigma(x,u_{n})\dot{W}(t)+R(x,u_{n}),\end{split}

where
\varepsilon_{n}\xrightarrow{n\uparrow\infty}0
. For the well-posedness of classical
solutions to (
1.2
), see
[
6
]

under the assumptions that
R\equiv 0
and

f=f(u)
does not depend on
x
. For
the general context provided by (
1.2
),
see
[
9
]
and

[
7
, Theorem 5.1]
.

In the study of SPDEs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, weight functions are sometimes
used. These weight functions are used to control
the growth of solutions as they approach infinity, which
in turn allows for the derivation of optimal
conditions on the coefficients of the equations.
The use of weighted
L^{p}
spaces facilitates
the analysis of stochastic conservation laws
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see, e.g.,
[
9
]
).
Denote by
L^{p}(\chi dx)
the
weighted
L^{p}
space of functions for which

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u(x)\right|^{p}\,\chi(x)\,dx<\infty,

where
\chi
is a weight function. The collection of
relevant weights, denoted by
\mathcal{W}
, consists
of
\chi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for which
\chi(x)>0
and

\left|\nabla\chi(x)\right|\leq C_{\chi}\chi(x)
,
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
C_{\chi}>0
is a
constant depending only on
\chi
.
A simple example of a (smooth) weight function includes

\chi(x)=\chi_{N}(x)=(1+\left|x\right|^{2})^{-N}
,
N>d/2
.
Any weight function
\chi\in\mathcal{W}
satisfies the properties

(1.3)

\left|\chi(x+z)-\chi(x)\right|\lesssim\chi(x)\left|z\right|,\quad\sup_{\left|x-y\right|\leq R}\frac{\chi(x)}{\chi(y)}\lesssim_{R}1,

which are used repeatedly in this paper.
Clearly,
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{p}(\chi dx)
,
p\in[1,\infty)
.
Moreover,
\chi^{-1}\in L^{\infty}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
implies that

L^{p}(\chi dx)\subset L^{p}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Since
\chi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we also have

L^{q}(\chi dx)\subset L^{p}(\chi dx)

for all
q,p
such that
1\leq p<q<\infty
.

Now regarding a priori estimates for
u_{n}
, one can prove that
there is an
n
-independent constant
C=C(\chi,p,r)
such that

(1.4)

\Ex\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}^{r}\leq C,\quad\Ex\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varepsilon_{n}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{2}\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\right|^{r}\leq C,

\forall p,r\in[2,\infty)
and for any
\chi\in\mathcal{W}
,
see
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
,
and
[
7
, Remark 5.9]
. In the general case,
u_{n}
does not
exhibit
n
-independent
L^{\infty}

and
BV
estimates
[
2
, p. 711]
.

In
[
2
]
(see also
[
5
]
),
the authors derived some basic quantitative compactness estimates.
These
n
-uniform estimates, which were used and
further refined in
[
9
,
10
]

and
[
3
]
, take the form

(1.5)

\Ex\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{\delta}(z)\left|u_{n}(t,x+z)-u_{n}(t,x-z)\right|\chi(x)\,dx\,dz\lesssim_{T}\delta^{\mu_{x}},

for any
t\in(0,T)
and some
\mu_{x}\in(0,1)
,
where
\left\{J_{\delta}\right\}_{\delta>0}
is a mollifier sequence.
One can prove that (
1.5
) implies
a “fractional
BV
” estimate of the form

(1.6)

\Ex\sup_{\left|z\right|<\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|u_{n}(t,\cdot+z)-u_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{1}(\chi dx)}\,dt\lesssim_{T}\delta^{\mu_{x}},

for any
\delta>0
, see,
e.g.,
[
2
, Lemma 2]

and Proposition
2.5
herein.
Estimates like (
1.5
) are
often linked to the
L^{1}
stability
àla
Kružkov
of the solution operator.

Using the approximating SPDE and a
modification
[
2
,
10
]

of an interpolation technique due to Kružkov, one
can use the spatial estimate (
1.5
)
to establish that there exists
\mu_{t}\in(0,1)
such that

(1.7)

\Ex\sup_{\tau\in(0,\delta)}\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\left\|u_{n}(t+\tau,\cdot)-u_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{1}(\chi dx)}\,dt\lesssim_{T}\delta^{\mu_{t}},

see Proposition
2.6
for
a general estimate of this type.

We refer to translation estimates like (
1.6
)
and (
1.7
) as quantitative compactness estimates,
see Section
2
for further discussion and refinements.
They can be used to derive convergence results (via Cauchy sequence
arguments) and error estimates for approximate solutions.
Moreover, as part of the stochastic compactness method, one
can use them to show that the laws
\mathcal{L}(u_{n})
of
u_{n}

form a tight sequence of probability
measures on
L^{1}(\chi dx)
, which allows for the
application of Skorokhod’s representation theorem.

In
[
6
]
, the authors establish convergence
of the viscosity approximations (
1.2
)
using compensated compactness, assuming
d=1
,
R\equiv 0
, and the
genuine nonlinearity of
f=f(u)
(
f^{\prime\prime}\neq 0
a.e.).
The main result of our paper is a
refinement of the compensated compactness approach—in the
spirit of
[
8
]
—that leads to
a spatial compactness estimate
like (
1.6
) for the viscosity
approximation, under a strengthened nonlinearity
condition (
\left|f^{\prime\prime}\texttrademark\right|\geq c>0
).
A temporal estimate (
1.7
)
follows from this estimate
via Proposition
2.6
.
Roughly speaking, in Section
3
, we prove
(
1.6
) with
\delta_{x}=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4p}
,
for any finite
p>2
, assuming that the viscosity
approximation
u_{n}
is uniformly bounded
in
L^{p}_{\omega,t,x}
for any
finite
p
, see (
1.4
).

In the case that
u_{n}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}_{\omega,t,x}
,
we recover
\delta_{x}=\frac{1}{4}
, which coincides with the
known Besov regularity exponent (in
x
) of entropy solutions to
conservation laws with one convex entropy
and an entropy production that is a signed
Radon measure
[
8
, Theorem 5]
.
The quantitative version of compensated compactness
allows for non-homogenous/discontinuous flux functions

f=f(x,u)
, in which case a signed measure arises naturally.
The details will be presented elsewhere.

For simplicity of presentation, we will
in what follows assume that
W
is
a real-valued Wiener process and that

\sigma(x,u)
is a real-valued function.
The extension to a cylindrical Wiener
process with corresponding
operator-valued noise amplitude
is standard, as discussed in
[
4
]

and the references cited earlier.

2.
Quantitative compactness estimates

A subset
K
of a metric space
(X,d)
is
precompact if its closure
\overline{K}
is compact.
A subset
K
of a complete metric space
(X,d)
is precompact
if and only if it is totally bounded, meaning that
for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a finite cover of
K
of open balls
of radius
\varepsilon
. We will use the well-known Kolmogorov–Riesz–Fréchet
characterization of precompact subsets
of
L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in terms of the uniform
continuity of the translation in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
see, e.g.,
[
1
, Theorem 4.26]
.

Using the fact that translation is continuous in
L^{1}
, we have
the following simple but useful lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{U}\subset_{b}L^{1}(\chi dx)
,
\chi\in\mathcal{W}
.
Fix any
J\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

\operatorname{supp}\,(J)\subset B(0,R)
,

R>0
. Then
\mathcal{K}:=J\star\mathcal{U}=\left\{J\star u:u\in\mathcal{U}\right\}

is precompact in
L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

Clearly, as
\chi>0
on any set
D\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
if
J\star\mathcal{U}\chi:=\left\{(J\star u)\chi:u\in\mathcal{U}\right\}

is precompact in
L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

\mathcal{K}=J\star\mathcal{U}
is precompact
in
L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as well.

Let us verify the precompactness of
J\star\mathcal{U}\chi
using
the Kolmogorov–Riesz–Fréchet theorem.
First, we claim that the set
J\star\mathcal{U}\chi

is bounded in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Indeed,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\|J\star u\chi\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|J(y)\right|\left|u(x-y)\right|\chi(x)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|J(y)\right|\left|u(x-y)\right|\chi(x-y)\frac{\chi(x)}{\chi(x-y)}\,dx\,dy\overset{\eqref{eq:weight-property}}{\lesssim_{R}}\left\|J\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{1}(\chi dx)}.

Next, we verify the translation condition.
For any translation
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\left\|J\star u\chi)(\cdot+z)-J\star u\chi\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|J\star u(x+z)-J\star u(x)\right|\chi(x)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J\star u(x+z)\left|\chi(x+z)-\chi(x)\right|\,dx=:I_{1}+I_{2},

where
I_{1}\overset{\eqref{eq:weight-property}}{\lesssim_{R}}\left\|J(\cdot+z)-J\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{1}(\chi dx)}

and

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:weight-property}}{\leq}\left|z\right|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|J\star u(x+z)\chi(x+z)\right|\frac{\chi(x)}{\chi(x+z)}\,dx

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:weight-property}}{\lesssim}\left|z\right|\left\|J\star u\chi\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\overset{\eqref{eq:J-u-chi-L1}}{\lesssim_{R}}\left|z\right|\left\|J\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{1}(\chi dx)}.

Consequently, as
\left|z\right|\to 0
,

\left\|(J\star u\chi)(\cdot+z)-J\star u\chi\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to 0
, uniformly in
J\star u\chi
with
u\in\mathcal{U}
.
An application of
[
1
, Theorem 4.26]

concludes the proof.
∎

Definition 2.2

(approximate identity)
.

An approximate identity (or a convolution kernel) is
a family
\left\{J_{\delta}\right\}_{\delta>0}
of

L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
functions
J_{\delta}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\text{(i) $J_{\delta}$ is nonnegative and
$\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{\delta}(x)\,dx=1$, for each $\delta>0$},

(ii) for each
h>0
,
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{\left|x\right|\geq h}J_{\delta}(x)\,dx=0
.

Remark 2.3
.

One simple (compactly supported)
example is the standard (Friedrichs) mollifier.
More generally, given a positive function
J
with
\int J=1
,
the rescaled family
J_{\delta}(x)=\frac{1}{\delta^{d}}J(x/\delta)

supplies an approximate identify.

Fix an approximate identity
\left\{J_{\delta}\right\}_{\delta>0}
.
Consider a sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}
for which

\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{1}(\chi dx)}\lesssim 1
and

(2.2)

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\|u_{n}-J_{\delta}\star u_{n}\right\|_{L^{1}(\chi dx)}=0.

Then
\left\{u_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}
is precompact in
L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Indeed, as
\left\{u_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded in
L^{1}(\chi dx)
,
Lemma
2.1
supplies the
precompactnes in
L^{1}(D)
of the sequence
\left\{J_{\delta}\star u_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}
,
for each fixed
\delta>0
, and for any
D\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Therefore, it is totally bounded in
L^{1}(D)
. In view
of (
2.2
) and since
\chi>0
on
D
,
the sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}
is
also totally bounded—and thus precompact—in
L^{1}(D)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\left\{J_{\delta}\right\}_{\delta>0}
be an approximate
identity, and consider a sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

of functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Suppose
\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{1}(\chi dx)}\lesssim 1
and,
for any
\delta>0
,

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{\delta}(z)\left|u_{n}(x+z)-u_{n}(x-z)\right|\chi(x)\,dz\,dx\leq\rho(\delta),

where
\rho:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is an
increasing function that is continuous at
0
with
\rho(0)=0

(
\rho
is independent of
n
). Then
\left\{u\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
precompact in
L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u_{n}(x)-J_{\delta}\star u_{n}(x)\right|\chi(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{\delta}(x-y)\left|u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)\right|\chi(x)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\quad=2^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{\frac{\delta}{2}}(z)\left|u_{n}(\tilde{x}+z)-u_{n}(\tilde{x}-z)\right|\chi(\tilde{x}+z)\,dz\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{x}

\displaystyle\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:weight-property}}{\lesssim}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{\frac{\delta}{2}}(z)\left|u_{n}(\tilde{x}+z)-u_{n}(\tilde{x}-z)\right|\chi(\tilde{x})\,dz\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{x}\overset{\eqref{eq:compact-cond-kernel-space}}{\leq}\rho\bigl(\delta/2\bigr)\leq\rho(\delta)\xrightarrow{\delta\downarrow 0}0,

uniformly in
n
, the compactness
condition (
2.2
) follows.
∎

In what follows, we return to functions

u_{n}=u_{n}(\omega,t,x):\Omega\times(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

n\in\mathbb{N}
, depending also on the probability (
\omega
)
and temporal (
t
) variables. The estimate
(
2.3
), for
suitable choices of the “modulus of continuity”
\rho(\cdot)

and the approximate identity
\left\{J_{\delta}\right\}_{\delta>0}
,
can be turned into a fractional
BV
estimate like
(
1.6
). This fact
is related to known links between Sobolev, Besov, and Nikolskii
fractional spaces (see, for example,
[
13
]
).
More generally, we have

Proposition 2.5

(quantitative compactness
estimate in space)
.

Fix a weight
\chi\in\mathcal{W}
and a standard Friedrichs
mollifier
\left\{J_{\delta}\right\}_{\delta>0}
.
Consider a sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

of functions satisfying

\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u_{n}(t,x)\right|\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\lesssim 1
and

(2.4)

\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{\delta}(z)\left|u_{n}(t,x+z)-u_{n}(t,x-z)\right|\chi(x)\,dz\,dx\,dt\leq\rho^{x}(\delta),

where
\rho^{x}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is an
increasing function that is continuous at
0
with
\rho^{x}(0)=0

(
\rho^{x}
is independent of
n
but may depend on
\chi
,
T
).

Then
u_{n}
satisfies the quantitative
compactness (spatial translation) estimate

(2.5)

\Ex\sup_{\left|z\right|<\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u_{n}(t,x+z)-u_{n}(t,x)\right|\,\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\leq C\rho^{x}(\delta),\quad\delta>0,

where the constant
C=C(\chi,T)
is independent of
n
.

Proof.

For
\left|z\right|>0
and
\delta>0
,

(2.6)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u_{n}(t,x+z)-u_{n}(t,x)\right|\,\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|J_{\delta}\star u_{n}(t,x+z)-J_{\delta}\star u_{n}(t,x)\right|\,\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad\qquad+2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|J_{\delta}\star u_{n}(t,x)-u_{n}(t,x)\right|\,\chi(x)\,dx\,dt=:A(z)+B,\end{split}

where, by assumption,

\displaystyle B\leq 2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{\delta}(x-y)\left|u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)\right|\chi(x)\,dx\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle\quad=2^{-d+1}\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{\frac{\delta}{2}}(z)\left|u_{n}(\tilde{x}+z)-u_{n}(\tilde{x}-z)\right|\chi(\tilde{x}+z)\,d\tilde{x}\,dz\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:weight-property}}{\lesssim}\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{\frac{\delta}{2}}(z)\left|u_{n}(\tilde{x}+z)-u_{n}(\tilde{x}-z)\right|\chi(\tilde{x})\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{x}\,dz\,dt\overset{\eqref{eq:fracBVx-ass1}}{\lesssim}\rho^{x}(\delta/2),

so
B\lesssim\rho^{x}(\delta)
. For any
\delta>0
, we
introduce the random variable

\sigma_{u}(\delta):=\sup_{\left|z\right|<\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u_{n}(t,x+z)-u_{n}(t,x)\right|\,\chi(x)\,dx\,dt,

which is a sub-additive/increasing
modulus of continuity (for each fixed
\omega\in\Omega
).
We obtain from (
2.6
) that

(2.7)

\Ex\sigma_{u}(\delta)\lesssim\Ex\sup_{\left|z\right|<\delta}A(z)+\rho^{x}(\delta).

Note that

\displaystyle J_{\delta}\star u_{n}(t,x+z)-J_{\delta}\star u_{n}(t,x)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\kappa_{z,\delta}(y)u_{n}(t,x-y)\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\kappa_{z,\delta}(y)\bigl(u_{n}(t,x-y)-u_{n}(t,x)\bigr)\,dy,

with
\kappa_{z,\delta}(y):=J_{\delta}(y+z)-J_{\delta}(y)
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\kappa_{z,\delta}(y)\,dy=0

and
\operatorname{supp}\,(\kappa_{z,\delta})\subset B(0,\left|z\right|+\delta)
.
For
\left|z\right|\leq r\delta
,
with
r\in(0,1)
to be fixed later,

\displaystyle A(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\kappa_{z,\delta}(y)\right|\left|u_{n}(t,x)-u_{n}(t,x-y)\right|\,dy\chi(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\kappa_{z,\delta}(y)\right|\,dy\,\sigma_{u}\bigl(\left|z\right|+\delta\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left|z\right|\left\|\nabla J_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left|B(0,\left|z\right|+\delta)\right|\sigma_{u}\bigl(\left|z\right|+\delta\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\frac{\left|z\right|\left(\left|z\right|+\delta\right)^{d}}{\delta^{d+1}}\sigma_{u}(\left|z\right|+\delta)\leq C_{3}r\sigma_{u}(r\delta+\delta)\leq C_{4}r\sigma_{u}(r\delta),

using also the sub-additivity of
\sigma_{u}(\cdot)
.

Consequently, going back
to (
2.7
),

\Ex\sigma_{u}(r\delta)\leq C_{5}r\Ex\sigma_{u}(r\delta)+C_{6}\rho^{x}(r\delta)
. Fixing
r\in(0,1)
such
that
C_{5}r=\frac{1}{2}
, we arrive at

\Ex\sigma_{u}(r\delta)\leq\frac{1}{2}\Ex\sigma_{u}(r\delta)+C_{6}\rho^{x}(\delta)
and thus
\Ex\sigma_{u}(\delta)\leq C_{7}\rho^{x}(\delta)
.
This concludes the proof of (
2.5
).
∎

One can use the spatial estimate
(
2.4
) to derive a
quantitative compactness estimate in time.
To do that we need a version of a celebrated
interpolation result due to Kružkov
[
11
]
,
which trades spatial regularity, here quantified in terms
of (
2.4
), for temporal

L^{1}(\chi dx)
continuity.

Proposition 2.6

(quantitative compactness estimate in time)
.

Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
and a weight
\chi\in\mathcal{W}\cap W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

such that
\left|D^{\alpha}\chi(x)\right|\lesssim\chi(x)
for
any multi-index
\alpha
with
\left|\alpha\right|\leq m
.
Let
\left\{J_{\delta}\right\}_{\delta>0}
be
an approximate identity such that the support of

J_{\delta}
is bounded independently of
\delta>0
and

\left\|D^{\alpha}J_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\delta^{-\left|\alpha\right|}
, for

\left|\alpha\right|\leq m
, which includes, e.g.,
a standard Friedrichs mollifier.
Consider a sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
functions on
\Omega\times(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
with
T>0
fixed, satisfying

(2.8)

\displaystyle\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u_{n}(t,x)\right|\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\lesssim 1,

(2.9)

\displaystyle du_{n}=\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq m_{F}}D^{\alpha}F_{n}^{(\alpha)}\,dt+\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq m_{G}}D^{\alpha}G_{n}^{(\alpha)}\,dW\quad\text{in $\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$, a.s.},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|F_{n}^{(\alpha)}(t,x)\right|\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\lesssim 1,\quad\forall\left|\alpha\right|\leq m_{F},\,\,m_{F}\leq m.

(2.11)

\displaystyle\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|G_{n}^{(\alpha)}(t,x)\right|^{2}\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\lesssim 1,\quad\forall\left|\alpha\right|\leq m_{G},\,\,m_{G}\leq m.

Suppose the spatial compactness
condition (
2.4
) holds.
Then, for any
\delta\in(0,T)
,

(2.12)

\Ex\sup_{\tau\in(0,\delta)}\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u_{n}(t+\tau,x)-u_{n}(t,x)\right|\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\leq\rho^{t}(\delta),

where
\rho^{t}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is an
increasing function that is continuous at
0

with
\rho^{t}(0)=0
(
\rho^{t}
is independent of
n
but may
depend on
T,\chi
), see also (
2.18
).

Proof.

Recall that
u_{n}
satisfies the
L^{1}
bound (
2.8
).
For
\nu>0
, set

(2.13)

v_{n,\nu}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{2^{d}}J_{\nu}\left(\frac{x-y}{2}\right)\operatorname{sign}\bigl(d_{n}(t,y)\bigr)\,dy,

where
d_{n}(t,x):=u_{n}(t+\tau,x)-u_{n}(t,x)
, for

\tau\in(0,\delta)
,
\delta\in(0,T)
.
We have

(2.14)

\left\|D^{\alpha}v_{n,\nu}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim 1/\nu^{\left|\alpha\right|},\quad\left|\alpha\right|\leq m,

where the right-hand side is independent of
n,t
.
In other words, we have a.s. that

v_{n,\nu}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

Note that, by
\bigl|\left|a\right|-a\operatorname{sgn}(b)\bigr|\leq 2\left|a-b\right|

\forall a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\bigl|\left|d_{n}(t,x)\right|-d_{n}(t,x)v_{n,\nu}(t,x)\bigr|\leq\frac{1}{2^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{\nu}\left(\frac{x-y}{2}\right)\left|d_{n}(t,x)-d_{n}(t,y)\right|\,dy,

As a result,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigl|\left|d_{n}(t,x)\right|-d_{n}(t,x)v_{n,\nu}(t,x)\bigr|\chi(x)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2^{d-1}}\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{\nu}\left(\frac{x-y}{2}\right)\left|d_{n}(t,x)-d_{n}(t,y)\right|\chi(x)\,dx\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle\quad=2\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{\nu}(z)\left|d_{n}(t,\tilde{x}+z)-d_{n}(t,\tilde{x}-z)\right|\chi(\tilde{x}+z)\,d\tilde{x}\,dz\,dt.

Utilising (
1.3
)
(and the bounded support of
J_{\nu}
),

J_{\nu}(z)\chi(\tilde{x}+z)\lesssim J_{\nu}(z)\chi(\tilde{x})
.
Hence

(2.15)

\Ex\int_{0}^{T-\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigl|\left|d_{n}(t,x)\right|-d_{n}(t,x)v_{n,\nu}(t,x)\bigr|\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\overset{\eqref{eq:fracBVx-ass1}}{\lesssim}\rho^{x}(\nu).

The weak form of the SPDE (
2.9
) implies

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d_{n}(t,x)v(t,x)\,\chi(x)\,dx\right|\leq\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq m_{F}}\left|\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F_{n}^{(\alpha)}(s,x)\cdot D^{\alpha}\bigl(v(t,x)\chi(x)\bigr)\,dx\,ds\right|

(2.16)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq m_{G}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{t}^{t+\tau}G_{n}^{(\alpha)}(s,x)\,dW(s)\right)D^{\alpha}\bigl(v(t,x)\,\chi(x)\bigr)\,dx\right|,

for all
v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
Combining (
2.16
)
and (
2.15
) yields

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle:=\Ex\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\sup_{\tau\in(0,\delta)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|d_{n}(t,x)\right|\,\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\leq C_{1}\rho^{x}(\nu)+A(\delta,\nu)+B(\delta,\nu),

where

\displaystyle A(\delta,\nu)=\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq m_{F}}\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\Ex\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|F_{n}^{(\alpha)}(s,x)\right|\left|D^{\alpha}\bigl(v_{n,\nu}(t,x)\chi(x)\bigr)\right|\,dx\,ds\,dt,

\displaystyle B(\delta,\nu)=\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq m_{G}}\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\Ex\sup_{\tau\in(0,\delta)}\left|M^{(\alpha)}(\tau;t)\right|\,dt,

\displaystyle M^{(\alpha)}(\tau;t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{t}^{t+\tau}G_{n}^{(\alpha)}(s,x)\,dW(s)\right)D^{\alpha}\bigl(v_{n,\nu}(t,x)\,\chi(x)\bigr)\,dx,

and
v_{n,\nu}
is defined in (
2.13
).
By assumption, the weight function
\chi
belongs to
W^{m,\infty}

and satisfies
\left|D^{\alpha}\chi(x)\right|\lesssim\chi(x)
for

\left|\alpha\right|\leq m
. Thus, by (
2.14
),

(2.17)

\left|D^{\alpha}\bigl(v_{n,\nu}(t,x)\chi(x)\bigr)\right|\lesssim\frac{\chi(x)}{\nu^{\left|\alpha\right|}},

and so

\displaystyle I\leq C_{1}\rho^{x}(\nu)+\frac{\delta}{\nu^{m_{F}}}\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq m_{F}}\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|F_{n}^{(\alpha)}(t,x)\right|\chi(x)\,dx\,dt+B(\delta,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:Fan-bound}}{\leq}C_{1}\rho^{x}(\nu)+C_{2}\frac{\delta}{\nu^{m_{F}}}+B(\delta,\nu),

for some
(n,\delta,\nu)
-independent constants
C_{1},C_{2}
.

Next, using first (
2.17
) and
then the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, it follows that

\left|M^{(\alpha)}(\tau;t)\right|\lesssim_{\chi}\frac{1}{\nu^{m_{G}}}\left\|\int_{t}^{t+\tau}G_{n}^{(\alpha)}(s)\,dW(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(\chi dx)}
.
By the (Hilbert-space valued) BDG
inequality
[
4
, page 174]
,
and again the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle B(\delta,\nu)\lesssim_{\chi,T}\frac{1}{\nu^{m_{G}}}\!\!\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq m_{G}}\!\!\left(\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\int_{t}^{t+\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|G_{n}^{(\alpha)}(s,x)\right|^{2}\,\chi(x)\,dx\,ds\,dt\right)^{1/2}\overset{\eqref{eq:Gan-bound}}{\lesssim}\frac{\delta^{1/2}}{\nu^{m_{G}}},

so that
I\leq C_{1}\rho^{x}(\nu)+C_{2}\frac{\delta}{\nu^{m_{F}}}+C_{3}\frac{\delta^{1/2}}{\nu^{m_{G}}}
.
The claim (
2.12
) follows by setting

(2.18)

\rho^{t}(\delta)=\inf\limits_{\nu>0}\left(C_{1}\rho^{x}(\nu)+C_{2}\frac{\delta}{\nu^{m_{F}}}+C_{3}\frac{\delta^{1/2}}{\nu^{m_{G}}}\right).

∎

3.
Quantitative compensated compactness

Consider the viscosity approximation
u_{n}
of (
1.1
)
with
d=1
,
R\equiv 0
, and
f=f(u)
.
According to
[
6
]
, see also

[
9
]
and
[
7
, Remark 5.9]
,
there exists a unique solution
u_{\varepsilon}
—continuous in
t
and
smooth in
x
—of the SPDEs

(3.1)

\displaystyle du_{\varepsilon}+\partial_{x}f(u_{\varepsilon})\,dt=\varepsilon\partial_{xx}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\,dt+\sigma(x,u_{\varepsilon})\,dW,

(3.2)

\displaystyle d\eta(u_{\varepsilon})+\partial_{x}q(u_{\varepsilon})\,dt=\varepsilon\partial_{xx}^{2}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\,dt-\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\eta^{\prime\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\sigma^{2}(x,u_{\varepsilon})\,dt+\eta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\sigma(x,u_{\varepsilon})\,dW,

where

\mu_{\varepsilon}:=\eta^{\prime\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\varepsilon\left|\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}

Moreover,
\forall p,r\in[2,\infty)
and
for any weight

(3.3)

\begin{split}&\Ex\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}^{r}\leq C_{\chi},\quad\Ex\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varepsilon\left|\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\right|^{r}\leq C_{\chi},\\
&\quad\text{and}\quad\Ex\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi\,d\left|\mu_{\varepsilon}\right|\right|^{r}\leq C_{\chi}.\end{split}

The entropy balance (
3.2
) follows
from the viscous SPDE (
3.1
) and
the spatial and temporal (Itô) chain rules.

The flux
f
and entropy
\eta

are assumed to satisfy the nonlinearity assumptions of
the next lemma, which is a global version of

[
8
, Lemma 5.2]
that
will be utilised later. We refer to
[
8
]

for the proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
f,\eta
are

functions
such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(v)-f^{\prime}(w)\geq C_{f}\left(v-w\right)^{p_{f}},\quad-\infty\leq w<v\leq\infty,\,\,p_{f}\geq 1,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}(v)-\eta^{\prime}(w)\geq C_{\eta}\left(v-w\right)^{p_{\eta}},\quad-\infty\leq w<v\leq\infty,\,\,p_{\eta}\geq 1,

for some constants
C_{f},C_{\eta}>0
.
Then, for all
v,w\in\mathbb{R}
,

(w-v)\bigl(q(w)-q(v)\bigr)-\bigl(\eta(w)-\eta(v)\bigr)\bigl(f(w)-f(v)\bigr)\geq C_{f,\eta}\left|w-v\right|^{p_{f}+p_{\eta}+2},

where
C_{f,\eta}=\frac{C_{f}C_{\eta}}{\left(1+p_{f}+p_{\eta}\right)\left(2+p_{f}+p_{\eta}\right)}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Suppose
f\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is such that

\left|f^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\right|\geq c>0
for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
.
This is a “quantitive” version of the classical
nonlinearity condition
f^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\neq 0

for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}

[
12
,
14
]
.
Then, choosing
\eta=f
as an entropy flux, it
follows that
(w-v)\bigl(q(w)-q(v)\bigr)-\bigl(f(w)-f(v)\bigr)^{2}\geq\frac{c^{2}}{8}\left|w-v\right|^{4}

Remark 3.3
.

Compared to
[
8
, Lemma 5.2]
,
the assumptions in Lemma

are global, since we do not know that

is
bounded in

In what follows, we always assume

are at most polynominally

\left|\eta(u)\right|\lesssim 1+\left|u\right|^{p_{0}},\quad\left|\eta^{\prime}(u)\right|\lesssim 1+\left|u\right|^{p_{0}-1},\quad\left|\eta^{\prime\prime}(u)\right|\lesssim 1+\left|u\right|^{p_{0}-2}.

To ensure the validity of the a priori
estimates in (

),
we assume that

\left|\sigma(x,u)\right|\lesssim 1+\left|u\right|.

The main result is the following spatial

Theorem 3.4

(quantitative compactness estimate,
viscosity approximation)
.

Fix a weight
\chi\in\mathcal{W}
. Let
u_{n}
be a classical
solution to the viscous SPDEs
(
3.1
), (
3.2
) with

\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Suppose
(
3.4
), (
3.5
),
(
3.6
), and (
3.7
) hold.
Set
\mu:=1-\frac{1}{p}
,

.
Then, for any

with

,

(3.8)

\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{n}(t,x+z)-u_{n}(t,x)\right|^{p_{f}+p_{\eta}+2}\,\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\lesssim_{\chi,p}\left|z\right|^{\mu}.

Remark 3.5
.

By Hölder’s inequality, it follows
from (
3.8
) that

(3.9)

\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{n}(t,x+z)-u_{n}(t,x)\right|\,\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\leq C\left|z\right|^{\mu_{x}},

where
\mu_{x}:=\frac{\mu}{p_{f}+p_{\eta}+2}
and
C=C(T,\chi,p)
.
Note carefully that this estimate appears to
be slightly weaker than the spatial compactness
estimate (
1.6
) as there is
a   missing
\sup_{\left|z\right|<\delta}
inside the expectation operator.
It is not immediately clear how to recover this supremum;
the available maximal (martingale) inequalities apply
only to the temporal variable
t
and not an arbitrary parameter
z
.
However, we can use Proposition
2.5

to recover the supremum by considering a standard

\left\{J_{\delta}\right\}_{\delta>0}
.
Observe that (
3.9
) implies

(3.10)

\begin{split}&\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}J_{\delta}(z)\left|u_{n}(t,x+z)-u_{n}(t,x-z)\right|\,\chi(x)\,dz\,dx\,dt\\
&\qquad\leq 2C\int_{\mathbb{R}}J_{\delta}(z)\left|z\right|^{\mu_{x}}\,dz\leq 2C\delta^{\mu_{x}}.\end{split}

Given (
3.9
),
Proposition
2.5
—via the estimate
(
3.10
)—supplies
the spatial estimate (
1.6
)
with
\sup_{\left|z\right|<\delta}
inside the
expectation operator. Besides, by Proposition
2.6
,
(
3.10
) also implies
the temporal compactness estimate (
1.7
).

It is worth noting that these spatial and
temporal estimates do not require the measure
\mu_{\varepsilon}
in
the entropy balance equation (

)
to be positive. This means that they can be
used to analyze stochastic conservation laws
with discontinuous (
BV
) flux, which
will be discussed further in future work.

Remark 3.6
.

Under the assumption of

3.1.
Stochastic interaction lemma

Consider the two Itô SPDEs

(3.11)

\begin{split}&dA+\partial_{x}B\,dt=C_{A}\,dt+\sigma_{A}\,dW,\\
&dD+\partial_{x}E\,dt=C_{D}\,dt+\sigma_{D}\,dW,\end{split}

which hold weakly in
x
, almost surely.
Here
A=A(\omega,t,x),D=D(\omega,t,x)

belong to
C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
a.s., satisfy

\Ex\left\|\bigl(A,D\bigr)(t)\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}<\infty

\forall t\in[0,T]
, and
A,D\to 0
as
\left|x\right|\to 0
, for
each fixed
(\omega,t)
. Besides,
\bigl(B,C_{A},E,C_{D}\bigr)=\bigl(B,C_{A},E,C_{D}\bigr)(\omega,t,x)

satisfy
\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\bigl(B,C_{A},E,C_{D}\bigr)(t)\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,dx\,dt<\infty
. The noise amplitudes
\bigl(\sigma_{A},\sigma_{D}\bigr)=\bigl(\sigma_{A},\sigma_{D}\bigr)(\omega,t,x)
satisfy
\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\bigl(\sigma_{A}(t),\sigma_{D}(t)\bigr)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\,dt<\infty

for
p=1,2
.

The processes
A,D,\sigma_{A},\sigma_{D}
and
W
live
on the stochastic basis introduced in Section
1
,
and they are assumed to be appropriately measurable with
respect to the filtration, so all
relevant stochastic integrals are well-defined
in the sense of Itô.

In what follows, we need the spatial
anti-derivatives of
A
and
D
,

\mathcal{A}(t,y):=\int_{-\infty}^{y}A(t,y)\,dx,\quad\mathcal{D}(t,x):=\int_{x}^{\infty}D(t,y)\,dy,

as well the spatial anti-derivatives
of
\sigma_{A}
and
\sigma_{D}
,

\Sigma_{A}(t,y):=\int_{-\infty}^{y}\sigma_{A}(t,x)\,dx,\quad\Sigma_{D}(t,x):=\int_{x}^{\infty}\sigma_{D}(t,y)\,dy.

The following lemma is a stochastic adaptation of
a result from Golse and Perthame
[
8
, Section 2]
.

Lemma 3.7

(stochastic interaction identity)
.

For
t\in[0,T]
, define

I(t)=\iint\limits_{x<y}A(t,x)D(t,y)\,dx\,dy.

Then the following identity holds a.s.:

(3.12)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(AE-DB\bigr)(t,x)\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}C_{A}(t,x)\mathcal{D}(t,x)\,dx\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}(t,y)C_{D}(t,y)\,dy\,dt\\
&\quad\qquad-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sigma_{A}(t,x)\mathcal{D}(t,x)\,dx\,dW-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}(t,y)\sigma_{D}(t,y)\,dy\,dW\\
&\quad\qquad-\int_{0}^{T}I_{\sigma}(t)\,dt+I(T)-I(0),\end{split}

where

I_{\sigma}(t):=\iint\limits_{x<y}\sigma_{A}(t,x)\sigma_{D}(t,y)\,dx\,dy

satisfies

(3.13)

I_{\sigma}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sigma_{A}(t,x)\Sigma_{D}(t,x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Sigma_{A}(t,y)\sigma_{D}(t,y)\,dy.

Proof.

Modulo an approximation argument involving spatial
mollification, we may assume that the SPDE system
(
3.11
) holds pointwise in
x
. Thus,
using the real-valued Itô product formula, we
compute as follows:

\displaystyle dI(t)=\iint\limits_{x<y}\Bigl(-\partial_{x}B(t,x)\,dt+C_{A}(t,x)\,dt+\sigma_{a}(t,x)\,dW\Bigr)D(t,y)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\iint\limits_{x<y}A(t,x)\Bigl(-\partial_{y}E(t,y)\,dt+C_{D}(t,y)\,dt+\sigma_{D}(t,y)\,dW\Bigr)\,dx\,dy+I_{\sigma}(t)

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\,\,\,\iint\limits_{x<y}C_{A}(t,x)D(t,y)\,dx\,dy\right)\,dt+\left(\,\,\,\iint\limits_{x<y}\sigma_{A}(t,x)D(t,y)\,dx\,dy\right)\,dW

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left(\,\,\,\iint\limits_{x<y}A(t,x)C_{D}(t,y)\,dx\,dy\right)\,dt+\left(\,\,\,\iint\limits_{x<y}A(t,x)\sigma_{D}(t,y)\,dx\,dy\,dt\right)\,dW

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left(\,\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}-B(t,y)D(t,y)\,dy\right)\,dt+\left(\,\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}A(t,x)E(t,x)\,dx\right)\,dt+I_{\sigma}(t),

so that

\displaystyle dI(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(AE-DB\bigr)(t,x)\,dx\right)\,dt+I_{\sigma}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\,\,\,\iint\limits_{x<y}C_{A}(t,x)D(t,y)\,dx\,dy\right)\,dt+\left(\,\,\,\iint\limits_{x<y}A(t,x)C_{D}(t,y)\,dx\,dy\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\,\,\,\iint\limits_{x<y}\sigma_{A}(t,x)D(t,y)\,dx\,dy\right)\,dW+\left(\,\,\,\iint\limits_{x<y}A(t,x)\sigma_{D}(t,y)\,dx\,dy\right)\,dW.

Observe that

\iint\limits_{x<y}C_{A}(t,x)D(t,y)\,dx\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}C_{A}(t,x)\mathcal{D}(t,x)\,dx

and

\iint\limits_{x<y}A(t,x)C_{D}(t,y)\,dx\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}(t,y)C_{D}(t,y)\,dy.

Similarly, we have

\left(\,\,\,\iint\limits_{x<y}\sigma_{A}(t,x)D(t,y)\,dx\,dy\right)\,dW=\left(\,\int_{R}\sigma_{A}(t,x)\mathcal{D}(t,x)\,dx\right)\,dW,

and

\left(\,\,\,\iint\limits_{x<y}A(t,x)\sigma_{D}(t,y)\,dx\,dy\right)\,dW=\left(\,\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}(t,y)\sigma_{D}(t,y)\,dy\right)\,dW,

and
I_{\sigma}(t)
satisfies (
3.13
).
Finally, upon integrating in time,
we obtain (
3.12
).
∎

Proof of Theorem

) gives

\displaystyle d\bigl(\chi\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}\bigr)+\partial_{x}\bigl(\chi\Delta_{h}f(u_{\varepsilon})\bigr)\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\partial_{x}\chi\Delta_{h}f(u_{\varepsilon})\,dt+\varepsilon\chi\partial_{xx}^{2}\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}\,dt+\chi\Delta_{h}\sigma(x,u)\,dW,

\displaystyle d\bigl(\chi\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\bigr)+\partial_{x}\bigl(\chi\Delta_{h}q(u_{\varepsilon})\bigr)\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\partial_{x}\chi\Delta_{h}q(u_{\varepsilon})\,dt+\varepsilon\chi\partial_{xx}^{2}\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\,dt-\chi\Delta_{h}\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\chi\Delta_{h}\left(\eta^{\prime\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\sigma^{2}(x,u_{\varepsilon})\right)\,dt+\chi\Delta_{h}\bigl(\eta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\sigma(x,u_{\varepsilon})\bigr)\,dW.

Setting

\displaystyle A=\chi\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon},\quad B=\chi\Delta_{h}f(u_{\varepsilon}),\quad

\displaystyle C_{A}=\partial_{x}\chi\Delta_{h}f(u_{\varepsilon})+\chi\varepsilon\partial_{xx}^{2}\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon},\quad\sigma_{A}=\chi\Delta_{h}\sigma(x,u_{\varepsilon})

and

\displaystyle D=\chi\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon}),\quad E=\chi\Delta_{h}q(u_{\varepsilon}),\quad C_{D}=C_{D,0}+\chi\bigl(C_{D,1}+C_{D,2}+C_{D,3}\bigr),

\displaystyle C_{D,0}=\partial_{x}\chi\Delta_{h}q(u_{\varepsilon}),\quad C_{D,1}=\varepsilon\partial_{xx}^{2}\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon}),\quad C_{D,2}=-\Delta_{h}\mu_{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle C_{D,3}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{h}\left(\eta^{\prime\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\sigma^{2}(x,u_{\varepsilon})\right),\quad\sigma_{D}=\chi\Delta_{h}\bigl(\eta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\sigma(x,u_{\varepsilon})\bigr),

the SPDE system (
3.11
) holds.

If
\chi
is a weight function, then it follows that
both
\sqrt{\chi}
and
\chi^{2}
are also weight functions.
In other words,
\chi\in\mathcal{W}\Longrightarrow\sqrt{\chi},\chi^{2}\in\mathcal{W}
.

Given the stochastic interaction
estimate (
3.12
)
and Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

(3.14)

\begin{split}&\Ex\iint\left|\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\right|^{p_{f}+p_{\eta}+2}\,\chi^{2}(x)\,dx\,dt\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{8}J_{i},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle J_{1}=-\Ex\iint\partial_{x}\chi\Delta_{h}f(u_{\varepsilon})\left(\int_{x}^{\infty}\chi\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\,dy\right)\,dx\,dt,

\displaystyle J_{2}=-\Ex\iint\chi\varepsilon\partial_{xx}^{2}\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{x}^{\infty}\chi\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\,dy\right)\,dx\,dt,

\displaystyle J_{3}=-\Ex\iint\bigl(\partial_{x}\chi\Delta_{h}q(u_{\varepsilon})\bigr(t,y)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{y}\chi\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)\,dy\,dt,

\displaystyle J_{4}=-\Ex\iint\bigl(\chi\varepsilon\partial_{xx}^{2}\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\bigr)(t,y)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{y}\chi\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)\,dy\,dt,

\displaystyle J_{5}=\Ex\iint\bigl(\chi\Delta_{h}\mu_{\varepsilon}\bigr)(t,y)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{y}\chi\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)\,dy\,dt,

\displaystyle J_{6}=-\frac{1}{2}\Ex\iint\bigl(\chi\Delta_{h}\left(\eta^{\prime\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\sigma^{2}(y,u_{\varepsilon})\right)\bigr)(t,y)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{y}\chi\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)\,dy\,dt,

and

\displaystyle J_{7}

\displaystyle=-\Ex\int_{0}^{T}\Biggl(\,\,\,\iint\limits_{x<y}\chi(t,x)\Delta_{h}\sigma(x,u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\times\chi(t,y)\Delta_{h}\bigl(\eta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon}(t,y))\sigma(y,u_{\varepsilon}(t,y))\bigr)\,dx\,dy\Biggr)\,dt.

Finally,

J_{8}=\Ex\iint\limits_{x<y}\bigl(\chi\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}\bigr)(t,x)\bigl(\chi\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\bigr)(t,y)\,dx\,dy\Big|_{t=0}^{t=T}
.

Estimates of
J_{1}
and
J_{3}
.

First,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\int_{x}^{\infty}\chi(y)\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon}(t,y))\,dy=\int_{x}^{\infty}\Delta_{h}\bigl(\chi(y))\eta(u_{\varepsilon}(t,y))\bigr)\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{x}^{\infty}\Delta_{h}\chi(y)\eta(u_{\varepsilon}(t,y+h))\,dy=:\mathcal{J}_{1,1}+\mathcal{J}_{1,2},

where

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{J}_{1,1}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{x}^{x+\left|h\right|}\chi(y)\eta(u_{\varepsilon}(t,y))\,dy\right|\leq\left|h\right|^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left\|\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}

and, using
\left|\partial_{x}\chi(x)\right|\lesssim\chi(x)

and (
1.3
),

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{J}_{1,2}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{x}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{h}\partial_{y^{\prime}}\chi(y+y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}\eta(u_{\varepsilon}(t,y+h))\,dy\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|\left\|\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\chi dx))}.

As a result,

(3.15)

\begin{split}\left|\mathcal{J}_{1}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))},\end{split}

and thus, by Young’s product inequality,

\displaystyle\left|J_{1}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\Biggl(\Ex\left|\iint\left|\Delta_{h}f(u_{\varepsilon})\right|\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\right|^{2}+\Ex\left\|\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}^{2}\Biggr).

Given (
3.3
)
and (
3.6
), it follows that

\left|J_{1}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
,

p\in[2,\infty)
.

Similarly,

\left|J_{3}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
,

p\in[2,\infty)
.

Estimate of
J_{2},J_{4}
.

Integration by parts gives

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\Ex\iint\varepsilon\partial_{x}\chi\Delta_{h}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{x}^{\infty}\chi\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\,dy\right)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad-\Ex\iint\varepsilon\Delta_{h}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\chi^{2}\,dx\,dt=:J_{2,1}+J_{2,2}.

By (
3.15
),

\left|\partial_{x}\chi(x)\right|\lesssim\chi(x)
,
and the Cauchy–Schwarz/Young product inequalities,

\displaystyle\left|J_{2,1}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\chi}\Ex\Biggl[\left(\iint\varepsilon\left|\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}\right)\Biggr]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\Biggl(\Ex\iint\varepsilon\left|\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\chi(x)\,dx\,dt+\Ex\left\|\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}^{2}\Biggr).

Making use of (
3.3
)
and (
3.6
),

\left|J_{2,1}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
,

p\in[2,\infty)
.

Regarding
J_{2,2}
, let us first estimate

\chi\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})
:

\displaystyle\left|\chi(x)\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon}(t,x))\right|=\left|\int_{0}^{\left|h\right|}\partial_{x^{\prime}}\eta(u_{\varepsilon}(t,x+x^{\prime}))\chi(x)\,dx^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\overset{\eqref{eq:weight-property}}{\lesssim}\int_{0}^{\left|h\right|}\left|\eta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon}(t,x+x^{\prime}))\right|\left|\partial_{x^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x+x^{\prime}))\right|\chi(x+x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\left|h\right|}\left|\eta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon}(t,x+x^{\prime}))\right|^{2}\chi(x+x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(\int_{0}^{\left|h\right|}\left|\partial_{x^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x+x^{\prime})\right|^{2}\chi(x+x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{1/2},

where, setting
A:=\left(\int_{0}^{\left|h\right|}\left|\eta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon}(t,x+x^{\prime}))\right|^{2}\chi(x+x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{1/2}
,

\displaystyle A\lesssim_{\chi}\left|h\right|^{\frac{p-1}{2p}}\left(\int_{0}^{\left|h\right|}\left|\eta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon}(t,x+x^{\prime}))\right|^{2p}\chi(x+x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{1/(2p)}

\displaystyle\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:eta-pol}}{\lesssim}\left|h\right|^{\frac{p-1}{2p}}\left(1+\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{q_{x}}(\chi dx))}^{q_{t}}\right),

for some finite
q_{t},q_{x}
, depending on
2p

and
p_{0}-1
, cf. (
3.6
). Therefore,

(3.16)

\begin{split}&\left|J_{2,2}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p}}\Ex\Biggl[\left(1+\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{q_{x}}(\chi dx))}^{q_{t}}\right)\\
&\qquad\times\underbrace{\iint\varepsilon\Delta_{h}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{0}^{\left|h\right|}\left|\partial_{x^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x+x^{\prime})\right|^{2}\chi(x+x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{1/2}\chi\,dx\,dt}_{=:B}\Biggr],\end{split}

where, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle B\lesssim\left\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{h}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\chi dx))}\left(\int_{0}^{\left|h\right|}\!\!\!\iint\varepsilon\left|\partial_{x^{\prime}}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x+x^{\prime})\right|^{2}\chi(x)\,dx\,dt\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sqrt{\left|h\right|}\left\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{h}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\chi dx))}\left\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\chi dx))},

using again (
1.3
) to replace
\chi(x)
by
a constant times
\chi(x+x^{\prime})
.
Hence, for any finite
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
such that

\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{p_{3}}=1
, the
generalised Hölder inequality yields

(3.17)

\begin{split}\left|J_{2,2}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{2p}}\left(\Ex\left|J_{2,1}^{(1)}\right|^{p_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\left(\Ex\left|J_{2,2}^{(1)}\right|^{p_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}}\left(\Ex\left|J_{2,3}^{(1)}\right|^{p_{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{3}}},\end{split}

where
J_{2,2}^{(1)}=\left(1+\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{q_{x}}(\chi dx))}^{q_{t}}\right)
,

J_{2,2}^{(2)}=\left\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{h}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\chi dx))}
,
and
J_{2,2}^{(3)}=\left\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\chi dx))}
.
By (
3.3
), we conclude that

(3.18)

\left|J_{2,2}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{2p}}.

Summarising,

\left|J_{2}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}+\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{2p}}\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
,
p\in[2,\infty)
.

Similarly, integration by parts gives

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle=\Ex\iint\bigl(\varepsilon\partial_{x}\chi\Delta_{h}\partial_{x}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\bigr)(t,y)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{y}\chi\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad-\Ex\iint\varepsilon\Delta_{h}\partial_{x}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}\chi^{2}\,dy\,dt=:J_{4,1}+J_{4,2}.

By slightly modifying the calculation leading up
to (
3.15
),

(3.19)

\left|\int_{-\infty}^{y}\chi(x)\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))},

for any finite
p\geq 2
, and so, by the
Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\left|J_{4,1}\right|\lesssim\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Ex\Biggl[\iint\left|\eta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\right|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\left|\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\right|\chi\,dy\,dt\left(\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}\right)\Biggr]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\Ex\Biggl[\left\|\eta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\chi dx))}\!\left\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\chi dx))}\!\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}\Biggr].

Arguing as in (
3.17
) (via the
generalised Hölder inequality) and
using (
3.3
) and
(
3.6
), we arrive at

\left|J_{4,1}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
.

Regarding
J_{4,2}
, let us first
do one more integration by parts:

\displaystyle J_{4,2}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\Ex\iint\varepsilon\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\Delta_{h}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\chi^{2}\,dy\,dt}_{=:J_{4,2}^{(1)}}+\underbrace{\Ex\iint\varepsilon\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}2\chi\partial_{x}\chi\,dy\,dt}_{=:J_{4,2}^{(2)}}.

Note that
J_{4,2}^{(1)}
is of the same
form as
J_{2,2}
, and by slightly modifying
the calculations leading up to
(
3.16
), (
3.18
), we obtain

\left|J_{4,2}^{(1)}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
.
For
J_{4,2}^{(2)}
, noting that
\left|\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}(t,y)\right|\leq\int_{0}^{\left|h\right|}\left|\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}(t,y+y^{\prime})\right|\,dy^{\prime}

and
\left|\partial_{x}\chi(y)\right|\lesssim\chi(y)
,
we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\left|J_{4,2}^{(2)}\right|\,\,

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:weight-property}}{\lesssim}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Ex\int_{0}^{\left|h\right|}\!\!\!\iint\left|\chi\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\right|\left|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}(t,y+y^{\prime})\chi(y+y^{\prime})\right|\,dy\,dt\,dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\chi}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Ex\int_{0}^{\left|h\right|}\left\|\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\chi dx))}\left\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\chi dx))}\,dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|h\right|\left(\Ex\left\|\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\chi dx))}^{2}+\Ex\left\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\chi dx))}^{2}\right).

Given (
3.3
) and (
3.6
),
we conclude that
\left|J_{4,2}^{(2)}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|

and thus
\left|J_{4,2}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
.

Summarising,

\left|J_{4}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
,
p\in[2,\infty)
.

Estimate of
J_{5}
.

We have

\displaystyle\left|J_{5}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Ex\iint\chi(y)\bigl(\left|\mu_{\varepsilon}\right|(t,y+h)+\left|\mu_{\varepsilon}\right|(t,y)\bigr)\left|\int_{-\infty}^{y}\chi(x)\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx\right|\,dy\,dt.

By (
3.19
)
and (
3.3
), we deduce that,
for any
p\in[2,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\left|J_{5}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\Ex\left[\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}\left|\iint\chi(y)\,d\left|\mu_{\varepsilon}\right|(t,y)\right|\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\Ex\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx)}^{2}+\Ex\left|\iint\chi(y)\,d\left|\mu_{\varepsilon}\right|(t,y)\right|^{2}\right)\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

Estimate of
J_{6}
.

Using (
3.19
),

\displaystyle\left|J_{6}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Ex\iint\left|\chi\Delta_{h}\left(\eta^{\prime\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\sigma^{2}(y,u_{\varepsilon})\right)\right|\left|\int_{-\infty}^{y}\chi\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right|\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\Ex\left[\iint\left|\Delta_{h}\left(\eta^{\prime\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\sigma^{2}(y,u_{\varepsilon})\right)\right|\chi(y)\,dy\,dt\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\Biggl(\Ex\left|\iint\left|\Delta_{h}\left(\eta^{\prime\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\sigma^{2}(y,u_{\varepsilon})\right)\right|\chi(y)\,dy\,dt\right|^{2}+\Ex\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}^{2}\Biggr).

In view of (
3.3
),
(
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we conclude that

\left|J_{6}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
,

p\in[2,\infty)
.

Estimate of
J_{7}
.

To estimate
J_{7}
, let us write

\displaystyle\iint\limits_{x<y}\chi(x)\Delta_{h}\sigma(x,u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)))\chi(y)\Delta_{h}\bigl(\eta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon}(t,y))\sigma(y,u_{\varepsilon}(t,y))\bigr)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{y}\chi(x)\Delta_{h}\sigma(x,u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)))\,dx\right)\chi(y)\Delta_{h}\bigl(\eta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon}(t,y))\sigma(y,u_{\varepsilon}(t,y))\bigr)\,dy.

Using (
3.19
) with
u_{\varepsilon}

replaced by
\sigma(x,u_{\varepsilon})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\infty}^{y}\chi(x)\Delta_{h}\sigma(x,u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)))\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\sigma(\cdot,u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:sigma-lin}}{\lesssim}\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left(1+\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}\right),

which implies that

\displaystyle\left|J_{7}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\Ex\left[\left(1+\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}\right)\iint\chi\Delta_{h}\bigl(\eta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\sigma(y,u_{\varepsilon})\bigr)\,dy\,dt\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\Biggl(\Ex\left|1+\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx)}\right|^{2}+\Ex\left|\iint\chi\Delta_{h}\bigl(\eta^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\sigma(y,u_{\varepsilon})\bigr)\,dy\,dt\right|^{2}\Biggr).

Given (
3.3
),
(
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we obtain

\left|J_{7}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
,
p\in[2,\infty)
.

Estimate of
J_{8}
.
For any fixed
t
,
we write

\displaystyle\iint\limits_{x<y}\bigl(\chi\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}\bigr)(t,x)\bigl(\chi(x)\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\bigr)(t,y)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{y}\chi(x)\Delta_{h}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx\right)\chi(y)\Delta_{h}\eta(u_{\varepsilon}(t,y))\,dy.

By combining this with (
3.19
),
Young’s product inequality, (
3.6
),
and (
3.3
), we
obtain the following bound for
J_{8}
,
for any finite
p\geq 2
:

\displaystyle\left|J_{8}\right|\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\Ex\left[\left\|u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\chi dx))}\left\|\eta(u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\chi dx))}\right]\lesssim\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

Conclusion.
Summarising,
(
3.14
) is bounded by

C(\chi,p)\left|h\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
,
p\in[2,\infty)
,
which, upon replacing
h
by
z
,
\varepsilon
by
\varepsilon_{n}
,
and
u_{\varepsilon}
by
u_{n}
, becomes (
3.8
).

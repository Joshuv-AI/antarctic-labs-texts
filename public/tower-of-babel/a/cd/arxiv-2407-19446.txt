Leave-One-Out Analysis for Nonconvex Robust Matrix Completion with General Thresholding Functions
Tianming Wang, Ke Wei

Abstract
We study the problem of robust matrix completion (RMC), where the partially observed entries of an underlying low-rank matrix is corrupted by sparse noise. Existing analysis of the non-convex methods for this problem either requires the explicit but empirically redundant regularization in the algorithm or requires sample splitting in the analysis. In this paper, we consider a simple yet efficient nonconvex method which alternates between a projected gradient step for the low-rank part and a thresholding step for the sparse noise part. Inspired by leave-one-out analysis for low rank matrix completion, it is established that the method can achieve linear convergence for a general class of thresholding functions, including for example soft-thresholding and SCAD. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first leave-one-out analysis on a nonconvex method for RMC. Additionally, when applying our result to low rank matrix completion, it improves the sampling complexity of existing result for the singular value projection method.



1
Introduction

Low rank matrix completion has received a lot of investigations in the last decade, both from theoretical
and algorithmic aspects. In the pioneering works of Candès, Recht and Gross
[
4
,
15
]
, it is shown that the nuclear norm based convex relaxation can achieve exact matrix completion under nearly optimal sampling complexity.
When the size of the matrix is large, nonconvex methods are more suitable for low rank matrix completion. Many computationally efficient approaches have been developed towards this line of research, including gradient descent
[
30
,
25
]
, iterative hard thresholding
[
17
,
23
]
, Riemannian optimization
[
22
,
26
]
, to name just a few.

The nonconvex methods also merit theoretical recovery guarantees under the notion of incoherence and the standard random sampling assumption. In order to exploit the incoherence property sufficiently, either explicit projection onto the incoherence region
[
30
]
or sample splitting
[
16
,
18
,
28
]
can be utilized in the convergence analysis of the nonconvex methods.
The latter one splits the observed samples into disjoint subsets in order to create independence across iterations. However, it is well recognized that neither of the two techniques is necessary in practice.
This gap has recently been filled based on the leave-one-out analysis technique
[
13
,
12
,
1
,
19
,
31
]
. When applying this technique to analyze nonconvex algorithms, the idea is to build auxiliary sequences that are exceedingly close to each other and also allows the decoupling of the statistical dependency. As a result, the utilization of leave-one-out allows us to show that the iterates always stay in a incoherence region and thus has successfully improved the theoretical guarantees of nonconvex methods for low rank matrix completion. In particular, Ma et al.
[
21
]
establish the linear convergence of the vanilla gradient descent method for low rank matrix completion, while Ding and Chen
[
11
]
establish the linear convergence of the singular value projection (SVP, also known as iterative hard thresholding) algorithm
[
17
,
23
]
.

In this paper, we consider the robust matrix completion (RMC) problem which arises for example in robust PCA from partial samples
[
10
]
. The goal in RMC is to recover the ground truth low-rank matrix
L^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
from partially observed measurements that are corrupted by sparse outliers. More precisely, each observation can be written as

\displaystyle M_{ij}=L^{\star}_{ij}+S^{\star}_{ij},\quad(i,j)\in\Omega,

(1)

where
\Omega\subseteq[n_{1}]\times[n_{2}]
is an index subset with
[n]:=[1,2,\dots,n]
, and
S^{\star}_{ij}
denotes the outlier. There are also different nonconvex methods developed for RMC. In
[
29
]
, a sorting operation is first used to update the outlier estimation, and then a gradient step is taken to update the low-rank part represented via Burer-Monteiro factorization. The analysis therein requires the explicit projection onto the incoherence region which, as in the matrix completion case, is often deemed as an artifact of the proof. In a later work
[
10
]
, an algorithm which combines SVP with hard-thresholding is considered to solve the RMC problem. In every iteration, it performs a projected gradient step for the low-rank matrix, and then uses hard-thresholding for the outlier estimation. However, the theoretical guarantee in
[
10
]
is derived based on the sample splitting trick. An extension of the leave-one-out analysis in
[
11
]
turns out to be quite challenging when there exist sparse outliers.

1.1
Main Contributions

To the best of our knowledge, there is no leave-one-out analysis for nonconvex methods targeting the RMC problem. In a closely related work
[
9
]
, theoretical recovery guarantee is improved for a convex program by building a connection between the optimal solution of the convex program with the iterates of a nonconvex algorithm. The nonconvex algorithm can be viewed as a projection-free version of the method used in
[
29
]
, except that the sorting operation is replaced by the soft-thresholding operation. However, the nonconvex algorithm in
[
9
]
is not a practical one, since it requires initialization at the ground truth and is only used to verify certain optimal conditions. That being said, it motivates us to derive the theoretical guarantee of certain nonconvex methods for RMC based on the leave-one-out analysis technique, thus achieving a projection and sample splitting free result. Overall, the novelty of this paper are summarized as follows.

1.

We consider a nonconvex method which alternates between SVP for the low rank estimation and entrywise thresholding for the sparse outlier estimation. The theoretical recovery guarantee of the nonconvex method has been established based on the leave-one-out analysis technique for a class of thresholding functions, including for example soft-thresholding
and SCAD. It is also worth pointing out that our analysis can handle any sparse enough outliers, without requiring the support and signs of the outliers to be random, as assumed in
[
9
]
. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first leave-one-out analysis for nonconvex methods targeting the RMC problem.

2.

When applying our result to the matrix completion problem, our sample complexity improves substantially upon the one in
[
11
]
over the dependency on
\kappa
,
\mu
and
r
, i.e., from
O(\frac{\kappa^{6}\mu^{4}r^{6}\log n}{n})
to
O(\frac{\kappa^{4}\mu^{3}r^{3}\log n}{n})
.

For the analysis, as inspired by
[
11
]
, we rewrite the update of the algorithm as a perturbation around the ground truth and then establish the contraction of
\|E^{t-1,\infty}\|_{2}
,
\|\Delta^{t,\infty}\|_{2,\infty}
, and
\|D^{t,\infty}\|_{F}
(see Section
3
for the definitions) by induction and the leave-one-out analysis. That being said, we would like to emphasize that the proof details are quite technically involved and new techniques have to be developed to achieve the improved analysis and to handle the outliers.

•

Firstly, to achieve the improved sample complexity, instead of first bounding
\|\Delta^{t,\infty}\|_{2,\infty}
and then bounding
\|D^{t,\infty}\|_{F}
based on
\|\Delta^{t,\infty}\|_{2,\infty}
, we have established two inequalities involving
\|\Delta^{t,\infty}\|_{2,\infty}
and
\|D^{t,\infty}\|_{F}
simultaneously. This allows us to relax the requirement on the distance to the ground truth when performing the analysis. In addition, the extension of the proofs in
[
11
]
for positive-definite matrices to general matrices needs to be done properly, including providing bounds for new terms appeared due to asymmetry.

•

Secondly, while the outlier terms in the initialization can be controlled easily by the sparsity of the outliers, the outlier terms in the induction steps need to be bounded carefully. In particular, our analysis does not require the pattern and the signs of the outliers to be random (as is assumed in
[
9
]
), but is applicable for any sort of outliers.

•

Last but not least, more effective technical tools have been used in the initialization and induction steps (for example using
[
7
, Lemma 2]
instead of
[
11
, Lemma 21]
) and new lemmas have also been developed (for example Lemmas
11
and
13
).

Assumptions and Notations

Without loss of generality, suppose the ground truth
L^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is a square, rank-
r
matrix. Denote by
L^{\star}=U^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}\left(V^{\star}\right)^{T}
the compact SVD of

, where
U^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r},V^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
, and
\Sigma^{\star}=\diag(\sigma_{1}^{\star},\cdots,\sigma_{r}^{\star})
. With the convention

\sigma_{1}^{\star}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{r}^{\star}
, the condition number of
L^{\star}
, denoted
\kappa
, is given by
\kappa:=\sigma_{1}^{\star}/\sigma_{r}^{\star}
.

Assumption 1.
Assume
L^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is
\mu
-incoherent, i.e.,

\left\|U^{\star}\right\|_{2,\infty}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}},~\left\|V^{\star}\right\|_{2,\infty}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}},

where for any matrix
M
,
M_{i,:}
denotes the
i
-th row of
M
, and
\|M\|_{2,\infty}:=\max\limits_{i}\left\|M_{i,:}\right\|_{2}
.

Let
\|M\|_{\infty}:=\max\limits_{i,j}|M_{ij}|
. It follows immediately from Assumption 1 that

\left\|L\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\mu r}{n}\sigma_{1}^{\star},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\max\left\{\left\|L\right\|_{2,\infty},\left\|L^{T}\right\|_{2,\infty}\right\}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\sigma_{1}^{\star}.

Assumption 2.
Each entry is independently observed with probability
p
, i.e.,

\mathbb{P}\left\{(i,j)\in\Omega\right\}=p.

Assumption 3.
Denote by
\Omega_{S^{\star}}
the support of the outliers in
\Omega
. It is assumed that
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}\left(S^{\star}\right)
is
2\alpha p
-sparse, i.e., it has no more than
2\alpha pn
nonzero entries per row and column.

Remark 1.
Assumptions 1 and 2 are standard assumptions in the matrix completion literature. Assumption 1, together with Assumption 3, can be considered as separation conditions on
(L^{\star},S^{\star})

[
5
,
9
]
. One can see that if each observed entry independently has probability
\alpha
to be corrupted by an outlier, Assumption 3 can be satisfied with high probability
1
1

1

Throughout the paper, this terminology means that the event occurs with probability at least
1-C_{1}n^{-C_{2}}
for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
and
C_{2}
sufficiently large.
. However, Assumption 3 can account for other sparsity patterns of the outliers as well.

Lastly, we introduce more notations that will be used throughout this paper. We denote by
e_{i}
the the
i
-th standard basis vector, by
\bm{1}
the all-one vector, and by
I
the identity matrix. Their dimensions will be clear from the context. We use
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to denote the standard inner product between two matrices. For a matrix
M
, we use
M_{:,j}
to denote the
j
-th column of
M
. The spectral norm and Frobenius norm of
M
are denoted by
\left\|M\right\|_{2}
and
\left\|M\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}
, respectively.

Nonconvex RMC with General Thresholding Functions

2.1
Algorithm and Main Result

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be the projection onto the set indexed by
\Omega
, i.e,

\left(\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(Z)\right)_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}Z_{ij}&(i,j)\in\Omega\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.,

and
\mathcal{P}_{r}
be the operator that computes the best rank-
r
approximation of a matrix. The iterates of the SVP algorithm
[
17
]
for low rank matrix completion (i.e.,
S^{\star}_{ij}=0
in (
1
)) can be written as

L^{t+1}=\mathcal{P}_{r}\left(L^{t}-p^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}\left(L^{t}-M\right)\right),

which is a projected gradient descent algorithm for a rank-constrained least squares problem,

\min_{Z}~\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}\left(Z-M\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\rank(Z)=r.

When the measurements are corrupted by sparse outliers, the R-RMC algorithm proposed in
[
10
]
combines SVP with hard-thresholding for the outliers,

\displaystyle S^{t}=\mathcal{HT}_{\eta^{t}}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}\left(M-L^{t}\right)\right),~L^{t+1}=\mathcal{P}_{r}\left(L^{t}-p^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}\left(\left(L^{t}+S^{t}\right)-M\right)\right),

(2)

where

\mathcal{HT}_{\eta}(z)=\begin{cases}0&|z|\leq\eta\\
z&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

is the hard-thresholding function with a parameter
\eta^{t}>0
computed from
L^{t}
.

The discontinuous nature of hard-thresholding function makes it difficult to extend the leave-one-out analysis in
[
11
]
to R-RMC. Therefore, we will consider continuous thresholding functions in this paper.
Since there are different options, for example soft-thresholding and SCAD
[
14
]
, which one should we consider? The phase transition tests presented in Figure
1
suggest that both of them work desirably. Therefore, instead of focusing on a particular thresholding function, we consider a general class of thresholding functions.
Specifically, letting
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}
be the thresholding function with parameter
\lambda>0
, we assume the following three properties hold:

P.1

\forall\,|x|\leq\lambda
,
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}(x)=0
;

P.2

there exists a constant
K>0
such that
|T_{\lambda}(x)-T_{\lambda}(y)|\leq K|x-y|,~\forall\,x,y
;

P.3

there exists a constant
B>0
such that
|T_{\lambda}(x)-x|\leq B\lambda,~\forall\,x
.

As can be seen later, 1) the first property will ensure that the estimated outliers have the correct support; 2) the second property will help us transfer the desirable properties of the low-rank part to the outlier part, and 3) the last property will allow us to control outliers with large magnitudes. Moreover, the aforementioned soft-thresholding and SCAD functions satisfy these properties, and a justification of this claim is provided in Lemma
14
.

3
Proof Outline

We first provide an outline of the proof, and defer the details to later sections. As already mentioned, we only consider the case
n_{1}=n_{2}=n
for simplicity. It is straightforward to extend the analysis to the rectangular case.

First the update of
L^{t}
in Alg.
1

can be written in the following form:

\displaystyle L^{t+1}=\mathcal{P}_{r}\left(L^{t}-p^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}\left(L^{t}+S^{t}-M\right)\right)=\mathcal{P}_{r}\left(L^{\star}-p^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}\left(S^{t}-S^{\star}\right)+\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}\left(L^{t}-L^{\star}\right)\right),

where
\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}:=\mathcal{I}-\frac{1}{p}\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
. Thus, the whole algorithm can be written as: Start from
L^{0}=0
and repeat

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}S^{t}&=&\mathcal{T}_{\xi^{t}}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}\left(M-L^{t}\right)\right),\\
L^{t+1}&=&\mathcal{P}_{r}\left(L^{\star}-p^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}\left(S^{t}-S^{\star}\right)+\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}\left(L^{t}-L^{\star}\right)\right)\end{array}\right.~(t\geq 0).

In order to establish its convergence, inspired by
[
21
,
11
]
, we introduce the following auxiliary leave-one-out sequences for
i\in\{1,\dots,2n\}
: Starting from
L^{0,i}=0
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}S^{t,i}&=&\mathcal{T}_{\xi^{t}}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}\left(M-L^{t,i}\right)\right),\\
L^{t+1,i}&=&\mathcal{P}_{r}\left(L^{\star}-p^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}\left(S^{t,i}-S^{\star}\right)+\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}\left(L^{t,i}-L^{\star}\right)\right)\end{array}\right.~(t\geq 0),

where

\left(\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}(Z)\right)_{jk}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\delta_{jk}Z_{jk}&j\neq i\\
0&j=i\end{array}\right.,~\left(\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}(Z)\right)_{jk}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(1-\frac{1}{p}\delta_{jk})Z_{jk}&j\neq i\\
0&j=i\end{array}\right.

if
1\leq i\leq n
, and

\left(\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}(Z)\right)_{jk}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\delta_{jk}Z_{jk}&k\neq i-n\\
0&k=i-n\end{array}\right.,~\left(\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}(Z)\right)_{jk}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(1-\frac{1}{p}\delta_{jk})Z_{jk}&k\neq i-n\\
0&k=i-n\end{array}\right.

if
n+1\leq i\leq 2n
.

Note that the original sequence of the proposed algorithm can then be written in a unified way, with the definition
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}^{(-0)}:=\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
, and
\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^{(-0)}:=\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}
. We can see that by construction,
\{S^{t,i}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
and
\{L^{t,i}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
are independent with respect to the random variables
\delta_{ij}
on the
i
-th row if
1\leq i\leq n
, and the
(i-n)
-th column if
n+1\leq i\leq 2n
. Such a decoupling of the statistical dependence plays a key role in proving Theorem
1
in an inductive way. More notations should be introduced in order to present the induction hypotheses.

For
i\in\{0,1,\cdots,2n\}
, letting
E_{1}^{t,i}:=p^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}\left(S^{t,i}-S^{\star}\right)
, and
E_{2}^{t,i}:=\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}\left(L^{t,i}-L^{\star}\right)
, the update for the low-rank part can be written as:

L^{t+1,i}=\mathcal{P}_{r}\left(L^{\star}-E_{1}^{t,i}+E_{2}^{t,i}\right):=\mathcal{P}_{r}\left(L^{\star}+E^{t,i}\right),

which can be deemed as a perturbation of
L^{\star}
. Denote by
U^{t+1,i}\Sigma^{t+1,i}\left(V^{t+1,i}\right)^{T}
the SVD of
L^{t+1,i}
. The deviation between

F^{t+1,i}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}U^{t+1,i}\\
V^{t+1,i}\end{array}\right]~\text{and}~F^{\star}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}U^{\star}\\
V^{\star}\end{array}\right]

can be measured by

\min_{R\in\mathcal{O}(r)}\left\|F^{t+1,i}-F^{\star}R\right\|_{\mathrm{F}},

where
\mathcal{O}(r):=\{R\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}~|~R^{T}R=I_{r}\}
. This is known as the orthogonal Procrustes problem
[
21
]
, and one optimal rotation matrix, denoted
G^{t+1,i}
, can be obtained by computing the SVD of
H^{t+1,i}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(F^{\star}\right)^{T}F^{t+1,i}=A^{t+1,i}\widetilde{\Sigma}^{t+1,i}\left(B^{t+1,i}\right)^{T}
, and setting
G^{t+1,i}:=A^{t+1,i}\left(B^{t+1,i}\right)^{T}
. We further define

\Delta^{t+1,i}:=F^{t+1,i}-F^{\star}G^{t+1,i}
.

When
\left\|E^{t,i}\right\|_{2}
is small enough,
\|L^{t+1,i}-L^{\star}\|_{\infty}
can be bounded by
\left\|\Delta^{t+1,i}\right\|_{2,\infty}
, as stated in the following lemma. This is an extension of
[
11
, Lemma 19]
to general matrices. For completeness, we include its proof in Appendix
A.2
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
L^{\star}=U^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}(V^{\star})^{T}
be the SVD, where
U^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r},\Sigma^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r},V^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
. Consider
L=\mathcal{P}_{r}\left(L^{\star}+E\right)
for some perturbation matrix
E
. Let
L=U\Sigma V^{T}
be its SVD. Define
F^{\star}=[(U^{\star})^{T}~(V^{\star})^{T}]^{T}
, and
F=[U^{T}~V^{T}]^{T}
. Suppose the rank-
r
SVD of
\left(F^{\star}\right)^{T}F
is
A\widetilde{\Sigma}B^{T}
. Set
G=AB^{T}
, and
\Delta=F-F^{\star}G
. If
\|E\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{r}^{\star}
, then

\displaystyle\left\|L-L^{\star}\right\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\|\Delta\|_{2,\infty}\left(\|F\|_{2,\infty}+\|F^{\star}\|_{2,\infty}\right)\|\Sigma\|_{2}+(3+4\kappa)\left\|F^{\star}\right\|_{2,\infty}^{2}\|E\|_{2}.

Once we have the bound for
\|L^{t+1,i}-L^{\star}\|_{\infty}
, the entrywise error for the outlier estimation can also be controlled, as stated in the following lemma. The proof of this lemma is deferred to Appendix
A.3
.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose

\frac{\mu r}{n}\sigma_{1}^{\star}\leq\beta\leq C_{\emph{init}}\cdot\frac{\mu r}{n}\sigma_{1}^{\star},

, and the thresholding function satisfies the properties

,

. For

\text{Supp}\left(S^{t,i}\right)\subseteq\Omega^{(-i)}\cap\Omega_{S^{\star}},~\left\|\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}\left(S^{t,i}-S^{\star}\right)\right\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\emph{thresh}}\cdot\left(\frac{\mu r}{n}\sigma_{1}^{\star}\right)\gamma^{t}.

Here,

Bounding
\left\|\Delta^{t+1,i}\right\|_{2,\infty}
is the crux of our proofs, and we achieve this by establishing proximity between different sequences. For

, the deviation between

and

can be measured by

Let

be the optimal rotation matrix, and define

. We will show that the following induction hypotheses hold for all the sequences.

Theorem 2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, for iteration
1\leq t\leq T
, where

,

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq 2n}\left\|E^{t-1,i}\right\|_{2}:=\left\|E^{t-1,\infty}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{C_{0}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\kappa\mu r}\sigma_{r}^{\star}\right)\gamma^{t}

(3a)

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq 2n}\left\|\Delta^{t,i}\right\|_{2,\infty}:=\left\|\Delta^{t,\infty}\right\|_{2,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5C_{10}}{C_{0}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\mu r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)\gamma^{t}

(3b)

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i,m\leq 2n}\left\|D^{t,i,m}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}:=\left\|D^{t,\infty}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8C_{10}}{C_{0}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\kappa\mu r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)\gamma^{t}

(3c)

hold true with high probability, for some positive constants
C_{10}
and

satisfying (

).

\displaystyle\left\|L^{t}-L^{\star}\right\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{t}\|_{2,\infty}\left(\|F^{t}\|_{2,\infty}+\|F^{\star}\|_{2,\infty}\right)\|\Sigma^{t}\|_{2}+(3+4\kappa)\left\|F^{\star}\right\|_{2,\infty}^{2}\|E^{t-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{5C_{10}}{C_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)\gamma^{t}\cdot\left(2+\frac{5C_{10}}{C_{0}}\right)\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{C_{0}}\right)\sigma_{1}^{\star}+\frac{\mu r}{n}\cdot\frac{7}{C_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu r}\sigma_{r}^{\star}\right)\gamma^{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{12C_{10}}{C_{0}}\cdot\frac{1}{\mu r}\left(\frac{\mu r}{n}\sigma_{1}^{\star}\right)\gamma^{t}\leq\left(\frac{\mu r}{n}\sigma_{1}^{\star}\right)\gamma^{t},

where the bounds (

Next let us establish

.
For

Step 1: The Bound For
\left\|E^{0,\infty}\right\|_{2}
.
First consider
\left\|E_{1}^{0,i}\right\|_{2}
. Recall from Assumption 3 that
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}\left(S^{\star}\right)
is

\displaystyle\left\|E_{1}^{0,i}\right\|_{2}=

\displaystyle\left\|p^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}\left(S^{0,i}-S^{\star}\right)\right\|_{2}\leq p^{-1}\cdot\left(2\alpha pn\right)\cdot C_{\text{thresh}}\frac{\mu r}{n}\sigma_{1}^{\star}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2C_{0}}\cdot\frac{1}{\kappa\mu r}\sigma_{r}^{\star},

(4)

where the first inequality follows from applying Lemma

2\alpha p
-sparse matrix
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}\left(S^{0,i}-S^{\star}\right)
, and the second inequality holds if

For
\left\|E_{2}^{0,i}\right\|_{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\left\|E_{2}^{0,i}\right\|_{2}=\left\|\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}\left(-L^{\star}\right)\right\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}\left(L^{\star}\right)\right\|_{2}

(5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{4}\left(\frac{\log n}{p}\left\|L^{\star}\right\|_{\infty}+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{p}}\cdot\max\left\{\left\|L^{\star}\right\|_{2,\infty},\left\|\left(L^{\star}\right)^{T}\right\|_{2,\infty}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{4}\left(\frac{\log n}{p}\frac{\mu r}{n}\sigma_{1}^{\star}+\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{p}}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\sigma_{1}^{\star}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2c_{4}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r\log n}{np}}\sigma_{1}^{\star}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2C_{0}}\cdot\frac{1}{\kappa\mu r}\sigma_{r}^{\star},

where in the second inequality we use Lemma

which holds with high probability for some universal constant

, and the last inequality holds if

Therefore,

\left\|E^{0,i}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|E_{1}^{0,i}\right\|_{2}+\left\|E_{2}^{0,i}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{C_{0}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\kappa\mu r}\sigma_{r}^{\star}\right)\gamma.

(6)

Step 2: The Bound For
\left\|\Delta^{1,\infty}\right\|_{2,\infty}
.
To this end, we need to consider the symmetrization of a general matrix. For a matrix

, its symmetrization is defined as

\widehat{M}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&M\\
M^{T}&0\end{array}\right].

Based on this notion, it is easy to see that the eigen-decomposition of
\widehat{L^{\star}}
is

\widehat{L^{\star}}=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}F^{\star}\right)\Sigma^{\star}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}F^{\star}\right)^{T}+\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\widetilde{F}^{\star}\right)\left(-\Sigma^{\star}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\widetilde{F}^{\star}\right)^{T},

where
\widetilde{F}^{\star}:=[(-U^{\star})^{T}~(V^{\star})^{T}]^{T}
. The top-
r
eigen-decomposition of
\widehat{L^{\star}}+\widehat{E^{0,i}}
is
\frac{1}{2}F^{1,i}\Sigma^{1,i}\left(F^{1,i}\right)^{T}
, and
\Sigma^{1,i}
is invertible since
\left\|\widehat{E^{0,i}}\right\|_{2}=\left\|E^{0,i}\right\|_{2}
is bounded by (
6
). Thus for
1\leq m\leq 2n
,

\displaystyle\Delta^{1,i}_{m,:}=

\displaystyle e_{m}^{T}\left(F^{1,i}-F^{\star}G^{1,i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{m}^{T}\left(\left(\widehat{L^{\star}}+\widehat{E^{0,i}}\right)F^{1,i}\left(\Sigma^{1,i}\right)^{-1}-F^{\star}G^{1,i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{m}^{T}F^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(F^{\star}\right)^{T}F^{1,i}\left(\Sigma^{1,i}\right)^{-1}-\left(\Sigma^{\star}\right)^{-1}G^{1,i}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}e_{m}^{T}\widetilde{F}^{\star}\left(-\Sigma^{\star}\right)\left(\widetilde{F}^{\star}\right)^{T}F^{1,i}\left(\Sigma^{1,i}\right)^{-1}+e_{m}^{T}\widehat{E^{0,i}}F^{1,i}\left(\Sigma^{1,i}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{e_{m}^{T}F^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}\left[H^{1,i}\left(\Sigma^{\star}\right)^{-1}-\left(\Sigma^{\star}\right)^{-1}G^{1,i}\right]}_{T_{1}}+\underbrace{e_{m}^{T}F^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}H^{1,i}\left[\left(\Sigma^{1,i}\right)^{-1}-\left(\Sigma^{\star}\right)^{-1}\right]}_{T_{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\underbrace{e_{m}^{T}\widetilde{F}^{\star}\Sigma^{\star}\left(\widetilde{F}^{\star}\right)^{T}F^{1,i}\left(\Sigma^{1,i}\right)^{-1}}_{T_{3}}+\underbrace{e_{m}^{T}\widehat{E^{0,i}}F^{1,i}\left(\Sigma^{1,i}\right)^{-1}}_{T_{4}}.

\bullet
Bounding
T_{1}
.
Consider

\displaystyle R:=

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\star}\left[H^{1,i}\left(\Sigma^{\star}\right)^{-1}-\left(\Sigma^{\star}\right)^{-1}G^{1,i}\right]=\left[\Sigma^{\star}H^{1,i}-G^{1,i}\Sigma^{\star}\right]\left(\Sigma^{\star}\right)^{-1}.

Applying Lemma
7

\displaystyle\left\|R\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|\Sigma^{\star}H^{1,i}-G^{1,i}\Sigma^{\star}\right\|_{2}\cdot\left\|\left(\Sigma^{\star}\right)^{-1}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{4}{C_{0}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\mu r}\right)\gamma.

We then get

\displaystyle\left\|T_{1}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{4}{C_{0}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\mu r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)\gamma.

\bullet
Bounding
T_{2}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\Sigma^{1,i}\right)^{-1}-\left(\Sigma^{\star}\right)^{-1}\right\|_{2}=\max_{1\leq k\leq r}\left|\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}^{1,i}}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}^{\star}}\right|=\max_{1\leq k\leq r}\frac{\left|\sigma_{k}^{1,i}-\sigma_{k}^{\star}\right|}{\sigma_{k}^{1,i}\sigma_{k}^{\star}}\leq\frac{2}{C_{0}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\kappa\mu r}\right)\gamma\cdot\frac{1}{\sigma_{r}^{\star}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|T_{2}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{2}{C_{0}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\mu r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)\gamma.

\bullet
Bounding
T_{3}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\widetilde{F}^{\star}\right)^{T}F^{1,i}\right\|_{2}=

\displaystyle\left\|\left(U^{\star}\right)^{T}U^{1,i}-\left(V^{\star}\right)^{T}V^{1,i}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\left(U^{\star}\right)^{T}U^{1,i}\left(G^{1,i}\right)^{T}-\left(V^{\star}\right)^{T}V^{1,i}\left(G^{1,i}\right)^{T}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\left(U^{\star}\right)^{T}\left[U^{\star}+\left(U^{1,i}\left(G^{1,i}\right)^{T}-U^{\star}\right)\right]-\left(V^{\star}\right)^{T}\left[V^{\star}+\left(V^{1,i}\left(G^{1,i}\right)^{T}-V^{\star}\right)\right]\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left(U^{\star}\right)^{T}\left(U^{1,i}-U^{\star}G^{1,i}\right)\right\|_{2}+\left\|\left(V^{\star}\right)^{T}\left(V^{1,i}-V^{\star}G^{1,i}\right)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left\|F^{1,i}-F^{\star}G^{1,i}\right\|_{2},

where the last inequality holds since

. Then by Lemma
6
,

\displaystyle\left\|F^{1,i}-F^{\star}G^{1,i}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{4}{\sigma_{r}^{\star}}\left\|\widehat{L^{1,i}}-\widehat{L^{\star}}\right\|_{2}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{L^{1,i}}-\widehat{L^{\star}}\right\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{L^{1,i}}-\left(\widehat{L^{\star}}+\widehat{E^{0,i}}\right)\right\|_{2}+\left\|\left(\widehat{L^{\star}}+\widehat{E^{0,i}}\right)-\widehat{L^{\star}}\right\|_{2}

where the second inequality follows from the fact that

is the best rank-

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|T_{3}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{20}{C_{0}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\mu r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)\gamma.

\bullet
Bounding

The main task is to bound

. Due to the symmetrization scheme,

\displaystyle\left\|e_{m}^{T}\widehat{E^{0,i}}F^{1,i}\right\|_{2}=\left\|e_{m}^{T}E^{0,i}V^{1,i}\right\|_{2}

if

. Due to our definition of the leave-one-out sequences, in both cases,

\displaystyle\left\|e_{m}^{T}\widehat{E^{0,i}}F^{1,i}\right\|_{2}=0

when

. Thus, it suffices to consider the case

. Without loss of generality, we only consider the case

and the proof can be done similarly for the other cases. For
\left\|e_{m}^{T}E_{1}^{0,i}V^{1,i}\right\|_{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\left\|e_{m}^{T}E_{1}^{0,i}V^{1,i}\right\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|e_{m}^{T}E_{1}^{0,i}V^{\star}G^{1,i}\right\|_{2}+\left\|e_{m}^{T}E_{1}^{0,i}\left(V^{1,i}-V^{\star}G^{1,i}\right)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{-1}\left(\left\|e_{m}^{T}\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}\left(S^{0,i}-S^{\star}\right)V^{\star}\right\|_{2}+\left\|e_{m}^{T}\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}^{(-i)}\left(S^{0,i}-S^{\star}\right)\Delta_{V}^{1,i}\right\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p^{-1}\cdot(2\alpha pn)\cdot C_{\text{thresh}}\frac{\mu r}{n}\sigma_{1}^{\star}\cdot\left(\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}+\left\|\Delta^{1,\infty}\right\|_{2,\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2C_{0}}\frac{\gamma}{\kappa\mu r}\sigma_{r}^{\star}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}+\left\|\Delta^{1,\infty}\right\|_{2,\infty}\right),

For

\displaystyle e_{m}^{T}E_{2}^{0,i}V^{1,i}=e_{m}^{T}E_{2}^{0,i}V^{1,m}G^{1,i,m}+e_{m}^{T}E_{2}^{0,i}\left(V^{1,i}-V^{1,m}G^{1,i,m}\right).

Step 3: The Bound For

\displaystyle\left\|D^{1,0,m}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}\left\|W^{0,m}F^{1,m}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}}{\delta-\left\|W^{0,m}\right\|_{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{\star}}\left\|W^{0,m}F^{1,m}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{\star}}\left(\left\|\left(E^{0}-E^{0,m}\right)V^{1,m}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\left\|\left(E^{0}-E^{0,m}\right)^{T}U^{1,m}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\right).

\displaystyle\left\|\left(E^{0}-E^{0,m}\right)V^{1,m}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\left[p^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}^{(-m)}\left(S^{0,m}-S^{\star}\right)-p^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}\left(S^{0}-S^{\star}\right)+\left(\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}^{(-m)}-\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}\right)\left(L^{\star}\right)\right]V^{1,m}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(4\times\frac{C_{10}}{20C_{0}}+2\times\frac{1}{2C_{0}}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{\sigma_{r}^{\star}}{\kappa\mu r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)\gamma^{t+1}

\displaystyle+\left(4\times\frac{C_{10}}{20C_{0}}+2\times\frac{1}{2C_{0}}\right)\frac{\sigma_{r}^{\star}}{\kappa}\left\|\Delta^{t+1,\infty}\right\|_{2,\infty}+\frac{\sigma_{r}^{\star}}{16}\left\|D^{t+1,\infty}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}},

and consequently,

\displaystyle\left\|W^{t,m}F^{t+1,m}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\frac{C_{10}}{2C_{0}}\cdot\left(\frac{\sigma_{r}^{\star}}{\kappa\mu r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)\gamma^{t+1}+\frac{\sigma_{r}^{\star}}{10\kappa}\left\|\Delta^{t+1,\infty}\right\|_{2,\infty}+\frac{\sigma_{r}^{\star}}{8}\left\|D^{t+1,\infty}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}.

(20)

Thus we can finally get

\displaystyle\left\|D^{t+1,\infty}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq 2\cdot\max_{m}\left\|D^{t+1,0,m}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\frac{2C_{10}}{C_{0}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\kappa\mu r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)\gamma^{t+1}+\frac{2}{5\kappa}\left\|\Delta^{t+1,\infty}\right\|_{2,\infty}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|D^{t+1,\infty}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}},

and as a result,

\left\|D^{t+1,\infty}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\frac{4C_{10}}{C_{0}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\kappa\mu r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)\gamma^{t+1}+\frac{4}{5\kappa}\left\|\Delta^{t+1,\infty}\right\|_{2,\infty}.

(21)

Combining (
21
) with (
17
),

\left(1-\frac{4+3C_{10}}{C_{0}}\right)\left\|\Delta^{t+1,\infty}\right\|_{2,\infty}\leq\frac{21+3C_{10}}{C_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)\gamma^{t+1}+\frac{1}{C_{0}}\left\|\Delta^{t+1,\infty}\right\|_{2,\infty},

\left(1-\frac{5+3C_{10}}{C_{0}}\right)\left\|\Delta^{t+1,\infty}\right\|_{2,\infty}\leq\frac{21+3C_{10}}{C_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)\gamma^{t+1}.

When
C_{0}\geq 5(5+3C_{10})
and
C_{10}\geq 21
,

\left\|\Delta^{t+1,\infty}\right\|_{2,\infty}\leq\frac{5C_{10}}{C_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)\gamma^{t+1},

substituting this bound into (
21
) and we get

\left\|D^{t+1,\infty}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\frac{8C_{10}}{C_{0}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\kappa\mu r}\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)\gamma^{t+1}.

Going through the proofs, one can see that the induction hypotheses holds provided that

C_{10}\geq 21,~C_{0}\geq 8640C_{10},

(22)

and
p
and
\alpha
satisfy

p\geq\frac{\max\left\{16c_{4}^{2}C_{0}^{2}\cdot\kappa^{4}\mu^{3}r^{3},~1152c_{5}^{2}C_{10}^{2}\cdot\kappa^{4}\mu^{2}r^{3},~230400C_{10}^{2}\cdot\kappa^{4}\mu^{2}r^{2},~640000C_{10}^{2}\cdot\kappa^{2}\mu r\right\}}{\gamma^{2}}\cdot\frac{\log n}{n},

\alpha\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{4C_{0}}\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}\mu^{2}r^{2}}\cdot\frac{\gamma}{C_{\text{thresh}}},~\frac{1}{\kappa\mu r^{3/2}}\cdot\frac{\gamma}{2000K}\right\}.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we study the robust matrix completion problem and consider a nonconvex alternating projection method. Theoretical recovery guarantee has been established for the method with general thresholding functions (for the sparse outlier part) based on the leave-one-out analysis, thus achieving the first projection and sample splitting free result for nonconvex robust matrix completion. For future work, we would like to consider a variant of the algorithm where the projection onto the low rank part is further accelerated via Riemannian optimization
[
3
,
28
]
. In addition, it is also interesting to extend the analysis to the robust completion problem of Hankel matrices
[
2
]
as well as to the robust tensor completion problem
[
27
]
.

Lemma 3

If

\alpha

Lemma 11
.

Suppose

A direct calculation yields that

\displaystyle\left\|e_{m}^{T}\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}\left(AB^{T}\right)C\right\|_{2}=

\displaystyle\left\|e_{m}^{T}\left(AB^{T}\right)H^{(m)}C\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|e_{m}^{T}A\cdot B^{T}H^{(m)}C\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|e_{m}^{T}A\right\|_{2}\cdot\left\|R^{(m)}\right\|_{2}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}\left(AB^{T}\right)C\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\left\|e_{m}^{T}\mathcal{H}_{\Omega}\left(AB^{T}\right)C\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\left\|e_{m}^{T}A\right\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\left\|R^{(m)}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\max_{m}\left\|R^{(m)}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)\cdot\sum_{m}\left\|e_{m}^{T}A\right\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|A\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\cdot\max_{m}\left\|R^{(m)}\right\|_{2}^{2},

which completes the proof.

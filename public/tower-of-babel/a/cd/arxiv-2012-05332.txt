Flat F-manifolds, F-CohFTs, and integrable hierarchies
Alessandro Arsie, Alexandr Buryak, Paolo Lorenzoni, Paolo Rossi

Abstract
. We define the double ramification hierarchy associated to an F-cohomological field theory and use this construction to prove that the principal hierarchy of any semisimple (homogeneous) flat F-manifold possesses a (homogeneous) integrable dispersive deformation at all orders in the dispersion parameter. The proof is based on the reconstruction of an F-CohFT starting from a semisimple flat F-manifold and additional data in genus 1 1 , obtained in our previous work. Our construction of these dispersive deformations is quite explicit and we compute several examples. In particular, we provide a complete classification of rank 1 1 hierarchies of DR type at the order 9 9 approximation in the dispersion parameter and of homogeneous DR hierarchies associated with all 2 2 -dimensional homogeneous flat F-manifolds at genus 1 1 approximation.



Introduction

Since Witten’s conjecture
[
Wit91
]
and its proof by Kontsevich
[
Kon92
]
, there have been growing and fruitful interactions between the area of integrable hierarchies of PDEs and algebraic geometry of the moduli spaces of algebraic curves. In this context, and in connection with topological field theory, Dubrovin introduced in the 90s the notion of
Frobenius manifold

[
Dub96
]
, a differential-geometric structure that encodes genus-zero information af a cohomological field theory (CohFT) on the moduli space of stable curves, besides having far reaching connections with other areas of mathematics.

From the point of view of integrable systems, given a Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold, there exists an associated integrable hierarchy of Hamiltonian quasilinear PDEs called
Dubrovin’s principal hierarchy
, or simply
principal hierarchy
. An important problem in the theory of integrable systems consists in constructing a full dispersive hierarchy starting from its dispersionless limit.

In the framework of moduli spaces, the principal hierarchy associated to a Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold and its dispersive deformation should satisfy additional constraints coming from the intersection theory of the CohFT. In the semisimple case, there exist two different (but conjecturally Miura-equivalent
[
Bur15
,
BDGR18
,
BGR19
]
) constructions defining such dispersive deformations:

(1)

The Dubrovin–Zhang construction
[
DZ01
]
is based on the idea that the partition function of the corresponding CohFT in all genera is the logarithm of the tau-function of a special solution (called the
topological solution
) to a full dispersive hierarchy (the
DZ hierarchy
). One can construct the hierarchy itself starting from this tau-function, and it turns out that the the principal hierarchy is the dispersionless limit of DZ hierarchy. Moreover the full DZ hierarchy and the principal hierarchy are related by a special change of dependent variables, called a
quasi-Miura transformation
, which can be uniquely determined in the semisimple case from genus zero information.

(2)

The double ramification construction, introduced by one of the authors in
[
Bur15
]
, is based on the definition of an infinite set of commuting Hamiltonian densities
[
BR16a
]
in terms of intersection numbers of the CohFT, the double ramification cycles and other natural tatutological classes on the moduli space of curves.

For both constructions and in the (homogeneous) semisimple case, the reconstruction of the full dispersive hierarchy from its dispersionless limit (the principal hierarchy of the Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold encoding the genus
0
part of the CohFT) is possible thanks to the Givental–Telemann reconstruction theorem for the CohFT itself from its genus
0
part
[
Tel12
,
Giv01
]
.

Notice that, by construction, the dispersionless limits of both the DZ and DR hierarchies coincide with the principal hierarchy of the Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold underlying the CohFT.

In the last 20 years, it has been observed that many constructions related to Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds can be extended to a more general setting (
[
Sab98
,
Get04
,
Man05
,
LPR09
,
SZ11
,
AL13a
,
Lor14
,
KMS15
,
AL17
,
DH17
,
BR18
,
KMS18
,
AL19
,
BB19
,
ABLR20
]
). For instance, it was observed in
[
LPR09
]
that the notion of principal hierarchy does not require the existence of an invariant flat metric. This leads naturally to the consideration of the generalization of Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds, called F-manifolds with compatible flat structure
[
Man05
]
or simply
flat F-manifolds

[
LPR09
]
, obtained by replacing a flat metric with a flat torsionless connection and keeping all the axioms of Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds apart from those involving explicitly the metric and not just the associated Levi–Civita connection. In flat coordinates for the flat connection, the flows of the principal hierarchy are systems of conservation laws. In the case of Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds, the presence of an invariant flat metric has to deal with the presence of a local Hamiltonian structure.

In this paper we construct (homogeneous) double ramification hierarchies starting from a (homogeneous) CohFT. In particular, in the semisimple case, leveraging on the results of
[
ABLR20
]
, this provides dispersive deformations of the principal hierarchy associated to a semisimple (homogeneous) flat F-manifold. The existence of these dispersive integrable deformations relies on:

(1)

a generalization of the notion of cohomological field theory, called
F-cohomological field theory
(or F-CohFT for short) introduced in
[
BR18
,
ABLR20
]
;

(2)

a reconstruction theorem for a semisimple (homogeneous) F-CohFT starting from a flat F-manifold and additional data in genus
1

[
ABLR20
]
;

(3)

the definition of an infinite set of commuting flows (the
DR hierarchy
) in terms of intersection numbers of the F-CohFT, the double ramification cycles, the top Hodge class, and psi classes on the moduli space of stable curves.

The paper is organized as follows.

Section
1
is devoted to the construction of the DR hierarchy of an F-CohFT (see also
[
BR18
]
). The main properties of this hierarchy are given in terms of densities of local vector fields on the formal loop space and a special basis for their integrals of motion. We also consider the additional properties of the hierarchy in the case of a homogeneous F-CohFT.

In Section
2
, after recalling the definition of a flat F-manifold and the construction of its associated principal hierarchy, we present our main result: given an arbitrary semisimple flat F-manifold and an associated principal hierarchy, we construct a family of dispersive integrable deformations of the principal hierarchy. These deformations, called the
descendant DR hierarchies
, come from the family of DR hierarchies associated to a family of F-CohFTs parameterized by a semisimple point of our flat F-manifold. The descendant DR hierarchy depends on a choice of a certain vector field on the flat F-manifold, which we call a
framing
. We prove that the descendant DR hierarchies corresponding to different framings are not related to each other by a Miura transformation that is close to identity.

In Section
3
, we discuss the role of (descendant) DR hierarchies in the problem of classification of integrable deformations of integrable dispersionless systems of conservation laws. One can impose various constraints for such integrable deformations, and we discuss the corresponding results (mostly at the approximation up to some finite power of
\varepsilon
) for flat F-manifolds of dimension
1
and
2
in Section
3.2
and
3.1.1
. In Section
3.3
, we briefly mention the problem of computing general integrable deformations of principal hierarchies of flat F-manifolds. It was conjectured in
[
AL18
]
that the equivalence classes of such deformations are labeled by certain functional parameters called
Miura invariants
. In the case of Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds and bihamiltonian deformations, these invariants are equivalent to central invariants, which are known to classify deformations of semisimple local bihamiltonian structures of hydrodynamic type (
[
DLZ06
,
CPS18
]
).

1.
Double ramification hierarchy of an F-CohFT

In this section, we associate to any F-CohFT with a vector space
V
an infinite sequence of commuting vector fields on the formal loop space of
V
, i.e., an infinite sequence of compatible systems of evolutionary PDEs of rank
N:=\dim V
(in particular, in the form of conservation laws). This construction is a generalization of the double ramification hierarchy of
[
Bur15
,
BR16a
]
to the context of F-CohFTs and enjoys most of its properties (for instance, recursion formulas for the higher symmetries), but loses in general the Hamiltonian nature.

1.1.
F-cohomological field theories

We recall from
[
BR18
,
ABLR20
]
the definition of an F-cohomological field theory on the moduli space
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
of stable curves of genus
g
with
n
marked points. We will denote by
H^{*}(X)
the cohomology ring with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
of a topological space
X
. When considering the moduli space of stable curves,
X={\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
, the even part
H^{\mathrm{even}}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
in the cohomology ring
H^{*}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
can optionally be replaced by the Chow ring
A^{*}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
.

Definition 1.1
.

An
F-cohomological field theory
(or F-CohFT) is a system of linear maps

c_{g,n+1}\colon V^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes n}\to H^{\mathrm{even}}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n+1}),\qquad 2g-1+n>0,

where
V
is an arbitrary finite dimensional vector space, together with a special element
e\in V
, called the
unit
, such that, chosen any basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{\dim V}
of
V
and the dual basis
e^{1},\ldots,e^{\dim V}
of
V^{*}
, the following axioms are satisfied:

(i)

The maps
c_{g,n+1}
are equivariant with respect to the
S_{n}
-action permuting the
n
copies of
V
in
V^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes n}
and the last
n
marked points in
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n+1}
, respectively.

(ii)

\pi^{*}c_{g,n+1}(e^{\alpha_{0}}\otimes\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}})=c_{g,n+2}(e^{\alpha_{0}}\otimes\otimes_{i=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes e)
for
1\leq\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\leq\dim V
, where
\pi\colon{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n+2}\to{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n+1}
is the map that forgets the last marked point.

Moreover,
c_{0,3}(e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\otimes e)=\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}
for
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq\dim V
.

(iii)

\mathrm{gl}^{*}c_{g_{1}+g_{2},n_{1}+n_{2}+1}(e^{\alpha_{0}}\otimes\otimes_{i=1}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}e_{\alpha_{i}})=c_{g_{1},n_{1}+2}(e^{\alpha_{0}}\otimes\otimes_{i\in I}e_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes e_{\mu})\otimes c_{g_{2},n_{2}+1}(e^{\mu}\otimes\otimes_{j\in J}e_{\alpha_{j}})
for
1\leq\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n_{1}+n_{2}}\leq\dim V
, where
I\sqcup J=\{2,\ldots,n_{1}+n_{2}+1\}
,
|I|=n_{1}
,
|J|=n_{2}
, and
\mathrm{gl}\colon{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g_{1},n_{1}+2}\times{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g_{2},n_{2}+1}\to{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g_{1}+g_{2},n_{1}+n_{2}+1}
is the corresponding gluing map.

There is an obvious generalization of the notion of an F-CohFT where the maps
c_{g,n+1}
take value in
H^{\mathrm{even}}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n+1})\otimes K
, where
K
is a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra. We will call such objects
F-cohomological field theories with coefficients in
K
.

Definition 1.2
.

An F-CohFT
c_{g,n+1}\colon V^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes n}\to H^{\mathrm{even}}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n+1})
is called
homogeneous
if there exists an operator
Q\in\mathrm{End}(V)
, a vector
\overline{r}\in V
, and a complex constant
\gamma
such that
Qe=0
and the following condition is satisfied:

(1.1)

\mathrm{Deg}\circ c_{g,n+1}+\pi_{*}\circ c_{g,n+2}\circ(\otimes\overline{r})=\\
=c_{g,n+1}\circ\left(-Q^{t}\otimes\mathrm{Id}^{\otimes n}+\sum_{i+j=n-1}\mathrm{Id}\otimes\mathrm{Id}^{\otimes i}\otimes Q\otimes\mathrm{Id}^{\otimes j}\right)+\gamma gc_{g,n+1},

where
\mathrm{Deg}\in\mathrm{End}(H^{*}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}))
is the operator acting on
H^{i}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
by the multiplication by
\frac{i}{2}
,
\pi\colon{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n+2}\to{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n+1}
is the map that forgets the last marked point,
\otimes\overline{r}\colon V^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes n}\to V^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes(n+1)}
is the operator of tensor multiplication from the right by
\overline{r}
, and
Q^{t}\in\mathrm{End}(V^{*})
is the transposed operator. The constant
\gamma
is called the
conformal dimension
of our F-CohFT.

Remark 1.3
.

Our definition of a homogeneous F-CohFT is slightly more general, than the one from the paper
[
ABLR20
]
where the operator
Q
was required to be diagonalizable. However, it is easy to see that all the results from
[
ABLR20
]
about homogeneous F-CohFTs are true with the new definition (see also Section
2.1
with a new definition of a homogeneous flat F-manifold). An example of a homogeneous F-CohFT with a nondiagonalizable operator
Q
will appear in Section
3
.

1.2.
Vector fields on the the formal loop space

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
and
\widehat{\Lambda}
be the spaces of differential polynomials and local functionals in formal (even) variables
u^{\alpha}_{k}
,
1\leq\alpha\leq N
,
k\geq 0
, and
\varepsilon
, with the differential grading
\deg_{\partial_{x}}u^{\alpha}_{k}=k
,
\deg_{\partial_{x}}\varepsilon=-1
, where the definitions and the notations are taken from
[
Ros17
, Section 2.1]
.

The
space of densities of local multivector fields
(on the formal loop space of
V
) is the supercommutative associative algebra

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}:=\mathbb{C}[[u^{*},\theta_{*}]][u^{*}_{>0},\theta_{*,>0}][[\varepsilon]],

where the new formal variables
\theta_{\alpha,k}
,
1\leq\alpha\leq N
,
k\geq 0
, are odd (anti-commuting among themselves and commuting with
\varepsilon
and
u^{\alpha}_{k}
) with
\deg_{\partial_{x}}\theta_{\alpha,k}:=k
,
u^{\alpha}:=u^{\alpha}_{0}
, and
\theta_{\alpha}:=\theta_{\alpha,0}
, and the symbol
*
, as an index, denotes any of the allowed values for that index. The algebra
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}
is endowed with the
super grading
, denoted by
\deg_{\theta}
, which is defined by
\deg_{\theta}\theta_{\alpha,k}:=1
and
\deg_{\theta}u^{\alpha}_{k}=\deg_{\theta}\varepsilon:=0
. The sub-vector space of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}
homogeneous of super degree
i\geq 0
is denoted by
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}
and called the
space of densities of local
i
-vector fields
. We have
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{0}
, while
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{1}
is called the
space of densities of local vector fields
. The homogeneous component of the space
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}
of differential degree
k
will be denoted by
(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{i})^{[k]}
.

The operator
\partial_{x}
is extended from
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
to
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}
as the super-derivation

\partial_{x}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(u^{\alpha}_{k+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{k}}+\theta_{\alpha,k+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{\alpha,k}}\right),

where, here and in what follows, we perform summation over repeated Greek indices. The
space of local multivector fields
is defined as

\widehat{\Lambda}^{\bullet}:=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}/(\rm Im\,\partial_{x}\oplus\mathbb{C}[[\varepsilon]])

and, for
i\geq 0
, the
space of local
i
-vector fields

\widehat{\Lambda}^{i}
is the image of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}
in the quotient. If
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}
, its image in
\widehat{\Lambda}^{\bullet}
is denoted by
\overline{f}=\int fdx
. As before,
\widehat{\Lambda}=\widehat{\Lambda}^{0}
, and
\widehat{\Lambda}^{1}
is called the
space of local vector fields
. Naturally, the spaces
\widehat{\Lambda}^{i}
inherit the differential grading
\deg_{\partial_{x}}
.

For any
1\leq\alpha\leq N
, we define the
(super) variational derivatives

\frac{\delta}{\delta u^{\alpha}}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-\partial_{x})^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{k}}\ ,\qquad\frac{\delta}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-\partial_{x})^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{\alpha,k}},

which are well defined on
\widehat{\Lambda}^{\bullet}
since they vanish on
\rm Im\,\partial_{x}\oplus\mathbb{C}[[\varepsilon]]
.

The
Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket

[\cdot,\cdot]\colon\widehat{\Lambda}^{i}\times\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{j}\to\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{i+j-1}
is defined by

(1.2)

[\overline{f},g]:=\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\partial_{x}^{k}\left(\frac{\delta\overline{f}}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}\right)\frac{\partial g}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{k}}+(-1)^{i}\partial_{x}^{k}\left(\frac{\delta\overline{f}}{\delta u^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{\partial g}{\partial\theta_{\alpha,k}}\right).

This Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket is a lift of the Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]\colon\widehat{\Lambda}^{i}\times\widehat{\Lambda}^{j}\to\widehat{\Lambda}^{i+j-1}
defined by

(1.3)

[\overline{f},\overline{g}]:=\int\left(\frac{\delta\overline{f}}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}\frac{\delta\overline{g}}{\delta u^{\alpha}}+(-1)^{i}\frac{\delta\overline{f}}{\delta u^{\alpha}}\frac{\delta\overline{g}}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}\right)dx.

A further lift of the Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket to
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}\times\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{j}
can be defined employing formal Dirac delta functions, similarly to what was done in
[
BR16b
]
for the quantum commutator of two differential polynomials,

(1.4)

[f(x),g(y)]:=\sum_{k,l\geq 0}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{\alpha,k}}(x)\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{l}}(y)\,\partial_{x}^{k}\partial_{y}^{l}\delta(x-y)+(-1)^{i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{k}}(x)\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial\theta_{\alpha,l}}(y)\,\partial_{x}^{k}\partial_{y}^{l}\delta(x-y)\right).

Taking the integral with respect to
x
of formula (
1.4
), using that
\int\delta(x-y)g(y)dx=g(y)
, reproduces indeed formula (
1.2
), and further integration with respect to
y
gives (
1.3
).

As usual, for
i=j=1
, the above Schouten–Nijenhuis brackets are called the
Lie brackets
. For
i=1
and
j=0
, the Schouten–Nijenhuis brackets reduce simply to the differentiation of (a density of) a local functional along (a density of) a vector field, from which we see that the symbol
\theta_{\alpha,k}
can be interpreted as the operator
\partial_{x}^{k}\circ\frac{\delta}{\delta u^{\alpha}}:\widehat{\Lambda}\to\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
.

Given a local vector field
\overline{X}\in\widehat{\Lambda}^{1}
, there is a unique representative
X\in\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{1}
of
\overline{X}
such that
X=X^{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha}
with
X^{\alpha}\in\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
. This representative is given by
X=\frac{\delta\overline{X}}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}\theta_{\alpha}
. The system of evolutionary PDEs associated to
\overline{X}
is

(1.5)

\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial t}=\frac{\delta\overline{X}}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}(u^{*}_{*};\varepsilon),\qquad\alpha=1,\ldots,N.

Two systems of evolutionary PDEs

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial t}=\frac{\delta\overline{X}}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}(u^{*}_{*};\varepsilon),\qquad\alpha=1,\ldots,N,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial s}=\frac{\delta\overline{Y}}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}(u^{*}_{*};\varepsilon),\qquad\alpha=1,\ldots,N,

are compatible, in the sense that, for any
1\leq\alpha\leq N
,
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial s}=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial t}
, if and only if the associated local vector fields
\overline{X},\overline{Y}\in\widehat{\Lambda}^{1}
satisfy
[\overline{X},\overline{Y}]=0
.

Under a Miura transformation (see
[
Ros17
, Section 2.1]
for more details) of the form

(1.6)

\displaystyle{\widetilde{u}}^{\alpha}={\widetilde{u}}^{\alpha}(u^{*}_{*};\varepsilon)\in\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{[0]}=(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{0})^{[0]},\qquad 1\leq\alpha\leq N,

(1.7)

\displaystyle{\widetilde{u}}^{*}|_{u^{*}_{*}=0}=0,\qquad\left.\det\left(\frac{\partial{\widetilde{u}}^{*}}{\partial u^{*}}\right)\right|_{u^{*}_{*}=0}\neq 0,

the generators
u^{*}_{*}
and
\theta_{*,*}
of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}
transform according to the formulae

u^{\alpha}_{k}=\partial_{x}^{k}u^{\alpha}({\widetilde{u}}^{*}_{*};\varepsilon),\qquad\theta_{\alpha,k}=\partial_{x}^{k}\left(\sum_{s\geq 0}(-\partial_{x})^{s}\left(\left.\frac{\partial{\widetilde{u}}^{\mu}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{s}}\right|_{u^{*}_{*}=u^{*}_{*}({\widetilde{u}}^{*}_{*};\varepsilon)}\widetilde{\theta}_{\mu}\right)\right),\qquad 1\leq\alpha\leq N,\quad k\geq 0,

where
u^{\alpha}({\widetilde{u}}^{*}_{*};\varepsilon)
is obtained by inverting
{\widetilde{u}}^{\alpha}={\widetilde{u}}^{\alpha}(u^{*}_{*};\varepsilon)
order by order in
\varepsilon
. For a local vector field, these formulae give

\overline{X}=\int(X^{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha})dx=\int\left(\left.\left(\sum_{s\geq 0}\frac{\partial{\widetilde{u}}^{\alpha}}{\partial u^{\mu}_{s}}\partial_{x}^{s}X^{\mu}\right)\right|_{u^{*}_{*}=u^{*}_{*}({\widetilde{u}}^{*}_{*};\varepsilon)}\widetilde{\theta}_{\alpha}\right)dx,

from which we obtain that a system of evolutionary PDEs (
1.5
) transforms into

\frac{\partial{\widetilde{u}}^{\alpha}}{\partial t}=\widetilde{X}^{\alpha}({\widetilde{u}}^{*}_{*};\varepsilon)=\left.\left(\sum_{s\geq 0}\frac{\partial{\widetilde{u}}^{\alpha}}{\partial u^{\mu}_{s}}\partial_{x}^{s}X^{\mu}\right)\right|_{u^{*}_{*}=u^{*}_{*}({\widetilde{u}}^{*}_{*};\varepsilon)},\qquad\alpha=1,\ldots,N.

Performing the change of formal variables

(1.8)

\displaystyle u^{\alpha}_{k}=\partial_{x}^{k}\left(\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}p_{a}^{\alpha}e^{iax}\right),\qquad\theta_{\alpha,k}=\partial_{x}^{k}\left(\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}q_{\alpha,a}e^{iax}\right),\qquad 1\leq\alpha\leq N,\quad k\geq 0,

one can rewrite a density of a local multivector field
f(u^{*}_{*},\theta_{*,*};\varepsilon)\in(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{m})^{[d]}
as a formal Fourier series

f=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,s\geq 0\\
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}f^{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}}_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m};s}\,\varepsilon^{s}\,p^{\alpha_{1}}_{a_{1}}\ldots p^{\alpha_{n}}_{a_{n}}\,q_{\beta_{1},b_{1}}\ldots q_{\beta_{m},b_{m}}\,e^{i\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}\right)x},

where the coefficient
f^{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}}_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m};s}
, as a function of the indices
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}
, is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
s+d
. Formal Fourier series of this type form a supercommutative associative algebra where the formal variables
q_{*,*}
are odd. Moreover, the local multivector field
\overline{f}
corresponds to the constant term of the Fourier series. Similarly to the variables
\theta_{*,*}
, one should interpret the variable
q_{\alpha,a}
to represent the vector
\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\alpha}_{-a}}
. This is coherent with the following formulae for the variational derivatives in the variables
p^{*}_{*}
and
q_{*,*}
:

\frac{\delta}{\delta u^{\alpha}}=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{iax}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\alpha}_{-a}},\qquad\frac{\delta}{\delta\theta_{\alpha}}=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{iax}\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{\alpha,-a}},

acting on local multivector fields to give densities of local multivector fields. Accordingly, using the formal Fourier expansion
\delta(x)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{iax}
for the formal Dirac delta function, it is easy to obtain the formula for the Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket (
1.4
) on
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}\times\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{j}
in the new variables:

(1.9)

[f(x),g(y)]=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial q_{\alpha,a}}(x)\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial p^{\alpha}_{-a}}(y)\,+(-1)^{i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial p^{\alpha}_{-a}}(x)\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial q_{\alpha,a}}(y)\,\right),

from which analogues of (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are easily obtained by integration in
x
and then
y
.

1.3.
Densities of local vector fields for the DR hierarchy

Denote by
\psi_{i}\in H^{2}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
the
i
-th
psi class
, which is the first Chern class of the line bundle over
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
formed by the cotangent lines at the
i
-th marked point. Denote by
\mathbb{E}
the rank
g
Hodge vector bundle over
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n}
whose fibers are the spaces of holomorphic one-forms on stable curves. Let
\lambda_{j}:=c_{j}(\mathbb{E})\in H^{2j}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
, these classes are called the
Hodge classes
.

For any
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=0
, denote by
\mathrm{DR}_{g}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in H^{2g}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n})
the
double ramification (DR) cycle
. We refer the reader, for example, to
[
BSSZ15
]

The restriction
\mathrm{DR}_{g}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\big|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{ct}}}

]

and the result of the paper
[
MW13
]

[

]

homogeneous polynomial in the coefficients

Theorem 1.5
.

For all

1.4.
Densities of integrals of motion for the DR hierarchy

The DR hierarchy of a CohFT is a Hamiltonian integrable system
[
Bur15
,
BR16a
]
, so the Hamiltonians both generate the commuting vector fields and provide integrals of motion for the hierarchy. In the non-Hamiltonian F-CohFT case, integrals of motion have a separate geometric definition in terms of intersection numbers on the moduli space of curves. For
1\leq\beta\leq N
and
d\geq 0
, we define the following system of formal Fourier series:

(1.19)

\begin{split}g^{\beta,d}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g,n\geq 0\\
2g+n-1>0\\
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\frac{(-\varepsilon^{2})^{g}}{n!}\left(\int_{\mathrm{DR}_{g}(-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})}\hskip-65.44142pt\lambda_{g}\psi_{1}^{d}c_{g,n+1}(e^{\beta}\otimes\otimes_{j=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{j}})\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}p^{\alpha_{j}}_{a_{j}}\right)e^{i\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\right)x},\end{split}

which, thanks to the polynomiality property of the DR cycle, can be rewritten as differential polynomials
g^{\beta,d}\in\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{[0]}
as

(1.20)

\begin{split}g^{\beta,d}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g,n\geq 0,\,2g+n-1>0\\
k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\geq 0\\
\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}=2g\end{subarray}}\frac{\varepsilon^{2g}}{n!}\mathrm{Coef}_{(a_{1})^{k_{1}}\ldots(a_{n})^{k_{n}}}\left(\int_{\mathrm{DR}_{g}(-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})}\hskip-65.44142pt\lambda_{g}\psi_{1}^{d}c_{g,n+2}(e^{\beta}\otimes\otimes_{j=1}^{n}e_{\alpha_{j}})\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}u^{\alpha_{j}}_{k_{j}}.\end{split}

To this definition, we add the extra densities of conserved quantities
g^{\beta,-1}:=u^{\beta}
,

, and the “primary” local vector field
\overline{Y}:=-\int g^{\beta,0}\theta_{\beta,1}\,dx
or, in other words,
\partial_{x}g^{\beta,0}=\frac{\delta\overline{Y}}{\delta\theta_{\beta}}

1\leq\beta\leq N

Finally, for
1\leq\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\leq N
and
d_{1},d_{2}\geq 0

(1.21)

\begin{split}g^{\beta_{1},d_{1}}_{\beta_{2},d_{2}}(x,y):=\hskip-8.5359pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g,n\geq 0,\,2g+n>0\\

To this definition, for future convenience, we add
g^{\beta_{1},-1}_{\beta_{2},d}(x,y)=g^{\beta_{1},d}_{\beta_{2},-1}(x,y):=0
,
1\leq\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\leq N
,

Theorem 1.6
.

For all

and

(i)

\displaystyle[Y_{\beta_{1},d_{1}}(y),g^{\beta_{2},d_{2}}(x)]=\partial_{x}g^{\beta_{2},d_{2}+1}_{\beta_{1},d_{1}}(x,y)-\partial_{y}g^{\beta_{2},d_{2}}_{\beta_{1},d_{1}+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle[\overline{Y}_{\beta_{1},d_{1}},\overline{g}^{\beta_{2},d_{2}}]=0

(iii)

\displaystyle[\overline{Y}_{{1\!\!1},1},g^{\beta_{2},d_{2}}]=\partial_{x}(D-1)g^{\beta_{2},d_{2}+1}

(iv)

\displaystyle[\overline{Y}_{\beta_{1},0},g^{\beta_{2},d_{2}}]=\partial_{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\beta_{1}}}g^{\beta_{2},d_{2}+1}

The proof of part (v) is the same as the proof of part (vi) in Theorem

For the proof of (vi), consider the equation of part (iii) with

and, on the right-hand side,

Part (vii) is proved in an analogous fashion starting from (iv).
∎

2.
Principal hierarchy of a flat F-manifold and dispersive deformations

In this section, using the results from the previous section, we construct a family of dispersive integrable deformations of a principal hierarchy associated to an arbitrary semisimple flat F-manifold. Moreover, we prove that different hierarchies from this family are not equivalent to each other by a Miura transformation that is close to identity.

2.1.
Flat F-manifolds

Here we recall the notion of a flat F-manifold (
[
Get04
,
Man05
]
, see also
[
AL18
]
and
[
LPR09
]
) and its main properties.

Definition 2.1
.

A
flat F-manifold

(M,\nabla,\circ,e)
is the datum of an analytic manifold
M
, an analytic connection
\nabla
in the tangent bundle
TM
, an algebra structure
(T_{p}M,\circ)
with unit
e
on each tangent space, analytically depending on the point
p\in M
, such that the one-parameter family of connections
\nabla_{z}=\nabla+z\circ
is flat and torsionless for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
, and
\nabla e=0
.

The algebras
(T_{p}M,\circ)
are commutative and associative. Let
t^{\alpha}
,
1\leq\alpha\leq N
,
N=\dim M
, be flat coordinates for the connection
\nabla
. Locally, there exist analytic functions
F^{\alpha}(t^{1},\ldots,t^{N})
,
1\leq\alpha\leq N
, such that the second derivatives

(2.1)

\displaystyle C^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}:=\frac{\partial^{2}F^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{\beta}\partial t^{\gamma}}

are the structure constants of the algebras
(T_{p}M,\circ)
,
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\beta}}\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\gamma}}=C^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\alpha}}
. Also, in the coordinates
t^{\alpha}
the unit
e
has the form
e=A^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\alpha}}
for some constants
A^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}
. Moreover, the following equations are satisfied:

(2.2)

\displaystyle A^{\mu}\frac{\partial^{2}F^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{\mu}\partial t^{\beta}}=\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta},

\displaystyle 1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq N,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}F^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{\beta}\partial t^{\mu}}\frac{\partial^{2}F^{\mu}}{\partial t^{\gamma}\partial t^{\delta}}=\frac{\partial^{2}F^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{\gamma}\partial t^{\mu}}\frac{\partial^{2}F^{\mu}}{\partial t^{\beta}\partial t^{\delta}},

\displaystyle 1\leq\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\leq N,

which are often called the
oriented WDVV equations
. The
N
-tuple of functions
\overline{F}=(F^{1},\ldots,F^{N})
is called a
vector potential
of the flat F-manifold.

Conversely, given an open subset
M
of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and analytic functions
F^{1},\ldots,F^{N}
on
M
satisfying equations (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), these functions define a flat F-manifold
(M,\nabla,\circ,A^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\alpha}})
with the connection
\nabla
given by
\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\alpha}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\beta}}=0
, and the multiplication
\circ
given by the structure constants (
2.1
).

A point
p\in M
of an
N
-dimensional flat F-manifold
(M,\nabla,\circ,e)
is called
semisimple
if
T_{p}M
has a basis
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{N}
satisfying
\pi_{\alpha}\circ\pi_{\beta}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\pi_{\alpha}
. Moreover, locally around such a point one can choose coordinates
u^{i}
such that
\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\alpha}}\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\beta}}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\alpha}}
. These coordinates are called
canonical coordinates
. In particular, this means that the set of semisimple points is open in
M
. In the canonical coordinates, we have
e=\sum_{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\alpha}}
. A flat F-manifold
(M,\nabla,\circ,e)
is called
semisimple
if the set of semisimple points is dense in
M
.

A flat F-manifold given by a vector potential
\overline{F}
is called
homogeneous
if there exists a vector field
E
of the form

(2.4)

E=(\underbrace{(\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}-q^{\alpha}_{\beta})t^{\beta}+r^{\alpha}}_{=:E^{\alpha}})\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\alpha}},\qquad q^{\alpha}_{\beta},r^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C},

satisfying
[e,E]=e
and such that

\displaystyle E^{\mu}\frac{\partial F^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{\mu}}=(2\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}-q^{\alpha}_{\beta})F^{\beta}+A^{\alpha}_{\beta}t^{\beta}+B^{\alpha}

for some
A^{\alpha}_{\beta},B^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}
. Note that this equation can be written more invariantly as
\mathrm{Lie}_{E}(\circ)=\circ
, where
\mathrm{Lie}_{E}
denotes the Lie derivative. The vector field
E
is called the
Euler vector field
. Around a semisimple point, the Euler vector field has the following form in canonical coordinates:
E=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(u^{i}+a^{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{i}}
for some
a^{i}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Remark 2.2
.

As we already mentioned in Remark
1.3
, our definition of a homogeneous flat F-manifold is slightly more general than the one from
[
ABLR20
]
, but all the results from that paper remains valid.

Remark 2.3
.

In
[

]
, the authors introduced the closely related notion of a

that is the datum of two different flat F-manifold structures

and

Given an F-CohFT

in

2.2.
Principal hierarchy of a flat F-manifold

Given a flat F-manifold
(M,\nabla,\circ,e)
, one can construct an integrable dispersionless hierarchy called a
principal hierarchy
associated to
(M,\nabla,\circ,e)
(see
[
LPR09
]
). This construction generalizes the notion of a principal hierarchy associated to a Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold. Before presenting the construction, let us introduce a small generalization of the space of densities of local multivector fields.

Let
U
be an open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, with coordinates
u^{1},\ldots,u^{N}
. Denote by
\mathcal{O}(U)
the space of analytic functions on
U
. Consider the following space:

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}_{U}:=\mathcal{O}(U)[u^{*}_{>0},\theta_{*,*}][[\varepsilon]].

Clearly, the space
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}
can be considered as the space
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}_{U}
where
U
is a formal neighborhood of
0
. The space
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}_{U}
will also be called the space of densities of local multivector fields. It is easy to see that all the constructions from Section
1.2
(except, probably, the constructions related to the change of variables (
1.8
)) work with the more general space
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}_{U}
. The space of local multivector fields corresponding to
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\bullet}_{U}
will be denoted by
\widehat{\Lambda}^{\bullet}_{U}
.

Consider a flat F-manifold
(M,\nabla,\circ,e)
. For any point of
M
, on its open neighbourhood
U
, one can consider a basis (over
\mathbb{C}[[z]]
)
X_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{d=-1}^{\infty}X_{\alpha,d}z^{d+1}
,
1\leq\alpha\leq N=\dim M
, in the space of flat sections of the deformed connection
\nabla_{-z}=\nabla-z\circ
:

(2.5)

0=(\nabla-z\circ)X_{\alpha}(z)=(\nabla-z\circ)\sum_{d=-1}^{\infty}X_{\alpha,d}z^{d+1}.

It is immediate to see from (
2.5
) that
X_{\alpha,-1}
,
\alpha=1,\dots N
, are flat vector fields for
\nabla
, while the vector fields
X_{\alpha,d}
are obtained via the recurrence relation
\nabla X_{\alpha,d+1}=X_{\alpha,d}\circ
. If
U
is connected, then the collection of flat sections
X_{\alpha}(z)
is determined uniquely up to a transformation of the form
X_{\alpha}(z)\mapsto X_{\beta}(z)G^{\beta}_{\alpha}(z)
, where
G(z)=(G^{\alpha}_{\beta}(z))\in\mathrm{Mat}_{N,N}(\mathbb{C})[[z]]
is invertible. If
M
is simply connected, then flat sections
X_{\alpha}(z)
can be constructed on the whole
M
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
calibration
of a flat F-manifold
(M,\nabla,\circ,e)
is a basis
X_{\alpha}=\sum_{d=-1}^{\infty}X_{\alpha,d}z^{d+1}
,
X_{\alpha,d}\in\mathcal{T}(M)
,
1\leq\alpha\leq\dim M
, in the space of flat sections of the deformed connection
\nabla-z\circ
. A flat F-manifold with a fixed calibration is called a
calibrated flat F-manifold
.

Consider now a flat F-manifold structure on
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
given by a vector potential
\overline{F}
, together with a calibration
X_{\alpha}(z)
. The
principal hierarchy
associated to our calibrated flat F-manifold is the following system of PDEs:

(2.6)

\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{\beta}_{d}}=\partial_{x}\left(\left.X^{\alpha}_{\beta,d}\right|_{t^{\gamma}=u^{\gamma}}\right),\qquad 1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq N,\quad d\geq 0,

where
X^{\alpha}_{\beta,d}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\alpha}}:=X_{\beta,d}
. We see that the system (
2.6
) has the form of a system of conservation laws. Moreover, this is a system of quasilinear evolutionary PDEs, which is dispersionless and integrable, in the sense that all the flows pairwise commute (see
[
LPR09
]
).

Suppose that
M
is a formal neighbourhood of
0\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. There exist unique flat sections
X_{\alpha}(z)
on
M
satisfying the condition
X_{\alpha,-1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\alpha}}
and the condition that
X_{\alpha,d}
vanish at
0
for
d\geq 0
. The corresponding principal hierarchy is called the
ancestor principal hierarchy
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Consider an F-CohFT and the associated flat F-manifold and the DR hierarchy. Then the dispersionless part of the DR hierarchy coincides with the ancestor principal hierarchy of the flat F-manifold.

Proof.

This immediately follows from the construction of the DR hierarchy and
[
ABLR20
, Proposition 3.2]
(see also an analogous statement in
[
Bur15
, Section 4.2.2]
).
∎

We see that this proposition can be immediately used for a construction of dispersive deformations of ancestor principal hierarchies. In order to construct dispersive deformations of arbitrary principal hierarchies, we need a generalization of the construction of the DR hierarchy, which we will introduce in the next section.

2.3.
Dispersive deformations of a principal hierarchy: descendant DR hierarchies

In order to construct dispersive deformations of a principal hierarchy associated to an arbitrary semisimple flat F-manifold, we first need to study analytic families of F-CohFTs depending on a semisimple point of a flat F-manifold.

Consider a semisimple flat F-manifold structure on
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
defined by a vector potential
\overline{F}
. Recall that for an arbitrary semisimple point on
M

U
, one has the following objects (we use the notations from
[
ABLR20

•

canonical coordinates

•

the matrix
\widetilde{\Psi}:=\big(\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial t^{\alpha}}\big)

•

the matrices
\widetilde{D}
and

defined by

\widetilde{D}
is a diagonal matrix consisting of one-forms,

is a matrix with vanishing diagonal entries, and

(in the homogeneous case this is the operator of multiplication by the Euler vector field);

•

(the entries of this matrix can be interpreted as the Lamé coefficients of a diagonal metric associated with the flat F-manifold);

the matrices

\Psi:=H\widetilde{\Psi}
and
\Gamma:=H\widetilde{\Gamma}H^{-1}

•

a sequence of matrices

,

Note that the matrix

,
R_{k}\mapsto AR_{k}A^{-1}

H
, then the matrices

are defined uniquely up to the transformation

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Id}+\sum_{i\geq 1}R_{i}z^{i}\mapsto\bigg(\mathrm{Id}+\sum_{i\geq 1}D_{i}z^{i}\bigg)\bigg(\mathrm{Id}+\sum_{i\geq 1}R_{i}z^{i}\bigg),

where

Using the notations from
[
ABLR20

where

Let

be formal variables. Recall from

(note, however, that we prefer to use a different notation here) that for an F-CohFT

its formal shift
\mathrm{Sh}_{\overline{\tau}}(c)_{g,n+1}\colon V^{*}\otimes V^{\otimes n}\to H^{\mathrm{even}}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g,n+1})[[\tau^{*}]]
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}_{\overline{\tau}}(c)_{g,n+1}:=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{m!}\pi_{m*}\circ c_{g,n+m+1}\circ\left(\otimes(\tau^{\alpha}e_{\alpha})^{\otimes m}\right),

\overline{\tau}=(\tau^{1},\ldots,\tau^{N})

Proposition 2.6
.

1. For any

is equal to

2. For any fixed

coincides with the formal shift of

Proof.

1
. We know that under the transformation

, where

, and therefore

Thus, for a fixed

the family

doesn’t depend on a choice of

(see equation (4.3) there).

2

2.4.

3.
Towards a classification of dispersive deformations

In this section we consider the problem of classification of dispersive integrable deformations of principal hierarchies for flat F-manifolds and observe the central role played in it by the DR hierarchies. We propose two a priori different classes of deformations and we classify them, up to some finite order in
\varepsilon

3.1.1.

Given a local vector field

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\overline{X}}Y(z):=\partial_{x}(D-1)Y(z)-z[\overline{X},Y(z)],

Suppose there exist

\mathcal{D}_{\overline{X}}Y_{\alpha}(z)=0

(3.1)

Y_{\alpha}(z)\mapsto a^{\mu}_{\alpha}(z)Y_{\mu}(z),

.

Assume that

satisfies the following properties:

(a)

there exist

\mathcal{D}_{\overline{X}}Y_{\alpha}(z)=0

(b)

\frac{\delta}{\delta u^{1\!\!1}}\overline{X}=-u^{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha,1}+\partial_{x}^{2}R

\frac{\delta}{\delta u^{1\!\!1}}=A^{\alpha}\frac{\delta}{\delta u^{\alpha}}

Then, up to a transformation of type (

(i)

\displaystyle Y_{{1\!\!1},0}=-u^{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha,1}+\partial_{x}^{2}(D-1)^{-1}R

(ii)

\displaystyle\overline{Y}_{{1\!\!1},1}=\overline{X}

(iii)

\displaystyle\left[\overline{Y}_{\alpha_{1},d_{1}},\overline{Y}_{\alpha_{2},d_{2}}\right]=0

The proof follows closely the proof of

Theorem 3.6
.

The vector field
\overline{X}=\int(uu_{x}+\varepsilon u_{xx})\theta dx

Let us first present a reformulation of the Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]\colon\widehat{\Lambda}^{1}\times\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{1}\to\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{1}

\overline{X}=\int X\theta dx\in\widehat{\Lambda}^{1}

\overline{X}

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\frac{\delta\overline{X}}{\delta\theta}=X,

and we consider also formal differential operators
\widetilde{L}_{\overline{X}}
and

defined by

\widetilde{L}_{\overline{X}}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-\partial_{x})^{k}\circ\frac{\partial X}{\partial u_{k}},\qquad L_{Y}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}Y_{k}\partial_{x}^{k}.

Directly from the definition (

), we obtain the following identity:

L_{[\overline{X},Y]}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}L_{Y}-L_{Y}\circ\widetilde{L}_{\overline{X}},

where we apply the differentiation
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
to the operator

coefficient-wise.

Let us now take
\overline{X}=\int(uu_{x}+\varepsilon u_{xx})\theta dx

by showing that a required solution

Since

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\circ\widetilde{D}L_{Y(z)}=z\left(\frac{\partial L_{Y(z)}}{\partial t}-L_{Y(z)}\circ(-u\partial_{x}+\varepsilon\partial_{x}^{2})\right),

where
\widetilde{D}:=\sum_{n\geq 0}u_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{n}}+\varepsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon}
, and we apply

to

coefficient-wise. Note that

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(e^{z\varepsilon\partial_{x}}\circ e^{z(u-2\varepsilon\partial_{x})}\circ\partial_{x}\right)=

3.2.

3.2.1.
Homogeneous deformations with string and dilaton property

Let us fix a flat F-manifold together with a homogeneous calibration of standard type. We consider systems of evolutionary PDEs of the form

(3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{\beta}_{d}}=\partial_{x}P^{\alpha}_{\beta,d},\qquad 1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq N,\quad d\geq 0,

\displaystyle P^{\alpha}_{\beta,d}=\sum_{l\geq 0}\varepsilon^{2l}P^{\alpha}_{\beta,d,l},\qquad P^{\alpha}_{\beta,d,l}\in\mathcal{A}^{[2l]}_{M},

such that the following properties are satisfied:

(1)

Commutativity of the flows
: the flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\beta}_{d}}
pairwise commute,

(2)

The dispersionless limit of the system (
3.7
) coincides with the principal hierarchy of the given calibrated flat F-manifold,

(3)

String property
:
\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{1\!\!1}}P^{\alpha}_{\beta,d+1}=P^{\alpha}_{\beta,d}
for
d\geq-1
, where
P^{\alpha}_{\beta,-1}:=\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}
,

(4)

Dilaton property
:
\frac{\partial P^{\alpha}_{{1\!\!1},1}}{\partial u^{\beta}}=DP^{\alpha}_{\beta,0}
,

(5)

Homogeneity condition
:
\widehat{E}_{\gamma}P(z)=z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}P(z)+[P(z),Q]+P(z)\widetilde{R}(z)
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
, where
P(z):=\sum_{d\geq-1}(P^{\alpha}_{\beta,d})z^{d+1}
.

In this section, working out the
N=2
case, we observe how descendant DR hierarchies appear in the problem of classification of dispersive integrable deformations of principal hierarchies of flat F-manifolds of the above form, which we refer to as a
homogeneous deformation with string and dilaton properties
. The role played by conditions (3), (4), and (5) is central in producing finite dimensional spaces of deformations even without having to quotient with respect to equivalence up to Miura transformations of the dependent variables.

Remark 3.7
.

Axioms (1), (3), and (4) above correspond closely to properties (iii), (iv), and (v) of Theorem
3.1
for hierarchies of local vector fields of DR type. Homogeneity (5) corresponds to property (iii) of Proposition
1.7
for homogeneous DR hierarchies. Finally, condition (2) above is satisfied by hierarchies of DR type, see Remark
3.2
. This means that homogeneous dispersive deformations with string and dilaton properties contain homogeneous descendant hierarchies of DR type whose local vector fields have only even powers of
\varepsilon
. It’s not a priori clear that the converse is true and it would be interesting to investigate this point.

3.2.2.
Classification of semisimple homogeneous flat F-manifolds in dimension
2

In the semisimple case, using canonical coordinates
u_{1},...,u_{N}
, the structure of a homogeneous flat F-manifold can be recovered from a solution of the following system (

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Gamma^{i}_{ij}}{\partial u_{k}}=-\Gamma^{i}_{ij}\Gamma^{i}_{ik}+\Gamma^{i}_{ij}\Gamma^{j}_{jk}+\Gamma^{i}_{ik}\Gamma^{k}_{kj},

\displaystyle i\neq k\neq j\neq i,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\partial\Gamma^{i}_{ij}}{\partial u_{k}}=0,

\displaystyle i\neq j,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\frac{\partial\Gamma^{i}_{ij}}{\partial u_{k}}=-\Gamma^{i}_{ij},

\displaystyle i\neq j.

For
N=2
, the above system reduces to (

\Gamma^{i}_{ij}=\frac{\epsilon_{j}}{u_{i}-u_{j}},

where
\epsilon_{1}

I.

\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}\neq 0,1

\displaystyle u=\left(\frac{u_{1}-u_{2}}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{m}},\qquad v=\frac{2+c}{4}u_{1}+\frac{2-c}{4}u_{2},

where
c=2\,\frac{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}}
,
m=\frac{1}{1-\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}\neq 0,1

\displaystyle(F^{1},F^{2})=\begin{cases}\left(uv-2c\frac{u^{m+1}}{m+1},\frac{v^{2}}{2}+\frac{4-c^{2}}{2}\frac{mu^{2m}}{2m-1}\right),&\text{if $m\neq-1,\frac{1}{2},0,1$},\\
\left(uv-2c\log u,\frac{v^{2}}{2}+\frac{4-c^{2}}{6}u^{-2}\right),&\text{if $m=-1$},\\
\left(uv-\frac{4}{3}cu^{3/2},\frac{v^{2}}{2}+\frac{4-c^{2}}{4}u\log u\right),&\text{if $m=\frac{1}{2}$}.\end{cases}

The unit is
\frac{\partial}{\partial v}
, the Euler vector field is
E=\frac{1}{m}u\frac{\partial}{\partial u}+v\frac{\partial}{\partial v}

\overline{\gamma}=\left(\frac{(2-c)(m-1)}{2m},\frac{(2+c)(m-1)}{2m}\right)

If

[
AL17
]

II.

\epsilon_{1}=c,\,\epsilon_{2}=1-c

(see the remark about the case

\displaystyle u=u_{1}-u_{2}+\frac{u_{2}}{c},\qquad v=-\ln{(u_{1}-u_{2})},

\displaystyle F^{1}=\frac{c}{2}u^{2}+\frac{1-c}{4}e^{-2v},\qquad F^{2}=cuv+(2c-1)e^{-v}.

The unit is
\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial u}

, and
\overline{\gamma}=(2-2c,2c)

In the case

III.

\epsilon_{1}=c,\epsilon_{2}=-c

\displaystyle F^{1}=

\displaystyle cuv+u^{2}\left(\frac{c+1}{2}-c\ln u\right),

\displaystyle F^{2}=

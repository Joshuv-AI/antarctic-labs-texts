Abstract




1
Wald OLS test for mildly integrated regressors

We consider separately the limiting distribution of the sup Wald-OLS statistic when the regressor is assumed to be generated via a mildly integrated process. The econometric intuition in this case is that since the regressor is mildly integrated then it is expected to behave asymptotically similar to the IVX instrument. Therefore, we replace
z_{t}
with
x_{t}
into the corresponding sample moments and obtain the corresponding limiting terms. Moreover, intuitively in the case we have a mildly integrated regressor and since the degree of persistence is controlled by the exponent rate
\gamma\in(0,1)
, then we expect that the limiting distribution of the sup Wald-OLS statistic, when testing for an unknown break-point
\pi\in\Pi
, to weakly converge to the standard NBB.

Consider the univariate predictive regression with multiple regressors

\displaystyle y_{t+1}=\left(\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}x_{t}\right)I_{1t}+\left(\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}x_{t}\right)I_{2t}+u_{t+1}

(1.1)

where
I_{1t}:=\mathbf{1}\{t\leq k\}
and
I_{2t}:=\mathbf{1}\{t>k\}
with
k=\lfloor T\pi\rfloor
. The set of regressors
x_{t}
is generated via the following process

\displaystyle x_{t}=\left(I_{p}-\frac{C}{T^{\gamma}}\right)x_{t-1}+v_{t},\ \ \ \ \text{with}\ \ x_{0}=0.

(1.2)

where
\gamma\in(0,1)
is the exponent rate of the degree of persistence.

1.1
Single regressor

Equivalently, to simplify the asymptotics of the following Proposition we consider the univariate predictive regression with a single mildly integrated regressor (no intercept)

\displaystyle y_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{1}x_{t}I_{1t}+\beta_{2}x_{t}I_{2t}+u_{t+1}

(1.3)

\displaystyle x_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{c}{T^{\gamma}}\right)x_{t-1}+v_{t}

(1.4)

We use the following asymptotic terms

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T}x_{t}^{2}}{T^{1+\gamma}}

\displaystyle\overset{p}{\to}\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T}x_{t}u_{t+1}}{T^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}}

\displaystyle\overset{p}{\to}\mathcal{N}\left(0,\frac{\sigma_{u}^{2}\omega_{v}^{2}}{2c}\right)

(1.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T}x_{t}I_{1t}}{T^{1+\gamma}}

\displaystyle\overset{p}{\to}\pi\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}

(1.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T}x_{t}I_{1t}}{T^{1+\gamma}}

\displaystyle\overset{p}{\to}(1-\pi)\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}

(1.8)

Proposition 1
.

Under conditions A1 and A2 of Assumption 1 of the paper, the standard Wald OLS statistic given by the following expression

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{T}(\pi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hat{\sigma}_{u}^{2}}\left(\hat{\beta}_{1}-\hat{\beta}_{2}\right)^{\prime}\left[\mathcal{R}\left(X^{\prime}X\right)^{-1}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\right]^{-1}\left(\hat{\beta}_{1}-\hat{\beta}_{2}\right)

(1.9)

for testing the null hypothesis
\mathbb{H}_{0}:\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}
, when the regressor is assumed to be generated via the following process

\displaystyle x_{t}=\left(1-\frac{c}{T^{\gamma}}\right)x_{t-1}+v_{t},\ \ \ \ \text{with}\ \ x_{0}=0,\ \ \text{and}\ \ \gamma\in(0,1).

(1.10)

is found to have the following limiting distribution

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{*}_{T}(\pi)=\underset{\pi\in[\pi_{1},\pi_{2}]}{\text{ sup }}\ \mathcal{W}_{T}(\pi)\Rightarrow\displaystyle\underset{\pi\in[\pi_{1},\pi_{2}]}{\text{ sup }}\ \frac{\bigg[W(\pi)-\pi W(1)\bigg]^{2}}{\pi(1-\pi)}

(1.11)

Remark 1
.

Proposition 1 above shows that when the regressor has persistence properties assumed to be fall in the realm of mildly integrated processes, then the limiting distribution when testing for a structural change in a univariate predictive regression with a single regressor and no intercept, follows a standard NBB limit similar to the classical linear regression case as proved by Andrews (1993).

Proof.

We obtain the standard OLS estimators
\hat{\beta}_{1}
and
\hat{\beta}_{2}
of the corresponding regression coefficients
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
as below

\displaystyle\hat{\beta}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}x_{t}I_{1t}y_{t+1}}{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{1t}}=\beta^{0}+\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}x_{t}I_{1t}u_{t+1}}{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{1t}}\displaystyle

(1.12)

\displaystyle\hat{\beta}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}x_{t}I_{2t}y_{t+1}}{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{2t}}=\beta^{0}+\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}x_{t}I_{2t}u_{t+1}}{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{2t}}

(1.13)

Thus, assuming that the structural break is at an unknown break point such as
k=[T\pi]
for
\pi\in(0,1)
we consider the limiting results under the null hypothesis,
\mathbb{H}_{0}:\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}
. Note that the FCLT does not apply in this case (mildly integrated predictors). However, we use the limit theory already derived in PM.

\displaystyle T^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}\left(\hat{\beta}_{1}-\beta^{0}\right)=\frac{\displaystyle\frac{1}{T^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}}\sum_{t=1}^{\lfloor T\pi\rfloor}x_{t}u_{t+1}}{\displaystyle\frac{1}{T^{1+\gamma}}\sum_{t=1}^{\lfloor T\pi\rfloor}x^{2}_{t}}\Rightarrow\frac{\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\left(0,\pi\frac{\sigma_{u}^{2}\omega_{v}^{2}}{2c}\right)}{\displaystyle\pi\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}}

(1.14)

\displaystyle T^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}\left(\hat{\beta}_{2}-\beta^{0}\right)=\frac{\displaystyle\frac{1}{T^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}}\sum_{t=\lfloor T\pi\rfloor+1}^{T}x_{t}u_{t+1}}{\displaystyle\frac{1}{T^{1+\gamma}}\sum_{t=\lfloor T\pi\rfloor+1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}}\Rightarrow\frac{\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\left(0,(1-\pi)\frac{\sigma_{u}^{2}\omega_{v}^{2}}{2c}\right)}{\displaystyle(1-\pi)\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}}

(1.15)

Thus, using (
1.14
) and (
1.15
) we obtain the following simplified expression

\displaystyle T^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}\left(\hat{\beta}_{1}-\hat{\beta}_{2}\right)\Rightarrow\frac{\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\left(0,\pi\frac{\sigma_{u}^{2}\omega_{v}^{2}}{2c}\right)}{\displaystyle\pi\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}}-\frac{\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\left(0,(1-\pi)\frac{\sigma_{u}^{2}\omega_{v}^{2}}{2c}\right)}{\displaystyle(1-\pi)\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}}

(1.16)

Denoting with
X=[x_{t}I_{1t}\ \ x_{t}I_{2t}]\equiv[X_{1}\ X_{2}]
then the Wald test has an equivalent representation as below

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{T}(\pi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hat{\sigma}_{u}^{2}}\left(\hat{\beta}_{1}-\hat{\beta}_{2}\right)^{\prime}\left[\mathcal{R}\left(X^{\prime}X\right)^{-1}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\right]^{-1}\left(\hat{\beta}_{1}-\hat{\beta}_{2}\right)

(1.17)

Firstly, it can be easily proved that the following equivalent expression holds, using the orthogonality property of
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
and via a standard matrix inversion application.

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{R}\left(X^{\prime}X\right)^{-1}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{1t}\right)^{-1}+\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{1t}\right)^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{1t}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{2t}}{\displaystyle\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{1t}\right)\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}x_{t}I_{2t}\right)}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}}{\displaystyle\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{1t}\right)\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{2t}\right)}

Therefore, the simplified expression of the Wald statistic is given by the expression below in the case of single predictors

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{T}(\pi)=\frac{\left(\hat{\beta}_{1}-\hat{\beta}_{2}\right)^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{u}}\left[\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}}{\displaystyle\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{1t}\right)\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{2t}\right)}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\hat{\beta}_{1}-\hat{\beta}_{2}\right)^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{u}}\frac{\displaystyle\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{1t}\right)\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}I_{2t}\right)}{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}x^{2}_{t}}

\displaystyle=T^{1+\gamma}\frac{\left(\hat{\beta}_{1}-\hat{\beta}_{2}\right)^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{u}}\frac{\displaystyle\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{x^{2}_{t}I_{1t}}{T^{1+\gamma}}\right)\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{x^{2}_{t}I_{2t}}{T^{1+\gamma}}\right)}{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{x^{2}_{t}}{T^{1+\gamma}}}

Moreover, the following asymptotic convergence result also holds

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{x^{2}_{t}I_{1t}}{T^{1+\gamma}}\right)\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{x^{2}_{t}I_{2t}}{T^{1+\gamma}}\right)}{\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{x^{2}_{t}}{T^{1+\gamma}}}\Rightarrow\frac{\displaystyle\pi\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}(1-\pi)\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}}{\displaystyle\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}}=\pi(1-\pi)\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}

(1.18)

Thus, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{T}(\pi)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}_{u}}\left\{\frac{\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\left(0,\pi\frac{\sigma_{u}^{2}\omega_{v}^{2}}{2c}\right)}{\displaystyle\pi\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}}-\frac{\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\left(0,(1-\pi)\frac{\sigma_{u}^{2}\omega_{v}^{2}}{2c}\right)}{\displaystyle(1-\pi)\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}}\right\}^{2}\pi(1-\pi)\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}_{u}}\frac{\displaystyle\left\{(1-\pi)\ \mathcal{N}\left(0,\pi\frac{\sigma_{u}^{2}\omega_{v}^{2}}{2c}\right)-\pi\ \mathcal{N}\left(0,(1-\pi)\frac{\sigma_{u}^{2}\omega_{v}^{2}}{2c}\right)\right\}^{2}}{\displaystyle\pi(1-\pi)\frac{\omega^{2}_{v}}{2c}}

(1.19)

Thus, simplifying the terms which do not depend on
\pi
from above expression and using the supremum functional as well since we consider an unknown break-point then we obtain the following expression

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{*}_{T}(\pi)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\underset{\pi\in[\pi_{1},\pi_{2}]}{\text{ sup }}\ \frac{\displaystyle\left\{(1-\pi)\ \mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\pi\bigg)-\pi\ \mathcal{N}\bigg(0,(1-\pi)\bigg)\right\}^{2}}{\displaystyle\pi(1-\pi)}

\displaystyle\equiv\underset{\pi\in[\pi_{1},\pi_{2}]}{\text{ sup }}\ \frac{\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\pi\bigg)-\pi\ \mathcal{N}\bigg(0,1\bigg)\right\}^{2}}{\displaystyle\pi(1-\pi)}

which shows indeed the weakly convergence to a NBB in the case of a single mildly integrated predictor in the predictive regression model.
∎

In summary, we show that the sup Wald-OLS statistic for testing for a single structural change predictive regressions with mildly integrated predictors, which is the case that the degree of persistence is controlled via the exponent rate
\gamma\in(0,1)
, then we obtain an asymptotically equivalent limiting distribution as in the standard linear regression.

Furthermore, since
\gamma\in(0,1)
and assuming that the exponent rate for the degree of persistence of the instrument satisfies
\gamma\in(0,\delta)
, then using Lemma 3.5 of PM the following asymptotic terms hold

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T^{1+\gamma}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}x_{t}\tilde{z}_{t}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{1+\gamma}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}x_{t}x_{t}^{\prime}+o_{p}(1)

(1.20)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T^{1+\gamma}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tilde{z}_{t}\tilde{z}_{t}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{1+\gamma}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}x_{t}x_{t}^{\prime}+o_{p}(1)

(1.21)

1.2
Multiple regressors

Next, we consider the case of multiple mildly integrated regressors. First, we consider the following example, which provides useful insights for the related asymptotic terms in the case of the univariate predictive regression with multiple predictors.

Example 1
.

Consider the predictive regression with multiple predictors given below

\displaystyle y_{t+1}=\beta_{0}+\underline{\beta}_{1}^{\prime}\ \underline{x}_{t}+u_{t+1}

(1.22)

We aim to examine the limiting distribution of the parameter vector
\underline{\beta}=(\beta_{0},\underline{\beta}_{1}^{\prime})
with
\beta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\underline{\beta}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times 1}
and
\widetilde{x}_{t}=\left(\underline{1},\underline{x}^{\prime}_{t}\right)^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times(p+1)}
. Note that since the intercept and the vector of predictors have a different convergence rate, we define the normalization matrix:
\mathcal{D}_{T}=diag(\sqrt{T},T^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}\text{I}_{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)\times(p+1)}
.

We have that

\displaystyle\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}-\underline{\beta}^{0}\right)=\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}^{\prime}\right)^{-1}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}u_{t+1}\right)

(1.23)

where
\underline{\beta}^{0}
the true parameter vector under the null hypothesis,
\mathbb{H}_{0}:\underline{\beta}=\underline{\beta}^{0}
.
We obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}^{\prime}\right)\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\displaystyle\frac{1}{T^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\underline{x}_{t}^{\prime}\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{T^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\underline{x}_{t}&\displaystyle\frac{1}{T^{1+\gamma}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\underline{x}_{t}\underline{x}_{t}^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\Rightarrow\begin{bmatrix}1&\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}0}\\
\\
\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}0}&\displaystyle V_{zz}\end{bmatrix}

(1.24)

Similarly, we also obtain the following expression

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}u_{t+1}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}u_{t+1}\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{T^{\frac{1+\gamma}{2}}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\underline{x}^{\prime}_{t}u_{t+1}\end{bmatrix}\Rightarrow\begin{bmatrix}B_{u}(r)\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)\end{bmatrix}

(1.25)

Therefore, combining the above results, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}-\underline{\beta}^{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}^{\prime}\right)\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\right]^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}^{\prime}u_{t+1}\right)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\begin{bmatrix}1&\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}0}\\
\\
\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}0}&\displaystyle V_{zz}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\times\begin{bmatrix}B_{u}(r)\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)\end{bmatrix}

(1.26)

Proposition 2
.

Under conditions A1 and A2 of Assumption 1 of the paper, the standard Wald OLS statistic given by the following expression

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{T}(\pi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hat{\sigma}_{u}^{2}}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{1}-\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{2}\right)^{\prime}\left[\mathcal{R}\left(X^{\prime}X\right)^{-1}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\right]^{-1}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{1}-\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{2}\right)

(1.27)

for testing the null hypothesis
\mathbb{H}_{0}:\underline{\beta}_{1}=\underline{\beta}_{2}
, when the regressor is generated via

\displaystyle\underline{x}_{t}=\left(I_{p}-\frac{C}{T^{\gamma}}\right)\underline{x}_{t-1}+\underline{v}_{t},\ \ \ \ \text{with}\ \ \underline{x}_{0}=0,\ \ \text{and}\ \ \gamma\in(0,1).

(1.28)

is found to have the following limiting distribution

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{T}(\pi)\Rightarrow\chi^{2}_{k}+\underset{\pi\in[\pi_{1},\pi_{2}]}{\text{ sup }}\ \ \frac{\displaystyle\mathcal{BB}(\pi)}{\pi(1-\pi)}

(1.29)

Proof.

Under the null hypothesis of no structural break,
\mathbb{H}_{0}:\underline{\beta}_{1}=\underline{\beta}_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{1}-\underline{\beta}^{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}^{\prime}I_{1t}\right)\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\right]^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}^{\prime}u_{t+1}I_{1t}\right)

where
\underline{\beta}^{0}
, the population value of the model coefficient.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{2}-\underline{\beta}^{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}^{\prime}I_{2t}\right)\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\right]^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widetilde{\underline{x}}_{t}^{\prime}u_{t+1}I_{2t}\right)

The weakly convergence result for the estimator of
\beta_{1}
follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{1}-\underline{\beta}^{0}\right)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\begin{bmatrix}1&\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}0}\\
\\
\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}0}&\displaystyle\pi V_{zz}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\times\begin{bmatrix}B_{u}(\pi)\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\pi\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)\end{bmatrix}

(1.30)

Similarly, for the estimator of
\beta_{2}
we have the following weakly convergence result

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{2}-\underline{\beta}^{0}\right)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\begin{bmatrix}1&\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}0}\\
\\
\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}0}&\displaystyle(1-\pi)V_{zz}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\times\begin{bmatrix}B_{u}(1)-B_{u}(\pi)\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,(1-\pi)\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)\end{bmatrix}

(1.31)

Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{1}-\underline{\beta}^{0}\right)-\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{2}-\underline{\beta}^{0}\right)\equiv\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{1}-\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{2}\right)

(1.32)

Recall that the expression for the Wald statistic is as below

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{T}(\pi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hat{\sigma}_{u}^{2}}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{1}-\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{2}\right)^{\prime}\left[\mathcal{R}\left(X^{\prime}X\right)^{-1}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\right]^{-1}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{1}-\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{2}\right)

(1.33)

Note that, the following holds

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{R}\left(X^{\prime}X\right)^{-1}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\left(X_{1}^{\prime}X_{1}\right)^{-1}+\left(X_{2}^{\prime}X_{2}\right)^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\left[\bigg(\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\left(X_{1}^{\prime}X_{1}\right)\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\bigg)^{-1}+\bigg(\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\left(X_{2}^{\prime}X_{2}\right)\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\bigg)^{-1}\right]\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}

(1.34)

Thus, the Wald statistic is expressed as below

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{T}(\pi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hat{\sigma}_{u}^{2}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{1}-\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{2}\right)\right)^{\prime}\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\left[\bigg(\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\left(X_{1}^{\prime}X_{1}\right)\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\bigg)^{-1}+\bigg(\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\left(X_{2}^{\prime}X_{2}\right)\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}\bigg)^{-1}\right]^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{T}^{-1}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \times\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{1}-\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{2}\right)

(1.35)

Now, we can consider the limiting distribution of the Wald OLS statistic by replacing the asymptotic terms for both the distance measure and the covariance matrix in the expression for the test statistic. We obtain the following

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{1}-\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1&\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}0}\\
\\
\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}0}&\displaystyle\pi V_{zz}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\times\begin{bmatrix}B_{u}(\pi)\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\pi\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle-\begin{bmatrix}1&\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}0}\\
\\
\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}0}&\displaystyle(1-\pi)V_{zz}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\times\begin{bmatrix}B_{u}(1)-B_{u}(\pi)\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,(1-\pi)\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)\end{bmatrix}

(1.36)

Note that the off-diagonal elements converge in probability to zero. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{1}-\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
\\
0&\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}V_{zz}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\times\begin{bmatrix}B_{u}(\pi)\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\pi\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle-\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
\\
0&\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\pi}V_{zz}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\times\begin{bmatrix}B_{u}(1)-B_{u}(\pi)\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,(1-\pi)\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}B_{u}(\pi)\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}V_{zz}^{-1}\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\pi\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}B_{u}(1)-B_{u}(\pi)\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\pi}V_{zz}^{-1}\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,(1-\pi)\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-B_{u}(1)\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}V_{zz}^{-1}\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\pi\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)-\frac{1}{1-\pi}V_{zz}^{-1}\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,(1-\pi)\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)\end{bmatrix}

(1.37)

which implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{1}-\underline{\widehat{\beta}}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-B_{u}(1)\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{V_{zz}^{-1}}{\pi(1-\pi)}\left\{\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\pi\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)-\pi\ \mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)\right\}\end{bmatrix}

(1.38)

The asymptotic convergence of the covariance matrix is given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
\\
0&\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}V_{zz}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
\\
0&\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\pi}V_{zz}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\right\}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
\\
0&\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi(1-\pi)}V_{zz}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
\\
0&\displaystyle\pi(1-\pi)V_{zz}\end{bmatrix}

(1.39)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{T}(\pi)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{1}{\sigma_{u}^{2}}\begin{bmatrix}-B_{u}(1)\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{V_{zz}^{-1}}{\pi(1-\pi)}\left\{\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\pi\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)-\pi\ \mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\sigma_{u}^{2}V_{zz}\bigg)\right\}\end{bmatrix}^{\prime}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
\\
0&\displaystyle\pi(1-\pi)V_{zz}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \times\begin{bmatrix}-B_{u}(1)\\
\\

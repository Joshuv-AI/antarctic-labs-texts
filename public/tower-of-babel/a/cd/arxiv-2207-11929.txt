Good Locally Testable Codes
Irit Dinur, Shai Evra, Ron Livne, Alexander Lubotzky, Shahar Mozes

Abstract
An explicit construction of locally testable codes of constant rate, constant distance and constant number of queries is given. Hence answering affirmatively the c 3 c^{3} -problem.

Introduction

An
[n,k,d]
binary linear error correcting code is a subspace
C
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, of dimension
k=\dim C
, and distance
d=\min\{\mbox{wt}(c)\,:\,0\neq c\in C\}
, where
\mbox{wt}(c)=|\{1\leq i\leq n\,|\,c_{i}\neq 0\}|
is the Hamming weight.
Call
\rho=\rho(C)=\frac{k}{n}
, the rate of
C
, and
\delta=\delta(C)=\frac{d}{n}
, the normalized distance of
C
.
A code (or more precisely, a family of codes where
n\rightarrow\infty
) is called good if there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\rho
and
\delta
are bounded from below by
\epsilon
.

Definition 1.1
.

The code
C
is called an LTC (locally testable code) if it has a
(q,\kappa)
-tester for
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
\kappa>0
independent of
n
, where a
(q,\kappa)
-tester
T
is a probabilistic algorithm which given a word
f\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, queries
q
bits from
f
and outputs Accept or Reject, such that

•

If
f\in C
, then
\mathbb{P}[T\mbox{ Accepts }f]=1
.

•

If
f\not\in C
, then
\mathbb{P}[T\mbox{ Rejects }f]\geq\kappa\cdot{\rm dist}(f,C)
, where
{\rm dist}(f,C)=\frac{1}{n}\min\{\mbox{wt}(f-c)\,:\,c\in C\}
.

A code is called LDPC (low density parity check) if it is defined by a set of constraints (namely, equations) involving a bounded number of coordinates. Locally testable codes are always LDPC but not vice versa.

In the late 1940’s Hamming
[
Ham
]
defined the notion of an error correcting code and proved that random linear subspaces are good codes with high probability. In the 1960’s Gallager
[
Gal
]
defined LDPC codes and showed that a random LDPC code is good with high probability.
It then took approximately thirty years until an explicit (non-random) construction of good LDPC codes was given by the breakthrough work of Sipser and Spielman
[
SS
]
in the 90’s, building on the previous work of Tanner
[
Tan
]
.

Locally testable codes were defined in the 90’s (
[
BFLS
,
Aro
,
RS
,
GS
]
) with motivation from the theory of probabilistically checkable proofs, yet the existence of good LTCs remained an outstanding problem, called the
c^{3}
-problem (constant rate, constant distance and constant number of queries).
Experts have gone back and forth on the question whether such codes can at all exist (see
[
Gol1
, Conjecture 3.4]
and
[
Gol2
, Section3.3.2]
, and see
[
DELLM
]
for a detailed history).
Part of the difficulty comes from the fact that random codes are
not
LTC (see
[
BHR
]
).

The goal of this paper is to show:

Theorem 1.2
.

There exist explicit good locally testable codes (for every rate
\rho<1
).

Remarkably, combining the theorem with earlier works
[
KMRS
,
GKORS
]
, we get as a corollary that the result in the main theorem holds for all
\rho,\delta
which satisfy the Gilbert-Varshamov bound
[
G
,
V
]
, namely such that
\rho+h(\delta)<1
, where
h(x)=-x\log_{2}x+-(1-x)\log_{2}(1-x)
, is the binary entropy function.
In other words, for every
\rho
and
\delta
for which binary error-correcting codes are known to exist, there also exist locally testable codes.

Corollary 1.3
.

For every
0<\rho,\delta<1
such that
\rho+h(\delta)<1
, there exist good locally testable codes of rate at least
\rho
and normalized distance at least
\delta
.

The reader is referred to Theorem
4.1
and Corollary
4.2
in Section
4
for a definitive formulations of Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
, respectively.

We note that, unlike Theorem
1.2
, the codes from Corollary
1.3
are randomized and not completely explicit.
We leave it as an open problem to find a deterministic construction of such binary locally testable codes approaching the Gilbert-Varshamov bound.

Let us now briefly give the construction of our locally testable codes.
Recall that a finite
r
-regular graph
X=(V,E)
is called a
\lambda
-expander, for
0<\lambda<1
, if its normalized second largest eigenvalue
\lambda(X)
satisfies
\lambda(X)\leq\lambda
.
The expander code of
[
SS
]
was constructed as a subspace of the space of functions
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{E}=\{f\,:\,E\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}\}
.
The local view of
f
at
v\in V
, denoted
f_{v}
, is a vector in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
, after fixing some bijection from the edges touching
v
to
\{1,\ldots,r\}
.
The code
C
is the space of all those functions whose local view
f_{v}
, from every vertex
v\in V
, is inside a well chosen fixed “small code”
C_{0}
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
.
By a simple constraint counting argument, if
\rho(C_{0})>\frac{1}{2}
then
\rho(C)\geq 2\rho(C_{0})-1
, and by using the
\lambda
-expansion of
X
, if
\delta(C_{0})>\lambda
then the local distance of
C_{0}
is propagated to give a global distance for
C
.

Our codes are built as a second floor above the expander codes.
More specifically, let
G
be a finite group with two symmetric sets of generators
A
and
B
of size
|A|=|B|=r
.
We look now at the left/right Cayley complex
\mbox{Cay}^{2}(A;G;B)=(V,E,S)
which is defined as follows.
Its set of vertices is
V=G
, its set of edges is
E=E_{A}\cup E_{B}
, where
E_{A}=\{\{g,ag\}\,:\,g\in G,\,a\in A\}
and
E_{B}=\{\{g,gb\}\,:\,g\in G,\,b\in B\}
, and its set of squares is
S=\{[a,g,b]\,:\,g\in G,\,a\in A,\,b\in B\}
, where
[a,g,b]
is the square consisting of edges
\{g,ag\}
,
\{ag,agb\}
,
\{agb,gb\}
,
\{gb,g\}
.
Further details on the left/right Cayley complexes are given in Section
2
.

We choose a fixed small code
C_{1}\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}^{A}\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}^{B}
.
This code defines an intermediate code
C_{0}=C_{1}\otimes C_{1}\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{A}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}^{B}\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}^{A\times B}
, the tensor code.
Now look at the space of functions on the squares
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{S}=\{f\,:\,S\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}\}
.
Our global code
C
is defined as the subspace of those functions whose local view
f_{e}
from every edge
e
is in
C_{1}
.
Note that
C
can be defined also as the subspace of those functions whose local view
f_{v}
from every vertex
v
is in
C_{0}=C_{1}\otimes C_{1}
.
Further details on the left/right Cayley expander codes are given in Section
3
.

Now, we prove that for a well chosen
C_{1}
; having sufficiently large rate and normalized distance as in
[
SS
]
, but also being smooth in the sense of
[
DSW
]
, the tensor code
C_{0}
is agreement testable (see Definition
3.6
).
We then propagate the “local” local testability of
C_{0}
to a “global” local testability of
C
.
Further details of the above argument are given in Section
4
.

Gar

-adic world left us with some interesting problems.

Expanders

Recall the definition of Cayley graphs.
Let
G
be a finite group and let
A\subset G
be a symmetric subset (i.e.
a\in A\;\Leftrightarrow\;a^{-1}\in A
) satisfying
1\not\in A
.
The left Cayley graph of
G
w.r.t.
A
, denoted by
\mbox{Cay}(A;G)
, is defined to be the finite simple graph whose set of vertices is
V=G
and whose edges connect
g
to
ag
for all
g\in G
and
a\in A
.
We denote an edge connecting
g
and
ag
by
(a,g]
, and this edge is also denoted by
(a^{-1},ag]
. It is easy to check that the total number of edges is thus
|G|\cdot|A|/2
.

A similar definition can be given to the right Cayley graph, denoted
\mbox{Cay}(G;A)
, for which
V=G
and edges connect
g
to
ga
for all
g\in G
and
a\in A
.
Here we will similarly denote the edge connecting
g
to
ga
by
[g,a)
or by
[ga,a^{-1})
.

Next we define the notion of a left/right Cayley complex.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group and
A,B\subset G
be two symmetric subsets.
The left/right Cayley complex of
G
w.r.t.
A
and
B
, denoted by
\mbox{Cay}^{2}(A;G;B)
, is defined to be the following
2
-dimensional square complex:

•

Its set of vertices is
V=G
.

•

The set
E_{A}
of left edges and the set
E_{B}
of right edges are given by

E_{A}=\{(a,g]\,:\,g\in G,\,a\in A\}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad E_{B}=\{[g,b)\,:\,g\in G,\,b\in B\}.

(2.1)

The set of edges is the disjoint union
E=E_{A}\sqcup E_{B}
. Observe that even if
(a,g]
and
[g,b)
contain the same pair of vertices
\{g,ag=gb\}
, they refer to two distinct edges (one is a “left” edge and one is a “right” edge).

•

Its set of squares is
S=A\times G\times B/\sim
, where for any
g\in G,\,a\in A,\,b\in B
,

(a,g,b)\sim(a^{-1},ag,b)\sim(a^{-1},agb,b^{-1})\sim(a,gb,b^{-1}),

(2.2)

and denote the equivalence class of
(a,g,b)
by
[a,g,b]
, so

[a,g,b]=\left\{(a,g,b),\,(a^{-1},ag,b),\,(a^{-1},agb,b^{-1}),\,(a,gb,b^{-1})\right\}.

(2.3)

Given a square
[a,g,b]
, define its set of vertices to be
\{g,ag,agb,gb\}
, and its set of edges to be
\{(a,g],[ag,b),(a^{-1},agb],[gb,b^{-1})\}
.
For any square
s\in S
, and any vertex
g\in V
(resp. edge
e\in E
), we write
g\in s
(resp.
e\in s
), to mean that
g
(resp.
e
) is a vertex (resp. an edge) of
s
.

Note that the graph
(V,E_{A})
is precisely the left Cayley graph
\mbox{Cay}(A;G)
.
Similarly,
(V,E_{B})
is the right Cayley graph
\mbox{Cay}(G;B)
.
The fact that
A
acts from the left and
B
acts from the right, gives local commutativity which generates many four-cycles, namely, squares.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that all edges of
\mbox{Cay}^{2}(A;G;B)
have two distinct vertices since we are assuming
1\not\in A,B
.
The analogous statement for the squares, i.e. that
[a,g,b]
has four distinct vertices and four distinct edges, holds if and only if
A
and
B
satisfy condition (TNC)

ga\neq bg,\qquad\forall g\in G,\;a\in A,\;b\in B.

(TNC)

See
[
DELLM
]
for explicit constructions of triplets
(G,A,B)
which satisfy condition (TNC).

Remark 2.3
.

The number of edges in
Cay^{2}(A;G;B)
is exactly
|G|(|A|+|B|)/2
. Let us count the number of squares.
Each edge
e\in E_{A}
participates in
|B|
squares, and each
e\in E_{B}
participates in
|A|
squares.
So the total number of pairs
(e,s)
such that
e\in s
is
|E_{A}||B|+|E_{B}||A|=|G||A||B|
.
To figure out how many squares there are, we need to know how many edges participate in each square.
In a typical square
[a,g,b]
this number is four, but there might be unexpected collisions, as follows:

•

If
g,ag,gb,agb
are four distinct vertices then the square clearly has four edges.

•

If
ag=gb
, but
g\neq agb
then the square has three (and not four) vertices,
g,ag=gb,agb
. There are four distinct edges:
[g,b),(a,g],[agb,b^{-1}),(a^{-1},agb]
.

•

If
g=agb
, but
ag\neq gb
then the square has three vertices and four edges, just like in the previous case.

•

If both
ag=gb
and
agb=g
then the square has only two vertices:
g=agb
and
ag=gb
, and only two edges:
(a,g]=(a,gb]
and
[g,b)=[ag,b)
. Observe that in this case necessarily
a^{2}=1=b^{2}
.

The last case is excluded whenever the following “no order-
2
conjugates” condition, which is weaker than (
TNC
), holds:

\forall a\in A,g\in G,\qquad a^{2}=1\;\Rightarrow\;g^{-1}ag\not\in B.

(N2C)

If (
N2C
) holds then the number of squares is
|G|\cdot|A|\cdot|B|/4
.
Otherwise the number of squares is at least
|G|\cdot|A|\cdot|B|/4
but it might be (slightly) larger.
The reason is that every vertex participates in
|A|\cdot|B|
distinct squares but every square contains either
2
or
4
edges.

The left/right Cayley complexes are examples of two-dimensional cubical complexes.
Cubical complexes are well-studied, and in particular there are constructions of Ramanujan cubical complexes of bounded degree in any dimension (see
[
JL
]
), whose walk dynamics was studied in
[
Moz
]
.
The left/right Cayley complexes have an additional matching labels feature that other complexes are not known to have.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mbox{Cay}^{2}(A;G;B)=(V,E,S)
be a left/right Cayley complex.

For any vertex
g\in V
, define its squares to be
S(g)=\{[a,g,b]\,:\,a\in A,\,b\in B\}
, and define the labelling map
\iota_{g}\,:\,A\times B\rightarrow S(g)
,
\iota_{g}(a,b)=[a,g,b]
.

For every edge
(a,g]\in E_{A}
(resp.
[g,b)\in E_{B}
), define its squares to be
S((a,g])=\{[a,g,b]\,:\,b\in B\}
(resp.
S([g,b))=\{[a,g,b]\,:\,a\in A\}
), and define the labelling map
\iota_{(a,g]}\,:\,B\rightarrow S((a,g])
,
\iota_{(a,g]}(b)=[a,g,b]
(resp.
\iota_{[g,b)}\,:\,A\rightarrow S([g,b))
,
\iota_{[g,b)}(a)=[a,g,b]
).

Remark 2.5
.

For every vertex
g\in G
, the
b
-th square of the
a
-th neighbor equals the
a
-th square of the
b
-th neighbor (and both are equal to
[a,g,b]
).
In symbols,

\iota_{[a,g)}(b)=\iota_{(g,b]}(a).

(2.4)

Let us call this the matching labels property.

The cubical complexes mentioned above
[
JL
]
exist in all dimensions, but they lack a matching-labels property.
It is an interesting open question whether there are (expanding, constant degree) cubical complexes of dimension above two with a similar type of matching-labels property.

Lemma 2.6
.

The labelling maps in Definition
2.4
are well defined and surjective.
The labelling maps are injective if condition (
TNC
) holds.

Proof.

The map
\iota_{g}
is clearly well defined and the map
\iota_{(a,g]}=\iota_{(a^{-1},ag]}
(resp.
\iota_{[g,b)}=\iota_{[gb,b^{-1})}
) is well defined since
[a,g,b]=[a^{-1},ag,b]
(resp.
[a,g,b]=[a,gb,b^{-1}]
), i.e. the choice of the root vertex
g
or
ag
(resp.
g
or
gb
) does not change the image of the map.
The maps are surjective since for a fixed
g\in V
, the squares
[a,g,b]
running over all
(a,b)\in A\times B
, give all possible squares which contain
g
.
Similarly for a fixed
(a,g]\in E_{A}
, (resp.
[g,b)\in E_{B}
), the squares
[a,g,b]
, running over all
b\in B
, (resp.
a\in A
), give all possible squares which contain
(a,g]
, (resp.
[g,b)
).
Finally the maps are injective if condition (
TNC
) holds, since if
\iota_{g}(a,b)=\iota_{g}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
(resp.
\iota_{(a,g]}(b)=\iota_{(a,g]}(b^{\prime})
, resp.
\iota_{[g,b)}(a)=\iota_{[g,b)}(a^{\prime})
), then by condition (TNC),
ag=a^{\prime}g
and
gb=gb^{\prime}
(resp.
gb=gb^{\prime}
, resp.
ag=a^{\prime}g
), hence
a=a^{\prime}
and
b=b^{\prime}
(resp.
b=b^{\prime}
, resp.
a=a^{\prime}
), which proves the injectivity.
∎

The rest of this section will focus on expansion properties of Cayley graphs and complexes.

Let
X=(V,E)
be an
r
-regular finite simple graph.
Let
\mathbb{R}^{V}=\{f\,:\,V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\}
be the real vector space of functions on the vertices of the graph, and let
\langle,\rangle\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{V}\times\mathbb{R}^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the inner product
\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{v\in V}f(v)g(v)
.
Let
T_{X}\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{V}
be the normalized adjacency operator of
X
, defined by
T_{X}f(v)=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}f(u)
.
Denote by
\lambda(X)
the second largest eigenvalue of
T_{X}
.

Definition 2.7
.

Call
X
(a one sided)
\lambda
-expander if
\lambda(X)\leq\lambda
, for
0<\lambda<1
.

Each left/right Cayley complex is comprised of two Cayley graphs, and we call the complex an expander if both of theses graphs are expanders.

Definition 2.8
.

\mbox{Cay}^{2}(A;G;B)
is a
\lambda
-expander if both
\mbox{Cay}(A;G)
and
\mbox{Cay}(G;B)
are
\lambda
-expanders.

Note that if
\mbox{Cay}^{2}(A;G;B)
is a
\lambda
-expander, then its underlying graph is also a
\lambda
-expander.
Indeed the normalized adjacency operator of the underlying graph of
\mbox{Cay}^{2}(A;G;B)
is
T=\frac{|A|T_{A}+|B|T_{B}}{|A|+|B|}
, where
T_{A}
and
T_{B}
are the normalized adjacency operators of
\mbox{Cay}(A;G)
and
\mbox{Cay}(G;B)
, respectively, which implies
\lambda(T)\leq\max\{\lambda(T_{A}),\lambda(T_{B})\}
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a finite set,
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{V}}=\{f\,:\,\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\}
the vector space of real functions on
\mathcal{V}
and
\langle,\rangle\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{V}}\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}f(v)g(v)
the standard inner product.
Let
\mathcal{M}\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{V}}
be a linear operator and let
0<\lambda<1
.

•

Say that
\mathcal{M}
is symmetric if
\langle\mathcal{M}f,g\rangle=\langle f,\mathcal{M}g\rangle
, for any
f,g\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{V}}
.

•

Say that
\mathcal{M}
is Markov if it is non-negative (namely
\mathcal{M}f\geq 0
whenever
f\geq 0
), and if
\mathcal{M}1_{\mathcal{V}}=1_{\mathcal{V}}
, where
1_{\mathcal{V}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{V}}
is the constant one function.

•

Say that
\mathcal{M}
is
\lambda
-expanding if
\langle\mathcal{M}f,f\rangle\leq\lambda\langle f,f\rangle
, for any
f\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{V}}
such that
\langle f,1_{\mathcal{V}}\rangle=0
.

The following Lemma is a generalization of the Alon-Chung Lemma (
[
AC
]
) for any symmetric, Markov
\lambda
-expanding operator.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{V}}
be a symmetric, Markov,
\lambda
-expanding linear operator.
Then for any
0<\delta\leq 1
and any
R\subset\mathcal{V}
, letting
1_{R}
be the indicator function of
R
,

\langle\mathcal{M}1_{R},1_{R}\rangle\geq\delta|R|\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad|R|\geq(\delta-\lambda)|\mathcal{V}|.

(2.5)

Proof.

Write
1_{R}=\frac{|R|}{|\mathcal{V}|}1_{\mathcal{V}}+f_{R}
and note that
\langle f_{R},1_{\mathcal{V}}\rangle=0
.
Then

\delta|R|\leq\langle\mathcal{M}1_{R},1_{R}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{M}(\frac{|R|}{|\mathcal{V}|}1_{\mathcal{V}}+f_{R}),\frac{|R|}{|\mathcal{V}|}1_{\mathcal{V}}+f_{R}\rangle=\frac{|R|^{2}}{|\mathcal{V}|}+\langle\mathcal{M}f_{R},f_{R}\rangle\\
\leq\frac{|R|^{2}}{|\mathcal{V}|}+\lambda\langle f_{R},f_{R}\rangle\leq\frac{|R|^{2}}{|\mathcal{V}|}+\lambda\langle 1_{R},1_{R}\rangle=\frac{|R|^{2}}{|\mathcal{V}|}+\lambda|R|.

(2.6)

Hence
\delta-\lambda\leq\frac{|R|}{|\mathcal{V}|}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Next we shall define several adjacency operators on the edges (Definitions

,
2.13
and (

-expanding left/right Cayley complex

First let us introduce a bit of notation.
We let
L=(A\times\{0\})\sqcup(B\times\{1\})
so
|L|=2r
and corresponds bijectively to the edges leaving a vertex

(without us having to worry about potential collisions of names).
For
\ell\in L
and
g\in G
, denote

g^{\ell}=\begin{cases}ag&\;\ell=(a,0)\\
gb&\;\ell=(b,1)\end{cases}.

(2.7)

We write
type(\ell)
to denote the second component in
\ell
which indicates if we are in
E_{A}
or
E_{B}
, and we denote by
[g;\ell]
the edge
(a,g]

[g,b)

.
Also, for

Definition 2.11
.

Define the following normalized adjacency operators,

T\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{V},\qquad Tf(g)=\frac{1}{2r}\sum_{\ell\in L}f(g^{\ell}),

(2.8)

the normalized adjacency operator of the underlying graph
(V,E)
,

D\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{V},\qquad Df(g)=\frac{1}{2r}\sum_{\ell\in L}f([g;\ell]),

(2.9)

the normalized unsigned boundary operator from the edges to vertices, and let
D^{t}
be the transpose of

, which is the normalized unsigned coboundary operator from vertices to edges, i.e.

D^{t}\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{E},\qquad D^{t}f([g;\ell])=\frac{1}{2}(f(g)+f(g^{\ell})).

(2.10)

Finally, let

M\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{E},\qquad M=D^{t}\circ T\circ D.

(2.11)

Lemma 2.12
.

The operator
M
from Definition
2.11
is symmetric, Markov and
\lambda
-expanding.

Proof.

The operator
M=D^{t}TD
is symmetric since
(AB)^{t}=B^{t}A^{t}
and
T
is symmetric. It is Markov since it is clearly non-negative and since
D1_{E}=1_{V}
,
T1_{V}=1_{V}
and
D^{t}1_{V}=1_{E}
. It is
\lambda
-expanding since for any
f\perp 1_{E}
,

\langle Mf,f\rangle=\langle D^{t}TDf,f\rangle=\langle TDf,Df\rangle\leq\lambda\langle Df,Df\rangle\leq\lambda\langle f,f\rangle,

(2.12)

where we have used the fact that
\langle Df,1_{V}\rangle=\langle f,D^{t}1_{V}\rangle=\langle f,1_{E}\rangle=0
, the fact that
T
is
\lambda
-expanding and the fact that
\|D\|\leq 1
.
∎

Definition 2.13
.

Define an auxiliary graph
{G^{\parallel}}
whose set of vertices is
E
, such that each
[g;\ell]\in E
is connected to
[g^{\ell^{\prime}};\ell]
for all
\ell^{\prime}\in L
such that
type(\ell^{\prime})\neq type(\ell)
. More explicitly, an edge
(a,g]

(a,gb]
for all

, and an edge
[g,b)
is connected to
[ag,b)
for all

Let
M^{||}\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{E}\to\mathbb{R}^{E}
be the normalized adjacency operator of this graph.

For
\ell\in L

\ell
labelled edges to be

E_{\ell}=\{[g;\ell]\in E\,:\,g\in G\},

(2.13)

and observe that
E_{\ell}=E_{\ell^{-1}}
.
Moreover,

Lemma 2.14
.

For each
\ell\in L
the graph
{G^{\parallel}}
has a connected component
{G^{\parallel}}(E_{\ell})
with vertex set
E_{\ell}
. Let
M_{\ell}^{\parallel}
be the normalized adjacency operator of
{G^{\parallel}}(E_{\ell})
. This operator is symmetric, Markov and
\lambda
-expanding, for any
\ell\in L
. It is explicitly given by

M_{\ell}^{\parallel}f([g;\ell])=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ell^{\prime}\in L}}{{type(\ell^{\prime})\neq type(\ell)}}}f([g^{\ell^{\prime}};{\ell}]).

(2.14)

Moreover, whenever
\ell\neq\ell^{-1}
the graph
{G^{\parallel}}(E_{\ell})
is isomorphic to the Cayley graph
\mbox{Cay}(A;G)
or
\mbox{Cay}(G;B)
depending on
type(\ell)
; and whenever
\ell=\ell^{-1}
and writing
\ell=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
the graph
{G^{\parallel}}(E_{\ell})
is isomorphic to the Schreier graph
\mbox{Sch}(A;G/\langle\ell_{1}\rangle)
or
\mbox{Sch}(G/\langle\ell_{1}\rangle;B)
depending on

.

Proof.

For an edge
e=[g;\ell]
, we call
\{\ell,\ell^{-1}\}
the label of the edge.
It is clear from the definition that in
{G^{\parallel}}
an edge is only connected to edges with the same label. So clearly there are no edges connecting elements in
E_{\ell}
with

as long as
\ell^{\prime}\not\in\{\ell,\ell^{-1}\}
.

We first prove the “moreover” part.
For
\ell\neq\ell^{-1}
the graph isomorphism is given by the bijection
[g;\ell]\leftrightarrow g
. For
\ell=\ell^{-1}
the graph isomorphism is given by the map
[g;\ell]\leftrightarrow\{g,g^{\ell}\}
which is a bijection between
E_{\ell}
and
G/<\ell_{1}>
. Indeed, one can check that whenever there is an edge
\{[g;\ell],[g^{\ell^{\prime}};\ell]\}\in E({G^{\parallel}}(E_{\ell}))
there is also an edge between
g
and
g^{\ell^{\prime}}
in the corresponding Cayley (or Schreier) graph.

Since the operator
M_{\ell}^{\parallel}
is defined as a normalized adjacency operator of an undirected regular graph, it is clearly symmetric and Markov.
By our assumption the largest second eigenvalue of the Cayley graphs (and therefore any quotient, including the Schreier graph under consideration) is at most
\lambda
. We deduce that
M_{\ell}^{\parallel}
is
\lambda
-expanding.
The explicit formula in (
2.14
) is immediate.
∎

Corollary 2.15
.

For any
\ell\in L
, the operator
M^{\parallel}
from Definition

, preserves the subspace of functions supported on

, which we identify with
\mathbb{R}^{E_{\ell}}
.
Moreover, the restriction of

to
\mathbb{R}^{E_{\ell}}
coincides with the operator
M_{\ell}^{\parallel}
.∎

We are now in a position to state the main result of this section.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\mbox{Cay}^{2}(A;G;B)=(V,E,S)
be a
\lambda
-expanding left/right Cayley complex.
Let
0<\gamma<1
and define the operator
M_{\gamma}=\gamma M+(1-\gamma)M^{\parallel}\,:\,\mathbb{R}^{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{E}
.
Then for any
0<\delta<1
and any
R\subset E
,

\langle M_{\gamma}1_{R},1_{R}\rangle\geq\delta|R|\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad|R|\geq\frac{\delta-\lambda}{2r}|E|.

(2.15)

Proof of Proposition
2.16
.

Since
M_{\gamma}
is the convex sum of
M_{1}=M
and
M_{0}=M^{\parallel}
, either
\langle M1_{R},1_{R}\rangle\geq\delta|R|
or
\langle M^{\parallel}1_{R},1_{R}\rangle\geq\delta|R|
.
If
\langle M1_{R},1_{R}\rangle\geq\delta|R|
then by Lemmas
2.12
and
2.10
, we get that
|R|\geq(\delta-\lambda)|E|
.
Assume that
\langle M^{\parallel}1_{R},1_{R}\rangle\geq\delta|R|
.
Let
L^{\prime}\subset L
be a set of representatives such that for any
\ell\in L
, exactly one element of
\{\ell,\ell^{-1}\}
belongs to
L^{\prime}
.
Denote
R_{\ell}=R\cap E_{\ell}
for any
\ell\in L^{\prime}
, and note that
R=\bigsqcup_{\ell\in L^{\prime}}R_{\ell}
and
E=\bigsqcup_{\ell\in L^{\prime}}E_{\ell}
.
Therefore by Corollary
2.15
,
\langle M^{\parallel}1_{R},1_{R}\rangle=\sum_{\ell\in L}\langle M_{\ell}^{\parallel}1_{R_{\ell}},1_{R_{\ell}}\rangle
.
Hence there exists
\ell\in L
, such that
\langle M_{\ell}^{\parallel}1_{R_{\ell}},1_{R_{\ell}}\rangle\geq\delta|R_{\ell}|
.
Then by Lemmas
2.14
and
2.10
, we get that
|R_{\ell}|\geq(\delta-\lambda)|E_{\ell}|
.
Since
|R|\geq|R_{\ell}|
and
|E_{\ell}|\geq\frac{1}{2r}|E|
, we get the claim.
∎

We end this section with some explicit constructions of very good expander Cayley graphs.
Recall that an
r
-regular graph
X
is called Ramanujan if
\lambda(X)\leq\frac{2\sqrt{r-1}}{r}
.
In
[
LPS
]
, Lubotzky, Phillips and Sarnak gave the first explicit construction of Ramanujan Cayley graphs.

Theorem 2.17

(
[
LPS
]
)
.

For any prime
p
,
p\equiv 1\,(\mbox{mod }4)
, and any prime
q
,
q\equiv 1\,(\mbox{mod }4p)
, there exist an explicit construction of a symmetric generating subset
S_{p,q}\subset PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, of size
p+1
, such that the Cayley graph
\mbox{Cay}(PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),S_{p,q})
is Ramanujan, i.e.

\lambda\left(\mbox{Cay}(PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),S_{p,q})\right)\leq\frac{2\sqrt{p}}{p+1}.

(2.16)

The following Proposition, which we shall need in Section
4
, enables us more freedom in choosing the degrees of the Cayley graphs, at the price of making them only quasi-Ramanujan, i.e.
\lambda
-expander with
\lambda\leq\frac{c\sqrt{r-1}}{r}
, where
c
is an absolute constant and
r
is the degree.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
p
,
q
and
S_{p,q}\subset PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be as in Theorem
2.17
.
Then for any
p+1-\sqrt{p}\leq r\leq p+1
and any symmetric subset
S\subset S_{p,q}
of size
r=|S|
, we have

\lambda\left(\mbox{Cay}(PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),S)\right)\leq 5r^{-1/2}.

(2.17)

Proof.

Denote by
M=M_{S_{p,q}}
,
M^{\prime}=M_{S}
and
M^{\prime\prime}=M_{S_{p,q}\setminus S}=M-M^{\prime}
, the adjacency matrices of
\mbox{Cay}(G,S_{p,q})
,
\mbox{Cay}(G,S)
and
\mbox{Cay}(G,S_{p,q}\setminus S)
, respectively.
Since
\mbox{Cay}(G,S_{p,q})
is
|S_{p,q}|
-regular (resp.
\mbox{Cay}(G,S)
is
|S|
-regular, resp.
\mbox{Cay}(G,S_{p,q}\setminus S)
is
|S_{p,q}\setminus S|
-regular), the largest eigenvalue of
M
is
|S_{p,q}|
(resp.
M^{\prime}
is
|S|
, resp.
M^{\prime\prime}
is
|S_{p,q}\setminus S|
), with corresponding eigenvector the constant function
1_{G}
.
Denote by
\lambda=\lambda\left(\mbox{Cay}(G,S_{p,q})\right)
and
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda\left(\mbox{Cay}(G,S)\right)
the normalized second largest eigenvalue of
M
and
M^{\prime}
, respectively.
By the Courant-Fischer Formula we get

\lambda\cdot|S_{p,q}|=\max_{0\neq v\perp 1_{G}}\frac{v^{t}Mv}{v^{t}v},\quad\lambda^{\prime}\cdot|S|=\max_{0\neq v\perp 1_{G}}\frac{v^{t}M^{\prime}v}{v^{t}v}\quad\mbox{and}\quad|S_{p,q}\setminus S|=\max_{0\neq v}\frac{v^{t}M^{\prime\prime}v}{v^{t}v}.

(2.18)

Therefore

\lambda^{\prime}\cdot|S|=\max_{0\neq v\perp 1_{G}}\frac{v^{t}M^{\prime}v}{v^{t}v}\leq\max_{0\neq v\perp 1_{G}}\frac{v^{t}Mv}{v^{t}v}+\max_{0\neq v}\frac{v^{t}M^{\prime\prime}v}{v^{t}v}\leq\lambda\cdot|S_{p,q}|+|S_{p,q}\setminus S|,

(2.19)

and after dividing by
|S|
, and noting that
r\geq p+1-\sqrt{p}\geq\frac{p}{2}
, we get

\lambda^{\prime}\leq\lambda\frac{|S_{p,q}|}{|S|}+\frac{|S_{p,q}\setminus S|}{|S|}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{p}}{p+1}\frac{p+1}{r}+\frac{p+1-r}{r}\leq\frac{3\sqrt{p}}{r}\leq 5r^{-1/2}.

(2.20)

∎

3
Codes

Recall that a (binary, linear) error correcting code
C
is a subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, where
n=n(C)
is called the block-length of
C
.
Define the rate and the normalized distance of the code to be

\rho(C)=\frac{\dim(C)}{n}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\delta(C)=\frac{\min\{\mbox{wt}(v)\,:\,0\neq v\in C\}}{n},

(3.1)

where
\mbox{wt}(v)=|\{1\leq i\leq n\,:\,v_{i}\neq 0\}|
is the Hamming weight.
A family of codes is called good if their rates and normalized distances are uniformly bounded away from zero.

Let us now describe the Sipser-Spielman
[
SS
]
construction of expander codes for Cayley graphs, building on the work of Tanner
[
Tan
]
.
Let
G
be a group,
A\subset G
a symmetric subset of size
r=|A|
,
1\not\in A
, and let
\mbox{Cay}(A;G)=(V,E)
be the (left) Cayley graph.
Let
C_{0}\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{A}
be a code of length
r
.
For any
g\in V
, the map
a\leftrightarrow(a,g]
gives a bijection between
A
and
E(g)=\{e\in E\,:\,g\in e\}
.
For any
f\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{E}
and any
g\in V
, define the local view of
f
at
g
to be the restriction
f_{g}=f|_{E(g)}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{E(g)}\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}^{A}
.
Define the expander code w.r.t. the Cayley graph
\mbox{Cay}(A;G)
and the small code
C_{0}
, to be

C[G,A,C_{0}]=\{f\,:\,\mathbb{F}_{2}^{E}\;:\;\forall g\in V,\quad f_{g}\in C_{g}\},

(3.2)

where
C_{g}\cong C_{0}
is defined explicitly by

C_{g}=\left\{w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{E(g)}\left|\;\vphantom{w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{E(g)}w((\cdot,g])\in C_{0}}\right.w((\cdot,g])\in C_{0}\right\}.

(3.3)

In their work
[
SS
]
, Sipser and Spileman proved the following lower bounds on the rate and normalized distance of the expander codes, in terms of the parameters of the small code and the second largest eigenvalue (i.e. the expansion) of the graph.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
SS
]
)
.

Let
\mbox{Cay}(A;G)
be an
r
-regular
\lambda
-expander Cayley graph and
C_{0}
a code of length
r
.
Then the expander code
C[G,A,C_{0}]
is of length
n=\frac{r}{2}|G|
, with parameters

\rho(C[G,A,C_{0}])\geq 2\rho(C_{0})-1\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\delta(C[G,A,C_{0}])\geq\delta(C_{0})(\delta(C_{0})-\lambda).

(3.4)

In particular, if
\rho(C_{0})>\frac{1}{2}
,
\delta(C_{0})>\lambda
and
\mbox{Cay}(A;G)
runs over a family of
r
-regular
\lambda
-expander graphs, then the resulting family of expander codes is good.

For the sake of completeness we give a proof for Proposition
3.1
.

Proof.

Let
\rho_{0}=\rho(C_{0})
,
\delta_{0}=\delta(C_{0})
and
C=C[G,A,C_{0}]
.
The dimension of the code
C=C[G,A,C_{0}]
is at least the number of degrees of freedom,
|E|
, minus the number of constraints,
(r-\dim(C_{0}))|V|=2(1-\rho_{0})|E|
, hence
\rho(C)=\frac{\dim C[G,A,C_{0}]}{|E|}\geq 1-2(1-\rho_{0})=2\rho_{0}-1
.

Let
0\neq f\in C
be such that
\delta(C)=\frac{\mbox{wt}(f)}{|E|}
, where
wt
denotes the Hamming weight.
Denote
S=\{e\in E\,:\,f(e)\neq 0\}
and
R=\{g\in V\,:\,\exists e\in S\cap E(g)\}
.
For any
g\in R
, the local view
f_{g}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{E(g)}
is a non-zero codeword of
C_{0}
, hence
\mbox{wt}(f_{g})\geq\delta_{0}r
, which implies that there are
\delta_{0}r
neighbors of
g
inside
R
.
Let
T
be the normalized adjacency operator of
\mbox{Cay}(A;G)
, which is a symmetric, Markov and
\lambda
-expanding.
Then
\langle T1_{R},1_{R}\rangle=\sum_{g\in R}T1_{R}(g)=\sum_{g\in R}\frac{|\{a\in A\,:\,(a,g]\in E\}|}{r}\geq\delta_{0}|R|
, combined with Lemma
2.10
, gives us
|R|\geq(\delta_{0}-\lambda)|V|
.
Finally, since each
v\in R
is contained in at least
\delta_{0}r
edges from
S
, we get
\delta(C)|E|=\mbox{wt}(f)=|S|\geq\frac{r}{2}\delta_{0}|R|\geq\frac{r}{2}\delta_{0}(\delta_{0}-\lambda)|V|=\delta_{0}(\delta_{0}-\lambda)|E|
, hence
\delta(C)\geq\delta_{0}(\delta_{0}-\lambda)
.
∎

Let us now define our construction of left/right Cayley expander codes. Recall that
S(e),S(g)
are the squares containing an edge
e
or a vertex
g
respectively.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mbox{Cay}^{2}(A;G;B)=(V,E,S)
be a left/right Cayley complex,
r=|A|=|B|
.
Fix a bijection between
A\leftrightarrow B\leftrightarrow\{1,\ldots,r\}
. Let
C_{1}\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}^{A}\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}^{B}
be a code of length
r
, and define
C_{0}
to be its tensor code,

C_{0}=C_{1}\otimes C_{1}=\{f\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{A\times B}\;:\;\forall a\in A,\;f(a,\cdot)\in C_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\forall b\in B,\;f(\cdot,b)\in C_{1}\}.

(3.5)

Define for each edge
e\in E
the local code at
e
by

C_{e}=\left\{w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{S(e)}\left|\;\vphantom{w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{S(e)}w\circ\iota_{e}\in C_{1}}\right.w\circ\iota_{e}\in C_{1}\right\}.

(3.6)

Define for each vertex
g\in G
the local code at
g
by

C_{g}=\left\{w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{S(g)}\left|\;\vphantom{w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{S(g)}w\circ\iota_{g}\in C_{0}=C_{1}\otimes C_{1}}\right.w\circ\iota_{g}\in C_{0}=C_{1}\otimes C_{1}\right\}.

(3.7)

For any
f\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{S}
and any edge
e
define the local view
f_{e}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{S(e)}
of
f
at
e
to be the restriction of
f
to the squares containing
e
.
Define the first left/right Cayley expander code w.r.t. the left/right Cayley complex
\mbox{Cay}^{2}(A;G;B)
and the code
C_{1}
, to be

C[G,A,B,C_{1}]=\{f\,:\,\mathbb{F}_{2}^{S}\;:\;\forall e\in E,\quad f_{e}\in C_{e}\}.

(3.8)

For any
f\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{S}
and any
g\in V
, define the local view
f_{g}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{S(g)}
of
f
at
g
, denoted
f_{g}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{A\times B}
, to be the restriction of
f
to the squares containing
g
.
Define the second left/right Cayley expander code w.r.t. the left/right Cayley complex
\mbox{Cay}^{2}(A;G;B)
and the code
C_{0}
, to be

C^{\prime}[G,A,B,C_{0}]=\{f\,:\,\mathbb{F}_{2}^{S}\;:\;\forall g\in V,\quad f_{g}\in C_{g}\}.

(3.9)

First let us note that the two left/right Cayley expander codes are in fact equal.

Lemma 3.3
.

In the notations of Definition
3.2
,
C[G,A,B,C_{1}]=C^{\prime}[G,A,B,C_{0}]
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{S}
and let
g\in V
,
a\in A
and
b\in B
. Note that
S((a,g]),S([g,b))\subset S(g)
and in fact

\iota_{(a,g]}(b)=\iota_{g}(a,b)=\iota_{[g,b)}(a)
(this comes from the matching labels property, see Remark
2.5
).
So for example

f_{g}\circ\iota_{g}(a,\cdot)=f_{(a,g]}\circ\iota_{(a,g]}(\cdot)
.
Hence
f_{g}\in C_{g}
if and only if
f_{e}\in C_{e}
for any edge
e\in E
containing
g
.
This implies that
f_{g}\in C_{g}
for any
g\in V
if and only if
f_{e}\in C_{e}
for any edge
e\in E
, which proves the claim.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Note that
C[G,A,B,C_{1}]
has less constraints in its definition than
C^{\prime}[G,A,B,C_{0}]
.
Indeed
C[G,A,B,C_{1}]
has
r(1-\rho(C_{1}))
constraints on each edge, and since there are at most
r|G|
edges, we get at most
(1-\rho(C_{1}))r^{2}|G|
constraints,
while
C^{\prime}[G,A,B,C_{0}]
has
r^{2}(1-\rho(C_{1})^{2})
constraints on each vertex (since
\rho(C_{0})=\rho(C_{1})^{2}
), hence a total of
(1-\rho(C_{1})^{2})r^{2}|G|
constraints.
This redundancy of (short) constraints should be expected for any LTC as proven in
[
BGKSV
]
.

Next we prove the analogue of Proposition

for left/right Cayley expander codes.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\mbox{Cay}(A;G;B)
be a left/right Cayley complex with

which is a
\lambda
-expander and let
C_{1}
be a code of length
r
.
Then the left/right Cayley expander code
C[G,A,B,C_{1}]
is of length
n=|S|\geq\frac{r^{2}}{4}|G|
, with parameters

\rho(C[G,A,B,C_{1}])\geq 4\rho(C_{1})-3\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\delta(C[G,A,B,C_{1}])\geq\frac{1}{4}\delta(C_{1})^{2}(\delta(C_{1})-\lambda).

(3.10)

In particular, if
\rho(C_{1})>\frac{3}{4}
,
\delta(C_{1})>\lambda
and
\mbox{Cay}^{2}(A;G;B)
runs over a family of
r
-regular (i.e.
|A|=|B|=r
)
\lambda
-expanding left/right Cayley complexes, then the resulting family of left/right Cayley expander codes
C[G,A,B,C_{1}]
is good.

Proof.

The dimension of
C[G,A,B,C_{1}]
is at least the number of degrees of freedom,
|S|
, minus the number of constraints,
(1-\rho(C_{1}))r|E|
.
Note that
|S|\leq r|E|\leq 4|S|
, since each edge sits in exactly
r
squares, i.e.
r|E|=\sum_{e\in E}|S(e)|
, and each square contains at least
1
and at most
4
edges (see Remark
2.3
).
(If condition (TNC) holds then
r|E|=4|S|
.)
Therefore

\rho(C)=\frac{\dim C[G,A,B,C_{1}]}{|S|}\geq\frac{|S|-(1-\rho(C_{1}))r|E|}{|S|}\geq 1-4(1-\rho(C_{1}))=4\rho(C_{1})-3.

(3.11)

Let
0\neq f\in C
be such that
\delta(C)=\frac{\mbox{wt}(f)}{|S|}
.
Since
f\neq 0
, there exists
e\in E_{A}
such that
0\neq f_{e}\in C_{1}
, and if
B^{\prime}:=\{b\in B\,:\,f_{e}(b)\neq 0\}
, then
|B^{\prime}|\geq r\delta(C_{1})
.
For each
b\in B^{\prime}
, define
f^{b}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{E_{A}}
by
f^{b}((a,g])=f([a,g,b])
. It is well defined since
f^{b}((a,g])=f([a,g,b])=f([a^{-1},ag,b])=f^{b}((a^{-1},ag])
.
We view
f^{b}
as a function on the edges of the Cayley graph
Cay(A;G)
. It is easy to check that on the edges touching a vertex
g
the restriction of
f^{b}
is in
C_{1}
; so
f^{b}
is in the expander code
C[G,A,C_{1}]
defined in (
3.2
) w.r.t. the Cayley graph
\mbox{Cay}(A;G)
and the small code
C_{1}
.
Hence by Proposition
3.1
,
\mbox{wt}(f^{b})\geq\delta(C_{1})(\delta(C_{1})-\lambda)|E_{A}|
, for any
b\in B^{\prime}
.
Combining all of this, together with the fact that
r|E_{A}|=\frac{1}{2}r|E|\geq\frac{1}{2}|S|
, we get

\delta(C)=\frac{1}{|S|}\mbox{wt}(f)\geq\frac{1}{2|S|}\sum_{b\in B}\mbox{wt}(f^{b})\geq\frac{1}{2|S|}\sum_{b\in B^{\prime}}\mbox{wt}(f^{b})\geq\frac{1}{2|S|}\sum_{b\in B^{\prime}}\delta(C_{1})(\delta(C_{1})-\lambda)|E_{A}|\\
\geq\delta(C_{1})(\delta(C_{1})-\lambda)\frac{|B^{\prime}||E_{A}|}{2|S|}\geq\delta(C_{1})^{2}(\delta(C_{1})-\lambda)\frac{r|E_{A}|}{2|S|}\geq\frac{1}{4}\delta(C_{1})^{2}(\delta(C_{1})-\lambda).

(3.12)

∎

In the rest of this section we prove the existence of a base code with sufficiently good rate and normalized distance, and a form of local testability on its tensor code, called agreement testability, defined below.

Let
C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
be a code of length
r
and let
C\otimes C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}=M_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
be its tensor code, where by linearity,
f\in C\otimes C
if and only if
f(v,\cdot)\in C
and
f(\cdot,u)\in C
for any
1\leq v,u\leq r
.

Definition 3.6
.

For any two
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
define the following normalized distances,

d(f,g)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\{(v,u)\,:\,f(v,u)\neq g(v,u)\}|=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\mbox{wt}(f-g),

(3.13)

d_{row}(f,g)=\frac{1}{r}|\{v\,:\,f(v,\cdot)\neq g(v,\cdot)\}|\quad\mbox{and}\quad d_{col}(f,g)=\frac{1}{r}|\{u\,:\,f(\cdot,u)\neq g(\cdot,u)\}|.

(3.14)

Define the row-column distance of the pair
(f,g)
from the tensor code
C\otimes C
to be

d_{rc}\left((f,g),C\otimes C\right)=\frac{1}{2}\min_{w\in C\otimes C}\left(d_{row}(f,w)+d_{col}(g,w)\right).

(3.15)

Define the agreement testability parameter
\sigma(C)
of the tensor code of
C
to be,

\sigma(C)=\min\left\{\frac{d(f,g)}{d_{rc}\left((f,g),C\otimes C\right)}\,:\,f\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\otimes C,\,g\in C\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r},\,f\neq g\right\},

(3.16)

Lemma 3.7
.

For any code
C
,
\sigma(C)\leq 2
.

Proof.

We wish to show that
\sigma(C)\leq 2
, i.e. that
d(f,g)\leq\min_{w\in C\otimes C}\left(d_{row}(f,w)+d_{col}(g,w)\right)
, for any
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
.
Note that
d_{row}(f,g)=d_{row}(f-g,0)
,
d_{col}(f,g)=d_{col}(f-g,0)
and
d(f,g)=d(f-g,0)
.
In particular, if
\omega\in C\otimes C
is such that
\left(d_{row}(f,\omega)+d_{col}(g,\omega)\right)=\min_{w\in C\otimes C}\left(d_{row}(f,w)+d_{col}(g,w)\right)
, then by setting
f^{\prime}=f-\omega
and
g^{\prime}=g-\omega
, it suffices to prove
d(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})\leq d_{row}(f^{\prime},0)+d_{col}(g^{\prime},0)
.
By the triangle inequality,
d(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})\leq d(f^{\prime},0)+d(g^{\prime},0)
, hence it suffices to prove
d(f,0)\leq d_{row}(f,0)
and
d(f,0)\leq d_{col}(f,0)
, for any
f\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
.
We shall prove
d(f,0)\leq d_{row}(f,0)
, the proof of the other bound follows analogously.
Let
t=|\{1\leq i\leq r\,:\,f(i,\cdot)\neq 0\}|
, hence
d_{row}(f,0)=\frac{t}{r}
, and note that the non-zero coordinates of
f
must be contained in the
t
non-zero rows of
f
, i.e.
\mbox{wt}(f)\leq tr
, and therefore
d(f,g)\leq\frac{tr}{r^{2}}=\frac{t}{r}=d_{row}(f,0)
, as claimed.
∎

The following proposition guarantees the existence of a base code with sufficiently good rate, normalized distance, and agreement testability parameters.

Proposition 3.8
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta_{1},\sigma_{1}>0
, and an infinite family of explicitly constructed codes
\{C_{i}\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r_{i}}\}_{i}
, of even lengths
r_{i}\rightarrow\infty
, such that for any
i
,

\rho(C_{i})\geq 1-\epsilon,\qquad\delta(C_{i})\geq\delta_{1},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\sigma(C_{i})\geq\sigma_{1}.

(3.17)

In
[
DELLM
, § 5.1]
it was shown that most random LDPC codes satisfy the proposition.
Here we shall give a more explicit proof, by using expander codes.
Both
[
DELLM
]
and our proof relies on the notion of smooth codes introduced and studied in
[
DSW
]
and
[
BV
]
.

Let us fix some notations.
For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, denote
[r]=\{1,\ldots,r\}
,
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}=\{f\,:\,[r]\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}\}
and
\cdot\,:\,\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\times\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
,
f\cdot g=\sum_{k\in[r]}f(k)g(k)
.
For any
H\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
and
d\leq r
, denote
H^{\perp}=\{g\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\,:\,\forall f\in H,\;f\cdot g=0\}
,
H_{\leq d}=\{f\in H\,:\,\mbox{wt}(f)\leq d\}
and
H_{\leq d}^{\perp}=\{g\in H^{\perp}\,:\,\mbox{wt}(g)\leq d\}
.

If
C
is a code then
C^{\perp}
is its set of constraints and
C_{\leq d}^{\perp}
is its subset of short (of weight at most
d
) constraints.
Recall that a code
C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
is called a
d
-LDPC (low density parity check) code if
C=(C^{\perp}_{\leq d})^{\perp}
, i.e. it is defined by its short constraints.

Below we give a slight strengthening of the definition of the notion of smooth codes from
[
DSW
]
, which we call uniformly smooth codes.
(In the original definition of
[
DSW
]
, the upper bound on the set
J
is proportional only to
r
, as opposed to
|I|
.)

Definition 3.9
.

Let
0<\alpha,\beta,\delta<1
,
d\leq r\in\mathbb{N}
and
C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
a code.
For any
I,J\subset[r]
, denote

C_{\leq d}^{\perp}(I)=\{f\in C_{\leq d}^{\perp}\,:\,f|_{I}\equiv 0\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad C(I,J)=\{f|_{[r]\setminus J}\,:\,f\in(C_{\leq d}^{\perp}(I))^{\perp}\}.

(3.18)

The code
C
is called
(\alpha,\beta,\delta,d)
-US (uniformly smooth), if
C
is a
d
-LDPC code and

\forall I\subset[r],\quad|I|\leq\alpha r,\quad\exists J\subset[r],\quad I\subset J,\quad|J|\leq\beta^{-1}|I|\qquad\mbox{with}\qquad\delta(C(I,J))\geq\delta.

(3.19)

Let us spell out the definition.
If
C
is a
d
-LDPC code then
C_{\leq d}^{\perp}
is the set of short constraints of
C
.
Then
C_{\leq d}^{\perp}(I)\subset C_{\leq d}^{\perp}
is the subset of short constraints supported on
[r]\setminus I
, hence
(C_{\leq d}^{\perp}(I))^{\perp}
is a code that contains
C=(C_{\leq d}^{\perp})^{\perp}
, and
C(I,J)
is obtained from this larger code by restricting its codewords to
[r]\setminus J
.
Call
C
uniformly smooth, if for any small set
I
, there is a small set
J
(small relative to
I
), such that
C(I,J)
has good distance.

Observe that if
C
is
(\alpha,\beta,\delta,d)
-US then
\delta(C)\geq\delta
.
Indeed, if we take
I=\emptyset
, then in the definition of US code
J=\emptyset
since
|J|\leq\beta^{-1}|I|
, and note that
C(\emptyset,\emptyset)=C
, hence
\delta(C)\geq\delta
.

The following Lemma shows that uniformly smooth codes have tensor codes which are agreement testable.
The proof essentially follows from the work of the first author with Sudan and Wigderson
[
DSW
]
, but for the sake of completeness we give it here.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
0<\alpha,\beta,\delta<1
be such that
\alpha\beta^{-1}<\min\{\frac{1}{2},\delta\}
and let
2\leq d\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
is a
(\alpha,\beta,\delta,d)
-US code, then
\sigma(C)\geq\frac{\alpha\delta}{d}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\otimes C
and
g\in C\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
such that
f\neq g
.
Let
m=f+g\in M_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, and for any
i\in[r]
, denote by
m(i,*),m(*,i)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
the
i
-th row and column of
m
, respectively.
Let
\sigma:=\frac{\mbox{wt}(m)}{r^{2}}=d(f,g)
and assume
\sigma<\sigma_{0}:=\frac{\alpha\delta}{d}
(otherwise there is nothing to prove, since
d_{rc}\left((f,g),C\otimes C\right)\leq 1
for any
f
and
g
).

Let
I_{2}=\{i\in[r]\,:\,\mbox{wt}(m(*,i))\geq\frac{\delta r}{d}\}
.
Note that
|I_{2}|\leq\frac{d\sigma r^{2}}{\delta r}
, and in particular
|I_{2}|<\alpha r
.
Since
C
is
(\alpha,\beta,\delta,d)
-US, there exists
J_{2}\subset[r]
such that
I_{2}\subset J_{2}
and
|J_{2}|\leq\beta^{-1}|I_{2}|
and such that
\delta(C(I_{2},J_{2}))\geq\delta
.
Define
J_{1}=\{j\in[r]\,:\,m(j,*)|_{[r]\setminus J_{2}}\neq 0\}
and
S=([r]\setminus J_{1})\times([r]\setminus J_{2})
.

For any
v\in C_{\leq d}^{\perp}(I_{2})
, then
m\cdot v=0
.
Indeed,
\mbox{wt}(m\cdot v)<\delta r
(by the definition of
I_{2}
),
f\cdot v=\sum_{v_{i}\neq 0}f(*,i)\in C
(since
f\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\otimes C
),
m\cdot v=f\cdot v
(since
m-f=g\in C\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
), and
\delta(C)\geq\delta
(since
C
is
(\alpha,\beta,\delta,d)
-US).
Therefore
m(j,*)|_{[r]\setminus J_{2}}\in C(I_{2},J_{2})
for any
j\in[r]
.
By the definition of
J_{1}
and since
\delta(C(I_{2},J_{2}))\geq\delta
, we get
\delta(r-|J_{2}|)|J_{1}|\leq\sum_{j\in J_{1}}\mbox{wt}(m(j,*))\leq\mbox{wt}(m)
, and since
|J_{2}|\leq\beta^{-1}|I_{2}|\leq\beta^{-1}\alpha r\leq\frac{r}{2}
, we get
|J_{1}|\leq\frac{2r\sigma}{\delta}
, and in particular
|J_{1}|<\frac{2\alpha}{d}r<\delta r
.

Next we shall prove that there exists
w\in C\otimes C
, such that
f|_{S}=w|_{S}=g|_{S}
.
By the definition of
J_{1}
, we get that
m|_{S}=0
, hence
f|_{S}=g|_{S}
.
Denote
C^{\otimes 2}=C\otimes C
,
C^{\otimes 2}_{S}=C|_{[r]\setminus J_{1}}\otimes C|_{[r]\setminus J_{2}}
and
\mbox{pr}_{S}\,:\,C^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow C^{\otimes 2}_{S}
,
\mbox{pr}_{S}(f)=f|_{S}
.
If
w\in C^{\otimes 2}
is such that
\mbox{pr}_{S}(w)=0
, then
w(i,*)|_{[r]\setminus J_{2}}\equiv 0
and
w(*,i)|_{[r]\setminus J_{1}}\equiv 0
for any
i\in[r]
.
Since
|J_{1}|<\delta r
,
|J_{2}|<\delta r
and
\delta(C)\geq\delta
, we get that
w(i,*)\equiv 0
and
w(*,i)\equiv 0
for any
i\in[r]
, hence
\mbox{pr}_{S}
is injective.
Since
\dim C^{\otimes 2}_{S}\leq\dim C^{\otimes 2}
, we get that
\mbox{pr}_{S}
is an isomorphism.
Note that
f|_{S}=g|_{S}\in C^{\otimes 2}_{S}
, and therefore it is the image of a (unique)
w\in C^{\otimes 2}
under
\mbox{pr}_{S}
, i.e.
f|_{S}=w|_{S}=g|_{S}
.

Finally, we give an upper bound on
d_{rc}\left((f,g),C\otimes C\right)\leq 1
in terms of
\sigma=d(f,g)
.
Since
f\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}\otimes C
,
g\in C\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}^{r}
,
w\in C\otimes C
and
f|_{S}=w|_{S}=g|_{S}
, ,and since
|J_{1}|<\delta r
,
|J_{2}|<\delta r
and
\delta(C)\geq\delta
, then
f
agrees with
w
on all rows outside
J_{1}
and
g
agrees with
w
on all columns outside
J_{2}
, i.e.
d_{row}(f,w)\leq\frac{|J_{1}|}{r}
, and
d_{col}(g,w)\leq\frac{|J_{2}|}{r}
.
Since
|J_{1}|\leq\frac{2}{\delta}\cdot r\sigma
and
|J_{2}|\leq\beta^{-1}|I_{2}|\leq\frac{d}{\beta\delta}\cdot r\sigma
, and since
\sigma_{0}<\frac{\beta\delta}{d}<\frac{\delta}{2}
, we get that
\frac{|J_{i}|}{r}\leq\sigma_{0}^{-1}\sigma=\sigma_{0}^{-1}\cdot d(f,g)
, for
i=1,2
.
Therefore,

d_{rc}\left((f,g),C\otimes C\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(d_{row}(f,w)+d_{col}(g,w)\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|J_{1}|}{r}+\frac{|J_{2}|}{r}\right)\leq\sigma_{0}^{-1}\cdot d(f,g).

(3.20)

∎

The next Lemma shows that expander codes are uniformly smooth.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
X=(V,E)
be a
d
-regular graph, for any
v\in V
, let
C_{v}\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{E_{v}}
be a local code around
v
, where
E_{v}=\{e\in E\,:\,v\in e\}
, and let
C=\left\{f\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{E}\;:\;\forall v\in V,\quad f|_{E_{v}}\in C_{v}\right\}
.
If
X
is a
\lambda
-expander,
\delta_{0}=\min_{v\in V}\delta(C_{v})
and
\lambda<\frac{\delta_{0}}{4}
, then
C
is an
(\frac{\delta_{0}}{8d},\frac{1}{4d},\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{8},d)
-US code.

Proof.

First note that
C
is an expander code w.r.t. the graph
X
and the local codes
\{C_{v}\}_{v\in V}
, and since
X
is
d
-regular the code is a
d
-LDPC code.
Next, recall that by the Alon-Chung Lemma (Lemma
2.10
), if
U\subset V
has an average degree
\kappa d
, where
0\leq\kappa\leq 1
, then
|U|\geq(\kappa-\lambda)|V|
.

Let
I\subset E
be a subset of edges of size
|I|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{8d}|E|
.
Let
U_{0}\subset V
be the subset of vertices which touch an edge from
I
.
Define
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots
, iteratively as follows:
If
U_{i-1}
is already defined and there exists
v_{i}\not\in U_{i-1}
with more than
\frac{\delta_{0}d}{2}
neighbours in
U_{i-1}
, then define
U_{i}=U_{i-1}\cup\{v_{i}\}
.
This process must stop after at most
t=|U_{0}|
steps.
Otherwise the set
U_{t}
has
2t
vertices and at least
\frac{\delta_{0}d}{2}t
edges, i.e. an average degree of at least
\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}d
, which by the Alon-Chung Lemma implies that
|U_{t}|\geq(\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}-\lambda)|V|>\frac{\delta_{0}}{4}|V|
, contradicting the fact that
|U_{t}|=2|U_{0}|\leq 4|I|=\frac{\delta_{0}}{2d}|E|=\frac{\delta_{0}}{4}|V|
.

Let
U\subset V
be the final set in the above process and let
J=\{e\in E\,:\,\exists u\in U,\,u\in e\}
.
Then: (i)
I\subset J
, (ii)
|J|\leq d|U|\leq 2d|U_{0}|\leq 4d|I|
, and (iii)
|E_{v}\cap J|<\frac{\delta_{0}d}{2}
, for any
v\in V\setminus U
.

Let
0\neq f^{\prime}\in C(I,J)
be such that
\delta(C(I,J))=\frac{\mbox{wt}(f^{\prime})}{|E\setminus J|}
and let
f\in(C_{\leq d}^{\perp}(I))^{\perp}
be such that
f^{\prime}=f|_{E\setminus J}
. Let us write
f^{\prime}(e)=0
for all
e\in J
, and set
R=\{v\in V\,:\,f^{\prime}|_{E_{v}}\neq 0\}
.
Since
\mbox{supp}(f^{\prime})\subset E\setminus J
, by the definition of
U_{0}
,
U
and
J
, we get that
v\not\in U
, in particular
v\not\in U_{0}
, which implies
E_{v}\cap I=\emptyset
, hence the constraints of
C_{v}
belongs to
C_{\leq d}^{\perp}(I)
, therefore
f^{\prime}|_{E_{v}}\in C_{v}
.
By property (iii) of
J
,
|E_{v}\cap J|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}d}{2}
, therefore
\mbox{wt}(f^{\prime}|_{E_{v}\setminus J})\geq\mbox{wt}(f|_{E_{v}})-|E_{v}\cap J|\geq\delta_{0}d-\frac{\delta_{0}d}{2}\geq\frac{\delta_{0}d}{2}
, i.e.
R
has an average degree of at least
\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}d
, and by the Alon-Chung Lemma,
|R|\geq(\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}-\lambda)|V|>\frac{\delta_{0}}{4}|V|
.
Hence,
\delta(C(I,J))=\frac{1}{2|E\setminus J|}\sum_{v\in R}\mbox{wt}(f^{\prime}|_{E_{v}\setminus J})\geq\frac{\delta_{0}d|R|}{4|E|}\geq\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}d|V|}{16|E|}>\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{8}
, which, combined with properties (i) and (ii) of
J
, proves the Lemma.
∎

.

In this section we state and prove the main result of this paper, namely, constructing locally testable codes (LTCs), with constant rate, constant normalized distance and constant query complexity.

Theorem 4.1

Let

(4.1)

Our codes, just like the Sipser-Spielman codes

Before proving Theorem

Corollary 4.2

Let

There exists

and

, and an infinite family of locally testable codes

Proof.

These codes are obtained from the codes in our main theorem via two local-transformation steps.

[

to obtain LTCs with large constant alphabet and parameters approaching the Singleton bound.
The second step, due to

of concatenation with a random invertible linear transformation, to get a binary code with rate and distance approaching the Gilbert-Varshamov bound.
∎

The codes in Theorem

The local tester is defined as follows,

Definition 4.3

Let

be the left/right Cayley expander code w.r.t. the left/right Cayley complex

Theorem 4.5

Let

(4.5)

In order to prove Theorem

we introduce the following correction algorithm.

Algorithm 4.6
.

On input

perform the following algorithm.

Start:

For each
g
, let

be chosen to be the closest element to
f|_{S(g)}
in
C_{g}
(break ties arbitrarily), and let
W=(W_{g})_{g\in G}
be the collection of local views.
Define the number of disagreeing edges in

by

\Delta(W)=|\{e=[g;\ell]\in E\,:\,W_{g}|_{S(e)}\neq W_{g^{\ell}}|_{S(e)}\}|.

(4.6)

Loop:

If there exists some
g\in G
and a codeword
w\in C_{g}
such that replacing
W_{g}
by
w
reduces
\Delta(W)
then replace
W_{g}
accordingly and repeat. If there is no such

, continue to the End step.

End:

If
\Delta(W)>0
, output “far”, otherwise output

F\,:\,S\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2},\qquad F(s)=W_{g}(s),\qquad\forall s\in S,\quad\forall g\in s.

(4.7)

Note that if Algorithm
4.6
ends with
\Delta(W)=0
, then the definition of

does not depend on the choice of
g\in s
for any

.
Indeed, since
\Delta(W)=0
, for any
s\in S

If they are not connected by an edge, then there is a third vertex so that

is a length two path contained in

.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
W^{0}=(W^{0}_{g})_{g\in G}
be the initial collection of local views in the start step of Algorithm

.
The algorithm terminates after at most
\Delta(W^{0})

\Delta(W^{0})\leq 2D(f)|E|.

(4.8)

Proof.

Since each loop step in the algorithm reduces

by at least

.
Note that if

then

, so for any edge

W^{0}_{g}=f|_{S(g)}
and
(W^{0})_{g^{\ell}}=f|_{S(g^{\ell})}

Therefore, if

contributes to the count of

, then either
g
or

contribute to the count of

, and since each vertex can be counted at most

times this way, we get that
\Delta(W^{0})\leq 2rD(f)|V|=2D(f)|E|
.
∎

, for any

, there exists a Ramanujan generating subset
S_{i}\subset G_{i}=PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
of size
p+1
, and by Proposition
2.18
, for any symmetric subset
A_{i}\subset S_{i}
of size
r=|A_{i}|
, the left/right Cayley complex
\mbox{Cay}^{2}(A_{i};G_{i};A_{i})
is a
\lambda
-expander for
\lambda=5r^{-1/2}\leq\frac{\sigma_{1}\delta_{1}}{20}
.
By Lemma
3.7
,
\sigma_{1}\leq 2
, hence
\lambda\leq\frac{\delta_{1}}{10}
and
\frac{\sigma_{1}\delta_{1}}{16+\sigma_{1}}\geq\frac{\sigma_{1}\delta_{1}}{18}
.
Define our family of codes to be the left/right Cayley expander codes
\{C_{i}=C[G_{i},A_{i},A_{i},C_{1}]\}_{i=2}^{\infty}
, of lengths
n(C_{i})\geq\frac{|A_{i}|^{2}|G_{i}|}{4}=\frac{1}{4}r^{2}(q_{i}^{3}-q_{i})\rightarrow\infty
.
By Theorem
3.5
, we get

\rho(C_{i})\geq 4\rho(C_{1})-3\geq 1-\epsilon\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\delta(C_{i})\geq\frac{1}{4}\delta_{1}^{2}(\delta_{1}-\lambda)\geq\frac{9}{40}\delta_{1}^{3}=:\delta_{\epsilon}

(4.35)

and by Theorem
4.5
we get

q(C_{i})=r^{2}=:q_{\epsilon}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\kappa(C_{i})=\frac{1}{4r}\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}\delta_{1}}{16+\sigma_{1}}-\lambda\right)\geq\frac{\delta_{1}\sigma_{1}}{720r}:=\kappa_{\epsilon}

(4.36)

which completes the proof of the Theorem.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

The constants in Theorem
4.1
depends on
\epsilon
poly-logarithmically.

5
High dimensional expanders: suggestions for further research

The current paper is mainly elementary and almost self-contained.
But it came up as a result of a much longer and intensive journey.
Some interesting open problems were left aside along the way.
It is, therefore, worthwhile to give the story here.

Although expander codes are typically not locally testable
[
BHR
]
the hope was that higher dimensional versions would be.
This optimistic belief was inspired by local to global expansion behavior of certain high dimensional simplicial complexes that was uncovered already by Garland in his seminal work
[
Gar
]
.

In
[
Gar
]
, Garland proved a conjecture of Serre, predicting the vanishing of the cohomology of co-compact lattices in high-rank simple
p
-adic groups.
Equivalently, if
X
is a finite simplicial quotient of a Bruhat-Tits building of dimension
d\geq 2
, its cohomology vanishes in dimensions
1\leq k\leq d-1
.
The proof of Garland is “local-to-global”: he showed that if the links of
(d-2)
-dimensional faces have (large) spectral gap, then so do the global Laplacians of
X
.
Namely, if
X
is locally an expander, then it is also globally so.
(For a purely combinatorial treatment and generalizations, see
[
Opp
]
).
Thus, the local spectral gap implies the vanishing of the cohomology.

This “local to global” approach is a high-dimensional phenomenon that does not hold for graphs!
In graphs, the local structure does not reveal any information about the global expansion.
To illustrate this, the reader may recall the LPS Ramanujan graphs
[
LPS
]
which are
(p+1)
-regular expander graphs with large girth.
One can easily get
(p+1)
-regular graphs with large girth (and hence locally isomorphic to the LPS ones) which are far from being expanders.
On the other hand, the Garland method shows that (strong) local expansion implies global expansion in the high dimensional case.

Inspired by all this, the idea was to construct LTC by using the local-to-global behavior of the Ramanujan complexes (
[
LSV1
,
LSV2
]
) in an analog to the way
[
SS
]
used Ramanujan graphs for LDPC codes.
For simplicity, we will describe it from now on only in dimension
2
, but one can do the same in higher dimensions.
We shall present two possible constructions of codes on Ramanujan complexes, which we call high dimensional arithmetic codes and high dimensional expander codes.

5.1
High dimensional arithmetic codes

Our first code will be defined using the theory of
p
-adic uniformization of Shimura varieties (see
[
Var
]
).
Recall that in
[
Mum
]
, Mumford used the theory of
p
-adic uniformization and deep result of Yau
[
Yau
]
, to construct a (connected component of a) Shimura surface appearing as a locally symmetric quotient of
PU(2,1)
, whose topology is related to the combinatorial structure of a specific quotient of a Bruhat-Tits building of
PGL_{3}(\mathbb{Q}_{2})
.
Our plan was to go in the opposite direction and to use the theory of Shimura surfaces to study finite quotients of Bruhat-Tits buildings.

Let
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{B}
be a finite quotient of the Bruhat-Tits building
\mathcal{B}
of
PGL_{3}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
by a cocompact torsion-free lattice
\Gamma\leq PGL_{3}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\mbox{Aut}(\mathcal{B})
.
We suppose that
\Gamma
is given as a congruence subgroup of a projective similitude unitary group as follows.
Let
G=PGU(A,\sigma)
be a projective similitude unitary algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
w.r.t. the matrix algebra
A=M_{3}(E)
,
E/\mathbb{Q}
an imaginary quadratic field, assumed for simplicity to be of class number one, such that
p=\mathfrak{p}\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
splits in
E
, and an involution of the second type
\sigma(g)=H^{-1}g^{*}H
, where
H
is a definite non-degenerate Hermitian matrix.
Then
\Gamma=G(\mathbb{Q})\cap K^{p,\infty}
, for some compact open
\{p,\infty\}
-adelic subgroup
K^{p,\infty}\leq G(\mathbb{A}^{p,\infty})
, where
\mathbb{A}^{p,\infty}=\prod^{\prime}_{v\neq p,\infty}\mathbb{Q}_{v}
is the ring of
\{p,\infty\}
-adeles of
\mathbb{Q}
.
Note that
\Gamma\leq G(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is a cocompact lattice, and since
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cong PGL_{3}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
G(\mathbb{R})\cong PU(3)
is compact,
\Gamma
is a cocompact lattice in
PGL_{3}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

By
[
Mum
, Section 1]
, there is a smooth projective scheme
\mathcal{X}
defined over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, satisfying the following.
Let
\bar{\mathcal{X}}=\mathcal{X}\,(\,\mbox{mod}\,p\,)
, be the special fiber of
\mathcal{X}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, which is a reducible algebraic surface whose irreducible components are isomorphic to
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, the blow up of the projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at the
p^{2}+p+1
rational points (so that each rational point is replaced by a copy of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
).
Let
\Delta(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
be the dual complex of
\bar{\mathcal{X}}
, i.e. the simplicial complex whose vertices are the irreducible components of
\bar{\mathcal{X}}
, two (resp. three) of which are connected by an edge (resp. form a triangle) if their intersection is non-empty.
Then
\Delta(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
is isomorphic to the quotient of the Bruhat-Tits building
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{B}
.

Using the theory of
p
-adic uniformization of Shimura varieties (see
[
Var
,
RZ
]
),
\mathcal{X}
can be described in the following way.
Let
G^{\prime}=PGU(A^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
be a projective similitude unitary algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
w.r.t.
A^{\prime}=D
the unique division algebra of degree
3
over
E
which ramifies precisely at the two places above
p
, and
\sigma^{\prime}
an involution of the second type whose signature at infinity is
(2,1)
.
Note that
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})=PU(2,1)
,
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=D_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
is compact and
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}^{p,\infty})\cong G(\mathbb{A}^{p,\infty})
.
Let
\Gamma^{\prime}=G^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})\cap K^{\infty}
, where
K^{\infty}=K_{p}K^{p,\infty}\leq G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}^{\infty})
,
K^{p,\infty}\leq G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}^{p,\infty})
is the level defining
\Gamma
above and
K_{p}=\mathcal{O}_{D_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{*}\leq G^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.
Let
X^{\prime}=\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
be the compact quotient of the
2
-dimensional complex unit ball
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, by
\Gamma^{\prime}\leq PU(2,1)=\mbox{Aut}(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})
.
By the theory of Shimura varieties, there exists an algebraic surface scheme
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
, defined over the ring of integers of some number field, such that the complex manifold
X^{\prime}=\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is a connected component of
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})
, and such that by taking a base change to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
we get our previous scheme
\mathcal{X}
.

In summary, our finite quotient of the Bruhat-Tits building
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{B}
, can be identified with the dual complex of a connected (since
E
is of class number one) reducible algebraic Shimura surface
\bar{\mathcal{X}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
, i.e.
X\cong\Delta(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
.
For any vertex
v\in X
(resp. edge
e=\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\in X
), denote by
\mathbb{P}_{v}
(resp.
\mathbb{P}_{e}
) the corresponding component in
\bar{\mathcal{X}}
(resp. the intersection of
\mathbb{P}_{v_{1}}
and
\mathbb{P}_{v_{2}}
).
Note that
\mathbb{P}_{v}\cong\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, the blow up of the projective plane at the
p^{2}+p+1

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-rational points, and
\mathbb{P}_{e}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the projective line.
Moreover, for any edge
e=\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\in X
, the line
\mathbb{P}_{e}
, appears in one of the two components,
\mathbb{P}_{v_{i}}
,
i=1,2
, as an ’old’ line (from
\mathbb{P}^{2}
) and in the other as a ’new’ line (i.e. a blow up of a point from
\mathbb{P}^{2}
).

Next we define the local codes, i.e. the codes that live on the links of of the complex.

Definition 5.1
.

(i) Let
\Omega^{1}=\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
be the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space of
1
-forms on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
which have only simple poles and only on the points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, the set of
p+1

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-rational points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
For any
t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, define the linear map
\mbox{res}_{t}\,:\,\Omega^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
\mbox{res}_{t}(\omega)
the residue of
\omega
at
t
, for any
\omega\in\Omega^{1}
, and define
\mbox{res}_{1}=\oplus_{t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}\mbox{res}_{t}\,:\,\Omega^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}
.

(ii) Let
\Omega^{2}=\Omega^{2}(\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2})
be the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space of
2
-forms on
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, which have only simple poles and only along the set of
2(p^{2}+p+1)

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-rational lines
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.
Note that
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})=\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{old}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\sqcup\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{new}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, where
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{old}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
are the ’old’ lines and
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{new}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
are the ’new’ lines.
For any line
e\in\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, define the linear map
\mbox{res}_{e}\,:\,\Omega^{2}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}
,
\mbox{res}_{e}(\omega)
is the residue of
\omega
along
e
.
Define
\mbox{res}_{2}=\oplus_{e\in\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{old}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}\mbox{res}_{e}\,:\,\Omega^{2}\rightarrow(\Omega^{1})^{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{old}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}
, and
\mbox{res}=\mbox{res}_{1}\circ\mbox{res}_{2}\,:\,\Omega^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\times\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{old}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}
.

We remark that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\Omega^{1}=p
,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\Omega^{2}=p^{3}
and
\mbox{res}\,:\,\Omega^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}^{(p+1)(p^{2}+p+1)}
is injective.

Finally, let us define the global code.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\Omega^{2}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
be the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space of
2
-forms on the algebraic surface
\bar{\mathcal{X}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
whose restrictions to each component
\mathbb{P}_{v}
,
v
a vertex in
X
, belong to
\Omega^{2}(\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2})
.
If
N
is the number of vertices of
X
, then we shall consider
\Omega^{2}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
as the following
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear subspace

\Omega^{2}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})\leq\oplus_{v}\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{P}_{v})\cong\Omega^{2}(\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{\oplus N}\leq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{(p+1)(p^{2}+p+1)N}.

(5.1)

We remark that for any
\omega\in\Omega^{(}\bar{\mathcal{X}})
and any edge
e=\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\in X
, the following holds

\mbox{res}_{e}\left(\omega|_{\mathbb{P}_{v_{1}}}\right)=-\mbox{res}_{e}\left(\omega|_{\mathbb{P}_{v_{2}}}\right).

(5.2)

Therefore the number of constraints of the code
\Omega^{2}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
is
(p+1)(p^{2}+p+1)N
, where
p+1
is the number of constraints on a single edge and
(p^{2}+p+1)N
is the number of edges in
X
.
This is equal to the number of degrees of freedom of
\Omega^{2}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})\leq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{(p+1)(p^{2}+p+1)N}
.
Hence the constraints counting argument of
[
SS
]
is of no use here, since it does not give any useful lower bound on the dimension of
\Omega^{2}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
.

Instead, by interpreting
\Omega^{2}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
in terms of the cohomology of
\mathcal{X}
and arguing similarly to
[
Mum
]
, but in the opposite direction (and relying on the result of Kazhdan
[
Kaz
]
), one can prove that the dimension of
\Omega^{2}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
grows linearly with
N
.

Theorem 5.3
.

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\Omega^{2}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})=(p-1)^{2}(p+1)N/3-1
.

Unfortunately, one can show that, after possibly passing to a finite cover of
X
, the Hamming distance of the code
\Omega^{2}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
is
O(N^{1/4})
.
This is due to the existence of an element in
\Omega^{2}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
which is supported on a single apartment and the fact that, for principal congruence subgroups
\Gamma
, the size of the image of an apartment in
X
is bounded by
|X|^{1/4}
.
In particular, this implies that the distance of the code is not good.

To overcome this, one might consider supplementing the above construction with a variant of the Sipser-Spielman method
[
SS
]
.
Namely, in Definition
5.1
, consider taking certain subspaces of
\Omega^{1}
, with good distance and rate.
This naturally leads us to our second code construction, which is much simpler to describe.

5.2
High dimensional expander codes

Let
X=(V,E,T)
be a finite
2
-dimensional simplicial complex.
For any
v\in V
and
e\in E
, define the sets
E(v)=\{e\in E\,:\,v\in e\}
,
T(v)=\{t\in T\,:\,v\in t\}
and
T(e)=\{t\in T\,:\,e\subset t\}
.
Define the link of
X
at
e
, to be the set
X_{e}=T(e)
.
Define the link of
X
at
v
to be the graph
X_{v}
, whose set of vertices is
E(v)
and two vertices
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(v)
forms an edge if
e_{1}\cup e_{2}\in T(v)
.

Say that
X
is
r
-regular (from the edges to triangles) if
|X_{e}|=r
for any
e\in E
.
Denote
[r]=\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
.
A labelling of
X
is a collection of bijections
\Phi=\{\phi_{e}\,:\,X_{e}\rightarrow[r]\}_{e\in E}
.
For any
e\in E
and
v\in V
, denote by
\Phi_{e}=\{\phi_{e}\}
and
\Phi_{v}=\{\phi_{e}\}_{v\in e\in E}
, respectively.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
X=(V,E,T)
be a finite
2
-dimensional
r
-regular simplicial complex,
\Phi=\{\phi_{e}\,:\,X_{e}\rightarrow[r]\}_{e\in E}
a labelling of
X
, and
C_{1}\leq\Sigma^{[r]}
a code of length
r
over the alphabet set
\Sigma
.

For any
e\in E
, define the (small) code on the link of
X
at
e
, to be

C_{e}=C[X_{e},\Phi_{e},C_{1}]=\left\{f\in\Sigma^{T(e)}\;:\;f\circ\phi_{e}^{-1}\in C_{1}\right\}.

(5.3)

For any
v\in V
, define the (intermediate) code on the link of
X
at
v
, to be

C_{v}=C[X_{v},\Phi_{v},C_{1}]=\left\{f\in\Sigma^{T(v)}\;:\;\forall v\in e\in E,\quad f|_{T(e)}\circ\phi_{e}^{-1}\in C_{1}\right\}.

(5.4)

Finally, define the (global) code on
X
, to be

C=C[X,\Phi,C_{1}]=\left\{f\in\Sigma^{T}\;:\;\forall e\in E,\quad f|_{T(e)}\circ\phi_{e}^{-1}\in C_{1}\right\}.

(5.5)

Let us now show how the rate and distance arguments of
[
SS
]
can be extended to the code
C=C[X,\Phi,C_{1}]
, under some assumptions on
X
and
C_{1}
.

If
\Sigma
is a field and
C_{1}
is a linear code, then the codes
C_{e}
for any
e\in E
,
C_{v}
for any
v\in V
and
C
are linear codes.
Assume
C_{1}
is a linear code of rate
\rho(C_{1})>\frac{2}{3}
.
By arguing as in
[
SS
]
we get that the rate of the code
C=C[X,\Phi,C_{1}]
(for any choice of
\Phi
), is lower bounded by

\rho(C)\geq 3\rho(C_{1})-2.

(5.6)

Let
X_{0}
be a fixed graph and assume that
X_{v}\cong X_{0}
for any vertex
v\in V
.
Then
C_{v}\cong C_{0}
for any
v\in V
, where
C_{0}=C[X_{0},C_{1}]
is the expander code of
[
SS
]
w.r.t. the graph
X_{0}
and the base code
C_{1}
.
Then arguing as in
[
SS
]
, we get that the normalized distance of the code
C=C[X,\Phi,C_{1}]
, is lower bounded by

\delta(C)\geq\delta(C_{0})(\delta(C_{0})-\lambda(X))\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\delta(C_{0})\geq\delta(C_{1})(\delta(C_{1})-\lambda(X_{0})).

(5.7)

Hence, if
C_{1}
is linear code of rate
>\frac{2}{3}
, the rate of argument of
[
SS
]
can be applied to our codes, and if
X
is a good enough high dimensional expander, then the distance argument of
[
SS
]
can be applied to our codes.

Question 5.5
.

Are there
X
,
\Phi
and
C_{1}
satisfying the above properties, such that
C[X,\Phi,C_{1}]
is a locally testable code?

Let us be more concrete with our candidates of
X
.
Fix a large prime
p
and take an infinite family of Ramanujan complexes
X
, quotients of the Bruhat-Tits building of
G=PGL_{3}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.
The complex
X=(V,E,T)
is a finite
2
-dimensional simplicial complex, such that
X_{e}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
for any
e\in E
, where
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the projective line over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
(i.e.
X
is
(p+1)
-regular), and
X_{v}\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}
for any
v\in V
, where
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is the graph of lines versus points of the projective plane over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Then
\lambda(X_{v})=\lambda(\mathbb{P}^{2})=p^{-1/2}
for any
v\in V
, and since
X
is Ramanujan
\lambda(X)\leq 6p^{-1}
(see
[
GP
]
).

Given a small code
C_{1}
of length
p+1
and a labelling
\Phi=\{\phi_{e}\,:\,X_{e}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\}_{e\in E}
, the intermediate code
C_{0}=C[\mathbb{P}^{2},\Phi,C_{1}]
will be the expander code w.r.t.
\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
\Phi
and
C_{1}
.
In
[
DDHR
]
the authors proved a method to propagating local testability from the intermediate code to the global code, assuming
X
is a high dimensional expander.
A concrete case of Question
5.5
is the following.

Question 5.6
.

Is there a linear code
C_{1}\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
of rate
>\frac{2}{3}
, normalized distance
>2\sqrt{3}p^{-1/2}
, and an appropriate labelling
\Phi
, such that the expander code
C[\mathbb{P}^{2},\Phi,C_{1}]
is a LTC?

One can ask a more general question.

Question 5.7
.

Can one define an LTC over the graph of lines points of the projective plane?

In Questions
5.5
,
5.6
and
5.7
, the query complexity of the LTC is supposed to be little o of the number of bits of the code (= the number edges in the graph
\mathbb{P}^{2}
).

We note that Definition
5.4
and Questions
5.5
and
5.6
can easily be generalized to higher dimensions.
However the above arguments of
[
SS
]
and
[
DDHR
]
shows that the rate, distance and local testability of the global code, whose bits are on the maximal faces of the complex, will follow from appropriate rate, distance and local testability conditions on the local codes, whose bits are on the links of the complex, by arguing inductively on the dimension of the complex.
Namely, the difficulty is to initialize this paradigm, which is precisely Question
5.6
.

On entropy solutions of scalar conservation laws with discontinuous flux
Evgeny Yu. Panov

Abstract
We introduce the notion of entropy solutions (e.s.) to a conservation law with an arbitrary jump continuous flux vector and prove existence of the largest and the smallest e.s. to the Cauchy problem. The monotonicity and stability properties of these solutions are also established. In the case of a periodic initial function we derive the uniqueness of e.s. Generally, the uniqueness property can be violated, which is confirmed by an example. Finally, we proved that in the case of single space variable a weak limit of a sequence of spatially periodic e.s. is an e.s. as well.

Introduction

In the half-space
\Pi=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,+\infty)
, we consider the conservation law

u_{t}+\operatorname{div}_{x}\varphi(u)=0

(1.1)

with a jump continuous flux vector
\varphi(u)=(\varphi_{1}(u),\ldots,\varphi_{n}(u))
. This means that at each point
u_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
there exist one-sided limits

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to u_{0}\pm}\varphi(u)\doteq\varphi(u_{0}\pm)
. For example, if the components
\varphi_{i}(u)
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, are BV-functions then the vector
\varphi(u)
is jump-continuous. It is known that the set

D=\{\ u_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\ |\ |\varphi(u_{0}+)-\varphi(u_{0})|+|\varphi(u_{0})-\varphi(u_{0}-)|>0\ \}

of discontinuity points of the vector
\varphi(u)
is at most countable (and may be an arbitrary at most countable set in
\mathbb{R}
). We used above and will use in the sequel the notation
|\cdot|
for Euclidean finite-dimensional norms (including the absolute value in one-dimensional case). We will treat
\varphi(u)
as a multi-valued vector function with values

\bar{\varphi}(u_{0})=[\varphi(u_{0}-),\varphi(u_{0})]\cup[\varphi(u_{0}),\varphi(u_{0}+)]
being a union of two segments in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
(one may use even more general continuous curves connecting
\varphi(u_{0}-)
with
\varphi(u_{0}+)
and passing through
\varphi(u_{0})
).
Clearly, these sets are different from a single points only if
u_{0}\in D
. Let us demonstrate that the graph of
\bar{\varphi}(u)
admits a continuous parametrization

u=b(v)\in C(\mathbb{R}),\quad\bar{\varphi}(u)\ni g(v)\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(1.2)

such that the function
b(v)
is non-strictly increasing and coercive, i.e.,
b(v)\to\pm\infty
as
v\to\pm\infty
.
This was shown in paper
[
2
]
, but only in the case when the set
D
admits monotone numeration
D=\{u_{k}\}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
u_{k+1}>u_{k}

\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e., when
D
is a completely ordered subset of
\mathbb{R}
. In the following lemma we construct the required parametrization for the general case.

Lemma 1.1
.

There exists a parametrization (
1.2
) with a non-strictly increasing and coercive
b(v)
.

Proof.

We consider the more complicated case when
D
is infinite (in the case of finite
D
we only need to replace the set
\mathbb{N}
in the proof below by its finite subset).
We numerate set
D
:
D=\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and choose positive numbers
h_{k}
such that
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}h_{k}=c<\infty
(we can take
h_{k}=2^{-k}
). We define the finite discrete measure
\displaystyle\mu(u)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}h_{k}\delta(u-u_{k})
, where by

\delta(u-u_{k})
we denote the Dirac mass at the point
u_{k}
. Then we introduce the strictly increasing function

\alpha(u)=u+\mu((-\infty,u))
with jumps at points in
D
. Notice that

u\leq\alpha(u)\leq u+c;\quad\alpha(u_{2})-\alpha(u_{1})\geq u_{2}-u_{1}\ \forall u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathbb{R},u_{2}>u_{1}.

(1.3)

The function
b(v)
is defined as the inverse to the function
\alpha(u)
considered as maximal monotone graph, that is,
the value
b(v)
is such

that
v\in[\alpha(u-),\alpha(u+)]
. It follows from (
1.3
) that

v-c\leq b(v)\leq v
. If
v_{1}<v_{2}
then denoting

,

, we have
v_{1}\leq\alpha(u_{1}+)\leq\alpha(u_{2}-)\leq v_{2}
whenever
u_{1}<u_{2}
. This relations implies that
v_{2}-v_{1}\geq\alpha(u_{2}-)-\alpha(u_{1}+)\geq u_{2}-u_{1}=b(v_{2})-b(v_{1})
.
Hence,
b(v_{2})-b(v_{1})\leq v_{2}-v_{1}
. In the case
u_{1}=u_{2}
we see that
b(v_{2})=b(v_{1})
and the inequality
b(v_{2})-b(v_{1})\leq v_{2}-v_{1}
is evident. The obtained inequality can be written in the form
|b(v_{2})-b(v_{1})|\leq|v_{2}-v_{1}|
. We find that

is Lipschitz continuous. Notice also that
b(v)
takes values

[a_{k},b_{k}]=[\alpha(u_{k}-),\alpha(u_{k}+)]

. To define the vector

, we have to set

g(v)=\varphi(b(v))
whenever
b(v)\notin D
. If

being the piecewise linear function

\displaystyle\frac{(b_{k}-v)\varphi(u_{k})+(v-c_{k})\varphi(u_{k}+)}{b_{k}-c_{k}}&,&c_{k}\leq v\leq b_{k},\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

and

).
Let us show that the vector

is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
. We verify that

is continuous at each point

It is clear if

, in view of (
1.4
). Further, suppose that
v_{0}\notin[a_{k},b_{k}]
for all

u_{0}=b(v_{0})\notin D

. Therefore, for every

(1.5)

If

then

and taking into account (

Since

at point

. By the similar reasons we prove that

\lim_{v\to a_{k}-}g(v)=\varphi(u_{k}-)=g(a_{k}),\ \lim_{v\to b_{k}+}g(v)=\varphi(u_{k}+)=g(b_{k})\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Since, in view of (

\lim_{v\to a_{k}+}g(v)=g(a_{k}),\ \lim_{v\to b_{k}-}g(v)=g(b_{k}),

we find that the vector

is continuous at remaining points
v=a_{k},b_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}

Some properties of e.s.

We denote

\operatorname{sign}^{\pm}z=\frac{d}{dz}z^{\pm}

Proposition 2.1
.

If
u=u(t,x)
is an e.s. of (
1.1
), (
1.9
),
c\in\mathbb{R}
, then for a.e.

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u(t,x)-c)^{\pm}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{0}(x)-c)^{\pm}dx.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we will suppose that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{0}(x)-c)^{\pm}dx<\infty

It follows from (
1
) with

,

, that for each

\int_{\Pi}\left[uf_{t}+\int g(v)d\nu_{t,x}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}f\right]dtdx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{0}(x)f(0,x)dx=0.

(2.1)

Taking into account that for every constant
k\in\mathbb{R}

\int_{\Pi}[b(k)f_{t}+g(k)\cdot\nabla_{x}f]dtdx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}b(k)f(0,x)dx=0,

we can rewrite the previous identity in the form

\int_{\Pi}\left[(u-b(k))f_{t}+\int(g(v)-g(k))d\nu_{t,x}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}f\right]dtdx+\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{0}(x)-b(k))f(0,x)dx=0.

Putting this equality together with entropy inequality (
1
) and taking into account that

\operatorname{sign}z+1=2\operatorname{sign}^{+}z
, we arrive at the relation

\displaystyle\int_{\Pi}\left[(u-b(k))^{+}f_{t}+\int\operatorname{sign}^{+}(v-k)(g(v)-g(k))d\nu_{t,x}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}f\right]dtdx+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{0}(x)-b(k))^{+}f(0,x)dx\geq 0.

(2.2)

By coercivity condition there is such

that

.
Let

,

,
\beta(s)=\min((s/\delta)^{+},1)^{m}

Integrating the inequality (

)
over the measure
\beta^{\prime}(b(k)-c)db(k)

\displaystyle\int_{\Pi}\left[\eta(u-c)f_{t}+\int q(v)d\nu_{t,x}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}f\right]dtdx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta(u_{0}(x)-c)f(0,x)dx\geq 0,

(2.3)

where

\displaystyle\eta(b(v)-c)=\int_{d}^{v}(b(v)-b(k))^{+}\beta^{\prime}(b(k)-c)db(k)=\int_{d}^{v}\beta(b(k)-c)db(k)=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}((b(v)-c)^{+})^{m+1}/((m+1)\delta^{m})&,&b(v)-c<\delta,\\
b(v)-c-m\delta/(m+1)&,&b(v)-c\geq\delta,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle q(v)=\int_{d}^{v}\operatorname{sign}^{+}(v-k)(g(v)-g(k))\beta^{\prime}(b(k)-c)db(k).

In particular, if

a.e. on
\Pi
, and
\displaystyle C=2\max_{|v|\leq M+d}|g(v)|

\operatorname{supp}\beta(y)\subset B_{1}(0)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}||y|\leq 1\}
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\beta(y)dy=1
. For a function

we consider the corresponding averaged functions

q^{h}(x)=h^{-n}\int q(y)\beta((x-y)/h)dy,\quad h>0,

which are the convolutions
q*\beta^{h}(x)
, where
\beta^{h}(x)=h^{-n}\beta(x/h)
. It is clear that
q^{h}(x)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for each
h>0
,
\|q^{h}\|_{\infty}\leq\|q\|_{\infty}
, and
q^{h}\to q
as
h\to 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, since
\nabla q^{h}=q*\nabla\beta^{h}(x)
, we have

\|\nabla q^{h}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{c}{h}\|q\|_{\infty},\quad c=\|\nabla_{y}\beta\|_{1}.

(3.3)

Applying (
3.1
) with
u=u_{r}
to the test function
f=(\theta_{\nu}(t-t_{0})-\theta_{\nu}(t-t_{0}-\Delta t))p(x)
, where

t_{0},\Delta t>0
,
p=p(x)\in C_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, and passing to the limit as
\nu\to\infty
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{r}(t_{0}+\Delta t)-u_{r}(t_{0},x))p(x)dx=\int_{(t_{0},t_{0}+\Delta t)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi_{r}(u_{r})\cdot\nabla pdx.

(3.4)

By Corollary
2.1

\|u_{r}\|_{\infty}\leq M=\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}
for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows from the coercivity assumption that there is such
R>0
that
b(-R)<-M
,
b(R)>M
. All the more,
b_{r}(-R)<b(R)<-M
,
b_{r}(R)>b(R)>M
for all

.
This implies that
(b_{r})^{-1}([-M,M])\subset(-R,R)
and therefore for a.e.
(t,x)\in\Pi

|\varphi_{r}(u_{r})|=g((b_{r})^{-1}(u_{r}))\leq N\doteq\max_{|v|\leq R}|g(v)|.

It now follows from (

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{r}(t_{0}+\Delta t)-u_{r}(t_{0},x))p(x)dx\right|\leq N\|\nabla p\|_{1}\Delta t.

(3.5)

Further, we make use of the following variant of Kruzhkov’s lemma
[

, Lemma 1]
(for the sake of completeness, we provide it with the proof).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
w(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then for each
h>0

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}||w(x)|-w(x)(\operatorname{sign}w)^{h}(x)|dx\leq 2\omega_{w}(h),

where
\omega_{w}(h)=\sup\limits_{|\Delta x|<h}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w(x+\Delta x)-w(x)|dx
is the continuity modulus of

w
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

First, notice that for each
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle||w(x)|-w(x)\operatorname{sign}w(y)|=||w(x)|-(w(x)-w(y))\operatorname{sign}w(y)-w(y)\operatorname{sign}w(y)|=

\displaystyle||w(x)|-|w(y)|-(w(x)-w(y))\operatorname{sign}w(y)|\leq

\displaystyle||w(x)|-|w(y)||+|w(x)-w(y)|\leq 2|w(x)-w(y)|.

With the help of above inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}||w(x)|-w(x)(\operatorname{sign}w)^{h}(x)|dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(|w(x)|-w(x)\operatorname{sign}w(x-y))\beta_{h}(y)dy\right|dx\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}||w(x)|-w(x)\operatorname{sign}w(x-y)|\beta_{h}(y)dydx\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}2|w(x)-w(x-y)|\beta_{h}(y)dydx=

\displaystyle 2\int_{|y|\leq h}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w(x)-w(x-y)|dx\right)\beta_{h}(y)dy\leq 2\omega_{w}(h),

as was to be proved.
∎

As it readily follows from Lemma
3.1
, for any
\rho=\rho(x)\in C_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w(x)|\rho(x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w(x)\rho(x)(\operatorname{sign}w)^{h}(x)dx\right|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}||w(x)|-w(x)(\operatorname{sign}w)^{h}(x)|\rho(x)dx\leq 2\|\rho\|_{\infty}\omega_{w}(h).

(3.6)

We apply this relation to the function
w(x)=u_{r}(t_{0}+\Delta t,x)-u_{r}(t_{0},x)
for fixed

,

.
In view of estimate (
3.2
) for every

,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w(x+\Delta x)-w(x)|dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{r}(t_{0},x+\Delta x)-u_{r}(t_{0},x)|dx+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{r}(t_{0}+\Delta t,x+\Delta x)-u_{r}(t_{0}+\Delta t,x)|dx\leq 2\omega_{x}(h),

so that
\omega_{w}(h)\leq 2\omega^{x}(h)
. It follows from (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w(x)|\rho(x)dx\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w(x)\rho(x)(\operatorname{sign}w)^{h}(x)dx\right|+4\|\rho\|_{\infty}\omega^{x}(h)=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{r}(t_{0}+\Delta t,x)-u_{r}(t_{0},x))\rho(x)(\operatorname{sign}w)^{h}(x)dx\right|+4\|\rho\|_{\infty}\omega^{x}(h)\leq

\displaystyle N\|\nabla(\rho(x)(\operatorname{sign}w)^{h}(x))\|_{1}\Delta t+4\|\rho\|_{\infty}\omega^{x}(h)\leq c_{\rho}(\Delta t/h+\omega^{x}(h)),

(3.7)

where
0<h<1

is a constant depending only on

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{r}(t_{0}+\Delta t,x)-u_{r}(t_{0},x)|\rho(x)dx\leq c_{\rho}\omega^{t}(\Delta t),

(3.8)

where
\displaystyle\omega^{t}(\Delta t)=\inf_{0<h<1}(\Delta t/h+\omega^{x}(h))

To construct the largest e.s., we choose a strictly decreasing sequence

2.2

2.2
regarded to the e.s.
u_{r+1}

u_{r}

a.e. on
\Pi

, by the minimum principle. Hence, the sequence

a.e. on
\Pi

2.3

is an e.s. of original problem (
1.1
), (
1.9
).

is the largest e.s. of this problem.
For that, we choose an arbitrary e.s.

of (
1.1
), (
1.9
). By the maximum principle,

u\leq d
. Therefore, for each

Lemma 6.1

u(t,x)=\int b(v)d\nu_{t,x}(v)=\tilde{u}(t,x)
.
We conclude that
u=\tilde{u}
a.e. in
\Pi
, which completes the proof.

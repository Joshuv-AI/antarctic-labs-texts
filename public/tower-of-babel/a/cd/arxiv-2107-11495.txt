1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper the product of
q
-shifted factorials are defined by

(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{l=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{l})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad(a;q)_{n}=\frac{(a;q)_{\infty}}{(aq^{n};q)_{\infty}},

for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
|q|<1
, with the following abbreviated
multiple parameter notation

(a,b,\cdots,c;q)_{k}=(a;q)_{k}(b;q)_{k}\cdots(c;q)_{k},\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z\cup\{\infty\}}}.

In a recent short paper
[
9
]
, the author and Zhang give the theorem below.

Theorem
1.1
.

( The first semi-finite form of the quintuple product identity )
There holds

(1.1)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1+zq^{k})\frac{(z^{2};q)_{k}q^{k^{2}}z^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}=(-z;q)_{\infty}(z^{2}q;q^{2})_{\infty},\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}.

This is proved to be a semi-finite form of
the celebrated quintuple product identity, which can be stated as

(1.2)

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}q^{k(3k-1)\over 2}z^{3k}(1+zq^{k})=\frac{(q,q/z^{2},z^{2};q)_{\infty}}{(z,q/z;q)_{\infty}},\quad z\neq 0.

The identity (
1.2
)
or its equivalent forms can be found in Fricke

[
3
]
, Ramanujan
[
6
]
and Watson
[
7
]
.
This is an important identity in combinatorics, number theory
and special functions. For the historical note and various proofs, we
refer the reader to the paper
[
2
]
.

In this short note, we give a new semi-finite
form of the quintuple product identity
from the very-well-poised
{}_{6}\phi_{5}
series, and then, the quintuple product identity (
1.2
) is deduced.

Theorem
1.2
.

(The second semi-finite form of the quintuple product identity)
There holds

(1.3)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-z^{2}q^{2k+1})\frac{(z^{2}q;q)_{k}q^{k^{2}}z^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}\!=(-zq;q)_{\infty}(z^{2}q;q^{2})_{\infty},\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}.

For more semi-finite forms of the bilateral basic
hypergeometric series, the reader can consult the papers

[
1
,
5
,
8
]
.

2.
The proofs of Theorem
1.2
and the quintuple product identity (
1.2
)

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We begin with the non-terminating very-well poised
{}_{6}\phi_{5}
series
[
4
, p. 356, Eq. (II.
20)]
:

(2.1)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a,qa^{1\over 2},-qa^{1\over 2},b,c,d;q)_{k}}{(q,a^{1\over 2},-a^{1\over 2},aq/b,aq/c,aq/d;q)_{k}}\left(\frac{qa}{bcd}\right)^{k}=\frac{(aq,aq/bc,aq/bd,aq/cd;q)_{\infty}}{(aq/b,aq/c,aq/d,aq/bcd;q)_{\infty}},

with
\left|\frac{qa}{bcd}\right|<1
. Replacing
(a^{1\over 2},b)
by

(zq^{1\over 2},zq)
and letting
c=d\rightarrow\infty
, in the
resulting identity, we multiply both sides by
1-z^{2}q
, to give
(
1.3
).

Now we deduce the quintuple product identity (
1.2
) from Theorem

1.2
. For a nonnegative integer
n
and
z\neq 0
, we replace

z
by
zq^{-n}
in (
1.3
) to have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-z^{2}q^{2k-2n+1})(z^{2}q^{1-2n};q)_{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}z^{k}q^{k^{2}-kn}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-n}^{\infty}\frac{(1-z^{2}q^{2k+1})(z^{2}q^{1-2n};q)_{k+n}}{(q;q)_{k+n}}z^{k+n}q^{k^{2}+kn}

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{n}(z^{2}q^{1-2n};q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}\sum_{k=-n}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(1-z^{2}q^{2k+1})(q^{n-k}/z^{2};q)_{k}}{(q^{n+1};q)_{k}}z^{3k}q^{3k^{2}+k\over 2}

\displaystyle=(-zq^{1-n};q)_{\infty}(z^{2}q^{1-2n};q^{2})_{\infty}=\frac{(z^{2}q^{1-2n};q)_{\infty}}{(zq^{1-n};q)_{\infty}}

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{n}(z^{2}q^{1-2n};q)_{n}(1/z^{2};q)_{n}(z^{2}q;q)_{\infty}}{(1/z;q)_{n}(zq;q)_{\infty}}.

From (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-n}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(1-z^{2}q^{2k+1})(q^{n-k}/z^{2};q)_{k}}{(q^{n+1};q)_{k}}z^{3k}q^{3k^{2}+k\over 2}=\frac{(q,1/z^{2};q)_{n}(z^{2}q;q)_{\infty}}{(1/z;q)_{n}(zq;q)_{\infty}}.

Then, restricting
z
in any compact subset of
0<|z|<\infty
and
letting
n\rightarrow\infty
gives

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}(1-z^{2}q^{2k+1})z^{3k}q^{3k^{2}+k\over 2}=\frac{(q,1/z^{2},z^{2}q;q)_{\infty}}{(1/z,zq;q)_{\infty}}.

By analytic
continuation, the restriction on
z
may be relaxed. This is an equivalent form of the identity (
1.2
).

It is easy to verify that the identity (
2.4
) is equivalent
to (
1.3
).

Note that the
{}_{6}\phi_{5}
formula (
2.1
) also implies the
identity in Theorem
1.1
.

A Mathematical Characterization of the Performance of the “Multi-Slice” Projector
C. Herzet, M. Diallo, P. Héas

Abstract
We consider an enhanced version of the well-kwown “Petrov-Galerkin” projection in Hilbert spaces. The proposed procedure, dubbed “multi-slice” projector, exploits the fact that the sought solution belongs to the intersection of several high-dimensional slices. This setup is for example of interest in model-order reduction where this type of prior may be computed off-line. In this note, we provide a mathematical characterization of the performance achievable by the multi-slice projector and compare the latter with the results holding in the Petrov-Galerkin setup. In particular, we illustrate the superiority of the multi-slice approach in certain situations. Nous considérons une version améliorée de la projection de “Petrov-Galerkin” dans un espace de Hilbert. La procédure proposée, appelée “projecteur multi-tranches”, exploite le fait que la solution recherchée appartient à l’intersection de plusieurs tranches de hautes dimensions. Dans cette note, nous fournissons une caractérisation mathématique des performances atteignables par le projecteur “multi-tranches” et comparons les résultats obtenus à ceux existants dans le contexte des projections de Petrov-Galerkin. Nous illustrons ainsi la supériorité de l’approche multi-tranches dans certaines situations.



1
Introduction

Let
{{\mathcal{H}}}
be a Hilbert space with inner product
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle
and induced norm
\left\|{\cdot}\right\|
. We focus on the following variational formulation:

\displaystyle\mbox{Find ${{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}\in{{\mathcal{H}}}$ such that }\ {a}({{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star},{{\boldsymbol{h}}})={b}({{\boldsymbol{h}}})\quad\forall{{\boldsymbol{h}}}\in{{\mathcal{H}}},

(1)

where
{a}:{{\mathcal{H}}}\times{{\mathcal{H}}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a bilinear operator and
{b}:{{\mathcal{H}}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
a linear operator. Problem (
1
) is quite common (it appears for example in the weak formulation of elliptic partial differential equations) and has therefore been well-studied in the literature. In particular, it has a unique solution under mild conditions, see Lax-Milgram’s and Necas Theorems in
[
1
, Theorems 2.1 and 2.2]
.

Unfortunately, solving (
1
) is generaly an intractable problem. A popular alternative to compute an approximation of (
1
) is known as “Petrov-Galerkin” projection. Formally, this approach consists of approximating (
1
) by the following problem:

\displaystyle\mbox{Find ${{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{PG}}}\in{V}_{n}$ such that }\ {a}({{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{PG}}},{{\boldsymbol{h}}})={b}({{\boldsymbol{h}}})\quad\forall{{\boldsymbol{h}}}\in{Z}_{m}

(2)

where
{V}_{n}\subset{{\mathcal{H}}}
is a linear subspace of dimension
{n}
and
{Z}_{m}\subset{{\mathcal{H}}}
is a linear subspace of dimension
{m}\geq{n}
. Since the dimension of
{V}_{n}
and
{Z}_{m}
are finite, (
2
) admits a simple algebraic solution under mild conditions. In the literature of model reduction (see
e.g.
,
[
1
]
), Petrov-Galerkin approximation is at the core of the family of “projection-based” reduced models.

In this note we elaborate on an alternative projection procedure exploiting several approximation subspaces. Indeed, in the context of model-order reduction, standard strategies to evaluate a good approximation subspace
{V}_{n}
,
e.g.
, reduced basis
[
1
]
or proper orthogonal decomposition
[
2
]
, typically generate a sequence of subspaces
\left\{{{V}_{k}}\right\}_{{k}=0}^{n}
and positive scalars
\left\{{{\hat{{\epsilon}}}_{k}}\right\}_{{k}=0}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle{V}_{0}\subset{V}_{1}\subset\ldots\subset{V}_{n}

(3)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}({{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star},{V}_{k})\leq{\hat{{\epsilon}}}_{k},\quad{k}=0\ldots{n}.

(4)

Clearly, (
4
) provides some useful information about the location of
{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}
in
{{\mathcal{H}}}
since it restrains the latter to belong to the intersection of a set of low dimensional slices,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}\in\cap_{{k}=0}^{n}{\mathcal{S}}_{{k}},

(5)

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{{k}}

\displaystyle=\left\{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}:\mathrm{dist}({{\boldsymbol{h}}},{V}_{k})\leq{\hat{{\epsilon}}}_{k}}\right\},\quad{k}=0\ldots{n}.

(6)

In standard Petrov-Galerkin projection (
2
), only
{V}_{n}
is used and the additional information provided by (
5
) is discarded. In this work, we consider a simple methodology to exploit the latter additional information into the projection process.
More specifically, we focus on the following optimization problem
1
1

1

In this note we assume that constraints are available
\forall{k}\in\left\{{1\ldots{n}}\right\}
. All the derivations presented in this paper may nevertheless be easily extended to the case where constraints in (
7
) are only available for
some

{k}\in\left\{{1\ldots{n}}\right\}
.

\displaystyle\mbox{Find }\,{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}\in

\displaystyle\operatornamewithlimits{arg\,min}_{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}\in{V}_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left({{b}({{\boldsymbol{z}}}_{j})-{a}({{{\boldsymbol{h}}}},{{\boldsymbol{z}}}_{j})}\right)^{2}

(7)

\displaystyle\mbox{subject to }\mathrm{dist}({{\boldsymbol{h}}},{V}_{k})\leq{\hat{{\epsilon}}}_{k},\quad{k}=0\ldots{n},

which can be seen as an extension of the standard Petrov-Galerkin approach. In particular, the constraints in (
7
) exploit the prior information (
4
) into the projection process: each constraint imposes that the solution belongs to some
{k}
-dimensional slice
{\mathcal{S}}_{{k}}
. Hence, in the sequel, we will dub this procedure as “multi-slice” projection.

The practical interest of the multi-slice approach has already been emphasized in several contributions. In
[
3
,
4
]
we presented some applications of the multi-slice decoder to the problem of model-order reduction of parametric partial differential equations. In
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
, the authors showed that multi-slice decoder can be of interest to enhance the performance of the “empirical interpolation method” or the simulation of Navier-Stokes equations. “Multi-slice” prior information of the form (
5
) has also been considered in
[
7
]
for data assimilation. However, in the latter contribution, the decoder considered by the authors differs from (
7
) since the solution is no longer constrained to belong to the low-dimensional subspace
{V}_{n}
.

In this note we provide a mathematical characterization of the performance achievable by the multi-slice decoder (
7
). More specifically, we derive an “instance optimality property” relating the projection error
\|{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}-{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}\|
to the distance between
{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}
and the different approximation subspaces
{V}_{k}
. Our result is presented in Theorem
2
in the next section.

2
Performance guarantees

One of the reasons which has ensured the success of Petrov-Galerkin projection is the existence of strong theoretical guarantees,
e.g.
, Cea’s Lemma
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
or the Babuska’s Theorem
[
1

7

We conclude this section by providing two examples in which the multi-slice projector leads to better guarantees of reconstruction than the standard Petrov-Galerkin approach.

We first introduce some quantities of interest. First, we let
\left\{{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}}\right\}_{j=1}^{n}

\left\{{{{\boldsymbol{z}}}_{j}}\right\}_{j=1}^{m}

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}=[\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{i},{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}}\right\rangle]_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{{m}\times{n}}.

(8)

With these notations, the well-known Babuska’s theorem (in a Hilbert space) can be formulated as follows:

Theorem 1

(Babuska’s Theorem)
.

If
{\sigma}_{n}>0
then the solution of (
2
) is unique and satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}-{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{PG}}}}\right\|\leq\frac{{\sigma}_{1}}{{\sigma}_{n}}\mathrm{dist}({{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star},{V}_{n}).

(9)

See for example
[
8
]
for a proof of this result.
Hereafter we provide a similar characterization of the performance of the multi-slice projector (
7

We first note that problem (

where

We then derive an upper bound on

—

f({{\boldsymbol{h}}})
over the set of feasible points, we have
f({{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}})\leq f({P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right))\leq\gamma^{2}{\epsilon}_{n}^{2}
and therefore
{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

We finally show that
{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)\in{\mathcal{S}}
implies
{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)-{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}\in{{\mathcal{D}}}_{1}
.
Let us first note that, if
{{\boldsymbol{h}}}\in{V}_{n}
, the cost function
f({{\boldsymbol{h}}})
can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle f({{\boldsymbol{h}}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left({{b}({{\boldsymbol{z}}}_{j})-{a}({{\boldsymbol{h}}},{{\boldsymbol{z}}}_{j})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left({\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{j},{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{j},{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}\right\rangle}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left({\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{j}^{*},{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{j}^{*},{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}\right\rangle}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left({\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{j}^{*},{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right\rangle-{\sigma}_{j}\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}\right\rangle}\right)^{2}+\sum_{j={n}+1}^{m}\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{j}^{*},{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right\rangle^{2},

(48)

where the third equality follows from the fact that
\left\{{{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{j}}\right\}_{j=1}^{m}
and
\left\{{{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{j}^{*}}\right\}_{j=1}^{m}
differ up to an orthonormal transformation; the last equality is a consequence of (
3.1.1
) and the fact that
{{\boldsymbol{h}}}\in{V}_{n}
by hypothesis.

We note that
{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)-{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}
can be written as
\sum_{j=1}^{{n}}\beta_{j}{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*}
by setting
\beta_{j}=\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)}\right\rangle-\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}}\right\rangle
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{{n}}{\sigma}_{j}^{2}\beta_{j}^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{{n}}\left({{\sigma}_{j}\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)}\right\rangle-{\sigma}_{j}\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}}\right\rangle}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{{n}}\Bigl({\sigma}_{j}\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)}\right\rangle-\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{j}^{*},{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right\rangle-{\sigma}_{j}\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}}\right\rangle+\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{j}^{*},{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right\rangle\Bigr)^{2},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=1}^{{n}}\left({{\sigma}_{j}\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)}\right\rangle-\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{j}^{*},{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right\rangle}\right)^{2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{{n}}\left({{\sigma}_{j}\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}}\right\rangle-\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{j}^{*},{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right\rangle}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2f({P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right))+2f({{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}),

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\gamma^{2}{\epsilon}_{n}^{2},

where the first inequality follows from the standard inequality
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
, the second from (
48
), and the last one from the fact that
{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

3.1.3
Definition of
{{\mathcal{D}}}_{2}

Let

\displaystyle\delta_{j}

\displaystyle={\eta}_{j}+\hat{{\eta}}_{j},

(49)

where

\displaystyle{\eta}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|{x_{ij}}\right|{\epsilon}_{i-1},

\displaystyle\hat{{\eta}}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|{x_{ij}}\right|{\hat{{\epsilon}}}_{i-1},

(50)

and the
x_{ij}
’s are the elements of the matrix
{\mathbf{X}}
appearing in the SVD decomposition (
37
). We define
{{\mathcal{D}}}_{2}
as

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{D}}}_{2}=\left\{{{{\boldsymbol{d}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{{n}}\beta_{j}{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*}:\left|{\beta_{j}}\right|\leq{\eta}_{j}}\right\}.

(51)

We show hereafter that
{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)-{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}\in{{\mathcal{D}}}_{2}
.

We first note that if
{{\boldsymbol{h}}}
is feasible for problem (
7
), we must have

\displaystyle\left|{\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}\right\rangle}\right|\leq\hat{{\eta}}_{j}.

(52)

Indeed, if
{{\boldsymbol{h}}}
is feasible, the constraint
\mathrm{dist}({{\boldsymbol{h}}},{V}_{k})\leq{\hat{{\epsilon}}}_{k}
simply writes as

\displaystyle\sum_{j={k}+1}^{n}\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j},{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}\right\rangle^{2}\leq{\hat{{\epsilon}}}_{k}^{2}.

In particular, this implies that

\displaystyle\left|{\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{{k}+1},{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}\right\rangle}\right|\leq{\hat{{\epsilon}}}_{k}.

Using the fact that

\displaystyle{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{k}=1}^{n}x_{{k}j}{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{k},

we obtain (
52
). In a similar way, we can find that

\displaystyle\left|{\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)}\right\rangle}\right|\leq{\eta}_{j},

(53)

by using the fact that
\mathrm{dist}({P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right),{V}_{k})\leq{\epsilon}_{k}
from (
47
).

Let us now show that
{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)-{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}\in{{\mathcal{D}}}_{2}
.
We first note that
{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)-{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}
can be written as
\sum_{j=1}^{{n}}\beta_{j}{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*}
by setting
\beta_{j}=\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)}\right\rangle-\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}}\right\rangle
. This leads to

\displaystyle\left|{\beta_{j}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)}\right\rangle-\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}}\right\rangle}\right|,

\displaystyle\leq\left|{\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{P}_{{V}_{n}}\left({{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}^{\star}}\right)}\right\rangle}\right|+\left|{\left\langle{{{\boldsymbol{v}}}_{j}^{*},{{\hat{{{{\boldsymbol{h}}}}}}_{\mathrm{MS}}}}\right\rangle}\right|,

\displaystyle\leq\hat{{\eta}}_{j}+{\eta}_{j}=\delta_{j},

where the last inequality follows from (
52
) and (
53
).

3.2
Expression of
\sup_{{{\boldsymbol{d}}}\in{{\mathcal{D}}}}\left\|{{{\boldsymbol{d}}}}\right\|^{2}

We consider the following problem:

\displaystyle\sup_{{{\boldsymbol{d}}}\in{{\mathcal{D}}}}\left\|{{{\boldsymbol{d}}}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right\|^{2}\ \mbox{ subject to }\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\sum_{j=1}^{{n}}{\sigma}_{j}^{2}\beta_{j}^{2}\leq 4\gamma^{2}{\epsilon}_{n}^{2}\\
\left|{\beta_{j}}\right|\leq\delta_{j}\end{array}\right..

If
\sum_{j=1}^{{n}}{\sigma}_{j}^{2}\delta_{j}^{2}\leq 4\gamma^{2}{\epsilon}_{n}^{2}
, the first constraint in (
3.2
) is always inactive and the solution simply reads

\displaystyle\sup_{{{\boldsymbol{d}}}\in{{\mathcal{D}}}}\left\|{{{\boldsymbol{d}}}}\right\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{{n}}\delta_{j}^{2}.

(56)

If
\sum_{j=1}^{{n}}{\sigma}_{j}^{2}\delta_{j}^{2}\geq 4\gamma^{2}{\epsilon}_{n}^{2}
,
the solution of (
3.2
) is given by

\displaystyle\sup_{{{\boldsymbol{d}}}\in{{\mathcal{D}}}}\left\|{{{\boldsymbol{d}}}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n}\delta_{j}^{2}+\rho\,\delta_{\ell}^{2},

(57)

where
\ell
is the largest integer such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=\ell}^{{n}}{\sigma}_{j}^{2}\delta_{j}^{2}\geq 4\gamma^{2}{\epsilon}_{n}^{2},

(58)

and
\rho\in[0,1]
is defined as

\displaystyle\rho{\sigma}_{\ell}^{2}\delta_{\ell}^{2}+\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{{n}}{\sigma}_{j}^{2}\delta_{j}^{2}=4\gamma^{2}{\epsilon}_{n}^{2}.

(59)

This can be seen by verifying the optimality condition of problem (
3.2
).
We note that problem (
3.2
) is the same (up to some constants) to the one considered in
[
7
, Section 3.1]
. The solution (
57
) is therefore similar, up to some different constants, to the one obtained in that paper.

\widetilde{G}^{A(BC):x-y}_{h,k}(\vec{\xi};x,y)=\sum_{d\in\Delta^{\prime}}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{t=0}^{K(\vec{\xi})}r^{h,k}_{d+m,t}(\vec{\xi},y)(x-y)^{d+m}\log^{t}(x-y).

Multiplying it by
(x-y)^{-{\rm gr}^{234}(\vec{\xi})}\log^{h}(x)\log^{k}(y)
for
h,k
and
taking a sum of them and replacing
\log(x)
by

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j+1}(\frac{x-y}{y})^{j+1}+\log(y)
,
we obtain an expansion of a principal branch

\widetilde{F}(\langle\theta,{\cal Y}^{A(BC)}(v,x){\cal Y}^{BC}(u,y)w\rangle)
of
F^{A(BC)}(\vec{\xi};x,y)
on

{\cal D}^{2}
.
We denote it by
\widetilde{F}^{A(BC)}(\vec{\xi};x,y)
.
Since
\dim P_{C}\!<\!\infty
, setting
\Delta\!=\Delta^{\prime}!-\!{\rm max}\{{\rm gr}(w)\mid w\in\!P_{C}\}
, we can write

\widetilde{F}^{A(BC)}(\vec{\xi};x,y)=\sum_{t=0}^{K(\vec{\xi})}\sum_{-s-1\in\Delta+{\mathbb{N}}-{\rm gr}(v)-{\rm gr}(u)}g_{s,t}(\vec{\xi};y)(x-y)^{-s-1}\log^{t}(x-y)

[formula]

for
\vec{\xi}=(\theta,v,u,w)\in\Omega
with
w\in P_{C}
on
{\cal D}^{2}
.
Although our argument is depending on the choice
{\cal J}_{N}
,
we will show that
(4.5)
holds for every
\vec{\xi}\in\Omega
with the same

\Delta
.

Since
g_{s,t}(\vec{\xi};y)
is multi-linear on
\vec{\xi}\in(A(BC))^{\vee}\times A\times B\times C
, we will view it as a map
A\times B\to{\rm Hom}(C,A(BC))\otimes{\cal O}({\mathbb{C}}\backslash{\mathbb{R}}^{\leq 0})
,
in other words, using formal operators
{\cal Y}^{3}
and
{\cal Y}^{4}
,
we can rewrite RHS of (4.5) into
\langle\theta,{\cal Y}^{3}({\cal Y}^{4}(v,x-y)u,y)w\rangle
such that

\widetilde{F}^{A(BC)}(\vec{\xi};x,y)=\langle\theta,{\cal Y}^{3}({\cal Y}^{4}(v,x-y)u,y)w\rangle

[formula]

on
{\cal D}^{2}
for
\vec{\xi}\in\Omega
.
In §4, using these notation, we will prove that
{\cal Y}^{3}
and

are logarithmic surjective intertwining
operators, which implies the existence of surjective homomorphism

and then we will get

We will also prove another axiom of monoidal category called pentagon axiom in §5.

Definition 2.1

A meromorphic system of linear differential equations is a
differential equation of the form

\frac{d}{dz}Y=AY
,
where
A\in M_{r\times r}({\cal M}(U))
is a square matrix whose coefficients
are meromorphic functions over some complex domain

U\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}
(open, nonempty).

\bullet
A singularity of such a meromorphic system is a point in
U
corresponding to a pole of
(at least one of the coefficients of)
A
.

\bullet
For technical reasons, one usually fixes a discrete subset
\Sigma\subseteq U
, and
speaks of meromorphic systems
\frac{d}{dz}Y=AY
over
U
with polar locus
\Sigma
if
the ”true polar locus” (i.e., the set of poles of
A
) is contained in
\Sigma
.

\bullet
A meromorphic system
\frac{d}{dz}Y=\frac{A}{z-z_{0}}Y
with a holomorphic matrix function
A
of size

r\times r
defined on
U
is called a Fuchsian system and its singularity at
z=z_{0}
is called a Fuchsian singularity.

Definition 2.2

Let
\frac{d}{dz}Y=AY
be a meromorphic system of rank
r
over
U\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
be open. A fundamental solution of
\frac{d}{dz}Y=AY
over
U^{\prime}
is a matrix-valued meromorphic function

\Phi\in M_{r\times r}({\cal M}(U^{\prime}))
such that

\frac{d}{dz}\Phi(z)=A(z)\Phi(z)\quad\forall z\in U^{\prime},

and moreover

\det(\Phi)(z)\not=0\quad\forall z\in U^{\prime}.

From now on, let
z_{0}\in U
and
let
A=\sum_{k\geq 0}(z-z_{0})^{k}A_{k}
with
A_{k}\in M_{r\times r}({\mathbb{C}})

and we will consider

\frac{d}{dz}Y=\frac{A}{z-z_{0}}Y,

[formula]

which we will call Fuchsian system. Set
{\mathbb{D}}_{R}(z_{0})=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\mid|z-z_{0}|<R\}
.
If
A=A_{0}\in M_{r\times r}({\mathbb{C}})
and
z_{0}\in U
,
then

\frac{d}{dz}Y=\frac{A_{0}}{z-z_{0}}Y

[formula]

is called Euler system. In this case,
there is
G\in GL_{r}({\mathbb{C}})
such that
GA_{0}G^{-1}
has a
Jordan standard form and

\frac{d}{dz}(GY)=G\frac{d}{dz}Y=\frac{GA_{0}G^{-1}}{z-z_{0}}(GY)
.

Lemma 2.3

If an Euler system (2.2) has a Jordan cell
A_{0}=J_{r}(a)
,
that is,
Y={}^{t}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
satisfies
(z-z_{0})\frac{d}{dz}f_{i}=af_{i}+f_{i+1}
for
i=1,...,r
with

f_{r+1}=0
, then

\Phi=\{(z-z_{0})^{a}\times{}^{t}(\log^{s}(z-z_{0}),...,\log(z-z_{0}),1,0,\cdots,0)\mid s=0,...,r-1\}

is a fundamental solution, where
{}^{t}(\cdots)
denotes the transpose of
(\cdots)
.

Therefore, in generally, all components of solutions of Euler system (2.2) are written as

f(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{t=0}^{r}r_{i,t}(z-z_{0})^{d_{i}}\log^{t}(z-z_{0})

[formula]

with
r_{i,t}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and eigenvalues
d_{i}
of
A_{0}
.
We also note that if
\frac{d}{dz}Y=\frac{A}{z-z_{0}}Y
, then

\frac{d}{dz}((z-z_{0})^{d}Y)=\frac{(A+dE_{r})}{z-z_{0}}((z-z_{0})^{d}Y),

[formula]

where
E_{r}
is the identity matrix of size
r
.

Definition 2.4

Let
\frac{d}{dz}Y=AY
be a meromorphic system over
U
with polar locus
\Sigma
.

\bullet
A holomorphic gauge transformation over
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
is the change of variable

Y=\Delta Z
,
yielding a meromorphic system
\frac{d}{dz}Z=BZ
over
U^{\prime}
with polar locus

\Sigma\cap U^{\prime}
, where

\Delta\in GL_{r}({\cal O}(U^{\prime}))
,
i.e.
\Delta
is a holomorphic matrix function over
U^{\prime}
with non-vanishing determinant.

\bullet
A meromorphic gauge transformation
over
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
is the change of variable
Y=\Delta Z
,
yielding a meromorphic system
\frac{d}{dz}Z=BZ
over
U^{\prime}
with polar locus

Z\cap U^{\prime}
, where

\Delta\in GL_{r}({\cal M}(U^{\prime}))
,
i.e.
\Delta
is a meromorphic matrix function over
U^{\prime}
whose determinant is not
identically zero on any connected component of
U^{\prime}
, such that moreover,

\Delta|_{U^{\prime}\backslash\Sigma}\in M_{r\times r}({\cal O}(U^{\prime}\backslash\Sigma))

and

\Delta^{-1}|_{U^{\prime}\backslash\Sigma}\in M_{r\times r}({\cal O}(U^{\prime}\backslash\Sigma))
.

\bullet
As usual, two meromorphic systems over
U
with polar locus
\Sigma
are said to be
holomorphically (resp. meromorphically) gauge equivalent, if they are related via a global holomorphic (resp. meromorphic) gauge transformation.

Definition 2.5
(Definition 3.6 in
[
2
]
)

A Fuchsian system
\frac{d}{dz}Y=\frac{B}{z}Y
of rank
r
over
{\mathbb{D}}
, with polar locus
\{0\}
, is said to be of Levelt normal form if

B=\sum_{k\geq 0}B_{k}z^{k}
such that for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the matrix
B_{k}\in M_{r,r}({\mathbb{C}})
satisfies

{\rm ad}(B_{0,s})(B_{k})=kB_{k}
, where
B_{0}=B_{0,s}+B_{0,n}
is the Dunford decomposition of
B_{0}
into semisimple part
B_{0,s}
and nilpotent part
B_{0,n}
with
B_{0,s}B_{0,n}=B_{0,n}B_{0,s}
.

Lemma 2.6

The eigenvalues of
B(1)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{k}
are equal to the eigenvalues of
B_{0}
.

[Proof]
We may assume that
B_{0,s}
is a diagonal matrix
(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{r})
and

B_{0,n}
is an upper triangular matrix. Furthermore, we may assume
\Re(\lambda_{1})\geq\cdots\geq\Re(\lambda_{r})
. Then since
{\rm ad}(B_{0,s})E_{i,j}=(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})E_{i,j}
,

{\rm ad}(B_{0,s})B_{k}=kB_{k}
means that
B_{k}
are strictly upper triangular and

B(1)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{k}
is an upper triangular matrix with diagonal entries

\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\}
.
\square

Theorem 2.7
(Theorem 3.8 in
[
2
]
)

Let
\frac{d}{dz}Y=\frac{A}{z}Y
be a Fuchsian system over

{\mathbb{D}}
. This system is holomorphically
gauge equivalent to some Fuchsian system

\frac{d}{dz}\widehat{Y}=\frac{B}{z}\widehat{Y}
of Levelt normal form over
{\mathbb{D}}
.

Remark 2.1

Under the setting of Theorem 2.7,
the set of eigenvalues of
B(0)
coincides with
the set of eigenvalues of
A(0)
counted with multiplicity.

Theorem 2.8
(Lemma 3.7 in
[
2
]
)

Let
B_{0,s}\in M_{r\times r}({\mathbb{C}})
be semisimple. Then
there exists the unique semisimple matrix
L
such that

L
commutes with
B_{0,s}
and has only integer eigenvalues and
any eigenvalues
\mu
of
B_{0,s}-L
satisfies
\Re(\mu)\in[0,1[
.
Moreover, if
\frac{d}{dz}Y=\frac{B}{z}Y
is a Fuchsian system of Level normal form with

B(0)=B_{0,s}+B_{0,n}
as above, then
B=z^{L}B(1)z^{-L}
.

Remark 2.2

As mentioned in the proof of Corollary 3.9 in
[
2
]
,
under the assumption in Theorem 2.8,

\tilde{Y}:=z^{-L}Y
satisfies an Euler system

\frac{d}{dz}\tilde{Y}=\frac{B(1)-L}{z}\tilde{Y}
.

Using the these results, we get the following result.

Proposition 2.9

Let
\frac{d}{dz}Z=\frac{B}{z}Z
be a Fuchsian system with
B\in M_{r\times r}({\cal O}(U))
and let

\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{r}\}
be the set of eigenvalues of the constant matrix
B_{0}
.
Choose
\ell_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
so that
0\leq d_{i}-\ell_{i}<1
.
Then all components of solutions of
\frac{d}{dz}Z=\frac{B}{z}Z

are written as

\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{t=0}^{r}r^{j}_{i,m,t}z^{d_{j}-\ell_{j}+\ell_{i}+m}\log^{t}(z)

[formula]

with
r^{j}_{i,m,t}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

[Proof]
If
\frac{d}{dz}Z=\frac{A}{z}Z
and
\frac{d}{dz}Y=\frac{B}{z}Y
are holomorphically equivalent, then
all components of solutions of one are linear combinations of components of solutions of the other with coefficients in

{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]
and so if all components of solutions of
\frac{d}{dz}Z=\frac{A}{z}Z

satisfy (2.5), then those of solutions of

\frac{d}{dz}Y=\frac{B}{z}Y
also satisfy (2.5).
Therefore, we may assume that

\frac{d}{dz}Z=\frac{B}{z}Z
is a Fuchsian system of Levelt normal form over
{\mathbb{D}}
by Theorem 2.7.
Furthermore we may assume that

B_{0,s}={\rm diag}(d_{1},...,d_{r})
satisfying

\Re(d_{1})\geq\cdots\geq\Re(d_{r})
and
B_{0,n}
is an upper triangular matrix. In this case,

L
in Theorem 2.8 is a diagonal matrix
{\rm diag}(\ell_{1},...,\ell_{r})
. Then by Theorem 2.8 and Remark 2.1, we have

B=z^{L}B(1)z^{-L}
and
\tilde{Y}:=z^{-L}Y
satisfies an Euler system

\frac{d}{dz}\tilde{Y}=\frac{B(1)-L}{z}\tilde{Y}
.
By Lemma 2.6,
B(1)
is an upper triangular matrix with
diagonal entries
\{d_{1},...,d_{r}\}
and
the eigenvalues of
B(1)-L
are
d_{1}-\ell_{1},\ldots,d_{r}-\ell_{r}
.
Hence all components of a solution
\tilde{Y}

are written as

\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{N}}}r^{j}_{t}z^{d_{j}-\ell_{j}}\log^{t}(z)

by Lemma 2.3. In this case,
since
Y=z^{L}\tilde{Y}
, all components of solutions of

\frac{d}{dz}Y=\frac{B}{z}Y
are written as

\sum_{i,j=1}^{r}\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{N}}}r^{j}_{i,t}z^{d_{j}-\ell_{j}+\ell_{i}}\log^{t}(z)
,
as we desired.

\square

2.2
Intertwining operators among
C_{1}
-cofinite modules

Let
A,B,C
be
V
-modules. Let
I\binom{C}{A\,\,B}
denote the set of logarithmic intertwining
operators of type
\binom{C}{A\,\,B}
. We introduce a generalized concept of (logarithmic)
intertwining operators. For a nonempty domain
U\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}\backslash\{0\}
, we define
a local intertwining operator
{\cal Y}
of type
\binom{C}{A\,\,B}
on
U
as
a linear map
A\otimes B\to C\otimes{\cal O}(U)
,
we denote it by
\langle\theta,{\cal Y}(v,z)u\rangle
for
v\in A,u\in B,\theta\in C^{\vee}
and
z\in U
, satisfying

{\rm(I\,\,1)}
[commutativity]
\langle\theta,\alpha_{m}{\cal Y}(v,z)u-{\cal Y}(v,z)\alpha_{m}u\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{m}{j}\langle\theta,{\cal Y}(\alpha_{j}v,z)u\rangle z^{m-j}
,

{\rm(I\,\,2)}
[associativity]

\langle\theta,{\cal Y}(\alpha_{m}v,z)u\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{m}{j}(-1)^{j}\langle\theta,\{\alpha_{m-j}z^{j}{\cal Y}(v,z)u-(-1)^{m}{\cal Y}(v,z)\alpha_{j}z^{m-j}u\}\rangle
, and

{\rm(I\,\,3)}
[
L(-1)
-derivative property]
\langle\theta,{\cal Y}(L(-1)v,z)u\rangle=\frac{d}{dz}\langle\theta,{\cal Y}(v,z)u\rangle

for
\alpha\in V
and
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
where
\langle\theta,\alpha_{m}{\cal Y}(v,z)u\rangle
denotes
\langle(\alpha_{m})^{\ast}\theta,{\cal Y}(v,z)u\rangle
with a contragredient operator
(\alpha_{m})^{\ast}
of
\alpha_{m}
.

An aim in this subsection is to show that every local intertwining operator for
A,B,C\in{\cal N}{\cal C}_{1}
on a domain
U
is
a branch of a logarithmic intertwining operator on
U
.

Let
W\in{\cal N}\!{\cal C}_{1}
. Since
C_{1}(W)={\rm Span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{\alpha_{-1}(P_{W}+C_{1}(W))\mid\alpha\in V_{\geq 1}\}
, we have:

Lemma 2.11

W={\rm Span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{\alpha^{1}_{-1}\cdots\alpha^{k}_{-1}w\mid w\in P_{W},\alpha^{j}\in V_{\geq 1},k\geq 0\}
.
In particular,
W
is a finitely generated
V
-module.

Therefore, there are finitely many
d_{1},...,d_{t}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that

W=\oplus_{j=1}^{t}\oplus_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}W_{d_{j}+n}
and
\dim W_{s}<\infty
for
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
, where
W_{s}
denotes a generalized eigenspace in
W
of
L(0)
with eigenvalue

s
. In particular, if
W
is indecomposable, then
W=\oplus_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}W_{d+m}
for some

d\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
W_{d}\not=0
. We use the notation

{\rm wt}(w)=s
for
w\in W_{s}
and
{\rm wt}(\alpha_{n})={\rm wt}(\alpha)-n-1

for
\alpha\in V
. Define
{\rm wt}()1\in{\rm End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(W)

by
({\rm wt}()1)w={\rm wt}(w)w
for
w\in W
.
Since
W_{(m)}\subseteq{\rm Span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{\alpha^{1}_{-1}\cdots\alpha^{k}_{-1}w\mid w\in P_{W},\alpha^{j}\in V_{\geq 1},\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\rm wt}(\alpha^{j})\leq m\}
, we obtain:

Lemma 2.12

For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there is
\mu(m)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\dim W_{(m)}\leq\mu(m)\times\dim P_{W}
. Moreover, the restricted dual
W^{\vee}=\oplus_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm Hom}(W_{(m)},{\mathbb{C}})
of an
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded module
W=\oplus W_{(m)}\in{\cal N}{\cal C}_{1}

is isomorphic to the contragredient module
\oplus_{s\in{\mathbb{C}}}{\rm Hom}(W_{s},{\mathbb{C}})
of

W=\oplus_{s\in{\mathbb{C}}}W_{s}
. In particular,
W^{\vee}
does not depend on the choice
of an
{\mathbb{N}}
-grading on
W
.

Since
{\rm wt}(\alpha_{n}w)={\rm wt}(w)+{\rm wt}(\alpha)-n-1
and

L(0)\alpha_{n}w=({\rm wt}(\alpha)-n-1)\alpha_{n}w+\alpha_{n}L(0)w=({\rm wt}(\alpha_{n}w)\alpha_{n}w+\alpha_{n}(L(0)-{\rm wt}(w))w
,
we get:

Lemma 2.13

L(0)-{\rm wt}()1
is a
V
-homomorphism.
If
W\in{\cal N}\!{\cal C}_{1}
, then there is
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
(L(0)-{\rm wt}()1)^{K}W=0

and
(L(0)-{\rm wt}()1)^{K}W^{\vee}=0
.

[Proof]
As we have just proved,
L(0)\!-\!{\rm wt}()1
is a
V
-homomorphism.
Since
\dim P_{W}\!<\!\infty
, there is
n\!\in\!{\mathbb{N}}
such that

P_{W}\!\subseteq\!\oplus_{i=0}^{n}W_{(i)}
, which is
L(0)\!-\!{\rm wt}()1
-invariant and

\dim\oplus_{i=0}^{n}W_{(i)}\!<\!\infty
. Hence

(L(0)\!-\!{\rm wt}()1)^{K}(\oplus_{i=0}^{n}W_{(i)})=0
for some
K
.
Since
W
is generated from
P_{W}
by the actions of
V
,
we have
(L(0)-{\rm wt}()1)^{K}W=0
. From
\langle(L(0)-{\rm wt}()1)^{K}W^{\vee},W\rangle=\langle W^{\vee},(L(0)\!-\!{\rm wt}()1)^{K}W\rangle=0
, we also
obtain
(L(0)\!-\!{\rm wt}()1)^{K}W^{\vee}=0
.
\square

Lemma 2.14

Let
A,B,C\in{\cal N}{\cal C}_{1}
and let

{\cal Y}
be a local intertwining operator of type
\binom{C}{A\,\,B}
on a domain
U

with a form
\langle\theta,{\cal Y}(v,z)u\rangle=\sum_{h=0}^{K(\theta,v,u)}\langle\theta,{\cal Y}_{h}(v,z)u\rangle\log^{h}(z)
with
{\mathbb{C}}
-formal power series
{\cal Y}_{h}(v,z)u
and
K(\theta,v,u)\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then there is
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

K(\theta,v,u)\leq K
.

[Proof]
Since

\sum_{h=0}^{K(\theta,v,u)}\langle\theta,{\cal Y}_{h}(L(-1)v,z)u\rangle\log^{h}(z)=\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}z}\{\sum_{h=0}^{K(\theta,v,u)}\langle\theta,{\cal Y}_{h}(v,z)u\rangle\log^{h}(z)\},

we have
{\cal Y}_{k+1}(v,z)u={\cal Y}_{k}(L(-1)v,z)u-\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}z}({\cal Y}_{k}(v,z)u)\times\frac{z}{k+1}
.
We also get

\begin{array}[]{l}\langle\theta,{\rm wt}()1({\cal Y}_{k}(v,x)u)\rangle=\langle\theta,{\rm wt}()1(\sum_{m}v_{m,k}uz^{-m-1})\rangle\cr\mbox{}\qquad=({\rm gr}(u)+{\rm gr}(v))\langle\theta,\sum_{m}v_{m,k}uz^{-m-1}\rangle+\langle\theta,\sum_{m}(-m-1)v_{m,k}uz^{-m-1}\rangle\cr\mbox{}\qquad=({\rm gr}(v)+{\rm gr}(u))\langle\theta,{\cal Y}_{k}(v,z)u\rangle+\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}z}\langle\theta,{\cal Y}_{k}(v,z)u\rangle z.\end{array}

Using the notation
\Xi=L(0)\!-\!{\rm wt}()1
and

(X+Y+Z)^{\ell}=\sum_{i,j,p\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda^{\ell}_{i,j,p}X^{i}Y^{j}Z^{p}
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\langle\theta,(k+1){\cal Y}_{k+1}(v,z)u\rangle\cr\mbox{}\qquad=\langle\theta,(L(0)\!-\!{\rm wt}()1){\cal Y}_{k}(v,x)u+{\cal Y}_{k}(v,x)(L(0)\!-\!{\rm wt}()1)u+{\cal Y}_{k}((L(0)\!-\!{\rm wt}()1)v,x)u\rangle\cr\mbox{}\qquad=\langle\theta,\Xi{\cal Y}_{k}(v,x)u\rangle+\langle\theta,{\cal Y}_{k}(v,x)\Xi u\rangle+\langle\theta,{\cal Y}_{k}(\Xi v,x)u\rangle\cr\mbox{}\qquad=\cdots=\sum_{\{i,j,p\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid i+j+p=\ell\leq k\}}\lambda^{\ell}_{i,j,p}\langle\theta,\Xi^{i}({\cal Y}_{k+1-\ell}(\Xi^{j}v,z)\Xi^{p}u\rangle.\end{array}

Since there is
\bar{K}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(\Xi)^{\bar{K}}A=(\Xi)^{\bar{K}}B=(\Xi)^{\bar{K}}C=0
by Lemma
2.13
,
we have
\langle\theta,{\cal Y}_{k}(v,z)u\rangle=0

for
k\geq 3\bar{K}
, that is, we can take

K(\theta,v,u)\leq 3\bar{K}
for any
\theta,v,u
.

\square

We will use the following theorem after the proof of Theorem 4.1.

Theorem 2.15

Let
A,B,C\in{\cal N}\!{\cal C}_{1}
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
For a local intertwining operator

{\cal Y}
of type
\binom{C}{A\,\,B}
over

\begin{array}[]{rl}\langle\theta,\phi_{[AB]C}({\cal Y}^{(AB)C}({\cal Y}^{AB}(v,x-y)u,y)w\rangle=&\langle\theta,{\cal Y}^{3}({\cal Y}^{4}(v,x-y)u,y)w\rangle\cr=&\langle\theta,{\cal Y}^{A(BC)}(v,x){\cal Y}^{BC}(u,y)w\rangle\end{array}

on
{\cal D}^{2}

for any
v\in A,u\in B,w\in C
and
\theta\in(A(BC))^{\vee}
.
Similarly, by starting from
\langle\theta^{\prime},{\cal Y}^{(AB)C}({\cal Y}^{(AB)}(v,x-y)u,y)w\rangle
, we also have a surjective homomorphism
\phi_{A[BC]}:(A\boxtimes B)\boxtimes C\to A\boxtimes(B\boxtimes C)
satisfying

\langle\theta^{\prime},\phi_{A[BC]}({\cal Y}^{A(BC)}(v,x){\cal Y}^{BC}(u,y)w)\rangle=\langle\theta^{\prime},{\cal Y}^{(AB)C}({\cal Y}^{AB}(v,x-y)u,y)w\rangle

on
{\cal D}^{2}
for all
v,u,w,\theta^{\prime}
.
From the construction, it is easy to see that

\phi_{A[BC]}\circ\phi_{[AB]C}=1
and
\phi_{[AB]C}\circ\phi_{A[BC]}=1
.

This completes the proof of the associativity law of fusion products.
\square

Corollary 4.11

If
V^{\vee}\cong V
and
A
and
A^{\vee}
are both
C_{1}
-cofinite

{\mathbb{N}}
-gradable modules,
then
for any non-zero
C_{1}
-cofinite
{\mathbb{N}}
-gradable
V
-module
B
,

A\boxtimes B
and
B\boxtimes A
are not zero.

[Proof]
There is a surjective intertwining operator
of type
\binom{V^{\vee}}{A\,\,A^{\vee}}
which comes from the

-module structure on

by skew-symmetry and duality.
Hence

5
Pentagon axiom

Theorem 5.1
(Pentagon axiom)

For
C_{1}
-cofinite
{\mathbb{N}}
-gradable modules
A,B,C,D
,
by using the above isomorphisms
\phi_{[\ast]\ast}
,
we have the following commutative diagram.

\begin{array}[]{c}((A\boxtimes B)\boxtimes C)\boxtimes D\xrightarrow{\phi_{([AB]C)}\times 1_{D}}(A\boxtimes(B\boxtimes C))\boxtimes D\xrightarrow{\phi_{[A(BC)]D}}A\boxtimes((B\boxtimes C)\boxtimes D)\cr\mbox{}\hskip 71.13188pt\searrow{}^{\phi_{[(AB)C]D}}\mbox{}\qquad\hskip 128.0374pt\swarrow{{}_{1_{A}\times\phi_{[BC]D}}}\cr(A\boxtimes B)\boxtimes(C\boxtimes D)\xrightarrow{\phi_{[AB](CD)}}A\boxtimes(B\boxtimes(C\boxtimes D))\end{array}

[Proof]

We consider a domain

{\cal D}^{3}\!=\!\{(x,y,z)\!\in\!{\mathbb{C}}^{3}\mid|x|\!>\!|y|\!>\!|z|\!>\!|x\!-\!z|\!>\!|y\!-\!z|\!>\!|x\!-\!y|\!>\!0,\mbox{ and }x,y,z,x\!-\!y,y\!-\!z,x\!-\!z\not\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\leq 0}\}
. We note
(7,6,4)\in{\cal D}^{3}\not=\emptyset
.
Simplify the notation, we omit the notation
\boxtimes
.
Let
\theta\in(A(B(CD)))^{\vee},v\in A,u\in B,w\in C,d\in D

and let
\vec{\xi}
denote a quadruple
(v,u,w,d)\in A\times B\times C\times D
.
We fix all intertwining operators for fusion products, say

{\cal Y}^{AB},{\cal Y}^{BC},{\cal Y}^{CD},{\cal Y}^{B(CD)},{\cal Y}^{(BC)D},{\cal Y}^{A(B(CD))},\ldots
, and
consider their five-point correlation functions:

\begin{array}[]{l}F^{A(B(CD))}(\theta^{1},\vec{\xi};x,y,z)=\langle\theta^{1},{\cal Y}^{A(B(CD))}(v,x){\cal Y}^{B(CD)}(u,y){\cal Y}^{CD}(w,z)d\rangle,\cr F^{A((BC)D)}(\theta^{2},\vec{\xi};x,y\!-\!z,z)=\langle\theta^{2},{\cal Y}^{A((BC)D)}(v,x){\cal Y}^{(BC)D}({\cal Y}^{BC}(u,y\!-\!z)c,z)d\rangle,\cr F^{(A(BC))D}(\theta^{3},\vec{\xi};x\!-\!z,y\!-\!z,z)=\langle\theta^{3},{\cal Y}^{(A(BC))D}({\cal Y}^{(A(BC)}(v,x\!-\!z){\cal Y}^{BC}(u,y-z)w,z)d\rangle,\cr F^{((AB)C)D}(\theta^{4},\vec{\xi};x\!-\!y,y\!-\!z,z)\!=\!\langle\theta^{4},{\cal Y}^{((AB)C)D}({\cal Y}^{(AB)C}({\cal Y}^{AB}(v,x\!-\!y)u,y\!-\!z)w,z)d\rangle,\!\mbox{ and}\cr F^{(AB)(CD)}(\theta^{5},\vec{\xi};x\!-\!y,y,z)=\langle\theta^{5},{\cal Y}^{(AB)(CD)}({\cal Y}^{AB}(v,x\!-\!y)u,y){\cal Y}^{CD}(w,z)d\rangle\end{array}

[formula]

for
\theta^{1}\in(A(B(CD)))^{\vee},\theta^{2}\in(A((BC)D))^{\vee},\theta^{3}\in((A(BC))D)^{\vee},\theta^{4}\in(((AB)C)D)^{\vee},\theta^{5}\in((AB)(CD))^{\vee}
.
By the same way as we did for four-point correlation functions, it is not difficult to see that
these five-point correlation functions are all locally normal convergent on

\{(x,y,z)\!\in\!{\mathbb{C}}^{3}\mid|x|\!>\!|y|\!>\!|z|\!>\!|x\!-\!z|\!>\!|y\!-\!z|\!>\!|x\!-\!y|\!>\!0\}
.
We next choose their principle branches

\widetilde{F}^{A(B(CD))}(\theta^{1},\vec{\xi};x,y,z),\widetilde{F}^{A((BC)D)}(\theta^{2},\vec{\xi};x,y-z,z),\ldots,\widetilde{F}^{(AB)(CD)}(\theta^{5},\vec{\xi};x-y,y,z)

on
{\cal D}^{3}

by taking the values of
\log(x),\log(y),\log(z),\log(x-y),\log(x-z),\log(y-z)
which satisfy

-\pi<\Im(\log(x)),\Im(\log(y)),\Im(\log(z)),\Im(\log(x-y)),\Im(\log(x-z)),\Im(\log(y-z))<\pi
and
viewing
x^{a},y^{b},z^{c},(x-y)^{d},(x-z)^{e},(y-z)^{f}
as

e^{a\log(x)}
,
e^{b\log(y)}
,
e^{c\log(z)}
,
e^{d\log(x-y)}
,
e^{e\log(x-z)}
,
e^{f\log(y-z)}
, respectively.

From our construction of isomorphisms
\phi_{[\ast,\ast]\ast}
, by Theorem 4.1,
we have equations:

\begin{array}[]{l}\widetilde{F}^{A(B(CD))}(\theta^{1},\vec{\xi};x,y,z)=\widetilde{F}^{A((BC)D)}((1_{A}\times\phi_{[BC]D}^{\ast})(\theta^{1}),\vec{\xi};x,y-z,z),\cr\widetilde{F}^{A((BC)D)}(\theta^{2},\vec{\xi};x,y-z,z)=\widetilde{F}^{(A(BC))D}(\phi_{[A(BC)]D}^{\ast}(\theta^{2}),\vec{\xi};x-z,y-z,z),\cr\widetilde{F}^{(A(BC))D}(\theta^{3},\vec{\xi};x-z,y-z,z)=\widetilde{F}^{((AB)C)D}((\phi_{[AB]C}^{\ast}\times 1_{D})(\theta^{3}),\vec{\xi};x-y,y-z,z),\cr\widetilde{F}^{A(B(CD))}(\theta^{1},\vec{\xi};x,y,z)=\widetilde{F}^{(AB)(CD)}(\phi_{[AB](CD)}^{\ast}(\theta^{1}),\vec{\xi};x-y,y,z),\mbox{ and}\cr\widetilde{F}^{(AB)(CD))}(\theta^{5},\vec{\xi};x-y,y,z)=\widetilde{F}^{((AB)C)D}(\phi_{[(AB)C]D}^{\ast}(\theta^{5}),\vec{\xi};x-y,y,z),\end{array}

on
{\cal D}^{3}
at least, where
\phi^{\ast}:U^{\vee}\to W^{\vee}
denotes the dual of

\phi:W\to U
. We hence have

\begin{array}[]{l}\widetilde{F}^{((AB)C)D}((\phi_{[AB]C}^{\ast}\otimes 1_{D})(\phi_{[A(BC)]D}^{\ast}((1_{A}\otimes\phi_{[BC]D}^{\ast})(\theta^{1}))),\vec{\xi};x-y,y-z,z)\cr\mbox{}\quad=\widetilde{F}^{A(B(CD))}(\theta^{1},\vec{\xi};x,y,z)=\widetilde{F}^{((AB)C)D}(\phi_{[(AB)C]D}^{\ast}(\phi_{[AB](CD)}^{\ast}(\theta^{1})),\vec{\xi};x-y,y-z,z)\end{array}

for all
\theta^{1}\in(A(B(CD)))^{\vee}
and
\vec{\xi}
.
As a consequence, we obtain
1_{A}\otimes\phi_{[BC]D}\circ\phi_{[A(BC)]D}\circ\phi_{[AB]C}\otimes 1_{D}=\phi_{[AB](CD)}\circ\phi_{[(AB)C]D}
,
which implies the commutativity of the diagram.

This completes the proof of Theorem 5.1.

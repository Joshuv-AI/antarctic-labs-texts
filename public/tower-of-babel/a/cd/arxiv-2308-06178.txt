On the local central limit theorem for interacting spin systems
Aldo Procacci, Benedetto Scoppola

Abstract
We prove the equivalence between integral and local central limit theorem for spin system interacting via an absolutely summable pair potential without any conditions on the temperature of the system.



1. Introduction

Since the pioneering work by Dobrushin and Tirozzi
[
3
]
, a certain effort has been spent in statistical mechanics in order to understand conditions for the equivalence between the integral and the local central limit theorem in the framework of interacting discrete spin systems. This kind of results are very useful for instance in the context of the problem of the equivalence of the ensembles.

In
[
3
]
the authors proved the equivalence between integral and local central limit theorem for spin systems in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with finite range interaction, while few years later Campanino, Capocaccia and Tirozzi
[
2
]
generalized the proof to long range (translational invariant) pair potential
J(x-y)
satisfying the condition

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|J(x)|^{1/2}<\infty.

[formula]

Recently Endo and Margarint, in
[
4
]
, presented a similar proof in which the conditions on the decay of the pair potential
J(x-y)
are conveniently weakened allowing
J(x)
to be absolutely summable, but with the additional assumption that the temperature is sufficiently high. In
[
4
]
a remarkably large literature on the subject has been provided, and we refer the reader to that discussion.

In this paper we prove the equivalence between integral and local central limit theorem for spin systems interacting via an absolutely summable pair potential (which does not need to be translational invariant), without any further assumption on the temperature of the system. In order to achieve this result we make a judicious use of classical results concerning cluster and polymer expansion, together with a suitable decimation of the space as in
[
2
]
. In particular, we use the estimates on polymer activities based on tree graphs inequalities
valid for pair potentials originally discussed in
[
9
,
10
]
, the recent generalization of the Penrose tree graph identity given in
[
11
]
and the results and estimates on the gas of non overlapping subsets discussed in
[
5
,
1
]
.

To our knowledge, although it has been known for a long time that the integral central limit theorem holds for spin systems interacting via an absolutely summable potential with some additional conditions (for instance FKG inequalities, see e.g.
[
7
,
8
]
), there are no results in literature about the validity of the local central limit theorem with the same conditions.

2. The model

We work in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. In each site
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we define a spin variable
s_{x}
which we suppose for simplicity to take values in a bounded interval
I
of
\mathbb{Z}
,
(i.e.
s_{x}
can take all value set
I=[m,m+1,\dots,n-1,n]
with
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
m<n
). However,
all results obtained in this paper can be generalized straightforwardly for bounded spin systems in which
the variable
s_{x}
is
lattice distributed
with maximal span, see
[
3
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
4
]
. We recall that random variable
s_{x}
is lattice distributed if there are two real numbers
a
and
h
such that
s_{x}=a+mh
with
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. The number
h
is called the span of the distribution and
h
is maximal if the same representation for
s_{x}
cannot be obtained for any
a^{\prime},h^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
h^{\prime}>h
(see
[
6
]
).

We set

\sigma=\max_{s_{x}\in I}|s_{x}|

and
|I|
the cardinality of
I
. Let
\Omega
denote the set of all spin
configurations in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and if
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
then
\Omega_{\Lambda}
is the set of all spin configurations in
\Lambda
. We denote by
s_{\Lambda}
a generic configuration in
\Lambda
. Note that if
\Lambda
is finite then

\Omega_{\Lambda}
contains
|I|^{|\Lambda|}
different configurations where
|I|
denotes the cardinality of
I
. Given a boundary condition
\omega\in\Omega
and given
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
finite, the Hamiltonian
H^{\omega}_{\Lambda}(s_{\Lambda})

of the system in
\Lambda
as the function from
\Omega_{\Lambda}
to
\mathbb{R}
given by

-H^{\omega}_{\Lambda}(s_{\Lambda})=\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\Lambda}J_{xy}s_{x}s_{y}+\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{y\in\Lambda^{c}}J_{xy}s_{x}\omega_{y}

[formula]

(the minus sign is there just to accord to physicists’ convention). We set

\Lambda^{c}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\Lambda
and

h^{\omega}_{x}(s_{x})=\sum_{y\in\Lambda^{c}}J_{xy}s_{x}\omega_{y}.

[formula]

The pair potential
J_{xy}\in\mathbb{R}
(with no definite sign) is supposed to be absolutely summable. Namely,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{y\neq x}|J_{xy}|=J<+\infty.

[formula]

The probability of a spin configuration
s_{\Lambda}
in
\Lambda
(Gibbs measure) is given by

{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{\Lambda}(s_{\Lambda})={e^{-H^{\omega}_{\Lambda}(s_{\Lambda})}\over Z_{\Lambda}^{\omega}}={e^{\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\Lambda_{n}}J_{xy}s_{x}s_{y}+\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}h^{\omega}_{x}(s_{x})}\over Z^{\omega}_{\Lambda}}

[formula]

where

Z_{\Lambda}^{\omega}=\sum_{s_{\Lambda}\in\Omega_{\Lambda}}e^{\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\Lambda_{n}}J_{xy}s_{x}s_{y}+\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}h^{\omega}_{x}(s_{x})}

[formula]

is the partition function.

We further define the single spin probability distribution at the site
x\in\Lambda
as

p^{\omega}_{x}(s_{x})={e^{h^{\omega}_{x}(s_{x})}\over Z_{x}^{\omega}}

[formula]

where
Z_{x}^{\omega}=\sum_{s_{x}\in I}e^{h^{\omega}_{x}(s_{x})}
. Hereafter
E^{\omega}_{x}(\cdot)
will denote hereafter the expectation w.r.t. the single spin probability measure
p_{x}^{\omega}(s_{x})
. Note that, due to (2.3) we have, for all allowed values of
s_{x}
and uniformly in
x
and
\omega
that

|h^{\omega}_{x}(s_{x})|\leq J\sigma^{2}
. Therefore
the single spin probability distribution satisfies, for any
s_{x}
and any
\omega
, the lower bound

p^{\omega}_{x}(s_{x})\geq{e^{-2J\sigma^{2}}\over|I|}\doteq\kappa(J,\sigma).

[formula]

Let now
\Lambda_{n}
be the cube of size
2n+1
centered at the origin. We set shortly
{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\cdot)={\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{n}(\cdot)

and

{\mathbb{E}}^{\omega}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\cdot)={\mathbb{E}}^{\omega}_{n}(\cdot)
for the Gibbs measure on
\Omega_{\Lambda_{n}}
and its expected value respectively.
We further define the random variables

S_{n}=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}s_{x}

and

{\bar{S}}_{n}={S_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}^{\omega}_{n}(S_{n})\over\sqrt{D_{n}}}

where
D_{n}
is the variance of
S_{n}
.

Definition 1

The integral central limit theorem holds for the spin system under study if the following conditions are satisfied.

\lim_{n\to\infty}{D_{n}\over|\Lambda_{n}|}=\alpha>0

[formula]

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\omega}(\bar{S}_{n}<x)={1\over\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-{z^{2}\over 2}}dz

[formula]

Definition 2

The local central limit theorem holds for the spin system under study if (2.8) holds
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{p}\Big|\sqrt{D_{n}}\,{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{n}(S_{n}=p)-{e^{-{z^{2}_{n}(p)\over 2}}\over\sqrt{2\pi}}\Big|=0

[formula]

where

z_{n}(p)={p-{\mathbb{E}}^{\omega}_{n}(S_{n})\over\sqrt{D_{n}}}.

The result of the present paper consists in the proof of the following theorem.

Theorem 1

Under the assumption (2.3), if the sequence of Gibbs measures
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\omega}
satisfies the Integral central limit theorem, then it satisfies also the local central limit theorem.

The rest of the paper is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1
.

3. Preliminaries

The starting point is to observe that

{1\over\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-{z^{2}_{n}(p)\over 2}}={1\over 2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-itz_{n}(p)}e^{-{t^{2}\over 2}}dt

[formula]

and

\sqrt{D_{n}}\,{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{n}(S_{n}=p)={1\over 2\pi}\int_{-\pi\sqrt{D_{n}}}^{+\pi\sqrt{D_{n}}}{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it\bar{S}_{n}})e^{-itz_{n}(p)}dt.

[formula]

Equality (3.1) is a standard Gaussian integral while (3.2) holds in general for spin random variables
s_{x}
which are
lattice distributed
(see above). We remind that in our case
p
can take only integer values.
Let us set

G_{n}=2\pi\left(\sqrt{D_{n}}\,{\mathbb{P}}_{n}^{\omega}(S_{n}=p)-{1\over\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-{z^{2}_{n}(p)\over 2}}\right).

Then, by (3.2) and (3.1) we have

G_{n}=\int_{-\pi\sqrt{D_{n}}}^{+\pi\sqrt{D_{n}}}{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it\bar{S}_{n}})e^{-itz_{n}(p)}dt-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-itz_{n}(p)}e^{-{t^{2}\over 2}}dt.

Let now
\delta<\pi
and let
A<\delta\sqrt{D_{n}}
a sufficiently large positive constant. Thus

|G_{n}|\leq\int\limits_{-A}^{+A}\Big|{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it\bar{S}_{n}})-e^{-{t^{2}\over 2}}\Big|dt+\int\limits_{A<|t|\leq\delta\sqrt{D_{n}}}|{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it\bar{S}_{n}})|dt+~\int\limits_{\delta\sqrt{D_{n}}<|t|\leq\pi\sqrt{D_{n}}}|{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it\bar{S}_{n}})|dt

+~\int\limits_{|t|\geq A}e^{-{t^{2}\over 2}}dt.

Therefore

\sup_{p}\Big|\sqrt{D_{n}}\,{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{n}(S_{n}=p)-{e^{-{z^{2}_{n}(p)\over 2}}\over\sqrt{2\pi}}\Big|\leq{1\over 2\pi}\Big[I^{(1)}_{n}+I^{(2)}_{n}+I^{(3)}_{n}+I^{(4)}_{n}\Big]

where

I^{(1)}_{n}=\int\limits_{-A}^{+A}\Big|{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it\bar{S}_{n}})-e^{-{t^{2}\over 2}}\Big|dt,~~~~~~~~~I^{(2)}_{n}=\int\limits_{A<|t|\leq\delta\sqrt{D_{n}}}|{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it\bar{S}_{n}})|dt

I^{(3)}_{n}=\int\limits_{\delta\sqrt{D_{n}}<|t|\leq\pi\sqrt{D_{n}}}|{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it\bar{S}_{n}})|dt,~~~~~~~~~~~I^{(4)}_{n}=\int\limits_{|t|\geq A}e^{-{t^{2}\over 2}}dt.

Now we have trivially that
I^{(4)}
is as small as we please due to arbitrarily of
A
. Moreover, by the integral central limit theorem, i.e. (2.9), we also have
that
\lim_{n\to\infty}I^{(1)}_{n}=0
. Therefore we need to bound
I^{(2)}_{n}
and
I^{(3)}_{n}
and prove that they go to zero as
n\to\infty
.

Observe that

|{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it\bar{S}_{n}})|~=|{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it{S_{n}\over\sqrt{D_{n}}}})|.

So, by the change of variables
\tau=t/\sqrt{D_{n}}
, and using that
|{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it{S_{n}\over\sqrt{D_{n}}}})|
is
an even function of
t
, the integrals
I^{(2)}_{n}
and
I^{(3)}_{n}
can be written as

I^{(2)}_{n}=2\sqrt{D_{n}}\int\limits_{A\over\sqrt{D_{n}}}^{\delta}|{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it{S_{n}}})|dt,~~~~~~~~I^{(3)}_{n}=2\sqrt{D_{n}}\int\limits_{\delta}^{\pi}|{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it{S_{n}}})|dt.

[formula]

We now define the decimation introduced in
[
2
]
. Let
r_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and define

\mathbb{Z}^{d}(r_{0})=\{(n_{1}r_{0},\dots,n_{d}r_{0}):~n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\},

i.e.
\mathbb{Z}^{d}(r_{0})
is a cubic sublattice of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
of step
r_{0}
. Let
{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}=\Lambda_{n}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}(r_{0})
and

\tilde{S}_{n}=\sum_{x\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}s_{x}
.
Then

{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it{S_{n}}})={\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}({\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{itS_{n}}|s_{\Lambda_{n}\setminus{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}\;{\rm fixed}))=|{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{itS_{n}-\tilde{S}_{n}}{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it\tilde{S}_{n}}|s_{\Lambda_{n}\setminus{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}\;{\rm fixed}))|.

Thus

\begin{aligned} |{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it{S_{n}}})|&\leq\sup_{\omega\in\Omega_{(\Lambda_{n}\setminus{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n})\cup\Lambda_{n}^{c}}}|{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it\tilde{S}_{n}}|s_{\Lambda_{n}\setminus{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}\;{\rm fixed})|=\sup_{\omega\in\Omega_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}^{c}}}|\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it\tilde{S}_{n}})|\end{aligned}.

[formula]

Here above
{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}^{c}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}=(\Lambda_{n}\setminus{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n})\cup\Lambda_{n}^{c}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}
is the expectation w.r.t. the measure

\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{\omega}_{n}(s_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}})={e^{\sum_{\{x,y\}\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}J_{xy}s_{x}s_{y}+\sum_{x\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}h^{\omega}_{x}(s_{x})}\over Z^{\omega}_{n}}

[formula]

where now

h^{\omega}_{x}(s_{x})=\sum_{y\in\tilde{\Lambda}_{n}^{c}}J_{xy}s_{x}\omega_{y}

and

Z^{\omega}_{n}=\sum_{s_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}\in\Omega_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}}{e^{-\sum_{\{x,y\}\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}J_{xy}s_{x}s_{y}+\sum_{x\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}h^{\omega}_{x}(s_{x})}}.

We set

J_{r_{0}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}(r_{0})}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}(r_{0})\atop y\neq x}|J_{xy}|

[formula]

Note that
J_{r_{0}}
can be done as small as we please by taking
r_{0}
sufficiently large. Moreover

We now state a key lemma from which Theorem
1
follows as an immediate corollary.

Lemma 2

Let
\kappa(J,\sigma)
as in (2.7), let
\delta
,
C
and
c
the positive numbers given by

\delta={\kappa(J,\sigma)\over 12\sigma},~~~~~~C=\sigma^{2}{\kappa(J,\sigma)\over 4}~,~~~~~~~~c=\kappa(J,\sigma)\sin^{2}\left(\delta/2\right)

[formula]

and let
r_{0}
be chosen such that

e^{{J_{r_{0}}\over 2}}J_{r_{0}}^{1\over 2}\leq\min\Big\{{[\kappa(J,\sigma)]^{3\over 2}\over 96\sqrt{2}\sigma^{3}e^{2}},{e^{-{5c\over 4}}(e^{c\over 4}-1)\over(1+\delta\sigma)e\sigma^{2}}\Big\},

[formula]

then

(a)

For any
t\in(0,\delta]

|\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it{S_{n}}})|\leq e^{-{C\over 2}|{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}|t^{2}}

[formula]

(b)

For any
t\in(\delta,\pi]

|\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it{\tilde{S}_{n}}})|\leq e^{-{c\over 2}|{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}|}

[formula]

Assuming Lemma
2
, Theorem
1
follows straightforwardly.
Indeed, by (3.9), (3.10) and using (3.4), the integrals
I^{(2)}_{n}
and
I^{(3)}_{n}
given in (3.3) can be bounded as

I^{(2)}_{n}\leq 2~\sqrt{{D_{n}}\over|{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}|}\int\limits_{A\sqrt{|{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}|\over D_{n}}}^{\delta\sqrt{|{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}|}}e^{-{C\over 2}\tau^{2}}d\tau,

[formula]

I^{(3)}_{n}\leq 2\sqrt{D_{n}}(\pi-\delta)e^{-{c\over 2}{|{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}|}}.

[formula]

The r.h.s. of (3.11) goes to zero as
n\to\infty
due to (2.8) and the arbitrariness of
A
while
the r.h.s. of (3.12) goes to zero as
n\to\infty
due to (2.8).

The rest of the paper is devoted to the proof of Lemma
2
.

4. Proof of Lemma
2
, part (a)

Recalling the definition of the single spin
distribution (2.6), the expectation
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\omega}_{n}(\cdot)
w. r. t. the measure (3.5) can be written as

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}^{\omega}_{n}(~\cdot~)={\sum_{s_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}\in\Omega_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}}e^{-\sum_{\{x,y\}\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}J_{xy}s_{x}s_{y}}(~\cdot~)\prod_{x\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}p^{\omega}_{x}(s_{x})\over\sum_{s_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}\in\Omega_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}}e^{-\sum_{\{x,y\}\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}J_{xy}s_{x}s_{y}}\prod_{x\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}p^{\omega}_{x}(s_{x})}.

[formula]

Therefore we can write

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it{\tilde{S}_{n}}})={\Xi^{\omega}_{n}(t)\over\Xi^{\omega}_{n}(0)}

[formula]

where

\Xi_{n}(t)=\sum_{s_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}\in\Omega_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}}\prod_{\{x,y\}\subset{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}e^{J_{xy}s_{x}s_{y}}\prod_{x\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}e^{its_{x}}\prod_{x\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}p^{\omega}_{x}(s_{x}).

[formula]

Proposition 3

The function
\Xi^{\omega}_{n}(t)
can be rewritten as the
t
-dependent grand canonical partition function
of a gas of non overlapping subsets of
{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}
. Namely the following identity holds.

\Xi^{\omega}_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}(t)=1+\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\{R_{1},\dots,R_{k}\}:\,R_{i}\subset{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}\atop R_{i}\neq\emptyset,~R_{i}\cap R_{j}=\emptyset}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{t}(R_{i})

[formula]

with activities given by

\xi_{t}(R)=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{s_{R}\in\Omega_{R}}\sum\limits_{g\in G_{R}}\sum\limits_{S\subset R}\prod\limits_{\{x,y\}\in E_{g}}(e^{J_{xy}s_{x}s_{y}}-1)\prod\limits_{x\in S}(e^{its_{x}}-1)\prod\limits_{x\in R}p_{x}^{\omega}(s_{x})&{\rm if}~|R|\geq 2\\
\\
\sum\limits_{s_{x}\in I}(e^{its_{x}}-1)p_{x}^{\omega}(s_{x})&{\rm if}~R=\{x\}\end{cases}

[formula]

where
G_{R}
denotes the set of all connected graphs with vertex set
R
and
E_{g}
denotes the edge set of
g\in G_{R}
.

Proof
.
The identity (4.4) is obtained via the standard Mayer trick on both the one point terms
of the form
e^{its_{x}}
and the two point terms of the form
e^{J_{xy}s_{x}s_{y}}
. Namely in the r.h.s. of (4.3) write

\prod_{x\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}e^{its_{x}}=\prod_{x\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}[(e^{its_{x}}-1)+1]
and
\prod_{\{x,y\}\subset{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}e^{J_{xy}s_{x}s_{y}}=\prod_{\{x,y\}\subset{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}[(e^{J_{xy}s_{x}s_{y}}-1)+1]
and develop the products.

\Box

In the next section we will make use of the following well know expression (see e.g.
[
5
]
) of the logarithm of
\Xi^{\omega}_{n}(t)
.

\ln\Xi^{\omega}_{n}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{1\over k!}\sum_{(R_{1},\dots,R_{k})\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{k}}\Phi^{T}(R_{1},\dots,R_{k})\prod_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{t}(R_{i})

[formula]

where
\mathcal{P}_{n}=\{R\subset{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}:~|R|\geq 1\}
and

\phi^{T}(R_{1},\dots,R_{k})=\begin{cases}1&{\text{i}f}~k=1\\
\sum\limits_{g\in G_{k}}\prod\limits_{\{i,j\}\in E_{g}}(e^{-V_{h.c}(R_{i},R_{j})}-1)&{\text{i}f}~k\geq 2\end{cases}

[formula]

with
V_{h.c}(R_{i},R_{j})=+\infty
if
R_{i}\cap R_{j}\neq\emptyset
and
V_{h.c}(R_{i},R_{j})=0
if
R_{i}\cap R_{j}=\emptyset
and
{G}_{k}
denotes the set of all connected
graphs with vertex set
\{1,2,\dots,k\}\doteq[k]
.

4.1 Estimate on
|\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it\tilde{S}_{n}})|
when
t\in(0,\delta]
with
\delta
given in (3.7)

By Proposition
3
,

|\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it{S_{n}}})|=\left|\exp\Big\{\ln\Xi^{\omega}_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}(t)-\ln\Xi^{\omega}_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}(0)\Big\}\right|=\exp\Big\{{\Re}\Big(\ln\Xi^{\omega}_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}(t)-\ln\Xi^{\omega}_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}(0)\Big)\Big\}.

Then, by (4.6) we have that

\Re\Big(\ln\Xi^{\omega}_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}(J,t)-\ln\Xi^{\omega}_{{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}(0)\Big)=\sum_{k\geq 1}{1\over k!}\sum_{(R_{1},\dots,R_{k})\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{k}}\Phi^{T}(R_{1},\dots,R_{k})\Re\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{t}(R_{i})-\prod_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{0}(R_{i})\Big].

[formula]

Set

F_{k}(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{t}(R_{i})-\prod_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{0}(R_{i}).

[formula]

It is not difficult (but tedious) to check that

\Re{dF_{k}(t)\over dt}\big|_{t=0}=0
.

Moreover, since we also have that
F_{k}(0)=0
, by the Taylor remainder theorem, we can conclude that there exists a
0<\theta<t<\delta
such that

\displaystyle|\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it{S_{n}}})|

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{{t^{2}\over 2}\sum_{k\geq 1}{1\over k!}\sum_{(R_{1},\dots,R_{k})\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{k}}\Phi^{T}(R_{1},\dots,R_{k})\Re{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{t}(R_{i})\Big]\Big|_{t=\theta}\right\}

where
\mathcal{P}_{n}=\{R\subset{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}:|R|\geq 1\}
. Let

G(\theta)=\sum_{k\geq 1}{1\over k!}\sum_{(R_{1},\dots,R_{k})\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{k}}\Phi^{T}(R_{1},\dots,R_{k}){d^{2}\over dt^{2}}\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{t}(R_{i})\Big]\Big|_{t=\theta}.

We can write

G(\theta)=G_{1}(\theta)+G_{2}(\theta)+G_{3}(\theta)+G_{4}(\theta)

where

G_{1}(\theta)=\sum_{x\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}\Phi^{T}(\{x\}){d^{2}\over dt^{2}}\xi_{t}(\{x\})|_{t=\theta},

[formula]

G_{2}(\theta)=\sum_{x\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}{1\over 2}\Phi^{T}(\{x\},\{x\}){d^{2}\over dt^{2}}\xi^{2}_{t}(\{x\})|_{t=\theta},

[formula]

G_{3}(\theta)=\sum_{x\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{n}}\sum_{k\geq 3}{1\over k!}\Phi^{T}(\underbrace{\{x\},\dots,\{x\}}_{k~{\rm times}}){d^{2}\over dt^{2}}\xi^{k}_{t}(\{x\})|_{t=\theta},

[formula]

G_{4}(\theta)=\sum_{k\geq 1}{1\over k!}\sum_{(R_{1},\dots,R_{k})\in\mathcal{P}_{n}^{k}\atop\exists i:\;|R_{i}|\geq 2}\Phi^{T}(R_{1},\dots,R_{k}){d^{2}\over dt^{2}}\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{t}(R_{i})\Big]\Big|_{t=\theta},

[formula]

so that

\displaystyle|\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(e^{it{S_{n}}})|

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{{t^{2}\over 2}\Re\Big(G_{1}(\theta)+G_{2}(\theta)+G_{3}(\theta)+G_{4}(\theta)\Big)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{{t^{2}\over 2}\Big(\Re G_{1}(\theta)+\Re G_{2}(\theta)+|G_{3}(\theta)|+|G_{4}(\theta)|\Big)\right\}.

In order to control
G_{i}(\theta)
(
i=1,2,3,4
) we need to evaluate
{d\over dt}\xi_{t}(\{x\})
and
{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}\xi_{t}(\{x\})
.
Recalling the definition of
\xi_{t}(\{x\})
given in (4.5), we have

{d\over dt}\xi_{t}(\{x\})=i\sum\limits_{s_{x}\in I}s_{x}e^{its_{x}}p_{x}^{\omega}(s_{x})~,~~~~~~~~~~{d^{2}\over dt^{2}}\xi_{t}(\{x\})=-\sum\limits_{s_{x}\in I}p_{x}^{\omega}(s_{x})s^{2}_{x}e^{its_{x}}

[formula]

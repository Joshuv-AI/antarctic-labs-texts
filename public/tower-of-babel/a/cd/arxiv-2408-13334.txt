On the Hochschild homology of curved algebras
Mark E. Walker

Abstract
. We compute the Hochschild homology of the differential graded category of perfect curved modules over suitable curved rings, giving what might be termed “de Rham models” for such. This represents a generalization of previous results by Dyckerhoff, Efimov, Polishchuk, and Positselski concerning the Hochschild homology of matrix factorizations. A key ingredient in the proof is a theorem due to B. Briggs, which represents a “curved version” of a celebrated theorem of Hopkins and Neeman. The proof of Briggs’ Theorem is included in an appendix to this paper.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, by a
curved ring
we mean a pair
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
, where
A
is a commutative
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ring that is concentrated in even degrees (i.e.,
A^{i}=0
for
i
odd)
and
w
, known as the
curvature
, is an element of cohomological degree
2
:
w\in A^{2}
.
A
curved module
over
\mathcal{A}
is a pair
(M,\partial)
consisting of a graded
A
-module
M
equipped with an
A
-linear endomorphism
\partial

of degree
1
satisfying
\partial^{2}(m)=w\cdot m
for all
m\in M
.
The collection of curved
\mathcal{A}
-modules form a differential graded (dg) category, written as
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
.
A curved module
(M,\partial)
will be called
perfect
if
M
is finitely generated and projective as a graded
A
-module.
We write
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
for the full dg subcategory on the collection of all perfect curved
\mathcal{A}
-modules.

The main Theorem of this paper computes the Hochschild homology of
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
over a suitable ground ring:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded commutative ring that is concentrated in even degrees, regular, and excellent,
and let
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
be a curved ring such that
A
is an essentially smooth
k
-algebra.
Then there is an isomorphism

HH(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))\cong{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k},dw)

in the derived category of dg
A
-modules.

Moreover, when
k
is an algebra over the field of rational numbers,
this isomorphism is represented by a natural quasi-isomorphism of explicit dg
k
-modules, under which the Connes’ operator
on Hochschild homology corresponds with the de Rham differential on
\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k}
.

Let us explain some of the terminology in this theorem.

HH(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))
denotes the Hochschild homology, taken relative to

k
, of the
k
-linear dg category
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
. It is defined in a manner analogous to the formula

HH(R)=R\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{R\otimes_{k}R}R
for an ordinary
k
-algebra
R
; see (
2.1
) below for the precise definition. We will sometimes write it as
HH(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})/k)
when the role of
the base ring needs emphasis.
By
(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k},dw)
we mean the dg
A
-module consiting of the graded
A
-module
\bigoplus_{q}\Sigma^{q}\Omega^{q}_{A/k}
equipped with the differential
given as multiplication by
dw
.
(Here,
\Omega^{1}_{A/k}
is the module of Kahler differentials, equipped with the grading induced by that on
A
,

\Omega^{q}_{A/k}
is its
q
-th exterior power over
A
and
\Sigma^{q}
refers to shifting the grading by
q
.)
Equivalently,
(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k},dw)
is the totalization of the complex

\cdots\to 0\to A\xrightarrow{dw}\Sigma^{2}\Omega^{1}_{A/k}\xrightarrow{dw}\Sigma^{4}\Omega^{2}_{A/k}\xrightarrow{dw}\cdots\xrightarrow{dw}\Sigma^{2d}\Omega^{d}_{A/k}\to 0\to\cdots

of graded
A
-modules.
By
\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})
we mean the Zariski closed subset
\{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Spec}(A)\mid w\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\}
of
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
and
{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})}
refers
to taking local cohomology along it.

As a simple example, if the curvature is trivial,
w=0
, then
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
is the dg category of perfect dg
A
-modules,
\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
, and thus our results
yield the (previously well-known) isomorphism

HH(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A))\cong\bigoplus_{j}\Sigma^{j}\Omega^{j}_{A/k}.

Of greater interest is when
w
is a non-zero-divisor in
A
(i.e., it is nonzero on each component of the regular scheme
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
).
In this case,
\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})
coincides with

\operatorname{Nonreg}(A/w)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Spec}(A)\mid w\in{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ and $(A/w)_{{\mathfrak{p}}}$ is not a regular ring}\},

the non-regular locus of the hypersurface
A/w
.
For the rest of the introduction, we assume
w
is a non-zero-divisor.

1.1.
The case when
k
is a perfect field

When
k
is a perfect field (and
w
is a non-zero-divisor) we have

\operatorname{Nonreg}(A/w)=\operatorname{Sing}_{k}(A/w):=\{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Spec}(A/w)\mid\text{$A/w$ is not smooth over $k$ near ${\mathfrak{p}}$}\}.

Let us write
\operatorname{Sing}(w)
for the singular locus of the morphism

\operatorname{Spec}(A)\xrightarrow{w}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}=\operatorname{Spec}(k[x])
of affine
k
-schemes induced by the
k
-algebra map
k[x]\to A
sending
x
to
w
.
The Jacobian criterion gives that
\operatorname{Sing}(w)
coincides with the support of
(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k},dw)
, and so we
have

\operatorname{Nonreg}(A/w)=\operatorname{Sing}(w)\cap V(w)=\operatorname{supp}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k},dw)\cap V(w).

We may thus deduce the following consequence of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem

1.1
, suppose in addition that
w
is a
non-zero-divisor and
k
is a perfect field.
Then there are isomorphism in the derived category

HH(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))\cong{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{V(w)}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k},dw).

of dg
A
-modules.
In particular, if
\operatorname{Sing}(w)\subseteq V(w)
, then

HH(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))\cong(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k},dw).

1.2.
Main Examples

We now discuss an important collection of examples. Throughout the rest of this introduction, we fix a field

F
, an essentially smooth (ungraded)
F
-algebra
Q
,
and elements
f_{1},\dots,f_{c}
in
Q
. Set
\tilde{Q}:=Q[t_{1},\dots,t_{c}]
for degree two indeterminants
t_{1},\dots,t_{c}
and
\tilde{f}:=\sum_{i}f_{i}t_{i}\in\tilde{Q}
.
Then
(\tilde{Q},\tilde{f})
is a curved algebra and it is essentially smooth over both
F
and
F[t_{1},\dots,t_{c}]
.
In this situation we have
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathbb{A}^{c}_{Q}\xrightarrow{\tilde{f}}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F})\subseteq V(\tilde{f})
, because
\tilde{f}=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\tilde{f}}{\partial t_{i}}t_{i}
,. Corollary
1.2
thus
specializes to give:

Corollary 1.3
.

With the notation just introduced, assume also that
F
is a perfect field.
Then there is an isomorphism

HH(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\tilde{Q},\tilde{f})/F)\xrightarrow{\cong}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{\tilde{Q}/F},d\tilde{f}).

in the derived category of
\tilde{Q}
-modules.
Moreover, if
\operatorname{char}(F)=0
, this isomorphism may be realized as a natural quasi-isomorphism of dg
F
-algebras, under which the Connes’ operator corresponds
with the de Rham differential.

Corollary
1.3
allows for a calculation of the Hochschild homology of the bounded derived category of modules over a complete intersection.
In detail, in the setting of this Corollary, suppose
f_{1},\dots,f_{c}
form a regular sequence and set
R=Q/(f_{1},\dots,f_{c})
.
Then it is known that the dg category
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\tilde{Q},\tilde{f})
is quasi-equivalent to
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{dg}}(R)
, a dg enhancement of the
bounded derived category of
R
-modules; see
[
24
]
. Since Hochschild homology in invariant under quasi-equivalence, Corollary
1.3
gives
an isomorphism

HH(\mathcal{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{dg}}(R)/F)\cong(\Omega^{\cdot}_{\tilde{Q}/F},d\tilde{f})

in the derived category of
Q
-modules.

1.3.
An example where
k
is not a field

In our running example, it also makes sense to take
k=F[t_{1}\dots,t_{c}]
.
Then
\Omega^{1}_{\tilde{Q}/k}\cong\Omega^{1}_{Q/F}[t_{1},\dots,t_{c}]
and
d\tilde{f}=\sum_{i}t_{i}df_{i}
, which leads to a simpler complex
(\Omega^{\cdot}_{\tilde{Q}/k},dw)
.
But the singular locus of
\mathbb{A}^{c}_{Q}\xrightarrow{\tilde{f}}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}=\mathbb{A}^{c}_{F}\times_{F}\mathbb{A}^{1}
often fails to be contained in
V(\tilde{f})
,
making the role of local cohomology meaningful.
For instance if
Q=F[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, or any localization of such, the singular locus of the morphism
\mathbb{A}^{c+n}_{F}\cong\operatorname{Spec}(\tilde{Q})\xrightarrow{\tilde{f}}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\cong\mathbb{A}^{c+1}_{F}

is the set of solutions of
(t_{1},\dots,t_{c})\cdot J=(0,\dots,0)

where
J
is the
c\times n
Jacobian matrix
(\partial f_{i}/\partial x_{j})
; this is typically not contained in
V(\tilde{f})
.

1.4.
The codimension one case and matrix factorizations

Let us specialize our running example to the case
c=1
, so that
\mathcal{A}=(Q[t],ft)
and
R=Q/f
is a hypersurface. Taking
k=F[t]
, we have

HH((Q[t],ft)/F[t])\cong{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{\operatorname{Nonreg}(Q[t]/ft)}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{Q/k}[t],tdf).

An important variant of this is given by the curved algebra
(Q[t,t^{-1}],ft)
over the base ring
k=F[t,t^{-1}]
.
In this case, we may identify
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(Q[t,t^{-1}],ft)
with the
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded
dg category of
matrix factorizations
of
f\in Q
, written
\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Q,f)
. The objects are
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded
finitely generated projective
Q
-modules equipped with odd degree endomorphisms that square to
f
, and the hom sets are
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded complexes of
Q
-modules.
The associated homotopy category is isomorphic to the
singularity
category
of
R=Q/f
; see
[
3
, Theorem 4.4.1]
and
[
28
, Theorem 3.9]
.
In general, a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded
F
-linear dg category
\mathcal{C}
is the same thing as a
F[t,t^{-1}]
linear dg category where
t
is an indeterminant of degree
2
.
We will write
HH_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\mathcal{C})
for
HH(\mathcal{C}/F[t,t^{-1}])
.
Our results thus specialize to give:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
F
be a field,
Q
an essentially smooth
F
-algebra, and
f\in Q
a non-zero-divisor.

(1)

If
F
is perfect and the only singular value of the morphism
\operatorname{Spec}(Q)\xrightarrow{f}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}
is the origin,
then we have an isomorphism

HH_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Q,f))\cong(\Omega^{\mathbb{Z}/2}_{Q/F},df)

where the superscript
\mathbb{Z}/2
indicates forming the
\mathbb{Z}/2
-folding of this complex.

(2)

More generally, there exists an isomorphism

HH_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Q,f))\cong{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{V(f)}(\Omega^{\mathbb{Z}/2}_{Q/F},df)=\Sigma^{-1}\operatorname{cone}\left((\Omega^{\mathbb{Z}/2}_{Q/F},df)\hookrightarrow(\Omega^{\mathbb{Z}/2}_{Q/F}[1/f],df)\right).

Example 1.5
.

Suppose
F
is a perfect field and
f\in F[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is such that the only singular value of the
morphism
f:\mathbb{A}^{n}_{F}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}
is at the origin.
Then
\frac{\partial f}{x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial f}{x_{n}}
form a regular sequence and hence
the Corollary gives an isomorphism

HH_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(F[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}],f))\cong\Sigma^{-n}\left(\frac{F[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]}{(\frac{\partial f}{x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial f}{x_{n}})}\right)

in the derived category.

If the morphism
\operatorname{Spec}(Q)\xrightarrow{f}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}
has only a finite number of singular values, which is always the case when
\operatorname{char}(F)=0
,
then, up to quasi-isomorphism, the dg
A
-module
(\Omega^{\cdot}_{Q/F},df)
is a finite direct sum indexed by these values, and

{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{V(f)}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{Q/F},df)
is the summand corresponding to the origin. In other words, in this situation we may localize
Q
a bit, without
affecting the dg category
\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Q,f)
up to quasi-equivalence, and we will arrive at a situation in which the local cohomology functor
{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{V(f)}
is unneeded.

In characteristic
p>0
things can be more complicated, and indeed
HH(\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Q,f))
need not even have finitely generated homology.
For instance if
Q=F[x]
and
f=x^{p}
with
p=\operatorname{char}(F)
, then every point of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}
is a singular value of the morphism
\operatorname{Spec}(Q)=\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}\xrightarrow{z\mapsto z^{p}}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}
.
In this case the Corollary gives

HH_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Q,f))\cong\Gamma_{(x)}(F[x]\xrightarrow{0}F[x]dx)=\left(\frac{F[x,x^{-1}]}{F[x]}\xrightarrow{0}\frac{F[x,x^{-1}]}{F[x]}dx\right),

and the homology of
HH(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))
is not finitely generated as a
Q
-module. This is related to the fact that
\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Q,f)
is not a “homologically smooth”

\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded category over
F
. Indeed, in Theorem
8.3
below, we show:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
Q
be an essentially smooth
F
-algebra with
F
a perfect field
and assume
f
is a non-zero-divisor in
Q
.
The
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded dg category
\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Q,f)
is homologically smooth over
F

if and only if the origin in
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}
is an isolated singular value of the morphism of affine schemes
\operatorname{Spec}(Q)\xrightarrow{f}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}
.

Finally, we give an example showing how badly things can behave for imperfect fields:
Suppose
\operatorname{char}(F)=p>0
and
there exists an element
a\in F\setminus F^{p}
. Take
Q=F[x,y]

and
f=y(x^{p}-a)
and set
{\mathfrak{m}}=(y,x^{p}-a)
, a maximal ideal of
Q
whose residue field is the purely inseparable field extension

F(\sqrt[p]{a})
of
F
.
Then
\operatorname{Nonreg}(Q/f)=\{{\mathfrak{m}}\}
and
\operatorname{Sing}(f)=V(x^{p}-a)\subseteq f^{-1}(0)
.
In particular, the only singular value of the morphism
\operatorname{Spec}(Q)\xrightarrow{f}\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}
is the origin.
But, as
df=dy(x^{p}-a)
, the complex
(\Omega^{\mathbb{Z}/2}_{Q},df)
may be identified with the
\mathbb{Z}/2
-folding of the complex

(F[x,y]\xrightarrow{x^{p}-a}F[x,y])\oplus\Sigma^{-1}(F[x,y]\xrightarrow{x^{p}-a}F[x,y]).

This complex is not supported on
V(f)
and thus

HH_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(mf(Q,f))
and
(\Omega^{\mathbb{Z}/2}_{Q/F},df)
differ.

. See

,

2.
Outline of the proofs of the main results

In this section we outline the proofs of the results announced in the introduction; the details are contained in the subsequent sections. We refer the reader to Section

3
for any undefined terms appearing in this section.

For a
k
-linear dg category
C
we write
C^{\mathrm{op}}
for its
opposite
dg category and
C^{e}=C\otimes_{k}C^{\mathrm{op}}
for its
enveloping
dg category. Objects of the latter are
ordered pairs
(X,Y)
of objects of
C
with hom complexes given by
\operatorname{Hom}_{C^{e}}((X,Y),(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}))=\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,X^{\prime})\otimes_{k}\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(Y^{\prime},Y)
.
We write
\Delta^{\mathrm{right}}_{C}
(resp.
\Delta^{\mathrm{left}}_{C}
) for the right (resp. left) dg
C^{e}
-modules given on objects by

\Delta^{\mathrm{right}}_{C}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(Y,X)
(resp.
\Delta^{\mathrm{left}}_{C}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Y)
). The Hochschild homology complex (over
k
) of
C
is by definition the derived tensor product of these two modules:

(2.1)

HH(C)=HH(C/k):=\Delta^{\mathrm{right}}_{C}\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{C^{e}}\Delta^{\mathrm{left}}_{C}

which is an object in the derived category of dg
k
-modules.

For a curved ring
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
, its
opposite
is the curved ring
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}:=(A,-w)
, and
its
enveloping algebra
(over
k
) is
\mathcal{A}^{e}:=\mathcal{A}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}=(A^{e},w^{e})
where
A^{e}:=A\otimes_{k}A

and
w^{e}:=w\otimes 1-1\otimes w
.

A central ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the fully-faithful embedding of
k
-linear dg categories

(2.2)

\psi:\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})^{e}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})

given on objects by
\psi(X,Y)=X\otimes_{k}Y^{*}
, where
Y^{*}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Y,A)\in\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}})
is the
A
-linear dual of
Y
.
The map on hom complexes is given by the natural isomorphisms

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})^{e}}((X_{1},Y_{1}),(X_{2},Y_{2}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X_{1},X_{2})\otimes_{k}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Y_{2},Y_{1})

\displaystyle\cong X_{1}^{*}\otimes_{A}X_{2}\otimes_{k}Y_{2}^{*}\otimes_{A}Y_{1}

\displaystyle\cong(X_{1}\otimes_{k}Y_{1}^{*})^{*}\otimes_{A^{e}}(X^{2}\otimes_{k}Y_{2}^{*})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})}(X_{1}\otimes_{k}Y_{1}^{*},X_{2}\otimes_{k}Y_{2}^{*}).

As we shall see, this functor is not always a Morita equivalence, and this is ultimately the reason why local cohomology enters into the formula for
HH(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))
.

Another important fact is that the dg
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})^{e}
-modules
\Delta^{\mathrm{right}}
and
\Delta^{\mathrm{left}}
can be extended along
\psi
, as follows:
Observe that, since the multiplication map
A^{e}\twoheadrightarrow A
sends
w^{e}
to
0
,
we may regard
A
as either a left or a right curved
\mathcal{A}^{e}
-module. Define
h^{A}
to be the right dg
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})
-module given on objects by

h^{A}(Z):=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(Z,A),\,\text{ for $Z\in\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})$}

and
t^{A}
to be the left dg
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})
-module given on object by

t^{A}(Z):=A\otimes_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}Z\,\text{ for $Z\in\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})$.}

For
X,Y\in\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})
we have isomorphisms

h^{A}(X\otimes_{k}Y^{*})\cong(X^{*}\otimes_{k}Y)\otimes_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}A\cong X^{*}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}Y\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})}(X,Y)

and

t^{A}(X\otimes_{k}Y^{*})\cong A^{\mathrm{right}}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}^{e}}(X\otimes_{k}Y^{*})\cong Y^{*}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}X\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})}(Y,X).

The naturality of these isomorphisms gives isomorphisms of dg
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})^{e}
-modules

(2.3)

\psi_{*}h^{A}\cong\Delta^{\mathrm{left}}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\psi_{*}t^{A}\cong\Delta^{\mathrm{right}}

where
\psi_{*}
denotes restriction of scalars along
\psi
(see
3.3
below).
This gives the identification

(2.4)

HH(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))\cong\psi_{*}h^{A}\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})^{e}}\psi_{*}t^{A}.

Now, for all
X,Y\in\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
, the curved
\mathcal{A}^{e}
-module
X\otimes_{k}Y^{*}
is supported
on the subset
W:=\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(k)}\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})
of
\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_{k}A)=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(k)}\operatorname{Spec}(A)

and thus
\psi
factors through
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}^{W}(\mathcal{A}^{e})
, the full dg subcategory of objects
supported on
W
; see Theorem
5.1
. That is, we have a commutative triangle

(2.5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})^{e}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}^{W}(\mathcal{A}^{e})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

of fully-faithful embeddings of dg categories, where
\iota
is an inclusion.

One may organize the proof of Theorem
1.1
into three major steps:

The first step is to show that the canonical map

(2.6)

\iota_{*}h^{A}\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}^{W}(\mathcal{A}^{e})}\iota_{*}t^{A}\xrightarrow{\cong}{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{W}\left(h^{A}\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})}t^{A}\right)

is an isomorphism in the derived category of dg
A
-modules.
This is part of a larger phenomenon concerning the relationship between tensor products of modules over dg categories and full
dg subcategories defined by support conditions; see Theorem
4.1
for the precise statement.

The second step is to prove
the fully-faithful embedding
\phi
is a Morita equivalence, and thus the canonical map

(2.7)

\psi_{*}h^{A}\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})^{e}}\psi_{*}t^{A}\xrightarrow{\cong}\iota_{*}h^{A}\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}^{W}(\mathcal{A}^{e})}\iota_{*}h^{A}

is an isomorphism in the derived category of dg
A
-modules.
This step is a consequence of a theorem due to B. Briggs presented in the appendix to this paper; see also Theorem
5.1
in the body of this paper.

Combining these two steps with (
2.4
) yields the isomorphism

(2.8)

HH(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))\cong{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{W}\left(h^{A}\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})}t^{A}\right)

in the derived category of dg
A
-modules.
The final step is a calculation of
h^{A}\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})}t^{A}
in terms of twisted de Rham complexes.
This is carried out, for the general case, in Section
6
.
Section
7
concerns the special case
when
k
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra, in which case Theorem
1.1
includes assertions regarding naturality and
the relationship between the Connes’
B
operator and the de Rham differential.
A key ingredient in the proofs of these results is the notion of “type II” derived tensor products and “type II” Hochschild homology, developed in
[
31
]
and
[
29
]
.

Finally, Section
8
builds on the results established in the proof of Theorem
1.1
to establish Theorem
1.6
, characteristing which
matrix factorization categories are homologically smooth.

3.
Background on homological algebra over dg categories

In this section, we collect the needed background
concerning the theory of derived functors of dg modules on dg categories. We start by fixing notation.

3.1.
Notation and terminology

Unless otherwise indicated, all gradings are
\mathbb{Z}
-gradings with components indexed using superscripts, and the differential in a chain complex has degree
1
.
If
M=M^{\cdot}
is a graded module, then
\Sigma^{n}M
is graded by
(\Sigma^{n}M)^{j}:=M^{j+n}
. If
M=(M^{\cdot},d_{M})
is a complex, then
\Sigma^{n}M
is the complex

(\Sigma^{n}M^{\cdot},(-1)^{n}d_{M})
.

Throughout the paper we fix a graded commutative ring
k=\bigoplus_{i}k^{i}
that is concentrated in even degrees (i.e.,
k^{i}=0
for
i

odd), and we assume
k
is regular and excellent (as an ordinary ring,
upon forgetting the grading). In most applications,
k
will be
F
,
F[t_{1},\dots,t_{c}]
or
F[t,t^{-1}]
for a field
F
and indeterminants
t,t_{1},\dots,t_{c}
of degree two.

3.2.
dg categories

Recall that a dg
k
-module is a graded
k
-module
M=\bigoplus_{i}M^{i}
equipped with a
k
-linear endomorphism
d
of degree
1
that squares to
0
.
When
k
is an ordinary ring (i.e.,
k^{i}=0
for all
i\neq 0
), this is the same thing as a complex of
k
-modules. But a dg module over
k
should not be
confused with a complex of graded
k
-modules — the latter determines a dg module upon totalization, but this functor is far from an equivalence.
We write
\mathrm{Mod}(k)
for the abelian category of all dg
k
-modules, in which morphisms are degree zero
k
-linear maps that respect the differentials.
This category comes with an internal tensor product pairing, written
-\otimes_{k}-
, making it a monoidal category.

On occasion, we will need to consider complexes of dg
k
-module,
in which case we will use subscripts, as in
\cdots\to M_{j}\to M_{j-1}\to\cdots
with each
M_{j}
equipped with an “internal” grading
M_{j}=\bigoplus_{i}M_{j}^{i}

and differential
d_{M_{j}}:M_{j}^{i}\to M_{j}^{i+1}
. Given such a complex of dg modules, its
direct sum totalization
is the dg module with
\bigoplus_{i-j=m}M^{i}_{j}
in degree
m

and its
direct product totalization
is the dg module with
\prod_{i-j=m}M^{i}_{J}
in degree
m
.

A
k
-linear dg category
(also known as a
dg category over
k
) is a category enriched over the monoidal category
(\mathrm{Mod}(k),\otimes_{k})
; that is, a
k
-linear dg category, say
C
, consists
of a collection of objects
\operatorname{ob}(C)
, for each pair
X,Y\in\operatorname{ob}(C)
a dg
k
-module
\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Y)
, and for each triple
X,Y,Z\in\operatorname{ob}(C)

a morphism of dg
k
-modules
\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Y)\otimes_{k}\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(Y,Z)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Z)
. This structure is required to satisfy the usual unital and associative axioms.
When
k=\mathbb{Z}
(concentrated in degree
0
), a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear dg category is just called a
dg category
.

For instance, a
k
-linear dg category with just one object is the same thing as a dga (differential graded algebra) over
k
.

The category of all chain complexes of abelian groups, with morphisms given by the usual chain complex of maps between two chain complexes, is a dg category.
More generally, for any graded
k
-algebra
A
, the collection of dg
A
-modules forms a
k
-linear dg category, written as
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)
.

For a curved ring
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
, the collection of all curved-
\mathcal{A}
-modules forms a
A
-linear dg category we write as
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
. The hom complex for a pair of curved modules

M=(M^{\natural},d_{M})
and
N=(N^{\natural},d_{N})
is given by the exact same formulas for dg
A
-modules; in detail,
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})}(M,N)
is the usual graded
A
-module
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)

equipped with differential
\partial
given by
\partial(g)=d_{N}\circ g-(-1)^{|g|}g\circ d_{M}
. Recall that, when
A
is regular, we write
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
for the full dg subcategory
consisting of those curved modules
P
such that
P^{\natural}
is finitely generated and projective as a graded
A
-module.

Given a dg category
C
, we write
Z^{0}C
for the ordinary, pre-additive category with the same objects as
C
and morphisms given by

\operatorname{Hom}_{Z^{0}C}(X,Y)=Z^{0}\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Y)
, the abelian group of degree
0
cycles. The
homotopy category
associated to
C
, written
[C]
, is the pre-additive
category with the same objects as
C
and with
\operatorname{Hom}_{[C]}(X,Y)=H^{0}\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Y)
. In many of the situations of interest in this paper,
Z^{0}C
will be an exact category and

[C]
will be triangulated. For example, the category
Z^{0}\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(k)
may be identified with
\mathrm{Mod}(k)
.

A
dg functor
between two
k
-linear dg categories, say from
C
to
D
, is a functor between categories enriched over
\mathrm{Mod}(k)
. So, it consists of
function
F:\operatorname{ob}(C)\to\operatorname{ob}(D)
on objects and for each pair
X,Y\in\operatorname{ob}(C)
a morphism of dg
k
-modules
F:\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Y)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{D}(F(X),F(Y))
. These maps are required
to respect units and compositions.

3.3.
dg modules over dg categories

Given a
k
-linear dg category
C
, a
(left) dg
C
-module

M
is a dg functor
M:C\to\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(k)
.
So,
M
consists of a collection of dg
k
-modules
M(X)
indexed by
X\in\operatorname{ob}(C)
together with morphisms

\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Y)\otimes_{k}M(X)\to M(Y)
of dg
k
-modules for all pairs of objects, such that the evident unital and associative axioms hold.
When
C
has just one object, and thus may be identified with a dg
k
-algebra
A
, a left dg
C
-module is the same thing as a left dg
A
-module.
Morphisms of dg modules are given by natural transformations of dg functors; when
C
has just one object, this coincides with the usual notion of
a morphism of dg modules over a dg algebra. In general, the collection of all left dg modules over
C
, written
\mathrm{Mod}(C)
, forms an abelian category.

For any integer
i
, the
i
-th suspension
of a dg
C
-module
M
, written
\Sigma^{i}M
, is the composition of
M
with the
i
-th suspension functor on the category
of dg
k
-modules; i.e,
(\Sigma^{i}M)(X)=\Sigma^{i}(M(X))
for all
X\in C
.
Similarly, if
g:M\to N
is a morphism in
\mathrm{Mod}(C)
, its
mapping cone
(or just
cone
for short) is the dg
C
-module defined via composition:
\operatorname{cone}(g)(X)=\operatorname{cone}(g(X):M(X)\to N(X))
.
We have a canonical short exact sequence
0\to N\to\operatorname{cone}(g)\to\Sigma M\to 0
of dg
C
-modules.

Just as
\mathrm{Mod}(k)
may be enriched to the dg category
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(k)
,
we may enrich the collection of all dg
C
-modules to a dg category, which we write as
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)
. In detail, given dg
C
-modules
M
and
N
,

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)}(M,N)
is the kernel of the map

\prod_{X\in C}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(k)}(M(X),N(X))\to\prod_{Y,Z\in C}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(k)}(\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(Y,Z),\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(k)}(M(Y),N(Z)))

sending a tuple
(\alpha_{X})_{X\in C}
to the tuple
(g\mapsto g_{*}\alpha_{Y}-\alpha_{Z}g_{*})_{Y,Z}
, where
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(k)}
refers to hom for the dg category of dg
k
-modules.
The categories
\mathrm{Mod}(C)
and
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)
are related by
\mathrm{Mod}(C)=Z^{0}\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)
.

For a fixed dg
C
-module
N
, we may interpret
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)}(-,N)
as a functor from
\mathrm{Mod}(C)
to
\mathrm{Mod}(k)
, and
as such it is contravariantly left exact, and similarly
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)}(M,-)
is covariantly left exact, just as in more classical settings.

A
graded category
is a dg category
C
in which
\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Y)
has zero differential for all objects
X
and
Y
, and for such a category,
a
graded C-module
is a dg
C
-module in which all differentials are
0
.
For a graded category
C
, we write
\operatorname{grMod}(C)
for the abelian category of all graded
C
-modules.

For any chain complex
M
we write
M^{\natural}
for the graded object obtained by forgetting the differential.
Given a dg category
C
, we write
C^{\natural}
for the graded category with the same objects and homs given by
\operatorname{Hom}_{C^{\natural}}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Y)^{\natural}
.
If
M
is a dg
C
-module, then
M^{\natural}
is the graded
C^{\natural}
-module given by
X\mapsto M(X)^{\natural}
. From the definition of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)}
we have

(3.1)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)}(M,N)^{\natural}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{grMod}(C^{\natural})}(M^{\natural},N^{\natural}).

For any complex
M
, let
H(M)=\bigoplus_{j}H^{j}(M)
be the graded object given by taking its homology.
By
H(C)
we mean the graded category with the same objects as
C
with morphisms given by

\operatorname{Hom}_{H(C)}(X,Y)=H(\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Y)).

Given a (right or left) dg
C
-module
M
, we let
H(M)
denote the graded
H(C)
-module given by
H(M)(X)=H(M(X))
.

The homotopy category associated to the dg category
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)
, namely
[\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)]
, is a triangulated category with translation functor
M\mapsto\Sigma M

and with a distinguished triangle being, by definition, a triangle isomorphic to one of the form
M\xrightarrow{g}N\xrightarrow{\mathrm{can}}\operatorname{cone}(g)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{can}}\Sigma M
for a morphism
g
of
dg modules. A
quasi-isomorphism
of dg
C
-modules is a morphism
M\to N
in
\mathrm{Mod}(C)
that induces a quasi-isomorphism in the usual sense

M(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}N(X)
for all
X\in C
. A dg
C
-module
M
is
quasi-trivial
if
M\to 0
is a quasi-isomorphism.
The
derived category
of a dg category
C
, written
\mathcal{D}(C)
, is the Verdier localization of the triangulated category
[\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)]
given by inverting
all quasi-isomorphisms of dg
C
-modules or, equivalently, modding out by all quasi-trivial dg
C
-modules.

For a dg category
C
, we write
C^{\mathrm{op}}
for the opposite dg category, which has the same objects as
C
but
its hom complexes are given by
\operatorname{Hom}_{C^{\mathrm{op}}}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(Y,X)
. A
right dg module
over
C
is by definition a left dg module over
C^{\mathrm{op}}
.
Equivalently, a right dg
C
-module
N
consists of a collections of dg
k
-modules
N(X)
indexed by
X\in\operatorname{ob}(C)
together with morphisms

N(Y)\otimes_{k}\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Y)\to N(X)
of dg
k
-modules that satisfy the usual unital and associative conditions.

If
\phi:D\to C
is a dg functor between dg categories, and
M
is a dg
C
-module, by
\phi_{*}(M)
we mean the dg
D
-module given by
restriction of scalars

along
\phi
; that is,
\phi_{*}(M)
is the composition of dg functors
M\circ\phi
. Explicitly,
\phi_{*}(M)
sends an object
X
of
D
to the dg
k
-module

M(\phi(X))
and the required pairings are given by the composition of

\operatorname{Hom}_{D}(X,Y)\otimes_{k}M(\phi(X))\xrightarrow{\phi\otimes\operatorname{id}}\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(\phi(X),\phi(Y))\otimes M(\phi(X))\to M(\phi(Y)).

3.4.
Various classes of dg
C
-modules

Throughout this section,
C
denotes a
k
-linear dg category. We review and develop some basic notions concerning the homological algebra of dg
C
-modules.
Many of the results presented here are drawn from
[
11
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
29
]
and
[
38
]
.
Proofs are included only for results that are not easily found in these references.

For
X\in C
, define
h_{X}
to be the left dg
C
-module

h_{X}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,-)

and
h^{X}
to be the right dg
C
-module

h^{X}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(-,X).

A left (resp. right) dg module is
representable
if it is isomorphic to
h_{X}
(resp.
h^{X})
for some object
X
.
For any object
X
of
C
and any left dg
C
-module
M
, we have an isomorphism of dg
k
-modules

(3.2)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)}(h_{X},M)\cong M(X)

and similarly for any right dg
C
-module
N
we have

(3.3)

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C^{\mathrm{op}})}(h^{X},N)\cong N(X)

A module is
free
(resp.,
finite free
) if it is a direct
sum (resp., finite direct sum) of suspensions of representable modules:
\bigoplus_{i}\Sigma^{j_{i}}h_{X_{i}}
or
\bigoplus_{i}\Sigma^{j_{i}}h^{X_{i}}
.
A dg
C
-module
F
is
semi-free
if it admits a
semi-free filtration
, which is defined to be a filtration

0=F_{-1}\subseteq F_{0}\subseteq F_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F
by submodules such that

F=\bigcup_{i}F_{i}
and
F_{i}/F_{i-1}
is free for all
i
.
All free modules are semi-free and the collection of semi-free modules is closed under suspension and arbitrary direct sums.

Proposition 3.4
.

[
20
, §3.1]
For any dg
C
-module
M
, there exist a quasi-isomorphism
F\xrightarrow{\sim}M
with
F
semi-free.

A dg
C
-module
P
is called
homotopically projective
, or
h-projective
for short, if
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)}(P,T)
is exact for all quasi-trivial
dg
C
-modules
T
.
Equivalently,
P
is h-projective if
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)}(P,-)
sends quasi-isomorphisms of dg
C
-modules to quasi-isomorphisms of chain complexes.
The collection of h-projective modules is closed under suspension, arbitrary co-products, and summands in the abelian category
\mathrm{Mod}(X)
.
It is also closed under homotopy equivalence; for instance, all contractible dg
C
-modules are h-projective.
A dg
C
-module
P
is
graded projective
if the functor
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)}(P,-)
sends surjections in
\mathrm{Mod}(C)
to surjections
of chain complexes. This condition depends only on the graded
C^{\natural}
-module
P^{\natural}
.
A dg
C
-module
P
is
semi-projective
if it is both h-projective and graded projective.
The collection of semi-projective modules is closed under suspension, arbitrary co-products, and summands in the abelian category
\mathrm{Mod}(X)
.
(It is not closed under homotopy equivalence.)

All semi-free modules are semi-projective.
Writing
\operatorname{SF}_{\mathrm{dg}}(C)
,
\operatorname{SP}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)
, and
\operatorname{HP}_{\mathrm{dg}}(C)
for the full dg subcategories of
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)
consisting of the semi-free, the semi-projective, and
the h-projective objects, respectively, we have containments

\operatorname{SF}_{\mathrm{dg}}(C)\subseteq\operatorname{SP}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)\subseteq\operatorname{HP}_{\mathrm{dg}}(C)\subseteq\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C).

Recall that
\mathcal{D}(C)
is the Verdier localization of
[\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)]
given by inverting all quasi-isomorphisms.
A morphism of h-projective modules is a quasi-isomorphism if and only if it is a homotopy equivalence, and thus the canonical functors

[\operatorname{SF}_{\mathrm{dg}}(C)]\hookrightarrow[\operatorname{SP}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)]\hookrightarrow[\operatorname{HP}_{\mathrm{dg}}(C)]\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}(C)
are fully-faithful.
An by Proposition
3.4
each is essentially onto and thus we have equivalences

(3.5)

[\operatorname{SF}_{\mathrm{dg}}(C)]\xrightarrow{\cong}[\operatorname{SP}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)]\xrightarrow{\cong}[\operatorname{HP}_{\mathrm{dg}}(C)]\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{D}(C)

of triangulated categories. We will often use
[\operatorname{SF}_{\mathrm{dg}}(C)]
in place of
\mathcal{D}(C)
.

3.5.
Yoneda embedding and pre-triangulated dg categories

The
dg Yoneda embedding
is the fully-faithful dg functor from
C
to the dg category of right modules over
C
,

\Upsilon_{\mathrm{dg}}:C\to\operatorname{SF}_{\mathrm{dg}}(C^{\mathrm{op}})\subseteq\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C^{\mathrm{op}})

that is given on objects by
\Upsilon_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)=h^{X}
and on morphisms by the canonical isomorphism
\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Y)=h^{Y}(X)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C^{\mathrm{op}})}(h^{X},h^{Y})
.

The dg Yoneda embedding induces a fully-faithful functor on the associated additive categories given by taking
0
cycles

\Upsilon:Z^{0}C\hookrightarrow Z^{0}\operatorname{SF}_{\mathrm{dg}}(C^{\mathrm{op}})

and a fully-faithful functor on homotopy categories

[\Upsilon]:[C]\hookrightarrow[\operatorname{SF}_{\mathrm{dg}}(C^{\mathrm{op}})]\cong\mathcal{D}(C^{\mathrm{op}}).

The category
Z^{0}\operatorname{SF}_{\mathrm{dg}}(C^{\mathrm{op}})
comes equipped with suspensions of objects and mapping cones of morphisms.
We say
C
is
pre-triangulated
if
Z^{0}C
admits suspensions and mapping cones too, that are compatible with the Yoneda embedding; that is,

C
is pre-triangulated if for each integer
j
, there is a functor
\Sigma^{j}:Z^{0}C\to Z^{0}C
such that
\Sigma^{j}\circ\Upsilon
is naturally isomorphic
to
\Upsilon\circ\Sigma^{j}
, and for each morphism
g:X\to Y
in
Z^{0}C
, there is an object
\operatorname{cone}(g)
of
C
and morphisms
Y\to\operatorname{cone}(g)\to\Sigma(X)

such that
\Upsilon(Y)\to\Upsilon(\operatorname{cone}(g))\to\Upsilon(\Sigma(X))
is naturally isomorphic to
\Upsilon(Y)\to\operatorname{cone}(\Upsilon(g))\to\Sigma(\Upsilon(X))
.
The assignment
g\mapsto C(g)
for morphisms in
Z^{0}C
is required to be functorial, in the sense that given a commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

in
Z^{0}C
, the maps
(\alpha,\beta)
induce a map from
\operatorname{cone}(g)
to
\operatorname{cone}(g^{\prime})
, compatibly via the Yoneda embedding with the usual construction in
\mathrm{Mod}(C^{\mathrm{op}})
.
In particular, given such a square we define its
totalization
as
\operatorname{cone}(\operatorname{cone}(g)\xrightarrow{(\alpha,\beta)}\operatorname{cone}(g^{\prime}))
.
As a special case of this, if
X\xrightarrow{g}Y\xrightarrow{h}Z
are morphisms in
Z^{0}C
with
h\circ g=0
,
then taking
X^{\prime}=0
and
Y^{\prime}=Z
, we define

\operatorname{Tot}(X\xrightarrow{g}Y\xrightarrow{h}Z)=\operatorname{cone}(\operatorname{cone}(g)\xrightarrow{(0,h)}\operatorname{cone}(0\to Z))

and we have

\Upsilon\left(\operatorname{Tot}(X\xrightarrow{g}Y\xrightarrow{h}Z)\right)\cong\operatorname{Tot}(\Upsilon(X)\xrightarrow{\Upsilon(g)}\Upsilon(Y)\xrightarrow{\Upsilon(h)}\Upsilon(Z)).

More generally, given a bounded complex

X_{\cdot}=(\cdots\to 0\to X_{m}\to\cdots\to X_{n}\to 0\to\cdots)

in
Z^{0}(C)
, we may form its totalization in
C
in a recursive manner, so that

\Upsilon(\operatorname{Tot}(X_{\cdot}))\cong\operatorname{Tot}(\Upsilon(X_{\cdot})).

For example, for a curved ring
\mathcal{A}
, the dg category
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
is pre-triangulated, with the notions of suspension and mapping cones
given in exactly the same was as for dg
A
-modules, and more generally the totalization of a bounded complex of objects in
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{A})
extends
the usual construction for dg
A
-modules.

3.6.
Tensor products and derived tensor products

Given a right dg
C
-module
M
and a left dg
C
-module
N
, their
tensor product
, written
M\otimes_{C}N
, is the dg
k
-module given by the cokernel of
the map

\bigoplus_{Y,Z\in C}M(Z)\otimes_{k}\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(Y,Z)\otimes_{k}N(Y)\to\bigoplus_{X\in C}M(X)\otimes_{k}N(X)

that sends
m\otimes g\otimes n
to
m\cdot g\otimes n-m\otimes g\cdot n
.
If
C
has just one object whose endomorphism dga is
R
, then
M\otimes_{C}N
coincides with the usual tensor product of dg
R
-modules.
The tensor product is made into a bifunctor, from
\mathrm{Mod}(C^{\mathrm{op}})\times\mathrm{Mod}(C)
to
\mathrm{Mod}(k)
, in the evident manner.
For a fixed
M
and
N
, the functors
M\otimes_{C}-
and
-\otimes_{C}N
are both right-exact functors that commute with taking suspensions.

From the defintion, we observe that the graded
k
-module underlying

does not depend on the differentials; that is,

(3.6)

(M\otimes_{C}N)^{\natural}=M^{\natural}\otimes_{C^{\natural}}N^{\natural}.

Given an object

, there are natural isomorphisms

(3.7)

h^{X}\otimes_{C}N\cong N(X)M\otimes_{X}h_{X}\cong M(X).

These isomorphisms generalize the classical formulas
R\otimes_{R}N\cong N
and
M\otimes_{R}R\cong M
for a dg modules over a dga
R
.

The tensor product of dg
C
-modules preserves arbitrary coproducts:
Given a collection of left dg
C
-modules
\{N_{i}\}_{i\in I}
we have a natural isomorphism

(3.8)

M\otimes_{C}\bigoplus_{i}N_{i}\cong\bigoplus_{i}(M\otimes_{C}N_{i})

for all right dg
C
-modules
M
, and similarly in the other argument.

Definition 3.9
.

The
derived tensor product
of a right dg
C
-module
M
and a left dg
C
-module
N
, written

M\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{C}N
, is the dg
k
-module

F\otimes_{C}N
where
F\xrightarrow{\sim}M
is a chosen quasi-isomorphism such that
F
is semi-free; see Proposition
3.4
.

The usual arguments in the classical setting show that
M\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{C}N
is independent, up to isomorphism in
\mathcal{D}(k)
, of the semi-free resolution of
M
chosen.
Moreover, we have
M\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{C}N\sim M\otimes_{C}F^{\prime}
if
F^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\sim}N
is a quasi-isomorphism with
F^{\prime}
semi-free.

3.7.
h-flat and semi-flat modules

A dg
C
-module
E
is
homotopically flat
, or
h-flat
for short, if the chain complex
M\otimes_{C}E
is exact
whenever
M
is a quasi-trivial right dg
C
-module. This is equivalent to the condition that
-\otimes_{C}E
preserves quasi-isomorphisms.
We say
E
is
graded-flat
if
-\otimes_{C}E
is an exact functor from
\mathrm{Mod}(C)
to
\mathrm{Mod}(k)
. Observe that by (
3.6
), the graded-flat property
does not depend on differentials; that is,
E
is a graded-flat dg
C
-module if and only if
E^{\natural}
is flat as a graded
C^{\natural}
-module.
Finally
E
is
semi-flat
if it is both h-flat and graded-flat.
It can be shown that
E
is semi-flat if and only if whenever
N^{\prime}\xhookrightarrow{\sim}N
is an injective quasi-isomorphism of right dg
C
-modules,

N^{\prime}\otimes_{C}M\xhookrightarrow{\sim}N\otimes_{C}M
is an injective quasi-isomorphism of dg
k
-modules. We extend these notions to right modules in the evident way.

Proposition 3.10
.

The collection of h-flat dg
C
-modules
is closed under suspension, summands, and filtered colimits taken in the abelian category
\mathrm{Mod}(C)
.
The totalization of any bounded below complex of h-flat dg
C
-modules is again h-flat.

The collection of semi-flat modules is also closed under suspension, summands and filtered colimits.
The totalization of a complex of h-flat dg
C
-modules that is bounded (both above and below) is again h-flat.
This collection is also closed under extension in
\mathrm{Mod}(C)
.

All semi-free dg
C
-modules are semi-flat.

Proposition 3.11
.

For a right dg
C
-module
M
and a left dg
C
-module
N
, given a quasi-isomorphism
G\xrightarrow{\sim}N
such that
G
is semi-flat
we have an isomorphism

M\otimes_{C}G\xrightarrow{\cong}M\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{C}N

in
\mathcal{D}(k)
, and similarly for semi-flat resolutions of
M
.

3.8.
Extension of scalars, Morita equivalences

Let
\phi:D\to C

Given a (right or left) dg

along

When

\phi^{*}(M)(X)=M\otimes_{D}\phi_{*}(h^{C}_{X}),\text{ for all $X\in C$,}

By construction,

a natural transformation from
\phi^{*}\circ\phi_{*}
to the identity functor on

\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(C)

If

for

, then we have

and thus there is a natural isomorphism

in which the vertical maps of the dg Yoneda embeddings.

For a right dg

Fixing

If

Definition 3.16
.

A dg functor

is a

if the induced functor

is also an equivalence, and it is the inverse up to natural isomorphism of

.

Every quasi-equivalence is a Morita equivalence, and this generalizes to the following result. Recall that the

of

.

\operatorname{Thick}_{[C]}(\{\phi(X)\mid X\in D\})=[C]
, then

The following will play an important role in establishing isomorphism (

).

.

By Proposition

4.
Derived tensor product for dg categories with supports

In this section we prove the theorem below.
As an immediate consequence, the map (
2.6
) is an isomorphism.
(For the latter, our assumptions that
k
is excellent and
A
is essentially of finite type over
k

ensures that
W
is a Zariski closed subset.)

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A
be a graded commutative ring concentrated in even degrees and assume that
C
is a pre-triangulated,
A
-linear dg category.
Let
Z=V(I)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}(A)
for a finitely generated homogeneous ideal
I\subseteq A
,
let
C^{Z}
denote the full dg subcategory on the collection of objects supported on
Z
and write
\iota:C^{Z}\hookrightarrow C
for the inclusion functor.
For any right dg
C
-module
M
and left dg
C
-module
N
, the canonical map

\iota_{*}M\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{C^{Z}}\iota_{*}N\xrightarrow{\cong}{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{Z}(M\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{C}N)

is an isomorphism in
\mathcal{D}(A)
.

Let us explain the terminology in this theorem: For an object
X
of
C
, we define its
support over
A
, written
\operatorname{supp}_{A}(X)
, to be the support of the graded
A
-algebra
H\operatorname{End}_{C}(X)
:

\operatorname{supp}_{A}(X):=\{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Spec}(A)\mid H\operatorname{End}_{C}(X)_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0\}\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}(A).

So, for a Zariski closed subset
Z
of
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
,
C^{Z}
is the full subcategory of
C
consisting of those objects
X

such that
\operatorname{End}_{C}(X)_{\mathfrak{p}}
is exact whenever
{\mathfrak{p}}\notin Z
.
For any pair of objects
X
and

of
C
, if at least one belongs to
C^{Z}
,
then the dg
A
-module
\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Y)
is also supported on
Z
.
This holds since the action of
A
on
H^{*}\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X,Y)
factors through both
H^{*}\operatorname{End}_{C}(X)
and
H^{*}\operatorname{End}_{C}(Y)
.
Since
C
is
A
-linear, so is
C^{Z}
, and both
M\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{C}N
and
\iota_{*}M\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{C^{Z}}\iota_{*}N

have the structure of dg
A
-modules. The latter is supported on
Z
and hence the canonical map factors through

{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{Z}(M\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{C}N)
, the local cohomology of
M\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{C}N
, which since
I
is finitely generated may be defined using the Čech complex;
the theorem asserts this map is a quasi-isomorphism.

Let us illustrate the theorem with a concrete example:

.

in the derived category, where we define

and
\mathcal{N}=j^{*}N
, with

being the inclusion of the dg category with one object

into

5.
Supports for curved modules and a Theorem of Briggs

The goal of this section is to prove:

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume
k
is regular and excellent and
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
is an essentially smooth curved
k
-algebra, and set
W=\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(k)}\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})

Let us say that a Zariski closed subset

Theorem 5.2

(Briggs’ Theorem)
.

Let

There is a bijection

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Big\{\text{thick subcategories of }[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]\Big\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Big\{\text{specialization closed, homogeneous subsets of }\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})\Big\},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}

We will also need the following technical result:

of homogeneous elements. Set

.

7.
Naturality and Connes operators in characteristic zero

In this section we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
by establishing the
assertions regarding naturality and compatibility with the Connes’ operators
in the case when

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra. A key ingredient in the proof is the notions of type II derived tensor products and type II Hochschild homology, developed by Positselski
[
31
]
and Polishchuk-Positselski
[
29
]
.
In detail, we will reinterpret the isomorphism
HH(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))\cong{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})}\left(h^{A}\otimes^{{\mathbb{L}}}_{\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})}t^{A}\right)
in terms of type II Hochschild homology.

7.1.
Type II tensor products

We start be recalling some definitions and basic properties. References for this material are
[
31
]
and
[
29
]
.

A
cdg
k
-algebra
consists of a triple
(A,d,w)
where
A
is a graded
k
-module,
d
a degree one
k
-linear map, and
w
is a degree two element such that
d^{2}(a)=[w,a]=wa-aw
for all
a\in A
and
d(w)=0
. Note that a curved algebra is the special case of this more general concept with
d=0
.
More generally, a
k
-linear cdg (curved differential graded) category

\mathcal{C}
consists a collection of objects
\operatorname{ob}\mathcal{C}
,
for each pair of objects
X
and
Y
, a pair
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)=(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)^{\natural},d_{X,Y})
consisting of a graded
k
-module and a degree one
k
-linear endomorphism (called the

pre-differential
),
for each single object
X
a degree two
element
w_{X}
of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
(called the
curvature
), and for each triple of objects
X,Y,Z
a composition rule

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)^{\natural}\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,Z)^{\natural}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Z)^{\natural}
. The compositions rules are required to be associative and unital just as for dg categories,
but the maps
d_{X,Y}
need not square to
0
.
Instead, the following relations hold: (1)
d^{2}=[w,-]
; that is,
for all pairs of objects
X,Y
and elements
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
, we have
d_{X,Y}^{2}(f)=w_{Y}\circ f-f\circ w_{X}
and (2)
d(w)=0
; that is, for
each object
X
, we have
d_{X,X}(w_{X})=0
.
The endomorphisms of any object of a cdg category form a cdg algebra, and a cdg category on just one object may be identified with a cdg algebra.

For example, the collection of
precomplexes
over
k
, written
\operatorname{Pre}(k)
, is a cdg category. Its objects are pairs
(M,d_{M})
consisting of a graded
k
-module
M
and a

k
-linear endomorphism
d_{M}
of degree one, with no condition on
d_{M}^{2}
. The homs and composition rules are given by the same formulas as for the dg category
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(k)
,
and we define the curvature of
(M,d_{M})
to be
w_{M}:=d_{M}^{2}\in\operatorname{End}_{k}(M)
.

A (strict) cdg functor between two cdg categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
consists of a function
F:\operatorname{ob}\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{ob}\mathcal{D}
on objects and for each pair of objects
X,Y
of
\mathcal{C}

a morphism of graded
k
-modules
F_{X,Y}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)^{\natural}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),F(Y))^{\natural}
that preserves compositions, identity elements, and curvatures, and
commutes with the pre-differentials.
There is a more general notion of a “non-strict” functor joining cdg categories, but since it only makes a brief appearance in this paper in a special setting,
we will not define it carefully.

A
left cdg module
on a
k
-linear cdg category
\mathcal{C}
is a (strict) cdg functor from
\mathcal{C}
to
\operatorname{Pre}(k)
.
This generalizes the notion of a left curved module on a curved algebra used throughout this paper.
The collection of left cdg modules over
\mathcal{C}
forms a (non-curved) dg category, generalizing the fact that the collection of curved modules over a curved algebra forms a
dg category.

There is also the notion of a
quasi-module
on an arbitrary cdg category, defined by using the notion of a non-strict cdg functor
taking values in
\operatorname{Pre}(k)
. Since we will only need this concept for curved algebras, we only define it at that level of generality:
Given a curved
k
-algebra
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
,
a

over
\mathcal{A}
consists of a left graded
A
-module
M
and an
A
-linear map
d_{M}
on
M
of degree
one, but there is no condition on
d_{M}^{2}
.
Such a quasi-module is
perfect
if
M
is finitely generated and projective as a graded
A
-module.
The collection of perfect quasi-modules form an
A
-linear cdg category, written
\operatorname{qPerf}_{{\mathrm{cdg}}}(\mathcal{A})
. The homs and pre-differentials for a pair of objects are defined just as for

\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
, and
for each

, the required curvature element is defined to be
w_{P}:=d_{P}^{2}-\lambda_{w}\in\operatorname{End}(P)
, where
\lambda_{w}
denotes left multiplication by
w
.
Observe that
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
is the full subcategory of
\operatorname{qPerf}_{{\mathrm{cdg}}}(\mathcal{A})
consisting of the objects with trivial curvature.

Although the
k
-algebra

equipped with the trivial differential is

an object of the dg category
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
,

determine an object of
\operatorname{qPerf}_{{\mathrm{cdg}}}(\mathcal{A})
.
Moreover, its endomorphisms form the curved algebra
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}=(A,-w)

Abusing notation a bit, we identity

with the full cdg subcategory of
\operatorname{qPerf}_{{\mathrm{cdg}}}(\mathcal{A})
on this object.
Similarly, we regard

as a full cdg subcategory of

and

as a full cdg subcategory of
\operatorname{qPerf}_{{\mathrm{cdg}}}(\mathcal{A}^{e})

Each

-linear cdg category
\mathcal{C}
admits an opposite category

and enveloping category

defined just as for dg categories, but with curvature taken into account: For

The fully-faithful dg functor

7.3.
Hochschild complexes

In order to establish the naturality of our isomorphism and to even define the Connes’ operators, we need a concrete model for the Hochschild homology complex.

For a small cdg category
\mathcal{C}
, the
Hochschild complex
, written
\operatorname{Hoch}(\mathcal{C})
, is the explicit dg
k
-module defined as a follows. The underlying graded
k
-module
is the direct
sum
totalization of the collection
\{\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{C})\}_{n\geq 0}
where

\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{C}):=\bigoplus_{X_{0},\dots,X_{n}}\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X_{1},X_{0})\otimes_{k}\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X_{2},X_{1})\otimes_{k}\cdots\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X_{n},X_{n-1})\otimes_{k}\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(X_{0},X_{n})

The differential on
\operatorname{Hoch}(\mathcal{C})
has three components, arising from the classical Hochschild differenital
b
, maps coming from the
(pre)differentials on
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
, and maps induced by the curvature elements.

For instance, suppose
\mathcal{C}
has just one object, and thus may be identified with a cdga
(A,d,h)
.
If we further specialize to the case when
d=0=h
and
A
is concentrated in degree
0
,
then
\operatorname{Hoch}(\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{Hoch}(A)
is the classical Hochschild complex

\cdots\xrightarrow{b}A^{\otimes n+1}\xrightarrow{b}A^{\otimes n}\xrightarrow{b}\cdots\xrightarrow{b}A^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{b}A\to 0

where

b(a_{0}\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=(-1)^{n}a_{n}a_{0}\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n-1}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}a_{0}\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}.

If
A
is graded, the signs are suitably modified, we get a complex of graded
A
-modules, and
\operatorname{Hoch}(A)
is its direct sum totalization.
More generality, if
A
is a dga, the formula for the differential also includes terms of the form

\pm a_{0}\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes d(a_{i})\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}

and if
A
is a cdga, the differential includes terms of the form

\pm a_{0}\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}\otimes h_{A}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}.

Finally, in full generality, the differential on
\operatorname{Hoch}(\mathcal{C})
is given by these same formulas, but now interpreting the
a_{i}
’s to be elements of

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i+1},X_{i})
(with
X_{n+1}:=X_{0}
).
See
[
29
, §2.4]
or
[
1
, §3.1]
for the precise formulas.

\operatorname{Hoch}(-)
is covariantly functorial for (strict) cdg functors, taking values in the category of dg
k
-modules (not merely the derived
category of such); see
[
29
, §2.4]
.

We also have the
type two Hochschild complex
, written

, defined in the same way, except that one takes the
direct product
totalization of the family of
graded
k
-modules
\{\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathcal{C})\}_{n\geq 0}
.

\operatorname{Hoch}^{\mathrm{II}}(-)
is also covariantly functorial for cdg categories, taking values in the category of dg
k
-modules, and there is a natural map
\operatorname{Hoch}(-)\to\operatorname{Hoch}^{\mathrm{II}}(-)
.

7.4.
Naturality and the Connes operator

For any
k
-linear cdg category
\mathcal{C}
, the dg
k
-modules
\operatorname{Hoch}(\mathcal{C})
and
\operatorname{Hoch}^{\mathrm{II}}(\mathcal{C})
each admit a
Connes operators

B
. These are
k
-linear endomorphisms of
homological degree
1
, determined by the formula

B|_{\mathcal{H}_{n}}=(1-\tau^{-1}_{n+2})\circ s_{0}\circ\sum_{l=0}^{n}\tau^{l}_{n+1}

where
s_{0}:\mathcal{H}_{n}\to\mathcal{H}_{n+1}
is the “extra degeneracy map” and
\tau_{m}:\mathcal{H}_{m}\to\mathcal{H}_{m}
is (up to a sign) given by cyclic permutation;
see
[
1
, §3.1]
for more details.
These operators are natural for (strict) functors of
k
-linear cdg categories.

It is important to be aware that the Connes operators need not preserve additional linearity that
\operatorname{Hoch}(\mathcal{C})
may possess. For instance,
\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))
is a dg
A
-module,
but the Connes operator
B
is
not

A
-linear, merely
k
-linear.

When
k
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra, for any smooth curved
k
-algebra
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
, we have established a sequence

(7.9)

\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))\xrightarrow{\mathrm{can}}\operatorname{Hoch}^{\mathrm{II}}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hoch}^{\mathrm{II}}(\operatorname{qPerf}_{{\mathrm{cdg}}}(\mathcal{A}))\xleftarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hoch}^{\mathrm{II}}(\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{\sim}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A},dw)

of maps of dg
A
-modules, with the arrows labeled by
\sim
being quasi-isomorphisms. (Only the last map requires
k
to be a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra.)
Moreover, each map in this sequence is natural for (strict) morphisms of curved algebras, the first three maps are compatible with the Connes operators, and
the final map,
\operatorname{Hoch}^{\mathrm{II}}(\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{\sim}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A},dw)
, relates the Connes operator with the de Rham differential
[
14
, 3.14]
.

Since the first map in (
7.9
) induces a quasi-isomorphism

\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})}\operatorname{Hoch}^{\mathrm{II}}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))
, we obtain a “zig-zag” diagram of natural quasi-isomorphisms of dg
A
-modules
joining
\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
and
{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A},dw)
.
This proves the naturality assertion in Theorem
1.1
,
but in order to deal with the non-
A
-linear Connes operators, we will need to be more careful about the role of local cohomology.

For any tuple
{\underline{g}}=(g_{1},\dots,g_{m})
, setting
W=V(g_{1},\dots,g_{m})
, recall that

{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{W}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k},dw)=\operatorname{Tot}\left((\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k},dw)\to\bigoplus_{i}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k},dw)[1/g_{i}]\to\cdots\right)

Using the natural isomorphisms
\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k}[1/g]\cong\Omega^{\cdot}_{A[1/g]/k}
, we may identify this with the totalization of

(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k},dw)\to\bigoplus_{i}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A[1/g_{i}]/k},dw)\to\cdots.

The maps in this sequence are compatible with the various de Rham differentials, and thus

{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{W}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k},dw)
acquires a de Rham differential.

In a similar way, for each homogeneous element
g
of
A
, we have a canonical map

\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))[1/g]\to\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}[1/g])),

which we claim is a quasi-isomorphism. This follows from the isomorphisms

\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))[1/g]\cong{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A/k},dw)[1/g]\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}[1/g]))\cong R\Gamma_{\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A}[1/g])}(\Omega^{\cdot}_{A[1/g]/k},dw)

in the derived category of dg
A[1/g]
-modules.
We are thus justified in setting

\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{W}\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tot}(0\to\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))\to\bigoplus_{i}\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}[1/g_{i}]))\to\cdots

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}[1/g_{1}\cdots g_{n}]))\to 0).

Taking
W=\operatorname{Nonreg}{\mathcal{A}}
and using these interpretations of local cohomology, we see that upon applying
{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})}
to (
7.9
) we
obtain a sequence of quasi-isomorphisms that are both natural and compatible with the Connes operators/de Rham differential.
Since
\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))
is supported on
\operatorname{Nonreg}{\mathcal{A}}
, the canonical map

{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{\operatorname{Nonreg}(\mathcal{A})}\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hoch}(\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}))

is also a quasi-isomorphism, compatible with the Connes operator.

We have thus proven the following result, which completes the proof of that portion of Theorem
1.1
that assumes
k
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra.

Theorem 7.10
.

For

and
\mathcal{A}
as in Theorem

, assume also that

Recall that for a

[

Remark 8.2

There is a canonical isomorphism

Let us assume

Assume

Theorem 8.3

Assume

is a non-zero-divisor.
The

\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}

Proof.

Since

\operatorname{Nonreg}(Q/f)=\operatorname{Sing}_{F}(Q/f)
and thus
\operatorname{Nonreg}(Q/f)=V(f)\cap\operatorname{Sing}(f)
.
The origin in

is an isolated singular value of

if and only if
\operatorname{Nonreg}(Q/f)
is an open and closed subset of

Let us write
\operatorname{LF}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Q,f)
for
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})

\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded

Set

Recall from Theorem
5.1
that we have a Morita equivalence

of

\operatorname{{\mathcal{P}}erf}(\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Q,f)^{e})\cong\operatorname{{\mathcal{P}}erf}(\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}^{W}(Q^{e},f^{e})).

Moreover, by (

\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Q,f)^{e}
-module

corresponds to the dg
\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}^{W}(Q^{e},f^{e})
-module

.
(Recall we view

Beware that

is not representable as a module on

It thus remains to prove

-module
if and only if

is an open and closed subset of
\operatorname{Sing}(f)
.

Assume that

is an open and closed subset of
\operatorname{Sing}(f)
, so that

decomposes as a disjoint union

; i.e., that the origin is the only singular value of

\operatorname{Nonreg}(Q^{e}/f^{e})=\operatorname{Sing}_{F}(Q^{e}/f^{e})=V(f^{e})\cap\operatorname{Sing}(f)\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(F)}\operatorname{Sing}(f)=\operatorname{Sing}_{F}(Q/f)\times_{\operatorname{Spec}F}\operatorname{Sing}_{F}(Q/f)=W

As in the proof of Lemma

, since

is regular and

Setting

of dg

In particular,

is isomorphic in the derived category to a representable module, and hence is perfect.

Now assume

is a perfect dg
\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}^{W}(Q^{e},f^{e}))

We claim that the homology of
\mathbb{Z}/2

Since the support of

By Theorems

and

Appendix A
Thick subcategories of categories of curved dg modules

A famous result of Hopkins
[
17
]
and Neeman
[
25
]
classifies thick subcategories of the perfect derived category of a noetherian ring in terms of their prime support. In this appendix we prove that the analogue for curved dg modules holds by reducing to the case of ordinary dg modules.

A.1.
Recollections on dg modules

If
A
is a graded ring, we write
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)
for the dg category of all dg
A
-modules. The complex of maps between two objects
X
and
Y
is denoted
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,Y)
. The associated homotopy category
[\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)]
has the same objects and hom sets

\operatorname{Hom}_{[\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)]}(X,Y)\coloneqq H^{0}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,Y)).

The
derived category

\mathcal{D}(A)
of all dg
A
-modules is defined as the Verdier localization of
[\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)]
that inverts all quasi-isomorphisms.

We write
\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(A)
for the full dg subcategory of
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)
consisting of dg modules that are finitely generated and projective as graded
A
-modules. The corresponding homotopy category of finitely generated projective dg modules is written
[\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(A)]
.

A dg
A
-module
X
having the property that
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,-)
preserves all quasi-isomorphisms is called
homotopically projective
. A dg
A
-module is called
perfect
if it is homotopically projective and in
\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(A)
. We write
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)
for the full dg subcategory of
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)
consisting of perfect dg
A
-modules.

When
X
is homotopically projective, the canonical map
H^{0}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,Y))\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}(A)}(X,Y)
is an isomorphism for all dg modules
Y
by
[
39
, Proposition 2.3.3]
. In particular, we may regard
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)]
as a full subcategory of
\mathcal{D}(A)
.

A full subcategory of a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is
thick
if it is triangulated and closed under summands. The smallest thick subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
that contains an object
X
is denoted
\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal{T}}(X)
. We will use the short-hand notation
Y\models_{\mathcal{T}}X
to mean that
X
is in
\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal{T}}(Y)
.

Remark A.1
.

As defined here, the homotopy category
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)]
may not be idempotent complete. The embedding
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)]\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal{D}(A)}(A)
realizes
\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal{D}(A)}(A)
as the idempotent completion of
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)]
, and in particular the two dg categories are Morita equivalent.

An analogous phenomenon was discovered in homotopy theory by Wall, who used obstructions in
K
-theory to show that there are spaces that are, up to homotopy, retracts of finite CW-complexes, but that are not themselves homotopy equivalent to finite CW-complexes
[
40
]
.

Taking inspiration from this, it follows from Thomason’s theorem
[
37
]
that
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)]
is idempotent complete if and only if the map
K_{0}([\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)])\to K_{0}(\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal{D}(A)}(A))\eqqcolon K_{0}(A)
is an isomorphism; this happens exactly when
K_{0}(A)
is generated by the classes of projective graded
A
-modules. For example, if
A=A^{\leqslant 0}
then there is a canonical isomorphism
K_{0}(A)\cong K_{0}(A_{0})
by
[
21
, Lemma 2.4]
, and so for these graded rings
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)]
is idempotent complete.

A.2.
Support for dg modules

When
A
is a graded commutative ring, the set of homogeneous prime ideals of
A
is denoted
\operatorname{Spec}^{*}(A)
. If
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{*}(A)
then the graded localization
A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
is the result of inverting all homogeneous elements of
A\smallsetminus{\mathfrak{p}}
. If
X
is a graded
A
-module then
X_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
is the graded
A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
-module
X\otimes_{A}A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
, and we define the graded support of
X
to be

\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(X)\coloneqq\{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{*}(A)~\mid~X_{({\mathfrak{p}})}\neq 0\text{ in }\mathcal{D}(X)\}.

Extending this slightly, if
X
is a dg
A
-module then
\operatorname{supp}^{*}(X)=\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(H(X))
, or equivalently, the set of homogeneous primes
{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
X_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
is not isomorphic to zero in
\mathcal{D}(A)
.

The next result, due to Carlson and Iyengar, is an analogue of Hopkins and Neeman’s theorem for dg modules.

Theorem A.2

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
A
be a graded commutative noetherian ring. If
X
and
Y
are in
\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal{D}(A)}(A)
and
\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(Y)
, then
X
is in
\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal{D}(A)}(Y)
.

In
[
7
]

A
is required to be concentrated in even degrees or to satisfy
2A
=0; this doesn’t seem to be necessary in their argument except to guarantee that
A
is commutative in the ordinary sense.

For the sake of novelty we give a different proof of Theorem
A.2
, explaining how it can be quickly deduced from an earlier result of Dell’Ambrogio and Stevenson on the graded derived category
[
9
]
.

Proof.

We work with the abelian category
\operatorname{grMod}(A)
of graded
A
-modules, and we denote its derived category by
\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{grMod}(A))
. As an excuse to fix our grading notation: a complex
C
of graded modules has a differential
d_{C}^{i,n}\colon C^{i,n}\to C^{i,n+1}
; it has a suspension
\Sigma^{n}C
with
(\Sigma^{n}C)^{i,m}=C^{i,m+n}
and
d_{\Sigma^{n}C}^{i,m}=(-1)^{n}d_{C}^{i,m+n}
; and it has a twist
C(j)
with
C(j)^{i,n}=C^{i+j,n}
and
d_{C(j)}^{i,n}=d_{C}^{i+j,n}
.

We need to set up two functors:

\textstyle{\mathcal{D}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{G}
\textstyle{\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{grMod}(A)).\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F}

The functor
G
(for grade) is defined by

G(X)^{i,n}=X^{i+n}\quad\text{with}\quad d_{G(X)}^{i,n}=(-1)^{i}d_{X}^{i+n}\colon X^{i+n}\to X^{i+n+1}.

The sign is needed to make
G(X)
a complex of graded
A
-modules.

The functor
F
(for fold) is defined by

F(C)^{m}=\bigoplus_{i+n=m}C^{i,n}\quad\text{with}\quad d^{n}_{F(C)}={\textstyle\sum_{i+n=m}}[(-1)^{i}d^{i,n}_{C}\colon C^{i,n}\to C^{i,n+1}].

Again, the sign is needed to make
F(C)
a dg module. We note that
F(C(i))=\Sigma^{i}F(C)
.

Both
F
and
G
preserve quasi-isomorphisms and so induce well-defined functors on the corresponding derived categories.

The functor
G
preserves support, in the sense that
\operatorname{supp}_{A}(G(X))\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{A}(G(Y))
. By Dell’Ambrogio and Stevenson’s theorem
[
9
, Theorem 5.8]

G(X)
is in the localising subcategory generated by
G(Y)(i)
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. (We note that the results of
[
9
]
are stated for
small support
, but since
X
and
Y
have finitely generated homology, the support sets of
G(X)
and
G(Y)
are the same as their small support sets.)

The functor
F
preserves coproducts, and it follows that
FG(X)=X^{\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}}
is in the localising subcategory generated by
F(G(Y)(i))=\Sigma^{i}FG(Y)=\Sigma^{i}(Y^{\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}})
, for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. This implies that
X
is in the localising subcategory generated by
Y
. Since
X
and
Y
are compact objects of
\mathcal{D}(A)
, Neeman’s lemma
[
26
, Lemma 2.2]
implies that
X
is in
\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal{D}(A)}(Y)
.
∎

A.3.
Curved dg modules

A curved ring
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
is a graded ring
A
equipped with a central element
w\in A^{2}
. A curved dg
\mathcal{A}
-module is a graded
A
-module
X
equipped with a degree one differential
d\colon X\to X
that is
A
-linear in the sense that
d(ax)=(-1)^{|a|}ad(x)
when
a\in A
and
x\in X
, and such that
d^{2}
is multiplication by
w
.

If
X
and
Y
are curved dg
\mathcal{A}
-modules then the set of
A
-linear maps
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,Y)
is a complex, given its usual differential. This makes the collection of all curved dg
\mathcal{A}
-modules into a pretriangulated dg category, denoted
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
, with homotopy category
[\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]
.

As in Section
A.1
, we write
\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})
for the full dg subcategory of
\mathrm{Mod}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
consisting of curve dg modules that are finitely generated and projective as graded
A
-modules. The corresponding homotopy category is written
[\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})]
.

Remark A.3
.

The homotopy category of (not necessarily finitely generated) projective dg modules has been studied in a number of contexts
[
27
,
18
]
, including under the names contraderived category, derived category of the second kind, and unseparated derived category, and including in the curved setting; see the references in
[
32
]
. In general it seems best to view
[\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})]
within this context, however we will see that in the regular context it is reasonable to view
[\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})]
as an analogue of the perfect derived category.

Suppose that
A
is graded commutative. Then
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,Y)
is further a dg
A
-module whenever
X
and
Y
are curved dg
\mathcal{A}
-modules. In this case the support of a curved dg
\mathcal{A}
-module
X
is defined to be the support of its endomorphism dg module:

\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(X)\coloneqq\operatorname{supp}_{A}^{*}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,X)).

In a similar vein,
X
is a curved dg
A
-module (with zero curvature) and
Y
is a dg
\mathcal{A}
-module, then the tensor product
X\otimes_{A}Y
is a curved dg
\mathcal{A}
-module, given its usual differential.

Theorem A.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
be a curved ring such that
A
is noetherian and graded commutative.
Assume
X,Y\in\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})
are such that each of the dg
A
-modules
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,X)
,
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Y,Y)
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Y,X)
are perfect.
If
\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(Y)
then
X
is in
\operatorname{Thick}_{[\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})]}(Y)
.

Proof.

Taking
X
and
Y
as stated, we have

\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,X))\subseteq\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Y,Y))

by definition. Since
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,X)
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Y,Y)
are perfect dg
A
-modules, they in particular lie in
\operatorname{Thick}_{\mathcal{D}(A)}(A)
, so from Theorem
A.2
we obtain

(A.5)

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Y,Y)\models_{\mathcal{D}(A)}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,X).

We wish to deduce that
Y\models_{[\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})]}X
.

By assumption we have
A\models_{\mathcal{D}(A)}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,Y)
. Since both
A
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,Y)
lie in
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)]
, an application of
[
39
, Proposition 2.3.1]
yields
A\models_{[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)]}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,Y)
(despite the fact that
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)]
as defined in
A.1
may not be idempotent complete in
\mathcal{D}(A)
).

Applying the exact functor
-\otimes_{A}Y\colon[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)]\to[\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})]
yields

Y\models_{[\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})]}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Y,X)\otimes_{A}Y\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Y,Y)\otimes_{A}X.

We similarly use
[
39
, Proposition 2.3.1]
on (
A.5
), and then apply
-\otimes_{A}X\colon[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A)]\to[\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})]
to obtain

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(Y,Y)\otimes_{A}X\models_{[\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})]}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,X)\otimes_{A}X.

Finally,
X
is a summand of
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,X)\otimes_{A}X
in
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]
; indeed, the composition of the map
X\to\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,X)\otimes_{A}X
,
x\mapsto\operatorname{id}\otimes x
with the map
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,X)\otimes_{A}X\to X
,
\theta\otimes x\mapsto\theta(x)
is the identity. Combining this with the already obtained inclusions of thick subcategories yields the desired result
Y\models_{[\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})]}X
.
∎

A.4.
Curved dg modules over regular rings

A graded commutative ring
A
is
regular
if it is noetherian and for every homogeneous prime
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{*}(A)
, the graded local ring
A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
has finite global dimension (i.e. all of its graded modules have finite projective dimension). The non-regular locus of a graded ring
A
is the set
\operatorname{Nonreg}^{*}(A)
of homogeneous primes
{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
is not a regular ring. We note that if
A
is local and regular then
A
must either be evenly graded or satisfy
2A=0
. The next lemma is well known; it explains why curved dg modules over regular rings are well behaved.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
A
be a regular graded commutative ring. If
X
is a dg
A
-module that is finitely generated and projective as a graded
A
-module, then
X
is perfect.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
be a curved ring such that
A
is graded commutative and regular. If
X
and
Y
are curved dg
\mathcal{A}
-modules that are finitely generated and projective as a graded
A
-modules, then
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,Y)
is a perfect dg
A
-module.

Proof.

Taking a dg module
X
that is finitely generated and projective as a graded module, our goal is to show that
X
is homotopically projective. Since
X
is finitely generated and projective,
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,Y)_{({\mathfrak{p}})}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}}(X_{({\mathfrak{p}})},Y_{({\mathfrak{p}})})
for any dg module
Y
and any homogeneous prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
. Therefore we may localise at
{\mathfrak{p}}
and assume that
A
has finite global dimension. In this situation, projective dg modules are homotopically projective by
[
10
, 3.1]
(this result is stated for ungraded rings, but the argument for dg modules is similar).

The second second statement follows from the first, since
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,Y)
is finitely generated and projective as a graded
A
-module.
∎

Definition A.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
be a curved ring such that
A
is graded commutative and regular. Based on Lemma
A.6
, curved dg
\mathcal{A}
-modules that are finitely generated and projective over
A
will be called
perfect
, and we will use the notation
\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})=\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
. We again emphasise that it only seems reasonable to use this terminology when
A
is regular. Compare with Section
1
above.

For a homogeneous prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
A
, we write
\mathcal{A}_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
for the curved ring
(A_{({\mathfrak{p}})},\frac{w}{1})
and, for a curved dg
\mathcal{A}
-module
X
, we let
X_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
denote the curved dg
\mathcal{A}_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
-module
X\otimes_{A}A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
.

Lemma A.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
be a curved ring such that
A
is graded commutative and regular, and let
X
be a perfect curved dg
\mathcal{A}
-module. Then

\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(X)\cap\mathcal{V}(w)=\Big\{{\mathfrak{p}}\mid w\in{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ and $X_{({\mathfrak{p}})}$ is not isomorphic to zero in $[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}_{({\mathfrak{p}})})]$}\Big\},

where
\mathcal{V}(w)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}\mid w\in{\mathfrak{p}}\}
. If
A
is evenly graded then
\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(X)\cap\mathcal{V}(w)=\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(X)
.

Proof.

Since
X
is projective and finitely generated,
\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}}(X_{({\mathfrak{p}})},X_{({\mathfrak{p}})})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,X)_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
for any
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{*}(A)
. Therefore
X_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
is isomorphic to zero in
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}_{({\mathfrak{p}})})]
if and only if
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,X)_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
is contractible. By Lemma
A.6

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,X)_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
is homotopically projective, so it is acyclic if and only if it is contractible. This establishes the first claim.

If
w\notin{\mathfrak{p}}
then
w\colon X_{({\mathfrak{p}})}\to X_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
is invertible, and it follows that the differential
d\colon X_{({\mathfrak{p}})}\to X_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
is invertible as well, with
A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
-linear inverse
(d)^{-1}
. Define
h\colon X_{({\mathfrak{p}})}\to X_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
by the rule
h(x)=(d)^{-1}(x)
is
|x|
is even and
h(x)=0
is
|x|
is odd, and note that
hd+dh=\operatorname{id}_{X_{({\mathfrak{p}})}}
. If
A
is evenly graded then so is
A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
, and this implies that
h
is
A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
-linear. It follows that
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,X)_{({\mathfrak{p}})}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}}(X_{({\mathfrak{p}})},X_{({\mathfrak{p}})})
is contractible. This argument shows that
\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{V}(w)
.
∎

Remark A.9
.

The hypothesis that
A
is evenly graded is necessary to ensure that
\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{V}(w)
. For example, let
A=k[x^{\pm 1}]
, where
k
is a field of characteristic
2
and
x
is a variable of degree
1
, and set
w=x^{2}
. The perfect curved dg module
X=A
, with differential
d_{X}=x
, is supported at the unique homogeneous prime
{\mathfrak{p}}=(0)
of
A
, despite the fact that
w\notin{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Lemma A.10
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
be a curved local ring. If
w
is a non-zero-divisor (and not a unit)
and the quotient
A/(w)
is regular, then
[\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})]\simeq 0
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a curved dg
\mathcal{A}
-module that is finitely generated and projective, and set
Y=\operatorname{End}_{A}(X)
.

Since
w
is a non-zero-divisor, it follows that the complex
X\otimes_{A}(A/(w))
is exact. Since
A/(w)
is regular, by lemma
A.6
the dg module
X\otimes_{A}A/(w)
is
h
-projective, and therefore contractible. In other words,
\operatorname{End}_{A/(w)}(X\otimes_{A}A/(w))=Y\otimes_{A}A/(w)
is exact.

From the exact sequence in homology associated to the triangle
\Sigma^{-2}Y\xrightarrow{w}Y\to Y\otimes_{A}A/(w)
, we find that
\Sigma^{-2}{\rm H}(Y)\xrightarrow{w}{\rm H}(Y)
is an isomorphism. Then by Nakayama’s lemma
{\rm H}(Y)=0
. This means that
X
is the zero object in
[\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathcal{A})]
, as required.
∎

We will say that
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
is
regular
at
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{*}(A)
if
\frac{w}{1}
is a non-zero-divisor in
A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
and the quotient
A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}/(\frac{w}{1})
is a regular local ring. The set of homogeneous primes at which
\mathcal{A}
is not regular is written
\operatorname{Nonreg}^{*}(\mathcal{A})
. If
w
is assumed to be a non-zero-divisor then we may make the identification
\operatorname{Nonreg}^{*}(\mathcal{A})=\operatorname{Nonreg}^{*}(A/(w))
.

We also remind the reader that a subset
Z\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{*}(A)
is called
specialization closed
if
{\mathfrak{p}}\in Z
and
{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{q}}
implies
{\mathfrak{q}}\in Z
, for any
{\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{q}}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{*}(A)
.

Theorem A.11
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
be a curved ring such that
A
is graded commutative and regular. If
X
and
Y
are in
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
and
\operatorname{supp}_{A}^{*}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{A}^{*}(Y)
, then
X
is in
\operatorname{Thick}_{[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]}(Y)
. When
A
is evenly graded this yeilds a bijection

\textstyle{\Big\{\text{thick subcategories of }[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]\Big\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma}
\textstyle{\Big\{\text{specialization closed subsets of }\operatorname{Nonreg}^{*}(\mathcal{A})\Big\},\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\theta}

where
\theta(Z)=\{X\in[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]\mid\operatorname{supp}_{A}^{*}(X)\subseteq Z\}
and
\sigma(T)=\bigcup_{X\in T}\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(X)
.

Proof.

The first statement is obtained by combining Theorem
A.4
with Lemma
A.6
.

The assignment
X\mapsto X_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
determines an exact functor
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]\to[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}_{({\mathfrak{p}})})]
, and therefore its kernel
\theta({\mathfrak{p}})
is a thick subcategory of
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]
. It follows from Lemma
A.8
that
\theta(Z)=\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in Z}\theta({\mathfrak{p}})
is also a thick subcategory of
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]
.

Suppose that
A
is evenly graded. The support of any perfect curved dg module is then a Zariski closed subset of
\operatorname{Nonreg}^{*}(\mathcal{A})
by Lemmas
A.8
and
A.10
. Since unions of Zariski closed subsets are specialization closed, it follows that
\sigma(T)
is a specialization closed subset of
\operatorname{Nonreg}^{*}(\mathcal{A})
for any thick subcategory
T
of
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]
.

The already established first statement implies that
\sigma\theta=\operatorname{id}
. Therefore, it remains to show that
\sigma
is surjective to obtain the claimed bijection. For this, we give a construction borrowing inspiration from
[
2
, 2.3]
.

Since
A
is regular it is a finite product
A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{m}
of regular graded domains, and the curvature element may be written
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})
. As each
A_{i}
is a projective graded
A
-module there are restriction functors
\rho_{i}\colon[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(A_{i},w_{i})]\to[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]
, and through the inclusions
\operatorname{Spec}^{*}(A_{i})\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{*}(A)
these satisfy
\operatorname{supp}_{A_{i}}^{*}(X)=\operatorname{supp}_{A}^{*}(\rho_{i}(X))
. Hence we may assume that
A
is a domain.

Every specialization closed subset is a union of irreducible Zariski closed subsets, so it suffices to show that for every
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Nonreg}^{*}(\mathcal{A})
there is a perfect curved dg module
X
with support
\mathcal{V}({\mathfrak{p}})=\{{\mathfrak{q}}\mid{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{q}}\}
.

Let
K
be the Koszul complex over
A
on a generating set
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
for
{\mathfrak{p}}
, thought of a dg
A
-algebra generated by exterior variables
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
with its Koszul differential
d(e_{i})=x_{i}
.

Since
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{Nonreg}^{*}(\mathcal{A})
either
\frac{w}{1}=0
in
A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
or
A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}/(\frac{w}{1})
is not regular, and in either case
\frac{w}{1}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}A_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
. Since
A
is a domain, it follows that
w\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}
, and we may write
w=x_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}y_{n}
with
y_{i}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Multiplication by the element
u=e_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+e_{n}y_{n}
defines a map
u\colon K^{*}\to K^{*+1}
, and a computation shows that
(d+u)^{2}=w
. Hence
X=(K,d+u)
is a perfect curved dg
\mathcal{A}
-module.

It remains only to verify that
\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(X)=\mathcal{V}({\mathfrak{p}})
. If
{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{q}}
then
X\otimes_{A}A_{({\mathfrak{q}})}/({\mathfrak{q}})
is an exterior algebra over
A_{({\mathfrak{q}})}/({\mathfrak{q}})
with trivial differential, and in particular
X_{({\mathfrak{q}})}
cannot be isomorphic to zero, so
{\mathfrak{q}}\in\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(X)
by Lemma
A.8
. Conversely if
{\mathfrak{p}}\not\subseteq{\mathfrak{q}}
then there is an
i
such that
x_{i}\notin{\mathfrak{q}}
. The map
h=\frac{e_{i}}{x_{i}}\colon X^{*}_{({\mathfrak{q}})}\to X^{*-1}_{({\mathfrak{q}})}
then satisfies
(d+u)h+h(d+u)=dh+hd=\operatorname{id}_{X}
, showing that
X_{({\mathfrak{q}})}
is isomorphic to zero in
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A}_{({\mathfrak{q}})})]
, and so
{\mathfrak{q}}\notin\operatorname{supp}^{*}_{A}(X)
by Lemma
A.8
.
∎

An object
X\in[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]
is said to be a classical generator if
\operatorname{Thick}_{[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]}(X)=[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]
. The next result follows directly from Theorem
A.11
. It is a curved analogue of the main result in
[
19
]
. We note that if
A
is excellent
then
\operatorname{Nonreg}^{*}(\mathcal{A})
is Zariski closed.

Corollary A.12
.

If
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
is a curved ring such that
A
is evenly graded, commutative and regular, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{Nonreg}^{*}(\mathcal{A})
is a Zariski closed subset of
\operatorname{Spec}^{*}(A)
;

(2)

there is an object
X
of
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]
whose support is
\operatorname{Nonreg}^{*}(\mathcal{A})
;

(3)

[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]
admits a classical generator;

(4)

[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]
is equivalent, up to summands, to
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(R)]
, for some dg algebra
R
.

Any object
X
as in (
2
) generates
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]
, and then we may take
R=\operatorname{End}_{A}(X)
in (
4
).

We end the appendix by highlighting several special cases.

Example A.13

(Hypersurface rings)
.

Let
Q
be a commutative noetherian ring and let
f\in Q
. We cosider the curved ring
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)=(Q[t^{\pm 1}],ft)
where
t
is a degree two indeterminant. The dg category
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})
may be identified with
\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Q,f)
, the dg category of matrix factorizations of
f
.

When
Q
is regular and
f
is a non-zero-divisor, the corresponding homotopy category
[\operatorname{mf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(Q,f)]
is equivalent to the singularity category to
\mathcal{D}^{\operatorname{sing}}(R)=\frac{\mathcal{D}^{\rm b}(R)}{\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(R)}
of the hypersurface ring
R=Q/f
; this was proven by Buchweitz
[
3
, Theorem 4.4.1]
, and later rediscovered by Orlov
[
28
, Theorem 3.9]
.

We may also identify
\operatorname{Nonreg}^{*}(\mathcal{A})
with
\operatorname{Nonreg}(R)
, and thus the Theorem
A.11
yields a bijection

\Big\{\text{thick subcategories of }\mathcal{D}^{\operatorname{sing}}(R)\Big\}\xleftrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\Big\{\text{specialization closed subsets of }\operatorname{Nonreg}(R)\Big\}

This has been proven before by Takahasi
[
36
]
, and later, using different methods, by Stevenson
[
35
, 7.9]
. Hirano has also established a more general classification result for matrix factorizations over non-regular rings
[
16
]
. This result suggests that the “homotopically projective” assumption in Theorem
A.4
may be unnecessary (but in this appendix we limit ourselves to results that can be easily deduced from the existing literature).

When
R
is local with an isolated singularity, that is, when
\operatorname{Nonreg}(R)
consists of only the maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}
, it follows that
\mathcal{D}^{\operatorname{sing}}(R)
is the thick closure of the class of the residue field
R/{\mathfrak{m}}
; this was first proven by Dyckerhoff
[

Example A.14

(Complete intersection rings)
.

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{c}
be a regular sequence in
Q
, and set
R=Q/(f_{1},\dots,f_{c})
. This time we consider the curved ring
\mathcal{A}=(A,w)
, where
A=Q[t_{1},\ldots,t_{c}]
for some degree two indeterminants
t_{1},\ldots,t_{c}
, and where
w=f_{1}t_{1}+\cdots+f_{c}t_{c}
.

Since
A
is evenly graded, every perfect curved dg module
X
over
\mathcal{A}
splits as a direct sum of two projective graded
A
-modules
X=X_{\rm even}\oplus X_{\rm odd}
, and this allows us to identify
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]
with the homotopy category graded
[\mathrm{gr\text{-}mf}(A,w)]
of graded matrix factorizations of
w
, in the sense of
[
4
]
. Therefore, in our language,
[
4
, 7.5]
says that there is an equivalence of triangulated categories
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]\cong D^{b}(R)
. Theorem
A.11
now yields a bijection

\Big\{\text{thick subcategories of }\mathcal{D}^{\rm b}(R)\Big\}\xleftrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\Big\{\text{specialization closed subsets of }\operatorname{Nonreg}^{*}\!\Big(\frac{Q[t_{1},\dots,t_{c}]}{f_{1}t_{1}+\cdots+f_{c}t_{c}}\Big)\Big\}.

Observe that
\operatorname{Spec}^{*}(A)
is the union of the closed subset
\mathcal{V}^{*}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{c})
, which may be identfied with
\operatorname{Spec}(Q)
,
and its open complement, which may be identified with
\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{Q}
. It follows that the nonregular locus of
A/(w)
contains
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
as a closed subset and its open complement is the nonregular locus of the projective hypersurface
Y:=\operatorname{Proj}\left(A/(w)\right)
in
\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{Q}
.

Burke and Walker prove that
\mathcal{D}^{\rm sing}(R)
is equivalent to
[\operatorname{mf}(\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{Q},\mathcal{O}(1),w)]
, the homotopy category of matrix factorizations of
w
, thought of as a section of
\mathcal{O}(1)

[
6
]
. It follows from
[
5
, Theorem 1]
that
[\operatorname{mf}(\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{Q},\mathcal{O}(1),w)]
is the Verdier quotient of
[\mathrm{gr\text{-}mf}(A,w)]
at the thick subcategory of those objects supported
\mathcal{V}^{*}(t_{1},\dots,t_{c})\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}^{*}(A)
. The commutative diagram at the end of
[
4
]
then identifies this thick subcategory with
\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(R)
, under the equivalence
[\operatorname{Perf}_{{\mathrm{dg}}}(\mathcal{A})]\cong D^{b}(R)
. Therefore, after taking the Verdier quotient we obtain a bijection

\Big\{\text{thick subcategories of }\mathcal{D}^{\operatorname{sing}}(R)\Big\}\xleftrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\Big\{\text{specialization closed subsets of }\operatorname{Nonreg}(Y)\Big\}.

This classification was obtained by Stevenson in
[
35
, 10.5]
.

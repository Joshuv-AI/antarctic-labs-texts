Small weights in Caccioppoli’s inequality and applications to Liouville-type theorems for non-standard problems
Michael Bildhauer & Martin Fuchs

Abstract
Using a variant of Caccioppoli’s inequality involving small weights, i.e. weights of the form ( 1 + | ∇ u | 2 ) − α / 2 (1+|\nabla u|^{2})^{-\alpha/2} for some α > 0 \alpha>0 , we establish several Liouville-type theorems under general non-standard growth conditions. 1 1 1 AMS subject classification: 49J40, 35J50



Dedicated to the 70th birthday of Gregory Seregin

Introduction

Throughout this manuscript we always suppose that
u
:
\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
,
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, is a solution of the nonlinear equation

\operatorname{div}\big[\nabla f(\nabla u)\big]=0\quad\mbox{on $\mathbb{R}^{2}$}\,.

(1.1)

Our main goal is the discussion of Liouville-type results under rather general hypotheses on the convex density

f
:
\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
including non-standard growth conditions such as the case of linear growth or even
allowing a certain degree of anisotropy in the superlinear case. For technical simplicity we restrict ourselves to the twodimensional case.

It is out of reach to give a complete overview on all the recent contributions on Liouville-type results.
We refer to the beautiful survey of Farina
[

in the case of general elliptic problems
including a lot of historical references. We also refer to Seregin’s discussion
[
2
]
of Liouville-type theorems
in the case of the Navier-Stokes equations.

Contributions in the case of linear or anisotropic growth are quite rarely found. We just mention the papers

[

]

]
,
[

]
,

Assumption 1.1
.

The convex energy density
f
:
\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is of class
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and satisfies the non-uniform

c_{1}\big(1+|Z|^{2}\big)^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}|Y|^{2}\leq D^{2}f(Z)\big(Y,Y\big)\leq c_{2}\big(1+|Z|^{2}\big)^{-\frac{\overline{\mu}}{2}}|Y|^{2}

for all

with exponents
\mu>1
,
\overline{\mu}\leq 1
and constants

,

.

Condition (

) also serves as one main assumption in the recent paper
[
6
]
on Liouville-type results
in two dimensions for functionals satisfying a linear growth condition. These results are restricted to the case
\overline{\mu}=1
, for instance we have:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem 1.1, c),
[
6
]
, the case
N=1
)
Let
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
denote a solution of (
1.1
) with density

such that for some real number
M>0
we have

|\nabla f(Z)|\leq M\qquad\mbox{for all}\quad Z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

and such (
1.2
) holds with the choice
\overline{\mu}=1
.

If we have

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{|u(x)|}{|x|}<\infty\,,

(1.3)

then

is affine.

Let us give some further explanations concerning the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
: from (
1.2
) and the boundedness
of

is of linear growth, and this actually holds for arbitrary exponents

. We refer

[

.

At the same time, if
f
is of linear growth satisfying inequality (

[

, whereas the bound

“limit case” for which
\overline{\mu}=1
. So the first question arises, whether the result of Theorem
1.1

keeps valid, if we allow exponents
\overline{\mu}<1
.

Closely related is the following setting in the case of superlinear growth. Suppose that we have (
1.2
) with exponent

\overline{\mu}=2-q
,
q>1
, on the right-hand side. Then we are interested in the anisotropic case, which means that we
do not narrow our discussion by assuming

. We just
impose the inequality

a|Z|^{s}-b\leq f(Z)\qquad\mbox{for all}\quad Z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,,

with exponent
1<s\leq q
and with constants
a
,
A>0
,
a
,

as lower bound for the density

(see Section

5

We emphasize that we are not aware of similar Liouville-type theorems w.r.t. this general kind of anisotropic
hypotheses.

Let us fix the notation.

Notation.
We always abbreviate

\Gamma:=1+|\nabla u|^{2}\,,\quad\Gamma_{Q}:=1+|\nabla u-Q|^{2}\qquad\mbox{for vectors}\quad Q\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,.

For fixed radii
r
,
R>0
we consider open disks
B_{r}
and define

T_{R}:=B_{2R}-\overline{B_{R}}\,,\quad\hat{T}_{R}:=B_{5R/2}-\overline{B_{R/2}}\,,

where the center
x_{0}
is not indicated.

Moreover, for
r>0
we let

(\nabla u)=(\nabla u)_{r}=-\hskip-10.76385pt\int_{B_{r}}\nabla u\,{\rm d}x\,.

Then we have

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that Assumption
1.1
holds. Moreover, suppose
that there are real numbers
\gamma
,
\gamma_{Q}\geq 0
such that

\gamma+\gamma_{Q}<\frac{1}{2}

(1.4)

and that there exist
Q=Q(R)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{R^{2}}\Xi(R):=\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{T_{R}}\Gamma^{-\frac{\gamma+\overline{\mu}}{2}}\Gamma_{Q}^{-\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{2}}|\nabla u-Q|^{2}\,{\rm d}x<\infty\,.

(1.5)

Then
u
is an affine function.

We note that Theorem
1.2
just relies on condition (
1.2
) and it does not matter, whether
the energy density
f
is of linear growth or even shows a completely anisotropic behaviour.
Moreover, the conclusion of the theorem is independent of the value of the exponent
\mu
.

Let us shortly comment on the main idea for the proof of Theorem
1.2
recalling the approach towards
Theorem
1.1
. This theorem is proved by first using a Caccioppoli-type inequality
for the differentiated Euler equation. Since we have
\overline{\mu}=1
as a hypothesis of Theorem
1.1
, the right-hand side of this inequality
can be measured in terms of the quantity

\nabla f(\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u
which in turn occurs on the left-hand side of the weak form of (
1.1
) applied to a suitable
testfunction.

In the case
\overline{\mu}<1
a serious gap arises which cannot be closed by obvious arguments. Here, as the main new feature, we introduce small weights
in Caccioppoli’s inequality such that both sides again fit together. The same arguments bridge the gap in the superlinear
anisotropic case.

Theorem
1.2
immediately gives the following elementary corollary.

Corollary 1.1
.

Suppose that we have Assumption
1.1
. Then
u
is an
affine function if one of the following conditions hold.

i)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u(x)|<\infty\,.

ii)

There exists some
\varepsilon>0
such that

-\hskip-10.76385pt\int_{T_{R}}\Gamma^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}-\overline{\mu}\right)+\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}x<c

with a constant
c>0
not depending on
R
.

Before formulating more refined corollaries, we establish our Caccioppoli-type estimate in the next section as the main tool for
proving Theorem
1.2
in Section
3
.

Section
4
is devoted to applications in the linear growth setting
while Section
5
concentrates on the main corollary in the superlinear case.

We finally note that the generality of Theorem
1.2
may be used to discuss a series of other applications
which is left to the particular interest of the reader.

2
A Caccioppoli-type inequality

We start with a Caccioppoli-type inequality weighted with negative powers of
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{Q}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Given Assumption
1.1
we fix
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, consider real numbers
s_{Q}>-1/4
,
s_{1}>-1/4
and let

c_{Q}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}4|{s_{Q}}|&\mbox{if}&{s_{Q}}\leq 0\,,\\[4.30554pt]
0&\mbox{if}&{s_{Q}}>0\,,\end{array}\right.\qquad c_{1}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}4|s_{1}|&\mbox{if}&s_{1}\leq 0\,,\\[4.30554pt]
0&\mbox{if}&s_{1}>0\,,\end{array}\right.\qquad

and suppose that
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}\big(\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
.

If
c_{Q}+c_{1}<1
, then we have (summation w.r.t.
\alpha=1
,
2
)

\displaystyle\big[1-c_{Q}-c_{1}\big]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u,\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u)\Gamma_{Q}^{{s_{Q}}}\Gamma^{s_{1}}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(2.1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\Bigg[\int_{\operatorname{spt}\nabla\eta}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u,\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u)\Gamma_{Q}^{{s_{Q}}}\Gamma^{s_{1}}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\Bigg[\int_{\operatorname{spt}\nabla\eta}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\eta,\nabla\eta\big)\big|\nabla u-Q\big|^{2}\Gamma_{Q}^{{s_{Q}}}\Gamma^{s_{1}}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,

where the constant
c
is not sdepending on
\eta
.
In particular we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u,\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u)\Gamma_{Q}^{{s_{Q}}}\Gamma^{s_{1}}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(2.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{\operatorname{spt}\nabla\eta}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\eta,\nabla\eta\big)\big|\partial_{\alpha}u-Q_{\alpha}\big|^{2}\Gamma_{Q}^{{s_{Q}}}\Gamma^{s_{1}}\,{\rm d}x\,.

Proof.
We first consider the case that
-1/4<s_{Q}\leq 0
. For
\alpha=1
,
2
and
for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
equation (
1.1
) yields

0=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u,\nabla\psi\big)\,{\rm d}x\,.

(2.3)

Inserting
\psi:=\eta^{2}\big(\partial_{\alpha}u-Q_{\alpha}\big)\Gamma_{Q}^{s_{Q}}\Gamma^{s_{1}}
in (
2.3
)
we obtain for
\alpha=1
,
2
and any testfunction
\eta

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u,\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u\big)\Gamma_{Q}^{{s_{Q}}}\Gamma^{s_{1}}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(2.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u,\big(\partial_{\alpha}u-Q_{\alpha}\big)\nabla\Gamma_{Q}^{{s_{Q}}}\big)\Gamma^{s_{1}}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u,\big(\partial_{\alpha}u-Q_{\alpha}\big)\nabla\Gamma^{s_{1}}\big)\Gamma_{Q}^{{s_{Q}}}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u,\nabla\eta\big)\big(\partial_{\alpha}u-Q_{\alpha}\big)\Gamma_{Q}^{{s_{Q}}}\Gamma^{s_{1}}\eta\,{\rm d}x\,.

We denote the bilinear form

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and discuss the left-hand side of (
2.4
)
by observing that

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2}\Big\langle\partial_{\alpha}\nabla u,\partial_{\alpha}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Gamma_{Q}^{s_{Q}}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2}\Big\langle\partial_{\alpha}\nabla u,\partial_{\alpha}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Big[\big(\partial_{1}u-Q_{1}\big)^{2}+\big(\partial_{2}u-Q_{2}\big)^{2}\Big]\Gamma_{Q}^{{s_{Q}}-1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2}\Big\langle\big(\partial_{\gamma}u-Q_{\alpha}\big)\partial_{\alpha}\nabla u,\big(\partial_{\alpha}u-Q_{\alpha}\big)\partial_{\alpha}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Gamma_{Q}^{{s_{Q}}-1}\,.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{s_{Q}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2}\Big\langle\big(\partial_{\alpha}u-Q_{\alpha}\big)\partial_{\alpha}\nabla u,\nabla\sum_{i=1}^{2}\big(\partial_{i}u-Q_{i}\big)^{2}\Big\rangle\Gamma_{Q}^{{s_{Q}}-1}

\displaystyle\Bigg|\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2}\Big\langle\partial_{\alpha}\nabla u,\big(\partial_{\alpha}u-Q_{\alpha}\big)\nabla\Gamma_{Q}^{{s_{Q}}}\Big\rangle\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 4|{s_{Q}}|\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2}\Big\langle\big(\partial_{\alpha}u-Q_{\alpha}\big)\partial_{\alpha}\nabla u,\big(\partial_{\alpha}u-Q_{\alpha}\big)\partial_{\alpha}\nabla u\Big\rangle\Gamma_{Q}^{{s_{Q}}-1}\,.

\displaystyle\leq 4|{s_{Q}}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\partial_{\alpha}\nabla u,\partial\gamma\nabla u\big)\Gamma_{Q}^{s_{Q}}\Gamma^{s_{1}}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

For the proof of Theorem
1.2
we fix a disk
B_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
We apply the Sobolev-Poincaré inequality to the solution
u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
under consideration and get the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\big|\nabla u-(\nabla u)\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\Bigg[\int_{B_{r}}\big|\nabla^{2}u\big|\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{2}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\Bigg[\int_{B_{r}}\Gamma^{-\frac{\mu}{4}}|\nabla^{2}u|\Gamma^{\frac{\mu}{4}}\Gamma^{-\frac{\gamma}{4}}\Gamma_{Q}^{-\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{4}}\Gamma^{\frac{\gamma}{4}}\Gamma_{Q}^{\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{4}}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\Bigg[\int_{B_{r}}\Gamma^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\Gamma^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Gamma_{Q}^{-\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{2}}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]\,\Bigg[\int_{B_{r}}\Gamma^{\frac{\mu+\gamma}{2}}\Gamma_{Q}^{\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{2}}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{B_{r}}\Gamma^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\Gamma^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Gamma_{Q}^{-\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{2}}\,{\rm d}x\,,

where we used the fact that
|\nabla u|
is bounded on the fixed ball
B_{r}
, however
c
may depend on the radius
r
.

Now we choose
R\gg r
and let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}\big(B_{2R}\big)
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, such that
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{R}
,

|\nabla\eta|\leq c/R
. Then (
2.1
) of Lemma
2.1
gives recalling (
1.4
)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\Gamma^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\Gamma^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Gamma_{Q}^{-\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{2}}\,{\rm d}x

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}}\Gamma^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\Gamma^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Gamma_{Q}^{-\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{2}}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\Bigg[\int_{\operatorname{spt}\eta}D^{2}f\big(\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u,\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u\big)\Gamma^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Gamma_{Q}^{-\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{2}}\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\Bigg[\frac{c}{R^{2}}\int_{T_{R}}\Gamma^{-\frac{\gamma+\overline{\mu}}{2}}\Gamma_{Q}^{-\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{2}}\big|\partial_{\alpha}u-Q_{\alpha}\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle cI(R)\cdot\Bigg[\frac{1}{R^{2}}\,\Xi(R)\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

We observe that (
2.2
) implies (again recalling (
1.4
))

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u,\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u\big)\Gamma^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Gamma_{Q}^{-\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{2}}\,{\rm d}x

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c}{R^{2}}\int_{T_{R}}\Gamma^{-\frac{\gamma+\overline{\mu}}{2}}\Gamma_{Q}^{-\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{2}}|\partial_{\alpha}u-Q_{\alpha}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,,

hence we can make use of our assumption (
1.5
) by choosing a suitable subsequence
R\to\infty
and obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}D^{2}f(\nabla u)\big(\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u,\nabla\partial_{\alpha}u\big)\Gamma^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Gamma_{Q}^{-\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{2}}\,{\rm d}x<\infty\,,

thus

as
R\to\infty
.

With this information we return to (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and obtain

\int_{B_{r}}\big|\nabla u-(\nabla u)\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq I(R)\cdot\Bigg[\frac{1}{R^{2}}\,\Xi(R)\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}

with
I(R)\to 0
as
R\to\infty
. This proves the theorem with the help of hypothesis (
1.5
). ∎

4
Applications to the linear growth case

1.1
by a suitable stronger variant specifying the linear

Assumption 4.1
.

The convex energy density
f
:
\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is of class
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and satisfies the non-uniform
ellipticity condition (
1.2
) with exponents
\mu>1
,
\overline{\mu}\leq 1
.

Moreover we assume that there exists a constant

such that for all

(4.1)

As outlined in

in this particular setting,

Proposition 4.1

Suppose that we have Assumption

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{|u(x)|}{|x|}<\infty\,

\int_{T_{R}}\Gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}\,{\rm d}x\leq c\big[1+R^{2}\big]\,.

Proof of Proposition

.
By the convexity of

(4.5)

hence for any

\displaystyle\int_{T_{R}}\Gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{\hat{T}_{R}}\big[1+f(\nabla u)\big]\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle cR^{2}+c\int_{\hat{T}_{R}}\nabla f(\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,.

Now we use the weak form of equation (

) with testfunction

, i.e.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla f(\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\big[u\eta^{2}\big]\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{T}_{R}}\nabla f(\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\hat{T}_{R}}\nabla f(\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\eta 2\eta u\,{\rm d}x\,,

hence we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla f(\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u\eta^{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq\frac{c}{R}\sup_{\hat{T}_{R}}|u|R^{2}\,.

(4.7)

Recalling our assumption (
4.3
) and combing (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) we find
the claim (
4.4
) of the proposition.∎

The next corollary gives an refinement of Corollary

by taking a measure for the relative oscillation

)).

Corollary 4.2
.

Suppose that we have Assumption
4.1
with
1/2<\overline{\mu}<1
.
For given
\gamma
,
\gamma_{Q}>0
,
1-\overline{\mu}<\gamma+\gamma_{Q}<1/2
, we let

p=\frac{1}{2-\gamma_{Q}-\gamma-\overline{\mu}}>1\,,\quad q=\frac{1}{\gamma_{Q}+\gamma+\overline{\mu}-1}

and define

\Theta_{Q}(x):=\Bigg[\frac{\Gamma_{Q}}{\Gamma}\Bigg]^{\frac{2-\gamma_{Q}}{2}q}\,,\quad\Theta(R):=\inf_{Q}\frac{1}{|T_{R}|}\int_{T_{R}}\Theta_{Q}(x)\,{\rm d}x\leq 1\,.

(4.8)

If we suppose that for all
R
sufficiently large

\sup_{\hat{T}_{R}}|u|\leq cR\Theta(R)^{-\frac{p}{q}}

(4.9)

Proof of Corollary
4.2
.
We estimate

\displaystyle\int_{T_{R}}\Gamma^{-\frac{\gamma+\overline{\mu}}{2}}\Gamma_{Q}^{-\frac{\gamma_{Q}}{2}}|\nabla u-Q|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{T_{R}}\Theta_{Q}^{\frac{1}{q}}\Gamma^{\frac{2-\gamma_{Q}-\gamma-\overline{\mu}}{2}}\,{\rm d}x

and obtain

\displaystyle\Xi(R)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{T_{R}}\Theta_{Q}^{\frac{1}{q}}\Gamma^{\frac{2-\gamma_{Q}-\gamma-\overline{\mu}}{2}}\,{\rm d}x

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigg[\int_{T_{R}}\Theta_{Q}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\Bigg[\int_{T_{R}}\Gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

We choose
Q=Q(R)
such that

\frac{1}{|T_{R}|}\int_{T_{R}}\Theta_{Q}\,{\rm d}x\leq 2\Theta(R)\,.

Then (
4.10
) implies

\Xi(R)\leq c\big[\Theta(R)R^{2}\big]^{\frac{1}{q}}\Bigg[\int_{T_{R}}\Gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

(4.11)

Discussing the right-hand side of (

) we exactly follow the proof of Proposition
4.1

and just insert hypothesis (
4.9
) in (
4.7

A rather important class of energy densities with linear growth is of splitting-type, i.e. of the form

with functions

,
f_{2}
of linear growth. The particular features of splitting type energy densities with linear growth are discussed
in

,

) with exponents

, respectively,
then we have
\overline{\mu}=0
for the energy density

.

Nevertheless we still can derive Liouville-type theorems from Theorem
1.2
.
In Corollary
4.3
we present an application, where in addition we make explicit use of

by choosing

as a mean value.
Then a smallness condition imposed on

provides the vanishing of the second derivatives.

Corollary 4.3
.

Suppose that we are given Assumption

with

\overline{\mu}>-1/2
. If we have for a finite constant

\sup_{T_{R}}|\nabla^{2}u|\leq cR^{-1}\,,

(4.12)

then

is an affine function.

Proof of Corollary (
4.3
).
We choose
\gamma=-\overline{\mu}<1/2
,

) is
satisfied. We have to show that (

5
Applications to the superlinear case

We adapt Assumption
1.1
to the case of superlinear growth, i.e. we now require

Assumption 5.1
.

Suppose that we are given numbers
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
q>1
such that
-\mu\leq q-2
and let
\overline{\mu}:=2-q
be the exponent
on the right-hand side of (
1.2
), i.e. the convex energy density
f
:
\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is of class
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and satisfies the non-uniform ellipticity condition

c_{1}\big(1+|Z|^{2}\big)^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}|Y|^{2}\leq D^{2}f(Z)\big(Y,Y\big)\leq c_{2}\big(1+|Z|^{2}\big)^{\frac{q-2}{2}}|Y|^{2}

(5.1)

for all
Z
,
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with exponents
\mu>1
,
q>1
and constants
c_{1}
,
c_{2}>0
.

Suppose that we have in addition

a|Z|^{s}-b\leq f(Z)\quad\mbox{for some $1<s\leq q$}

(5.2)

and with constants
a>0
,
b\geq 0
.

Note that, as in the linear growth case, the auxiliary parameter
\mu
needs no further specification in our hypotheses.

Conditions (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) are introduced in
[
10
]
describing energy densities of
(s,\mu,q)
-growth, we refer
to
[
11
]
, Section 3.2, for a more detailed discussion. In particular (
5.1
) implies with some constant
M>0
and for all
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

|\nabla f(Z)|\leq M\big(1+|Z|^{2}\big)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\,.

(5.3)

Examples are given, for instance, by

\displaystyle f_{1}(Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1+|Z_{1}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{s}{2}}+|Z_{2}|^{2}\,\quad 1<s\leq 2\,,

\displaystyle f_{2}(Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(1+|Z|^{2}\big)^{\frac{s}{2}}+\big(1+|Z_{2}|\big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\,\quad 1<s\leq q\,.

The main result of this section is

Corollary 5.1
.

Given Assumption
5.1
we suppose in addition that

s>q-\frac{1}{2}\,.

(5.4)

If we have

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{|u(x)|}{|x|}<\infty\,,

(5.5)

then
u
is an affine function.

Proof of Corollary
5.1
.

In order to apply Theorem
1.2
we let
Q=0
,
\gamma_{Q}=0
and since we have (
5.4
) we can choose

0<\gamma<1/2
such that

s>q-\gamma\,.

(5.6)

Then, in our main Theorem
1.2
we observe

\Xi(R)\leq\int_{\hat{T}_{R}}\Gamma^{\frac{q-\gamma}{2}}\,{\rm d}x\,.

(5.7)

We follow the proof of Proposition
4.1
, where now (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) are replaced by (recalling (
5.2
), (
5.3
)
(
5.5
) and choosing

sufficiently large)

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{T}_{R}}\Gamma^{\frac{s}{2}}\eta^{2l}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{\hat{T}_{R}}\big[1+f(\nabla u)\big]\eta^{2l}\,{\rm d}x

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle cR^{2}+\frac{c}{R}\sup_{\hat{T}_{R}}|u|\int_{\hat{T}_{R}}|\nabla f(\nabla u)|\eta^{2l-1}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle cR^{2}+c\int_{\hat{T}_{R}}\Gamma^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\eta^{2l-1}\,{\rm d}x\,.

Since

From (
5.6

5.8

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{T}_{R}}\Gamma^{\frac{q-\gamma}{2}}\eta^{2l}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{\hat{T}_{R}}\Gamma^{\frac{s}{2}}\eta^{2l}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle cR^{2}+c\Bigg[\int_{\hat{T}_{R}}\Gamma^{\frac{q-\gamma}{2}}\eta^{2l}\,{\rm d}x\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{\hat{q}}}R^{\frac{2}{\hat{p}}}\,.

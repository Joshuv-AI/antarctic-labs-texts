Slender Trees and the Approximation Property
Hannes Jakob

Abstract
. We obtain a relatively simple criterion for when a forcing has the < δ {<}\,\delta -approximation property, generalizing a result of Unger. Afterwards we apply this criterion to construct variants of Mitchell Forcing in order to answer questions posed by Mohammadpour.

03E05,03E35,03E55

1.
Introduction

In his PhD thesis, Christoph Weiss introduced a two-cardinal generalisation of a
\kappa
-tree called a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-list along with two “thinness“ properties, namely
thin
and
slender
. The corresponding cardinal properties, stating that every “not too wide“ list has an ineffable branch, behave very differently in practice. The
ineffable thin property
, denoted
\ITP
, is consistent with e.g. the approachability property and forced by many orders which were originally conceived with the tree property in mind. On the other hand, the
ineffable slender property
, denoted
\ISP
, forces that
\AP
fails and needs different preservation theorems compared to the tree property.

The proof structures for consistency proofs involving
\ISP
and
\ITP
are the same: Given a list in the extension
V[G]
, lift some ground-model embedding
V\to M
to an embedding
V[G]\to M[H]
inside
V[H]
, thus obtaining an ineffable branch in
V[H]
and “pull it back“ into
V[G]
using the thinness assumption and properties of the extension
V[H]
over
V[G]
. For thin lists, this is done using two branch lemmas stating that sufficiently closed forcings and forcings whose squares have small antichains do not add cofinal branches to thin lists. For
\delta
-slender lists however, we need that the pair
(V[G],V[H])
has the so-called
{<}\,\delta
-approximation property. In practice, this property is much harder to establish and so many things concerning
\ISP
are not known.

In this paper, we obtain two important tools to prove consistency results involving
\ISP
: We obtain a general criterion for when a forcing order preserves the statement
\ISP
and, more importantly, prove the following statement concerning the approximation property, generalizing a result of Unger from
[
13
]
:

Theorem A
.

Let
(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q},R)
be an iteration-like partial order and
\delta
a cardinal. Assume that the base ordering
(\mathbb{P},b(R))
is square-
\delta
-cc. and the term ordering
(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q},t(R))
is strongly
{<}\,\delta
-distributive. Then
(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q},R)
has the
{<}\,\delta
-approximation property.

Using this Theorem, we construct a number of variants of Mitchell Forcing to answer questions which were raised by Mohammadpour in
[
11
]
:

Theorem B
.

Let
\tau<\mu<\kappa\leq\lambda
be regular cardinals such that
\tau^{<\tau}=\tau
and
\kappa
is
\lambda
-ineffable. There exist forcing extensions satisfying the following (with
\kappa=\mu^{+}
):

(1)

\ISP(\tau^{+},\kappa,\lambda)
holds,
\ISP(\tau,\kappa,\lambda)
fails and
2^{\tau}
is an arbitrarily large cardinal below
\lambda
.

(2)

\ISP(\kappa,\kappa,\lambda)
holds,
\ISP(\mu,\kappa,\kappa)
fails and
2^{\tau}\neq 2^{\mu}
.

If
\kappa
is supercompact (i.e.
\lambda
-ineffable for all
\lambda\geq\kappa
), there exists a forcing extension such that:

(3)

\ISP(\tau^{+},\kappa,\geq\kappa)
holds and is indestructible under
{<}\,\kappa
-directed closed forcing.

The paper is organized as follows: After giving preliminaries and reviewing facts and definitions from
[
4
]
, we prove Theorem A. Afterwards we give the criterion for when a forcing order preserves
\ISP
. In the last sections, we prove the consistency results mentioned above.

Basic Forcing Facts

Our notation is fairly standard. We follow the convention that filters are upward closed, so that
q\leq p
means that
q
forces at least as much as
p
.
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright p
is defined as
\{q\in\mathbb{P}\;|\;q\leq p\}
.
V[\mathbb{P}]
denotes an arbitrary extension by
\mathbb{P}
, i.e. “
V[\mathbb{P}]
has property
P
“ means that for every
\mathbb{P}
-generic
G
,
V[G]
has property
P
. Lastly, for any forcing order
\mathbb{P}
, we let
\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}}
(omitting
\mathbb{P}
when it is clear which order we are talking about) be the canonical
\mathbb{P}
-name such that
\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}}^{G}=G
whenever
G
is a
\mathbb{P}
-generic filter.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
V\subseteq W
be models of set theory with the same ordinals and
\kappa
a regular cardinal in
V
.
(V,W)
has the
{<}\,\kappa
-covering property
if for any set
x\in W
,
x\subseteq V
of size
{<}\,\kappa
there is
y\in V
of size
{<}\,\kappa
such that
x\subseteq y
. A forcing order
\mathbb{P}
has the
\kappa
-covering property if
1_{\mathbb{P}}
forces that
(V,V[\mathbb{P}])
has it.

If
(V,W)
has the
{<}\,\kappa
-covering property, where
\kappa
is a regular cardinal,
\kappa
remains a regular cardinal in
W
: Otherwise there exists a cofinal subset of
\kappa
in
W
of size
{<}\,\kappa
. This set is covered by an element of
V
of size
{<}\,\kappa
, contradicting the regularity of
\kappa
. If
\mathbb{P}
is
\kappa
-cc. and
\dot{f}
any
\mathbb{P}
-name for a function from an ordinal into
V
, then there are fewer than
\kappa
possibilities for any value
\dot{f}(\check{\alpha})
, so we obtain:

Fact 2.2
.

If
\mathbb{P}
has the
\kappa
-cc.,
\mathbb{P}
has the
\kappa
-covering property.

When arguing the preservation of properties which are downwards absolute, we will frequently make use of projections, which are a way of showing that an extension by some order
\mathbb{Q}
is contained in an extension by another order
\mathbb{P}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be forcing orders. A function
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
is a
projection
if the following hold:

(1)

\pi(1_{\mathbb{P}})=1_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

(2)

For all
p\leq q
,
\pi(p)\leq\pi(q)

(3)

For all
p\in\mathbb{P}
, if
q\leq\pi(p)
, there is some
p^{\prime}\leq p
such that
\pi(p^{\prime})\leq q
.

If there exists a projection from
\mathbb{P}
to
\mathbb{Q}
, any extension by
\mathbb{Q}
can be forcing extended to an extension by
\mathbb{P}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be forcing orders,
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
a projection. Let
H
be
\mathbb{Q}
-generic. In
V[H]
, the forcing order
\mathbb{P}/H
consists of all
p\in\mathbb{P}
such that
\pi(p)\in H
. We let
\mathbb{P}/\mathbb{Q}
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-name for
\mathbb{P}/\dot{H}
and call
\mathbb{P}/\mathbb{Q}
the
quotient forcing
of
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
.

Fact 2.5
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be forcing orders and
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
a projection. If
H
is
\mathbb{Q}
-generic over
V
and
G
is
\mathbb{P}/H
-generic over

, then

is
\mathbb{P}
-generic over
V
and
H\subseteq\pi[G]
. In particular,
V[H][G]=V[G]
.

For completeness, we state the Product Lemma, which states that forcing with a product of orders can be viewed as successive forcing.

Lemma 2.6

(Product Lemma)
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be notions of forcing. For

,

, the following are equivalent:

(1)

G\times H
is
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q}
-generic over

.

(2)

-generic over

\mathbb{Q}

.

(3)

The approximation property was introduced by Hamkins to show certain impossibility results concerning the indestructibility of large cardinal notions. However, it is also highly related to slender lists.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
V\subseteq W
be models of set theory and
\delta
a cardinal in
V
.
(V,W)
has the
{<}\,\delta
-approximation property
if whenever
x\in W
is such that
x\cap z\in V
for every
z\in[x]^{<\delta}\cap V
,
x\in V
.

In his paper introducing the approximation property, Hamkins also showed that iterations
\mathbb{P}*\mathbb{Q}
where
\mathbb{P}
is of size
<\delta
and
\mathbb{Q}
is
{<}\,\delta
-strategically closed have the
{<}\,\delta
-approximation property. Over the years, this criterion has been improved step by step (and criteria of a very different flavor such as strong properness have been introduced). In this paper, we will obtain a further weakening of the necessary assumptions.

Clubs and Stationarity in
[\lambda]^{<\kappa}

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\kappa\leq\lambda
be cardinals of uncountable cofinality.
[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
is defined as the set of all
x\subseteq\lambda
with size
{<}\,\kappa
.

(1)

C\subseteq[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
is
club
in
[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
if it is unbounded and closed under ascending unions.

(2)

S\subseteq[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
is
stationary
in
[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
if it intersects every club.

The space
[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
behaves much like
\kappa
: Namely, the intersection of fewer than
\cf(\kappa)
club sets is club and the club filter is even closed under diagonal intersections, i.e. if
C_{\alpha}
is club for every
\alpha<\lambda
, so is

\Delta_{\alpha<\lambda}C_{\alpha}:=\{x\in[\lambda]^{<\kappa}\;|\;x\in\bigcap_{\alpha\in x}C_{\alpha}\}

There is also a connection between clubs in
[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
and functions
F\colon[\lambda]^{<\omega}\to[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
. If
F\colon[\lambda]^{<\omega}\to[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
, the set

C_{F}:=\{x\in[\lambda]^{<\kappa}\;|\;\forall y\in[x]^{<\omega}\;F(y)\subseteq x\}

is club in
[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
. It turns out that these sets generate the club filter (for a proof, see
[
10
]
):

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
C
be club in
[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
. Then there is
F\colon[\lambda]^{<\omega}\to[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
such that
C_{F}\subseteq C
.

As a corollary, we obtain that clubs in
[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
project to sets containing clubs in
[X]^{<\kappa}
whenever
X\subseteq\lambda
:

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
C\subseteq[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
be club and
X\subseteq\lambda
of size
\geq\kappa
. Then

C\upharpoonright X:=\{N\cap X\;|\;N\in C\}

contains a club in
[X]^{<\kappa}
.

Lists

(\kappa,\lambda)
-lists were introduced by Weiss in his PhD thesis. We introduce both notions of “thinness“ for completeness although we will only work with slender lists.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\kappa\leq\lambda
be regular cardinals.

(1)

A
(\kappa,\lambda)
-list
is a function
f\colon[\lambda]^{<\kappa}\to[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
such that
f(a)\subseteq a
for every
a\in[\lambda]^{<\kappa}

(2)

A
(\kappa,\lambda)
-list
f
is
thin
if for every
x\in[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
,

|\{f(z)\cap x\;|\;x\subseteq z\in[\lambda]^{<\kappa}\}|\;<\kappa

(3)

For a cardinal
\delta\leq\kappa
, a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-list
f
is
\delta
-slender
if for every sufficiently large
\Theta
there exists a club
C\subseteq[H(\Theta)]^{<\kappa}
such that for every
M\in C
:

\forall z\in[\lambda]^{<\delta}\cap M\;f(M\cap\lambda)\cap z\in M

(4)

If
f
is a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-list,
b\subseteq\lambda
is an
ineffable branch
for
f
if the set

\{z\in[\lambda]^{<\kappa}\;|\;z\cap b=f(z)\}

is stationary in
[\lambda]^{<\kappa}
.

For
\delta\leq\kappa
, the statement
\ISP(\delta,\kappa,\lambda)
says that every
\delta
-slender
(\kappa,\lambda)
-list has an ineffable branch.

3.
Strong Distributivity and Orders on Products

We expand the framework for working with arbitrary orders on products of sets by a new property and prove a result on when a forcing has the
{<}\,\delta
-approximation property.

We first review definitions and results from
[
4
]
.

Strong Distributivity

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a poset and
\delta
a cardinal.
\mathbb{P}
is
strongly
{<}\,\delta
-distributive
if for every
p\in\mathbb{P}
and all sequences
(D_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\delta}
of open dense subsets of
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright p
there is a descending sequence
(p_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\delta}
such that for every
\alpha<\delta
,
p_{\alpha}\in D_{\alpha}
.

Strong distributivity can be seen as a kind of uniform distributivity. It is related to the completeness game on a partial order which we will define now:

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a forcing order,
\delta
an ordinal. The
completeness game

G(\mathbb{P},\delta)
on
\mathbb{P}
with length
\delta
has players COM (complete) and INC (incomplete) playing elements of
\mathbb{P}
with COM playing at even ordinals (and limits) and INC playing at odd ordinals. COM starts by playing
1_{\mathbb{P}}
, afterwards
p_{\alpha}
has to be a lower bound of
(p_{\beta})_{\beta<\alpha}
. INC wins if COM is unable to play at some point
<\delta
. Otherwise, COM wins.

A forcing order
\mathbb{P}
is
{<}\,\kappa
-distributive if and only if for all
\alpha<\kappa
, INC does not have a winning strategy in
G(\mathbb{P},\alpha)
. The proof adapts to show the following:

Theorem 3.3

(Theorem 3.6 in
[
4
]
)
.

\mathbb{P}
is strongly
{<}\,\kappa
-distributive if and only if INC does not have a winning strategy in
G(\mathbb{P},\kappa)
.

An stronger property is
\kappa
-strategic closure:

Definition 3.4
.

A poset
\mathbb{P}
is
\kappa
-strategically closed
if COM has a winning strategy in
G(\mathbb{P},\kappa)
. It is
{<}\,\kappa
-strategically closed
if for all
\alpha<\kappa
, COM has a winning strategy in
G(\mathbb{P},\alpha)
.

It is clear that if
\mathbb{P}
is
\kappa
-strategically closed it is strongly
{<}\,\kappa
-distributive. However, there is no provable connection between
{<}\,\kappa
-strategic closure and strong
{<}\,\kappa
-distributivity.

Orders on Products

As variants of Mitchell Forcing are neither exactly products nor iterations, it is helpful to think of them as abstract orders on products of sets with certain regularity properties. This extends material from
[
4
]
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be sets and
R
a partial order on
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q}
. We will only consider orderings where for all
p,p^{\prime}

\exists q_{0},q_{1}((p,q_{0})R(p^{\prime},q_{1}))\longrightarrow\forall q((p,q)R(p^{\prime},q))

If we want to reference this property, we will say that
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q}
is
based
. We define the following partial orders:

(1)

The
base ordering

b(R)
is an ordering on
\mathbb{P}
given by
p(b(R))p^{\prime}
if there are
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
(p,q_{0})R(p^{\prime},q_{1})
.

(2)

The
term ordering

t(R)
is an ordering on
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q}
given by
(p,q)(t(R))(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
if
(p,q)R(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
and
p=p^{\prime}
.

(3)

For
p\in\mathbb{P}
, the
section ordering

s(R,p)
is an ordering on
\mathbb{Q}
given by
q(s(R,p))q^{\prime}
if
(p,q)R(p,q^{\prime})
.

We also fix the following properties:

(1)

(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q},R)
has the
projection property
if whenever
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})R(p,q)
, there is
q^{\prime\prime}
such that
(p,q^{\prime\prime})R(p,q)
and
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime\prime})R(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})R(p^{\prime},q^{\prime\prime})
.

(2)

(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q},R)
has the
refinement property
if
p^{\prime}(b(R))p
implies that
s(R,p^{\prime})
refines
s(R,p)
, i.e. whenever
(p,q^{\prime})R(p,q)
and
p^{\prime}(b(R))p
, also
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})R(p^{\prime},q)
.

(3)

(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q},R)
has the
mixing property
if whenever
(p,q_{0}),(p,q_{1})R(p,q)
, there are
p_{0},p_{1}\in\mathbb{P}
and
q^{\prime}
with
(p,q^{\prime})\leq(p,q)
such that
(p_{i},q^{\prime})\leq(p,q_{i})
.

We say that
(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q},R)
is
iteration-like
if
(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q},R)
is based and has the projection property, the refinement property and the mixing property.

The projection and refinement property hold in almost all cases, and always for iterations and products. They are necessary for most of the relevant techniques. The mixing property roughly states that we can mix elements of
\mathbb{Q}
modulo
\mathbb{P}
and holds e.g. in iterations
\mathbb{P}*\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
if
\mathbb{P}
is atomless.

We also note that the term ordering is the disjoint union of the section orderings, so if the term ordering is
{<}\,\delta
-closed (strategically closed, strongly distributive etc.)for some
\delta
, so are all the section orderings (and vice versa).

Lemma 3.6
.

If
(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q},R)
is based and has the projection and refinement property, the identity is a projection from
(\mathbb{P},b(R))\times(\mathbb{Q},s(R,1_{\mathbb{P}}))
onto
(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q},R)
.

If
(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q},R)
is an ordering on a product, forcing with
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q}
can also be regarded as first forcing with
\mathbb{P}
and then with a special ordering on
\mathbb{Q}
. However, this will not be of use to us in this paper.

In
[
13
]
, Unger showed that an iteration
\mathbb{P}*\mathbb{Q}
, where
\mathbb{P}
is square-
\delta
-cc. and
\mathbb{Q}
is forced to be
{<}\,\delta
-closed, has the
{<}\,\delta
-approximation property. We will improve this result in two ways: Firstly, we work with an arbitrary order on a product, allowing us to apply the result more easily to variants of Mitchell Forcing and secondly, we only need a “distributivity“ assumption as opposed to one about closure. This is important, as, unlike
{<}\,\delta
-closure, strong
{<}\,\delta
-distributivity is preserved by forcing with
\delta
-cc. forcings.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q},R)
be an iteration-like partial order and
\delta
a cardinal. Assume
(\mathbb{P},b(R))^{2}
is
\delta
-cc. and
(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q},t(R))
is strongly

-distributive. Then
(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{Q},R)
has the
<\delta
-approximation property.

Lemma 5.2

Proof.

The statements (1) and (2) are standard.

We give the proofs for (3) and (4) in order to familiarize ourselves with the definitions.

Regarding the base ordering, we observe that

in the ordering on

To show (4), let

|x|<\mu

(p,q_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\delta}

Regarding the projection property, given

\mathbb{M}_{0}

For the mixing property, let

As follows: Let

are incompatible since

is the successor of a cardinal. So we can let

Our forcing will be a guessing variant of Mitchell forcing, modified to collapse cardinals in a “non-fresh“ way to ensure the approximation property.

We will need the concept of a Laver function:

Let

\mathbb{M}(\kappa)

\mathbb{M}(\kappa)*\dot{\mathbb{L}}

\mathbb{M}(\nu)

\pi(\sigma)

\mathbb{M}(\nu)

•

8.
Open Questions

We finish by stating two well-known questions which might have become more tractable with the new techniques used in this paper.

Question 8.1
.

Is it consistent to have
\ISP(\omega_{1},\omega_{n+2},\lambda_{n})
for every
n\in\omega
?

A model of the former would most likely require
2^{\omega}=\aleph_{\omega+1}
. With a “better“ powerset sequence, one could hope to find a model of the following:

Question 8.2
.

Is it consistent to have
\ISP(\omega_{n+2},\omega_{n+2},\lambda_{n})
for every
n\in\omega
together with
2^{\omega_{n}}=\omega_{n+2}
?

We have shown a slight variation of the Iterands of the Cummings-Foreman iteration (see
[
2
]
) can force
\ISP(\tau^{+},\kappa,\geq\kappa)
to be indestructible under
{<}\,\kappa
-directed closed forcing (which is an important step in showing that the tree property holds at all
\aleph_{n+2}
), hopefully making progress toward an answer to the questions.

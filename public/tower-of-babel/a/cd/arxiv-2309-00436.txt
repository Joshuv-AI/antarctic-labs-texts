Hyperfine splitting in 6,7 Li +
Krzysztof Pachucki, Vojtěch Patkóš, Vladimir A. Yerokhin

Abstract
We present a detailed derivation of the QED effects of order α 7 ​ m \alpha^{7}\,m to the hyperfine structure (hfs) of the S 3 {}^{3}S states of heliumlike ions and perform numerical calculations for 6 Li + and 7 Li + . By comparing the theoretical point-nucleus results with the measured hfs of Li + , we determine the nuclear-structure contribution parametrized in terms of the effective Zemach radius. Using the experimental hfs results for Li + , we obtain accurate predictions for the hfs of 6 Li 2+ and 7 Li 2+ , for which no experimental data is available so far. By examining the normalized differences of the hfs of Li + and Li and of the corresponding isotope-shift differences, we test the consistency of the hfs measurements in 6,7 Li + and 6,7 Li.



I
Introduction

The hyperfine structure (hfs) of atomic levels with vanishing orbital angular momentum
arises from the interaction between the nuclear spin
and the intrinsic angular momentum (spin) of the electrons within an atom.
The energy level of an atomic
S
state can be conveniently represented
as a sum of the centroid energy level
E_{\rm cent}
, the
magnetic dipole hyperfine structure
E_{M1}

and the electric quadrupole hyperfine structure
E_{E2}
,

\displaystyle E=

\displaystyle\,E_{\rm cent}+E_{M1}+E_{E2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,E_{\rm cent}+A\,\langle\vec{I}\cdot\vec{S}\rangle+B\,\langle(I^{i}I^{j})^{(2)}(S^{i}S^{j})^{(2)}\rangle\,,

(1)

where
A
and
B
are the so-called hyperfine constants,

\vec{S}
is the total spin of electrons,
\vec{I}
is the nuclear spin operator,
(a^{i}a^{j})^{(2)}
denotes the
irreducible second-rank tensor, and the summation over repeated indices is implicit.
The matrix elements are given by

\displaystyle\langle\vec{I}\cdot\vec{S}\rangle=\big[F(F+1)-I(I+1)-S(S+1)\big]/2\,,

(2)

and

\displaystyle\langle(I^{i}I^{j})^{(2)}(S^{i}S^{j})^{(2)}\rangle=

\displaystyle\,\langle\vec{I}\cdot\vec{S}\rangle^{2}+\langle\vec{I}\cdot\vec{S}\rangle/2

\displaystyle-I(I+1)S(S+1)/3\,.

(3)

We note that the electric quadrupole structure
is present only for the electron states with the total momentum

J=S>1/2
; in particular, it vanishes for the ground state of atomic Li and
Li
2+
.

In the present work we are interested in the magnetic dipole hfs.
In order to obtain
experimental results for the
A
constant from the measured spectra, one needs
to eliminate the quadrupole structure by combining
several hfs transitions.
Using the most accurate experimental results summarized in Table
,
we get for the Li
+
ion

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{exp}}({}^{6}\mathrm{Li^{+}})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{6}\,\nu_{0-1}+\frac{5}{12}\,\nu_{1-2}\,,

(4)

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{exp}}({}^{7}\mathrm{Li^{+}})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{6}\,\nu_{1/2-3/2}+\frac{3}{10}\,\nu_{3/2-5/2}\,,

(5)

where
\nu_{F-F^{\prime}}
are the measured
F-F^{\prime}
transition energies.
For atomic Li, the quadrupole structure is absent, so we have just

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{exp}}({}^{6}\mathrm{Li})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{2}{3}\,\nu_{1/2-3/2}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{exp}}({}^{7}\mathrm{Li})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\,\nu_{1-2}\,.

(7)

II
Hfs theory of light hydrogen-like ions

We start with summarizing the existing theory for the magnetic dipole hfs of
S
states
of hydrogen-like atoms. To the leading order in the fine-structure constant
\alpha
, it is given by

\displaystyle E_{F}=\frac{4}{3}(Z\alpha)^{4}\frac{m_{\mathrm{r}}^{3}}{m\,M}\,g\,\langle\vec{I}\cdot\vec{S}\rangle\,,

(8)

where
m_{\mathrm{r}}=mM/(m+M)
is the reduced mass,

m
and
M
are the mass of the electron and nucleus,
respectively,

Z
is the nuclear charge number, and the nuclear
g
-factor is defined as

\displaystyle\vec{\mu}=\frac{Z\,e}{2\,M}\,g\,\vec{I}\,,

(9)

where
\vec{\mu}
is the nuclear magnetic moment operator.
The leading-order hfs contribution in Eq. (
8
) is also known as the Fermi energy.

The complete hyperfine structure of
S
states in hydrogenic systems
is represented as an expansion in terms of
\alpha
,

\displaystyle E_{M1}=

\displaystyle\ E_{F}\,(1+\delta)\,,

(10)

where

\displaystyle\delta=

\displaystyle\ \kappa+\delta^{(2)}+\delta^{(3)}+\delta^{(4)}+\delta^{(2)}_{\mathrm{rec.rel}}+\delta_{\mathrm{struct}}\,.

(11)

Here,
\kappa
is the magnetic moment anomaly of a free electron,
\kappa=\alpha/(2\pi)+O(\alpha^{2})
,

\delta^{(i)}
are QED corrections of order
\alpha^{i}E_{F}
,

\delta^{(2)}_{\mathrm{rec.rel}}
is
the relativistic recoil correction of order
\alpha^{2}\,E_{F}
,
and
\delta_{\mathrm{struct}}
is the nuclear structure correction.

The results for the QED corrections for an
S
state and a point and infinitely heavy nucleus
are given by
eides:01
;
tiesinga:21:codata18

\displaystyle\delta^{(2)}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{3}{2}\,(Z\,\alpha)^{2}+\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)\Bigl(\ln 2-\frac{5}{2}\Bigr)\,,

(12)

\displaystyle\delta^{(3)}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{2}}{\pi}\!\bigg[\!-\frac{8}{3}\ln(Z\,\alpha)\Bigl(\ln(Z\,\alpha)-\ln 4+\frac{281}{480}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\ +17.122\,338\,751\,3-\frac{8}{15}\,\ln 2+\frac{34}{225}\biggr]

\displaystyle\ +\frac{\alpha^{2}\,(Z\,\alpha)}{\pi}\,0.770\,99(2)\,,

(13)

\displaystyle\delta^{(4)}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{17}{8}\,(Z\,\alpha)^{4}+\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{3}\,\bigg[\Big(\frac{547}{48}-5\,\ln 2\Big)\,\ln(Z\,\alpha)

\displaystyle\ +G_{\rm SE}^{(4)}(Z\alpha)+\frac{13}{24}\,\ln 2+\frac{539}{288}\biggr]

\displaystyle\ -\frac{\alpha^{2}\,(Z\,\alpha)^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\,\biggl[\frac{4}{3}\,\ln^{2}(Z\,\alpha)+1.278\,\ln(Z\,\alpha)+10.0\pm 2.5\biggr]

\displaystyle\ \pm\frac{\alpha^{3}\,(Z\,\alpha)}{\pi^{2}}\,.

(14)

Here,
G_{\rm SE}^{(4)}(Z\alpha)
is the one-loop self-energy correction which needs to be calculated numerically.
For Li, we use the result from Ref.
yerokhin:08:prl
of
G_{\rm SE}^{(4+)}(3\alpha)=-4.587\,5(1)

which includes higher orders in
Z\,\alpha
for
Z=3
. Furthermore,
the last term in
\delta^{(4)}
represents the estimate of the
unknown three-loop binding QED correction.

The relativistic recoil correction was derived in Ref.
Bodwin:88
.
It has a finite point-nucleus limit and is given by

\displaystyle\delta^{(2)}_{\mathrm{rec.rel}}=

\displaystyle\ (Z\,\alpha)^{2}\,\frac{m_{\mathrm{r}}^{2}}{m\,M}\,\biggl\{-\bigg[-6+\frac{7}{2}g+\frac{14}{g}\biggr]\,\frac{\ln(Z\,\alpha)}{4}

\displaystyle\ \hskip-43.05542pt-\biggl[-2+\frac{11}{2}g+\frac{46}{g}\biggr]\,\frac{\ln 2}{4}+\frac{1}{36}\biggl[-51+\frac{31}{2}g+\frac{300}{g}\biggr]\biggr\}\,.

(15)

The last term in Eq. (
11
) is the nuclear-structure contribution
\delta_{\rm struct}
.
Its dominant part is the elastic contribution of order
\alpha\,E_{F}
, which is parametrized in terms of
the Zemach radius
r_{Z}
,

\displaystyle\delta^{(1)}_{\mathrm{struct}}(\mbox{\rm elastic})=

\displaystyle\ -2\,Z\,\alpha\,m_{\mathrm{r}}\,r_{Z}\,,

(16)

where

r_{Z}=\int d^{3}r_{1}\int d^{3}r_{2}\,\rho_{E}(\vec{r}_{1})\,\rho_{M}(\vec{r}_{2})\,|\vec{r}_{1}-\vec{r}_{2}|\,,

(17)

and
\rho_{E}
and
\rho_{M}
are the Fourier transforms of the electric and magnetic form factors
of the nucleus, normalized to unity.

There are many further contributions to
\delta_{\rm struct}
, both of the elastic and the inelastic kind.
So far there is no established theory for calculating the inelastic nuclear effects in hfs for
a compound nucleus. For this reason, we parametrize the
whole
nuclear structure contribution
in terms of the
effective
Zemach radius
\tilde{r}_{Z}
, which is, by definition,

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{struct}}\equiv-2\,Z\,\alpha\,m_{\mathrm{r}}\,\tilde{r}_{Z}\,.

(18)

It should be noted that the definition of the nuclear
structure contribution (and, therefore,
the effective Zemach radius) is not unique. In particular, in our previous study of He
+

patkos:23
we separated out from
\delta_{\mathrm{struct}}
the nuclear recoil correction

\delta^{(1)}_{\mathrm{rec}}
and the elastic higher-order nuclear
contribution
\delta^{(2)}_{\mathrm{nuc}}
. Moreover, many previous studies (among them,
Refs.
qi:20
;
sun:23
) disregarded all higher-order nuclear contributions, thus making
no difference between the elastic Zemach radius
r_{Z}
and the effective Zemach radius
\tilde{r}_{Z}
.
In the present work, we define the nuclear structure contribution by Eq. (
11
),
where we separated out only those corrections that have a well-defined point-nucleus
limit.

III
Hfs theory of light few-electron atoms

For the
S
states, the leading-order magnetic dipole hyperfine structure is
given by

\displaystyle E_{F}\equiv\langle V_{F}\rangle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{4\,\pi\,Z\alpha}{3\,m\,M}\,g\,\Big<\vec{I}\cdot\sum_{a}\vec{s}_{a}\,\delta^{3}(r_{a})\Big>\,.

(19)

The matrix element in the above expression is assumed to be calculated for a finite nuclear mass
and thus implicitly contains the reduced mass prefactor.
For a hydrogen-like atom, Eq. (
19
) reduces to Eq. (
8
).
It is often convenient to separate out from
E_{F}
the dependence on the total angular momentum by introducing
the hyperfine constant
A_{F}
which does not depend on the hyperfine state,

\displaystyle E_{F}=A_{F}\,\langle\vec{I}\cdot\vec{S}\rangle\,.

(20)

The complete magnetic dipole hyperfine structure is expressed analogously to Eq. (
10
)

\displaystyle E_{M1}=E_{F}(1+\delta)\,,

(21)

where

\displaystyle\delta=\kappa

\displaystyle+\delta^{(2)}\!+\delta^{(3)}+\delta^{(4)}+\delta^{(2)}_{\mathrm{rec}}+\delta_{\mathrm{struct}}\,.

(22)

Calculations of the leading-order
magnetic dipole hfs in helium and heliumlike atoms are presently well established

morton:06:cjp
. The leading QED correction of order
\alpha^{2}E_{F}
,
\delta^{(2)}
,
was derived and calculated for helium in our previous investigations

pachucki:01:jpb
;
pachucki:12:hehfs
.
For the Li
+
and Be
2+
ions, similar calculations were carried out in Refs.
qi:20
;
sun:23
;
qi:23
.
The higher-order QED correction
\delta^{(3)}
was reported by us for helium atom in
Ref.
patkos:unpunlished
. In the next section, we
present the detailed derivation of formulas and perform numerical calculations
of
\delta^{(3)}
for Li
+
. The higher-order QED correction

\delta^{(4)}
is approximated by the hydrogenlike value in Eq. (
14
).
While it is a relatively small correction, its uncertainty
will define the overall uncertainty of our theoretical predictions for the point-nucleus hfs.

The complete relativistic recoil correction
\delta^{(2)}_{\mathrm{rec}}
for few-electron systems is presently unknown.
In this work we approximate it by a sum of the relativistic recoil correction
for the corresponding hydrogenic ion and the mixing contribution

\delta^{(2)}_{\mathrm{rec.mix}}
,

\displaystyle\delta^{(2)}_{\mathrm{rec}}=\delta^{(2)}_{\mathrm{rec.rel}}(\mathrm{Li}^{2+})+\delta^{(2)}_{\mathrm{rec.mix}}\,.

(23)

The mixing correction
\delta^{(2)}_{\mathrm{rec.mix}}

is a second-order (in the magnetic moment) contribution due to mixing with closely lying excited states.
This correction is specific for the heliumlike ions, where the reference
2^{3}S
state
and the first excited

2^{1}S
state are separated by
a small energy interval. Namely, for the
2^{3}S
reference state, the
following mixing contribution is present:

\displaystyle\frac{\bigl\langle 2^{3}S|V_{F}|2^{1}S\bigr\rangle\,\bigl\langle 2^{1}S|V_{F}|2^{3}S\bigr\rangle}{E(2^{3}S)-E(2^{1}S)}

\displaystyle=I^{i}\,I^{j}\,\frac{\bigl\langle 2^{3}S|V_{F}^{i}|2^{1}S\bigr\rangle\,\bigl\langle 2^{1}S|V_{F}^{j}|2^{3}S\bigr\rangle}{E(2^{3}S)-E(2^{1}S)}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{i}{2}\,\epsilon^{ijk}\,I^{k}\,\frac{\bigl\langle 2^{3}S|V_{F}^{i}|2^{1}S\bigr\rangle\,\bigl\langle 2^{1}S|V_{F}^{j}|2^{3}S\bigr\rangle}{E(2^{3}S)-E(2^{1}S)}

\displaystyle=A_{F}\,\vec{I}\cdot\vec{S}\,\delta^{(2)}_{\mathrm{rec.mix}}\,,

(24)

where we retained only the contribution to the magnetic dipole hfs.
Despite being second order in the electron-nucleus mass ratio, this correction is significant because
of the small energy difference in the denominator.
We note that a similar correction contributes to the electric quadrupole structure
and is present even when the quadrupole moment of the nucleus is zero.

The nuclear-structure contribution
\delta_{\mathrm{struct}}
cannot be calculated at present.
Instead, we will extract it from the experimental value of hfs in Li
+
.
It is important that, with high accuracy, the nuclear structure contribution expressed in
terms of
\delta_{\mathrm{struct}}
is the same for Li
2+
, Li
+
, and Li.
This fact will allow us to predict the hyperfine structure of Li
2+
and to cross-check
the experimental results for Li
+
against those for Li.

IV
QED correction of order
\bm{\alpha^{3}E_{F}}

We now turn to the derivation of
the QED contribution of the order of
m\alpha^{7}
(
\equiv\alpha^{3}E_{F}
) to the
magnetic dipole hyperfine
structure of triplet
S
states in few-electron atoms. The
m\alpha^{7}
contribution to
E_{M1}
consists of
the photon-exchange terms (no radiative loops), the one-loop self-energy, the one-loop vacuum polarization, and the two-loop QED effects.
It can be represented in terms of the first- and second-order matrix elements as

\displaystyle\ E^{(7)}\equiv E_{F}\,\delta^{(3)}=E_{L}+2\,\bigg\langle H^{(4)}_{\rm hfs}\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}\,H^{(5)}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle\ +2\,\bigg\langle H^{(5)}_{\rm hfs}\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}\,H^{(4)}\bigg\rangle+\langle H^{(7)}_{\rm hfs}\rangle+E_{\mathrm{2loop}}\,,

(25)

where
E_{L}
is the low-energy Bethe-logarithm-type contribution;

H^{(4)}_{\rm hfs}
,
H^{(5)}_{\rm hfs}
, and
H^{(7)}_{\rm hfs}

are the effective hfs Hamiltonians of order
m\alpha^{4}
,
m\alpha^{5}
, and
m\alpha^{7}
, respectively;

H^{(4)}
is the Breit Hamiltonian,

H^{(5)}
is the effective QED Hamiltonian of order
m\alpha^{5}
,
and
H_{0}
and
E_{0}
are the nonrelativistic Hamiltonian and its reference-state eigenvalue. The lowest-order hfs Hamiltonian

H^{(4)}_{\rm hfs}
is given by Eqs. (5)-(11) of Ref.
pachucki:12:hehfs
, where one should put the electron
anomalous magnetic moment (amm) to zero. The next-order hfs Hamiltonian
H^{(5)}_{\rm hfs}
is the
amm correction to
H^{(4)}_{\rm hfs}
and is immediately obtained from
Eqs. (5)-(11) of Ref.
pachucki:12:hehfs
.
The Breit-Pauli Hamiltonian is well known and given, e.g., by Eq. (7) of Ref.
yerokhin:10:helike
.
The effective QED Hamiltonian of order
m\alpha^{5}
is

\displaystyle H^{(5)}=

\displaystyle\ \bigg(\frac{5}{6}-\frac{1}{5}+\ln\frac{\alpha^{-2}}{2\lambda}\bigg)\,\frac{4\,\alpha^{2}Z}{3\,m^{2}}\,\big[\delta^{3}(r_{1})+\delta^{3}(r_{2})\big]

\displaystyle\ -\frac{7\,\alpha^{2}}{3\,\pi\,m^{2}}\,\frac{1}{r^{3}}+H^{(5)}_{\rm fs}\,,

(26)

where
r=|\vec{r}_{1}-\vec{r}_{2}|
,
\lambda
is the low-energy cutoff of photon momenta, and

H^{(5)}_{\rm fs}
is the amm correction to the spin-dependent Breit-Pauli Hamiltonian,
given by Eq. (14) of Ref.
yerokhin:10:helike
.

E_{\mathrm{2loop}}
is the two-loop contribution which has
the same form as in hydrogen-like atoms, see the last term in Eq. (
13
),
and is given by

\displaystyle E_{\rm 2loop}=E_{F}\,\frac{\alpha^{2}\,(Z\,\alpha)}{\pi}\,0.770\,99(2)\,.

(27)

The derivation of the low-energy contribution
E_{L}
and the first-order
m\alpha^{7}
effective Hamiltonian

H^{(7)}_{\rm hfs}
is presented below.

IV.1
Low-energy contribution
\bm{E_{L}}

In this section, it will be convenient to use the atomic units,
and pull out the overall prefactor
\alpha^{7}
. Also, in the rest of the paper we will
set the electron mass to unity,
m=1
, unless specified otherwise.

In the low-energy region the momentum of the virtual photon
k
is of the order
k\approx\alpha^{2}
.
The corresponding low-energy hfs contribution comes from the perturbation of the Bethe logarithm by
the Fermi contact interaction operator

{\cal V}_{F}=\frac{g}{M}\frac{2\,\pi\,Z}{3}\,\vec{I}\cdot\vec{S}\,\big[\delta^{3}(r_{1})+\delta^{3}(r_{2})\big]\,.

(28)

The low-energy contribution
E_{L}
is

\displaystyle E_{L}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{2}{3\,\pi}\int_{0}^{\lambda}dk\,kP_{L}(k)\,,

(29)

where

\displaystyle\ P_{L}(k)=2\left<{\cal V}_{F}\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}\,\vec{P}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k}\,\vec{P}\right>

(30)

\displaystyle\ +\left<\vec{P}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k}\,\bigl[{\cal V}_{F}-\langle{\cal V}_{F}\rangle\bigr]\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k}\,\vec{P}\right>\,,

and
\vec{P}=\vec{p}_{1}+\vec{p}_{2}
is the total momentum operator.
The large-
k
expansion of
P_{L}(k)
is

\displaystyle kP_{L}(k)

\displaystyle\ =A+\frac{B}{\sqrt{k}}+\frac{C\,\ln k}{k}+\frac{D}{k}+\ldots\,,

(31)

where
A
,
B
,
C
, and
D
are the asymptotic constants summarized in Appendix
.
The
\lambda
-dependent part of
E_{L}
is separated as

E_{L}=E_{L}^{\prime}+\frac{2}{3\,\pi}\bigg(\frac{C}{2}\ln^{2}\lambda+D\,\ln\lambda\bigg)\,,

(32)

where the finite
\lambda
-independent part is expressed as

\displaystyle E^{\prime}_{L}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{2}{3\,\pi}\biggl\{\int_{0}^{K}kdk\,P_{L}(k)+\int_{K}^{\infty}dk\,\biggl[kP_{L}(k)

\displaystyle-A-\frac{B}{\sqrt{k}}-\frac{C\ln k}{k}-\frac{D}{k}\biggr]

\displaystyle-\biggl[A\,K+2\,B\,\sqrt{K}+\frac{C}{2}\ln^{2}K+D\,\ln K\biggr]\biggr\}\,,

(33)

where
K\geq 1
is a free parameter.

In order to remove the dominant
Z
and state dependence from
E^{\prime}_{L}
, it is convenient
to define the
\delta
-function-perturbed Bethe logarithm
\beta_{\delta}
as

\displaystyle E^{\prime}_{L}=\beta_{\delta}\frac{Z^{2}}{4\pi}\big<{\cal V}_{F}\big>-\frac{2}{3\pi}\Big(\frac{C}{2}\ln^{2}Z^{2}+D\ln Z^{2}\Big)\,.

(34)

Defined in such a way,
\beta_{\delta}
depends very weakly on
Z
and its
numerical values for few-electron atoms are very close to the hydrogenic values.

IV.2
\bm{m\alpha^{7}}
Hamiltonian
\bm{H^{(7)}_{\mathrm{hfs}}}

The effective
m\alpha^{7}
Hamiltonian
H^{(7)}_{\rm hfs}
can be represented as

\displaystyle H^{(7)}_{\rm hfs}=

\displaystyle\ H^{(7)}_{{\rm hfs},A}+H^{(7)}_{{\rm hfs},B}+\ldots\,,

(35)

where
\ldots
denotes terms that are proportional to the electron-nucleus Dirac
\delta
function,
\delta^{3}(r_{a})
.
At the first stage of the derivation we will routinely drop such terms; the corresponding
contribution will be restored later by matching the high-
Z
limit of the
obtained formulas to the known hydrogenic result; see Sec.
.
More specifically, we will omit terms proportional to
Z^{3}\,\delta^{3}(r_{a})
; all other
terms proportional to
\delta^{3}(r_{a})
will be preserved throughout the derivation.

The first part of
H^{(7)}_{\rm hfs}

comes from the spin-dependent terms in the generalized Breit-Pauli Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{BP}}

that are proportional to the electron amm
\kappa
. Specifically,

\displaystyle\delta H_{\rm BP}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{a}\kappa\,\biggl\{\frac{Z\alpha}{2}\,\vec{\sigma}_{a}\cdot\frac{\vec{r}_{a}}{r_{a}^{3}}\times\vec{\pi}_{a}+\frac{e}{8}\,\{\vec{\pi}_{a}\cdot\vec{B}_{a}\,,\,\vec{\pi}_{a}\cdot\vec{\sigma}_{a}\}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{e}{16}\,[\pi^{i}_{a}\,,\,[\pi^{i}_{a}\,,\,\vec{\sigma}_{a}\cdot\vec{B}_{a}]]\biggr\}

\displaystyle\ +\sum_{a\neq b}\frac{\alpha\,\kappa}{2\,r_{ab}^{3}}\vec{\sigma}_{a}\cdot\vec{r}_{ab}\times(\vec{\pi}_{b}-\vec{\pi}_{a})\,,

(36)

where
\vec{\pi}=\vec{p}-e\,\vec{A}
and

\displaystyle e\,\vec{A}(\vec{r})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{e}{4\pi}\vec{\mu}\times\frac{\vec{r}}{r^{3}}=-Z\alpha\,\frac{g}{2\,M}\vec{I}\times\frac{\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\,,

(37)

\displaystyle e\,B^{i}(\vec{r})=

\displaystyle\ (\nabla\times\vec{A})^{i}=-Z\alpha\,\frac{g}{2\,M}\frac{8\pi}{3}\delta^{3}(r)\,I^{i}

\displaystyle\ +Z\alpha\,\frac{g}{2\,M}\frac{1}{r^{3}}\bigg(\delta^{ij}-3\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{2}}\bigg)I^{j}\,.

(38)

Performing calculations as described in Appendix
we obtain

\displaystyle H^{(7)}_{{\rm hfs},A}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{g\,Z\alpha\,\kappa}{4\,M}\,\vec{I}\cdot\vec{S}\,\bigg\{\frac{2Z\alpha}{3\,r_{1}^{4}}-\frac{1}{6}\,\bigg(\frac{8\pi}{3}\,p_{1}^{i}\,\delta^{3}(r_{1})\,p_{1}^{i}

\displaystyle\ -p_{1}^{i}\,\frac{1}{r_{1}^{5}}\big(r_{1}^{2}\,\delta^{ij}-3\,r_{1}^{i}r_{1}^{j}\big)\,p_{1}^{j}\bigg)+\frac{\pi}{3}\,\Delta\,\delta^{3}(r_{1})

\displaystyle\ -\frac{4}{3}\alpha\,\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}{r^{3}\,r_{1}^{3}}\bigg\}+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

(39)

Some operators in the above expression are singular at the origin and thus are not well defined, but this ambiguity
will be eliminated by matching with the known hydrogenic result.

The second part of
H^{(7)}_{\rm hfs}
is a middle-energy contribution
that can be expressed in terms of slopes of form factors
and the one-loop vacuum polarization. The derivation described in Appendix

\displaystyle H^{(7)}_{{\rm hfs},B}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{2\pi}{3}\,\frac{g\,Z\alpha}{M}\,\vec{I}\cdot\vec{S}\,\bigg[F^{\prime}_{1}(0)+F^{\prime}_{2}(0)-\frac{\alpha}{15\,\pi}\bigg]

\displaystyle\ \times\Delta\,\delta^{3}(r_{1})+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,,

(41)

IV.3
Regularization of divergencies and restoration of the

\delta^{3}(r_{a})
part

From now on we will use atomic units and pull out the overall
\alpha^{7}
prefactor.
The second-order matrix elements in Eq. (
IV
) contain divergences coming from the
summation over the intermediate states. They arise when operators on the left
and on the right of the resolvent
1/(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}
are sufficiently singular, so that their first-order
matrix elements are finite but the second-order matrix elements diverge. Specifically, there
are two such “problematic” operators in our case, the electron-nucleus Dirac
\delta
function and
the spin-independent part of the Breit Hamiltonian
H^{(4)}_{\rm nfs}
, given by Eq. (6) of
Ref.
pachucki:09:hefs
. The divergences become more tractable if one moves them
to first-order matrix elements. This can be accomplished
pachucki:06:hesinglet
by representing the problematic singular
operators as an anticommutator with the Schrödinger Hamiltonian
H_{0}
plus some more regular operator.
Specifically, for the Dirac
\delta
function, we use the following identity

\displaystyle 4\pi Z\,\big[\delta^{3}(r_{1})+\delta^{3}(r_{2})\big]=

\displaystyle\ \{H_{0}-E_{0},Q\}+V_{R}\,,

(42)

\displaystyle Q=

\displaystyle\ 2\bigg(\frac{Z}{r_{1}}+\frac{Z}{r_{2}}\bigg)\,,

(43)

where
V_{R}
is the regularized operator
defined by its action on an eigenfunction
\phi
of Hamiltonian
H_{0}
on the right as

\displaystyle V_{R}|\phi\rangle=-2\bigg(\frac{Z\vec{r}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}\cdot\vec{\nabla}_{1}+\frac{Z\vec{r}_{2}}{r_{2}^{3}}\cdot\vec{\nabla}_{2}\bigg)|\phi\rangle\,.

(44)

For the spin-independent part of the Breit Hamiltonian we use a similar identity,

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{\mathrm{nfs}}=

\displaystyle\ \{H_{0}-E_{0},\tilde{Q}\}+H_{R}\,,

(45)

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\frac{Z}{r_{1}}+\frac{Z}{r_{2}}\bigg)\,,

(46)

where
H_{R}
is defined by its action on the eigenfunction of
H_{0}
on the right as

\displaystyle\ H_{R}|\phi\rangle=\bigg[\frac{1}{4}p_{1}^{2}p_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(E_{0}-V)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\,p_{1}^{i}\bigg(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{3}}\bigg)\,p_{2}^{j}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{Z}{4}\frac{\vec{r}_{1}\cdot\vec{\nabla}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}-\frac{Z}{4}\frac{\vec{r}_{2}\cdot\vec{\nabla}_{2}}{r_{2}^{3}}+\frac{\vec{r}}{2r^{3}}\cdot(\vec{\nabla}_{1}-\vec{\nabla}_{2})\bigg]|\phi\rangle\,,

(47)

where
V=-Z/r_{1}-Z/r_{2}+1/r
.
By applying these identities as described in Appendix
, we express the second-order
contributions in Eq. (
IV
) as

\displaystyle 2\,\bigg\langle H^{(4)}_{\rm hfs}

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}\,H^{(5)}\bigg\rangle+2\,\bigg\langle H^{(5)}_{\rm hfs}\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}\,H^{(4)}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=E_{\rm sec}(\mathrm{se})+E_{\rm sec}(\mathrm{vp})+E_{{\rm fo},A}\,,

(48)

where
E_{\rm sec}(\mathrm{se})
and
E_{\rm sec}(\mathrm{vp})
are the finite second-order contributions
given by Eqs. (
E
) and (
98
)
that correspond to the self-energy and vacuum polarization, respectively, and

E_{{\rm fo},A}
is an additional first-order contribution.
As previously, in our derivation we dropped terms proportional to the electron-nucleus Dirac
\delta
function
in the first-order matrix elements, which will be restored later.

A similar regularization of the Fermi contact interaction was carried out in the calculation of the
low-energy part
E_{L}
, for the integrand
P_{L}(k)
in Eq. (
30
). The integrand
P_{L}(k)
does not
contain any divergences, so the regularization is not obligatory but it greatly improves the
convergence of numerical basis-set calculations. For the first term in the right-hand side of Eq. (
30
) we
used the representation (
42
), whereas for the second term we employed a more general identity

\displaystyle 4\pi Z\,\big[\delta^{3}(r_{1})+\delta^{3}(r_{2})\big]=\big\{H_{0}-E_{0},Q\big\}+\widetilde{V}_{R}\,,

(49)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{R}

\displaystyle\,=4\,(E_{0}-V)\bigg(\frac{Z}{r_{1}}+\frac{Z}{r_{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\ -2\,\vec{p}_{1}\bigg(\frac{Z}{r_{1}}+\frac{Z}{r_{2}}\bigg)\,\vec{p}_{1}-2\,\vec{p}_{2}\bigg(\frac{Z}{r_{1}}+\frac{Z}{r_{2}}\bigg)\,\vec{p}_{2}\,,

(50)

and
Q
is defined in Eq. (
43
). It might be noted that
the dependence on
E_{0}
in the above equations cancels out, so that they represent
a general operator identity.

Now we turn to restoring the contribution proportional to the electron-nucleus
Dirac
\delta
function. This is accomplished by evaluating the large-
Z
limit of
the derived
m\alpha^{7}
contributions. In the
Z
\to
\infty
limit, all effects
of the electron-electron interaction vanish (since they are suppressed by a factor of
1/Z

as compared to the electron-nucleus interaction) and the result should agree
with the
m\alpha^{7}
correction derived for the hydrogenlike ions. This
matching gives us the coefficient at the electron-nucleus
Dirac
\delta
function. The evaluation of the large-
Z
limit of our formulas and the
matching with the hydrogenic results is described
in Appendix
. As a result, we obtain an additional first-order
contribution proportional to the electron-nucleus
Dirac
\delta
function,

\displaystyle E_{{\rm fo},B}=\eta\,\langle\vec{I}\cdot\vec{S}\rangle\,Z^{3}\pi\langle\big[\delta^{3}(r_{1})+\delta^{3}(r_{2})\big]\rangle\,,

(51)

with the coefficient
\eta
given by

\displaystyle\eta=

\displaystyle\ \frac{g}{4\,\pi\,M}\bigg[-\frac{5351}{1350}-\frac{44\pi^{2}}{27}-\frac{10}{3}\zeta(3)+\frac{896}{27}\ln 2

\displaystyle\ +\frac{16}{9}\ln^{2}2-\frac{4882}{135}\ln\alpha-\frac{64}{9}\ln^{2}\alpha+\frac{256}{9}\ln 2\ln\alpha\bigg]\,,

(52)

where we dropped the
\lambda
-dependent terms.

Finally we obtain the total first-order contribution as

\displaystyle\langle H^{(7)}_{{\rm hfs},A}\rangle+\langle H^{(7)}_{{\rm hfs},B}\rangle+E_{{\rm fo},A}+E_{{\rm fo},B}=E_{{\rm fo}}(\mathrm{se})+E_{{\rm fo}}(\mathrm{vp})\,,

(53)

where
E_{{\rm fo}}(\mathrm{se})
and
E_{{\rm fo}}(\mathrm{vp})
are given by Eqs. (

) and (

So far the individual
m\alpha^{7}
contributions depend on the
logarithm of the low-energy cutoff of photon momenta,
\ln\lambda

complete
m\alpha^{7}
correction should not depend on
\lambda

of the
\lambda
-dependent terms is demonstrated in Appendix
; this
constituted an important cross-check of the derivation. After the cancellation is
proven, we set
\lambda\to 1
in all formulas.

VI
Conclusion

We have performed calculations of the QED effects of order
m\alpha^{7}(=\alpha^{3}E_{F})
to
the magnetic dipole hyperfine structure in Li
+
. This calculation greatly improves the
theoretical value of hfs in Li
+
in the point-nucleus limit. By comparing the theoretical
point-nucleus result with the experimental Li
+
hfs value, we determine the nuclear-structure
contribution and parametrize it in terms of the effective Zemach radius.
We confirm the surprising result,
pointed out in Ref.
puchalski:13
, that the effective Zemach radius of
6
Li
is smaller than that of
7
Li, which is in contrast
with the charge radius of
6
Li being
larger than that of
7
Li. The probable explanation of this fact is large contributions of
inelastic effects, for which no calculations exist up to now.

It is demonstrated that the nuclear-structure contribution, when normalized by the
Fermi energy
E_{F}
, is nearly the same numerically in Li
2+
, Li
+
, and atomic Li.
The charge-state dependent contributions to
\delta_{\rm struct}
are of order

O(Z\,\alpha)^{2}
and very small numerically.
Using this statement, we obtain accurate predictions for the hfs in
6
Li
2+

and
7
Li
2+
, for which no experimental data is available so far.
Examination of the normalized differences of the hfs values of Li
+
and Li and
of the corresponding isotope-shift differences allowed us to demonstrate the consistency
of four different measurements of hfs in
6,7
Li
+
and
6,7
Li

sun:23
;
guan:20
;
beckmann:74
.
By contrast, the recent measurement of
6
Li hfs
li:20
leads to a 2
\sigma

tension in the consistency test.

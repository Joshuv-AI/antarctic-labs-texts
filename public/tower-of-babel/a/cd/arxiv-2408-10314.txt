Entanglement entropy in lattice models with quantum metric
Alexander Kruchkov, Shinsei Ryu

Abstract
We revisit the connection between entanglement entropy and quantum metric in topological lattice systems, and provide an elegant and concise proof of this connection. In gapped two-dimensional lattice models with well-defined tight-binding Hamiltonians, we show that the entanglement entropy is intimately related to the quantum metric of electronic states.



Introduction.

The concept of entanglement entropy is key in quantum information science for addressing the utility of qubit pairs, whether maximally or partially entangled
[
1
]
. Since it is feasible to convert pairs of partially entangled quantum states into fewer maximally-entangled states, the efficacy of partially entangled qubits compared to their maximally entangled counterparts is defined by their entanglement entropy
[
2
,
3
]
.

Ground states in quantum phases of matter are defined by entanglement, setting them apart from conventional phases that are characterized by order parameters and their symmetries. These quantum states necessitate a description rooted in quantum correlations, such as topological or quantum order [4,5]. The degree of quantumness of the ground state
|\Psi\rangle
is quantified by its entanglement entropy (von Neumann entropy),

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A})=-\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}\,\rho_{\mathcal{A}}\ln\rho_{\mathcal{A}},\ \ \ \rho_{\mathcal{A}}=\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{B}}|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|.

(1)

Here the system is bipartitioned into two subsystems
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, with
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
denoting the reduced density matrix for subsystem
\mathcal{A}
, derived by tracing out the subsystem
\mathcal{B}
from the total density matrix
\rho=|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|
. The entanglement entropy
S({\mathcal{A}})
is zero for classical product states whereas it takes a non-trivial value for generic quantum-entangled systems, see e.g Refs
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
.

Previous work

[
11
]
has established hidden relations between topological properties of a system, quantum metric in 1D, and bounds on entanglement entropy. In particular, for one-dimensional systems
Ref.
[
11
]

provides the exact relationship between the entanglement entropy
\mathcal{S}^{\rm(1D)}
the Berry phase
\gamma
in the case of the flat-band limit,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\rm(1D)}=-\frac{\gamma}{\pi}\ln\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}-\frac{2\pi-\gamma}{\pi}\ln\frac{2\pi-\gamma}{2\pi}.

(2)

Moreover, for the ground states with chiral symmetry, entanglement entropy has lower bound
S^{\rm(1D)}\geq\ln 2
(per boundary), i.e., the value of entanglement entropy for a maximally-entangled qubit pair
[
11
]
. Such relationships in one-dimensional systems substantiate the existence of definitive bounds on entanglement entropy. However, extending this reasoning to higher dimensions, including recently discovered two-dimensional topological phases, remains an open research question.

On the other hand, quantum metric is a fundamental concept that governs distance between quantum states in the projective Hilbert space
[
12
]
. If Bloch basis
|u_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle
is well defined, the solid-state quantum-geometric tensor is defined as
[
13
]

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})=\langle\partial_{i}u_{n\mathbf{k}}|(1-\mathcal{P}_{n\mathbf{k}})|\partial_{j}u_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle.

(3)

The projector
\mathcal{P}_{n\mathbf{k}}\equiv|u_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle\langle u_{n\mathbf{k}}|
ensures gauge-invariance of observables
[
13
]
.
The real part of
\mathcal{G}_{ij}=\text{Re}\mathfrak{G}_{ij}
is the Fubini-Study metric describing the geometry of the electronic bands, while the imaginary part
\mathcal{F}_{ij}=-2\text{Im}\mathfrak{G}_{ij}
is the Berry curvature reflecting the topology of quantum states.
Although used in earlier applications of quantum information, particularly through the Fischer information metric related to Fubini-Study metric
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
, the connection of the quantum metric to the standing question of entanglement entropy bounds remains obscure
[
17
]
.

Hence it is intriguing to seek for
connection between two a priori unrelated constructions
—entanglement and quantum metric—which is the main result of this Letter. We find that for the
lattice models with well-defined tight-binding
such connection does indeed exist, and, depending on partition, can be expressed through quantum metric invariants.

Methodology.
We here make use of the formalism introduced by Jin and Korepin
[
18
]
, and further developped in the works of Calabrese, Mintchev, and Vicari
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
; refer also to Paul
[
17
]
. Within this formalism, the system is bipartioned into entanglement area
{\mathcal{A}}
and the rest. The total entanglement entropy can be calculated as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}({\mathcal{A}})=\sum_{k}s_{\alpha}(\lambda_{k})

(4)

where Renyi entropies are given by

\displaystyle s_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\ln[\lambda^{\alpha}+(1-\lambda)^{\alpha}],

(5)

following limit
\alpha\to 1
to obtain von Neumann entropy. Here
\lambda_{k}
are eigenvalues of the
overlap matrix

[
7
,
19
]
. We here consider a two-orbital band insulator at half-filling.
For clarity, we consider a 2D material stripe, partitioned by a straight cut at
L_{\mathcal{A}}
, with momentum along the partition boundary denoted by
k
. The overlap matrix is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{k_{1}k_{2}}=\int\limits_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{A}}d\mathbf{x}\,\psi^{*}_{k_{1}}(\mathbf{x})\psi_{k_{2}}(\mathbf{x}).

(6)

The overlap matrix
\mathcal{W}_{k_{1}k_{2}}
encodes information on the overlap of Wannier orbitals, i.e. entanglement in the system. On the other hand, overlap between electronic orbitals can be characterized by the quantum metric
[
22
]
.
Therefore, we need to investigate the spectrum of the overlap matrix and compare it with Marzari-Vanderbilt functional. We show below that these two quantities are related.

Upper bounds on overlap matrix elements show asymptotical decay of
1/q
, with a prefactor which is significantly suprressed for the mesoscopic samples. For sample
1000a
x
1000a
(here
a
is lattice constant), the decay in matrix elements is abrupt and focused around
q\approx 0
. Larger entanglement regions provide significantly supressed values for
q\neq 0
terms.

We examine two-dimensional topological lattice models with well-defined tight-binding description. The tight-binding description and the underlying lattice are conceptually important for our derivation.
In what follows below, we consider solid-state materials with Bloch basis well-defined,

\psi_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{x})=e^{i\mathbf{k}\mathbf{x}}u_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{x}).

In tight-binding formalism, the overlap matrix is

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{k_{1}k_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in A}\sum_{j}e^{i(k_{2}-k_{1})x}u^{*}_{k_{1}}(j)u_{k_{2}}(j)

(7)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in A}e^{i(k_{2}-k_{1})x}\langle u_{k_{1}}|u_{k_{2}}\rangle.

(8)

Introducing now
\chi_{q}=\sum_{x\in A}e^{iqx}
, the overlap matrix reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{k_{1},k_{2}}=\chi_{k_{2}-k_{1}}\langle u_{k_{1}}|u_{k_{2}}\rangle.

(9)

For notational simplicity, we discretize momenta in the square-lattice Brillouin zone. Extending this approach to other lattice symmetries is straightforward.

Derivations for entanglement entropy
. We start with investigating asymptotical properties of off-diagonal elements of the overlap matrix (
9
).
The structure of matrix elements (
9
) is such that it rapidly decays in mesoscopic samples, oscillating between two strict bounds, (Fig. 1),

\displaystyle 0\leq\left|\frac{\mathcal{W}_{k,k+q}}{W_{k,k}}\right|\leq\frac{\eta(\mathcal{A})}{|q|},\ \ \ \eta(\mathcal{A})\ll 1.

(10)

hence the main contribution comes from small
q\approx 0
.
Note that the upper bound asymptote
a(\mathcal{A})/q
depends on the entanglement area
\mathcal{A}
, and is small for physically relevant entanglement regions of mesoscopic sizes (see Fig. 1), In fact,
\eta(\mathcal{A})
decays as approximately
1/L_{\mathcal{A}}
, inversely to the size of entanglement region, reaching values for around
\eta\sim 10^{-3}
for physically-relevant sizes
L_{\mathcal{A}}\sim 10^{3}
(lattice size
a=1
) and
\eta\sim 10^{-6}
for
L_{\mathcal{A}}\sim 10^{6}
sampling. Hence, the off-diagonal matrix elements decay sufficiently fast in mesoscopic and macroscopic samples, and the overlap matrix can be effectively truncated
[
23
]
. Therefore, this problem can be addressed from the perspective of ”tight-binding in momentum space”
[
13
]
, truncating the ”momentum hopping” to the first nearest neighbors. To treat problem analytically, we first truncate the overlap matrix to tridiagonal form,

\footnotesize\begin{pmatrix}\ddots&\ddots&0&0&0\\
\ddots&\mathcal{W}_{k-q,k-q}&\mathcal{W}_{k-q,k}&0&0\\
0&\mathcal{W}_{k,k-q}&\mathcal{W}_{k,k}&\mathcal{W}_{k,k+q}&0\\
0&0&\mathcal{W}_{k+q,k}&\mathcal{W}_{k+q,k+q}&\ddots\\
0&0&0&\ddots&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}

On the main diagonal we have
\chi_{0}
, the secondary diagonals have properties
\mathcal{W}^{{\dagger}}_{i,i+1}=\mathcal{W}_{i+1,i}
, hence it is a Hermitian matrix with real eigenvalues.

For the reasons which will become clear shortly, we further consider the
squared
overlap matrix
\mathcal{W}^{2}
. While it does not seem to be possible to find its eigenvalues in the closed form, we can write down its diagonal elements. Its
diagonal elements
are given by relatively simple expression

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{W}^{2}_{\text{tri}}\right]_{kk}=\chi_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle+|\chi_{q}|^{2}\,|\langle u_{k}|u_{k+q}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+|\chi_{q}|^{2}\,|\langle u_{k}|u_{k-q}\rangle|^{2},

(11)

which will become handy for evaluating entanglement entropy.

We further consider von Neuman entropy corresponding to the eignevalue of
\mathcal{W}
,

\displaystyle s_{\alpha\to 1}(\lambda)\approx-\lambda\ln\lambda-(1-\lambda)\ln(1-\lambda).

(12)

With a good accuracy, this formula can be approximated as (see Fig. 2 )

\displaystyle s(\lambda)\approx 4\ln(2)\ \lambda(1-\lambda).

(13)

Thereafter, the trace can be approximated as

\displaystyle S\simeq 4\ln 2\left[\text{Tr}\,{(\mathcal{W})}-\text{Tr}\,(\mathcal{W}^{2})\right].

(14)

Substituting now formula (
11
) in (
14
), we obtain

\displaystyle S_{\text{tri}}\simeq\frac{4\ln 2}{N}\sum_{q\neq 0}\chi_{0}-\chi_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle-|\chi_{q}|^{2}|\langle u_{k}|u_{k+q}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle-|\chi_{q}|^{2}|\langle u_{k}|u_{k-q}\rangle|^{2}

(15)

here
N
is the dimension of the matrix, given by discretization scheme
k_{j}=\frac{2\pi}{a}\frac{j}{N}
,
q=\Delta k=\frac{2\pi}{aN}
.
We now use the properties of quantum metric
[
13
]

\displaystyle|\langle u_{k}|u_{k+q}\rangle|^{2}=1-q^{2}\mathcal{G}_{k}+\mathcal{O}(q^{4})

(16)

And thus we obtain entropy as a function of quantum metric,

\displaystyle S_{\text{tri}}\simeq\frac{4\ln 2}{N}\sum_{k\in\text{BZ}}\chi_{0}-\frac{4\ln 2}{N}\sum_{k\in\text{BZ}}(\chi_{0}^{2}+|\chi_{q}|^{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{4\ln 2}{N}\sum_{k\in\text{BZ}}q^{2}|\chi_{q}|^{2}\left[\mathcal{G}_{k}+\mathcal{G}_{k-q}\right]

(17)

Therefore, even in tridiagonal truncation, we observe that the entanglement entropy contains information about the quantum metric,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\text{tri}}\simeq\text{Const}+\frac{8\ln 2}{N}\sum_{k\in\text{BZ}}q^{2}|\chi_{q}|^{2}\mathcal{G}_{k}.

(18)

We will show below that this trend preserves for ”longer-range” momentum-space hopping.

Figure 2:
Approximations used to calculate the entanglement entropy.

Pentadiagonal matrix.
To enhance our understanding, we consider next-nearest-neighbor (NNN) tight-binding hopping in the overlap matrix, resulting in a pentadiagonal matrix,—a matrix with five nonzero diagonals. Similar arguments apply, beginning with the matrix elements:

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{W}^{2}_{\text{penta}}\right]_{kk}=\chi_{0}^{2}+|\chi_{q}|^{2}|\langle u_{k}|u_{k+q}\rangle|^{2}+|\chi_{q}|^{2}|\langle u_{k}|u_{k-q}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+|\chi_{q}|^{2}|\langle u_{k+q}|u_{k+2q}\rangle|^{2}+|\chi_{q}|^{2}|\langle u_{k-q}|u_{k-2q}\rangle|^{2}

(19)

Using Eqs. (14) and (16), the entanglement entropy is then given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\text{penta}}\simeq\text{Const}+\frac{4\ln 2}{N}\sum_{k\in\text{BZ}}

\displaystyle q^{2}|\chi_{q}|^{2}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{G}_{k+q}+\mathcal{G}_{k}+\mathcal{G}_{k-q}+\mathcal{G}_{k-2q}\right].

(20)

We see that in this case as well, the entanglement entropy depends on quantum metric.

Septadiagonal matrix.
—Hopping further, we consider next-next-nearest-neighbor (NNNN) tight-binding hopping in the overlap matrix, resulting in a
septadiagonal matrix
,—a matrix with seven nonzero diagonals. The same principles apply, starting with the matrix elements:

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{W}^{2}_{\text{septa}}\right]_{kk}=

\displaystyle\chi_{0}^{2}+|\chi_{q}|^{2}|\langle u_{k}|u_{k+q}\rangle|^{2}+|\chi_{q}|^{2}|\langle u_{k}|u_{k-q}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle|\chi_{q}|^{2}|\langle u_{k+q}|u_{k+2q}\rangle|^{2}+|\chi_{q}|^{2}|\langle u_{k-q}|u_{k-2q}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle|\chi_{q}|^{2}|\langle u_{k+2q}|u_{k+3q}\rangle|^{2}+|\chi_{q}|^{2}|\langle u_{k-2q}|u_{k-3q}\rangle|^{2}.

(21)

Using Eqs. (14) and (16), the entanglement entropy is then given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\text{penta}}

\displaystyle\simeq\text{Const}+\frac{4\ln 2}{N}\sum_{k\in\text{BZ}}q^{2}|\chi_{q}|^{2}

\displaystyle\times\left[\mathcal{G}_{k+2q}+\mathcal{G}_{k+q}+\mathcal{G}_{k}+\mathcal{G}_{k-q}+\mathcal{G}_{k-2q}+\mathcal{G}_{k-3q}\right].

(22)

Below we generalize the quantum metric contribution to arbitrary hopping range.

General case.
—Consider now a general case of tight-binding range
\Lambda
which leads to
(2\Lambda+1)
-diagonal matrix. The diagonal elements of the squared matrix are given by

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{W}^{2}_{{\Lambda}}\right]_{kk}=

\displaystyle\chi_{0}^{2}+\sum_{Q=nq}^{|n|<\Lambda/2}|\chi_{q}|^{2}|\langle u_{k}|u_{k+Q}\rangle|^{2}

(23)

The entanglement entropy is then given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle\simeq\text{Const}+\frac{4\ln 2}{N}\sum_{k\in\text{BZ}}q^{2}|\chi_{q}|^{2}\sum_{Q=nq}^{|n<|\Lambda/2|}\mathcal{G}_{k+Q}

(24)

\displaystyle\simeq\text{Const}+[2\ln(2)\eta^{2}(\mathcal{A})]\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in\text{BZ}}\sum_{Q=nq}^{|n<|\Lambda/2|}\mathcal{G}_{k+Q}

(25)

where we have used that
|\chi_{q}|^{2}|q|^{2}\approx\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}
upon averaging over fast fluctuations (see Eq. 10 and Fig. 1) .

In practice, the quantum metric is highly nonhomogeneous, typically peaking at high symmetry points (see, e.g., Fig. 5 in Ref.
[
24
]
). Therefore, it is sufficient to consider only a finite number of points around these peaks to evaluate Eq. (25). One may, but not obliged to set
\Lambda
to large values, so that the integration covers the entire BZ. In this case one obtains

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\simeq\text{Const}+2\ln(2)\ \eta^{2}(\mathcal{A})\sum_{k}\mathcal{G}_{k}.

(26)

In homogeneous system,
\mathcal{G}_{xx}=\mathcal{G}_{yy}
, so one can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\simeq\text{Const}+2\ln(2)\ \eta^{2}(\mathcal{A})\sum_{k}\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(k).

(27)

This is the direct relation with Marzari-Vanderbilt invariant.

Arbitrary partition region
. The results described above can be in large extended to codimension-one partitioning of arbitrary shape. Indeed, in this case we have a band matrix with finite number of nonzero diagonals
\Lambda
. We use

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{W}^{2}\right]_{ii}=\sum_{j}\mathcal{W}_{ij}\mathcal{W}_{ji}=\sum_{j}|\mathcal{W}_{ij}|^{2},

(28)

where
i,j
label the 2D momentum discretisation, and we have used Hermitian symmetry of
\mathcal{W}
.
Using now
\mathcal{W}_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}=\chi_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{k}}\langle u_{\mathbf{k}}|u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\rangle
, we hence obtain

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{W}^{2}\right]_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}|\chi_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{k}}|^{2}|\langle u_{\mathbf{k}}|u_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\rangle|^{2}.

(29)

Further we take into account that the quantum metric in real materials is peaked around high-symmetry points of the BZ (for example, in twisted bilayer graphene it is the
\Gamma
point
[
24
]
). In this case, it is sufficient to expand (
31
) in the vicinity of the high-symmetry point(s),

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{W}^{2}\right]_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{Q}}|\chi_{\mathbf{Q}}|^{2}|\langle u_{\mathbf{k}}|u_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{Q}}\rangle|^{2}

(30)

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{\mathbf{Q}}|\chi_{\mathbf{Q}}|^{2}(1-\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})Q_{i}Q_{j})

(31)

Thus, the entanglement entropy has quantum metric contribution,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\text{geo}}\simeq 2\ln(2)\ \eta^{2}(\mathcal{A})\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}).

(32)

This formula represents quantum-geometric contribution to entanglement entropy. The trace of
\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})
, known as the Marzari-Vanderbilt invariant
[
22
]
of the quantum metric, is bounded below by the Chern number in the case of a Chern insulator.

Relation to particle number fluctuations
.
Klich pointed out that entanglement entropy must have lower bounds related to particle number fluctuations
[
7
]
. Below, we consider a generalized fluctuation operator
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
(here
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
) of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}=\left\langle\frac{\partial^{\alpha}n(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}n(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}}\right\rangle-\left|\left\langle\frac{\partial^{\alpha}n(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}}\right\rangle\right|^{2}.

(33)

After applying Fourier transform, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\mathbf{q}\neq 0}|\mathbf{q}|^{2\alpha}\langle n_{\mathbf{q}}n_{-\mathbf{q}}\rangle,

(34)

where
n_{\mathbf{q}}
is Fourier-transform of the particle density operator, which in the Bloch basis reads (see, e.g.,
[
25
]
),

\displaystyle n_{\mathbf{q}}=\sum_{n,m}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}|u_{m,\mathbf{k}}\rangle c^{{\dagger}}_{n,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}c_{m,\mathbf{k}}.

(35)

Substituting this into Eq. (
33
), and using Wick’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\mathbf{q}}|\mathbf{q}|^{2\alpha}\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}|u_{m,\mathbf{k}}\rangle\langle u_{m,\mathbf{k}}|u_{n,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}\rangle

\displaystyle\times\langle c^{{\dagger}}_{n,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}c_{n,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}\rangle\langle c_{m,\mathbf{k}}c^{{\dagger}}_{m,\mathbf{k}}\rangle,

(36)

We can further simplify this expression by noting that, for noninteracting fermions at equilibrium,
\langle c^{{\dagger}}_{n\mathbf{k}}c_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle=f_{n\mathbf{k}}
, and
\langle c_{n\mathbf{k}}c^{{\dagger}}_{n\mathbf{k}}\rangle=1-f_{n\mathbf{k}}
. Introducing the projector on the occupied states

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbf{k}}=\sum_{n}f_{n\mathbf{k}}\ |u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\rangle\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}|,

(37)

and the antiprojector,

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbf{k}}=1-\sum_{n}f_{n\mathbf{k}}\ |u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\rangle\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}|,

(38)

Eq. (
36
) now reads as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}|\mathbf{q}|^{2\alpha}\ \text{Tr}\,\left[\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbf{k}}\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}\right].

(39)

In this form, the expression (
39
) resembles the
Marzari-Vanderbilt invariant

\Omega_{I}
, which is the integrated trace of the quantum metric in its discretized form
[
22
]

\displaystyle\Omega_{I}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}w_{\mathbf{q}}\ \text{Tr}\,\left[\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbf{k}}\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}\right]

(40)

where
w_{\mathbf{q}}\propto/q^{2}
and its prefactor depends on lattice symmetry
[
22
]
. Comparing now Eq. (
39
) with (
40
), we conclude that the fluctuations of the excess particle number in the entanglement area
N_{\mathcal{A}}(t)=\int_{\mathcal{A}}d\mathbf{x}\,n(\mathbf{x},t)
are related to quantum metric,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\text{-}1}\propto\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}).

(41)

Following Klich’s argument
[
7
]
, this establishes a bound on entanglement entropy.

Discussion.
We have derived a concise formula relating quantum-geometric contributions to entanglement entropy,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\text{geo}}\simeq 2\ln(2)\ \eta^{2}(\mathcal{A})\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{G}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}).

(42)

The connection between entanglement entropy and the trace of the quantum metric can be intuitively understood through the overlap of electronic orbitals, expressed through invariants of quantum metric
[
22
]
. Greater orbital overlap leads to higher entanglement in Eq. (
42
).
It is also known that entanglement entropy is related to quantum noise in the system
[
26
]
. Recent studies have shown that quantum noise in topological systems is directly linked to quantum metric invariants
[
27
,
28
]
. Thus, the connection between entanglement entropy and quantum metric invariants is well-founded.

In conclusion, our analytical results demonstrate that entanglement entropy in topological lattice models is intrinsically linked to the underlying quantum metric.
Extending this framework to Fractional Chern Insulators could provide valuable insights.

Invariant measures of the topological flow and measures at infinity on hyperbolic groups
Stephen Cantrell, Ryokichi Tanaka

Abstract
. We show that for every non-elementary hyperbolic group, an associated topological flow space admits a coding based on a transitive subshift of finite type. Applications include regularity results for Manhattan curves, the uniqueness of measures of maximal Hausdorff dimension with potentials, and the real analyticity of intersection numbers for families of dominated representation, thus providing a direct proof of a result established by Bridgeman, Canary, Labourie and Sambarino in 2015.



1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to show that there exists a coding based on a transitive subshift of finite type for a flow associated with every non-elementary hyperbolic group.
Applications include the real analyticity of Manhattan curves (introduced by Burger in
[
Bur93
]
and generalized in
[
CT21
]
),
the uniqueness of measure of maximal Hausdorff dimension with potentials,
and the real analyticity of intersection numbers for perturbations of dominated representations, providing a direct proof of a result shown by Bridgeman
et al.
in
[
BCLS15
]
.
We also apply our results to give a multifractal analysis of harmonic measures for finite range random walks and discuss, for explicit examples, the relationships between various measure classes on the boundary of the group.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group and
\partial\Gamma
be the ideal boundary equipped with a quasi-metric.
We consider the boundary square
\partial^{2}\Gamma:=(\partial\Gamma)^{2}\setminus\{\text{diagonal}\}
endowed with the diagonal action by
\Gamma
.
Given a Hölder continuous cocycle
\kappa:\Gamma\times\partial^{2}\Gamma\to{\mathbb{R}}
coming from the Busemann function for a strongly hyperbolic metric,
we define a compact space
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}:=\Gamma\backslash(\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}})
equipped with an
{\mathbb{R}}
-action which we call a
topological flow
(see Section
3
for the precise definitions).
The space
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
is the one constructed by Mineyev
[
Min05
]
.
Our main coding theorem reads the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

For every non-elementary hyperbolic group
\Gamma
,
let
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
be the topological flow space associated with a Hölder continuous cocycle
\kappa
defined by a strongly hyperbolic metric. There exists a topologically transitive subshift of finite type
(\Sigma_{0},\sigma)
with a positive Hölder continuous function
r_{0}

such that a continuous map from the associated suspension flow based on
(\Sigma_{0},\sigma,r_{0})
to
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}

is surjective, equivariant with the flows and bounded-to-one.

The proof is given in Section
4.4
(see Theorem
4.9
).
A main contribution of this work is that, in the above theorem, the coding of our flow is based over a shift space
(\Sigma_{0},\sigma)
which is topologically transitive and we can find such a coding for every non-elementary hyperbolic group.
We call it an
enhanced coding
.
This enables us to apply thermodynamic formalism to the flow in a strength that has been missing so far, and to establish various regularity results concerning measures on the boundary.

A flow associated with hyperbolic groups was introduced by Gromov
[
Gro87
]
.
Since then, there have been a measure theoretical construction due to Furman (
[
Fur02
]
,
[
BF17
]
) and a topological construction due to Mineyev
[
Min05
]
.
The latter machinery is a key tool in the theory of Anosov representations; see e.g.,
[
BCLS15
]
, a survey
[
BCS18
]
and references therein.
Although there are known codings on such flows e.g., via horofunctions (Busemann functions)
[
CP02
]
, or via Cannon’s automatic structures
[
Bou93
]
and
[
Tan21
]
,
they are constructed over subshifts of finite type which may not be transitive (except for special cases such as Fuchsian groups associated with specific sets of generators).
For the case of Fuchsian groups, see the classical results by Bowen and Series
[
BS79
]
.
Recently, Constantine, Lafont and Thompson have established Bowen-Series type coding for flows on locally
\mathrm{CAT}(-1)
metric spaces
[
CLT20
]
.
Their construction is based on showing that the flows admit a metric Anosov structure in the sense of Pollicott
[
Pol87
]
.
Note that it is not known as to whether the Mineyev flow carries such a structure (see the discussion in
[
BCS18
, Introduction]
).
Their result also applies to the flow associated with a projective Anosov representation
[
CLT20
, Theorem C]
and thus provides a self-contained proof on the existence of a coding in the setting of
[
BCLS15
]
.
We note, however, that there exists a non-elementary hyperbolic group for which any representation to a general linear group (over any field) factors through a finite group
[
Kap05
, Theorem 8.1]
as discussed in
[
BPS19
, Remark 3.6]
.
Our result in Theorem
1.1
applies to such hyperbolic groups without imposing any additional assumptions.

We construct a coding of the flow based on Cannon’s automatic structure, which we know every hyperbolic group admits (for the proof, see e.g.,
[
Cal13
, Theorem 3.2.2]
).
The underlying finite directed graph may have several distinct components
and consequently known codings (e.g., the one used in
[
Tan21
]
)
are not necessarily based over a transitive subshifts of finite type.
Despite this issue it is still possible to use such a coding to prove some results.
Indeed, an approach due to Calegari and Fujiwara
[
CF10
]
is to analyze each maximal (in the sense of spectrum) component in Cannon’s automatic structure and to show that certain values of interest agree across components.
We show, by employing ergodic theory on the flow space, that
any one of the maximal components covers the flow space; see more precise discussions in Section
4.4
. (In a special setting where the group acts on a
\mathrm{CAT}(-1)
-space, an analogous argument has been carried out by Bourdon
[
Bou93
, Lemme 5.1.2]
.)
This part involves studying invariant measures under the flow whereas the conclusion is a purely topological one.
We note that there can exist more than one maximal component and the set of such components depend on the choice of the roof function (or, the cocycle).
Therefore, at first sight our coding is not fully constructive;
however, in fact, one may take
any
component whose adjacency matrix has the maximal modulus
e^{v_{S}}
, where
v_{S}
is the exponential growth rate for a chosen finite symmetric set of generators in the automatic structure (see Proposition
4.8
).
That component again may not be unique and depend on the choice of a set of generators and the automaton, but one can find and use it for computation whenever any reasonably small group presentation is available, e.g.,
[
CT21
, Section 5]
.
We discuss some concrete examples where one has access to explicit coding structures in Section
8
.

In the course of our discussion, we develop a general theory on invariant Radon measures on the boundary square without any reference to the topological flow (see Section
2.3
).
The approach was initiated by Kaimanovich
[
Kai90
]
in the setting of negatively curved manifold.
In the same paper, it was suggested to carry out the argument entirely on the group itself (ibid, Remark 2 in Section 2.1).
Our framework unifies known approaches in the theory of (generalized) geodesic flows, random walks and dominated (or, projective Anosov) representations for hyperbolic groups.
Let us briefly describe the case when we have a dominated representation
\rho:\Gamma\to{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
for an integer
m\geq 2
(see Section
7
for the precise definitions in the following discussion).
Let
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
be the set of hyperbolic metrics which are left-invariant, quasi-isometric to some (equivalently, any) word metric in
\Gamma
. (For more on the structure of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
, see recent results in
[
OR23
]
.)
If we define

\psi_{\rho}(x,y):=\log\|\rho(x^{-1}y)\|\quad\text{for $x,y\in\Gamma$},

where
\|\cdot\|
stands for a fixed Euclidean norm in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
then
\psi_{\rho}
defines a
\Gamma
-invariant tempered potential relative to a metric
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
(Definition
2.2
); informally speaking, it behaves as a rough geodesic metric in
\Gamma
in the same quasi-isometric class although it is not necessarily symmetric in
x
and
y
.
Given any ergodic
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure
\Lambda
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
,
the
local intersection number
of
\psi_{\rho}
relative to
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}

is defined by the following constant

\tau(\rho/d:\Lambda):=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\psi_{\rho}(\gamma(0),\gamma(t))}{d(\gamma(0),\gamma(t))},

for
\Lambda
-almost every
(\xi_{-},\xi_{+})\in\partial^{2}\Gamma
,
where
\gamma
is any quasi-geodesic converging to
\xi_{+}
and the limit exists independently of the choice of
\gamma
.

Considering the contragredient representation
\check{\rho}:\Gamma\to{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
,
we define
\psi_{\check{\rho}}(x,y)
for
\check{\rho}
similarly to the case of
\rho
,
and a pair of cocycle
c_{\rho}(x,\xi)
and
c_{\check{\rho}}(x,\xi)
analogous to Busemann cocycles in the case of hyperbolic metrics.
The pair
admits a Gromov product
[\xi|\eta]_{\rho}
for
(\xi,\eta)\in\partial^{2}\Gamma
in terms of an associated pair of equivariant representations with
\rho
on the boundary.
In the case of dominated representations,

[\xi|\eta]_{\rho}
defines a locally Hölder continuous function on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
.
If we consider the series

\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\exp(-s\log\|\rho(x)\|)\quad\text{for $s\in{\mathbb{R}}$},

then the divergence exponent
v_{\rho}
in
s
is positive and finite and
we call
v_{\rho}
the
exponential growth rate
for
\rho
.
By definition,
v_{\check{\rho}}=v_{\rho}
, and
associated with the pair of cocycles,
finite measures
\mu_{\rho}
and
\mu_{\check{\rho}}
on
\partial\Gamma
are defined by the Patterson-Sullivan procedure.
Combining with the Gromov product for the pair of cocycles,
we obtain a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure on
\partial^{2}\Gamma

given by

\Lambda_{\rho}=\exp(v_{\rho}[\xi|\eta]_{\rho})\mu_{\check{\rho}}\otimes\mu_{\rho}.

The measure
\Lambda_{\rho}
is ergodic with respect to the
\Gamma
-action on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
by a generalized Hopf argument (see Theorem
3.1
).
Furthermore,
\Lambda_{\rho}
is an analogue of a Bowen-Margulis measure in the case of hyperbolic metrics and we have the following uniqueness result with respect to the maximality of Hausdorff dimension.
For the flip-involution
\iota(\xi,\eta):=(\eta,\xi)
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
, let
\check{\Lambda}:=\Lambda\circ\iota
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d
be a hyperbolic metric in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
, and
\rho:\Gamma\to{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
be a dominated representation for
m\geq 2
. For any
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure
\Lambda
which is ergodic on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
,
if

\dim_{H}(\Lambda,q_{\times})\geq v_{\rho}(\tau(\check{\rho}/d:\check{\Lambda})+\tau(\rho/d:\Lambda)),

then
\Lambda
is a constant multiple of
\Lambda_{\rho}
, where
q_{\times}
is defined in
\partial^{2}\Gamma
by the maximum of quasi-metric
q(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=e^{-(\xi_{1}|\xi_{2})}
associated with
d
.

We prove the above result in a more general form in Theorem
5.7
.
For example, if
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of closed Riemann surface of genus
2
with the translation generators
S
, and further the representation
\rho
induces isometries admitting a regular octagon as a Dirichlet domain on the hyperbolic plane,
then
v_{\rho}=2
and a numerical experiment indicates that

\tau(\rho/d_{S}:\Lambda_{\rho})=1.13837...,

where
d_{S}
is the word metric relative to
S
(see Section
8.2
).

The organization of this paper is the following:
In Section
2
, we provide preliminaries on hyperbolic groups, the Patterson-Sullivan construction with potentials and invariant Radon measures on the boundary square. In Section
3
, we construct the topological flow space due to Mineyev
[
Min05
]
based on
[
Tan21
]
, whose results are generalized for our purposes.
In Section
4
, we introduce symbolic dynamics including thermodynamic formalism, and prove Theorem
1.1
in Theorem
4.9
.
In Section
5
, we provide some applications of Theorem
1.1
to the real analyticity of Manhattan curves for pairs of strongly hyperbolic metrics (Theorem
5.4
) and word metrics (Theorem
5.5
) improving upon results in
[
CT21
, Theorems 1.3 and 4.16]
and the uniqueness of measure of maximal Hausdorff dimension with potentials (Theorems
5.6
and
5.7
) generalizing
[
Tan21
, Theorem 1.1]
.
In Section
6
, we discuss finite range random walks on hyperbolic groups and their harmonic measures, showing that a multifractal profile function is real analytic (Theorem
6.1
and Remark
6.2
).
In Section
7
, we discuss dominated representations in the sense of Bochi, Potrie and Sambarino
[
BPS19
]
, prove Theorem
1.2
as a special case of Theorem
5.7
, define the intersection number for a pair of dominated representations via a large deviation result (Theorem
7.13
) without appealing to Bowen’s equidistribution theorem in the setting of metric Anosov flows, which may be of independent interest, and give a direct proof of the real analyticity under variations parameterized by real analytic manifolds.
In Section
8
, we discuss a free product group and a surface group with the translation generators and compare various finite measures defined on the boundary such as Patterson-Sullivan measures associated with different hyperbolic metrics and a harmonic measure, indicating a possible numerical approach to understand their measure classes based on the local intersection numbers.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Hyperbolic groups

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space.
The Gromov product is defined by

(x|y)_{w}:=\frac{1}{2}(d(w,x)+d(w,y)-d(x,y))\quad\text{for $x,y,w\in X$}.

For a non-negative real number
\delta\geq 0
, we say that a metric space
(X,d)
is
\delta
-
hyperbolic
if

(1)

(x|y)_{w}\geq\min\left\{(x|z)_{w},(y|z)_{w}\right\}-\delta\quad\text{for all $x,y,z,w\in X$}.

A metric space is called
hyperbolic
if it is
\delta
-hyperbolic for some
\delta\geq 0
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group, i.e., there exists a finite set of generators
S
such that each group element can be represented as a word with letters in
S
.
We assume that
S
is
symmetric
, i.e.,
s^{-1}\in S
whenever
s\in S
, enlarging a set of generators if necessary.
For any finite symmetric set of generators
S
, we define the word norm

|x|_{S}:=\min\{k\geq 0\ :\ x=s_{1}\dots s_{k}\ \text{for $s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\in S$}\}\quad\text{for $x\in\Gamma$},

where
|o|_{S}=0
(for the identity element
o:={\rm id}
), and a word metric
d_{S}(x,y):=|x^{-1}y|_{S}
for
x,y\in\Gamma
.
Note that
d_{S}
is invariant under left-translation.
We say that
\Gamma
is a hyperbolic group if
(\Gamma,d_{S})
is a hyperbolic metric space for some (equivalently, any) finite symmetric set of generators
S
.
A hyperbolic group is called
non-elementary
if it is non-amenable, and
elementary
otherwise.
Elementary hyperbolic groups are either finite groups or contain an infinite cyclic group as a finite index subgroup.
Basic examples of non-elementary hyperbolic groups include finite rank free groups of rank at least
2
,
cocompact lattices of
{\rm PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
,
and
the fundamental groups of closed Riemannian manifolds with negative sectional curvature.

We say that two metrics
d,d_{\ast}
in
\Gamma
are
quasi-isometric

if there exist constants
L>0
and
C\geq 0
such that

L^{-1}d(x,y)-C\leq d_{\ast}(x,y)\leq Ld(x,y)+C\quad\text{for all $x,y\in\Gamma$}.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
be the set of metrics which are left-invariant, hyperbolic and quasi-isometric to some (equivalently, any) word metrics in
\Gamma
.

For example, if
\Gamma
is a cocompact lattice of
{\rm PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
and torsion-free,
then a natural isometric action of
\Gamma
on the hyperbolic plane
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
yields a metric in
\Gamma
by restricting the Poincaré metric
d_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}
on an orbit
\Gamma w
for some fixed base point
w
in
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
.
If
\Gamma
is not torsion-free and the stabilizer at
w
is non-trivial,
then defining
d_{w}(x,y):=d_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(xw,yw)+1
if
x\neq y
and
d_{w}(x,y):=0
if
x=y
,
we obtain a metric
d_{w}
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
.

For a metric
d
in
\Gamma
,
we say that a map
\gamma:I\to\Gamma
for an interval
I
in
{\mathbb{R}}
(where
I
is possibly bounded, semi-infinite or bi-infinite) is
an
(L,C)
-quasi-geodesic
for constants
L>0
and
C\geq 0
if

L^{-1}|s-t|-C\leq d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq L|s-t|+C\quad\text{for all $s,t\in I$},

and a
C
-rough geodesic
for a constant
C\geq 0
if

|s-t|-C\leq d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq|s-t|+C\quad\text{for all $s,t\in I$}.

We say that
\gamma
is a
geodesic
if it is a
0
-rough geodesic.
Furthermore a metric space
(\Gamma,d)
is called
C
-
roughly geodesic

if for all
x,y\in\Gamma
there exists a
C
-rough geodesic
\gamma:[0,T]\to\Gamma
such that
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(T)=y
,
and
roughly geodesic
if it is
C
-roughly geodesic for some
C\geq 0
.
A geodesic metric space is a
0
-roughly geodesic metric space.
Every metric in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
is roughly geodesic;
this follows from a “rough reparameterization” of quasi-geodesics in a hyperbolic metric space—–but note that it is not necessarily geodesic.
Moreover if
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
is
C_{0}
-roughly geodesic,
then the
Morse lemma
holds:
for every
(L,C)
-quasi-geodesic
\gamma
in
(\Gamma,d)

there exists a
C_{0}
-rough geodesic
\gamma_{0}
such that
\gamma
and
\gamma_{0}
are within Hausdorff distance
C(L,C,C_{0},\delta)
,
where
\delta
is a hyperbolic constant of the metric
d

[
BS00
, Proposition 5.6]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A hyperbolic metric
d
in
\Gamma
is called
strongly hyperbolic
if it satisfies the following:
There exist positive constants
c,R_{0}>0
such that for all
R\geq R_{0}
, and all
x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}\in\Gamma
,
if

d(x,y)-d(x,x^{\prime})+d(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-d(y,y^{\prime})\geq R,

then

(2)

|d(x,y)-d(x^{\prime},y)-d(x,y^{\prime})+d(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|\leq\exp(-cR).

Mineyev
[
Min05
]
has shown that a strongly hyperbolic metric exists in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
for any hyperbolic group, see also
[
NŠ16
]
for another construction based on the Green metric.
Note that strongly hyperbolic metrics which are in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
are roughly geodesic.
The strongly hyperbolic condition means that
if
x,x^{\prime}
are fixed and
y,y^{\prime}
are far away,
then (
2
) gives a fine control on the difference between two Busemann functions (see below for the definition)
depending on two asymptotic directions in which
y
and
y^{\prime}
follow respectively.
We use the notation
\hat{d}
for a strongly hyperbolic metric following Mineyev’s hat metric.

Let us define the ideal boundary
\partial\Gamma
of
\Gamma
.
Fix a hyperbolic metric in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
and define the Gromov product associated with it.
We say that a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

diverges

if
(x_{n}|x_{m})_{w}
diverges as
\min\{n,m\}
tends to infinity for some (equivalently, any)
w\in\Gamma
.
Let us fix the identity
o={\rm id}
as a base point.
Two divergent sequences
\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
\{y_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
are called
equivalent
if

(x_{n}|y_{m})_{o}
diverges as
\min\{n,m\}
tends to infinity.
For a divergent sequence,
let us denote by
\xi:=[\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}]
the equivalence class, and write
x_{n}\to\xi
as
n\to\infty
.
We define
\partial\Gamma
as the set of equivalence classes of divergent sequences in
\Gamma

and call
\partial\Gamma
the
ideal boundary
of
\Gamma
.
If
d
is in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
and
C
-roughly geodesic,
then for each
\xi
in
\partial\Gamma
there exists a
C
-rough geodesic
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to\Gamma

such that
\gamma(0)=o
and
\gamma(n)\to\xi
as
n\to\infty
.
Furthermore for each pair
\xi,\eta
in
\partial\Gamma
with
\xi\neq\eta

there exists a
C
-rough geodesic
\gamma:(-\infty,\infty)\to\Gamma

such that
\gamma(-n)\to\xi
and
\gamma(n)\to\eta
respectively as
n\to\infty

[
BS00
, Proposition 5.2]
.

We extend the Gromov product to points in
\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma
.
Let

(\xi|\eta)_{o}:=\sup\left\{\liminf_{n\to\infty}(x_{n}|y_{n})_{o}\ :\ \xi=[\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}],\ \eta=[\{y_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}]\right\},

for
\xi,\eta\in\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma
,
where if
\xi
or
\eta
is in
\Gamma
, then we understand that
\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a constant sequence, i.e.,
x_{n}=\xi
for all
n\geq 0
.
If
\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
\{x_{n}^{\prime}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
(resp.
\{y_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
\{y_{n}^{\prime}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
) are divergent and equivalent,
then by (
1
),

\liminf_{n\to\infty}(x_{n}^{\prime}|y_{n}^{\prime})_{o}\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}(x_{n}|y_{n})_{o}-2\delta,

where
\delta
is a hyperbolic constant for the fixed metric.
Therefore it holds that

(\xi|\eta)_{o}\geq\min\left\{(\xi|\zeta)_{o},(\zeta|\eta)_{o}\right\}-3\delta\quad\text{for $\xi,\eta,\zeta\in\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma$}.

The
quasi-metric
in
\partial\Gamma
associated to a metric in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
is defined by

q(\xi,\eta):=\exp\left(-(\xi|\eta)_{o}\right)\quad\text{for $\xi,\eta\in\partial\Gamma$},

where
q(\xi,\xi)=0
.
A quasi-metric satisfies that
q(\xi,\eta)=0
if and only if
\xi=\eta
,
q(\xi,\eta)=q(\eta,\xi)
,
and

q(\xi,\eta)\leq e^{2\delta}\max\left\{q(\xi,\zeta),q(\zeta,\eta)\right\}

for
\xi,\eta,\zeta\in\partial\Gamma
.
We endow
\partial\Gamma
with a topology by declaring that sets
\{\eta\in\partial\Gamma\ :\ q(\xi,\eta)<\varepsilon\}
for
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
and
\varepsilon>0
form an open basis.
It is known that
q^{\varepsilon_{0}}
for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
is bi-Lipschitz to a genuine metric.
The Morse lemma implies that the ideal boundaries defined in terms of metrics from
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}

are mutually homeomorphic.

Letting
d
be a hyperbolic metric in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
and
w
be in
\Gamma
,
we define the Busemann function

\beta_{w}:\Gamma\times(\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma)\to{\mathbb{R}}
based at
w

for
x\in\Gamma
,

\beta_{w}(x,\xi):=\sup\left\{\limsup_{n\to\infty}(d(x,x_{n})-d(w,x_{n}))\ :\ \xi=[\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}]\right\}\quad\text{for $\xi\in\partial\Gamma$},

and

\beta_{w}(x,y)=d(x,y)-d(w,y)=d(w,x)-2(x|y)_{w}

for
y\in\Gamma
.
Note that we have

\beta_{w}(x,\xi)=d(w,x)-2(x|\xi)_{w}+O_{\delta},

and further,

\beta_{w}(xy,\xi)=\beta_{w}(y,x^{-1}\xi)+\beta_{w}(xw,\xi)+O_{\delta},

for
w,x,y\in\Gamma
and
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
.

If we fix a strongly hyperbolic metric
\hat{d}
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
,
then
we denote the corresponding (extended) Gromov product and Busemann functions by

\langle\xi|\eta\rangle_{w}
and
\hat{\beta}_{w}(x,\xi)
respectively. The definition of a strongly hyperbolic metric (Definition
2.1
) implies that
for each
w,x
in
\Gamma
,
the Busemann function
\hat{\beta}_{w}(x,\xi)
based at
w
is obtained as a genuine limit

\hat{\beta}_{w}(x,\xi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\hat{d}(x,x_{n})-\hat{d}(w,x_{n})\right)=\hat{d}(w,x)-2\langle x|\xi\rangle_{w}\quad\text{for $(x,\xi)\in\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma$},

where
\xi=[\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}]
,
and it is continuous with respect to
\xi
in
\partial\Gamma
.
Moreover, we have an identity

\hat{\beta}_{w}(xy,\xi)=\hat{\beta}_{w}(y,x^{-1}\xi)+\hat{\beta}_{w}(xw,\xi)\quad\text{for $w,x,y\in\Gamma$ and $\xi\in\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma$}.

This exact identity (without “quasification”) is used in later sections.

2.2.
Patterson-Sullivan construction with tempered potentials

Let us consider the quasi-metric
q(\xi,\eta)=\exp(-(\xi|\eta)_{o})
in
\partial\Gamma
associated with a metric
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}

and define

B(\xi,r):=\{\eta\in\partial\Gamma\ :\ q(\xi,\eta)<r\}\quad\text{for $\xi\in\partial\Gamma$ and real $r\geq 0$}.

We define the
shadow

{\mathcal{O}}(x,R):=\{\xi\in\partial\Gamma\ :\ (\xi|x)_{o}\geq d(o,x)-R\}\quad\text{for $x\in\Gamma$ and real $R\geq 0$}.

If the metric is
\delta
-hyperbolic for
\delta\geq 0
,
then for each
r\geq 0
and each
R\geq r+3\delta
,
the following holds:
For all
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
and all
x\in\Gamma
such that
(o|\xi)_{x}\leq r
, we have that

(3)

B\left(\xi,e^{-3\delta+R-d(o,x)}\right)\subset{\mathcal{O}}\left(x,R\right)\subset B\left(\xi,e^{3\delta+R-d(o,x)}\right),

(cf.
[
BHM11
, Proposition 2.1]
).
Let
{\mathcal{O}}(x,R)
(resp.
{\mathcal{O}}_{\ast}(x,R)
) be shadows associated with
d
(resp.
d_{\ast}
), where
d
and
d_{\ast}
are in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
.
Since these two metrics are quasi-isometric,
for each
R\geq 0
,

{\mathcal{O}}(x,R)\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{\ast}(x,R^{\prime})\quad\text{for every $x\in\Gamma$},

where
R^{\prime}
is a positive constant depending only on
R
, the quasi-isometric constants and the hyperbolic constants.
This follows from the Morse lemma.
Hence for each fixed
x\in\Gamma
, up to changing
R
, the shadows
{\mathcal{O}}(x,R)
are including each other for any metrics in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
.

For each hyperbolic metric
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
, let

B(x,r):=\{y\in\Gamma\ :\ d(x,y)\leq r\}\quad\text{for $x\in\Gamma$ and real $r\geq 0$}.

The
exponential growth rate
relative to the metric is defined by

v:=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r}\log\#B(o,r),

where
\#A
stands for the cardinality of a set
A
.
If
\Gamma
is a non-elementary hyperbolic group, then
v
is finite and strictly positive.

Recall that for each
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
,
we have a finite Borel regular measure
\mu
called a
Patterson-Sullivan measure
on
\partial\Gamma

satisfying that

\exp(-4\delta v)\leq\exp(v\beta_{o}(x,\xi))\cdot\frac{dx_{\ast}\mu}{d\mu}(\xi)\leq\exp(4\delta v),

for
x\in\Gamma
and for
\mu
-almost every
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
,
where
\delta
is a hyperbolic constant of the metric
(cf.
[
Coo93
, Théorème 5.4]
).
We apply the construction to more general functions.

For a function
\psi:\Gamma\times\Gamma\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
let

(x|y)^{\psi}_{w}:=\frac{1}{2}(\psi(x,w)+\psi(w,y)-\psi(x,y))\quad\text{for $x,y,w\in\Gamma$},

where we
do not
assume that
\psi(x,y)=\psi(y,x)
.
We say that
\psi
satisfies (QE) (“quasi-extension”)
if there exist a function
(\cdot\ |\ \cdot)_{w}^{\psi}:\Gamma\times(\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma)\to{\mathbb{R}}
and a constant
C\geq 0

such that

(QE)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}(x|x_{n})^{\psi}_{w}-C\leq(x|\xi)^{\psi}_{w}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}(x|x_{n})^{\psi}_{w}+C

for every
(x,\xi)\in\Gamma\times(\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma)
and for any
\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
in the class
\xi
.
Let

\beta^{\psi}_{w}(x,\xi):=\sup\left\{\limsup_{n\to\infty}(\psi(x,x_{n})-\psi(w,x_{n}))\ :\ \xi=[\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}]\right\},

for
(x,\xi)\in\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma
.
If
\psi
satisfies (QE), then we have

\beta^{\psi}_{w}(x,\xi)=\psi(x,w)-2(x|\xi)^{\psi}_{w}+O(1)\quad\text{for all $(x,\xi)\in\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma$}.

Moreover, if
\psi
is invariant under left-translations, i.e.,
\psi(gx,gy)=\psi(x,y)
for
g,x,y\in\Gamma
,
then

\beta^{\psi}_{w}(xy,\xi)=\beta^{\psi}_{w}(y,x^{-1}\xi)+\beta^{\psi}_{w}(xw,\xi)+O(1)\quad\text{for $x,y\in\Gamma$ and $\xi\in\partial\Gamma$}.

We say that
\psi
satisfies (RG) (“roughly geodesic”) relative to
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}

if for all large enough constants
C,R\geq 0
there exists a constant
C^{\prime}\geq 0

such that for all
C
-rough geodesics
\gamma
connecting
w
and
y
,
and for all
x
in the
R
-neighborhood of
\gamma
,

(RG)

|\psi(w,x)+\psi(x,y)-\psi(w,y)|\leq C^{\prime}.

The Morse lemma shows that if
\psi
satisfies (
RG
) relative to
d
,
then it satisfies (
RG
) relative to
d^{\prime}
for
d,d^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
,
furthermore, if
\psi
satisfies (
RG
) for some
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
,
then there exist
C,R\geq 0
such that

|\beta^{\psi}_{o}(x,\xi)+\psi(o,x)|\leq C\quad\text{for all $\xi\in{\mathcal{O}}(x,R)$ and all $x\in\Gamma$}.

Definition 2.2
.

A function
\psi:\Gamma\times\Gamma\to{\mathbb{R}}
is called a
tempered potential
(relative to
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
)
if
\psi
satisfies (
QE
) and (
RG
) for some (equivalently, any) metric
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
.
Moreover,
\psi
is called
\Gamma
-invariant
if
\psi
is invariant under left-translations.

Example 2.3
.

If
d
and
d_{\ast}
are hyperbolic metrics in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
,
then
d_{\ast}
defines a
\Gamma
-invariant tempered potential relative to
d
, which follows from the Morse lemma.
Moreover, for each
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the function
\psi_{s}=sd_{\ast}
defines a
\Gamma
-invariant tempered potential relative to
d
.

Let
c(x,\xi)
be a real-valued (additive) quasi-cocycle on
\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma
, i.e.,

c(xy,\xi)=c(y,x^{-1}\xi)+c(x,\xi)+O(1)\quad\text{for all $x,y\in\Gamma$ and $\xi\in\partial\Gamma$}.

We say that a finite Borel measure
\mu
on
\partial\Gamma
satisfies (QC) (“quasi-conformal”) with a quasi-cocycle
c(x,\xi)
if
there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on the quasi-cocycle such that

(QC)

C^{-1}\leq\exp\left(c(x,\xi)\right)\cdot\frac{dx_{\ast}\mu}{d\mu}(\xi)\leq C,

for
x\in\Gamma
and for
\mu
-almost all
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
.
For example,
if
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
, then any Patterson-Sullivan measure (based at

) associated with
d
satisfies (QC) with the corresponding Busemann quasi-cocycle
\beta_{o}(x,\xi)
.
More generally, we define the following.

Definition 2.4
.

For a real value
\theta
,
we say that
\psi
is a
\Gamma
-invariant tempered potential with exponent
\theta
relative to
d
if

\theta
is the abscissa of a convergence of the following series in

,

\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\exp\left(-\psi(o,x)-sd(o,x)\right).

Proposition 2.5

(Proposition 2.7 in
[
CT21
]
)
.

Let
d
be in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
and
\beta_{o}(x,\xi)
be the corresponding Busemann quasi-cocycle.
If
\psi
is a
\Gamma
-invariant tempered potential relative to

with exponent

,

on

satisfying (

) with the quasi-cocycle

.

,

such that

C^{-1}\leq\exp(\psi(o,x)+\theta d(o,x))\cdot\mu\left({\mathcal{O}}(x,R)\right)\leq C\quad\text{for all $x\in\Gamma$}.

Remark 2.6
.

Let us say that a Borel measure
\mu
on
\partial\Gamma
is
doubling
relative to a quasi-metric

if
\mu
has a positive measure on each non-empty open set and there exists a positive constant

where

are (open) balls defined in terms of

.
It has been shown that
for every
\Gamma
-invariant tempered potential relative to a metric

which is in

,

) is doubling relative to

,
where

.

-action on

,

\Gamma
-invariant Borel set in

has either

or the full measure

, Lemma 2.9]
.

Remark 2.7
.

If a
\Gamma
-invariant tempered potential has the exponent

relative to some

,

relative to every

in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
.
Indeed, the exponent relative to

\theta=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{x\in S(n,R_{0})}e^{-\psi(o,x)},\quad\text{where}\quad S(n,R_{0}):=\{x\in\Gamma\ :\ |d(o,x)-n|\leq R_{0}\}

,

, then Proposition

2.3.
Invariant measures on the boundary square

Let us define
the boundary square
\partial^{2}\Gamma:=(\partial\Gamma)^{2}\setminus\{\text{\rm diagonal}\}
endowed with the restriction of the product topology.
We define the diagonal action of
\Gamma
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
, where the action is continuous.

Let
c(x,\xi)
be a real-valued quasi-cocycle defined on
\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma
.
For any pair of such quasi-cocycles
c(x,\xi)
and
\check{c}(x,\xi)
,
we say that the pair admits a
Gromov product
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma

if there exists a family of locally bounded (i.e., uniformly bounded from above and from below on each compact set) Borel measurable functions
[\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,]_{w}:\partial^{2}\Gamma\to{\mathbb{R}}
for
w\in\Gamma
such that

[x\xi|x\eta]_{xw}=[\xi|\eta]_{w}\quad\text{for $x\in\Gamma$ and for $(\xi,\eta)\in\partial^{2}\Gamma$},

and

\check{c}(x,\xi)+c(x,\eta)=[\xi|\eta]_{x}-[\xi|\eta]_{o}+O(1),

for all
x\in\Gamma
and for all
(\xi,\eta)\in\partial^{2}\Gamma
.
For example, if
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
,
then the pair of two identical Busemann quasi-cocycles associated with
d
admits a Gromov product as twice of the one in the original sense.
This generalized notion of Gromov product has already appeared in
[
Sam14
]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
c(x,\xi)
and
\check{c}(x,\xi)
be real-valued quasi-cocycles on
\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma
.
If there exist finite Borel measures
\mu
and
\check{\mu}
on
\partial\Gamma
satisfying (
QC
) with
c(x,\xi)
and
\check{c}(x,\xi)
respectively,
and the pair admits a Gromov product
[\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,]_{w}
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
for
w\in\Gamma
,
then there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure
\Lambda
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
equivalent to

\exp([\xi|\eta]_{o})\check{\mu}\otimes\mu.

Moreover, if
\mu
and
\check{\mu}
have a positive measure on each open set in
\partial\Gamma
,
then
\Lambda
has a positive measure on each open set in
\partial^{2}\Gamma
.
In the case when
c(x,\xi)
and
\check{c}(x,\xi)
are cocycles with the equality

\check{c}(x,\xi)+c(x,\eta)=[\xi|\eta]_{x}-[\xi|\eta]_{o}\quad\text{for all $x\in\Gamma$, $(\xi,\eta)\in\partial^{2}\Gamma$},

then we may take

\Lambda=\exp([\xi|\eta]_{o})\check{\mu}\otimes\mu.

Proof.

This is a straightforward generalization of known cases.
If we define

\nu:=\exp([\xi|\eta]_{o})\check{\mu}\otimes\mu,

then for all
x\in\Gamma
, by using
[x^{-1}\xi|x^{-1}\eta]_{o}=[\xi|\eta]_{x}
, we have that

\frac{dx_{\ast}\nu}{d\nu}(\xi,\eta)=\exp([\xi|\eta]_{x}-[\xi|\eta]_{o})\frac{dx_{\ast}\check{\mu}}{d\check{\mu}}(\xi)\frac{dx_{\ast}\mu}{d\mu}(\eta),

for
\nu
-almost every
(\xi,\eta)\in\partial^{2}\Gamma
.
Since
\mu
and
\check{\mu}
satisfy (
QC
) with
c(x,\xi)
and
\check{c}(x,\xi)
respectively, and the pair admits the Gromov product
[\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,]_{w}
for
w\in\Gamma
,
the function

\varphi(\xi,\eta):=\sup_{x\in\Gamma}\frac{dx_{\ast}\nu}{d\nu}(\xi,\eta)

is essentially uniformly bounded from above and uniformly bounded away from
0
.
If we define
\Lambda:=\varphi(\xi,\eta)\nu
, then
\Lambda
is
\Gamma
-invariant.
Moreover
\Lambda
is Radon since the Gromov product is assumed to be locally bounded. For the second statement we note that, with the additional assumptions,
c(x,\xi)
and
\check{c}(x,\xi)
are genuine cocycles and the condition (
QC
) holds with
C=1
for both
\mu
and
\check{\mu}
.
It follows that we can take
\varphi(\xi,\eta)\equiv 1
for
\check{\mu}\otimes\mu
-almost every
(\xi,\eta)
(see
[
Tan21
, Remark 2.12]
) and the result follows.
∎

For a
\Gamma
-invariant tempered potential
\psi
relative to some
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
,
let

\check{\psi}(x,y):=\psi(y,x)\quad\text{for $x,y\in\Gamma$}.

Note that
\check{\psi}
is
\Gamma
-invariant.
If
\check{\psi}
is also a tempered potential relative to some metric in

, then the above results are applied to

\check{c}(x,\xi):=\beta_{o}^{\check{\psi}}(x,\xi)\quad\text{and}\quad c(x,\xi):=\beta_{o}^{\psi}(x,\xi)\quad\text{for $(x,\xi)\in\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma$},

for which the pair of the quasi-cocycles admits a Gromov product.

.

3.
Topological flows

3.1.
Topological flows: construction

Recall that
\partial^{2}\Gamma:=(\partial\Gamma)^{2}\setminus\{\text{\rm diagonal}\}
endowed with the diagonal action of
\Gamma
.
If we fix a strongly hyperbolic metric
\hat{d}
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
,
then for a fixed constant
C\geq 0
, for every
(\xi,\eta)\in\partial^{2}\Gamma
there exists a
C
-rough geodesic
\gamma_{\xi,\eta}:{\mathbb{R}}\to\Gamma

such that
\gamma_{\xi,\eta}(-t)\to\xi
and
\gamma_{\xi,\eta}(t)\to\eta
respectively as
t\to\infty
.
Let us parameterize
\gamma_{\xi,\eta}
by shifting
t\mapsto t+T
if necessary so that

\hat{d}(o,\gamma_{\xi,\eta}(0))=\inf\left\{\hat{d}(o,\gamma_{\xi,\eta}(t))\ :\ t\in{\mathbb{R}}\right\}.

We define the evaluation map by

{\bf ev}:\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}\to\Gamma,\quad(\xi,\eta,t)\mapsto\gamma_{\xi,\eta}(t),

where the space
\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}
equipped with the product topology.
Note that although
{\bf ev}
depends on the choice of
C
-rough geodesics,
any other choice yields a map whose image lies within a distance at most some constant depending only on the metric.
Furthermore we may define
{\bf ev}
as a Borel measurable map in the following way:
Let us define the set of
C
-rough geodesics
\gamma:{\mathbb{R}}\to\Gamma
and endow it with a pointwise convergence topology.
Fixing a finite set of generators equipped with a total order in
\Gamma
,
we assign each
(\xi,\eta)\in\partial^{2}\Gamma
to a
C
-rough geodesic
\gamma_{\xi,\eta}^{\min}

which is lexicographically minimal as a sequence of group elements
\{\gamma(n)\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
and define
\gamma_{\xi,\eta}(t):=\gamma_{\xi,\eta}^{\min}(\lfloor t\rfloor)
for
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

where
\lfloor t\rfloor
is the largest integer at most
t
.
Defining the evaluation map by this assignment yields a Borel measurable map
{\bf ev}
.

Let us define the cocycle

(4)

\kappa(x,\xi,\eta):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{\beta}_{o}(x^{-1},\xi)-\hat{\beta}_{o}(x^{-1},\eta)\right)\quad\text{for $(x,\xi,\eta)\in\Gamma\times\partial^{2}\Gamma$}.

The group
\Gamma
acts on
\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}
continuously via the
(\Gamma,\kappa)
-action
,

x\cdot\left(\xi,\eta,t\right):=(x\xi,x\eta,t-\kappa(x,\xi,\eta))\quad\text{for $x\in\Gamma$ and $(\xi,\eta,t)\in\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}$}.

It is known that the
(\Gamma,\kappa)
-action on
\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}
is properly discontinuous and co-compact,
i.e., the quotient topological space is compact
[
Tan21
, Lemma 3.2]
.
Let us denote the compact space by

{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}:=\Gamma\backslash(\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}).

Note that
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
is Hausdorff and in fact, metrizable (cf.
[
Min05
, Theorem 60(d)]
and
[
Tan21
, Remark 3.3]
).
We define a continuous
{\mathbb{R}}
-action, namely, a flow on
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
induced from the translation on the
{\mathbb{R}}
-component in
\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
More precisely,
let

\widetilde{\Phi}_{t}(\xi,\eta,s):=(\xi,\eta,t+s)\quad\text{for $t\in{\mathbb{R}}$ and $(\xi,\eta,s)\in\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}$}.

Since
\{\widetilde{\Phi}_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
and the
(\Gamma,\kappa)
-action commute,
it induces an
{\mathbb{R}}
-action on
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
,

\Phi_{t}:{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}\to{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa},\quad[\xi,\eta,s]\mapsto[\xi,\eta,t+s],\quad\text{for all $t\in{\mathbb{R}}$}.

Let us call
\{\Phi_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
the
topological flow
on
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
.

3.2.
Measures and ergodicity

Let us consider a Radon measure
\Lambda
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
,
i.e.,
\Lambda
is Borel regular and finite on every compact set.
Further
we consider the product measure

\Lambda\otimes dt
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}

where
dt
is the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
normalized so that the interval
[0,1]
has a unit mass.
For any
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure
\Lambda
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
,
the measure
\Lambda\otimes dt
is also Radon and invariant under the
(\Gamma,\kappa)
-action on
\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
Moreover,
\Lambda\otimes dt
is invariant under the flow
\{\widetilde{\Phi}_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
,
and we naturally associate with it a flow-invariant finite measure
m
on
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
in the following way.

Let us denote by
C_{c}(\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}})
the space of real-valued continuous functions with compact supports on
\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
For each
f\in C_{c}(\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}})
,
let

\overline{f}(\xi,\eta,t):=\sum_{x\in\Gamma}f(x\cdot(\xi,\eta,t)),

and since
\overline{f}
is well-defined and
(\Gamma,\kappa)
-invariant,
it is identified with a function (which we denote by the same symbol) on
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
.
It has been shown that
for every
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure
\Lambda
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
,
there exists a unique finite Borel measure
m
on
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
such that

\int_{\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}}f\,d\Lambda\otimes dt=\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}}\overline{f}\,dm\quad\text{for all $f\in C_{c}(\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}})$},

and furthermore,
m
is invariant under the topological flow
\{\Phi_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}

[
Tan21
, Lemma 3.4]
.
Note that
m
has a positive mass on every non-empty open set if
\Lambda\otimes dt
does.
We normalize
\Lambda
so that
m
is a probability measure.

The following theorem is known in
[
Kai94
, Theorem 2.5]
through a generalization of the classical Hopf argument (see also
[
BF17
]
).
Let us state it in the form we use in the present discussion.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that there are finite Borel measures
\mu
and
\check{\mu}
on
\partial\Gamma
and a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure
\Lambda
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
in the measure class of
\check{\mu}\otimes\mu
.
Let
m
be the Borel probability measure
m
on
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
invariant under the topological flow
\{\Phi_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
associated with
\Lambda
.
For every
f\in L^{1}({\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa},m)
, we have

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f\circ\Phi_{t}(x)\,dt=\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}}f\,dm,\quad\text{for $m$-almost every $x\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}$}.

In particular, the measure
m
is ergodic under the topological flow
\{\Phi_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
.
Moreover,
\Lambda
is ergodic with respect to the
\Gamma
-action on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
, i.e., for any
\Gamma
-invariant Borel set in
\partial^{2}\Gamma
either it or the complement has
\Lambda
-measure null.

Proof.

The proof proceeds as an adaptation to
[
Tan21
, Theorem 3.6 and Corollary 3.7]
;
we reproduce the main argument for the sake of convenience.
For every
f
in
L^{1}({\mathcal{F}}_{k},m)
,
by the Birkhoff ergodic theorem,
there exists the limit

f_{\infty}(x):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f\circ\Phi_{t}(x)\,dt,

for
m
-almost every
x
in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
.
The convergence takes place also in
L^{1}({\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa},m)
.
We show that
f_{\infty}
is constant
m
-almost everywhere.
Since
\|f_{\infty}\|_{1}\leq\|f\|_{1}
by the Fatou lemma, and
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
is a compact metrizable space,
it suffices to prove the claim for continuous functions.
Letting
\pi:\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
be the quotient map,
we define
\tilde{f}_{\infty}:=f_{\infty}\circ\pi
.
Note that
\tilde{f}
is
\{\widetilde{\Phi}_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
-invariant, whence
\tilde{f}_{\infty}
is defined on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
.
By the assumption,
\Lambda
and
\check{\mu}\otimes\mu
are mutually absolutely continuous on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
,
and we have that for
\check{\mu}\otimes\mu
-almost every
(\xi,\eta)\in\partial^{2}\Gamma
and for all
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}(f\circ\pi)(\widetilde{\Phi}_{t}(\xi,\eta,s))\,dt=\tilde{f}_{\infty}(\xi,\eta).

Let us take any compact set
K
in
\partial^{2}\Gamma
.
There exists a constant
c_{K}
such that
\langle\xi|\eta\rangle_{o}\leq c_{K}
for all
(\xi,\eta)\in K
.
For
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
, letting
x
be a point on a rough geodesic from
o
toward
\xi
at distance
T
,
we have

\hat{q}(x^{-1}\eta,x^{-1}\eta^{\prime})\leq Ce^{-T}\hat{q}(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\quad\text{for all $(\xi,\eta),(\xi,\eta^{\prime})\in K$},

where
C
is some positive constant and
\hat{q}
denotes the quasi-metric associated to
\hat{d}
(cf. Section
2.1
).
Since
f\circ\pi
is
\Gamma
-invariant and uniformly continuous,
for
\check{\mu}\otimes\mu
-almost every
(\xi,\eta),(\xi,\eta^{\prime})\in K
,
we have
\tilde{f}_{\infty}(\xi,\eta)=\tilde{f}_{\infty}(\xi,\eta^{\prime})
.
Noting that
\partial^{2}\Gamma
is
\sigma
-compact, we deduce that for
\check{\mu}
-almost every
\xi
in
\partial\Gamma
,
the function
\tilde{f}_{\infty}(\xi,\cdot\,)
is constant
\mu
-almost everywhere.
Similarly for
\mu
-almost every
\eta
in
\partial\Gamma
,
the function
\tilde{f}_{\infty}(\,\cdot,\eta)
is constant
\check{\mu}
-almost everywhere.
Therefore
\tilde{f}_{\infty}
is constant
\check{\mu}\otimes\mu
-almost everywhere, implying that
f_{\infty}
is constant
m
-almost everywhere.
∎

3.3.
Local intersection numbers

Let
\psi
be a
\Gamma
-invariant function on
\Gamma\times\Gamma
satisfying (
RG
). We then have that

\psi(x,z)=\psi(x,y)+\psi(y,z)+O(1),

for any
x,y,z
aligned on a rough geodesic in this order in
\Gamma
.
For
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
and for
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
,
let

\tau_{\inf}^{\psi}(\xi):=\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\psi(\gamma(0),\gamma(t))}{d(\gamma(0),\gamma(t))}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{\sup}^{\psi}(\xi):=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\psi(\gamma(0),\gamma(t))}{d(\gamma(0),\gamma(t))},

where
\gamma
is a quasi-geodesic converging to
\xi
in
\partial\Gamma
.
Note that since
\psi
is
\Gamma
-invariant, for each
\xi
in
\partial\Gamma
these two values are independent of the choice of quasi-geodesics toward it, or of the starting points by the Morse lemma.
Moreover, they are
\Gamma
-invariant.
If
\tau_{\inf}^{\psi}(\xi)=\tau_{\sup}^{\psi}(\xi)
,
then we define the value
\tau^{\psi}(\xi)
and call the
local intersection number
of
\psi
relative to
d
at
\xi
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\psi
be a
\Gamma
-invariant function on
\Gamma\times\Gamma
satisfying (
RG
) and
d
be a hyperbolic metric in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
.
If
\Lambda
is a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure which is ergodic on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
,
then the local intersection number
\tau^{\psi}(\xi_{+})
of
\psi
relative to
d
exists and is constant for
\Lambda
-almost every
(\xi_{-},\xi_{+})\in\partial^{2}\Gamma
.

In particular,
if there exist finite Borel measures
\mu
and
\check{\mu}
on
\partial\Gamma
and
a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure
\Lambda
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
in the measure class of
\check{\mu}\otimes\mu
,
then the local intersection number
\tau^{\psi}(\xi)
of
\psi
relative to
d
exists at
\mu
-almost every
\xi
, i.e.,

\tau_{\inf}^{\psi}(\xi)=\tau_{\sup}^{\psi}(\xi)\quad\text{for $\mu$-almost every $\xi$ in $\partial\Gamma$},

and further
\tau^{\psi}(\xi)
is constant
\mu
-almost everywhere on
\partial\Gamma
.

Proof.

Taking a Borel fundamental domain
F
in
\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}
for the
(\Gamma,\kappa)
-action and
a measurable section
\iota:{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}\to F
,
let
\tilde{w}:=\iota(w)
for
w\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
, and
\tilde{w}_{t}:=\widetilde{\Phi}_{t}(\tilde{w})
for
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Define

c_{\ast}^{\psi}(s,t):=\psi({\bf ev}(\tilde{w}_{s}),{\bf ev}(\tilde{w}_{t}))\quad\text{for $s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}$}.

Let
m
denote the Borel probability measure on
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
associated with
\Lambda
invariant under
\{\Phi_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
.
Note that
m
is ergodic under the flow by the assumption,
and
c_{\ast}^{\psi}(s,t)
is additive modulo a uniform constant such that
\sup_{|s-t|\leq 1}|c_{\ast}^{\psi}(s,t)|\leq C
for a constant
C>0
by the
\Gamma
-invariance of
\psi
.
Thus the Kingman subadditive ergodic theorem shows that
there exists a constant
\tau_{\ast}^{\psi}
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}c_{\ast}^{\psi}(0,t)=\tau_{\ast}^{\psi}\quad\text{for $m$-almost everywhere on ${\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}$}.

Applying the above argument to the case when
\psi=d
,
we show that there exists a positive constant
\tau_{\ast}^{d}>0
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}c_{\ast}^{d}(0,t)=\tau_{\ast}^{d}\quad\text{for $m$-almost everywhere on ${\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}$},

where we have that
\tau_{\ast}^{d}>0
since
d
and
\hat{d}
are quasi-isometric.
Therefore for
m
-almost every point
w
in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
,
we have that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{c_{\ast}^{\psi}(0,t)}{c_{\ast}^{d}(0,t)}=\frac{\tau_{\ast}^{\psi}}{\tau_{\ast}^{d}}.

Noting that
\Lambda\otimes dt=\sum_{x\in\Gamma}x_{\ast}\iota_{\ast}m
,
we conclude the first claim.
Concerning the second claim, since
\Lambda
is in the measure class of
\check{\mu}\otimes\mu
by the assumption,

\Lambda
is ergodic by Theorem
3.1
, and thus
we conclude that the local intersection number
\tau^{\psi}(\xi)
of
\psi
relative to
d
exists and is a constant
\mu
-almost everywhere in
\partial^{2}\Gamma
.
∎

4.
Symbolic coding

4.1.
Automatic structures and shift spaces

Fix a finite symmetric set of generators
S
of
\Gamma
, i.e.,
S=S^{-1}
.
Associated with
S
,
there exists a finite state automaton
{\mathcal{A}}=({\mathcal{G}},w,S)

where
{\mathcal{G}}=(V,E,s_{\ast})
is a finite directed graph with a distinguished vertex
s_{\ast}
(the
initial state
),
endowed with a labeling
w:E\to S
.
For a directed edge path
\omega=(\omega_{0},\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n-1})
(where the terminus of
\omega_{i}
coincides with the origin of
\omega_{i+1}
)
there is an associated path in the Cayley graph
{\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S)
beginning at the identity: the path corresponds to

w(\omega):=(o,w(\omega_{0}),w(\omega_{0})w(\omega_{1}),\dots,w(\omega_{0})\cdots w(\omega_{n-1})).

Let

w_{\ast}(\omega):=w(\omega_{0})\cdots w(\omega_{n-1}).

We say that
{\mathcal{A}}=({\mathcal{G}},w,S)
is a
strongly Markov automatic structure

if

(1)

for each vertex
v
in
{\mathcal{G}}
there exists a directed path from
s_{\ast}
to
v
,

(2)

for each directed path
\omega
in
{\mathcal{G}}
the associated path
w(\omega)
is a geodesic in
{\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S)
, and

(3)

the map
w_{\ast}
defines a bijection between the set of directed paths from
s_{\ast}
in
{\mathcal{G}}
and
\Gamma
.

It is known that every hyperbolic group admits a strongly Markov automatic structure for any finite symmetric set of generators (cf.
[
Cal13
, Section 3.2]
).
Let us fix an automaton
{\mathcal{A}}=({\mathcal{G}},w,S)
for
(\Gamma,S)
.
We denote by
\Sigma^{\ast}
(resp. by
\Sigma^{+}
) the set of finite directed paths (resp. the set of semi-infinite paths
(\omega_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
) in
{\mathcal{G}}
,
where paths are not necessarily starting from
s_{\ast}
in both cases.
Letting
\overline{\Sigma}^{+}:=\Sigma^{\ast}\cup\Sigma^{+}
,
we extend and define

w_{\ast}:\overline{\Sigma}^{+}\to\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma,

by assigning to each element of
\overline{\Sigma}^{+}
the terminus of the corresponding geodesic segment or ray issuing from
o
in
{\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S)
.

Let us define the space of bilateral directed paths in
{\mathcal{G}}
by

\Sigma:=\{(\omega_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\ :\ A(\omega_{i},\omega_{i+1})=1\ \text{for all $i\in{\mathbb{Z}}$}\},

where
A=(A(e,e^{\prime}))_{e,e^{\prime}\in E}
is the adjacency matrix on edges of
{\mathcal{G}}
,
i.e.,
A(e,e^{\prime})=1
if the terminus of
e
is the origin of
e^{\prime}
and
0
otherwise.
We define the shift

\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma,\quad\sigma(\omega_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}:=(\omega_{i+1})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}.

Note that
(\Sigma,\sigma)
is a subshift of finite type over alphabets
E
.
We define a metric in
\Sigma
by setting

d_{\Sigma}(\omega,\omega^{\prime}):=\begin{cases}\exp(-n)&\text{where $n:=\sup\{m\geq 0\ :\ \omega_{i}=\omega^{\prime}_{i}\ \text{for all $|i|\leq m$}\}$ if $\omega\neq\omega^{\prime}$},\\
0&\text{if $\omega=\omega^{\prime}$}.\end{cases}

Each bilateral path
\omega
yields a geodesic line passing through
o
in
{\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S)
:

(\xi_{n}(\omega))_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}:=(\dots,w(\omega_{-1})^{-1}w(\omega_{-2})^{-1},w(\omega_{-1})^{-1},o,w(\omega_{0}),w(\omega_{0})w(\omega_{1}),\dots),

where
\xi_{0}(\omega):=o
.
Abusing the notation, let us define the map which assigns the pair of two (distinct) extreme points in
\partial\Gamma
to each bilateral path by

w_{\ast}:\Sigma\to\partial^{2}\Gamma,\quad\omega\mapsto(\xi_{-}(\omega),\xi_{+}(\omega)),

where
\xi_{-}(\omega)
and
\xi_{+}(\omega)
are
[\{\xi_{-n}(\omega)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}]
and
[\{\xi_{n}(\omega)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}]
, respectively.
Note that the map
w_{\ast}
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to the associated quasi-metric by the hyperbolicity of
{\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S)
.

4.2.
Suspension flows

Given the cocycle
\kappa:\Gamma\times\partial^{2}\Gamma\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined by (
4
),
let

\tilde{\kappa}:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad\omega\mapsto\kappa(w(\omega_{0})^{-1},w_{\ast}(\omega)).

We will write

S_{n}\tilde{\kappa}(\omega):=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\tilde{\kappa}(\sigma^{i}\omega)\quad\text{for $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}$}.

There exist an
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}\leq S_{N}\tilde{\kappa}(\omega)\leq c_{2}
for all
\omega\in\Sigma

[
Tan21
, Lemma 4.3]
.
Fix such an
N
and let

r_{N}:=S_{N}\tilde{\kappa}.

Let us endow
\Sigma\times{\mathbb{R}}
with the product topology.
We define

{\rm Sus}(\Sigma,r_{N}):=\left(\Sigma\times{\mathbb{R}}\right)/\sim,

where

(\sigma^{N}\omega,t)\sim(\omega,t+r_{N}(\omega))
for
(\omega,t)\in\Sigma\times{\mathbb{R}}
,
and endow it with the quotient topology.
Further defining the continuous
{\mathbb{R}}
-action on
{\rm Sus}(\Sigma,r_{N})
by

\sigma_{t}[\omega,s]:=[\omega,t+s]\qquad\text{for $t\in{\mathbb{R}}$},

we obtain the flow
\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
{\rm Sus}(\Sigma,r_{N})
as a
suspension flow
over
(\Sigma,\sigma^{N})
.

The natural projection

\widetilde{\Pi}:\Sigma\times{\mathbb{R}}\to\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}},\quad(\omega,t)\mapsto(w_{\ast}(\omega),t)

induces the map

\Pi:{\rm Sus}(\Sigma,r_{N})\to{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa},\quad[\omega,t]\mapsto[w_{\ast}(\omega),t],

as a consequence of the cocycle relation for
\kappa
.
It has been shown that
\Pi
is continuous and equivariant with flows, i.e.,

\Pi\circ\sigma_{t}=\Phi_{t}\circ\Pi
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
furthermore,
\Pi
is surjective and the cardinality of each fiber is uniformly bounded
[
Tan21
, Proposition 4.4]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For the cocycle
\kappa:\Gamma\times\partial^{2}\Gamma\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined in (
4
) by the Busemann cocycle
\hat{\beta}_{o}
,
if we define

\tilde{\kappa}:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad\omega\mapsto\kappa(w(\omega_{0})^{-1},w_{\ast}(\omega)),

and further

\Psi_{B}:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad\omega\mapsto\hat{\beta}_{o}(w(\omega_{0}),\xi_{+}(\omega))\quad\text{and}\quad U:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad\omega\mapsto\langle\xi_{-}(\omega)|\xi_{+}(\omega)\rangle_{o},

then
the functions
\tilde{\kappa}
,
\Psi_{B}
and
U
are Hölder continuous on
(\Sigma,d_{\Sigma})
, i.e.,
there exist constants
L\geq 0
and
\alpha>0
such that

|\tilde{\kappa}(\omega)-\tilde{\kappa}(\omega^{\prime})|\leq Ld_{\Sigma}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})^{\alpha}\quad\text{for $\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\Sigma$},

and the same inequalities hold for
\Psi_{B}
and
U
.
Furthermore we have

\tilde{\kappa}=-\Psi_{B}+U\circ\sigma-U.

Proof.

The Hölder continuity follows since
\hat{\beta}_{o}
is defined by a strongly hyperbolic metric in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
.
The second claim follows the definition of the cocycle
\kappa
; for the details see
[
Tan21
, Lemma 5.1]
.
∎

4.3.
Thermodynamic formalism

Let us consider the set of unilateral (finite or infinite) paths
\overline{\Sigma}^{+}
(including the empty path
\emptyset
) and define the shift
\sigma:\overline{\Sigma}^{+}\to\overline{\Sigma}^{+}
by deleting the initial edge of each path.
Here we understand that
\sigma(\omega)=\emptyset
for paths
\omega
of length
1
(i.e., edges).
Further we define the metric

d_{\overline{\Sigma}^{+}}(\omega,\omega^{\prime}):=\begin{cases}\exp(-n)&\text{where $n:=\sup\{m\geq 0\ :\ \omega_{i}=\omega^{\prime}_{i}\ \text{for all $0\leq i\leq m$}\}$ if $\omega\neq\omega^{\prime}$},\\
0&\text{if $\omega=\omega^{\prime}$},\end{cases}

similarly to the case of bilateral paths
\Sigma
.

For each real-valued Hölder continuous function (potential)
\Psi:\overline{\Sigma}^{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}
relative to
d_{\overline{\Sigma}^{+}}
,
the transfer operator is defined on continuous functions
f
on
\overline{\Sigma}^{+}
by

{\mathcal{L}}_{\Psi}f(\omega)=\sum_{\sigma(\omega^{\prime})=\omega}e^{\Psi(\omega^{\prime})}f(\omega^{\prime}).

We say that a finite directed graph is
recurrent
if there exists a directed path between any pair of vertices.
A
component
of a finite directed graph is a maximal subgraph which is recurrent.
The underlying graph
{\mathcal{G}}
of an automaton
{\mathcal{A}}
can admit several distinct components which are not singletons.
This causes difficulties when we analyze the spectral properties of transfer operators on the associated shift space.

Each component
{\mathcal{C}}
of
{\mathcal{G}}
has a period
p_{\mathcal{C}}\geq 1
,
i.e., the length of any closed path in
{\mathcal{C}}
is a multiple of
p_{\mathcal{C}}
.
If the period
p_{\mathcal{C}}
is
1
, then
{\mathcal{C}}
is
topologically mixing
, i.e., there exists a directed path between any pair of vertices with length
n

for every
n\geq N
for some integer
N
.
If
p_{\mathcal{C}}>1
,
then the set of vertices
V({\mathcal{C}})
of
{\mathcal{C}}
admits a
cyclic decomposition

V({\mathcal{C}})=\bigsqcup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p_{\mathcal{C}}{\mathbb{Z}}}V_{i}
where
V_{i}
are disjoint subsets of vertices such that any edge whose origin is in
V_{i}
has the terminus in
V_{i+1}
.
If we denote by
\overline{\Sigma}^{+}_{i}
the set of paths starting from a vertex in
V_{i}
and the empty path,
then
\sigma:\overline{\Sigma}^{+}_{i}\to\overline{\Sigma}^{+}_{i+1}

and the restriction of
\sigma^{p_{\mathcal{C}}}
on
\overline{\Sigma}^{+}_{i}
is a topological mixing subshift of finite type for each
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p_{\mathcal{C}}{\mathbb{Z}}
.

For each component
{\mathcal{C}}
, define the transfer operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{C}}
by restricting
\Psi
on the set of paths in
{\mathcal{C}}
.
Since
{\mathcal{C}}
is recurrent,
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{C}}
has finitely many eigenvalues of maximal modulus
e^{{\rm Pr}_{\mathcal{C}}(\Psi)}

where
{\rm Pr}_{\mathcal{C}}(\Psi)
is called the
pressure
.
Let

{\rm Pr}(\Psi):=\max_{\mathcal{C}}{\rm Pr}_{\mathcal{C}}(\Psi),

where
{\mathcal{C}}
runs over all components in
{\mathcal{G}}
.
A component
{\mathcal{C}}
is called
maximal
for
\Psi
if
{\rm Pr}(\Psi)={\rm Pr}_{\mathcal{C}}(\Psi)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
, let
\psi
be a
\Gamma
-invariant tempered potential with exponent
0
relative to
d
; see Definition
2.4
.
If for a strongly Markov automatic structure
{\mathcal{A}}=({\mathcal{G}},w,S)
,
the corresponding unilateral shift space
(\overline{\Sigma}^{+},\sigma)
admits a Hölder continuous potential
\Psi
such that

S_{n}\Psi(\omega)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Psi(\sigma^{i}(\omega))=-\psi(o,w_{\ast}(\omega))+O(1)\quad\text{for all $\omega=(\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{n-1})\in\Sigma^{*}$},

then
{\rm Pr}(\Psi)=0
.

Proof.

The proof is based on the following estimate: For all
n
,

(5)

{\mathcal{L}}_{\Psi}^{n}{\bf 1}_{[E_{\ast}]}(\emptyset)=\sum_{|x|_{S}=n}e^{-\psi(o,x)+O(1)},

where
[E_{\ast}]
denotes the set of paths in
\overline{\Sigma}^{+}
starting at the initial state.
Since
\psi
has exponent
0
relative to
d
,
for an associated finite Borel measure
\mu
satisfying (
QC
) with respect to
\beta_{o}^{\psi}
,
there exist positive constants
C,R>0
such that

C^{-1}\leq\exp(\psi(o,x))\cdot\mu({\mathcal{O}}(x,R))\leq C\quad\text{for all $x\in\Gamma$}.

This shows that (
5
) is uniformly bounded from below and from above
(cf.
[
CT21
, Lemmas 2.8 and 4.7]
). This yields the claim since
{\rm Pr}(\Psi)
is the exponential growth rate
of (
5
) in
n
.
∎

Example 4.3
.

For the potential associated with the Busemann function
\hat{\beta}_{o}
by

\Psi_{B}:\overline{\Sigma}^{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
\omega\mapsto\hat{\beta}_{o}(w(\omega_{0}),w_{\ast}(\omega))
,
we note that
\Psi_{B}
is Hölder continuous on
\overline{\Sigma}^{+}
, and for
\omega\in\Sigma^{+}
(semi-infinite paths),

\displaystyle S_{n}\Psi_{B}(\omega)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Psi_{B}\circ\sigma^{i}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\hat{\beta}_{o}(w_{\ast}(\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{n-1}),w_{\ast}(\omega))

\displaystyle=-\hat{d}(o,w_{\ast}(\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{n-1}))+O(1),

where the implicit constant depends only on the metrics

Note that
m_{0}
is invariant under the topological flow
\{\Phi_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
.

Let us show that
m_{0}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
m_{\hat{d}}
.
If we define
T_{0}:=\sup_{\omega\in\Sigma_{0}}r_{0}(\omega)
,
then

\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ast}\nu_{0}\leq Cw_{\ast}\mu_{0}\otimes{\rm Leb}_{[0,T_{0})},

for the positive constant
C:=1/\mu_{0}\otimes dt(Z)>0
.
Further recalling that
\pi:\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
is the quotient map,
we have

m_{0}=\Pi_{0\ast}\nu_{0}=\pi_{\ast}\widetilde{\Pi}_{\ast}\nu_{0}\leq C\pi_{\ast}\left(w_{\ast}\mu_{0}\otimes{\rm Leb}_{[0,T_{0})}\right)\leq C^{\prime}m_{\hat{d}},

for some positive constant
C^{\prime}>0
by Proposition
4.7
.
Therefore
m_{0}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
m_{\hat{d}}
.

This implies that
m_{0}=m_{\hat{d}}
since
m_{0}({\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa})=1
and
m_{\hat{d}}
is ergodic with respect to
\{\Phi_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
(Theorem
3.1
).
Note that

m_{\hat{d}}(U)>0
for all non-empty open sets
U
in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
(see the second paragraph of Section
3.2
).
We also have that
m_{0}
is supported on
\Pi_{0}({\mathcal{X}}_{0})
.
Suppose that
\Pi_{0}({\mathcal{X}}_{0})
is a proper subset of
{\mathcal{X}}
;
then since
\Pi_{0}({\mathcal{X}}_{0})
is closed in
{\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
there exists a non-empty open set
U
disjoint from
\Pi_{0}({\mathcal{X}}_{0})
, implying that
m_{0}(U)=0
,
which leads a contradiction to that
m_{0}(U)=m_{\hat{d}}(U)>0
.
Therefore
\Pi_{0}({\mathcal{X}}_{0})={\mathcal{F}}_{\kappa}
, i.e.,
\Pi_{0}
is surjective.
∎

5.2.
Entropy

For a compact topological space
{\mathcal{X}}
, and a continuous map
f:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
,
let
{\mathcal{M}}(f,{\mathcal{X}})
be the space of all
f
-invariant Borel probability measures
\nu
on
{\mathcal{X}}
.
For any finite Borel partition
{\mathcal{P}}=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{k}\}
of
{\mathcal{X}}
,
let

H(\nu,{\mathcal{P}}):=-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\nu(A_{i})\log\nu(A_{i})\quad\text{for $\nu\in{\mathcal{M}}(f,{\mathcal{X}})$},

where
0\log 0:=0
.
Letting

h(f,\nu,{\mathcal{P}}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H\Big(\nu,\bigvee_{i=0}^{n-1}f^{-i}{\mathcal{P}}\Big)=\inf_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}H\Big(\nu,\bigvee_{i=0}^{n-1}f^{-i}{\mathcal{P}}\Big),

where
\bigvee_{i=0}^{n-1}f^{-i}{\mathcal{P}}:={\mathcal{P}}\vee f^{-1}{\mathcal{P}}\vee\cdots\vee f^{-(n-1)}{\mathcal{P}}
denotes the partition consisting of

A_{i_{1}}\cap f^{-1}A_{i_{2}}\cap\cdots\cap f^{-(n-1)}A_{i_{n}}\quad\text{for $i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in\{1,\dots,k\}$},

and the limit exists since the function
n\mapsto H(\nu,\bigvee_{i=0}^{n-1}f^{-i}{\mathcal{P}})
is subadditive,
we define the
measure theoretical entropy
of
f
for
({\mathcal{X}},\nu)
by

h(f,\nu):=\sup_{\mathcal{P}}h(f,\nu,{\mathcal{P}}),

where the supremum is taken over all finite Borel partitions
{\mathcal{P}}
.
In the case of a flow
\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
{\mathcal{X}}
with a flow invariant probability measure
\nu
,
the entropy is defined to be
h(\sigma_{1},\nu)
for the time-one map
\sigma_{1}
.

If
\Lambda
is a Radon measure on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
,
then we define

D_{q}(\xi_{-},\xi_{+}:\Lambda):=\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log\Lambda(B(\xi_{-},r)\times B(\xi_{+},r))}{\log r}\quad\text{for $(\xi_{-},\xi_{+})\in\partial^{2}\Gamma$},

where (open) balls
B(\xi,r)
are defined in terms of a quasi-metric
q
in
\partial\Gamma
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
\Lambda
is a Radon measure on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
and
\lambda
is a
\sigma
-invariant Borel probability measure on
\Sigma_{0}
such that

w_{\ast}\lambda\leq C\Lambda\quad\text{on $\partial^{2}\Gamma$}

for some positive constant
C>0
.
If for the strongly hyperbolic metric
\hat{d}
defining
r_{0}
in
{\rm Sus}(\Sigma_{0},r_{0})
,

D_{\hat{q}}(\xi_{-},\xi_{+}:\Lambda)\geq D\quad\text{for $\Lambda$-almost every $(\xi_{-},\xi_{+})$ in $\partial^{2}\Gamma$},

where
\hat{q}
is the associated quasi-metric in
\partial\Gamma
with
\hat{d}
,
then
we have that

h(\sigma,\lambda)+\frac{D}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{B}\,d\lambda\geq 0,

where
\Psi_{B}(\omega)=\hat{\beta}_{o}(w(\omega_{0}),w_{\ast}(\omega))
for
\omega\in\Sigma_{0}
.

Proof.

Let us identify
{\rm Sus}(\Sigma_{0},r_{0})
with
Z=\{(\omega,t)\in\Sigma_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}\ :\ 0\leq t<r_{0}(\omega)\}
.
Considering the family of cylinder sets
[\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{N-1}]
of length
N
in
\Sigma_{0}
(where
N
is the fixed natural number appearing in the definition of
r_{0}
),
we define the partition
{\mathcal{P}}
of
Z
by the sets

\{(\omega,t)\in\Sigma_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}\ :\ \omega\in[\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{N-1}],\ 0\leq t<r_{0}(\omega)\}

over the cylinder sets.
Let
\varepsilon_{0}:=(1/2)\min_{\omega\in\Sigma_{0}}r_{0}(\omega)
and
\sigma_{\ast}:=\sigma_{\varepsilon_{0}}
for the suspension flow
\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
in
{\rm Sus}(\Sigma_{0},r_{0})
, and we analyze the partitions

\sigma_{\ast}^{n}{\mathcal{P}}\vee\cdots\vee\sigma_{\ast}^{-n}{\mathcal{P}}\qquad\text{for $n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}$}.

For each
n\geq 1
,
let
{\mathcal{P}}_{[-n,n]}([\omega,t])
denote the set in this partition containing
[\omega,t]
in
{\rm Sus}(\Sigma_{0},r_{0})
(or, in
Z
).
We fix a rough geodesic
\gamma_{\omega}
from
{\mathbb{R}}
to the hyperbolic metric space
(\Gamma,\hat{d})

with extreme points
w_{\ast}(\omega)=(\xi_{-}(\omega),\xi_{+}(\omega))
such that the distance between
o
and
\gamma_{\omega}
is realized at
0
.
Note that for a large enough
R>0
, for every
[\omega^{\prime},t^{\prime}]\in{\mathcal{P}}_{[-n,n]}([\omega,t])
we have

w_{\ast}(\omega^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{O}}(\gamma_{\omega}(t-\varepsilon_{0}n),R)\times{\mathcal{O}}(\gamma_{\omega}(t+\varepsilon_{0}n),R).

By the assumption, we have
w_{\ast}\lambda\otimes{\rm Leb}_{[0,T_{0})}\leq C\Lambda\otimes dt
,
where
T_{0}:=\max_{\omega\in\Sigma_{0}}r_{0}(\omega)
.
Letting
\nu_{\lambda}:=\frac{1}{\lambda\otimes{\rm Leb}(Z)}\lambda\otimes dt|_{Z}
,
we obtain
for
C^{\prime}:=C/\lambda\otimes{\rm Leb}(Z)
,

\displaystyle\nu_{\lambda}({\mathcal{P}}_{[-n,n]}([\omega,t]))

\displaystyle\leq\nu_{\lambda}\left(\{w_{\ast}(\omega^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{O}}(\gamma_{\omega}(t-\varepsilon_{0}n),R)\times{\mathcal{O}}(\gamma_{\omega}(t+\varepsilon_{0}n),R),\ |t-t^{\prime}|\leq T_{0}\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(2T_{0}C^{\prime})\cdot\Lambda\left({\mathcal{O}}(\gamma_{\omega}(t-\varepsilon_{0}n),R)\times{\mathcal{O}}(\gamma_{\omega}(t+\varepsilon_{0}n),R)\right).

Hence taking the logarithms on both sides and dividing by
-2n
,
we have that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{2n}\log\nu_{\lambda}\left({\mathcal{P}}_{[-n,n]}([\omega,t])\right)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{2n}\Lambda\left({\mathcal{O}}(\gamma_{\omega}(t-\varepsilon_{0}n),R)\times{\mathcal{O}}(\gamma_{\omega}(t+\varepsilon_{0}n),R)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}D_{\hat{q}}(\xi_{-}(\omega),\xi_{+}(\omega):\Lambda),

where we have compared shadows with balls relative to
\hat{q}
(see (
3
)).
Furthermore,
h(\sigma_{\ast},\nu_{\lambda},{\mathcal{P}})
is equal to:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2n}H\big(\nu_{\lambda},\bigvee_{i=-n}^{n}\sigma_{\ast}^{-i}{\mathcal{P}}\big)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{2n}\int_{{\rm Sus}(\Sigma_{0},r_{0})}\log\nu_{\lambda}\left({\mathcal{P}}_{[-n,n]}([\omega,t])\right)\,d\nu_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\int_{{\rm Sus}(\Sigma_{0},r_{0})}D_{\hat{q}}(\xi_{-}(\omega),\xi_{+}(\omega):\Lambda)\,d\nu_{\lambda},

where the last inequality follows from the Fatou lemma.
Therefore if
D_{\hat{q}}(\xi_{-},\xi_{+}:\Lambda)\geq D
for
\Lambda
-almost every
(\xi_{-},\xi_{+})
in
\partial^{2}\Gamma
,
then since
w_{\ast}\lambda\leq C\Lambda
by the assumption and

h(\sigma_{\ast},\nu_{\lambda})\geq h(\sigma_{\ast},\nu_{\lambda},{\mathcal{P}})
by the definition of the measure theoretical entropy,
we have

h(\sigma_{\ast},\nu_{\lambda})\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}D.

The Abramov formula implies that

h(\sigma_{\ast},\nu_{\lambda})=\varepsilon_{0}h(\sigma_{1},\nu_{\lambda})\quad\text{and}\quad h(\sigma_{1},\nu_{\lambda})=h(\sigma^{N},\lambda)/\int_{\Sigma_{0}}r_{0}\,d\lambda,

(e.g.,
[
FH18
, Theorem 4.1.4 and Corollary 4.1.10]
).
Noting that
h(\sigma^{N},\lambda)=Nh(\sigma,\lambda)
and
\int_{\Sigma_{0}}r_{0}\,d\lambda=N\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\tilde{\kappa}\,d\lambda
,
and further
\tilde{\kappa}=-\Psi_{B}+U\circ\sigma-U
by Lemma
4.1
,
we obtain

h(\sigma_{1},\nu_{\lambda})\geq\frac{D}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad h(\sigma,\lambda)\geq\frac{D}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\tilde{\kappa}\,d\lambda\geq\frac{D}{2}\left(-\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{B}\,d\lambda\right),

concluding the claim (cf.
[
Tan21
, Lemma 6.3]
).
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\psi
be a
\Gamma
-invariant tempered potential on
\Gamma
,
and let
\mu
and
\check{\mu}
be finite Borel measures on
\partial\Gamma

such that there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
equivalent to
\check{\mu}\otimes\mu
.
If there exists a bounded measurable function
\Psi
on
(\Sigma_{0},\sigma)
such that

S_{n}\Psi(\omega)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Psi(\sigma^{i}(\omega))=-\psi(o,\xi_{n}(\omega))+O(1)\quad\text{for all $\omega\in\Sigma_{0}$},

and a
\sigma
-invariant Borel probability measure
\lambda
on
(\Sigma_{0},\sigma)

satisfying that

w_{\ast}\lambda\leq C\check{\mu}\otimes\mu,

then for the local intersection number
\tau
of
\psi
relative to
\hat{d}
, i.e.,
\tau^{\psi}(\xi)=\tau
for
\mu
-almost every
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
, we have that

\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi\,d\lambda=\tau\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{B}\,d\lambda,

where
\Psi_{B}(\omega)=\hat{\beta}_{o}(w(\omega_{0}),w_{\ast}(\omega))
for
\omega\in\Sigma_{0}
.

Proof.

Lemma
3.2
shows that
\tau^{\psi}(\xi)=\tau
for
\mu
-almost every
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
for a constant
\tau
relative to
\hat{d}
.
Since
S_{n}\Psi(\omega)=-\psi(o,\xi_{n}(\omega))+O(1)
by the assumption, and
S_{n}\Psi_{B}(\omega)=-\hat{d}(o,\xi_{n}(\omega))+O(1)
for
\omega\in\Sigma_{0}
(see Example
4.3
),
we have that by the definition of
\tau^{\psi}(\xi)
relative to
\hat{d}
and by the Birkhoff ergodic theorem,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}S_{n}\Psi(\omega)=\tau^{\psi}(w_{\ast}(\omega))\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}S_{n}\Psi_{B}(\omega)\quad\text{for $\lambda$-almost every $\omega\in\Sigma_{0}$ and in $L^{1}$}.

Since by assumption
w_{\ast}\lambda\leq C\check{\mu}\otimes\mu
,
and
\tau^{\psi}(\xi)
is constant
\mu
-almost everywhere,
we have that
\tau^{\psi}(w_{\ast}(\omega))=\tau
for
\lambda
-almost every
\omega\in\Sigma_{0}
,
and thus conclude the claim.
∎

5.3.
Real analyticity of Manhattan curves

Let us consider a pair of hyperbolic metrics
d
and
d_{\ast}
from
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
.
For
d
(resp.
d_{\ast}
),
we define the stable translation length

\ell[x]:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}d(o,x^{n})\quad\text{for $x\in\Gamma$},

(resp.
\ell_{\ast}[x]
), where the limit exists since
d(o,x^{n})
is subadditive in
n
.
Since
\ell[x]=\ell[yxy^{-1}]
, it defines a function on the set of conjugacy classes
{\bf conj}_{\Gamma}
in
\Gamma
.
Let

{\mathcal{Q}}(s,t):=\sum_{[x]\in{\bf conj}_{\Gamma}}\exp(-s\ell_{\ast}[x]-t\ell[x])\quad\text{for $s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}$},

where
[x]
is the conjugacy class of
x\in\Gamma
.
The
Manhattan curve

{\mathcal{C}}_{M}
for a pair of metrics
(d,d_{\ast})
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
is defined by the boundary of the convex domain where

{\mathcal{Q}}(s,t)<\infty
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
If we define

{\mathcal{P}}(s,t):=\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\exp(-sd_{\ast}(o,x)-td(o,x)),

then
it has been shown that
{\mathcal{Q}}(s,t)<\infty
if and only if
{\mathcal{P}}(s,t)<\infty

[
CT21
, Proposition 3.1]
.
For each fixed
s
, let
\theta(s)
be the abscissa of convergence in
t
for
{\mathcal{P}}(s,t)
.
We call
\theta
the
pressure curve

and identify the Manhattan curve
{\mathcal{C}}_{M}
with the graph of the function
\theta
.
It has been shown that
{\mathcal{C}}_{M}
is
C^{1}
for any pair of metrics from
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}

[
CT21
, Theorem 1.1]
,
and
C^{2}
for any pair of strongly hyperbolic metrics, or any pair of word metrics
[
CT21
, Theorems 1.3 and 4.14]
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group.
For any pair of strongly hyperbolic metrics
d
and
d_{\ast}
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
,
the Manhattan curve
{\mathcal{C}}_{M}
is real analytic.
Moreover,
\theta^{\prime}(0)=-\tau(d_{\ast}/d)
where
\tau(d_{\ast}/d)
is the local intersection number of
d_{\ast}
relative to
d
.

Proof.

Let
\beta_{\ast o}
and
\beta_{o}
be the Busemann cocycles for
d_{\ast}
and for
d
respectively,
and let

\Psi_{\ast}(\omega):=\beta_{\ast o}(w(\omega_{0}),w_{\ast}(\omega))\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi(\omega):=\beta_{o}(w(\omega_{0}),w_{\ast}(\omega))\quad\text{for $\omega\in\Sigma_{0}$}.

Since
d_{\ast}
and
d
are strongly hyperbolic metrics,

\Psi_{\ast}
and
\Psi
define Hölder continuous potentials on
(\Sigma_{0},\sigma)
.
For any
(s,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, let us define

\Psi_{s,t}:=s\Psi_{\ast}+t\Psi.

If
t=\theta(s)
, then
sd_{\ast}+td
has exponent
0
with respect to any hyperbolic metric in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
, and thus by Lemma
4.2
, we have
{\rm Pr}(\Psi_{s,t})=0
.
Therefore for each
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{M}
, we have

{\rm Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(\Psi_{s,t})\leq 0
.
If
t=\theta(s)
, then there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure
\Lambda_{s,t}
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
equivalent to
\mu_{s,t}\otimes\mu_{s,t}
(Example
2.9
).
For this measure
\Lambda_{s,t}
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
, by Proposition
5.1
(2),
there exists a
\sigma
-invariant Borel probability measure
\lambda_{s,t}
on
\Sigma_{0}
such that

w_{\ast}\lambda_{s,t}\otimes{\rm Leb}_{[0,T_{0})}\leq c\Lambda_{s,t}\otimes dt\quad\text{on $\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}$},

for some positive constants
T_{0},c>0
, and thus
w_{\ast}\lambda_{s,t}\leq C\mu_{s,t}\otimes\mu_{s,t}
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
for some positive constant
C>0
.
For the fixed strongly hyperbolic metric
\hat{d}
which is used to define
{\rm Sus}(\Sigma_{0},r_{0})
in Theorem
4.9
and the corresponding quasi-metric
\hat{q}
in
\partial\Gamma
,
we have that

D_{\hat{q}}(\xi_{-},\xi_{+}:\Lambda_{s,t})=2\tau_{s,t}\quad\text{for $\Lambda_{s,t}$-almost all $(\xi_{-},\xi_{+})\in\partial^{2}\Gamma$},

where
\tau_{s,t}
is the local intersection number of
sd_{\ast}+td
relative to
\hat{d}
for
\mu_{s,t}
-almost everywhere
(by Lemma
3.2
).
Moreover, Lemma
5.3
implies that

\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{s,t}\,d\lambda_{s,t}=\tau_{s,t}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{B}\,d\lambda_{s,t},

where
\Psi_{B}(\omega)=\hat{\beta}_{o}(w(\omega_{0}),w_{\ast}(\omega))
.
By Lemma
5.2
we have that

h(\sigma,\lambda_{s,t})+\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{s,t}\,d\lambda_{s,t}=h(\sigma,\lambda_{s,t})+\tau_{s,t}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{B}\,d\lambda_{s,t}\geq 0.

The variational principle (Proposition
4.5
) shows that
{\rm Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(\Psi_{s,t})\geq 0
.
Therefore for
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, if
t=\theta(s)
, then

{\rm Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(\Psi_{s,t})=0,

and Proposition
4.5

implies that
\lambda_{s,t}
is the unique equilibrium state for
\Psi_{s,t}
.
Note that the function
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
(s,t)\mapsto{\rm Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(\Psi_{s,t})
is real analytic by Theorem
4.4
(2).
Moreover if
t=\theta(s)
, then

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big|_{t=\theta(s)}{\rm Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(\Psi_{s,t})=\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi\,d\lambda_{s,t}<0,

where the last strict inequality follows since
S_{n}\Psi<0
for all large enough
n
.
This holds for each
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
The implicit function theorem implies that
\theta
is real analytic on the whole of
{\mathbb{R}}
, concluding the first claim.
The second claim holds since

\theta^{\prime}(0)=-\frac{\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{\ast}\,d\lambda_{0,\theta(0)}}{\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi\,d\lambda_{0,\theta(0)}},

and the right hand side coincides with
-\tau(d_{\ast}/d)
by Lemmas
3.2
and
5.3
.
∎

In fact, we have a short proof that applies to Manhattan curves
for pairs of word metrics.
Since it is of interest on its own right, we include it below.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group.
For any pair of word metrics in
\Gamma
,
the Manhattan curve
{\mathcal{C}}_{M}
is real analytic.

Proof.

For any pair of finite symmetric sets of generators
S,S_{\ast}
,
there exist a strongly Markov automatic structure
{\mathcal{A}}=({\mathcal{G}},w,S)
and a function
d\varphi_{\ast}
on the edges such that

|w_{\ast}(\omega)|_{S_{\ast}}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}d\varphi_{\ast}(\omega_{i}),

for all paths
\omega=(\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{n-1})
with
n=|w_{\ast}(\omega)|_{S}
from the initial state in the underlying directed graph
[
CF10
, Lemma 3.8]
.
Let
\Psi_{S_{\ast}}(\omega):=d\varphi_{\ast}(\omega_{0})
for
\omega\in\overline{\Sigma}^{+}
.
Since
\Psi_{S_{\ast}}
depends only on the first coordinate of
\omega
, it defines a Hölder continuous potential on
(\overline{\Sigma}^{+},\sigma)
.

For
s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we consider the series
{\mathcal{P}}(s,t)
for the pair
d_{S},d_{S_{\ast}}
.
Defining the potential
\Psi_{S}:=1
, we take a maximal component
{\mathcal{C}}
for
\Psi_{S}
.
If we define
\Gamma_{\mathcal{C}}
as the set of group elements which are obtained as images of the words corresponding to finite paths in
{\mathcal{C}}
,
then there exists a finite set
B
in
\Gamma
such that
B\Gamma_{\mathcal{C}}B=\Gamma
(cf. the proof of Proposition
4.8
; see
[
CT21
, the proof of Lemma 4.19]
).
For any
s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, letting

{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(s,t):=\sum_{x\in\Gamma_{\mathcal{C}}}\exp(-s|x|_{S_{\ast}}-t|x|_{S}),

we have that

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{C}}(s,t)\leq{\mathcal{P}}(s,t)=\sum_{x\in B\Gamma_{\mathcal{C}}B}\exp(-s|x|_{S_{\ast}}-t|x|_{S})\leq e^{c(s,t)}(\#B)^{2}\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{C}}(s,t),

where
c(s,t):=2|s|\max_{x\in B}|x|_{S_{\ast}}+2|t|\max_{x\in B}|x|_{S}
.
Therefore for each fixed
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the divergence exponents of
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{C}}(s,t)
and
{\mathcal{P}}(s,t)
coincide.
Furthermore this exponent given by

since for

By (

.
Since

is real analytic in

we conclude the claim.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let

6.
Random walks and their harmonic measures

For a probability measure
p
on
\Gamma
such that the support is finite and generates
\Gamma
as a semigroup,
let us consider the Green function

G(x,y):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p^{\ast n}(x^{-1}y)\quad\text{for $x,y\in\Gamma$},

where
p^{\ast n}
denotes the
n
-fold convolution of
p
.
If we define

\psi(x,y):=-\log G(x,y),

then
\psi
is a
\Gamma
-invariant tempered potential relative to any metric
d
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
.
Indeed,
\psi
satisfies (
RG
) by the Ancona inequality:
G(x,z)\leq CG(x,y)G(y,z)
whenever
x
,
y
and
z
are aligned on a geodesic segment (in a word metric) where
C
is a uniform constant.
Note that the inequality
G(x,z)\geq C^{\prime}G(x,y)G(y,z)
holds for all
x
,
y
and
z
where
C^{\prime}=G({\rm id},{\rm id})^{-1}
by the definition of the Green function.
These inequalities
imply that
the Green function is multiplicative up to uniform multiplicative constants along geodesic rays in a word metric (see
[
Gou15
]
for
p
with an infinite support).
Furthermore under the present assumption, (
QE
) holds with

\beta_{o}^{\psi}(x,\xi)=\psi(x,o)-2(x|\xi)_{o}^{\psi}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{o}^{\psi}(x,\xi)=-\log K_{o}(x,\xi)\quad\text{for $(x,\xi)\in\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma$},

where
K_{o}(x,\xi)
is the Martin kernel based at
o
.
It has been shown that
K_{o}(x,\xi)
is Hölder continuous in
\xi
(see
[
INO08
, Theorem 3.3]
) relative to some (equivalently, any) quasi-metric
q
for each
x
.
Note that
G(x,y)
is not necessarily symmetric in
x
and
y
unless
p
defines a symmetric random walk on
\Gamma
.
See
[
INO08
, Corollaries 2.4 and 3.4]
and
[
BHM11
, Section 3]
.

If we define
\check{G}(x,y):=G(y,x)
, then
\check{G}(x,y)
is the Green function associated with the measure
\check{p}
,
where
\check{p}(x):=p(x^{-1})
for
x\in\Gamma
.
The harmonic measure
\nu
defined by
p
(the
p
-stationary measure) on
\partial\Gamma
satisfies that

\frac{dx_{\ast}\nu}{d\nu}(\xi)=K_{o}(x,\xi)\quad\text{for $(x,\xi)\in\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma$},

and similarly, for the harmonic measure
\check{\nu}
defined by
\check{p}
with the associated Martin kernel
\check{K}_{o}(x,\xi)
.
The
Naim kernel

\phi(\eta,\xi)
is defined by the extension of the following function on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
:

\frac{G(x,y)}{G(x,o)G(o,y)}\quad\text{for $(x,y)\in\Gamma\times\Gamma$},

and
\phi(\xi,\eta)
is Hölder continuous on each compact subset in
\partial^{2}\Gamma

(see e.g.,
[
Tan17
, the proof of Proposition 5.7]
and
[
Led01
, Proposition 3.17]
).
Moreover, we have that

-\log\phi(\xi,\eta)+\log\phi(x^{-1}\xi,x^{-1}\eta)=-\log\check{K}_{o}(x,\xi)-\log K_{o}(x,\eta)\quad\text{for $(x,\xi,\eta)\in\Gamma\times\partial^{2}\Gamma$}

(for the derivation, see
[
Tan17
,
Led01
, ibid.]
).
Therefore the pair of cocycles
-\log\check{K}_{o}(x,\xi)
and
-\log K_{o}(x,\eta)
admits
a Gromov product
[\xi|\eta]_{o}:=\log\phi(\xi,\eta)
, and
we obtain a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure

\exp([\xi|\eta]_{o})\check{\nu}\otimes\nu
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
by Proposition
2.8
.

For
d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
,
let us consider the following series

{\mathcal{P}}_{G,d}(s,t):=\sum_{x\in\Gamma}G(o,x)^{s}e^{-td(o,x)}\quad\text{for $s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}$}.

For each
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the divergence exponent in
t
is finite, and if
s=1
, then it is
0
(cf.
[
BHM11
, Theorem 1.1(ii)]
and
[
Tan17
, Lemma 3.2]
; the proof can be adapted to apply to any metric in the class
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
).
Let us consider any strongly hyperbolic metric
\hat{d}
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
, and let
t=\theta_{G,\hat{d}}(s)
be the divergence exponent of
{\mathcal{P}}_{G,\hat{d}}(s,t)
in
t
for each fixed
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

The function
s\mapsto\theta_{G,\hat{d}}(s)
is real analytic on the whole
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

The proof proceeds as in Theorem
5.4
.
Let us define

\Psi_{G}(\omega):=-\log K_{o}(w(\omega_{0}),w_{\ast}(\omega))\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi_{B}(\omega):=\hat{\beta}_{o}(w(\omega_{0}),w_{\ast}(\omega))\quad\text{for $\omega\in\Sigma_{0}$},

where
K_{o}(x,\xi)
is the Martin cocycle and
\hat{\beta}_{o}(x,\xi)
is the Busemann cocycle associated with
\hat{d}
.
Define

\Psi_{s,t}:=s\Psi_{G}+t\Psi_{B}\quad\text{for $s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}$}.

Note that
\Psi_{G}
and
\Psi_{B}
are Hölder continuous potentials on
(\Sigma_{0},\sigma)
.
The rest follows as in Theorem
5.4
; we omit the details.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

The Legendre transform of the function
\theta_{G,\hat{d}}(s)
in Theorem
6.1
provides the
multifractal profile
for the harmonic measure
\nu
with respect to the quasi-metric
\hat{q}
associated with the strongly hyperbolic metric
\hat{d}
(see
[
Tan17
, Theorem 1.2]
for the case of word metrics and
[
CT21
, Theorem 3.8]
for a related result).
The result shows that the multifractal profile is a real analytic function.
Moreover, one can show that the domain is a bounded closed interval and the profile function extends continuously at extremes by adapting the method in
[
CT21

.
In particular this applies to finite range random walks on surface groups. In certain special cases, it has been recently shown that the harmonic measure is singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure on the boundary of the hyperbolic plane
[
Kos21
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
KT22
, Theorem 1]
.
In those cases,

is strictly convex.

7.1.

If

denotes an eigenvector of
\sqrt{g^{\ast}g}
for the eigenvalue
\sigma_{i}(g)
,
then the space
U_{p}(g)
is spanned by
\{gv_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,p}
.
Since
\sigma_{m-i+1}(g^{-1})=\sigma_{i}(g)^{-1}
for
i=1,\dots,m
,
for any
g
with a gap of index
p
, the inverse
g^{-1}
has a gap of index
m-p
.
Let

S_{m-p}(g):=U_{m-p}(g^{-1}).

By definition,
S_{m-p}(g)
consists of eigenvectors of
\sqrt{g^{\ast}g}
with eigenvalues
\sigma_{i}(g)
for
i=p+1,\dots,m
, and
S_{m-p}(g)
and
g^{-1}U_{p}(g)
are orthogonal.

Letting
\|\cdot\|
be the associated norm in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,
we define

\|g\|:=\sup\left\{\frac{\|gv\|}{\|v\|}\ :\ 0\neq v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\right\}\quad\text{for $g\in{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})$}.

Let us define a metric
d_{\bf P}
in the projective space
{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
(the space of one-dimensional subspace in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
) by

d_{\bf P}(x_{1},x_{2}):=\inf\{\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|\ :\ v_{i}\in x_{i},\ \|v_{i}\|=1,\ i=1,2\}.

For two closed sets
X_{1},X_{2}
in
{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
, we define

d_{\bf P}(X_{1},X_{2}):=\inf\{d_{\bf P}(x_{1},x_{2})\ :\ x_{i}\in X_{i},\ i=1,2\}.

Below it is useful to recall the following inequalities: For
g,h\in{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
, if
g
and
gh
have a gap of index
p
,
then

(10)

d_{\bf P}(U_{p}(g),U_{p}(gh))\leq\|h\|\|h^{-1}\|\frac{\sigma_{p+1}(g)}{\sigma_{p}(g)},

and

(11)

d_{\bf P}(U_{p}(gh),gU_{p}(h))\leq\|g\|\|g^{-1}\|\frac{\sigma_{p+1}(h)}{\sigma_{p}(h)},

(see
[
BPS19
, Lemmas A4 and A5]
).

7.2.
Dominated representations

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group.
For an integer
m\geq 2
, we consider a linear representation of
\Gamma
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
given by a homomorphism

\rho:\Gamma\to{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}}).

Let
\lambda(g)
be the spectral radius of the linear transformation
g
, i.e., the modulus of the largest eigenvalue of
g
in absolute value.
Recall that
g
is
proximal
if
g
has an eigenvalue of modulus
\lambda(g)
with multiplicity one.
If
g
is proximal, then its associated map on the projective space
{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
has an attracting fixed point given by the (one-dimensional) eigenspace and a repelling set determined by the complementary codimension one subspace in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
For
\varepsilon>0
, we say that
g
is
\varepsilon
-proximal
if
g
is proximal,
moreover, for the attracting fixed point
x_{g+}
and the repelling set
X_{g-}
,
defining

b^{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})\ :\ d_{\bf P}(x,x_{g+})\leq\varepsilon\}\quad\text{and}\quad B^{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})\ :\ d_{\bf P}(x,X_{g-})\geq\varepsilon\},

we have
d_{\bf P}(x_{g+},X_{g-})\geq 2\varepsilon
,

g(B^{\varepsilon})\subset b^{\varepsilon}
and
g
restricted on
B^{\varepsilon}
is
\varepsilon
-Lipschitz
[
Ben96
, Section 6]
.

For each element
g\in{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
,
let
\sigma_{1}(g)
(resp.
\sigma_{2}(g)
) be the largest (resp. the second largest) singular value of
g
,
and
\lambda_{1}(g)
(resp.
\lambda_{2}(g)
) be the modulus of the eigenvalue of
g
with the largest (resp. the second largest) modulus. We have
\sigma_{1}(g)=\|g\|
and
\lambda_{1}(g)=\lambda(g)
.
Note that
\lambda_{1}(g)
and
\lambda_{2}(g)
depend only on the conjugacy class of
g
.
We say that
\rho:\Gamma\to{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
is a
dominated representation
if
there exist positive constants
c,C>0
such that

(12)

\frac{\sigma_{2}(\rho(x))}{\sigma_{1}(\rho(x))}\leq Ce^{-c|x|}\quad\text{for all $x\in\Gamma$},

where
|x|
stands for a word norm of
x
with a fixed set of generators
S
.
In fact, such a representation is called a
1
-dominated representation
in
[
BPS19
]
;
we discuss only this case for the sake of brevity.
The definition does not depend on the choice of
S
, whence we omit the symbol from the notation.
Note that the condition (
12
) implies that

\frac{\sigma_{m}(\rho(x))}{\sigma_{m-1}(\rho(x))}\leq Ce^{-c|x|}\quad\text{for all $x\in\Gamma$},

since
\sigma_{i}(g^{-1})=\sigma_{m-i+1}(g)^{-1}
for
g\in{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
.

For any dominated representation
\rho:\Gamma\to{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
,
there exist positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

(13)

c_{1}^{-1}|x|-c_{2}\leq\log\|\rho(x)\|\leq c_{1}|x|\quad\text{for $x\in\Gamma$}.

Indeed, for each
\rho
the condition (
12
) holds for the representation
x\mapsto|\det\rho(x)|^{-1/m}\rho(x)
, and applying it to this new representation we have

\log\sigma_{2}(\rho(x))\geq-(m-1)\log\sigma_{1}(\rho(x))
;
combining with (
12
) implies

\log\sigma_{1}(\rho(x))\geq-(m-1)\log\sigma_{1}(\rho(x))+c|x|-\log C.

Rearranging the terms gives the first inequality in (
13
) since
\sigma_{1}(\rho(x))=\|\rho(x)\|
.
The second inequality of (
13
) holds with
c_{1}:=\max\{\|\rho(s)\|\ :\ s\in S\}
.

Note that taking the
n
-th roots of both sides in (
12
) for
x^{n}\in\Gamma
and letting
n
tend to infinity, we have

(14)

\frac{\lambda_{2}(\rho(x))}{\lambda_{1}(\rho(x))}\leq e^{-c\ell[x]}\quad\text{for all $[x]\in{\bf conj}_{\Gamma}$},

where
\ell[x]
denotes the stable length function defined by the chosen word metric of a conjugacy class
[x]
.
We observe that by (
14
) if
\rho(x)
is proximal and
\ell[x]
is large enough,
then
\rho(x)
has a strong contraction property away from the repelling set in
{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
.
This is used to show that some proximal element whose attracting fixed point and repelling set are far enough is
\varepsilon
-proximal for a given
\varepsilon>0
.
Appealing to (
13
), we obtain for a positive constant
c_{1}>0
,

(15)

c_{1}^{-1}\ell[x]\leq\log\lambda(\rho(x))\leq c_{1}\ell[x]\quad\text{for all $[x]\in{\bf conj}_{\Gamma}$}.

Associated with a dominated representation
\rho
,
there exists a pair of
\rho
-equivariant continuous maps from the boundary
\partial\Gamma
to the projective space of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and that of the dual
{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}^{\ast}
,

E_{\rho}:\partial\Gamma\to{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})\quad\text{such that $\rho(x)E_{\rho}(\xi)=E_{\rho}(x\xi)$ for $x\in\Gamma$ and $\xi\in\partial\Gamma$},

and

\check{E}_{\rho}:\partial\Gamma\to{\bf P}({{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}^{\ast})\quad\text{such that $\rho(x)\check{E}_{\rho}(\xi)=\check{E}_{\rho}(x\xi)$ for $x\in\Gamma$ and $\xi\in\partial\Gamma$}

where we understand that for
\theta\in{\bf P}({{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}^{\ast})
the action is the one induced by
\rho(x)\theta=\theta\circ\rho(x)^{-1}
.
Let us identify each
\theta\in{\bf P}({{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}^{\ast})
with the space of hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
determined by the kernel of a representative of
\theta
in the dual
{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}^{\ast}
, where the kernel is independent of the choice.
Through this identification
\check{E}_{\rho}(\xi)
and
E_{\rho}(\eta)
determine transversal subspaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
if
\xi\neq\eta
.
Note that for every infinite order element
x
in
\Gamma
, the image
\rho(x)
is proximal; for the attracting (resp. repelling) fixed point
x_{+}
(resp.
x_{-}
) of
x
in the boundary,
E_{\rho}(x_{+})
(resp.
\check{E}_{\rho}(x_{-})
) yields an attracting fixed point (resp. a repelling invariant set) in
{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
.

More concretely, the maps
E_{\rho}
and
\check{E}_{\rho}
are obtained as follows.
For each
\xi
in
\partial\Gamma
,
letting
\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a geodesic converging to
\xi
,
we define

E_{\rho}(\xi):=\lim_{n\to\infty}U_{1}(\rho(x_{n}))\qquad\text{and}\qquad\check{E}_{\rho}(\xi):=\lim_{n\to\infty}U_{m-1}(\rho(x_{n})),

where the limits are defined in
{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
and
{\bf P}({{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}^{\ast})
with the fixed metrics, respectively;
they exist by (
10
), see
[
BPS19
, Lemma 4.7]
.
Moreover, the limits do not depend on the choice of geodesics.
By (
10
),
we have that there exist positive constants
C,c>0
such that

d_{\bf P}(E_{\rho}(\xi),E_{\rho}(\eta))\leq Ce^{-c(\xi|\eta)_{o}}\quad\text{for $\xi,\eta\in\partial\Gamma$},

and an analogous inequality holds for
\check{E}_{\rho}
, i.e.,

E_{\rho}
(resp.
\check{E}_{\rho}
) defines a Hölder continuous map from
\partial\Gamma
to
{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
(resp.
{\bf P}({{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}^{\ast})
).
The maps
E_{\rho}
and
\check{E}_{\rho}
are
\rho
-equivariant by (
11
).
It holds that for any
\xi,\eta\in\partial\Gamma
with
\xi\neq\eta
,
the two subspaces
\check{E}_{\rho}(\xi)
and
E_{\rho}(\eta)
are transversal;
this is due to
[
BG09
, Theorem A]
(see also
[
BPS19
, Appendix A.4]
).

For each
(u,v)\in{\bf P}({{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}^{\ast})\times{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
,
let

\llangle u,v\rrangle:=\frac{|u_{0}(v_{0})|}{\|u_{0}\|\|v_{0}\|},

where
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
represent
u
and
v
respectively, and the right hand side does not depend on the choices.
Note that
(u,v)\mapsto\llangle u,v\rrangle
is continuous.
Identifying
u
with the corresponding set in
{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
determined by the hyperplane,
we have that

(16)

\llangle u,v\rrangle\leq d_{\bf P}(u,v)\leq 2\llangle u,v\rrangle.

This follows by noting that
\llangle u,v\rrangle
is the sine of angle between
v
and the corresponding hyperplane for
u
.

.

Fix a word metric in
\Gamma
.
For any dominated representation

and

Translating from the left hand side by

,

, and let

For
\eta\in{\mathcal{O}}(y,R)

d_{{\bf P}}(U_{1}(\rho(y)),E_{\rho}(\eta))\leq Ce^{-c|y|},

and
an analogous estimate holds for

and

with

C_{\ast}

Letting

.
Since

,
for all

in

∎

For
(g,\xi)\in\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma
,
if we define

for each fixed

For a unit vector

Lemma 7.2
.

For every
\xi\in\partial\Gamma
,
if a sequence

in
\Gamma
converges to

,
then for each

,

For

,
and for a unit vector

,
we have that

where

.

\sin(\pi/2-\alpha)=\llangle U_{1}(\rho(g^{-1}x)^{\ast}),U_{1}(\rho(x)^{\ast})\rrangle,

7.3.
Intersections for pairs of dominated representations

Fix an integer
m\geq 2
.
Let
\rho
and
\rho_{\ast}
be a pair of dominated representations from
\Gamma
to
{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
.
We define the
Manhattan curve
for any such pair
(\rho,\rho_{\ast})

as the boundary of the domain where

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\rho,\rho_{\ast}}(s,t):=\sum_{[x]\in{\bf conj}_{\Gamma}}\exp(-s\log\lambda(\rho_{\ast}(x))-t\log\lambda(\rho(x)))\quad\text{for $(s,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}$},

Note that the Manhattan curve is convex by the Hölder inequality.
Let

{\mathcal{P}}_{\rho,\rho_{\ast}}(s,t):=\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\exp(-s\log\|\rho_{\ast}(x)\|-t\log\|\rho(x)\|)\quad\text{for $(s,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}$}.

, let

in

, and let us call

the

for the pair
(\rho,\rho_{\ast})
.
Analogous to the Manhattan curve for a pair of metrics
[

, Proposition 3.1]

we show that

if and only if

in Proposition
7.4
below.

Lemma 7.3
.

.

For each

, let

Applying the cocycle

to Proposition

,

)

Fix a large enough

.
More precisely, for any open set

\displaystyle-\inf_{t\in U}I(t)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho,L}(n)}\#\left\{x\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho,L}(n)\ :\ \frac{1}{n}\log\|\rho_{\ast}(x)\|\in U\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho,L}(n)}\#\left\{x\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho,L}(n)\ :\ \frac{1}{n}\log\|\rho_{\ast}(x)\|\in V\right\}\leq-\inf_{t\in V}I(t),

and

is the pressure curve for the pair of dominated representations

.

Furthermore, for a large enough constant

,

.

Proof.

By Lemma

, for each

, we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho,L}(n)}\sum_{x:\ |\log\|\rho(x)\|-n|\leq L}\exp\left(s\log\|\rho_{\ast}(x)\|\right)=\theta(-s)-\theta(0).

Since

is continuously differentiable (in fact, real analytic) by Theorem

,
the Gärtner-Ellis theorem (e.g.,
[

, Theorem 2.3.6]

Concerning the second claim, by Proposition

,
we have that for each

C_{s,L,R}^{-1}\,n^{-2}e^{\theta(s)n}\leq\sum_{[x]:\ |\log\lambda(\rho(x))-n|\leq L}\exp\left(-s\log\lambda(\rho_{\ast}(x))\right)\leq C_{s,L,R}\,e^{\theta(s)n},

for all large enough

Hence for all large enough

and for each

,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{\#{\mathcal{C}}_{\rho,L}(n)}\sum_{x:\ |\log\lambda(\rho(x))-n|\leq L}\exp\left(s\log\lambda(\rho_{\ast}(x))\right)=\theta(-s)-\theta(0).

The rest follows as in the proof of the first claim.
∎

Theorem 7.11
.

Let

.

Theorem 7.13
.

7.4.
Real analytic variations of dominated representations

Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a real analytic manifold.
We say that a family of representation
\rho_{u}:\Gamma\to{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
parameterized by
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
is a
real analytic family
if for each
x\in\Gamma
, the map
u\mapsto\rho_{u}(x)
yields a real analytic map with values in
{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
.
Recall that associated with any dominated representation
\rho
,
there exists a pair of Hölder continuous
\rho
-equivariant maps,

\check{E}_{\rho}:\partial\Gamma\to{\bf P}({{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}^{\ast})\quad\text{and}\quad E_{\rho}:\partial\Gamma\to{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}).

Let us consider the spaces
C^{\alpha}(\partial\Gamma,{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}))
and
C^{\alpha}(\partial\Gamma,{\bf P}({{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}^{\ast}))
of
\alpha
-Hölder maps for some
\alpha>0
as Banach manifolds.

Theorem 7.14

(Theorem 6.1 in
[
BCLS15
]
and Theorem 6.1 in
[
BPS19
]
)
.

Let
\rho_{u}:\Gamma\to{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
for
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
be a real analytic family of representations.
If
\rho_{u_{0}}
is dominated at
u_{0}\in{\mathcal{U}}
,
then there exists a neighborhood
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
of
u_{0}
in
{\mathcal{U}}
such that
for every
u\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
,
the representations
\rho_{u}
are dominated.
Moreover, for some
\alpha>0
, the map from
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
to
C^{\alpha}(\partial\Gamma,{\bf P}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}))
,
u\mapsto E_{\rho_{u}}
and the map from
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
to
C^{\alpha}(\partial\Gamma,{\bf P}({{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}^{\ast}))
,
u\mapsto\check{E}_{\rho_{u}}
are real analytic, respectively.

The following is shown in
[
BCLS15
, Propositions 3.12 and 8.1]
.
We give a proof by using Theorem
4.9
.

Theorem 7.15
.

Let
\rho_{u}
for
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
and
\rho_{\ast v}
for

be two real analytic families of dominated representations from
\Gamma
to
{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
for

.
The exponential growth rate

is a real analytic function on
{\mathcal{U}}
, and
the intersection number
\tau(\rho_{\ast v}/\rho_{u})
is a real analytic function on
{\mathcal{U}}\times{\mathcal{V}}
.

Proof.

We consider
(\Sigma_{0},\sigma)
in Theorem
4.9
.
Letting
c_{\rho_{u}}(x,\xi)
be the cocycle associated with
\rho_{u}
for
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
,
we define

\Psi_{u}(\omega):=c_{\rho_{u}}(w(\omega_{0}),w_{\ast}(\omega))\quad\text{for $\omega\in\Sigma_{0}$}.

Note that
\Psi_{u}
is a Hölder continuous potential for each
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
.
Since
v_{\rho_{u}}c_{\rho_{u}}(x,\xi)
has the exponent
0
with respect to any metric in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Gamma}
,
by Lemma
4.2
, we have
{\rm Pr}(v_{\rho_{u}}\Psi_{u})=0
, and thus

{\rm Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(v_{\rho_{u}}\Psi_{u})\leq 0
.
For each
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
, there exists a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure
\Lambda_{u}
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
equivalent to
\mu_{\check{\rho}_{u}}\otimes\mu_{\rho_{u}}
.
By Proposition
5.1
(2),
there exists a
\sigma
-invariant Borel probability measure
\lambda_{u}
on
\Sigma_{0}
such that

w_{\ast}\lambda_{u}\otimes{\rm Leb}_{[0,T_{0})}\leq c\Lambda_{u}\otimes dt\qquad\text{on $\partial^{2}\Gamma\times{\mathbb{R}}$},

for some positive constants
T_{0},c>0
, further
w_{\ast}\lambda_{u}\leq C\mu_{\check{\rho}_{u}}\otimes\mu_{\rho_{u}}
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
for some positive constant
C>0
.
For the fixed strongly hyperbolic metric
\hat{d}
used to define
{\rm Sus}(\Sigma_{0},r_{0})
in Theorem
4.9
,
let
\hat{q}
be the corresponding quasi-metric in
\partial\Gamma
.
We then have that

D_{\hat{q}}(\xi_{-},\xi_{+};\Lambda_{u})=\hat{\tau}_{u}\qquad\text{for $\Lambda_{u}$-almost all $(\xi_{-},\xi_{+})\in\partial^{2}\Gamma$},

where
\hat{\tau}_{u}
is the local intersection number of

relative to
\hat{d}
for
\mu_{\rho_{u}}
-almost every point in the boundary by Lemma
3.2
.
Furthermore, Lemma
5.3
shows that

\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{u}\,d\lambda_{u}=\hat{\tau}_{u}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{B}\,d\lambda_{u}.

Lemma
5.2
shows that

h(\sigma,\lambda_{u})+\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{u}\,d\lambda_{u}=h(\sigma,\lambda_{u})+\hat{\tau}_{u}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{B}\,d\lambda_{u}\geq 0,

and by the variational principle (Proposition
4.5
),
we have
{\rm Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(v_{\rho_{u}}\Psi_{u})\geq 0
.
Hence
{\rm Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(v_{\rho_{u}}\Psi_{u})=0
.
Proposition
4.5
shows that
\lambda_{u}
is the unique equilibrium state for
\Psi_{u}
.
Note that
{\mathcal{U}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
(u,s)\mapsto{\rm Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(s\Psi_{u})

(2) and

.
Furthermore, we have that

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Big|_{s=v_{\rho_{u}}}{\rm Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(s\Psi_{u})=\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{u}\,d\lambda_{u}<0,

where the last strict inequality holds since
S_{n}\Psi_{u}<0
for all large enough

7.1
and
7.2
together with (
13
).
The implicit function theorem shows that on an open neighborhood
{\mathcal{U}}_{0}
of

in
{\mathcal{U}}
, the function
w\mapsto v_{\rho_{w}}
for
w\in{\mathcal{U}}_{0}
is real analytic.

u\in{\mathcal{U}}
, the function
u\mapsto v_{\rho_{u}}
for
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
is real analytic,
concluding the first claim.

Let
\Psi_{\ast v}(\omega):=c_{\rho_{\ast v}}(w(\omega_{0}),w_{\ast}(\omega))
for
\omega\in\Sigma_{0}
a family of Hölder continuous potentials associated with
\rho_{\ast v}
for
v\in{\mathcal{V}}
.
Since
the function
{\mathcal{U}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and the function
{\mathcal{U}}\times{\mathcal{V}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

(u,s)\mapsto{\rm Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(s\Psi_{u}),\quad\text{and}\quad(u,v,s)\mapsto{\rm Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(v_{\rho_{u}}\Psi_{u}+s\Psi_{\ast v}),

respectively, are real analytic by Theorems
4.4
(2) and
7.14
combined with the real analyticity of
v_{\rho_{u}}
in
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
in the first claim.
Hence for
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
and for
(u,v)\in{\mathcal{U}}\times{\mathcal{V}}
, the following values depend real analytically in
u
and in
(u,v)
, respectively,

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Big|_{s=v_{\rho_{u}}}{\rm Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(s\Psi_{u})=\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{u}\,d\lambda_{u}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Big|_{s=0}{\rm Pr}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{0}}(v_{\rho_{u}}\Psi_{u}+s\Psi_{\ast v})=\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{\ast v}\,d\lambda_{u}.

Noting that

\tau(\rho_{\ast v}/\rho_{u})=\frac{\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{\ast v}\,d\lambda_{u}}{\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\Psi_{u}\,d\lambda_{u}},

by Lemma

(cf. the proof of Theorem

).
Therefore, we conclude that

is a real analytic function on
{\mathcal{U}}\times{\mathcal{V}}
and thus the second claim.
∎

8.1.
A free product group

Consider the following group with presentation

\Gamma:=\langle s_{1},s_{2}\ |\ s_{1}^{4},s_{2}^{6}\rangle,

which is isomorphic to the free product
({\mathbb{Z}}/4)\ast({\mathbb{Z}}/6)
.
Let
S:=\{s_{1},s_{1}^{-1},s_{2},s_{2}^{-1}\}
.
We define a family of presentations
\rho_{u}
of
\Gamma
into
{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
by setting

\rho_{u}(s_{1}):=A_{-u}R(\pi/2)A_{u}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{u}(s_{2}):=R(\pi/3),

where

A_{u}:=\begin{pmatrix}e^{u}&0\\
0&e^{-u}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad R(t):=\begin{pmatrix}\cos{t}&-\sin{t}\\
\sin{t}&\cos{t}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{where $u\in{\mathbb{R}}$ and $t\in[0,\pi)$}.

It indeed defines a representation since
\rho_{u}(s_{1})^{2}=\rho_{u}(s_{2})^{3}=-I
,
and further it descends to
{\rm PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.
Note that
\{\rho_{u}\}_{u\in{\mathbb{R}}}
is a real analytic family.
If
|u|
is large enough, then
\rho_{u}
defines a dominated representation (cf.
[
BPS19
, Section 5.5]
).
For
u\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\rho_{u}
is dominated,
let
\mu_{\rho_{u}}
be an associated finite Borel measure on
\partial\Gamma
(see the end of Section
7.2
), and
\tau(\rho_{u}/d_{S})
be the local intersection number of the associated tempered potential with
\rho_{u}
over the word metric
d_{S}
.
By construction, if
u
is large, then
\tau(\rho_{u}/d_{S})
is large.
Since the local intersection number is constant
\mu_{\rho_{u}}
-almost everywhere on
\partial\Gamma
(see Lemma
3.2
),
if
\tau(\rho_{u}/d_{S})\neq\tau(\rho_{u^{\prime}}/d_{S})
,
then
\mu_{\rho_{u}}
and
\mu_{\rho_{u^{\prime}}}
are mutually singular for
u,u^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

8.2.
A surface group with the translation generators

Let us consider the fundamental group of closed Riemann surface of genus
2
with the set of generators
S:=\{s_{i},s_{i}^{-1},i=1,2,3,4\}
arising from identifying pairs of opposite sides of a regular octagon in the hyperbolic plane, i.e.,

\Gamma:=\langle s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4}\ |\ s_{4}^{-1}s_{3}^{-1}s_{2}^{-1}s_{1}^{-1}s_{4}s_{3}s_{2}s_{1}\rangle.

We will refer to
S
as the set of
translation generators
.
We define a representation of
\Gamma
into
{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
which acts via
{\rm PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
and admits a hyperbolic regular octagon as a fundamental polygon.
Letting

t:=\frac{\pi}{8}\quad\text{and}\quad u:=\log\frac{\sqrt{\cos 2t}+\cos t}{\sin t},

we define a homomorphism

\rho:\Gamma\to{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})

by

\rho(s_{1}):=A_{u},\quad\rho(s_{2}):=R(3t)A_{u}R(-3t),

\rho(s_{3}):=R(6t)A_{u}R(-6t),\quad\text{and}\quad\rho(s_{4}):=R(t)A_{u}R(-t),

where we have used the same notations in the previous Section
8.1
.
One can check that
\rho
is indeed a homomorphism since the group generated by
\rho(s_{i})
,
i\in\{1,2,3,4\}
admits a regular octagon as a Dirichlet fundamental domain and they satisfy the required relation.
Moreover, the representation
\rho
is dominated;
this follows since the associated topological flow is Hölder orbit equivalent to the classical geodesic flow on
\rho(\Gamma)\backslash{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})/{\{\pm I\}}
(cf.
[
BG09
, Theorem A]
).
More concretely, one can describe a strictly invariant family of multicones of index
1
(Figure
1
; see
[
BPS19
, Section 5]
for the details).

The group
\Gamma
with the set of generators
S
admits a deterministic automaton on
8

37

.
The underlying directed graph has a unique strongly connected component with
36

We have performed a Markov chain
\{w_{n}\}_{n=0,1,\dots}
on the strongly connected component where the transition probability is given by the Parry measure; the extreme point of sample path is distributed according to a Patterson-Sullivan measure
\mu_{S}
defined in terms of the word metric for
S
(cf.
[
CF10

and Figure

(left)).
A numerical experiment shows that

\tau(\rho/d_{S})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}{\bf E}\,\log\sigma_{1}(\rho(w_{n}))\quad\text{is approximately}\quad 1.13837...,

(where
{\bf E}\,
stands for the expectation—–we compute an average over the number of samples
10^{4}
with the number of steps
n=10^{3}
).
The exponential growth rate of
(\Gamma,S)
is

v_{S}=1.94303...,

and
v_{\rho}=2
(since the exponential growth rate for the hyperbolic metric is
1
and
2\log\sigma_{1}(\rho(w))
coincides with the displacement by
\rho(w)
in the hyperbolic plane).
Computations suggest that

\frac{v_{S}}{v_{\rho}}<1.13837....

In fact, we can actually show that a finite Borel measure
\mu_{\rho}
associated with
\rho
(where we note that
\mu_{\rho}
is in the Lebesgue measure class if it is realized on the boundary of the hyperbolic plane) and a Patterson-Sullivan measure
\mu_{S}
associated with the word metric for
S
are mutually singular.
The following is a sketch of the proof:
Suppose that two measures
\mu_{\rho}
and
\mu_{S}
are mutually absolutely continuous,
then the logarithm of spectral radius for
\rho
and the stable length function are proportional on the set of conjugacy classes of
\Gamma
; this follows from
[
CT21
, Theorem 1.2]
for the pair of the hyperbolic metric and the word metric.
However, the values for the one associated with
\rho
is not arithmetic, i.e., not included in a discrete subgroup in
{\mathbb{R}}

[
Dal99
, Proposition 2.1]
whereas the values for the one associated with the word metric are contained in a discrete set of rational numbers
{\mathbb{Z}}[1/N]
for some
N\geq 1

[
BH99
, Theorem 3.17, Chapter III.
\Gamma
]
, yielding a contradiction.

Concerning the harmonic measure, we have performed the simple random walks
\{w_{n}\}_{n=0,1,\dots}
on the Cayley graph for
(\Gamma,S)
(Figure
2
(right)).
Let
\nu
be the associated harmonic measure on the boundary
\partial\Gamma
, and
\Lambda_{\rm harm}
be a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure in the class of
\check{\nu}\otimes\nu
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
(see Section
6
).
A numerical experiment provides that

\tau(\rho/d_{S}:\Lambda_{\rm harm})
is approximately
1.12909...
.

Note that the local intersection number for
\tau(\rho/d_{S}:\Lambda)
relative to an ergodic measure
\Lambda
is constant
\Lambda
-almost everywhere,
and
\tau(\rho/d_{S})=\tau(\rho/d_{S}:\Lambda_{S})
where
\Lambda_{S}
is a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure in the class of
\mu_{S}\otimes\mu_{S}
on
\partial^{2}\Gamma
.
Therefore if
\tau(\rho/d_{S})\neq\tau(\rho/d_{S}:\Lambda_{\rm harm})
,
then a Patterson-Sullivan measure
\mu_{S}
and the harmonic measure
\nu
are mutually singular on
\partial\Gamma
;
the fact itself has been shown as a special case of
[
GMM18
, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3]
.
Moreover, we note that
\mu_{\rho}
and
\nu
are mutually singular
[
KT22
, Theorem 1]
;
see also the discussion in Remark
6.2
.

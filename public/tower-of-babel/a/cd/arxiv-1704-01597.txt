Abstract
We study the partial sum operator for a Sobolev-type inner product related to the classical Gegenbauer polynomials. A complete characterization of the partial sum operator in an appropriate Sobolev space is given. Moreover, we analyze the convergence of the partial sum operators.

Introduction

Let the Sobolev-type inner product be

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{S}=\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)g(x){\rm d}\mu_{\alpha}(x)+M(f(1)g(1)+f(-1)g(-1))

\displaystyle\hphantom{\langle f,g \rangle_S=}{}+N(f^{\prime}(1)g^{\prime}(1)+f^{\prime}(-1)g^{\prime}(-1)),

(1.1)

where
M\geq 0
,
N\geq 0
, and

\displaystyle{\rm d}\mu_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(2\alpha+2)}{2^{2\alpha+1}\Gamma^{2}(\alpha+1)}\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{\alpha}{\rm d}x,\qquad\alpha>-1/2,

is the probability measure corresponding to the Gegenbauer polynomials.

Let
\{Q_{n}^{\alpha}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
be the sequence of normalized Gegenbauer–Sobolev orthonormal polynomials with respect to the inner product (
1.1
). For each appropriate function
f
, we define its sequence of Fourier–Gegenbauer–Sobolev coefficients by

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(k)=\langle f,Q_{k}^{\alpha}\rangle_{S},\qquad k=0,1,\dots,

and the
n
-th partial sum operator as

\displaystyle G_{n}f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\widehat{f}(k)Q_{k}^{\alpha}(x),\qquad n=0,1,\dots.

Given
1<p<\infty
, we say that
f\in L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})
if
f
is a measurable function in
[-1,1]
and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}=\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|f(x)|^{p}{\rm d}\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Let us define the measure
\mu_{\alpha,M}:=\mu_{\alpha}+M(\delta_{1}+\delta_{-1})
. We consider the space
W_{p}^{\alpha}
, with
1<p<\infty
, as the set of equivalence classes, with respect to the (semi)norm in
L^{p}(\mu_{\alpha,M})
, of measurable functions defined on
[-1,1]
such that there exists an element in the class
f
for which
f^{\prime}(1)
and
f^{\prime}(-1)
are defined, and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W_{p}^{\alpha}}^{p}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}^{p}+M\big(|f(1)|^{p}+|f(-1)|^{p}\big)+N\big(|f^{\prime}(1)|^{p}+|f^{\prime}(-1)|^{p}\big)<\infty.

The main target of this paper is the study of the uniform boundedness of the operators
G_{n}
. In fact, we will prove the following characterization.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1/2
,
1<p<\infty
, and
f\in W_{p}^{\alpha}
. There exists a constant
C
, independent of
n
and
f
, such that

\displaystyle\|G_{n}f\|_{W_{p}^{\alpha}}\leq C\|f\|_{W_{p}^{\alpha}}

if and only if

\displaystyle\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha+3}<p<\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha+1}.

(1.2)

The uniform boundedness of the partial sum operators for Gegenbauer polynomials in
L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})\!
was given by Pollard
[
11
]
who extended it to the Jacobi setting in
[
12
]
. A general result including weights for Jacobi expansions can be seen in
[
9
]
. In
[
3
]
, by applying the boundedness with weights of the Hilbert transform, the authors did a complete study of the boundedness of the partial sum operators related to generalized Jacobi weights. The same authors studied the generalized Jacobi weights with mass points on the interval
[-1,1]
(see
[
4
]
). The uniform boundedness with weights of the partial sum operator for the generalized Jacobi polynomials has been used to proved, using an idea dating back to J. Marcinkiewicz, some results related to interpolating polynomials (see
[
15
,
16
]
and the references in
[
10
]
).

It would be natural to consider our problem for the Jacobi weight instead of the Gegenbauer one. This extension requires some results about the corresponding Jacobi–Sobolev polynomials that are unavailable in the literature at this moment. We hope to develop these tools in a forthcoming paper to obtain a complete characterization in that case as well.

As far as we know, a complete characterization of the uniform boundedness of the partial sums in the Sobolev setting is completely new. In
[
6
]
, the authors observed that the main obstacle to analyze this problem is the lack of Christoffel–Darboux formula for Sobolev orthogonal polynomials. As a consequence of this fact, except for certain particular cases, the convergence of Fourier expansions in Sobolev orthogonal polynomials has not been resolved. For example, the particular case of the Fourier series associated to the Jacobi–Sobolev polynomials defined by the inner product

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)g(x)(1-x)^{\alpha}(1+x)^{\beta}{\rm d}x+\int_{-1}^{1}f^{\prime}(x)g^{\prime}(x)(1-x)^{\alpha+1}(1+x)^{\beta+1}{\rm d}x

was treated in
[
5
]
but, unfortunately, the given results are not completely satisfactory.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on some results about multipliers and transplantation operators for Jacobi expansions proved by Muckenhoupt and other authors in the eighties of the last century (see
[
8
]
and the references therein).

From a standard argument, the uniform boundedness of the operator
G_{n}
will imply the convergence for functions in the class
W_{p}^{\alpha}
if the polynomials form a dense class. However, the reverse implication is not true because the space
W_{p}^{\alpha}
is not complete. The density of the polynomials is contained in the next result.

Theorem 1.2
.

The set of polynomials is dense in the space
W_{p}^{\alpha}
. That is, given
f\in W_{p}^{\alpha}
, for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a polynomial
q_{n}
of degree
n
such that

\displaystyle\|f-q_{n}\|_{W_{p}^{\alpha}}<\varepsilon.

Now, from Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we deduce the convergence for functions in the class
W_{p}^{\alpha}
of the partial sums
G_{n}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
f\in W_{p}^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>-1/2
and
1<p<\infty
. If

\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha+3}<p<\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha+1},

then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|G_{n}f-f\|_{W_{p}^{\alpha}}=0.

In Section
2
we present the necessary definitions and results concerning to the Gegenbauer and Gegenbauer–Sobolev polynomials. Section
3
and Section
4
are devoted to prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, respectively.

Definitions and auxiliary results

Let
\{R_{n}^{\alpha}\}_{n\geq 0}
be the sequence of Gegenbauer polynomials given by the Rodrigues formula

\displaystyle R_{n}^{\alpha}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{2^{n}\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{-\alpha}\frac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}x^{n}}\big(\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{n+\alpha}\big).

If we call
\{B_{n}^{\alpha}\}_{n_{\geq}0}
the sequence of orthogonal polynomials with respect to (
1.1

R_{n}^{\alpha}
and

\displaystyle B_{n}^{\alpha}(x)=\frac{a_{n}(n+2\alpha+1)_{4}(-n)_{4}}{2^{6}(\alpha+2)(\alpha+3)(\alpha+1)_{4}}\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{2}R_{n-4}^{\alpha+4}(x)

\displaystyle\hphantom{B_n^{\alpha}(x)=}{}+\frac{b_{n}(n+2\alpha+1)_{2}(-n)_{2}}{2^{2}(\alpha+1)_{2}}\big(1-x^{2}\big)R_{n-2}^{\alpha+2}(x)+c_{n}R_{n}^{\alpha}(x),

(2.1)

where
(a)_{n}
is the shifted factorial (or Pochhammer symbol), defined by
(a)_{n}=\frac{\Gamma(a+n)}{\Gamma(a)}
, and

\displaystyle a_{n}=MN\frac{4(2\alpha+3)_{n}(2\alpha+3)_{n-2}}{(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)n!(n-2)!}+N\frac{2(2\alpha+3)_{n-1}}{(\alpha+1)(n-1)!},

\displaystyle b_{n}=-\frac{N}{2}\frac{(2\alpha+3)_{n-1}(n-2)(n+2\alpha+3)}{(\alpha+1)(\alpha+3)(n-1)!}-2M\frac{(2\alpha+3)_{n-2}}{n!},

\displaystyle c_{n}=1-\frac{N}{2}\frac{(2\alpha+3)_{n+1}}{(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)(\alpha+3)(n-3)!}.

Here and elsewhere we use the convention that
R_{n}^{\gamma}\equiv 0
if
n<0
.

In

]

\displaystyle\|B_{n}^{\alpha}\|_{W_{2}^{\alpha}}^{2}=2Mc_{n}^{2}+2N\left(\frac{n(n+2\alpha+1)}{2(\alpha+1)}+\frac{M(2\alpha+3)_{n}}{(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)(n-1)!}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\|B_n^\alpha\|_{W_2^\alpha}^2=}{}+\frac{\Gamma(2\alpha+2)n!}{(2n+2\alpha+1)\Gamma(n+2\alpha+1)}\left(\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)(n+2\alpha+1)_{4}a_{n}^{2}}{16(\alpha+2)^{2}(\alpha+3)^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\hphantom{\|B_n^\alpha\|_{W_2^\alpha}^2=}{}\times n(n-1)(n+2\alpha+1)_{2}b_{n}^{2}-\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)(n+2\alpha+1)_{2}a_{n}b_{n}}{2(\alpha+2)_{2}}

\displaystyle\left.\hphantom{\|B_{n}^{\alpha}\|_{W_{2}^{\alpha}}^{2}=}{}+c_{n}^{2}-2n(n-1)b_{n}c_{n}+\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)a_{n}c_{n}}{2(\alpha+2)(\alpha+3)}\right).

Then
Q_{n}^{\alpha}(x)=\lambda_{n,\alpha}B_{n}^{\alpha}(x)
, where
\lambda_{n,\alpha}^{-2}=\|B_{n}^{\alpha}\|_{W_{2}^{\alpha}}^{2}
. Now, denoting by
\{P_{n}^{\alpha}\}_{n\geq 0}
the sequence of orthonormal Gegenbauer polynomials, given by
P_{n}^{\alpha}=\beta_{n,\alpha}R_{n}^{\alpha}
with

\displaystyle\beta_{n,\alpha}^{-2}=\|R_{n}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}^{2}=\frac{\Gamma(2\alpha+2)n!}{(2n+2\alpha+1)\Gamma(n+2\alpha+1)},

from (

) we can write

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{\alpha}(x)=A_{n,4}\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{2}P_{n-4}^{\alpha+4}(x)+A_{n,2}\big(1-x^{2}\big)P_{n-2}^{\alpha+2}(x)+A_{n,0}P_{n}^{\alpha}(x),

(2.2)

where

\displaystyle A_{n,4}=\frac{a_{n}(n+2\alpha+1)_{4}(-n)_{4}}{2^{6}(\alpha+2)(\alpha+3)(\alpha+1)_{4}}\frac{\lambda_{n,\alpha}}{\beta_{n-4,\alpha+4}},\qquad A_{n,2}=\frac{b_{n}(n+2\alpha+1)_{2}(-n)_{2}}{2^{2}(\alpha+1)_{2}}\frac{\lambda_{n,\alpha}}{\beta_{n-2,\alpha+2}},

and
A_{n,0}=c_{n}\frac{\lambda_{n,\alpha}}{\beta_{n,\alpha}}
.

We consider the notations

\displaystyle g(n,J)=\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\frac{d_{j}}{(n+1)^{j}}+O\left(\frac{1}{(n+1)^{J}}\right),\qquad J\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle h(n,\alpha)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor 2\alpha+2\rfloor}\frac{D_{j}}{(n+1)^{j}}+O\left(\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2\alpha+2}}\right),\qquad\alpha>-1/2,

for some constants

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(n+a)}{\Gamma(n+b)}=n^{a-b}g(n,J),

(2.3)

for any

, we deduce in an easy way that

\displaystyle\lambda_{n,\alpha}=\begin{cases}(n+1)^{-\alpha-\frac{11}{2}}g(n,J),&M=0,\ N>0,\\
(n+1)^{-3\alpha-\frac{15}{2}}h(n,\alpha),&M>0,\ N>0,\\
(n+1)^{-\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}h(n,\alpha),&M>0,\ N=0,\end{cases}

(2.4)

for any

.

.

Let

Let
S_{n}^{\gamma}f
be the

\displaystyle S_{n}^{\gamma}f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}d_{k}^{\gamma}(f)P_{k}^{\gamma}(x),\qquad d_{k}^{\gamma}(f)=\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)P_{k}^{\gamma}(x){\rm d}\mu_{\gamma}(x).

From the main result in

.

Let

\displaystyle\big\|\big(1-(\cdot)^{2}\big)^{a}S^{\gamma}_{n}f\big\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\gamma})}\leq C\big\|\big(1-(\cdot)^{2}\big)^{a}f\big\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\gamma})}

if and only if

\displaystyle\left|a+(\gamma+1)\bigg(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)\right|<\frac{1}{4}.

Let

\displaystyle T_{d}^{\beta,\gamma}f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}d_{k}^{\gamma}(f)P_{k+d}^{\beta}(x).

The operator

Lemma 2.5

Let
\gamma>-1
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|T_{d}^{\beta,\gamma}f(x)|^{p}\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}(\beta+1/2)+b}{\rm d}x\right)^{1/p}\leq C\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|f(x)|^{p}\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}(\gamma+1/2)+b}{\rm d}x\right)^{1/p}.

The last tool that we will need for the proof of Theorem

is related to the boundedness of a specific multiplier for Gegenbauer expansions. We define the operator

\displaystyle R^{\gamma}f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{d_{k}^{\gamma}(f)}{k+1}P_{k}^{\gamma}(x).

.

Let
\gamma>-1
and

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Taking the kernel

\displaystyle L_{n}(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}Q_{k}^{\alpha}(x)Q_{k}^{\alpha}(y),

it is easy to see that

\displaystyle G_{n}f(x)=\langle L_{n}(x,y),f\rangle_{S}.

Recall that

\displaystyle\|G_{n}f\|_{W_{p}^{\alpha}}^{p}=\|G_{n}f\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}^{p}+M\big(|G_{n}f(1)|^{p}+|G_{n}f(-1)|^{p}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\|G_nf\|_{W_p^{\alpha}}^p=}{}+N\big(|(G_{n}f)^{\prime}(1)|^{p}+(G_{n}f)^{\prime}(-1)|^{p}\big).

The necessity of the condition (
1.2
) is a consequence of
[
2
, Theorem 1]
and its sufficiency will be obtained from two following propositions.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1/2
and
1<p<\infty
. If (
1.2
) holds, then

\displaystyle\|G_{n}f(x)\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}\leq C\|f\|_{{W_{p}^{\alpha}}},

(3.1)

where
C
is a constant independent of
n
and
f
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\alpha>-1/2
and
1<p<\infty
. If (
1.2
) holds, then

M\big(|G_{n}f(1)|^{p}+|G_{n}f(-1)|^{p}\big)+N\big(|(G_{n}f)^{\prime}(1)|^{p}+|(G_{n}f)^{\prime}(-1)|^{p}\big)\leq C\|f\|_{W_{p}^{\alpha}},

where
C
is a constant independent of
n
and
f
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

From Minkowski’s inequality, we know that

\displaystyle\|G_{n}f(x)\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}\leq\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\int_{-1}^{1}f(y)L_{n}(x,y){\rm d}{\mu_{\alpha}}(y)\right|^{p}{\rm d}\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\|G_nf(x)\|_{L^p({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}\leq}{}+\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|M(f(1)L_{n}(x,1)+f(-1)L_{n}(x,-1)|^{p}{\rm d}\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\|G_nf(x)\|_{L^p({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}\leq}{}+\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\left|N(f^{\prime}(1)\frac{\partial L_{n}}{\partial y}(x,1)+f^{\prime}(-1)\frac{\partial L_{n}}{\partial y}(x,-1))\right|^{p}{\rm d}\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{1/p}.

First, it will be proved that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)L_{n}(x,y){\rm d}\mu_{\alpha}(y)\right|^{p}{\rm d}\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{1/p}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}.

(3.2)

Using (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}f(y)L_{n}(x,y){\rm d}\mu_{\alpha}(y)=\sum_{j,m\in\{4,2,0\}}M_{n}^{j,m}f(x),

where

\displaystyle M_{n}^{j,m}f(x)=\int_{-1}^{1}f(y)K_{n}^{j,m}(x,y){\rm d}\mu_{\alpha}(y),

\displaystyle K_{n}^{j,m}(x,y)=\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{j/2}\big(1-y^{2}\big)^{m/2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{k,j}A_{k,m}P_{k-j}^{\alpha+j}(x)P_{k-m}^{\alpha+m}(y).

By using a standard duality argument, to deduce (
3.2
) it is enough to prove

\displaystyle\|M_{n}^{j,m}f\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}

for
m\leq j
.

By Lemma
2.1
, each operator
M_{n}^{j,m}
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle M_{n}^{j,m}f(x)=S_{0}M_{n}^{j,m,0}f(x)+S_{1}M_{n}^{j,m,1}f(x)+S_{2}M_{n}^{j,m,2}f(x),

for some nonnegative constants
S_{0}
,
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, with

\displaystyle\begin{split}&M_{n}^{j,m,s}f(x)=\int_{-1}^{1}f(y)K_{n}^{j,m,s}(x,y){\rm d}\mu_{\alpha}(y),\qquad s=0,1,2,\\
&K_{n}^{j,m,s}(x,y)=\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{j/2}\big(1-y^{2}\big)^{m/2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(k+1)^{-s}P_{k-j}^{\alpha+j}(x)P_{k-m}^{\alpha+m}(y),\qquad s=0,1,\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\big|K_{n}^{j,m,2}(x,y)\big|\leq C\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{j/2}\big(1-y^{2}\big)^{m/2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}k^{-\theta}\big|P_{k-j}^{\alpha+j}(x)\big|\big|P_{k-m}^{\alpha+m}(y)\big|,

where
\theta>1
.

From the well-known estimate (it follows from
[
14
, Theorem 7.32.2, p. 169]
)

\displaystyle|P_{n}^{\alpha}(x)|\leq C\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{-(\alpha/2+1/4)},\qquad x\in[-1,1],

with
C
a constant independent of
n
, we deduce

\displaystyle\big\|\big(1-(\cdot)^{2}\big)^{j/2}P_{n-j}^{\alpha+j}\big\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}\leq C

for
p<4(\alpha+1)/(2\alpha+1)
. In this way, applying Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\big\|M_{n}^{j,m,2}f\big\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})},

for each
p
verifying (
1.2
).

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle M_{n}^{j,j,0}f(x)=K_{\alpha}\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{j/2}S_{n-j}^{\alpha+j}g(x),

for a constant
K_{\alpha}
, with

Proof of Theorem
1.2

Proof.

Let
f\in W_{p}^{\alpha}
and
\varepsilon>0
. From
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
, we have that the space
C_{c}^{\infty}([-1,1])
is dense in
L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})
. Then, there exists a function
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}([-1,1])
such that

\displaystyle\|f-g\|_{L_{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}<\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{4},

with
\tilde{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon/(1+M+N)
. We take now a function
h\in C_{c}^{\infty}([-1,1])
that satisfies

\displaystyle\|h\|_{L_{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}<\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{4},

and

\displaystyle h(1)=f(1)-g(1),\qquad h(-1)=f(-1)-g(-1),

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(1)=f^{\prime}(1)-g^{\prime}(1),\qquad h^{\prime}(-1)=f^{\prime}(-1)-g^{\prime}(-1).

Then

\displaystyle\|f-(g+h)\|^{p}_{W_{p}^{\alpha}}=\|f-(g+h)\|^{p}_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\|f-(g+h)\|^p_{W_p^{\alpha}} =}{}+M\big(|f(1)-(g+h)(1)|^{p}+|f(-1)-(g+h)(-1)|^{p}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\|f-(g+h)\|^p_{W_p^{\alpha}} =}{}+N\big(|f^{\prime}(1)-(g+h)^{\prime}(1)|^{p}+|f^{\prime}(-1)-(g+h)^{\prime}(-1)|^{p}\big)

and

\displaystyle\|f-(g+h)\|_{W_{p}^{\alpha}}\leq\|f-g\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}+\|h\|_{L^{p}({\rm d}\mu_{\alpha})}<\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}.

On the other hand, given
\tilde{\varepsilon}
there exists a polynomial
q_{n}
of degree
n
such that

\displaystyle\|g+h-q_{n}\|_{\infty}<\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2},\qquad\|(g+h)^{\prime}-q_{n}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}<\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}.

Then,
\|f-q_{n}\|_{W_{p}}^{p}<\varepsilon
and the proof is completed.
∎

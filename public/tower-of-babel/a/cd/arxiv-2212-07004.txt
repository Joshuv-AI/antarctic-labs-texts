1.
Introduction

The theory of frames for Hilbert spaces was first introduced in 1952 by Duffin and Schaefer
[
4
]
for the study of nonharmonic series. Later, Frank and Larson
[
6
,
7
]
introduced the notion of standard frame of multipliers for a Hilbert
C^{\ast}
-module and proved some of their basic properties.
In 2008, Joita
[
9
]
proposed frames of multipliers in Hilbert pro-
C^{\ast}
-modules and showed that many properties of frames in Hilbert
C^{\ast}
-modules are valid for frames of multipliers in Hilbert modules over pro-
C^{\ast}
-algebras.

Operator frames for
B(\mathcal{H})
is a new notion of frames, which was introduced by Li and Cao
[
12
]
and was more generalized by Rossafi in
[
16
]
. For more information and properties on frames, see
[
2
,
5
,
14
]
.

The aim of this work is to introduce the notion of operator frame for the space
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
of all adjointable operators on a Hilbert pro-
C^{\ast}
-module for
\mathcal{X}
. We begin by recalling some fundamental definitions
and notations of Hilbert pro-
C^{\ast}
-modules. In Section 2, we give the definition of operator frame and some properties. In Section 3, we study the stabilty of operator frame. In Section 4, we investigate the tensor product of Hilbert pro-
C^{\ast}
-modules and we show that the tensor product of operator frames for Hilbert pro-
C^{\ast}
-modules
\mathcal{X}
and

3.
Operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})

Definition 3.1
.

A family of adjointable operators
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
on a Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-module
\mathcal{X}
over a unital pro-
C^{\ast}
-algebra is said to be an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
if there exist constants
A,B>0
such that

A\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle\leq B\langle\xi,\xi\rangle,\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X}.

(3.1)

The numbers
A
and
B
are called lower and upper bounds of the operator frame, respectively. If
A=B=\lambda
, then the operator frame is
\lambda
-tight. If
A=B=1
, then it is called a normalized tight operator frame or a Parseval operator frame. If only upper inequality of (
3.1
) holds, then
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is called an operator Bessel sequence for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
.

If the sum in the middle of (
3.1
) is convergent in norm, then the operator frame is called standard.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Hilbert pro-
C^{\ast}
-module over itself with the inner product
\langle a,b\rangle=ab^{\ast}
.
Let
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in J}
be a frame for
\mathcal{A}
with bounds
A
and
B
, respectively. For each
i\in J
, we define
T_{i}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
by
T_{i}\xi=\langle\xi,x_{i}\rangle,\;\;\forall\xi\in\mathcal{A}
. Then
T_{i}
is adjointable and
T_{i}^{\ast}a=ax_{i}
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
. We have

A\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\sum_{i\in J}\langle\xi,x_{i}\rangle\langle x_{i},\xi\rangle\leq B\langle\xi,\xi\rangle,\forall\xi\in\mathcal{A}

and so

A\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle\leq B\langle\xi,\xi\rangle,\forall\xi\in\mathcal{A}.

Thus
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is an operator frame in
\mathcal{A}
with bounds
A
and
B
, respectively.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\{W_{i}\}_{i\in J}
be a frame of submodules with respect to
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in J}
for
\mathcal{X}
. Put
T_{i}=v_{i}\pi_{W_{i}},\forall i\in J
. Then we get a sequence of operators
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
and there exist constants
A,B>0
such that

A\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\sum_{i\in J}v_{i}^{2}\langle P_{W_{i}}\xi,P_{W_{i}}\xi\rangle\leq B\langle\xi,\xi\rangle,\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X}.

So we have

A\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle\leq B\langle\xi,\xi\rangle,\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X}.

Thus the sequence
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
becomes an operator frame for
\mathcal{X}
.

With this example, a frame of submodules can be viewed as a special case of operator frames.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
be an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
with bounds
A
and
B
. Let
Q
be a bounded element in
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{X})
such that
Q=Q^{\ast}.
If

is surjective, then
\{T_{i}Q\}_{i\in J}
is an operator frame for

Proof.

Since

m\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\langle Q\xi,Q\xi\rangle\leq M\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\quad\text{for all}\quad\xi\in\mathcal{X}.

Then

Am\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}Q\xi,T_{i}Q\xi\rangle\leq BM\langle\xi,\xi\rangle,\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X}.

Hence
\{T_{i}Q\}_{i\in J}

Let
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
be an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
. Define an operator

R:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow l^{2}(\mathcal{X})\;\;by\;\;Rx=\{T_{i}x\}_{i\in J},\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

The operator
R
is called an analysis operator of the operator frame
J\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
.

The adjoint
R^{\ast}(\{x_{i}\}_{i\in J}):l^{2}(\mathcal{X})\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
of the analysis operator

is defined by

R^{\ast}(\{x_{i}\}_{i\in J})=\sum_{i\in J}T_{i}^{\ast}x_{i},\forall\{x_{i}\}_{i\in J}\in l^{2}(\mathcal{X}).

The operator

is called the synthesis operator of the operator frame
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}

By composing
R

, the frame operator

for the operator frame is given by

S(x)=R^{\ast}Rx=\sum_{i\in J}T_{i}^{\ast}T_{i}x.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
be a family of adjointable operators on a Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-module
\mathcal{X}
. Then
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
if and only if there exist positive constants
A,B>0
such that

A\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi)^{2}\leq p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle)\leq B\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi)^{2},\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X}.

(3.2)

Proof.

Suppose that
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
. Combined with the definition of an operator frame, we know that (
3.2
) holds.

Now suppose that (
3.2
) holds. We know that the frame operator
S
is positive, self-adjoint and invertible, and hence

\langle S^{\frac{1}{2}}x,S^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\rangle=\langle S\xi,\xi\rangle=\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle.

So we have
Ap(\langle\xi,\xi\rangle)\leq p(\langle S^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi,S^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\rangle)\leq Bp(\langle\xi,\xi\rangle)
for all
\xi\in\mathcal{X}.
Thus
A\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi)^{2}\leq\bar{p}_{\mathcal{Y}}(S^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi)^{2}\leq B\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi)^{2}.
According to Proposition
2.6
, we have

\|S^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\infty}^{-2}\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\langle S^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi,S^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\rangle=\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle\leq\|S^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\langle\xi,\xi\rangle,

which implies that
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume that
S
is the frame operator of an operator frame
T=\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
with bounds
A
and
B
. Then
S
is positive, self-adjoint and invertible. Moreover, we have
AI\leq S\leq BI
and the reconstruction formula

\xi=\sum_{i\in J}S^{-1}T_{i}^{\ast}T_{i}\xi,\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X}.

(3.3)

Proof.

It is clear that
S
is positive and self-adjoint. For any
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
, since
T=\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is an operator frame with bounds
A,B
, we have

\langle A\xi,\xi\rangle=A\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle=\langle S\xi,\xi\rangle\leq B\langle\xi,\xi\rangle=\langle B\xi,\xi\rangle.

This shows that

AI\leq S\leq BI,

which implies that
S
is invertible. Further, for any
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\xi=S_{T}^{-1}S_{T}\xi=S_{T}^{-1}\sum_{i\in J}T_{i}^{\ast}T_{i}\xi=\sum_{i\in J}S_{T}^{-1}T_{i}^{\ast}T_{i}\xi.

This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\varphi(\langle S_{\mathcal{A}}\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in J}\varphi(\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in J}\langle(\theta T_{i})^{\ast}(\theta T_{i})\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\langle\sum_{i\in J}(\theta T_{i})^{\ast}(\theta T_{i})\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\langle S_{\mathcal{B}}\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Perturbation and stability of operator frames for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\left\{T_{i}\right\}_{i\in J}
be an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
with bounds
A
and
B. If
\left\{R_{i}\right\}_{i\in J}\in Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
is an operator Bessel sequence with a bound
M<A
, then
\left\{T_{i}-R_{i}\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{J}}
is an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
.

Proof.

For any
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle p(\sum_{i\in J}\left\langle\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi,\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi\right\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}\{(T_{i}-R_{i})\xi\}_{i\in J}

\displaystyle\leq p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}+p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle R_{i}\xi,R_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{B}\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi)+\sqrt{M}\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{M})\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle Ap(\langle\xi,\xi\rangle)

\displaystyle\leq p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle)

\displaystyle\leq p(\sum_{i\in J}\left\langle\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi,\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi\right\rangle)+p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle R_{i}\xi,R_{i}\xi\rangle)

\displaystyle\leq p(\sum_{i\in J}\left\langle\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi,\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi\right\rangle)+Mp(\langle\xi,\xi\rangle).

Thus

(A-M)p(\langle\xi,\xi\rangle)\leq p(\sum_{i\in J}\left\langle\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi,\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi\right\rangle).

Consequently,

(A-M)^{2}\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi)^{2}\leq p(\sum_{i\in J}\left\langle\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi,\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi\right\rangle)\leq(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{M})^{2}\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi)^{2}.

Hence
\left\{T_{i}-R_{i}\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{J}}
is an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
be an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(\mathcal{X})
with bound
A
and
B
and
\left\{R_{i}\right\}_{i\in J}\in Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(\mathcal{X}).
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\left\{R_{i}\right\}_{i\in J}
is an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(\mathcal{X}).

(ii)

There exists a constant
M>0
such that for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle p(\sum_{i\in J}\left\langle\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi,\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi\right\rangle)

(4.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq M\min\left(p(\sum_{i\in J}\left\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\right\rangle),p(\sum_{i\in J}\left\langle R_{i}\xi,R_{i}\xi\right\rangle)\right).

Proof.

First, suppose that
\left\{R_{i}\right\}_{i\in J}\in Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(\mathcal{X})
is an operator frame with bounds
C
and
D
. Then for any
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle p(\sum_{i\in J}\left\langle\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi,\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi\right\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}\{(T_{i}-R_{i})\xi\}_{i\in J}

\displaystyle\leq p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}+p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle R_{i}\xi,R_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sqrt{D}\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{D}{A}}p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{\frac{D}{A}})p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Similarly, we can obtain

p(\sum_{i\in J}\left\langle\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi,\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi\right\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(1+\sqrt{\frac{B}{C}})p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle R_{i}\xi,R_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Let
M=\min\left\{1+\sqrt{\frac{D}{A}},1+\sqrt{\frac{B}{C}}\right\}
. Then (
4.1
) holds.

Conversely, suppose that (
4.1
) holds. For every
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{A}\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}\{T_{i}\xi\}_{i\in J}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}\{(T_{i}-R_{i})\xi\}_{i\in J}+\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}\{R_{i}\xi\}_{i\in J}

\displaystyle\leq p(\sum_{i\in J}\left\langle\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi,\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi\right\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}+p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle R_{i}\xi,R_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{M}p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle R_{i}\xi,R_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}+p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle R_{i}\xi,R_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{M}+1)p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle R_{i}\xi,R_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Also we have

\displaystyle p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle R_{i}\xi,R_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}\{R_{i}\xi\}_{i\in J}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}\{(T_{i}-R_{i})\xi\}_{i\in J}+\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}\{T_{i}\xi\}_{i\in J}

\displaystyle\leq p(\sum_{i\in J}\left\langle\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi,\left(T_{i}-R_{i}\right)\xi\right\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}+p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{M}p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}+p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{M}+1)p(\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus

\frac{A}{(\sqrt{M}+1)^{2}}\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi)^{2}\leq p(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{J}}\left\langle R_{i}x,R_{i}x\right\rangle)\leq B(\sqrt{M}+1)^{2}\bar{p}_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi)^{2}.

So
\left\{R_{i}\right\}_{i\in J}
is an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(\mathcal{X}).

∎

5.
Tensor product

The minimal or injective tensor product of the pro-
C^{\ast}
-algebras
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, denoted by
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
, is the completion of the algebraic tensor product
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{alg }}\mathcal{B}
with respect to the topology determined by a family of
C^{\ast}
-seminorms. Suppose that
\mathcal{X}
is a Hilbert module over a pro-
C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{Y}
is a Hilbert module over a pro-
C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
. The algebraic tensor product
\mathcal{X}\otimes_{\text{alg }}\mathcal{Y}
of
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
is a pre-Hilbert
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
-module with the action of
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
on
\mathcal{X}\otimes_{\text{alg }}\mathcal{Y}
defined by

(\xi\otimes\eta)(a\otimes b)=\xi a\otimes\eta b

for all
\xi\in\mathcal{X},\eta\in\mathcal{Y},a\in\mathcal{A}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}

and the inner product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\left(\mathcal{X}\otimes_{\text{alg }}\mathcal{Y}\right)\times\left(\mathcal{X}\otimes_{\text{alg }}\mathcal{Y}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{alg }}\mathcal{B}

is defined by

\left\langle\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1},\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2}\right\rangle=\left\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right\rangle\otimes\left\langle\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\right\rangle.

We also know that for
z=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}
in
\mathcal{X}\otimes_{alg}\mathcal{Y}
, we have
\langle z,z\rangle_{\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}}=\sum_{i,j}\langle\xi_{i},\xi_{j}\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\langle\eta_{i},\eta_{j}\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}\geq 0
and
\langle z,z\rangle_{\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}}=0
if and only if
z=0
.

The external tensor product of
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
is the Hilbert module
\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
over
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
obtained by the completion of the pre-Hilbert
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
-module
\mathcal{X}\otimes_{\text{alg }}\mathcal{Y}
.

If
P\in M(\mathcal{X})
and
Q\in M(\mathcal{Y})
then there is a unique adjointable module morphism
P\otimes Q:\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
such that
(P\otimes Q)(a\otimes b)=P(a)\otimes Q(b)
and
(P\otimes Q)^{*}(a\otimes b)=P^{*}(a)\otimes Q^{*}(b)
for all
a\in A
and all
b\in B
(see
[
9
]
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be two Hilbert pro-
C^{\ast}
-modules over unitary pro-
C^{\ast}
-algebras
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, respectively. Let
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{L_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be two operator frames for
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
with frame operators
S_{T}
and
S_{L}
and operator frame bounds
(A,B)
and
(C,D)
, respectively. Then
\{T_{i}\otimes L_{j}\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
is an operator frame for the Hibert
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
-module
\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
with frame operator
S_{T}\otimes S_{L}
and lower and upper operator frame bounds
A\otimes C
and
B\otimes D
, respectively.

Proof.

By the definition of operator frames
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{L_{j}\}_{j\in J}
, we have

A\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\sum_{i\in I}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle\leq B\langle\xi,\xi\rangle,\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X},

C\langle\eta,\eta\rangle\leq\sum_{j\in J}\langle L_{j}\eta,L_{j}\eta\rangle\leq D\langle\eta,\eta\rangle,\forall\eta\in\mathcal{Y}.

Thus

\displaystyle(A\langle\xi,\xi\rangle)\otimes(C\langle\eta,\eta\rangle)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle\otimes\sum_{j\in J}\langle L_{j}\eta,L_{j}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle\leq(B\langle\xi,\xi\rangle)\otimes(D\langle\eta,\eta\rangle),\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X},\forall\eta\in\mathcal{Y}.

So

\displaystyle(A\otimes C)(\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\otimes\langle\eta,\eta\rangle)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle\otimes\langle L_{j}\eta,L_{j}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle\leq(B\otimes D)(\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\otimes\langle\eta,\eta\rangle),\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X},\forall\eta\in\mathcal{Y}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle(A\otimes C)\langle\xi\otimes\eta,\xi\otimes\eta\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi\otimes L_{j}\eta,T_{i}\xi\otimes L_{j}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle\leq(B\otimes D)\langle\xi\otimes\eta,\xi\otimes\eta\rangle,\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X},\forall\eta\in\mathcal{Y}.

Hence for all
\xi\otimes\eta\in\mathcal{X\otimes Y}
, we have

\displaystyle(A\otimes C)\langle\xi\otimes\eta,\xi\otimes\eta\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}\langle(T_{i}\otimes L_{j})(\xi\otimes\eta),(T_{i}\otimes L_{j})(\xi\otimes\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq(B\otimes D)\langle\xi\otimes\eta,\xi\otimes\eta\rangle.

The last inequality is satisfied for every finite sum of elements in
\mathcal{X}\otimes_{alg}\mathcal{Y}
and so it is satisfied for all
z\in\mathcal{X\otimes Y}
. This shows that
\{T_{i}\otimes L_{j}\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
is an operator frame for the Hibert
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
-module
\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
with lower and upper operator frame bounds
A\otimes C
and
B\otimes D
, respectively.

By the definition of frame operators
S_{T}
and
S_{L}
, we have

S_{T}\xi=\sum_{i\in I}T_{i}^{\ast}T_{i}\xi,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X},

S_{L}\eta=\sum_{j\in J}L_{j}^{\ast}L_{j}\eta,\quad\forall\eta\in\mathcal{Y}.

Thus

\displaystyle(S_{T}\otimes S_{L})(\xi\otimes\eta)

\displaystyle=S_{T}\xi\otimes S_{L}\eta

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}T_{i}^{\ast}T_{i}\xi\otimes\sum_{j\in J}L_{j}^{\ast}L_{j}\eta

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}T_{i}^{\ast}T_{i}\xi\otimes L_{j}^{\ast}L_{j}\eta

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}(T_{i}^{\ast}\otimes L_{j}^{\ast})(T_{i}\xi\otimes L_{j}\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}(T_{i}^{\ast}\otimes L_{j}^{\ast})(T_{i}\otimes L_{j})(\xi\otimes\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}(T_{i}\otimes L_{j})^{\ast}(T_{i}\otimes L_{j})(\xi\otimes\eta).

Now by the uniqueness of frame operator, the last expression is equal to
S_{T\otimes L}(\xi\otimes\eta)
. Consequently, we have
(S_{T}\otimes S_{L})(\xi\otimes\eta)=S_{T\otimes L}(\xi\otimes\eta)
. The last equality is satisfied for every finite sum of elements in
\mathcal{X}\otimes_{alg}\mathcal{Y}
and so it is satisfied for all
z\in\mathcal{X\otimes Y}
. This shows that
(S_{T}\otimes S_{L})(z)=S_{T\otimes L}(z)
. So
S_{T\otimes L}=S_{T}\otimes S_{L}
.
∎

6.
Dual of operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})

Definition 6.1
.

Let
T=\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
be an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
. A family of operators
\tilde{T}=\{\tilde{T}_{i}\}_{i\in J}
on
\mathcal{X}
is called a dual of the operator frame
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
if they satisfy

\xi=\sum_{i\in J}T_{i}^{\ast}\tilde{T}_{i}\xi,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X}.

(6.1)

Furthermore, we call
\{\tilde{T}_{i}\}_{i\in J}
a dual frame of the operator frame
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
if
\{\tilde{T}_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is also an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
and satisfies (
6.1
).

Theorem 6.2
.

Every operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
has a dual frame.

Proof.

If
T=\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
with bounds
A
and
B
, then the operator sequence
\tilde{T}=\{T_{i}S_{T}^{-1}\}_{i\in J}
is a dual frame of
T=\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
. In fact, we have

\xi=S_{T}S_{T}^{-1}\xi=\sum_{i\in J}T_{i}^{\ast}T_{i}S_{T}^{-1}\xi=\sum_{i\in J}T_{i}^{\ast}\tilde{T}_{i}\xi,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathcal{X}.

And
\tilde{T}=\{T_{i}S_{T}^{-1}\}_{i\in J}
satisfies

A\|S_{T}\|_{\infty}^{-2}\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\sum_{i\in J}\langle\tilde{T}_{i}\xi,\tilde{T}_{i}\xi\rangle=\sum_{i\in J}\langle\tilde{T}_{i}S_{T}^{-1}\xi,\tilde{T}_{i}S_{T}^{-1}\xi\rangle\leq B\|S_{T}^{-1}\|_{\infty}^{2}\langle\xi,\xi\rangle

for all
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
.
Hence
\{T_{i}S_{T}^{-1}\}_{i\in J}

is a dual frame of
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
., which is called the canonical dual frame of
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

Assume that
T=\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
with analytic operator
R_{T}
and
\tilde{T}=\{\tilde{T}_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is a dual frame of
T
with analytic operator
R_{\tilde{T}}
. Then for any
\xi
in
\mathcal{X}
, we have

\xi=\sum_{i\in J}T_{i}^{\ast}\tilde{T}_{i}\xi=R_{T}^{\ast}R_{\tilde{T}}\xi.

This shows that every element of
\mathcal{X}
can be reconstructed with an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
and its dual frame.

Moreover, we also have another fact that for any operator
A
on
\mathcal{X}
, we get

A\xi=\sum_{i\in J}T_{i}^{\ast}\tilde{T}_{i}A\xi.

That is, an association of operator frame and its dual frame can reconstruct pointwisely an operator on
\mathcal{X}
and so we can write

A=\sum_{i\in J}T_{i}^{\ast}\tilde{T}_{i}A.

For a frame of submodules
\{W_{i}\}_{i\in J}
with respect to the family of weights
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in J}
for
\mathcal{X}
with synthesis operator
T_{W,v}
, the sequence
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in J}=\{S_{W,v}^{-1}W_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is called a dual frame of
\{W_{i}\}_{i\in J}
with the operator
S_{W,v}=T_{W,v}T_{W,v}^{\ast}
.

Theorem 6.4
.

For a frame of submodules
\{W_{i}\}_{i\in J}
with respect to the family of weights
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in J}
for
\mathcal{X}
, define
T_{i}=v_{i}S_{W,v}\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W,v}^{-1}
and
Q_{i}=v_{i}\pi_{u_{i}}S_{W,v}^{-1}
such that
S_{W,v}
and its inverse are uniformly bounded. Then
Q=\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in J}
and
T=\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
are all operator frames for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
and
Q
is a dual frame of
T
.

Proof.

Assume that
\{W_{i}\}_{i\in J}
has frame bounds
A
and
B
.

(1)

T=\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
.

For any
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in J}\langle v_{i}S_{W,v}\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W,v}^{-1}\xi,v_{i}S_{W,v}\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W,v}^{-1}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|S_{W,v}\|_{\infty}^{2}B\langle S_{W,v}^{-1}\xi,S_{W,v}^{-1}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle\leq B\|S_{W,v}\|^{2}\|_{\infty}S_{W,v}^{-1}\|_{\infty}^{2}\langle\xi,\xi\rangle.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in J}\langle T_{i}\xi,T_{i}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in J}\langle v_{i}S_{W,v}\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W,v}^{-1}\xi,v_{i}S_{W,v}\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W,v}^{-1}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\|S_{W,v}^{-1}\|_{\infty}^{-2}A\langle S_{W,v}^{-1}\xi,S_{W,v}^{-1}\xi\rangle

\displaystyle\geq A\|S_{W,v}^{-1}\|_{\infty}^{-2}\|S_{W,v}\|_{\infty}^{-2}\langle\xi,\xi\rangle.

Thus
T=\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is an operator frame.

(2)

Q=\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is also an operator frame for
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
. The proof is similar to
(1)
.

(3)

If
T_{U,v}=S_{W,v}^{-1}T_{W,v}S_{W,v}
, then
T_{U,v}^{\ast}=S_{W,v}^{-1}T_{W,v}^{\ast}S_{W,v}
and

S_{U,v}=S_{W,v}
.

It is easy to check that
\pi_{u_{i}}=S_{W,v}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W,v}
.

Thus
T_{U,v}=S_{W,v}^{-1}T_{W,v}S_{W,v},T_{U,v}^{\ast}=S_{W,v}^{-1}T_{W,v}^{\ast}S_{W,v}
and so

\displaystyle S_{U,v}

\displaystyle=T_{U,v}T_{U,v}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=S_{W,v}^{-1}T_{W,v}S_{W,v}S_{W,v}^{-1}T_{W,v}^{\ast}S_{W,v}

\displaystyle=S_{W,v}^{-1}T_{W,v}T_{W,v}^{\ast}S_{W,v}

\displaystyle=S_{W,v}^{-1}S_{W,v}S_{W,v}

\displaystyle=S_{W,v}.

Hence, for any
\xi\in\mathcal{X}
, we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in J}T_{i}^{\ast}Q_{i}\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in J}v_{i}S_{W,v}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W,v}v_{i}S_{W,v}^{-1}\pi_{W_{i}}S_{W,v}S_{W,v}^{-1}x

\displaystyle=S_{W,v}^{-1}(\sum_{i\in J}v_{i}^{2}\pi_{W_{i}}\xi)

\displaystyle=S_{W,v}^{-1}S_{W,v}\xi

\displaystyle=\xi.

This shows that
Q=\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in J}
is an dual operator frame of the operator frame
T=\{T_{i}\}_{i\in J}
.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{R_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be two operator frames respectively in
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{X})
and
Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{Y})
, with duals
\{\tilde{T_{i}}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{\tilde{R_{j}}\}_{j\in J}
respectively. Then
\{\tilde{T_{i}}\otimes\tilde{R_{j}}\}_{i,j\in I,J}
is a dual of
\{T_{i}\otimes R_{j}\}_{i,j\in I,J}
.

Proof.

By definition, for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
y\in\mathcal{Y}
, we have

x=\sum_{i\in I}T_{i}^{\ast}\tilde{T_{i}}x,

y=\sum_{j\in J}R_{j}^{\ast}\tilde{R_{j}}y

and so

(x\otimes y)=\sum_{i\in I}T_{i}^{\ast}\tilde{T_{i}}x\otimes\sum_{j\in J}R_{j}^{\ast}\tilde{R_{j}}y,

\sum_{i\in I}T_{i}^{\ast}\tilde{T_{i}}x\otimes\sum_{j\in J}R_{j}^{\ast}\tilde{R_{j}}y=\sum_{i,j\in I,J}T_{i}^{\ast}\tilde{T_{i}}x\otimes R_{j}^{\ast}\tilde{R_{j}}y,

\sum_{i\in I}T_{i}^{\ast}\tilde{T_{i}}x\otimes\sum_{j\in J}R_{j}^{\ast}\tilde{R_{j}}y=\sum_{i,j\in I,J}(T_{i}^{\ast}\otimes R_{j}^{\ast}).(\tilde{T_{i}}x\otimes\tilde{R_{j}}y),

\sum_{i\in I}T_{i}^{\ast}\tilde{T_{i}}x\otimes\sum_{j\in J}R_{j}^{\ast}\tilde{R_{j}}y=\sum_{i,j\in I,J}(T_{i}\otimes R_{j})^{\ast}.(\tilde{T_{i}}\otimes\tilde{R_{j}}).(x\otimes y).

Thus
\{\tilde{T_{i}}\otimes\tilde{R_{j}}\}_{i,j\in I,J}
is a dual of
\{T_{i}\otimes R_{j}\}_{i,j\in I,J}
.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
\{T_{i,j}\}_{0\leq i\leq n;j\in J}
be a family of operator frames with their duals
\{\tilde{T}_{i,j}\}_{0\leq i\leq n;j\in J}
for
0\leq i\leq n
. Then
\{\tilde{T}_{0,j}\otimes\tilde{T}_{1,j}\otimes......\otimes\tilde{T}_{n,j}\}_{j\in J}
is a dual of
\{T_{0,j}\otimes T_{1,j}\otimes......\otimes T_{n,j}\}_{j\in J}
.

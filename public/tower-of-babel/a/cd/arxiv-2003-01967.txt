Sobolev Lifting over Invariants
Adam PARUSIŃSKI a and Armin RAINER b

Abstract
We prove lifting theorems for complex representations V V of finite groups G G . Let σ = ( σ 1 , … , σ n ) \sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}) be a minimal system of homogeneous basic invariants and let d d be their maximal degree. We prove that any continuous map f ¯ : ℝ m → V \overline{f}\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to V such that f = σ ∘ f ¯ f=\sigma\circ\overline{f} is of class C d − 1 , 1 C^{d-1,1} is locally of Sobolev class W 1 , p W^{1,p} for all 1 ≤ p < d / ( d − 1 ) 1\leq p<d/(d-1) . In the case m = 1 m=1 there always exists a continuous choice f ¯ \overline{f} for given f : ℝ → σ ⁡ ( V ) ⊆ ℂ n f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\sigma(V)\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n} . We give uniform bounds for the W 1 , p W^{1,p} -norm of f ¯ \overline{f} in terms of the C d − 1 , 1 C^{d-1,1} -norm of f f . The result is optimal: in general a lifting f ¯ \overline{f} cannot have a higher Sobolev regularity and it even might not have bounded variation if f f is in a larger Hölder class.

Introduction

1.1
Motivation and introduction to the problem

This paper arose from our wish to understand and extend the principles behind our proof of the optimal Sobolev regularity of roots of smooth families of polynomials
[
13
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
. Here we look at this problem from a representation theoretic view point. In fact, choosing the roots of a family of monic polynomials

\displaystyle P_{a(x)}(Z)=Z^{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}(x)Z^{n-j}\vskip-4.30554pt

means solving the system of equations

\displaystyle a_{1}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}(x),

\displaystyle a_{2}(x)=\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}\leq n}\lambda_{j_{1}}(x)\lambda_{j_{2}}(x),

\displaystyle\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots

\displaystyle a_{n}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}(x)\vskip-4.30554pt

for functions
\lambda_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,n
. In other words, it means lifting the map
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
over the map

\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
the components of which are the elementary symmetric functions in
n
variables,

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{i}\leq n}X_{j_{1}}X_{j_{2}}\cdots X_{j_{i}}.

The map
\sigma
can be identified with the orbit projection of the tautological representation of the symmetric group
\operatorname{S}_{n}

on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(it acts by permuting the coordinates).

In this paper we shall solve the generalized problem for complex finite-dimensional representations of finite groups.
Let
G
be a finite group.
Let
\rho\colon G\to\operatorname{GL}(V)
be a representation of
G
on a finite-dimensional complex vector space
V
. By Hilbert’s finiteness theorem the algebra of invariant polynomials
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
is finitely generated.
Let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}
be a system of generators, we call them
basic invariants
, and let

\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
be the resulting map
\sigma\colon V\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
The map
\sigma
separates
G
-orbits and hence induces a homeomorphism between the orbit space
V/G
and the image
\sigma(V)
.
(Notice that since
G
is finite and thus all
G
-orbits are closed, there is a bijection between the orbits and
the points in the affine variety
V{/\!\!/}G
with coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
; in other words the
categorical quotient

V{/\!\!/}G
is a
geometric quotient
.)
As a consequence we may identify
V/G
with
\sigma(V)
and the canonical orbit projection
V\to V/G
with
\sigma\colon V\to\sigma(V)
. We will also write
G\circlearrowleft V
for the representation
\rho
.

The basic invariants can be chosen to be homogeneous polynomials. A system of homogeneous basic invariants is
minimal
if
none among them is superfluous. In that case their number and their degrees are uniquely determined (cf.
[
5
, p. 95]
).

Assume that a map
f\colon\Omega\to\sigma(V)
defined on some open subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is given. We will assume that
f
possesses some degree of differentiability as a map into
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
The question we will address in this paper is the following:

How
differentiable
can lifts of
f
over
\sigma
be? By a
lift of
f
over
\sigma
we mean a map

\overline{f}\colon\Omega\to V
such that
f=\sigma\circ\overline{f}
.

Simple examples show that, in general, a big loss of regularity occurs from
f
to lifts of
f
. We will determine the optimal regularity of lifts among the Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}
under minimal differentiability
requirements on
f
. In particular, the optimal
p>1
will be determined as an explicit function of the maximal homogeneity degree
of the basic invariants.

Note that the results do not depend on the choice of the basic invariants since any two choices differ by a polynomial
diffeomorphism.

Our results could be useful in connection with the orbit space reduction of equivariant dynamical system
for lifting the solutions from orbit space (even though it is not clear when a lifted solution solves the
original differential equation).
Another application to multi-valued Sobolev functions is discussed at the end of the paper.

1.2
The main results

The first result concerns the lifting of curves. We recall that, since
G
is finite, each continuous
a\colon I\to\sigma(V)
, where
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is an interval,
has a continuous lift
\overline{a}\colon I\to V
, by
[
9
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
G\circlearrowleft V
be a representation of
G
on a finite-dimensional
complex vector space
V
. Let
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
be a (minimal) system of homogeneous
basic invariants of degrees
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
and set
d=\max_{i}d_{i}
.
Let
a\in C^{d-1,1}([\alpha,\beta],\sigma(V))
be a curve
defined on an open bounded interval

(\alpha,\beta)
with values in
\sigma(V)
.
Then each continuous lift
\overline{a}\colon(\alpha,\beta)\to V
of
a
over
\sigma
is absolutely continuous and
belongs to
W^{1,p}((\alpha,\beta),V)

with

\displaystyle\|\overline{a}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((\alpha,\beta))}\leq C(G\circlearrowleft V,(\beta-\alpha),p)\,\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|a_{j}\|^{1/d_{j}}_{C^{d-1,1}([\alpha,\beta])}

(1.1)

for all
1\leq p<d/(d-1)
,
where
C
is a constant which depends only on the representation
G\circlearrowleft V
, the length of the interval
(\alpha,\beta)
, and
p
.

The conclusion of the theorem is in general optimal among Sobolev spaces, the differentiability assumption on
a
is best possible;
see Remark
3.2
.
Here and below we use the notation

\displaystyle C^{d-1,1}([\alpha,\beta],\sigma(V)):=C^{d-1,1}([\alpha,\beta],\mathbb{C}^{n})\cap\sigma(V)^{(\alpha,\beta)},

the Hölder class
C^{d-1,1}
is defined in Section
2
.

Remark 1.2
.

(
a
) In general the constant in (
1.1
) is of the form

\displaystyle C(G\circlearrowleft V,p)\,\max\big\{1,(\beta-\alpha)^{1/p},(\beta-\alpha)^{-1+1/p}\big\}.

(
b
) If the curve
a
starts, ends, or passes through
0
(that is the most singular point in
\sigma(V)
),
then the constant in (
1.1
) is of the form

\displaystyle C(G\circlearrowleft V,p)\,\max\big\{1,(\beta-\alpha)^{1/p}\big\}.

(1.2)

(
c
) If the representation is coregular, then for all
a
satisfying the assumptions of
Theorem
1.1
the constant is of the form (
1.2
).
A representation
G\circlearrowleft V
is called
coregular
if
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
is isomorphic to a polynomial algebra, i.e.,
there is a system of basic invariants without polynomial relations among them. By the Shephard–Todd–Chevalley theorem
[
2
,
19
,
20
]
,
this is the case if and only if
G
is generated by pseudoreflections.

(
d
) The constant is also of the form (
1.2
)
if the curve
a
satisfies
a^{(j)}(\alpha)=a^{(j)}(\beta)=0
for all
j=1,\dots,d-1
.

Question 1.3
.

The constant in (
1.1
) tends to infinity as
p\to d/(d-1)=:d^{\prime}
.
Our proof yields that it blows up like a power of
(d^{\prime}-p)^{-1/p}
, since we have to iterate the
inequality (
2.1
) several times when we pass from
L^{d^{\prime}}_{w}
-(quasi)norm to
L^{p}
-norm.
This is necessary, since the former is not
\sigma
-additive.
We expect that the asymptotic behavior of the constant as
p\to d^{\prime}
is actually better:

Is the constant actually
O\big((d^{\prime}-p)^{-1/p}\big)
as
p\to d^{\prime}
?
Can one replace the
L^{p}
-norm of
\overline{a}^{\prime}
by the
L^{d^{\prime}}_{w}
-
(
quasi
)
norm in (
1.1
)?

The lifting of mappings defined in open domains of dimension
m>1
essentially
admits the same regularity as for curves, provided that continuous lifting is possible.
However, there are well-known topological obstructions for continuous lifting in general. We will prove the following

Theorem 1.4
.

In the setting of Theorem
1.1

let
f\in C^{d-1,1}\big(\overline{\Omega},\sigma(V)\big)
,
where
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is an open bounded box
\Omega=I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{m}
.
Then each continuous lift
\overline{f}\colon U\to V
of
f
over
\sigma

defined on an open subset
U\subseteq\Omega

belongs to
W^{1,p}(U,V)
for all
1\leq p<d/(d-1)

and satisfies

\displaystyle\big\|\nabla\overline{f}\big\|_{L^{p}(U)}\leq C(G\circlearrowleft V,\Omega,m,p)\,\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|f_{j}\|^{1/d_{j}}_{C^{d-1,1}(\overline{\Omega})}

(1.3)

for all
1\leq p<d/(d-1)
,
where
C
is a constant which depends only on the representation
G\circlearrowleft V
, on
\Omega
,
m
, and
p
.

The case
U=\Omega
is not excluded!
It is clear that Theorem
1.4
implies a version of the statement, where

\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is any bounded open set,

U\Subset\Omega
is relatively compact open in
\Omega
,
and the constant also depends on
U
(or more precisely on a cover of
U
by boxes contained in
\Omega
).
Concerning a global result we have the following

Remark 1.5
.

If
G\circlearrowleft V
is coregular, then Theorem
1.4
holds as stated for any bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
.

When continuous lifting is impossible,
we expect that a general
BV
-lifting result is true analogous to the existence of
BV
-roots
for smooth polynomials proved in
[
17
]
. We shall not pursue that question in this paper.

1.3
Linearly reductive groups

An algebraic group
G
is called
linearly reductive
if for each rational representation

V
and each subrepresentation
W\subseteq V
there is a subrepresentation
W^{\prime}\subseteq V
such that

V=W\oplus W^{\prime}
.

For rational representations of linearly reductive groups
G
Hilbert’s finiteness theorem is true,
that is the algebra of
G
-invariant polynomials
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
is finitely generated. Let
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
be a system of generators.
Then the map
\sigma\colon V\to\sigma(V)\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
can be identified with the morphism
V\to V{/\!\!/}G
induced by the
inclusion
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}\to\mathbb{C}[V]
; the
categorical quotient

V{/\!\!/}G
is the affine variety with
coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
. In general
V{/\!\!/}G
is not a
geometric quotient
, that is the

G
-orbits in
V
are not in a one-to-one correspondence with the points in
V{/\!\!/}G
. In fact,
for every point
z\in V{/\!\!/}G
there is a unique closed orbit in the fiber
\sigma^{-1}(z)
which lies in the
closure of every other orbit in this fiber.

In this setting it is not clear if a continuous curve in
\sigma(V)
admits a continuous lift to
V
. The notion of
stability
in geometric invariant theory provides a remedy.
A point
v\in V
is called
stable
if the orbit
Gv
is closed and the isotropy group

G_{v}=\{g\in G\colon gv=v\}
is finite.
The set
V^{s}
of stable points in
V
is
G
-invariant and open in
V
,
and its image
\sigma(V^{s})
is open in
V{/\!\!/}G\cong\sigma(V)
(cf.
[
11
, Proposition 5.15]
).
The restriction
\sigma\colon V^{s}\to\sigma(V^{s})
of the map
\sigma
provides a one-to-one correspondence
between points in
\sigma(V^{s})\cong V^{s}/G
and
G
-orbits in
V^{s}
, that is
V^{s}/G
is a geometric quotient.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
a\colon I\to\sigma(V^{s})
, where
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is an open interval, be continuous.
Then
a
has a continuous lift
\overline{a}\colon I\to V^{s}
.

Proof.

For every
v\in\sigma^{-1}(a(I))
there is a local continuous lift
\overline{a}_{v}
of
a
defined on some
open subinterval
I_{v}
of
I
with
\overline{a}_{v}(t_{v})=v
for some point
t_{v}\in I_{v}
. This follows from the
lifting theorem
[
9
, Theorem 5.1]
, since locally at any
v
the problem can be reduced to the
slice representation of the isotropy group
G_{v}
which is finite (cf. Theorem
4.2
).
Now each continuous lift
\overline{a}
of
a
defined on a proper subinterval
J
of
I
has an extension to a larger interval
J^{\prime}\subseteq I
. Thus there is a continuous lift on
I
.
Indeed, say the right endpoint
t_{1}
of
J
lies in
I
.
There is continuous lift
\overline{a}_{v}\colon I_{v}\to V^{s}
for
v\in\sigma^{-1}(a(t_{1}))
.
Choose
t_{0}\in J\cap I_{v}
and
g\in G
such that
\overline{a}(t_{0})=g\overline{a}_{v}(t_{0})
.
Then
g\overline{a}_{v}
extends the continuous lift
\overline{a}
beyond
t_{1}
.
∎

As a corollary of Theorem
1.1
we obtain

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
G
be a linearly reductive group and let
G\circlearrowleft V
be a rational representation of
G
on a finite-dimensional
complex vector space
V
. Let
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
be a (minimal) system of homogeneous
basic invariants of degrees
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
and set
d=\max_{i}d_{i}
.
Let
a\in C^{d-1,1}([\alpha,\beta],\sigma(V^{s}))
be a curve defined on a compact interval with

a([\alpha,\beta])\subseteq\sigma(V^{s})
.
Then there exists an absolutely continuous lift

\overline{a}\colon[\alpha,\beta]\to V^{s}
of
a
over
\sigma
which
belongs to
W^{1,p}([\alpha,\beta],V^{s})

with

\displaystyle\|\overline{a}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}([\alpha,\beta])}\leq C(G\circlearrowleft V,[\alpha,\beta],p)\,\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|a_{j}\|^{1/d_{j}}_{C^{d-1,1}([\alpha,\beta])}

(1.4)

for all
1\leq p<d/(d-1)
.

Proof.

Since the lifting problem can be reduced to the slice representations (cf. Theorem
4.2
and Lemma
4.5
), and for all
v\in V^{s}

the isotropy group
G_{v}
is finite, Theorem
1.1
implies that for all
v\in\sigma^{-1}(a([\alpha,\beta])

there exists a local absolutely continuous lift
\overline{a}_{v}
of
a
defined on a subinterval
I_{v}
of
[\alpha,\beta]
which is open in the relative topology on
[\alpha,\beta]
such that

\displaystyle\|\overline{a}_{v}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(I_{v})}\leq C(G\circlearrowleft V,|I_{v}|,p)\,\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|a_{j}\|^{1/d_{j}}_{C^{d-1,1}(\overline{I}_{v})},\qquad 1\leq p<\frac{d}{d-1},

and there is a point
t_{v}\in I_{v}
with
\overline{a}_{v}(t_{v})=v
. By compactness, there is a finite collection of local lifts which cover
[\alpha,\beta]
.
It is then easy to glue these pieces (after applying fixed transformations from
G
)
to an absolutely continuous lift
\overline{a}
defined on
[\alpha,\beta]
and satisfying (
1.4
).
∎

For a mapping
f
defined on a compact subset
K
of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
f(K)\subseteq\sigma(V^{s})
the situation is
more complicated. We can apply Theorem
1.4
to the slice representations at any point
v\in V^{s}
.
But it is not clear if these local (and partial) lifts can be glued together in a continuous fashion.

1.4
Polar representations

More can be said for polar representations (which include e.g. the adjoint actions). The following results can be found in
[
3
]
.
Let
G
be a linearly reductive group and let
G\circlearrowleft V
be a representation of
G
on a finite-dimensional
complex vector space
V
.
Let
v\in V
be such that
Gv
is closed and consider the linear subspace

\Sigma_{v}=\{x\in V\colon\mathfrak{g}x\subseteq\mathfrak{g}v\}
, where
\mathfrak{g}
denotes the Lie algebra of
G
.
All orbits that intersect
\Sigma_{v}
are closed, whence
\dim\Sigma_{v}\leq\dim V{/\!\!/}G
.
The representation
G\circlearrowleft V
is said to be
polar
if there exists
v\in V
with closed orbit
Gv

and
\dim\Sigma_{v}=\dim V{/\!\!/}G
. Then
\Sigma_{v}
is called a
Cartan subspace
of
V
.
Any two Cartan subspaces are
G
-conjugate. Let us fix one Cartan space
\Sigma
.
All closed orbits in
V
intersect
\Sigma
.

The
Weyl group

W
is defined by
W=N_{G}(\Sigma)/Z_{G}(\Sigma)
, where
N_{G}(\Sigma)=\{g\in G\colon g\Sigma=\Sigma\}
is the
normalizer and
Z_{G}(\Sigma)=\{g\in G\colon gx=x\text{ for all }x\in\Sigma\}
is the centralizer of
\Sigma
in
G
.
The Weyl group is finite and the intersection of any closed
G
-orbit in
V
with the Cartan subspace is precisely
one
W
-orbit.
The ring
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
is isomorphic via restriction to the ring
\mathbb{C}[\Sigma]^{W}
.
If
G
is connected, then
W
is a pseudoreflection group and hence
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}\cong\mathbb{C}[\Sigma]^{W}
is a polynomial ring,
by the Shephard–Todd–Chevalley theorem
[
2
,
19
,
20
]
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
G\circlearrowleft V
be a polar representation of a linearly reductive group
G
.
Let
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
be a (minimal) system of homogeneous
basic invariants of degrees
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
and set
d=\max_{i}d_{i}
.

1.

Let
a\in C^{d-1,1}([\alpha,\beta],\sigma(V))
be a curve
defined on an open bounded interval

(\alpha,\beta)
with values in
\sigma(V)
. Then there exists an absolutely continuous lift
\overline{a}\colon(\alpha,\beta)\to V
of
a

over
\sigma
which belongs to
W^{1,p}((\alpha,\beta),V)
for all
1\leq p<d/(d-1)
and satisfies (
1.1
).

2.

Let
f\in C^{d-1,1}(\overline{\Omega},\sigma(V))
,
where
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is an open bounded box
\Omega=I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{m}
.
Each continuous lift
\overline{f}
defined in an open subset
U\subseteq\Omega
with values in a Cartan subspace
\Sigma

is of class
W^{1,p}
on
U
for all
1\leq p<d/(d-1)
and satisfies (
1.3
).

3.

In the case that
G
is connected the constant in (
1.1
) is of the form (
1.2
)
and
\Omega
can be any bounded Lipschitz domain.

Proof.

Apply Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
to the Weyl group
W
acting on a Cartan subspace
\Sigma
.
If
G
is connected, then
W\circlearrowleft\Sigma
is coregular, so (3) follows from Remarks
1.2
and
1.5
.
∎

1.5
A related problem

In an analogous way one may consider the case that
V
is a
real
finite-dimensional vector space
and
\rho\colon G\to\operatorname{O}(V)
is an orthogonal representation of a finite group.
Again the algebra of
G
-invariant polynomials
\mathbb{R}[V]^{G}
is finitely generated, and a system of basic invariants
\sigma
allows us to
identify
\sigma(V)
with the orbit space
V/G
. In this case
\sigma(V)
is a semialgebraic subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In that setting the problem
was solved in
[
14
]
:

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
G\circlearrowleft V
be an orthogonal representation of
G
on a finite-dimensional
real vector space
V
. Let
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
be a (minimal) system of homogeneous
basic invariants of degrees
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
and set
d=\max_{i}d_{i}
.

1.

Let
a\in C^{d-1,1}([\alpha,\beta],\sigma(V))
.
Then each continuous lift
\overline{a}\colon(\alpha,\beta)\to V
of
a
over
\sigma
belongs to
W^{1,\infty}((\alpha,\beta),V)
with

\displaystyle\|\overline{a}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}((\alpha,\beta))}\leq C(G\circlearrowleft V,(\beta-\alpha))\,\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|a_{j}\|^{1/d_{j}}_{C^{d-1,1}([\alpha,\beta])}.

Every continuous curve in
\sigma(V)
has a continuous lift.

2.

Let
f\in C^{d-1,1}(\overline{\Omega},\sigma(V))
,
where
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is open and bounded.
Then each continuous lift
\overline{f}\colon U\to V
of
f
over
\sigma
defined on an open subset

U\subseteq\Omega
belongs to
W^{1,\infty}(U,V)
with

\displaystyle\|\nabla\overline{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq C(G\circlearrowleft V,\Omega,U,m)\,\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|a_{j}\|^{1/d_{j}}_{C^{d-1,1}(\overline{\Omega})}.

In the special case of the tautological representation of
\operatorname{S}_{n}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
this corresponds to the problem
of choosing the roots of
hyperbolic
polynomials, i.e., monic polynomials all roots of which are real;
see
[
13
]
.

The main difference between the complex and the real problem is that in the latter case
the map
v\mapsto\langle v,v\rangle=\|v\|^{2}
is an invariant polynomial which may be taken without loss of generality
as a basic invariant and thus as a component of the map
\sigma
. The key is that this basic invariant
dominates all the others, by homogeneity,

\displaystyle|\sigma_{j}(v)|\leq\max_{\|w\|=1}|\sigma_{j}(w)|\,\|v\|^{d_{j}}.

Even though we can always choose an invariant Hermitian inner product in
the complex case (by averaging over
G
) and hence assume that the representation is unitary, the
invariant form
v\mapsto\|v\|^{2}
is not a member of
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
.
The fact that there is no invariant that dominates all others makes the complex case much more difficult.

1.6
Elements of the proof

We briefly describe the strategy of the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The basic building block of the proof is that the result holds for finite rotation groups
C_{d}
in
\mathbb{C}
,
where
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{C}]^{C_{d}}
is generated by
z\mapsto z^{d}
and a lift of a map
f
is a solution of the equation

z^{d}=f
. This follows from
[
6
]
.
Among all representations of finite groups
G
of order
|G|
it is the one
with the worst loss of
regularity, since in general
d\leq|G|
, by Noether’s degree bound, and equality can only happen for
cyclic groups (see Section
3
).

In the general case we first observe that evidently one may reduce to the case that the linear subspace

V^{G}
of invariant vectors is trivial. Then
Luna’s slice theorem (see Theorem
4.2
) allows us to reduce the problem locally to the
slice representation
G_{v}\circlearrowleft N_{v}
of the isotropy group
G_{v}=\{g\in G\colon gv=v\}
on
N_{v}
, where

T_{v}V\cong T_{v}(Gv)\oplus N_{v}
is a
G_{v}
-splitting. Since in our case
G
is finite, we have
N_{v}\cong V
.
The assumption
V^{G}=\{0\}
entails that for all
v\in V\setminus\{0\}
the isotropy group
G_{v}

is a proper subgroup of
G
which suggests to use induction.

For this induction scheme to work we need that the slice reduction is uniform in the sense that it
does not depend on the parameter
t
of the curve
a
in
\sigma(V)\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We achieve this by considering the curve

\displaystyle\underline{a}=\big(a_{k}^{-d_{1}/d_{k}}a_{1},\dots,a_{k}^{-d_{n}/d_{k}}a_{n}\big),\qquad\text{when}\quad a_{k}\neq 0,

and the compactness of the set of all
\underline{a}\in\sigma(V)
such that
|\underline{a}_{j}|\leq 1
for all
j=1,\dots,n

and
\underline{a}_{k}=1
.
Let us emphasize that hereby we use a fixed continuous selection
\hat{a}_{k}
of the multi-valued function
a_{k}^{1/d_{k}}
which is absolutely continuous by the result for the rotation group
C_{d_{k}}\circlearrowleft\mathbb{C}
.

If
a\in C^{d-1,1}([\alpha,\beta],\sigma(V))
and
t_{0}\in(\alpha,\beta)
is such that
a(t_{0})\neq 0
, then we
choose
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}

dominant
in the sense that

\displaystyle\big|a_{k}^{1/d_{k}}(t_{0})\big|=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\big|a_{j}^{1/d_{j}}(t_{0})\big|\neq 0.

It is easy to extend the lifts to the points, where
a
vanishes, so we will not discuss them here. We work on a small interval
I
containing
t_{0}
such that
for all

j=1,\dots,n
and
s=1,\dots,d-1
,

\displaystyle\big\|a_{j}^{(s)}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq C(d)|I|^{-s}|a_{k}(t_{0})|^{d_{j}/d_{k}},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}_{I}\big(a_{j}^{(d-1)}\big)\leq C(d)|I|^{-d}|a_{k}(t_{0})|^{d_{j}/d_{k}}.

This can be achieved by choosing the interval
I
in such a way that
t_{0}\in I\subseteq(\alpha,\beta)
and

\displaystyle M|I|+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big\|\big(a_{j}^{1/d_{j}}\big)^{\prime}\big\|_{L^{1}(I)}\leq B|a_{k}(t_{0})|^{1/d_{k}},

where
B
is a suitable constant which depends only on the representation and the constant
M
depends on
the representation and the curve
a
. Notice that here we use again absolute continuity of radicals (i.e., the result for
complex rotation groups).
Uniform slice reduction allows us to switch to a reduced curve
b\colon I\to\tau(W)
of class
C^{d-1,1}
,
where
H\circlearrowleft W
is a slice representation of
G\circlearrowleft V
and the map
\tau=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{m})
consists
of a system of homogeneous generators for
\mathbb{C}[W]^{H}
.
For convenience we will refer to the tuple
(a,I,t_{0},k;b)
as
reduced admissible data
for
G\circlearrowleft V
.

The core of the proof (see Proposition
8.2
) is to show that, if
(a,I,t_{0},k;b)
is reduced admissible data for
G\circlearrowleft V
, then
every continuous lift
\overline{b}\colon I\to W
of
b
is absolutely continuous and satisfies

\displaystyle\big\|\overline{b}^{\prime}\big\|_{L^{p}(I)}\leq C(d,p)\,\bigg(\big\||I|^{-1}|a_{k}(t_{0})|^{1/d_{k}}\big\|_{L^{p}(I)}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\big\|(b_{i}^{1/e_{i}})^{\prime}\big\|_{L^{p}(I)}\bigg)

for all
1\leq p<d/(d-1)
, where
e_{i}=\deg\tau_{i}
. This is done by induction on the group order
and involves showing that the set of points
t
in
I
, where
b(t)\neq 0
, can be covered by a special countable
collection of intervals on which
b
defines reduced admissible data for
H\circlearrowleft W
.
The difficult part is to assure that each point is covered by
at most two intervals in the collection (see Proposition
7.1
)
which is needed for gluing the local
L^{p}
-estimates to a global estimate on
I
.
It would suffice that each point lies in no more than a uniform finite number of intervals,
but the crucial thing is that the intervals must not be shrunk (see Remark
7.2
).

1.7
An application:
{\boldsymbol{Q}}
-valued functions

In Section
10
we explore an interesting connection between invariant theory and the theory of
Q
-valued functions. These are functions with values in the metric space of unordered
Q
-tuples of points in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
(or
\mathbb{C}^{n}
). There is a natural one-to-one correspondence between unordered
Q
-tuples of points in

\mathbb{K}^{n}
(where
\mathbb{K}
stands for
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
) and the
n
-fold direct sum of the tautological representation of the symmetric
group
S_{Q}
on
\mathbb{K}^{Q}
.
Using the theory of
Q
-valued Sobolev functions rooted in variational calculus,
cf.
[
1
]
and
[
4
]
, we will show that our main results
entail optimal
multi-valued
Sobolev lifting theorems. Thanks to the multi-valuedness there are
no topological obstructions for continuity.

2
Function spaces

In this section we fix notation for function spaces and recall well-known facts.

2.1
Hölder spaces

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded. We denote by
C^{0}(\Omega)
the space of continuous complex valued functions on
\Omega
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
(and multi-indices
\gamma
) we set

\displaystyle C^{k}(\Omega)=\big\{f\in\mathbb{C}^{\Omega}\colon\partial^{\gamma}f\in C^{0}(\Omega),0\leq|\gamma|\leq k\big\},

\displaystyle C^{k}(\overline{\Omega})=\big\{f\in C^{k}(\Omega)\colon\partial^{\gamma}f\text{ has a continuous extension to }\overline{\Omega},0\leq|\gamma|\leq k\big\}.

For
\alpha\in(0,1]
a function
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
belongs to
C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
if it is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous

in
\Omega
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\operatorname{H\ddot{o}ld}_{\alpha,\Omega}(f):=\sup_{x,y\in\Omega,\,x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}<\infty.

If
f
is
Lipschitz
, i.e.,
f\in C^{0,1}\big(\overline{\Omega}\big)
, we write

\operatorname{Lip}_{\Omega}(f):=\operatorname{H\ddot{o}ld}_{1,\Omega}(f)
. We define

\displaystyle C^{k,\alpha}\big(\overline{\Omega}\big)=\big\{f\in C^{k}\big(\overline{\Omega}\big)\colon\partial^{\gamma}f\in C^{0,\alpha}\big(\overline{\Omega}\big),|\gamma|\leq k\big\},

which is a Banach space when provided with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}:=\max_{|\gamma|\leq k}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\big|\partial^{\gamma}f(x)\big|+\max_{|\gamma|=k}\operatorname{H\ddot{o}ld}_{\alpha,\Omega}\big(\partial^{\gamma}f\big).

2.2
Lebesgue spaces and weak Lebesgue spaces

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Then
L^{p}(\Omega)
is the Lebesgue space with respect to the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{n}
.
For Lebesgue measurable sets
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote by

\displaystyle|E|=\mathcal{L}^{n}(E)

the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
E
.
Let
p^{\prime}:=p/(p-1)
denote the conjugate exponent of
p

with the convention
1^{\prime}:=\infty
and
\infty^{\prime}:=1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let us assume that
\Omega
is bounded.
The
weak
L^{p}
-space

L_{w}^{p}(\Omega)
is the space of all measurable functions
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p,w,\Omega}:=\sup_{r>0}\big(r\,|\{x\in\Omega\colon|f(x)|>r\}|^{1/p}\big)<\infty.

It will be convenient to
normalize
:

\displaystyle\|f\|^{*}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}:=|\Omega|^{-1/p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\|f\|^{*}_{p,w,\Omega}:=|\Omega|^{-1/p}\|f\|_{p,w,\Omega}.

Note that
\|1\|^{*}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\|1\|^{*}_{p,w,\Omega}=1
.
For
1\leq q<p<\infty
we have (cf.
[
7
, Exercise 1.1.11]
)

\displaystyle\|f\|^{*}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|^{*}_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\|f\|^{*}_{q,w,\Omega}\leq\|f\|^{*}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\bigg(\frac{p}{p-q}\bigg)^{1/q}\|f\|^{*}_{p,w,\Omega}

(2.1)

and hence

L^{p}(\Omega)\subseteq L_{w}^{p}(\Omega)\subseteq L^{q}(\Omega)\subseteq L_{w}^{q}(\Omega)

with strict inclusions.

We remark that
\|\cdot\|_{p,w,\Omega}
is only a quasinorm: the triangle inequality fails, but for

f_{j}\in L_{w}^{p}(\Omega)

we still have

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}f_{j}\bigg\|_{p,w,\Omega}\leq m\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{p,w,\Omega}.

There exists a norm equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{p,w,\Omega}
which makes
L_{w}^{p}(\Omega)
into a Banach space if
p>1
.

The
L^{p}_{w}
-quasinorm is
\sigma
-subadditive: if

\Omega=\bigcup\Omega_{j}
is a countable open cover, then

\displaystyle\|f\|^{p}_{p,w,\Omega}\leq\sum_{j}\|f\|^{p}_{p,w,\Omega_{j}}\qquad\text{for every}\quad f\in L^{p}_{w}(\Omega).

But it is not
\sigma
-additive.

2.3
Sobolev spaces

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
we consider the Sobolev space

\displaystyle W^{k,p}(\Omega)=\big\{f\in L^{p}(\Omega)\colon\partial^{\alpha}f\in L^{p}(\Omega),\,0\leq|\alpha|\leq k\big\},

where
\partial^{\alpha}f
denote distributional derivatives, with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

On bounded intervals
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
the Sobolev space
W^{1,1}(I)

coincides with the space
AC(I)
of absolutely continuous functions on
I

if we identify each
W^{1,1}
-function with its unique continuous representative.
Recall that a function
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
on an open subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}

is absolutely continuous (
AC
) if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that
for every finite collection of non-overlapping intervals
(a_{i},b_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,n
, with

[a_{i},b_{i}]\subseteq\Omega
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}-b_{i}|<\delta\implies\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f(a_{i})-f(b_{i})|<\epsilon.

Notice that
W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\cong C^{0,1}\big(\overline{\Omega}\big)
on Lipschitz domains (or more generally quasiconvex domains)
\Omega
.

We shall also use
W^{k,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}
,
AC_{\operatorname{loc}}
, etc. with the obvious meaning.

2.4
Vector valued functions

For our problem we need to consider mappings of Sobolev regularity with values in a finite-dimensional complex vector space
V
.
Let us fix a basis
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
of
V
and hence a linear isomorphism
\varphi\colon V\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We say that a mapping
f\colon\Omega\to V
is of Sobolev class
W^{k,p}
if
\varphi\circ f
is of class
W^{k,p}
.
The space
W^{k,p}(\Omega,V)
of all such mappings does not depend on the choice of the basis of
V
.

For
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
we set

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}^{n})}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|f_{j}\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}.

(2.2)

If
f\in W^{k,p}(\Omega,V)
,
f\neq 0
, and
\varphi,\psi\colon V\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
are two different basis isomorphisms, then

\displaystyle c\leq\frac{\|\varphi\circ f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}^{n})}}{\|\psi\circ f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}^{n})}}\leq C

for positive constants
c,C>0
which depend only on the linear isomorphism
\varphi\circ\psi^{-1}
. We will denote by
{\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega,V)}}
or simply
{\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}}
any of the equivalent norms

\|\varphi\circ f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}^{n})}
.

Now suppose that we have a representation
\rho\colon G\to\operatorname{GL}(V)
of a finite group
G
on

V
. By fixing a Hermitian inner product on
V
and averaging it over
G
we obtain a Hermitian inner product
with respect to which the action of
G
is unitary. We could equivalently define

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}=\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega,V)}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\bigg)^{1/p},

where
\|\cdot\|
is the norm associated with the
G
-invariant Hermitian inner product. In that case
\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega,V)}
is
G
-invariant.

2.5
Extension lemma

The following extension lemma simply follows from the
\mathbb{C}
-valued version proved in
[
16
]
.
Similar versions can be found in
[
15
, Lemma 2.1]

and
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space.
Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be open and bounded, let
f\colon\Omega\to V
be continuous,
p\geq 1
,
and set
\Omega_{0}:=\{t\in\Omega\colon f(t)\neq 0\}
.
Assume that
f|_{\Omega_{0}}\in AC_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega_{0},V)
and
f|_{\Omega_{0}}^{\prime}\in L^{p}(\Omega_{0},V)
.
Then the distributional derivative of
f
in
\Omega
is a measurable function
f^{\prime}\in L^{p}(\Omega,V)
and

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,V)}=\|f|_{\Omega_{0}}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{0},V)},

where the
L^{p}
-norms are computed with respect to a fixed basis isomorphism.

3
Finite rotation groups in
\mathbb{C}

Let
C_{d}\cong\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
denote the cyclic group of order
d
and consider its standard action on
\mathbb{C}
by rotation.
Then
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{C}]^{C_{d}}
is generated by
\sigma(z)=z^{d}
. A lift over
\sigma
of a function
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
is a solution of the
equation
z^{d}=f
.

The solution of the lifting problem in this simple example is completely understood. We shall see that the general
solution is based on this prototypical case. Interestingly, it is also the case with the worst loss of regularity.

The following theorem is a consequence of a result of Ghisi and Gobbino
[
6
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
d
be a positive integer and
let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an open bounded interval. Assume that
f\colon I\to\mathbb{C}
is a continuous function such that

f^{d}=g\in C^{d-1,1}\big(\overline{I}\big)
.
Then we have
f^{\prime}\in L^{d^{\prime}}_{w}(I)
and

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}\|_{d^{\prime},w,I}\leq C(d)\max\Big\{\big(\operatorname{Lip}_{I}\big(g^{(d-1)}\big)\big)^{1/d}|I|^{1/d^{\prime}},\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}^{1/d}\Big\}.

(3.1)

In other words
any continuous lift
f
over
\sigma(z)=z^{d}
of a curve in
C^{d-1,1}\big(\overline{I},\sigma(\mathbb{C})\big)=C^{d-1,1}\big(\overline{I}\big)

is absolutely continuous and
f^{\prime}\in L^{d^{\prime}}_{w}(I)
with the uniform bound (
3.1
).

Remark 3.2
.

This result is optimal: in general,
f^{\prime}
is not in
L^{d^{\prime}}
even if
g
is real analytic (consider
g(t)=t
).
On the other hand, if
g
is only of class
C^{d-1,\beta}\big(\overline{I}\big)
for every
\beta<1
, then
f

does in general not need to have bounded variation
in
I
(see
[
6
, Example 4.4]
).

Remark 3.3
.

If we consider the
real
representation of
C_{d}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by rotation, basic invariants are given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(x,y)=z\overline{z},\qquad\sigma_{2}(x,y)=\mathrm{Re}\big(z^{d}\big),\qquad\sigma_{3}(x,y)=\mathrm{Im}\big(z^{d}\big),\qquad\text{where}\quad z=x+{\rm i}y,

with the relation
\sigma_{1}^{d}=\sigma_{2}^{2}+\sigma_{3}^{2}
.
Let
f
be a map that takes values in
\sigma\big(\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)
, where
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})
, and which is smooth as a map
into
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then the constraints
f
has to fulfill, in contrast to the complex case where there are no constrains,
give reasons for the more regular lifting in the real case (cf. Theorem
1.9
).

For instance, suppose that
f
is a smooth complex valued function. By Theorem
1.9
and the previous paragraph,
the equation
z^{d}=f
has a solution of class
W^{1,\infty}
provided that
|f|^{2/d}
is of class
C^{d-1,1}
.
Observe that for
d=2
and
f\geq 0
this condition is automatically fulfilled; it corresponds to the hyperbolic case.

4
Reduction to slice representations

Let
G\circlearrowleft V
be a complex finite-dimensional representation of a finite group
G
.
Suppose that
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
is a system of homogeneous basic invariants.
Let
V^{G}=\{v\in V\colon Gv=v\}
be the linear subspace of invariant vectors.
It is the subspace of all vectors
v
for which the isotropy subgroup
G_{v}=\{g\in G\colon gv=v\}
is
equal to
G
.

4.1
Removing invariant vectors

Since finite groups are linearly reductive,
there exists a unique subrepresentation
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
such that
V=V^{G}\oplus V^{\prime}
(cf.
[
5
, Theorem 2.2.5]
).
Then

\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}=\mathbb{C}\big[V^{G}\big]\otimes\mathbb{C}[V^{\prime}]^{G}
and

V/G=V^{G}\times V^{\prime}/G
.
A system of basic invariants of
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
is given by a system of linear coordinates on
V^{G}
together with a
system of basic invariants of
\mathbb{C}[V^{\prime}]^{G}
. Hence the following lemma is immediate.

Lemma 4.1
.

Any lift
\overline{f}
of a mapping
f=(f_{0},f_{1})
in
V^{G}\times V^{\prime}/G
has the form

\overline{f}=(f_{0},\overline{f}_{1})
,
where
\overline{f}_{1}
is a lift of
f_{1}
.

Consequently, we may assume without loss of generality that
V^{G}=\{0\}
.

4.2
Luna’s slice theorem

Let us recall Luna’s slice theorem.
Here we just assume that
V
is a rational representation of a linearly reductive group
G
.
The categorical quotient
\pi\colon V\to V{/\!\!/}G
is the affine variety with the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}

together with the projection
\pi
induced by the inclusion
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}[V]
. In this setting
\pi
does not separate orbits, but for each element
z\in V{/\!\!/}G
there is a unique
closed orbit in the fiber
\pi^{-1}(z)
.
If
Gv
is a closed orbit, then
G_{v}
is again linearly reductive. We say that
U\subseteq V
is
G
-saturated
if
\pi^{-1}(\pi(U))=U
.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
10
]
,
[
18
, Theorem 5.3]
)
.

Let
Gv
be a closed orbit.
Choose a
G_{v}
-splitting
T_{v}(Gv)\oplus N_{v}
of
V\cong T_{v}V
and let
\varphi
denote the mapping

\displaystyle G\times_{G_{v}}N_{v}\to V,\qquad[g,n]\mapsto g(v+n).

There is an affine open
G
-saturated subset
U
of
V
and an affine open
G_{v}
-saturated neighborhood
B_{v}
of
0
in
N_{v}

such that

\displaystyle\varphi\colon\ G\times_{G_{v}}B_{v}\to U

and the induced mapping

\displaystyle\bar{\varphi}\colon\ (G\times_{G_{v}}B_{v}){/\!\!/}G\to U{/\!\!/}G

are étale.
Moreover,
\varphi
and the natural mapping
G\times_{G_{v}}B_{v}\to B_{v}{/\!\!/}G_{v}

induce a
G
-isomorphism of
G\times_{G_{v}}B_{v}
with
U\times_{U{/\!\!/}G}(B_{v}{/\!\!/}G_{v})
.

Corollary 4.3

(
[
10
]
,
[
18
, Corollary 5.4]
)
.

In the setting of Theorem
4.2
,

G_{y}
is conjugate to a subgroup of
G_{v}
for all
y\in U
. Choose a
G
-saturated neighborhood
\overline{B}_{v}
of
0
in
B_{v}

(
classical topology
)

such that the canonical mapping
\overline{B}_{v}{/\!\!/}G_{v}\to\overline{U}{/\!\!/}G
is a complex analytic isomorphism,
where
\overline{U}=\pi^{-1}\big(\bar{\varphi}((G\times_{G_{v}}B_{v}){/\!\!/}G)\big)
.
Then
\overline{U}
is a
G
-saturated neighborhood of
v
and
\varphi\colon G\times_{G_{v}}\overline{B}_{v}\to\overline{U}

is biholomorphic.

4.3
Uniform slice reduction

Let
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a system of generators of
\mathbb{C}[N_{v}]^{G_{v}}
and
let
\tau=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{m})\colon N_{v}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m}
be the associated mapping.
Consider the
slice

\displaystyle S_{v}:=v+\overline{B}_{v},

where
\overline{B}_{v}
is the neighborhood from Corollary
4.3
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
be a curve in
\sigma(V)
with
a_{k}\neq 0
and such that the curve

\displaystyle\underline{a}:=\big({a_{k}}^{-d_{1}/d_{k}}a_{1},\dots,{a_{k}}^{-d_{n}/d_{k}}a_{n}\big)

lies in
\sigma(U_{v})
, where
U_{v}
is a neighborhood of
v
in
S_{v}
.
Composition of the curve
\underline{a}-\sigma(v)
with the analytic isomorphism of Corollary
4.3
gives a
curve
\underline{b}=(\underline{b}_{1},\dots,\underline{b}_{m})
in
\tau(U_{v}-v)
and

\displaystyle b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{m}):=\big(a_{k}^{e_{1}/d_{k}}\underline{b}_{1},\dots,{a_{k}}^{e_{m}/d_{k}}\underline{b}_{m}\big),\qquad e_{i}=\deg\tau_{i},

is a curve in

is a lift of

over

\displaystyle a_{k}^{1/d_{k}}v+\overline{b}

is a lift of

over
\sigma
.

Proof.

The curve

is a lift of
\underline{b}
over

\displaystyle\tau_{i}\big(a_{k}^{-1/d_{k}}\overline{b}\big)=a_{k}^{-e_{i}/d_{k}}\tau_{i}\big(\overline{b}\big)=a_{k}^{-e_{i}/d_{k}}b_{i}=\underline{b}_{i}.

Thus

over

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume that the systems of basic invariants

In order to make the slice reduction uniform, we consider

5.1
An interpolation inequality

For an interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and a function
f\colon I\to\mathbb{C}
we set

\displaystyle V_{I}(f):=\sup_{t,s\in I}|f(t)-f(s)|=\operatorname{diam}f(I).

Lemma 5.1

(
[
16
, Lemma 4]
)
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a bounded open interval,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, and
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
If
f\in C^{m,\alpha}(\overline{I})
, then for all
t\in I

and
s=1,\dots,m
,

\displaystyle\big|f^{(s)}(t)\big|\leq C|I|^{-s}\bigl(V_{I}(f)+V_{I}(f)^{(m+\alpha-s)/(m+\alpha)}\big(\operatorname{H\ddot{o}ld}_{\alpha,I}\big(f^{(m)}\big)\big)^{s/(m+\alpha)}|I|^{s}\bigr),

for a universal constant
C
depending only on
m
and
\alpha
.

5.2
The local setup

Let
G\circlearrowleft V
be a complex finite-dimensional representation of a finite group
G
.
Assume
V^{G}=\{0\}
. Let
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})
be a system of homogeneous basic invariants
of degrees
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
and let
d:=\max_{j}d_{j}
.
Let
a\in C^{d-1,1}\big(\overline{I},\sigma(V)\big)
, where
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is a bounded open interval.

It will be crucial to consider the radicals
a_{j}^{1/d_{j}}
of the components
a_{j}
of
a
which is justified by the following remark.

Remark 5.2
.

Every continuous selection
f
of
the multi-valued function
a_{j}^{1/d_{j}}
is absolutely continuous on
I
, by Theorem
3.1
.
(Clearly, continuous selections exist in this case.)
Moreover,
\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(I)}
is independent of the
choice of the selection. Indeed, if
g
is a different continuous selection then on each connected component
J
of

the functions

and

just differ by multiplication with a fixed
d_{j}
-th root of unity.
Thus
\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(J)}=\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(J)}
. The
\mathbb{C}
-valued version of Lemma
2.1
implies that
\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(I)}=\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(I)}
.

Henceforth we fix one continuous selection of
a_{j}^{1/d_{j}}
and
denote it by

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{j}\colon\ I\to\mathbb{C}

as well as, abusing notation, by
a_{j}^{1/d_{j}}
. We will also consider the absolutely continuous curve

\displaystyle\hat{a}=(\hat{a}_{1},\dots,\hat{a}_{n})\colon\ I\to\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Suppose that
t_{0}\in I
and
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
are such that

\displaystyle|\hat{a}_{k}(t_{0})|=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|\hat{a}_{j}(t_{0})|\neq 0.

(5.1)

Assume further that, for some fixed positive constant
B<1/3
,

\displaystyle\|\hat{a}^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(I)}\leq B|\hat{a}_{k}(t_{0})|.

(5.2)

At this point we just demand that the constant

is fixed and smaller than
1/3
; in the proof of Theorem
1.1

we will additionally specify

depending on the uniform reduction radius
\rho>0
and the maximal degree

, see (
8.1
),
and it is going to be fixed along said proof. In accordance with (
2.2
),
\|\hat{a}^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(I)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\hat{a}_{j}^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(I)}
.

Definition 5.3
.

By
admissible data
for
G\circlearrowleft V
me mean a tuple
(a,I,t_{0},k)
, where

is a curve in
\sigma(V)
for a representation
G\circlearrowleft V
with
V^{G}=\{0\}

defined on an open bounded interval

such that

and

) and (

).

5.3
The reduced curve
\boldsymbol{\underline{a}}

Let
(a,I,t_{0},k)
be admissible data for
G\circlearrowleft V
. We shall see in the next lemma that

a_{k}
does not vanish on the interval
I
and so the curve

\displaystyle\underline{a}\colon\ I

\displaystyle\to\{(\underline{a}_{1},\dots,\underline{a}_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\colon\underline{a}_{k}=1\},

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto\underline{a}(t):=\big(a_{k}^{-d_{1}/d_{k}}a_{1},\dots,a_{k}^{-d_{n}/d_{k}}a_{n}\big)(t)=\big(\big(\hat{a}_{k}^{-1}\hat{a}_{1}\big)^{d_{1}},\dots,(\hat{a}_{k}^{-1}\hat{a}_{n})^{d_{n}}\big)(t)

(5.3)

is well-defined. The homogeneity of the basic invariants implies that
\underline{a}(I)\subseteq\sigma(V)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(a,I,t_{0},k)
be admissible data for
G\circlearrowleft V
.
Then for all
t\in I
and
j=1,\dots,n
,

\displaystyle|\hat{a}_{j}(t)-\hat{a}_{j}(t_{0})|\leq B|\hat{a}_{k}(t_{0})|,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}<1-B\leq\bigg|\frac{\hat{a}_{k}(t)}{\hat{a}_{k}(t_{0})}\bigg|\leq 1+B<\frac{4}{3},

(5.5)

\displaystyle|\hat{a}_{j}(t)|\leq\frac{4}{3}|\hat{a}_{k}(t_{0})|\leq 2|\hat{a}_{k}(t)|.

(5.6)

The length of the curve
\underline{a}

is bounded by
3d^{2}\,2^{d}B
.

Proof.

First (
5.4
) is a consequence of (
5.2
),

\displaystyle\big|\hat{a}_{j}(t)-\hat{a}_{j}(t_{0})\big|=\bigg|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\hat{a}_{j}^{\prime}\,{\rm d}s\bigg|\leq\|\hat{a}_{j}^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(I)}\leq B|\hat{a}_{k}(t_{0})|.

Setting

in (
5.4
) easily implies (
5.5
). Together with (
5.1
), the inequalities (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) give (

). In order to estimate the length of
\underline{a}
observe that

\displaystyle\underline{a}_{j}^{\prime}=\partial_{t}\big(\big(\hat{a}_{k}^{-1}\hat{a}_{j}\big)^{d_{j}}\big)=d_{j}\big(\hat{a}_{k}^{-1}\hat{a}_{j}\big)^{d_{j}-1}\big(\hat{a}_{k}^{-1}\hat{a}_{j}^{\prime}-\hat{a}_{k}^{-2}\hat{a}_{j}\hat{a}_{k}^{\prime}\big).

Since
|\hat{a}_{k}^{-1}\hat{a}_{j}|\leq 2
, by (
5.6
), and thanks to (
5.5
) we obtain

\displaystyle|\underline{a}_{j}^{\prime}|\leq 3d\,2^{d}|\hat{a}_{k}(t_{0})|^{-1}\big(|\hat{a}_{j}^{\prime}|+|\hat{a}_{k}^{\prime}|\big).

Consequently, using (
5.2
),

\displaystyle\int_{I}|\underline{a}^{\prime}|\,{\rm d}s\leq 3d^{2}\,2^{d}B,

as required.
∎

) on suitable intervals

, we obtain a curve

Definition 6.1
.

6.2
Pointwise estimates for the derivatives of
\boldsymbol{b}
on
\boldsymbol{I}

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
(a,I,t_{0},k;b)
be reduced admissible data for
G\circlearrowleft V
.
Then for all

i=1,\dots,m
and
s=1,\dots,d-1
,

\displaystyle\big\|b_{i}^{(s)}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq C|I|^{-s}|\hat{a}_{k}(t_{0})|^{e_{i}},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}_{I}\big(b_{i}^{(d-1)}\big)\leq C|I|^{-d}|\hat{a}_{k}(t_{0})|^{e_{i}},

(6.3)

where
C
is a constant depending only on
d
and on the functions
\psi_{i}
.

Proof.

Let us prove the first estimate in (
6.3
).
Let
F
be any
C^{d}
-function defined on an open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
that contains

\underline{a}(I)
and assume
\|F\|_{C^{d}(\overline{U})}<\infty
. We claim that, for
s=1,\dots,d-1
,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{s}(F\circ\underline{a})\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq C|I|^{-s},

(6.4)

where

C
is a constant depending only on
d
and
\|F\|_{C^{d}(\overline{U})}
.
For any real exponent
r
, Faà di Bruno’s formula implies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{s}\big(a_{j}^{r}\big)=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}^{s}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\ell,s)}c_{\gamma,\ell,r}\,a_{j}^{r-\ell}a_{j}^{(\gamma_{1})}\cdots a_{j}^{(\gamma_{\ell})},

(6.5)

where
\Gamma(\ell,s)=\{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}^{\ell}\colon|\gamma|=s\}
and

\displaystyle c_{\gamma,\ell,r}=\frac{s!}{\ell!\gamma!}r(r-1)\cdots(r-\ell+1).

By (
6.1
) and (
5.5
), this implies for

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{s}\big(a_{k}^{r}\big)\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\geq 1}^{s}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\ell,s)}c_{\gamma,\ell,r}\,\|a_{k}^{r-\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\big\|a_{k}^{(\gamma_{1})}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\cdots\big\|a_{k}^{(\gamma_{\ell})}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq C(d)\sum_{\ell\geq 1}^{s}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\ell,s)}c_{\gamma,\ell,r}\,|a_{k}(t_{0})|^{r-\ell}|I|^{-s}|a_{k}(t_{0})|^{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq C(d)|I|^{-s}|a_{k}(t_{0})|^{r}.

(6.6)

Together with the Leibniz formula,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{s}\big(a_{k}^{-d_{j}/d_{k}}a_{j}\big)=\sum_{q=0}^{s}\binom{s}{q}a_{j}^{(q)}\partial_{t}^{s-q}\big(a_{k}^{-d_{j}/d_{k}}\big),

(
6.6
) and (
6.1
) lead to

\displaystyle\big\|\partial_{t}^{s}\big(a_{k}^{-d_{j}/d_{k}}a_{j}\big)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq C(d)|I|^{-s}.

(6.7)

Again by the Leibniz formula,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(F\circ\underline{a})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}((\partial_{j}F)\circ\underline{a})\,\partial_{t}\big(a_{k}^{-d_{j}/d_{k}}a_{j}\big),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{s}(F\circ\underline{a})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{t}^{s-1}\Big(((\partial_{j}F)\circ\underline{a})\,\partial_{t}\big(a_{k}^{-d_{j}/d_{k}}a_{j}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{p=0}^{s-1}\binom{s-1}{p}\partial_{t}^{p}((\partial_{j}F)\circ\underline{a})\,\partial_{t}^{s-p}\big(a_{k}^{-d_{j}/d_{k}}a_{j}\big).

For
s=1
we immediately get (
6.4
). For
1<s\leq d-1
, we may argue by induction on
s
. By induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{p}((\partial_{j}F)\circ\underline{a})\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\leq C\big(d,\|\partial_{j}F\|_{C^{s}(\overline{U})}\big)|I|^{-p},

for
p=1,\dots,s-1
. Together with (
6.7
) this entails (
6.4
).

Now the first part of (
6.3
) is a consequence of (
6.2
), (
6.6
) (for
r=e_{i}/d_{k}
),
and (
6.4
) (applied to
F=\psi_{i}
).

For the second part of (
6.3
)
observe that for functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
on
I
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}_{I}(f_{1}f_{2}\cdots f_{m})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{Lip}_{I}(f_{i})\|f_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\cdots\widehat{\|f_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}}\cdots\|f_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}.

Applying it to (

) and using

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}_{I}\big(a_{j}^{r-\ell}\big)\leq|r-\ell|\|a_{j}^{r-\ell-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}\|a_{j}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)}

we find, as in the derivation of (

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}_{I}\big(\partial_{t}^{d-1}(a_{k}^{r})\big)\leq C(d,r)|I|^{-d}|a_{k}(t_{0})|^{r}.

As above this leads to

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}_{I}\big(\partial_{t}^{d-1}\big(a_{k}^{-d_{j}/d_{k}}a_{j}\big)\big)\leq C(d)|I|^{-d},

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}_{I}\big(\partial_{t}^{d-1}(F\circ\underline{a})\big)\leq C\big(d,\|F\|_{C^{d}(\overline{U})}\big)|I|^{-d},

and finally to the second part of (

∎

\displaystyle\|\overline{a}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(I)}\leq C(G\circlearrowleft V,p)\Big(\big\||I|^{-1}{|\hat{a}_{k}(t_{0})|}\big\|_{L^{p}(I)}+\big\|\hat{b}^{\prime}\big\|_{L^{p}(I)}\Big).

By Corollary

we get for all

where the constant

and

At this stage we distinguish the two cases of strict inequality or equality in (

(i)

Strict inequality: we have

(8.15)

(ii)

\displaystyle M|I|+\|\hat{a}^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(I)}=B|\hat{a}_{k}(t_{0})|.

(8.16)

Case (

In this case we can reduce to the curve

on the whole interval

By (

Case (

Using (

Lemma 9.1
.

Let

be continuous and let
\overline{c}\colon J\to V
a continuous lift of

on an open proper subinterval

of

\overline{c}
can be extended to a continuous lift of

Proof.

Since we already know that

extends continuously to the endpoints of

\overline{c}
can be extended left and

after applying a fixed transformation from

So let

be the (say) right endpoint of

of

as

is contained in the orbit corresponding to

. On the other hand

must be connected, by the continuity of

.
Since every orbit is finite,

consists of just one point.
∎

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
\overline{c}_{1}
,
\overline{c}_{2}
be continuous lifts of a curve

.
If

is absolutely continuous,
\overline{c}_{2}\in W^{1,p}(I)

\displaystyle\|\overline{c}_{2}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(I)}\leq C\,\|\overline{c}_{1}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}(I)},

where

depends only on

and on the coordinate system on

.

Proof.

For each subset

.
It follows that

\displaystyle\operatorname{length}(\overline{c}_{2})\leq\sum_{g\in G}\operatorname{length}(g\overline{c}_{1})<\infty

and that

has the Luzin (N) property. Hence

Suppose that both
\overline{c}_{1}
and
\overline{c}_{2}
are differentiable at

.
After replacing

with

for a suitable

we may suppose that

.
Then after switching to the slice representation at

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{c}_{2}(t+h)-\overline{c}_{2}(t)}{h}=\frac{g_{h}\overline{c}_{1}(t+h)-g_{h}\overline{c}_{1}(t)}{h}=g_{h}\left(\frac{\overline{c}_{1}(t+h)-\overline{c}_{1}(t)}{h}\right),

∎

\boldsymbol{Q}
-valued functions

The basic reference for the background on
Q
-valued Sobolev functions used in this section is
[
4

.

10.1
The metric space
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

Unordered
Q
-tuples of points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be formalized as positive atomic measures of mass
Q
.
Let
\llbracket p_{i}\rrbracket
denote the Dirac mass at
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We consider the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\llbracket p_{i}\rrbracket\colon p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigg\}

of unordered
Q
-tuples of points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a complete metric space when endowed with the metric

\displaystyle d\bigg(\sum_{i}\llbracket p_{i}\rrbracket,\sum_{i}\llbracket q_{i}\rrbracket\bigg):=\min_{\sigma\in\operatorname{S}_{Q}}\bigg(\sum_{i}|p_{i}-q_{\sigma(i)}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

(10.1)

10.2
Invariants

There is a natural one-to-one correspondence between
the unordered
Q
-tuples
\sum_{i}\llbracket p_{i}\rrbracket\in\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

and the orbits of the
n
-fold direct sum
W:=\big(\mathbb{R}^{Q}\big)^{\oplus n}
of the tautological representation
\mathbb{R}^{Q}
of the
symmetric group
\operatorname{S}_{Q}
. By a result of Weyl
[
21
]
, the algebra
\mathbb{R}[W]^{\operatorname{S}_{Q}}
is generated by the polarizations of the elementary symmetric functions.
Up to integer factors the polarizations are

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(u)=\sum_{i}u_{i},

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(u,v)=\sum_{i\neq j}u_{i}v_{j},

\displaystyle\sigma_{3}(u,v,w)=\sum_{i,j,k\text{ all }\neq}u_{i}v_{j}w_{k},

\displaystyle\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots

\displaystyle\sigma_{Q}(u,v,\dots,w)=\sum_{i,j,\dots,k\text{ all }\neq}u_{i}v_{j}\cdots w_{k},

where
u=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{Q})
,
v=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{Q})
, etc.
A system of generators of
\mathbb{R}[W]^{\operatorname{S}_{Q}}
is obtained by substituting the arguments
x^{1},x^{2},\dots,x^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{Q}

for
u,v,w,\dots
in all possible combinations (including repetitions).
Note that the ring
\mathbb{R}[W]^{\operatorname{S}_{Q}}
is not polynomial unless
n=1
, e.g., by the Shephard–Todd–Chevalley theorem.

10.3
Subspaces
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}_{G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

Let
G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a representation of a finite group
G
. We define the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\bigg\{\sum_{g\in G}\llbracket gp\rrbracket\colon p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigg\}

of
G
-orbits. It is a closed subspace of the complete metric space
\mathcal{A}_{|G|}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, thus also complete.

A system of generators for
\mathbb{R}[V]^{G}
can be obtained from the generators of
\mathbb{R}[W]^{\operatorname{S}_{|G|}}

by means of the Noether map
\eta^{*}\colon\mathbb{R}[W]^{\operatorname{S}_{|G|}}\to\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{R}^{n}]^{G}
, where
\eta\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to W

is defined by
\eta(p)(g)=gp
and
W=\big(\mathbb{R}^{|G|}\big)^{\oplus n}
is identified with the space of mappings
G\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
;
for details see, e.g.,
[
12
]
.

10.4
\boldsymbol{Q}
-valued Sobolev functions

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
A measurable function
f\colon\Omega\to\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be in the Sobolev class
W^{1,p}
(for
1\leq p\leq\infty
)
if

1)

x\mapsto{\rm d}(f(x),P)\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
for all
P\in\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

2)

there exist functions
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{m}\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{+})
such that

\displaystyle|\partial_{j}{\rm d}(f,P)|\leq\varphi_{j}\qquad\text{a.e.\quad in}\quad\Omega\qquad\text{for all}\quad P\in\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad j=1,\dots,m.

The minimal functions
\varphi_{j}
satisfying (2) are denoted by
|\partial_{j}f|
and they are characterized as follows:
for every countable dense subset
\{P_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and all
j=1,\dots,m

we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{j}f|=\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}|\partial_{j}{\rm d}(f,P_{\ell})|\qquad\text{a.e.\quad in}\quad\Omega.

One sets
|Df|:=\big(\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\partial_{j}f|^{2}\big)^{1/2}
. This intrinsic approach is developed in
[
4
]
.

Alternatively, one may use Almgren’s extrinsic approach
[
1
]
to
Q
-valued Sobolev functions.
There is an injective Lipschitz map
\xi\colon\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
N=N(Q,n)
, with Lipschitz constant

\operatorname{Lip}(\xi)\leq 1
such that the inverse
\theta:=\xi|_{\xi(\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{-1}
is Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant

\leq C(Q,n)
.
Here the constants
N
and
C
depend only upon
Q
and
n
.
The inverse
\theta\colon\xi(\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has a Lipschitz extension
\Theta\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
It follows that
\rho:=\xi\circ\Theta
is a Lipschitz retraction of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
onto
\xi(\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

A function
f\colon\Omega\to\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is of class
W^{1,p}
if and only if

\xi\circ f
belongs to
W^{1,p}\big(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}\big)
, and in that case

\displaystyle|D(\xi\circ f)|\leq|Df|\leq C(Q,n)|D(\xi\circ f)|,

(10.2)

see
[
4
, Theorem 2.4]
.

10.5
\boldsymbol{Q}
-valued Sobolev functions and invariant theory

We may identify the
\operatorname{S}_{Q}

Then

\pi\colon\mathbb{R}^{Q\times n}\to\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\llbracket p_{i}\rrbracket

Let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}

\mathbb{R}[W]^{\operatorname{S}_{Q}}

\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r})

Theorem 10.1

Let
\Omega

\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Let

If

, then

.

\displaystyle\|Df\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C(Q,n,m,\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\Big(1+\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\|\Sigma_{j}\circ f\|^{1/d_{j}}_{C^{Q-1,1}(\overline{\Omega})}\Big).

Proof.

Corollary 10.2
.

The bijective mapping
\Sigma
induces a bounded mapping

10.6
Multi-valued Sobolev liftings

Let
G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a representation of a finite group
G
.
The surjective map
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathcal{A}_{G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined by
\pi(p)=\sum_{g\in G}\llbracket gp\rrbracket
is
clearly Lipschitz.
Let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}
be any system of homogeneous generators of
\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{R}^{n}]^{G}
.
There is a bijective map
\Sigma\colon\mathcal{A}_{G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\sigma(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{r}
such that
\sigma=\Sigma\circ\pi
, since

\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r})
separates orbits.
Let
d:=\max_{j}\deg\sigma_{j}
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We say that a function
f\colon\Omega\to\mathcal{A}_{G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is of class
W^{1,p}
,
and write
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathcal{A}_{G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, if
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathcal{A}_{|G|}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

Thus we obtain, analogously to Theorem
10.1
,

Theorem 10.3
.

Let
f\colon\Omega\to\mathcal{A}_{G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be continuous.
If
\Sigma\circ f\in C^{d-1,1}\big(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{r}\big)
, then
for each relatively compact open
\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega
we have

f\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{A}_{G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\|Df\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C(d,n,m,\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\Big(1+\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\|\Sigma_{j}\circ f\|^{1/d_{j}}_{C^{d-1,1}(\overline{\Omega})}\Big).

Corollary 10.4
.

The bijective mapping
\Sigma
induces a bounded mapping

\displaystyle\big(\Sigma^{-1}\big)_{*}\colon\ C^{d-1,1}(\Omega,\sigma(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to W^{1,\infty}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega,\mathcal{A}_{G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),\qquad\varphi\mapsto\Sigma^{-1}\circ\varphi.

10.7
Complex
\boldsymbol{Q}
-valued functions

It is evident that one can define the space
\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
of unordered
Q
-tuples of points in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
in analogy to
\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
It is a complete metric space with the metric
d
from (
10.1
).
Again there is a natural bijection between the points in
\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
and the orbits of the
\operatorname{S}_{Q}
-module
\big(\mathbb{C}^{Q}\big)^{\oplus n}
,
the basic invariants of which are again given by the polarizations of the elementary symmetric functions.

Given a complex representation
G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{C}^{n}
of a finite group
G
we may consider the closed subspace
\mathcal{A}_{G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{C}^{n}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

of
\mathcal{A}_{|G|}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

The theory of complex
Q
-valued Sobolev functions can simply be taken over from the identification

\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\cong\mathcal{A}_{Q}\big(\mathbb{R}^{2n}\big)
induced by
\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
With the analogous definition of the basic invariants
\sigma_{i}
and the maps
\pi
and
\Sigma
we may deduce from Theorem
1.1
the following

Theorem 10.5
.

Let
f\colon\Omega\to\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
be continuous.
If
\Sigma\circ f\in C^{Q-1,1}\big(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{C}^{r}\big)
, then
for each relatively compact open
\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega
and all
1\leq p<Q/(Q-1)
we have

f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\|Df\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C(Q,n,m,p,\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\Big(1+\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\|\Sigma_{j}\circ f\|^{1/d_{j}}_{C^{Q-1,1}(\overline{\Omega})}\Big).

Similarly we get

Theorem 10.6
.

Let
f\colon\Omega\to\mathcal{A}_{G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{C}^{n}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
be continuous.
If
\Sigma\circ f\in C^{d-1,1}\big(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{C}^{r}\big)
, then
for each relatively compact open
\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega
and all
1\leq p<d/(d-1)
we have

f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{A}_{G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{C}^{n}}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\|Df\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C(d,n,m,p,\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\Big(1+\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\|\Sigma_{j}\circ f\|^{1/d_{j}}_{C^{d-1,1}(\overline{\Omega})}\Big).

Again we may conclude that
the bijective mapping
\Sigma
induces a bounded mapping

\displaystyle\big(\Sigma^{-1}\big)_{*}\colon\ C^{Q-1,1}\big(\Omega,\sigma\big(\big(\mathbb{C}^{Q}\big)^{\oplus n}\big)\big)\to W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega,\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})),\qquad\varphi\mapsto\Sigma^{-1}\circ\varphi,

for all
1\leq p<Q/(Q-1)
. In the case of a
G
-module
\mathbb{C}^{n}
we find that

\displaystyle\big(\Sigma^{-1}\big)_{*}\colon\ C^{d-1,1}(\Omega,\sigma(\mathbb{C}^{n}))\to W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega,\mathcal{A}_{G\circlearrowleft\mathbb{C}^{n}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})),\qquad\varphi\mapsto\Sigma^{-1}\circ\varphi,

is a bounded mapping for all
1\leq p<d/(d-1)
.

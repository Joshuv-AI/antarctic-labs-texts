Abstract
. In this note we generalize perturbation results for positive C 0 C_{0} -semigroups on AM- and AL-spaces and give a Weiss–Staffans type perturbation result for generators of positive semigroups on Banach lattices. The abstract results are applied to domain perturbations of generators, a heat equation with boundary feedback and perturbations of the first derivative.

1.
Introduction

Many systems evolving in time can be described by an Abstract Cauchy Problem of the form

(ACP)

\begin{cases}{\tfrac{d}{dt}}\,x(t)=Gx(t),&t\geq 0,\\
x(0)=x_{0}\end{cases}

where
G
is an unbounded linear (e.g., differential) operator on a Banach space
X
, cf.
[
EN00
, Chap.VI]
. As shown in
[
EN00
, Sect.II.6]
, this problem is well-posed if and only if
G
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
X
. Moreover, in this case its unique solution is given by
x(t)=S(t)x_{0}
.

For this reason it’s vital to have tools at hand which allow to verify the generator property of a given operator
G
. In the dissipative case the Lumer–Phillips theorem is such a result which provides a characterization of generators of contraction semigroups in terms of
G
itself and applies to many concrete examples. Its counterpart in the non-dissipative case is the famous Hille–Yosida theorem.
This result, however, is not based on the given operator
G
but on growth estimates of
all
powers of its resolvent which frequently are impossible to verify.

In order to check well-posedness of (
ACP
) for (non dissipative) operators
G
where direct computations involving the resolvent are impossible to perform, one can try to split
G
into a sum “
G=A+P\,
” for a simpler generator
A
and a perturbation

and then use some kind of perturbation result to conclude that also
G
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
.

The Weiss–Staffans theorem on regular linear systems with feedback (cf.
[
Wei94
, Thms 6.1 & 7.2]
and
[
Sta05
, Sects.7.1 & 7.4]
) is one of the most general result in this direction. In the present paper we continue our study of purely operator theoretic versions of this theorem initiated in
[
ABE14
]
and
[
ABE17
]
avoiding notions and results from abstract linear systems theory.

The General Setup

To start with we introduce the main objects of our investigations.

Assumption 2.1
.

If not stated otherwise, in the sequel we consider

(i)

a Banach lattice
X
called the
state space
;

(ii)

a Banach lattice
U
called the
control/observation space
;

(iii)

a
state operator

A:D(A)\subset X\to X
generating a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
X
;

(iv)

a
control operator

B:U\to X_{-1}
;

(v)

an
observation operator

C:Z\to U
, where
Z
is a vector space satisfying

(2.1)

D(A)\subseteq Z\subseteq X.

Here the extrapolation space
X_{-1}
of
X
associated to the operator

is given by the completion
(X,\lVert\cdot\rVert_{-1})^{\sim}
where
\lVert x\rVert_{-1}:=\lVert R(\lambda,A)x\rVert
for some fixed

. Then for any

the operator

possesses a unique continuous extension

and

-semigroup on

with generator

satisfying

. This definition does not depend on the particular choice of

since the norms
\lVert R(\lambda,A)\cdot\rVert
are equivalent for any

. For a detailed treatment of these facts we refer to
[

, Chap.II.5]
.

In our case

is a Banach lattice, and it is a priori not clear how to extend the concept of positivity to

. If

generates a positive

-semigroup on

, we follow
[

, Def.2.1]
and define the positive cone in

by

X_{-1,+}:=\overline{X_{+}}^{\,\|\cdot\|_{-1}},

denotes the positive cone of

(see Appendix

). It is immediate that

. If

[

, Prop.2.3]
we also have

. Moreover, by
[

, Rem.2.2]
the following holds.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

be the generator of a positive semigroup on the Banach lattice

.
Then the operator
B:U\to X_{-1}
is positive, i.e.

, if and only if

is positive for all
\lambda>\operatorname{s}(A)
.

Throughout this note we denote by

2.1.
Admissibility

In this subsection we recall some standard notions from linear systems theory, cf.
[
Sta05
, Chap.10]
and the references therein, which are essential for our approach.

Definition 2.3
.

The control operator
B:U\to X_{-1}
is said to be
p
-
admissible
for
1\leq p<\infty
if there exists
t>0
such that

(2.2)

\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)\,ds\in X\quad\text{for all}\,\,u\in\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,t],U).

This means that the
controllability map

\mathcal{B}_{t}:\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,t],U)\to X_{-1}
given by

(2.3)

\mathcal{B}_{t}u:=\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)ds,\quad u\in\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,t],U)

has range
\operatorname{rg}(\mathcal{B}_{t})\subseteq X
. Since the operator
\mathcal{B}_{t}:\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,t],U)\to X_{-1}
is bounded and
X
is continuously embedded in
X_{-1}
, by the Closed Graph Theorem it follows that
\mathcal{B}_{t}:\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,t],U)\to X
is bounded (see
[
EN00
, Cor.B.7]
).

In case
p=\infty
we give the following definition.

Definition 2.4
.

The control operator
B:U\to X_{-1}
is said to be
\infty
-
admissible
if there exists
t>0
such that

(2.4)

\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)ds\in X\quad\text{for all}\,\,u\in\mathrm{C}([0,t],U).

As before, this definition can be equivalently rephrased by saying that the controllability map
\mathcal{B}_{t}:\mathrm{C}([0,t],U)\to X_{-1}
given by

(2.5)

\mathcal{B}_{t}u:=\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)ds,\quad u\in\mathrm{C}([0,t],U)

has range
\operatorname{rg}(\mathcal{B}_{t})\subseteq X
. By the same reasoning as above, this implies that
\mathcal{B}_{t}:\mathrm{C}([0,t],U)\to X
is bounded.
In
Section

3.1
we will verify the
\infty
-admissibility of a control operator
B
by using the following result.

Proposition 2.5
.

The control operator
B:U\to X_{-1}
is
\infty
-admissible if
there exists
t>0
, a subspace
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathrm{L}^{\infty}([0,t],U)
and
M\geq 0
such that
\mathrm{C}([0,t],U)\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{D}}
and

\displaystyle(i)\quad\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)\,ds\in X\,\,

\displaystyle\text{for all}\,\,u\in\mathcal{D},

\displaystyle(ii)\quad\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)\,ds\right\rVert_{X}\leq M\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}\,\,

\displaystyle\text{for all}\,\,u\in\mathcal{D}.

Moreover, in this case
\|\mathcal{B}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{C}([0,t],U),X)}\leq M
.

Proof.

In any case, we can define the bounded linear operator
\mathcal{B}_{t}:\mathrm{L}^{\infty}([0,t],U)\to X_{-1}
as in (
2.5
) for
u\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}([0,t],U)
. Now assumptions (i) and (ii) imply that the restriction
\mathcal{B}_{t}|_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{D}\to X
is bounded of bound
M
, hence possesses a unique bounded extension
\mathcal{R}:\overline{\mathcal{D}}\to X
of the same bound.
Since by assumption
\mathrm{C}([0,t],U)\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{D}}
, for every
u\in\mathrm{C}([0,t],U)
there exists a sequence
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{D}
converging to
u
in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}([0,t],U)
. Then
\mathcal{B}_{t}u_{n}\to\mathcal{B}_{t}u
in
X_{-1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{t}u_{n}=\mathcal{R}u_{n}\to\mathcal{R}u
in
X
as
n\to+\infty
. This implies
\mathcal{B}_{t}u=\mathcal{R}u\in X
for all
u\in\mathrm{C}([0,t],U)
, i.e.,
B
is
\infty
-admissible.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

Condition (
2.2
) becomes less restrictive as

grows. In particular, (

) is weaker than (

) for any

Moreover, a bounded control operator

For observation operators we recall an analogous notion

.

The observation operator

is said to be

for

if there exists

and
M\geq 0
such that

\int_{0}^{t}\bigl\|CT(s)x\bigr\|_{U}^{p}\,ds\leq M\cdot\lVert x\rVert_{X}^{p}\quad\text{for all}\,\,x\in D(A).

Since

is dense in

, the previous definition implies the existence of a bounded

\mathcal{C}_{t}:X\to\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,t],U)
satisfying

Note that condition (

grows.

Remark 2.8
.

For the operator
C:Z\to U
one could also introduce the concept of
\infty
-admissibility, i.e., ask for the existence of

and

However, it is easy to see that

A last notion we need in the sequel is the

of the triple

.

Definition 2.9
.

The triple

of a state operator

Here we call

,

are positive.

In this section we give a generalization of a positive perturbation theorem due to Bátkai et al. in

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A:D(A)\subseteq X\to X
be the generator of a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on a Banach lattice

. Assume that

are positive linear operators, where

(3.1)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826ptA_{BC}

\displaystyle:=\left(A_{-1}+BC\right)|_{X},

\displaystyle D(A_{BC})

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{x\in X:A_{-1}x+BCx\in X\bigr\}

-semigroup

on

Remark 3.2
.

As already mentioned, this result generalizes

, Thm.1.2]

In order to prove this theorem we need some preparation. First, by rescaling and in virtue of

we can in the sequel always assume that

and take

Next, under the assumptions of

the observation operator

is bounded, hence
\infty
-admissible for all

, cf.

2.8
. The following result shows that the control operator

is
\infty
-admissible as well. To verify this claim we use the spaces

of all continuous

-valued functions vanishing at infinity endowed by the
\infty
-norm and
T([0,t],U)

of all

-valued step functions defined on

for some

. Note that the latter is a normed sublattice of

, and its closure in

Lemma 3.3
.

If
B:U\to X_{-1}
is a positive linear operator, where
U
is an AM-space, then
B
is
\infty
-admissible for any

and
\mathcal{B}_{t}:\mathrm{C}([0,t],U)\to X
defined by (

) is positive and satisfies

(3.3)

\lVert\mathcal{B}_{t}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(C,X)}\leq\bigl\lVert A_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U,X)}\quad\text{for all}\,\,t>0.

Proof.

This proof follows in part the ones of
[
BJVW18
, Prop.4.2 and Lem.4.3.(ii)]
.
We verify the conditions (i) and (ii) of
Proposition

2.5
for
\mathcal{D}=T([0,t],U)
. Fix
t>0
and take
u\in T([0,t],U)
, i.e.,

u(s):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbbm{1}_{I_{n}}(s)\,u_{n},\quad s\in[0,t],

where
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\in U
,
I_{1},\dots,I_{N}\subseteq[0,t]
are pairwise disjoint intervals such that
\dot{\bigcup}_{n=1}^{N}I_{n}=[0,t]
and
\mathbbm{1}_{I_{n}}
is the characteristic function of
I_{n}
. Then

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)\,ds&=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu_{n}\,ds,\end{split}

where
t_{n-1}\leq t_{n}
denote the endpoints of
I_{n}
. Since

(3.4)

\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu_{n}\,ds=\bigl(T(t-t_{n-1})-T(t-t_{n})\bigr)A_{-1}^{-1}Bu_{n}\in X

we obtain (i), i.e.,
\mathcal{B}_{t}:T([0,t],U)\to X
is well-defined.

In order to prove condition (ii), we first assume that
0\leq u\in T([0,t],U)
, i.e.,
u_{n}\geq 0
for all
n=1,\dots,N
. Next define

\bar{u}:=\sup_{n=1,\dots,N}u_{n}\geq 0

which satisfies
0\leq u(s)\leq\bar{u}
for all
s\in[0,t]
. Using the positivity of
\lambda R(\lambda,A_{-1})B
for
\lambda>\max\{0,\operatorname{s}(A)\}
and
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
we conclude

\begin{split}0&\leq\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\lambda R(\lambda,A_{-1})Bu(s)\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}T(s)\lambda R(\lambda,A_{-1})B\bar{u}\,ds\\
&=-\lambda R(\lambda,A)A_{-1}^{-1}B\bar{u}\end{split}

for all
\lambda>\max\{0,\operatorname{s}(A)\}
.
Since
\lambda R(\lambda,A)\to Id
in
X
and, by
[
EN00
, Prop.A.3]
,

\lambda R(\lambda,A_{-1})T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)\to T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)

uniformly for
s\in[0,t]
in
X_{-1}
as
\lambda\to+\infty
this implies

(3.5)

0\leq\mathcal{B}_{t}u=\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)\,ds\leq-A_{-1}^{-1}B\bar{u}.

Hence,

\begin{split}\left\lVert\mathcal{B}_{t}u\right\rVert_{X}&\leq\bigl\lVert A_{-1}^{-1}B\bar{u}\bigr\rVert_{X}\leq\bigl\lVert A_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U,X)}\cdot\Bigl\lVert\sup_{n=1,\dots,N}u_{n}\Bigr\rVert_{U}\\
&=\bigl\lVert A_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U,X)}\cdot\sup_{n=1,\dots,N}\lVert u_{n}\rVert_{U}=\bigl\lVert A_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U,X)}\cdot\lVert u\rVert_{\infty},\end{split}

where in the second line we used that
U
is an AM-space.
Now take
u\in T([0,t],U)
arbitrarily then
|u|\in T([0,t],U)
as well, and we conclude

|\mathcal{B}_{t}u|=\bigl|\mathcal{B}_{t}(u^{+}-u^{-})\bigr|\leq\mathcal{B}_{t}u^{+}+\mathcal{B}_{t}u^{-}=\mathcal{B}_{t}|u|.

Hence, for every
u\in T([0,t],U)
it follows that

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{B}_{t}u\|=\bigl\||\mathcal{B}_{t}u|\bigr\|\leq\bigl\|\mathcal{B}_{t}|u|\bigr\|\leq\bigl\lVert A_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U,X)}\cdot\bigl\lVert|u|\bigr\rVert_{\infty}=\bigl\lVert A_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U,X)}\cdot\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}\end{split}

proving condition (ii). Hence, by
Proposition

2.5
,
B
is
\infty
-admissible and the estimate (
3.3
) holds. Finally, positivity of
\mathcal{B}_{t}
follows from (
3.5
) since the closure of
T([0,t],U)
in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}([0,t],U)
contains
\mathrm{C}([0,t],U)
, cf.
Proposition

2.5
and its proof.
∎

The next preliminary result extends the operator
\mathcal{B}_{t}
defined in (
2.5
) from the interval
[0,t]
to
\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
To this end for
u\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
we define
\mathcal{B}_{0}:=0
and for
t>0
(with a little abuse of notation)
\mathcal{B}_{t}u:=\mathcal{B}_{t}(u|_{[0,t]})
. In this way we can consider
\mathcal{B}_{t}:\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)\to X
.
Recall that without loss of generality we still assume that
\omega_{0}(A)<0
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
B:U\to X_{-1}
be positive. Then,
the operator family
(\mathcal{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U),X)
is positive, strongly continuous and uniformly bounded of bound
\|A_{-1}^{-1}B\|_{\mathcal{L}(U,X)}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
, the operator
B
is
\infty
-admissible for all
t>0
. Moreover,
\mathcal{B}_{t}
is positive, and the estimate (
3.3
) holds for every
t\geq 0
and therefore the family
(\mathcal{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
uniformly bounded. To show strong continuity we define for
u\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
and
0\leq r<t
the translated function
u_{t-r}\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
by

u_{t-r}(s):=\begin{cases}u(0)&\text{if }0\leq s<t-r,\\
u(s-t+r)&\text{if }s\geq t-r.\end{cases}

Then a simple calculation shows that

\mathcal{B}_{t}u-\mathcal{B}_{r}u=\mathcal{B}_{t}(u-u_{t-r})+(T(r)-T(t))A_{-1}^{-1}Bu(0).

Clearly,
(T(r)-T(t))A_{-1}^{-1}Bu(0)\to 0
as
t-r\to 0
.
Moreover,

\|\mathcal{B}_{t}(u-u_{t-r})\|_{X}\leq\big\lVert A_{-1}^{-1}B\big\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U,X)}\cdot\lVert u-u_{t-r}\rVert_{\infty}.

Since
u\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
is uniformly continuous, we conclude

\lim_{t-r\to 0}\lVert u-u_{t-r}\rVert_{\infty}=0

which implies strong continuity of the family
(\mathcal{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
∎

In the sequel we denote by
T_{c}([0,+\infty),U)
the space of all the compactly supported
U
-valued step functions on
[0,+\infty)
, i.e., satisfying
\text{supp}(u)\subset[0,b]
for some
0\leq b<+\infty
. In particular, this implies
u(s)=0
for all
s\geq b
. Moreover, the closure of
T_{c}([0,+\infty),U)
in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}([0,+\infty),U)
contains
\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
B:U\to X_{-1}
and
C:X\to U
be positive operators. Then

\big({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}u\big)(t):=C\mathcal{B}_{t}u\quad\text{for }u\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)\text{ and }t\geq 0

defines a positive operator
{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U))
satisfying the estimates

(3.6)

\bigl\lVert{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(C_{0})}\leq\bigl\lVert\left(CA_{-1}^{-1}B\right)^{n}\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U)}\quad\text{for every $n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
. Then by
Lemma

3.4
and the boundedness of
C:X\to U
it is clear that
{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}u:[0,+\infty)\to U
is a continuous and bounded function. Hence, in any case
{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}:\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)\to C_{b}([0,+\infty),U)
is a positive and bounded operator, where
C_{b}([0,+\infty),U)
denotes the Banach lattice of all continuous, bounded
U
-valued functions on
[0,+\infty)
equipped with the sup-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. Now assume in addition that
u
has compact support contained in the interval
[0,b]
. Since
\omega_{0}(A)<0
we obtain for
t>b

\displaystyle\bigl({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}u\bigr)(t)

\displaystyle=CT(t-b)\int_{0}^{b}T_{-1}(b-s)Bu(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=CT(t-b)\,\mathcal{B}_{b}u\to 0\quad\text{as }t\to+\infty,

i.e.,
{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}u\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
. Since the functions in
\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
having compact support are dense in
\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
this implies
\operatorname{rg}({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty})\subseteq\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
, hence
0\leq{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U))
as claimed.

In order to show the estimates (
3.6
), we first observe that by (
3.4
) it follows that for every
u\in T_{c}([0,+\infty),U)
the function
0\leq t\mapsto r(t):=C\mathcal{B}_{t}u\in U
is continuous and
r(t)\to 0
as
t\to+\infty
. Hence,
r\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
which implies that
{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u={\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n-1}r
is well-defined for all
u\in T_{c}([0,+\infty),U)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Next we prove by induction that for any
0\leq u\in T_{c}([0,+\infty),U)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

(3.7)

0\leq\big({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u\big)(t)\leq\bigl(-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)^{n}\bar{u}\quad\text{for all $t\geq 0$},

where for
u(s):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}u_{n}\,\mathbbm{1}_{I_{n}}(s)
,
s\in[0,+\infty)
, with pairwise disjoint intervals
I_{n}
, we define as above
\bar{u}=\sup_{n=1,\dots,N}u_{n}\in U_{+}
.

For
n=1
this estimate follows immediately by multiplying (
3.5
) from the left by
C\geq 0
.
Now assume that (
3.7
) holds for some fixed
n\geq 1
. Then using that
-A_{-1}^{-1}B\geq 0
we obtain

\begin{split}0\leq\big({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n+1}u\big)(t)&=C\mathcal{B}_{t}\big({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u\big)\leq C\mathcal{B}_{t}\bigl(-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)^{n}\bar{u}\\
&=C\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(s)B\bigl(-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)^{n}\bar{u}\,ds\\
&=-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigl(-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)^{n}\bar{u}-CT(t)\bigl(-A_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)\bigl(-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)^{n}\bar{u}\\
&\leq\bigl(-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)^{n+1}\bar{u},\end{split}

which proves (
3.7
) by induction.
Now take an arbitrary
u\in T_{c}([0,+\infty),U)
. Then
|u|\in T_{c}([0,+\infty),U)
as well, and we conclude

|{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u|=\bigl|{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}(u^{+}-u^{-})\bigr|\leq{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u^{+}+{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u^{-}={\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}|u|,

i.e.,
|{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u|(t)\leq({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}|u|)(t)
for all
t\geq 0
.
Using (
3.7
) it follows that

(3.8)

\begin{split}\bigl\|({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u)(t)\bigr\|_{U}&=\bigl\||{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u|(t)\bigr\|_{U}\leq\bigl\|\bigl({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}|u|\bigr)(t)\bigr\|_{U}\\
&\leq\bigl\lVert\bigl(CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)^{n}\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U)}\cdot\lVert\bar{u}\rVert_{U}=\bigl\lVert\bigl(CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)^{n}\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U)}\cdot\lVert u\rVert_{\infty},\end{split}

where in the last equality we used the AM-property of
U
. This shows that the operator

{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}:T_{c}([0,+\infty),U)\subset\mathrm{L}^{\infty}([0,+\infty),U)\to\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
is bounded of bound
\lVert(CA_{-1}^{-1}B)^{n}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U)}
. Since
\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
is complete,
{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}
has a unique bounded extension
\mathcal{R}_{n}={\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n-1}\cdot\mathcal{R}_{1}
to the closure of
T_{c}([0,+\infty),U)
in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}([0,+\infty),U)
having the same bound
\lVert(CA_{-1}^{-1}B)^{n}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U)}
.
Now as in the proof of
Proposition

2.5
it follows that
\mathcal{R}_{1}|_{\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)}={\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}
, hence
\mathcal{R}_{n}|_{\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)}={\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}
which implies (
3.6
).
∎

Our last preliminary result deals with the invertibility of
Id-\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
and the Laplace transform of its inverse. The statement heavily depends on the spectral assumption
\operatorname{r}(CR(\lambda,A_{-1})B)<1
. In the sequel
\mathscr{L}(\cdot)
denotes the Laplace transform as introduced in
[
ABHN11
, Sect.1.4]
. Recall that, as always, we assume
\omega_{0}(A)<0
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
B:U\to X_{-1}
and
C:X\to U
be positive and
assume that
\operatorname{r}(CA_{-1}^{-1}B)<1
. Then for any
\lambda>0
one has
1\in\rho({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty})\cap\rho(CR(\lambda,A_{-1})B)
and

(3.9)

\mathscr{L}\big((Id-{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty})^{-1}u(\cdot)\big)(\lambda)=\big(Id-CR(\lambda,A_{-1})B\big)^{-1}\cdot\hat{u}(\lambda)

for all
u\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
.

Proof.

We start by showing that
1\in\rho(CR(\lambda,A_{-1})B)
for all
\lambda>0
.
By
Lemma

2.2
we have
R(\lambda,A_{-1})B\geq 0
for any
\lambda>\operatorname{s}(A)
. Since
\operatorname{s}(A)\leq\omega_{0}(A)<0
, the resolvent identity implies

0\leq CR(\lambda,A_{-1})B=CR(0,A_{-1})B-C\lambda R(\lambda,A)R(0,A_{-1})B\leq-CA_{-1}^{-1}B

for all
\lambda>0
.
Hence, by Proposition
A.5
we conclude that for all
\lambda>0

(3.10)

\operatorname{r}\bigl(CR(\lambda,A_{-1})B\bigr)\leq\operatorname{r}\bigl(-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)=\operatorname{r}\bigl(CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)<1,

In particular this implies
1\in\rho(CR(\lambda,A_{-1})B)
for all
\lambda>0
as claimed.

Next observe that by (
3.6
) we have

(3.11)

\operatorname{r}({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty})=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\bigl\|{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}\bigr\|^{1/n}_{\mathcal{L}(C_{0})}\leq\lim_{n\to+\infty}\bigl\|(CA_{-1}^{-1}B)^{n}\bigr\|^{1/n}_{\mathcal{L}(U)}=\operatorname{r}(CA_{-1}^{-1}B)<1.

Hence,
Id-{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}
is invertible and its inverse is given by the Neumann series

(3.12)

(Id-{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty})^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}

which converges in
\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U))
.
It only remains to show (
3.9
).

To this end we first verify that for all
u\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(3.13)

\mathscr{L}({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u)(\lambda)=\big(C\,R(\lambda,A_{-1})\,B\big)^{n}\cdot\hat{u}(\lambda),\quad\lambda>0.

Indeed, for
n=1
, recalling that
C
is bounded, for
\lambda>0
and
u\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
we obtain

\begin{split}\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{F}_{\infty}u)(\lambda)&=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}(\mathcal{F}_{\infty}u)(t)\,dt\\
&=C\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)\,ds\right)\,dt\\
&=C\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\big(T_{-1}(\cdot)B\star u(\cdot)\big)(t)\,dt\\
&=C\cdot\mathcal{L}\big(T_{-1}(\cdot)B\star u(\cdot)\big)(\lambda)=CR(\lambda,A_{-1})B\cdot\hat{u}(\lambda),\end{split}

where in the last line we have used the convolution theorem for Laplace transform (see, e.g.,
[
BE14
, Lemma 3.12]
).
If (
3.13
) holds for some
n\geq 1
, then for
u\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
and
\lambda>0
we have

\begin{split}\mathscr{L}\bigl({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n+1}u\bigr)(\lambda)&=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\Big(C\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)B({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u)(s)\,ds\Big)\,dt\\
&=C\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\Big(T_{-1}(\cdot)B\star{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u(\cdot)\Big)(t)\,dt\\
&=C\cdot\mathcal{L}\Big(T_{-1}(\cdot)B\star{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u(\cdot)\Big)(\lambda)\\
&=CR(\lambda,A_{-1})B\cdot\mathcal{L}({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u)(\lambda)=\big(C\,R(\lambda,A_{-1})\,B\big)^{n+1}\cdot\hat{u}(\lambda),\end{split}

which implies (
3.13
) by induction.
Finally, we use (
3.13
) to prove that (
3.9
) holds for all
u\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
and
\lambda>0
. To his aim, we define the sequence of continuous functions
f_{n}\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
by

f_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{k}u(\cdot).

Using (
3.13
) we immediately obtain that

\hat{f}_{n}(\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\bigl(CR(\lambda,A_{-1})B\bigr)^{k}\cdot\hat{u}(\lambda)\quad\text{ for all }\lambda>0.

Moreover, norm convergence of the Neumann series (
3.12
) yields the uniform convergence of
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to

f:=(Id-{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty})^{-1}u(\cdot)
.
By
[
ABHN11
, Thm.1.7.5]
this implies

\begin{split}&\mathscr{L}\big((Id-{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty})^{-1}u(\cdot)\big)(\lambda)=\hat{f}(\lambda)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\hat{f}_{n}(\lambda)\\
&=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\bigl(CR(\lambda,A_{-1})B\bigr)^{k}\cdot\hat{u}(\lambda)=\big(Id-CR(\lambda,A_{-1})B\big)^{-1}\cdot\hat{u}(\lambda)\end{split}

for all
u\in\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
and
\lambda>0
, i.e., (
3.9
).
∎

We have now all the necessary tools to prove the main result of this section.

Proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

We define an operator family
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(X)
and prove that it is a
C_{0}
-semigroups with generator
A_{BC}
given in (
3.1
).

To this end we first introduce the positive bounded linear operator
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}:X\to\mathrm{C}_{0}([0,+\infty),U)
by
(\mathcal{C}_{\infty}x)(t):=CT(t)x
.
Then we use the invertibility of
Id-\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
proved in
Lemma

3.6
to define

(3.14)

S(t):=T(t)+\mathcal{B}_{t}(Id-\mathcal{F}_{\infty})^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{L}(X)\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

Now by

3.4
, the operator family
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is strongly continuous on

and uniformly bounded.
The same computations as in
[
ABE14
, Proof of Thm.10]
then show that

\mathscr{L}\bigl(S(\cdot)x\bigr)(\lambda)=R(\lambda,A_{BC})x\quad\text{for all $\lambda>0$ and $x\in X$}.

By
[
ABHN11
, Thm.3.1.7]
, this implies that the operator family (
3.14
) is a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
with generator
A_{BC}
defined in (

). Finally, the variation of parameters formula (

) follows as in
[

, Thm.10]
.
∎

3.2.
Positive Structured Perturbations factorized via AL-Spaces

The following result generalizes a perturbation theorem for positive semigroups due to Desch
[
Des88
]
and Voigt
[
Voi89
]
, see
Remark

3.8
. With respect to our setting from
Section

2
we assume throughout this subsection that
\operatorname{rg}(B)\subseteq X
which implies that
B\in\mathcal{L}(U,X)
is bounded.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
A:D(A)\subseteq X\to X
be the generator of a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on a Banach lattice
X
. Assume
B:U\to X
and
C:D(A)\to U
to be positive linear operators, where
U
is an AL-space.
If the spectral radius
\operatorname{r}(CR(\lambda,A)B)<1
for some
\lambda>\omega_{0}(A)
, then the operator

(3.15)

A_{BC}:=A+BC\quad\text{with domain}\,\,D(A_{BC})=D(A)

generates a positive
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
X
satisfying the variation of parameters formula

(3.16)

S(t)x:=T(t)x+\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\cdot BC\cdot S(s)x\,ds,\quad x\in D(A),\,\,t\geq 0.

Remark 3.8
.

As already mentioned, this result generalizes the main result in
[
Des88
]
and
[
Voi89
]
, see also
[
BKFR17
, Sect.13.3]
or
[
BA06
, Sect.5.2.1]
, where it is assumed that
X=U
and
B=Id
. In particular, in these works one needs
X
to be an AL-space while in
Theorem

3.7
only the boundary space
U
has to be of the type AL while
X
is allowed to be an arbitrary Banach lattice. See
Section

4.3
for an example where this generalization is crucial.

The proof of
Theorem

3.7
is structured similarly as the one of
Theorem

3.1
. We note that by the rescaling argument from
[
EN00
, Sect.II.2.2]
we again assume
\omega_{0}(A)<0
and choose
\lambda=0
throughout the proof.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem

3.7
the control operator
B:U\to X
is bounded and hence
1
-admissible for all
t>0
, cf.
Remark

2.6
. Next we show that the observation operator
C:D(A)\to U
is
1
-admissible as well.

Lemma 3.9
.

If
C:D(A)\to U
is a positive linear operator and
U
is an AL-space, then
C
is 1-admissible for any
t>0
and

\mathcal{C}_{t}:X\to\mathrm{L}^{1}([0,+\infty),U)
defined by (
2.7
) satisfies

(3.17)

\lVert\mathcal{C}_{t}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,\mathrm{L}^{1})}\leq\bigl\lVert CA^{-1}\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,U)}\quad\text{for all $t>0$}.

Proof.

We follow in part the proof of
[
BKFR17
, Prop.13.7]
.
First take
0\leq x\in D(A)
. Since
U
is an AL-space and
CT(s)x\geq 0
for all
s\geq 0
we conclude

(3.18)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl\lVert CT(s)x\bigr\rVert_{U}\,ds&=\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}CT(s)x\,ds\right\rVert_{U}\leq\left\lVert\int_{0}^{+\infty}CT(s)x\,ds\right\rVert_{U}\\
&=\bigl\lVert-CA^{-1}x\bigr\rVert_{U}\leq\bigl\lVert CA^{-1}\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,U)}\cdot\lVert x\rVert_{X}.\end{split}

If
x\in D(A)
we decompose it as
x=x^{+}-x^{-}
and define for
\lambda>0

\displaystyle x_{\lambda}

\displaystyle:=\lambda R(\lambda,A)x\in D(A),

\displaystyle x_{\lambda}^{P}

\displaystyle:=\lambda R(\lambda,A)x^{+}\in D(A)_{+},

\displaystyle x_{\lambda}^{N}

\displaystyle:=\lambda R(\lambda,A)x^{-}\in D(A)_{+}.

Then
x_{\lambda}\to x
,
x_{\lambda}^{P}\to x^{+}
and
x_{\lambda}^{N}\to x^{-}
in
X
as
\lambda\to+\infty
. Moreover,
if
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{X_{1}}
denotes the norm of
X_{1}=D(A)
given by
\|x\|_{X_{1}}=\|Ax\|_{X}
, then also

\begin{split}\lVert x_{\lambda}-x\rVert_{X_{1}}=\bigl\lVert\lambda R(\lambda,A)x-x\bigr\rVert_{X_{1}}=\bigl\lVert\lambda R(\lambda,A)Ax-Ax\bigr\rVert_{X}\to 0,\end{split}

i.e.,
x_{\lambda}\to x
in
X_{1}
as
\lambda\to+\infty
.
Since for all
s\in[0,t]
we have

\bigl\lvert CT(s)x_{\lambda}\bigr\rvert\leq CT(s)\big(x_{\lambda}^{P}+x_{\lambda}^{N}\big)

using (
3.18

\displaystyle A^{-1}\Bigl(T(t-a_{j})A_{-1}^{-1}Bv_{k}^{\prime}(a_{j})e_{k}-T(t-b_{j})A_{-1}^{-1}Bv_{k}^{\prime}(b_{j})e_{k}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle A^{-1}\int_{a_{j}}^{b_{j}}T(t-s)A_{-1}^{-1}Bv_{k}^{\prime\prime}(s)e_{k}\,ds.

Therefore, the series in (
3.37
) converges to an element in
D(A)\subseteq Z
.
As far as
R
is concerned, if
t\in(a_{j_{t}},b_{j_{t}})
, we recall that
v_{k}:=|u_{k}|_{|[a_{j_{t}},t]}\in C^{2}((a_{j_{t}},t),\mathbb{R})
and
v_{k}(a_{j_{t}})=0
. Hence, integrating again twice by parts, we obtain

(3.38)

\begin{split}\int_{a_{j_{t}}}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)B&v_{k}(s)e_{k}\,ds=-A_{-1}^{-1}Bv_{k}(t)e_{k}\\
+&A^{-1}\Big(T_{-1}(t-a_{j_{t}})A_{-1}^{-1}Bv_{k}^{\prime}(a_{j_{t}})e_{k}-A_{-1}^{-1}Bv_{k}^{\prime}(t)e_{k}\Big)\\
+&A^{-1}\int_{a_{j_{t}}}^{t}T(t-s)A_{-1}^{-1}Bv_{k}^{\prime\prime}(s)e_{k}\,ds.\end{split}

Since the triple
(A,B,C)
is compatible, the first term on the right-hand-side of (
3.38
) belongs to
Z
and the remaining ones to
D(A)
, hence the integral belongs to
Z
. By (
3.37
) this implies that
\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)B|u_{k}(s)|e_{k}\,ds\in Z
for all

. Hence,
({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}|u|)(t)
is well-defined for all
u\in\mathcal{D}
.
Since
u^{+}=\frac{|u|+u}{2}
and
u^{-}=\frac{|u|-u}{2}
this also implies that
({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}u^{+})(t)
and
({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}u^{-})(t)
are well-defined for all
u\in\mathcal{D}
.

Next we verify that
({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}u)(t)\geq 0
for all
u\in|\mathcal{D}|
. Using the positivity of
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
and
\lambda R(\lambda,A_{-1})B
for
\lambda>\max\{0,\operatorname{s}(A)\}
we conclude

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)\lambda R(\lambda,A_{-1})Bu(s)\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\lambda R(\lambda,A_{-1})T_{-1}(s)B\bar{u}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda R(\lambda,A_{-1})T_{-1}(s)B\bar{u}\,ds=-\lambda R(\lambda,A)A_{-1}^{-1}B\bar{u}

for all
\lambda>\max\{0,\operatorname{s}(A)\}
, where
\bar{u}
is given by (
3.31
).
Since
\lambda R(\lambda,A_{-1})\to Id
in
X
and
X_{-1}
we conclude that
-\lambda R(\lambda,A)A_{-1}^{-1}B\bar{u}\to-A_{-1}^{-1}B\bar{u}
and, by
[
EN00
, Prop.A.3]
,

\lambda R(\lambda,A_{-1})T_{-1}(s)Bu(t-s)\to T_{-1}(s)Bu(t-s)\quad\text{as $\lambda\to+\infty$}

uniformly for
s\in[0,t]
in
X_{-1}
. Hence, passing to the limit as

we obtain

0\leq\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)\,ds\leq-A_{-1}^{-1}B\bar{u}.

Multiplying this inequality by the positive operator
C:Z\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
from the left we conclude that

(3.39)

0\leq({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}u)(t)\leq-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bar{u}\quad\text{for all $u\in|\mathcal{D}|$}

as claimed.

We proceed by proving (i). Let
u\in\mathcal{D}
. First we verify that
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}u\in\mathrm{C}_{0}((0,+\infty),U_{\infty})
.

Clearly,
(\mathcal{F}_{\infty}u)(0)=0
. Moreover, by (
3.35
) we have

(3.40)

\begin{split}(\mathcal{F}_{\infty}u)(t)=-CA_{-1}^{-1}Bu(t)&-CA_{-1}^{-2}Bu^{\prime}(t)\\
&+CA^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)A_{-1}^{-1}Bu^{\prime\prime}(s)ds,\end{split}

which defines a continuous map
[0,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto(\mathcal{F}_{\infty}u)(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Next we show that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{\infty}u)(t)=0
. Since
u
is compactly supported there exists
b>0
such that
u(t)=0
for all
t\geq b
. Clearly, this implies
u^{\prime}(t)=u^{\prime\prime}(t)=0
for all
t>b
, and therefore

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to+\infty}CA_{-1}^{-1}Bu(t)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to+\infty}CA_{-1}^{-2}Bu^{\prime}(t)=0.\end{split}

Moreover, for

the last term in (
3.40
) becomes

\begin{split}CA^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)A_{-1}^{-1}Bu^{\prime\prime}(s)\,ds&=CA^{-1}\int_{0}^{b}T(t-s)A_{-1}^{-1}Bu^{\prime\prime}(s)\,ds\\
&=CA^{-1}T(t-b)\int_{0}^{b}T(b-s)A_{-1}^{-1}Bu^{\prime\prime}(s)\,ds.\end{split}

Here the integral term is independent of
t
, while
\lVert T(t-b)\rVert\to 0
as
t\to+\infty
. This implies that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}CA^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}T(t-s)A_{-1}^{-1}Bu^{\prime\prime}(s)\,ds=0

and hence
\lim_{t\to+\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{\infty}u)(t)=0
for any
u\in\mathcal{D}
.

Next, using (
3.39
) we obtain for any
u=u^{+}-u^{-}\in\mathcal{D}
and
t\geq 0

\displaystyle\bigl|(\mathcal{F}_{\infty}u)\bigr|(t)

\displaystyle=\left\lvert({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}u^{+})(t)-({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}u^{-})(t)\right\rvert

(3.41)

\displaystyle\leq({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}u^{+})(t)+({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}u^{-})(t)

\displaystyle=\bigl({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}|u|\bigr)(t)\leq-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\,\bar{u}.

This yields that for all
u\in\mathcal{D}
and
t\geq 0
we have

\bigl\lVert(\mathcal{F}_{\infty}u)(t)\bigr\rVert_{U_{\infty}}\leq\bigl\lVert CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bar{u}\bigr\rVert_{U_{\infty}}\leq\lVert CA_{-1}^{-1}B\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U_{\infty})}\cdot\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}.

Passing to the supremum over all
t\geq 0
we obtain

(3.42)

\lVert\mathcal{F}_{\infty}u\rVert_{\infty}\leq\lVert CA_{-1}^{-1}B\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U_{\infty})}\cdot\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}\quad\text{for all}\,\,u\in\mathcal{D}.

Summing up, this shows that
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}:\mathcal{D}\subset\mathrm{C}_{0}((0,+\infty),U_{\infty})\rightarrow\mathrm{C}_{0}((0,+\infty),U_{\infty})
is bounded, positive and satisfies (
3.42
). Hence, there exists a unique positive extension
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{C}_{0}((0,+\infty),U_{\infty}))
satisfying
(
3.33
) for
n=1
.

In order to verify (
3.33
) for arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N}
we proceed by induction and first show that

(3.43)

\big({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}|u|\big)(t)\leq\bigl(-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)^{n}\bar{u}\quad\text{for all $u\in\mathcal{D}$, $t\geq 0$ and $n\in\mathbb{N}$},

where
\bar{u}
is given by (
3.31
).
For
n=1
this was shown in (
3.41
). Now assume it holds for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\begin{split}\big({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n+1}|u|\big)(t)&={\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}\big({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}|u|\big)(t)=C\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(s)B\bigl({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}|u|\bigr)(t-s)\,ds\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{+\infty}T_{-1}(s)B\bigl(-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)^{n}\bar{u}\,ds\\
&=-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigl(-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)^{n}\bar{u}=\bigl(-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)^{n+1}\bar{u}\end{split}

proving (
3.43
).
Arguing as in (
3.8
) this gives
\|({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u)(t)\|_{U_{\infty}}\leq\|(CA_{-1}^{-1}B)^{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}(U_{\infty})}\cdot\|u\|_{\infty}
for every
t\geq 0
. Passing the supremum over all
t\geq 0
this proves (
3.33
) and concludes the proof of (i).

(ii). Take
u\in\mathcal{D}
. Then by Fubini’s Theorem we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{s}^{+\infty}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)\,dt\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}T_{-1}(r)Bu(s)\,dr\,ds

\displaystyle=-A_{-1}^{-1}B\int_{0}^{+\infty}u(s)\,ds.

Multiplying this equation by
C
from the left, using integration by parts and
Lemma

3.3
we obtain for
u\in\mathcal{D}
that

\displaystyle-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\!u(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\!-A_{-1}^{-1}Bu(t)+\int_{0}^{t}A_{-1}^{-1}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu^{\prime}(s)\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\!u(t)\,dt+CA^{-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu^{\prime}(s)\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\!-CA_{-1}^{-1}Bu(t)\,dt+\int_{0}^{+\infty}CA^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu^{\prime}(s)\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\!-CA_{-1}^{-1}\Bigl(Bu(t)-\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu^{\prime}(s)\,ds\Bigr)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}C\int_{0}^{t}T_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)\,ds\,dt

(3.44)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}u)(t)\,dt.

Next, since

is an AL-space, we conclude for

\begin{split}\lVert\mathcal{F}_{\infty}u\rVert_{1}&\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}|u|)(t)\|_{U_{1}}dt=\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{+\infty}({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}|u|)(t)dt\Bigr\|_{U_{1}}\\
&=\Bigl\lVert-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\int_{0}^{+\infty}|u(t)|dt\Bigr\rVert_{U_{1}}\leq\lVert CA_{-1}^{-1}B\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U_{1})}\cdot\lVert u\rVert_{1}.\end{split}

Summing up, we showed that

given by (
3.32
) defines a positive linear operator
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}:\mathcal{D}\subset\mathrm{L}^{1}([0,+\infty),U_{1})\to\mathrm{L}^{1}([0,+\infty),U_{1})

\mathcal{F}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{1}([0,+\infty),U_{1}))
satisfying (
3.34
) for

.

In order to verify (

) for arbitrary

we proceed by induction and first show
that for any

and

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\bigl\lvert({\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}^{n}u)(t)\bigr\rvert\,dt\leq\bigl(-CA_{-1}^{-1}B\bigr)^{n}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\bigl|u(t)\bigr|\,dt\quad\text{for all}\,\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Lemma 3.16

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826ptA_{BC}

Lemma 3.20
.

2.2

4.1.
Abstract Domain Perturbation of Generators

As we saw in

, operators of the form “

” naturally arise by closing a linear system. Next we show that also boundary perturbations in the sense of Greiner, cf.
[

More precisely, we consider the following abstract framework.

We start with a Banach space

3

3

“Maximal” in the sense of a “big” domain, e.g., a differential operator without boundary conditions.

In order to single out a restriction

For a Banach space

We note that in

As we will see this example fits into our framework also for unbounded operators

.

Assume that

. Moreover, suppose that

or

(4.4)

As we see next the so-called abstract

4

The claim follows from the inclusions

Finally, we apply

4.2.
Heat Equation with Boundary Feedback on
\mathbf{L^{2}(\Omega)}

We give an application of
Corollary

4.4
and
Theorem

3.19
.
To this end we consider the partial differential equation

(4.8)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)&=\Delta u(t,x),\quad&&x\in\Omega,\ t\geq 0,\\
u(0,x)&=u_{0}(x),\quad&&x\in\Omega,\\
u(t,z)&=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(z,x)\,u(t,x)\,dx,\quad&&z\in\partial\Omega,\ t\geq 0,\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where

•

\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
C^{2}
-boundary
\partial\Omega
,

•

\Delta
denotes the Laplace operator on
\Omega
,

•

u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, and

\varphi\in\mathrm{C}(\partial\Omega,\overline{\Omega})
.

The well-posedness of a related heat equation with dynamic boundary conditions was studied in
[
CENN03
, Sect.6]
using a matrix approach on the state space
\mathcal{X}=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. In contrast, here we consider (
4.8
) on the space
X=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
and show that the associated operator
A^{\Phi}
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup. To this end we introduce the following spaces and operators:

•

X:=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
\partial X:=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)
,

•

A_{m}:=\Delta:D(A_{m})\subset X\to X
,
D(A_{m}):=\{f\in\mathrm{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega):\Delta f\in X\}
,

•

L:D(A_{m})\to\partial X
,
Lf:=f|_{\partial\Omega}
in the sense of traces, cf.
[
LM72
, Chap.2]
,

•

\Phi:X\to\partial X
,
(\Phi f)(z):=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(z,x)\,f(x)\,dx
,
z\in\partial\Omega
, and

•

A,A^{\Phi}\subset A_{m}
,
D(A):=\ker L
,

As shown in
[
CENN03
, Sects.3&6]
, in this context the Dirichlet operators

introduced in
Lemma

4.1
exist and are positive for all
\lambda\geq 0
. Moreover, by
[

\omega_{0}(A)<0
.
Hence, by the results of the previous subsection we can represent the operator

as in (
4.6

\partial X=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)
is neither an AM- nor an AL-space.

To overcome this difficulty observe that
\varphi\in\mathrm{C}(\partial\Omega,\overline{\Omega})
is uniformly continuous which implies that

\operatorname{rg}(\Phi)\subset\mathrm{C}(\partial\Omega)=:\widehat{\partial X}.

Hence, we can define
\widehat{\Phi}:X\to\widehat{\partial X}
,
\widehat{\Phi}f:=\Phi f
for
f\in X
which is bounded by the closed graph theorem. Moreover, let

\widehat{L_{\lambda}}:={L_{\lambda}}|_{\widehat{\partial X}}\in\mathcal{L}(\widehat{\partial X},X)
and
\widehat{L_{A}}:=-A_{-1}\widehat{L_{0}}:\widehat{\partial X}\to X_{-1}
. Then clearly we also have

A^{\Phi}=\bigl(A_{-1}+\widehat{L_{A}}\cdot\widehat{\Phi}\bigr)

where
\widehat{\Phi}\in\mathcal{L}(X,\widehat{\partial X})
and
\widehat{L_{A}}\in\mathcal{L}(\widehat{\partial X},X_{-1})
. Since
\widehat{\partial X}
equipped with the sup-norm

is an AM-space we can now apply
Corollary

4.4
. To this end we first assume in addition that
\varphi\geq 0
which obviously implies
\widehat{\Phi}\geq 0
. Then

(4.9)

\operatorname{r}\bigl(\widehat{\Phi}\widehat{L_{\lambda}}\bigr)\leq\bigl\|\widehat{\Phi}\widehat{L_{\lambda}}\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}(\widehat{\partial X})}\leq\bigl\|\widehat{\Phi}\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,\widehat{\partial X})}\cdot\bigl\|\widehat{L_{\lambda}}\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}(\widehat{\partial X},X)}.

To estimate the norm of
\widehat{L_{\lambda}}
observe that this operator is positive and for the
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
-norm on
\widehat{\partial X}=\mathrm{C}(\partial\Omega)
it holds
|y|\leq\mathbbm{1}
for all
y\in\widehat{\partial X}
satisfying
\|y\|_{\widehat{\partial X}}\leq 1
, where
\mathbbm{1}(z):=1
for all
z\in\partial\Omega
. Hence, using (

) and
[

, Lem.II.3.4.(i)]
we conclude

\displaystyle\bigl\|\widehat{L_{\lambda}}\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}(\widehat{\partial X},X)}

\displaystyle=\sup\bigl\{\bigl\|\widehat{L_{\lambda}}y\bigr\|_{X}:y\in\widehat{\partial X},\|y\|_{\widehat{\partial X}}\leq 1\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\|L_{\lambda}\mathbbm{1}\|_{X}=\|-AR(\lambda,A)L_{0}\mathbbm{1}\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\bigl\|(Id-\lambda R(\lambda,A)\bigr)L_{0}\mathbbm{1}\bigl\|_{X}\to 0

as

. Therefore, by (

\lambda>0
such that

and by

generates a positive semigroup on

.

Now let

. Then obviously

for all

. Hence, by

we obtain that for arbitrary

\displaystyle A^{\Phi}:=\Delta:D(A^{\Phi})\subset\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega),

\displaystyle D(A^{\Phi}):=\Bigl\{f\in\mathrm{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega):\Delta f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega),\,f(z)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(z,x)\,f(x)\,dx,\,z\in\partial\Omega\Bigr\}

generates a

) can be rewritten as (

4.3.
Unbounded Perturbation of the First Derivative on
\mathbf{C_{0}(0,1]}

We give an application of
Theorems

3.7
and
3.19
. To this end we choose

•

,

which is an AL-space,

•

,

,

•

,
(Pf)(x):=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{b(x-r)}{r^{\alpha}}\cdot f(r)\,dr
for

and

, and

•

We claim that

generates a

-semigroup which is positive in case

.

For the proof we first observe that
A
generates the positive nilpotent right-shift semigroup on

, cf.
[
EN00
, Sect.I.4.17]
. Then we factorize

where

•

B:U\to X
,
(Bu)(x):=(b*u)(x)
, and

•

(Cf)(x):=\frac{f(x)}{x^{\alpha}}

Indeed,

\operatorname{rg}(C)\subset U
.

In order to apply
Theorem

3.7
we first assume that
b\geq 0
which implies
B,C\geq 0
. Then to verify that
\operatorname{r}(CR(\lambda,A)B)<1
for

Hence, for

\displaystyle\bigl\|CR(\lambda,A)f\bigr\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl|x^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-\lambda(x-r)}f(r)\,dr\Bigr|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}x^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-\lambda(x-r)}\,dr\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{-1}x^{-\alpha}\bigl(1-e^{-\lambda x}\bigr)\,dx

Since

and

and

. By Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem it follows that

\int_{0}^{1}r_{\lambda}(x)\,dx\to 0\quad\text{as $\lambda\to+\infty$}.

By (

and from (

for which

3.19
.

.

Appendix

A.1.
Banach Lattices and Positive Operators

In this appendix we briefly recall some basic facts about Banach lattices and positive operators. Fore more details we refer to
[
Nag86
, Chap.C-I]
,
[
BKFR17
, Chap.10]
or
[
BA06
, Sect.2.2]
. Moreover, we state and proof a Riesz-Thorin type theorem for
\mathbb{R}^{N}
-valued functions.

A real vector space
X
equipped with a partial order
\leq
is a
vector lattice
if for any pair
x,y\in X
of elements the greatest lower bound
\inf\{x,y\}\in X
and the least upper bound
\sup\{x,y\}\in X
exist, and the order is compatible with the vector space structure, i.e.,

•

x\leq y
implies
x+z\leq y+z
for all
x,y,z\in X
;

•

0\leq x
implies
0\leq\alpha x
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

For a vector lattice
X
we denote by

X_{+}:=\{x\in X:\,0\leq x\}

its
positive cone
. Moreover, for
x\in X
we define its
positive part

x^{+}=\sup\{x,0\}
, its
negative part

x^{-}=\sup\{-x,0\}
and its
absolute value

|x|=\sup\{x,-x\}
. In this case we have
x=x^{+}-x^{-}
and
|x|=x^{+}+x^{-}
.

A norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
on a vector lattice
X
is called a
lattice norm
if it is compatible with the order, i.e., if

(A.1)

|x|\leq|y|\Rightarrow\lVert x\rVert\leq\lVert y\rVert\quad\text{for }x,y\in X.

Note that for a lattice norm we always have
\||x|\|=\|x\|
for all
x\in X
.
If a real vector lattice
X
endowed with a lattice norm is complete, then
X
is called a real
Banach lattice
.
A Banach lattice
X
is said to be an

•

AL-space
, if
\|x+y\|=\|x\|+\|y\|
for all
x,y\geq 0
,

•

AM-space
, if
\|\sup\{x,y\}\|=\sup\{\|x\|,\|y\|\}
for all
x,y\geq 0
.

We remark that if for some Banach lattice
X
there exist two comparable norms
\|\cdot\|_{1}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
such that
(X\|\cdot\|_{1})
is of type AL and
(X\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
of type AM, then
X\simeq\mathbb{R}^{N}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

A vector subspace
\mathcal{C}
of a vector lattice
X
is called
vector sublattice
if for all
x\in\mathcal{C}
one has
|x|\in\mathcal{C}
, hence also
x^{+},x^{-}\in\mathcal{C}
.

Remark A.1
.

For a Banach lattice
X
, the operations
\sup\{\cdot,\cdot\}
and
\inf\{\cdot,\cdot\}
are continuous. This implies that the positive cone
X_{+}
is closed (cf.
[
BKFR17
, Prop.10.8]
).

Besides
\mathbb{R}^{N}
the standard examples of Banach lattices are the spaces
l^{p}
,
\mathrm{C}(K)
,
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega;\mu)
equipped with the natural order and the canonical norms. In particular

•

(\mathbb{R}^{N},|\cdot|_{1})
,
l^{1}
and
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mu)
are AL-spaces,

•

(\mathbb{R}^{N},|\cdot|_{\infty})
,
c
,
c_{0}
,
l^{\infty}
,
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mu)
,
\mathrm{C}(K)
and
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\Omega)
are AM-spaces,

see
[
BKFR17
, Expl.10.6]
.

Definition A.2
.

If
X
and
Y
are two real Banach lattices, an operator
T:X\to Y
is said to be
positive
if
Tx\in Y_{+}
for every
x\in X_{+}
. In this case we use the notation
T\geq 0
. Operators which can be written as the difference of two positive operators are called
regular
.

We remark that any linear positive operator
T:X\to Y
between Banach lattices is bounded, see
[
BKFR17
, Thm.10.20]
.
An important characterization of positive operators is the following.

Proposition A.3
.

[
BKFR17
, Lem.10.18]

For a linear operator
T:X\to Y
between two real Banach lattices, the following are equivalent:

(a)

T\geq 0
;

(b)

(Tx)^{+}\leq Tx^{+}
and
(Tx)^{-}\leq Tx^{-}
for all
x\in X
;

(c)

|Tx|\leq T|x|
for all
x\in X
.

We denote by
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)_{+}
the set of positive linear operators from the Banach lattice
X
to the Banach lattice
Y
. For positive operators the operator norm is given by

\lVert T\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}:=\sup\bigl\{\lVert Tx\rVert_{Y}:x\in X_{+}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\lVert x\rVert_{X}\leq 1\bigr\}.

When there is no risk of ambiguity the operator norm is simply denoted by
\lVert\cdot\rVert
.

Since the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{Y}
on the Banach lattice
Y
satisfies (
A.1
), the operator norm inherits the same property, i.e., if
S,T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)_{+}
satisfy
S\leq T
, then
\lVert S\rVert\leq\lVert T\rVert
.

We recall that for a bounded operator
T
on a Banach space
X
its
spectrum

\sigma(T):=\bigl\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\lambda-T\text{ is not bijective}\bigr\}

is a nonempty, compact subset of
\mathbb{C}
. Consequently, the
resolvent set

\rho(A):=\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(A)
is an open, nonempty subset of
\mathbb{C}
.
The
spectral radius
of
T
is defined as

\operatorname{r}(T):=\sup\bigl\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\sigma(T)\bigr\}

which satisfies
\operatorname{r}(T)\leq\lVert T\rVert
(see
[
EN00
, Cor.IV.1.4]
).

The spectral radius can be calculated using
Gelfand’s formula

Proposition A.4
.

[
BKFR17

For an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(X)
on a Banach space
X

(A.2)

\operatorname{r}(T)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert T^{n}\rVert^{1/n}.

Moreover, if
|\lambda|>\operatorname{r}(T)
then
R(\lambda,T)
is given by the
Neumann series

R(\lambda,T)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{-(n+1)}\cdot T^{n}.

The identity (
A.2

Proposition A.5
.

Let
S,T:X\to X
be linear operators on a Banach lattice
X
satisfying
|Sx|\leq Tx
for every
x\geq 0
. Then

(i)

|S^{n}x|\leq T^{n}|x|
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in X
;

(ii)

S,T\in\mathcal{L}(X)
and
\|S^{n}\|\leq\|T^{n}\|
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

(iii)

\operatorname{r}(S)\leq\operatorname{r}(T)
.

Proof.

We start by proving (i) by induction. For
n=1
we have for
x=x^{+}-x^{-}\in X

|Sx|=\bigl|S(x^{+}-x^{-})\bigr|\leq|Sx^{+}|+|Sx^{-}|\leq T(x^{+}+x^{-})=T|x|

as claimed. Now assume that (i) holds for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\bigl|S^{n+1}x\bigr|=\bigl|S^{n}Sx\bigr|\leq T^{n}|Sx|\leq T^{n}T|x|=T^{n+1}|x|

which proves (i). To prove (ii) we first observe that by
[
BKFR17
, Thm.10.20]
the operator
T
is bounded. Now take
x\in X
. Then from (i) we obtain

\|S^{n}x\|=\bigl\||S^{n}x|\bigr\|\leq\|T^{n}|x|\|\leq\|T^{n}\|\cdot\||x|\|=\|T^{n}\|\cdot\|x\|.

This implies (ii). Finally, using (ii) we then conclude that

\operatorname{r}(S)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert S^{n}\rVert^{1/n}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert T^{n}\rVert^{1/n}=\operatorname{r}(T)

as claimed.
∎

Finally, for the generator
A
of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
we introduce its
spectral bound

\operatorname{s}(A)
and
growth bound

\omega_{0}(A)
by

\displaystyle\operatorname{s}(A)

\displaystyle:=\sup\bigl\{\operatorname{Re}(\lambda):\lambda\in\sigma(A)\bigr\},

\displaystyle\omega_{0}(A)

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\omega\in\mathbb{R}:\exists M_{\omega}\geq 1\text{ such that }\bigl\|T(t)\bigr\|\leq M_{\omega}\cdot e^{\omega t}\ \forall t\geq 0\bigr\}.

Then by
[
EN00
, Cor.II.1.13]
we always have
-\infty\leq\operatorname{s}(A)\leq\omega_{0}(A)<+\infty
.

The following result is a version of the Riesz–Thorin convexity theorem for positive operators defined on spaces of vector-valued functions. The proof of this theorem is inspired by
[
Haa07
]
: we first give a Hölder-type inequality for these operators and then use the latter to provide an estimate on their norms.

Theorem A.6
.

Let
T_{0}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{C}_{0}((0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{N}))
and
T_{1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{1}([0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{N}))
be positive operators such that
\|T_{0}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C_{0})}\leq M_{0}
,
\|T_{1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{1})}\leq M_{1}
and

T_{0}f=T_{1}f
for all
f\in\mathcal{C}:=C_{c}((0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Then for any
1<p<\infty
the operator

T:\mathcal{C}\subset\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathrm{L}^{1}([0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap\mathrm{C}_{0}((0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{N})

given by
Tf:=T_{0}f=T_{1}f
for
f\in\mathcal{C}
, possesses a unique positive bounded extension (still denoted by
T
)
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{N}))
satisfying

(A.3)

\lVert T\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{p})}\leq M_{0}^{1-1/p}\cdot M_{1}^{1/p}.

Proof.

By elementary calculus one can easily verify that for real numbers
a,b\geq 0
and all
1\leq p\leq\infty
one has

(A.4)

ab=\inf_{t>0}\biggl(\dfrac{t^{p}}{p}\,a^{p}+\dfrac{t^{-p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\,b^{p^{\prime}}\biggr),

where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
, i.e.,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.
Using this fact we next prove a Hölder-type inequality for positive operators.

To this end we take
0\leq f,g\in\mathcal{C}
. If
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{N})^{T}
and
g=(g_{1},\dots,g_{N})^{T}
, we define the
p
-th power of
f
and the product of
fg
componentwise as

f^{p}:=\left(\begin{matrix}f_{1}^{p}\\
\vdots\\
f_{N}^{p}\end{matrix}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad fg:=\left(\begin{matrix}f_{1}g_{1}\\
\vdots\\
f_{N}g_{N}\end{matrix}\right).

Since (
A.4
) holds for any component of
(fg)(s)
for any
s\geq 0
we conclude

(A.5)

f\cdot g=\left(\begin{matrix}f_{1}g_{1}\\
\vdots\\
f_{N}g_{N}\end{matrix}\right)\leq\dfrac{t^{p}}{p}\left(\begin{matrix}f_{1}^{p}\\
\vdots\\
f_{N}^{p}\end{matrix}\right)+\dfrac{t^{-p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\left(\begin{matrix}g_{1}^{p^{\prime}}\\
\vdots\\
g_{N}^{p^{\prime}}\end{matrix}\right)=\dfrac{t^{p}}{p}f^{p}+\dfrac{t^{-p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}g^{p^{\prime}}\quad\text{for all $t>0$.}

Since also
f^{p},g^{p^{\prime}},fg\in\mathcal{C}_{+}
, we can apply the positive operator
T
to (
A.5
) and obtain

T(fg)\leq\dfrac{t^{p}}{p}\,Tf^{p}+\dfrac{t^{-p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\,Tg^{p^{\prime}},

i.e.,

\bigl[T(fg)\bigr](s)\leq\dfrac{t^{p}}{p}\bigl[Tf^{p}\bigr](s)+\dfrac{t^{-p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\bigl[Tg^{p^{\prime}}\bigr](s)\quad\text{for all $t>0$ and $s\geq 0$.}

Passing, for fixed
s\geq 0
, component-wise to the infimum over all
t>0
, by (
A.4
) it follows that for any
i=1,\dots,N

\bigl[T(fg)\bigr]_{i}(s)\leq\bigl[Tf^{p}\bigr]_{i}^{1/p}(s)\cdot\bigl[Tg^{p^{\prime}}\bigr]_{i}^{1/p^{\prime}}(s)\quad\text{for all $s\geq 0$,}

i.e., we obtain the Hölder-type inequality

(A.6)

T(fg)\leq\bigl(Tf^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}\cdot\bigl(Tg^{p^{\prime}}\bigr)^{1/p^{\prime}}\quad\text{for all $f,g\in\mathcal{C}_{+}$. }

To conclude the proof, for
f\in\mathcal{C}_{+}
we choose
0<a<b<+\infty
such that
\text{supp}(f)\subset[a,b]
. Then there exists a function
\mathbbm{1}_{f}:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfying

•

\mathbbm{1}_{f}\in\mathcal{C}
;

•

0\leq\mathbbm{1}_{f}(s)\leq 1
for all
s\geq 0
;

•

\mathbbm{1}_{f}(s)=1
for all
s\in[a,b]
;

•

\mathbbm{1}_{f}(s)=0
for all
s\in[0,\frac{a}{2}]\cap[b+1,+\infty)
.

Hence,
f\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{f}=f
. Since
f,\mathbbm{1}_{f}\in\mathcal{C}_{+}
, we can take
g=\mathbbm{1}_{f}
in (
A.6
) and obtain that

\bigl(T(f\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{f})\bigr)^{p}=(Tf)^{p}\leq\bigl(Tf^{p}\bigr)\cdot\bigl(T\mathbbm{1}_{f}^{p^{\prime}}\bigr)^{p/p^{\prime}},

which implies that for any
s\geq 0
we have

\begin{split}\bigl|(Tf)(s)\bigr\rvert_{p}^{p}&=\bigl\lvert(Tf)^{p}(s)\bigr\rvert_{1}\leq\bigl\lvert(Tf^{p})(s)\cdot(T\mathbbm{1}_{f}^{p^{\prime}})^{p/p^{\prime}}(s)\bigr\rvert_{1}\\
&\leq\bigl\lvert(Tf^{p})(s)\bigr\rvert_{1}\cdot\bigl\lvert(T\mathbbm{1}_{f}^{p^{\prime}})^{p/p^{\prime}}(s)\bigr\rvert_{\infty},\end{split}

where as usual
|\cdot|_{p}
denotes the
p
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Integrating for
s\geq 0
we conclude

\begin{split}\lVert Tf\rVert_{p}^{p}&=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\bigl\lvert(Tf)(s)\bigr\rvert_{p}^{p}\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\bigl\lvert(Tf^{p})(s)\bigr\rvert_{1}\cdot\bigl\lvert(T\mathbbm{1}_{f}^{p^{\prime}})^{p/p^{\prime}}(s)\bigr\rvert_{\infty}\,ds\\
&\leq\bigl\lVert Tf^{p}\bigr\rVert_{1}\cdot\bigl\lVert(T\mathbbm{1}_{f}^{p^{\prime}})^{p/p^{\prime}}\bigr\rVert_{\infty}=\bigl\lVert Tf^{p}\bigr\rVert_{1}\cdot\bigl\lVert T\mathbbm{1}_{f}^{p^{\prime}}\bigr\rVert_{\infty}^{p-1}\\
&\leq M_{1}\cdot\lVert f\rVert_{p}^{p}\cdot M_{0}^{p-1}\bigl\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{f}^{p^{\prime}}\bigr\rVert_{\infty}^{p-1}\leq M_{1}\cdot M_{0}^{p-1}\cdot\lVert f\rVert_{p}^{p},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used the fact that
\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{f}^{p^{\prime}}\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1
. Therefore, for any
f\in\mathcal{C}_{+}
we proved that

(A.7)

\lVert Tf\rVert_{p}\leq M_{0}^{1-1/p}\cdot M_{1}^{1/p}\cdot\lVert f\rVert_{p}.

Now take
f\in\mathcal{C}
. Then
|f|\in\mathcal{C}_{+}
, hence (
A.7
) implies

(A.8)

\lVert Tf\rVert_{p}\leq\bigl\lVert T|f|\bigr\rVert_{p}\leq M_{0}^{1-1/p}\cdot M_{1}^{1/p}\cdot\lVert f\rVert_{p}.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is dense in
\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
1\leq p<\infty
, the operator
T:\mathcal{C}\subset\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{N})
extends (uniquely) to a linear positive operator
T:\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{N})
still satisfying (
A.8
), hence (
A.3
).
∎

Subspace Semigroups

The following abstract result is needed in
Section

.

Lemma A.7
.

Let
\widetilde{A}:\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{X}
be the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(\widetilde{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on a Banach space
\widetilde{X}
. Suppose that
X
is a Banach space such that

(i)

\widetilde{X}_{1}\hookrightarrow X\hookrightarrow\widetilde{X}
with continuous injections, where
\widetilde{X}_{1}:=(D(\widetilde{A}),\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{A}})
,

(ii)

\widetilde{T}(t)X\subseteq X
for all
t\geq 0
,

(iii)

(T(t))_{t\geq 0}:=(\widetilde{T}(t)|_{X})_{t\geq 0}
defines a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
.

If
A:D(A)\subset X\to X
denotes the generator of
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
then the following holds:

(1)

\rho(A)=\rho(\widetilde{A})
and
R(\lambda,\widetilde{A})|_{X}=R(\lambda,A)
for all
\lambda\in\rho(A)
.

(2)

For
\lambda_{0}\in\rho(\widetilde{A})
there exists
M>0
such that
\|R(\lambda_{0},\widetilde{A})x\|_{X}\leq M\cdot\|x\|_{\widetilde{X}}
for all
x\in\widetilde{X}
.

(3)

\widetilde{X}\subseteq X_{-1}
with continuous injection.

(4)

R(\lambda,A_{-1})|_{\widetilde{X}}=R(\lambda,\widetilde{A})
for all
\lambda\in\rho(A)
and
T_{-1}(t)|_{\widetilde{X}}=\widetilde{T}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
.

(5)

A_{-1}|_{\widetilde{X}}=\widetilde{A}
.

Proof.

(1) is shown in
[
EN00
, Prop.IV.2.17]
. Assertion (2) follows by the continuity of
R(\lambda_{0},\widetilde{A}):\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{X}_{1}
and the continuous injection of
\widetilde{X}_{1}
into
X
. To show (3) we first note that by (1) and (2) the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{X}}
restricted to
X
is finer than the extrapolated norm
\|\cdot\|_{-1}:=\|R(\lambda_{0},A)\cdot\|_{X}
on
X
.
For the relative completions of
X
with respect to these norms this implies

\widetilde{X}=\bigl(\widetilde{X}_{1},\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{X}}\bigr)^{\sim}\subseteq\bigl(X,\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{X}}\bigr)^{\sim}\subseteq\bigl(X,\|\cdot\|_{-1}\bigr)^{\sim}=X_{-1},

where for the first equation we used that
\widetilde{X}_{1}
is dense in
\widetilde{X}
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{X}}
. This proves the third claim. In order to show (4) we define for
x\in\widetilde{X}
the sequence
x_{n}:=nR(n,\widetilde{A})x\in\widetilde{X}_{1}\subseteq X
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then as
n\to+\infty
by
[
EN00
, Lem.II.3.4.(i)]
we have
x_{n}\to x
in
\widetilde{X}
, hence by (3) also in
X_{-1}
. By (1) this implies for all
\lambda\in\rho(A)

\displaystyle R(\lambda,A_{-1})x

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}R(\lambda,A)x_{n}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}R(\lambda,\widetilde{A})\cdot nR(n,\widetilde{A})x

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}nR(n,\widetilde{A})\cdot R(\lambda,\widetilde{A})x=R(\lambda,\widetilde{A})x,

where the last equality follows again by
[
EN00
, Lem.II.3.4.(i)]
. Moreover, by the same reasoning we obtain for all
t\geq 0

\displaystyle T_{-1}(t)x

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}T(t)x_{n}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{T}(t)\cdot nR(n,\widetilde{A})x

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}nR(n,\widetilde{A})\cdot\widetilde{T}(t)x=\widetilde{T}(t)x.

This proves (4). Finally, to verify (5) take
x\in D(\widetilde{A})=\widetilde{X}_{1}\subseteq X=D(A_{-1})
. Then using (4) we conclude

\widetilde{A}x=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\tfrac{\widetilde{T}(t)x-x}{t}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\tfrac{T_{-1}(t)x-x}{t}=A_{-1}x

which implies (5) and completes the proof.
∎

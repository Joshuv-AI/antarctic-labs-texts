1.
Introduction

If
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open convex set, then
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be (classically) semiconvex if there exists
C\geq 0
such that the function

\displaystyle x\mapsto f(x)+C|x|^{2},\quad x\in G,

is convex. We say that
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
is (classically) semiconcave if
-f
is semiconvex. Then it is well-known (see
[
KZ
, Section 3]
for references) that

(1)

\displaystyle f:G\to\mathbb{R}\;\textnormal{is both semiconvex and semiconcave}\;\Longleftrightarrow f\in C^{1,1}(G).

The notion of semiconvex, resp. semiconcave, functions is generalized to the notion of funtions that are semiconvex, resp. semiconcave, with modulus
\omega
(see
[
CS
, Definition 2.1.1]
). We will call these functions
\omega
-semiconvex, resp.
\omega
-semiconcave, for short (see Definition
2.1
and Definition
2.3
below). Note that a function is semiconvex, resp. semiconcave, if and only if it is
\omega
-semiconvex, resp.
\omega
-semiconcave, with
\omega(t)=Ct
for some
C\geq 0
(see
[
CS
, Proposition 1.1.3]
). For the theory and application of
\omega
-semiconvex, resp.
\omega
-semiconcave, functions, see the monograph
[
CS
]
.

This article deals with functions that are both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave. Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open convex set and
\omega
a modulus. It is easy to show that if
f\in C^{1,\omega}(G)
, then there exists
C>0
such that
f
is both
(C\omega)
-semiconvex and
(C\omega)
-semiconcave (see
[
CS
, Proposition 2.1.2]
). So we will consider the natural problem whether also the opposite implication holds. This implication can be equivalently formulated as follows:

(2)

\displaystyle f:G\to\mathbb{R}\;\textnormal{is both}\;\omega\textnormal{-semiconvex and}\;\omega\textnormal{-semiconcave}\Longrightarrow f\in C^{1,\omega}(G).

Recall (see (
1
)) that implication (
2
) holds if
\omega(t)=Ct
for some
C\geq 0
. Further, by Remark
2.4
(i) below and
[
KZ
, p. 839]
, implication (
2
) holds if either
G
is bounded or contains a translation of a convex cone with non-empty interior. However, (
2
) doesn’t hold in general. Namely, we proved (see
[
KZ
, Theorem 1.9]
) that if
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an unbounded open convex set which does not contain a translation of a convex cone with non-empty interior, then there exists a concave modulus
\omega
such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega(t)=\infty
and (
2
) doesn’t hold. The main disadvantage of
[
KZ
, Theorem 1.9]
is that
\omega
depends on
G
and may be irregular. In this article we improve
[
KZ
, Theorem 1.9]
in the following way:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) be an unbounded open convex set that doesn’t contain a translation of a cone with non-empty interior. Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and set
\omega(t):=t^{\alpha}
for all
t\geq 0
. Then there exists
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
that is both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave and
f\notin C^{1,\alpha}(G)
.

So Theorem
1.1
gives a positive answer to
[
KZ
, Question 6.10 (3), (4)]
. By (
31
), Theorem
1.1
is a special case of Theorem
5.5
below which works with more general moduli (namely those with property (
*
‣
5
) below).

The strategy of the proof of Theorem
5.5
is similar to that used in
[
KZ
]
where we reduced (using the notion of recessive cones) the case of an open convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to the case of an open convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the special form
G_{\eta}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x>0,\;|y|<\eta(x)\}
where
\eta:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is concave and satisfies
\lim_{t\to\infty}\eta(t)/t=0
. However, the construction of
f:G_{\eta}\to\mathbb{R}
in the proof of Proposition
4.4
is much more sophisticated than the corresponding construction used in
[
KZ
]
. The present construction is based on the notion of modulus
\omega_{\eta}
(see Definition
3.1
below and for the motivation Remark
3.2
below).

It’s worth mentioning that if
\omega
is a modulus, then functions that are both
(C\omega)
-semiconvex and
(C\omega)
-semiconcave for some
C\geq 0
can be characterized via “Taylor approximation of the first order” or via “
C^{1,\omega}
-smoothness on all lines”. See Lemma
2.5
below for a precise formulation. Thus Theorem
1.1
can be reformulated as follows:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) be an unbounded open convex set that doesn’t contain a translation of a cone with non-empty interior. Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and set
\omega(t):=t^{\alpha}
for all
t\geq 0
. Then there exists
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
that is Fréchet differentiable,

(i)

for every
a\in G
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
|v|=1
, the derivative of the function
t\mapsto f(a+tv)
is uniformly continuous on
\{t\in\mathbb{R}:a+tv\in G\}
with modulus
\omega
,

(ii)

|f(x+h)-f(x)-f^{\prime}(x)[h]|\leq|h|^{1+\alpha}
for all
x,x+h\in G
and

(iii)

f\notin C^{1,\alpha}(G)
.

Let us remark that Corollary
1.2
improves
[
KZ
, Theorem 6.8]
. Note also that Theorem
5.5
can be reformulated (using Lemma
2.5
) in the same way as Theorem
1.1
and so we can get a more general statement than Corollary
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this article, the norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is always Euclidean. For
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
the symbol
\operatorname{span}(A)
denotes the linear span of
A
. By a cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we mean a set
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\lambda x\in C
for each
x\in C
and
\lambda>0
. If
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open set,
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
,
x\in G
and
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
f^{\prime}(x)
denotes the Fréchet derivative of
f
at
x
and
f^{\prime}(x)[h]
denotes its evaluation at
h
.

Definition 2.1
.

We denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of all
\omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
which are non-decreasing and satisfy
\lim_{h\to 0+}\omega(h)=0
. The members of
\mathcal{M}
are called moduli.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set,
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Then we denote by
C^{1,\omega}(G)
the set of all Fréchet differentiable
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous with modulus
C\omega
for some
C>0
. If
\omega(t)=t^{\alpha}
for every
t\geq 0
, then we also write
C^{1,\alpha}(G)
instead of
C^{1,\omega}(G)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open convex set and
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
.

•

We say that
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
is semiconvex with modulus
\omega
(or
\omega
-semiconvex for short) if

f(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\leq\lambda f(x)+(1-\lambda)f(y)+\lambda(1-\lambda)|x-y|\omega(|x-y|)

for every
x,y\in G
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
.

•

We say that
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
is semiconcave with modulus
\omega
(or
\omega
-semiconcave) if
-f
is semiconvex with modulus
\omega
.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open convex set,
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
. Then the following hold (see
[
CS
, Theorem 2.1.7]
and
[
CS
, Theorem 3.3.7]
):

(i)

If
f
is
\omega
-semiconvex or
\omega
-semiconcave, then
f
is continuous.

(ii)

If
f
is both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave, then
f\in C^{1}(G)
.

As already mentioned in the introduction, we have the following:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open convex set,
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

There exists
C_{1}>0
such that
f
is both
(C_{1}\omega)
-semiconvex and
(C_{1}\omega)
-semiconcave.

(ii)

There exists
C_{2}>0
such that for every
a\in G
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
|v|=1
, the function
t\mapsto f(a+tv)
is differentiable on
\{t\in\mathbb{R}:a+tv\in G\}
and its derivative is uniformly continuous with modulus
C_{2}\omega
.

(iii)

f
is Fréchet differentiable and there exists
C_{3}>0
such that

\displaystyle|f(x+h)-f(x)-f^{\prime}(x)[h]|\leq C_{3}|h|\omega(|h|),\quad x,x+h\in G.

Proof.

It follows from Remark
2.4
(i) and
[
KZ
, Proposition 6.4 (i), (iv)]
that (i) implies (ii) and (iii). Further, it follows easily from
[
K
, Lemma 2.3 (i)]
and
[
K
, Proposition 2.4 (ii)]
that (ii) implies (i). Finally, (iii) implies (i) by
[
KZ
, Proposition 6.4 (ii)]
.
∎

Recall that
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
is called sub-additive if
\omega(x+h)\leq\omega(x)+\omega(h)
for all
x,h\geq 0
.

Remark 2.6
.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
. Then the following hold:

(i)

If
\omega
is concave, then it is sub-additive.

(ii)

If
\omega
is sub-additive, then there exists a concave
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\omega\leq\varphi\leq 2\omega
.

Both of these statements are well-known. The proof of (i) is easy. Statement (ii) is due to Stechkin (see
[
DZ
, Lemma 2.3]
for references).

Remark 2.7
.

Let
f:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be concave. Then the following hold:

(i)

f
is non-decreasing.

(ii)

The function
t\mapsto f(t)/t
,
t>0
, is non-increasing.

(iii)

For every
t>0
and
c\geq 1
we have
f(ct)\leq cf(t)
.

All these facts are easy and well-known. Note that (iii) is just a reformulation of (ii). However, a useful one.

As in
[
KZ
]
we will use the following auxiliary notation:

.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
. Then we denote by
\mathcal{G}_{n}(\omega)
the set of all open convex sets
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which there exist
a\in G
,

and

such that
f
is both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave,
M:=\{a+\lambda x:\lambda\geq 0\}\subset G
and

is uniformly continuous on

with modulus
C\omega
for no
C>0
.

Remark 2.9
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
. Then:

(i)

By Remark
2.4
(ii) we can equivalently write
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
instead of
f\in C^{1}(G)
in the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{n}(\omega)
.

(ii)

If

, then (

) clearly doesn’t hold.

(iii)

If
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

, then it is easy to check that

.

For the proof of the following lemma see
[
KZ
, Example 5.1]
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
satisfy

\displaystyle\liminf_{h\to 0+}\frac{\omega(h)}{h}>0,\;\lim_{h\to\infty}\frac{\omega(h)}{h}=0.

Then
\mathbb{R}\times(0,1)\in\mathcal{G}_{2}(\omega)
.

As in
[
KZ
]
, we will work with recession cones of unbounded convex sets
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and use the notation
\operatorname{rec}(A)
(instead of
0^{+}A
used in
[
R
]
).

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\emptyset\neq A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex set. Then we set

\displaystyle\operatorname{rec}(A):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:(\forall a\in A)(\forall\lambda\geq 0)\;a+\lambda x\in A\}.

Now we recall some basic properties of recession cones. For the proof see
[
KZ
, Lemma 2.10]
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\emptyset\neq G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open convex set. Then the following hold:

(i)

\operatorname{rec}(G)=\operatorname{rec}(\overline{G})
.

(ii)

\operatorname{rec}(G)
is a closed convex cone and
0\in\operatorname{rec}(G)
.

(iii)

\operatorname{rec}(G)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:(\exists a\in A)(\forall\lambda\geq 0)\;a+\lambda x\in A\}
.

(iv)

If
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is unbounded, then
1\leq\dim(\operatorname{span}(\operatorname{rec}(G)))
.

(v)

\dim(\operatorname{span}(\operatorname{rec}(G)))=n
if and only if
G
contains a translation of a cone with non-empty interior.

We will also need the following special case of
[
KZ
, Lemma 5.11]
:

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\geq 2
, and
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
. Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be an open convex set and
L:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
a linear surjection. Suppose that
\omega
is concave,
L(G)\in\mathcal{G}_{2}(\omega)
and
\operatorname{rec}(L(G))\subset L(\operatorname{rec}(G))
. Then
G\in\mathcal{G}_{m}(\omega)
.

The following lemma is an easy consequence of
[
R
, Theorem 9.1]
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an unbounded open convex set and let
L:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a linear surjection such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{rec}(G)\cap L^{-1}(\{0\})\subset\operatorname{rec}(G)\cap(-\operatorname{rec}(G)).

Then
\operatorname{rec}(L(G))=L(\operatorname{rec}(G))
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.12
(i) we have
\operatorname{rec}(G)=\operatorname{rec}(\overline{G})
. So
\operatorname{rec}(\overline{G})\cap L^{-1}(\{0\})\subset\operatorname{rec}(\overline{G})\cap(-\operatorname{rec}(\overline{G}))
and thus
[
R
, Theorem 9.1]
(applied to
C:=\overline{G}
) implies that
L(\overline{G})
is closed and
\operatorname{rec}(L(\overline{G}))=L(\operatorname{rec}(\overline{G}))
. Hence we easily obtain that
L(\overline{G})=\overline{L(G)}
. Therefore,
\operatorname{rec}(L(\overline{G}))=\operatorname{rec}(L(G))
by Lemma
2.12
(i) and the equality
\operatorname{rec}(L(G))=L(\operatorname{rec}(G))
follows.
∎

3.
Modulus
\omega_{\eta}

One of the main ingredients of this article is the introduction of modulus
\omega_{\eta}
:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\eta:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
and
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
. Then for every
h>0
we set

(3)

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(h):=\inf\bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\bigg\},

where the infimum is taken over all partitions
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}=h
of
[0,h]
. Further we set
\omega_{\eta}(0):=0
.

The fact that
\omega_{\eta}
is indeed a modulus will be proved in Lemma
3.4
(i) below.

Remark 3.2
.

The original motivation for
\omega_{\eta}
was that the following statement holds:

•

Let
\eta:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be concave, let
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
and set

\displaystyle G:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x>0,\;|y|<\eta(x)\}.

Let
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
be both
\omega
-semiconvex and
\omega
-semiconcave. Then
f\in C^{1,\omega_{\eta}}(G)
.

However, we do not need this statement and therefore not prove it. Note also that
\omega_{\eta}
is “minimal” in the sense of Proposition
4.4
below which we use substantially at the end of the article.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
\eta:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
and
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
. Then

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(h)\leq\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(h)}\bigg\}\omega(h),\quad h>0.

Indeed, taking
n=1
in the sum in (
3
), we immediately obtain the estimate.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\eta:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be concave and let
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
. Then the following hold:

(i)

\omega_{\eta}\in\mathcal{M}
.

(ii)

For every
x,h>0
we have
\omega_{\eta}(x+h)\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+\omega_{\eta}(h)
.

Proof.

(i): Obviously
\omega_{\eta}(h)\geq 0
for every
h\geq 0
. By Remark
3.3
and Remark
2.7
(ii), we have

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(h)\leq\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(h)}\bigg\}\omega(h)\leq\max\bigg\{1,\frac{1}{\eta(1)}\bigg\}\omega(h),\quad h\in(0,1),

and thus
\lim_{h\to 0+}\omega_{\eta}(h)=\omega_{\eta}(0)=0
. To prove that
\omega_{\eta}
is non-decreasing, it is sufficient to show

(4)

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(x)\leq\omega_{\eta}(x+h),\quad x,h>0.

Let
x,h>0
and let
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}=x+h
be a partition of
[0,x+h]
. Then there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m\leq n
and
x_{m-1}<x\leq x_{m}
. According to Remark
2.7
(i), (ii), we have

\displaystyle\frac{x-x_{m-1}}{\eta(x)}=\frac{x}{\eta(x)}-\frac{x_{m-1}}{\eta(x)}\leq\frac{x_{m}}{\eta(x_{m})}-\frac{x_{m-1}}{\eta(x_{m})}=\frac{x_{m}-x_{m-1}}{\eta(x_{m})}

and thus

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x-x_{m-1}}{\eta(x)}\bigg\}\omega(x-x_{m-1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1}).

Hence (
4
) holds.

(ii): Let
x,h>0
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a partition
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}=x
of
[0,x]
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

and a partition
0=y_{0}<y_{1}<\cdots<y_{m}=h
of
[0,h]
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{y_{i}-y_{i-1}}{\eta(y_{i})}\bigg\}\omega(y_{i}-y_{i-1})\leq\omega_{\eta}(h)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Hence, for
x_{j}:=x+y_{j-n}
(
j=n+1,\dots,n+m
) we obtain using Remark
2.7
(i)

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(x+h)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n+m}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{y_{i}-y_{i-1}}{\eta(x+y_{i})}\bigg\}\omega(y_{i}-y_{i-1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{y_{i}-y_{i-1}}{\eta(y_{i})}\bigg\}\omega(y_{i}-y_{i-1})

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+\omega_{\eta}(h)+\varepsilon.

Thus (ii) holds.
∎

In the rest of this section we will prove several properties of
\omega_{\eta}
that we will need further.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\eta:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be non-decreasing and let
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
be concave. Then

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{h\omega(\eta(h))}{\eta(h)}\leq\omega_{\eta}(h),\quad h>0.

Proof.

Let
h>0
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a partition
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}=h
of
[0,h]
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\leq\omega_{\eta}(h)+\varepsilon.

Set

\displaystyle J_{1}:=\{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}:\eta(x_{j})\leq x_{j}-x_{j-1}\},

\displaystyle J_{2}:=\{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}:x_{j}-x_{j-1}<\eta(x_{j})\}.

Using Remark
2.7
(ii) we obtain

\displaystyle(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\frac{\omega(\eta(h))}{\eta(h)}

\displaystyle\leq(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\frac{\omega(\eta(x_{j}))}{\eta(x_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})

\displaystyle\leq\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1}),\quad j\in J_{1},

and

\displaystyle(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\frac{\omega(\eta(h))}{\eta(h)}

\displaystyle\leq(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\frac{\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})}{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1}),\quad j\in J_{2}.

Consequently

\displaystyle\frac{h\omega(\eta(h))}{\eta(h)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in J_{1}}(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\frac{\omega(\eta(h))}{\eta(h)}+\sum_{j\in J_{2}}(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\frac{\omega(\eta(h))}{\eta(h)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in J_{1}}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\in J_{2}}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\leq\omega_{\eta}(h)+\varepsilon.

Hence (
5
) holds.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\eta:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
and
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
. Then

(6)

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(x+h)\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(x+h)}\bigg\}\omega(h),\quad x,h>0.

Proof.

Let
x,h>0
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a partition
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}=x
of
[0,x]
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+\varepsilon.

Hence, for
x_{n+1}:=x+h
we have

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(x+h)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})

\displaystyle=\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(x+h)}\bigg\}\omega(h)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(x+h)}\bigg\}\omega(h)+\varepsilon.

Consequently (
6
) holds.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\eta:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be non-decreasing and let
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
. Then

(7)

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(x+h)\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+2\frac{h\omega(\eta(x))}{\eta(x)},\quad x,h>0,h\geq\frac{\eta(x)}{2}.

Proof.

Let
x,h>0
,
h\geq\eta(x)/2
, and
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a partition
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}=x
of
[0,x]
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+\varepsilon.

Denote by
m
the upper integer part of
h/\eta(x)
and set
x_{j}:=x+(j-n)h/m
for each
j=n+1,\dots,n+m
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2}\leq\frac{h}{\eta(x)}\leq m

and
h/m\leq\eta(x)\leq\eta(x_{j})
for each
j=n+1,\dots,n+m
. Thus

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(x+h)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n+m}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{x_{j}-x_{j-1}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega(x_{j}-x_{j-1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=n+1}^{n+m}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{\frac{h}{m}}{\eta(x_{j})}\bigg\}\omega\bigg(\frac{h}{m}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+\varepsilon+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\omega\bigg(\frac{h}{m}\bigg)\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+\varepsilon+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\omega(\eta(x))

\displaystyle=\omega_{\eta}(x)+m\omega(\eta(x))+\varepsilon\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+2\frac{h\omega(\eta(x))}{\eta(x)}+\varepsilon.

Hence (
7
) holds.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\eta:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
and
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
be both concave, let
g:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be differentiable and let
C>0
. Suppose that

(8)

\displaystyle|g(x+h)-g(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(x+h)}\bigg\}\omega(h),\quad x,h>0,

(9)

\displaystyle\eta(x)|g^{\prime}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\omega(\eta(x)),\quad x>0,

(10)

\displaystyle\eta(x)|g^{\prime}(x)-g^{\prime}(x+h)|

\displaystyle\leq C\omega(h),\quad x,h>0,

and set

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x>0,\;|y|<\eta(x)\},

\displaystyle f(x,y)

\displaystyle:=g(x)y,\quad(x,y)\in G.

Then the function
f
is both
(5C\omega)
-semiconvex and
(5C\omega)
-semiconcave.

Proof.

By
[
KZ
, Lemma 2.3]
it is sufficient to show that

|f^{\prime}(x+h,y+k)[h,k]-f^{\prime}(x,y)[h,k]|\\
\leq 5C|(h,k)|\omega(|(h,k)|),\quad(x,y),(x+h,y+k)\in G,h\geq 0.

Let
(x,y),(x+h,y+k)\in G
,
h\geq 0
. Then, according to Remark
2.7
(i), we have

(11)

\displaystyle|k|\leq|y+k|+|y|\leq\eta(x+h)+\eta(x)\leq 2\eta(x+h)

and thus by (
9
) and Remark
2.7
(ii)

\displaystyle|k||g^{\prime}(x+h)|

\displaystyle=|k|\frac{\eta(x+h)|g^{\prime}(x+h)|}{\eta(x+h)}\leq C|k|\frac{\omega(\eta(x+h))}{\eta(x+h)}

\displaystyle\leq C|k|\frac{\omega\big(\frac{|k|}{2}\big)}{\frac{|k|}{2}}\leq 2C\omega(|k|).

Further, by (
10
) we have

\displaystyle|y||g^{\prime}(x+h)-g^{\prime}(x)|\leq\eta(x)|g^{\prime}(x+h)-g^{\prime}(x)|\leq C\omega(h).

Consequently

(12)

|g^{\prime}(x+h)(y+k)-g^{\prime}(x)y|\leq|y||g^{\prime}(x+h)-g^{\prime}(x)|+|k||g^{\prime}(x+h)|\\
\leq C\omega(h)+2C\omega(|k|)\leq 3C\omega(|(h,k)|).

Since by (
11
) we have

\displaystyle|k|\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(x+h)}\bigg\}=\max\bigg\{|k|,\frac{|k|}{\eta(x+h)}h\bigg\}\leq 2\max\{|k|,h\},

we obtain by (
8
)

(13)

|k||g(x+h)-g(x)|\leq C|k|\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(x+h)}\bigg\}\omega(h)\\
\leq 2C\max\{|k|,h\}\omega(h)\leq 2C|(h,k)|\omega(|(h,k)|).

Hence (
12
) and (
13
) give

\displaystyle|f^{\prime}(x+h,y+k)[h,k]-f^{\prime}(x,y)[h,k]|

\displaystyle\leq|h|\bigg|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x+h,y+k)-\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x,y)\bigg|+|k|\bigg|\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x+h,y+k)-\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(x,y)\bigg|

\displaystyle=|h||g^{\prime}(x+h)(y+k)-g^{\prime}(x)y|+|k||g(x+h)-g(x)|

\displaystyle\leq 3C|(h,k)|\omega(|(h,k)|)+2C|(h,k)|\omega(|(h,k)|)=5C|(h,k)|\omega(|(h,k)|).

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\eta:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
and
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
be both concave. Define the function
\psi
by

\psi(x):=\sup\{\lambda\omega_{\eta}(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)\omega_{\eta}(x_{2}):x_{1},x_{2}\geq 0,\lambda\in[0,1],\\
x=\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2}\},\quad x\geq 0.

Then
\psi\in\mathcal{M}
,
\psi
is concave,
\omega_{\eta}\leq\psi\leq 2\omega_{\eta}
and

(14)

\displaystyle\psi(t+\eta(t))\leq\psi(t)+2\omega(\eta(t)),\quad t>0,\eta(t)\leq t.

Proof.

By Remark
2.6
(ii) and Lemma
3.4
, there exists a concave
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}
such that
0\leq\omega_{\eta}\leq\varphi\leq 2\omega_{\eta}
. Note that
\psi
is the upper concave envelope of
\omega_{\eta}
(see
[
R
, Corollary 17.1.5]
). In particular,
\psi
is concave and
0\leq\omega_{\eta}\leq\psi\leq\varphi
. Thus
\psi\leq 2\omega_{\eta}
,
\lim_{x\to 0+}\psi(x)=0
and it follows from Remark
2.7
(i) that
\psi
is non-decreasing. Therefore,
\psi\in\mathcal{M}
.

Let
t>0
,
x_{1},x_{2}\geq 0
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
be such that
\eta(t)\leq t
,
x_{1}\leq x_{2}
and
\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2}=t+\eta(t)
. Then to prove (
14
) it is sufficient to show

\displaystyle\lambda\omega_{\eta}(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)\omega_{\eta}(x_{2})\leq\psi(t)+2\omega(\eta(t)).

Note also that
\eta
is non-decreasing by Remark
2.7
(i).

If
t\leq x_{1}
, then
\eta(t)\leq t\leq x_{1}\leq x_{2}
and thus by Lemma
3.6
(with
h=\eta(t)
and
x=x_{1}-\eta(t)
or
x=x_{2}-\eta(t)
)

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(x_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{\eta}(x_{i}-\eta(t))+\max\bigg\{1,\frac{\eta(t)}{\eta(x_{i})}\bigg\}\omega(\eta(t))

\displaystyle=\omega_{\eta}(x_{i}-\eta(t))+\omega(\eta(t)),\quad i=1,2.

Hence

\displaystyle\lambda\omega_{\eta}(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)\omega_{\eta}(x_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\omega_{\eta}(x_{1}-\eta(t))+(1-\lambda)\omega_{\eta}(x_{2}-\eta(t))+\omega(\eta(t))

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\psi(x_{1}-\eta(t))+(1-\lambda)\psi(x_{2}-\eta(t))+\omega(\eta(t))

\displaystyle\leq\psi(\lambda(x_{1}-\eta(t))+(1-\lambda)(x_{2}-\eta(t)))+\omega(\eta(t))

\displaystyle=\psi(t)+\omega(\eta(t))\leq\psi(t)+2\omega(\eta(t)).

Now suppose that
x_{1}\leq t
. Then
\lambda<1
and therefore we may set

\displaystyle h:=\frac{\eta(t)}{1-\lambda},\;x:=x_{2}-\frac{\eta(t)}{1-\lambda}.

Then
(1-\lambda)t=t-\lambda t\leq t-\lambda x_{1}=(1-\lambda)x_{2}-\eta(t)=(1-\lambda)x
and thus

(15)

\displaystyle t\leq x.

In particular,
0<x
. If
h\leq\eta(x+h)
, then by Lemma
3.6
and Remark
2.7
(iii)

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(x_{2})

\displaystyle=\omega_{\eta}(x+h)\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(x+h)}\bigg\}\omega(h)

\displaystyle=\omega_{\eta}(x)+\omega\bigg(\frac{\eta(t)}{1-\lambda}\bigg)\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+\frac{\omega(\eta(t))}{1-\lambda}

and thus

\displaystyle\lambda\omega_{\eta}(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)\omega_{\eta}(x_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\omega_{\eta}(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)\omega_{\eta}(x)+\omega(\eta(t))

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\psi(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)\psi(x)+\omega(\eta(t))

\displaystyle\leq\psi(\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x)+\omega(\eta(t))

\displaystyle=\psi(t)+\omega(\eta(t))\leq\psi(t)+2\omega(\eta(t)).

If
\eta(x+h)\leq h
, then by Lemma
3.7
, Remark
2.7
(ii) and (
15
) we have

\displaystyle\omega_{\eta}(x_{2})

\displaystyle=\omega_{\eta}(x+h)\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+2h\frac{\omega(\eta(x))}{\eta(x)}

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{\eta}(x)+2h\frac{\omega(\eta(t))}{\eta(t)}=\omega_{\eta}(x)+2\frac{\omega(\eta(t))}{1-\lambda}

and therefore

\displaystyle\lambda\omega_{\eta}(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)\omega_{\eta}(x_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\omega_{\eta}(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)\omega_{\eta}(x)+2\omega(\eta(t))

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\psi(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)\psi(x)+2\omega(\eta(t))

\displaystyle\leq\psi(\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x)+2\omega(\eta(t))

\displaystyle=\psi(t)+2\omega(\eta(t)).

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\eta:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{M}
be both concave, let
\omega\in\mathcal{M}
and let
C>0
. Suppose that

(16)

\displaystyle\psi(t+\eta(t))-\psi(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\omega(\eta(t)),\quad t>0,\;\eta(t)\leq t,

(17)

\displaystyle\psi(h)

\displaystyle\leq C\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(h)}\bigg\}\omega(h),\quad h>0.

Then

\displaystyle\psi(x+h)-\psi(x)

\displaystyle\leq 2C\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(x+h)}\bigg\}\omega(h),\quad x,h>0.

Proof.

Let
x,h>0
. We will distinguish four cases.

(a)
x\leq h
: Then
\eta(x+h)\leq\eta(2h)\leq 2\eta(h)
by Remark
2.7
(i), (iii). And so, using Remark
2.6
(i) and (
17
), we obtain

\displaystyle\psi(x+h)-\psi(x)\leq\psi(h)\leq C\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(h)}\bigg\}\omega(h)

\displaystyle\leq C\max\bigg\{1,\frac{2h}{\eta(x+h)}\bigg\}\omega(h)\leq 2C\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(x+h)}\bigg\}\omega(h).

(b)
h\leq\eta(h)
: Then, according to Remark
2.6
(i) and (
17
), we have

\displaystyle\psi(x+h)-\psi(x)

\displaystyle\leq\psi(h)\leq C\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(h)}\bigg\}\omega(h)=C\omega(h)

\displaystyle\leq 2C\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(x+h)}\bigg\}\omega(h).

(c)
\eta(x)\leq h\leq x
: Then
\eta(x+h)\leq\eta(2x)\leq 2\eta(x)
by Remark
2.7
(i), (iii). Thus, using the concavity of
\psi
and (
16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\psi(x+h)-\psi(x)=h\frac{\psi(x+h)-\psi(x)}{h}\leq h\frac{\psi(x+\eta(x))-\psi(x)}{\eta(x)}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{h}{\eta(x)}\omega(\eta(x))\leq 2C\frac{h}{\eta(x+h)}\omega(h)\leq 2C\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(x+h)}\bigg\}\omega(h).

(d) Suppose that none of cases (a), (b) or (c) holds: Then clearly
h\leq x
and
\eta(h)\leq h\leq\eta(x)
. Since
\eta
is continuous, there exists
t\in[h,x]
such that
\eta(t)=h
. Hence, using the concavity of
\psi
and (
16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\psi(x+h)-\psi(x)

\displaystyle=\eta(t)\frac{\psi(x+\eta(t))-\psi(x)}{\eta(t)}\leq\eta(t)\frac{\psi(t+\eta(t))-\psi(t)}{\eta(t)}

\displaystyle\leq C\omega(\eta(t))\leq 2C\max\bigg\{1,\frac{h}{\eta(x+h)}\bigg\}\omega(h).

∎

(ii): Let

We will also need the following lemma:

Lemma 5.3

We will proceed by induction on

Now suppose that

Explaining the b → s ​ ℓ + ​ ℓ − b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-} anomalies in Z ′ {Z^{\prime}} scenarios with top-FCNC couplings
Xin-Qiang Li, Meng Shen, Dong-Yang Wang, Ya-Dong Yang, and Xing-Bo Yuan, [15pt] Institute of Particle Physics, Key Laboratory of Quark, Lepton Physics (MOE)[-0.2cm] Central China Normal University, Wuhan, Hubei 430079, China, [-0.2cm] Department of Physics, Jiujiang University, Jiujiang 332005, China, [-0.2cm] School of Physics, Microelectronics, Zhengzhou University, Zhengzhou, Henan 450001, China

Abstract
Motivated by the recent anomalies in b → s ​ ℓ + ​ ℓ − b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-} transitions, we explore a minimal Z ′ {Z^{\prime}} scenario, in which the Z ′ {Z^{\prime}} boson has a flavour-changing coupling to charm and top quarks and a flavour-conserving coupling to muons. It is found that such a Z ′ {Z^{\prime}} boson can explain the current b → s ​ ℓ + ​ ℓ − b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-} anomalies, while satisfying other flavour and collider constraints simultaneously. The Z ′ {Z^{\prime}} boson can be as light as few hundreds GeV. In this case, the t → c ​ μ + ​ μ − t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-} decay and the t ​ Z ′ t{Z^{\prime}} associated production at the LHC could provide sensitive probes of such a Z ′ {Z^{\prime}} boson. As a special feature, the Z ′ {Z^{\prime}} contributions to all rare B B - and K K -meson processes are controlled by one parameter. This results in interesting correlations among these processes, which could provide further insights into this scenario. In addition, an extended scenario, in which the Z ′ {Z^{\prime}} boson interacts with the S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) L SU(2)_{L} fermion doublets with analogous couplings as in the minimal scenario, is also investigated.

2
{Z^{\prime}}
scenarios with top-quark FCNC couplings

We consider two phenomenological scenarios involving a
{Z^{\prime}}
boson. In the first scenario (denoted as scenario I), the
{Z^{\prime}}
boson has flavour-changing couplings to
c
and
t
quarks and a flavour-conserving coupling to the
\mu
lepton. Their interactions are described by the effective Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Z^{\prime}}^{\rm I}=\big({X_{ct}^{L}}\,\bar{c}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}t\,Z^{\prime}_{\mu}+{\rm h.c.}\big)+{\lambda_{\mu\mu}^{L}}\,\bar{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\mu\,Z^{\prime}_{\mu},

(5)

where
P_{L}=(1-\gamma_{5})/2
, and the fermion fields
c
,
t
and
\mu
refer to the mass eigenstates. Generally, the couplings
{X_{ct}^{L}}
and
{\lambda_{\mu\mu}^{L}}
are complex and real, respectively. In this scenario, the
{Z^{\prime}}
boson affects the
b\to s\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
transitions via one-loop penguin diagram and could explain the anomalies observed in
B\to K^{(*)}\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
decays. Furthermore, sizable contributions to the
s\to d\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
transitions could arise from the
{Z^{\prime}}
penguin diagram.

In the second scenario (denoted as scenario II), the
{Z^{\prime}}
boson is assumed to interact with the
SU(2)_{L}
fermion doublets with similar couplings as in scenario I. In the mass eigenstate basis, the effective Lagrangian takes the form

3.4
s\to d\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

For the
s\to d\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

K_{L,S}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays. After taking into account the
{Z^{\prime}}
effects, the effective Hamiltonian inducing the short-distance (SD) contribution to

[

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}^{s\to d\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}=\frac{4G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{\alpha_{\rm e}}{4\pi s_{W}^{2}}\,\big(V_{ts}^{*}V_{td}\,Y+V_{cs}^{*}V_{cd}\,Y_{\rm NL}\big)\big(\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}P_{L}d\big)\big(\bar{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\mu\big)+{\rm h.c.}.

(23)

In the SM, the function
Y=Y(x_{t})
describes contributions from the penguin diagrams with internal top quark
[
96
,
95
,
69
,
70
]
, while
Y_{\rm NL}
involves the charm-quark contributions
[
108
]
. In both the
{Z^{\prime}}
scenarios I and II, the
s\to d\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
transition is induced by the penguin diagrams shown in figure
4
, which result in the NP contributions

\displaystyle Y_{\rm NP,I}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\sqrt{2}G_{F}}\bigg(\frac{V_{cd}}{V_{td}}\frac{{X_{ct}^{L}}^{*}{\lambda_{\mu\mu}^{L}}}{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}+\frac{V_{cs}^{*}}{V_{ts}^{*}}\frac{{X_{ct}^{L}}{\lambda_{\mu\mu}^{L}}}{m_{{Z^{\prime}}}^{2}}\bigg)f(x_{t}),

(24)

\displaystyle Y_{\rm NP,II}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\sqrt{2}G_{F}}\bigg(\frac{V_{cd}}{V_{td}}\frac{{X_{23}^{L}}^{*}{\lambda_{22}^{L}}}{m_{{Z^{\prime}}}^{2}}+\frac{V_{cs}^{*}}{V_{ts}^{*}}\frac{{X_{23}^{L}}{\lambda_{22}^{L}}}{m_{{Z^{\prime}}}^{2}}\bigg)f(x_{t}),

(25)

with the loop function
f(x_{t})
defined already in eq. (

). Here, the NP contributions in both of the two
{Z^{\prime}}
scenarios are of the same magnitude, which is different from what is observed in the
b\to s\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
transitions. Numerically,
Y=0.94-(1.03+0.02i){X_{ct}^{L}}{\lambda_{\mu\mu}^{L}}-(0.99+0.45i){X_{ct}^{L}}^{*}{\lambda_{\mu\mu}^{L}}
for
m_{Z^{\prime}}=1{\,\rm TeV}
in scenario I.

For the
K_{L,S}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays, only the SD part of a dispersive contribution can be reliably calculated. The branching ratio of
K_{L}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
can be written as
[

]

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(K_{L}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})_{\rm SD}=\kappa_{\mu}\left[\frac{{\rm Re}(\lambda_{t}Y)}{\lambda^{5}}+\frac{{\rm Re}\lambda_{c}}{\lambda}P_{c}(Y)\right]^{2},

with
\lambda_{t}=V_{ts}^{*}V_{td}
,
\lambda_{c}=V_{cs}^{*}V_{cd}
, and
\lambda\approx V_{us}
denoting the Wolfenstein parameter. The factor

contains the relevant hadronic matrix element that can be extracted from the
K^{+}\to\mu^{+}\nu_{\mu}
decay, and numerically we have
\kappa_{\mu}=(2.009\pm 0.017)\times 10^{-9}\,(\lambda/0.225)^{8}

[
107
,
108
]
.
The charm contribution
P_{c}(Y)
is found to be
P_{c}(Y)=0.115\pm 0.017
at the NNLO in QCD
[
108
]
. For the
K_{S}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay, the SD and long-distance contributions add incoherently in the total rate
[
109
,
110
,
111
,
112
]
. The SD part of the branching ratio is given by
[
109
,
110
,
111
,
112
]

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(K_{S}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})_{\rm SD}=\tau_{K_{S}}\frac{G_{F}^{2}\alpha_{\rm e}^{2}}{8\pi^{3}s_{W}^{4}}m_{K}f_{K}^{2}\bigg(1-\frac{m_{\mu}^{2}}{m_{K}^{2}}\bigg)^{1/2}m_{\mu}^{2}\,{\rm Im}^{2}\big(\lambda_{t}Y\big),

(27)

with
\tau_{K_{S}}
the lifetime of
K_{S}
and
f_{K}
the decay constant.

3.5
s\to d\nu\bar{\nu}
decays

The
K^{+}\to\pi^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
and
K_{L}\to\pi^{0}\nu\bar{\nu}
decays are induced by the
s\to d\nu\bar{\nu}
transition. They are both characterized by the theoretically clean virtue, since the relevant hadronic matrix elements can be extracted with the help of isospin symmetry from the leading semi-leptonic
K_{\ell 3}
decays
[
113
]
. With the
{Z^{\prime}}
effects taken into account, the
s\to d\nu\bar{\nu}
decays are governed by the effective Hamiltonian
[
95
,
96
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}^{s\to d\nu\bar{\nu}}=\frac{4G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{\alpha_{\rm e}}{2\pi s_{W}^{2}}\sum_{\ell=e,\mu,\tau}\big(V_{ts}^{*}V_{td}X^{\ell}+V_{cs}^{*}V_{cd}X_{\rm NL}^{\ell}\big)\big(\bar{s}\gamma_{\mu}P_{L}d\big)\big(\bar{\nu}_{\ell}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\nu_{\ell}\big)+{\rm h.c.}.

(28)

In the SM, similar to the
b\to s\nu\bar{\nu}
transition, the
Z
-penguin and
W
-box diagrams with internal top quark result in a flavour-universal Wilson coefficient
X_{\rm SM}^{\ell}=X(x_{t})
, with the Inami-Lim function
X(x_{t})
introduced already in eq. (
16
). The contribution from internal charm quark is represented by the function
X_{\rm NL}^{\ell}
. In the
{Z^{\prime}}
scenario II, the
{Z^{\prime}}
boson can only interact with the
\nu_{\mu}
neutrino. Its contributions to the operators with
\nu_{e}
and
\nu_{\tau}
neutrinos arise from two-loop level and can be therefore neglected, i.e.,
X_{{\rm NP},\rm II}^{e}=X_{{\rm NP},\rm II}^{\tau}=0
. However, the
{Z^{\prime}}
-penguin diagrams similar to the one shown in figure
4
contribute to the Wilson coefficient
X^{\mu}
, and we find
X_{\rm NP,II}^{\mu}=-Y_{\rm NP}^{\rm II}
. In the
{Z^{\prime}}
scenario I, as in the case of the
b\to s\nu\bar{\nu}
transition, the
{Z^{\prime}}
effects arise firstly at two-loop level and are neglected, i.e.,
X_{\rm NP,I}^{\ell}=0
.

In the SM, with the help of isospin symmetry, the branching ratio of
K^{+}\to\pi^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
decay after summing over the three neutrino flavours is given by
[
113
,
114
,
115
]

{\mathcal{B}}(K^{+}\to\pi^{+}\nu\bar{\nu})=\frac{1}{3}\kappa_{+}(1+\Delta_{\rm EM})\sum_{\ell=e,\mu,\tau}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{{\rm Im}[\lambda_{t}X^{\ell}]}{\lambda^{5}}\bigg)^{2}+\bigg(\frac{{\rm Re}\lambda_{c}}{\lambda}P_{c}(X)+\frac{{\rm Re}[\lambda_{t}X^{\ell}]}{\lambda^{5}}\bigg)^{2}\,\bigg].

(29)

The parameter
\kappa_{+}
contains the relevant hadronic matrix elements extracted from the
K_{\ell 3}
decays, and numerically we have
\kappa_{+}=(5.173\pm 0.025)\times 10^{-11}\,(\lambda/0.225)^{8}

[
97
]
. The parameter
\Delta_{\rm EM}=-0.003
describes the isospin breaking correction
[
114
,
115
]
. The parameter
P_{c}(X)
summarizes the SD charm contribution
[
95
,
107
,
116
,
117
]
and the long-distance contributions
[
118
]
. Numerically, it is found that
P_{c}(X)=0.404\pm 0.024

[
97
]
.

The rare decay
K_{L}\to\pi^{0}\nu\bar{\nu}
proceeds in the SM almost entirely through direct CP violation
[
119
,
120
]
. It is completely dominated by the SD loop diagrams with top-quark exchanges, and the charm contribution can be fully neglected
[
113
]
. With the help of isospin symmetry, the branching ratio of
K_{L}\to\pi^{0}\nu\bar{\nu}
decay after summing over three neutrino flavours reads
[
113
,
121
]

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(K_{L}\to\pi^{0}\nu\bar{\nu})=\frac{1}{3}\kappa_{L}(1-\delta_{\epsilon})\sum_{\ell=e,\mu,\tau}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{5}}\text{Im}\big[\lambda_{t}X^{\ell}\big]\right)^{2},

(30)

where
\kappa_{L}
encodes the hadronic matrix element extracted from the
K_{\ell 3}
data
[
114
,
122
]
, and numerically
\kappa_{L}=(2.231\pm 0.013)\times 10^{-10}\,(\lambda/0.225)^{8}
. The parameter
\delta_{\epsilon}
denotes the indirectly CP-violating contribution, and is highly suppressed by the
K^{0}-\bar{K}^{0}
mixing parameter
|\epsilon|

[
120
]
.

3.6
t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay

In the SM, the rare FCNC decay
t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
is highly suppressed by the Glashow-Iliopoulos-Maiani mechanism
[
123
]
, with a branching ratio of
\mathcal{O}(10^{-10})

[
124
,
125
]
. However, this process could be significantly enhanced by the
{Z^{\prime}}
boson through the tree-level diagrams shown in figure
5
. Let us discuss the
{Z^{\prime}}
effects in the cases
m_{Z^{\prime}}>m_{t}
and
m_{Z^{\prime}}<m_{t}
, respectively.

Figure 5:
Feynman diagrams for the
t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
(left) and
{Z^{\prime}}\to\bar{b}cW^{-}
(right) decays in the
{Z^{\prime}}
scenarios.

3.6.1
m_{Z^{\prime}}>m_{t}

The branching ratio of the
{Z^{\prime}}
-mediated
t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay can be written as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-})=\frac{\Gamma(t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-})}{\Gamma_{t}},

(31)

where
\Gamma_{t}
is the total width of the top quark. The differential decay width can be calculated from the left tree-level diagram shown in figure
5
, with the result in the
{Z^{\prime}}
scenario I given by

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{0}^{\rm I}(t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-})}{dq^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|{X_{ct}^{L}}\lambda^{L}_{\mu\mu}\right|^{2}}{768\pi^{3}m_{t}^{3}}\frac{(m_{t}^{2}-q^{2})(m_{t}^{4}+q^{2}m_{t}^{2}-2q^{4})}{(q^{2}-m_{{Z^{\prime}}}^{2})^{2}+m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}},

(32)

where
q^{2}
denotes the dilepton invariant mass squared, and
\Gamma_{{Z^{\prime}}}
the finite decay width of the
{Z^{\prime}}
boson. Since the
{Z^{\prime}}
boson cannot be on-shell in the case of
m_{Z^{\prime}}>m_{t}
, we can safely neglect the finite-width effect of the
{Z^{\prime}}
boson here. After including the NLO QCD correction, the differential decay width of
t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{\rm NLO}^{\rm I}(t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-})}{dq^{2}}=\frac{d\Gamma_{0}^{\rm I}(t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-})}{dq^{2}}\big[1+f_{\rm NLO}(q^{2})\big],

(33)

where the factor
f_{\rm NLO}(q^{2})
can be obtained from the NLO QCD calculation of the
t\to cZ
decay
[
126
,
127
,
128
]
, and is given explicitly by

\displaystyle f_{\rm NLO}(q^{2})=\frac{4\alpha_{s}}{3\pi}\biggl[

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}-3-\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{m_{t}^{2}}+\frac{12+135\beta^{2}-43\beta^{4}}{12\beta^{2}(3-\beta^{2})}+\left(2\log\beta-\frac{9-\beta^{2}}{3-\beta^{2}}\right)\log\beta

\displaystyle+\frac{(1-\beta^{2})(1-4\beta^{2}+\beta^{4})}{\beta^{4}\left(3-\beta^{2}\right)}\log(1-\beta^{2})+\mathrm{Li}_{2}(\beta^{2})+\mathrm{Li}_{2}(1-\beta^{-2})\biggr],

(34)

with
\beta=\sqrt{1-q^{2}/m_{t}^{2}}
. Numerically, the NLO QCD corrections decrease the LO width by
6.4\%\sim 9.1\%
for
m_{t}<m_{Z^{\prime}}<1{\,\rm TeV}
. Expressions in scenario II can be obtained from the above formulas with the replacement
({X_{ct}^{L}},{\lambda_{\mu\mu}^{L}})\to(X_{23}^{L},\lambda_{22}^{L})
. As will be shown in subsection
4.2.2
, the branching ratio
{\mathcal{B}}(t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
is predicted to be below
{\mathcal{O}}(10^{-5})
in the two
{Z^{\prime}}
scenarios. Therefore, their contributions to the top-quark total width
\Gamma_{t}
can be safely neglected.

3.6.2
m_{Z^{\prime}}<m_{t}

In the case of
m_{Z^{\prime}}<m_{t}
, the branching ratio of the
{Z^{\prime}}
-mediated
t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay can also be written as eqs. (
32
) and (
33
). However, since the intermediate
{Z^{\prime}}
boson can be on-shell in this case, it is necessary to consider the
{Z^{\prime}}
finite-width effect in eq. (
32
). When
m_{W}<m_{Z^{\prime}}<m_{t}
, the main
{Z^{\prime}}
decay modes in scenario I are
{Z^{\prime}}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
and
{Z^{\prime}}\to b\bar{c}W^{+}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}^{\rm I}=\Gamma^{\rm I}({Z^{\prime}}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})+2\Gamma^{\rm I}({Z^{\prime}}\to b\bar{c}W^{+}),

(35)

where the decay width of
{Z^{\prime}}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm I}({Z^{\prime}}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{24\pi}\left|\lambda^{L}_{\mu\mu}\right|^{2}m_{Z^{\prime}},

(36)

and the width of the top-quark mediated
{Z^{\prime}}\to b\bar{c}W^{+}
decay reads

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm I}({Z^{\prime}}\to b\bar{c}W^{+})=

\displaystyle\frac{G_{F}|V_{tb}|^{2}|X_{ct}|^{2}}{128\sqrt{2}\,\pi^{3}}\frac{m_{t}^{8}}{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{5}}\biggl\{4y_{W}^{3}y_{Z^{\prime}}^{3}\log\frac{y_{Z^{\prime}}}{y_{W}}+\frac{1}{12}(y_{Z^{\prime}}-y_{W})\Big[60+48y_{W}^{2}y_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle+20y_{W}y_{Z^{\prime}}+(30-39y_{W}y_{Z^{\prime}})(y_{W}+y_{Z^{\prime}})-88(y_{W}^{2}+y_{Z^{\prime}}^{2})+9(y_{W}^{3}+y_{Z^{\prime}}^{3})\Big]

\displaystyle+\Big[5+9y_{W}^{2}y_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}-9\big(y_{W}^{2}+y_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}\big)-4y_{W}^{3}y_{Z^{\prime}}^{3}+4\big(y_{W}^{3}+y_{Z^{\prime}}^{3}\big)\Big]\log\frac{\beta_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}{\beta_{W}^{2}}\biggr\},

with

,

As discussed in section
2
, the relevant model parameters in
{Z^{\prime}}
scenario I (II) contain the complex couplings
{X_{ct}^{L}}
and
{\lambda_{\mu\mu}^{L}}
(
X_{23}^{L}
and

), and the
{Z^{\prime}}
mass
m_{Z^{\prime}}
. When performing a global fit on these parameters and studying their effects, we consider the following parameter space:

S.I:

\displaystyle\lvert{X_{ct}^{L}}\rvert<2.0,\qquad\lvert{\lambda_{\mu\mu}^{L}}\rvert<2.0,\qquad 105{\,\rm GeV}<m_{Z^{\prime}}<1000{\,\rm GeV},

(45)

S.II:

\displaystyle\lvert{X_{23}^{L}}\rvert<2.0,\qquad\lvert{\lambda_{22}^{L}}\rvert<2.0,\qquad 105{\,\rm GeV}<m_{Z^{\prime}}<1000{\,\rm GeV},

where the upper limits on the magnitudes of the couplings correspond to about half of the perturbativity bound
\sqrt{4\pi}
. Here the lower bound of
m_{Z^{\prime}}
is chosen to avoid the potentially strong bound from the branching ratio
{\mathcal{B}}(t\to cZ)

[
141
]
(see subsection
4.2.2
for further details).

In our numerical analysis, we follow the approach used in ref.
[
86
]
and construct a likelihood function that only depends on the Wilson coefficients as

\displaystyle-2\log L(\bm{{\mathcal{C}}})=\sum_{i}\bm{x_{i}}^{T}(\bm{{\mathcal{C}}})\big[V_{\rm exp}+V_{\rm th}(\bm{\theta})]^{-1}\bm{x_{i}}(\bm{{\mathcal{C}}}),

(46)

with the definition

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{i}(\bm{{\mathcal{C}}})=\bm{{\mathcal{O}}}_{i}^{\rm exp}-\bm{{\mathcal{O}}}_{i}^{\rm th}(\bm{{\mathcal{C}}},\bm{\theta}).

Here,
\bm{{\mathcal{O}}}_{i}^{\rm exp}
are the central values of the experimental measurements, while
\bm{{\mathcal{O}}}_{i}^{\rm th}
denote the central values of the theoretical predictions, which depend on the Wilson coefficients
\bm{{\mathcal{C}}}
and the input parameters
\bm{\theta}
.
V_{\rm exp}
is the covariance matrix of the experimental measurements, while
V_{\rm th}
denotes the covariance matrix of the theoretical predictions, which contains all the theoretical uncertainties and their correlations. Generally, the covariance matrix
V_{\rm th}
depends on both the Wilson coefficients
\bm{{\mathcal{C}}}
and the input parameters
\bm{\theta}
. However, as all the experimental measurements do not show large deviations from the SM predictions, we have neglected in our numerical analysis the dependence of
V_{\rm th}
on the
{Z^{\prime}}
contributions to the Wilson coefficients
\bm{{\mathcal{C}}}
, i.e.,
V_{\rm th}
is evaluated with the Wilson coefficients
\bm{{\mathcal{C}}}
fixed to their SM values. Furthermore, the theoretical uncertainties are approximated as Gaussian and obtained by randomly sampling the observables with the input parameters
\bm{\theta}
distributed according to their probability density functions. With eq. (
46
), the
\Delta\chi^{2}
function, which depends on the Wilson coefficients
\bm{{\mathcal{C}}}
, can be written as
\Delta\chi^{2}(\bm{{\mathcal{C}}})=-2\log L(\bm{{\mathcal{C}}})/L_{\rm max}
, where
L_{\rm max}
denotes the maximum of
L
at different values of the Wilson coefficients. For more details on the fit methodology, we refer to refs.
[
86
,
142
]
. Our global fit is performed using an extended version of the package
flavio

[
142
]
.

In the case of

[

166

167
,
141

peak)

decays have been performed yet. In ref.
[
168
]
, an upper bound on
{\mathcal{B}}(t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
was estimated by using the experimental bounds on
{\mathcal{B}}(t\to cZ)
and
{\mathcal{B}}(Z\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})=3.37\%

[
132

{\mathcal{B}}(t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-})<4.4\times 10^{-6}
can be derived from the current bound
{\mathcal{B}}(t\to cZ)<1.3\times 10^{-4}
set by the ATLAS experiment with an integrated luminosity of
139{\,\rm fb}^{-1}
at
13{\,\rm TeV}

[
141
]
. In this way, constraints on the
{Z^{\prime}}
parameters can be derived from the bound
{\mathcal{B}}(t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-})<4.4\times 10^{-6}
. However, we find that such a constraint is at least one order of magnitude weaker than that obtained from the low-energy flavour processes discussed in the last subsection.

Within the effective field theory (EFT) framework, by using different signal regions of the LHC searches for the rare FCNC decay
t\to cZ
, an improved approach has been developed in ref.
[
169
]
. However, in the case when
m_{Z^{\prime}}
is not far from
m_{t}

{Z^{\prime}}
contributions to the decay

.

4.3
Predictions

As mentioned in the last subsection, experimental searches for the
t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay off the
Z
pole have not been performed yet. Using the allowed parameter space derived in the global fit, we can make prediction on the branching ratio
{\mathcal{B}}(t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
in the two
{Z^{\prime}}
scenarios as a function of the
{Z^{\prime}}
mass, which is shown in figure
11
. Although there are several studies of the expected sensitivities to the
t\to cZ
decay at the LHC
[
171
]
and other future colliders
[
172
,
173
,
174
]
, to our knowledge, detailed analysis of the
t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay at the LHC has not been performed yet. In order to estimate the current (future) experimental sensitivity to
{\mathcal{B}}(t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
, we adopt as a benchmark the product of
{\mathcal{B}}(Z\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})=3.37\%

[
132
]
and the current (future projected) experimental limit on
{\mathcal{B}}(t\to cZ)
. With the current ATLAS limit
{\mathcal{B}}(t\to cZ)<1.3\times 10^{-4}
based on the full Run-2 data
[
141
]
and the future expected sensitivity
5\times 10^{-5}
at the HL-LHC with
3\,\mathrm{ab}^{-1}

[
171
]
, the current and the future sensitivity to
{\mathcal{B}}(t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
are estimated to be
4.4\times 10^{-6}
and
1.7\times 10^{-6}
respectively, as shown by the dashed and the dotted line in figure
11
. Here we make the following two observations:

•

In the case of
m_{Z^{\prime}}<m_{t}
, the branching ratio of
t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay in the two
{Z^{\prime}}
scenarios is strongly enhanced by the resonance effect, which is several orders of magnitude higher than the one in the case of
m_{Z^{\prime}}>m_{t}
. In the mass region
105{\,\rm GeV}<m_{Z^{\prime}}<m_{t}
, the predicted
{\mathcal{B}}(t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
in scenario I (II) is higher (lower) than the current and future estimated bounds. Therefore, experimental searches for the

.

•

In the case of
m_{Z^{\prime}}>m_{t}
, the predicted
{\mathcal{B}}(t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
in scenario I is compatible with the estimated sensitivities, while the one in scenario II is several orders of magnitude lower the estimated future sensitivity. Therefore, direct searches for the decay in scenario II with a heavy
{Z^{\prime}}
boson could be very challenging for the LHC and its high-luminosity upgrade.

5
Conclusions

Motivated by the recent anomalies in the
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
transitions, we have considered a phenomenological
{Z^{\prime}}
scenario (denoted as scenario I), in which a heavy
{Z^{\prime}}
boson couples only to
t\bar{c}
and
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
with left-handed chirality. The
{Z^{\prime}}
effects on the
b\to s\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
processes automatically induce opposite contributions to the effective operators
{\mathcal{O}}_{9}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{10}
, which is favored by the model-independent analyses of the
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
anomalies.

We have performed a global fit to all relevant experimental data. It is found that such a minimal
{Z^{\prime}}
scenario can address the current
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
anomalies, while simultaneously satisfying other flavour and collider constraints. The mass of the
{Z^{\prime}}
boson can be less than
1\,{\,\rm TeV}
. In the region
105{\,\rm GeV}<m_{Z^{\prime}}<m_{t}
, the
t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay is significantly enhanced by resonance effects and can serve as a sensitive probe of such a
{Z^{\prime}}
boson. As an important feature of this scenario, all the low-energy flavour observables are controlled by the product
{X_{ct}^{L}}{\lambda_{\mu\mu}^{L}}/m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}
. We have found interesting correlations among the various flavour observables, which could provide further insights into this scenario.

We have also considered an extended scenario (denoted as scenario II), in which the
{Z^{\prime}}
boson interacts with the
SU_{L}(2)
fermion doublets with analogous couplings as in scenario I. Due to tree-level
{Z^{\prime}}
contributions, the
{Z^{\prime}}
couplings suffer from severe constraints from the
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
processes and the
B_{s}-\bar{B}_{s}
mixing, which makes the collider signals of
t\to c\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay below the estimated sensitivity at the HL-LHC. The correlations between flavour observables in scenario II are different from that observed in scenario I, and can be therefore used to distinguish between the two scenarios.

Our scenarios can be modified by replacing
t\bar{c}{Z^{\prime}}
with
t\bar{u}{Z^{\prime}}
coupling. Then the
K
-meson rare decays could become more relevant due to the large CKM factors involved, and the
t{Z^{\prime}}
associated production at the LHC may play crucial role in searching for such a
{Z^{\prime}}
boson. A right-handed
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}{Z^{\prime}}
coupling can also be taken into account to simultaneously accommodate the
(g-2)_{\mu}
anomaly. In addition, if the
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}{Z^{\prime}}
coupling is replaced by the
e^{+}e^{-}{Z^{\prime}}
coupling, the direct production of the
{Z^{\prime}}
boson at
e^{+}e^{-}
collider should then be taken into account. It is noted that, although our
{Z^{\prime}}
scenarios are not realistic, our studies have shown that a vector boson with top-quark FCNC interactions can explain the current
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
anomalies and may provide new avenues for model buildings. We also encourage our experimental colleagues to carry out relevant searches for such a
{Z^{\prime}}
boson at the LHC and its high-luminosity upgrade.

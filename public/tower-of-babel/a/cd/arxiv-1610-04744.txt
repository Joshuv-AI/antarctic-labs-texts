Abstract
. The Naruse hook-length formula is a recent general formula for the number of standard Young tableaux of skew shapes, given as a positive sum over excited diagrams of products of hook-lengths. In [ MPP1 ] we gave two different q q -analogues of Naruse’s formula: for the skew Schur functions, and for counting reverse plane partitions of skew shapes. In this paper we give an elementary proof of Naruse’s formula based on the case of border strips. For special border strips, we obtain curious new formulas for the Euler and q q -Euler numbers in terms of certain Dyck path summations.



1.
Introduction

In Enumerative Combinatorics, when it comes to fundamental results,
one proof is rarely enough, and one is often on the prowl for a better,
more elegant or more direct proof. In fact, there is a wide belief in
multitude of “proofs from the Book”, rather than a singular best approach.
The reasons are both cultural and mathematical: different proofs
elucidate different aspects of the underlying combinatorial objects
and lead to different extensions and generalizations.

The story of this series of papers is on our effort to
understand and generalize the
Naruse hook-length formula

(NHLF) for the number of standard Young tableaux of a skew shape in
terms of
excited diagrams
.
In our previous paper
[
MPP1
]
, we gave two

q
-analogues of the NHLF, the first with an algebraic proof and
the second with a bijective proof. We also gave a (difficult)
“mixed” proof of the first
q
-analogue, which combined the
bijection with an algebraic argument. Naturally, these provided
new proofs of the NHLF, but none which one would call “elementary”.

This paper is the second in the series. Here we consider a special
case of border strips which turn out to be extremely fruitful both
as a technical tool and as an important object of study.
We give two elementary proofs of the NHLF in this case,
both inductive: one using weighted paths argument and another
using determinant calculation. We then deduce the general case
of NHLF for all skew diagrams by using the

Lascoux–Pragacz identity
for Schur functions.
Since the latter has its own elementary proof
[
HaG
]

(see also
[
CYY
]
), we obtain an elementary proof of the HLF.

But surprises do not stop here. For the special cases of
the
zigzag strips
, our approach gives a number of
curious new formulas for the
Euler

and two types of
q
-Euler numbers
, the second of
which seems to be new. Because the
excited diagrams correspond to Dyck paths in this case,
the resulting summations have
Catalan number
of terms.
We also give type B analogues, which have a similar structure but
with
\binom{2n}{n}
terms. Despite their strong “classical feel”,
all these formulas are new and quite mysterious.

1.1.
Hook formulas for straight and skew shapes

[

]

We assume here the reader is familiar with the basic definitions, which

The

Let

(HLF)

f^{\lambda}\,=\,\frac{n!}{\prod_{u\in[\lambda]}h(u)}\,,

Most recently, Naruse generalized (
HLF

For a skew shape

[\mu]

.

Such move

We use

Let

(NHLF)

f^{\lambda/\mu}\,=\,|\lambda/\mu|!\,\sum_{D\in\mathcal{E}(\lambda/\mu)}\,\hskip 1.70709pt\hskip 1.70709pt\prod_{u\in[\lambda]\setminus D}\frac{1}{h(u)}\hskip 1.70709pt\hskip 0.85355pt\hskip 1.70709pt.

1.3
[

We have:

We have:

1.2.
Combinatorial proofs

Our approach to the combinatorial proof of the NHLF in
Section
5
-
7
is as follows. We
start by proving the case of border strips (connected skew shapes with no
2\times 2
square). In this case the NHLF is more elegant,

\frac{f^{\lambda/\mu}}{|\lambda/\mu|!}=\sum_{\gamma}\prod_{(i,j)\in\gamma}\frac{1}{h(i,j)},

where the sum is over lattice paths
\gamma
from
(\lambda^{\prime}_{1},1)
to

(1,\lambda_{1})
that stay inside
[\lambda]
. We give two self
contained inductive proofs of
this case. The first proof in Section
6
is based on
showing a multivariate identity of paths. The second proof in
Section
7
uses determinants to show that a multivariate
identity of paths equals a ratio of
factorial Schur functions
.

We then use a corollary of the Lascoux–Pragacz identity for skew Schur
functions: if
(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k})
is a decomposition of the
shape
\lambda/\mu
into outer border strips
\theta_{i}
(see
Section
7.5
) then

\frac{f^{\lambda/\mu}}{|\lambda/\mu|!}\,=\,\det\left[\,\frac{f^{\theta_{i}\#\theta_{j}}}{|\theta_{i}\#\theta_{j}|!}\,\right]_{i,j=1}^{k},

where
\theta_{i}\#\theta_{j}
is a certain substrip of the outer border
strip of
\lambda
.

Combining the case for border strips and this determinantal identity
we get

\frac{f^{\lambda/\mu}}{|\lambda/\mu|!}\,=\,\det\left[\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma:(a_{j},b_{j})\to(c_{i},d_{i}),\\
\gamma\subseteq\lambda\end{subarray}}\prod_{(r,s)\in\gamma}\frac{1}{h(r,s)}\,\right]_{i,j=1}^{k},

where
(a_{j},b_{j})
and
(c_{i},d_{i})
are the endpoints of the border
strip
\theta_{i}\#\theta_{j}
. Lastly, using the Lindström–Gessel–Viennot lemma this
determinant is written as a weighted sum over non-intersecting
lattice paths in
\lambda
. By an explicit characterization of excited
diagrams in Section
3
, the supports of such paths are exactly the complements of
excited diagrams. The NHLF then follows.

A similar approach is used in Section
5
to give a
combinatorial proof of the first
q
-NHLF for all skew shapes given in
[
MPP1
]
.
The Hillman–Grassl inspired bijection in
[
MPP1
]
remains the only combinatorial
proof of the second
q
-NHLF.

1.5
.

We should mention that our inductive proof is involutive, but basic enough
to allow “bijectification”, i.e. an involution principle proof of the NHLF.
We refer to
[
K1
,
Rem
,
Zei
]
for the involution principle proofs
of the (usual) HLF.

[

1.6
.

We have:

(EC)

.

Now, for the skew shape

.

6.

7.5

Let

An equivalent form of this statement was announced in
[

]

(see
[

, §8.4]

Proof.

Let

and

are exactly the numbers on the horizontal/vertical steps at row

\lambda
when writing the numbers

along the path from the bottom left to the top right end. Thus

whenever

and 0 otherwise. When

and by (

\displaystyle G_{\lambda/\mu}({\bf x}\mid{\bf y})\Bigg|_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i}=\lambda_{i}+d-i+1,\\

\displaystyle\,=\,\frac{D(\mu)}{D(\lambda)}\Bigg|_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i}=\lambda_{i}+d-i+1,\\

10.1.

Other known formulas for

The Jacobi-Trudi identity is one of the first nontrivial formulas to

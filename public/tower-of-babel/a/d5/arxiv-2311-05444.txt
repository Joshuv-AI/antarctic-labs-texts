The category of a partitioned fan
Maximilian Kaipel

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the notion of an admissible partition of a simplicial polyhedral fan and define the category of a partitioned fan as a generalisation of the τ \tau -cluster morphism category of a finite-dimensional algebra. This establishes a complete lattice of categories around the τ \tau -cluster morphism category, which is closely tied to the fan structure. We prove that the classifying spaces of these categories are cube complexes, which reduces the process of determining if they are K ⁡ ( π , 1 ) K(\pi,1) spaces to three sufficient conditions. We characterise when these conditions are satisfied for fans in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} and prove that the first one, the existence of a certain faithful functor, is satisfied for hyperplane arrangements whose normal vectors lie in the positive orthant. As a consequence we obtain a new infinite class of algebras for which the τ \tau -cluster morphism category admits a faithful functor and for which the cube complexes are K ⁡ ( π , 1 ) K(\pi,1) spaces. In the final section we also offer a new algebraic proof of the relationship between an algebra and its g g -vector fan.

[

]

The

The definition of the cluster morphism category was extended to

-cluster morphism category

]

[
18
]

[

]
. Together with the work of

In this paper, we generalise the

-cluster morphism category to the

-cluster morphism category sits in a lattice of categories associated to the fan, which is ordered by refinement of partitions. In a similar way, the lattice of categories of a hyperplane arrangement admits two distinguished partitions of its underlying fan which define the

Each category defines a classifying space, which is the geometric realisation of its chains of composable morphisms. We generalise results of

The structure of this paper is as follows: In Section 2 we give a short background on polyhedral fans and simplicial complexes, in Section 3 we define admissible partitions of a fan and the category of a partitioned fan. In Section 4 we study the classifying space of the category in relation to the picture groups of the fan and show the existence of the faithful functor. In Section 5 the lattice structure of admissible partitions and thus of categories is described and in the final Section 6 we study the
\tau
-cluster morphism category and the
g
-fan algebraically.

2.
Background on fans and simplicial complexes

A
convex polyhedral cone
\sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a set of the form
\sigma=\{\sum_{i=i}^{s}\lambda_{i}v_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lambda_{i}\geq 0\}
, where
v_{1},\dots,v_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We also denote these non-negative linear combinations by
\sigma=\cone\{v_{1},\dots,v_{s}\}
. For two cones
\sigma=\cone\{v_{1},\dots,v_{s}\}
and
\kappa=\cone\{w_{1},\dots,w_{t}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we sometimes write
\cone\{\sigma,\kappa\}=\cone\{v_{1},\dots,v_{s},w_{1},\dots,w_{t}\}
. Note that
\{0\}
is also regarded as a convex polyhedral cone. Unless otherwise specified, in this paper, a
cone

\sigma
is a polyhedral cone with the following two properties:

•

We say
\sigma
is
strongly convex
if
\sigma\cap(-\sigma)=\{0\}
holds.

•

A cone
\sigma
is
simplicial
if the generating set is linearly independent (up to duplicate generators).

The
dimension

\dim(\sigma)
of a cone is the dimension of the linear subspace
\spann\{\sigma\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Denote by
\langle-,-\rangle
the standard inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A
face
of a cone
\sigma
is the intersection of
\sigma
with a hyperplane
\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle u,v\rangle=0\}
for some
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
\langle u,w\rangle\geq 0
for all
w\in\sigma
. If
\sigma=\cone\{v_{1},\dots,v_{s}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a simplicial cone, then a face of
\sigma
is simply a cone generated by a proper subset of
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{s}\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
fan

\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a collection of cones in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the following:

(1)

Each face of a cone in
\Sigma
is also a cone contained in
\Sigma
.

(2)

The intersection of two cones in
\Sigma
is a face of each of the two cones.

Denote by
\Sigma^{i}\subseteq\Sigma
the subset of cones of dimension
i
. We call a fan
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

finite
if it consists of a finite number of cones and
complete
if
\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We may view fans in two different ways. Firstly, fans naturally have the structure of a poset
(\Sigma,\subseteq)
ordered by inclusion, which we view as a category whose objects are cones and which has a unique morphism
\sigma\to\tau
whenever
\sigma\subseteq\tau
. The
rank

\rank(\Sigma)
of finite and complete fan in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the maximal dimension of any maximal cone with respect to inclusion, hence
n
. We use the poset category of the fan as a basis for the construction of the category of the partitioned fan in
3.4
. For a thorough introduction to fans in the context of toric geometry we refer to
[
26
]
.

On the other hand we can view fans as simplicial complexes, which are finite sets
\Delta^{0}
together with a collection
\Delta
of subsets of
\Delta^{0}
such that if
X\in\Delta
and
Y\subseteq X
, then
Y\in\Delta
. Elements
v\in\Delta^{0}
such that
\{v\}\in\Delta
are called
vertices
and subsets consisting of vertices are called
faces
or more specifically
k-simplices
if they consist of exactly
k+1
vertices. A simplicial fan
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defines a simplicial complex
\Delta(\Sigma)
whose vertices are the dimension 1 cones
\Sigma^{1}
and whose simplices are sets of vertices which span a cone of the fan. When a fan
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is simplicial, finite and complete, the
geometric realisation
of
\Delta(\Sigma)
is a
simplicial sphere
, a triangulation of the
(n-1)
-sphere, obtained by intersecting the unit
(n-1)
-sphere with the cones of
\Sigma
. However, we remark that this geometric realisation is not necessarily a polytope
[
60
, Ex. 7.5]
.

An arbitrary category
\mathcal{C}
defines a topological space, called the
classifying space

\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}
. This space is the geometric realisation of a simplicial set known as the
simplicial nerve
of the category. The 0-simplices correspond to the objects of
\mathcal{C}
and the
k
-simplices correspond to the chains of composable non-identity morphisms
(X_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{2}}\dots\xrightarrow{f_{k}}X_{k})
in
\mathcal{C}
. In
Section

4
we view the fan as a simplicial complex and build the classifying space of the category of a partitioned fan from it. For this purpose we need the following two constructions for simplicial complexes:

(1)

The
link

\link_{\Delta}(\sigma)
of a simplex
\sigma\in\Delta
is the simplicial subcomplex of
\Delta
given by

\link_{\Delta}(\sigma)\coloneqq\{\tau\in\Delta:\sigma\cap\tau=\emptyset\text{ and }\sigma\cup\tau\in\Delta\}.

(2)

The
join

\Delta_{1}*\Delta_{2}
of two simplicial complexes
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
has vertex set
\Delta_{1}^{0}\cup\Delta_{2}^{0}
and simplices given by

\Delta_{1}*\Delta_{2}\coloneqq\{\sigma\in\Delta_{1}^{0}\cup\Delta_{2}^{0}:\sigma\cap\Delta_{1}^{0}\in\Delta_{1}\text{ and }\sigma\cap\Delta_{2}^{0}\in\Delta_{2}\}.

The join with a simplicial complex consisting of a single vertex is called the
(topological) cone
over a simplicial complex.

3.
The category of a partitioned fan

We now generalise the geometric construction of the
\tau
-cluster morphism category
[
55
]
to a simplicial fan. Cones which are identified in the construction of
[
55
]
“have the same relative fan structure around them” in the following sense: The collection of cones containing a cone
\sigma
is denoted by
\starr(\sigma)\coloneqq\{\tau\in\Sigma:\sigma\subseteq\tau\}
. Let
\pi_{\sigma}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\spann\{\sigma\}^{\perp}
be the projection onto its orthogonal complement. For each cone
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, this defines another fan
\pi_{\sigma}(\starr(\sigma))
. Then two cones
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
which get identified in the construction of
[
55
]
satisfy
\spann\{\sigma_{1}\}^{\perp}=\spann\{\sigma_{2}\}^{\perp}
and
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\starr(\sigma_{1}))=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\starr(\sigma_{2}))
. Importantly, not all pairs of cones sharing these properties are identified. However, when generalising from the
g
-fan of a finite-dimensional algebra to an arbitrary simplicial fan, the information of which cones to identify is lost. Therefore, let
\Sigma
be a fan in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and consider for each cone
\sigma_{1}\in\Sigma
the collection of
potential identifications

\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{1}}\coloneqq\{\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma:\spann\{\sigma_{1}\}^{\perp}=\spann\{\sigma_{2}\}^{\perp}\text{ and }\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\starr(\sigma_{1}))=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\starr(\sigma_{2}))\}.

It is clear that this is an equivalence relation and therefore
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{1}}=\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{2}}
for any two cones sharing these properties. Now the set of potential identifications may be partitioned into sets of
actual identifications
. Recall, that a
partition
of a set
X
is a set
P
of non-empty pairwise-disjoint subsets, called
blocks
, of
X
whose union is
X
.
In other words, we split each
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
into blocks
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{1},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{m_{\sigma}}
for some
1\leq m_{\sigma}\leq|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}|
such that these coincide for all representatives of
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
. This induces a partition
\mathfrak{P}
of the fan
\Sigma
and we write
\sigma_{1}\sim\sigma_{2}
whenever
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{k}
for some
1\leq k\leq m_{\sigma}
and
\sigma\in\Sigma
.

Definition 3.1
.

A partition
\mathfrak{P}
of
\Sigma
as described above is called
admissible
if whenever
\sigma_{1}\sim\sigma_{2}
are such that
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2})
for some
\tau_{1}\in\starr(\sigma_{1})
and
\tau_{2}\in\starr(\sigma_{2})
, then
\tau_{1}\sim\tau_{2}
. A
partitioned fan
is a pair
(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
of a simplicial fan
\Sigma
and an admissible partition
\mathfrak{P}
of
\Sigma
.

This says that if two cones
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
are in the same equivalence class, then any two cones which are “in the same relative position” should be identified. This restriction is necessary to make the composition of morphisms in the category of a partitioned fan well-defined. It is not obvious that non-trivial admissible partitions exist, because the cones
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
in the definition may not satisfy
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{\tau}
for some
\tau\in\Sigma
, in other words it may not be possible to identify
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
with the rules defined above. Before proving that non-trivial admissible partitions always exist, we state the following elementary result from linear algebra for the sake of completeness in our context.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\sigma\subseteq\tau\in\Sigma^{n}
, then
\pi_{\tau}\circ\pi_{\sigma}=\pi_{\tau}
.

Proof.

Every vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
has a unique orthogonal decomposition
v=\pi_{\sigma}(v)+p_{\sigma}(v)
, where
p_{\sigma}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\spann\{\sigma\}
is the orthogonal projection onto the subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
spanned by
\sigma
. Then

\pi_{\tau}(v)=\pi_{\tau}(\tau_{\sigma}(v)+p_{\sigma}(v))=\pi_{\tau}\circ\pi_{\sigma}(v)+\pi_{\tau}\circ p_{\sigma}(v).

But since
p_{\sigma}(v)\subseteq\spann\{\tau\}
, it follows that
\pi_{\tau}\circ p_{\sigma}=0
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\sigma_{1}\sim\sigma_{2}
in
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
. If there exist
\tau_{1}\in\starr(\sigma_{1})
and
\tau_{2}\in\starr(\sigma_{2})
such that
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2})
, then
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{\tau}
for some
\tau\in\Sigma
.

Proof.

By definition, we need to show that in this case
\spann\{\tau_{1}\}^{\perp}=\spann\{\tau_{2}\}^{\perp}
and
\pi_{\tau_{1}}(\starr(\tau_{1}))=\pi_{\tau_{2}}(\starr(\tau_{2}))
, which would imply
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{\tau_{1}}=\mathcal{E}_{\tau_{2}}
. Because
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})\cap\sigma_{1}=\{0\}
, we may take any basis
B_{1}
of
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2})
and any basis
B_{2}
of
\spann(\sigma_{1})=\spann(\sigma_{2})
, then
B_{1}\cup B_{2}
is a basis of
\spann\{\tau_{1}\}
and
\spann\{\tau_{2}\}
, which immediately implies
\spann\{\tau_{1}\}^{\perp}=\spann\{\tau_{2}\}^{\perp}
, as required. To show that
\pi_{\tau_{1}}(\starr(\tau_{1}))=\pi_{\tau_{2}}(\starr(\tau_{2}))
, we use
3.2
. Since
\sigma_{i}\subseteq\tau_{i}
for
i=1,2
, we have

\pi_{\tau_{1}}(\starr(\tau_{1}))=\pi_{\tau_{1}}(\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\starr(\tau_{1})))=\pi_{\tau_{2}}(\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\starr(\tau_{2})))=\pi_{\tau_{2}}(\starr(\tau_{2})),

because
\pi_{\tau_{1}}=\pi_{\tau_{2}}
and since
\sigma_{1}\sim\sigma_{2}
and
\starr_{\pi_{\sigma_{i}}(\starr(\sigma_{i}))}(\pi_{\sigma_{i}}(\tau_{i}))=\pi_{\sigma_{i}}(\starr_{\Sigma}(\tau_{i}))
for
i=1,2
imply
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\starr(\tau_{1}))=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\starr(\tau_{2}))
. Therefore,
\mathcal{E}_{\tau_{1}}=\mathcal{E}_{\tau_{2}}
.
∎

Therefore, admissible partitions exist and throughout this paper, let
\mathfrak{P}
denote an admissible partition unless stated otherwise. We are ready to define our central object.

Definition 3.4
.

Given a partitioned fan (
\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
, define the
category of the partitioned fan

\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
as follows:

(1)

The objects of
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
are equivalence classes
[\sigma]
of the partition
\mathfrak{P}
of
\Sigma
.

(2)

The set of morphisms
\Hom_{\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})}([\sigma],[\tau])
consists of equivalence classes of objects in

\bigcup_{\sigma_{i}\in[\sigma],\tau_{j}\in[\tau]}\Hom_{\Sigma}(\sigma_{i},\tau_{j})

under the equivalence relation where
f_{\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}}\sim f_{\sigma_{2}\tau_{2}}
if and only if
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2})
.

(3)

Given
[f_{\sigma\kappa}]\in\Hom_{\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})}([\sigma],[\kappa])
and
[f_{\kappa\tau}]\in\Hom_{\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})}([\kappa],[\tau])
, their composition is defined as
[f_{\kappa\tau}]\circ[f_{\sigma\kappa}]=[f_{\sigma\tau}]
.

Because there exists a unique morphism
f_{\sigma\tau}
in the poset category
\Sigma
whenever
\sigma\subseteq\tau
, we also need to identify any two compositions of morphisms
[f_{\kappa_{1}\tau}]\circ[f_{\sigma\kappa_{1}}]
and
[f_{\kappa_{2}\tau}]\circ[f_{\sigma\kappa_{2}}]
which map to the same representative of an equivalence class. Similar to
[
55
, Rem. 3.5]
it is not clear that the composition of morphisms in
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
is well-defined for two reasons:

(1)

In order to define the composition of two non-zero morphisms
[f_{\sigma_{1}\kappa_{1}}]
and
[f_{\kappa_{2}\tau_{2}}]
where
\kappa_{1}\sim\kappa_{2}
, we need to find a morphism
f_{\kappa_{1}\tau_{1}}\sim f_{\kappa_{2}\tau_{2}}
so that
[f_{\kappa_{2}\tau_{2}}]\circ[f_{\sigma_{1}\kappa_{1}}]=[f_{\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}}]
.

(2)

Given morphisms
f_{\sigma_{1}\kappa_{1}}\sim f_{\sigma_{2}\kappa_{2}}
and
f_{\kappa_{1}\tau_{1}}\sim f_{\kappa_{2}\tau_{2}}
, we need to show
[f_{\kappa_{1}\tau_{1}}]\circ[f_{\sigma_{1}\kappa_{1}}]=[f_{\kappa_{2}\tau_{2}}]\circ[f_{\sigma_{2}\kappa_{2}}]
.

Now we resolve the other two problems in an analogous way to
[
55
, Lem. 3.9, Lem. 3.10]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any two morphisms
[f_{\sigma_{1}\kappa_{1}}]
and
[f_{\kappa_{2}\tau_{2}}]
in
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
with
\kappa_{1}\sim\kappa_{2}
there exists a morphism
f_{\kappa_{1}\tau_{1}}\sim f_{\kappa_{2}\tau_{2}}
with
\tau_{1}\sim\tau_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
\kappa_{1}\sim\kappa_{2}
it follows by definition that
\pi_{\kappa_{1}}(\starr(\kappa_{1}))=\pi_{\kappa_{2}}(\starr(\kappa_{2}))
. Thus, for each
\tau_{2}\in\starr(\kappa_{2})
there exists
\tau_{1}\in\starr(\kappa_{1})
such that
\pi_{\kappa_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\kappa_{2}}(\tau_{2})
. Since the partition is admissible, it follows that therefore
\tau_{1}\sim\tau_{2}
. Thus there exists a morphism
f_{\kappa_{1}\tau_{1}}
which satisfies
f_{\kappa_{1}\tau_{1}}\sim f_{\kappa_{2}\tau_{2}}
.
∎

The following is a special case of
3.5
where
\sigma_{1}=\kappa_{1}
, which will be used throughout this paper.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be an admissible partition and let
f_{\sigma_{2}\tau_{2}}
be a morphism of the poset category
\Sigma
. If
\sigma_{1}\sim\sigma_{2}
in
\mathfrak{P}
, then there exists a morphism
f_{\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}}
in the poset category such that
f_{\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}}\sim f_{\sigma_{2}\tau_{2}}
in
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
f_{\sigma_{1}\kappa_{1}}\sim f_{\sigma_{2}\kappa_{2}}
and
f_{\kappa_{1}\tau_{1}}\sim f_{\kappa_{2}\tau_{2}}
be two pairs of identified morphisms in
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
. Then
[f_{\sigma_{1}\kappa_{1}}]\circ[f_{\kappa_{1}\tau_{1}}]=[f_{\kappa_{2}\tau_{2}}]\circ[f_{\sigma_{2}\kappa_{2}}]
.

Proof.

We know by assumption that
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\kappa_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\kappa_{2})
and
\pi_{\kappa_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\kappa_{2}}(\tau_{2})
. Now take
w_{1}\in\kappa_{1}
and
w_{2}\in\kappa_{2}
such that
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(w_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(w_{2})
and take
v\in\pi_{\kappa_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\kappa_{2}}(\tau_{2})
. Then, similar to the proof of
3.3
, a basis of
\spann\{\tau_{1}\}
consists of the union of a basis of
\kappa_{1}
and a basis of
\pi_{\kappa_{1}}(\tau_{1})
. Hence there exists a scalar
\epsilon_{1}>0
such that
w_{1}+\epsilon_{1}v\in\tau_{1}
. Similarly there exists
\epsilon_{2}>0
such that
w_{2}+\epsilon_{2}v\in\tau_{2}
. Set
\delta=\min\{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}\}
, then
w_{i}+\delta v\in\tau_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Then

\displaystyle\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(w_{1}+\delta v)=\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(w_{1})+\delta\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(v)=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(w_{2})+\delta\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(v)=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(w_{2}+\delta v).

Hence
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})\cap\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2})\neq\{0\}
. Because of the equivalence of fans
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\starr\sigma_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\starr\sigma_{2})
, the intersection is either zero or the two projections coincide. This implies
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2})
and thus
f_{\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}}\sim f_{\sigma_{2}\tau_{2}}
.
∎

3.1.
Cubical categories

Gromov
[
28
]
observed that for cube complexes, being
locally
CAT(0) and thus a
K(\pi,1)
space, is equivalent to a local combinatorial condition. Informally speaking, CAT(0) spaces are topological spaces whose geodesic triangle are “no fatter than Euclidean triangles” and locally CAT(0) spaces are those which admit a CAT(0) universal cover. Such spaces are important examples of
K(\pi,1)
spaces, a class of Eilenberg-MacLane spaces. A connected topological space
X
is a
K(\pi,1)
space if it satisfies the following equivalent conditions:

(1)

The homotopy groups of
X
above degree 1 are all trivial;

(2)

The universal cover of
X
is contractible;

(3)

The cohomology of
X
with arbitrary coefficients is isomorphic to the cohomology of its fundamental group.

Igusa
[
33
]
categorified this approach and introduced
cubical categories
, whose classifying spaces are cube complexes, where a cube complex is a topological space built from
k
-cubes, similar to a simplicial complex being built from
k
-simplices. In this setting, the conditions of Gromov give a set of sufficient conditions for the classifying space of the category to be a
K(\pi,1)
(cf.
[
33
, Prop. 3.4]
). For representation-finite hereditary algebras, Igusa and Todorov
[
35
]
show that the cluster morphism category is a cubical category and make use of the sufficient conditions to show it is a
K(\pi,1)
for
\pi
the picture group. Moreover, the
\tau
-cluster morphism category is a cubical category for all algebras whose
g
-fan is finite
[
31
]
. We remark that their proof actually works for all finite-dimensional algebras. Results about
K(\pi,1)
spaces have been obtained by Hanson and Igusa for Nakayama algebras
[
31
]
and in a follow-up paper for Nakayama-like and certain gentle algebras
[
30
]
. In this section we show that all categories associated to a partitioned fan are cubical categories, extending results of
[
31
]
,
[
33
]
and
[
35
]
. For the definition of a cubical category we need the following two categories:

•

The
standard
k
-cube category

\mathcal{I}^{k}
is the name-giving example of a cubical category. It is the poset category on subsets of
\{1,\dots,k\}
where morphisms are given by inclusion.

•

For any category
\mathcal{C}
and any morphism
(A\xrightarrow{f}B)\in\mathcal{C}
, the
factorisation category

\Faq(f)
is the category whose objects are factorisations
A\xrightarrow{g}C\xrightarrow{h}B
such that
h\circ g=f
and whose morphisms

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{2}}

are morphisms
\phi:C_{1}\to C_{2}
such that
\phi\circ g_{1}=g_{2}
and
h_{1}=h_{2}\circ\phi
.

Given an object
(A\xrightarrow{g}C\xrightarrow{h}B)
in
\Faq(f)
, we call
g
a
first factor of
f
if
g
is irreducible in
\mathcal{C}
and
h
a
last factor of
f
if
h
is irreducible in
\mathcal{C}
. We are now ready to define cubical categories.

Definition 3.10
.

A
cubical category
is a small category
\mathcal{C}
with the following properties:

(1)

Every morphism
f:A\to B
in
\mathcal{C}
has a
rank

\rank(f)
which is a non-negative integer such that
\rank(g\circ f)=\rank(f)+\rank(g)
for all composable morphisms
f,g\in\mathcal{C}
.

(2)

If
\rank(f)=k
then there is an isomorphism
\Faq(f)\cong\mathcal{I}^{k}
.

(3)

The forgetful functor
\Faq(f)\to\mathcal{C}
sending
(A\xrightarrow{}C\xrightarrow{}B)\mapsto C
is an embedding. Thus, every morphism of rank
k
has
k
distinct first factors and
k
distinct last factors.

(4)

Every morphism of rank
k
is determined by its
k
first factors.

(5)

Every morphism of rank
k
is determined by its
k
last factors.

Condition 2 says that in a cubical category, the classifying space of any morphism
f
is a solid cube, i.e.
\mathcal{B}\Faq(f)=[0,1]^{\rank(f)}
. For the category
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
of a partitioned fan, the rank of a morphism
[f_{\sigma\tau}]
in
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
is given by the difference of the dimensions of the cones, in other words,

\rank([f_{\sigma\tau}])=\dim\tau-\dim\sigma.

This is well-defined since two identified cones
\sigma_{1}\sim\sigma_{2}
in
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
have the same linear span in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by definition and therefore the same dimension. We begin by investigating the finest partition
\mathfrak{P}_{\text{poset}}
of the fan, in which case the the category
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{\text{poset}})
is just the poset category naturally associated with the fan.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\Sigma
be a simplicial fan. The category
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{\textnormal{poset}})
satisfies condition 2. of
3.10
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\subseteq\tau\in\Sigma
be two cones and consider the morphism
f_{\sigma\tau}\in\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{\text{poset}})
. Assume that
\dim\sigma=k
and
\dim\tau=\ell
. Then we may express
\tau
in the following way:

\tau=\cone\{v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell-k},\sigma\}\in\Sigma

where
v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell-k}\in\Sigma
are the linearly independent dimension 1 cones of
\Sigma
contained in
\tau
which are not contained in
\sigma
. This way of writing
\tau
is unique. The following bijection between objects of
\mathcal{I}^{\ell-k}
and
\Faq(f_{\sigma\tau})
induces an isomorphism of categories

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\ell-k}

\displaystyle\to\Faq(f_{\sigma\tau})

\displaystyle\{1,\dots,\ell-k\}\supseteq S

\displaystyle\mapsto(\sigma\to\cone\left\{\{v_{i}\}_{i\in S},\sigma\right\}\to\tau)

where morphisms are induced by the individual poset structures, in other words
S\subseteq P\in\mathcal{I}^{\ell-k}
if and only if
\cone\left\{\{v_{i}\}_{i\in S},\sigma\right\}\subseteq\cone\left\{\{v_{i}\}_{i\in P},\sigma\right\}
. It is clear how to define the inverse and that this is an isomorphism.
∎

The categories of a partitioned fan are constructed from the poset category
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{\textnormal{poset}})
via identifications. The following key lemma is essential in understanding how the category changes when cones are identified. In particular, it shows that two morphisms which are identified will have coinciding factorisation cubes.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
\sigma_{1}\neq\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma
and
\tau_{1}\neq\tau_{2}\in\Sigma
be distinct cones such that
f_{\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}}\sim f_{\sigma_{2}\tau_{2}}
in
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
. Then for every factorisation
\sigma_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{\sigma_{1}\kappa_{1}}}\kappa_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{\kappa_{1}\tau_{1}}}\tau_{1}
of
f_{\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}}
there exists a unique factorisation
\sigma_{2}\xrightarrow{f_{\sigma_{2}\kappa_{2}}}\kappa_{2}\xrightarrow{f_{\kappa_{2}\tau_{2}}}\tau_{2}

of
f_{\sigma_{2}\tau_{2}}
such that
\kappa_{1}\sim\kappa_{2}
. In this case we have
f_{\sigma_{1}\kappa_{1}}\sim f_{\sigma_{2},\kappa_{2}}
and
f_{\kappa_{1}\tau_{1}}\sim f_{\kappa_{2}\tau_{2}}
in
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
.

Proof.

If
\sigma_{1}
or
\sigma_{2}
is a maximal cone, then such
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
do not exist and the result is trivial. Therefore assume that
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are not maximal. By assumption
\sigma_{1}\sim\sigma_{2}
and
\tau_{1}\sim\tau_{2}
and thus
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\starr(\sigma_{1}))=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\starr(\sigma_{2}))
. So for every
\kappa_{1}\in\starr(\sigma_{1})
satisfying
\kappa_{1}\subseteq\tau_{1}
there exists a unique
\kappa_{2}\in\starr(\sigma_{2})
such that
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\kappa_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\kappa_{2})
. Because the partition
\mathfrak{P}
is admissible and
\sigma_{1}\sim\sigma_{2}
, this implies that
\kappa_{1}\sim\kappa_{2}
. This implies
f_{\sigma_{1}\kappa_{1}}\sim f_{\sigma_{2}\kappa_{2}}
by definition. We must have that
\kappa_{2}\subseteq\tau_{2}
, since

\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\kappa_{2})=\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\kappa_{1})\subseteq\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2})

implies that
\kappa_{2}
is a face of
\tau_{2}
. To show that
f_{\kappa_{1}\tau_{1}}\sim f_{\kappa_{2}\tau_{2}}
, we use
3.2
since
\sigma_{i}\subseteq\kappa_{i}
implies
\kappa_{i}^{\perp}\subseteq\sigma_{i}^{\perp}
for
i=1,2
. Thus,

\pi_{\kappa_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\kappa_{1}}\circ\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\kappa_{2}}\circ\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2})=\pi_{\kappa_{2}}(\tau_{2}),

since
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2})
follows from
f_{\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}}\sim f_{\sigma_{2}\tau_{2}}
by definition.
∎

Therefore, the category
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
of a partitioned fan satisfies condition 2. of
3.10
for any admissible partition. The following example illustrates the identification of factorisation cubes described in the previous lemma.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
[f_{\sigma\tau}]\in\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
. There exists exactly one morphism

([\sigma]\xrightarrow{[g_{1}]}[\kappa]\xrightarrow{[h_{1}]}[\tau])\to([\sigma]\xrightarrow{[g_{2}]}[\lambda]\xrightarrow{[h_{2}]}[\tau])

in
\Faq([f_{\sigma\tau}])
whenever
\kappa_{1}\subseteq\lambda_{1}
for some
\kappa_{1}\in[\kappa]
and
\lambda_{1}\in[\lambda]
and none otherwise.

Proof.

The assumption that two such maps
[f_{\kappa_{1}\lambda_{1}}]\neq[f_{\kappa_{2}\lambda_{2}}]
exist implies the existence of distinct representatives
\kappa_{1}\sim\kappa_{2}
and
\lambda_{1}\sim\lambda_{2}
in the equivalence classes. Assume that there exists only one representative
\sigma\in[\sigma]
, then
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}\in\starr(\sigma)
implies
\pi_{\sigma}(\kappa_{1})\neq\pi_{\sigma}(\kappa_{2})
. Hence
[f_{\sigma\kappa_{1}}]\neq[f_{\sigma\kappa_{2}}]
which is a contradiction since they both equal
[g_{1}]
. It follows that there need to be distinct representatives
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in[\sigma]
and thus we have two sets of inclusions
\sigma_{i}\subseteq\kappa_{i}\subseteq\lambda_{i}
satisfying
[f_{\sigma_{i}\lambda_{i}}]=[g_{2}]
for
i=1,2
. Then
3.12
implies that
[f_{\kappa_{1}\lambda_{1}}]=[f_{\kappa_{2}\lambda_{2}}]
, a contradiction. The (non-)existence is obvious.
∎

We remark that there is not necessarily a unique morphism
[\kappa]\to[\lambda]
in
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
in general. However, if one
\lambda_{1}\supseteq\kappa_{1}
exists, then there exist
\lambda_{i}\supseteq\kappa_{i}
for every
\kappa_{i}\in[\kappa]
, such that
\lambda_{i}\in[\lambda]
and
f_{\kappa_{i}\lambda_{i}}\in[f_{\kappa\lambda}]
by
3.12
. We are now able to prove that the third condition of the definition of a cubical category holds for the category of a partitioned fan.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
[f_{\sigma\tau}]
be a morphism in
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
. The forgetful functor
\Faq([f_{\sigma\tau}])\to\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
given by
([\sigma]\to[\kappa]\to[\tau])\mapsto[\kappa]
is an embedding i.e. faithful and injective-on-objects.

Proof.

Assume two factorisations of a morphism
[f_{\sigma\tau}]\in\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})

[\sigma_{1}]\xrightarrow{[f_{\sigma_{1}\kappa_{1}}]}[\kappa_{1}]\xrightarrow{[f_{\kappa_{1}\tau_{1}}]}[\tau_{1}]\quad\text{ and }\quad[\sigma_{2}]\xrightarrow{[f_{\sigma_{2}\kappa_{2}}]}[\kappa_{2}]\xrightarrow{[f_{\kappa_{2}\tau_{2}}]}[\tau_{2}]

satisfy
[\kappa_{1}]=[\kappa_{2}]
but
\kappa_{1}\neq\kappa_{2}
. We want to use
3.12
to show the pairwise identification of the morphisms and thus an equivalence of factorisations. We need to show that
\sigma_{1}\neq\sigma_{2}
and
\tau_{1}\neq\tau_{2}
. Since
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
have the same linear span but are distinct, it follows that there is no generator of one which is linearly independent with respect to the generators of the other and thus they cannot both be contained in the same simplicial cone
\tau
. Hence there must exist distinct
\tau_{i}\supseteq\kappa_{i}
for
i=1,2
. However, this implies the existence of two distinct representatives
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
in
[\sigma]
similar to the proof of
3.14
as otherwise
\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}
follows from the identification of the maps
[f_{\sigma\tau_{1}}]=[f_{\sigma\tau_{2}}]
. Therefore,
3.12
implies the pairwise-identification of the morphisms and hence the two factorisations are identical and the functor is injective-on-objects. The functor is faithful since there exists at most one morphism between any two objects in
\Faq([f_{\sigma\tau}])
by
3.14
.
∎

In
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
, just like in the poset category of the fan
\Sigma
, the irreducible morphisms are exactly the morphisms of rank 1. Hence, given a morphism
[f_{\sigma\tau}]\in\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
where
\dim(\tau)=\ell
and
\dim(\sigma)=k
, we may write
\tau=\cone\{\sigma,v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell-k}\}
like before. Then it is easily seen that the
\ell-k
first factors are the rank 1 morphisms
[f_{\sigma\kappa_{i}}]
, where
\kappa_{i}\coloneqq\cone\{\sigma,v_{i}\}\subseteq\tau
and the last factors are the rank 1 morphisms
[f_{\lambda_{i}\tau}]
, where

\lambda_{i}\coloneqq\cone\{\sigma,v_{1},\dots,v_{i-1},v_{i+1},\dots,v_{\ell-k}\}.

Putting the above results together we arrive at the following.

Theorem 3.16
.

The category
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
of a partitioned fan is cubical.

Proof.

Like stated before, the rank function is given by
\rank([f_{\sigma\tau}])=\dim\tau-\dim\sigma
. Condition 2. of
3.10
follows from combining
3.11
and
3.12
, whereas condition 3. was shown explicitly in
3.15
. Let
[f_{\sigma\tau}]\in\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
be a morphism. Given a choice of representative
f_{\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}\in[f_{\sigma\tau}]
, the first factors are given by
\{[f_{\sigma^{\prime}\kappa_{i}^{\prime}}]\}_{i=1}^{\ell-k}
, where
\kappa_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq\tau^{\prime}
are constructed from
\sigma^{\prime}
and
\tau^{\prime}
as above. It is then clear that they uniquely determine the morphism
[f_{\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}]
by writing
\tau^{\prime}=\cone\{\sigma^{\prime},\kappa_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\kappa_{\ell-k}^{\prime}\}
. On the other hand, the last factors
[f_{\lambda_{i}\tau}]
are easily seen to determine the morphism
[f_{\sigma\tau}]
, where
\sigma=\bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell-k}\lambda_{i}
.
∎

4.
The classifying space and picture group

We have established that the categories of a partitioned fan are cubical categories, hence it is natural to study their topological properties next. Throughout this section, let
\Sigma
be a finite and complete fan in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathfrak{P}
an admissible partition of
\Sigma
. In this case, we describe the classifying space of the category
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
as a finite CW-complex, similar to
[
31
]
,
[
33
]
and
[
35
]
. Moreover, we associate the
picture group
to a fan, by generalising the definitions of
[
31
]
and
[
38
]
, see also
6.19
. We investigate the sufficient conditions for the categories
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
to be a
K(\pi,1)
by studying the picture group. One of the conditions is the existence of a faithful functor from the category
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
to a group. We show that a faithful functor to the picture group exists for fans of rank 2 and a large class of hyperplane arrangements.

4.1.
Description of the CW-complex

A
CW-complex

X
is a topological space of particular importance in algebraic topology. It is constructed from a discrete set
X^{0}
, called
0
-cells
. The
k
-skeleton

X^{k}
is formed from
X^{k-1}
by attaching
k
-cells
e_{i}^{k}
via maps
\varphi_{i}:S^{k-1}\to X^{k-1}
for some index set
I
. Hence
X^{k}
is the quotient space of the disjoint union
X^{k-1}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{i\in I}D_{i}^{k}
of
X^{k-1}
with a collection of
k
-disks
\{D_{i}^{k}\}_{i\in I}
under the identification
x\sim\varphi_{i}(x)
for
x\in\partial D_{i}^{k}
. As a set,
X^{k}
is the disjoint union of
X^{k-1}
with open
k
-disks. The name CW-complex comes from two properties of such complexes: closure-finiteness and weak topology. Furthermore, the fundamental group of CW-complexes are completely determined by their 1-cells and 2-cells. For more details we refer the reader to
[
32
]
. In the following construction of the CW-complex
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
, each cell is the (topological) cone of the simplicial sphere described in the following definition, hence a disk.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
be a partitioned fan and
\sigma\in\Sigma
be a cone of dimension
k\neq n
. Define
\mathcal{S}(\sigma)
to be the simplicial complex whose vertices are the cones
\tau\in\starr(\sigma)^{k+1}
and for which
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\ell-k}
spans a simplex if and only if
\cone\{\pi_{\sigma}(\tau_{1}),\dots,\pi_{\sigma}(\tau_{\ell-k})\}\in\pi_{\sigma}(\starr(\sigma))
.

Lemma 4.2
.

\mathcal{S}(\sigma)
is homeomorphic to a
(n-k-1)
-sphere.

Proof.

By intersecting a finite and complete simplicial fan in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a
(n-1)
-sphere centered at the origin, we obtain the geometric realisation of
\Sigma
to be a
(n-1)
-sphere. Similarly for
\mathcal{S}(\sigma)
, the projection
\pi_{\sigma}(\starr\sigma)
is a finite and complete simplicial fan and thus the geometric realisation of it is a
(n-\dim(\sigma)-1)
-sphere.
∎

We remark that all representatives
\sigma_{i}\in[\sigma]
define isomorphic simplicial complexes because
\sigma_{i}\sim\sigma_{j}
implies that the fans
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\starr\sigma_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\starr\sigma_{2})
coincide. Therefore, given an equivalence class
[\sigma]\in\mathfrak{P}
, we denote by
[\mathcal{S}(\sigma)]
the isomorphism class of simplicial complexes

for
\sigma_{i}\in[\sigma]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite and complete fan in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathfrak{P}
an admissible partition of
\Sigma
. The classifying space
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
is a
n

of dimension

for each equivalence class
[\sigma]\in\mathfrak{P}
. The
k
-cell
e([\sigma])
is the union of the factorisation cubes of the morphisms
[f_{\sigma\tau}]
, where
\tau\in\starr(\sigma)^{n}
.

Let
e([\sigma])
be the union of factorisation cubes of all morphisms
[f_{\sigma\tau}]
, where
\tau\in\starr(\sigma)^{n}
. To obtain the CW-structure of
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
, we must show that each
e([\sigma])
is a disk of dimension
k=n-\dim(\sigma)
attached to lower dimensional cells along its boundary. We show this in three steps.

Step 1: Start with the disjoint union of factorisation cubes.

Let
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in[\sigma]
and
\tau_{1}\in\starr(\sigma_{1})
and
\tau_{2}\in\starr(\sigma_{2})
be maximal cones such that
f_{\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}}\sim f_{\sigma_{2}\tau_{2}}
. From
3.12
we know that factorisation cubes of the two morphisms
[f_{\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}}]
and
[f_{\sigma_{2}\tau_{2}}]
are identified, hence it suffices to consider only one representative
\sigma\in[\sigma]
by
3.6
. We begin by defining the disjoint union of factorisation cubes
X([\sigma])=\bigsqcup_{\tau\in\starr(\sigma)^{n}}[f_{\sigma\tau}]
. It is clear that a face of the factorisation cube of
[f_{\sigma\tau}]
corresponds to the factorisation cube of some morphism
[f_{\kappa\lambda}]
satisfying
\sigma\subseteq\kappa\subseteq\lambda\subseteq\tau
. Consider the equivalence relation
\sim_{1}
on
X([\sigma])
which identifies faces corresponding to the same morphism. By definition we obtain
e([\sigma])\equiv X([\sigma])/\sim_{1}
. We split
\sim_{1}
into two types of identifications. First, we only identify factorisation cubes of morphisms of the form
[f_{\sigma\lambda}]
. Denote this equivalence on
X([\sigma])
by
\sim_{2}
. After showing that the resulting space is a disk, we let
\sim_{3}
identify the factorisation cubes of morphisms
[f_{\kappa\tau}]
not starting at
\sigma
and show that the identifications of
\sim_{3}
occur only on the boundary. Thus since
X([\sigma])/\sim_{1}=(X([\sigma])/\sim_{2})/\sim_{3}
we obtain the desired result.

Step 2: Show that
X([\sigma])/\sim_{2}
is a disk.

We compare this quotient space with the classifying space of the following category.

Definition 4.5
.

Given an object
[\sigma]\in\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
, the

(or

)

[\sigma]\to[\tau]
in

([\sigma]\to[\tau_{1}])\to([\sigma]\to[\tau_{2}])

4.2.
The picture group of a fan

Igusa, Todorov and Weyman
[
38
]
associate the picture group to a representation-finite hereditary algebra and study its cohomology. Later, Hanson and Igusa
[
31
]
extended their definition to
\tau
-tilting finite algebras. In these cases it was shown by the respective authors, that the classifying space of the (
\tau
)-cluster morphism category has fundamental group isomorphic to the picture group and thus if the classifying space is a
K(\pi,1)
the cohomologies of the two coincide. An algebra induces a partial ordering on the maximal cones of its
g
-fan which defines the picture group, see
Section

6
. To generalise the picture group to finite and complete simplicial fans we assume the existence of a
weak fan poset
, generalising the notion of a fan poset introduced in
[
50
, Sec. 3]
.

Definition 4.7
.

A
fan poset
is a pair
(\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
where
\Sigma
is a finite complete fan in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{P}
is a finite poset whose elements are the maximal cones of
\Sigma
, subject to the following conditions:

(1)

For every cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the set of maximal cones
\starr(\sigma)^{n}
containing
\sigma
is an interval in
\mathcal{P}
, which we denote by
[\sigma^{-},\sigma^{+}]
and call a
facial interval
.

(2)

For every interval
I
of
\mathcal{P}
, the union of all maximal cones in
I
is a convex polyhedral cone, which may not be strongly-convex.

For example, the poset of regions of a central simplicial hyperplane arrangement with an arbitrary choice of base region as defined by Edelman
[
25
]
defines a fan poset by
[
50
, Thm. 4.2]
. Moreover,
g
-fans of
\tau
-tilting finite algebras are equipped with a natural fan poset induced by the poset of torsion classes as we show in
6.15
. To achieve greater generality we weaken the definition above in the following way.

Definition 4.8
.

A
weak fan poset
is a pair
(\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
where
\Sigma
is a finite complete fan in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{P}
is a finite poset whose elements are the maximal cones of
\Sigma
, subject to the following conditions:

(1)

For every cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the set of maximal cones
\starr(\sigma)^{n}
containing
\sigma
is an interval in
\mathcal{P}
, which we denote by
[\sigma^{-},\sigma^{+}]
and call a
facial interval
.

(2)

Every cover relation
\tau_{1}\lessdot\tau_{2}
in
\mathcal{P}
can be seen as a facial interval
[\sigma^{-},\sigma^{+}]
for some
(n-1)
-dimensional cone
\sigma=\tau_{1}\cap\tau_{2}
.

We call a triple
(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P},\mathcal{P})
a
partitioned fan poset
if
(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
is a partitioned fan and
(\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
is a weak fan poset. The result
[
50
, Prop. 3.3]
implies that if
(\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
is a fan poset then it is also a weak fan poset.

.

For a poset
\mathcal{P}
we denote by
\Hasse(\mathcal{P})
the oriented graph whose vertices are elements of
\mathcal{P}
and which has an arrow
x\xleftarrow{}y
whenever
x\lessdot y
is a cover relation in
\mathcal{P}
. If
(\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
is a weak fan poset, we label the arrows of

with the cone of codimension 1 giving rise to the cover relation. We are now ready to define the picture group of a partitioned fan with a choice of fan poset.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P},\mathcal{P})
be a partitioned fan poset. Define the
picture group

G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P},\mathcal{P})
to have generators
\{X_{[\sigma]}:\sigma\in\Sigma^{n-1}\}
and the following sets of relations:

(1)

X_{[\sigma_{1}]}\dots X_{[\sigma_{k}]}=X_{[\sigma_{1}^{\prime}]}\dots X_{[\sigma_{\ell}^{\prime}]}
whenever
(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k})
and
(\sigma_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\sigma_{\ell}^{\prime})
are two distinct ordered sequences of cones of codimension 1 labelling the arrows of some maximal chain of an interval
[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]
in
\mathcal{P}
. We abbreviate this group element by
X_{[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]}
.

(2)

X_{[\sigma_{1}^{-},\kappa_{1}^{-}]}=X_{[\sigma_{2}^{-},\kappa_{2}^{-}]}
, whenever
[f_{\sigma_{1}\kappa_{1}}]=[f_{\sigma_{2}\kappa_{2}}]
in
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
.

Notice that the picture group
G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P},\mathcal{P})
satisfies
X_{[\sigma^{-},\kappa^{-}]}X_{[\kappa^{-},\tau^{-}]}=X_{[\sigma^{-},\tau^{-}]}
for appropriate
\sigma,\kappa,\tau\in\Sigma
. We prove in
Section

6.3
that the picture group defined for
\tau
-tilting finite algebras in
[
31
]
satisfies these relations. Given a fan
\Sigma
, different choices of a weak fan poset
(\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
may define non-isomorphic picture groups. Moreover, it is possible that some generators become trivial due to the relation
X_{[\sigma]}=X_{e}
arising from relations of the second type in
4.10
. We call a fan poset which does not annihilate any generators in this way
non-degenerate
. For a partitioned fan poset
(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P},\mathcal{P})
we say that
\mathcal{P}
is
well-defined on identified stars
whenever the induced fan posets
\mathcal{P}|_{\pi_{\sigma_{1}}}
and
\mathcal{P}|_{\pi_{\sigma_{2}}}
on
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\starr\sigma_{1})
and
\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\starr\sigma_{2})
coincide for distinct cones
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma
such that
[\sigma_{1}]=[\sigma_{2}]
in
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
. These two notions are equivalent.

Lemma 4.11
.

A fan poset
(\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
is non-degenerate if and only if
\mathcal{P}
is well-defined on identified stars. In this case the second set of relations of
4.10
is trivially satisfied.

Proof.

The fan poset is determined by its covering relations of the form
\tau_{1}\lessdot\tau_{2}
whenever
\codim\tau_{1}\cap\tau_{2}=1
. Hence it is clear that if
\mathcal{P}
is well-defined on identified stars, then for
\sigma_{1}\sim\sigma_{2}
of codimension 1 we have
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\sigma_{1}^{-})=\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\sigma_{1})^{-}=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\sigma_{2})^{-}=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\sigma_{2}^{-})
. Thus the fan poset is non-degenerate. Conversely, if
(\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
is non-degenerate, assume for a contradiction that there is some pair of identified stars such that the induced poset on their projections does not coincide. Then at least one covering relation of the poset must be different in the projection and that wall would be crossed in two different directions in the fan poset and thus the poset would no longer be non-degenerate. Thus
\mathcal{P}
is well-defined on identified stars. In this case, the relations of type 2 in
4.10
are satisfied because by definition
[f_{\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}}]=[f_{\sigma_{2}\tau_{2}}]
implies
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2})
and since the poset is well-defined on identified stars we have
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\sigma_{1}^{-})=\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\sigma_{1})^{-}=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\sigma_{2})^{-}=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\sigma_{2}^{-})
and similarly for
\tau_{1}^{-}
and
\tau_{2}^{-}
. Since the paths from
\pi_{\sigma_{i}}(\tau_{i}^{-})
to
\pi_{\sigma_{i}}(\sigma_{i}^{-})
coincide in the projection for
i=1,2
and the partition is admissible, the labels of the paths from
\tau_{i}^{-}
to
\sigma_{i}^{-}
must be pairwise identified.
∎

The assumption on a (weak) fan poset

of the following lemma is referred to as

in

.

.

Let

Proof.

The poset is determined by its covering relation. Let
[\sigma_{1}]=[\sigma_{2}]\in\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P})
be an equivalence class of codimension 1 cones. We need to show that the poset induced on
\pi_{\sigma_{i}}(\starr\sigma_{i})
coincides. Assume for a contradiction that
\tau_{1}\lessdot\tau_{2}
is the covering relation given by the star of
\sigma_{1}
and
\tau_{3}\lessdot\tau_{4}
the covering relation given by the star of
\sigma_{2}
such that
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{4})
and
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{2})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{3})
. Then the normal vector
\nu_{1}
pointing from
\tau_{1}
to
\tau_{2}
is orthogonal to the hyperplane
\spann\{\sigma_{1}\}
and satisfies
b(\nu_{1})>0
, but at the same time the normal vector
\nu_{2}
pointing from
\tau_{3}
to
\tau_{4}
is orthogonal to the hyperplane
\spann\{\sigma_{2}\}
and satisfies

. Since

we have
\spann\{\sigma_{1}\}=\spann\{\sigma_{2}\}
, it follows that
\nu_{1}=-\nu_{2}
, but then either
b(\nu_{1})<0
or
b(\nu_{2})<0
, a contradiction.
∎

In a similar way to

we may give an alternate description of the picture group as follows.

Lemma 4.13
.

If
\mathcal{P}
is non-degenerate, the picture group

may be presented with the set of generators
\{X_{[\sigma]}:\sigma\in\Sigma^{n-1}\}\cup\{g_{\tau}:\tau\in\Sigma^{n}\}
and a relation

g_{\tau_{2}}=X_{[\sigma]}g_{\tau_{1}}

if there is an arrow
\tau_{1}\xleftarrow{\sigma}\tau_{2}
in
\Hasse(\mathcal{P})
labelled by by
\sigma
and the relation

.

Proof.

Let

be a group with presentation given as above and let

and
\tau_{2}\xrightarrow{\sigma_{1}^{\prime}}\dots\xrightarrow{\sigma_{\ell}^{\prime}}\tau_{1}
be two distinct sequences of codimension 1 cones labelling the arrows of some maximal chain in the interval

. These sequences of codimension 1 cones give rise to the relation

X_{[\sigma_{1}]}\dots X_{[\sigma_{k}]}g_{\tau_{1}}=g_{\tau_{2}}=X_{[\sigma_{1}^{\prime}]}\dots X_{[\sigma_{\ell}^{\prime}]}g_{\tau_{1}}

in

which implies that

satisfies the relation

as required. Moreover, since

has a minimal element

, there exists a sequence
\tau\xrightarrow{\sigma_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\dots\xrightarrow{\sigma_{s}^{\prime\prime}}0^{-}
labelling the arrows of a maximal chain in the interval

in

. We then obtain

since

. As a consequence of these two observations we can replace the generators

by expressions using only generators

and obtain the presentation of the picture group in

∎

Remark 4.15
.

Taking any fan with its trivial poset partition
\mathfrak{P}_{\text{poset}}
, since the classifying space is a ball, its fundamental group is trivial but the picture group is not. So, in contrast to the setting of finite-dimensional algebras, the picture group
G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P},\mathcal{P})
is not necessarily isomorphic to the fundamental group of
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
.

Nonetheless, for a special class of fan posets, we obtain an isomorphism between picture group and fundmantal group. Recall that a
lattice

L
is a partially ordered set for which any two elements
x,y
have a unique common maximal lower bound,
x\land y
, and a unique minimal upper bound,
x\lor y
. A
polygon
in a finite lattice
L
is an interval
[x,y]
such that
(x,y)
consists of two disjoint non-empty chains. The lattice is
polygonal
if for any two arrows
y_{1}\to x
and
y_{2}\to x
in
\Hasse(L)
the interval
[x,y_{1}\lor y_{2}]
is a polygon and for arrows
y\to x_{1}
and
y\to x_{2}
in
\Hasse(L)
the interval
[x_{1}\land x_{2},y]
is a polygon. We say two maximal chains in an interval
[x,y]\subseteq L
are related by a
polygon move
if the two chains differ only in that one chain covers one side of a polygon while the other covers the other side. For further background on polygonal lattices we refer to
[
52
, Sec. 9.6]
.

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P},\mathcal{P})
be a non-degenerate partitioned fan poset. If
\mathcal{P}
is a polygonal lattice, then it suffices to consider facial intervals coming from cones of codimension 2 to obtain all relations of
G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P},\mathcal{P})
. If additionally
\mathfrak{P}
identifies all maximal cones of
\Sigma
, then
G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P},\mathcal{P})
is isomorphic to
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}))
.

Proof.

The type 2 relations of
4.10
are satisfied due to non-degeneracy of the poset by
4.11
. Let
[x,y]
be an interval in
\mathcal{P}
. Since
\mathcal{P}
is a finite polygonal lattice, any two maximal chains in
[x,y]
are related by a sequence of polygon moves by
[
52
, Lem. 9-6.3]
. Trivially, the labels of two maximal chains which are related by a polygon move differ only in the labels of the two sides of the polygon. Thus it is sufficient to consider the group relations coming from polygons of
\mathcal{P}
to give a presentation of
G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P},\mathcal{P})
.

We now show that every cone of codimension 2 of
\Sigma
gives rise to a polygon of
\mathcal{P}
and that in fact every polygon of
\mathcal{P}
arises this way, as claimed. Thus, let
\sigma\in\Sigma
be a cone of codimension 2 and consider the induced weak fan poset
\mathcal{P}|_{\pi_{\sigma}}
on
\pi_{\sigma}(\starr\sigma)
, which has a maximal and minimal element, and two disjoint chains similar to
Fig.

5(b)
. Hence the interval
[\sigma^{-},\sigma^{+}]
is a polygon of
\mathcal{P}
. Now, take a polygon
[\tau_{1}\land\tau_{2},\tau_{3}]\subseteq\mathcal{P}
for some
\tau_{1}\lessdot\tau_{3}
and
\tau_{2}\lessdot\tau_{3}
, then
\kappa=\tau_{1}\cap\tau_{3}\cap\tau_{2}
is a cone of codimension 2 since
\tau_{1}\cap\tau_{3}
and
\tau_{2}\cap\tau_{3}
are both generated by distinct subsets of
(n-1)
vectors generating
\tau_{3}
. By the previous,

is a polygon which must contain
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
and
\tau_{3}
. More precisely we must have
\kappa^{+}=\tau_{3}
and by the uniqueness of the meet in a lattice we have
\kappa^{-}=\tau_{1}\land\tau_{2}
, hence every polygon arises as a facial interval of a cone of codimension 2.

Additionally, if all maximal cones are identified, then there exists a unique 0-cell in
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}))
and

implies that the generators of
G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P},\mathcal{P})
and
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}))
coincide. The relations of the fundamental group of a CW-complex are given exactly by the 2-cells which correspond with the cones of codimension 2.
∎

The previous result implies that different choices of non-degenerate fan posets define isomorphic picture groups when all maximal cones are identified by
\mathfrak{P}
and the fan poset is a polygonal lattice. A similar result holds for any fan in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite and complete fan in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
(\Sigma,\mathcal{P}_{1})
and

be non-degenerate fan posets. Then

.

Proof.

4.11
and non-degeneracy imply that the type 2 relations are satisfied by both

and
G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P},\mathcal{P}_{2})
. Denote the generators of
G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P},\mathcal{P}_{1})
by
X_{[\sigma_{i}]}
and the generators of

by

for

. Given an arbitrary cover relation
\tau_{i}\lessdot\tau_{j}
in
\mathcal{P}_{1}
with

Then the desired group isomorphism

Proposition 4.18

[

4.4.
Hyperplane arrangements

We briefly recall the theory of hyperplane arrangements, which are collections of subspaces of codimension 1. A
central simplicial hyperplane arrangement

\mathcal{H}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defines a simplicial fan
\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}
and dissects the space into regions. These closures of these regions are maximal cones of the fan and two regions are called
adjacent
whenever their closures intersect in a cone of codimension 1. Choosing any such region
B
as the base region and orienting the adjacency graph away from
B
defines a poset, called the
poset of regions

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H},B)
by
[
25
]
. For any choice of base region
B
, this defines a fan poset, see
[
50
, Sec. 4]
.

A
flat
of a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an intersection of hyperplanes of
\mathcal{H}
, and thus a linear subspace of the ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular, the empty intersection gives the ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as a flat. The
support
of a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}
is the smallest flat
s(\sigma)
which contains
\sigma
. This leads to the
flat-partition

\mathfrak{P}_{\flatt}
of the simplicial fan
\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}
given by
[\sigma_{1}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{\flatt}}=[\sigma_{2}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{\flatt}}
if and only if
s(\sigma_{1})=s(\sigma_{2})
. In other words, we identify cones whose support is the same flat.

Proposition 4.24
.

The partition
\mathfrak{P}_{\flatt}
is an admissible partition of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}
and thus the category of the flat-partition
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}},\mathfrak{P}_{\flatt})
is a well-defined category.

Proof.

Let
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}
be two cones such that the flat
X\coloneqq s(\sigma_{1})=s(\sigma_{2})
is the same intersection of hyperplanes. It follows immediately that

\spann\{\sigma_{1}\}=X=\spann\{\sigma_{2}\}.

It follows from
[
2
, Lem. 1.36]
that both
\starr(\sigma_{1})
and
\starr(\sigma_{2})
are “equivalent” to the
arrangement over the flat
X
whose
essentialisation
is precisely the projection onto the orthogonal complement, see
[
2
]
for more details. The partition is admissible by
3.3
since it identifies the whole set of possible identifications.
∎

Shards of hyperplane arrangements were introduced in

]

is a “piece of a hyperplane”. They are obtained as follows: Let

be a choice of base region and call the

hyperplanes defining it

. Each pair of hyperplanes

defines a

with an induced base region. We say that
H_{1}

H_{2}
if
H_{1}
is basic in
\mathcal{H}(H_{1},H_{2})
and
H_{2}

Lemma 4.29

5.
Lattice of admissible partitions

It is well-known that the collection of partitions of a set, ordered by refinement, forms a complete lattice, see
[
29
, Sec. IV.4]
. We show that the restriction to admissible partitions of a fan preserves the lattice structure, thus establishing a lattice of categories (one for each admissible partition) of a fan. In the special case where the underlying fan is the
g
-fan of a finite-dimensional algebra, see
Section

6
, the
\tau
-cluster morphism category is an element of this lattice. Similarly if the underlying fan is a hyperplane arrangement, the category of the flat-partition and the category of the shard-partition lie in this lattice. We begin by introducing some basic notions. It is a well-known that an equivalence relation induces a partition on a set
X
and vice versa. Let us define the following poset relation on partitions.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be partitions of
X
. We say that
P_{1}
is a
finer
partition than
P_{2}
if

x\sim_{P_{1}}y\Longrightarrow x\sim_{P_{2}}y,\quad\text{or equivalently,}\quad\{x,y\}\subseteq a\in P_{1}\Longrightarrow\{x,y\}\subseteq b\in P_{2}

for
x,y\in X
and some
a\in P_{1}
and
b\in P_{2}
. In this case, we write
P_{1}\leq P_{2}
and say
P_{2}
is
coarser
than
P_{1}
.

Denote by
\Part(X)
the set of all partitions of a set
X
. In view of
3.4
, denote by
\APart(\Sigma)
the set of all admissible partitions of a fan
\Sigma
. The definitions of join and the meet do not depend on the restriction to admissible partitions and are the operations defined for a general
partition lattice
. Given a subset
\mathcal{S}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}\subseteq\Part(X)
, define the meet
\bigwedge\mathcal{S}
to be the partition satisfying

x\sim_{\bigwedge\mathcal{S}}y\quad\text{if and only if}\quad x\sim_{P_{i}}y\text{ for all $i=1,\dots,k$}.

Define the join
\bigvee\mathcal{S}
to be the partitioned satisfying
x\sim_{\bigvee\mathcal{S}}y
if and only if there exists a natural number
n
, indices
i_{0},\dots,i_{n-1}\in\{1,\dots,k\}
and
x_{0},\dots,x_{n+1}\in X
such that
x=x_{0}
,
y=x_{n+1}
and
x_{j}\sim_{P_{i_{j}}}x_{j+1}
for
0\leq j\leq n
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The partially ordered set
\APart(\Sigma)
forms a complete lattice.

Proof.

Since
\Part(\Sigma)
is a complete lattice, we need to show that
\APart(\Sigma)
is closed under the lattice operations. Recall from
Section

3
that we need to show the join and meet partitions are
possible
and
admissible
. Let us start with the meet. Take a subset
\mathcal{S}=\{\mathfrak{P}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{P}_{k}\}\subseteq\APart(\Sigma)
of admissible partitions and take a block
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{\ell}\in\bigwedge\mathcal{S}
. If
|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{\ell}|=1
, then there is nothing to show, so assume
|\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{\ell}|>1
and take
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{\ell}
. By definition
\sigma_{1}\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{i}}\sigma_{2}
for all
i=1,\dots,k
, so the partition
\bigwedge\mathcal{S}
consists of possible identification. Similarly, whenever
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2})
, then
\tau_{1}\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{i}}\tau_{2}

i=1,\dots,k
and thus
\tau_{1}\sim_{\bigwedge\mathcal{S}}\tau_{2}
, hence
\bigwedge\mathcal{S}
is an admissible partition.

For the join, take
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{\ell}\in\bigvee\mathcal{S}
and
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{\ell}
, then if both
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are contained in one block
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{h}\in\mathfrak{P}_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
the result is immediate. Therefore assume that
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are not contained in the same block in any
\mathfrak{P}_{i}
. However, by the construction of the join, there exists a sequence

\sigma_{1}\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{i_{1}}}\sigma_{i_{1}}\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{i_{2}}}\dots\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{i_{r-1}}}\sigma_{i_{r-1}}\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{i_{r}}}\sigma_{2}

for some
r\geq 1
, such that each term is contained in
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{1}}=\mathcal{E}_{\sigma_{2}}
, hence
\bigvee\mathcal{S}
consists of possible identifications. This sequence of possible identifications implies that we have

\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\starr(\sigma_{1}))=\pi_{\sigma_{i_{j}}}(\starr(\sigma_{i_{j}}))=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\starr(\sigma_{2})),

for
j=1,\dots,r-1
. To show that
\bigvee\mathcal{S}
is admissible, assume we have
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2})
for some
\tau_{1}\in\starr(\sigma_{1})
and
\tau_{2}\in\starr(\sigma_{2})
, then from the above we must have a sequence of
\tau_{i_{j}}\in\starr(\sigma_{i_{j}})
for
j=1,\dots,r-1
such that

\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{i_{j}}}(\tau_{i_{j}})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2}).

Since each
\mathfrak{P}_{i_{j}}
is admissible for
j=1,\dots,r
we obtain a sequence
\tau_{1}\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{i_{1}}}\tau_{i_{1}}\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{i_{2}}}\dots\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{i_{r-1}}}\tau_{i_{r-1}}\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{i_{r}}}\tau_{2}
as required. Hence
\bigvee\mathcal{S}
is an admissible partition.
∎

It is easily seen that this is a bounded lattice, that is, there exists a maximal and a minimal element. The minimal element is the partition
\mathfrak{P}_{\text{poset}}
mentioned earlier, which is just the finest partition with trivial equivalence classes. The maximal element is the coarsest partition, whose equivalence classes are exactly the
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
of
3.4
. When the fan is a hyperplane arrangement the coarsest partition is exactly the flat-partition, see
4.24
, whereas the shard-partition, see
4.26
, sits somewhere in the middle. We conclude this section by describing the relationship between comparable partitions.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
be a fan poset and
\mathfrak{P}_{1},\mathfrak{P}_{2}
be two admissible partitions of
\Sigma
such that
\mathfrak{P}_{1}
is finer than
\mathfrak{P}_{2}
. Then the following hold:

(1)

There exists a faithful surjective-on-objects functor
F:\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1})\to\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{2})
.

(2)

The classifying spaces satisfy
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{2})\equiv\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1})/\sim
, where
\sim
identifies the cells
e([\sigma_{1}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}})
and
e([\sigma_{2}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}})
whenever
[\sigma_{1}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}}=[\sigma_{2}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}}
.

(3)

If
(\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
is non-degenerate, then the picture groups satisfy
G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{2},\mathcal{P})\cong G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1},\mathcal{P})/I
, where
I
is generated by the relations
X_{[\sigma_{1}]}=X_{[\sigma_{2}]}
whenever
[\sigma_{1}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}\neq[\sigma_{2}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}
but
[\sigma_{1}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}}=[\sigma_{2}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}}
.

Proof.

(1)

The functor
F:\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1})\to\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{2})
is given on objects by the map sending
[\sigma]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}\mapsto[\sigma]_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}}
for all cones
\sigma\in\Sigma
and on morphisms by sending
[f_{\sigma\tau}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}\mapsto[f_{\sigma\tau}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}}
for all
\sigma,\tau\in\Sigma
. Objects of
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{i})
are exactly the blocks of
\mathfrak{P}_{i}
for both
i=1,2
. By definition,
\mathfrak{P}_{2}
being coarser than
\mathfrak{P}_{1}
means
[\sigma]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}\subseteq[\sigma]_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}}
. Hence
\mathfrak{P}_{2}
has at most as many blocks as
\mathfrak{P}_{1}
. Thus there is a surjection on objects of the categories.

To see that the functor is faithful, one observes that the identification of morphisms in the construction of the category depends not on a choice but is induced when certain cones are identified. Take for example two different morphisms
[f_{\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}\neq[f_{\sigma_{2}\tau_{2}}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}\in\Hom_{\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1})}([\sigma]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}},[\tau]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}})
satisfying
\sigma_{1}\neq\sigma_{2}
or
\tau_{1}\neq\tau_{2}
but
\sigma_{1}\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}\sigma_{2}
and
\tau_{1}\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}\tau_{2}
. Assume for a contradiction that
[f_{\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}}=[f_{\sigma_{2}\tau_{2}}]_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}}
then the cones must satisfy
\pi_{\sigma_{1}}(\tau_{1})=\pi_{\sigma_{2}}(\tau_{2})
by definition. However, then they should have also been identified in
\mathfrak{P}_{1}
, a contradiction. Hence the induced map

F_{[\sigma],[\tau]}:\Hom_{\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1})}([\sigma]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}},[\tau]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}})\to\Hom_{\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{2})}([\sigma]_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}},[\tau]_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}})

is injective and hence the functor faithful.

(2)

This follows from the description of the CW-complex in
4.4
whose cells are the union of factorisation cubes, and the fact that the factorisation cubes of two identified morphisms get identified, by
3.12
.

(3)

Let
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3}\in\Sigma
. The quotient group homomorphism
H:G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1},\mathcal{P})\to G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1},\mathcal{P})/I
“identifies”
X_{[\kappa_{1}]},X_{[\kappa_{2}]}\in G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1},\mathcal{P})
whenever
\kappa_{1}\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}}\kappa_{2}
but
\kappa_{1}\not\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}\kappa_{2}
. More precisely, we have that
X_{[\kappa_{1}]}+I=X_{[\kappa_{2}]}+I\in G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1},\mathcal{P})/I
. Furthermore if
\kappa_{3}\sim_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}\kappa_{1}
, then also
X_{[\kappa_{3}]}+I=X_{[\kappa_{2}]}+I\in G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1},\mathcal{P})/I
. It is easy to see that we have
X_{[\kappa_{i}]}+I=X_{[\kappa_{j}]}+I\in G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1},\mathcal{P})/I
if and only if
X_{[\kappa_{i}]}=X_{[\kappa_{j}]}\in G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{2},\mathcal{P})
for any
\kappa_{i},\kappa_{j}\in\Sigma
. The first kind of relations in
4.10
hold since they only depend on the shared fan poset
(\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
and the second kind relations trivially hold in
G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1},\mathcal{P})
, thus its quotient and
G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{2},\mathcal{P})
by
4.11
since the fan poset is non-degenerate.

∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
(\Sigma,\mathcal{P})
be a fan poset and
\mathfrak{P}_{1}\leq\mathfrak{P}_{2}
be two admissible partitions of
\Sigma
. If there exists a faithful group functor
H:\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{2})\to G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{2},\mathcal{P})
, then there exists a faithful group functor
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1})\to G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{2},\mathcal{P})
.

Proof.

The functor is given by the composition
H\circ F
, where
F:\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1})\to\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{2})
is the faithful functor of
5.3
(1). In particular, let
[\sigma]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}},[\tau]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}\in\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1})
, then

\Hom_{\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{1})}([\sigma]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}},[\tau]_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}})\xhookrightarrow{F}\Hom_{\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{2})}([\sigma]_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}},[\tau]_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}})\xhookrightarrow{H}\Hom_{G(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{2})}(\bullet,\bullet)

is a composition of injective morphisms between
\Hom
-sets and thus injective. Hence
G\circ F
is faithful.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

In the setting of
4.28
, the category
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}},\mathfrak{P})
admits a faithful functor to the group
G(\Sigma_{\mathcal{H}},\mathfrak{P}_{\flatt},\mathcal{P})
for all admissible partitions
\mathfrak{P}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{P}_{\flatt}
is the maximal partition any other partition
\mathfrak{P}
is finer, i.e.
\mathfrak{P}\leq\mathfrak{P}_{\flatt}
. The result then follows from
5.4
and
4.28
.
∎

6.
Applications to the
\tau
-cluster morphism category

In the representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras, the Auslander-Reiten translation
\tau
defines an important relationship within the category of (finitely generated) modules of an algebra. Adachi, Iyama and Reiten
[
1
]
introduced
\tau
-tilting theory as a completion of classical tilting theory from the viewpoint of mutations. We refer to
[
6
]
for a general introduction to the representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras and to
[
57
]
for a survey of
\tau
-tilting theory. While the first sections were purely geometric, we now reintroduce the algebraic tools of
\tau
-tilting theory to study the
\tau
-cluster morphism category of a finite-dimensional algebra as a special case of the category of a partitioned fan.

We begin by giving the necessary background on
\tau
-tilting theory and provide a new algebraic proof of the connection between a finite-dimensional algebra and its
g
-fan. Then we show that there is a natural fan poset on the
g
-fan induced by the torsion classes and that our definition of the picture group generalises that of
[
31
]
and
[
38
]
. We conclude by giving an application of the previous sections and prove that
\tau
-cluster morphism category
\mathfrak{C}(A)
admits a faithful functor to some group whenever its
g
-fan
\Sigma(A)
is a hyperplane arrangement.

6.1.
\tau
-tilting theory

Let
\mods A
be the category of finitely generated right
A
-modules of a finite-dimensional basic algebra over an arbitrary field
K
and
\proj A
be the full subcategory of projective modules. Write
\bdim M
for the dimension vector of a module
M\in\mods A
, defined such that
(\bdim M)_{i}\coloneqq\dim_{\End_{A}(S(i))}(Me_{i})
, where
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a set of primitive orthogonal idempotents. Let
|M|
be the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable direct summands of
M
. Assume that
|A|=n
so that we may write
A=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}P(i)
, where
P(i)\in\proj A
are the projective covers of the simple modules. The focal objects of
\tau
-tilting theory are the
\tau
-rigid modules.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
M\in\mods A
and
P\in\proj A
, then

(1)

M
is
\tau
-rigid
if
\Hom_{A}(M,\tau M)=0
and
\tau
-tilting
if additionally
|M|=n
.

(2)

(M,P)
is a
(support)
\tau
-rigid pair
if
M
is
\tau
-rigid and
\Hom_{A}(P,T)=0
, and a
(support)
\tau
-tilting pair
if additionally
|M|+|P|=n
.

(3)

M
is
\tau^{-1}
-rigid
if
\Hom_{A}(\tau^{-1}M,M)=0
.

Denote by
\strigid A
(resp.
\sttilt A
) the
\tau
-rigid (resp.
\tau
-tilting) pairs of
A
. For a module
M\in\mods A
, denote by
\add M
(resp.
\Fac M
,
\Sub M
) the full subcategory of
\mods A
consisting of all direct summands (resp. factor modules, submodules) of finite direct sums of copies of
M
. Furthermore, define

M^{\perp}\coloneqq\{N\in\mods A:\Hom_{A}(M,N)=0\},\quad{}^{\perp}M\coloneqq\{N\in\mods A:\Hom_{A}(N,M)=0\}.

Proposition 6.2
.

[
9
, Prop. 5.6 and 5.8]

Let
M,N\in\mods A
, then

(1)

\Hom_{A}(M,\tau N)=0
if and only if
\Ext_{A}^{1}(N,\Fac M)=0
, and

(2)

\Hom_{A}(\tau^{-1}M,N)=0
if and only if
\Ext_{A}^{1}(\Sub N,M)=0
.

To every module, we associate the following integer vector.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
M\in\mods A
. Let
P_{-1}\to P_{0}\to T\to 0
be a minimal projective presentation, where
P_{0}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}P(i)^{a_{i}}
and
P_{-1}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}P(i)^{b_{i}}
. Define the
g
-vector
of
M
as

g^{M}\coloneqq(a_{1}-b_{1},a_{2}-b_{2},\dots,a_{n}-b_{n}).

Furthermore, define the
g
-vector of a
\tau
-rigid pair
(M,P)
as
g^{(M,P)}=g^{M}-g^{P}
. In this way we associate a polyhedral cone
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M,P)}\coloneqq\cone\{g^{M_{1}},\dots,g^{M_{k}},-g^{P_{k+1}},\dots,-g^{P_{t}}\}
to each
\tau
-rigid pair
(M,P)
, where
\{M_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
and
\{P_{j}\}_{j=k+1}^{t}
are the indecomposable direct summands of
M
and
P
respectively.

The
g
-vectors of
\tau
-rigid pairs of an algebra are important examples of
stability conditions
in the sense of King
[
42
]
, who translated the Geometric Invariant Theory of Mumford
[
47
]
to quiver representations. These stability conditions define a
stability scattering diagram

[
15
]
whose support is the
wall-and-chamber structure
of the algebra
[
17
]
. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the standard inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 6.4
.

[
42
]
Let
M\in\mods A
, take

. We say
M
is
v
-semistable
if
\langle v,\bdim M\rangle=0
and if for any nonzero proper subobject
L
of
M
we have
\langle v,\bdim L\rangle\leq 0
. We say
M
is
v
-stable
if these inequalities are strict.

Definition 6.5
.

Fix a nonzero module
M\in\mods A
, then the
stability space

\mathcal{D}(M)
of
M
is

\mathcal{D}(M)=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M\text{ is $v$-semistable}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We say
\mathcal{D}(M)
is a
wall
if
\codim\mathcal{D}(M)=1
. A
chamber
is an open connected component of

\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in\mods A\\
0\neq M\end{subarray}}\mathcal{D}(M)}.

The combination of all stability spaces
\mathcal{D}(M)
for nonzero modules
M
and the corresponding chambers they define is called the wall-and-chamber structure. A module

is called a

if

is a division ring, denote the collection of bricks of an algebra by
\brick A
. By
[
5
, Prop. 2.7]
and
[

it suffices to consider stability spaces of bricks to obtain the wall-and-chamber structure. We refer to
[
41
]
for a survey on the connection between King’s stability conditions and
\tau
-tilting theory.

Closely related to
\tau
-rigid pairs are

[
23
]
. A torsion pair is a pair of full subcategories

of
\mods A
such that
\Hom_{A}(T,F)=0
for all

and

and such that for each

, there exists a unique short exact sequence with submodule in
\mathcal{T}
and quotient in
\mathcal{F}
. The subcategory
\mathcal{T}
is called the

and the corresponding

the

. The study of stability conditions tells us that we can associate two torsion pairs to every element of

Proposition 6.6
.

[
12
, Prop. 3.1]

Let

, denote the small and the large torsion class associated to

by

(1)

\mathcal{T}_{v}=\{0\}\cup\{Y\in\mods A:\langle v,\bdim Z\rangle>0\text{ for quotients }Y\to Z\to 0\}

(2)

\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{v}=\{Y\in\mods A:\langle v,\bdim Z\rangle\geq 0\text{ for quotients }Y\to Z\to 0\}
.

The corresponding torsion-free classes are given, respectively, by

(1)

\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{v}=\{Y\in\mods A:\langle v,\bdim X\rangle\leq 0\text{ for submodules }0\to X\to Y\}
, and

(2)

\mathcal{F}_{v}=\{0\}\cup\{Y\in\mods A:\langle v,\bdim X\rangle<0\text{ for submodules }0\to X\to Y\}

Asai defines an equivalence relation such that

are

whenever
\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{v_{1}}=\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{v_{2}}
and
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{v_{1}}=\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{v_{2}}
. In this case we write
\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{E}
and
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{E}
to mean the large torsion and torsion-free class of any vector in the TF-equivalence class
E
.

Theorem 6.7
.

[
17
, Prop. 3.15]

[
5
, Thm. 3.17]

Let
(M,P)
be a
\tau
-rigid pair then the geometric interior of the polyhedral cone
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(T,P)}
, denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{(M,P)}
, is a TF-equivalence class.

It is well-known that each
\tau
-rigid module

defines two torsion classes

[

in the interior of the corresponding cone of

.

Theorem 6.8
.

[

, Thm. 3.11]

Let

be a
\tau
-rigid pair and

be the Nakayama functor. Then

(1)

(\mathcal{T}_{v},\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{v})=(\Fac M,M^{\perp})
for all

(2)

(\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{v},\mathcal{F}_{v})=({}^{\perp}(\tau M)\cap P^{\perp},\Sub(\tau M\oplus\nu P))

We remark that the torsion pairs coincide when
(M,P)
is a
\tau
-tilting pair
[

, Prop. 2.16]
and conclude this preliminary section by giving another key result for our proof of the fan structure. Approximations were introduced in
[

Lemma 6.9
.

[
1
, Lem. 2.6]

Let
0\to Y\to M^{\prime}\xrightarrow{f}X
be an exact sequence in
\mods A
, where
M
is
\tau
-rigid and

is a right (
\add M
)-approximation. Then
Y\in{}^{\perp}(\tau M)
.

The following is a well-known construction of such an approximation.

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
M,N\in\mods A
and let
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{p}\}
be a collection of generators of
\Hom_{A}(M,N)
. Then
f:M^{p}\to N
given by
f\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}&\dots&t_{p}\end{pmatrix}^{T}=f_{1}(t_{1})+\dots+f_{p}(t_{p})
is a right
(\add M)
-approximation.

Proof.

Clearly
M^{p}\in\add M
. We need to show that
\Hom_{A}(M^{\prime},M^{p})\to\Hom_{A}(M^{\prime},N)
is a surjection for all
M^{\prime}\in\add M
. Since
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{p}\}
is a generating set for
\Hom_{A}(M,M^{\prime})
any map
g:M^{\prime}\to N
may be written as
g=f_{1}a_{1}+\dots+f_{p}a_{p}
for some
a_{1},\dots,a_{p}\in A
. Then we may construct a map
h:M^{\prime}\to M^{p}
given as
h(t^{\prime})=\begin{pmatrix}t^{\prime}a_{1}&\dots&t^{\prime}a_{p}\end{pmatrix}^{T}
which satisfies
g=f\circ h
. Hence
f
is a right
(\add M)
-approximation.
∎

6.2.
The fan structure of
g
-vectors

Define the
g
-fan
of an algebra
A
to be

\Sigma(A)\coloneqq\{\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M,P)}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}:(M,P)\in\strigid A\}.

Faces of cones are cones of direct summands. We need to prove that two cones intersect at a face. The following technical lemma will be the key to obtain the fan structure.

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
(M,P),(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})\in\strigid A
be basic and such that
\mathcal{C}_{(M,P)}\cap\mathcal{C}_{(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})}\neq\emptyset
. Then
M\cong M^{\prime}
and
P\cong P^{\prime}
.

Proof.

First we show that
M\cong M^{\prime}
. Take
v\in\mathcal{C}_{(M,P)}\cap\mathcal{C}_{(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})}
, then
6.8
implies the identifications

\Fac M=\mathcal{T}_{v}=\Fac M^{\prime},\quad\text{and}\quad{}^{\perp}(\tau M)\cap P^{\perp}=\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{v}={}^{\perp}(\tau M^{\prime})\cap(P^{\prime})^{\perp}.

Therefore
M,M^{\prime}\in\Fac M=\Fac M^{\prime}
. Construct a right
(\add M)
-approximation
f:M^{p}\to M^{\prime}
like in
6.10
, then
\ker f\in{}^{\perp}(\tau M)
by
6.9
. Moreover,
\ker f\in P^{\perp}
, since a nonzero morphism from
P
may be composed with the natural inclusion to give a nonzero morphism from
P
to
M
, contradicting the assumption that
(M,P)
is
\tau
-rigid. It follows that
\ker f\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{v}
and hence
\Hom_{A}(\ker f,\tau M^{\prime})=0
.
6.2
implies
\Ext_{A}^{1}(M^{\prime},\ker f)=0
and hence the right
(\add M)
-approximation splits. Thus
M^{p}\cong M^{\prime}\oplus N
for some
N\in\mods A
. Since
M^{\prime}
is a basic direct summand of
M^{p}
it has at most
|M|
indecomposable direct summands. Thus
|M|\geq|M^{\prime}|
and reversing the the argument, it follows that
|M|=|M^{\prime}|
. Then
M\cong M^{\prime}
, because a basic direct summand of
M^{p}
with exactly
|M|
indecomposable direct summands has to be isomorphic to
M
. The argument to show
P\cong P^{\prime}
is similar. The previous argument and
6.8
imply

M^{\perp}=\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{v}=(M^{\prime})^{\perp}\quad\text{and}\quad\Sub(\tau M\oplus\nu P)=\mathcal{F}_{v}=\Sub(\tau M\oplus\nu P^{\prime}).

We observe that the module
\tau^{-1}(\tau M\oplus\nu P)\cong\tau^{–1}\tau M
as
\tau
is additive and
\nu P
injective and moreover
\Sub(\tau M\oplus\nu P)\subseteq M^{\perp}
, therefore
\tau M\oplus\nu P
is
\tau^{–1}
-rigid. Construct a left
\add(\tau M\oplus\nu P)
-approximation
g
dually to
6.10
. Then the dual of
6.9
implies that
\coker g\in(\tau^{-1}(\tau M\oplus\nu P^{\prime}))^{\perp}
. Hence by
6.2
the approximation
g
splits. It follows that
\tau M\oplus\nu P^{\prime}
is isomorphic to a basic direct summand of
(\tau M\oplus\nu P)^{r}
. Moreover,
\nu P^{\prime}
cannot be isomorphic to a a direct summand of
(\tau T)^{r}
and therefore
\nu P^{\prime}\cong(\nu P)^{r}
and thus
P\cong P^{\prime}
.
∎

We make the following immediate observation about cones of maximal basic direct summands.

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
(M,P)
be a
\tau
-rigid pair, and
(N,Q)
be a basic
\tau
-rigid pair such that
\add N=\add M
and
\add Q=\add P
. Then
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M,P)}=\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(N,Q)}
and
\mathcal{C}_{(M,P)}=\mathcal{C}_{(N,Q)}
.

We are now able to give a new proof of
[
21
, Thm. 1.9]
, establishing the fan structure of cones of
g
-vectors.

Theorem 6.13
.

Let
(M,P)
and
(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})
be
\tau
-rigid pairs such that
(N,Q)
is their maximal common direct summand, then
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M,P)}\cap\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})}=\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(N,Q)}
.

Proof.

By
6.12
it suffices to restrict to basic
\tau
-rigid pairs. Trivially,
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(N,Q)}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M,P)}\cap\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})}
. Conversely, if
(M,P)
is different from
(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})
then
6.11
implies that the interiors of the cones do not intersect. Hence consider an intersection at the boundary, which given by

\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:v=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}g^{M_{i}}-\sum_{j=k+1}^{t}\alpha_{j}g^{P_{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}g^{M_{i}^{\prime}}-\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{s}\beta_{j}g^{P_{j}^{\prime}}\right\},

where
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\geq 0
but not all strictly positive. Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{v}\coloneqq\{i:\alpha_{i}=0\}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{v}\coloneqq\{i:\beta_{i}=0\}
be the indices for which the coefficients are zero. Consider the direct summands of
(M,P)
and
(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})
given only by those direct summands in the intersection

\left(\bigoplus_{i\not\in\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{v}}M_{i},\bigoplus_{j\not\in\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{v}}P_{j}\right),\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\bigoplus_{i\not\in\boldsymbol{\beta}_{v}}M_{i}^{\prime},\bigoplus_{j\not\in\boldsymbol{\beta}_{v}}P_{j}^{\prime}\right).

By construction, the interior cones of these two
\tau
-rigid pairs intersects non-trivially at
v
. Then
6.11
implies that they are isomorphic and a thus a direct summand of
(N,Q)
. It follows that
v\in\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M,P)}\cap\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(N,Q)}
.
∎

The
g
-vectors spanning a
g
-vector cone
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M,P}
are linearly independent by
[
1
, Thm. 5.1]
. Therefore, the faces of
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M,P)}
are given by
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(N,Q)}
where
N\in\add M
and
Q\in\add P
. It is clear that if
(M,P)
is a
\tau
-rigid pair then
(N,Q)
is
\tau
-rigid pair, hence
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(N,Q)}
is also a cone of
\Sigma(A)
. Together with
6.13
this proves that
\Sigma(A)
is a polyhedral fan. For a summary of known properties of the
g
-fan we refer to
[
3
]
. We want to highlight the following result.

Theorem 6.14
.

[
5
, Thm. 4.7]
[
21
, Thm. 5.4]

The algebra
A
has finitely many
\tau
-tilting modules if and only if the
g
-fan
\Sigma(A)
is finite and complete. In this case, we call
A

\tau
-tilting finite.

In this case, there is a bijection between maximal cones
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(T,P)}\in\Sigma(A)
and torsion classes
\Fac T\in\tors A
by
[
1
, Thm. 2.7]
. In this way the inclusion of torsion classes induces a partial order on maximal cones. So, for a maximal cone
\sigma\in\Sigma(A)
, we write
\mathcal{T}_{\sigma}
to mean
\mathcal{T}_{v}
where
v
lies in the interior of
\sigma
. This defines a partial order
(\Sigma(A),\leq)=(\Sigma(A),\leq_{\tors A})
on maximal cones satisfying
\sigma_{1}\leq\sigma_{2}
if and only if
\mathcal{T}_{\sigma_{1}}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\sigma_{2}}
.

Proposition 6.15
.

Let
A
be a
\tau
-tilting finite algebra, then the poset of torsion classes
\tors A
induces a fan poset
(\Sigma,\leq_{\tors A})
on
\Sigma(A)
.

Proof.

By definition, every cone
\sigma\in\Sigma(A)
is the
g
-vector cone of a
\tau
-rigid pair
(M,P)
. Then the maximal cones containing it are exactly those corresponding to the interval
[\Fac M,{}^{\perp}\tau M\cap P^{\perp}]\subseteq\tors A
. This is because maximal
g
-vector cones containing
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M,P)}
are exactly those
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(N,Q)}\in\Sigma(A)
such that
(N,Q)
is a
\tau
-tilting pair with
M\in\add N
and
P\in\add Q
. By
[
1
, Thm. 2.10]
, see also
[
22
, Thm. 4.4]
and the bijection of
\tau
-tilting pairs with torsion classes
[
1
, Thm. 2.7]
when
A
is
\tau
-tilting finite we get the desired interval.

We are left with showing that each interval is a cone. For a torsion class
\mathcal{T}\in\tors A
and a torsion-free class
\mathcal{F}\in\torf A
, respectively, define the following subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}}^{+}\coloneqq\bigcap_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle v,\bdim T\rangle\geq 0\},\quad\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{-}\coloneqq\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle v,\bdim F\rangle\leq 0\}.

Both spaces are intersection of half-spaces and thus a convex cone. Take an interval
I=[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}]\in(\Sigma(A),\tors A)
corresponding with torsion classes
[\mathcal{T}_{\sigma_{1}},\mathcal{T}_{\sigma_{2}}]\subseteq\tors A
, define
\mathcal{T}_{i}\coloneqq\mathcal{T}_{\sigma_{i}}
. To prove that
(\Sigma(A),\leq_{\tors A})
is a fan poset we need to show

\bigcup_{\sigma_{i}\in I}\sigma_{i}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}^{+}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}_{2}^{\perp}}^{-}.

Take
v\in\bigcup_{\sigma_{i}\in I}\sigma_{i}
. From
[
17
, Lem. 3.12]
it follows that
\langle v,\bdim T\rangle\geq 0
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\sigma_{1}}
since
\mathcal{T}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{i}
for all
\sigma_{i}\in I
. Therefore
v\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}^{+}
. Dually, it follows for the torsion-free class
(\mathcal{T}_{2})^{\perp}
that
\langle v,\bdim F\rangle\leq 0
for all
F\in(\mathcal{T}_{2})^{\perp}
. Hence
\bigcup_{\sigma_{i}\in I}\sigma_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}^{+}\cap\mathcal{H}_{(\mathcal{T}_{2})^{\perp}}^{-}
.

Conversely, assume for a contradiction that there exists
v\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}^{+}\cap\mathcal{H}_{(\mathcal{T}_{2})^{\perp}}^{-}
such that there is no
\sigma_{i}\in I
satisfying
v\in\sigma_{i}
. By completeness of the
g
-fan, there exists some other maximal cone
\sigma^{\prime}
with
\sigma^{\prime}\not\in I
containing
v
in its interior. This interior has to be fully contained in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}^{+}\cap\mathcal{H}_{(\mathcal{T}_{2})^{\perp}}^{-}
. By construction of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}^{+}
,
\mathcal{T}_{w}
contains
\mathcal{T}_{1}
. Therefore
\mathcal{T}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\sigma^{\prime}}
. Dually, it follows that
(\mathcal{T}_{\sigma^{\prime}})^{\perp}\subseteq(\mathcal{T}_{2})^{\perp}
and hence
\sigma^{\prime}\in I
.
∎

Therefore any properties of the poset
\tors A
apply to the fan poset
(\Sigma(A),\leq_{\tors A})
, in particular it is a polygonal completely semi-distributive lattice
[
22
]
. We conclude the study of the fan structure by showing that
g
-vectors determine
\tau
-rigid pairs, giving a new proof of
[
21
, Thm. 6.5]
.

Theorem 6.16
.

Let
(M,P)
and
(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})
be
\tau
-rigid pairs such that
g\coloneqq g^{(M,P)}=g^{(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})}
. Then
M\cong M^{\prime}
and
P\cong P^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We repeatedly reduce the problem to the largest basic direct summands and show that they coincide. Write
M\cong B_{1}^{M}\oplus M_{1}
, where
B_{1}^{M}
is a basic direct summand of
M
such that
\add B_{1}^{M}=\add M
. Similarly, write
M^{\prime}\cong B_{1}^{M^{\prime}}\oplus M_{1}^{\prime}
, such that
B_{1}^{M^{\prime}}
is basic and
\add B_{1}^{M^{\prime}}=\add M^{\prime}
. In the same way, write
P\cong Q_{1}^{P}\oplus P_{1}
and
P^{\prime}\cong Q_{1}^{P^{\prime}}\oplus P_{1}^{\prime}
with
Q_{1}^{P}
and
Q_{1}^{P^{\prime}}
largest basic direct summands.
6.12
implies
\mathcal{C}_{(B_{1}^{M},Q_{1}^{P})}=\mathcal{C}_{(M,P)}
and
\mathcal{C}_{(B_{1}^{M^{\prime}},Q_{1}^{P^{\prime}})}=\mathcal{C}_{(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})}
. Since
g
is contained in the intersection of these cones,
6.11
implies that
B_{1}^{M}\cong B_{1}^{M^{\prime}}
and
Q_{1}^{P}\cong Q_{1}^{P^{\prime}}
. We repeat the process with
g_{1}\coloneqq g-g^{(B_{1}^{M},Q_{1}^{P})}
and the
\tau
-rigid pairs
(M_{1},P_{1})
and
(M_{1}^{\prime},P_{1}^{\prime})
. Since the modules are finitely generated, eventually
M\cong M^{\prime}
and
P\cong P^{\prime}
.
∎

6.3.
\tau
-cluster morphism category

The definition of the category of a partitioned fan,
3.4
, is inspired by the geometric construction of the
\tau
-cluster morphism category from the
g
-fan
[
55
]
. In the original constructions (cf.
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
35
]
) the objects of the category are
wide subcategories
of the form
\mathcal{W}_{E}\coloneqq\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{E}\cap\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{E}
for some TF-equivalence class
E=\mathcal{C}_{(M,P)}
coming from a
\tau
-rigid pair
(M,P)
. The relationship between TF-equivalence classes and the
g
-fan is described in
6.7
. Let
\TF_{A}
be the poset category of TF-equivalence classes coming from
\tau
-rigid pairs with poset relation
E_{1}\leq E_{2}
whenever
E_{1}\subseteq\overline{E_{2}}
.

Definition 6.17
.

[
55
, Defn. 3.3]

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional algebra. The
\tau
-cluster morphism category

\mathfrak{C}(A)
is the category

•

whose objects are objects of
\TF_{A}
under the identification
E_{1}\sim E_{2}
whenever
\mathcal{W}_{E_{1}}=\mathcal{W}_{E_{2}}
;

•

whose morphisms
\Hom_{\mathfrak{C}(A)}([E],[F])
are given by the set of morphisms

\bigcup_{E^{\prime}\in[E],F^{\prime}\in[F]}\Hom_{\TF_{A}}(E^{\prime},F^{\prime})

under the identification
f_{EF}\sim f_{E^{\prime}F^{\prime}}
whenever
\pi_{E}(F)=\pi_{E^{\prime}}(F^{\prime})
.

Translating from TF-equivalence classes to cones
\mathcal{C}_{(M,P)}
, we see that the objects of the
\tau
-cluster morphism category are interior cones of cones in
\Sigma(A)
with identification given by
\mathcal{C}_{(M_{1},P_{1})}\sim\mathcal{C}_{(M_{2},P_{2})}
whenever
M_{1}^{\perp}\cap{}^{\perp}\tau M_{1}\cap P_{1}^{\perp}=M_{2}^{\perp}\cap{}^{\perp}\tau M_{2}\cap P_{2}^{\perp}
. By taking the closure, this induces a partition of the fan
\Sigma(A)
, which we denote by
\mathfrak{P}_{\textnormal{WAC}}
. By
[
55
, Cor. 3.7, Lem. 3.8]
,
\mathfrak{P}_{\textnormal{WAC}}
is an admissible partition, so that the
\tau
-cluster morphism category
\mathfrak{C}(A)
is easily seen to be equivalent to the category of the partition fan
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma(A),\mathfrak{P}_{\textnormal{WAC}})
. It follows from the definition of
\tau
-rigid pairs and their correspondence with the cones of
\Sigma(A)
that the first factors of
\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma(A),\mathfrak{P}_{\textnormal{WAC}})
are given by pairwise compatibility conditions, see
[
31
,
35
,
37
]
.

Following
[
4
,
10
,
22
]
, we label the edges of
\Hasse(\tors A)
in the following way: Let
\mathcal{T}_{1}\lessdot\mathcal{T}_{2}
be a cover relation in
\tors A
, then
\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{T}_{2}=\Filt\{B\}
is the category of modules filtered by a brick
B
by
[
22
, Thm. 3.3]
. We call the labelling of arrows
\mathcal{T}_{1}\xrightarrow{B}\mathcal{T}_{2}
in
\Hasse(\tors A)
in this way the
brick-labelling
. Originally, the picture group of an algebra was defined in
[
38
]
for representation-finite hereditary algebras, and has since been extended to all
\tau
-tilting finite algebras in
[
31
]
as follows.

Definition 6.18
.

[
31
, Prop. 4.4a]

Let
A
be
\tau
-tilting finite. The
picture group

G(A)
is generated by
\{X_{S}:S\in\brick A\}
with a relation

X_{S_{1}}\dots X_{S_{k}}=X_{S_{1}^{\prime}}\dots X_{S_{l}^{\prime}}

if there exist torsion classes
\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\in\tors A
such that
(S_{1},\dots,S_{k})
and
(S_{1}^{\prime},\dots,S_{l}^{\prime})
are sequences of bricks labelling directed paths
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
in
\Hasse(\tors A)
, denote this element by
X_{[\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}^{\prime}]}
.

Proposition 6.19
.

Let
A
be
\tau
-tilting finite. Let
\mathfrak{C}(A)=\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{\textnormal{WAC}})
, then
G(\Sigma(A),\mathfrak{P}_{\textnormal{WAC}},\leq_{\tors A})
is isomorphic to
G(A)
, where
(\Sigma,\leq_{\tors A})
is the fan poset induced by
\tors A
.

Proof.

First we show that equivalence classes of
\mathfrak{P}_{\textnormal{WAC}}
are determined by bricks. Let
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M,P)}\in\Sigma(A)
be of codimension 1, then by
[
17
, Prop. 3.17]

\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M,P)}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(N)
, where
N
is
v
-stable for all
v\in\mathcal{C}_{(M,P)}
and a brick by
[
53
, Thm. 1]
. Take now a distinct cone
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})}\sim\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M,P)}\in\mathfrak{C}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{P}_{\textnormal{WAC}})
. Then
[
17
, Prop. 3.13, Thm. 3.14]
implies that
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(N)
and that this brick is unique in
M^{\perp}\cap{}^{\perp}\tau M\cap P^{\perp}=(M^{\prime})^{\perp}\cap{}^{\perp}\tau M^{\prime}\cap(P^{\prime})^{\perp}
. Hence the generators are in bijection. It follows from
[
17
, Prop. 4.5, Rmk. 4.6]
and the bijection between
\tau
-tilting pairs and torsion classes
[
1
, Thm. 2.7]
in the
\tau
-tilting finite case that the set of relations of
G(A)
coincide under the bijection of generators with the set of type 1 relations for
G(\Sigma(A),\mathfrak{P}_{\textnormal{WAC}},\leq_{\tors A})
. That
G(A)
satisfies the type 2 relations of
G(\Sigma(A),\mathfrak{P}_{\textnormal{WAC}},\leq_{\tors A})
follows from
[
22
, Thm. 4.12, Prop. 4.13]
which establishes a label-preserving isomorphism between the dual graphs of the stars of cones whose corresponding wide subcategories coincide and that of their shared projection.
∎

Therefore
4.10
is a generalisation of
6.18
. We also remark that by
[
31
, Thm. 4.10]
, the picture group
G(A)
is isomorphic to the fundamental group of
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{C}(A)
. We conclude by applying the theory developed in the previous sections to obtain a large class of algebras admitting a faithful functor.

Theorem 6.20
.

Let
A
be such that
\Sigma(A)
is a finite hyperplane arrangement. Then
\mathfrak{C}(A)
admits a faithful functor to
G(\Sigma(A),\mathfrak{P}_{\flatt},\leq_{\tors_{A}})
, and hence to
G(A)
.

Proof.

For any finite-dimensional algebra, cones of
\Sigma(A)
are contained in stability spaces
\mathcal{D}(B)
for some brick
B\in\brick A
. In particular,
\mathcal{D}(B)
is orthogonal to the dimension vector
\bdim B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, hence
\Sigma(A)
satisfies the assumptions of
4.28
and since the
\tau
-cluster morphism category
\mathfrak{C}(A)
corresponds to some admissible partition,
5.5
gives the desired result. Since
G(A)
is the fundamental group of
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{C}(A)
by
[
31
, Thm. 4.10]
, the faithful functor to
G(\Sigma(A),\mathfrak{P}_{\flatt},\leq_{\tors A})
factors through a faithful functor to
G(A)
, see the proof of
[
33
, Prop. 3.7]
.
∎

Corollary 6.21
.

Let
A
be such that
\Sigma(A)
is a finite hyperplane arrangement. Then the classifying space
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{C}(A)
is a
K(\pi,1)
space if
\mathfrak{C}(A)
satisfies the pairwise compatibility condition of last factors. In particular, if
\Sigma(A)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then
\mathcal{B}\mathfrak{C}(A)
is a
K(\pi,1)
space.

Proof.

The first part is the remaining sufficient condition of
4.18
, and the second result follows from
[
11
]
, where it is shown that the pairwise compatibility always holds in rank 3.
∎

The existence of a faithful group functor had previously been proven for
K
-stone algebras i.e. algebras
A
where every brick

satisfies

and

,

]
and hereditary algebras of finite or tame type

]
, preprojective algebras of Dynkin type ADE

states that their

define contraction algebras whose

.

For Nakayama(-like) algebras, the pairwise compatibility property of last factors is also satisfied in dimension 4 and higher

]
. However, this is not the case for preprojective algebras of Dynkin type ADE
[

]
, whose

]
and certain gentle algebras

]
. However, it is unknown whether

is a
